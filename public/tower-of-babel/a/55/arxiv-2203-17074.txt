Combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
Henrik Bachmann, Annika Burmester

Abstract
. We construct a family of q q -series with rational coefficients satisfying a variant of the extended double shuffle equations, which are a lift of a given ℚ \mathbb{Q} -valued solution of the extended double shuffle equations. These q q -series will be called combinatorial (bi-)multiple Eisenstein series, and in depth one they are given by Eisenstein series. The combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series can be seen as an interpolation between the given ℚ \mathbb{Q} -valued solution of the extended double shuffle equations (as q → 0 q\rightarrow 0 ) and multiple zeta values (as q → 1 q\rightarrow 1 ). In particular, they are q q -analogues of multiple zeta values closely related to modular forms. Their definition is inspired by the Fourier expansion of multiple Eisenstein series introduced by Gangl-Kaneko-Zagier. Our explicit construction is done on the level of their generating series, which we show to be a so-called symmetril and swap invariant bimould.

m_{1}\tau+n_{1}\succ m_{2}\tau+n_{2}
iff
m_{1}>m_{2}
or
m_{1}=m_{2}\wedge n_{1}>n_{2}
. Since
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau+1)=\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)
the multiple Eisenstein series possess a Fourier expansion, i.e. an expansion in
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, which was calculated in
[
GKZ
]
for the
r=2
case and for arbitrary depth by the first author (
[
B2
]
). In depth one we have for
k\geq 3

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k}(\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}\\
\lambda\succ 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>0\\
\vee\,(m=0\wedge n>0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m\tau+n)^{k}}=\zeta(k)+\sum_{m>0}\underbrace{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(m\tau+n)^{k}}}_{{\large=:\Psi_{k}(m\tau)}}\,.

For even
k\geq 4
these are just the classical Eisenstein series, which are modular forms for the full modular group. When
k
is even, these differ from the Eisenstein series (
1.5
) defined in the introduction just by a factor of
(2\pi i)^{k}
. We refer to
\Psi_{k}(\tau)
as the
monotangent function
(
[
Bo
]
), which satisfies for
k\geq 2
the Lipschitz formula

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\tau+n)^{k}}=\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{d>0}d^{k-1}q^{d}\,.

(2.2)

This gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\zeta(k)+\sum_{m>0}\Psi_{k}(m\tau)=\zeta(k)+\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>0\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}q^{md}=:\zeta(k)+(-2\pi i)^{k}g(k)\,.

Here the
g(k)
are the generating series of the divisor-sums and for higher depths multiple versions of these
q
-series appear, which are defined for
k_{1},\dots k_{r}\geq 1
by

\displaystyle g(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}>\dots>m_{r}>0\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{n_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\dots\frac{n_{r}^{k_{r}-1}}{(k_{r}-1)!}q^{m_{1}n_{1}+\dots+m_{r}n_{r}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket\,.

(2.3)

These
q
-series were studied in detail in
[
B2
]
,
[
BK
]
and they can be seen as
q
-analogues of multiple zeta values since one can show that for
k_{1}\geq 2

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 1}(1-q)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}g(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\,.

(2.4)

In the Fourier expansion of (multiple) Eisenstein series, the
q
-series
g
always appear together with a power of
-2\pi i
and therefore we set for
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1

\displaystyle\hat{g}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=(-2\pi i)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}g(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}[\pi i]\llbracket q\rrbracket\,.

A multiple version of
\mathbb{G}_{k}(\tau)=\zeta(k)+\hat{g}(k)
is given by the following.

Theorem 2.1

(
r=1,2

[
GKZ
]
,
r\geq 1

[
B2
]
)
.

For
k_{1}\geq 3,k_{2},\dots,k_{r}\geq 2
there exist explicit
\alpha^{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r},j}\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that for
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)=\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})+\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<j<r\\
l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}\\
l_{1}\geq 2,l_{2},\dots,l_{r}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\alpha^{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r},j}\,\zeta(l_{1},\dots,l_{j})\hat{g}(l_{j+1},\dots,l_{r})+\hat{g}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\,.

In particular,
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)=\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})+\sum_{n>0}a_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)q^{n}
for some
a_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)\in\mathcal{Z}[\pi i]
.

We will sketch the proof of Theorem
2.1
in the following and then give an explicit example at the end of the section. First, observe that for
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 2
we have by the Lipschitz formula (
2.2
), that the
q
-series
\hat{g}
can be written as an ordered sum over monotangent functions

\displaystyle\hat{g}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})=\sum_{m_{1}>\dots>m_{r}>0}\Psi_{k_{1}}(m_{1}\tau)\cdots\Psi_{k_{r}}(m_{r}\tau)\,.

(2.5)

In general the multiple Eisenstein series can be written as ordered sums over
multitangent functions
(
[
Bo
]
), which are for
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 2
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
defined by

\displaystyle\Psi_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(\tau):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\\
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\tau+n_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(\tau+n_{r})^{k_{r}}}.

(2.6)

These functions were originally introduced by Ecalle and then in detail studied by Bouillot in
[
Bo
]
. To write
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)
in terms of these functions, one splits up the summation in the definition (
2.1
) into
2^{r}
parts, corresponding to the different cases where either
m_{i}=m_{i+1}
or
m_{i}>m_{i+1}
for
\lambda_{i}=m_{i}\tau+n_{i}
and
i=1,\dots,{r}
(
\lambda_{r+1}=0
). Then one can check that the multiple Eisenstein series can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\hat{g}^{*}(k_{1},\dots,k_{j})\zeta(k_{j+1},\dots,k_{r})\,,

(2.7)

where the
q
-series
\hat{g}^{*}
are given as ordered sums over multitangent functions by

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{*}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
0=r_{0}<r_{1}<\dots<r_{j-1}<r_{j}=r\\
m_{1}>\dots>m_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\Psi_{k_{r_{i-1}+1},\ldots,k_{r_{i}}}(m_{i}\tau)\,.

(2.8)

Further, one can show (
[
B1
, Construction 6.7]
) that the
q
-series
\hat{g}^{*}
satisfy the harmonic product formula, e.g.
\hat{g}^{*}(k_{1})\hat{g}^{*}(k_{2})=\hat{g}^{*}(k_{1},k_{2})+\hat{g}^{*}(k_{2},k_{1})+\hat{g}^{*}(k_{1}+k_{2})
. We will generalize this construction later in terms of generating series (Lemma
6.21
) and then use an analogue of the formula (
2.7
) as the definition for the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series. To obtain the statement in Theorem
2.1
one then uses the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Bo
, Theorem 6]

For
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 2
with
k=k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}
the multitangent function can be written as

\displaystyle\Psi_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{l_{1}+\dots+l_{j-1}+k_{j}+k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\binom{l_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}\zeta(l_{1},\dots,l_{j-1})\,\Psi_{l_{j}}(\tau)\,\zeta(l_{r},l_{r-1},\dots,l_{j+1})\ .

Moreover, the terms containing
\Psi_{1}(\tau)
vanish.

This theorem can be proven by using partial fraction decomposition (see Example
2.3
) and then using the shuffle product to show that the coefficient of
\Psi_{1}(\tau)
vanishes.

Applying Theorem
2.2
to (
2.8
), we see by (
2.5
), that the
\hat{g}^{*}
can be written as a
\mathcal{Z}
-linear combination of
\hat{g}
. This proves Theorem
2.1
, since one can also show that all the appearing multiple zeta values have the correct depth.

Example 2.3
.

We give one explicit example in depth two. In this case, (
2.7
) reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\tau)=\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})+\hat{g}^{\ast}(k_{1})\zeta(k_{2})+\hat{g}^{\ast}(k_{1},k_{2})\,,

where
\hat{g}^{\ast}(k_{1})=\sum_{m_{1}>0}\Psi_{k_{1}}(m_{1}\tau)=\hat{g}(k_{1})
and

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{\ast}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}>0}\Psi_{k_{1},k_{2}}(m_{1}\tau)+\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Psi_{k_{1}}(m_{1}\tau)\Psi_{k_{2}}(m_{2}\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}>0}\Psi_{k_{1},k_{2}}(m_{1}\tau)+\hat{g}(k_{1},k_{2})\,.

Considering the special case
(k_{1},k_{2})=(3,2)
one sees by partial fraction decomposition

\displaystyle\Psi_{3,2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}}\frac{1}{(\tau+n_{1})^{3}(\tau+n_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(n_{1}-n_{2})^{2}(\tau+n_{1})^{3}}+\frac{2}{(n_{1}-n_{2})^{3}(\tau+n_{1})^{2}}+\frac{3}{(n_{1}-n_{2})^{4}(\tau+n_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(n_{1}-n_{2})^{3}(\tau+n_{2})^{2}}-\frac{3}{(n_{1}-n_{2})^{4}(\tau+n_{2})}\right)=3\zeta(3)\Psi_{2}(\tau)+\zeta(2)\Psi_{3}(\tau)\,,

and therefore
\hat{g}^{\ast}(3,2)=3\zeta(3)\hat{g}(2)+\zeta(2)\hat{g}(3)+\hat{g}(3,2)
. In total we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{3,2}(\tau)=\zeta(3,2)+3\zeta(3)\hat{g}(2)+2\zeta(2)\hat{g}(3)+\hat{g}(3,2)\,.

3.
Moulds, bimoulds and quasi-shuffle products

First, we will recall some basic facts on quasi-shuffle products (
[
Bo2
]
,
[
H
]
,
[
HI
]
). Let
L
be a countable set whose elements we will refer to as
letters
. A monic monomial in the non-commutative polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
will be called a
word
and we denote the empty word by
{\bf 1}
.
Suppose we have a commutative and associative product
\diamond
on the vector space
\mathbb{Q}L
. Then the
quasi-shuffle product

\ast_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
is defined as the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear product, which satisfies
{\bf 1}\ast_{\diamond}w=w\ast_{\diamond}{\bf 1}=w
for any word
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
and

\displaystyle aw\ast_{\diamond}bv=a(w\ast_{\diamond}bv)+b(aw\ast_{\diamond}v)+(a\diamond b)(w\ast_{\diamond}v)

(3.1)

for any letters
a,b\in L
and words
w,v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
. This gives a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
, which is called quasi-shuffle algebra. Moreover, one can equip this algebra with the structure of a Hopf algebra
[
H
, Section 3]
, where the coproduct is given for
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
by the deconcatenation coproduct

\displaystyle\Delta(w)=\sum_{uv=w}u\otimes v\,.

(3.2)

A well-known example is the shuffle Hopf algebra. Define the product on
\mathbb{Q}L
by
a\diamond b=0
for all
a,b\in L
. Then the corresponding quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
is the shuffle product, usually denoted by
\shuffle
. The antipode in the shuffle Hopf algebra is given by

\displaystyle S(a_{1}\dots a_{r})=(-1)^{r}a_{r}\dots a_{1},\qquad a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in L,

so the defining property of
S
yields the following relations in
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any non-empty word
w=a_{1}\dots a_{r}
in
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
, it is

\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}a_{i}a_{i-1}\dots a_{1}\shuffle a_{i+1}a_{i+2}\dots a_{r}=0.

To work with quasi-shuffle products it is convenient to consider generating series. For this, we will introduce the notion of moulds and bimoulds, which were introduced by Ecalle. We refer to the article
[
Bo2
]
for a good overview on mould theory and a thorough list of reference for the original works of Ecalle.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. A family
Z=(Z^{(r)})_{r\geq 0}
with
Z^{(0)}\in\mathcal{A}
and
Z^{(r)}\in\mathcal{A}\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{r}\rrbracket
for
r\geq 1
will be called a
mould
with values in
\mathcal{A}
.

Given a mould
Z=(Z^{(r)})_{r\geq 0}
we will call the
Z^{(r)}
the depth
r
part of
Z
. All moulds considered in this article satisfy
Z^{(0)}=1
, so we usually just give the depth
r\geq 1
parts when defining moulds. Since the depth is clear from the number of variables we will just write
Z(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
instead of
Z^{(r)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
in the following. Let
Z=(Z^{(r)})_{r\geq 0}
be an
\mathcal{A}
-valued mould, then we define for
r\geq 1
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
the elements
z(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathcal{A}
as the coefficients of its depth
r
part

\displaystyle Z(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=:\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1}z(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}\,.

(3.3)

Consider the set of letters
L_{z}=\{z_{k}\mid k\geq 1\}
. Then for any commutative and associative product
\diamond
on
\mathbb{Q}L_{z}
we obtain a quasi-shuffle algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra,
Z
an
\mathcal{A}
-valued mould with coefficients
z
as defined in (
3.3
), and
\diamond
a commutative and associative product on
\mathbb{Q}L_{z}
.

(i)

The mould
Z
is called
\diamond
-symmetril
if the
coefficient map

\varphi_{Z}:\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}
given on the generators by
\varphi_{Z}({\bf 1})=1
and

z_{k_{1}}\dots z_{k_{r}}\mapsto z(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})

is an algebra homomorphism from
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
to
\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

If
\diamond
is given by
z_{k_{1}}\diamond z_{k_{2}}=z_{k_{1}+k_{2}}
, then we call a
\diamond
-symmetril mould
symmetril
.

(iii)

If the product
\diamond
is given by
z_{k_{1}}\diamond z_{k_{2}}=0
, then we call a
\diamond
-symmetril mould
symmetral
.

Let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be moulds with values in
\mathcal{A}
. The
product

Z_{1}\times Z_{2}
is the mould given by

\displaystyle(Z_{1}\times Z_{2})(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{r}Z_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{j})Z_{2}(X_{j+1},\dots,X_{r})\,.

Since we usually have
Z^{(0)}=1
, the first term (
j=0
) in the above sum is simply given by
Z_{2}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
and the last term (
j=r
) is given by
Z_{1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
. Equipped with this product the space of all (
\mathcal{A}
-valued) moulds becomes a non-commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

Definition 3.4
.

(i)

For a mould
Z
we define the mould
Z^{\sharp}
by

\displaystyle Z^{\sharp}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})

\displaystyle=Z(X_{1}+\dots+X_{r},\dots,X_{1}+X_{2},X_{1}).

(ii)

Let
F=\sum_{r\geq 0}a_{r}T^{r}\in\mathcal{A}\llbracket T\rrbracket
be a formal power series with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}
. We can view
F
as a mould with values in
\mathcal{A}
, which we also denote by
F
and which is in depth
r\geq 0
defined by
F^{(r)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=a_{r}
. We call such a mould a
constant mould
.

Also notice that the product of two constant moulds is exactly the constant mould coming from the product of their power series.

(iii)

For an
\mathcal{A}
-valued mould
Z
with coefficients (
3.3
) we define the constant mould
\Gamma^{Z}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{Z}:=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{Z}_{r}T^{r}:=\exp\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}z(n)T^{n}\right)\,.

(3.4)

(iv)

For an
\mathcal{A}
-valued mould
Z
define the mould

Z_{\gamma}:=Z^{\sharp}\times\Gamma^{Z},

i.e. explicitly we have

\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}\gamma^{Z}_{j}Z(X_{1}+\dots+X_{r-j},\dots,X_{1}+X_{2},X_{1})\,.

Moreover, define its coefficients
z_{\gamma}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathcal{A}
by

\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=:

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1}z_{\gamma}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}\,.

Conversely, the mould
Z
can also be written in terms of
Z_{\gamma}

\displaystyle Z(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\tilde{\gamma}^{Z}_{j}Z_{\gamma}(X_{r},X_{r-1}-X_{r},\dots,X_{j+1}-X_{j+2})\,,

(3.5)

where (by using
[
HI
, (32)]
for the last equation) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\gamma}^{Z}_{k}T^{k}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k})T^{k}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}z(n)T^{n}\right)\,.

(3.6)

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
Z
an
\mathcal{A}
-valued mould. We say that the mould
Z

satisfies the extended double shuffle equations
if the mould
Z
is symmetril and the mould
Z_{\gamma}
is symmetral.

Example 3.6
.

For a mould
Z
, the extended double shuffle equations in depth two are

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z(X_{1})Z(X_{2})&=Z(X_{1},X_{2})+Z(X_{2},X_{1})+\frac{Z(X_{1})-Z(X_{2})}{X_{1}-X_{2}}\,,\\
Z_{\gamma}(X_{1})Z_{\gamma}(X_{2})&=Z_{\gamma}(X_{1},X_{2})+Z_{\gamma}(X_{2},X_{1})\\
&=Z(X_{1}+X_{2},X_{1})+Z(X_{1}+X_{2},X_{2})+\gamma_{2}^{Z}\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

The motivating example for these equations is the mould of (stuffle regularized) multiple zeta values
\mathfrak{z}
, whose depth
r
part is defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1}\zeta^{\ast}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}\,.

The mould
\mathfrak{z}
satisfies the extended double shuffle equations (
[
IKZ
]
,
[
E
]
) and the corresponding relations obtained for multiple zeta values are exactly the extended double shuffle relations mentioned in the introduction.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. A
bimould
with values in
\mathcal{A}
is a family
B=(B^{(r)})_{r\geq 0}
with
B^{(0)}\in\mathcal{A}
and
B^{(r)}\in\mathcal{A}\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{r},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{r}\rrbracket
for
r\geq 1
.

As is the case of moulds, we will call
B^{(r)}
the depth
r
part of
B
and since the depth is clear from the number of variables and we will just write
B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}
instead of
B^{(r)}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}
in the following. Moreover, all appearing bimoulds will also satisfy
B^{(0)}=1
, hence we often restrict to the case
r\geq 1
when defining bimoulds.

For bimoulds, we will consider the alphabet

L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}=\{z^{k}_{d}\mid k\geq 1,d\geq 0\}\,,

which can be seen as a generalization of
L_{z}
. For a commutative and associative product
\diamond
on
\mathbb{Q}L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}
we obtain a quasi-shuffle algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra,
B
a
\mathcal{A}
-valued bimould, and
\diamond
a commutative and associative product on
\mathbb{Q}L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}
.

(i)

If the coefficients
b\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}\in\mathcal{A}
of
B
in depth
r
are given by

\displaystyle B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{Y_1,\ldots, Y_r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1\\
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}b\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{d_{1}}}{d_{1}!}\cdots\frac{Y_{r}^{d_{r}}}{d_{r}!},

then we define the
coefficient map of
B
as the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\varphi_{B}:\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}\rangle\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
on the generators by
\varphi({\bf 1})=1
and

\displaystyle z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}\dots z^{k_{r}}_{d_{r}}\mapsto b\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}\,.

(ii)

The mould
B
is called
\diamond
-symmetril
if the coefficient map is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism

\varphi_{B}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\,.

(iii)

If
\diamond
is given by
z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}\diamond z^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}}=z^{k_{1}+k_{2}}_{d_{1}+d_{2}}
, then we call a
\diamond
-symmetril bimould
symmetril
.

(iv)

If the product
\diamond
is given by
z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}\diamond z^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}}=0
, then we call a
\diamond
-symmetril bimould
symmetral
.

If
B
is symmetril, then in depth two we have as an analogue of the first equation in (
3.7
)

\displaystyle B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}

\displaystyle=B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}+B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2,X_1}{Y_2,Y_1}+\frac{B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_2}-B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_2}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}\,.

Similar as for moulds, we define the
product
of two bimoulds
B
and
C
as the bimould
B\times C
given by

\displaystyle(B\times C)\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_j}{Y_1,\dots,Y_j}C\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_{j+1},\dots,X_r}{Y_{j+1},\dots,Y_r}\,.

Proposition 3.9
.

If
B
and
C
are
\diamond
-symmetril (bi)moulds then
B\times C
is
\diamond
-symmetril.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{B},\varphi_{C}
be the coefficient maps of the (bi)moulds
B
and
C
and write
m:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
for the multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
. Then we see by definition that the coefficient map of
B\times C
is the convolution product of
\varphi_{B}
and
\varphi_{C}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\varphi_{B\times C}=m\circ(\varphi_{B}\otimes\varphi_{C})\circ\Delta\,,

where
\Delta
is the coproduct (
3.2
) on
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
for
L=L_{z}
or
L=L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}
. This shows that
\varphi_{B\times C}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is an algebra homomorphism if
\varphi_{B}
and
\varphi_{C}
are and therefore
B\times C
is
\diamond
-symmetril.
∎

There is another important property of bimoulds, which is closely related to the conjugation of partitions (see
[
B1
]
,
[
B3
]
,
[
BI
]
and
[

]
).

Definition 3.10
.

A bimould
B
is called
swap invariant
if for all
r\geq 1

\displaystyle B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=B\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{Y_1+\dots+Y_r,Y_1+\dots+Y_{r-1},\dots,Y_1+Y_2,Y_1}{X_r, X_{r-1}-X_r,\dots,X_2-X_3,X_1-X_2}\,.

(3.8)

An explicit formula for the coefficients on the right-hand side of (
3.8
) can be found in
[

, Remark 3.14]
, where the swap is denoted by the involution
\iota
and the coefficients
b
are denoted by
P_{\mathrm{s}}
.

4.
From moulds to bimoulds and the bimould
\mathfrak{b}

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-valued mould, which satisfies the extended double shuffle equations and is in depth one given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{b}(X)=-\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}X^{k-1}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\zeta(2m)}{(2\pi i)^{2m}}X^{2m-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{X}-\frac{1}{e^{X}-1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

(4.1)

In particular, the coefficients
\beta
of
\mathfrak{b}
(as defined in (
3.3
)) are a
\mathbb{Q}
-valued solution to the extended double shuffle equations.

Remark 4.1
.

Such a mould
\mathfrak{b}
satisfying the extended double shuffle equations exists by the work by Racinet (
[
R
]
) or by combining the work of Drinfeld (
[
D
]
) and Furusho (
[
F
]
), we will give a short explanation how to obtain such an element. In
[
R
, section IV]
, the space
\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})\subset\mathbb{Q}\langle\langle L_{x}\rangle\rangle
is introduced, where
L_{x}=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. It is then shown that the space
\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})
is non-empty, so we can pick an element for
\lambda=\beta(2)=-\frac{1}{24}
, i.e.
b\in\operatorname{DM}_{-\frac{1}{24}}(\mathbb{Q})
. There is a canonical projection
\pi_{z}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\langle L_{x}\rangle\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\langle L_{z}\rangle\rangle
, which is given on the generators by
x_{0}^{k-1}x_{1}\mapsto z_{k}
and maps each word ending in
x_{0}
to
0
. So applying the map
z_{k_{1}}\dots z_{k_{r}}\mapsto X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}
to the depth
r
component of the element

b_{\ast}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}(\pi_{z}(b)\mid z_{n})z_{1}^{n}\right)\pi_{z}(b)

yields a family of generating series
\mathfrak{b}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}]
, which defines a mould
\mathfrak{b}
satisfying the extended double shuffle equations.

In
[
D
, §5]
, the space
\operatorname{M}_{\mu}(\mathbb{Q})
of associators is defined for each
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
. It is shown that the space
\operatorname{M}_{\mu}(\mathbb{Q})
is non-empty, thus we choose an element
b\in\operatorname{M}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. By
[
F
, Cor 0.4]
, there is an embedding
\operatorname{M}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{DM}_{-\frac{1}{24}}(\mathbb{Q})
(the definition of
\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})
in
[
F
]
slightly differs from the original one given in
[
R
]
, thus one has to be careful with the signs). So take the image of
b
under the embedding and proceed as before to obtain a mould
\mathfrak{b}
with values in
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the extended double shuffle equations.

Both approaches do not provide an explicit construction of such a solution, this is an open problem so far. In low depths, there exist explicit rational solutions (
[
E
]
,
[
Br
]
,
[
GKZ
]
), which then gives possible candidates for
\mathfrak{b}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
in the case
r\leq 3
. See also
[
B3
, Section 3]
for an explicit expression of the bimould
\mathfrak{b}
in depth two coming from the solution presented in
[
GKZ
]
or see
[
BKM
]
on how to construct directly such a bimould in depth two out of a power series satisfying the Fay-identity (e.g. a variant of
\coth
).

The mould
\mathfrak{b}
is not unique, there are different choices starting in weight
8
. In the following, we will fix a mould
\mathfrak{b}
with values in
\mathbb{Q}
, which satisfies the extended double shuffle equations and which is given by (
4.1
) in depth one. In particular, everything we will define in the following will depend on this choice.

The following gives natural constructions to obtain bimoulds out of moulds.

Definition 4.2
.

(i)

For a mould
Z
we define the two bimoulds
X^{Z}
and
Y^{Z}
by

\displaystyle X^{Z}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}:=Z(X_{1},\dots,X_{r}),\qquad Y^{Z}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}:=Z(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{r})

(ii)

For a mould
Z
we define the bimould
B^{Z}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}B^{Z}=Y^{Z_{\gamma}}\times X^{Z}\,,\end{split}

(4.2)

so explicitly we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}B^{Z}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}&=\sum_{j=0}^{r}Z_{\gamma}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{j})Z(X_{j+1},\dots,X_{r})\\
&=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq r}\gamma^{Z}_{i}Z(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{j-i},\dots,Y_{1}+Y_{2},Y_{1})Z(X_{j+1},\dots,X_{r})\,,\end{split}

(4.3)

where the coefficients
\gamma^{Z}_{i}
are given by (
3.4
).

This construction will be used to obtain a bimould version of
\mathfrak{b}
in Definition
4.6
. We will show that
B^{Z}
is always a swap invariant bimould and, if
Z
satisfies the extended double shuffle relations, then
B^{Z}
is additionally symmetril.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any mould
Z
the bimould
B^{Z}
is swap invariant.

Proof.

The swap of
B^{Z}
is given by

\displaystyle B^{Z}

\displaystyle\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{Y_1+\dots+Y_r,Y_1+\dots+Y_{r-1},\dots,Y_1+Y_2,Y_1}{X_r, X_{r-1}-X_r,\dots,X_2-X_3,X_1-X_2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq r}\gamma_{i}Z(X_{r-j+i+1},X_{r-j+i+2},\dots,X_{r})Z(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{r-j},\dots,Y_{1})\,.

Making the change of variables
j^{\prime}=r-j+i
, we see that above sum equals

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq j^{\prime}\leq r}\gamma_{i}Z(X_{j^{\prime}+1},X_{j^{\prime}+2},\dots,X_{r})Z(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{j^{\prime}-i},\dots,Y_{1})=B^{Z}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}\,.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If a mould
Z
satisfies the extended double shuffle equations, then the bimould
B^{Z}
is symmetril.

Proof.

Let
Z
satisfy the extended double shuffle equations, so
Z
is a symmetril mould and
Z_{\gamma}
is a symmetral mould. We immediately obtain that
X^{Z}
is a symmetril bimould and
Y^{Z_{\gamma}}
is a symmetral bimould. If a bimould does not depend on the variables
X_{i}
, symmetrility is equivalent to symmetrality. In particular, the bimould
Y^{Z_{\gamma}}
is also symmetril. In (
4.2
) we see that
B^{Z}=Y^{Z_{\gamma}}\times X^{Z}
, so by Proposition
3.9
also
B^{Z}
is symmetril.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
BIM
]
, the relationship between the classical extended double shuffle equations and the relations of the coefficients of swap invariant and symmetril bimoulds will be explained in detail. In particular, it will be shown that in the special case of moulds our Definition
3.5
coincides with the classical notion of extended double shuffle equations used in
[
IKZ
]
and
[
R
]
.

Definition 4.6
.

For the fixed
\mathbb{Q}
-valued mould
\mathfrak{b}
we define its corresponding bimould by
\mathfrak{b}=B^{\mathfrak{b}}
. By abuse of notation we denote the mould and the bimould by
\mathfrak{b}
, it will become clear from the the set of variables which one is meant. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq r}\gamma_{i}\mathfrak{b}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{j-i},\dots,Y_{1}+Y_{2},Y_{1})\mathfrak{b}(X_{j+1},\dots,X_{r})\,,

where
\gamma_{k}=\gamma^{\mathfrak{b}}_{k}
with the notation in (
3.4
), i.e. with (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}X^{k}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\beta(n)X^{n}\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\frac{B_{n}}{2n!}X^{n}\right)\,.

Corollary 4.7
.

The bimould
\mathfrak{b}
is swap invariant and symmetril.

Proof.

This is just a special case of Proposition
4.3
and
4.4
.
∎

5.
The bimould
\mathfrak{g}

For
m\geq 1
, we define the following power series in
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket

\displaystyle L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\frac{e^{X+mY}q^{m}}{1-e^{X}q^{m}}\,,

(5.1)

which will be used in the construction of combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series and which is the building block of the following bimould.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the bimould
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=\sum_{m_{1}>\dots>m_{r}>0}L_{m_{1}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}\dots L_{m_{r}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_r}{Y_r}\,.

Proposition 5.2

(
[
B1
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

The bimould
\mathfrak{g}
is swap invariant.

The coefficients
g
of
\mathfrak{g}
as defined in Definition
3.8
(i) are explicitly given by

\displaystyle g\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}>\dots>m_{r}>0\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{n_{1}^{k_{1}-1}m_{1}^{d_{1}}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\cdots\frac{n_{r}^{k_{r}-1}m_{r}^{d_{r}}}{(k_{r}-1)!}q^{m_{1}n_{1}+\dots+m_{r}n_{r}}\,.

(5.2)

These coefficients are generalizations of the
q
-series defined in (
2.3
).
The coefficient of
q^{n}
is given by the sum over all
m_{1}n_{1}+\dots+m_{r}n_{r}=n
with
m_{1}>\dots>m_{r}>0,n_{1},\dots,n_{r}>0
, i.e. all partitions of
n
with
r
different parts
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
and multiplicities
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}
.
This sum is invariant under the conjugation of partitions, which on the level of the generating series
\mathfrak{g}
corresponds exactly to the swap invariance (
3.8
). This describes a combinatorial proof of Proposition
5.2
.
Moreover, see
[
B3
]
for the interpretation of the coefficients of the bimould
\mathfrak{g}
as a generalization of the generating series of classical divisor-sums and their derivatives.

The bimould
\mathfrak{g}
is not symmetril, but we can define a product
\hat{\diamond}
such that it becomes
\hat{\diamond}
-symmetril. For this, we need the following property of the series
L_{m}
defined in (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
m\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_2}-L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_2}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle+\left(2\mathfrak{b}(X_{2}-X_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_2}+\left(2\mathfrak{b}(X_{1}-X_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_2}\,,

where
\mathfrak{b}(X)=-\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}X^{k-1}
is the depth one part of the mould
\mathfrak{b}
defined in (
4.1
).

Proof.

By direct calculation one checks that
L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\frac{e^{X+mY}q^{m}}{1-e^{X}q^{m}}
satisfies

\displaystyle L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}=\frac{1}{e^{X_{1}-X_{2}}-1}L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_2}+\frac{1}{e^{X_{2}-X_{1}}-1}L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_2},

which gives the above formula by using
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{X^{n}}{n!}=\frac{X}{e^{X}-1}
and the parity of
\mathfrak{b}
.
∎

From this lemma, one can deduce the quasi-shuffle product satisfied by the coefficients
g
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Explicitly, define for
k_{1},k_{2},j\geq 1
the rational numbers

\displaystyle\lambda^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{j}=-\left((-1)^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-1-j}{k_{2}-j}+(-1)^{k_{2}}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-1-j}{k_{1}-j}\right)\frac{B_{k_{1}+k_{2}-j}}{(k_{1}+k_{2}-j)!}\,

and define the commutative and associative product
\hat{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}L_{z}^{\rm{bi}}
by

\displaystyle z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}\hat{\diamond}z^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}}=z^{k_{1}+k_{2}}_{d_{1}+d_{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\lambda^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{j}z^{j}_{d_{1}+d_{2}}\,.

(5.4)

Proposition 5.4
.

The bimould
\mathfrak{g}
is
\hat{\diamond}
-symmetril.

Proof.

This follows from
[
B1
, Theorem 3.6]
and is a consequence of Lemma
5.3
. For example, in lowest depth we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}&=\left(\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}+\sum_{m_{2}>m_{1}>0}+\sum_{m_{1}=m_{2}>0}\right)L_{m_{1}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}L_{m_{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:productlm}}{=}\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}+\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2,X_1}{Y_2,Y_1}+\frac{\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_1}-\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_1}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}\\
&+\left(2\mathfrak{b}(X_{2}-X_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\right)\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_1}+\left(2\mathfrak{b}(X_{1}-X_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\right)\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_1}\,.\end{split}

(5.5)

Considering the coefficients of (
5.5
) one sees that
\hat{\diamond}
in (
5.4
) gives
\varphi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}\ast_{\hat{\diamond}}z^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}})=\varphi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}})\varphi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}})
. The general case can be proven by induction over the depth.

∎

Proposition
5.4
shows the relationship between the bimould
\mathfrak{g}
and the depth one part of the mould
\mathfrak{b}
. This will play a crucial role in the construction of the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series.

6.
Combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series

In this section, we will introduce combinatorial (bi-)multiple Eisenstein series, which are the coefficients of the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
. Before we can give the definition of
\mathfrak{G}
we need to introduce three other bimoulds
\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}
,
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
, and
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, which all depend on a fixed choice of a symmetril and swap-invariant bimould
\mathfrak{b}
given in Definition
4.6
.

6.1.
The bimoulds
\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}
,
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
, and
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}

Similar as in Definition
3.4
(ii) we can view the power series
\exp\!\big(-\frac{T}{2}\big)\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket T\rrbracket
as a constant bimould. Moreover, define for any mould
Z
the mould
Z^{-}
for each
r\geq 1
by

\displaystyle Z^{-}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})=Z(-X_{1},\ldots,-X_{r}).

With this we define the following analogue of
\mathfrak{b}=B^{\mathfrak{b}}=Y^{\mathfrak{b}_{\gamma}}\times X^{\mathfrak{b}}

Definition 6.1
.

Define the bimould
\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}
as a product of bimoulds

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}=Y^{\mathfrak{b}_{\gamma}^{-}}\times\exp\!\left(-\frac{T}{2}\right)\times X^{\mathfrak{b}},

(6.1)

i.e. for each
r\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}\!\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{Y_1,\ldots, Y_r}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{2^{i}i!}\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_{i+1},\ldots,X_r}{-Y_1,\ldots, -Y_{r-i}}.

For
m\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
be the bimould given in depth
r\geq 1
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots, X_r}{Y_1,\ldots, Y_r}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1-X_j,\ldots,X_{j-1}-X_j}{Y_1,\ldots,Y_{j-1}}L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_j}{Y_1+\dots+ Y_r}\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}\!\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_r-X_j,\ldots, X_{j+1}-X_j}{Y_r,\ldots,Y_{j+1}}\,.

Observe that the depth one part of
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
is exactly given by the series

defined in (

).

Remark 6.2
.

(i) The bimould
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
can be also defined by using the flexion markers introduced in
[

]
(cf.
[
Bo
, Section 7.5.3]
).

(ii) The definition of
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
is inspired by the calculation of the Fourier expansion of multiple Eisenstein series. First observe that the power series

can be seen as the generating series of the monotangent functions for

. Namely, define the ‘combinatorial version’ of the monotangent function
\Psi^{\text{comb}}_{k}(\tau)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{d>0}d^{k-1}q^{d}
for
k\geq 1
by using simply the right hand side of the Lipschitz formula (
2.2
). Then we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\Psi^{\text{comb}}_{k}(m\tau)X^{k-1}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{d>0}d^{k-1}q^{md}X^{k-1}=\sum_{d>0}e^{dX}q^{md}=\frac{e^{X}q^{m}}{1-e^{X}q^{m}}=L_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{0}\,.

So in analogy to Theorem
2.2
, the
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
can be seen as the generating series of the combinatorial version of the multitangent functions. In particular, the trifactorization of the mould of monotangent functions (used to prove Theorem

) in
[

is similar to our definition of
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
. The mould consisting of multiple zeta values in
[

]
is in the definition of
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
replaced by the mould
\mathfrak{b}
. Moreover, we omit the constant term for
\Psi_{1}(\tau)
, this is necessary for our construction of combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series for arbitrary indices (see the discussion before Remark 6.14 in
[
B1
]
).

Definition 6.3
.

We define the bimould
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
in depth

by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{Y_1,\ldots, Y_r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
0=r_{0}<r_{1}<\dots<r_{j-1}<r_{j}=r\\
m_{1}>\dots>m_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mathfrak{L}_{m_{i}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_{r_{i-1}+1},\ldots,X_{r_i}}{Y_{r_{i-1}+1},\ldots,Y_{r_i}}\,.

6.2.
The bimould

In analogy to (

Definition 6.4
.

(i)

We define the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times\mathfrak{b}\,.

(ii)

For
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
and
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\geq 0
we define the
combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series

G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket
as the coefficients of the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1\\
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots X_{r}^{k_{r}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{d_{1}}}{d_{1}!}\cdots\frac{Y_{r}^{d_{r}}}{d_{r}!}:=\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{Y_1,\ldots, Y_r}\,.

The number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}+d_{1}+\dots+d_{r}
will be called its
weight
and
r
its
depth
.

(iii)

In the special case
d_{1}=\dots=d_{r}=0
we define the
combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
for
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
by

\displaystyle G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{0,\dots,0}\,.

The mould given by their generating series will also denoted by
\mathfrak{G}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}):=\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{0,\ldots, 0}\,.

The main result of this work is the following.

Theorem 6.5
.

The bimould
\mathfrak{G}
is symmetril and swap invariant.

Proof.

We will show that
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is symmetril (Proposition
6.22
). Since
\mathfrak{b}
is also symmetril, we deduce by Proposition
3.9
that
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times\mathfrak{b}
is symmetril. For swap invariance we will show that
\mathfrak{G}
can be written as a sum of swap invariant bimoulds
\mathfrak{G}_{j}
(Proposition
6.27
, Theorem
6.26
) and therefore
\mathfrak{G}
is itself swap invariant.
∎

Before presenting the necessary results for the proof of Theorem
6.5
, we will give some examples and consequences.

Example 6.6
.

(i)

In depth
r=1
we have
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}
and therefore

\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}+\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}\,.

So the coefficients are for
k\geq 1,d\geq 0
given by

\displaystyle G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{d,0}\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}-\delta_{k,1}\frac{B_{d+1}}{2(d+1)}+\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{m,n\geq 1}m^{d}n^{k-1}q^{mn}.

(6.2)

We see that for
k>d\geq 0
the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}
is essentially the
d
-th derivative of the Eisenstein series
G(k-d)
, since

\displaystyle G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-d-1)!}{(k-1)!}\left(q\frac{d}{dq}\right)^{d}G(k-d)\,.

The swap invariance in depth one just states
\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{Y}{X}
. On the level of coefficients this gives
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}=\frac{d!}{(k-1)!}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{d+1}{k-1}
, which can also be obtained from (
6.2
).

(ii)

In depth
r=2
the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1, X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}=\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}+\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}+\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}

and from the definition of
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{m}
we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\mathfrak{L}_{m_{1}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}\mathfrak{L}_{m_{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}+\sum_{m>0}\mathfrak{L}_{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1, X_2}{Y_1, Y_2}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}+\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1-X_2}{Y_1}\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_1+Y_2}+\mathfrak{g}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1+Y_2}\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2-X_1}{Y_2}\,.

This gives an explicit expression of
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}
in terms of the
\beta
and the
q
-series
g
, which we will omit here.

(iii)

In depth
r=2
the swap invariance reads
\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}=\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{Y_1+Y_2,Y_1}{X_2,X_1-X_2}
, which gives for
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1,d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq a\leq d_{1}\\
0\leq b\leq k_{2}-1\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{b}}{a!\,b!}\frac{d_{1}!}{(k_{1}-1)!}\frac{(d_{2}+a)!}{(k_{2}-1-b)!}\,\,G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{d_2+1+a,\,d_1+1-a}{k_2-1-b,\,k_1-1+b}\,.

(6.3)

In Proposition
6.7
this formula is used to give an analogue of the shuffle product formula for combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series.

(iv)

We saw in Example
2.3
that
\mathbb{G}_{3,2}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{3,2}(\tau)=\zeta(3,2)+3\zeta(3)\hat{g}(2)+2\zeta(2)\hat{g}(3)+\hat{g}(3,2)\,.

In comparison, we get

\displaystyle G(3,2)=\beta(3,2)+2\beta(2)g(3)+g(3,2)=g(3,2)-\frac{1}{12}g(3)\,.

Notice that
\beta(3)=0
and therefore this is exactly the same expression after replacing
\zeta
by the rational numbers
\beta
and
\hat{g}
by
g
.

(v)

The combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
G(2,1,1)
is given by

\displaystyle G(2,1,1)

\displaystyle=\beta(2,1,1)+\frac{1}{6}g(2)-g(2,1)+g(2,1,1)\,.

By duality
\beta(4)=\beta(2,1,1)
, but one can check that
G(4)\neq G(2,1,1)
, i.e. the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series do not satisfy the duality relations. Another example for this is
G(3)\neq G(2,1)
, since

\displaystyle\sum_{n>0}\frac{nq^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}=q\frac{d}{dq}G(1)=G(3)-G(2,1)\,.

As an analogue of the double shuffle equations of multiple zeta values in depth two (
1.3
), the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series satisfy the following.

Proposition 6.7
.

For
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1,d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1}{d_1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_2}{d_2}&=G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}+G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_2,k_1}{d_2,d_1}+G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1+k_2}{d_1+d_2}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}\\
e_{1}+e_{2}=d_{1}+d_{2}\\
l_{1},l_{2}\geq 1,e_{1},e_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\binom{l_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}\binom{d_{1}}{e_{1}}(-1)^{d_{1}-e_{1}}+\binom{l_{1}-1}{k_{2}-1}\binom{d_{2}}{e_{1}}(-1)^{d_{2}-e_{1}}\right)G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{l_1,l_2}{e_1,e_2}\\
&+\frac{d_{1}!d_{2}!}{(d_{1}+d_{2}+1)!}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{k_{1}-1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1+k_2-1}{d_1+d_2+1}\,.\end{split}

Proof.

The first equality is just a direct consequence of the symmetrility. To show the second equality, first use the swap invariance to get
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1}{d_1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_2}{d_2}=\frac{d_{1}!d_{2}!}{(k_{1}-1)!(k_{2}-1)!}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{d_1+1}{k_1-1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{d_2+1}{k_2-1}
and then evaluate this product by the first equality. Using then again the swap invariance in depth one and depth two (
6.3
) yields the result.
∎

Proposition 6.15

For all

\displaystyle g\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,\dots,k_r}{d_1,\dots,d_r}\in\mathcal{G}^{{\rm bi}}\,.

\mathcal{Z}_{q}
of

In depth one we have by definition

(ii)

We will explain now why the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series interpolate between the rational solution
\beta
and multiple zeta values.
In the case

we get as a direct consequence of the proof of Proposition
6.15
and (

) that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 1}(1-q)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}G(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\,.

(6.5)

The limit is independent of the choice of the rational solution to the double shuffle equations
\mathfrak{b}
, since the limit

considers just the highest depth term of the
q
-series
g
in
G
. In the case

this limit does not exist, but we can consider a regularized limit, which we will describe now. Using the notation as in Section
3
we can, as in the introduction, view the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map defined on the generators by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G:\mathfrak{H}^{1}&\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}\\
w=z_{k_{1}}\dots z_{k_{r}}&\longmapsto G(w):=G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\,.\end{split}

(6.6)

Since
\mathfrak{G}
is symmetril,
G
gives an algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{H}^{1},\ast)
to
\mathcal{G}
. Due to
\mathfrak{H}^{1}=\mathfrak{H}^{0}[z_{1}]
(cf.
[

, Proposition 1]
) we can write

for any

uniquely as

with

. Then we define the regularized version of the limit (

) as

\displaystyle{\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}}^{*}(1-q)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}(1-q)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}G(w_{0})=\zeta(w_{0})\,.

(6.7)

Notice that if
k_{1}\geq 2
, then

and thus (
6.7
) is equal to (
6.5
).

Proposition 6.17
.

For any
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\beta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}),

\displaystyle{\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}}^{*}(1-q)^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}}G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\ast}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\,.

Proof.

First notice that the constant terms of the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series are by construction the coefficients in
\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{0,\dots,0}
. The bimould
\mathfrak{b}
was defined by the mould
\mathfrak{b}
(Definition
4.6
), which satisfies the extended double shuffle equations. Since the mould
\mathfrak{b}_{\gamma}
is symmetral and
\mathfrak{b}_{\gamma}(0)=0
(as

\mathfrak{b}_{\gamma}(0,\dots,0)=0
. We deduce
\mathfrak{b}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{0,\dots,0}=\mathfrak{b}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
which shows the first equation. For the second equation observe that the stuffle regularized multiple zeta values
\zeta^{\ast}
are essentially defined in the same way as we constructed the regularized limit (

). Then since
G
and
\zeta^{\ast}
are algebra homomorphisms, we obtain the claim from (
6.5
).
∎

\displaystyle q\frac{d}{dq}

Proposition 6.30
.

For

\displaystyle\begin{split}q\frac{d}{dq}G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})=&\,G(2)G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\

&-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq r\\

&-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\end{subarray}}k_{i}G(k_{1},\dots,k_{i}+1,\dots,k_{r},1)-G(k_{1},\dots,k_{r},2)\,.\end{split}

(6.18)

In particular, the space
\mathcal{G}
is closed under
q\frac{d}{dq}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{G}
is symmetril, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_{j},X,X_{j+1},X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y_{j},Y,Y_{j+1},Y_r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{X-X_{j}}\left(\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y + Y_j,\dots,Y_r}-\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_j,\dots,X_r}{Y_1,\dots,Y + Y_j,\dots,Y_r}\right)\,.

Sending all
X_{j}\rightarrow X
, taking the derivative with respect to
Y
and setting
Y=0
gives by Proposition

6.29

\displaystyle q\frac{d}{dq}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X,\dots,X}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}=\frac{\partial}{\partial Y}\left(\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X,\dots,X}{Y_1,\dots,Y_r}-\sum_{j=0}^{r}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X,\dots,X}{Y_1,\dots,Y_j,Y,Y_{j+1},\dots,Y_r}\right)_{|Y=0}\,.

Using the swap invariance and renaming the variables we obtain

\displaystyle q\frac{d}{dq}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y,0,\dots,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial X}\left(\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,\dots,X_r}{Y,0,\dots,0}\right)_{|X=0}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial X}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1+X,\dots,X_{j}+X,X_{j},X_{j+1},\dots,X_r}{Y,0,\dots,0}\right)_{|X=0}\,,

where in the last sum we set
X_{r+1}:=0
.
The case
Y=0
yields the result by calculating the coefficients of the right-hand side.
∎

With the interpretation of
G
as an algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{H}^{1},\ast)
to
\mathcal{G}
in (
6.6
), we can give the following reinterpretation and consequence of Proposition
6.30
.

Corollary 6.31
.

(i)

For
w\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
the derivative of
G(w)
is given by

\displaystyle q\frac{d}{dq}G(w)=G(z_{2}\ast w-z_{2}\shuffle w)\,.

(ii)

Let
h:\mathfrak{H}^{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{1}
be the linear map defined on the generators by

\displaystyle h:w\longmapsto z_{2}\ast w-z_{2}\shuffle w\,.

Then for any
v,w\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
we have

\displaystyle h(w\ast v)-h(w)\ast v-w\ast h(v)\in\ker G\,.

Proof.

The equation in (i) is a direct consequence of
6.30
since the sums on the right-hand side of (
6.18
) correspond exactly to those indices which appear in the shuffle product of
z_{2}=xy
with
z_{k_{1}}\dots z_{k_{r}}=x^{k_{1}-1}y\dots x^{k_{r}-1}y
.
For (ii) we use that
G
is an algebra homomorphism and
q\frac{d}{dq}
is a derivation on
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket
. By (i) we have
q\frac{d}{dq}G(w)=G(h(w))
and therefore get
G(h(w\ast v)-h(w)\ast v-w\ast h(v))=0
.
∎

Notice that the map
h
is not a derivation on
(\mathfrak{H}^{1},\ast)
, i.e. the relations we obtain among the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series from Corollary
6.31
(ii) are non-trivial. For example, for
v=w=z_{1}
we get
G(4)=2G(2,2)-2G(3,1)
. This is the first relation, and the only in weight
4
, among combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series, since the
q
-series
g(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})
do not satisfy relations in lower weight (see
[
BK
, (1.9)]
). It would be interesting to see if one can describe
G(v\ast w-v\shuffle w)
for arbitrary
w,v\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
explicitly and if this can be used to obtain relations among combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series similar to Corollary
6.31
(ii).

Remark 6.32
.

In
[
F2
]
the Alekseev-Torossian associator, whose coefficients satisfy the extended double shuffle equations, is computed. It turns out that in depth

Cyclic Proofs for iGL via Corecursion
Borja Sierra-Miranda

Abstract
In [ 4 ] Daniyar Shamkanov proved that three distinct systems of sequent calculi for GL are equivalent. These systems consist in one with finite proofs, another with ill-founded proofs and the last one with cyclic proofs. The main tool used for proving the equivalence is corecursion. In this project, we prove the equivalence between a finitary sequent calculus for i GL and a cyclic calculus, using also coinductive methods.

Our main goal will be to prove the equivalence between
two sequent calculi for
i
GL
, one whose proofs are finite and
one whose proofs are cyclic (i.e. of possibly infinite height but only
through loops). Part of our main goal is to define a translation from

[

for

Note that the result we are going to proof was already

In the first section we are going to introduce intuitionistic

In the second section we are going to introduce the necessary tools

In the third section we are going to introduce sequent-calculi

2
Ill-founded finitely branching trees by coinduction

Coinduction is the dual principle of induction. If
inductive objects have constructors to construct them, coinductive
objects have destructors to break them apart. One of the simplest
examples of coinductive object are binary streams. These are
the functions in the set
\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
. The idea is that
given a stream
s
we can destruct it into an ordered pair
(b,s^{\prime})
where
b=s(0)\in\{0,1\}
and
s^{\prime}
is a stream defined as
s^{\prime}(n)=s^{\prime}(n+1)
. We can visualize this process in figure 1.

Figure 1:
Destructing a stream

For our purposes we need ill-founded (finitely-branching)
trees. These are trees where each node has a finite amount of
successors but it may have infinite paths. One example is the full
binary tree whose nodes are binary finite sequences (i.e. elements of

\{0,1\}^{*}
). Let us show it with another example in figure 2.

Figure 2:
Examples of trees

Note that from the picture we see that leaves are not banned, in
particular finite trees with finite branching are a particular case of
our trees. This kind of trees fits under the category of coinductive
object. To define coinductive objects we need to
first define coalgebras. The idea of a coalgebra is just a set with
an additional structure that allows us to break the elements of the set
apart.

3
Sequent calculus

We introduce the common definitions used in sequent
calculus with our definition of tree as any final coalgebra of the
tree endofunctor.
We will use sequents with one multiset at the left and
exactly one formula at the right side. The condition of having exactly
one formula at the right gives an intuitionistic calculus.

Definition 24

(
Sequent
)
.

A

is an ordered pair
(\Gamma,\varphi)

of a multiset of formulas
\Gamma

\varphi
,

subset of

numbers describes the path from the root to the leaf where the cycle
is contained, the other list describes the sequents and rule names in
that path and the number describes when does that cycle start in the path.

Developing a corecursion

Note that this looks similar to an recursion, but there is a key
difference. Recursion starts the calculation on the leaves and the
computation propagates from the leaves to the root. However, here it
is the other way around: after the first step we already know how the
root is going to look, but we do not know about the leaves yet. In
particular, this new tree may be infinite, since

do not need to be subtrees of
\pi

\iota^{i}_{0},\ldots,\iota^{i}_{k_{i}}
do not need to be subtrees of

and so on. So in each of those steps the height of the
finite trees may be increasing, so it may never end (it may also
be the case that it reduces and we end up with a leaf, that is the
reason our trees are allowed to have leaves).

Remark on definition
43
:

the first two conditions in the definition of
infinitary proof translations are always necessary and do not depend
on the progress condition of the infinitary system. However, the third
condition is constructed to ensure that the progress condition is
fulfilled. It would be interesting to give some natural class of
progress conditions and generalize this definition to make it work for
any such progress condition. Note that the definition here works for
progress conditions that are claiming that some rule is applied
infinitely many times. It can also be generalized to conditions that
claim that a particular premise of a rule appears infinitely often
(just claim that when the rule appears only one of the
\iota_{i}
can grow in height, namely the one corresponding to the premise that can
appear infinitely often). Also note that for our purposes claiming that
the height does not grow is enough, but it may be the case that we
need a finer measure of the finite proofs.
\blacksquare

If

4.3
From finitary to cyclic

Given a multiset of formulas
\Gamma
we can write it in an unique
way as
\Gamma=\Gamma^{s}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}
where

\Gamma^{s}
is the set resulting from the multiset
\Gamma

and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a multiset.

During this section let us have the functions

\displaystyle\textsf{contract}:\text{Proof}_{\text{fin}}(\text{i}\textsf{GL})\longrightarrow\text{Proof}_{\text{fin}}(\text{i}\textsf{GL}),

\displaystyle\textsf{l\"{o}b}:\text{Proof}_{\text{fin}}(\text{i}\textsf{GL})\longrightarrow\text{Proof}_{\text{fin}}(\text{i}\textsf{GL}),

\dotnecessary\Gamma,\necessary\varphi\Rightarrow\varphi

There is an unique

\vdots

\dotnecessary\Gamma^{s}\Rightarrow\varphi

\Pi,\necessary\Gamma^{\prime},\necessary\Gamma^{s}\Rightarrow\necessary\varphi

\Gamma\Rightarrow\varphi

In order to show the existence of this function we just need to
define an adequate

and set

The definition of

Corollary 47
.

If
\mathrel{\vdash}_{\text{i}\textsf{GL}}\Gamma\Rightarrow\varphi
then
\mathrel{\vdash}_{\text{i}\textsf{K4}_{\text{circ}}}\Gamma\Rightarrow\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be the proof of
\Gamma\Rightarrow\varphi
in
i
GL
. Now, consider
h(\pi)
. Note that by the definition of
h
the premises of box rules are always sequents with a set in the
left side. In addition,
since our calculus doesn’t have cut we know that the formulas in the
premises are subformulas of the formulas in the conclusion.
There are only finitely many subformulas of
\Gamma\cup\{\varphi\}
,
so there is
only a finite amount of sets of those formulas. So there is a finite
amount of premises of box rules. Now, suppose we have an infinite
branch in our proof, then we know that there are infinitely many
applications of the box rule, so there are infinitely many premises of
the box rule. This implies that at least one is repeated, so from any
infinite branch we can create a
cycle, and note that the end of the cycle will be a premise of
\text{R}_{\textsf{K4}}
, so it is a good cycle, as wanted.

\square

4.4
From cyclic to finitary

First of all, let us distinguish between two types of
assumptions. Boxed assumptions are going to be the assumptions that
have an application of
(\text{R}_{\textsf{K4}})
rule in the path
from the root to themselves and non-boxed assumptions are going to be
the rest.

Definition 48

(
Boxed and non-boxed assumptions
)
.

Let us have a finite derivation in
\mathcal{R}_{\text{i}\textsf{K4}}

called
\tau
and we define the following sets:

\displaystyle\Node_{\necessary}(\pi)=\{s\in\Node_{\text{Ass}}(\pi)\mid\exists s^{\prime}<s.\lab_{s^{\prime}}(\pi)=\text{R}_{\textsf{K4}}\},

\displaystyle\Node_{\cancel{\necessary}}(\pi)=\Node_{\text{Ass}}(\pi)\mathbin{\backslash}\Node_{\necessary}(\pi).

\hskip 5.69054pt\hfill{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\blacksquare}

In order to establish the direction from cyclic to finitary we need to
prove a lemma, from which the direction trivially follows. The idea is
that instead of doing induction in the cyclic proof we do induction in
cyclic derivations, so instead of proving what we want we will have
some additions expressing the behaviour of the assumptions in the
cyclic derivation. Then the case when we have a cyclic proof trivially
follows, since a proof is simply a derivation with no
assumptions.

By induction on the structure of

5
Conclusion

Using coinductive methods we have been able to prove the equivalence
between a finitary sequent calculus for
i
GL
and a cyclic
version in theorem
51
. In the future we consider two main extensions of this work.

First, it seems that the definition of infinitary proof translation
for other finitary systems with an ill-founded system may work just
changing point (3) of the definition, adapting it to make sure that
the progress condition of the particular infinitary system is
achieved. A first step would be to look into more examples. After that
it would be interesting to look for a class of progress conditions
whose infinitary proof translation can be computed automatically and
for systems with those conditions.

Second, unlike in
[
4
]
we have not proven that ill-founded
proofs are equivalent to finitary and cyclic ones. Trying to use
Shamkanov’s method of proving contraction via induction in the
formula does not work in principle due to our
(\mathbin{\rightarrow}\text{L})

rule, since at the left premise the implication is not
decomposed. Trying to adapt the proof for the finitary
system also does not work since it uses induction in the height
of the proof. So the question arises: are infinitary proofs
equivalent to cyclic ones for our systems?

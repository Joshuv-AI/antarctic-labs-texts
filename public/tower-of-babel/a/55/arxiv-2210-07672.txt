Optimal estimation of local time and occupation time measure for an α \alpha -stable Lévy process Thanks: The authors gratefully acknowledge financial support of ERC Consolidator Grant 815703 “STAMFORD: Statistical Methods for High Dimensional Diffusions”.
Chiara Amorino, Arturo Jaramillo, Mark Podolskij

Abstract
We present a novel theoretical result on estimation of local time and occupation time measure of an α \alpha -stable Lévy process with α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \alpha\in(1,2) . Our approach is based upon computing the conditional expectation of the desired quantities given high frequency data, which is an L 2 L^{2} -optimal statistic by construction. We prove the corresponding stable central limit theorems and discuss a statistical application. In particular, this work extends the results of [ 20 ] , which investigated the case of the Brownian motion. Key words : high frequency data, local time, mixed normal distribution, occupation time, stable Lévy processes. AMS 2010 subject classifications : 62E17,60F05,11N60.

Introduction

1.1
The setting and overview

In this paper we consider a
pure jump

\alpha
-stable Lévy process

X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with
\alpha\in(1,2)
, defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}},\mathbb{P})
. The law of the process
X
is uniquely determined by the characteristic function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp(\textbf{i}\xi X_{t})]

\displaystyle=\exp({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\bm{i}\xi t\eta-\sigma t|\xi|^{\alpha}(1-\bm{i}\beta\tan(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2})\text{sgn}(\xi))})

(1.1)

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\geq 0

\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\in[-1,1]
and
\sigma>0
.
We will focus on estimation of occupation time measure and the local time of
X
given high frequency data
\{X_{i/n}\}_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor nT\rfloor}
, with
T>0
being fixed and
n\to\infty
. We recall that for

a<b
the occupation time of
X
at the set
(x,\infty)

over the interval
[a,b]
, denoted by
O_{[a,b]}(x)
, is defined as

O_{[a,b]}(x):=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbbm{1}_{(x,\infty)}(X_{s})ds.

The local time of
X
at point
x\in\mathbb{R}
over the interval
[a,b]
denoted as

L_{[a,b]}(x)
is defined implicitly via the

occupation density formula
:

\displaystyle O_{[a,b]}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}L_{[a,b]}(y)dy\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

(1.2)

(if it exists). Throughout the paper we use the abbreviation
L_{t}:=L_{[0,t]}
and
O_{t}:=O_{[0,t]}
. The existence and smoothness properties of local times of stochastic processes have been extensively studied in the 70’s and 80’s; we refer to articles
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
11
]
among many others. In particular, in the setting of pure jump
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes, the local time exists only for
\alpha\in(1,2)
(cf.
[
21
]
). Furthermore,
there exists a version of local time, which is continuous
in space and time.
Indeed, according to
[
4
, Theorems 2 and 3]
and

[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
,
there exists a version of the local time with
the following properties:

L_{t}(\cdot)\text{ is $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. locally H\"{o}lder continuous with index }(\alpha-1)/2-\varepsilon,

(1.3)

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,(\alpha-1)/2)
. Moreover, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1-1/\alpha)
and
T>0
, there exists a deterministic constant
C_{T}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}|L_{t}(x)-L_{s}(x)|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{T}}|t-s|^{(1-1/\alpha)-\varepsilon}\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(1.4)

In particular,
L_{(\cdot)}(x)
is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. locally Hölder continuous with index
(1-1/\alpha)-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1-1/\alpha)
.
Throughout the paper we consider the aforementioned Hölder continuous version of the local time.

The goal of this article is to introduce the
L^{2}
-optimal estimators of
L_{t}(x)
and
O_{t}(x)
, and study their asymptotic properties. For this purpose we define
the
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\sigma(\{X_{i/n}\ ;\ i\in\mathbb{N}\})

and construct the estimators via

\displaystyle S_{L,t}^{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}=\mathbb{E}[L_{t}(x)\ |\mathcal{A}_{n}]\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ S_{O,t}^{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}=\mathbb{E}[O_{t}(x)\ |\mathcal{A}_{n}].

(1.5)

By construction
S_{L,t}^{n}
(resp.
S_{O,t}^{n}
) is an
L^{2}
-optimal approximation of

L_{t}(x)
(resp.
O_{t}(x)
). We will show a functional
stable convergence

for both statistics, which exhibit a mixed normal limit
. Our main tool is Jacod’s stable central limit theorem for partial sums of high frequency data stated in
[
13
]
.

1.2
Related literature

Functional limit theorems for estimators of local times have been studied for numerous stochastic models. In the setting of
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes and related models, such estimators often take the form
G(x,\phi)^{n}=\{G(x,\phi)_{t}^{n};\ t\geq 0\}
, with

\displaystyle G(x,\phi)_{t}^{n}=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\phi\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x)\right),

(1.6)

where
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Consistency and asymptotic mixed normality for statistics
G(x,\phi)^{n}
and related functionals have been investigated in
[
7
,
14
]
in the setting of Brownian motion and continuous stochastic differential equations. Some optimality results for estimation of the local time and occupation time measure of a Brownian motion can be found in
[
20
]
.
Estimation errors for occupation time functionals of stationary Markov processes have been studied in
[
1
]
. Limit theorems for statistics of the form (
1.6
) in the case of the fractional Brownian motion are discussed in
[
15
,
17
,
18
,
23
]
, although the complete weak limit theory is far from being understood.

While estimation of local time and occupation time measure has an interest in its own right, accurate estimation of these objects can be useful for related statistical problems. For example, non-parametric estimators of the diffusion coefficient in a continuous stochastic differential equation often involve local times in the mixed normal limit, see e.g.
[
9
]
. In a similar spirit, local times and mixed normal local times appear as fundamental limits for additive functionals of a variety of Gaussian processes (see Papanicolaou, Stroock and Varadhan
[
22
]
, Jaramillo, Nourdin, Nualart and Peccati
[
16
]
as well as Minhao Hong, Heguang Liu and Fangjun Xu
[
12
]
), thus serving as simplifying probabilistic models for otherwise complex probabilistic objects. Efficient estimation of local times and occupation times is very beneficial for statistical inference in this framework.

Our main result about the asymptotic theory for local times is mostly related to the articles
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
26
]
. The paper
[
17
]
addresses consistency in the case of the linear fractional stable motion, a class which in particular includes stable Lévy processes.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 4 in
[
17
]
)
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function satisfying
\phi,\phi^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. Then, for every

,

\displaystyle G(x,\phi)_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}(\Omega)}}{{\rightarrow}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)dy\cdot L_{t}(x)\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{as }n\to\infty}.

In
[
18
,
19
,
26
]
the authors prove the asymptotic mixed normality for continuous version of the functional

, but only in the

.
From statistical point of view, this case is a less interesting one as we would like to use statistics of the type (
1.6
) as an estimator of the local time

. More importantly, in the setting

We will use stable convergence theorems for high frequency statistics introduced in
[

]
to show asymptotic mixed normality of standardised versions of

and

. These results are strongly related to an earlier work
[

, which considers the same problem in the setting of the Brownian motion. While their limit theorems are also based on the general results of
[

2
Preliminaries and main results

In this section we present the spectral representation of local times, which will be useful in the sequel. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of stable convergence and establish the asymptotic theory for the estimators
S^{n}_{L}
and
S^{n}_{O}
.

2.1
Local times and stable convergence

The analysis of occupation times and local times is an integral part of the theory of stochastic processes, which found manifold applications in probability during the past decades. We recall that, for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the local time of
the
\alpha
-stable Lévy process

X
,

\alpha\in(1,2)
, up to time
t
at
x
can be formally defined as
L_{t}(x):=\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(X_{s}-\lambda)ds
, where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta function. A rigorous definition of the local time is obtained by replacing
\delta_{0}
by the Gaussian kernel
\phi_{\epsilon}(x):=(2\pi\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}x^{2})
and taking the limit in probability for
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
For our purposes we will systematically use the following spectral representation for
L_{t}(x)
.
The proof of the representation gathered in the lemma below can be found in e.g.
[
17
, Proposition 11]
, for completeness we give the proof of the boundedness of the moments. It can be found in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence

\left\{\int_{[-m,m]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\textbf{i}\xi(X_{s}-x)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dsd\xi}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The limit as
m
tends to infinity, which we will denote by

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\textbf{i}\xi(X_{s}-x)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dsd\xi}

satisfies

\displaystyle L_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\textbf{i}\xi(X_{s}-x)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dsd\xi}.

(2.7)

Moreover, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{E}[(L_{t}(x))^{p}]<\infty
.

Recall furthermore that, by the definition of the occupation time
O_{t}(x)
, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{E}[(O_{t}(x))^{p}]<t^{p}
.

In what follows we will use the notion of
stable convergence
, which is originally due to Renyi
[
24
]
. Let
(\mathcal{S},\delta)
be a Polish space. Let
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of
\mathcal{S}
-valued and
\mathcal{F}
-measurable random variables defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and
Y
a random variable defined on an enlarged probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
. We say that
Y_{n}
converges stably to
Y
, if and only if for any continuous bounded function
g:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
and any bounded

\mathcal{F}
-measurable random variable
F
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[Fg(Y_{n})]=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[Fg(Y)].

(2.8)

In this case we write
Y_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Stably}}{{\rightarrow}}Y
. In this paper we deal with the space

of cádlág functions
D([0,T])
equipped with the Skorokhod
J_{1}
-topology
.

2.2
Main results

The following proposition provides an explicit expression of the statistics
S_{L,t}^{n}
and
S_{O,t}^{n}
.
The proof
of Proposition
2.2
is based upon the Markov property of
X
and the linearity in time of our objects.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
i\geq 1
, define the increments
\Delta_{i}^{n}X:=X_{\frac{i}{n}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}
. Consider the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x,y):=\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(x)|X_{1}=y]
and
F(x,y):=\int_{x}^{\infty}f(r,y)dr
. Then we obtain the identities

\displaystyle S_{L,t}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)+\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{n}(x)

(2.9)

\displaystyle S_{O,t}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}F\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)+\mathcal{E}_{O,t}^{n}(x)

(2.10)

where
\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{E}_{O,t}^{n}(x)
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\lfloor nt\rfloor/n,t]}(x)|\mathcal{A}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{O,t}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\lfloor nt\rfloor/n,t]}(x)|\mathcal{A}_{n}\right].

Moreover, the processes
\{n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{n}(x)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}\mathcal{E}_{O,t}^{n}(x)\}_{t\geq 0}
converge to zero in probability
uniformly on compact intervals
as
n\to\infty
.

The proof of Proposition
2.2
will be given in Section
4
.
The next theorem is a functional limit result for the error of the approximation of the occupation and local time by their
L^{2}
-optimal estimators.

Theorem 2.3
.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
and define the processes

\displaystyle W_{L,t}^{n}

\displaystyle:=n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}(S_{L,t}^{n}(x)-L_{t}(x))

\displaystyle W_{O,t}^{n}

\displaystyle:=n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}(S_{O,t}^{n}(x)-O_{t}(x)).

Then we obtain the functional stable convergence with respect to
J_{1}
topology:

\displaystyle W_{L}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Stably}}{{\rightarrow}}k_{L}B_{L(x)},\qquad W_{O}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Stably}}{{\rightarrow}}k_{O}B_{L(x)},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{as }n\to\infty,}

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion defined on an extended space and independent of
\mathcal{F}
. The constants
k_{L}
and
k_{O}
are defined as

\displaystyle k_{L}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[L_{1}(y)|X_{1}]-L_{1}(y))^{2}]dy

\displaystyle k_{O}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[O_{1}(z)|X_{1}]-O_{1}(z))^{2}\big]dz.

Remark 2.4
.

By the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem
(cf.
[
25
, Theorem 1.6, Section 5.1]
)
,
Theorem
2.3
implies
the stable convergence

\displaystyle W_{L}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Stably}}{{\rightarrow}}k_{L}{\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sqrt{L_{s}(x)}B(ds)},\qquad W_{O}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Stably}}{{\rightarrow}}k_{O}{\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sqrt{L_{s}(x)}B(ds)}.

as
n\to\infty
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

The statement above can be directly used to construct confidence regions for
L_{t}(x),O_{t}(x)
if the law of the Lévy process
X
is known.
Indeed, by properties of stable convergence, it holds for any fixed

t>0

as
n\to\infty
:

\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\left(S_{L,t}^{n}(x)-L_{t}(x)\right)}{k_{L}\sqrt{S_{L,t}^{n}(x)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)

(2.11)

and

\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}\left(S_{O,t}^{n}(x)-O_{t}(x)\right)}{k_{O}\sqrt{S_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L},t}^{n}(x)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

(2.12)

Asymptotic confidence sets for
L_{t}(x)
and
O_{t}(x)
readily follow from the above central limit theorem. ∎

Remark 2.6
.

The setting of
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes,
\alpha\in(1,2)
, is rather convenient as we often use self-similarity of
X
in our arguments. However, it might not be a necessary assumption. We conjecture that similar results can be shown for
locally
\alpha
-stable
Lévy processes although some bias effects may appear in the limit theory.
∎

From statistical point of view Theorem
2.3
provides lower bounds for estimation of the path functionals
L_{t}(x)
and
O_{t}(x)
. In particular, it shows that statistics
G(x,\phi)^{n}
introduced in (
1.6
) do not produce
L^{2}
-optimal estimates. On the other hand,
G(x,\phi)^{n}
can be computed from data even when the exact law of
X
is unknown (use e.g.
\phi=\mathbbm{1}_{[-1,1]}
) in contrast to
L^{2}
-optimal statistics (recall that the functions
f
and
F
depend on the parameters of the stable distribution). We remark however that the limit theory for statistics
G(x,\phi)^{n}
is expected to be much more involved; see the proofs
[
14
]
for more details in the case of Brownian motion. Hence, we postpone the discussion to future research.

3
Proof of main results

This section is devoted to the proof of the central limit theorem stated in the previous section. Throughout the proofs we denote by
C>0
a generic constant, which may change from line to line. In the proof of our main results the following lemma will be repeatedly used. Its proof can be found in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let us introduce
\varphi_{1}(y):=\mathbb{E}[L^{p}_{[0,1]}(y)]
and
\varphi_{2}(y):=\mathbb{E}[O^{p}_{[0,1]}(y)]
. Then, for
any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
, the following identities hold true.

(a)

\mathbb{E}[L^{p}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}]=n^{p(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varphi_{1}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))}
,

(b)

\mathbb{E}[O^{p}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}]=n^{-p}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varphi_{2}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))}
.

Moreover, define the functions
\varphi_{3}(y):=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(y)|X_{1}]L_{[0,1]}(y)]
and
\varphi_{4}(y):=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[O_{[0,1]}(y)|X_{1}]O_{[0,1]}(y)]
and recall that
f(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[L_{1}(x)|X_{1}=y]
and
F(x,y)=\int_{x}^{\infty}f(r,y)dr
.

Then,

(c)

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\right]

=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varphi_{3}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))}
,

(d)

\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)O_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\right]

=n^{-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varphi_{4}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))}
.

Regarding the well-posedness of the elements mentioned above, it is worth noting that
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are finite, as shown in Lemma
2.1
and the comment below it. It is also easy to verify that
\varphi_{3}
and
\varphi_{4}
are finite. In particular, demonstrating the finiteness of
\varphi_{3}
requires the use of Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen’s inequalities, in combination with the boundedness of the moments of
L_{1}
as established in Lemma
2.1
.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Proof.

Our argument is based on a martingale approach. We first deal with the estimation of the local time. Recall that
f(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[L_{1}(x)|X_{1}=y]
. Because of Proposition
2.2

and the linearity of the local time (in time)
, we can write

W_{L,t}^{n}=n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\left(S_{L,t}^{n}(x)-L_{[0,t]}(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}Z_{in}^{L}-n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}N_{L,t}^{n}+n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{n}(x),

(3.13)

where

Z_{in}^{L}:=n^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)-n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)

and

N_{L,t}^{n}=L_{[\lfloor nt\rfloor/n,t]}(x).

The term
N_{L}^{n}
appears due to the edge effect and it does not contribute to the limit as stated in the following lemma, whose proof can be found in the next section
and immediately follows from (
1.4
).

Lemma 3.2
.

The process
N_{L}^{n}=\{N_{L,t}^{n}\ ;\ t\geq 0\}
satisfies the following convergence in probability uniformly over compact sets

\lim_{n}n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}N_{L}^{n}=0.

Due to Proposition
2.2
and Lemma
3.2
we can write (
3.13
) as

W_{L,t}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}Z_{in}^{L}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1).

We introduce here the notation
\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[\cdot]
for
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}]
, which will be useful in the sequel.

In the next step we will apply Theorem 3-2 of
[
13
]
. In our setting,

\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[Z_{in}^{L}]=0
because of
Equation (
4.26
) below.
Then, it suffices to show
the following conditions:

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}k_{L}^{2}L_{t}(x)\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],

(3.14)

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[Z_{in}^{L}\Delta_{i}^{n}M]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],

(3.15)

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Z_{in}^{L}|>\epsilon\}}\right]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0\qquad\forall\epsilon>0,

(3.16)

where the condition (
3.15
) should hold for all square integrable continuous martingales
M
.
We emphasise that we have summarised the two conditions (3.12) and (3.14) from

[
13
, Theorem 3-2]
into our constraint (
3.15
)
. Indeed, conditions (3.12) and (3.14) in
[
13
, Theorem 3-2]
are formulated with respect to some continuous martingale
M
which has to be chosen by the user according to the problem at hand. The convergence in (
3.15
) holding for all bounded continuous martingales implies them both. It entails, in particular, that the continuous process
G
of
[
13
, Theorem 3-2]
is in our case identically zero.

We start by showing condition (
3.14
). Due to the identity
f(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(x)|X_{1}=y]
we can
check
that

\displaystyle{}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}]=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\psi\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})\right),

(3.17)

where
\psi
is given by

\psi(q):=\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(q)|X_{1}]-L_{[0,1]}(q))^{2}\big].

Indeed, from the definition of
Z_{in}^{L}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\big[n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[f^{2}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X)]+n^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[L^{2}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)]

\displaystyle-2\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[f(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X)L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)]\big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

We start studying
I_{1}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[f^{2}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X)]

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}g_{1}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

with
g_{1}(q):=\mathbb{E}[f^{2}(q,X_{1})]
. From Lemma
3.1
(a) the following is straightforward:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=n^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[L^{2}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)]

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}g_{2}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

where
g_{2}(q):=\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(q)^{2}]
.

We are left to study
I_{3}
. From Lemma
3.1
(c) we directly obtain

I_{3}=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}g_{3}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

with
g_{3}(q):=-2\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(q)\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(q)|X_{1}]]
.
Putting everything together we get (
3.17
) with
\psi(q)=g_{1}(q)+g_{2}(q)+g_{3}(q)
.

To apply Theorem
1.1
we need to check that
\psi,\psi^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By Minkowski and Jensen inequalities,
\psi(q)\leq 4\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{2}(q)]
.
We therefore aim at showing that the function
\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{2}(\cdot)]
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\tau_{q}
denote the first passage time of
X
over the level
q
. Conditioning over
\tau_{q}
, using the additivity of the local time, we deduce the inequality

\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{2}(q)]\leq\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{2}(0)]\mathbb{P}(\tau_{q}<1).

Moreover, by the Fourier representation of the local time (as stated in Lemma
2.1
),
\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{2}(0)]
is bounded. On the other hand,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\tau_{q}<1]dq=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\sup_{s\leq 1}X_{s}>q]dq=\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq 1}X_{s}].

(3.18)

We recall that
\mathbb{E}[(\sup_{s\leq 1}X_{s})^{p}]
is bounded for any
p\in(0,\alpha)

(see Corollary II.1.6 and Theorem II.1.7 in
[
25
]
)
. As
\alpha\in(1,2)
, this implies the boundedness of
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq 1}X_{s}]
. Hence, by symmetry,
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
k_{L}^{2}<\infty
. Applying the same argument it is easy to show that also
\psi^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, thanks to (
3.18
) and the boundedness of
\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{4}(q)]
.
By Theorem
1.1
we conclude that

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}k_{L}^{2}L_{t}(x),

where
k_{L}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)dy<\infty
.

In the next step we show condition (
3.15
). Let
M
be any continuous square integrable martingale and denote by
\mu
(resp.
\widetilde{\mu}
) the random measure (resp. compensated random measure) associated with
the pure jump Lévy process
X
. The martingale representation theorem for jump measures investigated in Lemma 3 (ii) and Theorem 6 of
[
8
]
implies that
Z_{in}^{L}
has an integral representation

Z_{in}^{L}=\int_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{i}^{n}(x,t)\widetilde{\mu}(dx,dt)

for some predictable square integrable process
\eta_{i}^{n}
.
Using that the covariation between any continuous martingale and any pure jump martingale is zero, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[Z_{in}^{L}\Delta_{i}^{n}M]=0.

Thus, we obtain (
3.15
).

Finally, we show condition (
3.16
).
Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality ensures that

\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{2}1_{\{|Z_{in}^{L}|>\epsilon\}}]\leq\epsilon^{-2}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{4}].

Then, by Markov inequality, it suffices
to prove that

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{4}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

(3.19)

We have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[(Z_{in}^{L})^{4}]\leq C\left(n^{2(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[f^{4}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X)]+n^{2(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[L^{4}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)]\right).

From Lemma
3.1
(a) we conclude that

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{L}|^{4}]\leq Cn^{2(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}h\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x)\right)

where
h(y)=\mathbb{E}[f^{4}(y,X_{1})+L_{[0,1]}(y)^{4}]
. It is easy to check that
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, similarly as in the proof of
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, Minkowski and Jensen inequalities imply

h(y)\leq C\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{4}(y)]\leq C\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{4}(0)]\mathbb{P}[\tau_{y}<1].

Then the boundedness of moments of the local time in
0
together with (
3.18
) provides
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. As
h^{2}(y)\leq C\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}^{8}(y)]
, following the same route it is easy to see that
h^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
\alpha>1
, we deduce by Theorem
1.1
that

n^{2(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}h\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x)\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.3
for local times.

Now we proceed to the analysis of the occupation time.
As for the local time case, the proof is based on a martingale approach. The definition of
W_{O,t}^{n}
together with the approximation of
S_{O,t}^{n}
as in Proposition
2.2
provides

W_{O,t}^{n}=n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}(S_{O,t}^{n}-O_{[0,t]}(x))=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}Z_{in}^{O}-n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}{N}_{O,t}^{n}+\mathcal{E}_{O,t}^{n}(x),

(3.20)

where
Z_{in}^{O}
is the principal term, given by

Z_{in}^{O}:=n^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}F(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X)-n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}O_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x),

while

N_{O,t}^{n}:=O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x).

In a similar way as for
N_{L,t}^{n}
we can show that
N_{O,t}^{n}
is negligible. Indeed, the following lemma holds true. Its proof can be found in the next section
and is a direct consequence of (
1.4
)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The process
N_{O}^{n}=\{N_{O,t}^{n}\ ;\ t\geq 0\}
satisfies the following convergence in probability uniformly over compact sets

\lim_{n}n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}N_{O}^{n}=0.

Due to Proposition
2.2
and Lemma
3.3
we can write (
3.20
) as

W_{O,t}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{Z}_{in}^{O}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1).

We are dealing with martingale differences. Indeed,
\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[{Z}_{in}^{O}]=0
because of Lemma
3.1
(b) with
p=1
, the definition of
F
and the independence of the increments of the process
X
. Therefore, similarly as before, our proof is based on
[
13
, Theorem 3-2]
. In particular, we want to show the following convergence statements:

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{O}|^{2}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}{k}_{O}^{2}L_{[0,t]}(x)\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],

(3.21)

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[Z_{in}^{O}\Delta_{i}^{n}M]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],

(3.22)

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|Z_{in}^{O}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Z_{in}^{O}|>\epsilon\}}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0\qquad\forall\epsilon>0.

(3.23)

The condition gathered in (
3.22
) should hold for all square integrable continuous martingales.

We start by proving (
3.21
). Similarly as for
{Z}_{in}^{O}
, from the definition of
F
and Lemma
3.1
(b) and (d) it follows

{}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|{Z}_{in}^{O}|^{2}]=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\tilde{\psi}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

(3.24)

with
\tilde{\psi}
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(z)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[O_{[0,1]}(z)|X_{1}]-O_{[0,1]}(z))^{2}\big].

We want to show
that
\tilde{\psi}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
c_{z}
be a deterministic constant only depending on
z
and observe that by Jensen’s inequality,
it is enough to show the integrability of
\mathbb{E}[(O_{[0,1]}(z)-c_{z})^{2}]
, where
c_{z}
is arbitrary. Take
c_{z}=0
for
z\geq 0
. Proceeding as in the case of local time, it is easy to see that

\mathbb{E}[O^{2}_{[0,1]}(z)]\leq C\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}<1)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

For
z<0
we choose instead
c_{z}=1
. Remarking that
\mathbb{E}[(1-O_{[0,1]}(z))^{2}]\leq\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}<1)
we obtain the same result. After that it is straightforward to prove that
\tilde{\psi}^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, observing
that
it is enough to show the integrability of
\mathbb{E}[(O_{[0,1]}(z)-c_{z})^{4}]
. We can then apply Theorem
1.1
, which implies (
3.21
) with
k_{O}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{\psi}(z)dz
.

Condition (
3.22
) is, as for the local time, a consequence of the martingale representation for jump measures in Lemma 3 (ii) and Theorem 6 of
[
8
]
.

Finally, we show condition (
3.23
). By
Cauchy-Schwarz
and Markov inequalities it is enough to prove that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|{Z}_{in}^{O}|^{4}]\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.}

By the definition of
{Z}_{in}^{O}
and Lemma
3.1
, following the same proof as to obtain (
3.24
), it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[|{Z}_{in}^{O}|^{4}]=n^{2(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\tilde{h}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

where
\tilde{h}(z):=\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[O_{[0,1]}(z)|X_{1}]-O_{[0,1]}(z))^{4}\big]
. Regarding the integrability of
\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{h}^{2}
, proceeding as for
\tilde{\psi}
it is sufficient to show the result for
\mathbb{E}\big[(O_{[0,1]}(z)-c_{z})^{4}\big]
and
\mathbb{E}\big[(O_{[0,1]}(z)-c_{z})^{8}\big]
respectively, with
c_{z}
arbitrary. As before, the result follows by choosing
c_{z}=0
for
z\geq 0
and
c_{z}=1
for
z<0
, and using (
3.18
). We deduce by Theorem
1.1
that

n^{2(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\tilde{h}\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x)\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,

which concludes the proof of our main theorem.
∎

4
Proof of the auxiliary results

This section is devoted to the proof of technical lemmas and proposition we have previously stated and used in order to obtain our main result, gathered in Theorem
2.3
. Before we proceed we introduce the function
r(y):=e^{-\textbf{i}y}
, which will be useful in the sequel.

4.1
Proof of Lemma
2.1

Proof.

The Fourier representation is proven in
[
17
, Proposition 11]
, we here prove the boundedness of the moments of
L_{t}(x)
. From the Fourier representation stated in the first part of this lemma we have, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

{}\mathbb{E}[(L_{[0,1]}(x))^{p}]\\
\begin{aligned} &=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{[0,1]}r({\xi}_{1}(X_{u_{1}}-x)du_{1}d\xi_{1}\right)\times..\times\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{[0,1]}r({\xi}_{p}(X_{u_{p}}-x)du_{p}d\xi_{p}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r(-({\xi}_{1}+...+{\xi}_{p})x)\mathbb{E}[r({\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r({\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{{\xi}}.\end{aligned}

(4.25)

The Fubini theorem, applied in the last identity, will be justified once we show that
|\mathbb{E}[r({\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r({\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})]|
is integrable, which will be proved next.
We can now assume without losing of generality that
p
is even and
u_{1}\leq u_{2}\leq...\leq u_{p}
. Then, the expectation above can be seen as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})X_{u_{1}})r((\xi_{2}+...+\xi_{p})(X_{u_{2}}-X_{u_{1}}))\times...\times r(\xi_{p}(X_{u_{p}}-X_{u_{p-1}}))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})X_{u_{1}})]\times...\times\mathbb{E}[r(\xi_{p}(X_{u_{p}}-X_{u_{p-1}}))]

having employed the self-similarity of
X
and the stationarity and independence of its increments. Relation (
1.1
) then implies that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})X_{u_{1}})r((\xi_{2}+...+\xi_{p})(X_{u_{2}}-X_{u_{1}}))\times...\times r(\xi_{p}(X_{u_{p}}-X_{u_{p-1}}))]|

\displaystyle=e^{-|\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p}|^{\alpha}u_{1}}\times...\times e^{-|\xi_{p}|^{\alpha}(u_{p}-u_{p-1})},

Now, up to apply the change of variables
\xi_{j}+...+\xi_{p}=:\eta_{j}
for any
j=1,...,p
, it is easy to check that the absolute value in the integrand of (
4.25
) is integrable, thus yielding the finiteness of the
p
-moment of
L_{t}
.

∎

4.2
Proof of Proposition
2.2

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\sigma(\{X_{\frac{i}{n}}\ ;\ i\in\mathbb{N}\})\subset\mathcal{F}
. We first deal with the analysis of the local time. Observe that the problem is reduced to proving the following two claims:

(i)

First of all show that

\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|X_{\frac{i-1}{n}},\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right]=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right),

(4.26)

for
f(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(x)|X_{1}=y]
.

(ii)

After that we prove that
\mathcal{E}_{L}^{(n)}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0\leq t\leq T}n^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}|\mathcal{E}_{L,t}^{(n)}(x)|\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,

(4.27)

for all
T>0.

If (i) and (ii) are satisfied, it is easy to check that the result on the local time stated in Proposition
2.2
holds true. To prove this reduction we observe that by the independent increments property of
X
,

\displaystyle S_{L,t}^{(n)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\lfloor nt\rfloor/n,t]}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]+\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}]}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\lfloor nt\rfloor/n,t]}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}\ |\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{A}_{n}}\right]+\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}]}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}\ |\ X_{\frac{k-1}{n}},\Delta_{k}^{n}X\right],

so that
relation
(
4.26
)
implies the desired result. We now proceed with the proof of (
4.26
) and (
4.27
). In order to show (
4.26
), we consider the approximated local time
L_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x),

defined by

L_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}-x)ds,

where
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x):=(2\pi\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\exp\{-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}x^{2}\}
. By
[
17
]
, we have the convergence towards zero of
\|L_{t}(x)-L_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
, which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}},\Delta_{i}^{n}X]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}[L^{\varepsilon}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}},\Delta_{i}^{n}X],

where
L_{[a,b]}^{\varepsilon}(x):=L_{b}^{\varepsilon}(x)-L_{a}^{\varepsilon}(x)
. By the self-similarity of
X
, for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}[L^{\varepsilon}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}=a,\Delta_{i}^{n}X=b]\\
\begin{aligned} &=\mathbb{E}[\int_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}-x)ds\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}=a,\Delta_{i}^{n}X=b]\\
&=\mathbb{E}[\int_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1/\alpha}X_{ns}-x)ds\ |\ n^{-1/\alpha}X_{i-1}=a,n^{-1/\alpha}(X_{i}-X_{i-1})=b]\\
&=\int_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1/\alpha}(X_{ns}-X_{i-1})+a-x)\ |\ n^{-1/\alpha}X_{i-1}=a,n^{-1/\alpha}(X_{i}-X_{i-1})=b]ds,\end{aligned}

where the last identity follows by Fubini’s theorem, which is applicable due to the fact that
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is bounded and the domain of integration is compact. The independent increments property of
X
implies that
X_{i-1}
is independent of the vector
(X_{s}-X_{i-1},X_{i}-X_{i-1})
. Moreover, by the stationarity of the increments of
X
, we have that

(X_{s}-X_{i-1},X_{i}-X_{i-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Law}}{{=}}(X_{s-i+1},X_{1}).

This identity combined with a suitable change of variables yields

\mathbb{E}[L^{\varepsilon}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}=a,n^{1/\alpha}\Delta_{i}^{n}X=b]\\
\begin{aligned} &=n^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1/\alpha}X_{u}+a-x)\ |\ X_{1}=n^{1/\alpha}b]ds\\
&=n^{1/\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\varepsilon n^{-2/\alpha}}(X_{u}+an^{1/\alpha}-xn^{1/\alpha})\ |\ X_{1}=n^{1/\alpha}b]ds\\
&=n^{1/\alpha-1}\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\varepsilon n^{-2/\alpha}}(X_{u}+an^{1/\alpha}-xn^{1/\alpha})ds\ |\ X_{1}=n^{1/\alpha}b].\end{aligned}

Using the fact that

\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\varepsilon n^{-2/\alpha}}(X_{u}+an^{1/\alpha}-xn^{1/\alpha})du\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}(\Omega)}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{1}(n^{1/\alpha}(x-a)),

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}[L^{\varepsilon}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}=a,n^{1/\alpha}\Delta_{i}^{n}X=b]

\displaystyle=n^{1/\alpha-1}\mathbb{E}[L_{1}(n^{1/\alpha}(x-a))\ |\ X_{1}=n^{1/\alpha}b].

This finishes the proof of (
4.26
).

We are left with the problem of showing (
4.27
).
Due to (
1.4
), for every
T,\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|L_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)|\leq Cn^{-1+\frac{1}{\alpha}+\epsilon},

for any
\epsilon\in(0,1-1/\alpha)
.
Relation (
4.27
) follows from here.

Next we deal with identity (
2.10
). By (
1.2
) and (
4.26
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[O_{t}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}[O_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}]+\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\int_{x}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(y)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}]dy+\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\int_{x}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(y-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)dy+\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}F\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right],

where the last identity follows from a suitable change of variables. Consequently, we deduce (
2.10
). In addition,
by definition of occupation
O_{[a,b]}(x)
we have that

|\mathbb{E}[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}]|\leq n^{-1}
which implies that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}n^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}\mathbb{E}\left[O_{[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t]}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{A}_{n}\right]\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}(1/\alpha-1)},

which converges towards zero.

The proof is now complete.

∎

4.3
Proof of Lemma
3.1

Proof.

Part (a)

Equation (
4.26
) provides the desired result for
p=1
as the increments are independent and so, in particular,
X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}
is independent from
\Delta_{i}^{n}X
. We thus study the case
p\geq 2
.
The representation of the local time as in Lemma
2.1
leads us to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[L^{p}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]^{p}}r(\xi_{1}(X_{s_{1}}-x))...r(\xi_{p}(X_{s_{p}}-x))d\bm{s}d\bm{\xi}\right],

where we recall that
\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}]
and
r(y)=e^{-\bm{i}y}
.

The change of variables
s_{j}=:\frac{i-1}{n}+\frac{u_{j}}{n}
for
j=1,...,p
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{p}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}[r(\xi_{1}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}+\frac{u_{1}}{n}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))\times...

\displaystyle\times r(\xi_{p}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}+\frac{u_{p}}{n}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))]d\bm{u}d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-p}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}[r(\xi_{1}X_{\frac{u_{1}}{n}})\times...\times r(\xi_{p}X_{\frac{u_{p}}{n}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{\xi},

having also used the independence and stationarity of the increments of
X
, as well as Fubini’s theorem, which is justified as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
. As the process
X
is self-similar, we obtain

\displaystyle n^{-p}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r((\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{p})(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}[r(\xi_{1}n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r(\xi_{p}n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}X_{u_{p}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{\xi}.

Applying the change of variables
\tilde{\xi}_{j}=n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{j}
for
j=1,...,p
, we get

\displaystyle n^{p(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r((\tilde{\xi}_{1}+...+\tilde{\xi}_{p})n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}[r(\tilde{\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r(\tilde{\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{\tilde{\xi}}

\displaystyle=n^{p(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}\varphi_{1}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})).

It is indeed

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r(-({\xi}_{1}+...+{\xi}_{p})y)\mathbb{E}[r({\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r({\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(L_{[0,1]}(y))^{p}]=\varphi_{1}(y),

because of (
4.25
).

It is important to remark that, in the proof above, it is possible to obtain the
desired

result as we consider the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}
, and we used several times the independence of the increments.

Part (b)

The definition of occupation time provides

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[O^{p}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(y_{1})dy_{1}\right)\times...\times\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(y_{p})dy_{p}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{\infty}...\int_{x}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(y_{1})\times...\times L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(y_{p})\right]d\bm{y}.

The change in the order of integration is justified by Tonelli’s theorem, which is valid due to the fact that
L_{[a,b]}(x)\geq 0
for all
a\leq b
and
x\geq 0
.

Acting as in the proof of Part (a) it is then easy to check that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[O^{p}_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{p(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)}}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{[x,\infty]^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r(\tilde{\xi}_{1}n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-y_{1}))\times...\times r(\tilde{\xi}_{p}n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-y_{p}))

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}\left[r(\tilde{\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r(\tilde{\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})\right]d\bm{u}d\bm{\tilde{\xi}}d\bm{y}.

To conclude the analysis we apply the change of variable
\tilde{y}_{j}:=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(y_{j}-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})
for
j=1,...,p
. We get

\displaystyle\frac{n^{-p}}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{[n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),\infty]^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}r(-\tilde{\xi}_{1}\tilde{y}_{1})\times...\times r(-\tilde{\xi}_{p}\tilde{y}_{p})

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}[r(\tilde{\xi}_{1}X_{u_{1}})\times...\times r(\tilde{\xi}_{p}X_{u_{p}})]d\bm{u}d\bm{\tilde{\xi}}d\bm{\tilde{y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-p}\varphi_{2}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}})),

with

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{[z,\infty]^{p}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\int_{[0,1]^{p}}\mathbb{E}[r({\xi}_{1}(X_{u_{1}}-{y}_{1}))\times...\times r({\xi}_{p}(X_{u_{p}}-{y}_{p}))]d\bm{u}d\bm{{\xi}}d\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{[z,\infty]^{p}}\mathbb{E}[L_{[0,1]}(y_{1})\times...\times L_{[0,1]}(y_{p})]dy_{1}...dy_{p}=\mathbb{E}[O_{[0,1]}^{p}(z)],

where the previous to last identity is justified by Tonelli’s theorem. The proof of this part is therefore completed.

Part (c)

According to
(
4.26
) it is

f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)=n^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)|X_{\frac{i-1}{n}},\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right].

Then, following the proof of Part (a), one can easily
obtain

{}f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[0,1]}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))|X_{1}\right].

(4.28)

From the representation of the local time as in Lemma
2.1

we conclude the
identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[f\left(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}),n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Delta_{i}^{n}X\right)L_{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[0,1]}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))|X_{1}\right]r(\xi(X_{s}-x))\right]dsd\xi.

The change of variable

provides the quantity above is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{1}r(\xi(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[0,1]}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))|X_{1}\right]r(\xi(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}+\frac{u}{n}}-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))\right]dud\xi

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{1}r({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{\xi}}n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}-x))\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[L_{[0,1]}(n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x-X_{\frac{i-1}{n}}))|X_{1}\right]r(\tilde{\xi}X_{u})\right]dud\tilde{\xi},

having used the independence of the increments, the self-similarity of the process
X
and the change of variable
\tilde{\xi}:=n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi
. The proof is concluded once we observe that this is

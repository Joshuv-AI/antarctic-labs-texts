Abstract
. For linearized Navier-Stokes equations, we first derive a Carleman estimate with a regular weight function. Then we apply it to establish conditional stability for the lateral Cauchy problem and finally we prove conditional stability estimates for inverse source problem of determining a spatially varying divergence-free factor of a source term.

Theorem 1 (Carleman estimate for the Navier-Stokes equations).

There exist constants

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\biggl\{\frac{1}{s}(|\partial_{t}\mbox{rot}\thinspace v|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\Delta(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)|^{2}+|\Delta v|^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s(|\nabla(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})+s^{3}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v|^{2}+|v|^{2})\biggr\}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+Cs^{3}\int_{\partial\Omega\times I}\sum_{j=0}^{1}(|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)|^{2}+|\nabla^{j}v|^{2})e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Cs^{3}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=0}^{1}|\nabla^{j}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v(x,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta))|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0}+\delta)}dx

We note that

Theorem 2 (continuation of solution).

Let
(v,p)\in H^{2,1}(Q)\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
satisfy (1.3)
with
\mbox{rot}\thinspace v\in H^{2,1}(Q)
and

[formula]

\sum_{j=0}^{1}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}+\|\nabla^{j}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))})+\|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq M

with arbitrarily fixed constant
M>0
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist constants
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, which depend on

M,\varepsilon,\Gamma,\Omega_{0}
such that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}\|\mbox{rot}\thinspace^{\ell}v\|_{H^{1,1}(\Omega_{0}\times(\varepsilon,\,T-\varepsilon))}+\|\Delta(\mbox{rot}\thinspace^{\ell}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0}\times(\varepsilon,\,T-\varepsilon))}+\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0}\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon))}\\
\leq C(D+D^{\theta}),

where

D:=\sum_{j=0}^{1}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}+\|\nabla^{j}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}).

This theorem asserts stability in determining a solution
(v,p)

in some subdomain by boundary data of
v
on
\Gamma\times(0,T)

under a priori bound (1.4).

A similar conditional stability for the continuation was proved in

[

In

which is compactly supported, and do not need to consider

Consequently, if we apply the cut-off, then we have to prove

Since we do not here require any cut-off at all,
the proofs of Theorems 1 - 3 are simplified.

Finally we state the main result for the inverse source problem.
In (1.1), we assume

[formula]

[formula]

|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t)|\leq C|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})|,\quad(x,t)\in Q,\,k=0,1.

[formula]

|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t)|\leq C(|\nabla F(x,t_{0})|+|F(x,t_{0})|),\quad(x,t)\in Q,\,k=0,1,2.

We notice that (1.6) implies (1.7), that is, condition (1.7) is more
generous than (1.7).

Theorem 3 (inverse source problem).

\overline{\Omega_{0}}\cap\partial\Omega\subset\mbox{Int}\,(\Gamma)

\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\{F\in L^{2}(Q);\,\mbox{rot}\thinspace F,\mbox{div}\thinspace F\in H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)),\,\mbox{(1.6) is satisfied}\}.

We set

[formula]

D_{1}:=\sum_{j,k=0}^{1}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times I)}+\|\nabla^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times I)})+\|v(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.

We assume that a solution
(v,p)\in H^{2,1}(Q)\times H^{1,0}(Q)
to (1.1)
satisfies
\partial_{t}\mbox{rot}\thinspace v\in H^{2,1}(Q)

\partial_{t}p\in H^{1,0}(Q)

\sum_{j,k=0}^{1}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times I)}+\|\nabla^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times I)})

+\sum_{\kappa=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace(\cdot,\,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{1}

with arbitrarily given constant

Then there exist constants
C>0

\|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(D_{1}^{\theta}+D_{1})

for each

.

(ii) We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}:=\{F\in H^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega));\,\|F(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq m_{1},\quad F(\cdot,t_{0})=|\nabla F(\cdot,t_{0})|=0\quad\mbox{on $\Gamma$},

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{(1.5) and (1.7) are satisfied}\}

with arbitrarily chosen constant
m_{1}>0

We further set

D_{2}:=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times I)}+\|\nabla^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times I)})+\|v(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{4}(\Omega)}.

We assume that a solution
(v,p)\in H^{2,1}(Q)\times H^{1,0}(Q)
to
(1.1) satisfies
\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace v\in H^{2,1}(Q)
,
\partial_{t}^{k}p\in H^{1,0}(Q)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}(\|\nabla_{x,t}^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times I)}+\|\nabla^{j}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times I)})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace v(\cdot,\,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{2}

with arbitrarily given constant

Then there exist constants

and
\theta\in(0,1)
, dependent

\Omega_{0},\Gamma,m_{2},M_{2}
, such that

\|F(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(D_{2}^{\theta}+D_{2})

for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

First, as the following example shows,

Obstruction to the uniqueness in determining
F
:

We consider a simple case

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v+\nabla p=f(x),\\
&\mbox{div}\thinspace v=0,\quad(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times I,\\
&v(x,t_{0})=0\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega$},\quad\mbox{supp $v\subset\Omega\times(0,T)$}.\end{array}\right.

Here
f
is an
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued smooth function.
It is trivial that
(v,p)=(0,0)
satisfies (1.10) with
f=0
.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

(v,p):=(0,\,\psi)

satisfies (1.10) with

Next we examine the conditions (1.5) - (1.7) by considering examples.

Example 1.

We see that (1.5) is replaced in terms of a vector potential:

F(x,t):=\mbox{rot}\thinspace q(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q,

where
q=q(x,t)
is smooth. Then (1.5) is automatically satisfied.
Moreover, we see that (1.7) is equivalent to

|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\mbox{rot}\thinspace(\partial_{t}^{k}q(x,t))|\leq C(|\nabla\mbox{rot}\thinspace q(x,t_{0})|+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace q(x,t)|),\quad(x,t)\in Q,\,k=0,1,2.

Example 2.

Let

F(x,t)=r(t)f(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,\quad r(t_{0})\neq 0,\quad\mbox{div}\thinspace f=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},

where real-valued
r\in C^{2}[0,T]
and
f=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})^{T}
are smooth.
Then (1.5) and (1.6) are satisfied. Indeed we can directly verify

\mbox{div}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=r(t_{0})\mbox{div}\thinspace f(x)=0
for
x\in\Omega
, which is (1.5).
Moreover, we have

|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t)|=\left|\frac{d^{k}r}{dt^{k}}(t)\mbox{rot}\thinspace f(x)\right|\leq C|\mbox{rot}\thinspace f(x)|,\quad k=0,1

and

|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})|=|r(t_{0})||\mbox{rot}\thinspace f(x)|,\quad(x,t)\in Q,

which is (1.6).

Example 3.

For more general
F
unlike Example 2, it is not always easy to verify
(1.6), but in the following case the condition (1.7) can be readily
verified.
Let

F(x,t)=R(x,t)f(x),

where
R(x,t)=(r_{jk}(x,t))_{1\leq j,k\leq 3}
and each component
r_{jk}
is
smooth in
(x,t)
, and
f(x)=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),f_{3}(x))^{T}
.
Then (1.7) is satisfied if

|\mbox{det}\,R(x,t_{0})|>0\quad\mbox{for $x\in\overline{\Omega}$}.

Indeed, we have

|\partial_{t}^{k}\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t)|\leq C(|\nabla f(x)|+|f(x)|),\quad(x,t)\in\overline{Q},\quad k=0,1,2.

Therefore it is sufficient to verify

[formula]

|\nabla f(x)|+|f(x)|\leq C(|\nabla F(x,t_{0})|+|F(x,t_{0})|),\quad x\in\Omega.

Verification of (1.11).

By
|\mbox{det}\,R(x,t_{0})|\neq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have

|f(x)|\leq C|F(x,t_{0})|,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Next, setting
F=(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})^{T}
, we have

\partial_{\ell}F_{k}(x,t_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}r_{kj}(x,t_{0})\partial_{\ell}f_{j}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{\ell}r_{kj})(x,t_{0})f_{j}(x),

and so

\sum_{j=1}^{3}r_{kj}(x,t_{0})\partial_{\ell}f_{j}(x)=\partial_{\ell}F_{k}(x,t_{0})-\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{\ell}r_{kj}(x,t_{0}))f_{j}(x),

that is,

R(x,t_{0})\partial_{\ell}f(x)=\partial_{\ell}F(x,t_{0})-(\partial_{\ell}R(x,t_{0}))f(x).

Therefore, by
|\mbox{det}\,R(x,t_{0})|\neq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
we see

|\partial_{\ell}f(x)|\leq C(|\partial_{\ell}F(x,t_{0})|+|F(x,t_{0})|),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq 3.

Thus (1.11) is verified.

Here for the form
F(x,t)=R(x,t)f(x)
with
3\times 3
matrix

R(x,t)
, we do not know a convenient sufficient condition for (1.6),
but condition (1.7)
can be more directly verified.
Therefore, Theorem 3 (ii) is more feasible in applications.

.

We set

Then

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}z(x,t)-\Delta z+(A(x,t)\cdot\nabla)z=\mbox{rot}\thinspace F-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\nabla A_{j}\times\partial_{j}v,\\
&\Delta v=-\mbox{rot}\thinspace z,\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T.\end{array}\right.

The system (2.1) provides a decomposition of the Navier-Stokes equations into
a parabolic equation in

and an elliptic equation in

, where

is eliminated.

First we show a Carleman estimate for a simple parabolic equation with constant
coefficients:

Henceforth

denotes generic constants which are independent of

s>0

Lemma 1.

There exist constants
C>0
and

such that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left\{\frac{1}{s}(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\Delta u|^{2})+s|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{3}|u|^{2}\right\}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|G|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+Cs^{3}\int_{\partial\Omega\times I}(|\nabla_{x,t}u|^{2}+|u|^{2})e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Cs^{3}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u(x,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta)|^{2}+|u(x,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta)|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0}+\delta)}dx

for all
s>s_{0}
and all

Lemma 1 is a standard Carleman estimate for a single parabolic equation,
and a direct proof is found
for example in Bellassoued and Yamamoto
[
3
]
(Lemma 7.1),
Yamamoto
[
22
]
(Theorems 3.1 and 3.2).

Next we show two Carleman estimates for the Laplace operator.

Lemma 2.

Let

Then

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(s|\nabla r(x,t)|^{2}+s^{3}|r(x,t)|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|g(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dxdt+Cs^{3}\int_{\partial\Omega\times I}(|\nabla r|^{2}+|r|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dSdt

for all
s>s_{0}
.

Lemma 3.

Let

satisfy

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(s|\nabla w(x)|^{2}+s^{3}|w(x)|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0})}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|h(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0})}dx+Cs^{3}\int_{\partial\Omega}(|\nabla w(x)|^{2}+|w(x)|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0})}dS

for all

The proof of Lemma 3 is standard and we can prove directly by integration by

[
3
]
), and Lemma 3 is used also for the

The proof of Lemma 2 is done by integrating the standard elliptic Carleman
estimate in Lemma 3 for

over the time interval

For completeness, the derivation of

Now we complete the proof of Theorem 1.
We apply Lemmata 1 and 2 to (2.1):

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s}(|\partial_{t}z|^{2}+|\Delta z|^{2})+s|\nabla z|^{2}+s^{3}|z|^{2}\right\}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}\left|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F-\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}\times(\partial_{j}v)\right|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+CJ_{1}

[formula]

\leq C\int_{Q}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+C\int_{Q}(|\nabla v|^{2}+|v|^{2})e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+CJ_{1}

and

[formula]

\int_{Q}(s|\nabla v|^{2}+s^{3}|v|^{2})e^{2s\varphi}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace z|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+CJ_{2}.

Here and henceforth we set

\displaystyle J_{1}:=s^{3}\int_{\partial\Omega\times I}(|\nabla_{x,t}(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v)|^{2}+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v|^{2})e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s^{3}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla(\mbox{rot}\thinspace v(x,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta))|^{2}+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v(x,t_{0}+(-1)^{\kappa}\delta)|^{2})e^{2s\varphi(x,t_{0}+\delta)}dx

Hence, substituting (2.3) into the second term on the right-hand side
of (2.1):

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left\{\frac{1}{s}(|\partial_{t}z|^{2}+|\Delta z|^{2})+s|\nabla z|^{2}+s^{3}|z|^{2}\right\}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

Absorbing the second term on the right-hand side into the left-hand side,
we complete the proof of Theorem 1.

5.
Concluding Remarks

5.1. Original Navier-Stokes equations

We mainly consider linearized Navier-Stokes equations.
The original Navier-Stokes equations reads as

[formula]

\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v(x,t)+(v\cdot\nabla)v(x,t)+\nabla p=F(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T.

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
satisfy (5.1) with
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively.
Then, setting
v:=v_{1}-v_{2}
, we have

\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v(x,t)+(v_{1}\cdot\nabla)v(x,t)+(v\cdot\nabla)v_{2}(x,t)+\nabla p=(F_{1}-F_{2})(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,

which corresponds to (1.1). Although the existence of
v_{1},v_{2}
to the
initial boundary value problems for the Navier-Stokes equations, is
in general, not completely solved with our assumed regularity, for our inverse
problems we assume the existence of
v_{1},v_{2}
with such regularity.
Moreover, we see by the proof that
T>0
can be arbitrarily small, and our
inverse problem requires the existence of the solution
(v,p)
local in time.
Moreover, we notice that for the inverse problems, we consider
the solutions only for positive time interval
t>t_{0}-\delta>0
,
so that there is a possibility that we rely on the smoothing property
in time of solution to the Navier-Stokes equations, in order to
gain the necessary regularity of solution for the inverse problem.

On the other hand, in our inverse source problems, we cannot choose

t_{0}=0
, in other words, our problem is not an inverse problem for
any initial boundary value problem. In particular, we remark
that we do not assume any initial conditions, but we need
v(\cdot,t_{0})
in

\Omega
with some
t_{0}>0
. In general, the inverse problem with our
formulation for parabolic equations as well as the Navier-Stokes
equations has been a long-standing open problem for the case of

t_{0}=0
.

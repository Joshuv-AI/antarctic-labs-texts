Abstract
. We consider the inverse mean curvature flow by parallel hypersurfaces in space forms. We show that such a flow exists if and only if the initial hypersurface is isoparametric. The flow is characterized by an algebraic equation satisfied by the distance function of the parallel hypersurfaces. The solutions to the flow are obtained explicitly when the distinct principal curvatures have the same multiplicity. This is an additional assumption only for isoparametric hypersurfaces of the hyperbolic space or of the sphere with two or four distinct principal curvatures. The boundaries of the maximal interval of definition, when finite, are determined in terms of the number g g of distinct principal curvatures, their multiplicities m m and the mean curvature H H of the initial hypersurface. We describe the collapsing submanifolds of the flow at the boundaries of the interval. In particular, we show in the Euclidean space the solutions are eternal, while in the hyperbolic space there are eternal and immortal solutions. Starting with a connected isoparametric submanifold of the sphere, we show that the flow is an ancient solution, that collapses into a minimal hypersurface whose square length of its second fundamental form and its scalar curvature are constants given in terms of g g and n n . The minimal hypersurface is totally geodesic when g = 1 g=1 , it is a Clifford minimal hypersurface of the sphere when g = 2 g=2 and it is a Cartan type minimal submanifold when g ∈ { 3 , 4 , 6 } g\in\{3,4,6\} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\overline{M}^{n+1}
be a Riemannian manifold with a given metric. Consider
F:M^{n}\longrightarrow\overline{M}^{n+1}
an oriented connected hypersurface with a unit normal vector field
N

{F}^{t}:M\longrightarrow\overline{M}
,
t\in I
,
0\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
, is a solution to the
Inverse Mean Curvature Flow
(IMCF), with initial data
F

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{F}^{t}}{\partial t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{{H}^{t}(x)}{N}^{t}(x)\qquad\mbox{ for }(x,t)\in M\times I,

\displaystyle{F}^{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(x)

where
{H}^{t}(.)
is the mean curvature and
{N}^{t}(.)

is the inward unit normal vector field of
{F}^{t}(.)
, with
{H}^{t}(x)>0
,
\forall(x,t)\in M\times I
. We say that
{F}^{t}
is an
ancient solution
if
I=(-\infty,t^{*})
, with
t^{*}>0
. If
I=(t^{*},+\infty)
, with
t^{*}<0
, then
{F}^{t}
is called an
immortal solution
and if
I=\mathbb{R}
then
{F}^{t}
is called an
eternal solution
.

The inverse mean curvature flow has been studied extensively in recent decades. We mention two important papers related to the IMCF. In
2001
, using the IMCF, Huisken and Ilmanen
[
9
]
proved the Riemannian Penrose Inequality. The Penrose inequality was conjectured in
1973
and it relates, under some conditions, the mass of certain spacetimes to the area of their black holes. The semi-Riemannian case remains an open problem. On the other hand, in
2004
, using IMCF, Bray and Neves
[
3
]
proved the Poincaré conjecture for
3
-manifolds with Yamabe invariant greater than the Yamabe invariant of real projective
3
-space. There are several papers that relate the IMCF with physical problems involving general relativity and black holes, see for example:
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

The mean curvature flow by parallel hypersurfaces in space forms was studied by Reis and Tenenblat
[
12
]
. The theory was extended by de Lima
[
5
]
to Weingarten flows by parallel hypersurfaces for Weingarten functions of the principal curvatures satisfying certain conditions. The author obtains existence results for such flows with initial isoparametric hypersurfaces of a simply connected space forms or of a rank-one symmetric space of noncompact type. Moreover, for complete connected hypersurfaces an embedding preserving property and an avoidance principle are proved. In
[
8
]
, the authors considered the evolution of the mean curvature flows in Riemannian manifolds, by isoparametric hypersurfaces.

In this paper, motivated by the importance of the inverse mean curvature flow, we study in detail the IMCF by parallel hypersurfaces in space forms.
We start showing that such a flow exists if and only if the initial hypersurface is isoparametric and we characterize such flows in terms of an algebraic equation satisfied by the distance function of the parallel hypersurfaces (Theorem
3.1
). We obtain explicitly the solutions to the IMCF, when the distinct principal curvatures have the same multiplicity
m
.
We point out that this is an additional assumption only for
isoparametric hypersurfaces of the hyperbolic space or the sphere
with
2
or
4
distinct principal curvatures.
The finite boundaries of the maximal interval of definition of the flow are given explicitly in terms of the number
g
of
distinct principal curvatures, their multiplicities
m
and the mean curvature of the initial hypersurface. We also show that when the flow tends to the boundary of the interval of definition, it converges to certain submanifolds of the space form.
In particular, starting with an isoparametric submanifold of the sphere, we show that the flow is an ancient solution, i.e., it is defined in the interval
(-\infty,t^{*})
, where

t^{*}=m/2\ln(H^{2}/n^{2}+1)
. The flow expands from
an
m(g-1)
-dimensional submanifold and it collapses into a minimal hypersurface (when
t\rightarrow t^{*}
), which is totally geodesic hypersurface when of the sphere if
g=1
,
it is a Clifford minimal hypersurface of the sphere, when
g=2
and it is a Cartan type minimal hypersurface
[
4
]
, when
g\in\{3,4,6\}
.
The square length of its second fundamental form
|A|^{2}
and its scalar curvatures
R
are constants
given by
|A|^{2}=n(g-1)
and
R=n(n-g)
respectively. (see Theorem
3.7
).

We should mention that the additional assumption mentioned above, in the case of 2 or 4 distinct principal curvatures, is due to the difficulty of solving the algebraic equation of the distance function whenever the distinct principal curvatures do not have the same multiplicity. It would be interesting to investigate the IMCF in this case.

Theorem 3.7

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
when
t\rightarrow-\infty
and it follows from Lemma
4.1
, that it converges to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
when
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

∎

Before proving Theorem
3.7
, we state some well known relations between the principal curvatures of an isoparametric hypersurface of the sphere that will be used in the proof.

Remark 4.2
.

Consider an
n
-dimensional isoparametric hypersurface of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
,
with
g
distinct principal curvatures,
k_{i}
,
i=1,...,g
. Then

1)
If
g=1
, we are considering
k_{1}>0
.

2)
If
g=2
, then

k_{2}=-{1}/{k_{1}}
, where w.l.o.g. we may consider
k_{1}>0
.

3)
If
g=3
, w.l.o.g we may consider
k_{1}>\sqrt{3}
, then

k_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}-3}{3k_{1}+\sqrt{3}},\qquad k_{3}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}+3}{\sqrt{3}-3k_{1}},\qquad k_{3}<0<k_{2}<k_{1}.

Thus,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}k_{i}=\frac{3k_{1}(k_{1}^{2}-3)}{3k_{1}^{2}-1},\qquad\sum_{i<j,i,j=1}^{3}k_{i}k_{j}=-3,\qquad k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}=-\frac{k_{1}(k_{1}^{2}-3)}{3k_{1}^{2}-1}.

4)
If
g=4
, w.l.o.g. we may consider
k_{1}>1
, hence

k_{2}=\frac{k_{1}-1}{k_{1}+1},\qquad k_{3}=-\frac{1}{k_{1}}\qquad k_{4}=\frac{k_{1}+1}{1-k_{1}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad k_{4}<k_{3}<0<k_{2}<k_{1}.

Thus,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}k_{i}=\frac{k_{1}^{4}-6k_{1}^{2}+1}{k_{1}(k_{1}^{2}-1)},\quad k_{1}k_{3}=k_{2}k_{4}=-1,\quad(k_{1}+k_{3})(k_{2}+k_{4})=-4,\quad k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}=1.

5)
If
g=6
, w.l.o.g. we may consider
k_{1}>\sqrt{3}
, then

k_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}-1}{k_{1}+\sqrt{3}},\qquad k_{3}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}-3}{3k_{1}+\sqrt{3}},\qquad k_{4}=-\frac{1}{k_{1}},\qquad k_{5}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}+3}{\sqrt{3}-3k_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad k_{6}=\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{1}+1}{\sqrt{3}-k_{1}},

where
k_{6}<k_{5}<k_{4}<0<k_{3}<k_{2}<k_{1}
. Thus,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{6}k_{j}=3\frac{k_{1}^{6}-15k_{1}^{4}+15k_{1}^{2}-1}{3k_{1}^{5}-10k_{1}^{3}+3k_{1}},\qquad k_{1}k_{4}=k_{3}k_{6}=k_{2}k_{5}=k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{5}k_{6}=-1.

We will now prove Theorem
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Let
F
be an isoparametric hypersurface of the sphere, with
g
distinct principal curvatures,
k_{j}
,
j=1...g
and
g\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
. Without loss of generality we may chose
k_{1}>0
, as in Remark
4.2
.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
, that the family of hypersurfaces
parallel to
F
is a solution to the IMCF if

{F}^{t}(x)=\cos(\mu(t))F(x)+\sin(\mu(t))N(x)
,

\forall(x,t)\in M\times I
,
0\in I
, where since all the curvatures have the same multiplicty
m
,
\mu(t)
satifies

(4.21)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{g}\left(\cos(\mu(t))-k_{j}\sin(\mu(t))\right)=e^{t/m}.

We introduce the notation

a=\frac{1}{g}\sum_{j=1}^{g}k_{j}=\frac{H}{n}.

Observe that if
g=1
then
a=k_{1}
and
m=n
. Moreover, note that
a>0
.

A straightforward computation shows that from
the relations in Remark
4.2
, and in particular (
4.18
)) - (
4.20
), we have
\forall t\in I
and
g\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\cos(g\mu(t))-a\sin(g\mu(t))=e^{t/m}.

The square of
(\ref{eq.p.171})
implies that
\cos(g\mu(t))
satisfies
the algebraic equation

(4.23)

\displaystyle(a^{2}+1)\cos^{2}(g\mu(t))-2e^{t/m}\cos(g\mu(t))+e^{2t/m}-a^{2}=0,

whose solution, since
\mu(0)=0
, is given by

(4.24)

\displaystyle\cos(g\mu(t))=\frac{e^{t/m}+a\sqrt{q(t)}}{a^{2}+1},\quad\mbox{ where }\quad q(t)=a^{2}+1-e^{2t/m}.

Note that
q(t)>0
, whenever
t<t^{*}
, and
q(t^{*})=0
, for

(4.25)

t^{*}=\frac{m}{2}\ln(a^{2}+1).

Note that
\cos(g\mu(t))>0
,
\forall t\leq t^{*}
. Moreover,
it follows from
(\ref{eq.p.171})
and
(\ref{eq.p.173})
that
\forall t\leq t^{*}
,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sin(g\mu(t))=\frac{-ae^{t/m}+\sqrt{q(t)}}{a^{2}+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\tan(g\mu(t))=\frac{-ae^{t/m}+\sqrt{q(t)}}{e^{t/m}+a\sqrt{q(t)}}.

We claim that

(4.27)

\cos(\mu(t))>0,\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\leq t^{*}.

In fact, otherwise, since
\cos(\mu(0))=1
if
\cos(\mu(t))
changes sign then there exists

t_{0}\leq t^{*}
such that
\cos(\mu(t_{0}))=0
.
Observe that it follows from the trigonometric identities

(4.28)

\cos(g\mu(t))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\cos(\mu(t)),&\mbox{ if }&g=1,\\
2\cos^{2}(\mu(t))-1,&\mbox{ if }&g=2,\\
4\cos^{3}(\mu(t))-3\cos(\mu(t)),&\mbox{ if }&g=3,\\
8\cos^{4}(\mu(t))-8\cos^{2}(\mu(t))+1,&\mbox{ if }&g=4,\\
32\cos^{6}(\mu(t))-48\cos^{4}(\mu(t))+18\cos^{2}(\mu(t))-1,&\mbox{ if }&g=6,\end{array}\right.

that, for
g=1,2,3,6
, such
t_{0}
does no exist since
\cos(g\mu(t_{0}))>0
,
\forall t\leq t^{*}
. If
g=4
,
\cos(\mu(t_{0}))=0
implies that
\cos(4\mu(t_{0}))=1
and hence
\sin(4\mu(t_{0}))=0
. However, in this case, (
4.24
) and (
4.26
) imply
(a^{2}+1)(e^{t_{0}/m}-1)=0
, i.e.,
t_{0}=0
, which contradicts
\cos(\mu(t_{0}))=0
.

Moreover, it follows from (
4.27
) and (
4.21
) that

(4.29)

\displaystyle(\cos(\mu(t)))^{g}\prod_{j=1}^{g}[1-k_{j}\tan(\mu(t))]=e^{t/m},

and therefore,

(4.30)

\displaystyle\tan(\mu(t))\not=\frac{1}{k_{j}},\quad j=1,...g,\quad\mbox{ and }\forall t\leq t^{*}.

We will now show that the hypersurface
F^{t}(M)
is well defined
for all
t\leq t^{*}
. Moreover, it converges to a minimal hypersurface of the sphere when
t\rightarrow t^{*}
and it converges to a submanifold of
dimension
n-m
when
t\rightarrow-\infty
.
Let
E_{j1},\dots,E_{jm}
,
j\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
, be an orthonormal frame of principal directions on
F(M)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
. Using Lemma
2.2
one obtains that,
\forall t\leq t^{*}
, the first fundamental form

(4.31)

\displaystyle I^{t}(E_{jr},E_{js})=\cos^{2}(\mu(t))\left[1-k_{j}\tan(\mu(t))\right]^{2}\delta_{rs},

\forall r,s
with
1\leq r,s\leq m
and
j=1,...,g
and hence
I^{t}(E_{jr},E_{jr})\not=0
, since (
4.27
) and (
4.30
) hold.

Moreover, from (
4.24
), we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\longrightarrow-\infty}\cos(g\mu(t))=\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}}

and since
a>0
, from (
4.29
) and (
4.28
) we conclude that

(4.32)

\lim\limits_{t\longrightarrow-\infty}\cos(\mu(t))\not=0.

It follows from
H>0
and (
4.5
), that
\tan(\mu(t))
is a decreasing function and hence
\tan(\mu(t))>0
, for all
t<0
.
Therefore, from (
4.29
),
(
4.32
),
k_{1}>0
and the order of the principal curvatures
k_{j}<k_{1}
, for all
j\neq 1
(see Remark
4.2
), we conclude that

\lim_{t\longrightarrow-\infty}\tan(\mu(t))=\frac{1}{k_{1}}.

Consequently,
(\ref{eq.p.180})
implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\longrightarrow-\infty}I^{t}(E_{1r},E_{1s})=0,

\forall r,s
with
1\leq r,s\leq m
. We conclude that
F^{t}(M)
converges to an
m(g-1)
-dimensional submanifold of the sphere, when

t\rightarrow-\infty
.

It follows from
Lemma
2.2
, that the principal curvatures of
F^{t}
are given by

(4.33)

k_{i}^{t}=\frac{\tan(\mu(t))+k_{i}}{1-k_{i}\tan(\mu(t))}.

Therefore, a straightforward computation, using Remark
4.2
and the relations given in
(
4.18
)-(
4.19
), implies that
the mean curvature
{H}^{t}
of
{F}^{t}
is given by

\displaystyle{H}^{t}=m\sum_{i=1}^{g}k_{i}^{t}=\frac{n}{e^{t/m}}cos(g\mu(t))(\tan(g\mu(t))+a),

Hence
{H}^{t}=0
if and only if
\tan(g\mu(t))=-a
. Thus, from
(\ref{eq.a.40})
, we have that
{H}^{t}=0
if and only if

q(t)=0
, i.e.,
t=t^{*}
. Therefore, the flow collapses into a minimal surface at
t^{*}
.

If
g=1
then
k_{1}=a
and it follows from (
4.33
) that all the principal curvatures at
t^{*}
are equal to
k_{1}^{t^{*}}=0
.
Thus, we conclude that
{F}^{t}(M)
expands from a point and converges to a totally geodesic hypersurface of
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
when
t\rightarrow t^{*}
.

If
g\in\{2,3,4,6\}
,
since
F^{t^{*}}(M)
is a minimal submanifold we have

\sum_{i=1}^{g}(k_{i}^{t^{*}})^{2}=-2\sum_{i,j=1,i<j}^{g}k_{i}^{t^{*}}k_{j}^{t^{*}}.

Using (
4.33
) and the expressions of
k_{i}
in terms of
k_{1}
given in Remark
4.2
, a straightforward computation shows that

\sum_{i,j=1,i<j}^{g}k_{i}^{t^{*}}k_{j}^{t^{*}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}-1,&\mbox{ if }&g=2,\\
-3,&\mbox{ if }&g=3,\\
-6,&\mbox{ if }&g=4,\\
-15,&\mbox{ if }&g=6,\end{array}\right.

i.e.,
\sum_{i,j=1,i<j}^{g}k_{i}^{t^{*}}k_{j}^{t^{*}}=-g(g-1)/2
.
Therefore, the square length of the second fundamental form of the minimal submanifold is given by

|A|^{2}=m\sum_{i=1}^{g}(k_{i}^{t^{*}})^{2}=mg(g-1)=n(g-1),

and its scalar curvature is given by
R=n(n-1)-|A|^{2}
, i.e.

R=m(m-1)g^{2}=n(n-g).

We conclude that
{F}^{t}(M)
is defined on
I=(-\infty,t^{*})
, i.e.,
it is an ancient solution that expands from an
m(g-1)
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
and converges to a minimal hypersurface of
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
when
t\longrightarrow t^{*}
.

{F}^{t^{*}}(M)
is a totally geodesic submanifold of the sphere when
g=1
, it is a Clifford minimal hypersurface of the sphere, when
g=2
, and it is a Cartan type minimal submanifold when
g=3,4,6
.

∎

Abstract
Kim and Vu made the following conjecture [ Advances in Mathematics , 2004]: if d ≫ log ⁡ n d\gg\log n , then the random d d -regular graph 𝒢 ⁡ ( n , d ) {\mathcal{G}}(n,d) can be “sandwiched” between 𝒢 ⁡ ( n , p ∗ ) {\mathcal{G}}(n,p_{*}) and 𝒢 ⁡ ( n , p ∗ ) {\mathcal{G}}(n,p^{*}) where p ∗ p_{*} and p ∗ p^{*} are both asymptotically equal to d / n d/n . This famous conjecture was previously proved for all d ≫ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) 3 / 4 d\gg(n\log n)^{3/4} . In this paper, we confirm the conjecture when d ≫ log 4 ⁡ n d\gg\log^{4}n . We also extend this result to near-regular degree sequences.

Introduction

The binomial random graphs, introduced by Erdős and Rényi
[
3
,
4
]
around 1960, are the best known and most thoroughly studied among random graph models.
In the binomial random graph
{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)
, the vertex set is
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and between each pair of vertices an edge is present independently with probability
p
.
More recently, the random regular graph
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
, and more generally
{\mathcal{G}}(n,{\boldsymbol{d}})
, the uniformly random graph with degree sequence
{\boldsymbol{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
, have received much attention.
In studies of random regular graphs, it was noticed that
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
shares many properties with
{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)
when
d\gg\log n
, where
p\approx d/n
.
For instance, asymptotically, they have the same chromatic number, the same diameter, the same independence number, and the same order of the spectral gap.
This motivated the following sandwich conjecture, proposed by Kim and Vu
[
11
]
in 2004.

Conjecture 1.1

(Sandwich conjecture)
.

For every
d\gg\log n
, there are
p_{*}=(1-o(1))d/n
,
p^{*}=(1+o(1))d/n
and a coupling
(G_{*},G,G^{*})
such that
G_{*}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,p_{*})
,
G^{*}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,p^{*})
,
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(G_{*}\subseteq G\subseteq G^{*})=1-o(1)
.

The condition
d\gg\log n
in the conjecture is necessary. When
d=O(\log n)
, there is a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that, with probability at least
\varepsilon
, there exist two vertices in
{\mathcal{G}}(n,p=d/n)
which degrees differ by at least
\varepsilon d
.
Therefore, Conjecture
1.1
cannot hold for this range of
d
. For
\log n\ll d\ll n^{1/3}/\log^{2}n
,
Kim and Vu proved a weakened version of the sandwich conjecture where
G_{*}\subseteq G
holds, but
G\subseteq{G^{*}}
is replaced by a bound on
\varDelta(G\setminus{G^{*}})
(see the precise statement in
[
11
, Theorem 2]

Given Theorem

The sandwich theorem not only explains the universality phenomenon between binomial random graphs and random regular graphs, it also gives a powerful tool for analysing random regular graphs.
Although
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
has been extensively studied, much less has been proved in comparison to
{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)

Proofs for properties of
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
where

typically involve highly technical analysis, as it is not easy to estimate subgraph probabilities.
One would use the switching method to estimate such probabilities when
d=o(n)
, and for
d=\Theta(n)

In the range of
d
where the sandwich conjecture is confirmed, one can immediately translate monotone or convex properties, phase transitions, and many graph parameters from
{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)

{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)

We remark that the conditions cannot be weakened to

\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{d})=o(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{d})),

1.1
Proof ideas

The coupling procedures used in all previous work
[
11
,
2
,
8
,
12
]
are essentially the same (for simplicity we explain the coupling procedure for
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
only, and the treatment for
{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d})
is similar): a triple of random graphs
(F_{*},F,F^{*})
are sequentially constructed simultaneously so that every time an edge is added to
F
according to the correct marginal distribution (of
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
containing that edge conditional on the current construction), it is added to the bottom graph
F_{*}
with a slightly smaller probability, independent of the previous construction.
When
F
is close to fully constructed, the construction of
F
is completed without changing
F_{*}
and
F^{*}
. The coupling output is
(F_{*},F,F^{*})
.
By choosing several parameters carefully,
F_{*}
and
F^{*}
are binomial random graphs with asymptotically equal edge density, and
F_{*}\subseteq F
with probability
1-o(1)
, but
F
is not a subgraph of
F^{*}
.
This idea was introduced by
[
11
]
and then extended to the hypergraph setting in
[
2
]
.
For more details about this coupling; see Section
2.1
.

To overcome the problem that
F
is not a subgraph of
F^{*}
, the authors
[
8
]
used a similar coupling construction for embedding
{\mathcal{G}}(n,1-p_{*})
inside
{\mathcal{G}}(n,n-d-1)
in the dense setting. Complementing this coupling gives the top side of the sandwich. This idea was also used in the sandwich results of
[
12
]
to allow degrees where
\min\{d,n-d\}\gg(n\log n)^{3/4}
.

In this paper, we propose a novel 2-round coupling scheme. First, we construct
(F_{*},F,F^{*})
as before but stop when
F
is sufficiently close to completion. Then, in the second round of coupling, we find a random subgraph
F^{\prime}
of
K_{n}\setminus F
with the correct distribution, so that
F\cup F^{\prime}
has the distribution
{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
. We do so by deleting edges from the complement of
F
according to the right marginal distribution until reaching a
d
-regular graph
G
. Since at every step edges are added or deleted from the complement according to the correct marginal distribution, we have that
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
. The edge deletion process for
G
is coupled with another edge deletion process
G^{\prime}
such that

G^{\prime}
and
F^{*}
are two independent binomial random graphs and
G\subseteq G^{\prime}\cup F^{*}

with probability
1-o(1)
. This yields a coupling that embeds
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,d)
inside
G^{\prime}\cup F^{*}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)
for some
p
. We describe the second round of the coupling scheme in detail in Section
2.2
.

A spanning subgraph with degree sequence
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
of a graph
S
is called
\boldsymbol{t}
-factor of
S
. Let
S_{\boldsymbol{t}}
denote a uniformly random
\boldsymbol{t}
-factor of
S
.
The analysis of our new 2-round coupling procedure requires that all edges are approximately equally likely to appear in
S_{\boldsymbol{t}}
for a near-regular sequence
\boldsymbol{t}
and a pseudorandom graph
S
.
Such results were previously known only for dense
S
but not for sparse
S
; see
[
8
, Theorem 10]
and references therein.
To allow sparse
S
, we use a switching method that involves counting walks in
S
up to logarithmic length, which to our knowledge has not been done before. Thus, the following theorem is of independent interest.

Let
J
denote the
n\times n
matrix with all entries equal to
1
. Given a graph
S
, let

A(S)
denote its adjacency matrix and let
|S|
be its number of edges. Throughout the paper, we let
N
denote
\binom{n}{2}
, which is the number of edges in a complete graph on
n
vertices.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
c\in(0,1)
be fixed and
S=S(n)
be graphs on vertex set
[n]
such that

(i)

\operatorname{rng}(S)=o(\varDelta(S)/\log n)
;

(ii)

\|A(S)-\frac{|S|}{N}J\|_{2}\leqslant c\varDelta(S)
;

(iii)

for any fixed
\delta>0
, we have

\sum_{(jk)\mathrel{:}jk\in S}x_{j}y_{k}=(1+o(1))\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(S)}{n}$}.

uniformly over all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,1]^{n}
with
\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1},\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}\geqslant\delta n
.

Let
\boldsymbol{t}=\boldsymbol{t}(n)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be degree sequences satisfying

(iv)

\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})=o(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})/\log n)
and
\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\log^{2}n}{\varDelta(S)}$}\ll\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})\ll\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(S)}{\log n}$}
.

Then, uniformly over
jk\in S
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(jk\in S_{\boldsymbol{t}})=(1+o(1))\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})}{\varDelta(S)}.

Theorem
1.8
is a corollary of Theorem
3.1
by taking
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\log c/\log n
and
\beta=2
. The proof of the latter theorem will be the topic of Section
3
.

The restriction of Theorem
1.8
on the relative density
\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})/\varDelta(S)\ll\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{1}{\log n}$}
is the fundamental reason determining our range

.
Any improvement on this would allow sparser degree sequences, in particular, an analog of Theorem
1.8
under a relaxed condition
\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})/\varDelta(S)=o(1)
would resolve Conjecture
1.1
completely.

Lemma 2.1

Then, for all

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(jk\in S_{\boldsymbol{t}})=\left(1+O\left(\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})+\varDelta(K_{n}\setminus S))}{tn}\right)\right)\frac{t_{j}t_{k}}{tn}.

Proof.

We follow the notation in

]

,

Claim 2.3

If

Remark 2.4
.

One might think that, since

does not change between the steps

and

and variations between the conditional edge probabilities in (

In particular, it follows from Claim

that
G^{(\infty)}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d})
.
Thus, it remains to prove the claimed probability bound for the event

G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\infty)}
. Note that, for our choice of
\mu
,

p_{0}=1-e^{-(1-\xi)M/N}\leqslant(1-\xi)M/N.

Applying the Chernoff bound for
|{\mathcal{G}}(n,(1-\xi)M/{N})|\sim\operatorname{\mathbf{Bin}}(N,(1-\xi)M/N)
(see Lemma
4.1
), we get that

M-m^{(\mathcal{T})}\geqslant M-m_{0}^{(\mathcal{T})}\geqslant M-|{\mathcal{G}}(n,(1-\xi)M/{N})|\geqslant\xi M/2

(2.4)

with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\xi M)}\geqslant 1-e^{-\omega(\log n)}
.
By our construction, we have
G^{(\mathcal{T})}\subseteq G^{(\infty)}
, therefore

G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\infty)}
implies that

G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\mathcal{T})}
. Moreover, if
G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\mathcal{T})}
occurs then one of the two events must occur:

•

m^{(\mathcal{T})}>(1-\xi/2)M

•

\eta(G^{(\tau_{i})},e)>\zeta
for some

and
i\leqslant(1-\xi/2)M
.

Note that we only need to consider steps
\tau_{i}
since
G^{(\tau)}=G^{(\tau_{i})}
for all
\tau_{i}\leqslant\tau<\tau_{i+1}
.
By the union bound,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\infty)})

\displaystyle\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\mathcal{T})})

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\omega(\log n)}+\sum_{i\leqslant(1-\xi/2)M}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{e\notin G^{(\tau_{i})}}\left\{\eta(G^{(\tau_{i})},e)\right\}>\zeta\right).

Next, for each
i\leqslant(1-\xi/2)M
, we estimate the probability that there is
e\notin G^{(\tau_{i})}
such that
\eta(G^{(\tau_{i})},e)>\zeta
.
By Lemma
2.1
with

S:=K_{n}\setminus G^{({\tau}_{i})},\qquad\boldsymbol{t}:=\boldsymbol{d}-\boldsymbol{g}^{({\tau}_{i})},

where

\boldsymbol{g}^{({\tau}_{i})}
is the degree sequence of
G^{({\tau}_{i})}
.
By Claim
2.3
, we have
G^{(\tau_{i})}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d},i)
. Thus we get that, for all
j\in[n]
,

t_{j}\sim\operatorname{Hypergeometric}(M,d_{j},M-i).

(2.6)

Indeed, for every graph
X\in{\mathcal{G}}(n,{\boldsymbol{d}})
, after sampling a uniformly random set of
M-i
edges from the set of total
M
edges of
X
,
t_{j}
counts the number of edges incident with
j
that are sampled, out of the total
d_{j}
edges incident with
j
in
X
.
Note that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\boldsymbol{t}=\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{M-i}{M}$}\boldsymbol{d}.

Applying the Chernoff bound for all

(see Lemma
4.1
with
\varepsilon=\xi
) and using (
2.4
), we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{t}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\boldsymbol{t}\right\|_{\infty}>\xi\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{M-i}{M}$}\,d\right)\leqslant ne^{-\Omega(\xi^{3}d)}=e^{-\omega(\log n)}.

Since
\boldsymbol{d}
is near
d
-regular, we have
M=(1+o(1))nd/2
. Note also that

t=\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{t_{1}+\cdots+t_{n}}{n}$}=2\,\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{M-i}{n}$}\geqslant\xi M/n.

Since
\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{d})\leqslant\xi d
, we get that with probability at least
1-e^{-\omega(\log n)}
,

\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})\leqslant\operatorname{rng}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\boldsymbol{t})+\|\boldsymbol{t}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\boldsymbol{t}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{M-i}{M}$}\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{d})+\xi\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{M-i}{M}$}d=O(\xi t).

(2.7)

We also bound

\varDelta(K_{n}\setminus S)=\varDelta(G^{({\tau}_{i})})=O(d).

Applying Lemma
2.1
and taking
C
in the definition of
\zeta
to be sufficiently large, we find that

\max_{e\notin G^{(\tau_{i})}}\left\{\eta(G^{(\tau_{i})},e)\right\}=O\left(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})-\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{2}}{tn}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})\varDelta(K_{n}-S)}{tn}$}\right)=O(\xi)<\zeta

(2.8)

with probability at least
1-e^{-\omega(\log n)}
.

Finally, combining (
2.5
) and (

), we get

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(G_{\zeta}^{(\mathcal{T})}\not\subseteq G^{(\infty)})\leqslant e^{-\omega(\log n)}+(1-\xi/2)Me^{-\omega(\log n)}=e^{-\omega(\log n)}

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.5

2.2
Top of the sandwich

In this section, we construct a coupling
{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)
based on the claim that all edges typically appear in our sequential construction with asymptotically the same probability.
Recall that

G_{\boldsymbol{d}}
is a random graph distributed according to

{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d})

and

N:=\binom{n}{2},\qquad M:=|G_{\boldsymbol{d}}|=\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{1}{2}$}\sum_{i\in[n]}d_{i}.

Recall also that
{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d},m)
is the model of a random graph obtained from

G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,\boldsymbol{d})
by choosing
m
edges uniformly at random.

Recall that Theorem
2.2
requires
\boldsymbol{d}
to be a positive near

2.1
) for some

and

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\boldsymbol{d}=\boldsymbol{d}(n)
be a near

-regular sequence, where

,

Proof.

Let

be an arbitrary graph on

with

and

and

, respectively.
Let

. For

\displaystyle H_{0}^{({\tau})}

\displaystyle:=H_{0}^{({\tau}-1)}\setminus e^{({\tau})};

\displaystyle H_{\zeta}^{({\tau})}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}H_{\zeta}^{({\tau}-1)}&\text{if $a^{({\tau})}\geqslant 1-\zeta$,}\\[2.15277pt]
H_{\zeta}^{({\tau}-1)}\setminus e^{({\tau})}&\text{otherwise;}\end{cases}

\displaystyle H^{({\tau})}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}H^{({\tau}-1)}&\text{if $a^{({\tau})}\geqslant 1-\eta_{F}^{({\tau})}$}\\
H^{({\tau}-1)}\setminus e^{({\tau})}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\eta_{F}(H^{({\tau}-1)},e^{({\tau})}):=\begin{cases}1&\hskip-145.00021pt\text{if $|H^{({\tau}-1)}|=M$ or $e^{({\tau})}\in F$, or $e^{({\tau})}\notin H^{({\tau}-1)}$}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle 1-\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(e^{({\tau})}\notin G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\mid F\subseteq G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\subseteq H^{({\tau}-1)}\right)}{\max_{e\in H^{({\tau}-1)}}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(e\notin G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\mid F\subseteq G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\subseteq H^{({\tau}-1)}\right)}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that if

has more than

edges
then

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(F\subseteq G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\subseteq H^{({\tau}-1)}\right)>0

and there is an edge
e\in H^{({\tau}-1)}

such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(e\notin G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\mid F\subseteq G_{\boldsymbol{d}}\subseteq H^{({\tau}-1)}\right)>0
.
Therefore
H^{({\tau})}
eventually becomes a graph with degree

\boldsymbol{d}
, which we denote by

.

Similarly to Claim
2.3
, we describe the distribution of

at the steps where the number of edges is decreased.
For

, we consider model

and increase its edge set
by

edges chosen uniformly at random from the complement of

. Equivalently, a graph from
{\mathcal{G}}^{+}_{F}(n,\boldsymbol{d},i)
is the compliment of a random set of
i
edges sampled as follows: take a uniform random element

of the set of graphs with degree sequence
\boldsymbol{d}
containing

and then pick
i

Lemma 3.3

Let

Fix

3.2

for

\displaystyle=(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})\varDelta(S))^{h}\exp\left(O\left(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{t_{\min}}$}\right)\right),

\displaystyle=\exp\left(O\left(\gamma+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{t_{\min}}$}\right)\right)\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{6}\varDelta(S)^{7}/n,

\displaystyle\langle(B^{\prime}A)^{2}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{j},(B^{\prime}A)^{3}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{k}\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(O\left(\gamma+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{t_{\min}}$}\right)\right)\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{7}\varDelta(S)^{6}/n.

First we consider the case when both graphs are regular.
Let

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a
t
-regular graph and

be a

-regular graph on the same vertex set

Assume that
\bigl\|A({S})-\frac{|S|}{N}J\bigr\|_{2}\leqslant{s}n^{-\alpha}

Then, for any integers

,

, we have

Proof.

Let
A=A(S)
and
B=A(T)
.
Consider any matrix
P
which is a product of
a
factors

and
b

Let

denote the matrix obtained by replacing all factors

Write

, where

is the vector with all components equal

. Note that

,

and
\boldsymbol{v}\perp\boldsymbol{1}
.
Since operators

and

act identically on the space orthogonal to

, we find that

Since

is symmetric

is equal to the largest eigenvalue of

.
Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|P\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\lVert P\boldsymbol{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{n}$}+\|P\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\infty}=\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\lVert P\boldsymbol{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{n}$}+\|\tilde{P}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\lVert P\boldsymbol{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{n}$}+\|\tilde{P}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{2}

(3.7)

Again, the first inequality in (

) above holds since

denotes the maximum number of walks starting from a given vertex, which use exactly
a
edges in
S
and
b

, and since
\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{2}^{2}=(1-1/n)^{2}+(n-1)/n^{2}=(n-1)/n<1

we have that
\lVert\tilde{P}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{2}\leqslant\lVert\tilde{P}\rVert_{2}\leqslant\|(A-\tfrac{|S|}{N}J)\|_{2}^{a}\cdot\|B\|_{2}^{b}

Taking the maximum over all

∎

We will need a bound similar to Lemma

(3.8)

The next lemma shows that if

then there exists a regular supergraph

which is not much bigger than

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices such that
\varDelta(G)+3\operatorname{rng}(G)<n/4
.
If

is an even number that

\varDelta(G)+\operatorname{rng}(G)\leqslant d\leqslant\varDelta(G)+2\operatorname{rng}(G)

then there exists a
d
-regular supergraph
\tilde{G}
of
G
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{rng}(G)=0
there is nothing to prove as we can take
\tilde{G}=G
. Thus, we may assume otherwise. Define sequence
\boldsymbol{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
by
r_{i}=d-d_{G}(i)
, where
d_{G}(i)
is the degree of vertex
i
in
G
.
It is sufficient to find an
\boldsymbol{r}
-factor of
K_{n}-G
because the union of this
\boldsymbol{r}
-factor and

gives our desired

.

By the assumptions, for all vertices

, we have

Then, for any

If, on the other hand,

Also,

{\boldsymbol{r}}

we find that

\displaystyle(n-\varDelta(G)-\varDelta(R))\operatorname{rng}(G)>(\varDelta(G)+\varDelta(R)-1)\varDelta(R).

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2s_{\min}^{a}t_{\min}^{b}}{n}\left(\frac{\varDelta(\tilde{S})}{s_{\min}}\cdot\frac{\varDelta(\tilde{T})}{t_{\min}}\right)^{\max\{a,b\}}\leqslant\frac{2s_{\min}^{a}t_{\min}^{b}}{n}\beta^{3\max\{a,b\}\alpha},

where the last inequality above holds by (
3.10
) and (A3).
The same bound holds for
w_{a,b}(S,T^{\prime})
.
Observing that
\ell=O(1/\alpha)
, we get that, for
1/\alpha\leqslant h\leqslant(\ell-1)/2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|(BA)^{h}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{1}}{\|(BA)^{h}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{(s_{\min}t_{\min})^{h}}{w_{h,h}}\geqslant n\beta^{-3h\alpha}/2\geqslant\delta n,

\displaystyle\frac{\|(B^{\prime}A)^{h}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{1}}{\|(BA)^{h}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{s_{\min}^{h}t_{\min}^{h+1}}{w_{h,h}}\geqslant n\beta^{-3(h+1)\alpha}/2\geqslant\delta n,

where
\delta=\delta(\beta)>0
corresponds to the largest
h
within the specified range since
\beta\geqslant 1
. We also get

\displaystyle\|(BA)^{h}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})\varDelta(S))^{h}\exp\left(O\left(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{\alpha s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha t_{\min}}$}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\|(B^{\prime}A)^{h}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=(\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t}))^{h+1}(\varDelta(S))^{h}\exp\left(O\left(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{\alpha s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha t_{\min}}$}\right)\right).

Using similar bounds for
\boldsymbol{e}_{k}
and recalling the definition of
\gamma_{S}
from (
3.2
) (and scaling by the infinity norm), we find that

\displaystyle\langle(BA)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\boldsymbol{e}_{j},(BA)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\boldsymbol{e}_{k}\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(O\left(\gamma_{S}(\delta)+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{\alpha s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha t_{\min}}$}\right)\right)\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell-1}\varDelta(S)^{\ell}/n,

\displaystyle\langle(B^{\prime}A)^{\frac{\ell-3}{2}}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{j},(B^{\prime}A)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}B^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}_{k}\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(O\left(\gamma_{S}(\delta)+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{\alpha s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha t_{\min}}$}\right)\right)\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-1}/n.

Next, using again
\ell=O(\alpha^{-1})
, we apply (
3.11
) to bound
quantities
w_{a,b}
and
w_{a,b}^{\prime}
that appear in the
lower bounds of Lemma
3.2
. Thus, we find that

\displaystyle\ell w_{\ell-1,\ell}

\displaystyle=O\Bigl(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-1}}{\alpha n}$}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\ell^{2}\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell-z-2}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-z-1}w_{z,z}=O\Bigl(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell-2}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-1}}{\alpha^{2}n}$}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\ell w_{\ell-2,\ell+1}

\displaystyle=O\Bigl(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell+1}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-2}}{\alpha n}$}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\ell^{2}\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell-z-1}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-z-2}w^{\prime}_{z,z}=O\Bigl(\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})^{\ell-1}\varDelta(S)^{\ell-2}}{\alpha^{2}n}$}\Bigr).

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{B}_{\ell}(T)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(T^{\prime})|}=\exp\left(O\left(\gamma_{S}(\delta)+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(S)}{\alpha s_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{rng}(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha t_{\min}}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})}{\alpha\varDelta(S)}$}+\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})\varDelta(S)}$}\right)\right)\frac{\varDelta(\boldsymbol{t})}{\varDelta(S)}.

Combining (

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A_{p}
be the adjacency matrix of
G\sim G(n,p)
, where

\log n\leqslant pn\leqslant n-\log n
. Assume

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)
satisfies

\sqrt{\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\log n}{pn}$}}\ll\varepsilon\ll 1
. Then

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(\|A_{p}-pJ\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon pn\right)=1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}n^{2})},

where
J
denotes the
n\times n
matrix with all entries equal
1
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda(A_{p})=\lVert A_{p}-pJ\rVert_{2}
. This is the spectral norm of
A_{p}
since the matrix is symmetric.
Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{p}=pJ-pI
and
p\ll\varepsilon pn
under our assumptions, we have
\lambda(A_{p})\leqslant\lVert A_{p}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{p}\rVert_{2}+\lVert pI\rVert_{2}=\lVert A_{p}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{p}\rVert_{2}+o(\varepsilon pn)
.

First consider
\log^{4}n\leqslant pn\leqslant 1-\log^{4}n
.
By
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
,
\lambda(A_{p})\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\lambda(A_{p})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon pn
with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}n^{2})}
.
Also, under our assumption on
p
,
[
15
, Theorem 1.4]

gives
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\lambda(A_{p})\leqslant 2p^{1/2}(1-p)^{1/2}\sqrt{n}+Cp^{1/4}n^{1/4}\log n+p
for some constant
C
.
Using our lower bound on
\varepsilon
,
this implies that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\lambda(A_{p})=o(\varepsilon pn)
.
Consequently,
\lambda(A_{p})\leqslant\varepsilon pn
with probability
at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}n^{2})}
.

For
\log n\leqslant pn\leqslant\log^{4}n
, we can use
[
1
, Corollary 3.3]
.
Note that the parameter
d
in
[
1
]
equals
(n-1)p
and that
our lower bound on
\varepsilon
implies
\varepsilon^{-2}\log^{1/2}n\,(\log\log n)^{-1/2}=o(pn)
,
so
[
1
, Eq. (3.7)]
is satisfied.
Therefore, we have
\lambda(A)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon pn+p\leqslant\varepsilon pn
with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{3}n^{3})}=1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}n^{2})}
.

For
n-\log^{4}n\leqslant pn\leqslant n-\log n
, note that

A_{p}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{p}=-(A_{1-p}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{1-p})
.
Applying
[
1
, Corollary 3.3]
to
A_{1-p}
, we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\lVert A_{p}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}A_{p}\rVert_{2}>\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon pn\bigr)=e^{-\Omega((1-p)\varepsilon^{2}n^{3})}=e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}n^{2})}
since
1-p\gg n^{-1}

and
p\sim 1
.
Since
\varepsilon pn\gg 1
, the same bound holds
for
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\lVert A_{p}-pJ\rVert_{2}>\varepsilon pn\bigr)
.
∎

Next, we prove that binomial random graphs are pseudorandom
in the following sense. Recall from (
3.1
) that, for
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a graph
G
,

\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}=\sum_{(jk)\mathrel{:}jk\in G}x_{j}y_{k}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,p)
for some
1\geqslant p\gg\log n/n
.
Assume

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)>0
such that

\sqrt{\lower 0.6458pt\hbox{\large$\textstyle\frac{\log n}{pn}$}}\ll\varepsilon\ll 1
.
Then, with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}pn^{2})}
, we have

\bigl|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}-p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}\bigr|\leqslant\varepsilon p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}

uniformly for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,1]^{n}
with
\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1},\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}=\Omega(n)
.

Proof.

For any

\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,1]^{n}
with
\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1},\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}=\Omega(n)
, we have that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}=p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}-p\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}y_{j}=\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right)p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}.

Note that
\operatorname{Var}\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}\leqslant 2pn^{2}
.
Using McDiarmid’s inequality
[
13
, Theorem 2.7]
(with
V=\operatorname{Var}\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}
,
b=2
and
t=\varepsilon p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}/2
), we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}|}{p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}}\geqslant\varepsilon/2\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\exp\left(-\frac{(\varepsilon p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}/2)^{2}}{2\operatorname{Var}\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}+(2/3)\varepsilon p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}pn^{2})}.

To make the probability estimate hold for all such
\boldsymbol{x}
,
\boldsymbol{y}
, we approximate them
with
\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\in\{j/n\mathrel{:}j=1,\ldots,n\}^{n}
such that

\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leqslant n^{-1}
and
\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leqslant n^{-1}
.
Denoting
\boldsymbol{1}=(1,\ldots,1)^{\mathrm{T}}\!
, we find that

\displaystyle|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}-\langle\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{G}-\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}|+|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}-\langle\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}|+|\langle\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\rangle_{G}|

\displaystyle=O(n^{-1})\left(\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{1}\rangle_{G}+\langle\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{1}\rangle_{G}\right)=O(n),

as

\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{1}\rangle_{G},\langle\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{1},\rangle_{G}\leqslant\langle\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{1}\rangle_{G}\leqslant n^{2}
. Observe that
\varepsilon p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{1}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{1}\gg n
, so all the error terms are within the required range.
Allowing
n^{2n}
for choice of
\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}
, using the union bound
and recalling that
n\log n\ll\varepsilon^{2}pn^{2}
, the proof is complete.
∎

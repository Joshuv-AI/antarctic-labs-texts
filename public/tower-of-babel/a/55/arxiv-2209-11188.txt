No-slip boundary condition for vorticity equation in 2D exterior domains
Aleksei Gorshkov

Abstract
In this article we derive the no-slip boundary condition for a non-stationary vorticity equation. This condition generates the affine invariant manifold and no-slip integral relations on vorticity can be transferred to a Robin-type boundary condition.

Preliminary

Fluid dynamics equations written in terms of vorticity favourably differ from ones covering velocity evolution. So, for both 2D and 3D Navier-Stokes systems the vorticity dynamics involves a fewer number of equations rather than in the velocity-pressure formulation. For barotropic fluid the curl removes the most problematic term - pressure. If the system doesn’t involve boundary condition (e.g. Cauchy problem), then the
\operatorname{curl}
operator significantly simplifies corresponding
initial-value problem
. But in the case of
initial-boundary-value problem
it isn’t true since Dirichlet no-slip boundary turns out to be a sophisticated integral condition. And so, vorticity boundary conditions mostly are deduced in vorticity-stream formulation
[
1
]
[
2
]
[
3
]
[
4
]
. In
[
5
]
was studied the parametric boundary condition covering vorticity and stream. Some new results on Newman and Dirichlet boundary conditions for vorticity on solid walls were given in
[
6
]
.

We will investigate the boundary vorticity which corresponds to the no-slip condition. At first sight there is no analogous to no-slip condition only in terms of vorticity without additional functions involved (e.g. stream function, velocity). Biot-Savar law restores the solenoidal velocity field
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})
induced by vorticity
w(\mathbf{x})
. It expresses vorticity via velocity field by some integral relation. If the flow interacts with solid by no-slip condition, then it turns to zero integral relation in Biot-Savar law which doesn’t admit explicit integration. If we look at dolphins, then their skin can read vorticity distribution in order to prevent turbulence. In a playful way we can say that dolphins know something about boundary vorticity.

In this paper will be established vorticity boundary condition for both linear and nonlinear Helmholtz equations without any stream function involved in. For the linear Stokes system in the exterior of the disc such boundary condition along with explicit formula to Stokes problem was obtained by the author in
[
7
]
. In this article we show that for nonlinear vorticity equation in the exterior of the disc of radius
r_{0}
the boundary condition can be defined in terms of vorticity Fourier coefficients
w_{k}(t,r)
as Robin-type boundary problem:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=u_{k}(t).

(0.1)

And the same boundary-value problem will be actual in more general domains with help of Riemann mapping.

For exterior flows it is typical to study solutions with infinite energy. These solutions with finite Dirichlet integral were studied in
[
8
]
[
9
]
. For Cauchy problem global existence and bounds on velocity as time
t\to\infty
were established in
[
10
]
[
11
]
[
12
]
. In this paper we will research the vorticity equation for infinite energy solutions. The main difficulty is the fact that the space
L_{2}
is not enough to describe vorticity dynamics. The solutions with finite vorticity energy but nonzero total circulation may have infinite kinetic energy. But in case of zero circularity it’s not getting considerably better. In order to restore velocity via Biot-Savar law and correctly define the nonlinear term
(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)
we need to impose additional requirements on the phase space.

The Laplace operator with Robin boundary condition (
0.1
) possesses the non-trivial kernel and as result it causes the presence of the stationary solution for the corresponding evolution equation. But from the no-slip condition follows an orthogonality relation between the solution and the kernel (see
[
7
]
for more details). It is entirely consistent with the Stokes Paradox.

The paper is arranged as follows. In Section 1 we study Biot-Savar law and the integral relation for no-slip condition. Then in Section 2 we will derive the precise integral boundary condition for Stokes system and its local approximation for Helmholtz vorticity equation. In Section 3 we will prove the local existence of no-slip condition for vorticity. In Appendix the solvability of the vorticity equation with boundary (
0.1
) will be established.

1
Biot-Savar law in exterior domains and no-slip integral condition

Now we study when the solenoidal velocity field
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})
can be uniquely restored from its vorticity
w(\mathbf{x})
. Consider the following elliptic problem in exterior domain
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\rm{div}~\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\rm{curl}~\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=w(\mathbf{x}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=0,~\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\to\mathbf{v}_{\infty},~|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty,.

(1.4)

Exterior domains are not simply connected and the problem above could haven’t unique solution. For example, equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) supplied with slip condition on the boundary

\left(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{n}\right)=0,~\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega,

(1.5)

and fixed flow at infinity (
1.4
) have a unique solution only if we fix circularity at infinity (
\mathbf{n}
is an outer normal to boundary). No-slip condition (
1.3
) is stronger than (
1.5
), and so some additional restrictions on
w(\mathbf{x})
are required. These restrictions can be realized via moment relations for vorticity. In
[
7
]
the solvability of the system above was researched in detail for slip and no-slip conditions in the exterior of the disc. Here we extend these results on more general domains and obtain integral no-slip condition.

So, we need to fix circularity at infinity. From a physical point of view it is natural to suppose zero-circularity:

\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{|\mathbf{x}|=R}\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=0.

(1.6)

Then the solution of the above problem if it exists is given by Biot-Savar formula

\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})^{\perp}}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{2}}w(\mathbf{y})\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}+\mathbf{v}_{\infty},

(1.7)

which we rewrite in polar coordinates. Boundary condition (
1.3
) obliges the vorticity
w(\mathbf{y})
to satisfy some additional equities.

The relationship between Cartesian and polar coordinate systems for
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}=(v_{\infty,x},v_{\infty,y})
is given by formulas:

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r}=v_{\infty,x}\cos\varphi+v_{\infty,y}\sin\varphi,

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi}=v_{\infty,y}\cos\varphi-v_{\infty,x}\sin\varphi.

Then its Fourier coefficients are determined as

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}(v_{\infty,x}-ikv_{\infty,y}),

(1.8)

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}(v_{\infty,y}+ikv_{\infty,x}),

(1.9)

and

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=\operatorname{sign}(k)iv_{\infty,r,k}.

(1.10)

All Fourier coefficients of the external flow equal to zero except
k=\pm 1
. For horizontal flow
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}=(v_{\infty},0)

\displaystyle v_{\infty,r,k}=\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}v_{\infty},

\displaystyle v_{\infty,\phi,k}=ik\frac{\delta_{|k|,1}}{2}v_{\infty}.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\frac{r^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

(1.12)

\displaystyle M_{N}=\Big\{w(\mathbf{x})\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})~\Big|~\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{\Phi^{\prime-1}(z)}\frac{w(\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=4\pi ikv_{\infty,r,k},

(1.28)

\displaystyle k=0,1,\dots,N\Big\}.

The following lemma says that since
M
corresponds to a no-slip condition, then
M_{N}
is the approximation of this boundary condition.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let for any fixed
t\in[0,T]

w_{N}(t,\cdot)
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
by
N
. Then the vector field
\mathbf{v}^{N}(t,\cdot)
given by (
1.25
) converges weakly in
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
to zero for
t\in[0,T]
as
N\to\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
\Omega=B_{r_{0}}
.

\displaystyle v_{r,k}(t,r_{0})=\operatorname{sign}(k)\frac{ir^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(t,s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,r,k}

\displaystyle v_{\varphi,k}(t,r_{0})=\frac{r^{|k|-1}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(t,s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,\phi,k}.

From
w(t,\cdot)\in M_{N}

v_{r,k}(t,r_{0})=v_{\varphi,k}(t,r_{0})=0
for
k=-N,\dots,N
. For
k>N

\displaystyle\left|\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(t,s)\operatorname{ds}+v_{\infty,r,k}\right|=\left|\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|+1}w_{k}(t,s)\operatorname{ds}\right|.

Then from (
1.15
)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\|w^{N}_{k}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,rdr)}.

For
w_{N}(t,\cdot)\in M_{N}
the Fourier coefficients of
\mathbf{v}_{r}
,
\mathbf{v}_{\phi}
with index
k=-N,\dots,N
equal to zero which implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\rightharpoonup 0,~\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega,~(weakly)

(1.29)

in
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.
∎

Remark
.

If
w_{N}(t,\cdot)
are uniformly bounded in
H^{2}
one can prove that
v(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
converges weakly in
H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)
to zero. And from the Rellich–Kondrachov Theorem due to the compact embedding follows that for any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\to 0,~\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega~(strongly)

(1.30)

in
L_{2}(\partial\Omega)
.

2
No-slip integral condition for vorticity in exterior domains

Consider the initial-boundary-value problem for the Navier-Stokes system defined in exterior domain
\Omega
modelling flow around solid with given constant horizontal flow at infinity
\mathbf{v}_{\infty}=(\mathbf{v}_{1,\infty},\mathbf{v}_{2,\infty})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}-\Delta\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{v},\nabla)\mathbf{v}=\nabla p

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm div}~\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=0

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(0,\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=0,~|\mathbf{x}|=r_{0}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})\to\mathbf{v}_{\infty},~|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty.

(2.5)

Here
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=(v_{1}(t,\mathbf{x}),v_{2}(t,\mathbf{x}))
is the velocity field and
p(t,\mathbf{x})
is the pressure.

Applying the curl operator

w(t,\mathbf{x})=

{\rm curl}~\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})

=\partial_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}v_{2}-\partial_{\mathbf{x}_{2}}v_{1}
we get boundary problem for vorticity equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta w+(\mathbf{v},\nabla)w=0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle w(0,\mathbf{x})=w_{0}(\mathbf{x})

(2.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}^{-1}w(t,\mathbf{x})\Big|_{|\mathbf{x}|=r_{0}}=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle w(t,\mathbf{x})\to 0,~|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty

(2.9)

with initial datum
w_{0}(\mathbf{x})={\rm curl}~\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
.

Vector field
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
can be derived from
w(t,\mathbf{x})
using Green function
G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
for Laplace operator
\Delta
:

\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}^{\perp}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})w(\mathbf{y})\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}+\mathbf{v}_{\infty},

where

\Delta_{x}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0,~\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}.

(2.10)

Indeed

{\rm div}~\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{x},\nabla_{x}^{\perp})G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})w(\mathbf{y})\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}=0,

and

{\rm curl}~\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{x}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})w(\mathbf{y})\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}=w.

Then no-slip condition gives the following integral expression

\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}^{\perp}G(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})w(t,\mathbf{y})\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}=0,~\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega,~\forall t>0.

Velocity field for Stokes system satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta v(t,\mathbf{x})=\nabla p

(2.11)

when vorticity evolution for Stokes system is described by the heat equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta w(t,\mathbf{x})=0.

(2.12)

Multiplying it by
\nabla_{x}^{\perp}G(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})
and integrating over exterior domain using Green formula with help of (
2.10
) we will have integral boundary condition

\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}^{\perp}G(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial n}-w(t,\mathbf{y})\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\nabla_{x}^{\perp}G(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})\right)\operatorname{d\mathbf{y}}=0,~\forall\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega.

It is still an integral condition but only with the surface integral over
\partial\Omega
involved.

For cylindrical domains this surface integral turns into boundary condition on Fourier coefficients. In this section for 2D Stokes and Navier-Stokes we derive boundary condition in terms of Fourier harmonics in exterior simply-connected domains.

2.1
Linear vorticity equation in the exterior of the disc

Consider Stokes flow for vorticity (
2.12
) and supply it with Robin-type boundary condition:

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=0,~k\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.13)

and

w(t,\mathbf{x})\to 0,~|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty.

(2.14)

Then
M
is invariant under the flow
w(t,\cdot)
. Indeed, fix
k>0
and divide equation (
2.12
) by
z^{k}
and integrate over the exterior of the disc
B_{r_{0}}
. From moment relations (
1.16
) follows

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(t,\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

and thus

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0.

In other hand

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(t,s)ds

\displaystyle=-2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}w_{k}(t,s)\right)-\frac{k^{2}}{s}w_{k}(t,s)\right)\operatorname{ds}

\displaystyle=-r_{0}^{-|k|+1}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-|k|}\left(|k|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}w_{k}(t,s)-\frac{k^{2}}{s}w_{k}(t,s)\right)\operatorname{ds}

\displaystyle=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=0,

(2.15)

where
\Delta_{k}w(t,r)
is defined in (
0.2
).

In a similar way using complex conjugation of moment relations (
1.16
) we obtain boundary condition for
k<0
:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}{\Delta w}\overline{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{-k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-2\pi r_{0}^{k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}-kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=0.

2.2
Helmholtz equation in the exterior of the disc

Now we are ready to derive the boundary condition to the vorticity equation. In fact it will be an integral condition. But its first approximation will be the same boundary condition (
2.13
) as for Stokes flow.

Consider Helmholtz equation for vorticity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x})}{\partial t}-\Delta w(t,\mathbf{x})+(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)=0,

(2.16)

where
\mathbf{v}
is redtored from
w
via Biot-Savar law (
1.7
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Given initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
,
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})
satisfying no-slip condition (
1.3
), infinity condition (
1.4
), zero-circularity (
1.6
) and
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
be the solution of (
2.1
)-(
2.5
) in
B_{r_{0}}
. Then
w(t,\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
satisfies

\displaystyle r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=

\displaystyle~~~~\left\{{\begin{matrix}\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}){z^{-|k|-1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},~k\geq 0,\\
\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}})z^{-|k|-1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},~k<0.\end{matrix}}\right.

(2.17)

Proof.

Fix
k>0
and divide this equation by
z^{k}
and integrate over the exterior of the disc
B_{r_{0}}
. From moment relations (
1.16
) follows

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{w(t,\mathbf{x})}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

Denote

\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}=v_{1}+iv_{2},~\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}=v_{1,\infty}+iv_{2,\infty}.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}(\mathbf{v},\nabla z^{-k})w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{v_{1}+iv_{2}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

From (
1.16
)

k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0

and

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}.

Using (
2.1
) we get

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right).

Then for
k\geq 0

\displaystyle\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right)=\frac{kr_{0}^{k}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}}{z^{k+1}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}.

Since
w_{-k}(t,r)=\overline{w_{k}(t,r)}
then (
2.17
) holds for
k<0
. Theorem is proved.

∎

Remark
.

If
\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|
is small in some integral norm for a well-streamlined body , then the right side in (
2.17
) transfers to boundary condition (
2.13
). So, (
2.13
) becomes a rather accurate approximation for Navier-Stokes system. From this fact naturally occurs boundary control problem with unknown function
u_{k}(t)
:

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=u_{k}(t),~k\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.18)

where feedback controls

are treated as a small correction of zero boundary condition.

2.3
Linear vorticity equation in a simply connected domains

Here we find out that Robin-type boundary (
2.13
) works well as the no-slip boundary condition in exterior domains. It keeps moment relations (
1.24
) under the Stokes flow, and thus
M
is an invariant affine subspace.

We impose additional requirement on
\Omega
that
\Phi^{-1}
can be represented by absolutely convergent series

\Phi^{-1}(z)=z+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{z^{n}}.

(2.19)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B
, where
B
is a bounded simple-connected domain with smooth boundary and
\Phi
be a Riemann mapping from
\Omega
into exterior of the disc satisfying (

\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

=
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))
satisfies (

Proof.

After Riemann mapping Stokes equations reduce to scalar equation on vorticity

|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\partial_{t}w(t,x)-\Delta w=0.

Fix

and divide this equation by

Using

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{z^{k}}w(t,x)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{n+k}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

In other hand

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta w}{s^{k}e^{ik\phi}}sdsd\varphi=2\pi\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-k+1}\Delta_{k}w_{k}(s)ds

2.13
) for

∎

2.4
Helmholtz equation in a simply connected domains

For the linear vorticity equation we found the boundary condition. Here we find it for nonlinear equation. Vorticity equation (
2.6
) corresponding to Navier-Stokes system after Riemann mapping reduces to

\displaystyle|(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\partial_{t}w(t,\mathbf{x})-\Delta w+B(v,w)=0,

(2.20)

where

B(v,w)=\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)-\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\mathbf{v}^{\perp},\nabla w).

with
\mathbf{x}\in B_{r_{0}}
.

Supply this equation with

\displaystyle r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+|k|w_{k}(t,r_{0})=

\displaystyle~~~~\left\{{\begin{matrix}\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}){z^{-|k|-1}}\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},~k\geq 0,\\
\frac{|k|r_{0}^{|k|}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\overline{(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}})z^{-|k|-1}}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},~k<0.\end{matrix}}\right.

(2.21)

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Phi
as in Theorem
2.2
, given initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
,
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L_{1}(\Omega)
satisfying no-slip condition (
1.3
), infinity condition (
1.4
), zero-

circularity (
1.6
) and
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
be the solution of (

)-(
2.5
) in
\Omega
. Then vorticity
w(t,\cdot)=

\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\Phi^{-1}(z))
satisfies (
2.21
).

Proof.

Apply the same steps as in previous subsections: for fixed
k>0
divide this equation by
z^{k}
and integrate over
B_{r_{0}}
. In the previous subsection we obtained

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{k}}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0,

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\Delta w}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-2\pi r_{0}^{-k}\left(r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})\right).

From (

), (

)

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}.

Then using Cauchy-Riemann equations

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{B(v,w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}(\mathbf{v}^{\perp},\nabla w)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\left(-\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}w\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}\right)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r_{0}}}w\left(\left(v,\nabla\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}\right)-\left(v^{\perp},\nabla\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}{z^{k}}\right)\right)\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}

k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=0,

then

\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{B(v,w)}{z^{k}}\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}}=k\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{\overline{(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}}}{z^{k+1}}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})w(t,\mathbf{x})\operatorname{d\mathbf{x}},

from which follows (
2.21
) for

.

For

(
2.21

w_{-k}(t,r)=\overline{w_{k}(t,r)}

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}-kw_{k}(t,r_{0})=\overline{u_{k}(t)}.

(3.4)

For
|k|>N
set

r_{0}\frac{\partial w_{k}(t,r)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=r_{0}}+kw_{k}(t,r_{0})=0.

(3.5)

We construct approximate boundary condition for Helmholtz equation under the assumption of unique resolvability of (

), (
3.1
)-(

For the Helmholtz problem we should use Sobolev space

as the phase space for

isn’t well defined in

In order to obtain

W=\left\{w\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}}),~w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1}\in L_{1}(r_{0},\infty;r)\right\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that for fixed

4.1
Stokes semi-group estimates

The boundary-value problem (
2.12
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
) can be solved using special Weber-Orr transform (see
[
7
]
for more details):

W_{k,l}[f](\lambda)=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{J_{k}(\lambda s)Y_{l}(\lambda r_{0})-Y_{k}(\lambda s)J_{l}(\lambda r_{0})}{\sqrt{J_{l}^{2}(\lambda r_{0})+Y_{l}^{2}(\lambda r_{0})}}f(s)s\operatorname{ds},~k\in\mathbb{Z},

(4.1)

where

J_{k}(r)
,
Y_{k}(r)
- are the Bessel functions of the first and second type (see
[
13
]
).

The inverse transform is defined by the formula

W^{-1}_{k,l}[\hat{f}](r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{k}(\lambda r)Y_{l}(\lambda r_{0})-Y_{k}(\lambda r)J_{l}(\lambda r_{0})}{\sqrt{J_{l}^{2}(\lambda r_{0})+Y_{l}^{2}(\lambda r_{0})}}\hat{f}(\lambda)\lambda\operatorname{d\lambda}.

(4.2)

The case of
W_{|k|,|k|-1}
is of special interest and this transform can be considered as hydrodynamical. In
[
7
]
was proved the following

Theorem 4.1
.

Let vector field
\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
satisfies (
1.1
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.6
), the vorticity
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

\in L_{1}(B_{r_{0}})
, and its Fourier series as well as Fourier series for its vorticity
w_{0}(\mathbf{x})
with coefficients
w_{k}^{0}(r)
converges,
\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
be the solution of (
2.11
), (
2.2
)-(
2.5
). Then
w(t,\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})
satisfies equation (
2.12
), boundary conditions (
2.13
), (
2.14
) and is given via Fourier coefficients:

\displaystyle w_{k}(t,r)=W^{-1}_{|k|,|k|-1}\left[e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{|k|,|k|-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\right](t,r).

(4.3)

This formula gives an explicit form of the solution for Stokes system in the exterior of the disc in terms of vorticity. From Biot-Savar law one can get the velocity field.

Generalised Weber-Orr transform has a non-trivial kernel. So, the transform (
4.2
) is the inverse one to (
4.1
) only for functions, which are orthogonal to the kernel. But from no-slip condition (
1.13
) follows orthogonality of Fourier coefficients
w_{k}(t,\cdot)
to the kernel of
W_{|k|,|k|-1}
. The invertibility of the generalised Weber-Orr transform was studied in detail in
[
7
]
.

So, Weber-Orr transforms satisfy Bessel-type inequality instead of the Plancherel equity:

\displaystyle\|W_{k,l}[f]\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;\lambda)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty;r)}^{2}.

(4.4)

From differentiation rules for Bessel functions follows

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}W^{-1}_{k,k-1}[f]=\frac{1}{2}\left(W^{-1}_{k-1,k-1}[\lambda f]-W^{-1}_{k+1,k-1}[\lambda f]\right),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{k}{r}W^{-1}_{k,k-1}[f]=\frac{1}{2}\left(W^{-1}_{k+1,k-1}[\lambda f]+W^{-1}_{k-1,k-1}[\lambda f]\right).

(4.6)

In polar coordinates with unit vectors
\mathbf{e_{r}}
,
\mathbf{e_{\varphi}}
the gradient is defined as
\nabla=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\mathbf{e_{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}\mathbf{e_{\varphi}}
with polar coordinates
\nabla_{r}
,
\nabla_{\varphi}
. Multiplier
\frac{k}{r}
corresponds to differentiation with respect to
\varphi
of function

.

Formula (
4.6
) involves Weber transform

which also possesses non-trivial kernel and satisfy Bessel inequality

\|W_{k+1,k-1}[f]\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;\lambda)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty;r)}^{2}.

And vice versa, multiplication by
\lambda
transfers to differentiation but in more general sense including not only
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
but also angle derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}
expressed by multiplier
\frac{k}{r}
. Indeed, using

\displaystyle\lambda J_{k}(\lambda r)=\frac{k-1}{r}J_{k-1}(\lambda r)-\lambda J^{\prime}_{k-1}(\lambda r),

\displaystyle\lambda Y_{k}(\lambda r)=\frac{k-1}{r}Y_{k-1}(\lambda r)-\lambda Y^{\prime}_{k-1}(\lambda r)

we will have

\lambda W_{k,k-1}[f]=W_{k-1,k-1}[\frac{kf}{r}]+W_{k-1,k-1}[f^{\prime}(\cdot)].

(4.7)

Formula (
4.3
) in theorem defines the Stokes semigroup

which corresponds to problem (
2.12
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
). The estimates of this semi-group are given by the following

.

\displaystyle\|S(t)w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq\|w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})},

From Bessel inequality we have

\displaystyle\|w_{k}(t,r)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq\|e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{|k|,|k|-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\|W_{|k|,|k|-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda)}\leq\|w^{0}_{k}(r)\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}.

Summarizing by

we obtain the first estimate.

Fix

and Bessel inequality (
4.4
) from (

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}w_{k}(t,r)\right\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq\|\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{k,k-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|w^{0}_{k}(r)\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}}{\sqrt{2et}}

and

\|\nabla_{r}S(t)w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2et}}\|w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}.

From (
4.6
) in a similar way follows

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{k}{r}w_{k}(t,r)\right\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq\frac{\|w^{0}_{k}(r)\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}}{\sqrt{2et}}.

Then we have

Now we will prove the last inequality. Using (

),(

)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}w_{k}(t,r)\right\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq\|\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{k,k-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\left\|e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{k-1,k-1}[\frac{kw^{0}_{k}}{r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial r}w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)]\right\|\leq\left\|\frac{kw^{0}_{k}}{r}\right\|+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)\right\|

and so

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{r}S(t)w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq\sqrt{2}\|\nabla w_{0}\|_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}.

(4.8)

In a similar way from (

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{k}{r}w_{k}(t,r)\right\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq\|\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}t}W_{k,k-1}[w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)](\lambda)\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\frac{kw^{0}_{k}}{r}\right\|+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}w^{0}_{k}(\cdot)\right\|\right)

and finally

combined with (

∎

3.5
), then its difference
w_{1}-w_{2}
is the solution of the same problem with zero initial and boundary conditions. Then
M
defined above equals zero and from the same Gronwall’s Lemma

\|w_{1}(t,\cdot)-w_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}}\leq 0,

and the theorem is completely proved.
∎

4.3
Existence theorem for Helmholtz equation in exterior of the disc

We consider the map

\displaystyle F\left(w(t,\cdot)\right)=S(t)w_{0}(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{k=-N}^{N}e^{ik\varphi}W_{k,k-1}\left[r_{k}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda^{2}(t-\tau)}u_{k}(\tau)d\tau\right]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)d\tau

(4.11)

associated with the boundary-value problem for the Helmholtz equation (
2.16
). In virtue of Lemma
1.4

F
will be well-defined in
Q=C\left([0,T],W)\right)
, where
W=\{w\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
,
w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1}\in L_{1}(r_{0},\infty;r)\}
. Denote cylinder
Z_{r_{0},T}=[0,T]\times B_{r_{0}}
.

Theorem 4.4

(resolvability of Helmholtz equation)
.

For any
L>0
there exists
T=T(L)>0
such that for all
w_{0}\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
,
u_{k}\in C[0,T]
,
k=0,\dots,N
,
\|w_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq L
,
\|u_{k}(\cdot)\|_{C[0,T]}<L
the problem (
2.16
), (
3.1
)-(
3.5
) has a unique solution
w(t,\mathbf{x})\in Q
.

Proof.

First we derive that
F\left(w(t,\cdot)\right)\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
for fixed
t\in[0,T]
. From the proposition
4.2

S(t)
is continuous mapping in
H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
.

From the estimate

\|\lambda r_{k}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda^{2}(t-\tau)}u_{k}(\tau)d\tau\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty,\lambda d\lambda)}\leq C\|u_{k}(\tau)\|_{C[0,T]}

the second term in (
4.3
) belongs to
H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
.

With help of the proposition
4.2
we have

\displaystyle\left\|F\left(w(t,\cdot)\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq\sqrt{3}\|w_{0}(\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})}+C\sum_{k=-N}^{N}\|u_{k}(\tau)\|_{C[0,T]}+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-\tau)\left(\mathbf{v},\nabla w(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})}d\tau\leq

\displaystyle M+\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{e(t-\tau)}}\right)\left\|\left(\mathbf{v},\nabla w(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})}d\tau

with

M=\sqrt{3}\|w_{0}(\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}+C\sum_{k=-N}^{N}\|u_{k}(\tau)\|_{C[0,T]}.

We invoke the following Sobolev inequality:

\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}.

From (
1.14
) follows

\|\nabla\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq C\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}.

Then in view of

\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq C\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}\|\nabla w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}

we have

\|\nabla\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{4}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq C\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}\|\nabla w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}.

For any
r
on circle
S_{r}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},~|\mathbf{x}|=r\}
,
r\geq r_{0}
holds:

\operatorname{vraisup}_{r\in[r_{0},\infty)}\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|_{L_{4}(S_{r})}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(S_{r})}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{2}(S_{r})}

Then from Lemma
1.1

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}|^{4}d\mathbf{x}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}+\sum_{k=-1,0,1}\|w_{k}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{1}(r_{0},\infty)}\right)\|\nabla\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}.

Finally in virtue of (
1.14
)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{v}_{\infty}\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}+\sum_{k=-1,0,1}\|w_{k}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{1}(r_{0},\infty)}\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}\times

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L_{2}(B_{r_{0}})}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L_{\infty}(Z_{r_{0},T})}\leq C\|w\|_{Q}.

(4.12)

So, with new constant
C>0

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)(\mathbf{v},\nabla w)d\tau\right\|_{H_{1}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(T+2\sqrt{\frac{T}{e}}\right)\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(Z_{r_{0},T})}\|w(\tau,\mathbf{x})\|_{C\left([0,T],H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})\right)}

and
F\left(w(t,\cdot)\right)\in H^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
.

Now we prove that the first Fourier coefficients for
k=-1,0,1
of the function
F\left(w(\tau,\cdot)\right)
belong to
L_{1}(r_{0},\infty;r)
.

From (
2.13
) zero Fourier coefficient of the Stokes semi-group
[S(t)]_{0}
generates radially symmetrical solution
w(t,\mathbf{x})
of the heat equation with Newman boundary condition

\frac{\partial w(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}{\partial n}=0,~|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|=r_{0}.

Semigroup coefficients
[S(t)]_{\pm 1}
correspond to solutions of the heat equation with Robin boundary

\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}+w(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=0,~|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|=r_{0}.

From
L_{p}-L_{q}
estimates for heat equation
[
16
]
with some
C>0

\left\|[S(t)]_{k}f\right\|_{L_{1}(r_{0},\infty,r)}\leq C\sqrt{t}\|f\|_{L_{2}(r_{0},\infty,r)}

for
k=-1,0,1
.

So we deduced that
F:Q\to Q
is well-defined and maps
Q
into itself. Now we prove that
F
is a strict contraction in
Q
in some ball
B=\{w\in Q~|~||w||_{Q}<L\}
.

Take
w_{1},w_{2}\in B
with corresponding velocity fields
\mathbf{v}_{1}
,
\mathbf{v}_{2}
:

\displaystyle F\left(w_{1}(\tau,\cdot)\right)-F\left(w_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\left(\mathbf{v}_{1},\nabla\left(w_{1}(\tau,\mathbf{x})-w_{2}(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)\right)d\tau+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\left(\mathbf{v}_{1}-\mathbf{v}_{2},\nabla w_{2}(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)d\tau

Then from (
4.12
) with some constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}

\displaystyle\|F\left(w_{1}(\tau,\cdot)\right)-F\left(w_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right)\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{vraisup}_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-\tau)\left(\mathbf{v}_{1},\nabla\left(w_{1}(\tau,\mathbf{x})-w_{2}(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}d\tau+

\displaystyle\operatorname{vraisup}_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-\tau)\left(\mathbf{v}_{1}-\mathbf{v}_{2},\nabla w_{2}(\tau,\mathbf{x})\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}d\tau\leq

\displaystyle\left(T+2\sqrt{\frac{T}{e}}\right)\left(\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}(Z_{r_{0},T})}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Q}+\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\mathbf{v}_{2}\|_{L_{\infty}(Z_{r_{0},T})}\|w_{2}\|_{Q}\right)\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(T+2\sqrt{\frac{T}{e}}\right)\left(\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}(Z_{r_{0},T})}+\|w_{2}\|_{Q}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Q}\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}L\left(T+2\sqrt{\frac{T}{e}}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Q}.

Estimates of Fourier coefficients for
k=-1,0,1
can be held in a similar way using
L_{p}-L_{q}
estimates. So, for any
L>0
we can find such a small
T>0
that
F
becomes the strict contraction map in
Q
. Then for small
T
by the Banach fixed point theorem, the map
F
has a unique fixed point
w(t,\mathbf{x})
. The theorem is completely proved.
∎

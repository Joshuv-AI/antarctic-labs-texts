A weakly monotonic, logically constrained, HORPO-variant
Cynthia Kop

Abstract
In this short paper, we present a simple variant of the recursive path ordering, specified for Logically Constrained Simply Typed Rewriting Systems (LCSTRSs). This is a method for curried systems, without λ \lambda but with partially applied function symbols, which can deal with logical constraints . As it is designed for use in the dependency pair framework, it is defined as reduction pair, allowing weak monotonicity.

We assume familiarity with Logically Constrained Simply Typed Rewriting

The main difference with

A

•

If also

2.1
Equivalence

The first of our relations can be defined without reference to the others.
For two terms

if
s
and
t

(Eq-mono)

t=x\ t_{1}\cdots t_{n}

(Eq-args)

We observe that

Lemma 2
.

The relation

is transitive, reflexive, symmetric, monotonic, and stable.

Proof.

By a straighforward induction on the size of a given term

Transitivity:

if
s\approx t\approx u
then
s\approx u
.

Reflexivity:

s\approx s

Symmetry:
if
s\approx t
then
t\approx s

Stability:
if
s\approx t
and
\gamma

s\gamma\approx t\gamma
;
for the case
s=x\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
we do a case analysis on
\gamma(x)
(which must either have
a form

or
\mathtt{f}\ w_{1}\cdots w_{k}
), and the reflexivity property we have
shown above (to find
w_{i}\approx w_{i}
)

For monotonicity, we do not need the induction. Suppose
s::\sigma\Rightarrow\tau
and
t::\sigma
, with
s\approx s^{\prime}
and
t\approx t^{\prime}
. There are two cases:

•

s=x\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
and
s^{\prime}=x\ s_{1}^{\prime}\cdots s_{n}^{\prime}

; since

also

s_{i}\sqsupset t_{i}

Hence: any extension of
[s_{j}\mid j\in\pi(\mathtt{f})\wedge j\in\{1,\dots,i\}]\ (\approx,\sqsupset)_{\mathtt{lex}}
any extension of

[t_{j}\mid j\in\pi(\mathtt{f})\wedge j\in\{1,\dots,i\}]
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Every function symbol is computable.

Proof.

We will prove that for all
\mathtt{f}::\sigma_{1}\Rightarrow\dots\Rightarrow\sigma_{m}\Rightarrow\iota
,
all terms
s_{1}::\sigma_{1},\dots,s_{m}::\sigma_{n}
such that
s_{i}
is computable for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\cap\pi(\mathtt{f})
, that
\mathtt{f}\ s_{1}\cdots s_{m}
is terminating.
We prove this by induction first on
\mathtt{f}
, ordered with
\rhd
, and second on

[s_{i}\mid i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\cap\pi(\mathtt{f})]
, ordered with

(\approx,\sqsupset)_{\mathtt{lex}}
.
To see that the latter is indeed a terminating relation, note that by
Lemma
9
,
\sqsupset
is terminating on the set of all computable terms
(and this set is closed under
\sqsupset
), and that the lexicographic extension of a terminating
relation is terminating when the length of sequences is bounded (as we assumed is the case: the
maximal arity of symbols
\equiv
-equivalent to
\mathtt{f}
is bounded).

To see that
\mathtt{f}\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
is terminating, it suffices to show that
t
is terminating if

\mathtt{f}\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}\sqsupset t
. But there are only two cases to consider: if
\mathtt{f}\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}\sqsupset t
by Gr-mono, then
t
is terminating by the second induction hypothesis because at
least one argument in
\pi(\mathtt{f})
is decreased; and if
\mathtt{f}\ s_{1}\cdots s_{n}\sqsupset t
by
Gr-rpo, then
t
is computable, and therefore terminating, by Lemma
10
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

\sqsupset
is a well-founded relation on the set of terms.

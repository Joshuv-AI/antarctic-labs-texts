Quantum Echo in Two-Component Bose-Einstein Condensates
Chang-Yan Wang

Abstract
The development of ultracold atom technology has enabled the precise investigations on quantum dynamics of quantum gases. Recently, inspired by experimental advancement, the S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 ) SU(1,1) echo, akin to the well-known S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) spin echo, has been proposed for single-component Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC). In this paper, we investigate the possibility of quantum echo in the more intricate two-component BEC by fully exploiting its underlying symmetry, which is the Lie group S ​ p ​ ( 4 , R ) Sp(4,R) . We demonstrate that quantum echo can occur for the two-component BEC by applying a driving protocol consisting of two steps in each period. The first step can be any Bogoliubov Hamiltonian, while the second step is a Hamiltonian with interactions turned off, which plays a similar role as the π \pi -pulse in spin echo. We confirm our theoretical results with numerical calculations for different examples of two-component BEC. We further consider the effect of interactions between the excited boson modes on the quantum echo process and discuss the possible experiment implementation of this quantum echo.

II
The quantum dynamics and
Sp(4,R)
symmetry

We consider the physical process that a two-component Bose gas is prepared in a fully condensed state, i.e. all particles condensed in the zero momentum modes, then the system evolves under the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian. The general form of this quench Bogoliubov Hamiltonian can be written as
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Bg}}(t)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{0}}\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)+\mathrm{const.}
, where

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}},\ \mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi_{\mathbf{k}}(t)&\eta(t)\\
\eta^{*}(t)&\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}(t)\end{array}\right),

with
\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}}=(a_{1,\mathbf{k}},a_{2,\mathbf{k}},a_{1,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},a_{2,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})^{T}

Pitaevskii and Stringari 2016
;
Wang and He 2022
. Here
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
is a
2\times 2
Hermitian matrix given by
\xi_{\mathbf{k},ij}(t)=\frac{1}{2}[\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},ij}(t)+g_{ij}(t)\psi_{i}\psi_{j}^{*}]
, and
\eta(t)
is a complex symmetric matrix given by
\eta_{ij}(t)=\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}(t)\psi_{i}\psi_{j}
, and
i,j=1,2
label the two species of bosons. The diagonal elements of
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
are the kinetic terms, whereas the off-diagonal elements are the spin-orbit coupling terms.
g_{ij}(t)
represent the intra- and inter-species interaction strengths of these two species of bosons.
\psi_{i}=\sqrt{N_{i}/V}e^{i\theta}
are the condensate wavefunctions of the zero momentum modes. And for simplicity, we set
\hbar=1
.

On the other hand, the two species of the boson operators naturally give a representation of the Lie algebra of the real symplectic group
Sp(4,R)

Perelomov 1986
;
Hasebe 2020
. The generators of this Lie algebra can be defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}=a_{i,\mathbf{k}}a_{j,-\mathbf{k}}+a_{j,\mathbf{k}}a_{i,-\mathbf{k}},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}=a_{i,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{j,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}+a_{j,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{i,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{l}^{k}=a_{k,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{l,\mathbf{k}}+a_{l,-\mathbf{k}}a_{k,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},

(4)

where
i,j,k,l=\{1,2\}
, and ten of them are independent. The Casimir operator of this Lie algebra is given by
Hasebe 2020

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}+\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}-2\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i}\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{i}^{j}.

(5)

To show that this is a conserved quantity, we can rewrite it as
Hasebe 2020

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=-2(\Delta N_{\mathbf{k}}+2)(\Delta N_{\mathbf{k}}-2),

(6)

where
\Delta N_{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(a_{i,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{i,\mathbf{k}}-a_{i,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{i,-\mathbf{k}})
counts the difference in the number of bosons between the momenta
+\mathbf{k}
and
-\mathbf{k}
. According to the definition of
\mathfrak{sp}(4,R)
generators Eq.(
II
), none of these generators change this number difference. Thus, the Casimir operator is conserved.

These operators have a one-to-one correspondence with the matrix representation of the
\mathfrak{sp}(4,R)
Lie algebra. This correspondence can be revealed by rewriting the operators defined in Eq.(
II
) as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}=\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\kappa Y_{ij}\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}},\ \hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}=\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\kappa Y^{ij}\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}},\ \hat{\mathcal{X}}_{l}^{k}=\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\kappa Y_{l}^{k}\hat{\Psi}_{\mathbf{k}},

(7)

where
\kappa=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)

Hasebe 2020
;
Wang and He 2022
. Here, these
4\times 4
matrices
\{Y_{ij},Y^{ij},Y_{l}^{k}\}
are the matrix representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(4,R)
, and their explicit form is given in Appendix
B
. Then, we can write the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}
in terms of these generators as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\xi_{\mathbf{k},ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}^{*}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij},

(8)

where we have adopted the Einstein summation convention, i.e. the repeated indices
i
and
j
are summed over. In the following, we always adopt this convention implicitly. Hence, the time evolution operator

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}

(9)

gives a representation of the real symplectic group
Sp(4,R)
, i.e.
\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
has a one-to-one correspondence to a matrix in the group
Sp(4,R)
, which is given by

\displaystyle U_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}.

(10)

Here,
\mathcal{T}
is the time ordering operator.
This can be seen by noticing that the matrix corresponding to the operator
\hat{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
is

\displaystyle\kappa\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\xi_{\mathbf{k},ij}(t)Y_{j}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}(t)Y_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}^{*}(t)Y^{ij}.

Since the matrix
U_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
is a real symplectic matrix, it has the form
Perelomov 1986

\displaystyle U_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{U}(t)&\mathcal{V}(t)\\
\mathcal{V}(t)^{*}&\mathcal{U}(t)^{*}\end{array}\right),

where
\mathcal{U}(t),\mathcal{V}(t)
are
2\times 2
matrices satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(t)\mathcal{U}(t)^{\dagger}-\mathcal{V}(t)\mathcal{V}(t)^{\dagger}=I,\ \mathcal{U}(t)\mathcal{V}(t)^{T}=\mathcal{V}(t)\mathcal{U}(t)^{T}.

(14)

In order to calculate the time evolution of the fully condensed state, one can decompose
\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
as

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}Z_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}}e^{\zeta_{kl}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{l}^{k}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}},

(15)

which is called normal order decomposition
Perelomov 1986
;
Wang and He 2022
. With this decomposition, the fully condensed state
|\mathbf{0}\rangle
evolves as

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)|\mathbf{0}\rangle=\mathcal{N_{\mathbf{k}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}Z_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}}|\mathbf{0}\rangle\equiv|Z(t)\rangle,

(16)

where
|Z(t)\rangle
is an
Sp(4,R)
coherent state and
\mathcal{N_{\mathbf{k}}}
is normalization factor. At any time
t
of the evolution,
Z(t)
is
Rowe et al. 1985
;
Wang and He 2022

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(t)[\mathcal{U}(t)^{*}]^{-1},

(17)

where
\mathcal{U}(t),\mathcal{V}(t)
is defined in Eq.(
II
). By using the Eq.(
14
), it can be shown that at any time t,
Z(t)
is a
2\times 2
symmetric complex matrix, and
I-Z(t)^{\dagger}Z(t)
is positive definite, where
I
is
2\times 2
identity matrix. Thus, the matrix
Z(t)
can be parameterized by three complex numbers
Z_{11}(t),Z_{12}(t),Z_{22}(t)
, or by six real parameters. Mathematically,
Z(t)
lies in a six-dimensional manifold called Cartan classical domain, and is isomorphic to the quotient space
Sp(4,R)/U(2)

Coquereaux and Jadczyk 1990
;
Perelomov 1986
, where
U(2)
is the unitary group of degree 2. Thus,
Z(t)
gives a trajectory in this six-dimensional manifold. And if the initial state is arbitrary coherent state
|Z_{0}\rangle
, the time evolution of this coherent is also a coherent state, i.e.
\hat{U}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)|Z_{0}\rangle=|Z^{\prime}(t)\rangle
, and
Z^{\prime}(t)
is given by
Wang and He 2022
;
Rowe et al. 1985

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}(t)=[\mathcal{U}(t)Z_{0}+\mathcal{V}(t)][\mathcal{V}(t)^{*}Z_{0}+\mathcal{U}(t)^{*}]^{-1}.

(18)

With this general formalism dealing with the time evolution of two-component BEC in hand, we are ready to study the quantum echo of the two-component BEC system.

III
The quantum echo

In this section, we will study the problem of quantum echo for boson modes in generic
\pm\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{0}
momenta by considering a periodic driving protocol similar to Ref.
Chen et al. 2020
;
Lyu et al. 2020
. In this driving protocol, each period consists of two steps: in the first step, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
is a generic two-component Bogoliubov Hamiltonian defined in Eq.(
8
); in the second step, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{2}
only includes these
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i}
operators, i.e. turning off all the intra- and inter-species interaction strengths
g_{ij}
, as shown in Figure
1
. We will show how to obtain the explicit form of the second-step Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{2}
that make the initial state, either
|\mathbf{0}\rangle
or a generic coherent state
|Z_{0}\rangle
, reverse to itself after two period of driving for any given Bogoliubov Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
.

Schematics for the driving protocol. In the first step of a period, the Hamiltonian can be any Bogoliubov Hamiltonian. However, in the second step of a period, the all the interaction strengths should be turned off.

In the
m
th period, when
(m-1)T<0\leq(m-1)T+t_{1}
, the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}^{*}(t)\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij},

(19)

and when
(m-1)T+t_{1}<t\leq mT

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ij}\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i},

(20)

where
T=t_{1}+t_{2}
is the duration of each period. Here we have omitted the subscript
\mathbf{k}
for simplicity. Thus, for such a quantum echo to occur, we require that the time evolution operators
\hat{U}_{1}(t_{1})
and
\hat{U}_{2}(t_{2})

|Z_{0}\rangle
gives

\displaystyle(\hat{U}_{2}(t_{2})\hat{U}_{1}(t_{1}))^{2}|Z_{0}\rangle=|Z_{0}\rangle.

(21)

According to Eq.(
18

\displaystyle U(T)^{2}=(U_{2}(t_{2})U_{1}(t_{1}))^{2}=\pm 1,

(22)

where

are the time evolution matrix corresponding to
\hat{U_{i}}(t_{i})
. Next we will focus the case
U(T)^{2}=-1
, since the result for
U(T)^{2}=1

from

.

Since

II

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{2}+\mathcal{V}\mathcal{V}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{*2}+\mathcal{V}^{*}\mathcal{V}=-1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{V}\mathcal{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(23)

By using the property of real symplectic matrix
\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}-\mathcal{V}\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}=I,\ \mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}^{T}=\mathcal{V}\mathcal{U}^{T}
, the Eqs.(
III

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=-\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}.

Next, we will construct an explicit form of

. We recall that for the

\displaystyle(e^{i\pi\frac{\sigma_{z}}{2}}e^{i(\alpha_{+}\sigma^{+}-\alpha_{-}\sigma^{-})})^{2}=-1,

, and

. Inspired by this resemblance, we expect that for the

\displaystyle U(T)

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(Y_{1}^{1}+Y_{2}^{2})}e^{\frac{i}{2}(\eta_{ij}Y^{ij}+\eta_{ij}^{*}Y_{ij})}

With the general form of
U(T)
, we can find the

that make the quantum echo occur for arbitrary
\hat{H}_{1}
. Since
\hat{H}_{1}

\eta_{ij}

\eta_{11}=1.25\epsilon_{0},\ \eta_{22}=\epsilon_{0},\ \eta_{12}=0.5\epsilon_{0}

0.21&2.75\end{array}\right)\epsilon_{0}.

Here, we can see that with the interaction strengths turned off, only the kinetic terms and the spin-orbit coupling terms left in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. However, the diagonal terms in
\varepsilon
are no longer equal, which means different chemical potentials for these two species of bosons are required. Meanwhile, the off-diagonal terms are also no longer

V
Interaction effect

In this section, we will consider the interaction effect in the quantum echo process. Similar to the one component BEC
Lyu et al. 2020
, the interaction between these boson modes in two-component BEC can be given as
\hat{V}=\sum_{i,j}(4\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}\hat{n}_{j,-\mathbf{k}}+\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}\hat{n}_{j,\mathbf{k}}+\hat{n}_{i,-\mathbf{k}}\hat{n}_{j,-\mathbf{k}})
. When there are a large amount of boson modes excited in the
\pm\mathbf{k}
momenta, the interaction between these excitations cannot be ignored. As a result, the quantum echo process will be broken. By calculating the particle numbers in the end of
2m
th period, we want to reveal the interplay of the interactions between boson modes and the parameters in the original first-step Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
in this quantum echo breaking process.

For simplicity, we consider the following two-step driving

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{1}+\tilde{g}\hat{V},

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ij}\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i},

(43)

where
\hat{H}_{1}=(E\delta_{ij}+\gamma\eta_{ij})\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{j}^{i}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\eta_{ij}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}^{ij}+\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{ij})
. Here we have assumed the condensate wavefunctions
\psi_{i}=\sqrt{N_{i}/V}
are real, and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Thus, the parameter
E
controls the kinetic term, and the dimensionless parameter
\gamma
controls the hopping between the excitations and the condensate in the zero momentum. The
\hat{H}_{2}
is chosen such that when
\tilde{g}=0
the above driving process will bring the initial state to itself after two period of evolution.

Figure 5:
Particle numbers at the end of the
2T
for each species of boson with (a) varying
\gamma
and (b) varying
E
. For all these cases, the matrix
\eta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1/2&1/6\\
1/6&1/4\end{array}\right)\epsilon_{0}
, where
\epsilon_{0}=2\eta_{11}
serves as an energy unit.

When
\tilde{g}\neq 0
, and
\tilde{g}
is much smaller than the energy scale of
\hat{H}_{1}
, we can treat the interaction term perturbatively. By keeping the Dyson series to the first order, we have

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}^{\prime}t_{1}}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t_{1}}\left[1-i\tilde{g}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\hat{V}(t)dt+O(U^{2})\right],

where
\hat{V}(t)=e^{i\hat{H}_{1}t}\hat{V}e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t}
. Since
\hat{H}_{1}
is a linear combination of the
\mathfrak{sp}(4,R)
Lie algebra, according to Ref.
Perelomov 1986
;
Wang and He 2022
, the operator
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}
evolves as

\displaystyle e^{i\hat{H_{1}}t}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i\hat{H_{1}}t}=e^{-i\kappa\mathcal{H}_{1}t}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}.

(45)

With this transformation,
\hat{V}(t)
can be calculated. Then, the time evolution operator after two period of driving up to the first order in
\tilde{g}
has the following form

\displaystyle\hat{U}(2T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{2}\hat{U}_{1}^{\prime}\hat{U}_{2}\hat{U}_{1}^{\prime}

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[1-i\tilde{g}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt\hat{A}\hat{V}(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-i\tilde{g}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt\hat{A}^{2}\hat{V}(t)(\hat{A}^{\dagger})^{2}+O(\tilde{g}^{2})\right]\hat{A}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[1-i\tilde{g}\hat{\mathscr{V}}+O(\tilde{g}^{2})]\hat{A}^{2},

where we have defined
\hat{A}=e^{-i\hat{H}_{2}t_{2}}e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t_{1}}
, and denoted the integral terms as
\hat{\mathscr{V}}
. Here,
\hat{A}\hat{V}(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{A}^{2}\hat{V}(t)(\hat{A}^{\dagger})^{2}
can also be calculated using the method in Eq.(
45
). As a result, the particle number at the end of the
2m
th period is given by

\displaystyle n_{i,\mathbf{k}}(2mT)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle 0|[\hat{U}^{\dagger}(2T)]^{m}\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}[\hat{U}(2T)]^{m}|0\rangle

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\tilde{g}^{2}\langle 0|\hat{\mathscr{V}}^{\dagger}\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}\hat{\mathscr{V}}|0\rangle+O(\tilde{g}^{3})

where
\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}=a_{i,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{i,\mathbf{k}}
. Here, we have used the relations
\hat{A}^{2}|0\rangle=-|0\rangle
and
\hat{A}^{2}\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}(\hat{A}^{\dagger})^{2}=\hat{n}_{i,\mathbf{k}}
. Then, the expectation value part of above expression can be evaluated numerically. And we can conclude that the to the leading order, the particle numbers increase quadratically with interaction strengths
\tilde{g}
.

In Figure
5
, we show the numerical results for the particle numbers
n_{i,\mathbf{k}}(2T)
at the end of the second driving period varying with the parameters
E
and
\gamma
with
\eta_{ij}
fixed. From Figure
5
(a), we can see that for different fixed values of
E
, the particle numbers increase exponentially with increasing
\gamma
. However, the particle numbers decrease as the parameter
E
increases. This result can be understood as follows:
\gamma
controls the hopping between the excitations and the condensate in zero momentum. When increasing
\gamma
with fixed kinetic term parameter
E
, more particles will be excited. Since the echo-breaking interactions are between the excited particles. Thus, more excited particles will result stronger echo-breaking effect. On the other hand, when increasing
E
with fixed
\gamma
, the ratio
\gamma/E
gets smaller, thus, the particles exciting is suppressed. Hence, the echo-breaking effect is weaker in this case.

VI
Possible experiment implementation

Figure 6:
Schematics for the shaken optical lattice implementation of hopping terms between opposite momenta of two-component BEC Hamiltonian. Red and blue dots represent two species of bosons.

In previous sections, we explored a general driving protocol to facilitate quantum echo in two-component Bose-Einstein Condensates (BECs) and examined the influence of symmetry-breaking interactions on this phenomenon. In this section, we will propose a possible experiment implementation for this quantum echo.

The model Eq.(
II
) involves the hopping between the bosonic modes in opposite momenta
\pm\mathbf{k}
. The similar hopping also appears in the single-component BEC case and is proposed to be implemented in a double well structure in momentum space in Ref.
Lyu et al. 2020
. This kind of double well structure can be implemented in various experimental setups such as shaken optical lattice, spin-orbit coupling and periodic driving
Lyu et al. 2020
. For the two-component case, we propose using a shaken optical lattice
Parker et al. 2013
setup. The experimental procedure would start with preparing a two-component BEC, initially condensing all particles in the zero momentum state. Subsequently, altering the band structure to form a double-well would populate the particles in opposite momenta as shown in Figure
6
. This process corresponds to the hopping described in Equation (
II
) from opposite momenta.

In the second step of our driving protocol, the coupling term between opposite momenta disappear, however, the spin-orbit coupling terms like
a_{i,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}a_{j,\mathbf{k}}
with
i\neq j
are always present. Importantly, the implementation of spin-orbit coupling is also feasible in cold atom experiments
Galitski and Spielman 2013
. Therefore, the driving protocol we propose can be effectively realized within the current frameworks of cold atom experimental setups.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have addressed the question of how quantum echo can be achieved in a two-component Bose-Einstein condensate system using the
Sp(4,R)
group formalism. We have shown that under a periodic driving protocol consisting of two steps in each period, any
Sp(4,R)
coherent state of two-component BEC can reverse to itself after two periods of driving. We have also developed a general method to construct the second-step Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{2}
from the first-step Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
based on the property of real symplectic matrices and applied our method to two examples, one with time-independent
\hat{H}_{1}
and one with time-dependent
\hat{H}_{1}
, then verified our theoretical prediction numerically. Furthermore, we have investigated how the interaction effect breaks the quantum echo process and found that the particle number deviation from the perfect revival is influenced by the ratio between the strengths of kinetic terms and interaction in the first-step Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
.

Our work extends the previous studies on quantum echo in single-component BEC and reveals new features of two-component BEC dynamics. Our work also showcases the usefulness and elegance of the
Sp(4,R)
group formalism for studying quantum systems with real symplectic structures. Since our proof on the occurrence of quantum echo in two-component BEC system is solely based on the symplectic properties of the time evolution matrix, our result can also have potential use in spinor BEC and
N
-component boson system. Meanwhile, given the presence of real symplectic matrices in various areas of physical study
Weedbrook et al. 2012
;
Simon 2000
;
Simon et al. 1988
;
Colas et al. 2022
;
Alhassid et al. 1983
;
Deenen and Quesne 1985
;
Enayati et al. 2023
;
Penna and Richaud 2017
;
Richaud and Penna 2017
;
Charalambous et al. 2020
, we hope that our work will stimulate further research on this topic and inspire new applications of quantum echo in ultracold atom systems and beyond.

Appendix A
The real symplectic group
Sp(2n,R)

In this appendix, we will give a brief introduction to the real symmetric group
Sp(2n,R)
, where
n
is positive integer, and
R
denotes the field of real numbers. When
n=2
, we have the group
Sp(4,R)
. More details can be found in
Hasebe 2020
.

The real symplectic group
Sp(2n,R)
is the set of
2n\times 2n
real matrices preserving a non-singular skew symmetric matrix
\Omega
, i.e. for a real matrix
M\in Sp(2n,R)
, it satisfies

\displaystyle M^{T}\Omega M=\Omega,

(48)

where
M^{T}
is the transpose of
M
. Here, the matrix
\Omega
is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{array}\right),

where
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. Then, one can diagonalize the matrix
\Omega
by a unitary transformation

\displaystyle U\Omega U^{\dagger}=i\kappa,

(52)

where
\kappa
is a diagonal matrix

\displaystyle\kappa=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{array}\right),

and the unitary transformation
U
is given by

\displaystyle U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&-iR\\
R&iR\end{array}\right),

where
R
is
n\times n
matrix

\displaystyle R=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&1&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)

Then, by substituting Eq.(
52
) to Eq.(
48
), we have

\displaystyle(UMU^{\dagger})^{\dagger}\kappa(UMU^{\dagger})=\kappa,

(65)

where we have used the fact that
M
is a real matrix. Thus, by defining
\mathcal{M}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=UMU^{\dagger},

(66)

we have another representation of the
Sp(2n,R)
group. And
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the constraint

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\kappa\mathcal{M}=\kappa.

(67)

By substituting Eq.(
52
) to Eq.(
65
), we can see that the matrix
\mathcal{M}
has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{U}&\mathcal{V}\\
\mathcal{V}^{*}&\mathcal{U}^{*},\end{array}\right)

where
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are
n\times n
matrices. And the constraint Eq.(
67
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}-\mathcal{V}\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}=I_{n},\ \ \mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}^{T}=\mathcal{V}^{T}\mathcal{U}.

(71)

Note that in this representation, the matrix
\mathcal{M}
is no longer real matrix in general. However, this representation is suitable for describing the quantum dynamics of two-component BEC.

Appendix B
The explicit expression of
\mathcal{U}_{0}

In this appendix, we will give the explicit expression of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of the matrix
U_{0}=e^{\frac{i}{2}(\eta_{ij}Y^{ij}+\eta_{ij}^{*}Y_{ij})}
. The explicit form of the matrix generators
\{Y_{ij},Y^{ij},Y_{l}^{k}\}
is
Hasebe 2020

\displaystyle(\kappa Y_{ij})_{ab}=\delta_{a,2+i}\delta_{b,j}+\delta_{a,2+j}\delta_{b,i},

\displaystyle(\kappa Y^{ij})_{ab}=\delta_{a,i}\delta_{b,2+j}+\delta_{a,j}\delta_{b,2+i},

\displaystyle(\kappa Y_{l}^{k})_{ab}=\delta_{a,k}\delta_{b,l}+\delta_{2+k,b}\delta_{2+l,a},

(72)

where
\kappa=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)
. Then, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}Y^{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}^{*}Y_{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\eta\\
-\eta^{*}&0\end{array}\right).

According to Ref.
Wang and He 2022
, its eigenvalues are of the form
\{\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-},-\lambda_{+},-\lambda_{-}\}
, where

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A}^{2})\pm\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A}^{2})^{2}-16\det(\mathcal{A})}}.

(76)

It can be calculated directly by Mathematica that
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0,11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\eta_{11}|^{2}-|\eta_{22}|^{2}}{2(\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2})}[\cos(\lambda_{-})-\cos(\lambda_{+})]+\frac{1}{2}[\cos(\lambda_{-})+\cos(\lambda_{+})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0,12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{11}\eta_{12}^{*}+\eta_{22}^{*}\eta_{12}}{\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}}[\cos(\lambda_{-})-\cos(\lambda_{+})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0,21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{11}^{*}\eta_{12}+\eta_{22}\eta_{12}^{*}}{\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}}[\cos(\lambda_{-})-\cos(\lambda_{+})],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0,22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|\eta_{11}|^{2}-|\eta_{22}|^{2}}{2(\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2})}[\cos(\lambda_{-})-\cos(\lambda_{+})]+\frac{1}{2}[\cos(\lambda_{-})+\cos(\lambda_{+})].

(77)

If
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is Hermitian,
\mathcal{U}_{0,11}
and
\mathcal{U}_{0,22}
should be real, meanwhile
\mathcal{U}_{0,12}=\mathcal{U}_{0,21}^{*}
. These conditions lead to that
\cos(\lambda_{+}),\cos(\lambda_{-})
and
\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}
should be real numbers. On the other hand, from Eq.(
76
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A}^{2})^{2}-16\det(\mathcal{A})}

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4(|\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2})^{2}-16|\eta_{12}^{2}-\eta_{11}\eta_{22}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}^{2}-\eta_{11}\eta_{22}|)(\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2}-2|\eta_{12}^{2}-\eta_{11}\eta_{22}|)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}^{2}-\eta_{11}\eta_{22}|)(\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2}-2|\eta_{12}|^{2}-2|\eta_{11}||\eta_{22}|)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}^{2}-\eta_{11}\eta_{22}|)(\eta_{11}|^{2}-|\eta_{22}|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}
is real. Meanwhile, since
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A}^{2})=-2(|\eta_{11}|^{2}+|\eta_{22}|^{2}+2|\eta_{12}|^{2})
, according to Eq.(
76
), both
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
are imaginary. Thus,
\cos(\lambda_{+})
and
\cos(\lambda_{-})
are real. Hence, the conditions of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
being Hermitian are all satisfied. As a result, we have proven that
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is a
2\times 2
Hermitian matrix.

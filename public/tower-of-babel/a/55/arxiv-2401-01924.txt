On the Hilbert Space of Dyons
Rishi Mouland and David Tong

Abstract
We revisit the construction of the Hilbert space of non-relativistic particles moving in three spatial dimensions. This is given by the space of sections of a line bundle that can in general be topologically non-trivial. Such bundles are classified by a set of integers—one for each pair of particles—and arise physically when we describe the interactions of dyons, particles which carry both electric and magnetic charges. The choice of bundle fixes the representation of the Euclidean group carried by the Hilbert space. These representations are shown to recover the ‘pairwise helicity’ formalism recently discussed in the literature.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we return to an old and very basic question: what is the Hilbert space of
n
particles, each moving on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
? Naively, this seems like the kind of question that was answered in our first course on quantum mechanics. However, as is well known, there can be a twist.

The twist comes because wavefunctions in quantum mechanics are not functions on configuration space. Instead, they are sections of line bundles over configuration space. And, for particles moving in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, these line bundles can have interesting topology. It turns out that these topologically non-trivial bundles arise when the particles in question carry both electric and magnetic charges. In other words, they arise for dyons.

Our interest in this problem was motivated by a series of papers by Csaki et. al.
csaba1
;
csaba2
, following earlier work of Zwanziger
zwan
;
zwan0
. They argued that the usual description of multi-particle Fock spaces as the tensor product of single particle Hilbert spaces needs some refinement when the particles in question are dyons. This refinement comes in the form of what these authors called “pairwise helicity”, an extra phase that dyonic particles pick up under a rotation
R\in SU(2)
. Specifically, the rotation acts on elements of the Fock space, with elements labelled by the momenta
{\bf p}_{i}
, as

\displaystyle|{\bf p}_{1},\ldots,{\bf p}_{n}\rangle\rightarrow e^{-i\varphi}|{\bf p}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\bf p}^{\prime}_{n}\rangle\ ,

(1.1)

where
{\bf p}_{i}^{\prime}=R{\bf p}_{i}
. There is a great deal of redundancy in the phase
\varphi
because there’s no canonical way to compare the phases of states that correspond to different rays in the Hilbert space. Indeed, the sole invariant information arises when the
\mathbf{p}_{i}
are colinear and we consider a rotation with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbf{p}_{i}
: then the phase
\varphi
is physical.

In general, the phase
e^{-i\varphi}
is a product over pairs of particles that takes the form

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{i<j}^{n}(e_{i}g_{j}-e_{j}g_{i})\varphi_{ij}\ .

(1.2)

Here
\varphi_{ij}
is a somewhat cumbersome (and largely gauge dependent!) function of the rotation
R
and the direction of
{\bf p}_{i}-{\bf p}_{j}
. Importantly, the proportionality factor is the duality invariant product of the electric charges
e_{i}
and magnetic charges
g_{i}
of the two particles.

For
n=2
particles, the existence of the phase follows in a reasonably straightforward way by looking at the profiles of the electric and magnetic fields and seeing how they transform under the little group that leaves the relative momentum
{\bf p}_{1}-{\bf p}_{2}
unchanged.
This is seen most clearly in the work of Wu and Yang
wuyang
which we review later. For
n\geq 3
particles, however, the calculation is less direct. It was intuited by Zwanziger as an obvious generalisation of the two-particle case, and subsequently derived by studying a kind of doubled field theory in which both electric and magnetic potentials are introduced before projection onto a particular duality frame
zwan0
. (See, also,
brown1
;
brown2
;
yale
for further commentary on these ideas.)

The purpose of this paper is to provide a clean derivation of the pairwise helicity formula (
1.1
). We do this by looking not at the Fock space of relativistic field theories, but instead at non-relativistic quantum mechanics. We will show that the additional phase is a direct consequence of topology. This arises because the wavefunctions that describe dyons are not functions, but sections of a line bundle that may be topologically non-trivial. We start just with a collection of
n
non-relativistic particles and show that the formula (
1.1
) naturally arises as the most general transformation under a rotation. In addition, we will also derive an expression for a lower bound on the relative angular momentum carried by a collection of dyons.

2
The Classical Theory

We start by describing a simple, classical set-up. We will be interested in the dynamics of
n
non-relativistic particles, each moving in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Crucially, we impose one condition: the particles cannot sit on top of each other.

This means that the configuration space is not
\mathbb{R}^{3n}
, as one might naively think, but is instead what mathematicians refer to as
the
configuration space
{\rm Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and what we will denote as
{\cal M}_{n}
. If each particle has position
{\bf x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, with
i=1,\ldots,n
, then the configuration space is spanned by the coordinates

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}=\Big\{{\bf x}_{i}\ :\ {\bf x}_{i}\neq{\bf x}_{j}\ {\rm for}\ i\neq j\Big\}\ .

(2.3)

Importantly, coincident points are excised.

Our particles will interact with some fixed, background
U(1)
connection. This requires some explanation because it is deceptively familiar. In electromagnetism, we’re used to thinking of the magnetic field as a background connection over space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Each particle then experiences the same Lorentz force law, with the strength determined by the particle’s electric charge. Here, instead, we will consider connections over the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. It’s not immediately obvious how to interpret this physically, but we will soon learn how these connections relate to things we know from electromagnetism.

It is at this point that we find the novelty that underlies much of this paper: a
U(1)
principal bundle over
{\cal M}_{n}
can be topologically non-trivial. The classification of such bundles was found in
cohen
(see also
dmann
), following
arnold
;
cohen2
, and is given by

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{n(n-1)/2}\ .

(2.4)

The fact that we need
{n\choose 2}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}n(n-1)
integers to specify a bundle is interesting. It is highly suggestive that these integers can be associated to pairs of particles. Later, when we construct these bundles explicitly, we will see that this is indeed the case and, in doing so, re-derive (
2.4
). For now, we take this as given and write the
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}n(n-1)
integers as

\displaystyle w_{ij}=-w_{ji}\ \ \ {\rm with}\ \ \ i,j=1,\ldots,n\ .

(2.5)

The first question that we would like to answer is: what is the physical interpretation of these integers?

2.1
The Classical Action

To understand the physics, it’s useful to first write down a classical action describing the motion of the
n
particles. We introduce the coordinates
x^{a}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and the metric
g_{ab}
, with
a,b=1,\ldots,3n
. If we introduce the
U(1)
gauge field
A_{a}
, then it’s tempting to write the action

\displaystyle S=\int dt\ \left(\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\dot{x}^{a}\dot{x}^{b}+kA_{a}(x)\dot{x}^{a}\right)\ ,

(2.6)

with
k
a parameter that is something akin to electric charge. This is fine when the
U(1)
bundle is topologically trivial, or when the action is restricted to a given patch of a topologically non-trivial bundle. For many issues, this will be sufficient. However, there will be times when we want to be more careful and work with an action that holds everywhere on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, even when
w_{ij}\neq 0
. For this, we need a minor modification.

We should really think of the target space of our theory as a
U(1)
principal bundle,

\displaystyle U(1)\hookrightarrow P\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{M}_{n}\ .

(2.7)

We will shortly see that such bundles are parameterised by the integers
w_{ij}
. We also choose a connection
\omega
, which is a 1-form on
P
. In some patch with coordinates
(x^{a},e^{i\sigma})
, it takes the form

\displaystyle\omega=d\sigma+A\ ,

(2.8)

where
A=A_{a}(x)dx^{a}
is the locally defined 1-form on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
that appeared in our action (
2.6
). The key point is that while
A
is defined only locally, the 1-form
\omega
is globally defined. This allows us to write down an action that holds everywhere.

This action should be thought of as a function of a curve
\bar{\gamma}
in
P
. Let
\gamma=\pi\circ\bar{\gamma}
be the projection of this curve down to
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and let
\dot{x}^{a}
be the tangent to this curve with respect to a parameter
t
. Then a better version of (
2.6
) is

\displaystyle S[\bar{\gamma}]=\int_{\gamma}dt\ \frac{1}{2}g_{ab}(x)\dot{x}^{a}\dot{x}^{b}+k\int_{\bar{\gamma}}\omega\ .

(2.9)

This action was previously considered in some detail by Friedman and Sorkin
sorkin
. If
\gamma
lies in some patch, then we have

\displaystyle S[\bar{\gamma}]=\int_{\gamma}dt\ \left(\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}(x)\dot{x}^{a}\dot{x}^{b}+k(\dot{\sigma}+A_{a}\dot{x}^{a})\right)\ .

(2.10)

If we drop the total derivative
\dot{\sigma}
then we get back to our previous, naive action (
2.6
) and it looks like this was a rather pedantic, pointless excursion. But, as we will see later, there will be occasions when we want to move between patches when the action (
2.10
) will prove useful.

There is a second, closely related, classical action which describes motion on the so-called
bundle metric
. We describe this in Appendix
A
.

2.2
Topology and Dyons

Now we can address our question: what is the physical meaning of the integers
w_{ij}
that characterise the topology of the bundle? This is simplest to see if we first look at two particles for which the configuration space takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\})\ .

(2.11)

Here the first factor describes the centre of mass, while the second factor is parameterised by the relative position,
{\bf r}={\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}
. The interesting topology sits in the second factor and arises because we have excised the origin.

For our immediate purposes, we can get away with the cheap version of the action (
2.6
). Ignoring the centre of mass motion, the relative motion is described by the action,

\displaystyle S_{2}=\int dt\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{{\bf r}}\cdot\dot{{\bf r}}+\dot{{\bf r}}\cdot{\bf A}({\bf r})\right)\ .

(2.12)

We have set the parameter
k=1
in (
2.6
). (It doesn’t really play much of a role in the story; just rescaling other charges by a constant amount. We will later reinstate it when we come to the quantum theory.) The topology in the connection means that the associated magnetic field
{\bf B}=\nabla\times{\bf A}
has first Chern class

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\bf S}^{2}_{\infty}}d{\bf S}\cdot{\bf B}=w\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ .

(2.13)

This, of course, is a magnetic monopole. But it’s a monopole in the relative position, which means that one particle is electrically charged and the other magnetically charged. We have no way to determine which is which based on the information above; it is just the relative charges that are captured by the topology. As shown by Friedman and Sorkin
sorkin
by studying the equations of motion arising from the action, this relative charge is identified as the duality invariant combination

\displaystyle w=\frac{1}{4\pi}(e_{1}g_{2}-g_{1}e_{2})\ \in{\mathbb{Z}}\ ,

(2.14)

where
e_{i}
and
g_{i}
are the electric and magnetic charges of the
i^{\rm th}
particle.

What about the case of general
n
? Once again, the equations of motion make evident that the bundle characterised by the winding numbers
w_{ij}
describes dyons with relative mutual charges

\displaystyle w_{ij}=\frac{1}{4\pi}(e_{i}g_{j}-g_{i}e_{j})\ .

(2.15)

However, this can’t be the full story: there are only
2n
electric and magnetic charges, while there are
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}n(n-1)
different winding numbers
w_{ij}
. For example, with
n=4
particles, the condition (
2.15
) implies that
w_{12}w_{34}-w_{13}w_{24}+w_{14}w_{23}=0
. But it is certainly possible to construct sensible bundles that do not obey this constraint (and we will do so later). This means that the action (
2.6
) must describe something more than particles carrying an electric and magnetic charge.

In fact, the right physics does not need that much more. In general, the action (
2.6
) (or its improved version (
2.10
)) describes particles with magnetic and electric charges under a
U(1)^{r}
. The winding numbers have the interpretation

\displaystyle w_{ij}=\frac{1}{4\pi}(\vec{e}_{i}\cdot\vec{g}_{j}-\vec{g}_{i}\cdot\vec{e}_{j})\ ,

(2.16)

where each
\vec{e}_{i}
and
\vec{g}_{i}
is now an
r
-vector. There is always some choice of
r
for which such vectors can be found for any choice of
w_{ij}
. (On general grounds, one expects that
r
should scale as
n
.)

There is something a little surprising about this. We started by introducing a single
U(1)
background gauge field over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and yet find that it describes the Lorentz force law between dyons carrying charges in some larger
U(1)^{r}
.

Note also that the winding numbers
w_{ij}
are duality invariant and the
U(1)
connection over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
should not be thought of as magnetic, or electric: it too is duality invariant. But the action (
2.6
) includes only the Lorentz force law, and not the Coulomb repulsion which must be put in separately. That is not surprising: the Lorentz force law and Coulomb law are related only by relativistic physics and we are firmly in the non-relativistic realm. If we were to add a Coulomb term, we would break the duality symmetry and specify the individual charges
\vec{e}_{i}
and
\vec{g}_{i}
, rather than just the combinations
w_{ij}
.

2.3
Symmetries

We would like to understand how symmetries of our theory act, with particular focus on rotations. The base configuration space
{\cal M}_{n}
admits an
SO(3)
rotational symmetry, generated by the vector fields

\displaystyle\xi^{\alpha}=-i\sum_{i}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\gamma}}\ .

(2.17)

We then ask: when and how does this give rise to a symmetry of the theory defined by the action (
2.9
)? This is the kind of question previously addressed by Jackiw and Manton
jackiw
. The key takeaway will be that as we rotate in the base, we must also move in the fibre.

The first task is to lift the
SO(3)
action to an action on the full bundle
P
. This will generically result in an action of the double cover
SU(2)
. A powerful result of Palais and Stewart
stewart
;
palais
ensures that such a lift exists, and furthermore that it is unique up to conjugation by a gauge transformation.

The first term in our action (
2.9
) is then invariant under this lifted group action, so long as the metric is rotationally-symmetric,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{\alpha}}g=0\ .

(2.18)

Indeed, if our particles move on flat
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, then the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
inherits a natural rotationally invariant metric. That leaves us with the second term in (
2.9
), involving the connection on the bundle. We require that the
SU(2)
action preserves our chosen connection; this can be ensured if and only if the field strength
F=dA
, which is a globally defined on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, is rotationally-symmetric,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{\alpha}}F=0\ .

In this case, the lift of the
SU(2)
action to the full bundle is unique and is generated by the vector fields

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}^{\alpha}=\xi^{\alpha}_{H}-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}F(\xi^{\beta},\xi^{\gamma})\eta\ .

(2.19)

There is some notation to unpack here. The vector field
\eta
on
P
is locally given by
\eta=\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}
, meaning that it moves us along the fibre of
P
. It is normalised so that
\omega(\eta)=1
. Meanwhile
\xi_{H}
is the horizontal lift of the original generators
\xi
, which locally takes the form

\displaystyle\xi_{H}=\xi^{a}\partial_{a}-(\xi^{a}A_{a})\eta\ .

(2.20)

This horizontal lift is the naive way of lifting a vector field from the base the full bundle; we will later see that this gives rise to the usual covariant derivative when we quantise. What’s more interesting is the extra term in (
2.19
) which, in coordinates, reads
F(\xi^{\beta},\xi^{\gamma})=F_{ab}\xi^{\beta\,a}\xi^{\gamma\,b}
. This is what will ultimately result in the additional phase in the wavefunction (
1.1
).

It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\xi}^{\alpha}}\omega=0\ ,

(2.21)

as required, and that

\displaystyle[\tilde{\xi}^{\alpha},\tilde{\xi}^{\beta}]

\displaystyle=i\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\tilde{\xi}^{\gamma}\ .

(2.22)

The additional term, involving the field strength
F
is (
2.19
) is needed for both results to hold.

2.4
Constructing the Bundles by Pullback

We would next like to construct the principal
U(1)
bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. In doing so, we will provide an alternative way to classify the bundles, providing an independent derivation of the result (
2.4
).

The case of two particles is straightforward. The base
\mathcal{M}_{2}
takes the simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times S^{2}\ .

(2.23)

The
U(1)
principal bundles
P_{2}[w]
over
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are well-known and are classified by a single integer
w_{12}=w\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Roughly speaking, this counts the number of times that the
U(1)\cong S^{1}
winds as we move around
S^{2}
. More precisely, up to an isomorphism these bundles always take the form

\displaystyle P_{2}[w]=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times B[w]\ .

(2.24)

Here
B[w]
is the bundle

\displaystyle U(1)\hookrightarrow B[w]\to S^{2}\ ,

(2.25)

with first Chern class
w\in H^{2}(S^{2},{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
. This integer completely specifies the bundle. In particular, the
w=0
bundle is trivial,
B[0]\cong S^{1}\times S^{2}
, while otherwise we have the lens space
B[w]\cong S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{|w|}
with orientation fixed by the sign of
w
.

The situation for
n\geq 3
particles is less tractable. The issue is that the base
\mathcal{M}_{n}
does not admit a nice factorisation of the form (
2.23
) for
n\geq 3
, and so we can’t arrive so immediately at the possible
U(1)
bundles over it.

At this stage, we use a trick. This is the trick that will ultimately allow us to arrive at the key result (
1.1
) for pairwise helicity.
The idea is to work not with the configuration space
{\cal M}_{n}
, but instead on a bigger space
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times\left({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\ .

(2.26)

The coordinates on this bigger space are the centre of mass
{\bf X}
, together with the separations
{\bf r}_{ij}
, with
i,j=1,\ldots,n
and
i<j
, between any pair of particles. Importantly, however, we don’t impose any constraint on these separations so, for example,
{\bf r}_{12}
and
{\bf r}_{23}
and
{\bf r}_{13}
are all independent coordinates on
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, while they are clearly related in the original configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

There is a natural embedding of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
into
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, defined simply by mapping a point with coordinates
{\bf x}_{i}
in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
to the point with coordinates
{\bf X}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}{\bf x}_{i}
and
{\bf r}_{ij}={\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{j}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. We write this map as

\displaystyle\phi:\mathcal{M}_{n}\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\ .

(2.27)

Any
U(1)
bundle over
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
can be pulled back by
\phi
to define a bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Furthermore, a connection on this bigger bundle pulls back to a connection on the bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Because
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
has a simple factorisation (
2.26
), the bundles
\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
over
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
are easy to classify; they take the form

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{n}[w]={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times\left(\bigtimes_{i<j}\Big({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\times B[w_{ij}]\Big)\right)\ .

(2.28)

These bundles are fully classified by a set of integers
w_{ij}=-w_{ji}
, since
H^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}^{n(n-1)/2}
. Given a connection, each integer specifies the flux of
F
through a 2-cycle that wraps the excised origin in the
\mathbf{r}_{ij}
plane.

We then define the bundle
U(1)\hookrightarrow P_{n}[w]\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
over our original configuration space by the pullback
P_{n}[w]=\phi^{*}\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
. For two particles, we just have
P_{2}[w]\cong\widehat{P}_{2}[w]
, and we trivially recover the above construction.

All we have done thus far is provided a tool by which we can explicitly construct
U(1)
bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. But that’s a far cry from our initial claim that this construction allows us to classify all such bundles. There are two things that could go wrong. First, even though the bundles
\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
are topologically inequivalent, we do not know whether their pullbacks
P_{n}[w]=\phi^{*}\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
are topologically inequivalent. Secondly, there could be further topologically inequivalent bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
which do not arise as the pullback of some bundle over
\widehat{M}_{n}
.

It turns out that neither of these issues actually arise: the isomorphism classes of bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
are precisely labelled by integers
w_{ij}=-w_{ji}
, with the bundles
P_{n}[w]=\phi^{*}\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
providing a full set of topologically-inequivalent representatives. We relegate a proof of this fact to Appendix
B
, thus providing an independent derivation of (
2.4
).

Symmetric Connections

This pullback construction also provides a way to write down symmetric connections on the bundle
P[w]
over configuration space.
Again, the case of two particles is straightforward. We have a connection of uniform curvature on the bundle
B[w]
with
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}F=w
. This is a familiar magnetic monopole field strength, now in the space parameterised by the relative position of the two particles,

\displaystyle F=\frac{w}{4}\frac{\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\mathbf{x}_{12}^{\gamma}}{|\mathbf{x}_{12}|^{3}}d\mathbf{x}_{12}^{\alpha}\wedge d\mathbf{x}_{12}^{\beta}\ ,

(2.29)

where
\mathbf{x}_{12}=\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}
. Wu and Yang
wuyangfirstpaper
showed that there is a two-patch open covering of
P_{2}[w]
such that the gauge field in each patch takes the familiar form,

\displaystyle A_{N}=-\frac{1}{2}w(\cos\theta-1)d\phi\ ,\qquad A_{S}=-\frac{1}{2}w(\cos\theta+1)d\phi\ ,

(2.30)

where
(\theta,\phi)
are spherical polar coordinates associated to the 3-vector
\mathbf{x}_{12}
.

Once again using the bigger bundles
\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
, this construction generalises immediately to multiple particles. We first fix the a curvature on
\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
, which takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{F}=\sum_{i<j}F^{(ij)}\ ,

(2.31)

where
\hat{F}^{(ij)}
is a curvature in
B[w_{ij}]
with
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S_{(ij)}^{2}}\hat{F}^{(ij)}=w_{ij}
. Given such a connection
\hat{\omega}
on
\widehat{P}_{n}
, we can then simply pullback to define a connection
\omega=\phi^{*}\hat{\omega}
on
P_{n}[w]
, whose curvature
F
by construction has the desired symmetry properties. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle F=\frac{1}{4}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_{ij}\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{\gamma}}{|\mathbf{x}_{ij}|^{3}}d\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{\alpha}\wedge d\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{\beta}\ ,

(2.32)

where, since we have pulled back to
P_{n}[w]
, the
\mathbf{x}_{ij}
are not independent functions on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
when
n\geq 3
. The gauge field can be written in a form analogous to (
2.30
) in the various patches on
P_{n}[w]
.

With this connection in hand, we can be a little more explicit in the expression (
2.19
). The all-important additional term in the generator for rotational symmetries is proportional to

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}F(\xi^{\beta},\xi^{\gamma})=-\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{\alpha}}{|\mathbf{x}_{ij}|}\ .

(2.33)

This, ultimately, will translate into the phase picked up by the wavefunction under rotations, as we now show.

3
The Quantum Theory

We now turn to the quantum theory. Our goal is to construct the Hilbert space of the theory and determine the representation of the Euclidean group that it carries.

3.1
Basic Quantisation

We work with quantum mechanics on the bundle
P_{n}[w]
where, recall, the winding of the
U(1)
fibre over the base configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is specified by the
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}n(n-1)
integers
w_{ij}
. The action is given by (
2.10
)

\displaystyle S[\bar{\gamma}]=\int_{\gamma}dt\ \left(\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}(x)\dot{x}^{a}\dot{x}^{b}+k(\dot{\sigma}+A_{a}\dot{x}^{a})\right)\ .

(3.34)

Upon quantisation, the constraint
\pi_{\sigma}=\partial L/\partial\dot{\sigma}=k
imposes a physical state condition. The physical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}[w]
is thus identified as the space of sections of a complex line bundle of charge
k
associated to
P_{n}[w]
. Our connection on
P_{n}[w]
then induces a covariant derivative on
\mathcal{H}_{n}[w]
.

For two particles, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{2}[w]
is straightforward. By the decomposition (
2.24
), we can write

It’s Just a Phase We’re Going Through

3.3
Momentum Eigenstates

Our ultimate goal is to make contact with the pairwise helicity formula (
1.1
). For this, we need a set of momentum eigenstates. Concretely, we would like a set of states
\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}[w]
that are eigenstates of the total momentum operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}=(\mathbf{p}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{p}_{n})\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}\ ,

(3.45)

and which furnish a representation of
SU(2)
,

\displaystyle R\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}=D_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(R)\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}^{\prime}}\quad{\rm with}\quad\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\prime}=R\mathbf{p}_{i}\quad{\rm and}\quad R=e^{i\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{J}}\ ,

(3.46)

for a phase
D_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(R)
that we must determine. Note, the transformation of the individual labels
\mathbf{p}_{i}
is
not
implied by the Euclidean algebra obeyed by
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{J}

(\mathbf{p}_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+\mathbf{p}_{n}^{\prime})=R(\mathbf{p}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{p}_{n})

In the topologically-trivial case

, we’re done: we just write down

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n})=e^{i\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1}}\dots e^{i\mathbf{p}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{n}}\ .

(3.47)

In particular,
\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}
is a simultaneous eigenstate of the single-particle momentum operators
-i\partial/\partial\mathbf{x}_{i}
.
The situation is very different when some
w_{ij}\neq 0
. Now, the gauge covariant single-particle momentum operators

\displaystyle-i\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}_{i}}-ik\mathbf{A}_{i}\right)\ ,

(3.48)

do not mutually commute
3
3

3

One might try to deform these operators with additional terms to make them commute. However, whenever some
w_{ij}\neq 0
, no such globally-defined term exists.
due to the necessarily non-zero curvature of

are just labels, not quantum numbers. Nonetheless, it is a well-posed, non-trivial problem to find states

satisfying (
3.45
) and (
3.46

We will now construct such states

. These will be sections of a non-trivial bundle and, as such, we will be forced to think about patches and all that. However, we can already glean the most important information contained in (
3.46

\mathbf{p}_{i}

\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}

and we have
(-\mathds{1}_{2})\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}=(-1)^{F}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}
with
F
given in (
3.44

\mathbf{p}_{i}
lie along an axis through the origin, the state picks up a physical phase under rotations about this axis

4

4

Once again, we can phrase this more precisely in representation theoretic language. The states in the orbit of a given state
\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}
span an induced representation of
SU(2)
which is determined entirely by the charge of
\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}
under its stabiliser subgroup. Generically this is

\mathbf{p}_{i}

.
. These phases are however now a little more fiddly to determine.

The key fact we need, and which we will shortly show, is that it is possible

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{y}}\cdot\mathbf{J}=-i\hat{\mathbf{y}}\cdot\sum_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}_{i}}-\frac{k}{2}\sum_{i<j}w_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}\ .

Next, for

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n})=e^{i\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1}}\dots e^{i\mathbf{p}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{n}}\ ,

(3.50)

\displaystyle R_{\phi}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n})=\exp\left(-\frac{ik\phi}{2}\sum_{i<j}w_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}\right)\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n})\ ,

3.4
Generic Rotations and Pairwise Helicity

To start, we will look at how states in the
n=2
particle Hilbert space transform under generic rotations. All non-triviality lies in the relative part
\mathcal{H}_{\text{rel}}
. Given some
\psi_{\text{rel}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{rel}}[w]
, and some
R\in SU(2)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(R\psi_{\text{rel}}\right)(\mathbf{x}_{12})\propto\psi_{\text{rel}}(R^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{12})\ .

(3.52)

Let’s pick, without loss of generality, the unit vector
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=(0,0,1)
. Then, we can implicitly use the
SU(2)
action to fix the phase of
\psi_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf{x}_{12})
in terms of that of
\psi_{\text{rel}}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|\hat{\mathbf{z}})
. This is the procedure: given any
\hat{\mathbf{r}}\in S^{2}
, we choose a canonical rotation
R_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})\in SU(2)
such that
R_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}
. Then, our gauge in a patch containing
|\mathbf{x}_{12}|\hat{\mathbf{z}}
can be entirely fixed by requiring that states satisfy

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf{x}_{12})=\left(U(R_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{12}))^{-1}\psi_{\text{rel}}\right)(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|\hat{\mathbf{z}})\ ,

(3.53)

where, here and throughout, we use the notation
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{12}=\mathbf{x}_{12}/|\mathbf{x}_{12}|\in S^{2}
for the direction of a 3-vector. (There is a subtlety in making this choice of gauge that we will comment on below.)

Having fixed this gauge, and after putting back in the centre-of-mass part, we arrive at the transformation of a generic state
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{2}[w]
in the 2-particle Hilbert space under some generic
R\in SU(2)
,

\displaystyle(R\psi)(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=\exp\left(-\frac{ikw}{2}\theta(R,R^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{ij})\right)\psi(R^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{1},R^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{2})\ .

(3.54)

Here,
\theta(R,\hat{\mathbf{r}})\in[0,4\pi)
is defined for all
R\in SU(2)
and
\hat{\mathbf{r}}\in S^{2}
as the unique angle such that

\displaystyle R_{\star}(R\hat{\mathbf{r}})^{-1}RR_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})=\exp\left(i\theta(R,\hat{\mathbf{r}})T^{3}\right)\ ,

(3.55)

where here
T^{3}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{3}
is the relevant
SU(2)
generator. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\theta(R_{2},\hat{\mathbf{r}})+\theta(R_{1},R_{2}\hat{\mathbf{r}})=\theta(R_{1}R_{2},\hat{\mathbf{r}})\quad\text{mod }4\pi\ ,

(3.56)

which ensures that (
3.54
) does indeed define a representation of
SU(2)
. In the special case that
R_{\phi}
is a rotation by
\phi
that preserves
\hat{\mathbf{r}}
, i.e.
R_{\phi}\hat{\mathbf{r}}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}
, we have
\theta(R_{\phi},\hat{\mathbf{r}})=\phi
. Another important special case is when
R
lies in the centre of
SU(2)
, for which we have
\theta(-\mathds{1}_{2},\hat{\mathbf{r}})=2\pi
.

Note, there is an ambiguity in how we choose
R_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})
. But it is straightforward to show that precisely this ambiguity can always be absorbed into a gauge transformation. This is simply a manifestation of the basic principle of induced representations.

Let us now construct the desired momentum eigenstates. The key observation is that we can always choose
R_{\star}
to be azimuthally-symmetric, which in turn ensures that
\theta(e^{i\phi T^{3}},\hat{\mathbf{r}})=\phi
for any
\hat{\mathbf{r}}
. That is to say, under a rotation by
\phi
about
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
, the wavefunction
\psi
just picks up a phase
e^{-ikw\phi/2}
. By noting the arbitrariness of
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
, and expanding this statement infinitesimally, we arrive precisely at the result (
3.49
) for 2 particles.

With this fact in hand, let us fix for any
\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}
such that
\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}=\alpha\hat{\mathbf{z}}
with
\alpha\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=e^{i\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1}}e^{i\mathbf{p}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2}}\ ,

(3.57)

in our patch. These states are then eigenstates of
J_{3}
,

\displaystyle J_{3}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}=-\frac{kw}{2}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}\ .

(3.58)

We can then simply define
\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}
at generic
\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}
by a transformation of these reference states by an appropriate canonical rotation
R_{\star}
. When the dust settles, we end up with the states

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=e^{i\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1}}e^{i\mathbf{p}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2}}\exp\left(\frac{ikw}{2}\theta(R_{\star}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{12})^{-1},\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{12})\right)\ .

(3.59)

As required, they satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=(\mathbf{p}_{1}+\mathbf{p}_{2})\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})\ .

(3.60)

Then, under a rotation
R\in SU(2)
, they transform as

\displaystyle(R\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}})(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=\exp\left(-\frac{ikw}{2}\theta(R,\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{12})\right)\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}),\quad\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\prime}=R\mathbf{p}_{i}\ .

(3.61)

In particular, if
\mathbf{p}_{i}=\alpha_{i}\hat{\mathbf{y}}
with
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
, then under a rotation
R_{\phi}
by
\phi
about
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
, we have

\displaystyle(R_{\phi}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}})(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=e^{-ikw\phi/2}\psi_{\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})\ .

(3.62)

Global expressions

Let us make a comment about patch-wise versus global expressions. There is a subtlety in (
3.53
) which is in fact vital. The issue is that it is not possible to choose the canonical rotation

smoothly over

This discontinuity in

3.59

The theory (
2.9
) has a yet larger symmetry: we are free to rotate the centre-of-mass coordinate
\mathbf{X}
independently from the relative coordinates
\mathbf{x}_{ij}
. In detail, we have the splitting

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{J}_{\text{com}}+\mathbf{J}_{\text{rel}}\ ,

(3.67)

where, when thought of as an operator on the bigger Hilbert space (
3.37
),
\mathbf{J}_{\text{com}}
acts only on the first factor while
\mathbf{J}_{\text{rel}}
acts only on all the rest, and so
[\mathbf{J}_{\text{com}},\mathbf{J}_{\text{rel}}]=0
. Furthermore, both
\mathbf{J}_{\text{com}}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\text{rel}}
survive the restriction to
\mathcal{H}_{n}[w]
. Then, each of
\mathbf{J}_{\text{com}}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\text{rel}}
generate their own
SU(2)
symmetry of the theory, of which our usual rotational symmetry is the diagonal subgroup.

We can then consider organising states into irreducible representations of
SU(2)_{\text{rel}}
. The possible spins of such representations are
j=j_{\text{min}},j_{\text{min}}+1,\dots
. Here,
j_{\text{min}}\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
is the spin of the lowest spin representation appearing in the tensor product
\otimes_{i<j}(\frac{1}{2}|kw_{ij}|)
of
SU(2)
representations of spins
\frac{1}{2}|kw_{ij}|\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
. In particular,
j_{\text{min}}
is (half-)integer if
k\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}
is (odd) even.

We have learnt that a composite of
n
particles carries half-integer spin whenever
k\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}
is odd. It is natural then to ask what happens when two identical such composites interact, and in particular, are exchanged. Precisely such a scenario was considered in
sorkinsecondpaper
, where it was argued that composites with half-integer spin do indeed realise fermionic statistics (see also
goldhaber
;
csaba3
).

3.6
A Comment on Other Dimensions

It is interesting to note that three spatial dimensions are very special. In any other dimension,
H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\mathbb{Z})
is trivial, and so there is only one (trivial) bundle. Then, in all but two spatial dimensions, the action of the Euclidean group on the Hilbert space is essentially fixed, and is boring. In the language of
csaba1
;
csaba2
, the only one-dimensional irreducible representation of the pairwise little group is the trivial one.

In two spatial dimensions, the non-simply-connectedness of the rotation group
U(1)
gives us more to play with. Although there is only a single, trivial bundle, the result of
palais
;
stewart
no longer applies, and there is a continuous family of lifts of the rotation group to a projective representation on this bundle, parameterised by an angle
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. The resulting Hilbert space is that of a system of Abelian anyons.

To see more structure—and anything non-trivial away from two and three spatial dimensions—we need to consider higher rank bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, corresponding to particles carrying their own internal degrees of freedom. It turns out that the task of classifying such bundles is significantly more involved, and indeed is an open question in general
dmann
. Furthermore, even with such a bundle in hand, there may be many lifts of the symmetries of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. It would be interesting to resolve each of these open questions, and thus derive a full classification of multi-particle Hilbert spaces.

Appendix A
The Bundle Metric Action

In section
2.1
, we described the classical action for a particle moving on the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, in the presence of a background connection. There is a different, albeit ultimately equivalent, description of the physics that it is useful to have in mind.

Given any principal bundle with connection over a Riemannian manifold, the metric on the base buddies up with the connection on the bundle to define a metric on the whole bundle. Globally, this metric is

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(X,Y)=\pi^{*}g(X,Y)+\alpha\omega(X)\,\omega(Y)\ .

Here the free variable
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
reflects our one-parameter choice of metric on the fibre. In the local coordinates defined in section
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\tilde{g}_{AB}dX^{A}dX^{B}=g_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}+\alpha\,(d\sigma+A_{a}dx^{a})^{2}\ .

(A.68)

This is the kind of metric that is familiar from Kaluza-Klein theory. We then consider the quadratic action

\displaystyle S^{\prime}[\bar{\gamma}]=\int_{\bar{\gamma}}dt\ \frac{1}{2}g_{AB}\dot{X}^{A}\dot{X}^{B}=\int_{\bar{\gamma}}dt\ \frac{1}{2}\left(g_{ab}(x)\dot{x}^{a}\dot{x}^{b}+\alpha(\dot{\sigma}+A_{a}\dot{x}^{a})^{2}\right)\ .

(A.69)

This differs from the action (
2.10
) because it has a quadratic, as opposed to linear, kinetic term for the fibre coordinate
\sigma
. At first glance that seems very different: the actions (
2.10
) and (
A.69
) describe systems with different numbers of degrees of freedom. The relationship between them arises when we realise that the latter action has an additional conserved charge

\displaystyle Q=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\sigma}}=\alpha(\dot{\sigma}+A_{a}\dot{x}^{a})\ .

(A.70)

For a fixed value of
Q
, the equations of motion of (
A.69
) coincides with those derived from our earlier action
2.10
, provided that we identify
Q=k
. The extra degree of freedom of (
A.69
) is simply that it includes solutions with different values of
Q
. Classically, we can have
Q\in{\mathbb{R}}
but, quantum mechanically, we must have
Q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The action (
A.69
) makes the
SU(2)
action described in section
2.3
particularly transparent: the connection is
SU(2)
invariant only if the metric (
A.68
) has an
SU(2)
isometry.

Appendix B
Proof of Bundle Construction

We would like to show that the bundles
U(1)\hookrightarrow P_{n}[w]\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
, defined for each set of
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
integers
w_{ij}=-w_{ji}
by the pullback
P_{n}[w]=\phi^{*}\widehat{P}_{n}[w]
, provide a full and minimal set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of
U(1)
principal bundles over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. That is, we claim that each of the
P_{n}[w]
is topologically inequivalent from every other, and furthermore that every
U(1)
principal bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is isomorphic to some
P_{n}[w]
.

We know that the bundles
U(1)\hookrightarrow\widehat{P}_{n}[w]\to\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
are topologically-inequivalent and span the isomorphism classes of bundles over
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. By the naturality of the second Chern class, it is therefore sufficient to show that the map

\displaystyle\phi^{*}:H^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\ ,

(B.71)

is an isomorphism. Since
H_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=0
, by the universal coefficient theorem we have
H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\text{Hom}(H_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}}),{\mathbb{Z}})
. Similarly for
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. Thus, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\phi_{*}:H_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\ ,

(B.72)

is an isomorphism. Next, since both
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
have trivial fundamental group, for both spaces the Hurewicz map is an isomorphism, and we thus have
H_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
and
H_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\pi_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n})
. Thus, we have reduced the problem to showing that

\displaystyle\phi_{*}:\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\to\pi_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n})\ ,

(B.73)

is an isomorphism. Let us see why this is true.
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
is a free Abelian group generated by elements
\rho_{ij}
for
i<j
, defined by moving the
i^{\text{th}}
point around a small
S^{2}
enclosing only the
j^{\text{th}}
point; a proof of this fact can be found for instance in Sec II of
fadell
. Conversely,
\pi_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
is a free Abelian group generated by elements
\sigma_{ij}
for
i<j
, defined by moving
\mathbf{r}_{ij}
in an
S^{2}
about the origin in its
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
while holding all other coordinates fixed. Observe then that
\sigma_{ij}
is homotopic to
\phi\circ\rho_{ij}
, and thus
\phi_{*}\rho_{ij}=\sigma_{ij}
. We have therefore established that (
B.73
) is indeed an isomorphism.

Note, our proof provides an independent derivation of the result
dmann

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}\ .

(B.74)

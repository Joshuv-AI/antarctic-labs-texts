Remarks on dimension of unions of curves
Seheon Ham, Hyerim Ko, Sanghyuk Lee, Sewook Oh

Abstract
. We study an analogue of Marstrand’s circle packing problem for curves in higher dimensions. We consider collections of curves which are generated by translation and dilation of a curve γ \gamma in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , i.e., x + t ​ γ x+t\gamma , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ d × ( 0 , ∞ ) (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty) . For a Borel set F ⊂ ℝ d × ( 0 , ∞ ) F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty) , we show the unions of curves ⋃ ( x , t ) ∈ F ( x + t ​ γ ) \bigcup_{(x,t)\in F}(x+t\gamma) has Hausdorff dimension at least α + 1 \alpha+1 whenever F F has Hausdorff dimension bigger than α \alpha , α ∈ ( 0 , d − 1 ) \alpha\in(0,d-1) . We also obtain results for unions of curves generated by multi-parameter dilation of γ \gamma . One of the main ingredients is a local smoothing type estimate (for averages over curves) relative to fractal measures.

1.
Introduction

Let
\gamma
be a smooth curve from
I_{\circ}=[0,1]
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. For a given
\gamma
, we consider the curves given by translation and dilation of
\gamma(I):=\{\gamma(s):s\in I\subset\subset I_{\circ}\}
.
For a Borel set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
, denote

(1.1)

\Gamma(F):=\bigcup_{(x,t)\in F}\big(x+t\gamma(I)\big),

where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty).
In this paper, we are concerned with the problem of determining how small the dimension of
\Gamma(F)
can be depending on that of
F
.

This problem has a rich history when
\gamma
is the unit circle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Besicovitch–Rado
[
4
]
and Kinney
[
15
]
showed that there exists a set
(so-called BRK set) of Lebesgue measure zero which contains a circle of every diameter, i.e.,
\Gamma(F)
with
F=\{(X(t),t):t\in(0,\infty)\}
for some function
X:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

(see also
[
8
,
18
]
for further generalization). In the same vein, it is natural to ask whether or not there exists a plane set of measure zero containing circles with centers at every point, i.e.,
\Gamma(F)
with
F=\{(x,T(x)):x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
for a function
T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
(see
[
19
]
or
[
9
, p.105]
).
Marstrand
[
20
]
showed that such a set cannot be of Lebesgue measure zero.

Those problems for the circle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
generalize naturally with subsets
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty)
.
Let
{\rm Proj}_{x}F
be the orthogonal projection of
F
onto
x
-space.
It was proved in
[
20
]
that if
{\rm Proj}_{x}F
has positive Lebesgue measure, then so does
\Gamma(F)

(see also
[
5
]
).
Later, Mitsis
[
22
]
showed that it is sufficient for
\Gamma(F)
being of positive measure that
\dim_{H}({\rm Proj}_{x}F)>3/2
. Here,
\dim_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension.
It was also conjectured that
\Gamma(F)
has positive Lebesgue measure if
\dim_{H}({\rm Proj}_{x}F)>1
. This condition is optimal in view of Talagrand’s construction
[
31
]
of a measure zero set which contains a circle centered at every point on a straight line.
The conjecture was later proved by Wolff
[
33
]
.
In fact, a stronger result was obtained:
For any set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty)
satisfying
\dim_{H}F>1
(not necessarily
\dim_{H}({\rm Proj}_{x}F)>1
), any set containing
\Gamma(F)
can not be of Lebesgue measure zero.

The main object of this paper is to study an extension of the aforementioned problem to space curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.
Similar problems for non-planar curves are less well understood (see
[
28
,
17
]
for partial results).
We assume that
\gamma
is nondegenerate, namely,

(1.2)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}(s)&\cdots&\gamma^{(d)}(s)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0,\quad\forall s\in I_{\circ}.

The next theorem is our first main result, which generalizes the circle packing problem to nondegenerate curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
\gamma
be a nondegenerate curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that a Borel set
F
satisfies
\dim_{H}F>\alpha
for some
0<\alpha\leq d-1
. Then,

(1.3)

\dim_{H}E\geq\alpha+1

holds whenever
a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
contains
\Gamma(F)
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Käenmäki–Orponen–Venieri
[
14
]
provided a geometric proof of (
1.3
) when
F
is an analytic set.

Theorem
1.1
is optimal in that
\dim_{H}(\Gamma(F))\leq\dim_{H}F+1
for a Borel family
\Gamma(F)
, as can be easily seen by the standard covering argument.
The case that
\Gamma(F)
has positive Lebesgue measure was of special interest.
In
[
17
, Corollary 1.6]
, it was shown that

\Gamma(F)
has positive Lebesgue measure if
\dim_{H}F>d-1
. Conversely,
modifying Kinney’s example
[
15
]
, one can show that the condition
\dim_{H}F>d-1
is necessary, in general, for
\Gamma(F)
to have positive Lebesgue measure.
See Proposition
4.1
.

Our result also extends to curves of different type.
We say that
\gamma
is of finite type if for each
s\in I_{\circ}
, there exists a
d
-tuple of positive integers
(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
such that

\det\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{(a_{1})}(s)&\cdots&\gamma^{(a_{d})}(s)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

Theorem
1.1
also continues to hold for finite type curves, since a finite type curve contains a nondegenerate sub-curve.
See
[
28
,
17
]
for a previous result for finite type curves. One can easily see that Theorem
1.1
does not hold in general if the finite type condition is not satisfied.
For example, consider the curve
\gamma
contained in a
k
-dimensional linear subspace
V_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
2\leq k\leq d-1
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(s),\dots,\gamma^{(k)}(s)
spans
V_{k}
for all
s\in I_{\circ}
.
For
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, let
F_{k}\subset V_{k}\times(0,\infty)

such that
\dim_{H}F_{k}=k-1+2\varepsilon
.
Then
\dim_{H}\Gamma(F_{k})\leq k
since
x_{k}+t\gamma(I)\subset V_{k}
for any
(x_{k},t)\in F_{k}
.
Let
G_{k}\subset V_{k}^{\perp}
such that
\dim_{H}G_{k}=\overline{\dim}_{B}G_{k}=1-\varepsilon
.
Here,
\overline{\dim}_{B}
denotes the upper box counting dimension.
Clearly,
\dim_{H}(F_{k}+G_{k})>k
.
If we take
F=F_{k}+G_{k}
, it follows that
\dim_{H}\Gamma(F)\leq\dim_{H}\Gamma(F_{k})+\dim_{H}{G_{k}}<k+1
even if
\dim_{H}F>k
.

Averages over curves

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on local smoothing estimates relative to fractal measures for averaging operators rather than geometric approach used in some of the previous works (see
[
32
,
22
,
34
,
14
]
).

We consider an averaging operator given by

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}f(x,t)=\chi(t)\int f(x+t\gamma(s))\psi(s)\,ds,

where
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1/2,4))
and
\psi
is a nonnegative smooth function satisfying
\psi=1
on
I
and
\text{supp\! }\psi\subset I_{\circ}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{d}(z,\rho)
denote the ball of radius
\rho
centered at
z
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a non-negative Borel measure
\mu
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we say
\mu
is
\alpha
-dimensional if there is a constant
C_{\mu}

such that

\mu(\mathbb{B}^{d}(z,\rho))\leq C_{\mu}\rho^{\alpha},\quad\forall(z,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}

for some
\alpha\in(0,d]
. We denote by
\mathfrak{C}^{d}(\alpha)
the set of all
\alpha
-dimensional measures in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d}(\alpha)
, we set

\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\rho>0}\rho^{-\alpha}\mu(\mathbb{B}^{d}(z,\rho)).

It is well-known that Marstrand’s results
[
20
]
can be deduced from
L^{p}
,
p\neq\infty
, estimate for the circular maximal function.
In
[
12
]
, some of the authors extended the
L^{p}
circular maximal estimate to that relative to
\alpha
-dimensional measures, which recovers the aforementioned Wolff’s result in
[
33
]
.
Moreover,
L^{p}
maximal bound was extended to space curves (see
[
28
,
16
,
1
,
17
]
).
Various forms of local smoothing estimates played important roles in proving those results.
Similarly, to prove Theorem
1.1
, we make use of local smoothing estimate relative to
\alpha
-dimensional fractal measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
:

(1.4)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Here,
\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
Let us set
p(d)=4
, if
d=2
, and
p(d)=4d-2
, if
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
0<\alpha\leq d+1
, and
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+1}(\alpha)
.
Suppose that
\gamma
is a smooth curve satisfying (
1.2
). If
p>p(d)
, then the estimate (
1.4
)
holds for

\displaystyle\sigma>(d-1-\alpha)/p.

Considering a specific function and
\alpha
-dimensional measure, one can show that
\sigma\geq(d-1-\alpha)/p
is optimal for (
1.4
) to hold.
See Proposition
4.2
with
m=1
.

Multi-parameter dilation

The approach for curves can be further generalized to other submanifolds via the corresponding local smoothing estimates for the associated averaging operators.
In particular, we can extend Theorem
1.1
to families of curves generated by multi-parameter dilations.

Let
1\leq m\leq d
and denote
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
,
t_{i}\in(1,2)
. For an onto map
\omega
from
\{1,\dots,d\}
to
\{1,\dots,m\}
,
we define a family of nondegenerate curves
\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}
generated by
m
parameters by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(s)=(t_{\omega(1)}\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,t_{\omega(d)}\gamma_{d}(s)).

For
F^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(1,2)^{m}
, we obtain a lower bound of Hausdorff dimension of

\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})=\bigcup_{(x,\mathbf{t})\in F^{\prime}}\big(x+\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(I)\big).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
2\leq m\leq d
, and
0<\alpha\leq d+m-2
. Suppose
F^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(1,2)^{m}
is a Borel set with
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>\alpha
.
Then,

(1.6)

\dim_{H}E\geq\max\{\alpha+2-m,0\}

holds whenever a Borel set
E
contains
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
.

When
d=3
and
m=2
, a special case was considered by
Pramanik and Seeger
[
28
]
. It was shown (
[
28
, Proposition 6.1]
) that the
union of a two-parameter family of helices
\gamma_{t_{1},t_{2}}(s)=(t_{1}\cos(2\pi s),t_{1}\sin(2\pi s),t_{2}s)
has Hausdorff dimension at least
8/3
.
Theorem
1.4
, in particular, proves the optimal result that the union of
\gamma_{t_{1},t_{2}}
has Hausdorff dimension
3
.
More generally, note that
\dim_{H}E=d
if
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>d+m-2
. Furthermore, from Theorem
2.1
, one can show
E
is of positive Lebesgue measure if
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>d+m-2
(see Remark
1
in p. 8).
This is sharp in that, for
1\leq m\leq d
, there exists a set
E
of Lebesgue measure zero containing
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
generated by a nondegenerate curve
\gamma
while
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}=d+m-2
(see Proposition
4.1
).

Relation to local smoothing estimates for averages

For
2\leq k\leq d-1
, one may consider a similar packing problem with
k
-dimensional submanifolds under a suitable curvature condition.
The problem for the case
k=d-1
which includes spheres and hyperplanes is relatively well known
(see
[
18
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a smooth compact
k
-dimensional submanifold. Let
d\mathbf{m}
denote the surface measure on
\mathfrak{S}
and consider

\mathbf{A}f(x,t)=\chi(t)\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(x+ty)\,d\mathbf{m}(y).

It is known that
the averaging operator has smoothing properties under a suitable curvature condition on
\mathfrak{S}
. There are various model
cases for which
f\to\mathbf{A}f(\cdot,t)
with a fixed
t\neq 0
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\sigma<0
and the best possible regularity exponent is
\sigma=-k/p
(e.g., see
[
23
,
29
,
2
,
17
]
). Taking additional integration over
t
allows an extra regularity gain on a certain range of
p
. This phenomenon is known as
local smoothing
. It is not difficult to see the smoothing order can not exceed
(k+1)/p
(e.g., see Section
4.2
). We refer to as sharp local smoothing estimate the following:

(1.7)

\|\mathbf{A}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},~\quad\sigma>-(k+1)/p.

If
k=1
, (
1.7
) has been established in
[
28
,
11
,
17
]
under the assumption that the curve satisfies (
1.2
).
For
k=d-1\geq 2
, the estimate (
1.7
) is equivalent to Sogge’s local smoothing conjecture when
\mathfrak{S}
is strictly convex (
[
6
]
) and
similar results are also known when
\mathfrak{S}
has non-vanishing gaussian (
[
7
,
3
]
) with principal curvatures of different signs.

We now intend to draw a connection between the sharp local estimate (
1.7
) and the problem determining dimension of union of
(x+t\mathfrak{S})
.
The following gives optimal lower bounds on dimension of union of
(x+t\mathfrak{S})
. It can be shown by adapting the proof of Theorem
1.1
, so we state it without proof.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d-1
. Suppose that (
1.7
) holds for some
p\neq\infty
.
Suppose that a Borel set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
satisfies
\dim_{H}F>\alpha
for some
0<\alpha\leq d-k
. Then,
if a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
contains

\bigcup_{(x,t)\in F}(x+t\mathfrak{S}),

then
\dim_{H}E\geq k+\alpha
.

Generalized averaging operators

Theorem
1.1
can also be extended to an analogous result for unions of variable curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and more generally variable hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.

Let
U_{\circ},V_{\circ}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be small open neighborhoods of
x_{\circ}
and
y_{\circ}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
Also, let
\Phi_{t}\in C^{\infty}(U_{\circ}\times V_{\circ})
vary smoothly in the parameter
t\in J_{\circ}:=[1,2]
.
We set
U\subset\subset U_{\circ}
,
V\subset\subset V_{\circ}
, and
J\subset\subset J_{\circ}
.
For
(x,t)\in U\times J
, we consider a family of variable hypersurfaces given by

\mathcal{G}_{x,t}=\{y\in V:\Phi_{t}(x,y)=0\}.

We assume that
\Phi_{t}
satisfies the rotational curvature condition:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{t}&\partial_{y}\Phi_{t}\\
\partial_{x}\Phi_{t}&\partial_{xy}^{2}\Phi_{t}\end{pmatrix}\neq 0\qquad\text{where}\quad\Phi_{t}(x,y)=0.

Then,
\partial_{y}\Phi_{t}\neq 0
for any
(x,y)\in U_{\circ}\times V_{\circ}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
is a smooth embedded hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Furthermore, there exists a homogeneous function
q
of degree
1
in
\xi
such that

q
is smooth if
\xi\neq 0
and satisfies

q(x,t,\partial_{x}\Phi_{t}(x,y))=\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}(x,y)
whenever
\Phi_{t}(x,y)=0

(see
[
30
]
for detailed construction of
q
). We assume that

(1.9)

\textrm{rank}\,\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}q(x,t,\xi)=d-1~\text{ for }~(x,t,\xi)\in U_{\circ}\times J_{\circ}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}).

We say that
\Phi_{t}
satisfies cinematic curvature condition if (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) hold.

Proving a sharp estimate for a maximal average over a neighborhood of
\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
Zahl
[
34
]
showed that a Borel set containing curves
\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
for every
0<t<1
has Hausdorff dimension
2
(see also
[
18
]
).
By utilizing the local smoothing estimates in
[
10
,
3
]
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
0<\alpha<1
. Suppose
F\subset U\times J
is a Borel set satisfying
\dim_{H}F>\alpha
and
\Phi_{t}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition. Then a Borel set containing
\mathcal{G}_{x,t}

for
(x,t)\in F
has Hausdorff dimension at least
d-1+\alpha
.

Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and
1.4
by using local smoothing type estimates and Bessel capacity.
In Section
3
, we provide a proof of Theorem
1.6
.
Finally, we discuss sharpness of Theorem
1.4
and
2.1
in Section

.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and
1.4

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
and
1.4
by applying Theorem
1.3
combined with Proposition
2.5
which shows that Hausdorff dimension of unions of curves can be determined in terms of the regularity of the smoothing estimate (
1.4
).

2.1.
Local smoothing estimates relative to fractal measure

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
.
In fact, we prove a slightly general result considering multi-parameter dilation.
Let
1\leq m\leq d
and
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
.
Recall that
\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}
is given by (
1.5
).
We consider

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\chi(t_{j})\int f(x+\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(s))\psi(s)\,ds

where
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1/2,4))
such that
\chi=1
on
[1,2]
and
\psi
is a real valued smooth function supported in
I_{\circ}
.
Theorem
1.3
follows from the next theorem with
m=1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq d
. Also let
0<\alpha\leq n:=d+m
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
.
Suppose that
\gamma
is a smooth curve satisfying (
1.2
). If
p>p(d)
, then

(2.1)

\big\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}}

for
\sigma>(n-\alpha-2)/p
.

If
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure, i.e.,
\alpha=n
, the smoothing order
\sigma>-2/p
does not depend on
m
.
We deduce Theorem
2.1
from the following sharp local smoothing estimates for
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
11
,
17
]
)
.

For
d\geq 2
, let
\gamma
be a smooth curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
1.2
).
Then, for
p>p(d)
and any
r>-2/p
, we have

(2.2)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{r}}.

We also use the next lemma which is commonly used to obtain estimates relative to fractal measures.

Lemma 2.3

(cf. Lemma 2.7 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
for
0<\alpha\leq n
. Suppose
\widehat{F}
is supported on
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\lambda)
. Then

\|F\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{n-\alpha}{p}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
p\geq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}((2^{-1},2))
such that
\sum_{j}\beta(2^{-j}s)=1
for
s\neq 0

and set
\beta_{0}=\sum_{j\leq 0}\beta(2^{-j}\cdot)
.
Let
P_{\lambda}
be a standard Littlewood-Paley projection operator given by
\widehat{P_{\lambda}f}(\xi)=\beta(\lambda^{-1}|\xi|)\widehat{f}(\xi)
.
Then, we have

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})=\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{0}f(x,\mathbf{t})+\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{2^{j}}f(x,\mathbf{t}),

where
\widehat{P_{0}f}=\beta_{0}(|\xi|)\widehat{f}(\xi)
.
Since the kernel of
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{0}f(x,\mathbf{t})
decays rapidly outside of the unit ball, we have

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{0}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}
by Schur’s test.
Thus it suffices to show that, for
\lambda\geq 1
,

(2.3)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\sigma}\|f\|_{L^{p}},~\quad\sigma>(n-\alpha-2)/p.

Since
\chi
is compactly supported, the support of the space-time Fourier transform of
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f
is unbounded.
To apply Lemma
2.3
, we decompose the frequency support of
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f
into a bounded set and its complement.

For
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m})
, let us set

\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)=\beta(\lambda^{-1}|\xi|)\iint e^{2\pi i(\xi\cdot\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(s)-\mathbf{t}\cdot\tau)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\chi(t_{j})\psi(s)\,ds\,d\mathbf{t},

so we have
\mathcal{F}_{x,\mathbf{t}}(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f)(\xi,\tau)=\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)\widehat{f}(\xi)
.
We define a frequency localized operator
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}
by

\mathcal{F}_{x,\mathbf{t}}(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}f)(\xi,\tau)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{0}\big((C\lambda)^{-1}\tau_{j}\big)\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)\widehat{f}(\xi)

for a sufficiently large constant
C\geq 1
to be chosen later.
We set

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{e}=\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}-\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}.

First, we show

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{e}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{p}

for any
N\geq 1
.
We may write
A_{\gamma}^{e}P_{\lambda}f(x,\mathbf{t})=\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(\cdot,\mathbf{t})\ast f(x)
, where

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(x,\mathbf{t})=\iint\Big(1-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{0}((C\lambda)^{-1}\tau_{j})\Big)\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi+\mathbf{t}\cdot\tau)}d\xi d\tau.

By integration by parts in
\mathbf{t}
, we have

|\partial_{\xi,\tau}^{\kappa}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|\tau|)^{-N}
for any multi-indices
\kappa
and
N\geq 1
, provided that
|\tau|\geq C|\xi|

for a sufficiently large
C
satisfying
C\geq 1+10\sup_{s\in I_{\circ}}|\gamma(s)|
.
Then, integration by parts yields

|\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(x,\mathbf{t})|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|x|)^{-N}(1+|\mathbf{t}|)^{-N}

for any
N\geq 1
.
By Schur’s test, we get the estimate (
2.4
) for any
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
.

Now, we show

(2.5)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{n-\alpha}{p}+r}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},~\quad r>-2/p.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{x,\mathbf{t}}(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f)
is supported in
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,C(d,m)\lambda)
, we can apply Lemma
2.3
to get

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{n-\alpha}{p}}\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Using
|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f|\leq|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f|+|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{e}P_{\lambda}f|
and (
2.4
),
we see

\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\prime}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{n-\alpha}{p}}\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{p}

for any
N\geq 1
.
In order to prove (
2.5
), it suffices to show

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{r}\|f\|_{L^{p}},~\ r>-2/p.

Using Fubini’s theorem, we make change of variables
(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{m})\mapsto(t_{1},t_{1}t_{2},\dots,t_{1}t_{m})
to get

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}P_{\lambda}f\big\|_{p}^{p}=\int\!\!\cdots\!\!\int\Big(\iint\big|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\omega}}P_{\lambda}f(x,t_{1})\big|^{p}t_{1}^{m-1}\prod_{j=2}^{m}\chi(t_{1}t_{j})\,dx\,dt_{1}\Big)\,dt_{2}\cdots dt_{d},

where
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}=(1,t_{2},\dots,t_{m})
.
For fixed
t_{2},\dots,t_{m}
, it is obvious that
\gamma_{\,\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\omega}
is nondegenerate.
Applying Theorem
2.2
to the inner integral, we obtain (
2.6
), and thus (
2.5
) follows.

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get
the desired estimate (
2.3
) for
\sigma=(n-\alpha)/p+r>(n-\alpha-2)/p
.
∎

2.2.
Bessel capacity

To prove Theorem
1.1
and
1.4
,
we begin with Bessel capacity which is closely related to Hausdorff dimension.
For a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let us define the Bessel capacity
B_{\sigma}^{p}(E)
by

B_{\sigma}^{p}(E)=\inf\big\{\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}}^{p}:f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,f\geq\chi_{E}\big\}

for
\sigma>0
and
p>1
.
By
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}
we denote
\sigma
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The following describes a relation between the Bessel capacity and the Hausdorff measure.

Theorem 2.4

(Theorem 2.6.16 in
[
35
]
)
.

Let
p>1
and
0<\sigma p<d
.
Also let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set.
If
\mathcal{H}^{d-\sigma p}(E)<\infty
, then
B_{\sigma}^{p}(E)=0
.
Conversely, if
B_{\sigma}^{p}(E)=0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{d-\sigma p+\epsilon}(E)=0
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Using (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.4
, we can obtain lower bounds of Hausdorff dimension of unions of curves.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq n:=d+m
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
.
Suppose that the estimate (
2.1
) holds with
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}
for some
\sigma_{\circ}=\sigma_{\circ}(\alpha,n,p)>0
and
p>1
.
If
F^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(1,2)^{m}
is a Borel set with
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>\alpha
, then a Borel set

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
has Hausdorff dimension at least
d-\sigma_{\circ}p
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a Borel set
E
containing
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
such that

\dim_{H}E<d-\sigma_{\circ}p.

Taking a number
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}
such that
\dim_{H}E<d-\sigma p<d-\sigma_{\circ}p
, we see
\mathcal{H}^{d-\sigma p}(E)<\infty
. By Theorem
2.4
, it follows

(2.7)

B_{\sigma}^{p}(E)=0.

For
\alpha_{1}
satisfying
\alpha<\alpha_{1}<\dim_{H}F^{\prime}
, there exists a compact set
F_{1}\subset F^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{1}}(F_{1})>0
.
By Frostman’s lemma (see
[
21
]
for example), there exists
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha_{1})
with
\text{supp\! }\mu\subset F_{1}
.
Let us consider a function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f\geq\chi_{E}
. Since
\text{supp\! }f\supset E\supset\cup_{(x,\mathbf{t})\in F_{1}}(x+\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(I))
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})\gtrsim 1
for all
(x,\mathbf{t})\in F_{1}
.
Since we are assuming (
2.1
) for
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}
, it follows that

(2.8)

\mu(F_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha_{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
\sigma,p
satisfying (
2.7
).
Note that (
2.8
) holds for any function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f\geq\chi_{E}
. So,
\mu(F_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}=0
from (
2.7
), which contradicts to
\text{supp\! }\mu\subset F_{1}
.
Hence
\dim_{H}E\geq d-\sigma_{\circ}p
for
E
containing
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\dim_{H}F>\alpha
, there exists
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+1}(\alpha)
which is supported in
F
by Frostman’s lemma.
For such
\mu
,
there exists
\sigma_{\circ}>0
such that the estimate (
1.4
) holds with
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}>(d-1-\alpha)/p
by Theorem
1.3
.
Since
\alpha\leq d-1
and
p>1
,
by Proposition
2.5
with
m=1
we see that a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
\Gamma(F)
has Hausdorff dimension at least
d-\sigma_{\circ}p
. Taking
\sigma_{\circ}
arbitrarily close to
(d-1-\alpha)/p
, we get (
1.3
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem
1.4
can be obtained in the same manner.
The difference is that we have (
2.1
) with
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}>(d+m-\alpha-2)/p
. If
\alpha\leq d+m-2
, we can apply Proposition
2.5
to obtain
\dim_{H}E\geq d-\sigma_{\circ}p
. We omit the details.
∎

Remark 1
.

By using Theorem
2.1
, one can show that
E
is of positive Lebesgue measure if
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>d+m-2
.
For
\alpha_{1}
satisfying
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>\alpha_{1}>d+m-2
, let
F_{1}
be a compact subset of
F
with
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{1}}(F_{1})>0
.
Then there exists
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+m}(\alpha_{1})
with
\text{supp\! }\mu\subset F_{1}
.
Applying Theorem
2.1
with
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+m}(\alpha_{1})
, we have

\mu(F_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}\chi_{E}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+m},\,d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha_{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\chi_{E}\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}}

for

.
Hence,

3.
Dimension of unions of variable hypersurfaces

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
by making use of the sharp local smoothing estimates for variable coefficient averaging operators.
We consider the Fourier integral operator given by

\mathfrak{A}f(x,t)=\int a(x,t,y)\delta(\Phi_{t}(x,y))f(y)\,dy

where
a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{\circ}\times J_{\circ}\times V_{\circ})
is a positive bump function such that
a=1
on
U\times J\times V
.
Sharp local smoothing estimates for
\mathfrak{A}f
were shown by Gao–Liu–Miao–Xi
[
10
]
for
d=2
, and Beltran–Hickman–Sogge
[
3
]
for higher dimensions.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
3
,
10
]
)
.

Suppose
\Phi_{t}
satisfies the conditions (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) on the support of
a
. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{A}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{r}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad r>-d/p

holds for
4\leq p<\infty
if
d=2
, and for
2(d+1)/(d-1)\leq p<\infty
if
d\geq 3
.

The range of
p
can not be generally extended when
d=2
and odd
d\geq 3
.
In the same manner as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the following from Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq d+1
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+1}(\alpha)
. Then,
we have

(3.2)

\|\mathfrak{A}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\,d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\sigma>(1-\alpha)/p

for
4\leq p<\infty
when
d=2
, and for
2(d+1)/(d-1)\leq p<\infty
when
d\geq 3
.

Proof.

In local coordinates, modulo a smoothing operator,
\mathfrak{A}f
can be written as a finite sum of the operators

(3.3)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}f(x,t)=\int e^{2\pi i\phi(x,t,\xi)}\mathfrak{a}(x,t,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi,

where
\mathfrak{a}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compactly supported in
(x,t)
and satisfies

|\partial_{x,t}^{\kappa_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{\kappa_{2}}\mathfrak{a}(x,t,\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}-|\kappa_{2}|}

for any
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
.
Also,
\phi
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
of homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition on
\text{supp\! }\mathfrak{a}
(see
[
13
,
24
]
).
So, it is enough to show that

(3.4)

\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\sigma}\|f\|_{p}

holds for
\lambda\geq 1
if
\sigma>(1-\alpha)/p
.

By (
3.1
), we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\lambda^{r}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad r>-d/p.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
,
we write

\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f=\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{{}^{\prime}}P_{\lambda}f+\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{e}P_{\lambda}f,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{{}^{\prime}}P_{\lambda}f)(\zeta,\tau)

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}\big((C\lambda)^{-1}|(\zeta,\tau)|\big)\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f)(\zeta,\tau),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{e}P_{\lambda}f)(\zeta,\tau)

\displaystyle=\big(1-\beta_{0}\big((C\lambda)^{-1}|(\zeta,\tau)|\big)\big)\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f)(\zeta,\tau)

for a sufficiently large
C\geq 1
.

By rapid decay of the kernel, one can show

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{e}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{p}

for any
N\geq 1
. Indeed, note that

\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}P_{\lambda}f)(\zeta,\tau)=\int\mathfrak{w}_{\lambda}(\zeta,\tau,y)f(y)\,dy

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\lambda}(\zeta,\tau,y)=\iiint e^{-2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\zeta+t^{\prime}\tau)}e^{2\pi i\phi(x^{\prime},t^{\prime},\xi)-2\pi iy\cdot\xi}\mathfrak{a}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime},\xi)\beta(\lambda^{-1}|\xi|)\,dx^{\prime}dt^{\prime}d\xi.

So, we have

\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{e}P_{\lambda}f(x,t)=\int\mathfrak{K}_{\lambda}(x,t,y)f(y)\,dy,

where

\mathfrak{K}_{\lambda}(x,t,y)=\iint e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\zeta+t\tau)}\big(1-\beta_{0}((C\lambda)^{-1}|(\zeta,\tau)|)\big)\mathfrak{w}_{\lambda}(\zeta,\tau,y)\,d\zeta d\tau.

Note that
|\partial_{x,t}^{\kappa_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{\kappa_{2}}\phi|\leq C\lambda^{1-|\kappa_{2}|}
by homogeneity of
\phi
. Thus repeated integration by parts in
x^{\prime},t^{\prime},\xi
gives
|\partial_{\zeta,\tau}^{\kappa}\mathfrak{w}_{\lambda}(\zeta,\tau,y)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|(\zeta,\tau)|)^{-N}(1+\lambda|y|)^{-N}
for any
N\geq 1
if
|(\zeta,\tau)|\geq C\lambda
for a sufficiently large
C\geq 1
.
So, integration by parts in
\zeta,\tau
gives

|\mathfrak{K}_{\lambda}(x,t,y)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|(x,t)|)^{-N}(1+\lambda|y|)^{-N}

for any
N\geq 1
, from which we obtain (
3.6
).

To deal with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{{}^{\prime}}P_{\lambda}f
, we apply Lemma
2.3
, (
3.6
), and (
3.5
) successively.
So, if
r>-d/p
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{{}^{\prime}}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{d+1-\alpha}{p}+r}\|f\|_{p}+\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{p}

for any
N\geq 1
.
By this and (
3.6
), we get (
3.4
) for
\sigma>(1-\alpha)/p
.
∎

Combining Corollary
3.2
and Proposition
2.5
, we prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.1
.
By following the proof of Proposition
2.5
, one can easily see Proposition
2.5
with
m=1
holds for
\cup_{(x,t)\in F}\,\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
which replaces
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
. Indeed, taking
f\geq\chi_{E}
such that
E\supset\cup_{(x,t)\in F}\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
, since
a=1
on
U\times J\times V
,
we see
\mathfrak{A}f(x,t)\gtrsim 1
holds whenever
(x,t)\in F
. So, by applying (
3.2
) in place of (
2.1
), we obtain

\mu(F)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\|\mathfrak{A}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

which replaces (
2.8
). In the same manner, it follows that Proposition
2.5
holds for
\cup_{(x,t)\in F}\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
replacing
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
.

By Corollary
3.2
, there is a
\sigma_{\circ}
such that (
3.2
) holds for
\sigma>\sigma_{\circ}>(1-\alpha)/p
. Combining this with Proposition
2.5
for
\cup_{(x,t)\in F}\,\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
,
we see that the set
E
containing
\cup_{(x,t)\in F}\,\mathcal{G}_{x,t}
has Hausdorff dimension at least
d-(1-\alpha)
if
\dim_{H}F>\alpha
.
∎

4.
Sharpness of Theorem
1.4
and
2.1

In this section, we discuss optimality of the exponent
\alpha=d+m-2
in Theorem
1.4
(and Theorem
1.1
), and sharpness of the regularity exponent
s
in Theorem
2.1
(and Theorem
1.3
).

4.1.
Construction of a type of BRK set for space curves

We show that the condition
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>d+m-2
in Theorem
1.4
(
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}>d-1
in Theorem
1.1
) is necessary, in general, for
\Gamma(F^{\prime})
to have positive Lebesgue measure.
Here, we may assume that
F^{\prime}
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1)^{m}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq m\leq d
, and let
\gamma
be a curve defined on
I_{\circ}
by
\gamma(s)=(s^{a_{1}},\dots,s^{a_{d}})
for integers
1\leq a_{1}<\dots<a_{d}
. Then, there exists a Borel set
F^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1)^{m}
with
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}=d+m-2
such that
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
is of Lebesgue measure zero.

To prove Proposition
4.1
, we use Kinney’s construction of Lebesgue measure zero set which consists of similitudes of a planar convex curve (
[
15
]
). When
m\leq d-1
, as is clear from the proof of Proposition
4.1
below, Proposition
4.1
remains valid as long as a projection of
\gamma
to 2-plane is convex. Thus, by a suitable change of coordinates and Taylor expansion, one can see that Proposition
4.1
continues to hold if
\gamma
is of finite type.
However, when
m=d
, we need an additional assumption that at least two components of
\gamma
are monomial.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
\gamma^{a,b}(s)=(s^{a},s^{b})
for
s\in I_{\circ}
and
1\leq a<b
.
Following the argument in
[
15
]
, we first construct a compact set
K^{a,b}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of Lebesgue measure zero such that
K
contains
t\gamma^{a,b}(I)
for every
0<t\leq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the standard
1/3
-Cantor set on
[0,1]
.
Note that
[0,1]
is the distance set of
\mathcal{C}
. So, for each
t\in(0,1]
, there exist the smallest numbers
\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t),\mathfrak{c}_{2}(t)\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{2}(t)-\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t)=t
. Then the curve
(\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t))+t\gamma^{a,b}(I)
connects two points
(\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t))
and
(\mathfrak{c}_{2}(t),\mathfrak{c}_{2}(t))
.
Since
\gamma^{a,b}
is a convex curve,
adapting the argument in
[
15
]
, one can show that the union of curves

K^{a,b}:=\bigcup_{0<t\leq 1}\Big((\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t))+t\gamma^{a,b}(I)\Big)

is of Lebesgue measure zero in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
15
, p.1080]
).

We treat the cases
m\leq d-1
and
m=d
, separately.
We consider the case
m\leq d-1
first. Since
m\leq d-1
,
\omega(i)=\omega(j)
for some
i\neq j
. There exists a dilation parameter, which we denote by
t_{1}
, appearing at least twice in
\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}=(t_{\omega(1)}\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,t_{\omega(d)}\gamma_{d}(s))
. We may assume
i=1
,
j=2
, that is to say,
t_{\omega(1)}=t_{\omega(2)}=t_{1}
.
We set

F^{\prime}=\big\{(x,\mathbf{t})\in[0,1]^{d}\times(0,1)^{m}:(x_{1},x_{2})=(\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t_{1}),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(t_{1}))\big\}.

Obviously,
\dim_{H}F^{\prime}=d+m-2
and

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})=\bigcup_{(x,\mathbf{t})\in F^{\prime}}(x+\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(I))\subset K^{a,b}\times[-C,C]^{d-2}

for some constant
C>0
where
(a,b)=(a_{i},a_{j})
for some
i<j
. Therefore, it is clear that
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
is of Lebesgue measure zero in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now we consider the case
m=d
, for which we make use of homogeneity of monomials.
We set

g_{\mathbf{t}}(s)=\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}((t_{1}/t_{2})^{1/(a_{2}-a_{1})}s).

The first two components of
g_{\mathbf{t}}(s)
are given by
b(\mathbf{t})s^{a_{1}},

b(\mathbf{t})s^{a_{2}}
, where
b(\mathbf{t})=(t_{1}^{a_{2}}/t_{2}^{a_{1}})^{1/(a_{2}-a_{1})}
.
Set
J_{*}=\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in(0,1]^{2}:t_{1}\geq t_{2},\,t_{1}^{a_{2}}\leq t_{2}^{a_{1}}\}
and consider

F^{\prime}=\big\{(x,\mathbf{t})\in[0,1]^{d}\times J_{*}\times(0,1)^{m-2}:(x_{1},x_{2})=(\mathfrak{c}_{1}(b(\mathbf{t})),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(b(\mathbf{t})))\big\}.

Then, we have

\bigcup_{(x,\mathbf{t})\in F^{\prime}}\big(x+g_{\mathbf{t}}(I)\big)\subset\bigcup_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in J_{*}}\Big((\mathfrak{c}_{1}(b(\mathbf{t})),\mathfrak{c}_{1}(b(\mathbf{t})))+b(\mathbf{t})\gamma^{a_{1},a_{2}}(I)\Big)\times[-C,C]^{d-2}

for some
C>0
. Note that

0<b(\mathbf{t})<1
and
\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(I)\subset g_{\mathbf{t}}(I)
if
\mathbf{t}\in J_{*}\times(0,1)^{m-2}
.
Thus, it follows that
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})\subset K^{a_{1},a_{2}}\times[-C,C]^{d-2}
. So,
\Gamma^{\omega}(F^{\prime})
is of Lebesgue measure zero.
∎

4.2.
Optimality of the regularity exponent in Theorem
2.1

We prove that the regularity exponent in Theorem
2.1
is sharp for some range of
\alpha
.
Recall that
\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(s)=(t_{\omega(1)}\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,t_{\omega(d)}\gamma_{d}(s))

for an onto map
\omega:\{1,\dots,d\}\rightarrow\{1,\dots,m\}
.
We set

k(\omega)=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\big|\{i\in[1,d]:\omega(i)=j\}\big|

which is the maximum number of repetition in
\{\omega(1),\dots,\omega(d)\}
.
For example, let
\gamma(s)=(\cos(2\pi s),\sin(2\pi s),s)
. If
\omega(1)=\omega(2)=1
and
\omega(3)=2
,
then
\gamma_{t_{1},t_{2}}(s)=(t_{1}\cos(2\pi s),t_{1}\sin(2\pi s),t_{2}s)
is a two parameter family of helices with
k(\omega)=2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq m\leq d
, and let
\gamma
be a finite type curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Set
n=d+m
. For
0<\alpha\leq n
, suppose (
2.1
) holds for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
.
Then, if
m\leq d-1
, we have

(4.1)

\sigma\geq\begin{cases}\,(n-\alpha-2)/p,\qquad\ &\text{if~}\hfill\quad n-k(\omega)-1<\alpha\leq n,\\[3.44444pt]
\,(k(\omega)-1)/p,\qquad\ &\text{if~}\hfill\quad 0<\alpha\leq n-k(\omega)-1.\end{cases}

If
m=d
and
\gamma(s)=(s^{a_{1}},\dots,s^{a_{d}})
for
1\leq a_{1}<\dots<a_{d}
and
s\in I_{\circ}
, then

(4.2)

\sigma\geq\begin{cases}\,(n-\alpha-2)/p,\qquad\ &\text{if~}\hfill\quad n-3<\alpha\leq n,\\[3.44444pt]
\,1/p,\qquad\ &\text{if~}\hfill\quad 0<\alpha\leq n-3.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
d
,
m
, and
k=k(\omega)
be fixed, and denote
\widetilde{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
\overline{x}=(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{d})
.

We consider the case
1\leq m\leq d-1
first. Note that
k\geq 2
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t_{1}
is the parameter repeated
k
-times.
Also, by a permutation, we may assume that
(t_{\omega(1)},\dots,t_{\omega(d)})=(t_{1},\dots,t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{m})
.
So, let us write

x+{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(s)=(\widetilde{x}+t_{1}\widetilde{\gamma}(s),~\overline{x}+\overline{\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}}(s))\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-k}.

Since
\widetilde{\gamma}
is of finite type in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, by Taylor’s expansion
\widetilde{\gamma}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\gamma}^{(\ell_{j})}(0)s^{\ell_{j}}+O(s^{\ell_{k}+1})
such that
\widetilde{\gamma}^{(\ell_{j})}(0)\neq 0
for some integers
1\leq\ell_{1}<\dots<\ell_{k}
if
|s|\leq\delta_{1}
for a sufficiently small
\delta_{1}
.
By a change of variables, we may assume that there is an interval
I_{*}=[\delta_{0},2\delta_{0}]\subset[0,1]
such that
|\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)|\geq c
on
I_{*}
for a constant
c>0
.

Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-2,2]^{k})
such that
\eta\geq 0
and
\eta=1
on
[-1,1]^{k}
.
We also choose a positive function
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})
such that
\text{supp\! }\widehat{h}\subset[-1,1]^{d-k}
and
h\geq 1
on
[-C,C]^{d-k}
for
C\geq 1+10\sup_{s\in I_{\circ}}|\gamma(s)|
.
For a fixed
\lambda\gg\delta_{0}^{-1}
, we set

g(\widetilde{x})=\sum_{\nu\in\lambda^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{*}}\eta\big(\lambda(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu))\big)

and
f(x)=g(\widetilde{x})h(\overline{x})
.
Let

Q=\big\{(x,\mathbf{t})\in[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]^{d}\times[2^{-1},2]^{m}:|\widetilde{x}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1},~|t_{1}-1|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1}\big\}.

Then, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\chi(t_{j})\int g(\widetilde{x}+t_{1}\widetilde{\gamma}(s))\,h(\overline{x}+\overline{\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}}(s))\psi(s)\,ds\gtrsim 1,

whenever
(x,\mathbf{t})\in Q
for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
To see this, note that

(4.4)

\displaystyle g(\widetilde{x}+t_{1}\widetilde{\gamma}(s))=\sum_{\nu\in\lambda^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{*}}\eta\big(\lambda(\widetilde{x}+(t_{1}-1)\widetilde{\gamma}(s)+\widetilde{\gamma}(s)-\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu))\big).

So,
g\big(\widetilde{x}+t_{1}\widetilde{\gamma}(s)\big)\gtrsim 1
for
s\in I_{*}
if
(x,\mathbf{t})\in Q
.
Also, note
g\big(\widetilde{x}+t_{1}\widetilde{\gamma}(s)\big)\geq 0
, for
s\in[-1,1]\setminus I_{*}
, and
h\big(\overline{x}+\overline{\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}}(s)\big)\gtrsim 1
, for
s\in[-1,1]
and
(x,\mathbf{t})\in Q
.
Hence, we get (
4.3
).

Now, we show

\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma-(k-1)/p}.

We observe that
\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\|h\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})}
for
\sigma\geq 0
, and similarly
\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})}
for
\sigma<0
.
Those can be shown by using the Mikhlin multiplier theorem.
Since
\|h\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k})}\lesssim 1
for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, it suffices to show that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma-(k-1)/p}.

Recall
\beta
,
\beta_{0}
which are defined in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
We decompose
g=\sum_{j\geq 0}g_{j}
such that
\widehat{g_{j}}(\widetilde{\xi})=\widehat{g}(\widetilde{\xi})\beta_{j}(\lambda^{-1}|\widetilde{\xi}|)
with
\beta_{j}=\beta(2^{-j}\cdot)
for
j\geq 1
.
Note that

\big((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\sigma/2}\widehat{g_{j}}\big)^{\vee}(\widetilde{x})=\sum_{\nu\in\lambda^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{*}}\mathfrak{G}_{j,\nu}(\widetilde{x}),

where

\mathfrak{G}_{j,\nu}(\widetilde{x})=\int e^{2\pi i(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu))\cdot\widetilde{\xi}}\lambda^{-k}\widehat{\eta}(\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{\xi})\beta_{j}(\lambda^{-1}|\widetilde{\xi}|)(1+|\widetilde{\xi}|^{2})^{\sigma/2}\,d\widetilde{\xi}.

By rescaling
\widetilde{\xi}\rightarrow\lambda\widetilde{\xi}
, it is easy to show that

|\mathfrak{G}_{j,\nu}(\widetilde{x})|\lesssim 2^{-jN}\lambda^{\sigma}(1+\lambda|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu)|)^{-N}
for any
N\geq 1
.
Since
|\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)|\geq c
on
I_{*}
for a constant
c>0
, we have
|\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu)-\widetilde{\gamma}(\nu^{\prime})|\geq c|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|
for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in I_{*}
.
Therefore, we see
\|\sum_{\nu}\mathfrak{G}_{j,\nu}\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-jN}\lambda^{\sigma-(k-1)/p}
for any
N\geq 1
.
This gives
\sum_{j\geq 0}\|g_{j}\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma-(k-1)/p}
and hence (
4.5
).

We now take

\displaystyle d\mu(x,\mathbf{t})=\begin{cases}\lambda^{n-\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x,\mathbf{t})\,dxd\mathbf{t},\quad\ &\text{if}\hfill\quad n-k-1<\alpha\leq n,\\[4.30554pt]
\lambda^{k+1}\chi_{Q}(x,\mathbf{t})\,dxd\mathbf{t},\quad\ &\text{if}\hfill\quad 0<\alpha\leq n-k-1.\end{cases}

It is easy to see that
\mu\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\alpha)
and
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}\lesssim 1
. Indeed,
when
n-k-1<\alpha\leq n
, we note that
\mu(\mathbb{B}^{n}(z,r))\lesssim\lambda^{n-\alpha}r^{n}
if
r\leq\lambda^{-1}
, and
\mu(\mathbb{B}^{n}(z,r))\lesssim\lambda^{n-\alpha-k-1}r^{n-k-1}
if
r\geq\lambda^{-1}
.
Similarly, when
0<\alpha\leq n-k-1
, we have
\mu(\mathbb{B}^{n}(z,r))\lesssim\lambda^{k+1}r^{n}
if
r\leq\lambda^{-1}
and
\mu(\mathbb{B}^{n}(z,r))\lesssim r^{n-k-1}
if
r>\lambda^{-1}
.
Combining the two cases, we see
\mu(\mathbb{B}^{n}(z,r))\lesssim r^{\alpha}
as desired.

Note that
|Q|\sim\varepsilon_{0}^{d+1}\lambda^{-k-1}
and
\mu(Q)\gtrsim\varepsilon_{0}^{d+1}\min\{\lambda^{n-\alpha-k-1},1\}
. Therefore,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{(n-\alpha-k-1)/p},1\}\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma-(k-1)/p}.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, we get (
4.1
).

We now consider the case
m=d
and
\gamma(s)=(s^{a_{1}},\dots,s^{a_{d}})
. As before, we exploit homogeneity of monomials.
We denote
\widetilde{x}
and
\overline{x}
same as above with
k=2
, i.e.,
\widetilde{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
\overline{x}=(x_{3},\dots,x_{d})
.
Set

c(\mathbf{t})=(t_{1}/t_{2})^{1/(a_{2}-a_{1})}
. By the change of variable
s\mapsto c(\mathbf{t})s
, we have

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\chi(t_{j})\int f\big(x-\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}(c(\mathbf{t})s)\big)\psi(c(\mathbf{t})s)c(\mathbf{t})\,ds.

Note that
\widetilde{\gamma_{\mathbf{t}}^{\omega}}(c(\mathbf{t})s)=(t_{1}^{a_{2}}/t_{2}^{a_{1}})^{1/(a_{2}-a_{1})}(s^{a_{1}},s^{a_{2}})
. Then, we can repeat the same argument as above. Let
f
and
\mu
be given in the same manner as before with
k=2
.
We also set

Q^{\prime}=\{(x,\mathbf{t})\in[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]^{d}\times[2^{-1},2]^{d}:|\widetilde{x}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1},~|(t_{1}^{a_{2}}/t_{2}^{a_{1}})^{1/(a_{2}-a_{1})}-1|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1}\}.

Then
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{\omega}f(x,\mathbf{t})\gtrsim 1
whenever
(x,\mathbf{t})\in Q^{\prime}
for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Note that for each fixed
t_{2}
, we have
|t_{1}-t_{2}^{a_{1}/a_{2}}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1}
whenever
(x,\mathbf{t})\in Q^{\prime}
.
Thus
|Q^{\prime}|\sim\varepsilon_{0}^{d+1}\lambda^{-3}
and hence
\mu(Q^{\prime})\gtrsim\varepsilon_{0}^{d+1}\min\{\lambda^{n-\alpha-3},1\}
. This gives (
4.2
).
∎

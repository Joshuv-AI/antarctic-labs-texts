Convolution identities for divisor sums and modular forms
Ksenia Fedosova, Kim Klinger-Logan, and Danylo Radchenko

Abstract
. We prove exact identities for convolution sums of divisor functions of the form ∑ n 1 ∈ ℤ ∖ { 0 , n } φ ⁡ ( n 1 , n − n 1 ) ​ σ 2 ​ m 1 ​ ( n 1 ) ​ σ 2 ​ m 2 ​ ( n − n 1 ) \sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0,n\}}\varphi(n_{1},n-n_{1})\sigma_{2m_{1}}(n_{1})\sigma_{2m_{2}}(n-n_{1}) where φ ⁡ ( n 1 , n 2 ) \varphi(n_{1},n_{2}) is a Laurent polynomial with logarithms for which the sum is absolutely convergent. Such identities are motivated by computations in string theory and prove and generalize a conjecture of Chester, Green, Pufu, Wang, and Wen from [ 6 ] . Originally, it was suspected that such sums, suitably extended to n 1 ∈ { 0 , n } n_{1}\in\{0,n\} should vanish, but in this paper we find that in general they give Fourier coefficients of holomorphic cusp forms.

expansion for the integrated correlator the cuspidal terms cancel as in (
13
), implying that these terms can be represented as lattice sums. This result further suggests that there should be a more optimal choice than generalized Eisenstein series as a basis for such computations.

2.
Jacobi functions with integer parameters

We note that in the case
\alpha,\beta,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the Jacobi functions
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
can be expressed in elementary terms and have the following characterization (compare with
[
22
, Eq. (5.7)]
).

Proposition 3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and let
P_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
denote the Jacobi polynomial defined in
[
34
, §4.1]
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\{-1,1\}
, we have

(14)

Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}}{2}P_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\log\Big|\frac{x+1}{x-1}\Big|+\frac{R(x)}{(x-1)^{\alpha}(x+1)^{\beta}},

where
R\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
is a polynomial of degree
d+\alpha+\beta-1
.
Moreover, let
F
be any function of the form

(15)

F(x)=P(x)\log\Big|\frac{x+1}{x-1}\Big|+\frac{R(x)}{(x-1)^{\alpha}(x+1)^{\beta}}

with
P,R\in\mathbb{R}[x]
, and
P
of degree
\leq d
such that
F(x)=O(x^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1})
,
x\to\infty
; then
F
must be a multiple of
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the integral representation (see
[
34
, Eq. (4.61.4)]
)

\begin{split}Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=&\frac{(x-1)^{-\alpha}(x+1)^{-\beta}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta}P_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)dt}{(x-t)}\,,\end{split}

and writing
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p(t)dt}{x-t}=p(x)\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dt}{x-t}-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p(x)-p(t)}{x-t}dt
. From (
6
) it immediately follows that

(16)

Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\sim Cx^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1}\,,\qquad x\to\infty

for some
C\neq 0
, so
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=O(x^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1})
as
x\to\infty
. Now assume that
F
is given by (
15
) and satisfies
F(x)=O(x^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1})
. Any polynomial can be written as a linear combination of Jacobi polynomials, thus

(17)

P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}P_{j}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

for some
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consider

G(x):=F(x)-2(-1)^{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}Q_{j}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\,,

where
c_{j}
are as in (
17
).
Then on one hand, (
14
), (
15
) and (
17
) imply that
G(x)
is of the form
(x-1)^{-\alpha}(x+1)^{-\beta}\widetilde{R}(x)
for some polynomial
\widetilde{R}
, and on the other hand, the assumption that
F(x)=O(x^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1})
and (
16
) imply that
G(x)=O(x^{-\alpha-\beta-1})
. This can only happen if
\widetilde{R}
vanishes, and hence
G=0
. Since
Q_{j}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\sim C_{j}x^{-j-\alpha-\beta-1}
for some
C_{j}\neq 0
and
F(x)=O(x^{-d-\alpha-\beta-1})
, we also see that
c_{j}=0
for
j=0,\dots,d-1
, and thus
F
is a multiple of
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
, as claimed.
∎

In view of the above characterization, the polynomial
R
from (
14
) can be computed from the following identity for formal Laurent series in
X

\begin{split}\frac{(1-X^{-1})^{\alpha}(1+X^{-1})^{\beta}}{2}P_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X^{-1})&\log\Big(\frac{1+X}{1-X}\Big)=R(X^{-1})+O(X)\,.\end{split}

We note that each Jacobi function can be represented in terms of the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
. More precisely, from
[
34
, Eq. (4.61.5)]
and the symmetry

Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta+d+1}Q_{d}^{(\beta,\alpha)}(-x),

we obtain

(18)

\begin{split}Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)&=\frac{2^{d+\alpha+\beta}(d+\alpha)!(d+\beta)!}{(2d+\alpha+\beta+1)!(x-1)^{\alpha}(x+1)^{d+\beta+1}}{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(d+\beta+1,d+1;2d+\alpha+\beta+2;\frac{2}{1+x}\Big)\,,\end{split}

where in order to incorporate the extension of
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
to
x\in(-1,1)
, we set for
t>1

(19)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;t):=\frac{1}{2}\big({}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;t+i0)+{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;t-i0)\big)\,.

We will use this definition of
Q_{d}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
in terms of hypergeometric functions to compute Mellin transforms of Whittaker functions in the following section. Thus we additionally need some results on the Mellin transform of the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
.
Specifically, by
[
31
, p. 314, Eq. 2.21.1.2]
,

(20)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}{\Gamma(c)}{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;1-x)x^{s-1}dx\\
&\qquad\qquad=\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s-(a+b-c))\Gamma(a-s)\Gamma(b-s)\,,\end{split}

from which one also gets

(21)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}&\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}{\Gamma(c)}{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;1+x)x^{s-1}dx\\
&\qquad\qquad=\cos(\pi s)\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s-(a+b-c))\Gamma(a-s)\Gamma(b-s)\,,\end{split}

where we define
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;t)
for
t>1
as in (
19
).

Lemma 5

.

, we obtain a polynomial in
\tfrac{n_{1}}{n}
with leading coefficient

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{r_{1}+1}}{2}\sum_{s=0}^{d}(-1)^{d}

\displaystyle\frac{(d+r_{1})!(d+r_{2})!}{s!(d-s)!(s+r_{1})!(d-s+r_{2})!}=\frac{(-1)^{r_{1}+d+1}}{2}\frac{(r_{1}+\beta+2d)!}{d!(r_{1}+r_{2}+d)!}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{n}\right)^{d}.

Comparing the leading coefficients of
\varphi_{n}(x)
and
Q_{d}^{(r_{1},r_{2})}(x)
we see that

\varphi_{n}(x)=(-1)^{r_{1}+d+1}n^{d}C_{d}\frac{2d!(r_{1}+r_{2}+d)!}{(r_{1}+r_{2}+2d)!}Q_{d}^{(r_{1},r_{2})}(x),

which fixes the values of
A_{j}
,
B_{j}
,
C_{j}
and
D_{j}
and concludes the proof.
∎

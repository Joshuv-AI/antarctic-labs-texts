Generalized quadrangles of order ( s , s 2 ) (s,s^{2}) . IV Translations, Moufang and Fong-Seitz
J. A. THAS Ghent University

Abstract
. In the period 1994-1999 Thas wrote a series of three papers on generalized quadrangles of order ( s , s 2 ) (s,s^{2}) . In this Part IV we classify all finite translation generalized quadrangles of order ( s , s 2 ) (s,s^{2}) having a kernel of size at least 3, containing a regular line not incident with the translation point. There are several applications on generalized quadrangles of order ( s , s 2 ) (s,s^{2}) having at least 2 translation points, on Moufang quadrangles, and concerning the theorem of Fong and Seitz classifying all groups with a B ​ N BN -pair of rank 2. Keywords: generalized quadrangle, projective space, translation, Moufang, generalized ovoid



1.
Introduction

In 1976 Tits
[
19
]
shows that each finite Moufang generalized quadrangle
\mathcal{S}
is classical or dual classical. This follows from the classification of all groups with a
BN
-pair of rank 2 by Fong and Seitz
[
4
,
5
]
. In their monograph ”Finite Generalized Quadrangles”, the first edition of which appeared in 1984, Payne and Thas
[
15
,
16
]
did an almost successful attempt to prove the Moufang theorem in a pure geometric way, using just some elementary group theory. As a corollary there would follow a pure geometric proof of the theorem of Fong and Seitz, namely the case where the Weyl group is
D_{8}
.

The case that was missing in the work of Payne and Thas is when the generalized quadrangle has order
(s,s^{2}),s\not=1
, and each point is a translation point. In 1991 Kantor
[
10
]
succeeded in handling this and compared to Fong and Seitz the amount of group theory employed was very small: information was required concerning a relatively restricted type of 2-transitive permutation group, together with a fact about very small degree representations of such a group. In
[
28
]
K. Thas gave an alternative proof relying on the classification of finite groups with a split
BN
-pair of rank 1, a classification independently obtained in 1972 by Hering, Kantor and Seitz
[
6
]
and Shult
[
17
]
.

In this paper we will give a pure geometric proof, without any group theory. In the even case we exclude the case where the ”kernel” has just 2 elements; we hope to be able to solve also that case. In fact we obtain much more general results, characterizing classes of generalized quadrangles which are not necessarily classical nor dual classical, and yielding as corollary the ”missing part” of our monograph (up to the mentioned exception). In particular we will classify the translation generalized quadrangles of order
(s,s^{2})
containing a regular line which is not incident with the translation point. As we start from a local situation the use of group theory is excluded. Hence, together with the results in the monograph of Payne and Thas
[
15
,
16
]
, this will provide a geometric proof, relying just on a few elementary results on groups, of a large part of the deep group theoretical result of Fong and Seitz.

Also we mention that the classification of all Moufang generalized polygons, including the infinite ones, was obtained by Tits and Weiss, and this huge achievement is the theme of their monograph ”Moufang Polygons”
[
20
]
. In this context we also mention the excellent monograph on Moufang quadrangles by De Medts
[
3
]
.

Finally we mention that in the period 1994-1999 Thas wrote a series of three papers
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
on generalized quadrangles of order
(s,s^{2})
. This paper can be considered as Part IV of that series.

2.
Finite generalized quadrangles

2.1.
Definitions

A (finite)
generalized quadrangle
(GQ) is an incidence structure
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
in which
P
and
B
are disjoint (nonempty) sets of objects called
points
and
lines
(respectively) and for which
\mathbf{I}
is a symmetric point-line incidence relation satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

Each point is incident with
1+t
lines
(t\geq 1)
and two distinct points are incident with at most one line.

(ii)

Each line is incident with
1+s
points
(s\geq 1)
and two distinct lines are incident with at most one point.

(iii)

If
x
is a point and
L
is a line not incident with
x
, then there is a unique pair
(y,M)\in P\times B
for which
x\mathbf{I}M\mathbf{I}y\mathbf{I}L
.

The integers
s
and
t
are the
parameters
of the GQ and
\mathcal{S}
is said to have
order

(s,t)
; if
s=t
, then
\mathcal{S}
is said to have
order

s
. There is a point-line duality for GQ for which in any definition or theorem the words ”point” and ”line” are interchanged and the parameters
s
and
t
are interchanged. Normally, we assume without further notice that the dual of a given theorem or definition has also been given.

Given two (not necessarily distinct) points
x,y
of the GQ
\mathcal{S}
, we write
x\sim y
and say that
x
and
y
are
collinear
, provided that there is some line
L
for which
x\mathbf{I}L\mathbf{I}y
. And
x\not\sim y
means that
x
and
y
are not collinear. Dually, for
L,M\in B
, we write
L\sim M
or
L\not\sim M
according as
L
and
M
are
concurrent
or nonconcurrent, respectively. The line (respectively point) which is incident with distinct collinear points
x,y
(respectively distinct concurrent lines
L,M
) is denoted by
xy
(respectively
LM
or
L\cap M
).

For
x\in P
, put
x^{\perp}=\{y\in P\ |\ y\sim x\}
, and note that
x\in x^{\perp}
. If
A\subseteq P
, then A
”perp”
is defined by
A^{\perp}=\cap\{x^{\perp}\ |\ x\in A\}
. So for
x,y\in P
, with
x\not=y
, we have
\{x,y\}^{\perp}=x^{\perp}\cap y^{\perp}
and
\{x,y\}^{\perp\perp}=\{u\in P\ |\ u\in z^{\perp}\ \forall z\in x^{\perp}\cap y^{\perp}\}
. So
|\{x,y\}^{\perp}|=s+1
or
t+1
according as
x\sim y
or
x\not\sim y
, and
|\{x,y\}^{\perp\perp}|=s+1
or
|\{x,y\}^{\perp\perp}|\leq t+1
according as
x\sim y
or
x\not\sim y
.

A
triad
(of points) is a triple of pairwise noncollinear points. Given a triad
T=\{x,y,z\}
, a
center
of
T
is just a point of
T^{\perp}
.

For terminology, notations, results, etc., concerning finite generalized quadrangles and not explicitly given here, see the monograph of Payne and Thas
[
15
,
16
]
.

2.2.
Restrictions on parameters

Let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a GQ of order
(s,t)
. If
|P|=v
and
|B|=b
, then
v=(s+1)(st+1)
and
b=(t+1)(st+1)
; see 1.2.1 of
[
15
,
16
]
. Also,
s+t
divides
st(s+1)(t+1)
; see 1.2.2 of
[
15
,
16
]
.

If
s>1
and
t>1
, then
t\leq s^{2}
, and dually
s\leq t^{2}
; see 1.2.3 of
[
15
,
16
]
. Also, for
s>1
and
t>1
,
s^{2}=t
if and only if each triad (of points) has a constant number of centers, in which case this constant number of centers is
s+1
; see 1.2.4 of
[
15
,
16
]
.

2.3.
Regularity

Let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a GQ of order
(s,t)
. If
x\sim y,x\not=y
, or if
x\not\sim y
and
|\{x,y\}^{\perp\perp}|=t+1
, we say that the pair
\{x,y\}
is
regular
. The point
x
is
regular
provided
\{x,y\}
is regular for all
y\in P,y\not=x
. A point
x
is
coregular
provided each line incident with
x
is regular.

If
\mathcal{S}
contains a regulair pair
\{x,y\}
, then either
s=1
or
s\geq t
; see 1.3.6 of
[
15
,
16
]
. A coregular point in a GQ of order
s,s>1,
is regular if and only
s
is even; see 1.5.2 of
[
15
,
16
]
.

3.
Translation generalized quadrangles

3.1.
Elations, translations, symmetries

Let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a GQ of order
(s,t)
, with
s\not=1,t\not=1
. A collineation
\theta
of
\mathcal{S}
is an
elation
about the point
p
if
\theta
= id or if
\theta
fixes all lines incident with
p
and fixes no point of
P\setminus p^{\perp}
. If there is a group
H
of elations about
p
acting regularly on
P\setminus p^{\perp}
, we say
\mathcal{S}
is an
elation generalized quadrangle
(EGQ) with
elation group

H
and
base point

p
. Briefly we say that
(\mathcal{S}^{(p)},H)
or
\mathcal{S}^{(p)}
is an EGQ. If the group
H
is abelian, then we say that the EGQ
(\mathcal{S}^{(p)},H)
is a
translation generalized quadrangle
(TGQ) with
base point
or
translation point

p
and
translation group

H
. In such a case
H
is the set of all elations about
p
; see 8.6.4 of
[
15
,
09
]
. For any TGQ
\mathcal{S}^{(p)}
the point
p
is coregular, hence the parameters
s
and
t
of a TGQ satisfy
s\leq t
; see Section 2.3. Also, by 8.5.2 of
[
15
,
16
]
, for any TGQ with
s\not=t
we have
s=q^{a}
and
t=q^{a+1}
with
q
a prime power and
a
an odd integer; if
s
(or
t
) is even, then by 8.6.1(iv) of
[
15
,
16
]
either
s=t
or
s^{2}=t
.

If a collineation
\theta
of the GQ
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
of order
(s,t),s\not=1\not=t
, fixes each line of
L^{\perp}
, with
L\in B
, then
\theta
is a
symmetry
about
L
. By 8.1 of
[
15
,
09
]
any symmetry about
L
is an elation about
L
. The line
L
is called an
axis of symmetry
provided its group of symmetries has order
s
. If
\mathcal{S}
is a TGQ with translation point
p
, then the lines
L_{0},L_{1},\ldots,L_{t}
incident with
p
are axes of symmetry and the translation group
G
contains the
t+1
full groups of symmetries about these lines; in fact
G
is generated by these symmetries; see 8.3.1 of
[
15
,
09
]
. Conversely, if
p
is a point of the GQ
\mathcal{S}
for which
t+1
lines incident with
p
are axes of symmetry, then
(\mathcal{S}^{(P},G)
is a TGQ, where
G
is generated by the symmetries about the lines through
p
; see 8.3.1 of
[
15
,
16
]
.

For a detailed study of TGQ we refer to the monograph ”Translation Generalized Quadrangles” by Thas, Thas and Van Maldeghem
[
27
]
.

3.2.
The projective model

In
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
consider a set
O=O(n,m,q)
of
q^{m}+1

(n-1)
-dimensional subspaces
\mathbf{PG}^{(0)}(n-1,q),\mathbf{PG}^{(1)}(n-1,q),\ldots,\mathbf{PG}^{(q^{m})}(n-1,q)
, every three of which generate a
\mathbf{PG}(3n-1,q)
and such that each element
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n-1,q)
of
O(n,m,q)
is contained in a
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n+m-1,q)
having no point in common with any
\mathbf{PG}^{(j)}(n-1,q)
, for
j\not=i
. It is easy to check that
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n+m-1,q)
is uniquely determined,
i=0,1,\ldots,q^{m}
. The space
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n+m-1,q)
is called the
tangent space
of
O(n,m,q)
at
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n-1,q)
. For
n=m
such a set
O(n,n,q)
is called a
pseudo-oval
or a
generalized oval
or an
[n-1]
-oval
of
\mathbf{PG}(3n-1,q)
; a generalized oval of
\mathbf{PG}(2,q)
is just an oval of
\mathbf{PG}(2,q)
. For
n\not=m
such a set
O(n,m,q)
is called a
pseudo-ovoid
or a
generalized ovoid
or an
[n-1]
-ovoid
or an
egg
of
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
; a
[0]
-ovoid of
\mathbf{PG}(3,q)

\mathbf{PG}(3,q)

Now embed
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
in a
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m,q)
and construct a point-line geometry
T(n,m,q)=T(O)
as follows.

Points are of three types:

(i)

the points of
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m,q)\setminus\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
;

(ii)

the
(n+m)
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m,q)
wich intersect
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
in one of the spaces
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n+m-1,q)
;

(iii)

the symbol
(\infty)
.

Lines are of two types:

(a)

the
n
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m,q)
which intersect
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m-1,q)
in one of the spaces
\mathbf{PG}^{(i)}(n-1,q)
;

(b)

the elements of
O(n,m,q)
.

Incidence in
T(n,m,q)
is defined as follows. A point of type (i) is incident only with lines of type (a); here the incidence is that of
\mathbf{PG}(2n+m,q)
. A point of type (ii) is incident with all lines of type (a) contained in it and with the unique element of
O(n,m,q)
contained in it. The point
(\infty)
is incident with no line of type (a) and with all lines of type (b).

Theorem 3.1

(8.7.1 of Payne and Thas
[

,
16
]
)
.

The point-line geometry

Corollary 3.2
.

The following hold for every

(i)

n=m
or
n(a+1)=ma
with

odd;

(ii)

if

or
m=2n
.

Each TGQ
\mathcal{S}
of order

, has a

\mathbb{K}
,
where

fixing linewise the point

and any given point not collinear with

; see 8.6.5 of Payne and Thas

]
.

We have

; see Payne and Thas
[

,
16
]

(q)

if and only if

is isomorphic to a

[

,
16
]
.

Consider any GQ
T(n,m,q)=T(O)
, with

and

odd or with

or 3.9 and 3.10 of

Let

5.
Moufang quadrangles

5.1.
Moufang conditions

Let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a GQ of order
(s,t)
. For a fixed point
p
define the following condition.

(M)_{p}
:
For any two distinct lines
A
and
B
of
\mathcal{S}
incident with
p
, the group of collineations of
\mathcal{S}
fixing
A
and
B
pointwise and
p
linewise is transitive on the set of lines distinct from
A
and incident with a given point
x
on
A
, with
x\not=p
.

The GQ
\mathcal{S}
is said to satisfy condition
(M)
provided it satsifies
(M)_{p}
for all points
p\in P
. For a fixed line
L\in B
let
(\widehat{M})_{L}
be the condition that is the dual of
(M)_{p}
, and let
(\widehat{M})
be the dual of
(M)
. If
s\not=1\not=t
and
\mathcal{S}
satisfies both
(M)
and
(\widehat{M})
it is said to be a
Moufang
GQ; if
s\not=1\not=t
and
\mathcal{S}
satisfies either
(M)
or
(\widehat{M})
it is said to be a
half Moufang
GQ.

A celebrated result of Tits
[
19
]
is that all finite Moufang GQ are classical or dual classical. In fact, Tits observes that his result easily follows from the classification of all groups with a
BN
-pair of rank 2 by Fong and Seitz
[
4
,
5
]
. A huge achievement of Tits and Weiss
[
20
]
is the classification of all (finite and infinite) Moufang GQ. In
[
26
]
Thas, Payne and Van Maldeghem prove that half Moufang implies Moufang for finite GQ; in
[
18
]
Tent proves the analogue for infinite GQ.

In their monograph Payne and Thas
[
15
,
16
]
did an almost successful attempt to prove the Moufang theorem (in the finite case) in a pure geometric way. To complete a geometric proof of the theorem of Tits (in the finite case) it would be sufficient to show in a geometric way that if, for each point
p
of
\mathcal{S}
, the GQ
\mathcal{S}^{(p)}
is a TGQ of order
(s,s^{2}),s\not=1
, then
\mathcal{S}
is isomorphic to the classical GQ
Q(5,s)
arising from a non-singular elliptic quadric in
\mathbf{PG}(5,s)
. As a corollary there would also follow a pure geometric proof of a large part of the theorem of Fong and Seitz, namely in the case where the Weyl group is
D_{8}
.

That ”missing part” will be a corollary of the much stronger results that follow.

6.
Translation generalized quadrangles of order
(s,s^{2})
with a regular line not containing the base point

6.1.
Introduction

In this section let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a TGQ of order
(s,s^{2}),s\not=1
, with base point
(\infty)
, and having a regular line
M
not incident with
(\infty)
. Assume that
\mathcal{S}=T(n,2n,q)=T(O)
, with
O=\{\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q^{2n}}\}
, and where
M
is concurrent with the line
\pi_{0}
of
\mathcal{S}
. By transitivity it is clear that all lines concurrent with
\pi_{0}
are regular. The projective space containing

will be denoted by
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
and the projective space containing

by
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
. The tangent space of

at
\pi_{i}
will be denoted by

, with

. Further, let
\widetilde{O}=\pi_{0}\cup\pi_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\pi_{q^{2n}}
.

6.2.
Conics on
\widetilde{O}

Consider lines

of
\mathcal{S}
, with
L_{0}\!\not\!\mathbf{I}(\infty)\!\not\!\mathbf{I}L_{1},L_{0}\sim\pi_{0},L_{1}\sim\pi_{1},L_{0}\not\sim L_{1}
. Let
\{L_{0},L_{1}\}^{\perp}=\{M_{0},M_{1},\ldots,M_{q^{n}}\},\{L_{0},L_{1}\}^{\perp\perp}=\{L_{0},L_{1},\ldots,L_{q^{n}}\}
and assume that notations are chosen in such a way that

, with
i=0,1,\ldots q^{n}
. Then
\pi_{i},L_{i},M_{i}
contain a common point

of
\mathcal{S}
, with

a
3n
-dimensional space containing
\tau_{i},i=0,1,\ldots,q^{n}
. So the
(n+1)
-dimensional space
\langle L_{i},M_{i}\rangle
generated by
L_{i}
and
M_{i}
intersects
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
in an
n
-dimensional space
\gamma_{i}
containing
\pi_{i}
and contained in
\tau_{i}

\Sigma^{\star}_{i}
.

Let
x^{\star}_{0}\in\tau^{\star}_{0}
and let
\eta^{\star}_{i}\in\Sigma^{\star}_{i}
contain
x^{\star}_{0}
. Then
(x^{\star}_{0})^{\alpha_{ij}}\in\eta^{\star}_{i}
. Let
\eta^{\star}_{j}\in\Sigma^{\star}_{j}
contain
(x^{\star}_{0})^{\alpha_{ij}}
. Then
(x^{\star}_{0})^{\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{ji}}=x^{\star}_{0}\in\eta^{\star}_{j}
. The spaces
\eta^{\star}_{i}
and
\eta^{\star}_{j}
are the spaces at infinity of a common
(n-1)
-regulus. Hence
\eta^{\star}_{i}=\eta^{\star}_{j}
. So
\Sigma^{\star}_{i}=\Sigma^{\star}_{j}=\Sigma^{\star}
.

Consider again
(\Pi,\Gamma)
, with
\Pi=\{\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q^{n}}\},\Gamma=\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{q^{n}}\}
, and let
(\Pi,\Gamma)
be determined by
\{\gamma_{0},\pi_{1}\}
. The corresponding
\Pi_{0}
-conics, defined by the
\pi_{0}
-conics in
\bar{\xi}_{i}
, are
C^{i}_{1},C^{i}_{2},\ldots,C^{i}_{q^{n-1}}
, with
C^{i}_{1}=\{\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q}\}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be the
\Pi_{0}
-conic in
\Pi
containing
\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{k}
, with
k\in\{q+1,q+2,\ldots,q^{n}\}
. We have:
\gamma_{0}\subset\tau_{0},\gamma_{0}
intersects
\langle\pi_{1},\pi_{k}\rangle
by
6.10
, so
\gamma_{0}\subset\langle\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{k}\rangle
, and hence the tangent space
\eta^{\prime\prime}
of
C^{\prime}
at
\pi_{0}
, that is
\langle\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{k}\rangle\cap\tau_{0}
, contains
\gamma_{0}
.

There is a
\pi_{0}
-conic
\mathcal{C}
intersecting
\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{k}
and having its tangent line at
\mathcal{C}\cap\pi_{0}
in
\gamma_{0}
. Projecting
\mathcal{C}
there arises a line
L
of
\Phi
. Assume that
L
is in
\xi_{l}
, so
\eta^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma^{\star}_{l}=\Sigma^{\star}
. Hence
\eta^{\prime\prime}
is tangent space at
\pi_{0}
of each of the
\Pi_{0}
-conics defined by
\{\gamma_{0},\pi_{1}\}
. So
\eta^{\prime\prime}
is the common tangent space at
\pi_{0}
of the
\Pi_{0}
-conics defined by all triples
\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{k}
, and hence clearly of all
\Pi_{0}
-conics contained in
\Pi
. It follows that all
\Pi_{0}
-conics in
\Pi
are contained in the
(3n-1)
-dimensional space
\langle\eta^{\prime\prime},\pi_{0}\rangle
.

Each
\Pi_{0}
-conic
C^{\prime\prime}
is contained in a
(\Pi,\Gamma)
-set. Hence the
(3n-1)
-dimensional space
\langle C^{\prime\prime}\rangle
contains
q^{n}+1
elements of
O
.

We conclude that
O
is good at
\pi_{0}
.
\blacksquare

Theorem 6.22
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=T(n,2n,q)
, with
q\not=2
, be a translation generalized quadrangle with base point
(\infty)
, and having a regular line
L
not incident wit
(\infty)
.

(i)

If
q
is odd, then
\mathcal{S}
is the point-line dual of the translation dual of a semifield flock translation generalized quadrangle.

(ii)

If
q
is even, then
\mathcal{S}
is classical.

Proof
Let
\mathcal{S}=T(O)
, with
O=\{\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q{2n}}\}
and where the regular line
L
is concurrent with
\pi_{0}
. By
6.21
the pseudo-ovoid
O
is good at
\pi_{0}
.

If
q
is odd, then by
4.3
the GQ
\mathcal{S}
is the point-line dual of the translation dual of a semifield flock TGQ.

Let
q
be even, let
T(O)
be contained in
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
and let
\langle O\rangle=\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
. Further, let
\langle\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\rangle=\mathbf{PG}(3n-1,q)
and let
\langle L,\mathbf{PG}(3n-1,q)\rangle=\mathbf{PG}(3n,q)
. In
\mathbf{PG}(3n,q)
a subquadrangle
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=T(O^{\prime})
of order
q^{n}
is induced, with
O^{\prime}
the pseudo-oval consisting of the
q^{n}+1
elements of
O
in
\mathbf{PG}(3n-1,q)
.The line
L
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is regular, so by the action of the translation group of the TGQ
T(O^{\prime})
(with base point
(\infty)
) on
L
we see that all lines of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
concurrent with
\pi_{0}
are regular. Now by 1.3.6(iv) of
[
16
]
, all lines of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are regular. Hence, by the well-known theorem of Benson, see 5.2.1 of
[
16
]
, the GQ

is isomorphic to the classical GQ arising from a nonsingular quadric of
\mathbf{PG}(4,q^{n})

[
1
]
it follows that
\mathcal{S}
is isomorphic to the classical GQ arising from a nonsingular elliptic quadric of
\mathbf{PG}(5,q^{n})
; see also 7.8 of
[
27
]

\blacksquare

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\mathbf{I})
be a TGQ of order
(s,t)
, with
s\not=1\not=t
, and
s\not=t
, having
(\infty)

\mathcal{S}
has a regular line
L
not incident with
(\infty)
, then

. Hence if the kernel has more than two elements, then

can be applied.

Proof
By 3.2 we know that for
s
or
t
even we necessarily have
t=s^{2}
. So from now on assume that
s
and
t
are odd. Let
L
be concurrent with the line
\pi_{0}\in O
of
\mathcal{S}=T(O)=T(n,m,q)
. Similar to the case

we prove that any

-dimensional space containing

and two distinct elements

, contains exactly

elements of

. Notice that

Approximate solutions of interval-valued optimization problems
NGUYEN VAN TUYEN 1

Abstract
. This paper deals with approximate solutions of an optimization problem with interval-valued objective function. Four types of approximate solution concepts of the problem are proposed by considering the partial ordering L ​ U LU on the set of all closed and bounded intervals. We show that these solutions exist under very weak conditions. Under suitable constraint qualifications, we derive Karush–Kuhn–Tucker necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for convex interval-valued optimization problems.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in approximate solutions of the following constrained interval-valued optimization problem:

(P)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{min}\,f(x)\\
&\text{s. t.}\ \ x\in X:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,g_{j}(x)\leq 0,j=1,\ldots,m\},\end{split}

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{K}_{c}
is an interval-valued function defined by
f(x)=[f^{L}(x),f^{U}(x)]
,
f^{L},

f^{U}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
are real-valued functions satisfying
f^{L}(x)\leq f^{U}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{K}_{c}
denote the class of all closed and bounded intervals in
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{K}_{c}=\{[a^{L},a^{U}]\,:\,a^{L},a^{U}\in\mathbb{R},a^{L}\leq a^{U}\},

g_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
j\in J:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, are real-valued constraint functions.

The interval-valued optimization problems recently have received increasing interest in optimization community; see, e.g.,
[
7
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
and references therein. The reason for this is that many problems in decision making, engineering and economics are affected by risk and uncertainty; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
14
,
15
,
20
]
. Hence, we usually cannot determine exactly the coefficients of objective functions in such problems. If the coefficients of objective functions are taken as closed intervals, we obtain interval-valued optimization problems of the form (
P
). These problems may provide an alternative choice for considering optimization problems with uncertain or imprecise data.

In interval-valued optimization, it is important to compare intervals by means of interval order relations. There is a variety of interval order relations known in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
15
]
. The well know lower-upper (
LU
) interval order relation and center-width (
CW
) one are introduced by Ishibuchi and Tanaka
[
11
]
. The lower-spread (
LS
) interval order relation was proposed by Chalco-Cano et. al.
[
7
]
. For these interval order relations, the corresponding solution concepts for the optimization problem with interval-valued objective function are introduced and studied.

As a mainstream in the study of interval-valued optimization problems, Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions for interval-valued optimization problems have attracted the attention of many researchers; see, e.g.,
[
7
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
and the references therein. However, to the best of our
knowledge, so far there have been no papers investigating optimality
conditions of KKT-type for approximate solutions of interval-valued optimization problems. It should be noted that, in general optimization problems, the study of approximate solutions is very
important because, from the computational point of view, numerical algorithms usually generate only approximate solutions because they stop after a finite number of steps. Furthermore, approximate solutions exist under very weak assumptions; see, e.g.,
[
6
,
13
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

In this paper, we focus for the first time on studying the existence and optimality conditions of KKT-type for approximate solutions of interval-valued optimization problems. We first introduce in the next section four kinds of approximate solutions with respect to
LU
interval order relation of (
P
). Then we show that the new concepts of approximate solutions are closed related to the approximate efficient solutions of multiobjective optimization problems in the sense of Loridan
[
13
]
. Section
3
is devoted to study the existence of proposed approximate solutions. In Section
4
, we establish KKT necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for approximate solutions to convex interval-valued optimization problems of the form (
P
) under suitable constraint qualifications.

2.
APPROXIMATE SOLUTIONS

We use the following notation and terminology. Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. The space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped with the usual scalar product and Euclidean norm. We denote the nonnegative orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. The topological closure, the topological interior and the convex hull of a subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{cl}\,{A}
,
\mathrm{int}\,{A}
and
\mathrm{conv}\,A
. The conical hull of
A
is defined by

\mathrm{cone}\,A:=\{\lambda x\,:\,\lambda\geq 0,x\in\mathrm{conv}\,A\}.

Let
A=[a^{L},a^{U}]
and
B=[b^{L},b^{U}]
be two intervals in
\mathcal{K}_{c}
. Then, by definition, we have

(i)

A+B=\{a+b\,:\,a\in A,b\in B\}=[a^{L}+b^{L},a^{U}+b^{U}]
;

(ii)

A-B=\{a-b\,:\,a\in A,b\in B\}=[a^{L}-b^{U},a^{U}-b^{L}]
.

We also see that

kA=\{ka\,:\,a\in A\}=\begin{cases}[ka^{L},ka^{U}]\ \ \text{if}\ \ k\geq 0,\\
[ka^{U},ka^{L}]\ \ \text{if}\ \ k<0,\end{cases}

where
k
is a real number, see
[
1
,
14
,
15
]
for more details.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Hausdorff metric
between
A
and
B
is defined by

d_{H}(A,B):=\max\bigg\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|,\sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}\|a-b\|\bigg\}.

Let
\{A_{n}\}
and
A
be closed and bounded intervals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that the sequence
\{A_{n}\}
converges to
A
, denoted by

\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=A,

if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
n\geq N
, we have
d_{H}(A_{n},A)<\varepsilon
.

We recall here the definitions of the
LU
interval order relation in
\mathcal{K}_{c}
and the corresponding solution concepts of (
P
).

Definition 2.1

(see
[
11
,
27
]
)
.

Let
A=[a^{L},a^{U}]
and
B=[b^{L},b^{U}]
be two intervals in
\mathcal{K}_{c}
. We say that:

(i)

A\preceq_{LU}B
if
a^{L}\leq b^{L}
and
a^{U}\leq b^{U}
.

(ii)

A\prec_{LU}B
if
A\preceq_{LU}B
and
A\neq B
, or, equivalently,
A\prec_{LU}B
if

\begin{cases}a^{L}<b^{L}\\
a^{U}\leq b^{U},\end{cases}

or
\begin{cases}a^{L}\leq b^{L}\\
a^{U}<b^{U},\end{cases}

or
\begin{cases}a^{L}<b^{L}\\
a^{U}<b^{U}.\end{cases}

(iii)

A\prec^{s}_{LU}B
if
a^{L}<b^{L}
and
a^{U}<b^{U}
.

Definition 2.2

(see
[
28
]
)
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. We say that

(i)

x^{*}
is an
{LU}
-solution of (

f(x)\prec_{LU}f(x^{*}).

(ii)

The set of weakly

and
\mathcal{S}\eqref{problem}
. Clearly,

\mathcal{S}\eqref{problem}\subset\mathcal{S}^{w}\eqref{problem}.

We now introduce approximate solutions of (

) with respect to

be two real numbers satisfying

.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. We say that:

(i)

x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-

-solution
of (

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

x^{*}

\mathcal{E}
-quasi-

We denote the set of

-

-solutions
,
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution, weakly
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-

-solutions) of (

\mathcal{E}
-
\mathcal{S}
(
P

(

\mathrm{Min}\,_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}\{\widetilde{F}(x)\,:\,x\in X\},

-efficient solution

(ii)

x^{*}
is a

\epsilon
-efficient solution
of (

x^{*}

\epsilon

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\epsilon:=(\epsilon^{U},\epsilon^{L})
. A point
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
) if and only if
x^{*}
is an
\epsilon
-efficient solution of the following multiobjective optimization problem:

(MP)

\mathrm{Min}\,_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\{F(x)\,:\,x\in X\},

where
F(x):=(f^{L}(x),f^{U}(x))
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
x^{*}
be an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
). Then, there is no
x\in X
satisfying

(1)

f(x)\prec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E}.

We claim that
x^{*}
is an
\epsilon
-efficient solution of (
MP
). Indeed, if otherwise, then there exists
\bar{x}\in X
such that

F(\bar{x})\in F(x^{*})-\epsilon-\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

or, equivalently,

\begin{cases}f^{L}(\bar{x})&\leq f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U},\\
f^{U}(\bar{x})&\leq f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L},\end{cases}

with at least one strict inequality. Hence,
f(\bar{x})\prec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E}
, which contradicts to (
1
).

Conversely, let
x^{*}
be an
\epsilon
-efficient solution of (
MP
). Then, there is no
x\in X
such that

F(x)\in F(x^{*})-\epsilon-\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\}.

This means that there is no
x\in X
satisfying

\begin{cases}f^{L}(x)&\leq f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U},\\
f^{U}(x)&\leq f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L},\end{cases}

with at least one strict inequality. This implies that

f(x)\prec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E}.

Hence,
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
).
∎

3.
EXISTENCE THEOREMS

In this section, we assume that
X
is a nonempty and closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the function
f
is
LU
-bounded from below on

X
if there exists an interval
B=[b^{L},b^{U}]\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
such that

B\preceq_{LU}f(x),\ \ \forall x\in X.

By definition, it is easily seen that the interval-valued function
f
is
LU
-bounded from below on
X
if and only if the function
f^{L}
is bounded from below on
X
.

Theorem 3.1

(Existence of
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solutions)
.

Assume that
f
is
LU
-bounded from below on
X
by an interval
B\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
. Then, for each
\mathcal{E}=[\epsilon^{L},\epsilon^{U}]\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
satisfying
0\prec_{LU}\mathcal{E}
, the problem (
P
) admits at least one
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution.

Proof.

Let
x^{0}\in X
and put

[f(X)]_{f(x^{0})}:=\{A\in f(X)\,:\,A\preceq_{LU}f(x^{0})\},

where
f(X):=\{f(x)\,:\,x\in X\}
.
We first claim that there exists a point
x^{*}\in f^{-1}([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})})
such that

(2)

f(x)\nprec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E},\ \ \forall x\in f^{-1}\big([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})}\big),

where
f^{-1}([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})}):=\{x\in X\,:\,f(x)\in[f(X)]_{f(x^{0})}\}
. Indeed, if such a point
x^{*}
does not exist, we can find a sequence
\{x^{k}\}\subset f^{-1}([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})})
such that

f(x^{k})\prec_{LU}f(x^{k-1})-\mathcal{E},\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Summarizing these inequalities up to
k
, we obtain

f(x^{k})\prec_{LU}f(x^{0})-k\mathcal{E},\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

or, equivalently,

(3)

\frac{1}{k}f(x^{k})\prec_{LU}\frac{1}{k}f(x^{0})-\mathcal{E},\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Due to the construction of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
and the the
LU
-boundedness from below on
X
of
f
, we have

B\preceq_{LU}f(x^{k})\prec_{LU}f(x^{0}),\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence,
\frac{1}{k}f(x^{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Then letting
k\to\infty
in (
3
), we obtain
\mathcal{E}\preceq_{LU}0
, which contradicts to the fact that
0\prec_{LU}\mathcal{E}
.

We now prove that (
2
) holds also for
x\in X\setminus f^{-1}([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})})
. Indeed, if otherwise, then there exists
x\in X\setminus f^{-1}([f(X)]_{f(x^{0})})
such that

f(x)\prec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E}.

Combining this with the fact that
f(x^{*})\preceq_{LU}f(x^{0})
, we obtain
f(x)\prec_{LU}f(x^{0})
, a contradiction. Therefore,

f(x)\nprec_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E},\ \ \forall x\in X.

This means that
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
).
∎

Example 3.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathcal{K}_{c}
be an interval-valued function defined by

f(x)=[f^{L}(x),f^{U}(x)]=[x_{1}^{2}+(x_{1}x_{2}-1)^{2},2x_{1}^{2}+(x_{1}x_{2}-1)^{2}]

for all
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
X=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then we have

0<f^{L}(x)\leq f^{U}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Hence,
f
is bounded from below on
X
. We claim that the problem (
P
) has no weakly
LU
-solution. Indeed, let
x^{*}
be an arbitrary point in
X
. Then,
0<f^{L}(x^{*})\leq f^{U}(x^{*})
. Let
\{x^{k}\}
be a sequence defined by
x^{k}=(\frac{1}{k},k)
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f^{L}(x^{k})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}=0<f^{L}(x^{*}),

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f^{U}(x^{k})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{2}{k^{2}}=0<f^{U}(x^{*}).

This implies that there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle f^{L}(x^{k})<f^{L}(x^{*}),

\displaystyle f^{U}(x^{k})<f^{U}(x^{*}),

for all
k\geq K
. Hence,
x^{*}
is not a weakly
LU
-solution of (
P
). This means that the set
\mathcal{S}^{w}\eqref{problem}
is empty. Consequently,
\mathcal{S}\eqref{problem}
is empty.

However, by Theorem
3.1
, for all
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
,
0\prec_{LU}\mathcal{E}
, (
P
) has at least an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution. Consequently, (
P
) admits at least one weakly
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution.

We say that the function
f
is
lower-semicontinuous
if
f^{L}
and
f^{U}
are lower-semicontinuous functions.

Theorem 3.2

(Existence of
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solutions)
.

Assume that
f
is lower-semicontinuous and
LU
-bounded from below on
X
by an interval
B\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
. Then, for every
0\prec^{s}_{LU}\mathcal{E}
, the problem (
P
) admits at least one
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we need the following vectorial Ekeland’s variational principle.

Lemma 3.1

(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
Y
a Banach space. Assume that
C\subset Y
is a closed, convex and pointed cone with
\mathrm{int}\,C\neq\emptyset
. Let
k^{0}\in\mathrm{int}\,C
and let
F\colon X\to Y
be a vector-valued function. For every
\varepsilon>0
there is an initial point
x_{0}\in X
such that
F(X)\cap(F(x_{0})-\varepsilon k^{0}-\mathrm{int}\,C)=\emptyset
and
F
satisfies

\{x^{\prime}\in X\,:\,F(x^{\prime})+d(x^{\prime},x)k^{0}\in F(x)-C\}\ \ \text{is closed for every}\ \ x\in X.

Then there exists
\bar{x}\in X
such that

(i)

F(\bar{x})\in F(x_{0})-\mathrm{int}\,C
,

(ii)

d(x_{0},\bar{x})\leq 1

(iii)

F(x)\notin F(\bar{x})-\varepsilon d(x,\bar{x})k^{0}-C
for all
x\neq\bar{x}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
x^{0}\in X
. Then, by Theorem
3.1
, the problem (
P
) has at least an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution, say
x^{*}
, satisfying
f(x^{*})\preceq_{LU}f(x^{0})
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x^{*}
is an
\epsilon
-efficient solution of (
MP
) and so is a weakly
\epsilon
-efficient solution of (
MP
), where
\epsilon=(\epsilon^{U},\epsilon^{L})
. Consequently,

F(X)\cap[F(x^{*})-\epsilon-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}]\ =\ \emptyset.

By the lower-semicontinuity of
f^{L},f^{U}
and the closedness of
X
, it is easy to see that for each
x\in X
the following set

\{u\in X\,:\,F(u)+\epsilon\|u-x\|\in F(x)-{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}

is closed. By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a point
\bar{x}\in X
such that
F(\bar{x})\in F(x^{*})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

F(x)\notin F(\bar{x})-\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|-\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\ \ \forall x\in X\setminus\{\bar{x}\}.

Hence,

F(x)\notin F(\bar{x})-\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|-\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\},\ \ \forall x\in X,

or, equivalently,
\bar{x}
is an
\epsilon
-quasi-efficient solution of (
MP
). Thus,
\bar{x}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
) due to Lemma
2.2
. The proof is complete.

.

Let

\mathcal{S}^{w}\eqref{problem}
are nonempty for all
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{K}_{c}
satisfying
0\prec^{s}_{LU}\mathcal{E}
.

4.
KKT OPTIMALITY CONDITIONS

In this section, we assume that
f^{L}
,
f^{U}
, and
g_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, are convex functions. Since every real-valued convex function is continuous, the constraint set
X
is closed and convex. In order to present optimality conditions for approximate solutions of (
P
), we recall some notations and basic results from convex analysis.

4.1.
The approximate subdifferential

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex function. The
conjugate function
of
\varphi
,
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, is defined by

\varphi^{*}(v):=\sup\{\langle v,x\rangle-\varphi(x)\,:\,x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi\}.

For
\varepsilon\geq 0
the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
x^{*}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi
is given by:

\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x^{*}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\varphi(x)-\varphi(x^{*})\geq\langle v,x-x^{*}\rangle-\varepsilon,\ \ \forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi\}.

When
\varepsilon=0
,
\partial_{0}\varphi(x^{*})
coincides with
\partial\varphi(x^{*})
, the subdifferential of
\varphi
at
x^{*}
(see, e.g.,
[
16
,
9
]
). It is well-known that

\partial_{\mu\varepsilon}(\mu\varphi(x^{*}))=\mu\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x^{*}),\ \ \forall\mu>0.

Lemma 4.1

(Sum rule
[
9
, Theorem 2.115]
)
.

Consider two proper convex functions
\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,2
, such that
\mathrm{ri}\,\mathrm{dom}\varphi_{1}\cap\mathrm{ri}\,\mathrm{dom}\varphi_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Then for
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
x\in\mathrm{dom}\varphi_{1}\cap\mathrm{dom}\varphi_{2}
,

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(x)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon_{1},\;\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\big(\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x)+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(x)\big).

We say that the constrain set
X
satisfies the
Slater constraint qualification
if there exists
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
g_{j}(\hat{x})<0
, for all
j\in J
. The following result gives necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for a feasible point to be an approximate solution of a convex programming problem.

Theorem 4.1

(See
[
9
, Theorem 10.9]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function and let
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Assume that
X
satisfies the Slater constraint qualification. Then
x^{*}\in X
is a
\varepsilon
-solution of
\varphi
on
X
, i.e.,
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(x^{*})-\varepsilon
for all
x\in X
, if and only if there exist
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, such that

0\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{0}}\varphi(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\lambda_{j}g_{j})(x^{*})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum_{j=0}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x^{*}).

4.2.
KKT conditions for weakly
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solutions

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. Then
x^{*}
is a weakly-
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}=1
such that

(4)

\mu^{L}f^{L}(x)+\mu^{U}f^{U}(x)\geq\mu^{L}f^{L}(x^{*})+\mu^{U}f^{U}(x^{*})-\mu^{L}\epsilon^{U}-\mu^{U}\epsilon^{L},\ \ \forall x\in X.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
x^{*}
is a weakly-
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
) if and only if
x^{*}
a weakly
\epsilon
-efficient solution (
MP
), where
\epsilon:=(\epsilon^{U},\epsilon^{L})
. This is equivalent to the inconsistent of the following system

\begin{cases}&f^{L}(x)<f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U},\\
&f^{U}(x)<f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L},\\
&x\in X.\end{cases}

By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, the above system is inconsistent if and only if there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}=1
such that

\mu^{L}[f^{L}(x)-f^{L}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{U}]+\mu^{U}[f^{U}(x)-f^{U}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{L}]\geq 0,\ \ \forall x\in X,

or, equivalently, (
4
) is valid.
∎

The following result gives KKT necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for a feasible point to be a weakly-
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. Assume that
X
satisfies the Slater constraint qualification. Then
x^{*}
is a weakly-
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}=1
,
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, such that

0\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\mu^{L}f^{L}+\mu^{U}f^{U})(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\lambda_{j}g_{j})(x^{*})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum_{j=0}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-\mu^{L}\epsilon^{U}-\mu^{U}\epsilon^{L}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x^{*}).

Proof.

The proof is directly from Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
4.1
, so omitted.
∎

4.3.
KKT conditions for
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solutions

For each
x^{*}\in X
, denote

X(x^{*},\mathcal{E}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,f(x)\preceq_{LU}f(x^{*})-\mathcal{E}\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. Then
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
) if and only if
X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})=\emptyset
or,

(5)

f^{L}(x)+f^{U}(x)=f^{L}(x^{*})+f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}-\epsilon^{L},\ \ \forall x\in X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E}).

Proof.

We will follow the proof scheme of
[
12
, Proposition 8.1]
(see also
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
).

(\Rightarrow)
: Let
x^{*}
be an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
). Then, by Lemma
2.1
,
x^{*}
is an
\epsilon
-efficient solution of (
MP
), where
\epsilon=(\epsilon^{U},\epsilon^{L})
. This means that there is no
x\in X
such that

\begin{cases}f^{L}(x)&\leq f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\\
f^{U}(x)&\leq f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\end{cases}

with at least one strict inequality. Hence,
X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})=\emptyset
or,

\begin{cases}f^{L}(x)&=f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\\
f^{U}(x)&=f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\end{cases}

for all
x\in X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})
and we therefore get (
5
).

(\Leftarrow)
: Clearly, if
X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})=\emptyset
, then
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
). We now assume that
X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})\neq\emptyset
and (
5
) holds. Suppose to the contrary that
x^{*}
is not an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution of (
P
). Then, there exists
x\in X
such that

\begin{cases}f^{L}(x)&\leq f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\\
f^{U}(x)&\leq f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\end{cases}

with at least one strict inequality. Hence,
x\in X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})
and

f^{L}(x)+f^{U}(x)<f^{L}(x^{*})+f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}-\epsilon^{L},

contrary to (
5
).
∎

We say the
closedness condition

(CC_{x^{*}})
holds at
x^{*}\in X
if

\mathrm{cone}\,\Big(\bigcup_{j\in J}\mathrm{epi}\,g_{j}^{*}\cup\mathrm{epi}\,(\tilde{f^{L}})^{*}\cup\mathrm{epi}\,(\tilde{f^{U}})^{*}\Big)\ \ \text{is closed},

where
\tilde{f^{L}}(x):=f^{L}(x)-f^{L}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{U}
and
\tilde{f^{U}}(x):=f^{U}(x)-f^{U}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{L}
.

By using Lemma
4.3
and modifying the proof of Theorem 8.1 in
[
12
]
, we can obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. Assume that
X\cap X(x^{*},\mathcal{E})\neq\emptyset
and that
(CC_{x^{*}})
holds. Then
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-
LU
-solution
of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
,
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
,
\gamma_{2}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
,
\mu_{1}\geq 0
,
\mu_{2}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, such that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{0}}(f^{L}+f^{U})(x^{*})+\mu_{1}\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f^{L}(x^{*})+\mu_{2}\partial_{\gamma_{2}}f^{U}(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\lambda_{j}g_{j})(x^{*})

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}+\mu_{1}\gamma_{1}+\mu_{2}\gamma_{2}-(1+\mu_{1})\epsilon^{U}-(1+\mu_{2})\epsilon^{L}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x^{*}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 8.1]
, and we omit it.
∎

4.4.
KKT conditions for
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solutions

We say that the
Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint
qualification

(MFCQ)
holds at
x^{*}\in X
if there do not exist
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J(x^{*})
not all zero, such that

0\notin\sum_{j\in J(x^{*})}\lambda_{j}\partial g_{j}(x^{*}),

where
J(x^{*}):=\{j\in J\,:\,g_{j}(x^{*})=0\}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
x^{*}\in X
. Assume that the condition
(MFCQ)
holds at
x^{*}
. Then
x^{*}
is a weakly
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, such that

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&0\in\mu^{L}\partial f^{L}(x^{*})+\mu^{U}\partial f^{U}(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\partial g_{j}(x^{*})+(\mu^{L}\epsilon^{U}+\mu^{U}\epsilon^{L})B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.\\
&\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x^{*})=0,\ \ \forall j\in J.\end{split}

Furthermore, if
f^{L}
and
f^{U}
are strictly convex, then (
6
) is also sufficient for
x^{*}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
).

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
: Assume that
x^{*}
is a weakly
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
). Then, by Lemma
2.2
,
x^{*}
is a weakly
\epsilon
-quasi-efficient solution of (
MP
), where
\epsilon=(\epsilon^{U},\epsilon^{L})
. Hence, the following system:

\begin{cases}f^{L}(x)<f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\|x-x^{*}\|,\\
f^{U}(x)<f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\|x-x^{*}\|,\end{cases}

has no solution
x\in X
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, put

\Phi(x):=\max\{f^{L}(x)-f^{L}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{U}\|x-x^{*}\|,f^{U}(x)-f^{U}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{L}\|x-x^{*}\|,g_{1}(x),\ldots,g_{m}(x)\}.

Then
\Phi(x^{*})=0
and
\Phi(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Clearly,
\Phi
is convex. Hence, by
[
9
, Theorem 2.89]
, we have

0\in\partial\Phi(x^{*}).

From this and
[
9
, Theorem 2.96]
it follows that there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J(x^{*})
such that
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}+\sum_{j\in J(x^{*})}\lambda_{j}=1
and

\displaystyle 0\in\mu^{L}\partial(f^{L}(\cdot)-f^{L}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{U}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|)(x^{*})+\mu^{U}\partial(f^{U}(\cdot)-f^{U}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{L}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|)(x^{*})+\sum_{j\in J(x^{*})}\lambda_{j}\partial g_{j}(x^{*}).

Combining this with the Moreau–Rockafellar Sum Rule
[
9
, Theorem 2.91]
and the fact that

\partial(\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|)(x^{*})=B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

we obtain

0\in\mu^{L}\partial f^{L}(x^{*})+\mu^{U}\partial f^{U}(x^{*})+\sum_{j\in J(x^{*})}\lambda_{j}\partial g_{j}(x^{*})+(\mu^{L}\epsilon^{U}+\mu^{U}\epsilon^{L})B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Clearly, the condition
(MFCQ)
implies that
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}>0
. For
j\notin J(x^{*})
, we put
\lambda_{j}=0
. Then, (
6
) holds.

(\Leftarrow)
: Assume that there exist
\mu^{L}\geq 0
,
\mu^{U}\geq 0
,
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, that satisfy (
6
). Hence, there exist
z^{L}\in\partial f^{L}(x^{*})
,
z^{U}\in\partial f^{U}(x^{*})
,
u_{j}\in\partial g_{j}(x^{*})
and
b\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that

\mu^{L}z^{L}+\mu^{U}z^{U}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}u_{j}+(\mu^{L}\epsilon^{U}+\mu^{U}\epsilon^{L})b=0,

or, equivalently,

(7)

\mu^{L}(z^{L}+\epsilon^{U}b)+\mu^{U}(z^{U}+\epsilon^{L}b)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}u_{j}=0.

Suppose to the contrary that
x^{*}
is not a weakly
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
). This implies that there exists
\hat{x}\in X
such that

\begin{cases}f^{L}(\hat{x})<f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|,\\
f^{U}(\hat{x})<f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|.\end{cases}

Hence,

(8)

\mu^{L}(f^{L}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{U}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*}))+\mu^{U}(f^{U}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{L}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{U}(x^{*}))<0,

due to
\mu^{L}+\mu^{U}>0
. Since the function
f^{L}(\cdot)+\epsilon^{U}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|
is convex, we have

\displaystyle f^{L}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{U}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*})\geq z^{*}(\hat{x}-x^{*}),\ \ \forall z^{*}\in\partial(f^{L}(\cdot)+\epsilon^{U}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|)(x^{*}).

This and the fact that

\partial(f^{L}(\cdot)+\epsilon^{U}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|)(x^{*})=\partial f^{L}(x^{*})+\epsilon^{U}B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

imply that

f^{L}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{U}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*})\geq(z^{L}+\epsilon^{U}b)(\hat{x}-x^{*}).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle f^{U}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{L}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{U}(x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq(z^{U}+\epsilon^{L}b)(\hat{x}-x^{*})

\displaystyle g_{j}(\hat{x})-g_{j}(x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq u_{j}(\hat{x}-x^{*}),\ \ \forall j\in J.

From these and (
8
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big[\mu^{L}(z^{L}+\epsilon^{U}b)+\mu^{U}(z^{U}+\epsilon^{L}b)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\Big](\hat{x}-x^{*})\leq\mu^{L}(f^{L}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{U}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle+\mu^{U}(f^{U}(\hat{x})+\epsilon^{L}\|\hat{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{U}(x^{*}))<0,

contrary to (
7
).

If
f^{L}
and
f^{U}
are strictly convex, then so are
f^{L}(\cdot)+\epsilon^{U}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|
and
f^{U}(\cdot)+\epsilon^{L}\|\cdot\,-x^{*}\|
. Now suppose to the contrary that
x^{*}
is not an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-
LU
-solution of (
P
). Then there exists
\tilde{x}\in X
such that

\begin{cases}f^{L}(\tilde{x})\leq f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|,\\
f^{U}(\tilde{x})\leq f^{U}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{L}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|,\end{cases}

with at least one strict inequality. Without loss of generality we assume that

f^{L}(\tilde{x})<f^{L}(x^{*})-\epsilon^{U}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|.

This imply that
\tilde{x}\neq x^{*}
. By the strictly convexity of
f^{L}
and
f^{U}
we obtain

\displaystyle f^{L}(\tilde{x})+\epsilon^{U}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*})

\displaystyle>(z^{L}+\epsilon^{U}b)(\tilde{x}-x^{*}),

\displaystyle f^{U}(\tilde{x})+\epsilon^{L}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{U}(x^{*})

\displaystyle>(z^{U}+\epsilon^{L}b)(\tilde{x}-x^{*}).

Hence,

\displaystyle 0=\Big[\mu^{L}(z^{L}+\epsilon^{U}b)+\mu^{U}(z^{U}+\epsilon^{L}b)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\Big](\tilde{x}-x^{*})<\mu^{L}(f^{L}

\displaystyle(\tilde{x})+\epsilon^{U}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{L}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle+\mu^{U}(f^{U}(\tilde{x})+\epsilon^{L}\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|-f^{U}(x^{*}))\leq 0,

a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Conclusions

In this work, we propose some kinds of approximate solutions of interval optimization problems with respect to
LU
interval order relation. We show that these approximate solutions exist under very weak assumptions. By establishing the relationships of approximate solutions between interval optimization problems and multiobjective optimization problems and using suitable constraint qualifications, we derive some KKT necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for approximate solutions of convex interval-valued optimization problems. As shown in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, approximate solutions of interval optimization problems are closed related to the approximate efficient ones of multiobjective optimization problems. Accordingly, we may use the schemes in
[
8
,
21
,
22
]
to present new results on KKT necessary and sufficient optimality conditions by virtue of the Clarke subdifferentials (or the limiting subdifferentials) for nonconvex and nonsmooth interval optimization problems in our further research.

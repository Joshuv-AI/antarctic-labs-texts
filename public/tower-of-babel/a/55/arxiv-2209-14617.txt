Introduction

Vertex algebras, chiral algebras and chiral operad

Nowadays vertex algebras appear in various branches of mathematics, mathematical physics and theoretical physics. They were introduced by Borcherds
[
Bo86
]
as an algebraic framework of the operator algebra structure appearing in the chiral part of two-dimensional conformal field theory. This framework fits very well with the representation theory of infinite-dimensional algebras, and has made enormous progress since its appearance until today.

On the other hand, at least since the mid-1980s, it was expected that there should be a geometric framework for chiral conformal field theory. Along this expectation, together with the viewpoint of the geometric Langlands program, Beilinson and Drinfeld started in the early 1990s to develop the algebro-geometric framework, which resulted in their book
[
BD04
]
of chiral algebras. At present, both vertex algebras and chiral algebras are used in the study of algebraic and geometric representation theory, and in the theoretical physics of conformal field theories and their cousins. Let us cite
[
FBZ04
]
for the reference that explains both the framework of vertex algebras and chiral algebras.

The theory of chiral algebras is a flexible framework of chiral conformal field theory, allowing geometrical studies. But it has one drawback: the definition is built on various complicated structures using the theory of
\mathcal{D}
-modules and that of operads, so that it is non-trivial for non-experts to check the relation
[
BD04
, 0.15]
between chiral algebras and Borcherds’ axiom of vertex algebras. A feature of the theory is that a chiral algebra is defined to be a Lie algebra in a non-standard monoidal structure. More precisely, in the category of
\mathcal{D}
-modules on a smooth algebraic curve, Beilinson and Drinfeld introduced the chiral tensor product and the
chiral operad
. The latter object might best be called a sheaf operad, which is neither an algebraic operad nor a topological operad appearing commonly. Then a (non-unital) chiral algebra is defined to be an operad morphism from the Lie operad to the chiral operad. Starting from such a definition, it is not an easy task to recover Borcherds’ axiom of vertex algebras.

In the late 2010s, Bakalov, De Sole, Heluani and Kac
[
BDHK19
]
started their investigation of the cohomology theory of vertex algebras, which continues up to present
[
BDHK20
,
BDK20
,
BDK21
,
BDKV21
]
. As far as we understand, their study sits in the sequence of the investigation of Lie conformal algebras, vertex Poisson algebras and their cohomology theories which started in the late 1990s (see
[
BKV99
,
DK13
]
for example). Among several results in
[
BDHK19
]
, they constructed an algebraic operad
P^{\textup{ch}}_{V}
for each linear superspace
V
, and showed that an operad morphism from the Lie operad
\oLie
to
P^{\textup{ch}}_{V}
corresponds bijectively to a structure of the vertex superalgebra on
V
:

(0.0.1)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathsf{Op}_{\overline{0}}}(\oLie,P^{\textup{ch}}_{V})\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\{\text{vertex algebra structures on $V$}\}.

Here
\mathsf{Op}_{\overline{0}}
denotes the category of operads (see
section

1.1
, (
1.1.5
) for the convention).
Thus,
P^{\textup{ch}}
(suppressing the linear space
V
) is a purely algebraic counterpart of the chiral operad of Beilinson and Drinfeld. The standard operadic cohomology theory then gives a natural cohomology theory of a vertex algebra, and one can study the relation between the obtained cohomology and that of vertex Poisson algebras.

SUSY vertex algebras and SUSY chiral operad

This note is written in a simple motivation to give an analogue of the algebraic operad
P^{\textup{ch}}
which will encode the structure of a
SUSY vertex algebra
. The latter object is a superfield analogue of a vertex algebra, introduced by Heluani and Kac
[
HK07
]
. The framework of SUSY vertex algebras looks very natural from the point of view of superconformal field theory, and there are many examples, especially of a geometric nature. However, as far as we know, mathematical investigations of SUSY vertex algebras have not been pursued as much as those of ordinary vertex (super)algebras.

There are two classes of SUSY vertex algebras:
N_{W}=N
and
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebras, which originate from the classification of superconformal Lie algebras
[
KL89
]
. Accordingly, our argument is divided into two parts. We will introduce the algebraic operad
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}}
in
section

2
and
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{K}=N}}
in
section

3
, which encode the structure of an
N_{W}=N
and
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra, respectively. These
section

2
and
section

3
are the main body of this note.

Our argument basically follows the non-SUSY case given in
[
BDHK19
, §5-§7]
. We first construct the operads

and
\mathop{\mathcal{C}{\kern-0.6pt}hom^{N_{K}=N}}
of SUSY Lie conformal algebras, and then construct the operads
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}}
and

of SUSY vertex algebras. Note that although the outline of our construction is quite the same as

]
, we need to make some technical modifications to treat the SUSY case appropriately. For example, in
section

of the construction of the operad

The operads

and

give the cohomology complexes of SUSY vertex algebras with coefficients in their modules, whose definition and analysis is given in

\displaystyle(-1)^{\widetilde{p}(a)\widetilde{p}(b)}X_{\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{1}}(b\otimes a)=X_{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}(a\otimes b)\quad(a,b\in V).

Since

\displaystyle[b_{\Lambda}a]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(b)(\overline{N}+\overline{1})}X_{\Lambda,-\Lambda-\nabla}(b\otimes a),

\displaystyle[a_{-\Lambda-\nabla}b]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)(\overline{N}+\overline{1})}X_{-\Lambda-\nabla,\Lambda}(a\otimes b)\quad(a,b\in V),

(\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{2})

in

•

The linear map

((iii) in

\displaystyle=X_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}}(X_{\Lambda_{1},-\Lambda_{1}-\nabla}(a\otimes b)\otimes c)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(b)(\overline{N}+\overline{1})}[[a_{\Lambda_{1}}b]_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}}c],

\displaystyle(X\circ_{2}X)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle\Res_{\Lambda_{1}}\Res_{\Lambda_{2}}(\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-1}F(a\otimes b\otimes c))=\Res_{\Lambda_{1}}\Res_{\Lambda_{2}}(\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-1}G(a\otimes b\otimes c)).

gives a binary operation

, which is a map
\mu_{\mathscr{A}}\colon j_{*}j^{*}\mathscr{A}\boxtimes\mathscr{A}\to\Delta_{*}\mathscr{A}
satisfying skew-symmetry and Jacobi identity. We call
\mu_{\mathscr{A}}
the chiral operation of
\mathscr{A}
, following the terminology in
[
BD04
, §3.1]
.

A
non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY chiral algebra on
X
is a pair
(\mathscr{A},\varphi)
of a right
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathscr{A}
and a superoperad morphism
\varphi\colon\oLie\to\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}
. In the language of
1.3.2
, such a morphism
\varphi
is nothing but a Lie algebra structure on the superoperad
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}
.

Let us denote by
\omega_{X}
the Berezinian bundle
[
H07
, 2.2.7]
of the smooth
1|X
-dimensional curve
X
, which can be regarded as a SUSY analogue of the canonical bundle of an even curve. It is a right
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module in the standard way, and moreover carries a non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY chiral algebra structure, established by Heluani in
[
H07
, 4.1.7]
. We denote by
\mu_{\omega}\in\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\omega_{X}}}(2)
the corresponding chiral operation.

Now we define an
N_{W}=N
SUSY chiral algebra
\mathscr{A}
on
X
to be a non-unital one equipped with a morphism
\iota\colon\omega_{X}\to\mathscr{A}
of right
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules such that the composition
\mu_{\iota\otimes\id_{\mathscr{A}}}
(the restriction of
\mu_{\mathscr{A}}
to
j_{*}j^{*}\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathscr{A}\to j_{*}j^{*}\mathscr{A}\boxtimes\mathscr{A}
) coincides with
j_{*}j^{*}\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathscr{A}\to(j_{*}j^{*}\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathscr{A})/\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Delta_{*}\mathscr{A}
(the latter in the even case is called the unit operation
[
BD04
, 3.1.12, 3.3.3]
).

2.4.2.
SUSY chiral algebras on the superline

We write down
N_{W}=N
SUSY chiral algebras over the affine superline
\mathbb{A}^{1|N}
, following the argument for the even case
[
BDHK19
, §A.2]
.

Let

be a
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable and

be the corresponding polynomial superring in the sense of
section

2.1

(1|N)
-supercurve

in the sense of
[
KV11
, Example 1.1.6]
. A right
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathscr{A}
is determined by the right module
A\coloneqq\Gamma(X,\mathscr{A})
over the superalgebra

.

Let

, and

(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable for

, regarded as the local coordinate of the

\Gamma(X^{n},\mathscr{O}_{X^{n}})

Also recall the superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{n}^{\star}=\mathbb{K}[Z_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}[z_{k,l}^{-1}]_{1\leq k<l\leq n}
in

. Then, by the argument in

, §A.2]
, we have

(2.4.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma(X,j_{*}j^{*}\mathscr{A}^{\boxtimes n})\cong\mathcal{O}_{n}^{\star}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{n}}A^{\otimes n},

which is a right module over the superalgebra

\mathcal{D}_{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[Z_{1},\dotsc,Z_{n}][\partial_{Z_{1}},\dotsc,\partial_{Z_{n}}]

of regular differential operators on

. The module structure is given by the action on the right factor in (
2.4.2
).

Let
I\subset\mathcal{D}_{n}
be the left ideal generated by

for

is a
\mathcal{D}_{1}
-
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-bimodule, where the

-action is the right multiplication, and the left action of
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathbb{K}[Z][\partial_{Z}]

and

. Then, by the argument in
[
BDHK19
, §A.2]
, we have

(2.4.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma(X^{n},\Delta_{*}\mathscr{A})\cong A\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(I\mathbin{\setminus}\mathcal{D}_{n}),

where the right
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module structure is given by right multiplication on the right factor. Combining (
2.4.2
) and (

), we obtain an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-supermodule isomorphism

(2.4.4)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}(n)\cong\Hom_{\crMod\mathcal{D}_{n}}\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{\star}_{n}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{n}}A^{\otimes n},A\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(I\mathbin{\setminus}\mathcal{D}_{n})\bigr),

\mathcal{D}_{n}
-supermodules.

2.4.3.
Equivariant SUSY chiral algebras on the superline

Next, we restrict the above description to the translation-equivariant part. We use the terminology on equivariant
\mathscr{D}
-modules in
[
BDHK19
, §A.3]
, which we extend to superschemes in an obvious way. In particular, we will use the notion of
weakly equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-modules
.

The affine superline

has a natural action of the group superscheme
\mathrm{Aff}^{1|N}
of affine transformations. Consider the even subgroup scheme
\mathrm{T}\coloneqq\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}^{N}\subset\mathrm{Aff}^{1|N}
consisting of translations. We have an equivalence of categories between
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant quasi-coherent
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules and linear superspaces by taking the stalk at the origin
0\in X
. The inverse functor sends a linear superspace
V
to the sheaf associated to the
\mathbb{K}[Z]
-supermodule
V[Z]=\mathbb{K}[Z]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}V
equipped with the action of
(u,\upsilon^{1},\dotsc,\upsilon^{N})\in\mathrm{T}
by
v(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})\mapsto v(z+u,\zeta^{1}+\upsilon^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N}+\upsilon^{N})
.

As for
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
X=\mathbb{A}^{1|N}
, following the even case
[
BDHK19
, §A.4]
, we have an equivalence of categories

(2.4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl(\text{weakly $\mathrm{T}$-equivariant quasi-coherent $\mathscr{D}_{X}$-modules $\mathscr{A}$ on $X$})\\
\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\bigl(\text{$\mathcal{H}_{W}$-supermodules $(V,\nabla)=(V,T,S^{1},\dotsc,S^{N})$}\bigr).\end{split}

For the latter, see
2.1.2
. Indeed, given a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
\mathscr{A}
, taking the stalk of
\mathscr{A}
at the origin
0\in X
, we obtain
V
as in the last paragraph, together with the endomorphisms
\nabla=(T,S^{1},\dotsc,S^{N})
on
V
induced by the differential operators
\partial_{Z}=(\partial_{z},\partial_{\zeta^{1}},\dotsc,\partial_{\zeta^{N}})
which satisfy the relations (
2.1.3
). Conversely, given a pair
(V,\nabla)
, we have a
\mathcal{D}_{1}
-module structure on
V[Z]=\mathbb{K}[z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{i}]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}V
by letting
Z
act by multiplication by
Z
, and
\partial_{z}
(resp.
\partial_{\zeta^{i}}
) act by
T-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
(resp.
S^{i}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta^{i}}
).

Now, following the argument in
[
BDHK19
, §A.6]
, let us consider a weakly
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-module
\mathscr{A}
on
X=\mathbb{A}^{1|N}
corresponding to the
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule
(V,\nabla)
. Then the associated
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module (
2.4.3
) is given by

V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\nabla]}\mathbb{K}[Z][\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}]

with
Z=(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})
and
\nabla_{k}=(T_{k},S_{k}^{1},\dotsc,S_{k}^{N})
satisfying the relations (
2.1.3
) for each
k\in[n]
. The commutative superalgebra
\mathbb{K}[Z][\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}]
is regarded as a
\mathbb{K}[\partial]
-
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-bimodule in the following way:

•

The left action of
\nabla=(T,S^{1},\dotsc,S^{N})
is given by

(\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_{k},\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}^{1},\dotsc,\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}^{N})
.

•

The right action of
\partial_{Z_{k}}=(\partial_{z_{k}},\partial_{\zeta_{k}^{1}},\dotsc,\partial_{\zeta_{k}^{N}})
is the right multiplication by
\nabla_{k}
, and the right action of

on

is given by

\displaystyle f\cdot z_{k}\coloneqq zf+\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}f,\quad f\cdot\zeta_{k}^{i}\coloneqq(-1)^{p(f)}\bigl(\zeta^{i}f+\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{k}^{i}}f\bigr),

where

denotes the parity of

.

Hence,
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}(n)
in (

) is equal to

(2.4.6)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}(n)=\Hom_{\crMod\mathcal{D}_{n}}\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{\star}_{n}\otimes V^{\otimes n},V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\nabla]}\mathbb{K}[Z][\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}]\bigr).

The translation group
\mathrm{T}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}^{N}
acts on

by letting

act on

to give

with

(2.4.7)

\displaystyle\varphi^{U}\bigl(f(Z_{0},\dotsc,Z_{n})\otimes v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n})\coloneqq\left.\varphi(f(Z_{1}-U,\dotsc,Z_{n}-U)\otimes v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n})\right|_{Z=Z+U}

for

and

,

. Then the superoperad
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}
is
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant in the sense of
[
BDHK19
, §A.5]
, and the
\mathrm{T}
-invariants

form a sub-operad

\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N,\mathrm{T}}_{\mathscr{A}}}\subset\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\mathscr{A}}}.

Now, we have the main statement of this
section

2.4
.

Proposition 2.4.1
.

Let
V=(V,\nabla)
be an
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule (
2.1.2
), and
\mathscr{A}
be the corresponding weakly
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-module on
\mathbb{A}^{1|N}
under the equivalence (
2.4.5
). Let
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{V}}
be the
N_{W}=N
SUSY chiral operad in
2.3.14
. Then there is an isomorphism of operads

\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N,\mathrm{T}}_{\mathscr{A}}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{V}}.

Proof.

The even case argument
[
BDHK19
, Lemma A.1]
works, so we will just explain the outline.

By (
2.4.6
), we want to determine the
\mathrm{T}
-invariant parts of
\mathcal{O}^{\star}_{n}\otimes V^{\otimes n}
and
V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\nabla]}\mathbb{K}[Z][\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}]
. As for the former, by (
2.3.11
), we have an isomorphism

(\mathcal{O}^{\star}_{n}\otimes V^{\otimes n})^{\mathrm{T}}=(\mathcal{O}^{\star}_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes V^{\otimes n}=\mathcal{O}^{\star\mathrm{T}}_{n}\otimes V^{\otimes n}

as
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}

with
\delta^{i}

2.3.11

As for the latter

2.2.3
, we have a
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}
-supermodule isomorphism

\displaystyle(V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\nabla]}\mathbb{K}[Z][\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}])^{\mathrm{T}}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\nabla]}\mathbb{K}[\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n}]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}V[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}/\Img(\nabla+\Lambda_{1}+\dotsb+\Lambda_{n}),

\displaystyle v\otimes f(\nabla_{1},\dotsc,\nabla_{n})

\displaystyle\longmapsto f(-\Lambda_{1},\dotsc,-\Lambda_{n})v.

Now, any

\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{V}}(n)
from

3.
N_{K}=N
SUSY chiral operad

In this section, we introduce
N_{K}=N
SUSY analogues of the operads
\mathop{\mathcal{C}{\kern-0.6pt}hom}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}}
in
[
BDHK19
]
. Most of the results and proofs in
section

2
for the
N_{W}=N
case apply to the
N_{K}=N
case with minor modifications, so we omit some details. We fix a positive integer
N
throughout this section.

3.1.
Polynomial superalgebra

Here we give a summary of the polynomial ring of supervariables in the
N_{K}=N
setting.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
A
be a set and
\Lambda_{\alpha}=(\lambda_{\alpha},\theta_{\alpha}^{1},\ldots,\theta_{\alpha}^{N})
be a sequence of letters for each
\alpha\in A
. We denote by
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by even generators
\lambda_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in A
) and odd generators
\theta_{\alpha}^{i}
(
\alpha\in A
,
i\in[N]
) with relations

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\lambda_{\alpha}\theta_{\beta}^{i}-\theta_{\beta}^{i}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\theta_{\alpha}^{i}\theta_{\beta}^{j}+\theta_{\beta}^{j}\theta_{\alpha}^{i}=-2\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\delta_{i,j}\lambda_{\alpha}\quad(\alpha,\beta\in A,\,i,j\in[N]).

Each
\Lambda_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in A
is called a
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariable
, and the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
is called the
N_{K}=N
polynomial superalgebra of the supervariables
(\Lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
.

As in the
N_{W}=N
case (see the lines after
2.1.1
), we will also use the simplified symbols
K[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
and
K[\Lambda]
. Note that the
N_{K}=N
polynomial superalgebra is not commutative, unlike the
N_{W}=N
polynomial superalgebra.

For a linear superspace
V
and a
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariable
\Lambda_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in A
) , we denote

\displaystyle V[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}V,

which is naturally a left
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
-supermodule.

Definition 3.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by an even generator
T
and odd generators
S^{i}

(i\in[N])
with relations

\displaystyle TS^{i}-S^{i}T=0,\quad S^{i}S^{j}+S^{j}S^{i}=2\delta_{i,j}T\quad(i,j\in[N]).

For simplicity, we denote
\nabla\coloneqq(T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N})
.
A linear superspace
V
equipped with a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule structure is denoted as
(V,\nabla)=(V,T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N})
.

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{K}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
as a superalgebra by the homomorphism defined by

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle T\longmapsto-\lambda,\quad S^{i}\longmapsto-\theta^{i}\quad(i\in[N]).

3.2.
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal operad

In this subsection, we introduce an
N_{K}=N
SUSY analogue of the operad
\mathop{\mathcal{C}{\kern-0.6pt}hom}
in
[
BDHK19
]
. All the results and proofs for the
N_{W}=N
case in
section

2.2
are also valid for the
N_{K}=N
case with the modifications:

•

Replace the superalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{W}
with
\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

•

Replace all
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariables with
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariables.

So we only refer to the results without proofs.

Let us fix a
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariable
\Lambda=(\lambda,\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{N})
.

Definition 3.2.1

(
[
HK07
, Definition 4.10]
)
.

Let
V
be a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule and
[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot]\colon V\otimes V\to V[\Lambda]
be a linear map of parity
\overline{N}
.
A triple
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
is called an
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra
if it satisfies the following conditions:

1

(
sesquilinearity
)
For any
a,b\in V
,

(3.2.1)

\displaystyle[S^{i}a_{\Lambda}b]=-(-1)^{N}\theta^{i}[a_{\Lambda}b],\quad[a_{\Lambda}S^{i}b]=(-1)^{p(a)}(\theta^{i}+S^{i})[a_{\Lambda}b]\quad(i\in[N]).

2

(
skew-symmetry
)
For any
a,b\in V
,

(3.2.2)

\displaystyle[b_{\Lambda}a]=-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+\overline{N}}[a_{-\Lambda-\nabla}b].

3

(
Jacobi identity
)
For any
a,b,c\in V
,

(3.2.3)

\displaystyle[a_{\Lambda_{1}}[b_{\Lambda_{2}}c]]=(-1)^{p(a)\overline{N}+\overline{N}}[[a_{\Lambda_{1}}b]_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}}c]+(-1)^{(p(a)+\overline{N})(p(b)+\overline{N})}[b_{\Lambda_{2}}[a_{\Lambda_{1}}c]],

where
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
are
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariables.

The linear map
[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot]
is called the
\Lambda
-bracket
of the left
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule
V
. For simplicity, we say
V
is an
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra.

Remark 3.2.2
.

In (
3.2.1
) and (
3.2.2
), the symbols
T
and
S^{i}
on the left of the
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariable

are identified with the element of
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
by the isomorphism (
3.1.1
). Also, (
3.2.3
) is the identity in

.

In the remaining of this section, let
(V,\nabla)
be a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule and
\Lambda_{k}=(\lambda_{k},\theta_{k}^{1},\ldots,\theta_{k}^{N})
be a
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariable for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

The polynomial superalgebra
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
carries a structure of an
(\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\otimes n},\mathcal{H}_{K})
-superbimodule as follows:
First, since any element of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
is expressed uniquely as
\varphi(\nabla)
for some
\varphi(\Lambda)\in\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
, the space
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
has a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\otimes n}
-supermodule structure by letting
\varphi_{1}(\nabla)\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{n}(\nabla)\in\mathcal{H}_{W}^{\otimes n}
act on

\bigl(\varphi_{1}(\nabla)\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{n}(\nabla)\bigr)a(\Lambda_{1}\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\coloneqq\varphi_{1}(-\Lambda_{1})\cdots\varphi_{n}(-\Lambda_{n})a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}).

Second, the space
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
is a right
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule by letting

\displaystyle a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\cdot T\coloneqq a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\Bigl(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\Bigr),\quad a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\cdot S^{i}\coloneqq a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\Bigl(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}^{i}\Bigr)

for
a(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
.
These left and right supermodule structures are consistent, and we have the desired superbimodule structure.

Thus, for an
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule
V=(V,\nabla)
and

, we obtain a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\otimes n}
-supermodule

\displaystyle V_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}V.

For

, we denote

\displaystyle V_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}\coloneqq V/\nabla V,

where
\nabla V\coloneqq TV+\sum_{i=1}^{N}S^{i}V\subset V
.

As in the
N_{W}=N
case (

), we have:

Lemma 3.2.3
.

The linear superspace
V_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
is isomorphic to

4.1.
Recollection on the Lie algebra cohomology

Here we give a brief recollection on the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a Lie algebra from the viewpoint of the operad theory. For the detail, we refer to
[
LV12
, Chap. 12, §13.2]
.

We use the language of operads reviewed in
section

1
, although in this subsection we work in the non-super setting. Let
V
be a linear space. Recall the convolution Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}=\bigl(\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}}(\oLie^{\mathord{\text{\textexclamdown}}},\oHom_{V}),[\cdot,\cdot]\bigr)

in (
1.2.4
) and the set of solutions of the Maurer-Cartan equation

\MC(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V})=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{1}\mid\tfrac{1}{2}[X,X]=0\},

both being specialized for the Lie operad
\oP=\oLie
. A Maurer-Cartan solution
X\in\MC(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V})
corresponds bijectively to a Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{X}
on
V
.

Given a Maurer-Cartan solution
X\in\MC(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V})
, we have a differential

d_{X}\coloneqq[X,-]\colon\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{\bullet+1},\quad d_{X}^{2}=0.

The obtained cochain complex
(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{\bullet},d_{X})
coincides with the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex
C^{\bullet}(L,L)
of the Lie algebra
L=(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{X})
with coefficients in itself as an adjoint module, up to a degree shift. It is known that the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{\bullet},d_{X})
has a Lie bracket, giving a dg Lie algebra structure, and that the Lie bracket is essentially equal to the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket on the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex.

We can extend the construction of the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{g}_{\oLie,V}^{\bullet},d_{X})
to the version with coefficients in a module over the Lie algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{X})
along the line of the general argument for a quadratic operad in
[
LV12
, §12.4]
. Let us give a brief recollection.

•

First, for an operad
\oP
and a linear space
V
, we have an isomorphism

(4.1.1)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}}(\oP,\oHom_{V})\cong C_{\oP}(V,V)\coloneqq\Hom(\oP(V),V)

by the hom-tensor adjunction and (
1.1.6
), where

\oP(V)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\oP(V)(n)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}V^{\otimes n}

is the image of the Schur functor of the operad
\oP

[
LV12
, §5.1.2]
. Thus, a
\oP
-algebra structure
\varphi\colon\oP\to\oHom_{V}
can be encoded by
C_{\oP}(V,V)
. The linear space
C_{\oP}(V,V)
has an
\mathbb{N}
-grading with
C_{\oP}^{n}(V,V)\coloneqq\Hom(\oP(V)(n)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}V^{\otimes n},V)
.

•

Next, recall from
[
LV12
, §12.3.1]
that a module
M
over a
\oP
-algebra
(V,\varphi)
is defined to be a linear space equipped with two morphisms
\gamma_{M}\colon\oP\circ(V;M)\to M
and
\eta_{M}\colon M\to\oP\circ(V;M)
satisfying some axioms. Here we used the composite product
[
LV12
, §6.1.1]

\oP\circ(V;M)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\oP(n)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\Bigl(\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n}V^{\otimes i}\otimes M\otimes V^{\otimes n-i}\Bigr).

As it implies, the notion of modules over a
\oP
-algebra is a natural operadic analogue of bimodules over the algebra structure considered.

•

Following
[
LV12
, §12.4.1]
, let
\oP
be a homogeneous quadratic operad,
V
be a linear space equipped with a
\oP
-algebra structure, and
M
be a module over the
\oP
-algebra
V
. Let us consider the linear space

(4.1.2)

\displaystyle C_{\oP}(V,M)\coloneqq\Hom(\oP^{\mathord{\text{\textexclamdown}}}(V),M)

which has the natural
\mathbb{N}
-grading induced by that on
\oP^{\mathord{\text{\textexclamdown}}}(V)
. In the case
M=V
, this graded space coincides with
C_{\oP}^{\bullet}(V,V)
in (
4.1.1
). According to
[
LV12
, §12.4.1]
, the graded space
C_{\oP}^{\bullet}(V,M)
has a natural differential
d
coming from the
\oP
-algebra structure on
V
and the
V
-module structure on
M
, and we obtain a cochain complex
(C_{\oP}^{\bullet}(V,M),d)
called the
operadic cochain complex
.

•

The cohomology of the operadic cochain complex
(C_{\oP}^{\bullet}(V,M),d)
is denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\oP}(A,M)
. The low degree cohomology have analogous interpretation as the classical cohomology. For example, we have
H^{0}_{\oP}(A,M)\cong\Der_{A}(A,M)
, the space of derivations. We refer to
[
LV12
, §12.4]
for the detail.

•

In the case
\oP=\oLie
, the operadic cochain complex
(C_{\oP}(V,M),d)
coincides, up to a degree shift, with the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of the Lie algebra
V
with coefficients in the
V
-module
M
. Similarly, we can recover the Harrison cochain complex in the case
\oP=\oCom
, and
recover the Hochschild cochain complex in the case
\oP=\oAss
.

In
[
BDHK19
, §7]
, the authors introduced a natural analogue of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex for vertex algebras, based on their operad
\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}}_{V}
. As explained in
section

1

\oLie
as it is, and replace the endomorphism operad
\oHom_{V}
by the chiral operad
\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}}_{V}
. The construction in loc. cit. can be understood as such a replacement in (
4.1.1
) and (
4.1.2
).
We will not give the detail of the construction in
[
BDHK19
, §7]
. Instead, we explain a straightforward modification for SUSY vertex algebras in the following
section

4.2
and
section

4.3
.

Lemma 4.2.7

For any

Definition 4.2.8

Let

Let us identify the SUSY vertex algebra cohomology

First, we describe the zeroth cohomology. For that, let us introduce:

Definition 4.2.9
.

A

\int x\in M/\nabla M
such that

for all

Proposition 4.2.10
.

The map

(4.2.9)

\displaystyle C^{0}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)\longrightarrow M/\nabla M,\quad Y\longmapsto Y(1_{\mathbb{K}})

gives the following isomorphism of linear superspaces:

\displaystyle H^{0}_{\textup{ch}}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)\cong\Cas(V,M).

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle C^{0}(\Pi^{N}V,\Pi^{N}M)=\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}(\Pi^{N+1}\mathbb{K},\Pi^{N+1}M/\nabla\Pi^{N+1}M).

It is clear that the map

4.2.9
is an isomorphism of linear superspaces.
For
Y\in C^{0}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)
, we can represent

with

. Then we have

\displaystyle(\partial^{0}Y)(a\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}})

\displaystyle=(X\circ_{1}Y)(a\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}})=X_{0,-\nabla}(x\otimes a\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\widetilde{p}(a)\widetilde{p}(x)}X_{-\Lambda,0}(a\otimes x\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\widetilde{p}(a)\widetilde{p}(x)}(-1)^{p(a)(\overline{N}+\overline{1})}a_{-\nabla}x

for any

.
Thus, the restriction of
eq.

4.2.9
to

gives the desired isomorphism.
∎

\displaystyle C^{1}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(V,M),\quad Y\mapsto(-1)^{p(Y)(\overline{N}+\overline{1})}Y_{-\nabla}(\cdot\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}})

By definition, we have

\displaystyle C^{1}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)=\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}}\bigl(\Pi^{N+1}V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\star\mathrm{T}},(\Pi^{N+1}M)_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{1}]\bigr).

the map (

It is clear that

For

\displaystyle(\partial^{1}Y)(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})

\displaystyle=(X\circ_{1}Y)(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})+(X\circ_{1}Y)^{(1,2)}(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})-(-1)^{\widetilde{p}(Y)}(Y\circ_{1}X)(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})

For

as above, there exists a surjection

(4.2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\{Y\in\MC\bigl(L(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\Pi^{N+1}(V\oplus M)}})\bigr)_{\overline{1}}\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}(X-Y)(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})\in M_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]\\
(X-Y)(a\otimes x\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})=0\\
Y(x\otimes y\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})=0\ (a,b\in V,\,x,y\in M)\end{array}\right\}\\
&\longrightarrow\{\text{isomorphism classes of $\mathcal{H}_{W}$-split extexsions of $V$ by $M$}\}.\end{split}

Here an isomorphism of
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-split extensions of
V
by
M
is defined to be an isomorphism of exact sequences of non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras.

Proof.

For
Y
in the left hand side of (
4.2.12
), let
E_{Y}
denote the
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule
V\oplus M
with the non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra structure corresponding to
Y
. Then we have an
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-split extension

0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\,\varphi\,}E_{Y}\xrightarrow{\,\psi\,}V\longrightarrow 0,

where
\varphi\colon M\to V\oplus M
is the canonical inclusion and
\psi\colon V\oplus M\to V
is the canonical projection. The map
Y\mapsto E_{Y}
gives rise to the surjection (
4.2.12
).
∎

Similarly as the Lie algebra cohomology
H^{n}_{\oLie}(L,M)
recalled in
section

4.1
, and as the non-SUSY case
[
BDHK19
, Theorem 7.6]
, the lower degree SUSY vertex algebra cohomology
H^{n}_{\textup{ch}}(V,M)
,
n=0,1,2
, have explicit description.

Theorem 4.2.15
.

Let
M
be a module over a non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra
V
.

(1)

For the zeroth cohomology, we have an isomorphism

H_{\textup{ch}}^{0}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)\cong\Cas(V,M)

of linear superspace,
where
\Cas(V,M)
is the linear superspace of Casimir elements (
4.2.9
).

(2)

For the first cohomology, we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle H_{\textup{ch}}^{1}(\Pi^{N+1}V,\Pi^{N+1}M)\cong\Der(V,M)/\Ind(V,M)

where
\Der(V,M)
is the linear superspace of derivations and
\Ind(V,M)
is the linear superspace of inner derivations (
4.2.11
).

(3)

For the odd part of the second cohomology, we have an linear isomorphism

\displaystyle H_{\textup{ch}}^{2}(V,M)_{\overline{1}}\cong\{\text{isomorphism classes of $\mathcal{H}_{W}$-split extensions of $V$ by modules $M$}\}.

Proof.

We have already proved (1), (2) in
4.2.10
and
4.2.12
. Let us show (3).

Define a map
F_{2}^{\prime}\colon C^{2}(V,M)_{\overline{1}}\to L^{1}\bigl(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\Pi^{N+1}(V\oplus M)}}\bigr)_{\overline{1}}
by
F_{2}^{\prime}(Y)\coloneqq X+Y
. If
Y\in(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}
, then
F_{2}^{\prime}(Y)
satisfies
F_{2}^{\prime}(Y)\square F_{2}^{\prime}(Y)=0
, so
F_{2}^{\prime}
gives a surjection from
(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}
to

\displaystyle\left\{Y\in\MC\bigl(L(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}_{\Pi^{N+1}(V\oplus M)}})\bigr)_{\overline{1}}\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}(X-Y)(a\otimes b\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})\in M_{\nabla}[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]\\
(X-Y)(a\otimes x\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})=0\\
Y(x\otimes y\otimes z_{1,2}^{-1})=0\ (a,b\in V,\,x,y\in M)\end{array}\right\}.

Thus, the composition with the surjection in
4.2.14
gives a surjection

\displaystyle F_{2}\colon(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}\longrightarrow\{\text{isomorphism classes of $\mathcal{H}_{W}$-split extexsions of $V$ by module $M$}\}.

Hence it is enough to show that
F_{2}(Y)=F_{2}(Y^{\prime})
is equivalent to
Y-Y^{\prime}\in(\Img\partial^{1})_{\overline{1}}
for
Y,Y^{\prime}\in(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}
.

Note that, for
Y,Y^{\prime}\in(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}
, we have
F_{2}(Y)=F_{2}(Y^{\prime})
if and only if there exists a morphism of non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras
f\colon E\to E^{\prime}
satisfying

(4.2.13)

\displaystyle f(a)-a\in M,\quad f(x)=x\quad(a\in V,\,x\in M).

Here the symbol
E
(resp.
E^{\prime}
) denotes the
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule
V\oplus M
with the non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra structure corresponding to
F_{2}^{\prime}(Y)
(resp.
F_{2}^{\prime}(Y^{\prime})
). For an even
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule homomorphism
f\colon E\to E^{\prime}
satisfying (
4.2.13
), define an even linear map
\Phi_{f}\colon V\to M
by
\Phi_{f}(a)\coloneqq f(a)-a
for
a\in V
. Then the mapping
f\mapsto\Phi_{f}
gives a bijection

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\{f\in\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(E,E^{\prime})_{\overline{0}}\ \text{satisfying \eqref{eq:exthom}}\}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(V,M)_{\overline{0}}.

For an even
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule homomorphism
f\colon E\to E^{\prime}
satisfying (
4.2.13
) and
a,b\in V
, we have

\displaystyle f\Bigl(\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f}\Bigl(\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E}\Bigr)+\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E},

\displaystyle\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[f(a)_{\Gamma}f(b)]

\displaystyle=\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma(\Phi_{f}(a)_{\Gamma}b)+\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma(a_{\Gamma}\Phi_{f}(b))+\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E^{\prime}}.

Thus,
f\colon E\to E^{\prime}
is a morphism of non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras if and only if

\displaystyle\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E}-\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E^{\prime}}=\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma(\Phi_{f}(a)_{\Gamma}b)+\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma(a_{\Gamma}\Phi_{f}(b))-\Phi_{f}\Bigl(\int^{\Lambda}d\Gamma[a_{\Gamma}b]_{E}\Bigr)

for any
a,b\in V
, which is equivalent to
Y-Y^{\prime}=\partial^{1}(F_{1}^{-1}(\Phi_{f}))
. Here
F_{1}
is the isomorphism
C^{1}(V,M)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(V,M)
defined in the proof of
4.2.12
. Therefore we find that
F_{2}(Y)=F_{2}(Y^{\prime})
is equivalent to
Y-Y^{\prime}\in(\Img\partial^{1})_{\overline{1}}
for
Y,Y^{\prime}\in(\Ker\partial^{2})_{\overline{1}}
.
∎

4.3.
Cohomology of
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebras

The theory of
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra cohomology in
section

4.2
carries over to the
N_{K}=N
case with minor modifications. So we only give the main definitions and statements.

Definition 4.3.1
.

Let
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
be an
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra,
(M,\nabla)
be a left
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-supermodule and
\rho_{\Lambda}\colon V\otimes M\to M[\Lambda]
be a linear map of parity
\overline{N}
. We denote
a_{\Lambda}x\coloneqq\rho_{\Lambda}(a\otimes x)
for
a\in V
,
x\in M
.
A triple
(M,\nabla,\rho_{\Lambda})
is called a
module over
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
if it satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) in
4.2.1
replacing the
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariables
\Lambda,\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
by the corresponding
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariables.

Definition 4.3.2
.

Let
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot],\mu)
be a non-unital
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra,
(M,\nabla,\rho_{\Lambda})
be a module over the
N_{K}=N
Lie conformal algebra
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
and
\rho\colon V\otimes M\to M
be an even linear map. We denote
a\cdot x\coloneqq\rho(a\otimes x)
. A tuple
(M,\nabla,\rho_{\Lambda},\rho)
is called a

(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot],\mu)
if it satisfies the conditions (i)–(iii) in
4.2.2
replacing the
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariables
\Lambda,\Gamma
by the corresponding
(1|N)_{K}
-supervariables.

For a module

over a non-unital
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra

with coefficients in

as in (

). Also, the notions of Casimir elements, derivations and extensions are defined in the similar way. Then we find that

.

4.4.
Concluding remarks

As mentioned in

0

[

]
continues to the development of the cohomology theory of vertex algebras in

Integrated Correlators in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM via S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) Spectral Theory


Abstract
We perform a systematic study of integrated four-point functions of half-BPS operators in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills theory with gauge group S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) . These observables, defined by a certain spacetime integral of ⟨ 𝒪 2 ​ 𝒪 2 ​ 𝒪 p ​ 𝒪 p ⟩ \langle\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle where 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} is a superconformal primary of charge p p , are known to be computable by supersymmetric localization, yet are non-trivial functions of the complexified gauge coupling τ \tau . We find explicit and remarkably simple results for several classes of these observables, exactly as a function of N N and τ \tau . Their physical and formal properties are greatly illuminated upon employing the S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) spectral decomposition: in this S-duality-invariant eigenbasis, the integrated correlators are fixed simply by polynomials in the spectral parameter. These polynomials are determined recursively by linear algebraic equations relating different N N and p p , such that all integrated correlators are ultimately fixed in terms of the integrated stress tensor multiplets in the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) theory. Our computations include the full matrix of integrated correlators at low values of p p , and a certain infinite class involving operators of arbitrary p p . The latter satisfy an open lattice chain equation for all N N , reminiscent of the Toda equation obeyed by extremal correlators in 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal theories. We compute ensemble averages of these observables and analyze our solutions at large N N , confirming and predicting features of semiclassical AdS 5 × {}_{5}\,\times S 5 supergravity amplitudes.

2.3
The
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
spectral decomposition

{SpectralDecomposition}

Many observables of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM are
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant. Such observables, call them
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
, admit a spectral decomposition into a complete
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant eigenbasis of the Laplacian on the upper half plane. Let us introduce the main elements of this decomposition. More complete explanations may be found in Sections 2-3 of
[
8
]
in the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM context, and in
[
61
,
62
]
in great mathematical detail; see also
[
63
]
for a pertinent review. The fundamental domain for
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
may be defined as

\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{H}/SL(2,\mathbb{Z})=\left\{\tau=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}\,\bigg|-{1\over 2}\leq x\leq{1\over 2},|\tau|\geq 1\right\}.

(2.23)

This space is endowed with the hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}={dx^{2}+dy^{2}\over y^{2}}

(2.24)

which defines a Laplacian

\begin{split}\Delta_{\tau}=-y^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})\,.\end{split}

(2.25)

We define inner products of square-integrable
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant functions via the Petersson inner product,

(f,g)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{F}}{dxdy\over y^{2}}f(\tau)\overline{g(\tau)}.

(2.26)

The observable
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
, which is square-integrable
[
8
]
, obeys the invariance equation

\mathbb{O}(\gamma\tau)=\mathbb{O}(\tau),\quad\gamma\tau={a\tau+b\over c\tau+d},\quad\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z}).

(2.27)

Restricting to real-valued
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
, we develop a Fourier decomposition with respect to
x
,

\mathbb{O}(\tau)=\mathbb{O}_{0}(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\cos(2\pi kx)\,\mathbb{O}_{k}(y).

(2.28)

In
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, where
x=\theta/2\pi
, the mode number
k
is the total instanton number. Instanton-anti-instanton pairs contribute integer powers of
q\bar{q}=e^{-4\pi y}
, where
q\coloneqq e^{2\pi i\tau}
, with total instanton number zero.

The Roelcke-Selberg spectral decomposition takes the following form:

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}(\tau)=\langle\mathbb{O}\rangle+\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\text{Re}\,s=\frac{1}{2}}ds\,\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}\,E^{*}_{s}(\tau)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{O},\phi_{n})\,\phi_{n}(\tau),

(2.29)

This defines a convergent decomposition for any
\tau\in{\cal F}
. There are three branches here.

The first is the constant function,
\phi_{0}={\rm vol}({\cal F})^{-1/2}
, so
\langle\mathbb{O}\rangle={\rm vol}({\cal F})^{-1}(\mathbb{O},1)
is the normalized average of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
over
{\cal F}
:

\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{O}\rangle:={\rm vol}({\cal F})^{-1}\int_{\cal F}{dxdy\over y^{2}}\,\mathbb{O}(\tau)\end{split}

(2.30)

The bracket notation indicates that this is equivalent to the normalized
ensemble
average of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
, that is, the average value of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
over the
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetry-preserving conformal manifold with respect to the Zamolodchikov measure. This identification between modular and ensemble averages is special to
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, in which the Zamolodchikov metric is exactly hyperbolic due to maximal supersymmetry
[
64
]
, unlike other sub-maximal theories whose conformal manifolds admit an
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
action (e.g.
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD in four dimensions
[
65
,
47
]
).

The second is the continuous branch of non-holomorphic Eisenstein series,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tau}E^{*}_{s}(\tau)=s(1-s)E^{*}_{s}(\tau)\,,\quad{\rm Re\,}(s)=\frac{1}{2}.

(2.31)

The star denotes that we use the “completed” Eisenstein series, whose Fourier expansion with respect to
x
is

E^{*}_{s}(\tau)=\Lambda(s)y^{s}+\Lambda(1-s)y^{1-s}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}4\cos(2\pi kx)\frac{\sigma_{2s-1}(k)}{k^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\sqrt{y}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi ky),

(2.32)

where
\Lambda(s):=\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)
is the completed Riemann zeta function and
\sigma_{2s-1}(k)
is the divisor function. This normalization is convenient because it manifests the reflection symmetry

\begin{split}E^{*}_{s}(\tau)=E^{*}_{1-s}(\tau)\,.\end{split}

(2.33)

The overlap with
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
is then a rescaled Petersson inner product, which may be simplified to

\begin{split}\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}&:=\Lambda(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{O},E_{s})\\
&=\Lambda(s)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,y^{-s-1}\,\mathbb{O}_{0}(y)\,.\end{split}

(2.34)

The second line will be important for the physics: the Eisenstein overlap is fully determined by the zero mode of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
, i.e. the projection of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
onto the sector of zero total instanton number. This sector includes the weak-coupling regime
g_{\rm YM}^{2}\ll 1
. The reflection symmetry of
E^{*}_{s}(\tau)
implies the same property of
\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}
. Note that the ensemble average may be extracted from the
s\rightarrow 1
limit of the Eisenstein overlap,

\langle\mathbb{O}\rangle={1\over 2}\,\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}\,,

(2.35)

which follows from
E_{0}(\tau)=1
.

The third is an infinite discrete branch of Maass cusp forms in the so-called
L^{2}
norm,
(\phi_{n},\phi_{n})=1
. These functions, infinite in number, obey the eigenvalue equation

\Delta_{\tau}\phi_{n}(\tau)=\left({1\over 4}+t_{n}^{2}\right)\phi_{n}(\tau)\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,\quad t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\,,\quad 0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots

(2.36)

Reality of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
restricts the basis to the even cusp forms, invariant under
x\rightarrow-x
. The cusp forms
\phi_{n}(\tau)
have no zero mode, instead decaying exponentially at the cusp
y\rightarrow\infty
, hence contributing to neither the perturbative nor ‘t Hooft regimes. The cusp forms are rich mathematical objects exhibiting various signs of arithmetic chaos
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
; however, quite intriguingly, the integrated correlators
\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N)}(\tau)
will be seen to have
vanishing
cusp form overlap, so we will not say anything further about the cusp forms here.

In summary, the entire content of
\mathbb{O}(\tau)
is stored in the spectral overlaps
\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}
and
(\mathbb{O},\phi_{n})
.

Looking ahead slightly, our computations will begin from the zero mode,
\mathbb{O}_{0}(y)

\begin{split}\mathbb{O}_{0}(y)=\langle\mathbb{O}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\operatorname{Re}s=\frac{1}{2}}ds\,\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}\Lambda(s)y^{s}\end{split}

(2.37)

As shown in
[
8
]

\mathcal{N}=4

\begin{split}\{\mathbb{O},E_{s}\}=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)f_{\text{p}}(s)+f_{\text{np}}(s)\end{split}

The function

]
that

must be regular at

3.2.1
p=3

In the
p=3

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{3}^{(3)}(s)&=\tfrac{1}{3}(2s-1)^{2}(s^{2}-s+18),\\

Considering many more cases up to

\begin{split}\boxed{N(N+1)\Big[f_{3}^{(N+1)}(s)-f_{3}^{(N)}(s)\Big]=2N(N-1)\,f_{2}^{(N+1)}(s)+2(N+1)(N+2)\,f_{2}^{(N)}(s)\,.}\end{split}

N=1

f_{3}^{(2)}(s)

f_{2}^{(2)}(s)\neq 0

An interesting feature of the above relation is that it has no explicit dependence on
s

p=2

2.42

p=3

This is not the only interesting relation the spectral overlaps

\displaystyle\begin{split}(N-2)(N-3)(N+1)f_{3}^{(N+1)}(s)&=\penalty\ \penalty\ (N-3)\Big[(N^{2}+2N-2)-s(1-s)\Big]\,f_{3}^{(N)}(s)\\
&\,\penalty\ \penalty\ +(N+2)\Big[(N^{2}-4N+1)-s(1-s)\Big]\,f_{3}^{(N-1)}(s)\\
&\,\penalty\ \penalty\ -(N-2)(N+2)(N+1)\,f_{3}^{(N-2)}(s)\,.\end{split}

Note that due to the factors of

3.2.2

Next,

3.3
Including instantons: completion to the full correlator

{IncludingInstantons}

Having computed the zero mode of
\mathcal{G}^{(N)}_{p}(\tau)
, and therefore the Eisenstein spectral overlap, what remains is to determine the cusp form overlap. We claim that it vanishes:

\boxed{\left(\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)},\phi_{n}\right)=0\quad\forall\penalty\ n>0\,.}

(3.22)

That is, the full integrated correlator is

\displaystyle\boxed{{\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(\tau)=\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}\rangle+\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\text{Re}\,s=\frac{1}{2}}ds\,\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)(2s-1)^{2}g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\,E^{*}_{s}(\tau)\,,}}

(3.23)

where recall that the average is
\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}\rangle={1\over 2}g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(0)
. Note how strongly constraining this is: because
g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)
is an even polynomial of degree given in (
3.7
), the integrated correlator may be completely fixed by the first
N+\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor-2
orders in weak-coupling perturbation theory.

We will provide strong support for (
3.22
) with explicit instanton computations below. The basic idea behind the check is as follows. First, one can compute the perturbative expansion of the integrated correlators in nonzero instanton sectors as predicted by (

3.4
Laplace difference equations

Having given evidence for vanishing cusp form overlap and thereby completion to the full correlator, the various recursion relations from Section
3.2
should be regarded as non-trivial identities between the
full
integrated correlators. Said another way, relations for
f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)
simply ‘uplift’ to relations for
\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N)}(\tau)
. Starting from a given recursion relation for the spectral overlap
f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)
, this is achieved by applying the replacement rules

\displaystyle f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\penalty\ \mapsto\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(\tau)-\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}\rangle\,,\qquad s(1-s)f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\penalty\ \mapsto\penalty\ \Delta_{\tau}\,\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(\tau)\,.

(3.34)

Prime among these relations is the
p=3
recursion relation (
3.9
), which uplifts to the following powerful difference equation obeyed by the integrated correlator
\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(N)}(\tau)
:

\displaystyle{N(N+1)\Big[\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(N+1)}(\tau)-\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(N)}(\tau)\Big]=2N(N-1)\,\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(N+1)}(\tau)+2(N+2)(N+1)\,\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(N)}(\tau)}\,.

(3.35)

The absence of an inhomogeneous term coming from the ensemble averages
\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}\rangle
follows from consistency of the recursion relations evaluated at
s=1
.

{ldiff}

One may, if desired, recast all of the previously derived recursion formulas as Laplace difference equations. Let us just give one more example explicitly, namely, the very simple relation (
3.13
) between
\mathcal{G}_{4}^{(N|2,2)}(\tau)
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(N)}(\tau)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{4}^{(N|2,2)}(\tau)=-(\Delta_{\tau}-2N^{2})\,\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(N)}(\tau)\,.

(3.36)

We leave other applications of (
3.34
) as an exercise for the enthusiastic reader.

The maximal-trace family of integrated correlators,

7
General
p
ansatz

There is unlikely to be a simple closed-form expression for generic integrated correlators for arbitrary multi-trace insertions
(i,j)
at arbitrary
p
, unlike for the maximal-trace correlators considered in Section
6
. However, our computations provide a good deal of insight into their general structure. In particular, the difficulties in obtaining general higher
p
results appear to be of computational nature only.
41
41

41

To be clear, generic integrated correlator computations encounter two main technical complications. The first is the degeneracy in the space of half-BPS operators and the associated mixing of chiral operators on the sphere: as explained in Section
2.2
, to obtain correlators on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
from the localisation relation (
2.14
) one first needs to solve the mixing problem on
S^{4}
. As a consequence of the growing number of multi-trace operators, the size of the corresponding mixing matrices increases rapidly with
p
. The second is the presence of multi-trace operators which leads to higher-point insertions in the matrix-model integrals. This complicates the evaluation of their expectation values considerably. As described in Appendix
B.3
this amounts to the computation of higher-index
N
-dependent coefficients, which one has to perform on a case-by-case basis.
As such, we do not expect any qualitative changes or new features to arise when considering correlators beyond the explicit cases considered so far.

{sec:genpansatz}

With some confidence afforded by the structural uniformity of our results, we now make some conjectural assertions about the form of generic integrated correlators. The ansatz is

\boxed{\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(\tau)={1\over 2}g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(0)+{1\over 4\pi i}\int_{{\rm Re\,}s={1\over 2}}ds\,{\pi\over\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)(2s-1)^{2}\,g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\,E^{*}_{s}(\tau)\,,}

(7.1)

where the function
g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)
is polynomial in
s
with rational coefficients. Let us explicate this ansatz in further detail:

•

We expect that the Eisenstein overlaps continue to be of the form

\{\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)},E_{s}\}={\pi\over\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\,,

(7.2)

with the structure

\displaystyle f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)=(2s-1)^{2}\,g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\text{deg}\left[g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)\right]=2N+2\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor-6\,.

(7.3)

The reflection-symmetric polynomials
g_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)
have the indicated degree, with expansion coefficients rational in
N
and
p
. Consequently, the integrated correlator may be completely fixed by the first
N+\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor-2
orders in weak-coupling perturbation theory.

•

In Section
3.3
, we gave robust evidence that the cusp form overlaps vanish for
\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(\tau)
by matching the form (
7.1
) to explicit calculations of instanton effects using localisation. We conjecture that this remarkable property extends to general

and all trace-structures
(i,j)
:

(\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N|i,j)},\phi_{n})=0\quad\forall\penalty\ (N|i,j),\,p,\,n>0\,.

(7.4)

•

In accord with our remarks above, the normalised ensemble averages will be
rational
functions of
N
and
p
. This is clearly visible in the maximal-trace case, see (
6.15
).

The individual averages computed in Section
4
do not seem to exhibit any particular pattern, beyond their rationality and some uniformity in the large
N
limit. On the other hand, the ensemble averages
\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N)}\rangle
of correlators of SPO’s
do
exhibit nice patterns, leading us to conjecture their general form

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N)}\rangle=Q_{p}(N)\,(N-p+1)_{p-1}\,,\qquad(\text{for SPO's})\,,

(7.5)

for some rational function
Q_{p}(N)
.
42
42

42

The fact that
Q_{p}(N)
is rational essentially follows from the rational
N
-dependence of the coefficients which appear in the weak-coupling expansion obtained from the matrix model integrals. See Appendix
B
.
The Pochhammer factor ensures the vanishing of
\langle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(N)}\rangle
for

. By considering the large

), we can further constrain the function

(i)

At leading order in large

\displaystyle Q_{p}(N)\penalty\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ N\to\infty\penalty\ }\penalty\ \frac{p-1}{2p}\,N+\ldots.

(ii)

To be consistent with the general large

\displaystyle Q_{p}(N)=\frac{p-1}{2p}\,N+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}(p)\,N^{-k}\,,

•

All recursion relations derived herein determine

•

The functional form of the recursion relations themselves is less clear. For

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{p}^{(N+1|i,j)}(s)-f_{p}^{(N|i,j)}(s)&=\Big[W_{p}^{(0)}(N)-W_{p}^{(1)}(N)\,s(1-s)\Big]\,f_{2}^{(N+1)}(s)\\
&\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\sum_{q=2}^{p-1}W_{p}^{(q)}(N)\,f_{q}^{(N|i,j)}(s)+W_{p}^{(p)}(N)\,f_{2}^{(N-1)}(s)\,,\end{split}

43
43

43

The integrated correlators

are remarkable observables: they are non-trivial functions of the complexified gauge coupling
\tau

This basis is, moreover, one in which S-duality invariance is manifest. This link suggests that there may be lessons in the offing for how to better understand
\tau

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM observables at large.

These properties raise many conceptual and fundamental questions. We close by discussing some of these, but we first describe some more concrete open problems. There are several computations that are ripe for completion, which include the following:

Another set of computations pertains to the genus-one correlators

Most fascinating are various foundational questions about these observables, centered around the question of why they exhibit such simplicity, and the relation to manifest S-duality invariance.

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}N^{p-2-2(m+n)}\tilde{\lambda}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\underset{s=-\frac{1}{2}-n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(s)f_{{p}}^{(m)}(s)\right]

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{g}=1}^{\infty}N^{p-2\mathfrak{g}}\sum_{n=0}^{\mathfrak{g}-1}\tilde{\lambda}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\underset{s=-\frac{1}{2}-n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(s)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g}-n-1)}(s)\right]

Next we ask, can we absorb the second line into the first by shifting the integration contour? The answer is yes, but under certain conditions on the overlaps.

First note that
f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)
has a finite number of poles at positive half integers,

\displaystyle s=\frac{3}{2}+n\penalty\ ,\qquad n=0,1,2,...,\mathfrak{g}-1\penalty\ ,

(G.18)

as follows from (
5.6
). For
\mathfrak{g}=0

G.3

f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)
mentioned above. In doing so we encounter the positive integer poles of
\sin(\pi s)
. Therefore we need to subtract the residue of these integer
\sin
poles. When all is said and done we land at the following expression for
\mathfrak{g}>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(\mathfrak{g}>0)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\text{Re}\,s=\mathfrak{g}+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}ds\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)\Lambda(1-s)\tilde{\lambda}^{s-1}f_{p}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{\mathfrak{g}-1}\tilde{\lambda}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\underset{s=-\frac{1}{2}-n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(s)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g}-n-1)}(s)\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\mathfrak{g}-1}\tilde{\lambda}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\underset{s=\frac{3}{2}+n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(1-s)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=2}^{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)^{k+1}\tilde{\lambda}^{k-1}k(k-1)\Lambda(1-k)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(k)

Here we note the condition for consistency of the weak-coupling expansion, which must not have half-integer powers of
\lambda

\displaystyle\underset{s=-\frac{1}{2}-n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(s)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g}-n-1)}(s)\right]=\underset{s=\frac{3}{2}+n}{\operatorname{Res}}\left[\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s}s(1-s)\Lambda(1-s)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)\right]

(G.20)

which is precisely eq. (7.9) of
[

. Hence, this consistency condition implies our final result, a spectral representation of the genus-
\mathfrak{g}
correlator purely in terms of the genus-
\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(\mathfrak{g}>0)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\text{Re}\,s=\mathfrak{g}+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}ds\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)\Lambda(1-s)\tilde{\lambda}^{s-1}f_{p}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(s)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=2}^{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)^{k+1}\tilde{\lambda}^{k-1}k(k-1)\Lambda(1-k)f_{{p}}^{(\mathfrak{g})}(k)

This is (
5.3.1

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{(0)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\text{Re}\,s=1+\epsilon}ds\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}s(1-s)\Lambda(1-s)\tilde{\lambda}^{s-1}f_{p}^{(0)}(s)

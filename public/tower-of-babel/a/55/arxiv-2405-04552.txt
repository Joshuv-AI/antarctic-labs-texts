Nonstandard arguments for results about infinite systems of equations in infinitely many variables
David A. Ross

Abstract
. Short nonstandard proofs are given for some results about infinite systems of equations in infinitely many variables.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
26E35, 46S20, 15A06, 40H05, 46A45, 12E12

This note is motivated by Nathanson
[
11
]
, which describes several results of the form:

If some property P is true for every finite subset of a set of objects, then P is true simultaneously for all the objects.

For example, he examines a result of Abian and Eslami
[
2
]
about certain sets
\mathfrak{F}
of equations in infinitely many variables, where it is shown that if every finite subset of
\mathfrak{F}
has a common solution of some form then
\mathfrak{F}
has a common solution of that form.

Results of this form are familiar to mathematical logicians as
compactness
results.
In this note I give nonstandard proofs for some of the results in Nathanson
[
11
]
, as well as others. In nonstandard analysis the compactness principle is built into the construction of the nonstandard model, and so these results become completely transparent with the nonstandard machinery. This also makes it possible to identify natural generalizations of the theorems; see, for example, Theorem
5
below.

Questions about infinite systems of equations in infinitely many variables go back to the early days of functional analysis; arguably the starting point for the whole field. The Abian–Eslami result is a relative of a 1913 result of F. Riesz
[
14
]
. Riesz’s proof, reproduced in Banach’s 1932 Theory of Linear Operations
[
4
]

1

1

an extension of Banach’s 1922 thesis
uses something today we would recognize as a special case of the Hahn–Banach Extension Theorem (Hahn
[
5
]
, Banach
[
3
]
), which should probably be attributed to Helly (
[
6
]
; see Narici and Beckenstein
[
10
]
for the historical discussion).

It should also be noted that nonstandard analysis has proved a useful tool in understanding the Hahn–Banach Theorem. In 1962 Wim Luxemburg
[
8
]
showed that the Boolean Prime Ideal Theorem implies Hahn–Banach. A few years later he refined the result

This paper can be viewed as a companion to the forthcoming Nathanson and Ross

\mathcal{L}=\langle\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1},+,\times,\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{c}_{j}\rangle_{i\leq n,j\in I}

\{p(\overline{\mathbf{c}})=\mathbf{0}:p(\overline{x})\in MFF\}

This result does not easily extend to infinite rings. For example, Abian gives the following example:

\displaystyle(x-1)-y_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle(x-2)-y_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle(x-3)-y_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-13.17496pt*\hskip 13.17496pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-13.17496pt\hskip 13.17496pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-13.17496pt*\hskip 13.17496pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-13.17496pt\hskip 13.17496pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-10.8299pt*\hskip 10.8299pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-10.8299pt\hskip 10.8299pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-10.11455pt*\hskip 10.11455pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-10.11455pt\hskip 10.11455pt}}}}\cdot\mathbf{x}|\qquad(\textrm{by definition of }\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N})

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-13.17496pt*\hskip 13.17496pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-13.17496pt\hskip 13.17496pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-13.17496pt*\hskip 13.17496pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-13.17496pt\hskip 13.17496pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-10.8299pt*\hskip 10.8299pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-10.8299pt\hskip 10.8299pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-10.11455pt*\hskip 10.11455pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-10.11455pt\hskip 10.11455pt}}}}\|_{p}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{q}\qquad(\textrm{by }{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-31.69449pt*\hskip 31.69449pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-31.69449pt\hskip 31.69449pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-31.69449pt*\hskip 31.69449pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-31.69449pt\hskip 31.69449pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-24.51399pt*\hskip 24.51399pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-24.51399pt\hskip 24.51399pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-20.18079pt*\hskip 20.18079pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-20.18079pt\hskip 20.18079pt}}}})

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{a}_{i}^{N}\|_{p}M

\displaystyle\to 0\textrm{ as }N\to\infty\quad\textrm{(by Proposition~\ref{theprop}(ii)}

which proves (3), and the theorem.
∎

3.
Generalizing the result

We now consider two possible generalizations: easing the bound
M
(as discussed in the remarks after Theorem
2
), and replacing the exact solution with an approximation. The following does both at once:

Theorem 5
.

Let
p,q>1
be conjugate, ie
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
M_{n}>0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
I
be an infinite set,
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\ell^{p}
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
i\in I
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists an
\mathbf{x}\in\ell^{q}
with
\mathbf{a}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}=b_{i}
for all
i\in I
and
|x_{n}|\leq M_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

There is a positive sequence
\{e_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}e_{N}=0
such that for
every finite
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
and every
\epsilon>0
, there is an
\mathbf{x}
with (a)
\|\mathbf{x}^{N}\|_{q}\leq{e_{N}}
for all
N
,
(b)
|x_{n}|\leq M_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and (c)
|\mathbf{a}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b_{i}<\epsilon
for
i\in I^{\prime}

Proof.

\mathbf{(1\Rightarrow 2)}
Given
\mathbf{x}
, let
e_{N}=\|\mathbf{x}^{N}\|_{q}
. (2) is immediate.

\mathbf{(2\Rightarrow 1)}
By saturation there is an
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\ell^{q}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.58302pt*\hskip 8.58302pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.58302pt\hskip 8.58302pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ell^{q}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.58302pt*\hskip 8.58302pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.58302pt\hskip 8.58302pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\ell^{q}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.32123pt*\hskip 7.32123pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.32123pt\hskip 7.32123pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\ell^{q}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.9601pt*\hskip 6.9601pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.9601pt\hskip 6.9601pt}}}}
such that (a)
\|\mathbf{x}^{N}\|_{q}\leq{e_{N}}
for all standard
N
,
(b)
|x_{n}|\leq M_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbb{N}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.90282pt*\hskip 5.90282pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.90282pt\hskip 5.90282pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{N}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-4.90286pt*\hskip 4.90286pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-4.90286pt\hskip 4.90286pt}}}}
(including standard
n
), and (c)
|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.57408pt*\hskip 7.57408pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.57408pt\hskip 7.57408pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.85873pt*\hskip 6.85873pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.85873pt\hskip 6.85873pt}}}}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b_{i}|\approx 0
for
i\in I
.

Put
y_{n}={\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{x_{n}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-11.1586pt\circ\hskip 11.1586pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-11.1586pt\hskip 11.1586pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{x_{n}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-11.1586pt\circ\hskip 11.1586pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-11.1586pt\hskip 11.1586pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{x_{n}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-9.43872pt\circ\hskip 9.43872pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-9.43872pt\hskip 9.43872pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{x_{n}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-8.86235pt\circ\hskip 8.86235pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.86235pt\hskip 8.86235pt}}}}
for standard
n
. As in the proof of Theorem
4
, it remains to prove three things:

(1)

\mathbf{y}\in\ell_{q}

(2)

|y_{n}|\leq M_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(3)

\mathbf{a}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{y}=b_{i}
for (standard)
i\in I

(2) is immediate. If
N<K
are standard then

\sum_{n=N+1}^{K}|y_{n}|^{q}\approx\sum_{n=N+1}^{K}|x_{n}|^{q}\leq\|\mathbf{x}^{N}\|_{q}^{q}\leq{e_{N}^{q}}

Let
K\to\infty
, obtain
\|\mathbf{y}^{N}\|_{q}\leq{e_{N}}
. By the earlier observation,
\mathbf{y}\in\ell^{q}
, proving (1).

For (3), let
N
be a standard positive integer, then

\displaystyle\left|b_{i}-\sum_{n\leq N}a_{in}y_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|b_{i}-{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.57408pt*\hskip 7.57408pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.57408pt\hskip 7.57408pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.85873pt*\hskip 6.85873pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.85873pt\hskip 6.85873pt}}}}\cdot\mathbf{x}\right|+\left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{in}x_{n}-\sum_{n\leq N}a_{in}y_{n}\right|+\left|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.57408pt*\hskip 7.57408pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.57408pt\hskip 7.57408pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.85873pt*\hskip 6.85873pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.85873pt\hskip 6.85873pt}}}}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\sum_{n\leq N}a_{in}x_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\approx|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.57408pt*\hskip 7.57408pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.57408pt\hskip 7.57408pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.85873pt*\hskip 6.85873pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.85873pt\hskip 6.85873pt}}}}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{N}|\qquad(\textrm{by definition of }\mathbf{x}^{N})

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-8.91951pt*\hskip 8.91951pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.91951pt\hskip 8.91951pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-7.57408pt*\hskip 7.57408pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.57408pt\hskip 7.57408pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbf{a}_{i}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.85873pt*\hskip 6.85873pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.85873pt\hskip 6.85873pt}}}}\|_{p}\|\mathbf{x}^{N}\|_{q}\qquad(\textrm{by }{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-31.69449pt*\hskip 31.69449pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-31.69449pt\hskip 31.69449pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-31.69449pt*\hskip 31.69449pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-31.69449pt\hskip 31.69449pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-24.51399pt*\hskip 24.51399pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-24.51399pt\hskip 24.51399pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\textrm{H\"{o}lder}}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-20.18079pt*\hskip 20.18079pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-20.18079pt\hskip 20.18079pt}}}})

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{a}_{i}\|_{p}e_{N}

\displaystyle\to 0\textrm{ as }N\to\infty

where for the second line the first term is second term is infinitesimal since
x_{n}\approx y_{n}|
for every
n\leq N
and the second term is infinitesimal by choice of
\mathbf{x}
. This proves (3), and the theorem.
∎

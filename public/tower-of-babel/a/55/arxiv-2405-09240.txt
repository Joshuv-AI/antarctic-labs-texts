Electromagnetic response of dense quark matter around color-superconducting phase transition and QCD critical point Journal: Annals of Physics
Toru Nishimura, Masakiyo Kitazawa, Teiji Kunihiro

Abstract
We explore how the electric conductivity and associated relaxation time are modified near the QCD critical point and the phase transition to a color superconducting phase using the two-flavor Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model with finite current quark masses. We give a comprehensive account of the nature of the soft modes associated with these phase transitions and how they affect the photon self-energy when the system approaches these phase transitions in a combined way with an emphasis on the common and different aspects in the two transitions. The formalism developed for describing the paraconductivity in metallic superconductors is used for the analysis of the photon self-energy. We show that the transport coefficients calculated from the self-energy show anomalous enhancements in both cases with different critical exponents for the individual transitions. We briefly discuss the possibility of detecting the enhancements in the relativistic heavy-ion collisions in the present and future facilities.



1
Introduction

Revealing the phase structure of hot and dense matter governed
by QCD is one of the central subjects of
the present theoretical and experimental nuclear physics.
It is expected that such a matter under extreme conditions admits rich phase transitions
[
1
]
including the first-order phase transition in the high-density
and low-temperature region,
the QCD critical point(s) (QCD-CP) as the endpoint(s) of the first-order transition line
[
2
,
3
]
,
and the onset of color superconducting (CSC) phases
[
4
]
.
The hot and dense matter can be created in the intermediate stage of the
relativistic heavy-ion collisions (HIC)
[
5
]
. In the
HIC experiments, the baryon number density of the produced matter can be varied by the beam-energy scan, i.e. changing the collision energy
[
6
,
7
]
.
Such experiments are now ongoing at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) at BNL, the Super Proton Synchrotron (SPS) at CERN, and the Heavy Ion Synchrotron SIS18 at GSI, and
more refined experimental studies will be pursued at future facilities
[
8
]
.

To explore the phase transitions of QCD in these experiments, it is of basic importance to clarify
adequate experimental observables for their identification.
Promising candidates that have been discussed widely are observables that are sensitive to
fluctuations of the order parameter enhanced around the critical point of the second-order phase transitions
[
9
]
.
In the case of the QCD-CP,
active studies have been being made to extract fluctuations of various observables including their higher-order cumulants by the event-by-event analyses
[
10
,
6
,
11
]
.

Because of the penetrating property in the strongly interacting medium,
the electromagnetic probes would also be useful to detect the fluctuations enhanced at the phase transitions.
In fact, the present authors have recently shown
[
12
,
13
]
that
the dilepton-production rate (DPR) is a promising probe for the search for not only the QCD-CP but also the two-flavor superconductor (2SC), which is one of the CSC phases.
The critical fluctuations near a second-order phase transition form the soft modes, i.e. the collective modes that become massless at the transition point. In Refs.
[
12
,
13
]
,
it was shown that the DPR is significantly affected by the soft modes near the phase transition to the 2SC (2SC-PT) and the QCD-CP.
More specifically, a prominent enhancement of the DPR occurs near these phase transitions, especially in the low energy/momentum region, triggered by the scatterings of photons via the soft modes.
If such enhancements
were observed in the HIC,
it could be a signature of these
phase transitions
[
14
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is twofold.
First, using the two-flavor Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model as an effective model of QCD,
we give a systematic and extended analysis of the soft modes of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP.
Although most of the calculational procedures are
based on the formulation given in Refs.
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
12
,
13
]
,
an emphasis will be put on the common and different properties of
the soft modes associated with these phase transitions.
Moreover, some extensions of the formalism are made, such as an inclusion of the finite quark mass in the analysis of the diquark mode.
The second purpose is the analysis of the electric conductivity and associated relaxation time.
We shall explore how these transport coefficients are affected by the soft modes near the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP.
Although the electric conductivity of hot and dense hadronic matter has been investigated
in various studies
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
, its quantitative behavior near the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP has not been studied so far to the best of our knowledge.
These coefficients are important inputs for the hydrodynamic models for the spacetime evolution of heavy-ion reactions
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
.
Moreover, since they are directly connected to the low energy-momentum structure of the DPR, their analysis will provide insights into the quantitative understanding of the DPR near the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP.

To investigate the effects of the soft modes on the transport coefficients,
we employ a similar formalism used for the analysis of the DPR in Refs.
[
12
,
13
]
.
We calculate the modification of the photon self-energy due to
the Aslamazov–Larkin (AL)
[
28
]
, Maki–Thompson (MT)
[
29
,
30
]
and density of states (DOS) terms
[
31
]
,
all of which involve the propagators of the soft modes.
These terms have been used in condensed matter physics to investigate anomalous excesses of conductivity near the critical temperature
T_{c}
of metallic superconductors.
Although they incorporate effects of the soft modes only at the mean-field level in the sense that nonlinear effects are not included, they are known to describe the excess of conductivity near
T_{c}
well in metallic superconductors
[
31
,
32
]
. Therefore, their analysis should be a good starting point to investigate the transport coefficients near the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP quantitatively.
Whereas the formalism to obtain the transport coefficients is basically the same as our previous study, we perform some extensions of the analysis and elucidate detailed calculational procedures skipped in Refs.
[
12
,
13
]
.
Through the simultaneous formalisms for the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP, we also clarify the similarities and differences in the analysis and physical properties.

We show that the transport coefficients are divergent at the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP. Their critical exponents are calculated analytically. It will be shown that the electric conductivities
have different critical exponents for the QCD-CP and 2SC-PT.
The behavior of the coefficients on the QCD phase diagram will also be calculated numerically.

The organization of the present paper is as follows.
Section
2
is devoted to the explanation of the model and its phase diagram.
In Sec.
3
, we discuss the propagators of the soft modes associated with the 2SC-PT and the QCD-CP. Effective descriptions for their low energy-momentum behavior are then introduced in Sec.
4
.
In Sec.
5
, we calculate the photon self-energy
including the soft modes.
We then show the analytical and numerical results of the transport coefficients in Sec.
6
.
Finally, we summarize and conclude in Sec.
7
.

2
Model and phase diagram

To investigate the phase transitions in dense quark matter and their effects on the transport coefficients,
we employ the 2-flavor NJL model
[
33
,
34
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}i(\not{\partial}-m)\psi+G_{S}[(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}+(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}]+G_{D}(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi^{C})(\bar{\psi}^{C}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi),

(1)

where
\psi(x)
is the quark field and

\psi^{C}(x)=i\gamma_{2}\gamma_{0}\bar{\psi}^{T}(x)
.

\vec{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})
are the Pauli matrices for the flavor
SU(2)_{f}

and
\lambda_{A}~(A=2,5,7)
are the antisymmetric components
of the Gell-mann matrices for the color
SU(3)_{c}
.
The scalar coupling constant
G_{S}=5.50~\rm{GeV^{-2}}
and
the three-momentum cutoff
\Lambda=631~{\rm MeV}
are determined
so as to reproduce the pion mass
m_{\pi}=138~\rm{MeV}
and
the pion decay constant
f_{\pi}=93~\rm{MeV}

at the current quark mass
m=5.5~{\rm MeV}

[
33
]
.
Since the value of the diquark coupling

G_{D}
is not constrained by the vacuum property, we treat it as a free parameter and vary within
G_{D}/G_{S}=0.6
–
0.7
, which is an intermediate range in various estimates
[
34
]
.

In the mean-field approximation (MFA) assuming the nonzero chiral condensate
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle

and diquark condensate
\langle\bar{\psi}^{C}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi\rangle

at temperature
T
and quark chemical potential
\mu
is calculated to be
[
15
]

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm MFA}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(M-m)^{2}}{4G_{S}}+\frac{|\Delta|^{2}}{4G_{D}}-4\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{E_{\bm{p}}+T{\rm log}\big(1+e^{-\xi_{+}/T}\big)\big(1+e^{-\xi_{-}/T}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt+\ \epsilon_{+}+{\rm sgn}(\xi_{-})\epsilon_{-}+2T{\rm log}\big(1+e^{-\epsilon_{+}/T}\big)\big(1+e^{-{\rm sgn}(\xi_{-})\epsilon_{-}/T}\big)\bigg\},

(2)

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359ptE_{\bm{p}}=\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}+M^{2}},\qquad\xi_{\pm}=E_{\bm{p}}\pm\mu,\qquad\epsilon_{\pm}=\sqrt{\xi_{\pm}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}},

(3)

where
M=m-2G_{S}\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle
and

\Delta=-2G_{D}\langle\bar{\psi}^{C}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi\rangle

are the constituent quark mass and the gap of the 2SC, respectively.
The expectation values
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle
and
\langle\bar{\psi}^{C}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi\rangle
for a given set of
T
and
\mu

are given by minimizing
\omega_{\rm MFA}
.
The stationary condition gives the gap equations
\partial\omega_{\rm MFA}/\partial M=0
and

\partial\omega_{\rm MFA}/\partial\Delta=0
,
which are explicitly given by

\displaystyle M-m=

\displaystyle~8G_{S}M\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{E_{\bm{p}}}\bigg\{1-n(\xi_{+})-n(\xi_{-})+\frac{\xi_{+}}{\epsilon_{+}}{\rm tanh}\frac{\epsilon_{+}}{2T}+\frac{\xi_{-}}{\epsilon_{-}}{\rm tanh}\frac{\epsilon_{-}}{2T}\bigg\},

(4)

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle~8G_{D}\Delta\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{+}}{\rm tanh}\frac{\epsilon_{+}}{2T}+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{-}}{\rm tanh}\frac{\epsilon_{-}}{2T}\bigg\},

(5)

where
n(x)=1/(1+e^{x/T})
is the Fermi distribution function.
In Fig.
1
, we show the phase diagram
in the
T
–
\mu
plane obtained by the MFA.
The solid line shows the first-order transition line
and the circle marker at

(T_{\rm CP},\,\mu_{\rm CP})\simeq(46.712,\,329.34)~{\rm MeV}

indicates the QCD-CP.
The dashed, dash-dotted, and dotted lines show the second-order 2SC-PT
for
G_{D}/G_{S}=0.70
,
0.65
, and
0.60
, respectively.
The critical temperature of the 2SC-PT increases as
G_{D}
becomes larger.

Soft modes

In this section, we examine the collective excitations of
the diquark
\bar{\psi}^{C}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi

and chiral
\bar{\psi}\psi
fields, which couple to the soft modes of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP, respectively
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Most of this section is
a combined recapitulation of Refs.
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
12

13
]
, with
an emphasis on the common and different properties of the soft modes associated with these phase transitions.
Some extensions of the formalism are also made, such as an inclusion of the finite quark mass in the analysis of the diquark field.

3.1
Response functions

According to the linear response theory
[
38
]
,
dynamical properties of the fluctuations of a field operator
{\cal O}(\bm{x},t)
are encoded in the response function, which is equivalent to the retarded Green’s function

\displaystyle D^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{3}xdte^{i\omega t-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}D^{R}(\bm{x},t),

(6)

\displaystyle D^{R}(\bm{x},t)=

\displaystyle\ -i\langle[{\cal O}(\bm{x},t),{\cal O}(\bm{0},0)]\rangle\theta(t),

(7)

for a bosonic operator
{\cal O}(\bm{x},t)
with
[A,\,B]=AB-BA
being the commutator.
The poles of
D^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
at
\omega=\omega(\bm{k})
,
which exist in the lower-half complex-energy plane, represent the collective modes that couple to
{\cal O}(\bm{x},t)
.
When the imaginary part of
\omega(\bm{k})
is small, the dynamical structure factor defined by

\displaystyle S(\bm{k},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{e^{\omega/T}-1}{\rm Im}D^{R}(\bm{k},\omega),

(8)

has a peak around
\omega={\rm Re}\,\omega(\bm{k})
,
which conspicuously shows the existence of the collective mode.
The response functions that couple to the soft modes of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP are given by substituting

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{A}(\bm{x},t)=\bar{\psi}^{C}(\bm{x},t)i\gamma_{5}\tau_{2}\lambda_{A}\psi(\bm{x},t)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\hat{\sigma}(\bm{x},t)=\bar{\psi}(\bm{x},t)\psi(\bm{x},t),

(9)

into the operator
{\cal O}(\bm{x},t)

in Eq. (
7
), respectively,
which are denoted as
D^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
D^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
in what follows.

The response functions are obtained from Eqs. (

\displaystyle D^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\ {\cal D}_{\gamma}(k)|_{i\nu_{n}\to\omega+i\eta},\quad\gamma=D,S,

(12)

with
\eta
being a positive infinitesimal number.

3.2
Random-phase approximation

We calculate Eqs. (

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\gamma}(k)=\frac{{\cal Q}_{\gamma}(k)}{1+G_{\gamma}{\cal Q}_{\gamma}(k)}={\cal Q}_{\gamma}(k)-{\cal Q}_{\gamma}(k)G_{\gamma}{\cal Q}_{\gamma}(k)+\cdots,

\displaystyle{\cal Q}_{D}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{3}xdte^{i\nu_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\langle T_{\tau}\hat{\delta}_{A}(\bm{x},\tau)\hat{\delta}_{A}^{\dagger}(\bm{0},0)\rangle_{\rm free}

\displaystyle\ -2N_{f}(N_{c}-1)\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[{\cal G}_{0}(p){\cal G}_{0}(k-p)],

\displaystyle{\cal Q}_{S}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{3}xdte^{i\nu_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\langle T_{\tau}\hat{\sigma}(\bm{x},\tau)\hat{\sigma}(\bm{0},0)\rangle_{\rm free}

\displaystyle\ \int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{c,f,D}[{\cal G}_{0}(p){\cal G}_{0}(k+p)]

\displaystyle\ -2N_{f}N_{c}\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[{\cal G}_{0}(p){\cal G}_{0}(k+p)].

\displaystyle\lim_{|\bm{k}|\rightarrow 0}{\rm Im}Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},a|\bm{k}|)=a\frac{N_{f}N_{c}M^{2}}{2\pi}\sum_{t=\pm}\bigg\{{\rm tanh}\frac{\lambda_{a}-2t\mu}{4T}-{\rm tanh}\frac{\bar{\Lambda}-2t\mu}{4T}\bigg\}\theta\bigg(\frac{2\Lambda}{\bar{\Lambda}}-|a|\bigg),

(23)

with
\lambda_{a}=\sqrt{4M^{2}/(1-a^{2})}
.

To calculate the real parts of
Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
,
it is convenient to use the Kramers-Kronig relation

\displaystyle{\rm Re}Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{P}{\pi}\int^{\bar{\Lambda}-2\mu}_{-\bar{\Lambda}-2\mu}d\omega^{\prime}\frac{{\rm Im}Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega^{\prime})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega},

(24)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{P}{\pi}\int^{\bar{\Lambda}}_{-\bar{\Lambda}}d\omega^{\prime}\frac{{\rm Im}Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega^{\prime})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega},

(25)

with Eqs. (
19
) and (
21
), where
P
denotes the principal value.
The integral regions in Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
)
are chosen so as to be consistent with the cutoff for the
E_{1}
integral in Eq. (
18
).
In fact, one can easily verify that Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
)
agree with the retarded functions obtained by the analytic continuation
of Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) with Eq. (
18
).

3.3
Thouless criterion

Since
D^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
D^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
possess
all the information of the collective modes coupled to
the operators in Eq. (
9
),
they encode the properties of the respective soft modes.
As mentioned already the soft modes become massless at the second-order phase transition. This property is ensured by the Thouless criterion
[
39
]
given as a peculiar property of

D^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
D^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)

that these functions have a pole
at the origin in the complex
\omega
plane at
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
at the critical point.

Figure 5
:

Diagrammatic representation of
\Xi^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)
.

To show the Thouless criterion,
it is convenient to introduce
the retarded
T
-matrix
\Xi^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)

defined by

\displaystyle\Xi^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{\gamma}^{-1}+Q^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)}=G_{\gamma}-G_{\gamma}D^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)G_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,G_{\gamma}-G_{\gamma}Q^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)G_{\gamma}+G_{\gamma}Q^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)G_{\gamma}Q^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)G_{\gamma}-\cdots,

(26)

which also means

D^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)=G_{\gamma}^{-1}Q^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)\Xi^{R}_{\gamma}(\bm{k},\omega)
.
The diagrammatic representations of
\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
\Xi^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
are given in Fig.
5
.
Then, it is readily confirmed that these functions satisfy

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{0},0)=2\frac{\partial^{2}\omega_{\rm MFA}}{\partial\Delta^{2}},\qquad{\Xi^{R}_{S}}^{-1}(\bm{0},0)=2\frac{\partial^{2}\omega_{\rm MFA}}{\partial M^{2}}.

(27)

Since the thermodynamic potential satisfies

\partial^{2}\omega_{\rm MFA}/\partial\Delta^{2}=0
at the 2SC-PT
and
\partial^{2}\omega_{\rm MFA}/\partial M^{2}=0
at the QCD-CP,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{\gamma}}^{-1}(\bm{0},0)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad{D^{R}_{\gamma}}^{-1}(\bm{0},0)=0,

(28)

at the respective critical points.
Equation (
28
)
proves the existence of a pole in
D_{\gamma}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
at the origin
(|\bm{k}|,\omega)=(0,0)
,
i.e. the Thouless criterion surely holds.

The pole at
\omega=0
at the critical point
moves continuously in the lower-half complex-energy plane as a function of
T
and
\mu
.
This means the existence of the soft mode, i.e.
the collective mode near the origin that eventually becomes massless at the critical point.

In the above argument, we derived the Thouless criterion based on the MFA and the RPA. However, the validity of the Thouless criterion generally holds even beyond the MFA, since the second-order phase transition is characterized by the flattening of the effective potential
[
31
]
. This means that the appearance of the soft modes is guaranteed even beyond the MFA as long as the phase transition is of second order.

When the effects beyond the MFA are incorporated, however, it is possible that the second-order phase transition becomes first-order or crossover. In this case, the soft mode does not become massless because of the absence of the second-order transition. Even in this case, however, the softening of the order-parameter field is expected if the first-order transition is weak or the crossover transition takes place sharply. In later sections, we calculate the transport coefficients assuming the second-order phase transition. While this always applies to the QCD-CP, the 2SC-PT may not be of second-order
[
4
]
. Even in this case, however, the qualitative feature of the following discussion would not change drastically as long as the 2SC-PT is close to the second-order transition and soft collective modes manifest themselves near the phase transition.

As discussed in Sec.
3.2
,

D^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
is analytic at the origin, while
D^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
is not.
This difference leads to
a qualitative difference in the analytic properties of the soft modes of 2SC-PT and QCD-CP.
In terms of the dynamical structure factor,

S_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
is a smooth function around the origin, while

S_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
is not.
In
D^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
, it is known that a pole in the space-like region behaves as the soft mode that moves
toward the origin in the complex
\omega
plane
[
16
,
17
]
,
while any poles in the time-like region do not show such a softening.
The latter property can be confirmed easily from the fact that the spectral supports of
S_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)

in the time-like region exist only
at
|\omega|>\sqrt{\bm{k}^{2}+4M^{2}}
.
Since
M
stays nonzero at the QCD-CP,
the pole in the time-like region never becomes massless.

Before closing this subsection, let us
briefly comment on the effect of the current quark mass
m
on the properties of the soft modes.
First, it is readily found by an explicit calculation that
the properties of the soft mode of the 2SC-PT are hardly affected by
m
.
This result is understood intuitively from
the fact that
the soft mode of the 2SC-PT is the diquark mode that
is governed by the excitations near the Fermi surface
that are less affected by the quark mass when
\mu\gg M
.
On the other hand, the properties of the soft mode of the QCD-CP are crucially affected by
m
since
the QCD-CP becomes a tri-critical point in the
m\to 0
limit.
One can also argue that the soft mode of the QCD-CP is less affected by the diquark gap
\Delta
,
while that of the 2SC-PT is
crucially
modified by it.

4
Effective theory of soft modes

In this section, we derive effective formulas for
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
and
\Xi_{S}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
that describe low-energy behavior of the soft modes near the 2SC-PT or QCD-CP.
These formulas will be utilized in the next section for the analysis of the photon self-energy.

4.1
Soft mode of 2SC-PT

Let us start from the soft mode of the 2SC-PT, which is encoded in the
T
-matrix
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
. Since the excitation energy of the soft mode approaches zero at the critical point,
it is reasonable to approximate
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)

by expanding its inverse
{\Xi^{R}_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)
with respect to
\omega
and pick up the first two terms as

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)\simeq A_{D}(\bm{k})+C_{D}\omega,

(29)

with

\displaystyle A_{D}(\bm{k})=G_{D}^{-1}+Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},0),\qquad C_{D}=\frac{\partial Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{0},\omega)}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega=0},

(30)

which are found to be real and complex numbers, respectively.
Owing to the Thouless criterion (
28
),

A_{D}(\bm{0})=0,

at
T=T_{c}
.
In Eq. (
29
), we have neglected the
\bm{k}
dependence of
C_{D}

as well as higher order terms of
\omega
.
We call Eq. (
29
) the low-energy (LE) approximation.
In Ref.
[
12
]
, this formula has been employed for the calculation of the photon self-energy and the DPR near the 2SC-PT.

To simplify the approximation further, one may replace
A_{D}(\bm{k})
with its Taylor expansion at
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
.
By picking up the first two terms of the expansion, one has

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)\simeq a_{D}+b_{D}\bm{k}^{2}+c_{D}\omega\simeq\tilde{a}_{D}\epsilon+b_{D}\bm{k}^{2}+c_{D}\omega,

(31)

where the coefficients
a_{D}
,
b_{D}
, and
c_{D}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{D}=~\frac{1}{G_{D}}+Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{0},0),\quad b_{D}=~\frac{\partial Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},0)}{\partial\bm{k}^{2}}\bigg|_{|\bm{k}|=0},\quad c_{D}=C_{D}.

(32)

with

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{T-T_{c}}{T_{c}},

(33)

being the reduced temperature.
In the far-right-hand side of Eq. (
31
),
we have utilized
the fact that
a_{D}=0
at
T=T_{c}
and
made a further approximation

\displaystyle a_{D}\simeq\tilde{a}_{D}\epsilon\hskip 8.5359pt{\rm with}\hskip 8.5359pt\tilde{a}_{D}=T\frac{\partial Q^{R}_{D}(\bm{0},0)}{\partial T}\bigg|_{T=T_{c}}.

(34)

Equation (
31
) is known as the linearized time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau (TDGL) approximation,
as
{\Xi^{R}_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)\Delta(\bm{k},\omega)=0
with Eq. (
31
) corresponds to the linearized TDGL equation
[
31
]
.
In this approximation, the pole of the soft mode determined by
\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)=0
is located at
\omega=-(a_{D}+b_{D}\bm{k}^{2})/c_{D}
. The stability of the TDGL equation requires
{\rm Im}c_{D}<0
, which means that the pole lies on the lower-half complex-energy plane.

When the real part of
c_{D}
is small,
|{\rm Re}c_{D}|\ll|{\rm Im}c_{D}|
, Eq. (
31
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{1}{a_{D}+b_{D}\bm{k}^{2}+c_{D}\omega}=\frac{i}{|{\rm Im}c_{D}|}\frac{1}{\omega+i\tau_{\rm GL}^{-1}\big(1+\xi_{D}^{2}\bm{q}^{2}\big)}.

(35)

Here,
\tau_{\rm GL}=|c_{D}|/a_{D}
and
\xi_{D}=\sqrt{b_{D}/a_{D}}
are interpreted as the relaxation time and the coherence length of the soft mode, respectively.

As was discussed in Sec.
3.2
,
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
is analytic in the shaded regions in the left panel of Fig.
4
. Since Eq. (
31
) is obtained from the expansion at the origin, it applies only to the region (2) in the panel that includes the origin. The same argument also applies to Eq. (
29
). Therefore, when we use Eq. (
29
) or (
31
) in the following analyses we assume that
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
vanishes outside the region (2), although this treatment hardly modifies the final result since
S_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
is well suppressed there.

.

As discussed in

, the coefficients in Eq. (

) can be calculated analytically in the large
\Lambda
limit,
\Lambda\gg T+|\mu|
, at
M=0
, which yields

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{D}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{\pi^{2}}\mu^{2}\bigg(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\frac{T^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\bigg),

(36)

\displaystyle b_{D}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{4\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{7\zeta(3)}{12\pi^{2}}\frac{\mu^{2}}{T^{2}}+\log\frac{\Lambda^{2}-\mu^{2}}{4T^{2}}+2\gamma_{\rm E}-2\log\frac{\pi}{4}-1\Big),

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\ c_{D}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{\pi^{2}}\mu\bigg(\log\frac{\Lambda^{2}-\mu^{2}}{4T^{2}}+2\gamma_{\rm E}-2\log\frac{\pi}{4}+1\bigg),

(38)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\ c_{D}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{4\pi}\frac{\mu^{2}}{T}.

(39)

Assuming further
T/\mu\ll 1
and neglecting the logarithmic terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{D}=\frac{2N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{\pi^{2}}\mu^{2},\quad b_{D}=\frac{7N_{f}(N_{c}-1)\zeta(3)}{48\pi^{4}}\frac{\mu^{2}}{T^{2}},\quad c_{D}=-i\frac{N_{f}(N_{c}-1)}{4\pi}\frac{\mu^{2}}{T},

(40)

where in the last equation we neglected the real part of
c_{D}
which is parametrically suppressed compared to the imaginary part.
On account of Eq. (
40
),
\tau_{D}
and
\xi_{D}
in Eq. (
35
)
are reduced to

\displaystyle\tau_{\rm GL}=\frac{\pi}{8T}\frac{1}{\epsilon},\qquad\xi_{D}=\sqrt{\frac{7\xi(3)}{96T^{2}}}\,\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/2}}.

(41)

This result shows that
\tau_{\rm GL}
and
\xi_{D}
are divergent for
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Moreover, the coefficients do not depend on
\mu
, which implies that the property of the soft mode is insensitive to
\mu
.

Finally, let us inspect the validity of the LE and TDGL approximations for
\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
numerically.

)

).

\omega
and
\bm{k}
.

4.2
Soft mode of QCD-CP

Next, we perform a similar analysis for the soft mode of the QCD-CP.
To this end, we adopt the LE approximation to
\Xi^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
as

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{S}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)\simeq A_{S}(\bm{k})+C_{S}(\bm{k})\omega,

(42)

with

\displaystyle A_{S}(\bm{k})=G_{S}^{-1}+Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},0)\quad\textrm{and}\quad C_{S}(\bm{k})=\frac{\partial Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega=0},

(43)

which are found to be real and pure imaginary, respectively.
Here, in contrast to the case of Eq. (
29
),
we keep the
\bm{k}
dependence of
C_{S}(\bm{k})
,
since an explicit manipulation shows that

C_{S}(\bm{k})
diverges as
1/|\bm{k}|
for

\bm{k}\rightarrow\bm{0}
.
This difference originates from the fact that
\Xi_{S}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
is not analytic at the origin as discussed in Sec.
3.2
, in contrast to
\Xi_{D}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
that is analytic there.
We also note that
\Xi_{S}^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)
is discontinuous at
|\omega|=\bar{k}(|\bm{k}|,\bar{\Lambda})\simeq|\bm{k}|
. Therefore, the LE approximation (
42
) is applicable only for
|\omega|<\bar{k}(|\bm{k}|,\bar{\Lambda})
, i.e. the region (2) in the right panel of Fig.
4
, which is within the space-like region.
Since the soft mode of the QCD-CP has a spectral support only in the space-like region as discussed already, the approximation (
42
) is adequate for describing it.
In Ref.
[
13
]
, Eq. (
42
) is adopted
for the analysis of the DPR near the QCD-CP.

To describe the low energy-momentum behavior,
one would further expand
the coefficients as

\displaystyle{\Xi^{R}_{S}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)\simeq\ a_{S}+b_{S}|\bm{k}|^{2}+c_{S}\frac{\omega}{|\bm{k}|},

(44)

with

\displaystyle a_{S}=\frac{1}{G_{S}}+Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{0},0),\qquad b_{S}=\frac{\partial Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},0)}{\partial\bm{k}^{2}}\bigg|_{|\bm{k}|=0},\qquad c_{S}=\frac{\partial(|\bm{k}|Q^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)\big|_{|\bm{k}|\to 0})}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega=0}.

(45)

We refer to Eq. (
44
) as the TDGL approximation for
\Xi^{R}_{S}(\bm{k},\omega)
in analogy with Eq. (
31
).
When we use Eqs. (
42
) or (
44
), it is assumed that
{\Xi^{R}_{S}}^{-1}(\bm{k},\omega)=0
outside the region (2) in the right panel of Fig.
4
. We also note that the last term in Eq. (
44
) is finite in the limit
|\bm{k}|\to 0
because
|\omega|<|\bm{k}|
is always satisfied in region (2).

The parameter
a_{S}
in Eq. (
44
) vanishes at the QCD-CP. However, its behavior around there is more
subtle than the case of the 2SC-PT.
As discussed in
B
, when
T
and
\mu
approach the QCD-CP linearly with a fixed ratio
T-T_{\rm CP}:\mu-\mu_{\rm CP}
, it behaves as

\displaystyle a_{S}\sim\begin{cases}~\epsilon_{\rm CP}&\text{parallel to the first-order line,}\\
~\epsilon_{\rm CP}^{2/3}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(46)

in the MFA with

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm CP}=\sqrt{~\bigg(\frac{T-T_{\rm CP}}{T_{\rm CP}}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(\frac{\mu-\mu_{\rm CP}}{\mu_{\rm CP}}\bigg)^{2}~}.

(47)

We have numerically checked Eq. (
46
) in our model.
We will use Eq. (
46
) in Sec.
6
to discuss the behavior of the transport coefficients near the QCD-CP.

5
Photon self-energy
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)

In this section, we calculate the photon self-energy
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)
incorporating the effects of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP soft modes.
In these analyses, we use the effective propagators of the soft modes obtained by the LE and TDGL approximations introduced in the previous section.
When the LE approximations (
29
) or (
42
) is adopted,
the formalism is similar to those in Ref.
[
12
,
13
]
, while we shall give detailed accounts
of the calculational procedures omitted there.
The difference between the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP and the analyses with the TDGL approximation (
31
) or (
44
) will also be discussed.

At the leading order of electromagnetic interaction, the photon self-energy
in the imaginary-time formalism is given by the electromagnetic current-current correlation function as
[
40
]

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\bm{k},i\nu_{l})=\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)=\int d^{4}xe^{i\nu_{l}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\langle T_{\tau}[j^{\mu}(\bm{x},\tau),j^{\nu}(\bm{0},0)]\rangle,

(48)

with the electromagnetic current
j^{\mu}(\bm{x},\tau)
.
The retarded self-energy
\Pi^{R\mu\nu}(\bm{k},\omega)
is then obtained through the analytic continuation
\Pi^{R\mu\nu}(\bm{k},\omega)=\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\bm{k},i\nu_{l})|_{i\nu_{l}\to\omega+i\eta}
.
As we will see in the next section, the transport coefficients are obtained from the low energy-momentum limit of its imaginary part of the spatial components,

{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}(\bm{k},\omega)
.

We assume that
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)
consists of three parts

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)=\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{\rm free}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{S}(k),

(49)

where
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(k)
and
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{S}(k)

are the contributions from the soft modes of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP, respectively, that will be constructed below and

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{\rm free}(k)=N_{c}C_{\rm em}\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p+k)\gamma^{\nu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p)],\quad\quad(C_{\rm em}\equiv e_{u}^{2}+e_{d}^{2})

(50)

is the self-energy of the free-quark system
where
e_{u}=2|e|/3
(
e_{d}=-|e|/3
) is the electric charge of the up (down) quark with
e
being the electron charge.

From the charge conservation, the total photon self-energy
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)
in Eq. (
49

\displaystyle k_{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)=0.

(51)

We assume that
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(k)
and
\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{S}(k)
in Eq. (
49
) separately satisfy

\displaystyle k_{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(k)=k_{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{S}(k)=0,

(52)

while
k_{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}_{\rm free}(k)=0
is readily shown from Eq. (
50
).

\displaystyle=3\int_{q}\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(q)\ \mathcal{R}_{{\rm DOS},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k),

(57)

denote the contributions of the AL, MT, and DOS terms, respectively,
and

q=(\bm{q},i\nu_{n})
is the four-momentum of the soft mode.
The vertex functions
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{D}(q,k)
,

\mathcal{R}_{{\rm MT},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)
and

\mathcal{R}_{{\rm DOS},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)

in Eqs. (
55
)–(
57
) are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{D}(q,q+k)

\displaystyle=4(N_{c}-1)e_{\Delta}\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[\mathcal{G}_{0}(p)\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p+k)\mathcal{G}_{0}(q-p)],

(58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{{\rm MT},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)

\displaystyle=8(N_{c}-1)~e_{u}e_{d}\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[\mathcal{G}_{0}(p)\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p+k)\mathcal{G}_{0}(q-p-k)\gamma^{\nu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(q-p)],

(59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{{\rm DOS},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)

\displaystyle=4(N_{c}-1)(e_{u}^{2}+e_{d}^{2})\sum_{s=\pm}\int_{p}{\rm Tr}_{D}[\mathcal{G}_{0}(p)\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p+sk)\gamma^{\nu}\mathcal{G}_{0}(p)\mathcal{G}_{0}(q-p)],

(60)

with
e_{\Delta}=e_{u}+e_{d}
being the electric charge of the diquarks.
It is nicely shown that Eqs. (
58
)–(
60
)
satisfy the WT identities

\displaystyle k_{\mu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{D}(q,q+k)

\displaystyle=e_{\Delta}[\tilde{\Xi}_{D}^{-1}(q+k)-\tilde{\Xi}_{D}^{-1}(q)],

(61)

\displaystyle k_{\mu}\mathcal{R}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(q,k)

\displaystyle=e_{\Delta}[\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{D}(q-k,q)-\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{D}(q,q+k)],

(62)

with
\mathcal{R}^{\mu\nu}_{D}(q,k)=\mathcal{R}_{{\rm MT},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)+\mathcal{R}_{{\rm DOS},D}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)
.
Using Eqs. (
61
) and (
62
), it is easy to show that Eq. (
54
) does satisfy the WT identity in Eq. (
52
).

In the above procedure,
\tilde{\Pi}_{D}^{\mu\nu}(q)
is constructed from the one-loop diagram in Fig.
8
. This means that our procedure corresponds to the linear approximation in the sense that the interaction
between the soft modes is not taken into account.
The validity of the linear approximation would be
lost when
T
is quite close to

T_{c}
where the amplitude of the diquark fluctuations is large.
Our analysis does not apply to such a temperature region,
where the non-linear effects give rise to the critical exponents of transport coefficients
specific to the dynamical universality class
[
41
]
.
Nevertheless, a formalism analogous
to ours for metallic superconductivity
is applied successfully
to describe the precursory phenomena like the anomalous enhancement
of the electric conductivity
in metals in a wide range of
T
above
T_{c}

[
31
,
32
]
.
It is thus expected that our following analysis
is also capable
of describing quantitative behaviors of the transport coefficients in a certain
range of
T
that is not too close to
T_{c}
.
To extend the formalism to the close vicinity of
T_{c}
, one may start from
the thermodynamic potential with multiple loops of
\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(q)

[
42
]
.
The properties of the soft mode will also be modified there, which will be amended
via the self-consistent treatment
[
43
]
.

Another issue in the present analysis
is that the equilibrium state is determined in the MFA
with use of the thermodynamic potential Eq. (
2
), where
the contribution of the fluctuations (
53
) are not
taken into account.
In this sense, our formalism is not self-consistent.
In the present study, however, we neglect these effects to investigate the magnitude
of fluctuations qualitatively in a simple formalism.

5.1.2
Vertices

The calculation of Eq. (
54

\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(q)
is a simple propagator.
We thus have recourse to an approximate method, which should have a good validity near the 2SC-PT.

For the construction of such an approximation, we employ the approximate formula (
29

31
) for the
T
-matrix
\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)
,
which reproduce the low energy-momentum behavior of the soft modes as was shown in the previous section.
When the TDGL approximation is adopted,
since Eq. (

is governed only by three parameters,

Once Eq. (

) or (

\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{D}(q,q+k)

) dependence.

\tilde{\Gamma}^{0}_{D}(q,q+k)

\displaystyle\lim_{k_{\mu}\to 0}\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}_{D}(q,q+k)

5.1.3
Photon self-energy

Now that the effective forms of the vertices are obtained,
the next step is the calculations of Eqs. (
55
)–(
57
).
However, using the fact that Eq. (
72
) is a real function
of momentum only, it is shown that

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\big[\Pi^{Rij}_{{\rm MT},D}(\bm{k},\omega)+\Pi^{Rij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(\bm{k},\omega)\big]=0,

(73)

i.e. the MT and DOS terms cancel out exactly in
{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}(\bm{k},\omega)
.

Equation (
73
) is proved as follows. We manipulate

\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm MT},D}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(k)
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm MT},D}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(k)=

\displaystyle~3\int_{q}\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(q)\mathcal{R}^{ij}_{D}(q,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~3\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}R^{ij}_{D}(\bm{q},\bm{k})\oint_{C}\frac{dq_{0}}{2\pi i}\frac{{\rm coth}\frac{q_{0}}{2T}}{2}\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(\bm{q},q_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~3\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}R^{ij}_{D}(\bm{q},\bm{k})\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi i}\frac{{\rm coth}\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{2T}}{2}\bigg(\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega^{\prime})-\Xi^{A}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~3\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}R^{ij}_{D}(\bm{q},\bm{k})\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}{\rm Im}\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega^{\prime})\coth\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{2T}.

(74)

In the second equality, we have used the fact that
{\cal R}_{D}^{ij}(q,k)

does not depend on Matsubara frequencies and transformed the Matsubara sum into the integral
over the contour
C
on the complex-energy plane that encircles the imaginary axis.
In the third equality, the contour is deformed so as to avoid the cut in
\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(q)
on the real axis with
\Xi^{A}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega)=\tilde{\Xi}_{D}(\bm{q},i\nu_{m})|_{i\nu_{m}\to\omega-i\eta}=\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega)^{*}
.
The last line of Eq. (
74
) shows that
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm MT},D}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(k)
is real, since this expression only contains real functions. Moreover, Eq. (
74
) shows that
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm MT},D}(k)+\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(k)
does not depend on
k_{0}
. Therefore, its analytic continuation to the retarded function
\Pi^{Rij}_{{\rm MT},D}(\bm{k},\omega)+\Pi^{Rij}_{{\rm DOS},D}(\bm{k},\omega)
is also real, which proves Eq. (
73
).
A similar argument on the MT and DOS terms in the metallic superconductivity is found in Ref.
[
31
]
.

To calculate the electric conductivity and the relaxation time, we need only the imaginary part of the spatial components,
{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}(\bm{k},\omega)
.
Equation (
73
) thus shows that
we only have to compute the AL term for this purpose.
The explicit form of
{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}_{D}(k)

is obtained from Eq. (
55
) by taking the analytic continuation
i\nu_{l}\rightarrow\omega+i\eta
as

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}_{D}(\bm{k},\omega)=

\displaystyle\ {\rm Im}\Pi^{Rij}_{{\rm AL},D}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~3\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Gamma_{D}^{i}(\bm{q},\bm{q}+\bm{k})\Gamma_{D}^{j}(\bm{q}+\bm{k},\bm{q})\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}{\rm coth}\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{2T}

\displaystyle\ {\rm Im}\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q},\omega^{\prime})\Big\{{\rm Im}\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q}+\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}+\omega)-{\rm Im}\Xi^{R}_{D}(\bm{q}-\bm{k},\omega^{\prime}-\omega)\Big\}.

(75)

To prove Eq. (
73
), we have used the fact that the vertex
R_{D}^{ij}(\bm{q},\bm{k})
is a real
function of momenta
\bm{q}
and
\bm{k}
only.
In the above argument,
this property of
R_{D}^{ij}(\bm{q},\bm{k})
is obtained through
the LE or TDGL approximation and the WT identities, which are well justified at small
\omega
and
|\bm{k}|
near
T_{c}
.
On the other hand, its validity may be violated at large energy or momentum.
Nevertheless, it is expected that this possible violation
does not significantly affect the critical enhancement of the transport coefficients
near
T_{c}
where the soft modes in the low energy-momentum region play crucial roles.

6
Transport coefficients

In this section, we calculate the electric conductivity
\sigma
and associated relaxation time
\tau_{\sigma}

near the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP using the photon self-energy obtained in the previous section.

We start with the spectral density at zero momentum

\displaystyle\rho(\omega)\equiv-\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm Im}\Pi^{Rii}(\bm{0},\omega).

(97)

It is reasonable to require that the low energy-momentum behavior of the electric current
j^{0}(\bm{x},t)
in the rest frame of the medium obeys

\displaystyle\Big(\tau_{\sigma}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-D\nabla^{2}\Big)j^{0}(\bm{x},t)=0,

(98)

Then, it can be shown
[

that

\displaystyle\rho(\omega)=3\frac{\sigma\omega}{\tau_{\sigma}^{2}\omega^{2}+1}.

(99)

Equation (

\sigma
and
\tau_{\sigma}
can be extracted from

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\partial\rho(\omega)}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega=0}\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{\sigma}=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{3!}\frac{\partial^{3}\rho(\omega)}{\partial^{3}\omega}\bigg/~\frac{\partial\rho(\omega)}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega=0}}\;.

(100)

Corresponding to the fact that

\displaystyle\rho(\omega)=\rho_{\rm free}(\omega)+\rho_{D}(\omega)+\rho_{S}(\omega),

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm free}(\omega)=-\sum_{i}{\rm Im}\Pi_{\rm free}^{Rii}(\bm{0},\omega),\qquad\rho_{D}(\omega)=-\sum_{i}{\rm Im}\Pi_{D}^{Rii}(\bm{0},\omega),\qquad\rho_{S}(\omega)=-\sum_{i}{\rm Im}\Pi_{S}^{Rii}(\bm{0},\omega).

7
Summary

In the present article,
we have explored how the electric conductivity
\sigma
and the associated relaxation time
\tau_{\sigma}
are affected by the soft modes of the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP near these phase transitions.
The basic motivation lies in the fact that if the phase transition is of second order there should exist the soft mode, which is the collective excitation associated with the
fluctuations of the order parameter that becomes massless at the critical point.
After clarifying the properties of the soft modes of these phase transitions with an emphasis on their similarities and differences, their effect on the photon self-energy for each transition is calculated with a similar formalism as in our previous study
[
12
,
13
]
.
The effects of the soft modes on the photon self-energy are taken into account using the idea developed in condensed matter physics to describe the para-conductivity in metals. We calculated the Aslamazov-Larkin (AL), Maki-Thompson (MT), and Density of states (DOS) terms for the photon self-energy in a gauge-invariant manner.
We have shown that the contributions of the MT and DOS terms to the self-energy cancel out with each other in the imaginary part of the spatial component, hence their calculation is not necessary for the analysis of the transport coefficients.
We have shown that
\sigma
and
\tau_{\sigma}
are both divergent at the 2SC-PT and QCD-CP with a power-like behavior with different critical exponents.

The present work can be extended in various directions.
Although the present analysis is restricted to the precursory phenomena in the normal phase with vanishing diquark condensates, the same analysis could be performed
within the CSC
phase
[
45
]
,
where it is necessary to take into account the effects of the nonzero
diquark condensates.
Another interesting subject is to
incorporate the coupling between the soft mode of the QCD-CP
and the density fluctuations and/or other fluid-dynamical fluctuations.
It is known that the soft mode of the QCD-CP is a diffusive mode due to the coupling to the density fluctuations
[
16
,
17
,
46
]
,
while this effect is not included in our formalism.
To investigate this coupling in a systematic way in the NJL model,
the introduction of the vector interaction
[
47
]
would be indispensable.
It would be intriguing to investigate the effects of such couplings on the various transport coefficients.

We are aware that our analysis is not self-consistent in the sense that the effects of the soft modes are not incorporated into the thermodynamic potential nor the quark propagators that compose the soft modes.
Because of this incompleteness, we have refrained from discussing the critical behavior of static observables, such as the quark-number susceptibility
\chi
, whereas the consistent analysis of
\chi

enables us to calculate interesting observables such as the diffusion coefficient
D=\sigma/\chi

and the shock velocity
c_{s}=(D/\tau_{\sigma})^{1/2}
near the critical points.
We leave these analyses for a future study.

Finally, let us briefly discuss the possibility of
an experimental confirmation of the
present theoretical work in the HIC.
The electric conductivity
\sigma

is related to the low energy-momentum limit of the DPR, and accordingly the DPR in the low invariant-mass region should virtually provide us with the value of
\sigma
.
Our result in Fig.
16
suggests that there are two “hot spots” of the DPR on the QCD phase diagram
reflecting the QCD-CP and 2SC-PT
[
12
,
13
]
.
This structure indicates that the enhancements of the DPR due to the QCD-CP and 2SC-PT
can be observed in separate regions in the beam-energy scan
[
14
]
.
With the aid of the recent and forthcoming developments of experimental technologies, it would be possible to extract the electric conductivity directly in the HIC
[
48
]
.
It is to be noted, however, that for obtaining clear-cut data it is indispensable to quantify other medium effects affecting the DPR
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
24
,
52
]
.
We also note that important low-energy phenomena around the critical points include the critical slowing down,
which is a nonequilibrium effect of the second-order phase transitions.
Although this effect will be significant near the critical point, it has not been considered throughout this study since our formalism assumes a thermal equilibrium.
Investigating the non-equilibrium effects of the critical fluctuations is another challenging extension of the present study.

Appendix B
Analysis for the exponents of
a_{S}

In this appendix, we discuss the effective potential and susceptibility near the critical point (CP) in the MFA based on the Landau theory. This allows us to understand the behavior of
a_{S}
in Eq. (
46
).

We start from the Landau free energy density

\displaystyle f(t,h;\Psi)=f_{0}+\frac{At}{2}\Psi^{2}+\frac{B}{4}\Psi^{4}-h\Psi,

(121)

of the order parameter
\Psi

for the reduced temperature
t
and the external magnetic field
h
with
A>0
,
B>0
. The CP is located at
(t,h)=(0,0)

In the MFA, the physical value of the order parameter
\Psi=\bar{\Psi}
is determined as the minimum of
f(\Psi)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Psi}\bigg|_{\Psi=\bar{\Psi}}=At\bar{\Psi}+B\bar{\Psi}^{3}-h=0.

(122)

Now, let us consider the behavior of the second-order derivative of
f(\Psi)

\displaystyle a=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial^{2}\Psi}\bigg|_{\Psi=\bar{\Psi}}=At+3B\bar{\Psi}^{2},

(123)

when the system approaches the CP.

First, for
h=0
and
t>0
we have
\bar{\Psi}=0
and

\displaystyle a=At.

(124)

For
t<0
, one has
\bar{\Psi}=(-A/B)^{1/2}
and

\displaystyle a=At+3B(-At/B)=-2At=2A|t|.

(125)

Notice that Eq. (
125
) is twice larger than Eq. (
124
) at the same
|t|
.

Next, when we take the limit
h\to 0
with
t=0
, one finds
\bar{\Psi}=(h/B)^{1/3}

from Eq. (
122
) and thus

\displaystyle a=3B^{1/3}h^{2/3}\sim h^{2/3}.

(126)

Finally, when the CP is approached linearly, i.e.
h\to 0
with
(t,h)=(ch,h)
for a constant
c
, from Eq. (
122
) we have

\displaystyle h=\frac{B\bar{\Psi}^{3}}{1-Ac\bar{\Psi}}\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}B\bar{\Psi}^{3}.

(127)

Equation (
127

\bar{\Psi}\sim(h/B)^{1/3}
in this limit, and hence the behavior of
a
is the same as Eq. (
126

In the vicinity of the QCD-CP, the singular part of the thermodynamic potential is given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}t\\

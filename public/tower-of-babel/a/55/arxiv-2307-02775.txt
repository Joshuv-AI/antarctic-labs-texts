Divergent geodesics in the Universal Teichmüller space
Xinlong Dong and Hrant Hakobyan

Abstract
. Thurston boundary of the universal Teichmüller space T ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) T(\mathbb{D}) is the space P ​ M ​ L b ​ d ​ d ​ ( 𝔻 ) PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D}) of projective bounded measured laminations of 𝔻 \mathbb{D} . A geodesic ray in T ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) T(\mathbb{D}) is of generalized Teichmüller type if it shrinks the vertical foliation of a holomorphic quadratic differential. We provide the first examples of generalized Teichmüller rays which diverge near Thurston boundary P ​ L ​ M b ​ d ​ d ​ ( 𝔻 ) PLM_{bdd}(\mathbb{D}) . Moreover, for every k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 we construct examples of rays with limit sets homeomorphic to k k -dimensional cubes. For the latter result we utilize the classical Kronecker approximation theorem from number theory which states that if θ 1 , … , θ k \theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k} are rationally independent reals then the sequence ( { θ 1 ​ n } , … , { θ k ​ n } ) (\{\theta_{1}n\},\ldots,\{\theta_{k}n\}) is dense in the k k -torus 𝕋 k \mathbb{T}^{k} .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we continue the study of asymptotic behavor of geodesics in the universal Teichmüller space
T(\mathbb{D})
started in
[
HŠ16
,
HŠ18
,
HŠ21
]
. In these works the main goal was to exhibit large families of geodesics which converge at Thurston’s boundary
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
. The latter can be identified with the space of projective classes of bounded geodesic measured laminations of the hyperbolic plane
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
, cf.
[
BŠ21
]
. In this paper we construct the first examples of divergent geodesics in
T(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, we show that the limit set of a divergent geodesic in
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
can be homeomorphic to a
k
-dimensional cube
[0,1]^{k}
, for every
k\geq 1
.

For finite dimensional Teichmüller spaces the first examples of divergent Teichmüller geodesics were given by Lenzhen in
[
Len08
]
. Limit sets of Teichmüller and Weil-Petersson geodesics in the context of finite dimensional Teichmüller spaces were actively investigated recently, see for instance
[
BLMR1
,
BLMR2
,
BLMR3
,
CMW19
,
LLR18
,
LMR18
]
. Thus the present paper can be thought of as a first step in obtaining analogous results for
T(\mathbb{D})
. The techniques used in this paper are very different from the finite dimensional ones and are more analytical in nature. Similar to
[
HŠ21
]
we rely heavily on careful estimates of classical modulus for degenerating families of curves in domains with chimneys. In
[
HŠ21
]
it was essentially shown that if
D
is a domain with chimneys s.t. the blowups of
D
near boundary points converge to a half plane, a complement of a quadrant, or a complement of a half line then the geodesic in
T(\mathbb{D})
corresponding to
D
was convergent. The idea behind constructing a divergent geodesic then is to consider domains so that the blowups near a boundary point do not converge (in Hausdorff topology). Specifically, we consider domains
D
with infinitely many chimneys accumulating to
\{0\}\times(0,\infty)
so that on some scales
D
“looks like a half-plane” and on other scales it “looks like a quadrant”. Interestingly, such a construction can yield both convergent as well as divergent geodesics. Theorems
1.1
and
3.1
characterize which of the two possibilities occurs depending on the widths and the relative positions of the chimneys. The rest of this introduction describes our results in more detail.

1.1.
Universal Teichmüller space and Thurston boundary

Let
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
denote the unit disk and the unit circle in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, respectively. The
universal Teichmüller space
, denoted by
T(\mathbb{D})
, is defined as the collection of all quasisymmetric (or qs) self-maps of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, which fix
1,i
and
-1
. Universal Teichmüller space may be equipped with the Teichmüller metric
d_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows. Given
f,g\in T(\mathbb{D})
, the
Teichmüller distanse
between
f
and
g
is defined by
d_{T}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{h}\log K_{h},

where, the infimum is over all the quasiconformal (or qc) extensions
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
of
g\circ f^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
K_{{h}}
denotes the maximal dilatation of the qc map
{h}
, see Section
2.1
for the definitions of these terms.

Thurston boundary of
T(\mathbb{D})
, denoted by
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
was defined and studied in
[
Šar05
,
BŠ21
]
. In particular, it was shown in
[
BŠ21
]
that
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
can be identified with the space of projective bounded measured laminations of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
.

In
[
HŠ16
,
HŠ18
,
HŠ21
]
Šarić and the second named author studied the asymptotic behavior of geodesic rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
. Specifically, suppose
\varphi
is a holomorphic quadratic differential
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
. If for every
t\in[0,1)
the Beltrami differential
\mu_{\varphi}(t)=t|\varphi|/\varphi
is extremal (see Section
2.3
for the definition of extremality) then the corresponding path

(1.1)

\displaystyle t\mapsto T_{\varphi}(t):=\left[\mu_{\varphi}(t)\right]

in
T(\mathbb{D})
is a geodesic ray, which will be called a
generalized Teichmüller geodesic ray corresponding to
\varphi
.
If
\varphi
is an integrable holomorphic quadratic differential then
\mu_{\varphi}(t)
is uniquely extremal by
[
Str64
]
and hence
T_{\varphi}
is a geodesic ray. We call such a ray simply a
Teichmüller geodesic ray corresponding to
\varphi
.

In
[
HŠ18
]
it was proved that
every
Teichmüller geodesic ray in
T(\mathbb{D})
converges (in weak
∗
topology) to a unique point in
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})=PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
, and that distinct Teichmüller geodesic rays converge to distinct points in
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular, there is an open and dense set of geodesics in
T(\mathbb{D})
which have distinct limits at Thurston boundary. In
[
HŠ21
]
if was shown that for a large class of
generalized
Teichmüller geodesic rays the convergence to unique points of
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
still holds, however, distinct geodesics can converge to the same point at infinity. In view of the above it is natural to ask if there are divergent geodesics in
T(\mathbb{D})
, and if so what are the possible sets in
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
which can occur as limit sets of such divergent geodesics.

1.2.
Main results

To describe our results more precisely we recall the construction of a natural class of generalized Teichmüller rays. Suppose we are given a simply connected domain
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
with a chimney. This means that there is a non-trivial finite interval
(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
(a,b)\times(0,\infty)\subset D
and
(\{a\}\cup\{b\})\times(t,\infty)\subset\partial D
for some
t>0
. Let
\phi_{D}:\mathbb{D}\to D
be a conformal map and consider the holomorphic quadratic differential
\varphi_{D}=dw^{2}
, where
w(z)=\phi_{D}(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It turns out, see e.g.
[
HŠ21
]
, that in this case Beltrami differential
t|\varphi_{D}|/\varphi_{D}
is extremal, and the path
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{D}}(t)=\left[t\frac{|\varphi_{D}|}{\varphi_{D}}\right]
is a generalized Teichmüller ray. Geometrically
t{|\varphi_{D}|}/{\varphi_{D}}
can be thought
of as the Beltrami coefficient corresponding to the vertical compression map
(x,y)\mapsto\left(x,\frac{1-t}{1+t}y\right)
of
D
, which degenerates as
t\to 1
, see Section
2.4
. Šarić and the second author considered rays
\{T_{\varphi_{D}}(t)\}
corresponding to domains with finitely many chimneys or infinitely many chimneys
(a_{i},b_{j})\times(0,\infty)
so that the sequences
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
do not have accumulation points in
\mathbb{R}
. They showed that for such domains the corresponding generalized Teichmüller rays have limits in Thurston’s boundary
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
, see
[
HŠ21
, Thm. 6.3]
.

In this paper we consider domains with accumulating chimneys.
Specifically, let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a strictly decreasing sequences of positive numbers so that
x_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Letting
Q_{4}=\{z:\Re z>0,\Im z<0\}
denote the forth quadrant in
\mathbb{C}
, we define the domain
W=W(\{x_{n}\})\subset\mathbb{C}
as follows:

(1.2)

\displaystyle W=Q_{4}\cup\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}((x_{2n+1},x_{2n})\times\mathbb{R}).

In Theorem
3.1
we give a complete description of the asymptotic behavior
of the generalized Teichmüller ray corresponding to
W
, provided the sequence
x_{n}
converges to
0
fast enough. In particular, we show that the ray
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{W}}(t)
either converges or it diverges and its limit set
\Lambda\subset PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
is homeomorphic to
[0,1]
. To state our results more precisely we introduce some notation.

Given a geodesic
\gamma
in the unit disk we denote by
\delta_{\gamma}
the Dirac mass at
\gamma
. For every chimney
C_{n}=(x_{2n+1},x_{2n})\times\mathbb{R}
we consider the point
z_{n}\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}
, which corresponds to points in
W
which are “in
C_{n}
and are near
\infty
”, i.e., if
z\to z_{n}
then
\varphi_{W}(z)\in C_{n}
and
\Im(\varphi_{W}(z))\to\infty
. Let
\gamma_{n}^{+}
and
\gamma_{n}^{-}
be the geodesics in
\mathbb{D}
connecting the preimages of
x_{2n}
and
x_{2n+1}
to
z_{n}
, respectively. Let
\lambda_{W}
be the geodesic measured lamination supported on these geodesics and giving each a unit mass, i.e.,
\lambda_{W}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\delta_{\gamma_{n}^{+}}+\delta_{\gamma_{n}^{-}})
. We also denote by
g
the geodesic connecting
-1
to
-i
. The following result shows that the limit set of
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{D}}(t)
is either a point or an interval.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
W
be a domain defined as in (
1.2
) s.t.
\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{n}}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
Then the limit set
\Lambda\subset PLM_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
of the generalized Teichmüller ray
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{W}}(t)
can be described as follows:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda=\{\llbracket s\delta_{g}+(2/3)\lambda_{W}\rrbracket:s\in[m,M]\},

where

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=1+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n+1}\frac{(-1)^{i}\log x_{i}}{\log x_{2n+1}},\quad M=1+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n+1}\frac{(-1)^{i}\log x_{i}}{\log x_{2n}}.\end{split}

In particular,
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{W}}(t)
diverges at
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
m<M
.

Theorem
1.1
is an easy consequence of Theorem
3.1
and is proved after the statement of the latter. Applying Theorem
1.1
to
x_{n}\asymp{1}/{n!}
yields an example of a convergent geodesic ray with the limit point
\llbracket\frac{3}{2}\delta_{g}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{W}\rrbracket\in PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
, see Examples
3.3
and
3.4
.

To obtain divergent geodesics we need to consider sequences
x_{n}
which converge to
0
faster than
1/n!
. For instance, we have the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
, see Example
3.5
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Fix
p>1
. Let
x_{0}=1,x_{1}=a
and
x_{n}=x_{n-1}^{p}
for
n>1
(equivalently
x_{n}=a^{p^{n-1}}
). Then
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{W}}(t)
is a generalized Teichmüller geodesic ray which diverges at Thurston boundary with a limit set given by (
3.5
), where
m=1+\frac{1}{p+1},
and
M=1+\frac{p}{p+1}
.

1.2.1.
Moduli of famililes of curves

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
3.1
we restate them in terms of the limiting behavior of moduli of families of curves.

Let
w=\phi(z)
be the conformal map of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto
W
such that
\phi^{-1}
takes
w=0
to
z=-1
,
w=1
to
z=1
, and as
z
approaches
-1
we have that
\phi(z)
tends to
\infty
while staying in the fourth quadrant of
\mathbb{C}
, see Figure
1.1
.

Given open disjoint arcs
I,J
on the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
, let
\Gamma_{I,J}=\Gamma(I,J;\mathbb{D})
be the curve family connecting
I
to
J
in
\mathbb{D}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
we denote by
V_{\varepsilon}
the vertical compession map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\varepsilon y)
. Observe that if
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(t)=\frac{1-t}{1+t}
, then
\varepsilon\to 0
as
t\to 1
, that is, as the corresponding point
[t|\phi_{W}|/\phi_{W}]\in T(\mathbb{D})
leaves every compact subset of
T(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}=V_{\varepsilon}(\phi(\Gamma_{I,J}))
. Using the connection between the Liouville measure and moduli of curve families, see Lemma
2.5
or
[
HŠ16
, Lemma 3.3]
, understanding the behavior of the geodesic
t\mapsto T_{\phi_{W}}(t)
is equivalent to understanding the behavior of
\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}
for different choices of pairs of arcs
I,J\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Namely, to show that a point
\llbracket\lambda\rrbracket\in PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
is a limit point of the generalized Teichmüller geodesic
t\mapsto T_{\phi_{W}}(t)
it is sufficient to show that
there is a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
so that an appropriate rescaling of the sequence
\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{I,J}
converges to
\lambda(I\times J)
.

It follows essentially from
[
HŠ21
]
that if the intersection of the box
I\times J
and geodesic lamination
\mathfrak{L}=\{g\}\cup\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{\gamma_{n}^{+},\gamma_{n}^{-}\}
is empty then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}}{\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=0.

On the other hand, if
(I\times J)\cap\mathfrak{L}
contains a single geodesic from
\mathfrak{L}
that is not
g
then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=\frac{2}{3}.

If
(I\times J)\cap\mathfrak{L}=g
we prove the following.

Lemma 1.3
.

Suppose
W
satisfies the conditions of Theorems
1.1
(or Theorem
3.1
). If
(I\times J)\cap\mathfrak{L}=g
then

(1.5)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}}{\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=m,\quad\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{mod}\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{I,J}}{\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=M.

Lemma
1.3
is a key result used in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
3.1
. It follows from Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
5.1
.

1.2.2.
Divergent geodesics with higher dimensional limit sets

Using Theorem
1.1
we construct for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
a domain
\mathcal{W}_{k}
such that the limit set corresponding to the ray
t\to T_{\varphi_{\mathcal{W}_{k}}}(t)
is homeomorphic to
[0,1]^{k}
. For instance, for
k=2
we have the following result, which follows from Theorem
6.1
.

Figure 1.1.
Supports of the limiting geodesic measured laminations corresponding to the divergent geodesic rays constructed in Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
a\in(0,1)
, and
p,q>1
so that
\log p/\log q
is irrational. Let
x_{n}=a^{p^{n-1}}
,
x_{n}^{\prime}=3-a^{q^{n-1}}
, and
C_{n},C_{n}^{\prime}
be chimneys over the intervals
(x_{2n+1},x_{2n})
and

(x^{\prime}_{2n},x_{2n+1}^{\prime})
, respectively. Then the generalized Teichmüller ray
t\mapsto T_{\varphi_{\mathcal{W}_{p,q}}}(t)
corresponding to the domain

W_{p,q}=\{z\,:\,0<\Re(z)<3,\,\Im(z)>0\}\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}\cup C^{\prime}_{n}

diverges at
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
and its limit set
\Lambda\subset PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
in Thurston boundary is homeomorphic to
[0,1]^{2}
.

A key result used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
is the dynamical fact that every orbit
\{T_{\theta}^{\circ n}(x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of the irrational rotation
T_{\theta}:x\mapsto\{\theta+x\}
is dense in the circle
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, where
\theta=\log p/\log q
and
\{y\}=y-\lfloor y\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of a real number
y
.

To prove a higher dimensional version of Theorem
1.4
, see Theorem
6.3
, we construct domains with
k
families of chimneys accumulating to distinct half-lines corresponding to sequences of the form
\{a^{p_{i}^{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},i\in\{1,\ldots,k\},
such that the numbers
\log p_{1},\ldots,\log p_{k}
are rationally independent. We use a well known approximation theorem of Kronecker, which generalizes the above mentioned fact about the density of the orbits of an irrational rotation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to higher dimensional tori
\mathbb{T}^{k}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

In Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
if
\llbracket\lambda_{1}\rrbracket,\llbracket\lambda_{2}\rrbracket\in\Lambda
then the supports of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the same. This raises the following question.

Question 1.5
.

Is there a limit set
\Lambda\subset PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
and geodesic measured laminations
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, so that
\llbracket\lambda_{1}\rrbracket,\llbracket\lambda_{2}\rrbracket\in\Lambda
and the supports of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are distinct.

It would be interesting to undestand the possible topology of limit sets of geodesic rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
. For instance, even the following basic question seems to be open.

Question 1.6
.

Is there a Teichmüller geodesic in
T(\mathbb{D})
so that the limit set
\Lambda\subset PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
has nontrivial topology (e.g.
\pi_{1}(\Lambda)\neq\{0\}
)?

The rest of this paper is organized as follow. In Section
2
we provide the necessary definitions, notation, and some auxiliary results. In Section
3
we state Theorem
3.1
, prove Theorem
1.1
and provide several explicit examples of divergent and convergent geodesics in
T(\mathbb{D})
. Sections
4
and
5
are the technical core of the paper, which are devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.1
. In Section

we prove the existence of generalized Teichmüller geodesics with higher dimensional limit sets.

2.
Background

2.1.
Visual boundary of the universal Teichmüller space

If
E
is a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
we will denote by
|E|
the arclength (or Hausdorff
1
-measure) of
E
.

A homeomorphism
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is said to be
quasisymmetric
if there is a constant
1\leq M<\infty
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\leq\frac{|h(I)|}{|h(J)|}\leq M,

for all circular arcs
I,J
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with a common boundary point and disjoint interiors such that
|I|=|J|
. A homeomorphism is quasisymmetric if and only if it extends to a quasiconformal map of the unit disk.

The
universal Teichmüller space
, denoted by
T(\mathbb{D})
, consists of all quasisymmetric
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, which fix
1,i
and
-1
.
The
Teichmüller distanse
between elements
f
and
g
in
T(\mathbb{D})
is defined as follows.:

\displaystyle d_{T}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\log K_{g\circ f^{-1}},

where, given a quasisymmetric mapping
h
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we denote

\displaystyle K_{h}=\inf\{K_{\tilde{h}}\,|\,\tilde{h}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}\mbox{ is a quasiconformal extension of }h\},

with
K_{\tilde{h}}
denoting the maximal dilatation of the quasiconformal map
\tilde{h}
extending
h
to
\mathbb{{D}}
. We refer to
[
Ahl06
]
for background on quasisymmetric and quasiconformal maps and, in particular, for the definitions of the maximal dilatation of a quasiconformal map and quasiconformal extensions.

The universal Teichmüller space
T(\mathbb{D})
may also be defined as the set of equivalence classes of Beltrami coefficients
[\mu]\in B_{1}/\sim
, where
B_{1}
is the unit ball in
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, and
\mu\sim\nu
whenever the corresponding quasiconformal mappings
f^{\mu},f^{\nu}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
coincide on the boundary circle, i.e.
f^{\mu}|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}=f^{\nu}|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
, see
[
GL00
]
. Given two Beltrami coefficients
\mu_{0}
and
\nu_{0}
the Teichmüller distance between
[\mu_{0}]
and
[\nu_{0}]
in
T(\mathbb{D})
is defined as follows

(2.2)

\displaystyle d_{T}([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in[\mu_{0}]\\
\nu\in[\nu_{0}]\end{subarray}}\log\frac{1+\left\|\frac{\mu-\nu}{1-\bar{\mu}\nu}\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\frac{\mu-\nu}{1-\bar{\mu}\nu}\right\|_{\infty}}.

Given a quasisymmetric mapping
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, a quasiconformal mapping
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
continuously extending
h
is called an
extremal quasiconformal mapping
(for its boundary values) if it has the smallest maximal dilatation
K_{f}
among all such extensions of
h
to
\mathbb{D}
.

A Beltrami coefficient
\mu
is said to be
extremal
or
uniquely extremal
if

\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nu\|_{\infty}\mbox{ or }\|\mu\|_{\infty}<\|\nu\|_{\infty},

respectively, whenever
\nu\sim\mu
and
\nu\neq\mu
. If
\mu
is an extremal Beltrami coefficient then

\displaystyle\mu_{s}=\frac{(1+|\mu|)^{s}-(1-|\mu|)^{s}}{(1+|\mu|)^{s}+(1-|\mu|)^{s}}\frac{\mu}{|\mu|},

is also extremal for every
s\in[0,\infty)
, see
[
FM07
]
. Moreover, with the notation as above we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{T}([0],[\mu_{s}])=s\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{T}([0],[\mu]).

Therefore, the path
s\mapsto[\mu_{s}]
is a geodesic ray in the Teichmüller metric in
T(\mathbb{D})
, i.e. it is a geodesic path starting at
[0]
, passing through
[\mu]
and leaving every compact set in
T(\mathbb{D})
as
s\to\infty
. The collection of all geodesic rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
is the
visual boundary
of the universal Teichmüller space, see
[
HŠ21
]
.

2.2.
Thurston boundary of
T(\mathbb{D})

In
[
Bon88
]
Thurston’s boundary of the Teichmüller space
T(S)
of a closed surface
S
was defined by embedding
T(S)
into the space of geodesic currents on
S
. In
[
Šar05
,
Šar15
,
BŠ21
]
the definition for a Thurston type boundary of Techmüller spaces was given for all Riemann surfaces, and in particular for
T(\mathbb{D})
. We briefly recall some of the relevant notation, see
[
HŠ21
]
.

The space
G(\mathbb{D})
of oriented geodesics on
\mathbb{D}
can be identified with
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus diag
. Observe that a quasisymmetric mapping
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
induces a self-map of the space of geodesics
G(\mathbb{D})
by mapping a hyperbolic geodesic
\gamma_{(x,y)}
to
\gamma_{(f(x),f(y))}
, for every pair of distinct point
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We denote by
\tilde{f}
the mapping induced by
f
on the space of geodesics.

A
geodesic current
is a Radon measure on
G(\mathbb{D})
.
The
Liouville current

\mathcal{L}
is a geodesic current such that for every Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus diag
we have

\mathcal{L}(A)=\int_{A}\frac{|dx||dy|}{|x-y|^{2}}.

An easy calculation shows that for a
box of geodesics

A=[a,b]\times[c,d]
we have

\mathcal{L}([a,b]\times[c,d])=\log\frac{(a-c)(b-d)}{(a-d)(b-c)}.

The universal Teichmüller space
T(\mathbb{D})
can be embedded into the space
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D})
of geodesic currents via the
Liouville embedding
by setting

\mathscr{L}(f)=(\tilde{f})^{*}(\mathcal{L})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D}),

where the right hand side denotes the pullback of the Liouville measure by
\tilde{f}
. Moreover, whenever
f
is a quasisymmetric then
\mathscr{L}(f)
is a
bounded geodesic current
in the sense that
\sup_{A}\mathscr{L}(f)(A)<\infty
, where the supremum is over all geodesic boxes
A
such that
\mathcal{L}(A)=\log 2
(thus
A
is a Mobius image of the box
[1,i]\times[-1,-i]
).

Liouville embedding
\mathscr{L}
is in fact a homeomorphism
of
T(\mathbb{D})
onto its image in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D})
equipped with the uniform weak* topology, see
[
MŠ12
,
Šar15
]
. As a set
Thurston boundary of
T(\mathbb{D})
, which is denoted by
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
,
is defined as the collection of asymptotic rays to
\mathscr{L}(T(\mathbb{D}))
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D})
. Equivalently,
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
can be
identified with the space of projective classes of bounded measured laminations on
\mathbb{D}
, or
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
, see

[
Šar05
,
Šar15
]
.

2.3.
Generalized Teichmüller rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
.

In this paper we continue the study of behavior of geodesic rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
as they approach infinity, i.e. leave every compact subset of
T(\mathbb{D})
started in
[
HŠ16
,
HŠ18
,
HŠ21
]
. Specifically, let
\varphi
be a holomorphic quadratic differential on
\mathbb{D}
. Suppose that
\mu_{\varphi}(t):=t|\varphi|/\varphi
is an extremal Beltrami differential for some (or equivalently all)
t\in(0,1)
. By (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle d_{T}([0],[\mu_{\varphi}(t)])=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\|\mu_{\varphi}(t)\|_{\infty}}{1-\|\mu_{\varphi}(t)\|_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+t}{1-t}.

Therefore, if
0<s<t<1
then by (
2.2
) again we have

d_{T}([\mu_{\varphi}(s)],[\mu_{\varphi}(t)])=d_{T}([0],[\mu_{\varphi}(t)])-d_{T}([0],[\mu_{\varphi}(s)]).

Hence the path

(2.3)

\displaystyle t\mapsto[t{|\varphi|}/{\varphi}]

is a geodesic ray in the Teichmüller metric in
T(\mathbb{D})
. We say that the path (
2.3
) is a
generalized Teichmüller ray
if the Beltrami coefficient
t\frac{|\varphi|}{\varphi}
is extremal for some (or equivalently all)
t\in(0,1)
.

2.4.
Generalized Teichmüller rays and vertical compression

A natural class of generalized Teichmüller rays can be obtained as follows. Given a simply connected domain
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
and a conformal map
\phi_{D}:\mathbb{D}\to D
consider the holomorphic quadratic differential
\varphi_{D}=dw^{2}
, where
w(z)=\phi_{D}(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If the Beltrami differential
t|\varphi_{D}|/\varphi_{D}
happens to be extremal for some
t\in(0,1)
then the generalized Teichmüller ray
T_{\varphi_{D}}(t)=[\mu_{\varphi_{D}}(t)]
for
\varphi=\varphi_{D}
can alternatively be described as follows, see
[
HŠ21
]

For every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, let
V_{\varepsilon}
be the vertical compression map of the plane, i.e.

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=(x,\varepsilon y),

and let
\phi_{D,\varepsilon}
be a conformal map of the disc
\mathbb{D}
onto
V_{\varepsilon}(D)
and let
\phi_{D}:=\phi_{D,1}:\mathbb{D}\to D.

The mapping

\psi_{D,\varepsilon}:=\phi_{D,\varepsilon}^{-1}\circ V_{\varepsilon}\circ\phi_{D}

is a quasiconformal mapping of
\mathbb{D}
onto itself with the Beltrami differential given by
\left(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\varphi_{D}|}{\varphi_{D}}
and the maximal dilatation
1/\varepsilon
.
If
V_{\varepsilon}
(respectively,
\phi_{D,\varepsilon}^{-1}\circ V_{\varepsilon}\circ\phi_{D}
) is an extremal quasiconformal map for its boundary values on
\partial{D}
(respectively, on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
), then the path

\varepsilon\mapsto\tau_{\varepsilon,{D}}=\left[\left(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\varphi_{D}|}{\varphi_{D}}\right]

with
\varepsilon
decreasing from
1
to
0
is a generalized Teichmüller ray in
T(\mathbb{D})
, and we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}([0],\tau_{\varepsilon,D})=\frac{1}{2}\log\varepsilon^{-1}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\infty.

2.5.
From Teichmüller rays to conformal modulus

Suppose
D
is a domain in the complex plane as above, and
\varepsilon\mapsto\tau_{\varepsilon,D}
is the corresponding Teichmüller ray. Abusing the notation slightly, we will denote by
\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
(or
\psi_{\varepsilon}
), if
D
is clear from the context) the corresponding quasisymmetric mapping of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Therefore, the Teichmüller ray corresponding to
D
(or
\varphi_{D}
) can also be represented as follows:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{\varepsilon}=\psi_{D,\varepsilon}.

To study the asymptotic behavior of the geodesic
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{\varepsilon}
in
T(\mathbb{D})
near
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
, i.e., as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in
[
HŠ16
,
HŠ18
,
HŠ21
]
the authors used Liouville embedding of the Teichmüller space and a connection between Liouville measure and the classical modulus of families and curves.

2.5.1.
Limit sets of geodesic rays in
T(\mathbb{D})

Let
\lambda
be a bounded measured lamination of the Hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{D}
. We will denote by
\llbracket\lambda\rrbracket
the projective class of
\lambda
. We say that a bounded projective measured lamination
\llbracket{\lambda}\rrbracket\in PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
is a
limit point
of a geodesic ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
if there is a sequence of positive numbers
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
approaching
0
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\llbracket{\mathscr{L}(\psi_{D,\varepsilon_{i}})}\rrbracket\underset{i\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\llbracket{\lambda}\rrbracket,

in the weak* topology. The
limis set
of the generalized Teichmüller ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
is the collection of all its limit points in
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
.

To obtain a more explicit expression of the limit points of the rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
we recall that for a given box
[a,b]\times[c,d]\in G(\mathbb{D})
and
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\psi_{\varepsilon})([a,b]\times[c,d])

\displaystyle=\tilde{(\psi_{\varepsilon})}^{*}(\mathcal{L})([a,b]\times[c,d])

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(\psi_{\varepsilon}([a,b])\times\psi_{\varepsilon}([c,d])).

Denoting
x^{\varepsilon}:=\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
we can write the equality above as

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\psi_{\varepsilon})([a,b]\times[c,d])=\mathcal{L}([a^{\varepsilon},b^{\varepsilon}]\times[c^{\varepsilon},d^{\varepsilon}]).

Thus, to find the limit points of a generalized Teichmüller geodesic we need to find the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{L}([a^{\varepsilon},b^{\varepsilon}]\times[c^{\varepsilon},d^{\varepsilon}])
as
\varepsilon
approaches
0
. To do this we recall the notion of modulus of families of curves.

2.5.2.
From Liouville measure to conformal modulus.

Let
\Gamma
be a family of curves in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
. A non-negative Borel function
\rho
on
\Omega
is called
admissible for
\Gamma
, if
l_{\rho}(\gamma):=\int_{\gamma}\rho(z)|dz|\geq 1
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Conformal modulus of
\Gamma
then is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}\Gamma=\inf_{\rho\mbox{\tiny{ is $\Gamma$-adm}}}\iint_{\Omega}\rho^{2}dxdy,

where the infimum is over all
\Gamma
admissible metrics
\rho
.

Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
below, summarize some of the main properties
of the modulus, which we will use below, see
[
GM05
,
LV73
,
Väi71
]
.

If
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are curve families in
\mathbb{C}
, we will say that

\Gamma_{1}

overflows

\Gamma_{2}
and will write
\Gamma_{1}>\Gamma_{2}
if every curve

\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{1}
contains some curve
\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\ldots
be curve families in
\mathbb{C}
. Then

1.

Monotonicity:
If
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma_{2}
then
\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{1})\leq\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{2})
.

2.

Subadditivity:

\mathrm{mod}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{i}).

3.

Overflowing:
If
\Gamma_{1}>\Gamma_{2}
then
\mathrm{mod}\Gamma_{1}\leq\mathrm{mod}\Gamma_{2}
.

4.

Conformal invariance:
If
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is a conformal map then

\mathrm{mod}f(\Gamma)=\mathrm{mod}\Gamma

for any curve family
\Gamma
in
\Omega
, where
f(\Gamma)
denotes the family of curves
\{f(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma\}
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

The next two examples of curve families are fundamental and will be used repeatedly throughout the paper, see
[
Ahl06
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Modulus of a rectangle)
.

Let
R=[0,l]\times[0,w]
and
\Gamma
be the family of curves in
R
connecting the vertical sides, i.e.,
\{0\}\times[0,w]
and
\{l\}\times[0,w]
. Then
\mathrm{mod}\Gamma=w/l
.

Lemma 2.3

(Modulus of an annulus)
.

Suppose
A=\{z:0<r_{1}<|z|<r_{2}\}
. Let
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{A}^{\prime}
be the families of curves in
A
separating and connecting the two boundary components of
A
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}\Gamma_{A}=\frac{\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}}{2\pi},\qquad\mathrm{mod}\Gamma_{A}^{\prime}=\frac{2\pi}{\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}}.

The following estimate for modulus will be used repeatedly below.

Given two continua
E
and
F
in
\mathbb{C}
we denote

\displaystyle\Delta(E,F):=\frac{\mathrm{dist}(E,F)}{\min\{\mathrm{diam}E,\mathrm{diam}F\}},

and call
\Delta(E,F)
the
relative distance
between
E
and
F
in

\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
HŠ18
]
)
.

For every pair of continua
E,F\subset\mathbb{C}
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}(E,F;\mathbb{C})\leq\pi\left(1+\frac{1}{2\Delta(E,F)}\right)^{2}.

In particular if
E_{n}
and
F_{n}
are such that
\Delta(E_{n},F_{n})
is bounded away from
0
then
\mathrm{mod}(E_{n},F_{n};\mathbb{C})
is bounded above.

The following result, connects Liouville measure and the moduli of curve fmilies and is central for our analysis.

Lemma 2.5

(See Lemma 3.3 in
[
HŠ16
]
)
.

Let
(a,b,c,d)
be a quadruple of points on

\mathbb{S}^{1}
in the counterclockwise order, and let
\Gamma_{[a,b]\times[c,d]}
be the family of curves in
\mathbb{D}
connecting

[a,b]
to
[c,d]
. Then

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{[a,b]\times[c,d]})-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{L}([a,b]\times[c,d])-\frac{2}{\pi}\log 4\to 0

as
\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{[a,b]\times[c,d]})\to\infty
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the
Liouville measure.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that by equation (
2.6
) we have that
\mathrm{mod}(\Gamma_{[a,b]\times[c,d]})\to\infty
if and only if
\mathcal{L}([a,b]\times[c,d])\to\infty
. Therefore it is enough to consider the asymptotic behavior of the modulus in order to find the asymptotic behavior of the Liouville measure.

We next give a criterion from
[
HŠ21
]
which will help identify which elements of
PLM_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
can occur as limit points at infinity of generalized Teichmüller rays in
T(\mathbb{D})
.

Definition 2.7
.

A set
\mathcal{B}
of boxes of geodesics is said to be dense among all boxes of geodesics if for any box
[a,b]\times[c,d]\subset G(\mathbb{D})
there exists a sequence
\{[a_{n},b_{n}]\times[c_{n},d_{n}]\}_{n}
in
\mathcal{B}
such that
a_{n}\to a
,
b_{n}\to b
,
c_{n}\to c
and
d_{n}\to d
as
n\to\infty
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain such that
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
is a generalized Teichmüller ray. Suppose that there is a bounded geodesic current
\lambda
, a function
m=m(\varepsilon)\to\infty,
as
\varepsilon\to 0,
and a sequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
such that the limit

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda([a,b]\times[c,d])=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{mod}V_{\varepsilon_{i}}(\phi_{D}(\Gamma_{[a,b]\times[c,d]}))}{m(\varepsilon_{i})}

exists for a box of geodesics
[a,b]\times[c,d]
. Then
\llbracket\lambda\rrbracket
is a limit point of the generalized Teichmüller ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
in the weak* topology if and only if (
2.7
) holds for a dense set
\mathcal{B}
of boxes of geodesics in
G(\mathbb{D})
.

We note that in
[
HŠ21
]
the sufficiency of the condition (
2.7
) for a dense set of boxes was proved for the entire ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
rather than for a sequence of points
\psi_{D,\varepsilon_{i}}
, but the same proof gives the more general result above. On the other hand, the necessity of (
2.7
) follows from (
2.4
) by using (
2.6
) and recalling the relevant definitions.

In some cases it is easier to find the limiting geodesic measured lamination using a localization result which we formulate next.

For
\eta\in(0,\pi)
we will denote by
B_{\eta}(\gamma)
the
\eta
-box containing
\gamma
, which may be thought of
as the
\eta
-neighborhood of
\gamma
. More precisely, if

\gamma=\gamma_{(a,b)}\in G(D)
for some
a=e^{i\alpha}
and
b=e^{i\beta}
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we let

\displaystyle B_{\eta}(\gamma)=(e^{i(\alpha-\eta)},e^{i(\alpha+\eta)})\times(e^{i(\beta-\eta)},e^{i(\beta+\eta)}).

With this notation we have the following consequence of Corollary
2.8
, which is an analogue of Lemma 6.6 in
[
HŠ21
]
and can be proved by selecting appropriately the family
\mathcal{B}
of boxes and using the basic properties of the modulus from Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain such that
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{D,\varepsilon}
is a generalized Teichmüller geodesic, and
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a positive sequence which converges to
0
as
i\to\infty
. Suppose for every
\gamma\in G(\mathbb{D})
there is a positive number
\eta=\eta_{\gamma}>0
s.t. the limit

(2.8)

\displaystyle m(\gamma)=m_{\eta}(\gamma):=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{mod}V_{\varepsilon_{i}}(\phi(\Gamma_{B_{\eta}(\gamma)}))}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}}}

exists for all
0<\eta<\eta_{\gamma}
and is independent of
\eta
. If there is a countable collection of geodesics
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
s.t.

if and only if

for some

\displaystyle\tau_{\varepsilon_{i},D}\underset{i\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left\llbracket\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}m(\gamma_{j})\delta_{\gamma_{j}}\right\rrbracket.

2.6.
Chimney domains and generalized Teichmüller rays

We say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a
domain with a chimney
if
\Omega
contains a product subset
C_{(a,b)}:=(a,b)\times(0,\infty)
such that
\partial C_{(a,b)}\cap\{z:\Im z>\alpha\}\subset\partial\Omega
for some
\alpha\geq 0
.

If
\Omega
is a domain with a chimney then by Theorem 6.1 in
[
HŠ21
]
the vertical compression map
V_{\varepsilon}:(x,y)\mapsto(x,\varepsilon y)
is an extremal quasiconformal map for its boundary values on
\partial{\Omega}
. Therefore, for any domain
\Omega
with a chimney the path
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{\Omega,\varepsilon}
defined in Section
2.4
is a generalized Teichmüller geodesic. Hence, to conclude that
\lambda\in PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
is a limit point of the generalized Teichmüller ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{\Omega,\varepsilon}
for a chimney domain
\Omega
, by Corollary
2.8
it is enough to verify that equality (
2.7
) holds for a dense set of boxes of geodesics.

3.
Main result and some examples

Let
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
be two sequences of positive numbers so that for
0<a_{0}<b_{0}=1
, and for
n\geq 1
we have

a_{n+1}<b_{n}<a_{n},
and
a_{n},b_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

For
n\geq 0
let
I_{n}:=(a_{n},b_{n})
and
C_{n}:=(a_{n},b_{n})\times[0,\infty)
be the “chimney” over the interval
I_{n}
. We define the domain
W=W(\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\})\subset\mathbb{C}
as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle W=\{z:\Re z>0,\Im z<0\}\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n},

and observe that
W
is a simply connected domain with a sequence of chimneys which accumulate to the part of the imaginary axis in the upper half-plane, i.e.,
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re{z}=0,\Im{z}\geq 0\}
. Note that
W
is completely determined by the sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Let
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to W
be the conformal map with the following properties

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{z\to 0}\phi^{-1}(z)&=-1\\
\lim_{z\to 1}\phi^{-1}(z)&=1\\
\lim_{\Im(z)\to-\infty}\phi^{-1}(z)&=-i.\end{split}

By the discussion in Section
2.6
the path
\varepsilon\mapsto\psi_{W,\varepsilon}
is a generalized Teichmüller geodesic. We will show that this geodesic does not converge to a unique point in
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, we will describe the limit set of the geodesic completely and will show that it is in fact homeomorphic to a one dimensional simplex (an interval). To describe the limit set of
\{\psi_{W,\varepsilon}\}
we first introduce some notation.

We denote by
z_{n}
the points on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
corresponding to the chimney
C_{n}
for
n\geq 0
. More specifically, denoting
w=\phi(z)\in W
, for
n\geq 0
we let

\displaystyle z_{n}:=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Im(w)\to+\infty\\
\Re(w)\in I_{n}\end{subarray}}\phi^{-1}(w).

We also define

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\lim_{w\to a_{n}}\phi^{-1}(w),\quad\beta_{n}=\lim_{w\to b_{n}}\phi^{-1}(w).

We will denote by
\gamma_{(x,y)}
the (unoriented) hyperbolic geodesic in
\mathbb{D}
with endpoints
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Moreover, for
n\geq 0
we denote by
\gamma_{n}^{+}
and
\gamma_{n}^{-}
the two geodesics starting at
z_{n}
and ending at
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{-}:=\gamma_{(z_{n},\alpha_{n})},\quad\gamma_{n}^{+}:=\gamma_{(z_{n},\beta_{n})}.

We also denote by
g
the gedesic from
-1
to
-i
, i.e.,
g:=\gamma_{(-1,-i)}
.

Below, as in Section
2.4
, we denote by
\tau_{\varepsilon,W}
the point in
T(\mathbb{D})
corresponding to the vertical compression of
W
by
\varepsilon>0
, i.e.,

\tau_{\varepsilon,W}=\left[\left(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\varphi_{W}|}{\varphi_{W}}\right].

The following result states that the generalized Teichmüller geodesic ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\tau_{\varepsilon,W},
does not converge to any point in Thurston boundary, and, moreover, it describes the limit set of the ray in Thurston boundary
\partial_{\infty}T(\mathbb{D})
, i.e., all the possible points in
PLM_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
which occur as weak star limits of the geodesic ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\tau_{\varepsilon,W}.

We recall that given a hyperbolic geodesic
\gamma=\gamma_{(x,y)}
in
\mathbb{D}
we will denote by
\delta_{\gamma}
the Dirac mass on
\gamma
. In particular, if
B=(a,b)\times(c,d)\subset G(\mathbb{D})
is a box of geodesics, we will have that

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma}(B)=\begin{cases}0,\mbox{ if }\gamma\in B,\\
1,\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Recall, that
g=\gamma_{(-1,-i)}
and
\delta_{g}
is the Dirac mass on the geodesic connecting
-1
and
-i
in
\mathbb{D}
. We will also denote
\lambda_{W}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\delta_{\gamma_{n}^{+}}+\delta_{\gamma_{n}^{-}})
. Thus,
\lambda_{W}
is a geodesic measured lamination supported on the geodesics connecting
z_{n}
to
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In what follows we use the notation

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{n}a_{k},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad B_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
W
be a domain defined as in (
3.1
) so that there is constant
0<c<1
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\max\left({b_{n+1}}/{a_{n}},{a_{n}}/{b_{n}}\right)<c<1

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sqrt[n]{a_{n}},\sqrt[n]{b_{n}}\right\}\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0.

Then the limit set
\Lambda
of the generalized Teichmüller ray
\varepsilon\mapsto\tau_{\varepsilon,W}
in
PML_{bdd}(\mathbb{D})
can be described as follows:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda=\{\llbracket s\delta_{g}+(2/3)\lambda_{W}\rrbracket:s\in[m,M]\},

where

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=1+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(A_{n-1}/B_{n})}{\log(1/a_{n})},\quad M=1+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(A_{n-1}/B_{n})}{\log(1/b_{n})}.\end{split}

Note that all the geodesic measured laminations
s\delta_{g}+\lambda_{W}
have the same support and they only differ by the weight on the geodesic

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Letting
x_{2n}=b_{n}
and
x_{2n+1}=a_{n}
for
n\geq 0
, with
x_{0}=1
we see that if
x_{n+1}/x_{n}\to 0

Abstract
. Let A A be a Dedekind domain of characteristic zero such that its localization at every maximal ideal has mixed characteristic with finite residue field. Let R = A ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}] be a polynomial ring and I = ( a 1 ​ U 1 , … , a c ​ U c ) ⊆ R I=(a_{1}U_{1},\ldots,a_{c}U_{c})\subseteq R an ideal, where a j ∈ A a_{j}\in A (not necessarily units) and U j U_{j} ’s are monomials in X 1 , … , X n X_{1},\ldots,X_{n} . We call such an ideal I I as a ℭ \mathfrak{C} -monomial ideal. Consider the standard multigrading on R R . We produce a structure theorem for the multigraded components of the local cohomology modules H I i ​ ( R ) H^{i}_{I}(R) for i ≥ 0 i\geq 0 . 1.
introduction

Since its introduction by A. Grothendieck in 1961, local cohomology modules have become a fundamental tool in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. The nonfinite generation of nonzero local cohomology modules makes them difficult to study. A great deal of research has been done by commutative algebraists and algebraic geometers to decode the structure and behaviour of these modules. Some finiteness properties over regular rings were observed by C. Huneke and R. Y. Sharp
[
6
]
in prime characteristic using the Frobenius action on the local cohomology modules. G. Lyubeznik
[
7
]
obtained all but one finiteness results over any regular ring containing a field of characteristic zero based on his breakthrough observation that local cohomology modules on complete regular local rings containing a field of characteristic zero are enriched with an additional structure of a ﬁnitely generated module over the ring of differential operators. Over the last few years, the D-module structure of local cohomology modules turns up as a major tool in their study over a ring containing a field. Some developments have been made for unramified and particular ramified regular local rings of mixed characteristic by using the theory of D-modules, see
[
8
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
2
]
. Unfortunately local cohomology modules may fail to have similar finiteness properties over singular rings. The local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{I}(R)
supported on monomial ideals
I
in a polynomial ring
R:=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
over a field
k
, gained interest of several researchers mainly for its connection with toric varieties. Besides, such modules are relatively simpler to handle and helps to construct examples or nonexamples. The modules
H^{i}_{I}(R)
have natural
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded structures and can be approached using combinatorial techniques. However, there is a dictionary between the D-module and the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded structure of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
, see
[
1
]
. It is well-known that
H^{i}_{I}(R)_{\underline{u}}
are finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces for all
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Readers are encouraged to look through the survey article
[
1
]
for an highlight of recent progress in studying such
H^{i}_{I}(R)
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{u\geq 0}R_{u}
be a standard graded Noetherian ring, and
R_{+}=\bigoplus_{u>0}R_{u}
be its irrelevant ideal. Let
N=\bigoplus_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{u}
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. It is long known that
H^{i}_{R_{+}}(N)_{u}
is finitely generated for all
u\in\mathbb{Z}
and vanishes for all large values of
u
. Such modules are extremely useful in the study of Hilbert coefficients, Rees algebras, associated graded rings, and several others. It is worthwhile to remark that components of a graded local cohomology module rarely carry similar properties when its support is an arbitrary homogeneous ideal. The first author
[
14
]
did a comprehensive study of the components of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
where
I
is an arbitrary homogeneous ideal of a standard graded polynomial ring
R=R_{0}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
over a regular ring
R_{0}
containing a field of characteristic zero. He tried out some questions related to the asymptotic behavior of graded local cohomology modules raised by M. Brodmann in
[
3
]
and proved that certain numerical invariants associated to
H^{i}_{I}(R)
are asymptotically stable. In
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
, we established some of these results under relaxed assumptions on the base ring
R_{0}
. However, these results are not valid in general even for local cohomology modules supported on irrelevant ideals.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain of characteristic zero such that its localization at every maximal ideal has mixed characteristic with finite residue field. Let
K
be its field of fractions. Notice that the ring of integers in a number field is an example of such ring. In
[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
, B. Bhatt et al. proved that the set of associated primes of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
as an
A
-module is finite. In light of the above discussions, we are now interested in analyzing the structure of multigraded components of local cohomology modules supported on monomial ideals
I
in a
polynomial ring
R=R_{\underline{0}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
with
R_{\underline{0}}=A
and
\deg X_{j}=e_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. But, in this case
H^{i}_{I}(R)_{\underline{u}}
are finitely generated
A
-modules for all
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as we see in Proposition
4.4
. Therefore, we turn our attention to a slightly more general ideal.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that an ideal in
R
is a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal
if it can be generated by some elements of the form
aU
where
a\in A
(possibly nonunit) and
U=X_{1}^{\xi_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\xi_{n}}
for some
\xi_{j}\neq 0
.

We point out that a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal is the ”
usual monomial ideal” (see
[
5
, Deﬁnition 1.1.1]
) if all the coefficients
a
occurring in a generating set are units.
If
I
is a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal, components of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
are not necessarily finitely generated, see Examples 6.1 and 6.2. Observe that
A
is locally a one dimensional regular local ring. One of our main achievements in this work is a structure theorem for the graded components of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
. To study the torsion-free part of
H^{i}_{I}(R)_{\underline{u}}
, we look into the module
H^{i}_{I}(R)\otimes_{A}K
in an obvious manner. If we set
S:=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
, then
H^{i}_{I}(R)\otimes_{A}K\cong H^{i}_{IS}(S)
whose structure is mostly known. It is in fact easily seen that
IS\subseteq S
is a usual monomial ideal. Throughout this article, the study will be carried out under the following setup.

1.2
.

Standard assumption.
Let
A
be a Dedekind domain of characteristic zero such that for each height one prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
A
, the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
has mixed characteristic with finite residue field. Suppose that
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
is a polynomial ring with
\deg X_{j}=e_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Let
I\subseteq R
be a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal
(see Definition
1.1
) with a generating set
\{a_{1}U_{1},\ldots,a_{c}U_{c}\},
where
a_{j}\in A
and
U_{j}
’s are monomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. We set
M:=H^{i}_{I}(R)=\bigoplus_{\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M_{\underline{u}}
and put
\mathcal{Z}:=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}H^{i}_{I}(R)\backslash\{0\}
.

Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Recall that
\mathcal{Z}
is a finite set due to
[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
. Besides, in a one-dimensional domain any two nonzero prime ideals are coprime. These two facts permit us to express the torsion part of

as a direct sum of its
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion submodules, where
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Since

is a finite field of prime characteristic by our hypotheses, we are able to utilize the results known over
\overline{R}:=R/\mathfrak{p}R\cong k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.

In this article, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume the hypothesis as in
1.2
. Take any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
A
. We set
T=\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}
and put
N=M\otimes_{A}T=H^{i}_{IS}(S)
, where
S=T[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. Let
K
and
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
denote the fraction fields of
R
and
T
, respectively. Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then the following holds.

(1)

Let

denote the torsion submodule of

. Then
t(M_{\underline{u}})=\oplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(M_{\underline{u}})
.

(2)

For each

, there are

such that

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)=E_{A}\left(\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{\ell(\underline{u})}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t(\underline{u})}\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}^{\beta_{j}(\underline{u})}A}\right).

(3)

Let

be a nonzero prime ideal in

such that

for all

. Then

cannot appear as a direct summand of

.

(4)

Write

. Then

\overline{N_{\underline{u}}}=T^{a(\underline{u})}\oplus K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{b(\underline{u})}

for some finite numbers

(5)

(6)

\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}:=\frac{M_{\underline{u}}}{t(M_{\underline{u}})}

-module.

(7)

(a)

The short exact sequence

(1.3.1)

splits

(b)

The induced short exact sequence

splits for every prime ideal

in

.

As a sequel of Theorem
1.3
(7), we show that the Bass numbers

are finite for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
in

and
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

But, we don’t know whether
(

) splits

2.
Understanding the torsion of the components

We first recollect the framework for this study.

2.1
.

Setup.
Let
A
be a Dedekind domain such that for each height one prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
A
, the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
has mixed characteristic with finite residue field. Suppose that
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
is a polynomial ring with
\deg a=\underline{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for all
a
in
A
and
\deg X_{j}=e_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Let
I=(a_{1}U_{1},\ldots,a_{c}U_{c})
be a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal in
R
(see Definition
1.1
), where
a_{j}
’s are nonzero elements in
A
and
U_{j}
’s are monomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
.
In view of the induced natural
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading on
H^{i}_{I}(R)
, we set
M:=H^{i}_{I}(R)=\bigoplus_{\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M_{\underline{u}}
. Further denote

(2.1.2)

\mathcal{Z}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M,\mathfrak{p}\neq 0\}=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}.

Apparently,
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M_{\underline{u}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M=\bigcup_{\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M_{\underline{u}}
is finite for each
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Observe that
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M
contains the zero ideal when
M
has some torsion-free element.

Notation
. Throughout this article, we refer the prime divisors of any ideal
J
of
R
by the associated primes of the
R
-module
R/J
, denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/J
.

We first discuss a few basic properties of the torsion submodule which are mostly well-known.
For the convenience of the reader, we provide the proofs.

Let
J
be an ideal in
A
and
\Gamma_{J}(-)
denote the
J
-torsion functor, which is defined by
\Gamma_{J}(N)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}(0:_{N}J^{n})
for any
A
-module
N
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I,J
be comaximal ideals in
A
and
N
be an
A
-module. Then
\Gamma_{IJ}(N)=\Gamma_{I}(N)\oplus\Gamma_{J}(N)
.

Proof.

Consider the
Mayer-Vietoris sequence

0\to H^{0}_{I+J}(N)\to H^{0}_{I}(N)\oplus H^{0}_{J}(N)\to H^{0}_{I\cap J}(N)\to H^{1}_{I+J}(N)\to\cdots.

Since
I,J
are comaximal ideals,
H^{i}_{I+J}(N)=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Particularly,
H^{0}_{I+J}(N)=0=H^{1}_{I+J}(N)
. Moreover,
I\cap J=IJ
. Thus
\Gamma_{IJ}(N)\cong\Gamma_{I}(N)\oplus\Gamma_{J}(N)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N
be an
A
-module. Then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)
is an
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module for each nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
A
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that any element
a\in A\backslash\mathfrak{p}
acts as an unit on
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)
. Observe that

\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)}{a\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\frac{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)_{\mathfrak{q}}}{\left(a\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}}=0

for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
A
. In fact,
\left(a\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
a\notin\mathfrak{p}
. Whereas,
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)_{\mathfrak{q}}=0
for each
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)\cong a\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
denote the completion of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Observe that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)
has a natural
\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-module structure. For instance take
u\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)
. If
\mathfrak{p}^{r}u=0
, then for any
a\in\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, choose
b\in A_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
a-b\in\left(\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{r}
. It is easy to verify that the action
a\cdot u=b\cdot u
gives a natural
\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-module structure on
N
. We note that if
a-c\in\left(\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{r}
for some
b\neq c\in A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
b-c=(a-c)-(a-b)\in\left(\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{r}
. So
(b-c)u=0
, i.e.,
bu=cu
. Thus the action is well-defined.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be as in (
2.1.2
). Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Take the torsion submodule
t(M_{\underline{u}})
of
M_{\underline{u}}
. Then

t(M_{\underline{u}})=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(M_{\underline{u}}).

Proof.

Notice that
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M_{\underline{u}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M
. Because
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r-1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{r}
are comaximal ideals, using Lemma
2.2
and an easy induction on
r
, we get that

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}}(N)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}(N).

Therefore, it is enough to show that
t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}}(M_{\underline{u}})
. For any
m\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}}(M_{\underline{u}})
,

(\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r})^{b}m=0

for some
b\geq 1
. Hence
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}}(M_{\underline{u}})\subseteq t(M_{\underline{u}})
. Now take
z\in t(M_{\underline{u}})
and set
J:=\operatorname{ann}_{A}z
. Suppose that
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}A/J=\{\mathfrak{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}_{t}\}
, i.e.,
\sqrt{J}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{q}_{i}
. As
Az\cong A/J
so

\operatorname{Ass}_{A}A/J=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}Az\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M.

Since
J\neq 0
, the zero ideal does not belong to
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}A/J
. Thus
\{\mathfrak{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}_{t}\}\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}
.
As
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
’s are comaximal ideals in
A
so we have
\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{q}_{i}
and hence

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{q}_{i}\right)^{w}=\left(\sqrt{J}\right)^{w}\subseteq J

for some
w\geq 1
. Therefore,
\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\right)^{w}\cdot z=0
which implies that
z\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}}(M_{\underline{u}})
.
∎

Let
T
be a PID and
N
be a finitely generated
T
-module. By the
structure theorem of finitely generated ‘modules over a PID
,

N\cong T^{s}\oplus T/(\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}})\oplus T/(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\alpha_{2}})\oplus\cdots T/(\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{\alpha_{t}})

for
s,\alpha_{i}\geq 0
and prime ideals
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}_{t}
in
T
.

Remark 2.6
.

There is a typo in
[
10

. The correct statement is:
if

is a ring homomorphism and

is flat over

\operatorname{Ass}_{B}(M\otimes_{A}B)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M}\operatorname{Ass}_{B}\left(\frac{B}{\mathfrak{p}B}\right).

Therefore, if

We now use it to describe the structure of graded components of certain local cohomology modules.
This is the main result of our article.

Theorem 2.7

(Structure theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be as in (
2.1.2
). Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)=E_{A}\left(\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{\ell(\underline{u})}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t(\underline{u})}\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}^{\beta_{j}(\underline{u})}A}\right)

for some finite numbers
\ell(\underline{u}),\beta_{j}(\underline{u}),t(\underline{u})
.

Furthermore, fix a nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
A
. Set
T=\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}
and put
N=M\otimes_{A}T=H^{i}_{IS}(S)
, where
S=T[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. We denote
\overline{N_{\underline{u}}}:=\frac{N_{\underline{u}}}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(N_{\underline{u}})}
. Then

\overline{N_{\underline{u}}}=T^{a(\underline{u})}\oplus K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{b(\underline{u})},

for some finite numbers
a(\underline{u}),b(\underline{u})\geq 0
, where
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
denotes the quotient field of
T
.

Remark 2.8
.

It is easily seen that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(N_{\underline{u}}\right)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)
. For instance, as
H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R}(H^{i}_{I}(R))
is a
T
-module by Remark
2.4
so we get

H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R}(H^{i}_{I}(R))\cong\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R}(H^{i}_{I}(R))\otimes_{R}R\right)\otimes_{T}T\cong H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R}(H^{i}_{I}(R))\otimes_{R}S\cong H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}S}(H^{i}_{IS}(S))

by repetitively using the
flat base change theorem
.

Proof.

We prove the statements in three parts. In the first part, we show that

(2.8.3)

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(N_{\underline{u}}\right)=E_{T}\left(\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{\ell(\underline{u})}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t(\underline{u})}\frac{T}{\mathfrak{p}^{\beta_{j}(\underline{u})}T}\right)

for
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}
and some finite
\ell(\underline{u}),\beta_{j}(\underline{u}),t\geq 0
. In the second part, we prove that

\overline{N_{\underline{u}}}=T^{a(\underline{u})}\oplus K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{b(\underline{u})}

for any nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
A
and some finite
a(\underline{u}),b(\underline{u})\geq 0
. Finally, in the third part, we deduce the structure of the
A
-module
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)

from (
2.8.3
).

Part 1.
Notice that
N_{\underline{u}}=M_{\underline{u}}\otimes T
and
T/\mathfrak{p}T=\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathfrak{p}\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong A/\mathfrak{p}
is a field of prime characteristic. Moreover,
T
is a complete DVR. Therefore,
\mathfrak{p}T=(\pi)
for some
\pi\in T
. We set
\overline{S}=S/\mathfrak{p}S
. The short exact sequence

0\to S\xrightarrow{\cdot\pi}S\to\overline{S}\to 0

induces a long exact sequence

(2.8.4)

\cdots\to H^{i-1}_{I}(\overline{S})\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}}H^{i}_{I}(S)\xrightarrow{\cdot\pi}H^{i}_{I}(S)\xrightarrow{\theta_{i}}H^{i}_{I}(\overline{S})\to\cdots.

Observe that
T/\mathfrak{p}T\cong B/\mathfrak{p}B\cong A/\mathfrak{p}
is a field. As
S/\mathfrak{p}S\cong\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
so
H^{i}_{I}(\overline{S})_{\underline{u}}
is a finite dimensional
A/\mathfrak{p}
-vector space for all
i\geq 0
and each
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We put
k=A/\mathfrak{p}
. Consider the short exact sequence

(2.8.5)

0\to H_{1}(\mathfrak{p},N_{\underline{u}})\to N_{\underline{u}}\xrightarrow{\cdot\pi}N_{\underline{u}}\to H_{0}(\mathfrak{p},N_{\underline{u}})\to 0.

From (
2.8.4
) we have
H_{1}(\mathfrak{p},N_{\underline{u}})=\ker\left(N_{\underline{u}}\xrightarrow{\cdot\pi}N_{\underline{u}}\right)=\left(\operatorname{image}d_{i-1}\right)_{\underline{u}}
is a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. Suppose that
\dim_{k}H_{1}(\mathfrak{p},N_{\underline{u}})=d_{0}
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(k,-)
to (
2.8.5
), we get

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(k,k^{d_{0}})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(k,N_{\underline{u}})\xrightarrow{0}\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(k,N_{\underline{u}}).

Hence
\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(k,N_{\underline{u}})

[

, Theorem 2.1 b)]
,
[
1
, 2.3]
.
It follows that for any
\underline{a}\in\{0,-1\}^{n}
,

(3.1.9)

H^{i}_{I}(V)_{\underline{u}}\cong H^{i}_{I}(V)_{\underline{a}}

for each

4.
Local cohomology modules with support
at usual monomial ideals

All the results in this section can be obtained from some known results when
A=k
is a field. We are stating them to convey an overview and for the ease of understanding of the readers.

As
A
has characteristic zero so
\operatorname{char}K=0
. We further have a natural inclusion

R:=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]\hookrightarrow V:=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.

The following result is proved in
[
11
, Theorem 3.1.4 and Theorem 3.3.3]
. Here, we provide a self-contained proof without introducing any new notations.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I\subseteq R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
be a usual monomial ideal. Then
I=\cap_{i=1}^{m}Q_{i}
, where
Q_{i}=(X_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}})
for some
a_{j}\geq 1
.

We first prove a lemma. Following the notations in
[
5
]
, we set

\mathrm{Mon}(V)=\{X_{1}^{\xi_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\xi_{n}}\mid\xi_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,n\}.

For any polynomial
f
in
V
, we write
f=\sum_{U\in\mathrm{Mon}(V)}\alpha_{U}U
where
\alpha_{U}\in K
. Then the
support of

f
, denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(f)
, is defined as

\mathrm{supp}(f):=\{U\in\mathrm{Mon}(V)\mid\alpha_{U}\neq 0\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
I
be a usual monomial ideal in
R
. Then
IV\cap R=I
.

Proof.

We know that
I\subseteq IV\cap R
. Conversely, let
f\in IV\cap R
. Suppose that
I=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{c})R
for some monomials
U_{1},\ldots,U_{c}
in
R
. Then
IV=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{c})V
is also a usual monomial ideal. Thus by
[
5
, Corollary 1.1.3]
,
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq IV
. So for any monomial
U\in\operatorname{supp}(f)
, by
[
5
, Proposition 1.1.5]
,
U=wU_{i}
for some monomial
w
in
V
. Notice that
w:=X_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{m}}^{a_{m}}
is also a usual monomial in
R
. Hence
U\in I
, i.e.,
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq I
.
Thus
f\in I
. The result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Suppose that
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{c}\}
is a usual monomial generating set of
I
. Then
IV=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{c})V
is a usual monomial ideal in
V
. Hence by
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.1]
,

IV=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i},

where
Q_{i}=(X_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}})V
for
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
. Notice that
Q_{i}^{\prime}=(X_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}})R
is a usual monomial ideal in
R
and
Q_{i}^{\prime}V=Q_{i}
. Therefore,
Q_{i}\cap R=Q^{\prime}_{i}
by Lemma
4.2
. Thus we get that

I=IV\cap R=\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}\right)\cap R=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\left(Q_{i}\cap R\right)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}^{\prime}.

The result follows.
∎

Note
.

Recall that if
I=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}Q_{i}
is an irredundant primary decomposition, then

\operatorname{Ass}R/I=\{\sqrt{Q_{i}}\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

Hence the associated primes of

are monomial prime ideals.

Let
C
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
W=C[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}]
be a polynomial ring over
C
. We set
\mathfrak{m}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})W
.
In the forthcoming theorem, we are going to use the following well-known result:

(4.2.10)

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(W)=\begin{cases}C[Y_{1}^{-1},\ldots,Y_{m}^{-1}](-1,-1,\cdots,-1)&\mbox{ if }i=m\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }.\\
\end{cases}

Note 4.3
.

Let
P
be a monomial prime ideal in
R
, that is,
P=(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{t}})
for some
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{t}\leq n
. We relabel the variables and assume that
P=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{t})
. Now we put
W=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}]
. Observe that
W\hookrightarrow W[X_{t+1},\ldots,X_{n}]=R
is a flat extension. Therefore,

\displaystyle H^{i}_{P}(R)

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{P}(W)\otimes_{W}R

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{P}(W)\otimes_{W}\left(W\otimes_{A}A[X_{t+1},\ldots,X_{n}]\right)

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{P}(W)\otimes_{A}A[X_{t+1},\ldots,X_{n}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}A[X_{1}^{-1},\ldots,X_{t}^{-1},X_{t+1},\ldots,X_{n}](-1,-1,\cdots,-1,0,\cdots,0)&\mbox{ if }i=t\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

(In the above formula we have shift of
-1
in the first

co-ordinates and no shift in the rest).
due to (
4.2.10
).

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
I
is a usual monomial ideal. Then

M_{\underline{u}}
are finitely generated
A
-modules for all
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{Ass}R/I=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}
. By Theorem
2.7
,
P_{i}=(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{r}})
for
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n
. Recall that
H^{i}_{I}(R)=H^{i}_{\sqrt{I}}(R)
for each
i\geq 0
. After re-arranging, we write

\sqrt{I}=P_{1}\cap P_{2}\cap\cdots\cap P_{s},

where
\operatorname{height}P_{1}\leq\operatorname{height}P_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\operatorname{height}P_{s}
. We use descending induction on height
h
of the ideal
I
. Suppose that
h=n
. Then
s=1
and
P_{1}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
. So
M_{\underline{u}}
is finitely generated due to (
4.2.10
).

It is now sufficient to show that the result is true when
h=r<n
assuming that it is true for ideals with height greater than or equal to
r+1
.
To prove this statement, we use ascending induction on
s
, the number of associated primes of
I
. Let
s=1
. In light of Note
4.3
, graded components
H^{i}_{P_{1}}(R)
are finitely generated. Now suppose that the result is known for
s-1
. We set
P^{\prime}:=P_{2}\cap\cdots\cap P_{s}
. Then
\sqrt{I}=P_{1}\cap P^{\prime}
.
Consider the long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{i}_{P_{1}+P^{\prime}}(R)\to H^{i}_{P_{1}}(R)\oplus H^{i}_{P^{\prime}}(R)\to H^{i}_{\sqrt{I}}(R)\to H^{i+1}_{P_{1}+P^{\prime}}(R)\to\cdots.

Since

\operatorname{height}(P_{1}+P^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{height}P_{1}+1=\operatorname{height}I+1=r+1
,
by our assumption graded components of

H^{i}_{P_{1}+P^{\prime}}(R)
are finitely generated. Again by induction hypothesis on
s
, graded components of

H^{i}_{P^{\prime}}(R)
are finitely generated. It follows that
graded components of
H^{i}_{I}(R)
are finitely generated.
∎

5.

Consider the exact sequence

0\to t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)\to M_{\underline{u}}\xrightarrow{f}M_{\underline{u}}\otimes K.

By

we denote the torsion part of some module. We set

0\to t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\left(\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\to 0

t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}E(A/\mathfrak{p}_{i})^{\ell_{i}(\underline{u})}\right)\oplus\mbox{ a finite length module}.

Proposition 5.1
.

For each
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
,

\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}

We need to show that

6.
Splitting

Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We first recall the short exact sequence

(6.0.12)

0\to t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)\to M_{\underline{u}}\to\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}\to 0,

which we obtained in
Section
5
. In consequence of Proposition
4.4
,
5.1
we get the following.

Corollary 6.1
.

If
I
is a usual monomial ideal, then the short exact sequence (
6.0.12
) splits.

Proof.

Recall that over a Dedekind domain any finitely generated, torsion-free module is projective. From Proposition
4.4
,
5.1
it thus follows that
\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}
is a projective module. Hence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}\left(\overline{M_{\underline{u}}},t\left(M_{\underline{u}}\right)\right)=0.

So (
6.0.12
) splits.
∎

We don’t know whether (
6.0.12
) splits when
I
is a
\mathfrak{C}
-monomial ideal. However, we now show that (
6.0.12
) splits when
A
is a PID. In particular, (
6.0.12
) splits locally. This is the main result in this section.

Proposition 6.2
.

If we further assume that
A
is a PID, then the short exact sequence (
6.0.12
) splits.

To prove Proposition
6.2
, we use a property of
Pontrjagin dual
, which is defined and discussed for
the ring of integers in
[
18
, Definition 3.2.3, Proposition 3.2.4]
. Although the same
treatment works for a PID, we repeat the proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Suppose that
A
is a PID. Then
A
has an injective resolution

0\to K\to K/A\to 0,

where
K
denotes the quotient field of
A
. By
[
4
, Corollary 3.1.5]
, both
K
and
K/A
are injective modules. Therefore,

K/A\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\\
\mathfrak{p}\neq 0\end{subarray}}E_{A}(A/\mathfrak{p}).

The Pontrjagin dual of an
A
-module
N
, denoted by
N^{*}
, is defined as

N^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,K/A).

Lemma 6.3
.

If
N
is a flat
A
-module, then
N^{*}
is an injective
A
-module.

Proof.

Let
M
be a
A
-module. By the
tensor-hom adjunction
,

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N^{*})=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,K/A))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M\otimes_{A}N,K/A)=(M\otimes_{A}N)^{*}.

By
Baer’s criterion
(see
[
4
, Proposition 3.1.2 d)]
),
N^{*}
is an injective module if and only if for any ideal
I
in
A
the map

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A,N^{*})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(I,N^{*}),

i.e.,
(A\otimes_{A}N)^{*}\to(I\otimes_{A}N)^{*}
is a surjective map. Since
K/A
is an injective
A
-module, in view of
[
4
, Proposition 3.1.2]
, it is enough to show that
I\otimes_{A}N\to A\otimes_{A}N
is an injective map. The latter condition holds if and only if
N
is a flat
A
-module, see
[
10
, Theorem 7.7]
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Using Theorem
2.7
, we get the short exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\left(E_{A}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{i})^{\ell_{i}}\oplus\bigg(\bigoplus_{j=0}^{t_{i}}\frac{A}{\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\beta_{ij}}}\bigg)\right)\to M_{\underline{u}}\to\overline{M_{\underline{u}}}\to 0

for some finite

and

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Since
A
is a PID,
\mathfrak{p}=(\pi)
for some nonzero element

in
A
. Consider the minimal free resolution

of

\mathcal{B}(n)=\{\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid u_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ if }i\in U\mbox{ and }u_{i}\leq-1\mbox{ otherwise}\}.

Corollary 6.8

(Tameness)
.

Fix
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Suppose that
M_{\underline{u}}\neq 0
and it has a torsion-free element. Then there is a block
\mathcal{B}(n)
such that
M_{\underline{v}}\neq 0
and it has a torsion-free element for all
\underline{v}\in\mathcal{B}(n)
.

Proof.

In view of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
,
N:=M\otimes_{A}K
is
\mathcal{D}(R\otimes_{A}K,K)
module. We set
V:=R\otimes_{A}K\cong K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. Then
N\cong H^{i}_{IV}(V)
. If
M_{\underline{u}}
has a torsion-free element, then
N_{\underline{u}}\neq 0
. Since
N
is an
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded generalized Eulerian
{\mathcal{D}}(V,K)
-module, by Terai’s result
[
17
]
, there is a block
\mathcal{B}(n)
by (
3.1.9
) such that
N_{\underline{v}}\neq 0
and hence
M_{\underline{v}}\neq 0
. Moreover,
M_{\underline{v}}
has a torsion-free element. The result follows.
∎

Note
.

From the above result, we get that if
0\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M_{\underline{u}}
for some
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then there is a block
\mathcal{B}(n)
such that
0\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}M_{\underline{v}}
.

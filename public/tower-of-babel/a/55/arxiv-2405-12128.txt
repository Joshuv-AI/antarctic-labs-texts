Double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras with degenerate center Thanks: SB and QE were supported by the grant NYUAD-065.
Sofiane Bouarroudj, Quentin Ehret

Abstract
. We develop the process of symplectic double extensions for Lie superalgebras with degenerate center. The construction is a superization of a recent work by Fischer, and generalize our previous work. We provide a standard model for such double extensions, where the symplectic form is either orthosymplectic or periplectic. Additionally, we show that every double extension is naturally equivalent to either of these two standard types of extensions. Several examples in low dimensions are given to illustrate the concept.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Double extensions of quadratic Lie (super)algebras

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra with a non-degenerate
symmetric
bilinear form (called
quadratic
in the literature). The notion of
double extension
of quadratic Lie algebras have been fully studied by Medina and Revoy in
[
MR1
]
. They showed that any irreducible non-simple quadratic Lie algebra can be obtained as a double extension. In particular, in the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a non-trivial center, then
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be obtained as a double extension of a smaller algebra by a
1
-dimensional space. Similar results were also obtained in
[
FS
]
and a classification in low dimension is also provided.

A superization of the results of
[
MR1
]
was first explored in
[
BeBe
]
when the odd part is
2
-dimensional. Since useful tools such as the Lie Theorem and the Levi decomposition do not hold in general for Lie superalgebras, new ideas are needed. Those results have been improved in
[
BBB
]
, and in subsequent papers, see
[
B
]
and references therein.

Over a field of characteristic
p\not=0
, the process of double extensions is still valid, except in characteristic two where new techniques are needed due to the presence of the squaring, see
[
BB1
,
BB2
,
BLS
]
.

The study of double extensions of
restricted
Lie superalgebras started in
[
BBH
]
, where quadratic Lie superalgebras were called
NIS
(an abbreviation for ‘non-degenerate invariant symmetric’). Under certain conditions, it is possible to endow a double extension with a restricted structure. There are also several conditions under which a quadratic restricted superalgebra can be obtained as a double extension. The conditions arise from the restricted cohomology, see
[
BBH
,
BEM
]
.

1.2.
Double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras

Roughly speaking, a Lie algebra is called quasi-Frobenius (resp. Frobenius) if it is equipped with a non-degenerate closed (resp. exact) antisymmetric bilinear form, called
symplectic
. Frobenius Lie algebras were introduced by Ooms in
[
O
]
to answer a question raised by Jacobson. Double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras (by a symplectic plane) were introduced by Medina and Revoy in
[
MR2
]
in order to classify nilpotent symplectic groups by their algebras. Indeed, there is a one-to-one correspondence between quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras and simply connected Lie groups admitting a left-invariant symplectic form. Medina and Revoy showed that any nilpotent quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions from the trivial algebra.
In addition, Baues and Cortés conducted an extensive study in
[
BC
]
, where they introduced the concepts of
symplectic reduction
and
symplectic oxidation
, which reverse each other. The symplectic oxidation process is closely related to double extensions. In particular, they applied their constructions to prove that any symplectic Lie group admits a sequence of symplectic reductions that leads to a unique irreducible symplectic Lie group.

1.3.
On the existence of isotropic ideals

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra and let
{\mathfrak{j}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be an
isotropic
ideal, see Section
2.3
. A symplectic reduction of
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a symplectic structure on the quotient Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}/{\mathfrak{j}}
. It is crucial to determine an appropriate isotropic ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
, not just for reduction but also for double extension. In
[
BC
]
,
the authors provide a procedure to find such an ideal in some particular cases (see
[
BC
, Thms. 8.3 and 8.5]
). They also give examples of 10-dimensional algebras without Lagrangian ideals. In
[
DM2
, Lemme 1.1]
, Dardié and Medina proved that any symplectic Lie algebra admits a non trivial abelian ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
, which is either isotropic or symplectic. Moreover, they showed that in the case where a symplectic algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
does not admit any isotropic ideal, then
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the semi-direct product
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\rtimes[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
(
[
DM2
, Théorème 1.3]
).

In the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a degenerate center (for instance, nilpotent non-abelian Lie algebras have a degenerate center), Fisher has developed a method for selecting the ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
canonically, see
[
Fi
]
. In particular, his method makes it possible to consider double extensions by ideals of arbitrary dimensions. Moreover, he constructed a standard model for these extensions and showed that any double extension is equivalent to this standard model. This result was used to prove that a quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra with a degenerate center is obtained by means of such an extension. Further, he performed a classification in dimension 6 for nilpotent algebras.
This double extension process cannot be performed in general in the case where the center of the algebra is trivial. Bajo and Benayadi overcame this difficulty in the case where the Lie algebra is abelian para-Kähler in
[
BaBe
]
and were able to describe double extensions even if the center is trivial. The case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable but not necessarily abelian para-Kähler has not yet been solved.

Complex symplectic oxidation (double extension) was introduced by Bazzoni et al. in
[
BFLM
]
, where they obtained all nilpotent complex symplectic Lie algebras in dimension 8.

1.4.
Double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

The results of
[
MR2
]
have been superized in
[
BM
]
, where the authors divided the study into four cases, depending on the parity of the symplectic form and of the derivation used to build the double extension. In particular, they showed that every quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be realized as double extension by a symplectic plane under some conditions and provided a classification of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras in dimension
4
. The study has been carried out later to the case of restricted Lie superalgebras in
[
BEM
]
. The main novelty of this work is that restricted cohomology captures the obstructions to symplectic double extensions.

As this paper follows Fisher’s ideas (
[
Fi
]
), we will look at the challenges involved in the super settings. We prove that choosing canonically the ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
leads a superization of Fisher’s work. There are two cases to consider: either the symplectic form is even or odd. It is necessary that the symplectic form is homogeneous; otherwise, the ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
is not homogeneous.

The paper is organized as follows. Basic concepts about Lie superalgebras, quasi-Frobenius structures and cohomological tools are introduced in Section
2
. The core of the paper is contained is Section
3
, where we introduce symplectic double extensions of a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra by an abelian Lie superalgebra (Definition
3.1.  Definition
) as well as equivalence of such extensions. We then provide a standard model in the orthosymplectic case (Section
3.2
) and the periplectic case (Section
3.3
). The main result of this paper is Theorem
3.4.2.  Theorem
which states that any double extension is equivalent to the appropriate standard model. We provide in Section
4
a list of explicit examples of double extensions based on the classification done in
[
Ba
]
.

Background

.

2.1.
Lie superalgebras

A
Lie superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
equipped with a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
such that, for all homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

(
i
)

[{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{i}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{j}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{\overline{i+j}}
;

(
ii
)

[x,y]=-(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x]
;

(
iii
)

(-1)^{|x||z|}[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|x||y|}[[y,z],x]+(-1)^{|y||z|}[[z,x],y]=0
(Jacobi identity).

From the definition
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is an ordinary Lie algebra, and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module.

A linear endomorphism
d:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
derivation
if it satisfies

d([x,y])=[d(x),y]+(-1)^{|d||x|}[x,d(y)],~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

In fact, this condition is cohomological. It means that
d
is a 1-cocyle on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in the adjoint representation.

An
ideal
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a subspace
{\mathfrak{j}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying
[{\mathfrak{j}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{j}}
. it is called
homogeneous
if, in addition,
{\mathfrak{j}}=({\mathfrak{j}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}})\oplus({\mathfrak{j}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}).

The
center
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the homogeneous ideal
{\mathfrak{z}}:=\{z\in{\mathfrak{g}},~[z,x]=0~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.

2.2.
Change of parity functor.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. We denote by
\Pi
the
change of parity functor

\Pi:V\mapsto\Pi(V)
, where
\Pi V
is another copy of
V
such that
\Pi(V)_{\bar{0}}:=V_{\bar{1}};~~\Pi(V)_{\bar{1}}:=V_{\bar{0}}
(see
[
L
]
). Elements of
\Pi(V)
shall be denoted by
\pi(v),~\forall v\in V
. We also shall identify
V
and
\Pi(\Pi(V))
in a natural way.

2.3.
Quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\omega
be a bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Recall that the form
\omega
is called
non-degenerate
if

\ker(\omega):=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\omega(x,y)=0~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}=\{0\}.

Following
[
BaBe
,
BM
]
, a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
quasi-Frobenius
if it is equipped with a non-degenerate 2-cocycle
\omega\in Z^{2}_{\text{CE}}({\mathfrak{g}};{\mathbb{K}})
(see Section
2.4
), that is

(1)

(-1)^{|a||c|}\omega(a,[b,c])+(-1)^{|c||b|}\omega(c,[a,b])+(-1)^{|b||a|}\omega(b,[c,a])=0.

We denote such an algebra by
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],\omega)
. The bracket will be omitted whenever it is clear from the context.

In
[
BM
]
, we described filliform Lie superalgebras as quasi-Frobenius and list quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras in small dimensions.

A bilinear form
\omega
satisfying (
1
) is called
closed
. In the case where
\omega\in B^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathbb{K})
, the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
Frobenius
while
\omega
is called
exact
.

A quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is called

(i)

orthosymplectic
if the form
\omega
is even, that is,
\omega({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=0
;

(ii)

periplectic
if the form
\omega
is odd, that is,
\omega({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}})=\omega({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=0
.

Let
{\mathfrak{j}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be a subspace of a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
. The
orthogonal
of
{\mathfrak{j}}
(with respect to the form
\omega
) is defined by

{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}:=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\omega(x,y)=0~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{j}}\}.

An ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
is called

(i)

isotropic
if
{\mathfrak{j}}\subset{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
;

(ii)

lagrangian
if
{\mathfrak{j}}
is maximal and if
{\mathfrak{j}}={\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
;

(iii)

degenerate
if
{\mathfrak{j}}\cap{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}\neq\{0\}
.

A morphism of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras
\varphi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})\rightarrow({\mathfrak{h}},\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras satisfying
\varphi^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}=\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
, where
\varphi^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,y):=\omega_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))
, for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Quasi-Frobenius Lie (super)algebras are also called symplectic Lie (super)algebras, see
[
BC
,
D
,
DM1
,
DM2
,
Fi
,
MR2
,
RS
]
.

Lemma

(Superization of
[
BC
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra and
{\mathfrak{j}}
be an isotropic homogeneous ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
{\mathfrak{j}}
is abelian.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in{\mathfrak{j}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{j}}\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
, we get
\omega([x,y],z)=0,~\forall z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. It follows that
[x,y]=0
.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra such that
\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is homogeneous and let
{\mathfrak{j}}
be an isotropic homogeneous ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}:={\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}/{\mathfrak{j}}={\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}_{\bar{0}}/{\mathfrak{j}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}_{\bar{1}}/{\mathfrak{j}}_{\bar{1}}
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Moreover,
|\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}|=|\omega_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}|
.

Proof.

Observe that
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
is homogeneous. Indeed, let
x=x_{\bar{0}}+x_{\bar{1}}\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
and
y=y_{\bar{0}}+y_{\bar{1}}\in{\mathfrak{j}}
. From
\omega(x,y_{\bar{0}})=0
, we obtain that
\omega(x_{\bar{0}},y_{\bar{0}})=\omega(x_{\bar{1}},y_{\bar{0}})=0
. From
\omega(x,y_{\bar{1}})=0
, we obtain that
\omega(x_{\bar{0}},y_{\bar{1}})=\omega(x_{\bar{1}},y_{\bar{1}})=0
. We deduce that
x_{{\bar{0}}}\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
and that
x_{{\bar{1}}}\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
is homogeneous. Since
{\mathfrak{j}}
is homogeneous, the quotient
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}={\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}/{\mathfrak{j}}
is a Lie superalgebra.

We define a symplectic form on
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
by
\omega_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\overline{x},\overline{y}):=\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(x,y)
. The form
\omega_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
is well defined, non-degenerate and closed. Therefore, the Lie superalgebra
(\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}},\omega_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}})
is quasi-Frobenius.
∎

2.4.
Cohomological constructions

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra and
M
be an
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Let us recall the space of cochains
C^{n}_{\text{CE}}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
in the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology. For
n=0
, we put
C^{0}_{\text{CE}}({\mathfrak{g}};M):=M
. For
n>0
, the space of cochains
C^{n}_{\text{CE}}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
is the space of
n
-linear super anti-symmetric maps. The differential is given as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{0}(m)(x)=(-1)^{|m||x|}x\cdot m\text{~~for any $m\in M$ and $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$};\\[5.69054pt]
d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{n}(\varphi)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\\
=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i<j}(-1)^{(|x_{j}|(|x_{i+1}|+\dots+|x_{j-1}|)+j}\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},[x_{i},x_{j}],x_{i+1}\cdots,\widetilde{x}_{j},\cdots,x_{n})\\
+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j}(-1)^{|x_{j}|(|f|+|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{j-1}|)+j}x_{j}\cdot\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{x}_{j},\cdots,x_{n})\\
\text{~~for any $\varphi\in C^{n-1}_{\text{CE}}({\mathfrak{g}};M)$ with $n>0$, and $x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.\\
\end{array}

Let us denote the spaces
Z^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(L;M):=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits\left(d^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}\right)
and
B^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(L;M):=\text{Im}\left(d^{n-1}_{\mathrm{CE}}\right)
, for all
n\geq 1
. The quotient space
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(L;M):=Z^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(L;M)/B^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(L;M)
is well defined for all
n\geq 0
and is called the
n^{th}

Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology space of

{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
.

Let
n,k
be natural integers. We denote by
\sh(n,k)
the set of all permutations
\tau
of
\left\{1,\cdots,n,\cdots,n+k\right\}
such that
\tau(1)<\tau(2)<\cdots<\tau(n)\text{ and }\tau(n+1)<\cdots<\tau(n+k).

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded space and let
X=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
be a subset of homogeneous elements of
V
. Let
\sigma
be a permutation of
\left\{1,\cdots,n,\cdots,n+k\right\}
. Following
[
BP
]
, we denote by

K(\sigma,X):=\text{Card}\bigl\{(i,j),~i<j,~x_{\sigma(i)}\in V_{\bar{1}},~x_{\sigma(j)}\in V_{\bar{1}},~\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\bigl\}.

Then, we denote by
\theta(\sigma,X):=\text{sgn}(\sigma)(-1)^{K(\sigma,X)}
, where
\text{sgn}(\sigma)
is the signature of the permutation
\sigma
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra and let
U,V,W
be
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules. Recall that a bilinear map
m:U\times V\rightarrow W
is called
{\mathfrak{g}}
-equivariant if

x\cdot m(u,v)=(-1)^{|x||m|}m(x\cdot u,v)+(-1)^{|x|(|u|+|m|)}m(u,x\cdot v),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\forall(u,v)\in U\times V\text{ homogeneous.}

The following definition is crucial to us.

Definition
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra, let

be
{\mathfrak{g}}

3.
Symplectic double extensions

The following definition is a straightforward superization of
[
Fi
]
.

Definition

(Symplectic double extension)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{{\mathfrak{a}}})
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra and let
{\mathfrak{l}}
be an abelian Lie superalgebra. Following
[
Fi
]
, a symplectic double extension of
{\mathfrak{a}}
by
{\mathfrak{l}}
is given by a quadruple
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{j}},i,p)
, where

(
i
)

{\mathfrak{g}}=({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra such that
\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is homogeneous;

(
ii
)

{\mathfrak{j}}
is a central isotropic homogeneous ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
(with respect to
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
);

(
iii
)

There is a short exact sequence of Lie superalgebras

(5)

0\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{j}}\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}{\mathfrak{l}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
i:{\mathfrak{a}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}/{\mathfrak{j}}
is an isomorphism of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras. Following
[
Fi
]
, we call such an extension
balanced
if
{\mathfrak{j}}={\mathfrak{z}}\cap{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
, where
{\mathfrak{z}}
is the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Actually, any central ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
is homogeneous. Therefore, the quotient
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}/{\mathfrak{j}}
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra by Prop.
2.3.2.  Proposition
.

Theorem

(Superization of
[
Fi
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra with degenerate center
{\mathfrak{z}}
. Suppose that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is homogeneous. Then
{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
is homogeneous and
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
has the structure of a balanced quadratic extension in a canonical way.

Proof.

The center
{\mathfrak{z}}
is always homogeneous. Since
\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is closed,
{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
is an homogeneous ideal. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{z}}\cap{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
is homogeneous. Choose
{\mathfrak{j}}={\mathfrak{z}}\cap{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
. The proof follows as in
[
Fi
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

.

\phi:{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
satisfying
\phi({\mathfrak{j}}_{1})={\mathfrak{j}}_{2}
and such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

3.4.
Equivalence to the standard model

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{{\mathfrak{a}}})
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra and let
{\mathfrak{l}}
be an abelian Lie superalgebra. Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{j}},i,p)
be a double extension of
{\mathfrak{a}}
by
{\mathfrak{l}}
(see Def.
3.1.  Definition
). Since
{\mathfrak{j}}
is isotropic, there exists an isotropic subspace
V_{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Lemma
.

Let
V_{\mathfrak{a}}:=({\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}})^{\perp}
. We have the decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}=V_{{\mathfrak{a}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

Proof.

Let
j+v\in({\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}})\cap({\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}\cap V_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})
. Since
j+v\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}
, and
{\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}\cap V_{\mathfrak{l}}=0
, it follows that
v=0
. Similarly, we obtain
j=0
. Therefore,
V_{\mathfrak{a}}\cap({\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}})=({\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}})\cap({\mathfrak{j}}^{\perp}\cap V_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})=0
. As a result, we obtain the desired decomposition, since
{\mathfrak{g}}=V_{\mathfrak{a}}+({\mathfrak{j}}\oplus V_{\mathfrak{l}})
.
∎

5.
Open problems

A list of open problems is given below.

(i)

Describe symplectic double extensions in the case where the center is trivial. For a 1-dimensional ideal, this problem has already been solved in
[
BM
]
.

(ii)

Over a field of characteristic
p
, if the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{a}}
is restricted, under which conditions its double extension is restricted as well? The 1-dimensional case has already been studied in
[
BEM
]
.

(iii)

In
[
H
, Corollaire 21]
, the author proved that if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a left-symmetric finite-dimensional Lie algebra, then
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\neq{\mathfrak{g}}
(see also
[
C
, Thms 6 and 7]
). Since quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras admit a compatible left-symmetric structure (see e.g.
[
DM2
]
), they do satisfy this condition. Is it also true for quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras?

(iv)

The existence of an isotropic ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
is crucial to the process of double extensions. In
[
DM2
, Lemme 1.1]
, Dardié and Medina proved that any quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra admits a non trivial abelian ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
, which is either isotropic or symplectic. The proof relies on
[
H
, Corollaire 21]
that we discussed in point (
iii
). In the case where the quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra does not admit any isotropic ideal, they showed that
{\mathfrak{g}}=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\rtimes[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
, a semi direct product of the abelian derived ideal
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
by
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
(
[
DM2
, Théorème 1.3]
). It would be very interesting to investigate this result in the super settings.

(v)

Classify quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras of dimension 6 based on the same ideas as in
[
Fi
]
.

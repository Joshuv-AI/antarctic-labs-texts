Lower bound for large local transversal fluctuations of Geodesics in Last Passage Percolation
Pranay Agarwal

Abstract
. For exactly solvable models of planar last passage percolation, it is known that geodesics of length n n exhibit transversal fluctuations at scale n 2 / 3 n^{2/3} and matching (up to exponents) upper and lower bounds for the tail probabilities are available. The local transversal fluctuations near the endpoints are expected to be much smaller; it is known that the transversal fluctuation up to distance r ≪ n r\ll n is typically of the order r 2 / 3 r^{2/3} and the probability that the fluctuation is larger than t ​ r 2 / 3 tr^{2/3} is at most C ​ e − c ​ t 3 Ce^{-c{t}^{3}} . In this note, we provide a short argument establishing a matching lower bound for this probability.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Exponential Last Passage Percolation model on the integer lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, where one assigns independently sampled values from the exponential distribution to each vertex of the lattice. We study up-right paths from the origin to the point
(n,n)
in this model, the paths with the maximal weight sum are called the geodesic
\Gamma_{n}
, which are almost surely unique in this model. It was proved in
[
Joh00b
]
that
\Gamma_{n}
shows transversal fluctuations of the order
n^{2/3+o(1)}
. Later the result was improved in
[
BSS14
, Theorem 11.1]
and subsequently in
[
BGZ21
, Proposition C.9]
, which showed that the geodesic
\Gamma_{n}
exhibits transversal fluctuations at scale
n^{2/3}
, and the probability that these fluctuations are greater than
tn^{2/3}
is at most
Ce^{-c{t}^{3}}
. One can establish a matching lower bound from the arguments of
[
HS18
, Proposition 1.4]
, even though their arguments focused on the Poissonian LPP model. As one would expect, the transversal fluctuations are much smaller near the endpoints of the geodesic. At a distance
r
from the endpoints, where
r\ll n
, the fluctuations are typically of the order
r^{2/3}
.

Now we define transversal fluctuations formally as

\mathrm{TF}^{n}(r):=\max\left\{{\left|{x-y}\right|:(x,y)\in\Gamma_{n},0\leq x+y\leq 2r}\right\}.

Thus,
\mathrm{TF}^{n}(n)
is the global transversal fluctuation of
\Gamma_{n}
and
\mathrm{TF}^{n}(r)
are the local transversal fluctuations. It was shown in
[
BBB23
, Proposition 2.1]
that for large
t
, for some constants
c,C,

\underset{}{{\mathbb{P}}}\left({{\mathrm{TF}^{n}(r)>tr^{2/3}}}\right)\leq C\exp\left({-ct^{3}}\right).

We will give a more precise statement later as Theorem
2.2
. Our main result gives a matching lower bound for this probability.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist positive constants
c_{1},C_{1},r_{0},t_{0}
such that for all
\epsilon>0
,
r>r_{0}
,
n\geq r
and
(1-\epsilon)r^{1/3}>t>t_{0}

\underset{}{{\mathbb{P}}}\left({{\mathrm{TF}^{n}(r)>tr^{2/3}}}\right)\geq C_{1}\exp\left({-c_{1}t^{3}}\right).

This completes the picture for local transversal fluctuations of finite geodesics. The main tools we use to prove the bound are moderate deviation estimates from integrable probability along with the ordering of geodesics. We shall simplify the arguments presented in
[
HS18
, Proposition 1.4]
for Exponential LPP and use them to prove Theorem
3.1
. The resulting construction that we get from Theorem
3.1
will then be used to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Some related expressions have appeared in the literature before as well. A similar lower bound for the transversal fluctuation of semi-infinite geodesics was shown in
[
SS20
, Theorem 2.8]
by utilizing stationary exponential LPP. A formula to calculate the probability of the geodesic to pass through a given point was derived in
[
Liu22
, Theorem 1.1]
. However, as remarked by the author, the complicated expression of their formula makes it seem intangible to use their result to derive any useful asymptotics.

We will use

to

to

.

to

to denote the geodesic from
\mathbf{o}
to

. Similarly, we define the functions,

\displaystyle\phi(u)=u_{2}+u_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{and,}

We use

2.1.
Estimates for temporal fluctuations

Johansson proved
[
Joh00a
, Remark 1.5]
that the last passage time is equal in distribution to the top eigenvalue of the Laguerre Unitary Ensemble, and upper bounds on the upper and lower tails on this eigenvalue were proved in
[
LR10
, Theorem 2]
. Their arguments gave us matching bounds for the upper tail and an upper bound for the lower tail. A matching lower bound for the lower tail was then proved in
[
BGHK21
, Theorem 2]
. We summarize the results in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}},h\in(0,1]
. For
u=(u_{1},u_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that
\phi(u)=n
and
h\leq\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\leq 1/h
. Then there exist positive constants
c_{1},C_{1},c_{2},C_{2},c_{3},C_{3},c_{4},C_{4},n_{0},t_{0}
depending on

such that for
n>n_{0}
and
t_{0}<t<n^{2/3}
,

(2.1)

C_{1}\exp\left({-c_{1}t^{3/2}}\right)\leq\underset{}{{\mathbb{P}}}\left({{T(\mathbf{o},u)-f(\mathbf{o},u)>tn^{1/3}}}\right)\leq C_{2}\exp\left({-c_{2}t^{3/2}}\right),

(2.2)

C_{3}\exp\left({-c_{3}t^{3}}\right)\leq\underset{}{{\mathbb{P}}}\left({{T(\mathbf{o},u)-f(\mathbf{o},u)\leq-tn^{1/3}}}\right)\leq C_{4}\exp\left({-c_{4}t^{3}}\right).

Proof.

Remark 3
.

The result directly puts us in a position to comment on the fluctuations of semi-infinite geodesics. Theorem
1.1
will give us a similar lower bound for large fluctuations of the semi-infinite geodesics in the direction of
x=y
since the finite geodesics converge subsequentially to the semi-infinite geodesic. Such a lower bound for semi-infinite geodesics was proven in
[
SS20
, Theorem 2.8]
. Our result also holds for finite geodesics while their result hold for all semi-infinite geodesics in non-axial directions.

Remark 4
.

The proof uses the event
E^{R}
, which is independent of the rectangle
R
and the symmetry of the problem. While considering other non-axial directions, one can still produce similar constructions for the event
E^{R}
, but the symmetry is lost. However, it is to be noted that the symmetry is used to claim that the
\underset{}{{\mathbb{P}}}\left({{E^{R}}}\right)
is greater than some constant; this should be achievable even in the non-axial directions. It is also to be noted that we did not use any explicit property of exponential LPP. All our arguments follow from the moderate deviation result from integrable probability. Thus, the same arguments should hold for other solvable models like the Poissonian LPP and Geometric LPP.

Remark 5
.

We have proven a lower bound for the maximal transversal fluctuation of the geodesic up to the line
{\mathcal{L}}_{r}
. The same lower bound (up to exponents) should also hold for large transversal fluctuation of the geodesic at the line
{\mathcal{L}}_{r}
. However, this requires some refinement of the arguments we make.

Axion Homeopathy: Screening Dilaton Interactions Preprint: CERN-TH-2021-176
C.P. Burgess, F. Quevedo

Abstract
Cosmologically active Brans-Dicke (or dilaton) scalar fields are generically ruled out by solar system tests of gravity unless their couplings to ordinary matter are much suppressed relative to gravitational strength, and this is a major hindrance when building realistic models of light dilatons coupled to matter. We propose a new mechanism for evading such bounds if matter also couples to a light axion, that exploits nonlinear target-space curvature interactions to qualitatively change how the fields respond to a gravitating source. We find that dilaton-matter couplings that would be excluded in the absence of an axion can become acceptable given an additional small axion-matter coupling, and this is possible because the axion-dilaton interactions end up converting the would-be dilaton profile into an axion profile. The trajectories of matter test bodies are then controlled by the much weaker axion-matter couplings and can easily be small enough to escape detection. We call this mechanism Axion Homeopathy because the evasion of the dilaton-coupling bounds persists for extremely small axion couplings provided only that they are nonzero. We explore the mechanism using axio-dilaton equations that are S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) SL(2,\mathbb{R}) invariant (as often appear in string compactifications), since for these the general solutions exterior to a spherically symmetric source can be found analytically. We use this solution to compute the relevant PPN parameters, γ P ​ P ​ N \gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN} and β P ​ P ​ N \beta_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN} , and verify that their deviation from unity can be much smaller than it would have been in the absence of axion-matter couplings and can therefore evade the experimental bounds.

2
Brans-Dicke scalars

This section summarizes the bare bones couplings of a Brans-Dicke scalar under the assumption that its scalar potential – and in particular its mass – is small enough to be negligible on the scales to be studied. Our interest is in how such scalars impinge on tests of GR in the solar system, and to do so we rederive the PPN parameter that appears in the most dangerous constraints (which the models of §
3
subsequently evade despite having couplings that at face value should have been ruled out). This section contains largely standard material and so impatient readers and axio-dilaton aficianados can skip this section without loss.

2.1
The vanilla model

Brans-Dicke model is a particular instance of class of scalar-tensor theories that couple a single light scalar
\phi
to matter and to gravity through a Lagrangian density of the form

{\cal L}=-\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{p}^{2}}{2}\,{\cal R}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\partial\phi)^{2}+V(\phi)\right]+{\cal L}_{m}(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\psi)\,.

(5)

Here
{\cal R}
is the Ricci scalar built from
g_{\mu\nu}
, while
M_{p}^{-2}=8\pi G
with
G
being Newton’s constant for universal gravitation and
\psi
is a representative matter field. The defining feature of this class of models is that
\phi
couples to matter only through the Jordan-frame metric, defined by

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=A^{2}(\phi)\;g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(6)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein-frame metric used in the rest of the lagrangian (
5
). Having matter only couple through
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
ensures that the model predicts no preferred-frame effects and so immediately evades all bounds coming from tests of the Equivalence Principle
[
6
]
.

Brans-Dicke theory corresponds to the special case where the scalar potential
V(\phi)
is negligible and where

A(\phi)=\exp\Bigl({\mathfrak{g}}\,\phi/M_{p}\Bigr)\,,

(7)

for some coupling
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For historical reasons
{\mathfrak{g}}
is often traded for the Brans-Dicke parameter
\omega
defined by
6
6

6

Notice we use a slightly different normalization for
\phi
than is common in the gravity literature
[
7
]
.

2{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}=1/(3+2\omega)
. This kind of coupling is theoretically well-motivated and is often encountered in the context of theories with approximate scale invariance for which
\phi
is called the dilaton and the above assumptions imply that matter fields have a universal
\phi
-dependent mass
m=m_{0}\,A(\phi)
.

Conserved currents

With these choices a matter stress-energy can be defined using either metric:

\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}}\;\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad T^{\mu\nu}:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\;\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,,

(8)

that are related to one another by

T^{\mu\nu}(x)=A^{6}(\phi)\,\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}(x)\,,\quad{T_{\mu}}^{\nu}=A^{4}\,{{\widetilde{T}}_{\mu}}^{\nu}\quad\hbox{and}\quad T_{\mu\nu}=A^{2}\,\widetilde{T}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(9)

where
{T_{\mu}}^{\nu}=g_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu}
while
{\widetilde{T}_{\mu}}^{\nu}=\tilde{g}_{\mu\lambda}\widetilde{T}^{\lambda\nu}
and so on. Of these, diffeomorphism invariance ensures that the matter equations of motion alone suffice to imply conservation,
\widetilde{D}_{\mu}\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}=0
, where
\widetilde{D}_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative built using the Christoffel symbol of
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
. By contrast, diffeomorphism invariance only ensures that the sum of
T^{\mu\nu}
with the
\phi
-field stress energy is covariantly conserved when the equations of motion for
both

\phi
and matter are satisfied.

It is sometimes useful to consider perfect fluids and for these the stress tensors define the pressure and energy density,

\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}=(\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{p})\,\widetilde{U}^{\mu}\widetilde{U}^{\nu}+\tilde{p}\,\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\quad\hbox{and}\quad T^{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)\,U^{\mu}U^{\nu}+p\,g^{\mu\nu}

(10)

where
U^{\mu}
and
\widetilde{U}^{\mu}
are the 4-velocities of observers co-moving with the fluid, normalized to satisfy
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{U}^{\mu}\widetilde{U}^{\nu}=-1
and
g_{\mu\nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}=-1
. Chasing through the definitions implies the Einstein-frame and Jordan-frame energy density and pressure are related by

p=A^{4}(\phi)\,\tilde{p}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\rho=A^{4}(\phi)\,\tilde{\rho}\,,

(11)

and so an equation of state like
w=p/\rho=\tilde{p}/\tilde{\rho}
takes the same form in either frame.

Response to nonrelativistic sources

Varying the action built from the lagrangian density (
5

\Box\phi(x)+\frac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{M_{p}}\,g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(12)

{\cal R}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{M_{p}^{2}}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi+\frac{1}{M_{p}^{2}}\left[T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}T_{\lambda\rho}\,g_{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,.

(13)

The trace-reversed Einstein equation simplifies in the nonrelativistic limit near flat space, for which the only significant source of stress energy is the energy density
\rho\gg p
and the only significant source of curvature is

In this case, if

The dilaton equation (

\displaystyle 0=\Box\phi+\frac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{M_{p}}\,g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}\simeq\nabla^{2}\phi-\frac{{\mathfrak{g}}\rho}{M_{p}}\,.

(15)

Defining the dimensionless field

2.2
Parameterized post-Newtonian metric

Tests of gravity compare the observed motion of test particles with the motion predicted by the above field configurations. In this theory test particles built from the matter part of the lagrangian move (in the absence of other forces) along the geodesics of the Jordan frame metric,
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

[

The parameterized post-Newtonian (PPN) framework

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}x^{\mu}{\rm d}x^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[1-\frac{2GM}{r}+2(\beta_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}-\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN})\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{2}+\cdots\right]{\rm d}t^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[1+2\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)+\cdots\right]{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}{\rm d}\Omega^{2}\,.

The Schwarzschild solution of General Relativity corresponds to
\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}=\beta_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}=1

[
14

currently requires the bound (

We next rederive the standard formula for how

depends on
{\mathfrak{g}}

\partial_{\mu}\Bigl[\sqrt{-g}\;{\cal G}_{ab}\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi^{b}\Bigr]+\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{a}{\cal G}_{bc}\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{b}\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi^{c}+\frac{\eta_{a}(\varphi)}{M_{p}^{2}}\,g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,,

(38)

where the coupling function is defined by

\eta_{a}(\varphi):=\frac{\partial_{a}A}{A}\,.

(39)

Eq. (
38
) can be more geometrically written as

\Box\varphi^{a}+\Gamma^{a}_{bc}\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{b}\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi^{c}+\frac{1}{M_{p}^{2}}\,\eta^{a}(\varphi)\,g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(40)

where
\eta^{a}:={\cal G}^{ab}\eta_{b}
and

\Gamma^{a}_{bc}:=\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal G}^{ad}\Bigl[\partial_{b}{\cal G}_{cd}+\partial_{c}{\cal G}_{bd}-\partial_{d}{\cal G}_{bc}\Bigr]

(41)

is the Christoffel symbol of the second kind built from the target-space metric
{\cal G}_{ab}
. It is the
\Gamma^{a}_{bc}\partial\varphi^{b}\partial\varphi^{c}
terms in (
40
) that are crucial in the discussions of §
3
. This term is not usually present in the single-scalar example because it can be eliminated using a field redefinition.

Kähler target space

In order to be more explicit, let us consider a Kähler target space typical of the supersymmetric case in which we have complex fields,
z^{a}
and
z^{\bar{a}}
and the metric is Kähler:
G_{ac}=G_{\bar{a}\bar{c}}=0
while locally
G_{a\bar{c}}=\partial_{a}\partial_{\bar{c}}K
for some choice of Kähler potential
K(z,\bar{z})
. For such geometries all components of the Christoffel symbols with mixed
a
and
\bar{a}
indices vanish, leaving as nonzero only the purely holomorphic combination
\Gamma^{a}_{bc}
and its complex conjugate:

\Gamma^{a}_{bc}=G^{\bar{e}a}\partial_{b}G_{c\bar{e}}=K^{\bar{e}a}K_{bc\bar{e}}\,,

(42)

where subscripts as usual denote derivatives. Motivated by
[
30
]
we are also interested in the case where the coupling to matter is controlled by
8
8

8

Supersymmetry underlies this motivation because the function
K
controls both the geometry of the scalar target-space metric and the non-minimal coupling of the scalars to the spacetime curvature.

A=e^{K/6}\,,

(43)

in which case the matter-coupling vector has complex components

\eta_{a}=\frac{\partial_{a}A}{A}=\frac{K_{a}}{6}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\eta^{a}=K^{\bar{c}a}\eta_{\bar{c}}=\frac{K^{\bar{c}a}K_{\bar{c}}}{6}\,.

(44)

Axio-dilaton example

The important yet minimal example used in §
3
involves two real fields combined into a single complex field
T=\frac{1}{2}(\tau+ia)
, for which
K=-3\ln(T+{\overline{T}})=-3\ln\tau
. These choices imply

G_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}{\overline{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}}=K_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}{\overline{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}}=\frac{3}{(T+{\overline{T}})^{2}}=\frac{3}{\tau^{2}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}{\scriptscriptstyle T}}=-\frac{2}{T+{\overline{T}}}=-\frac{2}{\tau}\,,

(45)

and the further choice

A=e^{K/6}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\quad\hbox{implies}\quad\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\frac{K_{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{6}=-\frac{1}{2\tau}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=-\frac{\tau}{6}\,.

(46)

With these choices — and choosing non-relativistic matter (for which
g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=-\rho
) — eq. (
40
) then becomes

\Box T-\frac{2}{\tau}\,\partial_{\mu}T\,\partial^{\mu}T+\frac{\tau}{6M_{p}^{2}}\,\rho=0\,,

(47)

whose real and imaginary parts then give

\Box a-\frac{2}{\tau}\,\partial_{\mu}\tau\,\partial^{\mu}a=0

(48)

and

\Box\tau-\frac{1}{\tau}\Bigl[\partial_{\mu}\tau\,\partial^{\mu}\tau-\partial_{\mu}a\,\partial^{\mu}a\Bigr]+\frac{\tau}{3M_{p}^{2}}\,\rho=0\,.

(49)

Notice that constant
a
solves these equations and switching in this case to the new variable
\tau=e^{\zeta\varphi}
[for which
\tau^{-1}\Box\tau=\zeta\,\Box\varphi+\zeta^{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi
] implies (
49
) reduces to the Brans-Dicke form
\Box\varphi=-\rho/(3\zeta M_{p}^{2})
(as in (
15
)). As elaborated below, canonical normalization corresponds to
\zeta=\sqrt{2/3}
, and so comparing to (
15
) shows that the Brans-Dicke coupling is
{\mathfrak{g}}=-1/\sqrt{6}
.

3
Axio-Dilaton

The previous section introduces a simple axio-dilaton model involving a complex field
T=\frac{1}{2}(\tau+ia)
for which the dilaton-matter coupling obtained for
\tau
when
a
is constant is
{\mathfrak{g}}=-1/\sqrt{6}\simeq-0.41
. At face value, using
\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}=(1-2{\mathfrak{g}}^{2})/(1+2{\mathfrak{g}}^{2})
then implies
|\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}-1|=\frac{1}{2}
, which is much too large to satisfy the observational constraint (
3
).

In this section we show how even extremely small matter couplings to the axion
a
can allow this size of a matter-dilaton coupling to escape solar system bounds. This happens because the nonlinear
\tau
-
a
derivative couplings modify the prediction (
34
) for
\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}
, and do so by having the matter-
\tau
coupling largely produce an external
a
profile rather than a
\tau
profile. This evades solar-system bounds because the axion field influences test-body motion much less efficiently than does the dilaton.

We continue to assume throughout this section that the axion and dilaton masses are negligible for solar-system applications (but return briefly in §
4
to comment on possible implications of an axion mass).

3.1
Symmetries and field equations

Consider therefore the special case of the axio-dilaton described in §
2.3
in somewhat more detail. The model’s lagrangian density is

{\cal L}=-\sqrt{-g}\;M_{p}^{2}\left[\frac{{\cal R}}{2}+\frac{3\,\partial^{\mu}{\overline{T}}\,\partial_{\mu}T}{(T+{\overline{T}})^{2}}\right]+{\cal L}_{m}=-\sqrt{-g}\;M_{p}^{2}\left[\frac{{\cal R}}{2}+\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\partial^{\mu}\tau\,\partial_{\mu}\tau+\partial^{\mu}a\,\partial_{\mu}a}{\tau^{2}}\right)\right]+{\cal L}_{m}\,,

(50)

where
T=\frac{1}{2}(\tau+ia)
and we assume the matter couples to
\tau
only through the Jordan-frame metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=A^{2}(\tau)\,g_{\mu\nu}
with
A=\tau^{-1/2}
. We do allow the possibility that matter can also couple directly to the axion independent of
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
, though only very weakly. Writing
\tau=e^{\zeta\varphi}
and demanding the kinetic term in (
50
) become
-\frac{1}{2}\,M_{p}^{2}\sqrt{-g}\;(\partial\varphi)^{2}
is what determines
\zeta=\sqrt{2/3}
, as quoted earlier.

Specialized to non-relativistic sources these choices give the following axio-dilaton field equations

\Box\tau-\frac{1}{\tau}\Bigl(\partial_{\mu}\tau\,\partial^{\mu}\tau-\partial_{\mu}a\,\partial^{\mu}a\Bigr)+\frac{\tau\,\rho}{3M_{p}^{2}}=0\,,

(51)

and

\Box a-\frac{2}{\tau}\,\partial_{\mu}\tau\,\partial^{\mu}a+\frac{\tau^{2}{\cal A}}{3M_{p}^{2}}=0\,,

(52)

where
\rho=-g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
is used for non-relativistic matter with

T^{\mu\nu}:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\;\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\cal A}:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\;\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta a}\,.

(53)

The trace-reversed Einstein equations are given by (
37
), specialized to the above choices:

{\cal R}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{3}{4\tau^{2}}\Bigl(\partial_{\mu}\tau\,\partial_{\nu}\tau+\partial_{\mu}a\,\partial_{\nu}a\Bigr)+\frac{1}{M_{p}^{2}}\left[T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}T_{\lambda\rho}\,g_{\mu\nu}\right]=0\,.

(54)

3.1.1
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
invariance and conservation laws

These equations prove to be relatively simple to integrate, largely because of the number of conservation laws that they admit. In particular notice that the non-matter part of the action is invariant under the following
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
transformation

T=\frac{1}{2}(\tau+ia)\to\frac{{\mathfrak{a}}T-i{\mathfrak{b}}}{i{\mathfrak{c}}T+{\mathfrak{d}}}

(55)

where the four real parameters
{\mathfrak{a}}
through
{\mathfrak{d}}
satisfy the constraint
{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{d}}-{\mathfrak{b}}{\mathfrak{c}}=1
. Notice that these three real parameters include as special cases the axionic shift symmetry
a\to a-2{\mathfrak{b}}
(when
{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{d}}=1
and
{\mathfrak{c}}=0
) and scale invariance
T\to{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}T
(when
{\mathfrak{d}}=1/{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{c}}=0
).

Noether’s theorem implies there must be three conserved currents, and these can be taken to be

J_{\scriptscriptstyle A}^{\mu}=\frac{\partial^{\mu}a}{\tau^{2}}\qquad\hbox{(axion shift symmetry)}

(56)

J_{\scriptscriptstyle S}^{\mu}=\frac{\partial^{\mu}\tau}{\tau}+\frac{a\,\partial^{\mu}a}{\tau^{2}}\qquad\hbox{(scaling symmetry)}

(57)

as well as

J_{\scriptscriptstyle N}^{\mu}=\frac{(\tau^{2}-a^{2})}{\tau^{2}}\;\partial^{\mu}a-\frac{2a}{\tau}\;\partial^{\mu}\tau\qquad\hbox{(nonlinear symmetry)}\,,

(58)

where the last conservation law corresponds to the nonlinear transformation
\delta T=-i\epsilon T^{2}
obtained when
{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{d}}=1
while
{\mathfrak{b}}=0
and
{\mathfrak{c}}=\epsilon\ll 1
. As is straightforward to verify, direct differentiation together with use of the field equations (
51
) and (
52
) implies these currents are all conserved in the absence of sources

D_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle A}=-\frac{{\cal A}}{3M_{p}^{2}}\,,\quad D_{\mu}J_{\scriptscriptstyle S}^{\mu}=-\frac{(\rho+a\,{\cal A})}{3M_{p}^{2}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad D_{\mu}J_{\scriptscriptstyle N}^{\mu}=\frac{(a^{2}-\tau^{2}){\cal A}+2a\,\rho}{3M_{p}^{2}}\,.

(59)

Spherically symmetric source

We next record the field equations and conservation laws for spherically symmetric solutions, for which
\tau=\tau(r)
and
a=a(r)
. Denoting differentiation with respect to
r
by primes, the field equations (
51
) and (
52
) reduce to

\tau^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\tau^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{(a^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{\tau\,\rho}{3M_{p}^{2}}=0\,,

(60)

and

a^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\,a^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{2\,a^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}+\frac{\tau^{2}{\cal A}}{3M_{p}^{2}}=0\,,

(61)

while the three conservation laws become

\left[r^{2}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}\right)\right]^{\prime}=-\frac{r^{2}{\cal A}}{3M_{p}^{2}}\qquad\hbox{($J_{\scriptscriptstyle A}^{\mu}$ conservation)}\,,

(62)

\left[r^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}+\frac{a\,a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}\right)\right]^{\prime}=-\frac{r^{2}}{3M_{p}^{2}}(\rho+a\,{\cal A})\quad\hbox{($J_{\scriptscriptstyle S}^{\mu}$ conservation)}

(63)

and

\left\{r^{2}\left[\frac{(\tau^{2}-a^{2})a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}-\frac{2a\,\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}\right]\right\}^{\prime}=\frac{r^{2}}{3M_{p}^{2}}\Bigl[(a^{2}-\tau^{2}){\cal A}+2\,a\,\rho\Bigr]\quad\hbox{($J_{\scriptscriptstyle N}^{\mu}$ conservation)}\,.

(64)

The conservation laws (
62
) and (
63
) provide useful information when they are integrated within the interior of the source, using the boundary condition
a^{\prime}(0)=\tau^{\prime}(0)=0
at the center of the matter distribution at
r=0
that is required by spherical symmetry. Assuming
a
and
\tau
themselves remain bounded at
r=0
we find expressions for the radial field derivatives just exterior to the edge of the source (which we imagine occurs at
r=R
):

\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}\right)_{r=R}=-\frac{1}{3M_{p}^{2}R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}{\rm d}r\;r^{2}{\cal A}(r)\,,

(65)

and

\left(\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}+\frac{a\,a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}\right)_{r=R}=-\frac{1}{3M_{p}^{2}R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}{\rm d}r\;r^{2}\Bigl[\rho(r)+a(r)\,{\cal A}(r)\Bigr]\,.

(66)

The implication of the third conservation law is explored in the next section.

3.2
Spherically symmetric exterior solutions

We next explicitly integrate the axio-dilaton field equations to obtain their general solution external to the gravitating source.

3.2.1
Trivial axion solution

For the purposes of making contact to the Brans-Dicke special case it is worth first exploring the limit
{\cal A}=0
. In this case the axion equation (
61
) is trivially solved by a constant axion field
a=a_{0}
and the dilaton equation (
60
) then becomes

\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\varphi^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{\rho}{3\zeta M_{p}^{2}}=0\,,

(67)

where
\tau(r):=e^{\zeta\varphi(r)}
.

This last form is the usual Brans-Dicke result, and is solved in the exterior region (where
\rho=0
) by the usual Coulomb-like solution found in previous sections

\varphi=\varphi_{\infty}+\frac{\varphi_{1}}{r}\,,

(68)

with integration constants
\varphi_{\infty}
and
\varphi_{1}
. In this case the above conservation laws degenerate into the single independent condition

\left[r^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}\right)\right]^{\prime}=\zeta\Bigl(r^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\Bigr)^{\prime}=-\frac{r^{2}\rho}{3M_{p}^{2}}

(69)

that implies the boundary condition

\varphi^{\prime}(R)=-\frac{1}{3\zeta M_{p}^{2}R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}{\rm d}r\;r^{2}\rho=-\frac{2GM}{3\zeta R^{2}}

(70)

just exterior to the source, where
M=4\pi\int_{0}^{R}{\rm d}r\;r^{2}\rho(r)
is the mass in the absence of gravitational back-reaction. Comparing to (
68

\zeta\varphi_{1}=\frac{2}{3}GM
and comparing this to (
16
) implies
{\mathfrak{g}}=-1/(3\zeta)=-1/\sqrt{6}
, as before. We wish to similarly determine how the boundary conditions (
65
) and (
66

{\cal A}\neq 0
.

3.2.2
Full axio-dilaton solutions

The first step to this end is to find the general exterior solutions to identify its integration constants and the existence of so many conservation laws allows this to be done more explicitly than is usually possible. In the absence of sources
{\cal A}=\rho=0
and eqs. (
60

61

\tau^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\tau^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau}+\frac{(a^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau}=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad a^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\,a^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{2\,a^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}=0\,.

(71)

The conservation laws provide immediate first integrals of these equations. In particular, when
\rho={\cal A}=0

\frac{a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}=\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}{r^{2}}

(72)

and

\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}=\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}{r^{2}}-\frac{a\,a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}=\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}-a\,C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}{r^{2}}\,.

(73)

Using these to eliminate
\tau^{\prime}
and

in the third conservation law (

\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}{r^{2}}=\frac{(\tau^{2}-a^{2})a^{\prime}}{\tau^{2}}-\frac{2a\,\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}=\frac{(\tau^{2}+a^{2})C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}{r^{2}}-\frac{2aC_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}{r^{2}}\,.

(74)

For later purposes it is noteworthy that the character of the solutions to these equations when
C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}=0
can differ qualitatively from those that arise when
C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}\neq 0

a^{\prime}=C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}=0
then equations (

\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}=\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}{r^{2}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{2a\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}=-\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}{r^{2}}\,,

(75)

and so
C_{\scriptscriptstyle N}+2aC_{\scriptscriptstyle S}=0

\tau
varies monotonically with

\tau^{2}+\left(a-\alpha\right)^{2}=\beta^{2}\,,

(76)

where

\alpha:=\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}{C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\beta^{2}:=\left(\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}{C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}\right)^{2}+\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}{C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}}\,.

(77)

This says that solutions sweep out circles of radius
\beta
centered at the point

on the
a

-
\tau
plane (or – more properly – semi-circles in the upper-half

-
\tau
plane since

Now comes the crucial point: retrieving Brans Dicke theories in the

This integrates to give

\int\frac{{\rm d}a}{\beta^{2}-(a-\alpha)^{2}}=C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}\int\frac{{\rm d}r}{r^{2}}\,,

and the result is only consistent with

\alpha
,

,

and so

\left(\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}\right)_{r=R}=-\frac{1}{3M_{p}^{2}R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}{\rm d}r\;r^{2}\rho(r)\Bigl\{1+\varepsilon\,[a(r)-a(R)]\Bigr\}\simeq-\frac{2GM}{3R^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\cosh X}=\frac{\beta}{\cosh\delta}\left[1-\frac{\beta\gamma}{r}\,\tanh\delta+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle a-\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\tanh X=-\beta\left[\tanh\delta+\frac{\beta\gamma/r}{\cosh^{2}\delta}+\cdots\right]\,.

Notice in particular that the dilaton ‘charge’ – defined as the coefficient of

3.3
Gravitational response

We now collect all the threads: having such an explicit exterior solution allows us to compute the metric response for this system and thereby determine its implications for the motion of matter test-particles.

In principle the presence of the direct matter-axion coupling embodied by
{\cal A}(r)
means that motion of matter particles differs from the treatment described in §
2.2
, because direct axion-induced forces can prevent them from moving along geodesics of the Jordan-frame metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
. We choose as our particular focus here the limit where
|{\cal A}|\ll\rho
so that these direct axion-related forces are negligible. This is a fairly natural limit given that the pseudoscalar nature of the axion makes its individual couplings to atoms not sum as coherently in macroscopic matter as does the energy density.

What is interesting is that despite this a tiny but nonzero
{\cal A}
nevertheless significantly changes how matter responds to the dilaton, because the dilaton-axion couplings can divert the external field of a source away from the dilaton and towards the axion.

To see how this works we therefore follow §
2.2
and assume that
{\cal A}
is small enough that direct axion forces are negligible in which case test particles move along the geodesics of the Jordan frame metric,
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
. Tests of gravity then test whether these differ from the geodesics predicted by General Relativity. Just as in §
2.2
there are two reasons why
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
differs from GR: (
i
) the Weyl factor
A
in
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=A^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
causes the Jordan- and Einstein-frame metrics to differ, and (
ii
) the Einstein-frame metric
g_{\mu\nu}
solves the Einstein equation with axiodilaton stress energy rather than being Ricci flat as in GR.

Consider first the second of these effects. The Einstein-frame metric’s response to scalar stress-energy is expressed by the field equation (
37

\rho={\cal A}=0

(91)

Specialized to

{\cal R}_{rr}=-\frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}+(a^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\right]=-\frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{C_{\scriptscriptstyle S}^{2}-2aC_{\scriptscriptstyle S}C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}+C_{\scriptscriptstyle A}^{2}(a^{2}+\tau^{2})}{r^{4}}\right]=-\frac{3\gamma^{2}\beta^{2}}{4r^{4}}\,.

4
Conclusions

In summary, we propose a concrete screening mechanism for Brans-Dicke scalar fields that can allow gravitational-strength couplings to be consistent with solar system tests of GR. In order to be effective the mechanism requires the existence of additional scalar fields with small but nonzero matter couplings and nontrivial target-space derivative couplings to the Brans-Dicke field.

We explore the mechanism in an explicit model consisting of an axio-dilaton whose target space enjoys an
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry. The conservation laws associated with this symmetry allow explicit solutions to the field equations outside a spherically symmetric source, and this allows us to determine the exact axion and dilaton profiles. We find these profiles to have several noteworthy features.

•

Test-particle motion dominantly responds to the Jordan-frame metric, and outside the source this differs in two ways from the metric in standard General Relativity. It first differs because of the Weyl factor
A
that relates the Einstein and Jordan frame metrics in eq. (
1
). The second difference arises because the Einstein-frame metric responds to the stress-energy in the axio-dilaton field exterior to the source. Of these, the former dominates because the curvature due to the presence of axio-dilaton stress-energy appears in the metric at order
1/r^{2}
and so is too small to contribute to the post-Newtonian parameter
\gamma_{PPN}
.

•

The contribution to
\gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle PPN}
coming from the Weyl factor comes through the coefficient
a_{1}
given in (
94
). This coefficient can be determined in terms of the constants of integration and – provided there is a small but nonzero coupling of axion to matter – it can easily be small enough to satisfy the experimental constraint (
3
).

•

The mechanism works because the target space curvature converts the field outside the star from dilaton to axion despite the dilaton-matter coupling being much larger than the axion-matter coupling. The axion field is not detected in solar-system tests because its coupling to matter is assumed to be small enough that direct axion-induced forces are negligible and because it does not appear in the Weyl factor, to which test-particle motion is mostly sensitive.

•

The Brans-Dicke limit of vanishing axion-matter coupling is a subtle one and is illustrated in Fig.
1
. In general the solutions trace out semicircles in the
\tau
–
a
plane, centered on a point along the
a
-axis. This degenerates to a vertical line in the limit of vanishing axion-matter coupling; a solution that becomes indistinguishable from the semicircle in the limit of extremely large circle radius (see region
A
of Fig.
1
). But for any finite radius, no matter how large, the other parts of the semicircle (such as region
B
in Fig.
1
) do
not
resemble a vertical straight line. This is why small nonzero axion couplings can change the field response relative to zero coupling in such a homeopathic way.

We believe our mechanism can help open up the exploration of cosmologies driven by axio-dilaton fields by providing a mechanism for them to evade solar-system constraints despite their gravitational-strength dilaton couplings. Although this does not in itself also solve the technical naturalness problems (small scalar masses and small vacuum energies) associated with such cosmologies, it provides a necessary ingredient once these problems are addressed. Indeed our mechanism emerged from parallel work aimed at these naturalness problems in which the axio-dilaton structure used here plays a central role (details of which can be found in companion papers
[
30

38
]
).

There is clearly much more that can be done to develop and better understand the homeopathic screening mechanism. GR is tested in a great many ways these days, and it is worthwhile establishing which of these tests is most sensitive to the presence of an axio-dilaton with the assumed matter couplings.

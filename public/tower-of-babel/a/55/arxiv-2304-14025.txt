Cluster of vortex helices in the incompressible 3 3 d Euler equations
Ignacio Guerra, Monica Musso

Abstract
. In an inviscid and incompressible fluid in dimension 3 3 , we prove the existence of several helical filaments, or vortex helices, collapsing into each others.



1.
Introduction

Ideal incompressible homogeneous fluid of density
\rho
in three dimensional space in a time interval
(0,T)

is governed by the Euler equations

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{t}+({\bf u}\cdot{\nabla}){\bf u}

\displaystyle=-{1\over\rho}{\nabla}p

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T),

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,{\bf u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T),

supplemented by the initial velocity
{\bf u}(\cdot,0)=u_{0}(x)
. Here
{\bf u}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity field and
p\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}

is the pressure, determined by the incompressibility condition. We will consider constant density

\rho=1
.
For a solution
{\bf u}
of (
1.1
),
its vorticity is defined as

\vec{\omega}=\mathop{\rm curl}{\bf u}
. Then
\vec{\omega}
solves the Euler system in
vorticity form
(
1.1
),

\displaystyle\vec{\omega}_{t}+({\bf u}\cdot{\nabla}){\vec{\omega}}

\displaystyle=(\vec{\omega}\cdot{\nabla}){\bf u}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\quad{\bf u}=\mathop{\rm curl}\vec{\psi},

\displaystyle-\Delta\vec{\psi}=\vec{\omega}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle{\vec{\omega}}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm curl}u_{0}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Vortex filaments
are solutions of the Euler equations whose vorticity is concentrated in a small tube near an evolving imaginary smooth curve
\Gamma(t)
embedded in entire
\mathbb{R}^{3}
so that the associated velocity field vanishes as the distance to the curve goes to infinity.
Da Rios
[
14
]
in 1906, and Levi-Civita
[
31
]
in 1908, found that, if the vorticity
concentrates smoothly and symmetrically in a small tube of size
\varepsilon>0
around a smooth curve for a certain interval of time,
then it is possible to compute the instantaneous velocity of the curve to leading order.
These computations suggest that the curve should evolve by its binormal flow, with a large velocity of order
|\log\varepsilon|
. If
\Gamma(t)
is parametrized as
\gamma(s,t)
where
s
designates its arclength parameter, then
\gamma(s,t)
asymptotically obeys a law of the form

\gamma_{t}=2\,\kappa\,|\log\varepsilon|\,(\gamma_{s}\times\gamma_{ss})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, or scaling
t=|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau
,

\gamma_{\tau}=2\,\kappa\,(\gamma_{s}\times\gamma_{ss})=2\,\kappa\,c\,{\bf b}_{\Gamma(\tau)}.

(1.3)

Here
\kappa
is the
circulation
of the velocity field on the boundary of sections to the filament, which is assumed to be a constant independent of
\varepsilon
. Besides
{\bf t}_{\Gamma(\tau)},\,{\bf n}_{\Gamma(\tau)},\,{\bf b}_{\Gamma(\tau)}
are the usual tangent, normal and binormal unit vectors to
\Gamma(\tau)
, and
c
its curvature. See
[
36
,
34
]
for a complete discussion on this topic.

Jerrard and Seis
[
25
]
rigorously proved Da Rios’ formal computation,
conditional upon knowing that the vorticity of a solution remains concentrated around some curve. In particular they consider a solution to (
1.2
) whose vorticity

\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
satisfies, as
\varepsilon\to 0

\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)-\delta_{\Gamma(\tau)}{\bf t}_{\Gamma(\tau)}\rightharpoonup 0,\quad 0\leq\tau\leq T,

(1.4)

where
\Gamma(\tau)
is a sufficiently regular curve
and
\delta_{\Gamma(\tau)}
denotes a uniform Dirac measure on the curve. They proved that in these circumstances the curve

\Gamma(\tau)
does indeed evolve by the law (
1.3
). See
[
27
]
and its references for results on the flow (
1.3
).

The
vortex filament conjecture
(
[
7
]
,
[
25
]
) refers to the question of existence of true solutions of
(
1.2
) that satisfy (
1.4
) near a given curve
\Gamma(\tau)

that evolves by the binormal flow (
1.3
). This is an open question,
except for very special cases.

A known solution of the binormal flow (
1.3
) that does not change its form in time is a circle
\Gamma(\tau)
with radius
R

translating with constant speed equal to
{2\over R}
along its axis.
Solutions to (
1.2
) whose vorticity is concentrated in a circular vortex filament are known as vortex rings, and the study of these objects dates back to Helmoltz and Kelvin’ work. In 1894, Hill found an explicit axially symmetric solution of (
1.2
) supported in a sphere (Hill’s vortex ring).
Fraenkel’s result
[
24
]

provided the first construction of a vortex ring concentrated around a torus with fixed radius and a small, nearly singular section
\varepsilon>0
, travelling with constant speed
\sim|\log\varepsilon|
, rigorously establishing the
vortex filament conjecture for the case of traveling rings. Vortex rings have been analyzed in larger generality in
[
23
,
35
,
2
,
20
]
.

Another known solution of the binormal flow (
1.3
) that does not change its form in time is the
rotating-translating helix
. It is a circular helix of radius
R
and pitch
h>0
, which rotates with constant speed

{2\,\kappa\,h\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}
and translates vertically in the direction of their axis of symmetry with constant speed
{2\,\kappa\,R^{2}\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}
.
Solutions to the Euler equations (
1.2
) whose vorticity is concentrated in a helical vortex filament are known as vortex helices, and the description of these objects started with the works of Joukowsky
[
29
]
, Da Rios
[
15
]
and Levi-Civita
[
32
]
. In
[
18
]

the authors provided the first construction of a vortex helix concentrated in an
\varepsilon
-tubolar neighborhood of a rotating-translating helix evolving by binormal flow, establishing the
vortex filament conjecture for the case of rotating-translating helices. In
[
18
]
the authors also find a solution to (
1.2
) with several vortex helices, rotating-translating with comparable but different speeds.

In this paper we are concerned with solutions to the Euler equations (
1.2
) consisting of several vortex helices which are rotating-translating with almost the same speed. They are global-in-time solutions to (
1.2
) and their vorticity has at main order the shape of several helical filaments which collapse into each others. We call this phenomena a cluster of vortex helices. Let us be more precise.

Let
N
be a given integer. For any
i=1,\ldots,N
consider points
(a_{i},b_{i})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, numbers
\sigma_{i}
and
\beta_{i}

\gamma_{i}(s,\tau)=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i}\cos\big(\frac{s-\sigma_{i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\big)-b_{i}\sin\big(\frac{s-\sigma_{i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\big)\\

\frac{hs+\beta_{i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(1.5)

where

At any instant

evolves by the binormal flow (

provided the speeds

are chosen to be

\sigma_{i}=\frac{2\,\kappa_{i}\,h}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}\quad\beta_{i}=\frac{2\,\kappa_{i}\,R_{i}^{2}}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}.

Each
\gamma_{i}(s,\tau)
can be recovered from

by a rotation and a vertical translation

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=Q_{-{\sigma_{i}\over\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}}\tau}\gamma(s,0)+\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
{\beta_{i}\over\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}}\tau\end{matrix}\right],\quad

(1.6)

\displaystyle Q_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{matrix}\right),

or equivalently from

{s+\beta_{i}\tau\over\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}}\end{matrix}\right].

We shall identify the base point

. Helical filaments with comparable but different speeds as in

We are interested in colliding helical filaments. Let

be a fixed number and assume that the points

We find that the points

needs to converge to

. We assume they have the form

\displaystyle P_{j}

\displaystyle=(r_{0}+s,0)+\frac{\hat{P}_{j}}{|{\log\varepsilon}|},\quad P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{N}),\quad\hat{P}=(\hat{P}_{1},\ldots,\hat{P}_{N}).

(2.3)

We assume the following bounds on
s
and
\hat{P}

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}\|\lesssim 1,\quad|s|\lesssim{\log|\log\varepsilon|\over|\log\varepsilon|}

(2.4)

In other words, we look for the stream function
\Psi
to have the asymptotic behaviour

\Psi(x)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\kappa_{j}\Psi_{j}(x),\quad{\mbox{with}}\quad-\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\Psi_{j})\sim 8\pi\delta_{P_{j}},\quad\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

We expect each function
\Psi_{j}
to be, locally around
P_{j}
, an approximate Green’s function for the operator
\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\cdot)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

This section is devoted to analyze the approximate Green’s function for the operator
\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\cdot)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and to construct an approximate stream function for the
N
-helical filaments.

2.1.
Approximate Green’s function for the operator
\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\cdot)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The purpose of this sub-section is to find an explicit regular function which locally around a point
P=(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies approximately

-\nabla\cdot\left(K\nabla\Psi\right)=8\pi\delta_{P}.

(2.5)

To this purpose we need to understand the structure of the operator in divergence form

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=-\nabla\cdot(K\,\nabla\,),\quad{\mbox{where}}\quad

(2.6)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)

when evaluated around a given point
P
. We will show that, after an ad-hoc change of variable, the operator
L
will look like the usual Laplace operator in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
when considered in a neighborhood of
P
.

The operator
L
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle={h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{1}}+{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{2}x_{2}}-2{x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{2}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle-{x_{1}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right){\partial}_{x_{1}}-{x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right){\partial}_{x_{2}}.

Indeed, using the notation
K=\left(\begin{matrix}K_{11}&K_{12}\\
K_{12}&K_{22}\end{matrix}\right)
, we get

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\cdot)=K_{11}\,{\partial}_{x_{1}}^{2}+K_{22}\,{\partial}_{x_{2}}^{2}+2K_{12}\,{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+({\partial}_{x_{1}}K_{11}+{\partial}_{x_{2}}K_{12})\,{\partial}_{x_{1}}+({\partial}_{x_{2}}K_{22}+{\partial}_{x_{1}}K_{12})\,{\partial}_{x_{2}}

\displaystyle={h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{1}}+{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{2}x_{2}}-2{x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{2}}

\displaystyle+\left({\partial}_{x_{1}}\left({h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-{\partial}_{x_{2}}\left({x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)\right){\partial}_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle+\left({\partial}_{x_{2}}\left({h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-{\partial}_{x_{1}}\left({x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)\right){\partial}_{x_{2}},

where
r=|x|
.
Formula (
2.7
) follows directly from the facts that

{\partial}_{x_{1}}\left({h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-{\partial}_{x_{2}}\left({x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)=-{x_{1}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right)

and

{\partial}_{x_{2}}\left({h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-{\partial}_{x_{1}}\left({x_{1}x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\right)=-{x_{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right).

Let us introduce the change of variables

\displaystyle x_{1}-a

\displaystyle={ah\over R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{1}-{b\over R}z_{2}

\displaystyle x_{2}-b

\displaystyle={bh\over R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{1}+{a\over R}z_{2},

where
R=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}.
This is equivalent to say

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle={\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over hR}\left[a(x_{1}-a)+b(x_{2}-b)\right]

\displaystyle z_{2}

\displaystyle={1\over R}\left[-b(x_{1}-a)+a(x_{2}-b)\right].

We will also use the matrix notation

x-P=A[P]z,\quad A[P]=\left(\begin{matrix}{ah\over R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}&-{b\over R}\\
{bh\over R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}&{a\over R}\end{matrix}\right).

(2.8)

When expressed in the
z
-variable, we recognize that the operator
L
takes the form

L=\Delta_{z}+B,

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left({h^{2}(R^{2}-r^{2})+z_{2}^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})\over(h^{2}+r^{2})h^{2}}\right){\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}

(2.10)

\displaystyle+{1\over(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left(\left(z_{1}\frac{h}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}+R\right)^{2}-r^{2}\right){\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}}

\displaystyle-2{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h(h^{2}+r^{2})}z_{2}\left(z_{1}{h\over\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}+R\right){\partial}_{z_{1}z_{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{1}(h^{2}+R^{2})+Rh\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}{h^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right){\partial}_{z_{1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{2}}{h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right){\partial}_{z_{2}}.

Here
r=r(z)
is

\displaystyle r^{2}=|x|^{2}=R^{2}+2R{h\over\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{1}+q_{2}(z)\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>q_{2}(z)={h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}.

Formulas (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are consequence of the following straightforward computations

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle={a\over R}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h}{\partial}_{z_{1}}-{b\over R}{\partial}_{z_{2}}

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x_{2}}

\displaystyle={b\over R}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h}{\partial}_{z_{1}}+{a\over R}{\partial}_{z_{2}}

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x_{1}\,x_{1}}

\displaystyle={a^{2}\over R^{2}}\,{(h^{2}+R^{2})\over h^{2}}\,{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{1}}+{b^{2}\over R^{2}}{\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}}-2{ab\over R^{2}}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h}{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{2}}

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x_{2}\,x_{2}}

\displaystyle={b^{2}\over R^{2}}\,{(h^{2}+R^{2})\over h^{2}}\,{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{1}}+{a^{2}\over R^{2}}{\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}}+2{ab\over R^{2}}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h}{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{2}}

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x_{1}\,x_{2}}

\displaystyle={ab\over R^{2}}\,{(h^{2}+R^{2})\over h^{2}}\,{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{1}}-{ab\over R^{2}}{\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}}+{a^{2}-b^{2}\over R^{2}}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}\over h}{\partial}_{z_{1}\,z_{2}}.

The operator
B
in (
2.10
) becomes a small perturbation of the Laplacian, when we restrict our attention to a small region around the point
P
, that in the
z
-variable can be described with
|z|<\delta
, for
a fixed
\delta
small. Indeed, in this region the operator
B
has the form

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left(-2{Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3/2}}\,z_{1}+O(|z|^{2})\right){\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}+O(|z|^{2}){\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}}

\displaystyle-\left(2{R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{2}+O(|z|^{2})\right){\partial}_{z_{1}z_{2}}

\displaystyle-\left({R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)+O(|z|)\right){\partial}_{z_{1}}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{z_{2}}{h^{2}+R^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)+O(|z|^{2})\right){\partial}_{z_{2}}.

Equation (
2.5
) thus becomes

-\left(\Delta+B\right)\psi=8\pi\delta_{0},\quad\psi(z)=\Psi\left(P+A[P]z\right).

(2.11)

We now choose a regularization of the Green’s function of the Laplace operator
\Delta_{z}
as a starting point for the construction of the approximate regularization to (
2.11
). The regularization we choose is a radial solution of the Liouville equation

\Delta u+e^{u}=0\quad{\quad\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u}\,<\infty.

(2.12)

All solutions to (
2.12
) that are radially symmetric with respect to the origin are given by

\Gamma_{\mu\varepsilon}(z)-2\log\varepsilon\mu,\quad{\mbox{where}}\quad\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)=\log{8\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

for any value of the constants
\varepsilon
and
\mu>0
. Indeed we have

-\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}=\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}e^{\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}}={1\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}}U\left({z\over\varepsilon\mu}\right)\quad{\mbox{with}}\quad U(y)=\frac{8}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}.

Hence

-\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}\rightharpoonup 8\pi\delta_{0},\quad\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\mu\to 0.

Besides, we compute

\displaystyle B[\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}]

\displaystyle=-{2Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3/2}}z_{1}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}-{2R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{2}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{2}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle-{R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}\left(1+{2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}\right){\partial}_{z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+E_{1}

where
E_{1}
is a smooth function, uniformly bounded for
\varepsilon\mu
small, in a bounded region for
z
.

We take advantage of the explicit expression of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}
to find

\displaystyle{\partial}_{z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)=-{4z_{1}\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}},

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)=-{4z_{1}\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}}+{8z_{1}^{3}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle z_{2}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{2}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)

\displaystyle={8z_{2}^{2}z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}.

Using that

z_{1}z_{2}^{2}={|z|^{2}z_{1}\over 4}-{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over 4},\quad z_{1}^{3}={3|z|^{2}z_{1}\over 4}+{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over 4}

we obtain

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{2Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3/2}}z_{1}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}-{2R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{2}{\partial}_{z_{1}z_{2}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle-{R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}\left(1+{2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}\right){\partial}_{z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle=\left[{8hR\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}+{4R(3h^{2}+R^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}\right]{z_{1}\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle-{16hR\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{z_{1}^{3}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}-{16R\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{1\over 2}}{z_{1}z_{2}^{2}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle={4hR\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{z_{1}\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}}+{4R^{3}\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle+{4R(4h^{2}+R^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

We can modify the function
\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}
to eliminate part of the above error. We define

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)\left(1+c_{1}z_{1}\right),\quad{\mbox{with}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}{Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}

Since

({\partial}_{z_{1}z_{1}}+{\partial}_{z_{2}z_{2}})(c_{1}z_{1}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu})=-8\,c_{1}\,{z_{1}\over\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}}-8\,c_{1}\,{\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}},

with this choice of
c_{1}

we get

\displaystyle L(\psi_{1})(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+{4R^{3}\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}+{4R(3h^{2}+R^{2})\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle+c_{1}B(z_{1}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu})+E_{1}

where
E_{1}
is an explicit function, which smooth in the variable
z
and uniformly bounded, as
\varepsilon\mu\to 0
.

We now use the fact that

z_{2}{\partial}_{z_{2}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)=-\dfrac{4z_{2}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2}}
, to write

\displaystyle c_{1}B(z_{1}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu})

\displaystyle=c_{1}z_{1}B(\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu})-c_{1}{4Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3/2}}z_{1}{\partial}_{z_{1}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}-c_{1}{2R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}z_{2}{\partial}_{z_{2}}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle-c_{1}{R\over h\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}\left(1+{2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+\bar{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=-{R^{2}\over 2(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+E_{2}

where
E_{2}
is another explicit function, smooth in the variable
z
and uniformly bounded, as
\varepsilon\mu\to 0
.

Combining these computations we obtain that the function
\psi_{1}
introduced in (

) satisfies

\displaystyle L(\psi_{1})(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+{4R^{3}\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}+{4R(3h^{2}+R^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle-{R^{2}\over 2(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+E_{1}+E_{2},

where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are explicit functions, smooth in the variable
z
and uniformly bounded, as
\varepsilon\mu\to 0
.

Our next step is to
introduce a further modification to
\psi_{1}
to eliminate the two terms

-{R^{2}\over 2(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad{4R^{3}\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}{{\mbox{Re}}(z^{3})\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}.

For the first one, we observe that

\displaystyle\Delta(c_{2}|z|^{2}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu})

\displaystyle-{R^{2}\over 2(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(4c_{2}-{R^{2}\over 2(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)\right)\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

\displaystyle+2c_{2}z\cdot\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}+c_{2}|z|^{2}\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}

and choose

as

c_{2}={R^{2}\over 8(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right).

To correct the second term , we introduce

h_{1}(s)=s^{3}\int_{s}^{1}{dx\over x^{7}}\int_{0}^{x}{\eta^{7}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu^{2}+\eta^{2})^{2}}\,d\eta.

It solves

h_{1}^{\prime\prime}+{1\over s}h_{1}^{\prime}-{9\over s^{2}}h_{1}+{s^{3}\over(\varepsilon^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}=0,

it is smooth and uniformly bounded as

, and

, as

.
Writing

, we have that

(2.14)

For any
\mu>0
, we define the approximate regularization for (

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)\left(1+c_{1}z_{1}+c_{2}|z|^{2}\right)+{4R^{3}\over h(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}H_{1}(z),

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}{Rh\over(h^{2}+R^{2})^{3\over 2}}

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle={R^{2}\over 8(h^{2}+R^{2})^{2}}\left({2h^{2}\over h^{2}+R^{2}}+1\right)

and
H_{1}
as in (

).
With this definition, we have that

where

.

In the original variables

\Psi_{\mu,P}(x)=\psi_{\mu}\left(A[P]^{-1}(x-P)\right)

2.2.
Approximate stream function for
N
-helical filaments.

Let
N
be a fixed integer and consider
N
points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{N}
of the form (
2.3
) and satisfying (
2.4
). For any
j=1,\ldots,N
, we write

P_{j}=(a_{j},b_{j}),

we fix positive constants
\mu_{j}
and define

\Psi_{j}(x)=\Psi_{P_{j},\mu_{j}}(x)

where
\psi_{P_{j},\mu_{j}}
is defined in (
2.17
). Hence, for each
j
we have

L(\Psi_{j})=\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu_{j}}+{4R_{j}(3h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}+E_{3,j}(z)

(2.18)

where

R_{j}=\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2}},\quad z=A[P_{j}]^{-1}(x-P_{j}).

The stream function of
N
-helical filaments looks at main order as a superposition of stream functions
\Psi_{j}
associated to each helical filament. Since the relative distance of the points
P_{j}
is of order
|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
, we multiply each
\Psi_{j}
by a cut-off function to get

\eta_{0}(x)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,\Psi_{j}(x)

where

\eta_{0}(x)=\eta\left(|x-(r_{0},0)|\right),

(2.19)

with
\eta
a fixed smooth function with

\eta(s)=1,\quad{\mbox{for}}\quad s\leq{1\over 2},\quad\eta(s)=0,\quad{\mbox{for}}\quad s\geq 1.

(2.20)

Using the notation introduced in (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle L\left(\eta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\,\kappa_{j}\,\Psi_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,\left(\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu_{j}}+{4R_{j}(3h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+g(x),\quad{\mbox{where}}

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,E_{3,j}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\kappa_{j}\left[\,L(\eta_{0}\Psi_{j})-\eta_{0}L(\Psi_{j})\right]

The function
g
has compact support and satisfies

\|g(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C_{\delta_{1}}

for some positive constant which depends on
\delta_{1}
.

It is convenient to slightly modify the ansatz
\eta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,\Psi_{j}
adding a term which is defined globally in the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to cancel
g(x)
.
Let
H_{2\varepsilon}(x)
solve

L(H_{2\varepsilon})+g=0,\quad{\mbox{in}}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.21)

For a smooth function
h(x)
satisfying the decay condition

\|h\|_{\nu}\,:=\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|x|)^{\nu}|h(x)|\,<\,+\infty\,,

for some
\nu>2
, there exists a solution
\psi(x)
to problem

L(\psi)+h=0,\quad{\mbox{in}}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}

(2.22)

which is of class
C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
0<\beta<1
, and defines a linear operator
\psi={\mathcal{T}}^{o}(g)
of
g

and satisfies the bound

|\psi(x)|\,\leq\,C{\|h\|_{\nu}}(1+|x|^{2}),

(2.23)

for some positive constant
C
. The proof of this fact can be found in Proposition
6.1
.

Using this result we obtain that the solution
H_{2\varepsilon}
to (
2.21
) satisfies the estimate

|H_{2\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C_{\delta_{1}}(1+|x|^{2}).

Besides, observe that such solution is given up to the addition of a constant.
We define the function
H_{2\varepsilon}(x)
to be the one which furthermore satisfies

H_{2\varepsilon}((x_{0},0))=0.

With this is mind, we get to the definition of a first approximate stream function for
N
-helical filaments

\Psi_{0}(x)=\eta_{0}(x)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,\Psi_{j}(x)+H_{2\varepsilon}(x),

(2.24)

so that

L\left(\Psi_{0}\right)=\eta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\kappa_{j}\,\,\left(\Delta\Gamma_{\varepsilon\mu_{j}}+{4R_{j}(3h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})\over h(h^{2}+R_{j}^{2})^{3\over 2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}\,z_{1}\over(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{j}^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}\right).

(2.25)

We recall that the definition of
\eta_{0}
is given in (
2.19
).
The function
\Psi_{0}
is defined in the whole

6.1
) becomes equivalent to the following infinite set of ODEs:

\displaystyle L_{k}[\psi_{k}]+g_{k}(r)=0,\quad r\in(0,\infty),

(9.1)

\displaystyle L_{k}[\psi_{k}]:={h^{2}\over r}\big({r\over r^{2}+h^{2}}\psi_{k}^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}-{k^{2}\over h^{2}+r^{2}}\left({h^{2}\over r^{2}}+1\right)\psi_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The operator
L_{k}
when
r\to 0
or
r\to+\infty
resembles

\displaystyle L_{k}[p]\ \sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}(rp^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{k^{2}p}{r^{2}}\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to 0

\displaystyle L_{k}[p]\ \sim

\displaystyle\frac{h^{2}}{r}(\frac{1}{r}p^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{k^{2}p}{r^{2}}\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to+\infty

For
k\geq 1
,
L_{k}
satisfies the maximum principle. This gives the existence of a positive function
z_{k}(r)
with
L_{k}[z_{k}]=0
with

z_{k}(r)\sim r^{k}\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to 0,\quad z_{k}(r)\sim r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{(k/h)r}\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to+\infty.

Take
k=1
. The function

\bar{\psi}(r)=z_{1}(r)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{(1+s^{2})ds}{sz_{1}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{1+\rho^{\nu}}z_{1}(\rho)\rho\,d\rho

solves
L_{1}[\bar{\psi}]+\frac{1}{1+r^{\nu}}=0
, and satisfies the bounds

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(r)=O(r^{2})\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to 0

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(r)=O(r^{-\nu+2})\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to+\infty.

We take
\bar{\psi}
as a barrier for the equation at
k=1
. Besides, this functions works as a barrier also for

k\geq 2
.

For
k\geq 2
, the function

\psi_{k}(r)=z_{k}(r)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{(1+s^{2})ds}{sz_{k}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}h_{k}(\rho)z_{k}(\rho)\rho\,d\rho,

is the unique decaying decaying solution (
9.1
), and it satisfies the estimate

|\psi_{k}(r)|\leq\frac{4}{k^{2}}{\|g\|_{\nu}}\bar{\psi}(r).

since

|g_{k}(r)|\leq\frac{\|g\|_{\nu}}{1+r^{\nu}}.

If
k=0
, the solution is given by the explicit formula

\psi_{0}(r)=-\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1+s^{2}}{h^{2}s}\,ds\int_{0}^{s}g_{0}(\rho)\rho\,d\rho

and satisfies the bound

|\psi_{0}(r)|\leq C\|g\|_{\nu}(1+r^{2}).

The function

\psi(x):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(r)e^{ji\theta},

with the
\psi_{k}
being the functions built above, clearly defines a linear operator of
g
and satisfies estimate (
6.2
).
The proof is concluded. ∎

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Setting
y=re^{i\theta}
,
we write

h(y)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h_{k}(r)e^{ik\theta},\quad\phi(y)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(r)e^{ik\theta}

The equation is equivalent to

{\tt L}_{k}[\phi_{k}]+h_{k}(r)=0,\quad r\in(0,\infty)

(9.2)

where

{\tt L}_{k}[\phi_{k}]=\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{r}\phi_{k}^{\prime}+e^{\Gamma_{0}}\phi_{k}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\phi_{k}

Using the formula of variation of parameters, the following formula (continuously extended to
r=1
defines a smooth solution of (
9.2
) for
k=0
:

\phi_{0}(r)=-z(r)\int_{1}^{r}\frac{ds}{sz(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}h_{0}(\rho)z(\rho)\rho\,ds,\quad z(r)=\frac{r^{2}-1}{1+r^{2}}

Noting that
\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{0}(\rho)z(\rho)\rho\,ds=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{0}(y)\,dy

we see that this function satisfies

|\phi_{0}(r)|\,\leq\,C\big[\,\log(2+r)\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{0}(y)\,dy\Big|\,+\,(1+r)^{2-m}\|h\|_{m}\big].

Now we observe that

\phi_{k}(r)=-z(r)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{ds}{sz(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}h_{k}(\rho)z(\rho)\rho\,ds,\quad z(r)=\frac{4r}{1+r^{2}}

solves (
9.2
) for
k=-1,1
and satisfies

|\phi_{k}(r)|\,\leq\,C\big[\,(1+r)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{j}(y)\,dy\Big|\,+\,(1+r)^{2-m}\|h\|_{m}\big].

For
k=2
there is a function
z(r)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{2}[z]=0
,
z(r)\sim r^{2}
as
r\to 0
and as
r\to\infty
. For
|k|\geq 2
we have that

\bar{\phi}_{k}(r)=\frac{4}{k^{2}}z(r)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{ds}{sz(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}|h_{k}(\rho)|z(\rho)\rho\,ds,

is a positive supersolution for equation (
9.2
), hence the equation has a unique solution
\phi_{k}
with
|\phi_{k}(r)|\leq\bar{\phi}_{k}(r)
. Thus

|\phi_{k}(r)|\,\leq\,\frac{C}{k^{2}}(1+r)^{2-m}\|h\|_{m},\quad|k|\geq 2.

Thus
\phi_{k}
defined

\phi(y)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(r)e^{ik\theta}

defines a linear operator of functions
h
which is a solution of equation (
6.3
) which, adding up the individual estimates above, it satisfies the estimate (
6.5
).
As a corollary we find that similar bounds are obtained for first and second derivatives. In fact, let us set
for a large
y=Re
,
R=|y|\gg 1
,

\phi_{R}(z)={R^{m-2}}\phi(R(e+z)).

Then in a neighborhood of
y
, we find

\Delta_{z}\phi_{R}+\frac{8R^{2}}{(1+R^{2}|e+z|^{2})^{2}}\phi_{R}+h_{R}(z)=0,\quad|z|<\frac{1}{2}

where
h_{R}(z)=R^{m}h(R(e+z))
.
Let us set,

\delta_{i}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{i}\Big|,\quad i=0,1,2.

Then from (
6.5
), and a standard elliptic estimate we find

\|{\nabla}_{z}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))}+\|\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))}\,\leq\,C\Big[\delta_{0}R^{m-2}\log R+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}R^{m-1}+\|h\|_{m}\Big],

using that
\|h_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))}\ \leq\ C\|h\|_{m}.

Now since
[h_{R}]_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\ \leq\ C\|h\|_{m,\alpha}
, from interior Schauder estimates and the bound for
\phi_{R}
we then find

\|D^{2}_{z}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))}+[D^{2}_{z}\phi_{R}]_{B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0),\alpha}\,\leq\,C\Big[\delta_{0}R^{m-2}\log R+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta_{i}R^{m-1}+\|h\|_{m,\alpha}\Big].

From these relations, estimates (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) follow. ∎

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

We consider a standard linear extension operator
h\mapsto\tilde{h}
to entire
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
in such a way that the support of
\tilde{h}
is contained in
B_{2R}
and
\|\tilde{h}\|_{m,\beta}\leq C\|h\|_{m,\beta,B_{R}}
with
C
independent of all large
\bar{R}
. The operator
B_{i}
as defined in (
5.11
) and the coefficients are of class
C^{1}
in entire
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and have compact support in
B_{2R}
.
Then we consider the auxiliary problem in entire space

\Delta\phi+e^{\Gamma_{0}}\phi+B_{i}[\phi]+\tilde{h}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}c_{ij}e^{\Gamma_{0}}Z_{j}{\quad\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{2}

(9.3)

where, assuming that
\|h\|_{m}<+\infty
and
\phi
is of class
C^{2}
,

c_{ij}=c_{ij}[h,\phi]
are the scalars defined as so that

\gamma_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(B_{i}[\phi]+\tilde{h}(y))Z_{j}=c_{ij},\quad\gamma_{j}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\Gamma_{0}}Z_{j}^{2}

For
j=1,2
, by (
5.7
) and (
5.12
), we have
B_{i}[Z_{j}]=O((1+|y|)^{-2})\varepsilon\mu_{i}+O((1+|y|)^{-(3+a)})\varepsilon\mu_{i}\log|\log\varepsilon|
. Similarly for
j=0
, we have
B_{i}[Z_{0}]=O((1+|y|)^{-3})\varepsilon\mu_{i}+O((1+|y|)^{-(2+a)})\varepsilon\mu_{i}\log|\log\varepsilon|
. Since
m>2
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B_{i}[\phi]Z_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi\tilde{B}_{i}[Z_{j}]=O(\|\phi\|_{m-2})\varepsilon\mu_{i}\log|\log\varepsilon|.

where
\tilde{B}_{i}
have same estimates as
B_{i}
mentioned above. On the other hand

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R}}h(y)Z_{0}=O(R^{2-m})\|h\|_{m,\beta,B_{R}},\>\>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R}}h(y)Z_{j}=O(R^{1-m})\|h\|_{m,\beta,B_{R}}

for
j=1,2.
In addition, we readily check that

\|B_{i}[\phi]\|_{m,\beta}\leq C\frac{\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}\|\phi\|_{*,m-2,\beta},

where

\|\phi\|_{*,m-2,\beta}=\|D^{2}_{y}\phi\|_{m,\beta}+\|D_{y}\phi\|_{m-1}+\|\phi\|_{m-2}.

Let us consider the Banach space
X
of all
C^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

functions with

\|\phi\|_{*,m-2,\beta}<+\infty.

We find a solution of (
9.3
)
if we solve the equation

\phi=\mathcal{A}[\phi]+\mathcal{H},\quad\phi\in X

(9.4)

where

\mathcal{A}[\phi]=\mathcal{T}\Big[B_{i}[\phi]-\sum_{j=0}^{2}c_{ij}[0,\phi]e^{\Gamma_{0}}Z_{j}\Big],\quad\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{T}\Big[\tilde{h}-\sum_{j=0}^{2}c_{ij}[\tilde{h},0]e^{\Gamma_{0}}Z_{j}\Big].

and
\mathcal{T}
is the operator built in Lemma
6.1
.
We observe that

\|\mathcal{A}[\phi]\|_{*,m-2,\beta}\leq C\frac{\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}\|\phi\|_{*,m-2,\beta},\quad\|\mathcal{H}\|_{*,m-2,\beta}\leq C\|h\|_{m,\beta,B_{R}}.

So we find that Equation (
9.4
) has a unique solution, that defines a linear operator of
h
, and satisfies

\|\phi\|_{*,m-2,\beta}\ \leq\ C\|h\|_{m,\beta,B_{R}}

The result of the proposition follows by just setting
T_{i}[h]=\phi\big|_{B_{R}}
. The proof is concluded. ∎

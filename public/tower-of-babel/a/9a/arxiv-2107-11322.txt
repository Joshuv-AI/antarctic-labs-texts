Sojourn Ruin of a Two-Dimensional Fractional Bronwian Motion Risk Process
Grigori Jasnovidov

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper derives the asymptotic behavior of

\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\Big(B_{H}(s)-c_{1}s>q_{1}u,B_{H}(s)-c_{2}s>q_{2}u\Big)ds>T_{u}\right\},\quad u\to\infty,

where
B_{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion,

c_{1},c_{2},q_{1},q_{2}>0,\ H\in(0,1),\ T_{u}\geq 0
is a measurable function and

\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
is the indicator function.

Key Words:
fractional Brownian motion; simultaneous ruin probability;
two-dimensional risk processes; sojourn ruin;

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G15; secondary 60G70

1.
Introduction & Preliminaries

Consider the risk model defined by

(1)

\displaystyle R(t)=u+\rho t-X(t),\ \ \ \ t\geq 0,

where
X(t)
is a centered Gaussian risk process
with a.s. continuous sample paths,

\rho>0
is the net profit rate and
u>0
is the initial capital.
This model is relevant to insurance and financial applications, see,
e.g.,
[
13
]
.
A question of numerous investigations
(see
[
12
,
14
,
19
,
15
,
8
,
10
,
7
,
5
,
3
,
6
,
1
,
4
,
24
,
23
,
25
,
22
]
)
is the study of the asymptotics of the classical ruin
probability

(2)

\displaystyle\lambda(u):=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\geq 0:R(t)<0\right\}

as
u\to\infty
under different levels of generality.
It turns out, that only
for
X
being a Brownian motion (later on BM)
\lambda(u)
can be calculated explicitly:
if
X
is a standard BM, then
\lambda(u)=e^{-2\rho u},\ u,\rho>0,

see
[
11
]
. Since it seems impossible to find the exact value
of
\lambda(u)
in other cases,
the approximations of
\lambda(u)
as
u\to\infty

is dealt with.
Some contributions (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
9
]
), extend the
classical ruin problem to the so-called sojourn ruin problem, i.e.,
approximation of the sojourn ruin probability defined by

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}(R(s)<0)ds>T_{u}\right\},

where
T_{u}\geq 0
is a measurable function of
u
.
As in the classical case, only for
X
being a BM
the probability above can
be calculated explicitly, see
[
9
]
:

\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}(B(s)-cs>u)ds>T\right\}=\Big(2(1+c^{2}T)\Psi(c\sqrt{T})-\frac{c\sqrt{2T}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{\frac{-c^{2}T}{2}}\Big)e^{-2cu},\ \ c>0,\ T,u\geq 0,

where
\Psi
is the survival function of a standard
Gaussian random variable,
B
is a standard BM and
\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
is
the indicator function. Motivated by

[
18
]
(see also
[
15
,
16
]
),
we study a generalization of the main problem in
[
18
]

for the sojourn ruin, i.e., we shall study the asymptotics of

\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u):=\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\Big(B_{H}(s)-c_{1}s>q_{1}u,B_{H}(s)-c_{2}s>q_{2}u\Big)ds>T_{u}\right\},\ \ \ \ u\to\infty,

where
B_{H}
is a standard fractional Brownian motion (later on fBM), i.e.,
a Gaussian process with zero expectation and covariance defined by

\displaystyle\cov(B_{H}(s),B_{H}(t))=\frac{|t|^{2H}+|s|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

The ruin probability above is of interest for
reinsurance models, see
[
18
]
and references therein.
By the self-similarity of fBM we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\Big(B_{H}(su)>c_{1}su+q_{1}u,B_{H}(su)>c_{2}su+q_{2}u\Big)d(su)>T_{u}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\Big(u^{H}B_{H}(s)>(c_{1}s+q_{1})u,u^{H}B_{H}(s)>(c_{2}s+q_{2})u\Big)ds>T_{u}/u\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\left(\frac{B_{H}(s)}{\max(c_{1}s+q_{1},c_{2}s+q_{2})}>u^{1-H}\right)ds>T_{u}/u\right\}.

In order to prevent the problem of degenerating
to the one-dimensional sojourn problem
discussed in
[
17
,
9
]

(i.e., to impose the denominator in the line above be nonlinear function)
we assume that

(4)

\displaystyle c_{1}>c_{2},\ \ \ q_{2}>q_{1}.

The variance of
the process two lines above can achieve its unique maxima only
at one of the following points:

(5)

\displaystyle t_{*}=\frac{q_{2}-q_{1}}{c_{1}-c_{2}},\ \ \ t_{1}=\frac{q_{1}H}{(1-H)c_{1}},\ \ \ t_{2}=\frac{q_{2}H}{(1-H)c_{2}}.

From (
4
) it follows that
t_{1}<t_{2}
;
as we shall see later, the order between
t_{1},t_{2}
and
t_{*}

determines the asymptotics of
\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)
as
u\to\infty
.
As mentioned in
[
5
]
,
for the approximation of the one-dimensional
Parisian ruin probability we need to control
the growth of
T_{u}
as
u\to\infty
. As in
[
5
]
,
we impose the following condition:

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}T_{u}u^{1/H-2}=T\in[0,\infty),\ H\in(0,1).

Note that
T_{u}
satisfying (
6
)
may go to
\infty
for
H>1/2
, converges to non-negative limit for
H=1/2

and approaches
0
for
H<1/2
as
u\to\infty
.
We see later on in Proposition
2.2
that the condition
above is necessary and it seems very difficult to derive
the exact asymptotics of
\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)
as
u\to\infty
without it.

The rest of the paper is organized in the following way. In the next
section we present the main results of the paper, in Section

3
we give all proofs, while technical
calculations are deferred to the Appendix.

2.
Main Results

Define for some function
h
and
K\geq 0
the sojourn Piterbarg constant by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{h}_{K}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}\Big(\sqrt{2}B(s)-|s|+h(s)>x\Big)ds>K\right\}e^{x}dx

when the integral above is finite and Berman’s constant by

\mathcal{B}_{2H}(x)=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{1}{S}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{S}\mathbb{I}(\sqrt{2}B_{H}(t)-t^{2H}+z>0)dt>x\right\}e^{-z}dz,\quad x\geq 0.

It is known (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
) that

\mathcal{B}_{2H}(x)\in(0,\infty)
for all
x\geq 0
;
we refer to
[
9
]

and references therein for the properties of relevant Berman’s
constants.
Let for
i=1,2

(7)

\displaystyle\ \ \mathbb{D}_{H}=\frac{c_{1}t_{*}+q_{1}}{t_{*}^{H}},\ K_{H}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2H}}\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{H(1-H)}},\ \mathbb{C}_{H}^{(i)}=\frac{c_{i}^{H}q_{i}^{1-H}}{H^{H}(1-H)^{1-H}},\ D_{i}=\frac{c_{i}^{2}(1-H)^{2-\frac{1}{H}}}{2^{\frac{1}{2H}}H^{2}}.

Now we are ready to give the asymptotics of
\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)
as
u\to\infty
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that (
4
) holds and
T_{u}
satisfies (
6
).

1)If
t_{*}\notin(t_{1},t_{2})
, then as
u\to\infty

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)\sim(\frac{1}{2})^{\mathbb{I}(t_{*}=t_{i})}\times\begin{cases}\Big(2(1+c_{i}^{2}T)\Psi(c_{i}\sqrt{T})-\frac{c_{i}\sqrt{2T}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{\frac{-c_{i}^{2}T}{2}}\Big)e^{-2c_{i}q_{i}u},&H=1/2\\
K_{H}\mathcal{B}_{2H}(TD_{i})(\mathbb{C}_{H}^{(i)}u^{1-H})^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\Psi(\mathbb{C}_{H}^{(i)}u^{1-H}),&H\neq 1/2,\end{cases}

where
i=1
if
t_{*}\leq t_{1}
and
i=2
if
t_{*}\geq t_{2}
.

2) If
t_{*}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
and
\lim\limits_{u\to\infty}T_{u}u^{2-1/H}=0
for

H>1/2
, then as
u\to\infty

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)\sim\Psi(\mathbb{D}_{H}u^{1-H})\times\begin{cases}1,&H>1/2\\
\mathcal{B}_{T^{\prime}}^{d},&H=1/2\\
\mathcal{B}_{2H}(\overline{D}T)Au^{(1-H)(1/H-2)},&H<1/2,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{B}_{T^{\prime}}^{d}\in(0,\infty)
,

(10)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}=T\frac{(c_{1}q_{2}-q_{1}c_{2})^{2}}{2(c_{1}-c_{2})^{2}},\ \ d(s)=s\frac{c_{1}q_{2}+c_{2}q_{1}-2c_{2}q_{2}}{c_{1}q_{2}-q_{1}c_{2}}\mathbb{I}(s<0)+s\frac{2c_{1}q_{1}-c_{1}q_{2}-q_{1}c_{2}}{c_{1}q_{2}-q_{1}c_{2}}\mathbb{I}(s\geq 0)

and

(11)

\displaystyle\!\!A=\Big(|H(c_{1}t_{*}+q_{1})-c_{1}t_{*}|^{-1}+|H(c_{2}t_{*}+q_{2})-c_{2}t_{*}|^{-1}\Big)\frac{t_{*}^{H}\mathbb{D}_{H}^{\frac{1}{H}-1}}{2^{\frac{1}{2H}}},\ \ \ \overline{D}=\frac{(c_{1}t_{*}+q_{1})^{\frac{1}{H}}}{2^{\frac{1}{2H}}t_{*}^{2}}.

Note that if
T=0
, then
the result above reduces to Theorem 3.1 in
[
18
]
.
As already mentioned in the introduction (
6
)
is a necessary condition for the theorem above.
To illustrate situation when it is not satisfied
we consider a ”simple” scenario with
T_{u}
being a positive
constant.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
H<1/2,\ T_{u}=T>0
and
t_{*}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, then

\displaystyle\bar{C}\Psi(\mathbb{D}_{H}u^{1-H})e^{-C_{1,\alpha}u^{2-4H}-C_{2,\alpha}u^{2(1-3H)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{T_{u}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2+o(1))\Psi(\mathbb{D}_{H}u^{1-H})\Psi\left(u^{1-2H}\frac{T^{H}\mathbb{D}_{H}}{2t_{*}^{H}}\right),\quad u\to\infty,

where
\bar{C}\in(0,1)
is a fixed constant that does not depend on
u
and

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{T^{2H}}{2t_{*}^{2H}},\ \ \ \ \ \ C_{i,\alpha}=\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i}\mathbb{D}_{H}^{2},\ \ \ i=1,2.

Note that the lower bound in the proposition above
decays to zero exponentially faster than the upper bound as
u\to\infty
.

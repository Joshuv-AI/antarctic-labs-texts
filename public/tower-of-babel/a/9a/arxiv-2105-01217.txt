Low-energy probes of small CMB amplitude in models of radiative Higgs mechanism
Kiyoharu Kawana

Abstract
The small CMB amplitude A s ≃ 10 − 9 A_{s}\simeq 10^{-9} (or, small temperature fluctuation δ ​ T / T ≃ 10 − 5 \delta T/T\simeq 10^{-5} ) typically requires an unnaturally small effective coupling of an inflaton λ ϕ ∼ 10 − 14 \lambda_{\phi}\sim 10^{-14} . In successful models, there usually is extra suppression of the amplitude, e.g. by large-field inflaton with non-minimal coupling ξ \xi , so that λ ϕ \lambda_{\phi} can be much larger. But λ ϕ \lambda_{\phi} and ξ \xi cannot be ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \sim{\cal O}(1) simultaneously; the naturalness burden is shared between them. We show that the absence of new physics signals at TeV scale may prefer a more natural size of ξ ≲ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 − 100 ) \xi\lesssim{\cal O}(1-100) with λ ϕ ≲ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 4 − 10 − 8 ) \lambda_{\phi}\lesssim{\cal O}(10^{-4}-10^{-8}) , constraining larger ξ \xi with larger λ ϕ \lambda_{\phi} more strongly. This intriguing connection between low- and high-energy physics is made in the scenarios with U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} where inflaton’s renormalization running also induces Coleman-Weinberg mechanism for the electroweak symmetry breaking. We particularly work out the prospects of LHC 13 and 100 TeV p ​ p pp colliders for probing the parameter space of the small CMB amplitude.

5
Summary

We have shown that the parameter space for the small CMB amplitude can be probed by collider experiments. In the inflation model with non-minimal coupling
\xi
, the absence of collider signals of
Z^{\prime}
at LHC 13 and 100 TeV
pp
will prefer a smaller and more natural size of
\xi\lesssim 1-100
with
\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})\lesssim 10^{-4}-10^{-8}
even though
\xi
could equally well take a much larger and less natural value to explain the CMB amplitude. Another possibility of
\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})\sim{\cal O}(1)
with
\xi\sim 10^{5}\gg 1
will be strongly constrained. A similar conclusion for an
\alpha
attractor model is obtained, albeit more weakly.

This probe was possible in models where an inflaton
\phi
also induces EWSB via perturbative quantum corrections (CW mechanism). The restrictions on the
\phi
potential from well-measured high-energy inflation and low-energy electroweak physics lead to intimate correlations, in particular between low-energy
M_{X}
(collider observables) and high-energy
\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})
(CMB amplitude
A_{s}\propto\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})
). More crucially, there exists an upper range of
M_{X}
prediction for each value of
\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})
, so that definite collider constraints on
\lambda_{\phi}(M_{\rm Pl})
could be derived.

This work not only proves an interesting possibility of probing the inflation sector with low-energy experiments, but may also relate the absence of new physics signals to the physics at a disparate energy scale. Although this connection does not explain why our universe is realized with such small CMB amplitude, the fact that such particular realization at high-energy scale also has consequences in low-energy physics is intriguing enough, worthwhile to be pursued in a more varieties of models.

On complex Lie algebroids with constant real rank
Dan Aguero

Abstract
. We associate a real distribution to any complex Lie algebroid that we call distribution of real elements and a new invariant that we call real rank , given by the pointwise rank of this distribution. When the real rank is constant we obtain a real Lie algebroid inside the original complex Lie algebroid. Under another regularity condition we associate a complex Lie subalgebroid that we call the minimum complex subalgebroid . We also provide a local splitting for complex Lie algebroids with constant real rank. In the last part, we introduce the complex matched pair of skew-algebroids; these pairs produce complex Lie algebroid structures on the complexification of a vector bundle. We use this operation to characterize all the complex Lie algebroid structures on the complexification of real vector bundles.



1.
Introduction

Lie algebroids were introduced by Pradines
[
17
]
, and nowadays have become a useful tool for many areas of differential geometry and mathematical physics. This influenced their study and made a fast development of the area. However, the study of Lie algebroids can be divided into real, holomorphic, algebraic and complex Lie algebroids, being real Lie algebroids the most studied. Complex Lie algebroids gained some notoriety after the introduction of generalized complex structures
[
11
,
10
]
and now we know that many geometrical structures are particular examples of complex Lie algebroids, for example, generalized complex structures, complex Dirac structures
[
2
]
, exceptional complex structures
[
19
]
,
B_{n}
-generalized complex structures
[
18
]
, etc.

The theory of real and holomorphic Lie algebroids is currently more developed than the theory of complex Lie algebroids. It is known their local structure, the obstructions for its integrability (Lie III)
[
6
,
14
]
, etc. Despite the general theory of generalized complex structures and complex Dirac structures being known
[
3
,
2
]
, it is still missing a general description of complex Lie algebroids including its local description. About the integrability of complex Lie algebroids there is not much known, some ideas can be found in
[
21
]
.

In this article, we study complex Lie algebroids, by introducing a real distribution that we call
distribution of real elements
and an invariant that we call the
real rank
which is just the rank of the aforementioned distribution. When the real rank is constant the distribution of real element is a real Lie algebroid and so we can associate a real foliation to the complex Lie algebroid. The Lie algebroid of real elements is an approximation to the Poisson and Dirac structures associated to generalized complex and complex Dirac structures, respectively. We associate two other invariants: the type and the class; and introduce the minimum complex Lie subalgebroids and the family of minimal complex Lie algebroids. Our main examples are involutives structures, complexifications and complex Poisson bivectors. We provide a local splitting theorem for complex Lie algebroids with constant real rank. We also study complex Lie algebroid structures on complexifications of real vector bundles, we introduce the complex matched pairs and an operation associated with them that we call complex sum. We prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between complex Lie algebroid structures on complexifications and complex matched pairs.

The content of this article is organized in the following way. In Section
2
, we recall some properties of the symmetries of complex vector bundles and complex anchored vector bundles. Since the author has not found the proofs of some of the statements, they are providen. After that we focus on complex anchored vector bundles (CAVB). We introduce the
distribution of real elements
and its rank which we call
real rank
. When the distribution of real elements has constant rank it is a real anchored vector bundle, in fact, it is involutive in case the CAVB is involutive. In Section
3
, we study complex Lie algebroids (CLA), under the assumption of constant real rank. We introduce
the Lie algebroids of real elements
denoted by
(A^{\real},[\cdot,\cdot]^{\real},\rho^{\real})
and we study some of its properties. This real Lie algebroid allows us to associate a real foliation to a CLA with constant real rank. Since we are dealing with complex distributions, we can have many types of regularity rather than the usual regularity (the image of the anchor map is a regular distribution), so we introduce strongly regular CLA. We also study the distribution given by
A_{\min}=A^{\real}+iA^{\real}
and whenever
A_{\min}
is regular it is automatically a CLA, which we call
the minimum complex Lie subalgebroid
. We introduce a new class of CLA that we call
minimal CLA
, which are CLAs that coincide with their minimum complex subalgebroid. We also introduce an
\mathbb{N}
-valued function called the
class
that takes a point and returns the dimension of the leaf passing through this specific point. CLAs of class
0
at a given point play an important role in the splitting theorem for CLAS. At the end of the section, we study the conjugate of a CLA.

In Section
4
, we study the local structure of CAVB and CLA with constant real rank using the techniques of
[
5
]
. We provide a proof of the splitting theorem for involutive CAVB with constant real rank and we end the section with the local description of CLA with constant real rank, obtaining the following:

Theorem
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
be a CLA,
m\in M
and
\Delta
the space of real elements of
\rho(A)
. Assume that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
has constant real rank in a neighborhood of
m
. Consider a submanifold
N\xhookrightarrow{\iota}M
such that
\Delta_{m}\oplus T_{m}N=T_{m}M
and denote by
P=\Delta_{m}
. Then, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
m
such that
A|_{U}
is isomorphic to
A^{\prime}\times T_{{\mathbb{C}}}P
, where
(A^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot]^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is a CLA of class
0
at
m
.

The property of
A^{\prime}
having class
0
at
m
already appears in the local description of generalized complex structures and complex Dirac structures.

As a main application we retrieve the Nirenberg-Frobenius Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 1]
.

In Section
5
, we introduce the
complex matched pairs of skew-algebroids
. Given two skew-algebroids
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\rho_{1})
and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\rho_{2})
over the same vector bundle
A\to M
, we say that they form a complex matched pair if the bracket are compatible and the Jacobiator of the brackets is the same. We observe that these pairs produce a complex Lie algebroid structure on the complexification of a real vector bundle which we call
complex sum
. As an application, we prove the following characterization:

Theorem
.

Let
A
be a real vector bundle over
M
. Any structure of CLA on
A_{{\mathbb{C}}}
comes from a complex sum of a complex matched pair of skew-algebroids over
A
.

We also study the CLA associated to complex vector fields and describe its distribution of real elements. In this case, we see that the assumption of constant real rank is inefficient for the local description of general complex vector fields. However, allowing a “singular associated real Lie algebroid” could provide a solution to this problem, this is the subject of future work. In the end of the section, we study the CLA associated to a complex Poisson bivector from the perspective of the complex sum.
In the end of this article, we present two appendices: Appendix
A
, where we recall some results and properties of real anchored vector bundles, vertical lifts and real Lie algebroids, and Appendix
B
, where we recall some results and properties about Euler-like vector fields and normal vector bundles.

Notation and convention

In this article, we only work with smooth manifolds. Given a vector bundle
A
over a smooth manifold
M
, a
distribution
is an assignment
m\in M\mapsto R_{m}\subseteq A_{m}
, where
R_{m}
is a vector subspace of the fiber
A_{m}
; when any
v\in R_{m}
can be extended to a local section of
A
taking values in
R
, we say that the distribution is
smooth
. Each distribution has associated a
\mathbb{N}
-valued function given by the assignment
\rk:m\in M\mapsto\dim R_{m}
, which is called
rank
. A distribution is said to be
regular
if it is a vector bundle.

Given a distribution
E\subset A_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
A_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is the complexification of a real vector bundle
A\to M
, we denote the space real elements of
E
by
\re E:=E\cap A
and call them the
real part of
E
. Given a smooth map
\varphi:M\to N
, we denote the complexification of
T\varphi
by
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi
.

Throughout this article, a real anchored vector bundle is referred to by RAVB and a real Lie algebroid by RLA. In Appendix
A
we recall some properties of RAVBs and RLAs.

Let
N
be a submanifold of
M
, the normal bundle of
N
is denoted by

\nu(M,N)=TM|_{N}/TN,

when the context is clear we denote it by
\nu_{N}
. In Appendix
B
we recall some properties of normal vector bundles.

2.
Complex anchored vector bundles

2.1.
Complex anchored vector bundles

A complex vector bundle
E\to M
has associated the canonical bundle map
j_{E}\in\mathrm{Diff}(E)
given by
j_{E}(e)=i\cdot e
, the multiplication by the imaginary
i
. The
group of (complex) automorphisms of
E
is:

\Aut(E)=\{(\Phi,\varphi)\in\Aut(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})\>\>|\>\>\Phi\circ j_{E}=j_{E}\circ\Phi\>\>\text{and}\>\>\Phi\>\>\text{covers}\>\>\varphi\in Diff(M)\},

where
E_{{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the realification of
E
and
\Aut(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})
is the automorphism group of
E_{{\mathbb{R}}}
as a real vector bundle.
The map
j_{E}
acts on
\Aut(E)
in the obvious way. Consider a 1-parameter subgroup
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
of
\Aut(E)
. Since
\Phi_{t}\in\Aut(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})
and
\Phi_{t}\circ j_{E}=j_{E}\circ\Phi_{t}
, we have that
\hat{X}=\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}|_{t=0}\in\mathfrak{aut}(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})
satisfies that
(j_{E})_{*}\hat{X}=\hat{X}
. So, the
Lie algebra of (complex) infinitesimal automorphism of
E
is given by:

\mathfrak{aut}(E)=\{\hat{X}\in\mathfrak{aut}(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})\>\>|\>\>(j_{E})_{*}\hat{X}=\hat{X}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{aut}(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})\subseteq\mathfrak{X}(E).

Another characterization of
\mathfrak{aut}(E)
is the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}(E)
is given by the pairs
(L,X)
such that
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
L:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear derivation with respect to
X
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{X}\in\mathfrak{aut}(E)\subseteq\mathfrak{aut}(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})
, there is associated a derivation given by the pair
(L,X)
, where
L:\Gamma(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})\to\Gamma(E_{{\mathbb{R}}})
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear and
X=\pr_{*}\widetilde{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, defined as
L(\sigma)^{\uparrow}=[\sigma^{\uparrow},\widetilde{X}]
,
\forall\sigma\in\Gamma(E)
, see Appendix. Note that
L
is
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear if and only if
L(i\sigma)=iL(\sigma)
. Note that

L(i\sigma)^{\uparrow}=[(i\sigma)^{\uparrow},\widetilde{X}]=[(j_{E})_{*}\sigma^{\uparrow},\widetilde{X}]=[(j_{E})_{*}\sigma^{\uparrow},(j_{E})_{*}\widetilde{X}]=(j_{E})_{*}[\sigma^{\uparrow},\widetilde{X}]=(j_{E})_{*}(D(\sigma)^{\uparrow})=(iL(\sigma))^{\uparrow}.

And by injectivity of the ”
\uparrow
”-map, we have that
L(i\sigma)=iL(\sigma)
and the lemma holds.
∎

Given a CAVB
(A,\rho)
, the anchor map decomposes
\rho=\rho_{1}+i\rho_{2}
. So we obtain two real anchored vector bundles

and

\rho_{1}\circ j_{A}=-\rho_{2}.

Proposition 2.3

The map

The
group of automorphism of a CAVB

(A,\rho)
is given by

\Aut(A,\rho)=\{(\Phi,\varphi)\in\Aut(A)\>\>|\>\>\rho\circ\Phi=T_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi\circ\rho\}.

Note that
\rho\circ\Phi=T_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi\circ\rho

\rho_{1}\circ\Phi=T\varphi\circ\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}\circ\Phi=T\varphi\circ\rho_{2}

The

For a CAVB

, we have that

(1)

\mathfrak{aut}(A,\rho)=\mathfrak{aut}(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},\rho_{1})\cap\mathfrak{aut}(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},\rho_{2})=\{\widetilde{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)\>\>|\>\>\widetilde{X}\sim_{\rho}X_{T_{{\mathbb{C}}}},\>\>\text{where}\>\widetilde{X}\sim_{\pr}X\},

, and it is the vector field defined by the one-parameter subgroup

, here

In terms of the differential operators: a pair

if and only if
\rho(L(\tau))=[X,\rho(\tau)]

\rho_{i}(L(\tau))=[X,\rho_{i}(\tau)],\>\>\text{for $i=1,2$}.

Definition 2.4
.

A real or complex anchored vector bundle
(A,\rho)
is
involutive
if
\rho(\Gamma(A))
is a Lie subalgebra of
\Gamma(TM)
or
\Gamma(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)

Consider the decomposition of the anchor map
\rho
in its real and imaginary parts
\rho=\rho_{1}+i\rho_{2}

is not directly related to the involutivity of

and

2.2.
Distributions associated to CAVB

A CAVB
(A,\rho)
has associated two real distributions in
TM
:

\Delta=\re(\rho(A)\cap\overline{\rho(A)})\>\>\text{and}\>\>D=\re(\rho(A)+\overline{\rho(A)}).

Note that
D
is a smooth distribution, while
\Delta
is not necessarily smooth. However, we have the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

If
D
is a regular distribution, then
\Delta
is a smooth distribution.

Proof.

Consider the map
\rho_{2}:A\to TM
, which is a (real) bundle map. Note that the image of
\rho_{2}
is
D
. Hence
\ker\rho_{2}
is a vector bundle and
\Delta=\rho(\ker\rho_{2})
is smooth.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Given a CAVB
(A,\rho)
we associate the distribution:

A^{\real}=\{e\in A\>\>|\>\>\rho_{2}(e)=0\},

which we called
distribution of real elements of
(A,\rho)
. In general

is only a distribution in
A
(not even smooth), although, when
A^{\real}
is regular,
(A^{\real},\rho^{\real})
is a RAVB, where
\rho^{\real}=\rho_{1}|_{A^{\real}}
. The
\mathbb{N}
-valued function

\text{real-rank A}:m\in M\mapsto\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}A^{\real}|_{m}

is called

A
.

Constant real rank is equivalent to
A^{\real}
being a vector bundle and by the proof of Lemma
2.5
, it is equivalent to the regularity of
D
as a distribution. Indeed, we can see that

(3)

{\text{real-rank A}}+\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}D=\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A_{{\mathbb{R}}}.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assuming that
(A,\rho)
has constant real rank. If
(L,X)\in\mathfrak{aut}(A,\rho)
, then
L(\Gamma(A^{\real}))\subseteq\Gamma(A^{\real})
and
(L|_{A^{\real}},X)\in\mathfrak{aut}(A^{\real},\rho^{\real})
.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

If
(A,\rho)
is an involutive CAVB with constant real rank, then
(A^{\real},\rho^{\real})
is involutive.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma(A^{\real})
, equivalently,
\rho(\alpha)
and
\rho(\beta)
are real, and so
[\rho(\alpha),\rho(\beta)]
. By involutivity of

, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(A)
such that
[\rho(\alpha),\rho(\beta)]=\rho(\gamma)
. So
\rho(\gamma)
is real and consequently
\gamma\in\Gamma(A^{\real})
.
∎

The image of the anchor map of an involutive RAVB is integrable, see for example

3.
Complex Lie algebroids

Definition 3.1
.

A
complex Lie algebroid (CLA)
over a manifold
M
is a complex anchored vector bundle
(A,\rho)
over
M
together with a Lie bracket on
\Gamma(A)
satisfying the Jacobi identity

[\cdot,\cdot]:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\to\Gamma(A)

and with a
{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle map
\rho:A\to T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
, that we call the anchor map, preserving brackets and satisfying the Leibniz identity

[\alpha,f\beta]=f[\alpha,\beta]+\rho(\alpha)(f)\beta,

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma(A)
and for any function
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
. A
complex skew-algebroid
is when the Jacobi identity is not necessarily satisfied, and an
almost complex Lie algebroid (almost CLA)
is a complex skew-algebroid where the anchor preserves the brackets.

Morphisms between CLAs are defined in the exactly same way as RLAs and so their automorphisms.

Examples 3.2
.

Some examples of CLA are the following:

(1)

An
involutive structure

E
is an involutive vector subbundle of
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
, see
[
20
]
. The triple
(E,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the bracket of
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
and
\rho
is the inclusion map of
E
in
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
, defines a CLA. In particular: complex structures, transverse holomorphic structures and CR structures define CLAs.

(2)

Complex Dirac structures, generalized complex structures,
B_{n}
-generalized complex structures and exceptional generalized complex structures.

(3)

A holomorphic Lie algebroid
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
has associated two CLA,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{1,0},\rho_{1,0})
and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{0,1},\rho_{0,1})
defined in
[
13
, Section 3.4]
.

(4)

Bundles of complex Lie algebras with vanishing anchor map.

(5)

The complexification
(A_{{\mathbb{C}}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathbb{C}}},\rho_{{\mathbb{C}}})
of a RLA
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
defines a CLA.

(6)

Given a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
we call a Lie algebra homomorphism

\mathcal{X}:\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)
a
complex infinitesimal action
. The vector bundle
L(\mathfrak{g})=M\times\mathfrak{g}
is a CLA with anchor map
\mathcal{X}
and bracket

[u,v](m)=[u(m),v(m)]+L_{\mathcal{X}(u)}v-L_{\mathcal{X}(v)}u,

where
u,v
and
[u,v]
are sections of
L(\mathfrak{g})
view as maps from
M
to
\mathfrak{g}
.

(7)

A complex Poisson bivector is a bivector
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)
satisfying
[\pi,\pi]=0
. The triple
(T^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\pi},\pi)
is a CLA, where the the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\pi}
is defined as:

(5)

[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}=L_{\pi(\alpha)}\beta-L_{\pi(\beta)}\alpha-d\pi(\alpha,\beta)

and anchor
\pi:T^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}}M\to T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. We denote this complex Lie algebroid by
(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}M)_{\pi}
.

(8)

A complex vector fields
Z=X_{1}+iX_{2}\in\Gamma(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)
defines a structure of CLA over the bundle
M\times{\mathbb{C}}
that we denote by
A_{Z}
. The anchor map is given by
\rho:\Gamma(M\times{\mathbb{C}})=C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\to T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
,
\rho(f)=fZ
and the bracket by

[f,g]=fL_{Z}(g)-gL_{Z}(f).

3.1.
Associated RLA

On one hand, RLAs have associated a distribution given by the image of the anchor map. On the other hand, the image of the anchor map of a CLA is a complex distribution of
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. To bypass this issue, we associate two real distributions:

\Delta=\re(\rho(A)\cap\overline{\rho(A)})\>\>\text{and}\>\>D=\re(\rho(A)+\overline{\rho(A)}).

Note that
D
is a smooth distribution, while
\Delta
is not smooth in general, see Example 6.1 of
[
2
]
.

Given a CLA
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
, decompose
\rho=\rho_{1}+i\rho_{2}
. Contrary to what happens with CAVB, neither
\mathcal{A}_{1}=(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},[\cdot,\cdot],\rho_{1})
nor
\mathcal{A}_{2}=(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},[\cdot,\cdot],\rho_{2})
necessarily define RLAs, since
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
do not preserve the bracket (and so do not satisfy the Leibniz identity).

Actually, the natural candidate for
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is
(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},i[\cdot,\cdot],-\rho_{2})
. The “error” for
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
being RLAs are:

(6)

[a,fb]_{1}-(f[a,b]_{1}+(\rho_{1}(a)f)b)=i(\rho_{2}(a)f)b,

(7)

[a,fb]_{2}-(f[a,b]_{2}-(\rho_{2}(a)f)b)=i(\rho_{1}(a)f)b,

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}=[\cdot,\cdot]
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}=i[\cdot,\cdot]
.

An immediate consequence of equations (
6
) and (
7
) is the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
be a CLA. If either
\mathcal{A}_{1}=(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},[\cdot,\cdot],\rho_{1})
or
\mathcal{A}_{2}=(A_{{\mathbb{R}}},i[\cdot,\cdot],-\rho_{2})
are RLAs, then
\rho=0
.

In general
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
satisfy the Jacobi identity but they are not RLAs, since there is an “error term” in the Leibniz identity controlled by the anchor of the other algebroid.

We avoid these error terms by working with
\ker\rho_{1}
and
\ker\rho_{2}
. Also note that the multiplication by
i
is a kind of “isomorphism” from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

In the same way as with CAVB, we consider the distribution

(8)

A^{\real}=\ker\rho_{2}=\rho^{-1}(\Delta)

and the real rank of a CLA as

\text{real-rank}\>A:m\in M\mapsto\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}A^{\real}|_{m}.

We also have the distribution given by
\ker\rho_{1}
. However, note that

\ker\rho_{1}=j_{A}(A^{\real})=\rho^{-1}(i\Delta),

where
j_{A}
is the multiplication by
i
. Moreover,

(9)

[A^{\real},A^{\real}]\subseteq A^{\real},\>\>\>\>[A^{\real},j_{A}(A^{\real})]\subseteq j_{A}(A^{\real})\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>[j_{A}(A^{\real}),j_{A}(A^{\real})]\subseteq A^{\real}.

Consider

\rho^{\real}=\rho_{1}|_{A^{\real}}\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\rho^{\text{im}}=-\rho_{2}|_{j_{A}(A^{\real})}.

As a consequence, we get the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
is a CLA with constant real rank, then
(A^{\real},[\cdot,\cdot]_{|_{A^{\real}}},\rho^{\real})
and

(j_{A}(A^{\real}),j_{A}[\cdot,\cdot]_{|_{j_{A}(A^{\real})}},\rho^{\text{im}})
are isomorphic RLAs, with the isomorphism given by the restriction of
j_{A}
. Moreover,
\rho^{\real}(A^{\real})=\rho^{\text{im}}(j_{A}(A^{\real}))=\Delta
.

Definition 3.5
.

When
A^{\real}
is a RLA, we shall call it as the
Lie algebroid of real elements
.

One of the importance of the Lie algebroid of real elements is that we can associate a foliation to any CLA with constant real index.

Corollary 3.6
.

If a CLA
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
has constant real index, then the set of real elements of the image of
\rho
,
\re\rho(A)
coincides with the image of
\rho(A^{\real})
. Consequently,
\re\rho(A)
is an integrable distribution.

CLAs have associated many distributions, so we should have many types of regularity with respect to these distributions.

Definition 3.7
.

A CLA
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
is
regular
if
\rho(A)
is a regular distribution and we say that it is
strongly regular
if
D
and
\Delta
are regular distributions.

Note that strongly regular CLAs are regular CLAs with constant real rank and vice-versa.

Remark 3.8
.

Another natural invariant associated to a CLA is the order which is a
\mathbb{N}
-function defined as follows

\order A:m\in M\mapsto\cork_{{\mathbb{R}}}D|_{m}.

The order was previously introduced in
[
2
]
for complex Dirac structures and in
[
19
]
, as “class”, for exceptional complex structures.
By equation (
3
), we have that

(10)

\order A|_{m}=\rerk A|_{m}+\dim M-\rk A_{{\mathbb{R}}},

and so constant order is equivalent to constant real rank.

In general, the distribution
D
is not integrable, even on strongly regular CLAs. Non-Levi-flat CR structures are examples of strongly regular CLAs where the distribution
D
is not integrable.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
be a CLA (or an RLA) and a point
m\in M

isotropy Lie algebra of

at

is the complex (or real) Lie algebra

When

is regular, we obtain a bundle of complex (or real) Lie algebras

Note that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}

\displaystyle=A^{\real}\cap j_{A}(A^{\real})\>\>\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(A^{\real})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{g}(j_{A}(A^{\real}))=\mathfrak{g}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}.

Proposition 3.14
.

The following identity holds:

(15)

\type A|_{m}=\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A-\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A_{\min}|_{m}

Proof.

By using the kernel and image theorem on
\rho
and
\rho_{\min}
, we have that

\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\rho(A)+\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{g}(A)=\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A\>\>\>\>{\text{and}}

\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{C}}}+\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{g}(A)=\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A_{\min}.

Subtracting the previous expression, we obtain the desired identity.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

A CLA has type
0
if and only if it is minimal.

So the type measures how close is a CLA to being minimal. A strongly regular CLA has constant type, and
\rho(A)
defines a transverse CR structure on

, (see
[

).

We introduce the following invariant:

Definition 3.16
.

The

of a CLA
A
at the point

is given by

\class A|_{m}=\rk\Delta|_{m}.

We say that a CLA is of

if it has constant class

.

Proposition 3.17
.

We have the following identities:

\class A|_{m}=\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A_{\min}|_{m}-\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{g}(A)|_{m}=\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A^{\real}|_{m}-\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathfrak{g}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}|_{m}.

While the type measures how close is a CLA to being minimal, the class measures how close is to being “real-degenerate”, i.e.
A^{\real}=\mathfrak{g}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Note that the class is lower bounded by
0
but it is not upper bounded, we shall see in Examples

that complexifications provide examples of CLAs with arbitrarily high class.
A CLA has class
0
at a point

if and only if
\Delta|_{m}=0
. Holomorphic Lie algebroid and CR structures are examples of CLA of class
0
as it will be seen. It is easy to see that the only constant real rank CLAs with type

and class

are the bundles of complex Lie algebras.

The real rank, the type and the class are related by the following.

Proposition 3.18
.

We have the following identity:

2\type+\class+\rerk=\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A_{{\mathbb{R}}}.

Proof.

Using the image and kernel theorem on
\rho
and
\rho_{2}
we have the following:

\displaystyle 2\type

\displaystyle=2(\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}D-\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\rho(A))

\displaystyle=2(\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A_{{\mathbb{R}}}-\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A^{\real})-2(\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}A-\rk_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{g}(A))

\displaystyle=\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A_{{\mathbb{R}}}-2\rerk+\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathfrak{g}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}

\displaystyle=\rk_{{\mathbb{R}}}A_{{\mathbb{R}}}-\class-\rerk

∎

\psi((A_{1})^{\real})\subseteq(A_{2})^{\real}\>\>\text{and}\>\>\psi((A_{1})_{\min})\subseteq(A_{2})_{\min}.

In particular,
\psi|_{(A_{1})^{\real}}
and
\psi|_{(A_{1})_{\min}}
are Lie algebroid morphisms in both cases. Moreover, the real rank and the class are invariants by Lie algebroid automorphisms.

Remark 3.22
.

Making a parallel with complex Dirac structures, given a complex Dirac structure
L\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
with constant real index we associate two Lie algebroids: the RLA
K=\re(L\cap\overline{L})
and the CLA

. Note that we are strongly using the fact that

has as environment
\mathbb{T}_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. Although, in general, a CLA

does not have an environment, the image of the anchor map

has one:

. We pass the information of the real elements of

via the inverse image of the anchor map. So instead of

we consider

and instead of

we consider

3.3.
Conjugate CLA

We recall that given a complex vector bundle
A
, its conjugate
\overline{A}
is the vector bundle defined by the following rule of multiplication by scalars

z\cdot_{\overline{A}}e=\overline{z}\cdot_{A}e,

where
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
e\in A
, see
[
15
]
.

Let
(A,\rho,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a CLA. The map
\overline{\rho}
became
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear when it is defined on
\overline{A}
. The bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\Gamma(\overline{A})\times\Gamma(\overline{A})\to\Gamma(\overline{A})
is well-defined and remains
{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear. So we have the following.

Proposition 3.23
.

The triple
(\overline{A},\overline{\rho},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a CLA.

Proof.

Since the bracket remains unchanged, the Jacobi identity is satisfied. It miss to prove the Leibniz identity:

\displaystyle[e_{1},f\cdot_{\overline{A}}e_{2}]

\displaystyle=[e_{1},\overline{f}\cdot_{A}e_{2}]=f[e_{1},e_{2}]+(\rho(e_{1})\overline{f})\cdot_{A}e_{2}

\displaystyle=f[e_{1},e_{2}]+\overline{\rho(e_{1})\overline{f}}\cdot_{\overline{A}}e_{2}=f[e_{1},e_{2}]+(\overline{\rho(e_{1})}f)\cdot_{\overline{A}}e_{2}.

∎

We call the triple
(\overline{A},\overline{\rho},[\cdot,\cdot])
as
conjugate complex Lie algebroid
.

The complex sum is an almost CLA (the anchor map preserves the brackets) if and only if

Jac[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}=Jac[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}
.

Proof.

(1) and (2): It is enough to prove it for real elements.

(3): The commutativity of the bracket and the equality of the Jacobiator of

and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}
are equivalent to the Jacobi identity for the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
.

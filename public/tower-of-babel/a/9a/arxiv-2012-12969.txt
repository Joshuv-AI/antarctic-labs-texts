Complete analytical solution to the quantum Yukawa potential
M. Napsuciale (1) , S. Rodríguez (2)

Abstract
We present a complete analytical solution to the quantum problem of a particle in the Yukawa potential, using supersymmetry and a systematic expansion of the corresponding super-potentials. Results for the critical screening of the ground state improve in several figures existing results based on both numerical solutions and approximation methods. Our calculation to order ( a 0 / D ) 2 (a_{0}/D)^{2} for the squared ground state wavefunction at the origin, which enter in darkonium transitions, yields a correction of π 4 / 216 \pi^{4}/216 to results based on variational techniques.

of this potential and on a perturbative expansion of the superpotentials.

The radial Schrodinger equation for the Yukawa potential

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}(r^{2}\frac{d}{dr})-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right)+V(r)\right]R(r)=E~R(r),

(2)

can be reduced to

H_{l}u_{l}=\left[-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+v_{l}(x)\right]u_{l}(x)=\epsilon_{l}u_{l},

(3)

with the effective potential

v_{l}(x)=\frac{l(l+1)}{x^{2}}-\frac{2}{x}e^{-\delta x},

(4)

where
x=r/a_{0}
,
R(r)=u(x)/x
and
\delta=a_{0}/D
, with the Bohr radius,
a_{0}=\frac{\hbar}{\mu c\alpha}
.
The energy levels are given by

E_{l}=\frac{1}{2}\mu c^{2}\alpha^{2}\epsilon_{l},

(5)

where
\mu
is the reduced mass of the system. We factorize the Yukawa Hamiltonian as

H_{l}=a_{l}a^{\dagger}_{l}+C(l,\delta),

(6)

where

a_{l}=-\frac{d}{dx}+W_{l}(x),\qquad a^{\dagger}_{l}=\frac{d}{dx}+W_{l}(x).

(7)

The superpotential
W_{l}
must satisfy

W^{2}_{l}(x,\delta)-W^{\prime}_{l}(x,\delta)+C(l,\delta)=\frac{l(l+1)}{x^{2}}-\frac{2}{x}e^{-\delta x},

(8)

where
W^{\prime}_{l}\equiv\frac{dW_{l}}{dx}
.
If we succeed in solving the Ricatti equation (
8
) we also generate a factorization for the partner Hamiltonian defined as

\tilde{H}_{l}=a^{\dagger}_{l}a_{l}+C(l,\delta)=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\tilde{v}_{l}(x),

(9)

where

\tilde{v}_{l}(x)=W^{2}_{l}(x)+W^{\prime}_{l}(x)+C(l,\delta)=v_{l}(x)+2W^{\prime}_{l}(x).

(10)

The two-component Hamiltonian

H=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\dagger}_{l}a_{l}&0\\
0&a_{l}a^{\dagger}_{l}\end{pmatrix},

(11)

can be written in terms of the charges

\displaystyle Q_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ia_{l}\\
ia^{\dagger}_{l}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{l}\\
a^{\dagger}_{l}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

These operators satisfy the
N=2
supersymmetry algebra
Witten:1981nf
COOPER1983262

\{Q_{i},Q_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}H,\qquad[Q_{i},H]=0.

(13)

Explicitly, the Hamiltonian is given by

H=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+U_{+}(x,l)&0\\
0&-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+U_{-}(x,l)\end{pmatrix},

(14)

with the associated potentials

U_{\pm}(x,l)=W^{2}_{l}(x)\pm W^{\prime}_{l}(x).

(15)

Expanding the effective potential in powers of
\delta
we get

\displaystyle W^{2}_{l}(x,\delta)-W^{\prime}_{l}(x,\delta)

\displaystyle+C(l,\delta)=\frac{l(l+1)}{x^{2}}-\frac{2}{x}

\displaystyle+2\delta-\delta^{2}x+\frac{1}{3}\delta^{3}x^{2}+....

(16)

The expansion on the right hand side (r.h.s.) of this equation is also an expansion in powers of
x
. The
\delta
-independent
terms correspond to the Coulomb potential. The
{\cal O}(\delta^{k})
term on the r.h.s. is
{\cal O}(x^{k-1})
. Working to

{\cal O}(\delta^{k})
, we find polynomial solution in
x
for
W_{l}(x,\delta)
, with the advantage
that powers of
\delta
and
x
are correlated. The general solution can be written as

\displaystyle W_{l}(x,\delta)

\displaystyle=w_{c}(x,l)+a_{1}\delta+(a_{2}\delta^{2}+a_{3}\delta^{3}+a_{4}\delta^{4}...)x

\displaystyle+(b_{3}\delta^{3}+b_{4}\delta^{4}+b_{5}\delta^{5}+...)x^{2}

\displaystyle+(c_{4}\delta^{4}+c_{5}\delta^{5}+c_{6}\delta^{6}+...)x^{3}+...,

(17)

\displaystyle C(l,\delta)

\displaystyle=c(l)+y_{1}(l)\delta+y_{2}(l)\delta^{2}+y_{3}(l)\delta^{3}+...

(18)

Here,
w_{c}(x,l)
is the
\delta
-independent part which corresponds to the Coulomb problem. The coefficients required
to a given order in
\delta
, can be fixed matching powers of
x
on both sides of this equation.

We find that to
{\cal O}(\delta^{2})
, the Yukawa problem is factorizable in the sense of Ref.
Infeld:1951mw
. A family of
supersymmetric Hamiltonians
\{H^{0}(l)\equiv H_{l},H^{1}_{l},H^{2}_{l}..,H^{r}_{l}\}
with ”shape invariant” potentials as described
in
Gendenshtein:1984vs
can be built and the spectrum can be straightforwardly obtained as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{r,l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(l+r+1)^{2}}+2\delta

\displaystyle-[(l+1)(l+\frac{3}{2})+3r(r+2(l+1))]\delta^{2},

(19)

which when written in terms of the principal quantum number
n=l+r+1
reads

\epsilon_{n,l}=-\frac{1}{n^{2}}+2\delta-\frac{1}{2}[3n^{2}-l(l+1)]\delta^{2}.

(20)

The angular momentum quantum number takes the values
l=n-1,n-2,...,1,0
.
The eigenstate
u_{n,n-1}(x)
satisfies
a^{\dagger}_{n-1}u_{n,n-1}=0
, a condition that can be used to obtain its explicit form as

\displaystyle u_{n,n-1}(x,\delta)

\displaystyle=N_{n,n-1}e^{-\int W_{n-1}(x,\delta)dx}

\displaystyle=N_{n,n-1}x^{n}e^{-\frac{x}{n}+\frac{1}{4}n\delta^{2}x^{2}}.

(21)

States with lower values of
l
can be obtained iteratively with the aid of the operator

u_{n,n-s}(x)=N_{n,n-s}a_{n-s}u_{n,n-s+1},

where

, and

\delta

To
{\cal O}(\delta^{3})
and beyond we loose shape invariance. However, supersymmetry is always present and can be used to solve the problem.
First, we expect the condition
a^{\dagger}_{n-1}u_{n,n-1}=0

\displaystyle u_{n,n-1}

\displaystyle(x,\delta)=N_{n,n-1}e^{-\int W_{n-1}(x,\delta)dx}

\displaystyle=N_{n,n-1}x^{n}e^{-\frac{x}{n}}e^{[\frac{n}{2}\delta^{2}-\frac{n}{6}(n+1)\delta^{3}]\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\frac{n}{6}\delta^{3}\frac{x^{3}}{3}}.

Using this function in Eq. (

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n,n-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n^{2}}+2\delta-n(n+\frac{1}{2})\delta^{2}

Another important parameter of the quantum Yukawa potential is the value of the wavefunction at the origin. It appears in the
phenomenology of darkonium (non-relativistic bound states of dark matter-dark matter interacting through a Yukawa

Performing the expansion we find that, at the origin, its square has the value

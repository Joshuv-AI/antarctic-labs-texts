Proof of two conjectures on Askey-Wilson polynomials
K. Castillo, D. Mbouna

Abstract
. We give positive answer to two conjectures posed by M. E. H Ismail in his monograph [Classical and quantum orthogonal polynomials in one variable, Cambridge University Press, 2005].



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
33D45

1.
Introduction and main result

The Askey-Wilson divided difference operator is defined by

(1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{q}f)(x)=\frac{\breve{f}\big(q^{1/2}z\big)-\breve{f}\big(q^{-1/2}z\big)}{\breve{e}\big(q^{1/2}z\big)-\breve{e}\big(q^{-1/2}z\big)},\quad z=e^{i\theta},

where
\breve{f}(z)=f\big((z+1/z)/2\big)=f(\cos\theta)
for each polynomial
f
and
e(x)=x
.
Here
0<q<1
and
\theta
is not necessarily a real number (see
[
2
, p. 300]
). Hereafter, we denote
x(s)=(q^{s}+q^{-s})/2
with
0<q<1
. Taking
e^{i\theta}=q^{s}
in (
1
),
\mathcal{D}_{q}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}f(x(s))=\frac{f\big(x(s+\frac{1}{2})\big)-f\big(x(s-\frac{1}{2})\big)}{x(s+\frac{1}{2})-x(s-\frac{1}{2})}.

Set
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{q}\,f=f
and
\mathcal{D}^{1}_{q}=\mathcal{D}_{q}
, and define
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}=\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{q})
for each
k=1,2,\dots
. The following two conjectures, which generalize the Sonin-Hahn problem, were posed by M. E. H Ismail in his monograph on Orthogonal Polynomials and Special Functions published in 2005 (see
[
2
, Conjecture 24.7.10 and Conjecture 24.7.11]
) and revised in 2009:

Conjecture 1.1
.

If
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{D}_{q}p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, or the latter with a limiting case of
\mathcal{D}_{q}
, are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials, then
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them.

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}p_{n+k})_{n\geq 0}
, or the latter with a limiting case of
\mathcal{D}_{q}
, are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials for some
k
,
k=1,2,\dots
, then
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them.

Define the average operator
\mathcal{S}_{q}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}f(x(s))=\frac{f\big(x(s+\frac{1}{2})\big)+f\big(x(s-\frac{1}{2})\big)}{2}.

for every polynomial
f
. In 2003, Ismail proved the following result (see
[
2
, Theorem 20.1.3]
):

Theorem 1.1
.

A second order operator equation of the form

(2)

\displaystyle f(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}\,y+g(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}\,y+h(x)\,y=\lambda_{n}\,y

has a polynomial solution
y_{n}(x)
of exact degree
n
for each
n=0,1,\dots
, if and only if
y_{n}(x)
is a multiple of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. In all these cases
f
,
g
,
h
, and
\lambda_{n}
reduce to

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=-q^{-1/2}(2(1+\sigma_{4})x^{2}-(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3})x-1+\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{4}),

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-q}(2(\sigma_{4}-1)x+\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3}),\quad h(x)=0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{4q(1-q^{-n})(1-\sigma_{4}q^{n-1})}{(1-q)^{2}},

or a special or limiting case of it,
\sigma_{j}
being the jth elementary symmetric function of the Askey-Wilson parameters.

Virtually the above conjectures are summed up in one if we are able to prove Conjecture
1.2
. To do this, we prove that the sequences of polynomials appearing in Conjecture
1.2
satisfy, for each
k
, a second order operator equation of the form (
2
). The important point to note here is that this argument would not lead to a satisfactory conclusion if we were not looking for the whole space of “Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them”.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}p_{n+k})_{n\geq 0}
, or the latter with a limiting case of
\mathcal{D}_{q}
, are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials for some
k
,
k=1,2,\dots
, then, for each
k
,
(\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{q}p_{n+k-1})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them.

Fix
k
,
k=1,2,\dots
. It is easily seen that
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence of orthogonal polynomials satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}p_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=-m}^{m}c_{n,j}\,p_{n+j}(x),\quad c_{n,-m}\not=0,

for a polynomial
\pi
which does not depend on
n
, if and only if
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}p_{n+k})_{n\geq 0}
are sequences of orthogonal polynomials. Now, we can apply Theorem
1.2
to conclude that for each
k
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
(\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{q}p_{n+k-1})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. In particular, taking
k=2
and
m=2
in (
3
), we have the main result proved in
[
1
]
:
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. Neither in this work nor in
[
1
]
was possible to exclude the ‘limiting cases’ in the last statement. If so, we would have positive answer to a particular case of another conjecture posed by Ismail (see
[
2
, Conjecture 24.7.9]
).

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The following properties are well known:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}(fg)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{D}_{q}f)(\mathcal{S}_{q}g)+(\mathcal{S}_{q}f)(\mathcal{D}_{q}g),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}(fg)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{U}_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{q}f)(\mathcal{D}_{q}g)+(\mathcal{S}_{q}f)(\mathcal{S}_{q}g),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{2}_{q}f

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathrm{U}_{2}\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}f+\mathrm{U}_{1}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}f+f,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}\mathcal{S}_{q}f

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}f+\mathrm{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}f,

for polynomials
f
and
g
, where
\mathrm{U}_{1}(x)=(\alpha^{2}-1)x
,
\mathrm{U}_{2}(x)=(\alpha^{2}-1)(x^{2}-1)
, and
2\alpha=q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}
.
We leave it to the reader to verify by induction that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}(Xf(x))=\gamma_{k}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{q}f(x)+\frac{q^{k/2}+q^{-k/2}}{2}X\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}f(x).

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\frac{q^{k/2}-q^{-k/2}}{q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}}.

Set
P_{n}^{[k]}=\gamma_{n}!/\gamma_{n+k}!\,\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}P_{n+k}
, and so
P_{n}^{[k]}=\gamma_{n+1}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}
. Since
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(P^{[k]}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, for a certain fixed
k
, are sequences of (monic) orthogonal polynomials, any three consecutive elements of these sequences satisfy

(4)

\displaystyle xP_{n+k}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+k+1}(x)+B_{n+k}P_{n+k}(x)+C_{n+k}P_{n+k-1}(x),

(5)

\displaystyle xP^{[k]}_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=P^{[k]}_{n}(x)+B^{[k]}_{n-1}P^{[k]}_{n-1}(x)+C^{[k]}_{n-1}P^{[k]}_{n-2}(x),

with
C_{n+k}\not=0
and
C^{[k]}_{n-1}\not=0
. We apply
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{q}
to (
4
) to get

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{q^{k/2}+q^{-k/2}}{2}x\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\gamma_{n+k}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)+B_{n+k-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{n+k-1}}C_{n+k-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n-1}(x).

From (
5
) we have

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}}x\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\gamma_{n+1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}}B^{[k]}_{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{n-1}}C^{[k]}_{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n-1}(x).

We now apply
\mathcal{S}_{q}
to (
6
) and (
7
) and, by combining the resulting equations, we can eliminate
\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n-1}(x)
, and obtain the equation

(8)

\displaystyle D_{n}(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+E_{n}\mathrm{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{n-1}}C_{n-1}^{[k]}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=F_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x),

where

\displaystyle D_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q^{(k+1)/2}+q^{-(k+1)/2}}{2}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n-1}}C_{n-1}^{[k]}-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma_{n+k-1}}C_{n+k-1}\right)x-\frac{1}{\gamma_{n-1}}B_{n+k-1}C^{[k]}_{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\gamma_{n+k-1}}B^{[k]}_{n-1}C_{n+k-1},

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k+1}}{\gamma_{n-1}}C_{n-1}^{[k]}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{n+k-1}}C_{n+k-1},

\displaystyle F_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{n+k}}{\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-1}}C^{[k]}_{n-1}-\frac{\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n+k-1}}C_{n+k-1}.

Similarly, we can eliminate
\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)
, and shift
n
to
n+1
to obtain the equation

(9)

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{n}(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)-\widetilde{E}_{n}\mathrm{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{n+2}}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=F_{n+1}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x),

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+2}\widetilde{D}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{k/2}+q^{-k/2}}{2}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{n+1}}x+B_{n+k}-\frac{\gamma_{n+k+1}}{\gamma_{n+1}}B_{n}^{[k]},

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n+2}}.

We now apply
\mathcal{D}_{q}
to (
6
) and (
7
) and, by combining the resulting equations, we can eliminate
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n-1}(x)
, and obtain the equation

(10)

\displaystyle D_{n}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+E_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)=F_{n}\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x).

Similarly, we can eliminate
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)
, and shift
n
to
n+1
to obtain the equation

(11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{n}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)-\widetilde{E}_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n+1}(x)=F_{n+1}\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x).

Note that if
\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}))=0
we have
P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}+1/2))=-P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}-1/2))
. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}))=0
. So

P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}+1/2))=P^{[k]}_{n}(x(s_{1}-1/2))=0,

which is impossible. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
have no common zeros. After shifting
n
to
n-1
in (
11
), to obtain a contradiction, suppose that
F_{n}=0
, i.e.

\widetilde{D}_{n-1}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)=\widetilde{E}_{n-1}\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x).

Since
\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
have no common zeros, we have
\widetilde{E}_{n-1}=0
, which is impossible. (We can also conclude that
F_{n+1}\not=0
.) Multiplying (
11
) by
F_{n}
and using (
10
) and (
8
), we get

(12)

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+g_{n}(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{n-1}}\widetilde{E}_{n}C^{[k]}_{n-1}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)=0,

where

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{n}\widetilde{E}_{n}\mathrm{U}_{2}-D_{n}(x)\widetilde{D}_{n}(x)+F_{n}F_{n+1},

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E}_{n}D_{n}(x)-E_{n}\widetilde{D}_{n}(x).

We next claim that there exist nonzero numbers
r_{n}
and two polynomials
f(x)
and
g(x)
of degree at most two and one, respectively, not simultaneously zero, such that

f_{n}(x)=r_{n}\,f(x),\quad g_{n}(x)=r_{n}\,g(x).

Indeed, multiplying (
10
) by
F_{n+1}
and (
11
) by
D_{n}(x)
, we can eliminate
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)
, and obtain, using (
9
) and shifting
n
to
n-1
, the equation

(13)

\displaystyle f_{n-1}(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+g_{n-1}(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{n+1}}E_{n-1}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)=0.

(If
D_{n}(x)=0
, we combine directly (
10
) and (
9
) to obtain (
13
).)
Suppose that
E_{n-1}=0
, i.e.

f_{n-1}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n-1}(x)=-g_{n-1}(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n-1}(x).

Since
\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n-1}(x)
and
\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n-1}(x)
have no common zeros, we have
f_{n-1}(x)=g_{n-1}(x)=0
, which is imposible according to (
12
) after shifting
n
to
n-1
. Thus, by combining (
12
) and (
13
), we can eliminate
P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)
, and obtain the equation

\displaystyle\left(f_{n}(x)-\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n-1}}\frac{\widetilde{E}_{n}}{E_{n-1}}C^{[k]}_{n-1}f_{n-1}(x)\right)\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=-\left(g_{n}(x)-\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n-1}}\frac{\widetilde{E}_{n}}{E_{n-1}}C^{[k]}_{n-1}g_{n-1}(x)\right)\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x).

Again, since
\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{S}_{q}P^{[k]}_{n}(x)
have no common zeros, the desired conclusion follows. This allows us to rewrite (
12
) as

(14)

\displaystyle f(x)\mathcal{D}^{2}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+g(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)+\lambda_{n}\,P^{[k-1]}_{n}(x)=0,

where
f(x)
and
g(x)
are polynomials of degree at most two and one, respectively, not simultaneously zero, and
\lambda_{n}=\gamma_{k}/(r_{n}\gamma_{n-1})\widetilde{E}_{n}C^{[k]}_{n-1}\not=0
. Thus, by Theorem
1.1
,
(P^{[k-1]}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. Since now
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(P^{[k-1]}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials, repeating the previous argument we conclude, for each
j=k-2,\dots,0
, that
(P^{[j]}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are multiples of the Askey-Wilson polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. The rest of the proof is trivial.

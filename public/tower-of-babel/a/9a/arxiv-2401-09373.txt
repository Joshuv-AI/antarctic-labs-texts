On the Archimedean Positivstellensatz in Real Algebraic Geometry
Konrad Schmüdgen

Abstract
. A variant of the Archimedean Positivstellensatz is proved which is based on Archimedean semirings or quadratic modules of generating subalgebras. It allows one to obtain representations of strictly positive polynomials on compact semi-algebraic sets by means of smaller sets of squares or polynomials. A large number of examples is developed in detail.

. 13J30 (Primary); 12D15, 14P10.

Key words:
Positivstellensatz, positive polynomial, semi-algebraic set, quadratic module, semiring, moment problem

1.
Introduction

Archimedean Positivstellensätze are important tools of real algebraic geometry to describe positive polynomials on compact semi-algebraic sets in terms of weighted sums of squares or as elements of semirings (see e.g
[
M08
, Section 7.4]
or
[
S17
, Section 12.4]
). In many situations there are finer versions of these theorems for which smaller sets of squares or elements are sufficient.
Some results in this direction have been obtained in
[
SS23
, Sections 4 and 6]
. The aim of this paper is to provide a variant of a general Archimedean Positivstellensatz of this kind and to treat a number of illustrating examples.

To formulate the result we need some terminology and facts from real algebra
[
M08
]
,
[
PD01
]
. Throughout this paper,

A
is a
commutative unital real algebra
and
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
,
m\in\mathds{N}
, are subalgebras of
A
which contain the unit element
1
of
A
. For
\alpha\in\mathds{R}
, we write
\alpha
for
\alpha\cdot 1
. The subalgebra of
A
generated by
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
,
n\leq m,
, is denoted by
\mathsf{alg}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
.

By a
cone
in
A
we mean a subset
C
of
A
such that
\lambda c\in C
and
c+d\in C
for all
c,d\in C
and
\lambda\geq 0
. A cone
C
in
A
is called
unital
if
1\in C
.

If
C_{j}
is a unital cone of
A_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, then
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
denotes the unital cone in
\mathsf{alg}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
which is generated by
C_{1}\cdot C_{2}\cdots C_{n}
. That is,
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
is the set of elements

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\,c_{1k}\cdots c_{nk},\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{ where}\penalty\ \penalty\ c_{jk}\in C_{j},\,j=1,\dots,n,\,k=1,\dots,r,\,r\in\mathds{N}.

We recall the following standard notions of real algebraic geometry.

Definition 1
.

A unital cone

in
A
is called a

\bullet

quadratic module
of

if
a^{2}b\in C
for all

and

\bullet

(or a

) of

if
C\cdot C\subseteq C
.

\bullet

preordering
of

if

is a quadratic module and a semiring.

The bounded part of

with respect to a unital cone

\displaystyle A_{\rm bd}(C):=\{a\in A:\penalty\ \textrm{there exists}\penalty\ \lambda>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{such that}\penalty\ \penalty\ (\lambda-a)\in C\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ (\lambda+a)\in C\}.

A unital cone

is called

if

. If

is a quadratic module or a semiring of

, then

is a subalgebra of

. Thus, in this case

is Archimedean if a set of generators of the algebra

belongs to

.

For a unital cone

of

, we define

Obviously,

is again a unital cone of

. Such cones were introduced in
[

]
and studied extensively in
[

]
.

A

is a unital algebra homomorphism of

. The set of all characters of

is denoted by

. For a subset

, let

\displaystyle\mathsf{char}(A;C):=\{\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A):\,\chi(c)\geq 0\penalty\ \textrm{for all}\penalty\ c\in C\,\}.

A unital cone

of

is called a

.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that the algebra
A
is generated by the subalgebras
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
of
A
. We assume that
C_{i}
is an Archimedean semiring or an Archimedean quadratic module of the algebra
A_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
. Let
M
be a unital cone of
A
. Then for any element
a\in A
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\chi(a)>0
for all
\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A;M)
and
\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A;C_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m
.

(ii)

There exists a number
\varepsilon>0
such that
a\in\varepsilon+\mathsf{cone}(M,C_{1},\dots,C_{m}).

We restate the result in the important special case
M=\mathds{R}_{+}\cdot 1
separately as

Corollary 3
.

Retain the assumptions of Theorem
2
and let
a\in A
. The the following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

\chi(a)>0
for all
\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A;C_{i})
and
i=1,\dots,m
.

(ii)

There is a number
\varepsilon>0
such that
a\in\varepsilon+\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m}).

Proof.

Set
M=\mathds{R}_{+}\cdot 1
. Then
\mathsf{cone}(M,C_{1},\dots,C_{m})=\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
and all characters of
A
are in
\mathsf{char}(A;M)
, so the assertion follows from
Theorem
2
.
∎

The following corollary is the main assertion of Theorem
2
in the case
m=1
. It can be considered as a Positivstellensatz for ”modules” of quadratic modules.

Corollary 4
.

Suppose
Q
is an Archimedean quadratic module of
A
and let
M
be a unital cone of
A
. If
a\in A
satisfies
\chi(a)>0
for all
\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A;M)
and
\chi\in\mathsf{char}(A;Q)
, then
a\in\varepsilon+\mathsf{cone}(M,Q)
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Let us briefly discuss the preceding results and notions.
If all cones
C_{i}
are semirings of
A_{i}
, then it is easily seen that
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
is semiring of
A=\mathsf{alg}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
. However, if some cones
C_{i}
are quadratic modules and others are semirings which are not preorderings, then
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
is in general neither a semiring nor a quadratic module of
A
(see Example
20
below). In fact, if
C_{i}
is a quadratic module of
A_{i}
, then
C_{i}
is invariant under multiplication by squares of
A_{i}
, but not under multiplication by squares of the larger algebra
A
. However, if all
C_{i}
are Archimedean semirings or quadrartic modules, then
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\dagger})
is even a preordering. This is shown in the next theorem which is also a main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
,

\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\dagger}
is an

Archimedean preordering of the algeba
A
and
\mathsf{cone}(M,\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\dagger})
is an
\mathsf{cone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\dagger}
-module.

The proofs of both theorems are given in the next section. A large number of examples, known and new ones, are developed in section
3
.

Remark 6
.

The results of this paper have direct applications to the classical multi-dimensional moment problem. For this we recall Haviland’s theorem
[
H36
]
, in the general form given in
[

, Theorem 1.14]
:

Suppose
A
is a finitely generated unital real algebra and

is a closed subset of
\mathsf{char}(A)
. Let

denote the preordering

(1)

\displaystyle C:=\{a\in A:\chi(a)>0,\chi\in K\}.

If

is a linear functional on

such that

for all
a\in C
, then

is a
K
-moment functional, that is, there exists a Radon measure

on
\mathsf{char}(A)
supported on

such that all functions of
A
are
\mu
-integrable and
L(f)=\int fd\mu
for

.

Based on Theorem
2
and Corollaries

and
4
we develop in Section
3
descriptions of the preordering

given by (

K(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
. Requiring that

for all these elements

yield new solvability criteria for the
K(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
-moment problem in the corresponding examples. We leave it to the reader to restate these criteria.

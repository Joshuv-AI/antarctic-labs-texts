Non-equilibrium spin polarisation via interfacial exchange-field spin filtering
Prasanta Muduli ‡, Naëmi Leo ‡, Mingran Xu, Zheng Zhu, Jorge Puebla, Christian Ortiz, Hironari Isshiki, YoshiChika Otani

Abstract
A key phenomenon that enables nanoscale spintronic devices is the efficient inter-conversion between spin and charge degrees of freedom. Here, we experimentally demonstrate a pathway to generate current-induced spin polarization at the interface between an insulating ferromagnet and a non-magnetic metal using interfacial exchange-field spin filtering. Measuring current-in-plane giant magnetoresistance in Py | | Cu | | EuS trilayer Hall cross devices, we induce a non-equilibrium spin polarization of P neq Cu|EuS = 0.6 % P_{\text{neq}}^{\text{Cu|EuS}}=$0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$ at a low charge current density of 1.88 × 10 3 A / cm 2 1.88\text{\times}{10}^{3}\text{\,}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}^{2} . This efficiency is comparable to that of conventional charge-to-spin conversion of spin-Hall or Rashba-Edelstein effects enabled by relativistic spin-orbit coupling. Interfacial exchange field filtering allows operation with largely reduced power consumption and magnetic reconfigurability, opening new pathways to nanoscale low-power insulator spintronics.



Future green information technologies with fast, non-volatile, and scalable low-power operation rely on the creation and detection of spin currents that carry information in the form of spin angular momentum instead of charge carriers. These desirable properties are crucial to spintronics applications ranging from magnetic memories, sensors, logic gates, and unconventional computing architectures
Wolf et al. 2001
;
Manipatruni et al. 2018
;
Puebla et al. 2020
;
Hirohata et al. 2020

To achieve further miniaturization of nanoscale spintronic devices and for the implementation with complementary metal-oxide semiconductor (CMOS) technologies, a key challenge is the efficient generation of spin polarization at low charge current densities
Manipatruni et al. 2018
;
Dieny et al. 2020
;
Hirohata et al. 2020
.

These mechanisms can be classified by two characteristics, namely whether they are mediated by relativistic spin-orbit interaction or magnetic exchange, and whether the conversion occurs in bulk or is confined to interfaces.

In the case of the spin Hall effect (SHE), spin-orbit coupling in heavy metals such as Pt, Ta, and W leads to spin-dependent deflection of charge carriers in bulk and at interfaces, leading to a non-equilibrium spin accumulation
Sinova et al. 2015
. With SHE, spin polarization values in the order of 3% to 12% have been demonstrated
Zhang et al. 2014
.
The Rashba-Edelstein effect allows for even larger current-induced spin polarization, with values up to 60% observed
Hong et al. 2012
;
Leis et al. 2020
;
Dankert et al. 2018
. The effect originates from the spin-momentum locking and momentum-dependent spin splitting at interfaces with broken inversion symmetry and large spin-orbit coupling, and occurs in topological insulators and two-dimensional electron gases
Bychkov and Rashba 1984
;
Edelstein 1995
;
Sánchez et al. 2013
;
Amin and Stiles 2016
;
Soumyanarayanan et al. 2016
.
While interfacial effects in topological insulators are, in principle, great to scale devices to the nanoscale, spurious bulk conduction produces significant Joule heating and thus makes miniaturization unfeasible.

The generation of spin currents and spin-polarised currents based on generally stronger exchange interaction therefore might pose an attractive alternative.
Indeed, bulk ferromagnetic metals are a commonly-used source for spin-polarized currents generated from unpolarised charge currents, with typically a few percent conversion efficiency
Meservey and Tedrow 1994
. Almost perfectly spin-polarised currents can theoretically be achieved in half-metals, however, these materials are not yet viable for technological applications
Katsnelson et al. 2008
.

A pathway to
interfacial exchange-mediated
generation of spin currents is explored within the field of insulator spintronics
Brataas et al. 2020
;
Emori and Li 2021
;
Davidson et al. 2020
, and spin polarised currents at (heavy metal)|(ferromagnetic insulator) interfaces have been demonstrated via the spin-Hall magnetoresistance (SMR)
Nakayama et al. 2013
and the unidirectional spin Hall magnetoresistance (USMR)
Avci et al. 2015
.
Nevertheless, in addition to the exchange interaction at the interface, both SMR and USMR rely on the presence of spin-orbit coupling. As such, these effects require expensive heavy metals, which furthermore have high resistances that are undesirable for energy-efficient device operation.

In this work, we experimentally demonstrate the generation of a current-induced spin polarisation without the need for heavy metals via interfacial exchange-field spin filtering (IESF) between a normal metal and an insulating ferromagnet, here highly-conducting copper (Cu) and prototypical europium sulphate (EuS).
We quantify the non-equilibrium spin polarization via magneto-resistance measurements on trilayer Py
|
Cu
|
EuS Hall-bar devices, obtaining an interfacial spin polarization of
P^{\text{Cu|EuS}}_{\text{neq}}=$0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
at a low charge current density of
1.88\text{\times}{10}^{3}\text{\,}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}^{2}
.
We show that this efficiency is comparable to that of conventional methods based on spin-orbit coupling, such as the spin-Hall or the Rashba-Edelstein effect. Interfacial exchange-field spin filtering thus offers itself for the implementation of low-power spintronic devices.

I
Interfacial exchange-field

spin filtering (IESF)

The interfacial exchange field (IEF) arises from short-ranged direct spin-spin interactions between adjacent layers of different – not necessarily magnetic – materials and thus is highly localized to the common interface, with a decay depth of a couple of nanometres only
Moodera et al. 2007
.
The contemporary technological importance of the interfacial exchange field is given by static equilibrium phenomena, including the exchange bias between ferromagnets and antiferromagnets
Meiklejohn and Bean 1956
, proximity-induced magnetization
Huang et al. 2012
;
Guo et al. 2014
and field-free spin splitting of up to several hundred Tesla in superconductors
Meservey et al. 1970
;
Hao et al. 1991
.

Figure 1:

Current-induced spin polarization by interfacial exchange-field spin filtering.

Schematic illustration of the mechanism of non-equilibrium spin polarization via spin-dependent reflection from an interfacial exchange field (IEF).
An unpolarised in-plane charge current
\mathbf{j}_{c}
in the conductor Cu layer (brown) leads to the scattering of the charge carriers at the IEF localized at the Cu
|
EuS interface (green).
No charge current flows in EuS (blue), due to it being highly insulating.
If the charge carrier spin (black and silver) is not aligned with the EuS magnetization (large black arrow), the exerted torque at the interfacial exchange field leads to different spin-dependent resistivities
\rho_{\uparrow}\neq\rho_{\downarrow}
, and thus to net non-equilibrium spin polarization in non-magnetic Cu.

In Fig.
1
we illustrate how the interfacial exchange field can also generate a non-equilibrium current-induced spin polarization at the interface between a ferromagnetic insulator and a non-magnetic conductor such as Cu.
An in-plane unpolarised charge current
\mathbf{j}_{c}\neq 0
(with equal spin-dependent resistivities
\rho_{\uparrow}=\rho_{\downarrow}
, resulting in
\mathbf{j}_{s}=0
) flowing in the metallic layer (brown) scatters off the interface to the ferromagnetic insulator (blue).
Depending on the relative orientation between charge carrier spins and the local magnetization of the ferromagnetic insulator (black arrows), spin-dependent reflection from the interfacial exchange field (IEF, green) modifies the spin-dependent resistivities,
\rho_{\uparrow}\neq\rho_{\downarrow}
, which leads to a net spin current
\mathbf{j}_{s}\neq 0
(this effect is also called spin filtering).
As consequence, a non-equilibrium interfacial spin polarization
P^{i}_{\text{neq}}
emerges with

P^{i}_{\text{neq}}=\frac{\rho_{\uparrow}^{i}-\rho_{\downarrow}^{i}}{\rho_{\uparrow}^{i}+\rho_{\downarrow}^{i}}\,.

(1)

A suitable material to experimentally prove the non-equilibrium current-induced spin polarization mediated by the IESF is europium sulphite (EuS), a highly insulating ferromagnetic semiconductor with a Curie temperature of
T_{C}^{\text{EuS}}=$16\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$

Mauger and Godart 1986
.
EuS is a popular material for superconducting tunnel junctions
Moodera et al. 1988
;
Miao and Moodera 2009
;
Moodera et al. 2007
, and the strength of the IEF from spin-splitting in superconductors in proximity with EuS has been estimated to be about
14\text{\,}\mathrm{T}

Wei et al. 2016
.
The influence of the IEF for EuS has also been demonstrated as exchange bias
Müller et al. 2006
;
Volobuev et al. 2008
, and in contact with various 2D materials
Wei et al. 2016
;
Zhao et al. 2017
as well as topological insulators
Katmis et al. 2016
.
Recent results of in EuS
|
Pt bilayers furthermore demonstrated the creation of an interfacial non-equilibrium spin accumulation via the spin Hall magnetoresistance
Gomez-Perez et al. 2020
.

Based on these properties, we chose EuS in combination with highly conducting non-magnetic Cu (a metal without sizable spin-orbit coupling, which avoids spurious additional spin-Hall or Rashba-Edelstein effects) for our proof-of-principle experiments for IESF-mediated current-induced spin polarization.

Experimentally, the current-induced spin polarization can be detected in various ways, including using optical techniques
Kato et al. 2004
;
Puebla et al. 2017
, spin-polarized positron beams
Kawasuso et al. 2013
;
Zhang et al. 2014
, and electrical methods
Nguyen and Pai 2021
.
One of the conceptually simplest techniques to quantitatively determine the value
P
of the interfacial spin polarization is via the giant magnetoresistance (GMR)
Baibich et al. 1988
, which is largely equivalent to spin potentiometric measurements used to characterize interfacial spin-orbit effects in heavy metals
Liu et al. 2014
and topological insulators
Dankert et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2015
;
Hong et al. 2012
.

Here, our GMR device is based on a (conducting ferromagnet)|(non-magnetic conductor)|(insulating ferromagnet) trilayer, where the conducting ferromagnet acts as a source for a spin-polarized current arising from partial shunting of a pure charge current – here, we chose widely used permalloy (Py, Fe
0.2
Ni
0.8
) (Supplemental Material Sec.
S1
).
If the intermediate non-magnetic conducting layer is much thinner than its spin diffusion length (for Cu this value is about
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
), the spin of the charge carrier is preserved during the transport between the two ferromagnetic interfaces.
Spin-dependent charge carrier scattering at the insulating ferromagnet (i.e., the detector) then leads to a magnetoresistance

\text{MR}=\frac{R_{\uparrow\downarrow}-R_{\uparrow\uparrow}}{\min\left(R_{\uparrow\downarrow},\,R_{\uparrow\uparrow}\right)}\,,

(2)

where
R_{\uparrow\uparrow}
and
R_{\uparrow\downarrow}
denote the resistance in the state with parallel and antiparallel alignment of the two ferromagnets, respectively.
For a current-in-plane geometry (CIP GMR) with a Py|Cu|EuS trilayer, the magnetoresistance
MR
then also can be written as (Supplementary Information Sec. S2)
Tsymbal et al. 2001
:

\text{MR}=\frac{{P^{\text{Py|Cu}}_{\text{eq}}\,P^{\text{EuS|Cu}}_{\text{neq}}}}{{1-P^{\text{Py|Cu}}_{\text{eq}}\,P^{\text{EuS|Cu}}_{\text{neq}}}}\,.

(3)

Knowledge of the spin polarization between the two metals, which is a material-dependent equilibrium property of the interface (here
P^{\text{Py|Cu}}_{\text{eq}}=48\%

Muduli et al. 2018
), allows determination of the unknown non-equilibrium spin polarisation
P^{\text{EuS|Cu}}_{\text{neq}}
at the metal|insulator interface.

Methods

Hall bar devices for magneto-transport measurements were fabricated using mask-less photo-lithography.
To ensure good electrical contact to the bottom Py electrode, and as EuS is insulating and therefore does not allow for post-growth wire-bonded contacts, a two-step lithography procedure was used:
In the first step, gold contact pads were patterned on Si/SiO
2
substrates, then
2\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
Ti to promote adhesion and
60\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
Au were deposited using electron-beam evaporation, followed by a lift-off process.
In a second lithography step, Hall devices as shown in Fig.
2
with
5\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
leg width and crosses separated by
200\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
,
320\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}

Trilayer Py(

2\text{\times}{10}^{-8}\text{\,}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{r}

During deposition, the substrate stage was cooled to

Muduli et al. 2018

All films and Hall-cross devices were capped with

The crystal structure of the EuS thin films was characterized using x-ray diffraction, indicating polycrystalline growth

The EuS thin films are highly insulating, with resistances measured in a two-point configuration much larger than

Temperature- and field-dependent magnetization profiles of multilayer films were measured using DC magnetometry in a Quantum Design MPMS magnetometer. IV
Discussion

A spin polarization of half a percent might look small compared to the large values of
P_{\text{neq}}^{\text{SHE}}\approx$11\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
obtained from the spin-Hall effect at Pt interfaces
Zhang et al. 2014
, or even
P_{\text{neq}}^{\text{REE}}\approx$60\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
for the Rashba-Edelstein effect in topological insulators
Hong et al. 2012
;
Leis et al. 2020
;
Dankert et al. 2018
.
However, this comparison ignores that our experiments were performed at current densities which are lower than in many reported experiments.

To
quantitatively
compare the efficiency of the spin polarization generated via different effects, we make use of the fact that for all these mechanisms, including the IESF, the current-induced non-equilibrium spin polarization scales linearly with the charge current density
P_{\text{neq}}\propto j_{c}

Sinova et al. 2015
;
Hong et al. 2012
.
This linear relationship allows us to calculate a normalized polarization
P^{\text{norm}}_{\text{neq}}=|P_{\text{neq}}/j_{c}|j_{c}^{\text{ref}}
for literature values, evaluated at the reference charge current density
j_{c}^{\text{ref}}
.
Here, we choose
j_{c}^{\text{ref}}=${10}^{4}\text{\,}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}^{2}$
, a value compatible with CMOS operation and thus a desirable target for low-power spintronic devices
IEEE 2021
.

Interfacial exchange-field spin filtering in the bilayer (normal non-heavy metal)|(ferromagnetic insulator) devices, therefore, offers an attractive method for future nanoscale generators of current-induced spin polarization.
As the converting material (here EuS) is highly insulating, parasitical resistivities are greatly reduced, and the dissipated power
P_{\text{loss}}
in the metallic part with resistivity
\rho
and wire cross section
A=wt
(with wire width

P_{\text{loss}}=RI^{2}\propto\frac{\rho}{A}\left(j_{c}A\right)^{2}\propto\rho j_{c}^{2}t\,.

(4)

This equation promises a profound reduction of waste heat generation by several orders of magnitude: First, via the lower device resistance
R
by using highly-conductive CMOS-compatible metals like Cu or Al.
Second, via the effective reduction of required current densities
j_{c}
via the IESF, as listed in Tab.
2
.
And third, the favourable scaling
P_{\text{loss}}\propto t
with the conductor thickness is further enhanced by the confinement of the interfacial exchange field to within nanometres at the metal|insulator interface, which leads to an increase of
P_{\text{neq}}^{\text{norm}}
(respectively a reduction of the required current density
j_{c}
to achieve the same non-equilibrium spin polarization).

Finally, the polarization
\mathbf{s}
of the spin-polarized current generated via interfacial exchange field filtering can both be tuned externally by fields, as well as allows for field-free operation due to the non-volatile magnetization in the ferromagnetic insulator.
As Cu has a long spin diffusion length, the resulting spin current furthermore can be transported over long distances.
These properties are useful for applications, e.g., for the implementation of next-generation low-power magnetic random access memory devices or for coupling spintronic oscillators

Puebla et al. 2020

Hirohata 2022

V
Outlook

In this work, we demonstrate a new pathway for current-induced spin polarization via interfacial exchange field filtering (IESF), based on the spin-dependent scattering at a ferromagnetic insulator that is highly confined to the interface between a nonmagnetic metal and an insulating ferromagnet.
From magnetoresistance measurements in Py|Cu|EuS trilayer Hall bar devices, we quantified the spin polarization arising from this effect to be in the order of
P_{\text{neq}}^{\text{Cu|EuS}}\approx$3\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
at low current densities of
{10}^{4}\text{\,}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}^{2}
, which is comparable to the charge-to-spin conversion efficiency based on spin-orbit coupling mechanisms, such as the spin-Hall and Rashba-Edelstein effect.

Interfacial exchange field filtering therefore is an attractive pathway towards insulating spintronics devices operating at low current densities and with additional magnetic reconfigurability and non-volatility.
Future developments, therefore, should aim at identifying highly-insulating ferromagnets, such as garnets
Gomez-Perez et al. 2018
;
Vélez et al. 2019
or other semiconductors and insulators, in particular those whose spin-mixing conductance exhibit a large imaginary contribution
Dieny and Chshiev 2017
;
Gomez-Perez et al. 2020
;
Rosenberger et al. 2021
.
Such materials would allow harnessing interchange exchange-field spin filtering for implementation in functional spintronic devices, ideally at room temperature and with CMOS compatibility.

Current shunting in trilayers

Typical resistances of EuS films at low temperatures are very large (see Methods), and we can safely disregard any charge conduction through the EuS layer.
In the Py|Cu|EuS trilayer Hall bars therefore the net charge current
I_{T}
flows parallel through both the Cu and Py layer, so that
I_{T}=I_{\mathrm{Py}}+I_{\mathrm{Cu}}
(current shunting).
Due to the partial shunting in the magnetic Py layer, the charge current
I_{\text{Cu}}
in the Cu layer is also spin-polarised with a spatially varying polarization direction determined by the local moment at the Py
|
Cu interface.

The ratio
\eta
between currents
I_{\mathrm{Py}}
and
I_{\mathrm{Cu}}
is inversely proportional to the resistances of the respective layers,

\eta=\frac{I_{\mathrm{Cu}}}{I_{\mathrm{Py}}}=\frac{R_{\mathrm{Py}}}{R_{\mathrm{Cu}}}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{Py}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{Cu}}}\,\frac{t_{\mathrm{Cu}}}{t_{\mathrm{Py}}}\,,

(S1)

and the fraction of the respective current flowing through both the Cu and Py layer then can be written as

\frac{I_{\mathrm{Cu}}}{I_{T}}=\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\frac{I_{\mathrm{Py}}}{I_{T}}=\frac{1}{1+\eta}\,.

(S2)

The resistances in the wires is given by
R_{i}=\rho_{i}\frac{l_{i}}{w_{i}t_{i}}

with material-dependent resistivity
\rho_{i}
(
i
= Py, Cu) and
l_{i}
,
w_{i}
, and
t_{i}
as lengths, widths, and thicknesses of the respective layer of the wire.
As the length and width of the wires are equal,
{l_{\mathrm{Cu}}=l_{\mathrm{Py}}}
and
{w_{\mathrm{Cu}}=w_{\mathrm{Py}}}
, only the layer thicknesses appear in Eq. (
S1
).

At
10\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
the resistivities are
\rho_{\mathrm{Cu}}\approx$2\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}$
and
\rho_{\mathrm{Py}}\approx$32\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}$

Muduli et al. 2018
.
With these values we obtain current ratios
{\eta(t_{\mathrm{Cu}}=$10\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}$)\approx 16}
and
{\eta(t_{\mathrm{Cu}}=$20\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}$)\approx 32}
for the Py
|
Cu
|
EuS devices used in this work.
We thus estimate that
94\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the net charge current flows through the trilayer device with Cu layer thickness
t_{\mathrm{Cu}}=$10\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}$
(respectively
97\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for
t_{\mathrm{Cu}}=$20\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}$

S2
CIP-GMR for Py|Cu|EuS trilayers

According to the two-carrier conduction model, the charge conduction can be considered in up-spin and down-spin channels, with spin-dependent resistivities
\rho^{i}_{\uparrow}
and
\rho^{i}_{\downarrow}
at the magnet|(non-magnetic metal) interfaces
i
, where
i
can be either Py
|
Cu or Cu
|
EuS.
With this, the magnetoresistance
\mathrm{MR}
of a CIP GMR trilayer is
Tsymbal et al. 2001

\mathrm{MR}=\frac{(\alpha_{\text{Py|Cu}}-1)(\alpha_{\text{Cu|EuS}}-1)}{\alpha_{\text{Py|Cu}}(1+q)+\alpha_{\text{Cu|EuS}}(1+q^{-1})}\,.

(S3)

Here,
{\alpha}^{i}=\rho_{\downarrow}^{i}/\rho^{i}_{\uparrow}
is the spin asymmetry at the interface, and
{q=\rho^{\text{Cu$|$EuS}}_{\uparrow}/\rho^{\text{Py$|$Cu}}_{\uparrow}}
denotes the ratio of the up-spin resistivities of the two ferromagnets.

Due to its high resistance, no charge current flows through the EuS layer, and, as described above, only a small fraction of the net current passes through Py. Thus we can assume that the spin-dependent resistances (
\rho^{i}_{\uparrow}
) at each interface are dominated by the Cu resistance, and
q\approx 1
:

q=\frac{\rho^{\text{Cu|EuS}}_{\uparrow}}{\rho^{\text{Py|Cu}}_{\uparrow}}\approx\frac{\rho^{\text{Cu|EuS}}}{\rho^{\text{Py|Cu}}}\approx 1\,.

(S4)

The spin polarization
P_{i}
then can be calculated from the interface spin asymmetry parameter
\alpha^{i}
as

P_{i}=\frac{\rho_{\downarrow}^{i}-\rho^{i}_{\uparrow}}{\rho^{i}_{\downarrow}+\rho^{i}_{\uparrow}}=\frac{1-\alpha^{i}}{1+\alpha^{i}}.

(S5)

From Eqs. (
S3
) and (
S5
) we obtain Eq. (
3
) in the manuscript:

\text{MR}=\frac{{P_{\text{Py|Cu}}P_{\text{EuS|Cu}}}}{{1-P_{\text{Py|Cu}}P_{\text{EuS|Cu}}}}\,.

(S6)

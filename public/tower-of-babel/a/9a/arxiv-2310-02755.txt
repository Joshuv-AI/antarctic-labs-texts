On Certain Recurrence Relations for Generalized Poly-Cauchy Numbers and Polynomials
Ghania Guettai, Diffalah Laissaoui, Mohamed Amine Boutiche, Mourad Rahmani

Abstract
. The main objective of this paper is to present recurrence relations for the generalized poly-Cauchy numbers and polynomials. This is accomplished by introducing the concept of generalized m-poly-Cauchy numbers and polynomials. Additionally, the paper delves into the discussion of the corresponding generalized m-poly-Bernoulli numbers and polynomials that are associated with the aforementioned generalized m-poly-Cauchy numbers and polynomials.

05A15, 11B73, 11B68.

1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 0,k\geq 1
be integers and let
a,q
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}
be
non-zero real numbers, with
L=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\right)
and
l={\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}}l_{i}
. The Cauchy numbers of the first kind, denoted as
c_{n}
, and the second kind, represented as
\widehat{c}_{n}
, are defined in
[
4
,
7
]
by the following formulas:

c_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}x\left(x-1\right)\cdots\left(x-n+1\right)dx

and

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{n}\int_{0}^{1}x\left(x+1\right)\cdots\left(x+n-1\right)dx.

The generalized poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind
c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
are defined in
[
5
]
by

c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\int_{0}^{l_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{l_{k}}\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\right)^{a}\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-q\right)\\
\cdots\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-\left(n-1\right)q\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k},

(1.1)

or by the following generating function

l^{a}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q};a\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

where
\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(z;a\right)
denotes the
extended polylogarithm factorial function

\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(z;a\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\frac{1}{\left(n+a\right)^{k}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(1.2)

We can express

c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
in the following explicit expression:

c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}s\left(n,i\right)\frac{q^{n-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i\right)^{k}},

(1.3)

in which
s\left(n,i\right)
are the Stirling numbers of the
first kind, defined as follows:

\frac{1}{i!}\left(\ln\left(1+x\right)\right)^{i}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}s\left(n,i\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

(1.4)

and given recursively by the formula

s\left(n+1,i\right)=s\left(n,i-1\right)-ns\left(n,i\right)\text{
\ }\left(1\leq i\leq n\right).

Note that, if
a=l=1,
then
c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(1,q,\left(1,\cdots,1\right)\right):=c_{n,q}^{\left(k\right)}
are the poly-Cauchy
numbers with a
q
parameter introduced in
[
9
]
and, if we set
q=l=1
in
(
1.3
) then,
c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,1,\left(1,\cdots,1\right)\right):=c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a\right)
are the
shifted poly-Cauchy numbers
[
6
]
.

Similar to
c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
, the generalized
poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind
\widehat{c}_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
are described in
[
5
]
by

\widehat{c}_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\left(-1\right)^{a-1}\int_{0}^{l_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{l_{k}}\left(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\right)^{a}\left(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-q\right)\\
\cdots\left(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-\left(n-1\right)q\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k},

(1.5)

or explicitly as

\widehat{c}_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}s\left(n,i\right)\left(-1\right)^{i}\frac{q^{n-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i\right)^{k}}

and have the generating function

l^{a}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(-\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q};a\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\widehat{c}_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

In our research, our objective is to complete the study conducted in
[
5
]
by providing a straightforward method for calculating the integrals (
1.1
) and (
1.5
). Subsequently, we extend all the results from
[
5
]
to the polynomial case. To achieve this, we introduce the concept of generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first and second kind.

We recall that, the combinatorial numbers which complement the
s\left(n,i\right)
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i},
while
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}_{r}
introduced by Broder
[
1
]
is an extension of
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i},
and can be expressed by the exponential generating function

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+r}{i+r}_{r}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{i!}e^{rz}\left(e^{z}-1\right)^{i}.

(1.6)

Taking
r=0
in (
1.6
), we get
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}.
The properties

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{r}_{r}=r^{n-r}

(1.7)

and

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n+r}{i+r}_{r}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n+r}{i+r}_{r-1}-(r-1)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n+r-1}{i+r}_{r-1}

The generalized

2.1.
The generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind

Let
n\geq 0,m\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and let
a,q
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}
be non-zero real numbers, with
L=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\right)
and
l={\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}}l_{i}
. We give a sequence
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
with two indices, which we call generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind, by

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\int_{0}^{l_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{l_{k}}\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\right)^{a+m}\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-q\right)\\
\cdots\left(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-\left(n-1\right)q\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k},

or simply by

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a+m,q,L\right)

Using (
1.3
), the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
may be expressed as

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}s\left(n,i\right)\frac{q^{n-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}},

with

\mathcal{C}_{0,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{l^{a}}{a^{k}}.

Theorem 1
.

The expression for the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is given by

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{j+n}_{n}q^{m-j}c_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

(2.1)

with

\mathcal{C}_{n,0}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right).\text{\ }

Proof.

Using the inverse Stirling transform for (
1.3
), we have

\frac{l^{a+n}}{q^{n}\left(a+n\right)^{k}}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}q^{-i}c_{i}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right).

Now, from result in
[
8
, p.
681
, Corollary
1
]
, we deduce the formula in Theorem
1
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

The
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is given by

\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q};a+m\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(2.2)

Proof.

We find, from(
1.4
) and (
1.2
), that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}}\left(\frac{l}{q}\right)^{i}\frac{1}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}s\left(n,i\right)\frac{\left(qx\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}}\frac{1}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q}\right)^{i}\frac{1}{i!}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q};a+m\right).

∎

Theorem 3
.

The
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)

satisfies the following recurrence relation

\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=l\left(1-\frac{1}{a+m+1}\right)^{k}\mathcal{C}_{n,m+1}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)-nq\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

(2.3)

with

\mathcal{C}_{0,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{l^{a}}{a^{k}}.

Proof.

According to (
2.1
) and (
1.8
), we can write

\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{q^{m}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n+1}{j+n+1}_{n}c_{n+j+1}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\\
-\frac{q^{m}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}}q^{-j}n\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{j+n+1}_{n}c_{n+j+1}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right).

Using (
1.7
), we get

\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{q^{m+1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m+1}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n+1}{j+n}_{n}c_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\\
-\frac{q^{m+1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}n{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{j+n}_{n}c_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\\
+\frac{q^{m+1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}n^{m+1}c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)-n\frac{q^{m+1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}n^{m}c_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

which gives the following equality

\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{q^{m+1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m+1}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n+1}{j+n}_{n}c_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}(a,q,L)\\
-nq\left(\frac{q^{m}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{j+n}_{n}c_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}(a,q,L)\right).

This is evidently equivalent to (
2.3
).
∎

Consequently, from Theorem
3
, we can deduce a recurrence formula
for the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind with negative upper indices
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
.

Corollary 4
.

The
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
satisfies
the recurrence equation

\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=l\left(1+\frac{1}{a+m}\right)^{k}\mathcal{C}_{n,m+1}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)-nq\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

(2.4)

with

\mathcal{C}_{0,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=a^{k}l^{a}.

Remark 1
.

Note that the double generating function of
\ \mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
can be easily obtained by using
(
2.2
).

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n,k\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}}\frac{l^{a}a^{k}}{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p\geq 0}}\frac{\left(a+m+p\right)^{k}}{p!}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q}\right)^{p}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=l^{a}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p\geq 0}}\frac{1}{p!}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q}\right)^{p}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}}\left(\frac{a\left(a+m+p\right)}{a+m}y\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle=l^{a}e^{ay}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p\geq 0}}\frac{1}{p!}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q}e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=l^{a}e^{ay}\exp\left(\ln\left(1+qx\right)^{\frac{l}{q}e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}\right)

\displaystyle=l^{a}e^{ay}\left(1+qx\right)^{\frac{l}{q}e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}.

Now, if
a=l=q=k=1,
then
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(1\right)}\left(1,1,\left(1,\cdots,1\right)\right):=\mathcal{C}_{n,m}
is
m-
Cauchy numbers of the first kind which are different from
p
-Cauchy numbers of the first kind
[
9
]
. The first few values for
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}
are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0,m}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\text{ \ }\mathcal{C}_{1,m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+m}{2+m},\text{ }

\displaystyle\text{\ }\mathcal{C}_{2,m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+m}{\left(2+m\right)\left(3+m\right)},\text{ }

\displaystyle\text{\ }\mathcal{C}_{3,m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1+m\right)\left(+m\right)}{\left(2+m\right)\left(3+m\right)\left(4+m\right)},

\displaystyle\text{\ }\mathcal{C}_{4,m}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\left(1+m\right)\left(38+12m+m^{2}\right)}{\left(2+m\right)\left(3+m\right)\left(4+m\right)\left(5+m\right)}.

Corollary 5
.

The numbers
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}
satisfy the
recurrence relation

\left(2+m\right)\mathcal{C}_{n+1,m}=\left(1+m\right)\mathcal{C}_{n,m+1}-n\left(2+m\right)\mathcal{C}_{n,m},

(2.5)

with
\mathcal{C}_{0,m}=1
is the initial sequence.

(\mathcal{C}_{n,m})_{n,m\geq 0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&1&\cdots\\
1/2&2/3&3/4&4/5&\cdots\\
-1/6&-1/6&-3/20&-2/15&\cdots\\
1/4&7/30&1/5&6/35&\cdots\\
-19/30&-17/30&-33/70&-83/210&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\\
\mathcal{C}_{n,0}&\mathcal{C}_{n,1}&\mathcal{C}_{n,2}&\mathcal{C}_{n,3}&&\end{pmatrix}

The Gregory coefficients
\mathcal{G}_{n}
are defined by

\mathcal{G}_{n}=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{n}}{n!},

and play an important role in Gregory’s formula
[
7
]
which has similarities to the
Euler-Maclaurin summation formula wherein finite differences replace
derivatives. Using (
2.5
), we can easily prove the following recurrence
formula for computing

.

Corollary 6
.

Let an initial sequence
\mathcal{G}_{0,m}=1,
be given. Then the matrix
\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,m}\right)_{n,m\geq 0}
associated with this sequence is given recursively by the formula

\left(1+n\right)\left(2+m\right)\mathcal{G}_{n+1,m}=\left(1+m\right)\mathcal{G}_{n,m+1}-n\left(2+m\right)\mathcal{G}_{n,m}

2.2.
The generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind

Similarly, we define the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the second
kind
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)

by

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\widehat{c}_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a+m,q,L\right).

Theorem 7
.

(1)

The exponential generating function for
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is

\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(-\frac{l\ln\left(1+qx\right)}{q};a+m\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(2)

The
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
may be expressed as

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}q^{n-i}s\left(n,i\right)\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}.

(3)

The expression for the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is given by

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(-q\right)^{m}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{l^{m}a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}}q^{-j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{j+n}_{n}\widehat{c}_{n+j}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right).

(4)

The
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
has the recurrence formula

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=-l\left(1-\frac{1}{a+m+1}\right)^{k}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m+1}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)-nq\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

with

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{l^{a}}{a^{k}}.

(5)

The
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
has the recurrence formula

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=-l\left(1+\frac{1}{a+m}\right)^{k}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m+1}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)-nq\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),

with

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=a^{k}l^{a}.

(6)

The double generating function of
\ \widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is given by

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n,k\geq 0}}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(-k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}=\frac{l^{a}e^{ay}}{\left(1+qx\right)^{\frac{l}{q}e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}}.

Next, we shall prove some relations between the aforementioned two kinds of the generalized

m
-poly-Cauchy numbers.

Theorem 8
.

For any integer
n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{n!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}q^{n-i}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!}.

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{n!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}q^{n-i}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!}.

(2.7)

Proof.

We use generating function formula, yields

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{n!}x^{n}=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1-qx\right)}{q};a+m\right).

On the other hand,

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}q^{n-i}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!}x^{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!q^{i}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left(qx\right)^{n}.

Since

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}z^{n}=\frac{z^{i}}{\left(1-z\right)^{i}},

we get

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}q^{n-i}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!}x^{n}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)}{i!}\frac{x^{i}}{\left(1-qx\right)^{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1-qx\right)}{q};a+m\right).

Thus, the proof of (
2.6
) is completed. Using the same method we can prove
(
2.7
).
∎

3.
The generalized
m
-poly-Bernoulli numbers

The generalized poly-Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,L\right)
are defined in
[
5
]
as follows:

l^{a-1}\frac{\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(l\left(1-e^{-z}\right);a-1\right)}{\left(1-e^{-z}\right)}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}B_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,L\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

wherein
\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(z;a\right)
denotes the generalized
polylogarithm function

\operatorname*{Li}{}_{k}\left(z;a\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}}\frac{z^{n}}{\left(n+a\right)^{k}}.

The
B_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,L\right)
can be computed explicitly by the formula

B_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,L\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-i}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\frac{i!l^{i+a}}{\left(a+i\right)^{k}}.

Here we introduce the generalized
m
-poly-Bernoulli numbers, denoted
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
, corresponding to the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy numbers and give the connections between them.

Definition 9
.

Let
n\geq 0,m\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and let
a,q
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}
be non-zero real numbers, with
L=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\right)
and
l={\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}}l_{i}
. The
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is defined by:

\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\frac{\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right);a+m-1\right)}{1-e^{-qz}}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(3.1)

Theorem 10
.

An explicit formula for
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)
is

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\frac{i!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}l^{i+a}}{\left(a+m+i\right)^{k}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}.

Proof.

Equations (
3.1
) and (
1.6
) imply that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{i}}{\left(i+a+m-1\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{i}}{\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i}i!l^{i+a}}{q^{i}\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\frac{\left(-qz\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\frac{\left(-q\right)^{n-i}i!l^{i+a}}{\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the first formula with the last one, we get Theorem
10
.
∎

Using the inverse Stirling transform
[
8
]
, we give the following corollary.

Corollary 11
.

One has

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}s\left(n,i\right)\left(-q\right)^{n-i}\mathcal{B}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{(a+m)^{k}n!l^{n+a}}{\left(a\left(n+a+m\right)\right)^{k}}.

In this paragraph, to find a simple method for computing the generalized
m
-poly-Bernoulli numbers, we consider the sequence
\left(H_{n,m,p}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\right)_{n,p\geq 0}
with

(3.2)

with

and

Theorem 12
.

The

H_{n+1,p}\left(m\right)=\frac{l\left(p+1\right)\left(p+a+m\right)^{k}}{\left(p+a+m+1\right)^{k}}H_{n,p+1}\left(m\right)-pqH_{n,p}\left(m\right),

\displaystyle H_{n,p+1}\left(m\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+a+m+1)^{k}}{\left(p+1\right)!l^{p+1}(a+m)^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}\left(s\left(p,i-1\right)-ps\left(p,i\right)\right)\left(-q\right)^{p+1-i}\mathcal{B}_{n+i,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)

which is obviously equivalent to

.

Now, setting

We have

\mathcal{H}_{n+1,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)=\frac{l\left(m+1\right)\left(m+a\right)^{k}}{\left(m+a+1\right)^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n,m+1}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)-mq\mathcal{H}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right),

.

The double exponential generating function of

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(-k)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}=\frac{l^{a}e^{ay}}{1-\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}.

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(-k)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{ql^{a-1}a^{k}}{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}\sum_{p\geq 0}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{p}\left(a+m+p-1\right)^{k}}{\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}\right)\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}e^{ay}}{1-\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}.

∎

Theorem 16
.

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}}q^{n-j}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}i!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1}{j-1}\mathcal{C}_{j,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),\ \ \ (n\geq 1).

We can write

\displaystyle RHS

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}}q^{n-j}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}i!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1}{j-1}\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{j}}q^{j-p}s\left(j,p\right)\frac{l^{a+p}}{\left(a+p+m\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{n}}q^{n-p}\frac{l^{a+p}}{\left(a+p+m\right)^{k}}\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}i!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1}{j-1}s\left(j,p\right)\right).

From

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1}{j-1}s\left(j,p\right)=\left(-1\right)^{i-p}\binom{i-1}{p-1}

and

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{i-p}i!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\binom{i-1}{p-1}=\left(-1\right)^{n-p}p!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{p},

we get the desired result.
∎

.

The following formulas hold true:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}q^{n-j}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i-j}}{i!}s\left(n,i\right)s\left(i,j\right)\mathcal{B}_{j,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right),\ \ \ (n\geq 1),

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{n}}\frac{q^{n-p}l^{p+a}}{\left(a+m+p\right)^{k}}s\left(n,p\right)

-poly-Cauchy polynomials

4.1.
The generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy polynomials of the first kind

Let
n\geq 0,m\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and let
a,q
and

l_{1},...,l_{k\text{ }}
be non-zero real numbers, with
L=\left(l_{1},...,l_{k}\right)
and
l=l_{1},...,l_{k\text{ }}.
By
\left(x\right)_{q,n}
we denote the generalized rising factorial given by

\left(x\right)_{q,n}=x\left(x+q\right)\cdots\left(x+q(n-1)\right)
with
\left(x\right)_{q,0}=1
.

We define
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
as follows

\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}\left(1+qz\right)^{\frac{x}{q}}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1+qz\right)}{q};a+m\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(4.1)

which we call the generalized
m
-poly-Cauchy polynomials of the first kind
and, we have the following expression for
\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
.

Theorem 19
.

One has

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\mathcal{C}_{j,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\left(x\right)_{q,n-j}.

(4.2)

Proof.

From (
4.1
) and

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(-1\right)^{n}\left(x\right)_{q,n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\left(1+qz\right)^{\frac{x}{q}}},

we find

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(-1\right)^{n}\left(x\right)_{q,n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\mathcal{C}_{j,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\left(x\right)_{q,n-j}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

The theorem follows from coefficient comparison.
∎

Theorem 20
.

One has

\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\frac{q^{n-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}},

wherein
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)
is the weighted Stirling
numbers of the first kind
[
2
,
3
]
, given by

\frac{1}{i!}\frac{\left(\ln\left(1+z\right)\right)^{i}}{\left(1+z\right)^{x}}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=i}^{\infty}}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

or explicitly by

\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}s\left(j,i\right)\left(x\right)_{1,n-j}.

(4.3)

Proof.

From
\left(\ref{GGF}\right)
and Theorem
2.1
, we can deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\mathcal{C}_{j,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L\right)\left(x\right)_{q,n-j}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}}s\left(j,i\right)\frac{q^{j-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}\left(x\right)_{q,n-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\frac{q^{n-i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}}\left(-1\right)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}s\left(j,i\right)\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)_{n-j}

and the theorem follows from
\left(\ref{GG2}\right)
.
∎

Theorem 21
.

The following identity is true

\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{n-i}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\mathcal{C}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}l^{n+a}}{a^{k}\left(a+n+m\right)^{k}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)
is the weighted Stirling
numbers of the second kind
[
2
,
3
]
given by

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=i}^{\infty}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{i!}e^{xz}\left(e^{z}-1\right)^{i},

(4.4)

or explicitly by

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{i!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}}\binom{i}{j}\left(-1\right)^{i-j}\left(x+j\right)^{n}.

Proof.

By using the weighted Stirling transform
[
2
]

\alpha_{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\beta_{i}\Leftrightarrow\beta_{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\alpha_{i},

and Theorem
20
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Theorem 22
.

We have

(1)

The

may be

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}}q^{n-i}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i}l^{a+i}}{\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}.

(4.5)

(2)

Next, we shall prove some relations between the aforementioned two kinds of the generalized

-poly-Cauchy polynomials.

Theorem 23
.

For
n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;-x\right)}{n!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}q^{n-i}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;-x\right)}{n!}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}q^{n-i}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Applying generating function formula, we can have

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;-x\right)}{n!}z^{n}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;-x\right)\frac{\left(-z\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\left(1-qz\right)^{\frac{x}{q}}\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1-qz\right)}{q};a+m\right).

On the other hand,

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}q^{n-i}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!}\right)z^{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!q^{i}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left(qz\right)^{n}.

Since

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq i}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}z^{n}=\frac{z^{i}}{\left(1-z\right)^{i}},

we get

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}q^{n-i}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!}\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{i,m}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)}{i!}\frac{z^{i}}{\left(1-qz\right)^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-qz\right)^{\frac{x}{q}}\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\operatorname*{Lif}\nolimits_{k}\left(\frac{l\ln\left(1-qz\right)}{q};a+m\right).

Thus, the proof of (
4.6
) is completed. Using the same method we can prove
(
4.7
).
∎

5.
The generalized
m-
poly-Bernoulli polynomials

Definition 24
.

Let
n\geq 0,m\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and let
a,q
and
l_{1},...,l_{k\text{ }}
non-zero real numbers, with
L=\left(l_{1},...,l_{k\text{
}}\right)
and
l=\prod_{i=1}^{k}l_{i}
. We define the generalized
m
-poly-Bernoulli
polynomials
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
by the formula

\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\frac{\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right);a+m-1\right)}{e^{qzx}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(5.1)

with

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right):=\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;0\right).

The following expression will prove that the polynomials
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
can be described in terms of
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)
:

Theorem 25
.

For
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right),
we have

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{i!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}l^{i+a}}{\left(a+m+i\right)^{k}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right).

(5.2)

Proof.

Equations (
5.1
) and (
4.4
) give

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}e^{qzx}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{i}}{\left(i+a+m-1\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}e^{qzx}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{i}}{\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i}i!l^{i+a}}{q^{i}\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}\sum_{n\geq i}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\frac{\left(-qz\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-q\right)^{n-i}i!l^{i+a}}{\left(i+a+m\right)^{k}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

By equating two power series, we get the result.
∎

In particular,

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;r\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{i!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}l^{i+a}}{\left(a+m+i\right)^{k}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+r}{i+r}_{r}

and

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;\frac{r}{h}\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{h^{n-i}}\frac{i!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}l^{i+a}}{\left(a+m+i\right)^{k}}W_{h,r}\left(n,i\right),

where
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)
are related to
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}_{r}
and
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind
W_{h,r}\left(n,i\right)

by

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(r\right)=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+r}{i+r}_{r}\ \text{and }\ \mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{r}{h}\right)=\frac{1}{h^{n-i}}W_{h,r}\left(n,i\right).

Theorem 26
.

We have

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)(-q)^{n-i}\mathcal{B}_{i,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}n!l^{n+a}}{a^{k}\left(n+a+m\right)^{k}}.

(5.3)

Proof.

By using the weighted Stirling transform and Theorem
25
, we get the result.
∎

Theorem 27
.

An explicit expression for
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
is given by

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}\mathcal{B}_{i,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)\text{ }x^{n-i}.

(5.4)

Proof.

(
5.1
) can be written as:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\frac{\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right);a+m-1\right)}{e^{qzx}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}

\displaystyle=e^{-qzx}\frac{ql^{a-1}\left(a+m\right)^{k}}{a^{k}}\frac{\operatorname*{Li}_{k}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right);a+m-1\right)}{\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}

\displaystyle=e^{-qzx}\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}\mathcal{B}_{i,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L\right)\text{ }x^{n-i}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 28
.

\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(-k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)
has the double exponential generating function

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(-k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}=\frac{l^{a}e^{ay-qzx}}{1-\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}.

Proof.

From (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(-k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{e^{-qzx}ql^{a-1}a^{k}}{\left(a+m\right)^{k}}\sum_{p\geq 0}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{p}\left(a+m+p-1\right)^{k}}{\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)}\right)\frac{y^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=l^{a}e^{-qzx}\sum_{p\geq 1}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)\right)^{p-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{a\left(a+m+p-1\right)y}{\left(a+m\right)}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle=l^{a}e^{ay}e^{-qzx}\sum_{p\geq 1}\left(\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{a}e^{ay-qzx}}{1-\frac{l}{q}\left(1-e^{-qz}\right)e^{\frac{ay}{a+m}}}.

∎

Theorem 29
.

For
n\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}i!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{i-j}\mathcal{S}_{i}^{j}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\mathcal{C}_{j,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right).

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{n-j}\sum_{i=0}^{n}i!\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\mathcal{S}_{i}^{j}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{j,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

(5.6)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{n-j}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i-j}}{i!}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\mathcal{T}_{i}^{j}\left(x\right)\mathcal{B}_{j,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right).

(5.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{n-j}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j}}{i!}\mathcal{T}_{n}^{i}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\mathcal{T}_{i}^{j}\left(x\right)\mathcal{B}_{j,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right).

(5.8)

Proof.

) give

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}i!\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x\right)\frac{\left(a+m\right)^{k}l^{i+a}}{a^{k}\left(a+i+m\right)^{k}}

Now, we generalize (
3.3
) to the polynomial case. For this, we define the polynomials

H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)
by means of the following expression

H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right):=H_{n,m,p}^{\left(k\right)}\left(a,q,L;x\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}H_{i,p}\left(m\right)\text{ }x^{n-i},

(5.9)

with

H_{0,p}\left(m;x\right)=\frac{l^{a}}{a^{k}}

and

H_{n,0}\left(m;x\right)=\mathcal{B}_{n,m}^{(k)}\left(a,q,L;x\right).

Here we can present another explicit expression for
H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)

H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)=\frac{\left(p+a+m\right)^{k}}{p!a^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left(i+p\right)!\left(-q\right)^{n-i}l^{i+a}}{\left(a+m+i+p\right)^{k}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{i}\left(x+p\right).

Theorem 30
.

The
H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)
satisfies the following recurrence

\text{\ }H_{n,p+1}\left(m;x\right)=\frac{l\left(p+1\right)\left(p+a+m\right)^{k}}{\left(p+a+m+1\right)^{k}}H_{n+1,p}\left(m;x\right)-q\left(x+p\right)H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right),

(5.10)

with
H_{0,p}\left(m;x\right)=\frac{l^{a}}{a^{k}}.

Proof.

By (
5.9
) and (
3.3
), we can write

\displaystyle x\frac{d}{dx}H_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)

\displaystyle=nH_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)-n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\frac{i}{n}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}H_{i,p}\left(m\right)x^{n-i}

\displaystyle=nH_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)-n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left(-q\right)^{n-i}H_{i,p}\left(m\right)x^{n-i}

\displaystyle=nH_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)-n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i-1}H_{i+1,p}\left(m\right)x^{n-i-1}

\displaystyle=nH_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)-n\frac{l\left(p+1\right)\left(p+a+m\right)^{k}}{\left(p+a+m+1\right)^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i-1}H_{i,p+1}\left(m\right)x^{n-i-1}

\displaystyle+npq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\left(-q\right)^{n-i-1}H_{i,p}\left(m\right)x^{n-i-1}.

Thus

\displaystyle-xnqH_{n-1,p}\left(m;x\right)

\displaystyle=nH_{n,p}\left(m;x\right)-n\frac{l\left(p+1\right)\left(p+a+m\right)^{k}}{\left(p+a+m+1\right)^{k}}H_{n-1,p+1}\left(m;x\right)

\displaystyle+npqH_{n-1,p}\left(m;x\right).

This evidently equivalent to (
5.10
).
∎

Computationally efficient and data-adaptive changepoint inference in high dimension
Guanghui Wang, Long Feng

Abstract
High-dimensional changepoint inference that adapts to various change patterns has received much attention recently. We propose a simple, fast yet effective approach for adaptive changepoint testing. The key observation is that two statistics based on aggregating cumulative sum statistics over all dimensions and possible changepoints by taking their maximum and summation, respectively, are asymptotically independent under some mild conditions. Hence we are able to form a new test by combining the p-values of the maximum- and summation-type statistics according to their limit null distributions. To this end, we develop new tools and techniques to establish asymptotic distribution of the maximum-type statistic under a more relaxed condition on componentwise correlations among all variables than that in existing literature. The proposed method is simple to use and computationally efficient. It is adaptive to different sparsity levels of change signals, and is comparable to or even outperforms existing approaches as revealed by our numerical studies.



Keywords
: Adaptive tests; Changepoint detection; Extreme value distribution; Gaussian approximation; High dimensions.

1
Introduction

Heterogeneity is a ubiquitous feature of high dimensional data. A particular form of heterogeneity is the changepoint structure,
that is, the data generation mechanism may occur a sudden change at some time point or location.
The primary aims of changepoint detection are (i) testing for the existence of any changepoint and (ii) estimation of one or a few more changepoints if they exist. We refer readers to, for example,
Aue and
Horváth 2013
and
Niu et al. 2016
for an overview.
In this paper, we focus on the testing aspect in high dimension.
Consider a sequence of
p
-dimensional vectors of sample size
n
, i.e.,
\{X_{i}=(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{ip})^{\!\top\!}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, from the following mean-change model

\displaystyle X_{i}=\mu_{0}+\delta\mathbb{I}(i>\tau)+\epsilon_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n,

where
\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
represents the baseline mean level,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the mean-change signal parameter,

\tau\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
is the possible changepoint, and
\{\epsilon_{i}=(\epsilon_{i1},\ldots,\epsilon_{ip})^{\!\top\!}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are random noises with zero mean.
Of interest is to test whether there exists a changepoint, that is,

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}H_{0}:\tau=n\ \text{and}\ \delta=0\ \text{versus}\\
H_{1}:\ \text{there exists}\ \tau\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\ \text{and}\ \delta\neq 0,\end{gathered}

under the paradigm that both the sample size
n
and dimension
p
grow to infinity.

A review of recent developments of various testing procedures for (
1
) can be found in
Liu et al. 2022
.
Most prominent are based on a sequence of individual cumulative sum (CUSUM) statistics, to wit,
\{C_{\gamma,j}(k),\ j=1,\ldots,p\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
with
\gamma=0
or
0.5
frequently used, where

\displaystyle C_{\gamma,j}(k)=\left\{\frac{k}{n}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\right\}^{-\gamma}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(S_{kj}-\frac{k}{n}S_{nj}\right)/\widehat{\sigma}_{j},

(3)

S_{kj}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{ij}
and
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}
’s are estimators for the (long-run) variances.
By first aggregating the individual CUSUMs over
p
dimensions at every possible changepoint
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, a test statistic can then be constructed based on the maximum or summation of all aggregations over all possible
k
’s.
The difference between
\gamma=0
or 0.5 lies in the power performance. To be specific, the power with
\gamma=0
may decay if the changepoint appears early or late
(
Csörgő and
Horváth 1997
)
.

Different ways of aggregations are discussed in the literature. Among them,
Bai 2010
,
Horváth and
Hušková 2012
and
Jin et al. 2016
considered
L_{2}
-aggregations followed by the maximum operator, i.e.,
\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}C_{0,j}^{2}(k)
.
A strategy that replaces each
C_{0,j}^{2}(k)
by a self-normalized U-statistic was suggested in
Wang et al. 2021
.
With proper normalization, such max-
L_{2}
-type statistic converges to the supremum of some function of a Gaussian process under necessary conditions if
H_{0}
holds, for which the corresponding quantile is usually obtained via simulations.
Instead of applying the maximum operator to all
L_{2}
-aggregations,
Wang et al. 2018
and
Wang et al. 2019
proposed a sum-
L_{2}
-type statistic, namely,
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}C_{0.5,j}^{2}(k)
,
which, after appropriate normalization, is shown to asymptotically admit a Gaussian distribution under
H_{0}
.
The
L_{\infty}
-aggregations in conjunction with the maximum operator has also attracted much attention in the literature.
For instance,
Jirak 2015
advocated the statistic
\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0,j}(k)|
and showed that it converges in distribution to the extreme value distribution of Gumbel type after suitable normalization under
H_{0}
.
In addition,
Yu and Chen 2021
considered the statistic
\max_{\lambda\leq k\leq n-\lambda}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0.5,j}(k)|
with
\lambda\in[1,n/2]
being a user-specified boundary removal parameter, and investigated how to approximate its null distribution via the multiplier bootstrap; they didn’t find the asymptotic null distribution of the test statistic.

It is well-known that the
L_{2}
-aggregation method is more effective in detecting dense and weak change signal in the sense that a large number of entries in
\delta
are non-zero each with a small magnitude, and the
L_{\infty}
-aggregation performs better for sparse but strong change signal when there exist a few number of non-zero entries in
\delta
with large magnitudes.
The impact of sparsity on the detection boundary was investigated by
Enikeeva and Harchaoui 2019
and
Liu et al. 2021
for Gaussian data sequences.
They also provided testing rules that can achieve the minimax detection rate and are adaptive to the sparsity by taking supremum over a grid of sparsity levels in conjunction with well-calibrated thresholds.
Recently, there is a great deal of effort to develop adaptive testing procedures that are effective for various alternative change patterns.
For instance,
Cho and Fryzlewicz 2015
and
Cho 2016
proposed only aggregating those CUSUMs at every possible changepoint
k
(i.e.,
\{C_{0.5,j}(k),\ j=1,\ldots,p\}
) that pass a certain threshold, and they showed that such testing rules have a vanishing Type I error.
More generally,
Liu et al. 2020
first introduced an adjusted
L_{q}
-aggregation strategy, to wit, with
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
1\leq s_{0}\leq p
,
T_{q,s_{0}}:=\max_{\lambda\leq k\leq n-\lambda}\big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s_{0}}|C_{0,(j)}(k)|^{q}\big\}^{1/q}
,
where
|C_{0,(1)}(k)|\geq\cdots\geq|C_{0,(p)}(k)|
is the order statistics of
\{|C_{0,j}(k)|\}_{j=1}^{p}
.
They then proposed an adaptive procedure by taking the minimum of p-values associated with every
T_{q,s_{0}}
among a range of choices of
q
(e.g.,
q\in\{1,2,3,4,5,\infty\}
) with a fixed
s_{0}
, where each p-value was approximated by the multiplier bootstrap.
Combining different
L_{q}
-aggregations (without adjustment) was considered by
Zhang
et al. 2021
. Specifically, for an even
q
,
they first proposed a sequence of self-normalized U-statistics at every possible changepoint to estimate
\|\delta\|_{q}^{q}
scaled by the location of possible changepoint, say
\{U_{q}(k)\}_{k=2q}^{n-2q}
.
Then the asymptotic null distribution of
T_{q}:=\max_{k=2q,\ldots,n-2q}U^{2}_{q}(k)
is derived and is provably pivotal.
At last, they used the minimum p-value combination over different values of
q
(e.g.,
q\in\{2,6\}
) to construct an adaptive test based on an important fact that
T_{q}
’s with different even
q
’s are asymptotically independent under
H_{0}
.
Projection is an alternative method for constructing adaptive testing rules.

Aston and Kirch 2018
proposed projecting the CUSUMs
(C_{0,1}(k),\ldots,C_{0,p}(k))^{\!\top\!}
for each
k=1,\ldots,n-1
along a random direction and proved that the projections, after suitable normalization, converge to a standard Brownian bridge under
H_{0}
; thus the maximum or summation operator can be applied over all
k
’s to conduct a test.

Wang and Samworth 2018
provided a data-driven estimator of the direction along which the CUSUMs
(C_{0.5,1}(k),\ldots,C_{0.5,p}(k))^{\!\top\!}
should be projected,
and showed that the test has a vanishing Type I error.

In this paper, we suggest a new adaptive method that is simple, fast yet effective.
The key observation is that the max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-type statistics are asymptotic independent under the null hypothesis that there is no changepoint.
Hence a test can be conducted by combining the two separate p-values.
We refer to the proposed test as double-max-sum (DMS) method, since we are essentially conducting the maximum or summation operator along both dimensions and possible changepoints.
The contributions of this paper is three-fold.

(a)

The DMS is computationally efficient. The test statistic can be implemented in linear time over both
n
and
p
. The critical value is determined according to the associated asymptotic null distribution, which is simple to use and thus avoids any numerical approximations such as by simulating Gaussian analogues or by using multiplier bootstrap.

(b)

We are able to show that each of two versions of max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistic, to wit,

\displaystyle M_{n,p}:=\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0,j}(k)|\ \text{and}\ M^{\dagger}_{n,p}:=\max_{\lambda_{n}\leq k\leq n-\lambda_{n}}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0.5,j}(k)|,

with
\lambda_{n}\in[1,n/2]
being a pre-specified boundary removal parameter,
converges in distribution to the extreme value distribution of Gumbel type after appropriate normalization under some mild conditions if
H_{0}
holds.
The ways of convergence of
M_{n,p}
and
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
differ in their normalizing factors.
In the literature,
Jirak 2015
studied the limit distribution of
M_{n,p}
under a logarithmic decay assumption on componentwise correlations among
p
variables, which generally requires a natural ordering among all variables and is hard to be verified in practice. With the presence of such ordering, he obtained the asymptotic null distribution by supplying the blocking arguments
(
Leadbetter 1974
)
. In this paper, we develop new tools and techniques based on the
inclusion-exclusion principle
without restrictions on any ordering patterns.
In addition, to our best knowledge, the study of the asymptotic distribution of
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
is new. In fact, it is related to the function
\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\sup_{\Lambda_{p}\leq t\leq 1-\Lambda_{p}}\{t(1-t)\}^{-1/2}|\mathcal{W}_{tj}-t\mathcal{W}_{tj}|
, with suitably chosen
\Lambda_{p}=o(1)
, of a sequence of dependent Brownian motions
\{\mathcal{W}_{tj}\}_{1\leq j\leq p}
, which may be of independent interest.

(c)

We further prove that either version of the max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistics is asymptotically independent of certain sum-
L_{2}
-type statistic under some mild conditions if
H_{0}
holds, which forms the basis of the DMS method.
Besides, we provide an asymptotic lower bound of the local power function of the proposed test due to the observation that the asymptotic independence also holds under a sequence of local alternative hypotheses. By doing so, we could immediately obtain the consistency of the DMS method and in addition present a detection regime for which the DMS outperforms both the max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-procedures.
The latter offers a new insight on adaptive testing methods beyond the consistency that investigated in the literature
(
Liu et al. 2020
;
Zhang
et al. 2021
)
.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we investigate asymptotic null distributions of two versions of the max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistics, namely,
M_{n,p}
and
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
. The proposed DMS procedure is presented in Section
3
, together with its asymptotic properties. Numerical studies are conducted in Section
4
. Section
5
concludes the paper, and all theoretical proofs are deferred to Supporting Information.

2
Max-
L_{\infty}
-aggregation

We first investigate two versions of max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistics that were considered in the literature
(
Jirak 2015
;
Yu and Chen 2021
;
Liu et al. 2020
)
, to wit,

\displaystyle M_{n,p}:=\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0,j}(k)|\ \text{and}\ M^{\dagger}_{n,p}:=\max_{\lambda_{n}\leq k\leq n-\lambda_{n}}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}|C_{0.5,j}(k)|,

(4)

respectively, where we recall that the CUSUM statistics
C_{\gamma,j}(k)
’s with
\gamma=0
or 0.5 are defined in (
3
), and
\lambda_{n}\in[1,n/2]
is a pre-specified boundary removal parameter. Specification of the estimators
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}
’s in
C_{\gamma,j}(k)
’s will be discussed later.

If
p=1
, it is well-known that, as
n\to\infty
,
M_{n,1}
converges in distribution to
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}B(t)
under
H_{0}
, where
B
denotes the standard Brownian bridge.
The power of
M_{n,1}
may decay if the changepoint appears early or late.
To address this, many weighted versions have been introduced, among which the most prominent is
M^{\dagger}_{n,1}
that is equivalent to the maximally selected likelihood ratio test assuming Gaussianity. The asymptotic null distribution of
M^{\dagger}_{n,1}
depends on the boundary removal parameter
\lambda_{n}
. It can be verified that
M^{\dagger}_{n,1}
converges in distribution to
\sup_{\lambda\leq t\leq 1-\lambda}B(t)/\sqrt{t(1-t)}
if
\lambda_{n}/n\to\lambda\in(0,0.5)
.
However, if
\lambda_{n}=1
or
\lambda_{n}/n\to 0
,
M^{\dagger}_{n,1}
would diverge under
H_{0}
.
It is clear now that
M^{\dagger}_{n,1}
, with necessary normalization, converges in distribution to the extreme value distribution of Gumbel type due to
Darling and Erdös 1956
.
More discussions under
p=1
can be found in
Csörgő and
Horváth 1997
and
Aue and
Horváth 2013
.

When
(n,p)\to\infty
in the sense that both
n
and
p
diverge jointly,
Jirak 2015
showed that
M_{n,p}
, appropriately normalized, weakly converges to the Gumbel distribution under certain decay conditions on the correlations if
H_{0}
holds.
For
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
,
Yu and Chen 2021
presented a valid approximation to its null distribution by using the multiplier bootstrap if

\lambda_{n}/\log^{7}(np)\to\infty
for sub-exponential data sequences.
However, little is known regarding the asymptotic null distribution of
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
as
(n,p)\to\infty
.
In this paper, we shall fill this gap and show that the Darling-Erdös-type convergence also holds for
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
, with suitable chosen
\lambda_{n}
, under
H_{0}
.
In addition, we are able to derive asymptotic null distributions of both
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
and
M_{n,p}
under a more relaxed assumption on componentwise correlations among
p
variables than that in
Jirak 2015
.

Before proceeding any further, we introduce some notations and assumptions on the dependence of the noises
\epsilon_{ij}
’s both in time and across all variables. Suppose there exist measurable functions
g_{j}
’s such that
\epsilon_{ij}=g_{j}(e_{i},e_{i-1},\ldots)
, where
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. To measure temporal dependence, define for
q\geq 1
,

\displaystyle a_{i}(q)=\limsup_{p\to\infty}\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}\|g_{j}(e_{i},e_{i-1},\ldots,e_{0},e_{-1},\ldots)-g_{j}(e_{i},e_{i-1},\ldots,e^{\prime}_{0},e_{-1},\ldots)\|_{q},

where
\{e^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an independent copy of
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
\sigma_{jj^{\prime}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\mathrm{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n}\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right)
be the long-run covariances, and denote
\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{jj}^{1/2}
. The componentwise correlations among
p
variables can thus be defined as
\rho_{jj^{\prime}}=\sigma_{jj^{\prime}}/(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}})
. Denote
R=(\rho_{jj^{\prime}})_{p\times p}
.
Let for some sequences
\delta_{p}>0
and
\kappa_{p}>0
,
B_{p,j}=\{1\leq j^{\prime}\leq p:|\rho_{jj^{\prime}}|\geq\delta_{p}\}
and
C_{p}=\{1\leq j\leq p:|B_{p,j}|\geq p^{\kappa_{p}}\}
.
To estimate the long-run variances
\sigma_{j}
’s, we consider Bartlett’s estimators, to wit,
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}=\sum_{|\ell|\leq b_{n}}(n-\ell)^{-1}\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{n}\left(X_{\ell j}-\bar{X}_{j}\right)\left(X_{i-\ell,j}-\bar{X}_{j}\right)
for some
b_{n}\geq 1
, where
\bar{X}_{j}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}
.

Assumption A1

(Temporal dependence)
.

There exist some constants
q>4
and
\mathfrak{a}>5/2
such that
a_{i}(q)\lesssim i^{-\mathfrak{a}}
. In addition,
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\min_{j=1,\ldots,p}\sigma_{j}\geq\underline{\sigma}
for some constant
\underline{\sigma}>0
.

Assumption A2

(Componentwise correlations)
.

(i)
|\rho_{jj^{\prime}}|\leq\varrho
for
1\leq j\neq j^{\prime}\leq p
and some constant
\varrho\in(0,1)
; (ii)
|C_{p}|/p\to 0
for some
\delta_{p}=o\{(\log p)^{-1}\}
and
\kappa_{p}\to 0
, as
p\to\infty
.

Remark 1
.

Assumption
A1
imposes a polynomial decay restriction on the temporal dependence, and was considered by
Jirak 2015
. A huge variety of popular linear and nonlinear time series models meet Assumption
A1

(
Wu 2005
;
Jirak 2015
)
. In addition, it implies that
\sigma_{jj^{\prime}}
’s are well defined
(
Jirak 2015
)
.
Assumption
A2
–(ii) demands the number of variables that are strongly-correlated (i.e.,
|\rho_{jj^{\prime}}|\geq\delta_{p}
) with many (
\geq p^{\kappa_{p}}
) other variables should not be too much (
o(p)
). It is more relaxed than the logarithmic decay assumption in
Jirak 2015
, to wit,
\rho_{jj^{\prime}}\lesssim\log^{-2-\zeta}(|j-j^{\prime}|+2)
for some
\zeta>0
. To see this, the latter requires a natural ordering among all variables that is not always easy to meet in practice.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
H_{0}
and Assumptions
A1
–
A2
hold. Assume
b_{n}\sim n^{\mathfrak{b}}
for some
0<\mathfrak{b}<1
and
p\lesssim n^{\nu}
for some
0<\nu<\min\{p/2-2,(1-\mathfrak{b})p/2-1\}
.

(i)

As
(n,p)\to\infty
,

\mathrm{P}\left(M_{n,p}\leq u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\}\right)\to\exp\{-\exp(-x)\},

where
u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\}=\sqrt{\{x+\log(2p)\}/2}
.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{n}\sim n^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then, as
(n,p)\to\infty
,

\mathrm{P}\left(M^{\dagger}_{n,p}\leq\frac{x+D(p\log h_{n})}{A(p\log h_{n})}\right)\to\exp\{-\exp(-x)\},

where
A(x)=\sqrt{2\log x}
,
D(x)=2\log x+2^{-1}\log\log x-2^{-1}\log\pi
and
h_{n}=\left\{(\lambda_{n}/n)^{-1}-1\right\}^{2}
.

Remark 2
.

One of the key ingredients in our proof for Theorem
1
is the study of the asymptotic distribution of
\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}Z_{\gamma,j}
with
\gamma=0
or 0.5 if
X_{i}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
independently with
\Sigma=(\sigma_{jj^{\prime}})_{p\times p}
, where
Z_{0,j}=\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}|C_{0,j}(k)|
and
Z_{0.5,j}=\max_{\lambda_{n}\leq k\leq n-\lambda_{n}}|C_{0.5,j}(k)|
. Then, under non-Gaussian scenario, we can apply the Gaussian approximation in conjunction with the truncation arguments
(
Chernozhukov et al. 2013
;
Jirak 2015
)
. However, under Assumption
A2
, the derivations are highly non-trivial even for independent Gaussian data sequences.
Jirak 2015
studied the limit distribution of
\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}Z_{0,j}
under the logarithmic decay assumption by supplying the blocking arguments
(
Leadbetter 1974
)
, which is infeasible under Assumption
A2
due to the absence of the ordering of
p
variables. To this end, we develop new tools and techniques based on the
inclusion-exclusion principle
to make it reachable.
To be specific, let
u_{p}:=u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\}
. According to Step 1 in the proof of Proposition
S.1
in Supporting Information, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\big(\max_{j\not\in C_{p}}Z_{0,j}>u_{p}\big)\to 1-\exp\{-\exp(-x)\},

(5)

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we can construct sharp lower and upper bounds of
\mathrm{P}\big(\max_{j\not\in C_{p}}Z_{0,j}>u_{p}\big)
. To wit, for any

\sum_{t=1}^{2k}(-1)^{t-1}\alpha_{t}\leq\mathrm{P}\big(\max_{j\not\in C_{p}}Z_{0,j}>u_{p}\big)\leq\sum_{t=1}^{2k+1}(-1)^{t-1}\alpha_{t},

where

and the sum runs over all combinations

such that

, see Steps 3–6 in the proof of Proposition

. As a consequence, by letting

and using the Taylor expansion of the function

, (

) immediately follows.
Some intermediate conclusions on asymptotic properties of random variables
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\mathcal{W}_{tj}-t\mathcal{W}_{1j}|
for

are demanded, see Lemmas
S.1
–

in Supporting Information, where

is a sequence of dependent Brownian motions.
Besides, we apply such ideas to study the limit distribution of
\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}Z_{0.5,j}
in conjunction with an investigation of asymptotic properties on dependent random variables
\sup_{\Lambda_{p}\leq t\leq 1-\Lambda_{p}}\{t(1-t)\}^{-1/2}|\mathcal{W}_{tj}-t\mathcal{W}_{tj}|
, with suitably chosen

.

Based on Theorem
1
, we can easily obtain the p-value associated with either

or

, namely,

\displaystyle{\rm p}_{M_{n,p}}

\displaystyle:=1-G\big(2M_{n,p}^{2}-\log(2p)\big)\ \text{or}

\displaystyle{\rm p}_{M^{\dagger}_{n,p}}

\displaystyle:=1-G\big(A(p\log h_{n})M^{\dagger}_{n,p}-D(p\log h_{n})\big),

.
If the p-value is below some pre-specified significant level, say

, then we rejected the null hypothesis that there is no changepoint in the data sequence.
It can be expected that either max-

-based testing procedure would be effective in detecting sparse and strong change signals.

The DMS method

3.1
Sum-
L_{2}
-aggregation

To detect dense but possibly weak changes, we consider a sum-
L_{2}
-based aggregation approach that was proposed by
Wang et al. 2018
for multinomial data and
Wang et al. 2019
for sub-Gaussian data, respectively. To be specific, we use

\displaystyle S_{n,p}:=\sum_{1\leq k<n}\sum_{j=1,\ldots,p}C^{2}_{0.5,j}(k).

(6)

Recall that
R=(\rho_{jj^{\prime}})_{p\times p}
.
Lemma
1
restates the asymptotic null distribution of
S_{n,p}
derived in
Wang et al. 2019
.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that (i)
\epsilon_{ij}=\Sigma^{1/2}\varepsilon_{ij}
, where
\varepsilon_{ij}
are i.i.d. sub-Gaussian variables; (ii)
\mathrm{tr}(R^{4})=o\{\mathrm{tr}^{2}(R^{2})\}
as
p\to\infty
; (iii)
p/n^{3-\upsilon}\to 0
for some
\upsilon>0
. Then, as
(n,p)\to\infty
,

\{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p\}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(S_{n,p})}\to N(0,1)

in distribution, and
\mathrm{Var}(S_{n,p})=\left[\frac{2\pi^{2}-18}{3}n^{2}\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})+\frac{15-\pi^{2}}{3}n\left\{\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}-p^{2}\right\}\right]\{1+o(1)\}
.
Further, if
\widehat{\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})}/\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})\to 1
and
\widehat{\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}}/\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}\to 1
in probability, then
\{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p\}/V^{1/2}_{p,n}\to N(0,1)
in distribution, where
V_{n,p}=\frac{2\pi^{2}-18}{3}n^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})}+\frac{15-\pi^{2}}{3}n\left\{\widehat{\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}}-p^{2}\right\}
.

Remark 3
.

Wang et al. 2019
suggested using
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}=\{2(n-1)\}^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}(X_{ij}-X_{i-1,j})^{2}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
. Further,
\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})
and
\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}
can be estimated by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(n-3)}\sum_{i=1}^{n-3}\left\{(X_{i}-X_{i+1})^{\!\top\!}\widehat{D}_{(i,i+1,i+2,i+3)}^{-1}(X_{i+2}-X_{i+3})\right\}^{2}\ \text{and}\

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{E}(\varepsilon^{\!\top\!}R\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)}\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\left\{(X_{i}-X_{i+1})^{\!\top\!}\widehat{D}_{(i,i+1,i+2)}^{-1}(X_{i+1}-X_{i+2})\right\}^{2}-3\widehat{\mathrm{tr}(R^{2})},

respectively, where for any
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
m\geq 1
,

\widehat{D}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{1(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}^{2},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{p(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}^{2}\right),

and
\widehat{\sigma}_{j(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}^{2}=\{2|\mathcal{A}_{m}|\}^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{m}}(X_{ij}-X_{i-1,j})^{2}
with
\mathcal{A}_{m}=\{2,\ldots,n\}\backslash\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
. With the usages of such difference-based estimators, Lemma
1
holds.

By Lemma
1
, the p-value associated with
S_{n,p}
is

\displaystyle{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}}

\displaystyle:=1-\Phi\left(\{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p\}/V_{n,p}^{1/2}\right),

where
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
N(0,1)
. Again, small values of
{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}}
indicate rejecting the null hypothesis.

3.2
Adaptive strategy

In practice, we seldom know whether the potential change signal is sparse or dense. To adapt to various alternative behaviors, we propose combining the max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-based testing procedures. The key message in this paper is that the max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-type statistics are asymptotically independent under some mild conditions if
H_{0}
holds.
Following most works on adaptive changepoint testing such as
Liu et al. 2020
and
Zhang
et al. 2021
, we restrict ourselves to the setting of independent observations. In such scenario, we propose to use the difference-based variance estimators, i.e.,
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}=\{2(n-1)\}^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}(X_{ij}-X_{i-1,j})^{2}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
.
To ease the presentation and highlight key ideas in the proof, we also introduce the following assumptions.

Assumption A3
.

The noises
\epsilon_{ij}=\Sigma^{1/2}\varepsilon_{ij}
, where
\varepsilon_{ij}
are i.i.d. sub-Gaussian variables, i.e., there exists a constant
\zeta>0
such that
\mathrm{E}\{\exp(t\varepsilon_{ij})\}\leq\exp(\zeta t^{2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Assumption A4
.

(i)
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\min_{j=1,\ldots,p}\sigma_{j}\geq\underline{\sigma}
for some constant
\underline{\sigma}>0
;
(ii)
|\rho_{jj^{\prime}}|\leq\varrho
for
1\leq j\neq j^{\prime}\leq p
and some constant
\varrho\in(0,1)
;
(iii) There exist some constants
0<\underline{c}<\overline{c}<\infty
such that
\underline{c}\leq\lambda_{\min}(R)\leq\lambda_{\max}(R)\leq\overline{c}
, where
\lambda_{\min}(R)
and
\lambda_{\max}(R)
denote the minimal and maximal eigenvalues of
R
.

Remark 4
.

Under Assumptions
A3
–
A4
, Lemma
1
trivially holds if
p/n^{3-\upsilon}\to 0
for some
\upsilon>0
. In addition, Assumption
A4
–(iii) implies that Assumption
A2
–(ii) holds, and thus it can be shown that Theorem
1
holds if
p\lesssim n^{\nu}
for some
\nu>0
. To see this, let
R=Q^{\!\top\!}\Lambda Q
, where
Q=(q_{jj^{\prime}})_{p\times p}
is an orthogonal matrix and
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})
with
\lambda_{j}
’s being the eigenvalues of
R
. Observe that
\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{p}\rho_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{p}q_{j^{\prime}j}^{2}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\overline{c}^{2}
. As a consequence,
|B_{p,j}|\delta_{p}^{2}\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{p}\rho_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\overline{c}^{2}
. Taking
\delta_{p}=(\log p)^{-1-c}
for some constant
c>0
and select
\kappa_{p}=4(1+c)\log\log p/\log p\to 0
, we conclude that
|B_{p,j}|<p^{\kappa_{p}}
for sufficiently large
p
. Hence
|C_{p}|=0
and Assumption
A2
–(ii) holds.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
H_{0}
and Assumptions
A3
–
A4
hold. Assume
p\lesssim n^{\nu}
for some
0<\nu<\min\{p/2-2,3-\upsilon\}
with some
\upsilon>0
.

(i)

As
(n,p)\to\infty
,
M_{n,p}
is asymptotically independent of
S_{n,p}
in the sense that

\mathrm{P}\Big(M_{n,p}\leq u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\},\frac{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)\to\exp\{-\exp(-x)\}\cdot\Phi(y);

(ii)

If
\lambda_{n}\sim n^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then, as
(n,p)\to\infty
,
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
is asymptotically independent of
S_{n,p}
in the sense that

\mathrm{P}\Big(M^{\dagger}_{n,p}\leq\frac{x+D(p\log h_{n})}{A(p\log h_{n})},\frac{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)\to\exp\{-\exp(-x)\}\cdot\Phi(y).

Remark 5
.

Proof of Theorem
2
is first carried under Gaussian data sequences, i.e.,
X_{i}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
, by leveraging the
inclusion-exclusion principle
and projection arguments. Then it is followed by the Gaussian approximation in conjunction with a smooth approximation of the maximum function
(
Chernozhukov et al. 2013
)
to handle sub-Gaussian data.
To fix ideas, we consider Theorem
2
–(i) under Gaussianity, and the goal is to demonstrate asymptotic independence between
\max_{j=1,\ldots,p}Z_{0,j}
with
Z_{0,j}=\max_{k=1,\ldots,n-1}|C_{0,j}(k)|
and
\widetilde{S}_{n,p}:=\{S_{n,p}-(n+2)p\}/{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}
. For any fixed
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, define
A_{p}(x)=\{\widetilde{S}_{n,p}\leq x\}
and
B_{j}(y)=\{Z_{0,j}>u_{p}\{\exp(-y)\}\}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
. By Theorem
1
and Lemma
1
, it suffices to show that

\mathrm{P}\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}A_{p}(x)B_{j}(y)\Big)\to\Phi(x)\cdot\{1-\exp\{-\exp(-y)\}.

By applying the inclusion-exclusion principle to
\mathrm{P}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}A_{p}(x)B_{j}(y)\right)
and
\mathrm{P}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}B_{j}(y)\right)
, we can show

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}A_{p}(x)B_{j}(y)\Big)\leq\mathrm{P}\left(A_{p}(x)\right)\mathrm{P}\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}B_{j}(y)\Big)+\sum_{t=1}^{2k}U(p,t)+V(p,2k+1),

(7)

where for each
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle U(p,t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq p}\left|\mathrm{P}\left(A_{p}(x)B_{j_{1}}(y)\cdots B_{j_{t}}(y)\right)-\mathrm{P}\left(A_{p}(x)\right)\cdot\mathrm{P}\left(B_{j_{1}}(y)\cdots B_{j_{t}}(y)\right)\right|

and
V(p,t):=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq p}\mathrm{P}\left(B_{j_{1}}(y)\cdots B_{j_{t}}(y)\right)
. It can be verified that for each
t\geq 1
,
U(p,t)\to 0
by using the projection method (cf. Lemma
S.7
in Supporting Information) and
V(p,t)\to\frac{1}{t!}\exp(-tx/2)
(see the proof of Theorem
2
), as
(n,p)\to\infty
. By letting
k\to\infty
, it can be concluded from (
7
) that
\limsup_{(n,p)\to\infty}\mathrm{P}\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}A_{p}(x)B_{j}(y)\Big)\leq\Phi(x)\cdot\{1-\exp\{-\exp(-y)\}\}
. Similarly, we have
\liminf_{(n,p)\to\infty}\mathrm{P}\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}A_{p}(x)B_{j}(y)\Big)\geq\Phi(x)\cdot\{1-\exp\{-\exp(-y)\}\}
. Hence the conclusion follows.

According to Theorem
2
, we suggest combining the corresponding p-values by using Fisher’s method
(
Littell and Folks 1971
;
Littell and Folks 1973
)
. To wit,

\displaystyle{\rm p}_{M,S}

\displaystyle:=1-F_{\chi^{2}_{4}}\Big(-2(\log{\rm p}_{M_{n,p}}+\log{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}})\Big)\ \text{or}

\displaystyle{\rm p}_{M^{\dagger},S}

\displaystyle:=1-F_{\chi^{2}_{4}}\Big(-2(\log{\rm p}_{M^{\dagger}_{n,p}}+\log{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}})\Big),

where
F_{\chi^{2}_{4}}
is the CDF of the chi-squared distribution with 4 degrees of freedom.
The rationality is that either
-2(\log{\rm p}_{M_{n,p}}+\log{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}})
or
-2(\log{\rm p}_{M^{\dagger}_{n,p}}+\log{\rm p}_{S_{n,p}})
converges in distribution to
F_{\chi^{2}_{4}}
under
H_{0}
due to Theorem
2
.
Then either
{\rm p}_{M,S}
or
{\rm p}_{M^{\dagger},S}
can be used as the final p-value for testing
H_{0}
. If it is less than some pre-specified significant level
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then we reject
H_{0}
.

Remark 6
.

The sum-
L_{2}
-type statistics were proposed by
Wang et al. 2018
and
Wang et al. 2019
, and have been shown to admit an asymptotic Gaussian distribution under
H_{0}
(cf. Lemma
1
). The authors also considered a power-enhancement term
(
Fan et al. 2015
)
based on the max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistic to form a new testing procedure. However, the rationality is rather different from ours. To wit, under
H_{0}
, the power-enhancement term does not alter the asymptotic Gaussianity of the test statistic. Under
H_{1}
, it would generally diverge and dominate the testing procedure if the change signal is sparse and strong, and thus enhance the testing power.
In contrast, we exploit the joint distribution of both max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-type statistics to adapt to different levels of sparsity. Our numerical studies reveal that the DMS method outperforms such power-enhancement ones in the sparse regime since it leverages the information from the max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistic more efficiently.

Remark 7
.

Sparsity-adaptive changepoint testing has been widely discussed in the literature, as reviewed in Section
1
.
To make comparisons among state of the art approaches, we consider the max-
L_{(s,q)}
-type statistic
(
Liu et al. 2020
)
which aggregates the CUSUMs or their variants, say
Z_{j}(k)
, over
p
dimensions by an adjusted
L_{q}
norm, namely,

T_{q,s}:=\max_{k}\big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s}|Z_{(j)}(k)|^{q}\big\}^{1/q}
,
where
|Z_{(1)}(k)|\geq\cdots\geq|Z_{(p)}(k)|
is the order statistics of
\{|Z_{j}(k)|\}_{j=1}^{p}
.

Enikeeva and Harchaoui 2019
and
Liu et al. 2021
proposed scan statistics that searches for the maximum of appropriately normalized
T_{2,s}
’s over a range of
s
values with well-calibrated normalizing factors. In addition, they showed that their methods can achieve the minimax testing rate for Gaussian data sequences; however, the corresponding asymptotic distributions are unknown.

Liu et al. 2020
suggested combining different
T_{q,s}
over
q
’s with a fixed
s=s_{0}
by taking the minimum of associated p-values, which is obtained via the multiplier bootstrap approximation.
The validity of bootstrap approximation was also justified, without the need of knowing the complicated joint distribution of all
T_{q,s_{0}}
’s over
q
’s.
For practical applications, they recommended using
q\in\{1,2,3,4,5,\infty\}
and
s_{0}=p/2
(although this choice results in a diverging
s
).

Zhang
et al. 2021
investigated the asymptotic distribution of a specialized max-
L_{q}
-type statistic (viz., a self-normalized U-statistic) with an even
q
,
and further justified the asymptotic independence of a finite number of the statistics indexed by different even
q
’s,
under the null hypothesis.
They then came up with the minimum p-value combination to aggregate different
q
level statistics.
Notice that they excluded the statistic with
q=\infty
. In practice, they recommended using
q\in\{2,6\}
.

The proposed DMS method only involves the max-
L_{\infty}
- and sum-
L_{2}
-type statistics, which can be computed in
O(np)
time.
Due to the asymptotic independence of the two statistics (cf. Theorem
2
) under
H_{0}
and the associated reachable p-values (cf. Theorem
1
and Lemma
1
),
it is very convenient to combine both testing procedures.
Consequently, the DMS is simple to use and computationally efficient;
it is free of well-calibrated thresholds or any numerical approximations such as by using multiplier bootstrap or by simulating Gaussian analogues.
As we will show in Section
4
, the power of the DMS is comparable or sometimes outperforms existing approaches; thus the DMS is also effective.

To theoretically investigate the power property of the DMS method, we consider the following sequence of local alternative hypotheses

\displaystyle H_{1;n,p}:|\mathcal{A}|=o\{p/(\log\log p)^{2}\}\ \text{and}\ \Delta_{S}=O\{\mathrm{Var}^{1/2}(S_{n,p})\},

(8)

where
\mathcal{A}=\{1\leq j\leq p:\delta_{j}\neq 0\}
is the support of
\delta
and

\Delta_{S}=\sum_{\tau=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{\tau(n-\tau)}{n}\Big\{\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mathrm{E}(X_{ij})-\frac{1}{n-\tau}\sum_{i=\tau+1}^{n}\mathrm{E}(X_{ij})/\sigma_{j}^{2}\Big\}^{2}

represents the scaled change signal corresponding to the sum-
L_{2}
-type statistic
S_{n,p}
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
H_{1;n,p}
and Assumptions
A3
–
A4
hold. Assume
p\lesssim n^{\nu}
for some
0<\nu<\min\{p/2-2,3-\upsilon\}
with some
\upsilon>0
.

(i)

As
(n,p)\to\infty
,
M_{n,p}
is asymptotically independent of
S_{n,p}
in the sense that

\mathrm{P}\Big(M_{n,p}\leq u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\},\frac{S_{n,p}-E(S_{n,p})}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)-\mathrm{P}\left(M_{n,p}\leq u_{p}\{\exp(-x)\}\right)\mathrm{P}\Big(\frac{S_{n,p}-E(S_{n,p})}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)\to 0;

(ii)

If
\lambda_{n}\sim n^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then, as
(n,p)\to\infty
,
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
is asymptotically independent of
S_{n,p}
in the sense that

\mathrm{P}\Big(M^{\dagger}_{n,p}\leq v_{p,n}(x),\frac{S_{n,p}-E(S_{n,p})}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)-\mathrm{P}\left(M^{\dagger}_{n,p}\leq v_{p,n}(x)\right)\mathrm{P}\Big(\frac{S_{n,p}-E(S_{n,p})}{V_{n,p}^{1/2}}\leq y\Big)\to 0,

where
v_{p,n}(x):=\{x+D(p\log h_{n})\}/{A(p\log h_{n})}
.

Remark 8
.

Theorem
3
extends Theorem
2
under the local alternatives
H_{1;n,p}
, which generally requires that the number of variables that occur changes should be not too much, i.e.,
|\mathcal{A}|=o\{p/(\log\log p)^{2}\}
. In conjunction with
\Delta_{S}=O\{\mathrm{Var}^{1/2}(S_{n,p})\}
, we can restrict ourselves to
S_{n,p}
on
\mathcal{A}^{c}
, say
S_{n,p}(\mathcal{A}^{c}):=\sum_{1\leq k<n}\sum_{j\not\in\mathcal{A}}C^{2}_{0.5,j}(k)
, which is provably asymptotic independent of either
M_{n,p}
or
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
on
\mathcal{A}
by following similar arguments used in the proof of Theorem
2
.

How does the proposed DMS test perform compared to a single use of the max-
L_{\infty}
- or sum-
L_{2}
-type test? To ease notations, let
M
be either
M_{n,p}
or
M^{\dagger}_{n,p}
, and
S=S_{n,p}
. Denote the associated p-values by
{\rm p}_{M}
and
{\rm p}_{S}
, respectively.
For a pre-specified significant level
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
\beta_{M,\alpha}
and
\beta_{S,\alpha}
be the corresponding power functions based on
M
and
S
, respectively.
According to
Littell and Folks 1971
;
Littell and Folks 1973
, the power of Fisher’s p-value combination-based test, say
\beta_{\alpha}
, should be larger than that of the test based on
\min\{p_{M},p_{S}\}
(referred to as the minimal p-value combination), say
\beta_{M\wedge S,\alpha}
. Due to Theorem
3
together with the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{\alpha}\geq\beta_{M\wedge S,\alpha}\geq\beta_{M,\alpha/2}+\beta_{S,\alpha/2}-\beta_{M,\alpha/2}\beta_{S,\alpha/2}.

(9)

For small
\alpha
, the difference between
\beta_{M,\alpha}
and
\beta_{M,\alpha/2}
should be small, and the same fact applies to
\beta_{S,\alpha}
. Consequently, by (
9
), the power of the adaptive DMS test could be larger than that of either max-
L_{\infty}
- or sum-
L_{2}
-type test by a large margin.

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we propose a simple, fast yet effective procedure for adaptive changepoint testing. The key message here is that the max-
L_{\infty}
-type and sum-
L_{2}
-type test statistics are asymptotically independent under some conditions. We can then perform Fisher’s p-value combination for the two separate p-values to form a new test, which is adaptive to different sparsity of change signals as demonstrated both theoretically and by numerical studies.

Although our discussion focuses on the mean change aspect, we believe it can be generalized to other scenarios including but not restricted to detecting changes in covariance matrix or regression coefficients. For example, we can exploit the framework of U-statistic
(
Liu et al. 2020
)
or the score-based framework
(
Zou et al. 2020
)
, and transform the problem into a general mean domain.

We have derived the asymptotic distribution for two versions of max-
L_{\infty}
-type statistic for both temporally and componentwisely dependent high-dimensional time series. The asymptotic independence of max-
L_{\infty}
-type and sum-
L_{2}
-type test statistics are investigated under the assumption that the data are independently collected over time. This temporal independence assumption is frequently considered in the literature of changepoint testing, see, for example,
Liu et al. 2020
,
Yu and Chen 2021
and
Zhang
et al. 2021
, under which the proof of asymptotic independence is rather highly non-trivial. Extension to a more complicated scenario that allows temporal dependence certainly deserves further researches. A related issue is does the asymptotic normality still holds for the sum-
L_{2}
-type statistic for temporally dependent high-dimensional time series (e.g., under Assumptions
A1
–
A2
). We leave them as future work.

Proposition S.1

\displaystyle V(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\beta^{-1}\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp\left(\beta n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{n}x_{tj}\delta_{tk}\right)\right)

where
\delta_{tk}=I(t\leq k)-k/n
.
By setting
\beta=n^{1/8}\log(np)
, (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(W(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\leq x,V(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\leq u_{p}\{\exp(-y)\}\right)\to\Phi(x)\cdot\exp\{-\exp(-y)\}.

(S.20)

Suppose
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
are i.i.d. from
N(0,\Sigma)
and are independent of
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
. The key idea is to show that
(W(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),V(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}))
has the same limit distribution as
(W(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}),V(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}))
, form which (
S.20
) holds according to the result in Section
2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
denote the class of bounded functions with bounded and continuous derivatives up to order 3. It is known that a sequence of random variables
\{Z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to a random variable
Z
if and only if for every
f\in\mathscr{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R}),\mathrm{E}(f(Z_{n}))\to\mathrm{E}(f(Z))
; see, e.g.,
Pollard 1984
.
It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left\{f(W(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),V(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}))\right\}-\mathrm{E}\left\{f(W(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}),V(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}))\right\}\to 0,

for every
f\in\mathscr{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
(n,p)\to\infty
.
We introduce
W_{d}=W(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1},Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{n})
and
V_{d}=V(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1},Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{n})
for
d=1,\ldots,n+1
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathrm{E}\{

\displaystyle f(W(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),V(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}))\}-\mathrm{E}\{f(W(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}),V(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}))\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d=1}^{n}\left|\mathrm{E}\{f(W_{d},V_{d})\}-\mathrm{E}\{f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})\}\right|.

Let

\displaystyle W_{d,0}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{S}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{k=2}^{d-1}b_{i,k}\xi_{i}^{{\!\top\!}}\xi_{k}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{k-1}\sum_{k=d+2}^{n}b_{i,k}Y_{i}^{{\!\top\!}}Y_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=d+1}^{n}b_{i,k}\xi_{i}^{{\!\top\!}}Y_{k}\right)\ \text{and}\

\displaystyle V_{d,0}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp\left(\beta n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{d-1}\xi_{tj}\delta_{tk}+\beta n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=d+1}^{n}Y_{tj}\delta_{tk}\right)\right),

which only rely on
\mathcal{F}_{d}=\sigma\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1},Y_{d+1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
.
By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(W_{d},V_{d})-f(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})=

\displaystyle f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})+f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+O(|(V_{d}-V_{d,0})|^{3})+O(|(W_{d}-W_{d,0})|^{3})

and

\displaystyle f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})-f(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})=

\displaystyle f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})+f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+O(|(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})|^{3})+O(|(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})|^{3}),

where for
f:=f(x,y)
,
f_{1}(x,y)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
,
f_{2}(x,y)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
,
f_{11}(x,y)=\frac{\partial f^{2}}{\partial^{2}x}
,
f_{22}(x,y)=\frac{\partial f^{2}}{\partial^{2}y}
and
f_{12}(x,y)=\frac{\partial f^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}
.

Notice that

\displaystyle W_{d}-W_{d,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}b_{i,d}\xi_{i}^{{\!\top\!}}Y_{d}+\sum_{k=d+1}^{n}b_{d,k}Y_{d}^{{\!\top\!}}Y_{k}\ \text{and}\ W_{d+1}-W_{d,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}b_{i,d}\xi_{i}^{{\!\top\!}}\xi_{d}+\sum_{k=d+1}^{n}b_{d,k}\xi_{d}^{{\!\top\!}}Y_{k}.

Due to
\mathrm{E}(\xi_{t})=\mathrm{E}(Y_{t})=0
and
\mathrm{E}(\xi_{t}\xi_{t}^{{\!\top\!}})=\mathrm{E}(Y_{t}Y_{t}^{{\!\top\!}})
, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(W_{d}-W_{d,0}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d})=\mathrm{E}(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d})\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{E}((W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d})=\mathrm{E}((W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\{f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\{f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})\}\ \text{and}\

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\{f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\{f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}\}.

Consider
V_{d}-V_{d,0}
.
For
l=k+(j-1)n
, let
z_{d,0,l}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{d-1}\xi_{tj}\delta_{tk}+n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=d+1}^{n}Y_{tj}\delta_{tk}
,
z_{d,l}=z_{d,0,l}+n^{-1/2}Y_{dj}\delta_{dk}
and
z_{d+1,l}=z_{d,0,l}+n^{-1/2}\xi_{dj}\delta_{dk}
.
Define
\bm{z}_{d,0}=(z_{d,0,1},\ldots,z_{d,0,np})^{{\!\top\!}}
and
\bm{z}_{d}=(z_{d,1},\ldots,z_{d,np})^{{\!\top\!}}
.
By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\displaystyle V_{d}-V_{d,0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{np}\partial_{l}F_{\beta}(\bm{z}_{d,0})(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{np}\sum_{k=1}^{np}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}F_{\beta}(\bm{z}_{d,0})(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})(z_{d,k}-z_{d,0,k})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{l=1}^{np}\sum_{k=1}^{np}\sum_{v=1}^{np}\partial_{v}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}F_{\beta}(\bm{z}_{d,0}+\delta(\bm{z}_{d}-\bm{z}_{d,0}))(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})(z_{d,k}-z_{d,0,k})(z_{d,v}-z_{d,0,v})

(S.21)

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Again, due to
\mathrm{E}(\xi_{t})=\mathrm{E}(Y_{t})=0
and
\mathrm{E}(\xi_{t}\xi_{t}^{{\!\top\!}})=\mathrm{E}(Y_{t}Y_{t}^{{\!\top\!}})
, we can verify that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\{(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})\mid\mathcal{F}_{d}\}=\mathrm{E}\{(z_{d+1,l}-z_{d,0,l})\mid\mathcal{F}_{d}\}\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{E}\{(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d}\}=\mathrm{E}\{(z_{d+1,l}-z_{d,0,l})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{d}).

By Lemma A.2 in
Chernozhukov et al. 2013
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l=1}^{np}\sum_{k=1}^{np}\sum_{v=1}^{np}\partial_{v}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}F_{\beta}(\bm{z}_{d,0}+\delta(\bm{z}_{d}-\bm{z}_{d,0}))\right|\leq C\beta^{2}

for some positive constant
C
.
By Assumption
A3
,

\mathrm{P}\left(\max_{1\leq t\leq n,1\leq j\leq p}|\xi_{tj}|>C\log(np)\right)\to 0
,
and since
Y_{tj}\sim N(0,1)
,
\mathrm{P}\left(\max_{1\leq t\leq n,1\leq j\leq p}|Y_{tj}|>C\log(np)\right)\to 0
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{np}\sum_{k=1}^{np}\sum_{v=1}^{np}\partial_{v}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}F_{\beta}(\bm{z}_{d,0}+\delta(\bm{z}_{d}-\bm{z}_{d,0}))(z_{d,l}-z_{d,0,l})(z_{d,k}-z_{d,0,k})(z_{d,v}-z_{d,0,v})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{2}n^{-3/2}\log^{3}(np)

holds with probability approaching one.
Consequently, we have, with probability approaching one,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\{f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})\}-\mathrm{E}\{f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})\}\right|\leq C\beta^{2}n^{-3/2}\log^{3}(np).

Similarly, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\{f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})^{2}\}-\mathrm{E}\{f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})^{2}\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{2}n^{-3/2}\log^{3}(np)

and

\displaystyle\Big|\mathrm{E}\{

\displaystyle f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})\}-\mathrm{E}\{f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})\}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{2}n^{-3/2}\log^{3}(np).

By (
2.2
),
\mathrm{E}(|V_{d}-V_{d,0}|^{3})=O(n^{-3/2}\log^{3}(np))
.
According to the proof of Theorem 1 in
Wang et al. 2019
,
\mathrm{E}((W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{4})=O(n^{-2})
and thus

\sum_{d=1}^{n}\mathrm{E}|W_{d}-W_{d,0}|^{3}\leq\sum_{d=1}^{n}\{\mathrm{E}(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{4})\}^{3/4}\leq C^{\prime}n^{-1/2}

for some positive constant
C^{\prime}
,
Combining all facts together, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{n}\left|\mathrm{E}\{f(W_{d},V_{d})\}-\mathrm{E}\{f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})\}\right|\leq C\beta^{2}n^{-1/2}\log^{3}(np)+C^{\prime}n^{-1/2}\to 0,

as
(n,p)\to\infty
. The conclusion follows.

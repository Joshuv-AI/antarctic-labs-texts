The Properties of Radially Excited Charmonia in the Light Front Quark Model
\firstname Muhammad Ridwan, \firstname Ahmad Jafar Arifi, \firstname Terry Mart

Abstract
Investigating the properties of excited charmonia is important to clarify its internal structure. In this paper, we present the mass spectra (MS) and decay constants (DC) for charmonia up to 3 S S states calculated by means of the light-front quark model based on a variational approach. In particular, we consider the QCD-motivated effective Hamiltonian, which includes both confinement (linear and screened) and Coulomb-like potentials. Furthermore, since the existence of the nature of heavy quark symmetry, we treat hyperfine interactions perturbatively. We developed the harmonic oscillator expansion method to approximate the wave function (WF) for excited states. We found that the results of our theoretical calculations, using screened potentials rather than linear ones, are in good agreement with experimental data. By looking at the mass and decay constant result, we found that our result on the ψ \psi (3S) state matched the properties of the ψ \psi (4040) resonance. Keywords: Charmonia, LFQM, Harmonic oscillator expansion method, Mass spectra, Decay cosntant



1
Introduction

Since 50 years ago, Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)
Gross:2022hyw
has been understood as a fundamental theory of strong interaction between quarks and gluon with a non-perturbative nature in low-energy region and asymptotic freedom in high-energy limit.
With the emergence of a connection between QCD and constituent quark model (CQM), QCD becomes the most useful tool for describing hadrons, including baryons (
qqq
) and mesons (
q\bar{q}
).
However, quark models cannot provide a detailed explanation of exotic hadrons, such as the
X
,
Y
, and
Z
states observed in experiments
Hosaka:2016pey
.
Additionally, since the finding of the heavy charmonium state
J/\psi
in 1974
Khare:1999zz
, experimentalists have also identified excited states of charmonium, particularly within the
\psi
family
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
, including
\psi(4040)
,
\psi(4160)
, and
\psi(4415)
.
While numerous unknown hadrons have been confirmed in experiments, theorists are still working to develop suitable models that can explain the properties of hadrons, including some basic properties, namely mass spectra (MS) and decay constants (DC).

Since Dirac proposed the three relativistic dynamics in 1949
Dirac:1949cp
, light-front dynamics (LFD) has emerged as an effective tool for describing hadron structure.
In addition to employing a relativistic framework, LFD also offers distinctive features, including a rational energy-momentum relation and seven time-independent kinematic quantities.
Hence, calculating the properties of all hadrons becomes easier.
In particular, the light-front quark model (LFQM) offers a distinctive way of constructing hadron models by treating hadrons as relativistic bound states, making the model provide reliable predictions for hadron properties.

There have been several efforts to determine the properties of mesons, including the calculation of MS and DC of the ground state
Choi:2015variational
, as well as investigation of the basis expansion effects of
\Phi(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{2}\phi^{HO}_{nS}(k)
in the calculation up to the
2S
state in LFQM
Arifi:2022pal
. The present work is mainly intended to calculate the properties of the
c\bar{c}
charmonia, including MS and DC up to the
3S
states.
In particular, we investigate the effect of the inclusion of additional 3
S
harmonic oscillator (HO) basis
\Phi(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{3}\phi^{\rm HO}_{nS}(k)
in the radial part of the WF.

2
Model Description

In LFQM, our formalism utilizes the hadron state approximation through the leading-order Fock state expansion.
The meson structure is considered at rest as an interacting bound system of effectively dressed quarks
q\bar{q}
, which satisfies the eigenvalue equation of the effective Hamiltonian

H_{q\bar{q}}\ket{\Psi_{q\bar{q}}}=M_{q\bar{q}}\ket{\Psi_{q\bar{q}}}

(1)

where
M_{q\bar{q}}
and
\Psi_{q\bar{q}}
are the mass eigenvalue and eigenfunction, respectively.
The Hamiltonian is given by

H_{q\bar{q}}=H_{0}+V_{q\bar{q}}=\sqrt{m_{q}^{2}+\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}+\sqrt{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}+\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}+V_{q\bar{q}},

(2)

where
H_{0}
is the kinetic energy part of
q\bar{q}
with three-momentum
\boldsymbol{k}
. The effective potential
V_{q\bar{q}}
is given by

V_{q\bar{q}}=V_{\rm Conf}+V_{\rm Coul}+V_{\rm Hyp},

(3)

where we use the linear confinement

V_{\rm Conf}=a+br,

(4)

where
a
and
b
are confinement parameters. The screened effect is given by

V_{\rm Conf}=a+\frac{b(1-{\rm e}^{-\mu r})}{\mu}.

(5)

where
\mu
is the screened parameter. The effective one-gluon exchanges for the
S
-wave mesons are assumed for the Coulomb and hyperfine interaction potentials, i.e.,

V_{\rm Coul}=-\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3r},\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636ptV_{\rm Hyp}=\frac{2\expectationvalue{ \pmb{S}_q \cdot\pmb{S}_{\bar{q}} }}{3m_{q}m_{\bar{q}}}\nabla^{2}V_{\text{Coul}}.

(6)

The coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
in this work is assumed to be a free parameter, whereas the values of
\expectationvalue{ \pmb{S}_q \cdot\pmb{S}_{\bar{q}} }
are
1/4
(
-3/4
) for the vector (pseudoscalar) mesons, respectively. In addition, we consider the
\nabla^{2}V_{\text{Coul.}}
as the Dirac delta function
\delta^{3}(r)
.

The light-front wave function can be expressed as

\Psi^{M}_{q\bar{q}}=\Psi^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q},\lambda_{\bar{q}}}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})=\Phi(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q},\lambda_{\bar{q}}}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}),

(7)

where
\Phi(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
represents the radial wave function, and
\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q},\lambda_{\bar{q}}}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
is the spin-orbit wave function derived from the Melosh transformation.
The covariant forms of
\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q}\lambda_{\bar{q}}}
are given by

\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q}\lambda_{\bar{q}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\tilde{M}_{0}}\bar{u}_{\lambda_{q}}(p_{q})\Gamma_{P(V)}v_{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}(p_{\bar{q}}),

(8)

where
\Gamma_{P(V)}
is the vertex for pseudoscalar(vector) meson,

\Gamma_{P}=\gamma_{5},\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636pt\Gamma_{V}=-\not{\epsilon}(J_{z})+\frac{\epsilon\cdot(p_{q}-p_{\bar{q}})}{M_{0}+m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}}},

(9)

with
\tilde{M}_{0}\equiv\sqrt{M_{0}^{2}-(m_{q}-m_{\bar{q}})^{2}}
.
The polarization vectors
\epsilon^{\mu}(J_{z})=(\epsilon^{+},\epsilon^{-},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\perp})

are given by

\epsilon^{\mu}(\pm 1)=\left(0,\frac{2}{P^{+}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\perp}(\pm)\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\perp}(\pm)\right),\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636pt\epsilon^{\mu}(0)=\frac{1}{M_{0}}\left(P^{+},\frac{-M^{2}_{0}+\boldsymbol{P}^{2}_{\perp}}{P^{+}},\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp}\right),

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\perp}(\pm 1)=\mp\left(1,\pm i\right)/\sqrt{2}
.
Since we only consider the radial part, the spin-orbit part satisfies the unitary condition, i.e.,

\Braket{\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q}\lambda_{\bar{q}}}|\mathcal{R}^{JJ_{z}}_{\lambda_{q}\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}=1
.
The light-front wave function is written in terms of the invariant Lorentz variable
x_{i}=p^{+}_{i}/P^{+}
,
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp i}=\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp i}-x_{i}\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}
, helicity
\lambda_{i}
,
P^{\mu}=(P^{+},P^{-},\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp})
is the meson’s momentum and
p^{\mu}
is the
i
-th (
i
=1,2) constituent quark’s momentum, and define
x\equiv x_{1}
with
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp 1}
. Furthermore, the three-momentum
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{z},\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
can be described as
\boldsymbol{k}=(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
through the relationship,

k_{z}=\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)M_{0}+\frac{m^{2}_{\bar{q}}-m^{2}_{q}}{2M_{0}},

(11)

where

M_{0}^{2}=\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}^{2}+m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}^{2}+m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}

(12)

the boost-invariant meson mass squared is introduced in Eq. (
12
). Note that the normalization is taken into account on our radial part following the variable transformation
\{k_{z},\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}\}\to\{x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}\}
emerges and influence the Jacobian factor
\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}

\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}=\frac{M_{0}}{4x(1-x)}\left[1-\frac{(m_{q}^{2}-m_{\bar{q}}^{2})^{2}}{M_{0}^{4}}\right],

(13)

For the ground state, we use the harmonic oscillator expansion method (HOEM) in our radial wave function as

\displaystyle\phi_{1S}^{H0}(\boldsymbol{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{3/4}\beta^{3/2}}e^{-\boldsymbol{k}^{2}/2\beta^{2}},

(14)

\displaystyle\phi_{2S}^{H0}(\boldsymbol{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2k^{2}-3\beta^{2})}{\sqrt{6}\pi^{3/4}\beta^{7/2}}e^{-\boldsymbol{k}^{2}/2\beta^{2}},

(15)

\displaystyle\phi_{3S}^{H0}(\boldsymbol{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(15\beta^{4}-20\beta^{2}k^{2}+4k^{4})}{2\sqrt{30}\pi^{3/4}\beta^{11/2}}e^{-\boldsymbol{k}^{2}/2\beta^{2}},

(16)

with
k=|\boldsymbol{k}|
and

\phi_{nS}^{H0}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot})=\sqrt{2(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}}\phi_{nS}^{H0}(\boldsymbol{k}),

(17)

whereas
\beta
is the parameter that is inversely proportional to the width of the WF. In this calculation,
\beta
is considered as the variational parameter. The WF
\phi_{nS}
include with the Jacobian factor
\partial k_{z}/\partial x
satisfy the normalization:

\int\frac{\differential x\differential^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}|\phi_{nS}^{H0}(x,\bm{k}_{\bot})|^{2}=1.

(18)

Furthermore, we expand the basis as a linear combination of the
1S
,
2S
, and
3S
HO bases.
It can be written in a matrix form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1S}\\
\Phi_{2S}\\
\Phi_{3S}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}s_{13}&s_{13}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}&c_{12}c_{13}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}&-c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}&c_{23}c_{13}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1S}^{\rm HO}\\
\phi_{2S}^{\rm HO}\\
\phi_{3S}^{\rm HO}\end{pmatrix}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}^{1S}&c_{2}^{1S}&c_{3}^{1S}\\
c_{1}^{2S}&c_{2}^{2S}&c_{3}^{2S}\\
c_{1}^{3S}&c_{2}^{3S}&c_{3}^{3S}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1S}^{\rm HO}\\
\phi_{2S}^{\rm HO}\\
\phi_{3S}^{\rm HO}\end{pmatrix}\,,

with
c_{ij}(s_{ij})=\cos\theta_{ij}(\sin\theta_{ij})
. Note that the matrix form in
R_{M}
we used is quite similar to the CKM matrix
Chau:1984fp
, but we ignore the CP violation terms.
In our LFQM analysis, the heavy mesons (in this case, charmonia) have a number of parameters, i.e., the constituent quark masses (
m_{c}
), the potential parameters
(a,b,\alpha_{s},\mu)
, the HO parameter
\beta_{cc}
for both pseudoscalar and vector charmonia. We begin with determining the parameters’ values by fitting the MS based on the variational calculation

\frac{\partial\bra{\Psi_{q\bar q}}H_{0}\ket{\Psi_{q\bar q}}}{\partial\beta}=0,

(20)

and then computing the other meson properties such as decay constant.
We note that the hyperfine interaction is treated perturbatively.

2.2
Decay Constant

All pseudoscalar(vector) mesons

\displaystyle\bra{0}\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}q\ket{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{P}P^{\mu},

\displaystyle\bra{0}\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}q\ket{V(P,\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{V}M_{V}\epsilon^{\mu}(\lambda),

(28)

where

) simultaneously, we obtain the explicit form of

\displaystyle f_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{6}\int\frac{\differential x\differential^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{{\Phi}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}^{2}+\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}}}~\mathcal{A},

(29)

\displaystyle f_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{6}\int\frac{\differential x\differential^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{{\Phi}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}^{2}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}^{2}}}\left[\mathcal{A}+\frac{2\boldsymbol{k}_{\bot}^{2}}{D_{0}}\right],

First, let us discuss the obtained masses of the charmonia.
Here, we use two confinement potentials separately to study its effect.
While

denotes the mass spectra using linear potential,

We present our prediction along with the experimental value from PDG as well as the results from other theoretical models in Table

We can clearly see that

It is worth noting that the inclusion of the mixing angle in our calculation, whether for

4
Conclusion and Outlook

We have analyzed the properties of charmonia (
\eta_{c},J/\psi
) from the ground state (
1S
) to the second radial excitation (
3S
) using the light-front quark model.
In this study, we calculated their mass spectra along with decay constants by employing the harmonic oscillator expansion method.
Furthermore, we expand the basis as a linear combination of
\phi_{1S},\phi_{2S}
, and
\phi_{3S}
. To obtain the parameters
\beta_{cc},a,b,\alpha_{s},\mu
, and
\theta_{ij}

we carried out the variational analysis using the mass formula we have derived.

For our mass spectra calculation, we consider the QCD-motivated effective Hamiltonian with several potentials such as confinement (linear and screened), Coulomb-like, and hyperfine term.
In this work, we consider the hyperfine potential perturbatively since its contribution is rather small.
Our predictions for mass spectra up to
3S
states with the screened potential are consistent with the experimental data and other theoretical models.
Also, our predictions for decay constants are in good agreement with experimental data and other theoretical models.
We note that
f_{P(V)}^{exp}
are extracted from
\Gamma_{e^{-}e^{+}}
(
\Gamma_{\gamma\gamma}
) for vector (pseudoscalar) mesons.
Interestingly, we find our model can reproduce the hierarchy of decay constant
f_{1S}>f_{2S}>f_{3S}
which is mainly due to the increasing number of the basis functions. If we use a single HO basis function, the hierarchy of the decay constant will be opposite
Arifi:2022pal
.

For future work, we would like to consider other mesons that contain bottom quarks such as
B_{c}^{(*)}
mesons and bottomonia (
\eta_{b},\Upsilon
). Computing other properties such as distribution amplitudes
Arifi:2023uqc
and radiative decays
Choi:2007se
with the obtained light-front wave function is also certainly of interest.
We would like also to apply the smeared function
Hong:2022sht
instead of the Dirac delta function in the spin-spin interaction.
It is important to note that the Harmonic Oscillator (HO) basis function is assumed for the wave function in the present work, but one should study different basis functions further to solve the Hamiltonian that can lead to more accurate predictions.

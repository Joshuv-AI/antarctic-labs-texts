Fate of measurement-induced phase transition in long-range interactions Preprint: APS/123-QED Preprint: APS/123-QED
Takaaki Minato 1 , Koudai Sugimoto 1 , Tomotaka Kuwahara 2 , and Keiji Saito 1

Abstract
We consider quantum many-body dynamics under quantum measurements, where the measurement-induced phase transitions (MIPs) occur when changing the frequency of the measurement. In this work, we consider the robustness of the MIP for long-range interaction that decays as r − α r^{-\alpha} with distance r r . The effects of long-range interactions are classified into two regimes: (i) the MIP is observed ( α > α c ) (\alpha>\alpha_{c}) , and (ii) the MIP is absent even for arbitrarily strong measurements ( α < α c ) (\alpha<\alpha_{c}) . Using fermion models, we demonstrate both regimes in integrable and non-integrable cases. We identify the underlying mechanism and propose sufficient conditions to observe the MIP, that is, α > d / 2 + 1 \alpha>d/2+1 for general bilinear systems and α > d + 1 \alpha>d+1 for general non-integrable systems ( d d : spatial dimension). Numerical calculation indicates that these conditions are optimal.

Analysis based on the entanglement growth rate
.—
Next, we discuss the underlying physical mechanism for the numerical findings in Fig.

\displaystyle H_{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}h_{i,j}\,,

Sufficient condition for the MIP in generic systems
.—
The free fermion model indicates that the behavior of the boundary interaction Hamiltonian is a key component for observing the MIP, as it governs the entanglement growth rate under pure quantum dynamics, and we now use this key component to make a statement applicable to generic systems. We discuss the sufficient conditions to observe the MIP for generic fermion systems. That is, we seek the value
\alpha_{sc}
(
\geq\alpha_{c}
), where for
\alpha>\alpha_{sc}
, the MIP exists for generic many-body quantum systems. To this end, we rigorously derive the following lemma:

Lemma 1

Let us consider a Hamiltonian on the

such that
\|h_{i,j}\|\leq g/r^{\alpha}_{i,j}

Then, under the condition of
\alpha>\alpha_{sc}
with

\displaystyle\alpha_{sc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}${d/2}+1$&~~~{\it Bilinear\, Systems},\\
$d+1$&~~~{\it Interacting\, Systems},\end{tabular}\right.

the operator norm
\|H_{AB}\|
is upper-bounded by the boundary area between
A
and
B
.

See the SM for rigorous proof
sup
. Using this lemma and the underlying physics, we can make a physical statement regarding the existence of the MIP:

Statement 1

\alpha>\alpha_{sc}
is a sufficient condition for the existence of the MIP for generic uniform and non-commuting systems.

Note that the statement is the sufficient condition for any system to have the MIP, and hence, this does not exclude that specific models can show the MIP for

True critical value for a specific model,

.
We consider the r.h.s. in (
S.4
). We first consider the case of bilinear systems. In the bilinear Hamiltonian case, the local Hamiltonian can be generically written as:

\displaystyle h_{i,j}

\displaystyle=(g_{i,j}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}+g_{i,j}^{\prime}c_{i}c_{j}+h.c.)/r_{i,j}^{\alpha},

(S.5)

where
g_{i,j}
and
g_{i,j}^{\prime}
are
O(1)
. Then, we estimate the bound in (
S.4
). To this end, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{i}&:={\mu_{i}}^{-1}\sum_{j\in B}g_{i,j}c_{j}/r_{i,j}^{\alpha}\,,~~~d_{i}^{\prime}:={\mu_{i}^{\prime}}^{-1}\sum_{j\in B}g_{i,j}^{\prime}c_{j}/r_{i,j}^{\alpha},,\\
\mu_{i}&:=\left[\sum_{j\in B}|g_{i,j}|^{2}/r_{i,j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2},~\mu_{i}^{\prime}:=\left[\sum_{j\in B}|g_{i,j}^{\prime}|^{2}/r_{i,j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(S.6)

Then, we can estimate the bound in (
S.4
) as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in A}\|\sum_{j\in B}h_{i,j}\|/{\cal A}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\cal A}^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}\left[\|c_{i}^{\dagger}\sum_{j\in B}g_{i,j}c_{j}/r_{i,j}^{\alpha}\|+\|c_{i}\sum_{j\in B}g_{i,j}^{\prime}c_{j}/r_{i,j}^{\alpha}\|\right]

\displaystyle=2{\cal A}^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}\left[\|c_{i}^{\dagger}\mu_{i}d_{i}\|+\|c_{i}\mu_{i}^{\prime}d_{i}^{\prime}\|\right]\leq 2{\cal A}^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}\mu_{i}+\mu_{i}^{\prime}\leq 4{\cal A}^{-1}g_{\rm max}\sum_{i\in A}\left[\sum_{j\in B}1/r_{i,j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=4{\cal A}^{-1}g_{\rm max}\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\sum_{i:{\rm dist}(i:B)=x}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i,j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=4g_{\rm max}\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2},

(S.7)

where
g_{\rm max}=\max(\{g_{i,j},g_{i,j}^{\prime}\})
,
{\rm dist}(i:B):=\min_{j\in B}r_{i,j}
, and
{\rm dist}(A:j):=\min_{i\in A}r_{i,j}
. The site
i_{0}
is some point in the regime
A
satisfying
{\rm dist}(i_{0}:B)=x
.
We have used
{\cal A}=\sum_{i:{\rm dist}(i:B)=x}=L^{d-1}
, irrespective of
x
.
Finally, we consider the r.h.s. in (
S.7
). For
d=1
, we can estimate the bound as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}1/(x+y)^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}=\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}1/(1+y)^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}+\sum_{x=2}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}1/(x+y)^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\alpha-1)^{-1/2}+(2\alpha-1)^{-1/2}(3/2-\alpha)^{-1}((L/2)^{3/2-\alpha}-1)\,,

(S.8)

where in advance, we assumed
\alpha>1/2
to obtain the final inequality. For
d\geq 2
, we can calculate the r.h.s. in (
S.7
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{1}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}+\sum_{x=2}^{L/2}\left[\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\Gamma_{d}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\xi^{d-2}/\left[(1+y)^{2}+\xi^{2}\right]^{\alpha}\right]^{1/2}\!\!\!\!+\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[\Gamma_{d}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\xi^{d-2}/\left[(x+y+1)^{2}+\xi^{2}\right]^{\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left[(\Gamma_{d}B/2)\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}(y+1)^{d-1-2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}+\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\left[(\Gamma_{d}B/2)\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}(x+y+1)^{d-1-2\alpha}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(\Gamma_{d}B/2)/(2\alpha-d)\right]^{1/2}+\left[(\Gamma_{d}B/2)/(2\alpha-d)\right]^{1/2}\left[(L/2)^{-\alpha+d/2+1}-1\right]/(-\alpha+d/2+1)\,,

(S.9)

where
\Gamma_{d}=2
for
d=2
and
2\pi
for
d=3
, which is the coefficient of the
(d-1)
-dimensional spherical surface area, and
B
is the beta function,
B(-1/2+d/2,\alpha+1/2-d/2)
. In the calculation, we assumed
2\alpha-d>0
in advance. Thus, the bilinear case in (
I
) is proven.

In generic Hamiltonian systems, we cannot use the trick (
S.6
); hence, we calculate the bound as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in A}\|\sum_{j\in B}h_{i,j}\|/{\cal A}

\displaystyle\leq g{\cal A}^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}1/r_{i,j}^{\alpha}\leq g\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{\alpha}\,.

(S.10)

For the
d=1
case, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}1/(x+y)^{\alpha}\leq 1/(\alpha-1)+\left[(L/2)^{-\alpha+2}-1\right]/\left[(\alpha-1)(-\alpha+2)\right]\,,

(S.11)

where, in advance, we have assumed
\alpha-1>0
. For
d\geq 2
, the bound is computed as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{1}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{\alpha}+\sum_{x=2}^{L/2}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\sum_{j:{\rm dist}(A:j)=y}1/r_{i_{0},j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{d}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\xi^{d-2}/\left[(1+y)^{2}+\xi^{2}\right]^{\alpha/2}+\sum_{x=1}^{L/2}\Gamma_{d}\sum_{y=1}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\xi^{d-2}/\left[(x+y+1)^{2}+\xi^{2}\right]^{\alpha/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{d}B^{\prime}/\left[2(\alpha-d)\right]+\Gamma_{d}B^{\prime}/\left[2(\alpha-d)\right]\left[(L/2)^{-\alpha+d+1}-1\right]/(-\alpha+d+1)\,,

(S.12)

where
\Gamma_{d}
is the same as in the bilinear case, and
B^{\prime}
is the beta function
B^{\prime}=B((d-1)/2,(\alpha-d+1)/2)
. In the calculation, we assumed
\alpha-d>0
in advance. Thus, the condition for generic systems in (
I
) is proven.

II
Numerical method for free fermion systems

Let
L
be the size of a one-dimensional lattice, and let
N
be the total number of fermions. We consider the free fermion system as:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{r=1}^{L/2}{1\over r^{\alpha}}\left[-c_{j+r}^{\dagger}c_{j}-c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+r}\right]\,.

(S.13)

Because the Hamiltonian is bilinear, the many-body wave function is always cast in the following form:

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{L}u_{j,m}(t)\,c_{j}^{\dagger}\,|{\rm vac}\rangle,

(S.14)

where
|{\rm vac}\rangle
is the vacuum state.
The relation
\sum_{j=1}^{L}u_{j,m}^{\ast}u_{j,m^{\prime}}=\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}
guarantees the normalization
\langle\psi(t)|\psi(t)\rangle=1
because
b_{m}^{\dagger}:=\sum_{j=1}^{L}u_{j,m}c_{j}^{\dagger}
defines a new fermion operator satisfying the anti-commutation relations
\left\{b_{m},b_{m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right\}=\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}
and
\left\{b_{m},b_{m^{\prime}}\right\}=0

The correlation function
\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle:=\langle\psi(t)|c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}|\psi(t)\rangle
is computed using the
L\times N
matrix
{\bm{u}}
. Note that
\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}-\langle\psi(t)|c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i}^{\dagger}|\psi(t)\rangle
, and
b_{0}^{\prime}=c_{i^{\prime}}
and
b_{0}^{\dagger}=c_{i}^{\dagger}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\rm vac}|b_{N}b_{N-1}\cdots b_{1}b_{0}^{\prime}b_{0}^{\dagger}b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{N-1}^{\dagger}b_{N}^{\dagger}|{\rm vac}\rangle=\det{\bm{A}}\,,

(S.15)

\displaystyle A_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle{\rm vac}|b_{0}^{\prime}b_{j}^{\dagger}|{\rm vac}\rangle&~~~i=0,~\&~j=0,\cdots,N\\
\langle{\rm vac}|b_{i}b_{j}^{\dagger}|{\rm vac}\rangle&~~~{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

where
{\bm{A}}
is the
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix. Note that
\langle{\rm vac}|b_{i}b_{j}^{\dagger}|{\rm vac}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
for
i,j=1,\cdots,N
. Then, after simple algebra using elementary mathematical properties on the determinant, we obtain the relation

\displaystyle\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{N}u_{i,m}u_{i^{\prime},m}^{\ast}\,.

(S.19)

The entanglement entropy is computed following the standard argument
Eisert et al. 2010
, once the correlation matrix is obtained. Suppose that we consider subsystem
A
consisting of
\ell
sites
j=1,\cdots,\ell
. Let
\rho_{A}
be a reduced density matrix for subsystem
A
, which is generically written in the following Gaussian form:

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}a_{m}d_{m}^{\dagger}d_{m}\right)/\prod_{m=1}^{\ell}(1+e^{-a_{m}})\,,

(S.20)

where
d_{m}
is a fermion operator connected to the original fermion operator
\{c_{j}\}
through a unitary transform
d_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{m,j}c_{j}
. Then, the entanglement entropy
S_{\ell}
is given as

\displaystyle S_{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\bigl[\langle d_{m}^{\dagger}d_{m}\rangle\log\langle d_{m}^{\dagger}d_{m}\rangle.+\langle d_{m}d_{m}^{\dagger}\rangle\log\langle d_{m}d_{m}^{\dagger}\rangle\bigr]\,,

(S.21)

where
\langle d_{m}^{\dagger}d_{m}\rangle
can be computed by diagonalizing the correlation matrix
{\bm{D}}
, the element of which is given by
D_{i,j}=\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}\rangle

(i,j=1,\cdots,\ell)
.

We explain the protocol for computing the time evolution of the wave function
\psi(t)
, that is, equivalently, the time evolution of the
L\times N
matrix
{\bm{u}}(t)

Alberton et al. 2021
.
(i): We determine the jump time
\tau=-\log(r)/(\gamma N)
with a uniformly distributed random number
r\in[0,1]
. (ii): During the time duration
\tau
, the unitary time evolution is performed with the one-particle Hamiltonian
{\bm{h}}
for the matrix
{\bm{u}}
as
{\bm{u}}(t+\tau)=e^{-i{\bm{h}}\tau}{\bm{u}}(t)
. (iii): Site
j
is measured according to the probability
P_{j}=\langle\psi(t+\tau)|c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}|\psi(t+\tau)\rangle/N
. (iv): The post-measurement state
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
after the measurement of site
j
is formally given as

\displaystyle|\psi^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle={c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}|\psi\rangle\over\sqrt{\langle c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}\rangle}}.

(S.22)

Using Wick’s theorem, one finds the relation for the correlation function

\displaystyle D_{i,i^{\prime}}:=\langle\psi^{\prime}|c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}|\psi^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1&~~~i=i^{\prime}=j\\
0&~~~(i=j,i^{\prime}\neq i)~{\rm or}~(i\neq i^{\prime},i^{\prime}=j)\\
\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i^{\prime}}\rangle\langle c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}\rangle/\langle c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}\rangle&~~~{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right..

The new matrix
{\bm{u}}
is obtained through an SVD decomposition
{\bm{D}}={\bm{u}}{\bm{S}}{\bm{u}}^{\dagger}
, where
S_{i,i}=1~(1\leq i\leq N)
and
0~(N+1\leq i\leq L)
.

1.
Introduction

A symplectic groupoid over a Poisson manifold
M
is a geometric object which is a heuristic semiclassical counterpart of an algebra of quantum observables on
M
. Symplectic groupoids were first introduced by Karasev
[
9
]
, Weinstein
[
11
]
, and Zakrzewski
[
12
]
. Symplectic groupoids can be treated as a starting point for quantization (see
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
).

In this paper we construct local symplectic groupoids over almost para-Kähler manifolds. These groupoids are based on an additional geometric structure, a Lagrangian field, which is a generalization of a Lagrangian polarization. A Lagrangian field on a symplectic manifold
M
is a family
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{x}|x\in M\}
of pointed Lagrangian submanifolds of
M
(with
x\in\Lambda_{x}
). It induces a Lagrangian distribution
\{T_{x}\Lambda_{x},x\in M\}
on
M
which is not necessarily integrable.

Two Lagrangian fields
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\Lambda}
are called transversal if
\Lambda_{x}
intersects
\tilde{\Lambda}_{x}
transversally at
x
for every
x\in M
. The corresponding transversal Lagrangian distributions determine an almost para-Kähler structure on
M
.

We construct a local symplectic groupoid on a neighborhood of the zero section of
T^{\ast}M
from two transversal Lagrangian fields on
M
. Such groupoids have a rich geometric structure. We expect that they can be used for quantization of almost para-Kähler manifolds.

2.
General facts on groupoids

In this section we give basic facts on groupoids and fix various conventions that will be used in the rest of the paper.

A groupoid
\Sigma\rightrightarrows M
is a small category with the set of objects
M
and the set of morphisms
\Sigma
, where all morphisms are invertible. The injective unit mapping
\epsilon:M\to\Sigma
maps an object
x
to the identity morphism
\mathrm{id}_{x}:x\to x
. The set
\epsilon(M)
of the identity morphisms in
\Sigma
is called the set of units of the groupoid. The source and target mappings
s,t:\Sigma\to M
map a morphism
\alpha:x\to y
to
s(\alpha)=x
and
t(\alpha)=y
. The involutive inverse mapping
i:\Sigma\to\Sigma
maps
\alpha\in\Sigma
to
i(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}
. We will write the composition of
\alpha:y\to z
and
\beta:x\to y
as
\alpha\beta:x\to z
. Two morphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
are thus composable if
s(\alpha)=t(\beta)
. An element
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\Sigma^{n}
is an
n
-cycle in
\Sigma
if the composition of the elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\Sigma
is defined and equals an identity morphism,

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{x},

where
x=s(\alpha_{n})=t(\alpha_{1})
. We denote the set of
n
-cycles in
\Sigma
by
\Sigma_{n}
. So, the graph of
i
is
\Sigma_{2}
.

The set of objects of every groupoid in this paper is denoted by
M
. We denote the diagonal of the set
M^{n}
by
M_{n}
, which should not lead to confusion

1

This notation agrees with that for the set of
n
-cycles if
M
is treated as the identity groupoid where every morphism is an identity.
.

Example
The pair groupoid of a set

has the set of objects
M
and the set of morphisms
M^{2}
with
\epsilon(x)=(x,x),i(x,y)=(y,x),s(x,y)=y

. The set of units is the diagonal
{M_{2}}\subset M^{2}
. The composition of

. An

\alpha_{i}:x_{i+1}\to x_{i}

A groupoid
\Sigma\rightrightarrows M
is a Lie groupoid if

and
\Sigma
are manifolds, all structure mappings are smooth, the unit mapping is an embedding, and the source and target mappings are surjective submersions. A local Lie groupoid has the same properties as a Lie groupoid, but in order for two elements to be composable they should also be close to each other (see

A symplectic groupoid
\Sigma\rightrightarrows M
is a Lie groupoid where
\Sigma
is a symplectic manifold, the space of units
\epsilon(M)
is Lagrangian,

\Sigma_{3}
of 3-cycles is a Lagrangian submanifold of
\Sigma^{3}

In the conventional definition of a symplectic groupoid an equivalent condition is used: the product space

\bar{\Sigma}

. There exists a unique Poisson structure on

such that

If

If

Given a symplectic manifold

3.
A local symplectic groupoid generated by two transversal Lagrangian fields

Below we give a technical definition of a Lagrangian field on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
of dimension
2m
.

Definition 3.1
.

A Lagrangian field
\Lambda=(E,\lambda)
on
M
is a vector bundle
\pi:E\to M
of rank
m
equipped with a surjective submersion
\lambda:E\to M
whose restriction to each fiber
E_{x},x\in M
, is an embedding onto a Lagrangian submanifold
\Lambda_{x}:=\lambda(E_{x})
of
M
and such that
\lambda=\pi
on the zero section
Z_{E}
of
E
.

If
z_{x}
is the unique element of
Z_{E}\cap E_{x}
, we have
\lambda(z_{x})=\pi(z_{x})=x
, whence
x\in\Lambda_{x}
for all
x\in M
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a unique horizontal 1-form
\phi
on the total space of
E
which vanishes on
Z_{E}
and satisfies the equation

(1)

\lambda^{\ast}\omega-\pi^{\ast}\omega=d\phi.

Proof.

Let
F:[0,1]\times E\to M
be the homotopy
F(t,z)=\lambda(t\cdot z)
between the mappings
\pi
and
\lambda
, where
z\mapsto t\cdot z
is the scalar multiplication by
t
on the fibers of
E
. Denote by
i_{t}:E\to[0,1]\times E
the inclusion mapping
i_{t}(z)=(t,z)
. We will show that the form

\phi:=\int_{0}^{1}i^{\ast}_{t}\iota_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}F^{\ast}\omega\,dt,

where
\iota_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}
is an insertion operator, satisfies the conditions of the lemma. Equality (
1
) holds by the homotopy formula. For any
x\in M
, the mapping
F
maps
[0,1]\times E_{x}
onto the Lagrangian manifold
\Lambda_{x}
, whence

F^{\ast}\omega|_{[0,1]\times E_{x}}=0.

If
z\in Z_{E}
, then
F
maps
[0,1]\times\{z\}
to the point
\pi(z)
. It implies that the form
i^{\ast}_{t}\iota_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}F^{\ast}\omega
on
E
is horizontal and its restriction to
Z_{E}
vanishes. Hence, the same holds true for the 1-form
\phi
.

Let
\psi
be a closed horizontal 1-form on
E
vanishing on
Z_{E}
. If
\eta
is a vertical vector field on
E
, then
\iota_{\eta}\psi=0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\psi=0
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
\eta
. It follows that
\psi
is the pullback of a 1-form on
M
by
\pi
. Since the restriction of
\psi
to
Z_{E}
vanishes, we see that
\psi=0
, which implies the uniqueness of
\phi
. Thus, the 1-form
\phi
satisfies all conditions of the lemma.
∎

Let
\Lambda=(E,\lambda)
and
\tilde{\Lambda}=(\tilde{E},\tilde{\lambda})
be two Lagrangian fields on
M
. We denote the bundle projection for
\tilde{E}
by
\tilde{\pi}
, the zero section by
Z_{\tilde{E}}
, the unique element of
Z_{\tilde{E}}\cap\tilde{E}_{x}
by
\tilde{z}_{x}
, and set
\tilde{\Lambda}_{x}:=\tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{E}_{x})
for each
x\in M
. According to Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique horizontal 1-form
\tilde{\phi}
on
\tilde{E}
vanishing on
Z_{\tilde{E}}
and satisfying

(2)

\tilde{\lambda}^{\ast}\omega-\tilde{\pi}^{\ast}\omega=d\tilde{\phi}.

Consider the vector bundle

\hat{E}:=E\oplus\tilde{E}=\{(z,\tilde{z})\in E\times\tilde{E}|\pi(z)=\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{z})\}.

The bundle projection
\hat{\pi}:\hat{E}\to M
is such that
\hat{\pi}(z,\tilde{z})=\pi(z)=\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{z})
. We introduce projections
\tau:\hat{E}\to E
and
\tilde{\tau}:\hat{E}\to\tilde{E}
given by
\tau(z,\tilde{z})=z
and
\tilde{\tau}(z,\tilde{z})=\tilde{z}
. Then
\hat{\pi}=\pi\tau=\tilde{\pi}\tilde{\tau}
. We get the following pullbacks of (
1
) and (
2
) by
\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
, respectively:

\tau^{\ast}\lambda^{\ast}\omega-\hat{\pi}^{\ast}\omega=d\tau^{\ast}\phi\mbox{ and }\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\lambda}^{\ast}\omega-\hat{\pi}^{\ast}\omega=d\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\phi}.

Subtracting these equations, we get that

(3)

\tau^{\ast}\lambda^{\ast}\omega-\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\lambda}^{\ast}\omega=-d(\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\phi}-\tau^{\ast}\phi).

The 1-form
\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\phi}-\tau^{\ast}\phi
is horizontal on
\hat{E}
and vanishes on the zero section
Z_{\hat{E}}
. It determines a fiberwise nonlinear mapping

\chi:\hat{E}\to T^{\ast}M

which maps a point
(z,\tilde{z})\in\hat{E}_{x}
to the unique element
\chi(z,\tilde{z})\in T^{\ast}_{x}M
whose pullback by
\hat{\pi}
is

(d\hat{\pi}_{(z,\tilde{z})})^{\ast}\chi(z,\tilde{z})=(\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\phi}-\tau^{\ast}\phi)(z,\tilde{z})\in T^{\ast}_{(z,\tilde{z})}\hat{E}.

The mapping
\chi
identifies the zero sections
Z_{\hat{E}}
and
Z_{T^{\ast}M}
as copies of
M
.

Let
\theta
be the canonical 1-form on
T^{\ast}M
. If
\{x^{i}\}
are local coordinates on
M
and
\{\xi_{i}\}
are the dual fiber coordinates on
T^{\ast}M
, then
\theta=\xi_{i}dx^{i}
, where we use summation over repeated lower and upper indices. We have

\chi^{\ast}\theta=\tilde{\tau}^{\ast}\tilde{\phi}-\tau^{\ast}\phi.

Thus, the left-hand side of (
3
) is equal to the pullback of the canonical symplectic form
-d\theta
on
T^{\ast}M
by the mapping
\chi
.

Definition 3.2
.

Lagrangian fields
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\Lambda}
on
M
are called transversal if
\Lambda_{x}
transversally intersects
\tilde{\Lambda}_{x}
at

for all
x\in M
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\Lambda}
be two transversal Lagrangian fields on
M
.

(i)

There exists a neighborhood
W_{1}
of
Z_{\hat{E}}
in
\hat{E}
such that
\chi|_{W_{1}}
is a diffeomorphism of
W_{1}
onto a

Z_{T^{\ast}M}
in
T^{\ast}M
.

(ii)

There exists a neighborhood
W_{2}
of
Z_{\hat{E}}
in
\hat{E}
such that the mapping

\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\tau}\times\lambda\tau|_{W_{2}}:(z,\tilde{z})\mapsto(\tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{z}),\lambda(z))

is a diffeomorphism of
W_{2}
onto a neighborhood of the diagonal
M_{2}
in
M^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
{x_{0}}
be an arbitrary point in
M
. Since
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
identify
Z_{E}
and
Z_{\tilde{E}}
, respectively, with

, there exists a coordinate chart
(\hat{U},\{x^{i}\})

{x_{0}}
, trivializations

of

\tilde{E}
over a neighborhood

{x_{0}}
in
\hat{U}
, and an open ball

\lambda(U\times B)\subset\hat{U}
and
\tilde{\lambda}(U\times B)\subset\hat{U}
. We denote the coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by
\{v^{\alpha}\}
and identify
U\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with coordinate charts on
E
and
\tilde{E}
. Then locally a point in
E
or
\tilde{E}
can be written as
(x,v)
. The transversality of the Lagrangian fields
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\Lambda}
means that the
2m\times 2m
-matrix

(4)

\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\partial\lambda^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&\frac{\partial{\tilde{\lambda}}^{i}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\end{bmatrix}

is nondegenerate for all
x\in U
. These trivializations of
E
and
\tilde{E}
induce a trivialization
U\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
\hat{E}
over
U
. A point
(z,\tilde{z})\in\hat{E}
with
z=(x,v)
and
\tilde{z}=(x,w)
will have the coordinates
(x,v,w)
.

To prove (i), consider the neighborhood
U\times B\times B
in
\hat{E}
and let
\{\xi_{i}\}
be the fiber coordinates on
T^{\ast}U
dual to
\{x^{i}\}
.
If
\phi=\phi_{i}(x,v)dx^{i}
on
U\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\approx E|_{U}
and
\tilde{\phi}=\tilde{\phi}_{i}(x,v)dx^{i}
on
U\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\approx\tilde{E}|_{U}
, the mapping
\chi
will be written in coordinates as

x^{i}=x^{i}\quad\xi_{j}={\tilde{\phi}}_{j}(x,w)-\phi_{j}(x,v).

The Jacobian of this mapping is

\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{i}_{k}&0&0\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{j}}{\partial x^{k}}(x,w)-\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial x^{k}}(x,v)&-\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,v)&\frac{\partial{\tilde{\phi}}_{j}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,w)\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\phi_{i}(x,0)=0
and
\tilde{\phi}_{i}(x,0)=0
, at the point
(x,0,0)
the Jacobian is equal to

(5)

\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{i}_{k}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&\frac{\partial{\tilde{\phi}}_{j}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\end{bmatrix}.

We will show that the matrix

(6)

\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&\frac{\partial{\tilde{\phi}}_{j}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\end{bmatrix}

is nondegenerate. In local coordinates we write

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{ij}(x)dx^{i}\wedge dx^{j}

and get from (
1
) that

\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}=\omega_{ij}(\lambda)\frac{\partial\lambda^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial\lambda^{j}}{\partial x^{k}}.

It follows from the equality

that

\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)=\frac{\partial\lambda^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)\omega_{ik}(x).

Similarly,

\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{k}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)=\frac{\partial\tilde{\lambda}^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)\omega_{ik}(x).

Therefore, we have the following matrix product representation of (
6
),

\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{k}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\partial\lambda^{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&\frac{\partial\tilde{\lambda}^{i}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{ik}(x)\end{bmatrix}.

Since the matrix (
4
) is nondegenerate, we see that (
6
) is also nondegenerate, which implies that the Jacobian (
5
) is nondegenerate as well.
Now part (i) of the lemma follows from the fact that the mapping
\chi
identifies
Z_{\hat{E}}
with
Z_{T^{\ast}M}
as copies of
M
and from the generalized inverse function theorem.

To prove part (ii), consider a neighborhood
U\times B\times B
as in part (i) and the mapping

(7)

(x,v,w)\mapsto(\tilde{\lambda}(x,w),\lambda(x,v))

from
U\times B\times B
to
M\times M
. It maps
(x,0,0)
to
(x,x)
. Its Jacobian at

is

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial\tilde{\lambda}^{i}}{\partial x^{k}}(x,w)&0&\frac{\partial\tilde{\lambda}^{i}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,w)\\
\frac{\partial\lambda^{j}}{\partial x^{k}}(x,v)&\frac{\partial\lambda^{j}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,v)&0\end{bmatrix}.

At the point
(x,0,0)
the Jacobian is equal to

\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{i}_{k}&0&\frac{\partial\tilde{\lambda}^{i}}{\partial v^{\beta}}(x,0)\\
\delta^{j}_{k}&\frac{\partial\lambda^{j}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}(x,0)&0\end{bmatrix}.

It is nondegenerate because (
4
) is nondegenerate. Part (ii) also follows from the generalized inverse function theorem.

4.
The intersection mapping
\gamma

In this section we will show that for two transversal Lagrangian fields
\Lambda=(E,\lambda)
and
\tilde{\Lambda}=(\tilde{E},\tilde{\lambda})
on
M
there exists a neighborhood
V_{\Gamma}
of the diagonal
M_{2}
in
M^{2}
and a mapping
\gamma:V_{\Gamma}\to M
such that
\gamma(x,y)\in\Lambda_{x}\cap\tilde{\Lambda}_{y}
for all
(x,y)\in V_{\Gamma}
. We will also show that
\Omega:=\gamma^{\ast}\omega
is a closed (1,1)-form with respect to the product structure on

.

Consider the surjective submersion
(z,\tilde{z})\mapsto(\lambda(z),\tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{z}))
of

onto

. The inverse image of the diagonal
M_{2}
of

is a
4m

\Gamma:=\{(z,\tilde{z})\in E\times\tilde{E}|\lambda(z)=\tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{z})\}

of
E\times\tilde{E}
. Because
\lambda(z)=\pi(z)
for

and
\tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{z})=\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{z})
for
\tilde{z}\in Z_{\tilde{E}}
, the zero section
Z_{\hat{E}}=\{(z,\tilde{z})\in Z_{E}\times Z_{\tilde{E}}|\pi(z)=\tilde{\pi}(z)\}
of

is also a submanifold of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If

5.

In

\alpha\circ(\beta|_{V^{\prime\prime}})=\epsilon\gamma|_{V^{\prime\prime}}\mbox{ and }\beta\circ(\alpha|_{V^{\prime}})=\epsilon\gamma|_{V^{\prime}}.

Let
{x_{0}}
be an arbitrary point in
M
and
U
be a contractible neighborhood of
{x_{0}}
in
M
. One can shrink
U
around
{x_{0}}
so that if
\alpha^{\ast}
is applied to (
14
), it will follow from (
30
) and (
31
) that

\alpha^{\ast}\Omega=\alpha^{\ast}\beta^{\ast}\Omega=\Omega

on
U^{2}
. Similarly, we can assume that
\beta^{\ast}\Omega=\Omega
on
U^{2}
. Let
\Phi
be a potential of
\Omega
on
U
, so that

\Omega=d_{x}d_{y}\Phi=dd_{y}\Phi,

where
d=d_{x}+d_{y}
is the decomposition of the exterior derivative with respect to the product structure on
M^{2}
. Shrinking
U
further, we can assume that
\Phi(\gamma(x,y),y),\Phi(x,\gamma(x,y))
, and
\Phi(\gamma(x,y),\gamma(x,y))
are defined for all
x,y\in U
. We obtain from
\alpha^{\ast}\Omega=\Omega
that

d(\alpha^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi-d_{y}\Phi)=0.

It is easy to check that the closed form
\alpha^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi-d_{y}\Phi
is of type (0,1) with respect to the product structure on
U^{2}
and vanishes on the diagonal of
U^{2}
. Therefore, it vanishes identically on
U^{2}
. Similarly, the form
\beta^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi-d_{x}\Phi
also vanishes on
U^{2}
. Thus,

(32)

\alpha^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi=d_{y}\Phi\mbox{ and }\beta^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi=d_{x}\Phi

on
U^{2}
. It follows from (
31
) that

T(x,\gamma(x,y),y)=\Phi-\alpha^{\ast}\Phi-\beta^{\ast}\Phi+\alpha^{\ast}\beta^{\ast}\Phi

for all
x,y\in U
. Differentiating this equality, we get from (
32
) that

\displaystyle d(T(x,\gamma(x,y),y))=d_{x}\Phi-\alpha^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi-\beta^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi+\alpha^{\ast}\beta^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi+

\displaystyle d_{y}\Phi-\alpha^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi-\beta^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi+\alpha^{\ast}\beta^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi=

\displaystyle-\alpha^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi+\alpha^{\ast}\beta^{\ast}d_{x}\Phi-\beta^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi+\beta^{\ast}\alpha^{\ast}d_{y}\Phi=

\displaystyle-\alpha^{\ast}(d_{x}\Phi-\beta^{\ast}\Phi)-\beta^{\ast}(d_{y}\Phi-\alpha^{\ast}\Phi)=0.

Since the function
T(x,\gamma(x,y),y)
vanishes on the diagonal of
U^{2}
and is constant, it vanishes identically on
U^{2}
.
Since
U^{2}
is a neighborhood of the diagonal point
({x_{0}},{x_{0}})\in M_{2}
in
M^{2}
and
{x_{0}}\in M
is arbitrary, we arrive at the following statement.

Lemma 7.2
.

There exists a neighborhood
V_{0}
of
M_{2}
in
V_{\Gamma}
such that
(x,\gamma(x,y),y)\in W_{T}
and the

T(x,\gamma(x,y),y)=0
holds for any
(x,y)\in V_{0}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

There exists a neighborhood
V_{1}
of
M_{2}
in
V_{\Gamma}
such that
(x,\gamma(x,y),y)\in W_{T}

and the point
\gamma(x,y)
is a nondegenerate critical point of the function
u\mapsto T(x,u,y)
for any
(x,y)\in V_{1}
.

Proof.

Assume that
U
is a neighborhood of an arbitrary point
{x_{0}}
in
M
such that
U^{2}\subset V^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime\prime}
, where
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
are as in Lemma
7.1
. We additionally assume that
U
lies in a contractible coordinate chart
(\hat{U},\{x^{i}\})
, is such that
\gamma(U^{2})\subset\hat{U}
, and
\Phi
is a potential of the form
\Omega
on
\hat{U}^{2}
. It will be convenient to denote
\gamma(x,y)
by
\bar{\gamma}
. It follows from (
32
) that in coordinates

(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(x,\bar{\gamma})=(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(x,y)\mbox{ and }(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},y)=(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(x,y).

Applying
\alpha^{\ast}
to the first identity and
\beta^{\ast}
to the second one, we get from Lemma
7.1
that

(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},\bar{\gamma})=(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},y)\mbox{ and }(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},\bar{\gamma})=(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(x,\bar{\gamma}).

Using these identities, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{i}}T(x,u,y)|_{u=\bar{\gamma}}=(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(x,\bar{\gamma})-(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},\bar{\gamma})-

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},\bar{\gamma})+(\partial^{1}_{i}\Phi)(\bar{\gamma},y)=0.

Thus,
\bar{\gamma}=\gamma(x,y)
is a critical point of the function
u\mapsto T(x,u,y)
for all
x,y\in U
. Now we will show that there exists a neighborhood
U_{1}
of
{x_{0}}
in
U
such that this critical point is nondegenerate for all
x,y\in U_{1}
. Writing the form
\Omega
on
U
in local coordinates as

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{kl}(\gamma)d\gamma^{k}\wedge d\gamma^{l},

we obtain from Lemma
4.2
that

(33)

\omega_{kl}(\gamma)\frac{\partial\gamma^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial\gamma^{l}}{\partial x^{j}}=0\mbox{ and }\omega_{kl}(\gamma)\frac{\partial\gamma^{k}}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\gamma^{l}}{\partial y^{j}}=0.

Consider the matrices

A:=((\partial^{1}_{i}\gamma^{k})(x,x)),B:=((\partial^{2}_{i}\gamma^{k})(x,x)),\mbox{ and }C:=(\omega_{kl}(x)),

where
x\in U
. Since
\gamma(x,x)=x
, we get that
A+B=1
(the identity matrix). We derive from (
33
) that

A^{t}CA=0\mbox{ and }B^{t}CB=0,

whence it follows that

C=A^{t}C+CA.

Therefore,
B=C^{-1}A^{t}C
and
BA=C^{-1}(A^{t}CA)=0
, which implies that
A
and
B
are complementary projectors.

Remark
It can be seen from (
33
) that for a fixed
x
both projectors
A
and
B
are of rank
m
.

We have that

\Omega=\omega_{kl}(\gamma)\frac{\partial\gamma^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial\gamma^{l}}{\partial y^{j}}dx^{i}\wedge dy^{j}=(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,y)dx^{i}\wedge dy^{j}

on
U
. Therefore,

\omega_{kl}(\gamma)\frac{\partial\gamma^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial\gamma^{l}}{\partial y^{j}}=(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,y).

Setting
y=x
, we get

(34)

A^{t}CB=((\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)),

whence

(35)

B^{t}CA=-((\partial^{1}_{j}\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(x,x)).

We want to show that if
x
and
y
are close, then the critical point
u=\gamma(x,y)
of the function
u\mapsto T(x,u,y)
is nondegenerate, that is, the Hessian matrix

((\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}T)(x,\gamma(x,y),y))

is nondegenerate. Here
\partial^{2}_{i}
is the partial derivative with respect to
u^{i}
. It suffices to show that it holds true if
x=y
, that is, the matrix

((\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}T)(x,x,x))

is nondegenerate. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}T(x,u,y)}{\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}}=(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,u)-(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(u,u)-(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(u,u)-

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(u,u)-(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(u,u)+(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(u,y),

whence

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}T)(x,x,x)=(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)-(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)-(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)-

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)-(\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)+(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{1}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)=

\displaystyle-(\partial^{1}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}\Phi)(x,x)-(\partial^{1}_{j}\partial^{2}_{i}\Phi)(x,x).

Using that
A+B=1
, we get from (
34
) and (
35
) that

\displaystyle((\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}T)(x,x,x))=-A^{t}CB+B^{t}CA=

\displaystyle-A^{t}C+A^{t}CA+CA-A^{t}CA=-A^{t}C+CA=

\displaystyle C(A-C^{-1}A^{t}C)=C(A-B).

Since
A
and
B
are complementary projectors,
A-B
is invertible. Therefore, the Hessian matrix
((\partial^{2}_{i}\partial^{2}_{j}T)(x,x,x))
is nondegenerate.

∎

.

There exists a neighborhood

Discussion

The geometric objects considered in this paper are germs of real smooth submanifolds of a vector bundle containing its zero section or of
M^{n}
containing the diagonal
M_{n}
. One can generalize the results of this paper in two possible ways. Instead of fields of real Lagrangian submanifolds, one can consider fields of Hörmander’s complex Lagrangian ideals. We expect that then one can use germs of complex almost analytic manifolds introduced in
[
10
]
in order to construct a local symplectic groupoid over an almost-Kähler manifold from two transversal fields of Lagrangian ideals. For this groupoid, the form
\Omega
, its local potentials, and the corresponding Calabi functions will be complex-valued. We expect that this groupoid should correspond to Berezin quantization via covariant symbols on general symplectic manifolds (see
[
4
]
and references therein). One can also consider formal symplectic groupoids introduced in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
, where germs of submanifolds are replaced with their
\infty
-jets. Such formal symplectic groupoids over almost-Kähler manifolds were constructed in
[
7
]
.

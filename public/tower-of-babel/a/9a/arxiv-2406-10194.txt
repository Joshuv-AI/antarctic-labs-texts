Entanglement entropy bounds for pure states of rapid decorrelation
Michael Aizenman 1 and Simone Warzel 2,3 1 Departments of Physics and Mathematics, Princeton University, Princeton, USA 2 Departments of Mathematics and Physics, TU München, Garching, Germany 3 Munich Center for Quantum Science and Technology, Germany

Abstract




\minisec

Abstract
For pure states of multi-dimensional quantum lattice systems, which in a convenient computational basis have amplitude and phase structure of sufficiently rapid decorrelation, we construct high fidelity approximations of relatively low complexity. These are used for
a conditional proof of area-law bounds for the states’ entanglement entropy. The condition is also shown to imply exponential decay of the state’s mutual information between disjoint regions,
and hence exponential clustering of local observables.
The applicability of the general results is demonstrated on the
quantum Ising model in transverse field. Combined with available model-specific information on spin-spin correlations, we establish an area-law type bound on the entanglement in the model’s subcritical ground states, valid in all dimensions and up to the model’s quantum phase transition.

1
Introduction

A striking feature of the quantum entropy, in which it differs from its classical analog,
is that in multicomponent systems, the entropy of a state can be zero even when that of its restriction to a subsystem is strictly positive.
In such cases the entropy of the reduced state is a measure of the quantum entanglement between the subsystem and the rest
[
44
,
54
]
.
One of our goals here is to present conditional decoupling criteria for an
area-law type bound on the entanglement entropy of quantum states in general space dimensions.

1.1
Entanglement entropy

Our discussion is set in the context of quantum lattice systems whose single site components are qudits, of state space associated with the complex Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
.
The extended system’s Hilbert space is the tensor-product
\mathcal{H}_{W}:=\otimes_{u\in W}\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
, indexed by sites of a finite subset
W\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For each choice of a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
, of
\nu
-elements whose collection we denote
\Omega_{0}
, there is a corresponding orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{W}
. Its elements
|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle
are labeled by

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{u})_{u\in W}\in\Omega_{0}^{W}=:\Omega_{W}
,
configurations of single-site variables with values in
\Omega_{0}
.

The composite system’s pure states are rank-one projections
associated with normalised vectors
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{W}
.
In Dirac’s notation these are presentable as
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
More generally, quantum states are associated with density operators
\varrho
which are non-negative and of unit trace,
each defining an expectation-value functional
O\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}O\varrho
on the self-adjoint operators
O
on
\mathcal{H}_{W}
(which are the system’s observables).

Given
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{W}
and a subset
A\subset W
, the reduced state on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
is defined through the restriction of the expectation values
\langle\psi|O|\psi\rangle
to observables of the form
O=O_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{W\backslash A}
. Stated equivalently, the reduced state is the partial trace over the remaining subsystem,

\varrho_{A}(\psi):=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{W\backslash A}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
A measure of
|\psi\rangle
’s bipartite entanglement is
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
’s von Neumann entropy:

S(\varrho_{A}):=-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\varrho_{A}\ln\varrho_{A}=-\!\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}}\lambda_{j}\log\lambda_{j}\,,

(1.1)

where
(\lambda_{j})
ranges over the eigenvalues of
\varrho_{A}
.

The entropy of any state in
A
is trivially bounded by the set’s volume
|A|
:

S(\varrho_{A})\leq\ln\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}\propto|A|\,.

(1.2)

Page’s law
[
45
,
30
]
states that for
vectors
|\psi\rangle
sampled uniformly from the unit sphere of

\mathcal{H}_{W}
the entropy is typically of the order of this upper bound, down by only a multiplicative factor of order
O(1)
.
In contrast, the entanglement entropy of ground states of a local lattice Hamiltonian on
\mathcal{H}_{W}
is expected to satisfy an
area law
, i.e.
S(\varrho_{A})\propto|\partial A|
, where
\partial A:=\{u\in A\ |\ d(u,W\backslash A)=1\}
, with an enhancement at a quantum phase transition, cf.
[
20
,
28
]
.

In this work, we shed light on this contrast through a decorrelation criterion under which
the entanglement entropy of a given state’s restriction to non-empty
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfies

S(\varrho_{A})\leq C\ l_{0}(A)\ |\partial A|\,,

(1.3)

at some
C<\infty
, which is independent of
A
, and a decoupling distance
l_{0}(A)
, whose dependence on
A
relies on model-specific input.
Section
elaborates on general conditions which ensure that the decoupling distance is bounded by a logarithm of the area,
l_{0}(A)\leq C\ln|\partial A|
.

1.2
Mutual information

The mutual information in a state
|\psi\rangle
between two disjoint sets
A_{1},A_{2}\subset W
is

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(A_{1}:A_{2}):=S(A_{1})+S(A_{2})-S(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=S(A_{1})-S(A_{1}\,|\,A_{2})\,.

(1.4)

where
S(A)\equiv S(\varrho_{A}(\psi))
is an abbreviation for the entropy of the reduced state
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
, and the last term is the conditional entropy

S(A_{1}\,|\,A_{2}):=S(A_{1}\cup A_{2})-S(A_{2})
. Viewed yet differently, the mutual information encodes the relative entropy of
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
relative to the product state
\varrho_{A_{1}}(\psi)\otimes\varrho_{A_{2}}(\psi)
.

Our approach to the entanglement entropy, which proceeds through entropy reduction in a comparison state, also allows to conclude that under similar decorrelation assumptions the mutual information between two disjoint cubes
A_{1},A_{2}
decays exponentially in their distance:

I_{\psi}(A_{1}:A_{2})\leq C\max\left\{|\partial A_{1}|,|\partial A_{2}|\right\}^{\kappa}\ \exp\left(-d(A_{1},A_{2})/\eta\right)\,,

(1.5)

at some
C,\kappa,\eta\in(0,\infty)
.
As a corollary we learn that the state
|\psi\rangle
exhibits
exponential clustering
. The two statements are linked through the quantum Pinsker inequality
[
37
,
54
,
55
]
, which bounds the covariance of any
pair of observables
O_{A_{j}}
, acting on their
\mathcal{H}_{A_{j}}
,
in terms of the mutual information of that state:

\left|\langle\psi|O_{A_{1}}\otimes O_{A_{2}}|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|O_{A_{1}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|O_{A_{2}}|\psi\rangle\right|\leq\|O_{A_{1}}\|\|O_{A_{1}}\|\sqrt{2(\ln 2)\ \ I_{\psi}(A_{1}:A_{2})}\,,

(1.6)

where the norm
\|\cdot\|
in the right side is the operator norm.

1.3
States of quasi-local structure in a computational basis

The enabling criteria presented below for entropy reduction
refer to the structure of a state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{W}
as it appears in a convenient
computational basis

in which it is presentable as

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Omega_{W}}e^{i\theta(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\,\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\,\,|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle

(1.7)

in terms of
a probability measure on
\Omega_{W}
of weights
p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}):=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma})|^{2}
,
with rapid decay of correlations, and
a phase function
\theta:\Omega_{W}\to(-\pi,\pi]
,
\theta(\boldsymbol{\sigma}):=\Arg\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
of similar properties.

The discussion is not limited to but it simplifies in the case of states for which
\theta(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\equiv 0
. Such states are referred to as “sign-problem free” or
stoquastic
- a term invoking the relevance of a probabilistic perspective. Among the examples of stoquasticity are the ground states of Hamiltonians
H
for which
e^{-\beta H}

is positivity-preserving in the specified computational basis. A sufficient condition for that is that the off-diagonal terms of
(-H)
are real and non-negative.

Various applications of states’ probabilistic structure, e.g. conditions for symmetry breaking, or slow decay of correlations, appeared in studies of specific models
[
50
,
5
,
6
,
33
,
14
,
23
,
51
,
13
,
4
]
.
Implications of stoquasticity have also been investigated from the complexity point of view
[
18
,
19
,
41
]
.

For stoquastic states our criterion will be expressed in terms of the decorrelation rate
of the classical looking probability
p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. An example to which this trivially applies, but to which our criterion is not limited, are
states
of classical Gibbs probability distribution under
additive local interactions, of the form

p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\propto\exp\left(\sum_{u,v\in W}J_{u,v}\ g(T_{u}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\ g(T_{v}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\right)\,.

(1.8)

Here
J_{u,v}
is a kernel of rapid enough decay,

g:\Omega_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
a bounded local function, and
T_{u}:\Omega_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\Omega_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
the translation by
u
. In case the kernel is finite range these states are examples of tensor-network states. Our criterion holds in that case irrespective of phase transitions, due to the classical state’s Markov property.

The general results presented here are not restricted to stoquasticity. They will include a condition which requires the phase function be decomposable into sums of
essentially local contributions. That is a limitation since even in the class of Hamiltonians build of commuting terms there are ground states of interest with more intricate phase structure, cf.
[
36
]
. On the constructive side:
phase functions for which the assumed condition is met include functions of the form

\theta(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\sum_{u,v\in W}\widehat{J}_{u,v}\ \widehat{g}(T_{u}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\ \widehat{g}(T_{v}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\,

(1.9)

with
\widehat{J}_{u,v}
a kernel of rapid enough decay, and
\widehat{g}:\Omega_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
a bounded local function.

1.4
Example: the quantum Ising model

Among the states to which our general discussion applies are the ground states of the quantum (or transversal field) Ising model (QIM).
The system’s Hilbert space is
\mathcal{H}_{W}=\otimes_{u\in W}\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
and the Hamiltonian (at
h^{z}\equiv 0
) is

H=-\sum_{u,v\in W}J_{u-v}\ S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}-b\sum_{u\in W}S^{x}_{u}\,,

(1.10)

written in terms of elements of the Pauli triples of spin operators
(S_{u}^{x},S_{u}^{y},S_{u}^{z})

which act on the
u
-component of the tensor product. The coupling
J_{u-v}\geq 0
is assumed to be finite range and ferromagnetic. A convenient computational basis in which the model’s thermal and ground state are stoquastic is the eigenbasis of the local Pauli
S^{z}
-operators. Another such basis is the one in which the local
S^{x}
are diagonal.

The system’s ground state undergoes a quantum phase transition at a threshold value
b_{c}>0
, which depends on
d
and the couplings
J
. The transition is manifested in the behavior of the model’s two point correlation function, and most clearly in its infinite volume limit

\langle S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}:=\lim_{L\to\infty}\langle S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(W_{L})}\,.

(1.11)

The superscript on the correlation indicates the domain over which the model is formulated, with
W_{L}=[-L,L]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In these terms, the model’s ground state undergoes the following transition (cf.
[
48
,
14
]
):

\bullet
For
b>b_{c}
, its ground state correlation function decay exponentially:

0\leq\langle S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C\ \exp\left(-|u-v|/\xi(b)\right)\,\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{ at some $\xi(b)\in(0,\infty)$}\,.

(1.12)

\bullet
For
b\in[0,b_{c})
the correlation function exhibits long range order:

\langle S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\geq M(b)^{2}>0

(1.13)

In that case the limit is decomposable into a superposition of two state functionals,

\langle O\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\frac{1}{2}\big[\langle O\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},+)}+\langle O\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},-)}\big]

with
\langle S^{z}_{u}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\pm)}=\pm M(b)
,
each with exponential decay of truncated correlations:

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle S^{z}_{u};S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\pm)}

\displaystyle:=\langle S^{z}_{u}S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\pm)}-\langle S^{z}_{u}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\pm)}\langle S^{z}_{v}\rangle^{(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\pm)}

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-|u-v|/\xi(b)\right)\,.

(1.14)

\bullet
At
b=b_{c}
:
M(b)=0

[
13
]
, but unlike at
b>b_{c}
the spin-spin correlation decays by a dimension-dependent power law.

We shall not delve here into the direct formulation of the model’s infinite-volume version (cf.
[
17
]
), in which the
(\pm)
states are presentable as ground states of an infinite spin array. Instead, we shall focus on bounds for finite volumes
W_{L}
which hold uniformly in
L
. Thus, unless specified explicitly, the states refer to the model formulated in a finite subset
W\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which will often be omitted in the notation.

We also refrain from the more complete discussion of boundary conditions. Unless specified otherwise there are to be assumed here as either free or constant (in terms of
\sigma^{z}
).
For each of these, the Perron-Frobenius theorem applied to
\exp\left(-\beta H\right)
in the
\sigma^{z}
-computational basis allows to conclude that the model’s finite volume ground states are unique at any
b\neq 0
.

The general results developed below, combined with known decay properties of the
S^{z}
-
S^{z}
-correlations in this model
[
14
,
24
]
enable us to prove the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Bounds for QIM)
.

For the quantum Ising model on
W\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, with the Hamiltonian (
) at
b>b_{c}(d)
(i.e. throughout the ground states’ subcritical regime):

1.

The entanglement entropy of the ground state’s restriction to non-empty rectangular domains
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfies:

S(\varrho_{A})\leq C\ |\partial A|\ \ln|\partial A|\,,

(1.15)

at some
C<\infty
.

2.

The ground state’s mutual information between disjoint rectangular domains decays exponentially, satisfying
(
).

3.

The ground state is exponentially clustering. In particular its truncated
x
-correlations decay exponentially: there is
C,\xi(b)\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
W
, and
u,v\in W
:

\left|\langle S^{x}_{u};S^{x}_{v}\rangle^{(W)}\right|\leq C\ \exp\left(-|u-v|/\xi(b)\right)\,.

(1.16)

It should be noted that for
d=1
where
\ln|\partial A|
is constant, (

The proof of Theorem
is presented in Section
. The argument suggests that (

b<b_{c}

would require improvements in the currently available model-specific information.

Relations to previous results specific to QIM:

The previous results on the entanglement specific to this model
were restricted to one dimension and there to significantly sub-critical states
[
33
,
34
,
21
]
.
The broader Theorem
addresses the entire subcritical regime, in all dimensions.
The difference is traced below to a key handicap in the approach previously taken.

The bound on the mutual information and exponential clustering (

[
23
]
that was derived using the model’s random current representation. It could potentially also be obtained using such methods.

2
State approximations of decreased complexity

A known approach towards bounds on the entanglement entropy
S(\varrho_{A}(\psi))
of a specified pure state
|\psi\rangle
is to seek high-fidelity approximations of the state
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
in
A
in terms of states of low-complexity, e.g. states of low rank – comparable with the exponential of the reduced state’s expected entropy
[
10
,
2
,
8
]
. Seeking such approximations, we present here two versions of approximations of
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
invoking quantum versions of conditioning on the state in a buffer
B
of adjustable width, enveloping the set
A
and separating it from the much bigger
C=W\setminus(A\sqcup B)
, cf. Figure
. The first is expressed directly in terms of the reduced state
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
. The second is expressed in terms of a Markovian approximation to the given state
|\psi\rangle
.

Figure 1:
Sketch of the decomposition of
W\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
into
A
, a buffer
B
, and the remaining set
C
.

2.1
Reduced state approximation

Given a state vector
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{W}
and a tripartition
W=A\sqcup B\sqcup C
in which
B
is a buffer separating
A
from the larger region
C
, let
\{|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}\rangle\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, e.g. labeled by spin configurations
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}\in\Omega_{B}
from a convenient computational basis.
Let
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle
denote the normalized vector defined by

\big[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}|\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{C}\big]\,|\psi\rangle=:\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\,|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle

(2.1)

with
p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\in[0,1]
.
In these terms, the state vector is
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\,\,|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle
, and its reduced state in
A
can be presented as

\varrho_{A}(\psi)=\;\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\ \varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{with}\hskip 9.24994pt\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right):=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{B\sqcup C}}|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})|\,.\;

(2.2)

To interpret this procedure, one may note that the restriction
of the reduced state of
|\psi\rangle
in
A
is not changed if at first the state is pinched in
B
,
that is transformed

|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\rightarrow\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\ |\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})|\,.

(2.3)

The pinching, which can be viewed as the result of non-destructive measurement of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}
,
allows one to apply to the quantum systems the classical notion of conditioning.

The next observation is that while the rank of
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
generically reaches the lower of the dimensions
\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\dim\mathcal{H}_{BC}
, the density matrices
\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)
may potentially be approximated by lower rank operators, being conditioned on specified states of the buffer.
In particular, an approximation to
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
of rank bounded by
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}
is given by

\boxed{\widehat{\varrho}_{A}\left(\psi\right):=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\,|\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\langle\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})|\,,}

(2.4)

where
{\widehat{\phi}}_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})
is a normalized eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue,
\lambda(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}):=\left\|\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)\right\|
, of the density matrix in (
).

For the fidelity of this approximation, as measured by the trace-norm (
\|\cdot\|_{1}
) distance, one has the bound

\left\|\varrho_{A}\left(\psi\right)-\widehat{\varrho}_{A}\left(\psi\right)\right\|_{1}\leq 2\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\,\left(1-\lambda(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)\,,

(2.5)

which follows from straightforward applications of the triangle inequality.
Note that
1-\lambda(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})=1-\left\|\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)\right\|
measures the proximity of
\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)
to a rank-one operator. If
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{BC}
factorizes into a product state, then
\lambda(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})=1
.
One may also explore more general approximations of the reduced state
\varrho_{A}\left(\psi\right)
through
sums over more than one of the leading terms in the spectral decomposition of
\varrho_{A}\left(\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\right)
.

2.2
Markovian state approximation

For another approach, one may start from a convenient computational orthonormal basis over
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
, indexed by spin configurations
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})\in\Omega_{A}\times\Omega_{B}\times\Omega_{C}
, which represent the eigenvalues of commuting observables.
Their joint probability distribution under a given state vector
|\psi\rangle
is

p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\sigma})|^{2}

which can be written as

p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\,p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\,p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})

(2.6)

in terms of the induced conditional probabilities
p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})=p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})/p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})
and

p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})
.
Seeking a Markovian approximation of
|\psi\rangle
it is natural to consider states
\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\sqrt{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\,e^{i\widehat{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle
with the
modified probability distribution

\mkern-100.0mu\mathrm{(I)}\mkern 80.0mu\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})=p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\ p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\ p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\,,

(2.7)

under which
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}
retain their marginal distribution but are conditionally independent,
and phase functions of the form

\mkern-160.0mu\mathrm{(II)}\mkern 80.0mu\widehat{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})=\alpha(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})+\gamma(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})

(2.8)

with some
\alpha:\Omega_{A}\times\Omega_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma:\Omega_{C}\times\Omega_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
which may be variationally selected.
With such a choice a markovian approximation takes the form

\boxed{|\psi_{(B)}\rangle:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\,|\phi_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\otimes|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle}

(2.9)

with normalized vectors

\displaystyle|\phi_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}}e^{i\alpha(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\,|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}\rangle

(2.10)

\displaystyle|\phi_{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}}e^{i\gamma(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\sqrt{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})}\,|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}\rangle\,.

For the vector (
) the state has the Markov property in the sense that
conditioned on
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}
it factorizes into a product state over
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
.
Its reduced state on
A
,

\varrho_{A}(\psi_{(B)})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}}p(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\ |\phi_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})\rangle\langle\phi_{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})|\,,

(2.11)

has a rank bounded by
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
Hence, in contrast to
\varrho_{A}(\psi)
, the induced state
\varrho_{A}(\psi_{(B)})
is guaranteed to satisfy

S\big(\varrho_{A}(\psi_{(B)})\big)\leq\ln\big(\nu^{|B|}\big)\propto l\,|\ \partial A|\,.

(2.12)

Remark:
For concreteness sake, it may be noted that a simple way to split an additive phase as a sum of the above form is to pick somewhat arbitrarily a configuration
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{W}
and set
\alpha
and
\gamma
as

\begin{split}\alpha(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})&=\theta(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{C})-\theta(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{C})\\
\gamma(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B})&=\theta(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})\,.\end{split}

(2.13)

In a purely additive case, as in (
), the sum
\alpha+\gamma
depends on
\boldsymbol{\eta}
only through the terms which link directly to both
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}
. In the absence of such, this injection of
\boldsymbol{\eta}
is for convenience only, its selection having no effect on the analysis.

3
Fidelity bound for the Markovian state approximation

Quantification of the deviation of
|\psi\rangle
from the idealized conditions listed above as (I) and (II), which hold for
|\psi_{(B)}\rangle
, will involve two terms.

(I)  To address the degree of correlations in
p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
between
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C}
, when both are conditioned on the configuration
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{B}
of the buffer, we use the following terminology and notation.

Without yet conditioning on
B
, we denote by
{\delta}(A\,|\,C)
the variational difference between the probability distribution of
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{A},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{C})
and the independent product of the
corresponding marginals:

\displaystyle{\delta}(A\,|\,C)

1.

In previous studies of the entanglement in QIM

Appendix A
Fannes’ type bound

In controling of entropy differences we made use of the following minor modification of Fannes’ entropy bound
[
29
]
, which appeared above as (
), on the difference of von Neumann entropies of two states
\varrho,\widehat{\varrho}
.
For the reader’s convenience we include a short proof, which is slightly different than the one in
[
29
]
.

Proposition A.1
.

For any pair of states
\varrho,\widehat{\varrho}
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space:

\left|S(\varrho)-S(\widehat{\varrho})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\varrho-\widehat{\varrho}\right\|_{1}\left(1+\ln\frac{2\,\mathrm{rank}(\varrho-\widehat{\varrho})}{\left\|\varrho-\widehat{\varrho}\right\|_{1}}\right).

(A.1)

Bounding
\mathrm{rank}(\varrho-\widehat{\varrho})
by the dimension of the Hilbert space on which the states are defined, (
) reduces to the Fannes bound of
[
29
]
. Unlike the optimal bound by Audenaert
[
11
]
, this bound
is not sharp.

Our proof is based on the subadditivity of the trace of the extension of the following function to non-negative operators.

F(x):=\begin{cases}x\left(1+\ln x^{-1}\right),&x\in[0,1],\\
1,&x>1.\end{cases}

Lemma A.2
.

For any pair
A,B\geq 0
of non-negative matrices

\mathrm{tr}F(A+B)\leq\mathrm{tr}F(A)+\mathrm{tr}F(B)

(A.2)

Proof.

Since
F
is differentiable on
(0,\infty)
with derivative
F^{\prime}(x)=\ln x^{-1}
in case
x\in(0,1)
and
F^{\prime}(x)=0
in case
x\geq 1
, we may compute for any
t\in(0,1)
:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{tr}F(A+tB)=\mathrm{tr}F^{\prime}(A+tB)B=-\mathrm{tr}B\ln(A+tB)\leq-\left(\mathrm{tr}B\ln t+\mathrm{tr}B\ln B\right).

The inequality resulted from the estimate
A+tB\geq tB
and the operator monotonicity of the logarithm. Integration of the above inequality using
\int_{0}^{1}\ln(t^{-1})dt=1
, yields the first claim.
∎

Proof of Proposition
.

We first use monotonicity of
F
to estimate
\mathrm{tr}F(\varrho)\leq\mathrm{tr}F(A+B)
with
A=\widehat{\varrho}
and
B=\left[\varrho-\widehat{\varrho}\right]_{+}
, where the latter denotes the positive part of the difference. Lemma
thus yields:

S(\varrho)-S(\widehat{\varrho})=\mathrm{tr}F(\varrho)-\mathrm{tr}F(\widehat{\varrho})\leq\mathrm{tr}F(A+B)-\mathrm{tr}F(\widehat{\varrho})\leq-\mathrm{tr}B\ln B+\mathrm{tr}B.

The first term on the right is trivially estimated by
\mathrm{tr}B\ln(\mathrm{rank}B/\mathrm{tr}B)
. Since
\mathrm{tr}(\varrho-\widehat{\varrho})=0
, we have
\mathrm{tr}B=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\varrho-\widehat{\varrho}\right\|_{1}
. This concludes the proof, since the argument holds also for the reversed order of the two states.
∎

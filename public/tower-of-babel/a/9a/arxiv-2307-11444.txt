Computations with polynomial evaluation oracle: ruling out superlinear SETH-based lower bounds
Tatiana Belova, Alexander S. Kulikov, Ivan Mihajlin, Olga Ratseeva, Grigory Reznikov, Denil Sharipov

Abstract
The field of fine-grained complexity aims at proving conditional lower bounds on the time complexity of computational problems. One of the most popular and successfully used assumptions, Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH), implies that 𝖲𝖠𝖳 \mathsf{SAT} cannot be solved in 2 ( 1 − ε ) ​ n 2^{(1-\varepsilon)n} time. In recent years, it has been proved that known algorithms for many problems are optimal under SETH. Despite wide applicability of SETH, for many problems, there are no known SETH-based lower bounds, so the quest for new reductions continues. Two barriers for proving SETH-based lower bounds are known. Carmosino et al. (ITCS 2016) introduced the Nondeterministic Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (NSETH) stating that 𝖳𝖠𝖴𝖳 \mathsf{TAUT} cannot be solved in time 2 ( 1 − ε ) ​ n 2^{(1-\varepsilon)n} even if one allows nondeterminism. They used this hypothesis to show that some natural fine-grained reductions would be difficult to obtain: proving that, say, 3 3 - 𝖲𝖴𝖬 \mathsf{SUM} requires time n 1.5 + ε n^{1.5+\varepsilon} under SETH, breaks NSETH and this, in turn, implies strong circuit lower bounds. Recently, Belova et al. (SODA 2023) introduced the so-called polynomial formulations to show that for many 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard problems, proving any explicit exponential lower bound under SETH also implies strong circuit lower bounds. In this paper, we combine the two barriers above. We prove that for a range of problems from 𝖯 \mathsf{P} , including k k - 𝖲𝖴𝖬 \mathsf{SUM} and triangle detection, proving superlinear lower bounds under SETH is challenging as it implies new circuit lower bounds. To this end, we show that these problems can be solved in nearly linear time with oracle calls to evaluating a polynomial of constant degree. Then, we introduce a strengthening of SETH stating that solving 𝖲𝖠𝖳 \mathsf{SAT} in time 2 ( 1 − ε ) ​ n 2^{(1-\varepsilon)n} is difficult even if one has constant degree polynomial evaluation oracle calls. This hypothesis is stronger and less believable than SETH, but refuting it is still challenging: we show that this implies circuit lower bounds. Finally, by considering computations that make oracle calls to evaluating constant degree polynomials depending on a small number of variables, we show connections between nondeterministic time lower bounds and arithmetic circuit lower bounds. Namely, we prove that if any of 𝖬𝖠𝖷 \mathsf{MAX} - k k - 𝖲𝖠𝖳 \mathsf{SAT} , Binary Permanent, or a variant of Set Cover problems cannot be solved in co-nondeterministic time 2 ( 1 − ε ) ​ n 2^{(1-\varepsilon)n} , for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , then one gets arbitrary large polynomial arithmetic circuit lower bounds.

Connections between nondeterministic complexity and arithmetic circuit complexity

Boolean and arithmetic circuits

Definition 2.2
.

A Boolean circuit

2.4
Minimum arithmetic circuit problem

Below, we consider the problem of finding an arithmetic circuit
of minimum size computing the given polynomial. It turns out
that if the polynomial has constant degree, then one can guess and verify an arithmetic
circuit of size close to optimal in polynomial time.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

2.6
Fine-grained reductions and SETH hardness

Below, we define fine-grained reductions and SETH-hardness.
Since SETH-hardness is defined as a sequence of reductions
from

-
\mathsf{SAT}
for every value of

we introduce a function

in the standard definition (

) of fine-grained reductions.

Definition 2.5

(Fine-grained reductions)
.

Let
P,Q
be problems,

be non-decreasing functions and
{\delta\colon\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
.
We say that
(P,p(n))

\delta

to
(Q,q(n))

\varepsilon>0
and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0

an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
for

with oracle access to

a constant

•

runs in time at most

•

as well as their answers of the oracle for

•

the sizes

of the produced instances (of the problem

We say that
(P,p(n))

to
(Q,q(n))

and write

It is not difficult to see that

.

Definition 2.6

(SETH-hardness)
.

For a time bound

, we say that a problem

is

-SETH-hard

a function
\delta\colon\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and for every

(\text{$k$-{$\mathsf{SAT}$}{}},2^{n})\leq_{\delta}(P,t(n))\,.

If a problem

is

-SETH-hard, then any algorithm

in time

implies an algorithm solving

\mathsf{SAT}
in time

2.7
Polynomial formulations

Informally, a polynomial formulation of a computational problem

is an explicit family of polynomials
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
and a family of functions

.

Definition 2.7

(Polynomial formulations)
.

Let

be a time bound, and

-explicit family of polynomials over

is a

•

a non-decreasing function

satisfying

, and an algorithm computing

in time

;

•

a family of mappings
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\phi_{n}\colon A_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{s(n)}
, and an algorithm evaluating
\phi_{n}
at any point in time

such that the following holds.
For every

and every

•

\,;

•

|\phi_{n}(x)|<2^{(s(n))^{o(1)}}
.

In
[
BGK+23
]
, it is proved that
for any
\alpha>1
, there exists

such that

In this paper,
for a wide range of problem from

such that the corresponding problem

3
New hypothesis (POSETH): solving
\mathsf{SAT}
with polynomial evaluation oracle

Carmosino et al.
[
CGI+16
]
have shown that proving an upper bound on both nondeterministic and co-nondeterministic complexity of a problem can be used to show the implausibility of SETH-based lower bounds. They also raised the question if studying some other computational models can be used to distinguish the fine-grained complexity
of various problems. We follow this suggestion and
introduce a new computational resource
{\mathsf{POTIME}}
.

Definition 3.1

(
\mathsf{POTIME}
and
\mathsf{PONTIME}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an explicit family of polynomials
and
t\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a time bound. By
{\mathsf{POTIME}}[t,\mathcal{Q}]
(
{\mathsf{PONTIME}}[t,\mathcal{Q}]
)
we denote the class of problems that can be solved
in time
t(n)
by a deterministic (nondeterministic, respectively) algorithm that
makes oracle calls to
\mathcal{Q}
:
for any
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
such that
|a_{i}|=2^{s^{o(1)}}
for all
i
,
it gets the value
Q_{s}(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{s})
in time
s
.

The usefulness of this notion comes from the fact that fine-grained reductions transfer savings for
\mathsf{POTIME}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(A,p(n))\leq_{\delta}(B,q(n))
and
B\in{\mathsf{POTIME}}(q(n)^{1-\varepsilon},Q)
, then
A\in{\mathsf{POTIME}}(p(n)^{1-\delta},Q)
.

Proof.

We run the reduction from
A
to
B
. Then we will substitute oracle calls to
B
from the reduction with the algorithm for
B
that can make oracle calls to
Q
.
By the properties of the fine-grained reductions, we have the desired running time.
∎

Now we introduce an even stronger version of SETH and show that refuting it is still challenging as it implies circuit lower bounds.

Definition 3.2

(POSETH)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and every explicit polynomial family
\mathcal{Q}
, there exists
k
such that

-
\mathsf{TAUT}
cannot be solved in time

by
a deterministic algorithm that makes oracle calls
to evaluating
\mathcal{Q}
:

\forall\varepsilon>0,\,\forall\text{ explicit $\mathcal{Q}$},\,\exists k\colon\text{$k$-{$\mathsf{TAUT}$}{}}\not\in{\mathsf{POTIME}}[2^{(1-\varepsilon)n},\mathcal{Q}].

Theorem 3.1
.

If POSETH is false, then either there exists a family
of Boolean functions
from
{\mathsf{E}}^{{\mathsf{NP}}}
that cannot be computed
by series-parallel Boolean circuits of size

or there exists
an explicit family of polynomials
that cannot be computed by arithmetic circuits of size

for some

Proof.

Assume that POSETH is false:

\exists\varepsilon>0,\,\exists\text{ explicit~$\mathcal{Q}$},\,\forall k\colon\text{$k$-{$\mathsf{TAUT}$}}\in{\mathsf{POTIME}}[2^{(1-\varepsilon)n},\mathcal{Q}].

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the corresponding deterministic algorithm with oracle access to
\Delta
-explicit polynomial

\mathcal{Q}
.
Let

,
\beta=\beta(\varepsilon)
,
\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon)
, and

be positive parameters whose values will be specified later.
Given a

makes a number of oracle calls

Let

If

Below, we describe

, this gives
Boolean circuit lower bounds. The corresponding algorithm consists of two stages. Below,

we choose the values for the parameters

,

, and

.

Preprocessing stage.

Find an arithmetic circuit

for all

(and Remark

This is at most

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,\,\,}O\left(2^{(1-\varepsilon)\gamma n}+(t_{1}^{1+2\alpha}+\dotsb+t_{m}^{1+2\alpha})\right)

4
Problems from
\mathsf{P}
that can be solved efficiently
with polynomial evaluation oracle

In this section,
we show that a wide range of problems from
\mathsf{P}

admit a
\Delta(\varepsilon)
-polynomial formulation of complexity
O(n^{1+\varepsilon})
(or
O((n+m)^{1+\varepsilon})
for graph problems),
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
To capture many such polynomial-time solvable problems,
we define a subclass of problems from
\mathsf{P}

where the goal is to find a constant size
subset of an input that can be verified locally.

Informally, we say that a decision problem
A

belongs to the class
\mathsf{LS}
,
if, for any instance
I\in A_{n}
, it is a yes-instance if and only if
there is a constant size set
Y\subseteq I

and a constant size set
N\subseteq\overline{I}

such that the pair
(Y,N)

satisfies some
easily verifiable property.
This is best illustrated with examples. The
3
-
\mathsf{SUM}
problem clearly satisfies this property as the goal is just to find three
elements of the input whose sum is equal to zero. Another example
of a problem with this property is Induced
4
-Cycle: in this case,
one needs to check whether the input graph contains
four edges and two non-edges that span four nodes. On the other hand, for the Hamiltonian Path problem it is not immediate whether
it satisfies this property as one looks for a subset of edges
of non-constant size. Another example of a problem whose straightforward statement does not put it into the considered class
is
k
-Vertex Cover (where the goal is to check whether the input graph contains a set of
k
nodes that cover all edges). In this problem, one
is looking for a set of nodes (or edges) of constant size,
but one cannot ensure that it covers the whole graph just
by checking a constant number of non-edges.

One technical remark is in order before we proceed to the
formal definition of the class of problems. Throughout the whole paper, by
n
we denote the natural complexity parameter
of a problem that is used to measure both the size
of an instance and the running time of an algorithm
for this problem.
In this chapter however it is important
that we measure the running time of graph algorithms
in terms of
n+m
, the number of nodes plus the number of edges.
To avoid naming conflicts, below we use an additional parameter
s
to denote the size of an instance.

We start with some preliminary definitions.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce a
universe

U_{n}
, which is a set of size
n^{O(1)}
that can be generated in polynomial time with a bijection
B_{n}\colon U_{n}\rightarrow[|U_{n}|]
that can be computed in time
O(1)
for any element from
U_{n}
.
For some fixed positive integers
\alpha
and
\beta
, we introduce a
verification algorithm

V:\mathbb{N}^{\alpha+\beta}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
that works in polynomial time. For
u_{1},\dotsc,u_{\alpha+\beta}\in U_{n}
, by
V(u_{1},\dotsc,u_{\alpha+\beta})
we denote
V(B_{n}(u_{1}),\dotsc,B_{n}(u_{\alpha+\beta}))
.

The common form of a problem from
\mathsf{LS}
is a triple
(\alpha,\beta,V)
and a family
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. It is
a decision problem accepting a tuple
(n,m,S)
, where
S
is a subset of
U_{n}
of size
m
, as an input and checking whether there exist a subset
X
of
S
of size
\alpha
and a subset
Y
of
U_{n}\setminus S
of size
\beta
such that
V
returns
1
on
(X,Y)
.

Before we finally define the class, let us illustrate that definition on the Induced 4-Cycle problem.
For this problem,
\alpha=4
,
\beta=2
, corresponding to the number of edges and non-edges of 4-Cycle, respectively.
Translatable universe
U_{n}
will be
[n]\times[n]
that stands for all pairs of nodes and
B_{n}
will be a natural bijection that maps
[n]\times[n]
into
[n^{2}]
in such a way that for all
u\in[n-1]\times[n-1]
,
B_{n}(u)=B_{n-1}(u)
.
The verification algorithm
V
will check whether edges
x_{1},\dots,x_{4}\in U_{n}
and non-edges
y_{1},y_{2}\in U_{n}
span a 4-cycle.

Now we can formulate Induced 4-Cycle as follows. Given
a graph
G
as
(n,m,S)
, where
n
is the number of nodes,
m
is the number of edges and
S\subseteq U_{n}
is a set of edges, check whether there exist
x_{1},\dots,x_{4}\in S
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in U_{n}\setminus S
such that
V(x_{1},\dots,x_{4},y_{1},y_{2})=1
.

Note that we run
V
on the result of encoding of pairs of nodes, and

does not depend on
n
, so we have to ensure that
V
interprets same pairs of nodes the same way even for different
n
. Fortunately, when we deal with cartesian products of integer intervals (such as

), such a bijection always exists.

Now we are ready to define the class
\mathsf{LS}
formally.

Definition 4.1

(Local Subset class)
.

The class
\mathsf{LS}
contains all
decision problems

for which
there exist positive integers
\alpha
and
\beta
,
a family of universes

(

)

such that

, so

.

It is not difficult to see that

Recall that in
[
BGK+23
]

for any
\alpha>1
, there exists
\Delta=\Delta(\alpha)
such that

k
-
\mathsf{SAT}
,
\mathsf{MAX}
-
k
-
\mathsf{SAT}

.
That is, at the expense of increasing the degree of a polynomial,

We show that

\mathsf{MAX}
-
k
-
\mathsf{SAT}
(for

by an algorithm that makes oracle calls to evaluating a polynomial

{\mathsf{coNTIME}}[O(2^{(1-\delta)n})]

for any
\delta>0
, then, for any
\gamma
, there exists an explicit polynomial family that cannot be computed by arithmetic circuits of size
O(n^{\gamma})
.
It should be noted that, currently, for none

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

be any of the following problems:

\mathsf{MAX}
-

\mathsf{SAT}
(for

.

It is interesting to compare this result with

We derive Theorem

Theorem 5.2

Assume that a problem

, such that for any

that solves

and makes oracle calls to computing

and

.

Proof.

First, note that if the property below holds for a problem

, then it holds for
\neg{A}
.
Assume that there exists
\gamma
such that every explicit family
of polynomials has arithmetic circuit size

. Below,
we show that in this case

can be solved in co-nondeterministic time
O(2^{(1-\delta)n})
(or

can be solved in nondeterministic time

) for some
\delta=\delta(\gamma)
. To do this,
we take the corresponding algorithm with polynomial evaluation
oracle for
\neg{A}
, nondeterministically guess arithmetic circuits for the corresponding polynomial, and replace every oracle call
with a call to the corresponding circuit.

Let
0<\beta<1
be fixed and
\mathcal{Q}
be the corresponding
\Delta
-explicit polynomial family.
For a parameter
\alpha
to be chosen later, for all

, find a circuit

of size
O(t^{\gamma})
computing
Q_{t}

(for all points
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{t})
such that
|x_{i}|\leq 2^{O(t^{2})}
for all

). By Lemma

O\left(2^{\alpha n}\cdot 2^{\alpha\gamma n}\cdot 2^{2\alpha\Delta n}\cdot\log^{2}\left(2^{\alpha n}\cdot 2^{O\left(2^{2\alpha n}\right)}\right)\right)=O\left(2^{\alpha n(5+\gamma+2\Delta)}\right)\,.

Take
\alpha=\frac{1}{6(5+\gamma+2\delta)}
to ensure that this

O(2^{n/2})
.

Now, take
\varepsilon=\alpha/\beta
(clearly,
0<\varepsilon<1/2
) and consider the algorithm from the problem statement that solves

O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)}n)

with oracle calls of size at most

of size

(that computes

Recall that the algorithm makes oracle calls for

By (

To compute the value of

[

]
. Generating such primes for all

and the prime

Plugging

To satisfy this, it suffices to take

such that the inequality is satisfied.

Let the problem

\mathcal{S}
of subsets of
[n]
(with possible repetitions) find the number of partitions
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=[n]

We first show that for every
\alpha>0
Set Cover can be reduced to
2^{(1-\alpha)n}
instances of #Set Partition of size
\alpha n
in time
O^{*}(2^{(1-\alpha)n})
.

To do that, we introduce a more general problem #Hybrid Cover Partition with parameters

\#HCV_{n,m,k}

\mathcal{S}
of subsets of
[n]

Lemma 5.1

Let

be an instance of Set Cover.
We replace every set
S_{i}
with

\{S_{i}^{T}:=S_{i}\setminus T\colon T\subseteq S_{i}\cap[m]\}
, and obtain a collection
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
which we consider as an instance of
\#HCV_{n,m,k}
. We show that
\mathcal{S}
is a yes-instance of Set Cover iff

Let

\mathcal{S}
, then
S_{i_{1}},S_{i_{2}}^{[m]\cap S_{i_{1}}},\dots,S_{i_{k}}^{[m]\cap(S_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup S_{i_{k-1}})}

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

Lemma 5.2
.

\#HCV_{n,m,k}

m

n^{O(1)}

Let

.

Using this equality we can reduce calculation of

\#HCV_{m,m,k}

There are

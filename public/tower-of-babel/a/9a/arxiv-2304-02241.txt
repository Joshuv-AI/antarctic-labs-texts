A Note on Quantum Phase Estimation
Yao-Ting Lin

Abstract
In this work, we study the phase estimation problem. We show an alternative, simpler and self-contained proof of query lower bounds. Technically, compared to the previous proofs [ NW99 , Bes05 ] , our proof is considerably elementary. Specifically, our proof consists of basic linear algebra without using the knowledge of Boolean function analysis and adversary methods. Qualitatively, our bound is tight in the low success probability regime and offers a more fine-grained trade-off. In particular, we prove that for any ε > 0 , p ≥ 0 \varepsilon>0,p\geq 0 , every algorithm requires at least Ω ⁡ ( p / ε ) \Omega(p/\varepsilon) queries to obtain an ε \varepsilon -approximation for the phase with probability at least p p . However, the existing bounds hold only when p > 1 / 2 p>1/2 . Quantitatively, our bound is tight since it matches the well-known phase estimation algorithm of Cleve, Ekert, Macchiavello, and Mosca [ CEMM98 ] which requires O ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) O(1/\varepsilon) queries to obtain an ε \varepsilon -approximation with a constant probability. Following the derivation of the lower bound in our framework, we give a new and intuitive interpretation of the phase estimation algorithm of [ CEMM98 ] , which might be of independent interest.

Technical Overview.

Our approach is inspired by Zhandry’s compressed oracle technique
[
Zha19
]
. First, we introduce the
phase distinguishing problem
parameterized by
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In the problem, a set of unitary operators which share a common eigenstate
\lvert u\rangle
are labeled by
y\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
. In the beginning, a unitary
U_{y}
with eigenvalue
e^{2\pi yi/N}
is sampled uniformly from the set and the algorithm is given the oracle access to controlled-
U_{y}
, denoted by
c-U_{y}
, and its eigenstate
\lvert u\rangle
. The goal of the algorithm is to determine which unitary operator was chosen.

Suppose an algorithm can obtain an
\varepsilon
-approximation of the phase with probability at least
p
. Then there exists a reduction that solves the phase distinguishing problem parameterized by
N=\lfloor 1/(2\varepsilon)\rfloor
with probability at least
p
simply by rounding the approximated phase to the closest
y/N
. Consequently, we can derive the query lower bound for the phase estimation problem by showing the query lower bound for the phase distinguishing problem (Corollary
3.3
). Under the same oracle distribution as in the phase distinguishing problem, we immediately conclude that it is also hard for any algorithm to approximate the phase
\emph{onaverage}
over the oracles (Corollary
3.4
).

The main technical part is to prove the hardness of the phase distinguishing problem (Theorem
3.2
). In reminiscence of
[
Zha19
]
, our proof precedes by
purifying
the computation and analyzing the algorithm in the
Fourier
basis.
In particular, besides the registers of the algorithm, we augment an additional register
\mathsf{C}
that records the choice of
y
.
Notice that the distribution of
y
is equivalent to measuring the uniform superposition state in the register
\mathsf{C}
. Since this measurement commutes with the operations performed by the algorithm, it can be deferred to the end. Therefore, the register
\mathsf{C}
is initialized with uniform superposition which is the zero in the Fourier basis.
Then we introduce the coherent version of the unitary, denoted by
c-\mathfrak{U}
, which implements
c-U_{y}
in superposition if the content on register
\mathsf{C}
is
y
.
As an important observation, when expressing
c-\mathfrak{U}
in the Fourier basis, each
c-\mathfrak{U}
increases the content of
\mathsf{C}
at most by
1
.
Given the above, by representing the register
\mathsf{C}
in the Fourier basis, the register
\mathsf{C}
can be viewed as a
counter
that records the number of queries. By leveraging this property and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we can derive the maximum success probability of the algorithm.

As a remark, our technique bears some similarities to
[
Zha19
]
in the following sense. In the computational basis, the register
\mathsf{C}
serves as a
control
register that
writes
the information to the algorithm; while in the Fourier basis, the register
\mathsf{C}
serves as a
counter
that
records
the behavior of the algorithm.

In the purified view, one can interpret the algorithm’s goal as generating a correlation between itself and the register
\mathsf{C}
. Therefore, one can view solving the phase distinguishing problem as manipulating a high-dimensional EPR state. Combining it with a property of (high-dimensional) EPR states, one can easily find the best strategy for solving the phase distinguishing problem. Surprisingly, it turns out that the strategy is the same as the phase estimation algorithm in
[
CEMM98
]
.

In the phase estimation problem, for any

\displaystyle c-\mathfrak{U}\lvert 1\rangle\lvert u\rangle\lvert\hat{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\omega_{N}^{ky}c-\mathfrak{U}\lvert 1\rangle\lvert u\rangle\lvert y\rangle=\lvert 1\rangle\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\omega_{N}^{ky}U_{y}\lvert u\rangle\lvert y\rangle

\displaystyle=\lvert 1\rangle\lvert u\rangle\sum_{y\in[N]}\frac{\omega_{N}^{(k+1)y}}{\sqrt{N}}\lvert y\rangle=\lvert 1\rangle\lvert u\rangle\lvert\widehat{k+1}\rangle

with addition modulo
N
and the first register is the control register of
c-\mathfrak{U}
. When the control register is
\lvert 0\rangle
,
c-\mathfrak{U}
simply becomes an identity.
Moreover, for any
\lvert v\rangle
orthogonal to
\lvert u\rangle
,
c-\mathfrak{U}
also acts as identity on
\lvert 1\rangle\lvert v\rangle\lvert\hat{k}\rangle
by the definition of
U_{y}
. Putting things together, we conclude that each query increases the counter
\mathsf{C}
at most by
1
(in superposition) in the Fourier basis. Therefore, this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We note the proof of Lemma
3.5
can be trivially extended to the setting in which the algorithm has the access to the inverse oracle
c-U^{-1}_{y}
or power oracles
c-U^{n}_{y}
. The only relevant quantity is the number of possible values
k
’s of the counter that can be “composed” by using those oracles at most
q
times. For example, using
c-U^{-1}_{y}
once will subtract
1
from the value of the counter; using
c-U^{n}_{y}
once will add
n
to the value of the counter.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
generates the output by measuring its output register
\mathsf{O}
which is a part of the register
\mathsf{A}
in the computational basis.

For every
y\in[N]
, define the projectors
\Pi_{\mathsf{O}}^{y}\coloneqq\lvert y\rangle\langle y\rvert_{\mathsf{O}}
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{C}}^{y}\coloneqq\lvert y\rangle\langle y\rvert_{\mathsf{C}}
.
Let
\lvert\psi\rangle_{\mathsf{AC}}
be the state after the whole computation and right before
{\mathcal{A}}
performs the final measurement to generate the output. By
\mathbf{y}
we mean the random variable whose outcome is the choice of
y

The success probability of
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in[N]}\Pr\left[{\mathcal{A}}^{c-U_{\mathbf{y}}}\text{ outputs }y\land\mathbf{y}=y\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in[N]}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{q}\alpha_{k}\frac{\omega_{N}^{ky}}{\sqrt{N}}\Pi_{\mathsf{O}}^{y}\lvert\psi_{k}\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in[N]}\left[\left(\sum_{k=0}^{q}\left|\alpha_{k}\frac{\omega_{N}^{ky}}{\sqrt{N}}\right|^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|\Pi_{\mathsf{O}}^{y}\lvert\psi_{k}\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}\right)\right]

(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|\lvert\psi_{k}\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle=\frac{q+1}{N},

(5)

where (
1
) is obtained by applying Lemma
3.5
to
\lvert\psi\rangle_{\mathsf{AC}}
; (
2
) follows from Lemma
2.9
; (

4
An Interpretation of Phase Estimation Algorithm

With the following well-known property of EPR states in mind, one can naturally come up with the optimal algorithm from the proof in Section
3
.

Fact 4.1
.

For every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the state
\lvert\Psi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
defined as
\lvert\Psi\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle\lvert y\rangle
satisfies

\lvert\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert-\hat{y}\rangle\lvert\hat{y}\rangle
,
where
\lvert-\hat{y}\rangle
is the complex conjugate of
\lvert\hat{y}\rangle
.

Proof.

We finish the proof by comparing the coefficients. For any
a,b\in[N]
, we have

\left(\langle a\rvert\langle b\rvert\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert-\hat{y}\rangle\lvert\hat{y}\rangle\right)=\frac{1}{N\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\omega_{N}^{-ay}\omega_{N}^{by}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\delta_{a,b}=\left(\langle a\rvert\langle b\rvert\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle\lvert y\rangle\right),

where
\delta_{a,b}
equals
1
if
a=b
and
0
otherwise.
∎

In the above analysis, we see that in order to achieve high success probability in the phase distinguishing problem, the algorithm’s output register
\mathsf{O}
must be highly correlated with the oracle register
\mathsf{C}
.
For intuition, consider the following state which has a success probability
1
.

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert y\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert-\hat{y}\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert\hat{y}\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}.

Notice that the values are perfectly correlated and the marginal distribution of the oracle is uniformly random.

Therefore, we can interpret the goal of the algorithm as preparing the state on the right-hand side. Combining the observation we made in Theorem
3.2
and Fact
4.1
, we show the following algorithm for solving
\mathsf{PD_{N}}
.

1.

Initial state:
\lvert 0\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert u\rangle_{\mathsf{W}}\lvert\hat{0}\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}
, where
\mathsf{A}=(\mathsf{O},\mathsf{W})
.

2.

Create uniform superposition:
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert u\rangle_{\mathsf{W}}\lvert\hat{0}\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}
.

3.

Perform the
controlled-add
defined by

\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle\langle y\rvert_{\mathsf{O}}\otimes\left(c-\mathfrak{U}\right)^{y}

in superposition which requires
N
queries.
The resulting state will be

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert y\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert u\rangle_{\mathsf{W}}\lvert\hat{y}\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}.

4.

Perform the inverse quantum Fourier transform on the register
\mathsf{A}
:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{y\in[N]}\lvert-\hat{y}\rangle_{\mathsf{O}}\lvert u\rangle_{\mathsf{W}}\lvert\hat{y}\rangle_{\mathsf{C}}.

The above procedures are exactly the same as the phase estimation algorithm of Cleve et al.
[
CEMM98
]
but rephrased in a different perspective.

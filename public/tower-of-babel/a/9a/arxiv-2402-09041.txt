Closure properties and heavy tails: random vectors in presence of dependence
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. This paper is related to closure properties of heavy-tailed random variables and vectors. Firstly, we consider two random variables X X and Y Y with distributions F F and G G respectively. We assume that the random variables satisfy a type of a weak dependence structure. Under mild conditions, we study if the distribution of X ​ Y X\,Y , denoted by H H , belongs to the same distribution class of F F . The closure property under this specific weak dependence structure, in the classes 𝒞 , 𝒟 , ℳ , ℳ ∗ , 𝒪 ​ 𝒮 , 𝒪 ​ ℒ , 𝒫 𝒟 \mathcal{C},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M^{*}},\mathcal{OS},\mathcal{OL},\mathcal{P_{D}} and 𝒦 \mathcal{K} is established. Secondly, we introduce a distribution class, which satisfies some closure properties with respect to product and finite mixture. Further, we provide some applications on randomly weighted sums and on discrete-time risk model with dependent actuarial and financial risks. The multivariate regular variation is already well-established, but in the case of other heavy tailed classes, the notions of their multivariate distributions is missing. For this reason we introduce the class of dominatedly varying and positively decreasing random vectors and we study the closure property of the independent scalar product. Furthermore, we explore the closure property of the first class under sum and mixture, and we examine the distribution of randomly stopped sums, whose summands are random vectors that belongs to this class. Some of these properties hold and for positively decreasing random vectors.



Keywords:

distribution of product ; long tailed positive decrease; dominatedly varying vectors; positively decreasing vectors

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\,\sup_{y\in S_{Y}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X>x\;|\;Y=y]}{h(y)\,\mathbf{P}[X>x]}-1\right|=0\,.

3.
Distribution class
\mathcal{T}

In this section we introduced a new class of heavy-tailed distributions. This class have some remarkable properties, as for example the closure properties (i) with respect to the distribution product (in both independent and dependent cases), (ii) with respect to convolution of independent random variables, (iii) with respect to the mixture of arbitrarily dependent random variables.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a distribution
F
with support the whole real axis, belongs to the long tailed positively decreasing distributions, symbolically
\mathcal{T}
, if
F\in\mathcal{L}
, and
F\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, symbolically
\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
.

It is obvious that, if
F\in\mathcal{T}
, then it follows
\beta_{F}>0
,
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}
, and
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be random variables, with distributions
F
and
G
respectively,
F
has support of the whole real axis and for
G
we assume

G(0-)=0\,,\;G(0)<1
. If
X
and
Y
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and
2.2
and
F\in\mathcal{T}
is true, then it holds
H\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

By combination of Theorem
2.2
and
[
12
, Th. 2.1(i)]
, we obtain the desired result. ∎

The following result renders closure properties of class
\mathcal{T}
, which are not relevant to the concept of the product distribution. For this reason we need some extra notations. Let remind for some random variables
X_{1},\,X_{2}
, with distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}
respectively, which belong to class
\mathcal{B}
, and now considering the
X_{1},\,X_{2}
are arbitrarily dependent, we say that this class satisfies the closure property with respect to mixture if
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, holds for any
p\in(0,\,1)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

Let
X_{1},\,X_{2}
be arbitrarily dependent random variables with distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
respectively, then, for any
p\in(0,\,1)
, it holds
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
.

(2)

Let
X_{1},\,X_{2}
be arbitrarily dependent random variables with
F_{1}\in\mathcal{T}
and either
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
, or
\overline{F}_{2}(x)=o\left[\overline{F}_{1}(x)\right]
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, then, for any
p\in(0,\,1)
, it holds
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let take arbitrarily chosen
v>1
. Then for any
p\in(0,\,1)
we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{p\,\overline{F}_{1}(v\,x)+(1-p)\,\overline{F}_{2}(v\,x)}{p\,\overline{F}_{1}(x)+(1-p)\,\overline{F}_{2}(x)}\leq\max\left\{\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{1}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)},\dfrac{\overline{F}_{2}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}_{2}(x)}\right\}<1\,,

hence
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
.

(2)

In the case
F_{1}\in\mathcal{T}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
, by
[
24
, Cor. 2.23]
we have that since
F_{1}\in\mathcal{L}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
, then it holds
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
. This result, together with part (1), implies
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
.

In the other case, where
F_{1}\in\mathcal{T}
and
\overline{F}_{2}(x)=o\left[\overline{F}_{1}(x)\right]
, with
F_{2}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, by
[
38
, Prop. 3.9]
we obtain that
p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
. Their combination, together with part (1), renders the required result.
∎

4.
Applications

Next, we provide some applications that demonstrate the previous results on randomly weighted sums and ruin probabilities in a discrete-time risk model with actuarial and financial risks. Let us introduce the notation

Example 4.1

(1)

(2)

Proof.

5.
Dominatedly varying random vectors

Recently the competition among the insurance companies and financial institutions created the need to study the heavy tailed distributions, not individually, but in vector form, where the dependence among components is permitted. The most known random vector of this kind, represented by the multivariate regular variation, was introduced in
[
27
]
. In this case, the approximation describes a random vector with regularly varying components, with identical regular variation index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
(see in
[
47
]
and in relation with risk theory applications in
[
34
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
14
]
).

There were several attempts to define random vectors in larger classes of heavy tailed distributions, mainly in multivariate subexponential distributions. In
[
17
]
was proposed the multivariate subexponentiality through point processes. Later, in
[
45
]
were given three different definitions of multivariate subexponential distributions, in more direct way. Finally in
[
50
]
was introduced another definition of multivariate subexponential distribution, which was accompanied by a continuous time risk model application. Our definitions in section 5 and 6 for the classes
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{P_{D}}
are inspired by these from
[
45
]
for the subexponential class
\mathcal{S}
, but there exists a basic difference. While in
[
45
]
was considered the distribution tail of the form
{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf t}\,x):=1-{\bf F}({\bf t}\,x)=1-\mathbf{P}[X_{1}\leq t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}\leq t_{n}\,x]
, namely, at least on excess for
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
, we suggest the distribution tail (
5.1
), where the excesses of the components happen all together.

Let consider a random vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n})
, whose distribution tail is defined by the formula

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf t},\,x)=\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]\,,

(5.1)

for any
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\,\ldots,\,t_{n})\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. We notice that, if for some component of
{\bf t}
, we have
t_{i}=\infty
, then we get automatically reduction of the dimension.

Let us introduce a new class of distributions and demonstrate its usage. The
{\bf t}
plays the role of tail convergence and in fact helps the convergence flexibility, and its domain of definition was chosen similarly to that in
[
45
]
. In relation to
[
45
]
, which focus mainly on multivariate subexponential and multivariate long-tailed distributions, here the characterization of the classes is carried out only through the joint tail, having in mind that the immediateness with uni-variate definitions, and the presence of single big jump principle of each component as well.

Definition 5.1
.

Let random vector
{\bf X}
with distribution
{\bf F}
and its marginal distributions
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}
. We assume that there exists a distribution
F
, such that
F\in\mathcal{D}
and
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. If further, it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf t},\,x)}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}<\infty\,,

(5.2)

for all
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
and all (or equivalently, for some)
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
, then we say that random vector
{\bf X}
follows multivariate dominatedly varying distribution with weak kernel
F
and we write
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

Remark 5.1
.

From condition
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, we obtain
F_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, (see, for example
[
38
, Prop. 3.7(i)]
. Specifically, in class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, there exist by definition a weak kernel.

In the case, when vector
{\bf X}
has non-negative components, a nice feature, which appears in multivariate dominatedly varying distributions, is the stability under non-negative and non-degenerated linear combinations. As mentioned in
[
50
]
, many well established multivariate extensions of one dimensional definitions in probability theory, have the following characteristic: if random vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n})
has the property
\mathcal{P}_{n}
(which symbolizes the n-dimensional property), then any non-negative, nondegenerated to zero, linear combination
\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}X_{i}
has the property
\mathcal{P}_{1}
. For example the multivariate normal and the multivariate regular variation have this feature. Actually, in the non-negative case, the class of multivariate dominatedly varying distributions is equipped this device. Let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n})
with distribution
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, then for any linear combination and any
b\in(0,\,1)
holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{P\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}X_{i}>b\,x\right]}{P\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}X_{i}>x\right]}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{P\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}>b\,\dfrac{x}{\check{l}}\right]}{P\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}>\dfrac{x}{\widehat{l}}\right]}<\infty\,,

where
\check{l}:=\max\{l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{n}\}
,
\widehat{l}:=\min\{l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{n}\}
, and the last step follows by the closure property of class
\mathcal{D}
with respect to the convolution of arbitrarily dependent random variables, see
[
9
, Prop. 2.1]
or
[
38
, Prop. 3.8(i)]
.

The Definition
5.1
, permits arbitrarily dependent components of
{\bf X}
. If the following relation holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{P(X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x)}{\overline{F}(x)}>0\,,

(5.3)

then the components of
\bf{X}
are pairwise asymptotically dependent. Furthermore, relation (
5.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}>0\,,

for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
and any (or equivalently, for some)
{\bf b}>{\bf 1}
(namely
b_{i}>1
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
). We observe that if the components of the random vector
{\bf X}
are independent and have weak equivalent marginal tails, then we get
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Furthermore, we find out that because the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
requires that relation (
5.2
) holds for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
, and having in mind the reductions of the dimension, when appears
t_{i}=\infty
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, it follows the implication: If
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, then
{\bf F}_{(n-k)}\in\mathcal{D}_{n-k}
, for any
k=1,\,\ldots,\,n-1
, where by
{\bf F}_{(n-k)}
is denoted the distribution of the vector, that contains
n-k
components from the initial
n
ones (namely, reducing each time the
k
components with infinite value for the components of
{\bf t}
).

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be random vector, with distribution
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]\asymp\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]\,,

(5.4)

as
x\to\infty
, for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
.

Proof.

The relation (
5.4
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}<\infty\,,

(5.5)

We study separately three cases. In the first case, we consider
{\bf t}\in(0,\,1]^{n}
, and since from the property of class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
we get directly the upper bound in (
5.5
), for the lower bound we find

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}=1>0\,,

for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,1]^{n}
, hence we established (
5.5
).

In the second case, we examine
{\bf t}\in(1,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. Now the lower bound of (
5.5
) is provided directly by class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
properties. So, it remains to check only if the upper bound is finite. Indeed, it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}=1<\infty\,.

Here, we should notice that in case
t_{i}=\infty
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, we find only dimension reduction.

Finally, in the third case, whence
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\,\ldots,\,t_{n})
has some components in the interval
t_{i}\in(0,\,1]
and the others in the interval
t_{j}\in(1,\,\infty]
, for at least one
i\neq j\in\{1,\,\ldots,\,n\}
. Without loss of generality, for the sake of simplicity, we consider the case
t_{1}\in(0,\,1]
and
t_{j}\in(1,\,\infty]
for
j=2,\,\ldots,\,n
, and all the other cases follow similarly. Thus, for the upper bound we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,X_{2}>t_{2}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,X_{2}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,X_{2}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,X_{2}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}<\infty\,,

where in the last step we used the class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
property of
{\bf X}
, since
t_{1}\in(0,\,1]
. For the lower bound we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,X_{2}>t_{2}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,X_{2}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,X_{2}>t_{2}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x,\,X_{2}>x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>x]}>0\,,

where in the last step we used again the
\mathcal{D}_{n}
property for
{\bf X}
, and the fact that
t_{j}\in(1,\,\infty]
for
j=2,\,\ldots,\,n
and this completes the proof.
∎

From Corollary
5.1
, follows that, if
{\bf X}
has distribution
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, and satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}{\overline{F}(x)}>0\,,

(5.6)

for some
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
, then has pairwise asymptotically dependent components. This permits even more flexibility in comparison with (
5.3
).

Remark 5.2
.

Let assume that
F\in\mathcal{D}
and

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)\,,

as
x\to\infty
, where
F_{i}
denotes a marginal distributions of vector
{\bf X}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, and let assume the asymptotic dependence among components, namely relation (
5.6
) holds, for all
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. Then these conditions are sufficient to obtain
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, that means to get (
5.2
). Indeed, for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
and for any
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
we find

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\delta\,\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x]}{\alpha\,\overline{F}(x)}\leq\dfrac{\delta}{\alpha}\,C_{1}<\infty\,,

where the real number
\alpha\in(0,\,1]
follows from the asymptotic dependence among the components of
{\bf X}
, the real number
0<\delta<\infty
follows from the fact that these components belong to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
C_{1}>0
appears because of

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)\,,

as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, and by properties of class
\mathcal{D}
. The same happens when the components are independent, and the proof is straight-forward. Hence, in these two extreme cases of dependence, namely the complete independence and the asymptotic dependence, relation (
5.2
) is unnecessary for the definition of class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, however there exist several intermediate cases of dependence, that do not imply (
5.2
) directly.

Next we consider closure properties for
\mathcal{D}_{n}
with respect to the product distribution. Here, for the product distribution of
Y
and
{\bf X}
, we keep in mind the distribution of their scalar multiplication, namely

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x):=\mathbf{P}[Y\,{\bf X}>{\bf t}\,x]=\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]\,,

for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. Therefore, assuming
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, in the case of independence between
Y
and
{\bf X}
, we wonder whether
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

An application of the scalar products in insurance or financial framework is the following. Let us consider n-lines of business, the net-losses, described by the random variables
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
, and the random variable
Y
denotes the common discount factor, over a given time interval. Then, the product
Y\,{\bf X}
represents the discount net claims of the
n
portfolios.

Assumption 5.1
.

There exists function
a\;:\;[0\,,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\,\infty)
such that hold

(1)

a(x)\longrightarrow\infty\,
,

(2)

a(x)=o(x)\,
,

(3)

\overline{G}[a(x)]=o\left[{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)\right]\,
,

as
x\to\infty
, for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
, where
G(x)=\mathbf{P}[Y\leq x]
.

It is easy to see that Assumption
5.1
is more strict than the Assumption
2.1
. This can be verified by Remark
2.1
and the inequalities

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf 1},\,x)\leq\overline{H}_{i}(x)\,,

for every
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. Because of the fact that the marginals of the vectors we use, follow heavy-tailed distributions, and therefore their right endpoint is infinite, Assumption
5.1
(3) is satisfied when distribution
G
has bounded from above support.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be random vector and
Y
be random variable, with distributions
{\bf F}
and
G
respectively, and let assume that
G(0-)=0\,,\;G(0)<1
. If
Y
and
{\bf X}
are independent, Assumption
5.1
is valid and holds
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, then it holds
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

Proof.

Likely to uni-variate case, we show only the case where
\overline{G}(x)>0
, for any
x>0
, since in case
G
has support with finite right endpoint, the argument follows similarly, putting instead of
a(x)
, the right endpoint of the support of
G
.

Let
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
and
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. From Assumption
5.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{a(x)}+\int_{a(x)}^{\infty}\right)\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x/y,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x/y\right]}{\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}\,G(dy)

\displaystyle=:\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{I_{1}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)+I_{2}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}\,.

Now, we calculate

\displaystyle I_{2}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)\leq\int_{a(x)}^{\infty}\,G(dy)=\overline{G}[a(x)]=o\left[{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)\right]\,,

as
x\to\infty
, due to Assumption
5.1
. Hence, taking into account relation (
5.7
) we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_{0}^{a(x)}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x/y,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x/y\right]}{\int_{0}^{a(x)}\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x/y,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x/y\right]\,G(dy)}\,G(dy)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sup_{0<y\leq a(x)}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x/y,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x/y\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x/y,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x/y\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x\right]}<\infty\,,

where in the last step we used the condition
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. This way, we obtain that the multivariate tail, satisfies the desired property of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

It remains now, to check if
{\bf H}
has weak kernel. Indeed, it is enough to choose in the initial vector
{\bf F}
as weak kernel
F
some of the marginal distributions
F_{i}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. Then holds
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{j}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i,\,j=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, by definition of class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, for
{\bf F}
and further by Assumption
5.1
, the inequality
{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf 1},\,x)\leq\overline{H}_{i}(x)
and the class
\mathcal{D}
property, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{i}>x]=\left(\int_{0}^{a(x)}+\int_{a(x)}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>\dfrac{x}{y}\right]\,G(dy)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a(x)}\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>\dfrac{x}{y}\right]\,G(dy)+\overline{G}[a(x)]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>\dfrac{x}{a(x)}\right]+o\left(\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{i}>x]\right)\,,

as
x\to\infty
. Hence, from class
\mathcal{D}
property and the previous relation, it follows

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{H}_{i}(x)}{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}<\infty\,.

(5.8)

From the other side, for any arbitrarily chosen
\varepsilon\in(0,\,1)
, we have the convergence

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>\dfrac{x}{y}\right]\,\mathbf{P}[Y\in dy]

(5.9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]\,\mathbf{P}[Y\in(\varepsilon,\,1]]+\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]\,\mathbf{P}[Y>1]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1\wedge d)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]\,\left(\mathbf{P}[Y\in(\varepsilon,\,1]]+\mathbf{P}[Y>1]\right)\longrightarrow(1\wedge d)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]\,,

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, where was used that
\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>x/y\right]\geq\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]
in the second integral, that means when
y\in(1,\,\infty)
, while
d\in(0,\,1]
comes as lower bound of this fraction for any
y\in(\varepsilon,\,1)
, with
X_{i}
from class
\mathcal{D}
. Thence by relation (
5.9
) we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{H}_{i}(x)}{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{(1\wedge d)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}>0\,,

so we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}{\overline{H}_{i}(x)}<\infty\,.

(5.10)

Whence, from (
5.8
) and (
5.10
) follows the relation
\overline{H}_{i}(x)\asymp\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
and therefore we get
\overline{H}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{H}_{j}(x)
as
x\to\infty
, for any
i,\,j=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, because
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{j}(x)
as
x\to\infty
, for any
i,\,j=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. Hence, we get
H_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
by the closure property of class
\mathcal{D}
with respect to weak equivalence (see
[
38
, Prop. 3.7(i)]
). Therefore, we find that
{\bf H}
has weak kernel, and concretely, we can choose anyone from
H_{1},\,\ldots,\,H_{n}
as weak kernel. So we conclude that
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.
∎

Assumption 5.2

Any n-dimensional vector, consisting of some components of

Example 5.1
.

Let
{\bf X_{1}}
and
{\bf X_{2}}
be independent random vectors, and hold

, with

their weak kernels respectively, satisfying

, as
x\to\infty

, for a vector whose one component is component of
{\bf X}_{1}

{\bf X}_{2}
, then the following upper limit is finite

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\;X_{22}>b_{2}\,t_{2}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[X_{22}>b_{2}\,t_{2}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]}\,,

for every

weak kernels, because of the assumption of weak equivalence between the two weak kernels. It is obvious that if one random vector have two components from the same primary vector (namely from

Now we need a preliminary lemma, which represents partial generalization of
[
54

, under arbitrary dependence but under the restriction in the frame of class
\mathcal{D}
. Let us remind, for any two non-negative random variables
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, are true the following elementary inequalities for

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{X}_{1}+{X}_{2}>x]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>\dfrac{x}{2}\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{2}>\dfrac{x}{2}\right]\,,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{X}_{1}+{X}_{2}>x]\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\,\left(\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x]+\mathbf{P}[X_{2}>x]\right)\,.

(5.12)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X,\,X_{i},\,Y,\,Y_{i}
be non-negative random variables with the distributions
F
,
F_{i}
,
G
,
G_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
respectively. If
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)
and
\overline{G}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{G}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, then it holds
\overline{F}_{X_{i}+Y_{i}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{X+Y}(x)
as
x\to\infty
, where
\overline{F}_{X+Y}(x)=\mathbf{P}[X+Y>x]
and
\overline{F}_{X_{i}+Y_{i}}(x)=\mathbf{P}[X_{i}+Y_{i}>x]
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)
and
\overline{G}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{G}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}<\infty\,,\;\;0<\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{G}_{i}(x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{G}_{i}(x)}{\overline{G}(x)}<\infty\,,

(5.13)

and taking into consideration that the random variables are non-negative, we apply the elementary inequalities (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) to find

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{X_{i}+Y_{i}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{X+Y}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)+\overline{G}_{i}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}{\overline{F}(x)+\overline{G}(x)}\leq 2\,\left(\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}{\overline{F}(x)}\bigvee\dfrac{\overline{G}_{i}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}{\overline{G}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty\,,

where in the last step we used that
F_{i},\,G_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
and the relations (
5.13
). From the other side, we get similarly

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{X_{i}+Y_{i}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{X+Y}(x)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}\left(x\right)+\overline{G}_{i}\left(x\right)}{\overline{F}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)+\overline{G}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\left(\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{i}\left(x\right)}{\overline{F}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}\bigwedge\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{G}(x)}{\overline{G}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}\right)>0\,.

From the previous inequalities we conclude that
\overline{F}_{X_{i}+Y_{i}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{X+Y}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\bf X}_{1},\;{\bf X}_{2}
be non-negative, arbitrarily dependent random vectors, with distributions
{\bf F}_{1},\;{\bf F}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
respectively, and assume Assumption
5.2
is true. Then it holds

{\bf F}_{{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

Proof.

At first, we show the assertion for the case of
n=2
and next we apply induction.

Indeed for
n=2
we obtain
\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]=\mathbf{P}[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,X_{12}+X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]
. So, by (
5.11
) we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]=\mathbf{P}[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}+X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>\dfrac{t_{1}}{2}\,x\,,\;X_{12}+X_{22}>\dfrac{t_{2}}{2}\,x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>\dfrac{t_{1}}{2}\,x\,,\;X_{12}+X_{22}>\dfrac{t_{2}}{2}\,x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>\dfrac{t_{1}}{2}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>\dfrac{t_{2}}{2}\,x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}2\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}2\,x\right]

(5.14)

\displaystyle\;+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>\dfrac{t_{1}}{2}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>\dfrac{t_{2}}{2}\,x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>\dfrac{t_{1}}{2}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>\dfrac{t_{2}}{2}\,x\right]\,.

For the lower bound, we use successively (
5.12
) to find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}+X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}+X_{22}>t_{2}\,x\right]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x\right]

(5.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x\right]\Big)\,.

In relations (
5
) and (
5
) there are joint probabilities, whose random variables belong to the same random vector, therefore they satisfy assumption
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, but there are some other joint probabilities whose random variables do not belong to the same random vector, as for example in the probability
\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x]
, but these probabilities also belong in the class
\mathcal{D}_{2}
because of Assumption
5.2
. Hence, from relations (
5
) and (
5
) we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\dfrac{x}{2}\right]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]}\leq 4\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{P_{1}(x)+P_{2}(x)+P_{3}(x)+P_{4}(x)}{P^{\prime}_{1}(x)+P^{\prime}_{2}(x)+P^{\prime}_{3}(x)+P^{\prime}_{4}(x)}\leq 4A^{2^{2}}(x)<\infty,

where

\displaystyle P_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\right]\,,\quad P_{2}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\right]\,,

\displaystyle P_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\right]\,,\quad P_{4}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,\dfrac{x}{4}\right]\,,

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x]\,,\qquad P^{\prime}_{2}(x)=\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]\,,

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x]\,,\qquad P^{\prime}_{4}(x)=\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]\,,

and
A^{2^{2}}(x)
denotes the maximum of the upper limits of the fractions, as is shown in the expression

\displaystyle A^{2^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\max\Bigg\{\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}\dfrac{x}{4},\;X_{12}>t_{2}\dfrac{x}{4}\right]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x]},\;\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{11}>t_{1}\dfrac{x}{4},\,X_{22}>t_{2}\dfrac{x}{4}\right]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x]},

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\dfrac{x}{4}\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{12}>t_{2}\,x\right]},\;\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\dfrac{x}{4}\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\dfrac{x}{4}\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x\,,\;X_{22}>t_{2}\,x\right]}\Bigg\}\,.

Because each term belongs in class
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, we find that
A^{2^{2}}(x)<\infty
, and furthermore by Lemma
5.1
we obtain that
\overline{F}_{X_{1}+X_{2}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{X_{1i}+X_{2i}}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, where
F_{X_{1}}
,
F_{X_{2}}
are the weak kernels of
{\bf F}_{1},\;{\bf F}_{2}
respectively, therefore we established the relation
{\bf F}_{{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

Next, we employ an induction argument. From the closure property with respect to sum for
n=2
, we assume that for some
n-1\geq 2
the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}^{\prime}+{\bf X}_{2}^{\prime}>{\bf t}\,x]\leq\sum_{z_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbf{P}(z_{i})\,,\qquad\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}^{\prime}+{\bf X}_{2}^{\prime}>{\bf t}\,x]\geq\dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{z^{*}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}}\mathbf{P}(z^{*}_{i})\,,

(5.16)

where the
{\bf X}_{1}^{\prime}
and
{\bf X}_{2}^{\prime}
represent
(n-1)
-dimensional vectors. The set
{\mathbb{Z}}
contains all the events of the type
\left\{X_{i_{1},1}>t_{1}\,x/2,\,\ldots,\,X_{i_{n-1},n-1}>t_{n-1}\,x/2\right\}
, where
i_{1},\,\ldots,\,i_{n-1}\in\{1,\,2\}
. The set
{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
contains all the events of the form
\left\{X_{i_{1},1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{i_{n-1},n-1}>t_{n-1}\,x\right\}
, where
i_{1},\,\ldots,\,i_{n-1}\in\{1,\,2\}
. We can observe that the
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
have cardinal number
2^{n-1}
.

Now, we show the first relation in (
5.16
) with
n
, for
{\bf X}_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
{\bf X}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\bigg[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1(n-1)}+X_{2(n-1)}>t_{n-1}\,x,\;X_{1n}>\dfrac{t_{n}}{2}\,x\bigg]

(5.17)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{P}\bigg[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1(n-1)}+X_{2(n-1)}>t_{n-1}\,x,\;X_{2n}>\dfrac{t_{n}}{2}\,x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbf{P}\left[z_{i},\;X_{1n}>\dfrac{t_{n}}{2}\,x\right]+\sum_{z_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbf{P}\left[z_{i},\;X_{2n}>\dfrac{t_{n}}{2}\,x\right]\,,

where the last step follows from the induction hypothesis in (
5.16
). Therefore, from (
5.17
) we find that the probability
\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]
is bounded from above by the sum of finite terms, whose cardinal number is
2^{n}
, and represent
n
-dimensional vectors.

Next, we consider the second relation in (
5.16
). Through relation (
5.12
) and the inductive assumption in (
5.16
), we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\Big(\mathbf{P}[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1(n-1)}+X_{2(n-1)}>t_{n-1}x,\,X_{1n}>t_{n}x]

(5.18)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{P}[X_{11}+X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1(n-1)}+X_{2(n-1)}>t_{n-1}\,x,\;X_{2n}>t_{n}\,x]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2^{n}}\,\left(\sum_{z^{*}_{i}\in Z^{*}}\mathbf{P}\left[z^{*}_{i},\;X_{1n}>t_{n}\,x\right]+\sum_{z^{*}_{i}\in Z^{*}}\mathbf{P}\left[z^{*}_{i},\;X_{2n}>t_{n}\,x\right]\right)\,.

Thence, by relations (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), we establish both inequalities (
5.16
) for any
n\geq 3
.

Next, we see that all
n
-dimensional vectors from the right hand sides in relations (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) belong to class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, and from these formulas we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,\dfrac{x}{2}\right]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}>{\bf t}\,x]}\leq 2^{n}\,A^{2^{n}}(x)<\infty\,,

(5.19)

where
A^{2^{n}}(x)
is defined as maximum of the corresponding
2^{n}
fractions, containing the distribution tails of
n
-dimensional random vectors, similarly to
A^{2^{2}}(x)
. The
A^{2^{n}}(x)
is finite, as far as all the
n
-dimensional random vectors belong to class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and again from Lemma
5.1
the components of the summands have weak equivalent tails, so anyone of the marinals of the new vector
{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}
, can play the role of weak kernel, since the class
\mathcal{D}
has the closure property with respect to sum. Hence
{\bf F}_{{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Relation (
5.19
) renders the closure property of class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
under non-negative, arbitrarily dependent random vectors, each of which has arbitrarily dependent components. This has important consequences, as in most risk models is allowed some dependence among the components of the random vectors but among the random vectors still remains independence. Furthermore, in most cases is applied the multivariate regular variation framework, which belongs to class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, as it follows from Theorem
6.2
below, in case of asymptotic dependent components. For example, on these risk models we refer to
[
34
]
and
[
39
]
. In some other works the dependence among random vectors but with their components mutually independent was studied, as for example in
[
67
]
and
[
68
]
.

Next we introduce the randomly stopped sum
S_{N}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}
, with
S_{0}=0
, where
\{X_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represents a sequence of random variables, with distributions
\{F_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
respectively, and
N
represents a discrete random variable, independent of
\{X_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, whose distribution has as support some subset of
\{0,1,...\}
. The probability mass function of
N
can be given through
p_{n}:=P[N=n]
, with
p_{0}<1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}=1\,.

We should mention that if
N
has support with lower bound
0
, then put
\kappa=1
, while if it has support with lower bound an integer greater or equal to unity, then we denote this bound by
\kappa
. The randomly stopped sum
S_{N}
has distribution
F_{S_{N}}
, whose tail is given by

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(x):=P(S_{N}>x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}P(S_{n}>x)\,,

for any
x>0
, with

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\,.

For example, in insurance framework the sequence of
\{X_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represents the losses (or profits), and the random variable
N
corresponds to the number of losses over the given time interval. As a result the quantity
S_{N}
represents the total loss of the insurance business. For some papers related to
S_{N}
, in presence of heavy tails, we refer to
[
29
]
,
[
37
]
, and
[
63
]
among others.

Following the previous arguments, we examine some closure properties for class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, in multivariate frame. Namely, we take into consideration the finite mixing property, as also the randomly stopped sum

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf X}_{i}\,,

with
{\bf S_{0}}={\bf 0}
, and here
N
is defined as above and the distribution tail of
{\bf S}_{N}
takes the form

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{F}}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf t},\,x)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N}>{\bf t}\,x\right]=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}p_{d}\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf t}\,x]\,,

with
p_{d}=\mathbf{P}[N=d]
with
p_{0}<1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{j}=1\,,

when
x>0
, where

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{F}}_{{\bf S}_{d}}({\bf t},\,x)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf t}\,x\right]=\mathbf{P}[X_{11}+\cdots+X_{d1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}+\cdots+X_{dn}>t_{n}\,x]\,,

(5.20)

for every
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2}
be
n
-dimensional random vectors, with distributions
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2}
respectively. If we denote by
{\bf M}
the finite mixture, and by
\overline{\bf M}({\bf t},\,x)=p\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{1}({\bf t},\,x)+(1-p)\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{2}({\bf t},\,x)
its tail, for any
p\in(0,\,1)
, namely for any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
,

\displaystyle\overline{\bf M}({\bf t},\,x)=p\,\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}>t_{n}\,x]+(1-p)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{2n}>t_{n}\,x]\,,

then we call as weak kernel of
{\bf M}
, some distribution
M
, representing a finite mixture of the weak kernels
F_{1},\,F_{2}
of the
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2}
respectively,
M=p\,F_{1}+(1-p)\,F_{2}
, for any
p\in(0,\,1)
.

We should remark that since the weak kernels of
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2}
satisfy the relations
\overline{F}_{1}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{1i}(x)
and
\overline{F}_{2}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{2i}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, where by
F_{ji}
we denote the
i
-th marginal of
j
-th vector, with
j=1,\,2
, it follows that
\overline{M}(x)\asymp\overline{M}_{i}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, with
M_{i}:=p\,F_{1i}+(1-p)\,F_{2i}
. Indeed, from one side, since the weak kernels satisfy the weak equivalence, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{M}_{i}(x)}{\overline{M}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{p\overline{F}_{1i}(x)+(1-p)\overline{F}_{2i}(x)}{p\,\overline{F}_{1}+(1-p)\,\overline{F}_{2}(x)}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{\overline{F}_{1i}(x)}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)}\bigvee\dfrac{\overline{F}_{2i}(x)}{\overline{F}_{2}(x)}\right)<\infty\,,

where in the last step we use that the weak kernels
F_{1},\,F_{2}
are weak equivalent with respect to the corresponding marginals.

From the other side, with similar arguments we find

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{M}_{i}(x)}{\overline{M}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{p\,\overline{F}_{1i}(x)+(1-p)\,\overline{F}_{2i}(x)}{p\,\overline{F}_{1}+(1-p)\,\overline{F}_{2}(x)}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{\overline{F}_{1i}(x)}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)}\bigwedge\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}_{2i}(x)}{\overline{F}_{2}(x)}\right)>0\,.

So from relations (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) follows that if
\overline{F}_{1}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{1i}(x)
and
\overline{F}_{2}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{2i}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, then it holds
\overline{M}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{M}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.

The next result extends some known partial results, as it contains arbitrarily dependent
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
but our restriction is the non-negative random variables. For some relative results in the class
\mathcal{D}
see
[
29
, sec. 4]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
be non-negative, arbitrarily dependent random variables with distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2},\,\ldots
respectively, where
F_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
N
represents a discrete, non-degenerated to zero random variable and is independent of
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
, then it holds
F_{S_{N}}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume, for sake of simplicity, that
k=1
. In case
N=m=1
, the result is trivial. In case
N=m
, with integer
m\geq 2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(x)=p_{1}\,\overline{F}_{1}(x)+\cdots+p_{m}\,\overline{F}_{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{m}}(x)\,,

for any
x>0
. Since
X_{i}
follows distribution
F_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and they are non-negative random variables, we find by
[
9
, Prop. 2.1]
or
[
38
, Prop. 3.8]
that
F_{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{d}}\in\mathcal{D}
. Hence, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any
b\in(0,\,1)
, there exists finite constant
c_{d}<\infty
, such that for any large enough
x>0
, it holds
\mathbf{P}[S_{d}>b\,x]\leq c_{d}\,\mathbf{P}[S_{d}>x]
, therefore we obtain
\widehat{c}:=\sup\{c_{1},\,c_{2},\,\ldots\}<\infty
. So we find

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(b\,x)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[S_{d}>b\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[N=d]\leq\widehat{c}\,\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[S_{d}>x]\,\mathbf{P}[N=d]=\widehat{c}\,\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(x)\,,

for any
b\in(0,\,1)
and any
x>0
. That means

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(x)}<\infty\,,

which implies that
F_{S_{N}}\in\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Now we are ready to give the closure property of class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
with respect to finite mixture and the multivariate randomly stopped sums. We note that as in case of sum of two random vectors, when we employed Assumption
5.2
, here we repeat the same for the sum of random number of summands, but now pairwisely. Namely, the conditions of Assumption
5.2
, are satisfied by
{\bf X}_{1}
with
{\bf X}_{2}
, by
{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}
with
{\bf X}_{3}
, by
{\bf X}_{1}+{\bf X}_{2}+{\bf X}_{3}
with
{\bf X}_{4}
, etc.

Corollary 5.2
.

(1)

Let
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2}
be arbitrarily dependent random vectors with distributions
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
respectively. Then it holds
{\bf M}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

(2)

Let
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2},\,\ldots
be non-negative, arbitrarily dependent random vectors with distributions
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2},\,\ldots\,\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
respectively, and Assumption
5.2
be true pairwisely. If
N
is a discrete random variable, independent from random vectors
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2},\,\ldots
, then it holds
{\bf F}_{{\bf S}_{N}}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

Proof.

(1)

For any
p\in(0,\,1)
, and any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
and any
{\bf b}\,\in(0,\,1)^{n}
holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{p\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{1}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)+(1-p)\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{2}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{p\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{1}({\bf t},\,x)+(1-p)\,{\bf\overline{F}}_{2}({\bf t},\,x)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{p\,\mathbf{P}_{1}({\bf b}\,{\bf t})+(1-p)\,\mathbf{P}_{2}({\bf b}\,{\bf t})}{p\,\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}>t_{n}\,x]+(1-p)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{2n}>t_{n}\,x]}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}>t_{n}\,x]}\,,\;\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{2n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{2n}>t_{n}\,x]}\right\}<\infty\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{1}({\bf b}\,{\bf t})=\mathbf{P}[X_{11}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{1n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2}({\bf b}\,{\bf t})=\mathbf{P}[X_{21}>b_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{2n}>b_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]\,,

and the last step is due to the assumptions
{\bf F}_{1},\,{\bf F}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Further, by the relations
F_{1i}\in\mathcal{D}
,
F_{2i}\in\mathcal{D}
, and the closure properties with respect to finite mixture (see
[
38
, Prop. 3.7.(iv)]
) we obtain
M_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.

Next, by Remark
5.4
we know that there exists weak kernel
M
such that
\overline{M}(x)\asymp\overline{M}_{i}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, therefore we get
M\in\mathcal{D}
. So we conclude
p\,{\bf F}_{1}+(1-p)\,{\bf F}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

(2)

When
N=m=1
, the result follows directly. We consider the case
N=m
for some integer
m\geq 2
. Initially for such
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf\overline{F}}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf t},\,x)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N}>{\bf t}\,x\right]=p_{1}\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}>{\bf t}\,x]+\cdots+p_{m}\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}_{1}+\cdots+{\bf X}_{m}>{\bf t}\,x]\,,

(5.23)

for any
x>0
,
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. By Theorem
5.2
we find that each term in the right hand side of (
5.23
) belongs to class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Hence, for any
x>0
, any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
and any
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf b}\,{\bf t}\,x]\leq c_{d}\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf t}\,x]\,,

(5.24)

for some finite constant
c_{d}\geq 1
. Thus, for
\widehat{c}:=\sup\{c_{1},\,c_{2},\,\ldots\}<\infty

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf b}\,{\bf t}\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[N=d]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{d}\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf t}\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[N=d]

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widehat{c}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{d}>{\bf t}\,x]\,\mathbf{P}[N=d]=\widehat{c}\,{\bf F}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf t},\,x)\,,

with
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
, for any
x>0
and any
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
, where in second step we used relation (
5.24
). Therefore from relation (
5.25
) and the arbitrary choice of
{\bf t}
,
x>0
and
{\bf b}
we find

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf F}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf F}_{{\bf S}_{N}}({\bf t},\,x)}<\infty\,,

which represents the multivariate property we need.

Similarly we examine if the multivariate randomly stopped sum has weak kernel. If the random variable
N
takes the value
d\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots\}
, where
p_{d}>0
, then by relation (
5.20
) we find that the
X_{11},\,\ldots,\,X_{d1}
follow the distributions
F_{11},\,\ldots,\,F_{d1}
, that belong to
d
-different random vectors
{\bf X}_{1},\,\ldots,\,{\bf X}_{d}
respectively, with corresponding distributions
{\bf F}_{1},\,\ldots,\,{\bf F}_{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, therefore all these multivariate distributions have weak kernels
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{d}
respectively. Thus we see that
\overline{F}_{i1}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{i}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
. So, if the distribution of the sum

\displaystyle S_{d1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i1}\,,

is denoted by
F_{S_{d1}}
and the distribution of the sum

\displaystyle S_{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}\,,

is denoted by
F_{S_{d}}
, (where
X_{i}
are the corresponding non-negative random variables, whose distributions has weak kernels
F_{i}
), then applying Lemma
5.1
we obtain the weak equivalence

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{d1}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{S_{d}}(x)\,,

as
x\to\infty
. Similarly for the rest components of the vector we get

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{di}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{S_{d}}(x)\,,

(5.26)

as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.

From
[
9
, Prop. 2.1]
, we obtain
F_{S_{di}}\in\mathcal{D}
and from relation (
5.26
) we see that
F_{S_{d}}\in\mathcal{D}
, see
[
38
, Prop. 3.7]
. Hence,

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{S_{Ni}}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{S_{N}}(x)\,,

(5.27)

as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, where
S_{Ni}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{ji}
, that follows distribution
F_{S_{Ni}}
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
and

S_{N}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
, that follows distribution
F_{S_{N}}
, namely it is the randomly stopped sum of each component. So, since applying Lemma
5.2
we obtain
F_{S_{Ni}}\in\mathcal{D}
, from relation (
5.27
) we find
F_{S_{N}}\in\mathcal{D}
. From this and relation (
5.27
) follows that
F_{S_{N}}
is the weak kernel of this randomly stopped sum of random vectors. In combination with the behavior of the tail, that was found finite, we reach the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

A specific example of
{\bf S}_{N}
is the compound Poisson, when the
N
represents a random variable with distribution Poisson and the random vectors
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2},\,\ldots
are independent and identically distributed, with
N
independent of
{\bf X_{i}}
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy to see that in this case, when the vectors
{\bf X}_{1},\,{\bf X}_{2},\,\ldots
follow distribution
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, then the distribution of
{\bf S}_{N}
belongs also in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, as follows by Corollary
5.2
, since Assumption
5.2
is valid for independent and identically distributed vectors, because we have a special case in Example
5.1
. The compound Poisson plays crucial role in Lévy processes, see for example
[
21
]
and its references.

6.
Positively decreasing random vectors

Analogously to class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, we define the corresponding class
\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
, as multivariate analogue of the positive decrease
\mathcal{P_{D}}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be a random vector, with distribution
{\bf F}
and marginal distributions
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}
. Let assume that there exists a distribution
F
, named strong kernel, such that
F\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
and
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\sim c_{i}\,\overline{F}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, with
c_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. If holds the inequality

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf v}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{F}}({\bf t},\,x)}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}<1\,,

(6.1)

for any (or equivalently, for some)
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\,\ldots,\,v_{n})>{\bf 1}=(1,\,\ldots,\,1)
and for all
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
, then we say that the random vector has multivariate positively decreasing distribution, symbolically
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

While in class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, there was a weak kernel, namely a distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}
, such that
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, here we restrict ourselves to a strong kernel, namely to a distribution
F
, such that
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\sim c_{i}\,\overline{F}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for
c_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. The reason for this, is that class
\mathcal{P_{D}}
is closed with respect to strong equivalence, but not with respect to weak equivalence. Hence,
F_{i}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, for all marginals of vectors in
\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

In next result we present a closure property of class
\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
with respect to scalar product under the assumption of identical components of
{\bf X}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be random vector and
Y
be random variable, with distributions
{\bf F}
and
G
respectively, and assume
G(0-)=0\,,\;G(0)<1
. Additionally we assume that
{\bf X}
have identically distributed components with common distribution
F
.
If
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
, and
{\bf X}
and
Y
are independent, satisfying Assumption
5.1
, then it holds
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

Proof.

As in Theorem
5.1
, here we show only the case
\overline{G}(x)>0
, for any
x>0
, since in opposite case, we follows the same proof with only change of
a(x)
to the upper endpoint of the support of
G
.

Let
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\,\ldots,\,v_{n})>{\bf 1}
and
{\bf t}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)\}
. From Assumption
5.1
we find

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf v}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[Y\,X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,x\right]}{\mathbf{P}[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{a(x)}+\int_{a(x)}^{\infty}\right)\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{y},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,\dfrac{x}{y}\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x\right]}\,G(dy)

\displaystyle=:\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y\,X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,Y\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x\right]}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{2}(x)\leq\overline{G}[a(x)]=o\left[{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)\right]\,,

as
x\to\infty
, and therefore

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf v}\,{\bf t},\,x)}{{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf t},\,x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_{0}^{a(x)}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{y},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,\dfrac{x}{y}\right]}{\int_{0}^{a(x)}\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{y},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,\dfrac{x}{y}\right]\,G(dy)}\,G(dy)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sup_{0<y\leq a(x)}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{y},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,\dfrac{x}{y}\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,\dfrac{x}{y},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,\dfrac{x}{y}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>v_{1}\,t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>v_{n}\,t_{n}\,x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x\right]}<1\,,

where in the last step we used that
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.
Since
{\bf X}
has identically distributed components, then also the products
Y\,X_{i}
for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
are identically distributed. So, by Assumption
5.1
and the fact that
{\bf\overline{H}}({\bf 1},\,x)\leq\overline{H}_{i}(x)
,
we obtain Assumption
2.1
(see Remark
2.1
), and since the independence is included in Assumption
2.2
, applying Theorem
2.2
we find that the distribution of
Y\,X_{i}
is
H_{i}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
. So any
H_{i}
can play the role of the strong kernel.
Thus we get
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.
∎

The next result is related with the intersection of these two multivariate classes. To avoid misunderstanding, let us remind that a random vector
{\bf X}
, with distribution
{\bf F}
, belongs to the class
\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
if there exists strong kernel
F\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
and it satisfies relations (
5.2
) and (
6.1
). It is easy to see that all the components of
{\bf X}
follow distributions
F_{i}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
, hence
0<\beta_{F_{i}}\leq\alpha_{F_{i}}<\infty
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. We notice that while class
\mathcal{D}_{n}
has the property of
\mathcal{D}
for the linear combinations of its components, see Remark
5.1
, however it is not known if this is true for the classes
\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
, even in the case when the linear combinations are degenerated to unity. The reason is that very few papers up to now considered the closure properties in class
\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
with respect to sum, and these are restricted to the independence case, see
[
4
]
. In the next result we provide a closure property with respect to the scalar product for the class
\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be random vector and
Y
be random variable, with distributions
{\bf F}
and
G
respectively, and assume
G(0-)=0\,,\;G(0)<1
. Assume that
{\bf X}
has identically distributed components, with common distribution
F
. If
{\bf X}
and
Y
are independent, with
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
satisfying Assumption
5.1
, then it holds
{\bf H}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

In several applications of risk theory, can be found the following assumption
\nu(({\bf 1},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)])>0
, for the Radon measure
\nu
of
MRV
, see for example
[
34
]
,
[
15
]
among others. As was mentioned in
[
34
]
or
[
41
, Sec. 2]
, this assumption follows directly from relation

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{\overline{F}(x)}\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>t_{1}\,x,\,\ldots,\,X_{n}>t_{n}\,x]=\nu(({\bf t},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)])>0\,,

(6.2)

for any
{\bf t}\in[0,\,\infty]^{n}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, recalling that
{\bf X}\in MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)
. It is well known that relation (
6.2
) means asymptotically dependent components, which further implies that the marginals of
MRV
have weak equivalent tails, namely
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{j}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
i,\,j=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, with
i\neq j
.

Let us denote

\displaystyle MRV(F,\,\nu):=\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)\,,

and in case of asymptotic dependence we find that
MRV(F,\,\nu)
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, as also under a bit harder restriction, it belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n})
be a random vector with distribution
{\bf F}\in MRV(F,\,\nu)
and its Radon measure
\nu
be such that
\nu(({\bf 1},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)])>0
. Then it is true that

(1)

{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, hence

\displaystyle MRV(F,\,\nu)\subsetneq\mathcal{D}_{n}\,.

(6.3)

(2)

If distribution
{\bf F}
has marginals, with strong equivalent tails, namely
\overline{F}_{i}(x)\sim c_{ij}\,\overline{F}_{j}(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, with constants
c_{ij}>0
, for any
i,\,j=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, then
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
, that implies

\displaystyle MRV(F,\,\nu)\subsetneq\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}\,.

(6.4)

(3)

If the assumptions of (2) hold, then
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
, that implies
MRV(F,\,\nu)\subsetneq\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
{\bf F}\in MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)
for some
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
. From relation (
6.2
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}>{\bf b}\,{\bf t}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}>{\bf t}\,x]}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\nu[{\bf b}\,{\bf t},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)]\,\overline{F}(x)}{\nu[{\bf t},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)]\,\overline{F}(x)}\leq\widehat{b}^{-\alpha}<\infty\,,

(6.5)

for any
{\bf b}\in(0,\,1)^{n}
, where
\widehat{b}:=\wedge_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}
, and in last step we used the homogeneity of the Radon measure
\nu
. Therefore, because of (
6.5
) and the fact that all marginals have weak equivalent tails we obtain
MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)\subsetneq\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Indeed, the marginals tails are weak equivalent because of the asymptotic dependence, which follows by relation
\nu(({\bf 1},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)])>0
, see Remark
6.1
, and the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\,,

that implies any marginal can play the role of weak kernel. From the arbitrariness of
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, we find (
6.3
).

(2)

Let
{\bf F}\in MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)
for some
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then, by relation (
6.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}>{\bf v}\,{\bf t}\,x]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}>{\bf t}\,x]}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\nu[{\bf v}\,{\bf t},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)]\,\overline{F}(x)}{\nu[{\bf t},\,(\infty,\,\ldots,\,\infty)]\,\overline{F}(x)}\leq\widehat{v}^{-\alpha}<1\,,

(6.6)

where
\widehat{v}:=\wedge_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}>1
, for any
{\bf v}>{\bf 1}
. From the assumption that all marginals of
{\bf F}
have strong equivalent tails and from
\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{P_{D}}
, we find that any marginal can play the role of strong kernel of
{\bf F}
, which in combination with relation (
6.6
) gives that
{\bf F}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
. Indeed, relation
\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{P_{D}}
can be verified from the uniqueness of the characterization of the classes by their Matuszewska indexes. Hence,
MRV(\alpha,\,F,\,\nu)\subsetneq\mathcal{P_{D}}_{n}
, which in combination with the arbitrarily chosen
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
we conclude the (
6.4
).

(3)

This follows immediately by combination of (1) with (2). ∎

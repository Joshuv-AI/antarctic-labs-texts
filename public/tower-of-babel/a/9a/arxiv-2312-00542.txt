Completion of two-parameter period maps by nilpotent orbits Thanks: This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Grant No. DMS-2304981.
Haohua Deng, Colleen Robles

Abstract
. We show that every two-parameter period map admits a Kato–Nakayama–Usui completion to a morphism of log manifolds.

32G20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

Let
S
be a smooth quasi-projective variety, and let
\mathbb{V}\to S
be a polarized variation of integral Hodge structures (
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS) of weight
\mathsf{w}
. Geometric examples
\mathbb{V}=R^{\mathsf{w}}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}
are given by smooth proper morphisms
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
.

Fix a point
s_{o}\in S
, and let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{V}_{s_{o}}
and
Q:V_{\mathbb{Z}}\times V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}
be the associated lattice and polarization, respectively. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
be the image of the monodromy representation
\pi_{1}(S,s_{o})\to\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
. Passing to a finite étale cover of
S
, if necessary, we may assume that
\Gamma
is neat. Parallel transportation under the Gauss–Manin connection identifies a general fibre
\mathbb{V}_{s}
with the fixed fibre
\mathbb{V}_{s_{o}}=V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Under this identification the Hodge structure on
\mathbb{V}_{s}
defines a Hodge structure
\Phi(s)
on
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The identification
\mathbb{V}_{s}\simeq V_{\mathbb{Z}}
is well-defined up to the action of
\Gamma
. This yields a period map

(1.1)

\Phi:S\ \to\ \Gamma\backslash D\,,

with
D
a period domain parameterizing
Q
-polarized Hodge structures on
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. This period map completely describes the
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS.

Without loss of generality
\Phi
is proper
[
Gri70a
]
. Then the image
\Phi(S)
of the period map is quasi-projective
[
BBT23
]
. It is a long-standing problem to construct an algebraic completion of
\Phi
that encodes Hodge-theoretically meaningful data at infinity (specifically, data encoded by the nilpotent orbits asymptotically approximating
\Phi
) that goes back to conjectures and problems posed by Griffiths in 1970
[
Gri70b
]
. The problem is well-understood, when
D
is hermitian (§
1.2
). In general
D
is not hermitian (for example, the period domain parameterizing the Hodge structures
H^{n}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of smooth hypersurfaces
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
of degree
d\geq n+2
and dimension
n\geq 3
is not hermitian), and in this case much less is known. Our main result is an extension theorem for two-parameter period maps.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{dim}\,S=2
. There exists a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
, with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
, and a logarithmic manifold
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
parameterizing
\Gamma
–conjugacy classes of nilpotent orbits on
D
so that
\Gamma\backslash D\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
and the period map (
1.1
) extends to a morphism
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
of logarithmic manifolds. The image
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})
is a compact algebraic space.

Theorem
1.2
is a corollary of the more general Theorem
1.7
(which extends arbitrary period maps to codimension
\leq 2
boundary strata) and Usui’s Theorem
1.13
. Theorem
1.7
is a consequence of Theorem
1.14
(which asserts the existence of a completion
\overline{S}\supset S
with the property that the nilpotent cones underlying the nilpotent orbits asymptotically approximating
\Phi
along strata of codimension
\leq 2
form a weak fan) and Kato–Nakayama–Usui’s Theorem
1.11
. The key technical result of the paper is a certain finiteness statement (Theorem
2.1
), from which we are able to deduce Theorem
1.14
.

Remark 1.3

(Geometric examples)
.

The extensions
\Phi_{\Sigma}
are known to exist when
D
is hermitian, or when
\mathrm{dim}\,S=1
, cf. §
1.2
. Examples with
D
non-hermitian and
\mathrm{dim}\,S\geq 2
have been constructed only very recently
[
Che23
,
Den22
]
. In those works the
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS is induced by a two-parameter family
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
of Calabi–Yau three-folds, with Hodge numbers
(1,2,2,1)
, arising as (the mirror of) a complete intersection in a toric variety. In both cases the authors explicitly exhibit the fan necessary to complete the period maps via Kato–Usui’s construction
[
KU09
]
.

Given an arbitrary family
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
of Calabi–Yau three-folds, there are four possible (discrete) types of Hodge theoretic degenerations that may arise
[
KPR19
, Example 5.8]
. The first author has shown that if there exists a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
so that all degenerations along the simple normal crossing divisor
\overline{S}\backslash S
are of type I or type IV, then (possibly after a proper modification) there is a morphism of locally analytically constructible spaces
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
that extends the period map
[
Den23
]
. The constraint on the degeneration type ensures that the nilpotent orbits asymptotically approximating
\Phi
form a weak fan of restricted type, and the extension of the period map is then a consequence of a slight generalization Kato-Nakayama-Usui’s theory (loc. cit.).

1.2.
Completions of period maps

Suppose that
D
is hermitian and that
\Gamma
is arithmetic. (Geometric families with
D
hermitian include curves, principally polarized abelian varieties, and K3 surfaces.) In this case, there exist compactifications of
\Gamma\backslash D
, and completions of period mappings are well-understood. The two salient constructions are those of Satake–Baily–Borel (SBB) and Ash–Mumford–Rapaport–Tai (AMRT). Satake constructed a family of topological compactifications of
\Gamma\backslash D

[
BJ06
,
Sat60
]
. Baily–Borel showed that the minimal Satake compactification
\Gamma\backslash D^{*}
is projective algebraic (the Hodge line bundle is ample)
[
BB66
]
. Moreover, given any smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
with simple normal crossing divisor
\overline{S}\backslash S
, there is a completion
\Phi^{*}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D^{*}

[
Bor72
]
. The SBB compactification
\Gamma\backslash D^{*}
is singular (with log canonical singularities
[
Ale96
]
). AMRT constructed normalizations
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}\to\Gamma\backslash D^{*}

[
AMRT75
,
Mum75
]
(introducing toric geometry in the process).

Much less is known about completions of the period map (
1.1
) when
D
is not hermitian. In the very special case that the period map takes value in a hermitian Mumford–Tate subdomain of
D
, as in
[
ACT02
,
ACT11
,
Bor97
,
LPZ18
,
Roh09
]
, the SBB and AMRT constructions apply. And Sommese has shown that every period map with one-dimensional image may be completed
[
Som73
]
. In general, the goal is to develop Hodge-theoretically meaningful analogs of SBB and AMRT for arbitrary period maps. One subtlety here is that one no longer expects a good compactification of the ambient
\Gamma\backslash D
. Instead the desired analogs are projective algebraic completions of (
1.1
) that encode the same Hodge-theoretic data as SBB and AMRT. That data are the nilpotent orbits asymptotically approximating
\Phi
.

1.3.
Nilpotent orbits

Fix a period map
\Phi:S\to\Gamma\backslash D
as in §
1.1
, and a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
. To every point
s\in\partial S
, Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem
[
Sch73
]
associates a nilpotent orbit (Definition
1.4
) that asymptotically approximates (a local lift of)
\Phi
in a neighborhood of
s
. So
it is natural to try to complete
\Phi
using nilpotent orbits.

Definition 1.4
.

Set
V_{\mathbb{Q}}=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the Lie algebra of linear maps
\xi:V_{\mathbb{Q}}\to V_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
Q(\xi(u),v)+Q(u,\xi(v))=0
for all
u,v\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\check{D}
denote the compact dual of
D
. A
nilpotent orbit pair

(\sigma,F)
consists of:

(i)

A rational nilpotent cone
\sigma=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\}
of pairwise commuting
N_{j}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
(possibly zero).

(ii)

A filtration
F\in\check{D}
so that
N_{j}(F^{p})\subset F^{p-1}
. In particular, the
nilpotent orbit

\nu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\ =\ \exp(z_{1}N_{1}+\cdots z_{k}N_{k})\cdot F

is a horizontal map
\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\check{D}
.

(iii)

The nilpotent orbit satisfies
\nu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in D
if
\mathrm{Im}\,z_{j}\gg 0
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}=\nu(\mathbb{C}^{k})\subset\check{D}
denote the image of
\nu
. In a mild abuse of nomenclature, we will also refer
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}
as a
nilpotent orbit
.

Example 1.5
.

If
F\in D
, then
\nu=(0,F)
is a nilpotent orbit pair.

Example 1.6
.

If
(\sigma,F)
is a nilpotent orbit pair and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}
, then
(\sigma,F^{\prime})
is also a nilpotent orbit pair and
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}=\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F^{\prime}}
.

The approximating nilpotent orbit
\nu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
in Schmid’s theorem depends on a choice of local coordinates about
s\in\partial S
. One may think of the theorem as describing how to complete the period map locally about
s
, at least set-theoretically. The challenge is to give a global, algebraic completion of the period map that retains all the information in the nilpotent orbit that is independent of the choice of local coordinates. The information that is independent of local coordinates is the
\Gamma
–equivalence class of the pair
(\sigma,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F})
. In the case that
\Gamma\backslash D
is locally hermitian symmetric, these equivalence classes are precisely the objects parameterized by the AMRT compactification
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
,
[
CCK80
]
. Kato–Usui
[
KU09
]
and Kato–Nakayama–Usui
[
KNU13
]
developed a theory generalizing the construction of AMRT to arbitrary period domains. In order to obtain the desired global extension
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
of the period map (
1.1
) it is necessary to exhibit a “weak fan”
\Sigma
that is compatible with
\Phi
. (See §
1.5
for details.) When the fan exists, the Kato–Usui space
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
parameterizes
\Gamma
–conjugacy classes of pairs
(\sigma,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F})
. Such fans are known to exist when
D
is hermitian (one recovers AMRT) and when
\mathrm{dim}\,\Phi(S)=1
. In general, it is quite difficult to demonstrate the existence of a suitable weak fan; to the best of our knowledge, the list in Example
1.3
is exhaustive. The main technical result of this paper is the existence of a compatible weak fan when
\mathrm{dim}\,S=2
(Theorem
1.14
).

Let
\overline{S}\supset S
be some smooth projective completion with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell}
denote the smooth irreducible components of
\partial S
. The closed strata of
\partial S
are the
S_{I}=\cap_{i\in I}S_{i}
, with
I\subset\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
. The open strata are the
S_{I}^{*}=S_{I}-\cup_{|I|<|J|}\,S_{J}
. To each (connected component of)
S^{*}_{I}
, Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem associates a
\Gamma
–conjugacy class
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{I})
of (open) rational nilpotent cones. These cones have the property that
\mathrm{dim}\,\sigma_{I}\leq|I|
, and
\sigma_{I}\subset\overline{\sigma}_{J}
if
S_{J}\subset S_{I}
(equivalently,
I\subset J
). Let
\overline{S}_{(2)}
be the union of
S
with all
S^{*}_{I}
so that
\mathrm{dim}\,\sigma_{I}\leq 2
.

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
, with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
and a logarithmic manifold
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
parameterizing
\Gamma
–conjugacy classes of nilpotent orbits on
D
so that
\Gamma\backslash D\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
and the period map (
1.1
) extends to a morphism
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}_{(2)}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
of logarithmic manifolds.

We have
\overline{S}_{(2)}=\overline{S}
whenever
\mathrm{dim}\,S=2
; so that Theorem
1.2
is an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.7
and Usui’s Theorem
1.13
. Theorem
1.7
is a direct consequence of Kato–Nakayama–Usui’s Theorem
1.11
, and Theorem
1.14
on the existence of a weak fan.

Question 1.8
.

If
\mathrm{dim}\,S=2
, then
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})
is a compact algebraic space (Theorem
1.2
). It is an interesting problem to identify an ample line bundle on
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})
. The work
[
GGR21
]
and
[
GGR20
, §5]
suggests the following natural question: let
\Lambda
be the augmented Hodge line bundle on
\overline{S}
(a.k.a. the Griffiths line bundle). Do there exist integers
0<m
and
0\leq a_{i}
so that
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})=\mathrm{Proj}(\overline{S},m\Lambda-\sum a_{i}[S_{i}])
?

1.4.
Relationship to the conjectural analog of SBB

Before turning to the details of Kato-Nakayama-Usui (KNU) construction, we wish to comment on the relationship to an analog of SBB.

Each nilpotent orbit pair
(\sigma,F)
determines a limiting mixed Hodge structure. Quotienting out the extension data, we obtain a polarized Hodge structure
\phi(\sigma,F)
. This Hodge structure is independent of the choice of
F\in\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}
, so that
\phi(\sigma,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F})
is well-defined. The map
(\sigma,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F})\mapsto\phi(\sigma,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F})
is
\Gamma
–equivariant. (See
[
GGR20
]
for details.) The AMRT normalization
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}\to\Gamma\backslash D^{*}
of SBB is the map induced by
\phi
. This is the sense in which AMRT and SBB are the maximal and minimal Hodge–theoretically meaningful compactifications of
\Gamma\backslash D
, respectively.

A conjectural analog of SBB has been defined for arbitrary period maps
[
GGR20
]
. The analog is an extension
\Phi^{*}:\overline{S}\to\wp^{*}
of the period map (
1.1
). Here
\wp^{*}=\Phi^{*}(\overline{S})
is a compact Hausdorff space compactifying the image
\wp=\Phi(S)
of the period map, and the map
\Phi^{*}
is a proper, continuous extension of
\Phi
. If the period map also admits a KNU extension
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
(for example, as in Theorem
1.2
) then the map
\Phi^{*}
will factor through
\Phi_{\Sigma}
, and in this case
\wp^{*}
is the image of
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})
under the map induce by
\phi
. When
D
is hermitian,
\wp^{*}
is precisely the closure of
\wp
in the SBB compactification
\Gamma\backslash D^{*}
, and
\Phi^{*}
is Borel’s extension. In general, it is conjectured that
\wp^{*}
is a complex variety (in which case the Riemann extension theorem implies
\Phi^{*}
is holomorphic); that
\Lambda
is semi-ample on
\overline{S}
; and that
\Phi^{*}
factors through
\overline{S}\to\mathrm{Proj}(\overline{S},\Lambda)
, with
\mathrm{Proj}(\overline{S},\Lambda)\to\wp^{*}
finite. The conjecture holds when
D
is hermitian (in which case we recover SBB, and have the stronger statement
\mathrm{Proj}(\overline{S},\Lambda)=\wp^{*}
); when
\mathrm{dim}\,\wp=1

[
Som73
]
; and when
\mathrm{dim}\,S=2
and
\Phi
satisfies local Torelli
[
GGLR20
]
.

1.5.
The Kato-Nakayama-Usui construction

The nilpotent orbit pair
(\sigma_{s},F_{s})
associated to
s\in\partial S
by the nilpotent orbit theorem is well-defined up to the action of
\Gamma
. Let

\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}\ =\ \{\sigma_{s}\ |\ s\in\partial S\}\,\cup\,\{0\}

denote the collection of all nilpotent cones arising in this way, plus the trivial cone.

Remark 1.9
.

(i)

If
\sigma=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\}\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
, then
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
also contains every face
\sigma_{I}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{i}\ |\ i\in I\}
of
\sigma
; here
I
runs over all subsets of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
. By convention
\sigma_{\emptyset}
is the zero cone
\{0\}
.

(ii)

The nilpotent cones
\sigma_{s}
are locally constant on the smooth strata of
\partial S
. Consequently
\Gamma
acts on
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
with finitely many orbits.

Definition 1.10
.

The collection
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
is a
weak fan
if for all
\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
we have
\sigma=\tau
whenever:

(i)

the intersection
\sigma\cap\tau
is nonempty, and

(ii)

there exists
F\in\check{D}
so that both
(\sigma,F)
and
(\tau,F)
are nilpotent orbit pairs.

Let

D_{\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}}\ =\ \{\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}\ |\ \sigma\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}\hbox{ and }(\sigma,F)\hbox{ is a nilpotent orbit pair}\}\,.

Note that
D\subset D_{\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}}
(Example
1.5
). The quotient
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}}\supset\Gamma\backslash D
parameterizes
\Gamma
–conjugacy classes of nilpotent orbits
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,F}
with
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
.

Theorem 1.11

(Kato–Nakayama–Usui
[
KNU13
]
)
.

If
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
is a weak fan, then
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}}
admits the structure of a logarithmic manifold, and the period map
\Phi
extends to a morphism
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}}
of logarithmic manifolds.

Remark 1.12
.

Theorem
1.11
holds with
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}^{(2)}
in place of
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
, and
\overline{S}_{(2)}
in place of
\overline{S}
. That is, if
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}^{(2)}
is a weak fan, then there is a morphism of log manifolds
\Phi_{\Sigma}:\overline{S}_{(2)}\to\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
extending the period map (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.13

(Usui
[
Usu06
]
)
.

If
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
is a weak fan, then the image
\Phi_{\Sigma}(\overline{S})
is a compact algebraic space.

Theorem
1.7
is a corollary of Theorem
1.11
(Remark
1.12
) and Theorem
1.14
.

Theorem 1.14
.

There exists a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
, with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
, so that
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}^{(2)}
is a weak fan.

The theorem is proved in §
3.3
. The basic idea is to fix some smooth projective completion
\hat{S}\supset S
, and show that after finite subdivision of the cones in
\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}^{(2)}
we obtain a weak fan
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
(Lemma
3.8
) that (possibly after additional finite subdivisions) may be realized as
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}
for a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
that is obtained from
\hat{S}
by a finite sequence of blow-ups. Lemma
3.8
is a consequence of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.3
. Theorem
2.1
is the key technical result of the paper, and there is no constraint on the dimension of the cones in that theorem or its corollary. However the restriction to dimension at most two is necessary for our proof of Lemma
3.8
. The difficulty generalizing the lemma is due to the combinatorial complications that arise when
\mathrm{dim}\,\sigma\geq 3
. In some cases these complications can be circumvented; see
[
Den23
]
.

2.
A finiteness property for
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}

Fix any two rational nilpotent cones
\sigma,\tau\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, as in Definition
1.4
(i)
. Given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, set
\tau_{\gamma}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\tau)
. Define

\Gamma_{\sigma,\tau}\ =\ \left\{\gamma\in\Gamma\ \left|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\sigma\cap\tau_{\gamma}\not=\emptyset\,,\ \exists\ F_{\gamma}\in\check{D}\hbox{\ s.t.\ }(\sigma,F_{\gamma})\hbox{ and}\\
(\tau_{\gamma},F_{\gamma})\hbox{ are nilpotent orbit pairs}\end{array}\right.\right\}\,,

and let

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma,\tau}\ =\ \{\sigma\cap\tau_{\gamma}\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\sigma,\tau}\}

be the associated collection of rational nilpotent cones.

Let

\mathbf{G}\ =\ \mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Q}},Q)\ =\ \{g\in\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Q}})\ |\ Q(gu,gv)=Q(u,v)\,,\ \forall\ u,v\in V_{\mathbb{C}}\}

be the
\mathbb{Q}
–algebraic group of invertible linear maps
V_{\mathbb{Q}}\to V_{\mathbb{Q}}
preserving
Q
. The Lie group
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})
of real points is the automorphism group of
D
. Let
\mathbf{Z}_{\sigma}
and
\mathbf{Z}_{\tau}
denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
preserving
\sigma
and
\tau
, respectively. We have
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let

\pi:\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Z})\ \to\ \mathbf{Z}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbf{Z}_{\tau}(\mathbb{Z})

denote the natural projection to the double coset space.

Theorem 2.1
.

The image
\pi(\Gamma_{\sigma,\tau})
is finite.

If
\pi(\gamma)=\pi(\gamma^{\prime})
, then
\sigma\cap\tau_{\gamma}=\sigma\cap\tau_{\gamma^{\prime}}
. Consequently, we have

Lemma 2.2
.

The map
\Gamma_{\sigma,\tau}\to\mathcal{I}_{\sigma,\tau}
given by
\gamma\mapsto\sigma\cap\tau_{\gamma}
factors through
\pi
to define a surjection

\pi(\Gamma_{\sigma,\tau})\ \twoheadrightarrow\ \mathcal{I}_{\sigma,\tau}\,.

Corollary 2.3
.

The collection
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma,\tau}
is finite.

The remainder of §
2
is occupied with the proof of Theorem
2.1
. There is no bound on the dimension of the cones
\sigma
in this argument.

2.1.
Fundamental sets

To each nilpotent
N:V_{\mathbb{Q}}\to V_{\mathbb{Q}}
is canonically associated a rational increasing filtration
W(N)
of
V_{\mathbb{Q}}

[
Cat14a
, §A.3]
. If
(\sigma,F)
is a nilpotent orbit pair, then the weight filtration is independent of our choice of
N\in\sigma

[
CK81
,
CK82
]
. So
W(\sigma)
is well-defined. Moreover,
(W(\sigma)[-n],F)
is a mixed Hodge structure
[
Sch73
]
; here
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is the weight of the Hodge structures on
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Set

B_{\sigma}\ =\ \{F\in\check{D}\ |\ (\sigma,F)\hbox{ is a nilpotent orbit pair with $\mathbb{R}$-split }(W(\sigma)[-n],F)\}\,.

The centralizer
Z_{\sigma}=\mathbf{Z}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
B_{\sigma}
with finitely many orbits, each of which is open and closed
[
KP16
]
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
makes use of the notation of a fundamental set for the action of
\mathbf{Z}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z})
on
B(\sigma)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
Bor66
, §1.4]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
–algebraic group. We say
\mathfrak{H}\subset H=\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{R})
is a
fundamental set
for
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Z})
if:

(i)

\mathfrak{H}K=\mathfrak{H}
for some maximal compact subgroup
K\subset H=\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{R})
,

(ii)

\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Z})\,\mathfrak{H}=H
, and

(iii)

\{\gamma\in\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Z})\ |\ \mathfrak{H}\,\cap\,\gamma\mathfrak{H}\not=\emptyset\}
is finite.

Remark 2.5

(

\Sigma^{\prime}
. The set of one-dimensional cones

\{\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\tau_{j})\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\varrho_{a},\tau_{j}}\,,\ j=1,\ldots,m\}\ \subset\ \varrho_{a}

is finite by Corollary
2.3
. The complement in
\varrho_{a}
is a finite, disjoint union of two-dimensional rational nilpotent cones
\{\varsigma_{a,i}\}
. Let
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\varsigma_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\varsigma_{k})
be the distinct
\Gamma
-orbits of all two-dimensional cones that we obtain in this way. Let
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
be the collection obtained from
\Sigma^{\prime}
by replacing the orbits
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\varrho_{a})
with the orbits
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\varsigma_{i})
.

The collection
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
also satisfies the properties (i-iv) of Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The collection
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is a weak fan.

Proof.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\varsigma_{i},\varsigma_{j}}
. By definition of the
\varsigma_{i}
, there exist
\varrho_{a}\supset\varsigma_{i}
and
\varrho_{b}\supset\varsigma_{j}
. Then Lemmas
3.2
(i)
and
3.3
imply
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\varrho_{a},\varrho_{b}}
. Lemma
3.7
implies
a=b
. By construction of the
\varsigma_{i}
this forces
\varsigma_{i}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\varsigma_{j})
or
\varsigma_{i}\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\varsigma_{j})=\emptyset
. Thus
|\mathcal{I}_{\varsigma_{i},\varsigma_{j}}|=\delta_{ij}
.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\varsigma_{i},\tau_{j}}
. Again, there exists
\varrho_{a}\supset\varsigma_{i}
, and Lemma
3.3
implies
\gamma\subset\Gamma_{\varrho_{a},\tau_{j}}
. The construction of the
\varsigma_{a,i}
as the disjoint union

\bigcup_{i}\,\varsigma_{a,i}\ =\ \varrho_{a}\ -\ \bigcup_{j}\{\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\tau_{j})\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\varrho_{a},\tau_{j}}\}

implies
\varsigma_{i}\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\tau_{j})=\emptyset
. Thus
|\mathcal{I}_{\varsigma_{i},\tau_{j}}|=0
.

Since the one-dimensional cones necessarily satisfy
|\mathcal{I}_{\tau_{i},\tau_{j}}|=\delta_{ij}
, the theorem now follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.14

Let
\hat{S}\supset S
be some smooth projective completion with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\hat{S}\backslash S
. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell}
denote the smooth irreducible components of
\partial S
. The closed strata of
\partial S
are the
S_{I}=\cap_{i\in I}S_{i}
, with
I\subset\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
. The open strata are the
S_{I}^{*}=S_{I}-\cup_{|I|<|J|}\,S_{J}
. To each (connected component of)
S^{*}_{I}
, Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem associates a
\Gamma
–conjugacy class
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{I})
of rational nilpotent cones. The nilpotent cones associated to the codimension one strata
S_{i}^{*}
are the (at most) one dimensional
\sigma_{i}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{i}\}
. The nilpotent cones associated to the codimension two strata
S^{*}_{ij}
are the
\sigma_{ij}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{i},N_{j}\}
. And the nilpotent cones associated to the codimension
|I|
strata
S^{*}_{I}
are the
\sigma_{I}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{i}\ |\ i\in I\}
.

The collection
\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}
is the set of all
\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\sigma_{I})
, with
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
I\subset\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.

3.3.1.

Consider the blow-up
B_{ij}(\hat{S})
of
\hat{S}
along the codimension two
S_{ij}
. The one-dimensional nilpotent cone associated to the exceptional divisor is
\sigma_{E}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\{N_{i}+N_{j}\}
. If
i,j\in I
, then let
\sigma_{I\backslash\{i\},E}
be the cone generated by
N_{i}+N_{j}
and the
N_{k}
with
k\in I\backslash\{i\}
. Then the collection
\Sigma_{\Phi,B_{ij}(\hat{S})}
is obtained from
\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}
by: (i) adding the orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{E})
; and (ii) replacing the orbits
\{\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{I})\ |\ i,j\in I\}
with the orbits

\{\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{I\backslash\{i\},E})\,,\ \mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{I\backslash\{j\},E})\ |\ i,j\in I\}
.

3.3.2.

As noted in Example
3.6
, the collection
\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}^{(2)}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}\ |\ \mathrm{dim}\,\sigma\leq 2\}
satisfies the properties (i-iv) of Lemma
3.4
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
be the weak fan obtained from
\Sigma_{\Phi,\hat{S}}
, as in Lemma
3.8
. We would like to realize
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
as
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}^{(2)}
for some smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
that is obtained from
\hat{S}
by a finite sequence of blow-ups. From §
3.3.1
we see that this may require further (finite) subdivision of the cones in
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
.

3.3.3.

To see that this is possible, let
\Sigma
be any weak fan of rational nilpotent cones satisfying the properties (i-iv) of Lemma
3.4
, and with the property that
\mathrm{dim}\,\sigma\leq 2
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Let
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{\ell})\subset\Sigma
denote the distinct
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}
–orbits of two-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
. Fix a one-dimensional rational nilpotent cone
\upsilon
that is contained in some
\sigma_{i_{o}}
. The set of one-dimensional cones

\{\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(\upsilon)\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\sigma_{j},\upsilon}\}\ \subset\ \sigma_{j}

is finite by Corollary
2.3
. The complement in
\sigma_{j}
is a finite, disjoint union of two-dimensional rational nilpotent cones
\{\varsigma_{j,a}\}
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
denote the collection of cones obtained from
\Sigma
by: (i) adding the orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\upsilon)
, and (ii) replacing each orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{j})\subset\Sigma
with the orbits
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma}(\varsigma_{j,a})
. Then
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a weak fan by essentially the same argument as established Lemma
3.8
.

3.3.4.

This establishes Theorem
1.14
: after taking finitely many blow-ups of
\hat{S}
we have a smooth projective completion
\overline{S}\supset S
, with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial S=\overline{S}\backslash S
, so that
\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}^{(2)}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Phi,\overline{S}}\ |\ \mathrm{dim}\,\sigma\leq 2\}
is a weak fan. ∎

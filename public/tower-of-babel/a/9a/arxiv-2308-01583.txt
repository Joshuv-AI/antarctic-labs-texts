Values and recurrence relations for integrals of powers of arctan and logarithm and associated Euler-like sums
Xiaoyu Liu, Xinhua Xiong

Abstract
In this paper, we give evaluations of integrals involving the arctan and the logarithm functions, and present several new summation identities for odd harmonic numbers and Milgram constants. These summation identities can be expressed as finite sums of special constants such as π \pi , the Catalan constant, the values of Riemann zeta function at the positive odd numbers and ln ⁡ 2 \ln 2 etc.. Some examples are detailed to illustrate the theorems.



keywords
Integral representation, Logarithmic integral, Arctan integral, Milgram constant, Euler-like sum, Harmonic number, Polygamma function

Recently, there has been extensive research conducted by numerous scholars on the representations of integrals that involve various special functions. For example, the authors

while the authors

In

X(a,\delta,p,q)=\int_{0}^{1}x^{a}\ln^{q}(x)(\arctan(\delta x))^{p}dx

was considered and the results can be used to show

\displaystyle X(1,1,2,2)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{2}\ln^{2}(x)(\arctan(x))^{2}dx=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}h_{n}}{n(n+1)^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\pi G+\frac{151}{64}\zeta(4)-\frac{7}{4}\zeta(3)\ln 2+\frac{1}{2}\zeta(2)\ln^{2}2-2Li_{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{9}{8}\zeta(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{7}{2}\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}\zeta(3)-\frac{3}{4}\pi-\frac{1}{12}\ln^{4}2,

\displaystyle X(-2,1,5,0)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\arctan^{5}(x)dx=\frac{3}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{n+j}h_{n}h_{k}}{n(2j+1)(n+j)k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{32}\pi^{3}G-\frac{\pi^{5}}{1024}-\frac{135}{256}\zeta(2)\zeta(3)-\frac{7905}{1024}\zeta(5)+\frac{225}{256}\zeta(4)\ln 2-\frac{15\pi}{4}\beta(4).

Where

, and

H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k},h_{n}=H_{2n}-\frac{1}{2}H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}.

In this note, we investigate integrals involving arctan and logarithmic functions in a more complex form. Define

I(a,p,q,r)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{a-p}\ln^{q}(x)(\arctan(x))^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{r}}dx,

where

and

and the Milgram constants are derived. For example, in Section 3, we prove

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}h_{n}}{n(2n+4)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}t_{n}(1)}{2(n+1)(n+2)}=\frac{1}{12}(1+\pi-4\ln 2),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}h_{n}}{n(2n+4)^{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}t_{n}(1)}{(n+1)(2n+2)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{288}(-26+2\pi(-19+18G+2\pi)+104\ln 2-63\zeta(3)).

We introduce some notations that are used in the subsequent sections.
The Milgram constant
t_{k}(n)
is defined in

t_{k}(n)=\sum_{m_{n}=0}^{k}\frac{1}{(m_{n}+\frac{n}{2})}\sum_{m_{n-1}=0}^{m_{n}}\frac{1}{(m_{n-1}+\frac{n-1}{2})}\cdot\cdot\cdot\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{m_{3}}\frac{1}{(m_{2}+1)}\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{m_{2}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}+\frac{1}{2})}.

The Hurwitz zeta function is defined by

\zeta(p,a)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+a)^{p}},(\Re p>1).

Moreover, the Hurwitz zeta function has the following relation with the polygamma function,

\psi^{(n)}(z)=(-1)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(z+k)^{n+1}}=(-1)^{n+1}n!\zeta(n+1,z).

For
|z|\leq 1
, the polylogarithm function is defined by

Li_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n}},n\in N\setminus\{1\}.

When

, from
BN
,

\displaystyle W(3):=

\displaystyle\Im\left(Li_{3}\left(\frac{1\pm i}{2}\right)\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\sin(\frac{n\pi}{4})}{2^{\frac{n}{2}}n^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^{2n}}\left(\frac{2}{(4n-3)^{3}}+\frac{2}{(4n-2)^{3}}+\frac{1}{(4n-1)^{3}}\right).

The Catalan Constant
G=\beta(2)
is a special case of the Dirichlet beta function
IM

\beta(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)^{z}},\Re(z)>0.

From

\psi^{(2q-1)}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\psi^{(2q-1)}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)=2^{4q}(2q-1)!\beta(2q).

Theorem 1
.

Let
a,p,q,r
be non-negative integers and
a\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle I(a,p,q,r)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{a}\ln^{q}(x)(\frac{\arctan x}{x})^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{r}}dx=\frac{(-1)^{q}q!\Gamma(p+1)}{2^{p}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(r)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}t_{n}(p-1)}{(n+\frac{p}{2})(a+2k+2n+1)^{q+1}}.

(1)

Proof.

From
MA
,

\left(\frac{\arctan x}{x}\right)^{p}=\frac{\Gamma(p+1)}{2^{p}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x^{2})^{n}t_{n}(p-1)}{n+\frac{p}{2}},

t_{n}(p-1)=\sum_{k_{p-1}=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k_{p-1}+\frac{p-1}{2}}\sum_{k_{p-2}=0}^{k_{p-1}}\frac{1}{k_{p-2}+\frac{p-2}{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{k_{3}}\frac{1}{k_{2}+1}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k_{2}}\frac{1}{k_{1}+\frac{1}{2}},

and

\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{r}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{-r(-r-1)\cdot\cdot\cdot(-r-k+1)}{k!}x^{2k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(r)}x^{2k},

then

\displaystyle I(a,p,q,r)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{a}\ln^{q}(x)(\frac{\arctan x}{x})^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{r}}dx=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Gamma(p+1)}{2^{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(r)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}t_{n}(p-1)}{(n+\frac{p}{2})}x^{2n+2k+a}\ln^{q}(x)dx=\frac{(-1)^{q}q!\Gamma(p+1)}{2^{p}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(r)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}t_{n}(p-1)}{(n+\frac{p}{2})(2n+2k+a+1)^{q+1}}.

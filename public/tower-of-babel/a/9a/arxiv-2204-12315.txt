Nonlocal H H -convergence for topologically nontrivial domains
Marcus Waurick

Abstract
The notion of nonlocal H H -convergence is extended to domains with nontrivial topology, that is, domains with non-vanishing harmonic Dirichlet and/or Neumann fields. If the space of harmonic Dirichlet (or Neumann) fields is infinite-dimensional, there is an abundance of choice of pairwise incomparable topologies generalising the one for topologically trivial Ω \Omega . It will be demonstrated that if the domain satisfies the Maxwell compactness property the corresponding natural version of the corresponding (generalised) nonlocal H H -convergence topology has no such ambiguity. Moreover, on multiplication operators the nonlocal H H -topology coincides with the one induced by (local) H H -convergence introduced by Murat and Tartar. The topology is used to obtain nonlocal homogenisation results including convergence of the associated energy for electrostatics. The derived techniques prove useful to deduce a new compactness criterion relevant for nonlinear static Maxwell problems.

Introduction

Many physical phenomena such as the classical wave equation, Maxwell’s equations or the heat equation consist of coefficients describing the specific properties of the material the effects are considered in. These specifics are also provided in so-called constitutive relations. Classically, these relations are represented by (matrix-valued) functions depending on the material point. With this it is possible to model a composite consisting of two materials with, say, different thermal conductivities. In order to model more sophisticated physical phenomena it is not enough to restrict oneself to matrix-valued functions modelling the material’s properties. (

Let

,

Next, we provide a sufficient condition for the closedness of the range of
C
. The corresponding result is similar to the ones in
[
7
]
and
[

]
. For convenience, we present the short argument in the present situation.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be Hilbert spaces, and let
C\colon\dom(C)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}
be densely defined, closed. If
H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}}
embeds compactly into
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, then
\ran(C)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
is closed.

Proof.

First we argue, that for closedness of
\ran(C)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
it suffices to show that there exists
c\geqslant 0
, such that for all
\varphi\in\dom(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
we have

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\leqslant c\|C\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}.

(2.2)

Indeed, assume that (
2.2
) holds, and consider
(\psi_{n})_{n}
in
\ran(C)
converging to some
\psi
in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we find
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\in\dom(C)
with
\psi_{n}=C\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
. Decomposing
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}=\varphi_{0,n}+\varphi_{n}
according to
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\operatorname{ker}(C)\oplus\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
, as both
\varphi_{0,n},\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\in\dom(C)
, we get
{\varphi}_{n}\in\dom(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
and
C\tilde{\varphi}_{n}=C{\varphi}_{n}=\psi_{n}
. By (
2.2
), for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\leqslant c\|C\varphi_{n}-C\varphi_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=c\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\to 0\quad(m,n\to\infty).

Hence,
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
is a Cauchy-sequence in
H^{1}(C)
; denote by
\varphi\in H^{1}(C)
its limit. Thus, from
C\varphi_{n}=\psi_{n}
, by continuity of
C\colon H^{1}(C)\to\mathcal{H}_{1}
, it follows that
C\varphi=\lim_{n\to\infty}C\varphi_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}=\psi
. Hence,
\psi=C\varphi\in\ran(C)
and
\ran(C)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
is closed.

In order to see that (
2.2
) holds, we assume by contradiction that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we find
\varphi_{n}\in\dom(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
such that

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}>n\|C\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}.

It follows that
\varphi_{n}\neq 0
and, by possibly dividing the inequality by
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}=1
already. Thus, the assumed estimate yields that
C\varphi_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and, in particular, that
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
. By possibly choosing a subsequence, we may assume without restriction that
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
converges weakly in
H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
to some
\varphi\in H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
. From
C\varphi_{n}\to 0
and weak continuity of
C\colon H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}
, it follows that
\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}\ni\varphi\in\operatorname{ker}(C)
. This yields
\varphi=0
. However,
H^{1}(C)\cap\operatorname{ker}(C)^{\bot}
embeds compactly into
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, which implies that
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
is strongly convergent in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and, thus,
1=\|\varphi_{n}\|\to\|\varphi\|
as
n\to\infty
producing a contradiction to
\varphi=0
. Hence, (
2.2
) holds and
\ran(C)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
is closed.
∎

It is the aim of this section to revisit the Helmholtz decomposition for coefficients satisfying the well-posedness criterion in Theorem
2.2

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open. Next, we
quickly recall the

2.5

\grad\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{2}(\Omega)\to L_{2}(\Omega)^{3},\varphi\mapsto\nabla\varphi
and
\operatorname{\mathring{grad}}\coloneqq\grad|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}

-\dive=\operatorname{\mathring{grad}}^{*}
and
\curl\colon H(\curl)\subseteq L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}\to L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}
endowed with their respective maximal domains

and

\curl^{*}=\operatorname{\mathring{curl}}

\curl^{*}
is the closure of

with respect to the graph norm of
\curl

As a general assumption in this section, we shall ask
\Omega
to be

; that is, both

\ran(\mathring{\grad})\subseteq L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}\text{ and }\ran(\curl)\subseteq L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}\text{ are closed,}

where one finds settings for
\Omega
being range closed in Example
2.7

The classical Helmholtz decomposition (see e.g.
[
25

) asserts that we can decompose any
L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}
-vector field

with
L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}
-orthogonal decomposition in the form

E=\operatorname{\mathring{grad}}u+\curl H+x,

where

and

are uniquely determined. Here,

\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\dive)\cap\operatorname{ker}(\mathring{\curl})

denotes the space of
harmonic Dirichlet fields
. Tailored for static Maxwell problems, the Helmholtz decomposition with variable coefficients looks similar and still asserts a direct though in general not orthogonal decomposition. For this, we call
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\Omega)^{3})

admissible
, if the following three conditions hold:

(a1)

\varepsilon
is invertible;

(a2)

is invertible, where
\iota\colon\ran(\operatorname{\mathring{grad}})\hookrightarrow L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}

(a3)

\kappa^{*}\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa
is invertible, where
\kappa\colon\ran(\curl)\hookrightarrow L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}
.

Remark 3.1
.

(a) The assumption (a2) on
\varepsilon
implies that Theorem

applies to
C=\operatorname{\mathring{grad}}
(then

) in order that for all

admits a unique solution

. In the following we shall refer to this variational problem also by employing the usual distributional divergence
\operatorname{\mathring{grad}^{\diamond}}
which extends the above defined

, reusing the notion
\dive
and just writing

(b) Quite similarly, the assumption (a1) in conjunction with (a3) enable the usage of Theorem

\ran(C)=\ran(\curl)

Theorem 3.2

(generalised Helmholtz decomposition)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open and range closed. Let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\Omega)^{3})
be admissible. Then the following decompositions are direct

\displaystyle L_{2}(\Omega)^{3}

\displaystyle=\ran(\operatorname{\mathring{grad}})\dot{+}\varepsilon^{-1}\ran({\curl})\dot{+}\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\ran(\operatorname{\mathring{grad}})\dot{+}\ran({\curl})\dot{+}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}.

Moreover,

\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon},x\mapsto\varepsilon x

is a bijection.

Proof.

Since the rationale for both decompositions is (almost) the same, we only show the first decomposition. We show that this decomposition is direct first. Let
E\in\ran(\mathring{\grad})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\ran({\curl})
. Then we find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and

Applying
\dive\varepsilon
, we infer

\dive\varepsilon E=\dive\varepsilon\mathring{\grad}u=\dive\curl H=0.

Thus,
u=0
, by Theorem
2.2
and (a2) and, hence,
E=0
.

If
E=\ran(\mathring{\grad})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
, then we find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and

E={\operatorname{\mathring{grad}}}u=x.

Again, applying
\dive\varepsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}

\displaystyle=a_{11}-a_{10}a_{00}^{-1}a_{01}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}[a^{-1}]_{10}

\displaystyle=-a_{10}a_{00}^{-1}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{01}[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-a_{10}a_{00}^{-1}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{00}-[a^{-1}]_{01}[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}[a^{-1}]_{10}

We show that

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}[a^{-1}]_{10}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{01}[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-a_{10}a_{00}^{-1}

\displaystyle[a^{-1}]_{00}-[a^{-1}]_{01}[a^{-1}]_{11}^{-1}[a^{-1}]_{10}

\displaystyle=a_{00}^{-1}.

be continuously invertible.

Firstly, let us assume that

and

and

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{H}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{n+1}
Hilbert spaces,
(A_{\iota}^{(\ell)})_{\iota}
,
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
bounded nets in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell},\mathcal{H}_{\ell+1})
respectively converging in the weak operator topology to some

\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
let

be compact. Then

A_{\iota}^{(n)}T_{n}A_{\iota}^{(n-1)}T_{n-1}\cdots A_{\iota}^{(1)}T_{1}A_{\iota}^{(0)}\to A^{(n)}T_{n}A^{(n-1)}T_{n-1}\cdots A^{(1)}T_{1}A^{(0)}

in the weak operator topology of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{n+1})
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on
n
. For
n=0
, there is nothing to show. For the inductive step, we recall that compact operators map weakly convergent sequences to norm convergent sequences. Thus, assuming the claim is true for some
n-1
, we let
x\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
y\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}
. Then, by the induction hypothesis,

weakly converges to
z\coloneqq A^{(n-1)}T_{n-1}\cdots A^{(1)}T_{1}A^{(0)}x
as
\iota\to\infty
. The aim is to show that
A_{\iota}^{(n)}T_{n}z_{\iota}
weakly converges to
A^{(n)}T_{n}z
as
\iota\to\infty
. As
T_{n}
is compact and
(z_{\iota})_{\iota}
is bounded, we obtain
T_{n}z_{\iota}\to T_{n}z
in norm, as
\iota\to\infty
. Since computing the adjoint is continuous in the weak operator topology, we infer for
\iota

\langle A_{\iota}^{(n)}T_{n}z_{\iota},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{n+1}}=\langle T_{n}z_{\iota},(A_{\iota}^{(n)})^{*}y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}\to\langle T_{n}z,(A^{(n)})^{*}y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}=\langle A^{(n)}T_{n}z,y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{n+1}}

as
\iota\to\infty
. This completes the proof.

11
Conclusion

In this article we have introduced a nonlocal
H
-convergence topology for
\Omega
not necessarily satisfying
\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)=\{0\}
. For the case of trivial
\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)
, this topological space coincides with the topology of nonlocal
H
-convergence introduced in
[
38
]
. Moreover, for local coefficients, the newly introduced topology coincides with the topology of local
H
-convergence as introduced by Murat and Tartar in particular if
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega))<\infty
.

We have analysed electrostatic equations with coefficients converging in the newly introduced topology thus obtaining new, nonlocal homogenisation results. These results furthermore led to new compactness results akin to the Picard–Weber–Weck selection theorem with moving coefficients. This type of compactness has potential impact on nonlinear static Maxwell problems, which will be addressed in future publications. Also, the presented notion can be generalised to different boundary conditions as well as other differential operators using the notion of compact Hilbert complexes of linear operators. This, too, will be subject of a future text on this topic.

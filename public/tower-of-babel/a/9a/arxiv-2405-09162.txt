Complete and Terminating Tableau Calculus for Undirected Graph
Yuki Nishimura, Tsubasa Takagi

Abstract
Hybrid logic is a modal logic with additional operators specifying nominals and is highly expressive. For example, there is no formula corresponding to the irreflexivity of Kripke frames in basic modal logic, but there is in hybrid logic. Irreflexivity is significant in that irreflexive and symmetric Kripke frames can be regarded as undirected graphs reviewed from a graph theoretic point of view. Thus, the study of the hybrid logic with axioms corresponding to irreflexivity and symmetry can help to elucidate the logical properties of undirected graphs. In this paper, we formulate the tableau method of the hybrid logic for undirected graphs. Our main result is to show the completeness theorem and the termination property of the tableau method, which leads us to prove the decidability.

Introduction

Background

Hybrid logic is an extension of basic modal logic with additional propositional symbols called nominals and an operator called a satisfaction operator
@
. A nominal is a formula that is true only in a single possible world in a Kripke model. Nominals are regarded as syntactic names of possible worlds. Hybrid logic is highly expressive because nominals allow arbitrary possible worlds to be designated. Hybrid logic was first invented by Prior
[
18
,
19
]
and has developed in various ways since then. (See
[
11
,
5
,
1
,
17
,
10
]
.)

One of the advantages of hybrid logic compared to basic modal logic is that we can treat the irreflexivity of Kripke frames. In basic modal logic, no axiom corresponds to the irreflexivity of the model
[
14
]
. In hybrid logic, however, we have a simple axiom,
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
, where
i
is a nominal
[
7
]
.

This advantage makes hybrid logic well-suited for dealing with undirected graphs, namely relational structures with irreflexivity and symmetry. There are some logics whose semantics are given by undirected graphs; for example, orthoframes used in (the minimal) quantum logic
[
15
]
and social networks used in Facebook logic
[
21
,
22
]
can be regarded as undirected graphs.

Motivation

The motivation of this paper is to find a procedure that can automatically determine the validity of formulas for undirected graphs. In general, tableau calculus gives such a procedure if its completeness and the termination property are shown. That is, the decidability of the validity of formulas follows from the existence of a complete and terminating tableau calculus.

There are some previous research on tableau calculi for hybrid logic. Tableau calculi for hybrid logic was first proposed by
[
24
]
as a prefixed tableau calculus, which was based on the method of
[
12
]
. Later,
[
8
]
proposed tableau calculi for hybrid logic in another way, as a labeled sequent calculus. It worked better with hybrid logic than the previous method because we can
internalize
labels owing to the satisfaction operator, and we no longer need labels to construct a proof system. A subsequent paper
[
9
]
of
[
8
]
proposed a tableau calculus for hybrid logic based on
\mathbf{K4}
, and claimed that the loop-checking approach can be extended in it to the hybrid logics based on
\mathbf{S4}
and
\mathbf{S5}
.

Our study, however, focuses on a combination of axioms corresponding to irreflexivity and symmetry, namely (irr) and (sym) in
[
9
]
. This combination is worth considering from the graph-theoretic point of view because it characterizes undirected graphs as Kripke frames. On the other hand, Bolander and Blackburn
[
9
]
have not dealt with the combination of (irr) and (sym). The reason is that they considered it challenging: in
[
9
]
, they wrote, “But then we are faced with the task of combining such conditions as (irr), (sym), (asym), (antisym), (intrans), (uniq) and (tree) with (trans), and here matters are likely to be much trickier.”

Results

With the above motivation, we propose a complete and terminating tableau calculus with respect to the class of irreflexive and symmetric frames: its completeness is shown in Theorem
3.2
, and its termination property is shown in Theorem
1
. For this purpose, we show key lemmas called the model existence lemma (Lemma
10
) and the bulldozing lemma (Lemma
13
).

To address a hybrid logic with the axioms (irr) and (sym), we employ the bulldozing method, which was originally proposed in
[
20
]
. (See also
[
16
]
.) Blackburn
[
4
]
used the bulldozing method in proving the completeness of hybrid logic with the axiom (irr). Our work shows that the bulldozing method also works in dealing with the tableau calculus for hybrid logic with the rule of irreflexivity
(\mathcal{I})
in Section
3
.

Our work can be regarded as an extension of
[
4
]
in the following sense. The paper
[
4
]
showed that none of the reflexive points in the model created by a maximal consistent set has a nominal and checked that there is no matter to apply bulldozing. In our work, however, all the models created by an open saturated branch are named — each world makes at least one nominal true. We show that this situation causes no problem since all the nominals true in reflexive worlds are not an element that determines the validity of a formula but just “labels” to point to worlds.

Related Work

Examples of previous study on graph theory using hybrid logic can be found in
[
2
,
3
,
13
]
. They studied hybrid graph logic, a hybrid logic with a special operator
\diamondsuit^{+}
, as a tool for analyzing some properties of graph theory. In particular, for undirected graphs,
[
23
]
discussed how to express the planarity of undirected graphs. (Note that
[
23
]
also showed the decidability of hybrid logic for undirected graphs.)

All of these studies adopted Hilbert-style axiomatization as their proof system. On the other hand, we adopt tableau calculus as a proof system. As is well known, tableau calculi have a clear advantage compared to Hilbert-style axiomatizations in that the proof construction can be automated.

Organization of the Paper

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2.1
introduces the semantics of hybrid logic, and Section
2.2
shows the tableau system of basic hybrid logic. After that, we introduce the tableau calculus corresponding to undirected graphs in Section
3.1
, and then we show the termination property and the completeness theorem in Section
3.2
and
3.3
, respectively. Finally, in Section
4

3.2
Termination

A tableau calculus has the
termination property
if, for every tableau, all branches in it are finite.

The first thing we do is to insert a relation between nominals occurring in a branch of a tableau. It is the case that some nominals that exist in a branch play similar roles. We need to make them be grouped together as
twins
.

First, we show the quasi-subformula property.
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
does not have the subformula property that tableau calculus of propositional logic has. However, by extending the concept of subformula, we can prove a similar property.

Definition 11
(quasi-subformula)

Given two formulas of the form
@_{i}\varphi
and
@_{j}\psi
,
@_{i}\varphi
is a
quasi-subformula
of
@_{j}\psi
if one of the following conditions holds:

•

\varphi
is a subformula of
\psi
.

•

\varphi
has the form
\neg\chi
, and
\chi
is a subformula of
\psi
.

Lemma 1

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For every formula of the form
@_{i}\varphi
in
\Theta
, one of the following conditions holds.

•

It is a quasi-subformula of the root formula of
\Theta
.

•

It is an accessibility formula.

•

It is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
for some
i
occurring in
\Theta
.

Proof

By induction on the number of applied rules. We provide the proof only for the cases [
\diamondsuit
],
[\neg\diamondsuit]
, and
[\mathit{Ref}]
. The other cases are left to the reader.

[\diamondsuit]

Since
@_{i}\diamondsuit j
is an accessibility formula, it suffices to show that
@_{j}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula.
@_{j}\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}\diamondsuit\varphi
, and by the induction hypothesis,
@_{i}\diamondsuit\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of the root formula.

[\neg\diamondsuit]

@_{j}\neg\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit\varphi
. By the induction hypothesis,
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit\varphi
is either a quasi-subformula of the root formula or the formula
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
. Therefore,
@_{j}\neg\varphi
is a quasi-subformula of either the root formula or
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
.

[\mathit{Ref}]

The lemma straightforwardly follows, because
@_{i}i
is a quasi-subformula of
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i
.

Next, we define the set
T^{\Theta}(i)
, a set of true formulas in the world pointed to by the nominal
i
.

Definition 12

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For every nominal

T^{\Theta}(i)=\{\varphi\mid@_{i}\varphi\in\Theta\text{ and }@_{i}\varphi\text{ is a quasi-subformula of the root formula}\}.

Lemma 2

For all

\Theta

Proof

We call a nominal

Now we move on to prove the termination property.

Lemma 3

Given a branch
\Theta
of a tableau, we define a structure
G=(N^{\Theta},\prec_{\Theta})
where

•

N^{\Theta}
is the set of nominals occurring in
\Theta
.

•

\prec_{\Theta}
is the binary relation defined in Definition
14
.

Then
G
is a finite set of well-founded and finitely branching trees.

Proof

First, we show that
G
is well-founded. To show that, Suppose otherwise, i.e., there exists an infinite path in
G
such that

i_{0}\succ_{\Theta}i_{1}\succ_{\Theta}i_{2}\succ_{\Theta}\cdots.

For the restriction of applying
[\diamondsuit]
, each nominal
i_{0},i_{1},i_{2},\dots
is different from any others. By the definition of
\prec_{\Theta}
,
i_{n+1}
occurs before
i_{n}
. However, since
\Theta
is well-founded, that is a contradiction.

The next work is to prove that all nodes in
G
have only finite branches. However, it is directly proved from two facts:
T^{\Theta}(i)
has only finite elements for all
i
, and for each
\varphi\in T^{\Theta}(i)
,
@_{i}\varphi
generates at most one new nominal.

Finally, we show that
G
is a finite set of trees. The only nominals which can be a root of
G
are the nominals in the root formula. Since the root formula has a finite length, nominals in the root formula are also finite. Moreover, it cannot be the case that for some
i,j,k\in G
we have both
i\prec_{\Theta}k
and
j\prec_{\Theta}k
, since
[\diamondsuit]
requests not to use a nominal which has already occurred.

Lemma 4

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. Then
\Theta
is infinite if and only if we have the following infinite sequence:

i_{0}\prec_{\Theta}i_{1}\prec_{\Theta}i_{2}\prec_{\Theta}\cdots.

Proof

Since the if-part is immediate, we only show the only-if-part. Suppose
\Theta
is infinite. Then
\Theta
has an infinite number of nominals; if otherwise, the number of formulas occurring in
\Theta
are finite by Lemma

, but this contradicts that
\Theta
is infinite. Thus, for
G=(N^{\Theta},\prec_{\Theta})
in Lemma

,

is an infinite set. However, since
G
is a finite set of well-founded and finitely branching trees, there is a tree and its infinite path in

by König’s lemma, as desired.

Theorem 3.1

The tableau calculus
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
has the termination property.

Proof

By
reductio ad absurdum
.

Suppose there is an infinite branch
\Theta
. Then by Lemma
4
, there is an infinite sequence of nominals as follows:

i_{0}\prec_{\Theta}i_{1}\prec_{\Theta}i_{2}\prec_{\Theta}\cdots.

For
\Theta
and its root formula
@_{i}\varphi
, we define
Q
and
n
as follows:

Q=\{\psi\mid@_{i}\psi\text{ is a quasi-subformula of }@_{i}\varphi\},

and
n
is the number of elements in
Q
. Also, let
\Theta^{\prime}
be a fragment of
\Theta
up to, but not including, the first occurrence of
i_{2^{n}+1}
. Then
i_{2^{n}+1}
is generated by applying
[\diamondsuit]
to some
@_{i_{2^{n}}}\diamondsuit\varphi
. Taking
(\mathcal{D})
into consideration,
i_{2^{n}}
is a quasi-urfather.

However, since all of
T^{\Theta^{\prime}}(i_{0}),T^{\Theta^{\prime}}(i_{1}),\ldots,T^{\Theta^{\prime}}(i_{2^{n}})
are subsets of
Q
and
n
is the cardinality of
Q
, there exists a pair
0\leq l,m\leq 2^{n}
such that
T^{\Theta^{\prime}}(i_{l})=T^{\Theta^{\prime}}(i_{m})
by the pigeonhole principle. Therefore, we find the twins
i_{l}
and
i_{m}
such that
i_{l},i_{m}\prec_{\Theta}^{*}i_{2^{n}}
, but that contradicts that
i_{2^{n}}
is a quasi-urfather.

3.3
Completeness

Next, we prove the completeness. The basic strategy is the same as in
[
8
, Section 5.2]
. That is, define an
identity urfather
and use it to create a model from an open saturated branch of tableau. When constructing the model, make sure that the relation
R^{\Theta}
is symmetric.

Definition 16

A branch
\Theta
of a tableau is
saturated
if every new formula generated by applying some rules already exists in
\Theta
. We call a tableau
saturated
if all branches of it are saturated.

The following are some properties that a saturated branch
\Theta
has.

•

If
@_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}\varphi,@_{i}\psi\in\Theta
.

•

If
@_{i}\diamondsuit\varphi\in\Theta
which is not an accessibility formula and
i
is a quasi-urfather, then there exists some
j\in\mathbf{Nom}
such that
@_{i}\diamondsuit j,@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
.

•

If
@_{i}\square\varphi
and
@_{j}\diamondsuit i\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
.

•

For all
i
occurring in
\Theta
,
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit i\in\Theta
.

Definition 17

Let
\Theta
be a tableau branch and

of

on
\Theta
(denoted as

2.

Note that there may be no identity urfather of some

Now we show some properties of

Lemma 5

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. If

Proof

Suppose
i
occurs in the root formula of
\Theta
. Then there exists no nominal

such that

, since when we use

, we must introduce a new nominal. Then

is a quasi-urfather. Therefore,

is definable.

Lemma 6

Let
\Theta
be a saturated branch and
i
be a quasi-urfather on
\Theta
. If

, then
j\in\mathrm{dom}(v_{\Theta})

Proof

If

is a quasi-urfather, the lemma immediately holds, so suppose otherwise. Then there exists a pair of twins

such that
k\prec_{\Theta}^{*}k^{\prime}\prec_{\Theta}^{*}j
. Since

is a quasi-urfather, either
k\not\prec_{\Theta}^{*}i
or

, and

in Lemma

consists of trees, we acquire

. Thus,

and

are twins. Also for the facts that

i\not\prec_{\Theta}j=k
, and
G
is a set of trees, we have
k\prec_{\Theta}^{*}i
. Thus
k
is a quasi-urfather. Therefore,
v_{\Theta}(j)
is definable, and it can be

.

Next, we show some properties of

.

Lemma 7

Let
\Theta

If

, there is nothing to prove. Even if else,

Lemma 9

Let

Straightforward from the definition of identity urfathers.

Lemma 10
(model existence lemma)

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch of a tableau in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
and
@_{i}\varphi
be a quasi-subformula of the root formula
@_{i_{0}}\varphi_{0}
of
\Theta
, where
i
denotes an identity urfather. Then we have the following proposition:

\text{if }@_{i}\varphi\in\Theta,\text{ then }\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\varphi.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i_{0})\models\varphi_{0}
.

Proof

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

[\varphi=p,\neg p]

Straightforward.

[\varphi=j]

Suppose
@_{i}j\in\Theta
, where
j\in\mathbf{Nom}
. Since
i
is an identity urfather and

(Lemma
5
), we have
v_{\Theta}(i)=v_{\Theta}(j)
by Lemma
8
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models j
.

[\varphi=\neg j]

Suppose
@_{i}\neg j\in\Theta
, where
j\in\mathbf{Nom}
. First, by Lemma
7
, we have
@_{v_{\Theta}(i)}\neg j\in\Theta
. Second, since
\Theta
is saturated, by
[\mathit{Ref}]
, we have
@_{j}j\in\Theta
. Since
j
is also a quasi-subformula of the root formula, by Lemma
7
, we have
@_{v_{\Theta}(j)}j\in\Theta
. Hence,
@_{v_{\Theta}(i)}\neg j,@_{v_{\Theta}(j)}j\in\Theta

\Theta

[\varphi=(\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}),\neg(\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2})]

In these cases, the proofs are simple. Then we leave them to the reader.

[\varphi=\diamondsuit\psi]

Suppose
@_{i}\diamondsuit\psi\in\Theta
. From the facts that
\Theta
is saturated,
i
is an identity urfather, and
@_{i}\diamondsuit\psi
is not an accessibility formula, by

there exists a nominal

such that

, there exists a

. Hence, we have

and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(j)\models\psi

and the induction hypothesis respectively. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\diamondsuit\psi
.

[\varphi=\neg\diamondsuit\psi]

Suppose
@_{i}\neg\diamondsuit\psi\in\Theta
, and take a nominal

such that
v_{\Theta}(i)R^{\Theta}v_{\Theta}(j)
. (If nothing,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\neg\diamondsuit\psi

R^{\Theta}
, either
@_{i}\diamondsuit j
or
@_{j}\diamondsuit i
is in
\Theta
. If the former exists, by
[\neg\diamondsuit]
and the saturation of
\Theta

@_{j}\neg\psi
. If else, we can obtain it using
[\square_{\mathit{sym}}]

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\neg\diamondsuit\psi
.

[\varphi=@_{j}\psi]

Suppose
@_{i}@_{j}\psi\in\Theta
. Since
\Theta
is saturated, by
[@]

@_{j}\psi\in\Theta
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(j)\models\psi

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models@_{j}\psi

[\varphi=\neg@_{j}\psi]

We can prove similarly to the case of
\varphi=@_{j}\psi

To solve this problem, we

\ldots

Lemma 13
(bulldozing lemma)

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch. Also, let
i
be an identity urfather of
\Theta
and
\varphi
be a formula such that
@_{i}\varphi
be a quasi-subformula of the root formula. Then we have

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models\varphi\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models\varphi
,

where

i_{B}=\begin{cases}\{(i,0)\}&\text{if }i\in W_{r},\\

Proof

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

[\varphi=p,\neg p]

Straightforward from the definition of
V^{\Theta}_{B}
.

[\varphi=j]

Note that
i
is not in
W^{\Theta}_{r}
owing to Lemma

. Then we can show the equivalence in the following way.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models j

\displaystyle\iff V^{\Theta}(j)=\{i\}

\displaystyle\iff V^{\Theta}_{B}(j)=\{i_{B}\}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models j.

[\varphi=\neg j]

Similar to the case
\varphi=\neg p
.

[\varphi=\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2},\neg(\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2})]

Straightforward.

[\varphi=\diamondsuit\psi]

First, we show the left-to-right direction.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models\diamondsuit\psi
. Then there exists a world

such that

and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},j\models\psi
. If

, we have

, and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},j_{B}\models\psi

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},v_{\Theta}(i)_{B}\models\diamondsuit\psi
.

The case to watch out for is

. In this case, we have

, and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,0)\models\psi
by the induction hypothesis. However, by Lemma

, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,1)\models\psi

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,0)\models\diamondsuit\psi

The proof of the other direction is straightforward since for every

[\varphi=\neg\diamondsuit\psi]

Similar to the case

.

[\varphi=@_{j}\psi]

We only show from right to left, and the other is left to the reader.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models@_{j}\psi

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},w\models\psi

. Therefore,

holds.

[\varphi=\neg@_{j}\psi]

Similar to the case
\varphi=@_{j}\psi
.

Finally, we reach our goal.

(completeness)

The tableau calculus
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}

Proof

We show the contraposition. Suppose
\varphi

\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
. Then we can find a tableau in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
, whose root formula is
@_{i}\neg\varphi

\varphi
, and which has an open and saturated branch
\Theta

, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\neg\varphi
. By Lemma
13
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},v_{\Theta}(i)_{B}\models\neg\varphi
. Moreover, by the construction,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B}
is an orthomodel. Therefore, we can find an orthoframe which falsifies
\varphi
.

Corollary 1

\mathbf{IB(@)}
is decidable.

Proof

From the termination property of
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{IB}}
and the fact that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B}
is finite.

Note that Takeuti and Sano
[
23
]
also showed the decidability of
\mathbf{IB(@)}
.

4
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we constructed a tableau calculus that has the termination property and is complete with respect to all frames of undirected graphs. To prove completeness, the bulldozing method worked well in dealing with tableau calculus as well as Hilbert-style axiomatization.

The first task that comes to our mind as a continuation of this paper — but with difficulty — is to create a tableau calculus corresponding to
\mathbf{I4(@)}
. The bulldozing method for a transitive model was proposed by
[
6
, Section 4.5]
. Furthermore, a method to realize transitivity was also proposed in
[
9
]
. Combining these methods with the method described in this paper, it should be possible to construct a tableau calculus for the class of irreflexive and transitive frames.

Moreover, there is much remaining work to formulate tableau calculi corresponding to frames with various conditions. Many axioms characterizing some frame conditions were proposed in
[
9
]
, such as anti-symmetry, trichotomy, and tree-like. If there is a relational structure that is widely studied, creating tableau calculus corresponding to it is worthwhile.

Another direction of future work is to add other operators. Besides the satisfaction operator
@
, hybrid logic can have more operators, such as the existential operator
E
and the downarrow operator
\downarrow
. Moreover, hybrid graph logic in
[
2
]
contains other modal operators
\square^{+}
and
\diamondsuit^{+}
, and
[
23
]
introduced new modal operators
\square^{*}
and
\diamondsuit^{*}
to express the planarity.

R. B. Paris

Abstract

Tricomi given by ∫ − ∞ ∞ x ​ e − x 2 ​ ( 1 2 + 1 2 erf ​ x ) m ​ 𝑑 x \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xe^{-x^{2}}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\mbox{erf}\,x)^{m}dx as m → ∞ m\to\infty . 1. Introduction

In a short note published in the
Accademia dei Lincei

[
4
]
, F.G. Tricomi obtained the leading asymptotic behaviour of the integral arising in probability theory

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xe^{-x^{2}}\biggl(\frac{1+\mbox{erf}\,x}{2}\biggr)^{\!m}dx

as
m\to\infty
, where
\mbox{erf}\,x
is the error function defined by

\mbox{erf}\,x=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt.

The factor in brackets in the above integral satisfies
0<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\mbox{erf}\,x<1
for
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
, with the upper and lower bounds approached as
x\to\pm\infty
, respectively.
By a suitable transformation of the integration variable, Tricomi showed that the leading behaviour of this integral is given by

\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{\sqrt{\pi\log\,m}}{m}\qquad(m\to\infty).

(1.1)

In the first part of this paper we extend the result in (
1.1
) by obtaining higher-order terms in the large-
m
expansion of the integrals

I_{n,m}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-x^{2}}\biggl(\frac{1+\mbox{erf}\,x}{2}\biggr)^{\!m}dx\qquad(n=0,1,2),

(1.2)

the case
n=0
being trivial. The second part considers the evaluation of an extension of an integral arising in airfoil theory.

2. Extension of Tricomi’s analysis

Following Tricomi
[
4
]
, we make the change of variable

e^{-t}=\frac{1+\mbox{erf}\,x}{2},\qquad\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{dx}{dt}=-e^{-t}

(2.1)

to yield

I_{n,m}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-x^{2}}\frac{dx}{dt}\,e^{-mt}\,dt=\sqrt{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-st}\,dt,\qquad s:=m+1.

(2.2)

When
n=0
, we then have the trivial result

I_{0,m}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{m+1}.

To deal with the cases
n=1
and
n=2
, we require the inversion of the first relation in (
2.1
) written in the form

\mbox{erfc}\,x=2-2e^{-t}

(2.3)

to yield
x\equiv x(t)
, which is finite and continuous for
0<t<\infty
. From the well-known asymptotic behaviour
[
2
, p. 164]

\mbox{erfc}\,x\sim\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x},\qquad\mbox{erfc}(-x)\sim 2-\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x}\qquad(x\to+\infty),

it is seen that
x(t)\sim\sqrt{-\log\,t}
as
t\to 0
and
x(t)\sim-\sqrt{t}
as
t\to+\infty
, with
x(t)=0
when
t=\log\,2
.

To proceed we split the integral into two parts:
[0,\alpha]
and
(\alpha,\infty)
, where
\alpha
is finite and bounded away from zero. Then, with the change of variable
t=u/s
, we have

I_{n,m}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{s}\int_{0}^{\alpha s}x^{n}e^{-u}du+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{s}\int_{\alpha s}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-u}du

(2.4)

where
x\equiv x(u/s)
. Since
x(t)>-K\sqrt{t}
for some suitable positive constant
K
, the second integral is bounded by

\frac{1}{s}\int_{\alpha s}^{\infty}|x(u/s)|^{n}e^{-u}du<\frac{K^{n}}{s^{n/2+1}}\int_{\alpha s}^{\infty}u^{n/2}e^{-u}du=\frac{K^{n}}{s^{n/2+1}}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}n+1,\alpha s)=O(s^{-1}e^{-\alpha s}),

as
s\to\infty
, where we have employed the result for the (upper) incomplete gamma function
\Gamma(a,z)\sim z^{a-1}e^{-z}
as
z\to+\infty
.

In the first integral in (
2.4
) the dominant contribution will arise from the neighbourhood of
u=0
as
m\to\infty
.
We therefore require the inversion of (
2.3
) as
t\to 0
expressed in the form

\mbox{erfc}\,x=\frac{2u}{s}\biggl(1-\frac{u}{2s}+\cdots\biggr)\qquad(u\to 0).

(2.5)

In the appendix it is shown that (see (
A.2
))

x^{2}=\log\,s\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{L}+\frac{B(u)}{2L^{2}}+\frac{C(u)}{4L^{3}}+O(L^{-4},(sL)^{-1})\biggr),

(2.6)

where we have defined

L=\log\,s,\quad L_{1}=\log\sqrt{\log\,s},\quad a=2\sqrt{\pi},

and the first few coefficients are

A(u)=-\log\,au-L_{1},\quad B(u)=-A(u)-1,\quad C(u)=A^{3}(u)+3A(u)+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{7}{2}}$}.

We define the integrals

\lambda_{n}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log\,u)^{n}e^{-u}du,\qquad(n=0,1,2,\ldots),

so that
\lambda_{0}=1
and

\lambda_{1}=-\gamma,\quad\lambda_{2}=\gamma^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},\quad\lambda_{3}=-\gamma^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}\gamma}{2}-2\zeta(3),

where
\gamma=0.57721...
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant and
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.

Then, in the case
n=2
, we have upon extending the upper limit of integration to
\infty
(thereby introducing an exponentially small error of O
(e^{-\alpha s})
)

\displaystyle I_{2,m}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\log\,s}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{L}+\frac{B(u)}{2L^{2}}+\frac{C(u)}{4L^{3}}+\cdots\biggr)e^{-u}du

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\log\,s}{s}\biggl\{1+\frac{{\cal A}_{2}}{L}+\frac{{\cal B}_{2}}{2L^{2}}+\frac{{\cal C}_{2}}{4L^{3}}+\cdots\biggr\}\qquad(m\to\infty),

where

{\cal A}_{2}=G-L_{1},\qquad{\cal B}_{2}=-G+L_{1}-1,

{\cal C}_{2}=G^{2}+(3-2L_{1})G+L_{1}^{2}-3L_{1}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{7}{2}

with
G:=\gamma-\log\,a
.

In Tricomi’s case with
n=1
, we find from (
2.6
) that

x=\sqrt{\log\,s}\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{2L}+\frac{2B(u)-A^{2}(u)}{8L^{2}}+\frac{2C(u)-2A(u)B(u)+A^{3}(u)}{16L^{3}}+O(L^{-4},(sL)^{-1})\biggr).

Hence we obtain the expansion

I_{1,m}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\pi\log\,s}}{s}\biggl\{1+\frac{{\cal A}_{1}}{2L}-\frac{{\cal B}_{1}}{8L^{2}}+\frac{{\cal C}_{1}}{16L^{3}}+\cdots\biggr\}\qquad(m\to\infty),

(2.8)

where

{\cal A}_{1}=G-L_{1},\qquad{\cal B}_{1}=G^{2}+2(1-L_{1})G+L_{1}^{2}-2L_{1}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+2,

{\cal C}_{1}=G^{3}+(4-3L_{1})G^{2}+(3L_{1}^{2}-8L_{1}+8+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6})G+4L_{1}^{2}-8L_{1}-L_{1}^{3}

+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}(4-3L_{1})+2\zeta(3)+7

and we recall that
s=m+1
. It is seen that the leading term in this expansion agrees with the result stated in (
1.1
).

3. An extension of an integral arising in airfoil theory

In airfoil theory, the expression for the downward velocity
w(a)
at a point
x=a
over the wingspan induced by a single vortex filament is
[
3
, p. 201]

w(a)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x-a}\,\frac{d\Gamma}{dx}\,dx\qquad(-1<a<1),

where
\Gamma
is the circulation and the
x
-coordinate is normalised to the wingspan so that
-1\leq x\leq 1
. For an elliptical distribution
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(1-x^{2})^{1/2}
, where
\Gamma_{0}
is the circulation in the middle of the airfoil, we have

w(a)=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{4\pi}\biggl\{\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}+a\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\biggr\}

=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{4\pi}\biggl\{\pi+a\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\biggr\}.

(3.1)

It is clear that
w(-a)=w(a)
so that it is sufficient to consider
0<a<1
.

The integral appearing in (
3.1
) must be treated as a Cauchy principal value; that is, we evaluate the integral up to
\epsilon
either side of
x=a
and then let
\epsilon\to 0
. Thus we have, with
R(w):=(1-a^{2}-2aw-w^{2})^{1/2}
,

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\int_{\epsilon}^{1-a}\frac{dw}{wR(w)}+\int_{1+a}^{\epsilon}\frac{dw}{wR(-w)}

=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\biggl\{\biggl[\log\,\frac{w}{2(1\!-\!a^{2}\!-\!aw\!+\!\sqrt{1-a^{2}}R(w))}\biggr]^{1-a}_{\epsilon}+\biggl[\log\,\frac{w}{2(1\!-\!a^{2}\!+\!aw\!+\!\sqrt{1-a^{2}}R(-w))}\biggr]^{\epsilon}_{1+a}\biggr\}

=-\frac{2a\epsilon}{(1-a^{2})^{3/2}}+O(\epsilon^{2})

after some routine algebra. Thus the integral vanishes as
\epsilon\to 0
with the result that

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\pi

(3.2)

independent of
a

[
3
, p. 202]
. Hence
w(a)=\Gamma_{0}/4
, which
means that in the case of an elliptical distribution of the circulation the induced downward velocity is constant across the wing.

The situation when
d\Gamma/dx=x^{2n+1}\Gamma_{0}/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
,
n=0,1,2,\ldots\
, corresponds to a flattening of the basic elliptic profile (
n=0
). For example, when
n=1,2
we have the profiles

\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2}(2+x^{2})\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\ \ \ (n=1),\qquad\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{15}(8+4x^{2}+3x^{4})\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\ \ \ (n=2);

as
n
increases the profile becomes progressively flatter in the central portion of the wing. Then the extension of the integral (
3.1
) we consider is

J_{n}(a;\mu):=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{(1-x^{2})^{\mu}}\qquad(\mu<1,\ n=0,1,2,\ldots).

(3.3)

which reduces to (
3.1
) when
n=0
and
\mu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
. It is easily seen that
J_{n}(-a;\mu)=J_{n}(a;\mu)
, so that again it is sufficient to consider
0<a<1
.

3.1 The evaluation of the integral
J_{n}(a;\mu)

We write
J_{n}(a;\mu)
in the form

J_{n}(a;\mu)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2n+1}-a^{2n+1}}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{(1-x^{2})^{\mu}}+a^{2n+1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x-a}\,\frac{dx}{(1-x^{2})^{\mu}}\equiv I_{1}+a^{2n+1}I_{2}.

In the first integral we employ the expansion

\frac{x^{m+1}-a^{m+1}}{x-a}=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a^{m-r}x^{r}\qquad(m=0,1,2,\ldots)

(3.4)

to find

I_{1}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a^{2n-2r}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2r}}{(1-x^{2})^{\mu}}\,dx=\frac{a^{2n}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu)}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{r}a^{-2r}}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu)_{r}}

=\frac{a^{2n}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu;a^{-2})|_{n},

where the subscript
n
on the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
denotes that only the first
n+1
terms are to be taken.

The second integral is

I_{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2k}}{x-a}\,dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\biggl\{a^{2k}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{x-a}+\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2k}-a^{2k}}{x-a}\,dx\biggr\}

=\frac{1}{(1-a^{2})^{\mu}}\log\biggl(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\biggr)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2k}-a^{2k}}{x-a}\,dx.

Use of (
3.4
) in the above integral shows that

S:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{2k}-a^{2k}}{x-a}\,dx=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}a^{2k-2r}\int_{-1}^{1}x^{2r}dx

=2a\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k+1}a^{2k}}{(k+1)!}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\frac{a^{-2r}}{2r+1}=2a\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{-2r}}{2r+1}\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k+1}a^{2k}}{(k+1)!}

=2a\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{k+r+1}a^{2k}}{(k+r+1)!},

where we have put
k\to k+r
in the last sum. From the identity
(\mu)_{k+r+1}=(\mu)_{r+1}(\mu+r+1)_{k}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r+1}}{(2r+1)(r+1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu+r+1)_{k}a^{2k}}{(r+2)_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)r!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(1,\mu+r;r+1;a^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{1-a^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)r!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(1,1-\mu;r+1;-\frac{a^{2}}{1-a^{2}}\biggr)

upon use of the well-known Euler transformation
[
2
, (15.8.1)]
for the hypergeometric function.

Then we have the result

J_{n}(a;\mu)=\frac{a^{2n}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}-\mu;a^{-2})|_{n}+\frac{a^{2n+1}}{(1-a^{2})^{\mu}}\,\log\biggl(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\biggr)

+\frac{2a^{2n+2}}{1-a^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)r!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(1,1-\mu;r+1;-\frac{a^{2}}{1-a^{2}}\biggr)

(3.5)

valid for
\mu<1
and non-negative integer
n
. We remark that the convergence of the infinite sum in (
3.5
) is slow, being controlled by
r^{\mu-2}
as
r\to\infty
. A simple means of accelerating the convergence is given in the sub-section below.

3.2 Improved convergence of (
3.5
)

We can write the hypergeometric function appearing in the infinite sum in (
3.5
) in the form

{}_{2}F_{1}(1,1-\mu;r+1;-X)=1-\frac{(1-\mu)X}{r+1}+\frac{(1-\mu)_{2}X^{2}}{(r+1)_{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,3-\mu;r+3;-X),

where, for convenience, we have put
X:=a^{2}/(1-a^{2})
. Then

S=\frac{2a}{1-a^{2}}\biggl(\sigma_{0}-(1-\mu)X\sigma_{1}+(1-\mu)_{2}X^{2}\sigma_{2}\biggr)

+\frac{2a}{1-a^{2}}(1-\mu)_{2}X^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)(r+2)!}\,\biggl\{{}_{2}F_{1}(1,3-\mu;r+3;-X)-1\biggr\},

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)r!}=1-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\mu)},

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)(r+1)!}=\frac{2-3\mu}{3(1-\mu)}-\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{3\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\mu)},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)(r+2)!}=\frac{16-25\mu+15\mu^{2}}{30(1-\mu)(2-\mu)}-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1-\mu)}{15\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\mu)}.

The convergence of the final sum in (
3.6
) is now controlled by terms of O
(r^{\mu-5})
as
r\to\infty
, which is an improvement
on that in (
3.5
). This procedure can be continued to produce higher rates of convergence, but at the expense of the evaluation of higher-order sums
\sigma_{m}
,
m\geq 2
.

3.3 The special case
n=0
,
\mu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}

We demonstrate that the result in (
3.5
) reduces to the value given in (
3.2
) when
n=0
and
\mu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
.
We have from (
3.5
) with
X=a^{2}/(1-a^{2})

J_{0}(a;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})=\pi+\frac{a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\,\log\biggl(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\biggr)+2X\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}}{(2r-1)r!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;r+1;-X).

The sum can be written in the form

T:=X\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{r+1}}{(r+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})(r+1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}(-X)^{k}}{(r+2)_{k}}=X\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}(-X)^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{r+1}}{(r+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})(r+k+1)!}

=X\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}(-X)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;k+2;1).

Application of Gauss’ summation theorem series
[
2
, (15.4.20)]
shows that the
{}_{2}F_{1}(1)
series has the value

(k+1)\Gamma(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})/\Gamma(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$})
, whence

T=2X\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$})_{k}k!}\,(-X)^{k}=2X\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};-X)

=\frac{2X}{\sqrt{1+X}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};\frac{X}{1+X}\biggr)=\frac{a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\,\log\biggl(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\biggr)

upon use of Euler’s transformation followed by evaluation of the resulting hypergeometric function by
[
2
, (15.4.2)]
.
Hence, we recover the result

J_{0}(a;\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})=\pi

as given in (
3.2
).

Appendix: The inversion of (
2.5
)

We require the inversion of the expression

\mbox{erfc}\,x=\frac{2u}{s}\biggl(1-\frac{u}{2s}+\cdots\biggr)\qquad(u\to 0).

Since the limit
u\to 0
corresponds to
x\to+\infty
, we employ the asymptotic expansion
[
2
, (7.12.1)]

\mbox{erfc}\,x=\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x}\biggl\{1-\frac{1}{2x^{2}}+\frac{3}{4x^{4}}+O(x^{-6})\biggr\}\qquad(x\to+\infty)

to yield

xe^{x^{2}}=\frac{s(1-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x^{-2}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$}x^{-4}-\cdots)}{au(1-u/(2s)+\cdots)},\qquad a=2\sqrt{\pi}.

Taking logarithms, we obtain

x^{2}=\log\,s\biggl(1-\frac{\log\,au}{L}-\frac{\log\,x}{L}-\frac{1}{2x^{2}L}+\frac{5}{8x^{4}L}+\cdots+O((sL)^{-1})\biggr),

(A.1)

where we set
L=\log\,s
,
L_{1}=\log\sqrt{\log\,s}
.

Following the iterative procedure described in
[
1
, pp. 25–26]
we have as a first approximation
x^{2}=\log\,s
, whence

x^{2}=\log\,s\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{L}\biggr),\qquad A(u)=-\log\,au-L_{1}.

Then with

\frac{\log\,x}{L}=\frac{L_{1}}{L}+\frac{A(u)}{2L^{2}}+\ldots,

we find a second approximation given by

x^{2}=\log\,s\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{L}+\frac{B(u)}{2L^{2}}+\cdots\biggr),\qquad B(u)=-A(u)-1.

This last result produces

\frac{\log\,x}{L}=\frac{L_{1}}{L}+\frac{A(u)}{2L^{2}}+\frac{B(u)-A^{2}(u)}{4L^{3}}+\cdots,

so that we obtain the third approximation

x^{2}=\log\,s\biggl(1+\frac{A(u)}{L}+\frac{B(u)}{2L^{2}}+\frac{C(u)}{4L^{3}}+O(L^{-4},(sL)^{-1})\biggr),

(A.2)

where

C(u)=A^{2}(u)+3A(u)+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{7}{2}}$}.

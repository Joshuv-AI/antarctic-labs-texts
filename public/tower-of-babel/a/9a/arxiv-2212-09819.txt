Degree lowering for ergodic averages along arithmetic progressions Thanks: The authors were supported by the Hellenic Foundation for Research and Innovation, Project: 1684.
Nikos Frantzikinakis and Borys Kuca

Abstract
. We examine the limiting behavior of multiple ergodic averages associated with arithmetic progressions whose differences are elements of a fixed integer sequence. For each ℓ \ell , we give necessary and sufficient conditions under which averages of length ℓ \ell of the aforementioned form have the same limit as averages of ℓ \ell -term arithmetic progressions. As a corollary, we derive a sufficient condition for the presence of arithmetic progressions with length ℓ + 1 \ell+1 and restricted differences in dense subsets of integers. These results are a consequence of the following general theorem: in order to verify that a multiple ergodic average is controlled by the degree d d Gowers-Host-Kra seminorm, it suffices to show that it is controlled by some Gowers-Host-Kra seminorm, and that the degree d d control follows whenever we have degree d + 1 d+1 control. The proof relies on an elementary inverse theorem for the Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms involving dual functions, combined with novel estimates on averages of seminorms of dual functions. We use these estimates to obtain a higher order variant of the degree lowering argument previously used to cover averages that converge to the product of integrals.

Definitions of various good properties

2.1.
Convergence along APs

We start with notions related to mean convergence properties of averages of the form (
9
), which we call averages along
\ell
-term arithmetic progressions.

Definition

(Mean convergence along APs)
.

We say that a sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is

good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term arithmetic progressions (APs)
for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
if for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)

(7)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}f_{\ell}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{k_{1}n}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}n}f_{\ell}

holds where both limits are taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
(the second limit is known to exist by
[
17
]
).

Remark
.

Using an ergodic decomposition argument, we see that if a sequence is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for every ergodic system, then the same property holds for every system.

Note that if a sequence is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for a specific system, then Furstenberg’s multiple recurrence result
[
15
]
implies that it is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs for this system (see definition in Section
2.3
).

Definition

(Pointwise convergence along APs)
.

We say that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
pointwise convergence along
\ell
-term APs for
\ell
-step nilsystems
if for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\ell
-step nilmanifolds
X=G/\Gamma
,
b\in G
, and

F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\in C(X)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}a(n)}x)\cdots F_{\ell}(b^{k_{\ell}a(n)}x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}n}x)\cdots F_{\ell}(b^{k_{\ell}n}x)

for every
x\in X
.

Remark
.

In order to verify this property, we can assume that
b
is an ergodic nilrotation. This is so because the closure of the set

\{b^{n}\cdot e_{X}\colon n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is a subnilmanifold
Y
of

and

acts ergodically in

(see Section
8.1
).

2.3.
Recurrence and divisibility

We record here a notion related to recurrence properties along
\ell
-term APs
and a related notion regarding divisibility.

Definition

(Multiple recurrence along APs)
.

We say that a sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs
if for every system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, set
A\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
, and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\mu(A\cap T^{-k_{1}a(n)}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-k_{\ell}a(n)}A)>0

for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Using Furstenberg’s correspondence principle
[
15
]
, we deduce that if

a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs, then every set of integers with positive upper density contains patterns of the form
m,\;m+k_{1}a(n),\;\ldots,\;m+k_{\ell}a(n)
for every
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Examples of periodic systems show that if a sequence is good for single recurrence (i.e. recurrence along
1
-term APs), then its range should contain multiples of every positive integer. This motivates the following divisibility property that is part of the assumptions of our main recurrence result Theorem
3.4
.

Definition

(Good divisibility property)
.

We say that a sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
has
good divisibility properties
if for every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
the set
\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon r|a(n)\}
has positive density.

Remark
.

If we relax the requirement on the set
\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon r|a(n)\}
to have positive upper density, then we could still prove Theorem
3.4
below, but this would necessitate some (straightforward) technical modifications in our argument.

Examples of sequences with good divisibility properties are integer polynomials with zero constant terms (or any other intersective polynomial), the primes shifted by 1 (or -1), as well as integer parts of fractional powers and other Hardy field sequences that have polynomial growth and stay away from rational polynomials. We refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 3.1]
for references and further examples.

2.4.
Equidistribution on nilmanifolds

Lastly, we record some notions related to equidistribution properties on nilsystems, see Section
8.1
for related definitions and basic facts.

Definition

(Equidistribution on nilmanifolds)
.

We say that a sequence
(x_{n})
is equidistributed on a nilmanifold
X
if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}F(x_{n})=\int F\,dm_{X}

for every
F\in C(X)
.

Definition

(Good equidistribution properties)
.

We say that a sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is

(i)

good for
\ell
-step equidistribution
if for every
\ell
-step nilmanifold

X=G/\Gamma
and ergodic
b\in G
, the sequence

(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
.

(ii)

good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution
,
if for every connected
\ell
-step nilmanifold

X=G/\Gamma
and ergodic
b\in G
, the sequence

(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
.

Remarks
.

\bullet
In both cases, we can deduce that
(b^{a(n)}\cdot x)
is equidistributed in
X
for every
x\in X
. To see this, write
x=g\cdot e_{X}
for some
g\in G
. Then using Leibman’s equidistribution criterion (see Section
8.1
) we get that
g^{-1}bg
is an ergodic element if
b
is, and our assumption gives that the sequence
b^{a(n)}\cdot x=g(g^{-1}bg)^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X}
is equidistributed in

. See also Lemma
8.1
below for equivalent ways to verify
\ell
-step equidistribution.

\bullet
If
X
is a connected nilmanifold and

is an ergodic rotation on
X
, then it is also totally ergodic, meaning that
b^{r}
is ergodic for every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Conversely, if the nilmanifold
X
admits a totally ergodic rotation, then it has to be connected. A proof of these basic properties can be found for example in
[
18
, Chapter 11, Corollary 7]
.

The sequence
([n^{c}])
where
c
is a positive non-integer, is an example of a sequence that is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Non-constant polynomial sequences typically fail to be good for
1
-step equidistribution due to congruence obstructions, but they are good for
\ell
-step equidistribution for every
\ell\geq 2
. An example of a sequence that is good for
(\ell-1)
-step
equidistribution but not for
\ell
-step equidistribution is given in the fourth remark following Theorem
3.1
.

We also remark that using Weyl’s equidistribution criterion, it is easy to verify that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
1
-step equidistribution if and only if the sequence

(a(n)\alpha)
is equidistributed on
{\mathbb{T}}
for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and it is good for
1
-step irrational equidistribution
if and only if the sequence
(a(n)\alpha)
is equidistributed on
{\mathbb{T}}
for every irrational
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

3.
Main results

We now present the main results of the paper.

3.1.
Mean convergence along arithmetic progressions

Given a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, we are interested in studying the limiting behavior in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
N\to\infty
of the multiple ergodic averages with iterates taken along arithmetic progressions

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{\ell a(n)}f_{\ell},

or the somewhat more general averages of the form

(9)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}f_{\ell}

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and

f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

Our model is the case
\ell=1
, in which case we are interested in characterizing sequences
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
for which

(10)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{a(n)}f=\int f\,d\mu

holds in
L^{2}(\mu)
for all ergodic systems
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. By considering rotations on
{\mathbb{T}}
, it is straightforward to see that any such sequence has to be
good for equidistribution on the circle
, in the sense that
(a(n)t)
is equidistributed in
{\mathbb{T}}
for all
t\in(0,1)
. Conversely, this equidistribution property is sufficient for (
10
) to hold; this is a consequence of the spectral theorem for unitary operators, or more specifically the Herglotz theorem on positive definite sequences. In effect, we get the following well known result.

Theorem
.

The sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies identity (
10
) for all ergodic systems if and only if it is good for equidistribution on the circle.

Motivated by this result, a slight variant of the following conjecture was made in
[
11
, Conjecture 3]
(see also
[
9
, Problem 3]
for a related conjecture) and seeks to give a satisfactory answer to Question
2
. Throughout, we write APs in place of arithmetic progressions.

Conjecture 1

(F.-Johnson-Lesigne-Wierdl
[
11
]
)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

a
is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all systems.

(ii)

a
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution.

Remark
.

The implication
(i)\implies(ii)
follows quite easily from known results (see Proposition
8.2
below). The difficult implication is
(ii)\implies(i)
, widely open even for
\ell=2
.

The next result verifies Conjecture
1
for sequences that are good for seminorm control of the averages (
9
) for all ergodic systems.

Theorem 3.1

(Criteria for convergence along APs - general systems)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence that is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic systems. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

a
is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all systems.

(ii)

a
is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs
for all ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystems.

(iii)

a
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution.

Remarks
.

\bullet
The difficult implication is
(iii)\implies(i)
(or
(ii)\implies(i)
). If we ask that the convergence properties hold for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then either implication follows from the main structural result in
[
17
]
(see Theorem
9.1
below), and we have nothing important to add. So our contribution is to prove these equivalences for every fixed
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which is a really non-trivial matter.

\bullet

3.2.
Recurrence along arithmetic progressions

Motivated by Question
1
, we seek to give criteria that enable us to prove that a sequence

is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs. Our model is the following result from the 1970s that we rephrase using our terminology.

Theorem

(Kamae-Mendès France
[

]
)
.

If the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is
good for
1
-step irrational equidistribution and
has good divisibility properties, then it
is good for single recurrence (i.e., recurrence along
1
-term APs).

Inspired by this result, the following conjecture was made in
[

, Conjecture I]
(see also
[
9
, Problem 4]
) and seeks to give a satisfactory answer to Question

.

Conjecture 2

(F.-Lesigne-Wierdl
[
14
]
)
.

If the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is
good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution and
has good divisibility properties, then it is
good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs.

The next result verifies that Conjecture
2
holds for sequences that are good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs.

Theorem 3.4

(Criteria for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs)
.

If the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic systems, is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution, and
has good divisibility properties, then it
is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs.

Remark
.

It can be shown that for every
\ell\geq 2
, there exists a sequence that is good for
(\ell-1)
-step equidistribution, good for seminorm control for all systems, and has good divisibility properties, but fails to be good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs. An example is given by the sequence constructed by putting the elements of the set

\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\left\|n^{\ell}\alpha\right\|\in[1/4,1/2]\}

in increasing order where
\alpha
is irrational. The first two good properties follow from
[
13
, Proposition 4.2]
and the implication
(i)\implies(iii)
in Proposition
8.2
below, the third good property follows easily using Weyl’s equidistribution result, and finally

shows that this sequence is bad for recurrence along

-term APs.

3.3.

Another family of patterns that has attracted considerable interest is “square” patterns

(Criteria for convergence of square averages)
.

Let

be sequences such that

are good for seminorm control
2
2

2

Meaning that the averages (

for all ergodic systems.
Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

For all systems

and functions

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T^{b(n)}f_{2}\cdot T^{a(n)+b(n)}f_{3}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\,T^{r}f_{1}\cdot T^{s}f_{2}\cdot T^{r+s}f_{3}.

\bullet
We can generalize this to higher order cubic averages (by using

3.4.
A stabilization property of the Host-Kra factors

To each system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, we can assign the Host-Kra factors
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0},{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\ldots
, originally defined in
[
17
]
for ergodic systems, some of their properties are discussed in Section
4.2
.
The following result was proved by Host and Kra in
[
18
, Chapter 16, Theorem 12]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Stabilisation property for Host-Kra factors)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a system such that

{\mathcal{Z}}_{d+1}={\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Then
{\mathcal{Z}}_{s}={\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
for every
s\geq d
.

Host and Kra proved this result in
[
18
]
using their deep structural result stated in Theorem
9.1
below. We give an alternative proof that avoids such deep machinery and is based on Theorem
3.11
below (or rather Theorem
3.12
, its multivariable extension that can be proved in exactly the same way). Here is a sketch. For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, a function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{s}
-measurable if and only if it is orthogonal to all functions
g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\lvert\!|\!|g|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}=0
. So it suffices to show the following property:

“If for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d+1}
and

\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
have the same null space, then for every
s\geq d
the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
and

\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
have the same null space.”
(The null space of a seminorm on
L^{\infty}(\mu)
is the subset of
L^{\infty}(\mu)
on which the seminorm vanishes.)

To see this, we let
s\geq d
and
consider the cubic averages

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{s}}\sum_{1\leq n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}\leq N}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}f_{\epsilon}

of degree
s
that define the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
by identity (
22
). By the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (see for example
[
18
, Chapter 8, Theorem 13]
), these averages are good for degree
s
seminorm control. Since by assumption the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d+1}
and

\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
have the same null space,
we deduce that if the degree
s
cubic averages are good for degree
d+1
seminorm control, then they are good for degree
d
seminorm control. Theorem
3.11
then implies that the cubic averages of degree
s
are good for degree
d
seminorm control. Hence, the null space of
the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
is contained in the null space of the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
. The other inclusion is obvious since
s\geq d
, hence the two seminorms have the same null space.

3.5.
Mixing properties of nilsystems and applications

The next result was proved in
[
31
]
for ergodic nilsystems defined on nilmanifolds
X=G/\Gamma
with
G
connected and in
[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
for general
2
-step nilsystems. It seems likely that the method in
[
27
]
can be modified to
extend this result to general nilsystems (we thank Or Shalom and John Griesmer for bringing this article to our attention). In any case, we are going to use Theorem

to give an alternative proof of this result that covers the case of general nilsystems. We then use this result to prove in Theorem
3.8
a similar result for general systems.

Theorem 3.7

(Mixing properties of nilsystems)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic nilsystem and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
, or equivalently,
{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{Z}}_{1})=0
.

(ii)

For every strictly increasing sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{a(n)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

(iii)

For every
g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int g\cdot T^{n}f\,d\mu=0.

Remark
.

The difficult implication is
(i)\implies(ii)
(or
(i)\implies(iii)
).
We could instead work with sequences
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
that have bounded multiplicity, in
the sense that
\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon a(n)=k\}|<\infty
. The same holds for the other results in this subsection.

Proof.

The implication
(iii)\implies(i)
is straightforward. Moreover, the equivalence
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
is pretty simple, see for example the proof of
[
1

or
[
2

It remains to establish the implication

.
If the conclusion fails, then there exists an ergodic
s
-step nilsystem
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, a function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
, a
strictly increasing sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
, and

, such that

(13)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{a(n)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\geq\varepsilon.

Hence, in order to get a contradiction, it suffices to verify that every strictly increasing sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for degree
2

Since any sequence is good for seminorm control for nilsystems (see the comment before Theorem

),
by the remarks following Theorem

we can assume that the function

(for nilsystems) and an approximation argument, we can assume that the system is an ergodic

In this case, by

.
The implication

then gives that

Lastly, we record a result about single recurrence. If
(G,+)
is a compact abelian group with a metric

we denote the

of

by
\left\|g\right\|_{G}:=d(0,g)
.

Definition
.

A sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is

if for every

,

\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\left\|a(n)\alpha\right\|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\leq\varepsilon\}

Remark
.

By considering the rotation by

, where

,
we deduce that if the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

and
\varepsilon>0
, the set
\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\left\|a(n)\beta\right\|_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\leq\varepsilon\}
has positive upper density. Here, we think of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
as embedded in
{\mathbb{T}}
in the natural way, hence the distance function on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
is the pullback of the distance function on
{\mathbb{T}}
, and the distance on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
can be taken to be the sum of distances on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
and
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.10

(Recurrence for single term averages)
.

Suppose that the strictly increasing sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
is good for averaged Bohr recurrence and seminorm control
for the system

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. Then the sequence is good for single recurrence for this system, in particular, for every
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
, we have

(14)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(A\cap T^{-a(n)}A)>0.

Remark
.

If we replace upper with lower density in the definition of the property “good for averaged Bohr recurrence”, then we can replace the
\limsup
with
\liminf
in
(

)
.

Proof.

Using an ergodic decomposition argument, we can assume that the system is ergodic.
Theorem
9.1
then gives that the system is an inverse limit of ergodic nilsystems,
and using an approximation argument (as in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
), we can assume that it is an ergodic nilsystem.
We decompose

{\bf 1}_{A}=f_{1}+f_{2}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad f_{1}:={\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{Z}}_{1})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad f_{2}\,\bot\,{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}

where
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}
is the Kronecker factor of the system.
By Theorem
3.7
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{a(n)}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,

hence it suffices to show that

(15)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k}}\int f_{1}\cdot T^{a(n)}f_{1}\,d\mu>0

for some sequence
N_{k}\to\infty
that will be determined later.
Since we assumed our system to be an ergodic nilsystem, the factor system on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}
is isomorphic to an ergodic rotation on a compact abelian Lie group. To prove (
15
), we can therefore assume that
T
is an ergodic rotation on
G:={\mathbb{Z}}_{r}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for some
d,r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with the Haar measure
\mu=m_{G}
. Since the function

F(t):=\int f_{1}(x)\cdot f_{1}(x+t)\,dm_{G}(x),\quad t\in G,

is continuous, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

(16)

\sup_{\left\|t\right\|_{G}\leq\varepsilon_{0}}\Big|\int f_{1}(x)\cdot f_{1}(x+t)\,dm_{G}(x)-\int(f_{1}(x))^{2}\,dm_{G}(x)\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int(f_{1}(x))^{2}\,dm_{G}(x).

(Note that
\int(f_{1}(x))^{2}\,dm_{G}(x)\geq(m_{G}(A))^{2}>0
.)
Since
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
is good for averaged Bohr recurrence, we can choose the sequence

so that the limit in (

) exists and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big|\{n\in[N_{k}]\colon\ \left\|a(n)\alpha\right\|_{G}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\}\big|}{N_{k}}>0.

We deduce from this and (
16
) that (
15
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

3.6.
Key seminorm control result

The key new ingredient used in the proofs of the previous results is a new result that enables us to find the optimal degree of seminorm control in various cases. Our argument works without additional trouble in a more general setting than the one needed for averages along APs and cubic patterns, so we describe this more general case here.
Given a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, we consider the following averages

(17)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}

for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

Theorem 3.11

(Degree reduction property)
.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
are good for seminorm control for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. Suppose also that for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, degree
d+1
seminorm control implies degree
d
seminorm control of the averages (
17
) for this system. Then the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for degree
d
seminorm control for this system.

To put this differently, assume that for a fixed system, the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm control, i.e. the averages (
17
) are controlled by Host-Kra seminorms of some (possibly very high) degree. In order to prove degree
d
seminorm control for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we can take for granted that we have degree
d+1
seminorm control, or equivalently that all the functions are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable.
If degree
d
control follows for all
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable functions, we will say that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are
good for
d
-step reduction
, a property that will be rigorously defined in the next section.

In various settings, this additional information simplifies matters quite a bit. For instance, Theorem
3.11
easily implies the joint ergodicity result
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, since verifying degree
1
seminorm control assuming degree
2
seminorm control (in which case we can assume that the system is a rotation on a compact abelian Lie group) is an easy consequence of the given good equidistribution assumptions.

The proof of Theorem
3.11
is inspired by the degree lowering approach taken in
[
10
]
, which in turn was inspired by finitary works of
Peluse
[
28
,
29
]
and Peluse and Prendiville
[
30
]
. However, there are severe technical difficulties in adapting the argument from
[
10
]
to our setting. The main challenge has been to find a suitable replacement for
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
, the inverse theorem for degree 2 seminorm that relates
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}
to correlations of
f
with eigenfunctions of
T
. Our first instinct for higher degree seminorms was to use the Host-Kra structure theorem (see Theorem
9.1
below) to relate
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
to correlations of
f
with nilcharacters, which enjoy similar orthogonality properties to eigenfunctions. An argument of this form has recently been performed in the finitary work of Leng on finding bounds for subsets of finite fields lacking complexity 1 polynomial progressions
[
25
]
. However, various technical obstacles encountered on the way pushed us to abandon this approach.

Instead, we use the trivial inverse theorem for degree
d
seminorms, one that expresses
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}
as a correlation of
f
with its level
d
dual function. We then carry out the degree lowering argument similarly to
[
10
]
but with level
d
dual functions in place of eigenfunctions. The utility of eigenfunctions for the argument in
[
10
]
comes from their orthogonality properties. Dual functions lack orthogonality, but they possess strong cancellative properties that we exploit to our advantage in Proposition
7.1
, the key novel technical input in this paper. Roughly speaking, Proposition
7.1
states that if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+s}=0
, then degree
d
seminorms of certain dual functions of level
d+1
associated with degree
s
multiplicative derivatives of
f
vanish on average.

In proving Theorem
3.11
, we also make crucial use of
[
12
, Proposition A.2]
, a general functional analytic result that allows us to boost a qualitative seminorm control of a multilinear functional into a soft quantitative one.

3.7.
Generalizations to other averaging schemes

Theorem

can be generalized in a straightforward way to weighted averages, sequences in several variables, and averages along Følner sequences as follows.

Theorem 3.12
.

3.8.
Further directions

Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
give optimal seminorm control (of degree
\ell
) for averages along
\ell
-term APs for sequences that satisfy some good
\ell
-step equidistribution properties. In the absence of such good equidistribution properties, the example given in the fourth remark after Theorem
3.1
shows that there exist sequences for which the averages along
\ell
-term APs are good for degree
\ell+1
seminorm control but not degree
\ell
seminorm control. On the other hand, we have not been able to find an example where seminorm control of degree higher than
\ell+1
is needed for these averages. This motivates the next conjecture.

Conjecture 3
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be strictly increasing. If the sequence is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, then it is good for degree
\ell+1
seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for this system.

Theorem
3.8
establishes Conjecture
3
for
\ell=1
.
In order to establish the conjecture in full generality, using an ergodic decomposition argument we can assume that the system is ergodic. Using the Host-Kra structure theorem (see Theorem
9.1
below), it suffices to verify the conjecture when the system is an ergodic nilsystem. In this case, a closely related problem is the following
higher order mixing property for nilsystems that would imply Conjecture
3
for weak instead of strong convergence. It is also not hard to show that Conjecture
3
implies Conjecture
4
.

Conjecture 4
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic nilsystem and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be non-zero and distinct. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_{0}\cdot T^{k_{1}n}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}n}f_{\ell}\,d\mu=0

as long as
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
are such that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{\ell+1}=0
for some
j\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
.

Remark
.

A related problem is mentioned in
[
24
]
(see the remark after Proposition 1.1 there).

Theorem
3.7
establishes Conjecture
4
for
\ell=1
. Using Theorem
3.11
and arguing as in the proof of the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
in Theorem
3.7
, we get that to prove Conjecture
4
, it suffices to establish the conjecture under the additional assumption that the system is an
(\ell+1)
-step nilsystem.
If the
(\ell+1)
-step nilsystem is given by a unipotent affine transformation on a torus, then using direct computation, we can verify the conjecture for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. On the other hand, the problem is open for general nilsystems even when
\ell=2
and

.

4.
Basic notation and background on seminorms

In this section, we present various notions from ergodic theory together with some basic results.

4.1.
Basic notation

We start with explaining basic notation used throughout the paper.

The letters
{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{N}},{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
stand for the set of complex numbers, real numbers, integers, positive integers, and non-negative integers. With
{\mathbb{T}}
, we denote the one dimensional torus, and we often identify it with
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
or with
[0,1)
. We let
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. With
{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
, we denote the collection of polynomials with integer coefficients.

For an element
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we let
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
.

If
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}^{s}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a bounded sequence for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A
is a non-empty finite subset of
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, we let

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in A}\,a(n):=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{n\in A}\,a(n).

We often write
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}
for a vector of 0s and 1s of length
s
. For
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}
and
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
, we set

•

\epsilon\cdot{\underline{h}}:=\epsilon_{1}h_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{s}h_{s}
;

•

\mathopen{}\left|{\underline{h}}\mathclose{}\right|:=|h_{1}|+\cdots+|h_{s}|
;

•

{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}:=(h_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,h_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}})
, where
h_{j}^{0}:=h_{j}
and
h_{j}^{1}:=h_{j}^{\prime}
for
j=1,\ldots,s
.

We write
{\mathcal{C}}z:=\overline{z}
for the complex conjugate of
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

4.2.
Ergodic seminorms

Given a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, we will use the family of ergodic seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
, also known as
Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms
, which were originally introduced in
[
17
]
for ergodic systems. A detailed exposition of their basic properties can be found in
[
18
, Chapter 8]
.
These seminorms are inductively defined for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
as follows (for convenience, we also define
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{0}
, which is not a seminorm):

(19)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{0}:=\int f\,d\mu,

and for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we let

(20)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}^{2^{s+1}}:=\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h\in[H]}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{h}f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}},

where

\Delta_{h}f:=f\cdot T^{h}\overline{f},\quad h\in{\mathbb{Z}},

is the
multiplicative derivative of
f
. If we deal with several transformations at a time, we may also denote the seminorm as
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T}
for clarity.
The limit can be shown to exist by successive applications of the mean ergodic theorem, and for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}
(see
[
17
]
or
[
18
, Chapter 8]
).
It follows immediately from the definition and the mean ergodic theorem that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=\left\|{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{I}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},

where
{\mathcal{I}}:=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\colon Tf=f\}
, and

(21)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{H_{1}\to\infty}\cdots\lim_{H_{s}\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h_{1}\in[H_{1}]}\cdots{\mathbb{E}}_{h_{s}\in[H_{s}]}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}}f\,d\mu,

where for
{\underline{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
, we let

\Delta_{{\underline{h}}}f:=\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{s}}f=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{h}}}f

be the
multiplicative derivative of
f
of degree
s
with respect to
T
.

It can be shown that the previous limits exist and we can take any
s^{\prime}\leq s
of the iterative limits to be simultaneous limits (i.e. average over
[H]^{s^{\prime}}
and let
H\to\infty
)
without changing the value of the limit in (
21
). This was originally proved in
[
17
]
using the main structural result of
[
17
]
; a more “elementary” proof can be deduced from
[
5
, Lemma 1.12]
once the convergence of the uniform Cesàro averages is known.
For
s^{\prime}:=s
, this gives the identity

(22)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}}\in[H]^{s}}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}}f\,d\mu.

Moreover, for
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
, we have

(23)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}}\in[H]^{s-s^{\prime}}}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{{\underline{h}}}f|\!|\!\rvert_{s^{\prime}}^{2^{s^{\prime}}}.

For every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
d\geq 1
, we also have the inequality

(24)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T^{d}}

proved in
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
.

The seminorms are intimately connected with a certain family of factors of the system. Specifically, for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{s}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
, known as the
Host-Kra factor
of
degree

s
, with the property that

(25)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0\text{ if and only if }f\text{ is orthogonal to }{\mathcal{Z}}_{s-1}.

Equivalently,
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
defines a norm on the space
L^{2}({\mathcal{Z}}_{s-1})

[
18
, Chapter 9, Theorem 15]
.

Lastly, we record the following simple inequality relating the seminorms of
f
(with respect to
T
) to the seminorms of
f\otimes\overline{f}
(with regards to
T\times T
and
\mu\times\mu
) for all
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
:

(26)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f\otimes\overline{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}^{2}.

4.3.
Dual functions and sequences

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\{0,1\}^{s}_{*}:=\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
. For a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, a function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, and
{\underline{m}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, we define

\Delta_{{\underline{m}}}^{*}f:=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}_{*}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{m}}}f

and

(27)

\mathcal{D}_{s}(f):=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{s}}\,\Delta_{{\underline{m}}}^{*}f=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{s}}\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}_{*}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}T^{{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{m}}}f,

where the limit exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
by
[
17
]
(or
[
18
, Chapter 8, Theorem 28]
).
We call
{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(f)
the
dual function of
f
of level

s
.
The name is motivated by the identity

(28)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\int f\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(f)\,d\mu,

a consequence of which is that the span of dual functions of level
s
is dense in
L^{1}({\mathcal{Z}}_{s-1})
.

Rewriting
{\underline{m}}=({\underline{m}}^{\prime},m_{s})
and observing that
\Delta_{{\underline{m}}}^{*}f=\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}^{*}f\cdot T^{m_{s}}\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}\overline{f}
, we can express the dual function
{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(f)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{s}(f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}\in[M]^{s-1}}\lim_{M_{s}\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{s}\in[M_{s}]}\,\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime},m_{s}}^{*}f

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}\in[M]^{s-1}}\mathopen{}\left(\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}^{*}f\cdot\lim_{M_{s}\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{s}\in[M_{s}]}\,T^{m_{s}}\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}\overline{f}\mathclose{}\right)

where our use of iterated limits is justified as in the case of formula (
21
).
In particular, for ergodic
T
, we deduce the identity

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{s}(f)=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}\in[M]^{s-1}}\,c_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}\cdot\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}^{*}f,

where for
{\underline{m}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s-1}
, we let

\displaystyle c_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}:=\int\Delta_{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}\overline{f}\,d\mu.

\delta>0
, such that if the functions
f_{j}\in L^{\infty}({\mathcal{Y}}_{j},\mu)
are
1
-bounded and
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}\leq\delta
, then

\lim_{K\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\varepsilon.

Remark
.

The important point in the previous result is that
\delta
does not depend on the
1
-bounded functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
.

We also record the following standard trick that we shall use a few times, and which allows us to get rid of the absolute value and weights from an average at the cost of passing to the product space.

Lemma 5.6

(Product trick)
.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a system, and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be functions. Then

(i)

For all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\mathopen{}\left|\int T^{k_{1}}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}}f_{\ell}\,d\mu\mathclose{}\right|^{2}=\int(T\times T)^{k_{1}}(f_{1}\otimes\overline{f_{1}})\cdots(T\times T)^{k_{\ell}}(f_{\ell}\otimes\overline{f_{\ell}})\,d(\mu\times\mu).

(ii)

For all sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, 1-bounded weights
w\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,w_{n}\cdot T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\\
\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,(T\times T)^{a_{1}(n)}(f_{1}\otimes\overline{f_{1}})\cdots(T\times T)^{a_{\ell}(n)}(f_{\ell}\otimes\overline{f_{\ell}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu\times\mu)}.

Proof.

The identity in (i) is obvious. To prove the estimate in (ii), we expand the square inside the integral on the left hand side, use the triangle inequality to eliminate the weights, and then use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the identity in (i) to arrive at the right hand side.
∎

6.
The degree lowering argument - reduction to a key seminorm estimate

6.1.
Initial maneuvers

In order to prove Theorem
3.11
, it is convenient to rephrase it in the following inductive form. We remind the reader that the notion of sequences good for
d
-step reduction is defined in Section
2.2
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences that are good for seminorm control and
d
-step reduction for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
.
Then for every

, the following property holds:

(P_{m})
If
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and the functions
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, then

(40)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu)
if
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
for some

.

In the base case
m=0
, property
(P_{0})
is equivalent to being good for
d
-step reduction (see definition in Section
2.2
). For
m\geq 1
, we will prove
(P_{m})
assuming
(P_{m-1})
. The case
(P_{\ell})
is equivalent to Theorem
3.11
.

We deduce from Proposition
5.2
the following result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences that are good for seminorm control
for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. Then there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the following holds: If
m\in[\ell]
and (
40
) fails for some
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
then

(41)

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}>0

where
\tilde{f}_{m}
is given by (
34
) (as a weak limit)
and satisfies (
35
).

The goal then is to establish the following statement.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences that are good for seminorm control and
d
-step reduction for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. Suppose that the property
(P_{m-1})
of Proposition
6.1
holds for some
m\in[\ell]
and
\tilde{f}_{m}
is given by (
34
) and satisfies (
35
) where all functions involved are
1
-bounded and
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable. Lastly, suppose that
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+d+1}>0
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+d}>0
.

Before proceeding to the proof of Proposition
6.3
, we show how it can be used to prove
Proposition
6.1
(and hence Theorem
3.11
).

Proof of Proposition
6.1
assuming Proposition
6.3
.

Property
(P_{0})
is a trivial consequence of the assumption that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for
d
-step reduction for the system. To prove
(P_{m})
for
m\geq 1
, we split into the cases
m=1
and
m\geq 2
; we need to do this because we will invoke
(P_{1})
while proving
(P_{m})
for
m\geq 2
.

The case
m=1
.

To prove property
(P_{1})
, we recall from (
32
) that

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{-a_{1}(n)}\overline{g_{k}}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}T^{a_{j}(n)-a_{1}(n)}f_{j},

where

\displaystyle g_{k}={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Propositions
6.2
and
6.3
then imply that if (
40
) fails, then
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
, from which it follows using (
28
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int\overline{g}_{k}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{1})\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j}\,d\mu>0.

The key point now is that since the functions
f_{2},\ldots,f_{\ell}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{1})
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, so is the function

F_{k}:={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{1})\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{j}.

Expanding the definition of
g_{k}
, we thus see that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\int T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{\ell}\cdot\overline{F_{k}}\,d\mu>0.

Since for
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the function

\displaystyle F_{n,k}:=T^{a_{2}(n)}f_{2}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\cdot\overline{F_{k}}

is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, we get from the definition of conditional expectation that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\int T^{a_{1}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}(f_{1}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d})\cdot T^{a_{2}(n)}f_{2}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\cdot\overline{F_{k}}\,d\mu>0.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}(f_{1}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d})\cdot T^{a_{2}(n)}f_{2}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

All the functions inside the
L^{2}(\mu)
-norm are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, therefore the fact that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for
d
-step reduction (which is equivalent to the
(P_{0})
property) yields
\lvert\!|\!|{\mathbb{E}}(f_{1}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d})|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
, and hence also
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=\lvert\!|\!|{\mathbb{E}}(f_{1}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d})|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
by (
25
). It also yields
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
for
j\in\{2,\ldots,\ell\}
, completing the proof of
(P_{1})
.

The case
m\geq 2
.

We fix
m\geq 2
and assume that property
(P_{m-1})
holds. Our goal is to show that property
(P_{m})
also holds.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that (
40
) does not hold, or equivalently, that (
31
) holds for some
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
where
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable. It suffices to show that then
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
for every
j\in[\ell]
.

Since the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm control, by combining Proposition
6.2
and (
31
), we deduce that if
\tilde{f}_{m}
is as in (
34
), then

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}>0
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
s\leq d
, then this immediately implies
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
by the monotonicity property of Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms. Otherwise we iterate the conclusion of Proposition
6.3
exactly
s-d
times, getting
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
. Together with (
28
), this implies that

\int\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{m})\,d\mu>0.

We use the definition (
34
) of
\tilde{f}_{m}
, compose with
T^{a_{m}(n)}
, and then apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to deduce that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{m}(n)}{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{m})\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

Since the functions
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}(\tilde{f}_{m}),f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, property
(P_{m-1})
implies that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0

for all
j\neq m
.

It remains to show that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0
. To do this, we first note that the argument above implies the identity

(42)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\prod_{j\in[\ell]}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j}-{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{m}(n)}f_{m}\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}(f_{j}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d-1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Hence, the statement
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0

follows by invoking the case
(P_{1})
after an appropriate permutation of the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that the argument for
(P_{1})
would quickly break if instead we considered averages of commuting transformations, i.e. each
a_{j}
would be an iterate of a different transformation
T_{j}
. This is the only point in the proof of Theorem
3.11
in which we make essential use of the fact that we are only dealing with one transformation.

6.2.
Proof of Proposition
6.3
: reduction to a key seminorm estimate

Having derived Proposition
6.1
from Proposition
6.3
, we move on to prove Proposition
6.3
. We will show that Proposition
6.3
can be reduced to a seminorm estimate whose proof will occupy the entirety of the next section.

Let
(N_{k})
be the sequence that defines the function
\tilde{f}_{m}
in Proposition
5.2
, and let
(N^{\prime}_{k})
the subsequence of
(N_{k})
given in Lemma
5.1
along which the sequence

(43)

A_{K}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})={\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N^{\prime}_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}

converges in

as

for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. By Proposition
5.2
, the function
\tilde{f}_{m}
is also equal to the strong limit

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{m}=\lim_{K\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}^{\prime}]}\,f_{n,k}

for

(44)

f_{n,k}:=T^{-a_{m}(n)}\overline{g}_{k}\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)-a_{m}(n)}\overline{f}_{j}.

Using the assumption
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+d+1}>0
, we deduce from Proposition
5.3
that

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\limsup_{K\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N^{\prime}_{k}]}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{n,k}\cdot f_{m,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\;d\mu>0

for the
1
-bounded functions

(45)

f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j},\;&j\neq m;\\
T^{-|{\underline{h}}|}\prod\limits_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}{\mathcal{D}}_{d+1}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}\tilde{f}_{m}),\;&j=m.\end{cases}

For each
j=m+1,\ldots,\ell
, the function
f_{j}
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable, hence so is
f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
. Likewise,
f_{m,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is

-measurable since each function

is.

Using the previous facts, the form of
f_{n,k}
from (
44

, we deduce after composing with
T^{a_{m}(n)}
and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\lim_{K\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N^{\prime}_{k}]}\,\prod_{j\in[\ell]}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

In particular, an elementary pigeonholing argument guarantees that there exist a subset
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{2s}
of positive lower density and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N^{\prime}_{k}]}\,\prod_{j\in[\ell]}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>\varepsilon_{0}

({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in\Lambda
.

Using the fact that the functions
f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
-measurable for

, we invoke the induction hypothesis
(P_{m-1})
to deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}^{\prime}]}\,\prod_{j\in[\ell]}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
. We boost this statement using Proposition

(applied for

if

and

otherwise) to deduce that for every

, there exists

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}^{\prime}]}\,\prod_{j\in[\ell]}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>\varepsilon

implies
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>\delta
. Note that the mean convergence property of the averages (

. Picking

as above, we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\lvert\!|\!|f_{m,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}>0.

(Recall the functions

were defined in (

That this implies
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+d}>0
is the content of Proposition
7.1
in the next section.

7.
Key seminorm estimate - concluding the proof of Theorem
3.11

We now prove the following key seminorm estimate needed to complete the degree lowering argument and hence the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a system and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+s}=0
, for some
d,s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lvert\!|\!|f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0

where

f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}:=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\mathcal{D}_{d+1}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f)

for
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
.

To get a better sense of this result, it is useful to consider some special cases.

The case
s=0
and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
states that if

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
, then
\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
; this may seem odd at first since the conclusion involves more differencing than the assumption.
For
d=1
we want to show that if
\int f\,d\mu=0
and the system is ergodic, then
\int\mathcal{D}_{2}f\,d\mu=0
, which can be verified by a direct computation. Indeed, the identity (
29
) implies that

\displaystyle\int{\mathcal{D}}_{2}f\,d\mu=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m\in[M]}\,c_{m}\int T^{m}f\,d\mu,

for some uniformly bounded constants
c_{m}
, and so the claim readily follows from the assumption. The statement is trickier to prove for
d\geq 2
, and a proof of this special case is given in Section
7.3
.
Note also that if we want to strengthen the conclusion to
\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+1}=0
, then we are forced to assume that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+1}=0
(which implies
\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f=0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
).

On the other hand, the case
d=0
and
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
follows from Lemma
5.4
, stating that cubic averages of order
s
twisted by low complexity weights are controlled by the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
, so again here the choice of constants is optimal.

The proof of Proposition
7.1
is given in Subsection
7.4
. For a better understanding, we include two additional examples that are notationally lighter.

7.1.
The case
d=2
,
s=1

We will show that if
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfies
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{3}=0
, then

(46)

\displaystyle\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lvert\!|\!|{f_{h}}\cdot\overline{f_{h^{\prime}}}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0

where
f_{h}:=\mathcal{D}_{3}(\Delta_{h}f)
for
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Using an ergodic decomposition argument, it suffices to prove this when the system is ergodic, which we assume. The identity (
29
) allows us to express the functions
f_{h}
as

f_{h}=\mathcal{D}_{3}(\Delta_{h}f)=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{1},m_{2}\in[M]}\,c_{m_{1},m_{2},h}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\Delta_{h}f

for the uniformly bounded constants
c_{m_{1},m_{2},h}:=\int\Delta_{m_{1},m_{2},h}\overline{f}\,d\mu
.

The identity (
23
) implies that the claimed identity (
46
) is equivalent to

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{n}(f_{h}\cdot\overline{f_{h^{\prime}}})|\!|\!\rvert^{2}_{1}=0.

Plugging in the formulas for
f_{h},f_{h^{\prime}}
and expanding, we see that it suffices to show that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\lvert\!|\!|\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}\in[M]\end{subarray}}\,C_{{\underline{m}},h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\\
\Delta^{*}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\Delta_{h}f\cdot\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{3},m_{4}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f}\cdot T^{n}(\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h}f}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f)|\!|\!\rvert^{2}_{1}=0,

where the averages
{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}\in[M]}
are taken over all
m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime},i=1,\ldots,4,
and the constants

\displaystyle C_{{\underline{m}},h,h^{\prime}}:=c_{m_{1},m_{2},h}\,\overline{c_{m_{3},m_{4},h^{\prime}}\,c_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime},h}}\,c_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime},h^{\prime}}

are uniformly bounded.
Since the system is ergodic, the ergodic theorem gives
\lvert\!|\!|g|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=\mathopen{}\left|\int g\,d\mu\mathclose{}\right|
, and so using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it suffices to show that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}\in[M]}\\
\Big|\int\Delta^{*}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\Delta_{h}f\cdot\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{3},m_{4}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f}\cdot T^{n}\big(\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h}f}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f\big)\,d\mu\Big|^{2}=0.

Note that the limits over
M
and
N
can be shown to exist, but we can also work with the limsups without trouble.
Using Lemma
5.6
(i) as well as the identities (
26
) and
\Delta_{h}(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})=\Delta_{h}f_{1}\cdot\Delta_{h}f_{2}
, we observe that upon replacing
T
by
T\times T
and
f
by
f\otimes\overline{f}
, it suffices to show the following: if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
, then

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}\in[M]}\\
\int\Delta^{*}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\Delta_{h}f\cdot\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{3},m_{4}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f}\cdot T^{n}\big(\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h}f}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f\big)\,d\mu=0.

Bringing the averages of
m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}
inside, we rewrite the equality above as

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\int\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{1},m_{2}\in[M]}\,\Delta^{*}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\Delta_{h}f\cdot\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{3},m_{4}\in[M]}\,\\
\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{3},m_{4}}\,\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f}\cdot T^{n}\Big(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}\in[M]}\,\overline{\Delta^{*}_{m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h}f}\cdot\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime}\in[M]}\,\Delta^{*}_{m_{3}^{\prime},m_{4}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f\Big)\,d\mu=0.

Using the definition of dual functions of level 2 we conclude that it suffices to show that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\int\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h}f)\cdot\overline{\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f)}\cdot\\
T^{n}\big(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h}f)}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f)\big)\,d\mu=0,

or that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h}f)\cdot\overline{\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f)}|\!|\!\rvert^{2}_{1}=0

by the definition of the Host-Kra seminorm of degree 1.
We have now effectively reduced our problem to a simpler one, as we replaced the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{3}
in our assumption with
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{2}
and in the conclusion, we replaced the seminorm

\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{2}
with
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{1}
and the dual function
\mathcal{D}_{3}(\Delta_{h}f)

with
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\Delta_{h}f)
. Repeating this argument one more time gives us a statement that can be verified directly using the ergodic theorem.

7.2.
Identities and estimates involving multiplicative derivatives

Before we derive another special case of Proposition
7.1
, we state several tedious but important identities that will show up in the proof. First we note the identity

(47)

\displaystyle\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\sum_{m\in I}a_{\epsilon,m}=\sum_{m_{\epsilon}\in I,\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}a_{\epsilon,m_{\epsilon}}

that holds for arbitrary finite sets
I
and allows us to commute products and averages.
For instance, when
d=1
,
I=\{0,1\}
, the identity takes the form
(a_{0,0}+a_{0,1})(a_{1,0}+a_{1,1})=\sum_{m_{0},m_{1}\in\{0,1\}}a_{0,m_{0}}\cdot a_{1,m_{1}}
.
Since for fixed

{\underline{n}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d-1}
we have
\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}f=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}f
,
the previous identity implies that

(48)

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\mathopen{}\left(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}f_{\underline{m}}\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}\mathopen{}\left(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}f_{\underline{m}}\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}f_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}.

Plugging the functions
f_{{\underline{m}}}:=c_{\underline{m}}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}f
with
c_{\underline{m}}:=\int\Delta_{{\underline{m}}}\overline{f}\,d\mu
into the identity above and recalling that for ergodic systems
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
we have

(49)

\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d+1}}\,\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}f=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}\,c_{\underline{m}}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}f,

we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}(\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\mathopen{}\left(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}\,c_{\underline{m}}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}f\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(c_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\,C_{({\underline{m}}_{\epsilon})}\cdot\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}f)

for
C_{({\underline{m}}_{\epsilon})}=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}c_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}
.
Integrating over
X
, and applying the triangle and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities gives the following estimate.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\underline{n}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d-1}
,
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic system, and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be 1-bounded. Then

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}(\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f)\,d\mu\mathclose{}\right|^{2}\leq\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\mathopen{}\left|\int\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}f)\,d\mu\mathclose{}\right|^{2}.

Lemma
7.2
will play an important role in deriving Proposition
7.1
when
s=0
in Section
7.3
. For the general case handled in Section
7.4
, we need the following extension, whose proof follows by
essentially the same argument.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}},s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
{\underline{n}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d-1}
,
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic system, and
f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be 1-bounded for every
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}
. Then

\Big|\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\Big(\prod_{\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon^{\prime}|}\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\Big)\,d\mu\Big|^{2}\leq\\
\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\in[M]^{d},(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}\Big|\int\prod_{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|+|\epsilon^{\prime}|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}}f_{\epsilon^{\prime}})\,d\mu\Big|^{2}.

Note that the limits on the right hand side of the estimates in Lemma
7.2
and
7.3
exist, as they can be reinterpreted as limits of cubic averages.

7.3.
The case of general
d
and
s=0

We move to another special case, namely when there is no additional averaging over
{\mathbb{N}}^{2s}
and differencing over
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
.
We will induct on
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
to prove the following statement:

(Q_{0,d})
: For every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have
\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
.

Example
.

Before we proceed to the general case, we illustrate how a more computational proof works for
d=2
and the affine system
T(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1}+\alpha,x_{2}+2x_{1}+\alpha)
on
X={\mathbb{T}}^{2}
for irrational
\alpha
. We give this example just for reassurance, as this computational approach is not appropriate for extension. We take our function to be
f(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\,c_{l}\,e(lx_{2})
, where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, noting that
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
.
The identity

e(l\cdot T^{n}(x_{1},x_{2}))=e(l(x_{2}+2nx_{1}+n^{2}\alpha))

and a simple computation show that
\mathcal{D}_{3}f

is a finite linear combination of functions of the form

f_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m_{1},m_{2}\in[M]}\,c_{m_{1},m_{2}}\cdot e((l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3})x_{2}+(m_{1}(l_{1}+l_{3})+m_{2}(l_{2}+l_{3}))x_{1})

for some constants
c_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and non-zero
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Note that if
l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3}\neq 0
, then
f_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}
depends non-trivially on the variable
x_{2}
, hence
\lvert\!|\!|f_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
. On the other hand, if
l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3}=0
, we cannot have

l_{1}+l_{3}=l_{2}+l_{3}=0
(since one of the
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}
is non-zero), hence the average defining the function
f_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}(x_{1},x_{2})
is zero a.e. (in fact for every
x_{2}
as long as
x_{1}
is irrational). It follows that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
and hence
\lvert\!|\!|{\mathcal{D}}_{3}f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
.

We move now to the proof of property
(Q_{0,d})
.

For
d=0
, the result follows from the identity

\lvert\!|\!|{\mathcal{D}}_{1}f|\!|\!\rvert_{0}=\int{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{I}}(T))\,d\mu=\int f\,d\mu=\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{0}.

So we assume that
d\geq 1
. In proving property
(Q_{0,d})
for a function
f
and a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, we will invoke property
(Q_{0,d-1})
for
f\otimes\overline{f}
and the system
(X\times X,{\mathcal{X}}\otimes{\mathcal{X}},\mu\times\mu,T\times T)
. We recall from formula (
26
) that
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
implies
\lvert\!|\!|f\otimes\overline{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{d-1}=0
. This gives us a chain of implications

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\lvert\!|\!|f\otimes\overline{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{d-1}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d}(f\otimes\overline{f})|\!|\!\rvert_{d-1}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d+1}(f)|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0,

where the first implication follows from (
26
) as explained above, the second implication is the induction hypothesis, and the third one we are about to prove.

Using an ergodic decomposition argument, it suffices to prove
\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d+1}(f)|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0
for ergodic systems. Using the iterative formula for Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms (
20
), it is enough to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}(\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f)|\!|\!\rvert^{2}_{1}=0,

or, since the system is ergodic, that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\Big|\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}(\mathcal{D}_{d+1}f)\,d\mu\Big|^{2}=0.

By Lemma
7.2
, this will in return follow from

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\Big|\int\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}f)\,d\mu\Big|^{2}=0.

Using Lemma
5.6
(i) and (
26
), we observe that the statement will follow if we show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}\in[M]^{d},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}\int\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}(T\times T)^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}}(f\otimes\overline{f}))\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f\otimes\overline{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{d-1}=0
. Using the identity (
48
) for
f_{\underline{m}}:=\Delta_{\underline{m}}^{*}(f\otimes\overline{f})
in order to bring the averages over
{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon}
inside
\Delta_{\underline{n}}
, we deduce that the identity above is equivalent to

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\mathopen{}\left(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}\,\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}(f\otimes\overline{f})\mathclose{}\right)\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0,

which in turn holds by (
27
) if and only if

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{d}(f\otimes\overline{f})\big)\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0.

Hence, using the iterative formula for Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms (
20
) and the formula (
19
), we deduce that it suffices to show that

\lvert\!|\!|\mathcal{D}_{d}(f\otimes\overline{f})|\!|\!\rvert_{d-1}=0

where the seminorms are taken with respect to the product system.
This follows from the induction hypothesis.

7.4.
The case of general
d
and general
s

Finally, we proceed to tackle the general case of Proposition
7.1
. Since the case
s=0
was covered in Section
7.3
we can assume that
s\geq 1
. So let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed. In analogy with the previous case, we will show the following property by induction on
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

(Q_{s,d})
: For every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lvert\!|\!|f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+s}=0
, where

(50)

f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}:=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\mathcal{D}_{d+1}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f).

Like in Section
7.3
, for
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and fixed
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we will derive property
(Q_{s,d})
for a function
f
and a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
from property
(Q_{s,d-1})
for the function
f\otimes\overline{f}
and the product system
(X\times X,{\mathcal{X}}\otimes{\mathcal{X}},\mu\times\mu,T\times T)
.

By an ergodic decomposition argument, it suffices to prove property
(Q_{s,d})
for ergodic systems. Under this assumption,
identity (
28
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{d+1}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d+1}}\,\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f)

(51)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}\,c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\cdot\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f)

for the uniformly bounded constants
c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}:=\int\Delta_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}\overline{f}\,d\mu
.

Basis of induction.
For
d=0
, we will show that property
(Q_{s,0})
holds for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It suffices to show that if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
, then

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lvert\!|\!|f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{0}=0,

where

\displaystyle f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}:=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\mathcal{D}_{1}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f)=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f\,d\mu

since the system is ergodic. Hence, the claim will follow if we can show that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f\,d\mu=0.

In fact, we will show something stronger: if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
, then for every
{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, we have

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}}\in[H]^{s}}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f\,d\mu=0.

Letting

c_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}:={\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|}\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f\,d\mu

for
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, we get that it suffices to show that

(52)

\displaystyle\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}}\in[H]^{s}}\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}_{*}}c_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}\cdot\int\Delta_{{\underline{h}}}f\,d\mu=0.

But for
\epsilon\neq 0
, the sequence
{\underline{h}}\mapsto c_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}
depends on at most
s-1
of the variables
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}
. Hence, (
52
) follows from Lemma
5.4
and the assumption that
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
.

Induction step.
Suppose that
property
(Q_{s,d-1})
holds for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and some fixed value of
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We will derive from this that
property
(Q_{s,d})
holds. Equivalently, using (
23
) (with
d
in place of
s
and
s^{\prime}=1
), we want to show that
if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d+s}=0
, then

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert^{2}_{1}=0,

where
f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
are given by (
50
).
Since the system is ergodic, it suffices to show that

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\Big|\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\,d\mu\Big|^{2}=0.

Plugging in the formula (
51
) for
f_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
, using Lemma
7.3
with
f_{\epsilon}:=\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon}}f
,
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, and averaging over
({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2s}
, allows us to reduce the claim to the identity

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\in[M]^{d},(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}\\
\Big|\int\prod_{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|+|\epsilon^{\prime}|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}}f)\,d\mu\Big|^{2}=0.

Using Lemma
5.6
and (
26
), it suffices to show that if
\lvert\!|\!|f\otimes\overline{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{d+s-1}=0
, then

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\in[M]^{d},(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}\\
\int\prod_{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon|+|\epsilon^{\prime}|}T^{\epsilon\cdot{\underline{n}}}(\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(f\otimes\overline{f}))\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0.

Using identity (
47
)
in order to bring the averages over
({\underline{m}}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})}
inside the product
\prod_{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\times\{0,1\}^{s}}
, we deduce that the identity above is equivalent to

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\\
\int\prod_{\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon^{\prime}|}\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\mathopen{}\left(\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{m}}\in[M]^{d}}\,\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{m}}}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(f\otimes\overline{f})\mathclose{}\right)\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0.

The definition of the dual function and the commutativity of
\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}
and
\Delta_{\underline{n}}
allow us to write the preceding identity more compactly as

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{n}}\in[N]^{d-1}}\int\Delta_{{\underline{n}}}\Big(\prod_{\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon^{\prime}|}\mathcal{D}_{d}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(f\otimes\overline{f}))\Big)\,d(\mu\times\mu)=0.

Identity (
22
)
then implies that the claim will follow from

\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in[H]^{s}}\,\lvert\!|\!|\prod_{\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\epsilon^{\prime}|}\mathcal{D}_{d}(\Delta_{{\underline{h}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(f\otimes\overline{f}))|\!|\!\rvert^{2^{d-1}}_{d-1}=0,

where the seminorm is taken with respect to the product system. This follows from our induction hypothesis (for the product system), completing the proof.

8.1.
Definitions and basic facts about nilsystems

We start with basic definitions and facts about nilsystems. Proofs of most of these facts can be found in
[
18
,
22
]
and we give precise references below.

Nilmanifolds and nilsystems.

An
s
-step nilmanifold
is a compact homogeneous space
X=G/\Gamma
where
G
is an
s
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
is a discrete cocompact subgroup. For
b\in G
, the transformation
T\colon X\to X
defined by
Tx=bx
is called an
s
-step nilrotation
on
X
and the system
(X,m_{X},T)
, where
m_{X}
is the projection of the Haar measure of
G
on
X
, is called an
s
-step nilsystem.
With
G_{0}
we denote the
connected component of the identity element in
G
, which can be easily shown to be a clopen normal subgroup of
G
.

A standing assumption.
A nilmanifold has several representations and depending on our setting we may use a representation that has particular features. For instance, whenever we work with an ergodic nilsystem defined by a rotation
b\in G
, we may and will assume that
G
is spanned by
G_{0}
and
b
.

Unique ergodicity.
If
X=G/\Gamma
is a nilmanifold and
b\in G
,
x\in X
, then the closure of the sequence
(b^{n}\cdot x)
is (isomorphic to)
a nilmanifold
Y
and the action of
b
in
Y
is uniquely ergodic
[
18
, Chapter 11, Theorem 17]
or
[
22
, Section 2]
.

Ergodic nilsystems on connected nilmanifolds.
The nilmanifold
X=G/\Gamma
is connected if and only if
G=G_{0}\Gamma

[
18
, Chapter 10, Lemma 11]
. Furthermore, a nilmanifold
X
is connected if and only if it admits a totally ergodic nilsystem, and in this case any ergodic nilsystem on
X
is totally ergodic
[
18
, Chapter 11, Corollary 7]
.

Ergodic nilsystems on disconnected nilmanifolds.

Let
X
be a nilmanifold and
(X,m_{X},T)
be an ergodic nilsystem.
If
X_{0}
is the connected component of
e_{X}
, then
X_{0}
is a nilmanifold, and there exists
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
the sets
X_{0},TX_{0},\ldots,T^{d-1}X_{0}
form a partition of
X
,
and
T^{d}
leaves each of these sets invariant. Then each nilsystem
(T^{i}X_{0},m_{T^{i}X_{0}},T^{d})
,
i=0,\ldots,d-1
, is totally ergodic.
For a proof of these basic facts see
[
18
, Chapter 11, Corollary 8]
.

The abelianization of a nilmanifold.
If
X
is a nilmanifold, then

Z:=X/[G,G]=G/([G,G]\cdot\Gamma)
is the factor of
X
called the
abelianization of
X
, with the factor map
\pi_{X,Z}:X\to Z
being the canonical projection. The space
Z
is a compact abelian Lie group and so it can be identified with
H\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}
for a finite abelian group
H
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
If
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
is an ergodic nilsystem, then necessarily
Z={\mathbb{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and the system
(Z,m_{Z},T)
is the Kronecker factor of the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
.

Leibman’s equidistribution criterion for linear sequences.
The nilsystem
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
is ergodic if and only if the system
(Z,m_{Z},T)
is ergodic (see
[
18
, Chapter 11, Theorem 6]

or
[
22
, Theorem 2.17]
). As a consequence, for
b\in G
, the sequence
(b^{n}x)
is
equidistributed on
X
if and only if the sequence
(b^{n}\cdot\pi_{X,Z}(x))
is equidistributed/dense in
Z
.

Polynomial sequences.
Let
G
be a nilpotent Lie group and
g(n)=b_{1}^{p_{1}(n)}\cdots b_{k}^{p_{k}(n)}
, where
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in G
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
. Let also
X=G/\Gamma
be a nilmanifold.
We call any sequence
(g(n))
of the previous form a
polynomial sequence in

G
and

(g(n)\cdot e_{X})
a
polynomial sequence in

X
.

Leibman’s equidistribution criterion for polynomial sequences.
Let
X=G/\Gamma
be a nilmanifold with
G
connected. Then the polynomial sequence
(g(n)\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
if and only if the sequence
(g(n)\cdot\pi_{X,Z}(x))
is equidistributed/dense in
Z
(see
[
18
, Chapter 14, Theorem 20]
or
[
22
, Theorem C]
).
We caution the reader that although for linear sequences this result continues to hold for disconnected
G
, it fails for general polynomial sequences when
G
is not connected (in which case one has to test equidistribution on the larger nilmanifold
X/[G_{0},G_{0}]
).

Factorization of linear sequences in
G_{0}
.
Suppose that
G=G_{0}\Gamma
where
G_{0}
is the connected component of
G
. Then for every
b\in G
there exist polynomial sequences
g_{0}(n)
and
\gamma(n)
of
G_{0}
and
\Gamma
respectively such that
b^{n}=g_{0}(n)\cdot\gamma(n)
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This is a rather standard fact and we give a quick proof of this for completeness.

By
[
18
, Chapter 10, Proposition 25]
, we can assume that
G
is a closed normal subgroup of a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group
\tilde{G}
(we do this so that later on,
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
becomes applicable). Our assumption gives that
b=b_{0}\gamma
for some
b_{0}\in G_{0}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Using this factorisation, the fact that
G_{0}
is a normal subgroup of
G
, as well as the inclusion
[G,G]\subset G_{0}
(which follows from
[
18
, Chapter 10, Lemma 5]
), we easily deduce that
b^{n}=(b_{0}\gamma)^{n}=b_{n}\gamma^{n}
for some
b_{n}\in G_{0}
. Hence,

b_{n}=b^{n}\gamma^{-n}
is a sequence in
G
, and therefore also in
\tilde{G}
. By
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
, the sequence
b_{n}
is a polynomial sequence in
G_{0}
, completing the proof.

8.2.
Nil-equidistribution results

We record some convenient equivalent ways to verify
\ell
-step equidistribution.

Lemma 8.1
.

If
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence, then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

a
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution.

(ii)

For every
\ell
-step nilmanifold
X=G/\Gamma
,
b\in G
, and
F\in C(X)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X}).

(iii)

For every
\ell
-step nilmanifold
X=G/\Gamma
,
b\in G
,
x\in X
, and
F\in C(X)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{a(n)}\cdot x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot x).

Proof.

We prove that
(i)\implies(ii)
. It is known (see Section
8.1
) that if
Y
is the closure
of the set
\{b^{n}\cdot e_{X}\}
, then
Y
is a subnilmanifold of
X
,
b
acts ergodically on
Y
, and the sequence
(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
Y
. Since
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})=\int F\,dm_{Y}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X}),

where the first identity follows from part
(i)
and the second identity because

(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
Y
.

We prove that
(ii)\implies(iii)
. Let
x=g\cdot e_{X}
for some
g\in G
.
Then

b^{a(n)}\cdot x=g(g^{-1}bg)^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{a(n)}\cdot x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(g(g^{-1}bg)^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(g(g^{-1}bg)^{n}\cdot e_{X})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot x),

where we used part
(ii)
for the continuous function
x\mapsto F(gx)
and the element
g^{-1}bg
in place of
b
in order to justify the second identity.

We prove that
(iii)\implies(i)
. Let
X=G/\Gamma
be an
\ell
-step nilmanifold,
b
be an ergodic element of
G
, and
F\in C(X)
. Then (see Section
8.1
) we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})=\int F\,dm_{X},

and part
(iii)
for
e_{X}
in place of
x
gives that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})=\int F\,dm_{X}.

Hence, the sequence
(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed on
X
, which implies
(i)
.
∎

Next, we record some convenient equivalent ways to verify
mean convergence properties for nilsystems.

Proposition 8.2
.

If
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

a
is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystems.

(ii)

a
is good for pointwise convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all
\ell
-step nilsystems.

(iii)

a
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution.

Remark
.

In the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
, we only have to assume in
(i)
weak convergence and we can deduce that (
53
) folds for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in X
in place of
x,\ldots,x
.

Proof.

We prove that
(iii)\implies(ii)
. Suppose that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution. Let
X=G/\Gamma
be an
\ell
-step nilmanifold,
b\in G
, and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. As explained in Section
8.1
, we can assume that
b
is an ergodic nilrotation of
X
.
It suffices to show that if
F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\in C(X)
, then for every
x\in X
we have

(53)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}a(n)}x)\cdots F(b^{k_{\ell}a(n)}x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}n}x)\cdots F(b^{k_{\ell}n}x).

We let
\tilde{X}:=G^{\ell}/\Gamma^{\ell}
, which is an
\ell
-step nilmanifold, and
\tilde{b}:=(b^{k_{1}},\ldots,b^{k_{\ell}})\in G^{\ell}
.
Then property
(iii)
combined with Lemma
8.1
(we use the implication
(i)\implies(iii)
there), gives that for every
\tilde{F}\in C(\tilde{X})
and
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}
, we have

(54)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\tilde{F}(\tilde{b}^{a(n)}\tilde{x})=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(\tilde{b}^{n}\tilde{x}).

If we apply this for
\tilde{F}:=F_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{\ell}
and
\tilde{x}:=(x,\ldots,x)\in\tilde{X}
,
x\in X
, we get that (
53
) holds for every
x\in X
.

The implication
(ii)\implies(i)
follows from the bounded convergence theorem.

We prove that
(i)\implies(iii)
.
Let
X=G/\Gamma
be an
\ell
-step nilmanifold,
b\in G
be ergodic, and
F\in C(X)
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we let

\psi(n):=F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X}).

It suffices to show that

(55)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\psi(a(n))=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\psi(n).

It follows from
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
that there exist
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the following holds: for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist an
\ell
-step nilsystem
(Y,m_{Y},S)
(which we may assume to be ergodic if we please) and functions
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in C(Y)
,
such that the sequence

c_{\varepsilon}(n):=\int g_{0}\cdot S^{k_{1}n}g_{1}\cdots S^{k_{\ell}n}g_{\ell}\,dm_{Y},\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

satisfies

(56)

\left\|\psi-c_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

Then property
(i)
implies that both limits below exist and we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,c_{\varepsilon}(a(n))=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,c_{\varepsilon}(n).

Combining this with the fact that (
56
) holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, we get that (
55
) holds.
∎

We will also need a variant of the implication
(iii)\implies(i)
of the previous statement that works under the assumption of
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution.

Proposition 8.3
.

If the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution, then
it is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all totally ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystems.

Remark
.

The converse implication is probably also be true but it does not seem easy to prove. The problem is that we cannot claim that the nilsystem
(Y,m_{Y},S)
constructed in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
is totally ergodic when
b
is totally ergodic.

Proof.

The argument is similar with the one used to establish the implications
(iii)\implies(ii)\implies(i)
in Proposition
8.2
but we need to make some non-trivial adjustments and use an additional result, so we give the details.

Suppose that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution. Let
(X,m_{X},T)
be an ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystem, where
X=G/\Gamma
is a connected
\ell
-step nilmanifold, and
Tx=bx
for some
b\in G
.
As remarked in Section
2.4
, the nilsystem
(X,m_{X},T)
is totally ergodic.
It suffices to show that if
F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\in C(X)
, then for almost every
x\in X
and all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

(57)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}a(n)}x)\cdots F(b^{k_{\ell}a(n)}x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F_{1}(b^{k_{1}n}x)\cdots F(b^{k_{\ell}n}x).

We let
\tilde{X}:=G^{\ell}/\Gamma^{\ell}
, which is an
\ell
-step nilmanifold, and
\tilde{b}:=(b^{k_{1}},\ldots,b^{k_{\ell}})\in G^{\ell}
. We also let

\tilde{X}_{x}
be the closure of the set
\{\tilde{b}^{n}\cdot(x,\ldots,x)\colon n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. It is known that
\tilde{X}_{x}
is an
\ell
-step sub-nilmanifold of
X^{\ell}
for every
x\in X
(see Section
8.1
). Moreover, for almost every
x\in X
, the action of
\tilde{b}
on
\tilde{X}_{x}
is totally ergodic (see
[
18
, Chapter 15, Lemma 9]
or
[
26
, Revised Theorem 7.1]
), or equivalently,
\tilde{X}_{x}
is connected and
\tilde{b}
acts ergodically on
\tilde{X}_{x}
(see Section
8.1
; importantly, this is not true for every
x\in X
).
Since the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution,
we deduce that for almost every
x\in X
, the following holds:
for every
\tilde{F}\in C(\tilde{X}_{x})
and
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}_{x}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\tilde{F}(\tilde{b}^{a(n)}\tilde{x})=\int\tilde{F}\,dm_{\tilde{X}_{x}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(\tilde{b}^{n}\tilde{x}),

where the first identity follows because the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution (see also the first remark after the relevant definition) and the second because
\tilde{b}
is an ergodic rotation of
\tilde{X}_{x}
and hence uniquely ergodic (see Section
8.1
).
If we apply this for
\tilde{F}:=F_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{\ell}
and
\tilde{x}:=(x,\ldots,x)\in\tilde{X}_{x}
, we get that (
57
) holds for almost every
x\in X
.
∎

9.
Proof of Theorems
3.1
-
3.5

We conclude the paper by deriving Theorems
3.1
-
3.5
from Theorem
3.11
.
While the proof of Theorem
3.11
made no use of the structure of the Host-Kra factors
{\mathcal{Z}}_{s}
other than the elementary equivalence (
25
), the proofs of Theorems
3.1
-
3.5
will utilise in an essential way the main structural result from
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
, Chapter 16, Theorem 2]
), which we now state.

Theorem 9.1

(Host-Kra structure theorem)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic system. Then for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{s}
is an inverse limit of
s
-step ergodic nilsystems.

9.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We first prove Theorem
3.1
that we restate for convenience.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence that is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic systems. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

a
is
good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all systems.

(ii)

a
is good for mean convergence along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystems.

(iii)

a
is good for
\ell
-step equidistribution.

Proof.

Since the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
is obvious and the equivalence
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
was established in Proposition
8.2
, it suffices to show that
(ii)\implies(i)
.

We first prove that the implication
(ii)\implies(i)
holds for
\ell=1
.
If we apply property
(ii)
for circle rotations, we get that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,e(a(n)t)=0

for all
t\in(0,1)
. Using the spectral theorem for unitary operators (or more specifically the Herglotz theorem on positive definite sequences), this easily implies that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
1
-convergence for all systems. (Note that in this case, we did not have to assume good seminorm control, but we will for
\ell\geq 2
.)

So it remains to prove that the implication
(ii)\implies(i)
holds for
\ell\geq 2
. Using an ergodic decomposition argument, we can assume that the system is ergodic. So let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We can assume that the integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
are non-zero and distinct. Our first goal is to use Theorem
3.11
in order to establish that the averages (
9
) are good for degree
\ell
seminorm control.
Suppose that the averages (
9
) are good for degree
\ell+1
seminorm control for this system. We claim that they are then good for degree
\ell
seminorm control. To see this, note first that our degree
\ell+1
seminorm control assumption implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}f_{\ell}-{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}\tilde{f}_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right)=0

where the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
and
\tilde{f}_{j}:={\mathbb{E}}(f_{j}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell})
for
j\in[\ell]
.
So in order to prove degree
\ell
seminorm control, it suffices to show that if
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{\ell}=0
for some
j\in[\ell]
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}\tilde{f}_{\ell}=0

where the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
By Theorem
9.1
, the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell}
is an inverse limit of
\ell
-step nilsystems, so using an approximation argument,
we can assume that the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
is an ergodic
\ell
-step nilsystem. In this case, property
(ii)

gives that the last limit is equal to

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{k_{1}n}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}n}\tilde{f}_{\ell}.

But it is known
[
23
, Theorem 8]
that if
\ell\geq 2
, then these averages are good for degree
\ell
seminorm control, hence the claim follows.

Thus, we have established that degree
\ell+1
seminorm control for the averages (
9
) implies degree
\ell
seminorm control and the identity (
7
). Theorem
3.11
then implies that the averages (
9
) are good for degree
\ell
seminorm control, and as we just mentioned, we obtain the identity (
7
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 9.5

(Criteria for convergence of square averages)
.

Let
a,b\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences such that
a,b,a+b
are good for seminorm control for all ergodic systems.
Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

For all systems
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and functions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

(58)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,T^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T^{b(n)}f_{2}\cdot T^{a(n)+b(n)}f_{3}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\,T^{r}f_{1}\cdot T^{s}f_{2}\cdot T^{r+s}f_{3}.

(ii)

Identity (
58
) holds for all ergodic
2
-step nilsystems.

Furthermore, property
(i)
holds for all totally ergodic systems if and only if property
(ii)
holds for all totally ergodic
2
-step nilsystems.

Proof.

The implication
(i)\implies(ii)
is obvious.

We establish the implication
(ii)\implies(i)
. Using an ergodic decomposition argument we can assume that the system is ergodic.
So lets fix an ergodic system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and suppose that the averages on the left hand side of (
58
) are good for degree
3
seminorm control for this system. We claim that they are then good for degree
2
seminorm control. To see this, note first that our assumption implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T^{b(n)}f_{2}\cdot T^{a(n)+b(n)}f_{3}-{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdot T^{b(n)}\tilde{f}_{2}\cdot T^{a(n)+b(n)}\tilde{f}_{3}\Big)=0

where the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
and
\tilde{f}_{j}:={\mathbb{E}}(f_{j}|{\mathcal{Z}}_{2})
for
j=1,2,3
.
So it suffices to show that if
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=0
for some
j=1,2,3
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdot T^{b(n)}\tilde{f}_{2}\cdot T^{a(n)+b(n)}\tilde{f}_{3}=0

where the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
By Theorem
9.1
the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{2}
is an inverse limit of ergodic nilsystems, so using an approximation argument
we can assume that the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
is an ergodic
2
-step nilsystem. In this case, property
(ii)

gives that the last limit is equal to

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{r,s\in[N]}\,T^{r}f_{1}\cdot T^{s}f_{2}\cdot T^{r+s}f_{3}.

These averages are good for degree
2
seminorm control, which follows by a simple application of the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (see for example
[
18
, Chapter 8, Theorem 13]
),
hence the claim follows.

Thus, we have established that degree
3
seminorm control for the averages on the left hand side of (
58
) implies degree
2
seminorm control and identity (
58
) holds. Recall also that by assumption the averages on the left hand side of (
58
) are good for seminorm control for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. Hence, Theorem
3.11
applies and gives that the averages on the left hand side of (
58
) are good for degree
2
seminorm control for this system and identity (
58
) holds.

The equivalence in the case where we restrict to totally ergodic systems in
(i)

and
(ii)
is proved in a similar fashion. We omit the details.
∎

9.5.
Proof of Theorem
3.4

For the proof of Theorem
3.4
we need a few preparatory results.

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence such that for every
\ell
-step nilmanifold
Y=H/\Delta
with
H
connected and simply connected and all equidistributed polynomial sequences
(g(n)\cdot e_{Y})
in
Y
, the sequence
(g(a(n))\cdot e_{Y})
is equidistributed in
Y
.
Then the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution.

Remark
.

The converse is also true and based on the fact that polynomial sequences can be represented as linear sequences on a larger nilmanifold.
We will not use this, though, so we do not prove it.

Proof.

Let
X=G/\Gamma
be a connected
\ell
-step nilmanifold.
Let
G_{0}
be the connected component of
G
. Then, as explained in Section
8.1
, we have
G=G_{0}\Gamma
and
X
can be identified with the nilmanifold
X_{0}:=G_{0}/\Gamma_{0}
where
\Gamma_{0}:=G_{0}\cap\Gamma

(see
[
18
, Chapter 11, Section 4.2]
). Let
\pi\colon X\mapsto X_{0}
denote
an isomorphism.

Let
b\in G
be arbitrary. Since
G=G_{0}\Gamma
, as explained in Section
8.1
the sequence
b^{n}
can be factorized as
b^{n}=g_{0}(n)\gamma(n)
where
g_{0}(n)
and
\gamma(n)
are polynomial sequences in
G_{0}
and
\Gamma
respectively. It follows
that
for every
F\in C(X)
, there exists
F_{0}\in C(X_{0})
(in fact
F_{0}:=F\circ\pi
) such that

(59)

\int F\,dm_{X}=\int F_{0}\,dm_{X_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})=F_{0}(g_{0}(n)\cdot e_{X_{0}}),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

and conversely, for every
F_{0}\in C(X_{0})
there exists
F\in C(X)
(in fact
F:=F_{0}\circ\pi^{-1}
) such that (
59
) holds.
Suppose now that
b\in G
is ergodic in
X
in which case by unique ergodicity we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F(b^{n}\cdot e_{X})=\int F\,dm_{X}

for every
F\in C(X)
.
Then (
59
) gives that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,F_{0}(g_{0}(n)\cdot e_{X_{0}})=\int F_{0}\,dm_{X_{0}}

for every
F_{0}\in C(X_{0})
. Hence,
the sequence
(g_{0}(n)\cdot e_{X_{0}})
is equidistributed in
X_{0}
. Since
X_{0}=G_{0}/\Gamma_{0}
where
G_{0}
is connected and simply connected, our assumption applies (for
Y:=X_{0}
,
H:=G_{0}
,
\Delta:=\Gamma_{0}
) and gives that the sequence
(g_{0}(a(n))\cdot e_{X_{0}})
is equidistributed in
X_{0}
. We deduce from this and (
59
) that the sequence
(b^{a(n)}\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
. This proves that the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for
\ell
-equidistribution and completes the proof.
∎

The only use of this result for our purposes is to prove the next lemma. The advantage is that in the case of a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G

the map
g\mapsto g^{k}
is surjective for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 9.7
.

Suppose that the sequence
(da(n))
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the sequence
(a(n))
is also good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution

Proof.

Let
X=G/\Gamma
be an
\ell
-step nilmanifold where
G
is connected and simply connected and
g(n)
be a polynomial sequence in
G
such that
(g(n)\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
.
By Lemma
9.6
, it suffices to show that the sequence
(g(a(n))\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
.
Note that since
G
is connected, it is divisible (see
[
18
, Chapter 10, Corollary 9]
), meaning that every element has a
k
-th root in
G
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, there exists a sequence
\tilde{g}
in
G
such that
\tilde{g}(dn)=g(n)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy to see that
\tilde{g}
is a polynomial sequence: since
g
can be written down as a product of sequences of the form
a^{n^{i}}
, the sequence
\tilde{g}
will be a product of sequences of the form
\tilde{a}^{n^{i}}
for
\tilde{a}:=a^{1/d^{i}}
a
d^{i}
-th root of
a
.

We claim that the sequence

(\tilde{g}(n)\cdot e_{X})
is also equidistributed in
X
. Indeed, by Leibman’s equidistribution criterion (see Section
8.1
) it suffices to verify this property when
G
is a finite dimensional torus. By Weyl’s equidistribution criterion, this amounts to showing that if a
p\in{\mathbb{R}}[n]
is a polynomial and the sequence
(p(dn))
is equidistributed in
{\mathbb{T}}
, then so is the sequence
(p(n))
. This is clearly the case since the first polynomial has a non-constant irrational coefficient if and only if the second one does. This proves the claim.

Since
the polynomial sequence

(\tilde{g}(n)\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
and by assumption the sequence
(da(n))
is good for irrational equidistribution, Lemma
9.6
gives that the sequence
(\tilde{g}(da(n))\cdot e_{X})=(g(a(n))\cdot e_{X})
is equidistributed in
X
. This completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma was proved in
[
13
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Lemma 9.8
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that the sequence
(a(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution and the set
I_{d}:=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon d|a(n)\}
has positive density. Then the sequence
(a(n))_{n\in I_{d}}
is also good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution.

Combining the previous two lemmas, we get the following.

Lemma 9.9
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that the sequence
(a(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution and the set
I_{d}:=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon d|a(n)\}
has positive density. Then the sequence
a_{d}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, defined by
(a(n)/d)_{n\in I_{d}}
, is also good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution.

We also need a similar fact about seminorm control.

Lemma 9.10
.

Let
d,\ell,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be non-zero and distinct, and
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence. Suppose that the sequences
k_{1}a,\ldots,k_{\ell}a
are good for degree
s
seminorm control for all ergodic systems, and the set
I_{d}:=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon d|a(n)\}
has positive density. Let
a_{d}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence defined by
(a(n)/d)_{n\in I_{d}}
. Then the sequences
k_{1}a_{d},\ldots,k_{\ell}a_{d}
are good for degree
s+1
seminorm control for all ergodic systems.

Proof.

The argument is similar to the one used in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 5.2]
.

An ergodic decomposition argument shows that the sequences
k_{1}a,\ldots,k_{\ell}a
are good
for degree
s
seminorm control for all systems (not necessarily ergodic).

We consider the system
(X_{d},{\mathcal{X}}_{d},\mu_{d},T_{d})
where

X_{d}:=X\times\{0,\ldots,d-1\},\quad{\mathcal{X}}_{d}:={\mathcal{X}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}(\{0,\ldots,d-1\}),\quad\mu_{d}:=\mu\times\nu_{d}

with
\nu_{d}:=\frac{\delta_{0}+\cdots+\delta_{d-1}}{d}
, and for
x\in X
, we let

T_{d}(x,i):=\begin{cases}(x,i+1),\,&i=0,\ldots,d-2,\\
(Tx,0),\;&i=d-1.\end{cases}

The system
(X_{d},{\mathcal{X}}_{d},\mu_{d},T_{d})
is often referred to as the
d
’th root of
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
.
Its key property is that

T_{d}^{d}(x,i)=(Tx,i)
for all
x\in X
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
, hence for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

(60)

(T_{d}^{d}(f\otimes 1))(x,i)=(Tf)(x).

Applying our good seminorm assumption for the product system

(X_{d}\times X_{d},{\mathcal{X}}_{d}\otimes{\mathcal{X}}_{d},\mu_{d}\times\mu_{d},T_{d}\times T_{d}),

we get that if
g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{d})
and
\lvert\!|\!|g_{j}\otimes\overline{g_{j}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T_{d}\times T_{d}}=0
for some
j\in[\ell]
, then

(61)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,(T_{d}\times T_{d})^{k_{1}a(n)}(g_{1}\otimes\overline{g_{1}})\cdots(T_{d}\times T_{d})^{k_{\ell}a(n)}(g_{\ell}\otimes\overline{g_{\ell}})=0.

Using the bound (
26
), Lemma
5.6
(ii) for
w_{n}:={\bf 1}_{I_{d}}(n)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and the fact that
I_{d}
has positive density, we deduce that

(62)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{d,N}}\,T_{d}^{k_{1}a(n)}g_{1}\cdots T_{d}^{k_{\ell}a(n)}g_{\ell}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu_{d})
whenever
\lvert\!|\!|g_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T_{d}}=0
for some
j\in[\ell]
, where for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
I_{d,N}:=I_{d}\cap[N]
.

We claim that for this
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the sequence
(a(n)/d)_{n\in I_{d}}

is good for degree
s+1
seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. To see this, let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T}=0
for some
j\in[\ell]
, say for
j=j_{0}
. It suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{d,N}}\,T^{k_{1}a(n)/d}f_{1}\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)/d}f_{\ell}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Let
g_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{d})
be defined by

g_{j}:=f_{j}\otimes 1
for each
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, and recall that
\mu_{d}=\mu\times\nu_{d}
. By (
60
), it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{d,N}}\,T_{d}^{dk_{1}a(n)/d}g_{1}\cdots T_{d}^{dk_{\ell}a(n)/d}g_{\ell}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu_{d})
, or equivalently that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{d,N}}\,T_{d}^{k_{1}a(n)}g_{1}\cdots T_{d}^{k_{\ell}a(n)}g_{\ell}=0.

By (
62
), this holds as long as we establish that
\lvert\!|\!|g_{j_{0}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T_{d}}=0
.
To see this, note that
since
g_{j_{0}}=f_{j_{0}}\otimes 1
and
\mu_{d}=\mu\times\nu_{d}
, our assumption
\lvert\!|\!|f_{j_{0}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T}=0
and (
60
) give that

\lvert\!|\!|g_{j_{0}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T_{d}^{d}}=\lvert\!|\!|f_{j_{0}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T}=0
. The implication

\lvert\!|\!|g_{j_{0}}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1,T_{d}}=0
then follows from (
24
). This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.4
, we restate it for convenience.

Theorem 9.11
.

If the sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs, is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution, and
has good divisibility properties, then it
is good for recurrence along
\ell
-term APs.

Proof.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a system and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that
f\geq 0
and
\int f\,d\mu>0
. Let also
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Our goal is to show that for some subset
I\subset{\mathbb{N}}
with positive density, we have

(63)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{N}}\int f\cdot T^{k_{1}a(n)}f\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}f\,d\mu>0,

where as before, for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
I_{N}:=I\cap[N]
.
We can assume that
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
are non-zero and distinct and the system is ergodic.

Since the sequence
(a(n))
is good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs, and the set
I
is assumed to have positive density, Lemma
5.6
(ii) for
w_{n}:={\bf 1}_{I}(n)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and the bound (
26
) imply that it is also good for seminorm control of the averages in (
63
). Hence, using Theorem
9.1
, we can assume that the system is an inverse limit of ergodic nilsystems,
and using an approximation argument (as in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
), we can assume that it is an ergodic nilsystem. As explained in Section
8.1
, there exists
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
X
can be partitioned as
X=\bigcup_{i=0}^{d-1}T^{i}X_{0}
, and for
i=0,\ldots,d-1
, the transformation
T^{d}
leaves the set
T^{i}X_{0}
invariant and the corresponding system is totally ergodic.

Since
\int f\,dm_{X}>0
and
X=\bigcup_{i=0}^{d-1}T^{i}X_{0}
, there exists
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
such that
\int f\,dm_{T^{i_{0}}X_{0}}>0
. We let

I_{d}:=\{n\colon d|a(n)\}.

We claim that
for
I:=I_{d}
the positivity property (
63
) holds, or equivalently, that

(64)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{d,N}}\int f\cdot T^{k_{1}a(n)}f\cdots T^{k_{\ell}a(n)}f\,dm_{X}>0

where for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
I_{d,N}:=I_{d}\cap[N]
.

Let
S:=T^{d}
. For
i\in[d]
, the transformation
S
leaves the set
T^{i}X_{0}
invariant, and the system
(T^{i}X_{0},m_{T^{i}X_{0}},S)
is totally ergodic.
Let
(a_{d}(n))
be the sequence defined by

(a(n)/d)_{n\in I_{d}}
. Since the set
I_{d}
has positive density, it suffices to show that

(65)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\int f\cdot S^{k_{1}a_{d}(n)}f\cdots S^{k_{\ell}a_{d}(n)}f\,dm_{T^{i_{0}}X_{0}}>0.

Our assumptions and Lemma
9.9
and
9.10
imply that the sequence
(a_{d}(n))
is good for
\ell
-step irrational equidistribution and
good for seminorm control along
\ell
-term APs for all ergodic systems.
So we are now in position to use the implication
(iii)\implies(i)
in Theorem
9.3
. It gives that the sequence
(a_{d}(n))
is good for
\ell
-convergence for all totally ergodic systems. We apply this for the totally ergodic nilsystem
(T^{i_{0}}X_{0},m_{T^{i_{0}}X_{0}},S)
, and we get that the average in (
65
) equals

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\int f\cdot S^{k_{1}n}f\cdots S^{k_{\ell}n}f\,dm_{T^{i_{0}}X_{0}}.

Since
f\geq 0
and
\int f\,dm_{T^{i_{0}}X_{0}}>0
, the last limit is positive by Furstenberg’s multiple recurrence result
[
15
]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Applications of P-functions to Fully Nonlinear Elliptic equations: Gradient Estimates and Rigidity Results
Dimitrios Gazoulis

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of P − P- functions for fully nonlinear equations and establish a general criterion for obtaining such quantities for this class of equations. Some applications are gradient bounds, De Giorgi-type properties of entire solutions and rigidity results. In particular, we establish a pointwise gradient bound and a rigidity result for Pucci’s equations. This pointwise gradient bound generalizes the Modica inequality in the case of fully nonlinear elliptic equations. Furthermore, we prove Harnack-type inequalities and local estimates for the gradient of solutions. In addition, we consider such quantities for higher order nonlinear equations and for equations of order greater than two we obtain Liouville-type theorems and pointwise estimates for the Laplacian.



1.
Introduction

In this work we introduce the notion of
P-
function
for fully nonlinear partial differential equations or differential inequalities and we incorporate the “
P-
function
technique” in a general setting. This abstract setting allow us to obtain many applications by only determining an example of a
P-
function
. Some of these applications are gradient bounds for entire solutions, Harnack -type inequalities for the gradient of solutions and rigidity results.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In section 2 we define the notion of
P-
functions
and study such quantities for fully nonlinear elliptic equations. We provide two general criteria for obtaining
P-
functions
for this class of equations. Different examples of such quantities may give various types of gradient bounds for solutions of a particular equation. For instance, in section 3, we obtain a gradient bound for entire solutions of the Allen-Cahn equation that differs from the Modica inequality.

In section 3, we prove an abstract pointwise estimate, i.e. Theorem
3.1
, for a class of
P-
functions
that are associated to any given fully nonlinear equation. This pointwise estimate is in fact a gradient bound for entire solutions in a wide variety of fully nonlinear elliptic equations and some examples of such gradient bounds are given. As an application, we prove a pointwise gradient bound for entire solutions of Pucci’s equations.

Additional consequences are also illustrated in section 4 where we establish a Liouville-type theorem and a De Giorgi-type property for entire solutions. One application is Theorem
4.1
, that is an abstract rigidity result for entire solutions of fully nonlinear elliptic equations such as Pucci’s equations. This result also recovers as particular cases the classical results of J. Serrin in
[
29
]
.

In section 5 we establish a Harnack-type inequality and local pointwise estimates for the gradient of solutions to fully nonlinear elliptic equations, such as for the Monge-Ampère equation.

Moreover, in section 6, we study such quantities for nonlinear equations of order greater than two together with some applications. For example, we establish an a priori bound for the Laplacian and pointwise estimates through the mean value properties for higher order equations. Also, some Liouville-type properties can be established for nonlinear equations of order greater than two. In this setting, one can obtain many other types of bounds for any order of derivatives, assuming a
C^{k,\alpha}
a priori estimate and provided that we have an appropriate
P-
function
related to the respective equation.

2.
P-
functions for Fully Nonlinear Elliptic equations

We begin by defining the notion of P-function

Definition 2.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a smooth solution or subsolution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m}u)=0

(2.1)

where
F
is a real valued function defined on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times...\times\mathbb{R}^{n^{m}}
.

We say that
P=P(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)
is a
P-
function of (
2.1
) if there exists an elliptic operator
L
and a non negative function
\mu=\mu(x)\geq 0

\begin{gathered}L=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}+c\;\;,\;\textrm{with}\;\>c\geq 0,\\
\textrm{such that}\;\>\mu L\>P\leq 0\;\;,\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega.\end{gathered}

(2.2)

An immediate corollary is that any
P-
function related to an equation or to a differential inequality attains its maximum at the boundary
\partial\Omega
or at a point
x\in\Omega
such that
\mu(x)=0
.

We initially state as a direct consequence a strong maximum principle that holds in general (see Theorem 2.2 in
[
6
]
or Theorem 4.7 in
[
10
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution or subsolution of

\begin{gathered}F(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m}u)=0\;\;\;,\;\;u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\textrm{where}\;\>\Omega\;\>\textrm{is a connected, bounded subset of}\;\>\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{gathered}

(2.3)

such that
\inf_{\overline{\Omega}}g(\nabla^{k}u)>0
for some
g:\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}\times d}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\;,\;k\in\{1,...,m-1\}
and suppose that
P=P(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)
is a
P-
function of (
2.3
) with
\mu=\mu(g(\nabla^{k}u))\;,\>\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall\>t>0
.

If there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

P(x_{0},u(x_{0}),...,\nabla^{m-1}u(x_{0}))=\sup_{\Omega}P(x,u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u),

(2.4)

then
P(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)
is constant in
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof is an immediate consequence of the strong maximum principle since
\\
\mu(g(\nabla^{k}u))>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

The most common choice of
g
in Theorem
2.2
above is the Euclidean norm. For example, if
k=1
,
g(\nabla u)=|\nabla u|.
If
\mu>0\;,\>\forall\>t\geq 0
, then the assumption
\inf_{\overline{\Omega}}g(\nabla^{k}u)>0
is dismissed.

Remark 2.3
.

The constancy of
P-
functions with a particular form hides geometric information on the level sets
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>u(x)=t\}
of the solution
u
, such as the property of being surfaces of zero mean curvature (see Proposition 4.11 in
[
10
]
).

We now focus on fully nonlinear elliptic equations. Let
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth solution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0,

(2.5)

where
F:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfies the ellipticity condition

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}F_{a_{ij}}(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\;\;\;,\;\forall\>x\in\Omega\;,\;\forall\>\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.6)

Here we use the notation
F=F(x,s,q,A)\;,\;s\in\mathbb{R}\;,\;q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;,\;A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
F_{a_{ij}}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}.

Some important examples of fully nonlinear elliptic equations are

(1)
Pucci’s equations.
We introduce the Pucci’s extremal operators. Let
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
A\in\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the class of symmetric
n\times n
matrices, we define

\begin{gathered}\mathscr{M}^{-}(A,\lambda,\Lambda)=\mathscr{M}^{-}(A)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}\\
\mathscr{M}^{+}(A,\lambda,\Lambda)=\mathscr{M}^{+}(A)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}\end{gathered}

(2.7)

where
e_{i}=e_{i}(A)
are the eigenvalues of
A.
It holds that
\mathscr{M}^{-}
and
\mathscr{M}^{+}
are uniformly elliptic with ellipticity constants
\lambda
and
n\Lambda
(see
[
4
]
).

Pucci’s equations are

\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)=f(x,u)

(2.8)

and

\mathscr{M}^{+}(\nabla^{2}u)=f(x,u)

(2.9)

respectively.

(2)
Monge-Ampère’s equation

\displaystyle det(\nabla^{2}u)=f(x)

for strictly convex solutions
u
and
f>0.\\
\\

(3)
Equation of Prescribed Gauss curvature

\displaystyle det(\nabla^{2}u)=K(x)(1+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{n+2}{2}}

K(x)
is the Gauss curvature of the graph
u
at
(x,u(x))
. Again, this equation is elliptic for strictly convex solutions
u.\\
\\

(4)
Quasi-Linear equations of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(\nabla u)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}=F(x,u,\nabla u)

where
a_{ij}
satisfy the ellipticity condition
\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}
. The
p-
Laplace equation, the Allen-Cahn equation and the minimal surface equation belong in this class of equations. Such equations are thoroughly studied in
[
6
,
10
,
13
]
among others.

There are many other examples of fully nonlinear elliptic equations, such as Bellman equation and Isaacs equation (see
[
4
]
).

We now provide two general criteria for obtaining
P-
functions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution of (
2.5
) and
F
satisfies (
2.6
). Consider the quantity

I:=(\lambda g^{\prime\prime}(u)-2F_{u})|\nabla u|^{2}+(g^{\prime}(u)\nabla_{q}F-2\nabla_{x}F)\nabla u+g^{\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}F_{a_{ij}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}

(2.10)

and assume that
I\geq 0
for some function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Then
P(u,\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{2}+g(u)
is a
P-
function of (
2.5
).

Proof.

Assume that
I\geq 0
for some
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and let
P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})=|\nabla u|^{2}+g(u)
.

We have

\begin{gathered}P_{x_{i}}=2\sum_{k}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}}+g^{\prime}(u)u_{x_{i}}\\
\Rightarrow P_{x_{i}x_{j}}=2\sum_{k}(u_{x_{k}x_{j}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}}+u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}})+g^{\prime\prime}(u)u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}+g^{\prime}(u)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\\
\Rightarrow\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}=2\sum_{i,j,k}(d_{ij}u_{x_{k}x_{j}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}}+d_{ij}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}})+g^{\prime\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}\\
+g^{\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\\
\geq 2\lambda|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,k}d_{ij}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}}+\lambda g^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}+g^{\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\end{gathered}

(2.11)

where
d_{ij}=F_{a_{ij}}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}
.

Differentiating (
2.5
) over
x_{k}
, and then multiplying by
u_{k}
, we obtain

\begin{gathered}F_{x_{k}}+F_{u}u_{x_{k}}+\sum_{m}F_{q_{m}}u_{x_{m}x_{k}}+\sum_{m,l}d_{ml}u_{x_{m}x_{l}x_{k}}=0\\
\Rightarrow\sum_{m,l,k}d_{ml}u_{k}u_{x_{m}x_{l}x_{k}}=-\nabla_{x}F\nabla u-F_{u}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P+\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(u)\nabla_{q}F\nabla u\end{gathered}

(2.12)

Therefore the last equation of (
2.11
) becomes

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P\geq 2\lambda|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+g^{\prime}(u)\nabla_{q}F\nabla u+(\lambda g^{\prime\prime}(u)-2F_{u})|\nabla u|^{2}\\
-2\nabla u\nabla_{x}F+g^{\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}F_{a_{ij}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\geq 0\end{gathered}

(2.13)

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution of (
2.5
) and
F
satisfies (
2.6
). Consider the quantity

\begin{gathered}J:=(\lambda B^{\prime\prime}(u)-2A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|^{2})F_{u})|\nabla u|^{2}+(B^{\prime}(u)\nabla_{q}F-2A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|^{2})\nabla_{x}F)\nabla u\\
+B^{\prime}(u)\sum_{i,j}F_{a_{ij}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\frac{\lambda(B^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{2A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|^{2})}\end{gathered}

(2.14)

and assume that
J\geq 0
for some functions
A,B:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
A^{\prime}>0\;,\>\forall t>0
and
A^{\prime\prime}\geq 0.

Then
P(u,\nabla u)=A(|\nabla u|^{2})+B(u)
is a
P-
function of (
2.5
).

Proof.

We argue as in Lemma
2.4
and obtain

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}=A^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}[|\nabla u|^{2}]_{x_{i}}[|\nabla u|^{2}]_{x_{j}})+2A^{\prime}(\sum_{i,j,k}d_{ij}[u_{x_{k}x_{j}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}}+u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}}])\\
+\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}(B^{\prime\prime}(u)u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}+B^{\prime}(u)u_{x_{i}x_{j}})\\
\geq 2A^{\prime}\lambda|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2A^{\prime}\sum_{i,j,k}d_{ij}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}}+\lambda B^{\prime\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}+B^{\prime}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\end{gathered}

(2.15)

by (
2.6
) and since
A^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
, where
d_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}.

We also calculate from the first equation of (
2.11
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i}(P_{x_{i}}-B^{\prime}u_{x_{i}})^{2}\leq 4(A^{\prime})^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\\
\Rightarrow 2A^{\prime}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2|\nabla u|^{2}A^{\prime}}(|\nabla P|^{2}-2B^{\prime}\nabla u\nabla P+(B^{\prime})^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})\end{gathered}

(2.16)

In addition, similarly to (
2.12
) we have

\sum_{m,l,k}d_{ml}u_{k}u_{x_{m}x_{l}x_{k}}=-\nabla_{x}F\nabla u-F_{u}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2A^{\prime}}(\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P-B^{\prime}\nabla u\nabla_{q}F)

(2.17)

We plug (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) into (
2.15
) and thus

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2|\nabla u|^{2}A^{\prime}}(|\nabla P|^{2}-2B^{\prime}\nabla u\nabla P+(B^{\prime})^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})+\lambda B^{\prime\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}\\
+B^{\prime}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+2A^{\prime}(-\nabla_{x}F\nabla u-F_{u}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2A^{\prime}}(\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P-B^{\prime}\nabla u\nabla_{q}F))\end{gathered}

(2.18)

which gives

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}+(\frac{\lambda B^{\prime}(u)}{A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}}\nabla u+\nabla_{q}F)\nabla_{x}P\geq J\end{gathered}

(2.19)

and we conclude.
∎

3.
Gradient Bounds for entire solutions of Fully Nonlinear equations

In this section we will see that utilizing the techniques of
[
6
,
13
]
, we can obtain gradient bounds for solutions of equations of the form (
2.5
). To be more precise, for any explicit example of
P-
function, we obtain a particular gradient bound.

Some of the regularity assumptions in this work can be relaxed for some classes of equations.
In the study of Quasi-linear equations for example, one can assume that
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as in assumption (i) in Theorem 1.6 in
[
6
]
and utilize regularity results in
[
33
]
afterwords. For fully nonlinear elliptic equations, we can relax the regularity of solutions and then utilize regularity results from
[
4
,
34
]
. However, our main goal is not the optimal regularity assumptions since we state the results in an abstract form. Therefore, we will assume that the solutions are smooth and satisfy an analog of assumption (ii) in Theorem 1.6 in
[
6
]
. Note that smoothness of solutions is often assumed for fully nonlinear equations (see for example
[
34
]
), that is sufficient to apply Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
. One application of interest is a pointwise gradient bound for entire solutions of Pucci’s equation that we establish in subsection 3.1. This gradient bound generalizes the Modica inequality in the case of fully nonlinear elliptic equations.

Assumption
.

\begin{gathered}u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;,\>\nabla u\in C^{\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\;\>\textrm{for some}\;\>\alpha\in(0,1)\\
\textrm{and}\;\>\textrm{there exists}\;\>C=C(||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})>0\;\>\textrm{such that}\;\>|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\>,\;\forall\;\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{gathered}

(3.1)

The next theorem provides an a priori pointwise estimate for solutions of (
2.5
). The theorem below holds for any
P-
function that satisfies
P(u,0)\leq 0
. When
P
is of the form
P=P(u,\nabla u)
we use the notation
P(u,0)
instead of
P(u,0,...,0)
and also we sometimes write
P=P(u;x)
for simplicity.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

(3.2)

that satisfy assumption (
3.1
). If
P=P(u,\nabla u)
is a
P-
function of (
3.2
), with
\mu=\mu(|\nabla u|)
,
\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall\>t>0
, such that
P(s,0)\leq 0
,

Then

P(u(x),\nabla u(x))\leq 0\;\;,\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
u
be a solution of (
3.2
) that satisfies assumption (
3.1
) and consider the family of all translations of
u
,

\mathscr{F}=\{v\>:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\>|\;\exists\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>\>\textrm{such that}\;\>v(x)=u(x+z)\;\>\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

(3.4)

\mathscr{F}
is non empty since
u\in\mathscr{F}
.

Let
P
be a
P-
function of (
3.2
), with
\mu=\mu(|\nabla u|)
,
\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall t>0
, such that
P(u,0)\leq 0
. For simplicity, we denote
P=P(u;x)
instead of
P=P(u(x),\nabla u(x))
.

Consider now

P_{0}=\sup\{P(v;x)\>|\>v\in\mathscr{F}\;,\;\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

(3.5)

We will prove that

\displaystyle P_{0}\leq 0

(3.6)

and from this we conclude.

We argue by contradiction, so we suppose that

P_{0}>0

(3.7)

Then, by (
3.5
) there exist
(w_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{F}
and
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}P(w_{k};x_{k})=P_{0}

(3.8)

Let
v_{k}(x)=w(x+x_{k})
. Also, by definition we have that
v_{k}\in\mathscr{F}
and
P(v_{k};0)=P(w_{k};x_{k})
, so that (
3.8
) can be rewritten as

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}P(v_{k};0)=P_{0}

(3.9)

Since
v_{k}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the Ascoli-Arzela theorem together with a diagonal argument, we can extract from
(v_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a subsequence, denoted by
(v_{k}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges with its first-order derivatives, uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\tilde{v}
the limit function.

By the assumption (
3.1
) we have that

\mathscr{F}\;\>\textrm{is relatively compact in}\;\>C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3.10)

Thus
\tilde{v}\in\mathscr{F}
and

P(\tilde{v};0)=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}P(v_{k}^{(k)};0)=P_{0}

(3.11)

by (
3.9
).

Consider now the set

U=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>P(\tilde{v};x)=P_{0}\}

(3.12)

from the continuity of
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\;,\>U
is closed and non empty since
0\in U
. We will prove that
U
is also open. Let
x_{0}\in U
, we observe that
|\nabla\tilde{v}(x_{0})|\neq 0
, otherwise we would have

\displaystyle P_{0}=P(\tilde{v};x_{0})=P(\tilde{v}(x_{0}),\nabla\tilde{v}(x_{0}))=P(\tilde{v}(x_{0}),0)\leq 0

against the assumption that
P_{0}>0
.

By continuity, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\inf_{\overline{B}_{\delta}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|>0\;\;\textrm{and thus}\;\>\inf_{\overline{B}_{\delta}(x_{0})}\mu(|\nabla\tilde{v}|)>0

(3.13)

and by Theorem
2.2
we conclude that

P(\tilde{v};x)\equiv P_{0}\;\;\;\textrm{in}\;\>B_{\delta}(x_{0})

(3.14)

So
U
is open and it follows that
U=\mathbb{R}^{n}
by connectedness.

On the other hand, since
\tilde{v}
is bounded it holds that there exists a sequence
(y_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nabla\tilde{v}(y_{l})=0

(3.15)

By the boundedness of
\tilde{v}
we also have
\tilde{v}(y_{l})=\tilde{v}_{l}\rightarrow v_{0}
up to a subsequence that we still denote as
y_{l}
, and so we obtain

\displaystyle 0<P_{0}=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}P(\tilde{v}(y_{l}),\nabla\tilde{v}(y_{l}))=P(v_{0},0)

which contradicts the assumption
P(s,0)\leq 0
. Therefore
P_{0}\leq 0
and we conclude.
∎

A direct consequence is a general gradient bound for fully nonlinear equations.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

(3.16)

that satisfy assumption (
3.1
) and
F
satisfies the ellipticity condition (
2.6
). Consider

is a
P-
function from Lemma

such that

.

Then

\begin{gathered}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\Psi(u)\\
\textrm{where}\;\>\Psi(s)=A^{-1}(-B(s))\end{gathered}

(3.17)

Similarly, if
P
is obtained from Lemma
2.4
and
g\leq 0
, we have
|\nabla u|^{2}\leq G(u)
, where

.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)
The assumption
P(s,0)\leq 0
can be replaced by assuming that there exists a sequence
(s_{k},q_{k})
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P(s_{k},q_{k})\leq 0.

(2)
Note also that the condition

can be removed. Consider for example

be a

function of (

) such that the condition

is not satisfied and set

. Then

is also a
P-
function of (

) and satisfies
\tilde{P}(u,0)\leq 0
. The only difference is that the gradient bound in this case takes the form

(3)
The
C^{2}
regularity of solutions in needed in the set of points where the gradient of

does not vanish.

3.1.
A Pointwise Gradient Bound for entire solutions of Pucci’s equations

We denote as
\mathscr{M}^{-}
and
\mathscr{M}^{+}
as the Pucci’s extremal operators defined in (
2.7
). The first application of Theorem
3.1
is the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{3}
bounded solution of

\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)=F^{\prime}(u)\;\;\;,\;F\geq 0

(3.18)

Then

\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{F(u)}{\lambda}

(3.19)

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{M}^{-}
is concave, by regularity theory in
[
4
]
(see also
[
33
]
) and the boundedness of
u
, we have that
u
satisfies (
3.1
).

We set in Lemma
2.5
,
A(t)=\frac{t}{2}\;,\;B(s)=-\frac{F(s)}{\lambda}
and we calculate

\displaystyle J=(-\lambda\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\lambda}+F^{\prime\prime})|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{F^{\prime}(u)}{\lambda}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}(\nabla^{2}u)}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\frac{(F^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow J=\frac{(F^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{\lambda}-\frac{F^{\prime}(u)}{\lambda}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}(\nabla^{2}u)}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}

Also we have the following,

\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}=v_{i}^{k}v_{j}^{k}\;\;,\;\>i,j=1,...,n,

(3.20)

where
v^{k}
is the unit length eigenvector of
e_{k}
(this identity holds since
e_{k}
are distinct).

Identity (
3.20
) is an algebraic fact and can be proved as follows. Let
A
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix with eigenvalues
e_{k}
and respective unit length eigenvectors
v^{k}
,

\displaystyle A\cdot v^{k}=e_{k}v^{k}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(dA)v^{k}+A(dv^{k})=de_{k}v^{k}+e_{k}dv^{k}

where
d
is a differential operator, say for example the partial derivative with respect to the element
a_{ij}
of
A
. Since
v^{k}
is unit length,
v^{k}dv^{k}=\frac{1}{2}d(|v^{k}|^{2})=0
, so

\displaystyle(v^{k})^{T}(dA)v^{k}=de_{k}

since
(v^{k})^{T}A=e_{k}(v^{k})^{T}
.

Thus,

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}=\lambda\sum_{e_{k}>0}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{k}<0}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\\
=\lambda\sum_{e_{k}>0}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}v_{i}^{k}v_{j}^{k}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{k}<0}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}v_{i}^{k}v_{j}^{k}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\end{gathered}

(3.21)

by (
3.20
).

Also,
(v^{k})^{T}(\nabla^{2}u)v^{k}=e_{k}
, since
(v^{k})^{T}v^{k}=1
, so

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}=\lambda\sum_{e_{k}>0}e_{k}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{k}<0}e_{k}=F^{\prime}(u)\end{gathered}

(3.22)

by (
3.18
) and (
3.20
).

Therefore,

\displaystyle J=\frac{(F^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{\lambda}-\frac{F^{\prime}(u)}{\lambda}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)}{\partial u_{x_{i}x_{j}}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}=0

thus, by Lemma
2.5
, we have that
P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{F(u)}{\lambda}
is a
P-
function of (
3.18
).

In addition,
P(u,0)=-\frac{F(u)}{\lambda}\leq 0
, therefore by Theorem
3.1
we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{F(u)}{\lambda}\;\;\;,\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

The gradient bound (
3.19
) also holds for the operator
\mathscr{M}^{+}
with similar calculations. The regularity theory from
[
4
]
can be applied in this case since
\mathscr{M}^{+}
is convex.

∎

4.
Rigidity results and properties of entire solutions of fully nonlinear equations

In this section we will see that if an equation admits a
P-
function of the form
P=|\nabla u|^{2}
, then the solutions that satisfy assumption (
3.1
) are constant. As a result we have a Liouville theorem for Pucci’s equations and special cases are some of the well-known results of J. Serrin in
[
29
]
.

In particular we have

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

F(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

(4.1)

that satisfy assumption (
3.1
) where
F
satisfies the condition (
2.6
) and assume
F_{u}\leq 0
.

Then
u
is a constant

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.4
by considering
P=|\nabla u|^{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(1)
If
F=F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)
and assume in addition that
\nabla_{x}F\cdot\nabla u\leq 0
, then the conclusion of Theorem
4.1
still holds by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.4
. Note also that Theorem 1, Theorem 6 and Theorem 8 in
[
29
]
are recovered.

(2)
We note that, in the special case where equation (
4.1
) takes the form

div(\Phi^{\prime}(|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u)=f^{\prime}(u)

(4.2)

then the condition
F_{u}\leq 0
reads
f^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq 0
which implies stability of the solutions, i.e. the second variation of the associated energy functional
J(u)=\int(\frac{1}{2}\Phi(|\nabla u|^{2})+f(u))dx
is non negative (see also Theorem 4.5 in
[
6
]
).

In addition, we have a Liouville-type result for Pucci’s equation as a direct application of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

\mathscr{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u)=F^{\prime}(u)

(4.3)

that satisfy (
3.1
) and assume that
F^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq 0
.

Then
u
is a constant.

Corollary
4.3
also holds for
\mathscr{M}^{+}
.
Another consequence of Theorem
3.1
is the following Liouville-type result

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of (
3.2
) that satisfies assumption (
3.1
) and
P
is a
P-
function from
2.5
that satisfies
P(u,0)\leq 0
. If there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
B(u(x_{0}))=0
, then
u\equiv\textrm{const.}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of Theorem 1.8 in
[
6
]
with slight modifications. For the convenience of the reader we provide the details.

Suppose that
B(u(x_{0}))=0
, let
u_{0}=u(x_{0})
and consider the set

V=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>u(x)=u_{0}\}

(4.4)

V
is a closed set and by the assumption, non empty. Let
x_{1}\in V
and consider the function
\phi(t)=u(x_{1}+t\omega)-u_{0}
, where
|\omega|=1
is arbitrarily fixed. We have
|\phi^{\prime}(t)|=|\nabla u(x_{1}+t\omega)|
. By the gradient bound in Corollary
3.2
we have,

|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\Psi(u)\;\;,\;\textrm{where}\;\>\Psi(s)=A^{-1}(-B(s))

(4.5)

Since
\Psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\Psi(u_{0})=0
, we have
\Psi(u)=O(|u-u_{0}|^{2})
, as
|u-u_{0}|\rightarrow 0
. So, we conclude from (
4.5
) that
|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\leq C|\phi(t)|
for
t
small enough. Since
\phi(0)=0
, we must have
\phi\equiv 0
on
[-\delta,\delta]
, for some
\delta>0
. Thus
V
is open, which gives that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Also, we have a De Giorgi type property for solutions that attain the equality at a point in the gradient bound obtained in Corollary
3.2
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

F(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

(4.6)

that satisfy assumption (
3.1
) and let
P=P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})
be a
P-
function of (
4.6
) obtained in Lemma
2.5
that satisfies
P(u,0)\leq 0
.
If there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

P(u(x_{0}),|\nabla u(x_{0})|^{2})=0

(4.7)

then there exists a function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{gathered}\textrm{either}\;\;\;\>u(x)=g(a\cdot x+b)\;,\;a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{with}\;\>|a|=1,\;,\>b\in\mathbb{R}\\
\textrm{or}\;\;\;\>u(x)=g(|x-z_{0}|+c)\;\>,\;z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>c\in\mathbb{R}\end{gathered}

(4.8)

Proof.

By Corollary
3.2
, we have that
P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})\leq 0
.

We begin by considering the set

\mathscr{A}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>:\>P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})=0\}

(4.9)

\mathscr{A}
is closed and by the assumption
\mathscr{A}\neq\emptyset
. We are going to prove that
\mathscr{A}
is open.

Let
x_{1}\in\mathscr{A}
, if
\nabla u(x_{1})=0
, we obtain by the form
P(s,t)=A(t)+B(s)
that
P(u(x_{1}),0)=-B(u(x_{1}))=0
. By Theorem
4.4
, we conclude that
u\equiv u(x_{1})
and
\nabla u\equiv 0
and hence
P\equiv 0
.

On the other hand, if
\nabla u(x_{1})\neq 0
, we have
\inf_{\overline{B}_{\delta_{1}}(x_{1})}|\nabla u|>0
for some
\delta_{1}>0

and by Theorem
2.2
we conclude that
P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})\equiv 0
in
B_{\delta_{1}}(x_{1})
and therefore
\mathscr{A}
is open.

By connectedness, we have that
\mathscr{A}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is,

P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})\equiv 0\;\;,\;\forall\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(4.10)

and
P_{t}=A^{\prime}(t)>0
, thus

|\nabla u|^{2}=\Psi(u)\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{in}\;\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\;,\;\textrm{where}\;\>\Psi(u)=A^{-1}(-B(u))

(4.11)

Now, if there exists
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Phi(u(x_{2}))=0
, so
|\nabla u(x_{2})|=0
, again by Theorem
4.4
we have that
u\equiv u(x_{2})
.

If, on the other hand
\Psi(u(x))>0\;\>,\;\forall\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

\begin{gathered}v=G(u)\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{where}\;\>G^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{\Psi(s)}\\
\textrm{and}\;\>|\nabla v|^{2}=1\;\;\;\textrm{in}\;\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{gathered}

(4.12)

Therefore, by the result in
[
5
]
, we have that

\begin{gathered}\textrm{either}\;\>v(x)=a\cdot x+b\;\>,\;a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{with}\;\>|a|=1\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>b\in\mathbb{R}\\
\textrm{or}\;\>v(x)=|x-z_{0}|+c\;\>,\;z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>c\in\mathbb{R}\end{gathered}

(4.13)

So we conclude that

\begin{gathered}\textrm{either}\;\;\;\>u(x)=g(a\cdot x+b)\;\>,\;a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{with}\;\>|a|=1\;,\;\>b\in\mathbb{R}\;\>\textrm{where}\;\>g(s)=G^{-1}(s)\\
\textrm{or}\;\;\;\>u(x)=g(|x-z_{0}|+c)\;\>,\;z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>c\in\mathbb{R}\end{gathered}

(4.14)

∎

Remark 4.6
.

Note that if
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where
\Omega
is an open and connected domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P=P(u,|\nabla u|^{2})=A(|\nabla u|^{2})+B(u)
with
A^{\prime}>0
that attains its maximum at a point then
u
will be a solution of the Eikonal equation
|\nabla u|^{2}=\Psi(u)
. If in addition
u_{x_{n}}>0
and consider
F_{i}=\frac{u_{x_{i}}}{u_{x_{n}}}
, by Proposition 2.1 in
[
19
]
, the function
F=(F_{1},...,F_{n-1})
will satisfy the Isobaric Euler equation.

5.
A Harnack-type inequality and Local Estimates for the gradient

We will establish a Harnack inequality and local estimates for the gradient of solutions to to fully nonlinear elliptic equations in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Lemma
2.4
with
g=0
or by Lemma
2.5
with
B=0
, we can utilize the elliptic inequality (
2.13
) or (
2.19
) respectively and assuming that
F_{a_{ij}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we can apply Local Estimates for subsolutions to general elliptic operators to the
P-
function and obtain similar local estimates for the gradient of solutions to fully nonlinear equations.

So, we consider solutions of the equation

F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

(5.1)

where
F:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function and satisfies the ellipticity condition (
2.6
). We denote
F=F(x,s,q,A)\;\>,\;F_{q_{i}}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial q_{i}}\;,\;F_{a_{ij}}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}
.

In this section we will assume the bound

|F_{a_{ij}}|,|F_{q_{i}}|,|F_{s}|\leq M.

(5.2)

We first establish a local pointwise estimate for the gradient of solutions.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth solution of (
5.1
) and assume
\nabla_{x}F\cdot\nabla u\leq 0
. Then for any
B_{2R}\subset\Omega
and
p>0

\sup_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\frac{||\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2p}(B_{2R})}}{|B_{2R}|^{1/p}}

(5.3)

where
C=C(n,p,\lambda,\Lambda,MR^{2})
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Set
P=|\nabla u|^{2}
, by Lemma
2.4
and the assumption
\nabla_{x}F\cdot\nabla u\leq 0
we have

\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P+2F_{u}P\geq 0

(5.4)

where
d_{ij}=F_{a_{ij}}
.
Thus, by classical local pointwise estimates for subsolutions of elliptic equations (see for example Theorem 9.20 in
[
22
]
) we obtain (
5.3
).
∎

Next, we obtain the following Harnack estimate

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth solution of (
5.1
) and assume
\nabla_{x}F\cdot\nabla u\geq 0
,
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\in L^{n}(\Omega)
.
Then for any
B_{2R}\subset\Omega
there holds that for
p>0
,

\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{R}{\lambda}||\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{2n}(B_{2R})}^{2}\right),

(5.5)

where
C=C(n,p,\lambda,\Lambda,MR^{2}).

Proof.

Set
P=|\nabla u|^{2}
. We argue as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
and by the assumption
\nabla_{x}F\cdot\nabla u\geq 0
to obtain

\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\nabla_{q}F\nabla_{x}P+2F_{u}P\leq 2\Lambda|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

(5.6)

So, by the Harnack inequality for supersolutions of elliptic equations (see Theorem 9.22 in
[
22
]
for instance) we conclude.
∎

In the end of this section, we conclude with the following Harnack inequality for the Monge-Ampère equation

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth and convex solution of

det(\nabla^{2}u)=f(u,\nabla u)

(5.7)

where
f>0
and assume
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\in L^{n}(\Omega)
and
|f_{q_{i}}|,|adj^{T}(\nabla^{2}u)_{ij}|\leq M.

Then for any
B_{2R}\subset\Omega
we have

\sup_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{R}{\lambda}||\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{2n}(B_{2R})}^{2}\right)

(5.8)

where
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,MR^{2}).

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
and since
\frac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}(A)=adj^{T}(A)_{ij}
, for
F(A)=det(A)
by Jacobi’s formula.
∎

6.
Higher order nonlinear equations

In this last section, we will provide examples of
P-
functions for higher order nonlinear equations and their applications. In particular, an analogous version of Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
, allow us to obtain properties and pointwise estimates of entire solutions even in this case. Moreover, we establish local pointwise estimates for nonlinear equations of order greater than two, through the mean value properties of the
P-
functions or with analogous arguments to that of section 5, applied in higher order equations. This method can be applied to many other classes of higher order nonlinear equations.

We begin by stating the analogous Theorem
3.1
for equations of general order.

Assumption

\begin{gathered}u\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;\;,\;\nabla^{m-1}u\in C^{\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;\>\textrm{for some}\;\>\alpha\in(0,1)\\
\textrm{and there exists}\;\>C>0\;\>\textrm{such that}\;\>|\nabla^{l}u|\leq C\;\>,\;l=1,...,m-1.\end{gathered}

(6.1)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
u
be an entire solution or subsolution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m}u)=0

(6.2)

that satisfies assumption (
6.1
) and let
P=P(u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)=P(u;x)
be a
P-
function of (
6.2
) such that one of the following holds:
(i)
\mu=\mu(g(\nabla^{k}u))
for some
g:\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;,\>g(z)>0\;,\forall\>z\neq 0\;,\>g((0,...,0))=0\;,\\
\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall\>t>0
and
P(u;x)\leq 0\;,
when
\nabla^{k}u=(0,...,0)\;,\;k\in\{1,...,m-1\}\>,\\

(ii)
\mu=\mu(g(\nabla^{k}u))
for some
g:\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;,\>g(z)>0\;,\forall\>z\neq 0\;,\>g((0,...,0))=0\;,\\
\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall\>t>0\;,\>P(u;x)\leq 0\;,
when
\nabla^{l}u=(0,...,0)\;,\>k\neq l\>,\;k,l\in\{1,...,m-1\}
and
g(\nabla^{k}u)>0\;,\>\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then
P(u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)\leq 0\;\>\forall\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
with minor modifications.
∎

We now provide the generalization of Theorem
4.1
in the higher order case.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
u
be an entire solution of

F(x,u,\nabla u,...,\nabla^{m}u)=0

(6.3)

and let
P=P(u,...,\nabla^{m-1}u)=P(u;x)
be a
P-
function of (
6.2
) such that
\mu=\mu(g(\nabla^{k}u))
for some
g:\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;,\>g(z)>0\;,\forall\>z\neq 0\;,\>g((0,...,0))=0\;,\>\mu(t)>0\;,\>\forall\>t>0
and

\begin{gathered}P=H(\nabla^{k}u)\;\;,\;\textrm{where}\;\>H\>:\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\\
\textrm{and}\;\>\{H=0\}=\{0\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{k}}\}\;\>,\>k\in\{1,...,m-1\}\end{gathered}

(6.4)

Then
\nabla^{k-1}u
is a constant.

Proof.

The proof is direct consequence of Theorem
6.1
since
P=H(\nabla^{k}u)=0
when
\nabla^{k}u
vanish which gives
P\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

6.1.
Local and Global Pointwise estimates

The arguments of section 5, can be applied for higher order nonlinear equations. In this case, we extract local and global estimates for higher order of derivatives of
u
, such as for the Laplacian.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth and convex subsolution of

\Delta^{2}u-F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{3}u)=0\;\;,\;\textrm{with}\;\>F\geq 0

(6.5)

Then for any
B_{R}\subset\Omega
, any
0<r<R
and any
p\geq 1
,

\sup_{B_{r}}(\Delta u)^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{n/p}}||\Delta u||^{2}_{L^{2p}(B_{R})}

(6.6)

where
C=C(n,p)
.

Proof.

\begin{gathered}P_{x_{i}}=2\Delta u\Delta u_{x_{i}}\\
P_{x_{i}x_{i}}=2(\Delta u_{x_{i}})^{2}+2\Delta u\Delta u_{x_{i}x_{i}}\\
\Rightarrow\Delta P=2|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+2\Delta u\Delta^{2}u\geq 2|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+F\Delta u\geq 0\end{gathered}

(6.7)

Therefore, by classical estimates for subsolutions of elliptic equations he conclude (see for example Theorem 4.14 in
[
24
]
).
∎

Furthermore, we give some examples of
P-
functions of the form
P=P(u,|\nabla u|,\Delta u)
related to forth order nonlinear equations together with applications.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution of

\begin{gathered}a(\Delta u)[|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u)]=b(u)|\nabla u|^{4}\\
\textrm{where}\;\>a,b\>:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>a>0\;,\;a^{\prime}\geq 0\end{gathered}

(6.8)

and set
P(s,t)=A(t)-B(s)
such that
A^{\prime}=a
and
B^{\prime\prime}=b
.

Then
P=P(u,\Delta u)=A(\Delta u)-B(u)
is a
P-
function of (
6.8
).

In addition, if
u
satisfies (
6.1
) with
m=4\;,\>B(u)\geq 0
and
u_{x_{n}}>0
, then

\Delta u\leq\Gamma(u)\;\;\>\forall\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;\>\textrm{where}\;\>\Gamma(u)=A^{-1}(B(u)).

(6.9)

Proof.

We have

P_{x_{i}}=P_{s}u_{x_{i}}+P_{t}\Delta u_{x_{i}}

(6.10)

and so,

\begin{gathered}\Delta u(\nabla P\cdot\nabla u)=P_{s}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u+P_{t}\Delta u\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{x_{i}}\Delta u_{x_{i}}\\
\Leftrightarrow-B^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u=\Delta u(\nabla P\cdot\nabla u)-A^{\prime}(\Delta u)\Delta u\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{x_{i}}\Delta u_{x_{i}}\end{gathered}

(6.11)

on the other hand we have

\begin{gathered}P_{x_{i}x_{i}}=P_{ss}u_{x_{i}}^{2}+2P_{st}u_{x_{i}}\Delta u_{x_{i}}+P_{tt}(\Delta u_{x_{i}})^{2}+P_{s}u_{x_{i}x_{i}}+P_{t}\Delta u_{x_{i}x_{i}}\\
\Rightarrow\Delta P=(-B^{\prime\prime}(u))|\nabla u|^{2}+A^{\prime\prime}(\Delta u)\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\Delta u_{x_{i}})^{2}-B^{\prime}(u)\Delta u+A^{\prime}(\Delta u)\Delta^{2}u\end{gathered}

(6.12)

and by (
6.11
) and the assumptions of
A
and
B
, (
6.12
) becomes

|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta P-\Delta u(\nabla P\cdot\nabla u)\geq a(\Delta u)[|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u)]-b(u)|\nabla u|^{4}=0

(6.13)

For the bound of the Laplacian, we have
P(u,0)=-B(u)\leq 0
and
\mu=|\nabla u|^{2}>0\;\>\forall\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
since
u_{x_{n}}>0
, so the assumption (i) in Theorem
6.1
is satisfied and we conclude.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution of

\begin{gathered}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}=F(u,|\nabla u|^{2},\Delta u)+\frac{u}{2}\Delta^{2}u\\
\textrm{where}\;\>F:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\textrm{is such that}\;\>F(s,t,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}w^{2}.\end{gathered}

(6.14)

Then
P=P(u,|\nabla u|^{2},\Delta u)=|\nabla u|^{2}-u\Delta u
is a
P-
function of (
6.16
).

In addition, if
u
is non negative, convex solution of (
6.16
) that satisfies assumption (
6.1
), then

|\nabla u|^{2}\leq u\Delta u\;\;\;,\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(6.15)

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle P_{x_{i}}=2\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{x_{j}}u_{x_{j}x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}}\Delta u-u\Delta u_{x_{i}}

and

\displaystyle\Delta P=2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2\nabla u\nabla\Delta u-(\Delta u)^{2}-2\nabla u\nabla\Delta u-u\Delta^{2}u

so by (
6.16
),

\displaystyle\Delta P=2F(u,|\nabla u|^{2},\Delta u)-(\Delta u)^{2}\geq 0

For the gradient bound we see that
P(u,0,\Delta u)=-u\Delta u\leq 0
since
u
is non negative and convex, so the assumption (i) of Theorem
6.1
is satisfied and we conclude.
∎

As a result, we have the following pointwise estimate

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
u:B_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth solution of

\begin{gathered}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}=F(u,|\nabla u|^{2},\Delta u)+\frac{u}{2}\Delta^{2}u\\
\textrm{where}\;\>F:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\textrm{is such that}\;\>F(s,t,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}w^{2}.\end{gathered}

(6.16)

Then

\begin{gathered}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}-u(x)\Delta u(x)\leq C(||u||_{H^{1}(B_{2})}+||\Delta u||_{L^{2}(B_{2})})\;,\\
\forall\>x\in B_{1}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>:\>|y|<1\}\;,\>\textrm{and}\;\>C\;\>\textrm{depends only on}\;\>n.\end{gathered}

(6.17)

Proof.

By Proposition
6.5
, we have that
P=|\nabla u|^{2}-u\Delta u=P(u;x)
is subharmonic. Therefore we have

P(u;x)\leq\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}P(u;y)dy\;\;,\;\forall\;B(x,r)\subset B_{2}

(6.18)

Also,
P\leq|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}+(\Delta u)^{2})
.

So,

\int_{B(x,r)}P(u;y)dy\leq||u||_{H^{1}(B_{2})}+||\Delta u||_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\;,\>\forall\;B(x,r)\subset B_{2}

(6.19)

Thus, for any
x\in B_{1}
(since
B(x,1)\subset B_{2}
), we have

P(u;x)\leq\frac{1}{|B_{1}|}(||u||_{H^{1}(B_{2})}+||\Delta u||_{L^{2}(B_{2})})

(6.20)

∎

A special case of Corollary
6.6
is the following estimate

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
u:B_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth solution of

\begin{gathered}det(\nabla^{2}u)=G(u,|\nabla u|^{2})-\frac{u}{4}\Delta^{2}u\\
\textrm{where}\;\>G:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\end{gathered}

(6.21)

Then

|\nabla u(x)|^{2}-u(x)\Delta u(x)\leq C(||u||_{H^{1}(B_{2})}+||\Delta u||_{L^{2}(B_{2})})

(6.22)

Proof.

We write
det(\nabla^{2}u)=\dfrac{1}{2}((\Delta u)^{2}-|\nabla^{2}u|^{2})
since
u
is defined a domain in the plane and then the proof is a consequence of Corollary
6.6
for
F=G(u,|\nabla u|^{2})+(\Delta u)^{2}\;\>,\;G\geq 0
.
∎

6.2.
A Liouville theorem and a De Giorgi-type property

A direct consequence of Theorem
6.2
is the following

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
u
be a convex entire subsolution of

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{3}u)\Delta u_{x_{i}x_{j}}-F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{3}u)=0

(6.23)

that satisfies assumption (
6.1
) with
m=4
and assume
a_{ij}
satisfy the ellipticity condition (
2.6
) and
F\geq 0
.

Then
u
is constant.

Proof.

Consider
P=P(u,\nabla u,\Delta u)=(\Delta u)^{2}
, so as in the proof of Proposition
6.3
we calculate

\begin{gathered}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}=2\Delta u_{x_{i}}\Delta u_{x_{j}}+2\Delta u\Delta u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\\
\Rightarrow\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}P_{x_{i}x_{j}}\geq 2\lambda|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+F\Delta u\geq 0\end{gathered}

(6.24)

and
P(u,\nabla u,0)=0
with
\mu=1
, so by Theorem
6.2
we obtain
\Delta u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u
is bounded by (
6.1
), so
u
is constant.
∎

Finally, we have a De Giorgi-type property

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth and bounded solution of

F(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{3}u,\nabla^{4}u)=0

(6.25)

such that
u_{y}>0
and assume
P=P(u,\Delta u)
is a
P-
function of (
6.25
), such that
P_{t}>0
.

If there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

P(u(x_{0}),\Delta u(x_{0}))=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P(u,\Delta u)<+\infty

(6.26)

then there exists a function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

u(x)=g(ax+by)\;\;\;,\;\>\textrm{for}\;\>a,b\in\mathbb{R}

(6.27)

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.5
we obtain that

P(u,\Delta u)\equiv c_{0}\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{where}\;\>c_{0}=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P(u,\Delta u)

(6.28)

since
P_{t}>0
we have

\Delta u=f(u)\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{for some}\;\>f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(6.29)

and
u
is bounded entire solution of (
6.29
) such that
u_{y}>0
.

Therefore, by Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
, we conclude that

u(x)=g(ax+by)\;\;\;,\;\>\textrm{for some}\;\>g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(6.30)

∎

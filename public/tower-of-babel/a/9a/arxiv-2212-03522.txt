Lie algebras with Frobenius dihedral groups of automorphisms
N. Yu. Makarenko

Abstract
Suppose that a Lie algebra L L admits a finite Frobenius group of automorphisms F ​ H FH with cyclic kernel F F and complement H H of order 2, such that the fixed-point subalgebra of F F is trivial and the fixed-point subalgebra of H H is metabelian. Then the derived length of L L is bounded by a constant.

Introduction

Let
L
be a Lie algebra,
G
its group of automorphisms. We
denote the fixed-point subalgebra of
G
in
L
by

C_{L}(G)=\{l\in L\,\,\,\mid\,\,\,l^{\varphi}=l\text{ for all }\varphi\in G\}.

Suppose
that
L
admits a Frobenius group of automorphisms
FH
with
kernel
F
and complement
H
of order
q
such that the
fixed-point subalgebra of
F
is trivial, i.e.
C_{L}(F)=0
. In

[
1
]
it was proved, that if the fixed-point
subalgebra
C_{L}(H)
of
H
is nilpotent of class
c
, then
L
is
nilpotent of class bounded by a function depending only on
q
and

c
.

In this paper we make the first attempt to extend this
result to the case of solvability of
C_{L}(H)
(instead of
nilpotency). We consider a Frobenius dihedral group acting on a
Lie algebra in such a way that
C_{L}(F)=0
and
C_{L}(H)
is
metabelian, and prove the solvability of bounded length of the
whole algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that a Lie algebra
L
of arbitrary dimension admits a finite Frobenius group of automorphisms
FH
with
cyclic kernel
F
and complement
H
of order 2, such that the
fixed-point subalgebra of
F
is trivial and the fixed-point
subalgebra of
H
is metabelian. Then the derived length of
L

is bounded by a constant.

The proof is based on combinatorial calculus in a
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
Lie algebra for
n
being the order of the Frobenius kernel
F
.
Such a
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded algebra arises naturally, as we extend the
ground field by a primitive
n
th root
\omega
of 1 and consider
the eigenspaces
L_{i}=\{x\in L\mid x^{\varphi}=\omega^{i}x\}
for
the eigenvalues
\omega^{i}
of the automorphism
\varphi
, a
generator of
F
. These subspaces satisfy the properties:

[L_{i},L_{j}]\subseteq L_{i+j\,(\rm{mod}\,n)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}L_{i},

and thus form a
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
-grading.
It is clear that
L_{0}=C_{L}(F)
. The assumptions of Theorem

1.1
can be translated into the language of graded algebras
by introducing two notions: so called “(-1)-independent
sequence” and the “selective metabelian condition”. We call a
sequence
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})
,
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(-1)
-independent
, if for any
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})
,

t_{i}\in\{0,1\}
,

t_{1}a_{1}+t_{2}a_{2}+t_{3}a_{3}+t_{4}a_{4}=0\,\,\,\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})=(0,0,0,0).

We say that a
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded Lie algebra

L
satisfies the
selective metabelian condition
if

\big[[L_{d_{1}},L_{d_{2}}],[L_{d_{3}},L_{d_{4}}]\big]=0\quad\text{
whenever }(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})\text{ is $(-1)$-independent}.

To demonstrate Theorem
1.1
it suffices to prove the
solvability of bounded length of a
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded Lie algebra
with
L_{0}=0
satisfying selective metabelian condition (see
section
3
).

Preliminaries

If
M,\,N
are subspaces of

L
, then
[M,N]

denotes the subspace, generated by all the products
[m,n]
for

m\in M
,
n\in N

are ideals, then
[M,N]
is
also an ideal; if

is a (sub)algebra, then
[H,H]
is an ideal
of
H
and, in particular, its subalgebra.

A simple product
[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{s}]
is by
definition the left-normalized product

[...[[a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3}],\ldots,a_{s}].

Note that any (complex) product in elements in
L
can be expressed as a linear combination of simple products of
the same length in the same elements. Also, it follows that the
ideal in
L
generated by a subspace
S
is the subspace
generated by all the simple products
[y_{j},x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots x_{i_{t}}]
, where
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{i_{k}}\in L,y_{j}\in S
. In particular, if
L
is generated by a subspace
M
,
then its space is spanned by simple products in elements of
M
.

The derived series of an algebra
L
is defined as

L^{(0)}=L,\;\;\;\,\,\,\,\,\,L^{(i+1)}=[L^{(i)},L^{(i)}].

Then
L
is solvable of derived
length at most
n
if
L^{(n)}=0
. An algebra of derived length 2
is called
metabelian
.

A Lie algebra
L
over a field
is

({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded
if

L=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}L_{i}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad[L_{i},L_{j}]\subseteq L_{i+j\,({\rm mod}\,n)},

where
L_{i}
are subspaces of
L
. Elements of
L_{i}
are referred
to as
homogeneous
and the subspaces
L_{i}
are called

homogeneous components
or
grading components
.
In particular,
L_{0}
is called the zero homogeneous component or
simply zero component.

An additive subgroup
H
of
L
is called
homogeneous
if

H=\bigoplus_{i}(H\cap L_{i})
and we set
H_{i}=H\cap L_{i}
. Obviously,
any subalgebra or an ideal generated by homogeneous subspaces is
homogeneous. A homogeneous subalgebra can be regarded as a

({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded algebra with the induced grading.
It follows that the terms of the derived series of
L
, the ideals

L^{(k)}
are also
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded algebras with
induced grading

L^{(k)}_{i}=L^{(k)}\cap L_{i}.

Index Convention
.

Henceforth a small Latin letter with an index
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
will denote a homogeneous element in the grading component

L_{i}
, with the index only indicating which component this element
belongs to:
x_{i}\in L_{i}
. We will not be using numbering indices
for elements of the
L_{i}
, so that different elements can be
denoted by the same symbol when it only matters which component
the elements belong to. For example,

and
x_{i}
can be
different elements of
L_{i}
, so that
[x_{i},\,x_{i}]
can be a
non-zero element of

.

.

Assume that

that in (

•

or

, but both

We
choose such an element with

as small as possible and use

represented
as a linear combination of
the products of the form (

\big[U,m_{j_{1}},\dots,m_{j_{k}},x_{i_{k+1}},\ldots x_{i_{t}}\big],

(40)

If

If

Suppose

Let

.

,

[L,L]

[U,x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}]

By Lemma
4.4
each of such product can be represented as a

Corollary 4.6

Suppose that a

,

Lemma 4.7

Suppose that a homogeneous ideal

has at most

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose that a
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-graded Lie algebra
L
with

,
n

odd, satisfies the selective metabelian condition (

).
Then

is solvable of derived length bounded by a constant.

Note that

is solvable of

Proof.

Let

be the ideal of
M=[L,L]
generated by all products

\big[[M_{i_{1}},M_{i_{2}}],[M_{i_{3}},M_{i_{4}}]\big]
, where

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
ranges through all
(-1)
-independent sequences of

and
i_{4}
ranges through all elements of

. The induced
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-grading of

condition (under the Index Condition):

\big[[x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}}],[x_{i_{3}},x_{j}]\big]=0

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
is
(-1)
-independent sequence. Indeed,
if
j\notin D(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
, then
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},j)
is

(-1)
-independent sequence and the product is trivial by

); otherwise it

J
. By Lemma
4.3

.

Consider now an arbitrary
(-1)
-independent sequence

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
and
i_{4}\in D(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
. The ideal
T
in

[L,L]
generated by
\big[[M_{i_{1}},M_{i_{2}}],[M_{i_{3}},M_{i_{4}}]\big]
has at most

(where, recall,

,

) non-trivial grading

. Therefore, by Lemma

,

components that do not centralize

.
Since

is also a homogeneous ideal, it follows that the

has at most

non-trivial components.
Since the induced
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
-grading of

trivial zero component, by Shalev’s generalization

]

is solvable of

. Therefore,

,

Since

does not depend on the choice of the

-independent seuence
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})

follows that

.
Hence,

∎

As shown in section

of a

-graded Lie algebra with trivial zero-component

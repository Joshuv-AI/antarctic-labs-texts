Galerkin-Bernstein Approximations of the System of Time Dependent Nonlinear Parabolic PDEs
Hazrat Ali, Nilormy Gupta Trisha, Md. Shafiqul Islam*

Abstract




Abstract

The purpose of the research is to find the numerical solutions to the system of time dependent nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) utilizing the Modified Galerkin Weighted Residual Method (MGWRM) with the help of modified Bernstein polynomials. An approximate solution of the system has been assumed in accordance with the modified Bernstein polynomials. Thereafter, the modified Galerkin method has been applied to the system of nonlinear parabolic PDEs and has transformed the model into a time dependent ordinary differential equations system. Then the system has been converted into the recurrence equations by employing backward difference approximation. However, the iterative calculation is performed by using the Picard Iterative method. A few renowned problems are then solved to test the applicability and efficiency of our proposed scheme. The numerical solutions at different time levels are then displayed numerically in tabular form and graphically by figures. The comparative study is presented along with
\displaystyle L_{2}
norm, and
\displaystyle L_{\infty}
norm.

Keywords:
Parabolic PDE System, Modified Galerkin Method, Modified Bernstein Polynomial, Backward Difference Method, Gray-Scott Model

Mathematical Formulation

Let us commence with the following system over the domain
[-L,L]

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\frac{\partial M}{\partial t}=\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial x^{2}}-f(M,N)+p(1-M)\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial N}{\partial t}=\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}N}{\partial x^{2}}+f(M,N)-(p+q)N\end{array}\right\}

The boundary and initial conditions are as follows:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle M(-L,t)=M(L,t)=\theta_{0}\\
\displaystyle N(-L,t)=N(L,t)=\gamma_{0}\end{array}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle M(x,0)=M_{0}(x)\\
\displaystyle N(x,0)=N_{0}(x)\end{array}\right\}

Let us assume the approximate solutions of System (
2
) be of the form

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\widetilde{M}(x,t)=\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}(t)B_{j}(x)\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{N}(x,t)=\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}(t)B_{j}(x)\\
\end{array}\right\}

where
\displaystyle B_{j}
’s are the modified Bernstein polynomials and
\displaystyle c_{j}
and
\displaystyle d_{j}
are the coefficients dependent on time. The first terms of the approximate solutions (
2
) have come from the boundary conditions of the system. The modified Bernstein polynomials are defined as follows:

\displaystyle B_{n,m}(x)=\binom{m}{n}\frac{(x-L)^{n}(U-x)^{m-n}(x-L)(U-x)}{(U-L)^{m}}\hskip 28.45274ptn=0,1,2,...,m

where
U
&
L
are the upper and lower limits of
x
.
The last terms of Solution (
2
) will vanish at the boundary points.
Therefore, the residual functions are

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle R_{1}(x,t)=\frac{\partial\widetilde{M}}{\partial t}-\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}}{\partial x^{2}}+f(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})-p(1-\widetilde{M})\\
\displaystyle R_{2}(x,t)=\frac{\partial\widetilde{N}}{\partial t}-\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{N}}{\partial x^{2}}-f(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})+(p+q)\widetilde{N}\\
\end{array}\right\}

Now we form the residual equations as:

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}R_{1}(x,t)B_{i}(x)dx=0

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}R_{2}(x,t)B_{i}(x)dx=0

(2.12)

From the first residual equation, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\Bigg[\frac{\partial\widetilde{M}}{\partial t}-\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}}{\partial x^{2}}+f(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})-p(1-\widetilde{M})\Bigg]B_{i}(x)dx=0

(2.13)

Now we apply integration by parts in the above equation

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial\widetilde{M}}{\partial t}B_{i}dx+\int_{{}_{L}}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial\widetilde{M}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}f(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})B_{i}dx-\int_{-L}^{L}p(1-\widetilde{M})B_{i}dx=\varepsilon_{1}\Big[\frac{\partial\widetilde{M}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}

(2.14)

Then we substitute solution (
2
) in Equation (
2.14
). Therefore, the equation becomes,

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j}\Big)\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}f\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j},\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{-L}^{L}p\Big(1-\big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j}\big)\Big)B_{i}dx=\varepsilon_{1}\Big[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}

\displaystyle\text{or,}\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial t}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial c_{j}}{\partial t}B_{j}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{-L}^{L}f\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j},\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx-\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}p\theta_{0}B_{i}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{j}B_{i}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt=\varepsilon_{1}\Bigg[\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}+\varepsilon_{1}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}

This finally becomes

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial t}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial c_{j}}{\partial t}B_{j}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{-L}^{L}\Gamma\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{i}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}\Omega\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{j}B_{i}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}p\theta_{0}B_{i}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{j}B_{i}dx=\varepsilon_{1}\Bigg[\frac{\partial\theta_{0}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}+\varepsilon_{1}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}

(2.15)

The first terms on both sides, and third terms on the left-hand side Equation (
2.15
) become zero because of boundary conditions. Therefore, the equation reduces to,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{dc_{j}}{dt}\int_{-L}^{L}B_{j}B_{i}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\Bigg(\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}\frac{dB_{i}}{dx}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{j}B_{i}dx-\varepsilon_{1}\Big[\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}\Omega\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{j}B_{i}dx=-\int_{-L}^{L}\Gamma\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{i}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt+\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{i}dx-\int_{-L}^{L}p\theta_{0}B_{i}dx

The derivative and non-derivative terms of Equation (

) can be summarized via standard matrix notation as follows:

\displaystyle[C_{1}]\Big\{\frac{dc_{j}}{dt}\Big\}+[K_{1}]\{c_{j}\}+[K_{2}]\{d_{j}\}=[F_{1}]

where

\displaystyle C_{1_{ij}}=\int_{-L}^{L}B_{j}B_{i}dx

\displaystyle K_{1_{ij}}=\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{1}\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}\frac{dB_{i}}{dx}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{j}B_{i}dx-\varepsilon_{1}\Big[\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}

\displaystyle K_{2_{ij}}=\int_{-L}^{L}\Omega\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{j}B_{i}dx

\displaystyle F_{1_{i}}=-\int_{-L}^{L}\Gamma\Big(\theta_{0},\gamma_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}B_{k},\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l}B_{l}\Big)B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}pB_{i}dx-\int_{-L}^{L}p\theta_{0}B_{i}dx

Here,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are
n\times n
matrices,
C_{1}
is
n\times n
matrix, and
F_{1}
is
n\times 1
matrix. The first two matrices
K_{1}
and

C_{1}
and

Therefore, we apply the backward difference method on the first term of Equation (
2.17
) and rearrange the resulting terms as follows:

\displaystyle[C_{1}]\Big\{\frac{c_{j}-c_{j-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\}+[K_{1}]\{c_{j}\}+[K_{2}]\{d_{j}\}=[F_{1}]

\displaystyle Or,\Big(\frac{1}{\Delta t}[C_{1}]+[K_{1}]\Big)\{c_{j}\}+[K_{2}]\{d_{j}\}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}[C_{1}]\{c_{j-1}\}+[F_{1}]

(2.18)

The second residual equation can be written as,

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\Bigg[\frac{\partial\widetilde{N}}{\partial t}-\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{N}}{\partial x^{2}}-f(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})+(p+q)\widetilde{N}\Bigg]B_{i}(x)dx=0

After employing integration by parts and then substitution of (
2
) reduces the above equation,

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}(p+q)\Big(\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\int_{-L}^{L}f\Big(\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}B_{j},\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}dx=\varepsilon_{2}\Big[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}B_{j}\Big)B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{\partial\gamma_{0}}{\partial t}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial d_{j}}{\partial t}B_{j}B_{i}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial\gamma_{0}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial B_{i}}{\partial x}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=-\int_{-L}^{L}(p+q)\gamma_{0}B_{i}dx+\varepsilon_{2}\Bigg[\frac{\partial\gamma_{0}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}+\varepsilon_{2}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\frac{\partial B_{j}}{\partial x}B_{i}\Bigg]_{-L}^{L}

Since the first, and third terms on the left-hand side and the first term on the right-hand side of Equation (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{dd_{j}}{dt}\int_{-L}^{L}B_{j}B_{i}dx+\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\Bigg(\int_{-L}^{L}\varepsilon_{2}\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}\frac{dB_{i}}{dx}dx+\int_{-L}^{L}(p+q)B_{j}B_{i}dx-\varepsilon_{2}\Big[\frac{dB_{j}}{dx}B_{i}\Big]_{-L}^{L}\Bigg)

Conclusion

This research study has provided numerical approximations of nonlinear reaction-diffusion systems with specified boundary and initial conditions through the employment of the modified Galerkin method. To generate the trial solution, modified Bernstein Polynomials have been used. The simplification of the weighted residual leads to a system of ordinary differential equations which is then transformed into the recurrent relation by applying the backward difference formula. At this stage, we have used Picard’s iterative procedure to approximate the trial solution. After successful derivation, we applied our proposed method to several models in order to test their applicability and effectiveness. We have solved and displayed the results both numerically and graphically. From those figures and numerical results, it is indisputable that our proposed method is an unconditionally stable, efficient, highly modular, and easily expandable method that can be applied to any type of system of nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations regardless of the type of the boundary conditions, type of non-linearity of the functions, coefficients are constants or function of independent variables.

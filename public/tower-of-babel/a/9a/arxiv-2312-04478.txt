On L p L^{p} -semigroup to Stokes equation with dynamic slip boundary condition in the half-space Thanks: The authors thank the Czech Science Foundation, Grant Number 20-11027X, for its support. Thanks: * Corresponding author.
Dalibor Pražák, Michael Zelina ∗

Abstract
. We consider evolutionary Stokes system, coupled with the so-called dynamic slip boundary condition, in the simple geometry of a d d -dimensional half-space. Using the Fourier transform, we obtain an explicit formula for the resolvent. Optimal regularity estimates and existence of analytic semigroup in the L p L^{p} -setting are then deduced using the methods of ℋ ∞ \mathcal{H}^{\infty} -calculus.

1.
Introduction

This paper is motivated by the system

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}-\operatorname{div}2\nu{\rm D}\bm{u}+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=\bm{f},\qquad\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=0\quad\textrm{ in $(0,T)\times\Omega$},

\displaystyle\beta\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\alpha\bm{u}+2\nu[{({\rm D}\bm{u})}\bm{n}]_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\beta\bm{h},\qquad\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0\quad\textrm{ on $(0,T)\times\Gamma$},

where
\Omega=\{x_{d}>0\}
is the
d
-dimensional
half-space, and
\Gamma=\{x_{d}=0\}
the corresponding
boundary. Note that (
1
) consists of two evolution
equations, coupled via the trace operator. The problem can be
efficiently treated in the Hilbert
L^{2}
-setting, since there
is an underlying symmetric operator
A:V\to V^{*}
, where
V
is
a suitable subspace to
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, see e.g.

[
1
]
, cf. also the weak formulation (
6
)
below. However, the
L^{p}
-theory is expectedly more difficult
if
p\neq 2
and up to our knowledge, the problem (
1
)
has not been treated in the literature (although a number of
closely related results are available, see also the discussion
below). In the present paper, we intend to contribute to this,
with two main results being: the existence of an analytic
semigroup in
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Gamma)
, and the optimal

W^{2,p}
and
W^{1,p}
-regularity estimates for the
corresponding stationary (resolvent) problem.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we
introduce function spaces and state two main results:
Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, providing
resolvent estimates of optimal regularity both for weak
and strong solutions. In Corollary
2.1
, we show the
existence of an analytic semigroup; we also identify the domain
of its generator. Main technical results of the paper
follow: in Section
3
, we use (partial) Fourier
transform, in the spirit of
[
9
]
, to express the
resolvent in terms of the boundary right-hand side. Key
estimates in
L^{p}
spaces are then obtained in
Section
4
. Our approach remains more elementary in that we
only rely on the classical Mikhlin multiplier theorem. One can however
note that Lemma
4.1
in fact asserts the existence
of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus for the operator
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
.
Finally, proofs of the main theorems are
completed in Section
5
, essentially by combining
results of Section
4
with the well-known
L^{p}
-theory
for the (homogeneous) Dirichlet problem, as given e.g.
in
[
10
]
.

Although our results are formulated for the case of half-space

\Omega=\{x_{d}>0\}
only, extension to more general domains is
straighforward, using standard localization and perturbation
arguments. Crucially, this requires extension of the estimates
to the case of functions
\bm{u}
with non-zero divergence. We
treat this situation explicitly in part (iii) of
Theorem
2.2
.

Let us conclude the introduction by discussing some related,
both classical and more recent, results. Concerning the
Stokes system subject to a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition,
the existence of
L^{p}
-analytic semigroup was proven in
[
12
]

via the theory of pseudo-differential operators. For a more elementary
approach based on the Fourier transform and classical multiplier
theorems, see
[
9
]
. Recently, a different method using
the maximal operator was proposed in
[
18
]
; however, the
method seems to be limited to bounded domains.

There is also an extensive literature devoted to problems related
to (
1
), but crucially, without the time derivative in the
boundary equation. For example, a fairly complete
L^{p}
-theory,
including the existence of an analytic semigroup, has been
established in
[
2
]
, see also
[
11
]
and the
references therein; yet the results again seem to be limited
to bounded
\Omega
only. In the case of a half-space,
the problem was treated in
[
17
]
, albeit for
\bm{h}=0
only.
A more modern approach based on the
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus was employed here.
More recently, a closely related class of problems with dynamic boundary
conditions was treated in
[
6
]
, and maximal regularity results
were obtained.

A rather comprehensive functional setup for treating resolvent problems
related to dynamic boundary conditions is described in
[
8
]
;
see also the references therein. This method does provide sharp
regularity estimates in fractional Sobolev spaces, yet direct application
to Stokes problem – due to the incompressibility condition and the
pressure term – does not seem viable.

Let us also mention recent paper
[
7
]
, where the existence
of an analytic semigroup for (
1
) is proven in case of smooth
bounded domains, in the Hilbert setting of the maximal regularity
space
L^{2}(\Omega)\times W^{1/2,2}(\Gamma)
.

Concerning possible applications, we remark that optimal regularity
estimates of the stationary Stokes problem (cf. Theorem
2.2
)
can be used to obtain higher regularity of evolutionary nonlinear problems,
via the standard bootstrap argument. See
[
16
]
for
the corresponding analysis in bounded domains, where the smoothness
of solutions was used to estimate dimension of the global attractor,
in the case of the 2D Navier-Stokes equations with dynamic boundary
conditions.

2.
Notation and main results

We set
\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R};x_{d}>0\}
and identify
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
with
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Furthermore,
\bm{n}=(0,-1)
is the outer normal, and

\tau:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
denotes the tangential component.
For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
k\geq 1
integer, we denote
by
L^{p}
and
W^{k,p}
the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev
spaces. Negative Sobolev space
W^{-1,p}
is defined as the completion
of
L^{q}
with respect to the norm

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}=\sup\Big\{\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x;\ \lVert\bm{\varphi}\rVert_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq 1\Big\}.

It it well-known (see e.g.
[
3
, Section 3.14]
) that
W^{-1,p}

can be identified with the dual to
W^{1,p^{\prime}}
.

We also need Besov spaces
B^{s,p}_{p}
with a general
real
s
; we allow for non-integer
s
in the spirit of
[
4
]
.
The subscripts
\sigma
or
n
stand for the solenoidality, and
zero normal component, respectively. Finally, we will extensively use
the partial Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{x^{\prime}\to\xi}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x^{\prime}\to\xi}\bm{u}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi,x_{d})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\bm{u}(x^{\prime},x_{d})e^{-ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Since it will not play any role in our paper, we set
\beta=\nu=1
in (
1
). We will be concerned with the resolvent problem related to it, i.e.

(2)

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{u}+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=\bm{f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

(5)

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha)\bm{u}+2[({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}]_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\bm{h}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

where
\lambda
is a complex number. Note that
\operatorname{div}2{\rm D}\bm{u}=\Delta\bm{u}
.
Multiplying (
2
) by

\bm{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\overline{\Omega})
and integrating
by parts, one obtains the integral formulation

(6)

\begin{gathered}\lambda\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x+(\lambda+\alpha)\int_{\Gamma}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}S+\int_{\Omega}2{\rm D}\bm{u}:{\rm D}\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x\\
=\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\bm{h}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}S.\end{gathered}

This problem is easy to handle in the
L^{2}
-setting, using Korn’s inequality, see
[
2
]
or
[
1
]
. However, our objective is to obtain
the
L^{p}
-theory, as stated in the following two main theorems.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
,
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
\bm{f}\in L^{p}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega)
and
\bm{h}\in L^{p}_{n}(\Gamma)
.
Then for any complex
\lambda
with
\mathfrak{Re}\,\lambda>0
there exists
unique
\bm{u}\in W^{1,p}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega)

a weak solution to (
2
)–(
5
).

If moreover
|\lambda|>\omega
for some
\omega>0
fixed, then

(7)

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|\lambda|}\big(\lVert\bm{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big),

where the constant
C_{0}
depends on
d
,
p
and
\omega
,
but is independent of
\lambda
and
\alpha
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
,
p\in(1,+\infty)

and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathfrak{Re}\,\lambda>0
.

(i)

Let
\bm{f}\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
, and let
\bm{h}\in B^{-1/p,p}_{p;n}(\Gamma)
. Then one has the estimate

(8)

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\big(\lVert\bm{f}\rVert_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{B^{-1/p,p}_{p}(\Gamma)}\big).

(ii)

Let
\bm{f}\in L^{p}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega)
and

\bm{h}\in B^{1-1/p,p}_{p;n}(\Gamma)
. Then the solution to resolvent
problem (
2
)–(
5
) is strong, and one has the estimate

(9)

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla\pi\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\big(\lVert\bm{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{B^{1-1/p,p}_{p}(\Gamma)}\big).

(iii)

If (
3
) is generalized to
\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=g
,
the estimates remain valid, with the norm of
g
in
L^{p}(\Omega)

or
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
added to the right-hand side of (
8
)
or (
9
), respectively.

The constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
depend on
d
,
p
and
|\lambda|
.

Postponing proofs of these theorems to Section
5
,
we can now establish the existence of analytic semigroup.

Corollary 2.1
.

System (
1
) generates an analytic semigroup
in
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Gamma)
, where the domain of
generator is contained in
B^{1+1/p-\varepsilon,p}_{p}(\Omega)
,

\varepsilon>0
arbitrary.

Proof.

Set
X=L^{p}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega)\times L^{p}_{n}(\Gamma)
,
and define
A\bm{u}=(\bm{f},\bm{h})
by the (weak) formulation
of the resolvent, i.e. (
6
). We set

\mathcal{D}(A)=\{\bm{u}\in W^{1,p}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega);\ A\bm{u}\in X\}

– with a slight abuse of notation, we identify the Sobolev
function
\bm{u}
with the couple
(\bm{u},\operatorname{tr}\bm{u})
.
It is easy to verify that
A
is densely defined, and
the closedness follows from the resolvent estimate (
7
);
see also (
43
) below.
By a standard result (see e.g.
[
5
, Corollary 3.7.14]
),
the existence of semigroup follows.

Let us identify the domain of
A
. In view of uniqueness
(cf. the argument after (
43
) below), we can treat
the regularity w.r. to
\bm{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and

\bm{h}\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
separately. If
\bm{h}=0
, if follows from
(
9
) that
\bm{u}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
. However,
in contrast to the Dirichlet problem, this cannot be the domain since
(
5
) would then imply
\bm{h}\in B^{1-1/p,p}_{p}(\Gamma)
.

In fact, we claim that for any
\varepsilon>0

(10)

B^{1+1/p+\varepsilon,p}_{p;\sigma,n}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)\subset B^{1+1/p-\varepsilon,p}_{p;\sigma,n}(\Omega).

Indeed, interpolating (
9
–
8
) with

\bm{f}=0
, we get the second inclusion. On the other hand,
if
\bm{u}\in B^{1+1/p+\varepsilon,p}_{p}(\Omega)
, then
the left-hand side of (
5
) belongs to

B^{\varepsilon,p}_{p}(\Gamma)
, in contradiction to
the fact that in general,
\bm{h}\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
only.

Note that if
p=2
, one has the equality

\mathcal{D}(A)=W^{3/2,2}_{\sigma,n}(\Omega)
,
as follows from
4.5
below.
∎

3.
Fundamental solution

Here, we deal with (
2
)–(
5
) for
\bm{f}=0
, i.e. we consider the system

(11)

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{u}+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(13)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

(14)

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha)\bm{u}+2[({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}]_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

We will (formally) solve the problem, using Fourier analysis.

3.1.
Reducing the equations

By
\operatorname{div}^{\prime}
,
\Delta^{\prime}
we denote the corresponding operator with respect to
x^{\prime}
. Let us now simplify (
13
)–(
14
). First,
\bm{n}=(0,-1)
and thus

(15)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0\Leftrightarrow u_{d}=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Next,

\displaystyle 2({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}

\displaystyle=-\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{d}u_{1}+\partial_{1}u_{d}\\
\partial_{d}u_{2}+\partial_{2}u_{d}\\
\vdots\\
\partial_{d}u_{d-1}+\partial_{d-1}u_{d}\\
2\partial_{d}u_{d}\end{pmatrix}\overset{\eqref{eq:LastComp}}{=}-\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{d}\bm{u}^{\prime}\\
2\partial_{d}u_{d}\end{pmatrix}

and

2[({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}]_{\tau}=2({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}-2(({\rm D}\bm{u})\bm{n}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{n}=-\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{d}\bm{u}^{\prime}\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, we can rewrite (
14
) as follows

(16)

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha)\bm{u}^{\prime}-\partial_{d}\bm{u}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Now, we aplly
\partial_{d}\operatorname{div}^{\prime}
to the first
(d-1)
equations of (
11
) and
-\Delta^{\prime}
to the last one; adding these equations together we obtain

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{d}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}+\cdots+\partial_{d-1}u_{d-1})-\Delta^{\prime}u_{d}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left[\partial_{d}(\partial_{1}^{2}\pi+\partial_{2}^{2}\pi+\cdots+\partial_{d-1}^{2}\pi)-\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{d}\pi\right]=0.

The pressure term clearly vanishes and in the first bracket we use
\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=0\Leftrightarrow\\
diver^{\prime}\bm{u}^{\prime}=-\partial_{d}u_{d}
to get

\displaystyle-(\lambda-\Delta)\Delta u_{d}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u_{d}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Finally, we deal with the boundary system. We apply
-\operatorname{div}^{\prime}
to (
16
) to get

\displaystyle-(\lambda+\alpha)\,\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{u}^{\prime}+\partial_{d}\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{u}^{\prime}=-\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{\phi},

and thanks to
\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{u}^{\prime}=-\partial_{d}u_{d}
we achieve

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha-\partial_{d})\partial_{d}u_{d}=-\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{\phi}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Therefore, we reduced the whole problem to the question of solving the following system for
u_{d}

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-(\lambda-\Delta)\Delta u_{d}&=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\
(\lambda+\alpha-\partial_{d})\partial_{d}u_{d}&=-\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{\phi}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,\\
u_{d}&=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.\end{split}

3.2.
Fourier transform

We solve (
17
) by introducing the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{x^{\prime}\to\xi}
. We need to deal with the system

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})(|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})\widehat{u_{d}}&=0\quad\text{ for }x_{d}>0,\\
(\lambda+\alpha-\partial_{d})\partial_{d}\widehat{u_{d}}&=-i\xi\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}\quad\text{ for }x_{d}=0,\\
\widehat{u_{d}}(\xi,0)&=0,\end{split}

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. We see that characteristic roots are

\pm\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\pm|\xi|\,;

certainly, only the terms with the minus sign are relevant.
It will be useful to introduce the function

(19)

\displaystyle m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})=\frac{e^{-x_{d}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}-e^{-x_{d}|\xi|}}{\lambda+(\lambda+\alpha)(\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}-|\xi|)}\,,

which is nothing else than the fundamental solution to (
18
), i.e.

\displaystyle(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})(|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})m_{0}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ for }x_{d}>0,

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha-\partial_{d})\partial_{d}m_{0}

\displaystyle=-1\quad\text{ for }x_{d}=0,

\displaystyle m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,0)

\displaystyle=0.

The solution for the last component is thus

\displaystyle\widehat{u_{d}}(\xi,x_{d})=i\xi\cdot m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi).

Now, we need to express
\widehat{\pi}
and
\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}
using
m_{0}
. We start with the pressure. Let us apply
\operatorname{div}^{\prime}
to the first
(d-1)
equations of (
11
); we get

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}\pi=-\operatorname{div}^{\prime}(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{u}^{\prime}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Because of
\operatorname{div}^{\prime}\bm{u}^{\prime}=-\partial_{d}u_{d}
we rewrite it in the form

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}\pi=(\lambda-\Delta)\partial_{d}u_{d}\quad\text{ in }\Omega

and use the Fourier transform to achieve

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}=-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})\partial_{d}\widehat{u_{d}}.

In other words

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}(\xi,x_{d})

\displaystyle=-(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})\partial_{d}m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda e^{-x_{d}|\xi|}}{\lambda+(\lambda+\alpha)(\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}-|\xi|)}\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi).

Up to now, everything was dimension-independent. At this point,
we need to express the function
\bm{u}^{\prime}
. If
d=2
, then everything is simpler. From (
12
) we easily find that

\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi,x_{d})=-\partial_{2}m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{2})\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi).

In the general case
d>2
we start in the same way, i.e. we take the Fourier transform of (
12
) and express our unknown vector field

i\xi\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}+\partial_{d}\widehat{u_{d}}=0\Leftrightarrow\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}=\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\partial_{d}\widehat{u_{d}},

which yields

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}(\xi,x_{d})=-\partial_{d}m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)\,.

This yields the solution to the Stokes system, but our condition (
14
) is not satisfied (in general). We need to add some correcting term
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}^{\prime},0)
. To this purpose, consider the following problem

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{v}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\bm{v}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha)\bm{v}^{\prime}-\partial_{d}\bm{v}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{h}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

where

\widehat{\bm{h}}=\widehat{\bm{\phi}}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}.

Now, by the Fourier transform we get

\displaystyle(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-\partial_{d}^{2})\widehat{\bm{v}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha-\partial_{d})\widehat{\bm{v}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\bm{h}}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Characteristic roots are now just
\pm\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}
, and thus

\widehat{\bm{v}^{\prime}}(\xi,x_{d})=\bm{b}(\xi)e^{-x_{d}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\,.

From the boundary condition we find

\bm{b}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\lambda+\alpha+\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{\bm{h}}(\xi).

Together,

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}(\xi,x_{d})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{d}m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda+\alpha+\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\left(\text{Id}_{d-1}\,-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)e^{-x_{d}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)\,.

To conclude this section, we summarize that for
d\geq 2
,
the solution to (
11
-
14
)
can be expressed as

(20)

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}(\xi,x_{d})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{d}m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda+\alpha+\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\left(\text{Id}_{d-1}\,-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)e^{-x_{d}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{u_{d}}(\xi,x_{d})

\displaystyle=i\xi\cdot m_{0}(\lambda,\xi,x_{d})\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}(\xi,x_{d})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda e^{-x_{d}|\xi|}}{\lambda+(\lambda+\alpha)(\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}}-|\xi|)}\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

where
m_{0}
was defined in (
19
).
In the case of
d=2
the second part of the first right-hand side vanishes.

4.
Multipliers and
L^{p}
-bounds

The aim of this section is to establish
L^{p}
-estimates of the solution
obtained above. To begin with, we note that
m_{0}
only depends on
z=|\xi|
.
Setting also for simplicity
y=x_{d}
, with a slight abuse of notation we
can write

(21)

\displaystyle m_{0}=m_{0}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(\lambda,z)+z}{\alpha+\lambda+\omega(\lambda,z)+z}\cdot\frac{e^{-y\omega(\lambda,z)}-e^{-yz}}{\lambda},

where
\omega(\lambda,z)=\sqrt{\lambda+z^{2}}
. From relations (
20
) it follows that

(22)

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{u}^{\prime}}(\xi,y)

\displaystyle=m_{2}(\lambda,|\xi|,y)\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)+m_{3}(\lambda,|\xi|,y)\left(\text{Id}_{d-1}-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{u_{d}}(\xi,y)

\displaystyle=m_{1}(\lambda,|\xi|,y)\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}(\xi,y)

\displaystyle=m_{4}(\lambda,|\xi|,y)\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi),

where we have set

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{1}(\lambda,z,y)&=zm_{0}(\lambda,z,y),\\
m_{2}(\lambda,z,y)&=-\partial_{y}m_{0}(\lambda,z,y),\\
m_{3}(\lambda,z,y)&=\frac{1}{\alpha+\lambda+\omega(\lambda,z)}\,e^{-y\omega(\lambda,z)},\\
m_{4}(\lambda,z,y)&=-\frac{\omega(\lambda,z)+z}{\alpha+\lambda+\omega(\lambda,z)+z}\,e^{-yz}.\end{split}

As the mutlipliers
\xi/|\xi|
and
\xi\otimes\xi/|\xi|^{2}

are easily seen to satisfy the Mikhlin condition (
28
),
it is enough to treat just the terms
m_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,4
.
Denote

(24)

\Sigma_{\theta}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\};\ |\arg z|<\theta\},

Let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
be the set of all all bounded, analytic functions
in
\Sigma_{\theta}
. We will use repeatedly the following estimate,
which follows easily from the
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus for the operator

(-\Delta)^{1/2}
on
L^{p}
, see e.g.
[
15
]
. For reader’s
convenience, we provide a short proof based on the classical
Mikhlin’s theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi,y)=m(|\xi|,y)\widehat{\bm{\phi}}(\xi)
,
where
m(\cdot,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
for each
y\geq 0
fixed.
Set

(25)

k(y)=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}|m(z,y)|\,,\qquad y\geq 0\,.

Then one has the estimate

(26)

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{q,d}\,\big(k(0)+\lVert k\rVert_{L^{p}(0,\infty)}\big)\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}.

Furthermore, if under these assumptions
\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi,y)=m(|\xi|,y)\widehat{\bm{v}}(\xi,y)

for any
y>0
fixed, then

(27)

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{q,d}\lVert k\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
y\geq 0
be fixed. We want to verify the Mikhlin condition

(28)

|\xi|^{k}|\nabla^{k}m(|\xi|,y)|\leq C_{k}(y),\qquad\xi\neq 0,\ k\geq 0,

see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 5.2.7]
. It follows once we prove

|s^{k}m^{(k)}(s,y)|\leq C(y)\,,\qquad s\in(0,\infty).

Let
s\in(0,\infty)
be fixed, and let
\Gamma_{s}
be the largest circle
centered in
s
, and contained in
\Sigma_{\theta}
. By Cauchy’s
theorem

s^{k}m^{(k)}(s,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{s}}\frac{s^{k}m(z,y)}{(z-s)^{k+1}}\,{\rm d}z.

Now it is easy to see that the right-hand side has a bound
only depending on
\theta
and the supremum of
m(\cdot,y)
.

As the Mikhlin condition is satisfied, we see that

\lVert\bm{u}(\cdot,y)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\leq C_{q,d}\,k(y)\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}

holds. The conclusion is immediate by Fubini’s theorem.
∎

The following two lemmas are the main technical estimates
of the paper.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/4)
be fixed. Then there exist constants
C
,

c>0
and
\delta>0
such that

(29)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\,\omega(\lambda,z)

\displaystyle\geq c(z+\sqrt{|\lambda|}),

(30)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z\left(e^{-y\omega(\lambda,z)}-e^{-yz}\right)}{\omega(\lambda,z)-z}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-\delta y|z|}}{1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(\lambda,z)|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\alpha+|\lambda|}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(\lambda,z)|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\alpha+|z|^{2}}\,,

for all
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
,
y\geq 0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}
.

Proof.

For (
29
) see Lemma 5.2(b), (c) in
[
17
]
, (
30
) follows from Lemma 5.3(b) therein. To show (
31
), we proceed as follows.

Since
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}
and
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
for some
\theta<\pi/4
, we have
\lambda+z^{2}\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}
. Hence
\omega(z)\in\Sigma_{\pi/4}
and
z+\omega(z)\in\Sigma_{\pi/4}
. As
\frac{1}{\alpha+\lambda}\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}
, we get
\frac{z+\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda}\in\Sigma_{3\pi/4}
, which means that this value is strictly away from
-1
, in particular

\displaystyle\Big|1+\frac{z+\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda}\Big|\geq C

holds for some
C>0
. Finally,

\displaystyle\Big|\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)\Big|

\displaystyle\geq C|\alpha+\lambda|=C\sqrt{(\alpha+\mathfrak{Re}\,\lambda)^{2}+\mathfrak{Im}^{2}\,\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq C\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+|\lambda|^{2}}\geq C(\alpha+|\lambda|),

where we used that
\alpha
and
\mathfrak{Re}\,\lambda
are positive.

Finally, as
z-z^{2}+\lambda+\omega(z)\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}
and
\alpha+z^{2}\in\Sigma_{2\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|1+\frac{z-z^{2}+\lambda+\omega(z)}{\alpha+z^{2}}\Big|\geq C,

and the (
32
) follows as before.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/4)
be fixed. Then there exist constant
C>0

and
\delta>0
such that for all
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
,
y\geq 0

and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}

(33)

\displaystyle|m_{i}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-\delta y|z|}}{(1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|})(\alpha+|\lambda|)}\,,

(34)

\displaystyle|zm_{i}(\lambda,z,y)|+|\partial_{y}m_{i}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-\delta y|z|}}{(1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|})\sqrt{\alpha+|\lambda|}}\,,

for
i=1
,
2
,
3
and

(35)

|m_{4}(\lambda,z,y)|\leq Ce^{-\delta y|z|}\,.

Proof.

We will first show the bounds for
m_{i}
and
zm_{i}
, for all
i
. The bound for
\partial_{y}m_{i}
will be a simple consequence of these.

Ad
m_{1}
.
Using (
30
) and (
31
) we find

\displaystyle|m_{1}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle=|zm_{0}(\lambda,z,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|}\cdot\frac{e^{-\delta|z|y}}{1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\Big|\frac{(\omega(z)-z)(\omega(z)+z)}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\delta|z|y}}{1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|})(\alpha+|\lambda|)}e^{-\delta|z|y}.

Similarly, using (
31
) and (
32
) together, we get

\displaystyle|zm_{1}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\delta|z|y}}{1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\cdot\frac{|z|}{|\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)|}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\delta|z|y}}{1+y\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+|\lambda|}}\cdot\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{\alpha+|z|^{2}}}\,,

from which the estimate for
zm_{1}
follows.

Ad
m_{3}
.
Due to (
29
) we see that

\displaystyle|e^{-y\omega(z)}|\leq e^{-cy(|z|+\sqrt{|\lambda|})}=e^{-cy\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\cdot e^{-cy|z|}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|}}e^{-\delta y|z|}\,,

where
(1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|})e^{-cy\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{e}=C^{\prime}
was used. Thanks to an obvious analogue of (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle|m_{3}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{\alpha+\lambda+\omega(z)}e^{-y\omega(z)}\Big|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{|\alpha+\lambda+\omega(z)|}\cdot\frac{e^{-\delta y|z|}}{1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|})(\alpha+|\lambda|)}e^{-\delta y|z|}.

Next, using a modification of both (
31
) and (
32
), we find

\displaystyle|zm_{3}(\lambda,z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\frac{|z|}{|\alpha+\lambda+\omega(z)|}\cdot\frac{e^{-\delta y|z|}}{1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1+cy\sqrt{|\lambda|})\sqrt{\alpha+|\lambda|}}e^{-\delta y|z|}.

Ad
m_{2}
.
We have

\displaystyle m_{2}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{y}m_{0}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{z+\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(e^{-y\omega(z)}-e^{-yz})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{z+\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}\cdot(-\omega(z)e^{-y\omega(z)}+ze^{-yz}\pm ze^{-y\omega(z)})

\displaystyle=\frac{z+\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{z(e^{-yz}-e^{-y\omega(z)})+(z-\omega(z))e^{-y\omega(z)}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-m_{1}(\lambda,z,y)-\frac{1}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}e^{-y\omega(z)}\,.

We have already shown that the first term is bounded, the second one can be estimated in the same way as
m_{3}(\lambda,z,y)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle z\partial_{y}m_{0}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=-zm_{1}(\lambda,z,y)-\frac{z}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}e^{-y\omega(z)}

is bounded as both terms are bounded due to the previous parts.

It remains to show part of (
32
), i.e. estimate of
\partial_{y}m_{i}
for all
i
. For
i=1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{y}m_{1}(\lambda,z,y)=zm_{2}(\lambda,z,y),

hence, the bound of this follows from the already proven estimate. Next, for
i=3
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{y}m_{3}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda+\omega(z)}e^{-y\omega(z)}.

There obviously holds
|\omega(z)|\leq C(\sqrt{|\lambda|}+|z|)
. Hence, the boundedness of
\partial_{y}m_{3}
follows from already established inequalities for
i=3
. And finally, for
i=2
, we can compute that

\displaystyle\partial_{y}m_{2}(\lambda,z,y)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{y}m_{1}(\lambda,z,y)+\frac{\omega(z)}{\alpha+\lambda+z+\omega(z)}e^{-y\omega(z)}.

Invoking (
32
) for
i=1
,
3
, the estimate of
m_{2}

is complete.

Concerning
m_{4}
, it is enough to invoke (
23
) and note
that
|e^{-yz}|=e^{-(cos\theta)y}
for
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The solution
\bm{u}
to (
11
–
14
)
satifies the estimates

(36)

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

In the Hilbert case
p=2
, one obtains the estimate of

\bm{u}\in W^{s,2}
directly.

Lemma 4.5
.

The solution
\bm{u}
to (
11
–
14
)
satifies the estimates

(38)

c\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{W^{s-1/2,2}(\Gamma)}\leq\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{W^{s-1/2,2}(\Gamma)}

for any
s\in[1/2,3/2)
. In particular,
\bm{u}\in W^{3/2,2}(\Omega)

if (and only if)
\bm{\phi}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

We will sketch the proof for
d=2
, the general case being similar.
The key point is that since
p=2
, we can use the Fourier transform to
characterize the Sobolev norm of
\phi
as

\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{s-1/2,2}(\Gamma)}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s-1/2}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi\,,

which we want to relate to the velocity gradient norm

\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}|\mathcal{F}(\nabla\bm{u})(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta\,,

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the full Fourier transform. We will also frequently use the symbol
A\simeq B
as a shorthand
for
A\leq c_{1}B
and
B\leq c_{2}A
, where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}>0
are some
irrelevant constants, and
A\lesssim B
for a one-sided estimate.

Since
d=2
, we have
\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
, and relations
(
21
), (
22
) lead to formulas for
the partial Fourier transform of
\widehat{\nabla}=(i\xi,\partial_{y})

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla u_{1}}(\xi,y)

\displaystyle=-\big(i\xi\partial_{y}m_{0}(z,y),\partial^{2}_{y}m_{0}(z,y)\big)\widehat{\phi}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla u_{2}}(\xi,y)

\displaystyle=\big(i\xi zm_{0}(z,y),z\partial_{y}m_{0}(z,y)\big)\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\widehat{\phi}(\xi),

where
z=|\xi|
and

m_{0}(z,y)=R(z)\big(e^{-\omega(z)y}-e^{-zy}\big).

Recall that
\omega(z)=\sqrt{\lambda+z^{2}}
; for simplicity,
we omit the dependence on
\lambda
, which is now fixed.
Note that likewise
R(z)
is not relevant as
|R(z)|\simeq 1
.

It will be useful to set

\displaystyle Z_{j}(z,\eta)=\mathcal{F}_{y\to\eta}[\partial^{j}_{y}(e^{-\omega(z)y}-e^{-zy})],\quad j=0,1,2;

even extension with respect to
y
is used before applying
the transform. The full Fourier transform of
\nabla\bm{u}
now consists of

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=-\big(i\xi Z_{1}(z,\eta),Z_{2}(z,\eta)\big)R(z)\widehat{\phi}(\xi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\big(i\xi zZ_{0}(z,\eta),zZ_{1}(z,\eta)\big)R(z)\widehat{\phi}(\xi).

The key step is to evaluate and estimate

Z_{j}(z,\eta)
. In view of the formula

\mathcal{F}_{y\to\eta}[\exp(-a|y|)]=\frac{a}{a^{2}+\eta^{2}}\,,\quad\mathfrak{Re}\,a>0,

we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{0}(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2}}-\frac{z}{z^{2}+\eta^{2}}=\frac{\lambda}{\omega(z)+z}\cdot\frac{\eta^{2}-z\omega(z)}{(\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2})(z^{2}+\eta^{2})}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{1}(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}}{z^{2}+\eta^{2}}-\frac{\omega^{2}(z)}{\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2}}=\frac{\lambda\eta^{2}}{(\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2})(z^{2}+\eta^{2})}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{2}(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{3}(z)}{\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2}}-\frac{z^{3}}{z^{2}+\eta^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(\eta^{2}(z^{2}+z\omega(z)+\omega^{2}(z))+z^{2}\omega^{2}(z)))}{(z+\omega(z))(\omega^{2}(z)+\eta^{2})(z^{2}+\eta^{2})}\,.

Recalling now that
\mathfrak{Re}\,\lambda>0
is fixed, it is straighforward to
deduce that
\omega(z)\simeq 1+z
,
\omega^{2}(z)\simeq 1+z^{2}
, and
hence also
|\eta^{2}-z\omega(z)|\lesssim\eta^{2}+z^{2}+z
, and

|\eta^{2}(z^{2}+z\omega(z)+\omega^{2}(z))+z^{2}\omega^{2}(z)|\simeq(\eta^{2}+z^{2})(1+z^{2})
. We thus can write

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\simeq\big(|zZ_{1}(z,\eta)|+|Z_{2}(z,\eta)|\big)|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|\simeq\frac{1+z}{1+z^{2}+\eta^{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle\simeq\big(|z^{2}Z_{0}(z,\eta)|+|zZ_{1}(z,\eta)|\big)|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1+z}{1+z^{2}+\eta^{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|.

This eventually leads to a key pointwise estimate

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\nabla\bm{u})(\xi,\eta)|\simeq\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}+\eta^{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|.

It is now straighforward to deduce

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}|\mathcal{F}(\nabla\bm{u})(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s-2}\,{\rm d}\eta\Big)|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s-1/2}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi=\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{s-1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

Here we have also used that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\rm d}\eta}{(b^{2}+\eta^{2})^{\sigma}}\simeq b^{1-2\sigma},\quad\sigma>1/2.

Proof of

.

We start with formally recovering the resolvent estimate (

Decompose the

\bm{u}=\bm{u}^{1}+\bm{u}^{2}

\bm{u}^{1}

\bm{u}^{2}
has zero right-hand side in
\Omega
and takes care of the boundary condition. To be specific; the function
\bm{u}^{1}
solves the system

(39)

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{u}^{1}+\nabla\pi^{1}

\displaystyle=\bm{f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\bm{u}^{1}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{1}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

\displaystyle(\lambda-\Delta)\bm{u}^{2}+\nabla\pi^{2}

\bm{\phi}=\bm{h}-2[({\rm D}\bm{u}^{1})\bm{n}]_{\tau}.

Assume that

with
|\lambda|>\omega
,
where
\omega>0

\displaystyle||\bm{u}^{1}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}||\bm{f}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,.

Because of Sobolev embedding we get

\displaystyle||[({\rm D}\bm{u}^{1})\bm{n}]_{\tau}||_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq c||\bm{f}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

and, thus, there also holds

\displaystyle||\bm{\phi}||_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=||\bm{h}-2[({\rm D}\bm{u}^{1})\bm{n}]_{\tau}||_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq c||(\bm{f},\bm{h})||_{{L^{p}(\Omega)}\times{L^{p}(\Gamma)}}.

Note that

.
Hence, it suffices to show

\displaystyle||(\bm{u}^{2},\text{tr}\,\bm{u}^{2})||_{{L^{p}(\Omega)}\times{L^{p}(\Gamma)}}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}||\bm{\phi}||_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

But this follows by
4.4
above,
in particular the estimate (
36
).

So far, the argument was formal. Using a suitable approximation,

2
–
5

has at least one solution. In view of (

\lVert\bm{u}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\lVert\bm{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big),

where

depends on
\lambda
. Let us show uniqueness; clearly,

\bm{u}
a solution to the homogeneous problem.
We set
\widetilde{\bm{f}}=|\bm{u}|^{p-2}\overline{\bm{u}}
,

\widetilde{\bm{h}}=|\bm{u}|^{p-2}\overline{\bm{u}}
and observe that

,

By the above, there is some

and vice versa, one deduces that

\bm{u}=0
.

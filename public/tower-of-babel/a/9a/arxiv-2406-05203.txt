On identities in connected topological groups

Introduction

In 1957 Mycielski
[
1
]
proved that if an identity holds in some neighbourhood of the identity of a connected locally compact group or of a connected abelian topological group, then it holds in the whole group. He also asked the following question: let
G
be a connected topological group such that the identity
x^{3}=e
holds in some neighbourhood of the identity of
G
; does the identity
x^{3}=e
hold in the whole group
G
? The same question was asked for the identity
gx^{2}=x^{2}g
, where
g
is a fixed element of the group.

The general question (due to N. Aronszajn) was included by V. P. Platonov in the 2nd issue of the Kourovka Notebook (1967) as Question 2.48
[
2
,
3
]
:

let
G
be a connected topological group, let
f
be an identity, and let
f|_{U}=e
for some neighbourhood
U
of the identity of
G
. Is it true that then
f|_{G}=e
?

In this paper we answer this question in the negative.

Theorem
.

Let
n\geq 665
be an odd number. There exist a path-connected Hausdorff topological group
G
and an open neighbourhood
V
of the identity of
G
such that
x^{n}=e
for all
x\in V
, but
x^{n}\neq e
for some
x\in G
.

The group
G
is constructed as the quotient of the free topological group
F([0,1],0)
by the closure of the subgroup generated by the
n
th powers of elements of small Graev norm. The proof consists of two independent parts: a topological one (Proposition
2.1
: the closure of such a subgroup is not much larger than the subgroup itself) and an algebraic one (Proposition
2.2
: a certain
n
th power does not belong to this subgroup). The second part reduces to the existence, in the free Burnside group
B(m,n)
, of a system of periods with two properties (Definition
4.1
): every element is conjugate to a power of a period, and the
n
th powers of the periods form a basis of the centre of the corresponding central extension. We give two proofs of this part. The first one relies on results of A. Yu. Ol’shanskii
[
9
,
10
]
and of I. S. Ashmanov and A. Yu. Ol’shanskii
[
11
]
and works for odd
n>10^{10}
. The second one relies on results of S. I. Adian
[
12
]
and of S. I. Adian and V. S. Atabekyan
[
14
]
and works for odd
n\geq 665
.

The present paper is a corrected and substantially simplified version of the preprint
[
16
]
, which is referred to in the comment to Question 2.48 in
[
3
]
. In the preprint and in that comment the theorem is stated for odd
n\geq 10^{10}
. The result was announced in 1985 at the research seminar on general topology of Moscow State University
[
15
]
.

1
Notation and preliminaries

We denote by
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
the set of natural numbers, by
\omega_{1}
the first uncountable ordinal, and
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
. The identity of a group is denoted by
e
, and
\langle M\rangle
is the subgroup generated by a set
M
. The closure of a set
M
in a topological space is denoted by
\overline{M}
. All topological groups under consideration are Hausdorff.

1.1
Norms and invariant metrics

Let
K
be a group. A function
\nu\colon K\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called an
invariant pseudonorm
if
\nu(e)=0
,
\nu(xy)\leq\nu(x)+\nu(y)
,
\nu(x^{-1})=\nu(x)
, and
\nu(yxy^{-1})=\nu(x)
for all
x,y\in K
; if, in addition,
\nu(x)=0
only for
x=e
, then
\nu
is called an
invariant norm
. A pseudometric
\rho
on
K
is called
invariant
if
\rho(zx,zy)=\rho(xz,yz)=\rho(x,y)
for all
x,y,z\in K
. The formulas
\nu(x)=\rho(x,e)
and
\rho(x,y)=\nu(xy^{-1})
establish a one-to-one correspondence between invariant pseudometrics and invariant pseudonorms (metrics and norms, respectively). A set
M\subset K
is called
invariant
if
gMg^{-1}=M
for all
g\in K
.

1.2
Free topological groups

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space and
e\in X
. The
free (Graev) topological group

F(X,e)
is a topological group that contains
X
as a subspace, has
e
as its identity, and is such that every continuous map
f\colon X\to K
to a topological group
K
with
f(e)=e
extends uniquely to a continuous homomorphism
\hat{f}\colon F(X,e)\to K

[
4
]
. Algebraically,
F(X,e)
is the free group with basis
X\setminus\{e\}
. For
g\in F(X,e)
, we denote by
\operatorname{supp}g
the set of elements of
X\setminus\{e\}
occurring in the reduced word representing
g
. If
e\in T\subset X
, then
F(T,e)
denotes the subgroup
\langle T\rangle\subset F(X,e)
; it is the free group with basis
T\setminus\{e\}
.

Let
\widetilde{X}=X\cup X^{-1}\subset F(X,e)
; then
X\cap X^{-1}=\{e\}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

j_{k}\colon\widetilde{X}^{k}\to F(X,e),\qquad(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mapsto x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k},

and
F_{k}(X,e)=j_{k}(\widetilde{X}^{k})
. The map
j_{k}
is continuous, and
F(X,e)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}F_{k}(X,e)
.

Fact 1.1

(
[
4
,
6
]
)
.

Let
X
be compact. Then the sets
F_{k}(X,e)
are compact, and a set
M\subset F(X,e)
is closed in
F(X,e)
if and only if
M\cap F_{k}(X,e)
is closed for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In
[
5
, Theorem 7.4.1 and Corollary 7.4.2]
this is proved for the Markov free topological group
F(X)
and its subsets
B_{k}(X)
of elements of reduced length
\leq k
. The assertion for
F(X,e)
follows. Indeed, the homomorphism
\pi\colon F(X)\to F(X,e)
extending the identity map of
X
is a quotient map: the group
F(X)/\ker\pi
with the quotient topology has the universal property of
F(X,e)
. Moreover,
\pi(B_{k}(X))=F_{k}(X,e)
. If the sets
M\cap F_{k}(X,e)
are closed for all
k
, then so are the sets
\pi^{-1}(M)\cap B_{k}(X)=(\pi|_{B_{k}(X)})^{-1}(M\cap F_{k}(X,e))
; hence
\pi^{-1}(M)
is closed, and therefore
M
is closed.

If
X
is a metrizable compactum, then so is
\widetilde{X}^{k}
, and
F_{k}(X,e)
, being a continuous Hausdorff image of a metrizable compactum, is also a metrizable compactum.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
X
is path-connected, then
F(X,e)
is path-connected.

Proof.

The space
\widetilde{X}
is path-connected as the union of the two path-connected sets
X
and
X^{-1}
with the common point
e
. Hence all the sets
F_{k}(X,e)=j_{k}(\widetilde{X}^{k})
are path-connected. All of them contain
e
, and their union is
F(X,e)
.
∎

1.3
The Graev extension of a metric

Theorem 1.3

(Graev
[
4
]
; see also
[
5
, Theorem 7.2.2 and Exercise 7.2.a]
,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
d
be a metric on
X
generating the topology of
X
. There exists an invariant metric
\hat{d}
on
F(X,e)
extending
d
such that
\rho\leq\hat{d}
for every invariant pseudometric
\rho
on
F(X,e)
extending
d
. The function
\hat{d}\colon F(X,e)\times F(X,e)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is continuous
(
equivalently, the norm
g\mapsto\hat{d}(g,e)
is continuous
)
.

The metric
\hat{d}
is called the
Graev extension
of
d
, and the corresponding invariant norm
N_{d}(g)=\hat{d}(g,e)
is called the
Graev norm
. An explicit formula for
N_{d}
in terms of cancellation schemes of words can be found in
[
4
,
7
,
8
]
; it is not used in this paper.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
d
be a metric on
X
generating the topology of
X
, and let
\rho
be an invariant pseudometric on
F(X,e)
such that
\rho(x,y)\leq d(x,y)
for
x,y\in X
. Then
\rho\leq\hat{d}
.

Proof.

The function
\rho^{\prime}=\max(\rho,\hat{d})
is an invariant pseudometric on
F(X,e)
, and
\rho^{\prime}(x,y)=\max(\rho(x,y),d(x,y))=d(x,y)
for
x,y\in X
, i.e.,
\rho^{\prime}
extends
d
. By Theorem
1.3
,
\rho^{\prime}\leq\hat{d}
, whence
\rho\leq\hat{d}
.
∎

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
d
be a metric on
X
generating the topology of
X
, let
K
be a group with an invariant pseudonorm
\nu
, let
L>0
, and let
f\colon X\to K
be a map such that
f(e)=e
and
\nu(f(x)f(y)^{-1})\leq L\,d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
. Let
\hat{f}\colon F(X,e)\to K
be the homomorphism extending
f
. Then
\nu(\hat{f}(g))\leq L\,N_{d}(g)
for all
g\in F(X,e)
.

Note that
f
is not assumed to be continuous.

Proof.

Let
\rho(g,h)=L^{-1}\nu(\hat{f}(g)\hat{f}(h)^{-1})
for
g,h\in F(X,e)
. Since
\hat{f}
is a homomorphism and
\nu
is an invariant pseudonorm,
\rho
is an invariant pseudometric on
F(X,e)
, and
\rho(x,y)\leq d(x,y)
for
x,y\in X
. By Lemma
1.4
,
\rho\leq\hat{d}
, whence
\nu(\hat{f}(g))=L\rho(g,e)\leq L\,\hat{d}(g,e)=L\,N_{d}(g)
.
∎

2
Construction of the group
G
and proof of the theorem

From now on,
X=[0,1]
,
e=0
,
d(s,t)=|s-t|
, and
N=N_{d}
is the Graev norm on
F([0,1],0)
. The point
1\in[0,1]\subset F([0,1],0)
is denoted by
\mathbf{1}
. Throughout,
n\geq 665
is a fixed odd number.

For
c>0
, let

U_{c}=\{g\in F([0,1],0):N(g)<c\},\qquad P_{c}=\{g^{n}:g\in U_{c}\},\qquad H_{c}=\langle P_{c}\rangle.

Since the norm
N
is invariant, the sets
U_{c}
and
P_{c}
are invariant, and hence the subgroups
H_{c}
are normal. Clearly,
H_{c}\subset H_{c^{\prime}}
for
c<c^{\prime}
. Since
N
is continuous (Theorem
1.3
), the sets
U_{c}
are open in
F([0,1],0)
.

The proof of the theorem is based on the following two propositions, which will be proved in §
3
and §
4
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
, then
\overline{H_{c_{1}}}\subset H_{c_{2}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

\mathbf{1}^{n}\notin H_{1}
.

Proof of the theorem.

Let
H=\overline{H_{1/2}}
. The closure of a normal subgroup of a topological group is a normal subgroup; thus
H
is a closed normal subgroup. Let
G=F([0,1],0)/H
be the quotient group with the quotient topology, and let
\varphi\colon F([0,1],0)\to G
be the quotient homomorphism. Since
H
is closed,
G
is a Hausdorff topological group;
\varphi
is a continuous open map. The group
G
is path-connected as a continuous image of the path-connected group
F([0,1],0)
(Lemma
1.2
).

Let
V=\varphi(U_{1/2})
; this is an open neighbourhood of the identity of
G
. If
x\in V
, then
x=\varphi(g)
for some
g\in U_{1/2}
, and
g^{n}\in P_{1/2}\subset H
, whence
x^{n}=\varphi(g^{n})=e
.

Let
x=\varphi(\mathbf{1})
. If
x^{n}=e
, then
\mathbf{1}^{n}\in H=\overline{H_{1/2}}\subset H_{1}
by Proposition
2.1
, which contradicts Proposition
2.2
. Therefore
x^{n}\neq e
.
∎

3
The closure of the subgroups
H_{c}

In this section we prove Proposition
2.1
.

3.1
Lipschitz endomorphisms

We denote by
\widetilde{[0,1]}=[0,1]\cup[0,1]^{-1}\subset F([0,1],0)
the set
\widetilde{X}
from §
1
for
X=[0,1]
,
e=0
; here
0=0^{-1}=e
.

Let
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
and
h(0)=0
. Since
F([0,1],0)
is the free group with basis
(0,1]
, the map
h
extends uniquely to an endomorphism
\hat{h}\colon F([0,1],0)\to F([0,1],0)
. Let
\widetilde{h}(t^{\pm 1})=h(t)^{\pm 1}
for
t\in[0,1]
; then
\widetilde{h}\colon\widetilde{[0,1]}\to\widetilde{[0,1]}
and

\hat{h}(j_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}))=j_{k}(\widetilde{h}(x_{1}),\dots,\widetilde{h}(x_{k})).

(1)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L>0
,
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
h(0)=0
, and
|h(s)-h(t)|\leq L|s-t|
for
s,t\in[0,1]
. Then
N(\hat{h}(g))\leq L\,N(g)
for
g\in F([0,1],0)
, and
\hat{h}(H_{c})\subset H_{Lc}
for
c>0
.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
1.5
to
K=F([0,1],0)
,
\nu=N
, and
f=h
: since
\hat{d}
extends
d
, we have
N(h(s)h(t)^{-1})=|h(s)-h(t)|\leq L|s-t|
. Hence
N(\hat{h}(g))\leq L\,N(g)
. Since
N(q^{-1})=N(q)
and
q^{-n}=(q^{-1})^{n}
, every element
g\in H_{c}
has the form
g=q_{1}^{n}\cdots q_{k}^{n}
with
q_{i}\in U_{c}
. Then
\hat{h}(g)=\hat{h}(q_{1})^{n}\cdots\hat{h}(q_{k})^{n}
and
N(\hat{h}(q_{i}))\leq L\,N(q_{i})<Lc
, i.e.,
\hat{h}(g)\in H_{Lc}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T\subset[0,1]
be a finite set with
0\in T
, and let
h^{*}\colon T\to[0,1]
be a map such that
h^{*}(0)=0
and
|h^{*}(s)-h^{*}(t)|\leq|s-t|
for
s,t\in T
. Then
h^{*}
extends to a map
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
|h(s)-h(t)|\leq|s-t|
for
s,t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
T=\{s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{p}\}
,
s_{0}=0
. Let
h
be linear on each segment
[s_{i-1},s_{i}]
, and let
h(t)=h^{*}(s_{p})
for
t\geq s_{p}
. The function
h
is continuous and piecewise linear, and the absolute values of the slopes of all its pieces do not exceed
1
; hence
h
is
1
-Lipschitz.
∎

3.2
The metric on
\widetilde{[0,1]}

For
t\in[0,1]
, let
|t|=|t^{-1}|=t
; this defines a function
|\cdot|\colon\widetilde{[0,1]}\to[0,1]
. Every element
y\in\widetilde{[0,1]}\setminus\{e\}
can be uniquely written as
y=t^{\sigma}
with
t\in(0,1]
and
\sigma=\pm 1
; we call
\sigma
the
sign
of
y
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The restriction
\tilde{d}
of the metric
\hat{d}
to
\widetilde{[0,1]}
is given by

\tilde{d}(s^{\sigma},t^{\sigma})=|s-t|,\qquad\tilde{d}(s^{\sigma},t^{-\sigma})=s+t\qquad(s,t\in[0,1],\ \sigma=\pm 1).

The metric
\tilde{d}
generates the topology of
\widetilde{[0,1]}
, and
\bigl||y|-|z|\bigr|\leq\tilde{d}(y,z)
for
y,z\in\widetilde{[0,1]}
.

Proof.

We have
\hat{d}(s,t)=|s-t|
and, by invariance,
\hat{d}(s^{-1},t^{-1})=N(s^{-1}t)=N(ts^{-1})=|s-t|
. Further,
\hat{d}(s,t^{-1})=N(st)\leq N(s)+N(t)=s+t
. On the other hand, applying Corollary
1.5
to
K=\mathbb{R}
,
\nu(v)=|v|
,
f(t)=t
, and
L=1
, we obtain a homomorphism
\hat{f}\colon F([0,1],0)\to\mathbb{R}
with
|\hat{f}(g)|\leq N(g)
, whence
N(st)\geq|s+t|=s+t
. The metric
\tilde{d}
is continuous on the compact space
\widetilde{[0,1]}
and therefore generates its topology. The last inequality is obvious from the formulas.
∎

3.3
The operation
[\,\cdot\,]_{1}
and the closure

For
M\subset F([0,1],0)
, let

[M]_{1}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{M\cap F_{k}([0,1],0)}.

Clearly,
M\subset[M]_{1}\subset\overline{M}
, and
[M]_{1}\subset[M^{\prime}]_{1}
for
M\subset M^{\prime}
. By induction on ordinals
\alpha\leq\omega_{1}
we define
[M]_{\alpha}
:
[M]_{0}=M
,
[M]_{\alpha+1}=\bigl[[M]_{\alpha}\bigr]_{1}
, and
[M]_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}[M]_{\beta}
for limit
\alpha
. Since
Q\subset[Q]_{1}
for every
Q
, by induction we obtain
[M]_{\alpha}\subset[M]_{\beta}
for
\alpha\leq\beta
.

Lemma 3.4
.

\overline{M}=[M]_{\omega_{1}}
for every
M\subset F([0,1],0)
.

Proof.

By induction on
\alpha
we obtain
[M]_{\alpha}\subset\overline{M}
; in particular,
[M]_{\omega_{1}}\subset\overline{M}
. It suffices to check that the set
Q=[M]_{\omega_{1}}
is closed. Suppose the contrary. By Fact
1.1
, there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the set
P=Q\cap F_{k}([0,1],0)
is not closed. Since
F_{k}([0,1],0)
is a metrizable compactum, there exist
x\in\overline{P}\setminus P
and a sequence
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset P
converging to
x
. For each
i
we have
x_{i}\in[M]_{\alpha_{i}}
for some
\alpha_{i}<\omega_{1}
. Let
\beta=\sup_{i}\alpha_{i}<\omega_{1}
. Then
x_{i}\in[M]_{\beta}\cap F_{k}([0,1],0)
for all
i
, and therefore
x\in\bigl[[M]_{\beta}\bigr]_{1}=[M]_{\beta+1}\subset Q
. Since
x\in F_{k}([0,1],0)
, we obtain
x\in P
, a contradiction.
∎

3.4
The main lemma and the proof of Proposition
2.1

Lemma 3.5
.

If
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
, then
[H_{c_{1}}]_{1}\subset H_{c_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in\overline{H_{c_{1}}\cap F_{k}}
, where
F_{k}=F_{k}([0,1],0)
. Let
A=j_{k}^{-1}(H_{c_{1}}\cap F_{k})\subset\widetilde{[0,1]}^{k}
. The map
j_{k}\colon\widetilde{[0,1]}^{k}\to F_{k}
is a continuous surjection of a compact space onto a Hausdorff space; hence it is closed and
\overline{j_{k}(A)}=j_{k}(\overline{A})
. Therefore there exists
\bar{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in\overline{A}
with
j_{k}(\bar{y})=y
.

Fix
\gamma
with
c_{1}/c_{2}<\gamma<1
. Let
R=\{0\}\cup\{|y_{i}|:i=1,\dots,k\}
,
\Delta=\min\{|a-b|:a,b\in R,\ a\neq b\}
(if
R=\{0\}
, let
\Delta=1
), and
\varepsilon=(1-\gamma)\Delta/2
. Since
\bar{y}\in\overline{A}
, there exists
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})\in A
such that
\tilde{d}(y_{i},z_{i})<\varepsilon
for all
i
; let
z=j_{k}(\bar{z})\in H_{c_{1}}
. It is also convenient to put
y_{0}=z_{0}=e
.

We check the following properties (for
i,j\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
).

(a)

If
|y_{i}|\neq|y_{j}|
, then
\gamma\bigl||y_{i}|-|y_{j}|\bigr|\leq\bigl||z_{i}|-|z_{j}|\bigr|
. Indeed, by Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\bigl||y_{i}|-|y_{j}|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{d}(y_{i},z_{i})+\bigl||z_{i}|-|z_{j}|\bigr|+\tilde{d}(z_{j},y_{j})<2\varepsilon+\bigl||z_{i}|-|z_{j}|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)\bigl||y_{i}|-|y_{j}|\bigr|+\bigl||z_{i}|-|z_{j}|\bigr|,

since
2\varepsilon=(1-\gamma)\Delta\leq(1-\gamma)\bigl||y_{i}|-|y_{j}|\bigr|
.

(b)

If
|z_{i}|=|z_{j}|
, then
|y_{i}|=|y_{j}|
: this follows from (a). In particular (for
j=0
), if
z_{i}=e
, then
y_{i}=e
.

(c)

If
y_{i}\neq e
, then
z_{i}\neq e
and the signs of
y_{i}
and
z_{i}
coincide. Indeed,
z_{i}\neq e
by (b); if the signs were different, then, by Lemma
3.3
,
\tilde{d}(y_{i},z_{i})=|y_{i}|+|z_{i}|>|y_{i}|\geq\Delta>\varepsilon
.

Let
T=\{|z_{i}|:i=0,1,\dots,k\}\ni 0
, and define
h^{*}\colon T\to[0,1]
by
h^{*}(|z_{i}|)=\gamma|y_{i}|
. By (b),
h^{*}
is well defined and
h^{*}(0)=0
; by (a),
|h^{*}(s)-h^{*}(t)|\leq|s-t|
for
s,t\in T
. By Lemma
3.2
, extend
h^{*}
to a
1
-Lipschitz map
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
;
h(0)=0
. Let also
h_{\gamma}(t)=\gamma t
.

We show that
\widetilde{h}(z_{i})=\widetilde{h_{\gamma}}(y_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,k
. If
y_{i}=e
, then
|y_{i}|=0
and
\widetilde{h}(z_{i})=h(|z_{i}|)^{\pm 1}=e=\widetilde{h_{\gamma}}(y_{i})
. If
y_{i}=t^{\sigma}\neq e
, then, by (c),
z_{i}=s^{\sigma}
with
s=|z_{i}|
, and
\widetilde{h}(z_{i})=h(s)^{\sigma}=(\gamma t)^{\sigma}=\widetilde{h_{\gamma}}(y_{i})
. By (
1
),
\hat{h}(z)=\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y)
.

By Lemma
3.1
(with
L=1
),
\hat{h}(z)\in H_{c_{1}}
, i.e.,
\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y)\in H_{c_{1}}
. Let
r(t)=\min(t,\gamma)/\gamma
. Then
r\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
r(0)=0
,
|r(s)-r(t)|\leq\gamma^{-1}|s-t|
, and
r\circ h_{\gamma}=\mathrm{id}_{[0,1]}
, so that
\hat{r}\circ\widehat{h_{\gamma}}=\mathrm{id}
. By Lemma
3.1
(with
L=\gamma^{-1}
), we obtain
y=\hat{r}(\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y))\in H_{c_{1}/\gamma}\subset H_{c_{2}}

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

By induction on
\alpha\leq\omega_{1}
we prove the assertion:
[H_{c}]_{\alpha}\subset H_{c^{\prime}}
for all

. For
\alpha=0
this is obvious. Let
\alpha=\beta+1
and

; put
c^{\prime\prime}=(c+c^{\prime})/2
. By the induction hypothesis,

, and by Lemma

[H_{c}]_{\beta+1}=\bigl[[H_{c}]_{\beta}\bigr]_{1}\subset[H_{c^{\prime\prime}}]_{1}\subset H_{c^{\prime}}.

For limit

. For

4
The element
\mathbf{1}^{n}
does not belong to
H_{1}

In this section we prove Proposition
2.2
.

4.1
Systems of periods

Let
\Phi
be the free group with basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
, and let
\operatorname{ab}\colon\Phi\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
be the abelianization homomorphism,
\operatorname{ab}(g_{i})=\mathbf{e}_{i}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{m}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. Let

W=\{b\in\Phi:\ \text{at least one coordinate of the vector }\operatorname{ab}(b)\text{ is }0\}.

Let
B(m,n)=\Phi/R
be the free Burnside group, where
R
is the normal subgroup generated by the
n
th powers of all elements of
\Phi
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
. A set of words
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\Phi
is called a
system of periods
for
B(m,n)
if the following conditions hold:

(O1)

every element of
\Phi
is conjugate modulo
R
to a power of some
A_{i}
, i.e., for every
w\in\Phi
there exist
u\in\Phi
,
i\in I
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
r\in R
such that
w=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
;

(O2)

R/[\Phi,R]
is a free abelian group with basis
\{A_{i}^{n}[\Phi,R]\}_{i\in I}
.

The algebraic part of the proof reduces to the following lemma and to the existence of systems of periods.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n
be odd,
m\geq 2
, and suppose that there exists a system of periods for
B(m,n)
. Then
(g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{m})^{n}\notin\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a system of periods for
B(m,n)
. Let
a=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}
; then
\operatorname{ab}(a)=(1,\dots,1)
.

By (O1),
a=uA_{i_{0}}^{l_{0}}u^{-1}r
for some
u\in\Phi
,
i_{0}\in I
,
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
r\in R
. Since
\operatorname{ab}(R)\subset n\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we have
(1,\dots,1)\equiv l_{0}\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\pmod{n}
. Hence
\gcd(l_{0},n)=1
: if a prime
p
divides both
l_{0}
and
n
, then
\frac{n}{p}(1,\dots,1)\equiv\frac{l_{0}}{p}\,n\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv 0\pmod{n}
, which is false. Let
l_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
l_{0}l_{0}^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
; then
\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv l_{0}^{\prime}(1,\dots,1)\pmod{n}
.

Let
\bar{\Phi}=\Phi/[\Phi,R]
and
\bar{R}=R/[\Phi,R]
; the subgroup
\bar{R}
is central in
\bar{\Phi}
. The image of an element
w\in\Phi
in
\bar{\Phi}
is denoted by the same letter. By (O2), there exists a homomorphism
\psi\colon\bar{R}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
such that
\psi(A_{i_{0}}^{n})=1
and
\psi(A_{i}^{n})=0
for
i\neq i_{0}
.

Let
w\in\Phi
and
w=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
, where
u\in\Phi
,
r\in R
. Since
r
and
A_{i}^{n}
lie in the centre of
\bar{\Phi}
, in
\bar{\Phi}
we have

w^{n}=(uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1})^{n}r^{n}=uA_{i}^{ln}u^{-1}r^{n}=A_{i}^{ln}r^{n}\in\bar{R},

and therefore
\psi(w^{n})=l\,\delta_{i,i_{0}}\bmod n
(because
\psi(r^{n})=n\psi(r)=0
).

In particular,
\psi(a^{n})=l_{0}\bmod n\neq 0
.

Now let
b\in W
and
b=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
. If
i=i_{0}
and
n\nmid l
, then
\operatorname{ab}(b)\equiv l\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv l\,l_{0}^{\prime}(1,\dots,1)\pmod{n}
, and all coordinates of
\operatorname{ab}(b)
are congruent to
l\,l_{0}^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{n}
(since
n\nmid l
and
\gcd(l_{0}^{\prime},n)=1
); this contradicts the fact that one of the coordinates of
\operatorname{ab}(b)
is zero. Thus
i\neq i_{0}
or
n\mid l
, and in both cases
\psi(b^{n})=0
.

The images of all elements
b^{n}
,
b\in W
, lie in the abelian group
\bar{R}
and are annihilated by the homomorphism
\psi
; hence
\psi
annihilates the image of the subgroup
\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
. Since
\psi(a^{n})\neq 0
, we obtain
a^{n}\notin\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
.
∎

is known in two versions, due to Ol’shanskii and to Adian. Accordingly, Lemma
4.2
yields two proofs of the algebraic part.

Fact 4.3

(

As a system of periods one takes the set

Let

4.5
A weighted norm and the proof of Proposition
2.2

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}=1
,
w_{k}=t_{k}-t_{k-1}
, and let
\mathbb{R}^{m}
be endowed with the norm
\|v\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}|v_{k}|
. Let
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
f(t)_{k}=\min\bigl(\max\bigl((t-t_{k-1})/w_{k},0\bigr),1\bigr)
, and let
\hat{f}\colon F([0,1],0)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the homomorphism extending
f
. Then
\|\hat{f}(g)\|\leq N(g)
for
g\in F([0,1],0)
, and
f(t_{k})=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{k}
for
k=0,1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

The equality
f(t_{k})=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{k}
is obvious; in particular,
f(0)=0
. Let
s<t
. All coordinates of
f
are nondecreasing, and
w_{k}\bigl(f(t)_{k}-f(s)_{k}\bigr)
equals the length of the segment
[s,t]\cap[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Hence
\|f(t)-f(s)\|=t-s
. Since
\|\cdot\|
is an invariant norm on the abelian group
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the assertion follows from Corollary
1.5
(with
L=1
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{1}^{n}\in H_{1}
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
\mathbf{1}^{n}=x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{k}^{n}
with
N(x_{j})<1
. Let
T=\{0,1\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{supp}x_{j}
and
T=\{t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}\}
; then
t_{0}=0
,
t_{m}=1
,
m\geq 1
, and all
x_{j}
lie in
F(T,0)
, the free group with basis
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
. The elements
g_{i}=t_{i-1}^{-1}t_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
(where
t_{0}=e
), also form a basis of
F(T,0)
, since
t_{i}=g_{1}\cdots g_{i}
. In particular,
\mathbf{1}=t_{m}=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}
. We identify
F(T,0)
with the free group
\Phi
from Lemma
4.5
via the basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
.

Let
f
and
\hat{f}
be as in Lemma
4.6
. Then
\hat{f}(g_{i})=f(t_{i})-f(t_{i-1})=\mathbf{e}_{i}
, so that the restriction of
\hat{f}
to
F(T,0)
coincides with the abelianization homomorphism
\operatorname{ab}\colon F(T,0)\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with respect to the basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
. By Lemma
4.6

\|\operatorname{ab}(x_{j})\|\leq N(x_{j})<1=\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}
. Since the coordinates of the vector

\operatorname{ab}(x_{j})

(g_{1}\cdots g_{m})^{n}=\mathbf{1}^{n}=x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{k}^{n}\in\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle

∎

Remark 5.1

The restriction

[

used Ol’shanskii’s version (Fact

[

.

Remark 5.2
.

For
n=2
the answer to the question is positive. Let
x^{2}=e
for all
x
in a neighbourhood
U
of the identity of a connected group
G
, and let
W
be a symmetric neighbourhood of the identity with
W^{2}\subset U
. For
x,y\in W
we have
x^{2}=y^{2}=(xy)^{2}=e
, whence
xy=(xy)^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}=yx
. Therefore the connected group
G=\langle W\rangle
is abelian, and the set
\{x\in G:x^{2}=e\}
is a subgroup of
G
containing
U
, i.e., an open, and hence also closed, subgroup. Thus it coincides with
G
.

Question 3

О тождествах в связных топологических группах

И.Н. Зябрев, Е.А. Резниченко

(оригинальный текст на русском языке)

Введение

В 1957 году Мыцельский
[
1
]
доказал, что если в связной локально компактной или в связной абелевой топологической группе в некоторой окрестности единицы выполняется какое-либо тождество, то оно выполняется и во всей группе. Там же был поставлен вопрос: пусть
G
— связная топологическая группа и в некоторой окрестности единицы группы
G
выполняется тождество
x^{3}=e
; верно ли, что тождество
x^{3}=e
выполняется во всей группе
G
? Тот же вопрос ставился и для тождества
gx^{2}=x^{2}g
, где
g
— фиксированный элемент группы.

Общая постановка (принадлежащая Н. Ароншайну) была внесена В.П. Платоновым во 2-й выпуск Коуровской тетради (1967) под номером 2.48
[
2
,
3
]
:

пусть
G
— связная топологическая группа,
f
— тождество и
f|_{U}=e
для некоторой окрестности единицы
U
группы
G
. Верно ли, что тогда
f|_{G}=e
?

В настоящей работе дан отрицательный ответ на этот вопрос.

Теорема
.

Пусть
n\geq 665
— нечётное число. Существуют линейно связная хаусдорфова топологическая группа
G
и открытая окрестность единицы
V
группы
G
такие, что
x^{n}=e
для всех
x\in V
, но
x^{n}\neq e
для некоторого
x\in G
.

Группа
G
строится как фактор-группа свободной топологической группы
F([0,1],0)
по замыканию подгруппы, порождённой
n
-ми степенями элементов малой граевской нормы. Доказательство состоит из двух независимых частей: топологической (предложение
2.1
: замыкание такой подгруппы не сильно больше самой подгруппы) и алгебраической (предложение
2.2
: некоторая
n
-я степень в эту подгруппу не попадает). Вторая часть сводится к существованию в свободной бернсайдовой группе
B(m,n)
системы периодов, обладающей двумя свойствами (определение
4.1
): каждый элемент сопряжён со степенью периода, и
n
-е степени периодов образуют базис центра соответствующего центрального расширения. Мы приводим два доказательства этой части. Первое опирается на результаты А.Ю. Ольшанского
[
9
,
10
]
и И.С. Ашманова и А.Ю. Ольшанского
[
11
]
и проходит при нечётных
n>10^{10}
. Второе опирается на результаты С.И. Адяна
[
12
]
и С.И. Адяна и В.С. Атабекяна
[
14
]
и проходит при нечётных
n\geq 665
.

1
Обозначения и предварительные сведения

Через
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
обозначаются натуральные числа,
\omega_{1}
— первый несчётный ординал,
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
. Единица группы обозначается через
e
,
\langle M\rangle
— подгруппа, порождённая множеством
M
. Замыкание множества
M
в топологическом пространстве обозначается через
\overline{M}
. Все рассматриваемые топологические группы хаусдорфовы.

1.1
Нормы и инвариантные метрики

Пусть
K
— группа. Функция
\nu\colon K\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
называется
инвариантной псевдонормой
, если
\nu(e)=0
,
\nu(xy)\leq\nu(x)+\nu(y)
,
\nu(x^{-1})=\nu(x)
и
\nu(yxy^{-1})=\nu(x)
для всех
x,y\in K
; если, кроме того,
\nu(x)=0
только при
x=e
, то
\nu
называется
инвариантной нормой
. Псевдометрика
\rho
на
K
называется
инвариантной
, если
\rho(zx,zy)=\rho(xz,yz)=\rho(x,y)
для всех
x,y,z\in K
. Формулы
\nu(x)=\rho(x,e)
и
\rho(x,y)=\nu(xy^{-1})
устанавливают взаимно однозначное соответствие между инвариантными псевдометриками и инвариантными псевдонормами (метриками и нормами соответственно). Множество
M\subset K
называется
инвариантным
, если
gMg^{-1}=M
для всех
g\in K
.

1.2
Свободные топологические группы

Пусть
X
— тихоновское пространство и
e\in X
.
Свободной (граевской) топологической группой

F(X,e)
называется топологическая группа, содержащая

как подпространство, в которой

является единицей, и такая, что всякое непрерывное отображение

в топологическую группу
K
с
f(e)=e
однозначно продолжается до непрерывного гомоморфизма
\hat{f}\colon F(X,e)\to K

[

|y_{i}|=0
и
\widetilde{h}(z_{i})=h(|z_{i}|)^{\pm 1}=e=\widetilde{h_{\gamma}}(y_{i})
. Если
y_{i}=t^{\sigma}\neq e
, то по (c)
z_{i}=s^{\sigma}
с
s=|z_{i}|
, и
\widetilde{h}(z_{i})=h(s)^{\sigma}=(\gamma t)^{\sigma}=\widetilde{h_{\gamma}}(y_{i})
. Из (
1
) получаем
\hat{h}(z)=\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y)
.

По лемме
3.1
(с
L=1
)
\hat{h}(z)\in H_{c_{1}}
, т.е.
\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y)\in H_{c_{1}}
. Пусть
r(t)=\min(t,\gamma)/\gamma
. Тогда
r\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
r(0)=0
,
|r(s)-r(t)|\leq\gamma^{-1}|s-t|
и
r\circ h_{\gamma}=\mathrm{id}_{[0,1]}
, так что
\hat{r}\circ\widehat{h_{\gamma}}=\mathrm{id}
. По лемме
3.1
(с
L=\gamma^{-1}
) получаем
y=\hat{r}(\widehat{h_{\gamma}}(y))\in H_{c_{1}/\gamma}\subset H_{c_{2}}
, поскольку
c_{1}/\gamma<c_{2}
.
∎

Доказательство предложения
2.1
.

Индукцией по
\alpha\leq\omega_{1}
докажем утверждение:
[H_{c}]_{\alpha}\subset H_{c^{\prime}}
для всех
0<c<c^{\prime}
. При
\alpha=0
это очевидно. Пусть
\alpha=\beta+1
и
0<c<c^{\prime}
; положим
c^{\prime\prime}=(c+c^{\prime})/2
. По предположению индукции
[H_{c}]_{\beta}\subset H_{c^{\prime\prime}}
, и по лемме
3.5

[H_{c}]_{\beta+1}=\bigl[[H_{c}]_{\beta}\bigr]_{1}\subset[H_{c^{\prime\prime}}]_{1}\subset H_{c^{\prime}}.

Для предельного
\alpha
утверждение следует из определения
[H_{c}]_{\alpha}
. При
\alpha=\omega_{1}
из леммы
3.4
получаем
\overline{H_{c_{1}}}=[H_{c_{1}}]_{\omega_{1}}\subset H_{c_{2}}
.
∎

\mathbf{1}^{n}

4.1
Системы периодов

Пусть
\Phi
— свободная группа с базисом
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
, и
\operatorname{ab}\colon\Phi\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
— гомоморфизм абелианизации,
\operatorname{ab}(g_{i})=\mathbf{e}_{i}
, где
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{m}
— стандартный базис
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. Положим

W=\{b\in\Phi:\ \text{хотя бы одна координата вектора }\operatorname{ab}(b)\text{ равна }0\}.

Пусть
B(m,n)=\Phi/R
— свободная бернсайдова группа, где
R
— нормальная подгруппа, порождённая
n
-ми степенями всех элементов
\Phi
.

Определение 4.1
.

Пусть
m\geq 2
. Множество слов
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\Phi
назовём
системой периодов
для
B(m,n)
, если выполнены условия:

(O1)

каждый элемент
\Phi
сопряжён по модулю
R
со степенью некоторого
A_{i}
, т.е. для любого
w\in\Phi
существуют
u\in\Phi
,
i\in I
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
и
r\in R
такие, что
w=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
;

(O2)

R/[\Phi,R]
— свободная абелева группа с базисом
\{A_{i}^{n}[\Phi,R]\}_{i\in I}
.

Алгебраическая часть доказательства сводится к следующей лемме и к существованию систем периодов.

Лемма 4.2
.

Пусть
n
нечётно,
m\geq 2
и для
B(m,n)
существует система периодов. Тогда
(g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{m})^{n}\notin\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
.

Доказательство.

Пусть
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
— система периодов для
B(m,n)
. Положим
a=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}
; тогда
\operatorname{ab}(a)=(1,\dots,1)
.

По (O1)
a=uA_{i_{0}}^{l_{0}}u^{-1}r
для некоторых
u\in\Phi
,
i_{0}\in I
,
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
r\in R
. Так как
\operatorname{ab}(R)\subset n\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, имеем
(1,\dots,1)\equiv l_{0}\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\pmod{n}
. Отсюда
\gcd(l_{0},n)=1
: если простое
p
делит
l_{0}
и
n
, то
\frac{n}{p}(1,\dots,1)\equiv\frac{l_{0}}{p}\,n\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv 0\pmod{n}
, что неверно. Пусть
l_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
l_{0}l_{0}^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
; тогда
\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv l_{0}^{\prime}(1,\dots,1)\pmod{n}
.

Положим
\bar{\Phi}=\Phi/[\Phi,R]
и
\bar{R}=R/[\Phi,R]
; подгруппа
\bar{R}
центральна в
\bar{\Phi}
. Образ элемента
w\in\Phi
в
\bar{\Phi}
будем обозначать той же буквой. По (O2) существует гомоморфизм
\psi\colon\bar{R}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
такой, что
\psi(A_{i_{0}}^{n})=1
и
\psi(A_{i}^{n})=0
при
i\neq i_{0}
.

Пусть
w\in\Phi
и
w=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
, где
u\in\Phi
,
r\in R
. Так как
r
и
A_{i}^{n}
лежат в центре
\bar{\Phi}
, в
\bar{\Phi}
имеем

w^{n}=(uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1})^{n}r^{n}=uA_{i}^{ln}u^{-1}r^{n}=A_{i}^{ln}r^{n}\in\bar{R},

и, следовательно,
\psi(w^{n})=l\,\delta_{i,i_{0}}\bmod n
(так как
\psi(r^{n})=n\psi(r)=0
).

В частности,
\psi(a^{n})=l_{0}\bmod n\neq 0
.

Пусть теперь
b\in W
и
b=uA_{i}^{l}u^{-1}r
. Если
i=i_{0}
и
n\nmid l
, то
\operatorname{ab}(b)\equiv l\,\operatorname{ab}(A_{i_{0}})\equiv l\,l_{0}^{\prime}(1,\dots,1)\pmod{n}
, и все координаты
\operatorname{ab}(b)
сравнимы с
l\,l_{0}^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{n}
(так как
n\nmid l
и
\gcd(l_{0}^{\prime},n)=1
); это противоречит тому, что одна из координат
\operatorname{ab}(b)
равна нулю. Значит,
i\neq i_{0}
или
n\mid l
, и в обоих случаях
\psi(b^{n})=0
.

Образы всех элементов
b^{n}
,
b\in W
, лежат в абелевой группе
\bar{R}
и аннулируются гомоморфизмом
\psi
; следовательно,
\psi
аннулирует образ подгруппы
\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
. Так как
\psi(a^{n})\neq 0
, получаем
a^{n}\notin\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
.
∎

4.5
Весовая норма и доказательство предложения
2.2

Лемма 4.6
.

Пусть
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}=1
,
w_{k}=t_{k}-t_{k-1}
, и на
\mathbb{R}^{m}
задана норма
\|v\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}|v_{k}|
. Пусть
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
f(t)_{k}=\min\bigl(\max\bigl((t-t_{k-1})/w_{k},0\bigr),1\bigr)
, и
\hat{f}\colon F([0,1],0)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
— гомоморфизм, продолжающий
f
. Тогда
\|\hat{f}(g)\|\leq N(g)
для
g\in F([0,1],0)
, и
f(t_{k})=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{k}
для
k=0,1,\dots,m
.

Доказательство.

Равенство
f(t_{k})=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{k}
очевидно, в частности
f(0)=0
. Пусть
s<t
. Все координаты
f
не убывают, и
w_{k}\bigl(f(t)_{k}-f(s)_{k}\bigr)
равно длине отрезка
[s,t]\cap[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Поэтому
\|f(t)-f(s)\|=t-s
. Так как
\|\cdot\|
— инвариантная норма на абелевой группе
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, утверждение следует из следствия
1.5
(с
L=1
).
∎

Доказательство предложения
2.2
.

Предположим, что
\mathbf{1}^{n}\in H_{1}
. Как в доказательстве леммы
3.1
,
\mathbf{1}^{n}=x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{k}^{n}
, где
N(x_{j})<1
. Пусть
T=\{0,1\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{supp}x_{j}
и
T=\{t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}\}
; тогда
t_{0}=0
,
t_{m}=1
,
m\geq 1
, и все
x_{j}
лежат в
F(T,0)
— свободной группе с базисом
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
. Элементы
g_{i}=t_{i-1}^{-1}t_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
(где
t_{0}=e
), также образуют базис
F(T,0)
, так как
t_{i}=g_{1}\cdots g_{i}
. В частности,
\mathbf{1}=t_{m}=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}
. Отождествим
F(T,0)
со свободной группой
\Phi
из леммы
4.5
посредством базиса
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
.

Пусть
f
и
\hat{f}
— как в лемме
4.6
. Тогда
\hat{f}(g_{i})=f(t_{i})-f(t_{i-1})=\mathbf{e}_{i}
, так что ограничение
\hat{f}
на

совпадает с гомоморфизмом абелианизации
\operatorname{ab}\colon F(T,0)\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
относительно базиса
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
. По лемме
4.6

\|\operatorname{ab}(x_{j})\|\leq N(x_{j})<1=\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}

\operatorname{ab}(x_{j})
целые, а при всех ненулевых координатах было бы

, хотя бы одна координата
\operatorname{ab}(x_{j})
равна нулю, т.е.

. Тогда
(g_{1}\cdots g_{m})^{n}=\mathbf{1}^{n}=x_{1}^{n}\cdots x_{k}^{n}\in\langle\{b^{n}:b\in W\}\rangle
, что противоречит лемме
4.5

∎

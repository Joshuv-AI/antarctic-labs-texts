Abstract
. The chromatic symmetric function (CSF) of Dyck paths of Stanley and its Shareshian–Wachs q q -analogue have important connections to Hessenberg varieties, diagonal harmonics and LLT polynomials. In the, so called, abelian case they are also curiously related to placements of non-attacking rooks by results of Stanley–Stembridge (1993) and Guay-Paquet (2013). For the q q -analogue, these results have been generalized by Abreu–Nigro (2020) and Guay-Paquet (private communication), using q q -hit numbers. Among our main results is a new proof of Guay-Paquet’s elegant identity expressing the q q -CSFs in a CSF basis with q q -hit coefficients. We further show its equivalence to the Abreu–Nigro identity expanding the q q -CSF in the elementary symmetric functions. In the course of our work we establish that the q q -hit numbers in these expansions differ from the originally assumed Garsia-Remmel q q -hit numbers by certain powers of q q . We prove new identities for these q q -hit numbers, and establish connections between the three different variants.

2.1.

Rook placements are a generalizations of permutation diagrams, and their

Definition 2.1

(

-rook numbers
[

]
)

Given a partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
the

-rook numbers

R_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{p}q^{\inv(p)},

where the sum is over all placements

k

\lambda

\lambda

Proposition 2.2

(Garsia-Remmel
[
GR86
]
)

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})

F(x;\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}R_{k}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-k}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[x+\lambda_{\ell-i+1}-i+1],

Proposition 2.16

(2.7)

\displaystyle H_{k}^{N}(m^{j})=q^{(N-j-m+k)k}\left[m\right]_{k}\left[N-j\right]!\dfrac{\left[N-m\right]_{j-k}\left[j\right]_{j-k}}{\left[j-k\right]!}.

\displaystyle H_{k}^{N}(m^{j})=R_{k}(m^{k})\cdot R_{j-k}((N-m)^{j})\cdot R_{N-j}((N-j)^{N-j}).

cancel as many columns in the shape
N^{N-j}
consisting of the last
N-j
rows of the board
B
.

By Proposition
2.2
, the overall contribution to the
q
-hit number from placing the remaining
N-j
rooks in the remaining shape
(N-j)^{N-j}
is
R_{N-j}((N-j)^{N-j})=[N-j]!
. This proves the claim and the result follows by using the formula in (
2.6
) for
R_{k}(a^{b})
.
∎

Proposition 2.17
.

H_{r}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=\begin{cases}q^{k}[m-k][m-1]_{n-1}&r=k,\\
[k][m-1]_{n-1}&r=k-1,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since every row of the
n\times m
board has a rook and the last column has at most one rook then the rooks can only “hit” the shape
(m-1)^{k}

r=k
or
r=k-1
times.

When
r=k
, the first
k
cells of the last column are not cancelled in any rook placement so they contribute to
\stat(\cdot)
. The contribution to the
q
-hit number from the
k
rooks placed on the shape
(m-1)^{k}
is
R_{k}((m-1)^{k})
. These rooks cancel
k
columns in the shape of the last
n-k
rows. Then the contribution to the
q
-hit number of placing
n-k
rooks on the remaining shape
(m-k)^{n-k}
is
R_{n-k}((m-k)^{n-k})
. See Figure
3C
. Thus,

H_{k}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=q^{k}R_{k}((m-1)^{k})\cdot R_{n-k}((m-k)^{n-k})=q^{k}[m-1]_{k}[m-k]_{n-k}=q^{k}[m-k][m-1]_{n-1}.

When
r=k-1
, there is a rook on one of the first
k
cells of the
m
th column which cancels all the cells of its corresponding row
i=1,\ldots,k
and the cells below the rook in its column. This rook contributes
i-1
to the statistic (the cells above the rook in the
m
th column). There contribution to the
q
-hit number from the
k-1
rooks placed on the remaining shape
(m-1)^{k-1}
(without row
i
) is
R_{k-1}((m-1)^{k-1})
. The
k
rooks on the first
k
rows cancel the cells of their columns in the shape of the last
n-k
rows. Then the contribution to the
q
-hit number of placing
n-k
rooks on the remaining shape
(m-k)^{n-k}
is
R_{n-k}((m-k)^{n-k})
. See Figure
3D
. Summing over all
i=1,\ldots,k
we obtain that

H_{k-1}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=[k]R_{k-1}((m-1)^{k-1})\cdot R_{n-k}((m-k)^{n-k})=[k][m-1]_{k-1}[m-k]_{n-k}=[k][m-1]_{n-1}.

∎

3.
New
q
-rook and
q
-hit identities

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
rely on many new identities between
q
-hit numbers, and their equivalent
q
-rook versions. While the
q
-hit and
q
-rook identities are independent of the chromatic symmetric function theory, we first prove them using elementary combinatorial methods and univariate generating functions.

For brevity, we will denote by
{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j}
the partition obtained from
\lambda
by removing its
j
th column, by
{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i}
the partition obtained by removing its
i
th row, and by
\lambda/(i,j)
the partition obtained from
\lambda
by removing its
j
th column and its
i
th row. Moreover, we denote
\ell=\ell(\lambda)
and the conjugate partition of
\lambda
by
\lambda^{\prime}
.

3.1.
Identities on
q
-rook numbers

The following
q
-rook identities can be proven directly using the generating function identity in Proposition
2.2
of Garsia-Remmel. We start with a simple algebraic identity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
,

q^{\lambda_{1}}[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}.

Proof.

We use induction on
\ell(\lambda)
and apply Proposition
2.2
. For
\ell(\lambda)=1
, we have

[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-1]+\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{-j}-q^{-j}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-1]}{[x+\lambda_{1}]}=q^{-\lambda_{1}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-1]}{[x+\lambda_{1}]}([x+\lambda_{1}]-[\lambda_{1}]).

Next, expanding the RHS of the above identity and doing standard manipulations gives

[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}=\\
=q^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-\ell]}{[x+\lambda_{1}-\ell+1]}\left([x+\lambda_{2}-(\ell-1)]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{2}}q^{\lambda_{2}-j}\prod_{t=2}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\right).

By induction hypothesis the parenthetical on the RHS above is
q^{\lambda_{2}}[x]F(x-1;\tilde{\lambda})/F(x;\tilde{\lambda})
where
\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
. Using
\tilde{\lambda}_{t}=\lambda_{t+1}
for the reindexing, we obtain the result.
∎

Next, we find the following
q
-analogue of the derivative of
F(x;\lambda)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have the following formula for the
q
-analogue of the derivative of
F(x;\lambda)
:

DF(x;\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}[k]R_{k}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-k}=q^{\ell-x}([x]F(x-1;\lambda)-[x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)).

Proof.

Following the notation of Proposition
2.2
,

F(x;\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}R_{k}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-k}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[x+\lambda_{\ell-i+1}-i+1].

Note that

\displaystyle[x]_{\ell-k}-[x-1]_{\ell-k}

\displaystyle=[x-1]_{\ell-k-1}\left(\frac{1-q^{x}-1+q^{x-\ell+k}}{1-q}\right)=\frac{[x]_{\ell-k}}{[x]}\frac{q^{x-\ell}(q^{k}-q^{\ell})}{1-q}

\displaystyle=\frac{[x]_{\ell-k}}{[x]}q^{x-\ell}([\ell]-[k]).

Apply this identity to each term in the following difference

\displaystyle F(x;\lambda)-F(x-1;\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}R_{k}(\lambda)\frac{[x]_{\ell-k}}{[x]}q^{x-\ell}([\ell]-[k]),

so that

\displaystyle[x](F(x;\lambda)-F(x-1;\lambda))=q^{x-\ell}[\ell]F(x;\lambda)-q^{x-\ell}DF(x;\lambda),

where
DF(x;\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}[k]R_{k}(\lambda)[x]_{l-k}
. This gives an equation for
DF(x;\lambda)
which we solve as

DF(x;\lambda)=-q^{\ell-x}([x]-q^{x-\ell}[\ell])F(x;\lambda)+q^{\ell-x}[x]F(x-1;\lambda)=q^{\ell-x}([x]F(x-1;\lambda)-[x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)),

giving us the desired formula.
∎

The following result shows the relationship between
q
-rook numbers for partitions obtained from deleting a column of
\lambda
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
i
fixed,

\sum_{j}q^{m-j}R_{i}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=R_{i}(\lambda)[m-i]-R_{i+1}(\lambda)(q^{m}-q^{m-i-1}).

Proof.

Multiplying on both sides by
[x]_{\ell-i}
, the above claim is equivalent to the generating function identity:

\sum_{j}q^{m-j}F(x;{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=\sum_{i}(R_{i}(\lambda)[m-i]-R_{i+1}(\lambda)(q^{m}-q^{m-i-1}))[x]_{\ell-i}\\
=\sum_{i}R_{i}(\lambda)\left([m-i][x]_{\ell-i}-(q^{m}-q^{m-i})[x]_{\ell-i+1}\right)=\sum_{i}R_{i}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-i}[m+x-\ell]-q^{m}[x][x-1]_{\ell-i}\\
=[m+x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)-q^{m}[x]F(x-1;\lambda),

where we use the observation that

[m-i][x]_{\ell-i}-(q^{m}-q^{m-i})[x]_{\ell-i+1}=[x]_{\ell-i}\frac{(1-q^{m-i})-(q^{m}-q^{m-i})(1-q^{x-\ell+i})}{1-q}\\
=[x]_{\ell-i}\frac{1-q^{m-i}-q^{m}+q^{m-i}+q^{m+x-\ell+i}-q^{m+x-\ell}}{1-q}=[x]_{\ell-i}[x+m-\ell]-q^{m}[x]_{\ell-i+1}.

We have that

F(x;{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}[x+\lambda_{i}-1-\ell+i]\prod_{i=\lambda^{\prime}_{j}+1}^{\ell}[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]=F(x;\lambda)\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{[x-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}{[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}.

Using Lemma
3.1
and that
{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j}=\lambda
for
j>\lambda_{1}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}F(x;{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=F(x;\lambda)\left([m-\lambda_{1}]+q^{m-\lambda_{1}}\sum_{j}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{[x-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}{[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}\right)\\
=F(x;\lambda)\left([m-\lambda_{1}]+q^{m-\lambda_{1}}[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-q^{m-\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}}[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}\right)\\
=F(x;\lambda)[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}+m-\lambda_{1}]-q^{m}[x]F(x-1;\lambda),

which is what we wanted to show and completes the proof.
∎

We also have the following relationship between
q
-rook numbers obtained from removing a single row from
\lambda
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all fixed
k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{i-1+\lambda_{i}}R_{k}({\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})=[n]R_{k}(\lambda)-[k]R_{k}(\lambda).

Proof.

First of all, notice that we can replace
n
with
\ell
since for
i>\ell
,
{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i}=\lambda
,
\lambda_{i}=0
and
[n]-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{n}q^{i-1}=[\ell]
.
Multiplying by
[y]_{\ell-k}=[y][y-1]_{(\ell-1)-k}
on both sides and summing over all
k
, the identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle[y]\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q^{i-1+\lambda_{i}}\sum_{k}R_{k}({\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})[y-1]_{(\ell-1)-k}=[n]\sum_{k}R_{k}(\lambda)[y]_{\ell-k}-\sum_{k}[k]R_{k}(\lambda)[y]_{\ell-k}.

Thus, the identity is then equivalent to the generating function identity

[y]\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q^{i-1+\lambda_{i}}F(y-1,{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})\\
=[\ell]F(y;\lambda)-DF(y;\lambda)=[\ell]F(y,\lambda)+q^{-y+\ell}[y-\ell]F(y,\lambda)-q^{\ell-y}[y]F(y-1,\lambda).

We have that for
i\leq\ell

F(y-1,{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}[y-1+({\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})_{j}-(\ell-1)+i]=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell+1+j]\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}[y-1+\lambda_{j}-(\ell-1)+j-1]\\
=\frac{1}{[y-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell]}F(y-1,\lambda),

and so we reach the following equivalent identity

[y]\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{q^{y-\ell+i-1+\lambda_{i}}}{[y-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell]}F(y-1,\lambda)\\
=q^{y-\ell}[\ell]F(y,\lambda)+[y-\ell]F(y,\lambda)-[y]F(y-1,\lambda).

We can rewrite it as

[y]\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{[y-\ell+i+\lambda_{i}]-[y-1-\ell+i+\lambda_{i}]}{[y-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}F(y-1,\lambda)\\
=q^{y-\ell}[\ell]F(y,\lambda)+[y-\ell]F(y,\lambda)-[y]F(y-1,\lambda),

which reduces again to

\displaystyle[y]F(y-1,\lambda)\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}-\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}\right)=[y]F(y,\lambda)-[y]F(y-1,\lambda).

After canceling the terms in the telescoping sum on the LHS, the identity reduces to

[y]F(y-1,\lambda)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{[y+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}{[y-1+\lambda_{j}-\ell+j]}-1\right)-[y]F(y,\lambda)+[y]F(y-1,\lambda)\\
=[y](F(y,\lambda)-F(y-1,\lambda))-[y]F(y,\lambda)+[y]F(y-1,\lambda)=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

We have the following identity for
q
-rook numbers:

\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}R_{k}(\lambda/(i,j))=q[k+1]R_{k+1}(\lambda).

Proof.

Translating this identity into generating functions, we have that it is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}R_{k}(\lambda/(i,j))[x]_{\ell-1-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}q[k+1]R_{k+1}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-k-1}=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}q[t]R_{t}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-t},

which can be rewritten, with the help of Lemma
3.2
, in terms of the
F
function as

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}F(x;\lambda/(i,j))=qDF(x;\lambda)=-q^{\ell-x+1}[x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)+q^{1+\ell-x}[x]F(x-1;\lambda).

Now, we notice that if
\mu=\lambda/(i,j)

\displaystyle F(x;\lambda/(i,j))

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\ell-1}[x+\mu_{t}-\ell+1+t]

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{i-1}[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+1+t]\prod_{t=i}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-1}[x+\lambda_{t+1}-1-\ell+1+t]\prod_{t=\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}[x+\lambda_{t+1}-\ell+1+t]

\displaystyle=F(x;\lambda)\frac{\prod_{t=i+1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{\prod_{t=i}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}.

Note that
q^{x-1-\ell+i+\lambda_{i}}=[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]-[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i-1]
, so we can rewrite this as

q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}F(x;\lambda/(i,j))\\
=q^{-x+1+\ell-j}F(x;\lambda)([x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]-[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i-1])\frac{\prod_{t=i+1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{\prod_{t=i}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\\
=q^{-x+1+\ell-j}F(x;\lambda)\left(\prod_{t=i+1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}-\prod_{t=i}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\right).

Fixing
j
and summing over all possible
i=1\ldots\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
we get telescoping cancellations and so

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}F(x;\lambda/(i,j))=\sum_{j}q^{-x+1+\ell-j}F(x;\lambda)\left(1-\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\right).

Substituting this into the LHS of (
3.1
), the needed identity transforms to the equivalent

\displaystyle\sum_{j}q^{1-j+\ell-x}F(x;\lambda)\left(1-\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\right)

\displaystyle=qDF(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle=q^{\ell-x+1}\left([x]F(x-1;\lambda)-[x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)\right),

which is equivalent to

[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-\ell]+\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{-j}-q^{-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}.

This last identity simplifies as

q^{\lambda_{1}}[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]},

and is equivalent to the formula in Lemma
3.1
, which completes the proof. ∎

3.2.
q
-hit identities

We now translate the above rook identities into
q
-hit identities using the relationship from Definition
2.3
. Let

G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)(q^{x})^{k}.

Then we have the following equivalences in terms of the generating functions

(3.2)

\displaystyle G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\dfrac{q^{\binom{k}{2}-|\lambda|}}{\left[m-n\right]!}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!\begin{bmatrix}i\\
k\end{bmatrix}(-1)^{i+k}q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}}q^{xk}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{-|\lambda|}}{\left[m-n\right]!}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}}(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i}q^{\binom{k}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}i\\
k\end{bmatrix}(-q^{x})^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{-|\lambda|}}{\left[m-n\right]!}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}}(-1)^{i}\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x+k}).

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
\lambda
inside an
n\times m
board and for every
k

\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}H_{k}^{m-1,n}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=[m-n]H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{n-k}.

Proof.

The collection of identities for
k=0,\ldots,n
is equivalent to the following generating function identities

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}H_{k}^{m-1,n}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})q^{xk}\\
=[m-n]\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{n-k}q^{xk}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}G^{m-1,n}(x;{\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=q^{n}[m-n]G^{m,n}(x-1;\lambda).

Using (
3.2
) and expanding in the
q^{x}
-polynomial basis
(q^{x};q)_{k}=\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x+k})
for
i=0,\ldots,n
, the
G
-identity is equivalent to the following rook identity for every
i
:

\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{q^{-|\lambda|+\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}}{\left[m-1-n\right]!}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})\left[m-1-i\right]!q^{mi-i-\binom{i}{2}}(-1)^{i}\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x+k})\\
=q^{n}[m-n]\frac{q^{-|\lambda|}}{\left[m-n\right]!}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}}(-1)^{i}\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x-1+k}),

which simplifies as

\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})\left[m-1-i\right]!q^{mi-i-\binom{i}{2}}(-1)^{i}\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x+k})\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}-i}(-1)^{i}q^{i}\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}(1-q^{x-1+k}).

Notice that

q^{i}(q^{x-1};q)_{i}=(q^{i}-q^{x+i-1})(q^{x};q)_{i-1}=(q^{i}-1)(q^{x};q)_{i-1}+(q^{x};q)_{i},

and so the RHS above expands in the
(q^{x};q)_{i}
basis as:

\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i+1}{2}}(-1)^{i}((q^{i}-1)(q^{x};q)_{i-1}+(q^{x};q)_{i})\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(q^{x};q)_{i}\left(R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i+1}{2}}-R_{i+1}(\lambda)\left[m-i-1\right]!q^{mi+m-\binom{i+2}{2}}(q^{i+1}-1)\right).

Therefore, this is equivalent to the following
q
-rook identity for every
i
:

\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}R_{i}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})\left[m-1-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i+1}{2}}\\
=\left(R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!q^{mi-\binom{i+1}{2}}-R_{i+1}(\lambda)\left[m-i-1\right]!q^{mi+m-\binom{i+2}{2}}(q^{i+1}-1)\right).

Simplifying last expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}R_{i}({\lambda}/^{c}\,{j})=R_{i}(\lambda)[m-i]-R_{i+1}(\lambda)(q^{m}-q^{m-i-1}),

which is exactly Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\leq n\leq m
be fixed, and
\lambda\subset n\times m
. We have the following
q
-hit identity:

[m-n+1]\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{i-1}H_{k}^{m,n-1}({\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})=H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{k}[n-k]+H_{k+1}^{m,n}(\lambda)[k+1].

Proof.

Multiplying both sides by
(q^{x})^{k}
and summing over all
k
, the identity becomes equivalent to the following generating function identity:

\displaystyle[m-n+1]\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{i-1}G^{m,n-1}(x;{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)(q^{x+1})^{k}[n-k]+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k+1}^{m,n}(\lambda)[k+1]q^{xk}.

To prove the above identity, we will use the following difference operator and its properties:

\Delta F(x):=\frac{F(x+1)-F(x)}{q^{x}(q-1)},\quad\Delta(q^{x})^{p}=[p](q^{x})^{p-1},\quad\Delta(q^{x},q)_{p}=-[p](q^{x+1},q)_{p-1}=-\frac{[p](q^{x};q)_{p}}{1-x}.

Notice also that
q^{k}[n-k]=[n]-[k]
and
[k+1]q^{xk}=\Delta q^{x(k+1)}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)\left(q^{x+1}\right)^{k}[n-k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{xk}[n]-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)[k]q^{xk}

\displaystyle=[n]G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)-q^{x}\Delta G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k+1}^{m,n}(\lambda)[k+1]q^{xk}

\displaystyle=\Delta G^{m,n}(x;\lambda).

Thus, the generating function identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle[m-n+1]\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{i-1}G^{m,n-1}(x;{\lambda}/^{r}\,{i})=[n]G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)-q^{x}\Delta G^{m,n}(x;\lambda)+\Delta G^{m,n}(x;\lambda).

Using the formula

Lemma 4.3

For

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}^{m+n}(M)=

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}^{m+n}(M-1)+x_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m+n}q^{m+n-i-\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}X^{m+n-1}_{\lambda/i}(M-1)

\displaystyle+x_{M}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-1+(m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime})}X^{m+n-2}_{\lambda/(i,j)}(M-1).

In the abelian case, i.e. when

\{1,\ldots,m\}

\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}

\lambda

\{1,\ldots,M\}

1.

No vertex is colored

X_{\lambda}^{m+n-1}(M-1)

2.

Only one vertex is colored
M
. Suppose this vertex is in column

(from left) and row

(from top to bottom). It creates ascents with all vertices above it but not in
\lambda
, giving

\lambda

\lambda/j
(deleting either row

\lambda

j
from
\lambda
). The remaining vertices and their ascents are not affected by this, so all their possible colorings contribute

3.

Two vertices are colored

. Suppose that the lower one is in column

and the higher one is in row

(counting from the top), necessarily with
(i,j)\in\lambda
. The lower vertex contributes

ascents. We can remove these two vertices, by removing row

and column

\lambda

6.
Variations and applications

6.1.
Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
in terms of unicellular LLT polynomials

Chromatic symmetric functions of Dyck paths are related to
unicellular LLT polynomials

[
LLT97
]
that can be defined as follows. For a Dyck path
d
, let
G(d)
be the associated graph and denote

\displaystyle\LLT_{G(d)}({\bf x},q):=\sum_{\kappa:V(G(d))\to\mathbb{P}}q^{\asc(\kappa)}{\bf x}^{\kappa},

where the sum is over all vertex colorings
\kappa
of
G(d)
and
\asc(\kappa)
is the same as in the definition of
X_{G}({\bf x},q)
.

The function
\LLT_{G(d)}({\bf x},q)
is actually symmetric (see
[
AP18
, Sec. 3.1]
) and the symmetric functions
X_{G(d)}({\bf x},q)
and
\LLT_{d}({\bf x},q)
are related via a plethystic substitution discovered independently by Carlsson–Mellit
[
CM18
, Prop. 3.4]
and Guay-Paquet
[
GPc
, Lemma 172]
:

\LLT_{G(d)}({\bf x},q)=(q-1)^{n}X_{G(d)}[{\bf x}/(q-1),q],

where
n
is the size of the Dyck path. As a consequence of this connection, any linear relation in one family implies the same relation in the other one. Since Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
yield a linear relation among certain chromatic symmetric functions, we immediately obtain the same relations for the corresponding unicellular LLT polynomials.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\lambda
be partition inside an
n\times m
board with
\ell(\lambda)=k\leq\lambda_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\LLT_{G(\lambda)}({\bf x},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[m+n-k]!}H_{k}^{m+n-k}(\lambda)\cdot\LLT_{K(m+n-k,k)}({\bf x},q)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q^{j}\frac{\left[m+n-2j\right]}{[m+n-j]!}H_{j}^{m+n-j-1}(\lambda)\cdot\LLT_{K(m+n-j,j)}({\bf x},q),

where
K(a,b)
is the disjoint union of complete graphs on vertices
\{1,\ldots,a\}
and
\{a+1,\ldots,a+b\}
.

Proof.

Since
X_{K(a,b)}({\bf x},q)=[a]![b]!e_{a,b}
, then Theorem
1.2
yields a linear relation among
X_{G(\lambda)}({\bf x},q)
,
X_{K(m+n,0)}({\bf x},q),\ldots,X_{K(m+n-k,k)}({\bf x},q)
. The result then follows from the fact that
every linear relation among a set of chromatic symmetric functions of Dyck paths has a corresponding relation among unicellular LLT polynomials
[
AP18
, Prop. 55]
.
∎

.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an

Theorem

Into:

is the number of cells that have double crossings after the rook cancellations used to obtain
\stat(p)
. See Remark
2.8
and Figure
7B
.

Example I.5
.

For the rook placement
p
in Example
I.3
and Figure
7B
we have that
\stat(p)=11
and
\cross(p)=4
.

First observe that for a rook placement
p
on the
m\times m
board we have that
\dstat(p)=\cross(p)
, since the rays in
\stat
and
\dstat
are complementary to each other and the crossings in one directly correspond to the empty boxes in the other. Therefore Lemma
I.2
follows from the next lemma.

Lemma I.6
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an
m\times m
Given a placement
p
of
m
non-attacking rooks on an
m\times m
board with
j
rooks inside
\lambda
then

(I.2)

\stat(p)-\cross(p)=j\cdot m-|\lambda|.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|\lambda|
for
\lambda\subset m\times m
. When
\lambda=\emptyset
we only have rook placements for
j=0
, and then it is clear that,

\stat(p)=\dstat(p)=\cross(p)=\inv(w),

the number of inversions of the permutation
w
corresponding to the rook diagram. Thus the identity (
I.2
) is verified.

Suppose the identity (
I.2
) holds for all
|\lambda|\leq N
and then for any
j=0,\ldots,m
. Let
\nu
be a partition of
N+1
and
\nu=\lambda+e
, where
e
is a corner cell. Let
p
be a rook configuration with
j
rooks in
\nu
, and let
p^{\prime}
be the same rook configuration on
\lambda
(so there are
j
or
j-1
rooks in
\lambda
).
Note that cell
e
cannot be empty since there is a rook in its row, which is either in
\nu
, and hence the rook’s “arm” crosses
e
or is outside in which case the wrap-around also crosses
e
. We now consider several cases:

Case 1.

Cell
e
has a rook and hence the horizontal arm stops at
e
as the border of
\nu
. Thus there are no crossings in the row of
e
. Suppose there are
k
empty boxes in the row of
e
, then there are also
m-k-1
vertical crossings in this row. Now consider
p^{\prime}
as a configuration with
j-1
rooks in
\lambda
. The rook in
e
is outside
\lambda
and this time the entire row is crossed, so all empty cells have now a horizontal line and all vertically crossed cells have now a double crossing. Thus

\qquad\qquad\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})=\stat(p)-k-(\cross(p)+m-k-1)=\stat(p)-\cross(p)-m+1.

By induction we have

\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})=m(j-1)-|\lambda|=j\dot{m}-|\nu|-m+1,

and matching sides we obtain the desired identity in this case.

Case 2.

The rook in the row of
e
is to the left of
e
. Then either
e
is a double crossing or is only a horizontal crossing. Then in the rook placement
p^{\prime}
there is no horizontal line crossing
e
. If
e
was a double crossing in
p
, then it is neither a double crossing nor empty cell in
p^{\prime}
, and if
e
was not a double crossing in
p
then it became an empty cell in
p^{\prime}
. In both cases we have

\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})=\stat(p)-\cross(p)+1.

Since the number of rooks inside
\lambda
is still
j
then we have

\stat(p)-\cross(p)=\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})-1=j\cdot m-|\lambda|-1=j\cdot m-|\nu|.

This gives the desired identity in this case.

Case 3.

The rook in the row of
e
is to right of
e
, so outside
\nu
. Then again there is a horizontal line crossing
e
, so
e
is either a double crossing in
p
or neither a double crossing nor an empty cell in
p
. In both cases when we remove
e
from
\nu
we either turn the double crossing on
e
in
p
to a not a double crossing in
p^{\prime}
or from not a double crossing in
e
to an empty cell in
p^{\prime}
. Thus, again

\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})=\stat(p)-\cross(p)+1.

Since the number of rooks inside
\lambda
is still
j
then we have

\stat(p)-\cross(p)=\stat(p^{\prime})-\cross(p^{\prime})-1=j\cdot m-|\lambda|-1=j\cdot m-|\nu|.

This gives the desired identity in this case.

This completes the proof.
∎

Symmetry of

-hit numbers of rectangular boards

Since the Garsia–Remmel

\mathbb{N}[q]

[
GR86
,
Dwo98
,
Hag98
]
, then so are
H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)
.

Corollary J.1
.

The
q
-hit numbers
H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)
are symmetric polynomials in
\mathbb{N}[q]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.9
we have that
H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)
are in
\mathbb{N}[q]
. By Lemma
2.13
and Proposition
H.1
we have that

H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{[m-n]!}q^{|jm-\lambda|}\widetilde{H}_{j}^{m}(\lambda).

Now

and so are the Garsia–Remmel
q
-hit numbers
\widetilde{H}_{j}^{m}(\lambda)

[

In this section we give a proof of the deletion-contraction relations for the

Lemma K.1

(
[

, Thm. 6.11]

\lambda

n\times m

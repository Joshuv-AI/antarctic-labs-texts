A Reilly type integral formula and its applications Thanks: The research of authors is supported by NSFC(No. 11971153).
Guangyue Huang, Bingqing Ma, Mingfang Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we achieve a Reilly type integral formula associated with the ϕ \phi -Laplacian. As its applications, we obtain Heintze-Karcher and Minkowski type inequalities. Furthermore, almost Schur lemmas are also given. They recover the partial results of Li and Xia in [ 15 ] . On the other hand, we also study eigenvalue problem for Wentzell boundary conditions and obtain eigenvalue relationships.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with the dimension
n\geq 3
, where
g
is the metric.
The
\phi
-Laplacian associated with
\phi
is defined by

\Delta_{\phi}v=e^{\phi}{\rm div}(e^{-\phi}\nabla v)=\Delta v-\nabla\phi\nabla v,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ v\in C^{\infty}(M),

which is symmetric with respect to the
L^{2}(M)
inner product under the weighted measure

d\mu=e^{-\phi}dv_{g},

that is,

\int_{M}\alpha\Delta_{\phi}\beta\,d\mu=\int_{M}\beta\Delta_{\phi}\alpha\,d\mu=-\int_{M}\nabla\alpha\nabla\beta\,d\mu,\ \ \ \forall\ \alpha,\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M).

Following
[
1
,
14
,
23
]
(or see
[
13
,
8
,
10
,
9
]
and the references therein), the
m
-dimensional Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature associated
with the above
\phi
-Laplacian is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}_{\phi,m}={\rm Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{m-n}d\phi\otimes d\phi,

(1.1)

where
m
is a real constant, and
m=n
if and only if
\phi
is a constant. Define

{\rm Ric}_{\phi}={\rm Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi.

Then
{\rm Ric}_{\phi}
can be seen as
the
\infty
-dimensional Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature, that is,
{\rm Ric}_{\phi}:={\rm Ric}_{\phi,\infty}
.

For the convenience, we still denote
\Delta,\nabla
by the Laplacian operator and gradient operator on

M
, and
\overline{\Delta},\overline{\nabla}
, respectively, by the Laplacian operator and gradient operator on the boundary
\partial M
. The mean curvature
H
of
\partial M
is given by

H={\rm tr}_{g}(II)
, where
II(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\nu,Y)
denotes the second fundamental form of
\partial M

with
\nu
the outward unit normal on
\partial M
. For any
positive
twice differentiable function
V
, we make the following conventions:

{\rm\widehat{Ric}}^{V}_{\phi,m}=\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{V}V_{ij}+{\rm Ric}_{\phi,m},

(1.2)

{\rm\widehat{Ric}}^{V}_{\phi,\infty}=\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{V}V_{ij}+{\rm Ric}_{\phi},

(1.3)

H_{\phi}=H-\phi_{\nu}
,
II^{V}=II-(\ln V)_{\nu}\overline{g}
and
d\sigma
denotes the measure induced on
\partial M
.

First, we prove the Reilly type formula associated with
\phi
-Laplacian:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be a positive twice differentiable function on a given compact Riemannian manifold
M
with the boundary
\partial M
.
For any smooth function
f
, we have the following equality:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[-VII^{V}\Big(V\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V},V\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big)-V^{3}H_{\phi}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-2V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big(\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)\Big]\,d\sigma+\int_{M}V\Big[\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{f}{V}\nabla^{2}V\Big|^{2}-{\rm\widehat{Ric}}^{V}_{\phi,\infty}\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)\Big]\,d\mu.

(1.4)

Denote by
A_{ij}=f_{ij}-\frac{f}{V}V_{ij}
and
\mathring{A}_{ij}=A_{ij}-\frac{{\rm tr}(A_{ij})}{n}g_{ij}
with
{\rm tr}(A_{ij})=\Delta f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta V
. Then, we have
{\rm tr}(\mathring{A}_{ij})=0
and

\displaystyle|A_{ij}|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}[{\rm tr}(A_{ij})]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}[{\rm tr}(A_{ij})]^{2}+\frac{m-n}{mn}[{\rm tr}(A_{ij})]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta_{\phi}V\Big)^{2}+\frac{2}{m}V[{\rm tr}(A_{ij})]\nabla\phi\nabla\frac{f}{V}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{m}V^{2}\Big(\nabla\phi\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}+\frac{m-n}{mn}[{\rm tr}(A_{ij})]^{2},

which gives

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{f}{V}\nabla^{2}V\Big|^{2}+{\rm Ric}_{\phi}\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta_{\phi}V\Big)^{2}+{\rm Ric}_{\phi,m}\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\sqrt{\frac{m-n}{mn}}{\rm tr}(A_{ij})+\sqrt{\frac{n}{m(m-n)}}V\nabla\phi\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta_{\phi}V\Big)^{2}+{\rm Ric}_{\phi,m}\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)

(1.5)

provided
m\in(-\infty,0)\cup[n,+\infty)
, and equality holds if and only if

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f-\frac{f}{V}\nabla^{2}V=\frac{1}{n}\Big(\Delta f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta V\Big)g

(1.6)

and

\displaystyle\Delta f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta V+\frac{n}{\sqrt{(m-n)^{2}}}V\nabla\phi\nabla\frac{f}{V}=0.

(1.7)

Proof of results

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Using the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}Vf_{ij}^{2}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\frac{1}{2}V|\nabla f|^{2}_{\nu}\,d\sigma-\int_{M}e^{\phi}(e^{-\phi}Vf_{ij})_{j}f_{i}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\frac{1}{2}V|\nabla f|^{2}_{\nu}\,d\sigma-\int_{M}(f_{ij}V_{j}+Vf_{ij,j}-Vf_{ij}\phi_{j})f_{i}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\frac{1}{2}V|\nabla f|^{2}_{\nu}\,d\sigma-\int_{M}\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}_{i}V_{i}+V(\Delta_{\phi}f)_{i}f_{i}+V{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla f,\nabla f)\Big]\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[\frac{1}{2}V|\nabla f|^{2}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}V_{\nu}-V(\Delta_{\phi}f)f_{\nu}\Big]\,d\sigma+\int_{M}\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}\Delta_{\phi}V

\displaystyle+(\Delta_{\phi}f)V_{i}f_{i}+V(\Delta_{\phi}f)^{2}-V{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla f,\nabla f)\Big]\,d\mu,

(2.1)

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}ff_{ij}V_{ij}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\partial M}ff_{i\nu}V_{i}\,d\sigma+2\int_{M}[f_{ij}V_{i}f_{j}+f(\Delta_{\phi}f)_{i}V_{i}

\displaystyle+f{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla f,\nabla V)]\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}[2f(\Delta_{\phi}f)V_{\nu}-2ff_{i\nu}V_{i}+|\nabla f|^{2}V_{\nu}]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{M}[-|\nabla f|^{2}\Delta_{\phi}V-2f(\Delta_{\phi}f)(\Delta_{\phi}V)

\displaystyle-2(\Delta_{\phi}f)f_{i}V_{i}+2f{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla f,\nabla V)]\,d\mu

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{f^{2}}{V}V_{ij}^{2}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\frac{f^{2}}{V}|\nabla V|^{2}_{\nu}\,d\sigma-\int_{M}\Big[\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{j}V_{ij}V_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{2}}{V}(\Delta_{\phi}V)_{i}V_{i}+\frac{f^{2}}{V}{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla V,\nabla V)\Big]\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{2}}{V}|\nabla V|^{2}_{\nu}-\frac{f^{2}}{V}(\Delta_{\phi}V)V_{\nu}\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\Big[\frac{f^{2}}{V}(\Delta_{\phi}V)^{2}+(\Delta_{\phi}V)\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{i}V_{i}-\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{j}V_{ij}V_{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{2}}{V}{\rm Ric}_{\phi}(\nabla V,\nabla V)\Big]\,d\mu.

(2.3)

Let
A_{ij}=f_{ij}-\frac{f}{V}V_{ij}
. Then we have
A_{ij}^{2}=f_{ij}^{2}-2\frac{f}{V}f_{ij}V_{ij}+\frac{f^{2}}{V^{2}}V_{ij}^{2}
and
{\rm tr}(A_{ij})=\Delta f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta V
. It follows from (
2.1
)-(
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{M}VA_{ij}^{2}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[\frac{1}{2}V|\nabla f|^{2}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}V_{\nu}-V(\Delta_{\phi}f)f_{\nu}+2f(\Delta_{\phi}f)V_{\nu}

\displaystyle-2ff_{i\nu}V_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{2}}{V}|\nabla V|^{2}_{\nu}-\frac{f^{2}}{V}(\Delta_{\phi}V)V_{\nu}\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\Big[-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}\Delta_{\phi}V-(\Delta_{\phi}f)V_{i}f_{i}+(\Delta_{\phi}V)\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{i}V_{i}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{j}V_{ij}V_{i}\Big]\,d\mu+\int_{M}V\Big[\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{f}{V}\Delta_{\phi}V\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-{\rm Ric}_{\phi}\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)\Big]\,d\mu,

(2.4)

where we note that
\nabla f-\frac{f}{V}\nabla V=V\nabla\frac{f}{V}
. It is easy to check that

\displaystyle{}[-(\Delta_{\phi}V)g_{ij}+V_{ij}]\Big(V\nabla\frac{f}{V},V\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big)=

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{\phi}V)\Big[|\nabla f|^{2}-\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{i}V_{i}\Big]

\displaystyle+V_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}-\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{V}\Big)_{j}V_{ij}V_{i},

(2.33)

On the other hand, from the Rayleigh-Ritz formula(we may refer to
[
12
,
21
]
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\beta}\leq

\displaystyle\beta\eta_{1}+\frac{\int_{M}V^{2}\Big|\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\mu}{\int_{\partial M}V(\frac{z}{V})^{2}\,d\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\eta_{1}+\frac{\int_{\partial M}V^{2}(\frac{f}{V})_{\nu}\frac{z}{V}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\partial M}V(\frac{z}{V})^{2}\,d\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\eta_{1}+\Big[\int_{\partial M}V^{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma\Big/\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{z}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.34)

Hence, the desired estimate (
1.20
) follows from (
2.33
) and (
2.5
).

2.6.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

(1) Let
f
be the first eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue
p_{1}
of the Steklov problem:

\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}f=0\ {\rm on}\ M,\ \ \ V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}=p\frac{f}{V}\ {\rm on}\ \partial M.

Then from (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle(m-1)

\displaystyle Kp_{1}\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-1)K\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\frac{f}{V}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-1)K\int_{M}V^{2}\Big|\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[c_{1}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}+c_{2}V^{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}+2V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big(\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[c_{2}p_{1}^{2}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}+(c_{1}-2p_{1})V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle(c_{1}-2p_{1})\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\sigma.

(2.35)

Applying the inequality

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma\leq\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\sigma,

(2.36)

in (
2.6
) gives

\displaystyle 0>[(c_{1}-2p_{1})\eta_{1}-(m-1)Kp_{1}]\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma.

(2.37)

Thus, we obtain (
1.21
).

(2) Let
f
be a solution of the following equation:

\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}f=0\ {\rm on}\ M,\ \ \ -\beta\Big(\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)+V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}=\lambda\frac{f}{V}\ {\rm on}\ \partial M,

then on
\partial M
, we have

\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}V}{V}f=\frac{1}{\beta}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}-\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}\frac{f}{V}.

Then (
1.2
) becomes

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[c_{1}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}+c_{2}V^{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}+2V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big(\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Big[\Big(c_{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}\Big)V^{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}-\frac{c_{1}+2\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\frac{f}{V}+\frac{c_{1}\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\Big]\,d\sigma.

(2.38)

Inserting the inequality

\Big(c_{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}\Big)V^{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\Big)^{2}-\frac{c_{1}+2\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\frac{f}{V}\geq-\frac{(c_{1}+2\lambda_{1,\beta})^{2}}{4\beta(2+\beta c_{2})}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}

into (
2.6
) yields

\displaystyle 0\geq\Big[\frac{c_{1}\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}-\frac{(c_{1}+2\lambda_{1,\beta})^{2}}{4\beta(2+\beta c_{2})}\Big]\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma,

(2.39)

which shows that

\displaystyle 4(2+\beta c_{2})c_{1}\lambda_{1,\beta}-(c_{1}+2\lambda_{1,\beta})^{2}\leq 0.

(2.40)

Solving this quadratic inequality with respect to
\lambda_{1,\beta}
, we have that either

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\beta}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\Big(1+\beta c_{2}+\sqrt{\beta^{2}c_{2}^{2}+2\beta c_{2}}\Big),

(2.41)

or

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\beta}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\Big(1+\beta c_{2}-\sqrt{\beta^{2}c_{2}^{2}+2\beta c_{2}}\Big).

(2.42)

Note that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}V\frac{f}{V}\Big(\Delta_{\phi}f-\frac{\Delta_{\phi}V}{V}f\Big)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}V^{2}\Big|\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\mu-\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)_{\nu}\frac{f}{V}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}V^{2}\Big|\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\mu-\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma+\beta\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\sigma.

(2.43)

By the Rayleigh-Ritz formula(or see (
2.5
) with
\beta=0
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}V^{2}\Big|\nabla\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\mu\geq p_{1}\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma

(2.44)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}V^{2}\Big|\overline{\nabla}\frac{f}{V}\Big|^{2}\,d\sigma\geq\eta_{1}\int_{\partial M}V\Big(\frac{f}{V}\Big)^{2}\,d\sigma.

(2.45)

Inserting (
2.44
) and (
2.45
) into (
2.6
) gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\beta}\geq p_{1}+\beta\eta_{1}>\frac{c_{1}}{2}+\beta c_{1}c_{2},

(2.46)

which shows that (
2.42
) does not occur.
Therefore, we have that (
1.22
) holds and the proof of
Theorem
1.10
is completed.

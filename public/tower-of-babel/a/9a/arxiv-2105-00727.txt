Complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds with finite volume ends
V. Cortés, M. Röser, D. Thung

Abstract
We construct examples of complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds with an end of finite volume, which are not locally homogeneous. The manifolds are aspherical with fundamental group which is up to an infinite cyclic extension a semi-direct product of a lattice in a semi-simple group with a lattice in a Heisenberg group. Their universal covering is a cohomogeneity one deformation of a symmetric space of non-compact type. Keywords: quaternionic Kähler manifolds, c c -map, one-loop deformation, isometry groups, cohomogeneity one MSC classification: 53C26.

Introduction

One of the milestones of twentieth century differential geometry is Berger’s classification of irreducible holonomy groups of non-locally symmetric Riemannian manifolds
[
Ber55
]
. The problem to construct non-locally symmetric Riemannian manifolds (or to show their non-existence) for each of the groups in Berger’s list has been a major driving force for research in the theory of Einstein manifolds, involving a combination of Lie theory
[
Ale68
,
Ale75
]
, differential geometry
[
Bry87
,
BS89
,
LeB89
,
LeB91
]
, geometric analysis
[
Yau78
,
Joy96a
,
Joy96b
]
and algebraic geometry
[
Bea83
]
. As a result, we have compact non-locally symmetric examples of Riemannian manifolds for all of the groups in Berger’s list with exception of the group
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
.

Riemannian manifolds with holonomy group a subgroup of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
for
n\geq 2
are known as quaternionic Kähler manifolds. These manifolds are Einstein and, in fact, generalize half-conformally flat Einstein four-manifolds, which are by definition the quaternionic Kähler manifolds of dimension four.

All the known compact examples of non-zero scalar curvature are locally symmetric. They fall into two classes: symmetric quaternionic Kähler manifolds of compact type, known as Wolf spaces
[
Wol65
]
, and locally symmetric spaces obtained as smooth quotients of the non-compact duals of the Wolf spaces by lattices of the isometry group
[
Bor63
]
. The manifolds in both classes are rigid
[
LS94
,
Hor96
]
which shows that further examples cannot be obtained from deformation theory. Moreover, in the case of positive scalar curvature it is even conjectured that the Wolf spaces exhaust all complete (and hence compact, by Bonnet-Myers) examples
[
LS94
]
.

In this article we show that complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds of negative scalar curvature can have ends of finite volume without being locally homogeneous. Our constructions are based on the recently proven fact that the known homogeneous quaternionic Kähler manifolds of negative scalar curvature and higher rank can be deformed into complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds with an isometric cohomogeneity one action
[
CST21
, Cor. 3.19]
.

In this paper we focus on the above deformation
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, for the symmetric spaces

(\bar{N}_{n},g^{0})=\left(\frac{\mathrm{SU}(2,n)}{\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(n))},g_{\mathrm{can}}\right).

The one-parameter family
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, is obtained from an indefinite hyper-Kähler manifold by the HK/QK correspondence. This implies that the metrics
g^{c}
are quaternionic Kähler
[
ACDM15
, Cor. 1]
. The completeness of
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
for
c\geq 0
was shown in
[
CDS17
, Cor. 15]
. The manifolds
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
are locally inhomogeneous for
c>0

[
CST21
, Thm. 4.8]
.

We show in dimensions
4
and
8
(i.e. for
n=1
and
2
) that the isometry group of
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, contains discrete subgroups which give rise to smooth quotient manifolds
X=X_{\Gamma}=\bar{N}/\Gamma
with exactly two ends, see
4.2
. The metric
g^{c}
on
\bar{N}_{n}
induces a complete quaternionic Kähler metric on
X_{\Gamma}
, which we denote by the same symbol. With respect to this metric one of the two ends is of finite volume and the other is of infinite volume, see
4.5
.

The fundamental groups
\Gamma
of the eight-dimensional complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds
(X_{\Gamma},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, have the structure of a cyclic quotient
(\Gamma_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2})/\mathbb{Z}
of a semi-direct product
\Gamma_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}
, where
\Gamma_{1}\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,1)
is the preimage of an arithmetic subgroup
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
in the universal covering and
\Gamma_{2}
is a lattice in the five-dimensional Heisenberg group. The subgroup
\mathbb{Z}\subset\Gamma_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}
is diagonal for
c>0
, in the sense that it has trivial intersection with each of the factors. We consider also
\bar{\Gamma}=\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}
, which is a lattice in
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)\ltimes\Heis_{5}
. In the undeformed case
c=0
, we have
\mathbb{Z}\subset\Gamma_{1}
and the above construction reduces to a semi-direct product
\Gamma=\bar{\Gamma}=\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}
, of an arithmetic group
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
with a lattice
\Gamma_{2}\subset\Heis_{5}
. In four dimensions,
\Gamma
reduces to a lattice in the three-dimensional Heisenberg group and
X_{\Gamma}
is diffeomorphic to
(\Heis_{3}/\Gamma)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Our results are developed in such a way that they immediately extend to higher dimensions given lattices
\bar{\Gamma}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
, which do always exist. In particular, the isometric action of
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
on a cyclic quotient of
\bar{N}_{n}
is proven in all dimensions, see
3.16
. The arithmetic part of the construction consists in finding lattices
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}
in
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
which normalize a lattice in
\Heis_{2n+1}
. For
n=2
we were able to construct co-compact lattices of this type using quaternion algebras. The lattices which we obtained can be described as follows.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be such that
b
is prime and
a
is a quadratic non-residue mod
b
, i.e.
a
does not have a square root in the field
\mathbb{Z}_{b}
. Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{a,b}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{C})
the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of the four matrices

\mathds{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad I=\sqrt{a}i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad J=\sqrt{b}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad K=\sqrt{ab}i\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\bar{\Gamma}_{1,a,b}=\mathcal{O}_{a,b}\cap\mathrm{SU}(1,1)=\{A\in\mathcal{O}_{a,b}:\det A=1\}
is a co-compact Fuchsian group, which preserves the lattice
\Gamma_{2}
in
\Heis_{5}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
generated by
\mathcal{O}_{a,b}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Up to passing to a suitable finite index normal subgroup,
\bar{\Gamma}=\bar{\Gamma}_{1,a,b}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}
gives a lattice of the desired type, as long as the one-loop parameter
c

\sqrt{ab}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist complete locally inhomogeneous quaternionic Kähler manifolds of dimension
4

•

In all dimensions

After this summary of the main results, we would like to briefly describe the structure of our paper. Our constructions are based on an explicit construction of quaternionic Kähler metrics of negative scalar curvature known as the one-loop deformed
c
-map. This is reviewed in
Section

2
.

In
Section

3

In

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The (one-loop deformed)
c
-map

The one-loop deformed
c
-map is a string theory construction
[
RSV06
]
which, to a given projective special Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
, associates a one-parameter family of quaternionic Kähler manifolds. A geometric proof of the quaternionic Kähler property was given in
[
ACDM15
, Theorem 5]
based on an extension
[
ACM13
]
of Haydys’ HK/QK correspondence
[
Hay08
]
. These constructions were elegantly recovered in
[
MS15
]
based on Swann’s twist approach
[
Swa10
]
, as we will now describe. For more information see
[
CST21
,
ACDM15
,
MS15
]
and references therein.

We begin by recalling
[
Fre99
,
ACD02
]
the notion of a projective special Kähler manifold, which is defined in terms of a corresponding conical affine special Kähler manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

A pseudo-Kähler manifold
(M,g,J,\omega)
endowed with a flat, torsion-free connection
\nabla
which satisfies
\nabla\omega=0
and
\mathrm{d}^{\nabla}J=0
, where
J
is viewed as a
TM
-valued one-form, is called an affine special Kähler (ASK) manifold.

An ASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla)
endowed with a vector field
\xi
is called conical, or a CASK manifold, if

(i)

\{\xi,J\xi\}
generate a principal (that is, free and proper)
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action,

(ii)

g|_{\langle\xi,J\xi\rangle}
is negative-definite while
g|_{\langle\xi,J\xi\rangle^{\perp}}
is positive-definite, and

(iii)

\nabla\xi=\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}\xi=\id_{TM}
, where
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.

The vector field
\xi
is called the Euler field.

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on a CASK manifold casts it as the total space of a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-principal bundle. The base of this bundle is what we call a projective special Kähler manifold.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
be a CASK manifold. Then
\bar{M}\coloneqq M/\mathbb{C}^{*}
is called a projective special Kähler (PSK) manifold.

That every PSK manifold is indeed Kähler follows from the fact that we can construct
\bar{M}
as a Kähler quotient. Given a CASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
,
-J\xi
generates an
S^{1}
-action which preserves the (pseudo-)Kähler structure. Its Hamiltonian function is
\mu=-\frac{1}{2}g(\xi,\xi)
, whose level sets intersect the orbits of the
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-action generated by the Euler field exactly once. Choosing the level
\frac{1}{2}
, we then find
M\mathbin{\hskip-2.0pt/\mkern-6.0mu/\hskip-2.0pt}S^{1}=\mu^{-1}\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)/S^{1}\cong M/\mathbb{C}^{*}=\bar{M}
. Since
g|_{\langle\xi,J\xi\rangle^{\perp}}
is positive-definite, so is the induced Kähler metric on
\bar{M}
.

By definition,
\bar{M}
arises from a CASK manifold
(M,g_{M},J_{M},\omega_{M},\nabla,\xi)
. The ASK structure on
M
gives rise to a pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure on its cotangent bundle.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
CFG89
,
Fre99
,
ACD02
]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M},J_{M},\omega_{M},\nabla)
be an ASK manifold. Then
N\coloneqq T^{*}M
carries a pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure. With respect to the splitting
TN\cong\pi^{*}TM\oplus\pi^{*}T^{*}M
induced by
\nabla
, the hyper-Kähler metric
g
and complex structures
I_{k}
,
k=1,2,3
, are given by the expressions

g=\begin{pmatrix}g_{M}&0\\
0&g^{-1}_{M}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 35.56593ptI_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{M}&0\\
0&J^{*}_{M}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 35.56593ptI_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega_{M}^{-1}\\
\omega_{M}&0\end{pmatrix}\hskip 35.56593ptI_{3}=I_{1}I_{2},

where
g^{-1}_{M}
and
J_{M}^{*}
denote the natural induced structures on
T^{*}M
,
\omega_{M}
is regarded as an isomorphism
TM\to T^{*}M
, and we have omitted pullbacks throughout to simplify notation. ∎

This pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure, which is known as the rigid
c
-map structure, is a natural generalization of the standard pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure on
\mathbb{H}^{n,1}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n,1}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{n,1})^{*}
.

In the conical case, we additionally have a natural circle action, generated by the vector field
-J_{M}\xi
.

Definition 2.4
.

We call a vector field
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
on a (pseudo-)hyper-Kähler manifold
(N,g,\omega_{k})
,
k=1,2,3
, a rotating Killing field if it satisfies
L_{Z}g=0
,
L_{Z}\omega_{1}=0
,
L_{Z}\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}
and
L_{Z}\omega_{3}=-\omega_{2}
.

Using coordinates adapted to the CASK structure
[
ACD02
]
, a straightforward computation shows:

Proposition 2.5

(
[
ACM13
]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M},J_{M},\nabla,\xi)
be a CASK manifold, and endow
N=T^{*}M
with the rigid
c
-map structure. Then
Z=-\widetilde{J_{M}\xi}\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
, where the tilde denotes the
\nabla
-horizontal lift, is a rotating Killing field. Moreover, it is
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian, with Hamiltonian function
f_{Z}=-\frac{1}{2}g(Z,Z)
. ∎

In summary, the CASK structure on
M
induces a pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure equipped with a free circle action, generated by an
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian rotating Killing field, on its cotangent bundle. This is precisely the input required for the so-called HK/QK correspondence
[
Hay08
,
ACM13
,
ACDM15
]
, which produces a one-parameter family of quaternionic Kähler manifolds
(\bar{N},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, endowed with a nowhere-vanishing Killing field, out of it. The fact that, for the data described in
2.5
, the resulting metric is precisely the one-loop deformed
c
-map metric of
[
RSV06
]
was proven in
[
ACDM15
]
.

The twist construction
[
Swa10
]
provides a duality between manifolds endowed with a distinguished, closed two-form and a vector field which is Hamiltonian with respect to it. In the case at hand, the relevant two-form is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}\coloneqq\omega_{1}+\mathrm{d}\iota_{Z}g
, and the vector field is the rotating Killing field
Z
introduced above. For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
f_{\mathrm{H}}=f_{Z}+g(Z,Z)=\frac{1}{2}g(Z,Z)-\frac{1}{2}c
is a Hamiltonian for
Z
with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
, i.e. satisfies
\mathrm{d}f_{\mathrm{H}}=-\iota_{Z}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. The triple
(Z,\omega_{\mathrm{H}},f_{\mathrm{H}})
makes up the so-called twist data for the HK/QK correspondence. In the following, we will assume that
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is an integral two-form, i.e. has integer periods.

Exploiting the integrality, we pass to a principal
S^{1}
-bundle
\pi_{N}:P\to N
endowed with a principal
S^{1}
-connection
\eta
with curvature
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. The next step is to lift
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
to a vector field on
P
which preserves
\eta
; such a lift is provided by
Z_{P}\coloneqq\tilde{Z}+\pi^{*}_{N}f_{\mathrm{H}}X_{P}
, where
\tilde{Z}\in\mathfrak{X}(P)
is the
\eta
-horizontal lift of
Z
and
X_{P}\in\mathfrak{X}(P)
is generator of the principal circle action on
P
. It is known that, after imposing an integrality condition on the additive constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
which appears in the definition of
f_{\mathrm{H}}
, one may assume that
Z_{P}
generates a circle action
[
Swa10
]
. If the circle action on
N
is free, as in the case at hand, we may moreover assume that the lifted action is free as well. The quotient of
P
by this lifted circle action is then a smooth manifold; this quotient realizes
P
as the total space of a second circle bundle
\pi_{\bar{N}}:P\to\bar{N}
. Since the two principal circle actions on
P
commute,
\bar{N}
inherits a twisted circle action, generated by
(\pi_{\bar{N}})_{*}X_{P}
.

The power of the twist construction lies in the fact that it enables one to use geometric structures on
N
to induce twisted structures on
\bar{N}
, assuming that they are invariant under the initial circle action. This is done by exploiting the double fibration structure (see (
2
)), which induces pointwise identifications of the tangent spaces to both
N
and
\bar{N}
with the
\eta
-horizontal subspaces of the tangent spaces to
P
.

In particular, the quaternionic structure bundle of the hyper-Kähler manifold
N
, which is preserved by the rotating Killing field
Z
, endows
\bar{N}
with an almost quaternionic structure. However, the hyper-Kähler metric
g
does not twist to a quaternionic Kähler metric on
\bar{N}
. In order to obtain a quaternionic Kähler structure, we must first perform a so-called elementary deformation
[
MS15
]
.

A (pseudo-)Riemannian metric
h
on
N
is called an elementary deformation if it takes the following form:

h=ag+b\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\alpha_{\mu}\otimes\alpha_{\mu}

where, after setting,
\omega_{0}\coloneqq g
, we use the notation
\alpha_{\mu}=\iota_{Z}\omega_{\mu}
, and
a,b\in C^{\infty}(N)
are nowhere-vanishing functions. Assuming that
Z
is nowhere-vanishing, the second term is proportional to the restriction of
g
to the quaternionic span
\mathbb{H}Z=\langle Z,I_{1}Z,I_{2}Z,I_{3}Z\rangle
of
Z
. Thus, we can think of an elementary deformation as arising from the composition of a conformal scaling with an independent conformal scaling along
\mathbb{H}Z
.

Building on earlier work of several authors, Macia and Swann showed that there is an essentially unique combination of elementary deformation and twist data which yield a (pseudo-)quaternionic Kähler metric:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
Hay08
,
ACM13
,
ACDM15
,
MS15
]
)
.

Let
(N,g,\omega_{k},Z,f_{Z})
be a (pseudo-)hyper-Kähler manifold endowed with an
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian, rotating Killing field. Assume that its Hamiltonian function
f_{Z}
is nowhere-vanishing and let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, with respect to the twist data
(Z,\omega_{\mathrm{H}},f_{\mathrm{H}})
, where

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=k_{1}(\omega_{1}+\mathrm{d}\iota_{Z}g)\qquad\qquad\qquad f_{\mathrm{H}}=k_{1}(f_{Z}+g(Z,Z))

the elementary deformation

g_{\mathrm{H}}\coloneqq k_{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{f_{Z}}g+\frac{1}{f_{Z}^{2}}\sum_{\mu}\alpha_{\mu}\otimes\alpha_{\mu}\bigg)

twists to a (pseudo-)quaternionic Kähler metric on
\bar{N}
. These are the only choices of elementary deformation and twist data which lead to a (pseudo-)quaternionic Kähler structure. Moreover, when the initial (pseudo-)hyper-Kähler manifold arises from a CASK manifold in the manner described above, the resulting quaternionic Kähler metrics are positive-definite, and of negative scalar curvature. ∎

The constants
k_{1},k_{2}
do not influence the local geometry of the resulting quaternionic Kähler manifold, and will be set to
1
in the following. However, the additive constant
c
parametrizing the freedom in the choice of the Hamiltonian function
f_{Z}
does play an important role as can be seen from the explicit appearance of
f_{Z}
in the formula for
g_{\mathrm{H}}
.

In our setup, where
N
is the cotangent bundle of a CASK manifold, we have
f_{Z}=-\frac{1}{2}(g(Z,Z)+c)
. Denoting the resulting metrics by
g^{c}
, we have in summary obtained a one-parameter family
(\bar{N},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, of quaternionic Kähler manifolds out of a PSK manifold
\bar{M}
. The metric
g^{0}
is known as the undeformed
c
-map metric (or Ferrara–Sabharwal metric); the metrics
g^{c}
,
c>0
, are called (one-loop) deformed
c
-map metrics.

We will rely on the following completeness results:

Theorem 2.7

(
[
CHM12
,
CDS17
]
)
.

Let
(\bar{N},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, be the image of the PSK manifold
\bar{M}
under the deformed
c
-map. Then
(\bar{N},g^{0})
is complete if and only if
\bar{M}
is complete. If, additionally,
\bar{M}
arises from the (supergravity)
r
-map, or has regular boundary behavior, then
(\bar{N},g^{c})
is complete for all
c\geq 0
. ∎

Another fact to keep in mind is:

Proposition 2.8

(
[
CDS17
]
)
.

Let
(\bar{N},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
, be the image of the PSK manifold
\bar{M}
under the deformed
c
-map. Then, for arbitrary
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
(\bar{N},g_{c_{1}})
is locally isometric to
(\bar{N},g_{c_{2}})
. ∎

The undeformed
c
-map metric, however, is generally distinct, as we will soon demonstrate in explicit examples.

2.2
Symmetries and the
c
-map

In this section we review what is known about the symmetries of the quaternionic Kähler metrics constructed via the
c
-map and its one-loop deformation. In particular, we outline how one may obtain Killing vector fields from infinitesimal automorphisms of the initial PSK manifold.

It is well-known that the
4n
-dimensional quaternionic Kähler manifold
(\bar{N},g^{c})
that arises from the
c
-map applied to a PSK manifold
\bar{M}
of complex dimension
n-1
admits an isometric action of a
2n+1
-dimensional Heisenberg group. In the examples considered in this paper, where
\bar{M}
is a so-called PSK domain, we can think of
(\bar{N},g^{c})
as a bundle over
\bar{M}
such that the Heisenberg group preserves fibers and acts on them freely. In particular, the orbits are submanifolds of the fibers of codimension one. For
c=0
, this can be improved: A one-dimensional solvable extension of the Heisenberg group acts isometrically, freely and transitively on the fibers, casting
(\bar{N},g^{0})
as a bundle of Lie groups. A detailed description, including an explicit formula for the action with respect to a standard coordinate system on the fibers, is given in
[
CST21
, Lem. 2.20]
.

Given this large group of fiber-preserving isometries, it is natural to ask whether (deformed)
c
-map metrics admit additional isometries that cover non-trivial diffeomorphisms of the initial PSK manifold
\bar{M}
. In particular, if the PSK structure of
\bar{M}
admits non-trivial automorphisms, we may ask if these can be lifted to symmetries of its image under the
c
-map.

In order to provide a precise answer, we must formalize what an automorphism of a PSK manifold is. Keeping in mind the extrinsic definition of this class of manifolds, the following is natural:

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
be a CASK manifold, and
\bar{M}
the corresponding PSK manifold. An automorphism of the CASK structure on
M
is a diffeomorphism of
M
which preserves the pseudo-Kähler structure and the flat connection
\nabla
, and commutes with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action induced by
\{\xi,J\xi\}
. An automorphism of the PSK manifold
\bar{M}
is a diffeomorphism of
\bar{M}
which is induced by a CASK automorphism of the corresponding CASK manifold
M
.

The matter of lifting PSK automorphisms to the image
(\bar{N},g^{c})
under the
c
-map was taken up in
[
CST21
]
. By definition, every one-parameter group of PSK automorphisms lifts to a one-parameter group of CASK automorphisms of the corresponding CASK manifold
M
. These CASK automorphisms can in turn be lifted canonically to
N=T^{*}M
, using the pullback on one-forms. It turns out that the canonical lift of a CASK automorphism to
N
preserves all data on
N
that we have discussed, i.e. the pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure and twist data
(Z,\omega_{\mathrm{H}},f_{\mathrm{H}})
. Moreover, every one-parameter family of canonically lifted CASK automorphisms is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian, and there is a canonical choice of Hamiltonian function. In fact, there exists an equivariant moment map for the lifted action of
\Aut M
:

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(M,g,\omega,\nabla,\xi)
be an CASK manifold. Then an equivariant moment map for the (canonically lifted) action of
\Aut M
on
N
, with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
, is given by
\mu:N\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
where
\langle\mu,X\rangle\eqqcolon\mu_{X}=\frac{1}{2}\big(g(Z,X)+\nu_{X}\big)
. Here,
\nu_{X}(\alpha)=(\omega^{-1})_{\pi(\alpha)}(\alpha\circ\nabla V^{X},\alpha)
,
\alpha\in T^{*}M
, with
V^{X}
the fundamental vector field corresponding to
X
.

Proof.

The expression given here for the Hamiltonian function corresponding to
X\in\mathfrak{aut}M
agrees (though the notation differs) with the results of
[
CST21
]
, so we will not verify (again) that it indeed provides a Hamiltonian.
One then checks the equivariance, i.e. that
\mu_{X}(g\cdot\alpha)=\mu_{\Ad_{g^{-1}}X}(\alpha)
for all
g\in\Aut M
and
\alpha\in T^{*}M
, by working through the definitions of
\mu_{X}
and
\nu_{X}
.
∎

Theorem 2.11

(
[
CST21
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
denote the algebra of infinitesimal automorphisms of the PSK manifold
\bar{M}
, and
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N},g^{c})
the algebra of Killing fields of its image
(\bar{N},g^{c})
under the (deformed)
c
-map. Then there exists an injective, linear map
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N},g^{c})
for every
c\geq 0
.

Sketch of Proof.

After lifting an infinitesimal PSK automorphism to the corresponding CASK manifold, and then to its cotangent bundle
N
, let us denote the generator of the lifted one-parameter family by
X
, and its
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian by
f_{X}
. Then we certainly have
L_{X}g_{\mathrm{H}}=0
, where
g_{\mathrm{H}}
is the elementary deformation from
2.6
. However, since the twist of a Killing field is generally not a Killing field with respect to the twisted metric, we must modify
X
to obtain a Killing field of the quaternionic Kähler metric
g^{c}
. This aim is achieved by
X-\frac{f_{X}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}Z
, whose twist
Y
indeed satisfies
L_{Y}g^{c}=0
. Note that
Y
implicitly depends on the deformation parameter
c
through the appearance of
f_{\mathrm{H}}
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

(i)

The proof shows that the entire procedure can be applied just as well to an infinitesimal CASK automorphism of
M
that does not arise as a lift of an infinitesimal PSK automorphism of
\bar{M}
. Such infinitesimal automorphisms can only exist in case the CASK manifold is flat (cf.
[
CST21
]
), in which case the generator of the distinguished circle action is an infinitesimal automorphism. The series of examples discussed in this paper is of this type.

(ii)

The principal circle action on
P
pushes down to a distinguished, isometric circle action on
(\bar{N},g^{c})
which commutes with the Killing fields constructed by means of
2.11
.

The map
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N},g^{c})
of
2.11
is in general not a Lie algebra homomorphism. Upon twisting, the commutator of vector fields (generically) picks up an additional term which is proportional to the vector field generating the distinguished circle action on
\bar{N}
, yielding a one-dimensional central extension:

Theorem 2.13

(
[
CST21
]
)
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
induces a Lie algebra of Killing fields on
(\bar{N},g^{c})
which is isomorphic to a one-dimensional central extension of
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
. ∎

Remark 2.14
.

If the central extension is trivial, then we may arrange matters such that we do obtain a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N},g^{c})
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
. This is the case in the series of examples on which we focus in this work.

The Killing fields constructed via
2.11
are linearly independent of the generators of the action of the
2n+1
-dimensional Heisenberg group. Putting them together and taking into account
2.12
, we have constructed an algebra of Killing fields of
(\bar{N},g^{c})
whose dimension is
\dim\mathfrak{aut}(M)+2n+1
, where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=n
. For
c=0
, this is improved to
\dim\mathfrak{aut}(M)+2n+2
. Under the assumptions of
2.7
, we know that
(\bar{N},g^{c})
is complete for all
c\geq 0
and can integrate these Killing fields to obtain a group of isometries of the (deformed)
c
-map metric.

3
The
c
-map applied to complex hyperbolic spaces

3.1
The infinitesimal action

We will now focus on an important series of examples. Consider
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, equipped with the flat pseudo-Kähler structure induced by the indefinite Hermitian form
h=-\mathrm{d}z^{0}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{0}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}z^{a}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{a}
. Since the Levi-Civita connection is flat, we may regard it as endowing
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the structure of an ASK manifold. The standard
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action by scalar multiplication endows the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant domain
M_{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid-\lvert z^{0}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert z^{a}\rvert^{2}<0\}
with the structure of a CASK manifold. The Kähler quotient by
\mathrm{U}(1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
is nothing but complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}
equipped with the (symmetric) Bergman metric

g_{\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{1-\lVert X\rVert^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert\mathrm{d}X^{a}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\lVert X\rVert^{2}}\bigg|\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{X}^{a}\mathrm{d}X^{a}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg)

(3)

The indefinite unitary group
\mathrm{U}(1,n-1)
acts on
M_{n}
by automorphisms of the CASK structure and projects down to yield a transitive action on
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}
, which is therefore a homogeneous PSK manifold.

It follows directly from the general theory developed in
Section

2.2
that the image
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{0})
of
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}
under the undeformed
c
-map is a homogeneous quaternionic Kähler manifold (of negative scalar curvature), and that the deformed metrics
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c>0
, are complete and of cohomogeneity at most one. An explicit expression for the metrics
g^{c}
, with respect to a global coordinate system
(X^{a},w^{k},\tilde{\phi},\rho)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, where
a=1,\dots,n-1
and
\lVert X\rVert^{2}=\sum_{a}\lvert X^{a}\rvert^{2}<1
, and
k=0,\dots,n-1
, was given in
[
CDS17
]
:

\displaystyle g^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}g_{\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho+c}\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+c}{\rho+2c}\bigg(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}-4\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\bar{w}^{0}\mathrm{d}w^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{w}^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\bigg)+\frac{2c}{1-\lVert X\rVert^{2}}\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{X}^{a}\mathrm{d}X^{a}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{\rho}\bigg(\mathrm{d}w^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a}\bigg)+\frac{\rho+c}{\rho^{2}}\frac{4}{1-\lVert X\rVert^{2}}\Big|\mathrm{d}w^{0}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}X^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\Big|^{2}

(4)

Note that the case
n=1
, where we apply the
c
-map to a single point, is exceptional. With the convention
\sum_{a=1}^{0}=0
, the expression (
4
) remains valid and greatly simplifies. The resulting quaternionic Kähler four-manifold is known as the universal hypermultiplet in the physics literature. Despite the qualitative difference with the case
n\geq 2
, our results in this paper remain valid if
n=1
, unless explicitly indicated.

As a smooth manifold,
\bar{N}_{n}
is just a copy of
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
. However, its Riemannian structure is interesting. In fact, it is known that the undeformed
c
-map metric casts
\bar{N}_{n}
as the non-compact symmetric space
\frac{\mathrm{SU}(n,2)}{\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(2))}
. Moreover, it was proven in
[
CST21
]
that
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
is of cohomogeneity one. In summary, the family
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c\geq 0
is a one-parameter deformation of a symmetric space through cohomogeneity one complete quaternionic Kähler metrics.

To develop a more concrete understanding of these manifolds, we will now show in detail how the general theory works out in these examples. Our main goal is to derive explicit formulas for the Killing fields obtained by means of
2.11
. The starting point is the (standard) action of
\mathrm{U}(1,n-1)
on
M_{n}
by CASK automorphisms, or rather the infinitesimal action of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
. Passing to
N_{n}=T^{*}M_{n}=M_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the canonically lifted vector field
\mathcal{X}_{A}
corresponding to
A\in\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
is given by
\mathcal{X}_{A}(z,w)=(Az+\overline{Az},-A^{\top}w-\overline{A^{\top}w})
, where we identify the tangent spaces with the underlying vector space.

The twisting two-form
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\frac{i}{2}\bigg(\mathrm{d}z^{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{0}-\mathrm{d}w^{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big(\mathrm{d}z^{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{a}-\mathrm{d}w^{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a}\big)\bigg),

so that the Hamiltonian corresponding to
\mathcal{X}_{A}
is given by
f_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}(z,w)=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}\big(\langle Az,z\rangle+\langle Aw,w\rangle\big)
, where
\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle=-z_{1}^{0}\bar{z}_{2}^{0}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}z_{1}^{a}\bar{z}_{2}^{a}
. The rotating Killing field
Z
, which is the horizontal lift of the (negative) generator of the standard
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on the first factor, is given by
Z(z,w)=(-iz,0)
. Its Hamiltonian is

f_{\mathrm{H}}=f_{Z}+g(Z,Z)=\frac{1}{2}(g(Z,Z)-c)=\frac{1}{2}(\langle z,z\rangle-c).

(Recall that for the particular vector field
Z
we denoted by
f_{Z}=-\frac{1}{2}(g(Z,Z)+c)
its Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{1}
. That is why
we are using the notation
f_{H}
for the
\omega_{H}
Hamiltonian.)
Following the proof of
2.11
, we now know that the twist of
\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{A}-\frac{f_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}Z
will be a Killing field on
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
.

In order to derive explicit expressions for these twisted vector fields, we first construct a circle bundle
P
, equipped with a connection
\eta
with curvature
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. Since
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is exact,
P
is trivial and we may choose
\eta=\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{\Xi}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
,
where
s
is the periodic coordinate parametrizing
S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
\Xi
is the position vector field

\Xi=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(z^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}+\bar{z}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{k}}+w^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{k}}+\bar{w}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{k}}\bigg).

Explicitly,

\eta=\mathrm{d}s+\frac{i}{4}\Big(z^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{0}-\bar{z}^{0}\mathrm{d}z^{0}-w^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}+\bar{w}^{0}\mathrm{d}w^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big(z^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{a}-\bar{z}^{a}\mathrm{d}z^{a}-w^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a}+\bar{w}^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\big)\Big).

We lift
Z
to the vector field
Z_{P}
, which generates the circle action defining the principal circle bundle
P\to\bar{N}_{n}
. Recall that
Z_{P}=\tilde{Z}+f_{\mathrm{H}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
, where the tilde denotes the
\eta
-horizontal lift and we regard
f_{\mathrm{H}}
as a function on
P=N_{n}\times S^{1}
. Regarding
Z
as a vector field on
P
, a short computation shows that

Z_{P}=Z-\frac{c}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}=-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(z^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}-\bar{z}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{k}}\bigg)-\frac{c}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}.

Similarly, for
A\in\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
, one may check that
\eta(\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}})=-\frac{f_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}\frac{c}{2}
, so that

\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}=\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}+\frac{f_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}\frac{c}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}=\mathcal{X}_{A}-\frac{f_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}Z_{P}.

We now want to push this vector field down to
\bar{N}_{n}
. To this end, we realize
\bar{N}_{n}
as a submanifold of
P
, using a global slice for the action obtained by integrating
Z_{P}
, which is given by
(z,w,s)\mapsto(e^{-it}z,w,s-\frac{c}{2}t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The flow is not periodic for arbitrary
c\geq 0
, but for
c\in 2\mathbb{Z}
we have a circle action covering the circle action on
N_{n}
(this is the previously mentioned quantization condition). Since the local geometry of the metrics
g^{c}
,
c>0
, is independent of
c
(cf.
2.8
) we will only consider the values
c=0
and
c=2
. The submanifold
\bar{N}_{n}\coloneqq\{(z,w,s)\in N_{n}\times S^{1}\mid\arg z^{0}=0\}
then provides a global slice for the action, and can therefore be identified as the twist manifold.

The final ingredient required is an identification of the horizontal tangent spaces to
P
with the tangent spaces of
\bar{N}_{n}
, given by an
S^{1}
-principal connection with respect to the circle action generated by
Z_{P}
, namely
\theta=\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}\eta
. Now we have to write
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}
in the form
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}=Y_{A}-\theta(Y_{A})Z_{P}
, where
Y_{A}
is tangent to
\bar{N}_{n}\subset P
(at every point in
\bar{N}_{n}
).
Y_{A}
is then be the twist of
\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}
, whose
\theta
-horizontal lift indeed agrees with
\eta
. Since
\theta(Z_{P})=1
and
\theta(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}})=0
by construction, we already have

\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}=\mathcal{X}_{A}-\theta(\mathcal{X}_{A})Z_{P},

but
\mathcal{X}_{A}
will not be tangent to
\bar{N}_{n}
in general. To remedy this, we have to consider a vector field of the form
\mathcal{X}_{A}+\varphi Z_{P}
, where
\varphi
is a function. This vector field restricts to
\bar{N}_{n}
if and only if it satisfies
\mathrm{d}(\arg z^{0})(\mathcal{X}_{A}+\varphi Z_{P})=\mathrm{d}(\arg z^{0})(\mathcal{X}_{A})-\varphi=0
, where we used that
\mathrm{d}(\arg z^{0})(Z_{P})=-1
, showing that we must have
\varphi=\mathrm{d}(\arg z^{0})(\mathcal{X}_{A})
. The twisted vector field
Y_{A}
on
\bar{N}_{n}
is therefore given by

Y_{A}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{A}+\mathrm{d}(\arg z^{0})(\mathcal{X}_{A})Z_{P}.

(5)

Now we turn to the algebra
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
of infinitesimal CASK automorphisms of
M_{n}
, which we consider as a real form of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Writing
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
in block form with respect to the splitting
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus e_{0}^{\perp}
(where
\{e_{k}\}_{k=0,\dots,n-1}
is an orthonormal basis with respect to the indefinite Hermitian form), i.e.

A=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&v^{\top}\\
w&B\end{pmatrix},

(6)

with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},v,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
B\in\mathfrak{gl}(n-1,\mathbb{C})
, we will think of
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
as the fixed-point set of the anti-linear involutive automorphism

\sigma:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}),\qquad A^{\sigma}\coloneqq\sigma(A)\coloneqq-I\bar{A}^{\top}I,

where
I=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&\mathds{1}_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}
.
We may now write any
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
uniquely in the form

A=\operatorname{Re}_{\sigma}(A)+i\operatorname{Im}_{\sigma}(A),

where

\operatorname{Re}_{\sigma}(A)=\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\sigma}),\qquad\operatorname{Im}_{\sigma}(A)=\frac{1}{2i}(A-A^{\sigma})

are both elements of
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
. Note that
A^{\sigma}=\operatorname{Re}_{\sigma}(A)-i\operatorname{Im}_{\sigma}(A)
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
is generated by the matrices

U_{a}=\begin{pmatrix}0&e_{a}^{\top}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad U_{a}^{\sigma}\coloneqq\sigma(U_{a})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
e_{a}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad a=1,\dots,n-1.

(7)

We observe that

[U_{a},U_{b}]=0,\qquad[U_{a}^{\sigma},U_{b}^{\sigma}]=0,\qquad[U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}]=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{ab}&0\\
0&-e_{b}e_{a}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

Thus,

\{U_{a},U_{a}^{\sigma},[U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}]:a,b=1,\dots,n-1\}

is a basis of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. From this, together with the central element
C=i\mathds{1}_{n}
, they form a basis for
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, which is therefore generated (as a complex Lie algebra) by
\{C,U_{a},U_{a}^{\sigma}\colon a=1,\dots,n-1\}
.

The infinitesimal action of
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
on
M_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
by CASK automorphisms extends complex-linearly to an infinitesimal action
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{X}^{\mathbb{C}}(M_{n})
by complex vector fields. Since both canonically lifting and the twisting procedure extend to complex vector fields without problems, we can complexify the above discussion of
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
to obtain a linear map
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{X}^{\mathbb{C}}(\bar{N}_{n})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The linear map
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})^{\mathbb{C}}

defines an infinitesimal action of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
by complex Killing fields on
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})

\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
into
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})

\displaystyle Y_{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(C)=-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(w^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{k}}-\bar{w}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{k}}\bigg)-2c\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},

(9)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(U_{a})=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}-X^{a}\sum_{b=1}^{n-1}X^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{b}}-w^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{a}}-\bar{w}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{0}}+icX^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},

\displaystyle\bar{Y}_{a}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(U_{a}^{\sigma}),

where

Corollary 3.2

The real vector fields

\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})

Next, we explicitly compute the commutators of the complex Killing fields

3.3
). We now extend the infinitesimal action
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})^{\mathbb{C}}
to an infinitesimal action
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})^{\mathbb{C}}
in the manner prescribed in the proof of
3.3
, i.e. by setting

\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(E_{k})=V_{k},\quad\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(\bar{E}_{k})=\bar{V}_{k},\quad\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}.

(12)

where
E_{k}=e_{k}-if_{k}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The linear map
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}\colon\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})^{\mathbb{C}}
as defined by
3.1
and
Equation

12
is an infinitesimal action on
(\bar{N},g^{c})
by complex Killing fields which sends
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
into
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
.

Proof.

It is clear from the preceding discussion that
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}
indeed maps into
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})^{\mathbb{C}}
, and that the image of
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
consists of real Killing fields of
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
. All that remains is to verify that
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}
defines a Lie algebra anti-homomorphism.

By
3.1
and
3.3
, we need only check those Lie brackets that involve one element of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
and one element of
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}
. In
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}^{\mathbb{C}}_{2n+1}
, the following brackets can be calculated from (
7
) and the fact that, by definition of the semi-direct product structure, the bracket of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}
evaluated on
A\in\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is just
[A,v]=-A^{\top}v
(where
A^{\top}
is identified with a real
2n\times 2n
-matrix). This prescription yields

\displaystyle[C,E_{k}]=-iE_{k},

\displaystyle\qquad[C,\bar{E}_{k}]=i\bar{E}_{k},

\displaystyle[U_{a},E_{k}]=-\delta_{k0}E_{a},

\displaystyle\qquad[U^{\sigma}_{a},E_{k}]=-\delta_{ka}E_{0},

\displaystyle[U_{a},\bar{E}_{k}]=-\delta_{ka}\bar{E}_{0},

\displaystyle\qquad[U^{\sigma}_{a},\bar{E}_{k}]=-\delta_{k0}\bar{E}_{a}

The remaining brackets involving one element of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
and one of
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}
can be deduced from these via the Jacobi identity. We now compare to the brackets of the corresponding vector fields on the quaternionic Kähler manifold, using the formulas (
9
) and (
10
). They yield the expected results, namely

\displaystyle[Y_{C},V_{k}]=iV_{k},

\displaystyle\qquad[Y_{C},\bar{V}_{k}]=-i\bar{V}_{k},

\displaystyle[Y_{a},V_{k}]=\delta_{k0}V_{a},

\displaystyle\qquad[\bar{Y}_{a},V_{k}]=\delta_{ka}V_{0},

\displaystyle[Y_{a},\bar{V}_{k}]=\delta_{ka}\bar{V}_{0},

\displaystyle\qquad[\bar{Y}_{a},\bar{V}_{k}]=\delta_{k0}\bar{V}_{a}.

This confirms that
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}
defines an infinitesimal action.
∎

This shows that, despite the
c
-dependence of the Killing fields constructed via the HK/QK correspondence, the isomorphism type of the Lie algebra of Killing fields is independent of
c
. The
c
-dependence arises only once we consider the (
c
-dependent) infinitesimal action
\alpha:\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
obtained by restricting
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}
.

3.2
\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
as an algebra of Killing fields

The goal of this section is to provide a characterization of
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\operatorname{Im}\alpha
as a subalgebra of the Killing algebra of
\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
, see
3.10
. We start by determining the stabilizer of a distinguished point:

Lemma 3.5
.

Consider for fixed
\rho_{0}>0
the point
n_{0}=(0,0,0,\rho_{0})\in\bar{N}_{n}
. Then the stabilizer of
n_{0}
in
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}=\Span\bigg\{Y_{C}+2c\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},\operatorname{Re}([Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{b}]),\operatorname{Im}([Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{b}])+2c\delta_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\colon a,b=1,\dots,n-1\bigg\}.

In particular,
\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
.

Proof.

Recall from the proof of
3.1
the formula

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\Bigg(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\bigg(X^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{d}}-\bar{X}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{d}}\bigg)+w^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{0}}-\bar{w}^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{0}}-2ic\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\Bigg)

(13)

\displaystyle+X^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{b}}-\bar{X}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}+\bar{w}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{b}}-w^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{a}}.

From this we read off

[Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{b}](n_{0})=-2ic\delta_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}.

Similarly,
Y_{C}(n_{0})=-2c\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
; the inclusion of the right-hand side in the left-hand side now follows. The other inclusion can be seen directly from (
9
), (
10
) and (
12
).

To see that this subalgebra is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
, consider its preimage under the (injective!) infinitesimal action
\alpha
, which is

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\Span\big\{C+2cT,\operatorname{Re}([U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}]),\operatorname{Im}([U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}])+2c\delta_{ab}T\colon a,b=1,\dots,n-1\big\}

(14)

Since
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\subset\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
is an ideal, the natural projection map
\pi_{1}:\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras. Its restriction to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is injective since
\alpha(\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})\cap\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}=\{0\}
, and from (
14
) one reads off that

\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=\Span\big\{C,\operatorname{Re}([U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}]),\operatorname{Im}([U_{a},U_{b}^{\sigma}])\colon a,b=1,\dots,n-1\big\},

which is nothing but the standard embedding
\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)\subset\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)
.
∎

Using this, we can determine the subalgebra consisting of Killing fields tangent to the submanifold
H\coloneqq\{X=0,\rho=\rho_{0}\}\subset\bar{N}_{n}
, which is acted on simply transitively and isometrically by
\Heis_{2n+1}
. Since the isometry group of the symmetric space
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{0})
is well-understood, we focus on
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
,
c>0
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
c>0
and let
K
be a Killing field on
(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
which is tangent to
H
at every point contained in
H
. Then there exists an element of the Lie subalgebra
\alpha((\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1))\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})\subset\mathfrak{g}

which coincides with
K
along
H
.

Proof.

Clearly,
K|_{H}
is a Killing field on
(H,g_{H}^{c})
where we wrote
g_{H}^{c}
for the metric on
H
induced by
g^{c}
. From (
4
), we find

\displaystyle g^{c}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathrm{d}w^{a}\big|^{2}+\bigg(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\bigg)\big|\mathrm{d}w^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\rho_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}+2c}\bigg(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}-2i\bigg(\bar{w}^{0}\mathrm{d}w^{0}-w^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(\bar{w}^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}-w^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a})\bigg)\bigg)^{2},

which is a left-invariant metric on the Heisenberg group
\Heis_{2n+1}\cong H
by construction. A result of Wilson
[
Wil82
]
asserts that the Killing algebra of
(H,g_{H}^{c})
can be constructed as a semi-direct product
\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
, where
\mathfrak{s}
is the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric derivations of
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}=T_{(0,0,0)}H
.

Note that

g^{c}_{H}(0,0,0)=\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert\mathrm{d}w^{a}\rvert^{2}+\bigg(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\bigg)\lvert\mathrm{d}w^{0}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{4\rho_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}+2c}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}^{2}

is diagonal, and recall, see (
11
), that the bracket on
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
is given by
[v_{1},v_{2}]=\omega(v_{1},v_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
, where
\omega=\frac{i}{2}\big(\mathrm{d}w^{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a}\big)
.

Now let
A\in\End(\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})
be a skew-symmetric derivation, so that in particular

\omega(v_{1},v_{2})A\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\bigg)=\big([Av_{1},v_{2}]+[v_{1},Av_{2}]\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

(15)

For
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we write
Av=\tilde{A}v+l_{A}(v)\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
and
A\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\big)=\lambda_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
(every derivation preserves the center) with
l_{A}\in(\mathbb{R}^{2n})^{*},\lambda_{A}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
g^{c}_{H}
is diagonal at
(0,0,0)
, skew-symmetry implies that
\lambda_{A}=0
,
l_{A}=0
. Then
equation (
15
) simplifies to

0=\omega(\tilde{A}v_{1},v_{2})+\omega(v_{1},\tilde{A}v_{2}),

that is
\tilde{A}\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
.

Introducing real coordinates
\{\tilde{\zeta}_{k},\zeta^{k}\}
by writing
w^{0}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\zeta}_{0}+i\zeta^{0})
and
w^{a}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\zeta}_{a}-i\zeta^{a})
, we have
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}\zeta^{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tilde{\zeta}_{k}
and

g^{c}_{H}=\frac{1}{8\rho_{0}}\bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(\mathrm{d}\zeta^{a})^{2}+(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\zeta}^{a})^{2}\bigg)+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\bigg)(\mathrm{d}\zeta^{0})^{2}+(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\zeta}^{0})^{2})+\frac{1}{4\rho_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}+2c}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}^{2}.

Write
\tilde{A}
in block form with respect to the splitting
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
.

\tilde{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}\end{pmatrix}

The Gram matrices of
g^{c}_{H}
(restricted to
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\Span\{\tilde{\zeta}_{k},\zeta^{k}\}
) and
\omega
are

G=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\big(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\big)\mathds{1}_{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\mathds{1}_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}J_{2}&0\\
0&J_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix},

where
J_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
J_{2n-2}
consists of
(n-1)
copies of
J_{2}
on its diagonal. Skew-symmetry of
A
implies
\tilde{A}^{\top}G+G\tilde{A}=0
and
\tilde{A}\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
implies that
\tilde{A}^{\top}\Omega+\Omega\tilde{A}=0
. This means that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}^{\top}&A_{3}^{\top}\\
A_{2}^{\top}&A_{4}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}\big(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\big)\mathds{1}_{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2\rho}\mathds{1}_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}\!\!+\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\big(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\big)\mathds{1}_{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\mathds{1}_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}\end{pmatrix}\!

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}^{\top}&A_{3}^{\top}\\
A_{2}^{\top}&A_{4}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}J_{2}&0\\
0&J_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}J_{2}&0\\
0&J_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}\end{pmatrix}\!

\displaystyle=0.

This implies

\displaystyle A_{1}+A_{1}^{\top}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\top}J_{2}+J_{2}A_{1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle A_{4}+A_{4}^{\top}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle A_{4}^{\top}J_{2}+J_{2}A_{4}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\bigg)A_{2}^{\top}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}A_{3}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\top}J_{2}+J_{2n-2}A_{3}

\displaystyle=0

The last two lines imply
A_{2}=0
and
A_{3}=0
, since the parameter
c>0
. Thus,
\tilde{A}
is of the form

\tilde{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{4}\end{pmatrix}

with
A_{1}
and
A_{4}
skew-symmetric and complex linear. Put differently, we have shown that
A\in\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
, and thereby that
\mathfrak{s}\cong\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
.

The stabilizer (in
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Im}\alpha
) of the point
(0,0,0,\rho_{0})
, determined in
3.5
, consists of Killing fields that are tangent to
H
and span a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
. Combining this with
\alpha(\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})
, we see that
\alpha((\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1))\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})\cong\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
, so the full algebra of Killing fields of
H
arises from
\alpha
as claimed.
∎

The image of
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, contains the subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\Big\{Y_{a},V_{a},V_{0},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}:a=1,\dots,n-1\Big\}

This is a
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra of complex dimension
2n
, whose center is

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\Big\{V_{a},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}:a=1,\dots,n-1\Big\}.

The only non-trivial brackets are
[Y_{a},V_{0}]=V_{a}
, for
a=1,\dots,n-1
. Analogous statements hold for
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
. Note that
\mathfrak{h}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathbb{C}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
, and that
\mathfrak{h}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}}
generates the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that the elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
preserve the coordinate function
\rho
and are therefore tangent to its level sets, which we shall denote by
\bar{N}_{\rho}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a natural isomorphism
T^{\mathbb{C}}\bar{N}_{\rho}\cong\bar{N}_{\rho}\times(\mathfrak{h}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Proof.

We show that the
4n-1
vector fields
Y_{a},V_{a},V_{0},\bar{Y}_{a},\bar{V}_{a},\bar{V}_{0},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
give a global frame for
T^{\mathbb{C}}\bar{N}_{\rho}
. Comparing dimensions, it is enough to check that these complex vector fields are pointwise linearly independent. We have the decomposition

T^{\mathbb{C}}\bar{N}_{\rho}\cong(\mathrm{pr}_{X}^{*}TB^{n-1})^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus(\mathrm{pr}_{w}^{*}T\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},

where
B^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
denotes the open unit ball, and we used the natural projections
\mathrm{pr}_{X}:\bar{N}_{\rho}=B^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to B^{n-1}
,
\mathrm{pr}_{w}:\bar{N}_{\rho}=B^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Now consider a linear combination

0=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{a}Y_{a}+\mu_{a}\bar{Y}_{a}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k}V_{k}+\epsilon_{k}\bar{V}_{k}+\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},

with coefficients
\lambda_{a},\mu_{a},\delta_{k},\epsilon_{k},\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. Projecting onto the
(TB^{n-1})^{\mathbb{C}}=T^{1,0}B^{n-1}\oplus T^{0,1}B^{n-1}
component and considering
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts separately yields the relations

\mu_{a}=X^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X^{j}\lambda_{j},\qquad\lambda_{a}=\bar{X}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bar{X}^{j}\mu_{j}.

Using vector notation
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and similarly for
\mu
and
X
, we can use the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
to rewrite these relations as
\mu=\langle\lambda,\bar{X}\rangle X
and
\lambda=\langle\mu,X\rangle\bar{X}
. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality now yields

\lVert\lambda\rVert\leq\lVert\mu\rVert\lVert X\rVert^{2},\qquad\lVert\mu\rVert\leq\lVert\lambda\rVert\lVert X\rVert^{2},

which implies
(1-\lVert X\rVert^{4})\lVert\lambda\rVert=0
since
\lVert X\rVert<1
(cf. the discussion preceding (
4
)). Since
\lVert X\rVert<1
, this means that
\lambda=0
, hence also
\mu=0
. Projecting onto the
T\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
-factor and considering
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts, we see immediately from (
10
) that
\delta_{k}=0=\epsilon_{k}
for all
k=0,\dots,n-1
, which in turn implies
\gamma=0
, completing the proof.
∎

Consider once again the submanifold
H=\{X=0\}\subset\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
and note that, at
p=(0,w,\tilde{\phi},\rho_{0})\in H
, we have

Y_{a}(p)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}-w^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{a}}-\bar{w}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{0}},

and

V_{a}(p)=\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{a}}-i\bar{w}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},\qquad V_{0}(p)=\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{0}}+i\bar{w}^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}.

The metric (given in (
4
)) also simplifies on
T_{p}\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
:

\displaystyle g^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathrm{d}X^{a}\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathrm{d}w^{a}\big|^{2}+\bigg(\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}\bigg)\big|\mathrm{d}w^{0}\big|^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\rho_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}+2c}\bigg(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}-2i\bigg(\bar{w}^{0}\mathrm{d}w^{0}-w^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(\bar{w}^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}-w^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a})\bigg)\bigg)^{2}.

Lemma 3.8
.

The vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}
,
a=1,\dots,n-1
form a parallel frame for the complexified normal bundle of the submanifold
H\subset\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
with respect to the connection induced by the Levi-Civita connection of
(\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}},g^{c})
.

Proof.

By looking at the form of the metric
g^{c}
at
p\in H
given in (
16
) we see that the vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}
,
a=1,\dots,n-1
are orthogonal to
T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}H
. Moreover, the complex bilinear extension of the metric satisfies

\displaystyle g^{c}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{b}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle g^{c}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{b}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle g^{c}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{b}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{0}+c}{\rho_{0}}\delta_{ab}.

Now it follows from the Koszul formula that for any vector field
W\in\mathfrak{X}^{\mathbb{C}}(H)
the covariant derivatives
\nabla_{W}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{b}}
and
\nabla_{W}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{b}}
must be tangent to
H
.
∎

Given a vector field
K
on
\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
, we shall denote the normal component of the restriction of
K
to the submanifold
H
by
K_{H}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{k}=\{K\in\mathfrak{X}^{\mathbb{C}}(\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}):\nabla^{\perp}(K_{H}^{\perp})=0\}
, the space of complex vector fields on
\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
whose normal component along the submanifold
H
is a parallel section of the normal bundle of
H
. Then
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{k}
.

Proof.

We can write any complex vector field in the form

K=\sum_{a}f_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}}+g_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}+\sum_{k}\phi_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{k}}+\psi_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{k}}+h\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}

with
f_{a},g_{a},\phi_{a},\psi_{k},h\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}})
. By
3.8
, we see that
K\in\mathfrak{k}
if and only if the functions
f_{a},g_{a}
are constant when restricted to the submanifold
H
. Clearly, this is satisfied by the vector fields
Y_{C},Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{a},V_{k},\bar{V}_{k},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}
. Moreover, at
p=(0,w,\tilde{\phi},{\rho_{0}})
we have

[Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{b}](p)=\delta_{ab}\Bigg(w^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{0}}-\bar{w}^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{0}}-2ic\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\Bigg)+\bar{w}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{b}}-w^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{a}},

which is also tangent to
H
and thus is an element of
\mathfrak{k}
. This means that we have verified our claim for the basis
\{Y_{C},Y_{a},\bar{Y_{a}},[Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{b}],V_{k},\bar{V_{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

For every
c>0
, the elements of the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be characterized, modulo Killing fields that are zero at each point of
H
, as those Killing fields on
(\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}},g^{c})
whose normal component along
H
is parallel.

Proof.

Let
K
be such a Killing field. Expand it in the frame
T^{\mathbb{C}}\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}\cong\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}\times(\mathfrak{h}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}})
constructed in Lemma
3.7
. Then the coefficient functions in front of
Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{a}
are constant along
H
. By subtracting a linear combination of
Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{a}
with constant coefficients, we can thus obtain a Killing field that is tangent to
H
. By Lemma
3.6
, this must then coincide with an element of
\alpha((\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1))\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})
along
H
. This shows that, up to addition of a Killing field vanishing along
H
, the vector field
K
lies in
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

3.3
The action of
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}

In the previous section we have constructed an injective (
c
-dependent) infinitesimal action
\alpha:\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
. Since
(\bar{N},g^{c})
is a complete Riemannian manifold, any Killing field on it is necessarily complete. We can therefore integrate the infinitesimal action
\alpha:\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
to an isometric action of the corresponding simply connected group
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
. In this way we obtain a group homomorphism
\beta:\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}\to\Isom(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
. Let us collect some useful pieces of notation, which we will repeatedly make use of in the following.

Notation
.

We will abbreviate
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
and denote its image
\beta(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
by
\mathcal{G}
. The subgroups
\beta(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1))
and
\beta(\Heis_{2n+1})
will be called
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. We will write
\mathcal{F}
for the intersection
\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{H}
. We will also consider
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
; the restriction of
\beta
to
\tilde{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
will be denoted by
\beta^{\prime}
. Its image is
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\beta^{\prime}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
, and we abbreviate
\beta^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))
to
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
(note that
\beta^{\prime}(\Heis_{2n+1})=\mathcal{H}
). Finally, we set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}
.

We are interested in
\mathcal{G}
, which acts effectively on
\Isom(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
. In order to understand
\mathcal{G}
, we have to determine the kernel of
\beta
.

Since the infinitesimal action
\alpha:\mathfrak{u}(1,n-1)\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{isom}(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
is injective,
\ker\beta
is discrete and normal, hence central. The elements in
\ker\beta
stabilize every point of
\bar{N}_{n}
, and in particular the point
n_{0}=(0,0,0,\rho_{0})
, for any fixed
\rho_{0}>0
. Thus,
\ker\beta
must be a discrete central subgroup of the stabilizer
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{n_{0}}
. We computed its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}
in
3.5
, and it turns out that we can integrate the vector fields in the center of
\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}
explicitly. This allows us to determine
\ker\beta
, as we will now show.

Lemma 3.11
.

For
n\geq 2
, the kernel of
\beta
is the subgroup of
Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1})\cong\mathbb{R}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
generated by
(2\pi,0,4\pi c)
and
\big(-\frac{2\pi}{n},-2\pi,4\pi\frac{n-2}{n}c\big)
. In particular,
\ker\beta\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

As shown in
3.5
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n_{0}}
of the stabilizer of
n_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)\times\mathfrak{u}(n-1)
. Its two-dimensional center is spanned by the vector fields

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq Y_{C}+2c\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\operatorname{Im}([Y_{a},\bar{Y}_{a}])+2(n-1)c\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}-\mathcal{C}_{1},

as can be easily checked using (
8
). Let us reproduce the explicit coordinate expressions, obtained from (
9
) and (
13
), for convenience:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(w^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{k}}-\bar{w}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{k}}\bigg),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=-in\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bigg(X^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{a}}-\bar{X}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{a}}+w^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{0}}-\bar{w}^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}^{0}}\bigg).

Integrating these vector fields, we see that the corresponding one-parameter groups of diffeomorphisms act as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(X^{a},w^{0},w^{a},\tilde{\phi},\rho)

\displaystyle=(X^{a},e^{-it}w^{0},e^{-it}w^{a},\tilde{\phi},\rho)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(X^{a},w^{0},w^{a},\tilde{\phi},\rho)

\displaystyle=(e^{-int}X^{a},e^{-int}w^{0},w^{a},\tilde{\phi},\rho)

It is clear that the flows are periodic with periods
2\pi
and
\frac{2\pi}{n}
, respectively. Thus, the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-subgroup generated by
\exp(2\pi\mathcal{C}_{1})
and
\exp(\frac{2\pi}{n}\mathcal{C}_{2})
acts trivially on all of
\bar{N}_{n}
and is therefore contained in
\ker\beta
. In fact, it is equal to
\ker\beta
, since we know that
\ker\beta
is certainly contained in the preimage of the stabilizer.

The center of
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
is

Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1})=\exp(\mathbb{R}\cdot C)\times\exp(2\pi\mathbb{Z}\cdot C^{\prime})\times\exp(\mathbb{R}\cdot T)\cong\mathbb{R}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R},

where
C^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{i}{n}\operatorname{diag}(1-n,1,\dots,1)\in\mathfrak{su}(1,n-1)
.

Using (
17
) and combining the fact that
\sum_{a}\operatorname{Im}_{\sigma}[U_{a},U_{a}^{\sigma}]=nC^{\prime}
and that
\alpha
is an anti-homomorphism, we can directly read off that the kernel of
\beta
is the subgroup generated by
(2\pi,0,4\pi c)
and
\big(-\frac{2\pi}{n},-2\pi,4\pi\frac{n-2}{n}c\big)
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

We observe that, for all
c>0
, the subgroup
\ker\beta\subset Z(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
has a non-trivial projection onto the subgroup
Z(\Heis_{2n+1})
.

We will now consider the subgroups
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

For every
n\geq 2
, the following hold:

(i)

The restricted map
\beta:\Heis_{2n+1}\to\mathcal{H}
is an isomorphism.

(ii)

The kernel
\ker\beta
is contained in the subgroup
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)
if
c=0
. If
c>0
, then the kernel of the restricted map
\beta:\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\to\mathcal{U}
is equal to the infinite cyclic group
2\pi\mathbb{Z}\cdot(n-1,n)\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}=Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1))
if
n
is odd and
\pi\mathbb{Z}\cdot(n-1,n)
if
n
is even.

(iii)

The intersection
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{H}
is given by
\langle\beta(0,0,\frac{8\pi c}{n})\rangle
if
n
is even and
\langle\beta(0,0,\frac{4\pi c}{n})\rangle
if
n
is odd. In particular, it is trivial if
c=0
and infinite cyclic if
c>0
.

Proof.

(i)

By
3.11
, the kernel of
\beta
is the subgroup of
Z(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})\cong\mathbb{R}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
generated by
(2\pi,0,4\pi c)
and
\big(-\frac{2\pi}{n},-2\pi,4\pi\frac{n-2}{n}c\big)
. But this group has trivial intersection with
\Heis_{2n+1}
for any value of
c
, so the restriction of
\beta
is injective, hence an isomorphism.

(ii)

We compute
\ker\beta\cap Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1))
, using the generators provided by
3.11
. For
c=0
, our claim is obvious. Now assume that
c>0
and let
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
. The third component of
x(2\pi,0,4\pi c)+y\big(-\frac{2\pi}{n},-2\pi,4\pi\frac{n-2}{n}c\big)
is
4\pi c(x+\frac{n-2}{n}y)
, which vanishes if and only if
nx+(n-2)y=0
, which in turn holds if and only if
(x,y)
is an integral multiple of
(2-n,n)
if
n
is odd and
\frac{1}{2}(2-n,n)
if
n
is even. Thus, if
n
is odd, then
\ker\beta
is generated by the element
(n-2)\cdot(2\pi,0,4\pi c)-n\cdot\big(-\frac{2\pi}{n},-2\pi,4\pi\frac{n-2}{n}c\big)=2\pi\cdot(n-1,n,0)
. If
n
is even we obtain
\pi\cdot(n-1,n,0)
as a generator instead.

(iii)

Since
\dim\mathcal{G}=\dim\mathcal{U}+\dim\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{F}
is discrete and normal in
\mathcal{U}
. Thus

\mathcal{U}\subset Z(\mathcal{U})
. Moreover, since left-translation by
\mathcal{U}
fixes the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\{0\}\subset\mathcal{H}
, we must have
\mathcal{F}\subset Z(\mathcal{H})
. It follows that
\mathcal{F}=Z(\mathcal{U})\cap Z(\mathcal{H})
. The preimage
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{F})=\beta^{-1}(Z(\mathcal{U}))\cap\beta^{-1}(Z(\mathcal{H}))
is therefore a discrete subgroup of the center
\mathbb{R}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
.

By part (i),
\beta
restricts to an isomorphism
\Heis_{2n+1}\to\mathcal{H}
. Consequently, we have
\beta^{-1}(Z(\mathcal{H}))=(\{(0,0)\}\times\mathbb{R})+\ker\beta
. On the other hand
\beta^{-1}(Z(\mathcal{U}))=(\mathbb{R}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\{0\})+\ker\beta
. Now, a point
2\pi\cdot(0,0,\lambda)
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
lies in
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{F})
if and only if we can find some
\kappa\in\ker\beta
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2\pi\cdot(0,0,\lambda)+\kappa=2\pi\cdot(a,b,0)
.
For any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle(0,0,2\pi\lambda)\!+x(2\pi,0,4\pi c)\!-y(\tfrac{2\pi}{n},2\pi,-4\pi\tfrac{n-2}{n}c)=2\pi\big(x\!-\!\tfrac{y}{n},-y,\lambda\!+\!2c(x+\tfrac{n-2}{n}y)\big).

To make the third entry zero, we must have
\lambda=-2c\big(x+\frac{n-2}{n}y\big)\in 2c\cdot\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{1,\frac{n-2}{n}\}
, which equals
\frac{4c}{n}\mathbb{Z}
if
n
is even and
\frac{2c}{n}\mathbb{Z}
if
n
is odd. Thus,
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{F})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot(0,0,\frac{8\pi c}{n})+\ker\beta
if
n
is even and
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{F})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot(0,0,\frac{4\pi c}{n})+\ker\beta
if
n
is odd, which means that
\mathcal{F}=\langle\beta(0,0,\frac{8\pi c}{n})\rangle
in the former case and
\mathcal{F}=\langle\beta(0,0,\frac{4\pi c}{n})\rangle
in the latter, just as claimed.∎

Next, we study
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, which is obtained by restricting
\beta
to
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

For every
n\geq 2
, the following hold:

(i)

If
c=0
, then the kernel of
\beta^{\prime}:\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)\to\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is equal to
2\pi n\mathbb{Z}\subset 2\pi\mathbb{Z}=Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))
. Thus,
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
.

(ii)

If
c>0
then
\beta^{\prime}:\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)\to\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.

(iii)

The subgroup
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}
is given by
\langle\beta^{\prime}(0,0,4\pi c(n-1))\rangle
, hence trivial if
c=0
and infinite cyclic if
c>0
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
c=0
,
3.11
implies that the kernel of
\beta
intersects
Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))\cong 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
in the cyclic subgroup generated by
2\pi n
. The assertion follows.

(ii)

We have
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1)\cong\mathbb{R}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=\beta(\{0\}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))
. By part (ii) of
3.13
, the intersection of
\ker\beta
and
\{0\}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)
is trivial.

(iii)

By the same arguments as in the proof of part (iii) of
3.13
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=Z(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})\cap Z(\mathcal{H})
. The center of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=(\{0\}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
is
\{0\}\times 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, which intersects the kernel of
\beta
in the cyclic subgroup
\mathbb{Z}\cdot(0,-2\pi n,4\pi c(n-1))
. This observation together with a calculation analogous to the proof of part (iii) of
3.13
shows that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\langle\beta^{\prime}(0,0,4\pi c(n-1))\rangle
.∎

The following Proposition is now an immediate application of the results proven in
3.11
,
3.13
and
3.14
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
n\geq 2
and consider
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset\Isom(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
.

(i)

If
c=0
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
.

(ii)

If
c>0
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\cong(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1})/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
,
where
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the infinite cyclic subgroup
\langle\beta^{\prime}(0,0,4\pi c(n-1))\rangle
.
∎

For
c>0
and
n\geq 2
we now consider the cyclic quotient
\hat{N}_{n}^{c}\coloneqq\bar{N}_{n}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, endowed with the induced quaternionic Kähler metric which we continue to denote by
g^{c}
. This amounts to considering the coordinate
\tilde{\phi}
periodic with period
4\pi c(n-1)
. We extend this family to
c=0
by setting
\hat{N}_{n}^{0}=\bar{N}_{n}
.

Since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is central, the action of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
on
\bar{N}_{n}
descends to
\hat{N}_{n}^{c}
. We extend our notation to the resulting action in the obvious way:

Notation
.

We denote by
\hat{\beta}:\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
and
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}:\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\to\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
the corresponding group homomorphisms with images
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
, respectively. We also define
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\hat{\beta}(\Heis_{2n+1})
and

, as well as
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}=\hat{\beta}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(1,n-1))
.

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.16
.

For
n\geq 2
and any value of
c\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\subset\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes(\Heis_{2n+1}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime})

By construction, and the proof of part (iii) of

, the kernel of
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
is generated by
(0,2\pi n,0)
and

With the notation introduced above, we may rephrase this result as follows. Firstly,
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\cong\Heis_{2n+1}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\cap\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\{\id\}
so that

The above results have been formulated for the case

only. The arguments in the case

are very similar and the differences are mostly notational. We state them separately in the next Proposition.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
n=1
and
c\geq 0
arbitrary. Then

(i)

\ker\beta=\mathbb{Z}\cdot(2\pi,4\pi c)\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\cong Z(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}(1)}\ltimes\Heis_{3})

(ii)

The restricted map

is an isomorphism.

(iii)

The kernel of the restricted map
\beta:\widetilde{\mathrm{U}(1)}\to\mathcal{U}
is
2\pi\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}(1)}
if

and trivial if

.

(iv)

The intersection
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{H}
is given by
\langle\beta(0,4\pi c)\rangle
. In particular, it is trivial if
c=0
and infinite cyclic if
c>0
.

(v)

\mathcal{G}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}(1)}\ltimes\Heis_{3})/\mathcal{F}
.

(vi)

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\Heis_{3}
.
∎

In the following, it will be convenient to set
\hat{N}_{1}^{c}\coloneqq\bar{N}_{1}/\mathcal{F}
for
c>0
and
\hat{N}_{1}^{0}\coloneqq\bar{N}_{1}
so that we can speak of
\hat{N}_{n}^{c}
for all

and

.

Our next aim is to prove that
\mathcal{G}
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
are closed subgroups of
\Isom(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
and
\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},\hat{g}^{c})
, respectively. We first consider the subgroup
\hat{\mathcal{U}}=\hat{\beta}(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(1,n-1))\subset\hat{\mathcal{G}}
.

Lemma 3.18
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
c\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathcal{U}}\subset\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
has compact center.

Proof.

The
\mathfrak{u}(1)
-factor of
\Lie(\hat{\mathcal{U}})\cong\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(1,n-1)
is generated by the vector field
Y_{C}
. If
c=0
, it is clear that
Y_{C}
generates a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-subgroup. In the case
c>0
, the periodicity of the coordinate
\tilde{\phi}
ensures that this assertion remains valid, so
\hat{\mathcal{U}}=\mathrm{U}(1)\cdot\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
, where
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
is the group of isometries generated by the subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(1,n-1)
. Since the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-factor is central, we have
Z(\hat{\mathcal{U}})=\mathrm{U}(1)\cdot Z(\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})
, and it therefore suffices to prove that
Z(\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})
is compact. In the case
n=1
,
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
is trivial so there is nothing to prove. For
n\geq 2
,
3.16
shows that
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
, so its center is cyclic of order
n
and in particular finite.
∎

For all
c>0
, the periodicity of the coordinate
\tilde{\phi}
implies that the center of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\hat{\beta}(\Heis_{2n+1})
is compact as well. By
[
Mac93
, Prop. 4.2]
the compactness of the centers of
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
implies that they are closed subgroups of
\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
.

Proposition 3.19
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is a closed subgroup of
\Isom(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})
and
\mathcal{G}
is a closed subgroup of
\Isom(\bar{N}_{n},g^{c})
.

Proof.

We start by proving the result in the case
c>0
. The idea is to exploit the closedness of the subgroups
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
of
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
to prove the first claim, and then apply a lifting argument to establish the second claim.

Note that
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\subset\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is normal, i.e.
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is contained in the normalizer
N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})
, which is a closed subgroup, since it coincides with the normalizer of the Lie algebra of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
. Thus, it suffices to prove that
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is closed as a subgroup of
N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})
. Let us therefore consider a sequence
\{x_{j}\}
in
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
which converges in
N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})
. Under the projection
N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})\to N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})/\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, which is a submersion because
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
is closed, this projects to a sequence in
\hat{\mathcal{U}}/(\hat{\mathcal{U}}\cap\hat{\mathcal{H}})\subset N(\hat{\mathcal{H}})/\hat{\mathcal{H}}
.

It follows from the description of

, and lifts to the covering space, producing a convergent sequence

By definition,

is a discrete subgroup of the center

of

In the case

is a closed subgroup of

and then performing the same lifting argument.
∎

We note the following consequence, which we will use later.

Corollary 3.20
.

Let

and

and consider arbitrary points

and

Quotients and ends of finite volume

4.1
Quotients from arithmetic lattices

The first step, thus, is to construct a suitable discrete subgroup of

In the case

The next case,

with respect to
(\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. It then follows from
[
MM94
, Thm. 2.2]
that the stabilizer
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
of
\Gamma_{2}
is a lattice in
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
. We now take
\Gamma_{2}=L_{d}
and the above discussion shows that
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)}(\Gamma_{2})
is a lattice. We may then form the semi-direct product to obtain a lattice
\bar{\Gamma}=\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\ltimes\Gamma_{2}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
.

If
n\geq 3
, and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a lattice compatible with
(h,\bar{\cdot})
generating a lattice
\Gamma_{2}\subset\Heis_{2n+1}
, then the lattice
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)}(\Gamma_{2})\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
contains a unipotent element and is therefore not co-compact by Godement’s compactness criterion
[
Mor15
, Prop. 5.3.1]
. To find such a unipotent element, we consider the indefinite rational quadratic form given by the real part of
h
on the
2n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Lambda)
. Since
n\geq 3
, i.e.
2n\geq 6
, we can apply Meyer’s theorem
[
Ser73
, Cor. 2 on p.43]
to find a vector
v\in\Lambda
such that
h(v,v)=0
. Furthermore, we can choose
w\in\Lambda
such that
v
and
w
are linearly independent and
h(v,w)=0
. Then
A\in\End(\mathbb{C}^{n})
given by
A=h(\cdot,v)w-h(\cdot,w)v
is not zero, skew-hermitian with respect to
h
and nilpotent, in particular
A\in\mathfrak{su}(1,n-1)
. Thus, the unipotent element
\exp(A)\in\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
stabilizes
\Lambda
, and hence
\Gamma_{2}
, i.e.
\exp(A)\in\Gamma_{1}
. For the lattices
L_{d}
constructed above, we can take
v=e_{1}+e_{2}
and
w=\sqrt{d}(f_{1}+f_{2})
. This shows that in fact also for
n=2
the stabilizer of
L_{d}
in
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)
is not co-compact.
∎

Proceeding as in the proof of
4.2
, we now easily obtain:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary and let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be square-free such that
d\equiv 1,2\mod 4
. Then there exists a lattice
\bar{\Gamma}\subset\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
such that
\bar{\Gamma}\cap Z(\Heis_{2n+1})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}T
, where
T
is the generator of
Z(\Heis_{2n+1})\cong\mathbb{R}
. Choose
c\geq 0
such that
4\pi c
and
\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
\bar{\Gamma}
contains a lattice
\tilde{\Gamma}
such that
(\hat{N}_{n}^{c},g^{c})/\tilde{\Gamma}
is a complete quaternionic Kähler manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times K
, where the fibers
\{t\}\times K
are locally homogeneous and of finite volume. ∎

4.2
The volume of fiberwise quotients by lattices

Given a lattice in
\mathrm{SU}(1,n-1)\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
, we have outlined above how to obtain a quaternionic Kähler manifold
\bar{N}_{n}/\Gamma
which can be viewed as a fiber bundle with locally homogeneous fibers of finite volume. In the previous section we have shown how to obtain infinitely many lattices for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and constructed infinitely many co-compact examples in the case
n\leq 2
. We are now interested in the dependence of the volume of the fibers
\bar{N}_{\rho}/\Gamma\subset\bar{N}_{n}/\Gamma
on
\rho
.

Recall the explicit expression for the one-loop deformed
c
-map metrics on
\bar{N}_{n}
given in (
3
) and (
4
):

\displaystyle g^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}g_{\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho+c}\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+c}{\rho+2c}\bigg(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}-4\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\bar{w}^{0}\mathrm{d}w^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{w}^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\bigg)+\frac{2c}{1-\lvert X\rvert^{2}}\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{X}^{a}\mathrm{d}X^{a}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{\rho}\bigg(\mathrm{d}w^{0}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{0}-\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}^{a}\bigg)+\frac{\rho+c}{\rho^{2}}\frac{4}{1-\lvert X\rvert^{2}}\Big|\mathrm{d}w^{0}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}X^{a}\mathrm{d}w^{a}\Big|^{2},

where

g_{\mathbb{C}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{1-\lvert X\rvert^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert\mathrm{d}X^{a}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\lvert X\rvert^{2}}\bigg|\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\bar{X}^{a}\mathrm{d}X^{a}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg).

Ordering our coordinates as
(\rho,X,w,\tilde{\phi})
, where we wrote
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{n-1})
and
w=(w^{0},\dots,w^{n-1})
, the Gram matrix
G^{c}
of
g^{c}
takes on the following block form:

G^{c}=\begin{pmatrix}g_{\rho\rho}&0&0&0\\
0&g_{XX}&g_{Xw}&g_{X\tilde{\phi}}\\
0&g_{Xw}^{\top}&g_{ww}&g_{w\tilde{\phi}}\\
0&g_{X\tilde{\phi}}^{\top}&g_{w\tilde{\phi}}^{\top}&g_{\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\phi}}\end{pmatrix}

where we have coefficients
g_{\rho\rho},g_{\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\phi}}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathbb{R})
,
g_{XX}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathrm{Mat}_{2n-2,2n-2}(\mathbb{R}))
,
g_{ww}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathrm{Mat}_{2n,2n}(\mathbb{R}))
,
g_{Xw}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathrm{Mat}_{2n-2,2n}(\mathbb{R}))
,
g_{X\tilde{\phi}}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathbb{R}^{2n-2})
and
g_{w\tilde{\phi}}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{N}_{n},\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

Since the level sets of
\rho
are locally homogeneous of finite volume, it
suffices to compute the Gram matrix and its determinant at a point
(\rho,p_{0})
where
X=0
and
w=0
. At such a point the metric
g^{c}
evaluates to

\displaystyle g^{c}(\rho,p_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}\Bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert\mathrm{d}X^{a}\rvert^{2}\Bigg)+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho+c}\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+c}{\rho+2c}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\rho}\bigg(\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho}\lvert\mathrm{d}w^{0}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\lvert\mathrm{d}w^{a}\rvert^{2}\bigg).

In particular, the off-diagonal components
g_{Xw},g_{X\tilde{\phi}},g_{w\tilde{\phi}}
of
G^{c}
all vanish at
(\rho,p_{0})
and we have

g_{\rho\rho}(\rho,p_{0})=\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho+c},\qquad g_{XX}(\rho,p_{0})=\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}\mathds{1}_{2n-2}

and

g_{ww}(\rho,p_{0})=\frac{2}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho}\mathds{1}_{2}&0\\
0&\mathds{1}_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}\qquad g_{\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\phi}}(\rho,p_{0})=\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}}\frac{\rho+c}{\rho+2c}.

It follows that

\det G^{c}(\rho,p_{0})=\frac{2^{2n-4}}{\rho^{2n+4}}\left(\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}\right)^{2n-2}\left(\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho}\right)^{2}.

Thus,
f=\sqrt{\det G^{c}}=\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}\left(\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho}\right)f_{\mathrm{inv}}
, where
f_{\mathrm{inv}}

is the volume density of the unique invariant volume form
f_{\mathrm{inv}}\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}
in the fiber normalized by
f_{\mathrm{inv}}(p_{0})=2^{n-2}
. Here
\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}
stands for the Lebesgue measure associated with the coordinate system.

Note that we have for any
c\geq 0
and
\rho>0
the inequality

f=\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}\left(1+\frac{c}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{2c}{\rho}\right)f_{\mathrm{inv}}\geq\frac{f_{\mathrm{inv}}}{\rho^{n+2}}.

(20)

On the other hand, if
\rho\geq\rho_{0}>0
then we have for any
c\geq 0
,

f=\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}\left(1+\frac{c}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{2c}{\rho}\right)f_{\mathrm{inv}}\leq C(\rho_{0})\frac{f_{\mathrm{inv}}}{\rho^{n+2}},

(21)

where
C(\rho_{0})=\left(1+\frac{c}{\rho_{0}}\right)^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{2c}{\rho_{0}}\right)>0
.

Fix
\rho_{0}>0
and let
D
denote a fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma
on the fiber
\bar{N}_{\rho_{0}}
. Then we deduce from (
21
) that there exists some
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\vol_{\bar{N}_{\rho\geq\rho_{0}}/\Gamma}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{0},\rho]}/\Gamma}=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\int_{D\times[\rho_{0},\rho]}f\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho^{n+2}=\frac{C_{1}}{(n+1)\rho_{0}^{n+1}}}

Similarly, we obtain from (
20
) the existence of a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\times[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}f\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho^{n+2}}=\frac{C_{2}}{(n+1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}_{1}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}^{n+1}}\bigg)

for any
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{0}
. We summarize this discussion as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
c\geq 0
and consider the quaternionic Kähler manifold
(\bar{N}_{n}/\Gamma,g^{c})
. Then for each
\rho_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we have

\vol(\bar{N}_{\rho\geq\rho_{0}}/\Gamma)<\infty,

and

\vol(\bar{N}_{[\rho,\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma)\to\infty,\quad\text{as $\rho\to 0$}.\vskip-19.91684pt

∎

Remark 4.6
.

We may write
\frac{f}{f_{\mathrm{inv}}}=\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}\left(\frac{\rho+c}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\rho+2c}{\rho}\right)=\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}P\left(\frac{c}{\rho}\right)
, where
P
is a polynomial of degree
n
with positive coefficients and constant term equal to
1
. From this one may obtain the explicit formula

\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma}=V(D)\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}}P\left(\frac{c}{\rho}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho,\qquad V(D)=\int_{D}f_{\mathrm{inv}}\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}.

Analyzing this expression gives the leading asymptotics

\vol_{\bar{N}_{\rho\geq\rho_{0}}/\Gamma}=k\rho_{0}^{-n-1}+O(\rho_{0}^{-n-2}),\qquad k=\frac{V(D)}{n+1}

when
\rho_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, while the asymptotics of
\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma}
for
\rho_{1}\rightarrow 0
depends on
c
.
For
c=0
the volume is simply

\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma}=k(\rho_{1}^{-n-1}-\rho_{0}^{-n-1})\sim k\rho_{1}^{-n-1},\quad\rho_{1}\rightarrow 0,

whereas for
c>0
it grows like
k_{1}\rho_{1}^{-2n-1}
:

\rho_{1}^{2n+1}\vol_{\bar{N}_{[\rho_{1},\rho_{0}]}/\Gamma}=k_{1}+O(\rho_{1}),\quad k_{1}=\frac{2c^{n}}{2n+1}k

In the case
n=1
there are no
X
-coordinates and the above computations simplify considerably. The Gram matrix
G^{c}
takes values in
\mathrm{Mat}_{4,4}(\mathbb{R})
and
\frac{i}{2}\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}
is a left-invariant volume form on
\Heis_{3}
. The determinant
\det G^{c}
is therefore a function of
\rho
alone, i.e.
f_{\mathrm{inv}}
is constant.

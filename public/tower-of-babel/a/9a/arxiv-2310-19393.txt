1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
T
will denote a bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. In the situations we consider,
T
also satisfies the following assumptions.

(A1)

T
is
analytic
, i.e.
\bigcap_{n\geq 1}T^{n}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
.

(A2)

T
is
expansive
, i.e.
\|Tx\|\geq\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

(A3)

\dim\ker T^{*}=1
.

(A4)

T
is
cyclic
with respect to any non-zero
e\in\ker T^{*}
.

It is known that
T
satisfies (A1)–(A3) if and only if
T
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
, the
forward shift
acting on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) of de Branges–Rovnyak type
[
24
, Theorem 4.6]
. A brief introduction to various concepts and notation will be given in the next section.

We are motivated by the study of certain classes of operators, namely
Dirichlet type operators
and
higher order isometries
. For this purpose, we define the higher order
defect operators

\{\Delta^{(n)}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
by
\Delta^{(0)}:=I
, and
\Delta^{(n)}:=T^{*}\Delta^{(n-1)}T-\Delta^{(n-1)}
for
n\geq 1
. Note that
T
is expansive if and only if the standard defect operator
\Delta:=\Delta^{(1)}\geq 0
.

The operator
T
is of
Dirichlet type
if it satisfies (A1) and (A3), together with the alternating defect condition
(-1)^{n}\Delta^{(n)}\leq 0
for all
n\geq 1
. The alternating defect condition clearly implies (A2). A result by Aleman
[
2
]
states that
T
is of Dirichlet type if and only if
T
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
, where
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is the superharmonically weighted Dirichlet space associated to some finite positive measure
\mu
on the closed unit disc
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Since polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, Dirichlet type operators satisfy (A4).

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is a RKHS, but at the same time notoriously difficult to identify the reproducing kernel associated to a given
\mu
. Another result by Aleman and Malman
[
3
]
implies that since the
backward shift

L
is a contraction on
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, the kernel is in fact of de Branges–Rovnyak type, i.e.
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is
equal to

\mathcal{H}[B]
for some function
B
in the (normalized)
Schur class

\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
. Our first result is a qualitative statement about the relation between
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
and
\mathcal{H}[B]
in the case where
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
has finite rank defect operator.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
, and
\Delta=T^{*}T-I
. Take
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
such that
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}(\mu)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\mu
is finitely atomic.

(ii)

\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta<\infty
.

(iii)

B
is rational.

If either condition holds, then
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\delta_{\lambda_{j}}
, where
n=\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=\mathrm{rank\ }B=\deg B
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
, and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are distinct.

As a complementary result, a given space
\mathcal{H}[B]
coincides with
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
for some discrete measure if and only if the defect operator of
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
takes a particular form.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
is such that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
and polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}[B]
. Let
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
and
\Delta=T^{*}T-I
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a finite positive discrete measure
\mu
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
.

(ii)

There exists
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots>0
and distinct
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
, where the sum converges in the strong operator topology.

We write
b
in place of
B
in the case where
B
is scalar-valued. The problem of identifying certain
\mathcal{H}(b)
with local Dirichlet spaces was indicated in
[
28
]
, pioneered by Sarason
[
31
]
, and then further studied in
[
10
,
12
,
15
]
. As a demonstration of our results, we recover the following result, which is a reformulation of
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a finite positive measure
\mu
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}(b)
.

(ii)

There exists
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{D}
such that
0<|\gamma|\leq 1-|\beta|
and
b(z)=\frac{\gamma z}{1-\beta z}
.

If either condition holds, then
\mu=c\delta_{\lambda}
for some
c>0
and
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

Assume
(i)
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have that
b
is rational of degree
1
with
b(0)=0
. So
b(z)=\frac{\gamma z}{1-\beta z}
for some
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
b
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|b(z)|\leq 1
, we obtain
\beta\in\mathbb{D}
and
0<|\gamma|\leq 1-|\beta|
. Moreover,
M_{z}\mathcal{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{H}(b)
, which is equivalent to the “non-extremal” condition that
\log(1-|b|^{2})
is integrable on the unit circle. This implies
\gamma\in\mathbb{D}
.

Assume
(ii)
. Then
M_{z}\mathcal{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{H}(b)
. By
[
18
, Theorem 18.22]
,
L^{\ast}=T-b\otimes Lb
. So
T^{\ast}=L+Lb\otimes b
and
\Delta=T^{*}T-I=(1+\left\|b\right\|^{2})Lb\otimes Lb
. Since
Lb=\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta z}
, it follows that
T^{*}(Lb)=\alpha(Lb),\alpha=\beta+\langle Lb,b\rangle
. Thus the smallest closed
T^{*}
-invariant subspace
[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}
containing
\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta
is
\mathbb{C}(Lb)
. So
Lb
is a reproducing kernel in
\mathcal{H}(b)
, see the remark after Lemma
3.2
.
Then Theorem
1.2
implies the existence of the discrete measure
\mu
.

The final assertion is immediate from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

In the corollary, the case
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
originally studied by Sarason
[
31
]
corresponds exactly to
|\gamma|=1-|\beta|
, see also
[
23
]
.

We now direct our attention to the so-called higher order isometries studied by Agler and Stankus
[
5
]
. The operator
T
is called an
n
-isometry
whenever
\Delta^{(n)}=0
. The case
n=2
was originally studied by Agler
[
1
]
. It is well-known that if
T
is a
2
-isometry satisfying (A1) and (A3), then (A2) and (A4) are also satisfied; in fact
T
is of Dirichlet type. For
n\geq 3
, these implications no longer hold. Hence the explicit mention of (A2) and (A4).

An operator
T
is an
n
-isometry satisfying (A1), (A2), (A4) if and only if
T
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})

[
29
]
. Here,
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})
is an allowable
n
-tuple of distributions on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, with
\mu_{0}
being normalized Lebesgue measure, and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
is the corresponding harmonically weighted space of analytic functions. If we add assumption (A3), then
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
coincides with some
\mathcal{H}[B]
, similar to the case of Dirichlet type operators.

It is rather plain that if
\vec{\mu}
consists exclusively of non-negative measures, then
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})
is expanding, see
[
29
, Remark 6.9]
. We shall see that if
n\geq 3
, then this excludes the possibility that
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta<\infty
, see Corollary
4.7
. In fact, we obtain a complete description of allowable tuples for which
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})
satisfies (A1)–(A4) and has finite rank defect operator. In the interest of space, we state only the rank one case here, and refer to Theorem
4.5
for the general case.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
satisfies (
2.1
), and that
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is a strict
n
-isometry for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then
n=2m
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Moreover, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and a polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}z^{j}
with the following properties:
p(\lambda)\neq 0
, and if
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
is given by

(1.1)

\hat{\mu}_{i}(k)=\overline{\hat{\mu}_{i}(-k)}\\
=\overline{\lambda}^{k}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=0}^{m-1}c_{j_{1}}\overline{c_{j_{2}}}\lambda^{j_{1}-j_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-1}{l}\binom{l+j_{1}}{m-1}\binom{l+k+j_{2}}{m-1},

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq i\leq 2m-1
, then
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

Conversely, if
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,
p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}z^{j}
with
p(\lambda)\neq 0
, and
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
is given by (

), then
\vec{\mu}
is a normalized allowable
2m
-tuple, and there exists
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
such that
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

As a final remark we point out that the notion of allowable tuples is far from fully understood. Theorem
1.5
and related results provide many new examples of allowable tuples.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation and terminology

We will use the basic notation
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<1\}
,
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|=1\}
,
\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\mathbb{D}\cup\mathbb{T}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq x}=\mathbb{Z}\cap[x,\infty)
. Integration with respect to area measure on
\mathbb{D}
and arc length measure on
\mathbb{T}
are denoted by
dA
and
|dz|
respectively. We use
\delta_{\lambda}
to denote the Dirac measure for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. The standard Kronecker delta is given by
\delta_{a,b}=\delta_{a}(\{b\})
.

Given a set
E\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, we let
\bigvee E
denote the closed linear span of
E
, and
[E]_{T}
the smallest closed
T
-invariant subspace containing
E
. For singletons
E=\{e\}
, we write
[e]_{T}
rather than
[E]_{T}
. If
[e]_{T}=\mathcal{H}
for some vector
e\in\mathcal{H}
, then we say that
T
is
cyclic
and that
e
is a
cyclic vector
for
T
.

Given an analytic function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
, we let
f^{(n)}
denote the
n
th derivative of
f
. We also define the corresponding forward shift

\displaystyle M_{z}f\colon z\mapsto zf(z),

and backward shift

\displaystyle Lf\colon z\mapsto\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}.

We write
(M_{z},\mathcal{X})
or
(L,\mathcal{X})
to denote the restriction of these operators to some space
\mathcal{X}
of analytic functions. We sometimes use the same notation to denote the corresponding operations acting on vector-valued functions.

Given a set
X
, and two functions
f,g\colon X\to[0,\infty]
, we write
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
to indicate the existence of
C\in[0,\infty)
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
whenever
x\in X
and
g(x)<\infty
. If
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
and
g(x)\lesssim f(x)
, then we write
f(x)\approx g(x)
.

2.2.
The Schur class
\mathcal{S}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})

Let
B\colon\mathbb{D}\to\ell^{2}
be analytic. If
\|B(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 1
for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then we say that
B
is a
Schur function
. The class of Schur functions is denoted
\mathcal{S}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
. We will be especially interested in Schur functions for which
B(0)=0
. This condition corresponds to a type of normalization. The class of normalized Schur functions is denoted
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
.

We equip
\mathcal{S}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
with the equivalence relation
\sim
given by
B_{1}\sim B_{2}
if and only if
\langle B_{1}(z),B_{1}(\lambda)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\langle B_{2}(z),B_{2}(\lambda)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
for all
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
. If the equivalence class
[B]
has a representative
(b_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
where only finitely many
b_{k}
are non-zero, then we say that
B
has
finite rank
. If
B
has finite rank and
n
is the minimal number of non-zero
b_{k}
among all representations of
[B]
, then we say that
\mathrm{rank\ }B=n
. If
B
does not have finite rank, then we say that
\mathrm{rank\ }B=\infty
. If
B\in\mathcal{S}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{rank\ }B=n<\infty
, then we sometimes write
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C})
, and allow ourselves the somewhat sloppy notation
B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
. Note that the particular case
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
is just the unit ball of the Hardy algebra
H^{\infty}
.

Let
H^{2}
be the Hardy space. Let
B\in\mathcal{S}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
. Suppose that
a\in H^{2}
satisfies
|a(z)|^{2}+\|B(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=1
for
|dz|
-a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
. Such an
a
is called a
mate
of
B
. The existence of a mate is equivalent to the condition

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left(1-\|B(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)\,\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}>-\infty,

see for instance
[
19
, Chapter II.4]
. In fact, the above condition guarantees the existence of an
outer
mate function
a
. Such functions are unique up to multiplicative factors
c\in\mathbb{T}
. In particular, if
a
is a mate of
B
, if
a
is outer, and if
a(0)>0
, then
a
is uniquely determined, and we call
a

the mate
of
B
. In the case of rank one Schur functions,
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
has a mate if and only if
B
is a non-extreme point in the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
.

Lastly, suppose
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C})
, and
B=\frac{1}{q}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
for some polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},q
. We then say that
B
is
rational
. For such a representation of
B
, define

\displaystyle N:=\max_{p\in\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},q\}}\deg p.

If
N
is minimal over all such representations, then we say that
B
has
degree
N
,
\deg B=N
. If
B
is not rational, then we say that
\deg B=\infty
.

2.3.
de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
\mathcal{H}[B]

For
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
we define

\displaystyle K^{B}_{w}(z)=\frac{1-\langle B(z),B(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

The function
K^{B}\colon(z,w)\mapsto K^{B}_{w}(z)
is positive definite, so
K^{B}
is the reproducing kernel of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). The RKHS is uniquely determined by
K^{B}
. However,
K^{B}
does not determine
B
, but rather the equivalence class
[B]
. To emphasize this, the RKHS with reproducing kernel
K^{B}
is denoted
\mathcal{H}[B]
. The exception is when
B\sim(b,0,0,\ldots)
, i.e.
\mathrm{rank\ }B=1
, in which we write
\mathcal{H}(b)
. We call
\mathcal{H}[B]
the de Branges–Rovnyak space.

\mathcal{H}[B]
spaces have a rich history originating from
[
13
]
. We refer to
[
7
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
30
]
for more general background. A characteristic property of the spaces
\mathcal{H}[B]
is that they are RKHS in which the backward shift is a contraction. We state a special case of
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a RKHS. The following are equivalent.

(i)

There exists
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
such that
K^{B}
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}
is closed under the backward shift
L
, and
(L,\mathcal{H})
is a contraction. Moreover, the constant function
1\in\mathcal{H}
, and
f\in\mathcal{H}
implies
f(0)=\langle f,1\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We henceforth use
\mathcal{H}[B]
as generic notation for any RKHS satisfying the equivalent conditions of Proposition
2.1
. Indeed, since a RKHS is uniquely determined by its reproducing kernel, any RKHS satisfying the above conditions is
equal
to
\mathcal{H}[B]
for some
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
.

The conditions in Proposition
2.1
do not guarantee that
\mathcal{H}[B]
is invariant under
M_{z}
. If on the other hand
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
, then
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
is bounded by the closed graph theorem. If
\mathrm{rank\ }B<\infty
, then a result by Aleman and Malman
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
states that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
if and only if (
2.1
) holds. Another formulation of this is that
M_{z}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}[B]
if and only if
B
has a mate function.

Assume now that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
. Since
(L,\mathcal{H}[B])
is a contraction,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}[B]}^{2}=\|LM_{z}f\|_{\mathcal{H}[B]}^{2}\leq\|M_{z}f\|_{\mathcal{H}[B]}^{2},

so
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
is expansive. The following result by Gu, Richter, and the first author
[
24
]
puts
\mathcal{H}[B]
-spaces in the context of general bounded analytic expansive operators.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with
\dim\ker T^{*}=1
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

T
is analytic and expansive.

(ii)

There exists
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
such that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
and
T
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
.

Similar to Proposition
2.1
, we have stated a special case of a more general result, in this case
[
24
, Theorem 4.6]
. The restriction
\dim\ker T^{*}=1
can be removed by considering vector-valued analogues of
\mathcal{H}[B]
.

3.
Dirichlet-type shifts on finite rank
\mathcal{H}[B]
-spaces.

3.1.
Weighted Dirichlet spaces
\mathcal{D}(\mu)

Let
f\in H^{2}
and
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. If
f(\lambda)
exists (in the sense of a radial limit), then we define the corresponding
local Dirichlet integral
by

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}(f)=\int_{z\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\frac{f(z)-f(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\right|^{2}\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}.

Guided by for instance
[
16
, Section 7.1, and Theorem 7.2.5]
, we adopt the convention that
D_{\lambda}(f)=\infty
if
f(\lambda)
does not exist.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. We define the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\{f\in H^{2}\mid\int_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}D_{\lambda}(f)\,d\mu(\lambda)<\infty\},

with norm given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}D_{\lambda}(f)\,d\mu(\lambda).

The measure
\mu
can be uniquely identified with the positive superharmonic function

\displaystyle U_{\mu}\colon z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\left|\frac{1-\zeta\bar{z}}{\zeta-z}\right|^{2}\,\frac{d\mu(\zeta)}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\,d\mu(\zeta),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

for which it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}D_{\lambda}(f)\,d\mu(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}U_{\mu}(z)\,dA(z).

Moreover, any positive superharmonic function may be obtained in this way. Hence the term
weighted Dirichlet space
.

The special case where
\mu=\delta_{\lambda}
is called a
local Dirichlet space
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{1}
. For measures supported on
\mathbb{T}
, the space
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
space was introduced in
[
27
]
. The general case was thoroughly investigated in
[
2
]
. The theory of
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
spaces has received a lot of attention in the recent years, and we refer to
[
2
,
6
,
16
]
for more background on the subject.

The fact that
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
is of Dirichlet type relies in part on the useful identity

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|M_{z}f\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}^{2}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}^{2}+\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f(z)|^{2}\,d\mu(z),

see for example
[
25
, (5.2)]
.

Since
H^{2}
is a RKHS and
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is contractively contained in
H^{2}
,
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is also a RKHS. Even though this does not yield an explicit formula for the kernel, it is plain that any
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mu)
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle f,1\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}=\langle f,1\rangle_{H^{2}}=f(0).

By Proposition
2.1
, we conclude that there exists
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
such that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
.

3.2.
The finite rank case

The first main result of this section is Theorem
1.1
which is about the relation between
\mathcal{H}[B]
with finite rank and
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
for finitely atomic measures.
As preparation for the proof of this theorem, we begin with a simple necessary condition for when an
\mathcal{H}[B]
-space is also a
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
-space.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
, and suppose that
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}(\mu)
for some
\mu
. Then
\mathrm{rank\ }B=\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=\deg B
.

Proof.

It is known that for an
\mathcal{H}[B]
space we have
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=\mathrm{rank\ }B
, see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 4.6 and Lemma 5.1]
. If
\mathrm{rank\ }B=\infty
, then
\deg B=\infty
by definition. It remains to prove that
\deg B=\mathrm{rank\ }B
whenever the latter is finite.

Assuming
B=(b_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
,
M_{z}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}[B]
if and only if (
2.1
) holds, see
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
. Since
M_{z}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, we conclude from
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
that
\deg B=\dim[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}
. To finish the proof it is now sufficient to show that
[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}=\overline{\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta}
. The fact that
\overline{\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta}
is
T^{*}
-invariant is implied by the inequality

\displaystyle T^{*2}T^{2}-2T^{*}T+I=T^{*}\Delta T-\Delta\leq 0,

which is immediate from (
3.1
).
∎

The following lemma can be derived from the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 7.2]
. This lemma plays an important role for our investigation of the relation between
\mu
and
B
, we include a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})
be a rational Schur function of degree
n
such that
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
is a bounded operator and
B(0)=0
. Let
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
and
\mathcal{N}=[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}
. Then
\mathcal{N}
is
L
-invariant and
\dim\mathcal{N}=n
. Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}[B]\ominus\mathcal{N}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M},\|\varphi\|=1
. Let
a
be the mate of
B
.

Suppose
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{i})
is the characteristic polynomial of
L|\mathcal{N}
, and
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\overline{\lambda_{i}})
is the characteristic polynomial of
T^{*}|\mathcal{N}
. Then

(i)

a(z)=a(0)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\overline{\lambda_{i}}z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)},

(ii)

\mathcal{H}[B]=\varphi H^{2}\oplus\mathcal{N}
,
|\varphi(z)|=|a(z)|
on
\mathbb{T}
and

\varphi(z)=e^{it}a(0)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)},t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Note that by
[
24
, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.2]
,
\mathcal{N}=[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}=[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{L}
, and
\dim\mathcal{N}=\deg B=n
. Then by
[
24
, (7.1)]
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\left\{\frac{p(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)}:p\text{ is a polynomial of degree }<n\right\},

and
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)
is a constant multiple of the lowest common denominator of the
b_{i}
’s.
It is clear that
T|\mathcal{M}
is an isometry. Since
\dim(\mathcal{H}[B]\ominus z\mathcal{H}[B])=1
,
\dim\mathcal{N}=n<+\infty
, we have
\dim(\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M})=1
, see e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
. So
T|\mathcal{M}
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},H^{2})
. Thus
\mathcal{M}=\varphi H^{2}
,
P_{\mathcal{M}}K^{B}_{w}(z)=\frac{\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}}{1-z\overline{w}}
and
\mathcal{H}[B]=\varphi H^{2}\oplus\mathcal{N}
. Since
K^{B}_{w}(z)=P_{\mathcal{N}}K^{B}_{w}(z)+P_{\mathcal{M}}K^{B}_{w}(z)
we conclude that

1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}|b_{i}(z)|^{2}=(1-|z|^{2})\|P_{\mathcal{N}}K^{B}_{z}\|^{2}+|\varphi(z)|^{2}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since all functions in
\mathcal{N}
are bounded and
\mathcal{N}
is finite dimensional we let
|z|\to 1
and obtain that
|\varphi|^{2}=|a|^{2}
on
\mathbb{T}
.

Let
C=(I-P_{\mathcal{N}})M_{z}P_{\mathcal{N}},A=P_{\mathcal{N}}M_{z}P_{\mathcal{N}}
. Then

T=\left[\begin{matrix}T|\mathcal{M}&C\\
0&A\end{matrix}\right].

It follows from

T^{*}T-I=\left[\begin{matrix}0&(T|\mathcal{M})^{*}C\\
C^{*}T|\mathcal{M}&C^{*}C+A^{*}A-I\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0

that
(T|\mathcal{M})^{*}C=0
. Note that
\ker(T|\mathcal{M})^{*}=\mathbb{C}\varphi
. Thus
\mathrm{rank\ }C=1
and there exists
f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}
such that
Cf_{1}=\varphi
. Then there exists
f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\varphi=zf_{2}-f_{1}
. Thus by (
3.2
),
\varphi(z)=\frac{h(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)}
for some polynomial
h
of degree
\leq n
.

Since
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}=\varphi H^{2}
, we have for any
g\in\mathcal{N}\subseteq H^{2}
,
\varphi g\in\mathcal{M}
. So
hg\in\mathcal{M}
or
P_{\mathcal{N}}h(M_{z})|\mathcal{N}=0
. Let
\tilde{h}(z)=\overline{h(\overline{z})}
, then
\tilde{h}(M_{z}^{*})|\mathcal{N}=0
. Since by
[
24
, Lemma 7.1]
, the minimal polynomial of
T^{*}|\mathcal{N}
equals the characteristic polynomial of
T^{*}|\mathcal{N}
and it has degree
n
, we conclude that
\tilde{h}
must be a multiple of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\overline{\lambda_{i}})
. This implies that
\varphi(z)=\gamma\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. Now
a
is outer, but has the same modulus as
\varphi
on
\mathbb{T}
, hence
a(z)=a(0)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\overline{\lambda_{i}}z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}z)}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We remark here that in the setting of Lemma
3.2
, the functions in
\mathcal{H}(B)
have nontangential limits at
\lambda_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,n
and
\mathcal{N}
is spanned by the reproducing kernels
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
or the derivatives of
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
when the
\lambda_{i}
has multiplicities
\geq 2
.

Now we can prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}(\mu)
for some
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\delta_{\lambda_{j}}
. By (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,f\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}|f(\lambda_{j})|^{2},

i.e.
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
. Hence,
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=n
and
(i)
implies
(ii)
. By Lemma
3.1
,
B
is rational with
\deg B=n
, so
(ii)
implies
(iii)
.

For the final implication, suppose
B
is rational with degree
n
. Let
\mathcal{N}=[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}}
, and
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}[B]\ominus\mathcal{N}
. By Lemma
3.1
,
\dim\mathcal{N}=\deg B=\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=n
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M}
,
\|\varphi\|=1
. By Lemma
3.2
we have that
\mathcal{H}[B]=\varphi H^{2}\oplus\mathcal{N}
, and
\varphi(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})}{q(z)}
for some polynomial
q(z)
and some
\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Since
\varphi\in\left(\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta\right)^{\perp}
, (
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle 0=\langle\Delta\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|\varphi(z)|^{2}d\mu(z).

Thus
|\varphi|^{2}=0

\mu
-a.e. Since
|\varphi(z)|^{2}>0
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\backslash\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
, we obtain that
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\delta_{\lambda_{j}}
. To see that
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are distinct in this case, we note that if this is not so, then
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=\deg B
would be strictly less than
n
.
∎

If
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
is a rational Schur function such that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
, then the polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}[B]
(
[
3
, Theorem 5.5]
). When
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
is such that
M_{z}\mathcal{H}[B]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[B]
and polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}[B]
, it was proved in
[
9
, Lemma 3.6]
that for
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
K_{\lambda}^{B}
exists if and only if
\overline{\lambda}
is an eigenvalue of
M_{z}^{*}
, which is equivalent to the condition that every function in
\mathcal{H}(B)
has a non-tangential limit at
\lambda
.

Now we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
. By (
3.1
),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,f\rangle=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f(z)|^{2}d\mu(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}|f(\lambda_{i})|^{2},\quad f\in D(\mu).

We conclude that
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
, i.e.
(i)\implies(ii)
.

For the reverse implication, we use that
T^{*}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}=\overline{\lambda_{i}}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
, so

\displaystyle T^{*}\Delta T=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}T^{*}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes T^{*}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}.

Thus for
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}T^{*j}T^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}(T^{*j}T^{j}-T^{*(j+1)}T^{(j+1)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}T^{*j}(-\Delta)T^{j}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{2j}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}(1-|\lambda_{i}|^{2})^{n-1}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\leq 0.

It follows that
T
is of Dirichlet-type. By
[
2
, Theorem IV.2.5]
there exists a finite positive measure
\mu
and a unitary operator
U\colon\mathcal{H}[B]\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mu)
that intertwines
T
with
T_{\mu}=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
, i.e.
UT=T_{\mu}U
. The intertwining relation implies that
U^{*}
maps
\ker T_{\mu}^{*}
onto
\ker T^{*}
. Since
\ker T_{\mu}^{*}
consists precisely of the constant functions on
\mathbb{D}
, we may conclude that
U1=c
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{T}
. Since polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, we conclude that
Uf=cf
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}[B]
. In other words,
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}(\mu)
. Since
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
, (
3.3
) implies that
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
.
∎

3.3.
Construction for the reproducing kernel

Now we discuss how to find the rational Schur function
B
such that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
when
\mu
is a finitely atomic measure. Suppose
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
with
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. If all the
\lambda_{i}
are in
\mathbb{T}
, then
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}(\mu))
is a
2
-isometry. In this case, it is explained in
[
24
, Section 11]
, see also
[
32
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
8
, Theorem 6.4]
. If
\mu
is supported at one point, i.e.
n=1
, then it is explained in
[
31
,
10
,
12
,
15
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider a finitely atomic measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
, where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. For any such measure, there exists a rational Schur function
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
with
\mathrm{rank\ }B=\deg B=n
, and
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
. Furthermore,
B\sim\frac{1}{q}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, where
q
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
satisfy the following relations,

\displaystyle|q(z)|^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}|z-\lambda_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}|z-\lambda_{j}|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{T},

and

\displaystyle p_{i}(z)=\langle PX(z),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

Here
P
is an upper triangular
n\times n
matrix,
X(z)=(z^{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
, and
\{e_{i}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

The norm in
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
is

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}D_{\lambda_{i}}(f).

Let
\mathcal{N}=[\mathrm{ran\ }\Delta]_{T^{*}},\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}(B)\ominus\mathcal{N}
. Suppose
B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})=\frac{1}{q}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, where
p_{i}
are polynomials with
\deg p_{i}\leq n
, and
q
is a constant multiple of the least common denominator of the
b_{i}
’s with
q(0)=1/a(0)
,
a
being the mate of
B
. Since
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{\mu}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{\mu}
, we see that
\mathcal{N}=\bigvee\{K_{\lambda_{i}}^{\mu}=K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\mid i=1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M},\|\varphi\|=1
. Then by Lemma
3.2
we have
\mathcal{H}[B]=\varphi H^{2}\oplus\mathcal{N}
,
\varphi(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})}{q(z)}
and
a(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\overline{\lambda_{i}}z)}{q(z)}
.
Now using
\varphi(\lambda_{i})=0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
we obtain for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle 0=\langle z^{k}\varphi,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle z^{k}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{H^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{z^{k}\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}}{|z-\lambda_{i}|^{2}}\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}z^{k}|a(z)|^{2}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{i}}{|z-\lambda_{i}|^{2}}\right)\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}.

Thus
|a(z)|^{2}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{i}}{|z-\lambda_{i}|^{2}}\right)=1,a.e.~\mathbb{T}
, and so

\displaystyle|q(z)|^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}|z-\lambda_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}|z-\lambda_{j}|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

Since the
p_{i}(z)
are polynomials, we can assume
p_{i}(z)=\langle PX(z),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
for some
n\times n
matrix
P
. Note that

\displaystyle B(z)B(w)^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q(z)\overline{q(w)}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle PX(z),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\overline{\langle PX(w),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q(z)\overline{q(w)}}\langle PX(z),PX(w)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q(z)\overline{q(w)}}\langle P^{*}PX(z),X(w)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}.

Since
P^{*}P
is positive semi-definite, by Cholesky’s theorem
[
26
, Theorem 4.4]
, we can take
P
to be an upper triangular matrix. The proof is complete.
∎

In the setting of the above lemma,

\displaystyle K^{\mu}_{w}(z)=K^{B}_{w}(z)=P_{\mathcal{N}}K_{w}^{B}(z)+\frac{\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}}{1-z\overline{w}}.

If
\varphi(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})}{q(z)}
is determined, then to obtain
K^{B}_{w}(z)
, it is left to determine
P_{\mathcal{N}}K_{w}^{B}(z)
, which can be done by using the dual basis of
\{K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

To be precise, suppose
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a dual basis of
\{K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, i.e.

\langle f_{i},K_{\lambda_{j}}^{B}\rangle=\delta_{ij},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Since the functions in
\mathcal{N}
have the form
\frac{p(z)}{q(z)}
with
p
polynomials of degree
<n
(
[
24
, (7.1)]
), we get that for each
i
,
f_{i}(z)=d_{i}\frac{\prod_{j\neq i}(z-\lambda_{j})}{q(z)}
, where
d_{i}
is determined by
f_{i}(\lambda_{i})=1
.
Note that

P_{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\otimes f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\otimes K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}.

Then
P_{\mathcal{N}}K_{w}^{B}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(z)\overline{K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}(w)}
. Also for each
j
,
f_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\langle f_{j},f_{i}\rangle
, where
\langle f_{j},f_{i}\rangle
can be calculated by (
3.4
). Finally we solve for
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}
from the linear equations
f_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\lambda_{i}}^{B}\langle f_{j},f_{i}\rangle
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, and obtain
P_{\mathcal{N}}K_{w}^{B}(z)
.

We use the following example to illustrate the above construction.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mu=\delta_{1}+\delta_{0}
. Then the norm in
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is

\|f\|^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+D_{1}(f)+D_{0}(f).

We have
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\mathcal{H}[B]
for some rational Schur function
B
. Note that
K_{0}^{B}(z)=K_{0}^{\mu}(z)=1
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Using the notation as above, we have
\Delta=K_{1}^{B}\otimes K_{1}^{B}+1\otimes 1
,
\varphi(z)=\frac{(z-1)z}{q(z)}
, and
a(z)=\frac{1-z}{q(z)}
, where
q(0)>0
and

|q(z)|^{2}=|z-1|^{2}|z|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|z-1|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

So
q(z)=2-z
. Let
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
be the dual basis of
\{K_{1}^{B},1\}
. Then

f_{1}(z)=\frac{z}{2-z},\quad f_{2}(z)=\frac{2(1-z)}{2-z}.

By calculation, we have
\|f_{1}\|^{2}=2,\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle=2,\|f_{2}\|^{2}=3
. We then obtain

\displaystyle K_{1}^{B}(z)=\frac{-z/2+2}{2-z}.

Thus

\displaystyle K^{B}_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{N}}K_{w}^{B}(z)+\frac{\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}}{1-z\overline{w}}

\displaystyle=f_{1}(z)\overline{K_{1}^{B}(w)}+f_{2}(z)+\frac{1}{1-z\overline{w}}\frac{(z-1)z}{q(z)}\frac{\overline{(w-1)w}}{\overline{q(w)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(z\overline{w})(1/2z\overline{w}-z-\overline{w}+5/2)/((2-z)(2-\overline{w}))}{1-z\overline{w}}.

For the Schur function
B
, we can take
B(z)=(\frac{2z^{2}/\sqrt{10}-\sqrt{10}z/2}{2-z},\frac{z^{2}/\sqrt{10}}{2-z})
.

4.
n
-isometric shifts on finite rank
\mathcal{H}[B]
-spaces

4.1.
The
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
-model for cyclic
n
-isometries

We essentially follow the notation in
[
29
]
, but deviate by using
\Delta^{(n)}
rather than
\beta_{n}(T)
to denote the higher order defect operators. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the space of
C^{\infty}
functions on
\mathbb{T}
, and
\hat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\zeta)\overline{\zeta}^{k}\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi}
denote the standard Fourier coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{D}
. Of particular interest will be the Poisson kernels associated to
z\in\mathbb{D}
, i.e. the smooth functions

\displaystyle P_{z}\colon\zeta\mapsto=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\overline{\zeta}z)^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\zeta\overline{z})^{k},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be the dual of
\mathcal{D}
, the space of distributions on
\mathbb{T}
, and
\hat{\mu}(k)=\mu(\overline{\zeta}^{k})
denote the standard Fourier coefficients of
\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. With these conventions the duality pairing becomes

\displaystyle\langle f,\mu\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}(k)\hat{\mu}(-k),\quad f\in\mathcal{D},\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}.

The Poisson extension of
\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is the harmonic function

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\colon z\mapsto\mu(P_{z})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{\mu}(k)z^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\mu}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

With the standard identification of
\mathcal{D}
as a subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, we say that
f\in\mathcal{D}
is analytic if its Poisson extension is analytic. This happens precisely when
\hat{f}(k)=0
for
k<0
, and we denote the corresponding class of analytic functions by
\mathcal{D}_{a}
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
, the harmonically weighted Dirichlet integral of order
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{\mu,i}(f)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{1}{\pi i!(i-1)!}\int_{r\mathbb{D}}|f^{(i)}(z)|^{2}P_{\mu}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{i-1}dA(z).

For
i=0
we define

\displaystyle D_{\mu,0}(f)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(rz)|^{2}P_{\mu}(rz)\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},

and for
i<0
we use the convention that
D_{\mu,i}(f)=0
.

Let
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{n}
, and define the quadratic form

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}D_{\mu_{i},i}(f),f\in\mathcal{D}_{a}.

Note that
\|1\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}=\hat{\mu}_{0}(0)
. If
\|1\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}=1
and there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq\|zf\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2},f\in\mathcal{D}_{a},

then we say that
\vec{\mu}
is a
normalized allowable
n
-tuple
. For any such tuple, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\vec{\mu}}=\{f\in\mathcal{D}_{a}\mid\|f\|_{\vec{\mu}}=0\},

and define
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
as the completion of
\mathcal{D}_{a}/\mathcal{K}_{\vec{\mu}}
with respect to the norm (induced by)
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\mu}}
. Since
M_{z}
is well defined on
\mathcal{D}_{a}/\mathcal{K}_{\vec{\mu}}
, it extends to be a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

We are now ready to state the model theorem from
[
29
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
29
]
)
.

If
\vec{\mu}\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{n}
is a normalized allowable
n
-tuple, then
T_{\vec{\mu}}=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})
is a bounded
n
-isometry, and
1
is a cyclic unit vector for
T_{\vec{\mu}}
.

Conversely, if
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is an
n
-isometry with a cyclic unit vector
e
, then
\vec{\mu}\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{n}
given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{i}(k)=\overline{\hat{\mu}_{i}(-k)}=\langle\Delta^{(i)}e,T^{k}e\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

is a normalized allowable
n
-tuple, and there exists a unitary map
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
such that
UT=T_{\vec{\mu}}U
and
Ue=1
.

If
T_{j}:\mathcal{H}_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{j},j=1,2
are bounded
n
-isometries with cyclic vectors
e_{j}
, then the associated
n
-tuple
\vec{\mu}_{j}
coincide if and only if there exists a unitary map
U:\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
T_{1}=U^{*}T_{2}U
and
Ue_{1}=e_{2}
.

When
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{n}
is a normalized allowable
n
-tuple, it is known that the leading distribution
\mu_{n-1}
is a nonnegative measure (e.g.
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
). Moreover, it follows from (
4.1
) that
\mu_{n-1}(|p|^{2})=\langle\Delta^{(n-1)}p(T)e,p(T)e\rangle
for any polynomial
p
. Since
e
is cyclic, it follows that
\Delta^{(n-1)}\geq 0
, and
T
is a strict
n
-isometry if and only if
\mu_{n-1}\neq 0
. The remaining distributions
\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{n-2}
may be less regular (a simple example is provided by Corollary
4.3
).

We note that the case
\mu_{0}=\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}
corresponds precisely to the case where the cyclic unit vector
e\in\ker T^{*}
. Recalling that any
n
-isometry is bounded below, one can show that if
e\in\ker T^{*}
, then
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\mu}}
defines a proper norm on
\mathcal{D}_{a}
, e.g.
[
29
, Proposition 7.3]
. If
e\in\ker T^{*}
and
T
is expansive, then we even obtain that
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}\subseteq H^{2}
in the sense of a contractive embedding.

4.2.
n
-isometric shifts on
\mathcal{H}(b)

Recall that if
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
, then
M_{z}\mathcal{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{H}(b)
if and only if
b
satisfies (
2.1
). In this case
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is bounded and expansive. We now outline some results from
[
24
, Section 9]
on when
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is a higher order isometry. The notion of
higher order local Dirichlet spaces
will be central to this discussion.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, then the local Dirichlet space of order
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
at
\lambda
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}=\{p+(z-\lambda)^{m}h:p\text{ is a polynomial of degree }<m\text{ and }h\in H^{2}\}.

The norm on
\mathcal{D}^{m}_{\lambda}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|p+(z-\lambda)^{m}h\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}}^{2}=\|p+(z-\lambda)^{m}h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

If
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}
, then the derivatives
f^{(j)}(\lambda)
exist in the sense of radial limits for
0\leq j\leq m-1
. This allows us to consider the Taylor polynomial

\displaystyle T_{m-1}(f,\lambda)(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{f^{(j)}(\lambda)}{j!}(z-\lambda)^{j}.

It holds that
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}
if and only if
T_{m-1}(f,\lambda)
exists and the higher order local Dirichlet integral

(4.2)

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}^{m}(f)=\int_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{f(z)-T_{m-1}(f,\lambda)(z)}{(z-\lambda)^{m}}\right|^{2}\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}

is finite. In this case,
f(z)=T_{m-1}(f,\lambda)(z)+(z-\lambda)^{m}h(z)
, and
D_{\lambda}^{m}(f)=\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
. We note that if
m=1
, then we obtain the local Dirichlet integral
D_{\lambda}^{1}(f)=D_{\lambda}(f)
discussed above.

If
T
is an
n
-isometry, but not an
(n-1)
-isometry, then we call
T
a
strict

n
-isometry.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Suppose
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
satisfies (
2.1
), and let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is not a strict
(2m+1)
-isometry. Moreover, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is a strict
2m
-isometry.

(ii)

There exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and a polynomial
p
of degree
<m
such that
p(\lambda)\neq 0
and

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}=\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+D_{\lambda}^{m}(pf).

If the above conditions hold, then
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}
with equivalence of norms.

4.3.
The relation between
\mathcal{H}(b)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}

It’s an exercise to show that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Delta^{(i)}=\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-1}{l}T^{*l}\Delta T^{l}

for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, cf.
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
. We will use this to prove Theorem
1.5
, which is the technical main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

If
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
satisfies (
2.1
) and
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is a strict
n
-isometry, then
n=2m
by Theorem
4.2
. Moreover, here exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and a polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}z^{j}
with
p(\lambda)\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}=\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+D_{\lambda}^{m}(pf).

By
[
3
, Theorem 5.5]
, polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}(b)
, in other words
1
is cyclic for
T
. Since
b(0)=0
,
\|1\|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}=1
. By Theorem
4.1
,
T
is unitarily equivalent to
T_{\vec{\mu}}=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})
, where
\vec{\mu}
is the normalized allowable
2m
-tuple given by (
4.1
), and the unitary equivalence preserves the cyclic vector
1
. It follows from
b(0)=0
that
1\in\ker T^{*}
, and therefore
\mu_{0}=\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.2
we obtain that
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

To conclude the proof of the direct part of the theorem, it remains to show that
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1}
are given by (
1.1
). It follows from
[
24
, Lemma 9.3]
that

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(b)}=\frac{(pf)^{(m-1)}(\lambda)\overline{(pg)^{(m-1)}(\lambda)}}{[(m-1)!]^{2}}.

In combination with (
4.3
), we can now compute
\mu_{i}
in accordance with (
4.1
), namely for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{i}(k)

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{(i)}1,T^{k}1\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(b)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-1}{l}\langle\Delta T^{l}1,T^{l}T^{k}1\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(b)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-1}{l}\frac{(z^{l}p)^{(m-1)}(\lambda)\overline{(z^{k+l}p)^{(m-1)}(\lambda)}}{[(m-1)!]^{2}}.

It is now an algebraic exercise to show that the above right-hand side agrees with (
1.1
).

For the converse direction, suppose
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,
p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}z^{j}
with
p(\lambda)\neq 0
, and
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
is given by (
1.1
). By Proposition
2.2
, there exists
b\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
such that (
2.1
) is satisfied and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+D_{\lambda}^{m}(pf).

Note that by Theorem
4.2
,
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
is a strict
2m
-isometry. Since the corresponding
\vec{\mu}
is given by (
1.1
), we see that
\vec{\mu}
is a normalized allowable tuple.
∎

Recall that differentiation
\mu\mapsto\partial\mu
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
corresponds to the Fourier multiplier
\hat{\mu}(k)\mapsto k\hat{\mu}(k)
. In the context of the above theorem, it is natural to consider the modified differentiation operator
D
corresponding to
\hat{\mu}(k)\mapsto|k|\hat{\mu}(k)
. Indeed, since
\hat{\delta}_{\lambda}(k)=\overline{\lambda}^{k}
, and the right-hand side of (
1.1
) takes the shape
p(k)\overline{\lambda}^{k}
for some polynomial
p
, the distributions associated to a
2m
-isometric shift
(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(b))
via Theorem
4.1
are given by
p(D)\delta_{\lambda}
. In the next result, we make this idea precise in the “simplest” case
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m}
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, then there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
|\hat{\mu}(k)|=|\mu(\zeta^{-k})|\lesssim(1+|k|)^{N},k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The smallest such
N
is called the order of
\mu
. In the following, we use the convention that
\binom{n}{j}=0
whenever
j<0
or
j>n
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. The normalized allowable tuple
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1}\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
corresponding to the expansive strict
2m
-isometry
(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{m})
is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\binom{i-1}{m-1}\frac{\prod_{j=i+1-m}^{m-1}(D+j)}{(2m-1-i)!}\delta_{\lambda}

for
1\leq i\leq 2m-2
, and
\mu_{2m-1}=\binom{2m-2}{m-1}\delta_{\lambda}
. In particular,
\mu_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
, and
\mu_{i}
is of order
2m-1-i
for
m\leq i\leq 2m-1
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
1.5
, the distributions
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1}
are given by (
1.1
), with
c_{0}=1
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m-1}=0
. In other words,

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{i}(k)=\overline{\lambda}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-1}{l}\binom{l}{m-1}\binom{l+k}{m-1},\quad k\geq 0.

Since all terms with
l<m-1
vanish, it is immediate that
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m-1}=0
. We henceforth consider
m\leq i\leq 2m-1
. One easily verifies that

\displaystyle\binom{i-1}{l}\binom{l}{m-1}=\binom{i-1}{m-1}\binom{i-m}{i-1-l}.

For
k\geq 0
, the
k
-th Fourier coefficient of
\prod_{j=i+1-m}^{m-1}(D+j)\delta_{\lambda}
is
\prod_{j=i+1-m}^{m-1}(k+j)\overline{\lambda}^{k}
, so in order to finish the proof it suffices to establish the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{l=m-1}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-l}\binom{i-m}{i-1-l}\binom{l+k}{m-1}=\binom{m+k-1}{2m-1-i}.

To this end, denote the above left- and right-hand sides by
\mathrm{LHS}(i,k,m)
and
\mathrm{RHS}(i,k,m)
. It is clear that
\mathrm{LHS}(m,k,m)=\mathrm{RHS}(m,k,m)
. The elementary identity

\displaystyle\binom{N}{M-1}+\binom{N-1}{M-1}=\binom{N}{M}

and a rearrangement of terms yields that

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}(i,k+1,m)-\mathrm{LHS}(i,k,m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=m-1}^{i}(-1)^{i-l}\left[\binom{i-m}{i-l}+\binom{i-m}{i-1-l}\right]\binom{l+k}{m-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=m-1}^{i}(-1)^{i-l}\binom{i+1-m}{i-l}\binom{l+k}{m-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{LHS}(i+1,k,m).

By simple calculation,

\mathrm{RHS}(i,k+1,m)-\mathrm{RHS}(i,k,m)=\mathrm{RHS}(i+1,k,m),

and the desired identity follows by induction with respect to
i
.
∎

4.4.
The relation between
\mathcal{H}[B]
and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}

Suppose
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
,
\mathrm{rank\ }B<\infty
, and
\mathcal{H}[B]
is
M_{z}
-invariant. Let
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
and
\Delta=T^{*}T-I
. Note that
\mathrm{rank\ }B=\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta
. In particular, if
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta=1
and
T
is an
n
-isometry, then
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{H}(b)
is a higher order local Dirichlet space equipped with an equivalent norm according to Theorem
4.2
. For higher finite ranks of
\Delta
,
\mathcal{H}[B]
instead is a finite intersection of such spaces.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
be such that
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}(B))
is bounded and satisfies
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta<\infty
.

Then
T
is a
2m
-isometry, if and only if there are
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
, pairs of integers
(m_{1},n_{1}),\dots(m_{k},n_{k})
such that
1\leq n_{j}\leq m_{j}\leq m
for all
j
, and there are polynomials
\{p_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq k,1\leq i\leq n_{j}}
with degree
p_{ij}\leq m_{j}-1
for
1\leq j\leq k
,
1\leq i\leq n_{j}
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B)}=\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}D^{m_{j}}_{\lambda_{j}}(p_{ij}f).

There is a choice of parameters so that
p_{1j}(\lambda_{j})\neq 0
for each
j
and such that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}=\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta
. If all that is the case, then
\mathcal{H}(B)=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{w_{j}}^{m_{j}}
with equivalence of norms.

In the setting of the above theorem, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}[B]}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\frac{(p_{ij}f)^{(m_{j}-1)}(\lambda_{j})\overline{(p_{ij}g)^{(m-1)}(\lambda_{j})}}{[(m_{j}-1)!]^{2}}.

So proceeding just as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we can now use (
1.1
) to calculate the normalized allowable tuple
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
such that
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}=\mathcal{H}[B]
. Before we state the corresponding higher rank analogue of Theorem
1.5
, we attempt to introduce a more compact notation. For
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, consider the
m\times m
-matrices

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}(k)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{N-1-l}\binom{N-1}{l}\binom{l+j_{1}}{m-1}\binom{l+k+j_{2}}{m-1}\right)_{0\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq m-1}.

To each polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}z^{j}
we associate the vector of monomials
\vec{p}(z)=(c_{j}z^{j})_{0\leq j\leq m-1}
. With this notation, (
1.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{N}(k)=\overline{\hat{\mu}_{N}(-k)}=\overline{\lambda}^{k}\langle\mathcal{M}_{N}(k)\vec{p}(\lambda),\vec{p}(\lambda)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}.

Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{N}(k)
be the matrices given by (
4.5
).

Suppose
B\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{C})
,
\mathrm{rank\ }B<\infty
, and
\mathcal{H}[B]
is
M_{z}
-invariant. If
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{H}[B])
is a
2m
-isometry for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, then there exists
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
, positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
with
1\leq n_{j}\leq m_{j}\leq m
, and polynomials
\{p_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq k,1\leq i\leq n_{j}}
with the following properties:
\deg p_{ij}\leq m_{j}-1
and
p_{1j}(\lambda)\neq 0
for

, and if
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
is given by

(4.6)

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{N}(l)=\overline{\hat{\mu}_{N}(-l)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\overline{\lambda}_{j}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\langle\mathcal{M}_{N}(l)\vec{p}_{ij}(\lambda_{j}),\vec{p}_{ij}(\lambda_{j})\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{m}},

where

and
1\leq N\leq 2m-1

\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

Conversely, if
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
are positive integers with
1\leq n_{j}\leq m_{j}\leq m
,
\{p_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq k,1\leq i\leq n_{j}}
are polynomials with
\deg p_{ij}\leq m_{j}-1
and
p_{1j}(\lambda)\neq 0
for
1\leq j\leq k
, and if
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
is given by (

), then
\vec{\mu}
is a normalized allowable
2m
-tuple, and there exists a rational

with

such that
\mathcal{H}[B]=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
.

We now present a condition for the defect operator on a
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}
space to be of finite rank.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},\mu_{1}\ldots,\mu_{n-1})\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{n}

(n\geq 2)
be a normalized allowable
n
-tuple, and
\mu_{n-1}\neq 0
. Then
T=(M_{z},\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}})
is expansive with
\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta<\infty
, if and only if
T
is expansive,
n=2m
for some
m\geq 1
, and there is a polynomial
p(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z-\lambda_{j})^{m_{j}}
with
k\leq\mathrm{rank\ }\Delta
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{T},j=1,\ldots,k
,
m=\max\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
such that the following holds

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{2m-1}D_{\mu_{s},s-1}(p(T)q)=0,~~q\text{ polynomials},

and (
4.7
) doesn’t hold if
p
is replaced by any factor
p_{1}
of
p
with
\deg p_{1}<\deg p
.

Proof.

By

Example 4.13

It was noted in

We now complement this with an example of

does not control

and

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{D_{\mu_{0},0}(f)+D_{\mu_{n-1},n-1}(f)}{\|f\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}}=\infty.

Once again we take
\vec{\mu}=(\frac{|dz|}{2\pi},0,(D+1)\delta_{1},2\delta_{1})
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mu}}=\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}=\mathcal{H}(b)
for some

is contractively contained in

. Consequently, we may disregard the term

in (
4.9
), so if (
4.9
) does not hold, then
D_{\mu_{3},3}(f)\lesssim\|f\|_{\vec{\mu}}^{2}
. We now show that this leads to a contradiction.

Consider a function
f=(z-1)^{2}h
, where
h\in H^{2}
, and recall that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}}^{2}=\|(z-1)^{2}h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\approx\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

The assumption that (

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{3},3}((z-1)^{2}h)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

This is in turn equivalent to

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+D_{\mu_{3},3}((z-1)^{2}h)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

We now show that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(3)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\,dA(z)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+D_{\mu_{3},3}((z-1)^{2}h).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(3)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\,dA(z)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

However, letting

. Hence, the assumption that (
4.9
) is false leads to a contradiction.

It remains to prove the estimate (
4.10
). Note that for
f(z)=(z-1)^{2}h(z)

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{3},3}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(3)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|6h^{\prime}(z)+6(z-1)h^{(2)}(z)+(z-1)^{2}h^{(3)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z).

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(3)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim D_{\mu_{3},3}(f)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(2)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(2)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z).

Since

for

, Lemma
4.11

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})\,dA(z)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(z-1)h^{(2)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|z-1|^{2}}\,dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{(2)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\,dA(z)\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

It thus follows that (

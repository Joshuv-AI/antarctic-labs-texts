Abstract
. This work deals with a family of Hardy-Sobolev doubly critical system defined in ℝ n \displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n} . More precisely, we provide a classification of the positive solutions, whose expressions comprise multiplies of solutions of the decoupled scalar equation. Our strategy is based on the symmetry of the solutions, deduced via a suitable version of the moving planes technique for cooperative singular systems, joint with the study of the asymptotic behavior by using the Moser’s iteration scheme.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the study of the doubly critical system:

(
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}
)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u\,=\gamma\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}+u^{2^{*}-1}+\nu\alpha u^{\alpha-1}v^{\beta}&\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\\
\displaystyle-\Delta v\,=\gamma\frac{v}{|x|^{2}}+v^{2^{*}-1}+\nu\beta u^{\alpha}v^{\beta-1}&\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\\
u,v>0&\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle\gamma\in[0,\Lambda_{n})
with
\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}
the best constant in the Hardy’s inequality for
\displaystyle n\geq 3
,
\displaystyle 2^{*}=\dfrac{2n}{n-2}
is the critical exponent in the Sobolev embedding,
\displaystyle\nu>0
is the coupling parameter and
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta>1
are real parameters satisfying

\alpha+\beta=2^{*}.

Along this article we address the analysis of the classification of positive solutions to (

S
∗

In the case
\displaystyle\gamma=\nu=0
, system (

S
∗

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u\,=u^{2^{*}-1}&\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\\
u>0&\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.\end{cases}

It is well-known that the so-called
Aubin-Talenti bubbles

(1.2)

\mathcal{V}_{\lambda,x_{0}}(x):=\left[\frac{\lambda\sqrt{n(n-2)}}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{n-2}{2}}

solve (
1.1

\displaystyle\lambda>0
and it is centered at
\displaystyle x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover, they realize the equality in the sharp Sobolev inequality in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In
[
29

\displaystyle u

1.1

\displaystyle u(x)=O\left(1/|x|^{m}\right),
at infinity for
\displaystyle m>0

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In the proof the authors used a refinement of the celebrated moving plane procedure developed by themselves in a previous paper. Later on, Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck in
[

classified all the solutions to (

Finally, they showed that all the solutions

to (
1.1

1.2

\displaystyle p

When

\displaystyle\nu=0

Terracini in
[
46

For the reader’s convenience we describe the strategy of our proofs that turn out to be tricky somehow.

1.1.
The symmetry result

The proof of Theorem
1.5
, as recalled above, is based on the moving plane technique. Unfortunately the adaptation of the technique is not straightforward since we work in unbounded domains and the coupling term, in general, is not locally Lipschitz continuous.
We overcame such a difficulty studying a suitable translated problem. To obtain symmetry in the
\displaystyle x_{1}
-direction move the Hardy potential to

x_{0}=(0,x^{\prime}_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Then we apply the Kelvin transformation. The translation argument allow us to deal with the presence of the Hardy potential. The problem is not invariant under this procedure but the equation that arises is not so bad and we succeed in the adaptation of the moving plane procedure.

1.2.
The asymptotic analysis

Once we know that the solutions are radially symmetric, we use a suitable transformation

(u_{\tau}(x),v_{\tau}(x)):=\left(|x|^{\tau}u(x),|x|^{\tau}v(x)\right)\,,

for the right choice of
\displaystyle\tau>0
. The Moser iteration scheme, applyed to the transformed equation provides a first asymptotic information. Then the study of the associated ODE (together with the Kelvin transform) allows us to deduce the precise asymptotic information at zero and at infinity.

1.3.
The classification result

Once we know the exact behavior of the solutions, we exploit the standard change of variable

\begin{split}y_{u}(t):=r^{\delta}u(r)\quad&\text{and}\quad y_{v}(t):=r^{\delta}v(r),\\
\end{split}

where
\displaystyle t:=\log r
with
\displaystyle r>0
and
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{n-2}{2}
. We prove that

there exists
\displaystyle C>0
such that
\displaystyle y_{u}=Cy_{v}

where
\displaystyle C
is a zero of the function

f(s)=s^{2^{*}-2}+\nu\alpha s^{\alpha-2}-1-\nu\beta s^{\alpha}.

The number of roots of
\displaystyle f
is equivalent to the solutions of the system (
1.8
), then such quantity gives us the number of synchronized solutions. Although in this final issue we are reduced to deal with an ODE analysis, the proof is no longer standard. To the best of our knowledge, only the case of a single root of
\displaystyle f
has been treated in the literature. Precisely, the hardest part is the case when the function
\displaystyle f(\cdot)
has more than one zero, a possible issue, see Remark
1.4
. When the proportionality of components
\displaystyle(y_{u},y_{v})
is guaranteed, one can conclude the classification result by direct computation.

Lemma 1

Theorem 6
.

Let a(x) be a Beurling’s weight function. Then

a) The generalized grand Lebesgue space

{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}

{0<\varepsilon\leqslant p-1}

Proof.

a) It is easy to show that
L_{a}^{p),\theta}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

Now let

for

Then

\displaystyle\left\|f\ast g\right\|_{L_{a}^{p),\theta}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leqslant p-1}\varepsilon^{\theta_{1}}\left\|f\ast g\right\|_{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leqslant p-1}\varepsilon^{\theta}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t)g(x-t)dt\right\|_{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leqslant p-1}\varepsilon^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mid f(t)\mid\|g(x-t)\|_{{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p})}}}dt

(5.1)

If we make the substitution

\displaystyle\left\|f\ast g\right\|_{L_{a}^{p),\theta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leqslant p-1}\varepsilon^{\theta}\|g\|_{{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mid f(t)\mid a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p(p-\varepsilon}}dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p(p-\varepsilon}}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L_{a}^{p),\theta_{1}}}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L_{a}^{p),\theta_{1}}}.\

Thus we proved that

(5.5)

Remember some definitions and theorems that are necessary for us.

Definition 3
.

Let
(X,\|.\|_{X})
be Banach function space and let
f\in X.
We say that
f
has absolutely continuous norm in
X
if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{X}=0

for all
\{E_{n}\},n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
E_{n}\rightarrow\emptyset.
We denote by
X_{a}
the set of functions in
X
with absolutely continuous norm. If
X_{a}=X,
then X is said to have absolute continuous norm.

Theorem 7
.

( See
[
2
]
, Corollary
4.3
). The dual space
X^{\ast}
of a
Banach function space
X
is canonically isometric to the associate space
X^{{}^{\prime}}
if and only if
X
has absolutely continuous norm.

Theorem 8
.

( See
[
2
]
, Corollary
4.4
). A Banach function space is
reflexive if and only if both
X
and its associate space
X^{{}^{\prime}}

have absolute continuous norm.

Proposition 5
.

There exists weight function
a(x)
such that the corresponding space
L_{a}^{p),\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
do not have absolutely continuous norm.

Proof.

For the proof, it will be enough to find a generalized grand Lebesgue space
L_{a}^{p),\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a non-absolute continuous function in this space. Let
f(t)=e^{-\mid t\mid},
let
a(t)={(1+\mid t\mid)^{-2}}
a weight function and let
E_{n}=(\frac{n}{n+1},1)\subset(0,1)\subset\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
for
n\in N.
Thus
(E_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}
is a sequence of subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. It is clear that
E_{n}\rightarrow\emptyset.

Since

f(t)=e^{-\mid t\mid}\in L_{a}^{p),\theta}(\mathbb{R}),
then

e^{-\mid t\mid}\chi_{E_{n}}\in L_{a}^{p),\theta}(\mathbb{R}),
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Also since
(\frac{n}{n+1},1)\subset(0,1),
we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-\mid t\mid}\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}}

\displaystyle>{\varepsilon_{0}^{\theta}}(\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{p(p-\varepsilon_{0})}}(\frac{1}{e^{p}})^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon_{0}}}\left[\frac{e^{\varepsilon_{0}}-e^{\frac{n\varepsilon_{0}}{n+1}}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon_{0}}}

for any fixed

It is easy to see that if
n\rightarrow\infty,
then

\displaystyle{\varepsilon_{0}^{\theta}}(\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{p(p-\varepsilon_{0})}}(\frac{1}{e^{p}})^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon_{0}}}\left[\frac{e^{\varepsilon_{0}}-e^{\frac{n\varepsilon_{0}}{n+1}}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon_{0}}}\rightarrow 0.

That means

Hence

is not absolutely continuous in

and so

do not have absolutely continuous norm.
∎

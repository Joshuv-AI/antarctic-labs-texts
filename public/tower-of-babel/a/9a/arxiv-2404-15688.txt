Observer-Based Realization of Control Systems
Daizhan Cheng, Xiao Zhang, Zhengping Ji, Changxi Li

Abstract
A novel model reduction framework for large-scale complex systems is proposed by introducing function-type dynamic control systems via the dimension-keeping semi-tensor product (DK-STP) of matrices. Utilizing bridge matrices, the DK-STP facilitates the construction of an approximate observer-based realization (OR) of a linear control system in the form of a function-type control system, where the functions serve as observers. A necessary and sufficient condition is established for the OR-system to admit exact observer dynamics. When an exact OR-system does not exist, an extended OR-system is developed by incorporating the original system’s observers into its state. Furthermore, a minimal feedback extended OR-system is constructed, and its relationship to Kalman’s minimal realization is analyzed. Finally, the proposed approach is extended to nonlinear control-affine systems.

If a particular system property, represented by a function involving only a subset of the state variables, is of interest, then an OR-system corresponding to that function can be formulated. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We establish necessary and sufficient conditions under which an OR-system exactly replicates the dynamics of the original system. When this is not achievable, we introduce the extended OR-system, which includes the original observers as part of its state.

•

We construct the minimal feedback OR-system, which achieves an exact realization with the smallest possible dimension, and compare it with Kalman’s minimal realization.

•

We extend the entire OR-system framework, originally developed for linear systems, to affine nonlinear control systems under mild regularity assumptions.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II provides preliminaries including the topological structure of the mixed-dimensional Euclidean space, and the DK-STP of matrices. Section III first introduce the weighted DK-STP and then demonstrate how the matrix function of square matrices can be extended to the ring of non-square matrices under the DK-STP. The DK-STP based dynamic (control) systems are proposed and their solutions are obtained in Section IV. Section V uses the DK-STP with bridge matrices to construct approximate OR-systems. As an example, the natural OR-system structure of a singular system is revealed, while necessary and sufficient conditions for a linear system to possess an exact OR-system are presented. Section VI investigates the OR-system of linear control systems, and the technique for constructing state-feedback exact OR-systems and state-feedback extended OR-systems is presented in details. Section VII extends the results obtained for linear control systems to affine nonlinear systems. Section VIII is a brief conclusion. The appendix provides detailed description of the computation of the largest

-invariant subspace contained in the kernel of the observers.

II
Mathematical Preliminaries

II-A

Topology and Vector Space Structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}

We begin by introducing the notion of dimension-free spaces. Consider the union of Euclidean spaces of all dimensions:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}={\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

This subsection shows how to endow
\mathcal{V}
with the structure of a topological (pseudo-) vector space
[
9
,
10
]
.

Definition II.1

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
, and
t=\lcm(p,q)
be the least common multiple of
p
and
q
. Then the semi-tensor addition of
x
and
y
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle x\vec{+}y:=\left(x\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/p}\right)+\left(y\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/q}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{t}.

(11)

Together with the conventional scalar multiplication, this addition turns
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
into a pseudo vector space
[
1
]
.

To define a topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
, we first introduce an inner product, from which a norm and metric are derived.

Definition II.2

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
and
t=\lcm(p,q)
. Define the inner product as:

\displaystyle\left<x,y\right>_{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\frac{1}{t}\left<x\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/p},y\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/q}\right>.

Then the norm is given by
\|x\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\sqrt{\left<x,x\right>_{{\mathcal{V}}}}
, and the distance between
x
and
y
is
d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(x,y):=\|x-y\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}
.

The topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{T}}_{d}
, is generated by the metric
d_{{\mathcal{V}}}
.

Remark II.3

1.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
.
x,y
are said to be equivalent, denoted by
x\leftrightarrow y
, if there exist
{\bf 1}_{i}
and
{\bf 1}_{j}
such that
x\otimes{\bf 1}_{i}=y\otimes{\bf 1}_{j}
. Equivalently,
x\leftrightarrow y
if and only if
d_{{\mathcal{V}}}(x,y)=0
.

2.

{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
with the classical scalar multiplication and addition defined by (
11
) satisfies all vector space requirements except that the zero element is not unique. All
{\bf 0}_{n}=(0,\cdots,0)^{\mathrm{T}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are equivalent are zero. It follows that the inverse of
x
is

\{y\;|\;y\leftrightarrow-x\}
. Hence
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
is called a pseudo vector space.

3.

Denote by

\bar{x}:=\{y\;|\;y\leftrightarrow x\}
,
r\bar{x}:=\overline{rx}
, and
\bar{x}+\bar{y}:=\overline{x\vec{+}y}
,
then the quotient space
\Omega:={\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}/\leftrightarrow
is a vector space.

4.

Under the quotient topology,
\Omega
is a Hausdorff space. Furthermore, let
\langle\bar{x},\bar{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathcal{V}}}
.
Then
\Omega
is an inner product space, but not a Hilbert space.

Next, we consider the projection from
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Definition II.4

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. The projection of
\xi
onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, denoted by
\pi^{m}_{n}(\xi)
, is defined as

\displaystyle\pi^{m}_{n}(\xi):=\underset{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{n}}{\argmin}\|\xi-x\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}.

(12)

The projection can be expressed in matrix form as follows.

Proposition II.5
(
[
9
,
14
]
)

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
x_{0}=\pi^{m}_{n}(\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
t=\lcm(m,n)
. Then

\displaystyle x_{0}=\Pi^{m}_{n}\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

(13)

where

\displaystyle\Pi^{m}_{n}=\frac{n}{t}\left(I_{n}\otimes{\bf 1}^{\mathrm{T}}_{t/n}\right)\left(I_{m}\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/m}\right),

(14)

Moreover,
x_{0}\bot\left(\xi-x_{0}\right)
.

[

and

II-B

From the Classical STP to the DK-STP

As mentioned in the introduction, the classical STP, proposed more than two decades ago, has found wide applications. Unfortunately, it cannot be used for linear mappings over
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
, because the (classical) STP between a matrix and a vector is generally not a vector. To address this, matrix-vector and vector-vector STPs have been defined
[
9
]
. A natural question is whether it is possible to define an STP that is compatible with the conventional matrix-matrix and matrix-vector products. The newly proposed DK-STP answers this question affirmatively. The concepts and results in this subsection are mainly taken from
[
15
]
.

Definition II.6

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
,
t=\lcm(n,p)
. The DK-STP of
A
and
B
, denoted by
A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times q}
, is defined as follows.

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B:=\left(A\otimes{\bf 1}^{\mathrm{T}}_{t/n}\right)\left(B\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/p}\right).

(15)

Proposition II.7

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
,
t=\lcm(n,p)
. Then
A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B:=A\Psi_{n\times p}B
,
where
\Psi_{n\times p}:=\left(I_{n}\otimes{\bf 1}^{\mathrm{T}}_{t/n}\right)\left(I_{p}\otimes{\bf 1}_{t/p}\right)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\times p}
is called a bridge matrix of dimension
n\times p
.

Similar to the classical STP, the DK-STP satisfies the following fundamental properties:

Proposition II.8

(i)

When
n=p
,
A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B=AB
.

(ii)

(Associativity)
A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,(B\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,C)=(A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B)\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,C.

(iii)

(Distributivity) Assume
A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
and
C
is of arbitrary dimension. Then

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(A\pm B)\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,C=A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,C\pm B\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,C,\\
C\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,(A\pm B)=C\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A\pm C\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B.\\
\end{array}

The following proposition characterizes the basic properties of the DK-STP.

Proposition II.9

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
. Then
A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
.

\left({\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n},+,\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,\right)
is a non-commutative ring without identity.
Consider
{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
as a vector space over
{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\left({\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n},\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,\right)
is an algebra over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Define the Lie bracket as

[A,B]_{\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,}:=A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,B-B\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A,\quad A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n},

then
\left({\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n},\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,\right)
is a Lie algebra.

For concepts and fundamental properties of rings and (Lie) algebras, refer to standard textbooks such as
[
23
]
,
[
3
]

III-A

Weighted and
\Psi
-Based DK-STPs

Definition III.1

A set of vectors is called a weight, denoted by
{\bf\xi}=\{\xi_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;|\;n=1,2,\cdots\}
,
if (i)
\xi_{1}=1
; (ii)
\xi_{i}=(\xi_{i}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{i}^{i})^{\mathrm{T}}\neq{\bf 0}
,
\xi_{i}^{j}\geq 0
,
i\geq 2
,
j\in[1,i]
.

Definition III.2

Let
{\bf\xi}
and
{\bf\eta}
be two weights,
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
,
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
, and
t=\lcm(n,p)
. Then the weighted DK-STP with respect to
{\bf\xi}
and
{\bf\eta}
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,^{{\bf\xi}}_{{\bf\eta}}B:=\left(A\otimes\xi^{\mathrm{T}}_{t/n}\right)\left(B\otimes\eta_{t/p}\right).

(18)

Proposition III.3

Let
{\bf\xi}
and
{\bf\eta}
be two weights,
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
,
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
, and
t=\lcm(n,p)
. Then the weighted DK-STP with respect to
{\bf\xi}
and
{\bf\eta}
can be calculated by

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,^{{\bf\xi}}_{{\bf\eta}}B:=A\Psi^{{\bf\xi}}_{{\bf\eta}}B,

(19)

where the corresponding bridge matrix

\displaystyle\Psi^{{\bf\xi}}_{{\bf\eta}}=\left(I_{n}\otimes\xi^{\mathrm{T}}_{t/n}\right)\left(I_{p}\otimes\eta_{t/p}\right)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\times p}.

(20)

Remark III.4

It is straightforward to verify that Propositions
II.8
and
II.9
also hold for the weighted DK-STP. The design of the bridge matrix (including the choice of weight vectors) is a key issue in applications. Here, we present two approximate approaches for the design: cross-dimensional projection and the Moore-Penrose inverse. All arguments concerning exact OR-realization focus on the design of appropriate bridge matrices for particular systems.

Note that if we choose
{\bf\xi}=\{\xi_{n}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf 1}_{n}\;|\;n=1,2,\cdots\}
and
{\bf\eta}=\{\eta_{n}={\bf 1}_{n}\;|\;n=1,2,\cdots\}
, then

\displaystyle\left[\Psi_{p}\right]_{n\times m}:=\left(\Psi^{{\bf\xi}}_{{\bf\eta}}\right)_{n\times m}=\Pi^{m}_{n},

(21)

where
\Pi^{m}_{n}
is the projection matrix, defined in (
14
). This matrix is called the projecting bridge matrix.

Equation (
21
) reveals the relationship between cross-dimensional projection and the DK-STP from the perspective of observer-based realization. The observer-based realization searches for the best projection from observer space
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
to the state space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which is realized by the bridge matrix
\Psi
. By definition, the projection from
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
finds the point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that is closest to the original point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Regardless of the original geometric meaning of the DK-STP, we can formally define more DK-STPs by different choices of bridge matrices.

Definition III.5
(
[
15
]
)

The set
\{\Psi_{m,n}\!\in\!{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}|m,n>0\}
is called a set of bridge matrices, if

(i)

\rank(\Psi_{m,n})=\min(m,n),\quad m,n=1,2,\cdots.

(ii)

\Psi_{n,n}=I_{n},\quad n=1,2,\cdots.

Given a set of bridge matrices, the corresponding DK-STP, denoted by
\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,_{\Psi}
, can be defined as follows: Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
. Then the corresponding DK-STP is defined by

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,_{\Psi}B:=A\Psi_{n\times p}B.

(22)

Assumption 1
: For convenience, we assume hereafter that

\Psi=\Psi_{p}
and
\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,=\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,_{\Psi_{p}}

Definition IV.7

(i)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)\approx A\Psi_{+}y(t)+Bu(t).

(45)

When

Remark V.6

Equation (

\displaystyle W\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\quad x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
\rank(W)=r<n
. If we decompose it into differential and algebraic parts, then it becomes

\displaystyle\begin{cases}E\dot{x}(t)=\tilde{A}x(t)+\tilde{B}u(t),\\
Fx(t)+Du(t)=0.\end{cases}

Then the above approach is applicable with a mild change of replacing (

\displaystyle x(t)\approx\Theta^{+}\begin{bmatrix}y(t)\\
-Du(t)\end{bmatrix}:=\Psi_{+}y(t)+\tilde{D}u(t).

The arguments for system (
42

V-C

Exact OR-Systems

In subsection A, we construct the OR-systems by using bridge matrices
\Psi_{p}
and
\Psi_{+}
respectively. When the original system is of large scale, its OR-system, concerning some particular properties of the original system, may have much smaller size. In subsection B it was shown that the OR-system might be exact. A critical question is: does the dynamics of observers of the OR-system coincide with the output dynamics of the original system? This subsection will provide an analysis for this.

By the self-reflexivity of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, any row vector can be viewed as a linear mapping from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
H\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times n}
. The subspace

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}:=\Span(\Row(H))\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*},

is called a dual subspace.

Definition V.8

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\times n}
. A dual subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
is called
A
-invariant, if
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}A\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
.

The following fact is well known from linear algebra.

Lemma V.9

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}=\Span(\Row(H))
be a dual subspace, where
H\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times n}
.

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
A
-invariant, if and only if, there exists

\Xi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times p}
such that

\displaystyle HA=\Xi H.

(48)

By Lemma
V.9
, we have the following result.

Proposition V.10

Consider the system (
5
). There exists a bridge matrix such that its corresponding OR-system represents the exact dynamics of the observers of the system (
5
), if and only if,
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
A
-invariant.

Proof.
(Sufficiency) Suppose that (
48
) holds. Then the SO-system of (
5
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{y}(t)&=&HAx(t)+HBu(t)\\
&=&\Xi y(t)+HBu(t).\end{array}

Here (
V-C
) is an OR-system. That is, the observers of (
5
) are the trajectories of this OR-system.

(Necessity) Suppose that there exists a bridge matrix
\Psi
such that the DK-STP
\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,=\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,_{\Psi}
satisfies

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{y}(t)&=&HAx(t)+HBu(t)\\
&=&HA\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,y(t)+HBu(t)\\
&=&HA\Psi Hx(t)+HBu(t).\end{array}

Then we must have
HA\Psi H=HA
.
Setting
\Xi:=HA\Psi
implies (
48
).

\Box

Corollary V.11

Suppose
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
A
-invariant. Then by letting

\Xi=HAH^{+}
,

\Psi=H^{+}
,
the corresponding OR-system is exact. Particularly, when
H
is of full row rank, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\Xi=HAH^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1},\\
\Psi=H^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}.\end{array}

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we prove it by assuming
H
is of full row rank. In this case,

H^{+}=H^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}.

It is easy to verify that
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
A
-invariant if and only if
\Xi=HAH^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}
satisfies
(
48
). From the proof of Proposition
V.10
one sees that
\Psi
satisfies

\displaystyle HA\Psi=\Xi=HAH^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}.

(53)

An obvious solution of (
53
) is
\Psi=H^{\mathrm{T}}(HH^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}
.

\Box

Remark V.12

Assume
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
A
-invariant, then the only choice of
\Xi
is the one in (
V-C
), (or (
V.11
) when
H
is of full row rank).
But the solution for
\Psi
may not be unique. In fact, any matrix in the form of

\Psi=H^{+}+G
,
with
G\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\times p}
in the kernel of
HA
is also a solution, while the non-uniqueness of the solution does not affect the expression of the corresponding OR-system.

Consider a control system

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\\
y(t)=Cx(t),\end{array}

where

\end{smallmatrix}\right]

\end{smallmatrix}\right].

VI
Extended OR-Systems

This section addresses the problem of constructing an exact OR-system when the output dual subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is not
A
-invariant. The basic idea is to enlarge the output dual subspace so that it satisfies the invariance requirement.

VI-A

A
-invariant Extended OR-Systems

Definition VI.1

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
. The smallest dual subspace that is
A
-invariant and contains
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
, is called the
A
-invariant closure of
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
, denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}
.

Remark VI.2

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{i}_{*}
,
i=1,2
be
A
-invariant dual subspaces. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{1}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{2}
is also
A
-invariant. So
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}
exists, because

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}=\bigcap\{{\mathcal{S}}\;|\;{\mathcal{S}}\supset{\mathcal{H}}_{*}~\mbox{is $A$-invariant}\}.

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}
can be calculated by an iteration as follows:

(1)

{\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{0}:={\mathcal{H}}_{*}.

(2)

{\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k+1}={\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k}+{\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k}A,\quad k\geq 0.

(3)

When the sequence reaches
k^{*}
such that

{\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k^{*}+1}={\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k^{*}}
, we have

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}={\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{k^{*}}.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}
is

-invariant, we can use it to construct an OR-system.

Definition VI.3

Consider system (
5
). Replace its observers by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A)}
. The resulting OR-system is called its extended OR-system.

We present a numerical example to illustrate the construction of an extended OR-system.

Example VI.4

Consider a system
\Sigma

, we have

Remark VI.5

The main difference between Kalman realization and observer-based realization is that Kalman realization preserves the input-output relation of the original system, whereas observer-based realization preserves the observers (outputs) themselves.
As mentioned before, the observer (as functions of states) is to describe certain properties of the original systems concerned. Hence for our purpose, we need to keep the observers unchanged, which shows the advantage of the OR approach over Kalman realization.

VI-B

Feedback Extended OR-Systems

Definition VI.6

Consider system (
5
). A dual space
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
is called
(A,B)
-invariant if there exists a state feedback control
u(t)=Fx(t)
such that
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is invariant under
A+BF
.

The following results are immediate consequences of the definition.

Proposition VI.7

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is an
(A,B)
-invariant dual subspace, if and only if, there exist
F
and
\Xi
, such that

H(A+BF)=\Xi H
.

Proposition VI.8

Consider the system (
5
). Using bridge matrix
\Psi
and feedback control

u(t)=Fx(t)+v(t)
,
we have the approximated OR-system

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{y}(t)&=&H(A+BF)x(t)+v(t)\\
&\approx&H(A+BF)\Psi y(t)+v(t).\end{array}

If
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is an
(A,B)
-invariant dual subspace, then the approximated OR-system becomes an exact system
\dot{y}(t)=\Xi y(t)+v(t)
.

Next, we consider the problem of how to verify if a dual subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
(A,B)
-invariant.

Proposition VI.9

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
is
(A,B)
-invariant dual subspace, if and only if,

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;|\;h(x)=0,\forall h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\right\}

is
(A,B)
-invariant.

Proof.
Assume
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
(A,B)
-invariant, then

\begin{array}[]{l}~~{\mathcal{H}}_{*}(A+BF){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\\
=\Span(\Row(H))(A+BF){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\\
=\Span(\Row(\Xi H)){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\subset\Span(\Row(H)){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}=0,\end{array}

that is,

(A+BF){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp},

which shows
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}
is
(A,B)
-invariant.

Conversely, assume
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}
is
(A,B)
-invariant,
then
(A+BF){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}
. Hence, we also have

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}(A+BF){\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}=0,

that is,

{\mathcal{H}}_{*}(A+BF)\subset\left\{{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}\right\}^{\perp}={\mathcal{H}}_{*}

which implies that
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is
(A,B)
-invariant.

\Box

Remark VI.10

It is well known that a subspace
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an
(A,B)
-invariant subspace if and only if
A{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{V}}+{\mathcal{B}}
,
where
{\mathcal{B}}=\Span(\Col(B))
.
Hence, it serves as a straightforward verification for
(A,B)
-invariant subspace. Then Proposition
VI.9
provides a method to check whether a dual subspace is
(A,B)
-invariant.

Next, consider the general case, where
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is not
(A,B)
-invariant. To get an exact OR-system, we try to find a smallest
\Sigma_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}\subset\Sigma_{*}
and
\Sigma_{*}
is
(A,B)
-invariant.

Definition VI.11

The smallest
(A,B)
-invariant subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{*}
containing
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is called the
(A,B)
-invariant closure of
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
, denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A,B)}
.

We will show the existence and uniqueness of
(A,B)
-invariant closure of
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
by constructing it explicitly.

Consider system (
5
), there has been a standard algorithm to calculate the largest
(A,B)
-invariant subspace contained in
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{\perp}

[
37
]
, by which we have the following result.

Proposition VI.12

Consider system (
5
). Let

be the largest
(A,B)
-invariant subspace contained in

. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{*}^{(A,B)}=W^{\perp}.

Proof.
First, by

, we have
W^{\perp}\supset{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
.
Since

is an
(A,B)
-invariant subspace,

is an
(A,B)
-invariant dual subspace.

Second, if

is the largest

-invariant subspace contained in

\Box

Definition VI.13

Consider system (

The feedback OR-system of (

Proof.
Since

is
(A,B)
-invariant, the feedback OR-system is exact. Since the original observers are part of the state variables of the feedback OR-system, their dynamic process is completely recovered by the feedback OR-system.

\Box

Since the largest
(A,B)
-invariant subspace contained in the kernel of

(i.e.,

Remark VI.17

(i)

One can see that (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{z}(t)&=&\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&0\\

Although this realization keeps the input-output relation of the original system unchanged, it does not keep the output functions unchanged. So this minimal realization can not be adopted for realizing the dynamics of certain chosen functions as the exact SO-systems do.

(ii)

Comparing Example
VI.16
with

VI.4

(A,B)
-invariant subspace to construct its perpendicular dual subspace, which is the largest feedback invariant dual subspace. The

-invariant dual subspace can be considered as a special
(A,B)
-invariant dual subspace, which uses feedback control

with

. Hence, it is in general smaller than the largest one.

(iii)

The feedback OR-system may simultaneously solve other control problems. For example, the disturbance decoupling problem (DDP). Consider a linear control system with disturbances

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t)+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{k=1}^{s}\xi(t),\quad y(t)=Cx(t).

where
\xi_{k}(t)
,
k\in[1,s]
are disturbances. The DDP is solvable, if and only if,
\xi_{k}\in{\mathcal{V}}
, where
{\mathcal{V}}
is the largest
(A,B)
-invariant subspace contained in kernel of
Cx

[
37
]
.

Now if the DDP is solvable, then we claim that all the disturbances will be eliminated from the feedback extended OR-system. This is because

C\xi_{k}=0
,
and then

C(A+BF)\xi_{k}\subset C(A+BF){\mathcal{V}}\subset C{\mathcal{V}}=0.

Continuing this process, one sees that

\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{*}^{(A,B)}\subset{\mathcal{V}}^{\perp}
.
Hence all the disturbances are within
{\mathcal{V}}_{*}^{\perp}
. The claim follows.
Similarly, if the feedback solves the stabilization problem, i.e.,
A+BF
is a Hurwitz matrix, then the feedback extended exact OR-system is also asymptotically stable. In fact, the feedback extended OR-system is the smallest subsystem, involving observers.

VIII
Conclusion

This paper presents a technique for constructing the OR-systems. First, by employing cross-dimensional projection and DK-STP, two types of approximate OR-systems are proposed. Subsequently, we address the conditions under which an OR-system is exact, meaning that it exhibits precisely the same dynamics as the observers of the original system. The methodology for constructing an exact OR-system is also provided.

When it is not possible to construct an exact OR-system, we propose algorithms for constructing an extended OR-system, in which the observers of the original system are included as part of the state variables. Furthermore, the (minimal) state feedback (extended) OR-system is developed. Finally, all the aforementioned results for linear control systems are extended to affine nonlinear control systems. The approaches for extended and feedback OR-systems in continuous-time control systems are also applicable to discrete-time control systems.

This work aims to propose a method for model reduction of complex systems. When dealing with large-scale complex systems, the entire state space may be too complicated to analyze or even to model. In such cases, we can focus on certain observed phenomena, represented by the OR-system of the original system. By studying various OR-systems derived from the original system, we are able to gain insight into, and potentially manipulate, the underlying complex system.

Essentially, OR-realization can be regarded as a novel model reduction technique. There remain many related problems for further investigation, some of which are listed below:

(i)

For approximate OR-realization, how can the approximation error be estimated? Furthermore, how can the error be reduced to meet application requirements?

(ii)

What constitutes the optimal OR-approximation for a given system? In particular, the minimal dimension of the best approximation remains an open question.

(iii)

The OR-systems technique has significant potential in model reduction in large-scale complex systems. In particular, its application to dimension-varying systems
[
12
]
brings notable challenges.

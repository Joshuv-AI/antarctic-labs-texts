Cucker-Smale model with finite speed of information propagation: well-posedness, flocking and mean-field limit
Jan Haskovec

Abstract
We study a variant of the Cucker-Smale model where information between agents propagates with a finite speed 𝔠 > 0 {\mathfrak{c}}>0 . This leads to a system of functional differential equations with state-dependent delay. We prove that, if initially the agents travel slower than 𝔠 {\mathfrak{c}} , then the discrete model admits unique global solutions. Moreover, under a generic assumption on the influence function, we show that there exists a critical information propagation speed 𝔠 ∗ > 0 {{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}>0 such that if 𝔠 ≥ 𝔠 ∗ {\mathfrak{c}}\geq{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}} , the system exhibits asymptotic flocking in the sense of the classical definition of Cucker and Smale. For constant initial datum the value of 𝔠 ∗ {{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}} is explicitly calculable. Finally, we derive a mean-field limit of the discrete system, which is formulated in terms of probability measures on the space of time-dependent trajectories. We show global well-posedness of the mean-field problem and argue that it does not admit a description in terms of the classical Fokker-Planck equation.



Keywords
: Cucker-Smale model, state-dependent delay, finite speed of information propagation, flocking, mean-field limit.

1
Introduction

The Cucker-Smale system is a prototypical individual-based model of collective behavior.
It was introduced in the seminal papers
[
7
,
8
]
, originally as a model for language evolution.
Later the interpretation as a model for flocking in animals (birds) prevailed.
The model describes a group of
N\in\mathbb{N}
autonomous agents
located in the physical space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.
The agents are described by their phase-space
coordinates
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
i=1,2,\dots,N
,
where
x_{i}(t)
denotes the position and
v_{i}(t)
the velocity of the
i
-th agent.
The agents are subject to the following collective dynamics,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i}(t),

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{i}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi(|x_{j}(t)-x_{i}(t)|)(v_{j}(t)-v_{i}(t)),

(2)

for
t>0
and
i\in[N]
, where here and in the sequel we denote
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
.
The nonnegative real function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
called
influence function
, measures the intensity of the influence between
agents depending on their distance.
Typically, it is assumed to be globally bounded, so that, by an eventual rescaling of time,
one has
0\leq\psi\leq 1
. We shall adapt this assumption in our paper.

The terms
x_{j}(t)
and
v_{j}(t)
appearing in the right-hand side of equation (
2
)
reflect the implicit modelling assumption that each agent receives information
about the current phase-space co-ordinates of all other agents immediately, without any time lags
(or, that the eventual time lags are negligibly small with respect to the typical time scales
relevant for the system).
However, for certain applications in biology
[
1
,
24
,
28
]
, socio-economics
[
18
,
20
]

or engineering (e.g., swarm robotics,
[
11
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
), delays in communication between agents
caused by finite speed of information propagation may be relevant.
For instance, in radio communication between satellites on the orbit or in outer space,
where the distances are not negligible with respect to the speed of light,
or in swarm robotics with acoustic communication between agents (i.e., underwater robots).
This motivates us to introduce a modification of the Cucker-Smale model (
1
)–(
2
)
where information propagates with a constant finite speed
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
, called
propagation speed
in the sequel.
Then, agent
i
located at
x_{i}=x_{i}(t)
at time
t>0
observes the
phase-space co-ordinates of agent
j
at time
t-\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)
, where
\tau_{{i}{j}}=\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)
solves

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)=|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{{i}{j}}(t))|.

(3)

In other words,
\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)
is the time that information (light, sound) needs to travel from location
x_{j}(t-\tau_{{i}{j}}(t))

to location
x_{i}(t)
.

In general it is neither guaranteed that a solution
\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)
of (
3
)
exists nor that it is unique. This issue is of course related
to the possibility of agents traveling faster than the propagation speed
{\mathfrak{c}}
.
We shall formulate sufficient conditions for the well-posedness
of the model in the course of our analysis.
For the time being, let us assume that (
3
) is uniquely solvable with solution
\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)\geq 0

for all
t\geq 0
and
i,j\in[N]
, and introduce the following notation

\displaystyle{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}:=x_{j}(t-\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)),\qquad{\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}:=v_{j}(t-\tau_{{i}{j}}(t)).

We also introduce the formal notation
\tau_{{i}{i}}:=0
and
{\widetilde{x}_{i}}^{\;i}:=x_{i}(t)
,
{\widetilde{v}_{i}}^{\;i}:=v_{i}(t)
,
and, if no danger of confusion, we shall usually drop the explicit time dependence,
writing just
x_{i}
for
x_{i}(t)
etc.
With this notation, the system that we study in this paper is written as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i},

(4)

\displaystyle\dot{v}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi(|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}|)\left({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{i}\right),

(5)

for
t>0
and
i\in[N]
.
We shall also frequently use the shorthand notation for the
communication rates

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}:=\psi\left(\left|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}\right|\right).

The system (
4
)–(
5
) is equipped with the initial datum

\displaystyle x_{i}(t)=x_{i}^{0}(t),\quad v_{i}(t)=v_{i}^{0}(t)\qquad\mbox{for }i\in[N],\quad t\leq 0,

(6)

where
x_{i}^{0}=x_{i}^{0}(t)
,
v_{i}^{0}=v_{i}^{0}(t)
are continuous paths on
(-\infty,0]
.
We shall impose the physically relevant assumption that

\displaystyle x^{0}_{i}(t)=x^{0}_{i}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}v^{0}_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s\qquad\mbox{for }i\in[N],\quad t\leq 0,

(7)

so that we in fact only prescribe
v_{i}^{0}=v_{i}^{0}(t)
for
t\leq 0
and
x_{i}^{0}(0)
.
We note that the results provided in this paper would not lose their validity
even if we dropped the ”compatibility” assumption (
7
),
however, it would lead to unnecessary technicalities in the proofs.
Moreover, in order to construct the solution of (
4
)–(
5
)
on a bounded time interval
[0,T]
, the values of the initial datum are only relevant
on a bounded time interval
[-S(T),0]
, with some
S(T)>0
depending on
{\mathfrak{c}}
,
{\mathfrak{s}}

and the configuration of the system at time
t=0
.
We shall make this dependence explicit later.

From the mathematical point of view, the system (
3
)–(
5
)
is a system of functional differential equations with state-dependent delay,
which stems from the fact that the delay
\tau_{ij}(t)
in (
5
)
depends on the configuration of the system
in a nontrivial (even implicit) way through (
3
).
This poses new analytical challenges: in particular,
the standard well-posedness theory for ODE systems
(the classical theorems of Peano and Picard-Lindelöf)
does not apply to (
3
)–(
5
).
Therefore, the first goal of this paper, addressed in Section
3
,
is to establish global existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
3
)–(
5
).

The second goal of this paper is to study the asymptotic
behavior of solutions of the system (
3
)–(
5
).
In particular, the usual question asked in the context
of the Cucker-Smale model is under which conditions
its solutions exhibit the
(asymptotic) flocking behavior
,
defined as follows.

Definition 1

(Asymptotic flocking)
.

We say that the system with particle positions
x_{i}(t)
and velocities
v_{i}(t)
,

i\in[N]
and
t\geq 0
, exhibits
(asymptotic) flocking
if

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)<\infty,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)=0,

(8)

where we denoted the spatial and, resp., velocity diameters

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|\quad\mbox{and}\quad{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t)|.

(9)

We shall derive sufficient conditions for flocking in the system (
3
)–(
5
)
in terms of the propagation speed
{\mathfrak{c}}
, the decay properties of the influence function
\psi

and certain properties of the initial datum (
6
). The presence of the state-dependent delay
poses severe challenges for the analysis. In particular, the Lyapunov functional-type approaches,
that were developed for Cucker-Smale-type systems with a-priori given (i.e., state-independent)
delay, fail. Consequently, new methods need to be developed to study the asymptotic flocking
for the system (
3
)–(
5
). This goal will be addressed in Section
4
.

Finally, the third goal of this paper, addressed in Section
5
,
is to study the mean-field limit of (
3
)–(
5
)
as
N\to\infty
. As is well known, mean-field limits of systems of interacting particles
are typically described in terms of a Fokker-Planck equation that governs the evolution
of a time-dependent particle density in the phase space.
However, for certain types of systems involving delay, such a description may not be available.
For a Cucker-Smale system with constant delay, this issue was discussed in
[
14
, Section 4]
.
It is not a-priori clear whether the system (
3
)–(
5
) with state-dependent delay
admits a Fokker-Planck-type description in the mean-field limit.
We approach the problem by first deriving a mean-field limit formulated
in terms of probability measures on the space of Lipschitz continuous curves.
In Section
5
we prove the well-posedness of such a description
and, using a stability result, show that it is indeed obtained from the discrete system
(
3
)–(
5
) in the limit as
N\to\infty
.
Many of the analytical techniques applied in Section
5
are adaptations
of the methods developed in
[
2
]
to the setting
of probability measures on the space of Lipschitz continuous curves.
Finally, we argue that the mean-field limit in fact
cannot
be formulated
as a (standard) Fokker-Planck equation.

Cucker-Smale-type systems with delay and their flocking behavior have been studied in a series of recent papers.
However, to our best knowledge, all the previous works

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
]

assume the delay to be state-independent, i.e., given a-priori either as a constant,
time-dependent function or probability distribution.
State-dependent delay induced by finite speed of information propagation
was considered in
[
13
]
for a Hegselmann-Krause-type model
of consensus formation
[
16
]
. This model can be seen as a first-order version
of (
3
)–(
5
). However, the mathematical challenges
stemming from the second-order model are significantly different.
Consequently, analysis of the system (
3
)–(
5
)
requires development of new techniques, which is the main focus of this paper.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2

we present an overview of our main results on well-posedness,
flocking behavior and mean-field limit of the system (
3
)–(
5
).
In Section
3
we provide the proof of well-posedness
(global existence and uniqueness) of solutions of the discrete system.
In Section
4
we provide the proof of our result
on asymptotic flocking behavior. Finally, in Section
5
we study the
mean-field limit
N\to\infty
.

2.1
Existence and uniqueness of solutions

We start with the observation that, in general, (
3
) can only be uniquely solvable if the agents
move with speeds
|v_{i}|
strictly less than the propagation speed
{\mathfrak{c}}
.
This motivates us, for
0<{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
and
T\geq 0
, to introduce the space

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in C((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{Nd});\,|v_{i}(t)|\leq{\mathfrak{s}}\mbox{ for all }t\leq T,\,i\in[N]\right\},

(10)

where we use the notation
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{Nd}
.
Then, it is natural to require that the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}=(v^{0}_{1},\ldots,v^{0}_{N})
in (
6
) is an element of
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}

Our first result provides existence and uniqueness of global solutions of the system (

Theorem 1

(Existence and uniqueness of global solutions)
.

Let the influence function
0\leq\psi\leq 1
be uniformly Lipschitz continuous on
[0,\infty)
.
Let
0<{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
be fixed and let the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}=(v^{0}_{1},\ldots,v^{0}_{N})\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
.
Then, for any

the system (
3
)–(

) admits a unique global solution in
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}

Moreover, the initial datum

only needs to be prescribed on the compact interval

, with

\displaystyle S(T):=\frac{{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+[{\mathfrak{c}}-3{\mathfrak{s}}]^{-}T}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}},

(11)

with
[a]^{-}:=\max\{-a,0\}
denotes the negative part of

.

2.2
Flocking behavior

Our second result provides sufficient conditions for asymptotic flocking behavior of the system (
3
)–(
7
) in sense of Definition
1
.
For this, we adopt the additional assumption that the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}
is constant,
while
\mathbf{x}^{0}
verifies (
7
). This assumption may be seen restrictive, although we do not consider
it unrealistic. In fact, a significant part of the proof of the flocking result in Section
4

applies to the general setting
\mathbf{v}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
and does not require
\mathbf{v}^{0}
to be constant.
Only in the last step (Lemma
8
), a solution of a nonlinear system of algebraic equations
has to be found, which only seems to be achievable analytically for constant
\mathbf{v}^{0}
.
For the general case, one would need to resort to numerical approaches to confirm solvability of the algebraic system
and so derive a sufficient condition for flocking in terms of the parameter values; see Remark
1
for details.

Theorem 2

(Critical propagation speed for flocking)
.

Let the influence function
0\leq\psi\leq 1
be uniformly Lipschitz continuous on
[0,\infty)
.
Let
{\mathfrak{s}}>0
be fixed and let the initial datum

\mathbf{v}^{0}=(v^{0}_{1},\ldots,v^{0}_{N})
be constant on
(-\infty,0]
with
\max_{i\in[N]}|v_{i}^{0}|\leq{\mathfrak{s}}
.
Assume that there exists
\eta>0
such that

\displaystyle\psi\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{\eta}\right)>\eta.

(12)

Then there exists
{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
, calculable from the values of
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)
,
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
,
{\mathfrak{s}}
and
\eta
, such that if
{\mathfrak{c}}\geq{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}
, the system (
3
)–(
7
)
exhibits flocking in the sense of Definition
1
.

Let us note that the assumption (
12
) is very natural
in the context of the Cucker-Smale model,
where slow enough decay of the influence function
is necessary (and sufficient) condition for (unconditional) flocking.
In particular, we have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Let the influence function
\psi=\psi(s)
be such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}\frac{\psi(s)}{s^{\alpha}}>0

(13)

for some
\alpha>-1
.
Then for any
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)
,
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)\geq 0
there exists
\eta>0
such that (
12
) holds.

Condition (
13
) is almost equivalent to the unconditional flocking
condition
\int^{\infty}\psi(s)\mathrm{d}s=\infty
for the classical Cucker-Smale model,
which is known to be sharp
[
7
,
8
,
10
]
.
Indeed, the nonintegrability of
\psi
at infinity is implied by (
13
)
with any
\alpha\geq-1
, so that Proposition
1
only excludes
influence functions that decay like
s^{-1}
.
In particular, for the generic choice of
\psi
introduced in
[
7
,
8
]

and considered in most of the subsequent papers,

\displaystyle\psi(s)=\frac{1}{(1+s^{2})^{\beta}},

(14)

assumption (
13
) of Proposition
1
, and thus assumption (
12
)
of Theorem
2
, is equivalent to
\beta<1/2
.
This is precisely the condition for unconditional flocking formulated in
[
7
,
8
]

for the classical Cucker-Smale model.

2.3
Mean-field limit

For fixed
{\mathfrak{s}}>0
and
T\geq 0
we introduce the set

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}:=\Bigl\{\gamma\in C_{b}^{1}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d});\;\dot{\gamma}|_{[0,T]}\in C_{2{\mathfrak{s}}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\Bigr\},

(15)

where we recall that
C_{b}^{1}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of continous bounded functions on
(-\infty,T]

with continuous first-order derivative, and
C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of (globally) Lipschitz continuous functions on
(-\infty,T]

with Lipschitz constant
{\mathfrak{s}}
.
We equip the set
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
with the topology induced by the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}:=\left\|\gamma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-\infty,T)}+\left\|\dot{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\qquad\mbox{for }\gamma\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}.

(16)

We note that with the particular choice
T:=0
in (
15
) we obtain the space
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}=C_{b}^{1}((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and (
16
) reduces to
\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}}=\left\|\gamma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-\infty,0)}
.

We denote
{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
the space of probability measures on
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
with finite first-order moment, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)<+\infty\qquad\mbox{for any }\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}).

In Section
5
we will show that the mean-field limit as
N\to\infty
of the discrete system (
3
)–(
7
) is represented
by the measure
\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
,

\displaystyle\rho=\mbox{law}(x),

(17)

with

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=v(t),\qquad\dot{v}(t)=F_{t}[\rho](x(t),v(t))

(18)

for
t\in(0,T)
,
with the operator
F_{t}[\rho]:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle F_{t}[\rho](x,v):=\int_{\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\psi\left(\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-x\right|\right)\left(\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma).

(19)

The mapping
\Gamma_{t,x}:\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]:=\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])

(20)

with
\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]:=\tau
the unique solution of

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau=|x-\gamma(t-\tau)|.

(21)

As we shall prove below, the existence and uniqueness of
\tau
is guaranteed by the
{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma\in\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
The mapping
\Pi_{t,x}:\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]:=\dot{\gamma}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]).

(22)

The system (
17
)–(
19
) is equipped with the initial datum
\rho^{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0})
,
which is imposed in terms of the push-forward identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\#\rho=\rho^{0},

(23)

with the mapping
\mathbb{I}:\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}:\gamma\mapsto\gamma|_{(-\infty,0]},

(24)

and we recall that the push-forward measure
\mathbb{I}\#\rho\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0})
is defined by
\mathbb{I}\#\rho(B):=\rho(\mathbb{I}^{-1}(B))
,
where
B
is any measurable subset of
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
.
Then, the initial conditions
x(0)
and
v(0)
for (
18
) are distributed according to
X_{0}\#\rho^{0}

and, resp.,
V_{0}\#\rho^{0}
, with the mappings

\displaystyle X_{t}[\gamma]:=\gamma(t),\qquad V_{t}[\gamma]:=\dot{\gamma}(t).

(25)

In Section
5
we shall provide a proof of the following result
on existence and uniqueness of solutions of the mean-field system
(
17
)–(
23
).

Theorem 3

(Existence and uniqueness of measure solutions)
.

Fix
T>0
and
{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Then, for any
\rho^{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0})
there exists a unique probability measure

\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
that verifies (
17
)–(
23
).

Moreover, we shall provide a stability result, controlling a suitable notion of distance of solutions
by the distance of the initial data. For this purpose, we equip the space
{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
with the Monge–Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\Lambda(\rho,\nu)}\iint_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}\times\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma,\xi),

(26)

with
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}
given by (
16
) and
\Lambda(\rho,\nu)
denoting the set of transference plans between the measures
\rho
and
\nu
,
i.e., probability measures on the product space
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}\times\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
with first and second marginals

\nu
, respectively.
We then have the following stability result.

Theorem 4

(Stability)
.

For each
T>0
there exists a constant
M_{T}>0
such that
for any
\rho^{0}
,
\nu^{0}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}})
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu)\leq M_{T}\mathscr{W}_{0}(\rho^{0},\nu^{0}),

(27)

where
\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
, and, resp.,
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
are the unique solutions of (
17
)–(
23
)
constructed in Theorem
3
subject to the initial data
\rho^{0}
and, resp.
\nu^{0}
.

The above stability theorem justifies the approximation of the system (
17
)–(
23
)
by finite sets of discrete particles satisfying (
3
)–(
7
).
Indeed, let us consider
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
a solution of (
3
)–(
7
)
with a given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and define
\rho_{N}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
the atomic measure being concentrated on the set of trajectories

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho_{N}(\gamma):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta(\gamma-x_{j}),

(28)

with
\delta
denoting the Dirac measure on
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
, concentrated on the constant zero function
\gamma\equiv 0
.
Then, from (
19
) we have

\displaystyle F_{t}[\rho_{N}](x,v)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi\left(\left|x_{j}(t-\tau_{t,x}[x_{j}])-x\right|\right)\left(\dot{x}_{j}(t-\tau_{t,x}[x_{j}])-v\right),

where
\tau_{t,x}[x_{j}]
is the unique solution of (
21
) with
\gamma:=x_{j}
.
Consequently, (
3
)–(
5
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)=v_{i}(t),\qquad\dot{v}_{i}(t)=\frac{N}{N-1}F_{t}[\rho_{N}](x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t)).

Note that
\rho_{N}=\mbox{law}(\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[N]})
. Consequently,
up to the factor
\frac{N}{N-1}
that can be removed by rescaling of time,

\rho_{N}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
given by (
28
)
is a solution of (
17
)–(
23
) subject to the initial datum

\displaystyle\rho^{0}_{N}(\gamma):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta(\gamma-x^{0}_{j}).

(29)

Therefore, the important consequence of Theorem
5
is that it provides
a method to derive the measure-valued mean-field description (
17
)–(
23
)
as the limit
N\to\infty
of the particle approximations (
3
)–(
7
).

Corollary 1

(Convergence of the particle method)
.

Given
\rho^{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0})
, take a sequence
\rho^{0}_{N}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0})

of atomic measures of the form (
29
) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathscr{W}_{0}(\rho^{0}_{N},\rho^{0})=0.

Let
\rho^{N}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
be given by (
28
) with
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})

a solution of (
3
)–(
7
).
Then, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho_{N},\rho)=0,

where
\rho^{N}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
is the unique solution of (
17
)–(
23
)
subject to the initial datum
\rho^{0}
.

Finally, in Section
5.4
we discuss the question
whether one can express the mean-field limit problem (
17
)–(
23
)
in terms of a Fokker-Planck equation for some time dependent phase-space particle density
g_{t}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
t\geq 0
.
A short consideration leads to the conclusion that no such description is possible,
i.e., one indeed has to resort to the formulation in terms of probability measures
on the space of time-dependent trajectories.

3
Global existence and uniqueness of solutions - proof of Theorem
1

We start by proving two auxiliary results establishing unique solvability of the equation (
3
)
and a bound on the delay
\tau_{ij}
.

Lemma 1
.

For some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
x\in C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,t];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the Lipschitz constant
{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Then, for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the equation

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau=|z-x(t-\tau)|

(30)

is uniquely solvable in
\tau\geq 0
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,t];\mathbb{R}^{Nd})
with all trajectories
x_{i}
uniformly Lipschitz continuous on
(-\infty,t]

with Lipschitz constant
{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Then for all
i,j\in[N]
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}(t)\leq\frac{{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}},

(31)

where
\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}(t)
is the unique solution of (
3
) and
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}={d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)
defined in (
9
).

Proof.

By (
3
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{ij}=|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}|.

On the other hand, due to the
{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuity of

,

\displaystyle|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{j}|=|x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij})-x_{j}(t)|\leq{\mathfrak{s}}\tau_{ij}.

(32)

Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{ij}=|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}|\leq|x_{i}-x_{j}|+|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{j}|\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)+{\mathfrak{s}}\tau_{ij},

and (
31
) follows.
∎

Next, we prove local in time existence and uniqueness of solutions of the system (
3
)–(
7
).
The proof is an adaptation of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem, based on construction of a contraction mapping.

Lemma 3
.

Let the influence function
0\leq\psi\leq 1
be uniformly Lipschitz continuous on
[0,\infty)
.
Let
0<{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
be fixed and let the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}=(v^{0}_{1},\ldots,v^{0}_{N})\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
,
Then the system (
3
)–(
7
) admits unique local solutions
in the class of Lipschitz continuous velocity trajectories.

Proof.

Let us fix any
{\mathfrak{m}}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{m}}<{\mathfrak{c}}

and for some
T>0
to be specified later, define the set
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}

of continuous velocity trajectories on
(-\infty,T]
,
which coincide with the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}
on the interval
(-\infty,0]
,
and are uniformly bounded by
{\mathfrak{m}}

and
2{\mathfrak{m}}
-Lipschitz continuous on the interval
[0,T]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbf{v}\in C((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{Nd});\,\mathbf{v}|_{(-\infty,0]}\equiv\mathbf{v}^{0},\;\mathbf{v}|_{[0,T]}\in C_{2{\mathfrak{m}}}([0,T];R^{Nd}),\Bigr.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Bigl.|v_{i}(t)|\leq{\mathfrak{m}}\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T],\,i\in[N]\Bigr\},

where we again use the notation
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{Nd}

and
C_{2{\mathfrak{m}}}([0,T];R^{Nd})
denotes the set of
2{\mathfrak{m}}
-Lipschitz continuous
trajectories on
[0,T]
. Let us note that since the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}
verifies

|v^{0}_{i}(t)|\leq{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{m}}
for all
t\leq 0
and
i\in[N]
, the set
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
is nonempty.

We equip the set
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
with the topology of uniform convergence,
i.e., with the
L^{\infty}
-norm on
(0,T)
.
Then
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
is a complete metric space.

We construct a unique local solution of (
3
)–(
7
) by finding a unique fixed point
of the Picard operator
\Upsilon:\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}\to\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
,
where the
i
-th component is the
d
-dimensional vector-valued function
\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t)
,
given for
t\in[0,T]
by

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t):=v_{i}^{0}(0)+\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\neq i}\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)(\omega_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s))\mathrm{d}s

(33)

with

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(t)=x_{i}^{0}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s\qquad\mbox{for }i\in[N],\quad t\leq T,

(34)

and we set
\Upsilon[\omega]
equal to
\mathbf{v}^{0}
on
(-\infty,0]
.
Clearly, with
\omega\in\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
, the trajectories
\xi_{i}
defined by (
34
) are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
{\mathfrak{m}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Then, Lemma
1
gives unique solutions
\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}(t)
of the equation

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{ij}(t)=\left|\xi_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))-\xi_{i}(t)\right|,

(35)

for all
i,j\in[N]
and
t\leq T
.

We show that for sufficiently small
T>0
the Picard operator
\Upsilon
defined by (
33
) maps the space
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
into itself and is a contraction on
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}

with respect to the
L^{\infty}(0,T)
-norm.

With the global bound
\psi\leq 1
we have for any
\omega\in\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
,

\displaystyle\left|\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|v_{i}^{0}(0)\right|+\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\neq i}\left|\omega_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}(s))\right|+\left|\omega_{i}(s))\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{s}}+2T{\mathfrak{m}},

so that choosing
T\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}{2{\mathfrak{m}}}
gives

\displaystyle\left|\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t)\right|\leq{\mathfrak{m}}

for all
i\in[N]
and
t\in[0,T]
.
Moreover, for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
we have

\displaystyle\left|\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t_{1})-\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)|\omega_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2{\mathfrak{m}}|t_{1}-t_{2}|,

so that
\Upsilon[\omega]
is
2{\mathfrak{m}}
-Lipschitz continuous.
Consequently, for small enough
T>0
,
\Upsilon
maps the space
\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}
into itself.

To prove contractivity of
\Upsilon
, let us pick
\omega,\kappa\in\mathbb{W}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{T}

and calculate, for any fixed
i\in[N]
,

\displaystyle\left|\Upsilon_{i}[\omega](t)-\Upsilon_{i}[\kappa](t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl|\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)(\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s))\bigr.

(36)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\bigl.-\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)(\kappa_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\kappa_{i}(s))\bigr|\mathrm{d}s,

where

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(t)=x_{i}^{0}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\eta_{i}(t)=x_{i}^{0}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

and
\tau_{ij}^{\xi}
and, resp.,
\tau_{ij}^{\eta}
are solutions of (
35
) with
\xi
, resp.,
\eta
.
Note that
\xi_{i}\equiv\eta_{i}
on
(-\infty,0]
for all
i\in[N]
and

\displaystyle\left\|\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{i}(s)-\kappa_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq T\left\|\omega_{i}-\kappa_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.

(37)

We now estimate, for
s\in[0,T]
, the integrand in (
36
) by

\displaystyle\bigl|\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)(\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s))-\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)(\kappa_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s)))-\kappa_{i}(s)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)-\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)\bigr|\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s)\bigr|

(38)

\displaystyle+\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\kappa_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s)+\kappa_{i}(s)\bigr|.

To estimate the first term we use the assumed uniform Lipschitz continuity of
\psi
with constant
L_{\psi}

and the uniform boundedness
|\omega_{i}|\leq{\mathfrak{m}}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\psi(|\xi_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|)-\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)\bigr|\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathfrak{m}}L_{\psi}\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|.

By the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+|\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|\xi-\eta\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|\xi-\eta\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+{\mathfrak{m}}|\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s)-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s)|,

where we used the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\eta_{j}
for the last inequality.
Now, with (
35
) we have

\displaystyle|\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s)-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s)|={\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|,

(39)

so that

\displaystyle\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|\xi-\eta\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|,

which immediately gives

\displaystyle\bigl||\xi_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s))-\xi_{i}(s)|-|\eta_{j}(s-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(1-{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\right)^{-1}\left\|\xi-\eta\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(1-{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\right)^{-1}T\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)},

where we used (
37
) for the second inequality.
Consequently, the first term in the right-hand side of (
38
) is bounded by the expression

\displaystyle 4{\mathfrak{m}}L_{\psi}\left(1-{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\right)^{-1}T\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.

The second term of the right-hand side in (
38
) is estimated as follows, using again
\psi\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\psi(|\eta_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\eta_{i}(s)|)\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\kappa_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{i}(s)+\kappa_{i}(s)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))-\kappa_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))\bigr|+\bigl|\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\xi}_{ij}(s))-\omega_{j}(s-\tau^{\eta}_{ij}(s))\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+2{\mathfrak{m}}|\tau_{ij}^{\xi}(s)-\tau_{ij}^{\eta}(s)|,

where we used the
2{\mathfrak{m}}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\omega
in the second inequality.
Then, using (
39
) and (
40
), we conclude that
the second term in the right-hand side of (
38
) is bounded by the expression

\displaystyle 2\left(1+2{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\left(1-{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\right)^{-1}T\right)\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.

Therefore, with (
36
) we finally arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|\Upsilon_{i}[\omega]-\Upsilon_{i}[\kappa]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq 2T\left(1+2{\mathfrak{m}}(L_{\psi}+{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1})\left(1-{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\right)^{-1}T\right)\left\|\omega-\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

(41)

and choosing
T>0
sufficiently small, the claim follows.

Finally, let us note that choosing
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\neq{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
may lead to different values
of
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
, however, due to the contraction property (

),
the solution remains unique on
[0,\min\{T_{1},T_{2}\}]
.
∎

With Lemma
3
we constructed a unique solution of (
3
)–(
7
)
on a sufficiently short time interval
[0,T]
.
In the next step we prove that, assuming
\mathbf{v}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
,
the velocity trajectories remain uniformly bounded by
{\mathfrak{s}}
,
which implies that the solution is in fact global in time.
For this purpose, let us define the velocity radius of the agent group,

\displaystyle R_{v}(t):=\max_{i\in[N]}|v_{i}(t)|.

(42)

Lemma 4
.

Let the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}
with
{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Then, along the solutions of (
3
)–(
7
), the diameter
R_{v}
defined in (
42
) satisfies

\displaystyle R_{v}(t)\leq{\mathfrak{s}}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\geq 0.

(43)

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
, we may fix
\varepsilon>0
such that
{\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon<{\mathfrak{c}}
.
We shall prove that for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle R_{v}(t)<{\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon.

(44)

By continuity of the velocity trajectories,
(
44
) holds on the maximal interval
[0,T)
for some
T>0
.
For contradiction, let us assume that
T<+\infty
.
Then we have
R_{v}(T)={\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon
.
Since
{\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon<{\mathfrak{c}}
, Lemma
3
allows us to extend the solution

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
past
T
. I.e., the solution exists on some right neighborhood of
T

and we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}\geq 0,

(45)

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}
denotes the right-hand side derivative of
R_{v}^{2}
at
t=T
.
Moreover, by continuity of the velocity trajectories,
there exists an index
i\in[N]
such that
R_{v}(t)\equiv|v_{i}(t)|

for
t\in[T,T+\delta)
for some
\delta>0
.
From (
5
) we then have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}|v_{i}(T)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left[v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))-v_{i}(T)\right]\cdot v_{i}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left[v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))\cdot v_{i}(T)-|v_{i}(T)|^{2}\right].

We now distinguish two cases:

•

If
\tau_{ij}(T)=0
for all
j\in[N]
, then (
3
) implies
x_{j}(T)=x_{i}(T)
for all
j\in[N]
.
Moreover, since by definition
|v_{j}(T)|\leq R_{v}(T)=|v_{i}(T)|
, we have by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))\cdot v_{i}(T)-|v_{i}(T)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{j}(T)\cdot v_{i}(T)-|v_{i}(T)|^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(|v_{j}(T)|-|v_{i}(T)|\right)|v_{i}(T)|\leq 0,

so that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}|v_{i}(T)|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left(|v_{j}(T)|-|v_{i}(T)|\right)|v_{i}(T)|\leq 0.

Then (
45
) implies
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}=0
, which means that equality takes place
in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
46
), i.e.,

\displaystyle v_{j}(T)\cdot v_{i}(T)=|v_{i}(T)|^{2}\qquad\mbox{for all }j\in[N].

That means that
v_{j}(T)=v_{i}(T)
for all
j\in[N]
, and since also
x_{j}(T)=x_{i}(T)
for all
j\in[N]
,
the system reached equilibrium at time
T
and does not evolve further
(note that Lemma
3
provides local uniqueness,
so the constant equilibrium solution is the only possible continuation
past the time
T
).
This trivially implies that (
44
) holds for all
t>0
.

•

If there exists at least one
j\in[N]
such that
\tau_{ij}(T)>0
, then again by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))\cdot v_{i}(T)-|v_{i}(T)|^{2}\leq\left(|v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))|-|v_{i}(T)|\right)|v_{i}(T)|<0,

since
|v_{j}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))|\leq R_{v}(T-\tau_{ij}(T))<{\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon

and
|v_{i}(T)|=R_{v}(T)={\mathfrak{s}}+\varepsilon>0
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t+}R_{v}(T)^{2}<0,

which is a contradiction to (
45
).

The uniform bound (
43
) is obtained by taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Combination of the local uniqueness and existence result of Lemma
3

with the uniform bound on the velocity diameter directly implies
the global existence and uniqueness claim of Theorem
1
.

It remains to observe that, by Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle t-\tau_{ij}(t)\geq t-\frac{{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}},

and from (
4
),

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+2{\mathfrak{s}}t,

where we used the bound
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)\leq 2R_{v}(t)\leq 2{\mathfrak{s}}
provided by (
43
).
Consequently,

\displaystyle t-\tau_{ij}(t)\geq\frac{({\mathfrak{c}}-3{\mathfrak{s}})t-{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}},

and

\displaystyle\min_{t\in[0,T]}\left(t-\tau_{ij}(t)\right)\geq-\frac{{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+[{\mathfrak{c}}-3{\mathfrak{s}}]^{-}T}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}.

Therefore, the initial datum is only relevant on the bounded interval
\left[-S(T),0\right]
,
with
S(T)
given by (
11
).

4
Flocking - proof of Theorem
2

In this Section we shall assume the influence function
\psi
to be a nonincreasing function of its argument on
[0,\infty)
.
This is without loss of generality, since if
\psi
was not monotone,
it could be replaced by its nonincreasing rearrangement
\Psi
in the below proofs,

\displaystyle\Psi(u):=\min_{s\in[0,u]}\psi(s)\qquad\mbox{for }u\geq 0,

(47)

without affecting their validity.

Lemma 5
.

Along the solutions of (
3
)–(
7
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)\leq-\frac{N}{N-1}\underline{\psi}(t){d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)+2D(t)\qquad\mbox{for almost all }t>0,

(48)

with
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}={d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)
given by (
9
),

\displaystyle\underline{\psi}(t):=\min_{i,j\in[N]}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}(t),

(49)

and

\displaystyle D(t):=\max_{i\in[N]}\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}|{\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}|.

(50)

Proof.

Due to the continuity of the solution trajectories
x_{i}(t)
,
there is an at most countable system of open, mutually disjoint
intervals
\{\mathscr{I}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{\mathscr{I}_{\sigma}}=[0,\infty)

and for each
{\sigma\in\mathbb{N}}
there exist indices
i(\sigma)
,
k(\sigma)

such that

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)=|v_{i(\sigma)}(t)-v_{k(\sigma)}(t)|\quad\mbox{for }t\in\mathscr{I}_{\sigma}.

Then, using the abbreviated notation
i:=i(\sigma)
,
k:=k(\sigma)
,
we have for every
t\in\mathscr{I}_{\sigma}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|v_{i}-v_{k}|^{2}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\bigl({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{i}\bigr)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq k}\widetilde{\psi}_{kj}\bigl({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;k}-v_{k}\bigr)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k}).

Let us work on the first term of the right-hand side. We have for any
j\in[N]
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{i}\right)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})=\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}\right)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})+\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left(v_{j}-v_{i}\right)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k}).

(52)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
50
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left({\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}\right)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})\leq\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left|{\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}\right|\left|v_{i}-v_{k}\right|\leq D(t){d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t).

For the second term in (
52
), we observe, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle(v_{j}-v_{i})\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{j}-v_{k})\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})-|v_{i}-v_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|v_{i}-v_{k}|\bigl(|v_{j}-v_{k}|-|v_{i}-v_{k}|\bigr)\leq 0,

since, by definition,
|v_{j}-v_{k}|\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}=|v_{i}-v_{k}|
.
Moreover, with (
49
) we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left(v_{j}-v_{i}\right)\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})\leq\underline{\psi}\,(v_{j}-v_{i})\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k}).

Carrying out analogous steps for the second term of the right-hand side of (
51
),
we finally obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2{d_{\mathbf{v}}}D+\frac{\underline{\psi}}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigl[(v_{j}-v_{i})-(v_{j}-v_{k})\bigr]\cdot(v_{i}-v_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[2D-\frac{N}{N-1}\underline{\psi}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}\right]{d_{\mathbf{v}}}.

This immediately gives the statement.
∎

For
t\geq 0
let us define the maximal delay at time
t
,

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}(t):=\max_{i,j\in[N]}\tau_{ij}(t).

(53)

Lemma 6
.

Let the initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}\in C((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R}^{Nd})
be Lipschitz continuous
on
(-\infty,0]
, with Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\geq 0
.
Then, along the solutions of (
3
)–(
7
), we have

\displaystyle D(t)\leq L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{-}+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{+}}^{t}D(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{+}}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s\qquad\mbox{for }t\geq 0,

(54)

where we denoted
[a]^{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
,
[a]^{-}:=\max\{-a,0\}
,
the quantity
D=D(t)
is defined in (
50
) and
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}={d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)
given by (
9
).

Proof.

For any
i,j\in[N]
with
i\neq j
and
t\geq 0
we have,

\displaystyle|{\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}|=|v_{j}(t-\tau_{ij})-v_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t-\tau_{ij}}^{t}\left|\dot{v}_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t-\overline{\tau}}^{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}\left|\dot{v}_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}\left|\dot{v}_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s.

For the first integral of the right-hand side we have

\displaystyle\int_{t-\overline{\tau}}^{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}\left|\dot{v}_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s\leq L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\overline{\tau}]^{-},

while for the second we use (
5
),

\displaystyle\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}\left|\dot{v}_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}\sum_{\ell\neq j}\widetilde{\psi}_{j\ell}\left|\widetilde{v}^{j}_{\ell}(s)-v_{j}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}\sum_{\ell\neq j}\widetilde{\psi}_{j\ell}\left(\left|\widetilde{v}^{j}_{\ell}(s)-v_{\ell}(s)\right|+\left|v_{\ell}(s)-v_{j}(s)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}D(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

where we used the estimate
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{\ell\neq j}\widetilde{\psi}_{j\ell}\leq 1
implied by universal bound
\psi\leq 1
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[N]}\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}|{\widetilde{v}_{j}}^{\;i}-v_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[N]}\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}\left(L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\overline{\tau}]^{-}+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}D(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{-}+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}D(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}]^{+}}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s.

∎

Lemma 7
.

Fix
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)
,
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
,
L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\geq 0
,
{\mathfrak{s}}>0
, and
\eta>0
provided by assumption (
12
).
Assume that there exist
{\mathfrak{c}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
,
\kappa>D(0)
and
\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
such that

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\tau^{\ast}+(\kappa+\sigma)\frac{e^{\eta\tau^{\ast}}-1}{\eta}\leq\kappa\qquad\mbox{with }\tau^{\ast}:=({\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}})^{-1}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right),

(55)

and

\displaystyle\frac{2\kappa}{\psi^{\ast}-\eta}\leq\sigma-{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)\qquad\mbox{with }\psi^{\ast}:=\psi({\mathfrak{c}}\tau^{\ast})>\eta.

(56)

Then

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)<\sigma e^{-\eta t}\mbox{ and }D(t)<\kappa e^{-\eta t}

(57)

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

Let us define the set

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}:=\left\{t>0;\;D(t)<\kappa e^{-\eta t}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)<\sigma e^{-\eta t}\right\}.

(58)

Since, by assumption,
\kappa>D(0)
and
\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
, the set
\mathscr{S}
is nonempty, so that we may define

T:=\sup\mathscr{S}
.
We aim at showing that
T=+\infty
. For contradiction, let us assume that
T<+\infty
.
By continuity of the functions
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}={d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)
and
D=D(t)
we then have

\displaystyle D(T)=\kappa e^{-\eta T}\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(T)=\sigma e^{-\eta T}.

(59)

By the definition (
9
) of the diameters
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}
and
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}
we have

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

and from the definition (
58
) of the set
\mathscr{S}
it follows for
t<T
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s<\sigma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\eta s}\mathrm{d}s<\frac{\sigma}{\eta},

so that

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)<{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}.

(60)

An application of Lemma (
2
) then gives

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}(t)=\max_{i,j\in[N]}\tau_{ij}(t)\leq\frac{{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}<\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)=\tau^{\ast}

(61)

for
t<T
.

Using (
54
) and (
58
), we have for
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{-}+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{+}}^{t}D(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{[t-\overline{\tau}(t)]^{+}}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\tau^{\ast}]^{-}+(\kappa+\sigma)\int_{[t-\tau^{\ast}]^{+}}^{t}e^{-\eta s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,[t-\tau^{\ast}]^{-}e^{\eta t}+(\kappa+\sigma)\frac{e^{\eta\tau^{\ast}}-1}{\eta}\right)e^{-\eta t}.

An elementary calculation, using the fact that
\eta<1
and realizing that (
55
) implies
\tau^{\ast}<1
, gives

\displaystyle[t-\tau^{\ast}]^{-}e^{\eta t}\leq\tau^{\ast}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\geq 0.

Consequently,

\displaystyle D(T)<\left(L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}\,\tau^{\ast}+(\kappa+\sigma)\frac{e^{\eta\tau^{\ast}}-1}{\eta}\right)e^{-\eta T},

and assumption (
55
) gives
D(T)<\kappa e^{-\eta T}
,
so that the first alternative in (
59
) is excluded.

To exclude the second alternative in (
59
), we recall that
\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}(t)=\psi(|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}|)

and apply the triangle inequality, (
32
) and (
31
), which yields

\displaystyle|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{i}|\leq|{\widetilde{x}_{j}}^{\;i}-x_{j}|+|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq{\mathfrak{s}}\tau_{ij}+{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t).

Since, by assumption,
\psi
is a non-increasing function, we have for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}(t)\geq\psi\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)\right).

Combining this with (
60
), we obtain
\widetilde{\psi}_{ij}(t)\geq\psi^{\ast}
for all
t<T
and all
i,j\in[N]
,
with
\psi^{\ast}
defined in (
56
).
Then, Lemma
5
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{N-1}\psi^{\ast}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)+2D(t)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\psi^{\ast}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(t)+2\kappa e^{-\eta t}

for almost all
t<T
. An integration on the interval
(0,T)
then yields

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)e^{-\psi^{\ast}T}+2\kappa\frac{e^{-\eta T}-e^{-\psi^{\ast}T}}{\psi^{\ast}-\eta}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left({d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)+\frac{2\kappa}{\psi^{\ast}-\eta}\right)e^{-\eta T},

where we used the fact that
\eta<\psi^{\ast}
in the second inequality.
Assumption (
56
) gives
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(T)<\sigma e^{-\eta T}
,
and, consequently, the second alternative in (
59
) is excluded.

We conclude that
T=+\infty
which finishes the proof.
∎

Obviously, (
57
) implies flocking in the sense of Definition
1
,
noting that the uniform boundedness of
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}={d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)
follows from

\displaystyle{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(t)\leq{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(s)\mathrm{d}s<{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\eta s}\mathrm{d}s<{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\qquad\mbox{for all }t\geq 0.

Clearly, the crucial step of the proof of Theorem
2

is to find values of
{\mathfrak{c}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
,
\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
and
\kappa>D(0)

such that the assumptions of Lemma
7
are satisfied.
This essentially means to resolve the nonlinear algebraic system (
55
)–(
56
).
We were only able to find an analytical solution under the additional assumption
that the initial velocity trajectories
\mathbf{v}^{0}
are constant on
(-\infty,0]
.
Then
L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}=0
and
D(0)=0
, which facilitates the following result.

Lemma 8
.

Assume that
L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}=0
and
D(0)=0
.
For any fixed
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)
,
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)\geq 0
,
{\mathfrak{s}}>0
, and
\eta>0

provided by assumption (
12
), there exist
{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
,

\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
and
\kappa>0
such that for any
{\mathfrak{c}}\geq{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}
the conditions (
55
) and (
56
) are verified.

Proof.

Assumption (
12
) and continuity of
\psi
implies that, for fixed
\eta>0
, there exist

\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
such that

\displaystyle\psi\left((1+\varepsilon)\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\right)>\eta.

(62)

We set

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{\sigma-{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{2}\left[\psi\left((1+\varepsilon)\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\right)-\eta\right].

(63)

Now, with the values of
{\mathfrak{s}}>0
,
\eta>0
,
\sigma>{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)
and
\kappa>0
fixed, the equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\ln\left(\frac{\eta\kappa}{\kappa+\sigma}+1\right)=\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)

(64)

is uniquely solvable in
{\mathfrak{c}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
, since
\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}
is a monotonically decreasing function of
{\mathfrak{c}}>{\mathfrak{s}}

with values in
(0,\infty)
.
We denote this unique solution
{\mathfrak{c}}_{1}
and set

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}:=\max\left\{{\mathfrak{c}}_{1},\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}{\mathfrak{s}}\right\}.

(65)

We claim that the above choice of
\sigma
,
\kappa
and
{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}

verifies the assumptions of Lemma
7
,
in particular, the conditions (
55
) and (
56
).
Indeed, recalling the definition of
\tau^{\ast}
given by (
55
),

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast}=\frac{1}{{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right),

(66)

equation (
64
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\ln\left(\frac{\eta\kappa}{\kappa+\sigma}+1\right)\leq\tau^{\ast},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\kappa+\sigma)\frac{e^{\eta\tau^{\ast}}-1}{\eta}\leq\kappa.

This proves (
55
) with
L_{\mathbf{v}}^{0}=0
.

By (
65
) we have
\frac{{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}}{{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\leq 1+\varepsilon
, which implies

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}\tau^{\ast}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right).

Due to the monotonicity of
\psi
, we then have for
\psi^{\ast}:=\psi({{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}\tau^{\ast})

\displaystyle\psi^{\ast}\geq\psi\left((1+\varepsilon)\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\right),

and, due to (
62
),
\psi^{\ast}>\eta
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{2\kappa}{\psi^{\ast}-\eta}\leq 2\kappa\left[\psi\left((1+\varepsilon)\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\right)-\eta\right]^{-1},

and substituting for
\kappa
from (
63
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{2\kappa}{\psi^{\ast}-\eta}\leq\sigma-{\mathfrak{s}},

which is (
56
).

Finally, let us note that for any
{\mathfrak{c}}\geq{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\leq\tau^{\ast}.

Consequently, (
55
) remains valid with
{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}
replaced by
{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau:=\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)\leq\frac{{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}}{{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{\sigma}{\eta}\right)={{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}\tau^{\ast},

since
\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}
is a decreasing function of
{\mathfrak{c}}>{\mathfrak{s}}
.
Consequently,
\psi({\mathfrak{c}}\tau)\geq\psi^{\ast}
and (
56
) keeps its validity as well. ∎

Remark 1
.

Note that for a given influence function
\psi
and values of
\eta
,
{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0
),
{d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)
and
{\mathfrak{s}}
,
the proof of Lemma
8
provides a recipe for calculating the value of
{{\mathfrak{c}}^{\ast}}
explicitly
(although it may not provide the optimal value).
On the other hand, admitting nonconstant initial datum
\mathbf{v}^{0}

makes it necessary to decide the solvability of the algebraic system (
55
)–(
56
),
which does not seem to be achievable analytically.
However, the problem is well approachable by numerical methods.

Finally, we provide the proof of Proposition
1
.
Clearly, (
12
) is satisfiable whenever
\inf_{s\in(0,\infty)}\psi(s)>0
.
Therefore, let us consider
\psi=\psi(s)
such that
\lim_{s\to\infty}\psi(s)=0
.
Moreover, recall that we assume
\psi=\psi(s)
to be monotone
(otherwise we can replace it by its nonincreasing rearrangement).
Assumption (
13
) implies that
\psi(s)\geq cs^{\alpha}
for some constant
c>0
and all
s>0
large enough.
Denoting
\Psi(s):=cs^{\alpha}
, we readily calculate for
\alpha>-1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0+}\Psi\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{\eta}\right)-\eta=0,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\left[\Psi\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{\eta}\right)-\eta\right]_{\eta=0+}=-\infty.

Consequently, for
\eta>0
small enough

\displaystyle\psi\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{\eta}\right)-\eta\geq\Psi\left({d_{\mathbf{x}}}(0)+\frac{{d_{\mathbf{v}}}(0)}{\eta}\right)-\eta>0,

and the claim of Proposition
1
follows.

5
Mean field limit

Let us fix
T>0
and
0<{\mathfrak{s}}<{\mathfrak{c}}
and recall the definition (
15
) of the set

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}:=\Bigl\{\gamma\in C_{b}^{1}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C_{\mathfrak{s}}((-\infty,T];\mathbb{R}^{d});\;\dot{\gamma}|_{[0,T]}\in C_{2{\mathfrak{s}}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\Bigr\},

equipped with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}
defined in (
16
),

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}:=\left\|\gamma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-\infty,T)}+\left\|\dot{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\qquad\mbox{for }\gamma\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}.

Obviously, due to the uniform boundedness of
\gamma\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
on
(-\infty,T)
and the uniform Lipschitz continuity of
\dot{\gamma}
on
(0,T)
,
we have
\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}<+\infty
for all
\gamma\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
.
We also recall that
{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
denotes the space of probability measures on
\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}

with finite first-order moment
and it is equipped with the Monge–Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance
\mathscr{W}_{T}

defined in (
26
).
For any
\rho
,
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
we have
\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu)<+\infty
since

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}\times\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\left\|\gamma\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)+\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\left\|\xi\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi),

and the measures
\rho
,
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
have, by definition, finite first-order moment.
The set
{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
endowed with the distance
\mathscr{W}_{T}
is a complete metric space.

We consider the problem

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{law}(x),

(67)

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(0)+\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s,

(68)

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(0)+\int_{0}^{t}F_{s}[\rho](x(s),v(s))\mathrm{d}s

(69)

with the operator
F_{t}[\rho]:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by (
19
), i.e.,

\displaystyle F_{t}[\rho](x,v):=\int_{\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\psi\left(\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-x\right|\right)\left(\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma).

Note that
F_{t}[\rho]
is well defined since with the universal bound
\psi\leq 1
and
\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\psi\left(\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-x\right|\right)\left(\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)\right|\leq\int_{\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left(\left|\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]\right|+|v|\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)\leq{\mathfrak{s}}+|v|.

We recall that the initial datum

Lemma 10
.

For any
\gamma
,
\xi\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}
and
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-\Gamma_{t,y}[\xi]\right|\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\Bigl(\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}|x-y|\Bigr),

(71)

where the mapping
\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]
is defined in (
20
), and

\displaystyle\left|\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-\Pi_{t,y}[\xi]\right|\leq\left\|\dot{\gamma}-\dot{\xi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\Bigl(\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+|x-y|\Bigr),

(72)

with
\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]
defined in (
22
).

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
.
By definition of
\Gamma_{t,x}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-\Gamma_{t,y}[\xi]\right|=\left|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\right|,

with

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]=\bigl|x-\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]\bigr|,\qquad{\mathfrak{c}}\tau_{t,y}[\xi]=\bigl|y-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi]\bigr|.

(73)

The triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])\bigr|+\bigl|\xi(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+{\mathfrak{s}}\bigl|\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]-\tau_{t,y}[\xi]\bigr|,

where we used the
{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\xi
for the last inequality.
Now, with (
73
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl|\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]-\tau_{t,y}[\xi]\bigr|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\bigl||\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-x|-|\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])-y|\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\Bigl(|x-y|+\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|\Bigr),

so that

\displaystyle\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}\Bigl(|x-y|+\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|\Bigr),

which immediately gives

\displaystyle\bigl|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\xi(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\bigr|\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{c}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\Bigl(\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}|x-y|\Bigr).

This proves (
71
).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-\Pi_{t,y}[\xi]\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\dot{\gamma}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\dot{\xi}(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\dot{\gamma}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\dot{\xi}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])\right|+\left|\dot{\xi}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-\dot{\xi}(t-\tau_{t,y}[\xi])\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\dot{\gamma}-\dot{\xi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+2{\mathfrak{s}}\bigl|\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]-\tau_{t,y}[\xi]\bigr|,

where we used the fact that
\dot{\xi}
is
2{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuous on
[0,T]
.
Combining the above estimates, we readily obtain

\displaystyle\bigl|\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]-\tau_{t,y}[\xi]\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\bigl(\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}+|x-y|\bigr),

which gives (
72
).
∎

Lemma 11
.

For any
T>0
there exists a constant

such that, for any
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})

\displaystyle\left\|(Z[\nu](\eta_{0})-Z[\nu](\xi_{0})\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\leq L_{Z}^{T}\left\|\eta_{0}-\xi_{0}\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}}\qquad\mbox{for all }\eta_{0},\xi_{0}\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}.

{\mathfrak{c}}
,
{\mathfrak{s}}
,
T
and the Lipschitz constant

Let us fix

\eta_{0}
,

\displaystyle|x(0)-y(0)|+T\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+\left|\Pi_{s,x(s)}[\gamma]-\Pi_{s,y(s)}[\gamma]\right|+|v(s)-w(s)|,

\psi\leq 1

\displaystyle|v(t)-w(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}\times\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left[\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,s)}+\left\|\dot{\gamma}-\dot{\xi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,s)}\right]\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma,\xi)\mathrm{d}s+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\int_{0}^{t}|v(s)-w(s)|+\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)|x(s)-y(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

Recalling that
x(0)=y(0)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|x(s)-y(s)|\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}|v(\sigma)-w(\sigma)|\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}s\leq t\int_{0}^{t}|v(s)-w(s)|\mathrm{d}s,

which gives

\displaystyle|v(t)-w(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{1,\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)\right\}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}\times\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{s}}\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma,\xi)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}t}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)+1\right)\int_{0}^{t}|v(s)-w(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\pi
is an optimal transference plan, we have
\iint_{\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}\times\Omega^{s}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left\|\gamma-\xi\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{s}}\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma,\xi)=\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)
,
so that

\displaystyle|v(t)-w(t)|\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)\right\}\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)\mathrm{d}s+\left(\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}t}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)+1\right)\int_{0}^{t}|v(s)-w(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

An application of the Gronwall lemma gives then

\displaystyle|v(t)-w(t)|\leq\bar{r}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)\mathrm{d}s

with

\displaystyle\bar{r}(t):=\max\left\{1,\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)\right\}\exp\left(\frac{2{\mathfrak{s}}t}{{\mathfrak{c}}-{\mathfrak{s}}}\left({\mathfrak{c}}L_{\psi}+1\right)+1\right).

(82)

We finally conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|(Z[\rho](\gamma^{0})-Z[\nu](\gamma^{0})\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+T)\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

(83)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+T)\bar{r}(T)\int_{0}^{T}\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+T)T\bar{r}(T)\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu),

where for the last inequality we used the fact that, by definition,
\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)

is a nondecreasing function of
s\in[0,T]
.
Consequently, (
79
) is verified with
r(T):=(1+T)T\bar{r}(T)
,
and (
80
) holds since
\bar{r}(T)
is bounded as
T\to 0
.
∎

5.2
Existence and uniqueness of solutions

For a fixed initial datum
\rho^{0}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}})
we define the set
{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}\subset{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}:=\left\{\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}});\;\mathbb{I}\#\rho=\rho^{0}\right\}.

(84)

The solution
\rho=\mbox{law}(x)
of the mean-field problem (
68
)–(
69
)
shall be constructed as the unique fixed point of the mapping

\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}]:{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}\to{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}](\rho):=Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0},

(85)

with the mapping
Z:\Omega^{0}_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\Omega^{T}_{\mathfrak{s}}
defined in (
70
).

Lemma 15
.

Fix
\rho^{0}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}})
.
For small enough
T>0
, the mapping
\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}]
given by (
85
) is a contraction on
{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}
in the topology
induced by the Monge-Kantorowich-Rubinstein distance
\mathscr{W}_{T}
defined in (
26
).

Proof.

We apply the Banach contraction theorem on the complete metric space
({\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]},\mathscr{W}_{T})
.
Clearly, by Lemma
9
,
\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}]
maps
{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}
into itself.
From Lemma
14
we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{T}(\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}](\rho),\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}](\nu))=\mathscr{W}_{T}(Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0},Z[\nu]\#\rho^{0})\leq r(T)\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu),

where
r=r(T)
is a continuous function with
\lim_{T\to 0}r(T)=0
.
Consequently,
\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}]
is a contraction on
{\mathscr{P}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}[\rho^{0}]}
for small enough
T>0
.
∎

The solution
\rho=\mathscr{Z}[\rho^{0}](\rho)
constructed in Lemma
15

for short times
T>0
can be extended
as long as the characteristics given by (
68
)–(
69
)
remain
{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuous with
2{\mathfrak{s}}
-Lipschitz continuous derivative.
This property is guaranteed by Lemma
9
for any
T>0
.
Consequently, the solutions are global in time, which concludes the proof of Theorem
3
.

5.3
Stability

Theorem 5
.

Fix
T>0
. Then there exists a constant
M_{T}>0
such that
for any
\rho^{0}
,
\nu^{0}\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}})
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{T}(\rho,\nu)\leq M_{T}\mathscr{W}_{0}(\rho^{0},\nu^{0}).

(86)

where
\rho=Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0}
and
\nu=Z[\nu]\#\nu^{0}
are the unique fixed points
of the mapping
\mathscr{Z}
provided by Lemma
15
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we have for any
t\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{t}(\rho,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{t}(Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0},Z(\nu)\#\nu^{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{t}(Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0},Z(\nu)\#\rho^{0})+\mathscr{W}_{t}(Z[\nu]\#\rho^{0},Z(\nu)\#\nu^{0}).

The first term of the right-hand side is estimated using Lemma
13
and (
83
),

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{t}(Z[\rho]\#\rho^{0},Z[\nu]\#\rho^{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma^{0}\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{0}}\left\|(Z[\rho](\gamma^{0})-Z[\nu](\gamma^{0})\right\|_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+t)\bar{r}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)\mathrm{d}s

with
\bar{r}(t)
defined in (
82
).
For the second term we use the estimate (
77
) of Lemma
12
.
We thus arrive at

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{t}(\rho,\nu)\leq(1+t)\bar{r}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{W}_{s}(\rho,\nu)\mathrm{d}s+L_{Z}^{t}\mathscr{W}_{0}(\rho^{0},\nu^{0}),

where
L_{Z}^{t}
is the constant provided by Lemma
11
.
We conclude by an application of the Gronwall lemma for
t\in[0,T]
, noting that both
(1+t)\bar{r}(t)
and
L_{Z}^{t}

are bounded on compact intervals.
∎

5.4
Remark about Fokker-Planck description

A natural question to ask is whether one can express the mean-field limit of the system
(
3
)–(
5
) in terms of a Fokker-Planck equation
for some time dependent phase-space particle density
g_{t}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
t\geq 0
.
An obvious candidate for such particle density is the push-forward measure
g_{t}:=(X_{t},V_{t})\#\rho
,
where the maps
X(t)
and
V(t)
were defined in (
25
),
and
\rho\in{\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}})
is a solution of the mean-field problem constructed in Theorem
3
.
Then, taking any test function
\varphi=\varphi(x,v)
and applying formally
the change-of-coordinates formula for the push-forward
g_{t}:=(X_{t},V_{t})\#\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}g_{t}(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\varphi(X_{t}[\gamma],V_{t}[\gamma])\,\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot\dot{X}_{t}[\gamma]+\nabla_{v}\varphi\cdot\dot{V}_{t}[\gamma]\,\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot V_{t}[\gamma]+\nabla_{v}\varphi\cdot F_{t}[\rho](X_{t}[\gamma],V_{t}[\gamma])\,\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma),

where we used the characteristic system (
68
)–(
69
) in the last equality.
Now, if we were able to express
F_{t}[\rho]
in terms of
g
as
G_{t}[g]
,
we could reverse the change-of-coordinates formula to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot V_{t}[\gamma]+\nabla_{v}\varphi\cdot F_{t}[\rho](X_{t}[\gamma],V_{t}[\gamma])\,\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot v+\nabla_{v}\varphi\cdot G_{t}[g](x,v)\,\mathrm{d}g_{t}(x,v).

Then we could formally describe the mean-field limit by the Fokker-Planck equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{t}+\nabla_{v}\cdot(G_{t}[g]g_{t})=0.

(87)

Note that with (
19
) we have

\displaystyle F_{t}[\rho](x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\psi\left(\left|\Gamma_{t,x}[\gamma]-x\right|\right)\left(\Pi_{t,x}[\gamma]-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\psi\left(\left|\gamma(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-x\right|\right)\left(\dot{\gamma}(t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma])-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{s}}^{T}}\psi\left(\left|X_{t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]}[\gamma]-x\right|\right)\left(V_{t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]}[\gamma]-v\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(\gamma),

where
\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]:=\tau
is the unique solution of (
21
), i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{c}}\tau=|x-X_{t-\tau}[\gamma]|.

However, the change-of-variables formula corresponding
to the push-forward
g_{t}=(X_{t},V_{t})\#\rho
cannot be applied here since the evaluation times
in
X_{t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]}
and
V_{t-\tau_{t,x}[\gamma]}

depend on
\gamma
. Consequently, the operator
F_{t}[\rho]
does not seem
to admit an equivalent form in terms of the push-forward measure
g_{t}=(X_{t},V_{t})\#\rho
.
In particular, one may be tempted to believe that the mean-field limit of (
3
)–(
5
)
should be given by (
87
) with the operator
G_{t}[f]
taking the form

\displaystyle G_{t}[g](x,v)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(|y-x|)(w-v)\,g(t-{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}|x-y|,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w.

However, apart from the obvious difficulties with giving a meaning to the expression
g(t-{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}|x-y|,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w

for measure-valued
g
, the above argument indicates that such an intuitive expectation is wrong.
We therefore conclude that the mean-field limit does not admit a description in terms of the (classical) Fokker-Planck equation (
87
),
and one has indeed to resort to the formulation with probability measures on the space of time-dependent trajectories,
as we did in this paper.

Local and Global Log-Gradient estimates of solutions to Δ p ​ v + b ​ v q + c ​ v r = 0 \Delta_{p}v+bv^{q}+cv^{r}=0 on manifolds and applications Thanks: *Corresponding author
Jie He, Yuanqing Ma*, Youde Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we employ the Nash-Moser iteration technique to study local and global properties of positive solutions to the equation Δ p ​ v + b ​ v q + c ​ v r = 0 \Delta_{p}v+bv^{q}+cv^{r}=0 on complete Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below, where b , c ∈ ℝ b,c\in\mathbb{R} , p > 1 p>1 , and q ≤ r q\leq r are some real constants. Assuming certain conditions on b , c , p , q b,\,c,\,p,\,q and r r , we derive succinct Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates for positive solutions, which is of sharp form. These gradient estimates allow us to obtain some Liouville-type theorems and Harnack inequalities. Our Liouville-type results are novel even in Euclidean spaces. Based on the local gradient estimates and a trick of Sung and Wang( [ 23 ] ), we also obtain the global gradient estimates for such solutions. As applications we show the uniqueness of positive solutions to some generalized Allen-Cahn equation and Fisher-KPP equation.



Key words: Gradient estimate; Nash-Moser iteration; Liouville type theorem

1.
Introduction

One trend in Riemannian geometry since the 1950’s has been the study of how curvature affects global properties of partial differential equations and global quantities like the eigenvalues of the Laplacian. It is well-known that gradient estimate is a fundamental and powerful technique in the analysis of partial differential equations on Riemannian manifolds.

One of most classical results about gradient estimates can be traced back to Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate for positive harmonic functions (see
[
20
,
31
]
). Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
, Cheng and Yau deduced that for any positive harmonic function
v
in a geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
, there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq C_{n}\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}.

(1.1)

An important feature of Cheng-Yau’s estimate is that the right-hand side of (
1.1
) depends only on
n,\kappa
and
R
, it does not depend on the injective radius or other global properties. Cheng-Yau’s estimate turned out to be very useful. Harnack inequality follows immediately from Cheng-Yau’s estimate. Liouville theorem for global positive harmonic functions on non-compact manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature is also a direct consequence of (
1.1
).
Cheng-Yau’s approach can also be used to derive the estimates of the spectrum of manifolds and investigate the geometry of manifolds(see
[
20
,
13
]
).

After Cheng-Yau’s work, gradient estimates for harmonic functions are generalized to more general space(
[
30
,
32
]
) and gradient estimates for many other equations defined on Riemannian manifolds are established (see for example
[
26
,
9

10

In this article, we are concerned with the equation

\displaystyle\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{4a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{8a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}+\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{a_{2}t}\leq\frac{a_{4}}{t},

furthermore, we can infer from (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q,r}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-c_{0}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}R^{-2}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}.\end{split}

(3.29)

Now we choose

\displaystyle t_{0}=c_{n,p,q,r}\left(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R\right)\quad\text{and}\quad c_{n,p,q,r}=\max\left\{c_{0}+1,\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}\beta_{n,p,q,r}}\right\},

(3.30)

and pick

(3.31)

Immediately, it follows from (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q,r}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\

(3.32)

(1)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dim(
n\geq 2
) complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa
where
\kappa
is a non-negative constant. Let

be a global positive solution of (
1.2
) in

.

(1)

If (
4.1
) is satisfied, equivalently
(b,c,q,r)\in W_{1}
,
we denote
y_{1}=(n-1)^{2}\kappa
. For any
\delta>0
and
\omega=(f-y_{1}-\delta)^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(k_{1}\omega-k_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right),

where
\alpha>2
and
k_{1},k_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
n,p,q,r,\kappa
and
\delta
.

(2)

If (
4.2
) is satisfied, i.e.
(b,c,q,r)\in W_{2}
,
we denote

\displaystyle y_{2}=\frac{4\kappa}{\left(\frac{n+3}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p-1}-1\right)}.

For any
\delta>0
,
\omega=(f-y_{2}-\delta)^{+}
and
\alpha
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right),

where
l_{1},l_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
n,p,q,r,\kappa
and
\delta
.

(3)

If (
4.3
) is satisfied, i.e.
(b,c,q,r)\in W_{3}
, we denote

\displaystyle y_{3}=\frac{4\kappa}{\left(\frac{n+3}{n-1}-\frac{r}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{r}{p-1}-1\right)}.

For any
\delta>0
,
\omega=(f-y_{3}-\delta)^{+}
and
\alpha
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right),

where
l_{1},l_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
n,p,q,r,\kappa
and
\delta
.

Proof.

Case 1.

According to Lemma
3.1
, if (
4.1
) is satisfied, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|.

(4.4)

On
\{f\geq y_{1}+\delta\}
, the definition of operator
\mathcal{L}
yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})={\rm div}(\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}A(\nabla f))=\alpha(\alpha-1)\omega^{\alpha-2}\langle\nabla\omega,A(\nabla f)\rangle+\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\mathcal{L}(f).

In the above expression, it is easy to see that
\nabla\omega=\nabla f
in
\{f>y_{1}+\delta\}
, but
\nabla\omega
causes a distribution on
\{f=y_{1}+\delta\}
. Now, if we assume
\alpha>2
, then the distribution caused by
\nabla\omega
on
\{f=y_{1}+\delta\}
is eliminated by
\omega
since
\omega=0
on
\{f=y_{1}+\delta\}
.

Since
f\geq\omega
and
\langle\nabla f,A(\nabla f)\rangle\geq 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ \omega^{\alpha-1}\left(\alpha(\alpha-1)f^{-1}\langle\nabla f,A(\nabla f)\rangle+\alpha\mathcal{L}(f)\right)=\ \frac{\omega^{\alpha-1}}{f^{\alpha-1}}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha}).

It follows from (
4.4
) and the above inequality that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\left(\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Since
f\geq y_{1}+\delta=(n-1)^{2}\kappa+\delta
, The above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{f}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\delta}{n-1}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Using the fact

y_{1}+\delta\leq f\leq C(n,p,q,r)\sqrt{\kappa}

on
\{f\geq y_{1}+\delta\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(k_{1}\omega-k_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right),\quad\forall\alpha>2,

where
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
n,p,q,r,\kappa,\delta
.

Case 2.

If (
4.2
) is satisfied, the inequality (
3.14
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ \beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}f^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|,

where

\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}=\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{4}\left(\frac{q}{p-1}-\frac{n+1}{n-1}\right)^{2}>0.

Then, on
\{f\geq y_{2}+\delta\}
,

f\geq y_{2}+\delta=\frac{\kappa(n-1)}{\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}}+\delta.

Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Since

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa=\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}f-(n-1)\kappa\geq\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}\delta,

we can choose
\alpha
large enough such that

\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa>\frac{1}{2}\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}\delta.

It follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}\delta}{2}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(f^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}\delta}{2}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Using the fact

y_{2}+\delta\leq f\leq C(n,p,q,r)\sqrt{\kappa}\quad\text{and}\quad f\geq\omega

on
\{f\geq y_{2}+\delta\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\beta^{(2)}_{n,p,q}\delta}{2}\omega-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|),

where
l_{1},l_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
n,p,q,r,\kappa,\delta
.

Case 3.

If (
4.3
) is satisfied, the inequality (
3.15
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ \beta_{n,p,r,\alpha}f^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|,

where

\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\beta_{n,p,r,\alpha}=\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{4}\left(\frac{r}{p-1}-\frac{n+1}{n-1}\right)^{2}>0.

Then, on
\{f\geq y_{3}+\delta\}
,

f\geq y_{3}+\delta=\frac{\kappa(n-1)}{\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}}+\delta.

Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\beta_{n,p,r,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right).

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\beta_{n,p,r,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa=\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}f-(n-1)\kappa\geq\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}\delta,

we can choose
\alpha
large enough such that

\beta_{n,p,r,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa>\frac{1}{2}\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}\delta.

It follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}\delta}{2}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(f^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}\delta}{2}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Using the facts

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\beta^{(3)}_{n,p,r}\delta}{2}\omega-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|),

and

, and

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dim(
n\geq 2
) complete Riemannian manifold satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
for some constant
\kappa\geq 0
. Let
v\in C^{1}(M)
be an entire positive solution of (
1.2
). For some
y>0
, we define
\omega=(f-y)^{+}
. If
\omega
satisfies the following inequality

\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq\omega^{\alpha-1}(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|),

where
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
and
\alpha
are some positive constants, then
\omega\equiv 0
, i.e.,
f\leq y
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a cut-off function to be determine later. We choose
\eta^{2}\omega^{\gamma}
as test function, then there holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathcal{L}(\omega^{\alpha})\omega^{\gamma}\eta^{2}\geq\int_{M}2\alpha\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}.

We omit the term
f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}
since
f
is uniform bounded on
M
. By integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\alpha\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\alpha\gamma\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\eta^{2}(|\nabla\omega|^{2}+(p-2)f^{-1}\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla u\rangle^{2})

\displaystyle-\int_{M}2\alpha\eta\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla\eta\rangle+(p-2)f^{-1}\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle\right).

It follows

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}+a_{2}\int_{M}\gamma\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\eta^{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(p+1)\int_{M}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega||\nabla\eta|\eta,

where
a_{2}=\min\{1,p-1\}>0
is defined in Section 2.

We note that Cauchy inequality implies

\displaystyle 2l_{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega|\eta^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ l_{2}\eta^{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\left(\frac{|\nabla\omega|^{2}}{\epsilon}+\epsilon\omega^{2}\right);

\displaystyle 2\eta(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega||\nabla\eta|\leq

\displaystyle\ (p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\left(\frac{\eta^{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}}{\epsilon}+\epsilon|\nabla\eta|^{2}\omega^{2}\right).

If we choose
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\frac{l_{2}+p+1}{\epsilon}=a_{2}\gamma,

then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}2l_{1}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}\leq\int_{M}\epsilon(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\int_{M}\epsilon l_{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}.

Picking
\gamma
large enough such that
\epsilon l_{2}<l_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle l_{1}\int_{M}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}\leq\int_{M}\epsilon(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Now, if
\omega\neq 0
, without loss of generality we may assume that
\omega|_{B_{1}}\neq 0
for some geodesic ball
B_{1}
with radius 1, it follows that there always holds true

\int_{B_{1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}>0.

By choosing
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{k+1})
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{k}
and
|\nabla\eta|<4
, then from the above argument we have

\displaystyle l_{1}\int_{B_{k}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\leq 4\epsilon(p+1)\int_{B_{k+1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}.

By iteration on
k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\geq\left(\frac{C}{\epsilon}\right)^{k}\int_{B_{1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}.

Since
\omega
is uniformly bounded and

{\rm{vol}}(B_{k})\leq e^{(n-1)k\sqrt{\kappa}}

by volume comparison theorem, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{(n-1)k\sqrt{\kappa}}\geq e^{k\ln\frac{C}{\epsilon}}.

We choose
\gamma
such that

\ln\frac{C}{\epsilon}>2(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa}+2

and then choose
k
large enough, immediately we get a contradiction.
This contradiction means
\omega\equiv 0
and we finish the proof of this lemma.
∎

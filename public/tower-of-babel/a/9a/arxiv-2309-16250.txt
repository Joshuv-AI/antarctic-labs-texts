Abstract
We present an axiomatisation of the fuzzy bi-Gödel modal logic 𝐊 ​ 𝖻𝗂𝖦 𝖿 {\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{f}} formulated in the language containing △ \triangle (Baaz Delta operator) and treating \Yleft \Yleft (coimplication) as the defined connective. We also consider two paraconsistent relatives of 𝐊 ​ 𝖻𝗂𝖦 𝖿 {\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{f}} — 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 2 ± 𝖿 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}} and 𝖦 ■ , ⧫ 2 ± 𝖿 \mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge} . These logics are defined on fuzzy frames with two valuations e 1 e_{1} and e 2 e_{2} standing for the support of truth and falsity, respectively, and equipped with two fuzzy relations R + R^{+} and R − R^{-} used to determine supports of truth and falsity of modal formulas. We construct embeddings of 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 2 ± 𝖿 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}} and 𝖦 ■ , ⧫ 2 ± 𝖿 \mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge} into 𝐊 ​ 𝖻𝗂𝖦 𝖿 {\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{f}} and use them to obtain the characterisation of 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 2 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2} - and 𝖦 ■ , ⧫ 2 \mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge} -definable frames. Moreover, we study the transfer of 𝐊 ​ 𝖻𝗂𝖦 𝖿 {\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{f}} formulas into 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 2 ± 𝖿 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}} , i.e., formulas that are 𝐊 ​ 𝖻𝗂𝖦 𝖿 {\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{f}} -valid on mono-relational frames 𝔉 \mathfrak{F} and 𝔉 ′ \mathfrak{F}^{\prime} iff they are 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 2 ± 𝖿 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}} -valid on their bi-relational counterparts. Finally, we establish 𝖯𝖲𝗉𝖺𝖼𝖾 \mathsf{PSpace} -completeness of all considered logics. Keywords: Gödel modal logic; paraconsistent modal logics; complexity; frame definability.

Introduction

When dealing with modalised propositions such as ‘I believe that Paula has a dog’, ‘I believe that Quinn thinks that Paula has a cat’, ‘Quinn has to submit the report by Friday’, etc., it is reasonable to assume that they can be equipped with a truth degree. After all, we can be more or less convinced of something, have stronger or weaker obligations, etc. On the other hand, it is not always justified that the agents can assign exact numerical values to the degrees of their beliefs or obligations (even if they can compare them). Thus, if we want to formalise these contexts, it makes sense to utilise modal expansions of Gödel logic
\mathsf{G}

\mathsf{G}

Paraconsistent modal logics

The second source of motivation is the study of
paraconsistent modal logics
. A logic is paraconsistent when its entailment does not satisfy the explosion property —
p,\neg p\models q
(or
ex contradictione quodlibet
). In modal contexts, the failure of this principle aligns very well with our intuition. Indeed, one can have contradictory beliefs but still
not believe in something else
; likewise, even if one has conflicting obligations, it does not mean that they have (or are permitted) to do
everything
.

One of the most common treatments of paraconsistent modal logics is to consider Kripke frames
with two independent valuations
as it is done in, e.g.,
[
37
,
20
,
31
,
28
,
29
]
. These valuations are interpreted as supports of truth and falsity of a formula in a given state. Historically, this idea can be traced to the Belnap–Dunn logic (
\mathsf{BD}
, alias ‘first-degree entailment’ or
\mathsf{FDE}
)
[
17
,
5
,
4
]
. Each proposition could be true, false, both true and false or neither true nor false depending on the available information.

An important consequence of having independent supports of truth and falsity is that it is no longer the case that any two propositions have
comparable values
. Indeed, it might be the case that both truth and falsity of
\phi
are supported but neither truth nor falsity of
\chi
is supported. In addition, both the support of the falsity and the support of the truth of
\phi
can be greater than those of
\chi
(or vice versa). This makes sense if we wish to model agents who cannot always compare the degrees of their beliefs in two given statements, who cannot always compare the strengths of their obligations, etc. This is justified if, e.g.,
\phi
and
\chi
do not have any content in common. In addition, in the multi-agent setting, we might not always be able to compare the strength of beliefs of different agents even in the same statement.

After introducing two valuations on a frame, it makes sense to consider frames with
two relations
designated
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
and utilised to determine supports of truth and falsity of modal formulas (cf., e.g.,
[
35
,
16
]
for examples of such logics). Intuitively, if we assume
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
to be fuzzy (i.e., if
W
is the set of states of a given frame, then
R^{+},R^{-}:W\times W\rightarrow[0,1]
) and the states in the frame to represent sources that refer to one another, they can be construed as, respectively, degrees of trust of one source in confirmations (assertions) or denials of the other source. For example, if a source is too sceptical, we might be inclined to trust its denials
less than
we trust its confirmations. On the contrary, if a sensationalist source denies something, it can be considered
more
trustworthy than when the very same source asserts something.

The idea of associating two independent relations to one modality was also utilised in the knowledge representation. E.g., in
[
25
]
, paraconsistent semantics of
\mathcal{ALC}
and its extensions are presented where each role name is associated to two relations on the carrier. This way, the authors managed to maintain paraconsistent behaviour of the knowledge bases when using role constructors (in particular, reflexive and irreflexive closures), self restrictions, and negative role assertions.

\Gamma

Convention 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
be a crisp frame and
\mathfrak{H}=\langle U,S\rangle
a fuzzy frame. We set

\displaystyle R(w)

\displaystyle=\{w^{\prime}:wRw^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle S(u)

\displaystyle=\{u^{\prime}:uSu^{\prime}>0\}

We have already discussed in the introduction that

can only define

fragments of

.

There are
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
-definable classes of fuzzy frames that are not
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
-definable

Proof.

Consider

. It is clear that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\tau

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\forall u\in\mathfrak{F}:0<\sup\{uRu^{\prime}:u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}\}<1

Denote the class of frames satisfying (

) with

. Observe now that
\mathfrak{F}\in\mathbb{S}
iff

in every

since it is always the case that

. On the other hand,

can take any value from

on a fuzzy frame. But there is no

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

is redundant in
\mathcal{H}{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}^{\mathsf{c}}

Proof.

First, observe that

-valid (and thus,

-provable). Thus, it suffices to check that

is

is provable. Thus, we need to prove

\mathcal{H}\mathsf{G}\triangle\vdash{\sim\sim\sim}\phi\leftrightarrow{\sim}\phi
and
\mathcal{H}\mathsf{G}\triangle\vdash{\sim\sim}\triangle\phi\leftrightarrow\triangle\phi
, whence, we reduce our task to the proof of

\mathcal{H}\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}\vdash{\sim}\lozenge\phi\leftrightarrow\Box{\sim}\phi

To do this, recall

, Proposition 3.1]
that

is provable in

without the use of

be a fuzzy frame. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F},x\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p\rightarrow\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\underbrace{\forall y,z:(xR^{h}y\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}xR^{j}z)\leq\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}zR^{k}w\}}_{\mathsf{FLS}}

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F},x\not\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p\rightarrow\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p
. We prove that the condition on
R
does not hold. We pick
e
on
\mathfrak{F}
s.t.
e(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p,x)>e(\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p,x)
and proceed as follows.

\displaystyle e(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p,x)>e(\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p,x)

\displaystyle\text{ then }\sup\limits_{y^{\prime}\in W}\{xR^{h}y^{\prime}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}e(\Box^{i}p,y^{\prime})\}>\inf\limits_{z^{\prime}\in W}\{xR^{j}z^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}e(\lozenge^{k}p,z^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\text{ then }\exists y^{\prime},z^{\prime}:\left[\begin{matrix}xR^{h}y^{\prime}>(xR^{j}z^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}e(\lozenge^{k}p,z^{\prime}))\\
\text{and}\\
e(\Box^{i}p,y^{\prime})>(xR^{j}z^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}e(\lozenge^{k}p,z^{\prime}))\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\text{ then }\exists y^{\prime},z^{\prime}:\left[\begin{matrix}xR^{h}y^{\prime}>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}e(p,w)\}\\
\text{and}\\
xR^{j}z^{\prime}>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}e(p,w)\}\\
\text{ and }\\
\inf\limits_{w\in W}\{y^{\prime}R^{i}w\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}e(p,w)\}>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}e(p,w)\}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\text{ then }\exists y^{\prime},z^{\prime}:\left[\begin{matrix}xR^{h}y^{\prime}>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}\right)\\
\text{and}\\
xR^{j}z^{\prime}>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}\right)\\
\text{ and }\\
\inf\limits_{y^{\prime}R^{i}w>e(p,w)}\{e(p,w)\}>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}\right)\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\text{ then }\underbrace{\exists y^{\prime},z^{\prime}:\left[\begin{matrix}xR^{h}y^{\prime}>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}\right)\\
\text{and}\\
xR^{j}z^{\prime}>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}\right)\\
\text{ and }\\
\inf\limits_{y^{\prime}R^{i}w>e(p,w)}\{e(p,w)\}>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{z^{\prime}R^{k}w\}\end{matrix}\right]}_{\Psi}

It is clear that
\mathsf{FLS}
and
\Psi
are incompatible.

For the converse, let
\langle\mathfrak{F},x\rangle
fail
\mathsf{FLS}
. Namely, let
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{F}
be s.t.
(xR^{h}y\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}xR^{j}z)>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}zR^{k}w\}
. It is clear that
(xR^{h}y\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}xR^{j}z)>\left(\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\}\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}\right)
. We set the valuation as follows:
e(p,w)=yR^{i}w
for every
w\in R^{i}(y)
and
e(p,u)=0
otherwise. We have that
e(\Box^{i}p,y)=1
and either

\displaystyle e(\lozenge^{k}p,z)=\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{e(p,w)\}=\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\}<1

\displaystyle\left(\text{if }\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\}<\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}\right)

(16)

or

\displaystyle e(\lozenge^{k}p,z)=\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}<1

\displaystyle\left(\text{if }\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\}\geq\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}\right)

(17)

If (
16
) is the case, we have that
e(\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p,x)\leq\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{i}w\}<xR^{h}y
and
e(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p,x)\geq xR^{h}y
. If (
17
) holds, then
e(\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p,x)\leq\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}
but
e(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p,x)\geq xR^{h}y>\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{zR^{k}w\}
. In both cases, we have that
e(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p\rightarrow\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p,x)<1
, as required.
∎

Let us now use Theorem
5.2
to obtain the transfer of Lemmon–Scott formulas.

Corollary 5.1
.

1.

Lemmon–Scott formulas are transferrable in mono-relational frames.

2.

Lemmon–Scott formulas are bi-transferrable.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
, it suffices to check that every Lemmon–Scott formula
\phi
is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
-valid on a mono-relational pointed frame
\langle\mathfrak{F},w\rangle
iff
{\sim}\phi^{\partial}
is valid on
\langle\mathfrak{F},w\rangle
. Now, observe that
\mathfrak{F},w\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\phi
iff
\mathfrak{F},w\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\triangle\phi
. Let
\triangle\phi=\triangle(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p\rightarrow\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p)
.

It is clear that since
\mathfrak{F}
is mono-relational,
{\sim}(\triangle(\lozenge^{h}\Box^{i}p\rightarrow\Box^{j}\lozenge^{k}p))^{\partial}={\sim\sim\sim}(\lozenge^{j}\Box^{k}p^{*}\Yleft\Box^{h}\lozenge^{i}p^{*})
. This is equivalent (in
\mathsf{biG}
) to
\triangle(\lozenge^{j}\Box^{k}p^{*}\rightarrow\Box^{h}\lozenge^{i}p^{*})
. Now, by Theorem
5.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F},x\models_{\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}}\triangle(\lozenge^{j}\Box^{k}p^{*}\rightarrow\Box^{h}\lozenge^{i}p^{*})

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\underbrace{\forall y,z:(xR^{j}y\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}xR^{h}z)\leq\sup\limits_{w\in W}\{yR^{k}w\wedge_{\mathsf{G}}zR^{i}w\}}_{\mathsf{FLS}^{\prime}}

It is clear that
\mathsf{FLS}
and
\mathsf{FLS}^{\prime}
are equivalent conditions on frames. Thus, indeed, a Lemmon–Scott formula
\phi
is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
-valid on a pointed frame iff
{\sim}\phi^{\partial}
is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
-valid on that same pointed frame. The result now follows.
∎

(

Proposition 6.3
.

The validity of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{c}}
,
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\mathsf{f}}
, and
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2\pm\mathsf{f}}

The
\mathsf{PSpace}

6.1

6.2

\phi

\{\mathbf{0},\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\Box\}

\phi^{!}
to be the result of replacing every occurrence
p

\triangle p\wedge\neg{\sim}\triangle p

\triangle

\Box

\phi^{!}

(1,0)

(0,1)

We show that
\mathbf{K}\models\phi

\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}\models\phi^{!}

\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}\not\models\phi^{!}
when

\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R^{+},R^{-},e_{1},e_{2}\rangle

\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}

or

. We check by induction that

\mathfrak{M}^{!},w\vDash\phi
. The basis case of

\mathfrak{M}^{!}
. The cases of propositional connectives can be proven directly from the induction hypothesis. Finally, if

and

or

\mathfrak{M},w^{\prime}\vDash\psi

, and thus
\mathfrak{M},w\vDash\Box\psi
.

Proposition 6.5
.

\mathfrak{F}
is finitely branching iff
\mathfrak{F}\models{\sim\sim}\Box(p\!\vee\!{\sim}p)

7
Conclusion

Let us summarise the results of the paper. We provided a strongly complete axiomatisation of the fuzzy bi-Gödel modal logic (Theorem
3.2
) and constructed the faithful embeddings of its paraconsistent relatives
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
into
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
and
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}(2)
depending on the number of relations in the frames. The embeddings hold for the valid formulas and for the valid entailments alike (Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
). Using these embeddings, we provided a characterisation of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
- and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
-definable classes of frames (Corollary
4.1
) as well as a characterisation of transferrable formulas (Theorem
5.1
). Moreover, we established that all
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s are
\mathsf{PSpace}
-complete (Propositions
6.3
and
6.4
).

Still, several questions remain open. First of all, the axiomatisations of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s. Indeed, now we can only ‘prove’ negative normal forms of
\overline{\mathcal{L}^{\neg}_{\triangle,\Box,\lozenge}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{L}^{\neg}_{\triangle,\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}}
formulas in
\mathcal{H}\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}(2)
and then use the transformations in (
14
) to obtain the intended formulas. However, since
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s do not extend
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
in the general case (
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s are non-standard, whence never extend
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
, while
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s extend
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
only for the crisp case), it would be instructive to obtain calculi designed specifically for these logics. One way to attempt this would be to construct a
bi-lateral
calculus where the notion of proof is supplemented with the dual notion of disproof. Such calculi exist for bi-Intuitionistic logic (cf., e.g.,
[
2
]
) which is the bi-Gödel logic without two prelinearity axioms
(p\rightarrow q)\vee(q\rightarrow p)
and
\mathbf{1}\Yleft((p\Yleft q)\wedge(q\Yleft p))
. Likewise, there are bi-lateral calculi for the Belnap–Dunn logic (cf., e.g.,
[
30
]
) which can also be helpful since
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s can be seen as Belnapian relatives of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{biG}
.

Second, as we have already noted, the propositional fragment of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
’s and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{\blacksquare,\blacklozenge}
’s is
\mathsf{G}^{2}
— a certain paraconsistent expansion of
\mathsf{G}
. Namely,
\mathsf{G}^{2}
is the linear extension of
\mathsf{I}_{4}\mathsf{C}_{4}

[
38
]
(cf. the axiomatisation of
\mathsf{G}^{2}
in
[
10
]
). It makes sense, then, to consider other modal expansions of paraconsistent logics and their linear extensions presented in
[
38
]
. Moreover, it makes sense to investigate the modalities whose support of falsity
dualises the support of truth
. In particular, for logics based on
\mathsf{G}^{2}
:

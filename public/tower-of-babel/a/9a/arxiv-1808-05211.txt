Construction and Stability of type I blowup solutions for non-variational semilinear parabolic systems Thanks: H. Zaag is supported by the ANR project ANAÉ ref. ANR-13-BS01-0010-03. ——————– August 8, 2026


Abstract
. We consider in this note the semilinear heat system ∂ t u = Δ ​ u + f ⁡ ( v ) , ∂ t v = μ ​ Δ ​ v + g ⁡ ( u ) , μ > 0 , \partial_{t}u=\Delta u+f(v),\quad\partial_{t}v=\mu\Delta v+g(u),\quad\mu>0, where the nonlinearity has no gradient structure taking of the particular form f ⁡ ( v ) = v ​ | v | p − 1 and g ⁡ ( u ) = u ​ | u | q − 1 with p , q > 1 , f(v)=v|v|^{p-1}\quad\textup{and}\quad g(u)=u|u|^{q-1}\quad\textup{with}\quad p,q>1, or f ⁡ ( v ) = e p ​ v and g ⁡ ( u ) = e q ​ u with p , q > 0 . f(v)=e^{pv}\quad\textup{and}\quad g(u)=e^{qu}\quad\textup{with}\quad p,q>0. We exhibit type I blowup solutions for this system and give a precise description of its blowup profiles. The method relies on two-step procedure: the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one via a spectral analysis, then solving the finite dimensional problem by a classical topological argument based on index theory. As a consequence of our technique, the constructed solutions are stable under a small perturbation of initial data. The results and the main arguments presented in this note can be found in our papers [ Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag , Ghoul et al.(2018d)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag ] .

Introduction.

In
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
,
Ghoul et al.(2018d)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
, we consider the semilinear parabolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u&=\;\;\Delta u+f(v),\\
\partial_{t}v&=\mu\Delta v+g(u),\end{array}\right.\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

with
N\geq 1
,
\mu>0
and
\big(u(0),v(0)\big)=\big(u_{0},v_{0}\big)
, where
(u,v)(t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the nonlinearity has no gradient structure taking of the particular form

f(v)=v|v|^{p-1}\quad\textup{and}\quad g(u)=u|u|^{q-1}\quad\textup{with}\quad p,q>1,

(1.2)

or

f(v)=e^{pv}\quad\textup{and}\quad g(u)=e^{qu}\quad\textup{with}\quad p,q>0.

(1.3)

System (
1.1
) represents a simple model of a reaction-diffusion system describing heat propagation in a two-component combustible mixture and, as such, it has been the subject of intensive investigation from the last two decades (see
[
Souplet(2005)
]
,
[
Zheng et al.(2002)Zheng, Zhao, and Chen
]
and references therein). We are here mainly interested in proving the existence and stability of finite time blowup solutions satisfying some prescribed asymptotic behavior. By finite time blowup, we mean that
T=T(u_{0},v_{0})
, the maximal existence time of the classical solution
(u,v)
of problem (
1.1
), is finite, and the solution blows up in finite time
T
in the sense that

\lim_{t\to T}\big(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big)=+\infty.

Moreover, a finite blowup solution
(u,v)
of system (
1.1
) is called
Type I
if there exists some positive constant
C
such that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{u}(t),\quad\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{v}(t),

(1.4)

where
(\bar{u},\bar{v})(t)
is the unique positive blowup solution of the ordinary differential system associated to (
1.1
), namely that

\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)=\Gamma(T-t)^{-\alpha},\quad\bar{v}(t)=\gamma(T-t)^{-\beta}

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{def:fg1}},

\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)=\ln\left[\big(p(T-t)\big)^{\frac{-1}{q}}\right],\quad\bar{v}(t)=\ln\left[\big(q(T-t)\big)^{\frac{-1}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{def:fg2}},

where
(\Gamma,\gamma)
is determined by

\gamma^{p}=\alpha\Gamma,\quad\Gamma^{q}=\gamma\beta,\quad\alpha=\frac{p+1}{pq-1},\quad\beta=\frac{q+1}{pq-1}.

(1.5)

Otherwise, the blowup solution is of
Type II
.

As for system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with
\mu=1
, the existence of finite time blowup solutions was derived by Friedman-Giga
[
Friedman and Giga(1987)
]
, Escobedo-Herrero
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1991a)
]
(see also
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1991b)
]
,
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1993)
]
, etc). From Andreucci-Herrero-Velázquez
[
Andreucci et al.(1997)Andreucci, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
, we know that estimate (
1.4
) holds true if

pq>1,\quad q(pN-2)_{+}<N+2\quad\textup{or}\quad p(qN-2)_{+}<N+2.

See also Caristi-Mitidieri
[
Caristi and Mitidieri(1994)
]
, Deng
[
Deng(1996)
]
, Fila-Souple
[
Fila and Souplet(2001)
]
for more results relative to estimate (
1.4
). Knowing that the solution exhibits Type I blowup, the authors of
[
Andreucci et al.(1997)Andreucci, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
were able to obtain more information about the asymptotic behavior of the solution near the singularity. Their results were later improved by Zaag
[
Zaag(2001)
]
. When
\mu\neq 1
, much less result has been known, a part from Mahmoudi-Souplet-Tayachi
[
Mahmoudi et al.(2015)Mahmoudi, Souplet, and Tayachi
]
who establishes a single point blowup result that improves the one obtained in
[
Friedman and Giga(1987)
]
. As for system (
1.1
) coupled with the nonlinearity (
1.3
), the only known result is due to Souplet-Tayachi
[
Souplet and Tayachi(2016)
]
who adapted the technique developed in
[
Mahmoudi et al.(2015)Mahmoudi, Souplet, and Tayachi
]
to obtain the single point blowup result for a class of radially descreasing solutions. To our knowledge, there are no results concerning the asymptotic behavior, even for the equidiffusive case, i.e.
\mu=1
. Also we recall that the study of the non-equidiffusive parabolic system (
1.1
) (
\mu
may or may not equal to
1
) are in particular much more involved, both in terms of behavior of solutions and at the technical level.

In this note we exhibit
Type I
blowup solutions for system (
1.1
) and give the first complete description of its asymptotic behavior. More precisely, we prove in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Type I blowup solutions for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and its asymptotic behavior,
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
T>0
. There exist initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for which system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has the unique solution
(u,v)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
such that

(i)

The solution
(u,v)
blows up in finite time
T
at the only point
a
.

(ii)

(Asymptotic profile)
There holds for all
t\in[0,T)
,

\left\|(T-t)^{\alpha}u(x,t)-\Phi^{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left\|(T-t)^{\beta}v(x,t)-\Psi^{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{(T-t)}},

(1.6)

where
z=\frac{x-a}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}
and the profiles
\Phi_{0}
and
\Psi_{0}
are explicitly given by

\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\Phi^{*}(z)=\Gamma\big(1+b|z|^{2}\big)^{-\alpha},\quad\Psi^{*}(z)=\gamma\big(1+b|z|^{2}\big)^{-\beta},

(1.7)

with
\Gamma,\gamma,\alpha,\beta
being introduced in (
1.5
) and

b=b(\mu,p,q)=\frac{(pq-1)(2pq+p+q)}{4pq(p+1)(q+1)(\mu+1)}>0.

(1.8)

(iii)

(Final blowup profile)
For all
x\neq a
,
\big(u(x,t),v(x,t)\big)\to\big(u^{*}(x),v^{*}(x)\big)\in\Big[\mathcal{C}^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\big)\Big]^{2}
with

u^{*}(x)\sim\Gamma\left(\frac{b|x-a|^{2}}{2\big|\log|x-a|\big|}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\quad v^{*}(x)\sim\gamma\left(\frac{b|x-a|^{2}}{2\big|\log|x-a|\big|}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}

(1.9)

as
|x-a|\to 0
.

The estimate (

T

a
.

(ii)

(Asymptotic profile)

\left\|(T-t)e^{qu(x,t)}-\Phi_{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left\|(T-t)e^{pv(x,t)}-\Psi_{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{(T-t)}},

•

Reduction of an infinite dimensional problem to a finite dimensional one, through either the spectral analysis of the linearized operator around the expected profile or the energy-type estimate via the derivation of suitable Lyapunov functional. Note that the energy-type method breaks down for our problem because of the non gradient structure of the nonlinearity.

•

The control of the finite dimensional problem thanks to a classical topological argument based on index theory.

As a consequence of our technique, we obtained the following stability result.

Theorem 1.6
.

The constructed solutions described in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
are stable with respect to initial data.

Remark 1.7
.

The idea behind the stability result can be formally understood from the space-time and scaling invariance of the problem as follows: The linearized operator around the expected profile has two positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}=1,\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, a zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=0
, then a an infinity discrete negative spectrum. From the analysis of stability of blowup problems, the component corresponding to
\lambda_{0}=1
has the exponential growth
e^{s}
, which can be eliminated by a changing of the blowup time; similarly for the mode
\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
by a shifting of the blowup point; the neutral mode
\lambda_{2}=0
usually has a polynomial growth and can be eliminated by using the scaling invariance of the problem. Since the remaining modes of the linearized operator corresponding to the negative spectrum decay exponentially, one derive the stability of the constructed solutions described in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
. From the stability result, we expect that the blowup profiles (
1.7
) and (
1.11
) are generic, i.e. the other blowup profiles are unstable. In our opinion, this is a difficult open question whose a partly particular answer was given by Herrero-Velázquez
[
Herrero and Velázquez(1992)
]
for the one dimensional semilinear heat equation.

2.
A formal computation of the blowup profile.

We brieftly recall in this section the formal approaches in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
,
Ghoul et al.(2018d)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
to construct a suitable approximate blowup profile for system (
1.1
). Similar approaches can be found in
[
Tayachi and Zaag(2015)
,
Tayachi and Zaag(2016)
]
,
[
Ghoul et al.(2017)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
,
[
Nouaili and Zaag(2018)
]
and references therein. The method is based on matched asymptotic expansions which mainly replies on the spectral properties of the linearized operator around an expected profile.

Similarity variables:

We perform the well known change of variables

\displaystyle\Phi(y,s)=(T-t)^{\alpha}u(x,t),\quad\Psi(y,s)=(T-t)^{\beta}v(x,t)

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for}\;\eqref{def:fg1},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(y,s)=(T-t)e^{qu(x,t)},\quad\Psi(y,s)=(T-t)e^{pv(x,t)}

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for}\;\eqref{def:fg2},

(2.2)

where
\alpha,\beta
are introduced in (
1.5
) and

y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t).

In this way,
(\Phi,\Psi)
satisfies the new system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{s}\Phi&=\mathcal{L}_{1}\Phi-\alpha\Phi+|\Psi|^{p-1}\Psi,\\
\partial_{s}\Psi&=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\Psi-\beta\Psi+|\Phi|^{p-1}\Phi,\end{array}\right.

for (
1.2
),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{s}\Phi=\mathcal{L}_{1}\Phi-\Phi-\dfrac{|\nabla\Phi|^{2}}{\Phi}+q\Phi\Psi,\\
\partial_{s}\Psi=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\Psi-\Psi-\mu\dfrac{|\nabla\Psi|^{2}}{\Psi}+p\Phi\Psi,\end{array}\right.

for (
1.3
),

where

\mathcal{L}_{\eta}f=\eta\Delta f-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla f=\frac{\eta}{\rho_{\eta}}\nabla\cdot\big(\rho_{\eta}\nabla f\big)\quad\textup{with}\quad\eta\in\{1,\mu\},

(2.9)

is the self-adjoint operator with respect to the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
equipped with the inner product

\big<f,g\big>_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)g(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy\quad\textup{with}\quad\rho_{\eta}(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\eta}}.

Linearized problem and spectral properties of the associated linearized operator:

Note that the nonzero constant solutions to systems (
2
) and (
2
) are
(\Gamma,\gamma)
and
(1/p,1/q)
respectively. This suggests the linearization

\big(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Psi}\big)=\big(\Phi-\Gamma,\Psi-\gamma\big)\;\;\textup{for \eqref{def:fg1}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\big(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Psi}\big)=\big(\Phi-1/p,\Psi-1/q\big)\;\;\textup{for \eqref{def:fg2}},

(2.10)

where
(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Psi})
solves the system

i=1,2,\quad\partial_{s}\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}=\left(\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}\right)\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}+\binom{Q_{i,1}}{Q_{i,2}},

(2.11)

where
i=1
stands for the polynomial nonlinearity (
1.2
) and
i=2
for the exponential case (
1.3
),
Q_{i,1}
and
Q_{i,2}
are built to be quadratic, and the linear operator
\mathcal{H}
and matrices
Mc_{i}
’s are defined by

\mathcal{H}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}_{1}&0\\
0&\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\end{matrix}\right),\quad Mc_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha&\;p\gamma^{p-1}\\
q\Gamma^{q-1}&\;-\beta\end{pmatrix},\quad Mc_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&q/p\\
p/q&0\end{matrix}\right).

(2.12)

The following lemma gives the spectral properties of
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1

(Diagonalization of
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}
)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist polynomials
f_{n},g_{n},\tilde{f}_{n}
and
\tilde{g}_{n}
of degree
n
such that

\Big(\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}\Big)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}=\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\quad\Big(\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}\Big)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}=\lambda^{-}_{i,n}\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}},

(2.13)

where

\lambda^{-}_{1,n}=-\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{(p+1)(q+1)}{pq-1}\right),\quad\lambda^{-}_{2,n}=-\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right).

Proof.

See Lemma 3.2 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
for the polynomial case (
1.2
) and Lemma 2.2 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018d)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
for the exponential case (
1.3
). The reader is kindly invited to have a look at precise formulas of the eigenfunctions as well as a proper definition of the projection according to these eigenmodes in those papers. ∎

Inner expansion:

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}_{n\geq 3}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}_{n\geq 0}
correspond to negative eigenvalues of
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{i}
, therefore, we may consider the following formal expansion under the radially symmetric assumption of the solution,

\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}(y,s)=a_{0}(s)\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}(y)+a_{2}(s)\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}},

(2.14)

where
|a_{0}(s)|+|a_{2}(s)|\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
. Plugging this ansatz into (
2.11
) and projecting onto
\binom{f_{k}}{g_{k}},k=0,2
yields the ordinary differential system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{0}^{\prime}&=a_{0}+\mathcal{O}(|a_{0}|^{2}+|a_{2}|^{2}),\\
a_{2}^{\prime}&=c_{*}a_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|a_{2}|^{3}+|a_{0}a_{2}|+|a_{0}|^{3}),\end{array}\right.

(2.15)

where

c_{*}=\frac{2pq+p+q}{4pq(p+1)(q+1)(\mu+1)}\;\;\textup{for \eqref{def:fg1}}\quad\textup{and}\quad c_{*}=2pq(\mu+1)\;\;\textup{for \eqref{def:fg2}}.

Assume that
|a_{0}(s)|=o(|a_{2}(s)|)
as
s\to+\infty
, we get

a_{2}(s)=-\frac{1}{c_{*}s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\textup{and}\quad|a_{0}(s)|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\textup{as}\;\;s\to+\infty.

From (
2.14
), (
2.10
) and the definition of the eigenfuntion
\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}
, we end up with the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&=\Gamma\left[1-\frac{p+1}{c_{*}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}-\frac{2p(1-\mu)}{c_{*}s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right),\\
\Psi(y,s)&=\gamma\left[1-\frac{q+1}{c_{*}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}-\frac{2q(\mu-1)}{c_{*}s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right),\end{array}\right.

for (
2
),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi(y,s)=\frac{1}{p}\left[1-\frac{pq}{c_{*}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}+\frac{2\mu pq}{c_{*}s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right),\\
\Psi(y,s)=\frac{1}{q}\left[1-\frac{pq}{c_{*}}\frac{|y^{2}|}{s}+\frac{2pq}{c_{*}s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right),\end{array}\right.

for (
2
),

where the convergence takes place in
L_{\rho_{1}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as well as uniformly on compact sets by standard parabolic regularity.

Outer expansion:

These above asymptotic expansions provide a relevant blowup variable

z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}.

We then try to search an approximate solution to (
2
) (respectively (
2
)) of the form

\binom{\Phi}{\Psi}(y,s)=\binom{\Phi_{0}}{\Psi_{0}}(z)+\frac{1}{s}\binom{\Phi_{1}}{\Phi_{1}}(z)+\cdots,

(2.22)

Plugging this anzats to (
2
) (respectively (
2

\displaystyle-\frac{z}{2}\Phi_{0}^{\prime}-\alpha\Phi_{0}+\Phi_{0}^{p}=0,\quad-\frac{z}{2}\Psi_{0}^{\prime}-\beta\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{0}^{q}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{sys:PhiPsi1}},

(2.23)

\displaystyle-\frac{z}{2}\Phi_{0}^{\prime}-\Phi_{0}+q\Phi_{0}\Psi_{0}=0,\quad-\frac{z}{2}\Psi_{0}^{\prime}-\Psi_{0}+p\Phi_{0}\Psi_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{sys:PhiPsi2}},

(2.24)

subject to the initial condition

\big(\Phi_{0},\Psi_{0}\big)(0)=\big(\Gamma,\gamma\big)\;\;\textup{for \eqref{asy:PhiPsi1}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\big(\Phi_{0},\Psi_{0}\big)(0)=\big(1/p,1/q\big)\;\;\textup{for \eqref{asy:PhiPsi2}}.

The solutions of these system are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(z)=\frac{\Gamma}{(1+b|z|^{2})^{\alpha}},\quad\Psi_{0}(z)=\frac{\gamma}{(1+b|z|^{2})^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{sys:ode1}},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{p(1+b|z|^{2})},\quad\Psi_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{q(1+b|z|^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\textup{for \eqref{sys:ode2}},

(2.26)

where
b>0
is an integration constant. By matching the asymptotic expansions (

) with (
2
) and (
2.26
) with (
2
), we obtain precisely the value of the constant

as stated in Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
respectively.

In conclusion, we have formally derived the following approximate blowup profile:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&\sim\varphi(y,s)=\Phi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)-\frac{2\Gamma p(1-\mu)}{c_{*}s},\\
\Psi(y,s)&\sim\psi(y,s)=\Psi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)-\frac{2\gamma q(\mu-1)}{c_{*}s},\end{array}\right.

for (
2
),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)=&\sim\varphi(y,s)=\Phi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{2\mu q}{c_{*}s},\\
\Psi(y,s)=&\sim\psi(y,s)=\Psi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{2p}{c_{*}s},\end{array}\right.

for (

3.1.
The polynomial case (
1.2
).

This subsection is devoted to the proof of part
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
. Parts
(i)
and
(iii)
are consequences of part
(ii)
. The reader can find all details of the proof in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018c)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag

Formulation of the problem:

In view of the similarity variables (
2.1
), we see that constructing blowup solutions for (
1.1
) coupled with (
1.2
) satisfying the asymptotic behavior (
1.6
) is equivalent to constructing for (
2
) a global in time solution
(\Phi,\Psi)
such that

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\big|\Phi(y,s)-\Phi^{*}(y/\sqrt{s})\big|+\big|\Psi(y,s)-\Psi^{*}(y/\sqrt{s})\big|\Big)\to 0\quad\textup{as}\;\;s\to+\infty,

(3.1)

where
\Phi^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
are the profiles defined in Theorem
1.1
. From the formal computation of an approximate blowup profile presented in the previous section, we linearize (
2
) around
(\varphi,\psi)
defined in (
2
) instead of
(\Phi^{*},\Psi^{*})
, namely that we introduce

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\binom{\Phi}{\Psi}-\binom{\varphi}{\psi},

(3.2)

which leads the linearized system

\partial_{s}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\Big(\mathcal{H}+Mc_{1}+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}+\binom{F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)}{F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)}+\binom{R_{1}(y,s)}{R_{2}(y,s)},

(3.3)

where
\mathcal{H}
and
Mc_{1}
are defined in (
2.12
),

V(y,s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&p\big(\psi^{p-1}-\gamma^{p-1}\big)\\
q\big(\varphi^{q-1}-\Gamma^{q-1}\big)&0\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&V_{1}\\
V_{2}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3.4)

\binom{F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)}{F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)}=\binom{|\Upsilon+\psi|^{p-1}(\Upsilon+\psi)-\psi^{p}-p\psi^{p-1}\Upsilon}{|\Lambda+\varphi|^{q-1}(\Lambda+\varphi)-\varphi^{q}-q\varphi^{q-1}\Lambda},

(3.5)

and

\binom{R_{1}(y,s)}{R_{2}(y,s)}=\binom{-\partial_{s}\varphi+\Delta\varphi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\varphi-\left(\frac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\varphi+\psi^{p}}{-\partial_{s}\psi+\mu\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi-\left(\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\psi+\varphi^{q}}.

(3.6)

Our aim turns to construct for system (
3.3
) a global in time solution
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
verifying

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\big|\Lambda(y,s)\big|+\big|\Upsilon(y,s)\big|\Big)\to 0\quad\textup{as}\;\;s\to+\infty.

(3.7)

Since the solution
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
goes to zero as
s\to+\infty
and the nonlinear term
(F_{1},F_{2})
is built to be quadratic and the error term
(R_{1},R_{2})
is of the size
s^{-1}
, we see that the dynamics of (
3.3
) are strongly influenced by the linear part
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{1}+V
. Here the potential
V
behaves differently as follows:

- Outer region, i.e.
|y|\gtrsim\sqrt{s}
: for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K_{\epsilon}>0
and
s_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\sup_{|y|\geq K_{\epsilon}\sqrt{s},s\geq s_{\epsilon}}|V(y,s)|\leq\epsilon.

From Lemma
2.1
, we see that the linear operator
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{1}+V
behaves as one with fully negative spectrum in the outer region, which makes analysis in this region simpler.

- Inner region, i.e.
|y|\lesssim\sqrt{s}
: the potential
V
is considered as a perturbation of the linear part
\mathcal{H}+Mc_{1}
.

Since the behavior of
V
in the inner and outer regions is different, this suggests to consider the dynamics of (
3.3
) for
|y|\lesssim\sqrt{s}
and
|y|\gtrsim\sqrt{s}
separately. To this end, we introduce the cut-off function

\chi(y,s)=\chi_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{K\sqrt{s}}\right),\quad\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},[0,1]),\quad\chi_{0}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textup{for}\;\;r\in[0,1],\\
0&\textup{for}\;\;r\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

where
K
is a positive constant to be fixed large enough. We then define

\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}=\big(1-\chi(y,s)\big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}

(3.9)

and consider the decomposition

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}(y,s)=\sum_{n\leq M}\left[\theta_{n}(s)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}+\tilde{\theta}_{n}\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right]+\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}(y,s),

(3.10)

where
\theta_{n}=\Pi_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}=\tilde{\Pi}_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
with
\Pi_{n}
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}
being the projections onto the modes
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
respectively, and
\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}=\Pi_{-,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
is called the infinite-dimensional part with
\Pi_{-,M}
being the projector on the eigen-subspace corresponding the spectrum of
\mathcal{H}
lower than
\frac{1-M}{2}
. Note that the decomposition
3.10
is unique.

Preparation of initial data and Definition of the shrinking set:

Given
A>1
and
s_{0}\geq e
, we consider the initial data for system (

) of the form

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}_{A,s_{0},d_{0},d_{1}}(y)=\frac{A}{s_{0}^{2}}\left[d_{0}\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}+d_{1}\cdot\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}}\right]\chi(y,s_{0}),

(3.11)

where

and

are parameters of the problem. Our aim is to show that for a fixed large constant

, then
s_{0}=s_{0}(A)
is fixed large as well, there exist
(d_{0},d_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+N}
so that system (
3.3
) with initial data at

given by (
3.11
) has the unique solution
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
satisfies (

). More precisely, we will show that the solution

belongs to the following shrinking set:

Definition 3.1

(Definition of a shrinking set)
.

For all
A\geq 1
and
s\geq e
, we defined
\mathcal{V}_{A}(s)
as the set of all
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

|\theta_{0}(s)|\leq\frac{A}{s^{2}},\quad|\theta_{1}(s)|\leq\frac{A}{s^{2}},\quad|\theta_{2}(s)|\leq\frac{A^{4}\log s}{s^{2}},

\left\|\frac{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{1+|y|^{M+1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{A^{M+1}}{s^{\frac{M+2}{2}}},\quad\left\|\frac{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}{1+|y|^{M+1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{A^{M+1}}{s^{\frac{M+2}{2}}},

where
\Lambda_{e},\Upsilon_{e}
are defined by (
3.9
),
\Lambda_{-},\Upsilon_{-}
,
\theta_{n}
,
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
are defined as in decomposition (

).

Remark 3.2
.

We can check that if

\|\Lambda(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\Upsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{CA^{M+2}}{\sqrt{s}},

for some positive constant

In the following we make sure that the initial data (

Proposition 3.3

(Properties of initial data (

For each
A\gg 1

s_{0}(A)\gg 1
and a cuboid

\mathcal{V}_{A}(s_{0})

Existence of a solution to (

From Remark

Proposition 3.4

(Existence of a solution of (

There exists

such that for all

Proof.

For a fixed constant

and

)-(

) in

Proposition 3.5

\displaystyle\Theta:\mathcal{D}_{s_{0}}

Equivalence of the final blowup profile:

We present the main argument for the proof of part
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1
. For each

with

, we introduce for all

where

is uniquely determined by

From the invariance of system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) under the scaling,
(g(x_{0},\xi,\tau),h(x_{0},\xi,\tau))
also satisfies (

)-(
1.2
). From (

), (

) and the asymptotic behavior (

), we have

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 2|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4}}\left|g(x_{0},\xi,0)-\Phi^{*}(K)\right|\leq\frac{C}{|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4}}\to 0,

and

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 2|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4}}\left|h(x_{0},\xi,0)-\Psi^{*}(K)\right|\leq\frac{C}{|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4}}\to 0,

as
|x_{0}|\to 0
. From the continuity with respect to initial data for system (

)-(
1.2
) associated to a space-localization in the ball

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 2|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4},0\leq\tau<1}\left|g(x_{0},\xi,0)-\hat{g}_{K}(\tau)\right|\leq\epsilon(x_{0})\to 0,

and

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 2|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4},0\leq\tau<1}\left|h(x_{0},\xi,0)-\hat{h}_{K}(\tau)\right|\leq\epsilon(x_{0})\to 0,

as

, where

\hat{g}_{K}(\tau)=\Gamma(1-\tau+bK^{2})^{-\alpha},\quad\hat{h}_{K}(\tau)=\gamma(1-\tau+bK^{2})^{-\beta},

)-(
1.2
) with constant initial data

.

Making
\tau\to 1
and using (

) yields

\displaystyle u^{*}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to T}u(x,t)=(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\alpha}\lim_{\tau\to 1}g(x_{0},0,\tau)\sim(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\alpha}\hat{g}_{K}(1),

\displaystyle v^{*}(x_{0})

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\;\Big|\;|x|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|(T-t)}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv\left\{x\;\big|\;|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}\right\}\equiv\left\{x\;\Big|\;|z|\leq K_{0}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\;\Big|\;\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|(T-t)}\leq|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}\right\}

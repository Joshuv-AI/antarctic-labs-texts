Abstract
. In this note we prove that j ! ​ 2 N ​ ( N + j − 1 j ) ​ F 1 2 ​ ( − j , − 2 ​ j − N − j + 1 , − 1 ) = ∑ l = 0 N ( N l ) ​ ∏ i = 0 j − 1 2 ​ ( 2 ​ i + 1 + l ) , j!\,2^{N}\,\binom{N+j-1}{j}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-j,-2j\\ -N-j+1\end{matrix};-1\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{N}\binom{N}{l}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}2(2i+1+l), where N N and j j are positive integers, which resolves a question posed by Ercolani, Lega, and Tippings.

05C30, 33C05

In
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, Ercolani, Lega, and Tippings have shown that the number of
2\nu
-valent maps with
j
vertices and two legs that can be embedded in a surface of genus
g\geq 1
is given by

j!\left[2\nu(\nu-1)\binom{2\nu-1}{\nu-1}\right]^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{3g-1}a_{l}(g,\nu)\binom{2g-2+l+j}{j}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-j,-\nu j\\
2-2g-l-j\end{matrix}\,;\frac{1}{1-\nu}\right)

for some coefficients
a_{l}(g,\nu)
. Based on numerical computations, they subsequently conjectured, see
[
2
, Conjecture 4.2]
or
[
1
, Conjecture 5.1]
, that when
\nu=2
the summands in the above expression can be stated without hypergeometric functions with the help of identity (
1
) further below. In this note we provide a proof of this fact.

Theorem 1
.

Let
N,j
be positive integers. Then

(1)

j!2^{N}\binom{N+j-1}{j}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-j,-2j\\
-N-j+1\end{matrix};-1\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{N}\binom{N}{l}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}2(2i+1+l).

We use the following notation for the falling and rising factorials:

(a)^{\underline{0}}=(a)^{\overline{0}}:=1,\hskip 10.00002pt(a)^{\underline{n}}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-n+1),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt(a)^{\overline{n}}:=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)

for
n\geq 1
. We prove (
1
) in three steps formulated as separate lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

The right-hand side of (
1
) is equal to
2^{N}R_{j}(N)
, where
R_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}R(i,j)(x)^{\underline{i}}
and
R(i,j)
are numbers uniquely determined by the relations

R(0,j)=2^{j}(2j-1)!!,\hskip 10.00002ptR(j,j)=1,\hskip 10.00002ptR(i,j+1)=2(2j+i+1)R(i,j)+R(i-1,j),

with the recurrence relation holding for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{i,j}
be the following coefficients:

\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(2i+1+x)=\sum_{k=0}^{j}C_{k,j}x^{k}.

Since

\prod_{i=0}^{j}(2i+1+x)=(2j+1+x)\sum_{k=0}^{j}C_{k,j}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{j}(2j+1)C_{k,j}x^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{j+1}C_{k-1,j}x^{k},

it holds that

C_{0,j}=(2j-1)!!,\hskip 10.00002ptC_{j,j}=1,\hskip 10.00002ptC_{k,j+1}=(2j+1)C_{k,j}+C_{k-1,j},\;\;k\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\}.

Exactly as in the case of the coefficients
R(i,j)
, the coefficients
C_{k,j}
are uniquely defined by the above relations since the knowledge of all the coefficients on the level
j
allows one to compute all the coefficients on the level
j+1
with the base case
C_{0,1}=C_{1,1}=1
. Recall
[
3
, Equation (26.8.10)]
that

x^{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}(x)^{\underline{i}},

where
\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}
are Stirling numbers of the second kind. Therefore,

\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(2i+1+x)=C_{0,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\sum_{k=i}^{j}C_{k,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}\right)(x)^{\underline{i}}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{N}\binom{N}{l}(l)^{\underline{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N}\frac{N!}{l!(N-l)!}(l)(l-1)\cdots(l-i+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=i}^{N}\frac{N!}{(l-i)!(N-l)!}=\sum_{l=0}^{N-i}\frac{N!}{l!(N-i-l)!}=2^{N-i}(N)^{\underline{i}}.

Therefore, the right-hand side of (
1
) is equal to

2^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\binom{N}{l}\left(C_{0,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\sum_{k=i}^{j}C_{k,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}\right)(l)^{\underline{i}}\right)=2^{N}R_{j}(N),

where
R_{j}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{j}R(i,j)(x)^{\underline{i}}
with

R(0,j):=2^{j}(2j-1)!!\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptR(i,j):=2^{j-i}\sum_{k=i}^{j}C_{k,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix},\;\;i\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\}.

Since
\begin{Bmatrix}j\\
j\end{Bmatrix}=1
, see
[
3
, Equation (26.8.4)]
, it indeed holds that
R(j,j)=1
. Thus, we only need to establish the recurrence relation. The recurrence relation for
C_{k,j}
yields that

\displaystyle R(i,j+1)

\displaystyle=2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i}^{j+1}C_{k,j+1}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}=2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i}^{j}C_{k,j+1}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}+2^{j+1-i}\begin{Bmatrix}j+1\\
i\end{Bmatrix}

\displaystyle=2(2j+1)R(i,j)+2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i}^{j+1}C_{k-1,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}

for any
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\}
, where we also used the fact that
C_{j,j}=1
. Furthermore, we get from
[
3
, Equation (26.8.22)]
that

\displaystyle 2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i}^{j+1}C_{k-1,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}

\displaystyle=2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}C_{k,j}\begin{Bmatrix}k+1\\
i\end{Bmatrix}=2^{j+1-i}\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}C_{k,j}\left(i\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i\end{Bmatrix}+\begin{Bmatrix}k\\
i-1\end{Bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=2iR(i,j)+R(i-1,j),

where, by convention,
\begin{Bmatrix}i-1\\
i\end{Bmatrix}=0
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The left-hand side of (
1
) is equal to
2^{N}L_{j}(N)
, where
L_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}L(i,j)(x)^{\underline{i}}
with

L(0,j)=\frac{(2j)!}{j!}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptL(i,j)=\frac{j!}{i!}\sum_{k=i}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-1}{i-1},\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
3
, Equation (15.2.1)]
that the left-hand side of (
1
) is equal to
2^{N}L_{j}(N)
, where

L_{j}(x):=(x)^{\overline{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{(-j)^{\overline{k}}(-2j)^{\overline{k}}}{(-x-j+1)^{\overline{k}}}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}

(we take this formula as the definition of
L_{j}(x)
and show that this polynomial also admits an expression as in the statement of the lemma). Since

(-a)^{\overline{k}}=(-1)^{k}(a)^{\underline{k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{(a)^{\overline{j}}}{(a+j-1)^{\underline{k}}}=(a)^{\overline{j-k}},

we have that

\displaystyle L_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j}(j)^{\underline{k}}(2j)^{\underline{k}}\frac{(x)^{\overline{j-k}}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{j!}{(j-k)!}\frac{(2j)!}{(2j-k)!}\frac{(x)^{\overline{j-k}}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{(2j)!}{(2j-k)!}(x)^{\overline{j-k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{(2j)!}{(j+k)!}(x)^{\overline{k}},

where we replaced
j-k
by
k
to get the last equality. It is known
[
4
]
that

(x)^{\overline{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\frac{k!}{i!}(x)^{\underline{i}},\hskip 10.00002ptk\geq 1.

Hence,

\displaystyle L_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2j)!}{j!}+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{(2j)!}{(j+k)!}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\frac{k!}{i!}(x)^{\underline{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2j)!}{j!}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\sum_{k=i}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{(2j)!}{(j+k)!}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\frac{k!}{i!}\right)(x)^{\underline{i}},

which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4
.

It holds that
L(i,j)=R(i,j)
for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,j\}
and
j\geq 1
. In particular, (
1
) is true.

Proof.

Clearly,
L(0,j)=R(0,j)
and
L(j,j)=R(j,j)=1
. Thus, we only need to show that

L(i,j+1)=2(2j+i+1)L(i,j)+L(i-1,j),\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,2,\ldots,j\},

since this recurrence relation and the marginals
L(0,j)
,
L(j,j)
uniquely determine the whole table
L(i,j)
. In what follows, we agree that binomial coefficients with out-of-range indices are set to zero. In what follows we repeatedly use the elementary identity

\binom{n+1}{l}=\binom{n}{l}+\binom{n}{l-1}.

Using this identity twice and our convention concerning binomial coefficients with out-of-range indices, we get that

\displaystyle L(i,j+1)

\displaystyle=2(j+1)L(i,j)+\frac{(j+1)!}{i!}\sum_{k=i}^{j+1}\left[\binom{2j}{j+k+1}+\binom{2j}{j+k-1}\right]\binom{k-1}{i-1}

\displaystyle=2(j+1)L(i,j)+\frac{(j+1)!}{i!}\left[\sum_{k=i+1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-2}{i-1}+\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k}{i-1}\right],

where the second row is obtained simply by a change of summation indices. Therefore, when
i=1
, we get that

\displaystyle L(1,j+1)

\displaystyle=4(j+1)L(1,j)+(j+1)!\left[\binom{2j}{j}-\binom{2j}{j+1}\right]

\displaystyle=4(j+1)L(1,j)+L(0,j)\big[j+1-j\big],

which establishes the desired recurrence relation for
i=1
. On the other hand, when
i>1
, the above sum in square brackets can further be rewritten with the help of the elementary identity as

\sum_{k=i+1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\left[\binom{k-1}{i-1}-\binom{k-2}{i-2}\right]+\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\left[\binom{k-1}{i-1}+\binom{k-1}{i-2}\right]=\\
2\sum_{k=i}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}+\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\left[\binom{k-1}{i-2}-\binom{k-2}{i-2}\right],

where the last sum simply reduced to
\binom{2j}{j+1}
when
i=2
. Thus, using the elementary identity once more, we get that

L(i,j+1)=4(j+1)L(i,j)+\frac{(j+1)!}{i!}\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-2}{i-3},

which holds even for
i=2
if we understand that
\binom{-1}{-1}=1
and
\binom{k-2}{-1}=0
,
k\geq 2
. That is, to prove the lemma we need to show that

2(i-1)L(i,j)+L(i-1,j)=\frac{(j+1)!}{i!}\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-2}{i-3},

or equivalently that

\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\left[2(i-1)\binom{k-1}{i-1}+i\binom{k-1}{i-2}-(j+1)\binom{k-2}{i-3}\right]=0.

When
i=2
, the left-hand side above becomes

\sum_{k=2}^{j}2k\binom{2j}{j+k}-(j-1)\binom{2j}{j+1}=\sum_{k=2}^{j}(j+k)\binom{2j}{j+k}-\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}(j-k)\binom{2j}{j+k}.

Similarly, when
i>2
, the left-hand side in question is equal to

\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\frac{(k-2)!}{(k-i+1)!(i-2)!}\big[2(k-1)(k-i+1)+i(k-1)-(j+1)(i-2)\big]=\\
\sum_{k=i-1}^{j}\binom{2j}{j+k}\frac{(k-2)!}{(k-i+1)!(i-2)!}\big[(k-i+1)(j+k)-(j-k)(k-1)\big],

which is the same as

\sum_{k=i}^{j}(j+k)\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-2}{i-2}-\sum_{k=i-1}^{j-1}(j-k)\binom{2j}{j+k}\binom{k-1}{i-2}

(as shown just before, this formula is valid for
i=2
as well). This difference is indeed equal to zero as the second sum above can be rewritten as

\sum_{k=i-1}^{j-1}(j+k+1)\binom{2j}{j+k+1}\binom{k-1}{i-2}=\sum_{l=i}^{j}(j+l)\binom{2j}{j+l}\binom{l-2}{i-2}.\qed

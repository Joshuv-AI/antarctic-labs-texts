Higgs field as a source of tachyons
Jerzy Paczos, Szymon Cedrowski, Krzysztof Turzyński, Andrzej Dragan

Abstract
We investigate the Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism of spontaneous symmetry breaking and show that before the symmetry was broken, the interaction of Higgs fields with massless gauge fields inevitably lead to the production of tachyons.



The celebrated Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism
Anderson 1963
;
Higgs 1964
;
Englert and Brout 1964
;
Guralnik et al. 1964
is crucial in explaining the non-zero mass of gauge bosons and fermions in the Standard Model. It involves a scalar field with a symmetric “Mexican hat” potential capable of spontaneous symmetry breaking
Srednicki 2007
. The gauge
bosons and fermions acquire
masses through their interactions with the scalar field
stabilized
at a local minimum of the potential. Only after this stabilization takes place the theory is quantized and excitations of the Higgs field around the minimum manifest themselves as Higgs particles.

In the ususally adopted picture,

Larkoski 2019
, the scalar field falls to a local minimum by itself due to the instability of the classical field configuration at the local maximum, where even a small disturbance will cause the field to fall in one direction leading to symmetry breaking.
Such an explanation, however, lacks the quantum description and disregards the tachyonic nature of the scalar field around the local maximum.

It was argued by Bilaniuk, Deshpande, and Sudarshan
Bilaniuk et al. 1962
and proven later in
Dragan and Ekert 2020
;
Dragan et al. 2023
that the existence of tachyons does not contradict special relativity.
A proposal for a
covariant quantum theory of tachyons overcoming the well-known obstacles
Arons and Sudarshan 1968
;
Dhar and Sudarshan 1968
;
Schwartz 2018
was
put forth
only recently
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
, thus allowing for a covariant quantum description of the scalar field with the negative square mass.

Based on these results, in this work, we revisit the process of spontaneous symmetry breaking and address the question of the evolution of the scalar field during the initial symmetric phase just before the symmetry is spontaneously broken.
Around the local maximum, the
potential can be approximated by an inverted quadratic potential corresponding to a tachyonic field
Feinberg 1967
;
Arons and Sudarshan 1968
;
Dhar and Sudarshan 1968
;
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
. We argue that because of the stability of the tachyonic vacuum
Arons and Sudarshan 1968
;
Dhar and Sudarshan 1968
;
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
, the symmetry-breaking process is possible only due to interactions with other fields. We propose a simple model of the symmetry breaking induced by the Yukawa interaction of the scalar field with massless particles. We show that such coupling leads to spontaneous emission of tachyons by massless particles, thereby destabilizing the quantum tachyonic vacuum.

Let us consider the simplest model of spontaneous symmetry breaking with the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\mu}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}-\frac{1}{24}\lambda\varphi^{4}

(1)

describing the dynamics of a real scalar field
\varphi
. The unusual feature of this Lagrangian, distinguishing it from the Lagrangian of the ordinary scalar field, is the plus sign in front of the mass term. This makes the scalar potential
V(\varphi)=-m^{2}\varphi^{2}/2+\lambda\varphi^{4}/24

have a local maximum at
\varphi=0
, and two local minima at
\varphi=\pm\sqrt{6m^{2}/\lambda}
. Note that this potential is symmetric under
\varphi\to-\varphi
. Typically one expands the field around one of the local minima, after which the symmetry is lost — this is referred to as the spontaneous symmetry breaking.

What is omitted in this type of reasoning is that the field around the point
\varphi=0
is tachyonic. The simplest way to see this is to neglect the term
\propto\lambda\varphi^{4}
in the Lagrangian (
1
) and write the corresponding field equation

\left(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{2}\right)\varphi=0.

(2)

Making use of the correspondence between the energy and momentum in classical and quantum theory, i.e.,
E\leftrightarrow\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}
and
\bm{p}\leftrightarrow-\mathrm{i}\nabla
, we conclude that Eq. (
2
) corresponds to the energy-momentum relation

E^{2}=\bm{p}^{2}-m^{2},

(3)

which is the relation satisfied by tachyons. Therefore, the Lagrangian (
1
) around
\varphi=0
describes superluminal particles
Dragan and Ekert 2020
;
Dragan 2021
;
Dragan et al. 2023
.

The above formula (
3
) only makes sense for momenta
|\bm{p}|\geq m
. It was shown in the literature
Arons and Sudarshan 1968
;
Dhar and Sudarshan 1968
;
Aharonov et al. 1969
;
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
, that such a restriction of momentum space makes the tachyonic vacuum state stable at
\varphi=0
. The limitation of momentum space implies that not all the field configurations are physically viable, hence the counter-intuitive stability of the field at the local maximum. Because of the stability of the tachyonic vacuum, the tachyon condensation description of the spontaneous symmetry breaking
Sen 1998
;
Sen and Zwiebach 2000
;
Sen 2002
is no longer justified, and another explanation is needed.

We propose to seek the spontaneous symmetry breaking in the interactions of the scalar field described by the Lagrangian (
1
) with other fields. We assume that the particles interacting with the scalar field gain mass only due to the Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism, therefore around
\varphi=0
, they are massless. The interaction between massless particles and tachyons enables processes that would be forbidden if we considered subluminal particles instead of tachyons. An example of such a process is a spontaneous emission of a tachyon by a massless particle. We will analyze this process as a possible origin of the spontaneous symmetry breaking. As a toy model, we consider a Yukawa-type interaction with the interaction Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}=g\varphi\psi^{2}

(4)

where
\psi
is a massless real scalar field.

The quantization of the tachyonic field
\varphi
has been performed in
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
, where it was shown that to preserve Lorentz covariance of the theory one has to describe the quantum states of the tachyonic field in an extended Fock space
with a double set of states that can be interrpreted as in and out states.
However, in a fixed reference frame one can separate the two Fock spaces
and perform calculations in complete analogy to the standard quantum field theory. Then, one can use the effective field operator

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(t,\bm{r})=\int_{|\bm{k}|\geq m}\text{d}^{3}\bm{k}\,\left(u_{\bm{k}}(t,\bm{r})\,\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}+u^{*}_{\bm{k}}(t,\bm{r})\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\right),

(5)

where
u_{\bm{k}}(t,\bm{r})
are the mode solutions to the tachyonic Klein-Gordon equation (
2
), orthonormal with respect to the inner product
(\chi,\eta)=i\int_{t=0}\text{d}^{3}\bm{r}\,\chi^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{t}}\eta
, and
\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}
are the tachyonic ladder operators satisfying standard commutation relations

[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{l}}]=2\omega_{k}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}-\bm{l}).

(6)

The mode solutions are given by

u_{\bm{k}}(t,\bm{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{k}}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}-\Omega_{k}t)},

(7)

where
\Omega_{\bm{k}}
satisfies the tachyonic dispersion relation
\Omega_{\bm{k}}^{2}=\bm{k}^{2}-m^{2}
with
|\bm{k}|\geq m
.

On the other hand, the massless field
\psi
is quantized in the standard way, with the corresponding field operator

\hat{\psi}(x)=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{k}\left(v_{\bm{k}}(x)\hat{b}_{\bm{k}}+v_{\bm{k}}^{*}(x)\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\right).

(8)

Here
v_{\bm{k}}
are mode solutions to the massless field equation
\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi=0
, namely

v_{\bm{k}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2|\bm{k}|}}\mathrm{e}^{-i(|\bm{k}|t-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x})},

(9)

and
\hat{b}_{\bm{k}}
,
\hat{b}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}
are the ladder operators satisfying the standard commutation rules

[\hat{b}_{\bm{k}},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{l}}]=\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{l})

(10)

with all the other commutators vanishing.

We focus on the tachyon emission in the first-order perturbative expansion. Some care is required in the discussion of the initial and final states, as we cannot model particles with wave packets of compact support
Jodłowski 2024
, so we have to employ wave packets whose overlap is parametrically small as the particles go apart. We consider the initial state
\ket{i}\propto\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\ket{0}
consisting of a single massless particle with four-momentum
k
, and the final state
\ket{f}\propto\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{l}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}}\ket{0}
consisting of a massless particle and a tachyon with four-momenta
p
and
l
, respectively. The
S
-matrix element for this process is derived in the Supplementary Material:

-i\bra{f}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}(x)\ket{i}=-ig(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+l-k).

(11)

Given the
S
-matrix element we can calculate the corresponding decay rate, which is equal to (see Supplementary Material)

\Gamma=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}}\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{4\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}\right)

(12)

for
0<m/|\bm{k}|<2
, and vanishes otherwise. Therefore, the emission of a tachyon with mass
m
is possible only if the initial massless particle has energy larger than
m/2
.

Note that the decay rate is not Lorentz covariant because of the term
\propto|\bm{k}|^{-3}
in Eq. (
12
). Indeed, given a massless particle in the initial state with energy
|\bm{k}|>m/2
(for which the decay rate is finite), we can always find a (boosted) reference frame in which this condition will not be satisfied (thus the decay rate will vanish). While the emission of a tachyon is possible in the first frame, it is forbidden in the latter. This apparent contradiction results from the fact that the emission process in the original reference frame is reinterpreted as an absorption process in the boosted reference frame
Arons and Sudarshan 1968
;
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
. From the point of view of the extended Fock space introduced in
Paczos et al. PRD, in press
, Lorentz covariance of the theory remains intact. In that space, tachyonic processes interpreted as emission and absorption are related by boosts, so one should not expect covariance of decay rates. Only by considering absorption as well can covariant quantities be defined. Moreover, even though the laws of physics remain frame-independent, physical objects we observe often define preferred reference frames, such as that of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation. Eq. (
12
) suggests we should expect that the emission process discussed here may also lead to a violation of Lorentz symmetry in the final state of radiation.

It should be pointed out that while we deal with the simplest model of spontaneous symmetry breaking the same results will hold when we consider a more realistic scenario, e.g. a complex tachyonic field interacting through the Yukawa interaction with a massless Dirac field — the model describing the Higgs mechanism for fermions
Larkoski 2019
. Therefore, the spontaneous emission of tachyons by massless particles can be considered a starting point for spontaneous symmetry breaking in the Higgs mechanism. A further exploration of this intriguing issue will be a subject for our future works.

Appendix A
Decay rate

We analyze the emission process
\psi\to\psi\varphi
, where a massless particle emits a tachyon. The corresponding (appropriately normalized) initial and final states are given by

\begin{split}&\ket{i}=\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2|\bm{k}|}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\ket{0},\\
&\ket{f}=\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2\Omega_{\bm{l}}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{l}}\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2|\bm{p}|}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}}\ket{0}.\end{split}

(13)

Here
\bm{k}
,
\bm{p}
, and
\bm{l}
denote the momenta of massless particles in the initial and final states, and the tachyonic momentum, respectively. In the first-order perturbation theory, the contribution to the absorption process is given by

\begin{split}S_{fi}\approx&-i\bra{f}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}(x)\ket{i}=-ig\bra{f}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\hat{\varphi}(x)\left(\hat{\psi}(x)\right)^{2}\ket{i}\\
=&-ig\sqrt{(2\pi)^{9}8\Omega_{\bm{l}}|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|}\bra{0}\hat{b}_{\bm{p}}\hat{a}_{\bm{l}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\hat{\varphi}(x)\left(\hat{\psi}(x)\right)^{2}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\ket{0}.\end{split}

(14)

Now, we use the fact that

[\hat{b}_{\bm{p}},\hat{\psi}(x)]=v^{*}_{\bm{p}}(x),\qquad[\hat{\psi}(x),\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}]=v_{\bm{k}}(x),\qquad[\hat{a}_{\bm{l}},\hat{\varphi}(x)]=u^{*}_{\bm{l}}(x),

(15)

to get

S_{fi}\approx-ig\sqrt{(2\pi)^{9}8\Omega_{\bm{l}}|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}xv^{*}_{\bm{p}}(x)u^{*}_{\bm{l}}(x)v_{\bm{k}}(x)=-ig(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+l-k)\equiv(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k+l-p)(-i\mathcal{A}_{fi}),

(16)

where
\mathcal{A}_{fi}\equiv g
is the amplitude of the process.

According to the general prescription, the decay rate of the particle of four-momentum
k
into
n
particles indexed by
j
is given by

\Gamma=\frac{1}{2E_{\bm{k}}}\int\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{p}_{j}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\bm{p}_{j}}}\right)\left|\mathcal{A}_{fi}\right|^{2}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}\left(k-\sum_{j}p_{j}\right),

(17)

where
E_{\bm{q}}
is the energy of a particle with four-momentum
q
. In our case the formula (
17
) can be rewritten as

\Gamma=\frac{1}{2|\bm{k}|}\int_{|\bm{l}|\geq m}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{l}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\Omega_{\bm{l}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\bm{p}|}g^{2}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(3)}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{l}-\bm{p}\right)\delta(|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|-\Omega_{\bm{l}}).

(18)

Let us choose the reference frame in which

\bm{k}=(0,0,|\bm{k}|),

(19)

i.e. the initial massless particle moves along the
z
-direction. Let us also introduce the angles
\theta
and
\varphi
determining the direction of motion of the final massless particle, so that

\bm{p}=(|\bm{p}|\sin\theta\cos\varphi,|\bm{p}|\sin\theta\sin\varphi,|\bm{p}|\cos\theta).

(20)

We can now integrate (
18
) over
d^{3}\bm{l}
using the delta function
\delta^{(3)}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{l}-\bm{p}\right)
, which will fix
\bm{l}=\bm{k}-\bm{p}
. Hence, we have

\Gamma=\frac{g^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|}\int_{|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|\geq m}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2|\bm{p}|}\frac{1}{2\Omega_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}}2\pi\delta(|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|-\Omega_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}),

(21)

where
\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{p}=|\bm{p}|^{2}\mathrm{d}|\bm{p}|\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi
. The integration over
\mathrm{d}\varphi
can be done trivially since the integrand does not depend on
\varphi
— this contributes a factor of
2\pi
. The integral over
\mathrm{d}|\bm{p}|
is more elaborate and requires working out the explicit form of the delta function argument. The delta function
\delta(|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|-\Omega_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}})
is non-zero only if the following condition is satisfied:

|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|-\sqrt{|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|^{2}-m^{2}}=0.

(22)

We have

\bm{k}-\bm{p}=(-|\bm{p}|\sin\theta\cos\varphi,-|\bm{p}|\sin\theta\sin\varphi,|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|\cos\theta),

(23)

which means

|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|^{2}=|\bm{k}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}-2|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|\cos\theta.

(24)

Thus, Eq. (
22
) is equivalent to

|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|=\sqrt{|\bm{k}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}-2|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|\cos\theta-m^{2}}.

(25)

We square both sides to get

|\bm{k}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}-2|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|=|\bm{k}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}-2|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|\cos\theta-m^{2},\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|\bm{p}|=\frac{m^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|(1-\cos\theta)}\leq|\bm{k}|.

(26)

The last condition
|\bm{p}|\leq|\bm{k}|
must be satisfied since we are considering the actual emission of the tachyon — the process in which the massless particle can only lose energy, which is also required by Eq. (
22
). This restricts the angle
\theta
as follows:

\cos\theta\leq 1-\frac{m^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|^{2}}.

(27)

Making use of the delta function properties we write

\begin{split}\delta(|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|-\Omega_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}})=&\frac{\delta\left(|\bm{p}|-\frac{m^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|(1-\cos\theta)}\right)}{\left|1+\frac{|\bm{p}|-|\bm{k}|\cos\theta}{\sqrt{|\bm{k}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}-2|\bm{k}||\bm{p}|\cos\theta-m^{2}}}\right|}=\frac{\delta\left(|\bm{p}|-\frac{m^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|(1-\cos\theta)}\right)}{\left|1+\frac{|\bm{p}|-|\bm{k}|\cos\theta}{|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|}\right|}\\
=&\delta\left(|\bm{p}|-\frac{m^{2}}{2|\bm{k}|(1-\cos\theta)}\right)\frac{|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|}{|\bm{k}|(1-\cos\theta)}.\end{split}

(28)

Let us also notice that (
24
) combined with (
26
) imply

|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|=\sqrt{m^{2}+(|\bm{k}|-|\bm{p}|)^{2}}\geq m.

(29)

With this in hand, we can perform the integrals over
\mathrm{d}|\bm{p}|
and
\mathrm{d}\theta
in (
21
) to get

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}}\int_{\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k} - \boldsymbol{p}}\geq m}\mathrm{d}\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{p}}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{p}}\sin\theta}{\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}(1-\cos\theta)}\delta\left(\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{p}}-\frac{m^{2}}{2\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}
}(1-\cos\theta)}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}}\frac{m^{2}}{2\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1-\frac{m^{2}}{2\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}(\cos\theta)}{(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}}\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{4\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}\right)\Theta\left(\frac{m}{|\bm{k}|}\right)\Theta\left(2-\frac{m}{|\bm{k}|}\right)

(31)

where
\Theta(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function and it appears due to inequalities
-1\leq 1-m^{2}/(2\absolutevalue{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2})\leq 1
. This decay rate is non-zero only for
0<m/|\bm{k}|<2
.

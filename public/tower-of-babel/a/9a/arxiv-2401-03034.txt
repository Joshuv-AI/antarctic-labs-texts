Lie algebroids
Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. This is an overview article on Lie algebroids, and their role as the infinitesimal counterparts of Lie groupoids.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
58H05, 53D17

1.
Introduction

Lie algebroids are differential-geometric structures unifying tangent bundles and Lie algebras. They appear naturally in foliation theory, Poisson geometry, geometric mechanics, generalized complex geometry, and many other fields of mathematics and physics.
Lie algebroids give rise to infinitesimal symmetries of manifolds, generalizing actions of Lie algebras.
Their global counterparts are Lie groupoids, and one of the major themes in the theory is the interplay between the infinitesimal and global aspects. Throughout the following overview, we will work with (real)
C^{\infty}
-manifolds; similar notions exist in the complex
holomorphic setting.

2.
Definitions and basic properties

2.1.
Definition and examples

Let
M
be a manifold, and denote by
\mathcal{L}_{X}
the Lie derivative with respect to a vector field
X
.

Definition 2.1

(Pradines
[
32
]
)
.

A
Lie algebroid
is a vector bundle
A\to M
, together with a
Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on its space of sections and a
vector bundle map

\mathsf{a}\colon A\to TM

called the
anchor
, such that the
Leibniz rule

[\sigma,f\tau]=f[\sigma,\tau]+(\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}(\sigma)}f)\ \tau

holds
for all
\sigma,\tau\in\Gamma(A)
and all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

We will use the notation
A\Rightarrow M
for Lie algebroids; the double arrow is meant to suggest the
infinitesimal version of Lie groupoids
G\rightrightarrows M
. Some standard examples:

(a)

A Lie algebra is the same as a Lie
algebroid
\mathfrak{g}\Rightarrow \operatorname{pt}

over a point. More generally, Lie algebroids with zero anchor are
families of Lie algebras
. (These need not be locally trivial, in
general.)

(b)

The tangent bundle is a Lie algebroid
TM\Rightarrow M
with anchor
\mathsf{a}=\operatorname{id}_{TM}
.

(c)

The subbundle of vectors tangent to a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is a Lie algebroid
T_{\mathcal{F}}M\Rightarrow M
, with anchor the inclusion.

(d)

A principal
G
-bundle
P\to M
defines an
Atiyah algebroid

\operatorname{At}(P)=(TP)/G\Rightarrow M.

Its sections are the infinitesimal automorphisms of
P
, that is,

G
-invariant vector fields on
P
.
For a vector bundle
V\to M
, one may define
\operatorname{At}(V)\Rightarrow M
as the Atiyah algebroid of the frame
bundle. Its sections are the infinitesimal automorphisms of
V
, that is,
linear vector fields on the total space. Equivalently, they are the first order differential operators acting on sections of
V
,
with scalar principal symbol.

(e)

An action of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on
M
is a Lie algebra morphism
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that

\mathsf{a}\colon\mathfrak{g}\times M\to TM,\ (\xi,x)\mapsto\rho(\xi)_{x}

is smooth. It defines an
action Lie algebroid

\mathfrak{g}\times M\Rightarrow M,

with the
\mathsf{a}
as the anchor, and with bracket on sections extending the given one on constant sections.

(f)

Given a hypersurface
Z\subseteq M
, there are Lie algebroids
[
30
]

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}TM\Rightarrow M,\ \ \phantom{\cdot}^{0}TM\Rightarrow M

whose sections are the vector fields tangent to
Z
and vanishing along
Z
, respectively.

(g)

A closed 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
defines an
Almeida-Molino Lie algebroid

[
1
]

A_{\omega}=TM\oplus\mathbb{R},

with bracket on sections

[X+f,Y+g]=[X,Y]+\mathcal{L}_{X}g-\mathcal{L}_{Y}f+\omega(X,Y).

(h)

A Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on
M
defines a
cotangent Lie algebroid

T^{*}M\Rightarrow M.

The bracket on 1-forms is uniquely determined by its values on exact 1-forms

[{\mbox{d}}f,{\mbox{d}}g]={\mbox{d}}\{f,g\};

the anchor takes
{\mbox{d}}f
to the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}=\{f,\cdot\}
.

The
product
of two Lie algebroids
A_{1}\Rightarrow M,\ A_{2}\Rightarrow M

is uniquely a Lie algebroid

A_{1}\times A_{2}\Rightarrow M_{1}\times M_{2},

in such a way that
the map
\Gamma(A_{1})\oplus\Gamma(A_{2})\to\Gamma(A_{1}\times A_{2})
,

(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\mapsto\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\sigma_{1}+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\sigma_{2}

is a Lie algebra morphism.

2.2.
Subalgebroids

A
Lie subalgebroid
of a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
is a vector subbundle
B\subseteq A
along
N\subseteq M
,
such that:

•

\Gamma(A,B)=\{\sigma\in\Gamma(A)|\ \sigma|_{N}\in\Gamma(B)\}
is a Lie subalgebra.

•

The anchor of
A
takes
B
to
TN
.

A Lie subalgebroid acquires a Lie algebroid structure
B\Rightarrow N
, with
the unique bracket such that restriction map
\Gamma(A,B)\to\Gamma(B),\ \sigma\mapsto\sigma|_{N}

preserves brackets.
Examples:

(a)

If
A=\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra, one recovers the usual notion of a Lie subalgebra.

(b)

If
A\Rightarrow M
is any Lie algebroid, and
N\subseteq M
is such that

B=\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
is a sub-bundle, then
B
is a sub-Lie algebroid. In particular,
if
\mathsf{a}\colon A\to TM
has constant rank, then
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a})\subseteq A
is a Lie subalgebroid.

(c)

Let
M
be Poisson manifold. The conormal bundle of a submanifold
N\subseteq M

is a Lie subalgebroid of
T^{*}M

if and only if
N
is a coisotropic submanifold.

2.3.
Morphisms and isomorphisms

Let
A\Rightarrow M,\ B\Rightarrow N
be Lie algebroids.
A
Lie algebroid morphism
(respectively,
comorphism
) from
B
to
A
along
f\colon N\to M
is a
Lie subalgebroid

\Gamma\subseteq A\times B

along the graph
\operatorname{Gr}(f)=\{(f(n),n)\}\subseteq M\times N
,
such that projection to
B
(respectively, to
A
) is a fiberwise vector space isomorphism.

Lie algebroid morphisms are, in particular, vector bundle morphisms
B\to A
; the description in terms of the graph is used to formulate compatibility with the bracket. A comorphism defines a bundle map
f^{*}A\to B
; compatibility with the bracket means that the
pullback map on sections
\Gamma(A)\to\Gamma(B)
is a Lie algebra morphism. We use the notation

B\dasharrow A
to indicate comorphisms; the direction of the arrow is given by the base map.

(a)

Examples of Lie algebroid morphisms include: morphisms of Lie algebras, the tangent map
Tf\colon TN\to TM
of
f\colon N\to M
, the inclusion of a Lie subalgebroid, the anchor map
A\to TM
of any Lie algebroid.

(b)

A bundle map
\alpha\colon TM\to\mathfrak{g}
to a Lie algebra
is a Lie algebroid morphism if and only if the associated 1-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{g})
is a Maurer-Cartan element,
{\mbox{d}}\alpha+{\frac{1}{2}}[\alpha,\alpha]=0
.

(c)

If
P\to M
is a principal bundle, a Lie algebroid morphism
TM\to\operatorname{At}(P)
(with base map the identity) is the same as a flat principal connection on
P
.

(d)

A Lie algebroid morphism
T[0,1]\to A
from the tangent bundle of an interval to a Lie algebroid
is called an
A
-path
. It is equivalent to a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to A
with the property

\mathsf{a}\circ\gamma=\frac{d}{dt}(\pi\circ\gamma)

where
\pi\colon A\to M
is the bundle projection.

(e)

A Lie algebroid comorphism
TM\dasharrow\mathfrak{g}
is the same as a
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
M
. Here
\Gamma\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\times TM
is identified with the action Lie algebroid. Generalizing, one defines an action of a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
on a manifold
N
to be a
Lie algebroid comorphism
TN\dasharrow A
along some map
f\colon N\to M
.

(f)

Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be Poisson manifolds.
A Poisson map
M_{1}\to M_{2}
gives a Lie algebroid comorphism
T^{*}M_{1}\dasharrow T^{*}M_{2}.

Lie algebroid morphisms can be composed, defining a category
\mathcal{LA}
. Similarly, comorphisms can be composed, defining a category
\mathcal{LA}^{\vee}
.

2.4.
Pullbacks

Let
A\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid, and
f\colon N\to M
a smooth map transverse to the anchor of
A
. That
is, for
x=f(y)
,

T_{x}M=\operatorname{ran}(T_{y}f)+\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{a}_{x}).

Then the fiber product

f^{!}A=A\times_{TM}TN

is a vector bundle over
N
, and acquires
a Lie algebroid structure

f^{!}A\Rightarrow N

as a Lie subalgebroid of

A\times TN
. Projection to the second factor is a Lie algebroid morphism
f^{!}A\to A
.
Under composition,
(f\circ g)^{!}A=g^{!}(f^{!}A)
, provided that the transversality conditions are satisfied.

(a)

For tangent bundles,
f^{!}TM=TN.

(b)

For Atiyah algebroids,

f^{!}\operatorname{At}(P)=\operatorname{At}(f^{*}P)

with the usual pullback of a principal bundle.

(c)

If
f
is the inclusion of a submanifold
N\subseteq M
, then
f^{!}A=\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
.

(d)

If
f
is a local diffeomorphism, then
f^{!}A
is the pullback as a vector bundle.

(e)

If
\operatorname{pr}_{1}\colon M\times F\to M
is projection to the first factor, then
\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{!}A=A\times TF
. This gives the local description of pullbacks under submersions.

2.5.
Other constructions

There are various interesting ways of producing new Lie algebroids out of given ones. The tangent bundle of
a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
has a Lie algebroid structure
TA\Rightarrow TM
, in such a way that the map on sections
\sigma\mapsto T\sigma
is a Lie algebra morphism. Similarly, the
k
-th jet bundle has a Lie algebroid structure
J^{k}(A)\Rightarrow M
, in such a way that
\sigma\mapsto j^{k}(\sigma)

is a Lie algebra morphism
[
21
]
. Given a Lie subalgebroid
B\subseteq A
along a submanifold
N\subseteq M
, the normal bundle is a Lie algebroid

\nu(A,B)\Rightarrow\nu(M,N).

If
N
is closed,
there is a
blow-up Lie algebroid

[
12
]

\operatorname{Bl}_{0}(A,B)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Bl}(M,N)

replacing the subbundle
A|_{N}\to N
with the projectivized normal bundle

\mathbb{P}(\nu(A,B))\Rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\nu(M,N)).

(The subscript indicates that this is different from
\operatorname{Bl}(A,B)
, where one would only remove
B
.)
The Lie algebroid structure is such that the blow-down map is a Lie algebroid morphism. If
N=Z
is a hypersurface, then
\operatorname{Bl}(M,Z)=M
, and so
\operatorname{Bl}_{0}(A,B)
is a Lie algebroid over
M
itself. Special cases include

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}TM
and
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}TM
(see
2.1
(
f
)) as blow-ups of
TM
along
TZ
and
0_{Z}
, respectively.

3.
Orbits, splitting theorem

3.1.
Orbits of a Lie algebroid

Following
[
2
]
, we define a
singular foliation
on
M
to be a subsheaf
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}_{M}
of the sheaf of vector fields,
such that
\mathcal{E}
is locally finitely generated and involutive:

[\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}]\subseteq\mathcal{E}.

The first condition means that every point in
M
has a neighborhood
U
such that
\mathcal{E}(U)
is spanned, as a
C^{\infty}(U)
-module, by finitely many vector fields in
\mathcal{E}(U)
. A
leaf
of a singular foliation is a maximal connected submanifold
\mathcal{O}\subseteq M
whose tangent bundle is
spanned by vector fields in
\mathcal{E}
; it is a theorem
[
2
]
that every singular foliation defines a decomposition of
M
into leaves.
A singular foliation is a
regular
foliation if the leaves have constant dimension.

Every Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
determines a singular foliation, with
\mathcal{E}(U)=\mathsf{a}(\Gamma(A|_{U}))
. Its leaves are called the
orbits
of the Lie algebroid
A
. The tangent space to the orbit through
x
is
T_{x}\mathcal{O}=A|_{x}/\mathfrak{h}_{x}
where

\mathfrak{h}_{x}=\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a}_{x})\Rightarrow\{x\}

is the
isotropy Lie algebra
at
x\in M
.
It is presently unknown
[
22
]
whether every singular foliation is locally the orbit foliation of a Lie algebroid.

(a)

The orbits of an action Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{g}\times M\Rightarrow M
are the usual orbits of the
\mathfrak{g}
-action.

(b)

The orbits of a foliation Lie algebroid are the leaves of the foliation.

(c)

For a Poisson manifold
M
, the orbits of
T^{*}M\Rightarrow M
are the symplectic leaves of the Poisson structure.

(d)

The orbits of an Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P)\Rightarrow M
are the components of
M
.

(e)

The orbits of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}TM=\operatorname{Bl}_{0}(TM,TZ)
are the components of
M-Z,\ Z
.

A Lie algebroid is called
regular
if it defines a regular foliation.
Equivalently, this means that the anchor has constant rank, and so the isotropy Lie algebras
fit into a family of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a})\subseteq A
. A Lie algebroid is
transitive
if the anchor is
surjective, so all its orbits are open. Examples include the action algebroids of transitive
\mathfrak{g}
-actions, the Atiyah algebroids of principal bundles, and the Almeida-Molino algebroid of a closed 2-form.

3.2.
Splitting theorem

Given a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
a submanifold
i\colon N\to M
is called a
Lie algebroid tranversal
if it is
transverse to the anchor (equivalently, transverse to all orbits). In this case, letting
p\colon\nu(M,N)\to N
be the normal bundle, the Lie algebroid
p^{!}i^{!}A\Rightarrow\nu(M,N)
is the
linear approximation
of
A
along
N
.

Theorem 3.1

(Linearization along transversals)
.

[
5
]

There exists an open neighborhood
U\subseteq\nu(M,N)
of the zero section and a tubular neighborhood embedding

\phi\colon U\to M
, lifting to a Lie algebroid isomorphism

(p^{!}i^{!}A)|_{U}\to A|_{\phi(U)}.

The following special case is particularly important:

Theorem 3.2

(Splitting theorem)
.

[
14
,
17
,
39
]

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq M
be an orbit of the Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
, and
N\subseteq M
a transversal of complementary dimension,
with
N\cap\mathcal{O}=\{x\}
.
Then
A
is isomorphic, near
x
, to the
product Lie algebroid

i^{!}A\times T\mathcal{O}\Rightarrow N\times\mathcal{O}.

If
A\Rightarrow M
is a regular Lie algebroid, then
i^{!}A\Rightarrow N
has zero
anchor, and so is given by a family of Lie algebras. In particular, if
A\Rightarrow M
is transitive, then
N=\{x\}
is just a point, and so we find that
A
is locally a product

A|_{U}\cong\mathfrak{k}\times TU

with
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{h}_{x}
. In particular,
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a})\subseteq A
is a Lie algebra bundle, with typical fiber
\mathfrak{k}
.

4.
The correspondence between Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids

4.1.
The Lie functor

Let
G\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid, with source and target maps denoted

\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon G\to M
. A vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(G)
is
left-invariant
if it is tangent to the
\mathsf{t}
-fibers, and

X_{g}=(L_{g})_{*}X_{\mathsf{s}(g)}

for all
g\in G
, where
L_{g}\colon\mathsf{t}^{-1}(\mathsf{s}(g))\mapsto\mathsf{t}^{-1}(\mathsf{t}(g))
denotes left translation. The Lie algebroid

A=\operatorname{Lie}(G)\Rightarrow M

of a Lie groupoid is defined as follows:

•

As a vector bundle,
A=\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{t})|_{M}
.

•

The bracket on
\Gamma(A)
is defined by its identification with the left-invariant vector fields.

•

The anchor is the restriction of
-T\mathsf{s}\colon TG\to TM
to
A\subseteq TG|_{M}
.

Some examples:

(a)

For a Lie group one recovers the usual construction of the Lie algebra.

(b)

The pair groupoid and the homotopy groupoid of
M
both have Lie algebroid
TM\Rightarrow M
.

(c)

The Lie algebroid of the holonomy groupoid or monodromy groupoid of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
T_{\mathcal{F}}M\Rightarrow M
.

(d)

The Lie algebroid of the gauge groupoid of a principal bundle
P\to M
is the Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P)\Rightarrow M
.

4.2.
Integrability

A Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
is called
integrable
if it is of the form
A=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
for a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
.
Examples from foliation theory dictate that one should allow for Lie groupoids
G\rightrightarrows M
whose total space is
non-Hausdorff
(but still insisting that the manifold of units and all
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}
-fibers are Hausdorff. If
A
is integrable, then there is a unique integration whose source fibers are simply connected.

(a)

The source-simply connected integration of
TM\Rightarrow M
is the homotopy groupoid
\pi(M)\rightrightarrows M
.

(b)

Given a foliation
\mathcal{F}
, the source-simply connected integration of
T_{\mathcal{F}}M\Rightarrow M
is the
holonomy groupoid
.
[
33
]
,
[
41
]
.

(c)

Every family of Lie algebras (as a Lie algebroid with zero anchor) is integrable to a family of Lie groups
[
13
]
.

(d)

Every action Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{k}\times M\Rightarrow M
is integrable (but the integration is not an action groupoid for a global group action, in general)
[
11
]
.

A counter-example to integrability was observed by Almeida-Molino
[
1
]
, who found that the Lie algebroid

A_{\omega}
defined by a closed 2-form
\omega
(see
2.1
(
g
))
cannot be integrable unless the group of spherical periods

\Gamma=\{\int_{S^{2}}j^{*}\omega|\ j\colon S^{2}\to M\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is discrete.
The precise criterion for integrability of Lie algebroids
A\Rightarrow M
will be given below.
It involves the
monodromy invariants
.

4.3.
Monodromy groups

The original definition
[
10
]
of the monodromy groups uses Lie algebroid homotopy. Here we present an alternative approach

[
28
]
.
Let
K
be a connected Lie group. Recall that isomorphism classes of principal bundles over 2-spheres
S^{2}
, with trivialization
P_{x_{0}}\cong K
at some fixed base point
x_{0}
, are labeled by the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(K)
. This is a group isomorphism, for the group structure on classes of principal bundles given by connected sum. The proof is by a clutching construction, and may be used
to obtain a similar result for Lie algebroids. Let
\mathfrak{k}
be a Lie algebra, denote by
\widetilde{K}
the connected and simply connected
Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{k}
, and let
\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{K})
be its center.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
28
]

There is a canonical isomorphism between the group of equivalence classes of transitive Lie algebroids
A\Rightarrow S^{2}
, together
with an isomorphism
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a}_{x_{0}})\cong\mathfrak{k}
, and the group
\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{K})
.
Given a connected Lie group
K
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, the natural map

\pi_{1}(K)\to\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{K})

takes the class of a principal
K
-bundle to the class of its Atiyah algebroid.

For instance, since
\operatorname{Cent}(\operatorname{SU}(2))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, there are two classes of Lie algebroids with

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{su}(2)
. They are represented by the Atiyah algebroids of the trivial and non-trivial principal
\operatorname{SO}(3)
-bundles.
On the other hand, for
\mathfrak{k}=\mathbb{R}
we obtain a 1-parameter family of classes of Lie algebroid, represented by the Almeida-Molino Lie algebroids for multiples of the volume form.

Suppose
A\Rightarrow M
is any transitive Lie algebroid, and
x\in M
, with isotropy Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{x}=\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{a}_{x})
.
Given a smooth map
j\colon(S^{2},x_{0})\to(M,x)
, the pullback

j^{!}A\Rightarrow S^{2}
is transitive, and so defines an element of
\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{H}_{x})
.
The set of elements obtained in this way define the
monodromy group

\Gamma_{x}\subseteq\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{H}_{x}).

By transitivity, the monodromy groups for different points
x\in M
are isomorphic. For general, not necessarily transitive Lie algebroids
A\Rightarrow M
, we may define the monodromy group at
x\in M
as the
monodromy group
\Gamma_{x}
of the restriction
A_{\mathcal{O}}
to the orbit
\mathcal{O}
through
x
.

4.4.
Crainic-Fernandes integrability theorem

Consider first the case of a transitive Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
. Fix
x\in M
, with isotropy algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{x}
. If
A
is integrable, then its integration is a transitive Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
, and as such is the gauge groupoid
of a principal
K
-bundle
P\to M
, where
K
is a Lie group (possibly disconnected) with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{h}_{x}
. Accordingly,

A
is the Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P)
. By the
second part of
4.1
, and since
j^{!}\operatorname{At}(P)=\operatorname{At}(j^{*}P)
, the elements of the monodromy groups are all contained in

\pi_{1}(K^{0})\subseteq\operatorname{Cent}(\widetilde{K})
. In particular, the monodromy group
\Gamma_{x}
must be discrete.

It turns out that this condition is also sufficient. In fact, there is an explicit construction
[
28
]
of a source-simply connected Lie groupoid integrating
A
, analogous to the description of a principal bundle in terms of the parallel transport with respect to a connection.

For non-transitive Lie algebroids
A\Rightarrow M
discreteness of the monodromy groups is a necessary condition for integrability, since an integration of
A
also gives an integration of its restriction to orbits. However, it is not sufficient, in general.

Theorem 4.2

(Crainic-Fernandes)
.

[
10
]

A Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
is integrable if and only if the monodromy groups are
uniformly discrete
, in the sense that
there is an open neighborhood
U
of the zero section of
A
with the property that if
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}_{x}\subseteq A_{x}
is contained
in
U
,
and
\exp\xi\in\Gamma_{x}
, then
\xi=0
.

Again, when these conditions are satisfied, there is an explicit construction of the source-simply connected
groupoid integrating
A
, as a quotient

G=\frac{\{\mbox{Lie algebroid paths}\ \ T[0,1]\to A\}}{\{\mbox{Lie algebroid homotopy}\}}.

A groupoid structure is defined by concatenation. The construction was suggested by Catteneo-Felder
[
7
]
in the case of cotangent Lie algebroids
and by Ševera
[
35
]
and Weinstein for general Lie algebroids. It is motivated by
the proof of Lie’s third theorem for Lie groups by Duistermaat-Kolk
[
15
]
. As shown in
[
10
]
,
the groupoid
G
is always defined as a topological groupoid; Theorem
4.2
gives a criterion for its smoothness.

5.
The Lie algebroid complex

5.1.
Contravariant descriptions of Lie algebroids

Given a vector bundle
A\to M
, consider the graded algebra

\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)=\bigoplus_{k}\mathsf{C}^{k}(A)
with

\mathsf{C}^{k}(A)=\Gamma(\wedge^{k}A^{*}).

Theorem 5.1
.

[
37
]

There is a bijective correspondence between Lie algebroid structures on vector bundles
A
and degree
1

differentials on the graded algebra
C^{\bullet}(A)
.
A vector bundle morphism
B\to A
between Lie algebroids is a Lie algebroid morphism if and only if
the induced map
\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(B)
is a cochain map.

In the language of super-geometry,
\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)
is the algebra of smooth functions on the super-manifold
A[1]
, and the differential is a homological vector field
Q
on
A[1]
. Given a Lie algebroid structure on
A
, the differential is given by

\displaystyle({\mbox{d}}\phi)(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}(\sigma_{i})}\phi(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma_{i}},\ldots)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\phi([\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}],\sigma_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma_{j}},\ldots).

The complex
(\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A),{\mbox{d}})

generalizes both the de Rham complex
\Omega(M)
of differential forms (for
A=TM
) and the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for
A=\mathfrak{g}
. If
A=T_{\mathcal{F}}M
for a foliation
\mathcal{F}
, one obtains the leaf-wise de Rham complex.
If
M
is a Poisson manifold, the Lie algebroid complex for
T^{*}M\Rightarrow M

is given by the Lichnerowicz complex
\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(T^{*}M)=\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)
, defining
Poisson cohomology
.
As another formulation of Theorem
5.1
, we have:

Theorem 5.2
.

Lie algebroid structures on vector bundles
A\to M
are equivalent to Poisson
structures on the dual bundle
A^{*}
which are
linear
, in the sense that the Poisson bracket between linear functions on
A
is again linear.

Identifying on
M
with their pullback to
A^{*}
, and letting
\phi_{\sigma}\in C^{\infty}(A^{*})
be the linear function defined
by the section
\sigma
, the Poisson bracket is determined by

\{f,g\}=0,\ \ \{\phi_{\sigma},f\}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}(\sigma)}f,\ \ \{\phi_{\sigma},\phi_{\tau}\}=\phi_{[\sigma,\tau]}.

In particular, for the cotangent Lie algebroid
A=T^{*}M
of a Poisson manifold
M
one obtains a linear Poisson structure on
A^{*}=TM
called the tangent lift of the Poisson structure.

5.2.

For Lie groupoids

\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)

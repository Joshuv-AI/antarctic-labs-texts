Abstract




Higher order log-concavity of the overpartition function and its consequences

Abstract.

Let
{\overline{p}}(n)
denote the overpartition function. In this paper, we study the asymptotic higher order
\log
-concavity property of the overpatition function in a similar framework done by Hou and Zhang for the partition function. This will enable us to move on further in order to prove
\log
-concavity of overpartitions, explicitly by studying the asymptotic expansion of the quotient
{\overline{p}}(n-1){\overline{p}}(n+1)/{\overline{p}}(n)^{2}
upto a certain order so that one can finally ends up with the phenomena of
2
-
\log
-concavity and higher order Turán property of
{\overline{p}}(n)
by following a sort of unified approach.

Keywords
: Overpartition function, Rademacher-type series,
r
-
\log
-concavity, Higher
order Turán inequalities.

AMS Classifications
: 05A20, 11N37, 65G99.

1
Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
. Let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
.
Recall that a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is called log-concave if

a_{n}^{2}-a_{n+1}a_{n-1}\geq 0,~~n\geq 1.

Moreover, this sequence is said to be asymptotically
r
-log-concave if there exists
N
such that

\displaystyle{\hat{\mathscr{L}}}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N},{\hat{\mathscr{L}}}^{2}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N},\ldots,{\hat{\mathscr{L}}}^{r}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}

(1.1)

are all non-negative sequences, where

{\hat{\mathscr{L}}}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}=\{a_{n+1}^{2}-a_{n}a_{n+2}\}_{n\geq 0}\quad\text{and}\quad{\hat{\mathscr{L}}}^{k}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}={\hat{\mathscr{L}}}\left({\hat{\mathscr{L}}}^{k-1}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

Based on the Hardy-Ramanujan-Rademacher formula
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
18
]
and the error estimation given by Lehmer
[
13
,
14
]
,
DeSalvo and Pak
[
6
]
showed that the partition function
p(n)
is log-concave for all
n>25
, conjectured by Chen
[
2
]
. Consequently, Chen, Wang and Xie proved the DeSalvo-Pak conjecture that states

Theorem 1.1

(Conjecture 1.3,
[
4
]
)
.

For
n\geq 45
, we have

\dfrac{p(n-1)}{p(n)}\Bigl(1+\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{24}n^{3/2}}\Bigr)>\dfrac{p(n)}{p(n+1)}.

Recently, Chen, Jia and Wang
[
3
]
proceed further to show that
\{p(n)\}_{n\geq 95}
satisfies the higher order Turán inequality and formulate a conjecture
[
3
, Conjecture 1.2]
in somewhat similar to Theorem
1.1
, settled by Larson and Wagner
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.
What’s more, Hou and Zhang
[
10
]
proved the asymptotic
r
-log-concavity of
p(n)
and as a consequence they showed
\{p(n)\}_{n\geq 221}
is
2
-
\log
-concave, whereas an alternative approach through studying determinant of certain class of matrix can be found in
[
12
]
.

The overpartition function also reflects the similar log-behavior.
Recall an overpartition
[
5
]
of a nonnegative integer
n
is a partition of
n
where the first occurrence of each distinct part may be overlined.
Let
{\overline{p}}(n)
denote the number of overpartitions of
n
.
Zukermann
[
21
]
gave a formula for the overpartition function, which is considered by Sills
[
19
]
as a Rademacher-type convergent series

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1\atop 2\nmid k}^{\infty}\sqrt{k}\sum_{h=0\atop(h,k)=1}^{k}\frac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}n}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

(1.2)

where

\omega(h,k)=\exp\left(\pi i\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\frac{r}{k}\left(\frac{hr}{k}-\left\lfloor\frac{hr}{k}\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)

for positive integers
h
and
k
.

Let
{\widehat{\mu}}(n)=\pi\sqrt{n}
.
From this Rademacher-type series (
1.2
), Engel
[
7
]
provided an error term for the overpartition function

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1\atop 2\nmid k}^{N}\sqrt{k}\sum_{h=0\atop(h,k)=1}^{k}\frac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}n}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+R_{2}(n,N),

where

\displaystyle|R_{2}(n,N)|\leq\frac{N^{\frac{5}{2}}}{n{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\sinh\left(\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{N}\right).

(1.3)

In particular, when
N=3
, we have

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}(n)=\frac{1}{8n}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)e^{-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}+\left(1-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right]+R_{2}(n,3),

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle|R_{2}(n,3)|\leq\frac{3^{\frac{5}{2}}}{n{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\sinh\left(\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}\right)\leq\frac{3^{\frac{5}{2}}e^{\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}}}{2n{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}.

(1.5)

Similar to the work done in the world of partitions, Engel initiated the study of
\log
-concavity property of the overpartition function in his work
[
7
]
. The second author and Liu established Theorem
1.1
in context of overpartitions in
[
16
, Equation (1.6)]
. They also proved the higher order Turán property of
{\overline{p}}(n)
for
n\geq 16
(cf. see
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
). Following the treatment done in
[
12
]
, the first author
[
17
, Theorem 1.7]
laid out a proof of
\{{\overline{p}}(n)\}_{n\geq 42}
is
2
-
\log
-concave.

In this paper, our main goal is to prove the asymptotic r-log-concavity for the overpartition function, stated in Theorem
1.2
.
In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we give a bound for
{\overline{p}}(n+1)/{\overline{p}}(n)
and an asymptotic expression of

2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we utilize the Rademacher-type convergent series and the error
estimation given by Engel to derive an estimation for
{\overline{p}}(n)
.
In view of (
1.4
),
{\overline{p}}(n)
can be written as

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}(n)={\widehat{T}}(n)+{\widehat{R}}(n),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle{\widehat{T}}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8n}\left(1-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)},

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\widehat{R}}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8n}\left(1+\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)e^{-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}+R_{2}(n,3).

(2.3)

2.1
Error estimation of
{\overline{p}}(n)

To obtain the error estimation of
{\overline{p}}(n)
, we need to introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any integer
m\geq 1
, there exists a real number

N_{0}(m):=\begin{cases}1,&\quad\text{if}\ m=1,\\
2m\log m-m\log\log m,&\quad\text{if}\ m\geq 2,\end{cases}

such that

\displaystyle x^{m}e^{-x}<1,~~~\text{for}~~x\geq N_{0}(m).

Proof.

For
m=1
, it is immediate that
N_{0}(m)=1
. For
m\geq 2
, rewrite the inequality
x^{m}e^{-x}<1
as
f(x):=x-m\log x>0
. Now
f(x)
is strictly increasing for
x>m
. In order to show
f(x)>0
for
x\geq N_{0}(m)
, first we show that
N_{0}(m)>m
and then it is enough to show
f(N_{0}(m))>0
. To prove
N_{0}(m)>m
, it is equivalent to show
m^{2}>e.\log m
which holds for
m\geq 2
. Next, we observe that

f(N_{0}(m))>0\Leftrightarrow\log m>\log 2+2\log\log m+\log\Bigl(1-\dfrac{\log\log m}{2\log m}\Bigr)\\

For
m\geq 3
,
\log\Bigl(1-\dfrac{\log\log m}{2\log m}\Bigr)<0
and hence, it is sufficient to prove

\log m>\log 2+2\log\log m\Leftrightarrow m>2\ (\log m)^{2},

which holds for
m\geq 14
. Therefore,
f(N_{0}(m))>0
for all
m\geq 14
and we conclude the proof by checking numerically that
f(N_{0}(m))>0
for
2\leq m\leq 13
.

With the aid of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following conclusion.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any integer
m\geq 2
, there exist an integer
N_{1}(m)
with

\displaystyle N_{1}(m)=\max\left\{184,\dfrac{9}{4\pi^{2}}N^{2}_{0}(m)\right\},

such that

\displaystyle\left|{\widehat{y}}_{n}\right|<\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m},

where
{\widehat{y}}_{n}={\widehat{R}}(n)/{\widehat{T}}(n)
.

Proof.

By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle{\widehat{y}}_{n}\leq e^{-\frac{2{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}}\left({\widehat{T}}_{1}(n)+{\widehat{R}}_{1}(n)\right),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle{\widehat{T}}_{1}(n)=\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)+1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)-1}e^{-\frac{4{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}},~~~~~~{\widehat{R}}_{1}(n)=4\cdot 3^{\frac{5}{2}}\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)-1}.

From (
2.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle{\widehat{T}}_{1}(184)+{\widehat{R}}_{1}(184)<\frac{3}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|{\widehat{y}}_{n}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{3}{2}e^{-\frac{2{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}},~~~\text{for}~~n\geq 184.

According to Lemma
2.1
, there exists the integer
N_{0}(m)
such that for
\frac{2}{3}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)\geq N_{0}(m)

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{2{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{3}}<\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}.

On the other side,

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}{\pi}.

Therefore, when

\displaystyle n\geq\max\left\{184,\dfrac{9}{4\pi^{2}}N^{2}_{0}(m)\right\}=N_{1}(m),

we have

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}(n)>\dfrac{3}{2}N_{0}(m),

which completes the proof.

2.2
Bounds for the ratio
{\overline{p}}(n+1)/{\overline{p}}(n)

In order to obtain an estimation of
{\overline{p}}(n+1)/{\overline{p}}(n)
, we need the following lower and upper bounds for
{\widehat{T}}(n+1)/{\widehat{T}}(n)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\widehat{\nu}}(n)={\widehat{\mu}}(n)({\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)-1)
and

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{1/2\choose k}\pi^{2k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2k},\qquad{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)=\left|{1/2\choose m^{\prime}+1}\right|\pi^{2(m^{\prime}+1)}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2(m^{\prime}+1)},

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{-3/2\choose k}\pi^{2k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2k},\qquad{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{2}(n)=\left|{-3/2\choose m^{\prime}+1}\right|\pi^{2(m^{\prime}+1)}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2(m^{\prime}+1)},

\displaystyle{\widehat{\nu}}_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=\left({\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)-{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k},

\displaystyle{\widehat{\nu}}_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\left({\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)+{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k}+2{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m-1}\right),

then

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}

\displaystyle>{\widehat{\nu}}_{1}(n)\left({\widehat{\mu}}_{2}(n)-{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{2}(n)\right)(1-{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n))\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{k}}{k!},

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}

\displaystyle<{\widehat{\nu}}_{2}(n)({\widehat{\mu}}_{2}(n)+{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{2}(n))(1+2{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n))\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{k}}{k!}+e^{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\right)

(2.6)

where
{\widehat{T}}(n)
is defined as in (
2.2
).

Proof.

By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}=\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)-1}\cdot\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{3}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{3}}\cdot e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}.

(2.7)

Now we consider the above ratio term by term.
For the first factor, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)-1}=\frac{\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}}{1-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}}.

By Taylor’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k},

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k}<\left(1-\frac{1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)^{-1}<\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k}+2{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m-1}.

(2.8)

Noting that

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)={\widehat{\mu}}(n)\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any integer
m
, let
m^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor
. Since

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{1/2\choose k}\pi^{2k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2k}+{1/2\choose m^{\prime}+1}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{m^{\prime}+1}\left(1+\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-m^{\prime}-1},

where
0<\xi<\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}
.
We have

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)-{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)<\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}<{\widehat{\mu}}_{1}(n)+{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n).

(2.9)

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we deduce that

\displaystyle{\widehat{\nu}}_{1}(n)<\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-1}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)-1}<{\widehat{\nu}}_{2}(n).

(2.10)

For the second factor, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{3}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{3}}=\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{-3/2}.

Since

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{-3/2\choose k}\pi^{2k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-2k}+{-3/2\choose m^{\prime}+1}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{m^{\prime}+1}\left(1+\xi\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}-m^{\prime}-1},

where
0<\xi<\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}
.
We have

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mu}}_{2}(n)-{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{2}(n)<\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{3}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{3}}<{\widehat{\mu}}_{2}(n)+{\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{2}(n).

(2.11)

For the last factor, using (
2.9
), then

\displaystyle e^{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)-{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)}<e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}<e^{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)+{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n)}.

Evidently, for
0<x<\frac{1}{2}
,

e^{-x}>1-x,\qquad e^{x}<1+2x

and for
x>0

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}<e^{x}<\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+e^{x}\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!},

so that

\displaystyle e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}>(1-{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n))\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{k}}{k!}

(2.12)

and

\displaystyle e^{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)-{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}<(1+2{\widehat{\mu}}(n){\widehat{\varepsilon}}_{1}(n))\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{k}}{k!}+e^{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)}\frac{{\widehat{\nu}}(n)^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\right).

(2.13)

Applying the estimates (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) to (
2.7
), we reach (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). This completes the proof.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any positive integer
m
, there exist integer
N
, real numbers
a_{k}
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for
n\geq N

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k}-C_{1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m-1}<\frac{{\overline{p}}(n+1)}{{\overline{p}}(n)}<\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-k}+C_{2}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m-1}.

(2.14)

Proof.

By (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.2
, for any
m\geq 2
there exists the integer
N_{1}(m)
such that

|{\overline{p}}(n)/{\widehat{T}}(n)-1|<\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m},~~\forall n\geq N_{1}(m).

We have

{\widehat{T}}(n)\left(1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}\right)<{\overline{p}}(n)<{\widehat{T}}(n)\left(1+\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}\right).

Since
{\widehat{\mu}}(n)
is a increasing function of
n
, we derive that

\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}\frac{1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}}{1+\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}}<\frac{{\overline{p}}(n+1)}{{\overline{p}}(n)}<\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}\frac{1+\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}}{1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}}.

We find that for
0<\lambda<1/3
,

\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}<1+3\lambda~~~and~~~\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}>1-2\lambda.

According to

0<\frac{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{m}}<1/3,

we have that for all
n\geq N_{1}(m)

\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}\left(1-4\cdot 2^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}\right)<\frac{{\overline{p}}(n+1)}{{\overline{p}}(n)}<\frac{{\widehat{T}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{T}}(n)}\left(1+6\cdot 2^{m}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-m}\right).

(2.15)

By Lemma
2.3
, we can see that
{\widehat{T}}(n+1)/{\widehat{T}}(n)
is bounded by a pair of polynomials in
{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1}
whose difference is a polynomials in
{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1}
with degree at least
m+1
. Combining (
2.15
) and
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\widehat{\mu}}(n)=+\infty
, we have (
2.14
). This completes the proof.

For any positive integer
m
, we further show that we can give the explicit numbers of these parameters. In this paper, we follow the Mathematica package of Hou and Zhang
[
10
]

The following lemma given by Hou and Zhang

plays an important role in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a positive sequence such that
\mathscr{R}^{2}a_{n}=a_{n}a_{n+2}/a_{n+1}^{2}
has the following asymptotic expression

\mathscr{R}^{2}a_{n}=1+\frac{c}{n^{\alpha}}+\cdots+o\left(\frac{c}{n^{\beta}}\right),~~n\rightarrow\infty,

where
0<\alpha\leq\beta
. If
c<0
and
\alpha<2
, then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is asymptotically
[\beta/\alpha]
-log-concave.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Based on (
2.14
), we consider the bound of
{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{-r}
and
1/h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})
, where
h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})
is a polynomial in
{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1}
with constant term
1
.

Let
c
denote the tail term of
h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})-1
. If
c>0
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that

h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})-1>0,~~\forall n\geq N.

By Taylor’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle 1-\lambda+\lambda^{2}-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\lambda^{m}-\lambda^{m+1}<\frac{1}{h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\left(h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})-1\right)}

\displaystyle<1-\lambda+\lambda^{2}-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\lambda^{m}+\lambda^{m+1},

where
\lambda=h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})-1
. If
c<0
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that

0<1-h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})<\frac{1}{2},~~\forall n\geq N.

So

\displaystyle 1+\lambda+\lambda^{2}+\cdots+\lambda^{m}+\lambda^{m+1}<\frac{1}{h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\left(1-h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})\right)}

\displaystyle<1+\lambda+\lambda^{2}+\cdots+\lambda^{m}+2\lambda^{m+1},

where
\lambda=1-h({\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{-1})
.

We now consider the bound of
{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{-r}
. It is easy to get that

\left(\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)^{-r}=\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)^{2}}\right)^{-r/2}.

Then we can derive the bounds of
\left(\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}\right)^{-r}
in a way similar to the estimation of
\frac{{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)}{{\widehat{\mu}}(n)}
, thus get an estimation of
{\widehat{\mu}}(n+1)^{-r}
.

Based on the above estimations, we compute the asymptotic expression of
{\overline{u}}_{n}:=\dfrac{{\overline{p}}(n-1){\overline{p}}(n+1)}{{\overline{p}}(n)^{2}}
by Mathematica, for any positive integer
m
,

{\overline{u}}_{n}=1-\frac{\pi}{4n^{3/2}}+\cdots+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{m}}\right).

By Lemma
2.5
, we complete the proof.

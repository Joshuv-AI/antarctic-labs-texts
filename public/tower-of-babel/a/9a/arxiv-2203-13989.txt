1.
Introduction

Throughout in this paper, a
semisimple Lie group
means a noncompact connected real semisimple Lie group
G
with finite centre.
However, because the methods are quite abstract, the ideas apply more generally, and in particular to the
p
-adic case and to the more general reductive groups considered by Harish-Chandra to enable inductive arguments.

To present our main results, we need some notation; details may be found below.
Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of a semisimple Lie group
G
.
Then
G
has a Cartan decomposition
KA^{+}K
, where
A^{+}
is a cone in a simply connected abelian subgroup
A
of
G
.
We write
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
for the corresponding cone in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
A
.
We take
\rho
to be a particular element of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{R})
;
each
\lambda
in
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{C})
induces a homomorphism
\exp H\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(H)}
from
A
to
\mathbb{C}
.

Here and later,
\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}y
denotes the element of Haar measure on the group over which
y
varies.

A
K
-bi-invariant function
\phi
on
G
is said to be a (zonal) spherical function if and only if

(1)

\int_{K}\phi(xky)\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}k=\phi(x)\ignorespaces\,\phi(y)\qquad\forall x,y\in G,

and a spherical function
\phi
on
G
is said to be hermitean if
\phi(x^{-1})=\bar{\phi}(x)
for all
x\in G
.
Harish-Chandra gave an integral formula for spherical functions
\phi_{\lambda}
, where
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
; these exhaust the spherical functions on
G
.
There is a closed subset
\mathfrak{a}^{*,\mathrm{Her}}
of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
, which we describe later, such that
\phi_{\lambda}
is positive-real-valued and hermitean if and only if
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*,\mathrm{Her}}
.
For
\lambda\in(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{+}
, the function
\phi_{\lambda}(\exp H)
is comparable in the whole of
(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{+}
to the product of an exponential
\mathrm{e}^{(\lambda-\rho)(H)}
and a polynomial

; see Theorem

Recall that, given unitary representations
\sigma
and
\pi
of
G
, we say that
\sigma
is weakly contained in
\pi
, written
\sigma\preceq\pi
, if and only if all positive definite functions associated to

\pi
.

Given a continuous function

on
G
, we define its

\mathcal{A}u(x):=\left(\int_{K}\int_{K}\left|u(kxk^{\prime})\right|^{2}\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}k\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}\qquad\forall x\in G.

We now state our main theorems, which were inspired by
[
Hz70
]
and
[

]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary representation of a semisimple Lie group
G
, and suppose that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
Consider the condition

(2)

\mathcal{A}\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\leq\left\|\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left\|\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\phi_{\lambda}\qquad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Then (
2
) holds if and only if all
\sigma\in\bar{G}
such that
\sigma\preceq\pi
satisfy (
2
) with
\pi
replaced by
\sigma
.

In particular, the inequality (
2
) passes to the irreducible representations of
G
that appear in the direct integral decomposition of
\pi
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary representation of a semisimple Lie group
G
, and suppose that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*,\mathrm{Her}}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(3)

\mathcal{A}\left\langle\pi(x)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\leq\left\|\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left\|\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)

for all

and for all
\xi
and

, and

(4)

\sup_{k,k^{\prime}\in K}\left|\left\langle\pi(kxk^{\prime})\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|\leq C(\xi,\eta)\ignorespaces\,\phi_{\lambda}(x)

for all
x\in G
and all

, and for all
K
-finite

.
Further, if these conditions hold, then we may take

to be given by

C(\xi,\eta)=\dim(\operatorname{span}(\pi(K)\xi))^{1/2}\left\|\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\dim(\operatorname{span}(\pi(K)\eta))^{1/2}\left\|\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}},

and further, (

) holds for all smooth

.

If moreover
\pi
is irreducible, then conditions (
3
) and (

\xi
and
\eta

\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\setminus\{0\}

such that

(5)

\mathcal{A}\left\langle\pi(x)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\leq C(\xi,\eta)\ignorespaces\,\phi_{\lambda}(x)\qquad\forall x\in G.

From these theorems, it follows that

(a)

if

is an irreducible unitary representation, then an estimate of the form (
2
) holds, where
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*,\mathrm{Her}}
and

(b)

if

where

Unless

Sharp estimates for the spherical functions

2.1.
Unitary representations and
\mathsf{B}(G)

In this section, we let
G
be a locally compact group.
For (suitable) functions
f
and
f^{\prime}
on
G
, we write
f*f^{\prime}
for their usual convolution and
f^{*}
for the function
x\mapsto\Delta(x)^{-1}\bar{f}(x^{-1})
, where
\Delta
is the modular function.

Denote by
\bar{G}
the “set” of all continuous unitary representations
\pi
of
G
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, and by
\hat{G}
the subset of
\bar{G}
consisting of irreducible representations, all modulo unitary equivalence.
(We write “set” to point out that care is required; treating this as a set can lead to some unedifying problems in set theory.)

For any unitary representation
\pi
of
G
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(G)
, we define the operator
\pi(f)
:

\pi(f):=\int_{G}f(x)\ignorespaces\,\pi(x)\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}x;

the integral converges in the weak operator topology.
Then
\pi(f*f^{\prime})=\pi(f)\pi(f^{\prime})
and
\pi(f^{*})=\pi(f)^{*}
, so that
\pi(\mathsf{L}^{1}(G))
is a star-algebra of operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
.

A
matrix coefficient of a unitary representation
\pi
of
G
is a function
u
of the form
\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi,\eta\right\rangle
, that is,

u(x)=\left\langle\pi(x)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\quad\forall x\in G,

where
\pi\in\bar{G}
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
.
We abbreviate this formula to
u=\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi,\eta\right\rangle
.

The
Fourier–Stieltjes algebra

\mathsf{B}(G)
is the space of all matrix coefficients of all unitary representations:

\mathsf{B}(G)=\{u\in C(G):u=\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi,\eta\right\rangle,\ \pi\in\bar{G},\ \xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\};

the same function
u
may arise in different ways.
Pointwise addition and multiplication of matrix coefficients correspond to direct sums and tensor products of representations.
Finally, we norm
\mathsf{B}(G)
: for
u\in\mathsf{B}(G)
,

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{B}}:=\min\{\left\|\xi\right\|\left\|\eta\right\|:u=\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi,\eta\right\rangle,\ \pi\in\bar{G},\ \xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\}.

The theory of
C^{*}
-algebras shows that the minimum is attained.

The set of matrix coefficients of a fixed representation
\pi
is not
a priori
a vector space, and to allow us to use the tools of functional analysis, we consider the closed linear span of the matrix coefficients in
\mathsf{B}(G)
.
Following Arsac
[
Ar76
]
, we define
\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
to be the linear space of all infinite linear combinations

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi_{j},\eta_{j}\right\rangle\qquad\text{such that}\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\xi_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left\|\eta_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}<\infty;

we norm
\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
by setting

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{A}_{\pi}}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\xi_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left\|\eta_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}:u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi_{j},\eta_{j}\right\rangle\right\}.

Then
\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
may be identified with the space of all functions of the form

u(x)=\operatorname{trace}(T\pi(x))\qquad\forall x\in G,

where
T
is a trace class operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, and
\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{A}_{\pi}}
coincides with the trace norm of
T
.
Alternatively,
\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
may be identified with the space of all matrix coefficients of the infinite sum of copies of
\pi
.
The theory of
C^{*}
-algebras shows that
\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{A}_{\pi}}=\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{B}}
for all
u\in\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
.
See
[
Ey64
]
for more on this.

Weak containment of group representations was developed by Fell
[
Fe60
]
and then Arsac
[
Ar76
]
, and the following theory is contained in their work.
A unitary representation
\sigma
of a locally compact group
G
is
weakly contained
in a unitary representation
\pi
of
G
if any one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(a)

\left\|\sigma(f)\right\|\leq\left\|\pi(f)\right\|
(the norms here are the operator norms on
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
);

(b)

every positive definite function
\left\langle\sigma(\cdot)\xi,\xi\right\rangle
(where
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
) associated to
\sigma
is the locally uniform limit of sums
\sum_{j}\left\langle\pi(\cdot)\xi_{j},\xi_{j}\right\rangle
of positive definite functions associated to
\pi
;

(c)

every
u\in\mathsf{A}_{\sigma}(G)
is the locally uniform limit of
v\in\mathsf{A}_{\pi}(G)
such that
\left\|v\right\|_{\mathsf{B}}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\mathsf{B}}
.

We write
\sigma\preceq\pi
if
\sigma
is weakly contained in
\pi
, and say that representations are weakly equivalent if each is weakly contained in the other.
Then every unitary representation
\pi
is weakly equivalent to the direct sum of all irreducible unitary representations
\sigma
that are weakly contained in
\pi
.

Discrete series

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary representation of a locally group
G
. Then
\pi
is unitarily equivalent to a subrepresentation of the regular representation of
G
if and only if one of its nontrivial matrix coefficients lies in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(G)
, or equivalently, all its matrix coefficients lie in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(G)
.
When any and hence all of these conditions hold, we say that
\pi
belongs to the
discrete series
of representations of
G
.

Tempered representations

We say that a representation
\sigma
of a locally compact group
G
is
tempered
if it is weakly contained in the regular representation of
G
.

If
H
is a closed subgroup of a locally compact group
G
, then we define
\bar{G}_{H,\mathrm{temp}}
to be the subcollection of
\bar{G}
of
H
-tempered unitary representations of
G
, that is, the representations of
G
whose restrictions to
H
are tempered on
H
.
The following lemma is obvious.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a locally compact group
G
and
\bar{G}_{H,\mathrm{temp}}
be as defined above.
The collection
\bar{G}_{H,\mathrm{temp}}
representations is closed under direct sums and integrals, and under taking tensor products with arbitrary unitary representations of
G
.
Every unitary representation of
G
that is weakly contained in an
H
-tempered representation of
G
is also
H
-tempered.
If
H_{1}\subseteq H_{2}
, then
\bar{G}_{H_{1},\mathrm{temp}}\supseteq\bar{G}_{H_{2},\mathrm{temp}}
.

Structure of semisimple Lie groups

Lie groups and their Lie algebras are denoted by upper case italic letters and the corresponding lower case fraktur letter.

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group.
Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a Cartan involution
\theta
, that is, a nontrivial Lie algebra isomorphism such that
\theta^{2}=1
, and
\mathfrak{g}
splits as a direct sum
\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the
+1
eigenspace and
\mathfrak{p}
is the
-1
eigenspace;
\mathfrak{k}
is a subalgebra but
\mathfrak{p}
is not.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a maximal commutative subspace of
\mathfrak{p}
, of dimension
r
, say; then the endomorphisms
\operatorname{ad}(H)
of
\mathfrak{g}
are simultaneously diagonalisable.
For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we define

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:\operatorname{ad}(H)X=\alpha(H)X\}.

A
(restricted) root

\alpha
is a nonzero element of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}
.
Then

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

where
\Sigma
is the root system formed by all the roots.

The inner product
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle:=\operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}(X)\operatorname{ad}(\theta Y))
on
\mathfrak{g}
enables us to identify
\mathfrak{a}
with
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and to put an inner product on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
Each element
\lambda
of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
or
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
gives rise to a homomorphism from
A
to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
:

\exp H\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(H)}.

The hyperplanes
\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:\alpha(H)=0\}
, where
\alpha\in\Sigma
, divide
\mathfrak{a}
into
Weyl chambers
.
We pick an arbitrary chamber, and say that it is positive; we write it as
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
.
We equip
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
with a partial order, by defining
\beta\leq\gamma
if and only if
\beta(H)\leq\gamma(H)
for all
H\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
.
We write
\Sigma^{+}
for the set of positive roots, that is, the roots
\alpha
such that
0\leq\alpha
.
We may choose a set
\Delta
of
simple positive roots
in
\Sigma^{+}
such that every positive root is a sum, with nonnegative integer coefficients, of roots in
\Delta
.
Let
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})\alpha
; then
\rho\in(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{+}
, the cone in
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
under the identification of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

The
Weyl group

W
is the finite group of orthogonal transformations of
\mathfrak{a}
generated by the reflections in the hyperplanes
\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:\alpha(H)=0\}
, where
\alpha\in\Sigma
; it acts simply transitively on the set of Weyl chambers.
There is an obvious induced action on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
In some cases,
W
contains
-I
(where
I
denotes the identity) and in some cases it does not.
When
G
is simple,
W
does not contain
-I
when the root system is of type
A_{r}
(where
r\geq 2
),
D_{r}
(where
r
is odd), and
E_{6}
; see
[
Hu72
, Exercise 5, page 71]
.

We define
\mathfrak{n}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
This is a Lie algebra because
[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}
.
The subgroup
K
of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
, and is connected; the subgroup
A
of
G
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{a}
is a maximal simply connected abelian subgroup; the subgroup
N
of
G
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent.
We write
A^{+}
for the image of
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
in
A
under the exponential map,
(A^{+})\overline{\phantom{x}}
for its closure in
A
, and
M
for the centraliser of
A
in
K
.
The groups
M
and
A
normalise
N
.

The continuous map
(k,a,k^{\prime})\mapsto kak^{\prime}
from
K\times(A^{+})\overline{\phantom{x}}\times K
to
G
is surjective, that is, every element
x
of
G
may be written in the form

x=kak^{\prime}

where
k,k^{\prime}\in K
and
a\in(A^{+})\overline{\phantom{x}}{}
.
If
x\in K
, then
a
is the identity, and there is lots of ambiguity in the choice of
k
and
k^{\prime}
; otherwise, there is less ambiguity.
In particular, for elements of the form
kak^{\prime}
where
a\in A^{+}
, the element
a
is uniquely determined while
kak^{\prime}=k^{\prime\prime}ak^{\prime\prime\prime}
if and only if there exists
m\in M
such that
k^{\prime\prime}=km
and
k^{\prime}=mk^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
This is known as the
Cartan decomposition
.
A Haar measure on
G
is given by

(6)

\int_{G}f(x)\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f(k\exp(H)k^{\prime})\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{w}(H)\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}k\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}H\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}k^{\prime},

where
\mathrm{w}(H)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}(\sinh\alpha(H))^{\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}}
, which may be rewritten as a weighted sum of exponentials.
The dominant term, that is, the biggest exponential on
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
, is
\mathrm{e}^{2\rho(H)}
.

The map
(k,a,n)\mapsto kan
from
K\times A\times N
to
G
is a diffeomorphism.
Hence we may write each
x
in
G
uniquely as a product
k(x)a(x)n(x)
, where
k(x)\in K
,
a(x)\in A
and
n(x)\in N
.
This is known as the
Iwasawa decomposition
of
G
.
The Haar measure on
G
may be written in terms of the Haar measures on
K
,
A
and
N
.

The subgroup
MAN
of
G
is called a
minimal parabolic subgroup
, and any closed subgroup of
G
that contains
MAN
is called a
parabolic subgroup
.
Every such subgroup
Q
may be parametrised by a subset
\Delta(Q)
of the set
\Delta
of simple positive roots:
at the Lie algebra level,
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{n}+\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{span}(\Delta(Q))}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
; we may write
Q
as
M_{Q}A_{Q}N_{Q}
where
M_{Q}
is reductive and contains
M
,
A_{Q}\subseteq A
and
N_{Q}\subseteq N
.
Further,
M_{Q}
and
A_{Q}
commute, and both normalise

.
Since
Q\supseteq P
, every element of
G
admits a (not necessarily not unique) decomposition of the form

, where
k\in K
and

.
For more details, see, for instance,
[
Kn86
, Section V.5]
.

Unitary representations

We recall that
K
has a maximal torus
T
, and the irreducible unitary representations of
K
are parametrised by a lattice in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, modulo the action of a Weyl group.
The inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
induces inner products and norms on
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{t}
and their dual spaces; we write
\left\|\tau\right\|
for the norm of the parameter in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
of
\tau
in
\hat{K}
.
We need two standard results of analysis on compact Lie groups.
See, for instance,
[
Ha15
, Theorem 7.43 and Proposition 10.6]
.

First, the (negative of the) Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{K}
on
K
is a canonical second order elliptic operator that acts by a scalar
C(\tau)
on the space of matrix coefficients of the irreducible representation
\tau
of
K
, and

(7)

c_{1}\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}\leq C(\tau)\leq c_{2}\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}.

Second, the Weyl dimension formula shows that

(8)

\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})\leq c_{3}\left\|\tau\right\|^{(\dim(K)-\dim(T))/2}.

\mathsf{L}^{q+}(G)

These representations have been considered extensively.
It has been long known that all matrix coefficients of all unitary representations of real-rank one groups lie in

Recently Samei and Wiersma

extended this to general semisimple groups, which served as a source of inspiration for this paper.
Essentially, they proved results similar to those in Section 3 of this paper, with “belonging to

The next corollary connects our results and theirs.
Recall that

Corollary 4.3

Suppose that

Restrictions of representations to subgroups

Much as immediately above, if we restrict a unitary representation of a semisimple group

5.
Afterthoughts

The estimates here arise from deep results of Harish-Chandra, Langlands and others, combined with functional analytic techniques.
The results of
[
CHH88
]
were used to prove that the algebras
\mathsf{E}_{(\lambda)}(G)
have faithful representations, but what was actually used is the measure theoretic
principe de majoration
of Herz
[
Hz70
]
, not the harder estimates of
[
C78
]
.

To explain heuristically why the functional analysis enables us to pass from results that hold in proper subcones of the cone
(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{+}
to results that hold globally, we recall (
18
):

\left\|\pi(f)\right\|=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle\left(\pi(f^{*}*f)^{(*n)}\right)\xi,\xi\right\rangle^{1/2n}.

For semisimple Lie groups, we have information on the behaviour of convolution powers of positive
\mathsf{L}^{1}(G)
-functions, going back to Oseledets
[
Os68
]
and Guivarc’h
[
Gu90
]
; this can be parlayed into information about convolution powers of arbitrary functions
f^{*}*f
.
In the special case of powers of the heat kernel, this information is very precise: see
[
AS82
]
.
The main point is that these powers
(f^{*}*f)^{(*n)}
concentrate in the radial directions, rather than spreading; indeed, in the Cartan decomposition, the bulk of the mass is near to
Ka_{n}K
, where
a_{n}
is a point in
A^{+}
of the form
\exp(nH)
;
H
depends on the “initial function”
f^{*}*f
.

The asymptotic behaviour of spherical functions shows that
\phi(\exp(nH))
behaves like a finite sum of terms of the form

p(nH)\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha-\rho)(nH)}
, where
p
is a polynomial.
As
(f^{*}*f)^{(*n)}
“concentrates” somewhat, we see heuristically that

\left(\int_{G}(f^{*}*f)^{(*n)}\phi_{\lambda}(x)\ignorespaces\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2n}

should converge, and the limit should be expressible in terms of
\alpha
and
H
.

There are global estimates for the spherical functions
\phi_{\lambda}
(see Theorem
2.5
), so Theorem B provides global estimates for matrix coefficients that have positive features of both the asymptotic expansion and the
\mathsf{L}^{q+}(G)
estimates; in particular, they hold everywhere in
G
.
For many irreducible representations, the spherical function parameter is sharp.
However, it is probably possible but quite difficult to prove sharper results.
In particular, for the group
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, estimates have been proved
[
BCNT22
]
for the matrix coefficients of almost all tempered representations in which the polynomial term of
\phi_{0}
does not appear.

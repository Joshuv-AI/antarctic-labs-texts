Construction and comparative study of Euler method with adaptive IQ and IMQ-RBFs
Samala Rathan, Deepit Shah

Abstract
The fundamental purpose of the present work is to constitute an enhanced Euler method with adaptive inverse-quadratic and inverse-multi-quadratic radial basis function (RBF) interpolation technique to solve initial value problems. These enhanced methods improve the local convergence of numerical solutions by utilizing a free parameter of radial basis functions. Consistency, convergence and stability analysis are provided to support our claims for each method. Numerical results show that the accuracy and rate of convergence of each proposed method are the same as the original Euler method or improved by making the local truncation error vanish; thus, the adaptive methods are optimal.



Keywords:
Finite Difference Method, Radial Basis Interpolation, Initial Value Problem, Order of accuracy, Rate of Convergence.

41A10, 65L05, 65L12, 65L20

Introduction

When numerically solving initial value problems (IVPs) for first-order ordinary differential equations, classical linear finite difference methods, such as Euler’s method, are first considered
[
1
]
. This linear approach yields a fixed order of accuracy as it is designed based on polynomial interpolation; that is, the accuracy order is calculated regardless of smoothness of the solution. If we know or can estimate the derivatives of the solution to a specific order, this information could be used to improve the local accuracy
[
2
]
. However, the conventional approach of polynomial interpolation lacks the same adaptability and versatility. Thus, this motivates us to study the radial basis function (RBF) interpolation-based modification of classical methods to improve their order and rate of convergence.

In
[
3
,
4
]
, the adaptive RBF solvers have been developed for solving ODEs such as Euler’s method, the midpoint method, and the Adams methods by replacing the polynomial basis with multi-quadratic and Gaussian RBFs respectively. The critical concept of adaptive RBF solvers is that the radial basis functions are defined with a free (shape) parameter which can be adjusted according to the local conditions of the solution. There is a possibility to determine such free parameter based on the local smoothness of the solution by substitution of the same for the polynomial basis, that is, derivatives, in order to terminate the leading truncation error terms. This idea can be seen in the applications of ENO and WENO for solving hyperbolic PDEs
[
5
,
6
]
using a non polynomial basis such as radial basis functions. The ENO and WENO finite difference methods are high-order accurate finite difference methods for problems with piecewise smooth solutions that contain discontinuities. The solution must be evaluated at each cell boundary in both methods, and the standard reconstruction is based on Lagrange interpolation
[
7
]
. Various approaches proposed in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
on finding the optimal values of the free parameter for the RBF approximation to get the accuracy or stability. However, there is no complete theory on how to choose the ‘right’ shape parameter value. The role is often decided by the essence of the issue under consideration.

The local truncation error analysis is essential for the numerical solution of IVPs, and our approach is to focus on the local truncation error. The best shape parameter removes the leading truncation error term(s) and meets the consistency requirement. Our adaptive shape parameter is optimal in this regard. However, it would be more appealing if there was another way to estimate the optimum shape parameter automatically, but such an automated algorithm does not exist. Including this, development of midpoint method and Adams methods with inverse quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic RBF methods for IVPs is our future interest.

In Section 2, we begin with adaptive inverse quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic RBF methods for IVPs. First, we develop inverse quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic RBF interpolation. In Section 3, the classical Euler’s method is modified to inverse quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic RBF variants. The shape parameter is locally optimized to make the leading error term(s) vanish to increase the order of the local truncation error and further increase the global order of accuracy. We derive the adaptive conditions for enhancing the order of accuracy with higher-order derivatives under the assumption that the solution is smooth. We also evaluate the optimal shape parameter value for higher-order accuracy. In Section 4, we show convergence and stability region for each proposed method. We compare the stability regions of each method i.e., the inverse-quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic version to the original Euler method. In Section 5, numerical results show that inverse quadratic and multi-quadratic RBF methods produce the desired order of accuracy. One method produce better accuracy than the other depending on the method and the problem. We limit our discussion to the case where the RBFs in question has only one shape parameter, though multiple shape parameters may be used for further improvement. The initial finite difference method is the limit case of the corresponding modified RBF method, one of the advantages of modified RBF methods. If the shape parameter is removed, the modified RBF method becomes equal to the original method. Thus, it concludes that even if the shape parameter does not fit or deviates from the optimal value, the modified RBF methods exhibit at least the same convergence rate as the original methods. As a consequence, the order of the modified methods can only get better. Finally, in Section 6, we give some concluding remarks.

RBF Interpolation

Let us consider RBF interpolation in one-dimension. Given
(N+1)
distinct data points
(x_{0},u_{0}),...,(x_{N},u_{N})
with
u_{k}
the value of the unknown function
u(x)
at
x=x_{k}
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We use the RBFs,
\phi_{k}(x)=\phi\big(|x-x_{k}|,\epsilon_{k}\big)
, where
\epsilon_{k}
is a shape parameter, to find an interpolant based on the given
(N+1)
data points. The value of
\epsilon_{k}
can vary over
x_{k}
. The interpolant
r(x)
takes the form of a weighted sum of RBFs

r(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\lambda_{k}\phi\big(|x-x_{k}|,\epsilon_{k}\big)

(2.1)

where
\lambda_{k}
are the unknown expansion parameters to be determined.
Using interpolation restraints

r(x_{k})=u_{k},k=0,1,...,N,

the expansion coefficients
\lambda_{k}
satisfy the following linear system

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi(|x_{0}-x_{0}|,\epsilon_{0})&\phi(|x_{0}-x_{1}|,\epsilon_{1})&....&\phi(|x_{0}-x_{N}|,\epsilon_{N})\\
\phi(|x_{1}-x_{0}|,\epsilon_{0})&\phi(|x_{1}-x_{1}|,\epsilon_{1})&....&\phi(|x_{1}-x_{N}|,\epsilon_{N})\\
...&...&....&....\\
...&...&....&....\\
...&...&....&....\\
\phi(|x_{N}-x_{0}|,\epsilon_{0})&\phi(|x_{N}-x_{1}|,\epsilon_{1})&....&\phi(|x_{N}-x_{N}|,\epsilon_{N})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
.\\
\lambda_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
.\\
u_{N}\end{pmatrix}.

Until and unless specified explicitly, we consider all the shape parameters
\epsilon_{k}
are same, i.e.,
\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon
, for all
k
. In this paper, we use inverse multi-quadratic (IMQ) and inverse quadratic (IQ)-RBFs.

2.1
Inverse Multi-Quadratic RBF Interpolation

The inverse multi-quadratic radial basis function
\phi_{k}(x)
is
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{k}^{2}(x-x_{k})^{2}}}
. Consider the interpolant for
N=1
in (
2.1
) is given by

r(x)=\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\phi_{0}(x)+\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x).

Using the interpolation condition
r(x_{k})=u_{k},k=0,1,
the interpolation matrix becomes a symmetric matrix with all diagonal entries
1
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{pmatrix},

where
h=x_{1}-x_{0}.
Solving for
\lambda_{k},k=0,1
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}\bigg(u_{0}-\frac{u_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}}\bigg),

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}\bigg(u_{1}-\frac{u_{0}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}}\bigg).

Differentiating the interpolant
r(x)
with respect to
x
, we get

\frac{d}{dx}r(x)=-\frac{\lambda_{0}(x-x_{0})\epsilon^{2}}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}(x-x_{0})^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}(x-x_{1})\epsilon^{2}}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}(x-x_{1})^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}},

and evaluating at
x=x_{0}
, we have

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\frac{\lambda_{1}\epsilon^{2}h}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(2.2)

Substituting the value of
\lambda_{1}
in (
2.2
), we have

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\frac{u_{1}\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}-u_{0}}{(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})h},

(2.3)

and as
\epsilon\to 0
, the equation (
2.3
) become

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\frac{u_{1}-u_{0}}{h}.

Thus, the method reduces to forward difference formula of
u^{\prime}_{x}
at
x=x_{0}.

2.2
Inverse Quadratic RBF Interpolation

The inverse quadratic radial basis function is
\phi_{k}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\epsilon_{k}^{2}(x-x_{k})^{2}}
. Then, for
N=1
in (
2.1
) and using the interpolation condition on the interpolant

r(x)=\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\phi_{0}(x)+\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x),

the interpolation matrix becomes a symmetric matrix with all diagonal entries 1, which is

\begin{pmatrix}1&\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Solving for
\lambda_{k},k=0,1
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\frac{(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})}{\epsilon^{2}h^{2}(2+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})}\bigg((1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})u_{0}-u_{1}\bigg),

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})}{\epsilon^{2}h^{2}(2+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})}\bigg((1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})u_{1}-u_{0}\bigg).

Differentiating
r(x)
with respect to
x
, we get

\frac{d}{dx}r(x)=-2\frac{\lambda_{0}(x-x_{0})\epsilon^{2}}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}(x-x_{0})^{2}})^{2}}-2\frac{\lambda_{1}(x-x_{1})\epsilon^{2}}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}(x-x_{1})^{2}})^{2}}.

(2.4)

At
x=x_{0}
in (
2.4
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=2\frac{\lambda_{1}\epsilon^{2}h}{{(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}})^{2}}.

(2.5)

Substituting value of
\lambda_{1}
in (
2.5
), we get

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\dfrac{2\bigg(u_{1}(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})-u_{0}\bigg)}{\bigg(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}\bigg)\bigg(2+\epsilon^{2}h^{2}\bigg)h},

(2.6)

and letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we get

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\frac{u_{1}-u_{0}}{h}.

Note that, again the method reduces to forward difference formula of
u^{\prime}_{x}
at
x=x_{0}
.

3
Adaptive RBF Euler Method for IVPs

We consider the initial value problem of the form

\dfrac{du}{dt}=f(t,u),\,\,a\leq t\leq b,

(3.1)

with initial condition

u(a)=u_{0},

(3.2)

where we assume
u(t)\in C^{\infty}[a,b]
and
f(t,u)
is a class of
C^{\infty}
function. We divide the interval
[a,b]
in uniform way
t_{n}=a+nh,

n=0,1,...,N,
where
h=\dfrac{b-a}{N}
is the grid size. In this section, we derive the adaptive inverse multi-quadratic, inverse quadratic RBF Euler methods and their modifications.

3.1
Consistency of adaptive IMQ-RBF Euler Method

Substitute
\epsilon=\epsilon_{n}
,
u_{1}=u_{n+1}
,
u_{0}=u_{n}
in (
2.3
), we get

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\frac{u_{n+1}\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}-u_{n}}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})h}.

(3.3)

Now, we approximate
\dfrac{du}{dt}
in (
3.1
) by the first derivative of inverse multi-quadratic RBF interpolant given in (
3.3
) at each discrete mesh point
t_{n}
,
n=0,1,...,N,
we obtain

u_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}},

(3.4)

where
f_{n}=f(t_{n},u_{n})
. This method differs from the original Euler method. Local truncation error is defined as the error of the method over a time step, normalized by
h.
Then the local truncation error,
\tau_{n}
, of the RBF Euler’s method is

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n+1}-\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}}{h}.

Using Taylor series expansion about a point
t=t_{n}
yields

\tau_{n}=h\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)+h^{2}\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}+\frac{u_{n}^{\prime}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)+h^{3}\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{4}}{24}-\frac{3u_{n}}{8}\epsilon_{n}^{4}\bigg)+h^{4}\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{5}}{120}-\frac{3u_{n}^{\prime}}{8}\epsilon_{n}^{4}\bigg)+O(h^{5}).

(3.5)

Notice that the modified Euler’s method with the IMQ-RBF still yields the first order accuracy as the leading error term is of
O(h)
. However, the coefficient of the first order term is not uniquely determined as it contains the free parameter
\epsilon_{n}.

O(h^{2})
: If we allow the leading error term to be zero, then we can eliminate the first term in the truncation error so that we arrive at second order of convergence. Thus,

\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}=0,
yields

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{n}}.

(3.6)

Note that
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
indexed by
n
, therefore the value
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
varies with
n
as the solution does.

O(h^{3})
: If we allow the two leading error terms to be zero, then we could arrive at third order of convergence. Thus

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)h+\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}+\frac{u_{n}^{\prime}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)h^{2}=0,

yields

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\displaystyle\dfrac{3u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{3(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})}.

(3.7)

As step size
h\to 0
, we get

\lim_{h\to 0}\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\lim_{h\to 0}-\displaystyle\dfrac{3u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{3(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})}\\
=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{n}},

(3.8)

which is identical to the value of
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
in (
3.6
).

O(h^{4})
: Similarly to achieve fourth of convergence, we annihilate the first three terms in the truncation error (
3.5
). Thus

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)h+\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}+\frac{u_{n}^{\prime}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)h^{2}+\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{4}}{24}-\frac{3u_{n}}{8}\epsilon_{n}^{4}\bigg)h^{3}=0,

produce

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\frac{6(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})\pm\sqrt{36(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})^{2}+9h^{2}u_{n}(12u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+4hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}+h^{2}u_{n}^{4})}}{9h^{2}u_{n}}.

(3.9)

Now, this equation (
3.9
) provide two values for
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
. These are

\epsilon_{n}^{2+}=\frac{6(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})-\sqrt{36(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})^{2}+9h^{2}u_{n}(12u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+4hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}+h^{2}u_{n}^{4})}}{9h^{2}u_{n}},

(3.10)

\epsilon_{n}^{2-}=\frac{6(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})+\sqrt{36(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})^{2}+9h^{2}u_{n}(12u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+4hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}+h^{2}u_{n}^{4})}}{9h^{2}u_{n}}.

(3.11)

Now, we derive a condition on consistency for
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}
and
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}
based on the value of
u_{n}
is non-negative and non-positive.
Let consider the first case as
u_{n}>0,u_{n}\not=0.
Upon checking for consistency,

\lim_{h\to 0}\epsilon_{n}^{2+}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{6(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})-\sqrt{36(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})^{2}+9h^{2}u_{n}(12u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+4hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}+h^{2}u_{n}^{4})}}{9h^{2}u_{n}}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{n}}

(3.12)

and

\lim_{h\to 0}h^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2-}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{6(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})+\sqrt{36(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})^{2}+9h^{2}u_{n}(12u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+4hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}+h^{2}u_{n}^{4})}}{9h^{2}u_{n}}=\frac{4}{3},

(3.13)

Thus
\tau_{n}\to 0
as
h\to 0
for
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}
and
\tau_{n}\not\to 0
as
h\to 0
for
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}
. This concludes that the consistency holds for
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}
which is similar to the condition (
3.6
) but not for
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}
.
Similarly, for the second case
u_{n}<0,u_{n}\not=0
, we arrive at
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}
is not consistent and
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}
is consistent. We can repeat this procedure for achieving fifth and higher-order accuracy but the algebraic procedure become complicated to determine the optimal shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
. The conjecture stated in
[

]
that there exists at least one shape parameter for the consistency to any order of accuracy and it is main reason why RBF approximations yields spectral accuracy. We do not provide much details here, interested readers can look at
[

We have derived modified Euler method (
3.4
), however, these have some technical difficulties.

•

The square root is involved in the denominator of (
3.4
) in the approximation of

and in some extreme cases, this value may become complex but the solution is real.

•

To achieve simplest second order of accuracy, the second derivative is involved in the optimal shape parameter

•

The optimal shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}

To avoid these situations, we further derive adaptive Euler method based on (
3.4
) as follows. The equation (
3.4

u_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}.

(3.14)

Now to avoid the square roots in the approximation, we use Taylor’s expansion on
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}
as

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}\approx 1-\displaystyle\dfrac{\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}{2}+O(h^{4}).

(3.15)

Using (
3.15
) in (
3.14
) and ignoring higher order terms in expansion, we obtain the modified adaptive Euler method as

u_{n+1}=\bigg(1-\displaystyle\dfrac{\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}{2}\bigg)\bigg((1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}\bigg).

(3.16)

Local truncation error at point
t=t_{n}
for (
3.16

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\tau_{n}=&\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n+1}-\bigg(1-\dfrac{\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}{2}\bigg)\bigg((1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}\bigg)}{h}\\
=&\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\bigg)h+\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}\bigg)h^{2}+O(h^{3}).\end{aligned}

For
O(h^{2})
accuracy, we arrive at condition on the optimal shape parameter as

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{n}}.

(3.17)

If we replace the second derivative from the relation
u_{n}^{\prime}=f_{n}
,
u_{n}^{\prime\prime}=f_{n}^{\prime}
and estimating the same using backward difference formula
\displaystyle\frac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{h}
, we get

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\dfrac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{hu_{n}}.

(3.18)

Therefore, we obtain adaptive modified Euler method is of the form

u_{n+1}=\bigg(1+\dfrac{h(f_{n}-f_{n-1})}{2u_{n}}\bigg)\bigg(\bigg(1-\dfrac{h^{2}(f_{n}-f_{n-1})}{u_{n}}\bigg)f_{n}+u_{n}\bigg).

(3.19)

Finally, to avoid the situation of denominator in (
3.6

\displaystyle\text{C1}:\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\begin{cases}0,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,u_{n}=0,\\
-\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{n}},\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\,u_{n}\neq 0,\end{cases}

(3.20)

and the second one is finite difference limit

\displaystyle\text{C2}:\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\begin{cases}\text{sgn}\bigg((f_{n}-f_{n-1})hu_{n}\bigg)L,&\text{if}\,\,\,|u_{n}|\leq h^{p},\\
-\dfrac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{hu_{n}},&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(3.21)

where

is a nonnegative number, sgn is the sign function and

Note that the condition C2 is the finite difference approximation of C1. For further details on these conditions, refer

4

3.2
Consistency of adaptive IQ-RBF Euler Method

In following similar lines as consistency analysis for IMQ-RBF Euler method, we get from (
2.6
), an adaptive Euler method based on inverse quadratic radial basis function as

\frac{d}{dx}r(x_{0})=\dfrac{2\bigg((1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})u_{n+1}-u_{n}\bigg)}{h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})\big(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}\big)}.

(3.22)

Thus, the approximation for (
3.1
) from (
3.22
) is

u_{n+1}=\dfrac{h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})f_{n}+2u_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}.

(3.23)

Then the local truncation error
\tau_{n}

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n+1}-\displaystyle\frac{h(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})f_{n}+2u_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon^{2}h^{2})}}{h}.

(3.24)

Using Taylor series expansion about point
t=t_{n}
, we get

\tau_{n}=h\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+u_{n}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)+h^{2}\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}-\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\bigg)+h^{3}\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}^{4}}{24}-u_{n}\epsilon_{n}^{4}\bigg)+h^{4}\bigg(\dfrac{u_{n}^{5}}{120}-\dfrac{3u_{n}^{\prime}}{2}\epsilon_{n}^{4}\bigg)+O(h^{5}).

(3.25)

Notice that the modified Euler’s method with the IQ-RBF still yields the first order accuracy
as the leading error term is of
O(h)
.

O(h^{2}):
The optimal value of shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
for second order of convergence is given by

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2u_{n}}.

(3.26)

O(h^{3}):
The optimal value of shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
for third order of convergence is given by

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\displaystyle\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}}{u_{n}-\displaystyle\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}},

\lim_{h\to 0}\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\lim_{h\to 0}-\displaystyle\dfrac{3u_{n}^{\prime\prime}+hu_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{3(u_{n}+hu_{n}^{\prime})}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2u_{n}}.

(3.28)

O(h^{4}):
For fourth order of convergence, we obtain two optimal shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
which are

\epsilon_{n}^{2+}=\displaystyle\dfrac{(u_{n}-\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime}}{2})-\sqrt{(u_{n}-\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime}}{2})^{2}+4(h^{2}u_{n})(\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+h\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}+h^{2}\dfrac{u_{n}^{4}}{24}}}{2h^{2}u_{n}},

(3.29)

\epsilon_{n}^{2-}=\displaystyle\dfrac{(u_{n}-\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime}}{2})+\sqrt{(u_{n}-\dfrac{hu_{n}^{\prime}}{2})^{2}+4(h^{2}u_{n})(\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}+h\dfrac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}+h^{2}\dfrac{u_{n}^{4}}{24}}}{2h^{2}u_{n}}.

(3.30)

Checking the consistency for
u_{n}>0,u_{n}\not=0
, we get

\lim_{h\to 0}\epsilon_{n}^{2+}=\frac{-u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2u_{n}},

(3.31)

and
\tau_{n}\to 0
as
h\to 0
. Thus, consistency holds for
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}.

Now, for

\lim_{h\to 0}h^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2-}=1,

(3.32)

which leads
\tau_{n}\not\to 0
as
h\to 0
. Thus, consistency does not holds for
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}.
Similiarly,
\epsilon_{n}^{2+}
is not consistent for
u_{n}<0
and
\epsilon_{n}^{2-}
is consistent for
u_{n}<0.

Again, we arrived at conditions

•

To achieve at least second order of accuracy, the second derivative is involved in the optimal shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
and should be estimated.

•

The optimal shape parameter
\epsilon_{n}^{2}
is given in fraction and the situation may encounter for denominator in (
3.6
) become zero,

and to remedy that, we propose modified method for

u_{n+1}=\frac{h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})f_{n}+2u_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}.

(3.33)

Using Taylor expansion of
\dfrac{1}{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}\approx 1-\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}+O(h^{4}),
and ignoring higher order terms, the equation (
3.33
) become

u_{n+1}=(1-\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})\bigg(\frac{h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})f_{n}+2u_{n}}{2}\bigg).

(3.34)

From the local truncation error at point
t=t_{n}
, we get

\tau_{n}=\bigg(u_{n}\epsilon_{n}^{2}+\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\bigg)h+\bigg(\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{6}-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}{2}\bigg)h^{2}+O(h^{3}).

(3.35)

For
O(h^{2})
,

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\frac{u_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{2u_{n}}.

(3.36)

Given
u_{n}^{\prime}=f_{n}
and therefore
u_{n}^{\prime\prime}=f_{n}^{\prime}
. Using backward difference formula for
f_{n}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\dfrac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{h}
, we obtain optimal shape parameter as

\epsilon_{n}^{2}=-\dfrac{(f_{n}-f_{n-1})}{2hu_{n}}.

(3.37)

Thus, we have adaptive modified IQ-RBF Euler method

u_{n+1}=\displaystyle\bigg(1+\dfrac{(f_{n}-f_{n-1})h}{2u_{n}}\bigg)\bigg(\dfrac{h}{2}\bigg(1-\dfrac{(f_{n}-f_{n-1})h}{2u_{n}}\bigg)\bigg(2-\dfrac{(f_{n}-f_{n-1})h}{2u_{n}}\bigg)f_{n}+u_{n}\bigg).

(3.38)

To avoid the denominator become zero, the condition C1 in (
3.20
) is same for IQ-RBF whereas the condition C2 become

\displaystyle\text{C2}:\epsilon_{n}^{2}=\begin{cases}\text{sgn}\bigg((f_{n}-f_{n-1})2hu_{n}\bigg)L&\text{if}\,\,\,|u_{n}|\leq h^{p},\\
-\dfrac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{2hu_{n}}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.39)

4
Stability and Convergence

In this section, we provide the convergence and stability of proposed RBF Euler method. In
[
12
]
, it is shown that the linear method is convergent if it is consistent and convergence. However, the proposed methods are not linear, thus we cannot
apply this technique but need to explicitly show the convergence and stability of each derived method.

4.1
Adaptive IMQ-RBF Euler method

The adaptive IMQ-RBF Euler method (
3.14
) takes the form

\displaystyle u_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}.

On simplifying, we get

\displaystyle u_{n+1}=\sqrt{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}hf_{n}+\frac{u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}},

and the truncation error is

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n+1}-\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}}{h}.

The exact solution satisfies

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+v_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}+h\tau_{n}.

On simplification, we get

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=\sqrt{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}hf_{n}+\frac{v_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}+h\tau_{n}.

Subtracting
u_{n+1}
from
v_{n+1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{n+1}=&\lvert v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}\rvert\\
=&\bigg\lvert\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}(v_{n}-u_{n})+h\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}[f(t_{n},v_{n})-f(t_{n},u_{n})]+h\tau_{n}\bigg\rvert,\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}\lvert(v_{n}-u_{n})\rvert+h\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}\lvert f(t_{n},v_{n})-f(t_{n},u_{n})\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert,\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}E_{n}+hL_{f}\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}\lvert v_{n}-u_{n}\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert,\\
=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2}}}\displaystyle(1+h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})E_{n}+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert.\end{aligned}

Here, we assume that the function
f(t,u)
is Lipschitz continuious in
u
,

\lvert f(t_{n},v_{n})-f(t_{n},u_{n})\rvert\leq L_{f}\lvert v_{n}-u_{n}\rvert=L_{f}E_{n}.

Using Induction process, we can show that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{n}\leq&\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{j}^{2}h^{2}}}\displaystyle(1+h(1+\epsilon_{j}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})E_{0}\\
&+h\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\prod_{m=j}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{m}^{2}h^{2}}}\displaystyle(1+h(1+\epsilon_{m}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})^{n-j}\lvert\tau_{j-1}\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n-1}\rvert.\end{aligned}

Consider
N
number of time steps needed to reach time b, that is,
N=(b-a)/h
and set

{\lvert\lvert\tau\rvert\rvert}_{\infty}=\underset{0\leq n\leq N-1}{\mathrm{max}}\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert.

Assuming initial condition is exact, we get
E_{0}=0
. Therefore, as
h\to 0
,

E_{n}\leq e^{L_{f}T}T{\lvert\lvert\tau\rvert\rvert}_{\infty}.

This is valid for all
n
which satisfy the condition
nh\leq T=b-a
. This proves that the adaptive IMQ-RBF Euler method is convergent.

4.2
Adaptive IQ-RBF Euler method

The adaptive IQ-RBF Euler method (
3.33
) takes the form

\displaystyle u_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+2u_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}.

On further simplification, we get

\displaystyle u_{n+1}=\frac{(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})},

and the truncation error is

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\displaystyle\dfrac{u_{n+1}-\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+2u_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}}{h}.

The exact solution satisfies

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}+2v_{n}}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}+h\tau_{n}

On simplification, we get

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=\frac{(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})hf_{n}}{2}+\frac{v_{n}}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}+h\tau_{n}.

Subtracting
u_{n+1}
from
v_{n+1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{n+1}=&\lvert v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}\rvert\\
=&\bigg\lvert\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}(v_{n}-u_{n})+\frac{h(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}{2}[f(t_{n},v_{n})-f(t_{n},u_{n})]+h\tau_{n}\bigg\rvert,\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}\lvert(v_{n}-u_{n})\rvert+\frac{h(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}{2}\lvert f(t_{n},v_{n})-f(t_{n},u_{n})\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert,\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}E_{n}+hL_{f}\frac{(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}{2}\lvert v_{n}-u_{n}\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert,\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}\displaystyle(2+h(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})\bigg)E_{n}+h\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert.\end{aligned}

By induction principlie,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{n}\leq&\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})}\displaystyle(2+h(1+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{n}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})E_{0}\\
&+h\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\prod_{m=j}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2(1+\epsilon_{m}^{2}h^{2})}\displaystyle(2+h(1+\epsilon_{m}^{2}h^{2})(2+\epsilon_{m}^{2}h^{2})L_{f})^{n-j}\lvert\tau_{j-1}\rvert+h\lvert\tau_{n-1}\rvert.\end{aligned}

Set
{\lvert\lvert\tau\rvert\rvert}_{\infty}=\underset{0\leq n\leq N-1}{\mathrm{max}}\lvert\tau_{n}\rvert,

E_{0}=0
and as
h\to 0
, we get

E_{n}\leq e^{L_{f}T}T{\lvert\lvert\tau\rvert\rvert}_{\infty},

which is valid for all
n
with
nh\leq T.
This proves that adaptive IQ-RBF Euler method is convergent.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed two enhanced Euler methods to solve initial value problems. These methods seek the approximation using inverse-quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic radial basis functions interpolation. The RBF functions have a free shape parameter and by optimising this, the modified Euler methods yields more accurate results in comparison to the classical Euler ODE solver. The consistency, convergence analysis and stability regions are studied. This is the preliminary study of the shape parameters for the inverse-quadratic and inverse multi-quadratic radial basis functions and extension to the multi-quadratic and Gaussian-RBFs
[
3
,
4
]
. Further research is going on that, how to choose the best performing shape parameter and how the modified methods do work for more complicated ODEs and PDEs.

Abstract
. Rigidity results are obtained for Riemannian d d -manifolds with sec ⩾ 1 \sec\geqslant 1 and spherical rank at least d − 2 > 0 d-2>0 . Conjecturally, all such manifolds are locally isometric to a round sphere or complex projective space with the (symmetric) Fubini–Study metric. This conjecture is verified in all odd dimensions, for metrics on d d -spheres when d ≠ 6 d\neq 6 , for Riemannian manifolds satisfying the Rakić duality principle, and for Kählerian manifolds.

Introduction

A complete Riemannian
d
-manifold
M
has
extremal curvature

\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}
if its sectional curvatures satisfy
\sec\leqslant\epsilon
or
\sec\geqslant\epsilon

with extremal curvature
\epsilon

is defined as the maximal number of linearly independent, orthogonal, and parallel vector fields

along
\gamma(t)

has (hyperbolic, Euclidean or spherical according as
\epsilon
is
-1,0
or 1) rank at least

k

Riemannian manifolds with
\sec\leqslant\epsilon
and admitting positive rank are known to be rigid.
Finite volume Riemannian manifolds with bounded nonpositive sectional curvatures and positive Euclidean rank are locally reducible or locally isometric to symmetric spaces of nonpositive curvature
[
1
,
6

. Generalizations include
[
11
]
and
[

\sec\leqslant-1
and positive hyperbolic rank are locally isometric to negatively curved symmetric spaces
[

]
; this fails in infinite volume
[
8
]
. Finally, closed Riemannian manifolds with
\sec\leqslant 1
and positive spherical rank are locally isometric to positively curved, compact, rank one symmetric spaces
[
25
]
.

Rank rigidity results are less definitive in the
\sec\geqslant\epsilon
curvature settings. Hyperbolic rank rigidity results for manifolds with
-1\leqslant\sec\leqslant 0
first appeared in
[
9
]
. Finite volume
3

\sec\geqslant-1
and positive hyperbolic rank are real hyperbolic
[
23
]
. Complete Riemannian
3

\sec\geqslant 0
and positive Euclidean rank have reducible universal coverings as a special case of
[

Our present focus is the curvature setting
\sec\geqslant 1

\sec\geqslant 1
and positive spherical rank are locally isometric to positively curved symmetric spaces. Note that the simply connected, compact, rank one symmetric spaces, normalized to have minimum sectional curvature 1, have spherical rank:
n-1=\dim(S^{n})-1
for the spheres;
2n-2=\dim(\mathbb{CP}^{n})-2
for complex projective space;
4n-4=\dim(\mathbb{HP}^{n})-4
for quaternionic projective space;
8=\dim(\mathbb{OP}^{2})-8
for the Cayley projective plane.
Our main theorems concern
d
-manifolds with spherical rank at least

It is instructive to compare the

case of rank-rigidity resolved in

contributing to its rank . Hence, for each

is contained in the singular set for

first singularities
of exponential maps when
\sec\leqslant 1

and

of exponential maps. Concerning

is globally symmetric

An alternative definition for the spherical rank of a geodesic

along

that make curvature one with

but not when defined in terms of parallel fields by Corollary

and positive rank when defined in terms of Jacobi fields

To describe our methods and the organization of the paper, let
\mathcal{I}=\{p\in M\,|\,\sec_{p}\equiv 1\}
and
\mathcal{O}=M\setminus\mathcal{I}
denote the subsets of isotropic and nonisotropic points in
M
, respectively. The goal is to prove that
M
is locally isometric to complex projective space when
\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset.

We start with a pointwise analysis of curvature one planes. Given a vector
v\in S_{p}M
, let
E_{v}
denote the span of all vectors
w
orthogonal to
v
with
\sec(v,w)=1
and let
D_{v}
denote the subspace of
E_{v}
spanned by vectors contributing to the rank of the geodesic
\gamma_{v}(t)
. The assignments
v\mapsto E_{v}
and
v\mapsto D_{v}
define two (possibly singular) distributions on each unit tangent sphere
S_{p}M
, called the
eigenspace
and
spherical
distributions, respectively (see
2.7
and
3.1
). The spherical rank assumption ensures that
d-2=\dim(S_{p}M)-1\leqslant\dim(D_{v})
for each
v\in S_{p}M
so that both distributions are of codimension at most one on
S_{p}M
.

The arrangement of curvature one planes at nonisotropic points
p
encodes what ought to be a complex structure, a source of rigidity. More precisely, the eigenspace distribution on
S_{p}M
is
totally geodesic
(see Lemma
2.12
) and of codimension at most one. Subsection
2.3
builds on earlier work of Hangan and Lutz
[
13
]
where they exploited the fundamental theorem of projective geometry to prove that codimension one totally geodesic distributions on odd dimensional spheres are
algebraic
: there is a nonsingular projective class
[A]
of skew-symmetric linear maps of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the property that the distribution is orthogonal to the Killing (line) field on
S^{n}
generated by
[A]
. In particular, such distributions are projectively equivalent to the standard contact hyperplane distribution. Note that when
M
is complex projective space, with complex structure
J:TM\rightarrow TM
, the codimension one eigenspace distribution on
S_{p}M
is orthogonal to the Killing (line) field on
S_{p}M
generated by
[J_{p}]
.

As the spherical distribution
D
is invariant under parallel transport along geodesics (
D_{\dot{\gamma}_{v}(t)}=P_{t}(D_{v})
), its study leads to more global considerations in Section
3.1
. The sphere of radius
\pi
in
T_{p}M
is also equipped with a
kernel distribution
,
v\mapsto K_{v}:=\ker(\mathrm{d}(\exp_{p})_{v})
(see
2.4
). As each
w\in D_{v}
is an initial condition for an initially vanishing spherical Jacobi field along
\gamma_{v}(t)
, parallel translation in
T_{p}M
identifies the spherical subspace
D_{v}
with a subspace of
K_{\pi v}
for each
v\in S_{p}M
(see Lemma
3.6
). When
p\in\mathcal{O}
, the eigenvalue and spherical distributions on
S_{p}M
coincide (see Lemma
3.4
). As a consequence, the kernel distribution contains a totally geodesic subdistribution of codimension at most one on
S(0,\pi)
. It follows that
\exp_{p}
is constant on
S(0,\pi)
(see Corollary
3.7
) and that geodesics passing through nonisotropic points
p\in\mathcal{O}
are all closed (see Lemma
3.8
). Moreover, when
p\in\mathcal{O}
, each vector
v\in S_{p}M
has rank exactly
d-2
(see Lemma
3.12
), or putting things together, the eigenspace distribution is a
nonsingular
codimension one distribution on
S_{p}M
. As even dimensional spheres do not admit such distributions,
M
must have even dimension, proving Theorem
A
. More generally, this circle of ideas and a connectivity argument culminate in a proof that every vector in
M
has rank
d-2
when the nonisotropic set
\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset
(see Proposition
3.13
).

The remainder of the paper is largely based on curvature calculations in radial coordinates with respected to frames
adapted
to the spherical distributions that are introduced in Section
3.2
. An argument based on these calculations and the aforementioned fact that the spherical distributions are contact distributions, establishes that if the nonisotropic set
\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset
, then
M=\mathcal{O}
(see Proposition
3.14
). The proof of Theorem
B
follows easily and appears in Section
3.3
. The proof of Theorem
C
appears in Section
3.4
. There, the Rakić duality hypothesis is applied to prove that the family of skew-symmetric endomorphisms
A_{p}:T_{p}M\rightarrow T_{p}M
,
p\in M
, arising from the family of eigenspace distributions on the unit tangent spheres
S_{p}M
, define an almost complex structure on
M
(see Lemma
3.22
). This fact, combined with additional curvature calculations in adapted framings, allows us to deduce that
M
is Einstein, from which the theorem easily follows (see the proof of Proposition
3.21
).

Finally, Sections
4
and
5
contain the proofs of Theorem
D
in real dimension at least six and in real dimension four, respectively. The methods are largely classical, relying on pointwise curvature calculations based on the Kähler symmetries of the curvature tensor and on expressions for the curvature tensor when evaluated on an orthonormal
4
-frame due to Berger
[
2
,
17
]
. Essentially, these calculations yield formulas that relate the eigenvalues of the endomorphisms
A_{p}:T_{p}M\rightarrow T_{p}M
to the curvatures of eigenplanes in invariant four dimensional subspaces of
T_{p}M
. When the real dimension is at least six, there are enough invariant four dimensional subspaces to deduce that
M
has constant holomorphic curvatures, concluding the proof in that case. The argument in real dimension four proceeds differently by proving that
M
satisfies the Rakić duality principle. When this fails, the decomposition of
TM
into eigenplanes of

is shown to arise from a metric splitting of

, contradicting the curvature hypothesis
\sec\geqslant 1
.

This section contains preliminary results, mostly well-known, that are used in subsequent sections. Throughout
(M,\mathrm{g})

d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
\mathcal{X}(M)

\mathbb{R}

\nabla

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\{X\mathrm{g}(Y,Z)+Y\mathrm{g}(Z,X)-Z\mathrm{g}(X,Y)\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\{\mathrm{g}([X,Y],Z)-\mathrm{g}([Y,Z],X)+\mathrm{g}([Z,X],Y)\}.

The curvature tensor

R(X,Y)Z=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z

(2.2)

R(X,Y,Z,W)=-R(Y,X,Z,W)=R(Z,W,X,Y)

2.1.
Jacobi operators and eigenspace distributions.

Let
SM
denote the unit sphere bundle of
M
; its fiber above a point
p\in M
is the unit sphere
S_{p}M
in
T_{p}M
. For
v\in S_{p}M
define the Jacobi operator
\mathcal{J}_{v}:v^{\perp}\rightarrow v^{\perp}
by
\mathcal{J}_{v}(w)=R(w,v)v
. The symmetries (
2.2
) imply that
\mathcal{J}_{v}
is a well-defined self-adjoint linear map of
v^{\perp}
. Its eigenvalues encode the sectional curvatures of
2
-plane sections containing the vector
v
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v,w\in S_{p}M
be orthonormal vectors and assume that
\sec_{p}\geqslant\epsilon
for some
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\sec(v,w)=\epsilon

(2)

w
is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
with eigenvalue
\epsilon
.

(3)

R(w,v)v=\epsilon w

Proof.

Only
(1)\implies(2)
is nontrivial. If
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
, then
\lambda_{i}\geqslant\epsilon
for each index
i
. Express
w=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}e_{i}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}^{2}=1
. Then
\epsilon=\mathrm{g}(R(w,v)v,w)=\mathrm{g}(\mathcal{J}_{v}(w),w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}.
Conclude that
\alpha_{i}=0
for indices
i
with
\lambda_{i}>\epsilon
. Therefore
w
is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
with eigenvalue
\epsilon
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

An analogous proof works when
\sec_{p}\leqslant\epsilon
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
v,w\in S_{p}M
be orthonormal vectors. If
w^{\perp}\cap v^{\perp}
consists of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
, then
w
is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
. Consequently,
R(v,w,w^{\prime},v)=\mathrm{g}(\mathcal{J}_{v}(w),w^{\prime})=0
for any
w^{\prime}\in w^{\perp}\cap v^{\perp}
.

Proof.

The orthogonal complement to an invariant subspace of a self-adjoint operator is an invariant subspace.
∎

Equivalently, conjugate vectors and points are described in terms of Jacobi fields along
\gamma

Initial conditions

uniquely determine a normal Jacobi field. Let

,

, and

. The geodesic variation
\alpha(s,t)=\exp_{p}(t(v+sw))
of

has variational field

, a normal Jacobi field along
\gamma
with initial conditions

and

given by

(2.5)

J(t)=\mathrm{d}(\exp_{p})_{tv}(tw).

If

. In this case

is a conjugate vector and

is a conjugate point to

along
\gamma
. All initially vanishing normal Jacobi fields along

\gamma(a)

along

if and only if there exists a nonzero normal Jacobi field

along

.

For
\gamma(t)
a geodesic and

is the bilinear symmetric map defined by

I_{\gamma}^{t_{0}}(X,Y)=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathrm{g}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})-\mathrm{g}(R(X,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma},Y)\,dt.

The null space of

consists of normal Jacobi fields

along
\gamma(t)

[

, there exists

if and only if there exists

such that

is conjugate to
\gamma(0)
at time

. In particular, the property of being a first conjugate point along a geodesic segment is a symmetric property.

Lemma 2.14
.

\operatorname{FConj}(p)
is a closed subset of
T_{p}M
.

Proof.

Assume that

converge to

. Let

,

, and

is a conjugate vector, there exists a normal Jacobi field

along
\gamma_{w_{i}}(t)
with

and

. A subsequence of the Jacobi fields

converges to a nonzero Jacobi field

along
\gamma_{w}(t)
with

. Therefore

is a conjugate vector. If

such that

is a conjugate vector. Therefore there exists

An orthonormal framing

induces parallel orthonormal framings

2.3.
Codimension one totally geodesic distributions on spheres.

Given a non-zero skew-symmetric linear map
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
v\in S^{d-1}
, parallel translation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
identifies
v^{\perp}
and
T_{v}S^{d-1}
. As
A
is skew-symmetric and non-zero, the assignment
S^{d-1}\ni v\mapsto Av\in T_{v}S^{d-1}
defines a Killing field on
S^{d-1}
. Let
E_{v}=\operatorname{span}\{v,Av\}^{\perp}
denote the subspace of
T_{v}S^{d-1}
orthogonal to
Av
. Then
S^{d-1}\ni v\mapsto E_{v}\subset T_{v}S^{d-1}
defines a codimension one totally geodesic distribution on
S^{d-1}
with singular set
\mathcal{X}:=\{x\in S^{d-1}\,|\,E_{x}=T_{x}S^{d-1}\}=\ker(A)\cap S^{d-1}
as a consequence of the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
X
be a Killing field on a complete Riemannian manifold
(S,\mathrm{g})
. If a geodesic
c(t)
satisfies
\mathrm{g}(\dot{c},X)(0)=0
, then
\mathrm{g}(\dot{c},X)(t)\equiv 0
.

The skew-symmetric linear map
A
and each nonzero real multiple
rA
yield the same codimension one totally geodesic distribution
E
on
S^{n}
. In
[
13
]
, Hangan and Lutz apply the fundamental theorem of projective geometry to establish the following:

Theorem 2.16

(Hangan and Lutz)
.

Let
E
be a nonsingular codimension one totally geodesic distribution on a unit sphere
S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
d-1
is odd and there exists a nonsingular projective class
[A]\in PGL(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of skew-symmetric linear maps such that for each
x\in S^{d-1}
,
E_{x}=\operatorname{span}\{x,Ax\}^{\perp}
.

The elegance of their approach lies in the fact that no a priori regularity assumption is made, while a posteriori the distribution is algebraic. The following corollary is immediate (see
[
13
]
).

Corollary 2.17
.

A nonsingular codimension one totally geodesic distribution on an odd dimensional unit sphere is real-analytic and contact.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
E
be a nonsingular codimension one totally geodesic distribution on an odd dimensional unit sphere
S^{d-1}
. The line field
L
on
S^{d-1}
defined by
L=E^{\perp}
is totally geodesic if and only if
[A^{2}]=[-\operatorname{Id}]
, where
[A]
is as in Theorem
2.16
above.

Proof.

Assume that
[A^{2}]=[-\operatorname{Id}]
. Choose a representative
A\in[A]
with unit-modulus eigenvalues. Then
\|Av\|=1
and
A^{2}v=-v
for each
v\in S^{d-1}
. The geodesic
c(t)=\cos(t)v+\sin(t)Av
satisfies
\dot{c}(0)\in L_{v}
. Then
\dot{c}(t)\in L_{c(t)}
since
\dot{c}(t)=-\sin(t)v+\cos(t)Av=Ac(t)
, concluding the proof that
L
is totally geodesic.

Conversely, assume that
L
is totally geodesic. Let
v\in S^{d-1}
and choose a representative
A\in[A]
satisfying
\|Av\|=1
. The geodesic
c(t)=\cos(t)v+\sin(t)Av
satisfies
\dot{c}(t)\in L_{c(t)}
for each

. Set

and conclude that the
2
-plane spanned by
v
and

is invariant under

. As
\|Av\|=1
and

is skew-symmetric,

, concluding the proof.
∎

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x\in S^{d-1}\,|\,E_{x}=T_{x}S^{d}\}
denote the singular set for a codimension one totally geodesic distribution

on

Lemma 2.19
.

The singular set

satisfies

.

Proof.

There is nothing to prove if
\mathcal{X}=\emptyset
. If

, then

since each great circle through

also passes through

. It remains to prove that for linearly independent

, the great circle
C_{1}:=\operatorname{Sp}(\{x_{1},x_{2}\})\subset\mathcal{X}
.

If

is a subspace of

since

. Let

be any other line in

and let

denote the great circle containing

with tangent line

.

Let

are linearly independent, the tangent lines at

to the great circles in the totally geodesic

∎

Corollary 2.20
.

The singular set
\mathcal{X}
of a codimension one totally geodesic distribution on

does not contain a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following simple lemma is applied to Riemannian exponential maps in subsequent sections.

Lemma 2.21

Let

Let

. The assumption implies that

∎

. By Lemmas
4.5
and
4.9
,
\lambda_{2}<4
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

If
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
, then
A_{p}
has a single eigenvalue for each
p\in M
.

Proof.

If not, Lemma
4.11
implies that there exist constants
0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
and
A_{p}
-eigenspaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
such that
T_{p}M
is the orthogonal direct sum
T_{p}M=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
and
\|A_{p}(v_{i})\|=\mu_{i}
for each unit vector
v_{i}\in E_{i}
,
i=1,2
. As
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
, one of the two eigenspaces
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
has real dimension at least four.

Case I:

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(E_{1})\geqslant 4

Choose orthogonal
A_{p}
-invariant
2
-planes
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\subset E_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}\subset E_{2}
. Let
\lambda_{i}=\sec(\sigma_{i})
for each
i=1,2,3
. As
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
, Corollary
4.3
implies that
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}
. Apply Lemmas
4.5
and
4.9
to the four dimensional subspaces
\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{3}
and
\sigma_{2}\oplus\sigma_{3}
to deduce
\lambda_{1}<4
and
\lambda_{2}<4
. Apply Lemmas
4.4
and
4.9
to the four dimensional subspace
\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{2}
to deduce
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=8
, a contradiction.

Case II:

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(E_{2})\geqslant 4

Choose orthogonal
A_{p}
-invariant
2
-planes
\sigma_{1}\subset E_{1}
and
\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\subset E_{2}
. Let
\lambda_{i}=\sec(\sigma_{i})
for each
i=1,2,3
. As
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
, Corollary
4.3
implies that
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{3}
. Apply Lemmas
4.5
and
4.9
to the four dimensional subspaces
\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{3}
to deduce
\lambda_{2}>4
and
\lambda_{3}>4
. Applying Lemmas
4.4
and
4.9
to the four dimensional subspace
\sigma_{2}\oplus\sigma_{3}
to deduce
\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=8
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

When
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
, Theorem
D
is easily derived from Lemma
4.12
and Theorem
C
. This approach is taken when
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}=4
in the next section.

In the remainder of this section, a more elementary proof is presented for the case when
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
. This alternative proof is based on the well-known classification
[
14
,
16
]
of simply-connected Kählerian manifolds having constant holomorphic curvatures.

Corollary 4.14
.

A
2
-plane
\sigma\subset T_{p}M
is holomorphic if and only if
A_{p}(\sigma)=\sigma
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in M
and let
\sigma\subset T_{p}M
be a
2
-plane. If
A_{p}(\sigma)=\sigma
then
J_{p}(\sigma)=\sigma
by Corollary
4.10
. Conversely, assume that
J_{p}(\sigma)=\sigma
and let
v\in\sigma
be a nonzero vector. The
2
-plane
\bar{\sigma}=\operatorname{span}\{v,A_{p}v\}
is
A_{p}
-invariant by Lemma
4.12
. By Corollary
4.10
,
\bar{\sigma}
is
J_{p}
-invariant. As
v
lies in a unique holomorphic
2
-plane,
\sigma=\bar{\sigma}
, so that
\sigma
is
A_{p}
-invariant.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

If
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
, then
\lambda(v)=4
for every unit vector
v\in SM
.

Proof.

Given
v\in S_{p}M
, the
2
-plane
\sigma_{1}=\operatorname{span}\{v,A_{p}v\}
is
A_{p}
-invariant by Lemma
4.12
. As
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)\geqslant 6
, there exist orthogonal
A_{p}
-invariant
2
-planes
\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\subset\sigma_{1}^{\perp}
. Let
\lambda_{i}=\sec(\sigma_{i})
for
i=1,2,3
and note that
\lambda(v)=\lambda_{1}
.

Applying Lemmas
4.4
and
4.9
to the three four dimensional subspaces
\sigma_{i}\oplus\sigma_{j}
,
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
distinct, yields the linear system

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=8,

whose solution
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=4
is unique.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

A Kählerian manifold with
\sec\geqslant 1
, real dimension
d\geqslant 6
, and spherical rank at least
d-2
is isometric to a globally symmetric
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d/2}
with holomorphic curvatures equal to
4
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that all holomorphic
2
-planes in
M
have sectional curvature equal to four by
[
14
,
16
]
. Let
p\in M
and let
\sigma\subset T_{p}M
be a holomorphic
2
-plane. Let
v\in\sigma
be a nonzero vector. By Corollary
4.14
,
\sigma
is
A_{p}
-invariant, so that
\sec(\sigma)=\sec(v,A_{p}v)=\lambda(v)
. By Corollary
4.15
,
\lambda(v)=4
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
D
in real dimension four

This final section completes the proof of Theorem
D
, establishing its veracity when
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)=4
. The approach, alluded to in Remark
4.13
, is to appeal to Theorem
C
. The main step in proving that
M
satisfies the Rakić duality principle is to establish the analogue of Lemma
4.12
when
d=4
. The following lemma, likely well-known, is used for this purpose.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B
be an open connected subset of a Riemannian manifold
(M,\mathrm{g})
admitting a pair of orthogonal and totally geodesic foliations
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then
B
is locally isometric to the product
\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

Proof.

If
H=T\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
V=T\mathcal{F}_{2}
, then the tangent bundle splits orthogonally
TB=H\oplus V
. By de Rham’s splitting theorem, it suffices to prove that the distribution
H
is parallel on
B
. Let
h,\bar{h}
denote vector fields tangent to
H
and let
v,\bar{v}
denote vector fields tangent to
V
.

As
H
is integrable,
0=\mathrm{g}([h,\bar{h}],v),
implying
\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{h}\bar{h},v)=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{h}}h,v)
. As
H
is totally geodesic,
\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{h}\bar{h},v)=-\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{h}}h,v)
. Conclude that

(5.1)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{h}}h,v)=0.

Similarly, the fact that
V
is integrable and totally geodesic implies that
\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}}v,h)=0
. As
H
and
V
are orthogonal, this implies

(5.2)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}}h,v)=0.

By (
5.1
) and (
5.2
),
H
is parallel on
B
, concluding the proof.
∎

Recall from the proof of item (3) of Theorem
B
that there exists a smooth section
p\ni M\mapsto A_{p}\in SL(T_{p}M)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(M)=4
. Then for each
p\in M
,
A_{p}
has a single eigenvalue.

Proof.

If not, then there exists a metric ball
B
in
M
with the property that for each
b\in B
,
A_{b}
has two distinct eigenvalues. For each
b\in B
, there exist constants
0<\mu_{1}(b)<\mu_{2}(b)
and orthogonal eigenplanes
\sigma_{1}(b)
and
\sigma_{2}(b)
of
A_{b}
satisfying
\|A_{b}(v_{i})\|=\mu_{i}(b)
for each unit vector
v_{i}\in\sigma_{i}(b)
. As the
A_{b}
vary smoothly with
b\in B
, the functions
\mu_{i}:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the orthogonal splitting
TB=\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{2}
are both smooth. Define
\lambda_{i}:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\lambda_{i}(b)=\sec(\sigma_{i}(b))
for
i=1,2
.

After possibly reducing the radius of
B
, there exist smooth unit vector fields
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
on
B
tangent to
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
respectively. By Corollary
4.10
, the two
2
-plane fields
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are
J
-invariant. Therefore, letting
\bar{v}_{i}=Jv_{i}
, the smooth orthonormal framing
\{v_{1},\bar{v}_{1},v_{2},\bar{v}_{2}\}
of
TB
satisfies
\sigma_{i}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i},\bar{v}_{i}\}
for
i=1,2
. Define
\gamma:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\gamma=R(v_{2},v_{1},\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2})
. Again by Corollary
4.10
, the
A_{b}
-invariant
2
-planes
\sigma_{i}(b)
are
J_{b}
-invariant and by Lemma
4.9
,
\gamma=1
on
B
.

Corollary
4.3
, implies that
\lambda_{1}(b)<\lambda_{2}(b)
and Lemma
4.5
implies

(5.3)

\lambda_{1}(b)<4<\lambda_{2}(b)

for each
b\in B
.

The goal of the following calculations is to show that the orthogonal distributions
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are integrable and totally geodesic. As
J
is parallel,

(5.4)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{X}JY,Z)=\mathrm{g}(J\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=-\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{X}Y,JZ)

for all smooth vector fields
X,Y,Z
.

Use (
5.4
) to conclude

(5.5)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}v_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=-\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},v_{1}).

Use the differential Bianchi identity,

0=(\nabla_{v_{2}}R)(v_{1},\bar{v}_{1},v_{1},v_{2})+(\nabla_{v_{1}}R)(\bar{v}_{1},v_{2},v_{1},v_{2})+(\nabla_{\bar{v}_{1}}R)(v_{2},v_{1},v_{1},v_{2})

to derive

(5.6)

(\lambda_{1}-1)\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}v_{2},\bar{v}_{1})+3\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},v_{1})=0.

Use (
5.3
), (
5.5
), and (
5.6
) to conclude

(5.7)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}v_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},v_{1})=0.

Set
w_{1}:=\bar{v}_{1}
and
\bar{w}_{1}:=Jw_{1}=-v_{1}
. Repeating the above calculations with
w_{1}
and
\bar{w}_{1}
in place of
v_{1}
and
\bar{v}_{1}
, respectively, yields the following analogue of (
5.7
)

(5.8)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}v_{2},\bar{w}_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},w_{1})=0,

or equivalently,

(5.9)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}v_{2},v_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=0.

Set
w_{2}:=\bar{v}_{2}
and
\bar{w}_{2}:=Jw_{2}=-v_{2}
. Repeating the above calculations with
w_{2}
and
\bar{w}_{2}
in place of
v_{2}
and
\bar{v}_{2}
, respectively, yields the following analogues of (
5.7
) and (
5.9
)

(5.10)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{w_{2}}w_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{w_{2}}\bar{w}_{2},v_{1})=0,

and

(5.11)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{w_{2}}w_{2},v_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{w_{2}}\bar{w}_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=0,

or equivalently,

(5.12)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}_{2}}v_{2},v_{1})=0,

and

(5.13)

\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}_{2}}\bar{v}_{2},v_{1})=\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{\bar{v}_{2}}v_{2},\bar{v}_{1})=0.

The
2
-plane field
\sigma_{2}
is integrable and totally geodesic by (
5.7
), (
5.9
), (
5.12
), and (
5.13
).

Switching the roles of the indices
1
and
2
in the differential Bianchi calculation above, yields the following analogue of (
5.6
)

(5.14)

(\lambda_{2}-1)\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{1}}v_{1},\bar{v}_{2})+3\mathrm{g}(\nabla_{v_{1}}\bar{v}_{1},v_{2})=0.

Now, arguing as in the case of the
2
-plane field
\sigma_{2}
, the
2
-plane field
\sigma_{1}
is also integrable and totally geodesic. As the tangent
2
-plane fields
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are orthogonal, integrable, and totally geodesic,
B
is locally isometric to a Riemannian product by Lemma
5.1
. This contradicts the curvature assumption
\sec\geqslant 1
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

A Kählerian manifold with
\sec\geqslant 1
, real dimension
d=4
, and spherical rank at least
2
is isometric to a globally symmetric
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with holomorphic curvatures equal to
4
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
M
satisfies the Rakić duality principle by Theorem
C
.

Let
p\in M
and let
v,w\in S_{p}M
be a pair of orthonormal vectors. The Jacobi operator
\mathcal{J}_{v}
has two eigenspaces, namely the two-dimensional
1
-eigenspace
E_{v}
and the one-dimensional
\lambda(v)
-eigenspace spanned by the vector
A_{p}v
. Similarly, the Jacobi operator
\mathcal{J}_{w}
has a two-dimensional
1
-eigenspace
E_{w}
and a one-dimensional
\lambda(w)
-eigenspace spanned by
A_{p}w
.

If
w\in E_{v}
, then
v\in E_{w}
by Lemma
2.1
. If
w
lies in the
\lambda(v)
-eigenspace of
\mathcal{J}_{v}
, then
w
is a multiple of
A_{p}v
. By Proposition
5.2
the
2
-plane
\sigma:=\operatorname{span}\{v,w\}
is
A_{p}
-invariant, whence
\lambda(w)=\sec(\sigma)=\lambda(v)
and
v
lies in the
\lambda(w)
-eigenspace of
\mathcal{J}_{w}
.
∎

Together, Theorems
4.16
and
5.3
complete the proof of Theorem
D
.

Higher-order generalized- α \alpha methods for parabolic problems Journal: Arxiv
Pouria Behnoudfar, Quanling Deng, Victor M. Calo

Abstract
We propose a new class of high-order time-marching schemes with dissipation user-control and unconditional stability for parabolic equations. High-order time integrators can deliver the optimal performance of highly-accurate and robust spatial discretizations such as isogeometric analysis. The generalized- α \alpha method delivers unconditional stability and second-order accuracy in time and controls the numerical dissipation in the discrete spectrum’s high-frequency region. Our goal is to extend the generalized- a ​ l ​ p ​ h ​ a alpha methodology to obtain a high-order time marching methods with high accuracy and dissipation in the discrete high-frequency range. Furthermore, we maintain the stability region of the original, second-order generalized- a ​ l ​ p ​ h ​ a alpha method foe the new higher-order methods. That is, we increase the accuracy of the generalized- α \alpha method while keeping the unconditional stability and user-control features on the high-frequency numerical dissipation. The methodology solve k > 1 , k ∈ ℕ k>1,k\in\mathbb{N} matrix problems and updates the system unknowns, which correspond to higher-order terms in Taylor expansions to obtain ( 3 / 2 ​ k ) t ​ h (3/2k)^{th} -order method for even k k and ( 3 / 2 ​ k + 1 / 2 ) t ​ h (3/2k+1/2)^{th} -order for odd k k . A single parameter ρ ∞ \rho^{\infty} controls the dissipation, and the update procedure follows the formulation of the original second-order method. Additionally, we show that our method is A-stable and setting ρ ∞ = 0 \rho^{\infty}=0 allows us to obtain an L-stable method. Lastly, we extend this strategy to analyze the accuracy order of a generic method.

Introduction

Jansen et al. 2000
introduced the generalized-
\alpha
method for parabolic problems as a time-marching scheme, with second-order accuracy and unconditional stability. Furthermore, the methodology allows the user to control the numerical dissipation in the high-frequency region. That is, the generalized-
\alpha
method controls the high- and low-frequency dissipations in the sense that for a given high-frequency dissipation, the algorithm minimizes the low-frequency dissipation. Thus, one obtains accurate approximations in both low- and high- frequency regions; see
Jansen et al. 2000
,
Chung and Hulbert 1993
. Despite these features, to date, the generalized-
\alpha
method is limited to second-order accuracy in time while the high-order Runge-Kutta and multistep schemes (e.g., Adams-Moulton, and backward differentiation formulae (BDF)) lack explicit control over the numerical dissipation of the high frequencies (see
Butcher 2016
,
Ascher et al. 1997
,
Burbeau et al. 2001
). (Remarkably, BDF2 corresponds to the generalized-
alpha
method with maximal high-frequency dissipation,
\rho_{\infty}=0
.) Another shortcoming of these high-order multistep methods is that their stability regions shrink as their order increases. Therefore, unconditional stability is not possible for higher orders than two. While Runge-Kutta methods show better stability regions and deliver A-stability with higher-order accuracy; they are not self-starting and require another scheme to retrieve solutions at initial time steps.

We propose a generalized-
\alpha
method with an arbitrary order of approximation that provides A-stability parabolic time marching. Our scheme completes a Taylor expansion adding higher-order terms to the residual to obtain an auxiliary system to solve. We first analyze the amplification matrix’s spectral properties to establish the parameter values that result in unconditional stability and control the high-frequency numerical dissipation. Next, we allow for complex entries in the amplification matrix to prove the method’s A-stability for arbitrary accuracy. Numerical experiments, verify that the stability regions remain unchanged for any accuracy order and show that the technique improves the behavior of the generalized-
\alpha
method in the moderate-frequency regions. Lastly,
Hughes 2012
examines a method’s accuracy using the Cayley-Hamilton theory; a commonly used technique in the literature (cf.,
Jansen et al. 2000
,
Behnoudfar et al. 2019
,
Chung and Hulbert 1993
) but limited to methods that result in
2\times 2
and
3\times 3
amplification matrices. We extend this methodology to encompass the general
k\times k
amplification matrices that result from our time-marching scheme.

We organize the remainder of the paper as follows. Section
2
describes the problem under consideration. Section
3
details a third-order generalized-
\alpha
method, proves its third-order accuracy in time and its unconditional stability. Section
4
introduces our
(3/2k)^{th}
and
(3/2k+1/2)^{th}
-order accuracy methods, demonstrates their unconditional stability region, and defines their numerical dissipation control parameters. Section
5
summarizes our contributions.

Problem Statement

We consider a parabolic, linear initial boundary-value problem:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla u(x,t))

\displaystyle=f(x,t),\qquad

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.1)

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=u_{D},\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0},\qquad\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega.

Let

\partial\Omega

2.1
Spatial discretization

We use a spatial finite element discretization. We define

a(w_{h},\dot{u}_{h})+b(w_{h},u_{h})=\ell(w_{h}),\qquad w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}(\Omega),\ t>0,

(2.2)

where

a(w,v)=(w,v)_{\Omega},\quad b(w,v)=(\kappa\nabla w,\nabla u)_{\Omega},\quad\ell(w)=(w,f)_{\Omega},\qquad\forall w,v\in V_{h}^{p}(\Omega).

(2.3)

We approximate

that belongs to a finite-dimensional space
V_{h}^{p}
. The spatial discretization leads to the following variational formulation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find }u_{h}(t)=u_{h}(\cdot,t)\in V_{h}^{p}(\Omega)\qquad&\text{ for }t>0:\\
a(w_{h},\dot{u}_{h})+b(w_{h},u_{h})=\ell(w_{h}),\qquad&\forall w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}\end{cases}

2.4

M\dot{U}+KU=F,

(2.5)

where
M
and
K
are the mass and stiffness matrices,
U
is the vector of the unknowns, and
F
is the source vector. The initial condition is

U(0)=U_{0},

(2.6)

where
U_{0}
is the given vector of initial condition
u_{0,h}
.

.

Herein, we propose a high-order generalized-
\alpha

Remark 2

In problem (

2.2
Time-discretization using generalized-
\alpha

Consider a uniform partitioning of the time interval

\alpha

\displaystyle MV_{n+\alpha_{m}}+KU_{n+\alpha_{f}}

\displaystyle=F_{n+\alpha_{f}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=U_{n}+\tau V_{n}+\tau\gamma\llbracket V_{n}\rrbracket,

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=M^{-1}(F_{0}-KU_{0}),

where

\displaystyle F_{n+\alpha_{f}}

\displaystyle=F(t_{n+\alpha_{f}}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle W_{n+\alpha_{g}}

\displaystyle=W_{n}+\alpha_{g}\llbracket W_{n}\rrbracket,\qquad\llbracket W_{n}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=W_{n+1}-W_{n},\quad W=U,V,\quad g=m,f.

According to (

\alpha_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3-\rho_{\infty}}{1+\rho_{\infty}}\Big),\qquad\alpha_{f}=\frac{1}{1+\rho_{\infty}}.

where,
\rho_{\infty}\in[0,1]

Third-order generalized-
\alpha
method

The sub-step time-marching affects the accuracy of the generalized-
\alpha
method (
2.7

\mathcal{O}(\tau^{3})

Behnoudfar et al. 2020b
,
Behnoudfar et al. 2019

as the
a
-th order derivative of the function

\alpha

2.5

A_{n}

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}

M\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})^{\alpha_{2}}+KA_{n}^{\alpha_{f}}=\mathcal{L}^{2}(F_{n})^{\alpha_{f}}
by taking two temporal derivatives from the first equation of (
2.7

\displaystyle MV_{n}^{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle=-KU_{n+1}+F_{n+1},

(3.1)

\displaystyle M\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})^{\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle=-KA_{n}^{\alpha_{f}}+\mathcal{L}^{2}(F_{n})^{\alpha_{f}},

with updating conditions

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=U_{n}+\tau V_{n}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}A_{n}+\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})+\gamma_{1}\tau Q_{n},

(3.2)

\displaystyle A_{n+1}

\displaystyle=A_{n}+\tau\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})+\tau\gamma_{2}\llbracket{\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})}\rrbracket,

where

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle=V_{n+1}-V_{n}-\tau A_{n}-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle V_{n}^{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle=V_{n}+\tau A_{n}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})+\alpha_{1}Q_{n},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})^{\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})+\alpha_{2}\llbracket{\mathcal{L}^{1}(A_{n})}\rrbracket,

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha_{f}}

\displaystyle=A_{n}+\alpha_{f}\llbracket{A_{n}}\rrbracket.

The initial data are also obtained by using the given information

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=M^{-1}(F_{0}-KU_{0}),

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=M^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{0})-KV_{0}),

4.1.1

Herein, we introduce a technique to study the accuracy of a time-marching method with arbitrary order. For this, we first discuss the general form of Cayley–Hamilton theorem. That is, for a general

4.3
A-Stability of the method

We now investigate our method to solve stiff systems with complex entries (i.e., advection problems). The second Dahlquist barrier states that the stable region of a multistep method for a stiff equation shrinks for accuracy orders higher than two (see,
Hairer and Wanner 2010
). Our approach delivers the amplification matrix of (
4.6
), which requires solving
k
systems that are form identical to the second-order generalized-
\alpha
method. Each block decouples from the others; therefore, their eigenvalues are independent as well. Thus, we solve
k
independent systems that lead to a high-order method with an invariant stability region. While, the analysis in
4.2
supports our claims, herein, we consider a problem with complex eigenvalues,
\lambda_{\theta}\in\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, we consider the amplification matrix (
4.6
) to obtain the region of stability as:

\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\frac{\alpha_{k}+(\alpha_{f}-1)\gamma_{k}\theta}{\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{f}\gamma_{k}\theta}\right|\leq 1,\,\,\left|\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\alpha_{j}+\gamma_{j}\theta}\right|\leq 1\right\},\qquad j=1,\cdots,k-1.

(4.23)

Considering (
4.19
), one can show that imposing
Re(\theta)\geq 0
is sufficient to satisfy (
4.23
), which proves that our method is A-stable.

Remark 6
.

Setting

and

5
Concluding remarks

We derive and analyze a new class of higher-order generalized-
\alpha
methods for solving parabolic problems that maintain all the attractive features of the original (second-order) generalized-
\alpha
method. In particular, at each time step, we obtain
(3/2k)^{th}
and
(3/2k+1/2)^{th}
order of accuracy in time, respectively, for even and odd
k
, by solving
k
matrix systems consecutively and implicitly. We then update the other
k
variables explicitly. We derive a one-parameter family with dissipation control using a user-specified parameter
\rho^{\infty}
. Our method is A-stable for arbitrarily high accuracy. Furthermore, setting
\rho^{\infty}=0
, our method shows L-stability behaviour.

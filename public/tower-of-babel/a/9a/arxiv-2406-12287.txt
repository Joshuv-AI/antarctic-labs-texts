Abstract
. Let X X be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety with an epimorphism ν : π 1 ​ ( X ) ↠ ℤ n \nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n} . We survey recent developments about the asymptotic behaviour of Betti numbers with any field coefficients and the order of the torsion part of singular integral homology of finite abelian covers of X X associated to ν \nu , known as the L 2 L^{2} -type invariants. We give relations between L 2 L^{2} -type invariants, Alexander invariants and cohomology jump loci. When ν \nu is orbifold effective, we give explicit formulas for L 2 L^{2} -invariants at homological degree one in terms of geometric information of X X . We also propose several related open questions for hyperplane arrangement complement.

1.
Introduction

There is a general principle to consider a classical invariant of a finite CW complex and to define its analog for some covering space. This leads to the
L^{2}
-type invariants.
Atiyah
[
Ati76
]
introduced
the notion of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers in the context of a regular covering of a closed
Riemannian manifold.
After that, there has been vast literature for the
L^{2}
-invariant theory, see
[
Luc02
]
. The
L^{2}
-invariant theory has attracted considerable interest and has relations to many other fields, such as operator theory and Algebraic
K
-theory. A particular important result is Lück’s approximation theorem
[
Luc94
]
, which states that the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of the universal cover of a finite polyhedron can be found as limits of normalised Betti numbers of finite sheeted normal coverings.

In this paper, we focus on a rather simple case of Lück’s approximation problem, see e.g. Lück’s survey papers
[
Luc13
,
Luc16
]
on this topic. Let
X
be a connected finite CW complex with a fixed epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Consider a descending chain of subgroups

\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\Gamma_{0}\supseteq\Gamma_{1}\supseteq\Gamma_{2}\supseteq\cdots

such that
\Gamma_{j}
is a sub-group in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with finite index and
\bigcap_{j=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{j}=\{0\}
.
Let
X_{j}
denote the finite covering of
X
associated to the composed epimorphism
\nu
with the quotient map
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma_{j}
.
We study the asymptotic behaviour of
the
i
-th Betti number with coefficients in a field coefficient
\mathbb{K}

and the logarithm of the cardinality of the torsion subgroup of the
i
-th homology group with integral coefficients, i.e., the two sequences

\big(\frac{\dim H_{i}(X_{j},\mathbb{K})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma_{j}|}\big)_{j\geq 0}

and

\big(\frac{\log|H_{i}(X_{j},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma_{j}|}\big)_{j\geq 0}.

Here
|H_{i}(X_{j},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|
denotes the order of the torsion part of
H_{i}(X_{j},\mathbb{Z}).

One can find the following approximation problem proposed by Lück in
[
Luc13
,
Luc16
]
.

Question 1.1
.

(Approximation Problem)

(i)

Does the sequence converge?

(ii)

If yes, is the limit independent of the chain?

(iii)

If yes, what is the limit?

Luckily for us Question
1.1
has positive answers in our case as we explain now.
For a subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of finite index, we set

\langle\Gamma\rangle=\min\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2}\mid x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Gamma,x\neq 0\right\}

Let
X^{\nu,\Gamma}
denote the covering space of
X
associated to the compositions of
\nu
and the quotient map
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma
.
Consider the following limits:

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{K})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}

for any field coefficients
\mathbb{K}
and

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})\coloneqq\limsup_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}.

It is known that the first limit always exists for any field coefficients and does not depend on the choice of chains, see
[
Luc13
, Theorem 3.2]
.
When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, the well known Lück’s approximation theorem
[
Luc94
]
shows that the first limit exists and does not depend on the choice of chains. On the other hand, it was proved by Le
[
Le14
]

that the upper limit
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
exists and can be computed by the Mahler measure of the
i
-th integral Alexander polynomial.
When
n=1
, the upper limit in the definition of
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
can be replaced by the ordinary limit.

The
L^{2}
-type invariants are particular useful for the following Singer-Hopf Conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(Singer-Hopf Conjecture)
.

Let
X
be an aspherical closed manifold with real dimension
2N
. Then

(-1)^{N}\chi(X)\geq 0.

A connected CW complex is said to be aspherical if its universal cover is
contractible. Conjecture
1.2
is still wide open even in the realm
of aspherical complex smooth projective varieties. This is particularly surprising as, in
the projective case, one can use a large variety of tools coming from algebraic
geometry approaches to such conjecture. For the recent developments in this direction, we refer to
[
AW23
,
AMW23
,
DL19
,
DL23
,
LMW21
]
.

Inspired by these developments, we focus on the aforementioned
L^{2}
-type invariants in the context of complex smooth quasi-projective varieties. These
L^{2}
-type invariants have a deep connection with cohomology jump loci.
The cohomology jump loci of a complex smooth quasi-projective variety have been intensively studied in recent years. In particular, the beautiful structure theorem for cohomology jump loci put strong constraints for the homotopy type of complex smooth quasi-projective variety, see Section
3.1
for a brief introduction. We also refer the readers to Budur and Wang’s survey paper
[
BW17
]
for a comprehensive background on this topic.

These results for cohomology jump loci can be used to prove many good properties about the
L^{2}
-type invariants. For example, as an application of structure theorem, we show that the upper limit in the definition of
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
can be replaced by the ordinary limit when
X
is a smooth complex quasi-projective variety (see Corollary
3.2
), which is not true for general topological space. This result is new to the author’s knowledge.

Since the aforementioned limits for these
L^{2}
-type invariants exist, we rephrase Question
1.1
(iii) as follows.

Question 1.3
.

How to compute these
L^{2}
-type invariants in terms of the algebraic geometric information of
X
, when
X
is a smooth complex quasi-projective variety?

In the case of hyperplane arrangement complement, one can ask if these
L^{2}
-type invariants are combinatorially determined.
Based on Lück’s approximation theorem
[
Luc94
]
, a related question is whether the fundamental group of hyperplane arrangement complement is residually finite?

The purpose of this survey is to provide a gentle introduction to these concepts and
results, with an emphasis on applications and open questions.
In section 2, we recall the definition of Alexander type invariants and cohomology jump loci and study their relations with
L^{2}
-type invariants.
In section 3, we focus on the case when
X
is a complex smooth quasi-projective variety. In section 3.1, we recall the structure theorem about cohomology jump loci and its applications to
L^{2}
-type invariants.
Section 3.2 is devoted to cohomology jump loci and
L^{2}
-type invariants at homological degree one. Section 4 is devoted to hyperplane arrangements. We propose many open questions regarding if these invariants are combinatorially determined. Section 5 is devoted for proofs.

Notations.

(1)

\mathbb{K}
is assumed to be an algebraically closed filed in the rest of the paper and
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})
denotes the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{K}
.

(2)

For any prime number
p\geq 2
, let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
denote the algebraic closure of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p
elements.

2.
Alexander invariants and cohomology jump loci

Let
X
be a connected finite CW complex with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
We study
L^{2}
-type invariants associated to the pair
(X,\nu)
.

2.1.
Alexander type invariants

Let
X^{\nu}
denote the covering space of
X
associated to the epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The group of covering transformations of the covering space
X^{\nu}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and acts on it. By choosing fixed lifts of the cells of
X
to
X^{\nu}
, we obtain a free basis for the cellular chain complex of
X^{\nu}
as
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules, where
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
is the multi-variable Laurentiu polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}_{1},\ldots,t^{\pm}_{n}]=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
. So the cellular chain complex of
X^{\nu}
,
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
, is a bounded complex of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules:

(2.1)

\cdots\to C_{i+1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\overset{\partial_{i}}{\to}C_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\overset{\partial_{i-1}}{\to}C_{i-1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\overset{\partial_{i-2}}{\to}\cdots\overset{\partial_{0}}{\to}C_{0}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Definition 2.1
.

The
i
-th integral Alexander homology

H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
of the pair
(X,\nu)
is by definition the

i
-th homology of
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
as complex of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules.
Similarly, for any field
\mathbb{K}
, the
i
-th Alexander homology with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficient

H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
of the pair
(X,\nu)
is by definition the

i
-th homology of
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}
as complex of
\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules, where
\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
denotes the multi-variable Laurent polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}_{1},\ldots,t^{\pm}_{n}].

With the above free basis for
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
,
\partial_{i}
can be written down as a matrix with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
is a Noetherian UFD.
Let
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
denote the greatest common divisor of all non-zero
(\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}\times\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i})
-minors of
\partial_{i}
.
When
\partial_{i}=0
,
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})=1
by convention.

Definition 2.2
.

\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is called
the
i
-th integral Alexander polynomial of the pair
(X,\nu)
.

Note that
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is defined uniquely up to multiplication with a unit in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
.

Remark 2.3
.

The complex
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
determines
\alpha_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
and
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
.
In fact, for any
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with finite index, consider
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]
as a quotient ring of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
.
Then
H_{*}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
can be computed by the following complex (viewed as a complex of abelian groups):

C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma].

So it is no surprise that the
L^{2}
-type invariants have a deep connection with the Alexander invariants.

Next we recall the definition of Mahler measure.
Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
be a nonzero polynomial. The Mahler measure of
h
is defined by

\mathbf{M}(h)\coloneqq\int_{(S^{1})^{n}}\log|h(s)|\mathrm{d}s,

where
\mathrm{d}s
indicates the integration with respect to the normalized Haar measure, and
(S^{1})^{n}
is the multiplicative
subgroup of
n
-dimensional complex space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
consisting of all vectors
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
with
|s_{1}|=\cdots=|s_{n}|=1
. When
n=1
Mahler measure has the following equivalent definition.

Definition 2.4
.

[
Mah60
,
Mah62
]

For a non-zero polynomial
h(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
with
n=1
, assume that

h(t)=a_{d}t^{d}+a_{d-1}t^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{0}=a_{d}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t-\alpha_{j})
with
a_{d}\neq 0
,
a_{0}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.

We define the
Mahler measure
of
h
to be

\mathbf{M}(h)=\log(|a_{d}|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}\max\{1,|\alpha_{j}|\}).

Recall that an element
h\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
is called a generalized cyclotomic polynomial
[
Sch15
, page 47]
if it is of the form
h(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\Phi(\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{i}})
(up to units in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
), where
0\neq(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\Phi
is a cyclotomic polynomial in a single variable.
The following theorem due to Schmidt characterizes when could the Mahler measure be zero.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
Sch95
, Theorem 19.5]

For
h\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
,
\mathbf{M}(h)=0
if and only if
h
is a product of generalized cyclotomic polynomials up to units.

Based on the work of Schmidt
[
Sch95
, Theorem 21.1]
, Le showed that
[
Le14
, Theorem 5]

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})
can be computed by the Mahler measure of
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
.
When
n=1
, the following result is also proved by Raimbault
[
Rai12
, Theorem 0.2]
independently.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
Le14
, Theorem 5]

Let
X
be a connected finite CW complex with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then for any
i

we have

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=\mathbf{M}(\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})).

If
n=1
then
\limsup
in the definition of
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})
can be replaced by the ordinary
\lim
.

Inspired by Theorem
2.5
, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a connected finite CW complex with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is a product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials with a non-zero integer
c_{i}
, then
\limsup
in the definition of
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})
can be replaced by the ordinary
\lim
and
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=\log|c_{i}|
.

The proof of this proposition is given in section 5.

2.2.
Cohomology jump loci

We first recall the definition of cohomology jump loci. For any algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
, the group of
\mathbb{K}
-valued characters,
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})
, is a commutative affine algebraic group. Each character
\rho
in
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})
defines a rank one local system on
X
, denoted by
L_{\rho}
. Since
\mathbb{K}^{*}
is abelian,
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})
in fact only depends on
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})=\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{K}^{*})
.

Definition 2.8
.

The cohomology jump loci of
X
are defined as

\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})\mid\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})\geq k\}.

Cohomology jump loci are closed sub-varieties of
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})
and homotopy invariants of
X
.
In cohomological degree one,
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
depends only on
\pi_{1}(X)
(e.g. see
[
Suc11
, Section 2.2]
).

The map
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
induces an embedding
\nu^{*}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{K}^{*})
. For a tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}
, let
\nu^{-1}L_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
X
whose monodromy representation factors through
\nu
.

Proposition 2.9
.

With the notations and assumptions as above, for any
i\geq 0
and
\lambda\in(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}
being general we have

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\dim H_{i}(X,\nu^{-1}L_{\lambda})=\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K}).

Here
\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
is the dimension of the vector space
H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathcal{F}

over the fractional field
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
.
In particular,
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
is always an integer. Moreover, if
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is not a unit in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
, every irreducible component of the zero locus of
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
is a irreducible component of
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
(via
\nu^{*}
) with codimension one for
k=\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})+1.

This proposition is not new and the claims are either already proved in
[
LL23
, Propsition 2.2]
or folklore. We give a brief proof in section 5 due to the lack of precise references. For more relations between cohomology jump loci and Alexander type invariants, we refer the readers to Suciu’s paper
[
Suc11
]
.

We also list some folklore properties of
L^{2}
-type invariants, which would be useful later.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
X
be a connected finite CW complex with a fixed epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then we have the following properties.

(i)

\alpha_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
and
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
are homotopy invariant of the pair
(X,\nu)
.

(ii)

Euler number
: For any field
\mathbb{K}
,

\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\chi(X).

(iii)

Poincare duality
: If
X
is an oriented compact manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X=N
, then

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\alpha_{N-i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
and
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=\mathbf{M}_{N-i-1}(X^{\nu})
.

(v)

Zero-th
L^{2}
-type invariants
:
\alpha_{0}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=0
and
\mathbf{M}_{0}(X^{\nu})=0
.

Complex smooth quasi-projective variety

In this section, we always assume that
X
is a complex smooth quasi-projective variety.

3.1.
Structure theorem

The following structure theorem for
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
put strong constraints for the homotopy type of complex smooth quasi-projective variety. It is contributed by many people and we name a few here: Green and Lazarsfeld
[
GL91
]
, Simpson
[
Sim93A
]
, Arapura
[
Ara97
]
, Dimca, Papadima and Suciu
[
DPS09
]
, Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
, Dimca and Papadima
[
DP14
]
, Schnell
[
Sch15
]
, etc. It is finalized by Budur and Wang in
[
BW15
,
BW20
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
BW15
,
BW20
]
Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety. Then for any
i
and
k
,
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
is a finite union of torsion translated sub-tori of
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})
. Moreover, for every positive dimensional irreducible component
V
of
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
, there exists a torsion point
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})
and an algebraic map
f\colon X\to G
with
G
being a complex semi-abelian variety such that

V=\rho\cdot\mathrm{im}\{f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(G),\mathbb{C}^{*})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})\}.

If
X
is a smooth projective variety,
G
has to be an abelian variety, hence every irreducible component of
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
is even dimensional.

As a direct application of the above structure theorem, Theorem
2.6
, Proposition
2.7
and Proposition
2.9
, we have the following results.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then for any
i
,
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials and a non-zero integer
c_{i}
. Then
\limsup
in the definition of
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})
can be replaced by the ordinary
\lim
and
we have

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=\log|c_{i}|
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth projective variety.
For any subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with finite index,
X^{\nu,\Gamma}
is also a complex smooth projective variety. In particular,
H^{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Q})
has pure Hodge structures. Instead of studying the asymptotic behaviour of
Betti number of
H^{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Q})
, one can also study the asymptotic behaviour of the Hodge numbers of
H^{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Q})
.
Let
h^{p,q}(X^{\nu,\Gamma})
denotes the dimension of
H^{q}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\Omega^{p}_{X^{\nu,\Gamma}})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above notations and assumptions, for any
(p,q)
we have

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{p,q}(X^{\nu,\Gamma})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=L^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
L^{\prime}
is a generic unitary rank one local system in
\mathrm{im}\nu^{*}
. Here
\nu^{*}
is the embedding
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})
induced by
\nu
.

The proof of this proposition is given in section 5.
When
\nu
is the natural map to
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
modulo
torsions and the Albanese map of
X
is semi-small,
Di Cerbo and Lombardi
[
DL19
]
give an effective estimate for the asymptotic behaviour of the Hodge numbers and showed that

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{p,q}(X^{\nu,\Gamma})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{q}\chi(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}),&\mathrm{if}\ p+q=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X,\\
0,&\mathrm{else}.\end{cases}

Using the generic vanishing results proved by Popa and Schnell
[
PS13
]
, Di Cerbo and Lombardi’s results are compatible with
Proposition
3.3
.

One may ask how about Proposition
3.3
for smooth quasi-projective varieties. In this case,
H^{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Q})
has mixed Hodge structures.

Question 3.4
.

Doe the limit for the Hodge numbers of
H^{i}(X^{\nu,\Gamma},\mathbb{Q})
exist?

Budur proved
[
Bud09
, Theorem 1.8, Theorem 1.9]
the polynomial
periodicity for certain Hodge numbers of congruence covers of complex smooth (quasi-)projective variety based on a structure theorem for line bundles
[
Bud09
, Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.4]
.
His work shed light on the above question at least for the congruence covers.
For more references about polynomial
periodicity of congruence covers, we refer the readers to Suciu’s survey paper
[
Suc01
, Section 5]
.

3.2.
Degree one case

3.2.1.
Cohomology jump loci

If we focus on cohomological degree one jump loci, a celebrity result due to Beauville
[
Bea92
]
, Arapura
[
Ara97
]
and Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
puts even stronger constraints for its fundamental group.
To explain their results, we introduce the notion of orbifold maps.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex quasi-projective variety. An algebraic map
f\colon X\to C_{g,r}
is called an orbifold map, if
f
is surjective, has connected generic fiber and
C_{g,r}
is a smooth algebraic curve of genus
g
with
r
points removed. We always assume that
C_{g,r}\neq\mathbb{CP}^{1},\mathbb{C}
. There exists a maximal Zariski open subset
U\subset C_{g,r}
such that
f
is a fibration over
U
. Say
B=C_{g,r}-U
(could be empty) has
s
points, denoted by
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}
. We assign
the multiplicity
\mu_{j}
of the fiber
f^{*}(q_{j})
(the
\gcd
of the coefficients of the divisor
f^{*}q_{j}
) to the point
q_{j}
. Such orbifold map
f
is called of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
, where
{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
. When
B=\emptyset
,
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}=1
by convention.

When
C_{g,r}
is clear in the context, we simply write
C
.
Our definition of orbifold maps is a bit of different from the classical one, e.g. see
[
DS14
, Section 4.1]
. The classical one will be called the hyperbolic orbifold map, where we adopt the name from
[
Delz08
]
.

Definition 3.6
.

An orbifold map
f\colon X\to C
of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
is called hyperbolic, if

2g+r-2+\sum_{j=1}^{s}(1-\dfrac{1}{\mu_{j}})>0.

This definition is used to rule out the case where
C
is either
\mathbb{C}^{*}
or Elliptic curve and
f
has no multiple fibers.

The orbifold group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}})
associated to these data is defined as

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}})\coloneqq\pi_{1}(C_{g,r}\backslash\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\})/\langle\gamma_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s\rangle,

where
\gamma_{j}
is a meridian of
q_{j}
.
An orbifold map
f\colon X\to C
of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
induces an surjective map to the orbifold group (see
[
ACM10
, Proposition 1.4]
)

f_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}).

Hence it induces an embedding for any
k\geq 1

\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(\pi_{1}^{orb}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}),\mathbb{K})\to\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K}).

The following theorem shows that every positive-dimensional component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is characterized by the orbifold map in this way.
The idea first appeared in Beauville’s work
[
Bea92
]
for the projective case and was extended to the
quasi-projective case by Arapura
[
Ara97
]
and Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
. Further properties were found by Dimca
[
Dim07
,
Dim09
]
, Dimca-Papadima-Suciu
[
DPS09
]
, etc.

Theorem 3.7
.

[
Ara97
,
ACM13
,
Dim07
]

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety.

(a)

We have

\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})=\bigcup_{f}{f^{*}}\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}})),\mathbb{C})\cup Z,

where
Z
is a finite set of torsion points and the union runs over all hyperbolic orbifold maps
f
.

(b)

Fix an orbifold map
f\colon X\to C
of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
. Then for any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}),\mathbb{C}^{*})
we have

\dim H^{1}(X,f^{*}L_{\rho})\geq\dim H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}),L_{\rho})

and equality holds with finitely many (torsion) exceptions.

Remark 3.8
.

This theorem indeed gives a complete description of all positive-dimensional components contained in
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
for all
k\geq 1
.

When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p>0
, not much is known for
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
. So far all the known results concerns about cohomological degree one jump loci.
Delzant
[
Delz08
]
enabled to generalize Theorem
3.7
(a) to any algebraically closed
\mathbb{K}
-coefficient case when
X
is a complex smooth projective variety.

Theorem 3.9
.

[
Delz08
]
Let
X
be a complex smooth projective variety and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field. Then we have

\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{K})=\bigcup_{f}{f^{*}}\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}})),\mathbb{K})\cup Z^{\prime},

where
Z^{\prime}
is a finite set of torsion points (depends on
\mathbb{K}
) and the union runs over all hyperbolic orbifold maps
f
. In particular, the structure theorem holds for
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{K})
.

The following theorem due to Li and the author generalizes Theorem
3.7
(b) to any algebraically closed field coefficients.

Theorem 3.10
.

[
LL21
, Theorem 1.6]
Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field. Given an orbifold map
f\colon X\to C
of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
and any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}),\mathbb{K}^{*})
,
we have

\dim H^{1}(X,f^{*}L_{\rho})\geq\dim H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(C_{g,r},{\bm{\mu}}),L_{\rho})

and equality holds with finitely many (torsion) exceptions.

Remark 3.11
.

As like in Remark
3.8
, Theorem
3.9
and Theorem
3.10
together give a complete
description for all positive-dimensional components contained in
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
for all
k
when
X
is smooth projective. Hence the structure theorem holds for
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
for all
k\geq 1
.

3.2.2.
BNS-invariants

One may wonder how about Theorem
3.9
for smooth quasi-projective varieties.
Following Delzant’s approach in
[
Delz08
,
Delz10
]
, one needs to study the BNS-invariants of
X
. Let us recall its definition.

Pick a finite generating set of
\pi_{1}(X)
and let
\Omega(X)
be the
corresponding Cayley graph of
\pi_{1}(X)
. Given an additive real character
\chi\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\Omega_{\chi}(X)
be the full subgraph on vertex set
\{g\in\pi_{1}(X)\mid\chi(g)\geq 0\}
. Bieri, Neumann, and Strebel defined
[
BNS87
]

a subset
\Sigma^{1}(X)
of
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, nowadays called the BNS-invariant of
X
as follows, see e.g.
[
PS10
, Definition 7.1]
.

Definition 3.12
.

The set
\Sigma^{1}(X)
consists of those non-zero homomorphisms
\chi\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{R}

for which the graph
\Omega_{\chi}(X)
is connected.

Papadima and Suciu studied the relations between
\Sigma^{1}(X)
and the orbifold maps in
[
PS10
, Section 16]
, and the results are refined by Suciu recently via tropical method
[
Suc21
]
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety.
Then

\Sigma^{1}(X)\subset\big(\bigcup_{f}\mathrm{im}(f^{*}\colon H^{1}(C,\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(X,\mathbb{R}))\big)^{c},

where the finite union runs over all hyperbolic orbifold maps
f
.

In fact, a result of Delzant
[
Delz10
]
shows that the above inclusion holds as an equality when
X
is a smooth projective variety (or even a compact Kähler manifold).
Based on a deep theorem due to Simpson
[
Sim93B
]
, Delzant gave a complete description of BNS-invariants of complex compact Kähler manifold
[
Delz10
]
, which are characterized by the hyperbolic orbifold maps. Using this description of BNS-invariants by orbifold maps, Delzant managed to prove Theorem
3.9
in
[
Delz08
]
.

Question 3.14
.

Inspired by these work, one may ask the following questions:

(i)

Does
[
Sim93B
, Theorem 1]
hold for complex smooth quasi-projective varieties?

(ii)

Does the inclusion in Theorem
3.13
hold as an equality?

(iii)

Does Theorem
3.9
hold for complex smooth quasi-projective varieties?

Ideally a positive answer to question (i) (resp. (ii)) would imply a positive answer to question (ii) (resp. (iii)).
The question (ii) was first raised by Papadima and Suciu in
[
PS10
]
and refined by Suciu in
[
Suc21
, Question 13.7]
for hyperplane arrangement complements.

.

-type invariants of

at homological degree one when
\nu
is orbifold effective.

Theorem 3.17
.

[
LL21
, Theorem 1.10]
,
[
LL23
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Suppose that
\nu
is orbifold effective of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\geq 0
.
Then

\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=2g+r-2+\#\{j\mid p\ \mathrm{divides}\ \mu_{j}\}

and

\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\log\mu_{j}.

On the other hand, assume that either
X
is projective or
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
has pure Hodge structure of type
(1,1)
. Then
\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})>0
or
\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})>0
happens only if
\nu
is orbifold effective.

Remark 3.18
.

A positive answer to Question
3.14
(iii) would enable us to drop the assumption that either
X
is projective or
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
has pure Hodge structure of type
(1,1)
in the last claim of the above theorem.

Remark 3.19
.

Due to Proposition
2.9
and Corollary
3.2
, the above theorem also implies the corresponding formulas for the rank of the Alexander homology and the leading coefficient of the integral Alexander polynomial at homological degree one.

The above theorem is just about
L^{2}
-type invariants at homological degree one. How about higher homological degrees?
Following the structure theorem, we propose the following higher homological degree analogues. Let
V
be a positive-dimensional irreducible component in
\mathcal{V}^{i}(X,\mathbb{C})
. According to the structure theorem there exists an algebraic map
f\colon X\to G
with
G
being a complex semi-abelian variety such that
V
is contained in a translate of

\mathrm{im}\{f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(G),\mathbb{C}^{*})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})\}.

Question 3.20
.

Note that
\pi_{1}(G)
is a finitely generated free abelian group. Consider the epimorphism
\nu=f_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(G)
induced by
f
. Can one compute
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
and
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})
by the geometric information of
f
? One may need to take the Stein factorization of
f
.

Using Theorem
3.17
and Proposition
2.10
, we give a complete description of the
L^{2}
-type invariants when
X
is a complex smooth projective surface.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth projective surface with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\geq 0
. If
\nu
is not orbifold effective,
then

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}\chi(X),&\mathrm{if}\ i=2,\\
0,&\mathrm{else}\end{cases}

and

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=0
for all
i
.

On other hand, if
\nu
is orbifold effective, say of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
, then

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}2g+r-2+\#\{j\mid p\ \mathrm{divides}\ \mu_{j}\},&\mathrm{if}\ i=1,3,\\
\chi(X)+4g+2r-4+2\#\{j\mid p\ \mathrm{divides}\ \mu_{j}\},&\mathrm{if}\ i=2,\\
0&\mathrm{else}\end{cases}

and

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\log\mu_{j},&\mathrm{if}\ i=1,2,\\
0,&\mathrm{else}.\end{cases}

4.
Hyperplane arrangement

In this section, we always assume that
X
is the complement of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
for some
N
and we focus on a fixed epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
for
n=1
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, we denote
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}
the corresponding element in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{K})
, respectively. Note that
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}\cup v_{\mathbb{Z}}=0,
hence
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}\cup\nu_{\mathbb{K}}=0
.
Then we get two chain complexes by cup product

\textstyle{(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})\colon}
\textstyle{H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots}
\textstyle{(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}})\colon}
\textstyle{H^{0}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{K}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{K}}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots}

which are called
the Aomoto complex
of

and
H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K})
associated to
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}
, respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

With the above notations, we define
the
i
-th Aomoto Betti number with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients
as

\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

and
the order of the
i
-th Aomoto torsion
as

\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq|H^{i+1}(H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{tor}}|.

Here the shift by 1 is due to the Universal Coefficient Theorem.

As shown by Orlik and Solomon
[
OS80
]
the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
are determined by the combinatorics data of the hyperplane arrangement.
Then
\beta_{*}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})
and
\tau_{*}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})
are combinatorially determined, once
\nu
, considered as an element in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, is fixed.
Papadima and Suciu discovered the following results in
[
PS10
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

With the above notations and assumptions, for any field
\mathbb{K}
we have

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\leq\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

Moreover, when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, the above inequality holds as a equality.

Papadima and Suciu
[
PS10
]
indeed proved the above inequality for any finite CW complex. When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, the equality
was first proved (by different methods) by Cohen and Suciu
[
CS99
]
, Libgober
[
Lib02
]
, and
Libgober and Yuzvinsky
[
LY00
]
for
i=1
and was generalized to the higher-degrees
by Cohen and Orlik
[
CO00
, Theorem 3.7]
.

On the other hand, the author jointly with Li gave the following combinatorial upper bound for
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
LL21
, Proposition 1.12]
Let
X
be the complement of a hyperplane arrangement.
For any epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
and any
i\geq 0
, we have

\exp(\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu}))\mid\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).

In particular, if
\nu
is orbifold effective of type
(g,r,{\bm{\mu}})
, then

(4.1)

\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}\mid\tau_{1}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).

The proof of this proposition is based on Papadima and Suciu’s work
[
PS10
, Theorem 12.6]
and two facts about hyperplane arrangement complement:

•

the hyperplane arrangement complement
X
is homotopy equivalent to a minimal CW complex, see
[
DP03
,
Ran02
]
.

•

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})=\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{C}})
for any
i
as in Theorem

.

There are examples of hyperplane arrangements where
\alpha_{i}(X,\mathbb{K})\neq\beta_{i}(X,\mathbb{K})
and
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}\neq\tau_{1}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})
, see
[
LL21
, Example 5.3]
and the next example.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the deleted
B_{3}
-arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with defining equations

zxy(x-y)(x-z)(y-z)(x-y-z)(x-y+z).

Order the
hyperplanes as the factors of the defining polynomial. Let
X
be the complement of the arrangement.
It was first discovered by Suciu
[
Suc02
]
that
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
has a translated component

V\coloneqq\rho\otimes\{t,t^{-1},1,t^{-1},t,t^{2},t^{-2}\mid t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}

with
\rho=(1,-1,-1,-1,1,1,1)\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{7}
, e.g. see
[
Dim17
, Example 6.16]
. Here we take
\{z=0\}
as the hyperplane at infinity.
This component is induced by the orbifold map
f\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
as following

f([x,y,z])=\frac{x(y-z)(x-y-z)^{2}}{y(x-z)(x-y+z)^{2}}

and
f
is of type
(0,2,2)
.
Consider
\nu=(1,-1,0,-1,1,2,-2)\in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
induced by
f
. Then

\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}1,&\mathrm{if}\ \mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=2,\\
0,&\mathrm{if}\ \mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})\neq 2,\end{cases}

and
\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})=\log 2
.

On the other hand, the Aomoto complex for
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}
can be computed by the formula given in
[
Dim17
, p.119]
. A direct computation shows that

\tau_{1}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})=4
and

\beta_{1}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})=\begin{cases}1,&\mathrm{if}\ \mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=2,\\
0,&\mathrm{if}\ \mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})\neq 2.\end{cases}

It is a long standing open question for hyperplane arrangement if
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is combinatorially determined, see e.g.
[
Suc14
, Problem 3.15]
.
It is known that the positive-dimensional components of
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
passing through the origin are combinatorially determined (see e.g.
[
Dim17
, Theorem 6.1]
) and they can be described by the multinet structure, for which we refer in particular to the work of Falk and Yuzvinsky
[
FY07
]
.
Since
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
only depends on the the fundamental group of
X
, using Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem, one can assume that
X
is the complement of a line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.
Let us now recall the definition of multinet.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. A multinet on
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
\mathcal{A}
into
k\geq 3
subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
, together with an assignment of multiplicities,
m\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and a subset
\mathfrak{X}
of intersection points in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}m_{H}=\kappa
, independent of
i
;

(b)

for each
H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
with
i\neq j
,
H\cap H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}
;

(c)

for each
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, the sum
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i},x\in H}m_{H}
is independent of
i
;

(d)

for every
1\leq i\leq k
and
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, there is a sequence
\{H_{0},H_{1},\cdots,H_{s}\}
with
H_{0}=H
and
H_{s}=H^{\prime}
such that
H_{j-1}\cap H_{j}\notin\mathfrak{X}
for all
1\leq j\leq s
.

(e)

\gcd(m_{H}\colon H\in\mathcal{A})
=1.

Such multinet is called a
(k,\kappa)
-multinet.

Note that the multinet only depends on the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{A}
.

By results of Pereira-Yuzvinsky
[
PY08
]
and Yuzvinsky
[
Yuz09
]
,
k=3
or
4
if
|\mathfrak{X}|>1
. Moreover, Yuzvinsky conjectured that
k=4
only happens for Hessian arrangement of 12 planes in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, defined by the polynomial

xyz\prod_{j=0}^{2}\prod_{k=0}^{2}(x+\omega^{j}y+\omega^{k}z)=0
with
\omega=\exp{2\pi i/3}
.

As shown by Falk and Yuzvinsky
[
FY07
]
, and Pereira and Yuzvinsky
[
PY08
]
, every
(k,\kappa)
-multinet gives an orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,k}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with
X=\mathbb{CP}^{2}-\mathcal{A}
by

f(x)\coloneqq[\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{1}}f_{H}^{m_{H}}(x),\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}f_{H}^{m_{H}}(x)].

Definition 4.6
.

Let
X=\mathbb{CP}^{2}-\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}
being a essential line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. We call a hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,k}
non-local, if it is not the restriction of another hyperbolic orbifold map over the complement of a sub-arrangement of
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

[
FY07
]
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with
X=\mathbb{CP}^{2}-\mathcal{A}
. Then every non-local hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,k}
with
k=3,4
is obtained from a
(k,\kappa)
-multinet for some
\kappa
.

The above theorem shows that the existence of the non-local hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,k}
with
k=3
or
4
are combinatorially determined (by the multinet structure).

Question 4.8
.

We have the following questions for
X=\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}
being a essential line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

(i)

Is the existence of hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,2}=\mathbb{C}^{*}
combinatorially determined?

(ii)

Is the existence of non-local hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X\to C_{0,k}
having multiple fibers for
k=3
or
4
combinatorially determined?

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
has
(d+1)
-many lines and let
f_{i}
be the degree one defining equation for the
i
-th line
H_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
with
0\leq i\leq d

Assumption 4.9
.

For non-zero
a=(a_{0},\cdots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
, we assume that
\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}=0
and
\gcd(a_{0},\cdots,a_{d})=1

Then each

\gcd(a_{0},\cdots,a_{d})=1
. Then
f^{a}
is a hyperbolic orbifold map if
f^{a}
is surjective and has multiple fiber.
In particular, every hyperbolic orbifold map has to be realized in this way.
Note that
f^{a}
induces a surjective map

f^{a}_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

This map only depends on
a
, which sends the meridian of
H_{i}
to
a_{i}
. It gives a cohomology class in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})

\nu^{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}

4.3
shows that

(i).

Question 4.10
.

For which

satisfying Assumption
4.9

\tau_{1}(X,\nu^{a}_{\mathbb{Z}})>1

On the other hand, we can also use Proposition
4.3
to study Question

or

Choosing any epimorphism

Proposition

or

Assumption 4.11

Given a
(3,\kappa)
-multinet on
\mathcal{A}

(a)

for any

5.1.
Proof of Proposition
2.7

Set
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}].

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-module. Consider a presentation of
M
by an exact sequence

\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]^{b^{\prime}}\overset{\partial}{\to}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]^{b}\to M\to 0

where
\partial
is given by a matrix of size
b^{\prime}\times b
with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
.
For any
j\geq 0
, let
\Delta^{j}(M)
denote the the greatest
common divisor of all the
(b-j)\times(b-j)
minors of
\partial
with the convention that
\Delta^{j}(M)=1
if
j\geq b
. Clearly
\Delta^{j}(M)
divides
\Delta^{j-1}(M)
for every
j\geq 1
. It
is known that
\Delta^{j}(M)
only depends on
M
, not on any particular presentation. The rank of the module
M
over
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
is the dimension of the vector space
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

over the fractional field
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
. If M has rank
r
, then
\Delta^{j}(M)=0
if
j<r
, and
\Delta^{j}(M)=\Delta^{j-r}(\mathrm{Tor}(M))
, where

\mathrm{Tor}(M)=\{x\in M\mid fx=0\text{ for some non-zero }f\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]\}.

As in
[
Le14
, section 1.3]
, a
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-module
M
is called pseudo-zero if for every prime ideal
P
of height 1, the localization
M_{P}
is 0. A
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-module morphism
M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a pseudo-isomorphism if the kernel and co-kernel
are pseudo-zero. Moreover, two finitely generated torsion
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are pseudo-isomorphic if and
only if
\Delta^{j}(M_{1})=\Delta^{j}(M_{2})
for any
j\geq 0
.

To prove Proposition
2.7
, let us first recall a theorem proved by Le.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
Le14
, Theorem 6]
Assume that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are two pseudo-isomorphic finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
modules. Then
|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|
and
|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|
have the same growth rate in the sense that

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\big(\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}-\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}\big)=0.

Here
|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|
denotes the order of the torsion part of
M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]
as an abelian group.

Note that the limit in this theorem is the ordinary limit, not the upper limit. The following result is a direct application of this theorem and Theorem
2.5
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-modules with rank
r
. If
\Delta^{r}(M)
is a product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials with a non-zero integer
c
,
then

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=\log|c|.

Proof.

Consider the sub-module
\mathrm{Tor}(M)
of
M
. We have that

\Delta^{j}(M)=\Delta^{j-r}(\mathrm{Tor}(M)).

Moreover, it follows from Theorem
2.5
and
[
Le14
, Theorem 1.2, Proposirion 3.4]
that

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\big(\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}-\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathrm{Tor}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}\big)=0.

So without loss of generality, we assume that
M
is a finitely generated torsion
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-module.
Assume that
\Delta^{j}(M)=1
for
j>k
.
Consider a finitely generated torsion
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]
-module

M^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}{\big(\Delta^{j}(M)/\Delta^{j+1}(M)\big)}.

It is easy to see that
\Delta^{j}(M)=\Delta^{j}(M^{\prime})
for any
j\geq 0
. Then
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
by
[
Hil02
, Theorem 3.5]
. Using Theorem
5.1
, the proof is reduced to
M^{\prime}
. Note that
\Delta^{j+1}(M)
divides
\Delta^{j}(M)
for all
j\geq 0
, hence
\Delta^{j}(M)/\Delta^{j+1}(M)
is a also product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials and some non-zero integer. Since
M^{\prime}
is a direct sum, the proof is further reduced to the module with the type
N=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]/(h)
, where
h
a product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials and some non-zero integer
d
.

Set
h^{\prime}=\frac{h}{d}
and
N^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]/(h^{\prime})
.
Clearly we have

|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|=|d|^{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}\cdot|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(N^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|.

Since
h^{\prime}
is a product of some generalized cyclotomic polynomials,
using Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
[
Le14
, Theorem 3]
we have

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathrm{tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(N^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}^{\pm}]}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma]\big)|}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=0.

Then the claim follows.
∎

5.3.
Proof of Proposition
3.3

Let
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
denote the identity component of the Picard variety of
X
, which parameterise topologically trivial line bundles on
X
. Set

S^{p,q}_{k}(X)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)\mid\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L})\geq k\}.

Green and Lazarsfeld showed
[
GL91
]
that
S^{p,q}_{k}(X)
is a finite union of torsion translated sub-abelian variety of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
. In particular, these are sub-varieties of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
.

Consider the moduli space of unitary rank one
\mathbb{C}
-local system on
X
defined as

\mathrm{Uchar}^{0}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\{\mathrm{torsion}\},U(1)).

Any unitary local system
L\in\mathrm{Uchar}^{0}(X)
corresponds to a topologically trivial line bundle
\mathcal{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This gives a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathrm{Uchar}^{0}(X)
and
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
as real torus.
By slight modification of the usual Hodge theoretic arguments, one gets the following Hodge decomposition

(5.1)

H^{i}(X,L)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=i}H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}).

The epimorphism
\nu
induces an embedding
\nu^{\#}\colon(U(1))^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Uchar}^{0}(X).
Let
\mathrm{im}\nu^{\#}
denote the corresponding image in
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
by the above identification.
Since
S^{p,q}_{k}(X)
are sub-varieties of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
, we can pick a generic unitary local system
L^{\prime}\in\mathrm{im}\nu^{\#}
with
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=L^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
(to be precise,
L^{\prime}
is generic with respect to all degrees
(p,q)
with
0\leq p,q\leq\dim X
). By semi-continuity, for any unitary local system
L
and its corresponding line bundle
\mathcal{L}
we have

\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\leq\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}).

Let
\pi\colon X^{\nu,\Gamma}\to X
denote the covering map.
Then

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\bigoplus\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}

where
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=L_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
and the direct sum runs over all unitary local system
L_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
We have

\dim H^{q}(X^{\Gamma},\Omega^{p}_{X^{\Gamma}})=\sum_{\lambda}\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\lambda})\geq|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|\cdot\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}).

Meanwhile, the Hodge decomposition gives us

\sum_{p+q=i}\dim H^{q}(X^{\Gamma},\Omega^{p}_{X^{\Gamma}})=\dim H^{i}(X^{\Gamma},\mathbb{C}).

Putting all together, we have that

\sum_{p+q=i}\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\leq\sum_{p+q=i}\dfrac{\dim H^{q}(X^{\Gamma},\Omega^{p}_{X^{\Gamma}})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=\dfrac{\dim H^{i}(X^{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}.

\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X)
are also closed sub-varieties. By further requiring the unitary local system
L^{\prime}
generic with respect to the jump loci, Proposition
2.9
and Universal Coefficients Theorem imply that the right hand of the above inequality converges to
\dim H^{i}(X,L^{\prime})
when
\langle\Gamma\rangle\to\infty
.
Note that the Hodge decomposition (
5.1
) gives us

\sum_{p+q=i}\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\dim H^{i}(X,L^{\prime}).

Therefore we have

\lim_{\langle\Gamma\rangle\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{p,q}(X^{\nu,\Gamma})}{|\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\Gamma|}=\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}).

On n n -partite digraphical representations of finite groups
Jia-Li Du, Yan-Quan Feng*, Pablo Spiga*

Abstract
. A group G G admits an n n -partite digraphical representation if there exists a regular n n -partite digraph Γ \Gamma such that the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma) of Γ \Gamma satisfies the following properties: (1) Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma) is isomorphic to G G , (2) Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma) acts semiregularly on the vertices of Γ \Gamma and (3) the orbits of Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma) on the vertex set of Γ \Gamma form a partition into n n parts giving a structure of n n -partite digraph to Γ \Gamma . In this paper, for every positive integer n n , we classify the finite groups admitting an n n -partite digraphical representation. Keywords: Semiregular group, regular representation, DRR, D n n SR, n n -PDR. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C25, 20B25.

Introduction

We start by summarising some basic definitions. A
digraph

\Gamma
is an ordered pair
(V\Gamma,A\Gamma)
, where
V\Gamma
is a non-empty set and
A\Gamma
is a subset of
V\Gamma\times V\Gamma
. It is customary to refer to
V\Gamma
and to
A\Gamma
as the
vertex set
and the

of
\Gamma
, respectively, and to refer to their elements as
vertices
and
arcs
. Given an arc
(u,v)\in A\Gamma
,

is an
out-neighbour
of
u
and
u
is an
in-neighbour
of
v
. The
out-valency
and the
in-valency
of

is the number of out-neighbours and the number of in-neighbours of
v
. Moreover,
\Gamma
is
regular
if there exists a non-negative integer

such that every vertex

has out-valency and in-valency
d
.
Given a subset

of
V\Gamma
, we denote by
\Gamma[X]:=(V\Gamma\cap X,A\Gamma\cap(X\times X))
the
sub-digraph induced
by
\Gamma
on
X
.

The digraph
\Gamma
is a

if
A\Gamma
is symmetric, that is,
A\Gamma=\{(u,v)\ |\ (v,u)\in A\Gamma\}
.

An

of
\Gamma
is
a permutation
\sigma
of
V\Gamma
such that, for every
(u,v)\in V\Gamma\times V\Gamma
,
(u^{\sigma},v^{\sigma})\in A\Gamma
if and only if
(u,v)\in A\Gamma
. The

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
is the set of all automorphisms of
\Gamma
and it is indeed a group under composition of permutations.

Let
G
be a permutation group on a set

and let
\omega\in\Omega
. We let
G_{\omega}
denote the

of
\omega
in
G
, that is, the subgroup
of

fixing
\omega
. We say that
G
is

if
G_{\omega}=1
for every
\omega\in\Omega
, and

if it is semiregular and transitive. We denote by

the cyclic group of order

and by

the quaternion group of order

.

We are now ready to give the definition of the main player in this paper. Let

be a group and let

be a positive integer. An

-partite digraphical representation

(

-
\rm PDR
for short) of

is a digraph

such that

•

\Gamma
is regular,

•

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is isomorphic to

,

•

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
acts semiregularly on
V\Gamma
,

•

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
has

and, for every orbit

, the sub-digraph

induced by

on

is the empty graph.

Observe that the last condition implies that
\Gamma
is

-partite. The scope of this paper is to classify finite groups admitting

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a finite group and let

be a positive integer. Then

admits no

-partite digraphical representation if and only if one of the following occurs:

(1)

and

,

(2)

and

,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
or

and

We now discuss the roots of our interest on Theorem

.
The study of group representations on digraphs starts with the classical work on DRRs. A
digraphical regular representation
of
G
is a digraph
\Gamma=(V\Gamma,A\Gamma)
such that
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)\cong G
and
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
is regular on

. Babai
[
1
]
has proved that, except for

Q_{8},\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\,\hbox{ and }{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{3},

every finite group admits a DRR. This classification plays a crucial role in our work. Observe that the case

in Theorem

is trivial. Indeed, the last condition in the definition of

-PDR implies and a

-PDR is an empty graph. Therefore, a group having order at least

does not admit a

-PDR, whereas

and

do admit a

-PDR.

After the classification of finite groups admitting a DRR was completed, researchers proposed and investigated various natural generalisations. For instance, Babai and Imrich
[
2
]
classified finite groups admitting a tournament regular representation. Morris and Spiga
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
, answering a question of Babai
[

]
, classified the finite groups admitting an oriented regular representation.
For more results, generalising DRRs in various directions, we refer to

,
8
,

,

,

,

.
To give some more context to Theorem

, we give details to two more particular generalisations.

Given a positive integer

, a

-semiregular representation
(D

is a regular digraph
\Gamma=(V\Gamma,A\Gamma)
such that
\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)\cong G
is semiregular on

with

-PDR is also a D

-PDR because it may not be

-partite. Therefore, Theorem

can be seen as an extension of the classification in
[
8
]
of the finite groups admitting a D

SR. The second generalisation we discuss is concerned with Haar digraphs. Given a group

, a

\Gamma
over

is a bipartite digraph having a bipartition

such that

is a group of automorphisms of

and on

such that its automorphism group is isomorphic to

. We have proved in
[

]
that, except for

Observe that a digraph

is a HDR of

if and only if
\Gamma
is a
2
-PDR of

. Therefore,

-PDRs offer also a natural generalisation of HDRs. Furthermore, the classification in
[

]
of finite groups not admitting HDRs gives a classification of finite groups not admitting

-PDRs. In particular, Theorem
1.1
(
2
) follows from
[

]
. Thus, in the proof of Theorem

, we may suppose that

(1.1)

\displaystyle n\geq 3.

Despite the natural argument used by Babai for the classification of finite groups admitting a DRR, the classification of finite groups admitting a GRR has required considerable more work. Indeed, as a rule-of-thumb, representation results dealing with undirected graphs are difficult. For instance, we classify the finite groups

having an abelian subgroup

of index

admitting a bipartite DRR having bipartition

. The analogous classification for Cayley graphs is much harder, see

,

]
.
In fact, we do not have a classification of finite groups admitting a Haar graphical representation and hence we have no classification of finite groups admitting an

-partite graphical representation, when

. Incidentally, the case

is in our opinion the most important. In this context, we believe that the classification of finite groups admitting a bipartite regular representation can be of some relevance, see the introductory section in

]

We conclude this section observing that the work of Grech and Kisielewicz

,

,
6
]
, which in spirit is trying to give an explicit description of some classes of permutation groups that are

Preliminaries and notations

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
G
be a finite group. Consistently throughout the whole paper, for not making our notation too cumbersome to use, we denote the element
(g,i)
of the cartesian product
G\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
simply by
g_{i}
. We often identify
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, that is, with the integers modulo
n
.

For every
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, let
T_{i,j}
be a subset of
G
. The
n
-Cayley digraph
of
G
with respect to
(T_{i,j}:i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})
is the digraph with vertex set

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}G_{i},

where
G_{i}=\{g_{i}\ |\ g\in G\}
, and with arc set

\bigcup_{i,j}\{(g_{i},(tg)_{j})~|~t\in T_{i,j},g\in G\}.

We denote this digraph by

\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T_{i,j}:i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}).

Observe that, when
n:=1
,
1
-Cayley digraphs are nothing more and nothing less than Cayley digraphs. Similarly, when
n:=2
,
2
-Cayley digraphs are also known as BiCayley digraphs in the literature. When dealing with Cayley digraphs, we omit the subscript, that is, we denote the Cayley digraph of
G
with connection set
S
with
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,S)
.

Set
\Gamma:=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T_{i,j}:i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})
. For every

, the mapping

\displaystyle R_{n}(g):\,

\displaystyle V\Gamma\to V\Gamma

\displaystyle x_{i}\mapsto(xg)_{i}

is an automorphism of
\Gamma
. Thus

is a subgroup of

isomorphic to
G
. Since we are not interested in
G
as an abstract group, but rather as a group of automorphisms of digraphs, for convenience, we identify
\{R_{n}(g)\ |\ g\in G\}
with
G
. Observe that

•

G
acts semiregularly on
V\Gamma=G\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

•

G
has
n
orbits on
V\Gamma
and these are
G_{i}
, with

.

It is easy to see that a digraph

is an

-Cayley digraph of

contains a semiregular subgroup isomorphic to

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

and, to do that, we need one more auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.1
.

If the finite group

admits a
2
-
\mathrm{PDR}
, then

admits an
n

\mathrm{PDR}
for every

Let

be a finite group admitting a
2

,

(3.1)

G\ncong{\mathbb{Z}}_{1},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}\hbox{ and }{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}.

In particular,
|G|\geq 4
.
We consider two cases in turn: first

admits a DRR, then
G
admits no DRR.

Case 1:

admits a DRR.

By Propositions
2.2
and
2.4
,
G
has subsets

and

such that
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R)
is a DRR and
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R\cup\{1\},L\cup\{1\})

,
|R|<(|G|-1)/2
,

and
|L|=|R|
. We now divide the proof in two further cases, depending on whether

is an elementary abelian
2

Case 1.1:
G
is an elementary abelian
2
-group.

Since
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R)
is a DRR and
|G|>2
,
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R)
is connected and hence

|R|\geq 2
. From (
3.1
), we have

with

and, since

,

has a non-identity element

. Let
\Gamma
be the
n
-Cayley digraph
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T_{i,j}:i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})
, where

(3.2)

\displaystyle T_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle R\cup\{1\}\mbox{ for }i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n};\ \ T_{1,0}:=L\cup\{1\},\ T_{i+1,i}:=R\cup\{b\}\mbox{ for }i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\setminus\{0\};

(3.3)

\displaystyle T_{j,k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\emptyset\ \mbox{ for }\ j,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\mbox{ with }j\not=k\pm 1.

It is readily seen that
\Gamma
is a regular
n
-partite Cayley digraph. To prove that
\Gamma
is an
n
-PDR, it suffices to show that
A:=\hbox{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)=G
. Note that
R=R^{-1}
and

, because

is an elementary abelian
2
-group.

Let
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. Observe that,

•

when
i\not=j\pm 1
,
\Gamma[G_{i}\cup G_{j}]
is the empty graph (this follows from (
3.3
)),

•

every vertex in

•

when

, every vertex
in
\Gamma[G_{i}\cup G_{i+1}]

3.2
),

It follows that every vertex in
G_{0}
and

Note that

\Gamma[G_{0}\cup G_{1}]=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,T_{0,1},T_{1,0})=\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R\cup\{1\},L\cup\{1\}).

Since
\hbox{\rm Cay}(G,R\cup\{1\},L\cup\{1\})

2

is
G
and hence

fixes
G_{0}
and
G_{1}
setwise and
A_{1_{0}}
fixes
G_{0}
and
G_{1}

From (

),
T_{1,2}^{-1}\cap T_{2,1}=R

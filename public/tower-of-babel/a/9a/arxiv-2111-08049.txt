Medium induced gluon spectrum in the Improved Opacity Expansion
*

Abstract
Over the last decades, analytical calculations of jet quenching observables have always needed to make a distinction between dense or dilute mediums. Although there are different theoretical formalisms suited for each one of these scenarios, taking into account multiple soft and single hard interactions between the probe and the background under a single approach has proven to be a difficult task. In this talk, we will introduce the Improved Opacity Expansion (IOE), which extends the well established Opacity Expansion framework beyond the hard momentum transfer tail, including the regime captured by the BDMPS-Z/ASW approximation. We will focus on the application of the IOE to the computation of the single gluon medium induced spectrum from a hard parton, which constitutes one of the most important theoretical results in jet quenching theory.

2
A working example: momentum broadening in the IOE approach

To introduce the IOE formalism
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
we consider first the simplest possible observable: single particle momentum broadening. At eikonal accuracy, momentum broadening can be fully characterized by an associated broadening distribution
{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}},t)
which gives the probability for a parton to acquire transverse momentum
{\boldsymbol{k}}
in the medium as a result of its propagation for a time
t
. In terms of the in-medium elastic collision rate
\gamma_{\rm el}({\boldsymbol{q}})\sim g^{4}n/{\boldsymbol{q}}^{4}
, it reads
[
7
]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}},t)}{\partial t}=C_{R}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}}\,\gamma_{\rm el}({\boldsymbol{q}})\left[{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}}-{\boldsymbol{q}},t)-{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}},t)\right]\,,

(1)

where
C_{R}
is the associated Casimir color factor and
n
is the density of in-medium scattering centers.

For thin mediums, i.e. when the hard parton interactions a small number of times with the background,
{\cal P}
is dominated by at most a single hard scattering (SH) and it reduces to

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{\rm SH}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)=C_{R}\gamma_{\rm el}({\boldsymbol{k}})\,L\propto\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}C_{R}nL}{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}}\,.

(2)

Conversely, when the medium is dense the probability of scattering becomes of order one and all possible (soft) tree-level gluon exchanges with the medium have to be taken into account. In this regime of multiple soft (MS) scattering with the medium, Eq. (
1
) can be reduced to a diffusion equation, so that the leading order result reads

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{\rm MS}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)=\frac{4\pi}{\hat{q}L}{\rm e}^{-\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}{\hat{q}L}}\,,

(3)

where
\hat{q}
is the jet quenching parameter

\displaystyle\hat{q}=C_{R}\,\int^{q_{\rm max}}_{\mu_{\ast}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,{\boldsymbol{q}}^{2}\,\gamma_{\rm el}({\boldsymbol{q}})\approx 4\pi\alpha_{s}^{2}C_{R}n\log\frac{q_{\rm max}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}\equiv\hat{q}_{0}\log\frac{q_{\rm max}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}\,.

(4)

Notice that the in the infrared this integral converges due to the thermal mass (
\mu_{\ast}
) screening and in the ultraviolet the logarithmic divergence must be regulated by a large momentum scale
q_{\rm max}
. Comparing Eq. (
2
) and Eq. (
3
), it is clear that the SH solution properly describes the expected
1/{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}
Coulomb form at large momentum, while the MS solution recovers the typical Gaussian solution associated to the resummation of multiple exchanges of soft instantaneous gluons. Although both formulas cover the full range of phenomenological interest for jet quenching when combined, each approximation fails to properly describe the underlying physics in the regime of validity of the other.

The goal of the IOE expansion is to bridge the gap between the SH and MS regimes. For that we note that Fourier transforming Eq. (
1
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}{\partial t}=-\,v({\boldsymbol{x}}){\cal P}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)\,,

(5)

with

\displaystyle v({\boldsymbol{x}})=C_{R}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}}\,\gamma_{\rm el}({\boldsymbol{q}})\left(1-{\rm e}^{i{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)\propto{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}\log\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}\mu_{\ast}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}({\boldsymbol{x}}^{4}\mu_{\ast}^{2})\,,

(6)

where in the last term we used the fact that at sufficiently small distances the interaction potential is assumed to be of the Coloumb form, and thus any medium model should recover Eq. (
6
) in that regime. Also, by direct comparison with the above MS result, we observe that Eq. (
3
) is recovered by freezing the logarithm in
v({\boldsymbol{x}})
(via an extra scale
q^{2}_{\rm max}
).
To combine both these regimes, in the IOE, one introduces a matching scale
Q_{b}^{2}
to rewrite
v({\boldsymbol{x}})
, such that

v({\boldsymbol{x}})\propto{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}\log\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}\mu_{\ast}^{2}}={\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}\left[\log\frac{Q_{b}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}Q_{b}^{2}}\right]\propto v_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\delta v({\boldsymbol{x}})\,.

(7)

The first term recovers the MS solution (assuming
Q_{b}^{2}=q^{2}_{\rm max}
). Requiring that
Q_{b}^{2}\gg\mu_{\ast}^{2}
, the second term only becomes dominant at small
{\boldsymbol{x}}
(large
{\boldsymbol{k}}
), and thus it can be treated perturbatively. As a result the broadening distribution can be written as
[
22
]

\begin{split}{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)&=\int_{\boldsymbol{x}}\,{\rm e}^{-i{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{k}}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{4}{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}Q^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{\rm max}}\,\frac{(-1)^{n}Q_{s0}^{2n}}{4^{n}n!}\,{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2n}\log^{n}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}Q_{b}^{2}}\equiv{\cal P}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)+{\cal P}^{\rm NLO}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)+\ldots\,,\end{split}

(8)

where
Q^{2}_{s0}=\hat{q}_{0}L
and the matching scale must obey the recursive relation

\displaystyle Q^{2}_{b}\equiv Q^{2}_{s0}\,\log\frac{Q_{b}^{2}(L)}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}\,,

(9)

in order to eliminate spurious divergences (see
[
25
,
22
]
for a detailed discussion). The first two terms in the previous power series can be computed exactly:

{\cal P}^{\rm{LO+NLO}}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)=\frac{4\pi}{Q_{s}^{2}}{\rm e}^{-x}-\frac{4\pi}{Q_{s}^{2}}\lambda\left\{1-2{\rm e}^{-x}+\left(1-x\right)\left[{\rm Ei}\left(4x\right)-\log 4x\right]\right\}\,,

(10)

where
x={\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}/Q_{s}^{2}={\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}/(\hat{q}_{0}L\log\frac{Q_{b}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}})
and
\lambda=\log^{-1}\frac{Q_{b}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}\ll 1
. From the above discussion, one expects that perturbative term
{\cal P}^{\rm NLO}
correctly describes the hard
{\boldsymbol{k}}
tail, while the LO contribution is easily seen to recover the MS result. Indeed, a short calculation shows that at large momentum the NLO becomes dominant and reduces to Eq. (
2
):

\displaystyle{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{k}},L)^{\rm{NLO}}\Big|_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\gg Q_{s}^{2}}=4\pi\frac{Q_{s0}^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{Q_{s0}^{4}}{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{6}}\right)\,.

(11)

In Fig.
1
, we compare Eq. (
10
) to an exact numerical evaluation of
{\cal P}
for a realistic
v({\boldsymbol{x}})
(see
[
22
,
26
]
for details), explicitly confirming the above observations.

3
Medium induced spectrum in the IOE approach

In this section we discuss how to apply the IOE approach, illustrated for the broadening case above, to the medium induced (soft) gluon spectrum, differential in the gluon’s energy
\omega
and transverse momentum
{\boldsymbol{k}}
. In general, this can be written as
[
26
]

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\omega\frac{{\rm d}I}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{2\bar{\alpha}\pi}{\omega^{2}}\text{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t_{2}\,{\rm e}^{-\epsilon t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}{\rm d}t_{1}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}}\,{\rm e}^{-i{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}}\,{\cal P}({\boldsymbol{x}},\infty;t_{2})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{y}}{\cal K}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})_{{\boldsymbol{y}}=0}-\frac{8\bar{\alpha}\pi}{k_{\perp}^{2}}\,.

(12)

Notice that the previous formula only takes into account purely medium induced radiation off the nascent parton and the adiabatic phase
{\rm e}^{-\epsilon t_{2}}
ensures that interactions are properly turned-off at asymptotically large times. In the present form, one observes that the full spectrum can be written as the broadening of a gluon final state in the time interval
(t_{2},\infty)
, being preceded by its production via an effective kernel
{\cal K}
during
(t_{1},t_{2})
.

The treatment of
{\cal P}
follows the discussion in the previous section. To deal with the kernel
{\cal K}
, we use the fact that it obeys the following Dyson equation
[
28
,
23
]

\displaystyle{\cal K}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})={\cal K}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})-\int_{\boldsymbol{z}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\text{d}s\,{\cal K}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{z}},s)\delta v({\boldsymbol{z}},s){\cal K}({\boldsymbol{z}},s;{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})\,,

(13)

where
\delta v
is given in Eq. (
7
), up to an overall normalizing factor. Truncating Eq. (
13
) to first order in
\delta v
, we can write Eq. (
3
) to first order in the IOE as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}I}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm LO}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}+\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm NLO}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta v^{2})\,.

(14)

The first term corresponds to the MS result with a matching scale
Q_{r}
given by (see
[
26
,
25
]
for details)

Q_{r}^{2}=\sqrt{\hat{q}_{0}\omega\log{\frac{Q_{r}^{2}}{\mu_{\ast}^{2}}}}\,,

(15)

where
\sqrt{\hat{q}_{0}\omega}
is the typical transverse momentum accumulated by the gluon during the emission process. The NLO term takes into account the leading order corrections in
\delta v
. Explicitly these it can be written as

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\omega\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm NLO}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\bar{\alpha}\pi}{\omega^{2}}\text{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t_{2}\,{\rm e}^{-\epsilon t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}{\rm d}t_{1}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}}\,{\rm e}^{-i{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}}\Big[{\cal P}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{x}},\infty;t_{2})\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{y}}{\cal K}^{\rm{NLO}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})_{{\boldsymbol{y}}=0}

\displaystyle+{\cal P}^{\rm NLO}({\boldsymbol{x}},\infty;t_{2})\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{y}}{\cal K}^{\rm{LO}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})_{{\boldsymbol{y}}=0}\Big]\,,

(16)

where we used
{\cal P}^{\rm NLO}({\boldsymbol{x}},\infty;t)=-{\cal P}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{x}},\infty;t)\int_{t}^{\infty}{\rm d}s\,\delta v({\boldsymbol{x}},s)
and the first order truncation of Eq. (
13
):
{\cal K}^{\rm NLO}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})=-\int_{\boldsymbol{z}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\rm d}s\,{\cal K}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{x}},t_{2};{\boldsymbol{z}},s)\delta v({\boldsymbol{z}},s){\cal K}^{\rm LO}({\boldsymbol{z}},s;{\boldsymbol{y}},t_{1})\,.
Notice that the first order correction to the MS result given in Eq. (
3
) has a term related to modification of the emission kernel (followed by the usual Gaussian broadening), and a term describing the production of a gluon in the MS approximation followed by broadening dominated by single hard scattering contribution. Analogously to the exercise done in the previous section, one can show that the IOE exactly recovers the SH scattering behavior at large gluon energy and transverse momentum. Performing a slightly evolved calculation, it results that (see details in
[
26
]
)

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\omega\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm NLO}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}\approx\frac{8\pi\bar{\alpha}\hat{q}_{0}L}{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}}\left[3\gamma_{E}-4+\log\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}{4Q_{b}^{2}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}L}{2\omega}\right)\right]\,.

(17)

Unlike in Eq. (
11
), it seems that there is still a dependence on the matching scale
Q_{b}
. However, this dependence vanishes once LO subleading corrections are taken into account. This feature, i.e. the independence of the IOE on the matching scales at high energies, seems to be generic. The full spectrum at this accuracy exactly gives

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\omega\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm LO+NLO}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi\bar{\alpha}\hat{q}_{0}L}{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{4}}\left[3\gamma_{E}-4+\log\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}{4\mu_{\ast}^{2}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}L}{2\omega}\right)\right]=(2\pi)^{2}\omega\frac{{\rm d}I^{\rm SH}}{{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,,

(18)

as expected.

The expressions given above can be further simplified; a numerical code to compute them can be found in
[
29
]
with comparable computational cost with respect to previous implementations of the MS result
[
30
]
. To illustrate the accuracy of the IOE approach, in Fig.
2
we show the differential spectrum for two gluon frequencies (
\omega_{c0}\equiv\hat{q}_{0}L^{2}
), in a set up close to LHC conditions. We compare the result to an exact novel numerical solution (see
[
19
]
for details) and to the SH
[
14
]
result, corresponding to the case where there is a single hard scattering center in the medium. At a qualitative level, the IOE leading order expansion already seems to perform rather well, especially when compared to the either the SH or MS (i.e. the LO term in the IOE) approximations.

4
Conclusion and Outlook

We have illustrated how to extend the Improved Opacity Expansion to the case of the double differential medium induced gluon spectrum. Along with single particle momentum broadening, these two observables constitute the back-bone of many phenomenological jet quenching models of full jets. Thus, the IOE formalism allows for the first time to include the effects of both multiple soft and single hard scattering in the medium under a single framework.

An upside of the IOE approach is that it admits a semi-analytical treatment
[
31
,
32
]
. As a result, one can use this formalism in, for example, jet substructure studies, gaining important analytical control over how the MS and SH regimes affect desired observables. In addition, the simple final expressions obtained can be generalized to arbitrary medium time profiles and thus could offer an efficient avenue to implementing jet evolution in dynamical backgrounds.

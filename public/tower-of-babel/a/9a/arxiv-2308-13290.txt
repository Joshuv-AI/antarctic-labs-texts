p p -group Galois covers of curves in characteristic p p II
Jędrzej Garnek

Abstract
. Let k k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 and let G G be a finite p p -group. The results of Harbater, Katz and Gabber associate to every k k -linear action of G G on k ⁡ [ [ t ] ] k[[t]] an HKG-cover, i.e. a G G -cover of the projective line ramified only over ∞ \infty . In this paper we relate the HKG-covers to the classical problem of determining the equivariant structure of cohomologies of a curve with an action of G G . To this end, we present a new way of computing cohomologies of HKG-covers. As an application of our results, we compute the equivariant structure of the de Rham cohomology of Klein four covers in characteristic 2 2 .

1.
Introduction

Studying the equivariant structure of the cohomologies of a curve
X
over a field
k
with an action of a finite group
G
is a natural and well-researched topic. In the classical case, that is, when
\cha k\nmid\#G
, the equivariant structure of the module of holomorphic differentials was completely determined by Chevalley and Weil using the character theory of finite groups, cf.
[
5
]
.
When
\cha k=p>0
and
p|\#G
, the structure of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
becomes much more complicated. Recall that in this setting the character theory is of limited use and classifying indecomposable representations is a “wild” problem. Even indecomposable representations of
G=\mathbb{Z}/p\times\mathbb{Z}/p
in characteristic
p>2
are thought to be impossible to classify (cf.
[
4
]
). In the tame or weakly ramification case, one may
obtain some information on the image of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
in the K-theory of
G
, cf. e.g.
[
10
]
. Moreover, there are several results
for specific groups (see e.g.
[
33
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
2
]
) or curves (cf.
[
26
]
,
[
8
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
25
]
). Most of those results are proven by giving an explicit basis
of the cohomology of
X
. In this article and in its prequel
[
12
]
we propose a different strategy for investigating the equivariant structure of cohomologies in the case when
G
is a
p
-group.

From now on, we assume that
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
and
G
is a finite
p
-group. Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
k
with an action of
G
. As explained in
[
12
]
, we predict that the Hodge cohomology
H^{1}_{Hdg}(X):=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\oplus H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and the de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{dR}(X)

should decompose as
k[G]
-modules into certain global and local parts:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{Hdg}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\textrm{(global part)}\oplus\bigoplus_{Q\in Y(k)}H^{1}_{Hdg,Q},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\textrm{(global part)}\oplus\bigoplus_{Q\in Y(k)}H^{1}_{dR,Q},

where
\pi:X\to X/G=:Y
is the quotient morphism. More precisely, the global part should depend only on the “topology” of the cover
\pi
(i.e. on the curve
Y
and the stabilizer subgroups) and be the same for both cohomologies.
Moreover, for any given point
Q\in Y(k)
the local parts
H^{1}_{Hdg,Q}
,
H^{1}_{dR,Q}
should depend only on the ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}:=(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})_{Q}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,Q}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q}
(where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q}
denotes completion of the ring
\mathcal{O}_{Y,Q}
with respect to the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{Y,Q}
).

The goal of this article is to propose a new way of computing the local parts, by establishing a connection with the Harbater–Katz–Gabber covers (in short:
HKG-covers
).
The HKG-covers proved to be an important tool in the study of local actions and the deformation theory
of curves with automorphisms, see e.g.
[
3
]
,

[
6
]
,

[
7
]

[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,

[
30
]
and
[
22
]
.
For any
Q\in Y(k)
, one may construct an HKG-cover
\mathscr{X}_{Q}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
that approximates the cover
\pi:X\to Y
locally over

Q
, see below for a precise definition. It is natural to try to relate its cohomology with the postulated
local parts
H^{1}_{Hdg,Q}
,
H^{1}_{dR,Q}
of the cohomologies of
X
. Such a result would reduce investigation of cohomologies of
G
-covers
to HKG-covers. We show that this
philosophy is correct for generic
p
-group covers.

Let
k
,
G
and
\pi:X\to Y
be as above.
Denote by
g_{Y}
the genus of
Y
and by
B\subset Y(k)
the branch locus of
\pi
.
For any
{P\in X(k)}
denote by
G_{P,i}
the
i
-th ramification group
at
P
and let:

d_{P}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\#G_{P,i}-1),\quad d_{P}^{\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}(\#G_{P,i}-1),\quad d_{P}^{\prime\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 2}(\#G_{P,i}-1).

Note that
d_{P}
is the exponent of the different of
k(X)/k(Y)
at
P
. We assume that the cover
\pi
satisfies the following assumptions,
introduced in
[
12
]
:

(1)

the stabilizer
G_{P}
of
P
in
G
is a normal subgroup of
G
for every
P\in X(k)
,

(2)

there exists a function
z\in k(X)
(a “magical element”) satisfying
\ord_{P}(z)\geq-d_{P}^{\prime}

for every
P\in X(k)
and
\tr_{X/Y}(z)\neq 0
.

Recall that a generic
p
-group cover satisfies
1
and
2
(cf.
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
). By the assumption
1
, for
Q\in Y(k)
we may denote
G_{Q}:=G_{P}
and
d_{Q}:=d_{P}

for any
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)
.

Results of Harbater (cf.
[
16
]
) and of Katz and Gabber (cf.
[
20
]
) imply that for any
G
-Galois algebra
\mathscr{B}
over
k[[x]]

there exists a unique
G
-cover
\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
ramified only over
\infty

and such that there exists an isomorphism
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathscr{X},\infty}\cong\mathscr{B}
of
k[G]
-algebras.
The cover
\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is called the
Harbater–Katz–Gabber cover
associated to
(\mathscr{B},G)
.
Suppose now that
\pi:X\to Y
is as above. Then
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}

is a
G
-Galois algebra over
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q}\cong k[[x]]
for any
Q\in Y(k)
. Denote by
\mathscr{X}_{Q}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

the corresponding HKG-cover. Note that it might be disconnected. In fact,

\mathscr{X}_{Q}=\bigsqcup_{G/G_{Q}}\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ}
,
where
\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a (connected)
G_{Q}
-HKG-cover.
We give now an example of computation of
\mathscr{X}_{Q}
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
p=2
,
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and
G=\mathbb{V}_{4}
, the Klein-four group. Let

Y
be the elliptic curve with the affine equation
w^{2}+w=u^{3}
over the field
k
.
Consider the
\mathbb{V}_{4}
-cover
\pi:X\to Y
given by the equations:

\displaystyle y_{0}^{2}+y_{0}=w^{3}+\frac{1}{w^{7}},\qquad y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}=w^{5}+\frac{1}{w^{7}}.

The cover
\pi
is branched over the points
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in Y(k)
,
where
Q_{1}=(0,0)\in Y(k)
and
Q_{2}
is the point at infinity of
Y
. We give now the equations of
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}
and
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{2}}
. Since
\ord_{Q_{1}}(w)=3
,
we have
w=x^{-3}
for some element
x\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q_{1}}
,
\ord_{Q_{1}}(x)=-1
. By Hensel’s lemma, there exist
s_{0},s_{1}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q_{1}}

such that
s_{0}^{2}+s_{0}=x^{-9}
,
s_{1}^{2}+s_{1}=x^{-15}
. Let
z_{0}:=y_{0}+s_{0}
,
z_{1}:=y_{0}+y_{1}+s_{0}+s_{1}
.
One easily checks that

z_{0}^{2}+z_{0}=x^{21},\qquad z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}=0.

These equations define
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}
. Note that
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}=\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}^{\circ}\sqcup\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}^{\circ}
,
where
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}^{\circ}:z_{0}^{2}+z_{0}=x^{21}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Similarly, using the fact that
\ord_{Q_{2}}(w^{3}+\frac{1}{w^{7}})=-9
and
\ord_{Q_{2}}(w^{5}+\frac{1}{w^{7}})=-15
, one can show that
\mathscr{X}_{Q_{2}}=\mathscr{X}_{Q_{2}}^{\circ}
is given by the equations:

\displaystyle z_{0}^{2}+z_{0}=x^{9},\qquad z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}=x^{15}.

Note that
\mathscr{X}_{Q}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a trivial
\mathbb{V}_{4}
-cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
for any
Q\in Y(k)\setminus\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
.

For any
k[G]
-module
V
, we write
V^{\vee}
for the dual
k[G]
-module. Let
I_{G}:=\{\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\in k[G]:\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}=0\}
be the augmentation ideal of the group
G
. For any subgroup
H\leq G

we consider also the relative augmentation ideal
I_{G,H}:=\Ind^{G}_{H}\,I_{H}
, which can be treated as a submodule of
k[G]
(see Section
4
). Finally, the
k[G]
-module

I_{X/Y}
is defined by:

I_{X/Y}:=\ker\left(\sum:\bigoplus_{Q\in Y(k)}I_{G,G_{Q}}\to I_{G}\right).

The following is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Keep the above assumptions. We have the following isomorphisms of
k[G]
-modules:

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})

\displaystyle\cong k[G]^{\oplus g_{Y}}\oplus I_{X/Y}\oplus\bigoplus_{Q\in B}H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{Q},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}}),

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\cong k[G]^{\oplus g_{Y}}\oplus I_{X/Y}^{\vee}\oplus\bigoplus_{Q\in B}H^{1}(\mathscr{X}_{Q},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}}),

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(X)

\displaystyle\cong k[G]^{\oplus 2\cdot g_{Y}}\oplus I_{X/Y}\oplus I_{X/Y}^{\vee}\oplus\bigoplus_{Q\in B}H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q}).

Moreover, one can further decompose the local terms as follows:

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{Q},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}})

\displaystyle\cong\Ind^{G}_{G_{Q}}H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ}}),

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathscr{X}_{Q},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}})

\displaystyle\cong\Ind^{G}_{G_{Q}}H^{1}(\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ}}),

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q})

\displaystyle\cong\Ind^{G}_{G_{Q}}H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q}^{\circ}).

In the paper
[
12
]
we showed a statement similar to Theorem
1.2
, but
with a different form of the local parts (cf. ibid, Theorem 1.1).
The new result surpasses it in two aspects. Firstly, the local parts in
[
12
]
depended not only on the local rings, but also on the element
z\in k(X)

from the condition
2
. The local parts given in Theorem
1.2
depend only on the completed local rings,
as they come from cohomologies of
\mathscr{X}_{Q}
. Secondly, as the local parts in
[
12
]
were defined
as quotients of certain infinitely dimensional vector spaces, their computation for concrete examples was considerably challenging.
The local parts in Theorem
1.2
can be computed by giving bases
of cohomologies of
\mathscr{X}_{Q}
, which seems much easier then giving bases of cohomologies of
X
.
We present this strategy for Klein four covers in characteristic
2
, see below.
In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we use
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
together with a new description of the cohomologies of a HKG-cover (cf. Theorem
3.2
in Section
3
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
.
Suppose that
\pi:X\to Y
is a
\mathbb{V}_{4}
-cover of smooth projective
curves over
k
that satisfies the condition
2
(this holds automatically for example
if
Y=\mathbb{P}^{1}
). There exist 7 isomorphism classes of indecomposable
k[\mathbb{V}_{4}]
-modules that may appear
as a direct summand of
H^{1}_{dR}(X)
:

k,\,k[\mathbb{V}_{4}],\,N_{2,0},\,N_{2,1},\,N_{2,\infty},\,M_{3,1}\,\textrm{ and }\,M_{3,2}.

In fact, we provide a more precise result. Namely, in Theorem
6.1
we give a decomposition of
H^{1}_{dR}(X)
into indecomposable summands
in terms of local invariants of the cover for curves satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
.
It seems that it would be much harder to obtain this
result using
[

.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be as in Example
1.1
. We compute now the equivariant structure of
H^{1}_{dR}(X)
.
In order to compute
I_{X/Y}
, note that
I_{G,G_{Q_{2}}}=I_{G}
. Hence:

\displaystyle I_{X/Y}\cong\ker(I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}\oplus I_{G,G_{Q_{2}}}\to I_{G})\cong I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}.

By
[
12
, Corollary 1.2]
,
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}^{\circ})\cong I_{G_{Q_{1}}}^{\oplus 20}
and hence:

H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}})\cong\Ind^{G}_{G_{Q_{1}}}H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}}^{\circ})\cong I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}^{\oplus 20}.

One shows that
I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}\cong I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}^{\vee}\cong N_{2,\infty}
, see the proof of Lemma
6.4
.
By computing an explicit basis of the de Rham cohomology of

(see Proposition

), one proves that:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{2}})\cong N_{2,0}^{\oplus 6}\oplus M_{3,1}^{\oplus 4}\oplus M_{3,2}^{\oplus 4}.

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(X)

\displaystyle\cong k[\mathbb{V}_{4}]^{\oplus 2}\oplus I_{G,Q_{2}}\oplus I_{G,Q_{2}}^{\vee}\oplus H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{1}})\oplus H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}_{Q_{2}})

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce notation concerning algebraic curves and recall basic facts used throughout the paper.
For an arbitrary smooth projective curve
Y
over a field
k
we denote by
k(Y)
the function field of
Y
and by
g_{Y}
its genus.
Also, we write
\ord_{Q}(f)
for the order of vanishing of a function
f\in k(Y)
at a point
Q\in Y(k)
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}_{Y,Q}^{n}:=\{f\in k(Y):\ord_{Q}(f)\geq n\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. To simplify notation, we write

\Omega_{Y}
,
\Omega_{k(Y)}
and
H^{1}_{dR}(Y)
instead of
\Omega_{Y/k}
,
\Omega_{k(Y)/k}
and
H^{1}_{dR}(Y/k)
.
We often identify a finite set
S\subset Y(k)
with a reduced divisor in
\Divv(Y)
.
Thus e.g.
\Omega_{Y}(S)
will denote the sheaf of logarithmic differential
forms with poles in
S
. In the sequel we often use residues of differential forms,
see e.g.
[
17
, Remark III.7.14]
for relevant facts.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\pi:X\to Y
be a finite separable
G
-cover of smooth projective curves over a field
k
.
We identify
\Omega_{k(Y)}
with a submodule of
\Omega_{k(X)}
and

k(Y)
with a subfield of
k(X)
. We denote the ramification index of
\pi
at
P\in X(k)
by
e_{X/Y,P}
and
by
G_{P,i}
the
i
-th ramification group of
\pi
at
P
, i.e.

G_{P,i}:=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma(f)\equiv f\mod{\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{i+1}}\quad\textrm{ for every }f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,P}\}.

Also, we use the following notation:

\displaystyle d_{X/Y,P}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\#G_{P,i}-1),\quad d_{X/Y,P}^{\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}(\#G_{P,i}-1),\quad d_{X/Y,P}^{\prime\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 2}(\#G_{P,i}-1)

(
d_{X/Y,P}
is the exponent of the different of
k(X)/k(Y)
at
P
, cf.
[
31
, Proposition IV.§1.4]
).
Recall that for any
P\in X(k)
and
\omega\in\Omega_{k(Y)}
:

(2.1)

\ord_{P}(\omega)=e_{X/Y,P}\cdot\ord_{\pi(P)}(\omega)+d_{X/Y,P}

(see e.g.
[
17
, Proposition IV.2.2 (b)]
). For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and
Q\in Y(k)
we abbreviate
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})_{Q}
to
\mathcal{F}_{Q}

and
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})_{Q}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,Q}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q}
to
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}
. We write briefly
\tr_{X/Y}
for the trace

\tr_{k(X)/k(Y)}:k(X)\to k(Y).

Note that it induces a map

\Omega_{k(X)}\cong k(X)\otimes_{k(Y)}\Omega_{k(Y)}\to\Omega_{k(Y)},

which we also denote by
\tr_{X/Y}
.
Recall that for any
\eta\in\Omega_{k(X)}
and
Q\in Y(k)
:

(2.2)

\sum_{P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)}\res_{P}(\eta)=\res_{Q}(\tr_{X/Y}(\eta))

(see
[
18
, Proposition 1.6]
or
[
32
, p. 154,
(R_{6})
]
).

In the sequel we use also the following description of cohomologies on a smooth projective curve
Y
. Let
S\subset Y(k)
be a finite non-empty set. Write
U:=Y\setminus S
and let
\eta
be the generic point of
Y
. For any locally free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of finite rank we have a natural isomorphism (cf.
[
12
, Lemma 5.2]
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\cong\coker(\mathcal{F}(U)\to\bigoplus_{Q\in S}\mathcal{F}_{\eta}/\mathcal{F}_{Q}),

Similarly, if
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}=(\mathcal{F}^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{F}^{1})
is a cochain
complex of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules of finite rank with a
k
-linear differential
then (cf.
[
12
, Lemma 6.2]
):

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}(Y,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\cong Z^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})/B^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}),

where:

\displaystyle Z^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\{(\omega,(h_{Q})_{Q\in S}):\omega\in\mathcal{F}^{1}(U),h_{Q}\in\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\eta},\,\omega-dh_{Q}\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{Q}\quad\forall{Q\in S}\},

\displaystyle B^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\{(dh,(h+h_{Q})_{Q\in S}):h\in\mathcal{F}^{0}(U),\,h_{Q}\in\mathcal{F}^{0}_{Q}\quad\forall{Q\in S}\}.

Suppose now that
Y=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}Y_{i}
is a disjoint union of smooth projective curves.
Note that
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(Y_{i},\Omega_{Y_{i}})
,
H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{1}(Y_{i},\mathcal{O}_{Y_{i}})
,
etc. By abuse of notation, we will denote by
k(Y)
the total fraction field of
Y
, i.e.
k(Y):=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}k(Y_{i})
.

3.
Cohomology of HKG-covers

Let
k
and
G
be as in Section
1
. Suppose
\mathscr{B}
is a
k
-algebra with a
k
-linear
action of
G
such that
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{B}^{G}
equals
k[[x]]
. Results of Harbater (cf.
[
16
]
) and of Katz and Gabber (cf.
[
20
]
) imply that there exists a unique
G
-cover
\pi:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
ramified only over
\infty

and such that there exists an isomorphism
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}\cong\mathscr{B}
of
k[G]
-algebras. We call
\pi:\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
the
Harbater–Katz–Gabber cover
(in short:
HKG-cover
) associated to
(\mathscr{B},G)
.
The following natural question arises:

Question 3.1
.

How to describe the invariants of the curve
\mathscr{X}
(e.g. its Hodge and de Rham cohomologies) in terms of
\mathscr{B}
?

This problem was studied in several papers, see e.g.
[
23
]
,
[
24
]

and
[
19
]
. The listed articles
give for instance a way of computing
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
. However, this description depends on the choice of the Artin–Schreier tower and thus is impractical for our purposes. We offer an alternative approach
using certain distinguished elements in the total fraction field of
\mathscr{B}
.

We start by introducing the needed notation. Let
\mathscr{L}
be be the total fraction field
of
\mathscr{B}
. Then
\mathscr{B}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}k[[t_{i}]]
and
\mathscr{L}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}k((t_{i}))
. Similarly, let
\mathscr{K}=k((x))
be the fraction field of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}:=(x)
be the
maximal ideal of
\mathscr{A}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{i}=(t_{i})
be the unique maximal ideal of
k[[t_{i}]]
; we will identify it with the corresponding ideal of
\mathscr{B}
. Also, let
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
be the Jacobson radical of
\mathscr{B}

and let
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}:\mathscr{L}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the composition of the valuation
k((t_{i}))\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}

associated to
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
with the surjection
\mathscr{L}\twoheadrightarrow k((t_{i}))
. We denote by
G_{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})
the
j
th ramification
group of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Note that in particular,
G_{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})\cong\Gal(k((t_{i}))/\mathscr{K})
is the decomposition group of

\mathfrak{m}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
. From now on we make the following assumption:

(*)

G_{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})
is a normal subgroup of
G
.

This implies in particular, that all the extensions
k((t_{1}))/\mathscr{K},\ldots,k((t_{r}))/\mathscr{K}
are isomorphic and
G_{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})
is the decomposition group
of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
. Thus we may identify
\mathscr{L}
with
\prod_{G/G_{0}}k((t_{1}))
. We denote also from now on
G_{i}:=G_{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})
.
Let
e=e(\mathfrak{m}_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}):=\#G_{0}
be the ramification index of

\mathfrak{m}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Similarly, let

d:=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\#G_{i}-1),\quad d^{\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}(\#G_{i}-1),\quad d^{\prime\prime}:=\sum_{i\geq 2}(\#G_{i}-1).

For any topological
k
-algebra
C
, denote by

\Omega_{C}
the module of continuous
k
-linear Kähler differentials.
In particular
\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}=k[[x]]\,dx
,
\Omega_{\mathscr{K}}=k((x))\,dx
,
\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathscr{B}\,dt_{i}
,

\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathscr{L}\,dt_{i}
. Similarly, let

\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}=\frac{1}{x}\Omega_{\mathscr{A}},\quad\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}=\frac{1}{t_{1}\cdot t_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot t_{r}}\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}.

be the logarithmic Kähler differentials.

Write
R=\pi^{-1}(\infty)
for the ramification set
of
\pi
and denote
U:=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
,
V:=\pi^{-1}(U)
. For a future use, note that:

(3.1)

\mathscr{X}=\Ind^{G}_{G_{0}}\mathscr{X}^{\circ},

where
\mathscr{X}^{\circ}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the HKG-cover associated to
(k[[t_{1}]],G_{Q})
and
\Ind^{G}_{G_{0}}\mathscr{X}^{\circ}
is the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{G/G_{0}}\mathscr{X}^{\circ}
with the natural action of
G
. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula (cf.
[
17
, Corollary IV.2.4]
) for the cover
\mathscr{X}^{\circ}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
implies that
g_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}=\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime\prime}
.

We say that
z\in\mathscr{L}
is a
magical element
for the extension
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
, if

\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(z)\geq-d^{\prime}
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)\in\mathscr{A}^{\times}
.
In this context, we denote by
z^{\vee}
the dual element of
z
with respect to the trace pairing. In other words,

z^{\vee}
is defined by the following equalities for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
:

\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(g_{1}(z)\cdot g_{2}(z^{\vee}))=\begin{cases}1,&g_{1}=g_{2},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Keep the above notation. In particular, let
\mathscr{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the HKG-cover corresponding to

(\mathscr{B},G)
. Suppose that
z\in\mathscr{L}
is a magical element.
Then we have the following isomorphisms of
k[G]
-modules:

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})

\displaystyle\cong\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}}{\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}},

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})

\displaystyle\cong\frac{\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z^{\vee})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\cong\left\{(\omega,\nu)\in\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}}{\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}}\times\frac{\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z^{\vee})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}}:\quad\omega-d\nu\in\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}\right\}.

For future use, recall that for any
f\in\mathscr{L}
:

(3.2)

\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(f)\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\alpha},

where
\alpha:=\min_{i=1,\ldots,r}\lfloor(\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(f)+d)/e\rfloor
.
For the proof see
[
21
, Lemma 1.4 (b)]
.

We list now some properties of magical elements.
Note that if
z\in\mathscr{L}
is a magical element for
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
then there exists
1\leq i\leq r
such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(z)=-d^{\prime}
. Indeed, if we would have
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(z)>-d^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, then
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
by (
3.2
), which would yield a contradiction.
We prove now a partial converse of this fact.

Lemma 3.3
.

Keep the above assumptions. If
z\in\mathscr{L}
satisfies
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(z)\geq-d^{\prime}

for all
i=1,\ldots,r
and equality holds for precisely one
i
, then
z
is a magical element for
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

Let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathscr{L}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}k((t_{i}))
. Suppose that

\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z)=-d^{\prime}
and
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}(z)>-d^{\prime}
for
i=2,\ldots,r
.
Note that by (
3.2
) we have
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z_{1})\in\mathscr{A}
and
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z_{i})\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
for
i=2,\ldots,r
.
Let
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{K}_{0}\subset\mathscr{K}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathscr{K}_{n}=k((t_{1}))
be a tower of
\mathbb{Z}/p
-extensions.
Note that
\mathscr{K}_{i}
is a field with a discrete valuation
\ord_{\mathscr{K}_{i}}
.
Let
\mathscr{K}_{i+1}/\mathscr{K}_{i}
be given by the Artin–Schreier equation:

y_{i}^{p}-y_{i}=h_{i}\in\mathscr{K}_{i}\quad\textrm{ for }i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Note that
\ord_{\mathscr{K}_{i}}(h_{i})<0
for every
i=0,\ldots,n-1
, since otherwise
\mathscr{K}_{i+1}
wouldn’t be a field.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p\nmid\ord_{\mathscr{K}_{i}}(h_{i})
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
.
In this context one often says that
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
are the Artin–Schreier generators for
k((t_{1}))/\mathscr{K}
in standard form.
Then
[
12
, Lemmas 7.2 and 8.1]
show that
z^{\prime}:=y_{1}^{p-1}\cdot\ldots\cdot y_{n}^{p-1}

satisfies
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z^{\prime})=\pm 1
and
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z^{\prime})=-d^{\prime}
. Thus, for some
c\in k^{\times}
,
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z_{1}-cz^{\prime})>-d^{\prime}
. But this implies by (
3.2
) that

\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z_{1}-cz^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Therefore

\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z_{1})\equiv\pm c\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}}
and
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tr_{k((t_{i}))/\mathscr{K}}(z_{i})\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathscr{A}^{\times}
.
This ends the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Every étale
G
-Galois algebra
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
satisfying
(*)
has a magical element.

Proof.

Let
z:=(t_{1}^{-d^{\prime}},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathscr{L}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}k((t_{i}))
.
Then
z
is a magical element for
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
by Lemma
3.3
.
∎

In case when
\mathscr{L}
is a field, the definition of a magical element simplifies.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose that
G_{0}=G
(i.e.
\mathscr{L}=k((t_{1}))
). Then

z\in\mathscr{L}
is a magical element for
\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}
if and only if
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z)=-d^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.3
implies that if

\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z)=-d^{\prime}
, then
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)\in\mathscr{A}^{\times}
.
For the converse implication, note that if
\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(z)>-d^{\prime}
,
then
\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}
by (
3.2
).
∎

The following result will be crucial in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Keep the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
.
Then:

\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}\oplus H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}).

We prove Proposition
3.6
by induction on
\#G
.
Before the proof we need two auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.7
.

Keep the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
. Then for any
H\unlhd G
,
the extension
\mathscr{L}^{H}/\mathscr{K}
satisfies the condition
(*)
and has

z_{H}:=\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{L}^{H}}(z)
as a magical element.

Proof.

Note that the maximal ideals of
\mathscr{B}^{H}
are
\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{H}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Since

G_{0}\unlhd G
, we have
G_{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{H}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})=G_{0}H/H\unlhd G/H
. Thus

\mathscr{L}^{H}/\mathscr{K}
also satisfies the condition
(*)
. By the transitivity of the different (cf.
[
31
, §III.4,
Proposition 8]
):

d^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})=d^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H})+e(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H})\cdot d^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}).

By (
3.2
) we see that for
i=1,\ldots,r
:

\displaystyle\ord_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{H}}(z_{H})\geq\left\lfloor\frac{-d^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}})+d(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H})}{e(\mathfrak{m}_{1}/\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H})}\right\rfloor=-d^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{H}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}).

Moreover,
\tr_{\mathscr{L}^{H}/\mathscr{K}}(z_{H})=\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{K}}(z)\in\mathscr{A}^{\times}
. This ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
is a free
k[G]
-module.

Proof.

Let
V^{\circ}
be the preimage of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
on
\mathscr{X}^{\circ}
.
Using Riemann–Roch theorem, one sees that
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}(d^{\prime}\cdot R))\neq H^{0}(\mathscr{X}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}((d^{\prime}-1)\cdot R))
, since
\deg(d^{\prime}\cdot R)=d^{\prime}>2g_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}-2=d^{\prime\prime}-2
.
Let
z\in H^{0}(\mathscr{X}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}(d^{\prime}\cdot R))\setminus H^{0}(\mathscr{X}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}((d^{\prime}-1)\cdot R))
. Then
z
is regular on
V^{\circ}
and
\tr_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}(z)\neq 0
by Corollary
3.5
. Therefore
z
is a normal element for
k((t_{1}))/\mathscr{K}
by
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
and

\pi_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}}|_{U}=\bigoplus_{g\in G_{0}}g^{*}(z)\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}|_{U}
as subsheaves of the constant sheaf
\pi_{*}\Omega_{k(\mathscr{X}^{\circ})}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus:

\displaystyle H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})=\Ind^{G}_{G_{0}}H^{0}(V^{\circ},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\circ}})=\Ind^{G}_{G_{0}}\bigoplus_{g\in G_{0}}g^{*}(z)H^{0}(U,\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})\cong k[G]H^{0}(U,\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}).

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

We prove this by induction on
\#G
. Denote:

\displaystyle S_{G}(z):=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}.

For
\#G=1
this is true, as then
z\in k^{\times}
and Proposition
3.6
comes down to the equality:

k((t))\,dt=\frac{1}{t}k[[t]]\,dt\oplus k[1/t]\frac{dt}{t^{2}}.

Suppose now that
\#G>1
. Let
H=\langle h\rangle\leq G
be a central subgroup of order
p
(it exists by
[
9
, Chapter 6, Theorem 1 (1)]
).
Denote
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{X}/H
and
G^{\prime}=G/H=\{g_{1}H,\ldots,g_{s}H\}
. Then
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the HKG-cover
associated to
(\mathscr{B}^{H},G^{\prime})
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\omega\in S_{G}(z)\cap H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
,
\omega\neq 0
. Let
i\geq 0
be the largest number for which

(h-1)^{i}\cdot\omega\neq 0
(note that
(h-1)^{p}=0
, thus there exists such a number).
Denote
\omega^{\prime}:=(h-1)^{i}\cdot\omega
. Then
(h-1)\cdot\omega^{\prime}=0
, i.e
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{k(\mathscr{X}^{\prime})}
.
On the other hand,
\omega^{\prime}\in H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
clearly implies
\omega^{\prime}\in H^{0}(V^{\prime},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})
, where
V^{\prime}
is the image of
V
through
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
. Let
z_{H}:=\tr_{\mathscr{L}/\mathscr{L}^{H}}(z)
. Note that

z_{H}
is a magical element for the extension
\mathscr{L}^{H}/\mathscr{K}
by Lemma
3.7
.
We show now that
\omega^{\prime}\in S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})
. Let
\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\omega^{\prime}_{g}
, where
\omega^{\prime}_{g}\in\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}
for every
g\in G
. The condition
h(\omega^{\prime})=\omega^{\prime}
implies that
\omega_{gh}^{\prime}=\omega_{g}^{\prime}
for any
g\in G
.
Thus we can define
\omega_{j}^{\prime}:=\omega_{g}^{\prime}
for any
g\in g_{j}H
. We have:

\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{g\in G}g^{*}(z)\omega_{g}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\omega_{j}^{\prime}\sum_{g\in g_{j}H}g^{*}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}g_{j}^{*}(z_{H})\omega_{j}^{\prime}\in S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H}).

This shows that
\omega^{\prime}\in S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})\cap H^{0}(V^{\prime},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})
. But by induction hypothesis,

S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})\cap H^{0}(V^{\prime},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})=\{0\}
. Thus
S_{G}(z)\cap H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})=\{0\}
.

Denote:

\mathcal{Q}:=\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}}{S_{G}(z)\oplus H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})}.

We’ll show now that
\mathcal{Q}=\{0\}
. Suppose to the contrary that
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}
has non-zero image in
\mathcal{Q}
.
Let
i\geq 0
be the largest number such that
(h-1)^{i}\cdot\omega\neq 0
in
\mathcal{Q}
and denote
\omega^{\prime}:=(h-1)^{i}\cdot\omega
.
Then
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}^{H}
. Using the long exact sequence of group cohomology for

0\to S_{G}(z)\oplus H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\to\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0

we obtain:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to S_{G}(z)^{H}\oplus H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})^{H}\to\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}^{H}\to\mathcal{Q}^{H}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(H,S_{G}(z)\oplus H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})).

Note that
H^{1}(H,S_{G}(z))=0
, since
S_{G}(z)
is a free
k[G]
-module and hence also a free
k[H]
-module.
On the other hand, using Lemma
3.8
we see that
H^{1}(H,H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}))=0
.
Thus (
3.3
) implies that

\Omega_{\mathscr{L}^{H}}=\Omega_{\mathscr{L}}^{H}\to\mathcal{Q}^{H}
is a surjection. Therefore, without loss of generality we may assume that

\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\mathscr{L}^{H}}
. By induction hypothesis,
\omega^{\prime}\in S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})\oplus H^{0}(V^{\prime},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})
.
But this implies that
\omega^{\prime}=0
in
\mathcal{Q}
! Indeed,
H^{0}(V^{\prime},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})\subset H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})

and if
\eta=\sum_{j=1}^{s}g_{j}^{*}(z_{H})\eta_{j}\in S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})
for
\eta_{j}\in\Omega_{\mathscr{A}}^{\log}
, then

\eta=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\eta_{j}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}(g_{j}h^{l})^{*}(z)\in S_{G}(z),

i.e.
S_{G^{\prime}}(z_{H})\subset S_{G}(z)

Contradiction ends the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Denote the right hand sides in Theorem
3.2
by
H^{0}(z)
,
H^{1}(z)
,
H^{1}_{dR}(z)

respectively.
Let
S_{G}(z)
be defined as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
and denote:

\displaystyle S_{G}^{\vee}(z):=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{*}(z^{\vee})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{A}}.

The quotients in Theorem
3.2
are well-defined. Indeed,
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
implies that

S_{G}(z)\subset\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}
and
[
12
, Lemma 3.4]
yields
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}\subset S_{G}^{\vee}(z)
.

Proposition
3.6
implies that

\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}=S_{G}(z)\oplus H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R)),

since
S_{G}(z)\subset\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}
and
H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\cap\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}=H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R))
. Moreover, the residue theorem (cf.
[
32
, Corollary after Theorem 3]
) implies that
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R))=H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
.
Thus we obtain the desired isomorphism:

H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\cong\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}}{S_{G}(z)}.

In the sequel we will use (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) for the sheaf
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}

and the complex
\pi_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}^{\bullet}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
S=\{\infty\}
. Note that since
\pi
is affine,
H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\cong H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})

and
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}^{\bullet})
. Moreover, if we denote by
\eta
the generic point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
then
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})_{\eta}\cong k(\mathscr{X})
.
The pairing

k(\mathscr{X})\times\Omega_{k(\mathscr{X})}\to k,\quad(f,\omega):=\res_{\infty}(\tr_{X/\mathbb{P}^{1}}(f\cdot\omega))

induces both the Serre’s duality
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})^{\vee}\cong H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})
and the duality between
\Omega_{\mathscr{B}}^{\log}/S_{G}(z)
and

S_{G}^{\vee}(z)/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}
(cf.
[
12
, Lemma 3.5 (1)]
). This proves that

H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\cong S_{G}^{\vee}(z)/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathscr{B}}.

Recall that
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})=H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R))
and
H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\cong H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(-R))
. Hence,
by the exact sequence
[
12
, (6.2)]
, the de Rham cohomology of
\mathscr{X}
might be computed
as the hypercohomology of the complex
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(-R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R))
.
We obtain:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\cong\frac{\{(\omega,\nu)\in H^{0}(V,\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\times k(\mathscr{X}):\omega-d\nu\in\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}(R)\}}{\{(df,f+\nu):f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(V),\nu\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}(-R)_{\infty}\}}

In the sequel we identify the relative augmentation ideal

For any

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that

is such that for every
Q\in B

there exists

such that

. Then:

\displaystyle I_{X/Y}\cong\bigoplus_{Q\in B\setminus S}I_{G,G_{Q}}\oplus I(S),

where
I(S):=\ker(\Sigma:\bigoplus_{Q\in S}I_{G,G_{Q}}\to I_{G})
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
|B\setminus S|
. For
B=S
this is straightforward.
To prove the induction step, it suffices to show that if
S=S^{\prime}\cup\{Q_{0}\}

and

for some

,
then:

I(S)\cong I_{G,G_{Q_{0}}}\oplus I(S^{\prime}).

We treat both sides as submodules of

The isomorphisms are given by:

\displaystyle\Phi:I(S)

\displaystyle\to I_{G,G_{Q_{0}}}\oplus I(S^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\sum_{Q,g}a_{Qg}\cdot g_{Q}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g_{Q_{0}}+\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g_{Q_{1}}+\sum_{Q\in S^{\prime}}\sum_{g}a_{Qg}\cdot g_{Q},

\displaystyle\Psi:I_{G,G_{Q_{0}}}\oplus I(S^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to I(S),

\displaystyle\sum_{Q,g}a_{gQ}\cdot g_{Q}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g_{Q_{0}}-\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g_{Q_{1}}+\sum_{Q\in S^{\prime}}\sum_{g}a_{Qg}\cdot g_{Q}

(we abbreviate

to
\sum_{Q}
and
\sum_{g\in G}
to
\sum_{g}
).
Note that

•

\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g\in I_{G,G_{Q_{0}}}\subset I_{G,G_{Q_{1}}}

•

\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g_{Q_{1}}+\sum_{Q\in S^{\prime}}a_{Qg}\cdot g_{Q}\in\ker(\bigoplus_{S^{\prime}}k[G]\to k[G])
, since

\sum_{g}a_{Q_{0}g}\cdot g+\sum_{g}\sum_{Q\in S^{\prime}}a_{Qg}\cdot g=\sum_{g}\left(\sum_{Q\in S}a_{Qg}\right)\cdot g.

and
\sum_{Q,g}a_{Qg}g_{Q}\in\ker(\bigoplus_{S}k[G]\to k[G])

Analogously one checks that

In this section we recall basic facts concerning

•

Theorem 7.3
.

The basis of
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
is given by the differential forms:

•

x^{i}\,dx
for
i=0,\ldots,M-M_{0}-2
,

•

y_{0}x^{i}\,dx
for
i=0,\ldots,M_{0}-1
,

•

(y_{1}+\alpha y_{0})\cdot x^{i}\,dx
for
i=0,\ldots,m^{\prime}-1
.

Proof.

This is basically
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
. The cited article uses the following notation:

u=u_{\infty}:=y_{0}
,
v=v_{\infty}:=y_{1}
,
p:=h_{0}
,
q:=h_{1}
,
\pi_{\infty}:=\frac{1}{x}
,
w_{\infty}(j):=y_{1}+[\alpha]_{\geq M^{\prime\prime}-j}\cdot y_{0}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
[\alpha]_{\geq t}:=\sum_{i=t}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{i}x^{i}
for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus by
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
the basis of
\mathscr{X}
is given by
\{f\,dx:f\in\mathcal{B}\}
,
where:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{\infty,1}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\infty,2}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\infty,3},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\infty,1}

\displaystyle=\left\{x^{i_{1}}:0\leq i_{1}\leq M-M_{0}-2\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\infty,2}

\displaystyle=\left\{y_{0}\cdot x^{i_{2}}:0\leq i_{2}\leq M_{0}-1\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\infty,3}

\displaystyle=\left\{y_{1}\cdot x^{i_{3}}:0\leq i_{3}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{m-5}{4}\right\rfloor\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{w_{\infty}\left(i_{3}-\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{4}\right\rfloor\right)\cdot x^{i_{3}}:\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{4}\right\rfloor\leq i_{3}\leq m^{\prime}-1\right\}.

By adding the elements of
\mathcal{B}_{\infty,2}
to the elements of
\mathcal{B}_{\infty,3}
, one may replace
\mathcal{B}_{\infty,3}
by

\{(y_{1}+\alpha y_{0})\cdot x^{i}:0\leq i\leq m^{\prime}-1\}.

This ends the proof.
∎

Observe that by (
2.2
):

(7.2)

\displaystyle\res_{P}\left(\frac{y_{0}y_{1}}{x^{i}}\,dx\right)=\begin{cases}1,&i=1,\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(7.3)

\displaystyle\res_{P}\left(\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{x^{i}}\,dx\right)=\res_{P}\left(\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{x^{i}}\,dx\right)=\res_{P}\left(\frac{dx}{x^{i}}\right)=0

for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We recall also that the Serre duality is given by the pairing:

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\times H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\to k,

\displaystyle\langle f,\omega\rangle:=\res_{P}(f\cdot\omega).

In order to prove that the elements listed above yield a basis of
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
,
we start by establishing the linear independence of some of them. The idea is to use the
Hodge–de Rham exact sequence:

0\to H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})\to H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\to 0.

First, we investigate the dependence of the images of the elements in
H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})

and use Serre duality along with Theorem
7.3
. Then we are left with a dependence in

H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}})
and we may use Theorem
7.3

for the second time.

Lemma 7.4
.

The elements
(a_{i})_{i\in I_{1}}
,
(e_{i})_{i\in I_{2}}
,
(f_{i})_{i\in I_{2}}
,
(u_{i})_{i\in I_{3}}
are linearly independent in
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
.

Proof.

We extend the definition of
a_{i}
and
e_{i}
as follows.
Denote
a_{i}=(x^{i}\,dx,0)
for
i=m^{\prime},\ldots,M^{\prime}-1
and

e_{i}:=u_{i}
for
i=m^{\prime}+1,\ldots,M^{\prime}
. Note that for
i>M^{\prime}
:

u_{i}=\left(\left[\frac{h_{1}^{\prime}}{x^{i}}\right]_{\geq 0}\,dx,\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}\right)=\left(\left[\frac{h_{1}^{\prime}}{x^{i}}\right]_{\geq 0}\,dx,0\right)\quad\textrm{ in }H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}).

Moreover,
\left[\frac{h_{1}^{\prime}}{x^{i}}\right]_{\geq 0}
is a polynomial of degree
M-1-i
.
Hence:

u_{i}\in\Span_{k}(a_{j}:j\leq M-1-i)\setminus\Span_{k}(a_{j}:j<M-1-i).

Therefore it suffices to show that the elements
(a_{i})_{i=0}^{M^{\prime}-1}
,
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}
,
(f_{i})_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}
are linearly independent
in
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
. Suppose that for some
A_{i},F_{i},E_{i}\in k
we have:

(7.4)

\sum_{i=0}^{M^{\prime}-1}A_{i}\cdot a_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot e_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot f_{i}=0

in
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
. Then in
H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})
:

(7.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i}}=0.

We divide the proof into four steps.

Step I:
For
j=1,\ldots,M_{0}
we have
E_{j}=0

and for every
j=1,\ldots,m^{\prime}
:

(7.6)

F_{j}=\sum_{l=0}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{l}\cdot E_{j+l}.

Proof of Step I:
Using Theorem
7.3
, (
7.5
) and relations (
7.2
), (
7.3
) we obtain for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,M_{0}\}
:

\displaystyle 0=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i}},\quad y_{0}\cdot x^{j-1}\,dx\right\rangle=E_{j}.

Analogously, for any
j=1,\ldots,m^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i}},\quad(y_{1}+\alpha y_{0})\cdot x^{j-1}\,dx\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{l}\cdot E_{j+l}+F_{j}.

This shows that (
7.6
) is true.

Step II:
For any
i=1,\ldots,M^{\prime}
:

(7.7)

E_{i}\cdot\sum_{l=i_{0}(i)}^{i-1}\alpha_{l}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i-l}}\equiv E_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+y_{0}\cdot P_{i}(x)\pmod{\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X},P}},

where
i_{0}(i):=\max(0,i-m^{\prime})
,
P_{i}\in k[x]
,
\deg P_{i}\leq M^{\prime\prime}-i
.

Proof of Step II:
Take
P_{i}(x):=E_{i}\cdot\sum_{l=i}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{l}\cdot x^{l-i}
.
For
i\leq M_{0}
the equality (
7.7
) is immediate, since
E_{i}=0

by Step I. If
i>M_{0}
, then:

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot\sum_{l=i_{0}(i)}^{i-1}\alpha_{l}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i-l}}+E_{i}\cdot\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+y_{0}\cdot P_{i}(x)=E_{i}\cdot\left(\frac{y_{1}+\alpha y_{0}}{x^{i}}+\sum_{l=0}^{i_{0}(i)-1}\alpha_{l}\cdot\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i-l}}\right).

But
\ord_{P}(\frac{y_{1}+\alpha y_{0}}{x^{i}})\geq 4(M_{0}+1)-2M+m\geq 0
. Moreover, for
0\leq l\leq i_{0}(i)-1
one has
i-l>m^{\prime}
, which implies that
y_{0}/x^{i-l}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X},P}
.
This proves (
7.7
).

Step III:
There exist polynomials
S_{i},T_{i}\in k[x]
,

\deg T_{i}\leq M-i
such that in
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
:

\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\left(y_{0}S_{i}(x)\,dx+T_{i}(x)\,dx,\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}\right).

Proof of Step III:
Using (
7.6
):

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\sum_{l=0}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{l}E_{l+i}\cdot\left([h_{0}^{\prime}/x^{i}]_{\geq 0}\,dx,\frac{y_{0}}{x^{i}}\right).

Thus substituting
t:=l+i
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{M^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{t=l+1}^{l+m^{\prime}}\alpha_{l}E_{t}\cdot\left([h_{0}^{\prime}/x^{t-l}]_{\geq 0}\,dx,y_{0}/x^{t-l}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{m^{\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{l=i_{0}(t)}^{t-1}\alpha_{l}E_{t}\cdot\left([h_{0}^{\prime}/x^{t-l}]_{\geq 0}\,dx,\frac{y_{0}}{x^{t-l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{t}\cdot\left(Q_{t}(x)\,dx,\sum_{l=i_{0}(t)}^{t-1}\alpha_{l}\frac{y_{0}}{x^{t-l}}\right),

where
Q_{t}(x):=\sum_{l=i_{0}(t)}^{t-1}\alpha_{l}[h_{0}^{\prime}/x^{t-l}]_{\geq 0}\in k[x]
. Moreover,
since
\alpha_{i}=0
for
i>M^{\prime\prime}
, we have
\deg Q_{t}\leq\deg[h_{0}^{\prime}/x^{t-M^{\prime\prime}}]_{\geq 0}=m+M^{\prime\prime}-1-t
.
By (
7.7
):

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}F_{i}\cdot f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\left(Q_{i}(x)\,dx,\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}+y_{0}\cdot P_{i}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\left(Q_{i}(x)\,dx+d(y_{0}\cdot P_{i}(x)),\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot\left(y_{0}S_{i}(x)\,dx+T_{i}(x)\,dx,\frac{y_{1}}{x^{i}}\right),

where
S_{i}(x):=P_{i}^{\prime}(x)
,
T_{i}(x):=Q_{i}(x)+h_{0}^{\prime}(x)\cdot P_{i}(x)
,
\deg T_{i}\leq m+M^{\prime\prime}-1-i<M-i
.

Step IV:
By applying the equality from Step III
to (
7.4
) we obtain:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{M^{\prime}-1}A_{i}\cdot(x^{i}\,dx,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot([h_{1}^{\prime}/x^{i}]_{\geq 0}\,dx+y_{0}S_{i}(x)\,dx+T_{i}(x)\,dx,0).

In other words:

\sum_{i=0}^{M^{\prime}-1}A_{i}x^{i}\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}E_{i}\cdot([h_{1}^{\prime}/x^{i}]_{\geq 0}\,dx+y_{0}S_{i}(x)\,dx+T_{i}(x)\,dx)=0\qquad\textrm{ in }H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}).

Note that the leading term of
[h_{1}^{\prime}/x^{i}]_{\geq 0}\,dx
is
x^{M-1-i}\,dx
.
But by Theorem
7.3
, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,M^{\prime}\}
, the form

x^{M-1-i}\,dx
is not in the
k
-linear span of the forms
x^{j}\,dx
for
j<M-1-i

and
y_{0}x^{l}\,dx
for
l=0,\ldots,M_{0}-1
. Thus
E_{i}=0
for any
i
. Analogously,
Theorem
7.3
implies that
A_{0}=\ldots=A_{M^{\prime}-1}=0
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

The images of the elements
(a_{i})_{i\in I_{1}}
,
(b_{i})_{i\in I_{1}}
,
(c_{i})_{i\in I_{1}}
,
(e_{i})_{i\in I_{2}}
,
(f_{i})_{i\in I_{2}}
,
(g_{i})_{i\in I_{2}}
,
(u_{i})_{i\in I_{3}}
,
(v_{i})_{i\in I_{3}}

form a basis of
H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X})
.

Proof.

Note that the number of listed elements is

\displaystyle 3\cdot|I_{1}|+3\cdot|I_{2}|+2\cdot|I_{3}|

\displaystyle=3\cdot m^{\prime}+3\cdot m^{\prime}+2\cdot(M-m)=2g_{\mathscr{X}}.

Thus it suffices to show that the listed elements are linearly independent.
Suppose to the contrary that for some
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i},E_{i},F_{i},G_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\in k
:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i\in I_{1}}(A_{i}\cdot a_{i}+B_{i}\cdot b_{i}+C_{i}\cdot c_{i})+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}(E_{i}\cdot e_{i}+F_{i}\cdot f_{i}+G_{i}\cdot g_{i})+\sum_{i\in I_{3}}(U_{i}\cdot u_{i}+V_{i}\cdot v_{i}).

Then, by applying
(\sigma-\id)
we obtain by Lemma
7.2
:

0=\sum_{i\in I_{1}}C_{i}\cdot a_{i}+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}G_{i}\cdot e_{i}+\sum_{i\in I_{3}}V_{i}\cdot u_{i}.

Therefore by Lemma
7.4

C_{i}=G_{i}=V_{i}=0
. Analogously, by applying
(\tau-\id)
we obtain

B_{i}=0
. Hence we obtain:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i\in I_{1}}A_{i}\cdot a_{i}+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}(E_{i}\cdot e_{i}+F_{i}\cdot f_{i})+\sum_{i\in I_{3}}U_{i}\cdot u_{i}.

The proof follows by applying Lemma
7.4
one more time.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.5
.

Lemma
7.2
shows that the maps:

\displaystyle N_{2,0}\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}),\qquad

\displaystyle(1,0)\mapsto u_{i},

\displaystyle(0,1)\mapsto v_{i},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for any }i\in I_{1},

\displaystyle M_{3,1}\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}),\qquad

\displaystyle(1,0,0)\mapsto a_{i},

\displaystyle(0,1,0)\mapsto b_{i},\quad

\displaystyle(0,0,1)\mapsto c_{i},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for any }i\in I_{2},

\displaystyle M_{3,2}\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}),\qquad

\displaystyle(1,0,0)\mapsto e_{i},

\displaystyle(0,1,0)\mapsto f_{i},\quad

\displaystyle(0,0,1)\mapsto g_{i}.

\displaystyle\textrm{ for any }i\in I_{3}.

are equivariant. The direct sum of those maps is a
k[\mathbb{V}_{4}]
-linear map:

N_{2,0}^{M-m}\oplus M_{3,1}^{m^{\prime}}\oplus M_{3,2}^{m^{\prime}}\to H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{X}),

which is an isomorphism by Corollary
7.5
.
∎

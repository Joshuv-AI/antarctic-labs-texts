Graphical constructions of simple exclusion processes with applications to random environments
Alessandra Faggionato

Abstract
. We show that the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) on a countable set is well defined by the stirring graphical construction as soon as the dynamics of a single particle is. The resulting process is Feller, its Markov generator is derived on local functions, duality at the level of the empirical density field holds. We also provide a general criterion assuring that local functions form a core for the generator . We then move to the simple exclusion process (SEP) and show that the graphical construction leads to a well defined Feller process under a percolation-type assumption corresponding to subcriticality in a percolation with random inhomogeneous parameters. We derive its Markov generator on local functions which, under an additional general assumption, form a core for the generator . We discuss applications of the above results to SSEPs and SEPs in random environments, where the standard assumptions to construct the process and investigate its basic properties (by the analytic approach or by graphical constructions) are typically violated. As detailed in [ 14 ] , our results for SSEP also allow to extend the quenched hydrodynamic limit in path space obtained in [ 11 ] by removing Assumption (SEP) used in there. Keywords : Feller process, Markov generator, exclusion process, graphical construction, duality, empirical density field, random environment. Introduction

Given a countable set
S

apart from the hard-core interaction. The SEP is called symmetric (and will be denoted as SSEP

has to be well defined for all times: the holding time parameter

and a.s. no explosion occurs (whatever the starting site is).

The analytic approach in

3.
Graphical construction, Markov generator and duality of SSEP

Let
S=\{s_{n}:n=1,2,...\}
be an infinite countable set. We denote by
\mathcal{E}_{S}
the family of unordered pairs of elements of
S
, i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{S}:=\{\{x,y\}\,:\,x\not=y,\;x,y\in S\}\,.

(5)

To each pair
\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}

we associate a number
c_{\{x,y\}}\in[0,+\infty)
. To simplify the notation, we write
c_{x,y}
instead of
c_{\{x,y\}}
. Note that

c_{x,y}=c_{y,x}

for all
x\not=y
in
S
.
Moreover, to simplify the formulas below, we set

c_{x,x}:=0\qquad\forall x\in S\,.

The following assumption,
in force throughout all this section
, will assure that the graphical construction of the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) as stirring process is well posed.

Assumption SSEP.

We assume that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(C1)

For all
x\in S
it holds
c_{x}:=\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\in[0,+\infty)
;

(C2)

For each
x\in S
the continuous-time random walk on
S
starting at
x
and with jump probability rates
\bigl({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{y,z}\,:\,\{y,z\}}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\bigr)
a.s. has no explosion.

When Conditions (C1) and (C2) are satisfied, we say that the random walk
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
S
with jump rates (or conductances)
c_{x,y}
is well defined (for all times). This random walk (also called
conductance model
, cf.
[
3
]
) is built in terms of waiting times and jumps as follows.
Arrived at (or starting at)
x
, the random walk
waits at
x
an exponential time with mean
1/c_{x}\in(0,+\infty]
. If
c_{x}=0
, this
waiting time is infinite. If
c_{x}>0
, once completed its waiting, the random walk jumps
to another site
y
chosen with probability
c_{x,y}/c_{x}
(independently from the rest). Condition (C2)
says that, a.s., the jump times in the above construction have no accumulation point and therefore
the random walk is well defined for all times.

(cf. Lemma
7.12
), it is simple to check that
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup.
Its infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
is then the Markov generator
\mathcal{L}
of the SSEP with conductances
c_{x,y}
. We recall that
\mathcal{L}
has domain

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}):=\big\{f\in C(\{0,1\}^{S})\,:\,\frac{S(t)f-f}{t}\text{ has limit in }C(\{0,1\})^{S}\text{ as }t\downarrow 0\big\}

and is defined as
\mathcal{L}f=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{S(t)f-f}{t}
where the above limit is in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

Proposition 3.4

(Infinitesimal generator
on local functions
)
.

Local functions
belong
to the domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. Moreover, for any local function
f
, we have

\mathcal{L}f(\eta)=\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\eta(x)\bigl(1-\eta(y)\bigr)\left[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\right]\,,\;\;\eta\in\{0,1\}^{S}

(7)

and

\mathcal{L}f(\eta)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}}c_{x,y}\bigl[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\bigr]\,,\;\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\eta\in\{0,1\}^{S}}\,.

(8)

The sum in the r.h.s. of (
7
) and (
8
) are absolutely convergent
series of functions in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

The configuration
\eta^{x,y}
is obtained from
\eta
by exchanging the occupation variables at
x
and
y
, i.e.

\eta^{x,y}(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(y)&\text{ if }z=x\,,\\
\eta(x)&\text{ if }z=y\,,\\
\eta(z)&\text{ otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(9)

Moreover
, we recall that a function
f:\{0,1\}^{S}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called

if, for some finite

,

is determined by
(\eta_{x})_{x\in A}
(note that any local function is also continuous on

).
In what follows, we will denote by

the set of local functions, which is dense in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

The proof of Proposition
3.4
, given in Section
7.3
,
has several similarities with then one in
[

, Appendix B]
, where another graphical construction is used.

As in
[

]
, given

, we set

\Delta_{f}(x):=\sup\big\{|f(\eta)-f(\xi)|:\eta,\xi\in\{0,1\}^{S}\text{ with }\eta(y)=\xi(y)\;\forall y\in S\setminus\{x\}\big\}\,.

(10)

We also define

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:=\sum_{x\in S}\Delta_{f}(x)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}:=\sum_{x\in S}c_{x}\Delta_{f}(x)\,.

(11)

Then Proposition
3.4
can be extended to a larger class of functions by approximation. Indeed we have:

Proposition 3.5

(Infinitesimal generator on further good functions)
.

Let
f\in C(\{0,1\}^{S})
satisfy
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}<+\infty
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}<+\infty

Then
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
and

\mathcal{L}f(\eta)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}}c_{x,y}\bigl[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\bigr]
,
where the r.h.s.
is an absolutely convergent series of functions in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

The proof of the above proposition is given in Section
7.4
. Note that if

is a local function, then

except for a finite set of elements

. In particular, local functions satisfy both
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}<+\infty
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}<+\infty

For the next result we recall that a set
\mathcal{A}
is a core of
\mathcal{L}
if
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
and the graph of
\mathcal{L}
in

. We denote by

the continuous–time random walk on

with jump probability rates

Proposition 3.6

(Core for
\mathcal{L}
)
.

Suppose that

Then the family

3.3.
Duality
with the rw
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}

Due to its relevance for the study of hydrodynamics and fluctuations of the empirical field for SSEPs in random environment (see e.g.
[
5
,
11
,
16
]
),
we focus here on duality at the level of the density field. Apart from Lemma
3.9
, the notions and results presented below are discussed in
[
11
, Sections 6 and 8]
(the proofs in
[
11
]
can be easily adapted to our notation, since it is enough to take
\varepsilon=1
there and to replace
\hat{\omega}
and
C_{\rm loc}(\varepsilon\hat{\omega})
there by
S
and
C_{c}(S)
respectively).
We denote by
C_{c}(S)
the set of functions
f:S\to{\mathbb{R}}
which are zero outside a finite subset of
S
. Since
S
has the discrete topology,
C_{c}(S)
corresponds to the set of real functions with compact support.
We write
\mathfrak{n}
for the counting measure on
S
and we introduce the set
\mathcal{D}
given by

\mathcal{D}:=\big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})\,:\,\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)^{2}<+\infty\big\}\,.

We then consider the bilinear form
\mathcal{E}
with domain
\mathcal{D}
given by

\mathcal{E}(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)\big(g(y)-g(x)\big)\,,\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{D}.

On
\mathcal{D}
we introduce the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}
with
\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}:=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})}+\mathcal{E}(f,f)
. One can easily derive from
Condition (C1) that
C_{c}(S)\subset\mathcal{D}
. We then call
\mathcal{D}_{*}

the closure of
C_{c}(S)
in
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t. the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}}
(see
[
11
, Section 6]
). By
arguing as in

[
15
, Example 1.2.5]
the bilinear form
\mathcal{E}
restricted to
\mathcal{D}_{*}
is a regular Dirichlet form. As a consequence, there exists a unique nonpositive self-adjoint operator
{\mathbb{L}}
in
L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})
such that
\mathcal{D}_{*}
equals the domain of
\sqrt{-{\mathbb{L}}}
and
\mathcal{E}(f,f)=\|\sqrt{-{\mathbb{L}}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})}
for any
f\in\mathcal{D}_{*}
(cf.
[
15
, Theorem 1.3.1]
). By
[
15
, Lemma 1.3.2 and Exercise 4.4.1]

{\mathbb{L}}
is the infinitesimal generator of the strongly continuous Markov semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})
associated to the random walk
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
S

with jump probability rates
c_{x,y}
(defined in terms of holding times and jump probabilities as after Assumption SSEP). In particular, we have
P_{t}f(x):=E_{x}\bigl[f(X_{t})\bigr]
where
E_{x}
is the expectation referred to the random walk starting
at

x
. In what follows we write
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{L}})
for the domain of the operator
{\mathbb{L}}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Given a function
f:S\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\sum_{x\in S}c_{x}|f(x)|<+\infty
, we define
\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}f:S\to{\mathbb{R}}
as
\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}f(x):=\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)
.

Note that
the series in the r.h.s. of the definition of
\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}f(x)
is absolutely convergent by the assumption on
f
and the symmetry of the
c_{x,y}
’s (indeed

\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}|f(x)|=c_{x}|f(x)|<+\infty
, while
\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}|f(y)|
is finite since
we have
\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}|f(y)|=\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{y,x}|f(y)|=\sum_{y\in S}c_{y}|f(y)|
). In particular, if
f\in C_{c}(S)
then
\tilde{\mathbb{L}}f
is well defined.

Although not necessary to prove the hydrodynamic limit of SSEPs on point processes, the following result has its own interest since it makes the generator
{\mathbb{L}}
explicit on local functions:

Lemma 3.9
.

If
f\in C_{c}(S)
, then
f\in\mathcal{D}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbb{L}}})
and
{\mathbb{L}}f=\tilde{\mathbb{L}}f
.

The above lemma is proved in Section
7.6
.

We can now describe the duality between the SSEP with conductances
c_{x,y}
and the random walk with probability rates
c_{x,y}
at the level of the density field (i.e. the empirical measure). To this aim we recall that given
\eta\in\{0,1\}^{S}
the empirical measure
\pi[\eta]
is the atomic measure on
S
given by

\pi[\eta]:=\sum_{x\in S}\eta(x)\delta_{x}\,.

(14)

Given a real function
f
on
S
integrable w.r.t.
\pi[\eta]
, we will write
\pi[\eta](f)
or simply
\pi(f)
for the sum
\sum_{x\in S}f(x)\eta(x)
of
f
w.r.t.
\pi[\eta]
.
Trivially, if
\sum_{x\in S}|f(x)|<+\infty
as in the lemma below, then
f
is integrable w.r.t.
\pi[\eta]
for all
\eta\in\{0,1\}^{S}

Recall that
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\to C(\{0,1\}^{S})
is the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
associated to the SSEP with conductances
c_{x,y}
.

Lemma 3.10

(see
[
11
, Lemma 8.2]
)
.

Suppose that
f:S\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

\sum_{x\in S}|f(x)|<+\infty\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{x\in S}c_{x}|f(x)|<+\infty\,.

(15)

Then the map
\{0,1\}^{S}\ni\eta\mapsto\pi[\eta](f)\in{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous and indeed

\sum_{x\in S}f(x)\eta(x)
is an absolutely convergent series in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
. This map belongs to the domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
and

\mathcal{L}\big(\pi(f)\big)=\sum_{x\in S}\eta(x)\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}f(x)\,,

(16)

the r.h.s. of (
16
) being an absolutely convergent series in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

If in addition to (
15
) we have
f\in\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{L}})\subset L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})
(for example, if
f\in C_{c}(S)
), then
{\mathbb{L}}f=\tilde{\mathbb{L}}f
and in particular we have the duality relation

\mathcal{L}\big(\pi(f)\big)=\sum_{x\in S}\eta(x){\mathbb{L}}f(x)\,.

(17)

Identities of the form (
17
) are relevant to study hydrodynamics and fluctuations of the density field since they are associated to Dynkin’s martingales.
We point out that above we have considered the Markov semigroup of the random walk in
L^{2}(\mathfrak{n})
since particularly convenient for the stochastic homogenization analysis as in
[
12
]
. Of course, one could have considered as well the Markov semigroup on other functional spaces, as the space
C_{0}(S)
of continuous functions on
S
vanishing at infinity endowed with the uniform norm
.

4.
Applications to SSEPs in a random environment

We discuss some applications of the results presented in the previous section to SSEPs in a random environment.
We consider for simplicity
S={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, but the arguments and results we will present can be extended to more general graphs (see e.g.
[
14
]
)
.

We take
S={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
We denote by
{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
the set of undirected edges of the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. We take
\Omega:={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{{\mathbb{E}}_{d}}
, endowed with the product topology and the Borel
\sigma
–algebra
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
. We let
\mathcal{P}
be a probability measure on
(\Omega,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{B}(\Omega)})
. Given
\omega\in\Omega
we write
\omega_{x,y}
instead of
\omega_{\{x,y\}}
. Given
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
we write
\tau_{z}:\Omega\to\Omega
for the shift
(\tau_{z}\omega)_{x,y}=\omega_{x-z,y-z}
.

Given the generic environment
\omega\in\Omega
, we set
c_{x,y}(\omega):=\omega_{x,y}
if
\{x,y\}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
and
c_{x,y}(\omega):=0
otherwise.
We write
(X_{t}^{\omega})_{t\geq 0}
for the continuous-time random walk in the environment
\omega
with jump rates
c_{x,y}(\omega)
and with state space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\cup\{\partial\}
,
\partial
being a cemetery state (in case of explosion).

The properties stated in Section
3
hold
\mathcal{P}
–a.s. if
Conditions (C1) and (C2) are satisfied
\mathcal{P}
–a.s..
Trivially
(C1) is always satisfied. For (C2) (i.e. the random walk
(X_{t}^{\omega})_{t\geq 0}
a.s. does not explode) we have the following criterion:

Proposition 4.1
.

Condition (C2) is satisfied
\mathcal{P}
–a.s. in the following three cases:

(i)

d=1
and
\mathcal{P}
is stationary w.r.t. shifts;

(ii)

\mathcal{P}
is stationary w.r.t. shifts and
\int\mathcal{P}(d\omega)c_{0}(\omega)<+\infty
;

(iii)

d\geq 2
and under
\mathcal{P}
the coordinates
\omega_{x,y}
,
as
\{x,y\}
varies in
{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
, are i.i.d.

The proof of Item (ii)
will be
an extension of the arguments used in
[
6

since we are not assuming here that
\mathcal{P}
is ergodic and that
\mathcal{P}(\omega_{x,y}>0)=1
for all

will follow
from the results of
[
2
]
.

Proof.

We start with Item (i). Since
\mathcal{P}
is stationary, it is enough to restrict to the random walk starting at the origin.
Let
\omega
be an environment for which the random walk
X^{\omega}_{t}
starting at the origin has explosion
in path space
with positive probability. Since the sum of infinite independent exponential variables with parameters upper bounded by a finite constant diverges a.s., if the random walk explodes with positive probability (in path space)
then the parameter
c_{x}(\omega)
has to diverge for
x\to+\infty
or for
x\to-\infty
.

Now, given
M
, consider the random set
\hat{\omega}_{M}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,:\,c_{x}(\omega)\leq M\}
.
For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
consider the event
A_{M,k}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{B}(\Omega)}
defined as
A_{M,k}:=\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\max\hat{\omega}_{M}=k\}
. By the stationarity of
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{P}(A_{M,k})
does not depend on
k
. On the other hand, the events
A_{M,k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are disjoint. Since
1\geq\mathcal{P}(\cup_{k}A_{M,k})=\sum_{k}\mathcal{P}(A_{M,k})
we conclude that
\mathcal{P}(A_{M,k})=0
for each
M
and
k
.

By reasoning similarly for
\min\hat{\omega}_{M}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{P}
–a.s. for any
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
the set
\hat{\omega}_{M}
is empty or it is unbounded from the left and from the right.

This implies that
\mathcal{P}
–a.s.
\lim_{x\to+\infty}c_{x}(\omega)=+\infty
is violated and, similarly,
\mathcal{P}
–a.s.
\lim_{x\to-\infty}c_{x}(\omega)=+\infty
is violated.
This allows to conclude that
for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
the random walk
X_{t}^{\omega}
a.s. does not explode
.

We now move to Item (ii). At cost to enlarge the probability space by marking
edges by i.i.d. non degenerate random variables, independent from the rest, we can assume that

\mathcal{P}\big(\omega\in\Omega\,:\,\tau_{z}\omega\not=\tau_{z^{\prime}}\omega\text{ for all }z\not=z^{\prime}\text{ in }{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\big)=1\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}.}

(18)

We let
\mathcal{Z}:=\int\mathcal{P}(d\omega)c_{0}(\omega)<+\infty
. If
\mathcal{Z}=0
then
c_{0}=0

\mathcal{P}
–a.s. and, by stationarity,
c_{x}=0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

\mathcal{P}
–a.s.. In this case,
\mathcal{P}
–a.s., all sites are absorbing for the random walk and therefore (C2) is trivially satisfied. From now on we restrict to the case
\mathcal{Z}>0
. We define
\mathcal{Q}
as the probability measure on
\Omega
given by
\mathcal{Q}(d\omega)=\mathcal{Z}^{-1}c_{0}(\omega)\mathcal{P}(d\omega)
.
Due to the stationarity of
\mathcal{P}
, it is enough to consider the random walk starting at the origin
and show that a.s. there is no explosion
.

We introduce the discrete-time Markov chain on
\Omega
with jump probability rates
r(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
defined as follows: if
c_{0}(\omega)=0
we set

r(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\delta_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}
, if
c_{0}(\omega)>0
we set

r(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=c_{0,x}(\omega)/c_{0}(\omega)
when
\omega^{\prime}=\tau_{x}\omega
for some
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
r(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=0
otherwise. We write
(\bar{\omega}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
for the above Markov chain when starting at
\omega
. Note that, due to (
18
),
(\bar{\omega}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is well defined for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
and therefore for
\mathcal{Q}
–a.a.
\omega
.
Since
c_{0}(\omega)r(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=c_{0}(\omega^{\prime})r(\omega^{\prime},\omega)
, the probability measure
\mathcal{Q}
is reversible for the above Markov chain. We write
P_{\mathcal{Q}}
for the law on the path space
\Omega^{{\mathbb{N}}}
of the Markov chain
(\bar{\omega}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with initial distribution
\mathcal{Q}
.
Since
\mathcal{Q}
is reversible,
P_{\mathcal{Q}}
is invariant w.r.t. shifts.

We introduce now
a sequence
(T_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of i.i.d. exponential times of mean one

. Then

shifts when thought of as probability measure on the path space

. We write
\mathcal{I}
for the

By the ergodic theorem the limit

\lim_{n\to_{\infty}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{k})
exists
P_{\mathcal{Q}}\otimes P
–a.s. and equals the expectation of
T_{0}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{0})
w.r.t.

P_{\mathcal{Q}}\otimes P
conditioned to
\mathcal{I}
, which a random variable with values in
(0,+\infty]
. As a consequence,
P_{\mathcal{Q}}\otimes P(\mathcal{W})=1

where
\mathcal{W}:=\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T_{k}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{k})=+\infty\}
.
We observe that
\mathcal{Q}
is concentrated on
\{\omega:c_{0}(\omega)>0\}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{P}
are mutually absolutely continuous when restricted to this set. On the other hand, if
c_{0}(\omega)=0
, we trivially have that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{P}}_{\delta_{\omega}}\otimes P(\mathcal{W})=1

(the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\omega}}
is similar to the one of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}
)
. We conclude that

P_{\mathcal{P}}\otimes P\,(\mathcal{W})=1
.

Finally we can build the continuous-time random walk
X^{\omega}_{\cdot}=(X^{\omega}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with conductances
c_{x,y}(\omega)
and starting at the origin by defining its jump process (i.e. the sequence of states visited by
X^{\omega}_{\cdot}
in chronological order) as an additive functional of the Markov chain

(\bar{\omega}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
(here we use again (
18
)) and by using the exponential times
T_{k}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{k})
as waiting times.
The construction is standard: when the environment is
\omega
,
X^{\omega}_{\cdot}
starts at the origin and remains there until time
T_{0}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{0})=T_{0}(\theta)/c_{0}(\omega)
, afterwards it jumps to the site
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
\bar{\omega}_{1}=\tau_{x}\omega
and remains there for a time
T_{1}(\theta)/c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{1})
and so on. By (
18
), for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
the above construction is well defined (e.g. the above
x
is univocally determined).
The event
\mathcal{W}
then corresponds to non-explosion of the trajectory. Since
P_{\mathcal{P}}\otimes P\,(\mathcal{W})=1
, we conclude that for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
condition (C2) is fulfilled.

We conclude with Item (iii). We define
\Gamma(\omega)
as the graph with edges
\{x,y\}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
with
\omega_{x,y}>0
and with vertexes given by the points belonging to the above edges.
We write
V(\omega)
and
E(\omega)
for the vertex set and the edge set of
\Gamma(\omega)
, respectively. For
e\in E(\omega)
we set
t(e):=\min\{1,\omega_{e}^{-1/2}\}
and, given
x,y\in V(\omega)
, we set
\tilde{d}(x,y):=\inf\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}t(e_{i})\}
, where the infimum is taken over
all
paths
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})
from
x
to
y
in
\Gamma(\omega)
. Then by
[
2
, Lemma 2.5]
the r.w.
X_{t}^{\omega}
a.s. does not explode if for any connected component
\mathcal{C}
of
\Gamma(\omega)
there exists
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
\theta>0
such that

\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}\exp\{-\theta\tilde{d}(x,y)\}<+\infty\,.

(19)

We now define
\omega^{\prime}_{e}:=\max\{1,\omega_{e}\}
and
t^{\prime}(e):=\min\{1,(\omega^{\prime}_{e})^{-1/2}\}
for any
e\in{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
. Given

x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we set
\tilde{d}^{\prime}(x,y):=\inf\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{\prime}(e_{i})\}
, where the infimum is taken over
all
paths
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})
from
x
to
y
in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. For
e\in E(\omega)
we have
t(e)\geq t^{\prime}(e)
since
\omega^{\prime}_{e}\geq\omega_{e}
. Since in addition paths in
\Gamma(\omega)
are also paths in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we get that

\tilde{d}^{\prime}(x,y)\leq d(x,y)
for any
x,y\in V(\omega)
. In particular,
given
x\in\mathcal{C}
as in (
19
), the bound in (
19
) is true if it holds

\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\exp\{-\theta\tilde{d}^{\prime}(x,y)\}<+\infty\,.

(20)

We observe that under
\mathcal{P}
the new conductances
\omega^{\prime}_{x,y}
, with
\{x,y\}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{d}
, are i.i.d and lower bounded by 1. This is exactly the context of
[
2
]
. Then the bound (
20
) holds for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
due to Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 2.11 in
[
2
]
. We conclude that for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
condition (C2) is fulfilled.
∎

The
result
in Proposition
4.1
–(ii) is extended in
[
14
]
to prove the a.s. non-explosion (i.e. Condition (C2)) for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
for a very large class of random walks on random graphs in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with also a random vertex set (given by a simple point process).

We now give a simple criterion (based on Proposition
3.6
) to verify that the set of local functions
\mathcal{C}
is a core for the generator
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(\omega)
for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a. environments
\omega
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is stationary w.r.t. shifts and
\int\mathcal{P}(d\omega)c_{0}(\omega)<+\infty
. Then for
\mathcal{P}
-a.a.
\omega
condition (
12
) is satisfied. In particular, for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
the family
\mathcal{C}
of local functions is a core for the generator
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(\omega)
.

We point out that the SSEP considered in Proposition
4.2
is well defined due to Proposition
4.1
–(ii).

Proof.

Given the environment
\omega\in\Omega
, consider the process
environment viewed from the particle

(\bar{\omega}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, i.e.
\bar{\omega}_{t}:=\tau_{X_{t}^{\omega}}\omega
. By the definition of the translations
\tau_{z}:\Omega\to\Omega
we have
c_{X^{\omega}_{t}}(\omega)=c_{0}(\tau_{X^{\omega}_{t}}\omega)=c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{t})
. Moreover, because of the symmetry of the jump rates, we have that
\mathcal{P}
is a reversible (and therefore invariant) probability measure for the process. As a consequence, we have

\int\mathcal{P}(d\omega){\rm E}_{\omega}\big[c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{t})\big]=\int\mathcal{P}(d\omega)c_{0}(\omega)\,,

(21)

where
{\rm E}_{\omega}
is the expectation w.r.t. the process
environment viewed from the particle
starting at the environment
\omega
. By our assumption, (
21
) is finite. This implies that, for any
t\geq 0
,
{\rm E}_{\omega}\big[c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{t})\big]<+\infty
for
\mathcal{P}
–a.a.
\omega
. Hence, there exists
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
measurable with
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})=1
such that, for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}
,
{\rm E}_{\omega}\big[c_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{t})\big]<+\infty
for any
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
. This means that, for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}
,
E_{0}\big[c_{X^{\omega}_{t}}(\omega)\big]<+\infty
for all
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
.
We set
\mathcal{A}_{*}:=\cap_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\tau_{z}\mathcal{A}
. By the stationarity of
\mathcal{P}
we have
\mathcal{P}(\tau_{z}\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})=1
and therefore
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}_{*})=1
. Moreover, for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{*}
and
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
\tau_{x}\omega\in\mathcal{A}
and therefore it holds
E_{0}\big[c_{X^{\tau_{x}\omega}_{t}}(\tau_{x}\omega)\big]<+\infty
for all
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
. Using that
E_{0}\big[c_{X^{\tau_{x}\omega}_{t}}(\tau_{x}\omega)\big]=E_{x}\big[c_{X^{\omega}_{t}}(\omega)\big]<+\infty
, we get that (
12
) is satisfied for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{*}
. Proposition
3.6
allows to conclude.

∎

The proof of Proposition

can be easily adapted to more general SSEP in a random environment

, with symmetric jump rates

and on a random graph
\mathcal{G}(\omega)
as in

,

]
. More precisely, when considering models as in

where

is the Palm distribution associated to

(of course, one does not need to require all the assumptions in

).
One can apply the above observation for example to the

also with random conductances (assuring anyway stationarity). In this case

would be the probability measure
\mathcal{P}
conditioned to the event that

is in the infinite cluster (see

).

5.
Graphical construction and Markov semigroup of SEP

We discuss here the graphical construction and the Markov semigroup of the simple exclusion process (SEP) on the countable set
S
, when the jump rates
c_{x,y}
are not necessarily symmetric.

We denote by
\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}
the family of ordered pairs of elements of
S
, i.e.

\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}:=\{(x,y)\,:\,x\not=y,\;x,y\in S\}\,.

To each pair
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}
we associate a number
c_{x,y}\in[0,+\infty)
. It is convenient to set

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{x,x}:=0\qquad\forall x\in S}\,.

Note that
c_{x,y}
is not assumed to be symmetric in
x
,
y
.

We consider the product space
D_{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
endowed with the product topology. This topology is induced by a
metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
, defined similarly to (
6
):

d(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}):=\sum_{i\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-(i+j)}\min\big\{1,\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{K}_{s_{i},s_{j}},\mathcal{K}_{s_{i},s_{j}}^{\prime})\big\}
.
We write
\mathcal{K}=(\mathcal{K}_{x,y})_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
for a generic element of
D_{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
.

Definition 5.1

(Probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}
)
.

We associate to each pair
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}
a Poisson process
(N_{x,y}(t))_{t\geq 0}
with intensity
c_{x,y}

and with
N_{x,y}(0)=0
, such that the
N_{x,y}(\cdot)
’s are independent processes when varying the pair
(x,y)
in
\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}
. We define
{\mathbb{P}}
as the law on
D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
of the random object
(N_{x,y}(\cdot))_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}

and we denote by
{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot]
the expectation associated to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

The graphical construction of the SEP presented below
is based on Harris’ percolation argument
[
7
,
18
]
. To justify this construction we need a percolation-type assumption. To this aim we define

\mathcal{K}_{x,y}^{s}(t):=\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{y,x}(t)\qquad\text{ for }t\geq 0\,,\;\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\,.

\mathcal{E}_{S}
above is defined as in (
5
)
.
Note the symmetry relation
\mathcal{K}_{x,y}^{s}(t)=\mathcal{K}_{y,x}^{s}(t)
and that
\mathcal{K}^{s}:=(\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y})_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}}
belongs to
D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}_{S}}
. When
\mathcal{K}
is sampled with distribution
{\mathbb{P}}
,

\mathcal{K}^{s}
is a collection of independent processes and in particular
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}
is a Poisson process with parameter

c^{s}_{x,y}:=c_{x,y}+c_{y,x}\,.

We set
c^{s}_{x,x}:=0
for all
x\in S
.

From now on we make the following assumption,
in force throughout all this section
:

Assumption SEP
.

There exists
t_{0}>0
such that
for
{\mathbb{P}}
–a.a.
\mathcal{K}\in D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}}

the undirected graph
\mathcal{G}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})
with vertex set
S
and edge set

\{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\,:\,\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}(t_{0})>{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}(0)}\big\}
has only connected components with finite cardinality.

We point out that for
{\mathbb{P}}
–a.a.
\mathcal{K}
it holds
\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(0)=0
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}
and therefore
\mathcal{K}_{x,y}^{s}(0)=0
for all
\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}
. Hence, Assumption SEP remains unchanged if we replace
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}(0)
by zero there. On the other hand, the above choice is more suited for the construction of similar graphs for further time intervals as in Lemma
5.2
below.

Due to the loss of memory of the Poisson point process,
Assumption SEP

implies
the following property (we omit the proof since standard):

Lemma 5.2
.

For
{\mathbb{P}}
–a.a.
\mathcal{K}\in D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}}
the following holds:

\forall r\in{\mathbb{N}}

the
undirected graph
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})
with vertex set
S
and edge set
\{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\,:\,\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}((r+1)t_{0})>\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}(rt_{0})\}
has only connected components with finite
cardinality.

Trivially,
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{G}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})
for
r=0
.
We also point out that the properties appearing in Assumption SEP and Lemma
5.2
define indeed measurable subsets of
D_{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
:

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
the set
\Gamma_{r}
of configurations
\mathcal{K}\in D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}}
such that the graph
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})
has only connected components with finite cardinality is a Borel subset of
D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}}
.

The proof of the above lemma is trivial and therefore omitted (simply note that
\Gamma_{r}^{c}
corresponds to the fact that, for some site
s_{n}
, for each integer
k\geq 1
there exist distinct sites
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}
in
S\setminus\{s_{n}\}
such that
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{y_{i},y_{i+1}}((r+1)t_{0})>\mathcal{K}^{s}_{y_{i},y_{i+1}}(rt_{0})
for all
i=0,1,\dots,k-1
, where
y_{0}:=s_{n}
).

Trivially, Assumption SEP can be reformulated as follows:

Equivalent formulation of Assumption SEP
:
Given
t_{0}>0
, consider the random graph with vertex set
S
obtained by putting an edge between
x\not=y
in
S
with probability
1-\exp\{-c^{s}_{x,y}t_{0}\}
, independently when varying
\{x,y\}
among
\mathcal{E}_{S}
. Then, for some
t_{0}>0
, the above random graph
has a.s. only connected components with finite cardinality.

Remark 5.4
.

By stochastic domination, to
check
Assumption SEP one can as well replace
\{c^{s}_{x,y}\,:\,\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\}

by any other family
\{\bar{c}_{x,y}\,:\,\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}\}
such that
c^{s}_{x,y}\leq\bar{c}_{x,y}
for any
\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{S}
.

In the above Assumption SEP we have not required any summability property as Condition (C1) in Assumption SSEP. Indeed, this is not necessary due to the following fact proved in Section
8
:

Lemma 5.5
.

Assumption SEP implies for all
x\in S
that
c^{s}_{x}:=\sum_{y\in S}c^{s}_{x,y}<+\infty
,
\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}<+\infty
,
\sum_{y\in S}c_{y,x}<+\infty
.

Recall the definition of
\Gamma_{r}
given in Lemma
5.3
.

Definition 5.6

(Set
\Gamma_{*}
)
.

We define
\Gamma_{*}
as the family of
\mathcal{K}\in D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}}
such that

(i)

\mathcal{K}\in\cap_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Gamma_{r}
;

(ii)

the sum
\sum_{y\in S\setminus x}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{x,y}(t)=\sum_{y\in S\setminus x}(\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{y,x}(t))
is finite for all
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
;

(iii)

given any
(x,y)\not=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}
the set of jump times of
\mathcal{K}^{o}_{x,y}

and the set of jump times of
\mathcal{K}^{o}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}

are disjoint and moreover all jumps
equal

+1
;

(iv)

\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(0)=0
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_{S}^{o}
.

It is simple to check (also by using Lemmas
5.3
and
5.5
) the following:

Lemma 5.7
.

\Gamma_{*}
is measurable, i.e.
\Gamma_{*}\in\mathcal{B}(D_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\mathcal{E}^{o}_{S}})
, and
{\mathbb{P}}(\Gamma_{*})=1
.

We briefly describe the graphical construction of the SEP for
\mathcal{K}\in\Gamma_{*}
under Assumption SEP.

Given
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{S}
we first define a trajectory
(\eta^{\sigma}_{t}[\mathcal{K}])_{t\geq 0}
in
D_{\{0,1\}^{S}}
starting at
\sigma
by an iterative procedure. We set

\eta^{\sigma}_{0}[\mathcal{K}]:=\sigma
.
Suppose that the trajectory has been defined up to time
rt_{0}
,
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
As
\mathcal{K}\in\Gamma_{*}
all connected components of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{K})
have finite cardinality.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be
such a connected component and let

\begin{split}&\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\}=\\
&\bigl\{s\,:\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(s)=\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(s-)+1\text{ for some }x\not=y\text{ in }\mathcal{C},\;rt_{0}<s\leq(r+1)t_{0}\bigr\}\,.\end{split}

(22)

The local evolution
\eta^{\sigma}_{t}[\mathcal{K}](z)
with
z\in\mathcal{C}
and
rt_{0}<t\leq(r+1)t_{0}
is described as follows.
Start with
\eta^{\sigma}_{rt_{0}}[\mathcal{K}]
as configuration at time
rt_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}
. At time
s_{1}
move a particle from
x
to
y\not=x
with
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
if, just before time
s_{1}
, it holds:

(i)

site
x
is occupied and site
y
is empty;

(ii)

\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(s_{1})=\mathcal{K}_{x,y}(s_{1}-)+1
.

Note that, since
\mathcal{K}\in\Gamma_{*}
,
the set (
22
) is indeed finite and
there exists at most one ordered pair
(x,y)

satisfying (i) and (ii)
. After this first step,
repeat the same operation as above orderly for times
s_{2},s_{3},\dots,s_{k}
. Then move to another connected component of
\mathcal{G}_{t_{0}}^{r}(\mathcal{K})
and repeat the above construction and so on.
As
the connected components are disjoint, the resulting path does not
depend on the order by which we choose the connected components in the above algorithm (we could
as well
proceed simultaneously with all connected components).
This procedure defines
(\eta^{\sigma}_{t}[\mathcal{K}])_{rt_{0}<t\leq(r+1)t_{0}}
.
Starting with
r=0
and progressively increasing
r
by
+1
we get the trajectory
\eta^{\sigma}_{\cdot}[\mathcal{K}]=(\eta^{\sigma}_{t}[\mathcal{K}])_{t\geq 0}
.

The filtered measurable space
(D_{\{0,1\}^{S}},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{F})
is defined as in Section
3.1
. Again the space
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
of real continuous functions on
\{0,1\}^{S}
is endowed with the uniform topology.
Given
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{S}
, we define
{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}
as the probability measure on the above filtered measurable space given by

{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}(A):={\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{K}\in\Gamma_{*}\,:\,\eta^{\sigma}_{\cdot}[\mathcal{K}]\in A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
.
By Lemma
8.3
in Section
8
the set

\{\mathcal{K}\in\Gamma_{*}\,:\,\eta^{\sigma}_{\cdot}[\mathcal{K}]\in A\}
is indeed measurable and therefore
{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}
is well defined.

Similarly to Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
we have the following results (see Section
8.1
and
8.2
for their proofs):

Proposition 5.8

(Construction of SEP)
.

The family
\big\{{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}:\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{S}\big\}
of probability measures
on the filtered measurable space
(D_{\{0,1\}^{S}},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{F})

is a Markov process (called
simple exclusion process with rates
c_{x,y}
), i.e.

(i)

{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}(\eta_{0}=\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{S}
;

(ii)

for any
A\in\mathcal{F}
the function
\{0,1\}^{S}\ni\sigma\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}(A)\in[0,1]
is measurable;

(iii)

for any
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{S}
and
A\in\mathcal{F}
it holds

{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}(\eta_{t+\cdot}\in A\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t})={\mathbb{P}}^{\eta_{t}}(A)

{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}
–a.s.

Remark 5.9
.

By changing
t_{0}
in the graphs
\mathcal{G}_{t_{0}}^{r}(\mathcal{K})
, for
{\mathbb{P}}
–a.a.
\mathcal{K}
the path
\eta^{\sigma}_{\cdot}[\mathcal{K}]
constructed above does not change, and this for any
\sigma
. In particular, the above SEP does not depend on the particular
t_{0}
for which Assumption SEP holds.

Proposition 5.10

(Feller property)
.

Given
f\in C(\{0,1\}^{S})
and given
t\geq 0
, the map
S_{t}f(\sigma):={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\big(\eta^{\sigma}_{t}[\mathcal{K}]\big)\right]=\int d{\mathbb{P}}^{\sigma}(\eta_{\cdot})f(\eta_{t})

belongs to
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
. In particular, the SEP with rates
c_{x,y}
is a Feller process.

Proposition 5.11

(Infinitesimal generator
on local functions
)
.

Local functions belong to the domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
of the infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
of the SEP with rates
c_{x,y}
. Moreover, for any local function
f
, we have

\mathcal{L}f(\eta)=\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}c_{x,y}\,\eta(x)\bigl(1-\eta(y)\bigr)\left[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\right]\,,\;\;\eta\in\{0,1\}^{S}\,.

(23)

The
series
in the r.h.s. is
an absolutely convergent series of functions in
C(\{0,1\}^{S})
.

We now show that Proposition
5.11
can be extended to a larger class of functions.
To this aim, given
f\in C(\{0,1\}^{S})
, recall the definition of
\Delta_{f}(x)
given in (
10
) and recall that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:=\sum_{x\in S}\Delta_{f}(x)
(cf. (
11
)). While in the symmetric case we considered

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}:=\sum_{x\in S}c_{x}\Delta_{f}(x)

Let us point out an important symmetry (called below

Proof of Lemma
3.9

We need to prove for any

Since functions

are finite linear combinations of Kronecker’s functions, it is enough to consider the case

for a fixed

.
To this aim we write

for the number of jumps performed by the random walk in the time window
[0,t]
.
Recall that
P_{t}f(x)=E_{x}\big[f(X_{t})\big]

We write

for the probability on the path space associated to the random walk starting at

We have

\begin{split}&\frac{P_{t}f(x)-f(x)}{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{L}}f(x)=f(x)\Big[\frac{P_{x}(N=0)-1}{t}-c_{x}\Big]\\
&+\sum_{y}f(y)\Big[\frac{P_{x}(N=1,\,X_{t}=y)}{t}-c_{x,y}\Big]+\frac{P_{x}(N\geq 2,\,X_{t}=x_{0})}{t}\,.\end{split}

As a consequence (recall that

)
to get (

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\Big[\frac{e^{-c_{x_{0}}t}-1}{t}-c_{x_{0}}\Big]^{2}=0\,,

(59)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{x\in S}\Big[c_{x,x_{0}}\Big(\frac{\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-c_{x}s}e^{-c_{x_{0}}(t-s)}}{t}-1\Big)\Big]^{2}=0\,,

(60)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{x\in S}\frac{1}{t^{2}}P_{x}\big(X_{t}=x_{0},N\geq 2\big)^{2}=0\,.

(61)

(
59
) is trivial.
(

) can be rewritten as (recall that

)

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{x\in S}c_{x_{0},x}^{2}F_{t}(x)=0\,,\qquad F_{t}(x):=\Big(\frac{\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-c_{x}s}e^{-c_{x_{0}}(t-s)}}{t}-1\Big)^{2}\,.

In the notation of

, Chapter I.3]

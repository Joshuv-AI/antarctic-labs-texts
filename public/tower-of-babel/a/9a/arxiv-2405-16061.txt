Galois descent of splendid Rickard equivalences for blocks of p p -nilpotent groups
Sam K. Miller

Abstract
. We strengthen results of Boltje and Yilmaz regarding Galois descent of equivalences of blocks of p p -nilpotent groups, and a result of Kessar and Linckelmann regarding Galois descent of splendid Rickard equivalences for blocks with compatible Galois stabilizers. A more general descent criteria for chain complexes is proven along the way, which requires the adaptation of a theorem of Reiner for chain complexes. This verifies Kessar and Linckelmann’s refinement of Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture for blocks of p p -nilpotent groups with abelian Sylow p p -subgroup.

Notation and conventions.

For this paper, we follow the notation and setup of
[

(K,\mathcal{O},k)\subseteq(K^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
be an extension of

\mathcal{O}

\mathcal{O}

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}

\sigma
. This lift is unique since
\mathcal{O}

We use the following notation for Galois twists for extensions of commutative rings

\mathcal{O}
-algebra

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}

We obtain functors

-_{\mathcal{O}}:{}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{mod}\text{ and }\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}-:{}_{A}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod},

Let

-module

and a morphism

-linear categories, where we regard

Lemma 4
.

Let
C\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
, and set
E(C):=\operatorname{End}_{A}(C)
and
\tilde{E}(C):=E(C)/J(E(C))
. Each decomposition of
C^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}C
into a direct sum of indecomposable subcomplexes

C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}D_{j}

induces a corresponding decomposition of
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C)
into indecomposable left ideals,

k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}L_{j},

where
D_{j}
and
L_{j}
correspond via the idempotent
\tau_{j}\in E(C^{\prime})
for which
\tau_{j}(C^{\prime})=D_{j}
. Moreover, for all
i,j
we have
D_{i}\cong D_{j}
as
A
-complexes if and only if
L_{i}\cong L_{j}
as left
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C)
-modules.

Proof.

First, note that for any chain complexes
C,D\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\prime}}(C^{\prime},D^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{O}})\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D),

where the first isomorphism arises from adjunction, and the second isomorphism is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{O}})

\displaystyle a\otimes f

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(m_{i}\mapsto a\otimes f_{i}(m_{i})\big)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

where
a\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
f=\{f_{i}:C_{i}\to D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a chain complex homomorphism
C\to D
(the author thanks D. Benson for his suggestion
[
7
]
). One may verify this is a well-defined homomorphism for chain complexes. Moreover, the homomorphism is surjective, as
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{O}})
is spanned by homomorphisms of the form
m\mapsto a\otimes f_{i}(m)
in degree
i
, where
a\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
f=\{f_{i}:C_{i}\to D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a chain complex homomorphism
C\to D
. This follows from the identification
D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{O}}\cong D^{\oplus d}
of
A
-chain complexes, which implies
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{O}})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(C,D)^{\oplus d}
. The surjection is therefore injective as well, by rank counting. Moreover if
C=D
, the composite of the two isomorphisms is an
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-algebra isomorphism, as in this case the composite is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{End}_{A}(C)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{End}_{A^{\prime}}(C^{\prime})

\displaystyle a\otimes f

\displaystyle\mapsto m_{a}\otimes f

where
m_{a}
is the multiplication by
a
map. Therefore, we have an isomorphism
E(C^{\prime})=\operatorname{End}_{A^{\prime}}(C^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C)
of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-algebras. Now, choose

I=p\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C)+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}J(E(C)),

then
I
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C)
contained in
J(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C))
. We may also regard
I
as a two-sided ideal of
E(C^{\prime})
via the isomorphism
E(C^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C).
Since
I
is contained in
J(E(C^{\prime}))
,
E(C^{\prime})
decomposes into left ideals in the same way as the factor ring
E(C^{\prime})/I
. However,

E(C^{\prime})/I\cong(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E(C))/I\cong k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C).

Now,
[
11
, Corollary 4.6.12]
asserts that a decomposition
C^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}D_{j}
into indecomposable summands corresponds bijectively to a decomposition of
\operatorname{id}\in E(C^{\prime})
into primitive idempotents
\tau_{i}
with
\tau_{i}(C^{\prime})=D_{i}
and
\tau_{i}
is
E(C^{\prime})
-conjugate to
\tau_{j}
if and only if
D_{i}\cong D_{j}
. Therefore, we obtain a corresponding decomposition of
E(C^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}K_{j}
into indecomposable left ideals, with
K_{i}=E(C^{\prime})\tau_{i}
,
E(K_{i})\cong\tau_{i}E(C^{\prime})\tau_{i}
, and moreover,
D_{i}\cong D_{j}
if and only if
K_{i}\cong K_{j}
as
E(C^{\prime})
-modules. On the other hand, since
I\subseteq J(E(C^{\prime}))
, the decomposition
E(C^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}K_{j}
also corresponds bijectively to a decomposition
E(C^{\prime})/I=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}L_{j}
into indecomposable left ideals, with
L_{i}\cong L_{j}
if and only if
K_{i}\cong K_{j}
. The result follows.

The following proposition is an extension of
[
13
, Theorem 3]
for chain complexes.

Proposition 5
.

For each indecomposable chain complex of finitely generated
A
-modules
C
, the chain complex of
A^{\prime}
-modules
C^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}C
is a direct sum of nonisomorphic indecomposable subcomplexes.

Proof.

We use the notation from the previous lemma. Note
\tilde{E}(C)
is semisimple, but since
C
is indecomposable,
\tilde{E}(C)
is a division algebra over
k
. However,
k
is finite; hence
\tilde{E}(C)
is as well, and thus a field by Wedderburn’s theorem. Then
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C)
is a semisimple commutative algebra by
[
5
, Theorem 7.8]
. Therefore, it is a direct sum of fields, none of which are isomorphic as
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}(C)
-modules. The result now follows from the previous lemma.
∎

The following theorem demonstrates the necessary and sufficient condition for a chain complex of
A^{\prime}
-modules to descend to a chain complex of
A
-modules is Galois stability.

Theorem 6
.

(a)

Suppose
C\in Ch^{b}({}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod})
is indecomposable and satisfies
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
, where we regard
\sigma
as the unique ring homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
lifting
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
. Then there exists a chain complex
\tilde{C}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
such that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}\cong C
. Moreover,
\tilde{C}
is unique up to isomorphism.

(b)

Conversely, let
\tilde{C}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
and define
C:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}
. Then
C
satisfies
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
.

Proof.

For (a), let
d=[k^{\prime}:k]
. By
[
10
, Proposition 6.3]
, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}C_{\mathcal{O}}\cong C^{\oplus d}
. We claim
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
has exactly
d
indecomposable summands. Indeed, suppose for contradiction that
C_{\mathcal{O}}
contains less than
[k^{\prime}:k]
indecomposable summands. Since extension of scalars is an exact functor, there exists a summand
D
of
C_{\mathcal{O}}
for which
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}D\cong C^{\oplus i}
for some
1<i\leq d
by the Krull-Schmidt theorem. However, this contradicts Proposition
5
which asserts that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}D
decomposes into a direct sum of nonisomorphic indecomposable subcomplexes. On the other hand,
C_{\mathcal{O}}
cannot contain more than
d
indecomposable summands since extension of scalars is exact. Thus,
C_{\mathcal{O}}
contains exactly
d
indecomposable summands,
C_{1},\dots,C_{d}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
. We have

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}C_{i})\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(C_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{d})=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}C_{\mathcal{O}}=\tau(C)\cong C^{\oplus d},

and by the Krull-Schmidt theorem, choosing
\tilde{C}:=C_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
demonstrates the first statement in (a).

For uniqueness in (a), suppose that
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
satisfy
C\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a free
\mathcal{O}
-module of rank
d
, we may take an
\mathcal{O}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
,
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
. Then,
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}\cong\tilde{C}_{1}^{\oplus d}
, since
a_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{1}
is an
A
-direct summand of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{1}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Similarly,
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}_{2})_{\mathcal{O}}\cong\tilde{C}_{2}^{\oplus d}
, so we have
\tilde{C}_{1}^{\oplus d}\cong\tilde{C}_{2}^{\oplus d}
and by the Krull-Schmidt theorem,
\tilde{C}_{1}\cong\tilde{C}_{2}
, as desired.

For (b), given any
A
-module
M
and
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
, we have an isomorphism of
A^{\prime}
-modules
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}M\cong{}^{\sigma}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}M)
, natural in
M
, given by
a\otimes m\mapsto\sigma^{-1}(a)\otimes m
. The result follows by applying this isomorphism in each component of
C
.

As a result, Galois stability allows us to determine when a splendid Rickard equivalence descends to the field which realizes two splendidly Rickard equivalent blocks.

Lemma 7
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a finite field extension, let
G
be a finite group, and let
b
be a block idempotent of
kG
. Then
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
acts transitively on the set of block idempotents
b^{\prime}
of
k^{\prime}G
for which
b^{\prime}b\neq 0
.

Proof.

We induct on the degree
d
of
[k^{\prime}:k]
. If
d=1
, then
k^{\prime}=k
and there is nothing to prove since
b
is a block idempotent. If
d=p
, then either
b
remains a block idempotent of
k^{\prime}G
, in which case this is again vacuously true, or
b
decomposes into at most
p
blocks (if
b
were to decompose into more than
p
blocks, then since a trace sum over
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
yields a block of
k
,
b
would not be primitive). If the latter occurs, then since
b
decomposes, each new block idempotent in the decomposition must have some coefficients strictly not in
k
, and therefore any element of
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
acts nontrivially on the set of block idempotents. Since
|\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)|=p
, the action must be faithful, so
b
decomposes into exactly
p
blocks in
k^{\prime}G
, the set of which is acted transitively and faithfully upon by
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k).

Now suppose
d>p
. Let
l
denote the unique finite field extension of
k
for which
[k^{\prime}:l]=p
. Then we have a decomposition
b=b_{1}+\dots+b_{n}
in
lG
, and by the inductive hypothesis,
\operatorname{Gal}(l/k)
acts transitively on the set
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}
. In particular, since the Frobenius endomorphism
x\mapsto x^{|k|}
generates
\operatorname{Gal}(l/k)
, for all integral
1\leq i,j\leq n
there exists some integer
x\in\mathbb{Z}
for which
b_{i}^{x}=b_{j}
.

Let

b=c_{1}^{1}+\dots+c_{1}^{a_{1}}+c_{2}^{1}+\dots+c_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}+c_{n}^{1}+\dots+c_{n}^{a_{n}}

be the decomposition of
b
into blocks of
k^{\prime}G
, where
c_{i}^{i^{\prime}}b_{j}\neq 0
if and only if
i=j
. In particular, for all integers
1\leq i\leq n
, we have a decomposition
b_{i}=c_{i}^{1}+\dots+c_{i}^{a_{i}}
(note from the base case
d=p
,
a_{i}
can only be 1 or
p
). By induction from considering the extension
k^{\prime}/l
, there exists some integer
\alpha
for which
(c_{i}^{j_{1}})^{\alpha}=c_{i}^{j_{2}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j_{1},j_{1}\leq a_{i}
. Moreover, since the Frobenius endomorphism
x\mapsto x^{|k|}
generates
\operatorname{Gal}(l/k)
, every element of
\operatorname{Gal}(l/k)
extends to an element of
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
. In particular, if
b_{i}^{x}=b_{j}
for some integers
1\leq i,j\leq n
, then there exist integers
i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
for which
(c_{i}^{i^{\prime}})^{x}=c_{j}^{j^{\prime}}
, and we conclude that
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
acts transitively on the set of block idempotents
b^{\prime}
of
k^{\prime}G
for which
b^{\prime}b\neq 0
. Note this also implies
a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{n}
.
∎

Corollary 8
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a finite field extension and let
G,H
be finite groups. Let
b,c
be block idempotents of
kG
and
kH
respectively. There exists a splendid Rickard equivalence
X^{\prime}\in Ch^{b}({}_{k^{\prime}Gb}\mathbf{triv}_{k^{\prime}Hc})
which satisfies
{}^{\sigma}X^{\prime}\cong X^{\prime}
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
if and only if there exists a splendid Rickard equivalence
X\in Ch^{b}({}_{kGb}\mathbf{triv}_{kHc})
.

Proof.

Let
(K,\mathcal{O},k)\subseteq(K^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
be an extension of
p
-modular systems with
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
absolutely unramified (this exists since we may take
\mathcal{O}=W(k)
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=W(k^{\prime})
).

To prove the reverse direction, suppose
X
exists. By unique lifting of splendid equivalences (
[
14
, Theorem 5.2]
), there exists a splendid Rickard equivalence
Y
of
(\mathcal{O}Gb,\mathcal{O}Hc)
-bimodules, unique up to isomorphism, which satisfies
k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Y\cong X
.
[
10
, Proposition 4.5(a)]
then asserts that
Y^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Y
induces a Rickard equivalence between
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}Gb
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}Hc
and
[
10
, Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2]
assert that
Y^{\prime}
is splendid. Then
X^{\prime}:=k^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}Y^{\prime}
is a splendid equivalence for
k^{\prime}Gb
and
k^{\prime}Hc
and is stable under
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
-action by Theorem
6
(b).

For the forward direction, let
X^{\prime}
be as given, and by
[
3

has no contractible summands. By unique lifting of splendid equivalences, there exists a splendid Rickard equivalence

of

-bimodules, unique up to isomorphism, which satisfies

. Note

and

Theorem
6
asserts that there exists a unique chain complex
Y^{\prime\prime}
of

\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Y^{\prime\prime}\cong Y

Theorem 9

Suppose that

(a)

There exists a splendid Rickard equivalence between
\mathbb{F}_{p}\tilde{S}\tilde{e}

\mathbb{F}_{p}\tilde{T}\tilde{f}

There exists a splendid Rickard equivalence between
\mathbb{F}_{p}G\tilde{b}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}H\tilde{c}

Proof.

(a)

By
[
2

\mathbb{F}_{p}\tilde{S}\tilde{e}

(b)

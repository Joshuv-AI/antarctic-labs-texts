Approximate 3 3 -designs and partial decomposition of the Clifford group representation using transvections
Tanmay Singal, Min-Hsiu Hsieh

Abstract
We study a scheme to implement an asymptotic unitary 3 3 -design. The scheme implements a random Pauli once followed by the implementation of a random transvection Clifford by using state twirling. Thus the scheme is implemented in the form of a quantum channel. We show that when this scheme is implemented k k times, then, in the k → ∞ k\rightarrow\infty limit, the overall scheme implements a unitary 3 3 -design. This is proved by studying the eigendecomposition of the scheme: the + 1 +1 eigenspace of the scheme coincides with that of an exact unitary 3 3 -design, and the remaining eigenvalues are bounded by a constant. Using this we prove that the scheme has to be implemented approximately 𝒪 ⁡ ( m + log ⁡ 1 / ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(m\ +\ \log 1/\epsilon) times to obtain an ϵ \epsilon -approximate unitary 3 3 -design, where m m is the number of qubits, and ϵ \epsilon is the diamond-norm distance of the exact unitary 3 3 -design. Also, the scheme implements an asymptotic unitary 2 2 -design with the following convergence rate: it has to be sampled 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ 1 / ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(\log 1/\epsilon) times to be an ϵ \epsilon -approximate unitary 2 2 -design. Since transvection Cliffords are a conjugacy class of the Clifford group, the eigenspaces of the scheme’s quantum channel coincide with the irreducible invariant subspaces of the adjoint representation of the Clifford group. Some of the subrepresentations we obtain are the same as were obtained in Helsen2016 , whereas the remaining are new invariant subspaces. Thus we obtain a partial decomposition of the adjoint representation for 3 3 copies for the Clifford group. Thus, aside from providing a scheme for the implementation of unitary 3 3 -design, this work is of interest for studying representation theory of the Clifford group, and the potential applications of this topic. The paper ends with open questions regarding the scheme and representation theory of the Clifford group.

The convergence rates in Theorems

IV.1
Basics: representations of finite groups on finite dimensional complex vector spaces

Let
G
be a finite group. A representation of
G
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a map
\mathrm{\Lambda}:G\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
which satisfies the following.

\mathrm{\Lambda}(g)\ \mathrm{\Lambda}(h)\ =\ \mathrm{\Lambda}(g.h),\ \forall\ g,h\in G.

(51)

If there exists a subspace
W
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that for all
w\in W
,
\mathrm{\Lambda}(g)\ w\in W
, then
\mathrm{\Lambda}
is said to be a reducible representation and
W
is said to be an invariant subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
under the action of
\mathrm{\Lambda}
, and
\mathrm{\Lambda}
restricted to
W
, i.e.,
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{W}:=\mathrm{\Lambda}\big|_{W}
is a sub-representation of
\Lambda
. If no such
W
exists then
\mathrm{\Lambda}
is said to be irreducible. For two representations
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{1}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
and
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}
, if there exists a non-singular linear operator
X:\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}
, such that

\mathrm{\Lambda}_{2}(g)\ X\ \mbox{$|v_{1}\rangle$}\ =\ X\ \mathrm{\Lambda}_{1}(g)\ \mbox{$|v_{1}\rangle$},\ \forall\ \mbox{$|v_{1}\rangle$}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}},\ \mathrm{and}\ \forall\ g\in G,

(52)

then
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{1}
and
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{2}
are said to be equivalent representations, and
X
is called the intertwiner between them. If
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{1}
and
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{2}
are such that the only solutions for
X
are singular, then
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{1}
and
\mathrm{\Lambda}_{2}
are said to inequivalent representations. If
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
,
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are inequivalent. Given the Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and some representation
\mathrm{\Lambda}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, there exists an equivalent unitary representation of
\mathrm{\Lambda}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. We henceforth assume that all representations of all groups are unitary, i.e.,
\mathrm{\Lambda}:G\longrightarrow\mathrm{U}(d)
. For finite dimensional groups, one can decompose a vector space
\mathbb{C}^{d}
as a direct sum of orthogonal subspaces,
\mathbb{C}^{d}=\oplus_{k}\ W_{k}
, such that
W_{k}
are invariant under
\mathrm{\Lambda}
, and
\mathrm{\Lambda}
restricted to
W_{k}
, i.e.,
\mathrm{\Lambda}\big|_{W_{k}}
is irreducible.

IV.3
Adjoint representation and inner product on
\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)

We will use the well-known Frobenius inner product between two linear operators
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
: for
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
represented as
|A\rangle
and
|B\rangle
respectively, it is defined as

\mbox{$\langle A|B\rangle$}\ =\ \tr\ A^{\dagger}\ B.

(53)

It is well-known that any quantum channel
\mathrm{\Phi}
admits a Kraus representation, that is, some set of operators
F_{k}\in\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
, such that for any
X\in\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
,
\mathrm{\Phi}(X)\ =\ \sum_{k}\ F_{k}\ X\ F_{k}^{\dagger}
, where
\sum_{k}F_{k}^{\dagger}F_{k}=\mathbbmss{1}
. The inner product on
\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
gives us the adjoint of
\mathrm{\Phi}
:
\mathrm{\Phi}^{\dagger}
such that for all
A,B
,

\tr\ A^{\dagger}\ \mathrm{\Phi}\left(B\ \right)\ =\ \tr\ \left(\mathrm{\Phi}^{\dagger}(A)\right)^{\dagger}B,

(54)

hence we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

For any quantum channel
\mathrm{\Phi}
with Kraus operators
F_{k}
,
\mathrm{\Phi}
and
\mathrm{\Phi^{\dagger}}
are related in the following way.

\mathrm{\Phi}(X)=\sum_{k}F_{k}\ XF_{k}^{\dagger}\ \Longrightarrow\mathrm{\Phi}^{\dagger}(X)\ =\ \sum_{k}\ F_{k}^{\dagger}\ X\ F_{k}.

(55)

The proof is in Subsection
A.3
of the Appendix.

Corollary 2
.

A sufficient condition for a quantum channel to be self-adjoint is that the adjoint of the Kraus operators
F_{k}
have the followin property:
F_{k}^{\dagger}=\pm F_{k}
. Then the quantum channel admits a spectral decomposition into orthogonal eigenspaces.

For any
U\in\mathrm{U}(N)
, one can define the adjoint representation of
\mathrm{U}(N)
on
\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)
as

\mathcal{U}_{U}\mbox{$|X\rangle$}\ =\ U^{\otimes t}\ X{U^{\dagger}}^{\otimes t}.

(56)

Note that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left({\mathbb{C}^{N}}^{\otimes t}\right)

V
Discussion and Conclusion

We have proposed a scheme to implement an asymptotic unitary
2
-design and a unitary
3
-design. In Remark
7
, we argue that this scheme is optimal, in terms of the number of times it has to be implemented for both, approximating a unitary
2
- and a unitary
3
-design within
\epsilon
-distance in diamond norm from the corresponding exact designs. The proof relies on the Frobenius-Perron theorem for irreducible Markov chains (which the transvections channel is), and the fact that the second largest eigenvalue of the scheme’s channel is below a constant which is independent of
m
. We anticipate that this scheme would be of interest for certain architectures, like those of ion traps, which can support multiple qubit interactions. In the process we also obtain some of the eigenspaces of the channel
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}\circ\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
, and discovered that these eigenspaces are, in fact, invariant subspaces of the action of the three-copy of the adjoint representation of the Clifford group. Thus, as a corollary of this work, we obtain some of the invariant subspaces of the three-copy adjoint representation of the Clifford group. Unsurprisingly, some of the irreps which are also found in the two-copy adjoint representation of the Clifford group in
Helsen2016
occurred multiple times in different subspaces of the three-copy case. Finally we also obtained some new invariant subspaces of the Clifford group adjoint action in the three-copy case. While some are clearly reducible, we are still not sure about the others. Going forward, we identify the following questions, which we would like to seek answers to. The first three of these questions stem from our inability to give a complete decomposition into irreducible subspaces of the three-copy adjoint action of the Clifford group, when it acts on the
\mathrm{span}\left\{\mbox{$|a,b,c\rangle$}\ |\ a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m},\ \mathrm{such}\ \mathrm{that}\ :\ a+b+c=0\right\}
.

(i)

The irreducibility of invariant subspace which were obtained: How do we know if the invariant subspaces which we obtained are irreducible or not? For comparison, the proof of the irreducibility of subspaces in
Helsen2016
relied on a result in
Zhu15
(see equations (8) to (11) and also, equation (12)). Computing the frame potentials for the three copy adjoint representation, one would need to compute
\Phi_{6}
. This is quite a difficult task. Thus, one would like to know if there is a simpler alternative to proving the irreducibility or not, of the corresponding invariant subspaces.

(ii)

The invariant eigenspaces in Theorem
4
and
5
lie in the subspace
\mathrm{span}\left\{\mbox{$|a,b,a+b\rangle$}\right\}
where
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=1
. The case of
\mathrm{span}\left\{\mbox{$|a,b,a+b\rangle$}\right\}
where
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=0
with
a,b,a+b\neq 0
remains to be done.

(iii)

Since
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
annihilates any Pauli basis elements of the form
|a,b,c\rangle
where
a,b,c
are not related by
a+b+c=0
, the decomposition of a large subspace of operators remains to be explored. We suspect that the structure for the
a+b+c=0
, though, is significantly richer (i.e., there are more subspaces in the
a+b+c=0
case, for the size of its dimension).

(iv)

The irreducible subrepresentations of the Clifford group for

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We prove the result by constructing the isomorphism. This construction maps a
symplectic basis
for
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
to some choice of generators of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
. A convenient choice is as follows:
e_{j}
denotes a vector in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
whose only non-zero component is the
j
-th component. Thus the
e_{j}
’s give a standard basis for
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
, and any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
can be expanded in terms of this basis. Let
X_{j}
and
Z_{j}
be the single-qubit
X
and
Z
operator for all
j=1
to
m
, i.e.,

X_{j}\ =\ \underbrace{\mathbbmss{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbbmss{1}_{2}\cdots\otimes\mathbbmss{1}_{2}}_{j-1}\otimes X\otimes\underbrace{\mathbbmss{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbbmss{1}_{2}\cdots\otimes\mathbbmss{1}_{2}}_{m-j},

(99)

and similarly for
Z_{j}
. Note that
X_{j}
’s and
Z_{j}
’s all belong to
2m
distinct cosets of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
. We choose them to be the representatives of their respective cosets in
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
. The
X_{j}
’s themselves are a set of
m
independent generators for an abelian subgroup of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}=\ \mathrm{span}\left\{e_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}
. Then for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}
, for the expansion
a=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}a_{j}e_{j}
, we find that
a_{l}=0
, for
l=m+1,m+2,\cdots,2m
.
\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}
is
m
dimensional. We map any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}
to a Pauli generated only by the
X_{j}
’s in the following way:

X(a)\ :=\ X_{1}^{a_{1}}X_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{m}^{a_{m}},

(100)

Similarly, define
\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{e_{j}\right\}_{j=m+1}^{2m}
. So when
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}
, for the expansion
b=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}b_{j}e_{j}
,
b_{j}=0
for
j=1,2,\cdots,m
, and
\dim\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}=m
. For any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}
, define a Pauli generated only by the
Z_{j}
’s as follows.

Z(b)\ :=Z_{1}^{b_{m+1}}Z_{2}^{b_{m+2}}\cdots Z_{m}^{b_{2m}},

(101)

Next, note that
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}=\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}
, hence for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
, there exists a unique
a_{X}\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{2m}
and
a_{Z}\in\mathbb{F}_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{2m}
, such that
a=a_{X}+a_{Z}
. Then for any
a
, with the decomposition
a=a_{X}+a_{Z}
as above, define the following:

E(a)\ :=\ i^{a_{X}.a_{Z}}\ X(a_{X})\ Z(a_{Z}),

(102)

where
a_{X}.a_{Z}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\ \left(a_{X}\right)_{j}\left(a_{Z}\right)_{m+j}
, but the sum is taken in
\mathbb{Z}
and not in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
E(a)
is always self-adjoint, since
a_{X}.a_{Z}
is even if and only if
\left\langle a_{X},a_{Z}\right\rangle=0
, i.e., if and only if
X(a_{X})
and
Z(a_{Z})
commute. As linear operators in
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{C}^{N}\right)
, we find that
E(a)
and
E(b)
in
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
compose in the following way.

\displaystyle E(a)\ E(b)\

\displaystyle=\ i^{a_{X}.a_{Z}+b_{X}.b_{Z}}\ X(a_{X})Z(a_{Z})\ X(b_{X})Z(b_{Z})

\displaystyle=\ i^{a_{X}.a_{Z}+b_{X}.b_{Z}+2a_{Z}.b_{X}}\ X(a_{X}+b_{X})Z(a_{Z}+b_{Z})

\displaystyle=i^{a_{Z}.b_{X}-a_{X}.b_{Z}}\ E(a+b).

(103)

This proves that the map in equation (
102
) is an isomorphism between
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
and
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
.
∎

A.2
Proofs of Lemma
2

We revise the terminology before we do the proof. For any linear operator
A:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{l}
, for some positive integer
l
:
\mathrm{rank}A:=\dim\mathrm{range}{A}
, and
\mathrm{nullity}A:=\dim\mathrm{kernel}A
. The rank-nullity theorem tells us that
2m=\mathrm{rank}A+\mathrm{kernel}A
.

Proof.

Consider the following linear map:
a^{*}:\ \mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}\ \longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

a^{*}(x)=\left\langle a,x\right\rangle.

(104)

Since
a\neq 0
, it is seen that
a^{*}
is a rank-one linear operator (it is a one-form) on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
. The rank-nullity theorem implies that
\mathrm{nullity}\ a^{*}\ =\ 2m-1
dimensional. So
\mathrm{kernel}\ a^{*}
has
N^{2}/2
distinct
h
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
such that
\left\langle a,h\right\rangle=0
, and all remaining
h
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
are such that
\left\langle a,h\right\rangle=1
.

For the more general case: define the following linear map
\left(a_{1}^{*},a_{2}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right):\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{l}
with the action

\left(a_{1}^{*},a_{2}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)(x)\ =\ \left(a_{1}^{*}(x),a_{2}^{*}(x),\cdots,a_{l}^{*}(x)\right).

(105)

Since
\dim\mathrm{span}\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{l}\right\}=d
,
\mathrm{rank}\left(a_{1}^{*},a_{2}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)=d
and
\mathrm{nullity}\left(a_{1}^{*},a_{2}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)=2m-d
. When
b\notin\mathrm{range}{\left(a_{1}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)}
, then there do not exist solutions
h\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
such that
a_{j}^{*}(h)=b_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,l
. Else if
b\in\mathrm{range}{\left(a_{1}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)}
, then the number of solutions for each such
h
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
, which satisfies
a_{j}^{*}(h)=b_{j}
is equal to the number of elements in the kernel of
\left(a_{1}^{*},\cdots,a_{l}^{*}\right)
, which is
N^{2}/2^{d}
. Hence proved.
∎

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\ d\mu(U)\ \mathcal{U}_{U^{\dagger}}\mbox{$|X\rangle$}

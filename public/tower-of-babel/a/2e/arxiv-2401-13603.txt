Curved Fukaya algebras and the Dubrovin spectrum Thanks: Partially supported by an AMS-Simons Travel Grant.
Marco Castronovo

Abstract
. Under simplified axioms on moduli spaces of pseudo-holomorphic curves, we show that weakly unobstructed Fukaya algebras of Floer-nontrivial Lagrangians in a compact symplectic manifold must have curvature in the spectrum of an operator introduced by Dubrovin, which acts on the big quantum cohomology. We use the example of the complex Grassmannian Gr ⁡ ( 2 , 4 ) \operatorname{Gr}(2,4) to illustrate a decoupling phenomenon, where the eigenvalues of finite energy truncations become simple under explicit bulk-deformations.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Cohomological
q
-deformations

Let
X
be a compact symplectic manifold, and denote
\Lambda

the Novikov field over
\mathbb{C}
with formal parameter
q
. The cohomology
\operatorname{H}(X;\mathbb{C})

of
X
has a
q
-deformation
\operatorname{QH}(X)=\operatorname{H}(X;\mathbb{C})\otimes\Lambda
known as quantum cohomology,
whose product is defined by counts of
J
-holomorphic spheres in
X
. This is an associative

\Lambda
-algebra, with the classical counterpart being recovered for
q=0
.
See Abouzaid-McLean-Smith
[
2
]
for the most recent approach to this construction,
which also yields
q
-deformations of many generalized cohomology theories.

1.2.
Categorical
q
-deformations

Starting with Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono
[
15
]
a relative analogue of the construction
above has been studied, whose output is a family of
A_{\infty}
categories

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)
over
\Lambda
known as Fukaya categories. General Lagrangians

L\subset X
have an obstructed
A_{\infty}
algebra of Floer cochains
\operatorname{CF}(L)
. To
deal with this, one takes as objects of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)
the pairs
(L,D)
with

D\in\operatorname{MC}(L)
a solution of the projective Maurer-Cartan equation in
\operatorname{CF}(L)
with
curvature
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Morphisms are given by transverse intersection points, with
composition maps arising from counts of
J
-holomorphic polygons in
X
with Lagrangian
boundary conditions, twisted by
D
. It is expected that taking Hochschild
cohomology recovers the generalized eigenspace
\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X))\cong\operatorname{QH}_{\lambda}(X)

of eigenvalue
\lambda\in\Lambda
for a suitable operator, whose action on quantum
cohomology is multiplication by the first Chern class
c_{1}
; see Perutz-Sheridan
[
27
]
and
Ganatra
[
18
]
for results in this direction.

1.3.
The status of computations

While computations of quantum cohomology algebras are available in many examples,
this is not the case at the categorical level. One could hope to begin the computation of
a Fukaya category by finding finitely many Lagrangians that
generate the split-closed derived category of its triangulated closure. General results of
this kind were obtained for non-compact targets: see Seidel
[
28
]
for Lefschetz fibrations;
Chantraine-Dimitroglou Rizell-Ghiggini-Golovko
[
8
]
and Ganatra-Pardon-Shende

[
19
]
for Weinstein sectors. In the compact case, generators have been found
in a handful of examples using a variety of techniques: toric Fano manifolds
(Evans-Lekili
[
13
]
); Calabi-Yau and Fano hypersurfaces (Sheridan
[
30
,
31
]
);
curves (Seidel
[
29
]
and Efimov
[
12
]
); some Grassmannians
(Castronovo
[
5
,
6
]
).

1.4.
The multiplicity issue

Suppose that one has found a finite set of Lagrangians
L_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)

which are nontrivial in the derived category, i.e. with
\operatorname{HF}(L_{i})\neq 0
for all
i
; this
can often be checked by examining the disk potential of
L_{i}
, see e.g. Sheridan

[
31
, Proposition 4.2]
for monotone tori. A criterion is available for showing that these
objects generate, due to Abouzaid
[
1
]
for Weinstein domains and announced in the
compact case by Abouzaid-Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono; see
Sheridan
[
31
, Proposition 1.10]
for a proof in the monotone setting. When
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{QH}_{\lambda}(X)=1
, this
criterion implies that any of the
L_{i}
generates, so a key difficulty
in computing Fukaya categories is to address what happens if
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{QH}_{\lambda}(X)>1
. In
the latter case one could hope that if
\operatorname{HF}(L_{i},L_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
then generation
holds, but Floer cohomologies of pairs of Lagrangians are hard to compute in
practice, unless one knows in advance that the Lagrangians
L_{i}
can be disjoined by
Hamiltonian isotopies.

1.5.
Generic simplicity

The starting point for this article is the work of Dubrovin
[
10
]
.
Assume that
\operatorname{H}(X;\mathbb{C})
is concentrated in even degrees, and replace
\operatorname{QH}(X)
with the
big quantum cohomology
\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)
. In many interesting
examples this algebra is semisimple for some values of the deformation parameter
B
, with
a decomposition into fields given by the generalized eigenspaces of the
B
-dependent
operator of multiplication by

K^{B}=c_{1}-\sum_{j\neq 2}\frac{j-2}{2}B_{j}\quad.

Here
c_{1}
is corrected by a term depending on
B
and the degrees of its homogeneous
components
B=\sum_{j}B_{j}
. When
X
is a Fano variety, Dubrovin conjectured
[
11
, Conjecture
4.2.2]
that

K^{B}
has simple spectrum (i.e. all eigenvalues have multiplicity one) for generic
B
if and only if the derived category of coherent
sheaves on
X
has a full exceptional collection; see Halpern-Leistner
[
20
]
for some
recent progress.

1.6.
Curvatures are eigenvalues

The big quantum cohomology
\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)
is a generalization of
\operatorname{QH}(X)
, where one includes
in the
q
-deformed product contributions coming from
J
-holomorphic spheres with extra
marked points constrained to map to a so-called
bulk cycle in
X
of homology class
B
(Figure
2
(A)). This construction has a
relative analogue, yielding a family of
A_{\infty}
categories
\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\lambda}(X)
whose
objects are pairs
(L,D)
with
D\in\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)
a solution of the projective Maurer-Cartan
equation in the
A_{\infty}
algebra of bulk-deformed Floer cochains
\operatorname{CF}^{B}(L)
with
curvature
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Composition maps in
\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\lambda}(X)
arise from counts of

J
-holomorphic polygons in
X
with Lagrangian boundary conditions, with extra interior
and boundary marked points mapping to the bulk cycle and the Maurer-Cartan chain respectively
(Figure
2
(B)). Even after
fixing
L
, the property
\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)\neq\emptyset

may depend on
B
, and one should think of the curvature
\lambda\in\Lambda
as a function
depending on the bulk-deformation
B
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
6.9
)
Assume that
D\in\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)
is a solution of the projective Maurer-Cartan equation in the

A_{\infty}
algebra of bulk-deformed Floer cochains
\operatorname{CF}^{B}(L)
, and call
\lambda\in\Lambda

the curvature of the corresponding
A_{\infty}
algebra
\operatorname{CF}^{B}(L,D)
twisted by
D
. Then

\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)\neq 0
implies that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of Dubrovin’s operator
K^{B}
.

This theorem generalizes a result of Auroux
[
3
, Proposition 6.8]
, which dealt with
the case where
B=0
,
K^{0}=c_{1}
and
D
is a local system, corresponding to the
Maurer-Cartan solution
D
having codegree one; see Auroux

[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
for a comparison between local system and Maurer-Cartan twists.
Our proof follows Auroux’s approach closely. First, we choose as model for Floer
cochains
\operatorname{CF}(L)=C(L)\otimes\Lambda
the normalized smooth singular chains, and spell out a set of simplified axioms on moduli of pseudo-holomorphic
curves that allows us to use Fukaya’s general picture
[
14
]
of correspondences and
pull-push maps without appealing to virtual techniques; see Section
2

for more on this approach. In Section
4
, we construct a chain level
operation
\cap_{B,D}
called BMC-deformed
q
-cap, which generalizes Auroux’s
q
-deformation of
the classical cap product
[
3
, Definition 6.2]
by allowing a bulk-deformation
parameter
B
coupled with a Maurer-Cartan deformation parameter
D
. When
D\in\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)
,
we prove that this operation descends to a map

\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\otimes\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)\to\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)
(Proposition
4.5
). In
Section
5
we verify that
\cap_{B,D}
endows the bulk-deformed Floer
cohomology
\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)

twisted by
D
with the structure of a
\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)
-module (Proposition
5.6
).
Finally, in Section
6
we reduce the main theorem to proving the identity

K^{B}\cap_{B,D}[L]=\lambda[L]
, where
\lambda=W^{B}(D)
is the curvature
of the
A_{\infty}
algebra of bulk-deformed Floer cochains
\operatorname{CF}^{B}(L,D)
twisted by
D
.
The notation
W^{B}(D)
suggests that
\lambda
can also be thought of as the value at
D

of the
B
-deformed disk potential
W^{B}
of
L
, for which we provide an explicit formula
(Corollary
6.4
). The computation of this BMC-deformed
q
-cap product
(Proposition
6.7
) is the hardest part of the proof, and the one that differs
the most from the case with no bulk or Maurer-Cartan deformations. Indeed, a similar
identity holds at the chain level in that case, while it is only true in homology at this
level of generality. The proof consists in checking that
K^{B}\cap_{B,D}L-W^{B}(D)L
is exact in

\operatorname{CF}^{B}(L,D)
, which is done by combining degree arguments on the terms of the projective
Maurer-Cartan equation (Proposition
6.3
) with Axioms (5) and (6) from Section
2
.
These two axioms are identities relating chains obtained from correspondences induced by
moduli spaces of disks with geodesic constraints contributing to
-\cap_{B,D}L
to
those induced by unconstrained disks, obtained by forgetting the vacuous boundary
constraint of mapping to
L
; see the discussion in Section
2
.
One should expect, by analogy with the case of no bulk, that taking Hochschild
cohomology will recover the generalized eigenspace

\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\lambda}(X))\cong\operatorname{QH}^{B}_{\lambda}(X)
of eigenvalue
\lambda\in\Lambda
for
Dubrovin’s operator
K^{B}
.
In light of this, Theorem
6.9
gives evidence that the multiplicity
issue arising in the computation of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)
will disappear in the bulk-deformed

\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\lambda}(X)
whenever Dubrovin’s operator
K^{B}
has generically simple spectrum, thus
simplifying the problem of describing generators in such cases.

1.7.
Finite energy decoupling

For explicit calculations, one might want to go beyond Dubrovin’s observation that the
spectrum of
K^{B}
is often generically simple and ask the following.

Question 1.2
.

Given
X
, for which bulk parameters
B
is the spectrum of
K^{B}
simple?

For example, combining Dubrovin’s conjecture with
results of Kapranov
[
24
]
and Kuznetsov-Polishchuk
[
25
]
, one can expect
that
K^{B}
will have generically simple spectrum for homogeneous varieties
G/P

at least in classical Lie types; see Iritani-Koto
[
22
, Proposition 5.11]
for
unconditional results in this direction.
In Section
7
we focus on what is perhaps the easiest example
of a new phenomenon called finite energy decoupling, that we believe will be important to
study further in order to address Question
1.2
in practice. After
fixing an energy level
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, one can explicitly determine those
B
for
which
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{QH}^{B}_{\lambda}(X)=1
for all eigenvalues

\lambda\in\Lambda
of the truncation of
K^{B}
modulo
q^{\alpha}
.
Truncation modulo a power of the formal parameter
q

corresponds to considering contributions of
J
-holomorphic spheres satisfying an energy bound independent
of the homotopy class.
While finite energy decoupling is not the same as decoupling the eigenvalues
in the spectrum of
K^{B}
, the two must be related: see Proposition
7.4

for some basic observations. An appealing feature of finite energy truncations of
K^{B}

is that they can be explicitly computed by combining the WDVV equations with
the knowledge of a few Gromov-Witten numbers. This allows us to prove the following.

By analogy with the case

\mathcal{CO}^{B}_{\lambda}:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\lambda}(X))

obtained by bulk-deformation from ordinary closed-open maps, and whose length
0
part

(\mathcal{CO}^{B}_{\lambda})^{0}
is related to our BMC-deformed
q
-cap operation by

m^{2}_{B,D}((\mathcal{CO}^{B}_{\lambda})^{0}(-),-)=-\cap_{B,D}-
. In this language, our Theorem

6.9
should follow after verifying that: (1)
(\mathcal{CO}^{B}_{\lambda})^{0}:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)
is a unital algebra map; (2)
(\mathcal{CO}^{B}_{\lambda})^{0}(K^{B})=\lambda[L]
,
where
\lambda=W^{B}(D)
is as in Corollary
6.4
.
See Sheridan
[
31
, Corollary 2.10]
for an alternative proof (in the monotone setting)
of Auroux’s result
[
3
, Proposition 6.8]
using this approach.
It is our understanding that Hugtenberg
[
21
]
carries out the construction of length

0

[
32
]
. Also, it is our understanding that
Venugopalan-Woodward-Xu
[

]

closed-open maps in the pearly model for Floer cochains, under rationality assumptions
that allow the use of stabilizing divisors.

2.
Axiomatic framework

This section introduces some moduli spaces of
J
-holomorphic curves used later
in the article to define various types of operations. We assume that such moduli spaces
satisfy a set of simplified axioms, and discuss the heuristic behind their choice.
Stating and proving virtual analogues of these axioms is an important and interesting
problem, particularly open for the forgetful axioms (5)-(6). See the monograph by
Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono
[
16
]
for the most recent approach to this kind of questions,
based on the notion of Kuranishi structure.

Definition 2.1
.

, sometimes
called pull-push, via the following construction.
A smooth singular chain on

is a linear combination of smooth maps

from the
\bullet

One can define

and extend by linearity
to any smooth singular chain.

2.3.
Forgetful axioms

The correspondence axioms listed so far can be grouped in two classes: (1)-(2) involve
no geodesic constraint, while (3)-(4) do. The results of Section

rely on two additional axioms, which relate chains obtained from moduli of disks with
and without geodesic constraints as in (3) and (2) respectively.

Since disks already have boundary on

sweep out the same chain by evaluation from a different moduli space where
the constraint is removed. The result of this operation depends on what kind of cycle
the remaining interior marked point on the hyperbolic geodesic was constrained to map to.

Notation 6.1

Theorem 6.9
.

For any
D\in\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)
,
\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)\neq 0

is an eigenvalue of

K^{B}\star_{B}-:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)

Proof.

The assumption
D\in\operatorname{MC}^{B}(L)
guarantees that the curvature
W^{B}(D)\in\Lambda
is defined,
and the BMC-deformed cap product of chains descends to a map on homology

\cap_{B,D}:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\otimes\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)\to\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)
(Proposition
4.5
).
The curvature

is an eigenvalue of
K^{B}\star_{B}-:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)
if and only if

(K^{B}-W^{B}(D)[X])\star_{B}-:\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)
is not invertible. Assume by contradiction
that this is not the case. Then such operator is surjective, and there is some
[Q]\in\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)

such that
(K^{B}-W^{B}(D)[X])\star_{B}[Q]=[X]
. Observe that
(K^{B}-W^{B}(D)[X])\cap_{B,D}[L]=0

(Proposition
5.6
and
6.7
). Capping
this equation by
[Q]
and using again the module structure of
\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)
over
\operatorname{QH}^{B}(X)

(Proposition
5.6
) one gets

0=[Q]\cap_{B,D}((K^{B}-W^{B}(D)[X])\cap_{B,D}[L])=

=([Q]\star_{B}(K^{B}-W^{B}(D)[X]))\cap_{B,D}[L]=[X]\cap_{B,D}[L]=[L]\quad.

This is against the fact
\operatorname{HF}^{B}(L,D)\neq 0
by assumption, because
[L]
is the unit of
this algebra (Proposition
3.4
).
∎

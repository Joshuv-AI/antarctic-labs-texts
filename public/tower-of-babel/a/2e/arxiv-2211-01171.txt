Entropy conservative high-order fluxes in the presence of boundaries
Simon-Christian Klein, Philipp Öffner

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a novel development in the context of entropy stable finite-volume/finite-difference schemes. In the first part, we focus on the construction of high-order entropy conservative fluxes. Already in [ 27 ] , the authors have generalized the second order accurate entropy conservative numerical fluxes proposed by Tadmor to high-order ( 2 ​ p 2p ) by a simple centered linear combination. We generalize this result additionally to non-centered flux combinations which is in particular favorable if non-periodic boundary conditions are needed. In the second part, a Lax-Wendroff theorem for the combination of these fluxes and the entropy dissipation steering from [ 23 ] is proven. In numerical simulations, we verify all of our theoretical findings.

including fluid mechanics, electromagnetism and wave phenomena. However, especially nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws cannot be solved

3
Entropy conservative boundary fluxes

The aforementioned entropy conservative fluxes are second order accurate.
The authors of
[
27
]
demonstrated how to construct a
2p
order accurate entropy conservative flux by using a linear combination of
Tadmor’s two-point fluxes and to ensure the entropy conservation for their considered scheme for periodic domains.
They further constructed fully discrete schemes of second and third order by applying implicit RK methods and validate the Lax-Wendroff theorem. However, we concentrate on the semi-discrete version only and generalize the used combination to be able to handle also non-periodic problems.
Further, we also validate a Lax-Wendroff type theorem. For the fully discrete setting, one can work with special implicit methods or the relaxation approach from
[
30
]
. However, this is not a topic of the current investigation.
We start by a result from
[
11
]
which was later generalized in
[

Lemma 1

(Symmetric flux expansions
[
11
,
28
]
)
.

If the numerical flux
h
is smooth, consistent with the flux
f
, and symmetric, a power series expansion of

can be written as

h(u(x_{0}),u(x))=f(u(x_{0}))+\frac{f^{\prime}(u(x_{0}))}{2}(x-x_{0})+\sum_{k\geq 2}\alpha_{k}(x-x_{0})^{k},

(5)

where
\alpha_{k}
are scalar coefficients and the multi-index notation is used.

Remark 1

We would like to point out that this result was used in a different context.
Fisher and Carpenter generalized the needed tools for high-order entropy conservative fluxes in the context of summation-by-parts (SBP)
operators and applied a flux-differencing (telescoping) ansatz where they changed the volume fluxes
inside each element (or block) depending on the used FD (or DG) framework.
This approach has attract many attentions since then, cf.
[

We use his result to simplify the proof from
[

]

Theorem 1

(High-order entropy conservative fluxes for periodic domains
[
27
]
)
.

Let
h(u_{i},u_{j})
be a smooth, consistent, symmetric and entropy conservative numerical two-point flux. Then, the numerical flux
defined by

f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=f(u_{k-p+1},\dots,u_{k+p})=\sum_{r=1}^{p}c^{r}_{p}\sum_{q=0}^{r-1}h(u_{k-q},u_{k+r-q})

(6)

is a semi-discrete entropy conservative flux of order
2p
with entropy flux

F_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=F(u_{k-p+1},\dots,u_{k+p})=\sum_{r=1}^{p}c^{r}_{p}\sum_{q=0}^{r-1}H(u_{k-q},u_{k+r-q}),

(7)

if the coefficients satisfy

\sum_{r=1}^{p}rc_{p}^{r}=1,\quad\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}r^{2k+1}=0,\quad\forall k\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}.

Proof.

The difference of two fluxes can be written as

\frac{\mathrm{d}{u_{k}}}{\mathrm{d}{t}}=\frac{f_{k-1/2}-f_{k+1/2}}{\Delta x}=\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}\frac{h(u_{k-r},u_{k})-h(u_{k},u_{k+r})}{\Delta x},

as all other contributions cancel out.
First, we present the entropy conservation. Using elementary calculations, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{U(u_{k})}}{\mathrm{d}{t}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\frac{\mathrm{d}{U}}{\mathrm{d}{u}},\frac{\mathrm{d}{u_{k}}}{\mathrm{d}{t}}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{v_{k},\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}-f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\right\rangle=\left\langle{v_{k},\sum_{r=1}^{p}c^{r}_{p}\frac{h(u_{k-r},u_{k})-h(u_{k},u_{k+r})}{\Delta x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p}c^{r}_{p}\left\langle{v_{k},\frac{h(u_{k-r},u_{k})-h(u_{k},u_{k+r})}{\Delta x}}\right\rangle=\sum_{r=1}^{p}c^{r}_{p}\frac{H(u_{k-r},u_{k})-H(u_{k},u_{k+r})}{\Delta x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(F_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-F_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

This demonstrates the semi-discrete per cell entropy conservation of the flux.

To demonstrate the
2p
order of accuracy, Lemma
1
is applied. Note that
\alpha_{0}=f(u(x_{0}))
and
\alpha_{1}=f^{\prime}(u(x_{0}))
hold, and therefore we can expand the fluxes on the grid into

h(u_{k},u_{k+r})=f(u(x_{k}))+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(u(x_{k}))\Delta xr+\sum_{k\geq 2}\alpha_{k}r^{k}(\Delta x)^{k}.

By applying this series expansion, one finds

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{u_{k}}}{\mathrm{d}{t}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}\frac{\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{l}(r^{l}-(-r)^{l})(\Delta x)^{l}}{\Delta x}=-\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}\frac{(f^{\prime}(u_{k})+f^{\prime}(u_{k}))r\Delta x}{2\Delta x}

\displaystyle-\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}\frac{2\sum_{l\geq 1}r^{2l+1}(\Delta x)^{2l+1}\alpha_{2l+1}}{\Delta x}=-f^{\prime}(u_{k})\sum_{r=1}^{p}rc_{p}^{r}-2\sum_{l\geq 1}(\Delta x)^{2l}\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}r^{2l+1}\alpha_{2l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{\prime}(u_{k})+\bigO\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Delta x\right\rvert}^{2p}\right),

where we use that even components of the sum cancel out due to the symmetry of the numerical fluxes. Uneven components of the sum vanish because the condition
\sum_{r=1}^{p}c_{p}^{r}r^{2l+1}=0
for
l\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
holds.

∎

.
Due to this fact, they can only be used if enough cells around cell

One example of methods using those linear combination are the TeCNO schemes developed in

Definition 1

(Linear combined fluxes)
.

Let
h(u_{L},u_{R})
be a two-point flux and

A\in\mathbb{R}^{{{\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}}\times{\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}}}}

\forall l\in Z=\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A_{ll}=0.

We define a new numerical flux through

(8)

z=p
corresponds to a symmetric stencil. The set
Z
contains the indices of the matrix
A

Obviously, the fluxes (

and used in Theorem

The requirement of a diagonal zero is not obviously needed. But we want to avoid that the two-point flux, evaluated with equal arguments, is used to approximate the analytical flux. Lemma 2
.

The high-order flux (
6

f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}A_{lm}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})

using the matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{{{\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}}\times{\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}}}}

A_{l,m}=\frac{c_{p}^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert l-m\right\rvert}}}{2}

and
z=p

Writing down the same flux for the variable

amounts to shifting the matrix
A
. If one denotes with
Z={\{-z+1,\dots,2p-z\}}
the index set for the matrix
A
and with
\Tr

\Tr\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{R}^{Z\times Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{(Z-1)\times(Z-1)},\quad A\mapsto\tilde{A},\quad\tilde{A}_{lm}=A_{l+1,m+1}

and
\tilde{z}=z+1

\displaystyle f_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}A_{lm}h(u_{i+l},u_{i+m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}A_{l,m}h(u_{k+l-1},u_{k+m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m=-\tilde{z}+1}^{2p-\tilde{z}}A_{l+1,m+1}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m=-\tilde{z}+1}^{2p-\tilde{z}}(\Tr A)_{l,m}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})=f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}.

Please be aware of the fact that
l\to l-1
and
m\to m-1
were shifted in the above derivation between the first and second row. This also implies that the matrix
\Tr A
has an index range lowered by one. The same applies to an operator
\Tl=\Tr^{-1}
that is used to transfer the variable to the left. We will assume in the following theorem that
A_{kl}=A_{lk}
holds, i.e. the coefficient matrices are symmetric. This is not a strong restriction as our used entropy conservative two-point fluxes are anyway symmetric. We will now regularly write sums as

f_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}(\Er A)_{lm}h(u_{i+l},u_{i+m}),\quad f_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z+1}(\El A)_{lm}h(u_{i+l},u_{i+m}).

Note that the used summations start one index lower than before or ends one index higher than before.
This will be needed to manipulate the difference of two fluxes one cell apart. We therefore use the embedding operator

\Er\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{R}^{Z\times Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{Z}\times\tilde{Z}},A\mapsto\Er A,(\Er A)_{lm}=\begin{cases}A_{lm}&(l,m)\in Z^{2}\\
0&(l,m)\not\in Z^{2}\end{cases}

with
\tilde{Z}=Z\cup\{\max Z+1\}
and the counterpart
\El
.
We will now, armed with the previous notation and lemmas, begin to derive boundary aware fluxes. Our first lemma gives sufficient conditions for two fluxes to provide a high-order scheme, when used together in a discretization.

Lemma 3

(Non-centered high-order fluxes)
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{Z\times Z}
. The fluxes defined by

f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m}),

(10)

f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}B_{lm}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})

(11)

are linear combination fluxes of finite difference order
2p-1
for
f
with the same stencil
\{k-z,\dots,k+2p-z\}=k+Z(z)
if the coefficients satisfy the relations

\Er\Tr A_{lm}-B_{lm}=0,\quad\forall(l,m)\in(Z\setminus\{0\})\times(Z\setminus\{0\}),

(12)

\displaystyle v_{m}=(\Er\Tr A_{0m}-B_{0m})+(\Er\Tr A_{m0}-B_{m0})\in\mathbb{R}^{Z},

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-z}^{2p-z}v_{m}{(m)^{j}}=2\delta_{j1},\quad j\in\{0,\dots,2p-1\},

\forall l\in Z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A_{ll}=0\wedge v_{0}=0,

(14)

\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}=1,

(15)

and the flux
h(u_{L}u_{R})
is a consistent, smooth and symmetric numerical two-point flux.

Proof.

We start with the ansatzes given in equation (
10
) and (
11
) above and look at their difference

f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}(B_{lm}-\Er\Tr A_{lm})h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})=\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}(v_{l})h(u_{k},u_{k+l}).

(16)

We will use the series expansion of
f(u_{k},u(x))
from Lemma
1
, for which we would like
f(u_{l},u_{r})
to satisfy either
u_{l}=u_{k}
or
u_{r}=u_{k}
, as

will be the expansion point. The first set of restrictions on the coefficients, as given in equation (

), enables exactly these Taylor expansions. Therefore,

(\Er\Tr A_{0l}+\Er\Tr A_{l0})-(B_{0l}+B_{l0})=v_{l}\iff\Er\Tr A_{0_{l}}-B_{0l}=\frac{v_{l}}{2}

are the only non-zero components in the differences between both matrices using the symmetry.
The expansion is in fact given as

\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}v_{l}h(u_{k},u_{k+l})=\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}v_{l}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}(u_{k})l^{j}(\Delta x)^{j}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}v_{l}\alpha_{j}l^{j}(\Delta x)^{j}

with
\alpha_{0}(u_{k})=f(u_{k})
,
\alpha_{1}(u_{k})=\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{u}}(u_{k})/2
by Theorem

This should in turn equal
\Delta x\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{x}}+\bigO((\Delta x)^{2p})

\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}v_{l}l^{j}=2\delta_{1j},\quad j=0,...,2p-1.

(17)

This ensures the order of exactness of the flux. We further note that this equation for

implies

\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}B_{lm}=\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}+\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}B_{lm}-\Er\Tr A_{lm}=1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}v_{l}=1.

(18)

Therefore, both fluxes are conservative/consistent if

is and equation (

) is satisfied. Finally, due to (

) and (

Theorem 2

(Entropy conservative linear combination fluxes)
.

The fluxes defined by

f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m}),

(19)

F_{k-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}H(u_{k+l},u_{k+m}),

(21)

F_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{l,m=-z+1}^{2p-z}B_{lm}H(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})

(22)

and the same stencil
\{k-z,\dots,k+2p-z\}=k+Z(z)
if the coefficients fulfil the relations

\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}TA_{lm}=1,

(23)

\Er\Tr A_{lm}-B_{lm}=0,\quad\forall(l,m)\in(Z\setminus\{0\})\times(Z\setminus\{0\})=N,

(24)

\forall l\in Z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A_{ll}=0\wedge v_{0}=0.

(25)

Proof.

Our proof follows as a generalization of the proof from

]
. Some more care has to be taken as the used fluxes are
not position independent

The needed compatibility relations are already a subset of the conditions from the previous Lemma

First, one can split the fluxes into components that are build out of linear combinations involving

\begin{aligned} f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}=&\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}\Er\Tr A_{lm}h(u_{k+l},u_{k+m})\\

(26)

Due to condition (
24
) in the difference between two fluxes, the contributions from the last line cancel out each other. We further realize that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=-z}^{2p-z}A_{0l}+A_{l0}

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq:Etaylorrestrict}}}{=}\sum_{l,m=-z}^{2p-z}A_{lm}-\sum_{(l,m)\in N}\Er\Tr A_{lm}\overset{\eqref{eq:Efconres}}{=}1-\sum_{(l,m)\in N}\Er\Tr A_{lm}

holds due to (

). If we analyze the difference between both fluxes in the semi-discrete scheme formulation, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{u_{k}(t)}}{\mathrm{d}{t}}=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k-\frac{1}{2}}-f_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\sum_{l=-z}^{2p}A_{0l}h(u_{k},u_{k+l})+A_{l0}h(u_{k+l},u_{k})-B_{0l}h(u_{k},u_{k+l})-B_{l0}h(u_{k+l},u_{k})\right)

3.1
Inflow, outflow and reflective boundary conditions using boundary aware fluxes

We will now outline how these boundary aware fluxes can be used to design boundary conditions for boundary types that are relevant for simulations of compressible inviscid flow, i.e. inflow, outflow and reflective boundary conditions
[
40
]
.

1.

Inflow boundaries: If an inflow boundary should be implemented, we add a ghost cell outside of the domain, as in figure
1
, and copy the boundary value into this inflow cell. The fluxes next to this interface should be the boundary fluxes that are designed in this publication.

2.

Outflow boundaries: If an outflow boundary should be implemented, we add a ghost cell outside of the domain, as in figure
1
, and add in the value of the last cell in the grid into this ghost cell. The fluxes next to this interface should be the boundary fluxes that are designed in this publication.

3.

Reflective boundaries: For a reflective boundary condition the most important property is the symmetry of the boundary condition - incoming particles are reflected of the boundary as if they came from the other side of the boundary with the same tangential speed, but opposite normal speed as the incoming particle before the reflection. Therefore the problem is equivalent to an enlargement of the domain, and the cells on the opposite of the boundary are initialized with the same pressure and density, but appropriately mirrored speeds. In this case, the fluxes for an interior cell interface of the domain are used.

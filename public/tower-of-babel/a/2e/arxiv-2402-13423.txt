Diagonal poset Ramsey numbers
Maria Axenovich, Christian Winter

Abstract
A poset ( Q , ≤ Q ) (Q,\leq_{Q}) contains an induced copy of a poset ( P , ≤ P ) (P,\leq_{P}) if there exists an injective mapping ϕ : P → Q \phi\colon P\to Q such that for any two elements X , Y ∈ P X,Y\in P , X ≤ P Y X\leq_{P}Y if and only if ϕ ( X ) ≤ Q ϕ ( Y ) \phi(X)\leq_{Q}\phi(Y) . By Q n Q_{n} we denote the Boolean lattice ( 2 [ n ] , ⊆ ) (2^{[n]},\subseteq) . The poset Ramsey number R ⁡ ( P , Q ) R(P,Q) for posets P P and Q Q is the least integer N N for which any coloring of the elements of Q N Q_{N} in blue and red contains either a blue induced copy of P P or a red induced copy of Q Q . In this paper, we show that R ⁡ ( Q m , Q n ) ≤ n ​ m − ( 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ log ⁡ m R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq nm-\big(1-o(1)\big)n\log m where n ≥ m n\geq m and m m is sufficiently large. This improves the best known upper bound on R ⁡ ( Q n , Q n ) R(Q_{n},Q_{n}) from n 2 − n + 2 n^{2}-n+2 to n 2 − ( 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ log ⁡ n n^{2}-\big(1-o(1)\big)n\log n . Furthermore, we determine R ⁡ ( P , P ) R(P,P) where P P is an n n -fork or n n -diamond up to an additive constant of 2 2 . A poset ( Q , ≤ Q ) (Q,\leq_{Q}) contains a weak copy of ( P , ≤ P ) (P,\leq_{P}) if there is an injection ψ : P → Q \psi\colon P\to Q such that ψ ( X ) ≤ Q ψ ( Y ) \psi(X)\leq_{Q}\psi(Y) for any X , Y ∈ P X,Y\in P with X ≤ P Y X\leq_{P}Y . The weak poset Ramsey number R w ​ ( P , Q ) R^{\text{w}}(P,Q) is the smallest N N for which any blue/red-coloring of Q N Q_{N} contains a blue weak copy of P P or a red weak copy of Q Q . We show that R w ​ ( Q n , Q n ) ≤ 0.96 ​ n 2 R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq 0.96n^{2} .

Introduction

A
poset
is a set
P
equipped with a partial order
\leq_{P}
, i.e., a binary, transitive, reflexive, and antisymmetric relation.
Usually, we refer to such a poset
(P,\leq_{P})
just as
P
and call the elements of
P

vertices
.
Let
P
and
Q
be two posets. A
(strong) embedding

\phi:P\to Q
of
P
into
Q
is an injective function where for any two
X,Y\in P
,
X\leq_{P}Y
if and only if
\phi(X)\leq_{Q}\phi(Y)
. We say that the image of
\phi
is an
(induced) copy
of
P
in
Q
.
Given a non-empty set
Z
, we denote by
\mathcal{Q}(Z)=(2^{Z},\subseteq)
the
Boolean lattice
on ground set
Z
with
dimension
is
|Z|
.
We use
Q_{n}
to denote a Boolean lattice with an arbitrary
n
-element ground set. For
\ell\in\{0,\dots,|Z|\}
, the
\ell
-th layer
of
\mathcal{Q}(Z)
is the set
\{X\in\mathcal{Q}(Z):~|X|=\ell\}
.

Ramsey-type problems in extremal combinatorics are widely studied for various graph structures.
Initiated by Nešetřil and Rödl
[
11
]
, such questions were also considered for posets.
A
blue/red coloring
of a poset
Q
is a mapping
c\colon Q\to\{\text{blue},\text{red}\}
. We say that a poset is
blue
if every of its vertices is colored blue, and
red
if every of its vertices is colored red.
For an integer
k
and a poset parameter
p
such as size, height, or width, Kierstead and Trotter
[
9
]
analysed the minimal
p(Q)
of a poset
Q
such that any blue/red coloring
c\colon Q\to\{\text{blue},\text{red}\}
contains a monochromatic induced copy of a poset
P
with
p(P)=k
.
When
p(Q)
is the dimension of the smallest Boolean lattice containing a copy of
Q
, this problem leads to the
poset Ramsey number
introduced by Axenovich and Walzer
[
1
]
.
For posets
P
and
Q
, let

R(P,Q)=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\colon\text{ every blue/red coloring of $Q_{N}$ contains either}\\
\text{a blue induced copy of $P$ or a red induced copy of $Q$}\}.

This extremal parameter has been actively studied in recent years with the central question being the poset Ramsey number
R(Q_{n},Q_{n})
for large
n
.
In this setting, first bounds were given by Axenovich and Walzer
[
1
]
who showed that
2n\leq R(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq n^{2}+2n
.
The upper bound was improved by Walzer
[
13
]
to
n^{2}+1
, and then by Lu and Thompson
[
10
]
to the best known value
n^{2}-n+2
.
Cox and Stolee
[
5
]
improved the lower bound to
2n+1
for
n\geq 13
, which was extended to all
n\geq 3
by Bohman and Peng
[
3
]
.
The best known lower bound for large
n
is given by Winter
[
14
]
, who showed that
R(Q_{n},Q_{n})\geq 2.24n
.

Note that any posets
P
and
Q
are contained as a copy in
Q_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
, therefore
R(P,Q)
is well-defined.
In the off-diagonal setting, Lu and Thompson
[
10
]
showed that for
n\geq m\geq 4
,

R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\Big(m-2+O\big(\tfrac{1}{m}\big)\Big)+m+3.

Further bounds are known if one or two of the parameters
m
and
n
are small, e.g.
R(Q_{2},Q_{n})=n+\Theta\big(\tfrac{n}{\log n}\big)
, where the upper bound is given by Grosz, Methuku and Tompkins
[
7
]
and the lower bound is by the authors
[
2
]
. Furthermore,
R(Q_{2},Q_{2})=4
,
R(Q_{2},Q_{3})=5
, and
R(Q_{3},Q_{3})=7
, see
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
, and Falgas-Ravry et al.
[
6
]
, respectively.

The first result of this paper is a strengthened upper bound on the poset Ramsey number of
Q_{m}
versus
Q_{n}
when both
m
and
n
large.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
2^{25}\leq m\leq n
. Then

R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\left(m-\big(1-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\log m}}\big)\log m\right).

Moreover, if
n\geq m
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\varepsilon<1
, such that
\tfrac{n+m}{n}\cdot\tfrac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\log m}+m^{-\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon
, then

R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\big(m-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\log m\big).

In this article,
\log
refers to the logarithm with base
2
.
Theorem
1
is the first result which improves the initial basic upper bound by Axenovich and Walzer
[
1
]
, see Lemma
6
, by a superlinear additive term.
Our result immediately provides an improved upper bound on
R(Q_{n},Q_{n})
.

Corollary 2
.

For
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
depending on
\varepsilon
,
R(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq n^{2}-(1-\varepsilon)n\log n
.

The diagonal poset Ramsey number
R(P,P)
is known exactly for some basic posets
P
.
Walzer
[
13
]
determined this number for antichains and chains.
Chen, Chen, Cheng, Li, and Liu
[
4
]
found
R(P,P)
when
P
is the poset which consists of two elementwise incomparable chains on a given number of vertices, with an added vertex smaller than all other vertices.

Here, we study the diagonal poset Ramsey number
R(P,P)
for further posets
P
.
The
n
-fork

V_{n}
is the poset consisting of an antichain on
n
vertices with an added vertex smaller than all other vertices.
Similarly, the
n
-diamond

D_{n}
is the poset consisting of an antichain on
n
vertices and a vertex smaller than all others as well as a vertex larger than all others.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\alpha(n)
the minimal dimension
N
such that
Q_{N}
contains an antichain of size
n
. We call
\alpha(n)
the
Sperner number
of
n
.
Sperner
[
12
]
showed that
\alpha(n)
is the minimal integer such that
\binom{N}{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\geq n
.
It is a basic observation that
\alpha(n)\leq\alpha(2n-1)\leq\alpha(n)+2
, which we will use repeatedly.
Note that
\alpha(n)=\big(1+o(1)\big)\log n
, see also an almost exact bound on
\alpha(n)
by Habib, Nourine, Raynaud and Thierry
[
8
]
.

Theorem 3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
2\alpha(n)\leq R(D_{n},D_{n})\leq\alpha(n)+\alpha(2n-1)
.
In particular,
2\alpha(n)\leq R(D_{n},D_{n})\leq 2\alpha(n)+2
and thus
R(D_{n},D_{n})=\big(2+o(1)\big)\log n
.

Note that for some values of
n
,
\alpha(2n-1)\leq\alpha(n)+1
, in which case the above upper and lower bounds on
R(Q_{n},Q_{n})
differ by
1
.

For the next result, we need two further extremal parameters. Given
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\geq\alpha(n)
, let
\beta(N,n)
and
N^{*}(n)
be integers with

\beta(N,n)=\min\big\{\beta:~\binom{N}{\beta}\geq n\big\}\ \text{ and }\ N^{*}(n)=\max\big\{N\geq\alpha(n):~N-\beta(N,n)<\alpha(n)\big\},

as illustrated in Figure
3
.
Note that
\binom{N}{\lfloor\alpha(n)/2\rfloor}\geq\binom{\alpha(n)}{\lfloor\alpha(n)/2\rfloor}\geq n>\binom{N}{0}
, so
1\leq\beta(N,n)\leq\alpha(n)/2
.
Thus,
\alpha(n)\leq N^{*}(n)<\beta(N^{*}(n),n)+\alpha(n)\leq 2\alpha(n)
, so in particular
\beta(N,n)
and
N^{*}(n)
are well-defined.

Theorem 4
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
N^{*}(n)+1\leq R(V_{n},V_{n})\leq N^{*}(n)+3
.

Let
d=\frac{1}{1-c}
where
c
is the unique real solution of
\log\big(c^{-c}(1-c)^{c-1}\big)=1-c
, i.e.,
d\approx 1.29
.
Then
R(V_{n},V_{n})=(d+o(1))\log(n).

Similarly to Theorem
3
, by using more careful estimates our proof provides
R(V_{n},V_{n})\leq N^{*}(n)+2
whenever
\alpha(2n-1)\leq\alpha(n)+1
.

The poset Ramsey number
R(P,Q)
concerns induced copies of posets
P
and
Q
. A variation of this Ramsey-type problem deals with so called
weak copies
.
Let
P
and
Q
be two posets. A
weak embedding

\psi\colon P\to Q
is an injective function such that for any two elements
X,Y\in P
,

X\leq_{P}Y
implies
\psi(X)\leq_{Q}\psi(Y)
. In this setting, we allow that
\psi(X)\leq_{Q}\psi(Y)
even if
X\not\leq_{P}Y
.
We say that the image of
\psi
is a
weak copy
of
P
in
Q
.
Analogously to the poset Ramsey number
R(P,Q)
, we define the
weak poset Ramsey number
for posets
P
and
Q
as

R^{\text{w}}(P,Q)=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\colon\text{ every blue/red coloring of $Q_{N}$ contains either}\\
\text{a blue weak copy of $P$ or a red weak copy of $Q$}\}.

It is a basic observation that
R^{\text{w}}(P,Q)\leq R(P,Q)
for any posets
P
and
Q
.
The best known bounds in the diagonal setting
Q_{n}
versus
Q_{n}
are a lower bound by Cox and Stolee
[
5
]
and an upper bound by Lu and Thompson
[
10
]
,

2n+1\leq R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq R(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq n^{2}-n+2.

Grósz, Methuku and Tompkins
[
7
]
showed in the off-diagonal setting that

R^{\text{w}}(Q_{m},Q_{n})\geq m+n+1
for
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 68
, and
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n+2^{m}-1
, where the second bound is derived from a result by Cox and Stolee
[
5
]
.

Our final result is an improvement of the upper bound on
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})
.

Theorem 5
.

For sufficiently large
n
,
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq 0.96n^{2}
.

Notation and preliminary results

2.1
Notation and definitions

In this paper we use capital letters to denote subsets of the ground set in a Boolean lattice, i.e., the vertices of Boolean lattices.
We use bold capital letters for significant ground sets.
Calligraphic capital letters are used for families of sets and thus for subposets of the Boolean lattice.
Abstract posets which are not considered as subposets of a specific Boolean lattice are denoted by capital letters, for example
Q_{n}
,
D_{n}
and
V_{n}
.

Given a set
\mathbf{Z}
with disjoint subsets
S,T\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
, we define a
blob
in a Boolean lattice
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{Z})
as

\mathcal{B}(S;T)=\{Z\subseteq\mathbf{Z}:S\subseteq Z\subseteq S\cup T\}.

We call
T
the
variable set
of this blob.
Note that
\mathcal{B}(S;T)
is a Boolean lattice of dimension
|T|
. We say that
|T|
is the
dimension
of the blob.
We remark that every
Z\in\mathcal{B}(S;T)
has the form
S\cup T_{Z}
where
T_{Z}\subseteq T
.
A
t
-
truncated blob
, denoted
\mathcal{B}(S;T;t)
, is the poset
\{Z\in\mathcal{B}(S;T):~|Z|\leq t\}
. We also say that
\mathcal{B}(S;T;t)
has
dimension

|T|
.
Given a Boolean lattice
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})
on ground set
\mathbf{X}
and a non-negative integer
t
,
t\leq|\mathbf{X}|
, let
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}
denote the
t
-truncated Boolean lattice
,
that is the subposet
\{Z\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X}):~|Z|\leq t\}=\mathcal{B}(\varnothing;\mathbf{X};t)
.
Given two non-negative integers
s
and
t
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq|\mathbf{X}|
, let
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})_{s}^{t}
denote the
(s,t)
-truncated Boolean lattice
,
that is the subposet
\{Z\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X}):~s\leq|Z|\leq t\}
. In particular,
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}=\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}_{0}
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
be disjoint, non-empty sets. Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}\to\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X}\cup\mathbf{Y})
be a (strong) embedding.
The function
\phi
is
\mathbf{X}
-good
if
\phi(X)\cap\mathbf{X}=X
for every
X\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}
. Note that a
t
-truncated blob
\mathcal{B}(S;T;t)
is the image of a
T
-good embedding of
\mathcal{Q}(T)^{t}
.
We say that
\phi
is
red
if its image is a red poset, i.e.
\phi
maps only to red vertices, and
blue
if its image is a blue poset.
If
\phi
is an embedding of a poset
\mathcal{A}
into a Boolean lattice, we use the notation
\phi(\mathcal{A})
for
\{\phi(X):~X\in\mathcal{A}\}
.
For a subposet
\mathcal{A}
of a Boolean lattice, we say that the
volume
of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted
{\rm Vol}(\mathcal{A})
, is the total number of ground elements in all vertices of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
{\rm Vol}(\mathcal{A})=|\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{A}}X|
.

2.2
Blob Lemmas

In order to show an upper bound on
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})
, we have to find a blue copy of
Q_{m}
or a red copy of
Q_{n}
in every coloring of a host Boolean lattice.
Using our notation, let us briefly reiterate the basic approach used for previously known upper bounds, see Lemma 1 in
[
9
]
, Blob Lemma in
[
1
]
, and Lemma 1 in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 6

(Blob Lemma)
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
N=nm+n+m
.
Any blue/red coloring of
\mathcal{Q}([N])
contains a blue copy of
Q_{m}
or a red copy of
Q_{n}
.

Proof.

Partition
[N]
arbitrarily into sets
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}^{(0)},\mathbf{Y}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}
such that
|\mathbf{X}|=n
and
|\mathbf{Y}^{(i)}|=m
,
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
.
We construct a red embedding
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
.
For each
X\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})
, consider the blob
\mathcal{B}_{X}=\mathcal{B}(X\cup\bigcup_{i=0}^{|X|-1}\mathbf{Y}^{(i)};\mathbf{Y}^{(|X|)})
,
where we use the convention that
\bigcup_{i=0}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}^{(i)}=\varnothing
.
If one of the blobs is monochromatic blue, it is a blue copy of
Q_{m}
as desired.

Suppose that there is a red vertex
Z_{X}\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
for every
X
.
Then the function
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
,
\phi(X)=Z_{X}
has a red image.
Observe that
\phi(X)\cap\mathbf{X}=X
for every
X\subseteq\mathbf{X}
.
Then it is straightforward to verify that
\phi
is an embedding. Therefore, there is a red copy of
Q_{n}
.
∎

The general proof idea of our bound on
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})
is to refine Lemma
6
by
considering truncated blobs instead of blobs, moreover those chosen based on already embedded layers.
In addition, we control the volume of truncated blobs while constructing the embedding.
For this we need parts (i),(ii),(iii) of the following variant of Lemma
6
.
Part (iv) will be applied to achieve an upper bound on
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})
.

Lemma 7

(Truncated Blob Lemma)
.

Let
n,m,t,N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{X}\subseteq[N]
. Fix a blue/red coloring of the Boolean lattice
\mathcal{Q}([N])
.

(i)

If
|\mathbf{X}|=n
,
t\leq n
, and there is an
\mathbf{X}
-good, red embedding
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
such that
{\rm Vol}({\phi}(\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t})\leq N-(n-t)m
,
then
\mathcal{Q}([N])
contains a blue copy of
Q_{m}
or a red copy of
Q_{n}
.

(ii)

If
|\mathbf{X}|=m
,
t\leq m
, and there is an
\mathbf{X}
-good, blue embedding
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
such that
{\rm Vol}({\phi}(\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t})\leq N-(m-t)n
,
then
\mathcal{Q}([N])
contains a blue copy of
Q_{m}
or a red copy of
Q_{n}
.

(iii)

If
|\mathbf{X}|=n
,
t\leq n
, and there is a set
S
disjoint from
\mathbf{X}
and a red truncated blob
\mathcal{B}(S;\mathbf{X};t)
such that
|S\cup\mathbf{X}|\leq N-(n-t)m
, then there is a blue copy of
Q_{m}
or a red copy of
Q_{n}
.

(iv)

If
0\leq s\leq t\leq n
, and
N=(t-s+2)n
, then
\mathcal{Q}([N])
contains a blue copy of
Q_{n}
or a red copy of
\mathcal{Q}([n])^{t}_{s}
.

Proof.

(i):
We shall extend
\phi
to a red embedding
\phi^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
.
As in the proof of Lemma
6
, we select disjoint sets of ground elements and use them to define a blob for each not yet embedded
X\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})
.
Let
\mathbf{U}=\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}}\phi(X)
and note that
|\mathbf{U}|={\rm Vol}(\phi(\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}))
.
Since
\phi
is
\mathbf{X}
-good,
\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbf{U}
. Thus,

|[N]\setminus(\mathbf{U}\cup\mathbf{X})|=|[N]\setminus\mathbf{U}|=N-{\rm Vol}(\phi(\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}))\geq(n-t)m.

Fix
n-t
pairwise disjoint
m
-element subsets
\mathbf{Y}^{(t+1)},\dots,\mathbf{Y}^{(n)}
of
[N]\setminus\mathbf{U}
.
For every
X\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})
with
|X|>t
, consider the blob

\mathcal{B}_{X}=\mathcal{B}\left(X\cup(\mathbf{U}\setminus\mathbf{X})\cup\bigcup_{i=t+1}^{|X|-1}\mathbf{Y}^{(i)};\mathbf{Y}^{(|X|)}\right).

If
\mathcal{B}_{X}
is blue, it is a blue copy of
Q_{m}
, so suppose that there is a red vertex
Z_{X}\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
.
Let
\phi^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathcal{Q}([N])
with

\phi^{\prime}(X)=\begin{cases}\phi(X)&\text{ if }|X|\leq t,\\
Z_{X}&\text{ if }|X|>t.\end{cases}

The image of
\phi^{\prime}
is red. Now we verify that
\phi^{\prime}
is an embedding.
Observe that
\phi^{\prime}(X)\cap\mathbf{X}=X
for every
X\subseteq\mathbf{X}
. Let
X_{1},X_{2}\subseteq\mathbf{X}
.
If
\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})\subseteq\phi^{\prime}(X_{2})
, then
X_{1}=\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})\cap\mathbf{X}\subseteq\phi^{\prime}(X_{2})\cap\mathbf{X}=X_{2}
.
Conversely, suppose that
X_{1}\subseteq X_{2}
.

If
|X_{1}|\leq|X_{2}|\leq t
, then
\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})=\phi(X_{1})\subseteq\phi(X_{2})=\phi^{\prime}(X_{2})
because
\phi
is an embedding.

If
|X_{1}|\leq t<|X_{2}|
, we have
\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})=X_{1}\cup(\phi(X_{1})\setminus\mathbf{X})\subseteq X_{2}\cup(\mathbf{U}\setminus\mathbf{X})\subseteq Z_{X_{2}}=\phi^{\prime}(X_{2})
.

If
t<|X_{1}|\leq|X_{2}|
, then
\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})\in\mathcal{B}_{X_{1}}
, so
\phi^{\prime}(X_{1})\subseteq X_{1}\cup(\mathbf{U}\setminus\mathbf{X})\cup\mathbf{Y}^{(t+1)}\cup\dots\cup\mathbf{Y}^{(|X_{1}|)}\subseteq Z_{X_{2}}=\phi^{\prime}(X_{2})
, as desired.

(ii):
This part is proven analogously to part (i).

(iii):
Observe that
\mathcal{B}(S;\mathbf{X};t)
is the image of an
\mathbf{X}
-good embedding
\phi\colon\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})^{t}\to\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{X})
,

First, we need the following technical proposition.

Proposition 8
.

Let

. Let
\varepsilon=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log m}}
. Then
\tfrac{n+m}{n}\cdot\tfrac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\log m}+m^{-\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

The bound

implies that
\varepsilon=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log m}}\leq\tfrac{1}{5}
,
so in particular
4\varepsilon\leq 1-\varepsilon
.
Since
m\leq n

\log m=\varepsilon^{-2}
, we obtain that

\frac{n+m}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\log m}\leq\frac{2n}{n}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

For
\varepsilon\leq\tfrac{1}{5}
, it is straightforward to check that
\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\geq 1-\log\varepsilon

Thus, using again that
\log m=\varepsilon^{-2}

m^{-\varepsilon}=2^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\leq 2^{-1+\log\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore,

\frac{n+m}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\log m}+m^{-\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

P_{0}\cup\cdots\cup P_{s-1}=\varnothing
or
P^{\prime}_{t+1}\cup\cdots\cup P^{\prime}_{n}=\varnothing
, respectively.
Observe that every member of
\mathcal{P}(n,s,t)
is indeed a weak copy of
Q_{n}
, where layer
i
of
Q_{n}
corresponds to
P_{i}
, for
i\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}
,
layer
j
of
Q_{n}
corresponds to
P^{\prime}_{j}
, for
j\in\{t+1,\ldots,n\}
and the remaining layers are contained in the
middle part

Q_{s}^{t}
.

5.2
Construction of a sausage chain in
\mathcal{Q}([N])

Let
N=0.96n^{2}
.
For our proof we need to define a constant
q
that satisfies certain properties. For
0<p<1
, the
binomial entropy function
is defined as

H(p)=-\big(p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p)\big),
where
\log
is base
2
.
Let
q
be the real number,
0<q<1/2
, which minimizes
(1-q)+2\int_{0}^{q}H(s)ds
. It can be verified analytically or numerically that such a
q
satisfies
H(q)=1/2
, i.e.,
0.11<q<0.111
, and
\int_{0}^{q}H(s)ds\leq 0.033
.
In particular, for sufficiently large
n
we have that

(1-q)n^{2}+2n^{2}\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\operatorname{d}\!s\leq 0.956n^{2}\leq N-\varepsilon n^{2},

(6)

for some constant
\varepsilon>0
.

Next we define a
sausage chain
in
\mathcal{Q}([N])
, see Figure
4
(b).
Let

Z_{0}\subset Z_{1}\subset\dots\subset Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\subset Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\subset Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor-1}\subset\dots\subset Z^{\prime}_{0}

be vertices in
\mathcal{Q}([N])
such that for
0\leq i\leq\lfloor qn\rfloor
,

|Z_{i}|=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\left(\left\lceil\log\binom{n}{j}\right\rceil+1\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad|Z^{\prime}_{i}|=N-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\left(\left\lceil\log\binom{n}{j}\right\rceil+1\right).

We will argue later that
|Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|\leq|Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|
, which implies that these vertices are well-defined.
We define subposets
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}
, which we refer to as
sausages
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}([N])\colon X\subset Z_{0}\}
, and

\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}([N])\colon Z_{i-1}\subseteq X\subset Z_{i}\}.

Similarly, let
sausages

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}([N])\colon Z^{\prime}_{0}\subset X\}
, and

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}([N])\colon Z^{\prime}_{i}\subset X\subseteq Z^{\prime}_{i-1}\}.

Moreover, let

\mathcal{B}=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}([N])\colon Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\subseteq X\subseteq Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\}.

The subposet
\mathcal{B}
is isomorphic to a Boolean lattice of dimension
|Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|-|Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|
.
Note that

\mathcal{S}_{0}<\mathcal{S}_{1}<\dots<\mathcal{S}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}<\mathcal{B}<\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}<\dots<\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}.

The subposet
\mathcal{S}_{0}\cup\dots\mathcal{S}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\cup\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}
of
\mathcal{Q}([N])
is referred to as
sausage chain
.
Note that the sausage chain is well-defined if all vertices
Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i}
,
i\in\{0,\dots,\lfloor qn\rfloor\}
, exist.
Note also that
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|<\cdots<|\mathcal{S}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|
and
|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|>\cdots>|\mathcal{S}_{0}|
.

5.3
Finding a member of
\mathcal{P}(n,s,t)
in the sausage chain

We shall find a monochromatic member of
\mathcal{P}(n,s,t)
for some
s
and
t
depending on the coloring, such that the middle part
Q_{s}^{t}
of
\mathcal{P}(n,s,t)
is embedded into
\mathcal{B}
, each
P_{i}
is embedded into its own
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
, and each
P^{\prime}_{i}
is embedded in its own
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}
for some
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}
.

Assume, without loss of generality, that among all sausages
\mathcal{S}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}
, most sausages have majority color red,
then at least
\lceil qn\rceil
sausages have this majority color.
Assume further, that there are
s
sausages among
\mathcal{S}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}
with majority color red,
which we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{i_{0}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{i_{s-1}}
,
i_{0}<\dots<i_{s-1}
. Note that possibly
s=0
.
Since
i_{0}\geq 0
, we see that
i_{1}\geq 1
, and similarly for any
j\in\{0,\dots,s-1\}
,
i_{j}\geq j
.
For
j\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}
, choose
P_{j}
arbitrarily such that
P_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{i_{j}}
,
|P_{j}|=\binom{n}{j}
, and
P_{j}
is red.
These subposets are well-defined since for any
i\in\{0,\dots,\lfloor qn\rfloor\}
,
|\mathcal{S}_{i}|=2^{|Z_{i}|-|Z_{i-1}|}-1=2^{\lceil\log\binom{n}{i}\rceil+1}-1\geq 2\binom{n}{i}-1,

so in particular there are at least
\binom{n}{i_{j}}\geq\binom{n}{j}
distinct red vertices in
\mathcal{S}_{i_{j}}
.

Similarly, we find
\lceil qn\rceil-s
sausages among
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}
with majority color red, denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}_{0}},\dots,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}_{\lceil qn\rceil-s-1}}
,
i^{\prime}_{0}<\dots<i^{\prime}_{\lceil qn\rceil-s-1}
. Here, it is possible that
\lceil qn\rceil-s=0
.
For
j\in\{0,\dots,\lceil qn\rceil-s-1\}
, choose
P^{\prime}_{n-j}
arbitrarily, such that
P^{\prime}_{n-j}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}_{j}}
,
|P^{\prime}_{n-j}|=\binom{n}{n-j}
, and
P^{\prime}_{n-j}
is red.
With a similar argument as before, we see that there are indeed at least
\binom{n}{n-j}
distinct red vertices in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}_{j}}
.

So far we selected
P_{j}
for
j\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}
and
P^{\prime}_{j}
for
j\in\{t+1,\dots,n\}
, where
t=n-\lceil qn\rceil+s
.
It remains to verify that
Q_{s}^{t}
is contained in
\mathcal{B}
. For that we shall show that the dimension of
\mathcal{B}
is large enough to apply Lemma
7
(iv).
Recall that
H(p)=-\big(p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p)\big)
.
Stirling’s formula implies that
\log\binom{N}{pN}=\big(1+o(1)\big)H(p)N
for any positive integer
N
and
0<p<1
. Thus

\displaystyle{\rm dim}(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=|Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|-|Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|

\displaystyle=N-2\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\left(\left\lceil\log\binom{n}{i}\right\rceil+1\right)

\displaystyle\geq N-4n-2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor qn\rfloor}\log\binom{n}{i}

\displaystyle\geq N-4n-\big(2+o(1)\big)n\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor qn\rfloor}H\left(\frac{i}{n}\right).

(7)

Since
H
is an increasing and continuous function on the interval
(0,1/2)
and is bounded by
1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor qn\rfloor}H\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\leq\int_{1}^{qn+1}H\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\,\operatorname{d}\!t=\int_{1/n}^{q+1/n}H(s)n\,\operatorname{d}\!s\leq n\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\,\operatorname{d}\!s+1,

Thus, using (
7
) and recalling the bound on
N
from (
6
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm dim}(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle N-4n-\big(2+o(1)\big)\left(n^{2}\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\operatorname{d}\!s+n\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle N-2n^{2}\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\operatorname{d}\!s-o(n^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[(1-q)n^{2}+2n^{2}\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\operatorname{d}\!s+\varepsilon n^{2}\right]-2n^{2}\int_{0}^{q}H(s)\operatorname{d}\!s-o(n^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-q)n^{2}+2n=(n-qn+2)n.

In particular,
|Z^{\prime}_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|-|Z_{\lfloor qn\rfloor}|\geq 0
, which implies that
Z_{i}
and
Z^{\prime}_{i}
,
i\in\{0,\dots,\lfloor qn\rfloor\}
, are well-defined.
Since
{\rm dim}(\mathcal{B})\geq(n-qn+2)n\geq(t-s+2)n
for
t=n-\lceil qn\rceil+s
,
Lemma
7
(iv) provides that
\mathcal{B}
contains either a blue (induced) copy of
Q_{n}
and we are done, or a red
Q_{s}^{t}
.
If there is a red
Q_{s}^{t}
in
\mathcal{B}
, we conclude that the sausage chain contains a red member of
\mathcal{P}(n,s,t)
, and thus a red weak copy of
Q_{n}
.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we first studied induced poset Ramsey numbers and
showed that for
n\geq m
and
0<\varepsilon<1
with
\tfrac{n+m}{n}\cdot\tfrac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\log m}+m^{-\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon
,

R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\left(m-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\log m\right).

When applying this result for specific
\varepsilon
, there is a trade-off between the best Ramsey bound and the smallest value of
m
for which the bound holds.
Our main result claims that
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\left(m-\big(1-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\log m}}\big)\log m\right)
for
2^{25}\leq m\leq n
.
In addition, for
1024\leq m\leq n
or
32\leq m\leq\frac{n}{32}
, one could obtain the upper bound
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\left(m-\tfrac{1}{4}\log m\right)
, using
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem
1
is an improvement of the basic upper bound, Lemma
6
, by a superlinear additive term and a step towards the following conjecture raised by Lu and Thompson
[
10
]
.

Conjecture 9

(
[
10
]
)
.

For
n\geq m
and sufficiently large
m
,
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})=o(n^{2})
.

We propose a stronger conjecture:

Conjecture 10
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a large enough
m_{0}
such that for any two
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq m\geq m_{0}
,

R(Q_{m},Q_{n})\leq n\cdot m^{\varepsilon}.

We remark that for small
m
, the authors conjectured the following in
[
2
]
.

Conjecture 11

(
[
2
]
)
.

For fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
and large
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
R(Q_{m},Q_{n})=n+o(n)
.

In the last part of this paper we discussed weak poset Ramsey numbers and improved the previously known upper bound
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq R(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq n^{2}-o(n^{2})
to
R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq 0.96n^{2}
.
It is still open whether or not the weak poset Ramsey number is significantly smaller than the (induced) poset Ramsey number.
The second author suggests the following conjecture.

Conjecture 12
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

R(Q_{n},Q_{n})-\Theta(n)\leq R^{\text{w}}(Q_{n},Q_{n})\leq R(Q_{n},Q_{n})
.

We shall find

such that
N^{*}=(d+o(1))\log n
, where

N^{*}=\max\big\{N\geq\alpha(n):~N-\beta(N,n)<\alpha(n)\big\}\ \text{ and }\ \beta(N,n)=\min\big\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}:~\binom{N}{\beta}\geq n\big\}.

For arbitrary
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
q
with
0<q<1
, Stirling’s formula provides that

\log\binom{N}{qN}=-\big(1+o(1)\big)N\big(q\log q+(1-q)\log(1-q)\big)=\big(1+o(1)\big)H(q)N,

(8)

where
H(q)=-\big(q\log q+(1-q)\log(1-q)\big)
is the
binary entropy function
.
Let
c
be the unique solution of
1-c=H(c)
, i.e.
c\approx 0.2271
.
We shall show that
N^{*}=\big(\tfrac{1}{1-c}+o(1)\big)\log n
.
Let
q
such that
qN^{*}=\beta(N^{*},n)
, and let
q^{\prime}
such that
q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)=\beta(N^{*}+1,n)
.
The definition of
\beta
implies that

\binom{N^{*}}{qN^{*}-1}<n\leq\binom{N^{*}}{qN^{*}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)-1}<n\leq\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)},

(9)

By the definition of
N^{*}(n)
, we know that

(1-q)N^{*}<\alpha(n)\leq(1-q^{\prime})(N^{*}+1).

(10)

In the following,
o(1)
always refers to the asymptotic behaviour for large
n
, so equivalently for large
N^{*}
as
\alpha(n)\leq N^{*}\leq 2\alpha(n)
.
Recall that

\alpha(n)=\big(1+o(1)\big)\log n.

(11)

We shall compute
q
. We label each step of our computation by an inequality from (
8
) to (
11
) which is being used.
For example, ‘
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Hq})}}{{=}}
’ means that the equality holds because of (
8
).
To highlight the idea of the upcoming computation, we give a one-line proof, where some steps are not yet justified:

(1-q)N^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Nstar})}}{{\approx}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\alpha(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:alpha})}}{{=}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:beta})}}{{\approx}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log\binom{N^{*}}{qN^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Hq})}}{{=}}\big(1+o(1)\big)H(q)N^{*},

which would imply
q=\big(1+o(1)\big)c
where

is the unique solution of

. However, some steps in the above computation require significant additional argumentation, which we give in the following.

Observe that

(1-q)N^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Nstar})}}{{<}}\alpha(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:alpha})}}{{=}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:beta})}}{{\leq}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log\binom{N^{*}}{qN^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Hq})}}{{=}}\big(1+o(1)\big)H(q)N^{*}.

Thus,
1-q\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)H(q)
, which implies that
q\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)c
.
Next we bound
q^{\prime}
from below. We see that

(1-q^{\prime})(N^{*}+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Nstar})}}{{\geq}}\alpha(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:alpha})}}{{=}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:beta})}}{{>}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\log\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)-1}.

We shall show that
\log\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)-1}\geq\big(1+o(1)\big)H(q^{\prime})(N^{*}+1)
. For that, we first need a rough lower bound on

.

We know from (
10
) that
N^{*}-qN^{*}\leq\alpha(n)-1

qN^{*}=\beta(N^{*},n)\leq\alpha(n)
, so

\binom{N^{*}+1}{\tfrac{1}{16}(N^{*}+1)}\leq\binom{2\alpha(n)}{\tfrac{1}{8}\alpha(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Hq})}}{{=}}\left(\frac{2^{2}}{(\tfrac{1}{8})^{1/8}(\tfrac{15}{8})^{15/8}}\right)^{(1+o(1))\alpha(n)}\leq 1.6^{(1+o(1))\log n}<n,

thus
q^{\prime}\geq\tfrac{1}{16}
. This bound implies that

\displaystyle\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)}{(1-q^{\prime})(N^{*}+1)+1}\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q^{\prime}}{2-q^{\prime}}\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)}\geq\frac{1}{31}\binom{N^{*}+1}{q^{\prime}(N^{*}+1)}.

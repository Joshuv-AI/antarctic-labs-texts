Abstract




Abstract:
Let
G
be a graph. The Laplacian ratio of
G
is the permanent of the Laplacian matrix of
G
divided by the product of degrees of all vertices. The computational complexity of Laplacian ratio is
\#{\rm P}
-complete. Brualdi and Goldwasser studied systematicly the properties of Laplacian ratios of graphs. And they proposed an open problem: what is the minimum value of the Laplacian ratios of trees with
n
vertices having diameter at least
k
? In this paper, we give a solution to the problem.

Keywords:
Permanent; Laplacian matrix; Laplacian ratio; Tree; Diameter

1
Introduction

The
permanent
of an
n
square matrix
M=[m_{ij}]
with
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm per}M=\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i\sigma(i)},

where
\Lambda_{n}
denotes the symmetry group of order
n
. Valiant
[
1
]
has shown that computing the permanent is
\#{\rm P}
-complete even when restricted to (0,1)-matrices.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a simple graph with
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
d_{i}
denote the degree of vertex
v_{i}
in
V(G)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Let
D(G)
be the diagonal matrix whose
(i,i
)-entry is
d_{i}
, and let
A(G)
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. The matrix
L(G)=D(G)-A(G)
is the
Laplacian matrix
of
G
. Let
PD(G)
be the product of degrees all vertices in
G
. If
G
has a
u,v
-path, then the
distance
from
u
to
v
, denoted
d(u,v)
, is the least length of a
u,v
-path. The
diameter
of
G
is
\max_{u,v\in V(G)}d(u,v)
. Suppose that
G-v
and
G-uv
denote the graph obtained from
G
by deleting vertex
v\in V(G)
, or edge
uv\in E(G)
, respectively (this notation is
naturally extended if more than one vertex or edge is deleted). The path and star of order
n
are denoted by
P_{n}
and
S_{n}
, respectively. A
caterpillar
is a tree in which a single path (the spine) is incident to (or containing) every edge. A double-star
DS(p,q)
is a tree obtained by joining the center of stars
S_{p}
and
S_{q}
by one edge, where
p\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
.

The
Laplacian ratio
of
G
is defined by

\displaystyle\pi(G)=\frac{{\rm per}L(G)}{PD(G)}.

By the definition as above, we can conclude that computing the Laplacian ratio of a graph is
\#{\rm P}
-complete because of Valiant’s result
[
1
]
mentioned earlier. Laplacian ratios of graphs were first considered by Brualdi and Goldwasser
[
2
]
. They determined the bounds for
\pi(G)
when
G
is a tree or generally, a bipartite graph.
In particular, they proposed three open problems for
Laplacian ratios
of trees in 1984. That is,

Problem 1
.

(
[
2
]
)
What is the minimum value of the Laplacian ratios of trees with
n
vertices having diameter at least
k
?

Problem 2
.

(
[
2
]
)
What is the minimum value of the Laplacian ratios of trees with
n
vertices having a
k
-matching?

Problem 3
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let
\mathscr{T}_{1}(n,k)
be the family of all trees of order
n
with diameter at most
k
, and
\mathscr{T}_{2}(n,k)
be the family of all trees of order
n
with matching number at most
k
. Determine the maximum value of Laplacian ratios of graphs in
\mathscr{T}_{1}
and in
\mathscr{T}_{2}
.

Over the last forty years, only Problem
2
is solved in
[
3
]
. Problems
1
and
3
remain open. In this paper, our interest is to investigate Problem
1
. Let
n
and
k
be integers. The Broom graph
B(n,k)
is a graph obtained by attaching
n-k
pendant edges to an endvertex of
P_{k}
. We prove the following main result.

Figure 1:
The Broom graph
B(n,k)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T
be a tree with
n
vertices and diameter at least
k
, Then

\pi(T)\geq\biggl[1+\sqrt{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2(n-k+1)}\biggl]\biggl(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}\biggl)^{k-2}+\biggl[1-\sqrt{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2(n-k+1)}\biggl]\biggl(\frac{1-\sqrt{2}}{2}\biggl)^{k-2},

where equality holds if and only if tree
T
is the broom graph
B(n,k)
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first develop some properties of the permanent of Laplacian matrices of graphs, and we introduce some graph operations that can be considered as graph transformations which will have smaller Laplacian ratios than that of the original graphs. Based on results as above, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. A brief summary will be given in section 3.

Lemma

Lemma 2.1
.

Lemma 2.2

Let

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)=d(v)~{\rm per}L_{v}(G)+\sum\limits_{u\in N(v)}{\rm per}L_{vu}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(v)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G).

Proof.

Suppose that

,
v_{2}
,

Then, as

(for any

are mutually disjoint and

, we have

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{n\sigma(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{1}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{n\sigma(n)}+\sum_{j}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}^{j}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{n\sigma(n)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{C}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{C}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{n\sigma(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(v)\sum_{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{1}}a_{2\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(2)}\cdots a_{n\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(n)}+\sum_{vv_{j}\in E(G)}\sum_{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{2}^{j}}a_{2\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(2)}\cdots a_{(j-1)\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(j-1)}a_{(j+1)\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(j+1)}

\displaystyle\cdots a_{n\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(n)}+2\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(v)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}\sum_{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{C}}a_{i_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(i_{1})}a_{i_{2}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(i_{2})}\cdots a_{i_{n-l}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(i_{i_{n-l}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(v)~{\rm per}L_{v}(G)+\sum\limits_{vv_{j}\in E(G)}{\rm per}L_{vu}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(v)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G),

where in the penultimate step

,
\ldots
,

are indices of the vertices of the graph

Lemma 2.3
.

If
v
is a pendant vertex of
G
, and
u
is the vertex adjacent to
v
. Then

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)={\rm per}L(G-v)+2~{\rm per}L_{u}(G-v).

Proof.

By Lemma 2.2 with
d_{v}=1
and
\mathscr{C}_{G}=\phi
, we have
{\rm per}L(G)={\rm per}L_{v}(G)+{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)
. Using the linearity of the permanent as a function of any row or column, it can be known that

{\rm per}L_{v}(G)={\rm per}L(G-v)+{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)
.
By the definition of
{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)
, we have

{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)={\rm per}L_{u}(G-v)
.
According to arguments as above, we obtain that

{\rm per}L(G)={\rm per}L(G-v)+2~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)={\rm per}L(G-v)+2~{\rm per}L_{u}(G-v).

∎

\displaystyle+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(v)\atop V(C)\cap V(H)=\emptyset}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)\cup V(H)}(G).

The proof is similar to that of Lemma 2.2, Without loss of generality, we denote

. Then, as

(for any

\displaystyle{\rm per}~L_{V(H)}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{1}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{h\sigma(h)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{1}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{h\sigma(h)}+\sum_{j}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}^{j}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{h\sigma(h)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{C}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{C}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{h\sigma(h)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(v)\sum_{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{1}}a_{2\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(2)}\cdots a_{h\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(h)}+\sum_{vv_{j}\in E(G)}\sum_{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\atop\gamma_{1}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in S_{2}^{j}}a_{2\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(2)}\cdots a_{(j-1)\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(j-1)}a_{(j+1)\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(j+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(v)~{\rm per}L_{V(H)\cup\{v\}}(G)+\sum\limits_{vv_{j}\in E(G)\atop u\notin V(H)}{\rm per}L_{V(H)\cup\{u,v\}}(G)

\displaystyle+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(v)\atop V(C)\cap V(H)=\emptyset}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)\cup V(H)}(G),

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
e=uv
be an edge of
G
, and let
\mathscr{C}_{G}(e)
be a set of cycles containing the edge

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{v}(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)

\displaystyle+2~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(e)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G).

Proof.

By Lemma 2.2, we have

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)=d(u)~{\rm per}L_{u}(G)+\sum\limits_{uw\in E(G)}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(u)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G)

and

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G-e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(u)-1)~{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)+\sum\limits_{uw\in E(G-e)}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G-e)

\displaystyle+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G-e).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)-{\rm per}L(G-e)

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(u)~{\rm per}L_{u}(G)-(d(u)-1)~{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)+\biggl[\sum\limits_{uw\in E(G)}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{uw\in E(G-e)}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G-e)\biggl]+2~\biggl[\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(u)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G-e)\biggl].

Since

\displaystyle{\rm per}L_{u}(G)={\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{uv}(G-e)={\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{uv}(G).

(2)

Similarly, we have both

\displaystyle\sum_{uw\in E(G)\atop w\neq v}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G)=\sum_{uw\in E(G-e)}{\rm per}L_{uw}(G-e)+\sum_{uw\in E(G)\atop w\neq v}{\rm per}L_{\{u,w,v\}}(G)

(3)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)\atop v\notin V(C)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)\atop v\notin V(C)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G-e)

(4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)\atop v\notin V(C)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)\cup\{v\}}(G-e).

Substituting (
2
), (
3
), (
4
) into (
1
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)-{\rm per}L(G-e)

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(u)+1)~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)+{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)

\displaystyle+\sum_{uw\in E(G)\atop w\neq v}{\rm per}L_{\{u,w,v\}}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(e)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)\atop v\notin V(C)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)\cup\{v\}}(G-e).

By Lemma 2.4, we have

\displaystyle{\rm per}L_{v}(G-e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(u)-1)~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G-e)+\sum_{uw\in E(G-e)\atop w\neq v}{\rm per}L_{\{u,w,v\}}(G-e)

(6)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G-e}(u)\atop v\notin V(C)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)\cup\{v\}}(G-e).

Substituting (
6
) into (

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)-{\rm per}L(G-e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm per}L_{v}(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)

\displaystyle+2~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(e)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G).

It follow that

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm per}L(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{v}(G-e)+{\rm per}L_{u}(G-e)

\displaystyle+2~{\rm per}L_{uv}(G)+2\sum\limits_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{G}(e)}(-1)^{|V(C)|}{\rm per}L_{V(C)}(G).

3
Summarize

In this paper, we determined the lower bound of Laplacian ratios of trees, and the corresponding extremal graph is also determined. In general it is difficult to evaluate Laplacian ratios of graphs, unless the graphs are of very special type. It is precisely because of the difficulty of the calculation of Laplacian ratio that there are a few published papers on Laplacian ratio. Except for the problems raised by Brualdi and Goldwasser, there exist some problems to worth study. Such as: characterizing the bound of Laplacian ratios of general graphs, and determining the bound of Laplacian ratios of general graphs with given graph parameters, etc.. These problems are the direction of our future research.

Topological Iwasawa invariants and Arithmetic statistics
Cédric Dion, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Given a prime number p p , we study topological analogues of Iwasawa invariants associated to ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -covers of the 3 3 -sphere that are branched along a link. We prove explicit criteria to detect these Iwasawa invariants, and apply them to the study of links consisting of 2 2 component knots. Fixing the prime p p , we prove statistical results for the average behaviour of p p -primary Iwasawa invariants for 2 2 -bridge links that are in Schubert normal form. Our main result, which is entirely unconditional, shows that the density of 2 2 -bridge links for which the μ \mu -invariant vanishes, and the λ \lambda -invariant is equal to 1 1 , is ( 1 − 1 p ) (1-\frac{1}{p}) . We also conjecture that the density of 2 2 -bridge links for which the μ \mu -invariant vanishes is 1 1 , and this is significantly backed by computational evidence. Our results are proven in a topological setting, yet have arithmetic significance, as we set out new directions in arithmetic statistics and arithmetic topology.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime number and
K
be a number field. Iwasawa studied growth patterns of
p
-primary class groups in certain towers of number field extensions of
K
. More precisely, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
is a Galois extension
K_{\infty}
of
K
with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For any positive integer
n
, let
K_{n}\subset K_{\infty}
be the unique extension of
K
with
[K_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]
, and let
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})
be the
p
-Sylow subgroup of the class group of
K_{n}
. Iwasawa
[
Iwa73
]
showed that for
n\gg 0
,

(1.1)

\#\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})=p^{p^{n}\mu+n\lambda+\nu},

where
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
are Iwasawa invariants associated to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
. The characterization of Iwasawa invariants is the subject of much mystery and speculation. Letting
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K
be the
cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
, the associated
\mu
and
\lambda
Iwasawa invariants shall (in this section) be denoted
\mu_{p}(K)
and
\lambda_{p}(K)
.

Our primary motivation is to study the statistical distribution of such invariants. Given a fixed prime number
p
, one is interested in the average behavior of
\mu_{p}(K)
and
\lambda_{p}(K)
. More precisely, we fix
n>0
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be an infinite family of number fields
K
with
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=n
, ordered by absolute discriminant
|\Delta_{K}|
. For instance, when
n=2
, it is natural to consider the family of all imaginary quadratic fields, or the family of all real quadratic fields. Furthermore, it is natural in this context to impose splitting conditions on
p
. Consider the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Fix a prime number
p
and
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Given a family of number fields
\mathcal{F}
of degree
n
, let
\mathcal{F}^{\mu,\lambda}
be the subset of
\mathcal{F}
consisting of number fields
K
such that
\mu_{p}(K)=\mu
and
\lambda_{p}(K)=\lambda
. Given a real number
x>0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{<x}
be the subset of
\mathcal{F}
of all
K
such that
|\Delta_{K}|<x
and set
\mathcal{F}^{\mu,\lambda}_{<x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{F}^{\mu,\lambda}\cap\mathcal{F}_{<x}
. Then, what can be said about the proportion of number fields
K
in
\mathcal{F}
with
\mu_{p}(K)=\mu
and
\lambda_{p}(K)=\lambda
, i.e., the limit

(1.2)

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{F}^{\mu,\lambda})=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}^{\mu,\lambda}_{<x}}{\#\mathcal{F}_{<x}}?

Note that the limit (
1.2
) need not exist, however, one may ask the same questions for the upper and lower limits, defined by replacing the limit by
\limsup
and
\liminf
respectively. Even for the simplest families of number fields, i.e. imaginary/real quadratic fields, such questions have proved difficult. On the other hand, the systematic study of arithmetic statistics for the Iwasawa invariants of elliptic curves was initiated by the second named author, and has gained considerable momentum in the following works:
[
KR21a
,
KR21b
,
HKR21
,
KLR22
,
BKR21
,
Ray21
,
RS21
]
.

The main goal of the present work is to study a topological analogue of question
1.1
, which has proven to be a more tractable problem. There is a deep analogy called
arithmetic topology
between number fields and
3
-manifolds. This analogy asserts a mysterious parallel between primes in number fields and knots embedded in a
3
-manifold. As a result, there are many arithmetic phenomena for number fields that are reflected on the topological side and vice versa. This philosophy originates from observations made by Mazur in
[
Maz63
]
, which he attributes to conversations with Mumford. For a modern account of various aspects of this analogy, we refer to
[
Mor11
]
. Guided by this analogy, Hillman, Matei and Morishita
[
HMM06
]
have defined analogues of Iwasawa invariants associated to a link
\mathcal{L}
embedded in a
3
-sphere. Such Iwasawa invariants are used to prove an asymptotic formula for the growth patterns of
p
-homology in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-cover of
S^{3}
that is branched along
\mathcal{L}
. We refer to
[
HMM06
, Theorem 5.1.7]
for the precise statement. This formula has a strong resemblance to the classical formula (
1.1
) due to Iwasawa. It is not difficult to prove that for any knot, the associated
\mu
and
\lambda
invariants vanish (see Lemma
3.4
). Therefore, the study of Iwasawa invariants is of genuine interest only for links with at least
2
knot components.

In this paper, we consider the family of links in
S^{3}
with
2
knot components. For such links, the
\lambda
-invariant is
\geq 1
. Such links are presented as
2-bridge links
, and there is an explicit parametrization called the
Schubert form
, which associates a rational number
a/b
with
(a,b)=1
,
b
even and
0<a<2b
to an explicit presentation which is denoted
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
. With respect to counting function
\operatorname{ht}(\mathcal{L}_{a/b})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\{a^{2},b\}
, our main result shows that for a density of
(1-1/p)
links, the
\mu
-invariant vanishes and the
\lambda
-invariant is equal to
1
. In greater detail, an integral vector
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
is called admissible if
z_{1}z_{2}\neq 0
and
\operatorname{gcd}(z_{1},z_{2})=1
. The choice of
z
fixes a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-cover of the link complement
S^{3}\backslash\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
. Associated with this cover are
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants,
\mu_{p,a/b,z}
and
\lambda_{p,a/b,z}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of all fractions
a/b
with
(a,b)=1
,
b
even,
0<a<2b
. For
x>0
, set
\mathcal{N}_{<x}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{N}
consisting of fractions
a/b
such that
\operatorname{ht}(\mathcal{L}_{a/b})<x
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{N}_{<x}
is finite, we set
\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}
to denote its cardinality. Given a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{N}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{<x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{N}_{<x}
. We say that
\mathcal{S}
has density
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S})
if the following limit exists

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}}{\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}}.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}^{p,z}
the set of all fractions
a/b
in
\mathcal{N}
such that
\mu_{p,\mathcal{L}_{a/b},z}=0
and
\lambda_{p,\mathcal{L}_{a/b},z}=1
. Our main result is as follows.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

With respect to notation above,
\mathcal{S}^{p,z}
has density given by
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S}^{p,z})=1-1/p
.

We stress that the above result entirely unconditional. Furthermore, in section
6
, we provide computational evidence for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant for
100\%
of links parametrized in this way, for
z=(1,1)
.

Organization:
In section
2
, we outline the parallels between topology and arithmetic and make preliminary observations. Section
3
is devoted to the study of the
Alexander polynomial
as well as certain
p
-adic analogs. In section
4
, we prove explicit criteria that relate
linking numbers
to Iwasawa invariants. We state and prove our main results in section
5
for the family of
2
-bridge links. Section
6
provides examples of links with positive
\mu
-invariant (for the total linking number covering space), and computational data showing that one may expect that
\mu=0
for
100\%
(i.e., a density
1
set) of
2
-bridge links.

2.
Parallels between topology and arithmetic

In this section, we introduce some preliminary notions and delineate the parallels between topology and arithmetic. This shall motivate our investigations in
topological Iwasawa theory
, i.e., the Iwasawa theory of the homology of various covers of
S^{3}
(the
3
-dimensional sphere), that are branched along a fixed link.

Before discussing the topological analog, we recall the classical Iwasawa theory of a number field. For a comprehensive account, the reader is referred to the original work of Iwasawa
[
Iwa73
]
. Fix a prime number
p
and a number field
K
. Let
\bar{K}
be a choice of algebraic closure of
K
and
\operatorname{G}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
the
absolute Galois group
of
K
. Given a finite set of prime numbers
S
, let
K_{S}
be the maximal extension of
K
contained in
\bar{K}
in which all primes
v\notin S
are unramified. Set
\operatorname{G}_{K,S}
to denote the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{S}/K)
. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is a Galois extension
K_{\infty}/K
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as a topological group. Note that all primes
v\nmid p
of
K
are unramified in
K_{\infty}
. The cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K
is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension contained in
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
, let
K_{n}
be the unique extension of
K
which is contained in
K_{\infty}
, such that
[K_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=p^{n}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})
the
p
-Sylow subgroup of the class group of
K_{n}
. Iwasawa proved the following asymptotic formula for the growth of the
p
-class groups

\#\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})=p^{p^{n}\mu+n\lambda+\nu}

for
n\gg 0
, where
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
are Iwasawa invariants associated to
K_{\infty}/K
. Thus in particular,
\mu=\lambda=0
if and only if
\#\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})
is bounded in
n
. For the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K
, it is conjectured by Iwasawa that the
\mu
-invariant of
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K
must vanish. This was proven by Ferrero and Washington
[
FW79
]
in the specific case when
K
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
, set
X\left(K_{\infty}\right)
to be the inverse limit of the
p
-primary class groups
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K_{n})
with respect to norm maps. Note that
X\left(K_{\infty}\right)
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module as well as a module over
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
. The Iwasawa algebra associated to
\Gamma
is defined to be the inverse limit of finite group algebras
\widehat{\Lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
. Letting
\gamma
be a topological generator of
\Gamma
, there is an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket X\rrbracket\simeq\widehat{\Lambda}
which identifies the formal variable
X
with
(\gamma-1)
. We note here that in most expositions in Iwasawa theory, it is customary to let
\Lambda
denote the Iwasawa algebra, however, we follow the notation used in
[
HMM06
]
and
[
Mor11
]
in which
\Lambda
is reserved for the Laurent series ring
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
.

Given a finitely generated and torsion
\widehat{\Lambda}
-module
M
, the structure theorem
[
Law97
, Theorem 13.12]
states that there is a map of
\widehat{\Lambda}
-modules

M^{\vee}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\widehat{\Lambda}/(p^{\mu_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\widehat{\Lambda}/(f_{j}(X))\right)

with finite kernel and cokernel. Here,
\mu_{i}>0
and
f_{j}(X)
is a distinguished polynomial (i.e., a monic polynomial with non-leading coefficients divisible by
p
).
The characteristic ideal of
M
is defined to be up to a unit generated by

f_{M}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{\sum_{i}\mu_{i}}\prod_{j}f_{j}(X)=p^{\mu}g_{M}(X),

where
\mu=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}
and
g_{M}(X)
is the distinguished polynomial
\prod_{j}f_{j}(X)
. The quantity
\mu
is the
\mu
-invariant, which is denoted
\mu_{p}(M)
, and the
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{p}(M)
is the degree of
g_{M}(T)
. For ease of notation, we simply set
\mu_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mu_{p}\left(X(K_{\infty})\right)
and
\lambda_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda_{p}\left(X(K_{\infty})\right)
. We let
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. Thus, the result of Ferrero and Washington states that if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian and
p
is any prime, then,
\mu_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)=0
. We shall simply refer to the characteristic polynomial of
X(K_{\infty})
as the
p
-primary Iwasawa polynomial of
K_{\infty}/K
.

Our main focus will be to study the behavior of various topological analogues of such Iwasawa invariants that arise naturally in the context of knot theory. First, we explain the parallels between the Galois theory of number fields with restricted ramification, and the topological theory of coverings of
S^{3}
that are branched along a link. Let
K
be a number field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers.

Class field theory is based on certain duality properties of the
1
-dimensional scheme
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\mathbb{G}_{m,X}
be the étale sheaf on
X
, which associates to an étale cover
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Spec}B\rightarrow X
, the group of multiplicative units
\mathbb{G}_{m,X}(\varphi)=B^{\times}
. Given a
constructible sheaf

\mathfrak{M}
on
X
(see
[
Mil06
, p.146]
for the definition), set
\mathfrak{M}^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathfrak{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,X})
(where
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is standard notation for the sheaf of homomorphisms, see
[
Har13
, Chapter 2]
or
loc. cit.
). We denote by
\hat{H}^{i}(X,\mathfrak{M})
the modified étale cohomology groups of
\mathfrak{M}
. The Artin-Verdier duality theorem (see
[
Mil06
, Theorem 1.8]
) states that there is a canonical isomorphism
\hat{H}^{3}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,X})\simeq\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
and a nondegenerate pairing of abelian groups

\hat{H}^{i}(X,\mathfrak{M}^{\vee})\times\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{3-i}(\mathfrak{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,X})\rightarrow\hat{H}^{3}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

The above pairing is analogous to the Poincare duality pairing for a compact oriented
3
-manifold
Y
and foreshadows a deep resemblance between the arithmetic properties of a number ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and the topological properties of an oriented
3
-manifold
Y
.

There is also a close analogy between embedded knots in
3
-manifolds and primes in number rings, which we briefly explain. For further details, the reader may refer to
[
Mor11
, chapter 3]
. Given a finite field
\mathbb{F}
, the étale fundamental group of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}
is given by

\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F})=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}/\mathbb{F})\simeq\hat{\mathbb{Z}}.

In fact,
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}
is the étale analogue of the Eilenberg-Maclane space
K(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},1)
(see
[
Hat
, p.87, p.365]
for the definition of this notion). On the topological side, since
\pi_{1}(S^{1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
and
\pi_{i}(S^{1})=0
for
i\geq 2
, the circle
S^{1}
is
K(\mathbb{Z},1)
. For every integer
n\geq 1
, there is a unique extension
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}
of degree
n
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime}/\mathbb{F})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. On the topological side, there is a unique
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
cover
[n]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{1}\rightarrow S^{1}
. Identifying
S^{1}
with the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
, the covering map
[n]
maps
z\mapsto z^{n}
. Thus the finite Galois covers of
\mathbb{F}
are in correspondence with finite covers of
S^{1}
.

Given a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in a number ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the valuation ring in
K
. In the diagram

(2.1)

\operatorname{Spec}K_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookleftarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}},

the map
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is induced by reduction mod-
\mathfrak{p}
and is an
étale homotopy equivalence
(see
[
AM06
]
). On the other hand, the map
\operatorname{Spec}K_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is induced by the inclusion of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

On the topological side, the ambient
3
-manifold is assumed for simplicity to be
S^{3}
, and there would be significant work required to extend the results to links embedded in more general
3
-manifolds. We note that some authors do work in slightly greater generality, for instance, in
[
Uek17
]
, results are proven for
rational homology
3
-spheres
. We come to the definition of a
knot
and more generally, a
link
. We refer to
[
Kaw96
, Chapter 1]
and
[
Lic12
, Chapter 1]
for a comprehensive treatment of some of the basic notions in knot theory, as well as definitions that are equivalent to that given below, which we have chosen since in our estimation, it is easiest to state.

Definition 2.1
.

A knot (in
S^{3}
) is an embedding
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{1}\hookrightarrow S^{3}
which is locally flat, i.e., locally homeomorphic to the canonical embedding
\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
sending
x
to
(x,0,0)
. Two knots are equivalent if there is an isotopy
f_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{3}\rightarrow S^{3}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
, taking one knot to the other. In order to simplify notation, we shall identify the embedding
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{1}\hookrightarrow S^{3}
with the
\mathcal{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\iota(S^{1})
. A
link
(in
S^{3}
) is a union of disjoint knots
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{K}_{r}
in
S^{3}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot in
S^{3}
, fix a
tubular neighbourhood
(see
[
Lee00
, p.137, ll.7-11]
for the definition)
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{K}
, and let
\partial\mathcal{V}
be the boundary of
\mathcal{V}
. Note that
\partial\mathcal{V}
is homotopic to
S^{1}\times\mathcal{K}
. The diagram (
2.1
) is analogous to

\partial\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}\hookleftarrow\mathcal{K}.

Note that the inclusion
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}
is a homotopy equivalence, and
\partial\mathcal{V}
is homeomorphic to
S^{1}\times\mathcal{K}
. By abuse of notation, we identify
\mathcal{V}
with the product
S^{1}\times\mathcal{K}
. Consider the composite of maps

\xi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}(\partial\mathcal{V})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathcal{V})\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{K}).

Given
a\in S^{1}
, we refer to a loop of the form
\{a\}\times\mathcal{K}
in
\partial\mathcal{V}
as a
longitude
of
\mathcal{K}
, and we denote the associated homotopy class by
\ell_{\mathcal{K}}\in\pi_{1}(\partial\mathcal{V})
. Note that this homotopy class is independent of the choice of
a
. The image of
\ell_{\mathcal{K}}
is a generator of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{K})
, and for this reason,
\ell_{\mathcal{K}}
is an analog of a Frobenius element
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}/K_{\mathfrak{p}})
. The kernel of
\xi
is referred to as the
inertia group
of
\mathcal{K}
. A
meridian

\tau_{\mathcal{K}}
is a generator of the inertia group of the form
S^{1}\times\{b\}
. Note that this homotopy class is independent of the choice of point
b
. The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\partial\mathcal{V})
is the generated by
\ell_{\mathcal{K}}
and
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}
, with the single relation
[\ell_{\mathcal{K}},\tau_{\mathcal{K}}]=\ell_{\mathcal{K}}\tau_{\mathcal{K}}\ell_{\mathcal{K}}^{-1}\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{-1}=1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{K}_{r}
be an
oriented link
, which is to say that each knot
\mathcal{K}_{j}
is oriented. Given distinct knots
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}
, let
\ell_{i,j}
be the
linking number
of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}
, we refer to
[
Kaw96
, p. 11]
for a precise definition. Let
X_{\mathcal{L}}
be the complement of
\mathcal{L}
in
S^{3}
, and let
x_{0}
be a choice of base-point in
X_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
link group
of
\mathcal{L}
is the fundamental group
G_{\mathcal{L}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{1}(X_{\mathcal{L}};x_{0})
.

A comprehensive study of the analogies between fundamental groups and Galois groups of number fields is undertaken in
[
VH96
]
. The
link group
is the analogue of the group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{S}/K)
, where
S
is a finite set of primes and
K_{S}
is the maximal algebraic extension of
K
which is unramified at primes
v\notin S
. The set of primes
S
here is the set at which
K_{S}
may ramify and likewise. On the other hand,
G_{\mathcal{L}}
is the deck group of
\pi_{\mathcal{L}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{X}_{\mathcal{L}}\rightarrow X_{\mathcal{L}}
, the universal cover of
X_{\mathcal{L}}
.

3.
The Alexander Module associated to a link in a
3
-sphere

The classical Alexander polynomial is an important topological invariant associated to a link in a
3
-sphere (or, more generally
3
-manifold). It was first introduced by Alexander
[
Ale28
]
. On the other hand, there is a
p
-adic version introduced by Hillman-Matei-Morishita
[
HMM06
]
which is widely regarded as the multi-variable topological analogue of the Iwasawa polynomial of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. In this setting, one may define

4.
Linking numbers and calculation of Iwasawa Invariants

In this section, we introduce various methods of detecting the Iwasawa invariants introduced in the previous section, and how they are related to
p
-adic Milnor invariants
introduced in
[
HMM06
]
. We shall describe the precise analogy with the number field case. Let
p
be an odd prime and
K
be an imaginary quadratic field in which

splits, and consider the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

. The splitting of

creates a trivial zero for the

is divisible by

[

]
). Therefore, in the case where

, we have that
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)\geq 1
. In this setting, Gold gives a criterion that distinguishes the case when
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)=1
from the case when
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)>1
. This essentially is due a formula due to Federer, Gross and Sinnott
[

]
for the leading (non-zero) term of the

-adic L-series which is expressed in terms of the
p
-part of the class group of
K
, and the
p
-adic regulator for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

. The result follows from work of Gold
[
Gol74
]
and Sands
[
San93
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime which is split in an imaginary quadratic field

. Write

as a product

, where
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}
are the primes of

. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)>1
,

(2)

(a)

,

(b)

or,
p
does not divide the class number of

. Let

\mathfrak{p}^{N}=\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K}
, a principal ideal in

\alpha^{p-1}\equiv 1\mod{(\mathfrak{p}^{*})^{2}}
.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
San93
, Proposition 2.1]
.
∎

In
[

]
, Horie shows that for any odd prime

, there are infinitely many imaginary quadratic fields

in which
p
splits such that
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)>1
. On the other hand, Jochnowitz shows in
[

]
that there are infinitely many

in which
p
splits such that
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)=1
. One may formulate a more refined question guided by arithmetic statistics. Given an imaginary quadratic field
K
, and a real number
x>0
, let
D_{K}
denote its discriminant and let
\mathcal{M}_{<x}^{p}
be the set of all imaginary quadratic fields in which
p
splits with
|D_{K}|<x
. Note that this is a finite set. Let
\mathcal{M}_{<x}^{\prime}
be the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{<x}
consisting of imaginary quadratic fields for which
\lambda_{p}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)=1
. Then, the upper (resp. lower density) of imaginary quadratic fields
K
in which
p
splits and
\lambda_{p}=1
is taken to be
\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}^{\prime}}{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}}
(resp.
\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}^{\prime}}{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}}
). If the limit
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}^{\prime}}{\#\mathcal{M}_{<x}}
exists, then it is called the density. Let
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}
,
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}
be the upper and lower density respectively, and note that if
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}=\underline{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}
, the density exists, and we denote it by
\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda=1}^{p}
.

Question 4.2
.

Let
p
be a prime. With respect to notation above, what can be said about the quantities
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}
and
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}_{\lambda=1}^{p}

In this paper, we shall study topological analogs of the above question in section

Definition 4.3
.

The
p
-adic Traldi matrix
\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
over

\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L}}(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\leq r,i_{n}\neq i}\hat{\mu}(i_{1}\dots i_{n}i)X_{i_{1}}\dots X_{i_{n}}\right)&\text{ if }i=j,\\

Theorem 4.4
.

The

-adic Traldi matrix

coincides with the Alexander matrix

.

Proof.

The above result is
[

∎

Let
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
be such that

and
\operatorname{gcd}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=1
. Set

\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},\mathbf{1}}(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\leq r,i_{n}\neq i}\hat{\mu}(i_{1}\dots i_{n}i)\right)X^{n}&\text{ if }i=j,\\

Given

\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},z}^{(2)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{t\neq i}-z_{t}\ell_{t,i}\right)X&\text{ if }i=j,\\
\ell_{i,j}z_{i}X&\text{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}.

Here, we have used the relation

, see (
4.1
). The

\ell_{i,j}z_{i}&\text{ if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

we find that
\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},z}^{(2)}
is represented by
XC_{\mathcal{L},z}\mod{X^{2}}
.

Given
i,j
such that

denote the

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
be an admissible integral and
\mathcal{L}
a link with exactly
r
components
\mathcal{K}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{K}_{r}
. Then, the order of vanishing satisfies the lower bound

\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)\geq r-1.

Write
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)
as a sum

\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)=a_{r-1}X^{r-1}+a_{r}X^{r}+\dots,

where
a_{r-1}
is the
leading coefficient
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)>r-1
if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C_{\mathcal{L},z}<r-1
.

(2)

Suppose that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C_{\mathcal{L},z}=r-1
, then,
|a_{r-1}|_{p}=c_{\mathcal{L},z}
.

Proof.

Note that each entry of the matrix
\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},z}
is divisible by
X
. Since
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)
is generated by the determinants of the
(r-1)\times(r-1)
minors of
\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},z}
, it follows that
\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)\geq r-1
. From the relation

\widehat{T}_{\mathcal{L},z}\equiv C_{\mathcal{L},z}\mod{X^{2}}

we thus find that
|a_{r-1}|_{p}
is equal to
c_{\mathcal{L},z}
. In particular,
\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)>r-1
if and only if
c_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
, if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C_{\mathcal{L},z}<r-1
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f(X)\in\widehat{\Lambda}
be given by the power series expansion

f(X)=a_{d}X^{d}+a_{d+1}X^{d+1}+\dots,

where
a_{d}\neq 0
, and let
\mu
and
\lambda
denote the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
f(X)
respectively. Then,
\lambda\geq d
and following conditions are equivalent

(1)

the
\mu=0
and the
\lambda=d
,

(2)

a_{d}
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

Write
f(X)=X^{d}g(X)
with
g(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=a_{r-1}+a_{r}X+a_{r+1}X^{2}+\dots
. Suppose that
\mu=0
and
\lambda=d
. Then,
f(X)=P(X)u(X)
, where
P(X)
is a distinguished polynomial of degree
d
and
u(X)
is a unit in
\widehat{\Lambda}
. Note that
X^{d}
is distinguished and divides
P(X)
and since the degree of
P(X)
is equal to
d
, it follows that
P(X)=X^{d}
and
g(X)=u(X)
is a unit in
\widehat{\Lambda}
. It follows that
a_{r-1}
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Conversely, if
a_{r-1}
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then
g(X)
is a unit in
\Lambda
and hence,
\mu=0
and
\lambda=\operatorname{deg}X^{d}=d
.
∎

We apply the above result to the study of Iwasawa invariants. Let
\bar{C}_{\mathcal{L},z}
be the mod-
p
reduction of
C_{\mathcal{L},z}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a link of
r\geq 2
component knots and
z
an integral vector. Then, we have that
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}\geq r-1
. Furthermore, the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\mu_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
and
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}=r-1
,

(2)

c_{\mathcal{L},z}
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

(3)

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bar{C}_{\mathcal{L},z}=r-1
.

Proof.

Let
f(X)
denote the completed Alexander polynomial

. Note that according to Proposition
4.5
,
\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}f(X)\geq r-1
. In particular, this implies that
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}\geq r-1
. We write

, and it follows from Proposition
4.5
that
|a_{r-1}|_{p}=c_{\mathcal{L},z}

that

is a unit in

\mu_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
and

if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bar{C}_{\mathcal{L},z}=r-1

∎

Let us explain the above result for small values of

.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}_{1}\cup\mathcal{K}_{2}
be a link consisting of
2
-components, and
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
an admissible integral vector. Then,
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}\geq 1
and the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\mathrm{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)=1
,

(2)

\ell_{1,2}\neq 0
.

Furthermore, the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\mu_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
and
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}=1
.

(2)

p\nmid\ell_{1,2}

Proof.

We have that
C_{\mathcal{L},z}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}-z_{2}\ell_{1,2}&z_{1}\ell_{1,2}\\
z_{2}\ell_{1,2}&-z_{1}\ell_{1,2}\\
\end{array}}\right)
. According to Proposition
4.5
,

\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)=1

if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C_{\mathcal{L},z}=1
. This is equivalent to the non-vanishing of

.

Since it is assumed that

z_{1}
or

(or both) are not divisible by

.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}_{1}\cup\mathcal{K}_{2}\cup\mathcal{K}_{3}
be a link consisting of
3
-components, and
z
an admissible integral vector. Then,
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}\geq 2
and the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)=2
,

(2)

(z_{2}\ell_{2,1}\ell_{3,2}+z_{1}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{3,2})\neq 0
, or,
z_{2}\ell_{2,1}\ell_{3,2}+z_{1}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{3,2}\neq 0
(or both).

Furthermore, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mu_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
and
\lambda_{\mathcal{L},z}=2
.

(2)

Both of the following conditions are satisfied

(a)

p\nmid z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}
,

(b)

p\nmid\left(z_{2}\ell_{2,1}\ell_{3,2}+z_{1}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{3,2}\right)

We have that
C_{\mathcal{L},z}=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}-(z_{2}\ell_{2,1}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1})&z_{1}\ell_{1,2}&z_{1}\ell_{1,3}\\
z_{2}\ell_{2,1}&-(z_{1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,2})&z_{2}\ell_{2,3}\\
z_{3}\ell_{3,1}&z_{3}\ell_{3,2}&-(z_{1}\ell_{1,3}+z_{2}\ell_{2,3})\\

. Proposition
4.5
asserts that
\operatorname{ord}_{X=0}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathcal{L},z}(X)=2
if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C_{\mathcal{L},z}=2
. Note that the sum of the rows of
C_{\mathcal{L},z}
is equal to
0
, hence,
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(C_{\mathcal{L},z})=2
if and only if the first two rows are linearly independent. This is the case when at least one of the determinants

\begin{split}&\operatorname{det}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}-(z_{2}\ell_{2,1}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1})&z_{1}\ell_{1,2}\\
z_{2}\ell_{2,1}&-(z_{1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,2})\\

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(C_{\mathcal{L},z})=2
if and only if
(z_{2}\ell_{2,1}\ell_{3,2}+z_{1}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{1,2}+z_{3}\ell_{3,1}\ell_{3,2})\neq 0

4.7

\mu_{\mathcal{L},z}=0
and

if and only if
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bar{C}_{\mathcal{L},z}

∎

5.
Statistics for Iwasawa invariants of
2
-bridge links

In this section, we study the average behavior of the Iwasawa invariants associated to links
\mathcal{L}
consisting of two component knots
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. We formulate a precise problem in arithmetic statistics. The links
\mathcal{L}
that we consider are presented as
2
-bridge links
(see
[
Kaw96
, p.18]
).

To each oriented
2
-bridge link
\mathcal{L}
there corresponds a relatively prime pair of integers
(a,b)
with
0<a<2b
, and
b
odd when
\mathcal{L}
is a knot and
b
even when
\mathcal{L}
is a link with two knot components
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. We shall denote the link associated to the pair
(a,b)
by
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
. This construction is standard parametrization, and referred to as the
Schubert normal form
and we refer to
[
Tul81
, p.542, l.-6 to l.-1]
or
[
Kaw96
, chapter 2]
for an explicit description of these links. Throughout, we shall assume that
b
is even, thus
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
has two components. Since
a
is assumed to be coprime to
b
, it follows that
a
must be odd. Choose an orientation on
S^{3}
. All links we shall consider shall also be provided with an orientation.

Definition 5.1
.

[
Kaw96
, Definition 0.3.1]

Two (oriented) piecewise linear links
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
in
S^{3}
have the same type if there is a piecewise linear orientation preserving homeomorphism
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{3}\rightarrow S^{3}
such that
h(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
h_{|L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is orientation preserving.

We shall consider equivalence classes of links
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
of the same type. The following result explain when two pairs
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
and
\mathcal{L}_{a^{\prime}/b^{\prime}}
have the same type. Note that we are assuming here that
b
and
b^{\prime}
are both even.

Theorem 5.2
.

The
2
-component links
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
and
\mathcal{L}_{a^{\prime}/b^{\prime}}
belong to the same type if and only if

b=b^{\prime}\text{ and }a^{\prime}a\equiv 1\mod{2b}.

Proof.

We refer to
[
Kaw96
, Theorem 2.1.3]
for this result.
∎

For example, it follows from the above that the oriented links
\mathcal{L}_{3/10}
and
\mathcal{L}_{7/10}
have the same type.

Remark 5.3
.

The conventions in
[
Kaw96
]
involve a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
that are coprime with
-\alpha<\beta<\alpha
, and
\alpha>0
. The conventions used here are equivalent to those of
[
Kaw96
]
, however, shall be of more convenience to work with. In order to see that the conventions indeed match up, compare the construction of 2-bridge links in
[
Kaw96
]
with that in
[
Tul81
]

Given a rational number
a/b
with
(a,b)=1
, and
a,b>0
, define the counting function
\operatorname{ht}(\mathcal{L}_{a/b})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{a^{2},b\}

of

. We do not work with the conventional notion of height of the fraction

, and as far as we are aware, there is no conventional notion of height that is used in such a context. In fact, our computations have led us to believe that the result is not true for an alternate definition of height

\operatorname{ht}(\mathcal{L}_{a/b})

We shall study the average behavior of Iwasawa invariants of
\mathcal{L}_{a/b}
, where
\operatorname{ht}(a/b)
goes to infinity. Let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of all fractions
a/b
with
(a,b)=1
,
b

\mathcal{S}

Denote by
\mathcal{S}^{p,z}

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S}^{p,z})=1-1/p

Remark 5.5
.

We thus study the proportion of

For

Lemma 5.6
.

The following relation is satisfied

\ell_{a/(b+2a)}=\ell_{a/b}+1.

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq(a-1)
, let
c_{j}(a/b)
be the number of odd integers
2i-1
such that

\frac{jb}{a}\leq 2i-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a}.

Note that
2i-1
is in the range
\frac{jb}{a}\leq 2i-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a}
, if and only if
\lfloor\frac{(2i-1)a}{b}\rfloor=j
. Therefore,
c_{j}(a/b)
is the number of odd numbers
2i-1
such that
\lfloor\frac{(2i-1)a}{b}\rfloor=j
. Thus the formula (
5.1
) yields

(5.2)

\ell_{a/b}=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{j}c_{j}(a/b).

Next, we show that
c_{j}(\frac{a}{b+2a})=c_{j}(\frac{a}{b})+1
. Note that
c_{j}(\frac{a}{b+2a})
is the number of odd integers
2i-1
such that

\frac{jb}{a}+2j\leq 2i-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a}+2j+2.

Let
\{2i_{1}-1,\dots,2i_{c_{j}}-1\}
be the odd numbers such that

\frac{jb}{a}\leq 2i_{k}-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a},

here,
c_{j}=c_{j}(a/b)
.
Then,
\{2i_{1}+2j-1,\dots,2i_{c_{j}}+2j-1\}
are the odd numbers
2i-1
such that

\frac{jb}{a}+2j\leq 2i-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a}+2j.

There is exactly one odd number
2i-1
in the range

\frac{(j+1)b}{a}+2j\leq 2i-1<\frac{(j+1)b}{a}+2j+2,

namely,
2i-1=2i_{c_{j}}+2j+1
. Hence, this shows that
c_{j}(\frac{a}{b+2a})=c_{j}(\frac{a}{b})+1
.
It follows from (
5.2
) that

\begin{split}\ell_{a/(b+2a)}=&\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{j}c_{j}\left(a/(b+2a)\right)\\
=&\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{j}\left(c_{j}(a/b)+1\right)\\
=&\ell_{a/b}+\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{j}\\
=&\ell_{a/b}+1.\\
\end{split}

The last equality holds since
a
is odd.
∎

Since the set
\mathcal{S}^{p,z}
consists of all fractions
a/b\in\mathcal{N}
with
p\nmid\ell_{a/b}
, we find that
\mathcal{S}^{p,z}
does not depend on the choice of
z
. Thus, to simplify notation, we denote this set by
\mathcal{S}^{p}
. Given a fixed number
a>0
, set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{b\mid 2b>a,a/b\in\mathcal{N}_{<x}\}.\\
&\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{b\mid 2b>a,a/b\in\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}\}.\\
\end{split}

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
a>0
, with respect to notation above

|\frac{1}{p}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)-\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}(a)|<2a.

Proof.

The relation
\ell_{a/(b+2a)}=\ell_{a/b}+1
shows that among every
2ap
consecutive numbers
b
in
\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)
, there are exactly
2a
of them that belong to
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{<x}(a)
. Therefore, we find that

|\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)-p\#\mathcal{S}^{p}_{<x}(a)|<2ap.

The result follows from this.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

With respect to notation above,

|\frac{1}{p}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}-\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}|<x+\sqrt{x}.

Proof.

Note that
\operatorname{ht}(a/b)=\operatorname{max}\{a^{2},b\}
, thus if
\operatorname{ht}(a/b)<x
, we have that
a<\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor
.
We find that

\begin{split}&|\frac{1}{p}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}-\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}|\\
\leq&\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor}|\frac{1}{p}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)-\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}(a)|\\
<&\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor}2a=\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor+1).\\
\end{split}

Note that in the last step, we have invoked Lemma
5.7
. The result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

With respect to notation above,

\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}\geq x^{3/2}-\frac{5}{4}(x+\sqrt{x}).

Proof.

We find that

\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}=\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a).

Recall that
\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)
is the set of numbers
b
such that
2b>a
and
a/b\in\mathcal{N}_{<x}
. Since
a\leq\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor
, the condition
a/b\in\mathcal{N}_{<x}
simply becomes
b\leq\lfloor x\rfloor
. Therefore, we have that
\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}(a)\geq x-a/2-1
, and thus,

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}\geq&\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor}(x-a/2-1).\\
=&\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor x-\frac{1}{4}\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor+1)-\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor\\
>&x^{3/2}-x-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{x}(\sqrt{x}+1)-\sqrt{x}\\
=&x^{3/2}-\frac{5}{4}(x+\sqrt{x}).\end{split}

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Recall that Lemma
5.7
asserts that

\left|\frac{1}{p}\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}-\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}\right|<x+\sqrt{x},

and therefore,

\left|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}}{\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}}\right|<\frac{x+\sqrt{x}}{\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}}.

Invoking Lemma
5.9
, we find that

\left|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{<x}^{p}}{\#\mathcal{N}_{<x}}\right|<\frac{x+\sqrt{x}}{\left(x^{3/2}-\frac{5}{4}(x+\sqrt{x})\right)},

which clearly goes to
0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
. The result thus follows.

∎

Remark 5.10
.

Note that the above proof in fact shows that the error term is bounded by
\frac{x+\sqrt{x}}{\left(x^{3/2}-\frac{5}{4}(x+\sqrt{x})\right)}
, a quantity which is asymptotic to
{x}^{-1/2}
.

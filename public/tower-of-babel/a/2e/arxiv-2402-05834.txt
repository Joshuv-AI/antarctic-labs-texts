Scalar curvature rigidity and the higher mapping degree
Thomas Tony

Abstract
A closed connected oriented Riemannian manifold N N with non-vanishing Euler characteristic, non-negative curvature operator and 0 < 2 ​ Ric N < scal N 0<2\Ric_{N}<\scal_{N} is area-rigid in the sense that any area non-increasing spin map f : M → N f\colon M\to N with non-vanishing A ^ \hat{A} -degree and scal M ≥ scal ⁡ ∘ N ⁡ f \scal_{M}\geq\scal_{N}\mathop{\circ}\nolimits f is a Riemannian submersion with scal M = scal ⁡ ∘ N ⁡ f \scal_{M}=\scal_{N}\mathop{\circ}\nolimits f . This is due to Goette and Semmelmann and generalizes a result by Llarull. In this article, we show area-rigidity for not necessarily orientable manifolds with respect to a larger class of maps f : M → N f\colon M\to N by replacing the topological condition on the A ^ \hat{A} -degree by a less restrictive condition involving the so-called higher mapping degree. This includes fiber bundles over even dimensional spheres with enlargeable fibers, e.g. pr 1 : S 2 ​ n × T k → S 2 ​ n \pr_{1}\colon S^{2n}\times T^{k}\to S^{2n} . We develop a technique to extract from a non-vanishing higher index a geometrically useful family of almost 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -harmonic sections. This also leads to a new proof of the fact that any closed connected spin manifold with non-negative scalar curvature and non-trivial Rosenberg index is Ricci flat.

Introduction and main results

A classical result in scalar curvature comparison geometry by
[
Lla98
]
states that a smooth area non-increasing map
f\colon M\to S^{n}
from an
n
-dimensional closed connected Riemannian spin manifold of non-zero degree onto the round sphere,
n\geq 2
, with
\scal_{M}\geq n\left(n-1\right)=\scal_{S^{n}}
, is necessarily an isometry. An important consequence is that the round sphere is area-extremal in the sense that one cannot increase the metric and the scalar curvature simultaneously.
[
GS02
]
generalized this result to area non-increasing spin maps
f\colon M\to N
of non-zero
\hat{A}
-degree onto a closed connected oriented Riemannian manifold of non-vanishing Euler characteristic and non-negative curvature operator. Further generalizations are by
[
Lot21
]
to manifolds with boundary, by
[
CHS24
]
to Lipschitz maps with lower regularity, and by
[
BG24
]
, who weakened the condition on the curvature operator of the target manifold in dimension four.

In this article, we generalize the extremality and rigidity statement of
[
GS02
, Theorem 2.4]
to spin maps between non-orientable manifolds and replace the topological condition on the
\hat{A}
-degree by a less restrictive index-theoretical condition involving the so-called higher mapping degree (see
5.2
). Here spin map means that the map is compatible with the first and second Stiefel-Whitney classes of the involved manifolds.

Theorem A

(
5.5
and
5.6
)

Let

Then

\scal_{M}=\scal_{N}\mathop{\circ}\nolimits f

The motivation for

does. A further obstruction to a positive scalar curvature metric on a closed spin manifold is enlargeability
[
GL80
, Theorem A]

n
-torus does not admit a positive scalar curvature metric. In general, the enlargeability obstruction for a closed spin manifold is dominated by the Rosenberg obstruction
[

,
HS07
]

5.8

]

The Mishchenko bundle and the Rosenberg index

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold of dimension
m
with fundamental group
\pi
and universal cover
\widetilde{M}
. Denote by
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
the maximal group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra of
\pi
equipped with the Real structure induced by the natural Real structure on
\mathbb{C}
. The
Mishchenko (line) bundle
of
M

[
MF80
]
is defined by

\mathcal{L}\left(M\right)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\widetilde{M}\times_{\pi}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi,

(2.11)

where
\pi
acts on
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
by left multiplication. The bundle
\mathcal{L}
comes with a canonical flat connection and a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
-valued product given by
\left\langle a\mkern 2.0mu,b\right\rangle\coloneqq a^{*}b
, hence
\mathcal{L}
is a flat bundle of finitely generated projective Hilbert
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
-modules together with a Real structure.

Suppose the manifold
M
is equipped with a spin structure. Twisting the
\mathrm{\mathbb{C}l}_{m}
-linear spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}
of
M
by the Mishchenko bundle
\mathcal{L}
as in
2.2
gives a graded Real
\mathrm{\mathbb{C}l}_{m}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
-linear Dirac bundle
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\mathcal{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{L}
. The higher index of the induced
\mathrm{\mathbb{C}l}_{m}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi
-linear Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}
, denoted by

\alpha\left(M\right)\coloneqq\ind\left(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)\in\KO_{0}\left(\mathrm{\mathbb{C}l}_{m}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\right)\cong\KO_{m}\left(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\right),

(2.12)

is called the
Rosenberg index of
M

[
Ros83
]
. There is a well-known connection between the Rosenberg index and the scalar curvature of
M
:

Proposition 2.6
.

If the scalar curvature of the manifold
M
is positive, then the Rosenberg index of
M
vanishes.

Proof.

Assume
\scal_{M}>0
. Since the Mishchenko bundle
\mathcal{L}
is flat, the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula yields
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{\scal_{M}}{4}
, and we obtain
\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}u\mkern 2.0mu,u\right\rangle_{\Ltwoblanc}\geq\min_{p\in M}\scal_{M}\left(p\right)\left\langle u\mkern 2.0mu,u\right\rangle_{\Ltwoblanc}
for all
u\in\Hblanc^{2}\left(M,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
. Note that
\min_{p\in M}\scal_{M}\left(p\right)
is strictly positive by assumption on the scalar curvature. By
2.4
part (1), the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}
is invertible and the Rosenberg index of
M
vanishes by part (2) of the same lemma.
∎

There is a meta-conjecture by
[
Sch14
, Conjecture 1.5]
, that any obstruction to positive scalar curvature on a closed spin manifold (of dimension at least five) based on index theory of Dirac operators can be read off the Rosenberg index. The following proposition is a well-known result in this direction and justifies why the Mishchenko bundle is the right bundle for the twist with the spinor bundle. In particular, it states that any obstruction to a positive scalar curvature metric on
M
, obtained from a higher index of the spin Dirac operator twisted with any Real flat bundle of finitely generated projective Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-modules, can be read off the Rosenberg index.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\to M
be a graded Real
\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac bundle,
\mathcal{B}
a unital Real
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra and

a Real flat bundle of finitely generated projective
\mathcal{B}
-modules. Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{E}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}
the induced Dirac operators of
\mathcal{S}
twisted with the bundle
E
, respectively the Mishchenko bundle
\mathcal{L}
. Then the following holds:

\ind\mathcal{D}_{E}\neq 0\in\KO\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{B}\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ind\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\neq 0\in\KO\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\right)

(2.13)

Proof.

We orientate towards the proof of a similar, but slightly weaker, result by
[
SW21
, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2]
. Let
P
be the typical fiber of the flat bundle
E
. Then there exists a representation
\rho\colon\pi\to U_{\mathcal{B}}\left(P\right)
on the unitary operators on the Real finitely generated projective Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
P
such that
E\cong\widetilde{M}\times_{\rho}P
. By the universal property of the maximal group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra, this representation extends to a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra homomorphism
\overline{\rho}\colon\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\to\Lin_{\mathcal{B}}\left(P\right)
that respects the grading and the Real structure. The representation

\psi\coloneqq\id_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\overline{\rho}:\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\to\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\Lin_{\mathcal{B}}\left(P\right)\cong\Lin_{\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}P\right)

(2.14)

induces as in the proof of
[
Roe17
, Lemma 12.17]
a homomorphism on Real K-theory, which takes in the Fredholm picture the form

\textstyle\left(\psi\right)_{0}\colon\textstyle\KO_{0}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1em]{}}\textstyle\KO_{0}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{B}\right),\;\textstyle\left[\left(F,Q\right)\right]\xmapsto{\mathmakebox[1em]{\mbox{ }}}\textstyle\left[\left(F\otimes\id_{\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}P},Q\otimes_{\psi}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}P\right)\right)\right].

(2.15)

Here

denotes the interior tensor product with respect to the representation
\psi
. Furthermore, the bundle

, which implies

\Ltwoblanc\left(M,\mathcal{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{L}\right)\otimes_{\psi}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}P\right)\cong\Ltwoblanc\left(M,\left(\mathcal{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{L}\right)\otimes_{\psi}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}P\right)\right)\cong\Ltwoblanc\left(M,\mathcal{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}E\right).

(2.16)

Note that the tensor product of a bundle of Hilbert
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-modules with a Hilbert
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-module is always taken fiberwise. Together with

Almost harmonic, almost parallel and almost constant sections

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a classical graded Dirac bundle over a closed connected Riemannian manifold
M
. If the index of the induced Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
is non-zero, there exists a non-trivial smooth section
u
in the kernel of
\mathcal{D}
. Furthermore, if
u
is additionally parallel, i.e.
\nabla u=0
, the map
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
,
p\mapsto\left\|u\right\|_{p}
, is constant. In this section, we generalize this result to an
\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac operator induced by a graded Real
\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac bundle over a closed connected Riemannian manifold. It turns out that if the higher index of the Dirac operator does not vanish in
\KO\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, there exists a family of almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic sections (see
3.1
). We will furthermore study under which conditions these sections are almost constant in the sense of
3.1
.

At first, we make the notion of almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic, almost parallel and almost constant precise. Second, we construct almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic sections under the hypothesis that the
\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac operator is not invertible (
3.2
), which is the case, if its higher index does not vanish. Last, we study the relation between almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic, almost parallel and almost constant sections in general.

We fix a graded Real
\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac bundle
\mathcal{S}
over a closed connected Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n
with induced Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A family
\{u_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
of smooth sections of
\mathcal{S}
, which is
\Ltwoblanc
-normalized, i.e.
\left\lVert u_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{\Ltwoblanc}=1
for all
\epsilon>0
, is called

(1)

almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic
if
\big\lVert\mathcal{D}^{j}u_{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{\Ltwoblanc}<\epsilon^{j}
for all
\epsilon>0
and all
j\geq 1
.

(2)

almost parallel
if it satisfies
\left\lVert\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{\Ltwoblanc}<\epsilon
for all
\epsilon>0
and all covariant derivatives are uniformly bounded in
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right)
, i.e. for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists a constant
C_{m}
such that
\left\lVert\nabla^{m}u_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{\infty}<C_{m}
for all
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right)
.

(3)

almost constant
if there exist constants
C,r>0
such that

\big\lVert\overline{u}_{\epsilon}-\left\lvert u_{\epsilon}\right\rvert_{p}^{2}\big\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}<C\epsilon^{r},\quad\text{with}\quad\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}M}\int_{M}\left\lvert u_{\epsilon}\right\rvert^{2}_{q}\mkern 4.0mu\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7muq,

(3.1)

for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and all
p\in M
.

In the next lemma, we give a connection between the existence of a family of almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic sections and the invertibility of the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
. Combined with
2.4
(2), we obtain: If the index of
\mathcal{D}
does not vanish in
\KO_{0}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, there exists a family of almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic sections.

Lemma 3.2
.

If the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
is not invertible, there exists a family of almost
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic sections.

Proof.

We fix for all
\epsilon>0
a smooth function
\feblanc\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with

(3.2)

Here we used in the second step that zero is in the spectrum of

. We conclude from

Non-trivial Rosenberg index and the Ricci curvature

In this section, we apply the techniques from
section

3
to give a direct proof of the well-known fact that any closed connected spin manifold with non-negative scalar curvature and non-vanishing Rosenberg index is Ricci flat.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a closed connected spin manifold of dimension
n
,
\mathcal{A}
a unital Real
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra and
E\rightarrow M
a Real flat bundle of finitely generated projective Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Let
\mathcal{D}_{E}
be the induced Dirac operator of the
\mathrm{\mathbb{C}l}_{n}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{A}
-linear Dirac bundle obtained by twisting the spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}
by
E
as in
2.2
. If
\scal\geq 0
and the higher index of
\mathcal{D}_{E}
is non-zero in
\KO_{n}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, the Ricci curvature vanishes.

Applying the previous theorem to the maximal group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra of the fundamental group of
M
, equipped with its natural Real structure, and the Mishchenko bundle yields:

Corollary 4.2
.

Any closed connected spin manifold with
\scal\geq 0
and non-vanishing Rosenberg index is Ricci flat.

Let us take a look at the proof of
4.2
in the case where the classical index of the spin Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
is non-zero: There exists a
\Ltwoblanc
-normalized
\mathcal{D}
-harmonic section
u
of the spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}
, which is by the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula even parallel and satisfies
\left\langle u\left(p\right)\mkern 2.0mu,u\left(p\right)\right\rangle_{p}=\left(\mathrm{vol}{M}\right)^{-1}
for all
p\in M
. For a local orthonormal frame
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
TM|_{U}
and any vector field
X
supported in
U
, we obtain

\left\lVert\Ric\left(X\right)\right\rVert_{\Ltwoblanc}=\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}\left(M\right)}\left\lVert\Ric\left(X\right)\cdot u\right\rVert_{\Ltwoblanc}\overset{\text{(\ref{RicCurvatureRealtion})}}{=}2\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}\left(M\right)}\big\|\sum_{j}e_{j}\cdot K_{X,e_{j}}u\big\|_{\Ltwoblanc}=0,

(4.1)

hence
\Ric\equiv 0
. Here
“
\;\cdot\;
”
denotes the Clifford multiplication by vector fields, and

denotes the curvature tensor of
\mathcal{S}
. We used in the second step

Remark 5.11

(Rigidity for non-orientable manifolds)
.

We assume in
5.5
that the map
f
is orientable, but not that the involved manifolds are orientable. The orientability assumption in the classical statement by
[
GS02
, Theorem 2.4]
does not restrict the applicability of the classical theorem, since one can always apply it to the lift of the map to the oriented double coverings. This trick also works in our case for the map
\pr_{1}\colon\mathbb{RP}^{2n}\times T^{k}\to\mathbb{RP}^{2n}
considered in
5.8
, but not for the map
\mathbb{RP}^{2n}\times\Sigma^{8k+j}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{\pr_{1}}}\mathbb{RP}^{2n}
in
5.10
. The reason is that the lift
S^{2n}\times\Sigma^{8k+j}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{\pr_{1}}}S^{2n}
does not fulfill the topological condition (
(T1)
), since the Euler characteristic of
S^{2n}
is two and the Hitchin invariant of
\Sigma^{8k+j}
is two torsion. This shows that including non-orientable manifolds in
5.5
extends the applicability of the theorem.

In
5.8
, we considered projections from a product of two manifold onto one of its factors. A generalization of this class of examples to possible non-trivial fiber bundles as in
5.10
requires more topological conditions on the target manifold
N
. The reason is that we have to ensure, additionally to the index-theoretical condition on the Euler characteristic and the Rosenberg index of the fiber, that the condition on the fundamental groups in (
(T2)
) is fulfilled. It is sufficient for the existence of a retraction from
M_{p}
to
M
on the level of fundamental groups that the target manifold is 2-connected (
5.13
).

All simply connected closed Riemannian manifolds of non-negative curvature operator are classified. See for instance
[
Wil07
, Theorem 1.13]
.
This leads, together with the classification of irreducible symmetric spaces by
[
Car26
]
, to
5.12
. In
5.13
, we state the rigidity statement for fiber bundles over these manifolds.

Lemma 5.12
.

Any factor of the de Rham decomposition of a closed 2-connected Riemannian manifold with non-negative curvature operator and non-vanishing Euler characteristic is isometric to a sphere
S^{2n}
,
n\geq 2
, equipped with a metric of non-negative curvature operator, a quaternionic Grassmannian or the Cayley projective plane.

The
quaternionic Grassmannian
is the
4pq
-dimensional symmetric space C II, given by
\Sp\left(p+q\right)/\penalty\Sp\left(p\right)\times\Sp\left(q\right)
, and the
Cayley projective plane
is the 16-dimensional symmetric space F II, given by
F_{4}/\penalty\Spin\left(9\right)
. Here we denote by
\Sp\left(k\right)
the compact symplectic group and by
F4
the simply connected Lie group associated to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}_{4}

[
Yok09
]
. Both manifolds are equipped with their unique (up to scaling) symmetric Riemannian metrics.

Proof.

Let
N
be a factor in the de Rham decomposition of a 2-connected closed manifold with non-zero Euler characteristic and non-negative curvature operator. We obtain by
[
Wil07
, Theorem 1.13]
that
N
is isometric to one of the following Riemannian manifolds:

•

Sphere
S^{2n}
,
n\geq 2
, with non-negative curvature operator.

•

Compact irreducible 2-connected symmetric space with non-zero Euler characteristic.

Any irreducible symmetric space of compact type is listed in
[
Hel01
, Table V on page 518]
(type I) or isometric to a compact connected simple Lie group equipped with a two-sided invariant metric
(type II). Since any Lie group admits a nowhere vanishing vector field, its Euler characteristic vanishes. It remains to rule out all symmetric spaces in
[
Hel01
, Table V on page 518]
, except the quaternionic Grassmannian and the Cayley projective plane by checking whether they are 2-connected and have non-zero Euler characteristic. Therefore, we use the results about the exceptional Lie groups
E6
,
E7
,
E8
,
F4
and
G2
given in
[
Yok09
]
and the fact that for any symmetric space
G/\penalty K
of compact type, with
G
a connected simple Lie group and
K
a compact connected subgroup, the following holds:

(1)

The Euler characteristic of
G/\penalty K
is non-zero if and only if
\rk{G}=\rk{K}
.

(2)

The symmetric space
G/\penalty K
is 2-connected if and only if the inclusion
\iota\colon K\xhookrightarrow{}G
induces an isomorphism
\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{1}\left(K\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(G\right)
.

The second part holds by
\pi_{2}\left(G\right)=0
and the long exact sequence of homotopy groups

0\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{2}\left(G/\penalty K\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{1}\left(K\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{i_{*}}}\pi_{1}\left(G\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{1}\left(G/\penalty K\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}0.\qed

(5.61)
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
N
be a product of Riemannian manifolds of the following form:

•

Sphere
S^{2n}
,
n\geq 2
, equipped with a metric satisfying
\mathcal{R}_{S^{n}}\geq 0
and
\scal_{S^{n}}>2\Ric_{S^{n}}>0
.

•

Quaternionic Grassmannian

•

Cayley projective plane

Let
f\colon M\to N
be a fiber bundle, where the total space
M
is a closed connected spin manifold and the typical fiber
F
satisfies

\chi\left(N\right)\cdot\alpha\left(F\right)\neq 0\in\KO_{\dim\left(F\right)}\left(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}\left(F\right)\right).

(5.62)

Then
f
is a Riemannian submersion with
\scal_{M}=\scal_{N}\mathop{\circ}\nolimits f
if the map
f
is area non-increasing and
\scal_{M}\geq\scal_{N}\mathop{\circ}\nolimits f
holds. Equation (
5.62
) holds if the typical fiber
F
is area-enlargeable, or
F
is aspherical and the strong Novikov conjecture holds for its fundamental group.

Proof.

The manifold
N
is 2-connected, has non-zero Euler characteristic and has non-negative curvature operator by
5.12
. Furthermore, it satisfies
\scal_{S^{n}}>2\Ric_{S^{n}}>0
by the discussion about locally symmetric spaces of compact type in
[
GS02
, end of section 1c]
. Let
f\colon M\to N
be a fiber bundle with typical fiber
F
as in the theorem. Since
N
is 2-connected, it is spin, hence
f
is a spin map. From the long exact homotopy sequence of fiber bundles

\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{2}\left(N\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{1}\left(F\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{\iota_{*}}}\pi_{1}\left(M\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\pi_{1}\left(N\right)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[2em]{}}\dots

(5.63)

together with the 2-connectedness of
N
, we obtain that
\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{1}\left(F\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(M\right)
is an isomorphism. The condition (
(T2)
) in
5.6
is fulfilled, hence the rigidity statement holds. The second part of the corollary follows from the same considerations as in
5.8
about area-enlargeable and aspherical manifolds.

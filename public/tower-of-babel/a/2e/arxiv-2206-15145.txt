Thick brane in Rastall gravity
Yi Zhong, Ke Yang, and Yu-Xiao Liu

Abstract
In this work, thick branes in Rastall gravity are investigated. Three types of maximally symmetric thick brane models are constructed and the linear tensor perturbation is analyzed. In the flat brane model, the tensor modes of the perturbation are either unstable or nonlocalizable for a nonvanishing Rastall parameter. In the de Sitter brane model, only the ground state of the tensor mode is localized. In the anti-de Sitter brane model, the number of the bound tensor states is infinity. For both the de Sitter and anti-de Sitter brane models, the condition of stability for the Rastall parameter is obtained.

Introduction

It has been an attractive idea for the last two decades that our spacetime may have extra dimensions and the observable universe could be a brane in a higher-dimensional spacetime. In the braneworld scenario, it is possible to explain the hierarchy between the Planck scale and the electroweak scale

It was found that the branes may have inner structure in some theories such as

In general relativity and many modified gravities, matter fields are minimally coupled to gravity, and the energy-momentum tensor conserves as a result of the Noether theorem. However, the energy-momentum conservation law is violated in the particle creation process in cosmology. Accordingly, it is reasonable to relax the conservation condition of the energy-momentum tensor.
P. Rastall proposed such kind of modified gravity
PhysRevD.6.3357
. In this theory, the covariant divergence of the energy-momentum tensor is assumed to be proportional to the scalar curvature, i.e.
\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\lambda\nabla_{\nu}R
. Therefore, the energy-momentum conservation law is recovered in a flat spacetime.
This theory has attracted much attention recently, and cosmology and compact stars have been investigated in Rastall gravity

Tang:2019dsk

Li:2019jkv

It was shown that Rastall gravity and its generalization is equivalent to the
f(R,T)
gravity with the Lagrangian given by
f(R,T)=R+\alpha T

Shabani:2020wja

R
is the Ricci scalar and
T

f(R,T)

Gu:2016nyo

In this work, we will consider the thick branes in Rastall gravity and investigate the localization of gravity and the stability against tensor perturbation. Three types of
maximally symmetric thick branes will be considered. We will show that the thick braneworlds in Rastall gravity are quite different from that in
f(R,T)
gravity in the context of tensor perturbation.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec. 2, we solve the thick brane generated by a scalar field in Rastall gravity.
In Sec. 3, the linear perturbation of the background metric is analyzed, and its Kaluza-Klein modes are solved when
|\lambda|\ll 1
.
Finally, we give out the conclusion and discussion.

2
The Model

We start with a scalar filed
\phi
of which the energy-momentum tensor
T_{MN}=\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi-g_{MN}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial^{M}\phi\partial_{M}\phi+V(\phi)\right)
is not conserved, and the covariant divergence is related to the scalar curvature
R
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{M}T_{MN}=\lambda\nabla_{N}R,

(1)

where the constant
\lambda
is a measure of the tendency of the nonminimal coupling of the matter field and gravity. It is reasonable to assume that
|\lambda|\ll 1
. Throughout this paper, we take the geometrized units in which the gravitational constant
\kappa^{2}=1
, and the indices
M,N\cdots=0,1,2,3,5
denote the coordinates in the bulk, and
\mu,\nu\cdots=0,1,2,3
denote the ones on the brane.
The field equation of the generalized Rastall gravity in
5
-dimensional spacetime is

\displaystyle G_{MN}+\lambda Rg_{MN}=T_{MN},

(2)

or

\displaystyle R_{MN}=T_{MN}+\alpha Tg_{MN},

(3)

where
\alpha=\frac{1-2\lambda}{10\lambda-3}
. Note that
\lambda\neq\frac{3}{10}
as the trace of Eq. (
2
) gives
(5\lambda-\frac{3}{2})R=T
.
The metric is assumed as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=a^{2}(z)(\gamma_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}+dz^{2}),

(4)

where
a(z)=\text{e}^{A(z)}
is the warp factor, and
\gamma_{\mu\nu}
is the metric of four-dimensional maximally symmetric spacetime, i.e

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=\begin{cases}\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu},&\mbox{flat brane},\\
-dt^{2}+\text{e}^{2kt}(dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}),&\mbox{de Sitter brane},\\
\text{e}^{2kx_{3}}(-dt^{2}+dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})+dx_{3}^{2},&\mbox{anti-de Sitter brane}.\end{cases}

(5)

Since the brane is maximally symmetric, the scalar field can be assumed as
\phi=\phi(z)
. With the metric assumption of (
4
), Eqs. (
2
) and (
1
) read

\displaystyle 3A^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime 2}-3A^{\prime\prime}-3\epsilon k^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(6)

\displaystyle 2\text{e}^{2A}V(\phi)+3(3+8\lambda)(A^{\prime 2}-\epsilon k^{2})+(3+16\lambda)A^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(7)

\displaystyle-24\lambda A^{\prime 3}+8\lambda A^{\prime\prime\prime}+4A^{\prime}\left(2\lambda A^{\prime\prime}+\phi^{\prime 2}+6\epsilon k^{2}\right)-\text{e}^{2A}\phi^{\prime}V^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(8)

where the primes denote the derivatives with respect to
z
, and the constant
\epsilon=0,1,-1
for the flat, de Sitter and anti-de Sitter branes, respectively.
Note that there are only two independent equations in Eqs. (
6
)-(
8
), we can construct the brane model by specifying the parameter
\lambda
and assuming the warp factor
a(z)
. The potential is solved as

\displaystyle V(\phi)=-\frac{1}{2}\text{e}^{-2A}\left[3(3+8\lambda)(A^{\prime 2}-\epsilon k^{2})+(3+16\lambda)A^{\prime\prime}\right].

(9)

3
Tensor perturbation

In this section, we investigate the tensor perturbation of the Rastall thick brane to analyze the stability and the localization of gravity.
The perturbed metric of the background is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{ds}^{2}=a^{2}(z)\left[(\gamma_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}(x^{\mu},z))dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}+dz^{2}\right],

(19)

where the tensor mode
h_{\mu\nu}
satisfies the transverse and traceless condition
\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\nu}=0
and
\eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}=0
. The corresponding perturbation of the Ricci tensor
\delta R_{MN}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta R_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Box^{(4)}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime}h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-(3A^{\prime 2}+A^{\prime\prime}-4\epsilon k^{2})h_{\mu\nu},

(20)

where
\Box^{(4)}\equiv\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}
denotes the four-dimensional d’Alember operator, and the primes denote the derivatives with respect to the extra dimension coordinate
z
.
Substituting Eq. (
20
) into the perturbation of Eq. (
2
), we can obtain the equation of the tensor perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Box^{(4)}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime}h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}+\left[\epsilon k^{2}+8\lambda(3\epsilon k^{2}-3A^{\prime 2}-2A^{\prime\prime})\right]h_{\mu\nu}=0.

(21)

To eliminate the first-order term in Eq. (
21
), we define
h_{\mu\nu}=a^{-\frac{3}{2}}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}
and the above equation turns to

\displaystyle-\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}+V_{t}(z)\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=(\Box^{(4)}-48\epsilon\lambda k^{2}-2\epsilon k^{2})\bar{h}_{\mu\nu},

(22)

where

\displaystyle V_{t}(z)=\Big(1-\frac{64}{3}\lambda\Big)\Big(\frac{9}{4}A^{\prime 2}+\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime\prime}\Big).

(23)

Applying the Kaluza-Klein decomposition
\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(x^{\alpha})t(z)
, where the four-dimensional part
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(x^{\alpha})
satisfies
(\Box^{(4)}-48\epsilon\lambda k^{2}-2\epsilon k^{2})\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(x^{\alpha})=\tilde{m}^{2}t\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(x^{\alpha})
, we obtain the Schrodinger-like equation of the tensor mode

\displaystyle-t^{\prime\prime}(z)+V_{t}(z)t(z)=\tilde{m}^{2}t(z),

(24)

with the effective potential given by Eq. (
23
).
This Schrodinger-like equation of the tensor mode is similar to the one of the thick brane in general relativity, except that the effective potential is modified by a factor
(1-\frac{64}{3}\lambda)
. When
\lambda=0
, the theory reduces to general relativity and Eq. (
24
) can be factorized as

\displaystyle\Big(\!-\partial_{z}+\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime}\Big)\Big(\partial_{z}+\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime}\Big)t(z)=m^{2}t(z),

(25)

of which the solution of the localized mode is

\displaystyle t_{0}(z)=\text{e}^{\frac{3}{2}A},

(26)

and the corresponding mass square is
m_{0}^{2}=0
.

In the Rastall gravity, Eq. (
24
) can not be factorized, which makes it difficult to obtain the solutions of localized modes. Nevertheless, it is possible to deduce the feature of the tensor mode by making use of the knowledge in quantum mechanism.
Comparing Eq. (
24
) with a stationary Schrodinger equation, one can obtain the following Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}=H-\frac{64}{3}\lambda V

(27)

where
H
and
V
are given by

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}+V,

(28)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{9}{4}A^{\prime 2}+\frac{3}{2}A^{\prime\prime}.

(29)

The Hellmann-Feynman theorem states

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{m}_{n}^{2}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle t_{n}|\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial\lambda}|t_{n}\rangle}{\langle t_{n}|t_{n}\rangle}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{64}{3}\frac{\langle t_{n}|V|t_{n}\rangle}{\langle t_{n}|t_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{64}{3}\overline{V}_{n}

for an arbitrary bound state
|t_{n}\rangle
. Since the Rastall parameter satisfies
|\lambda|<<1
, the mass spectrum of the tensor modes is similar with the ones in the general relativity, except for a shift, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{n}^{2}\approx m_{n}^{2}-\frac{64}{3}\lambda\overline{V_{n}}.

(31)

Note that the prerequisite for the above conclusion is that the bound states of Eq. (
24
) exist.

Next, we analyze the tensor perturbation further in the cases of flat, de Sitter and anti-de Sitter branes, respectively.

Case I: Flat Brane

In the flat brane model, the warp factor is given by Eq. (
10

V_{t}(z)

\displaystyle V_{t}(z)=-\frac{\beta^{2}(64\lambda-3)\left(5\beta^{2}z^{2}-2\right)}{4\left(\beta^{2}z^{2}+1\right)^{2}},

which is plotted in Fig.

\lambda=0

24

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{0}^{2}\approx-\frac{64}{3}\lambda\overline{V_{0}},

To prevent the ghost instability, the mass square must satisfy

On the other hand, it can be seen that

, which is not possible for nonvanishing
\lambda
. In conclusion, in the flat brane of Rastall gravity, the constraints from the stability and localization yield
\lambda=0

4
Conclusions and Discussions

In this paper, we investigated the thick brane model in the Rastall gravity.
In this theory, the thick brane is generated by a nonconserved scalar field, of which the covariant divergence is proportional to the scalar curvature.
The flat, de Sitter, and anti-de Sitter brane solutions were obtained and the tensor perturbation of the metric was analyzed for each model. We showed that the tensor perturbation in this theory is quite different from that in the general relativity and
f(R,T)
gravity
Gu:2016nyo
, though the Rastall gravity is equivalent to the
f(R,T)
gravity with the Lagrangian given by
f(R,T)=R+\alpha T

Shabani:2020wja
.
The new features of the tensor perturbation in the Rastall thick brane models come from that the effective potential of the Schrodinger-like equation is multiplied by the factor
1-\frac{64}{3}\lambda
.

In the flat brane model, for the case
\lambda\neq 0
, it was proved that the tensor mode of the perturbation is either unstable or nonlocalizable. In the de Sitter brane model, only the ground state of the tensor mode is localized, and the mass and solution of the ground state were obtained by the perturbation method. In the anti-de Sitter brane model, the number of the bound state is infinity. For both the de Sitter and anti-de Sitter brane models, the condition of stability for the parameter
\lambda
was obtained.

Subordination principle, Wright functions and large-time behaviour for the discrete in time fractional diffusion equation
Luciano Abadias †, Edgardo Alvarez ‡ and Stiven Díaz ∗

Abstract
The main goal in this paper is to study asymptotic behaviour in L p ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}) for the solutions of the fractional version of the discrete in time N N -dimensional diffusion equation, which involves the Caputo fractional h h -difference operator. The techniques to prove the results are based in new subordination formulas involving the discrete in time Gaussian kernel, and which are defined via an analogue in discrete time setting of the scaled Wright functions. Moreover, we get an equivalent representation of that subordination formula by Fox H-functions.

Preliminaries

2.1
Continuous fractional calculus.

In this part, we recall some concepts and basic results about fractional calculus in continuous time.
Let
0<\alpha<1
and
f
be a locally integrable function. The Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative of
f
of order
\alpha
is given by

{}_{R}D^{\alpha}_{t}f(t):=\frac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{(t-s)^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}f(s)\,ds,\ t\geq 0.

The Caputo fractional
derivative of order
\alpha
of a function
f
is defined by

{}_{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}f(t)

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}f^{\prime}(s)\,ds,\ t\geq 0,

where
f^{\prime}
is the first order distributional derivative of
f(\cdot)
, for example if we assume that
f(\cdot)
has locally integrable distributional derivative up to order one. Then, when
\alpha=1
we obtain
{}_{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}:=\dfrac{d}{dt}
. For more details, see for example
[
33
,
34
]
.

The Mittag-Leffler functions are given by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\beta}(z):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)},\qquad\alpha,\,\beta>0,\,z\in\mathbb{{C}}.

We write
E_{\alpha}(z):=E_{\alpha,1}(z).
They are solutions of the fractional differential problems

{}_{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(\omega t^{\alpha})=\omega E_{\alpha}(\omega t^{\alpha}),

and

{}_{R}D_{t}^{\alpha}\biggl(t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(\omega t^{\alpha})\biggr)=\omega t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(\omega t^{\alpha}),

for
0<\alpha<1,
under certain initial conditions. Their Laplace transform is

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\beta-1}E_{\alpha,\beta}(\omega t^{\alpha})\,dt=\frac{\lambda^{\alpha-\beta}}{\lambda^{\alpha}-\omega},\qquad\mbox{Re}(\lambda)>\omega^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\,\omega>0.

For more details about the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha,\beta}
see
[
15
, Chapter 18]
.

Recall the definition of the Wright type function (see
[
33
]
)

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{n}}{n!\Gamma(\lambda n+\mu)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{H_{a}}\sigma^{-\mu}e^{\sigma+z\sigma^{-\lambda}}d\sigma,\quad\lambda>-1,\,\mu\geq 0,\,z\in\mathbb{C},

where
H_{a}
denotes the Hankel path defined as a contour which starts and ends
at
-\infty
and encircles the origin once counterclockwise.

For
0<\alpha<1
and
\beta\geq 0,
the scaled Wright function in two variables
\psi_{\alpha,\beta}
(and which was introduced by Abadias and Miana in
[
5
]
) is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha,\beta}(t,s):=t^{\beta-1}W_{-\alpha,\beta}(-st^{-\alpha}),\qquad t>0,\ s\in\mathbb{{C}}.

(2.1)

Note that using the change of variable
z=\frac{\sigma}{t},
we get the integral representation

\psi_{\alpha,\beta}(t,s)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}z^{-\beta}e^{tz-sz^{\alpha}}\,dz,\qquad t,s>0.

Many properties about such functions that we will use along the paper appear in
[
5
]
.

Next, we recall the definition of Fox H-funtions. Let
m,n,p,q\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0}
such that
0\leq m\leq q
,
0\leq n\leq p
. Let
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{{C}}
and
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{{R}}_{+}.
The Fox H-function is defined via a Mellin-Barnes type integral,

\displaystyle H^{mn}_{pq}(z)

\displaystyle:=H^{mn}_{p,q}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}z\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{i},\alpha_{i})_{1,p}\\
(b_{j},\beta_{j})_{1,q}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{mn}_{pq}(s)z^{-s}\ ds,

where

\displaystyle(a_{i},\alpha_{i})_{1,p}:=(a_{1},\alpha_{1}),

\displaystyle\cdots,(a_{p},\alpha_{p}),

\displaystyle(b_{j},\beta_{j})_{1,q}:=(b_{1},\beta_{1}),

\displaystyle\cdots,(b_{q},\beta_{q}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{mn}_{pq}(s)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}+\beta_{j}s)\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{i}-\alpha_{i}s)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{i}+\alpha_{i}s)\displaystyle\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}-\beta_{j}s)},

and
\gamma
is the infinite contour in the complex plane which separates the poles

\displaystyle b_{jl}=\frac{-b_{j}-l}{\beta_{j}}\ (j=1,\cdots,m;\ l\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0})

of the Gamma function
\Gamma(b_{j}+\beta_{j}s)
to the left of
\gamma
and the poles

\displaystyle a_{ik}=\frac{1-a_{i}+k}{\alpha_{i}}\ (i=1,\cdots,n;\ k\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0})

to the right of
\gamma
. For more details of this type of functions, see
[
27
]
.

Finally, we recall that the Gaussian kernel
G_{t}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},\qquad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

(2.2)

Let

\widehat{u}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-N/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx

and

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(u)(\xi):=\mathcal{F}(u)(-\xi)

denote the Fourier and inverse Fourier transform of

, respectively.

The function

has the following properties (see for example

[

]

Proposition 2.1
.

The Gaussian kernel satisfies:

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)>0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}G_{t}(x)\,dx=1
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(G_{t})(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}},

\xi\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}G_{t}(x)\,dx=2Nt.

3
Discrete fractional calculus

In this section we recall the definition of Cesàro numbers and some useful properties of them. Also, we introduce the discrete time setting where we will work, and the corresponding associated fractional calculus.

For an arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathbb{{C}},
we denote by
k^{\alpha}(n)
the Cesàro numbers which are the Fourier coefficients of the holomorphic function on the disc
(1-z)^{-\alpha},
that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\alpha}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}(n)z^{n}.

(3.1)

It is known that the expression of the Cesàro numbers is given by

k^{\alpha}(n):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\dfrac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots\cdots(\alpha+n-1)}{n!},&\qquad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad 1,&\qquad n=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

Note that
k^{0}(n):=\delta_{0}(n)
is the Kronecker delta. Sometimes, to make more easily computations with the Cesàro sequence
k^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{{C}}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,...\}
, we use an equivalent expression of (
3.2
), namely

k^{\alpha}(n)=\dfrac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(n+1)},

(3.3)

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function.

We recall the following properties of
k^{\alpha}
which appear for example in
[
22
,
31
,
39
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following properties hold:

1.

For
\alpha>0
,
k^{\alpha}(n)>0
,
n\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0}
.

2.

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{{C}}
, we have the semigroup property

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^{\alpha}(n-j)k^{\beta}(j)=k^{\alpha+\beta}(n).

(3.4)

3.

For
\alpha>0,

\displaystyle k^{\alpha}(n)=\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

(3.5)

4.

For
\alpha>0

\displaystyle k^{\alpha}(n+1)=\dfrac{\alpha+n}{n+1}k^{\alpha}(n).

(3.6)

Let
h>0
and
f
be a sequence defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}:=\{0,h,2h,\ldots\},
the backward difference of the sequence
f
is defined by

\delta_{\text{left}}f(nh):=\dfrac{f(nh)-f((n-1)h)}{h},

n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Taking into account the previous definition, we get the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\rho_{\alpha}(nh):=h^{\alpha}k^{\alpha}(n)
. Then,

\delta_{\text{left}}\rho_{\alpha}(nh)=h^{\alpha-1}k^{\alpha-1}(n),\ n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Proof.

By (
3.6
) and the property of Gamma function
z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1)
the result follows.
∎

The definitions of the fractional sum and fractional difference operators (in the sense of Riemann-Liouville and Caputo) with the sequence
k^{\alpha}
were initially proposed by C. Lizama in
[
30
]
. Recently, R. Ponce in
[
38
]
defines a generalization. We make a slightly modification (since our index starts at one) to this definition.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f
be a sequence defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}
. For
\alpha\geq 0
, the
\alpha
-th fractional sum of
f
is defined by means of the formula

\delta^{-\alpha}f(nh):=h^{\alpha}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}k^{\alpha}(n-j)f(jh),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Note that, for
\alpha=0
,
\delta^{-\alpha}f(nh)=f(nh).

As a direct consequence of the previous definition, the operator
\delta^{-\alpha}
satisfies the semigroup property:

\displaystyle\delta^{-\alpha}\delta^{-\beta}f(nh)=\delta^{-(\alpha+\beta)}f(nh),\ n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
f
be a sequence defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}
. The
\alpha
-th fractional
h
-difference in the sense of Riemann-Liouville of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle{{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh):=\delta_{\text{left}}\delta^{-(1-\alpha)}f(nh),\qquad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

(3.7)

Proposition 3.5
.

For
0<\alpha<1
the relation

\delta^{-\alpha}{{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)=f(nh),\ n\in\mathbb{{N}},

holds.

Proof.

First of all, we adopt the notation:
\sum^{0}_{j=1}f(jh)=0
. Then, by (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle\delta^{-\alpha}{{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)

\displaystyle=h^{\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j){{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}f(jh)

\displaystyle=h^{\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\delta_{\text{left}}\delta^{-(1-\alpha)}f(jh)

\displaystyle=h^{\alpha-1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\delta^{-(1-\alpha)}f(jh)-h^{\alpha-1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\delta^{-(1-\alpha)}f((j-1)h)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\sum^{j}_{i=1}k^{1-\alpha}(j-i)f(ih)-\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\sum^{j-1}_{i=1}k^{1-\alpha}(j-1-i)f(ih)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\sum^{j}_{i=1}k^{1-\alpha}(j-i)f(ih)-\sum^{n}_{j=2}k^{\alpha}(n-j)\sum^{j-1}_{i=1}k^{1-\alpha}(j-1-i)f(ih)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{\alpha}(n-1-j)\sum^{j}_{i=0}k^{1-\alpha}(j-i)f((i+1)h)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}k^{\alpha}(n-2-j)\sum^{j}_{i=0}k^{1-\alpha}(j-i)f((i+1)h)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}f((i+1)h)-\sum^{n-2}_{i=0}f((i+1)h)=f(nh).

Consequently, we have
\delta^{-\alpha}{{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)=f(nh)
for all
n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
f
be a sequence defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}
. The Caputo fractional difference of order
\alpha
is defined by

{{}_{C}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh):=\delta^{-(1-\alpha)}\delta_{\text{left}}f(nh),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

(3.8)

Note that the previous definition gives
{{}_{C}}\delta^{\alpha}=\delta_{\text{left}}
for
\alpha=1.

The next result shows the relation between Caputo and Riemann fractional difference.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Then the following
identity holds

{{}_{C}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)={{}_{RL}}\delta^{\alpha}(f(nh)-f(0)),\qquad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Proof.

By (
3.8
) and (
3.7
),

\displaystyle{{}_{C}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{1-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)\delta_{\text{left}}f(jh)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)f(jh)-h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)f((j-1)h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)f(jh)-h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-1-j)f(jh)-h^{-\alpha}k^{1-\alpha}(n)f(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)f(jh)-h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-1-j)f(jh))

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-h^{-\alpha}f(0)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}k^{1-\alpha}(n-1-j)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-j)(f(jh)-f(0))-h^{-\alpha}\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{1-\alpha}(n-1-j)(f(jh)-f(0)).

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
f
be a sequence defined on
\mathbb{{N}}^{h}_{0}
. Then the following identity holds

\displaystyle\delta^{-\alpha}{{}_{C}}\delta^{\alpha}f(nh)=f(nh)-f(0).

Proof.

The result is immediate by Propositions
3.5
and
3.7
.
∎

The Proposition
3.7
allows to establish the following property between the fractional difference operators.

Proposition 3.9
.

For all
0<\alpha<1
and
0\leq\beta
the relation holds

\displaystyle\delta^{-(\beta+1)}{{}_{RL}\delta}^{1-\alpha}f(nh))=\delta^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(nh),\qquad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{-(\beta+1)}{{}_{RL}\delta}^{1-\alpha}f(nh))

\displaystyle=h^{\beta+1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\beta+1}(n-j){{}_{RL}\delta}^{1-\alpha}f(jh)

\displaystyle=h^{\beta+1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\beta+1}(n-j){{}_{C}\delta}^{1-\alpha}f(jh)+h^{\beta+\alpha}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\beta+1}(n-j)k^{\alpha}(j-1)f(0)

\displaystyle=h^{\beta+1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\beta+1}(n-j){{}_{C}\delta}^{1-\alpha}f(jh)+h^{\beta+\alpha}\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{\beta+1}(n-1-j)k^{\alpha}(j)f(0)

\displaystyle=\delta^{-(\beta+\alpha)}\delta^{-1}\delta_{\text{left}}f(nh)+h^{\alpha+\beta}\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}k^{\alpha+\beta}(n-1-j)f(0)

\displaystyle=\delta^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(nh)-\delta^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(0)+h^{\alpha+\beta}\sum^{n}_{j=1}k^{\alpha+\beta}(n-j)f(0)

\displaystyle=\delta^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(nh).

∎

4
Special functions and subordination formulae

In this section we introduce a discrete version of the Mittag-Leffler and scaled Wright functions, which generalize the ones defined in
[
7
]
. Also, we present some interesting properties which will be useful along the paper.

Let
\alpha,\beta,h>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{{C}}
. The Mittag-Leffler sequences are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda,n):=\frac{h^{\beta}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha j+\beta+n-1)}{\Gamma(\alpha j+\beta)}(h^{\alpha}\lambda)^{j},\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\quad|\lambda|<\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}.

(4.1)

The convergence of previous series can be justified by (
3.5
). Using the Cesàro numbers (
3.3
), one can rewrite (
4.1
) as

\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda,n)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h^{\alpha j+\beta}k^{\alpha j+\beta}(n-1)\lambda^{j},\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\quad|\lambda|<\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}.

Particularly, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{h}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(n-1,j-1)\frac{1}{h^{j}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/h}\frac{t^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}G_{t}(x)\ dt

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/h}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{h}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(n-1,j-1)\frac{1}{h^{j}}\frac{t^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}G_{t}(x)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s/h}\frac{s^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\psi_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(s,t)G_{t}(x)\ ds\ dt.

The result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
0<h
and
0<\alpha<1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n)\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}H^{30}_{13}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{|x|^{2}}{4h^{\alpha}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,\alpha)\\
(n,\alpha),(\frac{N}{2},1),(1,1)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right],

where
H^{03}_{31}
denotes the Fox H-function.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
5
, Theorem 15-
(ii)
.]
and
[
26
, Theorem 2.12]
, we have the following subordination formula

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}H^{02}_{21}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{4t^{\alpha}}{|x|^{2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1-\frac{N}{2},1),(0,1)\\
(0,\alpha)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(t,s)G_{s}(x)\ ds.

Now,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/h}t^{n-1}H^{12}_{32}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{4t^{\alpha}}{|x|^{2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1-\frac{N}{2},1),\ (0,1),\ (0,1)\\
(0,1),\ (0,\alpha)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n)\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}H^{13}_{42}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{4h^{\alpha}}{|x|^{2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1-n,\alpha),\ (1-\frac{N}{2},1),\ (0,1),\ (0,1)\\
(0,1),\ (0,\alpha)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n)\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}H^{03}_{31}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{4h^{\alpha}}{|x|^{2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1-n,\alpha),(1-\frac{N}{2},1),(0,1)\\
(0,\alpha)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n)\pi^{N/2}|x|^{N}}H^{30}_{13}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{|x|^{2}}{4h^{\alpha}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,\alpha)\\
(n,\alpha),(\frac{N}{2},1),(1,1)\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right],

where we have used Corollary 2.3.1, Proposition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3 of

]
.
∎

The following proposition states some basic properties of the fundamental solution.

Proposition 5.4
.

The function
\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}
satisfies:

1.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}(x)>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\ 0<h\leq 1.

2.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}(x)\,dx=1
.

3.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{G}}_{n,h}^{\alpha})(\xi)=\frac{1}{h}\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\alpha,1}(-|\xi|^{2},n),

\xi\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

4.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}(x)\,dx=\Gamma(3)Nh^{\alpha}k^{\alpha+1}(n-1).

Proof.

(i)
Follows from
(vi)
of Proposition
4.3
and
(i)
of the Proposition
2.2
.

(ii)
was showed in the proof of Theorem
5.1
(see (
5.4
)).
Next, let us prove
(iii)
. Since
\mathcal{F}({G}_{t})(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}
, for
\xi\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
by Proposition
2.1
part
(iii)
, it follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3]
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(s,t)e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\,dt=E_{\alpha,1}(-|\xi|^{2}s^{\alpha}).

Equation (
4.1
) implies that

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h})(\xi)=\frac{1}{h^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s/h}\frac{s^{n-1}}{\Gamma(n)}E_{\alpha,1}(-|\xi|^{2}s^{\alpha})\,ds=\frac{1}{h}\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\alpha}(-|\xi|^{2},n).

Finally, by Fubini’s Theorem, Proposition
2.1
and
[
5
, Theorem 3]
, we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}(x)\,dx=

\displaystyle 2N\frac{1}{h^{n}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s/h}\frac{s^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\psi_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(s,t)t\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2N\Gamma(2)\frac{1}{h^{n}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s/h}\frac{s^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}g_{\alpha+1}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(3)Nh^{\alpha}k^{\alpha+1}(n-1).

Thus, we get item
(iv)
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We recall that the total mass and first moment of the function

w(nh,x)=(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast f)(x)

are conservative (see
[
2
, Remark 2.6]
). Then, we have that the total mass of solution of (
5.1
), given by

u(nh,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{h}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(n-1,j-1)(\mathcal{G}_{j,h}\ast f)(x)

is conservative. Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}u(nh,x)\,dx=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{h}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(n-1,j-1)\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}(\mathcal{G}_{j,h}\ast f)(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}f(x)\,dx,

where in the last equality we have used (
4.3
).
The first moment is also conservative:

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}x\,u(nh,x)dx=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{h}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}(n-1,j-1)\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}x\,(\mathcal{G}_{j,h}\ast f)(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}xf(x)\,dx,

as long as
(1+|x|)f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
.
However, in the same way that
w
, the second moment of
u
is not conserved in time.

6
Asymptotic decay and large-time behavior of solutions for the fractional diffusion equation in discrete time

Now we will present the asymptotic decay of the solution of (
5.1
) (which is given by (
5.3
)) in
L^{p}
spaces and the corresponding large-time behaviour.

6.1
Asymptotic decay

In this part we show the following estimates of the fundamental solution
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}
-spaces. Finally we also state
L^{p}
-estimates for
\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha},
which are useful for study the large time behaviour of solutions of (
5.1
) in Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1.
Then

\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)}},\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},

for
p\in[1,\infty]
if
N=1,
for
p\in[1,\infty)
if
N=2,
and for
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-2})
if
N>2.

Proof.

It is well known (see
[
24
, p.334 (3.326)]
) that there exists
C_{p}
(independent of
t
) such that
||G_{t}||_{p}=C_{p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}}.
Then for
n
large enough and the values of
p
given in the hypothesis, by (
5.5
) and
[
5
, Theorem 3 (vi)]
one gets

\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-t}{h}}t^{n-\alpha\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})-1}\,dt=C_{p}\frac{\Gamma(n-\alpha\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p}))}{h^{\alpha\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\Gamma(n)}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{(nh)^{\alpha\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}},

where we have applied the asymptotic behaviour of the Gamma function (see
[
16
]
). Since the function
\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}
belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
for all
n\in\mathbb{{N}},
then the result is valid for all
n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

∎

Next, let us present a result about the
L^{p}-L^{q}
asymptotic decay for
u.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty.
If
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
, then the solution
u
of (
5.1
) satisfies

(i)

If
q=\infty
then
\|u(nh)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}.

(ii)

If
1\leq q<\infty
and
N>2q
, then for each
p\in[q,\frac{Nq}{N-2q})

\|u(nh)\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\|f\|_{q}.

(6.1)

(iii)

If
1\leq q<\infty
and
N=2q
, then for each
p\in[q,\infty)
the estimate (
6.1
) holds.

(iv)

If
1\leq q<\infty
and
N<2q
, then for each
p\in[q,\infty]
the estimate (
6.1
) holds.

Here,
C_{p}
is a constant independent of
h
and
n
.

Proof.

Take
r\geq 1
such that
1+1/p=1/q+1/r,
and applying Young’s inequality we get

\|u(nh)\|_{p}=\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}*f\|_{p}\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}\|_{r}\|f\|_{q}.

Now, we apply Lemma
6.1
to estimate
\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}\|_{r}.
For the case
(i)
, if
q=\infty,
then
p=\infty,r=1,
and therefore since
\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}\|_{1}=1,
the result follows. Note that in the case
(ii)
, if
1\leq q<\infty
and
N>2q,
then the condition
q\leq p<\frac{Nq}{N-2q}
implies
1\leq r<\frac{N}{N-2}.
So, by Lemma
6.1
we get the desired estimates. The cases
(iii)
and
(iv)
follow in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1.
Then

\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}^{\alpha}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)+\frac{\alpha}{2}}},\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},

for
p\in[1,\infty)
if
N=1,
and for
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-1})
if
N>1.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma
6.1
by use of
\|\nabla G_{t}\|_{p}=C_{p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})+1/2}}
(see
[
24
, p.334 (3.326)]
).
∎

6.2
Large-time behaviour of solutions

In this part we study the asymptotic behaviour of solution
u
of problem given by (
5.1
).
Set

M:=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}f(x)\,dx.

Before to show the main result of this section, we need the following decomposition lemma (see
[
14
]
).

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x||f(x)|dx<\infty.
Then there exists
F\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

f=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)dx\right)\delta_{0}+\mbox{div}\,F

in the distributional sense and

\|F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x||f(x)|dx.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
u
be the solution of (
5.1
).

(i)

Then

(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|u(nh)-M\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

for
p\in[1,\infty)
if
N=1,
and for
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-1})
if
N>1,

(ii)

Suppose in addition that
|x|f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}),
then

(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|u(nh)-M\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\lesssim(nh)^{-\alpha/2},

for
p\in[1,\infty)
if
N=1,
and for
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-1})
if
N>1.

Proof.

First we prove assertion
(ii)
. Since that
f,|x|f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),
by decomposition Lemma
6.4
there exists
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle u(nh,x)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\ast(M\delta_{0}+\mbox{div}\,\psi(\cdot)))(x)

\displaystyle=M_{c}\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}(x)+(\nabla\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\ast\psi)(x),

in the distributional sense, and

\|\psi\|_{1}\leq C\||x|f(x)\|_{1}<\infty.

Lemma
6.3
implies that

\|u(nh)-M\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\|xf(x)\|_{1}\leq C_{p,f}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)+\frac{\alpha}{2}}},

(6.2)

Hence the assertion
(ii)
is proved.

To prove
(i)
, we choose a sequence
(\eta_{j})\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{j}(x)\,dx=M
for all
j,
and
\eta_{j}\to f
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For each
j
, by Lemma
6.1
and (
6.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|u(nh)-M\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\ast(f-\eta_{j})\|_{p}+\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\ast\eta_{j}-M_{c}\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\ast\eta_{j}-M_{c}\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)}}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}+C_{p,\eta_{j}}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,(nh)^{\frac{\alpha N}{2}(1-1/p)}\|u(nh)-M\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}.

The assertion follows by letting
j\to\infty
.
∎

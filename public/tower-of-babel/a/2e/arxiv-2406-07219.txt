On the Bures metric, C*-norm, and the quantum metric Thanks: The first author is supported by NSF grant DMS-2316892
Konrad Aguilar, Karina Behera, Tron Omland, Nicole Wu

Abstract
. We prove that the topology on the density space with respect to a unital C*-algebra and a faithful induced by the C*-norm is finer than the Bures metric topology. We also provide an example when this containment is strict. Next, we provide a metric on the density space induced by a quantum metric in the sense of Rieffel and prove that the induced topology is the same as the topology induced by the Bures metric and C*-norm when the C*-algebra is assumed to be finite dimensional. Finally, we provide an example of when the Bures metric and induced quantum metric are not metric equivalent. Thus, we provide a bridge between these aspects of quantum information theory and noncommutative metric geometry.

In Section

2.
Comparison of C*-metric and Bures metric topologies

In this section, we show that the C*-metric topology is finer than the topology induced by the Bures metric. We also show that this containment can be strict by providing an explicit example in the C*-algebra of complex-valued continuous functions on
[0,1]
and the trace given by Lebesgue integration. First, we prove two lemmas that are likely well-known, but we provide their proofs for convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.
Let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}_{+}
that converges in the
C^{*}
-norm to
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{+}
.
Let
r\geqslant 0
be such that for any
n\in{\mathds{N}}
,
\|x_{n}\|_{\mathcal{A}},\|x\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leqslant r
.
If
f:[0,r]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then
(f(x_{n}))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
f(x)
in the
C^{*}
-norm.

Proof.

By Weierstrass approximation theorem, there exists polynomials
(p_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
that converges uniformly to
f
on
[0,r]
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By uniform convergence of
(p_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
to
f
, there exists
N\in{\mathds{N}}
such that, for any
n\geqslant N
,
\|p_{n}-f\|_{[0,r]}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
, and in particular
\|p_{N}-f\|_{[0,r]}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
.

Note that as
p_{N}
is a polynomial, there exits
N^{\prime}\geqslant N
such that, for any
n\geqslant N^{\prime}
,

\|p_{N}(x_{n})-P_{N}(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Let
n\geqslant N^{\prime}
. Then by functional calculus,

\|f(x_{n})-p_{N}(x_{n})\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\|f-p_{N}\|_{\sigma(x_{n})}\leqslant\|f-p_{N}\|_{[0,r]}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

since
\sigma(x_{n})\subseteq[0,r]
.
Similarly,

\|f(x)-p_{N}(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

since
\sigma(x)\subseteq[0,r]
.

By triangle inequality of the norm,

\begin{split}\|f(x_{n})-f(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}&\leqslant\|f(x_{n})-p_{N}(x_{n})\|_{\mathcal{A}}+\|p_{N}(x_{n})-p(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}\\
&\quad\quad+\|p_{N}(x)-f(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}\\
&<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon.\end{split}

Thus, for any arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, we can find
N^{\prime}
such that for any
n\geqslant N^{\prime}
,
\|f(x_{n})-f(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leqslant\varepsilon
. Thus,
(f(x_{n}))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
f(x)
in the
C^{*}
-norm.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.
If
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}
that converges in the
C^{*}
-norm to a positive element
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
, then
(|x_{n}|)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converges to
|x|
.

Proof.

Note that
|x|=\sqrt{x^{*}x}
and
|x_{n}|=\sqrt{x_{n}^{*}x_{n}}
for every
n\in{\mathds{N}}
.
For any
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, let
a_{n}=x_{n}^{*}x_{n}
and
a=x^{*}x
.

Then, since multiplication and the adjoint are continuous in the
C^{*}
-norm, we have that
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
a
in the
C^{*}
-norm.

For any
n\in{\mathds{N}}
,
|x|=\sqrt{a}
and
|x_{n}|=\sqrt{a_{n}}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
and continuity of the square root, we know that
(|x_{n}|)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converges to
|x|
.
∎

We now prove our main theorem that allows us to compare the C*-metric topology and the Bures metric topology.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
\tau
be a faithful tracial state.
Let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence in
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
and let
x\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
.
If
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converges to
x
, then
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
x
with respect to the Bures metric
d_{B}^{\tau}
.

Proof.

If
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
x
in the
C^{*}
-norm,
then, since
g(x)=\sqrt{x}
is continuous,

(\sqrt{x_{n}})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
\sqrt{x}
in the
C^{*}
-norm by Lemma
2.1
.

Since
x
is a positive and fixed,
(\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
\sqrt{x}\cdot\sqrt{x}
in the
C^{*}
-norm. Note that
\sqrt{x}\cdot\sqrt{x}=x
by definition, so
(\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converges to
x
. By Lemma
2.2
, this implies
(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converges to
|x|=x
.

Since
\tau
is
C^{*}
-norm continuous,
(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

C^{*}
-norm converging to
x
implies that
(\tau(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
\tau(x)
. Since
x\in D_{\tau}(A)
, we have
\tau(x)=1
. Hence,
(\tau(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
1
.

Consider

(d_{B}^{\tau}(x_{n},x))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}=\left(\sqrt{1-\tau\left(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|\right)}\right)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}.

Since

\left(\tau(|\sqrt{x_{n}}\cdot\sqrt{x}|)\right)_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

converges to
1
,

(d_{B}^{\tau}(x_{n},x))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}

converges to
\sqrt{1-1}=0
. Hence
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
x
with respect to the Bures metric
d_{B}^{\tau}
.
∎

We thus have.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
\tau
be a faithful tracial state.

It holds that the topology induced by
d_{\mathcal{A}}
is finer than the topology induced by
d_{B}^{\tau}
.

This raises the question of whether these topologies are the same in general. The answer is no, and the rest of this section is devoted to providing an example of when these topologies disagree.

Consider,
C([0,1])
, the C*-algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on
[0,1]
.
The map

\rho:f\in C([0,1])\longmapsto\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\ dx,

where
\int_{0}^{1}\cdot\ dx
is the standard Lebesgue integral,
is a faithful trace on
C([0,1])
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The topology induced by the C*-norm induced metric,
d_{C([0,1])}
, on the density space
D_{\rho}(C([0,1]))
is strictly finer than the topology induced by the Bures metric
d_{B}^{\rho}.

Proof.

The fact that the topology induced by
d_{C([0,1])}
is finer is provided by Corollary
2.4
. Thus, it remains to show that the topologies don’t equal.

To accomplish this, we will find a sequence in
D_{\rho}(C([0,1]))
that converges with respect to
d_{B}^{\rho}
, but does not converge uniformly (i.e. with respect to
d_{C([0,1])}
).

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
.
Consider

(2.1)

f_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}2nx&\text{if }x\in[0,\frac{1}{2n}]\\
1&\text{if }x\in(\frac{1}{2n},1-\frac{1}{2n})\\
2nx-2n+2&\text{if }x\in[1-\frac{1}{2n},1]\end{cases}

defined for all
x\in[0,1]
(see Figure
1
). Note that
f_{n}\in C([0,1]).

Figure 1.
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
of Expression (
2.1
). (Plotted in GeoGebra)

Define
f:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathds{R}}
defined by
f(x)=1
for all
x\in[0,1].
Note
f\in D_{\rho}(C([0,1])).

Next, we check that
f_{n}\in D_{\rho}(C([0,1])
for every
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. First
f_{n}\geqslant 0
by construction. Second, we have

\begin{split}\rho(f_{n})&=\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2n}}2nx\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}1\,dx+\int_{1-\frac{1}{2n}}^{1}(2nx-2n+2)\,dx\\
&=[nx^{2}]_{0}^{\frac{1}{2n}}+[x]_{\frac{1}{2n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}+[nx^{2}-2nx+2x]_{1-\frac{1}{2n}}^{1}\\
&=\frac{1}{4n}+1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{3}{4n}\\
&=1\end{split}

Hence
f_{n}\in D_{\rho}(C([0,1])).

We will now prove that the sequence of functions
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to the function
f
in the Bures metric,
d_{B}^{\rho}
, but fails to converge to
f
uniformly.

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}.
Then

\begin{split}\rho(\sqrt{f_{n}}\sqrt{f})&=\int_{0}^{1}(\sqrt{f_{n}}\sqrt{f})\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}(\sqrt{f_{n}})\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2n}}(\sqrt{2nx}\cdot 1)\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}(\sqrt{1}\cdot 1)\,dx\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{1-\frac{1}{2n}}^{1}(\sqrt{2nx-2n+2}\cdot 1)\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2n}}\sqrt{2nx}\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{2n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}1\,dx+\int_{1-\frac{1}{2n}}^{1}\sqrt{2nx-2n+2}\,dx\\
&=\sqrt{2n}\cdot[\frac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}}]_{0}^{\frac{1}{2n}}+[x]_{\frac{1}{2n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}+\sqrt{2}\cdot[\frac{2}{3n}\cdot(nx-n+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}]_{1-\frac{1}{2n}}^{1}\\
&=\frac{1}{3n}+1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3n}-\frac{1}{3n}\\
&=1-\frac{3-2\sqrt{2}}{3n}.\end{split}

Therefore,

d_{B}^{\rho}(f_{n},f)=\sqrt{1-\rho(\sqrt{f_{n}}\sqrt{f})}=\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{3-2\sqrt{2}}{3n}\right)}=\sqrt{\frac{3-2\sqrt{2}}{3n}}.

Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{B}^{\rho}(f_{n},f)=0.

Therefore, we have shown that
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
f
in the
\rho
-Bures metric.

Finally, suppose by way of contradiction that
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges uniformly to
f
. Then by definition, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
M\in{\mathds{N}}
such that for any
n>M
and any
x\in[0,1]
, we have
|f_{n}(x)-f|<\delta
. Therefore, for some
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
, there exists
M_{0}\in{\mathds{N}}
such that for any
n>M_{0}
and any
x\in[0,1]
, we have
|f_{n}(x)-f|<\delta_{0}
. However, let
x=1
, then
|f_{n}(1)-f(1)|=|2-1|=1>\delta_{0}
, a contradiction.
Hence, our assumption is false.
∎

3.
Topological Equivalence of Bures, C*-, and quantum metrics for finite-dimensional C*-algebras

Although we just saw an example of when the C*-metric topology and Bures metric topology do not agree on the density space, in this section, we will see that if the C*-algebra is assumed to be finite dimensional, then we will show that these topologies agree. Moreover, we will also show that in the finite-dimensional case, we will get that the Bures metric agrees with a metric on the density space induced by the quantum metric, which thus brings these two important metrics together. We first show how one can induce a metric on the density space using a quantum metric in a natural way.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
\tau
be a faithful trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
. For each
a\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
, define

\varphi_{a}^{\tau}(b)=\tau(ab)

for every
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

The next result might be well-known, but we provide a proof here for convenience.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
\tau
be a faithful trace on
{\mathcal{A}}
.
The map

\Phi_{\tau}:a\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})\longmapsto\varphi_{a}^{\tau}\in S({\mathcal{A}})

is well-defined and injective.

Proof.

Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{+}
and so there exists
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
a=b^{*}b
by
[
3
, Lemma I.4.3]
. Let
c\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then since
\tau
is a trace,

\varphi_{a}^{\tau}(c^{*}c)=\tau(b^{*}bc^{*}c)=\tau(cb^{*}bc^{*})=\tau(cb^{*}(cb^{*})^{*})\geqslant 0.

Thus
\varphi
is a positive linear functional, and in particular, we have that
\varphi_{a}^{\tau}(1_{\mathcal{A}})=\|\varphi_{a}^{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
by
[
3
, Lemma I.9.5]
. Hence as
a\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})

\|\varphi_{a}^{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\tau(a1_{\mathcal{A}})=\tau(a)=1.

Thus
\varphi_{a}^{\tau}
is a state, and therefore,
\Phi_{\tau}
is well-defined.

Next, let
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{+}
such that
\Phi_{\tau}(a)=\Phi_{\tau}(a^{\prime})
. Hence
\varphi_{a}^{\tau}((a-a^{\prime})^{*})=\varphi^{\tau}_{a^{\prime}}((a-a^{\prime})^{*})
. Thus
\tau(a(a-a^{\prime})^{*})=\tau(a^{\prime}(a-a^{\prime})^{*})
, and so
\tau((a-a^{\prime})(a-a^{\prime})^{*})=0
. Therefore,
a-a^{\prime}=0
as
\tau
is faithful. Hence
a=a^{\prime}.
Thus
\Phi_{\tau}
is injective.
∎

This allows us to define a new metric on
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}).
Indeed

Definition 3.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},L)
be a compact quantum metric space. For every
x,y\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
, define

d_{L}^{\tau}(x,y)=mk_{L}(\Phi_{\tau}(x),\Phi_{\tau}(y))=mk_{L}(\varphi_{x},\varphi_{y}),

which defines a metric on
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
since
\Phi_{\tau}
is well-defined and injective. We will still call
d_{L}^{\tau}
a quantum metric.

For the remainder of the section
{\mathcal{A}}
will be a finite-dimensional C*-algebra.
We will now prove that the C*-metric topology and quantum metric topology are the same in this case. But first, we establish that
(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{\mathcal{A}})
is a compact metric space.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite-dimensional C*-algebra, then
(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{\mathcal{A}})
is a compact metric space.

Proof.

We only need to show that
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
is closed and bounded with respect to the C*-norm. First, it is closed since
\tau
is continuous with respect to the C*-norm.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite dimensional, we have that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
are equivalent, where
\|a\|_{\tau}=\sqrt{\tau(a^{*}a)}
for every
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Now, let
a\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
and let
\sqrt{a}
denote its unique positive square root. We have by the C*-identity

\displaystyle\sqrt{\|a\|_{\mathcal{A}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\|(\sqrt{a})^{*}\sqrt{a}\|_{\mathcal{A}}}

\displaystyle=\|\sqrt{a}\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha\|\sqrt{a}\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{\tau((\sqrt{a})^{*}\sqrt{a})}=\alpha\sqrt{\tau(a)}=\alpha

since
\tau(a)=1
.
And so
\|a\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leqslant\alpha^{2}
. Hence
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
is bounded, and thus compact by the Heine-Borel theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},L)
be a compact quantum metric space and let
\tau
be a faithful trace. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite dimensional, then
d_{L}^{\tau}
and
d_{\mathcal{A}}
are topologically equivalent.

Proof.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence in
D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
that converges to
a\in D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})
with respect to
d_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then, for each
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{\tau}_{a_{n}}(b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(a_{n}b)=\tau(ab)=\varphi_{a}^{\tau}(b)

since multiplication is continuous and
\tau
is continuous.
Thus,
\Phi_{\tau}
is continuous and since
(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{\mathcal{A}})
is compact by Proposition
3.3
, we have that
\Phi_{\tau}
is a homeomorphism onto its image with respect to the weak* topology on
S({\mathcal{A}})
.

Since convergence in
mk_{L}

is equivalent to weak* convergence by definition, we have that
d_{\mathcal{A}}
and
d^{\tau}_{L}
are topologically equivalent.
∎

Now, we establish that topological equivalence of the C*-metric and Bures metric.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra and let
\tau
be a faithful trace. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite dimensional, then the C*-metric
d_{\mathcal{A}}
and the Bures metric
d_{B}^{\tau}
are topologically equivalent.

Proof.

Consider

id_{D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})}:(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{\mathcal{A}})\longrightarrow(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{B}^{\tau})

By Theorem
2.3
, we have that convergence in
d_{\mathcal{A}}
implies convergence in
d^{\tau}_{B}
. Hence
id_{D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})}
is continuous, and since
(D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}}),d_{\mathcal{A}})
is compact, we have that
id_{D_{\tau}({\mathcal{A}})}
is a homeomorphism. Hence
d_{\mathcal{A}}
and
d_{B}^{\tau}
are topologically equivalent.
∎

We conclude this section with topological equivalence of the Bures metric and quantum metric.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},L)
be a compact quantum metric space and let
\tau
be a faithful trace. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite dimensional, then the Bures metric
d_{B}^{\tau}
and quantum metric
d_{L}^{\tau}
are topologically equivalent.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorems
3.5
and
3.4
and since homeomorphic is an equivalence relation.
∎

4.
Metric inequivalence for
{\mathds{C}}^{2}

In this last section, we see that although the Bures metric and quantum metric are topologically equivalent for finite-dimensional C*-algebras, there exists finite-dimensional C*-algebras for which they aren’t equivalent as metric spaces. Our approach also proves that the Bures metric and C*-norm are not metrically equivalent since we find a quantum metric that in fact agrees with the metric on the density space induced by the C*-norm.

Consider the unital C*-algebra
{\mathds{C}}^{2}
. Define for every
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathds{C}}^{2}

L^{B}(x)=|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

It holds that
L^{B}
is an L-seminorm since
{\mathds{C}}^{2}
is finite dimensional and by
[
10
, Proposition 1.6]
. And so
mk_{L^{B}}
is a quantum metric

Consider the following faithful trace
\tau
on
{\mathds{C}}^{2}
defined for every
x\in{\mathds{C}}^{2}
by

\tau(x)=x_{1}+x_{2}.

Now, the Bures metric is given explicitly by the trace, but as the quantum metric is defined by way of a supremum, we first find a formula to explicitly calculate the quantum metric in this case, which in fact recovers the C*-metric.

Theorem 4.1
.

With the setting as above, we have that for every
x,y\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right)

d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)=|x_{1}-y_{1}|=\|x-y\|_{{\mathds{C}}^{2}}=d_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y).

Proof.

First, let
x\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right)
and let
x^{\prime}\in{\mathds{C}}^{2}
. Then

\Phi_{\tau}(x)(x^{\prime})=\phi^{\tau}_{x}(x^{\prime})=\tau(xx^{\prime})=x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1}+x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}.

Now, assume that
y\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right)
. Then as
\tau(x)=1=\tau(y)
, we have that
x_{1}+x_{2}=1=y_{1}+y_{2}
, and so
x_{1}-y_{1}=y_{2}-x_{2}
.

Hence, if
L^{B}(x^{\prime})\leqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\tau}_{x}(x^{\prime})-\varphi^{\tau}_{y}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}-y_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|(x_{1}-y_{1})x_{1}^{\prime}+(x_{2}-y_{2})x_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|(x_{1}-y_{1})x_{1}^{\prime}-(y_{2}-x_{2})x_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|(x_{1}-y_{1})x_{1}^{\prime}-(x_{1}-y_{1})x_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|x_{1}-y_{1}|\cdot|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leqslant|x_{1}-y_{1}|.

Now, consider
x^{\prime}=(1,0)
. Then

|\varphi^{\tau}_{x}(x^{\prime})-\varphi^{\tau}_{y}(x^{\prime})|=|x_{1}-y_{1}|.

Thus

d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)=\sup\{|\varphi^{\tau}_{x}(x^{\prime})-\varphi^{\tau}_{y}(x^{\prime})|:L^{B}(x^{\prime})\leqslant 1\}=|x_{1}-y_{1}|.

We also note that

\|x-y\|_{{\mathds{C}}^{2}}=\max\{|x_{1}-y_{1}|,|x_{2}-y_{2}|\}=\max\{|x_{1}-y_{1}|,|x_{1}-y_{1}|\}=|x_{1}-y_{1}|

as desired.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
d=d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}
, the Bures metric, or
d=d_{\mathcal{A}}
. With the setting as above, it holds that the quantum metric
d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}
and
d
are topologically equivalent but not metric equivalent.

Proof.

Corollary
3.6
provides topological equivalence.

Consider
x=(1,0)\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right)
. Let
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right)
. Then, by Theorem
4.1

d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)=|1-y_{1}|.

Also,

d_{B}^{\tau}(x,y)=\sqrt{1-\tau(|\sqrt{x}\sqrt{y}|)}=\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}.

Now, let us consider the ratio

\frac{d_{B}^{\tau}(x,y)}{d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)}=\frac{\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}}{|1-y_{1}|}.

But
\lim_{y_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}=0
and
\lim_{y_{1}\to 1^{-}}1-y_{1}=0
.

Now
\frac{d}{dy_{1}}(\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}})=\frac{-1}{4\sqrt{y_{1}}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}}
and
\frac{d}{dy_{1}}(1-y_{1})=-1
, and

\lim_{y_{1}\to 1^{-}}\frac{\frac{-1}{4\sqrt{y_{1}}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}}}{-1}=\lim_{y_{1}\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{4\sqrt{y_{1}}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{y_{1}}}}=\infty.

Thus by L’Hopital’s rule, we have

\lim_{y_{1}\to 1^{-}}\frac{d_{B}^{\tau}(x,y)}{d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)}=\infty.

Hence, the set

\left\{\frac{d_{B}^{\tau}(x,y)}{d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}(x,y)}:x,y\in D_{\tau}\left({\mathds{C}}^{2}\right),x\neq y\right\}

is unbounded, and so the metrics
d_{B}^{\tau}
and
d_{L^{B}}^{\tau}
are not metric equivalent.
∎

. 1.
Introduction

The classical Bohr inequality (see
[
18
]
) states that if
f
be an analytic function with the power series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
where
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

(1.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1,\;\;\text{for all}\;\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the constant
1/3
can not be improved. The constant
r_{0}=1/3
is known as Bohr’s radius, while the inequality
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1
is known as Bohr inequality. Bohr actually obtained the inequality (
1.1
) for
r\leq 1/6
and later Weiner, Riesz and Schur have independently proved it to
1/3
.

The Bohr inequality can be written in terms of distance formulation as follows

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq 1-f(0)=d(f(0),\partial\mathbb{D}),

where
d
is the Euclidean distance and
\partial\mathbb{D}
is the boundary of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}.
The notion of Bohr inequality can be generalized to any domain
\Omega
to find the largest radius
r_{{}_{\Omega}}>0
such that

(1.2)

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial\Omega)

holds for all
|z|=r\leq r_{{}_{\Omega}}
, and for all functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and such that
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\Omega.
Interestingly enough, it was exhibited in
[
7
]
that if
\Omega
is convex then the inequality (
1.2
) holds for all
|z|\leq 1/3
, and this radius is the best possible. Thus if
\Omega
is convex, then
r_{\Omega}
coincides with Bohr’s radius and most notably, the radius does not depend on
\Omega
. Abu-Muhana
[
1
]
established a result showing that when
\Omega
is a simply connected domain with
f(\mathbb{D})\subset\Omega
, then the inequality (
1.2
) holds for
|z|\leq 3-2\sqrt{2}=0,1715...
and this radius is sharp for the Koebe function
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}.

Operator algebraists began to get interested in the inequality after Dixon
[
20
]
exposed a connection between the inequality and the characterization of Banach algebras that satisfy von Neumann inequality. The generalization of Bohr’s theorem for different classes of analytic functions becomes now a days an active research area (see
[
27
,
29
,
34
]
). For example, for the holomorphic functions, Aizenberg
et al.

[
6
]
, Aytuna and Djakov
[
15
]
have studied Bohr phenomenon, and for the class of starlike logarithmic mappings, Ali
et al.

[
9
]
found Bohr radius. In
2018
, Ali and Ng
[
8
]
extended the classical Bohr inequality in Poincar
\acute{e}
disk model of the hyperbolic plane. In
2018
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
27
]
introduced the notion of
p
-Bohr radius for harmonic functions, and established result obtaining
p
-Bohr radius for the class of odd analytic functions. Powered Bohr radius for the class of all self analytic maps on
\mathbb{D}
has been studied in
[
28
]
while several different improved versions of the classical Bohr inequality were proved in
[
29
]
. In this connection, Kayumov
et al.

[
30
]
ascertained Bohr radius for the class of analytic Bloch functions and also for certain harmonic Bloch functions.

The main aim of this paper is to establish several improved versions of Bohr inequality, Refined-Bohr inequality and Bohr-Rogosinski inequality, finding the corresponding sharp radius for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
which has been studied by Ghosh and Vasudevarao in
[
24
]

\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)=\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}:{\operatorname{Re}\,}(zh^{\prime\prime}(z))>-M+|zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|,\;z\in\mathbb{D}\;\mbox{and }\;M>0\},

where

(1.3)

f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}.

To study Bohr inequality and Bohr radius for functions in
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
, we require the coefficient bounds and growth estimate of functions in
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. We have the following result on the coefficient bounds and and growth estimate for functions in
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
.

Lemma 1.4
.

[
24
]

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
for some
M>0
and be of the form (
1.3
). Then for
n\geq 2,

(i)

\displaystyle|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)};

(ii)

\displaystyle||a_{n}|-|b_{n}||\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)};

(iii)

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)}.

The inequalities are sharp with extremal function
f
given by

f^{\prime}(z)=1-2M\,\ln\,(1-z).

Lemma 1.5
.

[
24
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. Then

(1.6)

|z|+2M\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}|z|^{n}}{n(n-1)}\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+2M\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{|z|^{n}}{n(n-1)}.

Both inequalities are sharp for the function
f_{M}
given by
f_{M}(z)=z+2M\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{n}}{n(n-1)}.

The organization of this paper as follows: In section 2, we prove the sharp Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. In section 3, we prove the sharp results on improved-Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. In section 4, we prove the sharp results on refined Bohr inequality the sharp results on Bohr-type inequalities. In section 6, we give the proof of all the main results of this paper.

Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)

We prove the following sharp Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for function in the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

1.3

r_{{}_{N}}(M)

.

3.
Improved Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)

In
2017
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
26
]
proved the following improved version of Bohr’s inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
26
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
,
|f(z)|\leq 1
and
S_{r}
denote the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under mapping
f
. Then

(3.2)

B_{1}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}

and the numbers
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved. Moreover,

(3.3)

B_{2}(r):=|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{2}

and the numbers
1/2
and
9/8
cannot be improved.

Our aim is to prove a harmonic analogue of the Theorem
3.1
for functions
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. It will be interesting to investigate Theorem
3.1
in powers of
{S_{r}}/{\pi}
. Therefore, in order to generalize Theorem
3.1
, we consider a
N^{\rm th}
degree polynomial in
S_{r}/\pi
as follows

P\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)=\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{N}+\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{N-1}+\cdots+\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}.

In this connection, we recall the polylogarithm function which is defined by a power series in
z
, a Dirichlet series in
s
.

Li_{s}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{s}}=z+\frac{z^{2}}{2^{s}}+\frac{z^{3}}{3^{s}}+\cdots,

and this definition is valid for arbitrary complex order
s
and for all complex arguments
z
with
|z|<1.
Therefore, the dilogarithm, denoted as
Li_{2}(z)
, is a particular case of the polylogarithm.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
be given by (
1.3
). Then

(3.5)

r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bigg(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\bigg)r^{n}+P\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq d\left(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\right)

for
r\leq r_{{}_{N}}(M)
, where
P(w)=w^{N}+w^{N-1}+\cdots+w
, a polynomial in
w
of degree
N-1
, and
r_{{}_{N}}(M)\in(0,1)
is the smallest root of the equation

(3.6)

r-1+2M\left(r-1+(1-r)\ln(1-r)+2\ln 2+P\left(r^{2}+4M^{2}G(r)\right)\right)=0,

where
G(r)
is defined by

G(r):=r^{2}(Li_{2}(r^{2})-1)+(1-r^{2})\ln\;(1-r^{2}).

The radius
r_{{}_{N}}(M)
is the best possible.

By considering powers in the coefficients, we prove the following sharp result for functions in the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
be given by (
1.3
), then

(3.8)

r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bigg(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|+n(n-1)(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)^{2}\bigg)r^{n}\leq d\left(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\right),\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq r_{{}_{M}},

where
r_{{}_{M}}\in(0,1)
is the smallest root of the equation

(3.9)

r-1-2M(2M+1)\left(r+(1-r)\ln\,(1-r)\right)-2M(1-\ln 4)=0.

The radius
r_{{}_{M}}
is the best possible.

.

(4.3)

r_{p,t,N}(M)

5.
Proof of the main results

Before starting the proof of the main results, we recall here growth formula and distance bound for the class
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
. For
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
, we have

(5.1)

|f(z)|\geq|z|+2M\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}|z|^{n}\quad\mbox{for }\quad|z|<1.

Then the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of
f(\mathbb{D})
is given by

(5.2)

d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=\liminf\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|.

Since
f(0)=0
, from (
1.6
) and (
5.2
) we obtain

(5.3)

d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))\geq 1+2M\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
be given by (
1.3
). Using Lemmas
1.4
and
1.5
, for
|z|=r_{N}(M)
, we obtain

(5.4)

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2Mr^{n}}{n(n-1)}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{2Mr^{n}}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle=r+2M(r+(1-r)\ln(1-r))+2M\left(r+(1-r)\ln(1-r)-\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n(n-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=r+2M\left(2r+2(1-r)\ln(1-r)-\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\right).

A simple computation shows that

(5.5)

r+2M\left(2r+2(1-r)\ln(1-r)-\sum_{n=2}^{N-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n(n-1)}\right)\leq 1+2M(1-2\ln 2)

for
r\leq r_{{}_{N}}(M)
, where
r_{{}_{N}}(M)
is the smallest root of
F_{1}(r)=0
in
(0,1)
, here

F_{1}:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

Abstract
. Using the Yau-Tian-Donaldson type correspondence for v v -solitons established by Han-Li, we show that a smooth complex n n -dimensional Fano variety admits a Mabuchi soliton provided it admits an extremal Kähler metric whose scalar curvature is strictly less than 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) 2(n+1) . Combined with previous observations by Mabuchi and Nakamura in the other direction, this gives a characterization of the existence of Mabuchi solitons in terms of the existence of extremal Kähler metrics on Fano manifolds. An extension of this correspondence to v v -solitons is also obtained.

1.
Introduction

A number of different notions of
special
Kähler metrics have emerged in the last 20 years or so, in connection with Calabi’s seminal program
[
9
]
of finding a canonical representative of a given deRham class
\alpha\in H^{2}_{dR}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
of Kähler metrics on a smooth compact Kähler manifold
X
. Lead by a natural variational approach, Calabi himself proposed the notion of an
extremal
Kähler metric as a candidate for such a representative. Recall that a Kähler
\omega\in\alpha
is extremal if the flow of the gradient vector of its scalar curvature preserves the complex structure of
X
. Kähler metrics with constant scalar curvature (cscK) are a special case of extremal Kähler metrics.

In the case of a smooth Fano variety
X
a cscK metric in
\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X)
is necessarily a Kähler–Einstein metric with scalar curvature equal to
2n
. The existence problem for Kähler-Einstein metrics is now understood in terms of the Yau–Tian–Donaldson (YTD) conjecture
[
47
,
42
,
21
]
which states that
X
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
if and only if the anticanonical polarization
(X,K^{-1}_{X})
is K-polystable. There are, by now, many different proofs of this conjecture
[
43
,
16
,
48
,
5
,
31
]
, following
the initial work of Chen–Donaldson–Sun
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
(who proved K-polystability implies
existence) and Tian and Berman (who proved existence implies K-stablity)
[
42
,
4
]
.

Beyond the study of Kähler-Einstein metrics on
(X,2\pi c_{1}(X))
, other notions of canonical Kähler metrics have been considered. These allow to treat cases where a Kähler-Einstein metric does not exist due to obstructions in terms of the automorphisms of
X

[
35
,
32
,
23
]
. Following initial existence results
[
10
,
39
]
, Tian-Zhu
[
45
,
44
]
initiated a comprehensive study of the so-called
Kähler-Ricci solitons
(KRS) on
(X,2\pi c_{1}(X))
. Martelli–Sparks–Yau
[
34
]
developed the theory of Calabi–Yau cones (or, equivalently, Sasaki–Einstein structures) defined on the affine cone
K_{X}^{\times}
associated to
X
. Mabuchi
[
33
]
introduced the notion of an
M-soliton
(or
Mabuchi soliton
) and related it to the existence of extremal Kähler metrics in
\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X)
. These works prompted separate investigations of the corresponding existence theories, and the formulation and proofs of appropriate modifications of the YTD conjecture in each case, see respectively
[
19
,
17
,
29
]
.

More recently, there have been developments providing a framework to treat the existence problems mentioned above all together: Building on a foundational work by Berman–Witt Nyström
[
7
]
, Han–Li
[
27
]
studied the existence problem for the so-called
v
-soliton
Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
(called
g
-solitons in
[
27
]
). A
v
-soliton is defined in terms of a fixed maximal compact torus
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
with associated canonical polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}\subset\left({\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})\right)^{*}
, and a positive smooth function
v(x)>0
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, via the equation

{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log v(\mu_{\omega}).

In the above formula,
\omega
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
,
{\rm Ric}(\omega)\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
is its Ricci form and
\mu_{\omega}:X\to\mathrm{P}_{X}
is the canonically normalized
{\mathbb{T}}
-momentum map. Thus, Kähler–Einstein metrics correspond to
1
-solitons, KRS metrics to
e^{\ell}
-solitons
[
45
]
, Calabi–Yau cone structures on
K_{X}^{\times}
to
\ell^{-(n+2)}
-solitons
[
1
]
and Mabuchi solitons to
\ell
-solitons, where
\ell(x)
is a suitably defined (and in general different for each case) affine-linear function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. As an outcome, the work
[
27
]
gives a YTD type correspondence for the existence of a
v
-soliton on
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
, expressed in terms of a suitable notion of
uniform
v
-weighted Ding stability
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X)))
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test-configurations.

In this short paper, we use the Han-Li YTD type correspondence for
v
-solitons combined with a few elementary observations in order to establish the following result.

Theorem 1
.

[see Theorem
6
] Let
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
be a smooth complex
n
-dimensional Fano manifold and
v
a smooth positive weight function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
normalized so that
\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)=\mathit{vol}(X)
. Then
X
admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
v
-soliton metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
if and only if there exists a (different in general)
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
whose scalar curvature satisfies

{\rm Scal}(\omega)=2\Big((n+1)-v(\mu_{\omega})\Big).

Futaki-Mabuchi
[
24
]
observed that there exists a unique affine-linear function
\ell_{\rm ext}(x)
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
such that a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
is extremal iff
{\rm Scal}(\omega)=\ell_{\rm ext}(\mu_{\omega})
. Furthermore, Mabuchi
[
33
]
proved that if
\tilde{\omega}\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
is an
\ell
-soliton for a positive affine-linear function
\ell
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, then

\ell=(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\rm ext}.

He thus obtained that the condition

(1)

\max_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\ell_{\rm ext}<2(n+1)

is a necessary condition for the existence of an
\ell
-soliton. On the other hand, Nakamura (see
[
33
]
) proved, using the result of Chen-Cheng
[
12
]
and the complement by He
[
28
]
that if
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton then it also admits an extremal Kähler metric satisfying the above condition.
This observation was recently extended for the weights corresponding to the so-called
\sigma
-solitons
by Nakagawa and Nakamura (
[
36
]
).
Finally, in
[
38
,
37
]
, the authors constructed examples of smooth Fano manifolds admitting extremal Kähler metrics in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
for which the necessary condition (
1
) fails (and thus they do not admit Mabuchi solitons). As a direct corollary of Theorem
1
above, we close this circle of ideas by establishing

Corollary 1
.

A smooth Fano manifold
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if the extremal affine linear function
\ell_{\rm ext}
satisfies (
1
) and
2\pi c_{1}(X)
admits an extremal metric.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Fano manifolds: notation and normalization

In what follows,
X
denotes a smooth compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n
, for which the anti-canonical bundle
K^{-1}_{X}
is ample. Such an
X
is called a
smooth Fano variety
. The Fano condition implies that
X
is projective, and that the deRham class
\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X)=2\pi c_{1}(K_{X}^{-1})
contains Kähler metrics.

Any Kähler metric
\omega\in\alpha
is deRham cohomologous with the corresponding Ricci form
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)
, and thus we can write

(2)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}h_{\omega},

for a smooth function
h_{\omega}
which (by the maximum principle) is unique up to an additive constant. Such a function will be referred to as a
Ricci potential
of
\omega
;
we can further fix the additive constant
by requiring that

(3)

\int_{X}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\omega^{[n]}=:\mathit{vol}(X),

where
\omega^{[n]}:=\omega^{n}/n!
stands for the Riemannian volume form of the Kähler metric
\omega
. We shall then refer to this uniquely defined Ricci potential
\mathring{h}_{\omega}
as the
normalized Ricci potential
of
\omega
.
In these terms, the
Kähler–Einstein
condition

(4)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)=\omega

is equivalent to
\mathring{h}_{\omega}=0
.

We shall next fix once for all a maximal compact real torus
{\mathbb{T}}
inside the connected component of identity
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
of the group of complex automorphisms of
X
. The corresponding complex torus will be denoted by
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
. There is a canonical lift (still denoted by
{\mathbb{T}}
) of the action of
{\mathbb{T}}
on
X
to an action on the canonical bundle
K_{X}
. The latter bundle has a further
{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
-extension of the lifted
{\mathbb{T}}
action, given by fibre-wise multiplications with complex numbers
e^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. We denote by
\hat{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
the resulting
({\rm dim}({\mathbb{T}})+1)
-dimensional torus acting on
K_{X}
.
We shall respectively denote by
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
the Lie algebras of
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}
.

We consider the space

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics
\omega
on
X
, belonging to
\alpha
; by a standard averaging argument,
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\neq\emptyset
. Introducing a base-point
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
, we will identify
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
with the Fréchet space
{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}
, where

\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X):=\left\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\,\,|\,\,\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\varphi>0\right\}

is the space of smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler potentials with respect to
\omega_{0}
.

For each
\omega\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
, we let
H_{\omega}
denote the Hermitian metric on
K_{X}
whose Chern curvature is

R^{H_{\omega}}=-i\omega
, and by
\nabla^{H_{\omega}}
the
H_{\omega}
-Chern connection on
K_{X}
. A basic fact in the theory
[
25
]
is that for any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, and any smooth section
s\in C^{\infty}(X,K_{X})
, we have

{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}s=\nabla^{H_{\omega}}_{\xi}s-i\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}s,

where the smooth function
\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}
satisfies

\omega(\xi,\cdot)=-d\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}.

This gives rise to a
canonically normalized
momentum map
\mu_{\omega}:X\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
whose image
\mathrm{P}_{X}
is a compact convex polytope
[
2
,
26
]
; one can further show (see e.g.
[
7
,
30
]
) that
\mu_{\omega}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
is independent of the choice of
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
. In this paper, we shall refer to
\mathrm{P}_{X}
as the
canonical polytope
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
.

Remark 1
.

In general, the
{\mathbb{T}}
-momentum map
\mu_{\omega}:X\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
is defined only up to a translation with an element of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
; the fact that in the Fano case there is a canonical normalization for
\mu_{\omega}
follows from the existence of a canonical lift of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
X
to
K_{X}
.

An alternative way to define the canonical normalization for
\mu_{\omega}
(see e.g.
[
1
,
45
]
) is to require that for any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, the function
\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}:=\langle\mu_{\omega},\zeta\rangle
satisfies

(5)

\int_{X}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}e^{h_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=0,

where
h_{\omega}
is any Ricci potential of
\omega
.

Once we have suitably normalized
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, we can define the
Dustermaat-Heckman

measure
[
22
]

d\mu_{\rm DH}
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
as the push-forward via
\mu_{\omega}
of the Riemannian measure of
(X,\omega)
: for any continuous function
f(x)
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, we let

(6)

\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}f(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}:=\int_{X}f(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}.

The fact that the LHS is independent of the choice of
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
follows for instance from the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Moser’s lemma (see also
[
24
]
).

2.2.
Kähler–Ricci solitons

Following
[
45
]
, a
Kähler Ricci soliton
(KRS for short) is a Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
which satisfies

(7)

{\rm Ric}({\omega})-\omega=-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{J\tau}\omega,

where
\tau
is a Killing vector field for the Kähler structure
\omega
. In the case
\tau=0
, (
7
) reduces to the Kähler–Einstein condition (
4
). Tian–Zhu
[
45
]
have extended the Matsushima’s theorem to the case of a KRS, which in turn yields that any Kähler metric satisfying (
7
) must be invariant by the action of a maximal torus in
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
, containing the flow of
\tau
. Up to a pull back by an element of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
, we can and will assume that a KRS on
X
belongs to
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
and
\tau\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. Thus, similarly to the Kähler–Einstein case, the KRS condition can be rewritten as

(8)

h_{\omega}=\mu_{\omega}^{\tau}

or, equivalently,

(9)

{\rm Ric}({\omega})-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\mu_{\omega}^{\tau},\qquad\tau\in{\mathfrak{t}}.

By Remark
1
, (
6
) and (
8
), if
X
admits a KRS in
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
, then for any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we have

\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\langle\zeta,x\rangle e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}d\mu_{\rm DH}=0.

The above condition means that
\tau
is a critical point of the function
F:{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
:

F(\zeta):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}e^{\langle\zeta,x\rangle}d\mu_{\rm DH}.

Tian–Zhu
[
45
]
further show that
F
admits a unique critical point,
\tau
, independent of the existence of a KRS on
X
. We shall refer to
\tau
as the
KRS vector field
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
and to the positive smooth function
v(x):=e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}
on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
as the
KRS weight function
.

2.3.
v
-solitons

The notion of KRS extends to the following more general geometric situation, studied by Berman–Witt Nyström in
[
7
]
and, more recently, by Han–Li in
[
27
]
. We follow the notation of
[
1
, Sect.2]
.

Definition 1

(
v
-soliton)
.

In the setup as above, let
v(x)
be a given positive function defined on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
is called a
v
-soliton
if it satisfies

(10)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log v(\mu_{\omega}).

Clearly, Kähler–Einstein metrics are
1
-solitons whereas KRS are
v=e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}
-solitons.
Notice that if
\omega
is a
v
-soliton it is also a
\lambda v
-soliton for any
\lambda>0
. To get rid of this ambiguity, we shall sometimes consider
normalized
weight functions
\mathring{v}:=\left(\frac{\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}d\mu_{\rm DH}}{\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}vd\mu_{\rm DH}}\right)v
, i.e. such that

(11)

\mathit{vol}_{\mathring{v}}(X):=\int_{X}\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\omega^{[n]}=:\mathit{vol}(X).

We also notice that for any
v
-soliton,
h_{\omega}=\log(v(\mu_{\omega}))
, so by Remark
1
,
the linear function

(12)

{\rm Fut}_{v}:{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad{\rm Fut}_{v}(\zeta):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\langle\zeta,x\rangle v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}

identically vanishes.

Definition 2

(
v
-Futaki invariant)
.

The linear function defined by (
12
) is called the
v
-Futaki invariant
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
.

We next define a functional
{\bf I}_{v}
on the
space
\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler potentials (see
[
30
,
27
]
):

(13)

d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v}(\dot{\varphi})=\int_{X}v(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})\dot{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]},\qquad{\bf I}_{v}(0)=0.

Following
[
27
]
, we introduce

Definition 3

(
v
-Ding functional)
.

The
v
-Ding functional is the map

{\bf D}_{v}:{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

{\bf D}_{v}(\varphi):=-\left(\frac{{\bf I}_{v}(\varphi)}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\int_{M}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}-2\varphi}\frac{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}\right),

where we have set
\mathit{vol}_{v}(X):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}=\int_{X}v(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}
and
\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}
stands for the normalized Ricci potential of the base point
\omega_{0}
, see (
3
).

Notice that
{\bf D}_{v}
does not change if we add a constant to
\varphi
, so it actually descends to a functional, denoted
{\bf D}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})
, on the space
\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
.

It is not hard to see that the differential of
{\bf D}_{v}
is given by

(14)

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf D}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(\frac{e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}-\frac{v(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi},

so that the critical points of
{\bf D}_{v}
are precisely the Kähler metrics
\omega_{\varphi}
for which

e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}=\mathit{vol}(X)\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})

i.e. the
v
-solitons.

Another consequence of the formula (
14
) is the following

Lemma 1
.

The
v
-Ding functional is
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant, i.e. satisfies

{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma^{*}(\omega))={\bf D}_{v}(\omega)\qquad\forall\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}},

iff the
v
-Futaki invariant
{\rm Fut}_{v}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

{\bf D}_{v}
is clearly
{\mathbb{T}}
invariant. For any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we consider the flow of
-J\zeta

\sigma_{t}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
, and take derivative at
t=0
of
{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma_{t}^{*}(\omega))
. By (
14
)

\frac{d}{dt}_{|_{t=0}}{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma_{t}^{*}(\omega))=\int_{X}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}\left(\frac{e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}-\frac{v(\mu_{\omega})}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)\omega^{[n]}=-{\rm Fut}_{v}(\zeta),

where we have used
(
5
) for the canonically normalized momentum map
\mu_{\omega}
. The claim follows from the above by a standard argument.
∎

We end-up this section with stating one of the main results of
[
27
]
(see Theorem 1.6), which gives an anlaytic criterion for the existence of a
v
-soliton on
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
in terms of
{\bf D}_{v}
. Recall the definition
[
3
]
of the Aubin functional
{\bf J}:\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
:

{\bf J}(\varphi):=\int_{X}\varphi\omega_{0}^{[n]}-{\bf I}_{1}(\varphi).

The functional
{\bf J}
descends to
{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}
(so we denote it by
{\bf J}(\omega_{\varphi})
), and has the property that
{\bf J}(\omega_{\varphi})\geq 0
with
{\bf J}(\omega_{\phi})=0
iff
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega_{0}
.

Theorem 2
.

[
27
]
Let
X
be a Fano manifold,
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
a maximal compact torus with canonical momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
and
v
a positive smooth function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. Then
X
admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
v
-soliton in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
if and only if the
v
-weighted Ding functional
{\bf D}_{v}
is invariant and coercive with respect to the complex torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
, i.e. there exist constants
\Lambda>0
and
C
such that for any
\omega\in{\mathcal{K}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
,

{\bf D}_{v}(\omega)\geq\Lambda\inf_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}{\bf J}(\sigma^{*}(\omega))-C.

Versions of the above theorem have been known for KRS by the works of Cao–Tian–Zhu (see
[
11
, Theorems 0.1 and 0.2]
) and Darvas–Rubinstein (see
[
18
, Theorem 2.11]
).

3.
v
-solitons as weighted constant scalar curvature metrics

In
[
1
, Prop.1]
, it is observed that any
v
-soliton
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
can be equivalently described as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
with
(v,\tilde{v})
-constant scalar curvature in sense of Lahdili
[
30
]
, where the smooth function
{\tilde{v}}(x)
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
is defined by

(15)

{\tilde{v}}(x):=2\left(n+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\log v)_{,i}x_{i}\right)v(x).

To recall the construction in
[
1
]
, we consider the notion of a
(v,w)
-cscK metric
[
30
]
, introduced by the equation

(16)

{\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega):=v(\mu_{\omega}){\rm Scal}(\omega)+2\Delta_{\omega}v(\mu_{\omega})+\big\langle g_{\omega},\mu_{\omega}^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\right)\big\rangle=w(\mu_{\omega}),

where
g_{\omega}
is the riemannian metric associated to
\omega
and the contraction
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is taken between the smooth
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
-valued function
g_{\omega}
on
X
(the restriction of the riemannian metric
g_{\omega}
to
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset C^{\infty}(X,TX)
) and the smooth
{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}
-valued function
{\mu_{\omega}}^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\right)
on
X
(given by the pull-back by
\mu_{\omega}
of
\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{P}_{X},{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}})
); equivalently, if
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots\ell}

\big\langle g_{\omega},\mu_{\omega}^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\right)\big\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\ell}v_{,ij}(\mu_{\omega})g_{\omega}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}).

In these terms, we have

Proposition 1
.

[
1
]

\omega\in\mathcal{K}_{2\pi c_{1}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
is a
v
-soliton if and only if it is a
(v,\tilde{v})
-cscK metric, i.e. satisfies (

.

Notice that when

The advantage of this point of view is that it leads to a weighted version of the Mabuchi energy, which we shall denote by

, defined on
\mathcal{H}_{\omega_{0}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf M}_{v,w})(\dot{\varphi}):=-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left({\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})-w(\mu_{\varphi})\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]},\qquad{\bf M}_{v,w}(\omega_{0})=0.

The critical points of

are the
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
(v,w)
-cscK metrics in a given Kähler class
\alpha=[\omega_{0}]

which is independent of
\omega\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
and thus vanishes should a

-cscK metric exist in

is invariant under the natural action of

on

Theorem 3
.

[

]
If

admits a

on

In

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf M}_{v,w}(\omega_{\varphi})=\int_{X}\log\left(\frac{v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}}{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}\right)v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}&-2{\bf I}^{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})}_{v}(\varphi)+{\bf I}_{w}(\varphi)\\
&-\int_{X}\log(v(\mu_{0}))v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]},\end{split}

where the functional
{\bf I}_{w}:\mathcal{H}_{\omega_{0}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined in (

)
and for a fixed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant closed
(1,1)
-form
\rho
on

with momentum

, the functional
{\bf I}^{\rho}_{v}:\mathcal{H}_{\omega_{0}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\rho}_{v})(\dot{\varphi}):=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(v(\mu_{\varphi})\rho\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{[n-1]}+\langle(dv)(\mu_{\varphi}),\mu_{\rho}\rangle\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right),\quad{\bf I}^{\rho}_{v}(0)=0.

In

Lemma 2
.

On a Fano manifold
(X,{\mathbb{T}},\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X))
, the weighted Mabuchi energy
{\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}
corresponding to
v
-solitons has the following equivalent expression

(18)

\begin{split}{\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}(\omega_{\varphi})=&\int_{X}\log\left(\frac{v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}}{e^{h_{0}}\omega_{0}^{[n]}}\right)v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}+2\left(\int_{X}\varphi v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-{\bf I}_{v}(\varphi)\right)\\
&-\int_{X}\log(e^{-h_{0}}v(\mu_{0}))v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]},\end{split}

where
h_{0}
is a Ricci potential of
\omega_{0}
.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we index by
\varphi
the geometric quantities depending on the Kähler structure
\omega_{\varphi}
. We fix a basis of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
generators
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1,\cdots,\ell}
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Using the relations
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})=\omega_{0}+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}h_{0}
, we have that the momentum map with respect to
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})
is

\mu_{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})}^{\zeta}=\mu^{\zeta}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J\zeta}h_{0},\qquad\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}.

We further normalize
h_{0}
by
\int_{X}e^{h_{0}}\omega_{0}^{[n]}=1
and compute

\begin{split}(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})=&\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(v(\mu_{\varphi}){\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{[n-1]}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})\mu_{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})}^{\xi_{j}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\Big)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(-v(\mu_{\varphi})\Delta_{\varphi}(h_{0})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}h_{0})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\Big)+(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(-\langle d(v(\mu_{\varphi})),dh_{0}\rangle_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}h_{0})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\Big)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\langle dh_{0},d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})\langle d\mu^{\xi_{j}}_{\varphi},dh_{0}\rangle_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}h_{0})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\Big)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\langle dh_{0},d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\langle dh_{0},d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi}),\end{split}

where we used the identity
\langle d\mu^{\xi_{j}}_{\varphi},dh_{0}\rangle_{\omega_{\varphi}}=-\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}h_{0}
. On the other hand, noting that

(19)

d_{\varphi}[v(\mu_{\varphi})](\dot{\varphi})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{,i}(\mu_{\varphi})(d^{c}\dot{\varphi})(\xi_{i})=\langle d[v(\mu_{\varphi})],d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi},

we have

\begin{split}d_{\varphi}\left[\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right]=&\int_{X}h_{0}\langle d[v(\mu_{\varphi})],d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{\varphi})\Delta_{\varphi}(\dot{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[m]}\\
=&-\int_{X}\langle dh_{0},d\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\end{split}

and hence

(20)

{\bf I}^{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})}_{v}(\varphi)={\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v}(\varphi)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]}.

The last integral in the RHS arises from the normalization
{\bf I}_{v}^{{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})}(0)={\bf I}_{v}^{\omega_{0}}(0)=0
. Using
\mu^{\zeta}_{0}=\mu^{\zeta}_{\varphi}+\mathcal{L}_{J\zeta}\varphi
for
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we compute further

\begin{split}(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})=&\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{[n-1]}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})\mu_{\omega_{0}}^{\xi_{j}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\Big)\\
=&\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\Big(v(\mu_{\varphi})(\omega_{\varphi}-dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{[n-1]}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mu_{\varphi}^{\xi_{j}}+\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{[m]}\Big).\end{split}

Integrating by parts further leads to

\begin{split}(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})=&n(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\langle d(v(\mu_{\varphi})),d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
&+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mu_{\varphi}^{\xi_{j}}+\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&n(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[m]}\\
&+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{,j}(\mu_{\varphi})(\mu_{\varphi}^{\xi_{j}}+\mathcal{L}_{J\xi_{j}}\varphi)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&n(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})+\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\langle(dv)(\mu_{\varphi}),\mu_{\varphi}\rangle\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&n(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})+\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}(\mu_{\varphi})-nv(\mu_{\varphi})\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\frac{1}{2}(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{\tilde{v}})(\dot{\varphi}).\end{split}

By (
19
) we have

\int_{X}\langle d\dot{\varphi},d\varphi\rangle_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}=-d_{\varphi}\left[\int_{X}\varphi v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right]+(d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v})(\dot{\varphi}),

and hence

(21)

{\bf I}^{\omega_{0}}_{v}(\varphi)={\bf I}_{v}(\dot{\varphi})+\frac{1}{2}{\bf I}_{\tilde{v}}(\varphi)-\int_{X}\varphi v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}.

Substituting (
20
) and (
21
) in (
17
) we obtain the formula (
18
). ∎

Using Lemma
2
, we obtain

Lemma 3
.

Let
(X,{\mathbb{T}},\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X))
be a Fano manifold and
v>0
a smooth positive weight function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. Then the weighted Ding functional
{\bf D}_{v}
is related to the weighted Mabuchi energy
{\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}
by

{\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}(\omega_{\varphi})=2\mathit{vol}_{v}(X){\bf D}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})-\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{0}v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]}.

Proof.

Using the relations

\begin{split}dd^{c}\log\left(\frac{v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{m}_{\varphi}}{\omega_{0}^{m}}\right)&=dd^{c}\log v(\mu_{\varphi})+2({\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})-{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{\varphi}))\\
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{0})-{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{\varphi})&=-dd^{c}\left(\varphi+\frac{1}{2}(h_{\varphi}-h_{0})\right),\end{split}

we obtain

(22)

\log\left(\frac{v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}}{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}\right)=\log v(\mu_{\varphi})-h_{\varphi}+h_{0}-2\varphi+C(\varphi)/{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)},

where
C(\varphi)
is a constant depending on
\varphi
to be determined below. By using the identity (see e.g.
[
30
, Lemma 2]
)

\int_{X}\log v(\mu_{\varphi})v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\log v(x)v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}=\int_{X}\log v(\mu_{0})v(\mu_{0})\omega^{[n]}_{0},

and integrating both sides of (
22
) with respect to the measure
v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}
gives

\begin{split}C(\varphi)=&\int_{X}\log\left(\frac{v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}}{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}\right)v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}+\int_{X}h_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
&+2\int_{X}\varphi v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}\log v(\mu_{0})v(\mu_{0})\omega^{[n]}_{0}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2
we thus have

C(\varphi)=({\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}+2{\bf I}_{v})(\varphi)+\int_{X}h_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]}.

Substituting back in (
22
) gives

-h_{\varphi}+\frac{1}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\left(({\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}+2{\bf I}_{v})(\varphi)+\int_{X}h_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]}\right)=\log\left(\frac{\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi}}{e^{-2\varphi+h_{0}}\omega_{0}^{[n]}}\right).

Exponentiating and integrating the both sides with respect to the measure
\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}
yields

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{X}e^{h_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\left(({\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}+2{\bf I}_{v})(\varphi)+\int_{X}h_{\varphi}v(\mu_{\varphi})\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}-\int_{X}h_{0}v(\mu_{0})\omega_{0}^{[n]}\right)\right)\\
&=\int_{X}e^{-2\varphi+h_{0}}\omega^{[n]}_{0}.\end{split}

Taking the
\log
of both sides and comparing with the Definition
3
, we obtain the claim. ∎

The above link between
{\bf M}_{v,\tilde{v}}
and
{\bf D}_{v}
is the key for the following

Theorem 4
.

[

, Thm.3.6]
On a smooth Fano manifold

with Kähler class

iff the weighted Ding functional

Proof of main results

4.1.
Mabuchi’s observation for
v
-solitons

Let
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric. We denote by
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\omega}
the space of Killing potentials of vector fields in
{\mathfrak{t}}
, i.e.

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\omega}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(X)\,|\,\zeta=-\omega^{-1}(df)\in{\mathfrak{t}}\},

and by
\mathring{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{\omega}:=\{\mu^{\zeta}_{\omega}\mid\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}\}
be the subspace of
canonically normalized
Killing potentials, i.e.

\mathring{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{\omega}:=\left\{f\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\omega}\,\Big|\int_{X}fe^{h_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=0\right\},

where
h_{\omega}
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
\omega
-relative Ricci potential, see (
2
).
By virtue of the normalization (
5
), we thus have

\mathring{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{\omega}=\{\mu^{\zeta}_{\omega}\,|\,\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}\}.

Taking trace w.r.t.
\omega
and interior product with
\zeta
in (
2
) gives

{\rm Scal}(\omega)-2n=-\Delta_{\omega}h_{\omega},\qquad\Delta_{\omega}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}-2\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}-\langle dh_{\omega},d\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}\rangle_{\omega}=0.

Taking the
L_{2}
-product of the first relation with
\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}
, integrating by parts and using the second identity gives

(23)

\int_{X}\left({\rm Scal}(\omega)-2(n+1)\right)\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}\omega^{[n]}=0.

As we also have
\int_{X}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}e^{h_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=0
by virtue of (
5
), we derive the following

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
\mathring{h}_{\omega}
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
\omega
-relative Ricci potential normalized by

\int_{X}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\omega^{[n]}
. Then the
L_{2}
-orthogonal projection

{\mathrmsl proj}_{\omega}(e^{{\mathring{h}}_{\omega}})
of
e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}
to the space
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\omega}
of Killing potentials of elements in
{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by

{\mathrmsl proj}_{\omega}(e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}})=(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\rm ext}(\mu_{\omega}),

where
\ell_{\rm ext}
is the extremal affine-linear function of
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
.

Proof.

By the definition
[
24
]
of
\ell_{\rm ext}
,
{\mathrmsl proj}_{\omega}({\rm Scal}(\omega))=\ell_{\rm ext}(\mu_{\omega})
. It thus follows from (
23
) and (
5
) that

{\mathrmsl proj}_{\omega}(e^{h_{\omega}})=\lambda{\mathrmsl proj}_{\omega}\left((n+1)-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Scal}(\omega)\right),

so the claim follows by the normalization of
\mathring{h}_{\omega}
.
∎

In particular, if
v
is affine linear, i.e. if
\omega
is a Mabuchi soliton, then
v=(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\rm ext}
is the corresponding positive weight function.

4.2.
Nakamura’s argument for
v
-solitons

We first extend
[
36
, Lemma 2.3]
for an arbitrary weight function
v
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
v
be a positive smooth weight function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
such that

\mathit{vol}_{v}(X):=\int_{X}{v}(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}=\mathit{vol}(X).

Then, for any
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
we have

(24)

{\bf M}_{1,2[(n+1)-v]}(\omega)=\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}\omega_{0}^{[n]}-\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega}\omega^{[n]}+2\mathit{vol}(X){\bf D}_{v}(\omega).

In particular,

\frac{1}{2\mathit{vol}(X)}{\bf M}_{1,2[(n+1)-v]}(\omega)\geq{\bf D}_{v}(\omega)+C

where
C
is a constant depending only on
X
and
v
.

Proof.

We shall use the identification
\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
and for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
we use the under-script
\varphi
to denote the geometric quantities with respect to the Kähler metric
\omega_{\varphi}
.
In the computations below, we also let

w:=2[(n+1)-v].

Using the very definition of the
(1,w)
-Mabuchi energy in
[
30
]
, we have

\begin{split}(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf M}_{1,w})(\dot{\varphi})=&-\int_{X}\left({\rm Scal}(\omega_{\varphi})-w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})\right)\dot{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&-\int_{X}\left(-\Delta_{\omega_{\varphi}}h_{\omega_{\varphi}}+2n-w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})\right)\dot{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\left(h_{\omega_{\varphi}}\Delta_{\omega_{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}+(w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})-2n)\dot{\varphi}\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\\
=&-d_{\omega_{\varphi}}\left(\int_{X}h_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right)(\dot{\varphi})+\int_{X}\dot{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\int_{X}(w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})-2n)\dot{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\end{split}

where we used that
{\rm Scal}(\omega_{\varphi})-2n=-\Delta_{\omega_{\varphi}}h_{\omega_{\varphi}}
. We further assume that the Ricci potential is normalized, i.e.
h_{\omega_{\varphi}}=\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}
, see (
3
). We then have

\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}=\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}-2\varphi-\log\left(\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{m}}{\omega_{0}^{m}}\right)-\log\int_{X}e^{h_{\omega_{0}}-2\varphi}\frac{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}.

The variation with respect to
\varphi
of both sides of the above equation gives

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}})(\dot{\varphi})=\left(\Delta_{\omega_{\varphi}}-2\right)\Big(\dot{\varphi}-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}\Big).

It follows that for
h_{\omega_{\varphi}}=\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}

\int_{X}\dot{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}=2\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}-1\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}.

Substituting back gives

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf M}_{1,w})(\dot{\varphi})=-d_{\omega_{\varphi}}\left(\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right)(\dot{\varphi})+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(2e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}+w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})-2(n+1)\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}.

As the derivative of the
v
-Ding functional is given by (
14
), and using that
v:=\frac{1}{2}\left(2(n+1)-w\right)
satisfies
\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)=\mathit{vol}(X)
, we get

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf M}_{1,w})(\dot{\varphi})=-d_{\omega_{\varphi}}\left(\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\right)(\dot{\varphi})+2\mathit{vol}(X)(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf D}_{v})(\dot{\varphi}).

Using that
{\bf M}_{1,w}(\omega_{0})=0={\bf D}_{v}(\omega_{0})
, we thus obtain (
24
). Using
e^{x}\geq x+1
we can write

\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}+\mathit{vol}(X)=\int_{X}(\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}+1)\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}\leq\int_{X}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}=\mathit{vol}(X).

Letting
C:=\frac{1}{2\mathit{vol}(X)}\int_{X}\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}\omega_{0}^{[n]}
, we obtain the claimed inequality. ∎

v

Theorem 5
.

Let
v

.
If

admits a

-soliton then it also admits
(1,2[(n+1)-v])
-cscK metric. In particular, the existence of a Mabuchi soliton implies the existence of a Calabi extremal metric
\omega_{E}
in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
whose scalar curvature satisfies
{\rm Scal}(\omega_{E})<2(n+1)
.

Proof.

We let again
w:=2[(n+1)-v])
. If
(X,2\pi c_{1}(X))
admits a
v
-soliton, then by
[
27
]

{\bf D}_{v}
is coersive with respect to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
, and so it is
{\bf M}_{1,w}
by Lemma
5
, where we used that both
{\bf D}_{v}
and
{\bf M}_{1,w}
are
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant (see Lemma
1
and (
24
)). Now, we can conclude by using He’s extension
[
28
]
of Chen–Cheng’s result
[
12
]
, which holds for the
(1,w)
-cscK problem as
{\rm Fut}_{1,w}=0
and the term
w(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})
stays
C^{0}(X)
-bounded as
w
is a fixed smooth function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. ∎

4.3.
The converse

We provide a converse of Theorem
5
, as a consequence the YTD correspondence established in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
be a smooth Fano manifold and
v
a smooth positive weight function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
such that
\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)=\mathit{vol}(X)
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(X,{\mathbb{T}})
admits a
(1,2[(n+1)-v])
-cscK metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
;

(ii)

(X,K^{-1}_{X},{\mathbb{T}})
is uniformly
(1,2[(n+1)-v])
-K-stable relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, in the sense of Lahdili
[
30
]
.

(iii)

(X,K^{-1}_{X},{\mathbb{T}})
is uniformly
v
-Ding stable relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, in the sense of Han–Li
[
27
]
.

(iv)

(X,K^{-1}_{X},{\mathbb{T}})
is uniformly
v
-Ding stable relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
on special
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-equivariant test configurations.

(v)

(X,{\mathbb{T}})
admits a
v
-soliton in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is established in
[
1
, Cor.1]
. As a matter of fact, in this reference and in
[
30
]
the authors consider the class of
smooth

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test configurations with reduced central fibre in order to define the asymptotic slope
{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{v,w}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
of the weighted Mabuchi energy
{\bf M}_{v,w}
. However, in the case
v=1
, which is of interest here, we have that
{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,w}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})={\bf M}_{1,2n}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})+{\bf I}^{\rm NA}_{2n-w}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
where the quantities at the RHS are well-defined for arbitrary
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test configurations:
{\bf M}_{1,2n}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
is computed in
[
8
,
20
,
40
]

whereas the computation of
{\bf I}^{\rm NA}_{2n-w}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
for general test configuration appears in
see
[
27
]
(see also
[
46
]
). Thus, the proof of Cor. 1 in
[
1
]
extends to yield the desired result.

Below we outline (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv), noting that (iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v) is established in
[
27
]
. By (
24
), for any
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test configuration
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
,

{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,2[(n+1)-v]}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})-2\mathit{vol}(X){\bf D}_{v}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})={\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,2n}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})-2\mathit{vol}(X){\bf D}_{1}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}).

By
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
(which computes
{\bf D}_{1}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
) and
[
8
, Theorem A]
or
[
40
, Theorem 5.1]
(which gives
{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,2n}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
), if
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
is a
special
test configuration, we have the equality

{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,2n}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})=2\mathit{vol}(X){\bf D}_{1}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}),

which yields
{\bf D}_{v}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})=\frac{1}{2\mathit{vol}(X)}{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,2(n+1)-v}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
, hence the validity of (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv).

Finally, Theorem
5
gives (v)
\Rightarrow
(i).
∎

The above result yields

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
is a smooth Fano manifold for which the extremal Futaki–Mabuchi affine-linear function
\ell_{\rm ext}
satisfies on
\mathrm{P}_{X}

\ell_{\rm ext}<2(n+1).

Then
X
admits an extremal Kähler metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
iff
(X,K^{-1}_{X},{\mathbb{T}})
is uniform relatively K-stable with respect to special
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test configurations in the sense of Székelyhidi
[
41
]
.

Proof.

First note the condition
\ell_{\rm ext}<2(n+1)
guarantees that the weight function
(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\rm ext}
is positive on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
.
Hisamoto
[
29
]
showed that
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-relative Donaldson–Futaki invariant of a special
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-equivariant test configuration
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
in the sense of
[
41
]
equals
{\bf D}_{((n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\rm ext})}^{\rm NA}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
(and also to
{\bf M}^{\rm NA}_{1,\ell_{\rm ext}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L})
by the arguments in Theorem
6
). The results then follows from Theorem
6
.
∎

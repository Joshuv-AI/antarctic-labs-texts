Lower deviation probabilities for level sets of the branching random walk
Shuxiong Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:

Given a branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}
, let
Z_{n}([y,\infty))
be the number of particles located in
[y,\infty)
at generation
n
. It is known from
[
10
]
that under some mild conditions,
n^{-1}\log Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))
converges a.s. to
\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
, where
\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
is a positive constant. In this work, we investigate its lower deviation, in other words, the convergence rates of

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right),

where
a\in[0,\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))
. Our results complete those in
[
41
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
26
]
.

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020): 60F10, 60J80, 60G50.

Key words and phrases
: Branching random walk; Level sets; Lower deviation.

1
Introduction and Main results

1.1
Background

In this article, we study the lower deviation probabilities for local mass of the branching random walk (BRW). Given a probability distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}
and a real-valued random variable
X
, a branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with offspring law
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and step size
X
is defined as follows. At time
0
, there is one particle located at the origin. The particle dies and produces offsprings according to the offspring distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. Afterwards, the offspring particles move independently according to the law of step size
X
, which forms a process
Z_{1}
. For any point process
Z_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, we define it by the following iteration

Z_{n}=\sum_{x\in Z_{n-1}}\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x},

where
\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x}
has the same distribution as
Z_{1}(\cdot-S_{x})
and
\{\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x}:x\in Z_{n-1}\}
(conditioned on
Z_{n-1}
) are independent. Here and later, for a point process
\xi
,
x\in\xi
means
x
is an atom of
\xi
, and
S_{x}
is the position of
x
(i.e.,
\xi=\sum_{x\in\xi}\delta_{S_{x}}
).

For
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, let

Z_{n}(A)=\#\left\{u\in Z_{n}:S_{u}\in A\right\},

i.e. the number of particles located in the set
A
.

Let
m:=Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. According to Biggins
[
10
, Theorem 2]
: if
1<m<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0
, then
for
\theta\in[0,1)
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}),~\mathbb{P}-a.s.~\text{on-non extinction},

where
I(x):=\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\{tx-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\}
,
x^{*}=\sup\{x\geq 0:I(x)\leq\log m\}
. So, it’s natural to study the decay rate of

\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}),

where
a\in[0,\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))
.

In fact, there are some related results for the branching Brownian motion
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
: Öz
[
41
]
considered the lower deviation
\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}(\theta x^{*}t+B)<e^{at})
, where
B
is a fixed ball; Aïdekon, Hu and Shi
[
1
]
considered the upper deviation
\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}([\theta x^{*}t,\infty))>e^{at})
. We will see that the for the branching random walk, the strategy to study this problem and the answers will be very different from theirs.

We also mention here that, since the last few decades, the model BRW has been extensively studied due to its connection to many fields, such as Gaussian multiplicative chaos, random walk in random environment, random polymer, random algorithms and discrete Gaussian free field etc; see
[
31
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
1
]
references therein. One can refer to Shi
[
43
]
for a more detailed overview. The large deviation probabilities (LDP) for BRW and branching Brownian motion (BBM) on real line have attracted many researcher’s attention. For example: Hu
[
30
]
, Gantert and Höfelsauer
[
26
]
and Chen and He
[
17
]
considered the LDP and the moderate deviation of BRW’s maximum (for BBM’s maximum, see Chauvin and Rouault
[
14
]
and Derrida and Shi
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
); Chen, He
[
16
]
and Louidor, Perkins
[
37
]
studied the large deviation of empirical distribution. Some other related works include Rouault
[
42
]
, Buraczewski and Maślanka
[
13
]
and Bhattacharya
[
6
]
.

1.2
Main Results

Before giving our results, we need first introduce some notations. Let
|Z_{n}|:=Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
m:=\mathbb{E}[|Z_{1}|]
and
b:=\min\{k\geq 0:p_{k}>0\}
. Recall that
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is the offspring law, and
X
is the step size. In the sequel of this work, we always need the following assumptions.

Assumption 1.1
.

(i)
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
,
\mathbb{P}(X=0)<1

and

\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0;

(ii)
p_{0}=0
,
p_{1}<1
and
\mathbb{E}[|Z_{1}|]<\infty;

(iii)
a\in\left[0,\log m-I(\theta x^{*})\right)
,
\theta\in[0,1).

The first two theorems consider the Schröder case (i.e.,
p_{1}>0
).

Theorem 1.1
.

(Schröder case, light tail) Assume
p_{1}>0
,
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa<0
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]}\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(-\frac{d}{\rho}\right)\right\},

where
\bar{\rho}\in(0,1)
,
d\in[0,+\infty)
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):\log m-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}}{1-\rho}\right)-\frac{a}{1-\rho}\geq 0\right\},

(1.1)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle:=d(\rho)=\sup\left\{h\in[0,+\infty):\log m-I\left(\frac{h+\theta x^{*}}{1-\rho}\right)-\frac{a}{1-\rho}\geq 0\right\}.

When the condition
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa<0
fails, we will see, in the following theorem, the decay scale may depend on the tail of step size.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Schröder case, heavy tail) Assume
p_{1}>0
.

If
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\alpha.

If
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-x)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda x^{\alpha}}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=\begin{cases}-\lambda(1-\theta)x^{*},&a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})];\\
-\lambda\hat{c},&a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*})),\end{cases}

where
\hat{c}\in(0,x^{*}-\theta x^{*})
is the unique solution of

\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c})=a.

Remark 1.1
.

In fact, in the case
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
, the condition
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
can be removed in Assumption
1.1
.

The next two theorems consider the Böttcher case (i.e.
p_{1}=0
). As we can see in the
following, the decay scale depends on the tail of step size
X
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Böttcher case, bounded tail) Assume
p_{1}=0
,
ess\inf X=-L\in(-\infty,0)
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}\left([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty)\right)<e^{an}\right)\right]=\begin{cases}\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b,&a\in[0,a^{*});\cr\bar{c}\log b,&a\in[a^{*},\log m-I(\theta x^{*})),\end{cases}

where
a^{*}:=\left[(\log m-I(x^{*}))\frac{L+\theta x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}+\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b\right]\wedge[\log m-I(\theta x^{*})]
, and
\bar{c}\in(0,1)
is the unique solution of

\log m-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+L\bar{c}}{1-\bar{c}}\right)-\frac{a-\bar{c}\log b}{1-\bar{c}}=0.

Remark 1.2
.

If
I(x^{*})=\log m
, then we have
a^{*}<\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
. In fact, since
I(0)=0
and
I(x)
is convex, we have

I(\theta x^{*})=I((1-\theta)0+\theta x^{*})\leq(1-\theta)I(0)+\theta I(x^{*})\leq\theta\log m.

Thus,

\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b+I(\theta x^{*})<\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{x^{*}}\log m+\theta\log m\leq\log m,

which implies
a^{*}<\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
.

Remark 1.3
.

As we can see there is a phase transition at
a=a^{*}
. This is because
e^{a^{*}n}
is the minimum population size that
b^{t_{n}}
branching random walks located at
-Lt_{n}
can contribute to
[\theta x^{*}n,\infty)
in the remaining
n-t_{n}
time. Thus when
a<a^{*}
, to achieve
\{Z_{n}\left([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty)\right)<e^{an}\}
means there is no particles in
[\theta x^{*}n,+\infty)
at time
n
. Hence, in this case, the lower deviation behaviour is exactly the same as the maximum of BRW (see
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
).

The following theorem considers the step size has Weibull tail. Recall that
\hat{c}
is the unique solution of the equation

\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c})=a.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Böttcher case, Weibull tail) Assume
p_{1}=0
,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-x)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda x^{\alpha}}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
and
\lambda>0
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\lambda C(x^{*},\theta,b,\alpha,m,a),

where

C(x^{*},\theta,b,\alpha,m,a)=\begin{cases}b(x^{*}-\theta x^{*})^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(0,1],~a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]};\cr b\hat{c}^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(0,1],~a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))};\cr\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(x^{*}-\theta x^{*})^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(1,+\infty),~a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]};\cr\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}\hat{c}^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(1,+\infty),~a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.4
.

In fact, using the same idea of proving Theorem
1.4
, one could get the results below for Gumbel step size and Pareto step size.

If
p_{1}=0
,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-x)=\Theta(1)\exp\{-e^{x^{\alpha}}\}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log-\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)}{n^{-\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\log b\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}(x^{*}-\theta x^{*})^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}},&~a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})];\cr\left(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\log b\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}{\hat{c}}^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}},&~a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*})).\end{cases}

If
p_{1}=0
,
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\alpha b.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
and Section
3
, we study the Schröder case, where Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are proved. Section
2
considers the step size has a negative exponential moment. And Section
3
study the case when the step size has heavy tails. Section
4
and Section
5
consider the Böttcher case, where Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
are proved. In Section
4
, the step size is assumed to be bounded below. Section
5
treat the Weibull step size, and this section is divided into two subsections: in the first section, we study the sub Weibull case; in the second section, we study the super Weibull case. As usual,
f(x)=\Theta(1)g(x)
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
means there exist constants
C\geq C^{\prime}>0
such that
C^{\prime}\leq|f(x)/g(x)|\leq C
for all
x>1
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Schröder case, light tail

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.1
. We first present several lemmas.
The following lemma can be found in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
1<m<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0
.

If
\theta\in[0,1)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}),~\mathbb{P}-a.s.~\text{on-non extinction},

where
I(x):=\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\{tx-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\}
,
x^{*}=\sup\{x\geq 0:I(x)\leq\log m\}
.

If
\theta\in(1,+\infty)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))=0,~\mathbb{P}-a.s.

The following remark is a direct consequence of above Lemma.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of numbers such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=1
.

If
\theta\in[0,1)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}([\rho_{n}\theta x^{*}n,\infty))=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}),~\mathbb{P}-a.s.

(2.1)

If
\theta\in(1,+\infty)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Z_{n}([\rho_{n}\theta x^{*}n,\infty))=0,~\mathbb{P}-a.s.

(2.2)

Remark 2.2
.

Biggins
[
9
,
8
]
also studied the a.s. behaviour of
Z_{n}(\theta x^{*}n+B)
, where
B
is a bounded measurable set.

The next lemma is the well-known Cramér theorem; see
[
18
, Theorem 2.2.3]
. Here and later, we define
S_{n}:=X_{1}+X_{2}+...+X_{n}
, where
X_{i},i\geq 1
are i.i.d copies of the step size
X
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(S_{n}<-nx)=-I(-x).

The following lemma considers the asymptotic behavior of
I(x)
. Let
\lambda^{*}:=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}]<\infty\right\}
and
\Lambda(\lambda):=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda X}\right]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The rate function
I(x)
can be classified into the following three cases.

(i) If
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)\uparrow+\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
, then for any
x>0
, there exists some
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
such that

x=\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda),~I(x)=\lambda\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)-\Lambda(\lambda).

Furthermore,
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{I(x)}{x}=\lambda^{*}
.

(ii) If
\lambda^{*}=+\infty
,
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)
converges to some finite limit as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, then
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)\uparrow\text{ess}\sup X
. And

I(x)=\begin{cases}\text{positive finite},&x\in(0,\text{ess}\sup X);\cr-\log\mathbb{P}(X=\text{ess}\sup X),&x=\text{ess}\sup X;\cr+\infty,&x\in(\text{ess}\sup X,\infty).\end{cases}

(iii) If
0<\lambda^{*}<+\infty
,
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)
converges to some finite limit
T
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
, then
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda^{*}X}\right]<\infty
and

I(x)=\begin{cases}\text{positive finite},&x\in(0,T];\cr\lambda^{*}x-\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda^{*}X}\right],&x\in[T,\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

In fact, all the statements above can be found in
[
23
, Sec 2.6]
except
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{I(x)}{x}=\lambda^{*}
. So, we just prove this. Note that, by the former part of (i) for any
x>0
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
such that
x=\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)
. It is easy to see that
x\rightarrow+\infty
implies
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
. Furthermore,
\Lambda(\cdot)
is infinitely differentiable on
(0,\lambda^{*})
(see
[
18
, Exercise 2.2.24]
). Thus, by L’Hospital’s rule,

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{I(x)}{x}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}}\frac{\lambda\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)-\Lambda(\lambda)}{\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}}\frac{\lambda\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)+\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)-\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)}{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)}=\lambda^{*}.

\Box

The next lemma considers the lower deviation probabilities of the branching process, which can be found in
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of numbers such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=+\infty
and for any
t>0
,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}e^{-tn}=0
.

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(|Z_{n}|<a_{n})=\log p_{1}.

Set

\displaystyle f(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\log m-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}}{1-\rho}\right)-\frac{a}{1-\rho},~\bar{\rho}:=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):f(\rho)\geq 0\right\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle g_{\rho}(h)

\displaystyle:=\log m-I\left(\frac{h+\theta x^{*}}{1-\rho}\right)-\frac{a}{1-\rho},~d:=d(\rho)=\sup\left\{h\in[0,+\infty):g_{\rho}(h)\geq 0\right\}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
p_{1}>0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\kappa X}\right]<+\infty
for some
\kappa<0
. Then

(i)
\bar{\rho}\in(0,1)
,
d(\rho)\in(0,+\infty)
for
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho})
.

(ii)
g_{\rho}(d+\varepsilon)<0
for
\varepsilon>0
, and
g_{\rho}(d-\varepsilon)>0
for
\varepsilon\in(0,d).

(iii)
d(0)>0
,
d(\bar{\rho})=0
and
d(\rho)
is decreasing on
(0,\bar{\rho}]
.

(iv)
\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]}\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)\right\}\in(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

Since
f(\rho)
is strictly decreasing on
[0,1)
, and

~f(0)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*})-a>0,~f(1-)=-\infty<0,

we have
\bar{\rho}\in(0,1)
. Fix
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho})
.
It is easy to see that
g_{\rho}(\cdot)
is decreasing on
[0,+\infty)
, and

g_{\rho}(0)=f(\rho)>f(\bar{\rho})\geq 0,~g_{\rho}(+\infty)=-\infty<0,

where
f(\bar{\rho})\geq 0
follows by the fact that
I(\cdot)
is lower semicontinuous. Therefore
d\in(0,+\infty)
and
g_{\rho}(d)\geq 0
. Furthermore,
g_{\rho}(d+\varepsilon)<0
for
\varepsilon>0
. In fact, if
g_{\rho}(d)=0
, since
g_{\rho}(\cdot)
is strictly decreasing, we have

g_{\rho}(d+\varepsilon)<0
. If
g_{\rho}(d)>0
, then
\frac{d+\theta x^{*}}{1-\rho}=\text{ess}\sup X<\infty
, which implies

g_{\rho}(d+\varepsilon)=-\infty<0.

Thus (i) and (ii) hold. For (iii),
d(0)>0
follows by the fact that
f(0)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*})-a>0
and
I(\cdot)
is continuous at
\theta x^{*}
. We obviously have
d(\bar{\rho})=0
if
f(\bar{\rho})=0
. If
f(\bar{\rho})>0
, then
\frac{\theta x^{*}}{1-\bar{\rho}}=\text{ess}\sup X<\infty
. Thus,
g_{\bar{\rho}}(\varepsilon)=-\infty
for any
\varepsilon>0
, which implies
d(\bar{\rho})=0
.
d(\rho)
is decreasing follows by the fact that

g_{\rho^{\prime}}(\cdot)>g_{\rho}(\cdot)~\text{for}~\rho^{\prime}<\rho.

For (iv), since
\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\frac{d(\rho)}{\rho}=+\infty
, by Lemma
2.3
, we have

\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)=\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\frac{\rho}{d(\rho)}I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)d(\rho)>0.

Thus, there exist constants
\delta,~\rho^{*}>0
such that for every
0<\rho<\rho^{*}
,

\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)>\delta.

Furthermore, for
\bar{\rho}>\rho>\rho^{*}
,

\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)>\rho^{*}\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}.

Thus,

\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]}\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)\right\}>\delta\wedge\left(\rho^{*}\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)>0.

(2.4)

On the other hand, since
\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow\bar{\rho}}\frac{d(\rho)}{\rho}=0
, we have

\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow\bar{\rho}}\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(\frac{-d}{\rho}\right)=\bar{\rho}\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}.

(2.5)

As a result, (
2.4
), together with (
2.5
), implies (iii).

\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
: if
p_{1}>0
,
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa<0
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]}\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(-\frac{d}{\rho}\right)\right\}.

For the lower bound, the strategy is to force single births up to time
\rho n
(where
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]
). Then, we move the single particle at time
\rho n
to some position
-dn
(where
d
is chosen such that
Z_{n-\rho n}([dn+\theta x^{*}n,\infty))\approx e^{an}
). Optimizing for
\rho
yields the desired lower bound. The proof of the upper bound goes by showing that the above strategy is optimal, thus we consider a series of intermediate times
\lfloor ni/k\rfloor
,
0<i/k<1
. To argue that sub-BRWs emanating from time
\lfloor ni/k\rfloor
can easily produce
e^{an}

[\theta x^{*}n,\infty)
at time

\lfloor ni/k\rfloor
; each individual at time
\lfloor ni/k\rfloor
locates in a not very low position. This motivates the definitions of the following
\rho_{n}
and
E

Proof.

Lower bound
.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]
. By Markov property, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq p^{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}_{1}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\leq-(d+\varepsilon)n\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\left([(d+\varepsilon+\theta x^{*})n,\infty)\right)<e^{an}\right).

(2.6)

Since
g_{\rho}(d+\varepsilon)<0
, by Remark
2.1
and the dominated convergence theorem, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\left([(d+\varepsilon+\theta x^{*})n,\infty)\right)<e^{an}\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\log Z_{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\left(\left[\frac{(d+\varepsilon+\theta x^{*})n}{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}(n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor),\infty\right)\right)-\frac{an}{n-\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}<0\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\geq 0.9.

Thus, plugging above into (
2.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0.9p^{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}_{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}}{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\leq-\frac{(d+\varepsilon)n}{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\right)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\geq 0.9p^{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}_{1}\exp\left\{-I\left(-\frac{d+2\varepsilon}{\rho}\right)\lfloor\rho n\rfloor\right\},

where the last inequality comes from Cramér’s theorem. As a consequence, for every
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\geq-\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(-\frac{d+2\varepsilon}{\rho}\right)\right\}.

The desired lower bound follows by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and optimizing
\rho
on
(0,\bar{\rho}]
.

Upper bound
. Fix
\delta\in(0,\bar{\rho})
,
k\geq\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}-\delta}
(hence
\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor\geq 1
). Since
d=d(\rho)
is decreasing on
(0,\bar{\rho}]
(see Lemma
2.5
), there exists some constant
C_{\delta}>0
such that for every
1\leq i\leq\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor
,

d\left(i/k\right)>C_{\delta}.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,C_{\delta})
. Set

\rho_{n}:=\sup\{\rho\in[0,1]:Z_{\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\geq n^{3}\};~E:=\left\{\forall u\in Z_{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor},~S_{u}\in\left[-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n,+\infty\right)\right\}.

Let
Z^{u}_{n}
be the
n
th generation of the sub-BRW emanating from particle
u
. By the branching property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an},\rho_{n}\geq\frac{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}{k}\right)+\sum^{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}_{i=1}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an},\frac{i-1}{k}\leq\rho_{n}<\frac{i}{k}\right)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}_{i=1}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{u\in Z_{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}Z^{u}_{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an},~E,~\frac{i-1}{k}\leq\rho_{n}<\frac{i}{k}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(E^{c},~\frac{i-1}{k}\leq\rho_{n}<\frac{i}{k}\right)\right]

(2.12)

\displaystyle~~~+\mathbb{P}\left(\rho_{n}\geq\frac{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}{k}\right).

(2.13)

Let
Z^{j}_{n}
,
j\geq 1
be i.i.d copies of
Z_{n}
. For the first term on the r.h.s of (
2.10
), by the branching property, there exists
N(k,\varepsilon)
such that for all
1\leq i\leq\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor
and

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{u\in Z_{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}Z^{u}_{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an},~E,~\frac{i-1}{k}\leq\rho_{n}<\frac{i}{k}\right)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{j}_{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}\left(\left[\left(\theta x^{*}+d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n,\infty\right)\right)<e^{an},1\leq j\leq n^{3}\right)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}\log\left(Z_{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}\left(\left(\theta x^{*}+d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n+B\right)\right)<\frac{an}{n-{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}}\right)^{n^{3}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-n^{3}},

(2.17)

where the last inequality follows by (
2.1
), the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that (see Lemma
2.5
(ii))

\log m-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+d(i/k)-\varepsilon}{1-i/k}\right)-a>0.

Let
h_{n,i}:={\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}-{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}
. For the second term on the r.h.s of (
2.10
), observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists u\in Z_{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor},~S_{u}\leq-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n,~Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{u\in Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}}Z^{u}_{h_{n,i}}\left(\left(-\infty,-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n\right]\right)>1;~Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{n^{3}}_{j=1}Z^{j}_{h_{n,i}}\left(\left(-\infty,-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n-S^{j}_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\right]\right)>1\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)

(2.20)

\displaystyle\leq n^{3}\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{h_{n,i}}\left(\left(-\infty,-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n-S_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\right]\right)\right]\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right),

(2.21)

where
S^{j}_{n}
,
j\geq 1
are correlated random walks but have the same distribution as
S_{n}
, and the last inequality follows from Markov inequality. Let
\mathcal{F}:=\sigma\left(|Z_{l}|,1\leq l\leq h_{n,i}\right)
, since the branching and motion are independent, we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{h_{n,i}}\left(\left(-\infty,-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n-S_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\right]\right)\right]

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in Z_{h_{n,i}}}{\bf 1}_{\left(-\infty,-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n\right]}\left(S_{u}+S_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\right)\Bigg|\mathcal{F}\right]

(2.23)

\displaystyle=m^{{h_{n,i}}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{\lfloor ni/k\rfloor}\leq-\left(d\left(i/k\right)-\varepsilon\right)n\right)

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq m^{n/k+1}\exp\left\{-I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)-2\varepsilon}{i/k}\right)ni/k\right\},

where the last inequality follows from the Cramér’s theorem. Plugging above into (
2.18
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E^{c},~\frac{i-1}{k}\leq\rho_{n}<\frac{i}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n^{3}m^{n/k+1}\exp\left\{-I\left(\frac{d\left(i/k\right)-2\varepsilon}{i/k}\right)ni/k\right\}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\lfloor n(i-1)/k\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)

(2.25)

\displaystyle\leq n^{3}m^{n/k+1}\exp\left\{(\log p_{1}+\varepsilon)ni/k-I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)-2\varepsilon}{i/k}\right)ni/k\right\},

(2.26)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
. For the third term on the r.h.s of (
2.10
), again by Lemma
2.4
, there exists
N(\rho,\delta,\varepsilon)>0
such that for
n\geq N(\rho,\delta,\varepsilon)
and
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\rho_{n}\geq\frac{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}{k}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\lfloor{n\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}/{k}\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\lfloor n(\bar{\rho}-\delta)\rfloor}\leq n^{3}\right)\leq\exp\left\{(\log p_{1}+\varepsilon)(\bar{\rho}-\delta)n\right\}.

(2.27)

Plugging (
2.14
), (
2.14
) and (
2.27
) into (
2.10
) yields that for fixed
\varepsilon,k
,
\rho
,
\delta
there exists
N(\varepsilon,k,\rho,\delta)
such that for
n>N(\varepsilon,k,\rho,\delta)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}_{i=1}2\exp\left\{(\log p_{1}+\varepsilon)ni/k-I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)-2\varepsilon}{i/k}\right)ni/k\right\}+\exp\left\{(\log p_{1}+\varepsilon)(\bar{\rho}-\delta)n\right\}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(2.29)

\displaystyle\leq-\left[\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor(\bar{\rho}-\delta)k\rfloor}\left\{(-\varepsilon-\log p_{1})\frac{i}{k}-\frac{\log m}{k}+I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)-2\varepsilon}{i/k}\right)i/k\right\}\right]\wedge\left[\left(-\varepsilon-\log p_{1}\right)(\bar{\rho}-\delta)\right].

Let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and
\delta\rightarrow 0
, above yields that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(2.30)

\displaystyle\leq-\left[\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor\bar{\rho}k\rfloor}\left\{(-\log p_{1})\frac{i}{k}-\frac{\log m}{k}+I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)}{i/k}\right)i/k\right\}\right]\wedge\left[\left(-\log p_{1}\right)\bar{\rho}\right]

(2.31)

\displaystyle=-\left[-\frac{\log m}{k}+\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor\bar{\rho}k\rfloor}\left\{(-\log p_{1})\frac{i}{k}+I\left(-\frac{d\left(i/k\right)}{i/k}\right)i/k\right\}\right]\wedge\left[\left(-\log p_{1}\right)\bar{\rho}\right]

(2.32)

\displaystyle\leq-\left[-\frac{\log m}{k}+\inf\limits_{\rho\in[0,\bar{\rho})}\left\{(-\log p_{1})\rho+I\left(-\frac{d}{\rho}\right)\rho\right\}\right]\wedge\left[\left(-\log p_{1}\right)\bar{\rho}\right].

Then, let
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(2.33)

\displaystyle\leq-\left[\inf\limits_{\rho\in[0,\bar{\rho})}\left\{(-\log p_{1})\rho+I\left(-\frac{d}{\rho}\right)\rho\right\}\right]\wedge\left[\left(-\log p_{1}\right)\bar{\rho}\right]

(2.34)

\displaystyle=-\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]}\left\{\rho\log\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\rho I\left(-\frac{d}{\rho}\right)\right\},

where the last equality comes from the fact that

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2
: Schröder case, heavy tail

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
. We first present several lemmas. The first lemma can be found in
[
4
, p40, Corollary 1]
, which considers the asymptotic behaviour of branching process’s generating function.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
0<s<1
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[s^{|Z_{n}|}]}{p^{n}_{1}}=C_{0},

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant depending only on
s
.

The next lemma concerns large deviation probabilities of sums of i.i.d Pareto tail random variables.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha>0
. Then, there exists some constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
x>0
,
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leq-x)\leq C_{{\alpha}}n^{2}x^{-\alpha}.

Proof.

According to
[
39
, Corollary 1.5]
: if
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{t}{\bf 1}_{\{X\leq 0\}}]<\infty
for some
0<t\leq 1
, then

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leq-x)\leq n\mathbb{P}(X<-y)+\left(\frac{en\mathbb{E}[|X|^{t}{\bf 1}_{\{X\leq 0\}}]}{xy^{t-1}}\right)^{\frac{x}{y}}.

(3.1)

Since
\mathbb{P}(X<x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
, there exists some constant
C_{1}>0
such that for all
x>0
,

\mathbb{P}(X<-x)\leq C_{1}x^{-\alpha}.

Therefore,
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{t}{\bf 1}_{\{X\leq 0\}}]<\infty
for
t=\frac{\alpha}{2}\wedge 1
.
Let
y=\frac{t}{\alpha}x
, (
3.1
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leq x)

\displaystyle\leq n\mathbb{P}\left(X<-\frac{t}{\alpha}x\right)+\left(\frac{en\mathbb{E}[|X|^{t}{\bf 1}_{\{X\leq 0\}}]}{(\frac{t}{\alpha})^{t-1}x^{t}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{t}{\alpha}\right)^{-\alpha}x^{-\alpha}n+\left(\frac{e\mathbb{E}[|X|^{t}{\bf 1}_{\{X\leq 0\}}]}{(\frac{t}{\alpha})^{t-1}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}n^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}x^{-\alpha}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq C_{{\alpha}}n^{2}x^{-\alpha}.

\Box

The following lemma gives an upper bound of large deviation probabilities of sums of i.i.d Weibull tail random variables, which is a direct consequence of
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda x^{\alpha}}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive integer-valued numbers such than
t_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{t_{n}}{n}<\infty
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
x>0
, for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}(S_{t_{n}}\leq-xn)\leq e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\lambda x^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}}.

Let
G(c):=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+c)-a
,
\hat{c}:=\inf\left\{c\geq 0:G(c)<0\right\}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(i) If
a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]
, then
\hat{c}=(1-\theta)x^{*}
; If
a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
, then
\hat{c}
is the unique
solution of
G(c)=0
on
(0,(1-\theta)x^{*})
.

(ii) If
\varepsilon>0
, then
G(\hat{c}+\varepsilon)<0
. If
\varepsilon\in(0,\hat{c})
, then
G(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)>0
.

Proof.

If
a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]
, without loss of generality, we assume
\log m-I(x^{*})>0
. By the definition of
x^{*}
, we have
x^{*}=\text{ess}\sup X\in(0,+\infty)
. Thus, by Lemma
2.3
(ii), for
c>(1-\theta)x^{*}
,

G(c)=-\infty
. This, together with the fact that
G(\cdot)
is decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, implies
\hat{c}=(1-\theta)x^{*}
.
If
a\in\left[\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*})\right)
, then

G(0)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*})-a<0,~G((1-\theta)x^{*})=\log m-I(x^{*})-a>0.

Since
G(\cdot)
is continuous and strictly decreasing on
(0,(1-\theta)x^{*})
, thus
\hat{c}
is the unique solution of
G(c)=0
. Thus (i) holds. By above arguments, we can obtain (ii) easily.

\Box

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
: assume
p_{1}=0
,

if
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
as
x\rightarrow\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=-\alpha;

if
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-x)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda x^{\alpha}}
as
x\rightarrow\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)=\begin{cases}-\lambda(1-\theta)x^{*},&a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})];\\
-\lambda\hat{c},&a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*})).\end{cases}

For the lower bound, the strategy is to let the initial particle produces exactly one children and force its children to reach below some

-\hat{c}n
(where
\hat{c}
is chosen such that
Z_{n-1}([\theta x^{*}n+\hat{c}n,\infty))\approx e^{an}
). For the upper bound, we first prove: under the event
\{Z_{t_{n}}([-\hat{c}n,\infty))/|Z_{t_{n}}|\geq 2/3\}
, it is very hard for
\{Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\}
to happen. Thus, the desired upper bound comes from the probability

\mathbb{P}(Z_{t_{n}}((-\infty,-\hat{c}n))/|Z_{t_{n}}|>1/3),

which is very easy to handle by a Markov inequality.

Proof.

Lower bound
. Since
\mathbb{P}(X<-x)=\Theta(1)x^{-\alpha}
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for
x>1
,

\mathbb{P}(X<-x)\geq C_{2}x^{-\alpha}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Markov property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(|Z_{1}|=1,S_{1}\leq-(\hat{c}+\varepsilon)n\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-1}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\varepsilon)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\geq 0.9p_{1}C_{2}(\hat{c}+\varepsilon)^{-\alpha}n^{-\alpha},

(3.5)

where the last inequality follows from the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
G(\hat{c}+\varepsilon)<0
(see Lemma
3.4
). As a result,

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\geq-\alpha.

Upper bound
. Let
t_{n}=\lfloor h\log n\rfloor
, where
h>\frac{\alpha}{-\log p_{1}}
. Recall that
Z^{u}_{n}
is the
n
th generation of the sub-BRW emanating from particle
u
, and
Z^{i}_{n}
,
i\geq 1
are i.i.d copies of
Z_{n}
. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\hat{c})
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n,\infty))>\frac{2}{3}|Z_{t_{n}}|,\sum_{u\in Z_{t_{n}}}Z^{u}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{|Z_{t_{n}}|}Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n,\infty))\leq\frac{2}{3}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\forall 1\leq i<\frac{2}{3}|Z_{t_{n}}|,~Z^{i}_{n-t_{n}}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{|Z_{t_{n}}|}Z_{t_{n}}\left((-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n)\right)>\frac{1}{3}\right).

(3.8)

Note that since
G(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)<0
(see Lemma
3.4
), by (
2.1
) and the dominated convergence theorem, for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)<e^{-1}.

Thus, for the first term on the r.h.s of (
3.6
), by Lemma
3.1
, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\forall 1\leq i<\frac{2}{3}|Z_{t_{n}}|,Z^{i}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{2}{3}|Z_{t_{n}}|\rfloor}\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lfloor\frac{2}{3}|Z_{t_{n}}|\rfloor}\right]\leq 2C_{0}p^{h\log n}_{1}\leq 2C_{0}n^{-\alpha},

(3.11)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
h>\frac{\alpha}{-\log p_{1}}
. Let
\mathcal{G}=\sigma(|Z_{t_{n}}|)
. For the second term on the r.h.s of (
3.6
), by Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{Z_{t_{n}}\left((-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n)\right)}{Z_{t_{n}}}>\frac{1}{3}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{t_{n}}}Z_{t_{n}}((-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n))\right]

(3.13)

\displaystyle=3\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{t_{n}}}\sum\limits_{u\in Z_{t_{n}}}{\bf 1}_{(-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n)}(S_{u})\Bigg|\mathcal{G}\right]\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle=3\mathbb{P}(S_{{\lfloor h\log n\rfloor}}<-(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)n)\leq 3C_{\alpha}(h\log n)^{2}(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)^{-\alpha}n^{-\alpha},

(3.15)

where the last inequality comes from Lemma
3.2
. Plugging (
3.9
) and (
3.12
) into (
3.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\leq 2C_{0}n^{-\alpha}+3C_{\alpha}(h\log n)^{2}(\hat{c}-\varepsilon)^{-\alpha}n^{-\alpha}.

(3.16)

As a result,

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\leq-\alpha.

For the Weibull case, the proof is similar, the mainly changes are to let
t_{n}=\lfloor hn^{\alpha}\rfloor
in the upper bound (where
h>\frac{\lambda\hat{c}^{\alpha}}{-\log p_{1}}
) and use Lemma
3.3
. We feel free to omit its proof here.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.3
: Böttcher case, bounded tail

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.3
. We first present a lemma. Recall that

a^{*}=\left[(\log m-I(x^{*}))\frac{L+\theta x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}+\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b\right]\wedge[\log m-I(\theta x^{*})].

Let

F_{L}(c):=\log m-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc}{1-c}\right)-\frac{a-c\log b}{1-c}=0.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
a^{*}<\log m-I(\theta x^{*})
. Then for any
a\in\left(a^{*},\log m-I(\theta x^{*})\right)
,

(i) the equation
F_{L}(c)=0
has a unique solution on
\left(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\right)
, denoted by
\bar{c}(L)
(or
\bar{c}
);

(ii)
\bar{c}(L)
is continuous w.r.t
L
, and

F_{L}(\bar{c}(L)-\delta)>0~\text{for}~\delta\in(0,\bar{c}(L));~F_{L}(\bar{c}(L)+\delta)<0~\text{for}~\delta>0.

(4.1)

Proof.

It is easy to see that
F_{L}(c)=\log m-\log b-I\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc}{1-c}\right)+\frac{\log b-a}{1-c}
, and
F_{L}(\cdot)
is differentiable on
(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}})
(since
\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc}{1-c}<x^{*}
). Thus,

F_{L}^{\prime}(c)=-\frac{1}{(1-c)^{2}}\left[I^{\prime}\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc}{1-c}\right)\left(L+\theta x^{*}\right)+a-\log b\right].

Since
I^{\prime}(x)
is increasing,
I^{\prime}\left(\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc}{1-c}\right)
is increasing w.r.t
c
on
(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}})
. This implies the monotonicity of
F_{L}(c)
has three cases: (1) increasing on
(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}})
; (2) decreasing on
(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}})
; (3) increasing on some
(0,h)
, then decreasing on
[h,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}})
. Since
F(0)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*})-a>0
, the monotonicity implies that
F_{L}(c)=0
has at most one solution in all these three cases. On the other hand, since
a>a^{*}
, we have

F_{L}\left(\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\right)=\log m-I(x^{*})-\frac{a-\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b}{1-\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}}<0.

Hence,
F_{L}(c)=0
has a unique solution on
\left(0,\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\right)
. This concludes (i). For (ii), by implicit function theorem, it is easy to know that
\bar{c}(L)
is continuous w.r.t
L
. (
4.1
) follows be the definition of
\bar{c}(L)
.

\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
: if
p_{1}=0
and
\text{ess}\inf X=-L\in(-\infty,0)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}\left([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty)\right)<e^{an}\right)\right]=\begin{cases}\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}\log b,&a\in[0,a^{*});\cr\bar{c}(L)\log b,&a\in[a^{*},\log m-I(\theta x^{*})).\end{cases}

For the lower bound, the strategy is to force every particle produces
b
offsprings up to time
c^{*}(L)n
. Then let the displacement of every individual before time
c^{*}(L)n
close enough to
-L
. Optimizing for
c^{*}(L)
yields the desired lower bound. For the upper bound, our inspiration comes from the proof of Cramér’s theorem. By the branching property,
Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))
can be bounded below by
\sum^{b^{t_{n}}}_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))
(sums of i.i.d copies of BRW). Thus, by Chebycheff’s inequality, one can get an upper bound. To let this bound tends to zero, we use the dominated convergence theorem (which motivates us to define the following event
E_{n}
and
K_{n}
to satisfy the dominated convergence theorem’s condition).

Proof.

Lower bound.
Let

c^{*}(L)=\begin{cases}\bar{c}(L),&a^{*}<\log m-I(\theta x^{*})~\text{and}~a\in(a^{*},\log m-I(\theta x^{*}));\cr\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}},&\text{else}.\end{cases}

For every
L^{\prime}\in(0,L)
and
\delta\in(0,1-c^{*}(L^{\prime}))
,
let
t_{n}:=\lfloor(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)n\rfloor
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}\left([\theta x^{*}n,\infty)\right)<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(|Z_{t_{n}}|=b^{t_{n}},\forall u\in Z_{t_{n}},S_{u}\leq-L^{\prime}t_{n},Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}\mathbb{P}(X\leq-L^{\prime})^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+L^{\prime}t_{n},\infty))<\frac{e^{an}}{b^{t_{n}}}\right)\right]^{b^{t_{n}}}.

(4.3)

We first consider the case that
a^{*}<\log m-I(\theta x^{*})~\text{and}~a\in(a^{*},\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))
. In this case, since
c^{*}(L)
is continuous w.r.t
L
and
c^{*}(L)<\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}
(see Lemma
4.1
), we have

\lim\limits_{L^{\prime}\rightarrow L\atop\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\theta x^{*}+L^{\prime}(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)}{1-(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)}=\frac{\theta x^{*}+Lc^{*}(L)}{1-c^{*}(L)}<x^{*}.

Hence, for
L^{\prime}
close enough to
L
and
\delta>0
small enough,

\frac{\theta x^{*}+L^{\prime}(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)}{1-(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)}<x^{*}.

As a consequence, applying (
2.1
), for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+L^{\prime}t_{n},\infty))<\frac{e^{an}}{b^{t_{n}}}\right)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+L^{\prime}t_{n},\infty))<\frac{an-t_{n}\log b}{n-t_{n}}\right)\geq 0.9,

(4.5)

where the last inequality follows by the dominated
convergence theorem and the fact
F_{L^{\prime}}(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)<0
(see Lemma
4.1
). Now we consider the case of

a^{*}\geq\log m-I(\theta x^{*})~\text{or}~a\in[0,a^{*})
. In this case, since
c^{*}(L)=\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}
, we have

\frac{(\theta x^{*}+L^{\prime}(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta))}{1-(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)}>x^{*}.

Thus, by (
2.2
), for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+L^{\prime}t_{n},\infty))<\frac{e^{an}}{b^{t_{n}}}\right)\geq 0.9.

(4.6)

Plugging (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) into (
4.2
) yields that

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\geq p_{b}^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}\mathbb{P}(X\leq-L^{\prime})^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}(0.9)^{b^{t_{n}}}\geq\left[0.9p_{b}\mathbb{P}(X\leq-L^{\prime})\right]^{b^{t_{n}}},

where the last inequality follows by the fact that
\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}<b^{t_{n}}
. As a consequence, for
L^{\prime}
close enough to
L
and
\delta
small enough, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\right]\leq(c^{*}(L^{\prime})+\delta)\log b.

So, the desired lower bound follows by letting
L^{\prime}\rightarrow L
and
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

Upper bound.
Let
t_{n}:=\lfloor(c^{*}(L)-\delta)n\rfloor
for
\delta\in(0,c^{*}(L))
. Fix
\epsilon\in(0,1-\log 2)
. By the branching property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-Lt_{n},+\infty))\geq b^{t_{n}},Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{b^{t_{n}}}_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))<e^{an},|E_{n}|\geq\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(|E_{n}|<\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor\right),

(4.9)

where
Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}},~1\leq i\leq b^{t_{n}}
are i.i.d copies of
Z_{n-t_{n}}
and

E_{n}:=\left\{1\leq i\leq b^{t_{n}}:Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))\geq 1\right\}.

Set

K_{n}:=\left\{\forall 1\leq j\leq\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor,Z^{(j)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))\geq 1\right\}.

Observe that, by Stirling’s formula
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n!}{\sqrt{2\pi n}(\frac{n}{e})^{n}}=1
, we have the following upper bound for combinatorial number: for
n
large enough,

\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\leq\exp\left\{-\left[(1-\epsilon)\log(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon\log\epsilon\right]b^{t_{n}}\right\}\leq e^{(\log 2)b^{t_{n}}}.

(4.10)

Note that
F_{L}((c^{*}(L)-\delta))>0
(if
c^{*}(L)=\frac{(1-\theta)x^{*}}{L+x^{*}}
this comes from the definition of
a^{*}
, if
c^{*}(L)=\bar{c}(L)
this follows by Lemma
4.1
(ii)). For the first term on the r.h.s of (
4.7
), for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{b^{t_{n}}}_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))<e^{an},|E_{n}|\geq\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}_{j=1}\frac{1}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}Z^{(j)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))<\frac{e^{an}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor};K_{n}\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\limits_{j=1}\frac{1}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z^{(j)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))<\frac{an-\log\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}{n-t_{n}};K_{n}\right)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\frac{2}{\epsilon F_{L}((c^{*}(L)-\delta))}\left[\frac{an-\log\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}{n-t_{n}}\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor-\sum^{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\limits_{j=1}\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z^{(j)}_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)\right]\right\};K_{n}\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\mathbb{E}^{\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\Bigg[\exp\left\{\frac{2}{\epsilon F_{L}((c^{*}(L)-\delta))}\left[\frac{an-\log\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}{n-t_{n}}-\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)\right]\right\}

(4.15)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~;Z_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)\geq 1\Bigg],

(4.16)

where the second inequality follows by the concavity of
g(x)=\log x
, the third inequality comes from Chebycheff’s inequality. Note that

\frac{\theta x^{*}+L(c^{*}(L)-\delta)}{1-(c^{*}(L)-\delta)}<x^{*}.

Thus, by (
2.1
),

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{\epsilon F_{L}((c^{*}(L)-\delta))}\left[\frac{an-\log\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}{n-t_{n}}-\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)\right]=\frac{2}{\epsilon},~\mathbb{P}-a.s.

Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem and (
4.10
), (
4.11
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{b^{t_{n}}}_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty))<e^{an},|E_{n}|\geq\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor\right)\leq e^{-(1-\log 2)b^{t_{n}}}.

(4.17)

For the second term on the r.h.s of (
4.7
), for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|E_{n}|<\lfloor\epsilon b^{t_{n}}\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tbinom{b^{t_{n}}}{\lfloor(1-\epsilon)b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}^{\lfloor(1-\epsilon)b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)=0\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq e^{(\log 2)b^{t_{n}}}\mathbb{P}^{\lfloor(1-\epsilon)b^{t_{n}}\rfloor}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}\left([\theta x^{*}n+Lt_{n},\infty)\right)=0\right)

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\left[(1-\epsilon)-\log 2\right]b^{t_{n}}},

(4.20)

where the second inequality follows from (
4.10
), and the third inequality follows from (
2.1
). Plugging (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) into (
4.7
) yields that for
\epsilon\in(0,1-\log 2)
and
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\leq e^{-(1-\log 2)b^{t_{n}}}+e^{-\left[(1-\epsilon)-\log 2\right]b^{t_{n}}}\leq 2e^{-\left[(1-\epsilon)-\log 2\right]b^{t_{n}}}.

Therefore,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\right]\geq(c^{*}(L)-\delta)\log b.

So, the desired upper bound follows by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

\Box

5
Proof of Theorem
1.4
: Böttcher case, Weibull tail

In this section, we assume
p_{1}=0
and
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-z)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda z^{\alpha}}
as
z\rightarrow\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
and
\lambda>0
. This section will be divided into two sub sections. In the first subsection, we consider the sub Weibull case (i.e.
\alpha\in(0,1]
). In the second subsection, we consider the super Weibull case (i.e.
\alpha>1
).

5.1
Sub Weibull tail:
\alpha\in(0,1]

In this subsection, we consider the sub Weibull tail case, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-z)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda z^{\alpha}}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We are going to prove the following:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)=\begin{cases}-\lambda b(x^{*}-\theta x^{*})^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(0,1],~a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]};\cr-\lambda b\hat{c}^{\alpha},&{\alpha\in(0,1],~a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))},\end{cases}

where
\hat{c}
is the unique solution of
\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c})=a.

For the lower bound, the strategy is to let the initial particle produces exactly
b
children and force these children to reach below

-\hat{c}n
(where
\hat{c}
is chosen such that
Z_{n-1}([\theta x^{*}n+\hat{c}n,\infty))\approx e^{an}
). For the upper bound, we first prove: under the event
\{Z_{t_{n}}([-\hat{c}n,\infty))\geq b^{t_{n}}\}
, it is very hard for
\{Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\}
to happen. Thus, the leading order of
\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)
comes from the probability
\mathbb{P}(Z_{t_{n}}([-\hat{c}n,\infty))<b^{t_{n}})
, which has been already considered in
[
17
]
.

Proof.

Lower bound.
Recall that
G(c)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+c)-a
,
\hat{c}=\inf\left\{c\geq 0:G(c)<0\right\}
. We first consider the case

a>\log m-I(x^{*})
. In this case, we have shown (see Lemma
3.4
):

\hat{c}~\text{is the unique solution of}~G(c)=0~\text{on}~(0,(1-\theta)x^{*});~G(\hat{c}+\delta)<0~\text{for}~\delta>0.

Since
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-z)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda z^{\alpha}}
, there exists some constant
C_{4}>0
such that for all
z>0
,

\mathbb{P}(X\leq-z)\geq C_{4}e^{-\lambda z^{\alpha}}.

(5.1)

The branching property gives, for any
\delta>0
and
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1}=b,\forall u\in Z_{1},S_{u}\leq-(\hat{c}+\delta)n,\sum^{b}_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-1}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\delta)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1}=b,\forall u\in Z_{1},S_{u}\leq-(\hat{c}+\delta)n\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{(i)}_{n-1}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\delta)n,\infty))<e^{an}/b,~\forall 1\leq i\leq b\right)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}C^{b}_{4}e^{-\lambda(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-1}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\delta)n,\infty))<e^{an}/b\right)^{b}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}C^{b}_{4}e^{-\lambda(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log Z_{n-1}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\delta)n,\infty))}{n-1}<\frac{an-\log b}{n-1}\right)^{b}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\geq 0.9p_{b}C^{b}_{4}e^{-\lambda(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b},

(5.6)

where the last inequality follows by (
2.1
) and the fact
G(\hat{c}+\delta)<0
. Thus, for any
\delta>0
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)\geq-\lambda(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}b,

So the desired lower bound follows by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

Upper bound.
Let
t_{n}:=\lfloor\frac{2\alpha}{\log b}\log n\rfloor
. Fix
\delta\in(0,\hat{c})
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))\geq b^{t_{n}-1},~Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))<b^{t_{n}-1}\right).

For the first term on the right hand side above, note that by the branching property, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,+\infty))\geq b^{t_{n}-1},~Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum^{b^{t_{n}-1}}_{k=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log Z_{n-t_{n}}([(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))}{n-t_{n}}<\frac{an}{{n-t_{n}}}\right)^{b^{t_{n}-1}}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq(0.9)^{b^{t_{n}-1}},

where the last inequality follows by (
2.1
) and the fact that
G(\hat{c}-\delta)>0
(see Lemma
3.4
). For the second term, since
|Z_{1}|\geq b
, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
n
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))<b^{t_{n}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\forall u\in Z_{1},Z^{u}_{t_{n}-1}\left(\left(-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\delta)n\right)\right)\geq 1\right)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}-1}\left(\left(-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\delta)n-X^{\prime}\right)\right)\geq 1\right)^{b}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{t_{n}-1}\left(\left(-\infty,-(\hat{c}-\delta)n-X^{\prime}\right)\right)\right]^{b}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\left[m^{(t_{n}-1)}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{t_{n}}\leq-(\hat{c}-\delta)n\right)\right]^{b}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq m^{b(t_{n}-1)}e^{-\lambda(1-\epsilon)(\hat{c}-\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b},

(5.14)

where
X^{\prime}
is a copy of
X
and independent of
Z_{t_{n}-1}
and the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.3
. Hence, for
\delta\in(0,\hat{c})
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\leq m^{b(t_{n}-1)}e^{-\lambda(1-\epsilon)(\hat{c}-\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b}+(0.9)^{b^{t_{n}}}\leq 2m^{b(t_{n}-1)}e^{-\lambda(1-\epsilon)(\hat{c}-\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}b},

which implies the desired upper bound.

Now, it remains to deal with the case that
\log m-I(x^{*})>0
and
a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]
. In this case,
\hat{c}=(1-\theta)x^{*}
. The proof is basically the same as above. The only difference is to replace (
2.1
) with (
2.2
).

\Box

5.2
Super Weibull tail:
\alpha>1

In this subsection, we consider the super Weibull tail case, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq-z)=\Theta(1)e^{-\lambda z^{\alpha}}
for some
\alpha\in(1,+\infty)
. We are going to prove the following:

if
a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)=\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(x^{*}-\theta x^{*})^{\alpha};

if
a\in(\log m-I(x^{*}),\log m-I(\theta x^{*}))
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)=\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}\hat{c}^{\alpha}.

The proof of the lower bound that is given below uses a method similar to that of
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
. We force the branching random walk to behave a
b
-regular tree up to some time
t_{n}=\Theta(1)\log n
. Then, we let each individual on this regular tree to make displacement smaller than some
-a_{k}
(where
k
is the generation of the individual). Obviously, to make the lower bound as large as possible,
\{a_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
should be the solution of

\inf\limits_{\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{i=1}a_{k}=\hat{c}n\atop a_{k}\geq 0,k\geq 1}\sum^{t_{n}}_{k=1}a^{\alpha}_{k}b^{k}.

This help us to find the right
\{a_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. For the upper bound, we use the same method as sub Weibull case.

Proof.

Lower bound.
Recall that
G(c)=\log m-I(\theta x^{*}+c)-a
,
\hat{c}=\inf\left\{c\geq 0:G(c)<0\right\}
. Again, we first consider the case

a>\log m-I(x^{*})
. Set
t_{n}:=\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{2\log b}\log n\rfloor
,
b_{\alpha}:=b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}
and
a_{k}:=\frac{b_{\alpha}-1}{b^{k}_{\alpha}}(\hat{c}+\delta)n
, where
\delta
is a positive constant. By simple calculation, for
n
large enough,

(\hat{c}+\delta/2)n\leq\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}a_{k}\leq(\hat{c}+\delta)n,~\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}a^{\alpha}_{k}b^{k}\leq\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}.

(5.15)

Denote by
X_{u}
the displacement of particle
u
. By the branching property, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(5.16)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(|Z_{t_{n}}|=b^{t_{n}};\forall u\in Z_{i},1\leq i\leq t_{n},X_{u}\leq-a_{|u|};\sum^{b^{t_{n}}}\limits_{i=1}Z^{(i)}_{n-t_{n}}\left(\left[\theta x^{*}n+\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}a_{k},\infty\right)\right)<e^{an}\right)

(5.17)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}\prod^{t_{n}}_{k=1}\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\leq-a_{k})^{b^{k}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}\left(\left[\theta x^{*}n+\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}a_{k},\infty\right)\right)<\frac{e^{an}}{b^{t_{n}}}\right)\right]^{b^{t_{n}}}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}C^{\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}b^{k}}_{4}\exp\left\{-\lambda\sum^{t_{n}}_{k=1}a^{\alpha}_{k}b^{k}\right\}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n-t_{n}}\log Z_{n-t_{n}}\left(\left[\theta x^{*}n+(\hat{c}+\delta/2)n,\infty\right)\right)<\frac{an-t_{n}\log b}{n-t_{n}}\right)\right]^{b^{t_{n}}},

(5.19)

where
C_{4}
is defined in (
5.1
). Since (
2.1
) and
G(\hat{c}+\delta/2)<0
(see Lemma
3.4
), for
n
large enough, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log Z_{n-t_{n}}\big(\left[(\theta x^{*}+\hat{c}+\delta/2)n,\infty\right)\big)}{n-t_{n}}<\frac{an-t_{n}\log b}{n-t_{n}}\right)\geq 0.9.

(5.20)

So, plugging (
5.15
) and (
5.20
) into (
5.16
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}(\theta x^{*}n+B)<e^{an}\right)

\displaystyle\geq p_{b}^{\sum^{t_{n}-1}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}}C^{\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=1}b^{k}}_{4}(0.9)^{b^{t_{n}}}\exp\left\{-\lambda\sum^{t_{n}}_{k=1}a^{\alpha}_{k}b^{k}\right\}^{b^{t_{n}}}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\geq(0.9C_{4}p_{b})^{b^{t_{n}+1}}\exp\left\{-\lambda\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}\right\}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\geq(0.9C_{4}p_{b})^{bn^{\alpha/2}}\exp\left\{-\lambda\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}\right\},

(5.23)

where the second inequality use the fact
\sum^{t_{n}}\limits_{k=0}b^{k}<b^{t_{n}+1}
. Thus, for every small
\delta>0
, we have

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty)<e^{an}\right)\geq-\lambda\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(\hat{c}+\delta)^{\alpha}.

The desired lower bound finally follows by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

Upper bound.
The upper bound is similar to the sub Weibull tail case. Set
t_{n}:=\lfloor\frac{2\alpha}{\log b}\log n\rfloor
,
\delta_{n}:=\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{2\log b}\log n\rfloor
. For
\delta\in(0,\hat{c})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))\geq b^{t_{n}-\delta_{n}},~Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))<b^{t_{n}-\delta_{n}}\right).

For the first term on the r.h.s above, note that by the branching property and (
2.1
), we have

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,\infty))\geq{b^{t_{n}-\delta_{n}}},~Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,\infty))<e^{an}\right)\leq(0.9)^{b^{t_{n}-\delta_{n}}}.

For the second term,
[
17
, (3.36)]
gives that for any
\epsilon>0
and
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t_{n}}([-(\hat{c}-\delta)n,+\infty))<b^{t_{n}-\delta_{n}}\right)\leq\exp\left\{-\lambda(1-\epsilon)\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(1-b^{-\delta_{n}})^{1+\alpha}(\hat{c}-\delta)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}\right\}.

Thus, for any small
\epsilon>
and
\delta>0
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([\theta x^{*}n,+\infty))<e^{an}\right)\geq-\lambda(1-\epsilon)\left(b^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1\right)^{\alpha-1}(\hat{c}-\delta)^{\alpha},

which implies the result.

It remains to treat the case of
\log m-I(x^{*})>0
and
a\in[0,\log m-I(x^{*})]
. In this case, one can show that
\hat{c}=(1-\theta)x^{*}
. The proof is basically the same as above. The only difference is to replace (
2.1
) with (
2.2
).

Twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras and 3 3 -NS-Lie algebras
Taoufik Chtioui , Atef Hajjaji , Sami Mabrouk and Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 6, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to introduce twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras. We define a cohomology of a twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator T T as the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a certain 3 3 -Lie algebra induced by T T with coefficients in a suitable representation. Then we consider infinitesimal and formal deformations of twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators from cohomological points of view. Furthermore, we introduce and study 3 3 -NS-Lie- algebras as the underlying structure of twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras. Finally, we investigate twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras induced by Lie algebras.

\mathcal{O}
-operator, cohomology, deformation,
NS-Lie algebra,

3

Introduction

A natural generalization of binary operations appeared first when Cayley studied cubic matrices which are ternary operations. Furthermore one may consider in general
n
-ary operations of associative type or Lie type. In particular,

3
-Lie algebras
and more
generally,
n
-Lie algebras
[
22
]
are generalizations of Lie algebras to ternary and
n
-ary cases. Recall that a

3
-Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space together with a skew-symmetric 3-linear
map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},
such that for
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq 5
, the following Filippov-Jacobi Identity (sometimes called fundamental identity or Nambu identity)
holds

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

(1.1)

The first instances of ternary Lie algebras are related to Nambu Mechanics
[
30
]
, which was formulated algebraically by Takhtajan
[
37
]
. The first complete algebraic study of
n
-Lie algebras is due to Filippov
[
22
]
. We refer to
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
for the realizations
and classifications of
3
-Lie algebras and
n
-Lie algebras. Ternary operations turn to be useful in many Mathematics and Physics domains, like string theory. The quantization of the Nambu brackets in
[
8
]
was a motivation to present a general construction of
(n+1)
-Lie algebras induced by
n
-Lie algebras using the
n
-ary brackets and trace-like linear forms, see
[
6
,
7
,
26
]
. The structure of
3
-Lie (super)algebras induced by Lie (super)algebras, classification of
3
-Lie algebras and application to constructions of B.R.S. algebras have been considered in
[
2
,
3
,
1
]
.

A deformation theory based on one-parameter formal power series Deformations was introduced first by Gerstenhaber
[
23
]

for associative algebras and then extends to Lie algebras by Nijenhuis and Richardson in
[
31
]
. It is shown that deformations are controlled by suitable cohomologies, Hochschild cohomology in associative case and Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology in Lie case. The same approach was used for various algebraic structures. Deformations of 3-Lie algebras were studied in
[
21
]
. Cohomology is a main tool for deformation theory and provides also invariants to study algebraic structures.

In
[
11
]
, the authors studied the solutions of 3-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation, that lead to introduce the notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator on 3-Lie algebras with respect to a representation. In particular, Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras, introduced in
[
12
]
, are
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a 3-Lie algebra with respect to the adjoint representation.

Twisted Rota-Baxter operators introduced by Uchino in the context of associative algebras
[
38
]
are algebraic analogue of twisted Poisson structure introduced and studied in
[
34
,
27
]
. They are also related to NS-algebras considered by Leroux
[
28
]
.
Twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras were studied in
[
18
,
20
]
. A cohomology of twisted Rota-Baxter operator was derived, in
[
18
,
19
]
, from a suitable
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are given
by twisted Rota-Baxter operators. Such a cohomology can be seen as the Hochschild (resp. Chevalley-Eilenberg) cohomology of a certain Lie algebra with coefficients in a suitable representation. A cohomology
of a twisted relative Rota-Baxter operator as the Loday-Pirashvili
cohomology of a certain Leibniz algebra was constructed in
[
20
]
. One may see also in
[
14
,
25
]
for Hom version of Nijenhuis Bracket and cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras.

The main purpose of this paper is to study twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on 3-Lie algebras. We provide some characterizations and key constructions. We define a cohomology of twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators that controls their deformations. Moreover, we introduce a new ternary algebraic structure called 3-NS-Lie algebras that are related to
twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators in the same way as 3-pre-Lie algebras are related to
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Finally we provide a relationship between the theories for binary structures and ternary structures through the approach using trace-like maps.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly recall basics about representations and cohomology of 3-Lie algebras. Then
we introduce
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on 3-Lie algebras, provide some examples and characterization results.
Section 3 is devoted to define the
cohomology of a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a 3-Lie algebra using the underlying
3-Lie algebra of a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator.
In Section 4, we study deformations
of twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators and show that they are controlled by the cohomology theory established in
Section 3. In Section 5 we introduce 3-NS-Lie algebras which are derived naturally from twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators. In the last section, we investigate twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on
3
-Lie algebras induced by Lie algebras along trace-like maps and construct
3
-NS-Lie algebras from NS-Lie algebras.

In this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field

of characteristic
0
and all the vector spaces are over
\mathbb{K}

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra,
V
be a vector space and
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow gl(V)
be a linear map. The pair
(V,\rho)
is called a representation (or
V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module) of
\mathfrak{g}
if
\rho
satisfies for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})=\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})=\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},x_{1})\rho(x_{2},x_{4}).

(2.2)

Set

\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)=Hom(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}\wedge\mathfrak{g},V),\;(n\geq 1)

the space of
n
-cochains.

Consider the differential
\partial:\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g};V)
defined by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\rho(x_{2n+1},x_{2n-1})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x_{2n})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\rho(x_{2n},x_{2n+1})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\rho(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x_{2k-1}},\widehat{x_{2k}},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+1}(-1)^{k}f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x_{2k-1}},\widehat{x_{2k}},\cdots,[x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

(2.3)

for all
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Thus
(\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V),\partial)
is a cochain complex which is called Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of 3-Lie algebras.

The quotient space
H_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)=Z_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)\diagup B_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)
, where
Z_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)=\{f\in\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)|\;\partial f=0\}
is the space of
n
-cocycles and
B_{3Lie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)=\{f=\partial g|\;g\in\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g};V)\}
is the space of
n
-coboundaries, is called the cohomology group of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
V
.

Let

\Theta\in\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{2}(\mathfrak{g};V)
be a
2
-cocycle in the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex. That is
\Theta:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V

is a trilinear map satisfying

\displaystyle\Theta(x_{1},x_{2},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\Theta(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})-\Theta([x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}]_{\mathfrak{g}},y_{2},y_{3})

\displaystyle-\Theta(y_{1},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},y_{3})-\Theta(y_{1},y_{2},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\rho(y_{2},y_{3})\Theta(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1})

\displaystyle-\rho(y_{3},y_{1})\Theta(x_{1},x_{2},y_{2})-\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\Theta(x_{1},x_{2},y_{3})=0

(2.4)

for
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Under the above notations, the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
carries a
3
-Lie algebra structure given by

[(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)]_{\Theta}=\Big([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(z,x)v+\rho(y,z)u+\Theta(x,y,z)\Big),

(2.5)

which is called the
\Theta
-twisted semi-direct product, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V
.

Definition 2.1
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator if
T
satisfies

[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big)

(2.6)

for all
u,v,w\in V
.

Using the twisted
semi-direct product, one can characterize twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators by their
graphs.

Proposition 2.1
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator if and only if the graph
Gr(T)=\{(Tu,u)|\;u\in V\}
is a subalgebra of the

\Theta
-twisted semi-direct product
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V
.

Proof.

Let
(Tu,u)
,
(Tv,v)
and
(Tw,w)

\in Gr(T)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\;[(Tu,u),(Tv,v),(Tw,w)]_{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\Big([Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big).

Assume that
Gr(T)
is a subalgebra of the

\Theta
-twisted semi-direct product
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V
, then we have

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big).

On the other hand, if
T
be a
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\;[(Tu,u),(Tv,v),(Tw,w)]_{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\Big(T\big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)\big),

\displaystyle\quad\;\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big)\in Gr(T).

Hence
Gr(T)
is a subalgebra of the

\Theta
-twisted semi-direct product
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V
.
∎

Since
Gr(T)
is isomorphic to
V
as a vector space by the identification
(T(u),u)\cong u
. A

\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
induces a
3
-Lie algebra structure on
V
given by

[u,v,w]_{T}=\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw).

(2.7)

It is obvious that
T
is an algebra morphism, that is
T([u,v,w]_{T})=[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
T^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

satisfying

\displaystyle\psi(\rho(x,y)u)=\rho^{\prime}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\psi(u),\;\forall x,y\in g,\;u\in V,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\psi\circ\Theta=\Theta^{\prime}\circ(\phi\otimes\phi\otimes\phi),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\phi\circ T=T^{\prime}\circ\psi.

(2.10)

Example 2.2
.

Any
\mathcal{O}
-operator (in particular, Rota-Baxter operator of weight

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a

By applying

.

Let

be a Nijenhuis operator on a

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=N\Big([Nx,Ny,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Nx,y,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Ny,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}

In this case,

\displaystyle+[x,Ny,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. For any
2
-cocycle
\Theta\in\mathfrak{C}_{3Lie}^{2}(\mathfrak{g};V)
and
1
-cochain

\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V\cong\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta+\partial\theta}V.

Proof.

Define
\psi_{\theta}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta}V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Theta+\partial\theta}V
by
\psi_{\theta}(x,u)=(x,u-\theta(x))
, for
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta}([(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)]_{\Theta})

\displaystyle=\Big([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(z,x)v+\rho(y,z)u+\Theta(x,y,z)-\theta([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(z,x)v+\rho(y,z)u+\Theta(x,y,z)

\displaystyle-\rho(x,y)\theta(z)-\rho(z,x)\theta(y)-\rho(y,z)\theta(x)+(\partial\theta)(x,y,z)\Big)

\displaystyle=[(x,u-\theta(x)),(y,v-\theta(y)),(z,w-\theta(z))]_{\Theta+\partial\theta}.

This proves the result.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a
\Theta
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator. For any

-cochain

Proof.

Consider the subalgebra

-twisted semi-direct product. Thus by

is a subalgebra. Since the map

is invertible, we have
\psi_{\theta}(Gr(T))
is the graph of the linear map

T\circ(Id_{V}-\theta\circ T)^{-1}
. In this case, it follows from Proposition

\mathcal{O}
-operator.
∎

Next, we give a construction of a new

-twisted

-operator out of an old one and a

Proposition 2.7

Let

Proposition 2.8

Let

-twisted
\mathcal{O}

-admissible

-cocycle. Then the

-Lie algebra structures on

Proof.

Consider the linear isomorphism

\displaystyle[(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)(u),(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)(v),(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)(w)]_{T_{B}}

\displaystyle=\rho(Tv,Tw)(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)(u)+\rho(Tw,Tu)(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)(v)

\displaystyle+\rho(Tu,Tv)(Id_{V}+\theta\circ T)+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)

\displaystyle=\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\Theta(Tu,Tv,Tw)

\displaystyle+\rho(Tv,Tw)(\theta\circ T)(u)+\rho(Tw,Tu)(\theta\circ T)(v)+\rho(Tu,Tv)(\theta\circ T)(w)

\begin{cases}\rho(\mathfrak{X})\Theta(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})+\Theta(\mathfrak{X},T\Theta(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}))=\Theta([\mathfrak{X},z_{1}]_{\mathfrak{g}},z_{2},z_{3})\\

\Theta([\mathfrak{X},z_{1}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[\mathfrak{X},z_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[\mathfrak{X},z_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}})=0,\end{cases}

(4.8)

\begin{cases}T_{1}(u)+[\mathfrak{X},Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(\mathfrak{X})u+\Theta(\mathfrak{X},Tu)\big)+T^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}(u),\\

,

and

From the first condition of (

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)-T^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(\mathfrak{X})u+\Theta(\mathfrak{X},Tu)\big)-[\mathfrak{X},Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}=D_{\Theta}(\mathfrak{X})(u).

Definition 5.1

A

together with trilinear operations

\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}=-\{x_{2},x_{1},x_{3}\},

[\![x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(3)}]\!]=\epsilon(\sigma)[\![x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\!],\;\forall\sigma\in S_{3},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\quad\;\{x_{1},x_{2},\{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}\}=\{\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}^{C},x_{4},x_{5}\}+\{[\![x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\!],x_{4},x_{5}\}

\displaystyle+\{x_{3},\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\}^{C},x_{5}\}+\{x_{3},[\![x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]\!],x_{5}\}+\{x_{3},x_{4},\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}\}\},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad\;\{\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}^{C},x_{4},x_{5}\}+\{[\![x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\!],x_{4},x_{5}\}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1},x_{2},\{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}\}+\{x_{2},x_{3},\{x_{1},x_{4},x_{5}\}\}+\{x_{3},x_{1},\{x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}\}\},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad\;[\![x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{*}]\!]+\{x_{1},x_{2},[\![x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]\!]\}

for all

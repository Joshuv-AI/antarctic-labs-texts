Hom-Leibniz bialgebras and BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebras
Ismail Laraiedh, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
The notion of a Hom-Leibniz bialgebra is introduced and it is shown that matched pairs of Hom-Leibniz algebras, Manin triples of Hom-Leibniz algebras and Hom-Leibniz bialgebras are equivalent in a certain sense. The notion of Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebra is established, their bimodules and matched pairs are defined and their properties and theorems about their interplay and construction are obtained. Furthermore, the concept of BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebras is introduced and discussed, their bimodules and matched pairs are constructed and properties are described. Finally, the connections between all these algebraic structures using 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators are shown.

2
Preliminaries on Hom-Leibniz algebras

In this section definitions and basic facts on Hom-Leibniz algebras are reviewed.

Throughout this article all linear spaces are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
, and
multilinear maps
f\colon V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}\to W
on finite direct products and linear maps
F\colon V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}\to W\text{,}
on finite tensor products of linear spaces are identified in the usual way via
F(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=f(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
.

Hom-modules are pairs
(M,\alpha_{M})
consisting of a

\mathbb{K}
-module
M
over a field
\mathbb{K}
and a linear map
\alpha_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
. A morphism
f:(M,\alpha_{M})\rightarrow(N,\alpha_{N})
of Hom-modules is a linear map

f:M\longrightarrow N
such that
f\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{N}f
.
Hom-algebras are triples
(A,\mu,\alpha)
where
(A,\alpha)
is
a Hom-module and
\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A
is a bilinear map.
If, moreover, the linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfies the multiplicativity,

\alpha(\mu(x,y))=\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y)),
that is if
\alpha
is an algebra endomorphism of the algebra
(A,\mu)
, then the Hom-algebra
(A,\mu(\cdot,\cdot),\alpha)
is called multiplicative.

Definition 2.1

(
[
41
,
61
]
)
.

Hom-Leibniz algebras are triples
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
A
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]
:
A\times A\rightarrow A
and a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfying for all
x,y,z\in A
,

[\alpha(x),[y,z]]=[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+[\alpha(y),[x,z]].\quad\quad\text{(Hom-Leibniz identity)}

(2.1)

If, moreover, the linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
is an algebra endomorphism, meaning that it satisfies also the multiplicativity property

\displaystyle\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],

(2.2)

then the Hom-Leibniz algebra is called multiplicative.

Definition 2.2

(
[
29
,
61
]
)
.

Hom-Lie algebras are triples
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
A
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]
:
A\times A\rightarrow A
and a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfying for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=-[y,x]

(Skew-symmetry identity)

(2.3)

\displaystyle[\alpha(x),[y,z]]

\displaystyle+[\alpha(y),[z,x]]+[\alpha(z),[x,y]]=0.

(Hom-Jacobi identity)

(2.4)

When, moreover, the linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfies multiplicativity (
2.2
), that is when
\alpha
is an algebra endomorphism,
the Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is called multiplicative.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\alpha=id_{A}
, then

(A,[\cdot,\cdot])=(A,[\cdot,\cdot],id_{A})
in Definition
2.1
is a Leibniz algebra. So, Leibniz algebras are a special case of Hom-Leibniz algebras when the twisting linear map is the identity map, that is when the fundamental defining Leibniz identity is not deformed at all.

Examples 2.4
.

Some basic examples of Hom-Leibniz algebras are as follows.

1.

Any Hom-Lie algebra is automatically a Hom-Leibniz algebra as
in the presence of skew-symmetry and the Hom-Leibniz identity implies Hom-Jacobi identity.

2.

Given a Leibniz algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
and a Leibniz algebra morphism

\alpha:A\rightarrow A
, one always get a Hom-Leibniz algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},\alpha)
, where
[x,y]_{\alpha}=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)].

3.

Any Hom-module
(M,\alpha_{M})
, together with the trivial zero bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
, that is when
[x,y]=0
for all
x,y\in M
, is a Hom-Leibniz algebra, called abelian or commutative Hom-Leibniz algebra.

4.

The two-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-linear space
A
with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, the
bilinear operation given by
[e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{1}
and zero elsewhere, and the endomorphism
\alpha
given by
\alpha(e_{1})=e_{1},~~\alpha(e_{2})=e_{1}+e_{2}
define a Hom-Leibniz algebra. This Hom-Leibniz algebra is multiplicative, since

\alpha([e_{j},e_{k}])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha(0)=0=[\alpha(e_{j}),\alpha(e_{k})],\quad(j,k)\neq(2,2)\\
\begin{aligned} \alpha(e_{1})=e_{1}=[e_{2},e_{2}]=[e_{1}+e_{2},e_{1}+e_{2}]\\
=[\alpha(e_{2}),\alpha(e_{2})],\quad j=k=2\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

but it is not a Hom-Lie algebra since the skew-symmetry identity is not satisfied

[e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{1}\neq-e_{1}=-[e_{2},e_{2}].

\displaystyle\beta(l(x)v)

Proposition 2.7

Let

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2}]+(l(x_{1})v_{2}+r(x_{2})v_{1}),

\displaystyle\quad\quad-[\alpha(x_{2})+\beta(v_{2}),[x_{1},x_{3}]+l(x_{1})v_{3}+r(x_{3})v_{1}]^{\prime}

Definition 3.3

Definition 3.4

A quadratic Hom-Leibniz algebra is a Hom-Leibniz algebra equipped with a non-degenerate skew-symmetric invariant bilinear form.

(3.4)

4
Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebras

Definition 4.1
.

Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebras are quadruples
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
A
, bilinear maps
\prec,\succ:A\times A\rightarrow A,
and linear maps
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfying

\displaystyle([x,y])\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)-\alpha(y)\succ(x\succ z),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)+\alpha(y)\prec([x,z]),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec([y,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)+\alpha(y)\succ(x\prec z),

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle[x,y]=x\prec y+x\succ y.

(4.4)

A Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebra is called multiplicative if
the linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
is multiplicative with respect to
\prec
and
\succ
, that is,
for all
x,y\in A
,

\alpha(x\prec y)=\alpha(x)\prec\alpha(y),\quad\alpha(x\succ y)=\alpha(x)\succ\alpha(y).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha)
and
(A^{\prime},\prec^{\prime},\succ^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
be two Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebras. A linear map
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is a Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebra morphism if

\displaystyle\prec^{\prime}\circ(f\otimes f)=f\circ\prec,\quad\succ^{\prime}\circ(f\otimes f)=f\circ\succ\mbox{ and }f\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ f.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha)
be a Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebra. Then the binary operation

[\cdot,\cdot]:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
, given for all
x,y\in A
by

[x,y]=x\prec y+x\succ y,

defines a Hom-Leibniz algebra, which is called the sub-adjacent Hom-Leibniz algebra of
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha)
, and

(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha)
is called a compatible Hom-Leibniz dendriform algebra structure on
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle[\alpha(x),[y,z]]-[[x,y],\alpha(z)]-[\alpha(y),[x,z]]

\displaystyle=[\alpha(x),y\prec z+y\succ z]-[x\prec y+x\succ y,\alpha(z)]-[\alpha(y),x\prec z+x\succ z]

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z)+\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z+y\succ z)

\displaystyle\quad-(x\prec y+x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)-(x\prec y+x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha(y)\prec(x\prec z+x\succ z)-\alpha(y)\succ(x\prec z+x\succ z)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Big(-[x,y]\succ\alpha(z)+\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)-\alpha(y)\succ(x\succ z)\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{Hom-Leibniz dendriform1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Big(\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)-(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\succ([x,z])\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{Hom-Leibniz dendriform2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Big(\alpha(x)\prec([y,z])-(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\succ(x\prec z)\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{Hom-Leibniz dendriform3}}}=0.\qed

Example 4.4

Let

Then

\displaystyle(\alpha^{2}(x)\prec\alpha^{2}(y)+\alpha^{2}(x)\succ\alpha^{2}(y))\succ\alpha^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y)]\succ\alpha^{2}(z)

{}^{\text{($\mathcal{A}$ is Leibniz dendriform)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(x)\succ(\alpha^{2}(y)\succ\alpha^{2}(z))-\alpha^{2}(y)\succ(\alpha^{2}(x)\succ\alpha^{2}(z))

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(x)\succ\alpha(\alpha(y)\succ\alpha(z))-\alpha^{2}(y)\succ\alpha(\alpha(x)\succ\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ_{\alpha}(\alpha(y)\succ\alpha(z))-\alpha(y)\succ_{\alpha}(\alpha(x)\succ\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ_{\alpha}(y\succ_{\alpha}z)-\alpha(y)\succ_{\alpha}(x\succ_{\alpha}z),

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ_{\alpha}(y\prec_{\alpha}z)=

\displaystyle\alpha(x\succ\alpha(y\prec z))

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(x\succ(y\prec z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}((x\succ y)\prec z))+\alpha^{2}(x\succ(y\prec z))-\alpha^{2}((x\succ y)\prec z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}((x\succ y)\prec z))+\alpha^{2}(x\succ(y\prec z)-(x\succ y)\prec z)

{}^{\text{($\mathcal{A}$ is Leibniz dendriform)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}((x\succ y)\prec z))+\alpha^{2}(y\prec[x,z])

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z))+\alpha(\alpha(y)\prec\alpha([x,z]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z))+\alpha(\alpha(y)\prec\alpha(x\prec z+x\succ z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z))+\alpha(\alpha(y)\prec(\alpha(x\prec z)+\alpha(x\succ z)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z))+\alpha(\alpha(y)\prec(x\prec_{\alpha}z+x\succ_{\alpha}z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\succ_{\alpha}y)\prec_{\alpha}\alpha(z)+\alpha(y)\prec_{\alpha}([x,z]_{\alpha}),

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec_{\alpha}([y,z]_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec_{\alpha}(y\prec_{\alpha}z+y\succ_{\alpha}z)

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(x\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z))

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(x\prec[y,z])

{}^{\text{($\mathcal{A}$ is Leibniz dendriform)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}((x\prec y)\prec z+y\succ(x\prec z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}((x\prec y)\prec z)+\alpha^{2}(y\succ(x\prec z))

{}^{\text{(multiplicativity)}}=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z))+\alpha(\alpha(y)\succ\alpha(x\prec z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\prec_{\alpha}y)\prec_{\alpha}\alpha(z)+\alpha(y)\succ_{\alpha}(x\prec_{\alpha}z).

The second assertion follows from

\displaystyle f(x\prec_{\alpha}y)

\displaystyle=f(\alpha(x\prec y))=\alpha^{\prime}(f(x\prec^{\prime}y))=\alpha^{\prime}(f(x)\prec^{\prime}f(y))=f(x)\prec^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}f(y),

\displaystyle f(x\succ_{\alpha}y)

\displaystyle=f(\alpha(x\succ y))=\alpha^{\prime}(f(x\succ^{\prime}y))=\alpha^{\prime}(f(x)\succ^{\prime}f(y))=f(x)\succ^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}f(y).\qed

Proposition 4.6
.

6
BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebras

Definition 6.1
.

A BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebra is a quintuple
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta)
consisting of a linear space
A
on which the operations
\prec,\succ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and
\alpha,\beta:A\rightarrow A
are linear maps satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha,

(6.1)

\displaystyle([\beta(x),y])\succ\beta(z)=\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\succ z)-\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\succ z),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\prec z)=(\beta(x)\succ y)\prec\beta(z)+\beta(y)\prec([\alpha(x),z]),

(6.3)

\displaystyle\alpha\beta(x)\prec([y,z])=(\beta(x)\prec y)\prec\beta(z)+\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\prec z),

(6.4)

where
[x,y]=x\prec y+x\succ y
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta)
and
(A^{\prime},\prec^{\prime},\succ^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
be BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebras. A linear map
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is a BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebra morphism if

\displaystyle\prec^{\prime}\circ(f\otimes f)=f\circ\prec,\ \succ^{\prime}\circ(f\otimes f)=f\circ\succ,f\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ f\mbox{ and }f\circ\beta=\beta^{\prime}\circ f.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebra.

Then,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]=\prec+\succ,\alpha,\beta)
is a BiHom-Leibniz algebra.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle[\alpha\beta(x),[y,z]]-[[\beta(x),y],\beta(z)]-[\beta(y),[\alpha(x),z]]

\displaystyle=[\alpha\beta(x),y\prec z+y\succ z]-[\beta(x)\prec y+\beta(x)\succ y,\beta(z)]

\displaystyle\quad-[\beta(y),\alpha(x)\prec z+\alpha(x)\succ z]

\displaystyle=[\alpha\beta(x),y\prec z+y\succ z]-[\beta(x)\prec y+\beta(x)\succ y,\beta(z)]

\displaystyle\quad-[\beta(y),\alpha(x)\prec z+\alpha(x)\succ z]

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta(x)\prec(y\prec z)+\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\prec z)+\alpha\beta(x)\prec(y\succ z)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\succ z)-(\beta(x)\prec y)\prec\beta(z)-(\beta(x)\prec y)\succ\beta(z)

\displaystyle\quad-(\beta(x)\succ y)\prec\beta(z)-(\beta(x)\succ y)\succ\beta(z)-\beta(y)\prec(\alpha(x)\prec z)

\displaystyle\quad-\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\prec z)-\beta(y)\prec(\alpha(x)\succ z)-\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\prec z)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Big(\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\succ z)-\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\succ z)-(\beta(x)\prec y+\beta(x)\succ y)\beta(z)\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{first cond}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Big(\alpha\beta(x)\succ(y\prec z)-(\beta(x)\succ y)\prec\beta(z)-\beta(y)\prec(\alpha(x)\prec z+\alpha(x)\succ z)\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{second cond}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Big(\alpha\beta(x)\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z)-(\beta(x)\prec y)\prec\beta(z)-\beta(y)\succ(\alpha(x)\prec z)\Big)}_{\text{$=0$ by \eqref{third cond}}}=0.\qed

We call
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta)
the BiHom-Leibniz algebra of
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta),
and the quintuple
(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta)
is called a compatible BiHom-Leibniz dendriform algebra structure on the BiHom-Leibniz algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta)
.

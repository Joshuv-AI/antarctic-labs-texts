Motion of a spinning particle under the conservative piece of the self-force is Hamiltonian to first order in mass and spin Preprint: APS/123-QED
Francisco M. Blanco, Éanna É. Flanagan

Abstract
We consider the motion of a point particle with spin in a stationary spacetime. We define, following Witzany Witzany et al. 2019 and later Ramond Ramond 2022 , a twelve dimensional Hamiltonian dynamical system whose orbits coincide with the solutions of the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon equations of motion with the Tulczyjew-Dixon spin supplementary condition, to linear order in spin. We then perturb this system by adding the conservative pieces of the leading order gravitational self-force and self-torque sourced by the particle’s mass and spin. We show that this perturbed system is Hamiltonian and derive expressions for the Hamiltonian function and symplectic form. This result extends a previous result for spinless point particles Blanco and Flanagan 2023 .

A theoretical issue that arises in the study of binary dynamics is

gravitational wave dissipation is turned off. This has been

(26)

Here
\varphi_{\tau}:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma
is the Hamiltonian
flow associated with the zeroth order Hamiltonian system that takes any point

\tau
units along the corresponding integral curve. Writing
Q^{A}
for
abstract coordinates on
\Gamma
, the flow satisfies the relations

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}\circ\varphi_{\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau+\tau^{\prime}},

(27a)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}\varphi^{A}_{\tau}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}^{AB}\partial_{B}H_{0}.

(27b)

The function
G
is assumed to
satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle G(Q,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(Q^{\prime},Q),

(28a)

\displaystyle{\tilde{G}}(\tau,Q,\tau^{\prime},Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\ \ {\rm as}\ \tau\ {\rm or}\ \tau^{\prime}\to\pm\infty.

(28b)

In Ref.
Blanco and Flanagan 2023
we showed that any pseudo-Hamiltonian
dynamical system of the form (
23
) and (
24
) can be recast as a
Hamiltonian system, with Hamiltonian and symplectic form

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{0}(Q)+\varepsilon\tilde{H}_{1}(Q)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(29a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}_{AB}+\varepsilon\tilde{\Omega}^{1}_{AB}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

(29b)

Here the perturbation to the Hamiltonian is

\tilde{H}_{1}(Q)=\int d\tau^{\prime}{\tilde{G}}(0,Q,\tau^{\prime},Q),

(30)

and the perturbation to the symplectic form is

\tilde{\Omega}^{1}_{AB}(Q)=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q^{A}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Q^{B^{\prime}}}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\chi(\tau,\tau^{\prime}){\tilde{G}}(\tau,Q,\tau^{\prime},Q^{\prime})\right]_{Q^{\prime}=Q}

(31)

where

\chi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\big[\text{sgn}(\tau)-\text{sgn}(\tau^{\prime})\big]/2
.

A more convenient representation of the Hamiltonian system
(
29
) can be
obtained by performing a linearized diffeomorphism on phase
space
Blanco and Flanagan 2023
. Under such a diffeomorphism
parameterized by a vector field
\xi^{A}
, the perturbations to the
Hamiltonian and symplectic form transform as

\displaystyle{\tilde{H}}_{1}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H_{1}={\tilde{H}}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}H_{0},

(32a)

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Omega}}_{1\,AB}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Omega_{1\,AB}={\tilde{\Omega}}_{1\,AB}+(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega_{0})_{AB}.

(32b)

We choose the linearized diffeomorphism to be

\xi^{A}=\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{AB}_{0}\left[{\partial\over\partial Q^{B^{\prime}}}\int d\tau\int d\tau^{\prime}\chi\,{\tilde{G}}(\tau,Q,\tau^{\prime},Q^{\prime})\right]_{Q^{\prime}=Q}.

(33)

This yields for the new symplectic form perturbation

\Omega_{1\,AB}=0,

(34)

and the new Hamiltonian

H(Q)=H_{0}(Q)+\varepsilon H_{1}(Q)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(35)

with

H_{1}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau^{\prime}{\tilde{G}}(0,Q,\tau^{\prime},Q),

(36)

which differs from (
30
) by a factor of
1/2
.

IV
Pseudo-Hamiltonian description of the motion a self-gravitating spinning particle

In this section we cast the motion of a spinning particle including
the leading order self-force and self-torque as a pseudo-Hamiltonian
dynamical system of the type discussed in the previous section. This
will allow us to use the general result discussed there to deduce that
the motion is Hamiltonian.

We start by reviewing the similar
pseudo-Hamiltonian formulation of the motion of a spinless point
particle including the leading order self-force
Blanco and Flanagan 2023
.
For the zeroth order geodesic motion we use phase space coordinates

Q^{A}=(x^{\mu},p_{\mu})
with symplectic form
\Omega_{0}=dp_{\mu}\wedge dx^{\mu}

and Hamiltonian

H_{0}=-\sqrt{-g^{\mu\nu}(x)p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}.

For the first order motion, consider a particle at location
x^{\mu^{\prime}}

with initial 4-momentum
p_{\mu^{\prime}}
. Writing
Q^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})
, we denote
by

\varphi_{\tau^{\prime}}(Q^{\prime})=[x^{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime}),p_{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime})]

the geodesic with initial data
Q^{\prime}
, where
\tau^{\prime}
is proper time. From this geodesic we can
compute the Lorenz gauge metric perturbation

h^{\mu\nu}_{R}(x,Q^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}p_{\mu^{\prime}}p_{\nu^{\prime}}}}\int d\tau^{\prime}G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}[x,{\bar{x}}(\tau^{\prime})]p_{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime})p_{{\bar{\nu}}}(\tau^{\prime}).

(37)

Here the symmetric Green’s function

G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}
is the retarded
Green’s function regularized according to the
Detweiler-Whiting prescription
Detweiler and Whiting 2003
;
Poisson et al. 2011
.
The forced motion of the particle is then equivalent at linear order
to geodesic motion in the metric
g_{\mu\nu}+h_{R\ \mu\nu}
, where
Q^{\prime}

is held fixed when evaluating the geodesic equation and then evaluated
at
Q^{\prime}=Q

Detweiler and Whiting 2003
;
Barack and Pound 2019
.
We can therefore obtain a pseudo-Hamiltonian description of the
dynamics by replacing the metric
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
in the Hamiltonian
with
g_{\mu\nu}(x)+h_{R\ \mu\nu}(x,Q^{\prime})
and expanding to linear
order. We can also specialize to including just the conservative
piece of the self-force, by replacing the regularized retarded Green’s function

G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}
with the average

G^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}

of the retarded and advanced Green’s functions, regularized in the
same way, and replacing the metric perturbation
h_{R\ \mu\nu}

with its conservative piece
h_{\mu\nu}
.

Turn now to the corresponding story for spinning point particles.
For the zeroth order motion we use phase space coordinates

Q^{A}=(x^{\mu},\pi_{\mu},\sigma,\rho_{\sigma},\varsigma,\rho_{\varsigma})
on
\Gamma

defined in Eq. (
20
),
with symplectic form (
22
)
and Hamiltonian (
13
).
This motion is described by the equations of motion (
7
) and is zeroth
order in self-gravity, but contains effects first order in spin.

For the first order motion, consider a particle at location
x^{\mu^{\prime}}

with initial 4-momentum
p_{\mu^{\prime}}
and initial spin

S^{\Lambda^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}
[here the spin variable
S^{\Lambda^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}
should be understood
to be a shorthand for the four variables
\sigma,\rho_{\sigma},\varsigma,\rho_{\varsigma}
defined in Eq. (
20
)].
Writing
Q^{\prime}=(x^{\mu^{\prime}},\pi_{\mu^{\prime}},S^{\Lambda^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}})
, we denote
by

\varphi_{\tau^{\prime}}(Q^{\prime})=[x^{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime}),\pi_{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime}),S^{{\bar{\Lambda}}{\bar{\Sigma}}}(\tau^{\prime})]

the solution to the zeroth order motion and spin evolution
(
7
), where
\tau^{\prime}
is proper time.
We can compute from this zeroth order motion a metric perturbation as
follows. Inserting the stress energy tensor of a spinning point
particle given by Eq. (9) of Ref.
Mathews et al. 2022
into the
linearized Einstein equation gives the Lorenz gauge metric perturbation

\displaystyle h^{\mu\nu}_{R}(x,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\tau^{\prime}G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}[x,{\bar{x}}(\tau^{\prime})]\frac{p_{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime})p_{{\bar{\nu}}}(\tau^{\prime})}{\sqrt{-g^{{\bar{\lambda}}{\bar{\sigma}}}p_{\bar{\lambda}}p_{\bar{\sigma}}}}

(38)

\displaystyle-\int d\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{{\bar{\rho}}}G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}[x,{\bar{x}}(\tau^{\prime})]\frac{p_{{\bar{\mu}}}(\tau^{\prime})e_{{\bar{\Lambda}}{\bar{\nu}}}e_{\bar{\Sigma}}^{\ {\bar{\rho}}}S^{{\bar{\Lambda}}{\bar{\Sigma}}}}{\sqrt{-g^{{\bar{\lambda}}{\bar{\sigma}}}p_{\bar{\lambda}}p_{\bar{\sigma}}}}.

Here barred indices indicate quantities that are evaluated at

x^{\bar{\mu}}(\tau^{\prime})
, and
\nabla_{\bar{\rho}}
acts only on the
second argument of the Green’s function.
The factor of
\sqrt{-{\vec{p}}^{2}}

x^{\mu}

x^{\bar{\mu}}

(

Now it is known that the self-forced and self-torqued motion of the
spinning particle is given at linear order by evaluating the
equations of motion (
7

in the metric
g_{\mu\nu}+h_{R\ \mu\nu}

Q^{\prime}

is held fixed when evaluating the equations and then evaluated
at
Q^{\prime}=Q

Mathews et al. 2022

we can obtain a pseudo-Hamiltonian description of the

making the replacements

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)+h_{R\ \mu\nu}(x,Q^{\prime}),

(39a)

\displaystyle e_{\Lambda}^{\ \mu}

\displaystyle\to

(39b)

13

and expanding to linear

8

To complete the pseudo-Hamiltonian formulation of the dynamics,

(x^{\mu},\pi_{\nu},S_{\Lambda\Sigma})

We start by writing the metric perturbation (

\displaystyle h_{R}^{\alpha\beta}(x,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle h^{\alpha\beta}_{R(m)}(x,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int d\tau^{\prime}G^{\alpha\beta\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\big[x,\bar{x}(\tau^{\prime})\big]\frac{\pi_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau^{\prime})\pi_{\bar{\nu}}(\tau^{\prime})}{\sqrt{-g^{\bar{\rho}\bar{\sigma}}\pi_{\bar{\rho}}\pi_{\bar{\sigma}}}},

\displaystyle h^{\alpha\beta}_{R(S)}(x,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{R}(Q,Q^{\prime})

V
Hamiltonian formulation of the
conservative motion of a self-gravitating spinning particle

In this section we
show that the motion of a spinning point particle
under the action of the first order conservative self-force is
Hamiltonian, by combining
the pseudo-Hamiltonian formulation of the motion derived in
Sec.
IV
with
the general result of Sec.
III
.
To do this we need to read off the function

G(Q,Q^{\prime})
on phase space defined by Eqs. (
25
) and
(
26
), and to verify that it satisfies the required
properties (
28
).

We start by specializing to the conservative sector of the dynamics.
As described after Eq. (
37
) above in the nonspinning case, this
is achieved by replacing in the pseudo-Hamiltonian (
42
)
the regularized retarded Green’s function

G_{R}^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}
with the average

G^{\mu\nu\,{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{\nu}}}

of the retarded and advanced Green’s functions, regularized in the
same way, and replacing the metric perturbation
h_{R\ \mu\nu}

with its conservative piece
h_{\mu\nu}
. Note that this Green’s
function obeys the symmetry property

\displaystyle G^{\mu\nu\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})=G^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}\mu\nu}(x^{\prime},x).

(44a)

Next, by comparing the pseudo-Hamiltonian given by Eqs. (
40
) and
(
42
) with the general form given by Eqs. (
24b
),
(
25
) and (
26
), we obtain for the function

G(Q,Q^{\prime})
on phase space

\displaystyle G(Q,Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}NN^{\prime}\bigg[-2\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\pi_{\sigma^{\prime}}G^{\mu\nu\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

(45)

\displaystyle-2\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}S^{\Theta^{\prime}\Pi^{\prime}}\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\omega_{\sigma^{\prime}\Theta^{\prime}\Pi^{\prime}}G^{\mu\nu\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle-2\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\pi_{\sigma^{\prime}}S^{\Theta\Pi}\pi_{\mu}\omega_{\nu\Theta\Pi}G^{\mu\nu\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+2\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}e_{\Theta^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}e_{\Pi^{\prime}}^{\ \lambda^{\prime}}S^{\Theta^{\prime}\Pi^{\prime}}\nabla_{\lambda^{\prime}}G^{\mu\nu\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+2\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\pi_{\sigma^{\prime}}\pi_{\mu}e_{\Theta\nu}e_{\Pi}^{\ \lambda}S^{\Theta\Pi}\nabla_{\lambda}G^{\mu\nu\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle-N^{\prime\,2}\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}\pi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\pi_{\beta^{\prime}}\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\omega^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\ \Theta^{\prime}\Pi^{\prime}}S^{\Theta^{\prime}\Pi^{\prime}}G^{\mu\nu\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle-N^{2}\pi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\pi_{\beta^{\prime}}\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}\pi_{\rho}\omega^{\rho}_{\ \Theta\Pi}S^{\Theta\Pi}G^{\mu\nu\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})\bigg],

where

N=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{-g^{\alpha\beta}\pi_{\alpha}\pi_{\beta}}}},\ \ \ \ \ N^{\prime}=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{-g^{\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\pi_{\rho^{\prime}}\pi_{\sigma^{\prime}}}}}.

(46)

Because of the symmetry property (
44a

also satisfies the required asymptotic conditions (

It now follows from the result reviewed in section

(

VI
Conclusions

We have shown that the conservative dynamics of the two body problem
in general relativity is Hamiltonian to the first sub-leading order in
the mass and spin of the secondary.
This result may yield useful information for the effective one body framework and thereby aid waveform modeling for comparable mass binary systems for LIGO. Extending this result to the second-order
O(m^{2})
point-particle
self-force is a direction that is currently being explored.

Fractional revival between twin vertices
Hermie Monterde

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a characterization of fractional revival between twin vertices in a weighted graph with respect to its adjacency, Laplacian and signless Laplacian matrices. As an application, we characterize fractional revival between apexes of double cones.



Keywords:
fractional revival, perfect state transfer, twin vertices, adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix, signless Laplacian matrix

Introduction

Continuous-time quantum walks were introduced by Farhi and Gutmann in 1998
[
7
]
, and since then have become central in the study of various types of state transfer in quantum spin networks. In quantum information theory, continuous-time quantum walks that exhibit types of state transfer that allow for the generation of entanglements is considered desirable. One such type of state transfer is fractional revival.

Fractional revival from a mathematical standpoint had been studied under adjacency dynamics
[
3
,
2
]
, Laplacian dynamics
[
5
]
, in association schemes
[
4
]
, abelian Cayley graphs
[
1
]
, threshold graphs
[
11
]
and non-cospectral vertices
[
8
]
. In this paper, we study fractional revival between twin vertices with respect to the adjacency, Laplacian and signless Laplacian matrices.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we review a handful of known state transfer properties of twin vertices and show that proper fractional revival is monogamous when it involves a vertex with a twin. In Section
3
, we provide characterizations (Theorems
2
and
3
) as well as necessary and sufficient conditions (Corollaries
4
and
5
) for fractional revival to occur between twin vertices. In Section
4
, we explore the existence of periodicity, perfect state transfer, and pretty good state transfer between twin vertices that admit proper fractional revival. In Section
5
, we explore what happens to our results in Sections
3
and
4
if we add that
\phi(M,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. We then apply our results in Section
6
to obtain a characterization of double cones that admit proper and balanced fractional revival. The remainder of this section is devoted to relevant graph and matrix theory background, as well as basic notions of state transfer.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
X
is a connected weighted undirected graph with possible loops but no multiple edges, and we denote its vertex set by
V(X)
. We allow the edges of
X
to have non-zero real weights. We say that
X
is
simple
if
X
has no loops, and
X
is
unweighted
if all edges of
X
have weight one. For
u\in V(X)
, we denote the characteristic vector of
u
as
\textbf{e}_{u}
, which is a vector with a
1
on the entry indexed by
u
and
0
’s elsewhere, and the set of neighbours of
u
by
N_{X}(u)
. We also represent the characteristic polynomial of a square matrix
H
in the variable
t
by
\phi(H,t)
. Lastly, we denote the simple unweighted empty, cycle, complete, and path graphs on
n
vertices as
O_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
K_{n}
, and
P_{n}
, respectively.

The adjacency matrix
A(X)
of
X
is defined entrywise as
A(X)_{u,v}=\omega_{u,v}
if
u
is adjacent to
v
, where
\omega_{u,v}
is the weight of the edge
(u,v)
, and
A(X)_{u,v}=0
otherwise. The degree matrix
D(X)
of
X
is the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees of
X
, where
\operatorname{deg}(u)=2\omega_{u,u}+\sum_{j\neq u}\omega_{u,j}
for each
u\in V(X)
. The Laplacian matrix
L(X)
of
X
is the matrix
L(X)=D(X)-A(X)
, while the signless Laplacian matrix
Q(X)
of
X
is the matrix
Q(X)=D(X)+A(X)
. We use
M(X)
to denote
A(X)
,
L(X)
or
Q(X)
. If the context is clear, then we simply write
M(X)
,
A(X)
,
L(X)
,
Q(X)
and
D(X)
as
M
,
A
,
L
,
Q
and
D
, resp.

The
(continuous-time) quantum walk
on
X
with respect to
H
is determined by the transition matrix

U_{H}(t)=e^{itH}.

(1)

The matrix
H
is called the
Hamiltonian
of the quantum walk. Typically,
H
is taken to be
A
,
L
, or
Q
, but in general, any Hermitian matrix
H
that respects the adjacencies of
X
should work (that is,
H_{u,v}=0
if and only if there is no edge between
u
and
v
). If
H=M
and
M
is clear from the context, then we simply write
U_{M}(t)
as

. Since
M
is real symmetric, we can write

in its spectral decomposition as
M=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}E_{j}
, where the
\lambda_{j}
’s are the distinct eigenvalues of
M
and
E_{j}
is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the eigenspace associated with
\lambda_{j}
. If the eigenvalues are not indexed, then we also denote by
E_{\lambda}
the orthogonal projection matrix corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
of
M
. This allows us to write (
1
) as

U(t)=\sum_{j}e^{it\lambda_{j}}E_{j}.

(2)

Note that
U(t)
is a complex symmetric unitary matrix, and so
\sum_{j=1}|U(\tau)_{u,j}|^{2}=1
for any vertex
u
of
X
. For this reason, if
u
and
v
are vertices of
X
, then
|U(\tau)_{u,v}|^{2}
is interpreted as the fidelity (probability) of quantum state transfer between
u
and
v
at time
\tau
. It is known that if
X
is simple and weighted
k
-regular, the quantum walks with respect to
A
,
L
, and
Q
are equivalent up to a global phase, and so they all exhibit the same types of state transfer. Similarly, if
X
is bipartite, then the quantum walks with respect to
L
and
Q
are equivalent up to a local phase, and so they again exhibit the same types of state transfer.

We say that
perfect state transfer
(PST) occurs between
u
and
v
at time
\tau
if
|U(\tau)_{u,v}|^{2}=1
. We say that
u
is
periodic
at time
\tau
if
|U(\tau)_{u,u}|^{2}=1
. We say that
pretty good state transfer
occurs between
u
and
v
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a time
\tau_{\epsilon}
such that
|U(\tau_{\epsilon})_{u,v}|^{2}>1-\epsilon
. We say that
(\alpha,\beta)
-
fractional revival
(FR) occurs from

to

at time
\tau
, where
\alpha=|U(\tau)_{u,u}|
and
\beta=|U(\tau)_{u,v}|
, if
|U(\tau)_{u,u}|^{2}+|U(\tau)_{u,v}|^{2}=1
. If the particular
\alpha
and
\beta
are not important, then we simply say fractional revival. The case
\beta\neq 0
is called

FR, while the case
|\alpha|=|\beta|
is called
balanced
FR. Note that FR is a generalization of PST and periodicity. We sometimes say adjacency PST (resp., periodicity, FR) when we talk about PST (resp., periodicity, FR) whenever
M=A
; similar language applies when
M=L,Q
.

Twin vertices

Two distinct vertices
u
and
v
of
X
are
twins
if (i)
N_{X}(u)\backslash\{u,v\}=N_{X}(v)\backslash\{u,v\}
, (ii) the edges
(u,w)
and
(v,w)
have the same weight for each
w\in N_{X}(u)\backslash\{u,v\}
, and (iii) if there are loops on
u
and
v
, then they have the same weight. Given
\omega,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, a subset
T=T(\omega,\eta)
of
V(X)
with at least two vertices is a
set of twins
in
X
if the vertices in
T
are pairwise twins. That is, each vertex in
T
has a loop of weight
\omega
(which is absent if
\omega=0
), and every pair of vertices in
T
form an edge with weight
\eta
(which is absent if
\eta=0
).

We now state a spectral characterization of twin vertices due to Monterde
[
12
, Lemma 2.9]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
T=T(\omega,\eta)
be a set of twins in
X
. Then
u,v\in T
if and only if both conditions hold:

1.

\mathbf{e}_{u}-\mathbf{e}_{v}
is an eigenvector of
M
, and

2.

the eigenvalue corresponding to
\mathbf{e}_{u}-\mathbf{e}_{v}
is given by

\theta=\begin{cases}\omega-\eta,&\text{if $M=A$}\\
\text{deg}(u)-\omega+\eta,&\text{if $M=L$}\\
\text{deg}(u)+\omega-\eta,&\text{if $M=Q$}.\end{cases}

(3)

The
eigenvalue support
of
u
with respect to
M
, denoted
\sigma_{u}(M)
, is the set

\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\lambda_{j}:E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}\neq\textbf{0}\}.

With respect to
M
, we say that
u
and
v
are
cospectral
if
(E_{j})_{u,u}=(E_{j})_{v,v}
for each

,
parallel
if for each
j
,
E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=c_{j}E_{j}\textbf{e}_{v}
for some constant

, and

if
E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=\pm E_{j}\textbf{e}_{v}
for each

. Note that if
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral, then
\sigma_{u}(M)=\sigma_{v}(M)
and we can write
\sigma_{u}(M)=\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)\cup\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)
, where

\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)=\{\lambda_{j}:E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=E_{j}\textbf{e}_{v}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\lambda_{j}:E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=-E_{j}\textbf{e}_{v}\}.

If
u
and

are twins, then Lemma
1
implies that
\theta\in\sigma_{u}(M)
. Next, we state an algebraic characterization and a useful property of twin vertices (see Lemma 2.10 and Corollary 2.11 in

resprectively).

Lemma 2

Vertices

and

are twins in

if and only if there exists an involution on

that switches

and

and fixes all other vertices. Moreover, twin vertices are cospectral with respect to

The next result states that

is a singleton set whenever

and

are twin vertices that are strongly cospectral

Lemma 3
.

Let

be a set of twins in

, and consider

in (

and

are strongly cospectral, then
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
, and

Combining Lemma

. We also state a result of Kirkland et al. about transition matrices of graphs with twin vertices

Corollary 1
.

Let

. If

|U(t)_{u,u}|^{2}+\left(|T|-1\right)|U(t)_{u,v}|^{2}+\sum_{w\notin T}|U(t)_{u,w}|^{2}=1

for any

. If we add that

for any

From (

Corollary 2

Suppose

The following result is an immediate consequence of Corollary

.

Corollary 3
.

Let

be a vertex of

can only pair with at most one vertex in

Chan et al. have constructed infinite families of graphs where a vertex may be paired with two others for proper FR

Let

3
Fractional revival

Since proper
(\alpha,\beta)
-FR occurs from
u
to
v
if and only if proper
\left(-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\beta}{\bar{\beta}},\beta\right)
-FR occurs from
v
to
u

[

, we may say FR between
u
and
v
in place of FR from
u
to

. In this section, we characterize proper FR between twins. The following result is a consequence of
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
, Lemma

[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 4
.

If proper fractional revival occurs between twin vertices
u
and
v
in
X
, then they are strongly cospectral.

To characterize proper FR between twins, it suffices to consider a set of twins of size two by Lemma
4
and Corollary
2
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T=\{u,v\}
be a set of twins in
X
with
\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\theta,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
, where
\theta
is given in (
3
). If proper
(e^{i\zeta}\cos\gamma,ie^{i\zeta}\sin\gamma)
-fractional revival occurs between
u
and
v
at time
\tau
, then

1.

u
and
v
are strongly cospectral with
\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
and
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
, and

2.

for any
j
and
k
,
\tau(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\equiv 0
(mod
2\pi
)
and
\tau(\lambda_{j}-\theta)\equiv 2\gamma
(mod
2\pi
).

Moreover, the converse holds if and only if
\gamma
is not an integer multiple of
\pi
.

Proof.

Lemmas
3
and
4
prove (1). To prove (2), let
u
and
v
be strongly cospectral twins and
\theta,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}
be the eigenvalues in
\sigma_{u}(M)
with spectral idempotents
E_{\theta},E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}
, respectively, where
\theta
is given in (
3
). If
(\alpha,\beta)
-FR occurs between
u
and
v
, then there is a time
\tau
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
such that

U(\tau)\mathbf{e}_{u}=\alpha\mathbf{e}_{u}+\beta\mathbf{e}_{v}.

(5)

From Lemma
3
,
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
, and so using the fact that the spectral idempotents sum to identity, we get

\begin{split}\alpha\mathbf{e}_{u}+\beta\mathbf{e}_{v}&=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(M)}(\alpha E_{\theta}\mathbf{e}_{u}+\beta E_{\theta}\mathbf{e}_{v})=(\alpha-\beta)E_{\theta}\mathbf{e}_{u}+(\alpha+\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{r}E_{j}\mathbf{e}_{u}.\end{split}

(6)

Combining (
2
), (
5
) and (
6
) then gives us

\alpha-\beta=e^{i\tau\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha+\beta=e^{i\tau\lambda_{j}}

(7)

for each
j
. Solving for
\alpha
and
\beta
, we obtain
\alpha=e^{i\zeta}\cos\gamma
and
\beta=ie^{i\zeta}\sin\gamma
, where
\gamma,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
are given by

\gamma\equiv\tau(\lambda_{1}-\theta)/2\ (\text{mod}\ \pi)\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta\equiv\tau(\lambda_{1}+\theta)/2\ (\text{mod}\ \pi).

(8)

As
\alpha-\beta=e^{i(\zeta-\gamma)}
and
\alpha+\beta=e^{i(\zeta+\gamma)}
, we can rewrite (
7
) as

e^{i(\zeta-\gamma)}=e^{i\tau\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad e^{i(\zeta+\gamma)}=e^{i\tau\lambda_{j}}.

(9)

Now, (
9
) implies that
e^{i\tau(\lambda_{j}-\theta)}=e^{i2\gamma}
for each
j
. Thus, (
9
) holds if and only if the two equations in (2) hold. The last statement is straightforward.
∎

The FR in Theorem
2
is balanced if and only if
\gamma
is an odd multiple of
\frac{\pi}{4}
. Next, we determine necessary conditions for proper FR to occur between twins. In what follows, for strongly cospectral twins
u
and
v
, we let
\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}

\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
, where

,

Corollary 4
.

If
u
and
v
are twins in
X
that admit proper
(e^{i\zeta}\cos\gamma,ie^{i\zeta}\sin\gamma)
-fractional revival at time
\tau
, then
\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)
satisfies the ratio condition. In particular, if

are integers such that
\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}
and
\operatorname{gcd}\{p_{j},q_{j}\}=1
, then

\tau=\frac{2\pi qk}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\equiv qk\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\theta}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)\pi\ (\text{mod}\ \pi)

for some integer
k
, where
q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{2},\ldots,q_{n})
and
q\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\theta}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)
is not an integer.

Proof.

Let
u
and
v
be twins that admit proper FR at time
\tau
. By Theorem

,
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral with
\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
and
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
. The fact that
\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)
satisfies the ratio condition follows directly from
[
3
, Corollary 5.3]
. Now, the equation
\alpha+\beta=e^{it\lambda_{j}}
in (

for all

. Replacing

in the proof of Theorem 4.2 in

]

that satisfies

is

. Thus, the time
\tau
at which FR occurs between

and

must be an integer multiple of

. That is,
\tau=\frac{2\pi qk}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
for some integer

. From (

\gamma\equiv\frac{1}{2}\tau(\lambda_{1}-\theta)\ (\text{mod}\ \pi)\equiv qk(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\theta}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}})\pi\ (\text{mod}\ \pi)
. As proper FR occurs,

is not an integer multiple of

qk(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\theta}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}})
is not an integer, which implies that

is not an integer.
∎

4

The following result, which is a combination of Corollaries 5.8 and 5.11 in

Let

(b)

We now investigate what happens to the results from the previous section if we add that

Corollary 7

Let

Proposition 1
.

Let

\tau_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{g\sqrt{\Delta}}

\Delta>1
is a square-free integer otherwise.

Proof.

As

7
. Thus, the

’s in Corollary

Next, we have the following version of Theorem

On ( t 2 , t 3 ) − (t_{2},t_{3})- Zakharov-Shabat equations of generalized Kadomtsev-Petviashvili hierarchies
Jean-Pierre Magnot ∗, Enrique G. Reyes †, Vladimir Rubtsov ⋆

Abstract
. We review the integration of the KP hierarchy in several non-standard contexts. Specifically, we consider KP in the following associative differential algebras: an algebra equipped with a nilpotent derivation; an algebra of functions equipped with a derivation that generalizes the gradient operator; an algebra of quaternion-valued functions; a differential Lie algebra; an algebra of polynomials equipped with the Pincherle differential; a Moyal algebra. In all these cases we can formulate and solve the Cauchy problem of the KP hierarchy. Also, in each of these cases we derive different Zakharov-Shabat ( t 2 , t 3 ) (t_{2},t_{3}) -equations —that is, different Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equations— and we prove existence of solutions arising from solutions to the corresponding KP hierarchy.



Keywords:
differential associative algebra, formal pseudo-differential operators, Kadomtsev-Petviashvili hierarchy

MSC (2020): 37K10 , 37K20 , 37K30 , 47G30

1.
Introduction

The Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy is an integrable system in an infinite number of
independent variables
(t_{1},t_{2},..)
that contains numerous integrable equations in two and three independent variables. Among them, we mention the Boussinesq equation, the KP equation, the KdV equations and the Gelfand-Dickey flows. Usually, these equations are derived from the equations of the KP hierarchy posed on formal pseudodifferential operators
\Psi DO(A)
with coefficients in the algebra of smooth periodic functions

A=C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R})
, or in the algebra of rapidly decaying functions on the line, or in the
Gelfand-Dickey algebraic framework explained in
[
1
, Chapter 1]
. Classical discussions of this system of equations can be found in
[
1
,
12
]
.
A solution to the KP hierarchy satisfies the so-called Zhakarov-Shabat (ZS) relations, as explained in
[
1
,
3
]
. The ZS relations are zero-curvature equations (and hence they provide one of the justifications for stating that the KP hierarchy is an integrable system), from which the standard KP equation in the
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
variables can be derived.

It is already well-known that it is possible to change the algebra
A
and define a similar KP system on
\Psi DO(A)
even when
A
is not commutative. We mention the paper
[
14
]
by Mulase for a very
general construction of a KP hierarchy on a differential associative algebra, the paper
[
3
]
,
and also Kupershmidt treatise
[
7
]
for a thorough study of the Hamiltonian content of the non-commutative
KP hierarchy. A particular example appears in
[
7
,
5
]
, in which the algebra
A
is the algebra

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{H}),
where
\mathbb{H}
is the skew-field of quaternions.
In these two references we can already see that the equations deduced from the
(t_{2},t_{3})-
ZS equations
can be deeply different from the classical KP equation, see also Section

3.3
below. Other examples of “non-standard” KP hierarchies can be found in
[
20
,
9
,
11
]
; we also mention
[
10
]
for non-standard commutative examples, and

[
4
,
21
,
19
]
as representative works on a KP hierarchy posed on algebras equipped with a Moyal multiplication.

Now, the construction of general solutions to the KP system for differential algebras
A
has been systematically studied in
[
2
,
10
]
: in these references, the issues of existence and uniqueness of solutions, and well-posedness of the corresponding Cauchy problem, are formulated and stated in frameworks as general as possible. But we remark that, strangely, the analogues of the KP equations and their solutions in generalized settings appear to
have been mostly ignored, with very few exceptions (we mention just
[
7
,
5
,
4
,
10
]
). Even some of the authors of the present work, in a first step of investigations, have been doubtful to deduce other
(t_{2},t_{3})-
ZS equations than those already highlighted by other authors. We show in the present work that we can deduce many types of equations: for example, we find first order nonlinear equations, Navier-Stokes-type equations, and many others. To the best of our knowledge, the equations that we present are new.

The paper is organized as follows. We recall old and not-so-old constructions for formal pseudo-differential operators and KP hierarchy on a unital differential (non-graded) associative algebra
A
in section
2
, and then we exhibit the announced examples in section
3
. We remark that the results of
[
2
]
allow us to
state theorems on the well-posedness our non-commutative KP hierarchies, and that these theorems imply that we can prove the existence of a full class of smooth solutions to our non-commutative
(t_{2},t_{3})-
ZS equations. We present these existence results also in Section
3
.

2.
The KP hierarchy of a general differential associative algebra

2.1.
Algebraic setting

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a characteristic zero field and let
(A,+,*,\partial)
be a differential associative algebra
over
\mathbb{K}
. We fix the following framework.

Definition 2.1
.

We define

\Psi DO(A)=\left\{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}\xi^{n}:a_{n}\in A;a_{n}=0\mbox{ for }n>>0\right\}

where
\xi
is a formal variable.

Hereafter we assume that
A
is unital, that is, we assume that there exists
1\in A
such that
\forall a\in A,1*a=a*1=a.
We call the elements of
\Psi DO(A)
“pseudo-differential operators” even though
we are aware that we are incurring in a slight abuse of terminology.

Definition 2.2
.

Let us define addition and multiplication on
\Psi DO(A)
.

\begin{array}[]{cccc}+\,:&\Psi DO(A)\times\Psi DO(A)&\longrightarrow&\Psi DO(A)\\
&(\sum a_{n}\xi^{n},\sum b_{m}\xi^{m})&\longmapsto&\sum(a_{p}+b_{p})\xi^{p}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{cccc}*\,:&\Psi DO(A)\times\Psi DO(A)&\longrightarrow&\Psi DO(A)\\
&\displaystyle\left(a(\xi)=\sum a_{n}\xi^{n}\,,\,b(\xi)=\sum b_{m}\xi^{m}\right)&\longmapsto&\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(a_{n}\partial^{\alpha}b_{m})(D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\xi^{n})\xi^{m}\end{array}

Theorem 2.3
.

(\Psi DO(A),+,*)
is a unital (associative)
\mathbb{K}-
algebra.

This theorem is well-known, see for example
[
1
]
, or the more recent paper
[
2
]
. Hereafter we omit the multiplication symbol
*
or we use simply “
\cdot
” .
We also point out that
\Psi DO(A)
is a
graded
algebra with
deg_{\xi}(\sum a_{n}\xi^{n})=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:a_{n}\neq 0\}
and (by convention)
deg_{\xi}(0)=-\infty
. This grading allows us to define the vector subspaces

\Psi DO^{k}(A)=\{P\in\Psi DO(A):deg_{\xi}(P)\leq k\}
, which will appear below.

Proposition 2.4
.

The decomposition
\Psi DO(A)=DO(A)\oplus IO(A)
, in which
IO(A)=\Psi DO^{-1}(A)
is the algebra of integral operators and
DO(A)=\Psi DO(A)\;\setminus\;\Psi DO^{-1}(A)
is the subalgebra of differential operators, is a vector space splitting. We note that
IO(A)
may be a
non-unital algebra.

Proof.

The proof relies on standard arguments:

•

if
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{2},
then for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N},
we have that
deg_{\xi}\left((D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\xi^{n})\xi^{m}\right)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty\},
which shows that
DO(A)
is stable under multiplication,

•

while for
(n,m)\in(\mathbb{N}^{*})^{2},

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N},
we have that
deg_{\xi}\left((D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\xi^{-n})\xi^{-m}\right)<0,
which shows that
DO(A)
is stable under multiplication.

∎

A consequence of this proposition is that any operator
a\in\Psi DO(A)
decomposes uniquely under this direct sum as

a=a_{D}+a_{S}

for
a_{D}\in DO(A)
and
a_{S}\in IO(A)
.

In the most classical framework,
A
is an algebra of functions equipped with a derivation
\partial.
Standard choices are
A=C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{K})
with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
or, in
[
7
,
5
]
,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{H},
and
\partial=\frac{d}{dx}.
In these contexts, the algebras of functions
A
are Fréchet algebras equipped with natural notions of differentiability, and the operations of addition, multiplication and differentiation are smooth. It follows that addition and multiplication on
\Psi DO(A)
are smooth in the following sense:
if

a=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}\xi^{n},\quad b=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{n}\xi^{n},

we set

a+b=c=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\xi^{n}

and

ab=d=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{n}\xi^{n}\;.

Then, the map

\left((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\right)\mapsto\left((c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\right)

is smooth as a map from
\Pi_{\mathbb{Z}}A\times\Pi_{\mathbb{Z}}A\rightarrow\Pi_{\mathbb{Z}}A\times\Pi_{\mathbb{Z}}A
, namely,
each coefficient
c_{n}
and
d_{n}
depend smoothly (in the usual way) on finite numbers of coefficients
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
.

The foregoing set-up circumvent the use of the technical tools recently developed in
[
2
,
10
]
, where a fully rigorous framework for smoothness on objects such as
\Psi DO(A)
, is described and used.
The geometric approach to smoothness described therein is based on the theories of diffeologies and Frölicher spaces. We will not dwell on it here, we refer the reader to the mentioned papers
[
2
,
10
]
for details,
but we do mention that, for instance, our point of view allows us to develop a fully rigorous approach to smoothness on Frobenius algebras —so as to
justify all the formal calculations of
[
20
,
22
,
23
]
—
by adapting the considerations appearing in
[
2
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

We comment on the relation of our approach with more standard (and also more restrictive) viewpoints.
Our notion of smoothness restricts to the notion of smoothness in infinite dimensional geometry on manifolds (with
atlases) modelled on complete locally convex topological vector spaces. This latter theory is discussed in

[
15
]
. In particular,
see
[
10
]
, if we work with Fréchet manifolds of operators, being smooth in our sense is equivalent to
being Fréchet smooth.

2.2.
Integration of the KP hierarchy on a differential algebra

Let
T=\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be an infinite set of formal (time) variables and let us consider the
algebra of formal series
\Psi DO(A[[T]])
with infinite set of formal variables
t_{1},t_{2},\cdot
with
T-
valuation

val
defined by
val_{T}(t_{n})=n

[
13
]
.

If we assume that the algebra operations on
A
are smooth, then so are the corresponding operations
on
A[[T]]
, essentially because they are defined term by term using finite numbers of operations on
A
. This
intuitive description can be fully formalized in the context of ultrametric completions, see
[
2
]
.
Now we extend this notion of smoothness to
\Psi DO(A[[T]])

as in
[
2
,
10
]
, and we are able to talk about smoothness at the level of pseudo-differential operators. We will make some further remarks on this theme after Theorem
2.6
.

The Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy reads

(1)

\frac{dL}{dt_{k}}=\left[(L^{k})_{D},L\right]\;,\quad\quad k\geq 1\;,

with initial condition
L(0)=L_{0}\in\partial+IO(A)
. The dependent
variable
L
is chosen to be of the form

L=\xi+\sum_{\alpha\leq-1}u_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\in{\Psi}DO^{1}(A[[T]])\;.

A standard reference on (
1
) is L.A. Dickey’s treatise
[
1
]
, see also

[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

In order to solve the KP hierarchy, we need the following groups (see e.g.
[
2
,
10
]
for the latest adaptations of Mulase’s constructions appearing in
[
13
,
14
]
):

\bar{G}=1+IO(A[[T]])\;,

\overline{\Psi}=\left\{P=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{\alpha}\,\xi^{\alpha}:a_{\alpha}\in A[[T]]\;,\,val_{T}(a_{\alpha})\geq\alpha\hbox{ and }P|_{t=0}\in 1+\Psi DO^{-1}(A)\right\}\;,

where
P|_{t=0}
is the equivalence class
P\mbox{ mod }{\mathcal{I}}
, and
{\mathcal{I}}
is the ideal of

A[[T]]
generated by
\{t_{1},t_{2},\cdots\}
,
and

\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\left\{P=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{\alpha}\,\xi^{\alpha}:P\in\overline{\Psi}\mbox{ and }a_{\alpha}=0\mbox{ for }\alpha<0\right\}\;.

We have a matched pair (see for example
[
8
]
)

\overline{\Psi}=\bar{G}\bowtie\overline{\mathcal{D}}

which is smooth under the terminology we gave before.
The following result, from
[
2
]
, gives a synthesized statement of our results on existence and
uniqueness of solutions to the KP hierarchy (
1
), and on smooth dependence on the initial conditions.

Theorem 2.6
.

Consider the KP hierarchy
1
with initial condition
L(0)=L_{0}
. Then,

(1)

There exists a pair
(S,Y)\in\bar{G}\times\overline{\mathcal{D}}

such that the unique solution to Equation
(\ref{eq:KP})
with
L|_{t=0}=L_{0}
is

\displaystyle L(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots)=Y\,L_{0}\,Y^{-1}=SL_{0}S^{-1}\;.

(2)

The pair
(S,Y)
is uniquely determined by the smooth decomposition problem

exp\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\tau_{k}L_{0}^{k}\right)=S^{-1}Y\;,

and the solution
L
depends smoothly on the initial condition
L_{0}
.

(3)

The solution operator
L
is smoothly dependent on the
initial value
L_{0}.

In this theorem the expression “smoothly dependent” is to be understood essentially as explained
in the two paragraphs before Remark 2.5, namely, it means that each coefficient of the solution
L=L=\xi+\sum_{\alpha\leq-1}u_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
, understood as a series in the
T
variables, depends smoothly on a finite number of the coefficients of the initial value
L_{0}.
Indeed, if one reads the computations given in e.g.
[
14
,
2
,
10
]
where the computations of coefficients are sketched, it is possible to check that each coefficient of
L
is obtained via a large but finite number of smooth operations (addition, multiplication, inversion, derivation) in
A[[T]]
from the coefficients of
L_{0}.

2.3.
ZS-Equations deduced from the hierarchy

It is well-known that the zero curvature equations

(2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}L^{n}_{+}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}L^{m}_{+}=[L^{m}_{+},L^{n}_{+}]

are a consequence of the KP hierarchy, see for instance
[
1
, Proposition 5.1.4]
or
[
3
]
, and that these
equations are a system of non-linear equations in three independent variables, namely,
t_{n},t_{m}
and the “space
variable” implicit in the derivation
\partial
of the differential algebra
A
. Our Theorem
2.6

implies the following result (in the proposition below, we put
t_{k}=0
for
k\neq n,m
):

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
L=\xi+\sum_{\alpha\leq-1}u_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\in{\Psi}^{1}(A[[T]])
and assume that the zero curvature
equation
(\ref{zcc0})
is a system of equations for
N
dependent variables
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}
. Fix
u_{1,0},\cdots,u_{N,0}\in A
. Then, the system of equations
(\ref{zcc0})
has a unique smooth solution
L
with
u_{1}(0)=u_{1,0},\cdots,u_{N}(0)=u_{N,0}
, and this solution is smooth with respect to initial conditions.

Proof.

We define
L(0)=L_{0}\in\partial+IO(A)
as

L(0)=\xi+u_{1,0}\xi^{-1}+\cdots+u_{N,0}\xi^{-N}
, we solve the KP hierarchy with initial condition

L_{0}
, and we apply
[
1
, Proposition 5.1.4]
.
∎

We note that this proposition does not solve the full Cauchy problem for (
2
), since our initial condition fixes only the “space” dependence of the solution, while the dependence on
t_{m},t_{n}
is fixed by the solution
to the complete KP hierarchy with initial data
L_{0}
defined as in the above proof. Nonetheless we think it is a relevant result on solutions
to very general zero curvature equations.

We consider the set of independent variables

\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}
and write down explicitly the corresponding zero curvature equations; we are mostly interested
in the equations arising from the set
\{t_{2},t_{3}\}
, but we also try the
\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
and
\{t_{1},t_{3}\}
easy cases for completeness.

With the notation

L=\sum_{k\leq 1}u_{k}\xi^{k}

with

and

we have

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&L^{2}&L^{3}\\
\hline\cr\sigma_{3}&-&\xi^{3}\\
\hline\cr\sigma_{2}&\xi^{2}&0\\
\hline\cr\sigma_{1}&0&3u_{-1}\xi\\
\hline\cr\sigma_{0}&2u_{-1}\xi^{0}&(3u_{-2}+3\partial u_{-1})\xi^{0}\\

) of the
corresponding pseudo-differential operator, and therefore

\displaystyle\left[L^{2}_{+},L^{3}_{+}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\xi^{2},\xi^{3}\right]+3\left[\xi^{2},u_{-1}\xi\right]+3\left[\xi^{2},u_{-2}+\partial u_{-1}\right]

\displaystyle+2\left[u_{-1},\xi^{3}\right]+6\left[u_{-1},u_{-1}\xi\right]+6\left[u_{-1},u_{-2}+\partial u_{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+(6\partial u_{-1}\xi^{2}+3\partial^{2}u_{-1}\xi)+(6\partial u_{-2}\xi+3\partial^{2}u_{-2}+6\partial^{2}u_{-1}\xi+3\partial^{3}u_{-1})

\displaystyle+(-6\partial u_{-1}\xi^{2}-6\partial^{2}u_{-1}\xi-2\partial^{3}u_{-1})+(6[u_{-1},u_{-2}]-6\partial u_{-1}u_{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(3\partial^{2}u_{-1}+6\partial u_{-2})\xi+([u_{-1},u_{-2}]-6\partial u_{-1}u_{-1}+\partial^{3}u_{-1}+3\partial^{2}u_{-2})\;.

The ZS-equations for the pairs
(t_{1},t_{2})
and
(t_{1},t_{3})
read respectively as

(3)

\frac{dL_{+}^{2}}{dt_{1}}-\frac{dL_{+}}{dt_{2}}=\left[L_{+},L^{2}_{+}\right]

(4)

\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{1}}=\partial u_{-1}\;,

and

(5)

(9)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d(u_{-2}+\partial u_{-1})}{dt_{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{3}}

\displaystyle u_{y}

3.
KP equations and hierarchies in various contexts

3.1.
When
\partial
is an exterior derivation

We say that
\partial
is an exterior derivation if
\partial^{2}=0
.
In this case,

u\xi^{m}\cdot v\xi^{n}=(uv)\xi^{m+n}+(m\,u\,\partial v)\,\xi^{m+n-1}\;,

and therefore
the zero-curvature equations (
8
) and (
9
) read

(12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{2}}-2\partial u_{-2}&=&0\\
\frac{du_{-2}}{dt_{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{3}}&=&-2\partial u_{-1}u_{-1}+2[u_{-1},u_{-2}]\;,\end{array}\right.

in which we have used that the first equation implies that
\partial u_{-1,t_{2}}=0
.
Now we set
2u_{-1}=u
,
t_{2}=y
and
t_{3}=t
. Equation (
10
) yields

(13)

u=4\partial_{y}^{-1}\partial u_{-2}\;,

and therefore equation (
11
) becomes

\partial_{y}u_{-2}-\frac{4}{3}\partial_{y}^{-1}\partial_{t}\partial u_{-2}=4[\partial_{y}^{-1}\partial u_{-2},u_{-2}]\;,

this is

(14)

\partial^{2}_{y}u_{-2}-\frac{4}{3}\partial_{t}\partial u_{-2}=4\partial_{y}[\partial_{y}^{-1}\partial u_{-2},u_{-2}]\;,

an equation that reduces to a linear wave equation in the commutative case. Proposition
2.7
yields the result

.

Let us fix

. Equation

has a solution

, and this solution is smooth with respect to the initial condition

.

Proof.

We choose
u(y)
such that
\partial_{y}u(y)=4\partial\overline{u}
. Such a

exists because the derivation

\partial_{y}
is surjective on

. Now we apply Proposition
2.7
with
u_{1,0}=(1/2)\,u(0)
and

u_{2,0}=\overline{u}
,
thereby obtaining a solution

and

to (

), or, equivalently, to the
system (

)-(

). By construction, the solution

satisfies

and our general theory implies that it is smooth with respect to the initial condition

\overline{u}
.
∎

3.2.
On the case when
\partial
is the gradient

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and let us consider
A=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with component-wise operations (this is an instance
of a “Hadamard product”).
A
is therefore a commutative and associative algebra, but it not an integral domain.
The algebra
A
can be equipped with the topology of uniform convergence of derivatives at any order on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
For the first part of the computations, we can also consider
A=C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
(functions with compact support) equipped with the Whitney topology (see. e.g.
[
6
]
) or
A=S(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
(Schwartz space).

We denote by
x=(x_{1},...x_{n})^{t}
a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by

a point in

\nabla u=\nabla(f_{1}(x),...f_{n}(x))^{t}=\left(\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}(x),...,\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}(x)\right)^{t}\;.

Our notation is justified by the fact that “on the diagonal”,
f_{1}=f_{2}=\cdots=f_{n}

\nabla
is a derivation is clear; let us
check the Leibniz rule for benefit of the reader:

We take
u=(f_{1}(x),\cdots,f_{n}(x))^{t}
and
v=(g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{n}(x))^{t}
in
A
. Then (we omit the
argument

for brevity),

\displaystyle\nabla(u\cdot v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial(f_{1}g_{1})}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial(f_{n}g_{n})}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}g_{1}+f_{1}\frac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}g_{n}+f_{n}\frac{\partial g_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{t}(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n})^{t}+(f_{1},\cdots f_{n})^{t}\left(\frac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial g_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(u)\,v+u\,\nabla(v)\;.

We note that this computation shows that we can
replace the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
appearing in (
15
) for any
n

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and still obtain a meaningful derivation on

. We stick with our original

Equations (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{2}}=\nabla^{2}u_{-1}+2\nabla u_{-2}\\
-2\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{3}}+6u_{-1}\nabla u_{-1}-3\nabla^{2}u_{-1}=-2\nabla^{3}u_{-1}-3\nabla^{2}(u_{-2}+u_{-1})-3\frac{du_{-2}}{dt_{2}}\;.\end{array}\right.

Assuming that

equipped with the smooth compact-open topology, we can

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d^{2}u_{-1}}{dt_{2}^{2}}=\nabla^{2}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{2}}+2v\\

where
t_{2}
and

are external
\mathbb{R}-

the second line. In general we can make the following

Remark 3.2
.

We can get the Navier-Stokes equation at any dimension on

if we impose
the additional constraint
div(u_{-1})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial u_{-1,k}}{\partial x_{k}}=0

Indeed, let

we get a four lines system:

(18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d^{2}u_{-1}}{dt_{2}^{2}}=\nabla^{2}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{2}}+2v\\
\nabla h=-2\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{3}}+6u_{-1}\nabla u_{-1}+\nu\Delta u_{-1}\\
3\frac{dv}{dt_{2}}+3v=-h_{1}+2\nabla^{2}u_{-1}-3\nabla u_{-1}\\

The added line has a particular solution

only valid in

3.4.

3.5.
On Pincherle derivative

Let us consider
A=\mathbb{K}[X],
with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}.
The Pincherle derivative is defined as follows:

\forall T\in End(A),\quad\partial_{\xi}T=TX-XT=[-X,T].

Following its fundamental properties
[
17
,
18
]
, since
\partial_{\xi}X=0
and
\partial_{\xi}\frac{d}{dX}=1,
it follows that
\partial_{\xi}
is also known as a derivation on (polynomial) differential operators
A\left[\frac{d}{dX}\right].

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}((X))
or
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}((X^{-1})).
i.e.
Then:

(1)

\partial_{\xi}A^{\prime}=0

(2)

\partial_{\xi}
extends to a derivation on
A^{\prime}\left[\left[\frac{d}{dX}\right]\right].

(3)

\partial_{\xi}
extends to a derivation on the algebra

B=\Psi DO(A^{\prime})=\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\xi^{k}:a_{k}\in A^{\prime}\;,a_{k}=0,k>>0\right\}

by the relation
\partial_{\xi}\xi=1
.

In the sequel, the derivation in the formal
X-
variable will be noted by
\partial_{X}.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are straightforward from the definitions. In order to prove (3), we get by induction that
\partial_{\xi}\xi^{-n}=-n\xi^{n+1},
which defines it by
A^{\prime}-
linearity, and then we can check that
\partial_{\xi}
satisties the Leibnitz rule on
\Psi DO(A^{\prime}).

∎

We now consider
\Psi DO(B)=\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\zeta^{k}:a_{k}\in B,a_{k}=0,k>>0\right\}.

If
P\in\Psi DO(B)
, we can write
P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\zeta^{k}
(or “
P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\partial_{\xi}^{\;k}\,
”)
that is,
\zeta
is the formal variable that corresponds to the derivation
\partial_{\xi}
on
B
.

Thus, we have a “hybrid” system, a Lie algebra bracket and a commutator in an associative algebra:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{2}}=[X,[X,u_{-1}]-2[X,u_{-2}]\\
3\frac{du_{-2}}{dt_{2}}-2\frac{du_{-1}}{dt_{3}}=[u_{-1},u_{-2}]+6[X,u_{-1}]u_{-1}+2[X,[X,[X,u_{-1}]]]-3[X,[X,u_{-2}]]\,.\end{array}\right.

Once again, Proposition
2.7
allow us to conclude that the Cauchy problem of this system of equations can be solved in our algebraic setting.

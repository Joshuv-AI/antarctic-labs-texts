Abstract
. Let 𝐤 {\mathbf{k}} be a perfect field of characteristic ≠ 2 \neq 2 . We prove that the Schmidt rank (also known as strength) of a quartic polynomial f f over 𝐤 {\mathbf{k}} is bounded above in terms of only the Schmidt rank of f f over 𝐤 ¯ \overline{{\mathbf{k}}} , an algebraic closure of 𝐤 {\mathbf{k}} .



1.
Introduction

Recall that the
Schmidt rank
(also known as
strength
) of a homogeneous polynomial
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
(see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and references therein)
is defined as the minimal number
r
such that
f
admits a decomposition
f=g_{1}h_{1}+\ldots+g_{r}h_{r}
, with
\deg(g_{i})
and
\deg(h_{i})
smaller
than
\deg(f)
. We denote the Schmidt rank of
f
as
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)
.

It is conjectured in
[
1
]
that for a homogeneous polynomial
f
of degree
d
over a non-closed field
{\mathbf{k}}
one has

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq\kappa_{d}\cdot{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}}(f),

where
\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}
is an algebraic closure of
{\mathbf{k}}
. This is known to be true for
d\leq 3
with
\kappa_{d}=d
since in this case the Schmidt rank is equal to the
slice rank
defined as the minimal

r
such that
f\in(l_{1},\ldots,l_{r})
, where
\deg(l_{i})=1
(see
[
4
, Thm. A]
for the case of cubics).

One can also ask a weaker question whether there exists a function
c(r,d)
such that

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq c({\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}}(f),d).

Our main result is that this weaker question has a positive answer in the case of quartic polynomials.

Theorem A
.
Assume that the ground field
{\mathbf{k}}
is perfect of characteristic
\neq 2
. Then there exists a function
r\mapsto c(r)
such that for
any homogeneous quartic polynomial
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, such that
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}}(f)=r
, one has
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq c(r)
.

We find it convenient to use of the following refined version of Schmidt rank.

Definition 1.1
.

For a collection of nonnegative integers
(r_{k},\ldots,r_{1})
and a homogeneous polynomial
f
of degree
d
,
we say that the
refined Schmidt rank of
f
is at most
(r_{k},\ldots,r_{1})
, and write

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\leq(r_{k},\ldots,r_{1})
, if there exists a decomposition over
{\mathbf{k}}
,

f=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{j}}f_{ij}g_{ij},

where
\deg(f_{ij})=d-\deg(g_{ij})=j

for
j=1,\ldots,k
.

Thus, for a quartic polynomial
f
, we have
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\leq(r_{2},r_{1})
if there exist quadrics
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r_{2}}
and linear forms
l_{1},\ldots,l_{r_{1}}
, such that

f\in(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r_{2}},l_{1},\ldots,l_{r_{1}})
.
In our proof of Theorem A we show the existence of functions
(c_{2}(r_{2},r_{1}),c_{1}(r_{2},r_{1}))
, such that
if
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}}(f)\leq(r_{2},r_{1})
then
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq(c_{2}(r_{2},r_{1}),c_{1}(r_{2},r_{1}))
. Then one can set

c(r)=\max_{r_{1}+r_{2}=r}(c_{2}(r_{2},r_{1})+c_{1}(r_{2},r_{1})).

One can work through our proof of Theorem A and get explicit formulas for
(c_{2}(r_{2},r_{1}),c_{1}(r_{2},r_{1}))
.
As an illustration of this, we give formulas for
(c_{2}(1,r_{1}),c_{1}(1,r_{1}))
.

Theorem B
.
Let
f
be a homogeneous quartic polynomial defined over a perfect field
{\mathbf{k}}
with
{\operatorname{char}}({\mathbf{k}})\neq 2
.
Assume that
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}}(f)\leq(1,r)
. Then
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq(2,C(r))
, where

C(r)=8r(41+20\cdot(10r+1)^{10r+1}).

The main idea of the proof of Theorem A is to study decompositions of a quartic polynomial
f
of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}\mod(P),

where
q_{i}
,
q^{\prime}_{i}
are of degree
2
and
P
is a subspace of linear forms.
The main result about such decomposition is that if the rank of any linear combination of
(q_{\bullet},q^{\prime}_{\bullet})
is sufficiently large then
the above decomposition is essentially unique (possibly after enlarging
P
): the only way to get a new decomposition is by making an orthogonal
change of basis in the linear space with the basis
(q_{\bullet},q^{\prime}_{\bullet})
.
We then apply this result to the decompositions obtained from a given one over
\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}
by applying the Galois group action.
If the rank of
(q_{\bullet},q^{\prime}_{\bullet})
is sufficiently large, then we obtain a
1
-cocycle of the Galois group with values in the orthogonal group measuring how
the decomposition transforms under the Galois action. We can assume that this
1
-cocycle is trivial (after passing to an extension of
{\mathbf{k}}
of small degree).
We then use a certain linear algebra result from
[
3
]
to prove the existence of a decomposition with
(q_{\bullet},q^{\prime}_{\bullet})
defined over
{\mathbf{k}}
.
Furthermore, we have a bound on the slice rank of
f-\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}
over
\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}
, and hence over
{\mathbf{k}}
by
[
4
, Thm. A]
.
This gives the required bound on the Schmidt rank of
f
over
{\mathbf{k}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Criterion for an ideal generated by quadrics and linear forms to be prime

In this subsection we fix a ground field
{\mathbf{k}}
(and omit it from the notation). By a
quadric
we mean an element of
{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{2}
, i.e., a quadratic form.

Definition 2.1
.

For a subspace of quadrics
Q
, and a collection of quadrics
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}
, we define

{\operatorname{srk}}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},Q)
as the minimum of
{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i}c_{i}q_{i}+q)
, where
q\in Q
and
c_{i}
are constants, such that either
q\neq 0
or

(c_{\bullet})\neq 0
. In particular,
{\operatorname{srk}}(Q)
is the minimal slice rank of a nonzero element of
Q
.

We denote by
{\operatorname{rk}}(q)
the usual rank of a quadric
q
.
It is easy to see that the rank and the slice rank of a quadric
q
are related by

2{\operatorname{srk}}(q)-1\leq{\operatorname{rk}}(q)\leq 2{\operatorname{srk}}(q).

In other words, we have

{\operatorname{srk}}(q)=\lceil\frac{{\operatorname{rk}}(q)}{2}\rceil.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}
be quadratic forms such that

R=\min_{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})\neq 0}{\operatorname{rk}}(c_{1}\nu_{1}+\ldots c_{r}\nu_{r})\geq 2r+1.

Then the subscheme
q_{1}=\ldots=q_{r}=0
is normal of codimension
r
.

Proof.

Consider the Jacobian matrix
J(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})
.
The locus
S(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})
where
J(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})
has rank
<r
coincides with
the union of kernels of
\sum c_{j}\nu_{j}
over
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})\neq 0
. Hence,

{\operatorname{codim}}_{V}S(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})\geq R-(r-1)\geq r+2.

But this implies that
(q_{1}=\ldots=q_{r}=0)
is a complete intersection, nonsingular in codimension
1
, hence normal.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

(i) Let
Q
be a subspace of quadratic forms such that
{\operatorname{srk}}(Q)\geq\dim Q+1
. Then the ideal
(Q)
is prime.

(ii) Let
L
be a subspace of linear forms,
Q
a subspace of quadratic forms. Assume that
{\operatorname{srk}}(Q)\geq\dim Q+\dim L+1
.
Then the ideal
(Q,L)
is prime.

Proof.

(i) For any
q\in Q
we have
{\operatorname{rk}}(q)\geq 2{\operatorname{srk}}(q)-1\geq 2\dim Q+1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, the ideal
(Q)
is prime.

(ii) Consider the quotient
\overline{S}=S/(L)
of the algebra of polynomials
S
by the ideal
(L)
. For any
q\in Q
we have

{\operatorname{srk}}(\overline{q})\geq{\operatorname{srk}}(q)-\dim L
, where
\overline{q}
is the image of
q
in
\overline{S}
. Hence, the image
\overline{Q}
of
Q
in
\overline{S}
satisfies
the assumptions of (i), so the ideal
(\overline{Q})
in
\overline{S}
is prime. Therefore, its preimage in
S
, namely
(Q,L)
, is also prime.
∎

2.2.
Almost invariant quadratic forms

We will use the following result from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
E/{\mathbf{k}}
be a finite Galois extension with the Galois group
G
, and let
V_{0},V^{\prime}_{0}
be finite dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces.
Let us set
V=V_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbf{k}}}E
,
V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbf{k}}}E
.
Suppose
T:V\to V^{\prime}
is an
E
-linear operator such that for any
\sigma\in G
, one has

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(T)-T)\leq r,

for some
r\geq 0
.
Then there exists a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear operator
T_{0}:V_{0}\to V^{\prime}_{0}
, such that

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(T-(T_{0})_{E})\leq r(2+(r+1)^{r+1}),

where
(T_{0})_{E}:V\to V^{\prime}
is obtained from
T_{0}
by the extension of scalars.

We need the following consequence of this theorem for quadratic forms.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
E/{\mathbf{k}}
be a finite Galois extension with the Galois group
G
, where
{\operatorname{char}}(2)\neq 2
,
and let
V_{0}
be a finite dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space,
V=V_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbf{k}}}E
.
Assume that
q
is a quadratic form on
V
such that for any
\sigma\in G
, one has
{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(q)-q)\leq r
for some
r\geq 0
, where
{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}
is the usual rank of the quadratic form.
Then there exists a quadratic form
q_{0}
on
V_{0}
such that

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(q-q_{0})\leq 2r(2+(r+1)^{r+1})
.

Proof.

Let
T:V\to V^{*}
be the symmetric linear map associated with
q
. Our assumption implies that
{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(T)-T)\leq r
for any
\sigma\in G
. By Theorem
2.4
,
there exists an operator
T_{0}:V_{0}\to V_{0}^{*}
such that
{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(T-T_{0})\leq r(2+(r+1)^{r+1})
.
Let
T_{0}^{*}:V_{0}\to V_{0}^{*}
be the dual operator. Then

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(T-\frac{1}{2}(T_{0}+T_{0}^{*}))\leq 2r(2+(r+1)^{r+1}),

so we can let
q_{0}
be the quadratic form corresponding to
\frac{1}{2}(T_{0}+T_{0}^{*})
.
∎

3.
Schmidt rank for quartics

From now on we assume that the ground field
{\mathbf{k}}
is perfect and has characteristic
\neq 2
.

3.1.
Case
r_{2}=1

We start with a proof of Theorem B dealing with the case
r_{2}=1
, since it is simpler but still shows the main idea.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{k}}
be a quadratic extension, and let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial over
{\mathbf{k}}
such that
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}(f)\leq(r_{2},r_{1})
. Then
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq(2r_{2},2r_{1})
.

Proof.

By assumption
f\in(Q,P)
, where
Q
is a subspace of quadrics and
P
is a subspace of linear forms, both defined over
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}
. Hence,

f\in(Q+\sigma(Q),P+\sigma(P))
, where
\sigma
is the generator of the Galois group of
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{k}}
. Since the subspaces
Q
and
P
are defined over
{\mathbf{k}}
,
this implies the assertion.
∎

Proof of Theorem B.

We have to check that if
E/{\mathbf{k}}
is a finite Galois extension, and

f\equiv qq^{\prime}\mod(P),

where
q,q^{\prime}
are quadratic forms over
E
and

P
is an
r
-dimensional subspace of linear forms defined over
E
, then

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(f)\leq(2,C(r))
.

If
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q)\leq 9r
or
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q)\leq 9r
then
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(f)\leq 10r
, and so
{\operatorname{srk}}_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\leq 40r\leq C(r)
.
Thus, we can assume that
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q)>9r
and
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q^{\prime})>9r
. Let
G
be the Galois group of
E/{\mathbf{k}}
. For any
\sigma\in G
we have

f\equiv\sigma(q)\sigma(q^{\prime})\mod(\sigma(P)).

By assumption, the slice rank of
\overline{q}=q\mod(P+\sigma(P))
is
\geq 2
, hence the quadric
\overline{q}
is irreducible. In other words, the ideal
(q,P+\sigma(P))
is prime.
Since
qq^{\prime}\in(\sigma(q),P+\sigma(P))
, we have either
q\in(\sigma(q),P+\sigma(P))
or
q^{\prime}\in(\sigma(q),P+\sigma(P))
. Since the slice ranks of
q
and
q^{\prime}
are
>2r
, this means that either

\sigma(q)\equiv c(\sigma)\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma(P)),\ \sigma(q^{\prime})\equiv c(\sigma)^{-1}\cdot q^{\prime}\mod(P+\sigma(P)),\text{ or }

\sigma(q)\equiv c(\sigma)\cdot q^{\prime}\mod(P+\sigma(P)),\ \sigma(q^{\prime})\equiv c(\sigma)^{-1}\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma(P)),

for some
c(\sigma)\in E^{*}
.

Let
H\subset G
be the set of
\sigma\in G
for which the first possibility holds.
Let us consider separately two cases.

Case
{\operatorname{srk}}(q,q^{\prime})>3r
. Assume first that
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in H
. Then we have

\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(q)\equiv\sigma_{1}(c(\sigma_{2}))\cdot\sigma_{1}(q)\equiv c(\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}(c(\sigma_{2}))\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma_{1}(P)+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(P)).

If
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\not\in H
, we would get that a nontrivial linear combination of
q
and
q^{\prime}
is in
P+\sigma_{1}(P)+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(P)
, contradicting the assumption
{\operatorname{srk}}(q,q^{\prime})>3r
.
Hence,
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\in H
. Furthermore, since a nonzero multiple of
q
cannot be contained in
P+\sigma_{1}(P)+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(P)
, we have

c(\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}(c(\sigma_{2}))=c(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}),

i.e.,
c(\sigma)
is a
1
-cocycle of
H
. A similar argument shows that if exactly one of
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
belongs to
H
then
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\not\in H
, and if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\not\in H
then
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\in H
,
proving that
H
is a subgroup of index
\leq 2
in
G
.

Case
{\operatorname{srk}}(q,q^{\prime})\leq 3r
. In this case we have
q^{\prime}\equiv cq\mod(P_{0})
for some subspace of linear forms
P_{0}
of dimension
\leq 3r
(defined over
E
) and some
c\in E^{*}
.
This implies that for
\sigma\not\in H
we have

\sigma(q)\equiv c(\sigma)\cdot q^{\prime}\equiv c(\sigma)c\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma(P)+P_{0}).

Redefining
c(\sigma)
for
\sigma\not\in H
we obtain that

\sigma(q)\equiv c(\sigma)\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma(P)+P_{0}),\ \ \sigma(q)\equiv c(\sigma)\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma(P)+P_{0})

(3.1)

for all
\sigma\in G
. Now for
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in H
we have

\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(q)\equiv\sigma_{1}(c(\sigma_{2}))\cdot\sigma_{1}(q)\equiv c(\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}(c(\sigma_{2}))\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma_{1}(P)+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(P)+P_{0}+\sigma_{1}(P_{0})).

On the other hand,

\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(q)\equiv c(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\cdot q\mod(P+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(P)+P_{0}).

Since
{\operatorname{srk}}(q)>9
this implies that
c(\sigma)
is a
1
-cocycle of
H
.

In either case we obtain that for a subgroup
H\subset G
of index
\leq 2
and a subspace of linear forms
P_{0}
of dimension
\leq 3r
,
the congruences (
3.1
) hold for some
1
-cocycle
c:H\to E^{*}
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{k}}
be the subextension of
E
corresponding to the subgroup
H\subset G
, so that the extension
E/{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}
is Galois with the Galois group
H
.
By Hilbert’s Theorem 90, the cocycle
c(\sigma)
of
H
is trivial, so rescaling
q
and
q^{\prime}
, we can assume that

\sigma(q)\equiv q\mod(P+\sigma(P)+P_{0}),\ \ \sigma(q^{\prime})\equiv q^{\prime}\mod(P+\sigma(P)+P_{0})

for all
\sigma\in H
. But this implies that
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(\sigma(q)-q)\leq 5r
and
{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(\sigma(q^{\prime})-q^{\prime})\leq 5r
.
Hence, we obtain

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(q)-q)\leq 10r,\ \ {\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(q^{\prime})-q^{\prime})\leq 10r

for all
\sigma\in H
.
By Corollary
2.5
, there exist quadrics
q_{0}
and
q^{\prime}_{0}
defined over
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}
such that

\max({\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(q-q_{0}),{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(q^{\prime}-q^{\prime}_{0}))\leq 20r(2+(10r+1)^{10r+1}).

Hence,

\max({\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q-q_{0}),{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(q^{\prime}-q^{\prime}_{0}))\leq 10r(2+(10r+1)^{10r+1}).

It follows that

{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(f-q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0})\leq{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(f-qq^{\prime})+{\operatorname{srk}}_{E}(qq^{\prime}-q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0})\leq r+20r(2+(10r+1)^{10r+1})=r(41+20\cdot(10r+1)^{10r+1}).

By
[
4
, Thm. A]
, this implies that

{\operatorname{srk}}_{{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}(f-q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0})\leq 4r(41+20\cdot(10r+1)^{10r+1}),

hence,
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}(f)\leq(1,4r(41+20\cdot(10r+1)^{10r+1}))
. Since the extension
{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{k}}
is either trivial or quadratic, applying Lemma
3.1
we get the result.
∎

3.2.
Quadratic decompositions of quartics

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Q
be a subspace of quadrics,

q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{s},p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{s}

quadratic forms, where
r>s
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}p^{\prime}_{i}\mod(Q).

Then for some constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{r}
such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}=0
, we have

{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i}(a_{i}q_{i}+a^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),Q)\leq c(r,s,\dim Q):=2^{s}(r+\dim Q)+2^{s-1}(s-2).

Proof.

We use the induction on
s
. In the case
s=0
we have to prove that

{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i}(a_{i}q_{i}+a^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),Q)\leq c(r,0,\dim Q)=r+\dim Q-1

for some isotropic
(a_{\bullet},a^{\prime}_{\bullet})
.
Indeed, assume this is not true. Then
{\operatorname{srk}}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r-1},Q)\geq r+\dim Q
, so
the ideal
(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r-1},Q)
is prime. But we have

q_{r}q^{\prime}_{r}\in(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r-1},Q).

Hence, swapping
q_{r}
with
q^{\prime}_{r}
if necessary, we
deduce that
q_{r}\in(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r-1},Q)
which is impossible since
{\operatorname{srk}}(q_{\bullet},Q)\geq r+\dim Q\geq r>0
.

Assume the assertion holds for
s-1
. We have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=2}^{s}p_{i}p^{\prime}_{i}\mod(p_{1},Q).

Hence, by the induction assumption,

{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i}(a_{i}q_{i}+a^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),p_{1},Q)\leq c(r,s-1,\dim Q+1)

for some isotropic
(a_{\bullet},a^{\prime}_{\bullet})
.
Changing the basis in
(q_{\bullet})
, we can assume that either
{\operatorname{srk}}(q_{1},Q)\leq c(r,s-1,\dim Q+1)
, or
there exists a subspace
L
of linear forms of dimension
\leq c(r,s-1,\dim Q+1)
such that

p_{1}\in(q_{1},Q,L)
. In the former case we are done since
c(r,s,\dim Q)\geq c(r,s-1,\dim Q+1)
.
In the latter case, we have

\sum_{i=2}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=2}^{s}p_{i}p^{\prime}_{i}\mod(q_{1},Q,L).

Applying the induction assumption we obtain that there exists an isotropic vector
(a_{>1},a^{\prime}_{>1})
such that

{\operatorname{srk}}_{L}(\sum_{i=2}^{r}(a_{i}q_{i}+q^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),q_{1},Q)\leq c(r-1,s-1,\dim Q+1),

hence

\displaystyle{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i=2}^{r}(a_{i}q_{i}+q^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),q_{1},Q)\leq c(r-1,s-1,\dim Q+1)+\dim L\leq

\displaystyle c(r-1,s-1,\dim Q+1)+c(r,s-1,\dim Q+1)=c(r,s,\dim Q).

Since any linear combination of
\sum_{i=2}^{r}(a_{i}q_{i}+q^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i})
with
q_{1}
will correspond to an isotropic vector, the assertion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Q
be a subspace of quadrics,

q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{r}

quadratic forms, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}p^{\prime}_{i}\mod(Q).

(3.2)

Assume that for any constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{r}
such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}=0
, we have

{\operatorname{srk}}(\sum_{i}(a_{i}q_{i}+a^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}),Q)\geq C(r,\dim Q):=2^{r}(r+\dim Q)+2^{r-1}(r-2)+1.

Then there exists a subspace of linear forms
L

of dimension at most

\displaystyle c(r,r-1,\dim Q)+c(r,r-1,\dim Q+1)\leq C(r,\dim Q)-1,

\displaystyle C(r,\dim Q)-c(r,r-1,\dim Q+1)\geq C(r-1,\dim Q+1),

.

the ideal

It remains to observe that the linear transformation

\displaystyle Ae_{1}=e_{1},\ \ Af_{1}=f_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{r}(c_{i}f_{i}+c^{\prime}_{i}e_{i})-(\sum_{i=2}^{r}c_{i}c^{\prime}_{i})e_{1},

\displaystyle Ae_{i}=e_{i}+c_{i}e_{1},\ \ Af_{i}=f_{i}+c^{\prime}_{i}e_{1},\text{ for }i\geq 2,

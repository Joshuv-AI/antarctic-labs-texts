Chapter 1
Introduction

This is the first part of the lecture notes that grew out of the special course given by the author to the audience of exceptional undergraduate and first and second year graduate students at UCLA interested in geometry during the 2021/2022 academic year.
In these lecture notes we present an approach to the fundamental structures in differential geometry that uses the vernacular of sheaves, differential operators and horizontal subbundles.

This language is now standard in other disciplines, notably algebraic and complex geometry, but has been slow to enter the textbooks for students aspiring to work in differential geometry or use differential geometry in other geometric disciplines.

A notable exception is the recent textbook
[
Wed16
]
that uses the language of sheaves and cohomology to introduce the basics of smooth manifolds. This textbook, however, does not tap into the differential geometry territory per se, as it does not discuss metrics, connections, curvature, parallel transport or holonomy groups.

Another feature of this book is the treatment of the notion of connection from various different viewpoints: as a Koszul connection, as a covariant derivative, as an Ehresmann connection, as a parallel transport, etc. A similar extensive exposition of the notion of connection can be found in
[
Spi99
]
and in
[
Pos88
]
, but those expositions do not use the modern language that we try to develop in these notes.

A byproduct of the chosen approach is a relatively simple and conceptually clear construction of canonical connections (Levi-Civita, Chern, Bismut, etc.) without a heavy reliance on explicit local expressions as a starting point.

Finally, these lecture notes were heavily inspired by the course
[
Ver13
]
in differential geometry given by Misha Verbitsky in 2013.

Both of these topics are covered in the already mentioned

Chapter 2
Differential Operators

2.1
Differential operators over a ring

Definition 2.1
.

A
commutator
of two linear operators
A,B:V\to V
is a linear operator defined as

[A,B]:=A\circ B-B\circ A.

The next key definition is due to A. Grothendieck.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
R
be a (commutative)
k
-algebra. A
differential operator of order

\mathbf{0}
is a morphism of
R
-modules
D^{0}:R\to R
. Denote differential operators of order
0
by
Diff^{0}(R)
.

More generally, a
k
-linear morphism
D^{i}:R\to R
is a
differential operator of order

\mathbf{i}
,
D^{i}\in Diff^{i}(R)
, if for any
D^{0}\in Diff^{0}(R)
the commutator
[D^{i},D^{0}]
lies in
Diff^{i-1}(R)
.

We have

Diff^{0}(R)\subset Diff^{1}(R)\subset Diff^{2}(R)\subset...,

and the union
Diff^{\bullet}(R):=\cup_{i\geq 0}Diff^{i}(R)
is called the
algebra of differential operators on

\mathbf{R}
.

Example 2.3

(Differential operators of order
0
)
.

It follows from the above definition that

Diff^{0}(R)\simeq R

via
D^{0}\mapsto D^{0}(1)
. The inverse morphism is given by
a\mapsto(m_{a}:b\mapsto ab).
We will consistently use this identification throughout this chapter.

Example 2.4

(Differential operators of order
1
)
.

Let us show that

Diff^{1}(R)\simeq Der(R)\oplus R

as
k
-vector spaces. First note that for any derivation
X\in Der(R)
, any
m_{a}\in Diff^{0}(R)
and any
b\in R
we have

[X,m_{a}](b)=X(ab)-aX(b)=aX(b)+bX(a)-aX(b)=X(a)b,

so we see that
[X,m_{a}]
is just
m_{X(a)}\in Diff^{0}(R)
. Thus
Der(R)\subset Diff^{1}(R).

Now consider
D^{1}\in Diff^{1}(R)
. Define

X(a):=D^{1}(a)-D^{1}(1)a,\quad\forall a\in R.

Exercise 2.5
.

Check that
X
defined above is a derivation.

This exercise shows that any
D^{1}\in Diff^{1}(R)
can be written as

D_{1}=X+m_{D^{1}(1)}.

Note that if
D^{i}\in Diff^{i}(R)
and
D^{j}\in Diff^{j}(R)
, then for any
m_{a}\in Diff^{0}(R)
:

\displaystyle Diff^{i+j-1}

\displaystyle\ni[m_{a},D^{i}]\circ D^{j}+D^{i}\circ[m_{a},D^{j}]=m_{a}\circ D^{i}\circ D^{j}-D^{i}\circ m_{a}\circ D^{j}

\displaystyle+D^{i}\circ m_{a}\circ D^{j}-D^{i}\circ D^{j}\circ m_{a}=m_{a}\circ D^{i}\circ D^{j}-D^{i}\circ D^{j}\circ m_{a}=[m_{a},D^{i}\circ D^{j}]\

which shows that
D^{i}\circ D^{j}\in Diff^{i+j}(R)
. This way, we get a
filtered algebra

\mathbf{Diff^{\bullet}(R)}
of differential operators.

Exercise 2.6
.

Let
D\in Diff^{k}(R)
.

1.

Prove that
D(a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k})=D^{\prime}(a_{2}a_{3}...a_{k})+a_{1}D(a_{2}a_{3}...a_{k}),
where
D^{\prime}=[D,m_{a_{1}}]\in Diff^{k-1}(R)
.

2.

Let
I\subset R
be an ideal. Use induction to prove
D(I^{k+1})\subset I.

Exercise 2.7
.

Use induction and the Jacobi identity

[A,[B,C]]=[[A,B],C]+[B,[A,C]],\quad A,B,C\ -\text{linear operators},

to prove that if
D^{i}\in Diff^{i}(R),D^{j}\in Diff^{j}(R)
, then the commutator
[D^{i},D^{j}]
lies in
Diff^{i+j-1}(R)
.

2.1.1
Differential operators over the polynomial ring

For this subsection, let
R:=\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[t_{1},...,t_{n}]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
D\in Diff^{k}(R)
is a differential operator that vanishes on all polynomials of degree
\leq k
, then
D=0
.

Proof.

Note that if
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot...\cdot t_{n}^{i_{n}}
is a monomial of degree
|I|=i_{1}+...+i_{n}=k+1
, then by Exercise
2.6
(a)

D(t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot...\cdot t_{n}^{i_{n}})=D^{\prime}(t^{|I|-1})+t_{i}D(t^{|I|-1}),\quad D^{\prime}=[D,m_{t_{i}}]\in Diff^{k-1}(R),\quad|I|-1=k.

The first term is zero by induction on the order of a differential operator and the fact that
D^{\prime}
vanishes on polynomials of degree
\leq k-1
. The second term is zero by the assumption of the lemma.

The inductive step for monomials
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot...\cdot t_{n}^{i_{n}}
of degree
|I|=i_{1}+...+i_{n}>k+1
is handled using the same formula.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
R_{\leq k}=:\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[t_{1},...,t_{n}]_{\leq k}
be the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
. Let
A:R_{\leq k}\to R
be a linear operator. Then there exists
D\in Diff^{k}(R)
such that
D|_{R_{\leq k}}:R_{\leq k}\to R
coincides with
A
.

Proof.

Consider a differential operator
D_{f,i_{1}...i_{n}}=\frac{f(t_{1},...,t_{n})}{i_{1}!\cdot...\cdot i_{n}!}\left(\frac{d}{dt_{1}}\right)^{\circ i_{1}}\circ...\circ\left(\frac{d}{dt_{n}}\right)^{\circ i_{n}}
for
f\in R
. Note that
D_{f,i_{1}...i_{n}}
is zero on all monomials of degree
<|I|=i_{1}+...+i_{n}
,

D_{f,i_{1}...i_{n}}(t_{1}^{i_{1}}...t_{n}^{i_{n}})=f(t_{1},...,t_{n}),

and is zero on all other monomials of degree
|I|
.

Thus

D=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{n}\leq k}D_{A(t_{1}^{i_{n}}...t_{n}^{i_{n}}),i_{1}...i_{n}}

is the desired differential operator as monomials
A(t_{1}^{i_{n}}...t_{n}^{i_{n}})
with
i_{1}+...+i_{n}\leq k
form the basis of
R_{\leq k}
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

The algebra of differential operators
Diff^{\bullet}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[t_{1},...,t_{n}]\right)
is generated by
t_{i}
and
\frac{d}{dt_{j}}
for
i,j=1,...,n
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a differential operator of order
k
. Consider
D|_{R_{\leq k}}:R_{\leq k}\to R
. By Lemma
2.9
, there is
\tilde{D}\in Diff^{k}(R)
that lies in the subalgebra generated by
t_{i}
and
\frac{d}{dt_{j}}
and that satisfies
D|_{R_{\leq k}}=\tilde{D}|_{R_{\leq k}}
. The differential operator
D-\tilde{D}\in Diff^{k}(R)
vanishes on all polynomials of degree
\leq k
by construction and therefore, by Lemma
2.8
,
D=\tilde{D}
.
∎

Exercise 2.11
.

Prove that
Diff^{\bullet}(R)
is an
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra generated by
t_{1},...,t_{n},\frac{d}{dt_{1}},...,\frac{d}{dt_{n}}
with relations

[t_{i},t_{j}]=0,\quad\left[\frac{d}{dt_{i}},\frac{d}{dt_{j}}\right]=0,\quad\forall i,j,

\left[t_{i},\frac{d}{dt_{i}}\right]=1,

\left[\frac{d}{dt_{i}},t_{j}\right]=0,\quad i\neq j.

2.1.2
Differential operators over the ring of smooth functions

In this subsection, we consider differential operators over the ring
C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
of smooth function on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We denote by
x_{i}
the standard coordinate functions
x_{i}:(p_{1},...p_{n})\mapsto p_{i}.

Let
m_{x}=\{f\in R\ |f(x)=0\}
be the maximal ideal of smooth functions vanishing at point
x
. By Exercise
2.6
(b), for any differential operator of order
i
and
f\in m_{x}^{i+1}
we have
D(f)=0
. This observation allows us to prove the following key lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Any differential operator
D\in Diff^{i}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
that vanishes on polynomials of degree
\leq i
is trivial.

Proof.

Consider
D\in Diff^{i}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
that vanishes on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq i}
. Take any
f\in C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and any point
x\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Take the Taylor polynomial
P
for function
f
of degree
i
at point
x
. We have

f-P\in m_{x}^{i+1}.

By the observation above and the assumption that
D
vanishes on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq i}
we conclude

D(f-P)\in m_{x}\quad\implies\quad D(f-P)=0\quad\implies\quad D(f)=D(P)+D(f-P)=0.

∎

Next we prove an analogue of Proposition
2.10
for
Diff^{\bullet}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proposition 2.13
.

The algebra
Diff^{\bullet}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
is generated by
\frac{d}{dx_{i}}
as a
C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-algebra.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
2.9
, given an
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map

A:\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq k}\to C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

we can explicitly construct a differential operator

D\in C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right>\subset Diff^{k}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

whose restriction to
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq k}
coincides with
A
as a sum of differential operators

D=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{n}\leq k}D_{A(x_{1}^{i_{n}}...x_{n}^{i_{n}}),i_{1}...i_{n}}\ ,

D_{f,i_{1}...i_{n}}=\frac{f(x_{1},...,x_{n})}{i_{1}!\cdot...\cdot i_{n}!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{\circ i_{1}}\circ...\circ\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{\circ i_{n}},\quad f\in C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Now consider an arbitrary differential operator
D
of order
k
. Consider the restriction

D|_{\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq k}}:\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq k}\to C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

As above, construct a differential operator
\tilde{D}
of order
i
as a sum of
D_{f,i_{1},...,i_{n}}
with the property
D=\tilde{D}
on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]_{\leq k}
.

Consider
\overline{D}=D-\tilde{D}\in Diff^{k}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})).
By construction
\overline{D}
vanishes on all polynomials of order
\leq k
. By Lemma
2.12

\overline{D}=0
or, equivalently

D=\tilde{D}\in C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right>\subset Diff^{k}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})).

∎

This way, in the case
R=C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
Proposition
2.13
allows to relate Definition
2.2
to the historic analytical definition of a differential operator
D\in Diff^{k}(C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
as an expression

D=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{n}\leq k}f_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}(x_{1},...,x_{n})\frac{\partial^{i_{1}+...+i_{n}}}{\partial x_{1}^{i_{1}}...x_{n}^{i_{n}}},\quad f_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

The key Definition
2.2
can be sheafified if one replaces a ring
R
with a
sheaf
of algebras
\mathcal{R}
on a topological space
X
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a sheaf of (commutative)
k
-algebras on a topological space
X
. A
sheaf of differential operators on

\bm{\mathcal{R}}
is a functor

\{U\subset X\ \text{open}\}^{op}\to\text{Filtered $k$-algebras},

U\mapsto Diff^{\bullet}(\mathcal{R}(U)),

where
Diff^{\bullet}(\mathcal{R}(U))
is the filtered algebra of differential operators over
\mathcal{R}(U)
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. One readily checks that this assignment satisfies the sheaf axioms.

We will denote this sheaf by
Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}}
or simply by
Diff^{\bullet}
if the underlying sheaf
\mathcal{R}
is clear from the context. Observe that after forgetting the filtration the sheaf of differential operators is a subsheaf of the sheaf of internal homomorphisms

Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathscr{H}\text{\kern-3.0pt{\large om}}\,_{k}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}),

where homomorphisms are taken with respect to the
k
-vector space structure of
\mathcal{R}
.

Finally, if
D\in Diff^{i}_{\mathcal{R}}(U)=Diff^{i}(\mathcal{R}(U))
, then we say that
D
is a
differential operator of order
\bm{i}
on
\bm{U}
.

2.2
Algebra of symbols of differential operators over a ring

Given a
k
-algebra
R
or a sheaf of
k
-algebras
\mathcal{R}
, consider the filtered algebra of differential operators

Diff^{0}(R)\subset Diff^{1}(R)\subset Diff^{2}(R)\subset...\subset Diff^{\bullet}(R)

and the filtered sheaf of algebras of differential operators

Diff^{0}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset Diff^{1}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset Diff^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset...\subset Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}}.

Proposition 2.15
.

The associated graded algebra
\oplus_{i\geq 0}Diff^{i}(R)/Diff^{i-1}(R)
and the associated graded sheaf of algebras
\oplus_{i\geq 0}Diff^{i}_{\mathcal{R}}/Diff^{i-1}_{\mathcal{R}}
are commutative.

Proof.

By Exercise
2.7
, for
D^{i}\in Diff^{i}(R),D^{j}\in Diff^{j}(R)
, we have

[D^{i},D^{j}]=D^{i}\circ D^{j}-D^{j}\circ D^{i}\in Diff^{i+j-1}(R).

Hence as elements of
Diff^{i+j}(R)

D^{i}\circ D^{j}\equiv D^{j}\circ D^{i}\quad\mod Diff^{i+j-1}(R).

∎

Definition 2.16
.

The associated graded algebra

Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}(R)):=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Symb^{i}:=gr(Diff^{\bullet}(R))=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Diff^{i}(R)/Diff^{i-1}(R)

is called the
algebra of symbols of differential operators over

\bm{R}
. Note that
S^{0}=Diff^{0}(R)
is canonically isomorphic to
R
, so
Symb(Diff^{\bullet}(R))
is a (commutative)
R
-algebra.

We can similarly define a
sheaf of (commutative)
\bm{\mathcal{R}}
-algebras of symbols of differential operators over
\bm{\mathcal{R}}
:

Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}}):=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Symb^{i}:=gr(Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}})=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Diff^{i}_{\mathcal{R}}/Diff^{i-1}_{\mathcal{R}}.

Here

Example 2.17
.

Let us show that
Symb^{1}=Diff^{1}(R)/Diff^{0}(R)
is isomorphic to the
R
-module
Der(R)
of derivations of
R
.

Proof.

In Example
2.4
we showed that every derivation is also a differential operator of order
1
. This way we get a morphism

Der(R)\xhookrightarrow{}Diff^{1}(R)\twoheadrightarrow Diff^{1}(R)/Diff^{0}(R).

The inverse morphism is defined by

Diff^{1}(R)\ni D\to D-m_{D(1)}\in Der(R).

One easily checks that
D-m_{D(1)}
is indeed a derivation. Note that for
D=m_{a}\in Diff^{0}(R)
the difference
D-m_{D(1)}=m_{a}-m_{a\cdot 1}=0
, so the inverse morphism is in fact defined as a map

Diff^{1}(R)/Diff^{0}(R)\to Der(R).

∎

Proposition 2.18
.

There is a natural homomorphism of graded commutative
R
-algebras

Sym^{*}_{R}(Der(R))=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Sym^{i}_{R}(Der(R))\to\oplus_{i\geq 0}Symb^{i}=Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}(R))

that extends the isomorphism
Der(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}Symb^{1}
,
and an analogous natural homomorphism of sheaves of graded commutative
\mathcal{R}
-algebras

Sym^{*}(Der_{\mathcal{R}})\to Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}}).

Proof.

Consider the following diagram of solid arrows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Sym^{*}(Der(R))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}(R))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Der(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Symb^{1}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

By Proposition
2.15
the target algebra
Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}(R))
is commutative.
The upper horizontal dashed arrow is then just the universal property of the symmetric algebra applied to the bottom isomorphism of Example
2.17
followed by the inclusion
{Symb^{1}\xhookrightarrow{}Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}(R))}
on the right of the commutative diagram.
∎

2.3
Algebra of symbols of differential operators over the ring of smooth functions on a manifold

In this section we will specialize the results of §
2.2
on the symbols of differential operators to the setting of differential operators on a smooth manifold
M
.

Consider the following objects:

•

the ring
R=C^{\infty}(M)
of smooth functions on a smooth manifold
M
,

•

the sheaf
\mathcal{R}=C^{\infty}_{M}
of smooth functions on
M
(so that
R=C^{\infty}(M)=\Gamma(M,C^{\infty}_{M})=\Gamma(M,\mathcal{R})
),

•

the algebra
Diff^{\bullet}(M):=Diff^{\bullet}(C^{\infty}(M))
of global differential operators on
M
,

•

the sheaf of algebras
Diff^{\bullet}_{M}:=Diff^{\bullet}_{C^{\infty}_{M}}
of differential operators on
M
in the sense of Definition
2.14
(so that
{Diff^{\bullet}(M)=\Gamma(M,Diff^{\bullet}_{M}))}
.

While the similarity of notation for the global and local objects might appear slightly confusing at first, the reader will soon find that working with sheafified objects is almost identical to working with global objects. This justifies our choice of notation.

The well known equivalence between vector fields and derivations of the sheaf of smooth functions
C^{\infty}_{M}
yields an isomorphism of sheaves

Der(C^{\infty}_{M})\simeq TM,

where we identify the tangent bundle
TM
with the locally free sheaf of its sections (vector fields). In this setting, the morphism of Proposition
2.18
becomes

Sym^{*}(TM)\to Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}_{M}).

The first goal of this section will be to prove the following key theorem.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. The natural homomorphism of sheaves

Sym^{*}(TM)\to Symb^{*}(Diff^{\bullet}_{M})

is an isomorphism.

An immediate corollary of this theorem is that the sheaf of symbols of differential operators on a manifold is
locally free
. We will prove the above theorem as a series of lemmas.

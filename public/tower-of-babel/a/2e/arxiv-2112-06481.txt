Giant enhancement of atom-molecule interaction with dynamic magnetic fields


Abstract




1
abstract

We demonstrated that the inelastic collisions of ultracold atom and heteronuclear molecule can be manipulated and decreased or accelerated by several orders of magnitude in laboratory practicable dynamic magnetic fields. The mechanism of dynamic magnetic field control is based on matching the spectral peak of the modulation function of pulsed magnetic field with the Floquet engineered resonance structure of the collision. The resulting insight provides novel recipes to implement resonant Quantum Zeno Effect(QZE) and Anti-Zeno Effect (AZE) for specifically chosen reaction channels, and a universal strategy for selecting the final products of atomic and molecular interactions.

2
introduction

Adjusting microcosmic chemical reactions through external fields has always been the ultimate target in chemical reaction dynamics. This goal has stimulated the development of quantum control schemes, which have achieved amazing results in unimolecule chemical reactions. Attaining control over molecular reactions with simple physical pictures, however, has proven to be a much bigger challenge due to the complexity of the molecular interaction in the rotational, translational and spin degrees of freedom of the reactants. This complexity may be reduced by cooling molecules to low temperatures, suppressing the generation of higher energy excited states. Recent experiments and calculations demonstrated that inelastic collisions in an ultracold gas of CaH and He molecules, or NH and Mg molecules, can be effectively tuned by applying a static magnetic field. For molecular systems with magnetic moments induced by spins, an external magnetic field will vary the asymptotic energies of different molecular states, which eventually change the reaction dynamics between molecules.

Despite tuning the scattering cross sections well, the effects of varying external fields on the product state distributions and branching ratios for the different reaction channels remain largely unknown. For the transition between bound states, Zhou et al. found that the transition rate can be reduced by using a single frequency periodic magnetic field. In addition, for the scattering process with same initial and final channels,
D.Smith proposed that a single-frequency periodic magnetic field can enhence the pairwise interactions resonantly. At the same time, the rates of low-temperature collisions can be sensitive to scattering resonances, then we may drastically change the rate of a certain channel by changing the external field to tune the resonance. Furthermore, enhancing the rate of specifically chosen reaction channels stimulated the development of quantum control schemes such as optimal control and coherent control [cite M. Shapiro and P. Brumer, Quantum Control of Molecular Processes (Wiley-VCH, Weinheim, 2012). and S. A. Rice and M. Zhao, Optical Control of Molecular Dynamics (Wiley, New York, 2000).]

In this letter, we report the (first ?! to be confirmed) demonstration of resonant Quantum Zeno effect(RQZE) in scattering process of atom and molecule in a time-varying external field. For inelastic scattering, the resonance structure in the coupling spectrum between the incident channel and the outgoing channel is broadened, so that a multi-period combined magnetic field is needed to achieve resonance-enhanced inelastic collisions. Here we demonstrate an efficient method that permits inverse engineering of external control fields, which has clear physical pictures, then we may determine a suitable pulsed magnetic field. Using a newly developed multimode Floquet coupled channel theory in the space-fixed (SF) coordinate frame, we show that the cross section of the inelastic spin flip channel of atom-diatom scattering
O_{2}(M_{S}=+1)+He\to O_{2}(M_{S}=0,-1)+He
at low collision energies can be
precisely controlled through laboratory-realizable pulsed magnetic field. This work suggests that a wide range of related scattering problems involving time-varying fields are now submissive to theoretical study via RQZE model.

3
theory

We begin by outlining the multimode Floquet quantum scattering approach. For nonreactive collisions of diatomic molecules in the electronic states of
{}^{2}\Sigma
and
{}^{3}\Sigma
symmetry with structureless atoms in varying magnetic field, which changes periodically over time, we ues Floquet theory to simplify this problem. The Floquet Hamiltonian for the atom-molecule system may be written as (in units of
\hbar
)

\hat{H}_{F}=-\frac{1}{2\mu R}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial R^{2}}R+\frac{\hat{l}^{2}}{2\mu R^{2}}+\hat{V}(\textbf{R},\textbf{r})+\hat{H}_{as}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t},

(1)

where
R
is the atom-molecule separation vector,
\mu
is the reduced mass of the collision complex,
\hat{l}
is the orbital angular momontum for collision, and
r
is the separation vector between the atoms in the diatomic molecule. For diatomic molecules in its vibrational ground state, the distance between the atoms is constant, so the interaction potential
\hat{V}(\textbf{R},\textbf{r})
can be written as
\hat{V}(\textbf{R},\theta)
, where
\theta
is the angle between
R
and
r
.

The asymptotic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{as}
describes the rotational structure of a
{}^{2}\Sigma
molecule in its electronic and vibrational ground state in the presence of a periodic magnetic field
B(t)
,

\hat{H}_{as}=B_{e}\hat{N}^{2}+\gamma\hat{N}\cdot\hat{S}+2\mu_{0}B(t)\hat{M}_{S},

(2)

where
\hat{N}
is the rotational angular momentum,
\hat{S}
is the electron spin and
\hat{M}_{S}
is the projection of
\hat{S}
on the magnetic field axis,. For a
{}^{3}\Sigma
molecule, the asymptotic Hamiltonian can be obtained by adding to Eq.(2) a term describing the spin-spin interaction

\hat{V}_{SS}=\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{SS}\left(\frac{24\pi}{5}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{q=-2}^{2}(-)^{q}Y_{2,-q}(\hat{r})[\hat{S}\otimes\hat{S}]_{q}^{(2)},

(3)

where
[\hat{S}\otimes\hat{S}]_{q}^{(2)}
is a spherical tensor product of
\hat{S}
with itself.

We combine the last two terms of Eq.(1) and get the asymptotic Floquet Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{asF}=\hat{H}_{as}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
, then the remaining part of the Hamiltonian is independent of time. The total wave function of the collision complex can be expanded in a direct product basis,

\Psi=R^{-1}\sum_{\alpha KlM_{l}}F^{lM_{l}}_{\alpha K}(R)\Phi_{\alpha K}|{l,M_{l}}\rangle

(4)

where
M_{l}
denotes the projection of
\hat{l}
on the magnetic field axis,
\Phi_{\alpha K}
are the eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{asF}
, and
K
is an integer that denotes different Floquet states. Assume the angular frequency of the magnetic field is
\omega_{B}
and
B(t)=B_{0}+f(t)
, where
B_{0}
is the static part,
f(t)
is the periodic part whose average is zero. To solve the eigenvalue problem of
\hat{H}_{asF}
, we expand the eigenfunctions as

\Phi_{\alpha K}=\sum_{NM_{N}SM_{S}n}W_{\alpha K,NM_{N}SM_{S}n}|NM_{N}SM_{S}n\rangle,

(5)

where
M_{N}
is the projections of
N
on the magnetic field axis,
|n\rangle
are Fourier vectors that satisfy
\langle t|n\rangle=e^{in\omega_{B}t}
. Since the asymptotic Hamiltonian is periodic in time, it may be expanded in a Fourier series

\langle\alpha|\hat{H}_{as}|\beta\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}H_{\alpha\beta}^{n}e^{in\omega_{B}t},

(6)

then
H_{asF}
can be expressed as the time-independent Floquet Hamiltonian with matrix elements
\langle\alpha n|H_{asF}|\beta m\rangle=H^{n-m}_{\alpha\beta}+n\omega_{B}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{nm}
. According to the Floquet theory, the difference between the eigenvalues of
\Phi_{\alpha K}
and
\Phi_{\alpha,K+p}
is
p\omega_{B}
. This can be understood as the periodic magnetic field
B(t)
introducing a kind of quasi-particle, and the energy of one quasi-particle is
\omega_{B}
. The atom-molecule system of is coupled to the quasi-particle system, then one channel will split into an infinite number of different levels, each corresponds to a different number of quasi-particles. Notice that the time-varying field
f(t)

determines the coupling between different quasi-particle states, while the bias field
B_{0}
affects the coupling within the same particle number state.

The total wave function
\Psi
are eigenstates of
H_{F}
,
H_{F}\Psi=E_{F}\Psi
. The radial expansion coefficients
F^{lM_{l}}_{\alpha K}(R)
satisfy a system of coupled-channel(CC) equations

\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dR^{2}}-\frac{l(l+1)}{R^{2}}+k^{2}_{\beta K^{\prime}}\right]F^{l^{\prime}M^{\prime}_{l}}_{\beta K^{\prime}}(R)=2\mu\sum_{\alpha KlM_{l}}\langle l^{\prime}M^{\prime}_{l}|\langle\phi_{\beta K^{\prime}}|\hat{V}(\textbf{R},\theta)|\phi_{\alpha K}\rangle|lM_{l}\rangle F^{lM_{l}}_{\alpha K}(R),

(7)

where
k^{2}_{\beta K^{\prime}}=2\mu(E_{tot}-\epsilon_{\beta K^{\prime}})
,
E_{F}
is the Floquet energy and
\epsilon_{\beta K^{\prime}}
is the asymptotic energy of
\Phi_{\beta K^{\prime}}
. The solution is subject to the boundary conditions

F^{l^{\prime}M^{\prime}_{l}}_{\beta K^{\prime}}(R)\to\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{KK^{\prime}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{M_{l}M^{\prime}_{l}}exp[-i(k_{\alpha K}R-l\pi/2)]-\left(\frac{k_{\alpha K}}{k_{\beta K^{\prime}}}\right)^{1/2}S_{\alpha KlM_{l};\beta K^{\prime}l^{\prime}M^{\prime}_{l}}exp[i(k_{\beta K^{\prime}}R-l^{\prime}\pi/2)]

(8)

gives the
S
-matrix or the probability amplitudes for transitions between the eigenstates of
H_{asF}
. The total angular momentum projection
M
is conserved for collisions in a magnetic field, so computations can be carried out independently for different values of
M
. The cross section for elastic and inelastic scattering are computed from the
S
-matrix according to

\sigma_{\alpha K\to\beta K^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}_{\alpha K}}\sum_{M}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}\sum_{M_{l}M^{\prime}_{l}}\left|\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{KK^{\prime}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{M_{l}M^{\prime}_{l}}-S^{M}_{\alpha KlM_{l};\beta K^{\prime}l^{\prime}M^{\prime}_{l}}\right|^{2}.

(9)

The detailed derivation process is given in the Supplemental Material .

We now apply this methodology to study the effects of periodic magnetic fields on the inelastic collision
CaH(M_{s}=1/2)+He\xrightarrow{}CaH(M_{s}=-1/2)+He
. The choice of the collision is motivated by the substantial shape resonance in inelastic cross sections with potential computed by RCCSD(T). In a static magnetic field
B_{0}
, the CaH molecule will undergo Zeeman relaxation
M_{S}=1/2\to M_{S}=-1/2
in the N=0 state. The initial collision energy may be written as
T_{in}
, since the energy of the collision system is conserved during the scattreing process, the kinetic energy after the inelastic collision will be
T_{out}=T_{in}+2\mu_{0}B_{0}
. In the presence of a periodic magnetic field
B(t)=B_{0}+f(t)
, however, the coupling with quasi-particles may change the kinetic energy
T_{out}
. Assume the incident state is
\Phi_{\alpha 0}=|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=1/2\rangle|K=0\rangle
, because the Floquet energy
E_{F}=T_{out}+\epsilon_{\beta K^{\prime}}
is conserved, the emergent kinetic energy is still
T_{in}+2\mu_{0}B_{0}
if the final state is
\Phi_{\beta 0}=|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|K^{\prime}=0\rangle
. Since the asymptotic energy of
\Phi_{\beta K^{\prime}+p}
is
\epsilon_{\beta K^{\prime}+p}=\epsilon_{\beta K^{\prime}}+p\omega_{B}
, so if the emergent state is
\Phi_{\beta p}=|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|K^{\prime}=p\rangle
,
T_{out}
will be
T_{in}+2\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B}
. But notice that the quasi-particles energy
p\omega_{B}
is not observable experimentally. We assume that the asymptotic energy of
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=1/2\rangle
in the magnetic field
B(t)
is
\mu_{0}B(t)
, then the asymptotic energy of
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle
is
-\mu_{0}B(t)
and the total energy before the inelastic collision is
E_{in}=T_{in}+\mu_{0}B(t)=T_{in}+\mu_{0}B_{0}+\mu_{0}f(t)
. After the spin-flipping
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=1/2\rangle|K=0\rangle\to|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|K^{\prime}=p\rangle
the total energy will be
E_{out}=T_{out}-\mu_{0}B(t)=T_{in}+\mu_{0}B_{0}-\mu_{0}f(t)-p\omega_{B}
, i.e. Zeeman relaxation will produce a series of evenly spaced energy levels whose energy separation is
\omega_{B}
due to the influence of the periodic magnetic field.

On the other hand, inelastic scattering process can be viewed as a transition from initial state
|e\rangle
to a series of final states
\{|p\rangle\}
, where
|e\rangle
in the He-CaH system is
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=1/2\rangle|K=0\rangle
and
|p\rangle
is
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|K^{\prime}=p\rangle
. If the collision energy
T_{in}
is constat, we can neglect it in the eigenvalues of
|e\rangle
and
\{|p\rangle\}
. The total Hamiltonian is
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}(t)+H_{1}(t)
，where

\hat{H}_{0}=2\mu_{0}B_{0}|e\rangle\langle e|+\sum_{p}(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B})|p\rangle\langle p|,

(10)

with
\mu_{0}B_{0}
and
\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B}
being the energies of
|e\rangle
and
|p\rangle
that are independent of time, respectively; considering the kinetic energy
T_{out}
of
|p\rangle
is
2\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B}
, we add an irrelevant energy
\mu_{0}B_{0}
to the above formula to facilitate subsequent discussion.

\hat{V}(t)=\sum_{p}V_{ep}(t)|e\rangle\langle p|+H.c.,

(11)

standing for the off-diagonal coupling of
|e\rangle
with other states, which is determined by the scattering process and the specific form of the periodic field
B(t)
. Since the time scale of the scattering process is infinitely long,
V_{ep}(t)
should be understood as the average of coupling effects and are independent of t; and

\hat{H}_{1}(t)=\mu_{0}f(t)|e\rangle\langle e|-\sum_{p}\mu_{0}f(t)|p\rangle\langle p|,

(12)

denoting the diagonal time-dependant perturbations of the energies due to the evolution of magnetic field with time.

The wave function of this multi-state system at time
t
can be written as
c_{e}(t)e^{-i\mu_{0}B_{0}t-i\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{0}f(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}|e\rangle+\sum_{p}c_{p}(t)e^{-i(\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B})t+i\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{0}f(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}|p\rangle
, the initial condition being
c_{e}(t)=1,c_{p}(t)=0
. Inducing a modulation function
\epsilon(t)=exp[-i\int_{0}^{t}2\mu_{0}f(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}]
, it may be expanded in terms of Fourier series
\epsilon(t)=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}e^{ik\omega t}
. When
t>>\omega_{B}
, the variation of amplitude of level
|e\rangle
can be represent in the form
|c_{e}(t)|^{2}=exp[-R(t)\,t]
, where
R(t)
may be reduced to
R=2\pi\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})
. Here
G(\omega)=\sum_{p}|V_{ep}|^{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{p})
is the coupling spectrum that is determined by the atom-molecule system, where
\omega_{p}=2\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B}
is the kinetic energy of the unperturbed Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
of the final state
|p\rangle=|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|p\rangle
, i.e.,
G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-p\omega_{B})
indicates the the coupling strength between
|e\rangle
and
|p\rangle
. The coefficient R describes the scattering rate and, when the incident particle particle flow is constant, is proportional to the total inelastic cross section. In the static magnetic field
B_{0}
, the initial state
|e\rangle
can only jump to
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|0\rangle
and the inelastic cross section
\sigma^{0}
will be proportional to
R=2\pi G(2\mu_{0}B_{0})
. Let the total inelastic cross section under the periodic magnetic field
B(t)=B_{0}+f(t)
be
\sigma_{tot}
, according to
\epsilon(t)=exp[-i\int_{0}^{t}2\mu_{0}f(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}]=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}e^{-ik\omega t}
, we may obtain the ratio of
\sigma_{tot}
to
\sigma^{0}
that is
\sigma_{tot}/\sigma^{0}=\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}\frac{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})}{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0})}
. As a result, by adjusting the time-varying part
f(t)
of
B(t)
, we may change the size of the inelastic scattering cross section while keeping the static magnetic field
B_{0}
constant.

Take
\sigma_{k}
as the cross section from
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=1/2\rangle|0\rangle
to
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|k\rangle
, since
\sigma_{tot}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}\propto\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})
, the scattering cross section will satisfy
\sigma_{k}\propto|\lambda_{k}|^{2}G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})
. Given a trial magnetic field pulse, we can directly obtain
|\lambda_{k}|^{2}
and use the above algorithm to calculate the inelastic cross section
\sigma_{k}
numerically. Then the relative value of
G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})
may be obtained from
\sigma_{k}/|\lambda_{k}|^{2}
. For He-CaH system with the collision energy of
3\times 10^{-5}cm^{-1}
, use the potential calculated by RCCSD(T), the coupling spectrum
G(\omega)
is illustrated as Figure.2. Notice that
\sigma_{tot}/\sigma^{0}=\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}\frac{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})}{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0})}
, so y-axis here is
\frac{G(\omega)}{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0})}
and x-axis indicates the output kinetic energy
T_{out}
of the final state
|N=0,M_{N}=0,M_{S}=-1/2\rangle|k\rangle
.

Notice that there is an extremely sharp resonance peak around
T_{out}=0.02cm^{-1}
, and the probability of the spin-flipping transition increases by two to three orders of magnitude through the resonance. What’s more, when we change the static magnetic field
B_{0}
or the time-varying magnetic field
f(t)
, the position of the resonance peak relative to the kinetic energy
T_{out}
does not shift. Actually, the substantial peak arises from a shape resonance and the spectrum
G(\omega)
represents the relationship between inelastic scattering cross section and kinetic energy. Since the effective range of the interaction potential between He and CaH is about
10^{-9}m
, when the output energy
T_{out}<0.001cm^{-1}
, it is appropriate to use
Wigner’s threshold laws to express the asymptotic behavior of the cross section. Keep the collision energy
T_{in}
constant, specifically, the dependency of
G(\omega)
with
T_{out}
will be
G(T_{out})\propto T_{out}^{l^{\prime}+1/2}
, where
l^{\prime}
denotes the final partial wave. When the output kinetic energy approaches zero, this give a coupling spectrum
G(T_{out})
proportional to
T_{out}^{3}/2
, which implies the final partial wave is
l^{\prime}=1
. Because the
l=0\to l^{\prime}=1
transition is forbidden, the leading contribution to the
M_{S}=1/2\to M_{S}=-1/2
cross section in our condition will be from the
l=1\to l^{\prime}=1
transition, i.e. the p-wave scattering.

Due to the existence of substantial resonant peak in the scattering cross section, acceleration of the collision cross section can now be easily implemented by matching the peak of
\lambda_{k}
with shape resonance in the coupling spectrum. Specifically, we choose a periodic magnetic field
B(t)=B_{0}+f(t),\;f(t)=C_{1}cos\omega t+C_{2}cos2\omega t+C_{3}cos3\omega t
, where
B_{0}=150Gauss,\;\omega=0.003212cm^{-1},\;C_{1}=0.005614cm^{-1},\;C_{2}=0.005616cm^{-1},\;C_{3}=0.005621cm^{-1}
. According to
\epsilon(t)=exp[-i\int_{0}^{t}2\mu_{0}f(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}]=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}e^{ik\omega t}
, the distribution of
|\lambda_{k}|^{2}
is plotted in Figure.3. Notice that the maximum value of
|\lambda_{k}|^{2}
coincides with the position of the resonance peak in
G(\omega)
, because of
\sigma_{tot}/\sigma^{0}=\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}\frac{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0}-k\omega_{B})}{G(2\mu_{0}B_{0})}
, then the ratio of
\sigma_{tot}
under
B(t)
to
\sigma^{0}
under
B_{0}=150Gauss
will be approximately 100. The calculation shows
\sigma_{tot}=7.2604\times 10^{-7},\;\sigma^{0}=7.5611\times 10^{-9}
and
\sigma_{tot}/\sigma^{0}\approx 96
, which is consistent with the theoretical prediction.

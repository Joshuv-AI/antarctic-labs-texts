Abstract
Quadratic conjecture is a strengthening of oliver’s p p -group conjecture. Let G G be a p p -group of maximal class of order p n p^{n} . We prove that if n ≤ 8 n\leq 8 or n ≥ max ⁡ { 2 ​ p − 6 , p + 2 } n\geq\max\{2p-6,p+2\} then G G satisfies Quadratic Conjecture. Hence quadratic conjecture holds if G G is a p p -group of maximal class where p ≤ 7 p\leq 7 . Introduction

An open question in the theory of
p
-local finite groups claims that every fusion system has a unique
p
-completed classifying space. In
[
7
]
, Oliver introduced the characteristic subgroup
\mathfrak{X}(S)
for a finite
p
-group
S
. For odd primes he demonstrated that the conjecture outlined below would substantiate the unique existence of the classifying space. Recall that
J(S)
denotes the Thompson subgroup of
S
generated by all elementary abelian subgroups of the greatest rank.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
7
, Conjecture 3.9]
For any odd prime
p
and any
p
-group
S
,
J(S)\leq\mathfrak{X}(S)
.

Conjecture
1.1
is called Oliver’s p-group conjecture.
Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and
V
an elementary abelian
p
-group
on which
G
acts faithfully. Then
V
is an
F-module
for
G
if there exists a non-trivial
elementary abelian subgroup
E
of
G
such that
|E|\cdot|C_{V}(E)|\geq|V|
. In this case, we call
E

an
offender
.

In
[
1
]
, Green, H
\acute{e}
thelyi and Lilienthal obtained the following reformulation of Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
1
, Conjecture 1.3]
Let
p
be an odd prime and
G
a finite
p
-group. If the faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
V
is an
F
-module, then
there is an element
1\neq g\in\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
such that the minimal polynomial of the action of
g
on
V
divides
(x-1)^{p-1}
.

An element
g\in G
is said to be

quadratic
on the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
V
if its action has minimal polynomial
(X-1)^{2}
. Note that if
V
is
faithful then quadratic elements must have order
p
.

In
[
3
]
, Green, H
\acute{e}
thelyi and Mazza propose the following strengthening of Conjecture
1.2
.

Quadratic conjecture

[
3
, Conjecture 1.4]
Let
p
be a prime and
G
a finite
p
-group. If the faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
V
is an
F
-module, then
there are quadratic elements in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
.

Observe that Quardatic Conjecture is trivially true for
p=2
. For
p=3
, Quardatic Conjecture is just Conjecture
1.2
. By
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, Quadratic Conjecture holds if
G
is metabelian or of (nilpotence) class at most four. By
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
, Conjecture
1.2
holds if
G
is a
p
-group of maximal class. Hence Quadratic Conjecture holds if
G
is a
3
-group of maximal class. In this paper, we get the following main result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
G
a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{n}
. If
n\leq 8
or
n\geq\max\{2p-6,p+2\}
then
G
satisfies
Quadratic Conjecture.

By Theorem
1.3
, quadratic conjecture holds if
G
is a
p
-group of maximal class where
p\leq 7
.

2
Quadratic Offenders

Definition 2.1
.

[
6
, 11, 2.3]
Let
G
be a finite group and
V
a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module. For a
subgroup
H\leq G
one sets

j_{H}(V):=\frac{|H||C_{V}(H)|}{|V|}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Note that
j_{1}(V)=1
. Suppose that
E
is an element abelian subgroup of
G
. Then
E
is an offender on
V
if and only if
j_{E}(V)\geq 1
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
4
, 26.5]
Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and
V
a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module. We denote by
\mathscr{E}(G)

the poset of non-trivial elementary abelian subgroups of
G
. Define

\mathscr{P}(G,V):=\{E\leq G\mid E\in\mathscr{E}(G)\ \mbox{and}\ j_{E}(V)\geq j_{F}(V)\ \forall\ 1\leq F\leq E\}.

Note that
V
is an F-module if and only if
\mathscr{P}(G,V)
is non-empty. The subgroups
in
\mathscr{P}(G,V)
are sometimes called
best offenders
.

A
quadratic offender
is a subgroup which is both quadratic
(
[V,E,E]=0
) and an offender.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Timmesfeld’s replacement theorem)
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
Let
V
be a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
and
E\in\mathscr{P}(G,V)
. Then there is a quadratic offender
F\in\mathscr{P}(G,V)
which satisfies
j_{F}(V)=j_{E}(V)
and
F\leq E
.

By Timmesfeld’s replacement theorem, A minimal offender must be a quadratic offender.

Following from
[
3
]
, an abelian subgroup
A
of
G
is said to be
weakly closed
if

[A,A^{g}]\neq 1
holds for every
G
-conjugate
A^{g}\neq A
. The following theorem show that there is also a weakly closed quadratic offender on F-module
V
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
3
, Proposition 4.5]

Suppose that the faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
V
is an F-module. Set

j_{0}=\max\{j_{E}(V)\mid E\ \mbox{an offender}\}.

Then there is a weakly closed quadratic offender
E
with
j_{E}(V)=j_{0}
.
Moreover if
D\leq G
is any offender with
j_{D}(V)=j_{0}
, then there is such an
E
which is a subgroup of the
normal closure of
D
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
a finite
p
-group and
V
an
F
-module. Suppose that there is no quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
. Then

(1)

|E|\geq p^{2}
for any offender
E
on
V
;

(2)

|E|\geq p^{3}
for any weakly closed offender
E
on
V
.

Proof
(1) Otherwise, there is an offender
E
of order
p
. Since
V
is faithful,
C_{V}(E)\neq V
. Since
E
is an offender,
|E||C_{V}(E)|\geq|V|
. Hence
C_{V}(E)
is maximal in
V
. Since
C_{V}(E)
is
Z(G)
-invariant,
[V,Z(G)]\leq C_{V}(E)
. Since
[V,Z(G)]\leq C_{V}(E^{g})
for all
g\in G
,
[V,Z(G)]\leq C_{V}(E^{G})
. Take
1\neq z\in Z(G)\cap E^{G}
. Then
[V,z,z]=0
. Hence
z
is a quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
. This contradicts the hypothesis.

(2) Otherwise, there is a weakly closed offender
E
of order
p^{2}
. By (1),
E
is a minimal offender. Hence
E
is also a quadratic offender. If
E\trianglelefteq G
, then
E\cap Z(G)\neq 1
. Hence there is a quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
. This contradicts the hypothesis. In the following, we may assume that
N_{G}(E)\neq G
. Let
g\in N_{G}(N_{G}(E))\setminus N_{G}(E)
. Then
N_{G}(E^{g})=N_{G}(E)^{g}=N_{G}(E)
and
E^{g}\neq E
. Hence
E
and
E^{g}
normalize each other. It follows that
[E,E^{g}]\leq E\cap E^{g}
. Since
E
is weakly closed,
[E,E^{g}]\neq 1
and hence
E\cap E^{g}\neq 1
.

Let
F=E\cap E^{g}
. By (1),
F
is not an offender. Hence
|C_{V}(E)|\leq|C_{V}(F)|\leq\frac{1}{p^{2}}|V|
. On the other hand, since
E
is an offender,
|C_{V}(E)|\geq\frac{1}{p^{2}}|V|
. Hence
|C_{V}(E)|=\frac{1}{p^{2}}|V|
and
C_{V}(F)=C_{V}(E)
. The same reason gives that
C_{V}(F)=C_{V}(E^{g})
.
Since
E
and
E^{g}
are both quadratic,
[V,E]\leq C_{V}(E)=C_{V}(F)=C_{V}(E^{g})
and
[V,E^{g}]\leq C_{V}(E^{g})=C_{V}(F)=C_{V}(E)
. It follows that
[V,E,E^{g}]=[V,E^{g},E]=0
. By Three Subgroups Lemma,
[V,[E,E^{g}]]=0
. Hence
V
is not faithful. This is a contradiction.
\Box

3
Quadratic elements

For finding quadratic element, Lemma 4.1 of
[
1
]
is a key result. In the following, we refine this lemma, and give an elementary proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
p
is an odd prime, that
G
is a non-trivial
p
-group, and that

V
is a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module. Suppose that
a,b\in G
are such that
c:=[a,b]
is a non-trivial element of

C_{G}(a,b)
. If
a
is quadratic, then
[V,E,E]=0
where
E=\langle a,c\rangle
.

Proof
Let
d=a^{b}=ac
and
e=a^{b^{2}}=ac^{2}=a^{-1}d^{2}
. Then
E=\langle a\rangle\times\langle d\rangle
. Since
a
is quadratic,
d
and
e
are also quadratic. Since
[a,d]=1
,
[v,a,d]=[v,d,a]
for all
v\in V
. Let
v\in V
and
i,j,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

[v,a^{i}d^{j},a^{k}d^{l}]=(il+jk)[v,a,d].

In particular,
[v,e,e]=-4[v,a,d]
. It follows from
[v,e,e]=0
that
[v,a,d]=0
. Hence
[v,a^{i}d^{j},a^{k}d^{l}]=0
for all
v\in V
and
i,j,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus we have
[V,E,E]=0
.

\Box

Recall that the following terminology. Given a finite group
G
, the ascending central series is
defined inductively by
Z_{0}(G)=1
and
Z_{r+1}(G)
is the normal subgroup of
G
containing
Z_{r}(G)
and such that
Z_{r+1}(G)/Z_{r}(G)=Z(G/Z_{r}(G))
, for all
r\geq 1
. The class
c
of
G
is the smallest number such that
Z_{c}(G)=G
. In this paper we use

G>G_{2}=G^{\prime}>\cdots>G_{c+1}=1

to denote the descending
central series of
G
, where
G_{r+1}=[G_{r},G]
for all
r\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group and
V
an F-module.
E
is a weakly closed quadratic offender. If there is no quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
, then

(1)

there is no quadratic element in
Z_{2}(G)
;

(2)

Z_{2}(G)\cap E=1
,
[Z_{2}(G),E]=1
;

(3)

[Z_{3}(G),E]=1
;

(4)

[Z_{4}(G),E]\leq Z_{3}(G)\cap E
.

Proof
(1) Otherwise, let
a
be a quadratic element in
Z_{2}(G)
. Then
a\not\in Z(G)
since there is no quadratic element in
Z(G)
. Hence there is
b\in G
such that
c:=[a,b]\neq 1
. Since
c\in Z(G)\leq C_{G}(a,b)
, by Lemma
3.1
,
c
is also a quadratic element. This is a contradiction.

(2) By (1),
Z_{2}(G)\cap E=1
. Since
[Z_{2}(G),E]\leq Z(G)
,
[E^{g},E]=1
for all
g\in Z_{2}(G)
. Since
E
weakly closed,
E^{g}=E
for all
g\in Z_{2}(G)
. It follows that
[Z_{2}(G),E]\leq Z(G)\cap E=1
.

(3) Since
[Z_{3}(G),E]\leq Z_{2}(G)
, by (2),
[Z_{3}(G),E,E]=1
. It follows that
[E^{g},E]=1
for all
g\in Z_{3}(G)
. Since
E
weakly closed,
E^{g}=E
for all
g\in Z_{3}(G)
. Furthermore,
[Z_{3}(G),E]\leq Z_{2}(G)\cap E=1
.

(4) Since
[Z_{4}(G),E]\leq Z_{3}(G)
, by (3),
[Z_{4}(G),E,E]=1
. It follows that
[E^{g},E]=1
for all
g\in Z_{4}(G)
. Since
E
weakly closed,
E^{g}=E
for all
g\in Z_{4}(G)
. Furthermore,
[Z_{4}(G),E]\leq Z_{3}(G)\cap E
.

\Box

Corollary 3.3
.

[

, Theorem 5.2]

Suppose that
G
is a
p
-group and
V
is a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module such that

\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
has no quadratic elements. If

Otherwise,
V
is an F-module. By Lemma

(4),
G=Z_{4}(G)
normalize

. Hence

, which is a contradiction.
\Box

[

, Theorem 1.5]

Let

-group and

a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]

(1)

If
A

(2)

Suppose that

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group and
V
a faithful
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module. If there is a quadratic offender
E
such that
E^{G}=EK
where

is an abelian normal subgroup of

, then there is a quadratic element in

Let
K
be a maximal member such that
E^{G}=EK
where

is an abelian normal subgroup of

.

If
K=E^{G}
, then
K
contains a offender

. By Theorem
3.4
(1), there is a quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
.

If

, then there is an

such that
N=\langle a\rangle K\unlhd G
. Hence
N^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{1}(Z(G))\neq 1
. Since

is abelian,
N^{\prime}=\{[a,x]\mid x\in K\}
. Hence there exists an

such that
1\neq[a,x]\in\Omega_{1}(Z(G))

,
[a,x]
is quadratic.

\Box

4
p
-groups of maximal class

Firstly we recall some terminology. Let
G
be a

p
-group. The (characteristic) subgroup of
G
generated by
\{g^{p}\mid g\in G\}
is denoted
by
\mho_{1}(G)
. Now suppose that
G
has order
p^{n}
and is of maximal class. Set

G_{1}=C_{G}(G_{2}/G_{4})
. One says that
G
is
exceptional
if
n\geq 5
and there is some

3\leq i\leq n-2
such that
C_{G}(G_{i}/G_{i+2})\neq G_{1}
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
5
, Definition 3.2.1]
The degree of commutativity
l
of a
p
-group
G
of maximal class is defined to be the maximum integer such that
[G_{i},G_{j}]\leq G_{i+j+l}
for all
i,j\geq 1
if
G_{1}
is not abelian, and
l=n-3
if
G_{1}
is abelian.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
5
, Corollary 3.2.7]
The degree of commutativity of
G
is positive if and only if
G
is not exceptional.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
5
, Corollary 3.3.4 (i)]
Let
G
be a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{n}
with
n>p+1
. Then
\Omega_{1}(G_{1})=G_{n-p+1}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
5
, Theorem 3.3.5]
Let
G
be a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{n}
with
n>p+1
. Then
G
has positive degree of commutativity.

Theorem 4.5
.

[
5
, Theorem 3.2.11]
Let
G
be a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{n}
where
n
is odd and
5\leq n\leq 2p+1
. Then
G
has positive degree of commutativity.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
G
a
p
-group of maximal class of order
p^{n}
. If
n\geq\max\{2p-6,p+2\}
then
G
satisfies
Quadratic Conjecture.

Proof
Otherwise, there is an F-module
V
such that there is
no quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
. By Theorem
2.4
, there is a weakly closed quadratic offender
E
on
V
.
If
E\trianglelefteq G
, then
G_{n-1}=Z(G)\leq E
and hence there is a quadratic element in
\Omega_{1}(Z(G))
. In the following, we may assume that
E<E^{G}
.
By Theorem
3.4
(1), we may assume that
E^{G}
is not abelian.

By Lemma
3.2
(3),
[Z_{3}(G),E]=1
. Hence
E\leq C_{G}(G_{n-3}/G_{n-1})
. Since
n\geq p+2
, by Theorem
4.2

\&

4.4
,
G
is not exceptional. Hence
E\leq G_{1}
. Furthermore,
E\leq\Omega_{1}(G_{1})
. By Theorem
4.3
,
\Omega_{1}(G_{1})=G_{n-p+1}
. It follows that
E\leq G_{n-p+1}
.

Since
E^{G}
is not abelian,
1\neq[E,E^{G}]
. Since
G
has positive degree of commutativity,
[G_{n-p+1},G_{n-p+1}]=[G_{n-p+1},G_{n-p+2}]\leq G_{2n-2p+4}
. Since
n\geq 2p-6
,
2n-2p+4\geq n-2
. Hence
1\neq[E,E^{G}]\leq[G_{n-p+1},G_{n-p+1}]\leq G_{n-2}=Z_{2}(G)
. Thus there are
a\in E
and
b\in E^{G}
such that
1\neq c:=[a,b]\in Z_{2}(G)
. By Lemma
3.2
(2),
c\in C_{G}(a,b)
. By Lemma
3.1
,
c
is a quadratic element. This contradicts Lemma
3.2
(1).

\Box

.

Let
G
be a

-group of maximal class with

Proof
Otherwise, there is an F-module
V
such that there is

\Omega_{1}(Z(G))

, there is a weakly closed quadratic offender

on

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
A
is an abelian normal subgroup of a non-abelian
p
-group
G
,
G/A=\langle xA\rangle
is cyclic,
B\leq A
and
B\unlhd G
. Then
[B,G]\cong B/B\cap Z(G)
.

Proof
Since
G/A
is cyclic,
G^{\prime}\leq A
. Let
\varphi:B\mapsto[B,G]
be a mapping defined as follows:
\varphi(b)=[b,x]
for
b\in B
. If
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
, then

\varphi(b_{1}b_{2})=[b_{1}b_{2},x]=[b_{1},x]^{b_{2}}[b_{2},x]=[b_{1},x][b_{2},x]=\varphi(b_{1})\varphi(b_{2}),

since
[b_{1},x],{b_{2}}\in A
and
A
is abelian. Hence
\varphi
is a homomorphism. If
b\in B
and
g\in G
, Then

[b,x]^{g}=[b^{g},x^{g}]=[b^{g},x[x,g]]=[b^{g},[x,g]][b^{g},x]^{[x,g]}=[b^{g},x],

since
b^{g},[x,g],[b^{g},x]\in A
and
A
is abelian. It follows that
\hbox{\rm Im}\varphi
is normal in
G
. Since
B
centralizes
G/\hbox{\rm Im}\varphi
,
[B,G]\leq\hbox{\rm Im}\varphi
. Hence
\hbox{\rm Im}\varphi=[B,G]
. Next,
\hbox{\rm Ker}\varphi=\{b\in B\mid[b,x]=1\}=C_{B}(x)=B\cap Z(G)
. Hence
\hbox{\rm Im}\varphi=[B,G]\cong B/B\cap Z(G)
.

\Box

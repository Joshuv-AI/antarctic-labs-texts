Skew Symplectic and Orthogonal Schur Functions
Naihuan JING a, Zhijun LI b and Danxia WANG b

Abstract
Using the vertex operator representations for symplectic and orthogonal Schur functions, we define two families of symmetric functions and show that they are the skew symplectic and skew orthogonal Schur polynomials defined implicitly by Koike and Terada and satisfy the general branching rules. Furthermore, we derive the Jacobi–Trudi identities and Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns for these symmetric functions. Additionally, the vertex operator method yields their Cauchy-type identities. This demonstrates that vertex operator representations serve not only as a tool for studying symmetric functions but also offers unified realizations for skew Schur functions of types A, C, and D.

skew orthogonal/symplectic Schur functions; Jacobi–Trudi identity; Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns; vertex operators

Introduction

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, Schur functions, symplectic Schur functions, and orthogonal Schur functions in finitely many variables are respectively defined by
[
19
,
20
,
24

25
]

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\det\big(x^{\lambda_{j}+n-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}}{\det\big(x^{n-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}},

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\frac{\det\big(x^{\lambda_{j}+(n-j+1)}_{i}-x^{-\lambda_{j}-(n-j+1)}_{i}\big)^{n}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{n-j+1}_{i}-x^{-(n-j+1)}_{i}\big)^{n}_{i,j=1}},

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=2(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\frac{\det\big(x^{-\lambda_{j}+j-n}_{i}+\delta_{\lambda_{n}\neq 0}\delta_{j\neq n}x^{\lambda_{j}-j+n}_{i}\big)^{n}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{-j+1}_{i}+x^{j-1}_{i}\big)^{n}_{i,j=1}},

(1.3)

where
\delta_{a\neq b}
equals 1 if
a\neq b
and is
0
otherwise.
They appear in various contexts of mathematics, especially in the representation theory of classical Lie groups (general linear groups, symplectic groups and
even orthogonal groups) as characters of finite-dimensional irreducible representations
[
14
,
18
,
25
,
26
]
. They are also
studied in generalized vertex algebras and
q
-characters
[
5
,
17
]
.

With the advent of Schur symplectic and Schur orthogonal symmetric functions, arises the inquiry into skew versions for these symmetric functions.
Since the family of symplectic Schur or orthogonal Schur functions indexed by partitions does not constitute a complete basis in the ring of symmetric functions (in fact they belong to the ring of Laurent polynomials), the traditional approach of employing the adjoint operation is not viable for defining skew orthogonal or symplectic Schur functions. Instead the skew symplectic and (even) skew orthogonal symmetric functions were implicitly defined by
Koike and Terada
[
15
]
by restricting the irreducible characters of the classical groups into their subgroups and were
expressed by tableaux representations. In
[
2
]
, the tableaux representations were transformed into the Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern
representations by Ayyer and Fischer. It is natural to ask whether the skew symplectic Schur and orthogonal Schur functions can be computed by
Jacobi–Trudi type formulas, which would then lead to a parallel formulism as the Schur functions and skew Schur functions and also provide
a lifting method to define the skew Schur symplectic and orthogonal functions in infinitely many variables.

The
aim of this paper
is to address these questions by
the vertex operator
realization of the classical symmetric functions
[
9
]
and the symplectic Schur and orthogonal Schur functions
[
3
,
12
]
. The vertex algebraic method of studying Schur polynomials
can be traced back to the Kyoto school’s work on integrable systems
[
6
]
where the tau functions of the KP hierarchy are in terms of Schur polynomials.
Skew Schur functions
{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}
satisfy the celebrated decomposition formula, i.e., the general branching rule

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}{\rm s}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-k}){\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n-k+1},\dots,x_{n}),

which can be interpreted as the restriction of the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
to
\mathrm{GL}(n-k,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{C})
.
We will define skew (symplectic/orthogonal) Schur functions using vertex operators and derive their combinatorial properties such as the Jacobi–Trudi identities, Cauchy-type identities and verify they satisfy the Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern representations found by
[
2
]
.
We also show that our vertex operator representations for skew-type symmetric functions comply with the general branching rules in the agreement with
Koike and Terada’s approach.

The construction of skew Schur functions and their generalizations can be formulated in terms of the representation theory of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras.
One starts with the infinite-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
[
8
]
with the center
1
and defines the Fock space
\mathcal{M}^{*}
and its completion
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}^{*}}
generated by the vacuum
\langle 0|
(which is the vacuum vector in the right module) and the Heisenberg generators with positive modes. There are three families of special elements (
\langle\lambda|
,
\langle\lambda^{\rm sp}|
and
\langle\lambda^{\rm o}|

in the right module) defined by partitions
\lambda
, corresponding respectively to the Schur, symplectic Schur, and orthogonal Schur cases. We will prove that they are orthonormal by computing the inner product with the elements (
|\lambda\rangle
,
|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle~and~|\lambda^{\rm o}\rangle
in the left module) in the Fock space
\mathcal{M}
respectively (see Theorem
2.3
), which is key to construct (skew) symmetric functions and also savages the problem of adjoint operation with skew versions. For the half vertex operators
\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})
and
\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\sum_{\lambda}\langle\lambda|{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x),

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\langle\lambda^{\rm sp}|{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\langle\lambda^{\rm o}|{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big),

where each summation is over (generalized) partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
with zeros parts allowed in the end. The benefit of this approach is that we can easily obtain the well-known classical Cauchy identities for (symplectic/orthogonal) Schur functions
[
14
,
25
]
. This approach is logically independent of the Jacobi–Trudi formula.

It is well known that
\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\lambda\rangle={\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)

[

,
21
]
using the half vertex operator

\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})

\displaystyle{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\langle\mu^{\rm sp}|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle,

\displaystyle{\rm o}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\langle\mu^{\rm o}|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)|\lambda^{\rm o}\rangle,

which can be interpreted as the skew symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions from the general branching rule. We also show that each skew function can be written as a Jacobi–Trudi type determinant of complete homogeneous symmetric functions with variables
x_{1},x^{-1}_{1},\dots,x_{N},x^{-1}_{N}
.

Exploiting the combinatorial properties of
\langle\mu^{\rm sp}|
and
\langle\mu^{\rm o}|
, we also obtain Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern representations (generating
functions of some Young tableaux in an equivalent form
[
24

) for skew symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions, which provide alternative definitions of
symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions (see
[
2
]
). The vertex operator method depending on the combinatorial properties of some orthogonal elements of the Fock space

may shed light on specific descriptions of generalized shape
\pi

[
7

.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

\mathcal{M}
and

In Section

3
Skew symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions

and Jacobi–Trudi identities

This section first recalls the vertex operator construction of skew Schur functions

.
Then we extend the method to realize skew symplectic Schur functions

The Jacobi–Trudi identities for the Schur function
{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)
, the symplectic Schur function
{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)
, and the orthogonal Schur function
{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)

can be treated in the same manner by the vertex operator. Recall the classical Jacobi–Trudi identity for the Schur functions
[
19

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\nu}(x)=\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

(3.1)

where the complete homogeneous function
h_{n}(x)
is defined by
\prod^{N}_{i=1}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}z}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{n}(x)z^{n}
. For the symplectic and the orthogonal cases, Schur function
{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)
and orthogonal Schur function
{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big)
admit the Jacobi–Trudi formulas
[
14
, Theorems 1.3.2 and 1.3.3]
and
[
12

\displaystyle{\rm sp}_{\nu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\det\big(h_{\nu_{i}-i+j}\big(x^{\pm}\big)+h_{\nu_{i}-i-j+2}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\rm o}_{\nu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\det\big(h_{\nu_{i}-i+j}\big(x^{\pm}\big)-h_{\nu_{i}-i-j}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

(3.3)

where
h_{n}\big(x^{\pm}\big)

[
3
,
12
,
26
]
is defined by
\prod^{N}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(1-x_{i}z)(1-x^{-1}_{i}z)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{n}\big(x^{\pm}\big)z^{n}
. It is obvious that
h_{n}(x)=h_{n}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=0
for
n<0
.

Introduce the (half) vertex operators

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(z)=\exp\Biggl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{a_{n}}{n}z^{n}\Biggr),\qquad\Gamma_{-}(z)=\exp\Biggl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{a_{-n}}{n}z^{n}\Biggr).

(3.4)

For
\{x\}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
,
\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}=\big(x_{1},x^{-1}_{1},\dots,x_{N},x^{-1}_{N}\big)
, we denote

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\Gamma_{+}(x_{i}),\qquad\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\Gamma_{+}(x_{i})\Gamma_{+}\big(x^{-1}_{i}\big),\qquad\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\Gamma_{-}(x_{i}).

3.1
Skew Schur functions

For a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
, one can rewrite
X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}}
by permuting the factors
[
10
]

and (
2.6
):

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\sigma)X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}}=X^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(l)}+\sigma(l)-l}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1},

(3.5)

where
\sigma\in S_{l}
.
The skew Schur function
{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)
generalizes the Schur function and has a determinantal expression
[
19
, formula (5.4)]
for two partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
:

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)=\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
be two partitions. Then

\displaystyle\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\lambda\rangle={\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x).

(3.6)

Proof.

Using the relations

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(z)=\Gamma_{-}\big(z^{-1}\big)X^{*}\big(z^{-1}\big),

\displaystyle X^{*}\big(z_{2}^{-1}\big)\Gamma_{-}\big(z^{-1}_{1}\big)=\bigg(1-\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\bigg)^{-1}\Gamma_{-}\big(z^{-1}_{1}\big)X^{*}\big(z_{2}^{-1}\big),

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{-}(z)=\langle 0|,

we get that

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})={}

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}\langle 0|X^{*}\big(x_{N}^{-1}\big)\cdots X^{*}\big(x_{1}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle 0|X^{*}_{-n_{N}}\cdots X^{*}_{-n_{1}}x^{n_{N}}_{N}\cdots x^{n_{1}}_{1}.

For
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
, the relation (
3.5
) says that the coefficient of
\langle\nu|=\langle 0|X^{*}_{-\nu_{N}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\nu_{1}}
in
\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})

is

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\varepsilon(\sigma)x^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}-\sigma(1)+1}_{1}\cdots x^{\nu_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+i}_{i}\cdots x^{\nu_{\sigma(N)}-\sigma(N)+N}_{N}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\varepsilon(\sigma)x^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}-\sigma(1)+N}_{1}\cdots x^{\nu_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+N}_{i}\cdots x^{\nu_{\sigma(N)}-\sigma(N)+N}_{N}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=\frac{\det\big(x^{\nu_{j}+N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})}=\frac{\det\big(x^{\nu_{j}+N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{\det\big(x^{N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}={\rm s}_{\nu}(x),

where
{\rm s}_{\nu}(x)
is the Schur function (
1.1
) associated with the partition
\nu
. We therefore get the expansion

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l(\nu)\leq N\end{subarray}}\langle\nu|{\rm s}_{\nu}(x),

(3.7)

where the sum is over all partitions
\nu
with length
\leq N
. It then follows from (
3.5
) and the Jacobi–Trudi formula (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{N}\geq 0}\langle 0|X^{*}_{-n_{N}}\cdots X^{*}_{-n_{1}}h_{n_{N}}(x)\cdots h_{n_{1}}(x).

By (
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle X^{*}(z)\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(1-x_{i}z)}\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})X^{*}(z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}(x)z^{i}\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})X^{*}(z),

i.e.,

\displaystyle X^{*}_{-n}\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}(x)X^{*}_{-n-i}.

It follows from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})={}

\displaystyle\langle 0|X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}}\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\geq 0}X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}-i_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}-i_{1}}h_{i_{l}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{l+1},\dots,i_{l+N}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle 0|X^{*}_{-i_{l+N}}\cdots X^{*}_{-i_{l+1}}h_{i_{l+N}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{l+1}}(x)

\displaystyle{}\times\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\geq 0}X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}-i_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}-i_{1}}h_{i_{l}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{l+N}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle 0|X^{*}_{-i_{l+N}}\cdots X^{*}_{-i_{l+1}}X^{*}_{-\mu_{l}-i_{l}}\cdots X^{*}_{-\mu_{1}-i_{1}}

\displaystyle{}\times h_{i_{l+N}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{l+1}}(x)h_{i_{l}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{1}}(x).

(3.8)

For partition
\lambda
with
l\leq l(\lambda)\leq l+N
, the relations (
3.5
) and (
3.8
) imply that the coefficient of
\langle\lambda|
in
\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})
is

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{l+N}}\epsilon(\sigma)\prod^{l+N}_{j=1}h_{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\mu_{j}-\sigma(j)+j}(x)=\det\big(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(x)\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq l+N}={\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\leq l(\lambda)\leq l+N\end{subarray}}\langle\lambda|{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x).

Subsequently (by the orthogonality (
2.10
))

\displaystyle\langle\mu|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\lambda\rangle={\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

We now define a family of symmetric functions by vertex operators.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+N})
be two generalized partitions, and
\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}=\big\{x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}\ldots,x_{N},x_{N}^{-1}\big\}

\displaystyle\langle\mu^{\rm sp}|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle=\det(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq l+N},

(3.10)

(3.11)

3.10

{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)

\displaystyle{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)=\det(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq l+N}=\langle\mu^{\rm sp}|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle.

(3.12)

We will justify this by showing these symmetric functions obey the branching rule, therefore they agree with the skew symplectic Schur functions defined by Koike and Terada
[
15
]
.

Proof.

By definition of the vertex operator
Y^{*}(z)
, one has that

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod^{N}_{i=1}x^{i-1-N}_{i}}{\det\big(x^{-(N-j+1)}_{i}-x^{N-j+1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}\langle 0|Y^{*}(x_{N})\cdots Y^{*}(x_{1}),

where we have used the Vandermonde type identity
[
12

and
[
26
, p. 229]

\displaystyle\det\big(x^{j-1}_{i}-x^{2N-j+1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{j}-x_{i})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j}).

In view of (
3.9
) for partition
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
, the coefficient of
\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|
in
\langle 0|Y^{*}(x_{N})\cdots Y^{*}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\varepsilon(\sigma)\prod^{N}_{i=1}\big(x^{-\nu_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}_{i}-x^{\nu_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+2N+2-i}_{i}\big)=\det\big(x^{-\nu_{j}+j-i}_{i}-x^{\nu_{j}-j+2N+2-i}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|\frac{\prod^{N}_{i=1}x^{i-1-N}_{i}\det\big(x^{-\nu_{j}+j-i}_{i}-x^{\nu_{j}-j+2N+2-i}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{-(N-j+1)}_{i}-x^{N-j+1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|\frac{\det\big(x^{-\nu_{j}-(N-j+1)}_{i}-x^{\nu_{j}+(N-j+1)}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{-(N-j+1)}_{i}-x^{N-j+1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|\frac{\det\big(x^{\nu_{j}+(N-j+1)}_{i}-x^{-\nu_{j}-(N-j+1\big)}_{i})^{N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{N-j+1}_{i}-x^{-(N-j+1)}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|{\rm sp}_{\nu}\big(x^{\pm}\big),

(3.13)

where the sum is over all generalized partitions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
and we have used the bialternant formula of the symplectic Schur function

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{n_{1}\geq-N+1,n_{2}\geq-N+2,\dots,n_{N}\geq 0}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-n_{N}}\cdots Y^{*}_{-n_{1}}h_{n_{1}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\prod^{N}_{i=2}\big(h_{n_{i}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)+h_{n_{i}-2i+2}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\big).

3.3
Skew orthogonal Schur functions

We can treat the orthogonal Schur functions similarly. Using the commutation relations

\displaystyle\langle 0|W^{*}_{n}=\langle 0|W^{*}_{-n},\qquad W^{*}_{i}W^{*}_{j}=-W^{*}_{j-1}W^{*}_{i+1},

we see that the partition elements (
2.9
) with partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\sigma)\langle 0|\begin{pmatrix}{W^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(l)}+\sigma(l)-l}}\\
{\delta_{l}W^{*}_{\mu_{\sigma(l)}-\sigma(l)+l}}\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}{W^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}}\\
{\delta_{i}W^{*}_{\mu_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+2l-i}}\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}{W^{*}_{-\mu_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}}\\
{\delta_{1}W^{*}_{\mu_{\sigma(1)}-\sigma(1)+2l-1}}\end{pmatrix}=\langle\mu^{\rm o}|,

(3.20)

where
\delta_{i}
denotes
\delta_{\mu_{l}\neq 0}\delta_{\sigma(i)\neq l}
.

Recall the Vandermonde type identity
[
12
, formula (4.4)]
and
[
26
, p. 221]
in this case

\displaystyle\det\big(x^{N-j}_{i}+x^{N+j-2}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}=2\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})(1-x_{i}x_{j}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm o}|2(-1)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\frac{\prod^{N}_{i=1}x^{i-N}_{i}\det\big(x^{-\nu_{j}+j-i}_{i}+\delta_{j\neq N}\delta_{\nu_{N}\neq 0}x^{\nu_{j}-j+2N-i}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{-j+1}_{i}+x^{j-1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm o}|2(-1)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\frac{\det\big(x^{-\nu_{j}+j-N}_{i}+\delta_{j\neq N}\delta_{\nu_{N}\neq 0}x^{\nu_{j}-j+N}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{-j+1}_{i}+x^{j-1}_{i}\big)^{N}_{i,j=1}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})}\langle\nu^{\rm o}|{\rm o}_{\nu}\big(x^{\pm}\big),

(3.21)

where the sum is over all partitions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
and

is the orthogonal Schur function associated to partition

(see (

)). It follows from (

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad{}=\sum_{n_{1}\geq-N+1,n_{2}\geq-N+2,\dots,n_{N}\geq 0}\langle 0|W^{*}_{-n_{N}}\cdots W^{*}_{-n_{1}}\prod^{N}_{i=1}\big(h_{n_{i}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)-h_{n_{i}-2i}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\big).

(3.22)

4
Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern representations

for three skew-type functions

In this section, we first derive the formulas for skew Schur functions in Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns using vertex operators. We then do the same for
skew symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions.

We start by recalling a special case of skew Schur functions in terms of vertex operators
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
. We say a (generalized) partition

\lambda
interlaces another (generalized) partition
\nu
, denoted as
\nu\prec\lambda

\lambda_{i}\geq\nu_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}
for all

Lemma 4.1

(
[
19
, p. 72]
)
.

For two interlacing partitions
\nu\prec\lambda
,
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{l})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+1})
, one has that

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\lambda/\nu}(t)=t^{|\lambda|-|\nu|}.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that skew Schur functions can be written as a sum over Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns: for partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})\subset\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+N})

\displaystyle{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)=\sum_{\mu=z_{0}\prec z_{1}\prec\dots\prec z_{N}=\lambda}\prod^{N}_{i=1}x^{|z_{i}|-|z_{i-1}|}_{i},

summed over all sequences of partitions
\mu=z_{0}\prec z_{1}\prec\dots\prec z_{N}=\lambda
, where

z_{i}=(z_{i,1},z_{i,2},\dots,\allowbreak z_{i,l+i})
satisfy
the
Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern
:

\displaystyle z_{i+1,j}\leq z_{i,j-1}\leq z_{i+1,j-1}\qquad\text{for}\quad 2\leq j\leq l+i+1.

Using the similar method as in
[
11
]
, we can get the Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern formulas for skew symplectic (orthogonal) Schur functions
{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)

(
{\rm o}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)
)
. We start by proving three lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any integer
m
and generalized partitions
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k+2})
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\alpha^{\rm sp}|\sum_{i\leq m,\,j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{i}Y^{*}_{j}t^{i+j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle=0.

Proof.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), we know that
Y^{*}_{j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle=0
for
j<-\beta_{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq m,\,j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{i}Y^{*}_{j}t^{i+j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{-\beta_{1}-1\leq i\leq m,\,-\beta_{1}\leq j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{i}Y^{*}_{j}t^{i+j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{-\beta_{1}-1\leq i\leq m,\,-\beta_{1}\leq j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{j-1}Y^{*}_{i+1}t^{i+j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{-\beta_{1}-1\leq a\leq m,\,-\beta_{1}\leq b\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{a}Y^{*}_{b}t^{a+b}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle,

therefore
\sum_{i\leq m,\,j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{i}Y^{*}_{j}t^{i+j}|\beta^{\rm sp}\rangle=0
, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For any integer
m
and generalized partition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
,

\displaystyle\langle\alpha^{\rm sp}|\sum_{i\leq m,\,j\leq m+1}Y^{*}_{i}Y^{*}_{j}t^{i+j}=0.

Lemma 4.4
.

For generalized partitions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{l})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+1})
, one has

\displaystyle{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\nu}\big(t^{\pm}\big)=\sum_{\nu\prec\alpha\prec\lambda}t^{2|\alpha|-|\lambda|-|\nu|}

summed over all generalized partitions
\alpha
such that
\nu\prec\alpha\prec\lambda

Proof.

It follows from equation (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\langle\nu^{\rm sp}|\Gamma_{+}\big(t^{\pm}\big)=\sum_{i,i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\geq 0}h_{i}\big(t^{\pm}\big)\prod^{l}_{j=1}h_{i_{j}}\big(t^{\pm}\big)\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}\cdots Y^{*}_{-\nu_{1}-i_{1}},

(4.1)

where
h_{i}\big(t^{\pm}\big)=t^{-i}+t^{-i+2}+\dots+t^{i-2}+t^{i}=\frac{t^{-i}-t^{i+2}}{1-t^{2}}
.
Since
Y^{*}_{j-1}Y^{*}_{j}=0
and Corollary
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq\nu_{l}+1,j\geq\nu_{l}}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(t^{-i}-t^{i+2}\bigr)\bigl(t^{\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{i\geq\nu_{l}+1,j\geq\nu_{l}}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(-t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{j\geq i\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(-t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+\sum_{i-1>j\geq\nu_{l}}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(-t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{j\geq i\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(-t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+\sum_{i-1>j\geq\nu_{l}}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-j-1}Y^{*}_{-i+1}\bigl(t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}+t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{j\geq i\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(-t^{i+2+\nu_{l}-j}-t^{j-\nu_{l}+2-i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+\sum_{n\geq m\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-m}Y^{*}_{-n}\bigl(t^{n+4+\nu_{l}-m}+t^{m-\nu_{l}-n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{j\geq i\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-j}\bigl(t^{i-j}-t^{j-i+2}\bigr)\bigl(t^{-\nu_{l}}-t^{2+\nu_{l}}\bigr),

where we have split

into

i-j>1
and
i-j\leq 0

\displaystyle\sum_{i,i_{l}\geq 0}h_{i}\big(t^{\pm}\big)h_{i_{l}}\big(t^{\pm}\big)\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}

\displaystyle\quad{}=\frac{1}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{2}}\sum_{i,i_{l}\geq 0}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}\big(t^{-i}-t^{i+2}\big)\big(t^{-i_{l}}-t^{i_{l}+2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq\nu_{l},\,i_{l}\geq 0}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}h_{i}\big(t^{\pm}\big)h_{i_{l}}\big(t^{\pm}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\frac{1}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{2}}\sum_{i\geq\nu_{l}+1,\,i_{l}\geq 0}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}\big(t^{-i}-t^{i+2}\big)\big(t^{-i_{l}}-t^{i_{l}+2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq\nu_{l},\,i_{l}\geq 0}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}h_{i}\big(t^{\pm}\big)h_{i_{l}}\big(t^{\pm}\big)+\sum_{\nu_{l}+i_{l}\geq i\geq\nu_{l}+1}\langle 0|Y^{*}_{-i}Y^{*}_{-\nu_{l}-i_{l}}h_{\nu_{l}}\big(t^{\pm}\big)h_{\nu_{l}+i_{l}-i}\big(t^{\pm}\big).

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\sum_{\lambda}\langle\lambda^{\rm sp}|\sum_{\nu\prec\alpha\prec\lambda}t^{2|\alpha|-|\lambda|-|\nu|},

where we use the fact that

.

For generalized partitions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{l})

Using the same method for Lemma

\displaystyle\langle\nu^{\rm o}|\Gamma_{+}\big(t^{\pm}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\langle\lambda^{\rm o}|\big(t^{-\min\{\lambda_{l+1},\nu_{l}\}}+t^{\min\{\lambda_{l+1},\nu_{l}\}}\big)

5
Cauchy-type identities and general branching rules

In this section, we give a new proof of Cauchy identities for (symplectic/orthogonal) Schur functions by the method of vertex operators. This approach is logically independent of the Jacobi–Trudi formula. The method can be further extended to
skew Schur functions as well as skew symplectic/orthogonal Schur functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle=\sum_{\lambda}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)|\lambda\rangle,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\lambda}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\lambda}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)|\lambda^{\rm o}\rangle,

where the sum is over all partitions
\lambda
with
l(\lambda)\leq N
.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.1
) by a similar method as in Proposition
3.1
, which is different from that of
[
11
]
.
Using the relations

X(z)\Gamma_{+}\big(z^{-1}\big)=\Gamma_{-}(z)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gamma_{+}\big(z^{-1}_{1}\big)\Gamma_{-}(z_{2})=\left(1-\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)^{-1}\Gamma_{-}(z_{2})\Gamma_{+}\big(z^{-1}_{1}\big),

one has

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle

\displaystyle{}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}X(x_{1})\cdots X(x_{N})|0\rangle

\displaystyle{}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}x^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots x^{n_{N}}_{N}X_{-n_{1}}\cdots X_{-n_{N}}|0\rangle.

(5.2)

Using
X_{i}X_{j}=-X_{j+1}X_{i-1}
, the factors can be shuffled into the following expression:

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\sigma)X_{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{-\lambda_{N}}=X_{-\lambda_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}\cdots X_{-\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}\cdots X_{-\lambda_{\sigma(N)}+\sigma(N)-N},

(5.3)

where
\sigma\in S_{N}
. Thus the coefficient of
|\lambda\rangle=X_{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{-\lambda_{N}}|0\rangle
in
\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle
(
5.2
) is

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigg(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\bigg)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma}\varepsilon(\sigma)x^{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}-\sigma(1)+1}_{1}\cdots x^{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+i}_{i}\cdots x^{\lambda_{\sigma(N)}-\sigma(N)+N}_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\sigma}\varepsilon(\sigma)x^{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}-\sigma(1)+N}_{1}\cdots x^{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\sigma(i)+N}_{i}\cdots x^{\lambda_{\sigma(N)}-\sigma(N)+N}_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\frac{\det\big(x^{\lambda_{j}+N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})}=\frac{\det\big(x^{\lambda_{j}+N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{\det\big(x^{N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}={\rm s}_{\lambda}(x).

Then the relation (
2.10
) gives
\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle={\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)
for any
\lambda
, subsequently

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|0\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x)|\lambda\rangle.

The other two relations can be proved similarly with the help of

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\sigma)Y_{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots Y_{-\lambda_{N}}=Y_{-\lambda_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}\cdots Y_{-\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}\cdots Y_{-\lambda_{\sigma(N)}+\sigma(N)-N},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\sigma)W_{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots W_{-\lambda_{N}}=W_{-\lambda_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}\cdots W_{-\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\sigma(i)-i}\cdots W_{-\lambda_{\sigma(N)}+\sigma(N)-N},

(5.4)

where
\sigma\in S_{N}
.
∎

By direct calculation, we have the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

From the commutation relations (
2.1
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|0\rangle=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\prod^{K}_{j=1}\big(1-x_{i}y_{j}\big)^{-1},

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}),~y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{K})

Now we study some cases of the inner product
\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|0\rangle
related to symmetric functions. Due to (

)–(
2.12

3.2
), (
3.21
) and Lemma

, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
,

, we have

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|0\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y),

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|0\rangle=\prod_{1\leq k<l\leq N}(1-y_{k}y_{l})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y),

\displaystyle\langle 0|\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|0\rangle=\prod_{1\leq k\leq l\leq N}(1-y_{k}y_{l})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y).

Combining Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
, we can prove the classical Cauchy identities associated to Schur functions, symplectic and orthogonal Schur functions respectively.

Theorem 5.4

(
[
4
,
14
,
25
]
)
.

Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
,
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})
,
x^{\pm}=\big(x^{\pm}_{1},\dots,x^{\pm}_{N}\big)
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm s}_{\lambda}(x){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y)=\prod^{N}_{i,j=1}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1},

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm sp}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y)=\prod^{N}_{i,j=1}\bigl[(1-x_{i}y_{j})\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}y_{j}\big)\bigr]^{-1}\prod_{1\leq k<l\leq N}(1-y_{k}y_{l}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}{\rm o}_{\lambda}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm s}_{\lambda}(y)=\prod^{N}_{i,j=1}\big[(1-x_{i}y_{j})\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}y_{j}\big)\big]^{-1}\prod_{1\leq k\leq l\leq N}(1-y_{k}y_{l}),

where each summation is over partitions
\lambda
with
l(\lambda)\leq N
.

The following is a generalization of Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For a generalized partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
l(\lambda)\leq l+N\end{subarray}}{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)|\lambda\rangle,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu^{\rm sp}\rangle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+N})}{\rm S}^{*}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu^{\rm o}\rangle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+N})}{\rm S}^{*}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)|\lambda^{\rm o}\rangle,

where the second and third sums are over generalized partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l+N})
with zeros parts allowed in the end, and

\displaystyle{\rm S}^{*}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)=\frac{\det(c_{ij})^{l+N}_{i,j=1}}{\det\big(x^{N-j}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}

with

c_{ij}=\begin{cases}x^{\lambda_{j}-j+N}_{i},&1\leq i\leq N,\\
h_{\mu_{i-N}-\lambda_{j}+j-i}\big(x^{\pm}\big),&N+1\leq i\leq N+l.\end{cases}

Proof.

We only prove (
5.6
), as the others can be handled similarly. By definition (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})=\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}x_{j}\big)}Y(x_{1})\cdots Y(x_{N})\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big),

where
x^{\pm}=\big(x^{\pm}_{1},\dots,x^{\pm}_{N}\big)
. It is easy to check that (by vertex operator calculus)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)Y(z)=\frac{1}{\prod^{N}_{i=1}(1-x_{i}z)\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}z\big)}Y(z)\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big),

thus

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big)Y_{n}=\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}\big(x^{\pm}\big)Y_{n+i}\Gamma_{+}\big(\big\{x^{\pm}\big\}\big).

Then

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}x_{j}\big)\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu^{\rm sp}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=Y(x_{1})\cdots Y(x_{N})\sum_{k_{N+1},\dots,k_{N+l}\geq 0}h_{k_{N+1}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\cdots h_{k_{N+l}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)Y_{-\mu_{1}+k_{N+1}}\cdots Y_{-\mu_{l}+k_{N+l}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{k_{1}}_{1}\cdots x^{k_{N}}_{N}\sum_{k_{N+1},\dots,k_{N+l}\geq 0}h_{k_{N+1}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)\cdots h_{k_{N+l}}\big(x^{\pm}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times Y_{-k_{1}}\cdots Y_{-k_{N}}Y_{-\mu_{1}+k_{N+1}}\cdots Y_{-\mu_{l}+k_{N+l}}|0\rangle.

By (
5.4
), the coefficient of
|\lambda^{\rm sp}\rangle
in
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-x_{i}x_{j})\big(1-x^{-1}_{i}x_{j}\big)\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu^{\rm sp}\rangle
is

\displaystyle\det(c_{ij})^{l+N}_{i,j=1}

with

c_{ij}=\begin{cases}x^{\lambda_{j}-j+i}_{i},&1\leq i\leq N,\\
h_{\mu_{i-N}-\lambda_{j}+j-i}\big(x^{\pm}\big),&N+1\leq i\leq N+l.\end{cases}

By means of the Vandermonde identity, we can get (
5.6
).
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The symmetric rational function
{\rm S}^{*}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)
makes sense without the restriction
\mu\subset\lambda
, while
{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)
is zero unless
\mu\subset\lambda
. Unlike the skew symplectic Schur functions
{\rm sp}_{\lambda/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big)
, the symmetric
polynomials
{\rm S}^{*}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})

do not change if we add zeroes to
\lambda
and
\mu
simultaneously.
More precisely,

{\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M+N-1},\lambda_{M+N})/(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{M-1},0)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=0,

unless
\lambda_{M+N}=0
, and

{\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M+N-1},0)/(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{M-1},0)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})={\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M+N-1})/(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{M-1})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).

Moreover,

{\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M+N})/(0^{M})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=0
unless
\lambda_{N+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{M+N}=0
,
and

{\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N},0^{M})/(0^{M})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})={\rm S}^{*}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})/\varnothing}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})={\rm s}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).

We now offer a vertex algebraic proof of the Cauchy-type identity for skew Schur functions
[
19
, p. 93]
, which can be seen as a generalized Cauchy identity for Schur functions.

Theorem 5.7
.

For two partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
,

\sum_{\rho}{\rm s}_{\rho/\lambda}(x){\rm s}_{\rho/\mu}(y)=\prod_{i,j}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\nu}{\rm s}_{\mu/\tau}(x){\rm s}_{\lambda/\tau}(y),

where the sum is over all partitions
\rho
and
\tau
such that the skew Schur functions are defined.

Proof.

From (
2.10
), we can easily deduce that

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\sum_{\rho}|\rho\rangle\langle\rho|=\langle\lambda|,\qquad\sum_{\rho}|\rho\rangle\langle\rho||\lambda\rangle=|\lambda\rangle,

where the sum is over all partitions
\rho
.
Thus

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|\mu\rangle=\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\sum_{\rho}|\rho\rangle\langle\rho|\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|\mu\rangle=\sum_{\rho}{\rm s}_{\rho/\lambda}(x){\rm s}_{\rho/\mu}(y),

(5.7)

where we have used (
3.6
). We also have the following relation:

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle{}=\prod_{i,j}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle{}=\prod_{i,j}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{-}(\{y\})\sum_{\tau}|\tau\rangle\langle\tau|\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\mu\rangle

\displaystyle{}=\prod_{i,j}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}\sum_{\nu}{\rm s}_{\mu/\tau}(x){\rm s}_{\lambda/\tau}(y).

(5.8)

Comparing (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we get the identity.
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{l+N})}{\rm o}_{\rho/\mu}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm S}^{*}_{\rho/\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\prod^{K}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(1-x_{i}y_{j})(1-x^{-1}_{i}y_{j})}\sum_{\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{l})}{\rm o}_{\lambda/\tau}\big(x^{\pm}\big){\rm S}^{*}_{\mu/\tau}(y),

Appendix A
Another proof of Proposition
3.1

In this appendix, we give a simple vertex algebraic proof of Proposition
3.1
and (
5.5
).

Proof.

From the definition of
X(z)
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})X(z)=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}z}X(z)\Gamma_{+}(\{x\}),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})X_{n}=\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}(x)X_{n+i}\Gamma_{+}(\{x\}).

Thus

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|\lambda\rangle=\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})X_{-\lambda_{1}}\dots X_{-\lambda_{k}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\geq 0}h_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots h_{i_{k}}(x)X_{-\lambda_{1}+i_{1}}\dots X_{-\lambda_{k}+i_{k}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\!\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\!\!\epsilon(\sigma)h_{\lambda_{1}-\nu_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}(x)\!\cdots h_{\lambda_{k}-\nu_{\sigma(k)}+\sigma(k)-k}(x)X_{-\nu_{\sigma(1)}+\sigma(1)-1}\!\cdots X_{-\nu_{\sigma(k)}+\sigma(k)-k}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-\nu_{j}-i+j}(x))^{k}_{i,j=1}|\nu\rangle=\sum_{\nu}{\rm s}_{\lambda/\nu}(x)|\nu\rangle,

where we have used (
5.3
) and the fact
\Gamma_{+}(\{x\})|0\rangle=|0\rangle
. By (
2.10
), we have shown Proposition
3.1
. Similarly, using (
3.5
) and (
2.10
), we can get

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu\rangle={\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x).

It is clear that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(\{x\})|\mu\rangle=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}{\rm s}_{\lambda/\mu}(x)|\lambda\rangle.

∎

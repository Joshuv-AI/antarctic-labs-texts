1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a positive integer greater than or equal to 3. A Dirichlet character
\chi
mod
k
is defined to be
odd
if
\chi(-1)=-1
and
even
if
\chi(-1)=1
. The
Dirichlet
L
-function

L(s,\chi)
is defined by the infinite series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}
, where
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re\,(s)>1
. It is an important function in number theory especially due to its connection with the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
. For rational integer
r
, the problem of computing exact values of

(1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ mod }k\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{r}\end{subarray}}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}

has been attempted in various cases by many.

In 1982, Walum
[
16
]
gave an exact formula for the sum (
1
) with
r=1
. Louboutin
[
7
]
computed the sum of
|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
over all odd primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
k
. See
[
4
, Chapter 6]
for the definition of primitivity of Dirichlet characters. In
[
8
]
, Louboutin gave an exact formula for the sum of
|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
over all odd Dirichlet characters in terms of the prime divisors of
k
and the Euler totient function
\phi
. In
[
9
]
he derived an exact formulae for the general versions of these sums in two cases :
\chi
even and
\chi
odd. However, he did not suggest an efficient way to compute the coefficients appearing in his formula.

X. Lin
[
6
]
provided a general inductive formula for computing the sum (
1
) which seems to be the only one computationally feasible formula existing for computing (
1
) as of now. In
[
11
]
, T. Okamoto and T. Onozuka derived a formula for the sum

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\text{ mod }k\\
\chi_{1}(-1)=(-1)^{m}\\
\chi_{2}(-1)=(-1)^{n}\end{subarray}}L(m,\chi_{1})L(n,\chi_{2})L(m+n,\overline{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}})

using the technique employed by S. Louboutin in
[
9
]
. For more problems related to the sum of mean square values, please see
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
12
]
and the more recent paper
[
19
]
. Some other closely related problems were attempted by a few authors. See, for example,
[
20
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
.

E. Alkan derived an exact formula for the sums
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{odd}}|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
and
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{even}}|L(2,\chi)|^{2}
in terms of Jordan totient function and Euler totient function using weighted averages of Gauss and Ramanujan sums in
[
1
]
. See the next section for the definition of these sums and functions. His starting point was the formula

\displaystyle L(r,\chi)=\frac{i^{r}2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}{(-1)^{v+1}kr!}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r}{q}B_{q}S(r-q,\chi)\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Theorem 1]}},

where
v
and
r\geq 1
have same parity,
B_{q}
are the Bernoulli numbers and
S(r-q,\chi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{r-q}G(z,\chi)
. Here
G(z,\chi)
denotes the Gauss sum. The most difficult and complicated part of his evaluation was computing sums of the form

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\right)

and

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{-\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\right)

for which he did not suggest any general technique, but simplified it only for his special case.

Note that Alkan’s techniques in
[
1
]
were completely different from the other derivations which were employed to compute the square sums. It was also observed by Alkan that his techniques could be used to determine the sum (
1
) for any larger
r
, but at the cost of increasingly complex computations. We followed his techniques and gave exact formulae for
\sum\limits_{\chi(-1)=(-1)^{r}}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}
when
r=3
and
r=4
in
[
14
]
.

Using the similar techniques as that of Alkan in
[
1
]
and these authors in
[
14
]
, we here give a computationally feasible general formula for evaluating (
1
) in two cases:
r
odd (Theorem
3.2
), and
r
even (Theorem

3.4
). In both these cases, the formulae are almost the same, and involve the computation of certain binomial coefficients and sums of the form

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{2n}},\,n=1,2,\ldots,r.

For finding these sine sums, we give an inductive formula (Lemma
3.3
) which only involves the evaluation of certain binomial coefficients.

2.
Notations and basic results

In our computations to determine
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{r}\end{subarray}}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}
, we use certain properties of Chebyshev polynomials of first and second kinds. A detailed account of these polynomials and their properties can be found in
[
5
, Section 13.3]
. The
Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind

T_{n}
has the representation

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{k}\frac{(n-k-1)!}{k!(n-2k)!}(2x)^{n-2k}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{arfken2005mathematical}{}{}, Equation 13.119a]}}

and the
second kind

U_{n}
has the representation

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{k}\frac{(n-k)!}{k!(n-2k)!}(2x)^{n-2k}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{arfken2005mathematical}{}{}, Equation 13.119b]}},

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denote the greatest integer not exceeding
x
. Now these polynomials can be used to relate the sine and cosine of
\theta
and
n\theta
via the identities

T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
and
U_{n}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta=\sin((n+1)\theta)

[
5
, Equations 13.114a and 13.115a]
.

The
Gauss sum

G(z,\chi)
for any complex number
z
is defined by

\displaystyle G(z,\chi):=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\chi(m)e^{\frac{2\pi imz}{k}}.

The
Jordan totient function

J_{k}(n)
is defined by

\displaystyle J_{k}(n):=n^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right).

For
n\geq 0
, the
n
th Bernoulli number is denoted by
B_{n}
. It is known that
B_{0}=1
and
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
. For any other odd positive integer
n
, we have
B_{n}=0
. For the other properties of Bernoulli numbers, please see
[
4
, Chapter 12]
.

From
[
2
]
we have the following identities.
For positive integer
n
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{n}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}k^{j}\lim\limits_{w\rightarrow\frac{2\pi im}{k}}\frac{d^{n-j}}{dw^{n-j}}\left(\frac{1}{e^{w}-1}\right).

For any
q\geq 1
, we have

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{q}}{dw^{q}}\left(\frac{1}{e^{w}-1}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{q+1}\frac{A_{q,j}}{(e^{w}-1)^{j}},

where

(4)

\displaystyle A_{q,j}:=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{r+q}\binom{j-1}{r}(j-r)^{q}

for
1\leq j\leq q+1
.
Combining the two Equations (
2
) and (
3
) we get

(5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{n}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}k^{j}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n-j+1}\frac{A_{n-j,\alpha}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{\alpha}}.

Since the sum
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{n}}
occurs at many places in this paper, for notational brevity, we will use the notation
SIN(n)
to replace it.

3.
Main Results and proofs

We start with the evaluation of the real part of

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\right).

We use Equation (
5
) to evaluate the above and get it to be equal to

(6)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{p}{j}k^{j}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p-j+1}\frac{A_{p-j,\alpha}}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{p}{j}k^{j}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p-j+1}\frac{A_{p-j,\alpha}}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{p}{j}k^{j}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p-j+1}\frac{A_{p-j,\alpha}}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q}\binom{q}{s}k^{s}\sum\limits_{\beta=1}^{q-s+1}\frac{A_{q-s,\beta}}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{p}{j}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q}\binom{q}{s}k^{j+s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p-j+1}\sum\limits_{\beta=1}^{q-s+1}\frac{A_{p-j,\alpha}A_{q-s,\beta}}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}.

Now by the Euler’s formula
e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(e^{-\frac{\pi im}{k}}\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}{\left(e^{\frac{\pi im}{k}}-e^{-\frac{\pi im}{k}}\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)-i\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{(2i)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}.

Since we are interested only in the real part of the left-hand side of Equation (
6
), only the real part of
\frac{1}{\left(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}
in Equation (
7
) is relevant to us. To compute it, we consider two cases determined by the parity of
\alpha+\beta
as follows.

Case 1:
\alpha+\beta
even

When
\alpha+\beta
is even,
i^{\alpha+\beta}
is real and only the term
\frac{\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{(2i)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}
appears in the real part of Equation (
7
). Now

(8)

\displaystyle\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\alpha+\beta}\left(cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{c}\frac{(\alpha+\beta-c-1)!}{c!(\alpha+\beta-2c)!}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2c}.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{(2i)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{c}\frac{(\alpha+\beta-c-1)!}{c!(\alpha+\beta-2c)!}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2c}}{2^{\alpha+\beta}i^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\beta)\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{c+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}(\alpha+\beta-c-1)!}{2^{2c+1}c!(\alpha+\beta-2c)!\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2c}.

Now

\displaystyle\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2c}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}(-1)^{d}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}{d}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{2d}.

Therefore

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{(2i)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha+\beta)\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{c+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}(\alpha+\beta-c-1)!}{2^{2c+1}c!(\alpha+\beta-2c)!}\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{d}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}{d}}{\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2d}}.

Case 2:
\alpha+\beta
odd

When
\alpha+\beta
is odd,
i^{\alpha+\beta}
is imaginary and the term
-\frac{\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{2^{\alpha+\beta}i^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}
appears in the real part of Equation (
7
). Now

\displaystyle\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)

\displaystyle=U_{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum\limits_{e=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{e}\frac{(\alpha+\beta-e-1)!}{e!(\alpha+\beta-2e-1)!}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}\right]\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right).

Also

\displaystyle\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{f=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c-1}{2}}(-1)^{f}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}{f}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{2f}.

Hence

(10)

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{2^{\alpha+\beta}i^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}=(-1)^{\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}\frac{\sin\left(\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)(\alpha+\beta)\right)}{2^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{e=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{e+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}(\alpha+\beta-e-1)!}{2^{2e+1}e!(\alpha+\beta-2e-1)!}\sum\limits_{f=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{f}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}{f}}{\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi m}{k}\right)\right)^{\alpha+\beta-2f-1}}.

With these, from Equation (
6
), we have

Lemma 3.1
.

\displaystyle\Re\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q}\binom{p}{j}\binom{q}{s}k^{j+s}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p-j+1}A_{p-j,\alpha}\Bigg[\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta=1\\

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-1)^{c+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}(\alpha+\beta-c-1)!}{2^{2c+1}c!(\alpha+\beta-2c)!}\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}(-1)^{d}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2c}{2}}{d}SIN(\alpha+\beta-2d)\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta=1\\
\alpha+\beta\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{q-s+1}A_{q-s,\beta}\sum\limits_{e=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{e+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}(\alpha+\beta-e-1)!}{2^{2e+1}e!(\alpha+\beta-2e-1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{f=0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}(-1)^{f}\binom{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2e-1}{2}}{f}SIN(\alpha+\beta-2f-1)\Bigg)\Bigg].

Note that from the computations we performed to arrive at the result above, a similar looking identity can be computed for

\displaystyle\Re\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\

3.1.
Computing the sum when
r
is odd

Now we compute
\sum\limits_{\chi(\text{mod }k)}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}
over odd characters
\chi
when
r
is odd. We will use the identity in Lemma
3.1
in these computations.
By
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle L(r,\chi)=\frac{i^{r}2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}{(-1)^{v+1}kr!}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r}{q}B_{q}S(r-q,\chi),

where
v
and
r\geq 1
have same parity, and so in this case,
(-1)^{v+1}=1
.

Here

\displaystyle S(r-q,\chi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{r-q}G(j,\chi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{r-q}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\chi(m)e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}.

Since
r
is odd, write
r=2h+1
, where
h=0,1,\cdots
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle L(2h+1,\chi)=C_{1}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2h}\binom{2h+1}{q}B_{q}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{2h+1-q}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\chi(m)e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}},

where
C_{1}=\frac{i^{2h+1}2^{2h}\pi^{2h+1}}{k(2h+1)!}
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle|L(2h+1,\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}L(2h+1,\chi)\overline{L(2h+1,\chi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}\overline{C_{1}}\sum\limits_{q_{1}=0}^{2h}\sum\limits_{q_{2}=0}^{2h}B_{q_{1}}B_{q_{2}}\binom{2h+1}{q_{1}}\binom{2h+1}{q_{2}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{2h+1-q_{1}}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{s}{k}\right)^{2h+1-q_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(mj-ns)}{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}.

There are exactly
\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
odd Dirichlet characters modulo
k\geq 3
. Therefore
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(u)=\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
or
-\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
depending on if
u=1
or
u=-1
respectively. If
u
is neither of these modulo
k
, then
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(u)=0
. Thus

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(mn^{-1})=\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\text{ or }-\frac{\phi(k)}{2}

when
m=n
or
m=k-n
respectively and

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}=0

otherwise. So we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle|L(2h+1,\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|C_{1}|^{2}\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\sum\limits_{q_{1}=0}^{2h}\sum\limits_{q_{2}=0}^{2h}B_{q_{1}}B_{q_{2}}\frac{1}{k^{4h+2-q_{1}-q_{2}}}\binom{2h+1}{q_{1}}\binom{2h+1}{q_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{2h+1-q_{1}}s^{2h+1-q_{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j-s)}{k}}

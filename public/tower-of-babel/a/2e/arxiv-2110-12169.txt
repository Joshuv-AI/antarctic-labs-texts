Geometric inequalities for free boundary hypersurfaces in a ball
Yimin Chen, Yingxiang Hu, Haizhong Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a family of sharp geometric inequalities for free boundary hypersurfaces in a ball in space forms.

1.
Introduction

The sharp geometric inequalities for closed hypersurfaces in Riemannian manifolds have attracted a lot of attention during the past few decades. By means of curvature flows, the sharp geometric inequalities such as quermassintegral inequalities, Minkowski type inequalities, Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities and more general weighted inequalities have been established for closed hypersurfaces in space forms or warped product spaces, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

, etc. Another powerful approach to prove the sharp geometric inequalities is the (weighted) Reilly formula, which can be used to prove Heintze-Karcher type inequality and Minkowski type inequalities for closed hypersurfaces in Riemannian manifolds, see e.g.
[

Influenced by the pioneering works due to De Lellis-Müller
[
6
]
and De Lellis-Topping
[
7
]
, Perez
[
22
, Thm. 3.1]
proved the following geometric inequality for closed hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The equality case was characterized by Cheng and Zhou
[

.

Theorem A

(
[
22
,
5
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma
be a closed immersed oriented hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|H-\overline{H}|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{h}|^{2},

where
\mathring{h}=h-\frac{H}{n}g
and
\overline{H}=\int_{\Sigma}H/|\Sigma|
. Equality holds in (
1.1
) if and only if it is a sphere.

Inequality (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left|h-\frac{\overline{H}}{n}g\right|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{h}|^{2}.

It was pointed out by De Lellis and Topping
[
7
]
that Perez’s inequality (
1.1
) holds even for the closed hypersurfaces with nonnegative Ricci curvature in Einstein manifolds, see also
[
5
, Thm. 1.2]
. Recently, Perez’s inequality (
1.2
) was generalized by Agostiniani, Fogagnolo and Mazzieri
[
1
, Thm. 6.1]
to the bounded outward minimising open domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by nonlinear potential theory.

Kwong
[
16
, Thms. 2.2 & 4.1]
generalized Perez’s inequality (
1.1
) to the higher order mean curvatures for closed immersed submanifolds in space forms. Let
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
be the simply connected space form with constant sectional curvature
K\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Then
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(0)=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(1)=\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(-1)=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
H_{k}
be the
k
th mean curvature and
T_{k}
the
k
th Newton tensor respectively, see (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) in §
2
. We denote by
\mathring{T}_{k}=T_{k}-\frac{1}{n}(\operatorname{tr}T_{k})g
the tracefree part of
T_{k}
, in particular,
\mathring{T}_{1}=-\mathring{h}
. For closed immersed hypersurfaces with nonnegative Ricci curvature in space forms, Kwong’s results can be stated as follows.

Theorem B

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma
be a closed immersed oriented hypersurface in
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then for
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k})^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2},

where
\overline{H}_{k}=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}/|\Sigma|
. Equality is attained at geodesic spheres. Conversely, if
\Sigma
has positive Ricci curvature, then equality in (
1.3
) implies that
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

Due to Fraser-Schoen’s important work on the first Steklov eigenvalue and minimal free boundary surfaces
[

We use
x:M^{n}\rightarrow B\subset\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
to denote an isometric immersion of an oriented
n
-dimensional compact manifold with boundary
\partial M
into a (closed) geodesic ball
B
in
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
such that

x(\operatorname{int}M)\subset\operatorname{int}B\quad\text{and}\quad x(\partial M)\subset\partial B.

The hypersurface
\Sigma=x(M)
is called
free boundary
if it meets the geodesic sphere
\partial B
orthogonally. It is convenient to use the following models to represent the space forms
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
.

(i)

If
K=0
, then
(\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(0),\overline{g})=(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\delta)
and
B=B_{R}
is a Euclidean ball of radius
R
, where
\delta
is the Euclidean metric.

(ii)

If
K=-1
, then we use the Poincaré ball model
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},e^{2u}\delta)
to represent the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, where
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
is the open unit ball centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
e^{2u}=\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}
. Abuse of notation, here we also use
x
to denote the position vector with respect to the center of
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
and
|x|=\delta(x,x)^{1/2}
is its Euclidean norm. Let
B=B_{R}^{\mathbb{H}}
be a geodesic ball in
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},e^{2u}\delta)
with radius
R\in(0,\infty)
centered at the center of
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
.

(iii)

If
K=1
, then we use the model
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},e^{2u}\delta)
to represent the unit sphere without the south pole
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}\backslash\{\mathcal{S}\}
and
e^{2u}=\frac{4}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}
. Let
B=B_{R}^{\mathbb{S}}
be a geodesic ball in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with radius
R\in(0,\pi)
centered at the north pole.

For simplicity, we call a point
p\in\Sigma
is an
elliptic point
, if its second fundamental form satisfies
h_{ij}>cg_{ij}
at this point, where
c=0
when
K=0,1
and
c=1
when
K=-1
.

Our first result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma
be a compact immersed oriented hypersurface with free boundary in a ball in space forms. Assume that
\Sigma
has nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then for
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}.

Equality is attained at spherical caps or a flat disk. Conversely, if there exists an elliptic point in the interior of
\Sigma
, then equality in (
1.4
) implies that
\Sigma
is a spherical cap.

If we further assume that
x:(M^{n},g)\rightarrow B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is an isometric embedding, then the free boundary hypersurface
\Sigma
is called
convex
if its second fundamental form with respect to the unit outward normal
\nu
is positive semi-definite, i.e.,
h_{ij}\geq 0
. We obtain the following result for convex free boundary hypersurfaces in a Euclidean ball.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma
be a convex free boundary hypersurface in a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then for
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}.

Equality holds in (
1.5
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat disk.

In
[
28
]
, Scheuer, Wang and Xia found a natural counterpart of quermassintegrals
W_{k}
for convex free boundary hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in the unit Euclidean ball. Let
\partial\Sigma
be the boundary of
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We use
|\partial\Sigma|
to denote the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\partial\Sigma
, and
|\Sigma|
to denote the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\Sigma
. As an application of Corollary
1.2
, we obtain the following geometric inequalities.

Corollary 1.3
.

(i)

Let
\Sigma^{2}
be a convex free boundary surface in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{3}
. Then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\frac{(\int_{\Sigma}H)^{2}}{|\Sigma|}+|\partial\Sigma|\geq 2\pi.

Equality holds in (
1.6
) if and only if it is a flat disk or a spherical cap.

(ii)

Let
\Sigma^{3}
be a convex free boundary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{4}
. Then

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{1}{3}\frac{(\int_{\Sigma}H)^{2}}{|\Sigma|}+|\partial\Sigma|\right)\geq f_{3}\circ f_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{4}|\Sigma|\right),

where
f_{k}(r)=W_{k}(\hat{C}_{r})
is a strictly increasing function of
r
(see §
2
for details), and
f_{l}^{-1}
is the inverse of
f_{l}
. Equality holds in (
1.7
) if and only if it is a flat disk or a spherical cap.

Remark 1.4
.

(1)

By Hölder inequality, inequality (
1.6
) implies the Willmore type inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}H^{2}+|\partial\Sigma|\geq 2\pi,

which was proved by Volkmann
[
30
, Cor. 5.8]
.

(2)

By Hölder inequality, inequality (
1.7
) implies the Willmore type inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{1}{3}\int_{\Sigma}H^{2}+|\partial\Sigma|\right)\geq f_{3}\circ f_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{4}|\Sigma|\right),

which was proved by Scheuer, Wang and Xia
[
28
, Cor. 1.4]
.

To further generalize the inequality (
1.4
), we consider a Riemannian triple
(\Sigma,g,V)
which constitutes an
n
-dimensional smooth connected manifold
\Sigma
, a Riemannian metric
g
and a smooth nonzero function
V
on
\Sigma
.
V
is called a
potential function
. A Riemannian triple
(\Sigma,g,V)
is called
sub-static
if

(1.8)

\displaystyle\Delta Vg-\nabla^{2}V+V\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}\geq 0,\quad\text{on $\Sigma$},

where
\Delta
,
\nabla^{2}
and
\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}
are the Laplacian, Hessian and Ricci curvature of
(\Sigma,g)
, respectively. We also call it
strictly sub-static
if the strict inequality holds in (
1.8
) on
\Sigma
. The potential functions associated to free boundary hypersurfaces in a ball in space forms are the functions
V_{a}
which are defined as follows.

V_{a}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle x,a\rangle,&\qquad\text{if $K=0$};\\
\frac{2\langle x,a\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}},&\qquad\text{if $K=-1$};\\
\frac{2\langle x,a\rangle}{1+|x|^{2}},&\qquad\text{if $K=1$},\end{array}\right.

where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a constant vector and
\langle x,a\rangle=\delta(x,a)
. For simplicity, we use
B_{a+}=\{V_{a}>0\}\cap B
to denote the half ball of
B
in the direction of
a
. We obtain the following weighted geometric inequalities.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
x:M\rightarrow\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
be a compact immersed oriented hypersurface
\Sigma
with free boundary in a ball
B
. Assume
\Sigma
lies in
B_{a+}
and
(\Sigma,g,V_{a})
is sub-static. Then for
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V_{a}}|^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2},

where
\overline{H}_{k}^{V_{a}}=\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}H_{k}/\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}
. Equality is attained at spherical caps. Conversely, if
(\Sigma,g,V_{a})
is strictly sub-static and there exists an elliptic point in the interior of
\Sigma
, then equality in (
1.9
) implies that
\Sigma
is a spherical cap.

As a direct application, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
x:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth embedded oriented hypersurface
\Sigma
with free boundary in a ball
B
. If
\Sigma
is strictly convex, then there exists a unit vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
V_{a}>0
and for
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V_{a}}|^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}V_{a}|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}.

Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap.

The principal curvatures

The main tool in the proof is the weighted Reilly formula. Reilly
[
25
]
first established an important integral formula on hypersurfaces in Riemannian manifolds, which is very useful for manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Qiu and Xia
[

established a weighted Reilly formula which can be used for manifolds with sectional curvature bounded from below. Recently, Li and Xia
[
21
]
further generalized the Reilly formula and using this new integral formula to prove geometric inequalities in warped product spaces. Abuse of notations, we use
\overline{\nabla}
,
\overline{\Delta}
and
\overline{\nabla}^{2}
to denote the gradient, the Laplacian and the Hessian on
(\Omega,\overline{g})
respectively, while we use
\nabla
,
\Delta
and
\nabla^{2}
to denote the gradient, the Laplacian and the Hessian on
(\partial\Omega,g)
respectively. In this paper, we will use the following special form of the weighted Reilly formula.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
21
, Cor. 5.1]

Let
(\Omega,\overline{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth connected boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
V\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
be a smooth positive function on
\overline{\Omega}
such that
\frac{\overline{\nabla}^{2}V}{V}
is continuous up to
\partial\Omega
. Then for any
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, the following integral identity holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V\left(\overline{\Delta}f-\frac{\overline{\Delta}V}{V}f\right)^{2}-V\left|\overline{\nabla}^{2}f-\frac{\overline{\nabla}^{2}V}{V}f\right|^{2}d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\Delta}V\overline{g}-\overline{\nabla}^{2}V+V\operatorname{Ric}\right)\left(\overline{\nabla}f-\frac{\overline{\nabla}V}{V}f,\overline{\nabla}f-\frac{\overline{\nabla}V}{V}f\right)d\Omega

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}V\left(h-\frac{V_{\nu}}{V}g\right)\left(\nabla f-\frac{\nabla V}{V}f,\nabla f-\frac{\nabla V}{V}f\right)dA

(2.11)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}VH\left(f_{\nu}-\frac{V_{\nu}}{V}f\right)^{2}+2V\left(f_{\nu}-\frac{V_{\nu}}{V}f\right)\left(\Delta f-\frac{\Delta V}{V}f\right)dA.

Here
\nu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Omega
and
V_{\nu}=\overline{\nabla}_{\nu}V
,
f_{\nu}=\overline{\nabla}_{\nu}f
,
h(\cdot,\cdot)
and
H
are the second fundamental form and the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
, and
\operatorname{Ric}
is Ricci curvature of
\Omega
respectively.

3.
Proof of Main Results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first give the proof of the inequality (
1.4
) in Theorem
1.1
and the inequality (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.5
in a unified way.
We choose
V=1
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
V=V_{a}
in the proof of Theorem
1.5
respectively. In Theorem
1.5
, the hypersurface
\Sigma
is assumed to be contained in an open half-ball
B_{a+}
, which is equivalent to
V_{a}>0
.

Consider the Neumann boundary value problem

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta f-\frac{\Delta V}{V}f=&H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V},\quad&\text{in $\Sigma$},\\
f_{\mu}-\frac{V_{\mu}}{V}f=&0,\quad&\text{on $\partial\Sigma$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
. The existence and uniqueness (up to an additive
\alpha V
) of the solution to (
3.1
) follows from the Fredholm alternative. For simplicity, we denote by

A_{ij}=f_{ij}-\frac{V_{ij}}{V}f,\quad A=\Delta f-\frac{\Delta V}{V}f.

Let
\mathring{A}_{ij}=A_{ij}-\frac{A}{n}g_{ij}
be the tracefree part of
A_{ij}
.

Using Eq. (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V})^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V})(V\Delta f-f\Delta V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V})(Vf_{\mu}-fV_{\mu})-\int_{\Sigma}g(\nabla H_{k},V\nabla f-f\nabla V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-k}\int_{\Sigma}g(\operatorname{div}(\mathring{T}_{k}),V\nabla f-f\nabla V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-k}\left(\int_{\partial\Sigma}\mathring{T}_{k}(V\nabla f-f\nabla V,\mu)-\int_{\Sigma}V\sum_{i,j}(\mathring{T}_{k})_{ij}\mathring{A}_{ij}\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-k}\left(\int_{\Sigma}V|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}V\sum_{i,j}|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

In the second equality we used integration by parts, and in the third equality we used
f_{\mu}-\frac{V_{\mu}}{V}f=0
to eliminate the boundary integral on
\partial\Sigma
and (
2.4
). In the fourth equality we used integration by parts again. In the last line, the boundary integral on
\partial\Sigma
vanishes due to (
2.6
) and the inequality follows from the Hölder inequality.

We use
\tilde{\nabla}
to denote the gradient on
\partial\Sigma
. We take
\Omega=\Sigma
and
V=1
or
V=V_{a}
in the weighted Reilly formula (
2.5
), in view of the boundary condition
f_{\mu}-\frac{V_{\mu}}{V}f=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V\left(A^{2}-\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}V\left(h^{\partial\Sigma}-\frac{V_{\mu}}{V}g^{\partial\Sigma}\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}f-\frac{\tilde{\nabla}V}{V}f,\tilde{\nabla}f-\frac{\tilde{\nabla}V}{V}f\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left(\Delta Vg-\nabla^{2}V+V\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}\right)\left(\nabla f-\frac{\nabla V}{V}f,\nabla f-\frac{\nabla V}{V}f\right).

By the free boundary condition, we have
\mu=\overline{N}
. Then the induced metric
g^{\partial\Sigma}
from
(\Sigma,g)
coincides with the metric
g^{\partial B}|_{T(\partial\Sigma)\otimes T(\partial\Sigma)}
, where
g^{\partial B}
is the metric of
\partial B
. Furthermore, the second fundamental form
h^{\partial\Sigma}
coincides with
h^{\partial B}|_{T(\partial\Sigma)\otimes T(\partial\Sigma)}
, where
h^{\partial B}
is the second fundamental form of
\partial B
given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle h^{\partial B}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(1/R)g^{\partial B},\quad&\text{if $K=0$},\\
&(\coth R)g^{\partial B},\quad&\text{if $K=-1$},\\
&(\cot R)g^{\partial B},\quad&\text{if $K=1$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We have the following identity

(3.5)

\displaystyle h^{\partial B}-\frac{(V_{a})_{\overline{N}}}{V_{a}}g^{\partial B}=0,\quad\text{on $\partial B$},

see
[
34
, (5.12)]
. For convenience of readers, we give a proof of (
3.5
) in Remark
3.1
.

In case of Theorem
1.1
,
\Delta Vg-\nabla^{2}V+V\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}=\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}\geq 0
and
h^{\partial\Sigma}-\frac{V_{\mu}}{V}g^{\partial\Sigma}=h^{\partial\Sigma}\geq 0
.
In case of Theorem
1.5
,
\Delta V_{a}g-\nabla^{2}V_{a}+V_{a}\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}\geq 0
and
h^{\partial\Sigma}-\frac{(V_{a})_{\mu}}{V_{a}}g^{\partial\Sigma}=0
due to (
3.5
). It follows from (
3
) that
\int_{\Sigma}VA^{2}-V\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{2}\geq 0
, which yields

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V\sum_{i,j}|\mathring{A}_{ij}|^{2}\leq\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}VA^{2}.

Substituting (
3.6
) into (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V})^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-k}\left(\int_{\Sigma}V|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}VA^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-k}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}V|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}V|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, if
\int_{\Sigma}V|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V}|^{2}=0
, then the inequality (
1.4
) or (
1.9
) holds trivially. Otherwise, by eliminating
\int_{\Sigma}V|H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V}|^{2}
on both sides of (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}V(H_{k}-\overline{H}_{k}^{V})^{2}\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-k)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}V|\mathring{T}_{k}|^{2}.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we define the subset
\Sigma_{+}
of
\Sigma
which consists of elliptic points in
\Sigma
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{+}=\{x\in\operatorname{int}\Sigma~:~h_{ij}>cg_{ij}\},

where
c=0
when
K=0,1
and
c=1
when
K=-1
. By assumption,
\Sigma_{+}
is nonempty and it is obviously open. Now we show that
\Sigma_{+}
is also closed, by showing that

\displaystyle h_{ij}|_{\Sigma_{+}}\geq(c+\varepsilon)g_{ij}|_{\Sigma_{+}}

for some constant
\varepsilon>0
.

In view of (
3
), the equality in (
3.6
) implies that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}\left(\nabla f,\nabla f\right)=0.

On the other hand, the equality in (
3
) implies that there exists a constant
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\mathring{T}_{k})_{ij}=\beta\mathring{A}_{ij}=\beta(f_{ij}-\frac{\Delta f}{n}g_{ij}).

Let
p\in\Sigma_{+}
, then by Gauss equation

\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}_{ij}=(Hh_{ij}-h_{ik}h^{k}_{j})+(n-1)Kg_{ij}>0,

we have
\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}>0
in an open neighborhood
U\subset\Sigma_{+}
of
p
. Then by (
3.8
), we have
f\equiv\alpha
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and hence
H_{k}\equiv\overline{H}_{k}>H_{k}(cI)
in
U
by Eq. (
3.1
). So
\mathring{T}_{k}\equiv 0
in
U
by (
3.9
), which implies

h_{ij}=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}H_{k}g_{ij}=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\overline{H}_{k}g_{ij},\quad\text{in $U$}.

due to Lemma
2.3
. Thus, we have

h_{ij}\geq(c+\varepsilon)g_{ij},\quad\text{in $\overline{U}$}.

Therefore,
\Sigma_{+}
is closed and
\Sigma_{+}=\operatorname{int}\Sigma
by connectedness. It follows that
\Sigma
is umbilical and hence it is a spherical cap. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed. ∎

Remark 3.1
.

Since
\partial B
is a geodesic sphere of radius
R
in
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K)
, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\overline{N}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &x/R,\quad&\text{if $K=0$},\\
&x/\sinh R,\quad&\text{if $K=-1$},\\
&x/\sin R,\quad&\text{if $K=1$}.\end{aligned}\right.

On the other hand,
\partial B
can be viewed as the Euclidean sphere
|x|=R_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
R_{\mathbb{R}}
is given by

(3.11)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{R}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &R,\quad&K=0;\\
&\sqrt{\frac{\cosh R-1}{\cosh R+1}},\quad&K=-1;\\
&\sqrt{\frac{1-\cos R}{1+\cos R}},\quad&K=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be an orthonormal basis in
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\delta)
and
\overline{E}_{i}=e^{-u}E_{i}
. Then
\{\overline{E}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
is an orthonormal basis in
(\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K),\overline{g}=e^{2u}\delta)
. In view of the models of the space forms, any function
f
defined on
(\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(K),\overline{g})
can be also considered as a function defined on
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
respectively. Then there holds

\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}f=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}E_{i}(f)E_{i}=e^{2u}\overline{E}_{i}(f)\overline{E}_{i}=e^{2u}\overline{\nabla}f.

Then it follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{(V_{a})_{\overline{N}}}{V_{a}}=\overline{g}(\overline{\nabla}\log V_{a},\overline{N})=\langle\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}\log V_{a},\overline{N}\rangle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\langle N,a\rangle}{\langle x,a\rangle}=\frac{1}{R},\quad&\text{if $K=0$},\\
&\frac{\langle N,a\rangle}{\langle x,a\rangle}+\frac{2\langle x,\overline{N}\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}}=\coth R,\quad&\text{if $K=-1$},\\
&\frac{\langle N,a\rangle}{\langle x,a\rangle}-\frac{2\langle x,\overline{N}\rangle}{1+|x|^{2}}=\cot R,\quad&\text{if $K=1$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then the identity (
3.5
) follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.12
).

,

Remark 3.2
.

The sub-static condition of
(\Sigma,g,V_{a})
can be expressed in terms of certain convexity of the hypersurface. Since
\overline{\nabla}^{2}V_{a}=-KV_{a}\overline{g}
, it follows from the Gauss equations and Gauss formula that

\displaystyle\Delta V_{a}=

\displaystyle\overline{\Delta}V_{a}-\overline{\nabla}^{2}V_{a}(\nu,\nu)-H(V_{a})_{\nu}=-nKV_{a}-H(V_{a})_{\nu},

\displaystyle(V_{a})_{ij}=

\displaystyle(V_{a})_{,ij}-(V_{a})_{\nu}h_{ij}=-KV_{a}g_{ij}-(V_{a})_{\nu}h_{ij},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}_{ij}=

\displaystyle Hh_{ij}-h_{ik}h^{k}_{j}+(n-1)Kg_{ij},

where we use
(V_{a})_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}V_{a}
and
(V_{a})_{,ij}=\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}V_{a}
. Then we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Delta V_{a}g_{ij}-(V_{a})_{ij}+V_{a}\operatorname{Ric}^{\Sigma}_{ij}=(V_{a}h_{ik}-(V_{a})_{\nu}g_{ik})(Hg_{kj}-h_{kj}).

So if
\Sigma
is convex and
h_{ij}\geq\frac{(V_{a})_{\nu}}{V_{a}}g_{ij}
, then
(\Sigma,g,V_{a})
is sub-static.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Since
\Sigma
is a strictly convex free boundary hypersurface in a Euclidean ball, there exists a constant unit vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
V_{a}=\langle x,a\rangle>0
and
(V_{a})_{\nu}=\langle a,\nu\rangle<0
, see
[
17
, Lems. 11 & 12]
. Then
(\Sigma,g,V_{a})
is strictly sub-static in view of (
3.16
). Applying Theorem
1.5
, we complete the proof of Corollary
1.6
.
∎

Abstract
. We will show that an unconditional basis in a Banach space is equivalent to the unit vector basis of c 0 c_{0} or ℓ p \ell_{p} for 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty if and only if all finitely supported blocks of the basis generated by a unit vector and its dual basis are uniformly equivalent to the basis or all such blocks are uniformly complemented.



1.
Introduction

There are several classical results concerning when a basis for a Banach space is equivalent to the unit vector basis
of
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a basic sequence in a Banach space
X
. A sequence of non-zero vectors
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in X
of the form

u_{j}=\sum_{n=p_{j}+1}^{p_{j+1}}b_{n}x_{n}

with
\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
scalars and
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots
an increasing sequence of
integers, is called a
block basic sequence
or briefly a
block basis
of
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Definition 1.2
.

If
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are bases for Banach spaces
X,Y
respectively,
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is

K-equivalent
to
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
if there is a constant
1\leq K<\infty
so that for all scalars
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
we have

\frac{1}{K}\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}y_{n}\|\leq|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\|\leq K\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}y_{n}\|.

Equivalently, if
T:X\rightarrow Y
is given by
Tx_{n}=y_{n}
then
\|T\|,\|T^{-1}\|\leq K
. We say
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
equivalent

to
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
if they are K-equivalent for some K.

In
[
2
]
we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

let
X
be a Banach space with a normalized basis
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Then
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent
to the unit vector basis of
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
if and only if
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to
all of its normalized block bases.

Definition 1.4
.

A basis
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of a Banach space X is called
subsymmetric
if it is unconditional and, for every
increasing sequence of integers
\{n_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
\{x_{n_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
By switching to an equivalent norm we may assume the subsymmetric basis is 1-unconditional and 1-equivalent to all
of its subsequences.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a symmetric or subsymmetric basis for a Banach space X. Let
0\not=\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}x_{j}\in X
.
Given a subset of
\mathbb{N}

\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\ldots\}
partition this set into infinitely many disjoint subsets
\{(i,j)\}_{i,j=1}^{\infty}
. Then the
block
basis generated by
\alpha
is
\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
where

U_{i}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}x_{ij}\mbox{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots.

Remark 1.6
.

We have to be careful with this. In the symmetric case, for different
\{n_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and different partitions
\{(i,j)\}_{i,j=1}^{\infty}

the
\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are symmetric and equivalent to each other.
But for subsymmetric bases, neither of these properties may hold.

Also in
[
2
]
we have

Theorem 1.7
.

If
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a symmetric basis for a Banach space X, then
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and its dual basis
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are equivalent
to all their block bases generated by a single vector if and only if
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to the unit vector basis of
c_{0}
or

\ell_{p}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.

Recently
[
1
]
the subsymmetric version of this was proved.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a subsymmetric basis for a Banach space X then
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

are both equivalent to all block bases generated by a single vector if and only if
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to the unit vector basis
of either
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.

Remark 1.9
.

In the theorems above it is important that we work with possibly infinitely supported vectors since the theorems
fail if we can only work with finitely supported vectors as we will see in the next section.

In this paper we look at finitely supported block bases generated by a single vector and prove the corresponding
results for this family but need the additional requirement that the equivalences are uniform.

2.
Bases Equivalent to the unit vector basis of
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
0\not=\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}x_{n}
. The (finitely supported)

block basis generated by
\alpha
is the block basis

\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
where

U_{i}^{\alpha}=\sum_{n=(i-1)m+1}^{im}b_{n-km}x_{n},\mbox{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots.

For subsymmetric bases, blocks generated by a single vector are always equivalent to the basis, but as we will see, they
may not be uniformly equivalent.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a subsymmetric basis for a Banach space X. Assume
0\not=\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}x_{n}\in X
and

\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is the block basis generated by
\alpha
. Then
\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to

\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

We may assume that the basis is 1-unconditional and 1-equivalent to all of its subsequences. Assume
b_{j}\not=0
for some fixed

1\leq j\leq m
.
We compute for any
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}
:

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{(i-1)m+j}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b_{j}}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}b_{j}x_{(i-1)m+j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b_{j}}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}U_{i}^{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{(i-1)m+k}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b_{j}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}\right)\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}\|.

This completes the stated equivalence.
∎

Now we can prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a unconditional basis for a Banach space X. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to the unit vector basis for
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

(2)

There is a constant
1\leq K
so that whenever
\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}x_{n}
is a unit vector, the block basis generated
by
\alpha
is K-equivalent to
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, the the same result holds for
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

(3)

There is a constant
1\leq K
so that whenever
\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}x_{n}
is a unit vector then the block basis

\{U_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
generated by
\alpha
is complemented in the space by a projection
P
with
\|P\|\leq K
,
and the same is true for
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2),\ (3)
: This is obvious.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
: We may assume the basis is 1-unconditional.
Choose norm one vectors
v_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}x_{i}
and
w_{1}=\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}
and let
u_{1}=v_{1}+w_{1}
. Define

u_{i}=v_{i}+w_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots
by

v_{i}=\sum_{j=(i-1)n+1}^{(i-1)n+m}b_{j-(i-1)n}x_{i}\mbox{ and }w_{i}=\sum_{j=(i-1)n+m+1}^{in}c_{j-(i-1)n}x_{i}.

By assumption, there is a projection
Q
onto the block basis
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
generated by the vector
u_{1}
with
\|Q\|\leq K
.
For each
i=1,2,\ldots
assume

Qv_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{ij}u_{j}\mbox{ and }Qw_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{ij}u_{j}.

Then
b_{ii}+c_{ii}=1
for all
i=1,2,\ldots
. Define an operator
T:[Qv_{i}]\rightarrow[w_{i}]
by
T(Qv_{i})=w_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots
. So
\|TQ\|\leq K
and the matrix of
T
is

[b_{ij}+c_{ij}]
. And since the basis is 1-unconditional, the norm of the diagonal operator
D
of
TQ
is
\|D\|\leq\|TQ\|
. But the
diangonal operator maps
v_{i}
to
w_{i}
. So for all scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
we have

\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}w_{i}\|=\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}Tv_{i}\|\leq K\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}v_{i}\|.

Switching the roles of
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
we have for all scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}v_{i}\|\leq K\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}w_{i}\|

So
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.
Letting
v_{1}=x_{1}
, we discover that
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is equivalent to
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.

The same proof works for
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: We will do this in steps. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

\lambda(n)=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\|\mbox{ and }\mu(n)=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{*}\|.

Step 1
: We show that either
X
is isomorphic to
c_{0}
or
\ell_{1}
or there is a
1<p
so that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\frac{1}{K}\leq\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{1/p}}\leq K.

For this, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{\lambda(n)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
.
Consider the block basis generated by
\alpha_{n}
as

U_{i}^{\alpha_{n}}=\frac{1}{\lambda(n)}\sum_{j=(i-1)n+1}^{in}x_{i},\mbox{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots.

By (2), for every
m=1,2,\ldots
we have

\frac{1}{K}\lambda(m)\leq\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}^{\alpha_{n}}\|=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\lambda(mn)\leq K\lambda(m).

Hence,

\frac{1}{K}\leq\frac{\lambda(mn)}{\lambda(n)\lambda(m)}\leq K.

By the proof of
[
2
]
Theorem (2,a,9) either
sup_{n}\lambda(n)<\infty
, or we have for some
1\leq p<\infty
,

(1)

\frac{1}{K}\leq\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{1/p}}\leq K,\mbox{ for all }n=1,2,\ldots.

If
sup_{n}\lambda_{n}<\infty
then X is isomorphic to
c_{0}
and if
p=1
then X is isomorphic to
\ell_{1}
by
[
2
]
the remark
following (3,a,7). So we assume
1<p<\infty
and choose
1<q
so that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Step 2
: We have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\frac{1}{2K}\leq\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{1/q}}\leq 2K.

For this, we apply
[
2
]
Proposition (3,a,6), page 116:

n\leq\lambda(n)\mu(n)\leq 2n.

Hence

1\leq\frac{\lambda(n)\mu(n)}{n}=\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{1/p}}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{1/q}}\leq 2.

The result is now immediate from Equation
1
.

Step 3
: For all scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
we have

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}\right\|\leq 2K\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The same is true for all
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}^{*}\|\leq 2K\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

For this, we can copy line by line
[
2
]
Theorem (3,a,10), page121.

Step 5
:We conclude the proof.

By steps (2), (3), and (4) we have for all scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}^{*}\right\|\leq 2K\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

By duality, for all
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
we have

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We would hope to not have to assume the basis is unconditional since (3) implies that every subsequence of the basis
is uniformly complemented. Unfortunately, this property does not imply unconditionality of the basis as we see below.

Example
:
Lwt
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be the row vectors of the matrix:

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
1&1&0&0&0&\cdots\\
1&1&1&0&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\end{bmatrix}

The set
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is called the summing basis of
c_{0}
. We will now show that every subsequence of
this basis is 1-complemented. Fix natural numbers
n_{0}=0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
. For
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}x_{i}

in terms of the unit vector basis this vector is:

x=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i},\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}a_{i},\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}a_{i},\ldots\right),

and so the norm of this vector is:

\|x\|=max_{i=1}^{\infty}|\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}a_{j}|.

Define a projection
P
by:

P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=n_{i}+1}^{n_{i+1}}a_{j}\right)x_{n_{i}}.

It is clear that this is a projection and in terms of the unit vector basis this is

Px=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}(n_{1}),\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}a_{i}(n_{1}+1),\ldots,\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}a_{i}(n_{2}),\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}a_{i}(n_{2}+1),\ldots,\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}a_{i}(n_{3}),\ldots\right)

Hence,

\|Px\|=max_{i=1}^{\infty}|\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}a_{j}|=\|x\|.

An obvious question is:

Problem 2.5
.

Do we need the conditions in Theorem to include both
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{x_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
or is just
one of these enough?

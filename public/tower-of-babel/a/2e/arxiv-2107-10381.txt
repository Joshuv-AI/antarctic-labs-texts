Conformal Fundamental Forms and the Asymptotically Poincaré–Einstein Condition
Samuel Blitz ♭ , A. Rod Gover ♯ & Andrew Waldron ♮

Abstract
. An important problem is to determine under which circumstances a metric on a conformally compact manifold is conformal to a Poincaré–Einstein metric. Such conformal rescalings are in general obstructed by conformal invariants of the boundary hypersurface embedding, the first of which is the trace-free second fundamental form and then, at the next order, the trace-free Fialkow tensor. We show that these tensors are the lowest order examples in a sequence of conformally invariant higher fundamental forms determined by the data of a conformal hypersurface embedding. We give a construction of these canonical extrinsic curvatures. Our main result is that the vanishing of these fundamental forms is a necessary and sufficient condition for a conformally compact metric to be conformally related to an asymptotically Poincaré–Einstein metric. More generally, these higher fundamental forms are basic to the study of conformal hypersurface invariants. Because Einstein metrics necessarily have constant scalar curvature, our method employs asymptotic solutions of the singular Yamabe problem to select an asymptotically distinguished conformally compact metric. Our approach relies on conformal tractor calculus as this is key for an extension of the general theory of conformal hypersurface embeddings that we further develop here. In particular, we give in full detail tractor analogs of the classical Gauß Formula and Gauß Theorem for Riemannian hypersurface embeddings. Keywords: Extrinsic conformal geometry, hypersurface embeddings, Poincaré–Einstein metrics, Yamabe problem

Codimension one embedded submanifolds, or

In contrast to an asymptotic singular Yamabe metric, which always exists for generic embedding data, the existence of an asymptotic Poincaré–Einstein metric restricts the allowed conformal embeddings.
This motivates our search for further
conformally invariant obstructions to the existence of Poincaré–Einstein metrics. Indeed,
given a defining function

is the trace-free second fundamental form.
A general conformal embedding is not umbilic but, as discussed above, does determine an asymptotic singular Yamabe metric
g^{o}=g/s^{2}
. Thus, asking whether the higher tensors
h_{k}
with
k\in\{2,\cdots,d-2\}
are compatible with the Poincaré–Einstein condition
gives another perspective for why
the trace-free second fundamental form
\hskip 1.13809pt\mathring{\rm{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.0pt}
is the first in a sequence of
d-2

conformally invariant
tensors characterizing
the local obstruction to a conformal embedding being asymptotically Poincaré–Einstein.
This sequence of fundamental forms is provided by the jets of the Hessian expression in Equation
1.2
; see Section
4.1
.

Our main result establishes that conformal fundamental forms are the obstruction to a conformal embedding admitting an asymptotically Poincaré–Einstein metric. However, the detailed picture here is both rich and subtle. For example, in
d=5
dimensions the fourth fundamental form displayed in Equation (
1.3
) is ill-defined. However, for umbilic embeddings, the hypersurface invariant

(1.7)

C_{\hat{n}(ab)}^{\top}+HW_{\hat{n}ab\hat{n}}

is both conformally invariant and has transverse order three. We call a transverse order
n-1
hypersurface invariant a
conditional fundamental form
iff it is an
n
th conformal fundamental form on embeddings for which some lower transverse order fundamental form vanishes.
This suggests two generalizations of umbilicity:

Definition 1.6
.

We say that a conformal hypersurface embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},\bm{c})
is
hyperumbilic
if,
for each and every
n\in\{2,\ldots,\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil\}
, an
n
th fundamental form vanishes.

Lemma
3.12
) establishes that the above and following definitions are well-defined.
As an application, it follows from Theorem
1.3

that
\hat{D}^{T}_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}
for hyperumbilic embeddings in dimensions
d\geqslant 4
.

Definition 1.7
.

We say that a conformal hypersurface embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},\bm{c})
is
überumbilic
if the embedding is hyperumbilic
and,
for each and every
n\in\{\lceil\frac{d+3}{2}\rceil,\ldots,d-1\}
, an
n
th conditional fundamental form vanishes.

The above definitions concern the second through
(d-1)
th fundamental forms. At the weight where one would expect a
d
th fundamental form,
an obstruction to the Poincaré–Einstein problem of a different nature is known to appear, namely the symmetric, trace-free,
conformally invariant Fefferman–Graham tensor
[
16
]
. This tensor is intrinsic to the boundary conformal geometry
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})
and must necessarily vanish for
\bm{c}|_{M\backslash\Sigma}
to contain a smooth Poincaré–Einstein metric. The appearance of conditional fundamental forms for
n\geqslant\lceil\frac{d+3}{2}\rceil

in Definition

is a feature of the theory of normal operators of
[
25
]
, see in particular Lemma
4.7
.

Our main result solves Problem
1.1
in terms of Definition
1.7
:

Theorem 1.8
.

A conformal class of metrics
\bm{c}
admits
an asymptotic Poincaré–Einstein metric
iff the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},\bm{c})
is überumbilic.

by

(2.6)

\frac{1}{d+2\underline{w}-k}\,\lambda:=\frac{\lambda}{d-k}-\frac{2w^{\prime}}{(d-k)^{2}}\in\Gamma\big(\mathcal{F}M[w^{\prime}]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}M[0]\big)\,,

and

(2.7)

\frac{1}{d+2\underline{w}-k}\,(\lambda T):=T\frac{1}{d+2\underline{w}+2w-k}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\lambda\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w]\,\oplus\,{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w]\otimes\mathcal{F}M[w^{\prime}])\,.

Remark 2.5
.

It is even possible to define
an operator
\frac{1}{\alpha\underline{w}+\beta}
acting on sections of

\otimes^{k}\mathcal{F}M[w^{\prime}]

by appealing to the formal power series expression

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\underline{w}+\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{2}}\,\underline{w}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{3}}\,\underline{w}^{2}+\ldots\,,

since only finitely many terms are ever needed.

In general we prefer to avoid working with sections of Whitney sum bundles such as those arising when the inverse weight operator above acts on log-density bundles. Happily, the following result shows that composing the Thomas-
D
operator with the action of

\frac{1}{d+2\underline{w}-2}
on log densities results in a tractor.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
0\neq\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Big(D\circ\frac{1}{\alpha\underline{w}+\beta}\Big)\lambda=\frac{1}{\beta}D\lambda\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M[-1])\,.

Proof.

This follows from a straightforward computation in a choice of scale, or otherwise can be seen as a direct consequence of Equation (
2.6
).
∎

There is an analog of Proposition (
2.4
) for the algebra of Thomas-
D
operators and log densities; this is another case where the need for Whitney bundles is obviated.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\lambda
be any log density and let
w\neq\frac{2-d}{2}
. Then,

\hat{D}\circ\lambda-\lambda\circ\hat{D}:\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w])\rightarrow\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M\otimes{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w-1])\,,

and moreover

\hat{D}\circ\lambda-\lambda\circ\hat{D}=(\hat{D}\lambda)-\tfrac{2}{d+2\underline{w}}X{\hskip 0.85358pt}(\hat{D}\lambda){\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hat{D}\,.

Proof.

This is an easy computation in a choice of scale.
∎

A particularly useful application of the Thomas-
D
operator is the construction of tractors from
Riemannian tensors.

2.4.
Tractor Insertion

Given a conformal density-valued, weight
w+r
, trace-free, rank
r
Riemannian tensor
t_{abc\cdots}
, there exists a canonical map (see for example
[
21
]
) to insert
t_{abc\cdots}
into a tractor
T^{ABC\cdots}
with the same symmetries as
t_{abc\cdots}
. This map is denoted by
q
; there are three particular instances relevant for our computations:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
g\in\bm{c}
.

(i)

Given
v_{a}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M[w+1])
where
w\neq 1-d
, then

\displaystyle q(v_{a})

\displaystyle=:V^{A}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M[w])

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v^{a}\\
-\frac{\nabla\cdot v}{d+w-1}\end{pmatrix}\,,

where

D_{A}V^{A}=X_{A}V^{A}=0\,.

(ii)

Given
t_{ab}\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}M[w+2])
where
w\neq-d,1-d
, then

\displaystyle q(t_{ab})

\displaystyle=:T^{AB}\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}{\mathcal{T}}M[w])

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&t^{ab}&-\frac{\nabla\cdot t^{a}}{d+w}\\
0&-\frac{\nabla\cdot t^{b}}{d+w}&\frac{\nabla\cdot\nabla\cdot t+(d+w)P_{ab}t^{ab}}{(d+w)(d+w-1)}\end{pmatrix}\,,

where

D_{A}T^{AB}=0=X_{A}T^{AB}\,.

(iii)

Given
t_{abcd}\in\Gamma(\otimes^{4}T^{*}M[w+4])
, where
w\neq 1-d,2-d
, such that
t
has the algebraic symmetries of the Riemann tensor and is trace-free, then

q(t_{abcd})=:T^{ABCD}\in\Gamma(\otimes^{4}{\mathcal{T}}M[w])

where

\displaystyle{T}^{abcd}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}t^{abcd}

\displaystyle{T}^{abc-}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}-\frac{\nabla_{d}t^{dabc}}{d+w-1}

\displaystyle{T}^{a-b-}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\frac{\nabla_{a}\nabla_{c}t^{abcd}+(d+w-1)P_{ac}t^{abcd}}{(d+w-1)(d+w-2)}\,,

where
T
also has the algebraic symmetries of the Riemann tensor and

D_{A}T^{ABCD}=X_{A}T^{ABCD}=0=h_{AC}T^{ABCD}\,.

Proof.

The proofs of the above three results can either be given in a much more general setting (see
[
21
]
), or by explicit computation whose intricacy increases in concordance with tensor rank. Here we give the lowest rank example.

The “bottom slot”
V^{-}
of
q(v_{a}):=V^{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}(v^{+},v^{a},v^{-})
can be computed by writing out the constraint
D_{A}V^{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}0
for some
g\in\bm{c}{\hskip 0.85358pt}
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(d+2w-2)(wv^{-}+\nabla\cdot v+Jv^{+}+dv^{-})-(\Delta+(w-1)J)v^{+}+2\nabla\cdot v+dv^{-}

\displaystyle=(d+2w)(\nabla\cdot v+(d+w-1)v^{-})\,,

where the second equality comes from the requirement that
X_{A}V^{A}=0
. When
w\neq-\frac{d}{2}
, this yields the quoted result. If
w=-\frac{d}{2}
, we need to verify that the result for
V^{A}
given for a choice of
g\in\bm{c}
defines a section of
\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M[w])
. This is easily established by transforming the quoted result to a conformally related metric.
∎

Note that the insertion operator
q
is a right-inverse of the extraction map

q^{*}\hskip-1.42262pt\circ q=\text{Id}\,,

but
q
is
not
a left-inverse of the extraction map,

q\circ q^{*}\neq\text{Id}\,.

The above operator, acting on rank-
2
tractors, is computed in the following lemma for use later.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let the tractor
T\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}{\mathcal{T}}M[w])
, where
w\neq 1-\frac{d}{2},-\frac{d}{2},-d,1-d
, obey

X_{A}T^{AB}=0\>\mbox{ and }\>q^{*}(T)\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}M[w+2])\,.

Then

(q\circ q^{*})(T)=\tilde{T}\,,

where

\tilde{T}_{AB}:=T_{AB}-\tfrac{2}{(d+w)(d+2w)}X_{(A}D^{C}T_{B)C}+\tfrac{1}{(d+w)(d+w-1)(d+2w)}X_{A}X_{B}D^{C}\hat{D}^{D}T_{CD}\,.

Proof.

We will establish that there is a unique
\tilde{T}
that
satisfies

\hat{D}^{A}\tilde{T}_{AB}=X^{A}\tilde{T}_{AB}=0=h^{AB}\tilde{T}_{AB}\,,

and obeys
q^{*}(\tilde{T})=q^{*}(T)
whenever
q^{*}(T)\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}M[w+2])
. This ensures that
(q\circ q^{*})(T)=\tilde{T}
.
For that, we use the operator version of Proposition
2.4
, valid acting on tractors of weight
w\neq 1-d/2,-d/2
:

(2.8)

\hat{D}^{A}\circ X^{B}=X^{B}\hat{D}^{A}+h^{AB}-\tfrac{2}{d+2w}X^{A}\hat{D}^{B}\,.

We first verify that
X\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\tilde{T}=0
. Because
X^{2}=0=X^{A}T_{AB}
, we simply need to check that
X^{A}\hat{D}^{B}T_{AB}
vanishes. Applying Equation (
2.8
) we have that

\displaystyle X^{A}\hat{D}^{B}T_{AB}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{D}^{B}X^{A}-h^{AB}+\tfrac{2}{d+2w}X^{B}\hat{D}^{A}\right)T_{AB}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{d+2w}X^{B}\hat{D}^{A}T_{AB}\,,

where we have used that
h^{AB}T_{AB}=0=X^{A}T_{AB}
. Because
T
is symmetric, we are left with the identity

\tfrac{d+2w-2}{d+2w}X^{A}\hat{D}^{B}T_{AB}=0\,.

Thus, thanks to the weight assumptions, it follows that
X^{A}\hat{D}^{B}T_{AB}=0
, and hence
X^{A}\tilde{T}_{AB}=0
. Similarly, we have that
h^{AB}\tilde{T}_{AB}=0
.

Finally, we check that
\hat{D}^{A}\tilde{T}_{AB}=0.
We do this in stages. First, we evaluate
\hat{D}^{A}(X_{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC})
:

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{A}(X_{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC})

\displaystyle=\left[(w-1)+d+2-\tfrac{2(w-1)}{d+2(w-1)}\right]\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(d+w-1)(d+2w)}{d+2w-2}\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC}\,.

Next, we evaluate the term
\hat{D}^{A}(X_{B}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC})
:

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{A}(X_{B}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC})

\displaystyle=X_{B}\hat{D}^{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}+\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC}-\tfrac{2}{d+2(w-1)}X^{A}\hat{D}_{B}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}

\displaystyle=X_{B}\hat{D}^{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}+\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt-\tfrac{2}{d+2(w-1)}\left[\hat{D}_{B}(X^{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC})-h^{A}_{B}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}+\tfrac{2}{d+2(w-1)}X_{B}\hat{D}^{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(d+2w)(d+2w-4)}{(d+2w-2)^{2}}X_{B}\hat{D}^{A}\hat{D}^{C}T_{AC}+\tfrac{d+2w}{d+2w-2}\hat{D}^{C}T_{BC}\,.

Last, we evaluate the term
\hat{D}^{A}(X_{A}X_{B}\hat{D}^{C}\hat{D}^{D}T_{CD})
:

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{A}(X_{A}X_{B}\hat{D}^{C}\hat{D}^{D}T_{CD})=\tfrac{(d+w-1)(d+2w)}{d+2w-2}X_{B}\hat{D}^{C}\hat{D}^{D}T_{CD}\,.

Combining these terms, we find that
\hat{D}^{A}\tilde{T}_{AB}=0
, thus completing the proof.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Note that if a weight
w\neq 1-\frac{d}{2},-\frac{d}{2}
tractor
\tilde{T}^{AB\cdots}
obeys

X_{A}\tilde{T}^{AB\cdots}=0=\hat{D}_{A}\tilde{T}^{AB\cdots}\,,

then it follows directly from Equation (

) that

X_{A}\hat{D}^{C}\tilde{T}^{AB\cdots}=-\tilde{T}^{CB\cdots}\,.

Often it is useful to change the projecting part of tractor; the operator in the following lemma is an instance of this.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
V^{A}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M[w])
and
T\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}M[w])
. Then if
w\neq-1,-1-\frac{d}{2}
,

r(V^{A}):=V^{A}-\frac{1}{w+1}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hat{D}^{A}\big(X_{B}V^{B}\big)

obeys

X_{A}\,r(V^{A})=0\,,

while if
w\neq 0,-1,-\frac{d}{2},-1-\frac{d}{2},-2-\frac{d}{2}
,

r(T^{AB\circ}):=T^{(AB)\circ}-\tfrac{2}{w}\hat{D}^{(A}\big(X_{C}T^{|C|B)\circ}\big)+\tfrac{1}{w(w+1)}\hat{D}^{(A}\hat{D}^{B)\circ}\big(X_{C}X_{D}T^{CD}\big)\\
-\tfrac{8}{wd(d+2w+2)}X^{(A}\hat{D}^{B)\circ}\big(\hat{D}_{C}(X_{D}T^{CD})\big)\,,

obeys

X_{A}\,r(T^{AB})=0=h_{AB}\,r(T^{AB})\,.

Proof.

The proof is an elementary application of the identity

X_{A}\hat{D}^{A}T=wT\,,

valid for any weight
w\neq 1-\frac{d}{2}
tractor
T
, the fact that
X
and
D
are null, and Equation (
2.8
).
∎

Remark 2.12
.

The map
r
of the above lemma can be generalized, modulo distinguished weights, to tractors
T
of arbitrary tensor type so that
r(T)
is both tractor trace-free and in the kernel of contraction by the canonical tractor
X
.

The algebra of multiple Thomas-
D
operators, and in particular commutators of these, will be of particular importance. This leads to a
tractor tensor that generalizes the Weyl tensor.

2.5.
The
W
-Tractor

To begin with, we consider the curvature of the tractor connection. The commutator of tractor connections
gives the
tractor curvature

\Omega_{ab}{}^{AB}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{T}}M)
which acts on a standard tractor
V^{A}
according to

\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]V^{A}={{\Omega_{ab}}^{A}}_{B}V^{B}=:\Omega_{ab}{}^{\sharp}V^{A}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}M\otimes{\mathcal{T}}M)\,.

The operator
\Omega_{ab}{}^{\sharp}

extends by linearity to act on general tractor tensors.
In general, if
\Omega^{AB}
is any skew-symmetric tractor, then
\Omega^{\sharp}
denotes the operator on tractors
V^{AB\cdots}
defined by
\Omega^{\sharp}V^{AB\cdots}:=\Omega^{A}{}_{C}V^{CB\cdots}+\Omega^{B}{}_{C}V^{AC\cdots}+\cdots
.
For a choice of metric
g\in\mathbf{c}
, the tractor curvature is given in an obvious matrix notation by

\displaystyle{{\Omega_{ab}}^{C}}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
{C_{ab}}^{c}&{{W_{ab}}^{c}}_{d}&0\\
0&-C_{abd}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

The projecting part of the tractor curvature is the Weyl tensor, which is indeed conformally invariant. In three dimensions, the Weyl tensor vanishes, and the Cotton tensor is then the projecting part and hence invariant in this dimension.

At this juncture, to build its tractor analog, we could either insert the Weyl tensor into a tractor or instead view the Thomas-
D
operator as the tractor analog of the gradient operator and study commutators of Thomas-
D
operators. Both routes will lead us to the so-called
W
-tractor.
Let us begin with the first route.
In dimension
d>4
,
using Proposition
2.8
, we can define the weight
-2
,
rank 4,

W
-tractor

by

{W}^{ABCD}:=q(W_{abcd})\,,

where
W_{abcd}
is the Weyl tensor. By construction,
W
has the symmetries of the Weyl tensor

{W}^{[ABC]D}={W}^{ABCD}+{W}^{BACD}={W}^{ABCD}-{W}^{CDAB}=0={W}^{A}{}_{A}{}^{CD}\,.

We have used the same letter
W
for both the Weyl curvature and
W
-tractor because the projecting part of the latter,
W^{abcd}
is—by construction—the Weyl tensor.
The remaining tensor
content of the
W
-tractor is given below.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
d>4
. Then
in any scale
g\in\bm{c}
, the
{W}
-tractor is given by

\displaystyle{W}^{abc-}

\displaystyle=C^{abc}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle{W}^{a-b-}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta P^{ab}-\nabla_{c}\nabla^{b}P^{ca}+P_{cd}W^{adbc}}{d-4}\,.

All other components are either zero or determined by the symmetry of the
W
-tractor.

Proof.

This lemma follows from Proposition
2.8

and the curvature identities

C^{abc}=\frac{\nabla_{d}W^{dcab}}{d-3}\,,\qquad\nabla_{a}\nabla_{c}W^{abcd}=(d-3)\big(\Delta P^{bd}-\nabla_{c}\nabla^{b}P^{cd}\big)\,.

∎

Remark 2.14
.

In
d=4
dimensions, the
{W}
-tractor is not well-defined. Instead we may consider the equivalence class of tractors defined by the relation
{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{ABCD}\sim{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{ABCD}+X^{[A}V^{B][D}X^{C]}
for
V^{BD}
any rank 2, symmetric, trace-free, weight
-4
tractor. In any choice of scale
g\in\bm{c}

there is always a representative

{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{abcd}=W^{abcd}
,

{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{abc-}=C^{abc}

and
{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{a-b-}=0
.

Note that the residue of the
d-4
pole in Equation (
2.9
) continued to four dimensions is precisely the four dimensional Bach tensor

(2.10)

B^{ab}=\Delta P^{ab}-\nabla_{c}\nabla^{b}P^{ca}+P_{cd}W^{adbc}\,.

For that reason, the
W
-tractor is often defined with an overall factor of
d-4
so that it is well-defined in four dimensions; this has the disadvantage
that in higher dimensions, the projecting part only equals the Weyl tensor up to an overall non-zero multiplicative factor.
Also note that we use the above tensor expression to define a Bach tensor
B^{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}(d-4)W^{a-b-}
in dimensions higher than four.

Turning to the other route, the commutator of Thomas-
D
operators is given below:

Proposition 2.15
.

In dimension
d\neq 4
, the commutator of Thomas-
D
operators obeys

\displaystyle\left[D_{A},D_{B}\right]=(d+2w-4)(d+2w-2)W_{AB}{}^{\sharp}+4X_{[A}W_{B]C}{}^{\sharp}\circ D^{C}.

In
d=4
, the commutator of two Thomas-
D
operators is

\displaystyle\left[D_{A},D_{B}\right]=4w(w+1){\hskip 0.85358pt}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}_{AB}{}^{\sharp}+4X_{[A}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}_{B]C}{}^{\sharp}\circ D^{C},

and the sum of equivalence classes on the right hand side is a tractor-valued operator.

Proof.

This result was first given in
[
26
]

using an
ambient construction.
Alternatively one can—at the cost of quite some computation—verify the first displayed equation using Equation (
2.3
) and
the injectors introduced above.
The same applies for the second display, but we must first verify that its right hand side is defined as a tractor operator independently of the choice of equivalence class representative chosen for
{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}
. It is sufficient to check that the right-hand side of the second display, acting on a tractor
V^{C}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M[w])
and contracted with

, does not depend on the choice of metric representative.

\displaystyle Y_{A}Y_{C}\Big[4w(w\!

\displaystyle+{\hskip 0.85358pt}1){\hskip 0.85358pt}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{ABCE}V_{E}+\,4X^{[A}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{B]ECF}D_{E}V_{F}\Big]

\displaystyle=4w(w+1){{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-B-E}V_{E}+2{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{BE-F}D_{E}V_{F}-2X^{B}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-E-F}D_{E}V_{F}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2(d+2w-2){{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{Be-F}\nabla_{e}V_{F}-2X^{B}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-E-F}D_{E}V_{F}

\displaystyle=4(w+1){{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{Be-F}\nabla_{e}V_{F}-2X^{B}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-E-F}D_{E}V_{F}

\displaystyle=4(w+1)\left[X^{B}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-e-F}+Z^{B}_{b}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{be-F}\right]\nabla_{e}V_{F}-2(d+2w-2)X^{B}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{-e-F}\nabla_{e}V^{F}

In the above computation, we used that
{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{+ABC}=0
and that the Thomas-

{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{be-F}\sim{{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{be-F}+X^{[b}V^{e][F}X^{-]}={{\hskip 0.85358pt}\widetilde{\!W\!}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}}{}^{be-F}

3.
Conformally Embedded Hypersurfaces

We now develop the invariant theory for a hypersurface
\Sigma
smoothly embedded in
a conformal manifold
(M,\bm{c})
.
Here we mean that
\Sigma
is a smoothly embedded codimension-
1
submanifold of
(M,\bm{c})
, and denote this by
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
.
It is simplifying, although not strictly necessary for the study of local invariant theory, to assume that
\Sigma
is closed and orientable, and that its embedding
in
M
is separating. In this case
M=M^{+}\sqcup\Sigma\sqcup M^{-}
.
Given a metric
g\in\bm{c}
, we denote the unit conormal by
\hat{n}
, and take as convention that it points in the direction of
M^{+}
.
The first fundamental form
{\rm I}_{ab}=(g_{ab}-\hat{n}_{a}\hat{n}_{b})|_{\Sigma}

equals the induced metric
\bar{g}_{ab}
of
\Sigma
.
The second fundamental form is then

(3.1)

{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}={\rm I}_{a}^{c}\nabla_{c}\hat{n}_{b}^{\rm e}\big|_{\Sigma}=\nabla^{\top}_{a}\hat{n}_{b}^{\rm e}\big|_{\Sigma}\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}T^{*}\Sigma)\,,

where

\hat{n}^{\rm e}
on the right hand side of the above display is any smooth extension of
\hat{n}
to
M
.
Note that the image of the projection of the tangent bundle
TM
along the hypersurface by the endomorphism
I^{a}_{b}=g^{ac}I_{bc}
is isomorphic to
T\Sigma
. We therefore identify these spaces and use the same abstract index notation for hypersurface tensors as for ambient ones.
We use a
\top
notation
for projection to
T\Sigma
, and also for more general tensors. For example if
\omega
is a one-form in
\Gamma(T^{*}M)|_{\Sigma}
, then
\top(\omega_{a})\equiv\omega^{\top}_{a}:=I_{a}^{b}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\omega_{b}\in\Gamma(T^{*}\Sigma)
. Contraction by the unit conormal, followed by projection is denoted, for example for a two-form, by
\omega_{\hat{n}b}^{\top}:=(\hat{n}^{a}\omega_{ac})I^{c}_{b}
.
When
\omega\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)
we use
\omega^{\top}
to denote
(\omega|_{\Sigma})^{\top}
.
A ring over the projection symbol
\mathring{\top}
denotes an additional projection to the (hypersurface) trace-free part.
In these terms, the Gauß formula then reads

(3.2)

\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{v}^{b}=\nabla_{a}^{\top}v^{b}\big|_{\Sigma}+\hat{n}^{b}{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ac}\bar{v}^{c}\,,

where in the first term on the right hand side,
v^{a}
denotes any smooth extension of a hypersurface vector
\bar{v}^{a}\in\Gamma(T\Sigma)
to
M
. Also,
\bar{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric
\bar{g}
.
Quite generally, we use a bar notation on objects to indicate when they belong to the hypersurface
\Sigma
.
For example, curvature quantities
intrinsic to the hypersurface will be denoted by bars as will be the hypersurface dimension, so
d-1=\bar{d}
.
The Gauß equation is then given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle R^{\top}_{abcd}=\overline{R}_{abcd}-{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ac}{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{bd}+{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ad}{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{bc}\,.

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{[a}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{b]c}-\tfrac{1}{d-2}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{g}_{c[a}\bar{\nabla}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{b]}=\tfrac{1}{2}W^{\top}_{abc\hat{n}}\,,

3.1.
Hypersurface Tractor Calculus

Tractor calculus is a key technical tool for studying conformal embeddings. A basic observation
[
6
]
is that the unit conormal
\hat{n}^{g}
and mean curvature
H^{g}
of an embedded hypersurface depend on the conformal representative
g\in\bm{c}
as

\big(\hat{n}^{\Omega^{2}g},H^{\Omega^{2}g}\big)=\big(\Omega{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hat{n}^{g},\Omega^{-1}(H^{g}+\hat{n}^{g}\cdot{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\log\Omega)\big)\,,

where
0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}M
and its exterior derivative appearing above are restricted to
\Sigma
.
Henceforth we reuse the notation
\hat{n}^{a}
and
H
for the corresponding density-valued objects.
So, given a metric
g\in\bm{c}
, the triple

N^{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\,\hat{n}^{a}\\
-H\end{pmatrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}\hat{n}^{a}Z^{A}_{a}-HY^{A}

labels a section of the tractor bundle
{\mathcal{T}}M|_{\Sigma}
. The section
N^{A}
is termed the
normal tractor
and obeys

h(N,N)=1\,.

Note that the projecting part of
N
is the unit normal, which indeed defines a vector-valued weight
w=-1
conformal density.
The normal tractor mimics the
rôle
of the unit conormal in Riemannian hypersurface theory. In particular,
projection of the tractor bundle along
\Sigma
by
{\rm I}^{A}_{B}:=\delta^{A}_{B}-N^{A}N_{B}

gives a bundle isomorphic to the tractor bundle
{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma
of
(\Sigma,\bar{\bm{c}})
, where
\bar{\bm{c}}
is the induced conformal class of metrics
[
22
]
.
The tractor
{\rm I}_{AB}:=h_{AB}|_{\Sigma}-N_{A}N_{B}
is the

tractor first fundamental form
.
Hence, we shall identify these spaces and use the same abstract index notation for ambient and hypersurface tractors.
In particular the hypersurface tractor metric is given by
\bar{h}_{AB}={\rm I}_{AB}
.
Moreover, together the normal tractor and tractor metric give an isomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}M|_{\Sigma}\cong{\mathcal{N}}\Sigma\oplus{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma\,,

where
{\mathcal{N}}\Sigma
is the tractor normal bundle (defined by orthogonal decomposition completely analogously to the normal bundle for Riemannian hypersurface embeddings).
Note that for the canonical tractor
X^{A}
, we have
{\rm I}^{A}_{B}X^{B}=X^{A}
, whose image under the bundle isomorphism is the hypersurface canonical tractor
\bar{X}^{A}
; thus we often drop the bar and denote this by
X^{A}
.

Recall that the trace-free combination

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}:={{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}-H\,\bar{g}\,,

is conformally invariant and defines a section of
\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma[1])
. In dimensions
d>3
, it can be inserted into a symmetric, trace-free hypersurface tractor

L^{AB}:=\bar{q}({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab})\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma[-1])

termed the
tractor second fundamental form

[
35
,
46
]
. In the above, we have applied the insertion map of Lemma
2.8
on the conformal manifold
(\Sigma,\bar{\bm{c}})
. Note that in a choice of scale
g
,

L^{AB}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{ab}&-\tfrac{1}{d-2}\bar{\nabla}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{a}\\[2.84526pt]
0&-\tfrac{1}{d-2}\bar{\nabla}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{b}&\frac{\bar{\nabla}\cdot\bar{\nabla}\cdot{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}+(d-2)\bar{\it P}^{ab}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}}{(d-2)(d-3)}\end{pmatrix}.

The hypersurface conformal curvature quantity
L^{2}={\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{2}=:K\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[-2])
was termed the
rigidity density
because it was employed to describe rigid strings in
[
47
]
.

Remark 3.1
.

In the theory of surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the square of the second fundamental form is sometimes termed the third fundamental form
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}
. If
\Sigma\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is a surface expressed as the level set of a unit defining function
s:{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, then the second fundamental form
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}
is the Hessian of
s
, so that
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}=\nabla n
where
n=\nabla s
. The third fundamental form then obeys

{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}=-\nabla_{n}\nabla_{a}n_{b}\big|_{\Sigma}={{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{2}\,.

Indeed one might even define higher fundamental forms by taking successive normal derivatives, because for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
,

{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{k+1}_{ab}=\tfrac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\nabla_{n}^{k}\nabla_{a}n_{b}\big|_{\Sigma}\,.

In Section
3.5
we give a definition of the
transverse order
of an hypersurface invariant by viewing formulæ such as that above as functionals of a general curved ambient metric and then counting normal/transverse derivatives on this metric. The third fundamental form for a hypersurface embedded in a Riemannian manifold
defined by
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}:=-\nabla_{n}\nabla_{a}n_{b}\big|_{\Sigma}={{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{2}-R_{\hat{n}ab\hat{n}}

then has transverse order two. A main aim of this article is to develop a theory of conformally invariant higher fundamental forms.

In dimensions
d>3
, manipulating the trace of the Gauß equation (
3.3
) produces a conformally invariant third fundamental form, termed the Fialkow tensor
F\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[0])
and defined by
[
18
]
(see also
[
52
]
),

(3.5)

\Big({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{2}_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2(d-2)}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{2}\bar{g}_{ab}-W_{\hat{n}ab\hat{n}}\Big)=(d-3)\big({\it P}^{\top}_{ab}-\bar{\it P}_{ab}+H{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}+\tfrac{1}{2}H^{2}\bar{g}_{ab}\big)=:(d-3)F_{ab}\,.

We shall call the above relation the
Fialkow–Gauß equation
.

The trace-free Fialkow tensor, denoted
{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}
, is also of special interest. In particular, in dimensions
d>4
, it too can be inserted (using Lemma
2.8
) into a symmetric, trace-free tractor

F^{AB}:=q({\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab})\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma[-2])

termed the
Fialkow tractor
. In a choice of scale
g
,

F^{AB}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}&-\tfrac{1}{d-3}\bar{\nabla}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{a}\\[2.84526pt]
0&-\tfrac{1}{d-3}\bar{\nabla}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{b}&\frac{\bar{\nabla}\cdot\bar{\nabla}\cdot{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}+(d-3)\bar{\it P}^{ab}{\hskip 1.70717pt}\mathring{\!{F}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab}}{(d-3)(d-4)}\end{pmatrix}.

It is also useful to define a weight
-1
, rank three tractor that combines the Fialkow and trace-free second fundamental forms,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ABC}:=2N_{[C}L_{B]A}+2X_{[C}F_{B]A}+\tfrac{K}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X_{[C}\bar{h}_{B]A}.

From its Definition (
3.5
), we see that Fialkow tensor measures the difference between the bulk and hypersurface tractor connections through their
respective Schouten tensors.
This leads to an analog of Equation (
3.2
) (see
[
31

), that we term the Fialkow–Gauß formula

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{V}^{B}=\nabla^{\top}_{a}{V}^{B}\big|_{\Sigma}-{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{C}\bar{V}^{C}\,.

Here
\bar{V}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}\Sigma)
is any standard hypersurface tractor and we have used the isomorphism discussed above between the hypersurface tractor bundle and the projection of the bulk tractor bundle restricted to
\Sigma
, to extend this to
V\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M)
. The displayed formula does not depend on the choice of such an extension.

Formulæ like the right hand side of Equation (
3.7
)
that are expressed in terms of an operator acting on an extension of an object defined only along the hypersurface play an important
rôle
in the study of extrinsic geometry.
In general, we call an operator
{\rm O}
acting on sections
a
of a
vector bundle over the bulk manifold

tangential

if
(Oa)|_{\Sigma}
only depends on the restriction
\bar{a}=a|_{\Sigma}

(
i.e.
, it is independent of the choice of extension
a
of
\bar{a}
).
This defines an operator
{\rm O}_{\scriptscriptstyle\Sigma}

along
\Sigma
by
{\rm O}_{\scriptscriptstyle\Sigma}\,\bar{a}:=({\rm O}a)|_{\Sigma}
.
Note that the right hand side
is
a holographic formula for the quantity
{\rm O}_{\scriptscriptstyle\Sigma}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{a}
; this notion is developed in detail in Section
3.5
.
The operator
\nabla^{\top}
is tangential. Our next task is to develop a tangential analog of the Thomas-
D
operator in order to relate the bulk Thomas-
D
operator to its hypersurface counterpart.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
w+\tfrac{d}{2}\neq 1,\tfrac{3}{2},2

and
N^{\rm e}
be any extension of the normal tractor. Then, the operator

\hat{D}^{T}:\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M\otimes{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M[w-1])\,,

given by

\hat{D}^{T}_{A}:=\hat{D}_{A}-N_{A}^{\rm e}N^{B}_{\rm e}\hat{D}_{B}+\frac{X_{A}}{d+2w-3}\left(N^{B}_{\rm e}N^{C}_{\rm e}\hat{D}_{B}\hat{D}_{C}+\frac{wK}{d-2}\right)\,,

is tangential. Moreover, if
\operatorname{O}^{A}
is any operator acting on tractors of weight
\frac{1-d}{2}
that obeys

\operatorname{O}^{A}\circ X_{A}=0\,,

then the operator

\operatorname{O}\,\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\,{\hskip 0.85358pt}\tilde{D}^{T}:=\operatorname{O}^{A}\circ{\hskip 0.85358pt}\big(\hat{D}_{A}-N_{A}^{\rm e}N^{B}_{\rm e}\hat{D}_{B}\big)

is also tangential.

The tangential Thomas-
D
operator was first introduced in
[
23
]
; the proof of the above result is
given in
[
31
]
,
and is simplified using holographic ideas. Note that, unlike the Levi-Civita connection, which becomes tangential upon projection by the first fundamental form, projection by the tractor first fundamental form alone does not render the Thomas-
D
tangential. Also note that the last term proportional to
K
in the definition of
\hat{D}^{T}
is separately tangential, but has been added because the operator in parentheses will play a special
rôle
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We prove the Gauss-Thomas formula
1.3
acting on a tractor vector of arbitrary weight and then generalize. That is, we look to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
V\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}\Sigma[w])
. Then, the bulk tangential and hypersurface Thomas-
D
operators obey

\big(\hat{D}^{T}_{A}\big)_{\Sigma}V^{B}=\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}V^{B}+\Gamma_{A}{}^{\sharp}V^{B}\\
-\frac{X_{A}}{\bar{d}+2w-2}\bigg\{2\Gamma_{C}{}^{\sharp}\circ\hat{\bar{D}}^{C}V^{B}+\Gamma^{C}{}^{\sharp}\circ\Gamma_{C}{}^{\sharp}V^{B}\\
+\frac{1}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}\left[\left(\hat{\bar{D}}K\right)\wedge X\right]^{\sharp}V^{B}-\frac{(3\bar{d}+2)wK}{2\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}V^{B}\bigg\}\,.

Proof.

We rely heavily on
[
31
, Lemma 4.9]
, which states that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left(\hat{D}^{T}{}^{A}\right)_{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}w\\[2.84526pt]
\nabla^{\top}{}^{a}\\[2.84526pt]
-\frac{\Delta^{\top}+w\bar{J}}{\bar{d}+2w-2}+\frac{wK}{2(\bar{d}-1)(\bar{d}+2w-2)}\end{pmatrix}

We check the lemma by contracting both sides with the injectors and checking that it holds for all three. First note that
w=\bar{w}
and
X_{A}\Gamma^{A}{}_{BC}=0
. Thus, the lemma holds upon contraction with
X^{A}
.

Next, we check the lemma upon contraction with
\bar{Z}^{A}_{a}
. Note that, according to Equation (
3.8
),
\bar{Z}^{A}_{a}\hat{D}^{T}{}_{A}=\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}\right)_{\Sigma}
. Because
X_{A}\bar{Z}^{A}_{a}=0
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}\right)_{\Sigma}V^{B}=\bar{\nabla}_{a}V^{B}+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{C}\bar{V}^{C}\,,

which is the Fialkow-Gauss equation. Thus, the lemma holds upon contraction with
\bar{Z}^{A}_{a}
.

Finally, we check that the identity holds upon contraction with
\bar{Y}^{A}
. From Equation. (
3.8
), we have that

\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{A}\left[\hat{D}^{T}_{A}-\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}\right]=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\Delta^{\top}+w\bar{J}}{\bar{d}+2w-2}+\frac{wK}{2(\bar{d}-1)(\bar{d}+2w-2)}\right)+\frac{1}{\bar{d}+2w-2}\left(\bar{\Delta}+w\bar{J}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\bar{d}+2w-2}\left(\Delta^{\top}-\bar{\Delta}-\frac{wK}{2(\bar{d}-1)}\right).

We explicitly compute the tractor Laplacian difference
\Delta^{\top}-\bar{\Delta}
.

From the definition of the tractor Laplacian and defining
\Gamma^{ABC}_{\perp}:=\Gamma^{ABC}-2N^{[C}L^{B]A}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}^{3}\Sigma[-1])
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\top}V^{B}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{a}^{\top}\left(\bar{\nabla}^{a}V^{B}+{\Gamma^{aBC}}V_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\top}_{a}\left(\bar{\nabla}^{a}V^{B}+\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}V_{C}+2N^{[C}L^{B]a}V_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+{\Gamma^{aB}}_{C}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V^{C}+\bar{\nabla}_{a}(\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}V_{C})+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}_{\perp}V_{C}+\nabla^{\top}_{a}\left(2N^{[C}L^{B]a}V_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+{\Gamma^{aB}}_{C}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V^{C}+\left(\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}\right)V_{C}+\left({\Gamma^{a}}^{BC}-2N^{[C}L^{B]a}\right)\bar{\nabla}_{a}V_{C}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}V_{C}-2{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}N^{[C}L^{E]a}V_{C}+2\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}(N^{[C}L^{B]a})\right)V_{C}+2N^{[C}L^{B]a}\nabla^{\top}_{a}V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2{\Gamma^{aB}}_{C}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V^{C}+\left(\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}\right)V_{C}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}V_{C}-2{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}N^{[C}L^{E]a}V_{C}+2\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}(N^{[C}L^{B]a})\right)V_{C}+2N^{[C}L^{B]a}{\Gamma_{aC}}^{E}V_{E}\,.

Writing
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\perp}+2NL

allowed us to use the Fialkow–Gauß equation (
3.7
) for the above simplifications.

We now break up this calculation into smaller parts.

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{a}\left(\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\Gamma^{ABC}_{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{ABC}_{\perp}+\Gamma^{ABC}_{\perp}\left(\bar{J}\bar{X}_{A}-\bar{d}\bar{Y}_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{ABC}_{\perp}-\bar{d}\Gamma^{-BC}_{\perp}.

Here, the second equality comes from the fact that
\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{Z}^{b}_{A}=-\bar{P}^{b}_{a}\bar{X}_{A}-\bar{g}^{b}_{a}\bar{Y}_{A}
and the last equality holds because
X_{A}\Gamma^{ABC}=0
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\top}_{a}N^{[C}L^{B]a}

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}N^{[C}\right)L^{B]a}+N^{[C}\left[\bar{\nabla}_{a}\left(L^{B]A}\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\right)+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B]}}_{E}L^{Ea}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla^{\top}_{a}N^{[C}\right)L^{B]a}+N^{[C}\left[\left(\bar{\nabla}_{a}L^{B]A}\right)\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}-\bar{d}L^{B]-}+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B]}}_{E}L^{Ea}\right]

In order to simplify the above two displays, we need results that follow from Equation (
2.2
):

\displaystyle\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\bar{\nabla}_{a}L^{AC}

\displaystyle=2L^{-C}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{Z}^{a}_{A}\bar{\nabla}_{a}F^{AC}

\displaystyle=3F^{-C}\,,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\top}_{a}N^{B}

\displaystyle=L_{a}^{B}.

Using the above identities, we can write

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\Gamma^{aBC}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bar{\nabla}_{a}X^{[C}\right)F^{B]a}+6X^{[C}F^{B]-}+\frac{1}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}\left(\nabla_{a}K\right)X^{[C}\bar{h}^{B]a}-\bar{d}\Gamma^{-BC}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=4X^{[C}F^{B]-}+\frac{1}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}\left(X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+2KX^{[C}\bar{h}^{B]-}\right)-\bar{d}\Gamma^{-BC}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=(2-\bar{d})\Gamma^{-BC}_{\perp}+\frac{1}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K

and

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{\top}_{a}N^{[C}L^{B]a}=2(2-d)N^{[C}L^{B]-}+N^{C}\Gamma^{ABE}L_{AE}-N^{B}\Gamma^{ACE}L_{AE}.

We can now use these formulas to write

\displaystyle\Delta^{\top}\bar{V}^{B}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2{\Gamma^{aBC}}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V_{C}+(2-\bar{d})\Gamma^{-BC}_{\perp}V_{C}+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}V_{C}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-2{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}N^{[C}L^{E]a}V_{C}+2N^{[C}L^{B]a}{\Gamma_{aC}}^{E}V_{E}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\left(2(2-d)N^{[C}L^{B]-}+N^{C}\Gamma^{ABE}L_{AE}-N^{B}\Gamma^{ACE}L_{AE}\right)V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}\bar{V}^{B}+2{\Gamma^{aBC}}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V_{C}+(2-\bar{d})\Gamma^{-BC}V_{C}+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}V_{C}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-2{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}N^{[C}L^{E]a}V_{C}+2N^{[C}L^{B]a}{\Gamma_{aC}}^{E}V_{E}+\left(N^{C}\Gamma^{ABE}L_{AE}-N^{B}\Gamma^{ACE}L_{AE}\right)V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2{\Gamma^{aBC}}\bar{\nabla}_{a}V_{C}+(2-\bar{d})\Gamma^{-BC}V_{C}+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+{{\Gamma_{a}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{aEC}V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2{\Gamma^{ABC}}\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}V_{C}-(\bar{d}+2w-2)\Gamma^{-BC}V_{C}+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+{{\Gamma_{A}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{AEC}V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2\Gamma^{A}{}_{A}{}^{C}\hat{\bar{D}}_{C}V^{B}-\frac{(\bar{d}+1)wK}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}V^{B}+2{\Gamma^{ABC}}\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}V_{C}-(\bar{d}+2w-2)\Gamma^{-BC}V_{C}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K+{{\Gamma_{A}}^{B}}_{E}\Gamma^{AEC}V_{C}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}V^{B}+2\Gamma^{A}{}^{\sharp}\circ\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}V^{B}-(\bar{d}+2w-2)\Gamma^{-BC}V_{C}+\frac{V_{C}}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}X^{[C}\hat{\bar{D}}^{B]}K

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\Gamma^{A}{}^{\sharp}\circ\Gamma_{A}{}^{\sharp}V^{B}-\frac{(\bar{d}+1)wK}{\bar{d}(\bar{d}-1)}V^{B}\,.

In the display above, the second equality comes the definition of
\Gamma_{\perp}
. The third equality is a result of the last four terms canceling, and the fourth equality comes from the fact that
\Gamma\in\ker X_{\lrcorner}
(where
X_{\lrcorner}
denotes contraction by
X
). The last inequality follows from
\Gamma_{A}{}^{A}{}_{E}\Gamma^{EBC}V_{C}=0
.

But,

\displaystyle\left(\hat{D}^{T}_{A}-\hat{\bar{D}}_{A}\right)V^{B}=\bar{Z}_{a}^{A}{{\Gamma_{A}}^{B}}_{C}V^{C}-\frac{X_{A}}{\bar{d}+2w-2}\left(\Delta^{\top}-\bar{\Delta}-\frac{wK}{2(\bar{d}-1)}\right)V^{B},

so the proof is completed by combining terms involving
wK

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Applying the same techniques in the proof above but accounting for the possible linear actions on multiple indices, the proof of the general Gauß–Thomas formula follows easily.

Recall that the Gauß equation is a corollary of the

3.5.
Holography

A useful way to treat hypersurfaces is in terms of defining functions: recall that
s
is a
defining function

for
\Sigma
if
s\in C^{\infty}{M}
,
\Sigma=\{P\in{M}\,|\,s(P)=0\}
, and
{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s|_{\Sigma}
is nowhere vanishing. We shall also assume that
s>0
on
M^{+}
.
A weight
w=1
conformal density given by
\sigma=[g;s]
, where
s
is a defining function and
g\in\bm{c}
, is called a
defining density
.
Defining functions can be used to analyze hypersurface invariants; for example, the second fundamental form can be written as
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}={\mathscr{P}}_{ab}\big|_{\Sigma}

where

{\mathscr{P}}_{ab}=\Big(\nabla_{a}-|{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s|_{g}^{-1}\,(\nabla_{a}s)\nabla_{|{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s|_{g}^{-1}\operatorname{grad}s}\Big)\,\frac{\nabla_{b}s}{{\hskip 0.85358pt}|{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s|_{g}}\,.

The quantity
{\mathscr{P}}_{ab}
is an example of a
preinvariant
, namely a smooth (and suitably polynomial) function of
generic metrics
g
and defining function
s
, whose restriction to

\Sigma
is independent of the choice of defining function
s
, and
therefore defines some hypersurface invariant, see
[
28
]
for
the detailed definition. Let us choose local coordinates
(s,y^{i})
in a
neighborhood of
\Sigma
where
i=1,\cdots,d-1

such that along
\Sigma
the vector fields
\partial/\partial y^{i}
are tangent to

\Sigma
. Then the
transverse order
of a preinvariant
{\mathscr{P}}

is defined by writing
{\mathscr{P}}
as a function of the coordinate components of
g
and their
derivatives, and computing the minimum, over all such coordinate representations of
{\mathscr{P}}
, of the highest order of
\partial/\partial s

derivatives of
g
upon restriction
to
\Sigma
.
The transverse order is an invariant of the corresponding hypersurface
invariant: for example, the second fundamental form has transverse
order one. We will also say that an operator
\operatorname{O}
has
transverse order
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
when there exists
v
in
the domain of
\operatorname{O}
such that
\operatorname{O}(s^{k}v)\big|_{\Sigma}\neq 0
, but
\operatorname{O}(s^{k+1}v^{\prime})\big|_{\Sigma}=0
, for all
v^{\prime}
in the domain of

\operatorname{O}
.

We can use this notion of preinvariants to show that Definitions
1.6
and
1.7
are well-posed, as established by the following result.

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose
2\leqslant n\leqslant d-1
. Then if the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
is such that at least one
\ell
th fundamental form vanishes for every
2\leqslant\ell<n
, then up to an overall non-zero coefficient, there is a unique
{\it n}
th fundamental form.

Proof.

We begin by considering the leading transverse order term in the preinvariant expression for an
n
th fundamental form.
Using coordinates
\{s,y^{i}\}
in a collar neighborhood
I\times\Sigma\subset M
, we can express any preinvariant in terms of a defining function
s
, partial derivatives
\partial_{s}
and
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial y^{i}
, the metric components
g_{ab}
, and the components of its inverse
g^{ab}
. Because fundamental forms are conformally invariant, it is useful to view part of the preinvariant alphabet—the metric
g
and the defining function
s
—as representatives of weighted densities: the metric is a weight
2
representative of the conformal class of metrics
\bm{c}
and the defining function is a representative of a defining density
\sigma=[g;s]
with weight
1
. We next determine the leading transverse order term in the preinvariant expression for an
n
th fundamental form using Definition
1.2
.

From the definition of transverse order, the leading derivative term in the preinvariant for the
n
th fundamental form must be of the form

{\rm O}_{(ab)}{}^{cd}\,\partial_{s}^{n-1}g_{cd}|_{\Sigma}\,,

where, as an operator,
{\rm O}_{(ab)}{}^{cd}
has transverse order
0
.
Moreover the above is annihilated by the normal and hypersurface trace.
Note that the weight of an
n
th fundamental form is
3-n
and its transverse order is
n-1
. By considering only conformal transformations by a constant we may still analyze expressions such as that displayed above in terms of weights. Because the weight of the operator
\partial_{s}
is
-1
, the weight of the above display is
3-n+w_{\rm O}
where the operator
{\rm O}_{(ab)}{}^{cd}
has weight
w_{\rm O}
. Hence we must have that
w_{\rm O}=0
. By an elementary weight argument, ones sees that this operator is algebraic and therefore made only from the metric, its inverse, and a preinvariant for the conormal. Together with
elementary
O(d)
and
O(d-1)
representation theory, this implies that (along
\Sigma
) this operator must be a non-zero multiple of the trace-free hypersurface projector, and hence
proportional to

\mathring{\top}_{\!\rm e}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\big(\partial_{s}^{n-1}g_{ab}\big)\big|_{\Sigma}\,,

where
\mathring{\top}_{\!\rm e}
is any preinvariant expression for the operator
\mathring{\top}
.

Now suppose that
L^{(n)}
and
L^{(n)}{}^{\prime}
are two
n
th fundamental forms with the same coefficient for the above-displayed term and the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
is such that an
\ell
th fundamental form vanishes for every
\ell<n
. We then seek to show that
L^{(n)}-L^{(n)}{}^{\prime}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}0
. Clearly, because
L^{(n)}
and
L^{(n)}{}^{\prime}
have the same leading term, their difference must have transverse order at most
n-2
. Put another way,

L^{(n)}_{ab}-L^{(n)}_{ab}{}^{\prime}={\rm P}_{(ab)}{}^{cd}\partial_{s}^{n-2}g_{cd}\big|_{\Sigma}+\text{ lower-order terms}\,,

where
{\rm P}_{(ab)}{}^{cd}
is a preinvariant operator with weight
-1
and transverse operator order
0
. But then
\partial_{s}^{n-2}g_{ab}|_{\Sigma}
can be rewritten as an
(n-1)
th fundamental form plus lower-order terms.
Thus, a proof via induction only requires that we check that the second fundamental form is unique up to an overall non-zero coefficient, which is again easily verified
by an elementary weight and representation theoretic argument.
∎

Given a hypersurface embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,{\mathcal{S}})
,
where

of

In many cases, we use this terminology also when

The possibility to define hypersurface invariants
in terms of preinvariants that do not depend on any particular choice of defining function can be leveraged by a clever choice of defining function; such a choice leads to holographic formulæ.
In fact, a key result
[
40
,
5
,
42

3

is that there is a unique metric

on the
d

and such that in

for some suitably smooth defining function

.
The problem of finding

such that the pair

[

]

An important insight is that, because

by the data

\sigma

\Sigma

There is a neat conformal

(3.12)

where

In general, we call a tractor obtained by acting with the hatted Thomas-

Now, note that

Away from

Given the data

A holographic formula for the tractor second fundamental form is given in

(4.2)

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{A}I_{B}|_{\Sigma}=L_{AB}+\tfrac{2}{d-2}I_{(A}X_{B)}K|_{\Sigma}+\tfrac{3d-8}{(d-2)(d-3)}X_{A}X_{B}M\,.

∎

4.2.
Canonical Transverse Differential Operators

One could construct higher fundamental forms by extending Lemma

To compute

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\theta=[g;t]\in\Gamma(\otimes^{r}T^{*}M[w])

(when

Proof.

First note that, using the notation provided in the lemma, the Levi-Civita connection acting on

Here

Further, denoting

n^{\Omega^{2}g}={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}(\Omega s)=\Omega(n^{g}+\sigma\Upsilon)

Therefore,
n^{\Omega^{2}g}\otimes t^{\Omega^{2}g}=\Omega^{w+1}(n^{g}+\sigma\Upsilon)\otimes t^{g}
and
(n^{\Omega^{2}g}\circledast\Omega^{2}g)^{\sharp}=\Omega([n^{g}+s\Upsilon]\circledast g)^{\sharp}

∎

Note that if

4.3.
Constructing Higher Fundamental Forms

One might think that
having constructed the canonical conformally-invariant operator
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}
with transverse order
1
, as well as a canonical extension of
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}
, that we could now directly compute higher fundamental forms by applying powers of
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}
to
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}
. However, as could be predicted by
[
25
]
, the

th power of

{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}
does not always produce an operator of transverse order
k
.
More precisely, the candidate
(k-2)
th
fundamental form

{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}^{k}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}\big|_{\Sigma}\,,

has transverse order strictly less than
k+1
when

. In this section, we first prove that fundamental forms with transverse order
n\geqslant\frac{d+1}{2}
can indeed not be constructed
by applying powers of
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}
to
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}
.
We will then explicitly construct canonical fundamental forms with transverse order
n<\frac{d+1}{2}
. Finally we construct canonical conditional fundamental forms with transverse order
n\geqslant\frac{d+1}{2}

and use these ingredients to prove Theorem
1.8
.

We begin by defining operators that
often
have the appropriate transverse order.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},\bm{c})
be a conformal embedding with asymptotic unit defining density
\sigma
.
Then, when
k=0
, define
\delta_{d,w}^{(0)}:=\mathring{\top}:\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}M[w])\rightarrow\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma[w])
.
For
k=1
and

, call

\delta_{d,w}^{(1)}:=\delta_{\rm R}:\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}M[w])\rightarrow\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma[w-1])\,,

while for

and

, let

\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}:=\delta_{\rm R}\circ{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}^{k-1}_{\sigma}:\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}M[w])\rightarrow\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma[w-k])\,.

Acting on sections of
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}M[w]
for
w\neq 3
, the transverse order of
\delta_{d,w}^{(1)}
is
1
. When

, the operator
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
is always defined.
Moreover, when
2w
is not an integer the operators
\delta^{(k)}_{d,w}
for
k\geqslant 2

have
transverse order
k

(see Equation (
4.5
) below). However, when
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 2}
and
2w\in\mathbb{Z}
, the operator
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
may not be defined or it could fail to have transverse order
k
. In particular, for
w
an integer and
k\geqslant 2
, the operators
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
are only defined in the three regions where
w
obeys

w<2-d
,
k-d<w<3
, or
k+2<w

(the second of these could be empty).
The following lemma characterizes the transverse order of
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
in these cases:

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix

and
k\geqslant 2
, and let

be such that

is strictly less than

if and only if

\frac{7-d}{2}\leqslant w<3\>\mbox{ and }\>\frac{d+2w-3}{2}\leqslant k\leqslant d+2w-5\,.

Proof.

To evaluate the transverse order of
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
, we compute the coefficient of
\nabla_{n}^{k}
. For that we first examine the leading derivative structure of the operator
{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}
. From Lemma
4.4
, acting on a weight

the only terms with non-zero transverse order are

,
n_{c}\nabla_{(a}t^{c}_{b)\circ}
,

, and

(here

and we work in the scale

). In a choice of coordinates
(s,y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1})
, we can write

\nabla_{a}=n_{a}\partial_{s}+\text{ltots}\,,

where
n={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s
and
ltots
denotes

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle n_{c}\nabla_{(a}t^{c}_{b)\circ}

\displaystyle=n_{c}n_{(a}\partial_{s}t_{b)\circ}^{c}+\text{ltots}\;\text{ and }\;s\nabla_{(a}\nabla\cdot t_{b)\circ}=sn_{(a}\partial_{s}\nabla\cdot t_{b)\circ}+\text{ltots}\,.

Because
\sigma
is an asymptotic unit defining density, we have that

\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}

From the above, and again consulting Lemma

, we conclude that

\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}=\mathring{\top}\circ\nabla_{n}\circ\prod_{i=0}^{k-2}\left[(d+2w-2i-6)\nabla_{n}-s\Delta\right]+\text{ltots}\,.

Because

, we have that

\delta_{d,w}^{(2)}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\mathring{\top}\circ(d+2w-7)\nabla_{n}^{2}+\text{ltots}\,.

We now proceed inductively to find the general coefficient of the leading transverse derivative term. Suppose that for

\delta_{d,w}^{(k-1)}=\mathring{\top}\circ\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k-2}(d+2w-k-i-3)\right]\nabla_{n}^{k-1}+\text{ltots}\,.

Then, because

\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}=\delta_{d,w-1}^{(k-1)}\circ{{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.85358ptD}}{\hskip 0.28453pt}}_{\sigma}\,,

has transverse order strictly less than

and

that obey

\displaystyle d+2w-2k-3\leqslant 0\leqslant d+2w-k-5\,.

Because

, we find that the right inequality implies that

. Further, the inequality above can be rewritten in terms of

to obtain
\frac{d+2w-3}{2}\leqslant k\leqslant d+2w-5
.
Equation
4.6
has no solutions for

in the range

.
Therefore, when

, the transverse order of
\delta_{d,w}^{(k)}
is
k
.
The left inequality of
4.6
rules out

so
the only remaining case is

,
which, in combination with Equation

, gives the ranges of

and

quoted in the Lemma.

∎

An asymptotic unit defining density
\sigma
only allows the construction of local invariants with transverse order at most

, so the highest fundamental form we could

th fundamental form.

of transverse order less than

Definition 4.8

Proof.

From Equation
4.3
, in a choice of scale

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}_{ab}=\nabla_{(a}n_{b)\circ}+s\mathring{P}_{ab}

\nabla_{n}^{k}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}_{ab}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\nabla_{n}^{k}\nabla_{(a}n_{b)\circ}+k\nabla_{n}^{k-1}\mathring{P}_{ab}

. There exists a scale

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}\nabla_{a}n_{b}

\displaystyle=R_{nabn}-(\nabla_{a}n^{c})(\nabla_{c}n_{b})

\displaystyle=W_{nabn}-P_{ab}+2n_{(a}P_{b)n}-g_{ab}P_{nn}+\text{ltots}\,.

Applying the above display to

, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}^{k}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}_{ab}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\nabla_{n}^{k-1}W_{nabn}+(k-1)\nabla_{n}^{k-1}\mathring{P}_{ab}+2n_{(a}\nabla_{n}^{k-1}P_{nb)\circ}+\text{ltots}\,.

To verify that the first two transverse order

terms in the above do not cancel in general,
suppose that

is conformally flat: For generic

the combination

Using Lemma
4.7

Further, by construction,

∎

Lemma 4.11

Let

k=\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil

\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},\bm{c})

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}=0

\nabla_{n}^{\ell-1}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}0
for all

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle(\bar{g}_{a}^{c}+n_{a}n^{c})(\bar{g}_{b}^{d}+n_{b}n^{d})\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{cd}^{\rm e}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\mathring{\top}\circ\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}+\frac{\bar{g}_{ab}}{d-1}\bar{g}^{cd}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{cd}^{\rm e}+2n_{(a}(n\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{b)}^{\rm e})^{\top}+n_{a}n_{b}\,n\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt(\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649ptn

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\mathring{\top}\circ\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}+2n_{(a}(n\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{b)}^{\rm e})^{\top}+(n_{a}n_{b}-\tfrac{\bar{g}_{ab}}{d-1}){\hskip 0.85358pt}n\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt(\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649ptn\,,

where the third line holds because

. By hyperumbilicity, we have that
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{\ell\!-\!2}}}}=\delta_{d,1}^{(\ell)}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e}=0
for

. Consulting Equation
4.5

for all

(the lower order terms in Equation

also vanish by hyperumbilicity). Thus, it only remains to show that
n^{a}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}0
.

By the inductive assumption and Leibniz rule, we also have that

n^{a}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}(n^{a}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e})\,.

\displaystyle n^{a}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{d-1}\nabla_{n}^{\ell}(s\nabla^{a}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\tfrac{\ell}{d-1}\nabla_{n}^{\ell-1}\nabla^{a}{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}^{\rm e}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Pi_{n}=&&-(-1)^{n+k-1}\log\tau&\Big\{f_{1}(d){\hskip 0.85358pt}y^{\ell}Q_{AB}\\
&&&-f_{2}(d){\hskip 0.85358pt}y^{\ell+1}(X_{(A}T_{B)})+f_{3}(d)y^{\ell+2}(X_{A}X_{B}U)\Big\}\\
&&+(-1)^{n+k-1}&\Big\{y^{\ell}\big[Q_{AB}f_{1}({\sf h})(\log\tau)\big]\\
&&&-y^{\ell+1}\big[X_{(A}T_{B)}f_{2}({\sf h})(\log\tau)\big]+y^{\ell+2}\big[X_{A}X_{B}Uf_{3}({\sf h})(\log\tau)\big]\Big\}\,.\end{aligned}

Note that

(4.12)

\displaystyle f_{2}(u)=F_{\ell,-k,2}(u)f_{3}(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{1}(u)=F_{\ell,-k,1}(u)F_{\ell,-k,2}(u)f_{3}(u)\,.

Now, for any polynomial
f
for which
d
is a root, the operator
f({\sf h})=f(d+2\underline{w})
obeys

f({\sf h})=2f^{\prime}(d){\hskip 0.85358pt}\underline{w}+\mathcal{O}(\underline{w}^{2})\,,

and so

f({\sf h})\log\tau=2f^{\prime}(d)\,.

Thus, if
f_{3}(d)=0
then
f_{1}(d)=0=f_{2}(d)
and moreover
f_{1}({\sf h})\log\tau
,
f_{2}({\sf h})\log\tau
, and
f_{3}({\sf h})\log\tau
are independent of
\tau
and hence
\Pi_{n}
would be independent of
\tau
.

To establish
\tau
independence, we analyze three cases. In the first two cases, we determine for which choices of
n
we have that
f_{3}(d)=0
, the result of which depends on dimension parity. In the third case, we handle all of the choices of
n
excluded from the analysis in the first two cases. Note that, because
d\geqslant 5
,
k:=\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil
, and the lemma is only concerned with
n
satisfying
k+1\leqslant n\leqslant d-1
, we have that
k\geqslant 3
so
n\geqslant 4
.
Thus, when
k=3
we must have
d=5
and
n=4
. We begin with the first, even dimension parity, case.

Case 1:
If
k\geqslant 4
and
d\geqslant 6
is even, then
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}
is independent of
\tau
when
k+1\leqslant n\leqslant d-1
:

To see this first observe that
the polynomial
f_{3}
satisfies
f_{3}(d)=0
when

d-2k-\ell+2\leqslant 0\leqslant d-k-\ell-2\,.

Using that
\ell:=n-k-1
, the above display implies that
d-k+3\leqslant n
. From the hypothesis, we only consider
n\leqslant d-1
, so we have that
k\geqslant 4
. Rewriting the inequality above as a condition on
n
, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle d-k+3\leqslant n\leqslant d-1\,.

Using that
d
is even we have
d=2k-2
, and so Equation
4.13
becomes

k+1\leqslant n\leqslant d-1\,,

as required.

Case 2:
If
k\geqslant 4
and
d\geqslant 7
is odd, then
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}
is independent of
\tau
when
k+2\leqslant n\leqslant d-1
:

Because
d
is odd,
d=2k-1
. Then, Equation
4.13
becomes

k+2\leqslant n\leqslant d-1\,,

as required.

So far we have dealt neither with the case
k=3
nor the case
n=k+1\geqslant 5
and
d
odd. These are both encompassed by studying
n=k+1\geqslant 4
and
d
odd.

Case 3:
If
n=k+1
and
d
is odd, then
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}
is independent of
\tau
:

Because
d
is odd, we have that
d=2k-1
. Because
n=k+1
, we have that
\ell=0
. Moreover (see Equation (
4.10
))

F_{0,-k,2}(d)=0\mbox{ and }F_{0,-k,2}(d+2\underline{w})=4\underline{w}\,.

Thus, from Equations
4.11
and
4.12
and remembering that
\underline{w}\log\tau=1
, we have that the only terms in
\Pi_{n}
that depend on
\tau
are the terms proportional to

. Because
y^{2}\propto I\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\hat{D}^{2}
, we can
employ Equation (
2.8
) and Proposition
2.4

to see that

y^{2}\circ X_{A}X_{B}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{E}}(X)+{\mathcal{E}}(I)+{\mathcal{E}}(h)\,.

But, because
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}=\bar{q}^{*}\circ\bar{r}\circ\mathring{\top}(\Pi_{n})
and

(\bar{q}^{*}\circ\bar{r}\circ\mathring{\top})(t_{(ab)}Z^{a}_{A}Z^{b}_{B}+\alpha X_{(A}V_{B)}+\beta I_{(A}V_{B)}^{\prime}+\gamma h_{AB}S)=(\bar{q}^{*}\circ\bar{r}\circ\mathring{\top})(t_{(ab)}Z^{a}_{A}Z^{b}_{B})\,,

for any non-vanishing

,

,

, and

for generic

as well as any coefficients

we have that
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}
is independent of

when

and

odd.

The above three cases prove that, for all

and

satisfying

, the

th canonical conditional fundamental form
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{n}}}}
is independent of
\tau
.
∎

We have already seen that a conformal embedding

is hyperumbilic when all its canonical fundamental forms vanish. The above proposition in fact establishes that

The above relationship between

.

.

The values

Lemma 4.27
.

Let

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{2}=\dot{P}_{AB}\dot{P}^{AB}\big|_{\Sigma}-\tfrac{3d-2}{(d-1)(d-2)^{2}}K^{2}\,.

It follows from Definition
4.25
that
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{2}=\Pi_{(3){\hskip 0.85358pt}AB}\Pi_{(3)}^{AB}

\Pi_{(3)}
in this proof is when it is squared, any instances where
\tilde{\Pi}_{(3)}
would be required can be replaced with
\Pi_{(3)}
. So, the proof amounts to relating
\dot{P}
to
\Pi_{(3)}
. As previously noted,
q({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})=P

\Pi_{(3)}^{AB}=\bar{r}\circ\mathring{\top}(\dot{P})

\Pi_{(3)}
to
\dot{P}|_{\Sigma}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{A}\dot{P}^{AB}=&\phantom{-}\>I\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\hat{D}X_{A}P^{AB}-I_{A}P^{AB}+\tfrac{2\sigma}{d-2}\hat{D}_{A}P^{AB}\\

=&-\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{D}_{B}I^{2}+\tfrac{X_{B}}{d-2}(\hat{D}I)^{2}\big)\\
=&-\frac{KX_{B}}{d-2}+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-2})\,,\end{split}

where the first and third lines are results of Proposition
2.4
, the second line results from the properties of

, and the last line uses the definition of

Further, observe that

(4.16)

\displaystyle I\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\dot{P}^{B}=\tfrac{1}{d-2}\big(\dot{K}X^{B}+KI^{B}\big)-\tfrac{2\sigma}{d-4}\big(\tfrac{1}{d-2}\hat{D}^{B}K-P^{AC}\hat{D}_{C}P_{A}^{B}\big)+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-3})\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\tfrac{1}{d-2}(\dot{K}X+KN)\,.

and
\mathring{\top}

\displaystyle\Pi_{(3){\hskip 0.85358pt}AB}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}\dot{P}_{AB}-\tfrac{d}{(d-1)(d-2)}X_{(A}\hat{\bar{D}}_{B)}K-\tfrac{2}{d-2}\dot{K}I_{(A}X_{B)}-\tfrac{1}{d-2}KI_{A}I_{B}-\tfrac{1}{(d-1)(d-2)}KI_{AB}\,.

Squaring this identity gives the
quoted result.
∎

Lemma 4.28
.

Let

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptV}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}(d-4)\Big(P_{AB}\ddot{P}^{AB}+\tfrac{4}{(d-2)^{2}}K^{2}\Big)\,.

Proof.

First, because
d>5
, we see that
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptV}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}
is a canonical fundamental form (so not conditional). As in the previous lemma, we note that
{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}\cdot{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptV}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}=\Pi_{(2)\,AB}\Pi_{(4)}^{AB}
. Moreover, Equation (

) implies that

so

\Pi_{(2)}=P|_{\Sigma}-\tfrac{2K}{d-2}N\odot X\,.

Thus we are tasked with computing

P_{AB}\Pi_{(4)}^{AB}
along
\Sigma
.
Remembering that
X\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt{\hskip 0.85358pt}P=0
, it is sufficient to compute
\Pi_{(4)}^{AB}
modulo terms proportional to
X
.
Using Definition
4.25
, we compute
\Pi_{(4)}
in steps. Recall, from Equation (
4.4
), that

\Pi_{(4)}=(d-4)\,(\bar{r}\circ\mathring{\top}\circ\delta_{R}\circ q\circ q^{*}\circ r\circ I\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\hat{D}\circ q)({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})\,.

We outline this calculation proceeding from right to left in this sequence of operators.

First, as shown previously,
q({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})=P
, so
I\hskip-1.5649pt\cdot\hskip-1.5649pt\hat{D}{\hskip 0.85358pt}q({\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}^{\rm e})=\dot{P}
. Therefore, using Lemma
2.11
, we next compute
r(\dot{P})

r(\dot{P})_{AB}=\dot{P}_{AB}-\tfrac{d+2}{4d(d-2)}X_{(A}\hat{D}_{B)}K-\tfrac{2}{d(d-2)}h_{AB}K+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-2})+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-4})X_{(A}T_{B)}\,,

for some tractor
T_{B}
. Here we used Equation (
4.15
)
and the oft-used operator identity for
\hat{D}\circ X
given in Equation (
2.8
).

Next, we need to compute
(q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})
. Before we continue, we consider the operators that come next: We are only interested in the
\bar{Z}_{A}\bar{Z}_{B}
component of the tractor
\Pi_{(4)}
, so we can ignore terms proportional to
X
in
(\mathring{\top}\circ\delta_{R}\circ q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})
when doing this computation. Further, we can ignore terms proportional to
I_{A}
when computing
(\delta_{R}\circ q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})
because these terms are projected out by
\mathring{\top}
. The various projections, therefore, amount to ignoring terms proportional to
X
or
I
when computing
(q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})
, because
\delta_{R}\circ I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}{\mathcal{E}}(X)+{\mathcal{E}}(I)
and
\delta_{R}\circ X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}{\mathcal{E}}(X)+{\mathcal{E}}(I)
.
From
Lemma
2.9
,

(q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})-r(\dot{P})={\mathcal{E}}(X)
, so

(q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})=\dot{P}-\tfrac{2}{d(d-2)}Kh+{\mathcal{E}}(X)+{\mathcal{E}}(I)+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-2})\,.

Next, note that
(\delta_{R}\circ q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})=\ddot{P}-\tfrac{2}{d(d-2)}\dot{K}h+{\mathcal{E}}(X)+{\mathcal{E}}(I)+{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{d-3})
, and we can apply the operator
\mathring{\top}
to obtain

(\mathring{\top}\circ\delta_{R}\circ q\circ q^{*}\circ r)(\dot{P})=\mathring{\top}(\ddot{P})+{\mathcal{E}}(X)\,.

Next, it is useful to note that
\mathring{\top}(\ddot{P}_{AB})\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}I_{A}^{A^{\prime}}I_{B}^{B^{\prime}}\ddot{P}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+I_{AB}U
for some
U\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[-3])
, and also that
I_{AB}P^{AB}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}0
. Thus, finishing the calculation amounts to computing

(4.17)

\displaystyle P^{AB}\bar{r}(I_{A}^{A^{\prime}}I_{B}^{B^{\prime}}\ddot{P}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+I_{AB}U)\,.

For this, we need the identity

X^{A}\ddot{P}_{AB}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}-\tfrac{2}{d-2}\big(\dot{K}X_{B}+KN_{B}\big)\,,

which is derived from Proposition
2.4
, Equation (
2.8
), and Equation (
4.16
). Because we are contracting on
P
, any terms proportional to
X
or the tractor first fundamental form produced by
\bar{r}
in Equation
4.17
can be discarded. Hence, again consulting Lemma
2.11
, we find that

\displaystyle\Pi_{(2){\hskip 0.85358pt}AB}\Pi_{(4)}^{AB}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle(d-4)P_{AB}I^{A}_{A^{\prime}}I^{B}_{B^{\prime}}\ddot{P}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle(d-4)\big(P_{AB}-\tfrac{2}{d-2}KN_{(A}X_{B)}\big)\ddot{P}^{AB}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle(d-4)\big(P_{AB}\ddot{P}^{AB}+\tfrac{4}{(d-2)^{2}}K^{2}\big)\,,

where the first equality is a result of the identity
I_{AB}P^{AB}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny$\Sigma$}}}{=}}0
, the second equality is an application of Equation (
4.15
)
to yield an
identity for
I\cdot{P}
,
and the last equality is a consequence of the display above expressing
X\cdot\ddot{P}
.
∎

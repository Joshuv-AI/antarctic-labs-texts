Additive complementary dual codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} Thanks: This research is supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (12071001).
Minjia Shi, Na Liu, Jon-Lark Kim, Patrick Solé

Abstract
A linear code is linear complementary dual (LCD) if it meets its dual trivially. LCD codes have been a hot topic recently due to Boolean masking application in the security of embarked electronics (Carlet and Guilley, 2014). Additive codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} are 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} -codes that are stable by codeword addition but not necessarily by scalar multiplication. An additive code over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} is additive complementary dual (ACD) if it meets its dual trivially. The aim of this research is to study such codes which meet their dual trivially. All the techniques and problems used to study LCD codes are potentially relevant to ACD codes. Interesting constructions of ACD codes from binary codes are given with respect to the trace Hermitian and trace Euclidean inner product. The former product is relevant to quantum codes.

Introduction

To begin with, let us recall some basic definitions.
A
linear
[n,k]
code
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
The set of vectors orthogonal to
\cal C
under the usual inner product is the
dual
of
\cal C
, denoted by
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
.
A linear code
\cal C
is
self-orthogonal
if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\subset\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
, and
self-dual
if
\mbox{$\cal C$}=\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
. On the other hand, a linear
code
\cal C
is an
LCD code
(linear complementary dual code) if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}=\{0\}
. In particular,
\cal C
is a
binary LCD code
if
\cal C
is a binary linear code satisfying
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

Massey
Massey
introduced the notion of LCD codes in order to provide an optimum linear coding
solution for the two-user binary adder channel.
In a later work
Massey92
, he also showed that there exist asymptotically good LCD codes. Furthermore, Sendrier showed that LCD codes meet
the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound in
Sen
.

In 2014, Carlet and Guilley
CarGui
introduced several constructions of LCD codes and investigated an application of LCD codes against
Side-Channel Attacks (SCA) and Fault Injection Attacks (FIA).
Recall from
CarGui
that SCA consists in passively recording some leakage that is the source of information to retrieve the key,
and that FIA consist in actively perturbating the device so as to obtain exploitable differences at the output.

In the approach of
CarGui
, the direct sum
\mbox{$\cal C$}\oplus\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\bot}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is essential, and the minimum distance of
\cal C
(resp.
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\bot}
) acts
as a performance criterion for SCA (resp. FIA). Since this model does not use the linearity of
\cal C
but only its additivity, it makes sense to study additive complementary dual (ACD)
codes over finite fields or finite rings. Moreover, since linear codes are additive codes, LCD codes are ACD. Furthermore, Guilley
Gui
reported to
us that the application of ACD codes to security still makes sense. This motivates the current study.

In the same spirit, Shi et al.
ShiLiKimSol
studied ACD codes over a noncommutative non-unital ring
E
with four elements recently. Nevertheless,
little is known about a general theory of ACD codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.
All the techniques and problems used to study LCD codes
D+
are potentially relevant to ACD codes.
Interesting constructions of ACD codes from binary codes are given with respect to two trace inner products: the trace Euclidean inner product, and the trace Hermition inner product, familiar since the studies of quantum codes

CRSS
. We have also constructed ACD
(6,2^{5},4)
,
(35,2^{7},26)
, and
(96,2^{7},72)
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
under the trace Euclidean inner product, all of which have more codewords than optimal LCD
[6,2,4]
,
[35,3,26]
, and
[96,3,72]
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, respectively.

Our paper consists of five sections. Section 2 recalls basic definitions and notations from additive codes under the two inner products. Section 3
discusses ACD codes with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product. Section 4 discusses ACD codes with with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product. Section 5
concludes the article.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\{0,1\}
and
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\{0,1,\omega,\bar{\omega}\}
denote the finite field of order 2 and 4 respectively, where
\bar{\omega}=\omega^{2}=\omega+1
,
\omega^{3}=1
.

An
additive code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
of length
n

is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
.
As
\cal C
is a free
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-module, it has
size
2^{k}
for some
0\leq k\leq 2n
. We call
\cal C
an
(n,2^{k})
code.
It has a basis, as a
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-module, consisting of
k
basis vectors. Interest in additive codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
has arisen because of their
correspondence to quantum codes as described in
CRSS
. There
is a natural inner product on the additive codes
arising from the trace map. The

trace
map
\mbox{Tr}:\mathbb{F}_{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
is given by

\mbox{Tr}(x)=x+x^{2}.

In particular
\mbox{Tr}(0)=\mbox{Tr}(1)=0
and
\mbox{Tr}(\omega)=\mbox{Tr}(\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$})=1
.
The
conjugate
of
x\in\mathbb{F}_{4}
, denoted
\overline{x}
, is the image
of
x
under the Frobenius automorphism; in other words,
\overline{0}=0
,

\overline{1}=1
, and
\overline{\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}}=\omega
.

Definition 1

A generator matrix of an
(n,2^{k})
additive code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is a
k\times n
matrix
G
with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{4}
such that
\mbox{$\cal C$}=\{{\bf u}G:\ {\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\}
. Note that
G
has 2-rank
k
.

As usual, the
weight

\wt({\bf c})
of
{\bf c}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}
is the number of
nonzero components of
{\bf c}
. The minimum weight
d
of
\cal C
is the smallest
weight of any nonzero codeword in
\cal C
. If
\cal C
is an
(n,2^{k})
additive code
of minimum weight
d
,
\cal C
is called an
(n,2^{k},d)
code.

Example 1

Let
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{6}
be the
[6,3,4]
hexacode whose generator matrix as a
linear
\mathbb{F}_{4}
-code is

{\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&0&0&1&\omega&\omega\\
0&1&0&\omega&1&\omega\\
0&0&1&\omega&\omega&1\end{array}\right].}

This is also an additive code; thinking of
\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$}_{6}
as an additive code,
it has generator matrix

{\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&0&0&1&\omega&\omega\\
0&1&0&\omega&1&\omega\\
0&0&1&\omega&\omega&1\\
\omega&0&0&\omega&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}\\
0&\omega&0&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}&\omega&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}\\
0&0&\omega&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}&\mbox{$\overline{\omega}$}&\omega\end{array}\right].}

For
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\textbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
in
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
, we define the Hermitian inner product,
the trace Hermitian inner product and the trace Euclidean inner product of
x
and
y
as follows:

(i)~\textbf{x}\cdot\textbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\bar{y_{i}},\ (ii)~\textbf{x}\star\textbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}(x_{i}\bar{y_{i}}),\ (iii)~\textbf{x}\diamond\textbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}(x_{i}y_{i}).

If
\cal C
is an
(n,2^{k})
additive code, the dual of
\cal C
with respect to the Hermitian inner product, the trace Hermitian inner product and the trace Euclidean inner product are defined as follows:

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{\rm H}}=\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}:u\cdot v=0\ for\ all\ v\in\mbox{$\cal C$}\},

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}=\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}:u\star v=0\ for\ all\ v\in\mbox{$\cal C$}\},

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}=\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}:u\diamond v=0\ for\ all\ v\in\mbox{$\cal C$}\}.

Obviously,
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{\rm H}}
,
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}
and
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}
are
(n,2^{2n-k})
additive codes. If, in addition,
\cal C
is linear, we say
\cal C
is
linear complementary dual with respect to the Hermitian inner product (or quaternary Hermitian LCD)
if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm H}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
. If
\cal C
is additive, we say
\cal C
is
additive complementary dual (ACD) with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product
if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
, and
\cal C
is
additive complementary dual (ACD) with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product
if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
.

3
ACD codes with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product
\star

We have a criteria for ACD codes in terms of a generator matrix as follows.

Theorem 3.1

(
(
Car
, Proposition 1)
,
(
Massey92
, Proposition 1)
)
Let
G
be a generator matrix for an
[n,k]
linear code
\cal C
over a field. Then
\cal C
is a Euclidean (resp. a Hermitian) LCD
code if and only if, the
k\times k
matrix
GG^{T}
(resp.
G\bar{G}^{T}
) is invertible.

Theorem 3.2

(
BooJit
)
Let
\cal C
be an additive
(n,2^{k})
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with generator matrix
G
.
Then
\cal C
is ACD with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product if and only if
G\star G=G\bar{G}^{T}+\bar{G}G^{T}
is invertible.

The following lemma is straightforward from the definition of the inner products.

Lemma 1

(
(
DouKimLee
, Lemma 2.1)
)
Assume
\cal C
is a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Then

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}
is equal to
the dual of
\cal C
with respect to the Hermitian inner product. Similarly,

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}
is equal to the dual of
\cal C
with respect to the Euclidean inner product.

Corollary 1

Any Hermitian LCD
[n,k,d]
code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is an ACD
(n,2^{2k},d)
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product.

Proof

Suppose that
\cal C
is a Hermitian LCD code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Then
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm H}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
. By Lemma
1
,

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm H}}}=\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}
.
Thus,
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
, which means that
\cal C
is ACD with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product.
The parameters of
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are obvious.

Corollary 2

Suppose
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}
is a binary LCD
[n,k,d]
code with generator
matrix
G_{2}
. Let
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}^{4}
be a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with generator matrix
G_{2}
. Then by regarding
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}^{4}
as an additive code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
,
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}^{4}
is an ACD
(n,2^{2k},d)
code with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product.

Proof

Let
G_{2}
be a generator matrix for
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}
. Then, by Theorem
3.1
,
G_{2}G_{2}^{T}
is invertible over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Since
G_{2}=\bar{G_{2}}
,

G_{2}\bar{G_{2}}^{T}
is invertible over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
,
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}^{4}
as a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is an Hermitian LCD
[n,k,d]
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Therefore, by Corollary
1
,
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{2}^{4}
is an ACD
(n,2^{2k},d)
code with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product.

Lemma 2

(
HPR
)
If
A
is a symmetric integral matrix with zero diagonal, then 2-rank
(A)
is even.

Proof

We give a detailed proof here since the proof in
HPR
is concise.
Recall that a principal submatrix of a square matrix
A
is the matrix obtained by deleting any
m
rows and the corresponding
m
columns.

Let
A^{\prime}
be a non-singular principal submatrix of
A
such that 2-rank
(A)
= 2-rank
(A^{\prime})
. Then
A^{\prime}
is also symmetric integral with zero diagonal. We may assume that
A^{\prime}\equiv B\pmod{2}
for some skew symmetric integral matrix
B
.
Over
\mathbb{Z}
, any skew symmetric matrix
B
of odd order has determinant
0
(since
B=-B^{T}
implies that
\det(B)=-\det(B^{T})=-\det(B)
, hence
\det(B)=0
).
So, if 2-rank
(A^{\prime})
is odd, then
\det(A^{\prime})\equiv\det(B)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. This is a contradiction since
A^{\prime}
has a full rank, that is,
\det(A^{\prime})\not\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. Thus 2-rank
(A^{\prime})
is even. Therefore, 2-rank
(A)
is even.

Theorem 3.3

If
\cal C
is a trace Hermitian ACD
(n,2^{k})
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with generator matrix
G
, then
k
is even.

Proof

Let
A=G\star G=G\bar{G}^{T}+\bar{G}G^{T}
. Then
A
is a
k\times k
symmetric integral matrix with zero diagonal. Since
\cal C
is ACD, the 2-rank of

must be
k
by Theorem
3.2
. By Lemma
2
,
k
is even.

Lemma 3

Let
\cal C
and
\mathcal{D}
are two binary
[n,k_{1}]
and
[n,k_{2}]
linear codes. If
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}=a\mbox{$\cal C$}+b\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
, where
a\neq b
and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{4}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}}=a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
.

Proof

Let
\textbf{u}\in a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
; then there exist
\textbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}
and
\textbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
such that
\textbf{u}=a\textbf{d}^{\prime}+b\textbf{c}^{\prime}
. For
\textbf{v}=a\textbf{c}+b\textbf{d}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}
, where
\textbf{c}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}
and
\textbf{d}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{u}\star\textbf{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\textbf{d}^{\prime}+b\textbf{c}^{\prime})\star(a\textbf{c}+b\textbf{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\textbf{d}^{\prime}+b\textbf{c}^{\prime})\cdot(\bar{a}\textbf{c}+\bar{b}\textbf{d})+(\bar{a}\textbf{d}^{\prime}+\bar{b}\textbf{c}^{\prime})\cdot(a\textbf{c}+b\textbf{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\bar{a}\textbf{d}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{c}+a\bar{b}\textbf{d}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{d}+b\bar{a}\textbf{c}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{c}+b\bar{b}\textbf{c}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{d}+\bar{a}a\textbf{d}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{c}+\bar{a}b\textbf{d}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{d}+\bar{b}a\textbf{c}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{c}+\bar{b}b\textbf{c}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\bar{b}+\bar{a}b)\textbf{d}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{d}+(b\bar{a}+\bar{b}a)\textbf{c}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}\subseteq\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}}
.

Since
a\neq b
,
|\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}|=|\mbox{$\cal C$}||\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}|=2^{k_{1}}\cdot 2^{k_{2}}=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
, then
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}}=\frac{2^{2n}}{|\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}|}=2^{2n-(k_{1}+k_{2})}=2^{2n-k_{1}-k_{2}}
. And
|a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}|=2^{n-k_{1}}\cdot 2^{n-k_{2}}=2^{2n-k_{1}-k_{2}}
. Therefore,
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}}=a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
.

Proposition 1

If
\cal C
is a self-dual
[2n,n]
binary code, let
\mathcal{D}
be a binary linear code and
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}=\mbox{$\cal C$}\oplus\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
, then
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}=a\mbox{$\cal C$}+b\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
is an ACD
(2n,2^{2n})
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product, where
a\neq b
and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{4}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof

Since
\cal C
is self-dual,
\mbox{$\cal C$}=\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
. By
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}=\mbox{$\cal C$}\oplus\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
, we know that

\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}=\{\textbf{0}\},\ \mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}=(\mbox{$\cal C$}\oplus\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$})^{\perp}=(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n})^{\perp}=\{\textbf{0}\},

then
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}=\{\textbf{0}\}
.
By Lemma
3
,
\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}=a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}=a\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}+b\mbox{$\cal C$}
.

If there exists
{\textbf{0}}\neq{\textbf{v}}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}^{\perp_{{\rm TrH}}}
,
then there are
{\textbf{c}}\in\mbox{$\cal C$},{\textbf{d}}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
, and
{\textbf{c}},{\textbf{d}}
are nonzero such that
{\textbf{v}}=a{\textbf{c}}+b{\textbf{d}}
.
Similarly, there are
{\textbf{c}}^{\prime}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}
,
{\textbf{d}}^{\prime}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}
,
and
{\textbf{c}}^{\prime},{\textbf{d}}^{\prime}
are nonzero such that
{\textbf{v}}=a{\textbf{c}}^{\prime}+b{\textbf{d}}^{\prime}
.
Hence we have
a{\textbf{c}}+b{\textbf{d}}=a{\textbf{c}}^{\prime}+b{\textbf{d}}^{\prime}
, which implies that
{\textbf{c}}={\textbf{c}}^{\prime},{\textbf{d}}={\textbf{d}}^{\prime}
. Then we have
{\textbf{c}}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}^{\perp}
,
{\textbf{d}}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}
, where
{\textbf{c}},{\textbf{d}}
are nonzero, which is a contradiction. Therefore,

Corollary 3

Suppose that
\cal C
is a self-dual
[2n,n]
binary code and
\mathcal{D}
is a binary linear code such that
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}=\mbox{$\cal C$}\oplus\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
.

\mbox{$\cal C$}_{4}=a\mbox{$\cal C$}+b\mbox{$\mathcal{D}$}
. Then its

ACD codes with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product
\diamond

In this section, we construct ACD codes with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product.

Lemma 4

Any Euclidean LCD
[n,k,d]
code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is an ACD
(n,2^{2k},d)
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product.

Proof

Suppose that
\cal C
is an Euclidean LCD code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Then
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm E}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
, where
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm E}}}
denotes the dual of
\cal C
under the Euclidean inner product. By Lemma
1
,

\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm E}}}=\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}
.
Thus,
\mbox{$\cal C$}\cap\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp_{{\rm TrE}}}=\{{\bf 0}\}
, which means that
\cal C
is ACD with respect to the trace Euclidean inner product.
The parameters of
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are obvious.

We want a characterization of an ACD code with respect to the Euclidean inner product in terms of its generator matrix. We follow the idea from
BooJit
.

Definition 2

(
BooJit
)
Let
V
be an inner product space over a field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map

is called an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-orthogonal projection with respect to the prescribed inner product

if

(i)
T^{2}=T
, and

(ii)
<\textbf{u},\textbf{v}>=0
for all
\textbf{u}\in{\rm Im}(T)
and
\textbf{v}\in{\rm Ker}(T)
.

Lemma 5

Using the notation of Definition 2, if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is nondegenerate, then
{\rm Ker}(T)
is the dual of
{\rm Im}(T)
under
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Proof

From basic linear algebra,
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\rm Ker}(T))={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)-{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\rm Im}(T))
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the dual of
{\rm Im}(T)
under
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

Lemma 6

\cal C
be a linear code of length

over

\mathbb{F}_{4}-

Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied ACD codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with respect to the trace Hermitian inner product and the trace Euclidean inner product. Interesting constructions of ACD codes from binary codes are given with respect to the both
inner products. As a good motivation of ACD codes, we have also constructed several ACD
(n,2^{2k+1},d)
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
under the trace Euclidean inner product which are better than optimal Euclidean LCD
[n,k,d]
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

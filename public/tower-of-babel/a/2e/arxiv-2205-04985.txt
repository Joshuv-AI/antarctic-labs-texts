Relativistic Effects on Triple Black Holes II: The Influence of Spin in Burrau’s Problem 2022 Relativistic Effects on Triple Black Holes II: The Influence of Spin in Burrau’s Problem – References
A. S. Chitan, A. Mylläri, M. J. Valtonen

Abstract
We continue our study of triple systems of black holes in the context of Burrau’s problem by including the effect of spin. For a more general study we also include a study of the phase space in Agekian-Anosova region. This was done via numerical integration of orbits with ARCcode which uses relativistic corrections (post-Newtonian) up to the 2.5 th order. For the Agekian-Anosova region we study black holes with mass unit 10 6 M ⊙ and allow one of the black holes to be maximally rotating. The same configurations were tested without the inclusion of spin and we found that simulations that varied greatly from the spin to no spin cases had much closer approaches between one of the Schwarzschild black holes and the Kerr black hole. In the context of Burrau’s problem, Pythagorean triangles with different linear scales were selected where the largest black hole in these systems were given spin vectors in normalised units where maximum is close to unity, ranging from 0 to about 0.95. Mass unit was also varied between 10 0 M ⊙ - 10 12 M ⊙ . It was found that while there was no distinctive effect on the number of two-body encounters nor the fraction of mergers, the lifetimes of the systems may have been affected - particularly in the intermediary mass ranges (10 4 M ⊙ -10 7 M ⊙ ) in comparison to the zero spin problem. Systems also ended up moving from the two-dimensional to three-dimensional where we see increased motion in the z-axis with increasing spin magnitude for the large mass systems.

Introduction

The detection of black hole mergers is becoming more and more commonplace in modern astrophysics due to the advanced LIGO and VIRGO systems
(
Abbott et al. 2019
;
Abbott et al. 2021
)
. The mergers of stellar mass black holes (SBH) have dominated earth-based detections, but it is expected that with space-based detectors, like LISA, finding mergers of intermediate mass black holes (IMBH) as well as supermassive black holes (SMBH) should be possible
(
Rhook &
Wyithe 2005
)
. One theory of origin of these mergers are via isolated binaries
(
McMillan &
Portegies Zwart 2001
;
Belczynski et al. 2002
)
. A triple origin, however, is also a viable theory as two of the black holes can be driven to merge due to the gravitational influence of the third
(
Rodriguez &
Antonini 2018
)
. Triple SBH systems may form from isolated triple star systems
(
Vigna-Gómez et al. 2021
)
while triple IMBH systems may form within globular clusters
(
Sigurdsson &
Hernquist 1993
;
Vitral, Eduardo & Mamon, Gary A. 2021
)
and the hierarchical merging of galaxies can lead to the formation of triple SMBH’s
(
Valtonen 1996
;
Hoffman &
Loeb 2007
;
Kollatschny et al. 2020
;
Yadav, Jyoti et al. 2021
)
as most massive galaxies host a SMBH at their core
(
Kormendy &
Richstone 1995
)
. With such rapid advancements in the field of gravitational wave physics, the study of the dynamics of triple black hole systems is necessitated.

Triple systems, and the wider three-body problem, however, is complex and chaotic. In the paper preceding this one
(
Chitan
et al. 2021
)
(hereby referred to as Paper I), the authors have discussed the history of the three-body problem and recent strides made to develop a solution, both analytically
(
Heggie &
Rasio 1996
;
Stone &
Leigh 2019
;
Hamers &
Samsing 2019
;
Ginat &
Perets 2021
)
and numerically
(
Szebehely &
Peters 1967
;
Monaghan 1976
;
Hut &
Bahcall 1983
;
Hut 1983
;
Hut 1993
;
Heggie &
Hut 1993
;
Goodman &
Hut 1993
;
McMillan &
Hut 1996
;
Heggie
et al. 1996
)
.

Of interest has been the problem proposed by
Burrau 1913
, known as Burrau’s problem of three bodies. This problem, though popularized by Burrau, was initially brought to his attention by Ernst Meissel
(
Peetre 1995
;
Valtonen et al. 2016a
)
. In this problem he describes three bodies placed at the vertices of a 3,4,5 Pythagorean triangle with masses reflecting the side of the triangle opposite to each body. Meissel believed the problem to have a periodic solution while Burrau was unable to find, more rigorously, any proof of this. The evolution of such a system was later studied extensively by both
Szebehely &
Peters 1967
and
Valtonen
et al. 1995
. In the work of the former, they used computers to integrate the orbital motion and found that eventually such a triple system would end in a bound binary and an escaping body. Valtonen et al. studied, from a relativistic approach, the influence of mass variation from 10
5
M
⊙
to 10
9
M
⊙
in five different cases. They found that for smaller mass systems, binary formation and escape was how the system would typically end but for larger masses, mergers would take place instead.

In Paper I, we reanalysed this work and extended the mass ranges from from 10
0
M
⊙
to 10
12
M
⊙
for initial configurations of all Pythagorean triangles with hypotenuse
<
100pc. It was found that as the mass unit increased, the fraction of mergers also increased and the lifetimes of the triple systems decayed exponentially. Distinctions were also made between triangles that were close to a hierarchical configuration and those that were not, using the definitions proposed by
Anosova et al. 1990
. In the more hierarchical type triangles (HT) in the study, merging dominated even from low mass black holes (10
0
M
⊙
- 10
3
M
⊙
) and their evolution was more predictable than that of the more non-hierarchical triangles (NHT). The more NHT, like the 3,4,5, exhibited a more chaotic nature. We also saw that there was a transition from escape dominated dynamics to merger dominated dynamics in such triple systems as was shown by
Valtonen
et al. 1995
. Recently,
Boekholt et al. 2021
also studied the same 3,4,5 Pythagorean configuration using the code BRUTUS with 2.5
th
order post-Newtonian (pN) terms and 1pN cross terms. They also replicated the transition from escapes to mergers in such triple systems.

In our previous study of Paper I, all black holes in the triple systems studied were kept as non-rotating. However, it has been mathematically shown by
Kerr 1963
that black holes do rotate. Following the no-hair theorem, a fully isolated black hole can be described by only two parameters - its mass, M, and its spin, J
(
Israel 1967
;
Israel 1968
;
Hawking 1972
)
.

The next step then was to investigate how the effect of a rotating black hole would affect the outcome of triple systems in the framework of Paper I which forms the basis of this current paper. As gravitational wave physics and the discovery and study of black holes becomes more prominent in modern astrophysics, it is important to consider what the effect of spin will have on black hole dynamics. These idealized, Schwarzschild black holes are much ‘easier’ to evaluate than their much more realistic Kerr counterparts. However, almost all astrophysical bodies do rotate and the relativistic effects associated may have an impact on the evolution of n-body systems. One of the major effects of having a rotating black hole in the system is relativistic frame-dragging. The spacetime around the rotating black hole would be dragged and therefore affect other orbiting bodies (in this case the other two black holes of our triple system). From this comes the Lense-Thirring effect (LTE) whereby the argument of periapsis as well as the longitude of the ascending node precesses due to the frame-dragging effect of a rotating body in the system
(
Lense &
Thirring 1918
;
Ciufolini &
Pavlis 2004
)
.

Fang et al. in a series of papers looked at hierarchical triple systems with a rotating SMBH and inner binary comprised of SBH’s
(
Fang & Huang 2019
;
Fang
et al. 2019
;
Fang & Huang 2020
)
. The typical von Zeipel-Kozai-Lidov (ZKL) oscillations imposed in such systems are with respect to non-rotating bodies. Fang et al. compared the evolution of triple systems in the hierarchical formation with and without SMBH spin in order to compare the effect of the ZKL oscillations to other effects that may be introduced due to the spin of the SMBH. They found that there were significant differences in the evolution of the inner binary black hole system when spin was included - depending on the initial orbital angles, the merger time of the binary could be lengthened or shortened if spin was present
(
Fang
et al. 2019
)
. They also conducted numerical simulations to show that the effect of spin could be detectable by LISA. This could potentially lead to new ways of observing the spin effect in triple systems as well as the spin parameters of SMBH’s
(
Fang & Huang 2020
)
.

Kidder 1995
discuss in depth the effect of spin on a coalescing binary in the pN regime. They show that one of the major contributions of these spin terms to the orbital dynamics is that they are not confined to the orbital plane. They then result in the precession of the orbital plane if the spin vector is not aligned perpendicular to the orbital plane. They also demonstrate the spin contribution to energy loss as well as the angular momentum loss in gravitational radiation, where they show that the spin-orbit contribution can be significant. The role of spin, then, in the evolution of such systems can be expected to have a significant effect. This provides the motivation to test this effect via numerical simulations on not binaries but on the more complex but also astrophysically common, triple system. In this paper, we extend the work of Paper I by allowing the most massive black hole in the triple system to rotate and conducting numerical simulations using ARCcode. The spin vector as well as the mass unit of the black holes are varied. This is presented in three sections - in
Section

2
we will focus on the method and dataset. In
Section

3
, the results for the effect of increasing spin vector and increasing mass unit will be presented and discussed.

2.1
Code Implementation

ARCcode conducts orbital numerical integration of the system of triples based on initial conditions placed by the user. For this work we conduct simulations with post-Newtonian corrections up to the 2.5th order.

This code performs orbital integration using a logarithmic Hamiltonian leapfrog method. The transformed equations of motion for both the time and co-ordinates are obtained from the logarithmic Hamiltonian (a full derivation is presented in
Mikkola &
Tanikawa 2013b

2.1.1
Constraints

ARCcode does not use cross terms which over relativistic timescales become necessary for describing the evolution of systems
(
Will 2014b
;
Will 2014a
)
. However, for this study our aim is to see via the use of ARCcode, if there is any measurable effect of the inclusion of spin. Here, we focus on studying the influence of spin on the final outcomes of the triples statistically. All simulations use the same initial coding and the only changing parameter is the spin.

We also limit the case to where only one of the black holes in our triple system has spin. This is because ARCcode allows only one of the black holes to rotate within a given simulation, it is not representative of the actual astrophysical system where all three black holes would (most likely) be rotating. However, typically in numerical integration, if one black hole is much bigger then the spin of the smaller black hole is considered negligible
(
Mikkola 2020b
)
. In our simulations, though the masses are on similar scales we always let the largest black hole spin.

We are interested in the configurations following the ideas of Burrau. However, this may be a bit limited so, in order to make the problem more general and widen the variety of systems studied, we have conducted simulations of triple black hole systems within the Agekian-Anosova homology region which describes phase space for zero velocity, equal mass three body systems
(
Anosova &
Orlov 1984
;
Anosova &
Nebukin 1991
;
Anosova
et al. 1994
)
. In this space, two bodies are fixed at co-ordinates (0.5, 0) and (-0.5, 0) while the third body is placed in the region within the unit circle centered at (-0.5, 0) and bounded by the x and y axes. Simulations can then be carried out, varying the positioning of the third body. Since this space is for the equal mass, free-fall system and one of our black holes is rotating, we also conduct simulations in the case where the rotating black hole is initially fixed at (0.5, 0) and then at (-0.5, 0) to cover all possibilities. Of course, this region is still representative of planar cases only. The results of this are presented and discussed in
Section

3
.

2.2
Initial Conditions

All black holes begin with zero initial velocity and only one of them is allowed to rotate - this is the biggest black hole in each triple system. The magnitude of the spin vector is kept to
<
1 in normalised units as a spin magnitude
=
1 results in zero surface gravity of the black hole and the theoretical naked singularity which cannot exist
(
Bardeen
et al. 1973
;
Wald 1999
)
. All bodies start with co-ordinates limited to the xy-plane. Timescales were kept as in Paper I, with the crossing times of systems being utilized for comparisons of lifetimes. The distance unit was kept as 1 parsec throughout simulations with Pythagorean configurations.

2.3
Parameters

Main parameters under consideration are the lifetimes of the systems (how long they take to move from being a three-body system to a two-body one - whether via merger or escape), the motion in the z-axis as well as the variation of the argument of periapsis,
\omega
, and the longitude of ascending node,
\Omega
, of the non-rotating black holes with respect to the rotating black hole. Parameters from paper I will also be considered such as the fraction of mergers and the number of two-body encounters, which defines when there is a close approach of two of the three bodies and is used as an indicator of how interactive a system is
(
Chitan
et al. 2021
)
.

4
Astrophysical Implications

The number of two-body encounters as well as the fraction of mergers do not appear to have been influenced significantly from the added effect of spin. Instead, the motion of the entire dynamical system becomes much more complicated as it moves from the 2-D case to the 3-D one and the orbital elements (
\omega
and
\Omega
), show oscillatory motion in certain configurations more than others. This is of course, constrained to the distance unit of 1 pc, which in larger mass cases, becomes more theoretical.

The lifetimes of the systems show some effect of added spin on the systems. At the intermediary mass range of
\approx
10
4
M
⊙
- 10
7
M
⊙
, there is a potential increase in lifetimes due to the rotation of M
1
.
Valtonen &
Karttunen 2006
showed that when initial velocities, were kept to zero, the motion was restricted to the plane. They derived statistical distributions from numerical orbital calculations of the binary energy within a triple system for both the three-dimensional case and the planar case. They found that for each case the distributions were different statistically which could then affect, close encounters and lifetimes. Here, from our results (Figures
12
), this is corroborated as the inclusion of spin of one of the black holes results in three dimensional motion. This of course, makes the systems more complex and their behaviour even more chaotic and unpredictable.

In the real astrophysical context, the effect of spin in binary systems have been studied extensively.
Stella &
Possenti 2009
looked at compact object binary systems and the astrophysical environments around these compact systems. The surrounding accretion disks of black holes and neutron stars should be affected by the strong gravity and the LTE of these rotating bodies. These effects should be large and noticeable when looking at X-ray flares from the hot accretion disks. The LTE then could potentially be used to determine certain characteristics about host compact objects.
Valtonen
et al. 2010
have also studied in great detail the OJ287 system. They concluded that the spin of the primary SMBH of the OJ287 system must influence the orbit of the secondary black hole around it. Using this, they were able to estimate what the spin of the primary SMBH should be. They later observed the flares from this system and were able to conclude that the spin is 0.313
(
Valtonen
et al. 2016b
)
.

It is with no question that these binary systems are far less complicated than a triple system. Tracing the orbits and predicting the spin based on frame-dragging effects are difficult but feasible for binaries. For the triple system, these effects, especially on the more NHT’s appear to be greatly chaotic and unpredictable. The overall effect of the rotating black hole appears negligible when considering the final states of these systems i.e. mergers and two-body encounters. But, utilizing the orbits of these bodies and the potentially observable LTE on the other two bodies of these types of systems to glean information about the spin magnitude of a black hole may be quite difficult. The system may not be constrained to the orbital plane and orbital elements oscillate largely, all of which contribute to unpredictable outcomes.

For triplets of SMBH, which typically form hierarchically via galactic mergers
(
Valtonen 1996
)
, this type of orbital element oscillation may not appear, which can limit unpredictability. This coincides with simulations as HT’s merge much more quickly, at smaller mass units, than their NHT counterparts and therefore behave less chaotically. SBH triplets in the hierarchical formation however may experience this type of motion. Black holes of masses 10
0
M
⊙
-10
3
M
⊙
, may be highly theoretical but recently, the smallest black hole, of mass
\approx
10
3
M
⊙
has recently been discovered
(
Thompson
et al. 2019
)
.
Raidal et al. 2019
also recently studied primordial black holes in the mass range of 0.1 M
⊙
-10
3
M
⊙
and their merger rates for potential LIGO detections. In some cases they found that a single primordial black hole nearby the binary would be the main disruption of a merger event. Here we find that for the most part, small mass triple systems end in escape over merger with a remnant binary pair.

Again, differences in evolution of the system due to mass is observed. The effect of spin is strongest when looking at mass units of the intermediary ranges because it is at these mass units that lifetimes are longer than the large mass systems and black holes are large enough for their to be substantive triple interaction. They interact more strongly for long time periods such that the effect of added spin is more evident. Of course, this is also restricted to the distance unit, fixed at 1 pc. The influence of mass unit and distance unit have already been discussed in more detail in Paper I.

Conclusions

In order to fully understand the dynamics of black hole systems, the effect of spin must be considered. Though our study in the context of Burrau’s problem is not the most general representative of all possible three-body configurations, we expand this with the inclusion of the Agekian-Anosova region. Unsurprisingly, we see that there the evolution of triple systems can be influenced by spin as one of the bodies makes closer and closer approaches to the rotating black hole. We see this in the effect on the lifetimes of the systems as well as the number of two-body encounters. We also find that about 8% of simulations change how they end their lifetimes (from merger to escape or vice versa) with this inclusion of spin. We have also included all Pythagorean triangles with hypotenuse
<
100pc as an ensemble of triples in the context of Burrau’s problem. We have found that while there is no direct effect of spin in the outcome of these systems (i.e. fraction of mergers, number of two body encounters), the lifetimes of these systems may have been affected. We have also found that certain effects are more visible in the NHT’s than HT’s like the motion of the orbital elements
\omega
and
\Omega
. The inclusion of spin which is not perpendicular to the orbital plane leads to 3D motion.

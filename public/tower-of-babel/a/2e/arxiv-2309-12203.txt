Opers with real monodromy and Eichler-Shimura isomorphism
Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. The purpose of the present paper is to investigate G G -opers on pointed Riemann surfaces (for a simple algebraic group G G of adjoint type) and their monodromy maps. In the first part, we review some general facts on G G -opers, or more generally, principal G G -bundles with holomorphic connection having simple poles along marked points, including the correspondence with G G -representations of the fundamental group. One of the main results, proved in the second part, asserts that the space of certain G G -opers with real monodromy forms a discrete set. This fact generalizes the discreteness theorem for real projective structures, already proved by G. Faltings. As an application, we establish the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism for each PSL 2 \mathrm{PSL}_{2} -oper with real monodromy. The resulting decomposition of the (parabolic) de Rham cohomology group of its symmetric product defines a polarized real Hodge structure.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Theme of the present paper

The purpose of the present paper is to study the monodromy of flat connections on principal
G
-bundles for a linear algebraic group
G
over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

In order to state the framework and our results, let us take
a collection
\mathscr{X}:=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})
consisting of a compact Riemann surface
X
of genus
g\geq 0
and a (possibly empty) set of mutually distinct points
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{r}
in
X
with
2g-2+r>0
.
The fundamental group
\Gamma
of
X^{*}:=X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\{\sigma_{i}\}
at a fixed base point
x_{0}
is isomorphic to the group finitely generated by elements
A_{1},\cdots,A_{g}
,
B_{1},\cdots,B_{g}
, and
T_{1},\cdots,T_{r}
(where each
T_{i}
is represented by a loop around
\sigma_{i}
) with relation
\prod_{j=1}^{g}[A_{j},B_{j}]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}T_{i}=e
.
This explicit description helps us to investigate
the category
\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
of representations of
\Gamma
valued in the group
G(\mathbb{C})
of
\mathbb{C}
-rational points of
G
(cf. (
29
)).

The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence (in the most basic form) can be generalized to
the relationship between
objects in
\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))

and
flat
G
-bundles, i.e., principal
G
-bundles equipped with
a flat holomorphic connection.
In fact, each flat
G
-bundle on
X^{*}
has a flat trivialization on some local neighborhood of every point, which we call a
fundamental framing
(cf. Definition
2.2
).
If
\gamma
is a path beginning and ending at
x_{0}
, then there is an element of
G(\mathbb{C})
which expresses the transformation effected on a fixed fundamental framing at
x_{0}
, by the process of analytic continuation around
\gamma
.
In this way, each flat
G
-bundle associates a
G(\mathbb{C})
-representation of
\Gamma
, which is well-defined up to post-composition with an inner automorphism of
G(\mathbb{C})
and uniquely determined after fixing a trivialization of the fiber of the underlying principal
G
-bundle over
x_{0}
.
The resulting functor

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn(X^{*},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C})),

where
\mathcal{C}onn(X^{*},G)
denotes the category of flat
G
-bundles on
X^{*}
(cf. (
13
)), is verified to be an equivalence of categories (cf. Theorem
3.1
), as in the classical case of linear differential equations, i.e.,
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
; see §§
3.1
-
3.3
for the detailed discussion concerning this equivalence.

We can also consider flat
G
-bundles on the pointed Riemann surface
\mathscr{X}
, which are by definition principal
G
-bundles on
X
equipped with a meromorphic connection with simple poles along the
marked points.
The residue of such a flat
G
-bundle at each marked point
\sigma_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,r
) determines an element of the adjoint quotient
\mathfrak{c}:=\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
; this element is called the
radius
at

\sigma_{i}

(cf. §
2.4
).

Under the assumption that
G
is
a simple algebraic group of adjoint type (or a somewhat more general algebraic group),
there is an important class of flat
G
-bundles, called
G
-opers
, that appear in some areas of mathematics, including the geometric Langlands program.
Roughly speaking, a
G
-oper on
\mathscr{X}
is a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{X}
equipped with a Borel reduction
satisfying
a strict form of Griffiths transversality (cf. §
4.1
for the precise definition).
One obtains the moduli space

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

(cf. (
73
))
parametrizing
G
-opers on
\mathscr{X}
with certain parabolic conditions on its radii.
It is known that
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

can be represented by a complex manifold whose dimension is equal to the value

(3)

\displaystyle a(G):=\frac{2g-2+r}{2}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(G)-\frac{r}{2}\cdot\mathrm{rk}(G)

(cf.
[
BeDr
, Proposition 3.1.10]
,
[
Wak1
, Theorem A]
), where
\mathrm{rk}(G)
denotes the rank of
G
.
For
the study of

G
-opers and their moduli space, we refer the reader to, e.g.,

[
BeDr
]
,
[
Fre1
]
,

[
Fre2
]
,
[
Fre3
]
,
[
FrBe
]
, and
[
Wak1
]
.

We shall set

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))

(cf. (
57
)) to be
the set of irreducible representations
\Gamma\rightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
up to conjugation (i.e., isomorphism classes of

\mathrm{ob}(\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C})))
)
satisfying certain conditions on the images of the elements
T_{i}
; it forms a complex manifold of dimension
2\cdot a(G)
(cf.
[
Wei
]
).
The study of local monodromy
(cf. Theorem
3.2
, Corollary
3.3
)
leads us to show that
taking the monodromy maps yields, via (
1
),
an injection between complex manifolds

(5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))

(cf. Proposition
5.3
).
By this injection,
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)
can be regarded as a submanifold of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
.

On the other hand, since
G
admits a real form
G_{\mathbb{R}}
(by which it makes sense to speak of the subgroup
G(\mathbb{R})
of
G(\mathbb{C})
consisting of elements invariant under the complex conjugation), we have a real submanifold

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))

of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
parametrizing representations whose images are contained in
G(\mathbb{R})
(up to conjugation).
Since
the sum of the real dimensions of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))
and
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)
equals that of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
,

it is natural to ask whether or not these real submanifolds intersect transversally, or equivalently,
the intersection

(7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

(which classifies certain
G
-opers with real monodromy) forms a discrete set.
So we are mainly interested in
knowing what
the deformation space of
G
-opers with real monodromy looks like.

1.2.
Statement of results

To the best of the author’s knowledge,
there is only one explicit result on this subject, dealing with
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers.
In fact,
G. Faltings introduced
permissible connections
(cf.
[
Fal
, § 5]
) on a certain rank
2
vector bundle, and proved that their moduli space is transversal to the submanifold
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
(cf.
[
Fal
, Theorem 12]
).
Since permissible connections are equivalent to
objects in

\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})
after
a certain adjustment
at the marked points (cf. Proposition
4.3
),
this result can be regarded as
asserting about the discreteness of the intersection
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})
.
This case is of particular interest as
it contains the canonical
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers arising from the Fuchsian uniformization.
For topics on
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers (= projective structures) with real monodromy, we refer the reader to
[
BHS
]
,
[
Hel
]
,
[
Tak
]
, and
[
Wil
]
.

In the present paper, we partially generalize Faltings’ result to a general
G
.
To this end, we begin by reviewing some basic things about flat
G
-bundles on pointed Riemann surfaces, including their monodromy maps and parabolic cohomology groups.
Some of the facts described here with proofs (e.g., Corollary
2.7
, Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
, and Proposition
3.6
) may be well-known to experts in this field.
In fact, when
G
is, say, a matrix group,
the assertions would directly follow from the classical case of linear differential equations, i.e.,
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
At any rate, however, the author could not find any reasonable references to them, so decided to write the proofs with some care.

After studying some properties on flat
G
-bundles and

G
-opers,
we prove the following assertion; this is the first main theorem of the present paper.

Theorem A

(= Theorem
6.3
)
.

Let us keep the above notation.
Then, the two submanifolds
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}_{P}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))
,
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

of
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}_{P}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
transversally intersect at the points classifying
permissible
G
-opers (cf. Definition
4.5
for the definition of a permissible
G
-oper).
In particular,
the space of permissible
G
-opers with real monodromy forms
a discrete set.

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

Permissible
G
-opers are, by definition, essentially equivalent to

\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers via change of structure group by the morphism
\iota_{G}:\mathrm{PSL}_{2}\rightarrow G
induced from a suitable choice of an
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{g}
.
However, Theorem
A
does not follow
immediately
from Faltings’ result (dealing with
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers) and is essentially stronger than it.
This is because
we need to consider, in the present situation, the deformation spaces having
many more parameters not derived from deformations as a
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-oper.

(ii)

While
it is expected that
the above theorem still be true after removing the assumption of being “permissible”, we have no idea how to prove it at the time of writing the present paper (cf. Conjecture
6.4
).

Next, we shall describe an application of Theorem
A
concerning a generalization of the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism.
Let
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}
be
a
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-oper classified by
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})
; it has a real monodromy map
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
For
N\geq 2
,

\mu
(resp.,
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}
) induces
a
\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
-representation
\mu_{N}
of
\Gamma
(resp., a
\mathrm{PSL}_{N}
-oper
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
on
\mathscr{X}
) via change of structure group using the embedding
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PSL}_{N}
obtained by integrating a principal
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote by

(8)

\displaystyle T_{[\mu_{N}]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})\right)

the tangent space of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))
(resp.,
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})
) at the point classifying
\mu_{N}
(resp.,
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
).

Then, Theorem
A
says that
the subspace of
T_{[\mu_{N}]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))
consisting of elements invariant under the complex conjugation is disjoint from
T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})
.
By this fact,

T_{[\mu_{N}]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))
decomposes into a direct sum

(9)

\displaystyle T_{[\mu_{N}]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))=T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})\oplus\overline{T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})}.

of

T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})
and its complex conjugate

\overline{T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})}
.
Regarding the left-hand side of
this decomposition,
we recall that

T_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{N})
forms a structure of affine space modeled on the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N-1}H^{0}(X,\Omega^{\otimes(j+1)}(-D))
, where
\Omega
denotes the sheaf of holomorphic
1
-forms on
X
and
D
denotes the divisor on
X
defined by the union of the
\sigma_{i}
’s.

On the other hand,
the irreducible decomposition of the
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{N}\left(=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{PSL}_{N})\right)

determined by
\mu_{N}

gives rise to a decomposition

(10)

\displaystyle T_{[\mu_{N}]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}))=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N-1}H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu}),

where
H_{P}^{1}(-)
denotes the
1
-st parabolic cohomology (cf. (
40
)) and
V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu}
denotes the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of homogenous degree
2j
polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
equipped with a natural
\Gamma
-action induced from
\mu
(cf. (
151
)).
Since (
9
) preserves the direct sum decomposition,
we obtain the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism for the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-oper
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}
by considering its
j
-th factor.
The assertion is described as follows.
(Analogous results for opers in positive characteristic can be found in
[
Wak2
]
.)

Theorem B

(cf. Theorems
6.6
,
6.8
for the precise statements)
.

Let

\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}
be a
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-oper classified by
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})
with monodromy map
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then, for each positive integer
j
,
there exists a canonical decomposition

(11)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu})=H^{0}(X,\Omega^{\otimes(j+1)}(-D))\oplus\overline{H^{0}(X,\Omega^{\otimes(j+1)}(-D))}

of the
1
-st parabolic cohomology group
H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu})
of the
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu}
.
Moreover, the real form
H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,V_{2j,\mathbb{R},\mu})
of
H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,V_{2j,\mathbb{C},\mu})

admits a polarized real Hodge structure of weight
1
whose Hodge decomposition is given by (
11
).

Remark 1.2
.

As far as the author knows,
the Eichler-Shimura isomorphisms
established in the previous work are essentially the case of Fuchsian representations.
On the other hand, the above theorem actually involves new situations, since the representations associated to
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers are not only the Fuchsian ones.
In fact, if
r=0
, then
it is known
that every non-elementary liftable
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-representation of
\Gamma
arises from the monodromy map of a projective structure, i.e.,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-oper (cf.
[
GKM
, Theorem 1.1.1]
or
[
GGP
]
).
Hence, we can apply Theorem
B
to any such representation.

1.3.
Organization of the present paper

First of all, we note that many of the arguments and notational conventions follow
[
Wak1
]
(and
[
Wak2
]
).
Although
[
Wak1
]
only deals with pointed stable curves in a purely algebraic treatment,
various arguments discussed there can also be applied to our analytic situation.

In §
2
, we consider flat
G
-bundles on a pointed Riemann surface and some relevant basic things, including an explicit description of gauge transformation using the Maurer-Cartan form (cf. (
20
)).
The local triviality of a flat
G
-bundle (cf. Proposition
2.6
, Corollary
2.7
) asserted in that section generalizes classical results for linear differential equations; in fact, the proofs are entirely similar to the classical case.

In §
3
, we examine
G
-representations on a surface group and describe infinitesimal deformations of such a representation in terms of parabolic cohomology groups.
Also, two versions of the (most basic form of) Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for flat
G
-bundles are formulated in Theorems
3.1
,
3.7
.

In §
4
,
we discuss
G
-opers on a pointed Riemann surface
\mathscr{X}
, as well as the moduli space
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)
parametrizing those having specific radii.
Some of the facts on
G
-opers proved in
[
Wak1
]
(for pointed stable curves) or
[
BeDr
]
(for compact Riemann surfaces) are reformulated in order to use in the subsequent discussion.
After that, we recall the notion of a permissible connection defined by Faltings in
[
Fal
]
, and
construct a bijective correspondence between permissible connections and objects in
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})

(cf. Proposition
4.3
).
The definition of a permissible
G
-oper is described at the end of that section (cf. Definition
4.5
).

In §
5
,
we deal with the parabolic de Rham cohomology of
a flat vector bundle
and the comparison with
the parabolic sheaf cohomology of the corresponding local system (cf. Proposition
5.2
).
Moreover, it is shown that

\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},\mathrm{PSL}_{2})
may be embedded, via taking the monodromy groups, into the representation variety
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
(cf. (
125
)).

The first part of §
6
provides
a proof of Theorem
A
(cf. Theorem
6.3
) by investigating a canonical bilinear form on the tangent space of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
.
An essential ingredient in the proof is an explicit description of that form restricted to the tangent space of
\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)
(cf. Theorem
6.1
); this description shows the transversality of the intersection required in Theorem
A
.
In the second part,
the classical Eichler-Shimura isomorphism is generalized to the case of general
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers with real monodromy by applying Theorem
A
(cf. Theorem
6.6
), and we formulate it in terms of Hodge structures (cf. Theorem
6.8
).
This proves Theorem
B
.

2.
Flat
G
-bundles on a pointed Riemann surface

This first section discusses flat
G
-bundles on a pointed Riemann surface and some relevant notions used in the subsequent discussion.
At the end of this section, we prove the local triviality of a flat
G
-bundle (cf. Proposition
2.6
, Corollary
2.7
), which generalizes the existence assertion of local solutions for a linear differential equation.

2.1.
Flat
G
-bundles

Let
\mathscr{X}:=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})
(where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) be a pointed Riemann surface, i.e., a Riemann surface
X
(that is not necessarily compact) together with
a possibly empty set of mutually distinct marked points

\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{r}\in X
.
Write
\mathcal{O}_{X}
for the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
X
,

\Omega_{X}
for the sheaf of holomorphic
1
-forms on
X
, and

\mathcal{T}_{X}\left(:=\Omega_{X}^{\vee}\right)
for the dual of
\Omega_{X}
, i.e., the sheaf of holomorphic vector fields on
X
.
Also, we shall set
\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}:=\Omega_{X}(D)
and
\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}:=\mathcal{T}_{X}(-D)\left(=\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}^{\vee}\right)
, where
D
denotes the divisor on
X
defined as the union of the
\sigma_{i}
’s.

Let
G
be a connected linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
with identity element
e_{G}
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
and by
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
the adjoint representation of
G
.
By a
G
-bundle
on
X
, we shall mean a holomorphic principal
G
-bundle on
X
, where the elements of
G
act from the
right
.

Let us
fix a
G
-bundle

\pi:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow X
on
X
.
The pull-back
D_{\mathcal{E}}
of
D
via
\pi
specifies a divisor on
\mathcal{E}
.
The
G
-action on
\mathcal{E}
induces a
G
-action on the direct image
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{E}}(D_{\mathcal{E}}))
of the holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{E}}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
.
The subsheaf
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}:=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{E}}(D_{\mathcal{E}}))^{G}
of
G
-invariant sections of
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{E}}(D_{\mathcal{E}}))
fits into the following short exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules induced by
differentiating
\pi
:

(12)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\longrightarrow 0

(cf.
[
Wak1
, § 1.2.5]
), where
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}
denotes the adjoint bundle associated to
\mathcal{E}
, i.e., the vector bundle obtained from
\mathcal{E}
via change of structure group by
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}
.

Recall that a
log connection

on
\mathcal{E}
is, by definition, an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
\nabla:\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}
satisfying
d_{\mathcal{E}}\circ\nabla=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}}
.
When
r=0
(i.e.,
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i}=\emptyset
),
any log connection is referred to as a
non-logarithmic connection
, or simply, a
connection
.

Since
\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
is a holomorphic line bundle, any log connection is automatically flat, i.e., has vanishing curvature (cf.
[
Wak1
, Definition 1.23]
for the definition of curvature).
By a
flat
G
-bundle
on
\mathscr{X}
,
we shall mean a pair
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
consisting of a
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and a (flat) log connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}
.
One can define the notion of an isomorphism between flat
G
-bundles.
Thus, we obtain the category

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn(\mathscr{X},G)

consisting of flat
G
-bundles on
\mathscr{X}
and isomorphisms between them; it is verified to forms a
\mathbb{C}
-stack by considering families of such objects.

2.2.
Log connections on the trivial
G
-bundle

The
trivial
G
-bundle
on
X
is the
G
-bundle on
X
defined as the product
X\times G
, where
G
acts only on the second factor and
the first projection
X\times G\rightarrow X
is considered as its structure morphism.

For each morphism
h:X\rightarrow G
,
the left-translation
\mathrm{L}_{h}:X\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}X\times G
by
h
(cf.
[
Wak1
, § 1.1.2]
) determines an automorphism of the trivial
G
-bundle
X\times G
.
Conversely,
any automorphism of the trivial
G
-bundle on
X
coincides with the left-translation by some morphism
X\rightarrow G
.

The differentials of the first and second projections
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
,
\mathrm{pr}_{2}
from
X\times G
yield an
\mathcal{O}_{X\times G}
-linear isomorphism

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}})\oplus\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{G}).

By applying the functor
\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(-)^{G}
to this isomorphism, we obtain a decomposition

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}

of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}
.
The direct summand
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}
of the codomain is naturally isomorphic to the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{X\times G}
of the trivial
G
-bundle
X\times G
.
The surjection
d_{X\times G}:\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
(cf. (
12
)) may be identified, via (
15
), with the first projection
\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
.

Given a log connection
\nabla
on the trivial
G
-bundle
X\times G
, we shall write
\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}
for the composite

(16)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{\nabla}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{(\ref{Eq123})}\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\text{second projection}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}.

This morphism characterizes the log connection
\nabla
because the equality
\nabla=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}},\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}})
holds under the identification

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}=\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}
given by (
15
).
The assignment
\nabla\mapsto\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}
determines a bijective correspondence between the set of log connections on
X\times G
and the set of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphisms
\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}
.
The log connection

(17)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mathrm{triv}}:\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(X\times G)^{\mathrm{log}}}

corresponding to the zero map
\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}
via this bijection is
called the
trivial (log) connection
.

2.3.
Gauge transformation

We recall the Maurer-Cartan form on the algebraic group
G
, which is used to describe gauge transformations of a log connection.
Note that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be identified with the space
\Gamma(G,\mathcal{T}_{G})^{G}
of vector fields on
G
invariant under the various left-translations.
That is to say, given any element
v\in\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{T}_{G}|_{e_{G}}
, we can use left-translation to produce a global vector field
v^{\dagger}
on
G
with
v^{\dagger}|_{h}=d\mathrm{L}_{h}|_{e_{G}}(v)
for every
h\in G(\mathbb{C})
, where
d\mathrm{L}_{h}|_{e_{G}}
denotes the
\mathbb{C}
-linear morphism
\mathcal{T}_{G}|_{e_{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{G}|_{h}

obtained as the differential of
\mathrm{L}_{h}
at
e_{G}
.

Then, there exists an
\mathcal{O}_{G}
-linear isomorphism

\omega_{G}^{\triangleright}:\mathcal{T}_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}
determined uniquely by the condition that
\omega_{G}^{\triangleright}(v^{\dagger})=1\otimes v
for any
v\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form

(18)

\displaystyle\omega_{G}\in\Gamma(G,\Omega_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g})\left(\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(\mathcal{T}_{G},\mathcal{O}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g})\right)

corresponding to this morphism is called the (
left-invariant
)
Maurer-Cartan form
on
G
(cf.
[
Wak1
, Definition 1.1]
).

Let
\eta:\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}
be an automorphism of a
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
.
For a log connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}
, the composite

(19)

\displaystyle\eta_{*}(\nabla):\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{\nabla}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{d\eta^{G}}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}},

where
d\eta^{G}
denotes the automorphism of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}
obtained by differentiating
\eta
,
forms another log connection.
We shall refer to
\eta_{*}(\nabla)
as the
gauge transformation
of
\nabla
by
\eta
(cf.
[
Wak1
, Definition 1.20]
).

If
\mathcal{E}=X\times G
and
\eta=\mathrm{L}_{h}
for a morphism

h:X\rightarrow G
, then
the gauge transformation
\mathrm{L}_{h*}(\nabla)
satisfies the equality

(20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{h*}(\nabla)^{\mathfrak{g}}=(h^{-1})^{*}(\omega_{G})+\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(h)\circ\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}

of morphisms
\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
(cf.
[
Wak1
, Proposition 1.22]
).

Remark 2.1

(Gauge transformation for a matrix group)
.

Suppose that
G
is a matrix group, i.e., equipped with an injective morphism
\iota:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
In particular, each element of
G
may be considered as an
N\times N
matrix.
Keeping the above notation, we obtain the matrix
d(h^{-1})
(resp.,
dh
) with coefficients in
\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}})
obtained by differentiating every component of the
N\times N
matrix
h^{-1}
(resp.,
h
), and (
20
) reads

(21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{h*}(\nabla)^{\mathfrak{g}}=h\cdot d(h^{-1})+h\cdot\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}\cdot h^{-1}\left(=-(dh)\cdot h^{-1}+h\cdot\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}\cdot h^{-1}\right)

(cf.
[
Wak1
, Example 1.5]
).

A
framing
of a
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
\theta:X\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}
, which determines a trivialization of
\mathcal{E}
.
Choosing a framing
\theta
is equivalent to choosing a global section
h:X\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
of the projection
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow X
; this equivalence is given in such a way that
\theta(x,a)=h(x)\cdot a
and
h(x)=\theta(x,e_{G})
(for
x\in X
,
a\in G
).
The following definition generalizes the notion of a
fundamental matrix
for
a linear differential equation (cf.
[
vdPS
, Definition 1.9]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{X}
.
A
fundamental framing
for
\mathscr{E}
is a framing
\theta:X\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{E}
via which
\nabla^{\mathrm{triv}}
is compatible with
\nabla
.
In other words, a fundamental framing is an isomorphism of flat
G
-bundles

\theta:(X\times G,\nabla^{\mathrm{triv}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathscr{E}
.

Remark 2.3

(Equivalent definition of a fundamental framing)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be
a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{X}
with
\mathcal{E}=X\times G
, and let

h
be a morphism
X\rightarrow G
.
As discussed above, the graph
X\rightarrow X\times G
of
h
(regarded as a global section of the structure morphism of
\mathcal{E}
) corresponds to a framing
\theta:X\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}
.
Then,

\theta
defines a fundamental framing for
\mathscr{E}
if and only if the morphism
\nabla^{\mathfrak{g}}
coincides with the composite

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}\xrightarrow{dh}h^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{G})\xrightarrow{h^{*}(\omega^{\triangleright}_{G})}h^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g},

where the last arrow arises from the natural isomorphism
h^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

2.4.
Residue of a flat
G
-bundle

Let
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{X}
, and suppose that
r>0
.
Let us choose
i\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
, and moreover choose a local holomorphic function
t\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
defining

\sigma_{i}
.
Then,
the element

(23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla):=\overline{\nabla\left(t\frac{d}{dt}\right)}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}}|_{\sigma_{i}},

where
(-)|_{\sigma_{i}}
denotes the fiber of
(-)
over
\sigma_{i}
,
lies in

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\sigma_{i}}

and does not depend on the choice of
t
(cf. the discussion in
[
Wak1
, § 1.6.3]
).
We shall refer to
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)
as the
residue
(or, the
monodromy operator
, in the terminology of
[
Wak1
, Definition 1.46]
) of
\nabla
at
\sigma_{i}
.
Each
log connection
\nabla

with
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)=0
for every
i=1,\cdots,r
may be identified with a non-logarithmic connection.

Next,
denote by
\mathfrak{c}

the GIT quotient
of
\mathfrak{g}
by the adjoint
G
-action; it admits
a natural projection
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{c}
.
If we choose a
G
-equivariant trivialization
\mathcal{E}|_{\sigma_{i}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}G
(inducing
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\sigma_{i}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}
),
then
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)
specifies an element of
\mathfrak{g}
via this trivialization and induces

(24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rad}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla):=\chi(\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla))\in\mathfrak{c}(\mathbb{C}).

This element does not depend on the choice of
\mathcal{E}|_{\sigma_{i}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}G
, i.e., depends only on
\nabla
.
We refer to
\mathrm{Rad}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)
as the
radius
of
\nabla
at
\sigma_{i}
(cf.
[
Wak1
, Definition 2.29]
).

2.5.
Local description of flat
G
-bundles

Denote by
\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
the ring of formal power series in the variable
t
and with complex coefficients, and set
Q:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
.
The divisor given by the closed point
0\in Q
determines a log structure on
Q
; we denote the resulting log scheme by
Q^{\mathrm{log}}
.
Note that
\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
has a structure of differential ring over
\mathbb{C}
by considering the derivation
\partial:=\frac{d}{dt}
(resp.,
\partial^{\mathrm{log}}:=t\frac{d}{dt}
) on
\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
.
The tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{Q}
of
Q
(resp., the logarithmic tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{Q^{\mathrm{log}}}
of
Q^{\mathrm{log}}
) over
\mathbb{C}
is topologically generated by
the global section corresponding to
\partial
(resp.,
\partial^{\mathrm{log}}
).
We will consider the pair
\mathscr{Q}:=(Q,\{0\})
as a formal neighborhood of a marked point in a pointed Riemann surface.

The various definitions and arguments discussed so far are applicable even when “
X
” and “
\mathscr{X}
” are replaced by
Q
and
\mathscr{Q}
, respectively.
In particular, of course, one can define the notion of a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{Q}
, as well as its gauge transformation.
Each element
v
of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
associates a flat
G
-bundle
\mathscr{E}_{v}:=(Q\times G,\nabla_{v})
on
\mathscr{Q}
, where
\nabla_{v}
denotes the log connection on the trivial
G
-bundle
Q\times G
uniquely determined by the equality
\nabla_{v}^{\mathfrak{g}}=v
;
the resulting assignment
v\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{v}
defines a bijection between the set
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
and
the set of flat
G
-bundles on
\mathscr{Q}
whose underlying
G
-bundles are trivial.

Moreover,
two elements
v_{1}(t)
,
v_{2}(t)
of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
are called
equivalent
if there exists an element
h\in G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
such that the element

(25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{h*}(v_{1}(t))^{\mathfrak{g}}:=(h^{-1})^{*}(\omega_{G})(\partial^{\mathrm{log}})+\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(h)(v_{1}(t))

of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])

coincides with
v_{2}(t)
.
(Similarly to the comment in Remark
2.1
,
this condition
reads the equality
v_{2}(t)=h\cdot d(h^{-1})+h\cdot v_{1}(t)\cdot h^{-1}
after fixing an injective morphism of algebraic groups
G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.)
This binary relation in
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
specifies an equivalence relation, and hence we obtain
the quotient set
\overline{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])}
of

\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])

by this equivalence relation.
Since any
G
-bundle on
Q
is isomorphic to the trivial one,
the assignment
v\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{v}
induces
a bijection of sets

(26)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{isomorphism classes}\\
\text{of flat $G$-bundles on $\mathscr{Q}$}\end{matrix}\right\}.

For each
v(t):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}v_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
(where
v_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
),
the element

(27)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(v(t)):=v_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}

is called the
residue of
v(t)
; it
coincides with the residue of the flat
G
-bundle
\mathscr{E}_{v}
at the unique marked point
0\in Q
.

Definition 2.4
.

We shall say that an element
v
of
\mathfrak{g}
has
weakly prepared eigenvalues

if
there exists an injective morphism of algebraic groups
\iota:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
(where
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) such that
the distinct eigenvalues of
d\iota(v)\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{N}
, where
d\iota:\mathfrak{g}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{N}
denotes the differential of
\iota
, do not differ by integers (i.e.,
d\iota(v)
has weakly prepared eigenvalues, in the sense of
[
Ked
, Definition 7.3.3]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
let
v_{0},v_{n},v_{n+1},v_{n+2},\cdots
be elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We shall set
v(t):=v_{0}+\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}t^{i}v_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
, and
suppose that
v_{0}\left(=\mathrm{Res}(v(t))\right)
has weakly prepared eigenvalues.
Then, there exists an element
h
of
G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
such that
h\equiv e_{G}
(mod
t^{n}
) and
\mathrm{L}_{h*}(v(t))^{\mathfrak{g}}\equiv v_{0}
(mod
t^{n+1}
).

Proof.

Let us fix an injective morphism
\iota:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
(where
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) such that the distinct eigenvalues of
d\iota(v_{0})
do not differ by integers.
Then,
since
d\iota(v_{0})
and
nE_{N}+d\iota(v_{0})
(where
E_{N}
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{N}
) have no eigenvalues in common,
the
\mathbb{C}
-linear endomorphism
\alpha^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{N}
given by
w\mapsto nw+[d\iota(v_{0}),w]
is injective (cf. the proof of
[
vdPS
, Proposition 3.12]
).
Hence, the endomorphism
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{g}
given by

w\mapsto nw+[v_{0},w]
(i.e., obtained by restricting
\alpha^{\prime}
) is verified to be bijective.
This implies that there exists an element
u\in\mathfrak{g}
with
\alpha(u)\left(=nu+[v_{0},u]\right)=-v_{n}
.
Next,
let us
take an element
h
of
G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
with
h\equiv e_{G}+t^{n}u
(mod
t^{n+1}
).
Then, we have

(28)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \,\mathrm{L}_{h*}\left(v_{0}+\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}t^{i}v_{i}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=(h^{-1})^{*}(\omega_{G})+\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(h)\left(v_{0}+\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}t^{i}v_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv(e_{G}+t^{n}u)\partial^{\mathrm{log}}(e_{G}-t^{n}u)+(e_{G}+t^{n}u)(v_{0}+t^{n}v_{n})(e_{G}-t^{n}u)\ (\text{mod}\ t^{n+1})

\displaystyle\equiv v_{0}-t^{n}(nu+[v_{0},u]+v_{n})\ (\text{mod}\ t^{n+1})

\displaystyle\equiv v_{0}\ (\text{mod}\ t^{n+1})\ (\text{mod}\ t^{n+1}).

It follows that
h
satisfies the required properties.
This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

By applying successively the above lemma, we obtain the following assertion.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
v(t):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}v_{i}
(where
v_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
) be an element of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])

such that

v_{0}\left(=\mathrm{Res}(v(t))\right)
has weakly prepared eigenvalues.
Then, the following assertions hold:

(i)

The element
v(t)
is equivalent to

v_{0}
, i.e., there exists an element
h
of
G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])

with
v_{0}=\mathrm{L}_{h*}(v(t))
.
In particular, any element
v(t)
of

t\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])

(i.e., any element
v(t)
of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
with
\mathrm{Res}(v(t))=0
)
is equivalent to
0
.

(ii)

If, moreover,
v(t)
lies in
the subring
\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of
\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
consisting of all convergent power series,
then
the element
h
asserted in (i) can be taken as an element of
G(\mathbb{C}\{t\})
.

Proof.

First, let us consider assertion (i).
One can choose a collection of pairs
\{(h_{[i]},v_{[i]}(t))\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, where
h_{[i]}\in G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
and
v_{[i]}(t)\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
(
i=0,1,2,\cdots
), constructed inductively by the following rules:

•

(h_{[0]},v_{[0]}(t)):=(e_{G},v(t))
;

•

For each
i=0,1,2,\cdots
,
we set
h_{[i+1]}
to be an element “
h
” obtained by applying Lemma
2.5

to the case where “
n
” and “
v(t)
” are taken to be
i+1
and
v_{[i]}(t)
, respectively,
and we set
v_{[i+1]}(t):=\mathrm{L}_{h_{[i+1]}*}(v_{[i]}(t))^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Then, the product
h:=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}h_{i}\left(=\cdots h_{2}h_{1}h_{0}\right)
converges to an element of
G(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
and satisfies the required property.
This completes the proof of assertion (i).

Next, we shall consider assertion (ii).
Since
v_{0}
has weakly prepared eigenvalues,
there exists an injective morphism
\iota:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
(where
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) such that
the distinct eigenvalues of
d\iota(v_{0})
do not differ by integers.
Using this injection, we can reduce
the problem to the case of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.
Then, the assertion follows from an argument entirely similar to the proof of
[
vdPS
, Lemma 3.42]
.
∎

The following assertion, being a corollary of the above proposition,
may be a classical fact at least in

; see, e.g.,
[

, Proposition 3.3.20]
.
(However, the author could not find any references that were consistent with our setting and contained its proof without omission.)

Corollary 2.7
.

Let

be a Riemann surface and
\mathscr{E}
a flat

-bundle on

.
Then, each point

has an open neighborhood

in

such that
the restriction
\mathscr{E}|_{U}
of
\mathscr{E}
to

admits a fundamental framing.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Proposition
2.6
, (the latter assertion of) (i) and (ii), together with the comment at the end of the first paragraph in §

.
∎

3.
G
-representations of the fundamental group

This section discusses the monodromy maps of flat
G
-bundles, which specify

G
-representations of
the fundamental group
\Gamma
of the underlying Riemann surface.
The (most basic form of the) Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for flat
G
-bundles will be described in
Theorems
3.1
and
3.7
.
Also, we recall the parabolic cohomology of a local system and the relationship with the parabolic group cohomology of the corresponding
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module (cf. Proposition

).

3.1.
G
-representations

Let
X
be a Riemann surface, and fix a base point
x_{0}
in
X
.
Also, let
\widetilde{X}
be the space of homotopy classes
[\gamma]
of paths
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X
in

starting at
x_{0}
(i.e.,
\gamma(0)=x_{0}

In a usual manner, we equip
\widetilde{X}
with a topology by which

forms the universal covering of

.
We shall denote by
\varpi:\widetilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
the natural projection.
There exists a structure of Riemann surface on
\widetilde{X}
so that

\varpi
is locally biholomorphic.

Denote by
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
the fundamental group of
X
at
x_{0}
.
The Riemann surface

\widetilde{X}
admits a natural (properly discontinuous) right

-action as deck transformations

(for
[\delta]\in\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
).
Here,
a_{[\delta]}
is given by

a_{[\delta]}([\gamma]):=[\gamma\delta]
for any
[\gamma]\in\widetilde{X}
,
where

3.4.

We shall fix

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

denotes the exponential map

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, (i) and (ii), we may assume, after possibly replacing

with a smaller positive value, that

is of the form
(Q_{a}\times G,\nabla_{C})
, where

denotes the log connection on the trivial

Then, the (locally defined)

).
Hence, the assertion follows from the fact that analytic continuation around the generator of

∎

Corollary 3.3

Let us keep the notation in the above theorem.
Then, the residue

, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R},0}=\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R},1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\alpha}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R},-1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}^{-\alpha}
.
It associates a decreasing filtration
\{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
given by
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}^{j}:=\bigoplus_{l\geq j}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R},l}
.

Let us fix a collection

\,\,q_{1}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox[origin]{-10.0}{$\swarrow$}\hskip 1.42262pt:=\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Pi}
consisting of
generators
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and set

q_{1}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}x_{\alpha}
.
(If we take two such collections, then they are conjugate to each other by an element of
T_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For that reason, the results obtained in the subsequent discussion are essentially independent of the choice of
\,\,q_{1}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox[origin]{-10.0}{$\swarrow$}\hskip 1.42262pt
.)
By regarding
the fundamental coweight
\check{\omega}_{\alpha}
of
\alpha
as an element of
\mathfrak{t}
via differentiation, we obtain
\check{\rho}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}\check{\omega}_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, there is a unique collection
\{y_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Pi}
of generators
y_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}_{\mathbb{R}}

such that
the set

\{q_{-1},2\check{\rho},q_{1}\}
, where

, forms an
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple.
If
(\mathrm{PSL}_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the projective special linear group of degree two over
\mathbb{R}
with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
the resulting injection
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}:(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be integrated to a morphism of algebraic groups

(55)

\displaystyle\iota_{G}:(\mathrm{PSL}_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}

over
\mathbb{R}
.
We
will use the same notation “
\iota_{G}
” to denote
its base-change
\left((\mathrm{PSL}_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}\times_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}=\right)\mathrm{PSL}_{n}\rightarrow G
over
\mathbb{C}
.

Now,
let
K
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.
Given a representation
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow G(K)
,
we shall write
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
for the composite representation
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\mu}G(K)\hookrightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
(hence
\mu=\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
if

).
Denote by
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}(\Gamma,G(K))
the algebraic variety over
K

parametrizing all

G(K)
-representations

\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow G(K)
of
\Gamma

such that, for each
i=1,\cdots,r
, the element
\mu(T_{i})
is conjugate to
\iota_{G}(T_{i})
(which is also conjugate to
\mathrm{exp}(-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot q_{-1})
; see
[
Fal
]
).
It has
a natural
G(K)
-action by conjugation and contains
the Zariski open subset
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))

consisting of representations
\mu
such that
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
are irreducible and
the images
\mathrm{Im}(\mu_{\mathbb{C}})
have trivial centralizer.
The representations in
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))
define a full subcategory

(56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))

of
\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(K))
.

Moreover, we know (cf.
[
Men
, § 3]
,
[
Gol
]
) that the quotient set

(57)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K)):=\mathrm{Hom}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))/G(K)\left(\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}(\Gamma,G(K))\right)

forms a real (resp., a complex) manifold if

(resp.,
K=\mathbb{C}
).
Note that
\iota_{G}(T_{i})
(for each

) is a regular element of
G(K)
, so the conjugacy class of
\iota_{G}(T_{i})
has dimension
\mathrm{dim}(G)-\mathrm{rk}(G)
.
Hence, the
K
-dimension of

\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))

coincides with the value

2(g-1)\cdot\mathrm{dim}(G)+r\cdot(\mathrm{dim}(G)-\mathrm{rk}(G))=2\cdot a(G)
(cf. (
3
));
see
[
Wei
]
for a computation of the dimension.
Since the image of the representation
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
for each

has
trivial centralizer,
the inclusion
G(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
induces an injective morphism between real manifolds
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
.
By using this injection, we shall regard
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))
as a real submanifold of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
of half-dimension.

Next, let
\mu
be a
G(K)
-representation classified by
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))
.
The composite

determines a
K[\Gamma]
-module (resp., a local system)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(K)_{\mu}\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ \mathbb{L}_{\mu,K}\right)

whose underlying

-vector space (resp., whose fiber over every point) is isomorphic to

.
In particular, we have
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C})_{\mu}=\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{R})_{\mu}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and

.

The infinitesimal deformations of

\mu
in
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))

are determined by the elements of

(cf.
[

, § 2]
,
[

, Chap. VI]
).
Also,
the infinitesimal deformations tangential to the

are precisely the

It follows that
the tangent space

of
\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))
at the point classifying the equivalence class

admits an isomorphism of

(59)

\displaystyle T_{[\mu]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(K))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g}(K)_{\mu}).

Moreover, the following square diagram is commutative:

\textstyle{T_{[\mu]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{R}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\scriptstyle{(\ref{Eq240})\,\text{for}\,K=\mathbb{R}}
\textstyle{T_{[\mu]}\mathrm{Rep}_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}

\scriptstyle{\mathrm{inclusion}}
\textstyle{H^{1}_{P}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C})_{\mu}),}

where the upper horizontal arrow arises from the differential of the inclusion

.

3.8.
Flat
G
-bundles with prescribed nilpotent residue

A flat
G
-bundle
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
on
\mathscr{X}
is called
irreducible
if there are no pairs
(P,\mathcal{E}_{P})
such that
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
and
\mathcal{E}_{P}
is a
P
-reduction of
\mathcal{E}|_{X^{*}}
preserving the connection
\nabla|_{X^{*}}
(i.e., the image of
\nabla|_{X^{*}}
is contained in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{(\mathcal{E}|_{X^{*}})^{\mathrm{log}}}
).
Thus, we obtain the full subcategory (
\mathbb{C}
-substack)

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)

of
\mathcal{C}onn(\mathscr{X},G)
consisting of flat
G
-bundles
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)

satisfying the following three conditions:

(a)

\mathscr{E}
is irreducible;

(b)

\mathscr{E}
has no nontrivial automorphisms;

(c)

For each
i=1,\cdots,r
, the residue
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)
at
\sigma_{i}

coincides with
q_{-1}
under
the identification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\sigma}
induced from some
trivialization
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}|_{\sigma_{i}}
.

We shall prove the following assertion, generalizing
[
Con
, Theorem 4.2]
to flat
G
-bundles.

Theorem 3.7
.

The composite

(65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{restriction}}\mathcal{C}onn(X^{*},G)\xrightarrow{(\ref{Eq2000})}\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))

restricts to
an equivalence of categories

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{R}ep_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C})).

Proof.

First, let
\mathscr{E}:=(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a flat
G
-bundle classified by
\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)
.
By assumption,
the representation
\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathscr{E}|_{X^{*}}}:\Gamma\rightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
is irreducible
and
its image has trivial centralizer.
Moreover, since the equality
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)=q_{-1}
holds (for each
i=1,\cdots,r
)
under a suitable trivialization
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\sigma_{i}}
,
the local monodromy of
\mathscr{E}|_{X^{*}}
around
\sigma_{i}

is conjugate to
\mathrm{exp}(-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot q_{-1})
(cf. Theorem
3.2
), which is also conjugate to

\iota_{G}(T_{i})
.
It follows that
\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathscr{E}|_{X^{*}}}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}ep_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
.
Thus, the assignment
\mathscr{E}\mapsto\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathscr{E}|_{X^{*}}}
(which coincides with the composite (
65
)) defines a functor

(67)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{R}ep_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C})).

The problem is to prove
that this functor
is essentially surjective and fully faithful.
Let
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
be a homomorphism classified by
\mathcal{R}ep_{P}^{0}(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
; it corresponds to a flat
G
-bundle

\mathscr{E}^{*}:=(\mathcal{E}^{*},\nabla^{*})
on
X^{*}
.
For each
i=1,\cdots,r
, we shall choose a sufficiently small open neighborhood
U_{i}
of
\sigma_{i}
in
X
.
Then, we obtain the flat
G
-bundle
\mathscr{E}_{i}:=(U_{i}\times G,\nabla_{q_{-1}})
on the pointed Riemann surface
(U_{i},\{\sigma_{i}\})
, where
\nabla_{q_{-1}}
denotes the log connection on the trivial
G
-bundle
U_{i}\times G
determined by the equality
\nabla_{q_{-1}}^{\mathfrak{g}}=q_{-1}
.
The local monodromy of this flat
G
-bundle around
\sigma_{i}
is conjugate to
\mathrm{exp}(-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot q_{-1})
,
so
there exists an isomorphism
\alpha_{i}:\mathscr{E}_{i}|_{U_{i}^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathscr{E}^{*}|_{U^{*}_{i}}
(cf. Theorem
3.1
).
The isomorphism
\alpha_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,r
) can be used to
glue together
\mathscr{E}^{*}
and
\mathscr{E}_{i}
’s to obtain a flat
G
-bundle
\mathscr{E}
on
\mathscr{X}
.
By construction,
\mathscr{E}
specifies an object in
\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)
and its image via (
67
) is isomorphic to
\mu
.
This implies the essential surjectivity of (
67
).

The remaining portion is the fully-faithfulness of (
67
).
Let us take two flat
G
-bundles
\mathscr{E}_{i}:=(\mathcal{E}_{i},\nabla_{i})
(
i=1,2
)
classified by
\mathcal{C}onn_{P}^{0}(\mathscr{X},G)
.
The map

(68)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Isom}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Isom}(\mathscr{E}_{1}|_{X^{*}},\mathscr{E}_{2}|_{X^{*}})

between the sets of isomorphisms obtained via restriction is immediately verified to be injective.
Moreover, let us take an isomorphism
\alpha:\mathscr{E}_{1}|_{X^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathscr{E}_{2}|_{X^{*}}
.
Since
G
is affine, there exists a closed immersion

\iota:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
(
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
), so
we obtain a flat vector bundle
\iota_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{i})
(for each
i=1,2
) induced from
\mathscr{E}_{i}
via change of structure group by
\iota
.
The isomorphism
\alpha
induces an isomorphism
\iota_{*}(\alpha)_{X^{*}}:\iota_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{1})|_{X^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\iota_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{2})|_{X^{*}}
.
It follows from
[
Con
, Theorem 4.2]
that

\iota_{*}(\alpha)_{X^{*}}
extends to an isomorphism
\iota_{*}(\alpha)_{\mathscr{X}}:\iota_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\iota_{*}(\mathscr{E}_{2})
over
X
.
Since
\iota
is a closed immersion,

\iota_{*}(\alpha)_{\mathscr{X}}
lies in
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2})
.
This implies the surjectivity of (
68
), and hence completes the proof of the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.8

(Extending the equivalence of categories)
.

By an argument entirely similar to the proof of the above theorem,
we see that (
66
) can be extended to an equivalence of categories
between the category of flat
G
-bundles on
\mathscr{X}
having
nilpotent residues

and the full subcategory of
\mathcal{R}ep(\Gamma,G(\mathbb{C}))
consisting of representations
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
such that
\mu(T_{i})
is
unipotent
for every

.

4.
G
-opers on a pointed Riemann surface

This section deals with
G
-opers on a
pointed
Riemann surface and their moduli space.
The corresponding discussions for pointed stable curves (in a purely algebraic treatment) have already been made in
[
Wak1
]
.
Due to the similarity of the arguments, it will be sufficient to refer to that reference for the facts described here. Therefore, we will not provide their proofs in this text.
For the case of unpointed Riemann surfaces, we refer the reader to
[
BeDr
]
.
In the latter half of this section, we recall the notion of a permissible connection, introduced by G. Faltings, and
consider the relationship with
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-opers having specific radii (cf. Proposition
4.3
).

We keep the notation and assumptions concerning the algebraic group
G
described in §
3.7
.

4.1.
G
-opers

Let us fix a pair of nonnegative integers
(g,r)
with
2g-2+r>0
and an
r
-pointed compact Riemann surface
\mathscr{X}:=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})
of genus
g
.
To simplify slightly the notation, we write
\Omega:=\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
and
\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
.
Also, denote by
D
the divisor on
X
defined as the union of the
\sigma_{i}
’s.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{B}
be a
B
-bundle on
X
, which induces
a
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}_{G}
via change of structure group by the inclusion
B\hookrightarrow G
.
(Hence,
\mathcal{E}_{B}
specifies a
B
-reduction of
\mathcal{E}_{G}
.)
We regard
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{log}}}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}
by using the natural injection
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{log}}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}
.
Since the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{j}:=\mathfrak{g}^{j}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
of
\mathfrak{g}
(for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
)
is closed
under the adjoint
B
-action, it determines
a subbundle
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{j}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}
.
For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we shall set

(69)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{j}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{log}}}+\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{j}\left(\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}\right).

The inclusion
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}
gives an isomorphism

(70)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{j}/\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{j+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{j}/\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{j+1}.

On the other hand, since each
\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}:=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}^{-\alpha}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
(
\alpha\in\Pi
) is closed under the adjoint
B
-action,

\mathfrak{g}^{-1}/\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}
yields a canonical decomposition

\mathfrak{g}^{-1}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}/\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{-\alpha}
.
By combining it with the inverse of (
70
) for

, we obtain a decomposition

(71)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{-1}/\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}}^{-\alpha}.

Now, let us consider a pair
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}:=(\mathcal{E}_{B},\nabla)
consisting of a
B
-bundle
\mathcal{E}_{B}
on
X
and a log connection on the
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}_{G}:=\mathcal{E}_{B}\times_{B}G
induced by
\mathcal{E}_{B}
.
Recall that
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
is called a
G
-oper
on
\mathscr{X}
(cf.
[
Wak1
, Definition 2.1, (i)]
for the definition in the case of pointed stable curves) if

\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{X^{\mathrm{log}}})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{-1}
and the composite

(72)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\nabla}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}^{-1}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{0}_{\mathcal{E}_{G}^{\mathrm{log}}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}_{\mathcal{E}_{B}}

defined for each
\alpha\in\Pi
is an isomorphism, where the last arrow denotes the natural projection with respect to the decomposition (
71
).
In a natural manner, one can define the notion of an isomorphism between
G
-opers.

Denote by
[0]_{G}
(or simply,
[0]
, if there is no fear of confusion) the element of
\mathfrak{c}(\mathbb{C})
determined by the image of the zero section
0\in\mathfrak{g}
via
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{c}
.
Then, we obtain the category

(73)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

whose objects are
G
-opers
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}:=(\mathcal{E}_{B},\nabla)
on
\mathscr{X}
with
\mathrm{Rad}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla)=[0]
for every

, and whose morphisms are isomorphisms between such
G
-opers.
(In the case of

, we do not impose any condition on the radii of
G
-opers.)
Since each
G
-oper does not have non-trivial automorphisms (cf.
[
Wak1
, Proposition 2.9]
),

\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)
may be regarded as a set.

4.2.
Change of structure group

In what follows, we shall set

(74)

\displaystyle G^{\odot}:=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}

for simplicity.
This group contains the Borel subgroup
B^{\odot}
(resp., the maximal torus
T^{\odot}
)
consisting of the images of upper triangular matrices (resp., diagonal matrices)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
via the natural surjection
\mathrm{SL}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow G^{\odot}

Write
\mathfrak{g}^{\odot}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\odot}
for the Lie algebras of
G^{\odot}
and
B^{\odot}
, respectively (hence
\mathfrak{g}^{\odot}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}

In particular, we have

\iota_{G}(B^{\odot})\subseteq B
and

.

Let
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}:=(\mathcal{E}_{B^{\odot}},\nabla_{\odot})
be a

-oper on
\mathscr{X}
,
and write
\mathcal{E}_{B}:=\mathcal{E}_{B^{\odot}}\times^{B^{\odot},\iota_{G}}B
.
The injection

\iota_{G}
induces an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear injection

d\iota_{G}:\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}_{G^{\odot}}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}_{G}}
, and
the composite

\iota_{G*}(\nabla_{\odot}):\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\nabla_{\odot}}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}_{G^{\odot}}}\xrightarrow{d\iota_{G}}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{log}}_{G}}

specifies a log connection on
\mathcal{E}_{G}
.
The resulting pair

(75)

\displaystyle\iota_{G*}(\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}):=(\mathcal{E}_{B},\iota_{G*}(\nabla_{\odot}))

is verified to form a

-oper on
\mathscr{X}
(cf.
[
Wak1
, § 2.4.2]
).
Moreover, the equality

\mathrm{Rad}_{\sigma_{i}}(\iota_{G*}(\nabla_{\odot}))=[0]_{G}
holds if
\mathrm{Rad}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla_{\odot})=[0]_{G^{\odot}}
(

).
This implies that
the assignment
\mathscr{E}_{\odot}^{\spadesuit}\mapsto\iota_{G*}(\mathscr{E}_{\odot}^{\spadesuit})

determines a map of sets

(76)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G^{\odot})\rightarrow\mathrm{Op}_{P}(\mathscr{X},G)

(cf.
[
Wak1
, Remark 2.37]
).

4.3.
Canonical
B
-bundle

Just as in the discussion of
[
Wak1
, § 2.4.3]
,
one can construct a canonical
B
-bundle

{{}^{\dagger}}\mathcal{E}_{B}
(as well as a canonical
G
-bundle

)
on

that is isomorphic to the underlying

-bundle of any
G
-oper;
this

-bundle is constructed
as follows.

First, we shall consider the case of

(and
B=B^{\odot}
).
Let
U
be an open subset of
X
, and
assume that there exists a holomorphic line bundle

\varTheta
on
U
equipped with an isomorphism
\varTheta^{\otimes 2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega|_{U}
. (Such a line bundle always exists locally, and we refer to it as a

of

.)
We set
{{}^{\dagger}}\mathcal{E}_{G^{\odot},U}
to be the
G^{\odot}
-bundle on
U
defined as the projectivization of the rank
2
vector bundle
\mathcal{D}_{U,<j}\otimes\varTheta
, where for each

5.1.
Parabolic de Rham cohomology

Each morphism

of sheaves may be regarded as a complex concentrated at degrees

(In particular, we have

For each

Given an integer

(considered as a complex concentrated at degree

For a holomorphic vector bundle with log connection

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathcal{F}):=\Omega\otimes\mathcal{F}(-D)+\mathrm{Im}(\nabla)\left(\subseteq\Omega\otimes\mathcal{F}\right).

restricts to a

-linear morphism

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathrm{par}}:\Omega^{0}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathcal{F}).

The hypercohomology group
\mathbb{H}^{l}(X,\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla_{\mathrm{per}}])
(
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) associated to the complex
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla_{\mathrm{per}}]
is called the
l
-th parabolic de Rham cohomology group
of
\nabla
.

Let us suppose that the residue

at

is nilpotent
for every

.
Also, let

.

The following sequence is exact:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla_{\mathrm{par}}])\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla])\xrightarrow{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\nu_{i}^{*}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{H}^{1}(U_{i},\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla|_{U_{i}}]),

6.5.
Hodge structures associated to
G^{\odot}
-opers

In the case of
r=0
,
D. Gallo, M. Kapovich, and A. Marden proved
(cf.
[
GKM
, Theorem 1.1.1]
) that a
G^{\odot}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mu:\Gamma\rightarrow G^{\odot}(\mathbb{C})
is conjugate to the monodromy map of some
G^{\odot}
-oper (= projective structure) on a genus-
g
compact Riemann surface if and only if
\mu
lifts to a representation
\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and the image of
\mu
is non-elementary (in the sense of
[
GKM
, § 1.2]
).
For a representation
\mu
satisfying these properties,
there are infinitely many distinct pairs
(X,\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit})
of a genus-
g
compact Riemann surface
X
and a
G^{\odot}
-oper
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
on
X
with
\mu=\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}}
up to conjugation.
Also, according to
[
BaGu
, Theorem 1.1]
, such
X
’s (for a fixed
\mu
) form a dense subset of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of genus-
g
compact Riemann surfaces.
The study of characterizing
G^{\odot}
-opers with prescribed monodromy was investigated in, e.g.,
[
Bab
]
,
[
BaGu
]
,
[
Dum
]
,
[
Hub
]
, and
[
Kap
]
.
In what follows,
we propose a framework for doing this kind of research from the viewpoint of global Torelli problem by restating Theorem
6.6
in terms of Hodge structures.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space, so we have a complex conjugation
\overline{(-)}
on its complexification
V_{\mathbb{C}}:=V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
.
Recall that a
real Hodge structure of weight
k
(
\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
)
on
V
is a direct sum decomposition

(159)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=k,\\
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}\end{subarray}}V^{p,q}\ \ \text{with}\ \ V^{p,q}=\overline{V^{q,p}}.

Also, a
polarization
of a given Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p,q}V^{p,q}
is a nondegenerate bilinear form
(-,-)
on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
, defined over
\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the following conditions:

•

(x,y)=(-1)^{k}\cdot(y,x)
for any
x,y\in V_{\mathbb{C}}
(i.e.,
(-,-)
is symmetric or skew-symmetric depending on whether
k
is even or odd);

•

\langle x,y\rangle:=\sqrt{-1}^{p-q}\cdot(x,\overline{y})
is a positive definite hermitian inner product on
V^{p,q}
for every
(p,q)
;

•

the decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}V^{p,q}
is orthogonal, in the sense that
(x,y)=0
if
x\in V^{p,q}
,
y\in V^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
with
(p,q)\neq(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})
).

By a
polarized real Hodge structure of weight
k
on
V
, we mean a real Hodge structure of weight
k
on
V
together with its polarization.
P. Bayer and J. Neukirch (cf.
[
BaNe
, Theorem 5.2]
) gave, in the framework of Hodge theory using such notions, a direct proof of the Eichler-Shimura isomorphisms for Fuchsian groups of the first kind.
The following assertion may be regarded as a generalization of that result.

Theorem 6.8

(cf. Theorem
B
)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}
be a
G^{\odot}
-oper classified by

,
the decomposition

156

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}}\cdot(-,-)_{\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}_{\odot}}
(cf. (
135

-vector space

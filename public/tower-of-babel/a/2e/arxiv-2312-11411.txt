Foliated structure of weak nearly Sasakian manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Weak contact metric manifolds, i.e., the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor, defined by the author and R. Wolak, allowed a new look at the theory of contact manifolds. In this paper we study the new structure of this type, called the weak nearly Sasakian structure. We find conditions that are satisfied by almost contact manifolds and under which the contact distribution is curvature invariant and the weak nearly Sasakian structure foliates into two types of totally geodesic foliations. Our main result generalizes the theorem by B. Cappelletti-Montano and G. Dileo (2016) about the foliated structure of nearly Sasakian manifolds. Keywords : weak nearly Sasakian manifold, Killing vector field, Riemannian curvature, totally geodesic foliation. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C15, 53C25, 53D15

Introduction

Riemannian contact geometry plays an important role in both modern mathematics and mechanics in explaining physical processes.
A popular class of almost contact metric manifolds
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
consists of Sasakian manifolds characterized by the equality, see
[
2
]
,

(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y=g(X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)X,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

Here,
g
is a Riemannian metric,
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection,

\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor,
\xi
is a Reeb vector field and
\eta
is a 1-form, satisfying
\eta(\xi)=1
and

g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

and
\mathfrak{X}_{M}
is the space of vector fields on
M
.
D. Blair, D. Showers and Y. Komatu
[
3
]
defined nearly Sasakian structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)

using a similar condition for the symmetric part of
\nabla\varphi
:

(\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)Y=g(Y,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)Y,\quad Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

(1)

and showed that a normal nearly Sasakian structure is Sasakian and hence is contact.
Any 3-dimensional nearly Sasakian manifold is Sasakian, see
[
8
]
,
and any 5-dimensional nearly Sasakian manifold has Einstein metric of positive scalar curvature, see
[
4
]
;
an example is a sphere
S^{\,5}
with the almost contact metric structure induced by the almost Hermitian structure of
S^{\,6}
.

The Reeb vector field
\xi
of a nearly Sasakian structure is a unit Killing vector field (an infinitesimal generator of isometries or symmetries).
The influence of constant-length Killing vector fields on the Riemannian geometry has been studied by many authors, e.g.,
[
1
]
.

B. Cappelletti-Montano and G. Dileo proved in
[
4
]
that nearly Sasakian manifolds foliate into two types of
totally geodesic foliations.
Totally geodesic foliations (e.g., fibrations or submersions with totally geodesic fibers)
of Riemannian manifolds have the simplest extrinsic geometry of the leaves and appear
as kernels of degenerate tensors, e.g.,
[
14
,
15
]
.
A.D. Nicola, G. Dileo and I. Yudin in
[
6
]
, using results of
[
4
,
7
]
,
gave a criterion for the almost contact metric structure to be Sasakian; namely,
that every nearly Sasakian manifold of dimension greater than 5 is Sasakian.

In
[
9
,
10
,
11
]

and
[
15
, Section 5.3.8]
, we introduced and studies metric structures on a smooth manifold that generalize the almost contact, Sasakian, etc. metric structures.
These so-called “weak” structures (the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor)
made it possible to take a new look at the classical structures and find new applications.
In
[
12
]
we investigated new structures of this type, called weak nearly Sasakian structure and weak nearly cosymplectic structure.

In this paper we continue our study of the weak nearly Sasakian structure.
We find conditions (
5
) and (
6
) that are satisfied by almost contact manifolds and under which
the Reeb vector field is Killing, the contact distribution is curvature invariant and the weak nearly Sasakian structure foliates into two types of
totally geodesic foliations.
Section
2
, following the introductory Section
1
, reviews the basics of weak almost contact manifolds.
Section
3
contains auxiliary results on the geometry of weak almost Sasakian structure;
in particular, we find the expression of the Ricci tensor in the
\xi
-direction
and show that the contact distribution
\ker\eta
is curvature invariant under conditions (
5
) and (
6
).
In Section
4
, we prove that a weak almost Sasakian manifold satisfying (
5
) and (
6
), is foliated.
Our main result (Theorem
1
) generalizes Theorems 3.3 and 3.5 of
[
4
]
.
In Section
5
, using the approach of
[
13
]
we prove an auxiliary proposition necessary for the main result.
Our proofs use the properties of new tensors, as well as classical constructions.

Preliminaries

A
weak almost contact structure
on a smooth manifold
M^{\,2n+1}\ (n\geq 1)
is a set
(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta)
,
where
\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor,
\xi
is a vector field (called Reeb vector field),
\eta
is a
1-form and
Q
is a nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor on
TM
, satisfying, see
[
9
,
10
]
,

\varphi^{2}=-Q+\eta\otimes\xi,\quad\eta(\xi)=1,\quad Q\,\xi=\xi.

(2)

By (
2
),
\eta
defines a smooth
2n
-dimensional distribution
\ker\eta
.
We assume that
\ker\eta
is
\varphi
-invariant, i.e.,
\varphi(\ker\eta)\subset\ker\eta

(as in the classical theory
[
2
]
, where
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}
).
By this and (
2
),
\ker\eta
is
Q
-invariant, i.e.,
Q(\ker\eta)\subset\ker\eta
,
and the following is true:

\varphi\,\xi=0,\quad\eta\circ\varphi=0,\quad\eta\circ Q=\eta,\quad[Q,\,\varphi]:={Q}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ{Q}=0.

A “small” (1,1)-tensor
\widetilde{Q}=Q-{\rm id}_{\,TM}
is a measure
of the difference between a weak almost contact structure and an almost contact one.
Note that

[\widetilde{Q},\varphi]:=\widetilde{Q}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ\widetilde{Q}=0,\quad\eta\circ\widetilde{Q}=0,\quad\widetilde{Q}\,\xi=0.

If there is a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
such that

\displaystyle g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

(3)

then
(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
is called a
weak almost contact metric structure
on
M
.
A weak almost contact manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta)
endowed with a compatible Riemannian metric
g
is called a
weak almost contact metric manifold
and is denoted by
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
.

For a weak almost contact metric structure,
\varphi
is skew-symmetric and
Q
is self-adjoint.
Taking covariant derivative of the equality
g(\varphi Y,Z)=-g(Y,\varphi Z)
, we see that
\nabla_{X}\,\varphi
is skew-symmetric:

g((\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y,Z)=-g((\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Z,Y).

Setting
Y=\xi
in (
3
), we get
\eta(X)=g(\xi,X)
, as in the classical theory.
By (
3
),

g(X,Q\,X)=g(\varphi X,\varphi X)>0

is true for any nonzero vector
X\in\ker\eta
; thus, the tensor
Q
is positive definite.

A
weak contact metric structure
is a weak almost contact metric structure satisfying

d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

where the exterior derivative
d\eta
of
\eta
is given by (for example,
[
2
]
)

\displaystyle d\eta(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\,\{X(\eta(Y))-Y(\eta(X))-\eta([X,Y])\}.

A 1-form
\eta
on a manifold
M^{\,2n+1}
is said to be
contact
if
\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{n}\neq 0
, e.g.,
[
2
]
.
Recall that for a weak contact metric structure
(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
, the 1-form
\eta
is contact, see
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Definition 1

(see
[
12
]
)
.

A weak almost contact metric manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
is called
weak nearly Sasakian
if
(
1
) is true, or, equivalently,

(\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)Z+(\nabla_{Z}\,\varphi)Y=2\,g(Y,Z)\,\xi-\eta(Z)Y-\eta(Y)Z,\qquad Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

(4)

In addition to (
4
), the following two conditions for weak almost contact manifolds that are automatically satisfied by almost contact manifolds (
\widetilde{Q}=0
)
play the key role in this paper:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})\,Y=0,\quad X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},\ Y\in\ker\eta,

(5)

\displaystyle R_{\widetilde{Q}X,Y}Z\in\ker\eta,\quad X,Y,Z\in\ker\eta,

(6)

where the curvature tensor
R
is given by, e.g.,
[
5
]
,

R_{{X},{Y}}Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,\quad X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

By (
6
) and the first Bianchi identity, e.g.,
[
5
]
:

R_{\,X,Y}\,\widetilde{Q}Z\in\ker\eta
for all
X,Y,Z\in\ker\eta
.

Example 1
.

Let
M(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
be a three-dimensional weak nearly Sasakian manifold with the condition (
5
).
By (
2
), the symmetric tensor
Q
has on the plane field
\ker\eta
the form
\lambda\,{\rm id}_{\,\ker\eta}
for some positive
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
It was shown in
[
12
]
that this structure is reduced to the nearly Sasakian structure
(\tilde{\varphi},\xi,\eta,\tilde{g})
on
M
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}=\lambda^{\,-\frac{1}{2}}\,\varphi,\quad\tilde{g}|_{\,\ker\eta}=\lambda^{\,\frac{1}{2}}\,g|_{\,\ker\eta},\quad\tilde{g}(\xi,\,\cdot)={g}(\xi,\,\cdot).

Since
\dim M=3
, the
structure
(\tilde{\varphi},\xi,\eta,\tilde{g})
is Sasakian, see
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Using the equalities
(\nabla_{\xi}\,\varphi)\,\xi=0
and
\varphi\,\xi=0
for a weak nearly Sasakian manifold, we conclude that
\xi
is a geodesic vector field (
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
).
Recall
[
12
]
that on a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with the property (

) the unit vector field
\xi
is Killing (
\pounds_{\xi}\,g=0

[

Therefore,
\xi
-curves determine a Riemannian geodesic foliation.
Note that
-\nabla\xi
is its splitting operator (or, co-nullity tensor), see
[
15
, Section 1.3.1]
.
Moreover,
\eta
is a Killing 1-form:

(\nabla_{X}\,\eta)(X)=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
Using (
5
) and
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
, we obtain

\nabla_{\xi}\,\widetilde{Q}=0.

(7)

For a Riemannian manifold equipped with a Killing vector field
{\xi}
, we get, see
[
16
]

\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\,{\xi}-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}\,{\xi}=R_{\,X,\,{\xi}}\,Y.

(8)

Auxiliary results

In this section we generalize some properties of almost Sasakian manifolds to the case of weak almost Sasakian manifolds
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
with the conditions (
5
) and (
6

Define a (1,1)-tensor field
h
on
M
, as in the classical case, e.g.,
[
4
]
,

h=\nabla\xi+\varphi.

(9)

We get
\eta\circ h=0
and
h(\ker\eta)\subset\ker\eta
. Since
\xi
is a geodesic vector field,
we also get

h\,\xi=0.

(10)

Since
\xi
is a Killing vector field and
\varphi
is skew-symmetric, the tensor

is skew-symmetric:

g(hX,\,X)=g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi,X)+g(\varphi X,X)=\frac{1}{2}\,(\pounds_{\xi}\,g)(X,X)=0,

and
\nabla_{X}\,\eta=g((h-\varphi)X,\,\cdot)
is true.
The distribution
\ker\eta
is integrable,
[X,Y]\in\ker\eta\ (X,Y\in\ker\eta)
, if and only if

:

g([X,Y],\xi)=2\,g((h-\varphi)Y,X),\quad X,Y\in\ker\eta;

and in this case, our manifold splits along
\xi
and
\ker\eta
(is locally the metric product).

Lemma 1
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,h)\,\xi=-h(h-\varphi)X,

(11)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)\,\xi=-\varphi(h-\varphi)X,

(12)

\displaystyle h\,\varphi+\varphi\,h=-2\,\widetilde{Q},

(13)

\displaystyle h\,Q=Q\,h\quad(h\ {\rm commutes\ with}\ Q).

(14)

Moreover,

\displaystyle h^{2}\varphi=\varphi h^{2},\quad h\varphi^{2}=\varphi^{2}h,\quad h^{2}\varphi^{2}=\varphi^{2}h^{2}.

(15)

Proof.

Differentiating the equality
h\,\xi=0
and using the definition (
9
), we get (

):

0=\nabla_{X}\,(h\,\xi)=(\nabla_{X}\,h)\,\xi+h(\nabla_{X}\,\xi)=(\nabla_{X}\,h)\,\xi+h(h-\varphi)X.

Using

(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)\,\xi=-\varphi(\nabla_{X}\,\xi)=-\varphi(h-\varphi)X.

Differentiating the equality

Summing this with the equality

g((\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)X,\xi)+g(\varphi\,X,(h-\varphi)Y)=0

and applying (

By (

) and (

\eta\circ h=0
and
[\widetilde{Q},\varphi]=0
, we get (

Using (

∎

Main results

Here, we prove that weak nearly Sasakian manifolds with conditions (
5
) and (
6
) have a foliated structure.
First, we will generalize Lemma 2.1 in
[
8
]
,
which characterizes Sasakian manifolds among nearly Sasakian manifolds by the condition
h=0
.

Proposition 2
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with the property (
5
), the equality
h=0
holds if and only if the manifold is Sasakian.

Proof.

Let
h=0
, i.e.,
\varphi=-\nabla\xi
, see (
9
).
For every vector fields
X,Y
in
\ker\eta
we have

2\,d\eta(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi,Y)-g(\nabla_{Y}\,\xi,X)=2\,g(X,\varphi Y),

thus, our manifold is contact.
From (
13
) we find
\widetilde{Q}=0
, thus our manifold is nearly Sasakian.
Finally, by Lemma
2
and the Bianchi identity, we obtain

R_{\,X,Y}\,\xi=R_{\,\xi,Y}X-R_{\,\xi,X}Y=\eta(X)\,\varphi^{2}Y-\eta(Y)\,\varphi^{2}X=\eta(Y)\,X-\eta(X)\,Y

for all
X,Y\in\ker\eta
.
Thus,
by
[
2
, Proposition 7.6]
,
our manifold is Sasakian.
∎

The following proposition generalizes

Proposition 3
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with conditions (

) and (

),
the eigenvalues (and their multiplicities) of the symmetric operator

are constant.

Proof.

From (

), (

) and Lemma

\displaystyle\quad(\nabla_{X}\,h^{2})Y=h(\nabla_{X}\,h)Y+(\nabla_{X}\,h)hY

\displaystyle=h(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y+(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)hY-\eta(Y)h\,(h-\varphi)^{2}X+g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,hY)\,\xi

\displaystyle=-\varphi(\nabla_{X}\,h)Y-(\nabla_{X}\,h)\,\varphi Y-2(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})Y-\eta(Y)h\,(h-\varphi)^{2}X+g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,hY)\,\xi

\displaystyle=-\varphi(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y+\eta(Y)\varphi(h-\varphi)^{2}X-(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)\,\varphi Y-g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,\varphi Y)\,\xi

\displaystyle\quad-2(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})Y-\eta(Y)h\,(h-\varphi)^{2}X+g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,hY)\,\xi

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})Y-g((h-\varphi)X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)(h-\varphi)X+\eta(Y)\varphi(h-\varphi)^{2}X

\displaystyle\quad-g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,\varphi Y)\,\xi-2\,(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})Y+g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,hY)\,\xi-\eta(Y)\,h(h-\varphi)^{2}X

\displaystyle=-g\big((h-\varphi)(h^{2}+\widetilde{Q})X,Y\big)\,\xi-\eta(Y)(h-\varphi)(h^{2}+\widetilde{Q})X-(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})Y.

Applying (

\displaystyle 0=g((\nabla_{X}\,h^{2})Y,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\,(h^{2}Y),Y)-g(h^{2}(\nabla_{X}\,Y),Y)

Since

is skew-symmetric, the nonzero eigenvalues of

By Proposition
3
, the spectrum of the self-adjoint operator

Spec(h^{2})=\{0,-\lambda_{1}^{2},\ldots-\lambda_{r}^{2}\},

6
Conclusions

We have shown that the weak nearly Sasakian structure is useful for studying contact metric structures and totally geodesic foliations.
Some well known results on nearly Sasakian manifolds were extended
to weak nearly Sasakian manifolds with the conditions (
5
) and (
6
),
and our main result is that such weak nearly Sasakian manifolds have a foliated structure with two types of totally geodesic foliations.
Motivated by a criterion for an almost contact metric manifold to be Sasakian in
[
6
]
, we pose the following question:
are weak nearly Sasakian manifolds of dimension higher than five, satisfying the conditions (
5
) and (
6
), Sasakian manifolds?
Based on the numerous applications of the nearly Sasakian structure, we expect that certain weak structure will also be useful for geometry and physics,
e.g., in twistor string theory.

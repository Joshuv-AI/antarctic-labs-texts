Axial transition form factors of octet baryons in the perturbative chiral quark model
X. Y. Liu, A. Limphirat, K. Xu, Z. Zhao, K. Khosonthongkee, Y. Yan

Abstract
We study the axial transition form factors G A B → B ′ ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2}) as well as the axial charges g A B → B ′ g_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}} of the octet baryons in the perturbative chiral quark model (PCQM) with including both the ground and excited states in the intermediate quark propagators. The PCQM results on the G A B → B ′ ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2}) and the g A B → B ′ g_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}} are found in good agreement with the existing experimental data and the lattice-QCD values. The study figures out that the G A B → B ′ ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2}) for all transitions behave in the dipolelike form, which is dominantly caused by the three-quark core. The meson cloud with the ground-state quark propagator also plays an extremely important role but results in a flat contribution. The excited-state quark propagator contributing to the G A B → B ′ ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2}) could be regarded as the higher order correction and it is very limited.

II
Axial transition form factors in the PCQM

In the PCQM, the axial transition form factors
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
for the baryon semileptonic decays
B^{\prime}\to Bl\bar{\nu}
are identified in the Breit frame with the following perturbative expression:

\displaystyle\chi

{}_{B^{\prime}_{s}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\frac{\vec{\sigma}}{2}\chi_{B_{s}}G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})

(1)

\displaystyle=

\,{}^{B^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{0}|\sum_{n=0}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{n}}{n!}\int\delta(t)d^{4}xd^{4}x_{1}\cdots d^{4}x_{n}e^{-\mathrm{i}q\cdot x}

\displaystyle\times T[\mathcal{L}_{I}^{W}(x_{1})\cdots\mathcal{L}_{I}^{W}(x_{n})\vec{A}_{+}(x)]|\phi_{0}\rangle^{B},

where
\chi_{B_{s}}
and
\chi^{\dagger}_{B^{\prime}_{s}}
are the baryon spin wave functions in the initial and final states, and
\vec{\sigma}
is the baryon spin matrix. The state vector
|\phi_{0}\rangle^{B}
corresponds to the unperturbed three-quark states projected onto the respective baryon states, which are constructed in the framework of the
SU(6)
spin flavor and
SU(3)
color symmetry.

In the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
), the quark-meson interaction Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{I}^{W}(x)
is taken in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{I}^{W}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2F}\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{i}(x)\bar{\psi}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\lambda^{i}\psi(x)

(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{f_{ijk}}{4F^{2}}\Phi_{i}(x)\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{j}(x)\bar{\psi}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\lambda_{k}\psi(x),

where
F=88
MeV is the pion decay constant,
\Phi_{i}
are the octet meson fields, and
\psi(x)
is the triplet of the
u
,
d
, and
s
quark fields could be expanded as

\psi(x)=\sum_{\alpha}\left(b_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}(\vec{x})\,e^{-iE_{\alpha}t}+d^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\upsilon_{\alpha}(\vec{x})e^{iE_{\alpha}t}\right),

(3)

where
b_{\alpha}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\alpha}
are the single quark
annihilation and antiquark creation operators, and
u_{\alpha}
and
\nu_{\beta}
are the set of quark and antiquark wave functions in orbits
\alpha
and
\beta
. Generally, the quark wave functions
u_{\alpha}(\vec{x})
may be expressed as

u_{\alpha}(\vec{x})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{\alpha}(r)\\
\large{i\vec{\sigma}\cdot\hat{x}f_{\alpha}(r)}\end{array}\right)\chi_{s}\chi_{f}\chi_{c}.

(4)

Here,
g_{\alpha}(r)
and
f_{\alpha}(r)
are respectively the upper and lower components of the radial wave functions for a single valence quark.

The term
A^{\mu}_{+}
in Eq. (
1
) refers to the axial-vector current. For the transition with the
d\leftrightarrow u
, we label it as the variation of the isospin
\Delta I=1
,

\displaystyle A_{+}^{\mu}|^{\Delta I=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\frac{\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}}{4}\psi-\frac{f_{1ij}+if_{2ij}}{4F}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\lambda_{i}\psi\Phi_{j}

(5)

\displaystyle+\bar{\psi}(\hat{Z}-1)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\frac{\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}}{4}\psi+o(\Phi_{i}^{2}),

while the transition with the
u\leftrightarrow s
is labeled as the variation of the strangeness
\Delta S=1
,

\displaystyle A_{+}^{\mu}|^{\Delta S=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\frac{\lambda_{4}+i\lambda_{5}}{4}\psi-\frac{f_{4ij}+if_{5ij}}{4F}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\lambda_{i}\psi\Phi_{j}

(6)

\displaystyle+\bar{\psi}(\hat{Z}-1)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\frac{\lambda_{4}+i\lambda_{5}}{4}\psi+o(\Phi_{i}^{2}),

The renormalization constant
\hat{Z}
in Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) is determined by the nucleon charge conservation condition as

\hat{Z}=1-\frac{1}{3(4\pi F)^{2}}\sum_{\Phi,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\frac{a_{\Phi}k^{4}\mathcal{F}_{\Phi NN}^{\alpha}(k)}{\omega_{\Phi}(k^{2})\big[\omega_{\Phi}(k^{2})+\Delta E_{\alpha}\big]^{2}},

(7)

with
\omega_{\Phi}(k^{2})=\sqrt{M_{\Phi}^{2}+k^{2}}
, the constant
a_{\pi}=9
,
a_{K}=6
and
a_{\eta}=1
. In Eq. (
7
),
\Delta E_{\alpha}=E_{\alpha}-E_{0}
is the energy shift of a single quark, and

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\mathcal{F}_{\Phi NN}^{\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{I\alpha}(k)F_{I\alpha}^{\dagger}(k)-2\omega_{\Phi}(k^{2})F_{I\alpha}(k)F_{II\alpha}^{\dagger}(k)

(8)

\displaystyle+\omega_{\Phi}^{2}(k^{2})F_{II\alpha}(k)F_{II\alpha}^{\dagger}(k),

\displaystyle F_{I\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}\int_{\Omega}d\Omega\Big[g_{0}(r)g_{\alpha}(r)+f_{0}(r)f_{\alpha}(r)\cos 2\theta\Big]

(9)

\displaystyle\times e^{ikr\cos\theta}C_{\alpha}Y_{l_{\alpha}0}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle\hskip-284.52756ptF_{II\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}\Big[g_{0}(r)f_{\alpha}(r)-g_{\alpha}(r)f_{0}(r)\Big]

(10)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Omega}d\Omega\cos\theta e^{ikr\cos\theta}C_{\alpha}Y_{l_{\alpha}0}(\theta,\phi).

Here, we define
g_{0}(r)=g_{1s_{1/2}}(r)C_{0}Y_{00}(\theta,\phi)
and
f_{0}(r)=f_{1s_{1/2}}(r)C_{0}Y_{00}(\theta,\phi)
. The label
\alpha=(nl_{\alpha}jm)
in the above equations characterizes the quark state.
C_{\alpha}
in Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) are the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients
C_{\alpha}=\langle l_{\alpha}0\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}|j\frac{1}{2}\rangle
and
Y_{l_{\alpha}0}(\theta,\phi)
is the usual spherical harmonics with
l_{\alpha}
being the orbital quantum numbers of the intermediate states
\alpha
. The derivations on the renormalization constant
\hat{Z}
could be found in Refs.
Lyubovitskij et al. 2001a
;
Lyubovitskij et al. 2001b
.

(11)

G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})\big|_{CT}=(\hat{Z}-1)G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})\big|_{LO}.

(12)

(c) Self-energy I (SE:I) diagram

\displaystyle G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})\big|_{SE:I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{(4\pi F)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\int_{-1}^{1}dxk^{4}\mathcal{F}_{\Phi\alpha}^{BB^{\prime}}(k)

(13)

\displaystyle\times\big[(1-x^{2})F_{III\alpha}(k_{-})

\displaystyle+(\frac{Qx}{k}+1-2x^{2})F_{IV\alpha}(k_{-})\big],

where
k_{-}=\sqrt{k^{2}+Q^{2}-2k\sqrt{Q^{2}}x}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Phi\alpha}^{BB^{\prime}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}^{BB^{\prime}}(\omega_{\pi}(k^{2})F_{II\alpha}(k)-F_{I\alpha}(k))}{\omega_{\pi}(k^{2})(\omega_{\pi}(k^{2})+\Delta E_{\alpha})}

(14)

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{3}^{BB^{\prime}}(\omega_{K}(k^{2})F_{II\alpha}(k)-F_{I\alpha}(k))}{\omega_{K}(k^{2})(\omega_{K}(k^{2})+\Delta E_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{4}^{BB^{\prime}}(\omega_{\eta}(k^{2})F_{II\alpha}(k)-F_{I\alpha}(k))}{\omega_{\eta}(k^{2})(\omega_{\eta}(k^{2})+\Delta E_{\alpha})},

\displaystyle F_{III\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}g_{0}(r)f_{\alpha}(r)\int_{\Omega}d\Omega\cos\theta

(15)

\displaystyle\times e^{ikr\cos\theta}C_{\alpha}Y_{l_{\alpha}0}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle F_{IV\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}drr^{2}[g_{\alpha}(r)f_{0}(r)-g_{0}(r)f_{\alpha}(r)]

(16)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Omega}d\Omega\cos\theta e^{ikr\cos\theta}C_{\alpha}Y_{l_{\alpha}0}(\theta,\phi).

(d) Self-energy II (SE:II) diagram

\displaystyle G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})\big|_{SE:II}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})\big|_{SE:I},

(17)

since the SE I and SE II diagrams obey the T symmetry.

IV
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we have investigated and predicted the axial transition form factor
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
and the axial transition charges
g_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}
in the framework of the PCQM for the various octet baryon transitions, such as
n\to p
,
\Sigma^{-}\to\Sigma^{0}
,
\Xi^{-}\to\Xi^{0}
,
\Sigma^{+}\to\Lambda
,
\Sigma^{-}\to n
,
\Sigma^{0}\to p
,
\Xi^{-}\to\Sigma^{0}
,
\Xi^{0}\to\Sigma^{+}
,
\Xi^{-}\to\Lambda
and
\Lambda\to p
. The calculations are extended to the SU(3) flavor symmetry, and include both the ground and excited states in the intermediate quark propagators. The ground and excited quark wave functions have been derived in our previous works by solving the Dirac equation with the Cornell-like potential numerically. Therefore, there is not any free parameter in the present work.

In summary, one may conclude that the PCQM results on the
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
and the
g_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}
agree well with the existing experimental data and the lattice-QCD estimates. The study shows clearly that the
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
for all the
\Delta I=1
and
\Delta S=1
transitions behave a dipolelike form and stem mainly from the 3q-core. The meson cloud with the ground-state quark propagator plays an extremely important role in the
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
, while the excited-state quark propagator contributing to the
G_{A}^{B\to B^{\prime}}(Q^{2})
could be regarded as the higher order corrections and it is very limited. The study also reveals that the sea quarks distribute in a very small region, as indicated by the flat contribution from the meson cloud.

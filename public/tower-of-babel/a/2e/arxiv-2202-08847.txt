Jet broadening in dense inhomogeneous matter
João Barata, Andrey V. Sadofyev, Carlos A. Salgado

Abstract
In this work, we study the jet momentum broadening in an inhomogeneous dense QCD medium. The transverse profile of this nuclear matter is described within a gradient expansion, and we focus on the leading gradient contributions. The leading parton is allowed to interact multiple times with the background through the soft gluon exchanges. We derive the associated final particle distribution using both the GLV opacity series and the BDMPS-Z formalism. We further discuss the modified factorization of the broadening process and the initial distribution of partons produced in a hard scattering, as well as its consequences for phenomenological applications in the context of heavy-ion collisions and deep inelastic scattering. Finally, we present the broadening probability (describing the final state effects) in several limiting regimes, and give its numerical estimates for phenomenologically motivated sets of parameters.

Introduction

Jets are collimated sprays of particles, produced by hadronization and branching of an energetic quark or gluon (parton), which are often found in the final state of experiments on ultrarelativistic particle collisions. If before the hadronization stage the parton cascade develops in the presence of an underlying medium, produced in the same collision, the jet substructure gets modified due to the interactions with the background. The simplest manifestation of this process is the suppression of jet energy by matter, commonly referred to as jet quenching, which has attracted significant attention in the literature

III
The BDMPS-Z Formalism

In this section, we re-derive the leading gradient effects on the jet momentum broadening within the BDMPS-Z approach. From a practical point of view, in this formalism the resummation of multiple field insertions is first performed at the amplitude level by constructing the dressed in-medium propagator. We obtain the in-medium propagator for an inhomogeneous medium, including the leading gradient contributions.

Since the interactions with the medium are dominated by tree-level gluon exchanges, the dynamics of the background field is dominated by the classical Yang-Mills equations, and it can be treated as a stochastic variable. In the BDMPS-Z approach, it is typically assumed that the statistics of the field take a white-noise form due to the large number of uncorrelated degrees of freedom in the medium, analogous to the McLerran-Venugopalan model
Mehtar-Tani:2006vpj
;
McLerran:1993ka
;
McLerran:1993ni
. This is equivalent to the assumption used in the previous section that only pairwise averages are non-negligible.

Thus, we again start with a model for the in-medium color field. The model commonly used in the BDMPS-Z approach can be conveniently summarized with

\displaystyle gA_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mu a}(q)=(2\pi)\,g^{\mu 0}\,v^{a}(q)\>\delta\left(q^{0}\right)\,,

(17)

where the scattering potential should be set to
v^{a}(q)=\sum_{j}e^{-i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{x}_{j}+q_{z}z_{j})}\,t_{j}^{a}\,v_{j}(q)
to coincide with the GW model used in the previous section. It should be also noticed that in general the individual potentials of the scattering centers can be left unspecified in both approaches, although one should be careful treating the DB interactions.

z
-dependent medium profile can be readily obtained. After a straightforward generalization of the derivation in the longitudinally homogeneous case, one finds

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}\simeq\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{L}\,d\tau\,\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x},\tau\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\left\{\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right.\left[1-\frac{i}{E}\int\limits_{0}^{L}\,d\tau\,\bm{\nabla}\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x},\tau\right)\cdot\left(\int\limits_{L}^{\tau}\,d\zeta\,\int\limits_{0}^{\zeta}\,d\xi+(L-\tau)\int\limits^{L}_{0}\,d\xi\right)\hat{\bm{g}}(\xi)\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x},\xi\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{i}{E}\int\limits_{0}^{L}d\zeta\int\limits^{\zeta}_{0}\,d\xi\,\hat{\bm{g}}\left(\xi\right)\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x},\xi\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}\left.\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right\}\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}\,,

(42)

where
\hat{\bm{g}}\left(\tau\right)=\left(\bm{\nabla}\rho(\tau)\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}+\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}(\tau)\frac{\delta}{\delta\mu^{2}}\right)
.

IV
The Final State Distribution and Its Properties

In this section, we proceed to discuss the properties of the final jet momentum distribution given by (
II
) (or equivalently (
III
)). Heuristically, the leading effect of the matter gradients is that the broadening becomes anisotropic, and the final jet momentum distribution is direction dependent, see Fig.
3
.
In other words, propagating through an inhomogeneous matter, probes pick up an additional transverse momentum, and even on average it is non-zero due to the non-trivial matter structure.
The modifications to the jet structure are thus qualitatively different from the ones observed in the case of a homogeneous background. In this section, we will analyze the properties of the distribution (
II
) and illustrate our results with simple numerical estimates for the broadening probability, using phenomenologically relevant parameters.

An illustration of a single parton evolving in the presence of an inhomogeneous static slab of matter. The initial jet direction gets modified due to the presence of the temperature gradient. This modification depends on the angle
\theta

\bm{\nabla}T

Let us first notice that the broadening of the jet distribution has to be unitary – at a fixed energy, the number of jets (partons) cannot be changed and the in-medium propagation results only in a reshuffling of the underlying momentum modes.
To check that, one can consider the value of the
\bm{x}
-space distribution at
\bm{x}={\bm{0}}
, which corresponds to the volume integral of the momentum-space distribution.
At this point, the
\bm{x}
-space dipole potential is zero
\mathcal{V}(\bm{0})=0
as well as its transverse gradient
\bm{\nabla}\mathcal{V}(\bm{0})={\bm{0}}
, and the full distribution (
I
) is equal to
\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,\frac{dN^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{p}\,dE}
. Thus, the unitarity condition is satisfied by the broadened jet momentum distribution even in an inhomogeneous medium

\displaystyle\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{d^{2}\bm{p}dE}=\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}\Bigg|_{{\bm{x}}=0}=\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}\Bigg|_{{\bm{x}}=0}=\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{p}dE}\,.

(43)

One could also notice that in (
III
) the initial distribution is not fully factorized from probe-medium interactions. Indeed, in the uniform case, the
\bm{x}
-space form of the final jet distribution can be represented as a product of the initial distribution with an interaction factor. The gradient effects in turn enter not only through the probe-medium interactions, but also through a shift of the argument in the initial hard distribution
\frac{dN^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{p}\,dE}
. Thus, the relation between the final and initial distributions involves an operator

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}

\displaystyle={\cal P}\left(\bm{x}\right)\hat{\mathsf{S}}\left(\bm{x}\right)\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}\,,

(44)

where

\displaystyle{\cal P}({\bm{x}})=\exp\left\{-\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)L\right\}\left[1-\frac{iL^{3}}{6E}\bm{\nabla}\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\cdot\hat{\bm{g}}\,\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\right]

is a transform of
{\cal P}({\bm{p}})
, the (broadening) probability for a particle of energy
E
to acquire transverse momentum
{\bm{p}}
due to propagation in the medium for a distance
L
, and

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{S}}\left(\bm{x}\right)=1+\frac{iL^{2}}{2E}\,\hat{\bm{g}}\,\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}\simeq\exp\left\{\frac{iL^{2}}{2E}\,\hat{\bm{g}}\,\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}\right\}

is a shift operator being an identity operator in the absence of gradients. One can immediately see that
{\cal P}({\bm{p}})
is self-normalized, and that
\hat{\mathsf{S}}\left(\bm{x}\right)
does not change particle number at a given fixed energy.

It is instructive to compare the resummed final state distribution to the one obtained at the first order in opacity by evaluating the leading moments:

\displaystyle\left\langle F(\bm{p})\right\rangle=\frac{\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,F(\bm{p})\,\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{d^{2}\bm{p}dE}}{\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{p}dE}}\,,

(45)

where
F(\bm{p})
is an arbitrary function. Following
Sadofyev:2021ohn
, we focus on a Gaussian initial distribution

\displaystyle E\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{p}\,dE}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(E)}{2\pi w^{2}}e^{-\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{2w^{2}}}\,,

(46)

where
f(E)
is an unspecified energy dependence, and
w
is the characteristic width. We also assume that the matter is uniform in the longitudinal direction. One generally expects that the terms linear in gradients cannot modify the scalar moments, so
\langle p_{\perp}^{2k}\rangle=\langle p_{\perp}^{2k}\rangle_{\nabla\rho=\nabla\mu^{2}={\bm{0}}}
, and we may focus on the odd moments
\langle{\bm{p}}\>p_{\perp}^{2k}\rangle
sensitive to the anisotropy
7
7

7

For an earlier attempt to study the matter anisotropy with directional jet quenching, see
Majumder:2006wi
.
, see e.g.
Sadofyev:2021ohn
;
Antiporda:2021hpk
.

Starting with the simplest case of the averaged momentum
\left\langle\bm{p}\right\rangle
at the given energy,
we find it convenient to use the opacity expanded form of the distribution (
II
) rather than its resummed form. Doing so, one finds that the moment is controlled by the following integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi w^{2}}\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,p^{\alpha}\,p_{m}^{\beta}\,e^{-\frac{p_{in\perp}^{2}}{2w^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\alpha\beta}w^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{\alpha}_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}q^{\beta}_{j}\,,

(47)

where
\bm{p}_{n}=\bm{p}_{f}-\sum\limits_{m=n}^{N}\bm{q}_{m}
,
\bm{p}_{in}=\bm{p}_{1}
, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are the indices in the transverse 2D space. We also notice that in the absence of the gradients the averaged momentum is zero due to the lack of a preferred direction in the problem, and the corresponding part of (
II
) gives zero. Thus, for the contribution at the
N
th order in opacity, we find

\displaystyle\left\langle p^{\alpha}\right\rangle^{(N)}

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{N+1}}{E\,(N+1)!}\prod\displaylimits_{n=1}^{N}\left[\int\frac{d^{2}q_{n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\right]\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{\alpha}_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}q^{\beta}_{j}\right)\sum_{k=m}^{N}\hat{g}^{\beta}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times(-1)^{N}\mathcal{V}_{1}(\bm{q}_{1})\,...\,\mathcal{V}_{N}(\bm{q}_{N})\,,

(48)

where we have additionally used that
\int\,d^{2}\bm{q}_{n}\,\mathcal{V}_{n}(\bm{q}_{n})=0
removing the
w^{2}
term. For any
N>1
this expression is zero due to the same property of
\mathcal{V}_{n}(\bm{q}_{n})
, since the integrand involves only two powers of
\bm{q}_{i}
, and at least one of the dipole potential averages to zero. In turn, the
N=1
contribution is zero since it involves only the
w^{2}
term. Thus, we find that
\left\langle\bm{p}\right\rangle={\bm{0}}
to all orders in opacity.

The first non-zero moment of the momentum broadening distribution at the first order in opacity is
\left\langle\bm{p}\>p_{\perp}^{2}\right\rangle

Sadofyev:2021ohn
. We can again perform the final momentum averaging, and find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi w^{2}}\int\,d^{2}\bm{p}\,p^{\alpha}\,p_{m}^{\beta}\,p_{\perp}^{2}\,e^{-\frac{\bm{p}_{in}^{2}}{2w^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left[4w^{4}+w^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{q}_{i}\right)^{2}\right]\delta^{\alpha\beta}+4w^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}q^{\beta}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt+2w^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}q^{\beta}_{j}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}q^{\beta}_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{l=1}^{N}\bm{q}_{l}\right)^{2}\,.

(49)

Thus, one can see that the only non-zero contributions may come at
N=1

\displaystyle\left\langle p^{\alpha}\>p_{\perp}^{2}\right\rangle^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{w^{2}L^{2}}{E}\,\hat{g}^{\alpha}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,q_{\perp}^{2}\,\mathcal{V}(\bm{q})\,

(50)

and at
N=2

\displaystyle\left\langle p^{\alpha}\>p_{\perp}^{2}\right\rangle^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{3}}{6E}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,q_{1\perp}^{2}\,\mathcal{V}(\bm{q}_{1})\,\hat{g}^{\alpha}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,q_{2\perp}^{2}\,\mathcal{V}(\bm{q}_{2})\,,

(51)

while the higher orders in opacity decouple. In the case of the GW potential, the terms proportional to
\bm{\nabla}\rho
are divergent, and should be regularized at some scale. Comparing with
Sadofyev:2021ohn
, we set
|\bm{q}_{\text{max}}|=\sqrt{E\mu}
, and find

\displaystyle\left\langle p^{\alpha}\>p_{\perp}^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{w^{2}L^{2}\mu^{2}}{E\,\lambda}\frac{\nabla^{\alpha}\rho}{\rho}\ln\frac{E}{\mu}+\frac{L^{3}\mu^{4}}{6E\,\lambda^{2}}\frac{\nabla^{\alpha}\rho}{\rho}\left(\ln\frac{E}{\mu}\right)^{2}\,,

(52)

where
\lambda=\frac{1}{\rho\sigma_{0}}
with
\sigma_{0}=\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\mathcal{C}\,|v(q_{\perp}^{2})|^{2}
. The first term in the expression above precisely agrees with the result obtained in
Sadofyev:2021ohn
. In turn, the second term is new and indicates that the higher
N
contributions to the transverse momentum moments with integer
k\geq 1
are generally dominating as long as the potential integrals are divergent. One should also notice that the second term is independent of
w
, and can be identified as a purely final state effect (described by the broadening probability
{\cal P}({\bm{p}})
), since in the limit
w\to 0
the initial distribution is constant in coordinate space and not affected by the shift operator
\hat{\mathsf{S}}
. In general, non-zero odd moments are the primary effect of the hydrodynamic gradients on the jet broadening, and they can be used to learn about the medium profile with jets. We leave the moments with general real
k
and their phenomenological implications for future studies.

We now turn back to the partial factorization, and focus on the final state effects. The corresponding portion of (
44
) is the self-normalized broadening probability
{\cal P}(\bm{p})
, which has multiple phenomenological applications
Caucal:2019uvr
;
Blaizot:2013vha
;
Kutak:2018dim
;
Barata:2021byj
;
Soudi:2021aar
. Its Fourier transform can be obtained from (
III
) if we set
E\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{(0)}}{d^{2}\bm{x}dE}
to be a constant in the transverse directions, decoupling in this way the shift operator
\hat{\mathsf{S}}(\bm{x})
:

\displaystyle{\cal P}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}e^{-\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)L}\left[1-\frac{iL^{3}}{6E}\bm{\nabla}\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\cdot\hat{\bm{g}}\mathcal{V}\left(\bm{x}\right)\right]\,.

(53)

At this point one has to specify the particular model for the medium, fixing the dipole potential, and evaluate
{\cal P}(\bm{p})
explicitly. We consider the GW model, and in this case the potential reads

\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})=\frac{\mathcal{C}g^{4}\rho}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\left(1-\mu\,x_{\perp}K_{1}(\mu\,x_{\perp})\right)\,,

(54)

However, even in this simple case and in the absence of gradients, the Fourier transform cannot be written in a closed form, restricting phenomenological applications.

By this reason, it is instructive to consider
{\cal P}(\bm{p})
in some limiting regimes.
If the gradients are zero, then the probability (
53
) is a function of two dimensionless combinations of the parameters and momentum:
\frac{p_{\perp}}{\mu}
and
\chi\equiv\frac{\mathcal{C}g^{4}\rho}{4\pi\mu^{2}}L
, where
\chi
is the opacity in the GW model. The dipole potential
\mathcal{V}(x_{\perp})
tends to a constant at large
\mu\,x_{\perp}
, and the large
x_{\perp}
limit of the integration is controlled by the fast oscillation of the Fourier exponential. For sufficiently large momenta such that
p_{\perp}\gg\mu
, one can expand the dipole potential in powers of
\mu\,x_{\perp}
, and for the GW model it reads

\displaystyle\frac{4L}{\chi}\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})=\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}\log\frac{4e^{1-2\gamma_{E}}}{\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\mu^{4}x^{4}_{\perp}\right)\,,

(55)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler constant. One should notice that the first term in the expansion is common between most of the medium models, since it is fixed by the ultraviolet (UV) Coulomb-like behavior
for large momentum exchanges, see e.g.
Barata:2020sav
;
Barata:2020rdn
;
Djordjevic:2009cr
.

If we further require that
p_{\perp}^{2}\gg\chi\,\mu^{2}
, then the exponential of the dipole potential itself can be expanded, and, after expanding the in-medium potentials, we find

\displaystyle{\cal P}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}\Bigg[1-\mathcal{V}\left(x_{\perp}\right)L+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}^{2}\left(x_{\perp}\right)L^{2}-\frac{iL^{3}}{6E}\bm{\nabla}\mathcal{V}\left(x_{\perp}\right)\cdot\hat{\bm{g}}\mathcal{V}\left(x_{\perp}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\simeq\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}\chi}{p_{\perp}^{4}}+\frac{16\pi\mu^{4}\chi^{2}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\left(\log\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-2\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{4\pi\mu^{4}\chi^{2}L}{3E}\left[\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{\rho}\left(\log\frac{p_{\perp}^{4}}{\mu^{4}}-4\right)-\frac{\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right]\cdot\frac{\bm{p}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\,,

(56)

where we have omitted terms proportional to
\delta^{(2)}(\bm{p})
since the argument is away from zero. The first two terms in this expansion come from the homogeneous case, with the first giving the dominant Coulomb tail expected at large momentum transfers. In turn, the gradient term has an odd (negative) power
of
p_{\perp}
and can only be generated in an inhomogeneous medium. In fact, one can compare the asymptotic structure of (
IV
) directly with the first non-trivial moment
(
52
) in the limit of infinitely narrow source (i.e.
w\to 0
). The only contribution to (
52
) in this limit comes from the
N=2
term, which gets a double Coulomb logarithmic enhancement. Indeed, looking at the last term in (
IV
), one may notice that its dominant contribution to
\langle{\bm{p}}\>p_{\perp}^{2}\rangle
scales as
\left|\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{E\rho}\right|\int dp_{\perp}\frac{\log\,p_{\perp}}{p_{\perp}}
, and with a similar UV regularization it gives
\left|\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{E\rho}\right|\left(\log\frac{E}{\mu}\right)^{2}
in qualitative agreement with the previous result.

Expanding the probability distribution
{\cal P}
in two parameters, one has to pay a particular attention to the applicability of the result. Indeed, if the large momentum expansion parameter
\frac{\mu}{p_{\perp}}
is too small, the gradient contributions could take the leading role. Here we assume that this is not the case, and that the first order gradient correction appearing in the last term of (
IV
) is at least smaller than the dominant Coulomb tail contribution, although it can compete with the second term in the expression.

Another kinematic regime commonly considered in the literature
Barata:2020rdn
;
Caucal:2019uvr
corresponds to the intermediate momentum region
\mu^{2}\ll p_{\perp}^{2}\leq\chi\,\mu^{2}
. In this case, the exponential of the dipole potential cannot be expanded, while the approximation (
55
) can still be utilized. Moreover, the weak logarithmic dependence of the expanded dipole potential on
\bm{x}
can be neglected. Indeed, one can introduce a new scale
\mathcal{Q}^{2}\gg\mu^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{4L}{\chi}\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})\simeq\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}\left(\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{4e^{1-2\gamma_{E}}}{\mathcal{Q}^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}}\right)\simeq\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,.

(57)

In practice, it can be fixed up to an overall coefficient based on phenomenological arguments, see e.g.
Barata:2020rdn
;
Barata:2021wuf
for further discussion. In this regime, the dipole potential is quadratic, and the leading broadening probability (
53
) becomes Gaussian. This is a regime of multiple soft interactions, which is widely used in phenomenological models for jet quenching, see e.g.
Caucal:2019uvr
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2014bpa
;
Blaizot:2013vha
, and often referred to as BDMPS-Z/ASW approximation
Zakharov:1997uu
;
Armesto:2003jh
. Taking into account the gradient effects, we find

\displaystyle{\cal P}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\chi\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}\left[1-\frac{i\mu^{4}\chi^{2}L}{48E}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,x_{\perp}^{2}\,\bm{x}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{\rho}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\frac{4\pi}{\chi\mu^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}\Bigg[1+\frac{L}{6E}\,\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}-2\chi\mu^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}{\chi\mu^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}\,\left(\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}\frac{\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\cdot\bm{p}\Bigg]e^{-\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{\chi\mu^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}}}\,.

(58)

The usual Gaussian result gets an overall
{\bm{p}}
-dependent modulation by a new term containing all the gradient effects. We note that the effect of this contribution should be more important when
p^{2}_{\perp}\sim\chi\mu^{2}\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}
, roughly at the peak of the
p_{\perp}
-distribution
Barata:2020rdn
.
In this region, the leading behavior of
{\cal P}
is fully described neither by a single hard scattering nor by the multiple soft scatterings
, but rather there is a competition between these two regimes. Thus, one may expect a more pronounced deviation from the homogeneous solution in this region. We numerically verify this observation below in Fig.
4
.

Finally, for
\mu^{2}\ll p_{\perp}^{2}\leq\chi\,\mu^{2}
, we can also consider the small logarithmic correction to the dipole potential, neglected in (
57
), as a perturbation

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})\simeq\frac{\chi\mu^{2}}{4L}x_{\perp}^{2}\left(\log\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{4e^{1-2\gamma_{E}}}{\mathcal{Q}^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}}\right)=\mathcal{V}_{0}(x_{\perp})+\delta\mathcal{V}(x_{\perp})\,.

(59)

This approach to capture the leading effects beyond the quadratic Gaussian approximation is known as the improved opacity expansion (IOE), see e.g.
Mehtar-Tani:2019ygg
;
Mehtar-Tani:2019tvy
;
Barata:2020sav
. Substituting the potential into the broadening probability, we write it as an expansion

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\rm IOE}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{0+1}(\bm{p})+{\cal P}_{1+0}(\bm{p})+{\cal P}_{2+0}(\bm{p})+{\cal P}_{1+1}(\bm{p})+{\cal P}_{2+1}(\bm{p})+{\cal P}_{3+1}(\bm{p})+...\,,

(60)

where the first number in the subscript corresponds to the order in
\delta\mathcal{V}
, and the second one counts the order in gradients. The leading contribution
{\cal P}_{0+1}(\bm{p})
is given by (
IV
), the term
{\cal P}_{1+0}(\bm{p})
has been discussed in details in
Barata:2020rdn
, and we have to evaluate the mixed contributions. The full derivation of the sub-leading terms goes beyond the scope of this paper, and we focus on their large momentum regime. That will allow us to see that the terms sub-leading in
\delta\mathcal{V}
give the correct large momentum limit (
IV
) even in the presence of gradients,
c.f.

Barata:2020rdn
.

All the
\bm{x}
-integrals entering (
60
) up to the second order can be obtained from three master integrals if we act on them with the appropriate number of momentum derivatives. They read

\displaystyle I_{1}(\bm{p},a)

\displaystyle=\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}e^{-ax_{\perp}^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{4a}}\,,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\bm{p},a,b)

\displaystyle=\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}e^{-ax_{\perp}^{2}}\,\log\frac{1}{b^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{4a}}\left[Ei\left(\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{4a}\right)-\log\frac{b^{2}p_{\perp}^{2}}{4a^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4\pi}{p_{\perp}^{2}}+\frac{16\pi a}{p_{\perp}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a^{2}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle I_{3}(\bm{p},a,b)

\displaystyle=\int\,d^{2}\bm{x}\,e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}e^{-ax_{\perp}^{2}}\,\log^{2}\frac{1}{b^{2}_{\perp}x_{\perp}^{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{16\pi}{p_{\perp}^{2}}\left(\gamma_{E}+\log\frac{p_{\perp}}{2b}\right)+\frac{64\pi a}{p_{\perp}^{4}}\left(\gamma_{E}-1+\log\frac{p_{\perp}}{2b}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a^{2}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\log\frac{p_{\perp}}{2b}\right)\,,

(61)

where we omit the explicit form of
I_{3}
for brevity. In the large
p_{\perp}
limit, the momentum derivatives acting on a term reduce its contribution removing powers of
p_{\perp}
. Consequently, one can compare the relative importance of the different contributions in (
60
) by counting the powers of
\bm{x}
entering through
\mathcal{V}
and
\delta\mathcal{V}
outside the Gaussian exponential. However, since the structure of the gradient terms in (
53
) involves gradients of the dipole potential, we have to consider higher order terms in
\delta\mathcal{V}
to be sure that we keep all the relevant contributions.

We first notice that the leading term in the IOE expansion
{\cal P}_{0+1}(\bm{p})
is exponentially suppressed for
p_{\perp}^{2}\gg\chi\mu^{2}
, while
{\cal P}_{1+0}(\bm{p})\propto\frac{1}{p_{\perp}^{4}}
gives the leading Coulomb term in this limit. Similarly, the sub-leading non-gradient contribution satisfies
{\cal P}_{2+0}(\bm{p})\propto\frac{1}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\log p_{\perp}
.
The mixed gradient terms in turn scale as
{\cal P}_{1+1}(\bm{p})\propto\frac{1}{p_{\perp}^{5}}
and
{\cal P}_{2+1}(\bm{p})\propto\frac{1}{p_{\perp}^{5}}\log p_{\perp}
, while one can check that the next contribution
{\cal P}_{3+1}(\bm{p})\propto\frac{1}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\log^{2}p_{\perp}
is suppressed both by a higher power of inverse momentum as well as by the gradients, and can be omitted. Finally, we find

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\rm IOE}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}\chi}{p_{\perp}^{4}}+\frac{16\pi\mu^{4}\chi^{2}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\left(\log\frac{p_{\perp}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-2\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{4\pi\mu^{4}\chi^{2}L}{3E}\left[\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{\rho}\left(\log\frac{p_{\perp}^{4}}{\mu^{4}}-4\right)-\frac{\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right]\cdot\frac{\bm{p}}{p_{\perp}^{6}}\,,

(62)

which precisely agrees with the large momentum limit (
IV
), showing that the IOE covers both limits considered above. It could be also noticed that the logarithmic terms in
{\cal P}_{\rm IOE}(\bm{p})
are sensitive to a cancellation of
\mathcal{Q}
between terms of different orders in the IOE,
c.f.

Barata:2021wuf
.

Finally, to have a more quantitative understanding of the broadening distribution
{\cal P}({\bm{p}})
, we evaluate (
53
) using the full GW potential given in (
54
). We also assume that the nuclear matter is near to equilibrium, and its properties are controlled by a single parameter.
For instance, in the case of QGP in the large temperature limit, one can use parametric scaling motivated by the equilibrium thermodynamics
\mu\propto gT
and
\rho\propto T^{3}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{\nabla}\rho}{\rho}=3\frac{\bm{\nabla}T}{T}\,,\quad\frac{\bm{\nabla}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=2\frac{\bm{\nabla}T}{T}\,.

(63)

The probability
{\cal P}({\bm{p}})
is now a function of
\theta
, the angle between
{\bm{p}}
and
\bm{\nabla}T
, and a dimensionless quantity
c_{T}\equiv\left|\frac{\bm{\nabla}T}{ET}\right|\ll 1
, see Fig.
3

Performing the remaining angular integration, we can write
{\cal P}
as

\displaystyle{\cal P}({\bm{p}})

\displaystyle=2\pi\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}dx_{\perp}\,x_{\perp}\,e^{-\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})L}\Bigg\{J_{0}(p_{\perp}x_{\perp})-\frac{\chi^{2}\mu^{2}L}{6}\,c_{T}\,x_{\perp}\,K_{0}(\mu\,x_{\perp})\,J_{1}(p_{\perp}x_{\perp})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left[1-3\mu\,x_{\perp}K_{1}(\mu\,x_{\perp})+\mu^{2}x_{\perp}^{2}K_{2}(\mu\,x_{\perp})\right]\cos{\theta}\Bigg\}\,.

For the particular case of the GW model,
\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})
asymptotically tends to a constant. Therefore, the integral in (

) needs to be regulated for values of

\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(x_{\perp})

;
Barata:2020rdn
, we consider a regularized broadening distribution

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{r}({\bm{p}})

\displaystyle={\cal P}({\bm{p}})-2\pi\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}dx_{\perp}\,x_{\perp}\,e^{-\mathcal{V}^{\rm GW}(\infty)L}\,J_{0}(p_{\perp}x_{\perp})\,,

which differs from (

) by a singular term (delta function) at
{\bm{p}}={\bm{0}}
.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

In this work, we have derived the parton momentum broadening distribution in an inhomogeneous dense nuclear matter. The final distribution has been obtained using both the GLV and BDMPS-Z formalisms. In the homogeneous case, these two approaches considered above were proven to agree order by order in opacity, see e.g.
Wiedemann:2000ez
;
Wiedemann:2000za
;
Gyulassy:2002yv
;
Qiu:2003pm
. Here we make the next step, showing that the agreement holds even if the matter is not translationally-invariant in the transverse directions. Importantly, the effects of the medium inhomogeneity only emerge once the medium averaging is performed. As a consequence, all the results for the jet-medium interactions which are independent of the averaging procedure stay unmodified with respect to the homogeneous case.
Qualitatively, the modification of the resulting broadening distribution (comparing to the homogeneous baseline) becomes relevant around and above its characteristic peak, introducing a novel angular dependence. As a consequence, the gradient effects can have sizable impact in phenomenological applications, even for momentum averaged quantities.

Our results for the jet broadening can be extended to the case of inelastic energy loss in a dense inhomogeneous medium. Primarily, such an exercise will allow to study how the gradient effects alter the in-medium gluon production rate, giving rise to a non-trivial angular structure of the radiation. One may expect that the effective time factorization of the emission spectrum found in the limit of soft induced radiation in homogeneous media
Mehtar-Tani:2012mfa
;
Blaizot:2013vha
would be modified in accordance with (
44
). Understanding the resulting new factorized form is critical for some aspects of jet quenching phenomenology
Blaizot:2013vha
;
Caucal:2019uvr
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2014bpa
.

Another important goal we leave for the future studies is to design a jet observable sensitive to the matter effects discussed in this manuscript,
c.f.

Antiporda:2021hpk
. Particularly, it would be interesting to explore
to what extent the jet substructure grooming/tagging techniques, see e.g.
Larkoski:2014wba
;
Chien:2016led
;
Larkoski:2017jix
;
Apolinario:2017qay
;
Caucal:2021cfb
;
Karlberg:2021kwr
could be used to look for matter inhomogeneity effects on the angular spectrum of the hardest substructures within the jet. Such new observables would provide a window to further probe the medium properties locally along the ideas of jet tomography.

We also note that the presented results are independent of the physical process which generates the background medium and, thus, they are in principle applicable to either the QGP phase or the glasma phase in the HIC context
Ipp:2020mjc
;
Carrington:2021dvw
. The latter one could be considerably anisotropic, resulting in stronger directional effects. It would be interesting to better understand the relation
between the jet quenching formalisms considered here and other approaches which can describe jet evolution in inhomogeneous and evolving media, see e.g.
Agostini:2019avp
;
Hauksson:2021okc
;
Altinoluk:2021lvu
. In turn, extension to the DIS context would require the present calculation to be updated to the corresponding kinematical regime with particular focus on the factorization of the jet production and its propagation through the medium, see e.g.
Armesto:2013fca
;
Wang:2001ifa
;
Kang:2013raa
;
Kang:2014ela
;
Sirimanna:2021sqx
and references therein.

Finally, it should be mentioned that the dependence of the jet-medium interaction on hydrodynamic gradients could be used to probe the strength of the interactions in the underlying theory. Indeed, comparing the characteristic transport properties and the general jet behavior in an inhomogeneous matter between pQCD and holographic models for jet-medium interactions, see e.g.
Casalderrey-Solana:2014bpa
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2015vaa
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2016jvj
;
Rajagopal:2016uip
;
Brewer:2017dwd
;
Brewer:2017fqy
;
Brewer:2018mpk
, one may hope to identify a set of new observables distinguishing the two regimes. We leave this intriguing opportunity for future work.

Appendix B
Shift Operator and
\delta
-function

In this Appendix, we briefly discuss the imaginary term in the EOMs (
31
), arising due to the gradient effects. This term results in an imaginary shift of the zeroth order trajectory, which enters for instance into the argument of
\delta
-functions in the last line of (
III
). Since the
\delta
-functions are originally defined in the real space, this notation is only formal, and the functions should be re-defined.

In the main text, the resulting delta functions are treated as in the case of a real argument. To justify this treatment, consider the following one-dimensional integral

\displaystyle\int dx\,e^{-ipx}e^{ax}\equiv(2\pi)\,\delta(p+ia)\,.

(80)

For a real
a
, one can understand it as a momentum representation of the shift operator
e^{ax}=e^{ia\frac{\partial}{\partial p}}
with a purely imaginary argument
ia
. Thus, we can write

\displaystyle\int dx\,e^{-ipx}\,e^{ax}=(2\pi)e^{ia\frac{\partial}{\partial p}}\delta(p)=(2\pi)\,\delta(p+ia)\,\,.

(81)

This simple calculation shows that the imaginary term entering the EOM will indeed give rise to a momentum-space shift operator, and its effect can be formally written with the complex argument of a

Abstract
We study non-planar corrections in two special 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) gauge theories that are planar-equivalent to 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM theory: two-nodes quiver model with equal couplings and 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 vector multiplet coupled to two hypermultiplets in rank-2 symmetric and antisymmetric representations. We focus on two observables in these theories that admit representation in terms of localization matrix model: free energy on 4-sphere and the expectation value of half-BPS circular Wilson loop. We extend the methods developed in arXiv:2207.11475 to derive a systematical expansion of non-planar corrections to these observables at strong ’t Hooft coupling constant λ \lambda . We show that the leading non-planar corrections are given by a power series in λ 3 / 2 / N 2 \lambda^{3/2}/N^{2} with rational coefficients. Sending N N and the coupling constant λ \lambda to infinity with λ 3 / 2 / N 2 \lambda^{3/2}/N^{2} kept fixed corresponds to the familiar double scaling limit in matrix models. We find that in this limit the observables in the two models are related in a remarkably simple way: the free energies differ by the factor of 2 2 , whereas the Wilson loop expectation values coincide. Surprisingly, these relations hold only at strong coupling, they are not valid in the weak coupling regime. We also discuss a dual string theory interpretation of the leading corrections to the free energy in the double scaling limit suggesting their relation to curvature corrections in type IIB string effective action.

Matrix model representation

In this section we discuss the matrix model representation for the partition function of the
\mathsf{SA}
and quiver
\mathsf{Q}_{2}
models with a gauge group
SU(N)
on the unit sphere
S^{4}

The partition function of the
\mathsf{SA}
model is given by a matrix
integral

(see
Beccaria:2021vuc
for details)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathsf{SA}}=\int\prod_{r=1}^{N}da_{r}\,\delta\Big(\sum_{r}a_{r}\Big)\,\Delta^{2}(\bm{a})\,e^{-S_{\mathsf{SA}}(\bm{a})}\,,

(18)

where integration goes over eigenvalues
\bm{a}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
of a hermitian traceless
N\times N
matrix
A
describing zero modes of a scalar field on
S^{4}
.
Here
\Delta(\bm{a})=\prod_{r<s}(a_{r}-a_{s})
is a Vandermonde determinant and the potential
S_{\mathsf{SA}}(\bm{a})

has the following form

\displaystyle S_{\mathsf{SA}}(\bm{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi^{2}N}{\lambda}\sum_{r=1}^{N}a_{r}^{2}+\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\Big[\log H(a_{r}+a_{s})-\log H(a_{r}-a_{s})\Big].

(19)

It contains the function

given by the product of the Barnes

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-(1+\gamma_{\rm E})\,x^{2}}\,G(1+ix)G(1-ix)=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}\zeta(2n+1)\,x^{2n+2}\right)\,.

(20)

The second relation yields its expansion at small

and it involves the Riemann zeta values.

In a similar manner, the partition function of the quiver
\mathsf{Q}_{2}
model with equal coupling constants on the two nodes is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\mathsf{Q}_{2}}=\int\prod_{r=1}^{N}da_{1,r}da_{2,r}\,\delta\Big(\sum_{r}a_{1,r}\Big)\delta\Big(\sum_{r}a_{2,r}\Big)\,\big[\Delta(\bm{a}_{1})\Delta(\bm{a}_{2})\big]^{2}\,e^{-S_{\mathsf{Q}_{2}}(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2})}\,,

(21)

where
a_{\alpha,i}
are eigenvalues of the
SU(N)
matrices
A_{\alpha}
(with
\alpha=1,2
). The potential is given by

\displaystyle S_{\mathsf{Q}_{2}}(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}){}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi^{2}N}{\lambda}\sum_{r=1}^{N}(a_{1,r}^{2}+a_{2,r}^{2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\Big[2\log H(a_{1,r}-a_{2,s})-\log H(a_{1,r}-a_{1,s})-\log H(a_{2,r}-a_{2,s})\Big],

(22)

where the function

is defined in (

18

), we neglected the instanton contribution to the partition function as it is exponentially small at large

It is convenient to express the potentials (

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{i}(A)=\tr\Big({A\over\sqrt{N}}\Big)^{i}=\sum_{r=1}^{N}\left(a_{r}\over\sqrt{N}\right)^{i}\,,

(23)

where
\mathcal{O}_{1}(A)=0
for the

Expanding the

\displaystyle S_{\mathsf{SA}}(\bm{a}){}

\displaystyle=\tr A^{2}-S_{\rm int}(A)\,,

\displaystyle S_{\mathsf{Q}_{2}}(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}){}

\displaystyle=\tr A_{1}^{2}+\tr A_{2}^{2}-S_{\rm int}(A_{1},A_{2})\,.

(24)

The interaction terms in both models are given by infinite bilinear combinations of the single traces (

\displaystyle S_{\rm int}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}C^{-}_{ij}(\lambda)\,\mathcal{O}_{2i+1}(A)\mathcal{O}_{2j+1}(A)\,,

(25)

\displaystyle S_{\rm int}(A_{1},A_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}C^{-}_{ij}(\lambda)\left[\mathcal{O}_{2i+1}(A_{1})-\mathcal{O}_{2i+1}(A_{2})\right]\left[\mathcal{O}_{2j+1}(A_{1})-\mathcal{O}_{2j+1}(A_{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}C^{+}_{ij}(\lambda)\left[\mathcal{O}_{2i}(A_{1})-\mathcal{O}_{2i}(A_{2})\right]\left[\mathcal{O}_{2j}(A_{1})-\mathcal{O}_{2j}(A_{2})\right]\,,

\displaystyle C^{-}_{ij}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=8\,\Big(\frac{\lambda}{8\pi^{2}}\Big)^{i+j+1}\,(-1)^{i-j+1}\,\zeta(2(i+j)+1)\,\frac{\Gamma(2(i+j)+2)}{\Gamma(2i+2)\Gamma(2j+2)}\,,

\displaystyle C^{+}_{ij}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=8\,\Big(\frac{\lambda}{8\pi^{2}}\Big)^{i+j}\,(-1)^{i-j+1}\,\zeta(2(i+j)-1)\,\frac{\Gamma(2(i+j))}{\Gamma(2i+1)\Gamma(2j+1)}\,.

(27)

They define two semi-infinite matrices whose entries are proportional to a power of ’t Hooft coupling and odd Riemann zeta values.

\mathsf{SA}
model (

\displaystyle{Z_{\mathsf{Q}_{2}}\over[Z_{\mathcal{N}=4}]^{2}}=\int DA_{1}DA_{2}\,e^{-\tr A_{1}^{2}-\tr A_{2}^{2}+S_{\rm int}(A_{1},A_{2})}\equiv\big\langle e^{S_{\rm int}(A_{1},A_{2})}\big\rangle\,,

In what follows we shall use the notation

\displaystyle\bm{K}_{\ell}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,K_{\ell}(x,y)\chi{\left({\sqrt{y}\over 2g}\right)}f(y)\,,

\displaystyle(K_{\ell})_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\sqrt{2i+\ell-1}\sqrt{2j+\ell-1}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\,J_{2i+\ell-1}(\sqrt{t})\,J_{2j+\ell-1}(\sqrt{t})\chi{\left({\sqrt{t}\over 2g}\right)}\,.

Given the explicit strong coupling results obtained in this paper and summarized in the Introduction, it is

In the maximally supersymmetric case of

7.2
Comments

Let us now add a few reservations and comments.
The above argument was based on the assumption that the higher-order curvature corrections in (
161
), that should vanish
in the maximally symmetric
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
case, become non-zero once supersymmetry is reduced as in the orbifold/orientifold case. As already mentioned above, to show this explicitly remains an open problem.
Another puzzle is that since the orbifold/orientifold projection applies to
S^{5}
only, one would expect that the
IR divergent factor of the

AdS_{5}
volume should still remain and thus, as in the leading tree-level part,
should then produce a
\log\lambda
contribution after introducing an IR
regularization. However, such
\log\lambda
terms should be absent in non-planar corrections
to
F
beyond the torus order as seen from (
6
)–(
9

It is possible that there is a subtle “
0\times\infty
” cancellation mechanism at work that gives a finite contribution.

The above discussion involved the special
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
terms in (
161
) that get contributions only from a finite number
of perturbative
g^{2n}_{s}
terms; this made it possible to isolate the leading
\alpha^{\prime}
correction at each of the lowest

g^{2n}_{s}
(
n=0,1,2
)
orders.
This pattern appears to change starting with the string 4-loop
f_{3}{\alpha^{\prime}}^{3}D^{8}R^{4}
term.
For example,
the 1-loop
\zeta(3)\log(-\alpha^{\prime}D^{2})
term in (
163
) would naively lead to a
\zeta(3)\log\lambda
term in (
4
) and (
5
)
but the
\log\lambda
term there has a rational coefficient (which should come from the torus partition function).
18
18

18

Still, it is interesting to note that
a constant term that may accompany this 1-loop
\zeta(3)\log(-\alpha^{\prime}D^{2})
term in (
163
)
as a coefficient of the
\alpha^{\prime}{}^{3}D^{8}R^{4}
term in (
161
)
could be a string counterpart of the

term in
\mathsf{F}_{0}
in (
4
) and (

).

Thus extracting the leading
\alpha^{\prime}
term at each of

the structure of

.

It is possible that the above discussion based on low-energy effective action is only a short-cut to understand
the leading large tension scaling of string loop corrections that appear in the full string partition function (
159

and then take the limit

for the

and
\mathsf{SA}

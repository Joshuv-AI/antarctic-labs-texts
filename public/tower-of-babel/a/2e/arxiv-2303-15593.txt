Limits of polyhedral multinomial distributions Thanks: The author was supported by Charles University project PRIMUS/21/SCI/014.
Aniket Shah

Abstract
. We consider limits of certain measures supported on lattice points in lattice polyhedra defined as the intersection of half-spaces { m ∈ ℝ n | ⟨ v i , x ⟩ + a i ≥ 0 } \{m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0\} , where ∑ i v i = 0 \sum_{i}v_{i}=0 . The measures are densities associated to lattice random variables obtained by restriction of multinomial random variables. We find the limiting Gaussian distributions explicitly.



1.
Introduction

Let
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
define hyperplanes
H_{i}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}=0\}
, such that the corresponding intersection of half-spaces
P=\bigcap_{i}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0\}
is compact, and each
H_{i}
touches
P
. Then, we define the random vector
\textbf{X}_{\textbf{a}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\mathbb{P}(\textbf{X}_{\textbf{a}}=x)=\frac{1}{b}\cdot\binom{\sum_{i}\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+a_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+a_{r}},

for each lattice point
x\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We call its distribution the
polyhedral multinomial distribution
associated to
a
.

Imposing the further condition that
\sum_{i}v_{i}=0
, the top term in the multinomial simplifies to
|\textbf{a}|:=\sum_{i}a_{i}
, so

\mathbb{P}(\textbf{X}_{\textbf{a}}=x)=\frac{1}{b}\cdot\binom{|\textbf{a}|}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+a_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+a_{r}}.

In the special case that
r=n+1
,
v_{i}=e_{i}
for
i
from
1
to
n
, and
v_{n+1}=-\sum_{i}e_{i}
, the corresponding distribution is the usual multinomial. More generally,
\textbf{X}_{\textbf{a}}
follows a conditional distribution of a multinomial distribution on a higher dimensional vector space.

We are interested in the limiting behavior of
\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}
as
k
goes to infinity. For multinomial distributions, the limit is well-known to approach a Gaussian, due to the central limit theorem. More generally, the distribution of
\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}
does not remain within the the region (which grows sublinearly in
k
around the mean) controlled by e.g. the central or local limit theorems
[
D
,
O21
]
for higher dimensional multinomial distributions. Our approach is to instead approximate directly using Stirling’s formula.

Our main result is that after recentering and scaling appropriately, the polyhedral multinomial distributions
\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}
converge to a specific Gaussian distribution supported on the subspace
L
generated by differences of vectors in
P
. We let
I_{\textbf{a}}\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
denote the indices
i
such that
\langle v_{i},x\rangle
is not constant on
P
. The point
m_{\textbf{a}}\in P
is defined in Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

When
\sum_{i}v_{i}=0
, the sequence
\textbf{Y}_{k}=\frac{\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}-\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]}{\sqrt{k}}
converges weakly to
Y
with density given by the Dirac measure
\delta_{L}
times a Gaussian with mean
0
and variance given by the quadratic form

x\mapsto\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle\right)^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}.

Remark 1.2
.

This research was motivated by consideration of an analogue of Duistermaat-Heckman measure in
[
AS23
]
while studying line bundles on the toric arc scheme as defined in
[
AK06
]
. Those familiar with toric geometry may recognize that besides
\sum_{i}v_{i}=0
, the conditions on
v_{i}
and
a
relate to
\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
defining a nef divisor on an associated toric variety.

2.
The potential corresponding to the data
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})

Let
\delta_{x}
be the Dirac probability measure supported at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We let
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
define
H_{i}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}=0\}
as in the introduction. We assume the intersection
P=\bigcap_{i}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0\}
is compact, and each
H_{i}
touches
P
. The distribution of the random vector
\textbf{X}_{\textbf{a}}
is

\mu_{\textbf{a}}=\frac{1}{b}\cdot\sum_{x\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\binom{|\textbf{a}|}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+a_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+a_{r}}\delta_{x}.

and the distribution for
\textbf{Y}_{k}=\frac{\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}-\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]}{\sqrt{k}}
is

\nu_{k}:=\left(\tau_{\sqrt{k}}\right)_{*}\left(\mu_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}\ast\delta_{-\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]}\right).

We will relate
\nu_{k}
to the following function, which we call the
potential
of
a
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}:P\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be the function

x\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}}.

In this product we read
0^{0}
as
1
, so
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}}=\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}}.

We let
\textrm{rel.int.}(P)
be the interior of
P
viewed as a subspace of the affine linear span of vectors in
P
. For
x\in\textrm{rel.int.}(P)
and each
i\in I_{\textbf{a}}
,
\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}>0
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The function
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
is convex on
P
, and there is a unique
m_{\textbf{a}}\in\textrm{rel.int.}(P)
minimizing
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
.

Proof.

Let
x
be in the relative interior of
P
, so
\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}>0
for
i\in I_{\textbf{a}}
, and let
x^{\prime}
be in
L
. We calculate

\frac{d\left(\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x+tx^{\prime})\right)}{dt}=\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x)\cdot\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\left(\log(\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i})+1\right).

We have assumed that
0=\sum_{i}v_{i}=\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}v_{i}+\sum_{i\not\in I_{\textbf{a}}}v_{i}
. Any
x^{\prime}\in L
can be written as a sum of differences of elements of
P
, so for
i\not\in I_{\textbf{a}}
, we have that
\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle=0
. Then we can calculate that
\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle=0
as well.
Thus,

\frac{d\left(\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x+tx^{\prime})\right)}{dt}=\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x)\cdot\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\log(\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i})\right).

If
x
is in the relative interior of
P
,
\langle v_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}>0
for
i\in I_{\textbf{a}}
. As
x+tx^{\prime}
approaches the boundary of
P
at some finite positive
t_{0}
, we have that for some
i\in I_{\textbf{a}}
,
\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i}
decreases to
0
. This implies both that
\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle<0
, and that
\log(\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i})
goes to negative infinity.

Thus,
\frac{d\left(\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x+tx^{\prime})\right)}{dt}
is positive near the boundary, so
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x+tx^{\prime})
cannot be minimized there.

On the other hand, we see that the second derivative

\frac{d^{2}\left(\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x+tx^{\prime})\right)}{dt^{2}}=\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x)\cdot\left(\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\log(\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i})\right)^{2}+\sum_{i}\frac{\langle v_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},x+tx^{\prime}\rangle+a_{i}}\right),

is strictly positive when
x+tx^{\prime}\in rel.int.(P)
, so
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x)
is convex.

Thus, there is a unique minimizer
m_{\textbf{a}}
in the relative interior of
P
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
v_{1}=1
,
v_{2}=-1
in
\mathbb{R}
, and let
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})=(0,l)
. Then
P
is the interval
[0,l]\subset\mathbb{R}
, and
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
is

\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(x)=x^{x}(l-x)^{l-x},

which is minimized at
\frac{l}{2}
.

We now show the following technical lemma for
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
, which we will use later. Note that for any
x\in L
, and
k
large enough,
\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)
is defined.

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
x\in L
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle m_{\textbf{a}},v_{i}\rangle+a_{i}}}.

We show this by showing a fortiori that if
x\in L
and
|x|<k^{c}
for some
0<c<\frac{1}{6}
, then

\left|\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)}\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\right|=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\cdot O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}).

Proof.

We can rewrite the
k
-dependent expression as a product of three parts which we will deal with separately:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)}\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\sqrt{\frac{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}}}

(2)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}}\right)^{k(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}

(3)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{1}{\left(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\sqrt{k}\langle v_{i},x\rangle}}.

If
x\in L
and
|x|<k^{c}
, then

\sqrt{\frac{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}}}=1+O(k^{c-\frac{1}{2}}).

For the second, which can be written
\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}}\right)^{k(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}
we have

\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}}\right)^{k(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}=\exp\left(-\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}k(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{l}\left(\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}\right)^{l}\right).

The argument of
\exp
can be written

-\sqrt{k}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x\rangle\right)+\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{3}}{(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l+3}\left(\frac{-\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}\right)^{l}\right).

Since
x\in L
,
\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x\rangle=0
. If
|x|<k^{c}
, then the above is

\left(\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}\right)+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}),

so

\left(\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}}\right)^{k(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a{\rho_{i}}}}\cdot(1+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}})).

Finally, the last term in the product can be factored further:

\prod_{i}\frac{1}{\left(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\sqrt{k}\langle v_{i},x\rangle}}=\prod_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}}\right)^{\sqrt{k}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}}\right)^{\sqrt{k}\langle v_{i},x\rangle}.

Recall that
m_{\textbf{a}}
is defined as the unique critical point of the function
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
from Proposition
2.2
, and so for any
x
,
\frac{d\left(\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(m_{\textbf{a}}+tx)\right)}{dt}|_{t=0}=0.
Computing the derivative, we get

0=\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(m_{\textbf{a}})\cdot\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x\rangle\log(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}).

Since
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(m_{\textbf{a}})
is positive, we have
\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},x\rangle\log(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})=0
, and consequently

1=\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\left(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}\right)^{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}.

The other term is easy to estimate in a manner similar to the second product, i.e.

\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle}{\sqrt{k}(\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i})}}\right)^{\sqrt{k}\langle v_{i},x\rangle}=e^{-\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a{\rho_{i}}}}\cdot(1+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}})).

Thus,

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)}\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}=

\displaystyle 1\cdot(1+O(k^{c-\frac{1}{2}}))

(5)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a{\rho_{i}}}}\cdot(1+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}))

(6)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle e^{-\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a{\rho_{i}}}}\cdot(1+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}))

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a{\rho_{i}}}}\cdot(1+O(k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}})).

We’ve assumed that
c<\frac{1}{6}
, so the error term goes to
0
.
∎

3.
The auxiliary distribution
\nu^{\prime}_{k}

Because we are not able to directly access
\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]
, it is easier to calculate the limit of an auxiliary distribution rather than the distributions of
\textbf{Y}_{k}
(the
\nu_{k}
). Define

\nu^{\prime}_{k}:=\left(\tau_{\sqrt{k}}\right)_{*}\left(\mu_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}\ast\delta_{-km_{\textbf{a}}}\right).

From the definitions,
\nu^{\prime}_{k}
is a sum of Dirac measures with multinomial coefficients over lattice points in
kP
, but the next proposition shows that as
k
goes to infinity, there is an alternative formula.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
0<c<\frac{1}{6}
. There are constants
d_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap\frac{M-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{x}.

Proof.

By definition,

(9)

\nu^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{x\in kP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\ \binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{r}}\delta_{\frac{x-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}},

for
b_{k}
normalizing constants (explicitly,
b_{k}=\sum_{x\in kP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\ \binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{r}}
). We will start by showing that most of the terms can be ignored as as
k
goes to infinity.

Let

c_{L}=\sup_{x\in L}\inf_{m\in L\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|x-m|,

and let
m_{\textrm{inner},k}
be a point in
B_{c_{L}+1}(km_{\textbf{a}})\cap\left(km_{\textbf{a}}+L\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(which certainly must be non-empty).

The mass from terms in the sum outside of
B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})
can be bounded:

\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{x\in\left(kP\smallsetminus B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{i}}\leq\sum_{x\in\left(kP\smallsetminus B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{i}}}{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}}}.

Using Stirling’s formula (see e.g.
[
WW
, Section 12.33]
), we can bound the summands:

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{i}}}{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}}}.

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
\langle v_{i},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\langle v_{i},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\langle v_{i},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
\langle v_{i},x\rangle+ka_{i}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle+ka_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\langle v_{i},x\rangle+ka_{i}}},

(11)

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime\prime}k^{l}\cdot\left(\frac{\varphi_{\textbf{a}}\left(\frac{m_{\textrm{inner},k}}{k}\right)}{\varphi_{\textbf{a}}\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)}\right)^{k},

where
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime},
and
l
are constants independent of
x
.

By assumption,
|\frac{m_{\textrm{inner},k}}{k}-m_{\textbf{a}}|<\frac{c_{L}+1}{k}
. If
x\in kP\smallsetminus B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}}),
then
|\frac{x}{k}-m_{\textbf{a}}|\geq\frac{1}{k^{\frac{1}{2}-c}}
. Let us define distinguished points
m_{\textrm{outer},k}
such that
|\frac{m_{\textrm{outer},k}}{k}-m_{\textbf{a}}|=\frac{1}{k^{\frac{1}{2}-c}}
and additionally among all
x
satisfying
|\frac{x}{k}-m_{\textbf{a}}|=\frac{1}{k^{\frac{1}{2}-c}}
, the point
m_{\textrm{outer},k}
minimizes
x\mapsto\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(\frac{x}{k})
.

Then, since
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
is convex, for any
x
such that
|\frac{x}{k}-m_{\textbf{a}}|\geq\frac{1}{k^{\frac{1}{2}-c}}
, we have
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(\frac{m_{\textrm{outer},k}}{k})\leq\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(\frac{x}{k})
. On the other hand, by writing
\varphi_{\textbf{a}}
as a Taylor polynomial around
m_{\textbf{a}}
, we have that
\frac{\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(\frac{m_{\textrm{inner},k}}{k})}{\varphi_{\textbf{a}}(\frac{m_{\textrm{outer},k}}{k})}<R<1
for all
k
sufficiently large.

Thus,

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left(kP\smallsetminus B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{i}}}{\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},m_{\textrm{inner},k}\rangle+ka_{i}}}<

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left(kP\smallsetminus B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c^{\prime\prime}k^{l}R^{k}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(k)c^{\prime\prime}k^{l}R^{k},

where
p(k)
is a polynomial in
k
(e.g. the Ehrhart polynomial of
P
which counts integer points in
kP
). This quantity goes to
0
as
k
goes to infinity, showing that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{\frac{1}{2}+c}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\cap kP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\ \binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},x\rangle+ka_{r}}\delta_{\frac{x-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}.

We can rewrite the terms within the limit on the right-hand side of the above as

\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\ \binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{r}}\delta_{x},

so we can then start comparing. Note: to reduce the line sizes below, we write
\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}
for
\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{r}}
.

Let
d_{k}=b_{k}\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}}{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}
. Then we can compare
v^{\prime}_{k}
and
\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{x}
via

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\left|\frac{1}{b_{k}}\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}-\frac{1}{d_{k}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left|\binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}-\frac{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\left|\frac{1}{b_{k}}\frac{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}-\frac{1}{d_{k}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\right|.

As
k
goes to infinity, the first sum goes to
0
by applying standard bounds associated to Stirling’s approximation.

The terms of the second can be rewritten

\frac{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}{b_{k}\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}}\left|\frac{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x)\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}+\sqrt{k}x\rangle+ka_{i}}}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\right|.

By Lemma
2.4
, the whole sum is therefore bounded by some constant times

k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\frac{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}.

Recall that
b_{k}=\sum_{y\in kP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\ \binom{k\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\langle v_{1},y\rangle+ka_{1},\ldots,\langle v_{r},y\rangle+ka_{r}}
, so using Stirling’s formula and Lemma
2.4
, we have for some small
\epsilon>0
that

b_{k}\geq(1-\epsilon)\sum_{y\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}\ \frac{(2\pi)^{1-|I_{\textbf{a}}|}(k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}+k\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}a_{i}}}{\varphi_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}(km_{\textbf{a}})\sqrt{\prod_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\langle v_{i},km_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+ka_{i}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}},

when
k
is large enough. Thus besides the
k^{3c-\frac{1}{2}}
factor, the expression is bounded above by a constant, and so the whole sum vanishes.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The measures
\nu^{\prime}_{k}
weakly converge to a scalar multiple of the measure

e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\cdot\delta_{L}.

This is true because the sequence

\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}f(x),

converges to some multiple of
\int_{L}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}f(x)dx
by the definition of the Riemann integral.

Since
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{x\in B_{k^{c}}(0)\cap L\cap\frac{\mathbb{Z}^{n}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{x}
is equal to
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}
which is a probability measure, we can determine finally that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{L}}{\int_{L}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}dx}
.

4.
The limit of
\nu_{k}

Now we can directly address the distribution of
\textbf{Y}_{k}
, which is the rescaling of the polyhedral multinomial random vector
X_{k}
, translated to have mean
0
. The distribution has the formula

\nu_{k}=\left(\tau_{\sqrt{k}}\right)_{*}\left(\mu_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}\ast\delta_{-\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]}\right).

According to the results of the previous section,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\tau_{\sqrt{k}}\right)_{*}\left(\mu_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}\ast\delta_{-k\cdot m_{\textbf{a}}}\right)=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{L}}{\int_{L}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}dx}.

Theorem 4.1
.

The limit distribution of
\textbf{Y}_{k}
is the probability measure

\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}\delta_{L}}{\int_{L}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{\textbf{a}}}\frac{\langle v_{i},x\rangle^{2}}{\langle v_{i},m_{\textbf{a}}\rangle+a_{i}}}dx}

Proof.

Integrating
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
against
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}
is
0
, so
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]-k\cdot m_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}}=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\textbf{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{X_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}-\mathbb{E}[X_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}]}{\sqrt{k}},

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{X_{k\cdot\textbf{a}}-km_{\textbf{a}}}{\sqrt{k}},

which is the random variable with distribution
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{k}
.
∎

Chiral Virasoro algebra from a single wavefunction
Isaac H. Kim, Xiang Li, Ting-Chun Lin, John McGreevy, Bowen Shi

Abstract
Chiral edges of 2+1D systems can have very robust emergent conformal symmetry. When the edge is purely chiral, the Hilbert space of low-energy edge excitations can form a representation of a single Virasoro algebra. We propose a method to systematically extract the generators of the Virasoro algebra from a single ground state wavefunction, using entanglement bootstrap and an input from the edge conformal field theory. We corroborate our construction by numerically verifying the commutation relations of the generators. We also study the unitary flows generated by these operators, whose properties (such as energy and state overlap) are shown numerically to agree with our analytical predictions.

Good modular flow generators and CFT

The numerical results in Section

This subspace,

Now the natural question is how the good modular flows act on

More specifically, we apply these concepts to gapped ground states with purely-chiral gapless edges, laying the groundwork for the construction of Virasoro generators in Section

5.1
Virasoro algebra and coherent states

Consider a good modular flow generator
\mathbb{L}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}

\mathbb{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\int d\theta\,f(\theta){T}(\theta).

\left|\Psi(t)\right\rangle=e^{{\bf i}\mathbb{L}t}\left|\Psi\right\rangle=\mathbb{V}\exp\left({\bf i}t\int d\theta\,f(\theta){T}(\theta)\right)\left|\Omega\right\rangle,

(5.2)

In purely chiral CFTs, the group generated by

{\mathcal{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{edge}}\equiv\mathrm{Span}\left\{e^{{\bf i}\mathbb{L}t}\left|\Psi\right\rangle|\mathbb{L}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}\right\}.

This is our carrier space for

\widetilde{L}_{\pm 1}

, while for the other generators,

The first term

In Section

. Using the result of Eq. (

. The purposes of studying these quantities are not only for their own physical meaningfulness but also for comparisons. Later in Section

We remark that one can further improve the construction by a procedure that reduces the discrepancy between

6.1
Explicit construction of
\widetilde{L}_{n}

In this subsection, we introduce the explicit construction of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
using good modular flow generators from a 2+1D purely-chiral state
\left|\Psi\right\rangle
. The general idea of the construction can be understood as an approximate spatial Fourier transformation. Recall the Virasoro generator
L_{n}
is

L_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}{L}_{n}={1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta e^{{\bf i}n\theta}{T}(\theta).

(6.2)

Based on Hypothesis
1
, a good modular flow generator in
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
under the isometry
\mathbb{V}
is a weighted integral of stress-energy tensors, i.e. a linear combination of them. That is:

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\mathbb{L}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta){T}(\theta),\quad\mathbb{L}_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet},\quad\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}.

(6.3)

Therefore, to approximate
L_{n}
, one can find a certain linear combination of suitable good modular flow generators in
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
such that the weight function
f(\theta)
is approximately
e^{{\bf i}n\theta}
.

We shall introduce the construction of
L_{0}
and
L_{n\neq 0}
separately, as their construction schemes are different. For
L_{0}
, it can be obtained directly from a single good modular flow generator [Section
6.1.1
]. However, the construction for
L_{n},n\neq 0
takes more steps [Section
6.1.2
]. First of all, since
L_{n}
for
n\neq 0
are not Hermitian while good modular flow generators are, a certain linear combination of good modular flow generators with imaginary coefficients is necessary. Secondly, in the construction of
\widetilde{L}_{n\neq 0}
, we utilize a method to which we shall refer as “twisting trick” (illustrated in Figure
14
). The twisting trick enables us to obtain a certain set of good modular flow generators with desirable weight functions whose linear combinations can approximate
e^{{\bf i}n\theta}
.

6.1.2
Construction of
\widetilde{L}_{n},n\neq 0
with the twisting trick

In this subsection, we introduce a scheme to construct the approximate Virasoro generator
\widetilde{L}_{n}
which is generically applicable for all
n\neq 0
. Our construction for
n=\pm 1
shall give the exact
L_{\pm 1}
, i.e.
\widetilde{L}_{\pm 1}=L_{\pm 1}
. On the other hand,
\widetilde{L}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
is equal to
L_{n}
plus a sum of sub-leading higher-harmonic terms
L_{kn},|k|\geq 2
, as we mentioned in Eq. (
6.1
).

As we mentioned at the beginning of this subsection, to construct an approximate Virasoro generator, our goal is to design a suitable set of good modular flow generators which realize approximate spatial Fourier transformations. We shall later introduce a
twisting trick
[Figure
14
] to achieve this goal. This trick leads to a judicious choice of linear combination of modular Hamiltonians that only create excitations on the edge. Below we explain this idea in detail with an example, followed by the discussion on the more general cases.

The main task of the construction is to find two types of good modular flow generators, denoted as
\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}
, such that

\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{\approx}}{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\sin(n\theta){T}(\theta),\quad\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{\approx}}{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\cos(n\theta){T}(\theta).

(6.7)

Once
\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}
are constructed, one can obtain the approximate Virasoro generators
\widetilde{L}_{\pm n}
as

\widetilde{L}_{\pm n}=\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}\pm{\bf i}\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{\approx}}{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta e^{\pm{\bf i}n\theta}{T}(\theta)={L}_{\pm n},\quad n\geq 1,

(6.8)

since
e^{\pm{\bf i}n\theta}=\cos(n\theta)\pm{\bf i}\sin(n\theta)
.

Now we discuss the construction of
\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}
and
\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}
. Let us use
\beta^{(o)}_{n}(\theta)
and
\beta^{(e)}_{n}(\theta)
to denote their weight function:

\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\beta^{(o)}_{n}(\theta){T}(\theta),\quad\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\beta^{(e)}_{n}(\theta){T}(\theta).

(6.9)

We aim to choose
\beta^{(o)}_{n}(\theta),\beta^{(e)}_{n}(\theta)
in Eq. (
6.9

\sin(n\theta)
and
\cos(n\theta)
. Based on Hypothesis
1

shall be a linear combination of the coolness functions
\beta^{[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}(\theta)

4.2

, one needs to answer the following two questions: (1) Which linear combination of coolness functions gives a good approximation of
\sin(n\theta),\cos(n\theta)
? (2) Which good modular flow generator can result in such a linear combination in its weight function?

6.1.3
Expansion of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
in Virasoro modes

With the approximate
\widetilde{L}_{n}
constructed, we now discuss the exact form of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
in terms of Virasoro generators
L_{m}
. Such an expression of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
is useful in analytically computing several quantities that are related to them, such as those discussed in Section
6.2
. The explicit expression of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
also reveals the approximation “error” of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
to
L_{n}
.

For
n=0
, as we mentioned before in Section
6.1.1
, the construction gives
\widetilde{L}_{0}=L_{0}
. Therefore, we shall focus on
\widetilde{L}_{n\neq 0}
. We remark that even though the construction of
\widetilde{L}_{\pm 1}
also gives exactly
L_{\pm 1}
, we still include
n=\pm 1
into the discussion below, because the construction for all
n\neq 0
follows the same procedure, so the analysis is applicable to all
n\neq 0
.

Based on the construction Eq. (
6.19
), the approximate Virasoro generator takes the form

\widetilde{L}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\beta_{n}(\theta){T}(\theta),\quad n\neq 0

(6.20)

where

\beta_{\pm n}(\theta)=\beta^{(e)}_{n}(\theta)\pm{\bf i}\beta^{(o)}_{n}(\theta),\quad n\geq 1.

(6.21)

Employing
{T}(\theta)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{m}e^{-{\bf i}m\theta}
in Eq. (
6.20
), one can obtain the exact form of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
in terms of exact Virasoro generators
L_{n}

\widetilde{L}_{n}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{n,m}L_{m},

(6.22)

where the
\lambda_{n,m}
are the Fourier coefficients of
\beta_{n}(\theta)
:

\lambda_{n,m}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\beta_{n}(\theta)e^{-{\bf i}m\theta}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\text{ if }|n|=1,m=n\\
&{n^{3}-n\over m^{3}-m},&&\text{ if }|n|\geq 2,m=(-1)^{k}(2k+1)n,k\in\mathbb{N}\\
&0,&&\text{ otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

(6.23)

We make several remarks regarding the exact form
\widetilde{L}_{n},n\neq 0
.

•

For
|n|=1
, we emphasize that
\widetilde{L}_{n}
is exactly
L_{n}
, i.e.
\widetilde{L}_{\pm 1}=L_{\pm 1}
. This can be seen directly by checking
\beta_{\pm 1}(\theta)=e^{\pm{\bf i}\theta}
.

•

For
|n|\geq 2
, we can see that the Virasoro mode labels appearing in
\widetilde{L}_{n}
are

n
multiplied by odd integers with alternating signs:
L_{n},L_{-3n},L_{5n}
, etc. Among these modes, the leading order contribution is
L_{n}
, as
\lambda_{n,n}=1
. As
|m/n|
increases, the magnitude of its coefficient
|\lambda_{n,m}|
decay as
O(|m/n|^{-3})
, which ensures the contribution from these sub-leading terms are considerably small.

Moreover, having obtained the exact form of
\widetilde{L}_{n},n\neq 0
, we can also design a procedure to reduce the subleading terms in
\widetilde{L}_{n}
, so that it becomes a better approximation to
L_{n}
. The procedure is explicitly introduced in Appendix
C
. As a limit of such a procedure, one can obtain the
exact
Virasoro generator
L_{n}
as a linear combination of good modular flow generators.

\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}
in terms of Virasoro generators

\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{(o)}_{n,m}L_{m},

(6.25)

where

\lambda^{(o)}_{n,m}=-{{\bf i}\over 2}(\lambda_{n,m}-\lambda_{-n,m}),\quad n\geq 1.

(6.26)

As we mentioned in Section
6.2.1
, the variance of a good modular flow generator can be expressed in terms of its Fourier coefficient as Eq. (
5.15
). Therefore, for
\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}
, we obtain

\sigma(\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n})^{2}={c\over 12}\sum_{m\geq 0}|\lambda^{(o)}_{n,m}|^{2}(m^{3}-m).

(6.27)

This quantity can be explicitly computed by making use of the analytic formula of
\lambda^{(o)}_{n,m}
obtained from Eq. (
6.26
) and Eq. (
6.23
):

\sigma(\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n})^{2}={c\over 192}n(n^{2}-1)^{2}\left[2\psi\left({1\over 2}\right)-\psi\left({n+1\over 2n}\right)-\psi\left({n-1\over 2n}\right)\right],

(6.28)

where
\psi(z)
is the polygamma function, defined as
\psi(z)\equiv\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
from the gamma function
\Gamma(z)
and its first derivative
\Gamma^{\prime}(z)
. Thus, at large
n
,
\sigma(\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n})^{2}\sim n^{3}
.

6.2.2
Expectation values of commutators

We are also interested in expectation values of commutators among
\widetilde{L}_{n},n\neq 0
since they encode information about the Virasoro algebra relations. Explicitly, we shall consider
\left\langle\Psi\right|[\widetilde{L}_{n},\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle,n\geq 1
. Recall
\widetilde{L}_{\pm n}=\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}\pm{\bf i}\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}
, therefore the expectation value of the commutator can be expressed as

\left\langle\Psi\right|[\widetilde{L}_{n},\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle=2{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle,\quad n\geq 1.

(6.29)

Before we explicitly compute
{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle,n\geq 1
, we first discuss the general case, namely, the expectation value
{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle
for any two good modular flow generators
\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
. There are, in general, two methods to compute it.

One way is to follow the same idea we used to compute the variance and use Fourier expansion of
\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}

\mathbb{L}^{1}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{1}_{m}L_{m},\quad\mathbb{L}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{2}_{m}L_{m},

(6.30)

then the expectation value of their commutators is

\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle={c\over 12}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{m}^{1}\lambda_{-m}^{2}(m^{3}-m).

(6.31)

The other way is to utilize the result in
[
58
]
: The expectation value of the commutator between two chiral CFT modular Hamiltonian on interval
ab,bc
is

{\bf i}{\left\langle\Omega\right|}[{K}_{ab}^{\chi},{K}_{bc}^{\chi}]\left|\Omega\right\rangle={\pi c_{-}\over 6}(2\eta_{a,b,c}-1),

(6.32)

where
\eta_{a,b,c}={|a|\cdot|c|\over|ab|\cdot|bc|}
is the cross-ratio of the successive intervals
(a,b,c)
, and
c_{-}
is the chiral central charge. With Eq. (
6.32
) at hand, we can, in general, compute expectation values between any two good modular flow generators

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}\ni\mathbb{L}^{1}=\sum_{A^{1}}\lambda_{A}^{1}K_{A^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\sum_{a^{1}}\lambda_{A^{1}}{K}^{\chi}_{a^{1}},

(6.33)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}\ni\mathbb{L}^{2}=\sum_{A^{2}}\lambda_{A}^{2}K_{A^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}\sum_{a^{2}}\lambda_{A^{2}}{K}^{\chi}_{a^{2}}.

In the equation above,
a^{1},a^{2}
are the intervals where
A^{1},A^{2}
intersect with the gapless edge. Using the isometry relation in Hypothesis
1
, the expectation value of the commutator
[\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}]
is

{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{1},\mathbb{L}^{2}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle=\sum_{A^{1},A^{2}}\lambda_{A^{1}}\lambda_{A^{2}}\left({\bf i}\left\langle\Omega\right|[{K}_{a^{1}},{K}_{a^{2}}]\left|\Omega\right\rangle\right),

(6.34)

where each commutator
{\bf i}\left\langle\Omega\right|[{K}_{a^{1}},{K}_{a^{2}}]\left|\Omega\right\rangle
in the right hand side can be computed via Eq. (
6.32
).

We shall use the second method to compute
{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle
, as it provides a succinct analytic formula of the result. Based on the construction of
\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}
in Eqs. (
6.15
) and (
6.16
),

\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}A_{n}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k-1}{K}_{x_{k}},\quad\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}A_{n}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k-1}{K}_{y_{k}},

(6.35)

where
x_{k}=\left[{(k-1)\pi\over n},{k\pi\over n}\right]
and
y_{k}=\left[{(k-3/2)\pi\over n},{(k-1/2)\pi\over n}\right]
are sequences of intervals on a circle, as explained in Section

{\bf i}\left\langle\Psi\right|[\mathbb{L}^{(e)}_{n},\mathbb{L}^{(o)}_{n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle=A_{n}^{2}\cdot 4n\cdot{\pi c_{-}\over 6}(1-2\eta_{n}),

(6.36)

where

\eta_{n}={\sin(\pi/(4n))^{2}\over\sin(\pi/(2n))^{2}}.

(6.37)

Therefore, plugging in the expression of
A_{n}
in Eq. (
6.11
), we can obtain the final expression of

\left\langle\Psi\right|[\widetilde{L}_{n},\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle={\pi c_{-}\over 24}n(n^{2}-1)^{2}{\sin^{2}(\pi/(4n))\over\cos(\pi/(2n))},\quad c_{-}=c.

(6.38)

where the chiral central charge
c_{-}
is equal to the holomorphic central charge
c
, since we are working with purely chiral CFT

21

\bar{c}=0

6.2.3
Expectation value of double commutators

Besides the expectation values of commutators, we shall also consider expectation values of double commutators
\left\langle\Psi\right|[[\widetilde{L}_{m},\widetilde{L}_{n}],\widetilde{L}_{k}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle
. The results can be explicitly computed by utilizing the Fourier expansion of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
(Eq. (
6.22
)) in terms of Virasoro generators.
Such quantities will allow us to test further the closure of our realization of the Virasoro algebra.

One particular double commutator that we will compute is
\left\langle\Psi\right|[[\widetilde{L}_{n},L_{0}],\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle
,
n\geq 1
. By making use of the Fourier expansion of
\widetilde{L}_{\pm n}
as follows

\widetilde{L}_{n}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{n,m}L_{m},\quad\widetilde{L}_{-n}=(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{\dagger}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{n,m}^{*}L_{-m},\quad n\geq 1,

(6.40)

and the Virasoro algebraic relations, we can derive

\left\langle\Psi\right|[[\widetilde{L}_{n},L_{0}],\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle={c\over 12}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|\lambda_{n,m}|^{2}(m^{4}-m^{2}).

(6.41)

With the analytic formula of
\lambda_{n,m}
given in Eq. (
6.23
), we are able to explicitly compute Eq. (
6.41
) and the result is

\left\langle\Psi\right|[[\widetilde{L}_{n},L_{0}],\widetilde{L}_{-n}]\left|\Psi\right\rangle={\pi c\over 48}n(n^{2}-1)^{2}\tan\left({\pi\over 2n}\right).

(6.42)

The result shall be compared with numerical results. Such a test examines not only the Fourier expansion of
\widetilde{L}_{\pm n}
but also the construction of
L_{0}
.

Note that Eq. (
6.41
) is the
{\cal O}(t^{2})
term in the quantity

\left\langle\Psi(t)\right|L_{0}\left|\Psi(t)\right\rangle
, with
\left|\Psi(t)\right\rangle=e^{{\bf i}t\widetilde{L}_{-n}}\left|\Psi\right\rangle
, and therefore as explained in
Section
5.3
is the leading contribution to a Schwarzian functional.

8
Discussion

8.1
Summary

We have described a way to construct Virasoro generators from a purely-chiral wavefunction. The argument is based on entanglement bootstrap axioms within the bulk and a hypothesis [Hypothesis
1
] about the chiral CFT description of the physical edge. The theoretical predictions are tested against numerical data. A notable concept we introduce is the good modular flow; the idea is that such flows preserve the bulk density matrix, but create edge excitations that form a representation of the Virasoro algebra.

We have provided theoretical arguments (based on a hypothesis) and numerical evidence that our method to extract the Virasoro algebra works for both invertible phases and chiral topological orders. In particular, we have tested
p+{\bf i}p
superconductor ground states and found an excellent match with the theoretical prediction. We also tested our prediction on a semion wavefunction, and the error is consistent with what we should expect on systems of small sizes, thus supporting our prediction.

8.2
Further remarks

Our construction of Virasoro generators is effective for purely-chiral systems but not for systems with both left and right movers.
This is because the vector fixed point equation (
4.13
) holds only when the cross-ratios associated with the left- and right-moving modes are the same
\eta_{L}=\eta_{R}
.

This issue can be resolved by separating the left- and right-moving modes using the commutators of the modular Hamiltonians, similar to the construction of the global conformal generators in
[
27
]
. Alternatively, another approach involves performing a Fourier transformation in space followed by a Fourier transformation in time.

Another difference between the purely-chiral system and the systems with both left and right movers is how they change under a modular flow.
We start from a state that satisfies the vector fixed-point equation (
4.13
) and apply a finite time modular flow.
For a system with both left and right movers, since the modular flow acts differently to the left- and right-movers, the cross-ratios become different
\eta_{L}(t)\neq\eta_{R}(t)
and the vector fixed-point equation no longer holds.

On the other hand, for a purely-chiral theory, where there is only one set of modes and one corresponding cross-ratio, we can always set
\eta
in the vector fixed-point equation (
4.13
) to be the cross-ratio.
This ensures that the vector fixed-point equation holds, which enables us to generate the family of states utilized in the section on coherent states, §
5
.

8.3
Future directions

One might think that good modular flows only exhibit nontrivial features near the edge. Nevertheless, we believe that similar results can also be extracted directly from the bulk. In pursuit of this, we will introduce the notion of an “instantaneous modular flow” and generalize the vector fixed-point equation, Eq. (
4.13
).

We would like to further explore the relation between the conformal data of the 1+1D chiral edge and the anyon data of the 2+1D bulk under the framework of entanglement bootstrap. Such results would support the axioms widely believed in the category theory approach to this problem
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
.

One may hope to extend our formalism to include Kac-Moody algebra, which arises in CFT with continuous global symmetry.
Presumably, this involves replacing the entanglement Hamiltonian with the logarithm of the flux-insertion operator
U_{A}(\theta)=\prod_{x\in A}u_{x}(\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{V}}}{{=}}e^{{\bf i}\theta\int_{b(A)}dxj_{0}(x)}
, where
\mathbb{V}
is a generalization of the isometry in Hypothesis
1
to include other degrees of freedom.

Our definition of good modular flow allows the inclusion of regions with disconnected components (this is the distinction between

\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet}
). The modular Hamiltonians of such regions in 1+1D CFT are no longer local
[
40
,
39
,
41
,
42
]
, and depend on the detailed operator content of the CFT (not just
c
and the Hamiltonian density).
We thus expect that such generators involve operators beyond the Virasoro generators, but still preserve the bulk density matrix. This provides a possible route towards generalizing the isometry
\mathbb{V}
in Hypothesis
1
to access other CFT operators besides the stress tensor.
Note that, in contrast to the good modular flow studied in this paper, this type of modular flow with disconnected components likely does not preserve the vector fixed-point equation.

We have expressed the elements of the Virasoro algebra as quantum information quantities from the chiral ground state.
It is known that for a system with CFT behaviors (e.g. Hypothesis
1
), the Virasoro algebra forms a Lie algebra, which is a vector space with commutator relations.
However, the current construction only implies that the constructed Virasoro algebra forms a vector space.
Can we find a checkable condition on the chiral state which implies these commutator relations?

8.4
Preview of results about irregular edges

In Nature, many chiral edges appear to be irregular, such as those in experiments (e.g.
[
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
]
).
The theoretical reason for the persistence of edge modes in such situations is that chiral edges are robust and cannot be gapped out if we add disorder (locally perturbing the Hamiltonian) to the edge. However, as we shall discuss in
[
49
]
, with some general considerations, such irregular edges are secretly regular in a broader sense: they have a well-defined cross-ratio, measured using information-theoretic quantities. We believe that our way of extracting the Virasoro algebra can also work in the context of irregular edges and give evidence for this in the forthcoming paper.

‘piecewise

is unbounded. By

.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
f(\theta)
be a real-valued
2\pi
-periodic function. If
f(\theta)
is piecewise
C^{m}
, then its Fourier coefficient

\tilde{f}_{n}\equiv{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta f(\theta)e^{-in\theta},

(B.1)

has the property
|\tilde{f}_{n}|\sim O(1/n^{m+1})
. That is, to be more explicit,
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{m+1}|\tilde{f}_{n}|
is bounded.

Proof.

Since
f(\theta)=\sum_{n}\tilde{f}_{n}e^{{\bf i}n\theta}
,

f^{(k)}(\theta)=\sum_{n}{{\bf i}}^{k}n^{k}\tilde{f}_{n}e^{{\bf i}\theta n}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\tilde{f}_{n}\cdot n^{k}={(-{\bf i})^{k}\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta f^{(k)}(\theta)e^{-{\bf i}n\theta}.

(B.2)

f(\theta)
is piecewise
C^{m}
. Therefore, let
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{N}
be the discontinuous points of
f^{(m)}(\theta)
, then
f^{(m)}(\theta)
can be written as

f^{(m)}(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Theta(\theta-\theta_{k})\Theta(\theta_{k+1}-\theta)F_{k}(\theta),

(B.3)

where
\theta_{N+1}\equiv\theta_{1}
,
\Theta(x)
is the Heaviside step function.
F_{k}(\theta)
is some smooth function, which is identical to
f^{(m)}(\theta)
on the interval
[\theta_{k},\theta_{k+1}]
. The derivative of
f^{(m)}(\theta)
involves various
\delta
functions:

\begin{split}f^{(m+1)}(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[&\delta(\theta-\theta_{k})\Theta(\theta_{k+1}-\theta)F_{k}(\theta)-\Theta(\theta-\theta_{k})\delta(\theta_{k+1}-\theta)F_{k}(\theta)\\
&+\Theta(\theta-\theta_{k})\Theta(\theta_{k+1}-\theta){dF_{k}(\theta)\over d\theta}\Big].\end{split}

(B.4)

Therefore, even though the function
f^{(m+1)}(\theta)
itself is not upper bounded, the absolute value of its integral

\left|{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta f^{(m+1)}(\theta)e^{-{\bf i}n\theta}\right|

(B.5)

shall be a upper bounded by some finite number
M
. This is because there are finite number of
\delta
functions, and each
\delta
function only contribute a finite number to the result of the integral.

Then utilizing Eq. (
B.2
), one can see that

|\tilde{f}_{n}|\cdot|n^{m+1}|=\left|{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta f^{(m+1)}(\theta)e^{-{\bf i}n\theta}\right|\leq M,

(B.6)

Hence
|\tilde{f}_{n}|\sim O(1/n^{m+1})
.

Appendix C
Improvement of the approximate Virasoro generators

We introduced the construction of the approximate Virasoro generators
\widetilde{L}_{n}
and their expansions in terms of Virasoro generators

\widetilde{L}_{n}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{n,m}L_{m},

(C.1)

with
\lambda_{n,m}
given in Eq. (
6.23
). In this section, we introduce a procedure to reduce the contribution of the higher harmonic terms in
\widetilde{L}_{n}
. As a result of the procedure, we shall obtain the Virasoro generator as a linear combination of the approximate Virasoro generators
\widetilde{L}_{n}
. Furthermore, this indicates that
\{\widetilde{L}_{n}\left|\Psi\right\rangle\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
forms a (non-orthorgonal) basis of the vector space spanned by
\{L_{n}\left|\Psi\right\rangle\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

C.1
Improvement procedure

We first discuss the improvement of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
to produce a better approximation to
L_{n}
. Throughout this section, we shall focus on
|n|\geq 2
cases, as
\widetilde{L}_{0},\widetilde{L}_{\pm 1}
are already equal to
L_{0},L_{\pm 1}
by their constructions.

Upon a close examination of the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{n,m}
in Eq. (
6.23
), we can see that the mode numbers of Virasoro generators in
\widetilde{L}_{n}
are
(-1)^{j}(2j+1),j\in\mathbb{N}
. To be more explicit,

\widetilde{L}_{n}=L_{n}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\lambda_{n,(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}L_{(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n},

(C.2)

where
\mathbb{N}^{*}
is defined as
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and

\lambda_{n,(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}=(-1)^{j}{n^{2}-1\over(2j+1)^{3}n^{2}-(2j+1)}.

(C.3)

Moreover, it is also these modes
\left\{L_{(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
that contribute in the Virasoro mode expansion of
\widetilde{L}_{(-1)^{k}(2k+1)n},k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. This can be seen by

\begin{split}&(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n\cdot(-1)^{k}(2k+1)\\
=&(-1)^{j+k}[2(2jk+j+k)+1]n\\
=&(-1)^{m}(2m+1)n,\quad\text{with }m=2jk+j+k.\end{split}

(C.4)

Therefore, it’s plausible that one can use the approximate Virasoro generators
\left\{\widetilde{L}_{(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
themselves to cancel the contribution of
\left\{L_{(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
in
\widetilde{L}_{n}
, as it won’t introduce new Virasoro modes.

The exact expression for
L_{n}
in terms of
\left\{\widetilde{L}_{(-1)^{j}(2j+1)n}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
can be obtained by the following procedure. Starting from
\widetilde{L}_{n}
, we can see the first sub-leading order contribution is from
L_{-3n}
. This term can be canceled by the replacement

\widetilde{L}_{n}\equiv(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(0)}\to(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(1)}\equiv\widetilde{L}_{n}-\lambda_{-3n}\widetilde{L}_{-3n}.

(C.5)

Then we can cancel the first sub-leading order contribution in
(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(1)}
(which is from
L_{5n}
) by
\widetilde{L}_{5n}
, and so on. Denoting the improved approximate Virasoro generator after the
k
-th correction as
(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k)}
, we can summarize the procedure as the following two steps: (1) Find the first sub-leading order
L_{m}
in
(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k)}
and its coefficient
\lambda^{(k)}_{n,m}
. (2) Cancel this term by

(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k)}\to(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k+1)}=(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}_{n,m}\widetilde{L}_{m}.

(C.6)

Then the Virasoro generator
L_{n}
can be obtained as the limit

L_{n}=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\widetilde{L}_{n})^{(k)},

(C.7)

in the sense that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{n,m}^{(k)}=0,\quad\forall m\neq n.

(C.8)

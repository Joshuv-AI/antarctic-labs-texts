Seifert forms and slice Euler characteristic of links
Vincent Florens, Stepan Orevkov

Abstract
. We define the Witt coindex of a link with non-trivial Alexander polynomial, as a concordance invariant from the Seifert form. We show that it provides an upper bound for the (locally flat) slice Euler characteristic of the link, extending the work of Levine on algebraically slice knots and Taylor on the genera of knots. Then we extend the techniques by Levine on isometric structures and characterize completely the forms of coindex 1 1 under the condition that the symmetrized Seifert form is non-degenerate. We illustrate our results with examples where the coindex is used to show that a two-component link does not bound a locally flat cylinder in the four-ball, whereas any other known restriction does not show it.



1.
Introduction

A knot in
S^{3}
is (topologically)
slice
if it bounds a locally flat disk in
B^{4}
.
If
K
is a slice knot then any Seifert form of
K
is
metabolic

(it vanishes on a half-dimensional subspace). In this case
K
is said
algebraically slice
.
In
[
Lev69a
,
Lev69b
]
Levine gave necessary and sufficient conditions
for a Seifert form of a knot to be metabolic.
These are:

(1)

the Alexander polynomial has the form
f(t)f(t^{-1})

(the Fox-Milnor condition);

(2)

the Tristram-Levine signature function vanishes;

(3)

certain invariants depending on a prime number
p
vanish for all
p
.

Livingston
[
Liv10
]
has shown that it is enough to check (3) for a finite number
of primes only. It follows that there is an algorithm to check whether a given knot
is algebraically slice or not.

Our aim is to extend the work of Levine to links with non-trivial
Alexander polynomial. It is convenient to formulate our results
in terms of the
coindex

\beta(L)
of an oriented link
L
in

S^{3}
. We define
\beta(L)
as the
Witt coindex

of any Seifert form of
L
(on the homology with rational coefficients),
which is the codimension minus dimension of a maximal
isotropic subspace (see §
2.4
).
We show that it does not depend on the choice of the form.
In the case of knots, the coindex coincides with
an invariant introduced by Taylor
[
Tay79
]
.

1.1.
Concordance and slice Euler characteristic

Two oriented links are
concordant
if there exists a properly embedded collection of
locally flat

S^{1}\times I
in
S^{3}\times I
, intersecting
S^{3}\times 0
and
S^{3}\times 1

at the given links.
We first observe that for links whose Alexander polynomial is not zero, the coindex is
invariant by concordance:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be a link in
S^{3}
with
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
.
If
L^{\prime}
is concordant to
L
then

\beta(L)=\beta(L^{\prime}).

Note that the property
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
is invariant by concordance
[
Kaw78
,
NP17
]
;
see Proposition
5.4
.

The
slice Euler characteristic
is defined as

\chi_{s}(L)=\max_{F}\chi(F),

where the maximum
is taken over all locally flat oriented surfaces with boundary
L
,
without closed components in
B^{4}
and transverse to
S^{3}
.
We show that the coindex provides an upper bound for
\chi_{s}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
L
be a link in
S^{3}
. Then the following inequality holds

\chi_{s}(L)\leq 1-\beta(L).

Closely related to the slice Euler characteristic is the
slice genus

of
L
, defined as
g_{s}(L)=\min_{F}\text{genus}(F)
.
Since
2g_{s}(L)\geq 2-n-\chi_{s}(L)
where
n
is the number
of components of
L
, Theorem
1.2
provides also a lower bound for
g_{s}

(note that this inequality between
g_{s}
and
\chi_{s}
is not always sharp;
see the end of §
6.2
).
In the case of a knot
K
,
Theorem
1.2
reduces to the estimation

2g_{s}(K)\leq\beta(K)
proved by Taylor
[
Tay79
]
.

We observe that Theorem
1.2
implies the Murasugi-Tristram inequality

[
Mur65b
,
Tri69
]
, see Corollary
5.8
. In the case
\chi_{s}(L)=1
,
the result implies also the Fox-Milnor theorem for the Alexander polynomial
of links
[
FM66
,
Flo04
]
, see Corollary
5.7
(more precisely, the

one-variable
version). In particular, the Alexander polynomial of a slice knot
has the form
f(t)f(t^{-1})
.
We illustrate in Section
6
that Theorem
1.2
may
offer better estimates of
\chi_{s}(L)
than the Murasugi-Tristram inequality and
the Fox-Milnor Theorem. We present in particular (see Example
6.6
)
two-component links with vanishing Arf-Robertello invariant
[
Rob65
]

where the coindex is used to show that the links do not bound a cylinder in
B^{4}
.

Let us mention that Taylor
[
Tay79
]
observed
that a Seifert form is realizable by a knot of slice genus
g
if and only if
its coindex is less or equal to
2g
. This motivates naturally the definition
of the
algebraic slice genus

of a knot
K
as
g_{s}^{\operatorname{alg}}=\frac{1}{2}\beta(K)
.
In particular,
K
algebraically slice corresponds to
g_{s}^{\operatorname{alg}}(K)=0
.
In view of Theorem
1.2
, one might naturally define the

algebraic slice Euler characteristic
as
1-\beta(L)
. However,
the condition that the coindex of a Seifert form of a link is
1-\chi

does not imply that the form is realizable by a link
L
with
\chi_{s}(L)=\chi
.
For this reason, we do not know for the moment what should be the appropriate
notion of algebraic slice Euler characteristic. See §
6.3
.

1.2.
Algebraic concordance and isometric structures

In order to apply Theorem
1.2
one needs to estimate
\beta(L)
.
We do not know how to compute
\beta(L)
in general. However,
by Levine
[
Lev69a
]
(and
[
Liv10
]
), there is an algorithm to decide whether
\beta(L)=0

(see above). We extend algebraic concordance techniques of Levine on
isometric structures and decide algorithmically whether
\beta(L)=1

(in the case
\Delta_{L}(-1)\neq 0
).

In
[
Lev69a
,
Lev69b
]
, Levine considers integral matrices of even size with

\det(A-{A}^{\top})=\pm 1
and defines the group
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Z}}
of
their concordance classes.
The construction of
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Z}}
works as well for rational matrices and
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Z}}

is in fact a subgroup of the rational algebraic concordance group, denoted
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Q}}
.
The strategy to study
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Q}}
is then to consider
isometric structures
.
These are triple
(V,b,T)
where
b
is a nonsingular symmetric bilinear form on a finite
dimensional space
V
and
T
is an isometry with respect to
b
, with some hypothesis on the
characteristic polynomial of
T
.
The Witt group
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of (concordance) classes of isometric
structures is then shown to be isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Q}}
, see
[
Lev69b
]
.

We extend the notion of concordance to matrices of arbitrary size and non-trivial Alexander
polynomial and show that the coindex is an algebraic concordance invariant.
In order to characterize matrices with coindex
1
, we extend also the notion of
isometric structures and study structures corresponding to forms of coindex
1
.
Then we show that any Seifert form of a link
L
with
\Delta_{L}(-1)\neq 0
has coindex
1

if and only if it is concordant to a very special one determined by
\Delta_{L}
. See
Definition
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
for a precise statement.

Finally, one may recall that there is a group homomorphism
from the concordance group of knots to the algebraic concordance group

\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, sending (the class of) a knot to the concordance class of a Seifert
matrix. This homomorphism is not injective in dimension

3
(see
[
CG86
,
CG78
]
) but it is an isomorphism in higher odd dimensions (see
[
Ker71
]
).
We do not know if the coindex may characterize completely the existence of bounding
manifolds with prescribed Euler characteristic.

Moreover Theorem
1.2
should be extended to Seifert matrices whose Alexander
polynomial is zero. This is the object of a future work, as well as the generalization
to Seifert forms of colored links
[
CF08
]
.

In §
2
, we define the concordance of admissible matrices and
give basic properties of the coindex. In §
3
we introduce isometric structures.
§
4
is devoted to the main result concerning the forms of coindex 1.
In §
5
we relate the topological and algebraic invariants discussed in the
paper. We conclude with §
6
where we illustrate our results by several
examples and discuss some related topics.

2.
Algebraic concordance and coindex

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic zero (in fact, we need only the case when it is
the field of rational, real, or
p
-adic rational numbers:
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{R}

In this section, we consider square matrices with entries in

2.1.
Normalized Alexander polynomial

Given a square matrix
A
, we define its normalized Alexander polynomial
(which we shall call just
Alexander polynomial
) as

\Delta_{A}(t)=t^{-k}\det(A-t{A}^{\top}),\qquad\text{$k$ is such that $\Delta_{A}\in\mathbb{F}[t]$ and $\Delta_{A}(0)\neq 0$}

(in particular,
\Delta_{A}(t)=\det(A-t{A}^{\top})
when
A
is non-singular).
It is clear that

\Delta_{A}(t)=(-t)^{n}\Delta_{A}(t^{-1}),\qquad n=\deg\Delta_{A}.

(1)

One readily checks that
\Delta_{A}(t)
is invariant under the S-equivalence, and then
the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t)
of a link
L
is a well-defined invariant
(not up to multiplication by
ct^{n}
for
c\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
). It can be equivalently defined as

\Delta_{L}(t)=t^{n/2\,}\nabla_{L}(t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})
with
n=\operatorname{span}\nabla_{L}
,
where
\nabla_{L}(z)
is the Conway polynomial defined by

\nabla_{L_{+}}(z)-\nabla_{L_{-}}(z)=z\nabla_{L_{0}}(z)
and
\nabla_{O}(z)=1
.

Our choice of the sign of
\nabla_{L}
corresponds to that in

[
Kau87
]
and
[
Kaw96
, §8.2]
but it is opposite to the choice in

[
Kaw96
, §5.4]
and
[
Lic97
]

in the case of even-component links.

Proposition 2.3

The concordance of admissible matrices is an equivalence relation.
Congruent matrices are concordant.

The symmetry is obvious. The concordance of congruent matrices
(in particular, the reflexivity):

{P}^{\top}\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{Q}^{\top}\!\!AQ&0\\

Remark 2.4

The concordance is no longer transitive if we omit the requirement that the

any matrix is concordant to the zero matrix of the same size.
It would be interesting to find

Lemma 2.5

Let

Suppose that

For all

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=x_{i}+y_{i}+z_{i},

(2)

Since the polynomial

2.3.
A concordance condition for proportional matrices

For a polynomial
F(t)\in\mathbb{F}[t]
, we denote
\bar{F}(t)=t^{n}F(t^{-1})
where
n=\deg F
.

Let
F(t)\in\mathbb{F}[t]
,
F(0)\neq 0
. We say that
F
is
symmetric
if
\bar{F}=cF

with

. Since
\bar{\bar{F}}=F
, we have

in this case. Thus any symmetric polynomial

is either

,

, or
antipalindromic
, i.e.
F=-\bar{F}
.
Evaluating
F
at

, we see that

is a multiple of

when
F

is antipalindromic, and a multiple of

when
F
is palindromic of odd degree.
The equation (
1
) means that the Alexander polynomial of an admissible
matrix of even (resp. odd) size is palindromic (resp. antipalindromic).

Lemma 2.8
.

If two nonsingular matrices

2.4.

Remind that the Witt index

Definition 2.10
.

The

of a matrix

\beta(A)=\beta_{\mathbb{F}}(A)=n-2\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb{F}}(A).

By definition,

Proposition 2.11
.

If admissible matrices
A
and

are concordant, then
\beta(A)=\beta(A^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
A
and

are concordant, which means that there exists

. Hence

By reflexivity, there exists

. By Lemma

.

(4)

3.1.
The concordance group
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{*}

Definition 3.2
.

An isometric structure

(5)

Let

[

3.2.

Given an admissible isometric structure

-module through the action of

The

of

V_{\delta}=\ker\delta(T)^{m}\ \text{ for a large }\ m.

Recall that

By

, Lemma 10]

Note that an irreducible symmetric polynomial different from

[

, Lemma 11]
The group

We also need the following Levine’s result.

[

3.3.
Minimal isometric structures

An isometric structure
(V,b,T)

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(V,b,T)
be a minimal isometric structure.
Then

does not have nontrivial isotropic

-invariant subspaces.

Proof.

Suppose that
b
vanishes on a
T
-invariant subspace

.
Without loss of generality we suppose that
W
is generated by some vector

as a
\mathbb{F}[t^{\pm 1}]
-submodule of

, and that the minimal polynomial which
annihilates

is an irreducible factor
\delta
of
\Delta_{T}
.
Let us choose a vector

such that

Such a vector exists because otherwise

would vanish on
V_{\delta}
,
which is impossible since

is non-singular and
V_{\delta}
is an orthogonal
summand of
V
. Let
W^{\prime}
be the submodule generated by

.
By Proposition
3.6
, the minimal polynomial of

is
\delta
.
Hence
W\cap W^{\prime}=\{0\}
.

Let us show that

is non-singular.
Suppose that

is orthogonal to
W\oplus W^{\prime}
for some
u\in W
and
u^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
.
Then
(u+u^{\prime})\perp W
and
u\perp W
(because

), hence
u^{\prime}\perp W
.
Since
W^{\prime}
is cyclic and
\delta
irreducible,
W^{\prime}
is generated by any its non-zero

where non-zero, we would have

, which
contradicts the condition

Thus
u^{\prime}=0
and hence

is orthogonal to
W^{\prime}
. Since
W
is also cyclic,
the same arguments show that

.

Thus

is non-singular, hence

splits into the orthogonal sum of two

-invariant subspaces

(W\oplus W^{\prime})\oplus(W\oplus W^{\prime})^{\perp}
. Moreover,
the restriction of

to

is metabolic,
which contradicts the minimality of

in the concordance class.
∎

We do not use the following fact, but it seems to be interesting by itself
and it is an almost immediate consequence from the previous lemma.

Let

has a

. Then

is a

∎

Definition 5.1
.

Let
L
be an oriented link and
S
be a Seifert surface of
L
,
with Seifert matrix
A
in some basis of
H_{1}(S)
. The coindex

\beta(L):=\beta(A)

depends only on the isotopy class of
L
and does not depend on the choice of
S
.

The fact that
\beta(L)
is well-defined follows
from the invariance by S-equivalence (Proposition
2.14
).
The rest of this subsection is devoted to a proof of Theorem
1.1
about
the invariance of
\beta(L)
by (topological) concordance
under the assumption
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
.
We start with the following two elementary lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
E,F,
and
G
be
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces of finite dimension. Consider linear
maps
f:E\rightarrow F
and
g:F\rightarrow G
. Then, the following holds

\dim\ker(g\circ f)\geq\dim E-\dim\operatorname{im}g.

Proof.

Indeed,

\dim\ker(g\circ f)=\dim E-\dim\operatorname{im}(g\circ f)\geq\dim E-\dim\operatorname{im}g
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact connected oriented surface and
M
be a compact connected
three-manifold with
\partial M=\Sigma
. Let
i_{*}:H_{1}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H_{1}(M)
be the
map induced by the inclusion in homology with rational coefficients.
Then
\dim\ker i_{*}=\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\dim H_{1}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram, whose rows come from the long exact sequence
in rational (co)homology of the pair
(M,\Sigma)
and the vertical arrows follow from
Poincaré duality.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(M,\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(M)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(\Sigma)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}

Hence
\dim\ker i_{*}=\text{rank}\,\partial=\text{rank}\,i^{*}=\text{rank}\,i_{*}

and the result follows from the rank theorem applied to
i_{*}
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

[
Kaw78
]

If a link
L
is concordant to
L^{\prime}
and
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
, then
\Delta_{L^{\prime}}\neq 0
.

In
[
Kaw78
]
this result is proven for PL-concordance, but the proof easily
extends to the locally flat case. Otherwise, this fact follows from

[
NP17
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
C\subset S^{3}\times I
be the (locally flat) concordance with boundary

L\lx@nobreakspace\subset S^{3}\times\{0\}
and
L^{\prime}\subset S^{3}\times\{1\}
.
Consider Seifert surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
for
L
and
L^{\prime}
,
and let
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
be the corresponding Seifert forms.
One can show that there exists a compact connected oriented
3
-submanifold

M
in
S^{3}\times I
, with boundary

\partial M=S\cup_{L}C\cup_{L^{\prime}}S^{\prime}.

The proof of this fact uses the same arguments as Lemma
5.6
.
Let
h=\text{genus}(\partial M)
and let
K
be the kernel of the map

i_{*}\circ j_{*}
induced by the inclusions, with rational coefficients:

H_{1}(S\sqcup S^{\prime})\simeq H_{1}(S)\oplus H_{1}(S^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(\partial M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(M).

By computing the Euler characteristics, we obtain

\dim H_{1}(S\sqcup S^{\prime})=\dim H_{1}(\partial M)=2h
. By Lemma
5.3
we also
have
\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\dim H_{1}(\partial M)=h
.
Hence, by Lemma
5.2
,

\dim K\geq\dim H_{1}(S\sqcup S^{\prime})-\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}=2h-h=h.

Consider the bilinear form
\Theta=\theta\oplus(-\theta^{\prime})
on

H_{1}(S)\oplus H_{1}(S^{\prime})
and let
x\oplus x^{\prime}
and
y\oplus y^{\prime}
be two elements of
K
.
Let
\alpha,\beta
be 2-chains in
M
such that
\partial\alpha=x\oplus x^{\prime}
and

\partial\beta=y\oplus y^{\prime}
, and
\beta_{+}
denote the push of
\beta

from
M
in the positive normal direction in
S^{3}\times I
.
Then
\Theta
is computed by the intersections in
S^{3}\times I
:

\Theta(x\oplus x^{\prime},y\oplus y^{\prime})=\theta(x,y)-\theta^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\alpha\cdot\beta_{+}.

Since
\alpha\subset M
and
\beta_{+}\subset(S^{3}\times I)\setminus M
,
these chains are disjoint and
\Theta(x\oplus x^{\prime},y\oplus y^{\prime})=0
.
Hence
K
is totally isotropic and
\dim K\geq h
, which means that

\Theta
is metabolic.
Thus
\theta^{\prime}
is concordant to
\theta
. Since
\Delta_{L}
and
\Delta_{L^{\prime}}

are non-zero (see Proposition
5.4
), the Seifert matrices are
admissible and the result follows from Proposition
2.11
.
∎

5.2.
Bounding the slice Euler characteristic via the coindex

The following theorem is equivalent to Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 5.5
.

If a link
L
bounds a locally flat embedded surface
F
in
B^{4}
without closed
components, then the following inequality holds:

\chi(F)\leq 1-\beta(L).

We need the next lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
L
bounds a locally flat surface
F
in
B^{4}
and a Seifert surface
S
in
S^{3}
,
then there is a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
in
B^{4}
with
\partial M=F\cup_{L}S
.

The proof of Lemma
5.6
is folklore; the detailed proof (for knots) can be found
in
[
FL18
, Section 1.5]
. However the same arguments work for links and follow essentially
from topological transversality
[
FQ90
, Chapter 9]
.

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

By Lemma
5.6
, the closed surface
F\cup_{L}S
bounds a
3
-manifold
M

in
B^{4}
. Let
i:F\cup_{L}S\hookrightarrow M
and
j:S\hookrightarrow F\cup_{L}S

be the inclusions. Consider the composition

H_{1}(S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(F\cup S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(M).

The Seifert form
\theta
on
H_{1}(S)
vanishes on
\ker(i_{*}\circ j_{*})
.
Indeed, suppose that
x
and
y
are two elements of
\ker(i_{*}\circ j_{*})
.
There exist 2-chains
\alpha
and
\beta
in
M
with
\partial\alpha=x
and

\partial\beta=y
. Then, as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we push
\beta

off from
M
and we obtain
\theta(x,y)=0
.
Thus
\operatorname{ind}A\geq\dim\ker(i_{*}\circ j_{*})
whence, by Lemma
5.2
,

\operatorname{ind}A\geq\dim H_{1}(S)-\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}.

By Lemma
5.3
we also have

2\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}=\dim H_{1}(F\cup S)=2-\chi(F\cup S)
, thus

\displaystyle\beta(A)

\displaystyle=\dim H_{1}(S)-2\operatorname{ind}A

\displaystyle\leq 2\dim\operatorname{im}i_{*}-\dim H_{1}(S)=\big(2-\chi(F\cup S)\big)-\big(1-\chi(S)\big)=1-\chi(F).

∎

5.3.
Alexander polynomial and signatures

Let
L
be an oriented link.
The next result was due to Fox and Milnor in the case of knots, and extended to (colored)
links by the first author. Note that since we work with rational Seifert forms,
the statement is slightly weaker than the original ones.

Corollary 5.7
.

[
FM66
,
Flo04
]

If
L
bounds a locally flat surface in
B^{4}
with Euler characteristic
1
,
then there exists
F\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that
\Delta_{L}(t)=F(t)\bar{F}(t)

(here
\Delta_{L}
and
\bar{F}
are as defined in Sections
2.1
and

2.3
respectively).

Proof.

Combine Theorem
5.5
and Proposition
2.7
.
∎

Let
A
be a Seifert matrix of
L
. Given
\omega\in S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
the
Levine-Tristram signature and nullity
of
L
are defined as
the signature and nullity of the hermitian matrix

(1-\omega)A+(1-\bar{\omega}){A}^{\top}
.
They give rise to functions
\sigma_{L},\eta_{L}:S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 5.8
.

[
Mur65b
,
Tri69
]

If
L
bounds a locally flat surface
F
in
B^{4}
without closed components,
then for all
\omega\in S^{1}\setminus 1
, the following inequality holds

|\sigma_{L}(\omega)|-\eta_{L}(\omega)\leq 1-\chi(F).

Note that Corollary
5.8
were first proven for smooth surfaces.
A proof for locally flat surfaces can be found in
[
Pow
]
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a Seifert matrix of
L
.
Let
i_{-}
and
i_{+}
be the number of positive and negative eigenvalues of

A(\omega)=(1-\omega)A+(1-\bar{\omega}){A}^{\top}
. Without loss of generality,
suppose that
i_{-}\leq i_{+}
. By definition,
\sigma_{L}(\omega)=i_{+}-i_{-}
and

i_{-}+i_{+}+\eta(\omega)=\dim V
, and
\beta(L)=\dim V-2\operatorname{ind}(V,\theta)
.
Since
\operatorname{ind}(V,\theta)\leq i_{-}+\eta(\omega)
, one gets

\beta(L)=i_{+}+i_{-}+\eta(\omega)-2\operatorname{ind}(V,\theta)\geq i_{+}+i_{-}+\eta(\omega)-2i_{-}-2\eta(\omega)=|\sigma_{L}(\omega)|-\eta(\omega).

By Theorem
1.2
,

|\sigma_{L}(\omega)|-\eta(\omega)\leq\beta(L)\leq 1-\chi(F)
.
∎

Some examples

6.1.
Levels of lower bounds for
\chi_{s}
.

Let
L
be a link with
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
. Note that
L
can be a knot.
Let
A
be a Seifert matrix of
L
. The coindex of
L
is denoted
\beta(L)=\beta(A)
,
see Definition
5.1
. By Theorem
1.2
, we have

\chi_{s}(L)\geq 1-\beta(L).

It is natural to distinguish the following levels of lower bounds of
\beta(A)

(on each level we discuss only the bounds which are stronger that those on lower levels).

•

(Level 1)
The Murasugi-Tristram inequality and the Fox-Milnor Theorem, see Corollaries
5.7
and
5.8
.

•

(Level 2)

\beta(A)\geq\beta(A+{A}^{\top})

(this can be used to prove that
\beta(A)\geq c
for some constant
c
,
only in the case
c\leq 4
; see
2.6
).

Let
\alpha_{L}
be the concordance class of the isometric structure of
A
.
The next two levels are applicable only for proving the estimate
\beta(A)\geq c

for
c=2
or
c=3
.

•

(Level 3)
Application of Levine’s theorem
[
Lev69a
]
(for
c=2
), or Theorem
1.2

(for
c=3
) using the splitting of
\alpha_{L}
according to the factorization
of
\Delta_{L}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

•

(Level 4)
The same as Level 3 but for a splitting of
\alpha_{L}
according to the factorization
of
\Delta_{L}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for some prime
p
.

Example 6.1
.

\chi_{s}(L)
using a Level 2 lower bound for

.

Let

be the link

The
Arf invariant

of a knot

[

]
) of the quadratic
form

q(x)=\theta(x,x)

\theta
is
the reduction mod 2 of a Seifert form on a Seifert surface

[
Rob65
]
). By
[

]
,
\operatorname{Arf}(K)=0

\Delta_{K}(-1)\equiv\pm 1\mod 8
.

Following Robertello
[
Rob65
]
, we say that a knot

is
related
to

if there exists a locally flat surface

of genus 0
in

such that
\partial F\cap(S^{3}\times 0)=L

and
\partial F\cap(S^{3}\times 1)=K

A link

(

are knots) is called in
[
Rob65
]

proper
if all the linking numbers
\operatorname{lk}(k_{i},L\setminus k_{i})
are even.

Let
L
be a proper link. By
[
Rob65
, Theorem 2]
, the Arf invariant
of any knot related to a proper link

is an invariant of
L
.
We call it the
Arf-Robertello invariant
of
L
and denote by
\operatorname{Arf}(L)
.
Note that some interesting interpretations of the
Arf-Robertello invariant are found in
[
Mou
]
for algebraically split links.
If
g_{s}(L)=0
, then the unknot is related to
L
, which gives the following
lower bound for the slice genus.

Theorem 6.7
.

(Essentially
[
Rob65
]
)

If
L
is a proper link and
g_{s}(L)=0
, then
\operatorname{Arf}(L)=0
.

Given a diagram of a proper link
L
with
\mu
components,
one can compute
\operatorname{Arf}(L)
using any knot obtained from
L
by resolving

\mu-1
crossings.
\operatorname{Arf}(L)
can be also computed from a Seifert form of

.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
S
be a Seifert surface of a proper link
L
,
and
\theta
be the Seifert form on

. Let
q(x)=\theta(x,x)

be the corresponding quadratic form on
V
.
Let

be any complementary subspace for
\ker\theta
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Arf}(L)
is the Arf invariant of

As we pointed out in the introduction, for an
n
-component link
L
,
the bound

is not always sharp.
A strict inequality takes place when the maximal Euler characteristic

.

The simplest example is the Whitehead link

6.3.

As shown in
[
Tay79
, Theorem 3]
, if
A
is a Seifert matrix of a knot

then there exists a knot

The corresponding statement for links is false as one can see in the following example.

Indeed, suppose that such a link

6.4.

The results of Levine

or

It is said in
[

, p. 87]

“Note that the corresponding statement for an arbitrary field would
definitely be false (e.g. for the field of rational numbers)”.

.

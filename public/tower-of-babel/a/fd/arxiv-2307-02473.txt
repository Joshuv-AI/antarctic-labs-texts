Fixed elements of pircon automorphisms
Mikael Hansson and Vincent Umutabazi

Abstract
. We prove that the subposet induced by the fixed elements of any automorphism of a pircon is also a pircon. By [ 1 , Theorem 6.4] , the order complex of any open interval in a pircon is a PL ball or a PL sphere. We apply our main results to symmetric groups of the form S 2 ​ n S_{2n} . A consequence is that the fixed point free signed involutions form a pircon under the dual of the Bruhat order on the hyperoctahedral group. Finally, we prove that this poset is, in fact, EL-shellable, which is a type B B analogue of [ 7 , Theorem 1] .



1.
Introduction

A pircon is a poset in which every non-trivial principal order ideal is finite and admits a special partial matching (SPM).
The notion of SPMs was introduced in
[
2
]
as a combinatorial tool for studying the Kazhdan-Lusztig-Vogan polynomials for fixed point free involutions.
Later in
[
1
]
, pircons were introduced as a generalisation of zircons, which were originally invented by Marietti in
[
17
]
.
Pircons have since been studied in different settings of Kazhdan-Lusztig theory like
[
18
,
8
]
.

The Bruhat order on any Coxeter group is a zircon.
More generally, if
\phi
is an involutive automorphism of a Coxeter system
(W,S)
,
then the induced Bruhat order on the set
\mathfrak{I}(\phi)=\{w\in W\mid\phi(w)=w^{-1}\}
of twisted involutions is a zircon
[
12
]
.
Examples of pircons include the Bruhat order on parabolic quotients
W^{J}
,
J\subseteq S
, and on the set
\iota(\phi)=\{\phi(w)w^{-1}\mid w\in W\}
of twisted identities, whenever
W
is finite and not of type
A_{2n}

[
1
]
.

The order complex of any open interval in a zircon is a PL sphere
[
17
]
.
In a pircon, every open interval is a PL ball or a PL sphere
[
1
]
.
In
[
13
]
, it was proved that for a zircon
P
with any automorphism
\theta
, the subposet
P^{\theta}
induced by the fixed elements of
\theta
is itself a zircon.

In this paper, the main results are as follows.
It is first proved that if a finite poset
P
has a maximum and an SPM, then
P^{\theta}
, where
\theta
is any automorphism of
P
, also admits an SPM.
Then, it is proved that if
P
is a pircon, then so is
P^{\theta}
.
As an application, where
P
is taken to be the dual of the Bruhat order on the fixed point free involutions in the symmetric group, we deduce that the fixed point free signed involutions form a pircon.
Therefore, by
[
1
, Theorem 6.4]
, the order complex of (the proper part of) this poset is a PL ball, which is a type
B
analogue of a result of Can, Cherniavsky, and Twelbeck
[
7
]
.
They actually proved that the fixed point free involutions are EL-shellable.
We extend this result by proving that the fixed point free signed involutions are EL-shellable, too.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some definitions and properties of SPMs, pircons, and shellability.
Section
3
contains the main results, where we generalise the main results in
[
13
]
.
In Section
4
, we apply our main results to fixed point free involutions.
Finally, in Section
5
, we establish EL-shellability of the fixed point free signed involutions.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Posets and pircons

Let
P
and
Q
be two posets (partially ordered sets).
Recall that a map
\theta\colon P\to Q
is
order-preserving
if for all
x,y\in P
,
x\leq y
in
P
implies
\theta(x)\leq\theta(y)
in
Q
.
An

is a bijective order-preserving map whose inverse is also order-preserving.
If
P=Q
,
\theta
is called an
automorphism
of
P
.
In this case, let
P^{\theta}
be the subposet of
P
induced by the fixed elements of
\theta
.

Suppose that

with
x<y
.
We say that
x
is
covered
by

, and write

, if there is no

such that
x<z<y
.
An
order ideal

I\subseteq P
is an induced subposet of
P
such that, if
x\leq y
and
y\in I
, then
x\in I
.
An order ideal with a
maximum
(i.e., an element
\hat{1}
such that
x\leq\hat{1}
for all
x\in P
) is called

.
In particular, let
P_{\leq p}=\{x\in P\mid x\leq p\}
.

A

on
P
is an involution
M\colon P\to P
such that
x\vartriangleleft M(x)
or
M(x)\vartriangleleft x
for all
x\in P
.
As invented by Brenti
[
5
,
6
]
, a matching

on
P
is
special
if for all
x,y\in P
with
x\vartriangleleft y
, either
M(x)=y
or
M(x)<M(y)
.
When
P
is Eulerian, special matchings are similar to
compression labellings
introduced by du Cloux
[
10
]
.

The following definition is taken from
[
13
]
.
Zircons were originally defined by Marietti
[
17
]
in a different way, but the two definitions are equivalent as proved in
[

, Proposition 2.3]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

A poset
P
is a
zircon
if, for every non-minimal element
p\in P
, the principal order ideal
P_{\leq p}
is finite and admits a special matching.

The next definition generalises special matchings.
Indeed, we note that a special matching is an SPM without fixed elements.

Definition 2.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
P
be a finite poset with
\hat{1}
and covering relation
\vartriangleleft
.
A
special partial matching
, or
SPM
, on
P
is a function
M\colon P\to P
such that

•

M^{2}=\mathrm{id}
,

•

M(\hat{1})\vartriangleleft\hat{1}
,

•

for all
x\in P
, we have
M(x)\vartriangleleft x
,
x\vartriangleleft M(x)
, or
M(x)=x
, and

•

if
x\vartriangleleft y
and
M(x)\neq y
, then
M(x)<M(y)
.

The following lemma is the “lifting property” for SPMs.
It will specifically serve as the main tool in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
1
]
, Lifting property)
.

Suppose that
P
is a finite poset with
\hat{1}
and an SPM
M
. If
x,y\in P
with
x<y
and
M(y)\leq y
, then

(i)

M(x)\leq y
,

(ii)

M(x)\leq x\Rightarrow M(x)<M(y)
, and

(iii)

M(x)\geq x\Rightarrow x\leq M(y)
.

We now have the following definition.

Definition 2.4

(
[
1
]
)
.

A poset
P
is a
pircon
if, for every non-minimal element
p\in P
, the principal order ideal
P_{\leq p}
is finite and admits an SPM.

From Definitions
2.1
and
2.4
, it is clear that every zircon is a pircon.
Pircons are the main objects of study in this paper.

2.2.
Shellability and signed permutations

A finite poset is
bounded
if it has a minimum and a maximum, and

if every maximal chain has the same length.
A chain
x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}
is

if
x_{i-1}\vartriangleleft x_{i}
for all

, and an
x
-
y

is a saturated chain from

to
y
.

Let

be a finite, bounded, and graded poset.
An

of

is a function
\lambda\colon\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\vartriangleleft y\}\to Q
, where
Q
is some totally ordered set.
If
\lambda
is an edge-labelling of

and

is an

-

-chain, let
\lambda(C)=(\lambda(x_{0},x_{1}),\lambda(x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots,\lambda(x_{k-1},x_{k}))
.
The chain is called

if the sequence
\lambda(C)
is weakly increasing, and
decreasing
if
\lambda(C)
is strongly decreasing.
An edge-labelling
\lambda
of
P
is an

if, for all
x<y
in
P
, there is exactly one increasing
x
-
y
-chain and this chain is
lex-minimal
(i.e., minimal in the lexicographic order) among the
x
-
y
-chains in
P
.
If
P
has an EL-labelling,

is called

, because then its order complex
\Delta(P)
is shellable
[
3
]
.

Let
S_{2n}
denote the group of permutations of
[\pm n]=\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,\pm n\}
.
With the adjacent transpositions
(i,i+1)
as generators, this is a Coxeter group of type
A_{2n-1}
.
Consider the subgroup
S_{n}^{B}
of permutations
\sigma
of

such that
\sigma(-i)=-\sigma(i)
for all

.
This is a standard way to represent the hyperoctahedral group

as the group of “signed permutations”.
(The Coxeter generators are

and

We shall use

for the involutions in

,

The Bruhat order on

is an induced subposet of the Bruhat order on

.
It may be defined as follows, where

(see, e.g.,
[

).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\sigma,\tau\in S_{n}^{B}
. Then
\sigma\leq\tau
if and only if
\sigma[i,j]\leq\tau[i,j]
for all

3.
Pircons and automorphisms

The following theorem and its corollary generalise the main results of
[
13
]
from special matchings to special partial matchings.
The proof ideas are similar, but the possibility of
M
fixing elements introduces additional complications.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P
be a finite poset with a maximum and a special partial matching
M
.
Let also
\theta
be any automorphism of
P
.
Then the subposet
P^{\theta}
induced by the fixed elements of
\theta
has a special partial matching.

Proof.

Since
P
is finite,
\theta
has finite order
K
.
Observe that each automorphism
\theta^{i}
,
i\in[K]
, transforms the SPM
M
into an SPM
M_{i}
, i.e.,
\theta^{i}\circ M=M_{i}\circ\theta^{i}
.
In particular,
M_{K}=M
.

For a given
p\in P
, define

C(p)=\{a\in P\mid\text{$a=M_{i_{t}}\circ M_{i_{t-1}}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}(p)$ for some $i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\in[K]$}\}.

Thus,
C(p)
consists of the elements that are connected to
p
by the SPMs
M_{i}
.
By abuse of notation, we also let
C(p)
denote the subposet of
P
induced by the set
C(p)
.
For
a\in C(p)
given by
a=M_{i_{t}}\circ M_{i_{t-1}}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}(p)
,
define
b\in C(p)
as
b=M_{i_{t}}^{\prime}\circ M_{i_{t-1}}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(p)
, where the
M_{i_{j}}^{\prime}
are recursively given by

(3.1)

M_{i_{j}}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}M_{i_{j}}&\text{if $M_{i_{j}}\circ M_{i_{j-1}}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(p)<M_{i_{j-1}}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(p)$,}\\
\mathrm{id}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For
a_{j},b_{j}\in C(p)
, where
a_{j}=M_{i_{j}}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}(p)
and
b_{j}=M_{i_{j}}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ M_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(p)
,
1\leq j\leq t
, we have
b_{j}\leq a_{j}
.
To see it, we use induction by assuming that
b_{j-1}\leq b_{a-1}
.
We have the following three cases.

(i)

If
a_{j}\geq a_{j-1}
, there is nothing to prove.

(ii)

If
a_{j}<a_{j-1}
and
M_{i_{j}}^{\prime}=M_{i_{j}}
, we have
b_{j}=M_{i_{j}}(b_{j-1})<b_{j-1}
and
M_{i_{j}}(a_{j-1})=a_{j}<a_{j-1}
.
Hence, we may apply the lifting property to get
b_{j}\leq a_{j}
(with equality if and only if
a_{j-1}=b_{j-1}
).

(iii)

Finally, if
a_{j}<a_{j-1}
and
M_{i_{j}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}
, we have
M_{i_{j}}(b_{j-1})\geq b_{j-1}
and
M_{i_{j}}(a_{j-1})=a_{j}<a_{j-1}
.
We can then apply the lifting property to get
b_{j}=b_{j-1}\leq a_{j}
.

Thus
b_{j}\leq a_{j}
, proving the claim. In particular,
b\leq a
.
By construction,
b\leq p
, so if
p
and
a
are both minimal in
C(p)
, we have
a=b=p
.
Hence
C(p)
contains a unique minimal element.
Analogously, if we reverse the inequality in (
3.1
),
C(p)
also contains a unique maximal element.

1

If

.

Claim 1
.

If

Claim 2
.

For any

Let

For any

•

Let

By Claim

.

(i)

(ii)

If

An application of Corollary

As an application of Corollary

, we shall derive an example of a pircon from the fixed point free involutions in the symmetric group

By using

By
[

, we therefore have the following result.
It is a type

[

, which asserts that (the proper part of)

, with the dual of the Bruhat order inherited from

, is a PL ball.

Corollary 4.1
.

The order complex of (the proper part of) the fixed point free signed involutions is a PL ball.

Incitti characterised the covering relation for the involutions as follows.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
16
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
\sigma,\tau\in I_{n}
. Then
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau
in
I_{n}
if and only if
\tau=\ct_{(i,j)}(\sigma)
for some (necessarily unique) pair
(i,j)
.

For
\sigma<\tau
in
I_{n}
, define
\di=\di(\sigma,\tau)=\min{\{i\mid\sigma(i)\neq\tau(i)\}}
and
\ci=\ci(\sigma,\tau)=\min{\{j\geq{\di}+1\mid\sigma(j)\in[\sigma(\di)+1,\tau(\di)]\}}
,
where
\di
and
\ci
are short for
difference index
and
covering index
, respectively.
It is clear from Figure
5.1
that if
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau
, then
i=\di(\sigma,\tau)
and
j=\ci(\sigma,\tau)
.
If
\sigma<\tau
, we sometimes write
\mct_{\tau}(\sigma)
instead of
\ct_{(\di,\ci)}(\sigma)
.
Here
\mathrm{m}
stands for
minimal
:
(\di,\ci)
is lex-minimal among all pairs
(i,j)
such that
\ct_{(i,j)}(\sigma)\leq\tau
.

Let us now consider the signed involutions.

It follows from

.
The necessary parts of the transformation
\Bct

Lemma 5.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\sigma,\tau\in I_{n}^{B}
. Then
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau
in
I_{n}^{B}
if and only if
\tau=\Bct_{(i,j)}(\sigma)
for some (necessarily unique) pair

.

If
\tau=\Bct_{(i,j)}(\sigma)
for some pair

, let
\lambda(\sigma,\tau)=(i,j)
.
By Lemma

, this defines an edge-labelling of

, with

totally ordered by the lexicographic order.
We call

the

on a cover
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau
if

We are now ready to prove EL-shellability of the fixed point free signed involutions.
As mentioned earlier, we follow the strategy in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
.
That is, we prove that the decreasing
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
is contained in
F_{n}^{B}
and that this chain is lex-maximal.
The major difference between the two proofs is that we now have to consider many more possible coverings.
Moreover, some arguments have been simplified.

Theorem 5.5
.

The poset
F_{n}^{B}
is EL-shellable.

Proof.

Let
\sigma<\tau
in
F_{n}^{B}
, let
C_{D}=(\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau_{k}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft\tau_{1}\vartriangleleft\tau)
be the decreasing
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
, where
k\geq 1
,
and let
(i_{\tau},j_{\tau})
be the label on
\tau_{1}\vartriangleleft\tau
.
Observe that if we can prove that
\tau_{1}\in F_{n}^{B}
, it follows that
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{k}\in F_{n}^{B}
, because the decreasing
\sigma
-
\tau_{1}
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
is
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau_{k}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft\tau_{2}\vartriangleleft\tau_{1}
.

Let
h=\di(\sigma,\tau)
.
Since
\sigma
has no fixed points,
h
is an exceedance of
\sigma
(i.e.,
\sigma(h)>h
).
Indeed, if
\sigma(i)<i
, then
\sigma(i)
is an exceedance of
\sigma
, and
\sigma(\sigma(i))=\tau(\sigma(i))\Leftrightarrow\sigma(i)=\tau(i)
.
Consider any cover
\pi\vartriangleleft\pi^{\prime}
in
[\sigma,\tau]
.
Two applications of Definition
2.5
yield
\di(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\geq\di(\pi,\tau)\geq h
.
Since the label
(i,j)
on
\pi\vartriangleleft\pi^{\prime}
satisfies
i=\di(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
, it follows that
h
is in some label on
C_{D}
, and because
C_{D}
is decreasing,
i_{\tau}=h
.

Suppose
\tau_{1}\notin F_{n}^{B}
so that
\tau_{1}\vartriangleleft\tau
is a case where the number of fixed points decreases.
The only such cases are E1.1, N1.1, N4.2, and H2.1, and in all of them,
h
is a fixed point of
\tau_{1}
.
Observe that
\tau_{1}
has fixed points also in E2.6a, N1.2a, N1.2b, N1.3a, N1.3b, N2.2, H1.2, H2.2a, H2.2b, and H2.3, and
h
is not always one of them, but the number of fixed points does not decrease in those cases.
Therefore,
\sigma[h,h+1]>\tau_{1}[h,h+1]
.
Since
\sigma\leq\tau_{1}
, it follows from Definition
2.5
that
\tau_{1}\in F_{n}^{B}
.

We have proved that the decreasing
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
is contained in
F_{n}^{B}
.
The next step is to show that this chain is lex-maximal.
This is done in the same way as in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
, where Incitti’s type
A
version of Lemma
5.3
is used.
We include the argument here in order to make the proof complete.

To obtain a contradiction, consider the lex-maximal
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
, and suppose it is not decreasing;
call it
C=(\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft\pi_{k+2})
and say that
\lambda(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\leq\lambda(\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
.
By Lemma
5.3
,
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}\vartriangleleft\pi_{3}
is lex-minimal among the
\pi_{1}
-
\pi_{3}
-chains in
I_{n}^{B}
.
Thus,
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}^{\prime}\vartriangleleft\pi_{3}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft\pi_{k+2}
, where
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}^{\prime}\vartriangleleft\pi_{3}
is the decreasing
\pi_{1}
-
\pi_{3}
-chain, is lex-larger than
C
, a contradiction.

So, the decreasing
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain in
I_{n}^{B}
is contained in
F_{n}^{B}
and it is lex-maximal.
Now, by reversing the lexicographic order on the set
\{(i,j)\in[\pm n]^{2}\mid i<j\}
, we obtain an edge-labelling of
F_{n}^{B}
such that in each interval
[\sigma,\tau]
, there is an increasing
\sigma
-
\tau
-chain which is lex-minimal.
By Lemma
5.4
and the remark following it, this is an EL-labelling of
F_{n}^{B}
.
∎

The fact that the decreasing chain in
I_{n}^{B}
is contained in
F_{n}^{B}
allows as to compute the dimension of the PL ball
\Delta(F_{n}^{B}-\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\})
as

\rho(\hat{1})-\rho(\hat{0})-2
, where
\rho
is the rank function in
I_{n}^{B}
.
In
F_{n}^{B}
,
\hat{1}
is the reverse permutation
w_{0}
and
\hat{0}
is the product
(-n,-n+1)(-n+2,-n+3)\cdots(n-1,n)
of adjacent transpositions.

The length of
\sigma
in
S_{n}^{B}
is

\ell(\sigma)=\frac{\inv(\sigma)+\negg(\sigma)}{2},

where

\inv(\sigma)=|\{(i,j)\in[\pm n]^{2}\mid\text{$i<j$ and $\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)$}\}|

(the number of inversions of
\sigma
, i.e., the length of
\sigma
in
S_{2n}
) and

\negg(\sigma)=|\{i\in[n]\mid\sigma(i)<0\}|.

By
[
15
, Theorem 3.7]
,

\rho(\sigma)=\frac{\ell(\sigma)+\dna(\sigma)}{2}

where

\dna(\sigma)=|\{i\in[n]\mid-i\leq\sigma(i)<i\}|.

It follows that
\rho(\hat{1})=\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}
.
Furthermore,
\rho(\hat{0})=\frac{n}{2}
if
n
is even and
\frac{n+1}{2}
if
n
is odd, so the dimension is

\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}}{2}-2&\text{if $n$ is even,}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}-2&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

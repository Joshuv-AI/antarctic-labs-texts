Monoidal Structures on Generalized Polynomial Categories
Joseph Dorta, Samantha Jarvis, Nelson Niu

Abstract
Recently, there has been renewed interest in the theory and applications of de Paiva’s dialectica categories and their relationship to the category of polynomial functors. Both fall under the theory of generalized polynomial categories, which are free coproduct completions of free product completions of (monoidal) categories. Here we extend known monoidal structures on polynomial functors and dialectica categories to generalized polynomial categories. We highlight one such monoidal structure, an asymmetric operation generalizing composition of polynomial functors, and show that comonoids with respect to this structure correspond to categories enriched over a related free coproduct completion. Applications include modeling compositional bounds on dynamical systems.

Free (co)product completions and polynomial categories

We begin by recalling two constructions on a category
\mathbbl{C}
: the free product completion and its dual, the free coproduct completion.
Here we follow Moss
[
18
]

Definition 2.1
.

The
free product completion

of a category
\mathbbl{C}
is the category
\Pi\mathbbl{C}
, where

•

an object, denoted
\prod_{i\in I}c_{i}
, consists of

–

a set
I
;

–

for each
i\in I
, an object
c_{i}
in
\mathbbl{C}
;

•

a morphism
\varphi\colon\prod_{i\in I}c_{i}\to\prod_{j\in J}d_{j}
consists of

–

a function
\varphi^{\sharp}\colon J\to I
;

–

for each
j\in J
, a morphism
\varphi_{j}\colon c_{\varphi^{\sharp}j}\to d_{j}
in
\mathbbl{C}
.

The category
\mathbbl{C}
embeds into

as a full subcategory via

, where

As implied by the name “free product completion,” the category

equipped with the embedding

\mathbbl{D}
with small products equipped with functors

We may alternatively characterize

as follows, using the fact that

equipped with the Yoneda embedding
\mathbbl{C}\hookrightarrow[\mathbbl{C},\mathbf{Set}]^{\mathsf{op}}
is the free limit completion of
\mathbbl{C}
and restricting to products.

Proposition 2.2
.

The category
\Pi\mathbbl{C}
is equivalent to the full subcategory of
[\mathbbl{C},\mathbf{Set}]^{\mathsf{op}}
spanned by products of representable functors.

Definition 2.3
.

The
free coproduct completion

of a category

is the category

, where

•

an object, denoted
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}
, consists of

–

a set
I
;

–

for each
i\in I
, an object
c_{i}
in
\mathbbl{C}
;

•

a morphism
\varphi\colon\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\to\sum_{j\in J}d_{j}
consists of

–

a function
\varphi\colon I\to J
;

–

for each
i\in I
, a morphism
\varphi_{i}\colon c_{i}\to d_{\varphi i}
in
\mathbbl{C}

There is a fully faithful functor

Comparing the definitions, we find that

2.2

Proposition 2.4

The category

is equivalent to the full subcategory of

The category
\Sigma\mathbbl{C}
equipped with the embedding

As for products in

Proposition 2.5

If

has all small products given by a distributive law

\prod_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}c_{i,j}\cong\sum_{\overline{j}\in\prod\limits_{i\in I}J_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}c_{i,\overline{j}_{i}}.

(1)

Proof 2.6
.

Hence

spanned by coproducts of representables.
Then the conclusion follows from

If we freely add products, then freely add coproducts, we obtain the central construction of this paper.

Definition 2.7
.

The category

–

–

an

\varphi\colon I\to J
;

–

for each

Proposition 2.8

The hom-sets of
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
are given by

\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}\!\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}c_{i,a},\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}d_{j,b}\Big)\cong\prod_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}\sum_{a\in A_{i}}\mathbbl{C}(c_{i,a},d_{j,b}).

Even though we added products before we added coproducts,

3
Monoidal structures on polynomial categories

Most of the applications of
\mathbf{Poly}
and
\mathbf{Dial}(\mathbf{Set})
rely on their monoidal structures; in this section, we will generalize such structures to
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
.
Throughout, let
(\mathbbl{C},e,\cdot)
be a monoidal category with unit
e\in\mathbbl{C}
and product
\cdot\colon\mathbbl{C}\times\mathbbl{C}\to\mathbbl{C}
.
The monoidal structure on
\mathbbl{C}
then induces monoidal structures on
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
.

3.1
The parallel product

A monoidal product
\cdot
on
\mathbbl{C}
always induces a monoidal product
\odot
on the free colimit completion
[\mathbbl{C}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
of
\mathbbl{C}
: the Day convolution
[
8
]
, which agrees with
\cdot
on the full subcategory
\mathbbl{C}\hookrightarrow[\mathbbl{C}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The Day convolution
\odot
on
[\mathbbl{C}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
restricts to a monoidal product on the free coproduct completion
\Sigma\mathbbl{C}
of
\mathbbl{C}
, yielding a distributive monoidal category
(\Sigma\mathbbl{C},e,\odot)
.

Proof 3.2
.

The Day convolution is a coend construction and thus preserves coproducts.
Hence
\Sigma\mathbbl{C}
is closed under
\odot
, and
\odot
distributes over coproducts:

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\Big)\odot\Big(\sum_{j\in J}d_{j}\Big)\cong\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}(c_{i}\odot d_{j})\cong\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(c_{i}\cdot d_{j}).

(2)

Eq.

2
tells us how to evaluate
\odot
on arbitrary objects in
\Sigma\mathbbl{C}
.
We dualize this construction to obtain an analogous monoidal product on
\Pi\mathbbl{C}\approx(\Sigma\mathbbl{C}^{\mathsf{op}})^{\mathsf{op}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a monoidal structure on
\Pi\mathbbl{C}
with unit
e
whose monoidal product
\circledcirc
is given by

\Big(\prod_{a\in A}c_{a}\Big)\circledcirc\Big(\prod_{b\in B}d_{b}\Big)\cong\prod_{(a,b)\in A\times B}(c_{a}\cdot d_{b}).

Thus, to obtain a monoidal structure on
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
, we may first lift the monoidal structure on
\mathbbl{C}
to
\Pi\mathbbl{C}
, then lift the monoidal structure on
\Pi\mathbbl{C}
to
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

There is a monoidal structure on
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
with unit
e
whose monoidal product, which we call the
parallel product
and denote by
\otimes
, is given by

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}c_{i,a}\Big)\otimes\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}d_{j,b}\Big)\cong\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}(c_{i,a}\cdot d_{j,b}).

Proof 3.5
.

Apply
Proposition

3.3
on
(\mathbbl{C},e,\odot)
to obtain
(\Pi\mathbbl{C},e,\circledcirc)
, then apply
Proposition

3.1
on
(\Pi\mathbbl{C},e,\circledcirc)
to obtain
(\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C},e,\otimes)
.

Example 3.6
.

To justify our use of the name “parallel product," we consider an example.
Let
\mathbbl{C}\coloneqq\mathbbl{1}
, whose unique object we call
\mathcal{y}
.
There is a unique monoidal structure on
\mathbbl{C}
given by
\mathcal{y}\cdot\mathcal{y}=\mathcal{y}
.

Following
[
20
]
, in
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{1}\approx\mathbf{Poly}
, an object
\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\mathcal{y}
, which we denote by
\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{y}^{A_{i}}
for short, can be thought of as an
interface
, with a number of possible positions from
I
it could expose and, according to the position
i\in I
it is currently exposing, a number of possible directions from
A_{i}
it could receive.
A morphism
\varphi\colon\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{y}^{A_{i}}\to\sum_{i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{A^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}
in
\mathbf{Poly}
can then be viewed as an
interaction protocol
between interfaces.
On positions,
\varphi
converts any position
i\in I
that the domain could expose to a position
\varphi i\in I^{\prime}
for the codomain to expose; then on directions,
\varphi
converts any direction
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}_{\varphi i}
that the codomain could receive to a direction
\varphi^{\sharp}_{i}a^{\prime}\in A_{i}
for the domain to receive.

Then taking the parallel product of two such interaction protocols yields a single interaction protocol that models the two original protocols simultaneously—or in parallel.
More concretely, given interaction protocols
\varphi\colon\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{y}^{A_{i}}\to\sum_{i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{A^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}
and
\psi\colon\sum_{j\in J}\mathcal{y}^{B_{j}}\to\sum_{j^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{B^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}}
, their parallel product
\varphi\otimes\psi
converts a pair of positions
(i,j)\in I\times J
from its domain

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{y}^{A_{i}}\!\Big)\otimes\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\mathcal{y}^{B_{j}}\!\Big)\cong\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(\mathcal{y}\cdot\mathcal{y})^{A_{i}\times B_{j}}\cong\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\mathcal{y}^{A_{i}\times B_{j}}

to the pair of positions
(\varphi i,\psi j)\in I^{\prime}\times J^{\prime}
from its codomain

\Big(\sum_{i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{A^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}\!\Big)\otimes\Big(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{B^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}}\!\Big)\cong\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in I^{\prime}\times J^{\prime}}\mathcal{y}^{A^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}\times B^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}}

by applying the on-positions functions of
\varphi
and
\psi
in parallel; then converts a pair of directions
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in A^{\prime}_{\varphi i}\times B^{\prime}_{\psi j}
from its codomain to the pair of directions
(\varphi^{\sharp}_{i}a^{\prime},\psi^{\sharp}_{j}b^{\prime})
from its domain by applying the on-directions functions of
\varphi
and
\psi
in parallel.

3.2
The composition product

Here we introduce another monoidal structure on
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
induced by the monoidal product on
\mathbbl{C}
.

Definition 3.7
.

The
composition product

\mathbin{\triangleleft}
of two objects in
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
is given by

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}u_{i,a}\Big)\mathbin{\triangleleft}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}v_{j,b}\Big)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}(u_{i,a}\cdot v_{j,b}).

We call this the
composition
product as it generalizes the composition operation on polynomial functors when
\mathbbl{C}=\mathbbl{1}
: composing
\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\mathcal{y}
with
\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}\mathcal{y}
yields the functor
\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}\mathcal{y}
.
Distributivity, as given by
Eq.

1
, yields the following alternate form for this product.

Lemma 3.8
.

The composition product can be rewritten as

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}u_{i,a}\Big)\mathbin{\triangleleft}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}v_{j,b}\Big)\cong\sum_{i\in I}\>\sum_{j\colon A_{i}\to J}\>\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\prod_{b\in B_{ja}}(u_{i,a}\cdot v_{ja,b}).

Proposition 3.9
.

There is a monoidal category
(\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C},e,\mathbin{\triangleleft})
.

Proof 3.10
.

Routine, but we will describe the behavior of
\mathbin{\triangleleft}
on morphisms: given

\varphi\colon\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}u_{i,a}\to\sum_{k\in K}\prod_{c\in C_{k}}w_{k,c}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi\colon\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}v_{j,b}\to\sum_{\ell\in L}\prod_{d\in D_{\ell}}x_{\ell,d},

the morphism

\varphi\mathbin{\triangleleft}\psi\colon\sum_{i\in I}\>\sum_{j\colon A_{i}\to J}\>\prod_{a\in A_{i}}\prod_{b\in B_{ja}}(u_{i,a}\cdot v_{ja,b})\to\sum_{k\in K}\>\sum_{\ell\colon C_{k}\to L}\>\prod_{c\in C_{k}}\prod_{d\in D_{\ell c}}(w_{k,c}\cdot x_{\ell c,d})

(whose domain and codomain we have rewritten using

) consists of the following data:

•

an on-positions function
\varphi\mathbin{\triangleleft}\psi\colon\sum_{i\in I}J^{A_{i}}\to\sum_{k\in K}L^{C_{k}}

–

–

•

for each

, sent to

Application: compositional bounds on dynamical systems

Here we give an example of how the structure of

This case study is by no means comprehensive; we seek only to hint at the possibilities of how

Conversely, given

Other monoidal structures on polynomials

Given a monoidal category
(\mathbbl{C},e,\cdot)
, there are at least two other monoidal structures on
\Sigma\Pi\mathbbl{C}
with unit
e
: one given by

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}u_{i,a}\Big)\Bowtie\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}v_{j,b}\Big)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\,\prod_{a\colon J\to A_{i}}\,\prod_{b\colon I\to B_{j}}(u_{i,aj}\cdot v_{j,bi})

and another given by

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\prod_{a\in A_{i}}u_{i,a}\Big)\rtimes\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\prod_{b\in B_{j}}v_{j,b}\Big)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\,\prod_{a\colon J\to A_{i}}\,\prod_{b\in B_{j}}(u_{i,aj}\cdot v_{j,b}).

We would like to know if there are interpretations or applications for these monoidal products as there are for the parallel and composition products.

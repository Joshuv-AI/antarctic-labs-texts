Introduction

We say that a graph
G
is Ramsey for the pair of graphs
(F,H)
, and write
G\rightarrow\big(F,H\big)
, if in every red-blue colouring of the edges of
G
, there is either a red monochromatic copy of
F
, or a blue monochromatic copy of
H
.
When
F=H
, we write simply
G\rightarrow H
.
With this notation, the Ramsey number
r(F,H)
is defined to be the minimum
n
such that
K_{n}\rightarrow\big(F,H\big)
.

For any connected graph
H
the complete
(r-1)
-partite graph with each part of size
v(H)-1
implies that

r(K_{r},H)\geqslant(v(H)-1)(r-1)+1.

A graph
H
is said to be
r
-good if
r(K_{r},H)=(v(H)-1)(r-1)+1
.
Motivated by a result of
Chvátal 1977
, which implies that trees are
r
-good for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the study of Ramsey goodness for general graphs
H
was initiated by
Burr and Erdős 1983
in 1983.
In addition to proving that many sparse graphs are
r
-good, they proposed a number of related problems, several of which remained open for over 25 years.

Since then, Ramsey numbers for dense versus sparse graphs have been extensively studied.
Notably,
Nikiforov and Rousseau 2009
solved all but one of
Burr and Erdős 1983
’s problems.
The main conjecture of
[
11
]
, that all sufficiently large connected graphs with bounded average degree are
r
-good, was disproved by
Brandt 1996
, but
Allen et al. 2013
, among other very interesting results, proved that, for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, every connected bounded-degree graph
H
with bandwidth
o(v(H))
is
r
-good.
Another well known result is due to
Fiz Pontiveros et al. 2014
, who proved that the hypercube is
r
-good for any fixed
r\in\mathbb{N}
as long as its dimension is sufficiently large.
There are many other recent related results in different directions of this area (see, for example,
[
31
,
16
,
18
,
26
]
).
In particular,
Pokrovskiy and Sudakov 2017
proved a natural and important generalization of
Chvátal 1977
’s result for paths, determining
r(H,P_{n})
for any
n\geqslant 4v(H)
, and
Balla et al. 2018
extended this generalization to bounded degree trees.

Moreira 2021
studied Ramsey goodness in the random graph
G(n,p)
, proving a sparse random analogue of
Chvátal 1977
’s result for paths, and
Araújo et al. 2023
proved a similar theorem for all bounded-degree trees.

Another recent line of work is built on the following question.
For a graph
G
with at least
r(F,H)
vertices, what minimum degree condition guarantees that
G\rightarrow\big(F,H\big)
?
The first person to ask this question in the symmetric case was
Schelp 2012
.
More precisely, he asked for which graphs
H
there exists a constant
0<c<1
such that, for a graph
G
with
r(H)
vertices, if

\delta(G)\geq cv(G)

then
G\rightarrow H
(Question 22 in
[
32
]
).

This phenomenon of attaining Ramsey properties with only a dense graph, as opposed to a complete graph, has been extensively explored, particularly in the context of paths and cycles.
For instance,
Nikiforov and Schelp 2008
, in one of the pioneering results related to this topic, showed that if
G
is a graph with
n
vertices then

\delta(G)\geqslant(1-10^{-7})n\quad\Rightarrow\quad G\rightarrow C_{\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}.

More generally, they actually proved that, with the above minimum degree condition, there are all cycles up to length
\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil
in one of the colours

There are several variations of

For instance,

We solve an asymmetric version of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

If

Let

Let

1.1.
Overview of the proof

The first step in the proof of both of our main theorems is the following structural result.
In any colouring of the edges of
G
with no blue
P_{t}
we can find a partition
V(G)=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m}
such that for any
i\neq j
there are no blue edges in
G[A_{i},A_{j}]
and

\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant t-1.

Note that to find a red
K_{r}
in
G
, it is enough to find a clique in
G
with at most one vertex in each part, since there are no blue edges in between parts.
This is equivalent to finding a transversal independent set of the
m
-partite auxiliary graph given by the nonedges of
G
going in between the parts.
(A transversal independent set of an
m
-partite graph is an independent set with at most one vertex in each part.)
Conditions for finding transversal independent sets have been widely studied, and can be seen as generalizations of Hall’s theorem.
In particular, Haxell (see Theorem 3 in
[
20
]
) gave a tight sufficient condition for finding a transversal independent set of size
r
in an
r
-partite graph.
In
Section

3
, we use this result to give a similar condition for finding a transversal independent set of size
r
in an
m
-partite graph, for any
m\geqslant r
(see
Theorem

3.1
).
This condition, which relates to the non-existence of small dominating sets in the graph, can be verified for our auxiliary
m
-partite graph.

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
follows a similar strategy, but the argument described above for finding a red triangle in this
m
-partite graph does not work.
That is, when the number of vertices of the graph is greater than
2t-1
, the weaker lower bound on the minimum degree is not sufficient to verify the hypothesis of
Theorem

3.1
, as we do in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.
To amend this, we replace this part of the argument by a classical theorem of
Bollobás et al. 1974
(see
Theorem

5.2
),
which provides a sharp minimum degree condition for finding triangles in balanced
m
-partite graphs.
The authors of
[
9
]
actually proved an unbalanced version of this result,
but to avoid some technicalities in stating the unbalanced version, we apply the balanced version of this result to an appropriate blow-up of the
m
-partite graph given by the edges of
G
between the parts.
The blow-up will make the graph balanced, and it is enough to find a triangle in this modified graph to find a red triangle in
G
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we give examples showing that the minimum degree conditions in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
and
1.3
are tight, in
Section

3
we use Haxell’s theorem to obtain a sufficient condition for finding a transversal independent set of size

in an
m
-partite graph, and in Sections
4
and
5
we give the proofs of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
, respectively.

2.
Constructions

We present two constructions showing that the minimum degree conditions in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
cannot be improved.
First, we describe a graph
G
with
n=(r-1)(t-1)+1
vertices and

\delta(G)=n-\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil-1

with
G\nrightarrow\big(K_{r},P_{t}\big)
, showing that the minimum degree condition in
Theorem

1.1
is tight.

Example 2.1
.

The graph
G
is obtained by removing a copy of the complete bipartite graph with part sizes
\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor
and
\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil
from the complete graph
K_{n}
.
The blue edges form
r
vertex-disjoint cliques: the two parts of the bipartite graph we removed from
G
, and
r-2
cliques of size
t-1
.
The remaining edges of
G
are all coloured red.

More formally, let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
be disjoint sets with
|V_{i}|=t-1
for every
i\in[r-2]
,
|V_{r-1}|=\lceil t/2\rceil
and
|V_{r}|=\lfloor t/2\rfloor
.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by joining every pair of vertices in
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
, except for those pairs
uv
with
u\in V_{r-1}
and
v\in V_{r}
.
Note that
n=v(G)=(r-1)(t-1)+1
and

\delta(G)=(r-2)(t-1)+\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor-1=n-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil-1.

Now, colour every edge inside
V_{i}
blue, for
i\in[r]
, and colour every other edge of
G
red.
Each part
V_{i}
has size at most
t-1
, so there is no blue copy of
P_{t}
.
Moreover, since the graph induced by the red edges is
(r-1)
-partite with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r-2},V_{r-1}\cup V_{r}
, this colouring also contains no red copy of
K_{r}
.

We will next show that for every
(r-1)(t-1)k<n\leqslant(r-1)(t-1)(k+1)
, where
r\geqslant 3
and
k,t\geqslant 1
, there exists a graph
G
with
n
vertices and

\delta(G)=n-\Bigg\lceil\frac{k}{k+1}\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil\Bigg\rceil-1,

such that
G\nrightarrow\big(K_{r},P_{t}\big)
.
This construction motivates the bound on
\delta(G)
in
1.3
.

Example 2.2
.

The graph
G
is obtained by placing
k+1
cliques of size
\approx n/(r-1)(k+1)
into each part of the Turan graph
T_{r-1}(n)
.
The edges of these cliques are coloured blue, and the remaining edges of
G
are coloured red.

More formally, we will define a graph
G
with
n
vertices as follows.
Let

V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r-1}A_{i}

be a partition of

into
r-1
parts of size that differ at most by one.
Now for each
i\in[r-1]
define

A_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k+1}A_{i,j}

a partition of

into
k+1
parts of size that differ at most by one.
Two vertices

and

if
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
and also if

It is easy to see that the minimum degree of

Thus,

\delta(G)=n-\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil\right\rfloor-1=n-\Bigg\lceil\frac{k}{k+1}\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil\Bigg\rceil-1.

Since

, and the largest blue path has size at most

Note that in

3.
Transversal Independent Sets

In this section we will use a result of Haxell to prove
Theorem

3.1
, below.
Recall that a transversal independent set in an
m
-partite graph is an independent set with at most one vertex in each part.
The theorem gives a sufficient condition for an
m
-partite graph to contain a transversal independent set of size
r
.
A set of vertices
A
in a graph
G
is
dominated
by another set of vertices
B
if
A\subseteq N_{G}(B)
(that is, every vertex in
A
has a neighbour in
B
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m\geqslant r\geqslant 1
, let
G
be a graph, and let
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
be a partition of the vertex set.
Suppose that for every
S\subseteq[m]
, the set of vertices

V_{S}=\bigcup_{i\in S}V_{i}

is not dominated by any set of
2\big(|S|-m+r-1\big)
vertices of
V_{S}
.
Then there exists an independent set
I
with
|I|\geqslant r
, such that
\big|I\cap V_{i}\big|\leqslant 1
for every
i\in[m]
.

A result equivalent to the case
m=r
of
Theorem

3.1
follows from the proof in
[
19
]
and is stated explicitly in
[
20
]
.
For a topological proof of a more general result, see
[
25
]
, and for a simple combinatorial proof, see the survey
[
21
]
.
We will deduce the general case from the case
m=r
.
The proof uses a standard technique for deducing such “defect versions” of variants of Hall’s theorem (see for instance
[
1
]
for a similar approach), but for the reader’s convenience we give the details.

Proof.

Define
G^{\prime}
to be the disjoint union of
G
with
k
disjoint copies of
K_{m}
, which we denote by
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
.
For each
i
, we place exactly one vertex of
T_{i}
in each of the parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
.
More precisely, let
V(T_{i})=\big\{u_{1}(i),\ldots,u_{m}(i)\big\}
for each
i\in[k]
, and define, for each
j\in[m]
,

V_{j}^{\prime}=V_{j}\cup\big\{u_{j}(i)\mid i\in[k]\big\}.

Note that a transversal independent set of size
m
in
G^{\prime}
cannot contain more than one vertex from
V(T_{i})
for each
i\in[k]
, since
T_{i}
is clique.
Therefore, a transversal independent set of size
m
in
G^{\prime}
contains a transversal independent set of size
m-k=r
in
G
.
Thus, it is enough to show that the
m
-partite graph
G^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions of
Theorem

3.1
with
r=m
.
That is, we need to show, for every
S\subset[m]
, that the set

(1)

V_{S}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{i\in S}V_{i}^{\prime}

is not dominated by any set of
2(|S|-1)
vertices of
V_{S}^{\prime}
.

Fix
S\subset[m]
, let
X\subset V_{S}^{\prime}
with
|X|=2(|S|-1)
and assume for a contradiction that
X
dominates
V_{S}^{\prime}
.
Observe that
X
must contain at least
2
vertices from
V(T_{i})
for each
i\in[k]
, since
T_{i}
is a connected component of
G^{\prime}
.
Therefore,

(2)

\big|X\cap V_{S}\big|=|X|-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big|X\cap V(T_{i})\big|\leqslant 2(|S|-1)-2k=2\big(|S|-m+r-1\big).

Note also that
X\cap V_{S}
dominates
V_{S}
, since a vertex in
X\cap V(T_{i})
has no neighbours in
V_{S}
.
Together with (
2
), this contradicts our assumption that
V_{S}
is not dominated by any set of
2\big(|S|-m+r-1\big)
vertices of
V_{S}
, and this contradiction proves (
1
).
As explained above, we may therefore apply the theorem of Haxell (the case
m=r
of Theorem 3.1) to find a transversal independent set of size
r
in
G
, as required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

The first step in the proof is the following lemma, which gives the decomposition described in the introduction.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
G
be a
P_{d}
-free graph with
n
vertices and
\delta(G)\geqslant\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor
.
There exists a partition
V(G)=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m}
of the vertex set such that the following hold for every
i\in[m]
:

(3)

\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant d-1,

A_{i}
is a connected component of
G
, and
G[A_{i}]
has a hamiltonian cycle.

We note that somewhat similar results were proved by
Allen et al. 2013

[
2
, Lemma 19]
and
Moreira 2021

[
27
, Proposition 3.2]
, but neither of these results is suitable for our purposes; the former since it requires a stronger minimum degree condition, and the latter since it does not give a partition of all the vertices.
We will deduce
Lemma

4.1
from the following fundamental lemma of Pósa (see
[
8
]
, Chapter IV, Theorem 2).
For the reader’s convenience, we give a short proof of this result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
P
be a path of maximum length in a graph
G
, let
U=V(P)
, and let
u
and
v
be the end vertices of
P
.
If

(4)

d(u)+d(v)\geqslant|U|

then
G[U]
has a hamiltonian cycle.
Moreover, there are no edges between
U
and
V(G)\setminus U
.

Proof.

Let
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\}
be the vertex set of the path
P
with edge set
\{u_{1}u_{2},\ldots,u_{\ell-1}u_{\ell}\}
.
For
i\in\{1,\ell\}
we have
N_{G}(u_{i})\subset U
, otherwise we could extend our path, contradicting its maximality.
If
u_{1}u_{\ell}\in E(G)
, then this edge together with

forms a hamiltonian cycle in

.
If not, then by (

) and the pigeonhole principle, there is some

such that

and
u_{i}\in N_{G}(u_{\ell})
.
Therefore, the sequence of vertices

u_{1}u_{i+1}u_{i+2}\ldots u_{\ell}u_{i}u_{i-1}\ldots u_{1}

induces a hamiltonian cycle in
G[U]
.

Finally, note that if there were an edge between
U
and

then we could find a path longer than

in

, contradicting our choice of

.
∎

It is enough to verify that

Since
\delta(G_{i})\geqslant\delta(G)\geqslant\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor
and by the assumption that
G_{i}
is
P_{d}
-free, we have

\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant d-1.

Since
\delta(G_{i})\geqslant\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor\geqslant|A_{i}|/2
, we can apply
Lemma

4.2
to the path
P_{i}
in the graph
G_{i}
to show that there is a hamiltonian cycle in
G_{i}[A_{i}]
and moreover that there are no edges between
A_{i}
and
V_{i}\setminus A_{i}
, so
A_{i}
is a connected component of
G_{i}
(and hence of
G
), as required.
∎

Finally, let’s prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The proof will proceed by induction on
r\geqslant 2
.
If
r=2
, we have

\delta(G)\geqslant n-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil\geqslant\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor\hskip-2.0pt,

where the second inequality hold since
n\geqslant t
,
and every edge of
G
is blue.
Thus by Dirac’s theorem we can find a hamiltonian path in
G
and, in particular, a blue
P_{t}
.

Let
r\geqslant 3
, and assume the theorem holds for
r-1
.
Let
n\geqslant(r-1)(t-1)+1
, let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
\delta(G)\geqslant n-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil
, and let
R
and
B
be the graphs of red edges and blue edges, respectively, in a
2
-edge-colouring of
E(G)
.
Assume that this colouring contains no blue copy of
P_{t}
; we will show it contains a red copy of
K_{r}
.

Suppose first that there exists
u\in V(G)
with
d_{R}(u)\geqslant n-t+1\geqslant(r-2)(t-1)+1
.
We claim that, in this case,

\delta\big(G[N_{R}(u)]\big)\geqslant d_{R}(u)-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil\hskip-2.0pt.

Indeed, for every
v\in N_{R}(u)
we have that

\displaystyle\big|N_{G}(v)\cap N_{R}(u)\big|

\displaystyle\geqslant\big|N_{G}(v)\big|-\big|N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{R}(u)\big|

\displaystyle\geqslant(n-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil)-(n-d_{R}(u))

\displaystyle=d_{R}(u)-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil\hskip-2.0pt.

Therefore,
G[N_{R}(u)]
satisfies the induction hypothesis and it follows that

G[N_{R}(u)]\rightarrow\big(K_{r-1},P_{t}\big).

But
G
has no blue
P_{t}
, so there is a red
K_{r-1}
in
N_{R}(u)
.
Together with
u
this forms a red copy of
K_{r}
in
G
, as we wanted to find.

We can therefore assume that
d_{R}(u)\leqslant n-t
, and hence,

d_{B}(u)\geqslant\delta(G)-d_{R}(u)\geqslant n-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil-(n-t)=t-\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil=\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor

for every
u\in V(G).

Applying
Lemma

4.1
to the graph
B
with
d=t
we obtain a partition

V(G)=V(B)=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m}

with

(5)

\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant t-1

for each
i\in[m]
, and hence

(6)

r\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rceil\leqslant m,

since
n\geqslant(r-1)(t-1)+1
.
Moreover, all the edges of
G
between different parts are red.

Observe that any
K_{r}
in
G
with at most one vertex in each part must be a red
K_{r}
, which motivates the following definition.
Define
H
to be the
m
-partite graph with parts
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
, in which two vertices are connected in
H
if they are not an edge of
G
.
In this way, it is enough for us to find an independent transversal set of size
r
in
H
to yield a contradiction.

In order to do so, we will show that
H
with the partition
V(H)=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m}
satisfies the conditions of
Theorem

3.1
.
Let
S\subset[m]
and note that

\Delta(H)\leqslant n-1-\delta(G)\leqslant\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil-1.

Let
X\subset A_{S}
be any set with
|X|\leqslant 2(|S|-m+r-1)
, and observe that
X
dominates at most

\Delta(H)\cdot|X|\leqslant 2\big(|S|-m+r-1\big)\big(\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil-1\big)

vertices.
Now, observe that

(7)

\displaystyle 2\big(|S|-m+r-1\big)\big(\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil-1\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(r-|S^{c}|-1\big)(t-1)

(8)

\displaystyle\leqslant n-|S^{c}|(t-1)-1

(9)

\displaystyle<|A_{S}|,

where (
7
) holds because
|S|+|S^{c}|=m
and
2\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil\leqslant t+1
, (
8
) is just our assumption that
n\geqslant(r-1)(t-1)+1
, and (
9
) follows because
|A_{i}|\leqslant t-1
for every
i\in S^{c}
.
It follows that
X
cannot dominate
A_{S}
, and therefore, by
Theorem

3.1
, there exists an independent transversal set of size
r
in
H
.
By the observations above, this is exactly a red copy of
K_{r}
in
G
, as required.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we will prove
Theorem

1.2
; the proof uses some of the same ideas as that of
Theorem

1.1
, but instead of using
Theorem

3.1
, we will use a different idea to find a triangle in some blow-up of the red graph.

We will need the following straightforward (and presumably well-known) proposition, which gives a tight lower bound condition on the minimum degree of a graph with
n
vertices to contain a path with at least
\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil
vertices.
It is a simpler variant of an old result of
Alon 1986
(see also
[
13
]
), which says that every graph with minimum degree
\left\lceil n/(k+1)\right\rceil
contains a cycle of length
\left\lfloor n/k\right\rfloor
, but since it follows easily from
Lemma

4.1
, we give the proof for completeness.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices. If
G
has minimum degree at least
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rfloor
then it contains a path with
\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil
vertices.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
G
is
P_{\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil}
-free.
We will apply
Lemma

4.1
to the graph
G
with
d=2\left\lfloor n/(k+1)\right\rfloor+1
.
The reader can easily verify that

(10)

\delta(G)\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rfloor\qquad\text{ and }\qquad d\geqslant\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{k}\bigg\rceil\hskip-2.0pt.

Since
G
is
P_{\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil}
-free, it follows that
G
is also
P_{d}
-free.
Thus, by
Lemma

4.1
, we obtain a partition
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
of the vertices of
G
with

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{k}\bigg\rceil-1

for each
i\in[m]
, where the upper bound holds since
G[A_{i}]
is hamiltonian and
P_{\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil}
-free.
Then, using
\left\lceil x\right\rceil-1<x<\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor+1
,

k=\frac{n}{\frac{n}{k}}<\frac{n}{\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil-1}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rfloor+1}<\frac{n}{\frac{n}{k+1}}=k+1,

yielding the desired contradiction since
m
must be an integer.
∎

For the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, applying
Theorem

3.1
does not work.
The reason for that is because, in this case, the condition about the non-existence of small dominating sets is too strong.
Instead, we use the following theorem of
Bollobás et al. 1974
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
H
be a balanced
m
-partite graph where each part has size
N
.
If

\delta(H)>\bigg\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\bigg\rfloor N

then
H
contains a triangle.

The authors of
[
9
]
actually prove an unbalanced version of this theorem.
To avoid using this more technical statement, we will use a simple blow-up argument in order to apply the balanced case directly.
We do this in the following simple corollary, which is tailored for our application.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
H
be an
m
-partite graph with parts
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
where
m=2k+1
.
If for each
i,j\in[m]
and
u\in A_{i}
we have

(11)

\big|N_{H}(u)^{c}\setminus A_{i}\big|<k|A_{j}|,

then
H
contains a triangle.

Proof.

We would like to apply
Theorem

5.2
to
H
, but since
H
is not necessarily balanced, we first need to construct from
H
an auxiliary graph
H^{\prime}
with balanced parts.
Consider a blow-up of
H
into a balanced
m
-partite graph
H^{\prime}
with parts
A_{1}^{\prime},A_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{m}^{\prime}
obtained in the following way: for each
i\in[m]
, we replace each vertex
u\in A_{i}
by a set
F(u)\subset A_{i}^{\prime}
of
\frac{N}{|A_{i}|}
vertices, where
N:=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|A_{i}|
.
More precisely, for each
i\in[m]

A_{i}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{u\in A_{i}}F(u)

and

E(H^{\prime})=\big\{xy:x\in F(u)\text{ and }y\in F(v)\text{ for some }uv\in E(H)\big\}.

Observe that every triangle in
H^{\prime}
corresponds to a triangle in
H
and therefore, by
Theorem

5.2
, to finish the proof it will suffice to show that

\delta(H^{\prime})>\bigg\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\bigg\rfloor N=kN.

Let
M=\min_{j\in[n]}|A_{j}|
and observe that
|F(u)|\leqslant N/M
for every
u\in V(H)
, and therefore for every
i\in[m]
,
u\in A_{i}
and
u_{0}\in F(u)
, we have

\big|N_{H^{\prime}}(u_{0})^{c}\setminus A_{i}^{\prime}\big|\leqslant\frac{N}{M}\big|N_{H}(u)^{c}\setminus A_{i}\big|<kN,

where the second inequality holds from the hypothesis (
11
).
Therefore, since
|A_{i}^{\prime}|=N
,

\delta(H^{\prime})>mN-kN-|A_{i}^{\prime}|=kN,

as we wanted to prove.
∎

Finally, let’s proceed to the proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Let
n=2(t-1)k+s
vertices, where
1\leqslant s\leqslant 2(t-1)
, let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and

\delta(G)\geqslant\bigg\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor\hskip-2.0pt,

and let
R
and
B
be the graphs of red edges and blue edges, respectively, in a
2
-edge-colouring of
E(G)
.
Assume that this colouring contains no blue copy of
P_{t}
; we will show that it contains a red copy of

.

Suppose first that there exists

\displaystyle\big|N_{G}(v)\cap N_{R}(u)\big|

\displaystyle\geqslant\big|N_{G}(v)\big|-\big|N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{R}(u)\big|

as claimed, where the last inequality uses that the function

Now, by equation (
12

5.1
in

which implies there is a red edge in

.

Therefore, we can assume that
\Delta(R)\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-1
, and hence,

\displaystyle\delta(B)\geqslant\delta(G)-\Delta(R)\geqslant

\displaystyle\bigg\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor-\left(\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil-1\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor\hskip-2.0pt,

since

.
We will apply
Lemma

4.1
to the graph
B
with

d=2\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor+1.

We may do so since

(13)

\delta(B)\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor,

and since

is

-free, which follows since

, and therefore

Hence, by

4.1

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant t-1

Define an auxiliary graph

Our goal now is to apply

Let’s verify first that, in fact,

To do so, observe that, by (

\frac{n}{2(k+1)}\leqslant\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil<\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leqslant|A_{i}|\leqslant t-1<\frac{n}{2k}

Now it is enough to verify the bound in (

Let

Hence, for every

and

\displaystyle\leqslant(n-|A_{i}|)-(\delta(G)-|A_{i}|+1)

\displaystyle\leqslant n-\left(\bigg\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg\lceil\frac{n}{2}\bigg\rceil\right\rfloor+1\right)

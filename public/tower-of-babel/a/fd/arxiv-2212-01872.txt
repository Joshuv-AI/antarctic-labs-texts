A local-to-global inequality for spectral invariants and an energy dichotomy for Floer trajectories
Lev Buhovsky, Shira Tanny

Abstract
We study a local-to-global inequality for spectral invariants of Hamiltonians whose supports have a “large enough” tubular neighborhood on semipositive symplectic manifolds. In particular, we present the first examples of such an inequality when the Hamiltonians are not necessarily supported in domains with contact type boundaries, or when the ambient manifold is irrational. This extends a series of previous works studying locality phenomena of spectral invariants [ Pol14 , Sey15 , Ish15 , HLRS16 , GT20 , Tan21 ] . A main new tool is a lower bound, in the spirit of Sikorav, for the energy of Floer trajectories that cross the tubular neighborhood against the direction of the negative-gradient vector field.



1
Introduction and results.

Hamiltonian spectral invariants were introduced by Oh and Schwartz
[
Oh05
,
Sch00
]
and are a central tool in studying dynamical properties of Hamiltonian flows. On a closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, these invariants assign to each Hamiltonian
F:M\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and a non-zero quantum homology class
a\in QH_{*}(M)
a real number,
c(F;a)\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The spectral invariants are defined using Floer homology, which is a global invariant of the manifold. However, it is known that in some cases these invariants admit a local behavior. The first evidence for a local behavior of spectral invariants was due to Humilière, Le Roux and Seyfaddini
[
HLRS16
]
. They considered Hamiltonians supported in certain disconnected open subsets on aspherical symplectic manifolds
1

1

Recall that a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is called aspherical if
\omega|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=c_{1}|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
.
. In this setting they showed that the spectral invariant with respect to the fundamental class
[M]\in QH_{*}(M)
is determined by the invariants of the restrictions to the connected components.
More explicitly, if
F
is a Hamiltonian supported on a disconnected subset
V:=\bigsqcup V_{i}
satisfying certain conditions
2
2

2

Humilière, Le Roux and Seyfaddini assume that the connected components
V_{i}
are incompressible Liouville domains.
, and
F_{i}
is the restriction of
F
to
V_{i}
, Humilière, Le Roux and Seyfaddini showed that

c(F;[M])=\max_{i}c(F_{i};[M]).

This formula does not hold for a general homology class. In

[
GT20
]
, Ganor and Tanny proved that an inequality holds for all classes, again on aspherical manifolds and the same assumptions on the supports. Moreover, they showed that in this setting spectral invariants are determined by the restriction of the Hamiltonian to a neighbourhood of its support and are independent of the ambient manifold. Such strong local behavior is known to fail on general non-aspherical symplectic manifolds. However, there are no known counterexamples on any closed symplectic manifold to the following inequality, which we refer to as the
max-inequality
:

c(F;a_{1}*\cdots*a_{L})\leq\max_{i}c(F_{i};a_{i}),

(1)

where
F=F_{1}+\cdots+F_{L}
,
F_{i}
are disjointly supported,
a_{i}\in H_{*}(M)\subset QH_{*}(M)
and
*
is the quantum product. There is also an extension of this inequality for general quantum homology classes, which is stated in Theorem
3
below. The max-inequality can be interpreted as a local-to-global inequality, which is a weaker locality phenomenon than the aforementioned results on aspherical symplectic manifolds.

In
[
Tan21
]
the max-inequality (
1
) is proved for
a_{i}=[M]\in QH_{*}(M)
, in various settings. That paper extended a line of previous works on locality of spectral invariants, including
[
Pol14
,
Sey15
,
Ish15
,
HLRS16
,
GT20
]
.
The methods of all of the aforementioned works allowed studying locality of spectral invariants only for restricted types of supports and closed symplectic manifolds.
In particular,
the Hamiltonians are assumed to be supported in domains with
contact-type
boundaries and the ambient manifold is assumed to be
rational
, meaning that
\omega(\pi_{2}(M))
is a discrete subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
. These technical assumptions seem to be a side-effect of the tools used or developed in the above works. It is currently unknown whether the max-inequality holds without any assumptions on the manifold or the support:

Question 1
.

Does there exist a closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
and disjointly supported Hamiltonians
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that

c(F_{1}+F_{2};a*b)>\max\big\{c(F_{1};a),c(F_{2};b)\big\}

for some
a,b\in H_{*}(M)\subset QH_{*}(M)
?

In the current paper we prove that the max-inequality holds for all quantum homology classes,
under the assumption that the connected components of the support are “far enough” from each other. More explicitly, we present a measurement of (disjoint) collar neighborhoods around the connected components of the support, that is inspired by Sikorav’s energy bounds for holomorphic curves
[
Sik94
]
. We show that whenever these sizes are larger than the spectral invariants of the restrictions
F_{i}
, then the max-inequality (
1
) holds. We work on semipositive manifolds (see definition in (
3
)) due to the simpler foundations of Floer homology in this case. However, we expect our methods to be applicable on any closed symplectic manifold, using virtual techniques (e.g.
[
Par16
]
).

1.1
A Sikorav-type energy bound for Floer trajectories.

To state the results more formally, let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold and let
V
be an open subset of
M
with smooth boundary. Fix a tubular neighborhood
N
of
\partial V
in
M\setminus V
, namely,
N\subset M\setminus V
is diffeomorphic to
[0,1]\times\partial V\tilde{\rightarrow}N
, with
\{0\}\times\partial V
mapped to
\partial V\subset N
. Denote by
\widehat{V}:=\overline{V}\cup N
, of which we think as an extension of
V
, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1
:
An illustration of the geometric setting. Here
N
is a smooth tubular neighborhood of the boundary of
V
.

In
[
Sik94
]
, Sikorav showed that the energy of a pseudoholomorphic curve that crosses such a neighborhood
N
is bounded below by a constant depending on
N
. In the current paper, we concern Hamiltonian Floer homology and therefore work with Floer trajectories rather than holomorphic curves (see Section
2
for preliminaries on Floer homology). Lower bounds for the energy of Floer trajectories that cross certain regions were studied in various works, e.g.,
[
Ush09
,
Hei12
,
GV21
]
and have been used frequently. These energy lower bounds depend on the Hamiltonian in question and tend to zero when the Hamiltonian does. This phenomenon reflects the fact that Morse flow lines are a special case of Floer trajectories, and they can travel large distances with small energy, when the gradient of the Hamiltonian is small. On the other hand, when the Hamiltonian is zero, Floer trajectories are pseudoholomorphic curves. Therefore we see a dichotomy between the energies of crossing pseudoholomorphic curves and Morse flow lines, as two particular cases of Floer trajectories. A natural question, which is also motivated by the study of locality in Floer homology, is whether there is a lower bound, that does not tend to zero with the Hamiltonian, for the energies of Floer trajectories that cross “against” the negative-gradient direction, as illustrated in Figure
2
. The following theorem gives an affirmative answer to this question.

Theorem 2
.

Let
h:{N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function without critical points such that
h|_{\partial V}=0
and
h|_{\partial\widehat{V}}=1
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}
and a constant
C(N,g_{J},h)>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and for any homotopy of Hamiltonians
H:M\times S^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
H|_{N}(x,t,s)=\varepsilon\cdot h(x){+\beta(s,t)}
for some
\beta:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, the following holds. Any Floer trajectory
u
with respect to
(H,J)
that intersects both
V
and
M\setminus\widehat{V}
satisfies

•

either
x_{-}\subset M\setminus\widehat{V}
and
x_{+}\subset V
, where
x_{\pm}:=\lim_{s\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(s,-)
,

•

or,
E(u)\geq C(N,g_{J},h)
.

(a)

Floer trajectories whose energies are bounded below, analogously to pseudoholomorphic curves.

(b)

Floer trajectories that go “down-hill” and might have small energies, like Morse flow-lines.

Figure 2
:
An illustration of crossing trajectories with and without energy lower bound, as stated in Theorem
2
.

Remark 1.1
.

(a)

Theorem
2
is formulated for homotopies of Hamiltonians, but applies to Hamiltonians as well. Indeed, given a Hamiltonian
F:M\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we can identify it with a constant homotopy, namely
H(x,t,s)=F(x,t)
.

(b)

In Theorem
2
, the lower bound depends only on the “modeling” function

and does not shrink when
H
tends to a constant on
N
(that is, when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
).

(c)

We actually prove Theorem
2
for small perturbations of such homotopies as well. Namely, we show that the assertion of Theorem
2
holds for any homotopy
H
such that
H|_{N}(x,t,s)=\varepsilon h(x)+h^{\prime}(x,t,s)+\beta(s,t)
where
h^{\prime}:M\times S^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a
C^{\infty}
small homotopy and the support of
\partial_{s}h^{\prime}
is uniformly bounded. Note that we assume that
h^{\prime}
is much smaller than
\varepsilon h
.

The proof of Theorem
2
is given in Section
4
. It uses the arguments of Sikorav
[
Sik94
]
as well as Hein
[
Hei12
]
, but requires new arguments as well.
Motivated by the Theorem
2
we define a measurement of the tubular neighborhood

, to be the maximal energy lower bound for Floer trajectories that crosses
N
“against” the negative-gradient direction.

Definition 1.2
.

The
Floer width
of

w(N):=\sup\left\{C(N,g_{J},h)\ |\ J\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{reg}},\ h:{N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\ Crit(h)=\emptyset,h|_{\partial V}=0,h|_{\partial\widehat{V}}=1\right\}.

(2)

Here
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
is a residual subset of the space of all
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures, see Section

.

Example 1.3
.

Suppose that there exists a symplectic embedding
\psi
of a ball
B^{2n}(R_{+})
of radius
R_{+}
into
M
. Fix
R_{-}<R_{+}
and let
N:=\psi\big(B^{2n}(R_{+})\setminus B^{2n}(R_{-})\big)

w(N)\geq C\cdot(R_{+}-R_{-})^{2}

.

Remark 1.4
.

The measurement

is weakly continuous in the following sense. For every
\delta

such that
w(N^{\prime})\geq w(N)-\delta

is obtained as

for some

1.2
The max-inequality.

Our next main result states that the max-inequality (
1
) holds for non-negative Hamiltonians supported in domains having disjoint tubular neighborhoods
N_{i}
, such that the widths
w(N_{i})
(as in Definition
1.2
) are large enough, see Figure
3
.
In this section we assume that the closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is semipositive, namely, for every
A\in\pi_{2}(M)
,

3-n\leq c_{1}(A)<0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\omega(A)\leq 0.

(3)

Semipositive manifolds include Calabi-Yau manifolds, as well as all closed symplectic manifolds of dimension up to 6 (see, e.g.,
[
MS12
, Section 6.4]
).
See Remark
1.6
below for a discussion regarding general closed symplectic manifolds.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a semipositive symplectic manifold and let
F_{i}:M\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be non-negative Hamiltonians supported in domains
V_{i}\subset M
. Suppose that:

•

There exist tubular neighborhoods
N_{i}
around
V_{i}
, such that
\widehat{V}_{i}:=\overline{V_{i}}\cup N_{i}
are pairwise disjoint,

•

a_{i}\in QH_{*}(M)
are such that
c(F_{i};a_{i})-{\it val}(a_{i})<w(N_{i})
.

Then,

c\Big(\sum_{i}F_{i};*_{i}a_{i}\Big)\leq\left(\sum_{i}{\it val}(a_{i})\right)+\max_{i}\{c(F_{i};a_{i})-{\it val}(a_{i})\}.

(4)

In particular, if
a_{i}\in H_{*}(M)
,
{\it val}(a_{i})=0
and
c\Big(\sum_{i}F_{i};*_{i}a_{i}\Big)\leq\max_{i}c(F_{i};a_{i})
.

:
An illustration of Hamiltonians for which we prove the max-inequality.

Here
{\it val}:QH_{*}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is the valuation map whose definition is recalled in Section
2
. We remark that inequality (
1
) does not hold for general quantum homology class without correcting with respect to the valuations, as done in (
4
). This can be seen by taking
F=0
and
a_{i}=[M]q^{A}
, since
c(0;a)={\it val}(a)
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension at most
6
, namely,
n\leq 3
. For example, consider
(M,\omega)=(S^{2}\times S^{2},\omega_{a}\oplus\omega_{b})
where
\omega_{a}
(respectively
\omega_{b}
) is the area form on
S^{2}
of total area
a
(respectively

, the symplectic manifold

is irrational.

There exists a universal constant
\kappa>0
such that the following holds. Let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L}
be finite collection of symplectic embedding of balls

into

with disjoint images

This follows from Theorem

Proposition 4
.

Let

Remark 1.6

(Foundations of Floer homology on general closed manifolds)
.

When
(M,\omega)
is a general closed manifold (i.e., not necessarily semipositive), one needs to consider virtual counts of moduli spaces, such as
[
Par16
]
, in order to construct Floer homology. We expect our results and methods to hold for any type of virtual counts, for which (apart from the well-definedness of Floer homology) the following holds:

1.

Given an open subset
U\subset M
and a moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}
of stable maps such that all of its elements are contained in
U
, the virtual count of
{\mathcal{M}}
depends only on the Floer data (i.e. an almost complex structure and a Hamiltonian / homotopy) restricted to
U
.

2.

The virtual count of an empty moduli space (when there are no stable maps between two given generators) is zero.

3.

The Floer chain complex of a
C^{\infty}
-small time independent Hamiltonian coincides with its Morse chain complex.

1.3
Application to Polterovich’s Poisson bracket invariant

A central application of the max-inequality (
1
) concerns the Poisson bracket invariant of covers, which was defined by Polterovich in
[
Pol14
]
. This invariant assigns a non-negative number,
pb({\mathcal{U}})
, to a finite open cover
{\mathcal{U}}=\{U_{i}\}
of a closed symplectic manifold. The Poisson bracket invariant is known to be strictly positive when the cover consists of displaceable sets. Polterovich conjectured a lower bound for this invariant, which can be interpreted as an uncertainty principle:

Conjecture 1.7

(Polterovich,
[
Pol14
]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold. There exists a constant
C_{M}
, depending only on the symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, such that for every finite open cover
{\mathcal{U}}=\{U_{i}\}
of
M
,

pb({\mathcal{U}})\geq\frac{C_{M}}{e({\mathcal{U}})},

where
e({\mathcal{U}}):=\max_{i}e(U_{i})
is the maximal displacement energy of a set from
{\mathcal{U}}
.

This conjecture was proved for the case where
M
is any surface in
[
BLT20
]
, and for surfaces other than the sphere in
[
Pay18
]
. In higher dimensions the conjecture is still open. In
[
EPZ07
]
, Entov, Polterovich and Zapolsky proved a lower bound for
pb
that decays with the number of sets. Using the arguments of Polterovich
[
Pol14
]
one can use the max inequality (whenever it is proved) to obtain a better lower bound for
pb
that decays with the
degree
of the cover, which is the maximal number of sets intersected by a single set:

d({\mathcal{U}}):=\max_{i}\#\{j:\bar{U}_{i}\cap\bar{U}_{j}\neq\emptyset\}.

This approach was taken in
[
Pol14
,
Sey15
,
Ish15
]
, though these works do not prove a max-inequality, but weaker statements that are sufficient to obtain the lower bound for
pb
. We refer to
[
Tan21
]
for a more detailed overview of the above works and the relation between the max-inequality and the Poisson bracket invariant.

Note that the works mentioned above are all restricted to open covers by sets which are domains with contact-type boundaries. Moreover, they all require the manifold to be rational, i.e. that
\omega(\pi_{2}(M))
is a discrete subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Theorem
3
does not require the supports to be contained in domains with contact-type boundaries and therefore significantly extends the scope of open covers for which the Poisson bracket invariant can be bounded in terms of the degrees of covers. Moreover, Theorem
3
applies to all semipositive manifolds. In particular, our results provide the first examples of irrational symplectic manifolds on which the improved bound for the Poisson bracket invariant is achieved.

Example 1.8
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension at most
6
. Let
{\mathcal{U}}:=\{U_{i}\}_{i}
be a cover of
M
by symplectically embedded balls,
U_{i}:=\psi_{i}(B^{2n}(r_{i}))
such that for each
i
the embedding
\psi_{i}
extends to a symplectic embedding of the ball
B^{2n}(r_{i}/\kappa)
, where
\kappa>0
is the constant from Example
1.5
. Denoting
\widehat{U}_{i}:=\psi_{i}(B^{2n}(r_{i}/\kappa))
and
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}:=\{\widehat{U}_{i}\}
, we have

pb({\mathcal{U}})\geq\frac{1}{2\cdot d(\widehat{\mathcal{U}})^{2}\cdot\max_{i}\{\pi r_{i}^{2}\}}\geq\frac{1}{2\cdot d(\widehat{\mathcal{U}})^{2}\cdot e({\mathcal{U}})}.

2
Preliminaries

Let us review the necessary preliminaries and fix some notations. Note that most of the exposition is restricted to the case when
(M,\omega)
is semipositive, but we occasionally mention the analogous results for general closed manifolds.

2.1
Hamiltonian flows and the action functional.

Given a Hamiltonian
F:M\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, its symplectic gradient is the vector field defined by the equation
\omega(X_{F},\cdot)=-dF
and the flow
\varphi_{F}^{t}
of this vector field is called the Hamiltonian flow of
F
.
The set of 1-periodic orbits of
\varphi_{F}^{t}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}}(F)
. The Hamiltonian
F
is called
non-degenerate
if the graph of
\varphi_{F}^{1}
is transversal to the diagonal in
M\times M
. Equivalently,
F
is non-degenerate if every
x\in{\mathcal{P}}(F)
is non-degenerate, that is, if 1 is not an eigenvalue of
d\varphi_{F}^{1}(x(0))
for every
x\in{\mathcal{P}}(F)
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{L}}M
the space of smooth contractible loops in
M
. A capping disk of
x\in{\mathcal{L}}M
is a map
D:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow M
from the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
to
M
, satisfying
D|_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}=x
. Two capping disks
D_{1},D_{2}
of
x
are equivalent if
[D_{1}\#(-D_{2})]\in\ker\omega\cap\ker c_{1}
, where
c_{1}
is the first Chern class of
M
.
Throughout the paper we identify
(x,D)
with the equivalence classes of this relation. We write

(x_{1},D_{1})=(x_{2},D_{2})\quad\text{if}\quad x_{1}=x_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad[D_{1}\#(-D_{2})]\in\ker\omega\cap\ker c_{1}.

We denote by
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
the space of equivalence classes of capped loops,
(x,D)
.
The action functional corresponding to
F
is defined on the space
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{F}(x,D)=\int_{0}^{1}F(x(t),t)\ dt-\int_{D}\omega.

The critical points of the action functional are (equivalence classes of) capped 1-periodic orbits of
\varphi_{F}^{t}
and the set of their values is denoted by
{\it spec}(F)
.

Almost complex structures.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}
be the space of almost complex structures on
M
that are compatible with
\omega
. Denote by
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{reg}}\subset{\mathcal{J}}
the set of
regular
almost complex structures, i.e., all smooth almost complex structures
J\in{\mathcal{J}}
such that the linearized Cauchy-Riemann operator
D\bar{\partial}_{J}(v)
is
surjective for every simple
J
-holomorphic sphere
v:S^{2}\rightarrow M
. Note that
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
is a residual set and thus a generic almost complex structure
J
on
M
is regular (see
[
HS95
]
). All of the almost complex structures throughout the paper are assumed to be regular.

2.2
The Floer chain complex.

Let us describe briefly the construction of the Floer chain complex over semipositive manifolds, following
[
HS95
]
.

For a non-degenerate Hamiltonian
F
and a regular almost complex structure
J
, the Floer chain complex, denoted by
CF_{*}(F,J)
or
CF_{*}(F)
, is spanned over a field
\Bbbk
(as the simplest case, one can consider
\Bbbk={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
) by the critical points of the action functional, namely, equivalence classes of capped periodic orbits.
The grading of
CF_{*}(F,J)
is given by the Conley-Zehnder (abbreviated to CZ) index, see
[
RS93
,
Gut14
]
for definitions and properties of this index.

The differential of this chain complex is defined by counting negative-gradient flow lines of
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}
, with respect to a metric induced by
J
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
. These negative-gradient flow lines
are maps
u:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\rightarrow M
that solve the Floer equation

\partial_{s}u(s,t)+J\circ u(s,t)\cdot\left(\partial_{t}u(s,t)-X_{F}\circ u(s,t)\right)=0.

(FE)

The
energy
of such a solution is defined to be

E(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|\partial_{s}u\|_{J}^{2}\ ds\ dt
, where
\|\cdot\|_{J}
is the norm induced by the inner product associated to
J
,
g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
. When the Hamiltonian
F
is non-degenerate, for every solution
u
with finite energy, there exist
x_{\pm}\in{\mathcal{P}}(F)
such that
\lim_{s\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(s,t)=x_{\pm}(t)
. If, in addition,
[D_{-}\#u\#(-D_{+})]=0
, we say that
u

connects

(x_{\pm},D_{\pm})
. The well known energy identity for such solutions is a consequence of Stokes’ theorem:

E(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|\partial_{s}u\|^{2}_{J}\ ds\ dt={\mathcal{A}}_{F_{-}}(x_{-},D_{-})-{\mathcal{A}}_{F_{+}}(x_{+},D_{+}),

(5)

It immediately follows from the following computation, which uses the Floer equation (
FE
):

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|\partial_{s}u\|^{2}_{J}\ ds\ dt=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,J\partial_{s}u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,\partial_{t}u-X_{F}\circ u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,\partial_{t}u)\ ds\ dt-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,X_{F}\circ u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}u^{*}dF\wedge dt.

(6)

For two capped 1-periodic orbits
(x_{\pm},D_{\pm})
of
F
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{(F,J)}((x_{-},D_{-}),(x_{+},D_{+}))
the set of all solutions
u:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\rightarrow M
of the Floer equation (
FE
) that connect

2.3
Floer homology and quantum homology.

The homology of the complex
(CF_{*}(F),\partial_{(F,J)})
is denoted by
HF_{*}(F,J)
or
HF_{*}(F)
. A fundamental result in Floer theory states that Floer homology is isomorphic to the quantum homology, which, as a vector space, coincides with the singular homology tensored with the Novikov ring. In more detail, let
\Gamma:=\pi_{2}(M)/(\ker(\omega)\cap\ker(c_{1}))
, then the Novikov ring is:

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\Big\{\sum_{A\in\Gamma}c_{A}q^{A}\ \big|\ c_{A}\in\Bbbk,\ \forall C\in{\mathbb{R}},\#\{A\in\Gamma:c_{A}\neq 0,\omega(A)>C\}<\infty\Big\}.

The Novikov ring has a natural functional, called
valuation
, defined by

{\it val}:\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\qquad{\it val}\left(\sum_{A\in\Gamma}c_{A}q^{A}\right):=\max\{\omega(A):c_{A}\neq 0\}.

The quantum homology is then given by
QH_{*}(M)=H_{*}(M;\Bbbk)\otimes\Lambda
, with the grading of
q^{A}
being
2c_{1}(A)
. The valuation extends to it in the trivial way,
{\it val}:QH_{*}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. The quantum homology carries a product, called the
quantum product
and denoted by
*
, which is a deformation of the classical intersection product on singular homology (see, for example,
[
PR14
, Section 12.1]
).

When
F
is a time independent
C^{2}
-small Morse function, its 1-periodic orbits are its critical points,
{\mathcal{P}}(F)\cong Crit(F)
, and their cappings correspond to elements in
\pi_{2}(M)
, or in
\Gamma
after passing to equivalence classes. In this case, the Floer complex with respect to
J\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
coincides (up to a degree shift) with the Morse complex tensored with the Novikov ring:

\left(CF_{*}(F),\partial_{(F,J)}^{Floer}\right)=\left(CM_{*+n}(F)\otimes\Lambda,\partial_{(F,\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{J})}^{Morse}\otimes{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}}_{\Lambda}\right).

For a proof, see, for example,
[
HS95
,
MS12
]
for semipositive manifolds,
[
ADE14
]
for aspherical manifolds and
[
Par16
, Chapter 10]
for general closed symplectic manifolds.

2.4
Communication between Floer complexes using homotopies.

Let

be a homotopy of Hamiltonians. Throughout the paper, we consider only homotopies that are constant outside of a fixed compact set. Namely, there exists

, and we denote by

Given an almost complex structure

\partial_{s}u(s,t)+J\circ u(s,t)\cdot\left(\partial_{t}u(s,t)-X_{H_{s}}\circ u(s,t)\right)=0,

where

. We sometimes refer this equation as “the
s
-dependent Floer equation”, to stress that it is defined with respect to a homotopy of Hamiltonians. For capped 1-periodic orbits
(x_{\pm},D_{\pm})
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{(H,J)}((x_{-},D_{-}),(x_{+},D_{+}))
the set of all solutions

of the
s
-dependent Floer equation that satisfy

and

The energy identity for homotopies is:

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|\partial_{s}u\|^{2}_{J}\ ds\ dt=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,J\partial_{s}u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,\partial_{t}u-X_{H_{s}}\circ u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,\partial_{t}u)\ ds\ dt-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\omega(\partial_{s}u,X_{H_{s}}\circ u)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}dH(\partial_{s}u)ds\wedge dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}d(H\circ u\ dt)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\partial_{s}H\circ u\ ds\ dt.

(7)

From this computation one can see that

E(u)={\mathcal{A}}_{H_{-}}(x_{-},D_{-})-{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{+}}(x_{+},D_{+})+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\partial_{s}H\circ u\ ds\ dt.

(8)

As in the case of Hamiltonians, there exists a residual set of homotopies called regular, for which the spaces

2.5
Spectral invariants.

The Floer complex admits a natural filtration by the action value. Let
CF_{*}^{\lambda}(F,J)
be the sub-complex generated by (equivalence classes of) capped 1-periodic orbits whose action is bounded by
\lambda
from above. Since the Floer differential is action decreasing, it restricts to the sub-complex
CF_{*}^{\lambda}(F,J)
and the homology
HF_{*}^{\lambda}(F,J)
is well defined.
The spectral invariant with respect to a non-zero class
a\in QH_{*}(M)
is defined to be the smallest value of
\lambda
for which the class
a
appears in
HF^{\lambda}_{*}(F,J)
, namely,

c(F;a):=\inf\{\lambda:a\in\operatorname{im}(\iota^{\lambda}_{*})\},

(10)

where
\iota^{\lambda}_{*}:HF_{*}^{\lambda}(F,J)\rightarrow HF_{*}(F,J)
is the map induced by the inclusion
\iota^{\lambda}:CF_{*}^{\lambda}(F,J)\hookrightarrow CF_{*}(F,J)
.
Spectral invariants have several useful properties, let us state the relevant ones:

•

(stability) For any Hamiltonians
F
and
G
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\min_{x\in M}(F(x,t)-G(x,t))dt\leq c(F;a)-c(G;a)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\max_{x\in M}(F(x,t)-G(x,t))dt.

In particular,
c(-;a):C^{\infty}(M\times S^{1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous functional and is extended by continuity to degenerate Hamiltonians. Moreover, this implies that the spectral invariant is monotone: If
G(x,t)\leq F(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in M\times S^{1}
, then
c(G;a)\leq c(F;a)
.

•

(spectrality)
c(F;a)\in{\it spec}(F)
.

•

(subadditivity) For every Hamiltonians
F
and
G
, and non-zero classes
a,b\in QH_{*}(M)
, one has
c(F\#G;a*b)\leq c(F;a)+c(G;b)
, where
F\#G:=F+G\circ(\varphi_{F}^{t})^{-1}
and
a*b
is the quantum product of
a
and
b
. Note that if
F
and
G
are disjointly supported then
F\#G=F+G
.

•

(identity) For every non-zero
a\in QH_{*}(M)
,
c(0;a)={\it val}(a)
.

•

(energy-capacity inequality) If the support of
F
is displaceable, its spectral invariants are bounded by the displacement energy of the support, namely,
c(F;a)-{\it val}(a)\leq e(supp(F))
.
We remind that a subset
X\subset M
is displaceable if there exists a Hamiltonian
G
such that
\varphi_{G}^{1}(X)\cap X=\emptyset
. In this case, the displacement energy of
X
is given by

e(X):=\inf_{G:\varphi_{G}^{1}(X)\cap X=\emptyset}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\max_{M}G(\cdot,t)-\min_{M}G(\cdot,t)\right)\ dt.

(11)

For a wider exposition see, for example,
[
MS12
,
PR14
]
.

In
[

, Usher defined the

of a Hamiltonian

to be the largest action gap between a boundary term in

and its primitive having the smallest action.

Definition 2.6
.

The boundary depth of

is:

\mathbf{b}(F):=\inf\left\{b\in{\mathbb{R}}\ \big|\ CF^{\lambda}_{*}(F)\cap\partial_{F,J}(CF_{*}(F))\subset\partial_{F,J}(CF_{*}^{\lambda+b}(F)),\ \forall\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}.

The boundary depth satisfies the following stability property
[

]
:

\mathbf{b}(F)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big(\max_{x\in M}F(x,t)-\min_{x\in M}F(x,t)\big)\ dt.

In particular, when the Hamiltonian

c(F_{1}+\cdots+F_{L};a_{1}*\cdots*a_{L})\leq\left(\sum_{i}{\it val}(a_{i})\right)+\max_{j}\{c(F_{j};a_{j})-{\it val}(a_{j})\}.

(12)

Proof.

The following argument is an adaptation of a proof from
[
Sey15
]
for general quantum homology classes.
Using the stability and subadditivity properties of spectral invariants and noticing that the Hamiltonians
\{F_{i}+K_{i}\}
are all disjointly supported, we have

\displaystyle c(F_{1}+\cdots+F_{L};a_{1}*\cdots*a_{L})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\sum_{i}(F_{i}+K_{i})\ ;\ a_{1}*\cdots*a_{L}\right)+\Big\|-\sum_{i}K_{i}\Big\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}c(F_{i}+K_{i};a_{i})+\Big\|\sum_{i}K_{i}\Big\|_{C^{0}}=\sum_{i}{\it val}(a_{i})+\max_{j}\|K_{j}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}{\it val}(a_{i})+\max_{j}\ \{c(F_{j};a_{j})-{\it val}(a_{j})\}.\qed

4
Energy bounds for “up-hill” Floer trajectories

Our main goal for this section is proving Theorem
2
, which gives a lower bound for the energy of Floer trajectories that cross a tubular neighborhood “against” the direction of the negative gradient of
H
.
We start by fixing some notations.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold.

•

For a smooth curve
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M
, denote

\ell_{g}(\gamma):=\int_{a}^{b}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g}\ dt\quad\text{and}\quad E_{g}(\gamma):=\int_{a}^{b}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g}^{2}\ dt.

•

For a vector field
X
, a
k
-form
\lambda
and a subset
N\subset M
, denote

\|X\|_{N,g}:=\sup_{x\in N}|X(x)|_{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\lambda\|_{N,g}:=\sup_{x\in N}|\lambda(x)|_{g},

where

|\lambda(x)|_{g}:=\sup_{v_{1},\dots v_{k}\in T_{x}M}\frac{|\lambda(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})|}{|v_{1}|_{g}\cdots|v_{k}|_{g}}

When
N=M
we abbreviate to
\|X\|_{g}
and
\|\lambda\|_{g}
.
Moreover, we denote
\|\lambda\|_{C^{1},g}:=\|\lambda\|_{g}+\|d\lambda\|_{g}
.

The proof of Theorem
2
requires the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.2

(Isoperimetric inequality)
.

Let
(N,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold (possibly with boundary). There exists a constant
C_{iso}>0
depending only on
(N,g)
, such that for every smooth 1-form
\lambda
on
N
and any smooth loop
\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow N
we have

\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\ \Big|\leq\|\lambda\|_{C^{1},g}\cdot C_{iso}\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)^{2}.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is a standard isoperimetric-inequality type argument. We include it here for the convenience of the reader.
Consider a finite open cover of
N
by balls and half-balls (near the boundary, if
\partial N\neq\emptyset
) with charts. More formally, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
be coordinates on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and consider domains
B_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
d=\dim{N}
, such that for each
i
either
B_{i}=B_{0}(r_{i})
is an open ball around the origin of radius
r_{i}
, or
B_{i}=B_{0}(r_{i})\cap\{x_{1}\geq 0\}
. For each
i
, denote

\partial B_{i}:=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\text{ if }B_{i}=B_{0}(r_{i}),\\
B_{0}(r_{i})\cap\{x_{1}=0\},&\text{ if }B_{i}=B_{0}(r_{i})\cap\{x_{1}\geq 0\}.\end{cases}

Consider in addition smooth embeddings

\varphi_{i}:(B_{i},\partial B_{i})\rightarrow(N,\partial N),\qquad U_{i}:=\varphi_{i}(B_{i})\quad\text{such that}\quad\cup_{i}U_{i}=N.

By the Lebesgue lemma there exists
\rho>0
with the following property. For all
x\in N
there exists
i
such that
B_{g}(x,\rho)\subset U_{i}
, where
B_{g}(x,\rho):=\{y\in N:d_{g}(x,y)<\rho\}
is the ball around
x
of radius
\rho
, with respect to the metric induced by
g
. We split into two cases, depending on the length of the loop
\gamma
.

Starting with the case where
\ell_{g}(\gamma)\geq\rho
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda\|_{g}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g}\ dt=\|\lambda\|_{g}/\rho\cdot\rho\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)\leq\|\lambda\|_{g}/\rho\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)^{2}.

(13)

Now suppose
\ell_{g}(\gamma)<\rho
and set
x=\gamma(0)
. Since every path is contained in a ball of radius at most its length,
\gamma\subset B_{g}(x,\ell_{g}(\gamma))\subset B_{g}(x,\rho)
. By our choice of
\rho
,
\gamma\subset B_{g}(x,\rho)\subset U_{i}
for some
i
. Consider the loop
\widehat{\gamma}:=\varphi_{i}^{-1}\circ\gamma
in
B_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the pullback 1-form
\widehat{\lambda}:=\varphi_{i}^{*}\lambda
.
Let us first prove the lemma for
\widehat{\gamma}
and
\widehat{\lambda}
. Consider the capping disk

a:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow B_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\qquad a(s,t)=s\widehat{\gamma}(t)+(1-s)\widehat{\gamma}(0).

We remark that the image of
a
is indeed contained in
B_{i}
since
B_{i}
is convex and
\widehat{\gamma}\subset B_{i}
. Using Stokes’ theorem, we see

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\gamma}}\widehat{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}d\widehat{\lambda}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\widehat{\lambda}\Big(\frac{\partial a}{\partial s},\frac{\partial a}{\partial t}\Big)\ ds\ dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\widehat{\lambda}\Big(\widehat{\gamma}(t)-\widehat{\gamma}(0),s\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\gamma}(t)\Big)\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle\leq\|d\widehat{\lambda}\|_{g_{0}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s|\widehat{\gamma}(t)-\widehat{\gamma}(0)|_{g_{0}}\cdot\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\gamma}(t)\Big|_{g_{0}}\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle\leq\|d\widehat{\lambda}\|_{g_{0}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s\ell_{g_{0}}(\widehat{\gamma})\cdot\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\gamma}(t)\Big|_{g_{0}}\ ds\ dt

\displaystyle=\|d\widehat{\lambda}\|_{g_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\ell_{g_{0}}(\widehat{\gamma})\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\gamma}(t)\Big|_{g_{0}}\ dt=\|d\widehat{\lambda}\|_{g_{0}}/{2}\cdot\ell_{g_{0}}(\widehat{\gamma})^{2}.

(14)

Having proved the lemma for
\widehat{\gamma}
and
\widehat{\lambda}
, let us relate the relevant measurements in
B_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to the ones on
N
.
Starting with the length of
\gamma
and
\widehat{\gamma}
, we see

\displaystyle\ell_{g_{0}}(\widehat{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\gamma}(t)\Big|_{g_{0}}dt=\int_{0}^{1}|d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\dot{\gamma}|_{g_{0}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{g}dt=\|d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma).

(15)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\widehat{\gamma}}\widehat{\lambda}\Big|=\Big|\int_{\varphi_{i}^{-1}\gamma}\varphi_{i}^{*}\lambda\Big|=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\lambda(d\varphi_{i}d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\dot{\gamma}(t))\ dt\ \Big|=\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\Big|.

(16)

Lastly, a straightforward computation shows that
\|d\widehat{\lambda}\|_{g_{0}}\leq\|d\lambda\|_{g}\cdot\|d\varphi_{i}\|_{C^{0}}^{2}
.
Together with (
4
), (
4
) and (
16
) this yields

\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\Big|\leq\|d\lambda\|_{g}/2\cdot\|d\varphi_{i}\|_{C^{0}}^{2}\cdot\|d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}^{2}\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)^{2}

(17)

Denote

L:=\max_{i}\|d\varphi_{i}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\|d\varphi_{i}^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}.

Then,
\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\Big|\leq\|d\lambda\|_{g}L^{2}/2\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)^{2}
.

Overall, setting
C_{iso}:=\max\{L^{2}/2,1/\rho\}
and recalling that
\|\lambda\|_{C^{1},g}:=\|\lambda\|_{g}+\|d\lambda\|_{g}
, we conclude that, whatever the length of
\gamma
is,

\Big|\int_{\gamma}\lambda\Big|\leq\|\lambda\|_{C^{1},g}\cdot C_{iso}\cdot\ell_{g}(\gamma)^{2}.\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(N,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold (possibly with boundary) and let
X
be a non-vanishing vector field on
N
. There exists
0<\varepsilon_{0}
, depending on
X
, such that
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and every loop
\gamma:S^{1}\rightarrow N
, we have

E_{g}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g}^{2}\ dt\leq 5\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma(t)|_{g}^{2}\ dt.

Proof.

Set
\sigma:=(3/2)^{1/4}>1
. For every
x\in N
, there exists
\lambda_{x}\in T^{*}_{x}N
such that
\lambda_{x}(X(x))=1
and
|\lambda_{x}|_{g}\cdot|X(x)|_{g}=1
.
There exists a small open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
N
, and an exact extension of
\lambda_{x}
to this neighborhood, such that

\|\lambda_{x}\|_{U_{x},g}\cdot\|X\|_{U_{x},g}<\sigma,\qquad\lambda_{x}(X(y))>1/\sigma,\quad\forall y\in U_{x}.

(18)

The open sets
\{U_{x}\}_{x\in N}
cover the compact manifold
N
, and hence there exists a finite subcover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Let
\rho
be the Lebesgue number of this cover, namely, for every
x\in N
, the metric ball
B_{g}(x,\rho)
is contained in
U_{i}
for some
i
.
Set
\varepsilon_{0}:={\rho}/{(2\|X\|_{g})}
and split into two cases with respect to the length of
\gamma
:

1.

\ell(\gamma)<\rho
: In this case,
\gamma
is contained in a metric ball
B_{g}(x,\rho)
around some point
x
, which, by our choice of
\rho
, is contained in
U_{i}=U_{x_{i}}
for some
i
. Let
\lambda_{i}
be the exact 1-form extending
\lambda_{x_{i}}
such that the inequalities (
18
) hold. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|\lambda_{i}\|_{U_{i},g}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{i}(-\dot{\gamma}+\varepsilon X\circ\gamma)\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|\lambda_{i}\|_{U_{i},g}}\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{i}(-\dot{\gamma})\ dt+\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{i}(\varepsilon X\circ\gamma)\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{\|\lambda_{i}\|_{U_{i},g}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{i}(X\circ\gamma)\ dt\overset{(\ref{eq:exact_extension})}{\geq}\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma\cdot\|\lambda_{i}\|_{U_{i},g}},

where the equality in the bottom row follows from the exactness of
\lambda_{i}
.
By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt\geq\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}\ dt\right)^{2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\cdot\|\lambda_{i}\|_{U_{i},g}^{2}}\overset{(\ref{eq:exact_extension})}{\geq}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\|X\|_{U_{i},g}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}.

(19)

Recalling that
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
for any real numbers
a
and
b
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt+2\int_{0}^{1}|\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt+2\varepsilon^{2}\|X\|_{U_{i},g}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:lower_bound_derivative_minus_X})}{\leq}(2+2\sigma^{4})\cdot\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt=5\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt,

where the last equality follows from our choice of
\sigma=(3/2)^{1/4}
.

2.

\ell(\gamma)\geq\rho
: In this case,

\rho^{2}\leq\ell(\gamma)^{2}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}\ dt\right)^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt.

Since
(a-b)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}-b^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt-\int_{0}^{1}|\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt-\varepsilon^{2}\|X\|_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\|X\|_{g}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\|X\|_{g}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt.

Since
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}={\rho}/({2\|X\|_{g})}
, we conclude that

\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}-\varepsilon X\circ\gamma|_{g}^{2}\ dt\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}\ dt.

∎

.

Setup 4.5
.

Throughout this section we work under the following notations and assumptions:

1.

(M,\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold with an almost complex structure
J
, and

is the compatible Riemannian metric, i.e.,

.

2.

N\subset M
is a compact submanifold with boundary, such that

has at least two connected components. Let

be a decomposition of the boundary into two components corresponding to disjoint connected components of

.

3.

is a function having no critical points in

and such that

on

and

.

4.

is a small perturbation of a homotopy of Hamiltonians that, on

coincides with
\varepsilon\cdot h+\beta(s,t)
for some
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

H|_{N}(x,t,s)=\varepsilon\cdot h(x)+h^{\prime}(x,t,s)+\beta(s,t)

where
h^{\prime}:M\times S^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is any homotopy such that
\partial_{s}h^{\prime}
is supported in

for fixed

and
\max\{\|\partial_{s}h^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}},\|X_{h^{\prime}}\|_{g}\}\leq\min\{\delta,\delta/(2R)\}
for

. We stress that many of the assertions in this section hold under the assumption that

is small enough (in particular much smaller than

).

Lemma 4.6
.

Consider Setup
4.5
and fix
c_{\pm}\in h(N)
. Let

u:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\rightarrow M
be a solution to the Floer equation with respect to
(H,J)
, and assume there exists
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
u(s,-)
intersects
h^{-1}(c_{-})
and
h^{-1}(c_{+})
. Then,

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s,t)\Big|_{g}\ dt\geq\frac{c_{+}-c_{-}}{{2}\|dh\|_{g}}\ .

Proof.

Since the image of
u(s,-)
intersects both
h^{-1}(c_{-})
and
h^{-1}(c_{+})
there exist
t_{\pm}\in[0,1]
such that
u(s,t_{\pm})\in h^{-1}(c_{\pm})
and
u(s,t)\in N
for all
t
between
t_{-}
and
t_{+}
. Assume without loss of generality that
t_{-}<t_{+}
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s,t)\Big|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s,t)\Big|_{g}\ dt=\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(s,t)-X_{H}\circ u(s,t)\Big|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|dh\|_{g}}\cdot\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\Big|dh\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(s,t)-X_{H}\circ u(s,t)\Big)\Big|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|dh\|_{g}}\cdot\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\Big|dh\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(s,t)-(\varepsilon X_{h}+X_{h^{\prime}})\circ u(s,t)\Big)\Big|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|dh\|_{g}}\cdot\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\Big|dh\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(s,t)\Big)-dh(X_{h^{\prime}})\Big|_{g}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|dh\|_{g}}\cdot\Big|\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}dh\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(s,t)\Big)\ dt\Big|-\|X_{h^{\prime}}\|_{g}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\|dh\|_{g}}\cdot\big|h(u(s,t_{+}))-h(u(s,t_{-}))\big|-\delta=\frac{c_{+}-c_{-}}{\|dh\|_{g}}-\delta.

Clearly,

is small enough, the assertion of the lemma holds.
∎

The next lemma is taken from
[

, Lemma 3.6]
. We briefly repeat its proof, paying a little more attention to constants, since we wish to obtain an energy bound that depends only on the “model” function

and not on

.

Lemma 4.7

(Hein’s Usher lemma)
.

Consider Setup
4.5
. There exists a constant
C(N,\omega|_{N},J|_{N},h|_{N})
such that for every Floer trajectory
u
with respect to
(H,J)
that intersects both
\partial_{-}N
and
\partial_{+}N
, it holds that

E(u)+m(u^{-1}(N))\geq C(N,\omega|_{N},J|_{N},h|_{N}),

where
m(-)
is the standard Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
.

Proof.

Set
S:=u^{-1}(N)\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
. By Setup
4.5
,
H(x,t,s)=\varepsilon h(x)+h^{\prime}(x,t,s)+\beta(s,t)
on
N
. Replacing
H
with
H-\beta
, the corresponding Floer equation does not change (since
X_{H}
stays the same), and therefore the Floer trajectories of
H
and
H-\beta
are the same. Therefore, we assume from now on that
H=\varepsilon h+h^{\prime}
on
N
. The graph
\tilde{u}:S\rightarrow N\times{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
of the (restriction to
N
of the) Floer trajectory
u
is holomorphic with respect to the almost complex structure

\tilde{J}(v):=\begin{cases}J(v),&\text{if }v\in TN,\\
\partial_{t}+X_{H},&\text{if }v=\partial_{s}.\end{cases}

This almost complex structure is tamed by the symplectic form

\tilde{\omega}=(ds-dH)\wedge dt-\partial_{s}H\cdot ds\wedge dt+\omega=(1-\partial_{s}H)ds\wedge dt-dH\wedge dt+\omega,

where
dH=\omega(-,X_{H})
is the differential of
H
is the
M
-directions (see, e.g.
[
MS12
, Section 8.1]
). Note that
\tilde{\omega}
is symplectic since
|\partial_{s}H|=|\partial_{s}h^{\prime}|<\delta<1
when
\delta
is small. The area of
\tilde{u}
is

\displaystyle\int_{S}\tilde{u}^{*}\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}ds\wedge dt+\int_{S}u^{*}\omega-\int_{S}u^{*}dH\wedge dt-\int_{S}\partial_{s}H\circ u\ ds\wedge dt

\displaystyle=m(S)+\int_{S}u^{*}\omega-\int_{S}u^{*}dH\wedge dt-\int_{S}\partial_{s}H\circ u\ ds\wedge dt

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:energy_identity_homotopies})}{=}m(S)+E(u|_{S})-\int_{S}\partial_{s}H\circ u\ ds\wedge dt

\displaystyle\leq m(S)+E(u|_{S})+2R\cdot\|\partial_{s}h^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq m(S)+E(u|_{S})+\delta.

(20)

Note that the last inequality above is due to our assumption on the support of
\partial_{s}h^{\prime}
and its norm.

Given (
4
), it is sufficient to prove a lower bound for
\int_{S}\tilde{u}^{*}\tilde{\omega}
, that depends only on
\omega,J
and
h
, and does not depend on
\varepsilon
.
We claim that such a lower bound can be obtained from Sikorav
[
Sik94
, Proposition 4.3.1(ii)]
.
To see this, let
g
be the product Riemannian metric on
N\times{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
, namely,
\tilde{g}(-,-)=ds\wedge dt(-,j-)+\omega(-,J-)
, where
j
is the standard complex structure on
{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
, restricted to
S
. Let
\Sigma\subset N
be a hypersurface such that
\partial_{\pm}N
lie in different connected components of
N\setminus\Sigma
. For each
x\in\Sigma
, let
r_{x}
be the maximal radius of a ball centered at
x
and contained in
N
. Finally, denote by
r_{\star}:=\min_{a\in S}r_{x}
. If
r_{\star}
is bigger than the injectivity radius of the cylinder, which is 1, we reduce it to be 1. Since
u
intersects both
\partial_{\pm}N
, there exists
z_{0}\in S
such that
u_{0}:=u(z_{0})\in\Sigma
. Let
B\subset N\times{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
be a ball of radius
r_{\star}
centered at
(u_{0},z_{0})
(such a ball can be found in the polydisc of radius
r_{\star}
, which exists by our choice of
r_{\star}
).

[
Sik94
, Proposition 4.3.1(ii)]
states that

E(\tilde{u}|_{\tilde{u}^{-1}(B)})\geq cr_{\star}^{2},

(21)

where
c
depends on:

•

The isoperimetric constant:
C_{1}>0
such that every loop
\gamma
that is contained in a ball
B(x,r)
of radius

bounds a disc in
B(x,r)
of Riemannian area less than
C_{1}\ell_{\tilde{g}}(\gamma)^{2}
,

•

The taming constant:
C_{2}>0
such that for every

,
|v|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\leq C_{2}\omega_{x}(v,\tilde{J}v)
.

In fact, one can take
c=(4C_{1}C_{2})^{-1}
.
Recall that the metric
\tilde{g}
on
N\times{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
does not depend on
H
and therefore so does
C_{1}
.
As for the taming constant, let us show that it is smaller than 2, when
\varepsilon
is small enough. That is, for every tangent vector
v
,

|v|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\leq 2\tilde{\omega}(v,\tilde{J}v).

(22)

Write
v=(v_{M},v_{s},v_{t})\in TM\oplus T{\mathbb{R}}\oplus TS^{1}

On the average hitting times of Cay ⁡ ( ℤ N , { + 1 , + 2 } ) \cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
Yuuho Tanaka

Abstract
. The exact formula for the average hitting time (HT, as an abbreviation) of simple random walks on Cay ⁡ ( ℤ N , { ± 1 , ± 2 } ) \cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\}) was given by Y. Doi et al. [ Discrete Applied Mathematics , 313 (2022) 18-28]. Y. Doi et al. give a simple formula for the HT’s of simple random walks on Cay ⁡ ( ℤ N , { ± 1 , ± 2 } ) \cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\}) by using an elementary method. In this paper, using an elementary method also used by Y. Doi et al. [ 3 ] , we give a simple formula for HT’s of simple random walks on Cay ⁡ ( ℤ N , { + 1 , + 2 } ) \cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\}) .



Key Words:

simple random walk, hitting time, Cayley graph, Jacobsthal number

1.
Introduction

A simple random walk on a graph
G
is a discrete stochastic model
such that a random walker at a vertex
u\in V(G)
moves to a vertex
v

adjacent to the vertex
u
at the next step with the probability of

\frac{1}{\deg_{G}(u)}
, where
\deg_{G}(u)
denotes the degree of the vertex
u

of
G
.
The number of steps required for the random walker starting at vertex
p
of
G
to reach vertex
q
of
G
for the first time is called
the hitting time
from
p
to
q
of the simple random walk on
G
.

The average hitting time
(HT, as an abbreviation) from
p
to
q
on
G
,
denoted by
h(G;p,q)
, means the expected value of the hitting times
from
p
to
q
of simple random walks on
G
.
Note that
h(G;p,p)=0
.
The exact formula for the average hitting time from one vertex to any other vertex is far from
available in general.
For some very special graph classes with high symmetries, it may be possible to obtain such exact formulas.

Let
G
be a finite group and
S\subseteq G
be a subset.
The corresponding
Cayley graph

\cay({\it G,S})
has vertex set equal to
G
.
Two vertices
g,h\in G
are joind by a directed edge from
g
to
h
if and only if there exists
s\in S
such that
g=sh
.
In N. Chair
[
2
]
, by calculating the exact value of Wu’s formula
[
4
]
of the effective resistances in terms of the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of the Laplacian matrices of
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\})
, the author gave the expression depending on the parity of
N
.
In Y. Doi et al.
[
3
]
, by using an elementary method, they gave a much simpler formula than Chair’s formula
[
2
]
.
This formula does not depend on the parity of
N
.

Theorem 1.1

(Y. Doi et al., 2022
[
3
]
)
.

Let
F_{i}
be the
i
-th Fibonacci number.
The exact formula for the HT’s of simple random walks on
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\})

(N\geq 5)
is,

h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\});0,l)=\frac{2}{5}\left(l\left(N-l\right)+2N\frac{F_{l}F_{N-l}}{F_{N}}\right).

Its proof is considerably short and fully combinatorial.
Furthermore, it does not require any spectral graph theoretical arguments.

In this paper, using an elementary method also used by Y. Doi et al.
[
3
]
, we give a simple formula for HT’s of simple random walks on
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
.
The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we fix the notation for the graphs
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
and give some properties of the Jacobsthal sequence.
In Section 3, we give the entries of the inverse of
H_{N}
.
As a consequence, we obtain the exact formula for HT on
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
by calculation.
This equation does not depend on the parity of
N
.

2.
Preliminary

The sequence of numbers
J_{n}
defined by the recurrence relation
J_{0}=0
,
J_{1}=1
,
J_{n+2}=J_{n+1}+2J_{n}

(n=0,1,2,\ldots)
is called the
Jacobsthal sequence
.
We prepare some well-known formulas on Jacobsthal numbers,
which will be used throughout this paper.
For proofs of these formulas, please refer to the appropriate
literature on Jacobsthal numbers (e.g.,
[
1
]
).

\displaystyle J_{n+1}-2J_{n}

3.
Proof of main theorem

The laplacian matrix of graph
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
is a matrix
L
whose entries
L(i,j)
are given by

L(i,j)=\begin{cases}\deg_{\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})}(i)&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
-1&\text{if $i\neq j$ and there is an arc from $i$ to $j$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
L^{\prime}
be the matrix obtained from
L
by deleting the last row and column.
Let
\vec{h}
be the column vector whose
i
-th entry is
h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,i)
and
let
\vec{1}
be a column vector of proper dimension whose entries are all
1
.
In the case of a random walk on
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
, a random walker moves with probability
\frac{1}{2}
to an arbitrary vertex adjacent to the vertex where the walker is.
Hence we have that
\forall l\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\{0\}
,

h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l)=\frac{1}{2}((1+h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});1,l)+(1+h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});2,l)))
,
and hence
\forall l\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\{0\},2h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l)-h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l-1)-h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l-2)=2
.
So we have
{}^{t}L^{\prime}\vec{h}=2\vec{1}
.
Note that
h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l)=h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});k,k+l)=h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});k+N-l,k)
for all
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.
Let
L^{\prime}(i,j)
denote the
(i,j)
-th entry of
L^{\prime}
.
Let
H_{N}
be the
(N-1)\times(N-1)
-matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is

{}^{t}L^{\prime}(i,j)
.
Then we have
H_{N}\vec{h}=2\vec{1}
.
For our problem it is sufficient to solve this matrix equation.

Then we have the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

The entries of
H_{N}^{-1}
is as follows:

H_{N}^{-1}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\frac{J_{i}J_{N-i-1}}{J_{N}}&(j=i+1)\\
\frac{J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-2}+J_{N-i+j-1})-(-1)^{i+j}J_{j-1}J_{N-i-1}}{J_{N}}&(j<i+1,\;(i,j)\neq(N-1,1))\\
\frac{J_{i}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i-1}+J_{j-i})}{J_{N}}&(j>i+1)\\
\frac{J_{N-1}}{J_{N}}&((i,j)=(N-1,1))\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
S_{N}
denote the matrix as follows.

S_{N}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\frac{J_{i}J_{N-i-1}}{J_{N}}&(j=i+1)\\
\frac{J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-2}+J_{N-i+j-1})-(-1)^{i+j}J_{j-1}J_{N-i-1}}{J_{N}}&(j<i+1,\;(i,j)\neq(N-1,1))\\
\frac{J_{i}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i-1}+J_{j-i})}{J_{N}}&(j>i+1)\\
\frac{J_{N-1}}{J_{N}}&((i,j)=(N-1,1))\end{cases}

We will show
H_{N}S_{N}
equals to the identity matrix.
By simple matrix-computation, the entries of
H_{N}S_{N}
can be calculated as follows.

In the case that
(i,j)=(1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{1}(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})-2J_{0}J_{N-2}-J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})-J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=1.

In the case that
(i,j)=(1,2)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{1}J_{N-2}-J_{N-2}(J_{1}+J_{2})+(-1)^{N+1}J_{1}J_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{N-2}-2J_{N-2})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i=1,\;j\notin\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{1}J_{N-j}(J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})-J_{N-j}(J_{j-1}+J_{j})+(-1)^{N+j-1}J_{j-1}J_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{N-j}(J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})-J_{N-j}(J_{j-1}+J_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{N-j}}{J_{N}}(2J_{j-2}+J_{j-1}-J_{j})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
(i,j)=(2,1)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{1}(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})+J_{0}J_{N-2}+2J_{2}(J_{N-3}+J_{N-2})+2J_{0}J_{N-3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{N-2}-J_{N-1}+2(J_{N-3}+J_{N-2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{N-3}+J_{N-2}-J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
(i,j)=(2,2)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{1}J_{N-2}+2J_{1}(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})-2J_{1}J_{N-3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{N-2}+2(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})-2J_{N-3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-2J_{N-3}+J_{N-2}+2J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(2J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=1.

In the case that
(i,j)=(2,3)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{1}J_{N-3}(J_{1}+J_{2})+2J_{2}J_{N-3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-2J_{N-3}+2J_{N-3})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i=2,\;j\notin\{1,2,3\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{1}J_{N-j}(J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})+2J_{2}J_{N-j}(J_{j-3}+J_{j-2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{N-j}(J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})+2J_{N-j}(J_{j-3}+J_{j-2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{N-j}}{J_{N}}(-J_{j-1}+J_{j-2}+2J_{j-3})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i>j+1,i\notin\{1,2\},(i,j)\neq(N-1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{i-j-1}(J_{N-i+j}+J_{N-i+j+1})+(-1)^{i+j-4}J_{j-1}J_{N-i+1}

\displaystyle~-J_{i-j}(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j})+(-1)^{i+j-3}J_{j-1}J_{N-i}

\displaystyle~+2J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-2}+J_{N-i+j-1})-2(-1)^{i+j-2}J_{j-1}J_{N-i-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-2J_{i-j-1}(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j})-J_{i-j}(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j})

\displaystyle~+J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j})

\displaystyle~-(-1)^{i+j}J_{j-1}(2J_{N-i-1}+J_{N-i}-J_{N-i+1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-(2J_{i-j-1}+J_{i-j}-J_{i-j+1})(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j}))

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i=j+1,i\notin\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{i-2}J_{N-i+1}-J_{i-j}(J_{N-i+j-1}+J_{N-i+j})+(-1)^{i+j-1}J_{j-1}J_{N-i}

\displaystyle~+2J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-2}+J_{N-i+j-1})-2(-1)^{i+j}J_{j-1}J_{N-i-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{j-1}J_{N-j}-J_{1}(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})+J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1}

\displaystyle~+2J_{2}(J_{N-3}+J_{N-2})+2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{j-1}J_{N-j}-(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})+J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1}

\displaystyle~+2(J_{N-3}+J_{N-2})+2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(J_{j-1}(2J_{N-j-2}+J_{N-j-1}-J_{N-j})+(2J_{N-3}+J_{N-2}-J_{N-1}))

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i=j,i\notin\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{i-2}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i+1}+J_{j-i+2})-J_{i-1}J_{N-i}

\displaystyle~+2J_{i-j+1}(J_{N-i+j-2}+J_{N-i+j-1})-2(-1)^{i+j}J_{j-1}J_{N-i-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{j-2}J_{N-j}(J_{1}+J_{2})-J_{j-1}J_{N-j}

\displaystyle~+2J_{1}(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})-2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-2J_{j-2}J_{N-j}-J_{j-1}J_{N-j}+2(J_{N-2}+J_{N-1})

\displaystyle~-2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-(2J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})J_{N-j}+(J_{N-1}+J_{N})-2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{j}J_{N-j}-2J_{j-1}J_{N-j-1}+J_{N-1}+J_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{N-1}+J_{N-1}+J_{N})

by (
2.5
)

\displaystyle=1.

In the case that
i=j-1,i\notin\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{i-2}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i+1}+J_{j-i+2})-J_{i-1}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i}+J_{j-i+1})

\displaystyle~+2J_{i}J_{N-i-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{j-3}J_{N-j}(J_{2}+J_{3})-J_{j-2}J_{N-j}(J_{1}+J_{2})+2J_{j-1}J_{N-j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-4J_{j-3}J_{N-j}-2J_{j-2}J_{N-j}+2J_{j-1}J_{N-j})

\displaystyle=\frac{2J_{N-j}}{J_{N}}(-2J_{j-3}-J_{j-2}+J_{j-1})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
i<j-1,i\notin\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{i-2}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i+1}+J_{j-i+2})-J_{i-1}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i}+J_{j-i+1})

\displaystyle~+2J_{i}J_{N-j}(J_{j-i-1}+J_{j-i}))

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{N-j}}{J_{N}}(-2J_{i-2}(J_{j-i}+J_{j-i+1})-J_{i-1}(J_{j-i}+J_{j-i+1})

\displaystyle~+J_{i}(J_{j-i}+J_{j-i+1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{N-j}}{J_{N}}(-2J_{i-2}-J_{i-1}+J_{i})(J_{j-i-1}+J_{j-i})

\displaystyle=0.

In the case that
(i,j)=(N-1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-J_{N-3}(J_{2}+J_{3})+(-1)^{N-2}J_{0}J_{2}-J_{N-2}(J_{1}+J_{2})

\displaystyle~+(-1)^{N-1}J_{0}J_{1}+2J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J_{N}}(-4J_{N-3}-2J_{N-2}+2J_{N-1})

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore, we have
S_{N}=H_{N}^{-1}
.
∎

\vec{h}=\begin{bmatrix}h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,1)\\

Theorem 3.2
.

For
1\leq l\leq N-1
, the exact formula for the HT’s of simple random walks on
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
is,

\displaystyle h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l)=\frac{1}{3J_{N}}(2J_{l-1}(3lJ_{N-l-1}+2lJ_{N-l})

\displaystyle~+J_{l}((N+l+3)J_{N-l-1}+(N+3l+1)J_{N-l})).

Proof.

For
1\leq l\leq N-2
,

\displaystyle h(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,l)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}H_{N}^{-1}(l,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{J_{N}}(\sum_{j=1}^{l}(J_{l-j+1}(J_{N-l+j-2}+J_{N-l+j-1})-(-1)^{l+j}J_{j-1}J_{N-l-1})

\displaystyle~+J_{l}J_{N-l-1}+\sum_{j=l+2}^{N-1}J_{l}J_{N-j}(J_{j-l-1}+J_{j-l}))

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{J_{N}}(\sum_{j=1}^{l}2^{N-l+j-2}J_{l-j+1}-(-1)^{l}J_{N-l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}(-1)^{j}J_{j-1}

\displaystyle~+J_{l}J_{N-l-1}+J_{l}\sum_{j=l+2}^{N-1}2^{j-l-1}J_{N-j})

by (
2.2
)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{J_{N}}(2^{N-l-2}\sum_{j=1}^{l+1}2^{j}J_{l-j+1}+J_{N-l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}(-1)^{l+j}J_{j}

\displaystyle~+J_{l}\sum_{j=1}^{N-l-1}2^{j}J_{N-l-j-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3J_{N}}(2^{N-l-1}((l+1)J_{l}+lJ_{l+1})+J_{N-l-1}(lJ_{l-1}-(l-2)J_{l})

\displaystyle~+J_{l}((N-l-1)J_{N-l-2}+(N-l-2)J_{N-l-1}))

by (
2.6
),(
2.7
)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3J_{N}}(2(J_{N-l-2}+J_{N-l-1})((2l+1)J_{l}+2lJ_{l-1})

\displaystyle~+J_{N-l-1}(lJ_{l-1}-(l-2)J_{l})+J_{l}((N-l-1)J_{N-l-2}

\displaystyle~+(N-l-2)J_{N-l-1}))

by (
2.2

Secluded Dark Sector and Muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) in the Light of Fast Expanding Universe
Sougata Ganguly, Sourov Roy, Ananya Tapadar

Abstract
The lack of information before Big Bang Neucleosynthesis (BBN) allow us to assume the presence of a new species ϕ \phi whose energy density redshifts as a − ( 4 + n ) a^{-(4+n)} where n > 0 n>0 and a a is the scale factor. This non-standard cosmological setup facilitates a larger portal coupling ( ϵ ) (\epsilon) between the dark and the visible sectors even when the two sectors are not in thermal equilibrium. Here, we have considered U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ ⊗ U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}\otimes U(1)_{X} gauge extension of the Standard Model (SM) and studied different phases of the cosmological evolution of a thermally decoupled dark sector such as leak-in, freeze-in, reannihilation, and late-time annihilation in the presence of fast expansion. Due to the tree level kinetic mixing between U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} and U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} gauge bosons, the dark sector couples with the μ \mu and τ \tau flavored leptons of the SM. We show that in our scenario it is possible to reconcile the dark matter relic density and muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) anomaly. In particular, we show that for 2 × 10 − 4 ≲ ϵ ≲ 10 − 3 2\times 10^{-4}\lesssim\epsilon\lesssim 10^{-3} , 5.5 ​ MeV ≲ m Z ′ ≲ 200 ​ MeV 5.5{\rm MeV}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 200{\rm MeV} , n = 4 n=4 , and 1 ​ TeV ≲ m χ ≲ 10 ​ TeV 1{\rm TeV}\lesssim m_{\chi}\lesssim 10{\rm TeV} relic density constraint of dark matter, constraint from muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) anomaly, and other cosmological, astrophysical constraints are satisfied.

4
Properties of non-adiabatic evolution in fast expanding Universe

Dark matter freeze-out through non-adiabatic evolution in fast expanding Universe
is quite different from either of non-adiabatic evolution scenario as discussed
earlier in
Evans:2017kti
;
Tapadar:2021kgw
or dark matter freeze-out in
non-standard cosmological scenario
DEramo:2017gpl
. We shall start with some
analytical calculation of DM freeze-out in this scenario and also put some bounds
on portal coupling.

Dark matter freeze-out within DS through non-adiabatic evolution is referred to as “leak-in”
dark matter
Evans:2019vxr
. Difference between non-adiabatic evolution of DM number density
with dark sector freeze-out is that in the former scenario,
\xi
is a dynamical quantity which
depends on the portal coupling as well as the SM temperature whereas
\xi
is constant in the latter
scenario. Now, the relevant Boltzmann equation for dark sector freeze-out is given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{dY_{\chi}}{dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T^{\prime}}s(x)}{xH(x)}\left({Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}}(x^{\prime})^{2}-Y_{\chi}^{2}\right)\,\,,\

(16)

where,
x=m_{\chi}/T
,
Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})=n_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})/s(x)
, where
n_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})
is the equilibrium DM number density,
s(x)
is the
entropy density of the Universe, and
x^{\prime}=m_{\chi}/T^{\prime}
.
\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T^{\prime}}
is the thermally averaged cross-section of DM annihilation,
calculated at
T^{\prime}
and
H(x)
is the Hubble parameter which is given in Eq.
11
.

Initially, DM comoving number density follows
Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})
and at later times it decouples
from dark sector thermal bath and freezes-out at
x^{\prime}_{f}=\dfrac{m_{\chi}}{T^{\prime}_{f}}
where,
T^{\prime}_{f}
is the
dark sector temperature at the time of DM freeze-out. In case of fast expanding Universe, due to the higher expansion rate,
number changing processes for DM decouple much earlier in comparison to the radiation dominated Universe.
Therefore, in this scenario
x_{f}^{\prime}
appeared to be smaller than the radiation dominated Universe.
Exact value of
x_{f}^{\prime}
can be obtained by solving the following equation
DEramo:2017gpl

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{x^{\prime}_{f}}{\sqrt{x_{f}^{\prime}}}}{\xi\langle\sigma v\rangle}\left(\dfrac{x_{r}}{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{c\left(c+2\right)}{\left(c+1\right)}\dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi^{3}}\dfrac{g_{\chi}}{\sqrt{g_{*s}}}m_{\chi}M_{\rm{Pl}}\,\,,\

(17)

where
g_{\chi}
is the degrees of freedom of DM, which is
2
in our scenario,
x_{r}=m_{\chi}/T_{r}
,

g_{*s}
is effective degrees of freedom contributing to entropy density, and

c
is a constant which is numerically found to be equal to
c(c+2)=\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)

Scherrer:1985zt
.
For
s
-wave DM annihilation cross-section Eq.
17
can be approximated as,

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{f}\simeq\ln A-\dfrac{n}{2}\ln x_{r}\,\,,\

(18)

where
A=\dfrac{c\left(c+2\right)}{\left(c+1\right)}\dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi^{3}}\dfrac{g_{\chi}}{\sqrt{g_{*s}}}\langle\sigma v\rangle m_{\chi}M_{\rm Pl}
.

To get an analytical expression of final relic abundance
Y_{\infty}
, we have considered
s
-wave dominated annihilation cross section.
As DM freeze-out temperature from DS thermal bath is
T^{\prime}_{f}
then SM bath temperature at the time of DM freeze-out is
T^{\prime}_{f}/\xi
.
At late time after freeze-out
Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(T^{\prime})\simeq 0
compared to
Y_{\infty}
which is the final value of DM comoving number density.
Therefore, final comoving number density of DM is given by,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Y_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Y_{f}\left(\xi x^{\prime}_{f}\right)}+\langle\sigma v\rangle_{0}\int_{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}^{x_{r}}\dfrac{s(x)}{xH(x)}dx+\int_{x_{r}}^{\infty}\dfrac{s(x)}{xH_{\rm{rad}}(x)}dx\,\,,\

(19)

where
\langle\sigma v\rangle_{0}
is the s-wave thermally averaged DM annihilation cross section,

H(x)
is the Hubble parameter in fast expanding Universe as approximated in Eq.
12
.
For
T<T_{r}
, Universe is radiation dominated and the Hubble parameter in this
regime is defined by
H_{\rm{rad}}(x)=\dfrac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}}\sqrt{\dfrac{8\pi}{3}\rho_{r}(T)}
.

Therefore, by integrating Eq.
19
, approximated expression for the comoving number density is given by,

\displaystyle Y_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\dfrac{45}{\pi}}\dfrac{\sqrt{g_{\rho}(x)}}{g_{*s}(x)}\dfrac{2x_{r}}{\langle\sigma v\rangle m_{\chi}M_{\rm Pl}}\left[\dfrac{2}{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}\left(\dfrac{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}{x_{r}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}+\dfrac{1}{n-2}\left(1-\left(\dfrac{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}{x_{r}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\right)+1\right]^{-1}\,,\ n\neq 2

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\dfrac{45}{\pi}}\dfrac{\sqrt{g_{\rho}(x)}}{g_{*s}(x)}\dfrac{x_{r}}{\langle\sigma v\rangle m_{\chi}{M_{\rm Pl}}}\left[\dfrac{2}{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}+{\rm ln}\left(\frac{x_{r}}{\xi x^{\prime}_{f}}\right)+1\right]^{-1}\,,\ n=2\,\,.\

Eq.
21
reduces to standard WIMP
scenario as soon as we put
\xi=1
and
n=0
. Thus, it is clear that apart
from the interactions within DS, final relic abundance for
internally thermalized DS
in non-standard cosmology also depends on
n
, portal interaction
between DS and VS and
T_{r}
.

DM production through this mechanism can take place within a certain range
of portal coupling. Leak-in mechanism is only possible when dark and
visible sectors are not in thermal equilibrium, but there is small exchange
of energy between the two sectors. If portal coupling is sufficiently large
then both the sectors equilibrate and hence no leak-in mechanism is observed.
On the other hand, if portal coupling is very small then there would never
be sufficient DM produced at early Universe that could reach observed
relic abundance of DM after freeze-out.

Analytically, we can obtain a lower limit on portal coupling by comparing
observed relic density with the maximum relic abundance of DM in leak-in
scenario. To do this, we first need to find the maximum value attained by equilibrium yield,

\displaystyle Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}=\dfrac{g_{\chi}}{g_{*s}T^{3}}\dfrac{45}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\dfrac{m_{\chi}T^{\prime}}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_{\chi}/T^{\prime}}\,\,.\

(22)

We maximize
Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}
with respect
to
T^{\prime}
to obtain the dark sector temperature
T^{\prime}_{\rm max}
at which the equilibrium yield is maximum. The value
of
T^{\prime}_{\rm max}
is given by

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{\rm max}=\dfrac{2m_{\chi}\left(6-n\right)}{3n+30}\,\,.\

(23)

One can notice that Eq.
23
reduces to standard result of
T^{\prime}_{\rm max}=\frac{2m_{\chi}}{5}

Evans:2019vxr

as we choose
n=0
. Another point to note is that as
n
increases,
T^{\prime}_{\rm max}

decreases. Using Eq.
23
, the equilibrium yield at
T^{\prime}_{\rm max}
is given by,

\displaystyle{Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}}_{\rm max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{g_{\chi}}{g_{*s}}\dfrac{45}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\dfrac{m_{\chi}}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_{\chi}/T^{\prime}_{\rm max}}\dfrac{{T^{\prime}_{\rm max}}^{-(30+3n)/2(6-n)}}{K^{-24/(6-n)}}\,\,,\

(24)

where
K=\frac{10^{4}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{1/4}}T_{r}^{n/8}
.

Now, in case of fast expanding Universe, the DM can be produced via leak-in mechanism if
{Y_{\chi}^{\rm eq}}_{\rm max}\geq Y_{\rm obs}

where
Y_{\rm{\rm obs}}=4.355\times 10^{-10}\left(1{\rm GeV}/m_{\chi}\right)
. From this condition, one can derive an upper limit
on
\epsilon
and it is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon\gtrsim 2.616\times 10^{-12}\left(3.561\times 10^{8}\right)^{n/12}e^{n/8}\sqrt{\left(2+n\right)}\,\ \left(\dfrac{g_{\chi}}{g_{*s}}\right)^{\dfrac{n-6}{12}}\left(\dfrac{2\left(6-n\right)}{3\left(10+n\right)}\right)^{\dfrac{10+n}{8}}T_{r}^{-n/4}m_{\chi}^{n/3}\,\,.\

(25)

Thus, Eq.
25
tells us that unlike DM freeze-out in non-adiabatic
scenario in standard cosmology, lower bound on
\epsilon
is proportional to

m_{\chi}
. Thus, for leak-in mechanism, we need larger value of
\epsilon
as we increase the mass of the DM.
The upper limit given in Eq.
25
is known as “Absolute coupling floor” below which
it is not possible to get correct relic density via leak-in mechanism.

5
Numerical result for relic density

In this section, we discuss the evolution of DM comoving number density in fast expanding Universe where
visible sector and dark sector are not in thermal equilibrium. The temperature evolution of DS is
different from the evolution of
T
and we use the semi-analytic expression of
\xi
, given in Eq.
15

to calculate
T^{\prime}
.

5.1
Boltzmann equation

Evolution of the DM number density depends on the production of DM from SM fields
and annihilation of DM into
Z^{\prime}
. Relevant Boltzmann equation for the evolution of total number density of
DM is given by,

\displaystyle\dfrac{dY_{\chi_{\rm tot}}}{dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{s(x)h_{\rm eff}(x)}{H(x)x}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T^{\prime}}_{\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}\left(Y_{\chi_{\rm tot}}^{\rm eq}(x,x^{\prime})^{2}-Y_{\chi_{\rm tot}}^{2}\right)\right]+2\sum_{f}\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T}_{{f}\bar{f}\to{\chi}\bar{\chi}}Y_{f}^{\rm eq}(x)^{2}\right]\,,

where
Y_{\chi_{\rm tot}}^{\rm eq}(x,x^{\prime})=\dfrac{n_{\chi_{\rm tot}}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})}{s(x)}

is the total equilibrium comoving number density of DM where,

n_{\chi_{\rm tot}}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})=n_{\chi}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})+n_{\bar{\chi}}^{\rm eq}(x^{\prime})

is the total equilibrium DM number density at
T^{\prime}
.
Y_{f}^{\rm eq}(x)=\dfrac{n_{f}^{\rm eq}(x)}{s(x)}
where,
n_{f}^{\rm eq}(x)

is the equilibrium number density of SM fermion
f
at SM temperature
T
,
h_{\rm eff}(x)=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{d\ln g_{*s}(x)}{d\ln x}\right)
, and

H(x)
is the Hubble parameter, defined in Eq.
11
.

The first term inside the square bracket on the right hand side (RHS) of Eq.
5.1
denotes the contribution to the change
in
Y_{\chi_{\rm tot}}
due to the annihilation of DM into
Z^{\prime}
and we call this term as “annihilation term” in the rest of
our discussion.
\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T^{\prime}}_{\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}

is the thermally averaged cross section of
\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
,
calculated at
T^{\prime}
and the
s
-wave approximated form of
\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T^{\prime}}_{\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v\rangle_{{\chi}\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\dfrac{4\pi\alpha_{X}^{2}}{m_{\chi}^{2}}\dfrac{m_{\chi}\left(m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}}{\left(2m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\right)^{2}}\,\,.

(27)

The second term inside the square bracket on the RHS of Eq.
5.1
denotes the production of DM from the SM bath and we call this term as “freeze-in” term
in the rest of our discussion. Here,
\langle\sigma v\rangle^{T}_{{f}\bar{f}\to{\chi}\bar{\chi}}
is the thermally
averaged cross section of
f\bar{f}\to\chi\bar{\chi}
, calculated at temperature
T
. The cross sections of the relevant processes for the freeze-in are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{{f}\bar{f}\to{\chi}\bar{\chi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\kappa\alpha_{X}\epsilon^{2}}{3\hat{s}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\hat{s}-4m_{\chi}^{2}}{\hat{s}-4m_{f}^{2}}}\dfrac{(\hat{s}+2m_{f}^{2})(\hat{s}+2m_{\chi}^{2})}{(\hat{s}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}}\,\,,

(28)

where
m_{f}
is the mass of the SM fermion
f
,
\kappa=1
for
f=\mu,\,\tau
and
\kappa=1/2
for
f=\nu_{\mu},\,\nu_{\tau}
.

In the following section, we discuss the effect of annihilation and freeze-in terms on the DM relic density in the
fast expanding Universe. The numerical results related to the DM relic density has also been discussed in the next section.

6.4
BBN constraint

Phenomenology of early Universe near
1\rm{MeV}
is highly constrained from BBN predictions
Mukhanov:2003xs
;
Mangano:2011ar
.
Due to presence of light
Z^{\prime}
, it is possible that
Z^{\prime}
can thermalise with the SM neutrinos via
Z^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\bar{\nu}_{i}\nu_{i}
(
i=\mu,\,\tau
)
and contribute to the effective number of neutrino species (
N_{\nu}
) at the time of BBN.

To check the thermalisation of
Z^{\prime}
at the time of BBN, we have compared the reaction rate of
\bar{\nu}_{i}\nu_{i}\leftrightarrow Z^{\prime}
with the Hubble parameter
at the time of BBN (
i.e.
at
T=1\rm MeV
) and found that the
Z^{\prime}
is in thermal equilibrium with the neutrino bath for
10^{-4}\leq\epsilon\leq 10^{-2}
and

{\rm 1MeV}\leq m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 40\rm MeV
. This region is outlined by the black dashed line in Fig.
9
. In this region of parameter
space, depending on the mass of
Z^{\prime}
, it may give additional contribution to the effective number of neutrino species
(
\Delta N_{\nu}\equiv N_{\nu}-3
) whose upper limit is 0.168 at 95% C.L
Fields:2019pfx
.
We have calculated the constraint on the
Z^{\prime}
mass from
\Delta N_{\nu}
and found that
Z^{\prime}
of mass greater than
5.5\rm MeV

is allowed from the
\Delta N_{\nu}
constraint even if it is in thermal equilibrium with the neutrino bath. The grey region of Fig.
9

depicts the constraint on
m_{Z^{\prime}}
from
\Delta N_{\nu}
. Let us note in passing that in our parameter space of interest,
non-thermal production
Z^{\prime}
can happen for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 40\rm MeV
and it does not contribute to the
\Delta N_{\nu}
. This is because
i) the energy density of the non-thermally produced
Z^{\prime}
s are smaller compared to the thermally produced
Z^{\prime}
and they
are non-relativistic at the time of BBN, and ii) the decay lifetime of
Z^{\prime}
in our parameter space of interest is much smaller
than
1s
. Thus the produced
Z^{\prime}
decays much earlier than SM neutrino decoupling and hence it cannot heat up the neutrino bath.

7
Summary and conclusions

In this work we have considered two sectors, dark and visible sectors in non-standard cosmological background.
Here, by non-standard cosmology, we mean the presence of a new species
\phi
which redshifts faster
than the radiation and total energy density in the early Universe is dominated by
\phi
.
We have analyzed DM evolution in the fast expanding Universe when dark and visible sectors are not in thermal equilibrium.
To provide a detailed discussion, we have considered
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}\otimes U(1)_{X}
gauge extension of SM.
A Dirac fermion
\chi
is the DM candidate which is charged only under
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry. We have
assumed tree level kinetic mixing between
U(1)_{X}
and
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
which generates an interaction at tree level
between
\mu
,
\tau
flavored SM leptons with the dark gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
. In standard cosmological scenario i.e. in the radiation dominated
Universe, there exists an upper limit of
\epsilon
for a fixed value of
m_{\chi}
above which both the sectors will be in thermal equilibrium.
In the presence of
\phi
, the upper limit of the portal coupling
is larger compared to the radiation dominated Universe. We have shown that for
n=1,\,2
, correct relic abundance of
DM can be produced either via reannihilation or by freeze-in mechanisms
whereas for
n=3,\,4
, DM production mechanisms are freeze-in and late time annihilation. In case of late time annihilation, excess DM is
produced from freeze-in mechanism and annihilates into
Z^{\prime}
.

Furthermore by considering the fast expanding Universe, it is possible to reconcile DM relic density and muon
(g-2)
anomaly
in our framework. We have also investigated the constraints from CCFR, Borexino, BABAR, WD cooling and BBN and found that
m_{Z^{\prime}}-\epsilon
plane is
stringently constrained from these observations. In particular, we have shown that for
n=4
, reconciliation of DM relic density for a thermally decoupled dark
sector and muon anomalous magnetic moment is possible while satisfying the other cosmological and astrophysical constraints
if
2\times 10^{-4}\lesssim\epsilon\lesssim 10^{-3}
,
5.5{\rm MeV}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 200{\rm MeV}
, and

1{\rm TeV}\lesssim m_{\chi}\lesssim 10{\rm TeV}
.

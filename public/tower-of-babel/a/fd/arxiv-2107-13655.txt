Charged Fluids in Porous Media
Mihaela Ignatova, Jingyang Shu

Abstract
. The Nernst-Planck-Darcy system models ionic electrodiffusion in porous media. We consider the system for two ionic species with opposite valences. We prove that the initial value problem for the Nernst-Planck-Darcy system in periodic domains in two or three dimensions has global weak solutions in W 1 , p W^{1,p} ( p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 ). We obtain furthermore global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions for arbitrary large data.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Nernst-Planck (NP) system

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{i}+\nabla\cdot\left(uc_{i}-D_{i}\nabla c_{i}-z_{i}D_{i}c_{i}\nabla\Phi\right)=0,\qquad i=1,\dotsc,N,

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta\Phi=\rho,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i},

coupled with Darcy’s law

(1.4)

\displaystyle u+\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\Phi,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u=0,

where
c_{i}\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
are the
i
-th ionic species concentrations,
z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
are corresponding valences,
D_{i}>0
are constant diffusivities,
\Phi\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is the nondimensional electrical potential,
\rho\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is the nondimensional charge density,
u\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the fluid velocity field,
p\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is the fluid pressure, and
\varepsilon>0
is a constant proportional to the square of the Debye length
[
6
,
20
]
. Here,
d=2,3
is the space dimension and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a
d
-dimensional torus.

We refer to the equations (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) as the Nernst-Planck-Darcy (NPD) equations. The NPD system models the transport and diffusion of ions by incompressible flow in porous media. The Nernst-Planck equation (
1.1
) describes the evolution of ions which are carried by an incompressible fluid and interact among themselves via an electric potential and thermal fluctuations. The Poisson-Boltzmann equation (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) relates the electrostatic potential to the ionic charge density. Darcy’s law replaces the viscous dissipation with friction, representing the damping of flow through porous media. We take the friction coefficient to be one, for simplicity. Darcy’s law (
1.4
) and the incompressibility condition (
1.5
) describe then the flow of an incompressible fluid through a porous medium forced by the macroscopic electrostatic Lorentz force created by the ions.

Ionic electrodiffusion is important to many fields, including biology, chemistry and physics, and has wide applications
[
20
]
. There are extensive mathematical studies of models coupling the Nernst-Planck equations with various fluid dynamical systems. For the fluids that are described by the Navier-Stokes equations, the system is known as the Nernst-Planck-Navier-Stokes (NPNS) system. In the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
), local existence of solutions of the NPNS system is obtained in
[
15
]
, and later weak solutions are proved to exist globally in time
[
19
]
, with some
L^{2}
decay if the dimension is two
[
26
]
. In bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the NPNS system has global solutions under various appropriate boundary conditions. For example, with blocking boundary conditions, global weak solutions exist in both two and three dimensions
[
10
,
16
]
. For ionic concentrations satisfying blocking boundary conditions while the electrical potential satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition, global weak solutions exist in two and three dimensions if the initial data is small
[
21
]
. In the case of blocking boundary conditions for the ionic concentrations and homogeneous Neumann boundary condition for the electrical potential, weak solutions are global in two dimensions
[
22
]
. Moreover, with blocking boundary conditions for the ionic concentrations and Robin boundary condition for the electrical potential, two dimensional strong solutions are global
[
2
]
. The same result holds in three dimensions if the fluid velocity remains regular for all time
[
17
]
. When the ionic concentrations satisfy either the blocking boundary conditions or the uniformly selective boundary conditions, strong solutions are global in two dimensions
[
6
]
, and in three dimensions provided that the initial data is a small perturbation of a steady state
[
8
]
. If both the ionic concentrations and the electrical potential obey the Dirichlet boundary conditions, global strong solutions exist in three dimensions as long as the fluid velocity is regular
[
9
]
. With periodic boundary conditions, two dimensional strong solutions exist globally in time, and long time behaviors of the solutions are studied in
[
1
]
under the influence of body forces or body charges. Some zero Debye length limit (
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
1.2
)) results for the NPNS system are proved in
[
7
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
. In the limit of zero viscosity in the Navier-Stokes equations, the solutions of NPNS system in two dimensions converges to the solutions of the corresponding Nernst-Planck-Euler (NPE) system, whose solutions exist and are global
[
14
,
25
,
27
]
. For the Nernst-Planck system coupled with time dependent Stokes equations, solutions are known existing globally in time in three dimensions
[
9
,
17
]
.

For the NPD system, global existence of weak solutions exist in
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

We focus on the initial value problem for the NPD equations (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) in two or three space dimensions (
d=2,3
) with two ionic species (
N=2
) with opposite valences (

) and with equal diffusivities (

). The initial data of the system is

(1.6)

\displaystyle c_{i}(\cdot,0)=c_{i}(0),\quad i=1,2,

where the ionic concentrations are nonnegative,
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
, and the electric charge obeys

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}z_{i}c_{i}(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}c_{1}(x,0)-c_{2}(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}{x}=0.

It follows from (

For regular solutions of NPNS it is shown in

We denote

,
\sigma=c_{1}+c_{2}
. Using (

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho&=-u\cdot\nabla\rho+D(\Delta\rho+\nabla\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi+\sigma\Delta\Phi),\end{split}

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\sigma&=-u\cdot\nabla\sigma+D(\Delta\sigma+\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi+\rho\Delta\Phi).\end{split}

We have from (
1.2

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta\Phi=\rho,

and from (

)–(
1.5

(1.11)

\displaystyle u+\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\Phi,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u=0.

The system (
1.8
)–(
1.12
) has initial data from (
1.6
),

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho(0)=c_{1}(0)-c_{2}(0),\\
&\sigma(\cdot,0)=\sigma(0)=c_{1}(0)+c_{2}(0),\end{split}

and

c_{1}=\frac{\sigma+\rho}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{2}=\frac{\sigma-\rho}{2}

solve the original Nernst-Planck-Darcy system (
1.1
)–(
1.5
).

We fix the parameters
\varepsilon>0
and
D>0
in (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) and will not specify them in the rest of the paper.

In this paper, we establish the existence of global weak solutions in
W^{1,r}
Sobolev spaces and the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions. Both results hold for arbitrary large data. The main theorems are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d=2,3
and
r\geq 2
. Let
c_{1}(0),c_{2}(0)\in W^{1,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be nonnegative functions satisfying (
1.7
). Then for any
T>0
, there exist
c_{1}(x,t)\geq 0
,
c_{2}(x,t)\geq 0
and
u(x,t)
, divergence free, such that
c_{1}-c_{2}=\rho
and
c_{1}+c_{2}=\sigma
obey
\rho,\sigma\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,
u(x,t)
obeys
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, and
(\rho,\sigma,u)
solve the initial value problem (
1.8
)–(
1.13
) in the sense of distributions. The charge density
\rho
and total concentration
\sigma
satisfy the following bounds

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(i)\quad\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\sigma(t)-\bar{\sigma}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}e^{-C^{\prime}t},\quad\quad\forall p\geq 2,\\
&(ii)\quad\|\nabla\Phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ce^{-C^{\prime}t},\\
&(iii)\quad\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\sigma(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\sigma(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}\leq C,\\
&(iv)\quad\left\|\nabla\rho(t)\right\|_{L^{r}}+\left\|\nabla\sigma(t)\right\|_{L^{r}}\leq Ce^{Ct},\end{split}

with constants
C,C_{p}>0
depending on
D
,
\varepsilon
,
p
,
r
, and the initial data
\|\rho(0)\|_{W^{1,r}}
and
\|\sigma(0)\|_{W^{1,r}}
and with
C^{\prime}>0
bounded below independently of
p
. Moreover, the fluid velocity satisfies the bound

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{r}}\leq Ce^{C^{\prime\prime}t},

where
C^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant depending on
\|\rho(0)\|_{W^{1,r}}
and
\|\sigma(0)\|_{W^{1,r}}

The existence of globally smooth solutions of three dimensional Nernst-Planck equations with arbitrary large data coupled to Stokes equations driven by the Lorentz force has been obtained only recently in
[

17
]

Replacing viscous dissipation by friction diminishes the regularizing effect by two differential orders. In this paper we use the nonlinear structure of the NP equations coupled to Darcy’s law, in order to prove the global regularity. The construction of solutions can be achieved by a two-tier approximation procedure as in
[

. The two-tier approximation scheme is needed because the global a priori

-estimate (

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

. Let

). Then for any

The proof of Theorem

2.
Global existence of weak solutions

In this section, we present the a priori estimates in Theorem
1.1
. We split the proof of the a priori estimates into several lemmas.

The first lemma concerns with the zeroth energy balance of the system (
1.8
)–(
1.12
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d=2,3
and
r\geq 2
. Let
c_{1}(0),c_{2}(0)\in W^{1,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be nonnegative functions satisfying (
1.7
). Suppose
(\rho,\sigma,u)
solves (
1.8
)–(
1.12
) with initial data (
1.13
) on the interval
[0,T]
. Then for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla\Phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}+\frac{2D}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}\leq 2\|\nabla\Phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Using (
1.8
) and (
1.10
), we have an evolutionary equation

\partial_{t}(-\Delta\Phi)=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u\cdot\nabla\rho+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}(\Delta\rho+\nabla\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi+\sigma\Delta\Phi).

Testing this equation with
\Phi
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int u\cdot\nabla\rho\Phi+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\Delta\rho\Phi+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\Phi)\Phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int u\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi)+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\rho\Delta\Phi-\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\sigma|\nabla\Phi|^{2}.

By equations (
1.11
) and (
1.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\sigma|\nabla\Phi|^{2}=0.

Integrating in time and discarding the last term on the left hand side (notice that
\sigma=c_{1}+c_{2}\geq 0
) give the inequality (
2.1
).
∎

The following lemma gives some useful bounds for the fluid velocity field
u
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d=2,3
. Let
(\rho,u)
satisfy (
1.11
)–(
1.12
). Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\rho\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{r}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}},

for all
p,r\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Applying the Leray projector to (
1.11
) and noting that the Leray projector is bounded on
L^{p}
when
p\in(1,\infty)
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
), we obtain (
2.2
). As for (
2.3
), we take the curl of (
1.11
) and have

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u=-\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi\quad\text{if}\ d=2,

\displaystyle\curl u=-\nabla\rho\times\nabla\Phi\quad\text{if}\ d=3.

Then the proof is completed by invoking the well-known estimate

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\|\curl u\|_{L^{r}}

for all
r\in(1,\infty)
.
∎

A key step of proving global a priori bounds for the weak solutions is to obtain (
1.14
)(ii) and thus (
1.14
)(i). In either two or three dimensions, in view of (

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

for

.

The following lemma states the pointwise exponential decay of
\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
d=2,3
and
r\geq 2
. Let
c_{1}(0),c_{2}(0)\in W^{1,r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be nonnegative functions satisfying (
1.7
). Suppose
(\rho,\sigma,u)
solves (
1.8
)–(
1.12
) with initial data (
1.13
) on the interval
[0,T]
. Then for any

, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}e^{-C^{\prime}t},\quad\quad\forall p\geq 2,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ce^{-C^{\prime}t},

for some constants
C_{p},C^{\prime}>0
, with
C^{\prime}
independent of
p
.

Proof.

We first observe that (
1.9
) is equivalent to

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})=-u\cdot\nabla(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})+D\left(\Delta(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})+\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi+\rho\Delta\Phi\right).

Note that the average
\bar{\sigma}\geq 0
since
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
and
\bar{\sigma}
is conserved in time due to (
1.9
).
Let
p\geq 2
. We multiply (
1.8
) by
\frac{1}{p-1}\rho|\rho|^{p-2}
and (
2.8
) by
\frac{1}{p-1}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}
, and then integrate by parts,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=-D\int|\rho|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}-D\int|\rho|^{p-2}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi-D\int|\rho|^{p-2}\bar{\sigma}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi,\end{split}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=-D\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}|\nabla(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})|^{2}-D\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}\rho\nabla(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\cdot\nabla\Phi.

Taking
p=2
, summing (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), and using (
1.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\sigma\rho^{2}=0.

Recall that the ionic concentrations
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
, so that
\sigma=c_{1}+c_{2}\geq 0
, and thus, the last term on the left hand side is nonnegative. Furthermore, since
|\rho|=|c_{1}-c_{2}|\leq c_{1}+c_{2}=\sigma
, we have that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\leq 0.

By the Poincaré inequality and Grönwall’s inequality, we deduce the following exponential pointwise decay

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma(t)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma(0)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)e^{-C^{\prime}t},

and the bounds

(2.13)

\displaystyle 2D\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\sigma(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}+\frac{2D}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}\leq\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma(0)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We obtain from (
2.4
) and (
2.12
) that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla\Phi(t)\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-C^{\prime}t}.\end{split}

For
p\geq 4
, we have from (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=-D\int|\rho|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\frac{D\bar{\sigma}}{(p-1)\varepsilon}\int|\rho|^{p}-D\int|\rho|^{p-2}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=-D\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}-D\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}\rho\nabla\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi.

Notice that by Hölder’s inequality, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{3(p-2)}{p-3}}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{dp}{6(p-2)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{dp}{6(p-2)}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}},

and Young’s inequality, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&D\int|\rho|^{p-2}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi\\
&\quad\leq\frac{2D}{p}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla|\rho|^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\||\rho|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})\|_{L^{3}}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{2D}{p}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla|\rho|^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\||\rho|^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{3(p-2)}{p-3}}}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla|\rho|^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{6}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{(6-d)p}{12}-1}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla|\rho|^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{D}{2}\int|\rho|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p-\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p-2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&D\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}\rho\nabla\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi\\
&\quad\leq\frac{D}{2}\int|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}|^{p-2}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-2}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.\end{split}

Using (
2.17
)–(
2.18
) into (
2.15
)–(
2.16
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}+\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\right)+\frac{D\bar{\sigma}}{\varepsilon}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\right)\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}+\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\right).

Dropping the dissipation term and applying Grönwall’s inequality and (
2.14

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\sigma(t)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left(\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\sigma(0)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}\right)e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}\leq C,\end{split}

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
p

Now we use (

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p-\frac{12}{6-d}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}+C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}}^{p-2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\right).

Integrating in time and applying (

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq e^{-\frac{D\bar{\sigma}p}{\varepsilon}t}\bigg(\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{12}{6-d}}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}\bigg)\leq Ce^{-\frac{D\bar{\sigma}p}{\varepsilon}t},

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}+\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}\right).

Gathering the estimates (
3.16
)–(
3.21
) into (
3.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\left(\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{5}\left(\|\Delta^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\sigma|\nabla\Delta\rho|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(1+\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\rho\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)+C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}+\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{20-2d}{7-d}}+\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{20-2d}{7-d}}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{6}{7-d}}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{6}{7-d}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\left(\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{24}{12-d}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{24}{12-d}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

(3.22)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\sigma\|_{L^{2}}\right).

We drop the dissipation terms, integrate in time, and use the bounds (
1.14
)(i)–(iii) and (
3.1
) to obtain

\|\nabla\Delta\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta\sigma(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C.

Going back to (
3.22
), we conclude that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta^{2}\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta^{2}\sigma(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}\leq C.

Finally, the Leibnitz rule, Hölder’s inequality, and (
3.12
) conclude (
3.13
).
∎

The last lemma establishes the uniqueness of strong solutions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d=2,3
and
s>1+\frac{d}{2}
. Let
(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})
,
(\rho_{2},\sigma_{2})\in\left(L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\right)^{2}
be two solutions of the initial value problem (
1.8
)–(
1.13
). Then
(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})=(\rho_{2},\sigma_{2})
.

Proof.

We denote the differences of
(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},\sigma_{2})
by
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})=(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})
. Then the equations for
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})
are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}=-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\rho_{2}+D\Delta\tilde{\rho}+D(\nabla\sigma_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\Phi}+\nabla\tilde{\sigma}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{2})+D(\sigma_{1}\Delta\tilde{\Phi}+\tilde{\sigma}\Delta\Phi_{2}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\sigma}=-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\sigma}-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\sigma_{2}+D\Delta\tilde{\sigma}+D(\nabla\rho_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\Phi}+\nabla\tilde{\rho}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{2})+D(\rho_{1}\Delta\tilde{\Phi}+\tilde{\rho}\Delta\Phi_{2}),

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{\rho},

\displaystyle\tilde{u}+\nabla(p_{1}-p_{2})=-\tilde{\rho}\nabla\Phi_{1}-\rho_{2}\nabla\tilde{\Phi},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}=0,

with initial data

\tilde{\rho}(\cdot,0)=\tilde{\sigma}(\cdot,0)=0.

The
L^{2}
-estimates for
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{u})
lead to

(3.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\tilde{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\tilde{\rho}^{2}\sigma_{1}\\
&\quad=-\int\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\rho_{2}-\int\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\sigma_{2}+D\int\tilde{\rho}\nabla\sigma_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\Phi}+D\int\tilde{\sigma}\nabla\rho_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\Phi}-\frac{D}{\varepsilon}\int\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\sigma}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})\\
&\quad\leq C\left(\|\nabla\rho_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Because
\sigma_{1}\geq 0
, we can drop the last two terms in the first line of (
3.23
). Finally, in view of Sobolev embeddings and Grönwall’s inequality, we obtain
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\sigma})=(0,0)
, which proves the uniqueness.
∎

Abstract
. In this article we study local constancy of the mod p p reduction of certain 2 2 -dimensional crystalline representations of Gal ​ ( ℚ ¯ p / ℚ p ) \text{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right) using the mod p p local Langlands correspondence. We prove local constancy in the weight space by giving an explicit lower bound on the local constancy radius centered around weights going up to ( p − 1 ) 2 + 3 (p-1)^{2}+3 and the slope fixed in ( 0 , p − 1 ) (0,\ p-1) satisfying certain constraints. We establish the lower bound by determining explicitly the mod p p reductions at nearby weights and applying a local constancy result of Berger.

Introduction

Let
p
be an odd prime and
f
be a normalized eigenform of weight
k\geq 1
, character
\psi
and level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
such that
p\nmid N
. The work of Deligne, Deligne-Serre, Eichler-Shimura associates to
f
a
p
-adic Galois representation
\rho_{f}:\text{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)\rightarrow\text{GL}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
such that
\rho_{f}
is unramified at all primes
l\nmid pN
. Further, the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\text{Frob}_{l})
(the arithmetic Frobenius at
l
) is given by
X^{2}-a_{l}X+l^{k-1}\psi(l)
, where
a_{l}
is the Hecke operator
T_{l}
-eigenvalue of
f
. In the ordinary case, a result of Deligne determines the mod
p
reduction
\bar{\rho}_{f}|_{D_{p}}^{ss}
(upto semisimplification) at the decomposition group
D_{p}\cong\mathrm{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
over
p
for weights
k\geq 2
. In the non ordinary case, Fontaine and Edixhoven determine
\bar{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{p}}
(
I_{p}
is inertia at
p
) for
2\leq k\leq p+1
. Faltings proved that if
p\nmid N
(
k\geq 2
) then
\rho_{f}|_{D_{p}}
is a crystalline representation of Hodge-Tate weights
(0,k-1)
. Thus, purely local methods computing
\bar{\rho}_{f}|_{D_{p}}^{ss}
exist that exploit the observation above. The problem of determining the mod
p
reduction of
2
-dimensional crystalline representations of
\mathrm{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
is a hard problem wherein the local techniques involve
p
-adic Hodge theory and more recently the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
due to Breuil and Berger (
[
B03a
]
,
[
B03b
]
,
[
BB10
]
,
[
B10
]
). Substantial work has been done using above local methods on computing the mod
p
reduction in various ranges of slopes and weights (see for instance
[
B03b
]
,
[
BLZ04
]
,
[
BG09
]
,
[

]
,
[
BG15

,

In this article we consider the problem of local constancy of the
\bmod\ p
reduction of certain
2
-dimensional crystalline representations of
\mathrm{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
. Broadly speaking, we obtain local constancy in the weight space for weights
k
up to
(p-1)^{2}+3
and the slope
\nu(a_{p})
fixed in
(0,\ p-1)
satisfying certain interdependency conditions (see Theorem
1.1
below). We determine an explicit radius of local constancy for these weights thereby obtaining a lower bound for the same. The key step in finding the radius above is the computation of the mod
p
reduction of the crystalline representations that come from this neighbourhood of the weight using the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence.

Let
p\geq 7
be a prime and
\nu:\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
be the normalized valuation such that
\nu(p)=1
. Let
0\not=a_{p}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with
\nu(a_{p})>0
, and
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
V_{k,a_{p}}
be the irreducible,
2
-dimensional crystalline Galois representation of
\mathrm{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
with Hodge-Tate weights
(0,\ k-1)
such that
D_{cris}(V^{*}_{k,a_{p}})\cong D_{k,a_{p}}
where
D_{cris}
is the Fontaine’s functor and
D_{k,a_{p}}
is the admissible filtered module given in
[
BLZ04
]
. We note in passing that the crystalline Frobenius on
D_{k,a_{p}}
has the characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}X+p^{k-1}
. Let
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
be the reduction of a
\mathrm{Gal}\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)
-stable lattice of
V_{k,a_{p}}
upto semisimplification. Our aim is to obtain local constancy of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
in the weight space with a fixed positive slope
\nu(a_{p})
. The evidence for local constancy is seen in results computing
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
for small slope. From these results and Berger’s theorem (Theorem B,
[
B12
]
,
[
B
]
or Theorem
2.3
below) we expect local constancy to hold if
k
and
k^{\prime}
are
p
-adically close enough and are in the same class modulo
p-1
.

The first result giving an explicit upper bound for Berger’s constant
m(k,a_{p})
is given in
[
SB20
]
for small weights with conditions on the slope similar to Theorem
1.1
.
More precisely, we write the weight
k
in the form
b+c(p-1)+2
, where
b,c
are assumed to be in the range
2\leq b\leq p-1,\ 0\leq c\leq 3
respectively, and such that
b\geq 2c
and
k\not\equiv 3\bmod(p+1)
. If the slope is in
(c,\frac{p}{2}+c)
and weight

it is shown in
[
SB20
]
that the Berger constant

exists and bounded above by
2\nu(a_{p})+1
. Our main result of this article is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k=b+c(p-1)+2
with
2\leq b\leq p
and
0\leq c\leq p-2
. Fix
a_{p}
such that
k>2\nu(a_{p})+2
and
c<\nu(a_{p})<\text{min}\{\frac{p}{2}+c-\epsilon,\ p-1\}
where
\epsilon
is defined as in (
2.4
). Further if
b\not\in\{2c+1,\ 2c-1,\ 2c-p,\ 2(c-1)-p\}
and
(b,c)\not=(p,0)
then the Berger’s constant
m(k,a_{p})
exists such that
m(k,a_{p})\leq\lceil 2\nu(a_{p})\rceil+\epsilon+1
. Moreover,
\bar{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\cong\mathrm{ind}\left(\omega^{k-1}_{2}\right)
for all
k^{\prime}\in k+p^{t}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, where
t\geq\lceil 2\nu(a_{p})\rceil+\epsilon
.

We take
p\geq 7
in order to apply Berger’s theorem in Corollary
6.3
. In our theorem, the conditions
2c\leq b\leq p-1
and
c\leq 3
in
[
SB20
]
are no longer there to include all
2\leq b\leq p
and
0\leq c\leq p-2
with above constraints. For example, with
c=p-2
we cover all the values of
b
for sufficiently large slope
p-2<\nu(a_{p})<p-1
. More precisely, if
c\geq\frac{p}{2}+1
then one could take the slope
\nu(a_{p})
in
(c,\ p-1)
. Note that the upper bound of
p/2+c
for the slope in
[
SB20
]
is assumed to be at most
p-1
. This is because with
\nu(a_{p})<p-1
(and
k-2>2\nu(a_{p})
) one is able to apply Lemma
3.2
in
[
SB20
]
(Lemma
2.4
below). In the theorem above the lower bound on

holds in any case for
c\geq 2
. Allowing for
c\geq 3
and
b\leq 2c-2
in our result makes the analysis significantly more involved, also revealing interesting phenomena discussed below.

The approach in
[
SB20
]
and our result is to show that the surjection
P:\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}\left(V_{r}\right)\rightarrow\bar{\Theta}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}
factors through a successive quotient
\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}\left(\frac{V^{(n)}_{r}}{V^{(n+1)}_{r}}\right)
for
k^{\prime}=r+2\in k+p^{t}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}^{>0}
, and for some
n\leq\lfloor\nu(a_{p})\rfloor
(see (
2.1
)). Using the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence, we obtain the result above in the generic irreducible case (Proposition
6.2
). In
[
SB20
]
,
n
remains constant and is equal to
c
where the hypothesis
b\geq 2c
plays a crucial role. Interestingly in our case, for a fixed
c
,
n
varies accordingly as
b
lies in
[2,\ 2c-2-p-1],[2c-2-p,\ 2c-2]
or
[2c-1,\ p]
. More precisely,
n=c-\epsilon
(if
(b,c)\neq(p,0)
, Theorem
5.3
) where
\epsilon
is as defined in (
2.4
). We show that all the Jordan Holder factors coming from
\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}\left(\frac{V^{(m)}_{r}}{V^{(m+1)}_{r}}\right)
where
0\leq m\leq\lfloor\nu(a_{p})\rfloor
and
m\not=n
do not contribute to
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{p}}
.
In fact, our proof splits naturally into two parts:
0\leq m<n
and
n<m\leq\lfloor\nu(a_{p})\rfloor
with a substantial difference in the analysis treating these two regimes. A crucial observation in
[
SB20
]
(Lemma
2.4
below) is that the successive quotients
\frac{V^{(m)}_{r}}{V^{(m+1)}_{r}}
are generated by the polynomial
F_{m}(x,y)
.

In Propositions
4.2
&
4.3
, we give a family of monomials say
\{P_{a,m}\}
for each
1\leq m<c-\epsilon
that are in
\mathrm{Ker}(P)
. A subset of these monomials are used in Proposition
4.6
to obtain (for each
m
) a family of monomials
\{Q_{a,m}\}
depending also on
\epsilon
that are
F_{m}(x,y)
(up to a unit) modulo
V_{r}^{(m+1)}+\mathrm{Ker}(P)
. We next obtain a monomial that is in both
\{P_{a,m}\}
and
\{Q_{a,m}\}
for each
1\leq m<c-\epsilon
.
Exploiting this we show (with some technical computation for
m=0
) that the successive quotients do not contribute to
\bar{\Theta}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}
for

(Proposition
5.1
, Lemma

). The cases

differ from above in that the polynomial

is directly shown to be in
\mathrm{Ker}(P)
(Proposition
5.2
& Lemma
2.2
). We further use the monomials

to eliminate the possibility of

being reducible in some non generic cases.

To generate the monomials
P_{a,m}
(and

if

) we consider for each

a matrix
A
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
coming naturally from Propositions

&
4.4
, and show that the columns space of

contains the vectors needed to generate these monomials. The matrices are typically of size

(

) with entries given by products of binomial coefficients, and thus require proving certain binomial identities (see §3).
The computations involving the Hecke operator
T
in Propositions
4.1
&
4.4
require a delicate choice of functions to obtain the specific polynomials. The lower bounds
\lceil 2\nu(a_{p})\rceil+\epsilon
and

.

For the weight
k
in our range we have
\lfloor\frac{k-2}{p-1}\rfloor=c<\nu(a_{p})
barring a few exceptions. Therefore,
[
BLZ04
]
implies
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}\cong\mathrm{ind}(\omega^{k-1}_{2})
whenever

and reducible otherwise. Using this fact together with the mod

in Proposition

. Theorem

and Proposition

imply that the reducible cases can occur only if

or if

. If there is local constancy, we expect from

]
that

always be reducible if

or

2.
Background

2.1.
The mod
p
local Langlands correspondence

We begin by recalling some notations and definitions. We fix an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the ring of integers
\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and the residue field
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
G_{p}
and
G_{p^{2}}
be the absolute Galois groups of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
respectively where
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
is the unique unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\omega_{1}=\omega
be the mod
p
cyclotomic character, and
\omega_{2}
be a fixed fundamental character of level
2
. We view
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
as characters of
\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}
via local class field theory (identifying uniformizers with geometric Frobenii). For
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that
(p+1)\nmid a
let
\mathrm{ind}(\omega^{a}_{2})
denote the unique two dimensional irreducible representation of
G_{p}
with determinant
\omega^{a}
and whose restriction to inertia is isomorphic to
\omega^{a}_{2}\oplus\omega^{ap}_{2}
.

We denote the group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by
G
, its maximal compact subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
by
K
and the center of
G
by
Z\cong\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}
. For
r\geq 0
let
V_{r}:=\text{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2})
be the symmetric power representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of dimension
r+1
. We can also view
V_{r}
as representations of
KZ
by defining the action of
K
through the natural surjection
K\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and by letting
p
act trivially. For
0\leq r\leq p-1,\ \lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and a smooth character
\eta:\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{F}}^{*}_{p}
, the representation

\pi(r,\lambda,\eta):=\frac{\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(V_{r})}{T-\lambda}\otimes(\eta\circ\det)

is a smooth admissible representation of
G
where
\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}
denotes compact induction (see
[
B03a
]
,
[
BG09
]
). The operator
T
(see §
2.2
) is the Hecke operator
T_{p}
generating the Hecke algebra
\text{End}_{G}(\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(V_{r}))=\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[T_{p}]
. These representations give all the irreducible smooth admissible representations of
G
(
[
BL94
]
,
[
BL95
]
,
[
B03a
]
). For
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, let
\mu_{\lambda}
be the unramified character of
G_{p}
that sends the geometric Frobenius to
\lambda
. Then Breuil ’s semisimple
\bmod\ p
local Langlands correspondence
LL
(see
[
B03b
]
) is as follows:

•

\lambda=0:

\mathrm{ind}(\omega^{r+1}_{2})\otimes\eta\xleftrightarrow{LL}\pi(r,0,\eta)

•

\lambda\not=0:

(\mu_{\lambda}\omega^{r+1}\oplus\mu_{\lambda^{-1}})\otimes\eta\xleftrightarrow{LL}\pi(r,\lambda,\eta)^{ss}\oplus\pi([p-3-r],\lambda^{-1},\omega^{r+1}\eta)^{ss}

where
\{0,1,...,p-2\}\ni[p-3-r]\equiv p-3-r\bmod(p-1)
.

For integers
k\geq 2
we define
\Pi_{k,a_{p}}:=\frac{\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{2}))}{T-a_{p}}
as representations of
G
where
r=k-2
and
T
is the Hecke operator from §
2.2
. We consider the
G
-stable lattice
\Theta_{k,a_{p}}
in the irreducible representation
\Pi_{k,a_{p}}
(see
[
B03b
]
,
[
BB10
]
) given by

\Theta_{k,a_{p}}:=\text{image}\left(\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}))\rightarrow\Pi_{k,a_{p}}\right)\cong\frac{\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}))}{(T-a_{p})\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{2}))}.

By the compatibility of the
p
-adic and mod
p
local Langlands correspondences (
[
B03b
]
,
[
B10
]
,
[
BB10
]
) we have

\bar{\Theta}^{ss}_{k,a_{p}}\cong LL(\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}})\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{p}}:=\Theta_{k,a_{p}}\otimes\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

Since the
\bmod\ p
local Langlands correspondence is injective, to determine
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
it is enough to compute
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{p}}^{ss}.

2.2.
Hecke Operator T

We give an explicit definition of the Hecke operator
T=T_{p}
below (see
[
B03b
]
for more details). For
m=0
, set
I_{0}=\{0\}
and for
m>0
, let
I_{m}=\{[\lambda_{0}]+p[\lambda_{1}]+...+p^{m}[\lambda_{m-1}]\ |\ \lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
where square brackets denote Teichmüller representatives. For
m\geq 1
there is a truncation map
[\ ]_{m-1}:I_{m}\rightarrow I_{m-1}
given by taking the first
m-1
terms in the
p
-adic expansion above. For
m=1
,
[\ ]_{m-1}
is the zero map. For
m\geq 0
and
\lambda\in I_{m}
, let

g^{0}_{m,\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}p^{m}&\lambda\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and }\quad g^{1}_{m,\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
p\lambda&p^{m+1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have

G=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0,\lambda\in I_{m}\\
i\in\{0,1\}\end{subarray}}{\coprod}KZ(g^{i}_{m,\lambda})^{-1}.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra and
V=\text{Sym}^{r}R^{2}
be the symmetric power representation of
KZ
, modelled on homogeneous polynomials of degree
r
in the variables
x
and
y
over
R
. For
g\in G,\ v\in V
, let
[g,\ v]
be the function defined by:
[g,v](g^{\prime})=g^{\prime}g\cdot v
for all
g^{\prime}\in KZg^{-1}
and zero otherwise. Since an element of
\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(V)
is a
V
-valued function on
G
that has compact support modulo
KZ
, one can see that every element of
\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(V)
can be written as a finite sum of
[g,v]
with
g=g^{0}_{m\lambda}
or
g=g^{1}_{m,\lambda}
, for some
\lambda\in I_{m}
and
v\in V
. Then the action of
T
on
[g,v]
can be given explicitly when
g=g^{0}_{n,\mu}
with
n\geq 0
and
\mu\in I
. Let
v=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}c_{j}x^{r-j}y^{j}
, with
c_{j}\in R
. We write
T=T^{+}+T^{-}
where

\displaystyle T^{+}([g^{0}_{n,\mu},v])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\lambda\in I_{1}}{\sum}\left[g^{0}_{n+1,\mu+p^{n}\lambda},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}p^{j}\left(\sum\limits_{i=j}^{r}c_{i}{i\choose j}(-\lambda)^{i-j}\right)x^{r-j}y^{j}\right]

\displaystyle T^{-}([g^{0}_{n,\mu},v])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g^{0}_{n-1,[\mu]_{n-1}},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\left(\sum\limits_{i=j}^{r}p^{r-i}c_{i}{i\choose j}\left(\frac{\mu-[\mu]_{n-1}}{p^{n-1}}\right)^{i-j}\right)x^{r-j}y^{j}\right]\quad\text{for}\ n>0

\displaystyle T^{-}([g^{0}_{n,\mu},v])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\alpha,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}p^{r-j}c_{j}x^{r-j}y^{j}\right]\quad\text{for}\ n=0,\ \text{where}\ \alpha:=g^{1}_{0,0}.

2.3.
The filtration

Let
r=k^{\prime}-2\geq(\nu+1)(p+1)
, where
\nu:=\lfloor\nu(a_{p})\rfloor
. From the definition of
V_{r}
and
\bar{\Theta}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}
it follows that there is a natural surjection

P:\mathrm{ind}^{G}_{KZ}(V_{r})\twoheadrightarrow\bar{\Theta}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}.

Now let us consider the Dickson polynomial

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
c,m,b,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
m\leq b-c,\ k\geq 1
then

\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}{{b-m-c+k-i}\choose b-m-c}=0

\text{and}\quad\sum_{0\leq l\leq c}(-1)^{c-l}{{b-m-c+1}\choose{b-m-c-l}}{{b-m-l}\choose{c-l}}=(-1)^{c}{{b-m+1}\choose b-m-c}.

Proof.

Consider the following

(x-1)^{b-m-c+1}x^{k-1}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq b-m-c+1}(-1)^{i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}x^{b-m-c+k-i}.

On differentiate with respect to
x,(b-m-c)
time, putting
x=1
and dividing by
(b-m-c)!
, gives

\sum_{0\leq i\leq b-m-c+1}(-1)^{i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}{{b-m-c+k-i}\choose b-m-c}=0.

Observe
b-m-c+k-i\geq 0\ \ \forall\ \ i
, and if
k<b-m-c+1
then
{{b-m-c+k-i}\choose b-m-c}=0\ \ \forall\ \ i\geq k+1
and if
k>b-m-c+1
then
{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}=0\ \ \forall\ \ i>b-m-c+1
. Therefore above summation runs over
0
to
k
so first part is done.

Now for the second part, we put
l=i-1
, and so we need to prove the following

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq c+1}(-1)^{c+1-i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}{{b-m+1-i}\choose b-m-c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{c}{{b-m+1}\choose b-m-c}\hskip 80.00012pt

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \sum_{0\leq i\leq c+1}(-1)^{c+1-i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}{{b-m+1-i}\choose b-m-c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\hskip 140.00021pt

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \sum_{0\leq i\leq c+1}(-1)^{i}{{b-m-c+1}\choose i}{{b-m+1-i}\choose b-m-c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\hskip 140.00021pt

which is part one of this Lemma for
k=c+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For every

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j}(-1)^{i+1}{{m+1}\choose i}{{m+j-i}\choose j-i}={{m+j}\choose j}.

We prove Lemma by induction on

. For

and need to prove

.
Now

\displaystyle{{m+k+1}\choose k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+k+1)}{k+1}{{m+k}\choose k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+k+1)}{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}{{m+1}\choose i}{{m+k-i}\choose k-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}{{m+1}\choose i}\left(\frac{(m+k+1-i)}{k+1}+\frac{i}{k+1}\right){{m+k-i}\choose k-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}{{m+1}\choose i}\left(\frac{(k+1-i)}{k+1}{{m+k+1-i}\choose k+1-i}+\frac{i}{k+1}{{m+k-i}\choose k-i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}{{m+1}\choose i}{{m+k+1-i}\choose k+1-i}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{i}{k+1}{{m+1}\choose i}{{m+k-i}\choose k+1-i}.

So to prove our result we need to prove following

\displaystyle-(-1)^{k}{{m+1}\choose k+1}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{i}{k+1}{{m+1}\choose i}{{m+k-i}\choose k+1-i}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i+1}{m\choose i-1}{{m+k-i}\choose k+1-i}+(-1)^{k}{m\choose k}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq k-1}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}{{m+k-1-i}\choose k-i}+(-1)^{k}{m\choose k}=0\quad\text{by replacing $i-1$ by $i$ }

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}{{m+k-1-i}\choose m-1}=0.

Now we consider the following

\displaystyle(x-1)^{m}x^{k-1}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}x^{m+k-1-i}

differentiate with respect to
x,(m-1)
time, divide by
(m-1)!
and putt
x=1

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}{{m+k-1-i}\choose m-1}=0.

. If

then for

. So in all the cases we got our result.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
p\geq 7
be a prime and
k=s+2
. Assume all the hypotheses of Theorem
5.3
. If we further assume
b\not\in\{2c+1,\ 2c-1,\ 2c-p,\ 2(c-1)-p\}
and
(b,c)\not=(p,0)
then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}\cong\mathrm{ind}\left(\omega^{k-1}_{2}\right)
.

Proof.

We begin by observing that if
\nu(a_{p})>c+1
then the conclusion follows by
[
BLZ04
]
(note that
p+1\nmid k-1
from hypothesis). So from now on we will assume
\nu(a_{p})\leq c+1
. Observe that since
\nu(a_{p})\leq c+1
we have

\displaystyle 3\nu(a_{p})+\frac{(k-1)p}{(p-1)^{2}}+1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4(c+1)+\frac{b+1}{(p-1)}+\frac{k-1}{(p-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\begin{cases}4(c+1)+2&\text{if}\quad 2\leq b\leq p-3\\
4(c+1)+3&\text{if}\quad p-2\leq b\leq p.\end{cases}

The last inequality follows as
k\leq(p-1)^{2}+3
and
p\geq 5
. If
c=0
then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}\cong\mathrm{ind}\left(\omega^{k-1}_{2}\right)
by
[
B03b
]
as
k\leq p+1
. Therefore, assuming
c\geq 1
and
p\geq 7
we get
k-4(c+1)\geq b
, giving us
k>3\nu(a_{p})+\frac{(k-1)p}{(p-1)^{2}}+1
. So by Theorem
2.3
there exists a constant
m=m(k,a_{p})
such that for all
k^{\prime\prime}\in k+p^{m-1}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
we have
\bar{V}_{k^{\prime\prime},a_{p}}\cong\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
. For
t
as in Proposition
6.2
we have
\bar{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\cong\mathrm{ind}\left(\omega^{k-1}_{2}\right)
for
k^{\prime}\in k+p^{t}(p-1)\mathbb{N}
. Hence these two facts together gives
m(k,a_{p})\leq t+1
, and so we have the desired result.
∎

We have our main result below.

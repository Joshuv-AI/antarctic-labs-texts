Shadowing as a Structural Property of the Space of Dynamical Systems
Jonathan Meddaugh

Abstract
. We demonstrate that there is a large class of compact metric spaces for which the shadowing property can be characterized as a structural property of the space of dynamical systems. We also demonstrate that, for this class of spaces, in order to determine whether a system has shadowing, it is sufficient to check that continuously generated pseudo-orbits can be shadowed.

1.
Introduction

For a function
f:X\to X
on a metric space
X
, a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit is a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle
in
X
which satisfies
d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\delta
. These approximations of orbits arise naturally when orbits are computed numerically and can diverge significantly from the orbits that they are approximating. However, there is a large class of functions for which sufficiently precise pseudo-orbits can be tracked within a given level of tolerance. Such systems are said to have the
shadowing property
(or
pseudo-orbit tracing property
). It is immediately clear that this property is important in numerical modeling of dynamical systems, especially for those in which errors are likely to grow (see
[
6
,
7
,
19
,
20
]
).

While it has natural applications in the setting of numerical modeling, shadowing has significant theoretical importance. Indeed, the origin of the shadowing property begins with analysis of of Anosov and Axiom A diffeomorphisms–Bowen implicitly used this property in his proof that the nonwandering set of an Axiom A diffeomorphism is a factor of a subshift of finite type
[
2
]
. Later, Sinai and Bowen demonstrated explicitly that the shadowing property holds for large subsystems of these diffeomorphisms
[
3
,
24
]
. Since then, it has been observed that shadowing plays an important role in stability theory
[
21
,
23
,
25
]
and in characterizing
\omega
-limit sets
[
1
,
2
,
14
]
. Shadowing has also been shown to be a relatively common property in the space of dynamical systems on certain classes of spaces
[
5
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
22
,
26
]
.

In this paper, we demonstrate that, for a large class of spaces, the shadowing property is a
structural property
of the space of dynamical systems (
X^{X}
or
\mathcal{C}(X)
), in that whether a system has shadowing can be observed only by examining its neighborhood basis in that space. In particular, the main results of this paper are the following theorems.

Theorem
7
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit.

Theorem
18
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space which is perturbable and let
f\in C(X)
. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we formalize the definitions and notation that we will use for the remainder of the paper. In Section
3
, we focus on the space
X^{X}
of not necessarily continuous dynamical systems and develop the theory leading up to Theorem
7
. We also prove results for nonautonomous dynamical systems. Section
4
discusses analogous results for the space
\mathcal{C}(X)
and culminates in the proof of Theorem
18
. Finally, in Section
5
we discuss the results of Sections
3
and
4
from different angles. We develop variations of the shadowing property that encapsulate these results and also demonstrate that shadowing can be viewed as a weak form of continuity for the function which maps a dynamical system to its set of orbits.

2.
Preliminaries

A
dynamical system
is a pair
(X,f)
consisting of a compact metric space
X
and a function
f:X\to X
. This function need not be continuous, and is not assumed to be unless otherwise stated.

For a fixed space
X
, we can identify a dynamical system with the function alone, allowing us to consider the space of dynamical systems on
X
. In particular, let
X^{X}
denote the space of functions from
X
to
X
with topology generated by the supremum metric

\rho(f,g)=\sup_{x\in X}d(f(x),g(x)).

Also, let
\mathcal{C}(X)
denote the subspace of
X^{X}
consisting of the continuous functions (maps) from
X
to
X
.

For
x\in X
and
f\in X^{X}
, the
orbit of
x
under
f
(or, the
f
-orbit of
x
) is the sequence
\langle f^{i}(x)\rangle_{i\in\omega}
where
f^{0}
denotes the identity. For
\delta>0
, a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
f
is a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle_{i\in\omega}
such that for all
i\in\omega
,
d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\delta
. For
\epsilon>0
, we say that a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle_{i\in\omega}
in
X

\epsilon
-shadows
a sequence
\langle y_{i}\rangle_{i\in\omega}
in
X
provided that
d(x_{i},y_{i})<\epsilon
for all
i\in\omega
.

A dynamical system
(X,f)
has
shadowing
(or the
pseudo-orbit tracing property
, sometimes denoted
POTP
) provided that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
f
, then there exists
z\in X
such that for all
i
,
d(x_{i},f^{i}(z))<\epsilon
, i.e. the orbit of
z

\epsilon
-shadows
the pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
.

For the purposes of this paper, it is useful to consider dynamical systems consisting of
sequences
of functions from
X
to
X
. When a distinction is necessary, we will refer to dynamical systems consisting of a single function as
autonomous
and those consisting of several functions as
nonautonomous
. We will endow the space,
(X^{X})^{\omega}
, of such systems with the uniform topology, with metric given by

\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)=\sup_{i\in\omega}\rho(f_{i},g_{i}).

Let
\langle f_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
. For
x\in X
, the
orbit of
x
under
\langle f_{i}\rangle
(or, the
\langle f_{i}\rangle
-orbit of
x
) is the sequence
\langle f^{i}_{0}(x)\rangle
where
f^{i}_{0}
denotes the composition of the first
i
-many functions in the sequence, i.e.
f^{i}_{0}=f_{i-1}\circ f_{i-2}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}\circ f_{0}
and
f^{0}_{0}
denotes the identity. For
\delta>0
, a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
is a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle
such that for all
i\in\omega
,
d(f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\delta
.

We say that a nonautonomous dynamical system
(X,\langle f_{i}\rangle)
has
shadowing
provided that for all that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
, then there exists
z\in X
such that for all
i
,
d(x_{i},f^{i}_{0}(z))<\epsilon
, i.e. the orbit of
z

\epsilon
-shadows
the pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
.

It is worth noting that the autonomous dynamical system
(X,f)
can be naturally identified with the dynamical system
(X,\langle f\rangle)
since it is clear that the dynamical properties of the systems
(X,f)
and
(X,\langle f\rangle)
are identical.

3.
Shadowing as a structural property in
X^{X}

In this section, we examine shadowing as a structural property of dynamical systems in the
space
of (not necessarily continuous) dynamical systems. We begin by developing a characterization of the shadowing property in the space
(X^{X})^{\omega}
of nonautonomous dynamical systems on
X
.

As a first step, we make the following observation:

Remark 1
.

If
\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
with
\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)<\delta
, then every
\langle g_{i}\rangle
-orbit is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
.

Proof.

Fix
\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle
with
\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)<\delta
and let
x\in X
. Then
\langle g^{i}_{0}(x)\rangle
satisfies

d(f_{i}(g^{i}_{0}(x)),g^{i+1}_{0}(x))=d(f(g^{i}_{0}(x)),g_{i}(g^{i}_{0}(x)))\leq\rho(f_{i},g_{i})\leq\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)<\delta

and is, therefore, a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
. ∎

In other words, for a dynamical system, orbits of nearby systems are pseudo-orbits for the original system. As we see in the following lemma, if
\langle f_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
, the converse is true as well.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\langle f_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
,
\delta>0
, and
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
. Then there exists
\langle g_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
such that
\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)\leq\delta
and
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is an orbit for
\langle g_{i}\rangle
.

Proof.

For each
i\in\omega
, define
g_{i}
as follows:
g_{i}(y)=f_{i}(y)
for all
y\in X\setminus\{x_{i}\}
and
g_{i}(x_{i})=x_{i+1}
. By construction, the sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is an orbit of
\langle g_{i}\rangle
.

Finally, it is easily seen that for each
i\in\omega
,
\rho(f_{i},g_{i})=d(x_{i+1},f_{i}(x_{i}))<\delta
, and thus
\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)\leq\delta
.
∎

Since pseudo-orbits are identifiable with orbits for nearby systems in this setting, we can easily verify that the shadowing property can be characterized as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
\langle f_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
. The dynamical system
(X,\langle f_{i}\rangle)
has shadowing if and only if for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\langle g_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
with
\rho(\langle g_{i}\rangle,\langle f_{i}\rangle)<\delta
, then each
\langle g_{i}\rangle
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
\langle f_{i}\rangle
-orbit.

Proof.

First, suppose that the dynamical system
(X,\langle f_{i}\rangle)
has shadowing. Fix
\epsilon>0
and choose
\delta>0
to witness shadowing with respect to
\epsilon
. If
\langle g_{i}\rangle
is a sequence with
\rho(\langle g_{i}\rangle,\langle f_{i}\rangle)<\delta
, then every
\langle g_{i}\rangle
-orbit is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
, and is thus shadowed by some
\langle f_{i}\rangle
-orbit.

Now, suppose that
(\langle f_{i}\rangle,X)
has the indicated property. Fix
\epsilon>0
and choose
\delta_{0}>0
to witness. Define
\delta=\delta_{0}/2
and let
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle
. Then, by Lemma
2
, there exists
\langle g_{i}\rangle
with
\rho(\langle f_{i}\rangle,\langle g_{i}\rangle)\leq\delta<\delta_{0}
such that
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\langle g_{i}\rangle
-orbit, and as such, is
\epsilon
-shadowed by some
\langle f_{i}\rangle
-orbit.
∎

Since autonomous systems can be viewed as members of
(X^{X})^{\omega}
, as an immediate corollary, we have the following characterization of shadowing for autonomous systems.

Corollary 4
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
f\in X^{X}
. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\langle g_{i}\rangle\in(X^{X})^{\omega}
with
\rho(\langle g_{i}\rangle,\langle f\rangle)<\delta
, then each
\langle g_{i}\rangle
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit.

Of course, this characterization requires knowledge of an entire neighborhood of
\langle f\rangle
in
(X^{X})^{\omega}
. For an autonomous dynamical system, a characterization requiring only information in
X^{X}
would be far preferable. The primary obstacle to such a characterization is that a non-periodic pseudo-orbit which visits the same point twice cannot be realized as an orbit of an autonomous system—in order to guarantee shadowing, such pseudo-orbits need to be accounted for.

If we assume that our domain space contains no isolated points, we are able to bypass this obstacle for continuous dynamical systems by perturbing such pseudo-orbits. This will allow us to characterize shadowing as a structural property for autonomous dynamical systems on spaces with no isolated points.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
\gamma>0
. Then if
\langle x_{i}\rangle
a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit for
f
for which
x_{i}=x_{j}
implies that
x_{i+1}=x_{j+1}
, then there exists
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(g,f)\leq\gamma
and
z\in X
such that for all
i\leq N
,
g^{i}(z)=x_{i}
.

Proof.

Fix
\gamma>0
and let
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit for
f
satisfying the hypotheses of the lemma.

Now, define
g:X\to X
by

g(x)=\begin{cases}x_{i+1}&x=x_{i}\\
f(x)&x\notin\{x_{i}:i\in\omega\}\end{cases}.

Note that
g
is well-defined since if
x_{i}=x_{j}
, then
g(x_{i})=x_{i+1}=x_{j+1}=g(y_{i})
.

By construction,
g(x_{i})=x_{i+1}
and for all
x\in X
,
d(f(x),g(x))\leq\sup\{d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1}):i\in\omega\}\leq\gamma
, i.e.
\rho(f,g)\leq\gamma
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
f\in C(X)
such that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit. If
p\in X
is isolated, then for every
\delta^{\prime}
there exists
\gamma_{p}>0
such that if
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\gamma_{p}
-pseudo-orbit for
f
, then either

(1)

|\{i\in\omega:p=x_{i}\}|\leq 1
or

(2)

p
is periodic and there exists
M\in\omega
with
x_{M}
in the orbit of
p
and for any such
M
,
\sup\{d({x_{M+i}},{f^{i}(x_{M})})\}<\delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta^{\prime}>0
. Let
p\in X
be isolated and suppose that for every
\gamma>0
there exists a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit for
f
in which
p
appears at least twice (else we can choose
\gamma_{p}>0
such that condition (1) holds for every
\gamma_{p}
-pseudo-orbit).
Since
p
is isolated, we can choose
\epsilon>0
with
B_{\epsilon}(p)=\{p\}
, and by supposition, there is
\delta>0
such that if
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then every
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit. Finally, choose
\delta>\gamma>0
.

By supposition, we can find a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
for
f
in which
p
appears at least twice—let
M\in\omega
and
N>0
be minimally chosen so that
p=x_{M}=x_{N+M}
.

Define, for
i<N
,
y_{i}=x_{M+i}
and consider the finite sequence
y_{0},\ldots,y_{N-1}
. Notice that
y_{0}=p
,
d(f(y_{N-1}),y_{0})<\gamma
, and for all
i<N-1
, we have
d(f(y_{i}),y_{i+1})<\gamma
. Suppose that there is an element of
X
that occurs more than once in this list. Choose
n<N-1
and
t>0
such that
y_{n}=y_{n+t}
and define the finite sequence
y^{\prime}_{0},\ldots y^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}-1}
as follows. Define
N^{\prime}=N-t
and

y^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}y_{i}&i\leq n\\
y_{i+t}&i>n\end{cases}

and observe that
y^{\prime}_{0}=p
,
d(f(y^{\prime}_{N-1}),z_{0})<\gamma
, and for all
i<N^{\prime}-1
, we have
d(f(y^{\prime}_{i}),y^{\prime}_{i+1})<\gamma
. By iterating this process as necessary, we arrive at a finite sequence
z_{0},\ldots z_{P-1}
with no repeated elements, such that
z_{0}=p
,
d(f(z_{P-1}),z_{0})<\gamma
, and for
i<P-1
,
d(f(z_{i}),z_{i+1})<\gamma
. It is easy to see then that if we define
z_{i}=z_{i\mod P}
for
i\geq P
,
\langle z_{i}\rangle
is a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit with
z_{kP}=p
for all
k\in\omega
which satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
5
.

As such, we can find a function
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(g,f)\leq\gamma<\delta
such that
\langle z_{i}\rangle
is a
g
-orbit, and therefore is
\epsilon
-shadowed by the
f
-orbit of some point. But that point and its image under
f^{P}
must lie in
B_{\epsilon}(p)=\{p\}
, and therefore the shadowing point is
p
itself and
p
has period
P
.

Now, by uniform continuity of
f,f^{2},\ldots f^{P-1}
, choose
\gamma_{p}>0
such that
\gamma_{p}<\min\{\epsilon,\delta^{\prime}/2\}/P
and if
d(a,b)<\gamma_{p}
, then
d(f^{i}(a),f^{i}(b))<\min\{\epsilon,\delta^{\prime}/2\}/P
for all
i<P
. Now, let
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\gamma_{p}
-pseudo-orbit for
f
with
x_{M}
in the orbit of
p
with
k<P
chosen such that
f^{k}(x_{M})=p
. Then, for
i\leq P
, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{M+i},f^{i}(x_{M}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d\left(f^{(i-j)}(x_{M+j}),f^{(i-j-1)}(x_{M+j+1})\right)

\displaystyle=d(f(x_{M+i-1}),x_{M+i})+\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}d\left(f^{(i-j)}(f(x_{M+j})),f^{(i-j)}(x_{M+j+1})\right)

\displaystyle<\gamma_{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}\min\{\epsilon,\delta^{\prime}/2\}/P<\min\{\epsilon,\delta^{\prime}/2\}

In particular,
x_{M+k}\in B_{\epsilon}(f^{k}(x_{M}))=B_{\epsilon}(p)=\{p\}
and by induction (applying the above argument to
x_{M+k}=p
), we can establish that
d(x_{M+k+i},f^{i}(p))<\delta^{\prime}/2
for all
i\in\omega
, and thus
\sup\{d({x_{M+i}},f^{i}(p))\}\leq\delta^{\prime}/2<\delta^{\prime}
as desired.

∎

Lemmas
5
and
6
allow us to prove the following characterization of shadowing.

Theorem 7
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit.

Proof.

As with Theorem
3
, if
(X,f)
has shadowing, then the conclusion follows easily.

So, let us now assume that
(X,f)
has the indicated property and fix
\epsilon>0
. Choose
\epsilon/2>\delta>0
to witness that if
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(f,g)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon/2
shadowed by an
f
-orbit. Finally, choose
\delta>\gamma>0
.

Now, by uniform continuity of
f
, fix
\gamma/3>\beta
such that if
d(a,b)<\beta
, then
d(f(a),f(b))<\gamma/3
, and define

S=\{x\in X:B_{\beta}(x)\textrm{ is infinite}\}.

Observe that
X\setminus S
is finite—otherwise, it would have a limit point
p
and a point
q\in X\setminus S
with
p\in B_{\beta}(q)
. But then
B_{\beta}(q)
is infinite, a contradiction. For each
p\in X\setminus S
, use Lemma
6
to choose
\gamma_{p}>0
such that either (1)
|\{i\in\omega:p=x_{i}\}|\leq 1
or (2)
p
is periodic and there exists
M\in\omega
with
x_{M}
in the orbit of
p
and for any such
M
,
\sup\{d({x_{M+i}},{f^{i}(x_{M})})\}<\gamma/3
.

Now, fix
\delta_{0}
to be the lesser of
\beta
and
\min\{\gamma_{p}:p\in X\setminus S\}
. We claim that every
\delta_{0}
-pseudo-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an orbit for
f
.

In order to verify this, let
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\delta_{0}
-pseudo-orbit for
f
. We will now define a nearby
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit
\langle y_{i}\rangle
for which
y_{i}=y_{j}
implies
y_{i+1}=y_{j+1}
to which we will apply Lemma
5
to achieve our claimed results.

There are two cases to consider.

Case 1:

\langle x_{i}\rangle
has the property that if
i<j
with
x_{i}=x_{j}
, then
x_{i}\in S
. In this case, we proceed inductively, defining
y_{0}=x_{0}
and, once
y_{i}
is defined for all
i<n
, we define
y_{n}=x_{n}
if
x_{n}\notin S
and choose
y_{n}\in B_{\beta}(x_{n})\cap S\setminus\{y_{i}:i<n\}
otherwise. Clearly,
\sup\{d({y_{i}},{x_{i}})\}\leq\beta
and for
i\in\omega
,

\displaystyle\@text@daccent{(}f(y_{i}),y_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq d(f(y_{i}),f(x_{i}))+d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})+d(x_{i+1},y_{i+1})

\displaystyle<\gamma/3+\delta_{0}+\beta<\gamma.

i.e.,
\langle y_{i}\rangle
is a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit. Furthermore, by construction if
i\neq j
, then
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
.

Case 2:
There exists
p\in X\setminus S
and
m<n
with
x_{n}=x_{m}=p
. Choose
M
minimal so that
x_{M}
is in the orbit of some point
p\notin S
and there exists
M\leq m<n
with
x_{m}=x_{n}=p
. By choice of
\delta_{0}
and the existence of
m,n
we are in case (2) of Lemma
6
, and so
x_{M}
is periodic and
\sup\{d({x_{M+i}},f^{i}(x_{M}))\}<\gamma/3
.

For
i<M
, we choose
y_{i}
as in Case 1, with the additional condition that
y_{i}
not be in the orbit of
x_{M}
.

For
i\geq M
, define
y_{i}=f^{i-M}(x_{M})
. It is then clear that
\langle y_{i}\rangle
is a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit (by the same argument above) and that
\sup\{d({x_{i}},{y_{i}})\}\leq\max\{\beta,\gamma/3\}=\gamma/3
. By construction we see that the hypotheses of Lemma
5
are met.

In either case, we have a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit
\langle y_{i}\rangle
satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma
5
such that
\sup\{d({x_{i}},{y_{i}})\}\leq\gamma/3

By applying Lemma

\rho(g_{N},f)\leq\gamma<\delta
and

By our choice of

\epsilon/2

In other words, the

4.
Shadowing as a structural property in
\mathcal{C}(X)

In this section, we develop results analogous to those of Section
3
in the space of
continuous
dynamical systems. It is worth highlighting that the results of that section depended heavily on freedom with which functions can be modified if continuity is not under consideration. However, modifying continuous functions appropriately is far more delicate. Broadly, if the domain under consideration allows for continuous functions to be perturbed with enough freedom, then we can demonstrate that shadowing is a structural property of the space of continuous dynamical systems.

We begin by defining the notion of perturbability.

Definition 8
.

A metric space
X
is
perturbable
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every continuous function
f:X\to X
, every finite subset
F=\{x_{i}:i\leq N\}\subseteq X
, and every function
g_{0}:F\to X
with
\rho(g_{0},f|_{F})<\delta
, there exists a continuous function
g:X\to X
with
\rho(f,g)<\epsilon
and
g|_{F}=g_{0}
.

It is worth noting that the collection of perturbable spaces is quite large—totally disconnected spaces are perturbable, for example. Spaces with a degree of generalized local convexity are also perturbable.

Definition 9

(
[
10
]
)
.

A
c-structure
on a topological space
X
is a function
F
from the set of nonempty finite subsets of
X
into the collection of nonempty contractible subspaces of
X
such that if
A\subseteq B
, then
F(A)\subseteq F(B)
.

A nonempty subset
E
of
X
is an
F
-set
provided that
F(A)\subseteq E
for all finite nonempty
A\subseteq E
.

A space
X
with c-structure
F
is an
m.c.-space
provided that each point has a neighborhood base consisting of
F
-sets.

In the above definition, the function
F
essentially plays the role in an m.c.-space that the convex hull plays in a locally convex space. Spaces which are m.c.-spaces include manifolds, topological graphs, and many other classes of continua.

The following extension theorem for m.c.-spaces is a generalization of Dugundji’s extension theorem
[
9
]
and will be useful going forward.

Theorem 10

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
A\subseteq X
closed,
Y
a m.c.-space with c-structure
F
and
f:A\to Y
continuous. Then there exists a continuous function
g:X\to Y
which extends
f
such that
g(X)\subseteq E
for any
F
-set
E
in
Y
which contains
f(A)
.

In particular, we apply this theorem to establish the following result.

Proposition 11
.

If a compact metric space
X
is totally disconnected or has a c-structure which makes it an m.c.-space, then
X
is perturbable.

Proof.

Fix
X
,
\epsilon>0
and
F\subseteq X
finite.

First, suppose that
X
has c-structure
F
which makes it an m.c.-space.
Since
X
is compact and metric, we can choose
\delta<\epsilon/2
such that for every point
x\in X

B_{\delta}(x)
is an
F
-set

Now, fix
f:X\to X
continuous and choose
g_{0}:F\to X
a function with
\rho(g_{0},f|F)<\delta
. Since
f
is continuous on the compact metric space
X
, choose
\eta>0
such that if
d(a,b)<\eta
, then
d(f(a),f(b))<\delta
and such that if

.

Now, for each
p\in F
, let
X_{p}=\{x\in X:d(x,p)\leq\eta/2\}
and let

h_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&x\in\partial X_{p}\\
g_{0}(p)&x=p\end{cases}.

By construction, for each

and hence
d(f(x),f(p))<\delta
. Since
g_{0}(p)
is also within

of

, we see that
h_{p}(C_{p})\subseteq B_{\delta}(f(p))
. Since the latter is convex, and

is a closed subset of
X_{p}
, by Theorem 2 of
[
10
]
, since
B_{\delta}(f(p))
is an

g_{p}:X_{p}\to X

with

.

By choice of
\eta

is a collection of pairwise disjoint closed sets, and thus we can define the desired function

g(x)=\begin{cases}g_{p}(x):x\in X_{p}\\
f(x):x\notin\bigcup X_{p}.\end{cases}

Clearly, for

. To see that

, observe that if

f(x)=g(x)
, and if
x\in X_{p}

. But

d(f(x),g(x))<2\delta<\epsilon
.

If

is instead totally disconnected, we proceed as follows. First, choose
\delta<\epsilon
.

Now, fix

and

with
\rho(g_{0},f|F)<\delta

\eta>0
such that if

d(f(a),f(b))<\epsilon-\delta

p\in F
, choose a neighborhood

of

is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets. Now, we define the desired function

g(x)=\begin{cases}g_{0}(x):x\in X_{p}\\
f(x):x\notin\bigcup X_{p}.\end{cases}

By construction,

is continuous, maps

for all

and hence

.
∎

For the purposes of establishing analogs to Theorem
3
and Corollary
4
, a weaker form of perturbability is sufficient.

Definition 12
.

A metric space
X
is
weakly perturbable
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every continuous function

It is immediately clear that every perturbable space is weakly perturbable. It is not difficult to see that the converse statement is false—the product of the interval and the Cantor set is weakly perturbable but not perturbable.

Lemma 13
.

Suppose

is weakly perturbable. Let
\langle f_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{C}(X)^{\omega}
and
\epsilon>0

\delta>0

\langle x_{i}\rangle

\langle g_{i}\rangle
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and choose
\delta>0
to witness the weak perturbability of

\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
\langle f_{i}\rangle

For each

In order to prove an analog for Theorem

Lemma 17
.

Let

By supposition, we can find a

With these tools in hand, we can now prove that, in perturbable spaces, shadowing is a structural property of
\mathcal{C}(X)
. As with the previous lemma, this theorem and its proof are very analogous to Theorem

Theorem 18
.

Let
X
be a perturbable compact metric space and let

. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each

-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an

-orbit.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3
, if
(X,f)
has shadowing, then the conclusion follows immediately.

So, let us now assume that
(X,f)
has the indicated property and fix
\epsilon>0
. Choose
\epsilon/4>\delta>0
to witness that if
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(f,g)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon/4
shadowed by an
f
-orbit. Now, by Lemma
16
, choose
\delta>\gamma>0
such that if
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit for
f

x_{i}=x_{j}
implies
x_{i+1}=x_{j+1}
, then the function

in the conclusion of that lemma satisfies
\rho(g,f)<\delta
.

Now, by uniform continuity of

, fix
\gamma/3>\beta
such that if
d(a,b)<\beta

d(f(a),f(b))<\gamma/3
, and define

S=\{x\in X:B_{\beta}(x)\textrm{ is infinite}\}.

Observe that
X\setminus S
is finite—otherwise, it would have a limit point
p
and a point

with

. But then
B_{\beta}(q)
is infinite, a contradiction. For each

to choose
\gamma_{p}>0
such that either (1)
|\{i\in\omega:p=x_{i}\}|\leq 1
or (2)

is periodic and there exists
M\in\omega
with
x_{M}
in the orbit of

and for any such

,
\sup\{d({x_{M+i}},{f^{i}(x_{M})})\}<\gamma/3
.

Now, fix
\delta_{0}
to be the lesser of
\beta
and
\min\{\gamma_{p}:p\in X\setminus S\}
and let
\langle x_{i}\rangle
be a
\delta_{0}
-pseudo-orbit for

. We will now define a nearby
\gamma
-pseudo-orbit
\langle y_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\@text@daccent{(}f(y_{i}),y_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq d(f(y_{i}),f(x_{i}))+d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})+d(x_{i+1},y_{i+1})

\displaystyle<\gamma/3+\delta_{0}+\beta<\gamma.

\langle y_{i}\rangle
is a

-pseudo-orbit. Furthermore, by construction if

\epsilon/4

By passing to a subsequence if necessary, let

i\in\omega
, we have
d(f^{i}(q),x_{i})=\lim_{N\to\infty}d(f^{i}(q_{N}),x_{i})\leq\epsilon/2<\epsilon
, i.e. the
\delta_{0}
-pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is
\epsilon
-shadowed by the
f
-orbit of
q
. Thus
f
has shadowing as claimed.
∎

Again, by Proposition
11
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 19
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space which is either totally disconnected or has a c-structure which makes it an m.c.-space and let
f\in C(X)
. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each
g
-orbit is
\epsilon
-shadowed by an
f
-orbit.

The notions of perturbability above are properties of the space under consideration, however they can easily be rephrased as properties of individual functions.

Definition 20
.

A function
f:X\to X
on a metric space
X
is
perturbable
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every finite subset
F=\{x_{i}:i\leq N\}\subseteq X
and every function
g_{0}:F\to X
with
\rho(g_{0},f|_{F})<\delta
, there exists a continuous function
g:X\to X
with
\rho(f,g)<\epsilon
and
g|_{F}=g_{0}
.

Replacing the perturbability of
X
with perturbability of
f
in Lemma
16
and Theorem
18
yields the following.

Theorem 21
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
f\in C(X)
be perturbable. The dynamical system
(X,f)
has shadowing if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(g,f)<\delta
, then each

-orbit is
\epsilon

5.
Other Results

There has been significant recent interest in the study of variations of the shadowing property
[
4
,
12
,
17
,
18
]
. Broadly, many of these variations arise by expanding or restricting the class of ‘pseudo-orbits’ that need to be shadowed (or the degree to which they need to be shadowed). By Remark
1
, if
(X,f)
is a dynamical system and
g
is a nearby function, then the orbits of
g
are pseudo-orbits for
f
, and these pseudo-orbits are a proper subset of the full collection of pseudo-orbits. This observation motivates the following variations of the shadowing property.

Definition 22
.

For
f\in X^{X}
and
\delta>0
, a
functionally generated
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
is a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle
such that there exists
g\in X^{X}
with
\rho(f,g)<\delta
and
z\in X
such that
x_{i}=g^{i}(z)
for all
i\in\omega
.

Functionally generated pseudo-orbits are a very natural type of pseudo-orbit to consider. They arise naturally in finite precision computations of orbits, for example.
Notice that the functionally generated pseudo-orbits are a proper subset of the collection of all pseudo-orbits, and thus the following notion of shadowing is,
prima facie
, weaker than the standard shadowing property.

Definition 23
.

A dynamical system
(X,f)
has the
finitely generated pseudo-orbit tracing property (FGPOTP)
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every functionally generated
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
, there exists
z\in X
such that the orbit of
z
under
f

\epsilon
-shadows
\langle x_{i}\rangle
.

Of course, we can restrict the class of pseudo-orbits under consideration further by requiring them to be generated by nearby continuous maps.

Definition 24
.

For
f\in X^{X}
and
\delta>0
, a
continuously generated
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
is a sequence
\langle x_{i}\rangle
such that there exists
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(f,g)<\delta
and
z\in X
such that
x_{i}=g^{i}(z)
for all
i\in\omega
.

Restricting our class of pseudo-orbits to the continuously generated ones gives rise to the following even weaker notion of shadowing.

Definition 25
.

A dynamical system
(X,f)
has the
continuously generated pseudo-orbit tracing property (CGPOTP)
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every functionally generated
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\langle x_{i}\rangle
, there exists
z\in X
such that the orbit of
z
under
f

\epsilon
-shadows
\langle x_{i}\rangle
.

With these notions in mind, Theorems
7
and Theorem
18
immediately give us the following result.

Corollary 26
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
. Then

(1)

f
has shadowing if and only if it has FGPOTP.

(2)

If
f
has FGPOTP, then it has CGPOTP.

(3)

If
X
is perturbable and
f
has CGPTOP, then it has shadowing and hence FGPOTP.

Of course, it would be interesting to know how ‘sharp’ this result is.

Question 27
.

Are there systems which have CGPOTP but not FGPOTP?

Another interesting viewpoint of the results from Section
4
comes about by considering the
set
of orbits generated by a dynamical system. Orbits for
(X,f)
belong to the set
X^{\omega}
, and if we wish to consider shadowing-type properties, the natural metric on
X^{\omega}
to consider is the
uniform metric
, i.e.

\rho(\langle x_{i}\rangle,\langle y_{i}\rangle)=\sup_{i\in\omega}\{d(x_{i},y_{i})\}.

We can then define the function
\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{C}(X)\to X^{\omega}
which maps a function to its set of orbits, i.e.
\mathcal{O}(f)=\{\langle f^{i}(x)\rangle:x\in X\}.

When considering set-valued functions between topological spaces, there are several distinct generalizations of continuity. For the purposes of this paper, we consider the notion of
upper Hausdorff semicontinuity
. It is worth mentioning that for non-compact codomains, upper Hausdorff semicontinuity is generally weaker than upper semicontinuity
[
8
]
.

Definition 28
.

A set-valued function
g:A\to B
between metric spaces
(A,d_{A})
and
(B,d_{B})
is
upper Hausdorff semicontinuous
at
a\in A
provided that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
d_{A}(a,x)<\delta
, then
g(x)\subseteq B_{\epsilon}(g(a))
.

Using Theorem
18
, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 29
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space which is perturbable and let
f\in C(X)
. Then
f
has shadowing if and only if
\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{C}(X)\to X^{\omega}
is upper Hausdorff semicontinuous at
f
.

Proof.

First, assume that
f
has shadowing. Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
witness that every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit is
\epsilon/2
-shadowed. Observe that if
\rho(f,g)<\delta
, and
\langle x_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{O}(g)
, then by Remark
1
,
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit for
f
, and as such, there exists
z\in X
such that the
f
-orbit of
z

\epsilon/2
-shadows
\langle x_{i}\rangle
. But then
\rho(\langle x_{i}\rangle,\langle f^{i}(z)\rangle)\leq\epsilon/2<\epsilon
, and since
\langle f^{i}(z)\rangle\in\mathcal{O}(f)
, we have that
\langle x_{i}\rangle\in B_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{O}(f))
. Thus
\mathcal{O}
is upper Hausdorff semicontinuous at
f
.

Now, suppose that
\mathcal{O}
is upper Hausdorff semicontinuous at
f
. Fix
\epsilon>0
and choose
\delta>0
so that if
\rho(f,g)<\delta
, then
\mathcal{O}(g)\subseteq B_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{O}(f))
. Let
g\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
\rho(f,g)<\delta
and let
x\in X
. Then
\langle g^{i}(x)\rangle\in\mathcal{O}(g)\in B_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{O}(f))
. In particular, there exists
z\in X
such that
\rho(\langle g^{i}(x)\rangle,\langle f^{i}(z)\rangle)<\epsilon
. In particular, for each
i\in\omega
,
d(g^{i}(x),f^{i}(z))<\epsilon
, i.e. the
g
-orbit of
x
is
\epsilon
-shadowed by the
f
-orbit of
z
. Thus, by Theorem
18
,
f
has shadowing.
∎

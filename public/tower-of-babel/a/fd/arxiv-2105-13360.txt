Experimental constraints on the neutrino and gauge parameters of the super-weak U(1) extension of the standard model
Timo J. Kärkkäinen, Zoltán Trócsányi

Abstract
The super-weak force is a minimal, anomaly-free U(1) extension of the standard model (SM), designed to explain the origin of (i) neutrino masses and mixing matrix elements, (ii) dark matter, (iii) cosmic inflation, (iv) stabilization of the electroweak vacuum and (v) leptogenesis. We discuss the neutrino sector of this model in detail and study the allowed parameter space of the neutrino Yukawa matrices and mixing matrix elements. The model generates nonstandard neutrino interactions, whose allowed experimental limits are used to constrain the parameter space of the model. We provide benchmark points in the relevant parameter space that fall within the sensitivity region of the SHiP and MATHUSLA experiments.

In the super-weak extension of the SM

, the

right-handed fermion fields that become massive sterile neutrinos after

II.3
Scalar extension

The scalar sector of the model consists of a complex doublet

\phi=\binom{\phi^{+}}{\phi^{0}}
and a complex singlet
\chi
.
The scalar Lagrangian can be written as

\mathcal{L}_{\text{scalar}}=(D_{\mu}\phi)^{*}(D^{\mu}\phi)+|D_{\mu}\chi|^{2}-\mu_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\phi-\mu_{\chi}^{2}|\chi|^{2}-\lambda_{\phi}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)^{2}-\lambda_{\chi}|\chi|^{4}-\lambda(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)|\chi|^{2},

are real. The portal coupling

\chi

minimal at the scalar field values

\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{0}{v}\,,\quad\chi_{0}=\frac{w}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(II.19)

where

\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\chi}\mu_{\phi}^{2}-\lambda\mu_{\chi}^{2}}{4\lambda_{\phi}\lambda_{\chi}-\lambda^{2}}}\,,\quad\frac{w}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\phi}\mu_{\chi}^{2}-\lambda\mu_{\phi}^{2}}{4\lambda_{\phi}\lambda_{\chi}-\lambda^{2}}}\,.

(II.20)

In this paper we perform our analyses at tree level, hence we can
choose the unitary gauge, in which after spontaneous symmetry
breaking (SSB) the fields
\phi
and
\chi
can be parametrized in
terms of two real scalar fields

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{0}{v+h^{\prime}}\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }\chi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(w+s^{\prime})\,.

(II.21)

The mass matrix for scalars in
(h^{\prime},s^{\prime})

M^{\text{scalar}}_{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3\lambda_{\phi}v^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda w^{2}+\mu_{\phi}^{2}&\lambda vw\\

(II.22)

which has eigenvalues

M_{h_{\pm}}^{2}=\lambda_{\phi}v^{2}+\lambda_{\chi}w^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{\phi}v^{2}-\lambda_{\chi}w^{2})^{2}+(\lambda vw)^{2}}.

(II.23)

We adopt the convention
M_{h_{-}}\leq M_{h_{+}}
. Diagonalizing the mass
matrix with an orthogonal rotation

, we obtain the mass eigenstates

and

\binom{h}{s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{S}&-\sin\theta_{S}\\
\sin\theta_{S}&\cos\theta_{S}\end{array}\right)\binom{h^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}

(II.24)

where the scalar mixing angle is given by

\tan 2\theta_{S}=-\frac{\lambda vw}{\lambda_{\phi}v^{2}-\lambda_{\chi}w^{2}}\equiv-\frac{\lambda\tan\beta}{\lambda_{\phi}-\lambda_{\chi}\tan^{2}\beta}\,,

with
\tan\beta=w/v

that the stability of the vacuum is assured up to the Planck scale
within the super-weak model if the SM Higgs particle is lighter than the
new scalar.

II.4
Neutrino extension

The fermion sector of the SM is extended by three massive right-handed
neutrinos that are charged under U(1)
z

only, i.e. sterile under the SM gauge group. They can have either Dirac
or Majorana nature. Since the Yukawa term that produces the Majorana
mass term for Dirac-type neutrinos is allowed by the gauge symmetries,
we include both Dirac- and Majorana-type Yukawa terms in the
Lagrangian.

We write the leptonic part of the Yukawa Lagrangian as

-\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{(N_{\text{R}})^{c}}\>Y_{N}\chi^{*}\>N_{\text{R}}+\overline{L_{\text{L}}}\>\phi Y_{\ell}\>\ell_{\text{R}}+\overline{L_{\text{L}}}\>\phi^{c}Y_{\nu}\>N_{\text{R}}+\text{h.c.}

(II.26)

where the fermion fields are triplets in flavour space,

N_{\text{R}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}N_{1}\\
N_{2}\\
N_{3}\end{array}\right)\,,\quad L_{\text{L}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}L_{\text{L}\text{e}}\\
L_{\text{L}\mu}\\
L_{\text{L}\tau}\end{array}\right)\,,\quad\ell_{\text{R}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e_{\text{R}}\\
\mu_{\text{R}}\\
\tau_{\text{R}}\end{array}\right)\,.

(II.27)

The superscript
c
denotes charge conjugation,
\varphi^{c}=\text{i}\sigma_{2}\varphi^{*}
for a generic two-component field
\varphi
(left-handed Weyl spinor or
complex scalar doublet).

We can

to work on a basis where the

and
Y_{\ell}
are diagonal

N

\ell

Before diagonalization, i.e. on the flavour basis, the Majorana-type
Yukawa matrix

is orthogonal. In contrast, the charged lepton Yukawa matrix

Y_{\ell}^{\prime}

(II.28)

In this context, we define the corresponding Dirac neutrino Yukawa
matrix to be
Y_{\nu}\equiv Y^{\prime}_{\nu}O_{N}
without losing generality.

After SSB, the Yukawa Lagrangian of the leptons becomes

-\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\ell}=\frac{w+s^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{2}}\overline{(N_{\text{R}})^{c}}\>Y_{N}\>N_{\text{R}}+\frac{v+h^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{\ell_{\text{L}}}\>Y_{\ell}\>\ell_{\text{R}}+\frac{v+h^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{\nu_{\text{L}}}\>Y_{\nu}\>N_{\text{R}}+\text{h.c.}\,.

(II.29)

Neutrino masses and mixing

III.3
Parametrization of the mass matrix

Having established the validity of the mass matrix (
III.9
),
we use the Casas-Ibarra parameterization
Casas and Ibarra 2001
to write the diagonalized light neutrino mass matrix as

m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}}=U_{2}^{T}m_{\nu}U_{2}=-U_{2}^{T}m_{\text{D}}m_{\text{R}}^{-1}m_{\text{D}}^{T}U_{2}=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}U_{2}^{T}Y_{\nu}m_{\text{R}}^{-1}Y_{\nu}^{T}U_{2}.

(III.11)

Introducing the matrix

R=\text{i}\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}m_{\text{R}}^{-1/2}Y_{\nu}^{T}U_{2}(m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}})^{-1/2}\,,

(III.12)

we obtain

R^{T}R=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}(m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}})^{-1/2}\left(U_{2}^{T}Y_{\nu}m_{\text{R}}^{-1/2}m_{\text{R}}^{-1/2}Y_{\nu}^{T}U_{2}\right)(m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}})^{-1/2}\,,

(III.13)

which equals the unit matrix because the expression in the parenthesis
can be simplified using Eq. ((
III.11
)). The relation
R^{T}R=I

is fulfilled by any orthogonal matrix. We can solve Eq. ((
III.12
))
for the neutrino Yukawa matrix, and find

Y_{\nu}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}U_{2}^{*}(m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}})^{1/2}(-\text{i}R^{T})m_{\text{R}}^{1/2}\,.

(III.14)

The inclusion of sterile neutrinos results in non-unitary active-light neutrino
mixing matrix

Blennow and Fernandez-Martinez 2011

(I-\alpha)U_{\rm PMNS}
where the matrix
\alpha

proportional to the active-sterile mixing squared, which is tiny. Hence, we
neglect
\alpha

unitary matrix
U_{\rm PMNS}=U_{\ell\text{L}}^{\dagger}U_{2}
(Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata
matrix). We may choose to set
U_{\ell\text{L}}=I
, leading to
U_{\text{PMNS}}=U_{2}
,
which is possible if we assume that the charged lepton Yukawa matrix
Y_{\ell}^{\prime}

is invertible
3
3

3

measure of the set of given size such matrices is zero.
, so the
right-diagonalizing matrix is
U_{\ell\text{R}}=(Y_{\ell}^{\prime})^{-1}Y_{\ell}

This choice ensures that for the charged leptons the flavour and mass
eigenstates coincide. For the active neutrinos the same choice is not
possible. The
U_{2}
PMNS matrix may also include the CP violating and
the unknown, complex Majorana phases, but we set those to zero in this study, as we do
not expect that such phases will change our conclusions significantly.

Using that
m_{\text{R}}
is real and diagonal, we can write the mixing

U_{\rm as}=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}Y_{\nu}^{*}m_{\text{R}}^{-1}\,.

U_{\rm as}=U_{\text{PMNS}}\sqrt{m_{\nu}^{\text{diag}}}(\text{i}R^{\dagger})m_{\text{R}}^{-1/2}\,.

(III.16)

Nonstandard interactions

As the neutral
Z^{\prime}
boson couples to both active neutrinos and charged
fermions, it will lead to nonstandard neutrino interactions (NSI), see
Fig. (
5
) for a relevant Feynman diagram. Integrating out the

Z^{\prime}
boson, we find the non-renormalizable dimension-6 effective
operator
Grossman 1995

\mathcal{L}_{\text{NSI}}=-2\sqrt{2}G_{\rm F}\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{ff^{\prime},X}(\overline{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma^{\mu}P_{\text{L}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})(\overline{f^{\prime}}\gamma_{\mu}P_{X}f)

(IV.1)

that in general distorts the neutrino oscillation probabilities.
In Eq. ((
IV.1
))
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}=
e,
\mu
,
\tau

denote flavour indices,
f
and
f^{\prime}
are any fermions,
P_{X}
(
X=
L, R)
are chiral projection operators and
G_{\rm F}
is Fermi’s constant.
Summation over flavours, fermions and chiralities is implicitly understood.
If
f=f^{\prime}
, we denote
\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{ff,X}=\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{f,X}
.
The NSI parameters
\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{ff^{\prime},X}
and
\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{f,X}
are dimensionless, and
in general can be complex numbers, but when they emerge from the
super-weak force they are real. Given that
Z^{\prime}
is light, the mass
suppression will not ensure that the NSI is small. Instead, the
suppression is due to the small couplings.

Neutral gauge boson exchange Feynman diagram. The
Z^{\prime}
boson

Man-made neutrinos are utilized in oscillation experiments. Neutrinos
propagate through Earth matter, consisting of electrons, protons and
neutrons (i.e. u and d quarks).
Z^{\prime}
-mediated
\nu\ell\to\nu\ell

and
\nu q\to\nu q
scatterings will produce an extra term in the
neutral current Mikheyev-Smirnov-Wolfenstein potential

Wolfenstein 1978
;
Mikheyev and Smirnov 1985
;
Mikheev and Smirnov 1986
,
which is responsible for the matter effects in neutrino oscillations.
However, the
Z^{\prime}
boson couples to neutrino flavours universally.
If there were no sterile neutrinos, this would not affect oscillation
probabilities, but in super-weak model this is not the case. As the full
light neutrino mixing matrix
(I-\alpha)U_{\text{PMNS}}
is not unitary, the
NSI contribution to the neutrino oscillation transition probabilities
will be nonzero, but suppressed by the small absolute values of the
elements of active-sterile mixing matrix
U_{\rm as}
. An oscillation
experiment probing matter NSI parameters will measure only effective NSI,

\varepsilon^{\rm m,eff}\sim\varepsilon^{\rm m}U_{\rm as}
,
when both NSI and nonunitary contributions are present
Blennow et al. 2017
.
We see from Eq. (
III.15
) that for the keV, MeV and GeV scale neutrinos
that we are
considering, the absolute values of the elements of matrix
U_{\rm as}
are much
smaller than the present and near-future experimental limits on the NSI parameters.
Thus we conclude that the near-future neutrino oscillation experiments will not
be sensitive enough to probe NSI originating from the super-weak model
(see the Appendix for more details).

The
Z^{\prime}
boson retains the lepton universality also for charged leptons,
so it will not contribute to any charged lepton flavour violating
processes. The super-weak force does not affect the charged
currents of the SM, so there will not be any charged current NSI
operators. Also, the super-weak force does not imply the existence of
flavour-changing neutral currents. Nevertheless, the existence of the
Z^{\prime}
boson
will contribute to the effective mass of the neutrinos in matter and to neutrino-electron
and neutrino-quark scattering processes. In fact, in neutrino scattering experiments the
NSI are not suppressed by active-sterile mixing, thus those are more sensitive to the
NSI as compared to oscillation experiments, and provide the primary constraints for the
super-weak model in the neutrino sector. In what follows, we explore those constraints.

IV.1
Nonstandard interactions in medium

The matter NSI is obtained by integrating out the
Z^{\prime}
boson mediator
in the elastic

\mathcal{L}_{\rm NSI}=-\frac{1}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}(eC_{Z^{\prime}\nu_{\ell}\nu_{\ell}}^{\text{L}})(eC^{X}_{Z^{\prime}ff})(\overline{\nu_{\ell}}\gamma^{\mu}P_{\text{L}}\nu_{\ell})(\overline{f}\gamma_{\mu}P_{X}f)\,.

Matching Eq. ((
IV.2

\varepsilon^{f,X}_{\ell\ell}=\frac{v^{2}}{2M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}(eC_{Z^{\prime}\nu_{\ell}\nu_{\ell}}^{\text{L}})(eC_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{X})\,,\quad\varepsilon^{f}\equiv\varepsilon^{f}_{\ell\ell}=\varepsilon^{f,\text{L}}_{\ell\ell}+\varepsilon^{f,\text{R}}_{\ell\ell}

(IV.3)

where we indicated that in the super-weak model the NSI coupling is the

\varepsilon^{f}_{ee}=\varepsilon^{f}_{\mu\mu}=\varepsilon^{f}_{\tau\tau}

In principle, flavour non-universal couplings are also possible, and would cause

(IV.4)

where
N_{\rm n}

N_{\text{e}}

\theta_{Z}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\rm m}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{v^{2}}{8M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\frac{N_{\rm n}}{N_{\text{e}}}\left[g_{\text{L}}\sin\theta_{Z}\frac{2g_{y}^{\prime}-g_{z}^{\prime}}{\cos\theta_{W}}-\frac{g_{\text{L}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{Z}}{\cos^{2}\theta_{W}}-g_{y}^{\prime 2}+g_{y}^{\prime}g_{z}^{\prime}\right]

Conclusions

In this paper we studied the experimental feasibility of the neutrino sector
of the super-weak U(1) extension of the standard model. We demonstrated
that there exist benchmark points that are consistent with current observational
constraints from neutrino oscillation and scattering experiments.
At these points the lightest sterile neutrino, with mass of
\text{O}(10)
MeV is
a viable freeze-out dark matter candidate, while with mass of
\text{O}(10)
keV,
is so in the freeze-in scenario and can also provide a possible explanation of
the 3.5 keV X-ray line observed in galaxy spectra. The nearly degenerate
heavy sterile neutrinos with mass of
\text{O}(1)
GeV may be probed
independently by the MATHUSLA
Curtin et al. 2019
and SHiP

Anelli et al. 2015
;
Alekhin et al. 2016
experiments in the future.
We have shown that the one-loop corrections and seesaw expansion terms provide
negligible contributions to neutrino masses with our choices of benchmark
values.

The extra neutral light
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson in the model acts as a mediator
between the neutrinos and other fermions, similar to
Z^{0}
boson in the SM.
This
Z^{\prime}
boson generates flavour-universal nonstandard interactions, and
their strength is consistent with current experimental limits if the gauge
sector couplings
g_{y}^{\prime}
,
g_{z}^{\prime}
and the mixing angle
|\theta_{Z}|
are less than

\text{O}(10^{-4})
. Larger values are possible only within a very narrow range
if
\theta_{Z}>0
, but disfavoured if the model is to explain the origin of dark
matter abundance. The NSI can be made manifest by observing deviations from
the neutrino-nucleus or neutrino-electron scattering cross sections as
predicted in the standard model.

We have deliberately chosen the masses of the sterile neutrinos
N_{2}
and
N_{3}

in our benchmark points such that they can fit in the
\nu
MSM scenario

Asaka and Shaposhnikov 2005
;
Shaposhnikov and Tkachev 2006
;
Canetti et al. 2013
with the exception of CP
violation, which is absent in our benchmarks. In addition, we have demonstrated
the compatibility of the super-weak extension with the near-future experimental
sensitivity and its potential solution to the dark matter problem. Hence our
conclusions motivate a detailed scan of the allowed parameter space, including non-vanishing CP phases, which we leave for later studies.

Appendix A
Flavour-universal NSI and non-unitary mixing

It is possible to perform a phase rotation in effective neutrino oscillation Hamiltonian,
but it turns out that we cannot rotate away the NSI fully even if the NSI matrix

\varepsilon_{\rm true}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{ee}&\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{e\mu}&\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{e\tau}\\
\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{\mu e}&\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{\mu\mu}&\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{\mu\tau}\\
\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{\tau e}&\varepsilon^{m}_{\tau\mu}&\varepsilon^{\rm m}_{\tau\tau}\end{pmatrix}

(A.1)

is isotropic (that is, proportional to unit matrix), since the active neutrino mixing matrix,
which rotates the neutrino flavour basis to neutrino mass basis, is non-unitary. Instead,
the NSI is merely suppressed by unitarity deviations. The transformed NSI matrix is actually

\varepsilon^{\prime}=N^{\dagger}\varepsilon_{\text{true}}N,

(A.2)

where
N
is 3
\times
3 non-unitary matrix in neutrino flavour basis. In the super-weak model,
the new NSI matrix is not isotropic, but the isotropic part can be isolated and removed.
One gets an interesting interplay from the NSI and non-unitarity, which means in practise
that in long baseline neutrino oscillations (LBNO) the effect from these two sources is
indistinguishable. Consider an example: suppose we measure a 2 % distortion in neutrino
oscillation probabilities, and we cannot fit it in standard three-neutrino no-BSM framework.
This distortion may be caused fully by the NSI, fully by non-unitarity or by both in unknown
ratio. In this case the LBNO experiments constrain only the combined effect. Short-baseline
experiments in turn constrain non-unitarity.

Let
N\equiv(I-\alpha)U_{\text{PMNS}}
be the non-unitary neutrino mixing matrix. The deviation matrix

\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{ee}&0&0\\
\alpha_{\mu e}&\alpha_{\mu\mu}&0\\
\alpha_{\tau e}&\alpha_{\tau\mu}&\alpha_{\tau\tau}\end{pmatrix}

(A.3)

has small elements, so we may ignore
\text{O}(\alpha^{2})
part. Thus,

H_{\text{NSI}}=N^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0&0\\
0&\varepsilon&0\\
0&0&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}N\approx\varepsilon I-U_{\text{PMNS}}^{\dagger}(\alpha+\alpha^{\dagger})U_{\text{PMNS}}\varepsilon\,.

(A.4)

The first term is an irrelevant isotropic phase term in neutrino oscillations.
The second term is neither isotropic nor diagonal. It is the leading order part of the NSI,
which cannot be cancelled by phase-rotation, suppressing the NSI by about

|\alpha|\sim|U_{\rm as}|\lesssim 10^{-3}
. Thus one must consider two different
sets of bounds for oscillation and scattering experiments: the suppressed bounds for neutrino
oscillations and unsuppressed ones for scattering.

Appendix B
Light NSI mediator

Upon deriving the NSI operator, we integrate out the mediator by assuming that the propagator
may be approximated by negative inverse mass squared:

\frac{1}{p^{2}-M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\approx\frac{-1}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}.

This is valid only if the momentum transfer in the scattering is small compared to the
mass of the mediator,
M_{Z^{\prime}}
. In neutrino propagation in matter only forward scattering
interactions are relevant. In those cases the momentum transfer via mediator is zero.
With nonzero momentum transfer the outgoing neutrino will be deflected away from the
source-to-detector trajectory even if the momentum transfer is tiny. It is therefore
possible to consider NSI with light mediators. However for scattering experiments one
needs to have mediator mass at least about 10 MeV in order to have a meaningful
approximation
Esteban et al. 2018
;
Farzan and Shoemaker 2016
.

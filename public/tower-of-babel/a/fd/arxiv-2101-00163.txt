Commutatively Deformed General Relativity: Foundations, Cosmology, and Experimental Tests
P. G. N. de Vegvar

Abstract




An integral kernel representation for the commutative
\star
-product on curved classical spacetime is introduced.
Its convergence conditions and relationship to a Drin’feld differential twist are established. A

\star
-Einstein field equation can be obtained, provided the matter-based twist’s vector generators are fixed to self-consistent values during the variation in order to
maintain
\star
-associativity. Variations not of this type are non-viable as classical field theories.
\star
-Gauge
theory on such a spacetime can be developed using
\star
-Ehresmann connections.
While the theory preserves Lorentz invariance and background independence, the standard ADM
3+1
decomposition of 4-diffs
in general relativity breaks down, leading
to different
\star
-constraints. No photon or graviton ghosts are found on
\star
-Minkowski spacetime.

\star
-Friedmann equations are derived and solved for
\star
-FLRW cosmologies. Big Bang Nucleosynthesis restricts expressions for the twist generators.
Allowed generators can be constructed which account for dark matter as arising from a twist producing non-standard model matter field.
The theory also provides a robust qualitative explanation for the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the observable Universe.
Particle exchange quantum statistics encounters
thresholded
modifications due to violations
of the cluster decomposition principle on the nonlocality length scale
\sim 10^{3-5}\,L_{P}
. Precision Hughes-Drever measurements of spacetime anisotropy
appear as the most promising experimental route to test deformed general relativity.

Some basic aspects of
\star

\star

The first noteworthy point is if

\star

\star

\star

\star

(5)

\displaystyle\langle V,\mathrm{d}f\rangle_{\star}

\displaystyle=\langle\omega,V\rangle_{\star}=V^{\mu}\star\omega_{\mu}

(6)

\displaystyle\neq V^{\mu}\cdot\omega_{\mu}=\langle V,\mathrm{d}f\rangle_{\cdot}=V^{\mu}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}f)\doteq V[f]\doteq\mathcal{L}_{V}\,f,

\star

\star

3.
The integral kernel twist

An integral form of the twist on
flat
(Euclidean or Minkowskian) spacetimes was first introduced by Galluccio and coworkers in 2009
[
8
]
[
9
]
.
We briefly recapitulate that Fourier-based approach as it motivates
the integral twist on curved manifolds, even though because of its restriction to flat manifolds it is inapplicable to gravitational physics.
Start from the familiar Fourier transform of a smooth function
f(x)
on a
d
-dimensional flat manifold
\mathcal{M}
,

(22)

\tilde{f}(q)\doteq(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{M}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\exp[-iq\cdot x]\,f(x).

Then the Galluccio
\star
-product is defined as

(23)

(f\star_{\mathscr{G}}g)\,(x)\doteq(2\pi)^{-d}\int\mathrm{d}^{d}p\,\mathrm{d}^{d}q\,\tilde{f}(q)\,\tilde{g}(p-q)\exp\left[ipx+\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)\right],

where the desired properties of the
\star
-product constrain
\tilde{\alpha}
as:

(1) Associativity:
\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)+\tilde{\alpha}(q,r)=\tilde{\alpha}(p,r)+\tilde{\alpha}(p-r,q-r)

(2) Commutativity:
\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)=\tilde{\alpha}(p,p-q)

(3) Unitality:
\tilde{\alpha}(p,p)=0=\tilde{\alpha}(p,0)
, and

(4) Reality:
\overline{\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)}=\tilde{\alpha}(-p,q-p).

Setting
\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)=\beta(q)-\beta(p)+\beta(p-q)
assures both associativity and commutativity. The function
\beta(q)
is called a 2-cocycle.
Reality then constrains
\overline{\beta(q)}=\beta(-q),
so the real (imaginary) part of
\beta(q)
is
q
-even (odd).
Finally, unitality is simply
\beta(0)=0.
To be equivalent to a DDT means
\beta
and
\tilde{\alpha}
are analytic functions of their arguments.
The standard product is just
\beta(q)=0
for all
q.
The next order naïve guess
\beta(q)=(k\cdot q)^{2}
for some constant
d
-vector
k
is a DDT, but it violates strict convergence conditions, so that the Fourier transforms
\tilde{f}(q)
and
\tilde{g}(q)
must have
Gaussian roll-offs at large
\|q\|.
This occurs because for this choice of
\beta,

\alpha(q_{1},q_{2})\doteq\tilde{\alpha}(q_{1}+q_{2},q_{1})=(-2)(k\cdot q_{1})(k\cdot q_{2})

does not have uniform sign on
(q_{1},q_{2})
-space.
To avoid having to place restrictions on the spectra of
f,g
, it would be desirable to have
\alpha(q_{1},q_{2})
be bounded above by some real constant. This will be examined further below.

The Galluccio
\star
-product’s use of Fourier methods is by no means accidental, it encodes the translational invariance of the flat geometry it inhabits. Indeed, the Fourier kernel
\exp[-iq\cdot x]

is the eigenfunction of the translation operator. The intuition behind the Galluccio twist is that
\exp\tilde{\alpha}(p,q)
filters momenta in the standard convolution of Fourier transforms
corresponding to the Fourier transform of the dot-product. Harmonic analysis on a group requires that group to be locally compact and Abelian, here that would be the group of translations on flat
d
-space(time).
A general curved manifold, however, does not possess such a high degree of symmetry, initially suggesting that any version of the Galluccio twist would be doomed in a curved habitat.
An integral kernel twist (IKT) for
gravitational physics calls for some other insight. And here it is: Notice that classical
\star
-manifolds come equipped with a set of
N_{X}\leq 2
smooth vector field twist generators
X_{A}
.
As a result one can construct a set of one-parameter
locally compact Abelian groups from those vector fields by their integral curves. More specifically, choose any point
x
on
\mathcal{M}
(now generally curved)
with coordinates
x^{\mu}
, let
Y=X_{A}
for some
A\in\{1,\ldots,N_{X}\},
and define for

\displaystyle(f\star g)(x)|_{\mathrm{NR}}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\;\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}=0}^{p}

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\,k^{2p}\,\Big[\big(\sum_{A}Y_{A}^{2}\big)^{p_{1}}\otimes\big(\sum_{B}Y_{B}^{2}\big)^{p_{2}}\Big]\Big[\sum_{C}Y_{C}\otimes Y_{C}\Big]^{p_{3}}\triangleright

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)|_{x}\doteq\mathcal{F}\triangleright(f\otimes g)|_{x}\;.

Here

as a Lie operator, and

and in a recursive twist, the

Eqn. (
3

Within the NR approximation, this is a Drin’feld twist by definition as a formal power series
[
7
]
using the

standard
Lie derivative since it is invertible:

\mathcal{F}=\hat{1}\otimes\hat{1}+\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
, associative and unital. One also can see that a recursive twist is no longer formally a DDT, and is defined only as an IKT,
because it is equivalent to utilizing a non-standard Lie derivative that recursively employs the
\star
-product. Such a twist generally loses the Hopf algebra structure that the DDT possesses.

To illustrate the utility of the IKT, one can introduce
\star

The action of the

\displaystyle\times\exp\big[-iq_{1}t_{1}-iq_{2}{t}_{2}+\alpha(q_{1},q_{2})\big]\,\delta_{z}\big[\sigma(x,t_{1})\big]\,f\big[\sigma(x,t_{2})\big],

where

is the standard

\delta

and

Choosing

discussed earlier, reduces (

\delta_{z}^{\star}\,\triangleright f\simeq\Big(\frac{\pi}{2k^{2}}\Big)^{N_{X}/2}\int_{\mathrm{TSM}(z)}\mathrm{d}^{N_{X}}\tau\,\exp[-\tau^{2}/8k^{2}]\,f\big[\sigma(z,\tau)\big],

(z),
centered at

Physically this means one can only probe fields (

from

Still using

As a reminder, if there are two generators,

Now consider varying fundamental fields about some configuration

Suppose we start the variation from some configuration

will be constrained by this condition.
By contrast, the fully varied twist (
\star

\delta\Psi

(66)

\exp\big[-\frac{\lambda}{2}\theta^{AB}\,\mathcal{L}_{Y_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{Y_{B}}\big]\triangleright(f\otimes g)(x)

and if
N_{X}=2
then the other

is
Y_{1}
’s Abelian twin.
Taking

The constraint

within the

operator itself

So far

is any given classical field configuration.

that

The simple variations possess the additional virtue that if one has a Nice Basis

\star

We now focus attention on the torsion-free
\star
-Einstein-Hilbert action in metric variables

whose Lagrangian density is proportional to

Here

|g|\doteq|\det_{\star}g_{\mu\nu}|
and
(f^{\star 1/2})\star(f^{\star 1/2})(x)\doteq f(x)
; general rational
\star
-powers are defined analagously.
Using the same conventions as Weinberg
[
16
]

\star
-Riemann curvature and
\star
-Ricci tensors in a Nice Basis read

(67)

\displaystyle R^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu\kappa}

\displaystyle\doteq e_{\kappa}(\Gamma^{\star\lambda}_{\mu\nu})-e_{\nu}(\Gamma^{\star\lambda}_{\mu\kappa})+\Gamma^{\star\eta}_{\mu\nu}\star\Gamma^{\star\lambda}_{\kappa\eta}-\Gamma^{\star\eta}_{\mu\kappa}\star\Gamma^{\star\lambda}_{\nu\eta}\quad\text{and}

(68)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\kappa}

\displaystyle\doteq g^{\lambda\nu}\star R_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}\;,

and similarly for the
\star
-curvature scalar

We now sketch how this leads to the
\star
-Einstein field equation using a Nice Basis in combination with simple dynamics.

First a few
\star
-calculational tools need to be introduced. Start with

(69)

\exp_{\star}(f)|_{x}\doteq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\,(f)^{\star n}|_{x}

and its inverse
\ln_{\star}F=f

It is easy to verify that

(70)

\displaystyle\exp_{\star}(f\star g)

\displaystyle=\exp_{\star}(f)+\exp_{\star}(g)\;\text{and}

(71)

\displaystyle\ln_{\star}(F\star G)

\displaystyle=\ln_{\star}(F)+\ln_{\star}(G)\;,

so

(72)

\ln_{\star}(F^{*-1})=(-)\ln_{\star}(F).

If

(73)

\displaystyle\delta[\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,M(x)]

\displaystyle=\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,(M+\delta M)-\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,(M)

(74)

\displaystyle=\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,\big[(M+\delta M)\star M^{\star-1}\big]=\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,\big[1_{4}+\delta M\star M^{\star-1}\big].

As

Set
\delta M=(\partial_{\lambda}M)\cdot\delta x^{\lambda}

\delta x^{\lambda}\to 0
is independent of position, so
\delta M=(\partial_{\lambda}M)\star\delta x^{\lambda}
as well.
Then

(75)

\displaystyle(\delta x^{\lambda})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\star}}\partial_{\lambda}\big[\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,M(x)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\big[M^{\star-1}\star(\delta x^{\lambda}\star\partial_{\lambda}M)\big]

(76)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\big[M^{\star-1}\star(\partial_{\lambda}M)\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\star}}(\delta x^{\lambda})\;.

Here
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\star}}
indicates either choice of product is valid.
Thus we have the useful result

(77)

\partial_{\lambda}\big[\ln_{\star}\mathrm{det}_{\star}\,M(x)\big]=\mathrm{Tr}\big[M^{\star-1}\star\partial_{\lambda}M(x)\big]\;.

Applying this with matrix

set to the
\star
-metric tensor, one obtains

(78)

\Gamma^{\star\mu}_{\mu\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\rho}\star(\partial_{\lambda}g_{\rho\mu})=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\lambda}(\ln_{\star}|g|)=|g|^{\star-1/2}\star\partial_{\lambda}(|g|^{\star 1/2}),

where the last equality has used

(79)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln_{\star}(u)}{\mathrm{d}u}=u^{\star-1}\quad\text{for}\;u>0,

and the
\star
-Chain Rule with
\ln_{\star}\,(f^{\star\alpha})=\alpha\ln_{\star}f
for real
\alpha.

Then for
\star
-vector field

In a Nice Basis
e_{\hat{\mu}}
where the Lie-derivative is

Proceeding further along standard lines, one defines the

For simple dynamics the stationarity of the action under

(83)

\displaystyle\delta R_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big\{g^{\lambda\rho}\star\big[(\delta g_{\rho\mu})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\lambda}+(\delta g_{\rho\lambda})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\mu}-(\delta g_{\mu\lambda})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\rho}\big]\Big\}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\nu}

(84)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big\{g^{\lambda\rho}\star\big[(\delta g_{\rho\mu})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\nu}+(\delta g_{\rho\nu})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\mu}-(\delta g_{\mu\nu})_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\rho}\big]\Big\}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}\lambda}\,.

5.
\star
-gauge theory and friends

With a little work matter gauge theories can be exported from undeformed manifolds to commutative
\star

Consider the
\star

(92)

\star

Conclusions and perspective

So where are we? What has been learned about DGR, what remains obscure? DGR has been outfitted with a curved spacetime compatible integral kernel, whose convergence and equivalence to
an extended DDT have been clearly delineated. Practical working details of

It further predicts thresholded violations of the Spin-Statistics Theorem and Pauli Exclusion Principle that lead to an unexpected

However for each of these “successes” there are corresponding unresolved issues, no-shows, or loud calls of “out of bounds.”
When applied to cosmology

There is no

It is able to enter into the (Birrell-Davies) non-adiabatic regime, where the particle concept (ladder operator algebra) and micro-causality are not valid,

A detailed understanding of the predicted non-locality dominated early cosmological acausal epoch (ACE) has posted “No Trespassing!” writ large for classical DGR.
Finally, aside from the potentially hopeful exceptions of Hughes-Drever and CPT-violation measurements,
the possible experimental tests of DGR examined so far lie many orders of magnitude beyond present technology.

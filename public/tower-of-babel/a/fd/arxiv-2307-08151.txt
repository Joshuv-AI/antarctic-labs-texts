Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and parallel translations
Akihiro Higashitani, Satoshi Murai, Masahiko Yoshinaga

Abstract
. Given a rational polytope P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , the numerical function counting lattice points in the integral dilations of P P is known to become a quasi-polynomial, called the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial ehr P \operatorname{ehr}_{P} of P P . In this paper we study the following problem: Given a rational d d -polytope P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , is there a nice way to know Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of translated polytopes P + 𝒗 P+\bm{v} for all 𝒗 ∈ ℚ d \bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d} ? We provide a way to compute such Ehrhart quasi-polynomials using a certain toric arrangement and lattice point counting functions of translated cones of P P . This method allows us to visualize how constituent polynomials of ehr P + 𝒗 \operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}} change in the torus ℝ d / ℤ d \mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d} . We also prove that information of ehr P + 𝒗 \operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}} for all 𝒗 ∈ ℚ d \bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d} determines the rational d d -polytope P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} up to translations by integer vectors, and characterize all rational d d -polytopes P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} such that ehr P + 𝒗 \operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}} is symmetric for all 𝒗 ∈ ℚ d \bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d} .



1.
Introduction

Enumerations of lattice points in a convex polytope is a classical important theme relating to algebra, combinatorics and geometry of convex polytopes.
A fundamental result on this subject is Ehrhart’s result which says that,
for any rational polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the function
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ni t\mapsto\#(tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
becomes a quasi-polynomial in
t
,
where
tP
is the
t
th dilation of
P

and
\#X
denotes the cardinality of a finite set
X
.
This function is called the
Ehrhart quasi-polynomial
of
P
and we denote it by
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
.
Let
P+\bm{v}=\{\bm{x}+\bm{v}\mid\bm{x}\in P\}
be the convex polytope obtained from a convex polytope
P
by the parallel translation by a vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The purpose of this paper is to develop a way to understand behaviors of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
when
\bm{v}
runs over all vectors in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
where
P
is a fixed rational polytope.

One motivation of studying this problem is special behaviors of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}

when we choose
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
somewhat randomly.
Let us give an example to explain this. Let
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the trapezoid whose vertices are
(0,0),(1,0),(2,1)
and
(0,1)
.
The Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of
T+(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100})
becomes the following quasi-polynomial having minimum period 100:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ehr}_{T+(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100})}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{5}{2}t+1&(\ t\equiv 0\ ),\\
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t&(\ t\equiv 25,50,75\ ),\\
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t&\left({\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{ll}t\equiv 1,3,6,7,9,12,13,15,18,19,21,23,24,26,30,\\
\ 32,36,38,42,44,48,49,53,55,59,61,65,66,67,\\
\ 69,71,72,73,78,83,84,86,89,90,92,95,96,98\end{array}}\right),\\
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t&\left({\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{ll}t\equiv 2,4,5,8,10,11,14,16,17,20,22,28,29,31,33,34,\\
\ 35,37,40,41,45,47,51,52,54,56,57,58,60,62,64,68,\\
\ 70,74,76,77,79,80,81,82,85,87,88,91,93,94,97,99\end{array}}\right),\end{cases}

where “
t\equiv a
” means “
t\equiv a
(mod
100
)”.
This quasi-polynomial has several special properties.
For example, one can see

(
\alpha
)

It has a fairly large minimum period
100
, but it consists of only 4 polynomials.

(
\beta
)

The polynomials
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}t
appear quite often comparing other two polynomials.

(
\gamma
)

The polynomial
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t
appears when
t\equiv 1,3,6,7,\dots
, while the polynomial
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t=\frac{3}{2}(-t)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(-t)
appears when
t=\dots,93,94,97,99
. There seem to be a kind of reciprocity about the appearance of these two polynomials.

Our first goal is to explain why these phenomena occur by using a certain generalization of an Ehrhart quasi-polynomial, which was considered by McMullen
[
20
]
and is called a
translated lattice point enumerators
in
[
12
]
.

1.1.
First result

We introduce a few notation to state our results.
A function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a
quasi-polynomial
if there is a natural number
q
and polynomials
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{q-1}
such that

f(t)=f_{i}(t)\mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}\mbox{ with }t\equiv i\mbox{ (mod }q).

A number
q
is called a
period
of
f
and the polynomial
f_{k}
is called the
k
th
constituent
of
f
.
For convention, we define the
k
th constituent
f_{k}
of
f
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by setting
f_{k}=f_{k^{\prime}}
with
k^{\prime}\equiv k
(mod
q
).
For example, if
f
has period
3
,
then the
7
th constituent equals the
1
st constituent
f_{1}
and the
(-1)
th constituent equals the
2
nd constituent
f_{2}
.
We note that this definition does not depend on a choice of a period.
We will say that a function
L
from
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(or
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) to
\mathbb{R}
is a quasi-polynomial if there is a quasi-polynomial
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
L(t)=f(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(or
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
),
and in that case we regard
L
as a function from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{R}
by identifying
L
and
f
.

For a convex set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the function
\mathrm{TL}_{X,\bm{v}}:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathrm{TL}_{X,\bm{v}}(t)=\#\big((tX+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)

and call it the
translated lattice points enumerator
of
X
with respect to
\bm{v}
.
When
X
is a convex polytope
P
,
we actually consider that
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a function from
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
to
\mathbb{R}
by considering that
tP=\{\bm{0}\}
when
t=0
.
Clearly
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{0}}
is nothing but the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of
P
.
Generalizing Ehrhart’s results,
McMullen
[
20
, §4]
proved that,
if
P
is a rational polytope such that
qP
is integral then
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a quasi-polynomial with period
q
,
and showed that there is a reciprocity between
\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{v}}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}
, where
\mathrm{int}(P)
is the interior of
P
.
As we will see soon in Section 2, for a rational polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
it follows from the above result of McMullen that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{the $k$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}$}=\mbox{the $k$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}$}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This equation (
1.1
) was used in
[
12
]
when
P
is a lattice polytope, and is quite useful to study Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of translated polytopes.
Indeed, the equation says that knowing
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is essentially equivalent to knowing
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.
Our first goal is to explain that the latter information can be described as a finite information although
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
could have arbitrary large minimum period.

To do this, we first discuss when

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
equal for different
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
using toric arrangements.
For
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
let
H_{\bm{a},b}
be the hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by the equation
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=b
.
Let
P
be a rational convex
d
-polytope having
m
facets
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
such that each
F_{k}
lies in the hyperplane
H_{\bm{a}_{k},b_{k}}
with
\bm{a}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
b_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathrm{gcd}(\bm{a}_{k},b_{k})=1
.
We consider the arrangement of hyperplanes

\mathcal{A}_{P}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\{H_{\bm{a}_{i},k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

and let
\Delta_{P}
be the open polyhedral decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
determined by
\mathcal{A}_{P}
.
Both
\mathcal{A}_{P}
and
\Delta_{P}
are invariant under translations by integer vectors,
so by the natural projection
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}

they induce an arrangement of finite hyperplanes on the torus
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and a finite open cell decomposition
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Let
[\bm{x}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denote the natural projection of
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above,
for
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
if
[\bm{u}]
and
[\bm{v}]
belong to the same open cell of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
then

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)\ \ \mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

For an open cell
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
define a quasi-polynomial
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
by

\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}\ \ \ \mbox{ with }[\bm{v}]\in C,

which is well-defined by Theorem
1.1
.
Then (
1.1
) implies that the
k
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is
the polynomial which appears as the
k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
with
[k\bm{v}]\in C
.
This provides us a way to compute
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
from translated lattice points enumerators
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
.

Let us compute
\operatorname{ehr}_{T+(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100})}(t)
using this idea,
where
T
is the trapezoid whose vertices are
(0,0),(1,0),(2,1)
and
(0,1)
.

Figure 1
.
Trapezoid
T
, arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{T}
and the cell complex
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Figure
1
shows the arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{T}
and the cell complex
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
The complex
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
has two
2
-dimensional cells
F_{1},F_{2}
,
three
1
-dimensional cells
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
and one
0
-dimensional cell
V_{1}
shown in Figure

.
Since
T
is a lattice polygon, each
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
is a polynomial by McMullen’s result, and here is a list of
\mathrm{TL}_{T,C}(t)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{TL}_{T,F_{1}}(t)=\frac{3}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t,\\
\mathrm{TL}_{T,F_{2}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{T,E_{1}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{T,E_{2}}(t)=\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t,\\
\mathrm{TL}_{T,E_{3}}(t)=\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t,\\
\mathrm{TL}_{T,V_{1}}(t)=\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t+1.\end{array}

Also, for
k=0,1,2,\dots,99
, a computer calculation says

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left[k\left(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100}\right)\right]\in\begin{cases}V_{1}&(\ k\equiv 0\ ),\\
E_{3}&(\ k\equiv 25,50,75\ ),\\
E_{2}&(\ k\equiv 20,40,60,80\ ),\\
F_{1}&\left({\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{ll}k\equiv 1,3,6,7,9,12,13,15,18,19,21,23,24,26,30,\\
\ 32,36,38,42,44,48,49,53,55,59,61,65,66,67,\\
\ 69,71,72,73,78,83,84,86,89,90,92,95,96,98\end{array}}\right),\\
F_{2}&\left({\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{ll}k\equiv 2,4,5,8,10,11,14,16,17,22,28,29,31,33,34,\\
\ 35,37,41,45,47,51,52,54,56,57,58,62,64,68,70,\\
\ 74,76,77,79,81,82,85,87,88,91,93,94,97,99\end{array}}\right).\end{cases}

Since (
1.1
) says that the
k
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
equals the
k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}
,
which equals
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
with
[k\bm{v}]\in C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) recover the formula of
\operatorname{ehr}_{T+(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100})}(t)
given at the beginning of this section.

As we will see,
the proof of Theorem
1.1
is somewhat straightforward,
and the way of computing
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}(t)
from
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)
explained above may be considered as a kind of an observation rather than a new result.
But we think that this is a useful observation.
For example,
this way allows us to visualize how the constituents of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
change by plotting the points
[k\bm{v}]
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Also, we can see why properties (
\alpha
), (
\beta
) and (
\gamma
) occur from this observation.
For the property
(\alpha)
,
we only see 4 polynomials in
\operatorname{ehr}_{T+(\frac{17}{100},\frac{52}{100})}
because we have only 4 types of translated lattice point enumerators.
More generally, it can be shown that, if we fix a rational polytope
P
, then we can only have a finite number of polynomials as constituents of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
(Theorem
3.10
).
For the property (
\beta
),
the polynomials
\frac{3}{2}t^{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}t
appear many times simply because they are polynomials assigned to maximal dimensional cells of
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}

(indeed, if we choose
\bm{v}
randomly, then
[k\bm{v}]
is likely to belong to a maximal dimensional cell).
Finally, we will see in Section 5 that the property (
\gamma
) can be figured out from the reciprocity of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}

(see Corollary
5.4
).

1.2.
Second Result

Recently real-valued extension of Ehrhart functions,
namely, the function
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
given by
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)=\#(tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
catch interests
[
4
,
5
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
One surprising result on this topic is the following result of Royer
[
16
,
17
]
proving that
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}^{\mathbb{R}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
determines the polytope
P
.

Theorem 1.2

(Royer)
.

Let
P
and
Q
be rational polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)=\operatorname{ehr}_{Q+\bm{v}}^{\mathbb{R}}(t)

for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
then
P=Q
.

Our second result is somewhat analogous to this result of Royer.
We prove that
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
determines the polytope
P
up to translations by integer vectors.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P
and
Q
be rational
d
-polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}(t)=\operatorname{ehr}_{Q+\bm{v}}(t)

for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
then
P=Q+\bm{u}
for some
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

After we submitted the paper, we realized that Theorem
1.3
is not new and appears in the thesis of Alhajjar in a bit stronger form
[
2
, Theorem 3.9]
. But we keep the proof of Theorem
1.3
since we feel that our proof is more precise and some argument in the proof is also used to prove the third result.

1.3.
Third result

The original motivation of this study actually comes from an attempt to generalize results of de Vries and Yoshinaga in
[
12
]
,
who found a connection between symmetries on constituents of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
and geometric symmetries of
P
.
Indeed, the following result is one of the main results in
[
12
]
.
We say that a quasi-polynomial
f
is
symmetric
if the
k
th constituent of
f
equals the
(-k)
th constituent of
f
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Also, a convex polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
centrally symmetric
if
P=-P+\bm{x}
for some
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.4

(de Vries–Yoshinaga)
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a lattice
d
-polytope.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.

(2)

P
is centrally symmetric.

As posed in
[
12
, Problem 6.7]
,
it is natural to ask if there is a generalization of this result for rational polytopes.
Theorem
1.4
actually proves that,
if a rational polytope
P
satisfies the property (1) of the above theorem, then
P
must be centrally symmetric (see Corollary
7.4
).
We generalize Theorem
1.4
in the following form.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.

(2)

P
is centrally symmetric and
2(P-\bm{c})
is integral, where
\bm{c}
is the center of symmetry of
P
.

Organization of the paper

This paper is organized as follows:
We first quickly review basic known properties of Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and translated lattice points enumerators in Section 2.
In Section 3,
we study translated lattice points enumerators using arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{P}
and prove Theorem

.
Then, after seeing two examples in Section 4,
we discuss a reciprocity of translated lattice points enumerators on maximal cells of

in Section 5.
In Section 6, we prove that translated lattice point enumerators determine the polytope
P
up to translations by integer vectors.
In Section 7, we study translated lattice points enumerators of polytopes with some symmetry,
in particular, prove Theorem
1.5
.
In Section 8,
we discuss a connection to commutative algebra,
more precisely, we discuss a connection between translated lattice points enumerators and conic divisorial ideals in Ehrhart rings.
We list a few problems which we cannot solve in the last section 9.

2.
Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and translated lattice point enumerators

In this section,
we recall basic results on Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and explain a connection between Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of translated polytopes and translated lattice point enumerators.

2.1.
Ehrhart quasi-polynomial

We quickly recall Ehrhart’s theorems.
We refer the readers to
[
6
,
13
,
23
]
for basics on convex polytopes.
A
convex polytope

P
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a convex hull of finitely many points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The
dimension
of a polytope
P
is the dimension of its affine hull.
A
k
-dimensional convex polytope will be simply called a
k
-polytope
in this paper.
A convex polytope
P
is said to be
integral
(resp.
rational
) if all the vertices of
P
are lattice points (resp. rational points).
The
denominator
of a rational polytope
P
is the smallest integer
k>0
such that
kP
is integral.
The following result is a fundamental result in Ehrhart theory. See
[
6
, Theorems 3.23 and 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Ehrhart)
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational polytope and
q
the denominator of
P
.
Then the function
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\operatorname{ehr}_{P}(t)=\#(tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})

is a quasi-polynomial with period
q
.

As we noted in the Introduction,
we regard
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
as a function from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{R}
by identifying it with the corresponding quasi-polynomial
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
that coincides with
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Thus, if
q
is a period of
f
, then for a positive integer
t>0
we set
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}(-t)=f_{k}(-t)
, where
f_{k}
is the
k
th constituent of
f
with
-t\equiv k
(mod
q
).
The quasi-polynomial
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
is called the
Ehrhart quasi-polynomial
of
P
.

The following reciprocity result is another important result in Ehrhart theory.

Theorem 2.2

(Ehrhart reciprocity)
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
Then

\#\big(\mathrm{int}(tP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=(-1)^{d}\operatorname{ehr}_{P}(-t)\ \ \mbox{ for }\ t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

2.2.
Translated lattice points enumerator

Recall that,
for a convex set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the
translated lattice points enumerator
of
X
w.r.t.
\bm{v}
is the function

\mathrm{TL}_{X,\bm{v}}
defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{TL}_{X,\bm{v}}(t)=\#\big((tX+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)\ \ \mbox{ for }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

When
X
is a polytope
P
,
we actually consider that
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a function from
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
to
\mathbb{R}
by
setting

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(0)=\#(\{\bm{v}\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
Thus
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(0)=1
if
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(0)=0
if
\bm{v}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
In this way, we may consider that
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a counting function of lattice points in the translated cone.
Indeed,
for a convex polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if we write
\mathcal{C}_{P}
for the cone generated by
\{(\bm{x},1)\mid\bm{x}\in P\}
,
Then we have

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)=\#\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap H_{x_{d+1}=t}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\ \ \mbox{ for }t\geq 0,

where
H_{x_{d+1}=t}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d+1})\mid x_{d+1}=0\}
.

McMullen
[
20
, §4]
proved the following generalization of Ehrhart’s results.
(We will give an algebraic proof of this theorem later in section 8.)

Theorem 2.3

(McMullen)
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope and
q
the denominator of
P
.
Then

(1)

For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the function
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a quasi-polynomial with period
q
.

(2)

For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{v}}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}(-t)\ \ \mbox{ for $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$.}

We remark that
\bm{v}
is not necessarily a rational point in the above theorem.
Also the theorem says that, if
P
is integral, then the function
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a polynomial.

The following connection between Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of translated polytopes and translated lattice points enumerators, which essentially appeared in
[
12
, Corollary 3.4]
, is fundamental in the rest of this paper.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.
For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
one has

\mbox{\rm the $k$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}$}=\mbox{\rm the $k$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}$}.

Proof.

We may assume
k\geq 0
.
Let
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
be positive integers such that
\rho P
is integral and
\rho^{\prime}\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Let
q
be a common multiple of
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
.
Then
q
is a common period of quasi-polynomials
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
.
For every integer
t\geq 0
with
t\equiv k
(mod
q
) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}(t)

\displaystyle=\#\big((tP+t\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\#\big((tP+k\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}(t),

where the second equality follows from
(t-k)\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Since both
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
are quasi-polynomials with a period
q
, the above equation proves the desired property.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Like usual Ehrhart quasi-polynomials,
each constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
is a polynomial of degree
d
whose leading coefficient equals the volume of
P
.
Indeed, if
f_{k}
is the
k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
and
q
is a period of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
, then
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f_{k}(qt+k)}{(qt+k)^{d}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\#((qt+k)P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}{(qt+k)^{d}}
is the volume of
P
.
Since
f_{k}
is a polynomial,
this means that
f_{k}
has degree
d
and the coefficient of
t^{d}
in
f_{k}
equals the volume of
P
.

3.
Translated lattice points enumerators and toric arrangements

In this section,
we study when
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
equals
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}
for different
\bm{v},\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
using toric arrangements.
Recall that the translated lattice points enumerator
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
can be identified with a generating function of a translated cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)

because of the equality

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1})\in(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}z^{a_{d+1}}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\big(\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)\big)z^{t}.

This in particular says that if
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0)
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
have the same lattice points, then we have
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
.
To prove Theorem
1.1
,
we mainly study when
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0)
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
have the same lattice points.

We note that such a study is not very new.
Indeed, lattice points in the translated cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v}
is closely related to conic divisorial ideals of Ehrhart rings studied in
[
8
,
10
]
,
and Bruns
[
8
]
explains for which
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
the lattice points in
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u}
equal those in
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v}
.
We will explain this connection to commutative algebra later in Section 8.

3.1.
Regions associated with hyperplane arrangements

We first introduce some notation on arrangements of hyperplanes.
For
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
for the standard inner product.
Also, for
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
we write

H^{\geq}_{\bm{a},b}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid(\bm{a},\bm{x})\geq b\}\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ H^{>}_{\bm{a},b}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid(\bm{a},\bm{x})>b\}

for closed and open half space defined by the linear inequalities
(\bm{a},\bm{x})\geq b
and
(\bm{a},\bm{x})>b
, respectively,
and write

H_{\bm{a},b}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid(\bm{a},\bm{x})=b\}

for the hyperplane defined by the linear equation
(\bm{a},\bm{x})=b
.
In the case where
\bm{a}
can be chosen from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
b
is from
\mathbb{Z}
, we call the hyperplane
H_{\bm{a},b}

rational
.
Let
N=\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}\}
be a set of elements in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
Define the arrangement of hyperplanes

\mathcal{A}_{N}=\{H_{\bm{a},k}\mid\bm{a}\in N,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

See Figure
2
.
From now on, we fix an order
\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}
of elements of
N
.
We define the map
\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{x})=\big((\bm{a}_{1},\bm{x}),(\bm{a}_{2},\bm{x}),\dots,(\bm{a}_{m},\bm{x})\big).

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
we write
\lfloor x\rfloor=\max\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\ell\leq x\}

and

\lceil x\rceil=\min\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\ell\geq x\}
.
Also, given an integer sequence
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
,
we define

U_{\bm{c}}^{N}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\lceil\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{x})\rceil=\bm{c}\}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid c_{i}-1<(\bm{a}_{i},\bm{x})\leq c_{i}\mbox{ for }i=1,2,\dots,m\}

where
\lceil(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\rceil=(\lceil x_{1}\rceil,\dots,\lceil x_{m}\rceil)
.
We call
U_{\bm{c}}^{N}
an
upper region
of
N
.
Note that
U_{\bm{c}}^{N}
could be empty.
Also we have the partition

\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigsqcup_{\bm{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}U_{\bm{c}}^{N}

where
\bigsqcup
denotes a disjoint union.
We write
\Lambda_{N}
for the set of all upper regions of
N
.
The set
\Lambda_{N}
is stable by translations by integer vectors,
so
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
acts on these sets.
Indeed, since
\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}
are integer vectors,
for any
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

U_{\bm{c}}^{N}+\bm{n}=U_{\bm{c}+\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{n})}^{N}.

We write
\Lambda_{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}

for the quotient of these sets by this
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action defined by translations by integer vectors.
This set can be considered as a partition of the
d
-torus
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
N=\{(1,0),(-1,2)\}
.
Then the set
\Lambda_{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
consists of two elements
with the following representatives:

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=U_{(1,1)}^{N}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid 0<x\leq 1,0<-x+2y\leq 1\},

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=U_{(1,0)}^{N}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid 0<x\leq 1,-1<-x+2y\leq 0\}.

See Figure
2
for the visualization of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and
\Lambda_{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

.
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and

when

.

3.2.
Upper regions and lattice points in translated cones.

We now explain how upper regions relate to lattice points in translated cones.
We first recall two basic facts on lattice points.
The following lemma is an easy consequence of Euclidian algorithm.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
g=\mathrm{gcd}(\bm{a})
.
A linear equation
(\bm{a},\bm{x})=b
has an integral solution if and only if
b\in g\mathbb{Z}
.

We also need the following statement which easily follows from Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational hyperplane and let
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a point such that
H+\bm{v}\neq H
.
There is an
\varepsilon>0
such that
H+s\bm{v}
contains no lattice points for any
0<s\leq\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational hyperplane.
Any
(d-1)
-dimensional convex cone in
H
contains a lattice point.

Proof.

Let
H=H_{\bm{a},b}
for some
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
b=0
.
Since any
d
-dimensional convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
contains a lattice point,
the lemma follows from the fact that
H\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.
∎

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
By the Weyl-Minkowski theorem for convex cones,
the cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}
has the unique presentation

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{P}=H_{\bm{a}_{1},0}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},0}^{\geq}

such that

(1)

each
\bm{a}_{i}
is primitive (that is,
\mathrm{gcd}(\bm{a}_{i})=1
), and

(2)

the presentation is irredundant, that is, each
H_{\bm{a}_{i},0}^{\geq}
cannot be omitted from the presentation.

Note that the second condition says that
\mathcal{C}_{P}\cap H_{\bm{a}_{i},0}
is a facet of
\mathcal{C}_{P}
.
The vectors
\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}
in (
3.2
) are called (inner)
normal vectors
of
\mathcal{C}_{P}

and we write
\widetilde{N}(P)=\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}\}
for the set of all normal vectors of
\mathcal{C}_{P}
.
The next statement was given in
[
8
]

Proposition 3.5

(Bruns)
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex polytope and

\widetilde{N}(P)=\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}\}
.
Let
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.

(2)

\lceil\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{u})\rceil=\lceil\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{v})\rceil
, that is,
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
belong to the same upper region of
\Lambda_{\widetilde{N}(P)}
.

Proof.

Let
\lceil\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{u})\rceil=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})
and let
\lceil\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{v})\rceil=(d_{1},\dots,d_{m})
.
Since

\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u}=H_{\bm{a}_{1},(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{u})}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},(\bm{a}_{m},\bm{u})}^{\geq}

and since Lemma
3.2
says

H_{\bm{a},b}^{\geq}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=H_{\bm{a},\lceil b\rceil}^{\geq}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\ \ \mbox{ for any }\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\ b\in\mathbb{R},

we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(H_{\bm{a}_{1},c_{1}}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},c_{m}}^{\geq}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}

and

(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(H_{\bm{a}_{1},d_{1}}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},d_{m}}^{\geq}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}.

These prove (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

We prove (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
We assume
c_{1}<d_{1}
and prove
(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\neq(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.
In this case
F=(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u})\cap H_{\bm{a}_{1},c_{1}}
contains a
d
-dimensional cone in
H_{\bm{a}_{1},c_{1}}
, so it contains a lattice point by Lemma
3.4
.
On the other hand,
since
c_{1}<d_{1}
we have

\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\cap(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap H_{\bm{a}_{1},c_{1}}=\varnothing
. These prove
(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\neq(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

If
N=\widetilde{N}(P)
for some rational
d
-polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(that is,
N
is the set of normal vectors of the cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
),
then the set
\Lambda_{N}
has a special property that every element of
\Lambda_{N}
has dimension
d+1
.
Indeed, if
R\in\Lambda_{N}
and
\bm{x}\in R

then we have
\bm{x}-\bm{y}\in R
for all
\bm{y}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})
that is sufficiently close to the origin,
which implies that
R
has dimension
d+1
.
As we see in Example
3.9
,
this property does not hold when
N
is the set of normal vectors of a polytope (not the cone over a polytope).

We have studied lattice points in translated cones
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v}
,
but we are actually interested in a special case when
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}^{\prime},0)
since this is the case which is related to translated lattice points enumerators.
Below we describe when
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0)
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
have the same lattice points.
Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
By the fundamental theorem on convex polytopes,
there is the unique presentation

P=H_{\bm{a}_{1},b_{1}}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},b_{m}}^{\geq}

such that

(1)

each
(\bm{a}_{i},b_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
is primitive, and

(2)

the presentation is irredundant.

The vectors
\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}
are called
normal vectors
of
P
.
We define

N(P)=\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}\}.

We note that

\widetilde{N}(P)=\{(\bm{a}_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(\bm{a}_{m},b_{m})\}

since if
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a hyperplane defined by
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=b
then the cone
\mathcal{C}_{H}
is the hyperplane defined by
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=bx_{d+1}
.
We write

\mathcal{A}_{P}=\mathcal{A}_{N(P)}\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \Lambda_{P}=\Lambda_{N(P)}.

The following statement is essentially a consequence of Proposition
3.5
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope and let

\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.

(2)

(\bm{u},0)
and
(\bm{v},0)
belong to the same upper region in
\Lambda_{\widetilde{N}(P)}
.

(3)

\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
belong to the same upper region in
\Lambda_{P}

Proof.

The equivalence between (1) and (2) is Proposition
3.5
.
Let
(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})
be the sequence of normal vectors of
P
and let
((\bm{a}_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(\bm{a}_{m},b_{m}))
be that of
\mathcal{C}_{P}
.
The equivalence between (2) and (3) follows from the fact that
\varphi_{(\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m})}(\bm{x})=\varphi_{((\bm{a}_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(\bm{a}_{m},b_{m}))}(\bm{x},0)
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

We now discuss a consequence of Corollary
3.7
to translated lattice point enumerators
and Ehrhart quasi-polynomials.
Recall that

[\bm{x}]
denotes the image of
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the natural projection
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope
and let
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1)

If
[\bm{u}]
and
[\bm{v}]
belong to the same region in
\Lambda_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(2)

The set
\{\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{w}}\mid\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is a finite set.

Proof.

(1) Corollary
3.7
says that if
[\bm{u}]
and
[\bm{v}]
belong to the same region in
\Lambda_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
then

\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}+(\bm{n},0),

where
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the vector such that
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}+\bm{n}
belong to the same region of
\Lambda_{P}
.
Then (
3.1
) implies
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{u}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(2)
Since there are only finitely many hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
that intersect
[0,1)^{d}
,
the set
\{R\in\Lambda_{P}\mid R\cap[0,1)^{d}\neq\varnothing\}
is finite,
which implies that

\Lambda_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a finite set.
This fact and (1) prove the desired statement.
∎

,
the second statement of the above theorem gives the following finiteness result for constituents of Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of translated polytopes.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a rational
d
-polytope, then

\textstyle\#\big(\bigcup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}}\mathrm{Const}\big(\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}\big)\big)<\infty.

3.3.
Polyhedral decompositions associated with hyperplane arrangements

Theorem
3.8
is slightly different to Theorem
1.1
in the Introduction (indeed the cell complex
\Lambda_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
has
4
cells while
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
has 6 cells),
but it can be considered as a refined version of Theorem
1.1
.
We explain this in the rest of this section.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope and
N(P)=\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{m}\}
.
The arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{P}
gives a natural polyhedral decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose maximal open cells are connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{P}}H)
.
We write
\Delta_{P}
for this polyhedral complex.
Note that this
\Delta_{P}
is the same as the one defined in the Introduction.
Since any half line
\bm{v}+\{s\bm{w}\mid s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}
,
where
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{0}\neq\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
must hit one of
H_{\bm{a}_{i},k}\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
, each connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{P}}H)
is a bounded set, so
\Delta_{P}
is actually a polytopal complex.
By the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{P}
,
each open cell
A
of
\Delta_{P}
can be written in the form

A=A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}

such that each
A_{i}
is either
H_{\bm{a}_{i},k}
or
\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid k<(\bm{a}_{i},\bm{x})<k+1\}
.
This means that
each upper region in
\Lambda_{P}
can be written as a disjoint union of open cells in
\Delta_{P}
, in particular,
each element in
\Lambda_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be written as a disjoint union of elements in
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
This proves Theorem
1.1
.

Example 3.11
.

Consider the trapezoid
T
from the Introduction.
As one can see from Figures
1
and
3
,

\Lambda_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
consists of 4 elements
V,E,R_{1},R_{2}
and
\Delta_{T}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
consists of
6
elements
V_{1},E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},F_{1},F_{2}
.
We have

V=V_{1},\ E=E_{3},\ R_{1}=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup F_{2},\ R_{2}=F_{1}.

While
\Lambda_{P}
and
\Delta_{P}
are different in general,
there is a nice case that we have
\Lambda_{P}=\Delta_{P}
.
If the set of normal vectors of
P
is the set of the form
\{\pm\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\pm\bm{a}_{l}\}
then each upper region
R\in\Lambda_{P}
must be a region of the form

R=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\big\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid c_{i}-1<(\bm{a}_{i},\bm{x})\leq c_{i}\ \mbox{ and }c_{i}^{\prime}-1<(-\bm{a}_{i},\bm{x})\leq c_{i}^{\prime}\big\}.

Each non-empty content in the RHS equals either
H_{\bm{a}_{i},c_{i}}
or
\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid c_{i}-1<(\bm{a}_{i},\bm{x})<c_{i}\}

so we have
\Lambda_{P}=\Delta_{P}
in that case.
To summarize, we get the following statement.

Proposition 3.12
.

If
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
d
-polytope with
N(P)=-N(P)
then
\Lambda_{P}=\Delta_{P}
.

A typical example of a polytope
P
satisfying
N(P)=-N(P)
is a centrally symmetric polytope
P
with
P=-P
(or more generally, a polytope
P
with
P=-P+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
).

Remark 3.13
.

Each element of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is an open cell ball, so

\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is indeed a CW complex.
To see that each element of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a ball, it suffices to check that for each
C\in\Delta_{P}
the restriction of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to
C
is injective.
This injectivity follows from Corollary
3.7
since,
if we have
\bm{u}=\bm{v}+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in C
and
\bm{0}\neq\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{u},0)=\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)+(\bm{n},0)
must have different sets of integer points.

5.
Reciprocity in maximal regions

In this section, we explain that the quasi-polynomials
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
for maximal dimensional cells
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
have a reciprocity which comes from the reciprocity in Theorem
2.3
.

5.1.
Reciprocity

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
The reciprocity in Theorem
2.3
says that, for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{v}}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}(-t)\ \ \ \mbox{ for }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Since the affine hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{P}
is centrally symmetric, that is
-\mathcal{A}_{P}=\mathcal{A}_{P}
,
we have
R\in\Delta_{P}
if and only if
-R\in\Delta_{P}
.
For each
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with a representative
R\in\Delta_{P}
,
we write
-C
for the element of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
corresponding to the cell
-R
.
By Theorem
1.1
and (
5.1
)
we have
\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{v}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{u}}(t)
when
[\bm{u}]
and
[\bm{v}]
belong to the same cell in
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Thus, for each
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
we write
\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),C}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),\bm{v}}(t)
with
[\bm{v}]\in C
.
Using this notation, (
5.1
) can be written in the following form.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
For any
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one has

\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),C}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}(-t)\ \ \ \mbox{for }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Note that the above equation says that

\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),C}
is a quasi-polynomial on
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Let

(5.2)

\displaystyle P=H_{\bm{a}_{1},b_{1}}^{\geq}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\bm{a}_{m},b_{m}}^{\geq}.

We write
F_{i}=P\cap H_{\bm{a}_{i},b_{i}}
for the facet of

The next lemma follows from Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4

Lemma 5.3
.

With the same notation as above,
for
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the cone
\mathcal{C}_{F_{i}}+(\bm{v},0)
contains a lattice point if and only if
\bm{v}\in H_{\bm{a}_{i},k}
for some

.

The lemma says that the cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
has no lattice points in its boundary if
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{P}}H
,
equivalently, if
[\bm{v}]
belongs to a
d
-dimensional cell of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Hence we have

Corollary 5.4
.

If
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a rational
d
-polytope
and
C
is a
d
-dimensional cell of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then

\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}(-t)\ \ \mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Proof.

Let
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
[\bm{v}]\in C
.
Then
\bm{v}\not\in H
for any

, which implies that the cone
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
has no lattice points in its boundary.
Thus by Proposition

we have

\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{\mathrm{int}(P),C}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}(-t)\ \ \ \mbox{ for }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Since
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}
are quasi-polynomials on

,
this implies the desired equality.
∎

The above reciprocity has a special meaning for centrally symmetric polytopes.
Looking at the quasi-polynomials
\mathrm{TL}_{Q,F_{i}}
in Example

, one may notice that each constituent is a polynomial in

. In other words, the linear term

vanishes.
We explain that this has a reason.
We first remind the following easy fact.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational polytope with
-P=P+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}(t)
for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows since, for each integer
k\geq 0
, the correspondence
\bm{x}\to-\bm{x}-k\bm{n}

give a bijection between lattice points in
kP+\bm{v}

and those in
-kP-\bm{v}-k\bm{n}=kP-\bm{v}
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope with
-P=P+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and let
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional cell.
Let

be the

th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}

and let

be the
(-k)
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
. Then

f(t)=(-1)^{d}g(-t).

Proof.

Corollary
5.4
and Lemma

say

\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}(-t)=(-1)^{d}\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(-t)\ \ \mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

By considering the
k
th constituent in the above equality, we get the desired assertion.
∎

If a polynomial

of degree

satisfies

,
then it must be a polynomial in
t^{2}
when

is even
and
t
times a polynomial in

when

is odd.
Hence we get the following corollary,
which explains a reason why we get polynomials in

in Example

.

Corollary 5.7
.

With the same notation as in Theorem

,

(1)

the

th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)
is either a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[t^{2}]
or

;

(2)

if

is integral, then the
1

\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}(t)
is either a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[t^{2}]
or
t\mathbb{Q}[t^{2}]
.

Note that when

is integral the quasi-polynomial
\mathrm{TL}_{P,C}
has period 2, so its
1
st constituent equals its
(-1)
th constituent.

6.
Translated lattice points enumerators determine polytopes

It is clear that if
P=Q+\bm{n}
for some integer vector
\bm{n}
,
then
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}=\mathrm{TL}_{Q,\bm{v}}
for all vectors
\bm{v}
.
The goal of this section is to prove the converse of this simple fact,
which is equivalent to Theorem
1.3
in the Introduction by Lemma
2.4
1
1

1

The condition “
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}=\mathrm{TL}_{Q,\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
” is equivalent to the condition “
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}=\mathrm{TL}_{Q,\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
”.
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
P
and
Q
be rational
d
-polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{Q,\bm{v}}(t)
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
then
P=Q+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

To simplify notation, we use the notation

\Gamma_{P}=\big\{\big(\bm{v},\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{QP}\mid\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\big\},

where
\mathcal{QP}
is the set of all quasi-polynomials in
t
.
Thus,
what we want to prove is that
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{Q}
implies
P=Q+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

To prove the theorem,
we first recall Minkowski’s theorem,
which says that normal vectors and volumes of facets determine a polytope.
Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-polytope with irredundant presentation
P=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}H_{\bm{a}_{i},b_{i}}^{\geq}
,
where
\|\bm{a}_{i}\|=1
,
and let
F_{i}=P\cap H_{\bm{a}_{i},b_{i}}

be the facet of
P
which lies in the hyperplane
H_{\bm{a}_{i},b_{i}}
.
We write

\mathcal{M}(P)=\big\{\big(\bm{a}_{1},\mathrm{vol}(F_{1})\big),\dots,(\bm{a}_{m},\mathrm{vol}(F_{m}))\big\},

where
\mathrm{vol}(F_{i})
is the relative volume of
F_{i}
.
The following result is known as Minkowski’s theorem (see
[
1
, §6.3 Theorem 1]
).

Theorem 6.2

(Minkowski)
.

If
P
and
Q
are
d
-polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathcal{M}(P)=\mathcal{M}(Q)
, then
P=Q+\bm{v}
for some
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To apply Minkowski’s theorem in our situation,
we will show that we can know volumes of facets of a polytope from translated lattice points enumerator on codimension
1
cells of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We say that a point
\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{a},k}\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
is
generic
in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
if
\bm{x}\not\in H
for any
H\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
with
H\neq H_{\bm{a},k}
.
Note that
\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{a},k}
is generic if and only if it is contained in a
(d-1)
-dimensional cell of
\Delta_{P}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope,

\bm{a}\in N(P)
,
and let
F
be a facet of
P
corresponding to the normal vector
\bm{a}
.
If
\bm{v}\in H_{\bm{a},k}
is generic in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
, then for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, one has

(1)

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}-\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+\varepsilon\bm{a}}=\mathrm{TL}_{F,\bm{v}}\neq 0
.

(2)

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}-\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}-\varepsilon\bm{a}}=0
if there is no
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
-c\bm{a}\in N(P)
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathrm{TL}_{F,\bm{v}}\neq 0
follows from Lemma
5.3
.
By Lemma
3.3
, there is an
\varepsilon>0

such that

\displaystyle\partial\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}+s\bm{a},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\partial\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}-s\bm{a},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\varnothing\ \ \ \mbox{for all }0<s\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}=\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}+\varepsilon\bm{a},0)

and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}=\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}-\varepsilon\bm{a},0)
.
By the above equation, we have

(i)

(\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\subset(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
;

(ii)

\mathrm{int}\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\subset\big(\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.

Also, regarding lattice points in the boundary of
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
, we have

(iii)

if
\bm{x}
is a lattice point in a facet

(\mathcal{C}_{P}\cap H_{(\bm{b},k),0})+(\bm{v},0)
of
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
with
H_{(\bm{b},k),0}^{\geq}\supset\mathcal{C}_{P}
, then

(6.1)

\displaystyle\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\ \ \Leftrightarrow(\bm{b},\bm{a})<0\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}\ \ \Leftrightarrow(\bm{b},\-\bm{a})>0.

In particular, since
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}
have no lattice points in their boundaries,
a lattice point in the boundary of
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
is contained in exactly one of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}
.

Now we assume that
F
is the only facet of
P
that is orthogonal to
\bm{a}
and prove (1) and (2).
Observe
\mathcal{C}_{F}=\mathcal{C}_{P}\cap H_{(\bm{a},k),0}
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
.
By the assumption and Lemma
5.3
,

\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}_{F}+(\bm{v},0)
is the only facet of
\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
that contains lattice points, so

(6.2)

\displaystyle\partial\big(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}_{F}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}.

On the other hand, lattice points in
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
are not contained in
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}
by (iii),
so by (i) and (ii) we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}+\varepsilon\bm{a},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\mathrm{int}\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}.

Then the equations (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) prove (1).
Similarly,
all lattice points in
\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)
are contained in
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}
by (iii),
so again by (i) and (ii) we have

\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}-\varepsilon\bm{a},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1},

proving (2).

Second, we assume that there is a facet
G\neq F
of
P
that is orthogonal to
\bm{a}
.
This condition is equivalent to the condition that there is
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
-c\bm{a}\in N(P)
.
Also,
the normal vector corresponding to the facet
G
must be equal to
-c\bm{a}

and by the assumption and Lemma
5.3
we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\partial\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{F}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)\cup\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{G}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big).

The property (iii) says

\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{F}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}=\varnothing\mbox{ and }\big(\mathcal{C}_{G}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{+}.

Then
by (i), (ii) and (
6.4
),
we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v}+\varepsilon\bm{a},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}

\displaystyle=\big(\mathrm{int}\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)\cup\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{G}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)\setminus\big(\big(\mathcal{C}_{F}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big)

proving (1).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

If
P
and
Q
are rational
d
-polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{Q}
,
then
\mathcal{M}(P)=\mathcal{M}(Q)
.

Proof.

What we must prove is that the set
\Gamma_{P}
determines the directions of inner normal vectors of
P
as well as volumes of the facets of
P
.

By Theorem
3.8
(1) and Lemma
6.3
(1),
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{P}}H
if and only if there is an open ball
B\ni\bm{x}
such that
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{x}}=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{y}}
for all
\bm{y}\in B
.
This says that the set
\Gamma_{P}
determines
\mathcal{A}_{P}
,
and the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{P}
says that
\mathcal{A}_{P}
determines the set
\overline{N}=\{\pm(\bm{a}/\|\bm{a}\|)\mid\bm{a}\in N(P)\}
.
For each
\bm{a}\in\overline{N}
, Lemma
6.3
also says
c\bm{a}\in N(P)
for some

c>0
if and only if, for a generic
\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{a},0}\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
,
we have
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{x}}\neq\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{x}+\varepsilon\bm{a}}
for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
Hence the set
\Gamma_{P}
determines
\{(\bm{a}/\|\bm{a}\|)\mid\bm{a}\in N(P)\}
.

It remains to prove that
\Gamma_{P}
determines the volumes of facets of
P
.
Let
F
be a facet of
P
and let
\bm{a}\in N(P)

be the normal vector associated with the facet
F
.
For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{0}(t)
denote the
0
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
,
which must be a degree
d
polynomial whose leading coefficient is the normalized volume of
P
.
If we take a generic point
\bm{x}\in H_{\bm{a},0}
in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
, then by Lemma
6.3
we have

\textstyle\mathrm{lim}_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t^{d-1}}\mathrm{TL}^{0}_{F,\bm{x}}(t)=\mathrm{lim}_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t^{d-1}}\big(\mathrm{TL}^{0}_{P,\bm{x}}(t)-\mathrm{TL}^{0}_{P,\bm{x}+\varepsilon\bm{a}}(t)\big),

where
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small.
Since
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{x}}^{0}(t)-\mathrm{TL}^{0}_{P,\bm{x}+\varepsilon\bm{a}}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
\leq d-1
, this limit exists and must be equal to the relative volume of
F
since
\mathrm{TL}_{F,\bm{x}}
can be considered as a translated lattice points enumerator in the Euclidean space
H_{\bm{a},0}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
with the lattice
H_{\bm{a},0}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
.
Thus volumes of facets of
P
are determined by
\Gamma_{P}
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

If one know
\Gamma_{P}
then we can know the volume of
P
since it appears in the leading coefficient of a constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
.
There is another way to compute the volume of
P
that was considered in
[
2
]
.
Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational convex polytope.
For each cell
C\in\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
we call the number
\mathrm{TL}_{P,-C}(1)

the multiplicity of
C
.
This number is indeed the multiplicity in the sense that, if
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the natural projection, then
for
[\bm{v}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
one has

\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}(1)=\#((P-\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\#(P\cap(\bm{v}+\mathbb{Z}^{d}))=\#(\rho^{-1}([\bm{v}])).

This equation says that the volume of
P
equals to the sum of volumes of (maximal dimensional) cells of
\Delta_{P}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
times their multiplicities.
Volumes of facets of
P
can be also computed using similar argument given in the proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Let
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
be the projection given by

\pi_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}).

We next show that translated lattice points enumerators of
\pi_{i}(P)
can be determined from those of
P
.
Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-polytope.
We define

\partial_{i}^{-}P=\{\bm{x}\in P\mid\bm{x}\not\in(P+\varepsilon\mathbf{e}_{i})\mbox{ for all }\varepsilon>0\},

where
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
are the standard vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Intuitively,
\partial_{i}^{-}P
is the set of points in
P
which is visible from
-\infty\mathbf{e}_{i}
(see Figure
6
).
Indeed,
\partial_{i}^{-}P
has the following description:
Let
\mathrm{Facets}(P)
be the set of facets of
P

and assume
P=\bigcup_{F\in\mathrm{Facets}(P)}H^{\geq}_{\bm{a}_{F},b_{F}}
.
Then, for any
\bm{x}\in P
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have
\bm{x}\not\in P+\varepsilon\mathbf{e}_{i}

if and only if
(\bm{a}_{F},\bm{x})-\varepsilon(\bm{a}_{F},\mathbf{e}_{i})=(\bm{a}_{F},\bm{x}-\varepsilon\mathbf{e}_{i})<b_{F}
for some
F\in\mathrm{Facets}(P)
.
This means

(6.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{-}P=\bigcup_{F\in\mathrm{Facets}(P),\ (\bm{a}_{F},\mathbf{e}_{i})>0}F,

and the RHS of the above equation is nothing but the set of points in
P
which is visible from
-\infty\mathbf{e}_{i}
(see
[
13
, §5.2]
for more information on visible faces of a polytope).

.

\partial_{1}^{-}P
and
\partial_{2}^{-}P
when
P
is the hexagon with vertices
\pm(2,0),\pm(1,1),\pm(1,-1)
. Thick lines correspond to
\partial_{1}^{-}P
and

.

Lemma 6.6
.

With the same notation as above,
for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there is an
\varepsilon_{i,\bm{v}}>0
such that

(tP+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\left(\big(t(\partial_{i}^{-}P)+\bm{v}\big)\bigsqcup\big(tP+\bm{v}+\varepsilon_{i,\bm{v}}\mathbf{e}_{i}\big)\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ \ \mbox{ for all $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.$}

We note that when
t=0
, we consider that
t(\partial^{-}_{i}P)=\{0\}
in Lemma
6.6
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\big(\mathcal{C}_{P}\!+\!(\bm{v}\!+\!\varepsilon\mathbf{e}_{i},0)\big)\bigsqcup\big\{\bm{x}\!+\!(\bm{v},0)\in\mathcal{C}_{P}\!+\!(\bm{v},0)\mid\bm{x}\not\in\mathcal{C}_{P}\!+\!s(\mathbf{e}_{i},0)\ \mbox{ for all }s>0\big\}\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}.

Cutting the above equation by the hyperplane
x_{d+1}=t
,
we get the desired equality.
∎

We define
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}(t)
by

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}(t)=\#\big(\big(t(\partial_{i}^{-}P)+\bm{v}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big).

Lemma
6.6
says that

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)-\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+\varepsilon_{i,\bm{v}}\mathbf{e}_{i}}(t),

where
\varepsilon_{i,\bm{v}}
is a number given in Lemma
6.6
.
We note that the function
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}
is zero for almost all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Indeed,
we have the following statement.

Lemma 6.7
.

With the same notation as above,

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}
is not a zero function only when there is
\bm{a}\in N(P)
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\bm{v}\in H_{\bm{a},k}
and
(\bm{a},\mathbf{e}_{i})>0
.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(-i)}\neq 0
only when

\big(\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}^{-}P}+(\bm{v},0)\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\neq\varnothing.

By (
6.5
) and Lemma
5.3
this condition is equivalent to
\bm{v}\in H_{\bm{a},k}
for some
\bm{a}\in N(P)
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(\bm{a},\mathbf{e}_{i})>0
.
∎

The next proposition shows that translated lattice points enumerators of
\pi_{i}(P)
can be determined from those of
P
.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope. For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, one has

\mathrm{TL}_{\pi_{i}(P),\pi_{i}(\bm{v})}(t)=\sum_{0\leq s<1,\ \mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(-i)}(t)\neq 0}\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(-i)}(t).

We note that the RHS in the proposition is a finite sum by Lemma
6.7
since the segment
\{\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}\mid 0\leq s<1\}
meets only finitely many hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
.
See Figure
7
for a visualization of the proposition.

.
Lattice points in the projection.

Proof.

We may assume
i=d.
Fix
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a lattice point
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d-1})\in\pi_{d}(tP+\bm{v})
.
It suffices to prove that there is a unique integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(\bm{n},r)\in\bigcup_{0\leq s<1}(t(\partial_{d}^{-}P)+\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{d})
.

(Existence) By the assumption, there is an
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(\bm{n},\alpha)\in t(\partial_{d}^{-}P)+\bm{v}.

Then,
r=\lceil\alpha\rceil
satisfies the desired condition since
(\bm{n},\lceil\alpha\rceil)
is contained in
t(\partial_{d}^{-}P)+\bm{v}+(\lceil\alpha\rceil-\alpha)\mathbf{e}_{d}
.

(Uniqueness) The uniqueness of
r
follows from the fact that,
for any
(\bm{n},\alpha),(\bm{n},\alpha^{\prime})
which are contained in
\bigcup_{0\leq s<1}(t(\partial_{n}^{-}P)+\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{d})
, we have
|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|<1
.
∎

We will also use the following variation of Proposition
6.8
.
For a
d
-polytope
P
,
let

\partial_{i}^{+}P=\{\bm{x}\in P\mid\bm{x}\not\in P-\varepsilon\mathbf{e}_{i}\mbox{ for all }\varepsilon>0\}

and

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}^{(+i)}(t)=\#\big(\big(t(\partial_{i}^{+}P)+\bm{v}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)\ \ \mbox{ for }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

The next statement can be proved by the same argument given in the proof of Proposition
6.8
.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope. For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, one has

\mathrm{TL}_{\pi_{i}(P),\pi_{i}(\bm{v})}(t)=\sum_{0\leq s<1,\ \mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}-s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(+i)}(t)\neq 0}\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}-s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(+i)}(t).

We now prove Theorem
6.1
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

We use induction on
d
.
Suppose
d=1
and
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{Q}
.
Let
\ell=\#(P\cap\mathbb{Z})
and let
p
be the maximal integer which is equal to or smaller than
\min P
.
Then, by setting

a=\min\{s\in[0,1)\mid\#((P+s)\cap\mathbb{Z})-\#((P+s+\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z})\neq 0\}

and

b=\min\{s\in[0,1)\mid\#((P-s)\cap\mathbb{Z})-\#(P-s-\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq 0\},

where
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small.
we have

P=[p+(1-a),p+\ell+b]

Since
\ell,a,b
only depend on
\Gamma_{P}
,
this implies
P=Q+n
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Assume
d>1
and
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{Q}
.
By Lemma
6.4
, we already know
Q=P+\bm{v}
for some
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By Proposition
6.8
and the assumption
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{Q}
, we have
\Gamma_{\pi_{1}(P)}=\Gamma_{\pi_{1}(Q)}
and
\Gamma_{\pi_{2}(P)}=\Gamma_{\pi_{2}(Q)}
.
Since
Q=P+\bm{v}
, the induction hypothesis says that
\pi_{1}(\bm{v}),\pi_{2}(\bm{v})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
which guarantees
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
∎

7.
Group symmetry

In the previous section, we saw that the translated lattice points enumerators determine polytopes up to translations by integer vectors.
In this section,
we study translated lattice points enumerators of polytopes having some symmetries,
in particular, we prove Theorem
1.5
in the Introduction.

Let
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
be the subgroup of the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of all elements
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
with
g(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
If we identify each element of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
with
d\times d
non-singular matrix in a standard way, then
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
may be considered as the set of unimodular matrices.
For a rational
d
-polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(P)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\mid g(P)=P+\bm{n}\mbox{ for some }\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

and

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Gamma_{P})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\mathrm{TL}_{P,g(\bm{v})}=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}\mbox{ for all }\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

Proposition 7.1
.

For a rational
d
-polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
one has
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Gamma_{P})=\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(P)
.

Proof.

We first prove “
\subset
”.
Let
g\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Gamma_{P})
.
Then, for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)=\mathrm{TL}_{P,g(\bm{v})}(t)=\#\big(\big(tP+g(\bm{v})\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\#\big((tg^{-1}(P)+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\mathrm{TL}_{g^{-1}(P),\bm{v}}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Thus we have
\Gamma_{P}=\Gamma_{g^{-1}(P)}
so
P=g^{-1}(P)+\bm{n}
for some
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by Theorem
6.1
.
Then
g\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(P)
since

P=g(P)-g(\bm{n})
and
g(\bm{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

We next prove “
\supset
”.
Let
g\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(P)
.
Then for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\#\big(\big(tP+g(\bm{v})\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\#\big(\big(tg(P)+g(\bm{v})\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)=\#\big((tP+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)

for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
where the last equality follows from the fact that
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
.
This implies
\mathrm{TL}_{P,g(\bm{v})}=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Example 7.2
.

Consider the rhombus
Q
in Example
4.2
.
From the list of translated lattice points enumerators in the example,
one can see that they are equal on
E_{1},E_{2},E_{7}
and
E_{8}
.
This can be explained using the symmetry.
Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be a reflection by the
x
-axis and the
y
-axis, respectively.
Then
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
do not change
Q
so they are elements of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(Q)
.
We have

\rho_{1}(E_{1})=E_{7},\ \rho_{1}(E_{2})=E_{8},\mbox{ and }\rho_{2}(E_{1})=E_{2},

which say that translated lattice points enumerators are equal on
E_{1},E_{2},E_{7}
and
E_{8}
.

We now focus on centrally symmetric polytopes.
Recall that a quasi-polynomial

is said to be symmetric if its
k
th constituent equals its
(-k)
th constituent for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We first prove the following criterion for the symmetry of Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of
P+\bm{v}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope. The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.

(ii)

For all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, one has

\mbox{the $k$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}$}=\mbox{the $(-k)$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}$}.

Proof.

We first prove “(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)”.
Fix
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then

k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$k$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\frac{1}{k}\bm{v}}$}

(by Lemma
2.4
)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\frac{1}{k}\bm{v}}$}

(by (i))

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}$}

\displaystyle\mbox{(by Lemma \ref{ehrToTrans})},

as desired.

The proof for “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)” is similar.
Indeed, we have

k
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$k$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}$}

(by Lemma
2.4
)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,-k\bm{v}}$}

(by (ii))

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}$}

\displaystyle\mbox{(by Lemma \ref{ehrToTrans})},

as desired.
∎

Recall that a polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be centrally symmetric if
-P=P+\bm{x}
for some
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
If
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, then

(i)

P
is centrally symmetric.

(ii)

\operatorname{ehr}_{\pi_{i}(P)+\bm{u}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d-1}
and
i\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
q
be a positive integer such that
qP
is integral.
It suffices to prove that
qP
is centrally symmetric.
Since
\operatorname{ehr}_{qP+q\bm{v}}(\ell)=\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}(q\ell)
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
the
k
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{qP+q\bm{v}}
is obtained from the
qk
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
by substituting
t
with
\frac{t}{q}
(as polynomials in

t
).
This fact and the assumption say that
\operatorname{ehr}_{qP+q\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.
Since
qP
is a lattice polytope,
it follows from Theorem
1.4
that
qP
is centrally symmetric.

(ii)
For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, we have

k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{\pi_{i}(P),\pi_{i}(\bm{v})}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$k$th constituent of $\sum_{0\leq s<1}\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(-i)}$}

\displaystyle(\mbox{by Proposition \ref{7.6}})

\displaystyle=\mbox{$k$th constituent of $\sum_{0\leq s<1}\big(\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}}-\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}+s\mathbf{e}_{i}-\varepsilon_{s}\mathbf{e}_{i}}\big)$}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\sum_{0\leq s<1}\big(\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}-s\mathbf{e}_{i}}-\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}-s\mathbf{e}_{i}+\varepsilon_{s}\mathbf{e}_{i}}\big)$}

\displaystyle(\mbox{by Lemma \ref{symcriteiron}})

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\sum_{0\leq s<1}\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}-s\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{(+i)}$}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$(-k)$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{\pi_{i}(P),-\pi_{i}(\bm{v})}$}

\displaystyle(\mbox{by Proposition \ref{7.6*}}),

where each
\varepsilon_{s}
is a sufficiently small positive number which depends on

.
This proves that
\pi_{i}(P)
satisfies the condition (ii) of Lemma

.
∎

We now come to the goal of this section.
Let
P
be a centrally symmetric polytope with
-P=P+\bm{x}
.
Then
\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}
is a center of
P
, and if
\bm{p}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}+\bm{p}^{\prime}
is a vertex of
P
,
the point
\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}-\bm{p}^{\prime}
is also a vertex of
P
by the central symmetry.
We write this vertex
\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}-\bm{p}^{\prime}
as
\bm{p}^{*}
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is symmetric for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.

(ii)

P
is centrally symmetric and
\bm{p}-\bm{p}^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for every vertex
\bm{p}
of
P
.

To prove the theorem,
we recall the following basic fact on
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional cone with apex
\bm{0}
.
There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which is contained in
\mathrm{int}(X)
.

Proof.

Take any integer vector
\bm{n}\in\mathrm{int}(X)

with
\gcd(\bm{n})=1
.

First, we claim that
there are
\bm{n}_{1},\dots,\bm{n}_{d-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\bm{n},\bm{n}_{1},\dots,\bm{n}_{d-1}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
In fact, by the assumption, the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(\mathbb{Z}\bm{n})
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
d-1
since it is torsionfree.
If we choose
\bm{n}_{1},\dots,\bm{n}_{d-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
so that they form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(\mathbb{Z}\bm{n})
,
the sequence
\bm{n},\bm{n}_{1},\dots,\bm{n}_{d-1}
becomes a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Now, we show that we can choose
\bm{n}_{1},\ldots,\bm{n}_{d-1}
from
\mathrm{int}(X)
.
For each
\bm{n}_{i}
, since
\bm{n}
is in the interior of
X
, by taking a sufficiently large integer

, the point
\bm{n}_{i}+k_{i}\bm{n}
is contained in
\mathrm{int}(X)
.
Then
\bm{n},\bm{n}_{1}+k_{1}\bm{n},\dots,\bm{n}_{d-1}+k_{d-1}\bm{n}
is a desired
\mathbb{Z}
-basis.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.5
.

((ii)
\Rightarrow
(i))
By taking an appropriate translation, we may assume

.
Then
\bm{p}^{*}=-\bm{p}
for every vertex
\bm{p}
of

,
so the condition (ii) says that

is integral.
In particular, every quasi-polynomial

has period 2.
We prove that
P
satisfies the condition (ii) of Lemma
7.3
.

Let
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
.
Since
P=-P
, Lemma
5.5
says

the
k
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{the $k$th constituent of $\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}$}.

However, since
\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}
has period
2
,
the RHS in the above equation equals
the
(-k)
th constituent of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,-\bm{v}}
.

((i)
\Rightarrow

We have already seen that (i) implies that

.
We prove the second condition of (ii) by induction on

.
Suppose

. Then we may assume

\textstyle P=[0,x+\frac{p}{q}]

for some

.
Then we have

\textstyle\mbox{ the first constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P}$}=\mathrm{vol}(P)t-\frac{p}{q}+1

\textstyle\mbox{ the $(q-1)$th constituent of $\operatorname{ehr}_{P}$}=\mathrm{vol}(P)t-(q-1)\frac{p}{q}+\lfloor(q-1)\frac{p}{q}\rfloor+1.

.
Hence

Suppose

.
Let
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
be a vertex of
P
.
Consider the normal cone at the vertex
\bm{p}

X=\{\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\max\{(\bm{a},\bm{x})\mid\bm{x}\in P\}=(\bm{a},\bm{p})\}.

This is a

By Lemma

there is a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\mathbf{e}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}^{\prime}_{d}

Consider the linear transformation

Since

we have
\operatorname{ehr}_{g(P)+\bm{u}}(t)=\operatorname{ehr}_{P+g^{-1}(\bm{u})}(t)

This says that

If

8.
A connection to commutative algebra

In this section,
we briefly explain a connection between translated lattice points enumerators and conic divisorial ideals of Ehrhart rings in commutative algebra.
In particular, we explain that Theorem
2.3
can be proved algebraically using the duality of Cohen–Macaulay modules.

8.1.
Conic divisorial ideals

Let
S=\mathbb{F}[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{d+1}^{\pm}]
be the Laurent polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{F}.

We will consider the grading of
S
defined by
\deg(x_{1})=\cdots=\deg(x_{d})=0
and
\deg(x_{d+1})=1
.
For
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
,
we write

x^{\bm{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{d+1}^{a_{d+1}}.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
The
Ehrhart ring

\mathbb{F}[P]
of
P
(over
\mathbb{F}
) is the monoid algebra generated by the monomials
x^{\bm{a}}
such that
\bm{a}
is in the monoid
\mathcal{C}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.
As vector spaces, we can write

(8.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}[P]=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid\bm{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\}.

For a finitely generated graded
\mathbb{F}[P]
-module
M
, its
Hilbert function
is the function defined by
\mathrm{hilb}(M,k)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}M_{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
M_{k}
is the degree
k
component of
M
,
and the
Hilbert series
of
M
is the formal power series
\operatorname{Hilb}(M,z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{hilb}(M,k)z^{k}
.
Ehrhart rings are closely related to Ehrhart quasi-polynomials.
Indeed, from (
8.1
),
we can see that the Hilbert function of
\mathbb{F}[P]
is nothing but the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of
P
.

For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
the vector space

I_{\bm{v}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid\bm{a}\in(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\}\subset S

becomes a finitely generated graded
\mathbb{F}[P]
-module.
The modules
I_{\bm{v}}
are called
conic divisorial ideals
of
\mathbb{F}[P]
.
We note that different vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
could give the same conic divisorial ideal,
more precisely, we have
I_{\bm{v}}=I_{\bm{u}}
if and only if the cones
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v}
and
\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{u}
have the same lattice points.

Let us call a conic divisorial ideal
I

standard
if
I=I_{(\bm{v},0)}
for some
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hilbert functions of standard conic divisorial ideals are nothing but translated lattice points enumerators.
Indeed, for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

(8.2)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(I_{(\bm{v},0)})_{t}=\#\{\bm{a}\!=\!(a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1})\in(\mathcal{C}_{P}+(\bm{v},0))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\!\mid a_{d+1}\!=\!t\}=\#\big((tP+\bm{v})\!\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big).

We will not explain algebraic backgrounds on (conic) divisorial ideals of Ehrhart rings since it is not relevant to the theme of this paper.
But in the rest of this section we briefly explain how algebraic properties of conic divisorial ideals can be used to consider properties of translated lattice points enumerators.
For more detailed information on conic divisorial ideals,
see
[
10
]
and
[
11
, §4.7]
.

8.2.
Hilbert series of conic divisorial ideals
and an algebraic proof of Theorem
2.3

We need some basic tools on commutative algebra such as the Cohen–Macaulay property and canonical modules.
We refer the readers to
[
9
, §3 and §4]
for basics on commutative algebra.

We introduce one more notation.
For
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
we define

(8.3)

\displaystyle I^{\circ}_{\bm{v}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\big\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid\bm{a}\in\big(\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})+\bm{v}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\big\}.

The space
I^{\circ}_{\bm{v}}
is also a conic divisorial ideal. Indeed, if
\bm{w}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})
is a vector which is sufficiently close to the origin, then we have

\big(\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})+\bm{v}\big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=(\mathcal{C}_{P}+\bm{v}+\bm{w})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1},

which says
I_{\bm{v}}^{\circ}=I_{\bm{v}+\bm{w}}
.
The following facts are known. See
[
11
, Corollary 3.3 and Remark 4.4(b)]
.

•

I_{\bm{v}}
is a
(d+1)
-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay module.

•

I_{\bm{v}}^{\circ}
is the canonical module of
I_{-\bm{v}}
, more precisely, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}[P]}(I_{\bm{v}},\omega)\cong\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid\bm{a}\in(\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})-\bm{v})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\}=I_{-\bm{v}}^{\circ},

where

\omega=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid\bm{a}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{P})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\}
is the graded canonical module of
\mathbb{F}[P]
.

These properties give the following consequences on Hilbert series of conic divisorial ideals.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope and
q
the denominator of
P
.
Let
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
\alpha=\lceil v_{d+1}\rceil
.

(1)

\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{\bm{v}}^{\circ},z)=(-1)^{d+1}\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{-\bm{v}},z^{-1})
.

(2)

\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{\bm{v}},z)=\frac{z^{\alpha}}{(1-z^{q})^{d+1}}Q(z)
for some polynomial
Q(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[z]
of degree
<q(d+1)
.

Proof.

The equality (1) is the well-known formula of the Hilbert series of a canonical module. See
[
9
, Theorem 4.45]
.
We prove (2).
Consider the subring

A=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\big\{x^{\bm{a}}\mid x^{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{C}_{P}\mbox{ and $\deg(x^{\bm{a}})\in q\mathbb{Z}$}\big\}\subset\mathbb{F}[P].

Since
qP
is integral,
\mathbb{F}[qP]
is a semi-standard graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, that is,
\mathbb{F}[qP]
is a finitely generated as a module over a standard graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\mathbb{F}[x^{\bm{a}}x_{d+1}\mid x^{\bm{a}}\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]

(see
[
22
, Theorem 9.3.6]
(d)).
Then, since
A\cong\mathbb{F}[qP]
, where the degree
k
part of
\mathbb{F}[qP]
corresponds to the degree
qk
part of
A
,
any finitely generated
A
-module
M
of Krull dimension
m
has the Hilbert series of the form
Q(z)/(1-z^{q})^{m}
for some polynomial
Q(z)
,
and if
M
is Cohen–Macaulay then
Q(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[z]

(
[
9
, Corollaries 4.8 and 4.10]
).

Since
\mathbb{F}[P]
is a finitely generated
A
-module,

I_{\bm{v}}
is a finitely generated Cohen–Macaulay
A
-module of Krull dimension
d+1
. Thus there is a polynomial
Q(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[z]
such that

\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{\bm{v}},z)=\frac{1}{(1-z^{q})^{d+1}}Q(z).

Since
(I_{\bm{v}})_{k}=0
for
k<\alpha=\lceil v_{d+1}\rceil
by the definition of
I_{\bm{v}}
,
the polynomial
Q(z)
must be of the form

Q(z)=c_{0}t^{\alpha}+c_{1}t^{\alpha+1}+\cdots+c_{m}t^{\alpha+m}

for some
m\geq 0
,
where
c_{0},\dots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
c_{m}\neq 0
, so it follows that

\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{\bm{v}},z)=\frac{z^{\alpha}}{(1-z^{q})^{d+1}}(c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{m}z^{m}).

Now it remains to prove
m<q(d+1)
.
By statement (1), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{-\bm{v}}^{\circ},z)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d+1}\frac{z^{-\alpha}}{(1-z^{-q})^{d+1}}(c_{0}+c_{1}z^{-1}+\cdots+c_{m}z^{-m})

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{q(d+1)-\alpha-m}}{(1-z^{q})^{d+1}}(c_{m}+c_{m}z+\cdots+c_{0}z^{m}).

This says

-\alpha<\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid(I_{-\bm{v}}^{\circ})_{k}\neq 0\}=q(d+1)-\alpha-m

proving the desired inequality
m<q(d+1)
.
∎

The statements in Proposition
8.1
are known to imply the quasi-polynomiality and reciprocity of translated lattice points enumerators in Theorem
2.3
.
Recall that
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
coincides with the Hilbert function of
I_{(\bm{v},0)}
.
Proposition
8.1
(2) says that
the Hilbert series of
I_{(\bm{v},0)}
can be written in the form
\frac{1}{(1-z^{q})^{d+1}}Q(z)
for some polynomial
Q(z)
of degree
<q(d+1)
,
which is known to imply that
\mathrm{hilb}(I_{(\bm{v},0)},t)

(=\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}(t)
) coincides with a quasi-polynomial with period
q
for
t\geq 0
.
See e.g.,
[
6
, §3.8]
or
[
21
, §4]
.
Also, Proposition
8.1
(1) is essentially equivalent to the reciprocity in Theorem
2.3
(2).
See
[
6
, §4.3]
.

Finally,
we note that the proposition gives some restriction to the possible values of
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
.
If
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a lattice
d
-polytope and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
then the proposition says

\operatorname{Hilb}(I_{(\bm{v},0)},z)=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{d+1}}(h_{0}+h_{1}z+\cdots+h_{d}z^{d})

for some
h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
These
h
-numbers must satisfy the following conditions

(I)

h_{0}=1
if
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
h_{0}=0
if
\bm{v}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
;

(II)

h_{0}+\cdots+h_{d}=d!\mathrm{vol}(P)
.

The first condition follows from
h_{0}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(I_{(\bm{v},0)})_{0}
,
and the second condition follows since
\frac{1}{d!}(h_{0}+\cdots+h_{d})
is the top degree coefficient of the polynomial
\mathrm{hilb}(I_{(\bm{v},0)},t)
.
Below we give a simple application of this.
Consider a lattice polygon
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose volume is
\frac{3}{2}
.
Then the possible values of
h_{0}+h_{1}z+h_{2}z^{2}
are

1+z+z^{2},\ 1+2z,\ 1+2z^{2},\ 3z,\ 2z+z^{2},\ z+2z^{2},\ 3z^{2}.

If
f(t)
is a polynomial
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}f(t)z^{t}=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{3}}(h_{0}+h_{1}z+h_{2}z^{2})
,
then
f(t)=h_{0}{t+2\choose 2}+h_{1}{t+1\choose 2}+h_{2}{t\choose 2}
.
So a translated lattice points enumerator of an integral polygon with volume
\frac{3}{2}
must be one of the following polynomials

\textstyle\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t+1,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{5}{2}t+1,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t+1,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t,\ \frac{3}{2}t^{2}-\frac{3}{2}t.

Four of them appear as translated lattice points enumerators of the trapezoid in the Introduction.
See (
1.1
).

.

9.
Problems

In this last section,
we list a few problems which we cannot answer.

Gcd property and zonotopes

A quasi-polynomial
f
with period
q
is said to have the
gcd property
if its
k
th constituent only depends on the gcd of
k
and
q
for all

k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We note that if
f
has the gcd property then
f
must be symmetric.
It was proved in
[
12
]
that,
for a lattice
d
-polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
has the gcd property for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
if and only if
P
is a zonotope.
Considering the statement in Theorem
1.5
,
one may ask if a similar statement holds for zonotopes
P
such that
2P
is integral,
but this is not the case.
Indeed, the rhombus
Q
in Example
4.2

is a zonotope and
2Q
is integral but the computation given in the example says that

\operatorname{ehr}_{Q+(\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8})}
does not satisfy the gcd property.
We repeat the following question asked in
[
12
, Problem 6.7(2)]
.

Problem 9.1
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a rational
d
-polytope.
Is it true that,
if
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
has the gcd property for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
then
P=Q+\bm{u}
for some integral zonotope
Q
and some
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
?

To consider this problem
we can assume that
P
is a zonotope by the argument similar to the proof of Corollary
7.4
(i)
and
2P
is integral by Theorem
1.5
.

Period collapse

Recall that the denominator of a rational polytope
P
is always a period of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
.
If the minimum period of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}
is not equal to the denominator of
P
, we say that period collapse occurs to
P
.
A period collapse is a major subject in the study of Ehrhart quasi-polynomials (see e.g.,
[
7
,
14
,
18
,
19
]
).
We ask the following vague question:
Can a relation between
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
and
\mathrm{TL}_{P,\bm{v}}
be used to produce polytopes giving period collapse?
For translations of a lattice polytope,
a period collapse cannot occur.
Indeed, if
P
is a lattice polytope, then the minimum period of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
must be the smallest integer
k
such that
k\bm{v}
is integral since
the constant term of the
k
th constituent of
\operatorname{ehr}_{P+\bm{v}}
is
\mathrm{TL}_{P,k\bm{v}}(0)=\#(\{k\bm{v}\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
,
which is non-zero only when
k\bm{v}
is integral.

Local solubility for a family of quadrics over a split quadric surface
Tim Browning, Julian Lyczak, Roman Sarapin

Abstract
. We study the density of everywhere locally soluble diagonal quadric surfaces, parameterised by rational points that lie on a split quadric surface.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14G05 (11N36, 14D10)

1.
Introduction

A great deal of recent activity has been directed at the question of
local solubility for families of varieties.
Let
X
and
Y

be smooth, proper and geometrically irreducible varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
, equipped with a dominant morphism
\pi\colon X\to Y
, with geometrically integral generic fibre. Suppose that

Y
is Fano and equipped with an anticanonical height function

H\colon Y(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{R}
. This short note is concerned with the behaviour of the counting function

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)=\#\left\{y\in Y(\mathbb{Q})\cap\pi(X(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}))\colon H(y)\leq B\right\},

as
B\to\infty
,
where
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the ring of adèles. We shall consider an explicit example, with
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
a split quadric surface,
which calls into question our expectations about the behaviour of
N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)
.

The quantity
N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)
measures the density of fibres of
\pi
which are everywhere locally soluble, and it has received the most attention when
Y
is projective space.
For example, it follows from work of
Poonen and Voloch
[
10
, Thm. 3.6]
that

(1.1)

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)\sim c_{d,n}B,

as
B\to\infty
, for an explicit constant
c_{d,n}>0
, when
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the family of degree
d
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, with
(d,n)\neq(2,2)
and
N=\binom{n+d}{d}-1
.
Moreover, when
Y=\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
work of Loughran and Smeets
[
7
]
shows that

(1.2)

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)\ll\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\Delta(\pi)}},

for a certain quantity
\Delta(\pi)\geq 0
, whose definition will be recalled in
§
2
and which can be extended to arbitrary fibrations
\pi\colon X\to Y
.
For
Y=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, it is conjectured in
[
7
]
that the upper bound
(
1.2
) should be sharp whenever the fibre

\pi^{-1}(D)

above each codimension one point
D
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
contains a component of multiplicity one.

It is natural to ask what happens when
Y
is a Fano variety over
\mathbb{Q}
that is
not projective space. We shall assume that
Y(\mathbb{Q})
is Zariski dense in
Y
and that
Y

conforms to the version of the Manin conjecture
[
2
]
without accummulating subvarieties, so that

\#\{y\in Y(\mathbb{Q})\colon H(y)\leq B\}\sim c_{Y}B(\log B)^{\rho_{Y}-1},

as
B\to\infty
, where
c_{Y}>0
is a constant and
\rho_{Y}
is the rank of the Picard group of
Y
.
Assuming that
Y
is a Fano variety over
\mathbb{Q}
without accummulating subvarieties,
a naive combination of the Manin conjecture with the upper bound (
1.2
) might lead us to suppose that

(1.3)

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)\sim\frac{c_{\pi}B(\log B)^{\rho_{Y}-1}}{(\log B)^{\Delta(\pi)}},

as
B\to\infty
, for a suitable constant
c_{\pi}>0
. This is compatible with (
1.1
), since in this case
\Delta(\pi)=0
and
\rho_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}=1
.
It also compatible with an upper bound of
Browning and Loughran
[
1
, Thm. 1.10]
,
which concerns the case where
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a smooth quadric over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension at least
3
, since then
\rho_{Y}=1
.

We study an explicit example that
addresses a case where the variety
Y
has Picard number
\rho_{Y}>1
and which contradicts the expectation (
1.3
). Let

Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the split
quadric surface

(1.4)

y_{0}y_{1}=y_{2}y_{3}.

Clearly
Y
is smooth and
\rho_{Y}=2
.
Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the variety defined by the pair of equations

(1.5)

\begin{split}y_{0}x_{0}^{2}+y_{1}x_{1}^{2}+y_{2}x_{2}^{2}+y_{3}x_{3}^{2}&=0\\
y_{0}y_{1}&=y_{2}y_{3}.\end{split}

Then

X
is equipped with a dominant morphism
\pi\colon X\to Y
whose generic fibre is geometrically irreducible. We shall see in §
2
that the total space
X
is singular and so we let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularisation. Taking

\widetilde{\pi}
to be the composition with
\pi\colon X\to Y
, we shall prove the following bounds
for the associated counting function
N_{\text{loc}}(\widetilde{\pi},B)
in §
3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

B\ll N_{\text{loc}}(\widetilde{\pi},B)\ll B.

Since the Hasse principle holds for quadratic forms, we deduce from Theorem
1.1
that
the same upper and lower bounds hold for the quantity

N_{\text{glob}}(\widetilde{\pi},B)
, counting the number of

y\in Y(\mathbb{Q})\cap\pi(\widetilde{X}(\mathbb{Q}))
such that
H(y)\leq B
.

The next result, proved in §
2
, shows that
our example is not compatible with the naive expectation
(
1.3
), since

\rho_{Y}=2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

We have
\Delta(\widetilde{\pi})=2
.

Just as thin sets have been found to have a large influence on modern formulations of the Manin conjecture, as discussed by Peyre
[
8
]
,
so we expect that the naive expectation
(
1.3
) should hold after the removal of appropriate thin sets from
Y(\mathbb{Q})
.
In fact, for our example (
1.5
), we conjecture that

\#\left\{y\in Y(\mathbb{Q})\cap\pi(\widetilde{X}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}))\colon H(y)\leq B,~-y_{0}y_{2}\neq\square,~-y_{0}y_{3}\neq\square\right\}\sim c\frac{B}{\log B},

for an appropriate constant
c>0
.
It seems plausible that the character sum method developed by Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
4
]
will prove useful for tackling this counting problem, although the hyperboloid height conditions that arise from parameterising the points in
Y(\mathbb{Q})
will undoubtedly complicate the argument.

In this note we have focused attention on the quadric surface bundle
X\to Y
, where
X
is given by (
1.5
). One can also study the conic bundle
\eta\colon W\to Y
, where
W\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
is given by

\displaystyle y_{0}x_{0}^{2}+y_{1}x_{1}^{2}+y_{2}x_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle y_{0}y_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{2}y_{3}.

This variety is singular and so we consider the morphism
\widetilde{\eta}\colon\widetilde{W}\to Y
, where
\widetilde{\eta}
is the composition of a desingularisation

\widetilde{W}\to W
with
\eta
.
The methods of this paper carry over to this case with little adjustment and can be used to prove that

B\ll N_{\text{loc}}(\widetilde{\eta},B)\ll B
. Moreover, one also has
\Delta(\widetilde{\eta})=2
in this case, providing a further example that counters the expectation (
1.3
), but one which we believe can be repaired via the removal of appropriate thin sets.
(In fact, for
i\in\{0,1\}
, the fibre of
\eta
above the line
y_{i}=y_{2}=0
has multiplicity two in
W
, whereas the corresponding fibre of
\widetilde{\eta}
admits an additional component of multiplicity one.)

2.
Geometric input

We begin by extending the definition
of

\Delta(\pi)

from
[
7
]
to general fibrations.
Let
X
and
Y

be arbitrary
proper and geometrically irreducible varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
, equipped with a
dominant morphism
\pi\colon X\to Y
, with geometrically
integral generic fibre. Assume that
Y
is smooth and
associate the residue field

\kappa(D)
to any codimension one point
D\in Y^{(1)}
. Let

S_{D}
be the set of geometrically irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(D)
and
choose a finite group
\Gamma_{D}
through which the action of
\Gal(\overline{\kappa(D)}/\kappa(D))
on
S_{D}
factors. Then
\Delta(\pi)
is defined to be

(2.1)

\Delta(\pi)=\sum_{D\in Y^{(1)}}\left(1-\delta_{D}(\pi)\right),

where

\delta_{D}(\pi)=\frac{\#\{\sigma\in\Gamma_{D}\colon\text{$\sigma$ acts with a fixed point on
$S_{D}$}\}}{\#\Gamma_{D}}.

Henceforth, we take
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
to be the split quadric (
1.4
) and
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
to be the variety (
1.5
).
Consider the fibration
\pi\colon X\to Y
. There are precisely
4
codimension one points in
Y
which produce reducible fibres. These are the four lines

(2.2)

D_{i,j}=\{y_{i}=y_{j}=0\},\quad\text{for $i\in\{0,1\}$, $j\in\{2,3\}$}.

Above each of these the fibre of
\pi
is split by a quadratic extension, so that
\delta_{D_{i,j}}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}
. It follows that
\Delta(\pi)=4\cdot\frac{1}{2}=2

in (
2.1
).

However, the Jacobian of
X
is

J=\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}^{2}&x_{1}^{2}&x_{2}^{2}&x_{3}^{2}&2y_{0}x_{0}&2y_{1}x_{1}&2y_{2}x_{2}&2y_{3}x_{3}\\
y_{1}&y_{0}&-y_{3}&-y_{2}&0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
X
is singular, with singular locus supported on the union of subschemes

\Sigma_{i,j}=V(y_{i},y_{j},x_{i^{\prime}},x_{j^{\prime}},y_{j^{\prime}}x_{i}^{2}+y_{i^{\prime}}x_{j}^{2}),\quad\text{ for $\{i,i^{\prime}\}=\{0,1\}$ and $\{j,j^{\prime}\}=\{2,3\}$},

where we note that a choice of
i
and
j
uniquely determines
i^{\prime}
and
j^{\prime}
. In fact, away from the intersection of two such subschemes, the singular locus
\Sigma
is precisely the union of these four reduced subschemes.
Let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularisation of
X
and let
\widetilde{\pi}
be the composition with
\pi\colon X\to Y
. The main aim of this section is to prove that

(2.3)

\Delta(\widetilde{\pi})=2,

as claimed in
Theorem
1.2
.

We begin by establishing the following geometric lemma that will be useful in our treatment.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be two integral curves smooth and projective over a field
k
. Let us write
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
for their function fields. Consider a proper morphism

\rho\colon W\to C\times C^{\prime}.

If
W\times C^{\prime}_{\kappa}
is smooth over
\kappa
and

W\times{C}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is smooth over
\kappa^{\prime}
, then the image of the singular locus of
W/k
under
\rho
has dimension
0
.

Proof.

Consider the composite morphism

W\to C\times_{k}C^{\prime}\to C,

which has a smooth generic fibre by assumption. By spreading out
[
9
, Thm. 3.2.1]
, we see that
W_{U}\to U
is smooth for a dense open subset
U\subset C
. We conclude that
W_{U}\to U\to\Spec k
is smooth. Similarly, we have a
V\subset C^{\prime}
such that
W_{V}\to V
is smooth. We conclude that the image of the smooth locus of
W\to\Spec k
under
\rho
contains both
U\times C^{\prime}
and
C\times V
.
∎

It suffices to prove (
2.3
) for a single desingularisation.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X^{\prime}\to X
be the blowup in the union of the closed subschemes
\Sigma_{i,j}
. Let
\widetilde{X}\to X^{\prime}
be a fixed strong desingularisation.

We have seen that the image of the singular locus
\Sigma
under
X\to Y
is one-dimensional and supported on the union of lines
D_{i,j}\subset Y
in (
2.2
).
Let
\eta_{i,j}
be the generic point of the line
D_{i,j}.

We will prove that the image of the singular locus of
X^{\prime}
under
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
has lower dimension.

Lemma 2.3
.

The image of the singular locus
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
X^{\prime}
under
X^{\prime}\to Y
is (at most) zero-dimensional. The fibre
X^{\prime}_{\eta_{i,j}}
has four geometrically irreducible components, of which two make up the strict transform of
X_{\eta_{i,j}}
and two come from the exceptional divisor of the blowup
X^{\prime}\to X
.
These components are conjugated in pairs.

Proof.

We identify
Y
with
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where the projective lines have coordinates
u=\frac{y_{i}}{y_{j}}=\frac{y_{j^{\prime}}}{y_{i^{\prime}}}
and
t=\frac{y_{i}}{y_{j^{\prime}}}=\frac{y_{j}}{y_{i^{\prime}}}
, respectively. By Lemma
2.1
and symmetry we only need to show that
X^{\prime}\times_{Y}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
is smooth over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
. Again, by symmetry, we can restrict to the affine opens where
t\neq\infty
.

Since blowing up commutes with pulling back along flat morphisms we find that
X^{\prime}\times_{Y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
and the blowup of
X\times_{Y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
in the pullback of
\Sigma
are naturally isomorphic over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
. The three subschemes
\Sigma_{i^{\prime},j}
,
\Sigma_{i,j^{\prime}}
and
\Sigma_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
pull back to the empty scheme under
X\times_{Y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}\to X
and it will therefore suffice to compute the blowup of
X\times_{Y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
in the pullback of
\Sigma_{i,j}
.

We see that
X\times_{Y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}\times_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
is given by
utx_{i}^{2}+x_{i^{\prime}}^{2}+tx_{j}^{2}+ux_{j^{\prime}}^{2}=0
. The pullback of
\Sigma_{i,j}
equals
V(t,x_{i^{\prime}},x_{j^{\prime}},ux_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2})
, which is contained in the affine open given by
x_{i}\neq 0
. By abuse of notation we will use the same notation for the variables on this affine open.

We can compute the blowup
Z
of
\{ut+x_{i^{\prime}}^{2}+tx_{j}^{2}+ux_{j^{\prime}}^{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}\times_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
in the subscheme
V(t,x_{i^{\prime}},x_{j^{\prime}},u+x_{j}^{2})
explicitly. We find

Z=\Proj\mathbb{Q}(u)[t][x_{i^{\prime}},x_{j},x_{j^{\prime}}][A,B,C,D]/I

where the grading comes from
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
, and
I
is the ideal generated by the
2\times 2
-minors of

\begin{pmatrix}A&B&C&D\\
t&x_{i^{\prime}}&x_{j^{\prime}}&u+x^{2}_{j}\end{pmatrix},

together with the polynomials

ut+x_{i^{\prime}}^{2}+tx_{j}^{2}+ux_{j^{\prime}}^{2},\quad(x_{i^{\prime}}^{2}+ux_{j^{\prime}}^{2})B+tx_{i^{\prime}}D,\quad ux_{j^{\prime}}C+x_{i^{\prime}}B+tD,\quad AD+B^{2}+uC^{2}.

One can now directly check that
Z\to\Spec\mathbb{Q}(u)
is indeed smooth. Thus the first statement follows from Lemma
2.1
.

For the second part we note that the fibre of
Z\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}(u)}
over
t=0
is an affine open of
X^{\prime}_{\eta_{i,j}}
. It consists of the strict transform of
X_{\eta_{i,j}}
, which we already know to consist of two conjugate components. Any other component comes from the exceptional divisor, which we can compute from the equations above to be

\Proj\mathbb{Q}(u)[x_{j}][A,B,C,D]/(u+x_{j}^{2},AD+B^{2}+uC^{2}).

Hence,
X^{\prime}_{\eta_{i,j}}
has four geometrical components which are conjugated in pairs.
∎

It follows from this result
\delta_{D_{i,j}}(\pi^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, the singular locus of
X^{\prime}
does not meet the fibres
X^{\prime}_{\eta_{i,j}}
over the generic points
\eta_{i,j}
of the
D_{i,j}
. By using a strong desingularisation, which was proven to exist by Hironaka
[
5
]
, we can conclude that
\widetilde{X}_{\eta_{i,j}}\xrightarrow{\cong}X^{\prime}_{\eta_{i,j}}
over
Y
.
It therefore follows that

\delta_{D_{i,j}}(\widetilde{\pi})=\frac{1}{2},

which thereby completes the proof of (
2.3
), via (
2.1
).

3.
Analytic input

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.
We note that the map
\widetilde{X}\to X
is an isomorphism outside the locus of
y\in\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that

y_{0}\dots y_{3}=0
. Let
|\mathbf{y}|=\max_{0\leq i\leq 3}|y_{i}|
if
y\in\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{Q})
is represented by a vector

\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{prim}}^{4}
. The anticanonical height function on
Y(\mathbb{Q})
is then
H(y)=|\mathbf{y}|^{2}
.
Since there are
O(\sqrt{B})
choices of
y\in\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{Q})
of height at most
B
for which

y_{0}\dots y_{3}=0
, it follows that

N_{\text{loc}}(\widetilde{\pi},B)=N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)+O(\sqrt{B}),

where
\pi\colon X\to Y
.
For a given vector
\mathbf{y}
, let

X_{\mathbf{y}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the diagonal quadric

y_{0}x_{0}^{2}+y_{1}x_{1}^{2}+y_{2}x_{2}^{2}+y_{3}x_{3}^{2}=0.

Then

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)=\frac{1}{2}\#\left\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{prim}}^{4}\colon|\mathbf{y}|\leq\sqrt{B},~X_{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset\right\}.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it will suffice to prove that

(3.1)

B\ll N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)\ll B.

To see the lower bound, we take

\mathbf{y}=(u,v,-u,-v)
, for coprime
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|u|,|v|\leq\sqrt{B}
.
For such
\mathbf{y}
we obviously have

(1\colon 1\colon 1\colon 1)\in X_{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}).

It easily follows that

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)\gg B
.

Remark 3.1
.

The lower bound for
N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)
arises from points on the anti-diagonal line
D\subset Y
defined by the equations

y_{0}+y_{2}=y_{1}+y_{3}=0
. Consider the fibration
\pi_{D}\colon X_{D}\to D
.
The reducible fibres occur precisely above the closed points
y_{0}=0
and
y_{1}=0
in
D\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
However, the fibres above these points are split and so it follows from
(
2.1
) that

\Delta(\pi_{D})=0
. On the other hand
\rho_{D}=1
and so our lower bound is consistent with
(
1.3
), which would predict that

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi_{D},B)\sim cB
for an appropriate constant
c>0
.

It remains to prove the upper bound in (
3.1
).
Let us first dispatch the contribution to
N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)
from
\mathbf{y}
for which
y_{0}y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}=0
.
But then at least two components of
\mathbf{y}
must vanish and the fibre over any such point has an obvious rational point. There are

\frac{4}{\zeta(2)}B+O(\sqrt{B})

vectors
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{prim}}^{2}
with
|a|,|b|\leq B
.

We denote by
N^{\circ}(B)
the contribution from

\mathbf{y}
for which
y_{0}y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}\neq 0
. Then our work so far shows that

(3.2)

N_{\text{loc}}(\pi,B)=N^{\circ}(B)+\frac{8}{\zeta(2)}B+O(\sqrt{B}).

For each
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{4}
with
y_{0}y_{1}=y_{2}y_{3}
, there exist
\mathbf{t}=(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{4}
such that

y_{0}=t_{0}t_{2},\quad y_{1}=t_{1}t_{3},\quad y_{2}=t_{0}t_{3},\quad y_{3}=t_{1}t_{2}.

In this way it follows that

(3.3)

N^{\circ}(B)\ll\#\left\{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{4}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}|t_{0}t_{2}|,|t_{0}t_{3}|,|t_{1}t_{2}|,|t_{1}t_{3}|\leq\sqrt{B}\\
X_{t_{0}t_{2},t_{1}t_{3},t_{0}t_{3},t_{1}t_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset\end{array}\right\}.

We shall begin by studying a related counting function in which only square-free integers appear, before later deducing our final estimate for

N^{\circ}(B)
.

3.1.
Square-free coefficients

For given
U_{0},\dots,U_{3}>0
, let

\mathscr{U}=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}:U_{i}/2<|u_{i}|\leq U_{i}\text{ for $0\leq i\leq 3$}\right\}.

In this section we shall be concerned with estimating

(3.4)

N^{*}(U_{0},\dots,U_{3})=\#\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{U}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}X_{u_{0}u_{2},u_{1}u_{3},u_{0}u_{3},u_{1}u_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset\\
\mu^{2}(u_{0})\cdots\mu^{2}(u_{3})=1\end{array}\right\},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.
We shall prove the upper bound

(3.5)

N^{*}(U_{0},\dots,U_{3})\ll\frac{{U_{0}U_{1}U_{2}U_{3}}}{(1+\log U_{\text{min}})^{2}},

if
U_{\text{min}}=\min\{U_{0},\dots,U_{3}\}\geq 1
.

The upper bound
(
3.5
) is trivial if
1\leq U_{\text{min}}<2
and so we may proceed under the assumption that

U_{\text{min}}\geq 2
. Our primary tool will be the following
multidimensional form of the large sieve inequality, as worked out by Kowalski
[
6
, Thm. 4.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

X=\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\colon-N_{j}\leq n_{j}\leq N_{j}\text{ for $1\leq j\leq r$}\}

and let
\Omega_{p}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{r}
for all
p
. Then

\#\{x\in X\colon x\!\!\!\mod p\notin\Omega_{p}~\forall p\leq L\}\ll\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(N_{i}+L^{2})}{F(L)},

where

F(L)=\sum\limits_{n\leq L}\mu^{2}(n)\prod\limits_{p\mid n}\dfrac{|\Omega_{p}|}{p^{r}-|\Omega_{p}|}.

Define

\Omega_{p}=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{3}\Omega_{p,i},

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{p,0}=\{(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\colon r_{0}=0,\ r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\neq 0,\ -r_{2}r_{3}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times,2}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{p,1}=\{(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\colon r_{1}=0,\ r_{0}r_{2}r_{3}\neq 0,\ -r_{2}r_{3}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times,2}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{p,2}=\{(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\colon r_{2}=0,\ r_{0}r_{1}r_{3}\neq 0,\ -r_{0}r_{1}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times,2}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{p,3}=\{(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\colon r_{3}=0,\ r_{0}r_{1}r_{2}\neq 0,\ -r_{0}r_{1}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times,2}\}.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a vector counted by

N^{*}(U_{0},\dots,U_{3})
. The following result shows that

\mathbf{u}\bmod{p}\not\in\Omega_{p}
for any prime
p
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}
be such that

\mu^{2}(u_{0})\cdots\mu^{2}(u_{3})=1
. If

\mathbf{u}\bmod p\in\Omega_{p}
then

X_{u_{0}u_{2},u_{1}u_{3},u_{0}u_{3},u_{1}u_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

This is standard but we include a full proof for the sake of completeness.
Assume without loss of generality that
\mathbf{u}\bmod p\in\Omega_{p,0}
. We suppose for a contradiction that there exists
a solution
(x_{0}\colon x_{1}\colon x_{2}\colon x_{3})
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
to the equation

u_{0}u_{2}x_{0}^{2}+u_{1}u_{3}x_{1}^{2}+u_{0}u_{3}x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}u_{2}x_{3}^{2}=0.

We can assume that
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{4}
is primitive. Since
p\mid u_{0}
, it follows that

u_{1}u_{3}x_{1}^{2}+u_{1}u_{2}x_{3}^{2}\equiv 0\bmod p
. But
p\nmid u_{1}
and so

u_{3}x_{1}^{2}+u_{2}x_{3}^{2}\equiv 0\bmod p.

But
-u_{2}u_{3}
is a non-square modulo
p
and so
x_{1}\equiv x_{3}\equiv 0\bmod p
. Write
x_{1}=px_{1}^{\prime}
,
x_{3}=px_{3}^{\prime}
and
u_{0}=pu_{0}^{\prime}
,
where
p\nmid u_{0}^{\prime}
since

u_{0}
is square-free.
Then it follows that

u_{0}^{\prime}u_{2}x_{0}^{2}+pu_{1}u_{3}x_{1}^{\prime 2}+u_{0}^{\prime}u_{3}x_{2}^{2}+pu_{1}u_{2}x_{3}^{\prime 2}=0,

whence

u_{2}x_{0}^{2}+u_{3}x_{2}^{2}\equiv 0\bmod p
. But this implies that
x_{0}\equiv x_{2}\equiv 0\bmod p
, which contradicts the primitivity of
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
.
∎

In order to apply the large sieve we need to calculate the size of

\Omega_{p}
. But for
p>2
we clearly have

|\Omega_{p,i}|=(p-1)^{2}\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{(p-1)^{3}}{2},

for

0\leq i\leq 3
. Hence it follows that

(3.6)

|\Omega_{p}|=2p^{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{3}.

Lemma
3.2
implies that

N^{*}(U_{0},\dots,U_{3})\leq\#\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{U}\colon\mathbf{u}\bmod{p}\not\in\Omega_{p}~\forall p\leq L\right\},

for any
L\geq 1
. On appealing to
Lemma
3.1
, we conclude
that

(3.7)

N^{*}(U_{0},\dots,U_{3})\ll\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{3}(U_{i}+L^{2})}{F(L)},

where

\displaystyle F(L)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\leq L}\mu^{2}(n)\prod\limits_{p\mid n}\dfrac{|\Omega_{p}|}{p^{4}-|\Omega_{p}|}.

The following result gives a lower bound for this quantity.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have
F(L)\gg(\log L)^{2}
, for any
L\geq 2
.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\Omega_{p}|}{p^{4}-|\Omega_{p}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{2p^{3}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{3}}{p^{4}(1-\frac{2}{p}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{3})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{3}.

Hence

\displaystyle F(L)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n\leq L}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)\tau(n)\phi^{*}(n)^{3}}{n},

where
\tau(n)
is the divisor function and

\phi^{*}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

We now apply a standard result on the asymptotic evaluation of multiplicative arithmetic functions supported on square-free integers, as supplied by Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
3
, Thm. A.5]
.
Let
g(n)=\mu^{2}(n)\tau(n)\phi^{*}(n)^{3}/n
. Then

\sum_{p\leq x}g(p)\log p=2\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}+O(1)=2\log x+O(1),

by Mertens’ theorem. Moreover, the conditions (A.16) and (A.17) of

[
3
, Thm. A.5]
are easily verified. Hence, on taking
k=2
in this result,
it follows that

\sum_{n\leq L}g(n)\gg(\log L)^{2},

which thereby establishes the lemma.
∎

Let
U_{\text{min}}=\min\{U_{0},\dots,U_{3}\}
and recall that we are assuming that
U_{\text{min}}\geq 2
.
Inserting Lemma
3.3
into (
3.7
) and taking
L=\sqrt{U_{\text{min}}}
, we finally arrive at the upper bound (
3.5
).

3.2.
Deduction of the general case

We now turn to the estimation of
N^{\circ}(B)
, starting from (
3.3
).
Pick
T_{0},\dots,T_{3}
such that

(3.8)

T_{0},\dots,T_{3}\geq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{0}T_{2},T_{0}T_{3},T_{1}T_{2},T_{1}T_{3}\leq 4\sqrt{B}.

We shall examine the contribution
N(T_{0},\dots,T_{3})
, say, from those
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{4}
for which

T_{i}/2<|t_{i}|\leq T_{i}
, for
0\leq i\leq 3
.
Then

(3.9)

N^{\circ}(B)\ll\sum_{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}}N(T_{0},\dots,T_{3}),

where
T_{0},\dots,T_{3}
run over powers of
2
subject to the inequalities in (
3.8
).

Each non-zero integer
t_{i}
admits a factorisation
u_{i}m_{i}^{2}
for square-free
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Moreover,

X_{t_{0}t_{2},t_{1}t_{3},t_{0}t_{3},t_{1}t_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset\Longleftrightarrow X_{u_{0}u_{2},u_{1}u_{3},u_{0}u_{3},u_{1}u_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset.

Hence it follows that

N(T_{0},\dots,T_{3})\leq\sum_{m_{0}\leq\sqrt{T_{0}}}\dots\sum_{m_{3}\leq\sqrt{T_{3}}}N^{*}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{m_{0}^{2}},\dots,\frac{T_{3}}{m_{3}^{2}}\right),

in the notation of
(
3.4
). On appealing to (
3.5
), we deduce that

\displaystyle N(T_{0},\dots,T_{3})

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m_{0}\leq\sqrt{T_{0}}}\dots\sum_{m_{3}\leq\sqrt{T_{3}}}\sum_{j\in\{0,\dots,3\}}\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{m_{0}^{2}m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}m_{3}^{2}(1+\log(T_{j}/m_{j}^{2}))^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}\sum_{j\in\{0,\dots,3\}}\sum_{m\leq\sqrt{T_{j}}}\frac{1}{m^{2}(1+\log(T_{j}/m^{2}))^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{\text{min}})^{2}},

where
T_{\text{min}}=\min\{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}\}
.
Returning to
(
3.9
), we finally conclude that

N^{\circ}(B)\ll\sum_{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{\text{min}})^{2}},

where
T_{0},\dots,T_{3}
run over powers of
2
subject to the inequalities in (
3.8
).

By symmetry, we can suppose without loss of generality that

T_{0}\leq T_{1}
and
T_{2}\leq T_{3}
.
Summing first over
T_{1}\leq 4\sqrt{B}/T_{3}
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{\text{min}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sqrt{B}\sum_{T_{0},T_{2},T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{2}}{(1+\log\min\{T_{0},T_{2}\})^{2}}.

Suppose first that
T_{0}\leq T_{2}
. Then we sum over
T_{2}\leq T_{3}
and then
T_{3}\leq 4\sqrt{B}/T_{0}
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{\text{min}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sqrt{B}\sum_{T_{0},T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll B\sum_{T_{0}}\frac{1}{(1+\log T_{0})^{2}},

where the sum is over
T_{0}=2^{j}
with
1\leq 2^{j}\leq 2B^{1/4}
. This sum is convergent, whence this case contributes
O(B)
. Alternatively, if

T_{0}>T_{2}
, then we sum over
T_{0}\leq 4\sqrt{B}/T_{3}
and then
T_{3}\geq T_{2}

to get

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{0},\dots,T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{1}T_{2}T_{3}}{(1+\log T_{\text{min}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sqrt{B}\sum_{T_{0},T_{2},T_{3}}\frac{T_{0}T_{2}}{(1+\log T_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll B\sum_{T_{2},T_{3}}\frac{T_{2}}{T_{3}(1+\log T_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll B\sum_{T_{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\log T_{2})^{2}}.

This also makes the satisfactory contribution
O(B).

Hence we have shown that
N^{\circ}(B)=O(B).

Once inserted into (
3.2
), this therefore completes the proof of the upper bound in (
3.1
).

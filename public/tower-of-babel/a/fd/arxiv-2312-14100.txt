Dynamics on spaces of quasimorphisms and applications to approximate lattice theory
Michael Björklund, Tobias Hartnick

Abstract
. We study the dynamics of countable groups on their respective spaces of quasimorphisms. For cohomologically non-trivial quasimorphisms we show that there are no invariant measures and classify stationary measures. Within the equivalence class of any given quasimorphism we find both uniquely stationary orbit closures which are in fact boundaries and orbit closures with uncountably many ergodic stationary probability measures. We apply these results to study hulls of uniform approximate lattices which arise from twists by quasimorphisms. We show that these hulls do not admit invariant probability measures (extending results by Machado and Hrushovski) and classify stationary probability measures on these hulls.



1.
Introduction

This article is concerned with the classification of stationary measures on spaces of quasimorphisms. It is motivated from a problem in the theory of approximate lattices, and our results have applications to Delone dynamical systems arising in this context.

1.1.
Uniform approximate lattices without invariant measures

To state our original motivation we recall that a subset
\Lambda
of a locally compact group
G
is called an
approximate subgroup

[
12
]
if it is symmetric, contains the identity and satisfies
\Lambda\Lambda\subset\Lambda F
for some finite subset
F\subset G
. It is called a
uniform approximate lattice

[
5
]
if it is moreover a Delone set in
G
, i.e. uniformly discrete and relatively dense. Typical examples of uniform approximate lattices include uniform lattices as well as a large class of mathematical quasicrystals known as Meyer set.

With every uniform lattice
\Gamma\subset G
one can associate a dynamical system
G\curvearrowright G/\Gamma
, and this system admits a unique
G
-invariant probability measure. A natural generalization of this dynamical system to a uniform approximate lattice
\Lambda\subset G
is the so called
hull system

G\curvearrowright\Omega_{\Lambda}
, where
\Omega_{\Lambda}
denotes the orbit closure of
\Lambda
in the Chabauty space of closed subsets of
G
. By analogy with the lattice case we asked in
[
5
]
whether the hull of a uniform approximate lattice in
G
always admits a
G
-invariant probability measure.

A negative answer to this question follows from recent work of Hrushovski and Machado. More precisely, Hrushovski provided in
[
9
, Section 7.9]
an explicit example of a uniform approximate lattice which is non-laminar, and it was later shown by Machado
[
10
]
that the hull of a non-laminar uniform approximate lattice cannot admit an invariant probability measure. While this provides examples of uniform approximate lattices without invariant measures, the proof of this fact in
[
10
]
is very indirect.

In the present article we provide further examples of uniform approximate lattices whose hulls do not admit invariant probability measures, based on the following construction.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
P\subset\R
be a closed subset, let
\Gamma
be a countable group and let
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued quasimorphism. Then the
\varphi
-twist
of
P
is defined as the subset of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
given by

P(\varphi):=\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\mid\varphi(\gamma)+t\in P\}.

By elementary ergodic theoretic methods we can show:

Proposition 1.2
.

Assume that
P\subset\R
is closed and that
\R
acts freely on
\Omega_{P}
. If
\varphi:\Gamma\to\R
is a quasimorphism with
\varphi(e)=0
which is not at bounded distance from a homomorphism, then the orbit closure
\Omega_{P(\varphi)}
does not admit a
(\Gamma\times\R)
-invariant probability measure.

It is easy to see that if
P\subset\R
happens to be a uniform approximate lattice and
\varphi:\Gamma\to\Z
is integer-valued and anti-symmetric, then
P(\varphi)
is a uniform approximate lattice in
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
(see Corollary
7.3
). This produces plenty of examples of uniform approximate lattices whose hulls do not support invariant probability measure and motivates a more detailed study of
dynamical systems of the form
\Gamma\times\R\curvearrowright\Omega_{P(\varphi)}
.

1.2.
Stationary measures on hulls of twist sets

For the following results, let
\Gamma
be a countable group, let
P\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed subset such that the action of
\R
on
\Omega_{\R}
is free and uniquely ergodic. Moreover, let
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a quasimorphism with
\varphi(e)=0
, which is not at bounded distance from a homomorphism.

We fix an absolutely continuous symmetric probability measure
p
on
\Gamma\times\R
such that
\varphi
is
p
-integrable. We then denote by
p_{\Gamma}
the pushforwards of
p
to
\Gamma
and consider the space
\Prob_{p}(\Omega_{P(\varphi)})
of
p
-stationary probability measures on
\Omega_{P(\varphi)}
. We write
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi)
for the space of left-
p
-harmonic quasimorphisms which are at bounded distance from
\varphi
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\varphi
is left-
p
-harmonic, then
\Omega_{P(\varphi)}
admits a unique
p
-stationary probability measure
\nu
and
(\Omega_{P(\varphi)},\nu)
is a relatively measure-preserving extension over a non-trivial
(\Gamma,p_{\Gamma})
-boundary.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi)
and
\nu_{\psi}
denotes the unique
p
-stationary probability measure on
\Omega_{P(\psi)}
, then for every
\nu\in\Prob_{p}(\Omega_{P(\varphi)})
there exists a measure-preserving
\nu
-measurable map
(\Omega_{P(\varphi)},\nu)\to(\Omega_{P(\psi)},\nu_{\psi})
.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
\nu\in\Prob_{p}(\Omega_{P(\varphi)})
, then
(\Omega_{P(\varphi)},\nu)
admits a measurable
(\Gamma\times\mathbb{R})
-factor, which is a non-trivial
(\Gamma,p_{\Gamma})
-boundary.

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists a quasimorphism
\varphi^{\prime}
at bounded distance from
\varphi
such that
\Omega_{P(\varphi^{\prime})}
admits uncountably many ergodic
p
-stationary probability measures.

If
\Gamma
surjects onto
\mathbb{Z}
, then the quasimorphism
\varphi^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.6
can be chosen to be antisymmetric and to take integer values. This implies:

Theorem 1.7
.

If
\Gamma
surjects onto
\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a uniform approximate lattice in
\Gamma\times\R
which admits uncountably many ergodic
p
-stationary probabiliy measures, but no
(\Gamma\times\R)
-invariant probability measure. In particular, there exists such a uniform approximate lattice in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\R)
.

All of these results follow readily from corresponding results about dynamics of quasimorphisms, which we now explain.

1.3.
Relation to dynamics of quasimorphisms

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and equip
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma):=\{\varphi\in\R^{\Gamma}\mid\varphi(e)=0\}
with the product topology. Then
\Gamma
acts continuously on
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
via

(\gamma.\varphi)(\gamma^{\prime})=\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma),\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma,\enskip\varphi\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma).

(1.1)

We now fix a quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
which is not at bounded distance from a homomorphism. We then denote by
[\varphi_{o}]
the set of all quasimorphisms
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
which are at bounded distances from
\varphi_{o}
and by
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
the orbit closure of
\varphi_{o}
in
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
. We then have the following elementary observation:

Proposition 1.8
.

\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
is a compact metrizable subset of
[\varphi_{o}]
, which does not admit any
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure.

Proposition
1.2
is thus an immediate consequence of the following proposition; here given a
\Gamma
-space
\Delta
, an
\mathbb{R}
-space
\Omega
and a continuous cocyle
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\R
we denote by
\Delta\times_{c}\Omega
the skew product
\Delta\times\Omega
with
(\Gamma\times\mathbb{R})
-action given by

(\gamma,t)(\varphi,\omega):=(\gamma.\varphi,\omega+t+c(\gamma,\varphi))\quad(\gamma\in\Gamma,t\in\mathbb{R},\varphi\in\Delta,\omega\in\Omega).

Proposition 1.9
.

If
P\subset\mathbb{R}
is a closed subset such that
\R
acts freely on
\Omega_{P}
, then
\Omega_{P(\varphi_{o})}\cong\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\times_{c}\Omega_{P}
, where
c(\gamma,\varphi)=\varphi(\gamma)
. In particular,
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
is a continuous factor of
\Omega_{P(\varphi_{o})}
.

Concerning stationary measures we have the following result; here
p
is as in Subsection
1.2
.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed subset such that the
\R
-action on
\Omega_{P}
is free and uniquely ergodic with invariant measure
\theta
. Then the map

\Prob_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})\to\Prob_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\times_{c}\Omega_{P}),\quad\nu\mapsto\nu\otimes\theta

is bijective. In particular,
\Prob_{p}(\Omega_{P(\varphi_{o})})\cong\Prob_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})
.

To establish the results from Subsection
1.2
we thus need to analyze the
\Gamma
-action on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. This study is also of independent interest in the theory of quasimorphisms and will occupy most of the present article.

1.4.
The left-harmonic core of a quasimorphism

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and let
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a quasimorphism with
\varphi_{o}(e)=0
. We fix a symmetric probability measure
p
on
\Gamma
whose support generates
\Gamma
and such that
\varphi_{o}
is
p
-integrable. We recall that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})\subset[\varphi_{o}]
denote the subspace of left-
p
-harmonic quasimorphisms; note that this space is invariant under
\Gamma
.

Definition 1.11
.

The
left-
p
-harmonic core
of
\varphi_{o}
is the subset

\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o}):=\bigcap_{\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})}\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\subset\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})\subset[\varphi_{o}].

The following theorem implies that
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
is non-empty; it is the main technical result of this article and implies Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.12

(Stationary measures and left-harmonic core)
.

If the quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is not at bounded distance from a homomorphism, then the following hold:

(i)

Every
p
-stationary measure on
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
admits a unique
p
-stationary probability measure
\nu_{\varphi_{o}}
.

(iii)

(\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o}),\nu_{\varphi_{o}})
is a non-trivial
(\Gamma,p)
-boundary.

(iv)

If
\nu
is any
p
-stationary measure on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, then there exists a measure-preserving
\nu
-measurable map
(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}},\nu)\to(\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o}),\nu_{\varphi_{o}})
.

We complement this by the following result, which implies both Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Every quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\R
is equivalent to a quasimorphism
\varphi_{1}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\Delta_{\varphi_{1}}
admits uncountably many
p
-stationary and
\Gamma
-ergodic probability measures. If
\Gamma
surject onto
\Z
, then
\varphi_{1}
can be chosen to be symmetric.

2.
Preliminaries on boundary theory

Throughout this section let let
\Gamma
be a countable group and let
p\in\mathrm{Prob}
be a symmetric probability measure on
\Gamma
whose support generates
\Gamma
; we then refer to
(\Gamma,p)
as a
symmetric measured group
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p}=p^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
the induced measure on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
; the
sequence
(Z_{n})
of
\Gamma
-valued random variables given by

Z_{n}:(\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}},\mathbb{P}_{p})\to\Gamma,\quad(\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}.

is called the associated
random walk
.

A function
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma\mapsto f(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})
(respectively
\gamma\mapsto f(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)
is
p
-integrable for every
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
is called
left-
p
-harmonic
(respectively
right-
p
-harmonic
) if
p\ast f=f
(respectively
f\ast p=f
). We denote by
\mathscr{H}_{l}^{\infty}(\Gamma,p)
(respectively
\mathscr{H}_{r}^{\infty}(\Gamma,p)
the spaces of all bounded left-
p
-harmonic (respectively bounded right-
p
-harmonic) functions. If
B
is a standard Borel space with an action of
\Gamma
by Borel automorphisms, then a Borel probability measure
m
on
B
is called
p
-stationary
if
p\ast m=m
; in this case we call
(B,m)
a
(\Gamma,p)
-space
. We say that a
(\Gamma,p)
-space
(B,m)
is
ergodic
if
m
is
\Gamma
-ergodic.

Given a standard Borel space
B
with an action of
\Gamma
by Borel automorphisms, we denote by
\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(B)
the space of
p
-stationary Borel probability measures on
B
. We write
\Prob_{p}^{\textrm{erg}}(B)
and
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma}(B)
for the subspaces of all
\Gamma
-ergodic, respectively
\Gamma
-invariant measures in
\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(B)
. For the following classical statement see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 2.13]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

For every symmetric measured group
(\Gamma,p)
there exists an ergodic
(\Gamma,p)
-space
(B,m)
such that the following hold:

(i)

The Poisson transform
\mathscr{P}_{m}:L^{\infty}(B,m)\rightarrow\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{r}(\Gamma,p)
defined by

\mathscr{P}_{m}f(\gamma)=\int_{B}f(\gamma.b)\,dm(b)\quad(\gamma\in\Gamma)

is a surjective isometry.

(ii)

For every countable subset
\mathcal{A}\subset L^{\infty}(B,m)
, there exist a
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-conull Borel set
\Omega\subset\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
and a Borel map
Z_{\infty}:\Omega\rightarrow B
such that

(Z_{\infty})_{*}\mathbb{P}_{p}|_{\Omega}=m\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{P}_{m}f(Z_{n}(\omega))=f(Z_{\infty}(\omega)),

for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

Furthermore,
(B,m)
is unique up to measure-preserving
\Gamma
-isomorphisms.

The space
(B,m)
is called the
Poisson boundary
of the measured group
(\Gamma,p)
. If
(B,m)
is the Poisson boundary of
(\Gamma,p)
and
\pi:B\to B^{\prime}
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant Borel map, then
(B^{\prime},\pi_{*}m)
is called a
(\Gamma,p)
-boundary.
Note that every
(\Gamma,p)
-boundary is
\Gamma
-ergodic. Finally, a
(\Gamma,p)
-boundary
(B^{\prime},m^{\prime})
is called
trivial
if
m^{\prime}=\delta_{b^{\prime}}
for some
\Gamma
-fixed point
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, and
non-trivial
otherwise.

3.
Hulls of quasimorphisms

Throughout this section, let
\Gamma
be a countable group.

3.1.
Quasimorphisms

Definition 3.1
.

The
defect set
of a function
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathscr{D}(\varphi):=\{\varphi(gh)-\varphi(g)-\varphi(h)\mid g,h\in\Gamma\}.

A function
\varphi
is called a
quasimorphism
if
\mathscr{D}(\varphi)
is bounded; in this case the number

D(\varphi):=\sup_{g,h\in\Gamma}|\varphi(gh)-\varphi(g)-\varphi(h)|

is called the
defect
of
\varphi
.

In this article we will only consider quasimorphisms which are
normalized
in the sense that
\varphi(e)=0
. Two quasimorphisms
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
with
\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}<\infty
are said to be
equivalent
. Every quasimorphism
\varphi
is equivalent to a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued quasimorphism, e.g.
\lfloor\varphi\rfloor
, hence to a quasimorphism with a
finite
defect set. A quasimorphism is called
cohomologically trivial
if it is equivalent to a homomorphism.

Remark 3.2

(Antisymmetric quasimorphism)
.

We say that a quasimorphism is
antisymmetric
if
\varphi(\gamma^{-1})=-\varphi(\gamma)
. If
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is a quasimorphism and
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is any relatively dense subset, then we can always find an antisymmetric quasimorphism
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
which takes values in
A-A
and is equivalent to
\varphi_{o}
. For example, if
A=\mathbb{Z}
we may take

\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z},\quad\varphi(\gamma):=\lfloor\varphi_{o}(\gamma)/2\rfloor-\lfloor\varphi_{o}(\gamma^{-1})/2\rfloor.

3.2.
Hulls and invariant measures

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
with the product topology and consider the continuous action of
\Gamma
on

\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma):=\{\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}\mid\varphi(e)=0\}.

given by (
1.1
). The orbit closure of
\varphi_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
will be denoted by
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and referred to as the
hull
of
\varphi_{o}
. By definition of the product topology, point evaluation defines a continuous map
c:\Gamma\times\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
,
(h,\varphi)\mapsto\varphi(h)
. This map is a

normalized
\Gamma
-cocycle
on
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
, i.e. for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
we have

c(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\varphi)=c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.\varphi)+c(\gamma_{2},\varphi)\text{ and }c(e,\varphi)=0.

(3.1)

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a normalized quasimorphism.

(i)

The hull
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
is a compact metrizable space.

(ii)

For all
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
we have
\|\varphi-\varphi_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\varphi_{o})
. In particular, the hull
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
consists of quasimorphisms which are equivalent to
\varphi_{o}
.

(iii)

If
\varphi_{o}
is cohomologically non-trivial, then
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Pick
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and choose a sequence
(\gamma_{n})
in
\Gamma

such that
\gamma_{n}.\varphi_{o}\rightarrow\varphi
in
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
we obtain

\|(\gamma_{n}.\varphi_{o})(\gamma)-\varphi_{o}(\gamma)\|=\|\varphi_{o}(\gamma\gamma_{n})-\varphi_{o}(\gamma_{n})-\varphi_{o}(\gamma)\|\leq D(\varphi_{o}),

which implies (ii) and shows that

\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\subseteq(\varphi_{o}+\overline{B}(0,{D(\varphi_{o})})^{\Gamma})\cap\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma).

(3.2)

It thus follows from Tychonoff’s theorem that
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
is relatively compact (and hence compact and metrizable) with respect to the product topology. This proves (i).

Now assume that
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
admits a
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure
\mu
. By (
3.2
) the integral

\psi(\gamma)=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,d\mu(\varphi)

converges and satisfies
\|\psi-\varphi_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\varphi_{o})
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Moreover, invariance of
\mu
yields

\displaystyle\psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\,d\mu(\varphi)=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\gamma_{2}.\varphi(\gamma_{1})+\varphi(\gamma_{2})\,d\mu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma_{1})\,d\mu(\varphi)+\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma_{2})\,d\mu(\varphi)=\psi(\gamma_{1})+\psi(\gamma_{2}),

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, which shows that
\psi\in\Hom(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})
and hence
\varphi
is cohomologically trivial. We thus obtain (iii) by contraposition.
∎

3.3.
Harmonic quasimorphisms

From now on let
(\Gamma,p)
be a symmetric measured group and let
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a
p
-integrable quasimorphism. Note that integrability holds automatically if
p
is finitely supported or if
\Gamma
is finitely-generated and the word length with respect to some finite generating set is
p
-integrable. We will write

\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})=\{\psi\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)\mid p\ast\psi=\psi,\;\|\psi-\varphi_{o}\|_{\infty}<\infty\}

for the space of left-harmonic quasimorphisms at bounded distance from
\varphi_{o}
and
\mathcal{R}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
for the space of right-harmonic quasimorphisms at bounded distance from
\varphi_{o}
. To show that these spaces are non-empty we can make use of a
p
-stationary probability measure on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. Note that such measures exist in view of Proposition
3.3
.(i).

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
\nu\in\Prob_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})
the function
\psi_{\nu}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\psi_{\nu}(\gamma):=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,d\nu(\varphi),\quad\gamma\in\Gamma.

satisfies
\psi_{\nu}\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}_{p}(\varphi_{o})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
.(ii) we have
\|\psi_{\nu}-\varphi_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\varphi_{o})
. Moreover, since since
p
is symmetric and
\nu
is
p
-stationary, we have

\displaystyle(\psi_{\nu}*p)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p(\gamma^{\prime})\psi_{\nu}(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p(\gamma^{\prime})\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p(\gamma^{\prime})\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}((\gamma^{\prime}.\varphi)(\gamma)+\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. If we abbreviate

\ell_{\nu}(n):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\varphi)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

then this can be written as

\psi_{\nu}*p=\psi_{\nu}+\ell_{\nu}(1),

and we thus have to show that
\ell_{\nu}(1)=0
. Note that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\ell_{\nu}(m+n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma}p^{*m}(\gamma_{1})p^{*n}(\gamma_{2})\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\,d\nu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma}p^{*m}(\gamma_{1})p^{*n}(\gamma_{2})\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}(\gamma_{2}.\varphi(\gamma_{1})+\varphi(\gamma_{2}))\,d\nu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\ell_{\nu}(m)+\ell_{\nu}(n)

by
p
-stationarity of
\nu
. In particular,
\ell_{\nu}(n)=n\ell_{\nu}(1)
and thus it remains to show only that the sequence
(\ell_{\nu}(n))
is bounded.

By Remark
3.2
there exists an antisymmetric quasimorphism
\eta_{o}
which is at bounded distance from
\varphi_{o}
. By Proposition
3.3
.(ii) there then exists a constant

such that
\|\varphi-\eta_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq M
for all
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. Moreover,
\eta_{o}
is
p
-integrable, since
\varphi_{o}
is. Since
\eta_{o}
is antisymmetric and

is symmetric, we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\eta_{o}(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma^{-1})\eta_{o}(\gamma^{-1})=-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\eta_{o}(\gamma),

hence
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\eta_{o}(\gamma)=0
for all

. We conclude that

\displaystyle|\ell_{\nu}(n)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}\varphi(\gamma)\,d\nu(\varphi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\eta_{o}(\gamma)+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma)\int_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}(\varphi-\eta_{o})(\gamma)\,d\nu(\varphi)\right|\leq M

4.2.
Ergodic joinings of stationary measures

We keep the notation of Theorem
4.2
. Moreover, we fix
\psi_{o}^{(1)},\psi_{o}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
. We abbreviate
\Delta_{i}:=\Delta_{\psi_{o}^{(i)}}

and denote by
\mathrm{pr_{i}}:\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}\to\Delta_{i}
the coordinate projections. Since
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

are compact we can find
\nu_{i}\in\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(\Delta_{i})

\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
the space

M:=\{\eta\in\Prob(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\mid\mathrm{pr_{1}}_{*}\eta=\nu_{1}\text{ and }\mathrm{pr_{2}}_{*}\eta=\nu_{2}\}

is weak*-compact, convex and
p
-invariant, hence admits a
p
-invariant element, which we may assume to be
\Gamma
-ergodic. We refer to such a probability measure
\eta
as an
ergodic joining
of
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\nu_{i}\in\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(\Delta_{i})
and
\eta
is an ergodic joining of
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
, then
for
\eta

(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}
we have
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}

Proof.

We first observe that if
\psi_{i}\in\Delta_{i}
for

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
(and hence
\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}

p
-harmonic (see Construction

) and
\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq M

\beta:\Gamma\times(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\to\mathbb{R}

\beta(\gamma,(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})):=\psi_{1}(\gamma)-\psi_{2}(\gamma)
is uniformly bounded. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
(and since
\mathbb{R}
has no non-trivial compact subgroups) it is thus equivalent to the trivial cocycle, i.e. there exists a Borel function
u:\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\beta(\gamma,(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))=u(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-u(\gamma.(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))

for
\eta
-almost all
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
and all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. For
M_{1}>M_{o}>0
we consider the sets

A:=\{x\in\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}\mid|u(x)|<M_{o}\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad B:=\{x\in\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}\mid|u(x)|>M_{1}\}.

Then
\eta(A)>0
for all sufficiently large
M_{o}
, and if
u
is not essentially bounded, then also
\eta(B)>0
for all
M_{1}>M_{o}
. In this case the ergodic theorem for stationary actions
[

yields

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma)\eta(A\cap\gamma^{-1}.B)\to\eta(A)\eta(B)>0,

hence there exists some
\gamma\in\Gamma
with

. For any
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in A\cap\gamma^{-1}.B

|\beta(\gamma,(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))|=|u(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-u(\gamma.(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))|>M_{1}-M_{o},

contradicting boundedness of
\beta
. Thus
u\in L^{\infty}(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2},\eta)
is essentially bounded. On the other hand, for
\eta
-almost all
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
we have (by left-

-harmonicity of
\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p(\gamma)(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p(\gamma)\beta(\gamma,(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}p(\gamma)(u(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-u(\gamma.(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))=u(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-p\ast u(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),

i.e.
u
is an essentially bounded left-
p
-harmonic function. By

[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
, this implies that
u
is essentially
\Gamma
-invariant, and since
\eta
is ergodic, it follows that

is essentially constant. This implies that
\beta(\gamma,(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))=0
for
\eta
-almost all

\psi_{1}(\gamma)=\psi_{2}(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and for
\eta
-almost all
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
.
∎

4.3.
Unique stationarity

From Lemma

Proof of Theorem
4.2

Firstly, if

Now let

We now turn to the proof of Part (iii) of Theorem

If
\psi_{o}\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(\varphi_{o})

We now work towards the proof of Lemma
4.4

, and denote by
(B,m)
the Poisson boundary of the symmetric measured group
(\Gamma,p)
(as in Lemma

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a Borel function
\alpha:\Gamma\times B\to\R
with the following properties:

(i)

\alpha
is a (strict) Borel cocycle, i.e.

\alpha(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},b)=\alpha(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.b)+\alpha(\gamma_{2},b)\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma$ and $b\in B$},

and

\psi_{o}(\gamma)=\int_{B}\alpha(\gamma,b)\;\mathrm{d}m(b),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

(ii)

The map
\pi_{\alpha}:B\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
given by
\pi_{\alpha}(b)(\gamma):=\alpha(\gamma,b)
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant Borel map.

(iii)

\|\pi_{\alpha}(b)-\psi_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\psi_{o})
for all
b\in B
. In particular, for every
b\in B
the function
\pi_{\alpha}(b)
is a quasimorphism on
\Gamma
which
is equivalent to
\psi_{o}
.

Proof.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
by
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\prime}):=\psi_{o}(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})-\psi_{o}(\gamma^{\prime})
. Note that

\|\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\psi_{o})+|\psi_{o}(\gamma)|,\quad\textrm{
for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

Since
\psi_{o}
is right-
p
-harmonic,
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{r}(\Gamma,p)

2.1

f_{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(B,m)

\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}=\|\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}=\mathscr{P}_{m}f_{\gamma}
. For each
\gamma

g_{\gamma}
such that
f_{\gamma}=g_{\gamma}

m

|g_{\gamma}(b)|\leq\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\infty},\quad\textrm{for all $b\in B$ and $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

Define
\beta:\Gamma\times B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\beta(\gamma,b)=g_{\gamma}(b)
. We claim that for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
,

\beta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},b)=\beta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.b)+\beta(\gamma_{2},b),\quad\textrm{for $m$-almost every $b\in B$}.

(4.1)

If (
4.1
) holds, then, by
[
14
, Proposition B.9]
, there exists a Borel map

\alpha:\Gamma\times B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

\alpha(\gamma,b)=\beta(\gamma,b)
for all
m
-almost every
b\in B
, and

\alpha(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},b)=\alpha(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.b)+\alpha(\gamma_{2},b),\quad\textrm{for every $b\in B$}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\int_{B}\alpha(\gamma,b)\,dm(b)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\beta(\gamma,b)\,dm(b)=\int_{B}g_{\gamma}(b)\,dm(b)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}f_{\gamma}(b)\,dm(b)=\mathscr{P}_{m}f_{\gamma}(e)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}(e)=\psi_{o}(\gamma),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

This proves (i), modulo (
4.1

\mathscr{P}_{m}
is a surjective isometry, it is enough to show
that for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, we have

\mathscr{P}_{m}\beta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\gamma\cdot)=\mathscr{P}_{m}\beta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\gamma\cdot)+\mathscr{P}_{m}\beta(\gamma_{2},\gamma\cdot),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$},

or, equivalently, since
g_{\gamma}=f_{\gamma}

m
-almost everywhere,

\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}(\gamma)=\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2}\gamma)+\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma$}.

Unwrapping definitions, we see that this amounts to proving

\psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma)-\psi(\gamma)=(\psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma)-\psi(\gamma_{2}\gamma))+(\psi(\gamma_{2}\gamma)-\psi(\gamma)),

for all
\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, which is trivial, and thus (
4.1
) follows.

(ii) For all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
and

we have, using that
\alpha

is a strict cocycle,

\displaystyle(\pi_{\alpha}(\gamma.b))(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma.b)=\alpha(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma,b)-\alpha(\gamma,b)=(\gamma.\pi_{\alpha}(b))(\gamma^{\prime}),

so

is
\Gamma
-equivariant. Borel measurability is immediate.

(iii) For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let
h_{\gamma}(b)=\alpha(\gamma,b)-\psi_{o}(\gamma)
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{m}h_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\prime})=\int_{B}(\alpha(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}.b)-\psi_{o}(\gamma))\,dm(b)=\psi_{o}(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})-\psi_{o}(\gamma^{\prime})-\psi_{o}(\gamma),

for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. In particular, since
\mathscr{P}_{m}
is an isometry,

\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\|h_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}|\psi_{o}(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})-\psi_{o}(\gamma^{\prime})-\psi_{o}(\gamma)|=D(\psi_{o})<\infty.

In particular, for every

, we have
|\alpha(\gamma,b)-\varphi_{o}(\gamma)|\leq D(\psi_{o})
for all

, so

is indeed equivalent to
\psi_{o}
.
∎

To finish the proof of Lemma
4.4
it now suffices to establish the following:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\alpha
as in Lemma
4.5
and define
\nu_{\psi_{o}}:=(\pi_{\alpha})_{*}m\in\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma))
.

(i)

\mathrm{supp}(\nu_{\psi_{o}})\subset\Delta_{\psi_{o}}
, and hence
(\Delta_{\psi_{o}},\nu_{\psi_{o}})
is a
(\Gamma,p)
-boundary.

(ii)

If
\nu_{\psi_{o}}=\delta_{\psi}
for some
\psi\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
, then
\psi_{o}
(hence
\varphi_{o}
) is cohomologically trivial.

Proof.

(i) We consider the countable subset
\mathcal{A}=\{\alpha(\gamma,\cdot)\,:\,\gamma\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(B,m)
. By Lemma
2.1
we may fix a
p^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
-conull subset
\Omega\subset\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}

and a Borel map
Z_{\infty}:\Omega\rightarrow B
such that
(Z_{\infty})_{*}p^{\mathbb{N}_{o}}=m|_{\Omega}
and

f(Z_{\infty}(\omega))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}f(Z_{n}(\omega))\quad\textrm{for all $\omega\in\Omega$ and $f\in\mathcal{A}$}.

We then deduce from Lemma
4.5
that for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

\alpha(\gamma,Z_{\infty}(\omega))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B}\alpha(\gamma,Z_{n}(\omega).b)\,dm(b)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Z_{n}(\omega).\psi_{o}(\gamma).

(4.2)

We now fix
\psi\in\supp(\nu_{\psi_{o}})
. For every finite set
F\subset\Gamma
and
\varepsilon>0
we set

U_{F,\varepsilon}=\{\psi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)\,:\,|\psi^{\prime}(\gamma)-\psi(\gamma)|<\varepsilon,\enskip\textrm{for all $\gamma\in F$}\}.

We then define
V_{F,\varepsilon}=\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{F,\varepsilon})
and
W_{F,\varepsilon}=Z_{\infty}^{-1}(V_{F,\varepsilon})
. Since
U_{F,\varepsilon}
is an open neighbourhood of
\psi
and
\psi\in\supp(\nu_{\psi_{o}})
we then have

p^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}(W_{F,\varepsilon})=m(V_{F,\varepsilon})=\nu_{\psi_{o}}(U_{F,\varepsilon})>0.

(4.3)

Note that explicitly we have

W_{F,\varepsilon}=Z_{\infty}^{-1}(V_{F,\varepsilon})=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid|\alpha(\gamma,Z_{\infty}(\omega))-\psi(\gamma)|<\varepsilon,\enskip\textrm{for all $\gamma\in F$}\}.

By (
4.2
) and (

) we now find for eery finite set
F\subset\Gamma
and every

an index

and a subset

such that

p^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}(\Omega_{n}\cap W_{F,\varepsilon})>0\quad\textrm{and}\quad|\alpha(\gamma,Z_{\infty}(\omega))-Z_{n}(\omega).\psi_{o}(\gamma)|<\varepsilon\;\textrm{for all $\gamma\in F$ and $\omega\in\Omega_{n}$}.

For all
\omega\in\Omega_{n}\cap W_{F,\varepsilon}

We now fix an increasing exhaustion of
\Gamma
by finite sets
(F_{k})

and
\varepsilon_{k}=1/k
for every

. This produces a sequence
(n_{k}=n_{k}(F_{k},1/k))
of positive integers and a corresponding sequence
\Omega_{n_{k}}\subset\Omega

\Gamma

(ii) If

. By Lemma

.(iii) this implies that

. If we set

. Consequently,

is a homomorphism, hence cohomologically trivial.

.

5.1.

Throughout this section let

If we assume that

:

If
\varphi_{o}
is not assume to be left-
p
-harmonic, then the space
\mathrm{Prob}_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})
is much harder to understand. Nevertheless we can get a partial understanding of
p
-stationary measures on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
by relating them to the unique
p
-stationary measure
\nu_{\varphi_{o}}
on the left-
p
-harmonic core
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
of
\varphi_{o}
(cf. Theorem
4.2
). In fact we will show that for every (not necessarily
\Gamma
-ergodic)
p
-stationary probability measure
\nu

on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, there is a
\nu
-almost everywhere defined Borel
\Gamma
-map on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
which maps into the core
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
and takes
\nu

to
\nu_{\varphi_{o}}
:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\nu
be a
p
-stationary probability measure on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. Then there
is a
\nu
-conull and
\Gamma
-invariant Borel set
\Delta_{o}\subset\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and a Borel
\Gamma
-map
\pi:\Delta_{o}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
such that
\pi_{*}(\nu|_{\Delta_{o}})=\nu_{\varphi_{o}}
.

Remark 5.3
.

We will in fact prove that the
\Gamma
-map
\pi
can be everywhere defined on the hull
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, but only as a
universally measurable

\Gamma
-map. Our construction of
\pi
can be done in quite a straightforward manner if one is willing to assume the existence of so called Mokbodski means on
\mathbb{N}
(see Lemma
5.5
below). Unfortunately, these means cannot be constructed within
ZFC, but requires us to adopt the Martin axiom. However, with a little bit more effort, the construction can be carried out within ZFC, and we refer to the reader to
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
where a similar construction is made using Komlos’ theorem (and thus entirely within ZFC).

5.2.
Universally measurable sets and Mokobodski means

Before we prove Theorem
5.2
we recall some basic definitions concerning universal measurability and Mokobodski means.

Remark 5.4

(Universally measurable sets)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B}_{X})
be a measurable space. If
\mu
is a probability measure on
\mathscr{B}_{X}
, let
\mathscr{B}_{X}^{\mu}
denote the
\mu
-completion of
\mathscr{B}_{X}
, i.e. the
\sigma
-algebra on
X
generated by
\mathscr{B}_{X}
and all subsets of
\mu
-null sets in
\mathscr{B}_{X}
. For instance, if
\mu=\delta_{x}
for some
x\in X
, then
\mathscr{B}_{X}^{\mu}
consists of all subsets of
X
. We define

\mathscr{B}_{X}^{*}=\bigcap_{\mu\in\Prob(X)}\mathscr{B}_{X}^{\mu}.

The elements in
\mathscr{B}_{X}^{*}
are said to be
universally measurable
. If
(Y,\mathscr{B}_{Y})
is another measurable space, then a map
f:X\to Y
is called
universally measurable
if it is
\mathscr{B}_{X}^{*}
-
\mathscr{B}_{Y}
-measurable.

The following result is essentially due to Mokobodski
[
11
, Section 2]
(see
[
4
, Proposition A.1]
for this particular version).

Lemma 5.5
.

There is an invariant mean
\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathbb{N}
with the property that whenever

(X,\mathscr{B}_{X})
is a measurable space and
(f_{n})
is a uniformly bounded sequence
of
\mathscr{B}_{X}
-measurable functions on
X
, then the function

f^{*}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n),\quad x\in X,

is
\mathscr{B}_{X}^{*}
-measurable and moreover, the equality

\int_{X}f^{*}(x)\,d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{X}f_{n}(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)

holds for every Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X
.

Any invariant mean
\mathfrak{m}
as in Lemma
5.5
is called a
Mokobodski mean
.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
5.2

The following lemma is a more precise version of Theorem
5.2
.

Lemma 5.6
.

In the situation of Theorem
5.2
, let
\mathfrak{m}
be a Mokobodski mean. Gicen
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
we define

\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}p^{*n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}))\right)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n).

(5.1)

Then the following hold:

(i)

(
5.1
) defines a
\Gamma
-equivariant and universally measurable map
\pi:\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
. Furthermore,

\|\pi(\varphi)-\varphi_{o}\|_{\infty}\leq 2D(\varphi_{o}),\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}$},

and thus
\pi(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})\subset\varphi_{o}+[-2D(\varphi_{o}),2D(\varphi_{o})]^{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\overline{\pi(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})}\subset\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
is a separable metrizable space.

(ii)

For every
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, the function
\gamma\mapsto\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)
is a left-
p
-harmonic quasimorphism which is equivalent to
\varphi_{o}
. In particular,
\pi(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})
is contained in the left-
p
-harmonic core
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
.

(iii)

For every
p
-stationary probability measure
\nu
on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
,
there is a
\nu
-conull
\Gamma
-invariant Borel set
\Delta_{o}\subset\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
such that
\pi|_{\Delta_{o}}:\Delta_{o}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
is a Borel
\Gamma
-map and
\pi_{*}(\nu|_{\Delta_{o}})=\varphi_{\varphi_{o}}
, where
\nu_{\varphi_{o}}
denotes the unique
p
-stationary probability measure on
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
.

Proof.

(i) For fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define

\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{*n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})).

Note that
\|\pi_{n}(\cdot)(\gamma)\|_{\infty}\leq 2D(\varphi_{o})+|\varphi_{o}(\gamma)|
for all
n
, and thus
(\pi_{n}(\cdot)(\gamma))
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\mathscr{B}_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}
-measurable functions on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
.
Hence

\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n),

is well-defined for all
(\varphi,\gamma)\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\times\Gamma
and
\varphi\mapsto\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)
is a
\mathscr{B}_{\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}}^{*}
-measurable function on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
. It readily follows that
\pi
is universally measurable as a function from
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
to
\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
. To show that
\pi
is
\Gamma
-equivariance, it suffices to check that
\pi_{n}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant for every
n
. Note that

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(\gamma_{o}.\varphi)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\gamma_{o}.\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)-\gamma_{o}.\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\gamma_{o})-\varphi(\gamma_{o})-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{o})+\varphi(\gamma_{o}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\gamma_{o})-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{o})+\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma\gamma_{o})-\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma_{o})=(\gamma_{o}.\pi_{n}(\varphi))(\gamma),

for all
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and
\gamma_{o},\gamma\in\Gamma
, and thus
\pi_{n}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant. (ii)
To see that
\gamma\mapsto\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)
is left-
p
-harmonic, we compute

\displaystyle(p\ast\pi_{n}(\varphi))(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{o}\in\Gamma}p(\gamma_{o})\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma_{o}^{-1}\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma_{o}\in\Gamma}\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p(\gamma_{o})p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{o}^{-1}\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast(n+1)}(\gamma^{\prime})\varphi(\gamma^{\prime\prime}\gamma)-\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\pi_{n+1}(\varphi)(\gamma)+r_{n+1}(\varphi)-r_{n}(\varphi),

where
r_{n}(\varphi):=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})
. We claim that
(r_{n})
is a uniformly bounded sequence of functions on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. Indeed, let
\eta_{o}
denote the homogeneous quasi-morphism associated with
\varphi_{o}
. Then
\|\varphi-\eta_{o}\|_{\infty}<\infty
for all
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
and

\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})\eta_{o}(\gamma^{\prime})=0,

since
p
is symmetric and
\eta_{o}
is anti-symmetric. Hence, for all
n
,

|r_{n}(\varphi)|=\left|\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}p^{\ast n}(\gamma^{\prime})(\varphi-\eta_{o})(\gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq D(\varphi_{o})+\|\varphi_{o}-\eta_{o}\|_{\infty},\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}$}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}
is invariant and
(r_{n}(\varphi))
is bounded, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{N}}(p*\pi_{n}(\varphi))(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{N}}(\pi_{n+1}(\varphi))(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)+\int_{\mathbb{N}}(r_{n+1}(\varphi)-r_{n}(\varphi))\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\pi(\varphi)(\gamma),\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}$ and $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

It remains to show that

\int_{\mathbb{N}}(p*\pi_{n}(\varphi))(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)=(p*\pi(\varphi))(\gamma),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

(5.2)

To do this, fix
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, and note that
u_{n}(\gamma)=\pi_{n}(\varphi)(\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma)
satisfies
\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq D(\varphi_{o})

for all
n
, and

u_{\infty}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}u_{n}(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)=\pi(\varphi)(\gamma)-\varphi(\gamma),\quad\gamma\in\Gamma

In particular,

(p*u_{\infty})(\gamma)=(p*\pi(\varphi))(\gamma)-(p*\varphi)(\gamma),\quad\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

Furthermore, by the second property of
\mathfrak{m}
in Lemma
5.5
, applied with
X=\Gamma
and
\mu=p*\delta_{\gamma}
for fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we see that

(p*u_{\infty})(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}(p*u_{n})(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)=\int_{\mathbb{N}}(p*\pi_{n}(\varphi))(\gamma)\,d\mathfrak{m}(n)-(p*\varphi)(\gamma).

Comparing the last two expression proves (
5.2
).
(iii) Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
.
Since
\pi
is a universally measurable map from
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
into a separable metric space by (i),
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
tells us that we can find a
\nu
-conull Borel set
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
such that
\pi|_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is Borel. If
\nu
in addition is
p
-stationary, then
\nu
is
\Gamma
-quasi-invariant, and thus
\Delta_{o}=\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma.\Delta^{\prime}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant
\nu
-conull Borel set, contained in
\Delta^{\prime}
, so clearly the restriction of
\pi
to
\Delta_{o}
is again Borel.
∎

6.
Hulls with many stationary measure

Let
(\Gamma,p)
be a symmetric measured group and let
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a
p
-integrable quasimorphism.
We have seen in Corollary
5.1
that if
\varphi_{o}
is left-
p
-harmonic, then there is a unique
p
-stationary measure on
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
. If
\varphi_{o}
is not assumed to be left-
p
-harmonic then this uniqueness statement is lost; in fact we are going to show:

Theorem 6.1
.

Every quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\R
is equivalent to a quasimorphism
\varphi_{1}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
|\mathrm{Prob}^{\mathrm{erg}}_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{1}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}
. If
\Gamma
surjects onto
\mathbb{Z}
, then
\varphi_{1}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
can be chosen to be antisymmetric.

Note that the elements of
\Delta_{\varphi_{1}}
are still heavily restricted by Theorem
5.2
.

6.1.
Reduction to the case of bounded functions

We start the proof of Theorem
6.1
with some easy reductions.
Since by Remark
3.2
every real-valued quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}
is equivalent to an antisymmetric quasimorphism which takes values in
3\mathbb{Z}
, we may as well assume that this is the case for
\varphi_{o}
. We are then going to define

\varphi_{1}:=\varphi_{o}+s_{o},

(6.1)

where
s_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
is a carefully chosen antisymmetric function with
s_{o}(\Gamma)\subset\{-1,0,1\}
. The proof of Theorem
6.1
will be based on the following observation:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to 3\mathbb{Z}
be an (antisymmetric) quasimorphism and let
\varphi_{1}:=\varphi_{o}+s_{o}
, where

s_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
be (antisymmetric) with
s_{o}(\Gamma)\subset\{-1,0,1\}
. Then
\varphi_{1}:\Gamma\to\Z
is an (antisymmetric) quasimorphism and there is a
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous
surjection
\beta:\Delta_{\varphi_{1}}\rightarrow\Delta_{s_{o}}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}
denote the joint hull of
(\varphi_{o},s_{o})
, i.e.

\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}=\overline{\Gamma.(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}\subset\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\times\Delta_{s_{o}}.

We claim that the map

\alpha:\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}\rightarrow\Delta_{\varphi_{o}+s_{o}},\enskip(\varphi,s)\mapsto\varphi+s

is a
\Gamma
-equivariant homeomorphism. Indeed, continuity, surjectivity and
\Gamma
-equivariance of
\alpha
are easy to check, and since
\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}
is compact, it suffices to check that the map is injective. To see this, pick two pairs
(\varphi_{1},s_{1})
and
(\varphi_{2},s_{2})
in
\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}
such that

\varphi_{1}+s_{1}=\varphi_{2}+s_{2}
. Then, since both
\varphi_{1}(\Gamma)
and
\varphi_{2}(\Gamma)
are contained in
3\mathbb{Z}
and
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
take values in
\{-1,0,1\}
, we get

\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}=s_{2}-s_{1}\in 3\mathbb{Z}\cap\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}=\{0\},

and thus
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}
and
s_{1}=s_{2}
. To construct the map
\beta
, we compose the inverse of
\alpha
with the projection
\Delta_{(\varphi_{o},s_{o})}\rightarrow\Delta_{s_{o}}
,
(\varphi,s)\mapsto\varphi
.
∎

We deduce in particular, that

|\Prob_{p}^{\mathrm{erg}}(\Delta_{\varphi_{1}})|\geq|\Prob_{p}^{\mathrm{erg}}(\Delta_{s_{o}})|.

(6.2)

We have thus reduced the proof of Theorem
6.1
to the case where
\varphi_{o}=s_{o}
takes values in
\{-1,0,1\}
.

6.2.
The unconstrained case

To finish the proof of Theorem
6.1
we need to construct an antisymmetric function
s_{o}:\Gamma\to\{-1,0,1\}
with
s_{o}(e)=0
such that

|\mathrm{Prob}^{\mathrm{erg}}_{p}(\Delta_{s_{o}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}
. If we do not insist of antisymmetry of
\varphi_{1}
, then constructing such a map
s_{o}
is easy:

Construction 6.3
.

Consider the
\Gamma
-space
2^{\Gamma}_{*}:=2^{\Gamma}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
with
\Gamma
-action given by
\gamma.A:=A\gamma^{-1}
.
We have a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map

\eta:2^{\Gamma}\to\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma),\quad\eta(A)(\gamma):=\chi_{A}(\gamma)-\chi_{A}(e),

which is injective on
2^{\Gamma}_{*}
and satisfies
\eta(A)(\Gamma)\in\{-1,0,1\}
for every
A\in 2^{\Gamma}_{*}
. If we choose
A_{o}
in such a way that the
\Gamma
-orbit of
A_{o}
is dense in
2^{\Gamma}_{*}
and set
s_{o}:=\eta(A)
, then
\eta
restricts to an equivariant embedding
2^{\Gamma}_{*}\hookrightarrow\Delta_{s_{o}}
. This readily implies that
|\Prob_{p}^{\mathrm{erg}}(\Delta_{s_{o}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}
.

This proves the first part of Theorem
6.1
.

6.3.
The antisymmetric case

To prove the second part of Theorem
6.1
we need to find an
antisymmetric
function
s_{o}:\Gamma\to\{-1,0,1\}
with
s_{o}(e)=0
such that

|\mathrm{Prob}^{\mathrm{erg}}_{p}(\Delta_{s_{o}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}
, wherewe assume the existence of a surjection
\delta:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
. We may then assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, if
s_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\mathbb{Z})
has the desired properties, then so has
\delta^{*}s_{o}\in\mathscr{F}_{o}(\Gamma)
. We thus consider the antisymmetrization of the map
\eta:2^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ast}\to\mathscr{F}_{o}(\mathbb{Z})
from Construction
6.3
as given by

\xi:2^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ast}\to\mathscr{F}_{o}(\mathbb{Z}),\quad\xi(A):=\eta(A)-\eta(-A)=\chi_{A}-\chi_{-A}.

By construction, this map is continuous, and
\xi(A)
is antisymmetric and takes values in
\{-1,0,1\}
for all
A\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ast}
.

Definition 6.4
.

We say that
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
is
left-generic
(respectively
right-vanishing
) if

\overline{\{n.A\,:\,n\geq 0\}}=2^{\mathbb{Z}}\quad(\text{respectively}\lim_{n\rightarrow-\infty}n.A=\emptyset).

Lemma 6.5
.

If
A
is left-generic and right-vanishing and
s_{o}=\xi(A)
, then

\Delta_{s_{o}}=\mathbb{Z}.s_{o}\cup\eta(2_{*}^{\mathbb{Z}})\cup(-\eta(2_{*}^{\mathbb{Z}})),\quad\text{and hence}\quad|\Prob_{p}^{\mathrm{erg}}(\Delta_{s_{o}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}.

Proof.

Note that for all
k,n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle(k.s_{o})(n)

\displaystyle=s_{o}(n+k)-s_{o}(k)=\chi_{A}(n+k)-\chi_{A}(-n-k)-\chi_{A}(k)+\chi_{A}(-k)

\displaystyle=(\chi_{A-k}(n)-\chi_{A-k}(0))-(\chi_{A+k}(-n)-\chi_{A+k}(0)),

which we can rewrite as

(k.s_{o})(n)=\eta(k.A)(n)-\eta((-k).A)(-n)\quad\text{for all }k,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(6.3)

To show that
\eta(2^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ast})\subset\Delta_{s_{o}}
, let
B\in 2_{\ast}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
A
is left-generic and right-varnishing, we find a sequence
k_{j}\to\infty
such that
k_{j}.A\rightarrow B
and
(-k_{j}).A\rightarrow\emptyset
. We thus conclude from (
6.3
) that

\displaystyle\eta(B)(n)=\eta(B)(n)-\eta(\emptyset)(-n)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\eta(k_{j}.A)(n)-\eta((-k_{j}).A)(-n)=\lim_{j\to\infty}(k_{j}.s_{o})(n)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
\eta(B)=\lim_{j\to\infty}k_{j}.s_{o}\in\Delta_{s_{o}}
. Since
B\in 2_{\ast}^{\mathbb{Z}}
was arbitrary, this shows that
\eta(2^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ast})\subset\Delta_{s_{o}}
. Replacing the sequence
(k_{j})
with
(-k_{j})
, we see that

-\eta(-B)(n)=\eta(\emptyset)(n)-\eta(B)(-n)=\lim_{j\to\infty}((-k_{j}).s_{o})(n),

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
-\eta(-B)=\lim_{j\to\infty}(-k_{j}).s_{o}\in\Delta_{s_{o}}
. Consequently,
-\eta(2_{*}^{\mathbb{Z}})\subset\Delta_{s_{o}}
,
and thus

\mathbb{Z}.\xi_{A}\cup\eta(2_{\ast}^{\mathbb{Z}})\cup(-\eta(2_{\ast}^{\mathbb{Z}}))\subset\Delta_{\xi_{A}}.

(6.4)

Finally, if
(k_{j})
is any unbounded sequence, then upon passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
(k_{j})
tends to either
+\infty
or
-\infty
and that
(\pm k_{j}).A
converge. There thus exists
B\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
so that either

(k_{j}.A\rightarrow B\text{ and }(-k_{j}).A\rightarrow\emptyset)\quad\textrm{or}\quad(k_{j}.A\rightarrow\emptyset\text{ and }(-k_{j}).A\rightarrow B).

In the first case,
k_{j}.s_{o}\rightarrow\eta(B)
, while in the second case,
k_{j}.s_{o}\rightarrow-\eta(-B)
. This shows that equality holds in (
6.4
) and finishes the proof.
∎

To conclude the proof of Theorem
6.1
we thus have to construct a left-generic and right-vanishing subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Construction 6.6
.

We consider the one-sided Bernoulli shift

T:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}},\enskip(T\omega)_{k}=\omega_{k+1},\enskip k\geq 0.

The product measure
\mu=\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{o}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}\right)^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
is
T
-invariant and ergodic, hence
\mu
-almost every point
x
in
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
has a dense
T
-orbit. For any such
x
we set

A(x):=\{k\in\ \mathbb{Z}\,:\,k\geq 0,\enskip x_{k}=1\}.

We then observe that for all
n\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle A(T^{n}x)

\displaystyle=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,k\geq 0,\enskip x_{k+n}=1\}=(A(x)-n)\cap\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 6.7
.

If
x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
has a dense
T
-orbit, then
A(x)
is left-generic and right-vanishing.

Proof.

Since the
T
-orbit of
x
is dense, we see that for every
y\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and for every finite set
F\subset\mathbb{N}
, there exists
n=n(y,F)
such
that

(A(x)-n)\cap F=A(y)\cap F.

(6.5)

We claim that this implies that
A
is left-generic. Indeed, fix
B\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and a finite set
F\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Choose a large positive integers
m
such that
F+m\subset\mathbb{N}
. Define
y\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
by

y_{k}=1\iff k\in(B+m)\cap\mathbb{N}.

Note that
A(y)=(B+m)\cap\mathbb{N}
. By (
6.5
), we can find
n
such that

(A(x)-n)\cap(F+m)=A(y)\cap(F+m)=(B+m)\cap(F+m)=(B\cap F)+m,

and thus

(A(x)-(n+m))\cap F=B\cap F.

In other words, for every finite set
F\subset\mathbb{Z}
, we can find
k\geq 0
such that

(A(x)-k)\cap F=B\cap F.

Since
B
is arbitrary, this shows that
\overline{\{k.A\,:\,k\geq 0\}}=2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and thus
A(x)
is left-generic. Furthermore, since
A(x)\subset\mathbb{N}
, we have

n.A(x)=A(x)-n\longrightarrow\emptyset\quad\textrm{as $n\rightarrow-\infty$},

so
A(x)
is right-vanishing as well.
∎

This finishes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

7.
Hulls of twisted sets

7.1.
Twisted sets and uniform approximate lattices

Throughout this section let
(\Gamma,p)
be a symmetric measured group. We recall from the introduction that if
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\R
is a quasimorphism and
P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a closed subset, then the
\varphi_{o}
-twist of
P_{o}
is defined as the subset

P_{o}(\varphi_{o})=\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\R\mid\varphi_{o}(\gamma)+t\in P_{o}\}\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}.

Note that
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
is always a
closed
subset of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, if
\pr_{\Gamma}
and
\pr_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the projections onto the two factors of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
, then
\pr_{\Gamma}(P_{o}(\varphi_{o}))=\Gamma
and
\pr_{\mathbb{R}}(P_{o}(\varphi_{o}))=P_{o}-\varphi_{o}(\Gamma)
. The twist construction can be used to produce interesting examples of approximate subgroups of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
; we recall that a subset
\Lambda\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
is called an approximate subgroup if it is symmetric, contains the identity and satisfies
\Lambda\Lambda\subset\Lambda F
for some finite subsets
F\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

If
P_{o}\subset\R
is an approximate subgroup and
\varphi_{o}
is an antisymmetric quasimorphism with finite defect set, then
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
is an approximate subgroup of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
0\in P_{o}
and
\varphi_{o}(e)=0
we have
(e,0)\in P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
, and since
P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
is symmetric and
\varphi_{o}
is anti-symmetric, the subset
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
is symmetric. If we denote by
\mathscr{D}(\varphi_{o})
the finite defect set of
\varphi_{o}
and choose
F\subset\mathbb{R}
finite such that
P_{o}+P_{o}\subset P_{o}+F
, then

\displaystyle P_{o}(\varphi_{o})P_{o}(\varphi_{o})

\displaystyle\subseteq\{(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},t_{1}+t_{2})\mid t_{1}+t_{2}+\varphi_{o}(\gamma_{1})+\varphi_{o}(\gamma_{2})\in P_{o}+P_{o}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},t_{1}+t_{2})\mid t_{1}+t_{2}+\varphi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\in P_{o}+F+\mathscr{D}(\varphi_{o})\}

\displaystyle=P_{o}(\varphi_{o})(\{e\}\times(F+\mathscr{D}(\varphi_{o}))),

and thus
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
is an approximate subgroup of
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Twisted Delone sets)
.

If
G
is a locally compact group, then a subset
\Lambda\subset G
is called
Q
-relatively dense
for some compact set
Q\subset G
, if
\Lambda Q=G
and
U
-uniformly discrete
for some identity neighbourhood
U
if
\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}\cap U=\{e\}
. It is called a
Delone set
if it is
Q
-relatively dense and
U
-uniformly discrete for some compact set
Q\subset G
and some identity neighbourhood
U
. An approximate subgroup of
G
, which is also a Delone set is called a
uniform approximate lattice
.

One readily checks that if
P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
is
U
-uniformly discrete or
Q
-relatively dense, then for every normalized quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}
the twisted set
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
is
\{e\}\times U
-uniformly discrete and
\{e\}\times Q
-relatively dense in
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
. In particular, twists of Delone sets in
\mathbb{R}
are Delone sets in
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
. If
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in some locally compact group
G
, they are thus also Delone sets in
G\times\mathbb{R}
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}
-valued quasimorphisms have a finite defect set, Proposition
7.1
and Remark
7.2
imply the following corollary.

Corollary 7.3
.

If
P_{o}
is a uniform approximate lattice in
\mathbb{R}
,
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in a locally compact group
G
and
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
is an antisymmetric quasimorphism,
then
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})
is a uniform approximate lattice in
G\times\mathbb{R}
.

For example, this construction allows us to construct uniform approximate lattices in
\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\R)\times\R
by starting from a counting quasimorphism
\varphi_{o}:\Gamma_{g}\to\mathbb{Z}
on a surface group
\Gamma_{g}
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the study of hulls of twisted sets. We will now realize these as twisted skew products over hulls of quasimorphisms.

7.2.
Hulls of twisted sets as skew products

Given a locally compact second countable group
G
we denote by
\mathscr{C}(G)
the space of closed subsets of
G
equipped with the Chabauty-Fell topology and
G
-action given by
g.P:=Pg^{-1}
. Then
\mathscr{C}(G)
is a compact metrizable space, and a sequence
P_{n}
in
\mathscr{C}(G)
converges to some
P\in\mathscr{C}(G)
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(CF1)

If
p_{n_{k}}\in P_{n_{k}}
for some subsequence
(n_{k})
and
p_{n_{k}}\to p
in
G
, then
p\in P
.

(CF2)

For every
p\in P
, there exist elements
p_{n}\in P_{n}
such that
p_{n}\rightarrow p
in
G
.

Given
P_{o}\in\mathscr{C}(G)
we denote by
\Omega_{P_{o}}
the orbit closure of
P_{o}
in
\mathscr{C}(G)
and refer to
\Omega_{P_{o}}
as the
hull
of
P_{o}
.
We recall that if
\Delta
is a compact space with a
\Gamma
-action by homeomorphisms, then a continuous map
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
normalized cocycle
if
c(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},y)=c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.y)+c(\gamma_{2},y)\text{ and }c(e,y)=0\text{ for all }y\in\Delta\text{ and }\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
.

Definition 7.4
.

If
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
be a normalized continuous cocycle and
Z
is a compact
\R
-space, then the
c
-twisted action
of
\Gamma\times\R
on
\Delta\times Z
is the continuous action given by

(\gamma,t).(y,z):=(\gamma.y,(c(\gamma,y)+t).z)\quad(\gamma\in\Gamma,t\in\R,y\in\Delta,z\in Z).

The associated
(\Gamma\times\R)
-space is denoted
\Delta\times_{c}Z
and referred to as the
skew product
of
\Delta
and
Z
over
c
.

Remark 7.5
.

Note that the projection
\pi_{1}:\Delta\times_{c}Z\to\Delta
onto the first factor is a
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous map. In particular,
\Delta
is a continuous
\Gamma
-factor of the skew product
\Delta\times_{c}Z
.

We now investigate conditions on a set
\widetilde{P}\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
which guarantee that the hull of
\widetilde{P}
splits as a skew product. Our starting point is the following observation:

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous cocycle and let
P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
be closed. Then
there is a
(\Gamma\times\R)
-equivariant continuous map

\pi:\Delta\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}\to\mathscr{C}(\Gamma\times\R),\;(y,P)\mapsto\left\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\mid c(\gamma,y)+t\in P\right\}.

Proof.

Equivariance is straight-forward to check. To prove continuity, let
(y_{n},P_{n})
be a sequence in
\Delta\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}
which converges to some
(y,P)
in
\Delta\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}
. Since
\mathscr{C}(\Gamma\times\mathbb{R})
is compact, we can find
\widetilde{P}\in\mathscr{C}(\Gamma\times\mathbb{R})
and a sub-sequence
(n_{k})
such that

\pi(y_{n_{k}},P_{n_{k}})\rightarrow\widetilde{P}\quad\textrm{in $\mathscr{C}(\Gamma\times\R)$}.

To prove continuity we need to show that
\widetilde{P}=\pi(y,P)
. To do this, pick
(\gamma,t)\in\widetilde{P}
. Then, by (CF2) we can find
(\gamma_{k},t_{k})\in\pi(y_{n_{k}},P_{n_{k}})
such
that
(\gamma_{k},t_{k})\rightarrow(\gamma,t)
. Note that by definition

p_{n_{k}}:=c(\gamma_{k},y_{n_{k}})+t_{k}\in P_{n_{k}},\quad\textrm{for all $k$}.

Since
c
is continuous, we conclude by (CF1) that
c(\gamma,t)+a\in P
, and thus
(\gamma,t)\in\pi(y,P)
. Since
(\gamma,t)\in\widetilde{P}
was chosen arbitrarily, this shows
\widetilde{P}\subseteq\pi(y,P)
.

Conversely, suppose
(\gamma,t)\in\pi(y,P)
and define

(y^{\prime},P^{\prime}):=(\gamma,t).(y,P)\quad\textrm{and}\quad(y_{n}^{\prime},P_{n}^{\prime}):=(\gamma,t).(y_{n},P_{n}).

By equivariance of
\pi
we then have
(e,0)\in\pi(y^{\prime},P^{\prime})
and hence
0=c(e,y^{\prime})+0\in P^{\prime}
.
Moreover, since
P_{n}\to P
we have
P_{n}^{\prime}\to P^{\prime}
, and hence by (CF2) we find a sequence
t_{n}^{\prime}\in P_{n}^{\prime}
such that
t_{n}^{\prime}\to 0
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have, by equivariance of
\pi
,

\displaystyle c(e,y_{n}^{\prime})+t_{n}^{\prime}=t_{n}^{\prime}\in P_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\;\implies(e,t_{n}^{\prime})\in\pi(y_{n}^{\prime},P_{n}^{\prime})=\pi(y_{n},P_{n})(\gamma,t)^{-1}

\displaystyle\;\implies(\gamma,t+t_{n}^{\prime})\in\pi(y_{n},P_{n}).

Since
t_{n}^{\prime}\to 0
it then follows from (CF1), that

(\gamma,t)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\gamma,t+t_{n_{k}}^{\prime})\in\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi(y_{n_{k}},P_{n_{k}})=\widetilde{P}.

Since this holds for all
(\gamma,t)\in\pi(y,P)
we have
\pi(y,P)\subset\widetilde{P}
and hence
\pi(y,P)=\widetilde{P}
.
∎

We will need the following property of the map
\pi
.

Lemma 7.7
.

If the
\R
-action on
\Omega_{P_{o}}
is free, then for all
(y,P)\in\Delta\times\Omega_{P_{o}}
we have

\pi^{-1}(\pi(y,P))=\{(y^{\prime},P)\in\Delta\times\Omega_{P_{o}}\mid c(\gamma,y)=c(\gamma,y^{\prime})\enskip\textrm{for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}\}.

Proof.

For
(y,P),(y^{\prime},P^{\prime})\in\Delta\times\Omega_{P_{o}}
we have the equivalences

\displaystyle\pi(y,P)=\pi(y^{\prime},P^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iff(c(\gamma,y)+a\in P\iff c(\gamma,y^{\prime})+a\in P^{\prime})\text{ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma,a\in A$}

\displaystyle\iff P-c(h,y)=P^{\prime}-c(h,y^{\prime})\textrm{ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}.

If we plug in
h=e
into the latter statement, then we see that these statements imply that
P=P^{\prime}
. Since the action
A
-action is free we deduce that

\displaystyle\pi(y,P)=\pi(y^{\prime},P^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;(P=P^{\prime})\text{ and }(P=P-(c(\gamma,y^{\prime})-c(\gamma,y))\text{ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;(P=P^{\prime})\text{ and }(c(h,y)=c(h,y^{\prime})\text{ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$}).\qed

This allows us to show that the hulls of certain subsets of
\Gamma\times\R
are skew products:

Corollary 7.8
.

Let
\Delta
be a compact metrizable space on which a countable group
\Gamma
acts by homeomorphisms, let
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
be a normalized continuous cocycle and let
P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed subset. Assume that

(1)

the
\R
-action on
\Omega_{P_{o}}
is free;

(2)

c
separates points in
\Delta
, i.e. if for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\Delta
there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
c(\gamma,y_{1})\neq c(\gamma,y_{2})
.

(3)

some
y_{o}\in\Delta
has a dense
\Gamma
-orbit.

Then for any
y_{o}
as in (3) the map
\pi
from Proposition
7.6
restricts to a
(\Gamma\times\R)
-equivariant homeomorphism
\Delta\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}\to\Omega_{\pi(y_{o},P_{o})}
. In particular,
\Delta
is a continuous factor of
\Omega_{\pi(y_{o},P_{o})}
and if
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma}(\Delta)=\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma\times\R}(\Omega_{\pi(y_{o},P_{o})})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.7
and since
c
separate points the non-empty fibers of
\pi
are singletons; since
Y\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}
is compact and
\pi
is continuous, this implies that
\pi
is a homeomorphism onto its image. Since
(y,P_{o})
is dense in
Y\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}
, then
\pi(y_{o},P_{o})
is dense in this image by equivariance and continuity of
\pi
. This proves the first statement, and the other two statements readily follows from Remark
7.5
and Lemma
7.11
.
∎

Corollary
7.8
applies in particular to hulls of certain twisted sets. We will work in the following setting:

Setting 7.9
.

Throughout this subsection we use the following notation:

(1)

\Gamma
is a countable discrete group;

(2)

\varphi_{o}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a quasimorphism with hull
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
denotes its hull;

(3)

c:\Gamma\times\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the canonical cocycle given by
c(\gamma,\varphi)=\varphi(\gamma)
;

(4)

P_{o}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a closed subset such that the
\mathbb{R}
-action on
\Omega_{P_{o}}
is free;

(5)

\widetilde{p}
is a probability measure on
\Gamma\times\R
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure on
\Gamma\times\R
and whose support generates
\Gamma\times\R
as a semigroup. We denote by
p
the push-forward of
\widetilde{p}
to
\Gamma
and assume that
\varphi_{o}
is
p
-integrable.

Proposition 7.10

(Skew product realization)
.

In the situation of Setting
7.9
, consider the twisted set
P_{o}(\varphi_{o})\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then the map

\pi_{P_{o},\varphi_{o}}:\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\times_{c}\Omega_{P_{o}}\to\Omega_{P_{o}(\varphi_{o})},\quad(\varphi,P)\mapsto\left\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\mid\varphi(\gamma)+t\in P\right\}

(7.1)

is a well-defined
(\Gamma\times\mathbb{R})
-equivariant homeomorphism.

Proof.

By (
3.1
) the map

c:\Gamma\to\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}\to\R,\quad(\gamma,\varphi)\mapsto\varphi(\gamma).

is a continuous normalized cocycle. This cocycle separates points, since for all
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
we have

\varphi(\gamma)=c(\gamma,\varphi)=c(\gamma,\varphi^{\prime})=\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)\enskip\textrm{for all $\gamma\in{\Gamma}$}\implies\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}.

Since
y_{o}:=\varphi_{o}
has a dense orbit in
\Delta_{\varphi_{o}}
, we can thus apply Corollary
7.8
with
y:=\varphi_{o}
. The proposition then follows from the fact

\displaystyle\pi(\varphi_{o},P_{o})

\displaystyle=\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\R\mid c(\gamma,\varphi_{o})+t\in P_{o}\}

\displaystyle=\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma\times\R\mid\varphi_{o}(\gamma)+t\in P_{o}\}=P_{o}(\varphi_{o}).\qed

7.3.
Stationary and invariant measures on skew products

Let
c:\Gamma\times\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
be a normalized continuous cocycle and let
Z
be a compact
\R
-space. We investigate stationary and invariant measures on the skew-product
\Delta\times_{c}Z
. The following lemma is an immediate consequence of Remark
7.5
:

Lemma 7.11
.

If
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma}(\Delta)=\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma\times\R}(\Delta\times_{c}Z)=\emptyset
.∎

If
Z
admits an
\R
-invariant probability measure
\theta
, then we can relate stationary (in particular, invariant) measures on
\Delta
on
\Delta\times_{c}Z
. To make this precise, we fix a symmetric probability measure
\widetilde{p}
on
\Gamma\times\R
; we are going to assume that
\widetilde{p}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure on
\Gamma\times\R
and that it supports generates
\Gamma\times\R
as a semigroup. We then denote by
p\in\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
the pushforward of
\widetilde{p}
to
\Gamma
. We then obtain a well-defined injection

\sigma_{\theta}:\Prob_{p}(\Delta)\rightarrow\Prob_{\widetilde{p}}(\Delta\times_{c}Z),\enskip\nu\mapsto\nu\otimes\theta

Lemma 7.12
.

If
Z
is uniquely ergodic with
\mathrm{Prob}_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)=\{\theta\}
, then
\sigma_{\theta}
is onto, i.e. every
\widetilde{p}
-stationary probability measure on
\Delta\times_{c}Z
is of the form
\nu\otimes\theta
for some unique
\nu\in\Prob_{p}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a
\widetilde{p}
-stationary probability measure on
\Delta\times_{c}Z
, and let
p_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the push-forward of
p
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then

(\delta_{e}\otimes p_{\mathbb{R}})*\widetilde{p}=\widetilde{p}*(\delta_{e}\otimes p_{\mathbb{R}}).

so by
[
3
, Corollary 3.2]
,
\mu
is also
(\delta_{e}\otimes p_{\mathbb{R}})
-stationary. Fix a Borel set
B\subset Y
and define the positive Borel measure
\mu_{B}
on
Z
by

\mu_{B}(C)=\mu(B\times C),\quad C\in\mathscr{B}_{Z}.

Since
\mu
is
(\delta_{e}\otimes p_{\mathbb{R}})
-stationary, we conclude that
\mu_{B}
is
p_{\mathbb{R}}
-stationary, and thus
\mathbb{R}
-invariant by the Choquet-Deny Theorem
[
6
, Theorem 3]
. Since
\Prob_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)=\{\theta\}
, we conclude that there exists a non-negative constant
\nu(B)

such that

\mu_{B}(C)=\mu(B\times C)=\nu(B)\theta(C).

One readily checks that
B\mapsto\nu(B)
is a Borel probability measure on
\Delta
, which is the push-forward of
\mu
under the canonical projection
Y\times_{c}Z\rightarrow Y
.
Since
\mu
is
\widetilde{p}
-stationary,
\nu
is
p
-stationary.
∎

Stationary and invariant measures on hulls of twisted sets

Combining Proposition
7.10
with Proposition
3.3
.(iii), Corollary

, Lemma

Theorem 7.13
.

In the situation of Setting

If
\varphi_{o}
is cohomologically non-trivial, then
\mathrm{Prob}_{\Gamma\times\R}(\Omega_{{P}_{o}(\varphi_{o})})=\emptyset
.

(ii)

There is a continuous
\Gamma

If

is uniquely ergodic with

-invariant probability measure

, then the map

If

Remark 7.14

(Classification of stationary measures)
.

If
\Omega_{P_{o}}
is uniquely ergodic, but
\varphi_{o}
is not left-
p
-harmonic, then the classification of
\widetilde{p}
-stationary measures on

is more complicated. In this situation we can argue as follows:

Let
\mu
be a
\widetilde{p}
-stationary measure on
\Omega_{P_{o}(\varphi_{o})}
; by Theorem

we have
\mu=(\pi_{P_{o},\varphi_{o}})_{*}(\nu\otimes\theta)
for some (unique)

\psi_{o}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(\varphi_{o})
and denote by

the unique

-stationary measure on

there is a
\Gamma
-equivariant
\nu
-measurable map

-conull set) such that

. We now consider the set

, which by Lemma
is bounded displacement equivalent to

.
If we now define

, then we obtain a commuting diagram of measurable (but not Borel) maps

\textstyle{(\Omega_{P_{o}(\varphi_{o})},\mu)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\Omega_{P_{o}(\psi_{o})},\mu^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with the following properties:

(3)

\nu^{\prime}

(\Delta(\psi_{o}),\nu^{\prime})

In general, the spaces on the left are

|\mathrm{Prob}^{\textrm{erg}}_{\widetilde{p}}(\Omega_{P_{o}(\varphi_{o})})|=|\mathrm{Prob}^{\textrm{erg}}_{p}(\Delta_{\varphi_{o}})|=2^{\aleph_{o}}.

Example 7.15
.

Let

be irrational and consider the subset

P_{o}:=\{m+n\alpha\mid m,n\in\mathbb{Z},m-n\alpha\in[-1,1]\}.

Then

We now define three subsets of

as follows: Firstly, let
\varphi_{o}:F\to\mathbb{Z}
be a counting quasimorphism and

. Secondly, let
\psi_{o}
be the unique

-biharmonic quasimorphism equivalent to

and set

. Finally, let
\varphi_{1}:F\to\mathbb{Z}

\varphi_{o}

(cf. Theorem

) and let
Q_{3}:=P_{o}(\varphi_{1})
. Then the following hold:

(1)

Q_{1}

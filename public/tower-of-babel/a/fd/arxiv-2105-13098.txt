Optimized Implementation for Calculation and Fast-Update of Pfaffians Installed to the Open-Source Fermionic Variational Solver mVMC Journal: Computater Physics Communications
RuQing G. Xu, Tsuyoshi Okubo, Synge Todo, Masatoshi Imada

Abstract
In this article, we present a high performance, portable and well templated implementation for computing and fast-updating Pfaffian and inverse of an even-ranked skew-symmetric (antisymmetric) matrix. It is achieved with a skew-symmetric, blocked variant of the Parlett-Reid algorithm and a blocked update scheme based on the Woodbury matrix identity. Installation of this framework into the geminal-wavefunction-based many-variable Variational Monte Carlo (mVMC) code boosts sampling performance to up to more than 6 6 times without changing Markov chain’s behavior. The implementation is based on an extension of the BLAS-like instantiation software (BLIS) framework which has optimized kernel for many state-of-the-art processors including Intel Skylake-X, AMD EPYC Rome and Fujitsu A64FX.

During the past few years, several packages for variational Monte Carlo have been developed and opened to the public
[

, among them is mVMC
[

]
which focuses on the study of lattice-based systems. In mVMC, the variational wavefunction with
N_{\mathrm{pair}}
spin-up Fermions and the same number of spin-down Fermions is taken to be the form:

\left|{\Psi}\right\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{p}\mathcal{P}_{J}\mathcal{P}_{G}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle.

(1)

In

\left|{\phi}\right\rangle

\left|{\phi}\right\rangle=\left(\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\dagger}\right)^{N_{\mathrm{pair}}}\left|0\right\rangle,

(2)

usually referred as a “pair product” (PP) or “geminal wavefunction” with

as the Fermion creation operator on the

\mathcal{P}_{J},\mathcal{P}_{G}
are real-space correlation operators with Jastrow and Gutzwiller factors, respectively and

[
18

With mean-field based
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
[
18
]
providing a very good initial state and
\mathcal{P}_{J},\mathcal{P}_{G}
further considering effects introduced by interactions like Coulomb repulsion, mVMC is able to grab important features of many-body quantum mechanical systems
[
19
,
20
]
, providing excellent approximations to their ground states as well as low-energy excited states
[
25
,
26
]
.

Within the process of calculation, mVMC samples real space configurations
\left\{\left|{x^{(k)}}\right\rangle\right\}
using a Markov chain (MC) process. The probability for some configuration
\left|{x}\right\rangle
to be sampled is proportional to its overlap with the wavefunction ansatz, i.e.:

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\left|{x}\right\rangle\textrm{ is sampled}\right)=\frac{{\left\langle{x}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle}^{2}}{\left\langle{\Psi}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle}.

(3)

According to the detailed balance condition of the Markov chain,
eq.

3
yields a stochastic process dominated by:

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\left|{x}\right\rangle\longrightarrow\left|{x^{\prime}}\right\rangle\right)=\min\left(1,\frac{{\left\langle{x^{\prime}}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle}^{2}}{{\left\langle{x}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle^{2}}}\right).

(4)

As
\mathcal{P}_{J}
and
\mathcal{P}_{G}
are diagonal in configuration representation, while
\mathcal{L}_{p}
and
\mathcal{L}_{s}
expands to summed spatial operators,
\left\langle{x}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle
expands to:

\left\langle{x}\middle|{\Psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{\theta}\beta_{\theta}\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\alpha_{\mathbf{r}}\left\langle{T_{\mathbf{r}}(x)}\middle|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle.

(5)

Here
\left|R_{\theta}(\phi)\right\rangle
is
\left|\phi\right\rangle
rotated by angle
\theta
and
\left|T_{\mathbf{r}}(x)\right\rangle
is
\left|x\right\rangle
spatially translated by vector
\mathbf{r}
. Meshes for
(\theta,\mathbf{r})
and their corresponding weights are defined so as the original symmetry of Hamiltonian can be correctly restored. We recommended readers to refer to
[
18
,
1
]
for further details of those quantities.

Equation

5
tells us that to generate samples satisfying
eq.

3
we have to compute:

1.

\left\{\left\langle{T_{r}(x^{(0)})}\middle|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle\middle|r,\theta\right\}
for the first sample;

2.

\left\{\left\langle{T_{r}(x^{(k)})}\middle|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle\middle|r,\theta\right\}
with

\left\{\left\langle{T_{r}(x^{(k-1)})}\middle|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle\middle|r,\theta\right\}
known;

which are exactly the hotspots of the whole mVMC code.

Because
\left|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle
generates a constant set of pair products
\left|{R_{\theta}(\phi)}\right\rangle=\left(\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}^{\theta}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\dagger}\right)^{N_{\mathrm{pair}}}\left|{0}\right\rangle
that does not change throughout the MC sampling process, and
\left|{T_{r}(x)}\right\rangle
can be viewed as from another chain of samples with a constant displacement, we omit these transformation operators in later discussion and only use
\left\langle{x}\middle|{\phi}\right\rangle
which is evaluated as:

\left\langle{x}\middle|{\phi}\right\rangle=\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}\textrm{ where }X_{\alpha\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&x_{\alpha}=x_{\beta}\\
f_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}&x_{\alpha}<x_{\beta}\\
-f_{x_{\beta}x_{\alpha}}&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

(6)

where
x_{\alpha}
denotes spin-orbital index of the
\alpha
-th Fermion under real-space configuration
\left|{x}\right\rangle
.

Current implementation of mVMC
[
1
]
already features a nice threading behavior, making it able to run on large-scale computer clusters. However, this implementation bears defective microarchitecture usage on modern processor hardware due to the following two reasons:

1.

Heaviest calculation of the mVMC method involves operations on skew-symmetric (antisymmetric) matrices, which is not available via the standard BLAS interface. The alternative library, known as Pfapack77
[
27
]
, is programmed without considering pipeline-level hardware efficiency hence not optimal for those state-of-the-art processors.

2.

To avoid recalculating Pfaffian every time for each Markov chain update, a fast-update scheme is adopted in current mVMC implementation. However, only rank-1 update scheme is implemented. The rank-1 update strategy is, by its name, mainly invoking rank-1 updates of matrices
\left(\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{uv}^{T}\right)
, which is doomed to hit the memory bound and impact performance.

To get around these two severe hurdles upon mVMC’s performance, a new fast-update scheme as well as a new implementation of the skew-symmetric matrix library is necessary so that:

1.

All computational hotspots are effectively handled with assemblies fully optimized for the hosting hardware.

2.

Fast-update of Pfaffian invokes matrix-matrix operations instead of rank-1 updates.

Such are exactly what our optimization does.

Skew-symmetric Matrix and Pfaffian

Pfaffian of a size
2n\times 2n
skew-symmetric matrix
\mathbf{X}
is defined as:

\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i}^{n}X_{\sigma(2i-1),\sigma(2i)},

(7)

and satisfies:

\left(\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}\right)^{2}=\det\mathbf{X},

where
S_{2n}
is the permutation group of sets with
2n
elements. Directly evaluating
eq.

7
is of exponential complexity due to the number of elements in
S_{2n}
. However, there are linear transformations that can be applied to simplify computation just like the case of calculating determinants.

Before introducing tridiagonalization computation of Pfaffian, let us first go over definition of the Gaussian elimination matrix:

\mathbf{M}_{k}=\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}^{T},

(8)

where
\mathbf{I}_{2n}
is the
2n\times 2n
identity,
\mathbf{e}_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary vector of Euclidean space
(i.e.
\mathbf{e}_{k}=(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{k-1\text{ zeros}},1,\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{2n-k\text{ zeros}})^{T}
)
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}
is constructed with entries:

\left(\alpha_{k}\right)_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&i\leq k\\
\left.X_{i,k-1}\middle/X_{k,k-1}\right.&i>k\end{array}\right.

(9)

so that left-applying
\mathbf{M}_{k}
to
\mathbf{X}
eliminates all

\left.X_{i,k-1}\right|_{i>k}
with

\left.X_{ij}\right|_{i\leq k,\forall j}
untouched.
Applying
\mathbf{M}_{k}
to both sides of a skew-symmetric matrix
\mathbf{X}
eliminates non-tridiagonal part of the
\left(k-1\right)
-th column and row without changing its Pfaffian
[
27
]
. This will be the main weapon for efficient Pfaffian calculation in the formulation of this section.

Blocked Tridiagonal Decomposition

Blocked tridiagonal decomposition of skew-symmetric matrices (
sktdf
) was first formulated by Wimmer in Pfapack77
[
27
]
. In their work a blocked and skew-symmetric variant of the Parlett-Reid algorithm
[
28
]
is adopted so that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}^{T}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{L}^{T},

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{n-1}\cdots\mathbf{P}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}^{T}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}\mathbf{P}^{T}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\left[i::i+r^{(t)}\right]}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}_{i+r^{(t)}}\mathbf{P}_{i+r^{(t)}}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{i+1}\mathbf{P}_{i+1}\mathbf{M}_{i}\mathbf{P}_{i}.

Here
\mathbf{P}_{k}
are permutation matrices that swap the
k
-th and
\left(p(k)>k\right)
-th columns (rows) of
\mathbf{X}
when multiplied on the right (left) side.
We call linear transformation
\mathbf{M}_{\left[i::i+r^{(t)}\right]}
a rank-
r^{(t)}
pivoted Gaussian elimination where
r^{(t)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a parameter for performance tuning. It is constructed by merging multiple Gaussian elimination matrices:

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\left[k::k+1\right]}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}_{k+1}\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\mathbf{M}_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k+1}\mathbf{e}_{k+1}^{T}\right)\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k+1}&\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(p)}_{k}\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{V}_{\left[k::k+1\right]}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{k+1}^{T}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{T}\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{E}_{\left[k::k+1\right]}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{k+1}

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\left[k-r::k+1\right]}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}_{k+1}\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\mathbf{M}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k+1}\mathbf{e}_{k+1}^{T}\right)\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\mathbf{V}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}\mathbf{E}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}^{T}\right)\mathbf{P}_{k}\cdots\mathbf{P}_{k-r}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{2n}-\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k+1}&\mathbf{V}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}^{(p)}\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{V}_{\left[k-r::k+1\right]}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{k+1}^{T}\\
\mathbf{E}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}^{T}\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{E}_{\left[k-r::k+1\right]}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\mathbf{P}_{k}\cdots\mathbf{P}_{k-r},

(12)

where
\mathbf{V}_{\left[...k\right]}^{(p)}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(p)}_{k}
denote a matrix and column vector with
k
-th and
p(k)
-th rows swapped. Note also that in derivation of
eqs.

11
and
12
we used
\mathbf{e}_{k+1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}=0
and
\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{P}_{k+r}\mathbf{e}_{k}
implied by
eq.

8
.
We refer to
[
27
]
for further details of this algorithm.

Once factorization (
10
) is done, with the fact that neither
\mathbf{P}_{k}
or
\mathbf{M}_{k}
affects the matrix’s Pfaffian,
\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}
can be computed as:

\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}=\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{T}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{2i-1,2i}.

(13)

Inversion from Tridiagonalization

With
eq.

13
completes Wimmer’s derivation in
[
27
]
, but mVMC requires computation of
\mathbf{X}^{-1}
in addition to
\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}
. Current version of the implementation is initializing another

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{P}^{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)^{T}\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}^{T}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}^{T}\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}P^{T}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mathbf{X}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]}^{T}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}^{T}\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{\left[n-1-r^{(s)}::n-1\right]}\cdots\mathbf{M}_{\left[2::2+r^{(1)}\right]},

(15)

so that one can either invert
\mathbf{X}
by expanding
eq.

15
from the center or by directly evaluating
eq.

since

is obtainable by properly merging and swapping all

entries of
\mathbf{M}
[
27
]
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}\mathbf{X}^{(k-r-1)}\mathbf{M}_{\left[k-r::k\right]}^{T}

where we have used during the derivation that

Blocked Pfaffian Update from Modified Woodbury Matrix Identity

Theory

mVMC takes configuration samples
\left\{\left|{x^{(k)}}\right\rangle\middle|k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
with a Markov chain (MC) process, as is shown in
eq.

4
. As each step
\left|{x^{(k)}}\right\rangle\rightarrow\left|{x^{(k+1)}}\right\rangle
within this MC only changes the spin-orbitals of one or two electrons of
\left|{x^{(k)}}\right\rangle
, we do not need to redo
section

3
calculations each time a new sample is generated. Instead, from the Woodbury matrix identity
[
29
]
we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pf}\left[\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{B}^{T}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}\times\frac{\mathrm{Pf}\left[\mathbf{C}^{-1}+\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\right]}{\mathrm{Pf}\left[\mathbf{C}^{-1}\right]}

(18)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{B}^{T}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{-1}-\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\left(\mathbf{C}^{-1}+\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{-1},

(19)

which allows Pfaffian in
eq.

6
to be updated as (To give a more intuitive image of what is happening during the MC fast-update, we also plug in here a
4\times 4
exemplar matrix for
\mathbf{X}
.):

\displaystyle\left|{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|{{j_{1}}{j_{2}}{j_{3}}{j_{4}}}\right\rangle

(20)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&F_{j_{1}}^{j_{2}}&F_{j_{1}}^{j_{3}}&F_{j_{1}}^{j_{4}}\\
-F_{j_{1}}^{j_{2}}&0&F_{j_{2}}^{j_{3}}&F_{j_{2}}^{j_{4}}\\
-F_{j_{1}}^{j_{3}}&-F_{j_{2}}^{j_{3}}&0&F_{j_{3}}^{j_{4}}\\
-F_{j_{1}}^{j_{4}}&-F_{j_{2}}^{j_{4}}&-F_{j_{3}}^{j_{4}}&0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\left\langle{x}\middle|{\phi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pf}\,\mathbf{X}

(25)

\displaystyle\downarrow

\displaystyle\left|{x^{(1)}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}i_{1}}{j_{2}}{j_{3}}{j_{4}}}\right\rangle

We run a Markov chain sampling a random many-body spin wavefunction on a

Our experiment only studies the sampling process itself against this random wavefunction so that the Hamiltonian is omitted.
The total number of samples generated by the MC is proportional to the number of spins:

because MC needs more steps to decorrelate as system size grows.

k

accepted

and

NDataQtySmp

NVMCSample
and

7
Conclusion

We have shown in this article a framework for computing and updating Pfaffians that is fast, flexible and runs across different microarchitectures. mVMC optimized with this framework has achieved significant speed up compared to its original release. Theory as well as library implementation in this work can also be applied to other kinds of variational methods that deals with skew-symmetric wavefunctions, i.e. Fermions, like TurboRVB
[
24
]
. On the implementation side, this blocking would also allow potential portings of the related methods onto heterogeneous platforms like a general-purpose GPU machine, although the sequential nature of Markov chains might require additional parallelizations to unleash GPUs’ full power.
Further, our work shows a new possible pattern of open-source communities collaborating with hardware people to deliver an assembly-level optimized code for HPC applications.

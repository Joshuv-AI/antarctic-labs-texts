A recurrence relation for generalised connection coefficients
Jing Gao, Arieh Iserles

Abstract
We formulate and prove a general recurrence relation that applies to integrals involving orthogonal polynomials and similar functions. A special case are connection coefficients between two sets of orthonormal polynomials, another example is integrals of products of Legendre functions.



1
A universal recurrence for connection coefficients

Let two Borel measures,
\,\mathrm{d}\mu
and
\,\mathrm{d}\nu
, be given. We denote by
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
\mathcal{Q}=\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

\,\mathrm{d}\mu
and
\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
obey three-term recurrence relations of the form

\displaystyle b_{n}p_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-a_{n})p_{n}(x)-b_{n-1}p_{n-1}(x),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(1.1)

\displaystyle d_{n}q_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-c_{n})q_{n}(x)-d_{n-1}q_{n-1}(x),

(1.2)

b_{-1}=d_{-1}=0
and
b_{n},d_{n}>0

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\mathcal{D}
, where

\mathcal{D}_{m,n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{m}(x)q_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\eta(x),\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(1.3)

where
\,\mathrm{d}\eta
is yet another, possibly signed, Borel measure – note that
\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\,\mathrm{d}\eta

Expressions of the form (

While both the recurrence and its proof are elementary, they are (to the best of our knowledge) new and they have significant ramifications, some of which are described in the sequel.

Lemma 1

It is true that

b_{m-1}\mathcal{D}_{m-1,n}+a_{m}\mathcal{D}_{m,n}+b_{m}\mathcal{D}_{m+1,n}=d_{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{m,n-1}+c_{n}\mathcal{D}_{m,n}+d_{n}\mathcal{D}_{m,n+1}

(1.4)

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof
It follows from (
1.1
) and
b_{m}>0
that

p_{m+1}(x)=\frac{1}{b_{m}}[(x-a_{m})p_{m}(x)-b_{m-1}p_{m-1}(x)]

and substitution in (
1.3
) results in

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{m}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[(x-a_{m})p_{m}(x)-b_{m-1}p_{m-1}(x)]q_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\eta(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{m}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xp_{m}(x)q_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\eta(x)-\frac{a_{m}}{b_{m}}\mathcal{D}_{m,n}-\frac{b_{m-1}}{b_{m}}\mathcal{D}_{m-1,n}.

However, it is a consequence of (
1.2

xq_{n}(x)=d_{n}q_{n+1}(x)+c_{n}q_{n}(x)+d_{n-1}q_{n-1}(x)

and substitution within the integral yields (
1.4
) after elementary algebra.

Few examples

2.1
A pair of Legendre weights

The trivial case
\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\,\mathrm{d}\eta
results in a unit matrix, as it of course should. As a somewhat less elementary example let us consider
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
and
\,\mathrm{d}\eta(x)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(x)x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Thus,
p_{n}=q_{n}=\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{P}_{n}
, normalised standard Legendre polynomials, and one can prove easily from the standard three-term recurrence for Legendre polynomials that

a_{n},c_{n}\equiv 0,\qquad b_{n}=d_{n}=\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{(2n+1)(2n+3)}}.

Therefore it follows from (
1.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{m}{\sqrt{(2m-1)(2m+1)}}\mathcal{D}_{m-1,n}+\frac{m+1}{\sqrt{(2m+1)(2m+3)}}\mathcal{D}_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{\sqrt{(2n-1)(2n+1)}}\mathcal{D}_{m,n-1}+\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{(2n+1)(2n+3)}}\mathcal{D}_{m,n+1}.

Finally,
\mathcal{D}
is symmetric,
\mathcal{D}_{0,n}=0
for
n\geq 3
(because
x^{2}p_{0}
is then orthogonal to
p_{n}
) and

\mathcal{D}_{0,0}=\frac{1}{3},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{0,1}=0,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{0,2}=\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{15}.

In general,
\mathcal{D}_{m,n}=0
for

|m-n|\geq 3

\mathcal{D}_{n,n}=\frac{2n^{2}+2n-1}{(2n-1)(2n+3)},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{n+2,n}=\mathcal{D}_{n,n+2}=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{(2n+3)\sqrt{(2n+1)(2n+5)}}.

This can be verified at once from the above recurrence relation.

The matrix

\mathrm{L}_{n}^{(\beta)}
for
n\leq m

\alpha,\beta>0

\mathrm{L}_{n+1}^{(\beta)}(x)=\left(-\frac{x}{n+1}+\frac{2n+\beta+1}{n+1}\right)\!\mathrm{L}_{n}^{(\beta)}(x)-\frac{n+\beta}{n+1}\mathrm{L}_{n-1}^{(\beta)}(x),

\tilde{\mathrm{L}}_{n}^{(\beta)}(x)=(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\mathrm{\Gamma}(n+\beta+1)}}\mathrm{L}_{n}^{(\beta)}(x)

b_{n}=\sqrt{(n+1)(n+1+\beta)},\quad a_{n}=2n+1+\beta,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

The recurrence (
1.4

\displaystyle\sqrt{m(m+\alpha)}\mathcal{D}_{m-1,n}+(2m+1+\alpha)\mathcal{D}_{m,n}+\sqrt{(m+1)(m+1+\alpha)}\mathcal{D}_{m+1,n}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{n(n+\beta)}\mathcal{D}_{m,n-1}+(2n+1+\beta)\mathcal{D}_{m,n}+\sqrt{(n+1)(n+1+\beta)}\mathcal{D}_{m,n+1},\hskip 25.0pt

where

\mathcal{D}_{m,n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\beta)}(x)x^{\beta}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This need be accompanied by boundary conditions. It is clear that
\mathcal{D}_{0,n}\equiv 0

\mathcal{D}

\tilde{\mathrm{L}}_{0}^{(\beta)}\equiv 1/\sqrt{\mathrm{\Gamma}(1+\beta)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{\Gamma}(1+\beta)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)x^{\beta}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\sqrt{\frac{m!}{\mathrm{\Gamma}(m+\alpha+1)\mathrm{\Gamma}(1+\beta)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)x^{\beta}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

To derive
\mathfrak{d}_{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)x^{\beta}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)z^{m}=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1+\alpha}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{xz}{1-z}\right)\!,\qquad 0<z<1.

Therefore

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{d}_{m}z^{m}=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x}{1-z}\right)\!x^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathrm{\Gamma}(1+\beta)(1-z)^{\beta-\alpha}.

Expanding the binomial function we obtain

\mathcal{D}_{m,0}=(-1)^{m}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{\Gamma}(1+\beta)}{m!\mathrm{\Gamma}(m+1+\alpha)}}(\alpha-\beta)_{m},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Substitution in (

\mathcal{D}_{m,n}=(-1)^{m-n}\frac{(\alpha-\beta)_{m-n}}{(m-n)!}\sqrt{\frac{m!\mathrm{\Gamma}(n+1+\beta)}{n!\mathrm{\Gamma}(m+1+\alpha)}},\qquad 0\leq n\leq m.

3
Associated Legendre functions

The solutions of the differential equation

(1-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}-2xy^{\prime}+\left[\nu(\nu+1)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{1-x^{2}}\right]\!y=0,\qquad x\in(-1,1),

where
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
, are
Legendre functions

\mathrm{P}_{\nu}^{\mu}
and
associated Legendre functions

\mathrm{Q}_{\nu}^{\mu}
. These functions feature in a wide range of applications, not least as a main component of
spherical harmonics.
They admit a number of confusingly different normalisations
[
abramowitz66hma
,
arfken66mmp
,
courant53mmp
,
dlmf
]
yet, in this paper, being interested in
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\mu\in\{0,1,\ldots,\nu\}
and restricting our attention to the Legendre functions
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}
, we use the expression

\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)=\frac{(-n)_{m}(1+n)_{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{\!m/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{l}-n,n+1;\\
m+1;\end{array}\frac{1-x}{2}\right]\!,

(3.1)

where
(z)_{k}=z(z+1)\cdots(z+k-1)
is the familiar
Pochhammer symbol
and
{}_{2}F_{1}
is a
hypergeometric function.

Note that the
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}
s are, in general, non-polynomial. Thus, while
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{0}=\mathrm{P}_{n}
, the standard Legendre polynomial,
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{1}(x)=-\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{P}_{n}^{\prime}(x)
– cf.
[
dlmf
, 14.6.1]
for explicit expressions for
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}
.

In our forthcoming work on multivariate expansions we have encountered the problem to calculate expressions of the form

g^{m}_{\ell,n}=g_{n,\ell}^{m}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{P}_{\ell}^{m}(x)\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad m\leq\min\{\ell,n\}.

(3.2)

Since it follows readily from (
3.1
) that
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}^{m}(-x)=(-1)^{m+\ell}\mathrm{P}_{\ell}^{m}(x)
, we deduce that
g^{m}_{\ell,n}=0
when
\ell+n
is odd.

Our starting point is the three-term recurrence relation

(n-m+1)\mathrm{P}_{n+1}^{m}(x)-(2n+1)x\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)+(m+n)\mathrm{P}_{n-1}^{m}(x)=0,

(3.3)

which can be easily derived from
[
dlmf
, 14.10.3]
.

We proceed similarly to Section 1. Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{P}^{m}_{\ell}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\!\left[\frac{n-m+1}{2n+1}\mathrm{P}_{n+1}^{m}(x)+\frac{m+n}{2n+1}\mathrm{P}_{n-1}^{m}(x)\right]\!\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x\mathrm{P}_{\ell}^{m}(x)\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\!\left[\frac{\ell-m+1}{2\ell+1}\mathrm{P}_{\ell+1}^{m}(x)+\frac{m+\ell}{2\ell+1}\mathrm{P}_{\ell-1}^{m}(x)\right]\!\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

and deduce the recurrence

\frac{n-m+1}{2n+1}g_{n+1,\ell}^{m}+\frac{m+n}{2n+1}g_{n-1,\ell}^{m}=\frac{\ell-m+1}{2\ell+1}g_{n,\ell+1}^{m}+\frac{m+\ell}{2\ell+1}g_{n,\ell-1}^{m},

(3.4)

valid for all
m\leq\min\{\ell,n\}
. Once
n+\ell
is even, all the terms vanish, but not so for an odd
n+\ell
.

For completeness, the recurrence (
3.4
) requires boundary conditions. Given
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we need
g_{n,m}^{m}=g_{m,n}^{m}
,
n\geq m
. In addition we need
g_{-1,m}^{m}+g_{m,1}^{m}=0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
: this follows at once from (
3.1
).

It is an immediate consequence of (
3.1
) that

\mathrm{P}_{m}^{m}(x)=(-1)^{m}\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}m!}(1-x^{2})^{m/2},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

therefore

g_{m,n}^{m}=(-1)^{m}\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}m!}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{(m-1)/2}\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad n\geq m.

Of course, our interest is restricted to even values of
m+n
, otherwise
g_{m,n}^{m}
vanishes.

Let

G_{n}^{m}=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{(m-1)/2}\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad H_{n}^{m}=\int_{-1}^{1}x(1-x^{2})^{(m-1)/2}\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.5)

for
0\leq m\leq n
,
m+n
even. Thus,
g_{m,n}^{m}=(-1)^{m}(2m!)/(2^{m}m!)G_{n}^{m}
. It follows from (
3.3
), though, that

(n-m+1)G^{m}_{n+1}+(m+n)G^{m}_{n-1}=(2n+1)H_{n}^{m}.

Because of
[
dlmf
, 14.10.5]
it is true that

(1-x^{2})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x}=(m+n)\mathrm{P}_{n-1}^{m}(x)-nx\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}(x)

and it follows that

H_{n}^{m}=\frac{1}{m+1}[(m+n)G_{n-1}^{m}-nH_{n}^{m}]

– we deduce that

H_{n}^{m}=\frac{m+n}{m+n+1}G_{n-1}^{m}.

It now follows from (
3.5
) that

G^{m}_{n+1}=\frac{n^{2}-m^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}-m^{2}}G^{m}_{n-1}

and

g_{m,n+1}^{m}=\frac{n^{2}-m^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}-m^{2}}g_{m,n-1}^{m},\qquad m\leq n-1.

(Of course, all this makes sense only for an odd
m+n
.). To start the recursion we need

and to this end we note from (
3.1
) that

\mathrm{P}_{m}^{m}(x)=(-1)^{m}\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}m!}(1-x^{2})^{m/2}

and direct integration yields

g_{m,m}^{m}=\pi(2m)!\!\left[\frac{1}{4^{m}}{{2m}\choose m}\right]^{\!2},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

It is now a straightforward (yet tedious calculation) that

g^{m}_{m,m+2k}=g^{m}_{m+2k,m}=\pi\frac{(2m)!}{4^{2m+2k}}{{2k}\choose k}{{2m}\choose m}{{2m+2k}\choose{m+k}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(3.6)

Using the recurrence (
3.4
) we derive explicitly, by long yet straightforward algebra,

g_{m+1,m+2k+1}^{m}=g_{m+2k+1,m+1}=\frac{2\pi(m+1)}{4^{2m+2k+1}}(2m)!{{2k}\choose k}{{2m+1}\choose m}{{2m+2k+1}\choose{m+k}}

(3.7)

for all
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Remaining values of
g_{n,\ell}^{m}
,
m\leq\min\{n,\ell\}

3.4
). Bearing in mind formulæ (
3.6
) and (

vindicating this expectation. However,

g^{m}_{m+2,m+2}=\frac{\pi(2m+1)!}{2\cdot 4^{2m+2}}\frac{(8m^{2}+20m+11)}{2m+3}{{2m}\choose m}{{2m+3}\choose{m+1}}\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

and expressions do not become nicer for larger values of

. Yet, for practical uses in approximation algorithms, all we need is numerical values of the
g_{n,\ell}^{m}
s, and this can be easily produced by the recurrence (
3.4
).

4
Conclusions

Our point of departure, Lemma 1, is an exceedingly simple result with trivial proof. Yet, its ramifications are far and wide, since it allows for simple evaluation of connection coefficients and their generalisations and applies whenever a three-term recurrence relation is available for a set of functions: this is obviously true for orthogonal polynomials but also, for example, for Legendre functions
\mathrm{P}_{n}^{m}
which in general are neither polynomial nor orthogonal.

There is no free lunch: to use the recurrence (
1.4
) or its generalisations we need first to determine boundary conditions. Yet, this is typically much easier than computing
\mathcal{D}_{m,n}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and it often suffices to do so numerically.

On the orthogonality of measures of different spectral type with respect to twisted Eberlein convolution
Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. In this paper we show that under suitable conditions on their Fourier–Bohr coefficients, the twisted Eberlein convolution of a measure with pure point diffraction spectra and a measure with continuous diffraction spectra is zero. In particular, the diffraction spectrum of a linear combinations of the two measures is simply the linear combinations of the two diffraction spectra with absolute value square coefficients.

1.
Introduction

Born from the discovery of quasicrystals
[
36
]
, the field of Aperiodic Order is focused on the study of objects which show long range order, typically in the form of a large Bragg diffraction spectrum, but lack translational symmetry.

As introduced by Hof
[
20
]
, mathematical diffraction is defined as follows. Given an object, typically a point-set
\Lambda
denoting the positions of atoms in an idealized solid, or more generally a translation bounded measure
\omega
, its autocorrelation
\gamma
is defined as the vague limit

(1.1)

\gamma=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\omega_{n}*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}=:\text{\textlquill}\omega,\omega\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\,,

where
A_{n}
is a nice (van Hove) sequence and
\omega_{n}
denotes the restriction of
\omega
to
A_{n}
. Here, when
\Lambda
is a pointset, we use
\omega=\delta_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\delta_{x}
in (
1.1
) to define its autocorrelation. By eventually replacing
A_{n}
by a subsequence, the autocorrelation can always be assumed to exist
[
11
,
26
]
, and is positive definite. Therefore, its Fourier transform
\widehat{\gamma}
exists and is a positive measure
[
1
,
15
,
32
]
. The measure
\widehat{\gamma}
is called the diffraction measure of
\Lambda
and
\omega
, respectively. As any translation bounded measure on the second countable locally compact Abelian group(LCAG)
\widehat{G}
, it has a (unique) refined Lebesgue decomposition

\widehat{\gamma}=(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{pp}}+\underbrace{(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{ac}}+(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{sc}}}_{(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{c}}}

into a pure point measure
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{pp}}
, a measure
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{ac}}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure
\theta_{\widehat{G}}
and a measure
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{sc}}
which is continuous and singular with respect to the Haar measure
\theta_{\widehat{G}}
.

Systems with pure point spectrum, meaning
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{c}}=0
, are now relatively well understood. Pure point diffraction was classified via dynamical spectra
[
22
,
11
,
17
,
28
]
, via the almost periodicity of the autocorrelation measure
[
21
,
12
,
18
,
32
]
. More recently, generalizing previous work in this direction
[
38
,
37
,
12
,
31
,
18
]
, pure point diffraction was shown to be equivalent to mean almost periodicity of the underlying structure
[
24
,
25
]
.

The Eberlein decomposition of the autocorrelation measure plays an important role in the study of diffraction of structures. Indeed the autocorrelation
\gamma
has an unique decomposition
\gamma=\gamma_{\mathsf{s}}+\gamma_{0}
into two Fourier transformable measures
[
21
,
32
]
such that

\widehat{\gamma_{\mathsf{s}}}=(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{pp}}\,;\,\widehat{\gamma_{0}}=(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{c}}\,.

This decomposition allows us study the pure point and continuous diffraction spectrum, respectively, in the real space by studying the two components
\gamma_{\mathsf{s}}
and
\gamma_{0}
of
\gamma
. This approach proved effective in the study of the diffraction spectra of measures with Meyer set support
[
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
and
the diffraction of compatible random substitutions in 1 dimension
[
30
,
13
,
39
]
. For Meyer sets, one can further decompose
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0s}+\gamma_{0a}
in such a way that the Fourier transforms of
\gamma_{0s}
and
\gamma_{0a}
are
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{sc}}
and
(\widehat{\gamma})_{\mathsf{ac}}
, respectively
[
43
,
44
]
.

For many examples of compatible random 1-dimensional Pisot substitutions, one gets a decomposition of the generic element
\omega
of the hull into two measures
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
such that the diffraction of
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, respectively, are the pure point and continuous diffraction spectrum, respectively, of
\omega

[
30
,
13
,
39
]
. A similar decomposition hold for 1-dimensional PV substitutions
[
14
]
and for dynamical systems of translation bounded measures
[
2
]
. It is the one of the goals of this paper to investigate this type of decomposition, at the level of measures and not autocorrelations, in more general settings.

We will approach this question from a different angle. If
\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
is such a potential decomposition, and
{\mathcal{A}}
is any van Hove sequence, then there exist a subsequence
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
so that (see (
2.4
))

(1.2)

\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\text{\textlquill}\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\text{\textlquill}\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}}\,,

where
\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are the autocorrelations of
\omega,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
,respectively, with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
\text{\textlquill}\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}
denote the twisted Eberlein convolutions (see Def.
2.8
). Therefore, we obtain such a decomposition whenever when
\omega_{1}
is pure point diffractive,
\omega_{2}
has continuous diffraction spectrum and, the following orthogonality with respect to the twisted Eberlein decomposition holds:

(1.3)

\text{\textlquill}\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}=0\,.

Note here also that for positive measures
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
, (
1.2
) implies that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
is equivalent to (
1.3
).

The main goal of the article is to prove that, under suitable conditions, if
\omega_{1}
has pure point diffraction spectrum and
\omega_{2}
has continuous diffraction spectrum, then (
1.3
) holds. In particular, the pure point spectrum of
\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
is the spectrum of
\omega_{1}
and the continuous spectrum of
\omega
is the spectrum of
\omega_{2}
. We prove few results in this direction. First, in Thm.
5.5
we show that if
\omega_{1}
has pure point diffraction spectrum,
\omega_{2}
has continuous diffraction spectrum, and the Fourier–Bohr coefficients of
\omega_{2}
exist uniformly then (
1.3
) holds. Next, in Cor.
5.8
we show that (
1.3
) also holds if
\omega_{1}
has pure point diffraction spectrum,
\omega_{2}
has continuous diffraction spectrum, the Fourier–Bohr coefficients of
\omega_{1}
exist uniformly and the Consistent Phase Property (CPP) (see Def.
2.4
) holds for
\omega_{2}
. We continue by restricting our attention to the case when
\omega_{1}
is Besicovitch almost periodic, and we show in Cor.
6.2
that, if
\omega_{1}
is Besicovitch almost periodic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
,
\omega_{2}
has continuous diffraction spectrum with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
and the CPP holds for
\omega_{2}
then (
1.3
) holds. Finally, in Thm.
7.7
we show that if
(\mathbb{X},G,m)
and
(\mathbb{Y},G,n)
are ergodic dynamical systems of translation bounded measures, one with pure point spectrum and the other with continuous diffraction spectra, then (
1.3
) holds for
m
-almost all
\omega_{1}
and
n
-almost all
\omega_{2}
. In particular, some type of generalized Eberlein decomposition holds for such dynamical systems of translation bounded measures such that (
1.3
) holds almost surely (Thm.
7.9
).

The Fourier–Bohr coefficients are a central tool we use in many proofs, and along the way we prove various properties for the Fourier–Bohr coefficients of the twisted Eberlein convolution, which are interesting by themselves. First, we show in Thm.
3.5
that whenever when the twisted Eberlein convolution
\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
exists,
a_{\chi}(\mu)
exist uniformly and
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\nu)
exist, then we have the generalized (CPP) relation

a_{\chi}(\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}})=a_{\chi}(\mu)\overline{a_{\chi}(\nu)}\,.

This implies that any measure
\mu
with uniform Fourier–Bohr coefficients satisfies CPP, a result which was previously proven in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
[
20
]
and for dynamical systems of translation bounded measures in
[
23
]
. This result has an intriguing consequence: in Thm.
3.7
we show that the existence of the twisted Eberlein convolution
\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
combined with the uniform existence
a_{\chi}(\mu)
exist uniformly yields the following:

•

For all
\chi
with
a_{\chi}(\mu)=0
we have
a_{\chi}(\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}})=0
.

•

For all
\chi
with
a_{\chi}(\mu)\neq 0
, the Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\nu)
exists and
a_{\chi}(\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}})=a_{\chi}(\mu)\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\nu)}
.

It is perhaps surprising that in the second situation we get the existence of
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\nu)
for free, which hints that there may be an even deeper connection in general between the Fourier–Bohr coefficients and the twisted Eberlein convolution.

2.1.
Fourier Bohr coefficients

In this section we review the basic properties of Fourier–Bohr coefficients. For more details we refer the reader to
[
24
]
.

We start with the definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
, let
\chi\in\widehat{G}
and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be van Hove sequence.
We say that the
Fourier–Bohr coefficient

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)
exists if the following limit exists:

a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(f):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\overline{\chi(t)}f(t)\mbox{\rm d}t\,.

We further say that the Fourier–Bohr coefficient exists
uniformly
if the limit

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x+A_{n}}\overline{\chi(t)}f(t)\mbox{\rm d}t\,,

exists uniformly in
x
.

Same way we say that the
Fourier–Bohr coefficient

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)
exists if the following limit exists:

a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mu):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}\mu(t)\,.

We further say that the Fourier–Bohr coefficient exists
uniformly
if the limit

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x+A_{n}}\overline{\chi(t)}\mbox{\rm d}\mu(t)\,,

exists uniformly in
x
.

The next result plays a central role in the rest of the paper (compare
[
16
,
32
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
24
, Cor. 1.12]
Let
f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
and
\chi\in G
. If
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)
exists uniformly, then
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{B}}}(f)
exists uniformly for all van Hove sequences
{\mathcal{B}}
and

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{B}}}(f)=a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)\,.

∎

The same result holds for measures:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
24
, Cor. 1.12]
Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}
. If the Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mu)
of
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
exists uniformly with respect to the van Hove sequence
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{n}\}
, and
{\mathcal{B}}=\{B_{n}\}
is another van Hove sequence, then the Fourier–Bohr coefficient of
\mu
exists uniformly with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}
and

a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{B}}(\mu)=a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mu)\,.

∎

Due to Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, whenever when the Fourier–Bohr coefficient
f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
or
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
, respectively, exists uniformly, we can simply write
a_{\chi}(f)
and
a_{\chi}(\mu)
, respectively. We will do this in the remaining of the paper. Moreover, whenever when we write
a_{\chi}(f)
and
a_{\chi}(\mu)
, respectively, it is understood that the Fourier–Bohr coefficients exist uniformly.

Since we will often need to refer to the non-zero Fourier–Bohr coefficients, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that

(a)

f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
(or
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
) has a
well defined Fourier–Bohr spectrum
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if for all
\chi\in\widehat{G}
the Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(f)
(or
a^{\mathcal{A}}_{\chi}(\mu)
) exists. In this case, the
Fourier–Bohr spectrum of

(or
\mu
) with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}B}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)

\displaystyle=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}:a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)\neq 0\}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}B}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}:a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)\neq 0\}\,.

If furthermore the Fourier–Bohr coefficients exist uniformly, we say that
f
or
\mu
, respectively, has
uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum
.

(b)

We say that
f
or
\mu
, respectively, has a
null Fourier–Bohr spectrum with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if the Fourier–Bohr spectrum exists with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
and is empty.
If the Fourier–Bohr spectrum exists uniformly and is empty we say that
f
or
\mu
, respectively,
uniformly-null Fourier–Bohr spectrum
.

(c)

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
and
{\mathcal{A}}
be a van Hove sequence so that the autocorrelation
\gamma
of
\mu
exists with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
. We say that
\mu
satisfies the
Consistent Phase Frequency (CPP)

if the Fourier–Bohr spectrum is well defined with respect to

and

(2.1)

\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=|a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)|^{2}\quad\forall\chi\in\widehat{G}\qquad\mbox{\bf\rm(CPP) }\,.

As pointed above, if

{\mathcal{F}B}(f)
and

Remark 2.5
.

(a)

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)

Then

(b)

Next, we study how the Fourier–Bohr coefficients behave with respect to the basic operations on functions.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G),\chi\in\widehat{G}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
a van Hove sequence. If
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)
exists (uniformly) then

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\overline{f}),a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\tilde{f}),a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(f^{\dagger})
exist (uniformly) and

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{\bar{\chi}}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\overline{f})

\displaystyle=\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)}

\displaystyle a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\tilde{f})

\displaystyle=\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)}

(2.3)

\displaystyle a_{\bar{\chi}}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(f^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)\,.

Proof.

The computations are straightforward: for each
x\in G
and
n
we have

\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x+A_{n}}\chi(t)\overline{f(t)}\mbox{\rm d}t=\overline{\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x+A_{n}}\overline{\chi(t)}f(t)\mbox{\rm d}t}\,.

Taking the limit we get (
2.2
).

Next,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x-A_{n}}\chi(t)f^{\dagger}(t)\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x-A_{n}}\overline{\chi(-t)}f(-t)\mbox{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{-x+A_{n}}\overline{\chi(s)}f(s)\mbox{\rm d}s

Taking the limit we get (
2.3
).

Finally,

a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\tilde{f})=a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\overline{f^{\dagger}})=\overline{a_{\bar{\chi}}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(f^{\dagger})}=\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)}\,,

with all limits existing (uniformly) by the above.

∎

Since,
\widetilde{\mu*\varphi}=\widetilde{\mu}*\tilde{\varphi}
, we can use
[
24
, Cor. 1.11]
to transfer these relations to measures.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\mu\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G),\chi\in\widehat{G}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
a van Hove sequence. If
a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)
exists (uniformly) then

a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\overline{\mu}),a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\tilde{\mu}),a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu^{\dagger})
exist (uniformly) and

\displaystyle a_{\bar{\chi}}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\overline{\mu})

\displaystyle=\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)}

\displaystyle a_{\chi}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\tilde{\mu})

\displaystyle=\overline{a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)}

\displaystyle a_{\bar{\chi}}^{-{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=a_{\chi}^{{\mathcal{A}}}(\mu)\,.

Let us note here in passing that, when

exists uniformly, we have

2.2.
Twisted Eberlein convolution of measures

Here we briefly review the basic properties of Eberlein convolution of measures from
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
f,g:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
have a well defined
twisted Eberlein convolution
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if for all
t\in G
the function
s\to f(s)\overline{g(s-t)}
is locally integrable and the following limit exists

\text{\textlquill}f,g\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(t):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}f(s)\overline{g(s-t)}\mbox{\rm d}s\,.

We say that
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
have a well defined
Eberlein convolution
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if the following vague limit exists

\text{\textlquill}\mu,\nu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\mu|_{A_{n}}*\widetilde{\nu|_{A_{n}}}\,.

Note here that, given
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
and a van Hove sequence
{\mathcal{A}}
, the autocorrelation
\gamma
of
\mu
exists with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if

\text{\textlquill}\mu,\mu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
exists. In this situation we have
\gamma=\text{\textlquill}\mu,\mu\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)

{\mathcal{A}}

(ii)

(iii)

Moreover, in this case, for all

Proposition 3.1

Let

\epsilon>0

4.
The diffraction measure

Here we list some consequences of the Fourier–Bohr results to the diffraction. We start by showing that the uniform existence of the Fourier–Bohr coefficient implies that the intensity of the Bragg peak at that point is the square of the absolute value of the Fourier–Bohr coefficient. For
G={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
this result is well known, see
[
19
]
. For uniquely ergodic dynamical systems the result also follows from
[
23
, Thm. 5]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a van Hove sequence, and let
\gamma
be the autocorrelation of
\mu
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
. If for some
\chi\in\widehat{G}
the Fourier–Bohr coefficient
a_{\chi}(\mu)
exists uniformly, then we get

\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=\left|a_{\chi}(\mu)\right|^{2}\,.

In particular, if
\mu
has uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum, then
\mu
satisfies CPP (
2.1
).
∎

It follows from Thm.
4.1
that, for measures with uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum, null Fourier–Bohr spectrum is equivalent to continuous diffraction spectrum.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
be a measure with uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

The measure
\mu
has null Fourier–Bohr spectrum with respect one van Hove sequence
{\mathcal{A}}
.

(ii)

The measure
\mu
has null Fourier–Bohr spectrum with respect all van Hove sequences.

(iii)

There exists an autocorrelation
\gamma
of
\mu
such that
\widehat{\gamma}_{\mathsf{pp}}=0
.

(iv)

For every autocorrelation
\gamma
of
\mu
we have
\widehat{\gamma}_{\mathsf{pp}}=0
.

∎

Let us also note in passing that if the Fourier–Bohr spectrum exists uniformly, then the Bragg diffraction spectrum is independent of the choice of the van Hove sequence.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
be a measure with uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
be autocorrelations of
\mu
with respect to the van Hove sequences
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
, respectively. Then

(\widehat{\gamma_{1}})_{\mathsf{pp}}=(\widehat{\gamma_{2}})_{\mathsf{pp}}\,.

Moreover, if
\gamma
is the autocorrelation of
\mu
with respect to some van Hove sequence
{\mathcal{A}}
, the Bragg diffraction spectrum

7.
The generalized Eberlein decomposition

Here we discuss the existence of the so called generalized Eberlein decomposition, the known progress in this direction as well as the importance of any such result for diffraction theory.
We start with the following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
and
{\mathcal{A}}
a van Hove sequence. We say that
\omega
admits a
generalized Eberlein decomposition with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
if there exist some measures

\omega_{\mathsf{s}},\omega_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)

\omega=\omega_{\mathsf{s}}+\omega_{0}\,.

(a)

We have
\omega_{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathcal{B}\hskip-1.0pt{\mathsf{ap}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(G)
.

(b)

The twisted Eberlein convolution
\text{\textlquill}\omega_{\mathsf{s}},\omega_{0}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
exists and
\text{\textlquill}\omega_{\mathsf{s}},\omega_{0}\text{\textrquill}_{{\mathcal{A}}}=0
.

(c)

The autocorrelation
\Psi
of
\omega_{0}
exists with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\widehat{\Psi}
is purely continuous measure.

If the measures
\omega_{\mathsf{s}},\omega_{0}
can be further chosen to have uniform Fourier–Bohr spectrum, then we say that
\omega
admits a
uniform generalized Eberlein decomposition with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Remark 7.2
.

(a)

If
\omega
admits a uniform generalized Eberlein decomposition with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
, then the Fourier–Bohr coefficients
a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\omega),a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\omega_{\mathsf{s}}),a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\omega_{0})

satisfy the (CPP) and, for all

a_{\chi}(\omega)=a_{\chi}(\omega_{\mathsf{s}})\quad;\quad a_{\chi}(\omega_{0})=0\,.

(b)

If
\omega

{\mathcal{A}}

[

\omega=\omega_{\mathsf{s}}+\omega_{0}
being a generalized Eberlein decomposition with respect to all van Hove sequences.

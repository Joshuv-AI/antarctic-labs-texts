Equivariant Tutte polynomial
Mario Bauer and Matěj Doležálek and Magdaléna Mišinová and Semen Słobodianiuk and Julian Weigert

Abstract
. We use the equivariant cohomology ring of the permutohedral variety to study matroids and their invariants. Investigating the pushforward of matroid Chern classes defined in [ BEST ] to the product space ℙ n × ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n} , we establish an equivariant generalization of the Tutte polynomial of a matroid. This was suggested in [ Mi , §4.5] . We discuss how this polynomial encodes properties of the matroid by looking at special evaluations. We further introduce an equivariant generalization of the reduced characteristic polynomial of a matroid.

Definition 1.2

(Equivariant Tutte polynomial)
.

Let
M
be a matroid with ground set
E
. Then we define the
equivariant Tutte polynomial
of
M
as the following four-variable polynomial with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}[E]
:

\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e}).

By convention, we set
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=1\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for
M
supported on the empty ground set.

The remainder of Section
4
is dedicated to checking
basic properties of this polynomial such as recursive behavior, compatibility with direct sums and duals of matroids and valuativity.

One reason for the popularity of the usual Tutte polynomial is that this invariant can specify to any other generalized Tutte-Grothendieck invariant, that is any invariant behaving nicely with respect to deletion and contraction of elements of the matroid. In Section
5
we will show that the equivariant Tutte polynomial satisfies a similar universality result among functions assigning some value in a ring to labeled matroids and behaving well with respect to deletion and contraction.

In the final chapter we are interested in the combinatorial properties of the equivariant Tutte polynomial. One of the key results is Proposition
6.2
which states that setting the last two variables
(r,s)
to
(1,0)
(respectively
(0,1)
) read as a polynomial in the variables
t_{e}
has coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
that are usual Tutte polynomials of smaller matroids obtained from
M
via contraction (respectively deletion) of subsets. This allows us to identify a number of combinatorial interpretations for certain evaluations of the equivariant Tutte polynomial, they are summarized in table
1
. We also define an equivariant analogue of the reduced characteristic polynomial of a matroid, which for graphic matroids is closely related to the chromatic polynomial. Just like for the Tutte polynomial this is done by performing the pushforward of
c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}])
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
equivariantly. The result is the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
M
be a matroid on a groudset
E
. We define the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial
\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][q]
of
M
as follows:

\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)=\frac{(-1)^{\rank(M)}}{q-1}\cdot\widehat{T}_{M}(1-q,0,q,1)=\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{S\subset E}q^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(-1)^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{e\notin S}(1+t_{e})\prod_{e\in S}(1+qt_{e}).

We observe that both
\widehat{T}_{M}
and
\widehat{\chi}_{M}
as well as many of their specializations recover the labelled matroid
M
. Our final result investigates precisely which evaluations of
\widehat{T}_{M}
can still recover
M
.

2.1.
Localization and Gysin pushforward

For the discussion which follows we make a technical definition:

Definition 2.1
.

We say that characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
of a torus
T
are
relatively prime
if there are no
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\chi_{1}^{a}=\chi_{2}^{b}
.

We recall a version of the localization principle, known as Goresky-Kottwitz-MacPherson theorem, see
[

.

.

Let
X
be a nonsingular variety with an action of a torus
T
such that the set of torus fixed points

finite. Let

be the equivariant cohomology ring with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Assume that
H_{T}(X)
is free over
H_{T}(\mathord{\mathrm{pt}})
. Suppose that for each
p\in X^{T}
, the characters acting on the tangent space over

are relatively prime.
Then the ring
H_{T}(X^{T})
is just
\Pi_{p\in X^{T}}H_{T}(\mathord{\mathrm{pt}})
, and
H_{T}(X)\subset H_{T}(X^{T})
consists of exactly those tuples
(f_{p})_{p\in X^{T}}\in H_{T}(X^{T})

where the difference
f_{p}-f_{q}
is divisible by the character acting on
1
-dimensional orbit
c_{p,q}
connecting points
p,q\in X^{T}
for all such
c_{p,q}
.

Suppose that we have smooth varieties
X,Y
equipped with action of a torus
T
, such that the torus-fixed loci

and

are finite. Fix an equivarant map
f:X\to Y
. Let
\iota_{X}:X^{T}\to X

\iota_{Y}:Y^{T}\to Y

f^{*}:H_{T}(Y)\to H_{T}(X)

viewed from the perspective of localization principle looks as follows:

(f\circ\iota_{X})^{*}(c)=\big(\iota_{Y}^{*}(c)_{f(p)}\big)_{p\in X^{T}}.

For every

and

denote by
\mathcal{T}^{X}_{p}
and
\mathcal{T}^{Y}_{q}

For a class

2.2.
The permutohedron

Let
S_{n+1}
be the group of all bijections of the set
[n]=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
onto itself. For every
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
we define the corresponding point
p_{\sigma}
to be

p_{\sigma}:=(\sigma^{-1}(0),\sigma^{-1}(1),\dots,\sigma^{-1}(n))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

The
permutohedron

P_{n}
is
defined as the convex hull of the
p_{\sigma}
. One can easily show that it has nonempty interior in the affine hyperplane
\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:\sum_{i=0}^{n}v_{i}=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\}
, hence it is an
n
-dimensional polytope, and its vertices are exactly the
p_{\sigma}
.
For every nonempty, proper subset
S\subsetneq[n]
we have a corresponding facet
F_{S}
of
P_{n}
, where

F_{S}=\conv(\{v\in P_{n}\mid v_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,|S|-1\}\text{ for all }i\in S\}).

Consider the linear functional
\ell_{S}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}},v\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}v_{i}
. Since every
v\in P_{n}
clearly satisfies
\ell_{S}(v)\geq 0+1+\ldots+(|S|-1)=:c_{S}
,
P_{n}
is contained in the closed halfspace
\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ell_{S}(v)\geq c_{s}\}
and the intersection of
P_{n}
with the affine hyperplane
\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ell_{S}(v)=c_{S}\}
is exactly
F_{S}
.
In fact every facet of
P_{n}
arises in this way. This also determines all of the faces of
P_{n}
, since every face is the intersection of the facets containing it. Two vertices
p_{\sigma},p_{\sigma^{\prime}}
of
P_{n}
are connected by an edge, if and only if there is some
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
such that
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\circ(i,i+1).

2.3.
The permutohedral variety

Let us consider an action of the
(n+1)
-dimensional torus
T\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n+1}
on a vector space
V\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
given in coordinates by

(t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\cdot(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(t_{0}x_{0},t_{1}x_{1},\dots,t_{n}x_{n})

for every
(t_{i})_{i}\in T
and
(x_{i})_{i}\in V
. The above action induces a natural action of
T
on
\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
as well as on
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1}
, where
k
is any positive integer.
Let
\binom{[n]}{k}
denote the set of all
k
-element subsets of
[n]
. If
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}
is the standard basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, the vectors
(\bigwedge_{i\in S}e_{i})_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}
form a basis of
\bigwedge^{k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, which is why we use coordinates
p=[p_{S}]_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}
or often just
p=[p_{S}]_{S}
for a point
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1}
.

For all
k=1,\ldots,n
we have an embedding

(2)

\phi_{k}:T\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1},(t_{i})_{i}\mapsto\big[\prod_{i\in S}t_{i}\big]_{S}.

Let
I_{k}
be the ideal of
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
generated by all monomials of degree
k
. We define the map

(3)

\phi:T\to\prod_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1}

as the product of the maps
\phi_{k}
.
The
n
-dimensional
permutohedral variety

\Pi_{n}
is defined as the closure of the graph of the map
\phi
. Since the graph of
\phi
is constructible, Zariski- and Euclidean closure coincide.
Using the Segre embedding
\psi:\prod_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k}-1}
one can also view the above construction as a blowup of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

along the closed scheme given by the homogeneous ideal
I:=\prod_{k=2}^{n}I_{k}
. One can also realize this blowup as a sequence of consecutive blowups: starting from
I_{2}
and ending at
I_{n}
, which corresponds to firstly blowing up the points that have all but one coordinate nonzero, secondly blowing up the strict transforms of the projective lines given by the vanishing all but two coordinates, and so on. Note that at every step the strict transforms will be disjoint, because the previous blowup was along an intersection. In particular every consecutive blowup will be smooth and toric, since we always blow up along smooth, torus invariant loci. It can be shown that
\Pi
is a resolution of singularities of the graph of the
Cremona transformation

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}),

which simply inverts homogeneous coordinates. Note that this rational map is
T
-equivariant.
If we consider the map

(4)

\psi\circ\phi:T\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n+1}{k}-1},

one can easily see that the permutohedron
P_{n}
is the polytope corresponding to the permutohedral variety, since
P_{n}
is the Minkowski-sum of the hypersimplices
\Delta_{k}:=\conv(\sum_{i\in S}e_{i}:S\subseteq[n],|S|=k)
, and the Minkowski-sum of polytopes corresponds exactly to the Segre-embedding of varieties.

2.4.
Torus orbits and the Cohomology Ring of
\Pi_{n}

So far we worked with
n+1
dimensional torus
T\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n+1}
acting on
\Pi_{n}
, but there is a smaller, embedded torus
i:T^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
which is just quotient of
T
by the one-dimensional isotropy subtorus scaling all coordinates of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
simultanously.
Note that the map
p:T\to T^{\prime}
is surjective. In particular orbits of the action of
T
are orbits of the action of
T^{\prime}
and we can use toric geometry to easily describe the latter.
For any projective toric variety that is given by a polytope, the torus fixed points correspond to the vertices, and one-dimensional orbits correspond to the edges of the polytope. It is thus convenient to index fixed points of
\Pi_{n}
by permutations.

Remark 2.4
.

A torus-fixed point
q_{\sigma}
corresponding to a vertex
p_{\sigma}
can be obtained as a limit of a special monomial curve
c^{\sigma}
defined on
T^{\prime}
. Firstly, consider the curve

c_{1}^{\sigma}:{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to T,\ t\to(t^{\sigma^{-1}(0)},t^{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,t^{\sigma^{-1}(n)})).

Composing with the map
i\circ p
yields a curve
c^{\sigma}
in the permutohedral variety
\Pi_{n}
. Identifing
\Pi_{n}
with the image of its embedding coming from the polytope
P_{n}
(cf. (
4
)), we see that
\lim_{|t|\to\infty}c^{\sigma}(t)=[0:\dots:0:1:0:\dots:0]=q_{\sigma}
, where the only nonzero coordinate is the one indexed by the vertex
p_{\sigma}
.

In a similar manner one can show that the one-dimensional orbits are tori given by the nonvanishing of coordinates
p_{\sigma}
and
p_{\sigma\circ(i,i+1)}
and compute that
T
acts on this orbit with the character
t_{\sigma(i)}\cdot t^{-1}_{\sigma(i+1)}
.
Combining the above data with Theorem
2.2
we arrive at the following result:

Proposition 2.5
.

The equivariant cohomology ring
H_{T}(\Pi_{n})
of
\Pi_{n}
can be viewed as a subring of

H_{T}(\Pi_{n}^{T})=\prod_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}}{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]

via restriction to the fixed points. An element
(f_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}}\in H_{T}(\Pi_{n}^{T})
belongs to the image of this map, if and only if
f_{\sigma}-f_{\sigma\circ(i,i+1)}=0\pmod{\ t_{\sigma(i)}-t_{\sigma(i+1)}}
holds for all
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

2.5.
Equivariant cohomology of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and the map
\pi^{n}:\Pi_{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

Let us consider again the
(n+1)
-dimensional torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n+1}
that acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
by scaling each coordinate, as in Section
2.3
. The space
\bigwedge^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
can be identified with the dual vector space of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
. We thus fix the convention that
t=(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})\in T
acts on
\bigwedge^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
by scaling the
i
-th basis vector
\bigwedge_{j\neq i}e_{j}
with
t_{i}^{-1}
, where the
e_{i}
are the standard basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
.
Proposition
6.1
from
[
AF
, §2]
allows us to describe the equivariant cohomology ring of
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
:

H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}))=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}][\alpha]}{\big(\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\alpha+t_{i})\big)}.

Here the variables
t_{i}
correspond to the equivariant first Chern class of the trivial bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
, with action given by rescaling the
i
-th coordinate by
t_{i}
,
and
\alpha
is the equivariant first Chern class of the dual of the equivariant tautological bundle. Similarly, because we fixed the convention of the action of
T
on
\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, we get the natural identification

H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}))=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}][\beta]}{\big(\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\beta-t_{i})\big)}.

Here
\beta
is again the equivariant first Chern class of the dual tautological bundle.
The product
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\times{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
is one of the main varieties we will discuss in this article. By abuse of notation we will often just write
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The Kunneth formula yields that

(5)

H_{T}\left({\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n})\right)=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}][\alpha,\beta]}{\big(\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\alpha+t_{i}),\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\beta-t_{i})\big)}.

Every element of the equivariant cohomology ring can be identified with a polynomial
F
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}][\alpha,\beta]
of degree less than
n+1
with respect to both
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Note that for every torus fixed points
a\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
and
b\in{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
we have a fixed point
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\times{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
in the product, and every fixed point arises in such way.
We now show how to pass from the description (
5
) to the description via localization.

Proposition 2.6
.

The restriction map
H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n})\to H_{T}([{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}]^{T})
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}][\alpha,\beta]}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\alpha+t_{i}),\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\beta-t_{i})}\to\prod_{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{0},\dots,t_{n}],

where each
t_{k}
is mapped to
t_{k}
,
\alpha
is mapped to
(-t_{i})_{i,j}
and
\beta
is mapped to
(t_{j})_{i,j}
.

Indeed, the torus acts on the product of tautological bundles
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})}(-1)\times\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})}(-1)
over fixed point
(i,j)
with characters
t_{i}
and
-t_{j}
respectively.

For
n>1
we define

(6)

\pi^{n}:\Pi_{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\times{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})

to be the composition of the closed embedding
\Pi_{n}\to\prod_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}))
with the natural projection to the first and the last factors. For
n=1
we see that
\Pi_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and we define
\pi^{1}:\Pi_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
to be the identity on the first factor and the Cremona transformation on the second (note that the Cremona transformation is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and in particular a regular map).
In the case
n=0
we define
\Pi_{0}
to be a point and formally
\pi^{0}:\Pi_{0}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{0}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{0}
is the identity.

Remark 2.7
.

T
-fixed points of
\Pi_{n}
are mapped to
T
-fixed points of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
While the fixed points of
\Pi_{n}
are indexed by permutations, the fixed points of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
can be indexed by
[n]\times[n]
in a natural way.
Note that the image of
\phi,\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{n}
is dense in respectively
\Pi_{n},{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
and
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
. In particular, fixed points can be described as limits of monomial curves as in Remark
2.4
.
Composing a curve
c_{1}^{\sigma}
(as in Remark
2.4
) with
\pi^{n}\circ\phi=\phi_{1}\times\phi_{n}
, and looking at the coordinate with the largest exponent, we see that the torus-fixed point
q_{\sigma}
gets mapped to
(\sigma(n),\sigma(0))
by
\pi^{n}
.

2.6.
Matroids and their tautological (Chern) classes

A
matroid

M
is a pair
M=(E,B)
, where
E
is a finite set and
B
is a non-empty collection of subsets of
E
, called the
bases
of
M
. The bases of
M
are required to satisfy the
base exchange property
, which is inspired by the Steinitz exchange lemma for vector spaces: If
B_{1},B_{2}\in B
are two bases and if
v\in B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
, then there exists some
w\in B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
such that
(B_{1}\setminus\{v\})\cup\{w\}\in B
is a basis. Matroids generalize the concept of linear dependence in a vector space. For a comprehensive introduction to matroid theory we recommend
[
Ox
]
.

When a subset
S\subseteq E
is considered, we denote its
rank
as
\rank_{M}(S)
and its
nullity
as
\nullity_{M}(S):=|S|-\rank_{M}(S)
. We also write
\rank(M):=\rank_{M}(E)
and
\corank(M):=\nullity_{M}(E)
for the rank of
M
and its dual respectively. We further say that an element
e\in E
is
\emph{general}
(in
M
), if it is neither a loop nor a coloop in
M
. By abuse of notation, we will sometimes write
\emptyset
for the unique matroid with groundset
E=\emptyset
.

For this article the most important invariant of a matroid will be its
Tutte polynomial

T_{M}(x,y)
, which was defined in
[
BO
]
as

T_{M}(x,y)=\sum_{S\subset E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}\cdot(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y].

This invariant satisfies the following deletion-contraction relation for every matroid
M=(E,B)
and each element
e\in E
:

T_{M}(x,y)=\begin{cases}T_{M\setminus e}(x,y)+T_{M\mathbin{/}e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is a general element in $M$,}\\
yT_{M\setminus e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is a loop in $M$,}\\
xT_{M\mathbin{/}e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is a coloop in $M$.}\end{cases}

Together with the base case
T_{\emptyset}(x,y)=1
this also uniquely determines
T_{M}(x,y)
for every matroid.

In
[
BEST
]
, the authors assign classes in the equivariant cohomology ring of the permutohedral variety to a matroid
M
with groundset
[n]:=\{0,\ldots,n\}
. To do so they consider the
lex-first-basis
of
M
associated to a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
, which can be constructed as follows.
First order the elements of the groundset according to
\sigma
:

(\sigma(0),\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(n)).

Set
I_{-1}=\emptyset
and traverse the above list from left to right in
n+1
steps. For
k=0,\ldots,n
set
I_{k}:=I_{k-1}\cup\{\sigma(k)\}
if
I_{k-1}\cup\{\sigma(k)\}
is independent and set
I_{k}:=I_{k-1}
otherwise. Then
I_{n}
will be a basis of
M
and varying
\sigma
every basis of
M
will arise in this way. Equivalently
I_{n}
is the first basis that appears if one orders all bases of
M
lexicographically according to
\sigma
.

Definition 2.8

(lex-first-basis)
.

The basis
I_{n}
in the above is called the
\emph{lex-first-basis}
of
M
associated to the permutation
\sigma
and is denoted by
B_{\sigma}
.

For any finite set
E
, we define

{\mathbb{Z}}[E]:={\mathbb{Z}}[t_{e}\mid e\in E]

to be the polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with free variables indexed elements of
E
. For
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
we will consider
{\mathbb{Z}}[E^{\prime}]
as a subring of
{\mathbb{Z}}[E]
.

Definition 2.9

(tautological classes of matroids)
.

Let us fix a matroid
M
on ground set
E=[n]
. We define the
i
-th tautological sub Chern class
of the matroid
M
as
c_{i}([\mathcal{S}_{M}])\in\prod_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}}{\mathbb{Z}}[E]
with

(7)

c_{i}([\mathcal{S}_{M}]):=(\elem_{i}(\{-t_{e}\}_{e\in B_{\sigma}}))_{\sigma},

where
\elem_{j}
stands for the
j
-th elementary symmetric polynomial.

Similarly, we define the
i
-th tautological quotient Chern class

c_{i}([\mathcal{Q}_{M}])
through

(8)

c_{i}([\mathcal{Q}_{M}]):=(\elem_{i}(\{-t_{e}\}_{e\notin B_{\sigma}}))_{\sigma}.

Using Theorem
2.2
one can check that the class
c_{i}([\mathcal{S}_{M}])
lies in the image of the restriction
H_{T}(\Pi_{n})\to H_{T}(\Pi_{n}^{T})
. In Section
2.7
, we supply a concrete geometrical meaning of the tautological classes for representable matroids: this is the first equivariant Chern class of the bundle
\mathcal{S}
in the Lemma.

We will also work with
i
-th Chern classes of the duals of the above which are defined simply by changing
-t_{e}
to
t_{e}
in all polynomials and geometrically indeed corresponds to taking the dual. For example:

c_{i}([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}]):=\elem_{i}(\{t_{e}\}_{e\in B_{\sigma}})_{\sigma}.

To keep track of all classes at the same time we will consider the following graded classes.

Definition 2.10
.

We define the
graded total Chern sub-class

c([\mathcal{S}_{M}],x)\in H_{T}(\Pi_{n})[x]
of a matroid
M
to be

c([\mathcal{S}_{M}],x):=\sum_{j=0}^{\rank(M)}c_{j}([\mathcal{S}_{M}])x^{j}.

Similarly we have the
graded total Chern quotient-class

c([\mathcal{S}_{M}],y)\in H_{T}(\Pi_{n})[y]
of a matroid which is

c([\mathcal{S}_{M}],y):=\sum_{j=0}^{\corank(M)}c_{j}([\mathcal{Q}_{M}])y^{j}.

2.7.
The representable case

A matroid
M
on a groundset
[n]
is called
representable
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
if there exists a set of vectors
\{v_{0},\dots,v_{n}\}
inside some vector space over
{\mathbb{K}}
such that
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is independent if and only if
I
is independent in the matroid
M
.

We now describe how one can associate a geometric structure to a matroid
M
. At first suppose that
M
is representable by a set of vectors
\{\bar{e}_{0},\dots,\bar{e}_{n}\}
in a quotient space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}/L
for some
r
-dimensional space
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
.
To the subspace
L
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
we associate an equivariant bundle
\mathcal{S}_{L}
.
This bundle is a subbundle of an equivariant trivial bundle isomorphic to
\Pi_{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
with
t_{i}\in T_{n+1}
acting on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
by scaling
e_{i}
by
t_{i}^{-1}
. As seen in Section
2.3
, we have a morphism
\Phi:T\to T/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{n}
of the
(n+1)
-dimensional torus to the permutohedral variety. We start by defining
\mathcal{S}_{L}
to be
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
over
\Phi(1:\ldots:1),
and use the torus action of
T
on
L
to extend it to the whole image of
\Phi
, by putting the vector space
t\cdot L:=\{(t_{0}^{-1}v_{0},\ldots,t_{n}^{-1}v_{n})\mid v\in L\}
over the point
\Phi(t)
. Note that if
t,t^{\prime}\in T
differ by a constant factor
c\in C^{*}
, then
t\cdot L=t^{\prime}\cdot L
, so this is well-defined.
Since
\Phi(T)
is dense in
\Pi_{n}
, for arbitrary
p\in\Pi_{n}
there exists a sequence
(t^{(m)})_{m}\subset T
such that
t_{m}\to p
. We get a corresponding sequence
(t^{(m)}\cdot L)
in the Grassmannian
\Gr(r,{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
. It is a remarkable property of the permutohedral variety that this limit does not depend on the choice of the sequence
(t^{(m)})_{m}
, see
[
BEST
, Lemma 3.5]
.
By this we get a vector bundle
\mathcal{S}_{L}
over the permutohedral variety.
By doing this for the quotient space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}/L
instead of
L
, we get a vector bundle
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
, which is also the dual bundle of
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
.

In the following lemma we describe the characters acting on the bundle
\mathcal{S}_{L}
over the fixed points. In other words we have to determine what subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
corresponds to the fiber of
\mathcal{S}_{L}
over a fixed point and read off the characters of the torus action on this space.

Lemma 2.11
.

In description oh
H_{T}(\Pi_{n})
via localization,
the first equivariant Chern class
c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{L})
is equal to
(-\sum_{i\in B_{\sigma}}t_{i})_{\sigma}
. In other words, if
L_{\sigma}
is the fiber of
\mathcal{S}_{L}
over
q_{\sigma}
, then

L_{\sigma}=\Span(e_{i}\mid i\in B_{\sigma}).

We will only sketch a proof here. For a formal proof we refer to
[
BEST
, 3.7]
.

We look at a torus fixed point
q_{\sigma}
corresponding to a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
.
Consider the ordered basis
e_{\sigma(0)},e_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,e_{\sigma(n)}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
.
Recall that we can reach
q_{\sigma}
as a limit of a curve
c_{\sigma}
as described in Remark
2.4
. Note that the curve is connecting the point
\Phi(1:\ldots:1)
with
q_{\sigma}
and we know the fiber over
\Phi(1:\ldots:1)
. All we have to do is to act on the fiber along the segment joining the two points and see what we get in the limit. Namely on
e_{\sigma(i)}
we act with
t^{-\sigma^{-1}(\sigma(i))}=t^{-i}
.

Write the spanning vectors of
L
in the rows of a matrix and take the reduced row echelon form of the matrix. By definition
B_{\sigma}=:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
is the set of indices of columns that contain a leading 1 in the reduced row echelon form.
Consider the
k
-th row of our matrix, where
k\leq r=\dim(L)
:

(0,\ldots,0,1,*,\ldots,*)

where
1
appears on position
i_{k}
.
Acting on the span of this vector and taking
t\to\infty
we obtain
\Span(e_{i_{k}})
, so
e_{i_{k}}\in L_{\sigma}
. Performing the above for all
k
we see that the subspace
L
degenerates to the subspace spanned by
(e_{i_{1}},e_{i_{2}},...,e_{i_{r}})
.

3.
Pushforward of graded total chern classes

We consider the result in this section as an equivariant analog of
[
BEST
, Theorem A]
.
We will establish a closed formula for the pushforward of product of
c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)
along the map
\pi^{n}:X_{E}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
(see formula (
6
) in Section
2.5
for the definition of
\pi^{n}
and Definition
2.10
for graded Chern classes).
Adapting the approach of
[
BEST
, §4]
, we will accomplish this by proving analogous deletion-contraction relations for both the pushforward and the desired closed formula, allowing for a straightforward inductive proof. Relating to notation from Proposition
2.3
we will abbreviate
\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}
for
\mathcal{T}_{q_{\sigma}}
for a fixed point
q_{\sigma}\in\Pi_{n}
. Similarly we set
\mathcal{T}_{a,b}
to be the product of the characters acting on the tangent space over a point in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})\times{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
indexed by
(a,b)\in[n]\times[n]
(see Remark
2.7
for more about indexing).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be a matroid on a ground set
E
of cardinality
n+1
, where
n\geq 0
. Then for any
a,b\in E
, we have

\pi^{n}_{*}\Big(c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)\Big)_{a,b}=F_{M}(-t_{a},t_{b},z,w),

where
F_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][\alpha,\beta,z,w]
is a polynomial given by

\displaystyle F_{M}(\alpha,\beta,z,w)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\sum_{S\subset{E}}(1-\alpha z)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(1+\alpha w)^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}(1+\beta z)^{\rank_{M}(S)}\cdot{}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt{}\cdot(1-\beta w)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(\alpha+t_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(\beta-t_{e}).

Observe that
F_{M}
is indeed a polynomial, since mod
\alpha+\beta
, the sum becomes

\sum_{S\subseteq E}(1+\beta z)^{\rank(M)}(1-\beta w)^{\corank(M)}\prod_{e\in E}(\beta-t_{e})\cdot(-1)^{|S|}=\\
=\left[(1+\beta z)^{\rank(M)}(1-\beta w)^{\corank(M)}\prod_{e\in E}(\beta-t_{e})\right]\cdot(1-1)^{|E|}=0.

Note that when viewing the cohomology of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
as (
5
), the Theorem simply states that

\pi^{n}_{*}\Big(c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)\Big)=F_{M}(\alpha,\beta,z,w)

holds in
H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n})[z,w]
.
For convenience, let us denote
\xi_{M}:=c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)
. We first establish a deletion-contraction relation for
F_{M}
:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\hat{e}\in E
. Then

F_{M}=\begin{cases}(1+\alpha w)(\beta-t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\setminus\hat{e}}+(1+\beta z)(\alpha+t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}},&\text{if $\hat{e}$ is a general element in $M$,}\\
(1-t_{\hat{e}}w)(\alpha+\beta)F_{M\setminus\hat{e}},&\text{if $\hat{e}$ is a loop in $M$,}\\
(1+t_{\hat{e}}z)(\alpha+\beta)F_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}},&\text{if $\hat{e}$ is a coloop in $M$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

In the sum over
S\subseteq E
defining
F_{M}
, let us split the terms into a sum over
{\hat{e}}\notin S\subseteq E
and another sum over
{\hat{e}}\in S\subseteq E
.

Let us first work in the case when
{\hat{e}}
is general. For
{\hat{e}}\notin S\subseteq E
, we then have
\rank_{M}(S)=\rank_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)
, and thus
\nullity_{M}(S)=\nullity_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)
, as well as
\rank(M)=\rank(M\setminus{\hat{e}})
and
\corank(M)=\corank(M\setminus\hat{e})+1
, giving

\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\sum_{\hat{e}\notin S\subset{E}}(1-\alpha z)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(1+\alpha w)^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}(1+\beta z)^{\rank_{M}(S)}(1-\beta w)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\cdot{}\\
{}\cdot\prod_{e\in S}(\alpha+t_{e})\prod_{e\in E\setminus S}(\beta-t_{e}).

=(1+\alpha w)(\beta-t_{e})\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\sum_{S\subseteq E\setminus\{{\hat{e}}\}}(1-\alpha z)^{\rank(M\setminus\hat{e})-\rank_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)}(1+\alpha w)^{\corank(M\setminus{\hat{e}})-\nullity_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)}\cdot{}\\
{}\cdot(1+\beta z)^{\rank_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)}(1-\beta w)^{\nullity_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(\alpha+t_{e})\prod_{e\in(E\setminus\{{\hat{e}}\})\setminus S}(\beta-t_{e})

=(1+\alpha w)(\beta-t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}.\\

In a similar vein, if we consider
{\hat{e}}\in S\subseteq E
and denote
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus\{{\hat{e}}\}
, we observe
\rank_{M}(S)=\rank_{M\mathbin{/}{\hat{e}}}(S^{\prime})+1
,
\nullity_{M}(S)=\nullity_{M\mathbin{/}{\hat{e}}}(S^{\prime})
(in particular, this holds for
S=E
) and obtain

\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\sum_{\hat{e}\in S\subset{E}}(1-\alpha z)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(1+\alpha w)^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}(1+\beta z)^{\rank_{M}(S)}(1-\beta w)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\cdot{}\\
{}\cdot\prod_{e\in S}(\alpha+t_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(\beta-t_{e}).

=(1+\beta z)(\alpha+t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\mathbin{/}{\hat{e}}},\\

which finishes the proof of the case when
{\hat{e}}
is a general element.

Since the loop and coloop cases are very analogous, let us only show the former. As in the previous case, the
{\hat{e}}\notin S\subseteq E
terms contribute
(1+\alpha w)(\beta-t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}
. But since we know
{\hat{e}}
is a loop, we may relate the
\hat{e}\in S\subseteq E
terms to
F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}
as well. In particular, denoting
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{{\hat{e}}\}
, we get
\rank_{M}(S)=\rank_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S^{\prime})
and
\nullity_{M}(S)=\nullity_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S^{\prime})+1
(again, this holds for
S=E
as a special case), so the
{\hat{e}}\in S\subseteq E
terms contribute

(1-\beta w)(\alpha+t_{\hat{e}})F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}},

giving

F_{M}=\Big((1+\alpha w)(\beta-t_{\hat{e}})+(1-\beta w)(\alpha+t_{\hat{e}})\Big)F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}=(1-t_{\hat{e}}w)(\alpha+\beta)F_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}

in total.
∎

Now we focus on establishing an analogous relation for
\pi^{n}_{*}(\xi_{M})
. Since in the map
\pi^{n}:X_{E}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, the torus-fixed point of
X_{E}
corresponding to a permutation
\tilde{\sigma}\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E)
will be mapped to the torus-fixed point of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
corresponding to the pair
(\tilde{\sigma}(n),\tilde{\sigma}(0))
, we write the pushforward in terms of the localizations at fixed points using the respective tangent directions as

(9)

\pi^{n}_{*}(\xi_{M})_{a,b}=\mathcal{T}^{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}_{a,b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{\sigma}\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E)\\
\tilde{\sigma}(n)=a\\
\tilde{\sigma}(0)=b\end{subarray}}\frac{(\xi_{M})_{\tilde{\sigma}}}{\mathcal{T}^{X_{E}}_{\tilde{\sigma}}}.

In the following, we will treat permutations as ordered tuples of elements. The main idea for the deletion-contraction relation for this pushforward is that we group together the terms for those
\tilde{\sigma}\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E)
that, as tuples, yield the same tuple
\sigma\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E\setminus\{\hat{e}\})
by omitting the element
\hat{e}
. For this, we use several observations from
[
BEST
]
connecting
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\sigma
in this situation. We denote by
\sigma^{\ell}
, for
\ell=0,\dots,n
, the permutation in
{\operatorname{Bij}}(E)
that is obtained from
\sigma
by inserting
\hat{e}
in position
\ell
, i.e. so that
\sigma^{\ell}(\ell)=\hat{e}
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
BEST
]
, Lemma 4.4, Definition 4.5)
.

Consider a matroid
M
on ground set
E
and a permutation
\sigma\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E\setminus\{\hat{e}\})
. Then there exists an index
k_{\sigma}=k_{\sigma}(M)\in\{-1,\dots,n\}
such that

•

B_{\sigma^{\ell}}(M)=B_{\sigma}(M\mathbin{/}\hat{e})\sqcup\{\hat{e}\}
whenever
\ell\leq k_{\sigma}
,

•

B_{\sigma^{\ell}}(M)=B_{\sigma}(M\setminus\hat{e})
whenever
\ell>k_{\sigma}
, and

•

B_{\sigma}(M\mathbin{/}\hat{e})\sqcup\{k_{\sigma}\}=B_{\sigma}(M\setminus\hat{e})
.

Note that
\hat{e}
is a loop exactly when
k_{\sigma}=-1
, and a coloop exactly when
k_{\sigma}=n
.

As an immediate consequence of these relations of bases, we can observe the behavior of Chern classes under this omission-insertion:

Lemma 3.4

(
[
BEST
]
, Lemma 4.6)
.

Let
M
be a matroid on ground set
E\ni\hat{e}
and consider
\sigma\in{\operatorname{Bij}}(E\setminus\{\hat{e}\})
.

(a)

For any
\ell=0,\dots,n
, we have

\displaystyle c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M}],z)_{\sigma^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(1+t_{\hat{e}}z)\cdot c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}],z)_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell\leq k_{\sigma}(M)$,}\\
c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}],z)_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell>k_{\sigma}(M)$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)_{\sigma^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}c([\mathcal{Q}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}],w)_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell\leq k_{\sigma}(M)$,}\\
(1-t_{\hat{e}}w)\cdot c([\mathcal{Q}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}],w)_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell>k_{\sigma}(M)$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle(\xi_{M})_{\sigma^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(1+t_{\hat{e}}z)\cdot(\xi_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}})_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell\leq k_{\sigma}(M)$,}\\
(1-t_{\hat{e}}w)\cdot(\xi_{M\setminus\hat{e}})_{\sigma},&\text{if $\ell>k_{\sigma}(M)$.}\end{cases}

(b)

If
\hat{e}
is a general element in
M
, i.e. if

is neither

nor

\displaystyle(1+t_{\sigma(k)}z)\cdot c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}],z)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}],z)_{\sigma},

\displaystyle(1-t_{\sigma(k)}w)\cdot c([\mathcal{Q}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}],w)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=c([\mathcal{Q}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}],w)_{\sigma}.

With these lemmas, we may prove the deletion-contraction relation for the pushforward:

Proposition 3.5

Let

and
\pi^{n}_{*}(\xi_{M})_{a,b}
satisfy the same deletion-contraction relations. Hence, once we verify their equality on

E
will prove the theorem.

Given
a

from it and apply the deletion-contraction relations along with the inductive hypothesis to prove the theorem. Thus, as base cases, it suffices to check the cases when

4.
Equivariant Tutte polynomial

In this section, we define a polynomial invariant of matroids that generalizes the usual Tutte polynomial to an equivariant context, and relate it to the pushforward of
c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)
. We are motivated in this by a non-equivariant result of
[
BEST
]
, Theorem A. There the authors prove that

(11)

\int_{X_{E}}c([\mathcal{S}_{U_{n,E}}^{\vee}],x)c([\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1,E}}],y)c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)=\\
=\frac{(y+z)^{\rank(M)}(x+w)^{\corank(M)}}{x+y}T_{M}\left(\frac{x+y}{y+z},\frac{x+y}{x+w}\right),

where
\int_{X_{E}}
denotes the degree map (i.e. pushforward to a point) and everything is considered non-equivariantly.

The left-hand side can be viewed as a non-equivariant analog of
F_{M}(\alpha,\beta,z,w)
. As we proved in Section
3
, the polynomial
F_{M}
is the pushforward of
c([\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee}],z)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],w)
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, with the variables
\alpha
,
\beta
corresponding to pullbacks of hyperplanes coming from the two projective spaces. In contrast, (
11
) is obtained by first intersecting with pullbacks of
\alpha
,
\beta
to
X_{E}
a number of times and then pushing forward to a point. Since non-equivariantly intersecting with
\alpha
,
\beta
to a power higher than the dimension of a subvariety will give zero, one may think of the repeated intersection with
\alpha
,
\beta
as a way to eliminate terms of high degree, while the degree map annihilates terms of low degree. Hence, varying the exponents
i
,
j
in
\alpha^{i},\beta^{j}
will precisely give the coefficients of the pushforward of
c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],z)c([\mathcal{S}_{M}],w)
in the non-equivariant cohomology ring of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
extended with two formal variables
z,w
, namely in
\left({\mathbb{Z}}[r,s]/\langle r^{n+1},s^{n+1}\rangle\right)[z,w]
.
These are therefore precisely the coefficients of the non-equivariant version of
F_{M}(r,s,z,w)
read as a polynomial in
r
,
s
, i.e. the polynomial in
r
,
s
that we obtain when we substitute all
t_{e}=0
in the
F_{M}
. However, note that the grading is reversed: The term that comes from intersecting with
\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}
will give the coefficient of
r^{n+1-i}s^{n+1-j}
. Therefore we will reverse the grading of the first two variables
r
,
s
in
F_{M}(r,s,z,w)
by replacing them by
\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s}
and cancelling the denominators. In other words, we will now consider

\displaystyle(rs)^{|E|-1}F_{M}\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][r,s,z,w].

From (
11
) we know that after substituting all
t_{e}=0
this will exactly be

\displaystyle\left.(rs)^{|E|-1}F_{M}\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w\right)\right|_{t_{e}=0,e\in E}=\frac{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}{r+s}T_{M}\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z},\frac{r+s}{r+w}\right).

We will now use this to motivate a definition an equivariant verion of the Tutte polynomial of a matroid
M
by not substituting the
t_{e}
-variables to zero. The polynomial
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)
that we want to define should therefore satisfy

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z},\frac{r+s}{r+w},r,s\right)

\displaystyle=(rs)^{|E|-1}\frac{r+s}{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}F_{M}\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w\right).

Unraveling the closed form of
F_{M}
, a straightforward manipulation reveals the following:

\displaystyle F_{M}\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}}\sum_{S\subset E}\bigg[\left(1+\frac{z}{s}\right)^{\rank_{M}(S)}\left(1-\frac{w}{s}\right)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\left(1-\frac{z}{r}\right)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\left(1+\frac{w}{r}\right)^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\notin S}\left(\frac{1}{s}-t_{e}\right)\prod_{e\in S}\left(t_{e}+\frac{1}{r}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{rs}{r+s}\cdot\frac{1}{(rs)^{|E|}}\sum_{S\subset E}\bigg[(s+z)^{\rank_{M}(S)}(s-w)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\left(r-z\right)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\left(r+w\right)^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\notin S}\left(1-st_{e}\right)\prod_{e\in S}\left(1+rt_{e}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}{(rs)^{|E|-1}(r+s)}\sum_{S\subset E}\bigg[\left(\frac{r-z}{s+z}\right)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}\left(\frac{s-w}{r+w}\right)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\prod_{e\notin S}\left(1-st_{e}\right)\prod_{e\in S}\left(1+rt_{e}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}{(rs)^{|E|-1}(r+s)}\sum_{S\subset E}\bigg[\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z}-1\right)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}\left(\frac{r+s}{r+w}-1\right)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\prod_{e\notin S}\left(1-st_{e}\right)\prod_{e\in S}\left(1+rt_{e}\right)\bigg].

Hence, after substituting back into (
12
), all the factors cancel and we get

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z},\frac{r+s}{r+w},r,s\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subset E}\bigg[\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z}-1\right)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}\left(\frac{r+s}{r+w}-1\right)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\prod_{e\notin S}\left(1-st_{e}\right)\prod_{e\in S}\left(1+rt_{e}\right)\bigg].

Therefore, we propose the following definition of an equivariant version of the Tutte polynomial associated to a matroid
M
:

Definition 4.1

(Equivariant Tutte polynomial)
.

Let
M
be a matroid with ground set
E
. Then we define the
equivariant Tutte polynomial
of
M
as the following four-variable polynomial with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}[E]
:

\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e}).

By convention, we set
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=1\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for
M
supported on the empty ground set.

Note that setting either
r
and
s
, or
t_{e}
for all
e
to zero recovers the usual Tutte polynomial.

The usual Tutte polynomial satisfies a number of notable properties, for example passing to the dual matroid corresponds to exchanging the variables. Further, the Tutte polynomial is also multiplicative under direct sum of matroids. It turns out that the equivariant Tutte polynomial satisfies similar properties.

Proposition 4.2

(dual matroid, sum of matroids)
.

Let
M
and
N
be matroids and
M^{*}
the dual of
M
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M^{*}}(x,y,r,s)

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M}(y,x,s,r),

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M\oplus N}

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M}\cdot\widehat{T}_{N}.

Proof.

We will prove the claim about the dual matroid first.
Since
\rank_{M^{*}}(S)=|S|+\rank_{M}(E\setminus S)-\rank(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\rank(M^{*})-\rank_{M^{*}}(S)

\displaystyle=|E\setminus S|-\rank_{M}(E\setminus S)=\nullity_{M}(E\setminus S)

and

\displaystyle\nullity_{M^{*}}(S)

\displaystyle=\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(E\setminus S)

and so

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M^{*}}(x,y,r,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq E}(x-1)^{\rank(M^{*})-\rank_{M^{*}}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M^{*}}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq E}(y-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(E\setminus S)}(x-1)^{\nullity_{M}(E\setminus S)}\prod_{e\in E\setminus S}(1+st_{e})\prod_{e\notin E\setminus S}(1+rt_{e})=

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,s,r).

In the last equality we used the fact that summing over subsets is the same as summing over their complements.

Now we will prove the second claim for matroids
M
and
N
. Set
P=M\oplus N
. If the ground sets of
M
and
N
are
E
and
F
respectively, then the ground set of
P
may be identified with the disjoint union
E\cup F
.
If we consider
S\subseteq E\cup F
and denote
S_{1}=S\cap E
,
S_{2}=S\cap F
, then
\rank_{P}(S)=\rank_{M}(S_{1})+\rank_{N}(S_{2})
. As a special case, this means
\rank(P)=\rank(M)+\rank(N)
. Lastly, since cardinality is additive with respect to disjoint unions, we also have

\nullity_{P}(S)=\nullity_{M}(S_{1})+\nullity_{N}(S_{2}).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{P}(x,y,r,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq E\cup F}(x-1)^{\rank(P)-\rank_{P}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{P}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\in(E\cup F)\setminus S}(1+st_{e})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{S_{1}\subseteq E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S_{1})}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S_{1})}\prod_{e\in S_{1}}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\in E\setminus S_{1}}(1+st_{e})\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad{}\cdot\left(\sum_{S_{2}\subseteq F}(x-1)^{\rank(N)-\rank_{N}(S_{2})}(y-1)^{\nullity_{N}(S_{2})}\prod_{e\in S_{2}}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\in F\setminus S_{2}}(1+st_{e})\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)\cdot\widehat{T}_{N}(x,y,r,s).

The second equality holds because of the additive behaviors above and the bijective correspondence between subsets
S\subseteq E\cup F
and pairs of subsets
S_{1}\subseteq E
,
S_{2}\subseteq F
.
∎

One of the definitions of the usual Tutte polynomial is by a deletion-contraction relation. Our equivariant Tutte polynomial satisfies a similar relation. In our case, the relation involves expressions that are linear in
t_{e}
, where
e
is the element being deleted-contracted. Once again, by setting all
t_{e}
to zero, one recovers the deletion-contraction relation of the usual Tutte polynomial.

Proposition 4.3

(Deletion-contraction relation of equivariant Tutte polynomial)
.

The equivariant Tutte polynomial of a matroid
M
satisfies the following relation with respect to deleting or contracting an edge
\hat{e}
depending on whether
\hat{e}
is a general element, a loop or a coloop:

(a)

If
\hat{e}
is a general element, then

\widehat{T}_{M}=(1+st_{\hat{e}})\cdot\widehat{T}_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}+(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\cdot\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}{\hat{e}}}.

(b)

If
\hat{e}\in E
is a loop, then

\widehat{T}_{M}=\Big((y-1)(1+rt_{\hat{e}})+(1+st_{\hat{e}})\Big)\cdot\widehat{T}_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}.

(c)

If
{\hat{e}}\in E
is a coloop, then

\widehat{T}_{M}=\Big((x-1)(1+st_{\hat{e}})+(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\Big)\cdot\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}{\hat{e}}}.

Proof.

First we resolve the case when
{\hat{e}}
is a general element. We split the sum indexed by
S\subseteq E
in the definition of the equivariant Tutte polynomial according to whether
{\hat{e}}
belongs to
S
or not:

(13)

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq E\\
{\hat{e}}\notin S\end{subarray}}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e})+{}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq E\\
{\hat{e}}\in S\end{subarray}}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e}).

If
{\hat{e}}\notin S
, then we can identify
S
as a subset in the matroid
M\setminus{\hat{e}}
. Note that
\rank(M\setminus{\hat{e}})=\rank(M)
and
\rank_{M\setminus\hat{e}}
is merely a restriction of
\rank_{M}
, so if we compare the first sum in (
13
) to the definition of
\widehat{T}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}
, we see that exponents are the same, meaning the only difference is the additional factor
1+st_{e}
. Thus the first sum adds up to
(1+st_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}
.

Now we will similarly prove that the second sum in (
13
) is equal to
(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M/{\hat{e}}}
. To see this, note that the rank of
M
decreases by one after contraction of
{\hat{e}}
. Under the natural identification of subsets of
S
containing
{\hat{e}}
of
M
with subsets
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus\{\hat{e}\}
of
M/{\hat{e}}
, we further have
|S|-1=|S^{\prime}|
,
\rank_{M}(S)-1=\rank_{M/{\hat{e}}}(S^{\prime})
. So as above, in comparing the second sum in (
13
) to the definition of
\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}
, the expressions differ only by an additional factor of
1+rt_{e}
, meaning we obtain
(1+rt_{e})\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}
. Adding the two sums yields our claim (a).

Next, let us assume that
{\hat{e}}
is a loop. Then we have
\rank(M\setminus{\hat{e}})=\rank(M)
. Also, if
{\hat{e}}\notin S
, then
\rank_{M}(S)=\rank_{M\setminus{\hat{e}}}(S)
, so the first sum in (
13
) is equal to
(1+st_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M/{\hat{e}}}
as before. As to the second sum, we can naturally identify a subset
S
of
M
containing
{\hat{e}}
with the subset
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus\{\hat{e}\}
of
M\setminus{\hat{e}}
. We then have
\rank_{M}(S)=\rank_{M\setminus\hat{e}}(S^{\prime})
but
|S|-1=|S^{\prime}|
. Using this, we see that compared to the definition of
\widehat{T}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}
, the second sum in (
13
) differs in the exponent of
y-1
being greater by
1
as well as in an additional factor of
1+rt_{e}
, so the second sum adds up to
(y-1)(1+rt_{e})\widehat{T}_{M\setminus\hat{e}}
.
Adding the two results together, we obtain our claim (b).

The case of a coloop proceeds analogously, so we omit it.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that the assignment
M\mapsto\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][x,y,r,s]
is valuative, i.e. is compatible with subdivisions of the base polytope of
M
into base polytopes of smaller matroids. We quickly recall the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

(a)

Let
M
be a matroid of rank
r
on the groundset
E
, then the
base polytope of

M
is by definition

\displaystyle P(M):=\conv\left(\left\{\sum_{b\in B}e_{b}\mid B\text{ is a basis of }M\right\}\right)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{E}

where
e_{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
denotes the
b
-th standard basis vector. This polytope is contained in the affine subspace of vectors with coordinate sum
r
. We denote by
\mathbbm{1}_{P(M)}:{\mathbb{R}}^{E}\to\{0,1\}
its indicator function which takes value one exactly when the input belongs to
P(M)
.

(b)

Fix
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, a finite set
E
and an abelian group
G
. Denote by
\text{Mat}_{r}(E)
the free abelian group generated by rank
r
matroids on
E
. A group morphism
f:\text{Mat}_{r}(E)\to G
is said to be
valuative
if for every element
\sum_{M}z_{M}M\in\text{Mat}_{r}(M)
, where the sum ranges over all rank
r
matroids on
E
and
z_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are integers, we have the following implication:

\displaystyle\sum_{M}z_{M}\mathbbm{1}_{P(M)}\equiv 0\text{ on all of }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\implies f\left(\sum_{M}z_{M}M\right)=0\in G

For more details on valuativity we refer to
[
EHL
]
. Here we will only prove the following result which follows from a valuativity result shown in
[
BEST
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

The map
M\mapsto\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][x,y,r,s]
assigning to a matroid its equivariant Tutte polynomial extends to a valuative group morphism
\text{Mat}_{r}(E)\to{\mathbb{Z}}[E][x,y,r,s]
for every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and every finite set
E
. In particular the same is true for any evaluation of
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)
, and therefore for any of the entries of table
1
.

Proof.

Proposition 5.6 from
[
BEST
]
shows that the assignment
M\mapsto c([\mathcal{S}^{\vee}],a)c([\mathcal{Q}_{M}],b)
extends to a valuative morphism. Since the pushforward map is additive, we conclude that the same holds for the pushforward of this product to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, that is for the assignment
M\mapsto F_{M}(a,b,z,w)\in H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n})[a,b]
. Any sort of substitution preserves this property, but to obtain
\widehat{T}_{M}
we also need to multiply by some factor. For this to preserve valuativity it is important that this factor does not depend on the matroid
M
but only on its rank and on
E
, which are both fixed. The first step to get
\widehat{T}_{M}
from
F_{M}
was to revert the grading of the first two variables. To do so we substitute
a\to\frac{1}{r},b\to\frac{1}{s}
and then multiply by
(rs)^{|E|-1}
. Since this factor is independent of
M
we see that the assignment
M\mapsto(rs)^{|E|-1}F_{M}(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][r,s,z,w]
extends to a valuative group morphism. Next note that by
12

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}\left(\frac{r+s}{s+z},\frac{r+s}{r+w},r,s\right)

\displaystyle=(rs)^{|E|-1}\frac{r+s}{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}F_{M}\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{s},z,w\right).

Since the factor
\frac{r+s}{(s+z)^{\rank(M)}(r+w)^{\corank(M)}}
depends only on the rank of
M
and on
E
, this is still valuative. Lastly we perform the substitution
z\to\frac{r+s-sx}{x},w\to\frac{r+s-ry}{y}
to obtain
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)
. As substitutions preserve valuativity, we get the desired result.
∎

To close out this section, we examine some particular examples of the equivariant Tutte polynomial in families of matroids where we can express it in a simple form.

Example 4.6
.

Let us a consider a matroid
M
on ground set
E
that contains no general elements, i.e. is a collection of loops and coloops. Denoting its set of loops as
L\subseteq E
, an easy induction via Proposition
4.3
(b), (c) then yields

\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=\prod_{e\in L}\bigl(y+(yr-r+s)t_{e}\bigr)\prod_{e\notin L}\bigl(x+(xs-s+r)t_{e}\bigr).

Example 4.7
.

Next we express the equivariant Tutte polynomial of the corank
1
uniform matroid
M=U_{n,E}
on ground set
E
of size
n+1
, that is the graphic matroid coming from a cycle of length
n+1
. In Definition
4.1
, each term corresponding to
S\subseteq E
depends just on the set itself, its rank
\rank_{M}(S)
and the overall rank
\rank(M)
. The rank function of
U_{n,E}
agrees with that of
U_{n+1,E}
for all
S
except
S=E
, while the overall rank is smaller by
1
. Further, we know the equivariant Tutte polynomial of
U_{n+1,E}
from the previous example, since it is just a collection of coloops. So, accounting for these discrepancies, we get

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{T}_{U_{n+1,E}}-(x-1)^{0}(y-1)^{0}\prod_{e\in E}(1+rt_{e})}{x-1}+(x-1)^{0}(y-1)^{1}\prod_{e\in E}(1+rt_{e})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-1}\left(\prod_{e\in E}\bigl(x+(xs-s+r)t_{e}\bigr)-\prod_{e\in E}(1+rt_{e})\right)+(y-1)\prod_{e\in E}(1+rt_{e}).

Notice that uniform matroids are preserved under relabeling their groundset, hence this polynomial has to be symmetric in the
t_{e}
-variables. Indeed, if we factor out the product above to write it in terms of the monomial basis in the
t_{e}
-variables, we see that for each
A\subseteq E
the coefficient of the monomial
t_{A}:=\prod_{e\in A}t_{e}
only depends on
|A|
. This makes it easy to write the expression in terms of elementary symmetric polynomials.

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq E}\left(\frac{(xs-s+r)^{|A|}x^{|E\setminus A|}-r^{|A|}}{x-1}+(y-1)r^{|A|}\right)\prod_{e\in A}t_{e}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\left(\frac{(xs-s+r)^{k}x^{n+1-k}-r^{k}}{x-1}+(y-1)r^{k}\right)\elem_{k}(\{t_{e}\}_{e\in E}).

5.
Equivariant Tutte-Grothendieck

Our tool to understand the pushforward of
c([Q_{M}],w)c([S_{M}^{\vee}],z)
was by analyzing how these invariants change under deletion and contraction of an element in
M
. In general there is a way to express a matroid invariant that satisfies certain deletion-contraction-relations in a closed form. The classically known way to do this is via the following result from (
[
BO
]
, Section 6.2).

Proposition 5.1

(Generalized Tutte-Grothendieck invariants)
.

Associated to a matroid
M
with finite ground set
E
, there is a unique polynomial
G_{M}(u,v,a,b,\gamma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[u,v,a,b,\gamma]
that satisfies the following recursive relations:

Base case:
If
E=\emptyset
then

\displaystyle G_{M}(u,v,a,b,\gamma)=\gamma.

Recursion:
If
E\neq\emptyset
and
e\in E
then

\displaystyle G_{M}(u,v,a,b,\gamma)=\begin{cases}aG_{M\setminus e}(u,v,a,b,\gamma)+bG_{M\mathbin{/}e}(u,v,a,b,\gamma),&\text{if $e$ is a general element in $M$,}\\
vG_{M\setminus e}(u,v,a,b,\gamma),&\text{if $e$ is a loop in $M$,}\\
uG_{M\mathbin{/}e}(u,v,a,b,\gamma)&\text{if $e$ is a coloop in $M$.}\end{cases}

In closed form this polynomial is given as

\displaystyle G_{M}(u,v,a,b)=\gamma b^{\rank(M)}a^{\corank(M)}T_{M}\left(\frac{u}{b},\frac{v}{a}\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[u,v,a,b,\gamma],

where

\displaystyle T_{M}(s,t)=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{E}}(s-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(t-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}

is the usual Tutte polynomial.

We say that some matroid invariant
G_{M}
with values in some ring
R
is a
specialization of the Tutte polynomial
if we can find elements
x,y,z,w\in R
such that
G_{M}=x^{\rank(M)}y^{\corank(M)}T_{M}(z,w)
. The statement above then says that every matroid invariant that satisfies deletion-contraction relations with
a,b\neq 0
(in the notation of the Proposition) is a specialization of
T_{M}
. However, if for some matroid invariant we have
a=0
or
b=0
, then this invariant cannot be obtained by multiplying some evaluation of
T_{M}(s,t)
by a factor that only depends on the rank of the matroid. Such invariants can still be recovered from
T_{M}(s,t)
but the process requires to read of certain coefficients rather than just performing substitutions.

Notice that for the invariants that we considered so far this result cannot be applied since the deletion-contraction relations for us linearly depend on the variable
t_{e}
indexed by the element
e
that we choose to remove from the matroid. For this situation we obtain the following extension of the usual result.

Proposition 5.2

(Equivariant Tutte-Grothendieck)
.

(a)

Associated to a matroid
M
on the finite ground set
E
there exists a unique 7-variable polynomial
H_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},\alpha,\beta,\gamma]
that satisfies the following recursion.

Base case:
If
E=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle H_{M}=\gamma.

Recursion:
If
E\neq\emptyset
and
e\in E
then

\displaystyle H_{M}=\begin{cases}(a_{1}t_{e}+a_{2})H_{M\setminus e}+(b_{1}t_{e}+b_{2})H_{M\mathbin{/}e},&\text{if $e$ is a general element in $M$,}\\
((b_{1}\alpha+a_{1})t_{e}+(b_{2}\alpha+a_{2}))H_{M\setminus e},&\text{if $e$ is a loop in $M$,}\\
((a_{1}\beta+b_{1})t_{e}+(a_{2}\beta+b_{2}))H_{M\mathbin{/}e}&\text{if $e$ is a coloop in $M$.}\end{cases}

This polynomial is given in closed form as

\displaystyle H_{M}=\gamma a_{2}^{\corank(M)}b_{2}^{\rank(M)}\widehat{T}_{M}\left(\frac{\beta a_{2}}{b_{2}}+1,\frac{\alpha b_{2}}{a_{2}}+1,\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}},\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right).

(b)

The above result is optimal in the following sense: Let
R
be any integral ring and fix
\gamma,a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},c_{1},c_{2},d_{1},d_{2}\in R
. Assume there exists a well-defined matroid invariant
G_{M}\in R[t_{e}\mid e\in E]
such that the following recursion holds for all matroids
M
over the ground set
E
.

Base case:
If
E=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle G_{M}=\gamma.

Recursion:
If
E\neq\emptyset
and
e\in E
, then

\displaystyle G_{M}=\begin{cases}(a_{1}t_{e}+a_{2})G_{M\setminus e}+(b_{1}t_{e}+b_{2})G_{M\mathbin{/}e},&\text{if $e$ is a general element in $M$,}\\
(c_{1}t_{e}+c_{2})G_{M\setminus e},&\text{if $e$ is a loop in $M$,}\\
(d_{1}t_{e}+d_{2})G_{M\mathbin{/}e},&\text{if $e$ is a coloop in $M$.}\end{cases}

Assume further that
a_{2},b_{2}\neq 0
and at least one of the parameters
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
is not zero (otherwise

\displaystyle\left.\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)\right|_{t_{e}=-\frac{1}{r},e\in A}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{|A|}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank(E\setminus A)}\widehat{T}_{M\setminus A}(x,y,r,s).

Proof.

We proceed similar to the proof of
6.2
.

\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)|_{t_{e}=-\frac{1}{s},e\in A}=

=\sum_{A\subset S\subset E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in A}\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)\prod_{e\in S\setminus A}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e})+

+\sum_{A\not\subset S\subset E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\cdot 0=

=\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)^{|A|}\sum_{A\subset S\subset E}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(A)+\rank_{M}(A)-\rank_{M}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(S)-\nullity_{M}(A)+\nullity_{M}(A)}\cdot

\cdot\prod_{e\in S\setminus A}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e})=

=\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)^{|A|}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(A)}\sum_{S\subset E\setminus A}(x-1)^{\rank(M\mathbin{/}A)-\rank_{M\mathbin{/}A}(S)}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M\mathbin{/}A}(S)}\prod_{e\in S\setminus A}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+st_{e})=

=\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)^{|A|}(y-1)^{\nullity_{M}(A)}\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}A}(x,y,r,s).

For the second statement, we again pass to the dual matroid.

\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)|_{t_{e}=-\frac{1}{r},e\in A}=\widehat{T}_{M^{*}}(y,x,s,r)|_{t_{e}=-\frac{1}{r},e\in A}=

=\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{|A|}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank(E\setminus A)}\widehat{T}_{M^{*}\mathbin{/}A}(y,x,s,r)=

=\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{|A|}(x-1)^{\rank(M)-\rank(E\setminus A)}\widehat{T}_{M^{\setminus}A}(x,y,r,s).

∎

6.2.
Equivariant characteristic polynomial

Another polynomial associated to a matroid is its reduced characteristic polynomial

\displaystyle\chi_{M}(q):=(-1)^{\rank(M)}\frac{T_{M}(1-q,0)}{q-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[q].

In the case of a graphic matroid coming from some graph
G
, the above notion agrees with the reduced chromatic polynomial of the graph
G
up to a factor
q^{c(G)}
where
c(G)
denotes the number of connected components of
G
and therefore is not a matroid invariant. The latter polynomial, when multiplyied by
q-1
, evaluates for any natural number
q
to the number of valid vertex colorings of the graph
G
.

Just as we did to define the equivariant Tutte polynomial, we will recall a geometric interpretation of the reduced characteristic polynomial and lift it to an equivariant setting. The geometric interpretation was first observed by June Huh in
[
Hu
]
. However, it can also be seen from
11
. Indeed non-equivariantly we have

\displaystyle\int_{X_{E}}c([\mathcal{S}_{U_{n,E}}^{\vee}],x)c([\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1,E}}],y)c_{\corank(M)}([\mathcal{Q}_{M}])\left|{}_{y=-1}\right.=\chi_{M}(x)

Hence to define an equivariant analog of the reduced characteristic polynomial, we will pushforward the top Chern class of the
\mathcal{Q}_{M}
bundle. Since we already know how to pushforward the whole graded Chern class already, this is straightforward.

Proposition 6.7

(Pushforward of the top Chern class of the quotient bundle)
.

\pi^{n}_{*}\Big(c_{\corank(M)}([\mathcal{Q}_{M}])\Big)=P_{M}(\alpha,\beta)

holds in
H_{T}({\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
, where
P_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][\alpha,\beta]
is given by

P_{M}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\sum_{S\subset{E}}\alpha^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}(-\beta)^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\cdot\prod_{e\in S}(\alpha+t_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(\beta-t_{e}).

Proof.

This follows from
3.1
by looking at the coefficient of
z^{0}w^{\corank(M)}
.
∎

Again we want to reverse the grading in the variables
\alpha,\beta
for the reasons described in Section
4
. Then we do the same substitutions as in the non-equivariant case, i.e. we set
\beta=-1
and read
\alpha
as a formal variable. This results in the following definition.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
M
be a matroid on a groudset
E
. We define the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial
\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[E][q]
of
M
as follows:

\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)=\frac{(-1)^{\rank(M)}}{q-1}\cdot\widehat{T}_{M}(1-q,0,q,1)=\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{S\subset E}q^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}(-1)^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{e\notin S}(1+t_{e})\prod_{e\in S}(1+qt_{e}).

Indeed we have the following relation between the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial and the pushforward of the top Chern class of the quotient bundle of
M
:

Proposition 6.9

(Relating equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial and the pushforward)
.

Let
P_{M}
be as in the previous proposition. Then

P_{M}\left(\frac{1}{q},-1\right)=\frac{1}{(-q)^{|E|-1}}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)

Proof.

P_{M}\left(\frac{1}{q},-1\right)=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q}-1}\sum_{S\subset E}\frac{1}{q^{\corank(M)-\nullity_{M}(S)}}\cdot 1^{\nullity_{M}(S)}\cdot\prod_{e\in S}\left(\frac{1}{q}+t_{e}\right)\prod_{e\notin S}(-1-t_{e})=

=\frac{-q}{q-1}\sum_{S\subset E}q^{\rank(M)-\rank_{M}(S)}q^{-|E|}q^{|S|-|S|}\cdot(-1)^{|E\setminus S|}\cdot\prod_{e\in S}(1+qt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+t_{e})=

=\frac{1}{(-q)^{|E|-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{S\subset E}\cdot(-1)^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{e\notin S}(1+t_{e})\prod_{e\in S}(1+qt_{e})=\frac{1}{(-q)^{|E|-1}}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)

∎

Notice that setting
t_{e}=0
for all
e\in E
,
we recover the usual characteristic polynomial. More generally, we have the following result.

Proposition 6.10

(Combinatorial interpretation of the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial)
.

Let
A\subset E
. Then setting
t_{e}=-1
for
e\in A
and
t_{e}=0
for
e\notin A
in the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial
\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)
gives

(1-q)^{|A|}(-1)^{|A|}\chi_{M\mathbin{/}A}(q).

Proof.

We will use Proposition
6.6
to show that substituting
t_{e}=-1
for
e\in A
and
t_{e}=0
for
e\notin A
yields the desired result.

\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)|_{t_{e}=-1,e\in A}=\frac{(-1)^{\rank(M)}}{q-1}\cdot\widehat{T}_{M}(1-q,0,q,1)|_{t_{e}=-1,e\in A}=

=\frac{(-1)^{\rank(M)}}{q-1}\cdot\left(1-q\right)^{|A|}(0-1)^{\nullity_{M}(A)}\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}A}(1-q,0,q,1)=

=\frac{(-1)^{\rank(M\mathbin{/}A)}}{q-1}\cdot\left(q-1\right)^{|A|}\widehat{T}_{M\mathbin{/}A}(1-q,0,q,1)=(1-q)^{|A|}(-1)^{|A|}\widehat{\chi}_{M\mathbin{/}A}(q).

By setting the remaining
t_{e}
to zero we obtain the usual reduced characteristic polynomial.
∎

An easy corollary to the valuativity of
\widehat{T}_{M}
(Proposition
4.5
) is that the equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial is also valuative. Alternatively this also follows immediately from noting that
c_{\rank{M}}([{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}])
is valuative by
[
BEST
]
, Proposition 5.6 and the pushforward map is additive.

Corollary 6.11
.

The assignment
M\mapsto\widehat{\chi}_{M}
that assigns to a matroid
M
its equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial is valuative.

Since we already computed
\widehat{T}_{M}
for
M=U_{n,n+1}
in Examples
4.7
and
6.5
, we can now easily obtain its equivariant reduced characteristic polynomial.

Example 6.12
.

Let
M=U_{n,n+1}
, recall from
4.7
that we have

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\left(\frac{(xs-s+r)^{k}x^{n+1-k}-r^{k}}{x-1}+(y-1)r^{k}\right)\elem_{k}(\{t_{e}\}_{e\in E}).

Note that the substitution
(x,y,r,s)=(1-q,0,q,1)
makes the term
(xs-s+r)^{k}
vanish for
k>0
, so we split the sum to get

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{q-1}\left(\frac{(1-q)^{n+1}-1}{-q}-1+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(q^{k-1}-q^{k})\elem_{k}(\{t_{e}\}_{e\in E})\right)

In fact if we fix some
k
-element subset
A
and set
t_{e}=-1
for
e\in A
and
t_{e}=0
else, only the terms
t_{S}
for
S\subseteq A
are non-zero. We hence obtain contributions from the first
k
summands only. Hereby the summand corresponding to a cardinality
l\leq k
subset of
A
contributes once for each
l
-element subset of
A
, i.e. with factor
(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{l}
. We hence get

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)|_{t_{e}=-1,e\in A,t_{e}=0,e\notin A}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{q-1}\left(\frac{(1-q)^{n+1}-1}{-q}-1+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(q^{l-1}-q^{l})(-1)^{l}\right).

Careful manipulations of this expression indeed yield

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{M}(q)|_{t_{e}=-1,e\in A,t_{e}=0,e\notin A}=(1-q)^{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}(1-q)^{i}=(1-q)^{k}(-1)^{k}\chi_{U_{n-k,n+1-k}}(q)

and hence confirms the connection to the usual reduced characteristic polynomial of an
n+1-k
-cycle, which was predicted by
6.10
.

6.3.
Uniqueness of evaluation for matroids

We have seen that after evaluating at
(1,1,1,0)
in
\widehat{T}_{M}
,
t_{A}
has non-zero coefficient if and only if
A
is independent. In particular, we can recover
M
from
\widehat{T}_{M}
. It turns out there are not many evaluations destroying this uniqueness as long as we keep the
t_{e}
-variables untouched.

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
R
be an integral domain and pick
x,y,r,s\in R
. Assume there exist two different matroids
M_{1},M_{2}
on the same groundset
E
such that
\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}(x,y,r,s)=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}(x,y,r,s)
holds in
R[E]
, then either
r=s
or
(x-1)(y-1)=1
.

Proof.

For brevity we will write
\widehat{T}_{M}
instead of
\widehat{T}_{M}(x,y,r,s)
for any matroid
M
. Also note that since we can pass to the fraction field of
R
, we may assume that
R
is a field.

We will proceed by a contradiction. Suppose that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are different matroids,
r\neq s
,
(x-1)(y-1)\neq 1
but
\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}
. Furthermore, suppose that the cardinality of
E
is the smallest possible among such examples of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
.
We consider three cases:

(a)

There exists
\hat{e}\in E
such that it is general in both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. Then

(1+st_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}+(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}=(1+st_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}+(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}.

These polynomials can be regarded as one variable polynomials in
t_{\hat{e}}
with coefficients in
R[t_{e}\mid e\in E\setminus\{\hat{e}\}]
. Their equality yields the equality of their corresponding coefficients. Comparing the constant term we get

(15)

\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}-\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}-\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}

and similarly for the linear terms we get

(16)

s\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}-s\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}=r\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}-r\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}.

Denoting
c
the constant term, that is equal to both sides of (
15
), we can rewrite (
16
) as
(s-r)c=0
. Since
r\neq s
, we obtain
c=0
. By minimality of
|E|
this can be true only if
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}=M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}
and
M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}=M_{2}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}
. But this implies that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are the same matroid, a contradiction
2
2

2

Notice that we really use the fact that
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}=M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}
and
M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}=M_{2}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}
are equalities of labeled matroids. In general for a matroid
M
and an element
e
from its groundset, it is not true that knowing
M\mathbin{/}e
and
M\setminus e
up to isomorphism uniquely determines
M
up to isomorphism.
.

(b)

There exists
\hat{e}\in E
such that it is general in one of
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
but it is loop or coloop in the other one. Since the situation is symmetric with respect to
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, let us presume
\hat{e}
is general in
M_{1}
. Similarly, passing to dual matroids changes
\hat{e}
being a coloop in
M_{2}
to it being a loop, so let us presume that
\hat{e}
is a loop in
M_{2}
.

Then we have

(1+st_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}+(1+rt_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}=(y+(yr-r+s)t_{\hat{e}})\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}.

Comparing the coefficients with respect to the variable
t_{\hat{e}}
, we get

(17)

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}+\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}

\displaystyle=y\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}},

(18)

\displaystyle s\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}+r\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}\hat{e}}

\displaystyle=(yr-r+s)\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}.

We can substitute
y\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}
from the first expression into the second, obtaining

(s-r)\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}=(s-r)\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}.

Dividing by
s-r
we obtain
\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}}
. Thanks to the minimal choice of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, this yields
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}=M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}
.

Now, if there were a general element in the matroid
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}=M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}
, it would remain general in both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. This would allow us to reduce to the case (a). Consequently, if suffices for us to treat just the case where no element of
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}=M_{2}\setminus\hat{e}
is general.

First let us assume
M_{1}
has no loops nor coloops. As deletion does not create any new loops,
M_{1}\setminus\hat{e}
is a collection of coloops. So
M_{1}=U_{n,n+1}
. Further, we deduce that
M_{2}
consists of
n
coloops and one loop.
If
s\neq 0
, we can use Proposition
6.6
. We choose some

and set

to
-\frac{1}{s}
.
The element
e^{\prime}
is a coloop in
M_{2}
and a general element in
M_{1}
.

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}e^{\prime}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}|_{t_{e^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}|_{t_{e^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{s}}=\left(1-\frac{r}{s}\right)\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\mathbin{/}e^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\mathbin{/}e^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\mathbin{/}e^{\prime}}

Since
M_{1}\mathbin{/}e^{\prime}=U_{n-1,n}
and

is a collection of
n-1

. So

.

From
(

we get

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{U_{n-1,n}}

\displaystyle=(y-1)\widehat{T}_{U_{n,n}}

We can expand those expressions by the definition of the equivariant Tutte polynomial. Since

and

\displaystyle(x-1)^{0}(y-1)^{1}\prod_{e\in E}(1+rt_{e})+\sum_{S\subsetneq E}

\displaystyle(x-1)^{n-1-|S|}(y-1)^{0}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+0\cdot t_{e})=

\displaystyle=(y-1)\sum_{S\subset E}

\displaystyle(x-1)^{n-|S|}(y-1)^{0}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})\prod_{e\notin S}(1+0\cdot t_{e}),

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subsetneq E}(x-1)^{n-1-|S|}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})

\displaystyle=(y-1)(x-1)\sum_{S\subsetneq E}(x-1)^{n-1-|S|}\prod_{e\in S}(1+rt_{e})

We get a contradiction with

Then the coefficients of

Now we assume there exists
e^{\prime}\in E
such that it is a loop in
M_{1}
. It has to be a loop in

(y+(yr+s-r)t_{e})\widehat{T}_{M_{1}\setminus e^{\prime}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{1}}=\widehat{T}_{M_{2}}=(y+(yr+s-r)t_{e})\widehat{T}_{M_{2}\setminus e^{\prime}}.

(c)

Both

\displaystyle(x-1)(y-1)

Hyers-Ulam stability for finite-dimensional nonautonomous dynamics
Davor Dragičević

Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to obtain necessary and sufficient conditions under which a nonautonomous, finite-dimensional and two-sided dynamics generated by a sequence of matrices or a linear ODE exhibits Hyers-Ulam stability. Specifically, in the case of discrete time we consider a nonautonomous difference equation with possibly noninvertible coefficients, while in the case of continuous time we deal with a nonautonomous ordinary differential equation without any bounded growth assumptions. Keywords : exponential trichotomy; summable trichotomy; Hyers-Ulam stability. 2020 MSC : 34D09, 34D10



1.
Introduction

In the recent years many works have been devoted to the investigation of Hyers-Ulam stability for various classes of differential and difference equations. Roughly speaking, we say that a given differential or difference equation exhibits Hyers-Ulam stability if in a vicinity of its approximate solutions, we can construct an exact solution. The importance of this notion stems from the fact we are usually unable to explicitly solve a given difference or differential equation, and that any numerical scheme will only result in an approximate solution of the equation. Thus, it is important to know that close to an approximate solution, there exists a true solution of our equation.

In the present paper, we consider two classes of nonautonomous dynamics. More precisely, we deal with a nonautonomous difference equation of the form

(1)

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as well as ordinary differential equations

(2)

x^{\prime}=A(t)x\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
A(t)
is a linear operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and the map
t\mapsto A(t)
is continuous.
In
[
3
]
, Backes and Dragičević proved that Eq (
1
) and Eq. (
2
) are Hyers-Ulam stable provided that these equations admit an exponential trichotomy (in the sense of Elaydi and Hajek
[
16
]
). Moreover, they established converse result implying that Hyers-Ulam stability of Eq. (
1
) and Eq (
2
) yields the existence of exponential trichotomy for these equations provided that operators
A_{n}
are invertible and that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|A(t)\|<+\infty
(actually for the approach in
[
3
]
to work, it is sufficient that Eq. (
2
) admits bounded growth). For related results that deal with the case when maps
n\mapsto A_{n}
and
t\mapsto A(t)
are periodic, we refer to
[
1
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
22
]
and references therein. For other relevant contributions to the Hyers-Ulam stability and shadowing of nonautonomous dynamics, see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
,
18
]
. In particular, in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
18
]
sufficient conditions under which nonlinear difference (or differential) equations obtained by perturbing Eq. (
1
) (or Eq. (
2
)) exhibit (conditional) Hyers-Ulam, Hyers-Ulam-Rassias, or general (parametrized) shadowing properties were discussed. These works contain no converse results guaranteeing appropriate hyperbolicity of Eq. (
1
) (or Eq. (
2
)) under Hyers-Ulam stability.

The main objective of the present paper is to revisit the above mentioned converse results from
[
3
]
, and to remove the assumption on invertibility of coefficients
A_{n}
in Eq. (
1
) and of bounded growth related to Eq. (
2
). More precisely, we prove that the Hyers-Ulam stability of Eq. (
1
) is equivalent to exponential trichotomy under a relatively mild condition regarding the uniqueness of backward bounded solutions. In Example
1
we show that this condition cannot be omitted.

On the other hand, in the case of continuous time we show that the Hyers-Ulam stability of Eq. (
2
) is equivalent to the concept of
summable trichotomy
. This notion is inspired by the previously mentioned concept of exponential trichotomy, as well as the notion of summable dichotomy which goes back to the work of Coppel
[
11
]
. For different concepts of trichotomy and their role in the study of asymptotic behavior of nonautonomous dynamical systems we refer to
[
19
,
20
]
and references therein.

2.
Discrete time case

2.1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
will denote the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with some norm
|\cdot|
. By
\|\cdot\|
we will denote the associated matrix norm on the space of all linear operators acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of (not necessarily invertible) linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We consider the associated linear difference equation given by

(3)

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By
\mathcal{A}(m,n)
we will denote the
linear cocycle
associated with (
3
) which is given by

\mathcal{A}(m,n)=\begin{cases}A_{m-1}\cdots A_{n}&m>n;\\
\Id&m=n,\end{cases}

where
\Id
denotes the identity operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We recall the notion of Hyers-Ulam stability for (
3
).

Definition 1
.

We say that Eq. (
3
) is
Hyers-Ulam stable
if there exists
L>0
with the property that for each
\delta>0
and a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(4)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|y_{n+1}-A_{n}y_{n}|\leq\delta,

there exists a solution
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of Eq. (
3
) such that

(5)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|\leq L\delta.

We will also consider the stronger concept of Hyers-Ulam stability.

Definition 2
.

We say that Eq. (
3
) is
Hyers-Ulam stable with uniqueness
if there exists
L>0
with the property that for each
\delta>0
and a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that (
4
) holds, there exists a unique solution
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of Eq. (
3
) satisfying (
5
).

Remark 1
.

Obviously, if (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable with uniqueness then it is Hyers-Ulam stable.

We also recall the notion of exponential dichotomy.

Definition 3
.

Let
J\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{+},\mathbb{Z}^{-}\}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\ n\geq 0\}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{-}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\ n\leq 0\}
. We say that Eq. (
3
) admits an
exponential dichotomy
on
J
if there exist a family of projections
P_{n}
,
n\in J
and constants
D,\lambda>0
such that the following conditions hold:

•

for
n,n+1\in J
,

P_{n+1}A_{n}=A_{n}P_{n},

and
A_{n}\rvert_{\Ker P_{n}}\colon\Ker P_{n}\to\Ker P_{n+1}
is invertible;

•

for
m,n\in J
such that
m\geq n
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}\|\leq De^{-\lambda(m-n)};

•

for
m,n\in J
such that
m\leq n
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)(\Id-P_{n})\|\leq De^{-\lambda(n-m)},

where

\mathcal{A}(m,n):=\left(\mathcal{A}(n,m)\rvert_{\Ker P_{m}}\right)^{-1}\colon\Ker P_{n}\to\Ker P_{m},\quad m<n.

Remark 2
.

The following facts are well-known (see for example
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
):

(1)

if Eq. (

) admits an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with respect to projections

,

, then

\Ima P_{n}^{+}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \sup_{m\geq n}|\mathcal{A}(m,n)v|<+\infty\right\};

(2)

if Eq. (

) admits an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
with respect to projections
P_{n}^{-}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then
\Ker P_{n}^{-}
consists of

with the property that there exists a sequence
(x_{m})_{m\leq n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
x_{n}=v
,
x_{m}=A_{m-1}x_{m-1}
for
m\leq n
and
\sup_{m\leq n}|x_{m}|<+\infty
.

Finally, we recall the notion of exponential trichotomy originally introduced by Elaydi and Hajek

]

Definition 4
.

We say that (

) admits an
exponential trichotomy
if the following conditions hold:

•

Eq. (
3
) admits an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with respect to projections
P_{n}^{+}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

•

Eq. (

,

we have that

(6)

P_{0}^{-}=P_{0}^{-}P_{0}^{+}=P_{0}^{+}P_{0}^{-}.

Our first result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the only bounded sequence
(x_{n})_{n\leq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
x_{0}=0
and
x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}
,
n\leq-1
is the zero-sequence, i.e.
x_{n}=0
for
n\leq 0

the following properties are equivalent:

(a)

Eq. (
3

(b)

Eq. (
3

Proof.

(a)\implies(b)

Suppose that Eq. (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable and let
L>0
be as in the Definition
1
.

Lemma 1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(w_{n})_{n\geq-m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\sup_{n\geq-m}|w_{n}|<+\infty
, there exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(7)

z_{n+1}=A_{n}z_{n}+w_{n}\quad\text{for $n\geq-m$,}

and

(8)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|z_{n}|\leq L\sup_{n\geq-m}|w_{n}|.

Proof of the lemma.

We define a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

y_{n}=\begin{cases}0&n\leq-m;\\
A_{n-1}y_{n-1}+w_{n-1}&n>-m.\end{cases}

Observe that

y_{n}-A_{n-1}y_{n-1}=\begin{cases}0&n\leq-m;\\
w_{n-1}&n>-m.\end{cases}

In particular, we have that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|y_{n}-A_{n-1}y_{n-1}|=\sup_{n\geq-m}|w_{n}|.

Since Eq. (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable, there exists a solution
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of Eq. (
3
) such that

(9)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|\leq L\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|y_{n+1}-A_{n}y_{n}|=L\sup_{n\geq-m}|w_{n}|.

Set

z_{n}:=y_{n}-x_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By (
9
), we have that (
8
) holds. Moreover, since
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution of Eq. (
3
), we have that

z_{n+1}=y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}=A_{n}y_{n}+w_{n}-A_{n}x_{n}=A_{n}z_{n}+w_{n}\quad n\geq-m,

which yields (
7
).
The proof of the lemma is thus completed.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For each sequence
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{n}|<+\infty
, there exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(10)

z_{n+1}=A_{n}z_{n}+w_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

and

(11)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|z_{n}|\leq L\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{n}|.

Proof of the lemma.

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a sequence
(w_{n}^{m})_{n\geq-m}
by
w_{n}^{m}=w_{n}
,
n\geq-m
. By Lemma
1
, we conclude that there exists a sequence

(z_{n}^{m})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(12)

z_{n+1}^{m}=A_{n}z_{n}^{m}+w_{n}^{m}=A_{n}z_{n}^{m}+w_{n}\quad\text{for $n\geq-m$,}

and

(13)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|z_{n}^{m}|\leq L\sup_{n\geq-m}|w_{n}^{m}|\leq L\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{n}|.

It follows from (

, the sequence
(z_{n}^{m})_{m}
is a bounded sequence in

Take now an arbitrary

for

By applying (

Example 1
.

Let us now give an explicit example (inspired by
[

) which illustrates that the assumption we made in the statement of Theorem

cannot be eliminated. For this purpose,
take

and consider a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
by

A_{n}=\begin{cases}0&n\geq-1\\
2&n<-1.\end{cases}

We claim that the associated equation Eq. (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable. To this end, let

\ell^{\infty}:=\left\{w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}:\ \|w\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{n}|<+\infty\right\}.

Then,
(\ell^{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a Banach space. We define
\Gamma\colon\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{\infty}
by

(\Gamma w)_{n}:=\begin{cases}w_{n-1}&n\geq 0;\\
0&n=-1;\\
-\sum_{i=1}^{-(n+1)}\frac{1}{2^{i}}w_{n+i-1}&n\leq-2,\end{cases}

for
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
. Clearly,
\|\Gamma w\|_{\infty}\leq\|w\|_{\infty}
. Moreover, it is easy to verify that

(16)

(\Gamma w)_{n+1}=A_{n}(\Gamma w)_{n}+w_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Take now a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|y_{n+1}-A_{n}y_{n}|\leq\delta
for some
\delta>0
. Set

(17)

w_{n}:=y_{n+1}-A_{n}y_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Furthermore, let
w:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
and

x_{n}:=y_{n}-(\Gamma w)_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By (
16
) and (
17
), we have that the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution of Eq. (
3
). Moreover,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|=\|\Gamma w\|_{\infty}\leq\|w\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.

Thus, Eq. (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable. On the other hand, Eq. (
3
) does not admit exponential trichotomy as it does not admit exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
.

We can also give a complete characterization of Hyers-Ulam stability with uniqueness.

\Ima P_{0}^{-}\subset\Ima P_{0}^{+}

, set

\tilde{x}_{n}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}(n,0)v_{2}&n\geq 0;\\
x_{n}&n<0.\end{cases}

Clearly,
(\tilde{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution of Eq. (
3
) and
M:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\tilde{x}_{n}|<+\infty
. Finally, we introduce a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
y_{n}=\frac{L}{M}\tilde{x}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that the sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution of Eq. (
3
). In particular, (
4
) holds with
\delta=1
. Hence,
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
L
-shadowed by itself and the constant solution
(0)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of Eq. (
3
). By the uniqueness in Definition
2
, we conclude that
y_{n}=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
\tilde{x}_{0}=v_{2}=0
. Consequently,
v=v_{1}\in\Ima P_{0}^{-}
and we conclude that
\Ima P_{0}^{-}=\Ima P_{0}^{+}
. Since
\Ker P_{0}^{+}\subset\Ker P_{0}^{-}
, we have that
\Ker P_{0}^{+}=\Ker P_{0}^{-}
. This yields that
P_{0}^{-}=P_{0}^{+}
and therefore Eq. (
3
) admits an exponential dichotomy.

Suppose that Eq. (
3
) admits an exponential dichotomy. By Theorem
1
, we have that Eq. (
3
) is Hyers-Ulam stable. It remains to establish the uniqueness. Suppose that for a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
4
) with some
\delta>0
, there are two solutions
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(\tilde{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of Eq. (
3
) such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|\leq L\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\tilde{x}_{n}-y_{n}|\leq L\delta.

Set
w_{n}:=x_{n}-\tilde{x}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a solution of Eq. (
3
) such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{n}|<+\infty
. Since Eq. (
3
) admits an exponential dichotomy, we have that
w_{n}=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We conclude that
x_{n}=\tilde{x}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

3.
Continuous time case

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a continuous map. We consider the associated linear differential equation given by

(18)

x^{\prime}=A(t)x,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

By
T(t,s)
we denote the evolution family associated to (
18
).

Definition 5
.

We say that Eq. (
18
) is
Hyers-Ulam stable
if there exists
L>0
with the property that for each
\delta>0
and a continuously differentiable function
y\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(19)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|y^{\prime}(t)-A(t)y(t)|\leq\delta,

there exists a solution
x\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of Eq. (
18
) such that

(20)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x(t)-y(t)|\leq L\delta.

Similarly, one can introduce the notion of
Hyers-Ulam stability with uniqueness
for Eq. (
18
) by requiring that
x
in Definition
5
is unique.

Definition 6
.

Let
J\in\{\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}\}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}^{-}=(-\infty,0]
. We say that Eq. (
18
) admits a
summable dichotomy
on
J
if there exist a family of projections
P(t)
,
t\in J
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a constant
K>0
such that the following holds:

•

for
t,s\in J
,
T(t,s)P(s)=P(t)T(t,s)
;

•

for
t\in J
,

(21)

\int_{\inf J}^{t}\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\,ds+\int_{t}^{\sup J}\|T(t,s)Q(s)\|\,ds\leq K,

where
Q(s)=\Id-P(s)
.

Remark 4
.

We recall that (
18
) admits an exponential dichotomy on
J
if there exist constants
D,\lambda>0
such that

(22)

\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\leq De^{-\lambda(t-s)}\quad\text{for $t,s\in J$ with $t\geq s$,}

and

(23)

\|T(t,s)Q(s)\|\leq De^{-\lambda(s-t)}\quad\text{for $t,s\in J$ with $t\leq s$.}

Clearly, exponential dichotomy is a particular case of a summable dichotomy (as (
22
) and (
23
) imply (
21
)).

Take now a continuously differentiable function
\phi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
0<\phi(t)\leq 1
for
t\geq 0
,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\phi(t)}-1\right)\,dt=1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi(n)}{\phi(n-2^{-n})}=\infty
. We consider the equation

(24)

x^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi(t)}-1\right)x.

It is proved in
[
12
, p.27]
that (
24
) admits a summable dichotomy (with
P(t)=\Id
) but does not admit an exponential dichotomy.

Remark 5
.

It is observed in
[
11
, p.134]
the following holds true:

•

if Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with respect to projections
P(t)
,
t\geq 0
, then

\Ima P(0)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \sup_{t\geq 0}|T(t,0)v|<+\infty\right\}=:\mathcal{S};

Furthermore,
\Ker P(0)
can be an arbitrary subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
complemented to
\mathcal{S}
;

•

if Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
with respect to projections
P(t)
,
t\leq 0
, then

\Ker P(0)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \sup_{t\leq 0}|T(t,0)v|<+\infty\right\}=:\mathcal{U};

Furthermore,
\Ima P(0)
can be an arbitrary subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
complemented to
\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 7
.

We say that Eq. (
18
) admits a
summable trichotomy
if the following conditions hold:

•

Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with respect to projections
P^{+}(t)
,
t\geq 0
;

•

Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
with respect to projections
P^{-}(t)
,
t\leq 0
;

•

we have that

(25)

P^{-}(0)=P^{-}(0)P^{+}(0)=P^{+}(0)P^{-}(0).

Remark 6
.

We recall (see
[
16
]
) that Eq. (
18
) admits an
exponential trichotomy
if:

•

Eq. (
18
) admits an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with respect to projections
P^{+}(t)
,
t\geq 0
;

•

Eq. (
18
) admits an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
with respect to projections
P^{-}(t)
,
t\leq 0
;

•

(
25
) holds.

It follows from the discussion in Remark
4
that the notion of exponential trichotomy is a particular case of the notion of a summable trichotomy.

Theorem 3
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

Eq. (
18
) is Hyers-Ulam stable;

(b)

Eq. (
18
) admits a summable trichotomy.

Proof.

(a)\implies(b)

Suppose that Eq. (
18
) is Hyers-Ulam stable.

Lemma 6
.

For each continuous function
z\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|z(t)|<+\infty
, there exists a continuously differentiable
x\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(26)

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)+z(t)\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

and

(27)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x(t)|\leq L\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|z(t)|.

Proof of the lemma.

Set
\|z\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|z(t)|<+\infty
, and let
y\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary solution of the equation

y^{\prime}(t)=A(t)y(t)+z(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then,
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|y^{\prime}(t)-A(t)y(t)|=\|z\|_{\infty}
. Hence, there exists a solution
\tilde{x}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of Eq. (
18
) such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|y(t)-\tilde{x}(t)|\leq L\|z\|_{\infty}
. Set
x(t):=y(t)-\tilde{x}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
x
has the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 7
.

We have that

(28)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{U}.

Proof of the lemma.

Take
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Choose a nonnegative continuous function
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
whose support is contained in
[0,1]
and such that
\int_{0}^{1}\phi(s)\,ds=1
. Let

z(t)=\phi(t)T(t,0)v,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
z
is continuous and
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|z(t)|<+\infty
. By Lemma
6
, there exists a continuously differentiable function
x\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
26
) and such that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x(t)|<+\infty
. Observe that
x(t)=T(t,0)x(0)
for
t\leq 0
, and thus
x(0)\in\mathcal{U}
. Moreover, for
t\geq 1
we have that

\begin{split}x(t)&=T(t,0)x(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)T(t,s)T(s,0)v\,ds\\
&=T(t,0)x(0)+\left(\int_{0}^{1}\phi(s)\,ds\right)T(t,0)v\\
&=T(t,0)(x(0)+v).\end{split}

Hence,
x(0)+v\in\mathcal{S}
. We conclude that

v=x(0)+v-x(0)\in\mathcal{S}+\mathcal{U}.

∎

The following lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma
6
.

Lemma 8
.

The following holds:

•

for each continuous function
z\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{t\geq 0}|z(t)|<+\infty
, there exists a continuously differentiable
x\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(29)

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)+z(t)\quad t\geq 0,

and
\sup_{t\geq 0}|x(t)|\leq L\sup_{t\geq 0}|z(t)|
;

•

for each continuous function
z\colon(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{t\leq 0}|z(t)|<+\infty
, there exists a continuously differentiable
x\colon(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(30)

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)+z(t)\quad t\leq 0,

and
\sup_{t\leq 0}|x(t)|\leq L\sup_{t\leq 0}|z(t)|
.

By (
28
), there exist subspaces
Z\subset\mathcal{U}
and
Z^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{S}
such that

(31)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathcal{S}\oplus Z\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}=Z^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{U}.

It follows from Lemma
8
, Remark
5
and
[
11
, Theorem 1, p.131]
that Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with respect to projections
P^{+}(t)
,
t\geq 0
and a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
with respect to projections
P^{-}(t)
,
t\leq 0
. Moreover,
\Ker P^{+}(0)=Z
and
\Ima P^{-}(0)=Z^{\prime}
. This easily implies that (
25
) holds. Therefore, Eq. (
18
) admits a summable trichotomy.

(b)\implies(a)
Suppose that Eq. (
18
) admits a summable trichotomy. By
[
11
, Lemma 1, p.68]
, there exists
N\geq 1
such that
\|T(t,0)P^{+}(0)\|\leq N
for
t\geq 0
and
\|T(t,0)P^{-}(0)\|\leq N
for
t\leq 0
.
We need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 9
.

There exists
L>0
with the property that for each continuous
z\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|z(t)|<+\infty
, there exists a continuously differentiable
x\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
sastifying (
26
) and (
27
).

Proof of the lemma.

For
t\geq 0
, let

x_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T(t,s)P^{+}(s)z(s)\,ds-\int_{t}^{\infty}T(t,s)Q^{+}(s)z(s)\,ds,

where
Q^{+}(s)=\Id-P^{+}(s)
. Then, (
21
) implies that
\sup_{t\geq 0}|x_{1}(t)|\leq K\sup_{t\geq 0}|z(t)|
. Observe that

(32)

x_{1}^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x_{1}(t)+z(t)\ \text{for $t>0$}\ \text{and}\ x_{1}^{\prime}(0+)=A(0)x_{1}(0)+z(0),

where
x_{1}^{\prime}(0+)
denotes the right-derivative of
x_{1}
at
0
. On the other hand, observe that
x_{1}(0)\in\Ker P^{+}(0)\subset\Ker P^{-}(0)
. Hence, by setting
x_{1}(t):=T(t,0)x_{1}(0)
for
t<0
we can extend
x_{1}
to a bounded function on
\mathbb{R}
. In fact,
|x_{1}(t)|\leq N|x_{1}(0)|
for
t\leq 0
and thus (assuming without any loss of generality that
K\geq 1
)

(33)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x_{1}(t)|\leq NK\sup_{t\geq 0}|z(t)|.

In addition,

(34)

x_{1}^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x_{1}(t)\ \text{for $t<0$}\ \text{and}\ x_{1}^{\prime}(0-)=A(0)x_{1}(0),

where
x_{1}^{\prime}(0-)
denotes the left-derivative of
x_{1}
at
0
.

For
t\leq 0
, let

x_{2}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P^{-}(s)z(s)\,ds-\int_{t}^{0}T(t,s)Q^{-}(s)z(s)\,ds,

where
Q^{-}(s)=\Id-P^{-}(s)
. Then, (
21
) implies that
\sup_{t\leq 0}|x_{2}(t)|\leq K\sup_{t<0}|z(t)|
. Observe that

(35)

x_{2}^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x_{2}(t)+z(t)\ \text{for $t<0$}\ \text{and}\ x_{2}^{\prime}(0-)=A(0)x_{2}(0)+z(0).

Moreover,
x_{2}(0)\in\Ima P^{-}(0)\subset\Ima P^{+}(0)
. Hence, by setting
x_{2}(t):=T(t,0)x_{2}(0)
for
t>0
we can extend
x_{2}
to a bounded function on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, similarly to (
33
), we have that

(36)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x_{2}(t)|\leq NK\sup_{t<0}|z(t)|.

In addition,

(37)

x_{2}^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x_{2}(t)\ \text{for $t>0$}\ \text{and}\ x_{2}^{\prime}(0+)=A(0)x_{2}(0).

Set
x:=x_{1}+x_{2}
. Then, (
32
), (
34
), (
35
) and (
37
) imply that (
26
) holds. Finally, (
33
) and (
36
) yield that (
27
) holds with
L:=2NK
.
The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Take
\delta>0
and let
y\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuously differentiable function such that (
19
) holds. It follows from the previous lemma that the equation

\tilde{x}^{\prime}(t)=A(t)\tilde{x}(t)+A(t)y(t)-y^{\prime}(t)\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

has a solution with the property that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{x}(t)|\leq L\delta
. Then,
x:=\tilde{x}+y
is a solution of Eq.(
18
) such that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|x(t)-y(t)|\leq L\delta
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Remark 7
.

In
[
3
]
, the version of Theorem
3
was established in the particular case when
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|A(t)\|<+\infty
. It turns out that in that case, Hyers-Ulam stability of Eq. (
18
) is equivalent to exponential trichotomy (see Remark
6
).

We now have the following version of Theorem
2
for continuous time.

Theorem 4
.

The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

Eq. (
18
) is Hyers-Ulam stable with uniqueness;

(2)

Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Suppose that Eq. (
18
) is Hyers-Ulam stable with uniqueness. Then, one can easily show that
x
in the statement of Lemma
6
is unique. By the result stated in
[
11
, p.136]
, we have that Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy. The converse can be easily established by using that if Eq. (
18
) admits a summable dichotomy, then the only bounded solution of Eq. (
18
) is the trivial one (see again
[
11
, p.136]
).
∎

Remark 8
.

We would like to briefly compare the results in this section with those obtained in
[
13
]
. In
[
13
]
the author has studied the problem of characterizing dichotomies with very general growth rates for Eq. (
18
) posed on the half-interval
\mathbb{R}^{+}
in terms of certain modified versions of Hyers-Ulam stability. These results in particular yield (see
[
13
, Corollary 2]
) characterization of
exponential
dichotomy of Eq. (
18
) (on
\mathbb{R}^{+})
in terms of two types of Hyers-Ulam stability without imposing any bounded growth conditions.

Our Theorems
3
and
4
yield characterizations of
summable
dichotomies and
trichotomies
in terms of a single and classical concept of Hyers-Ulam stability for Eq. (
18
) on
\mathbb{R}
. We stress that the concepts of trichotomy considered in this paper can only be related to Eq. (
18
) posed on
\mathbb{R}
(see Definition
7
and Remark
6
).

Thus, none of the results in
[
13
]
imply the results in this paper and vice-versa.

Isolated singularities for elliptic equations with Hardy


Abstract
In this paper, we concern the isolated singular solutions for semi-linear elliptic equations involving Hardy-Leray potential − Δ ​ u + μ | x | 2 ​ u = u p in Ω ∖ { 0 } , u = 0 on ∂ Ω . -\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u=u^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\qquad u=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega. (0.1) We classify the isolated singularities and obtain the existence and stability of positive solutions of ( 0.1 ). Our results are based on the study of nonhomogeneous Hardy problem in a new distributional sense.

Introduction

It is well known that the fundamental solutions of Laplacian operator play an essential role in the study of isolated singularities
of semilinear elliptic equations. Brezis and Lions in
[
4
]
made use of the Schwartz theorem to show that any nonnegative solution of

-\Delta u=f
in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
must be a distributional solution of
-\Delta u=f+k\delta_{0}
with
k\geq 0
and
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac mass concentrated at the origin.
As a consequence, the solution would behavior as multiple of fundamental solutions. Later on, Lions in
[
22
]
made use of this observation to
classify the isolated singularities of semilinear elliptic equation

-\Delta u=u^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\qquad u=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(1.1)

by building the connection with distributional solution of

-\Delta u=u^{p}+k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega.

(1.2)

This work shows that when
p\geq\frac{N}{N-2}
, then
k=0
; when

1<p<\frac{N}{N-2}
, there exists
k^{*}>0
such that (
1.2
) has two solutions for
k\in(0,k^{*})
, which are classical solutions of (
1.1
), does one for
k=k^{*}
and
no solution for
k>k^{*}
. Here
\frac{N}{N-2}
is called as
Serrin’s exponent. Motivated by the study of Lions’ work, Naito-Sato in
[
24
]
obtained two solutions for the model

-\Delta u+u=u^{p}+k_{i}\sum_{x_{i}}\delta_{x_{i}}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

by variational methods.

Note that when
p\geq\frac{N}{N-2}
, the isolated singularity of (
1.1
) is invisible in the distributional sense by Dirac mass as in (
1.2
).
Using dynamic analysis, the classification of positive singularities of (
1.1
) has been done by Aviles in
[
2
]
for
p=\frac{N}{N-2}
and
N\geq 3
, by Gidas and Spruck in
[
19
]

for
\frac{N}{N-2}<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, by Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck in
[
7
]
for

p=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
. The books
[
18
,
29
]
give a survey on positive singularities of semilinear elliptic equations. Using this classification, solutions of equation (
1.1
) with many singular points were constructed in
[
23
,
25
]
.
Recently, in
[
10
,
11
]
, we developed the Lions’ method to classify isolated singularities of Choquard equation in the subcritical case.

During the last years, there has been a renewed and increasing
interest in the study of linear and nonlinear elliptic equations involving Hardy operator,
denoting

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-\Delta+\mu|x|^{-2},

motivated by great
applications and important advances on the theory of nonlinear
partial differential equations, for instant,
[
8
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
26
]
and the references therein.
The main tool to derive solutions is the variational methods due to the Hardy type inequality, see
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
]
.

When
\mu\geq\mu_{0}:=-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
, by Hardy inequality and Lax-Milgram theorem, it is known that
for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, non-homogeneous Hardy problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=f\qquad{\rm in}\quad{\Omega}\setminus\{0\},\qquad u=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega

(1.3)

has a unique solution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Here and in the sequel, we always assume that
\Omega

is a bounded, smooth domain containing the origin. A natural question raised: what is the sharp condition of
f
for the existence or nonexistence of (
1.3
)?
An attempt done by Dupaigne in
[
13
]
is to consider problem (
1.3
) when
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
and
N\geq 3
in the distributional sense,

\int_{\Omega}u\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\xi\,dx,\quad\forall\,\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

(1.4)

When
N\geq 3
and
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
, the corresponding semi-linear problem has been studied by
[
3
,
14
]
.

While a critical defect is that the singularity of the fundamental solution
\Phi_{\mu},\Gamma_{\mu}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
is invisible from (
1.4
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and even
\Phi_{\mu}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
fails for (
1.4
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, when
\mu>0
,
where

\Phi_{\mu}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}&{\rm if}\quad\mu>\mu_{0}\\[2.84526pt]
|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}(-\ln|x|)&{\rm if}\quad\mu=\mu_{0}\end{array}\right.\quad{\rm and}\quad\Gamma_{\mu}(x)=|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)},

are two radially symmetric solutions of problem

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.5)

Here

\tau_{\pm}(\mu)=-(N-2)/2\,\pm\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}

(1.6)

are two roots of
\mu-\tau(\tau+N-2)=0
.

To overcome this defect arising from the Hardy potential, a new distributional identity has been proposed in
[
9
]
recently.
For
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
\Phi_{\mu}
could be seen as a
d\mu
-distributional solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=b_{\mu}\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.7)

in the distribution sense

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}\xi\,d\mu=b_{\mu}\xi(0),\quad\forall\,\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.8)

with the measure
d\mu(x)=\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx
and

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu}=-\Delta-2\frac{\tau_{+}(\mu)}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla

(1.9)

and the normalized constant

b_{\mu}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}2\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}\,|\mathcal{S}^{N-1}|&{\rm if}\quad\mu>\mu_{0},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}|\mathcal{S}^{N-1}|&{\rm if}\quad\mu=\mu_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{S}^{N-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
|\mathcal{S}^{N-1}|
is the area of the unit sphere.

For problem (
1.3
), the
d\mu
-distributional sense provides a complete understanding of the existence, non-existence and the singularities of isolated singular
solutions.

Theorem 1.1

[
9
, Theorem 1.3]

Let

be a function in
C^{\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

(i)
Assume that

\int_{\Omega}|f|\,d\mu<+\infty,

(1.10)

then problem (
1.3
), subject to
\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=k
with
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
has a unique solution
u_{k}
, which satisfies the distributional identity

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}(\xi)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}f\xi\,d\mu+b_{\mu}k\xi(0),\quad\forall\,\xi\in C^{1.1}_{0}(\Omega).

(1.11)

(ii)
Assume that
f
verifies (
1.10
) and
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.3
), then
u
satisfies (
1.11
) for some
k\geq 0

and verifies that
\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=k
.

(iii)
Assume that
f\geq 0
and

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}(0)}f\,d\mu=+\infty,

(1.12)

then problem (
1.3
) has no nonnegative solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we concentrate on the classification of isolated singular solutions to (
1.13
). In what follows, we denote by
c_{i}
a generic positive constant.
We first introduce some basic tools in the classical sense. One basic tool is the Comparison Principle.

Lemma 2.1

Let
O
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
L:O\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a continuous function satisfying that for any
x\in O
,

L(x,s_{1})\geq L(x,s_{2})\quad{\rm if}\quad s_{1}\geq s_{2},

then
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+L
with
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
verifies the Comparison Principle,
that is, if

u,\,v\in C^{1,1}(O)\cap C(\bar{O})

verify that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u+L(x,u)\geq\mathcal{L}_{\mu}v+L(x,v)\quad{\rm in}\quad O\qquad{\rm and}\qquad u\geq v\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial O,

then

u\geq v\quad{\rm in}\quad O.

Proof.
Let
w=u-v
and then
w\geq 0
on
\partial O
.
Let
w_{-}=\min\{w,0\}
and our purpose is to prove that
w_{-}\equiv 0
.
If
O_{-}:=\{x\in O:\,w(x)<0\}
is not empty, then it is a bounded

C^{1,1}
domain in
O
and
w_{-}=0
on
\partial O
by the assumption that
u\geq v
on
\partial O
. From Hardy inequality with
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, there holds,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{O_{-}}(-\Delta w_{-}+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}w_{-})w_{-}dx+\int_{O_{-}}[L(x,u)-L(x,v)](u-v)_{-}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{O_{-}}\left(|\nabla w_{-}|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}w_{-}^{2}\right)dx\geq c_{1}\int_{O_{-}}w_{-}^{2}dx,

then
w_{-}=0
in a.e.
O_{-}
, which is impossible with the definition of
O_{-}
.
\Box

As a consequence, we have the following lemma which plays an important role in the obtention of the uniqueness for classical solution.

\Omega
is a bounded

\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=k\end{array}\right.

for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
. If
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
in

, then

u_{1}\leq u_{2}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\}.

Let

3
Existence and Stability

In this section, we search for the singular solutions of problem (

u_{k}
.

We observe that the mapping
k\mapsto u_{k}
is increasing and
u_{k^{*}}:=\lim_{k\to k^{*}}u_{k}
a.e. in
B_{1}(0)
and
u_{k^{*}}\in L^{p}(B_{1}(0),d\mu)
, thus,
u_{k^{*}}
is radially symmetric and non-increasing with respect to
|x|
. Now we claim that
u_{k^{*}}
is locally bounded in
B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
. In fact, if there exists
x_{0}\not=0
such that
u_{k^{*}}(x_{0})=+\infty
, then
u_{k^{*}}=+\infty
in
\overline{B_{|x_{0}|}(0)}
, which is impossible with
u_{k^{*}}\in L^{p}(B_{1}(0),d\mu)
.

Denote

k^{**}=\sup\{k>0:\ (\ref{eq 1.2})\ {\rm has\ minimal\ solution}\}.

Obviously, we have that
k^{**}\geq k^{*}
. We show that

k^{**}=k^{*}.

(3.20)

In fact, for any
k<k^{**}
, let
w_{k}
be a nonnegative solution of (
1.14
),
then problem (
3.2
) has solution
v_{n}
, which is a
d\mu
distributional solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}v_{n}=v_{n-1}^{p}+k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0),\qquad v_{n}=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial B_{1}(0),

where
v_{0}=kV_{0}
is the solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}V_{0}=\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0),\qquad V_{0}=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial B_{1}(0).

(3.21)

Note that
w_{k}
is an upper bound of
\{v_{n}\}
, so the limit of
\{v_{n}\}
is the minimal solution of (
1.14
).
From the symmetry and monotonicity of
v_{n}
implies that
w_{k}
is a classical solution of problem (
1.13
) subjecting to (
1.16
).
So we have that
k^{*}\geq k^{**}
. As a consequence, (
3.20
) holds true.

From the monotonicity of the mapping
k\mapsto u_{k}
, the convergence
u_{k^{*}}=\lim_{k\to k^{*}}u_{k}
and uniformly bounded locally in
B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
, so
it implies by the inner regularity results, we have that
\{u_{k}\}
converges to
u_{k^{*}}
in
C^{2}(B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\})
. Furthermore,
u_{k^{*}}
is a very weak solution of (
1.14
) with
k=k^{*}
, which implies that
u_{k^{*}}
verifies (
1.16
) with
k=k^{*}
.

We prove the uniqueness for
k=k^{*}
.

Since
u_{k^{*}}
is semi-stable, we have that

\sigma_{1}:=\inf_{\xi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mu}}{p\int_{\Omega}u_{k^{*}}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx}\geq 1.

We prove
\sigma_{1}=1
. If not, we may assume that
\sigma_{1}>1
. We note that the minimal solution
u_{k}
could be written as

u_{k}=kV_{0}+w_{k},

where
V_{0}
is the solution of (
3.21
) and
w_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))
is a solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}w_{k}=(kV_{0}+w_{k})^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0).

Denote

E:(0,+\infty)\times H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))\to H^{-1}(B_{1}(0))
as

E(k,u)=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u-(kV_{0}+u)^{p}\quad{\rm and}\quad E_{u}(k,w_{k})w=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}w-pu_{k}^{p-1}w.

Since

\displaystyle\langle E_{u}(k^{*},w_{k^{*}})w,\,w\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}w^{2}-pu_{k^{*}}^{p-1}w^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}})\|w\|_{\mu}^{2}.

So for any
f\in H^{-1}(B_{1}(0))
, there exists a unique solution
w_{f}
in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))
such that

E_{u}(k^{*},w_{k^{*}})w_{f}=f,

that is,
E_{u}(k^{*},w_{k^{*}}):H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))\to H^{-1}(B_{1}(0))
is invertible. Then, by the implicit function theorem, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

E(k,w_{k})=0
has a solution
w_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))
for
k\in(k^{*}-\epsilon,\,k^{*}+\epsilon)
, then
kV_{0}+w_{k}
is a
d\mu
-distributional solution of (
1.14
) with

k>k^{**}
by the fact
k^{*}=k^{**}
. This contradicts the definition of
k^{**}
. Thus,
\sigma_{1}=1
.

By the compact embedding theorem,
\sigma_{1}=1
is achievable and its achieved function
\xi_{1}
could be setting to be nonnegative and satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\xi_{1}=pu_{k^{*}}^{p-1}\xi_{1}\ \ {\rm in}\ \ B_{1}(0),\qquad\xi=0\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \partial B_{1}(0).

If problem (
1.13
) admits a solution
u>u_{k^{*}}
.
Let
w=u-u_{k^{*}}>0
, then it verifies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}w=u^{p}-u_{k^{*}}^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\ {B_{1}(0)}\setminus\{0\},\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}\,}\displaystyle w=0&{\rm on}\quad\ \partial{B_{1}(0)},\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}w(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}pu_{k^{*}}^{p-1}w\xi_{1}\,dx=\int_{B_{1}(0)}w\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}\xi_{1}\,dx=\int_{B_{1}(0)}\xi_{1}(u^{p}-u_{k^{*}}^{p})\,dx>\int_{B_{1}(0)}pu_{k^{*}}^{p-1}w\xi_{1}\,dx,

which is impossible. As a conclusion,
u_{k^{*}}
is the unique solution of (
1.13
) with
k=k^{*}
.
\Box

3.3
Mountain Pass Solution

For the second solution of (
1.13
), we would like to apply the Mountain-Pass theorem to find a positive weak solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=(u_{k}+u_{+})^{p}-u_{k}^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

(3.22)

where
k\in(0,k^{*})
and
u_{k}
is the minimal positive solution of (
1.13
) obtained by Thoerem
1.2
.
The second solution of (
1.13
) is derived by following proposition.

Proposition 3.3

Assume that
p\in(1,p_{\mu}^{*})
,
k\in(0,k^{*})
and

u_{k}
is the minimal positive solution of (
1.13
) subject to (
1.16
).
Then problem (
3.22
) has a positive solution
v_{k}
satisfying that
v_{k}>u_{k}
in
\Omega
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=(u_{k}+u_{+})^{p}-u_{k}^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.23)

Proof.

We would like to employe the Mountain Pass Theorem to look for the weak solution of (
3.22
). A function
v
is said to be a weak solution of (
3.22
) if

\langle u,\xi\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\left[(u_{k}+u_{+})^{p}-u_{k}^{p}\right]\xi\,dx,\quad\forall\xi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.24)

The natural functional associated to (
3.22
) is the following

E(v)=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u_{k},v_{+})\,dx,\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(3.25)

where

F(s,t)=\frac{1}{p+1}\left[(s+t_{+})^{p+1}-s^{p+1}-(p+1)s^{p}t_{+}\right].

(3.26)

We observe that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists some
c_{\epsilon}>0
, depend only on
p
, such that

0\leq F(s,t)\leq(p+\epsilon)s^{p-1}t^{2}+c_{\epsilon}t^{p+1},\quad s,t\geq 0,

then for any
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(u_{k},v_{+})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(p+\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{p-1}v_{+}^{2}\,dx+c_{\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}v_{+}^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{12}\|v\|_{\alpha}^{2},

thus,
E
is well defined in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We observe that
E(0)=0
and let
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|v\|_{\alpha}=1
, then
for
k\in(0,k^{*})
, choosing
\epsilon>0
small enough, it infers from (
3.13
) that

\displaystyle E(tv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u_{k},tv_{+})\,\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle t^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}-(p+\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}v_{k}^{p-1}v^{2}\,dx\right)-c_{13}t^{p+1}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{14}t^{2}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}-c_{15}t^{p+1}\|v\|_{\mu}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}c_{14}t^{2}-c_{15}t^{p+1},

where we used (
3.4
) in the first inequality.
Then there exists
\sigma_{0}>0
small such that for
\|v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1
, we have that

E(\sigma_{0}v)\geq\frac{c_{14}}{4}\sigma_{0}^{2}=:\beta>0.

We take a nonnegative function
v_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then

F(u_{k},tv_{0})\geq\frac{1}{p+1}t^{p+1}v_{0}^{p+1}-tu_{k}^{p}v_{0}.

Since the space of
\{tv_{0}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is
a subspace of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with dimension 1 and all the norms are equivalent, then
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)^{p+1}\,dx>0
.
Then there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle E(tv_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|v_{0}\|_{\mu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u_{k},tv_{0})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|v_{0}\|_{\mu}^{2}-c_{16}t^{p+1}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{p+1}\,dx+t\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{p}v_{0}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{17}(t^{2}+t-t^{p+1})\leq 0.

Choosing
e=t_{0}v_{0}
, we have that
E(e)\leq 0
.

We next prove that
E
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
condition. We say that
E
has
(PS)_{c}
condition, if for any sequence

\{v_{n}\}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
E(v_{n})\to c
and
E^{\prime}(v_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, there
is a convergent subsequence. Here the energy level
c
of functional
E
is characterized by

c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Upsilon}\max_{s\in[0,1]}E(\gamma(s)),

(3.27)

where
\Upsilon=\{\gamma\in C([0,1]:\,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)):\gamma(0)=0,\ \gamma(1)=e\}
. We observe that

c\geq\beta.

Let
\{v_{n}\}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
E(v_{n})\to c
and
E^{\prime}(v_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, then we only have to show that there are a subsequence, still denote it by
\{v_{n}\}

and
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

v_{n}\to v\quad{\rm in}\ \ L^{2}(\Omega,u_{k}^{p-1}\,dx)\quad{\rm and}\ \ L^{p+1}(\Omega)\quad{\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

We see that

c_{18}\|w\|_{\mu}\geq E^{\prime}(v_{n})w=\langle v_{n},w\rangle_{\mu}-\int_{\Omega}f(u_{k},(v_{n})_{+})w\,dx

and

\displaystyle c+1\geq E(v_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{\mu}-\int_{\Omega}F(u_{k},(v_{n})_{+})\,dx.

(3.28)

Let
c_{p}=\min\{1,p-1\}
, then it follows by
[
24
, C.2
(iv)
]
that

f(s,t)t-(2+c_{p})F(s,t)\geq-\frac{c_{p}p}{2}s^{p-1}t^{2},\quad s,t\geq 0,

thus, we multiply (
3.28
) by
(2+c_{p})
and minus
\langle E^{\prime}(v_{n}),(v_{n})_{+}\rangle
, we get that

\displaystyle c+c_{18}\|v_{n}\|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{p}}{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{\mu}-\int_{\Omega}\left[(2+c_{p})F(u_{k},(v_{n})_{+})-f(u_{k},(v_{n})_{+})(v_{n})_{+}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{p}}{2}\left[\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{\mu}-p\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{p-1}v_{n}^{2}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{19}\frac{c_{p}}{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{\mu}.

Then we derive that
v_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for
k\in(0,k^{*})
.
Thus, there exists a subsequence
\{v_{n}\}
and
v
such that

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\quad{\rm in}\quad H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

v_{n}\to v\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \Omega\quad{\rm and\ \ in}\quad L^{p+1}(\Omega),\ \ L^{2}(\Omega,u_{k}^{p-1}\,dx),

when
n\to\infty
. Here we have used that

u_{k}(x)^{p-1}\leq c_{20}(1+|\Phi_{\mu}|)^{p-1}(x),\quad x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\},

(3.29)

where
|\Phi_{\mu}(x)|^{p-1}=|x|^{(p-1)\tau_{-}(\mu)}
if
\mu>\mu_{0}
or
|\Phi_{\mu}(x)|^{p-1}=|x|^{(p-1)\tau_{-}(\mu)}|\ln|x||^{p-1}
if
\mu=\mu_{0}
,
((p-1)\tau_{-}(\mu)>-2
and from
[
24
]
, the embedding:
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega,(1+|\Phi_{\mu}|)^{p-1}\,dx)

is compact for
q\in[1,\frac{2N+2(-\tau_{-}(\mu))(p-1)}{N-2})
, particularly, for
q=2
.

We observe that

\displaystyle|F(u_{k},v_{n})-F(u_{k},v)|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{p+1}|(u_{k}+(v_{n})_{+})^{p}-(u_{k}+v_{+})^{p}-(p+1)u_{k}^{p}((v_{n})_{+}-v_{+})|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c_{21}u_{k}^{p-1}((v_{n})_{+}-v_{+})^{2}+c_{31}((v_{n})_{+}-v_{+})^{p+1},

which implies that

F(u_{k},v_{n})\to F(u_{k},v)\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \Omega\quad{\rm and\ \ in}\quad L^{1}(\Omega).

Then, together with
\lim_{n\to\infty}E(v_{n})=c
,
we have that
\|v_{n}\|_{\mu}\to\|v\|_{\mu}
and

v_{n}\to v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
.

Now Mountain Pass Theorem (for instance,
[
28
, Theorem 6.1]
; see also
[
27
]
) is applied to obtain that
there exists a critical point
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

of
E
at some value
c\geq\beta>0
. By
\beta>0
, we have that
v
is nontrivial and nonnegative. Then
v
is a positive weak solution
of (
3.22
). By using bootstrap argument in
[
20
]
, the interior regularity of
v
could be improved to be in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
, since
u_{k}
is locally bounded in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and
p<p_{\mu}^{*}
. From (
3.29
) with
p<p_{\mu}^{*}
, we have that there is some
q>\frac{N}{2}
such that

u_{k}^{p-1}\in L^{q}(\Omega),

then
v_{k}
is bounded at the origin. Therefore, we have that
v_{k}
is a solution of (
3.23
). Moreover, by Maximum Principle, we conclude that
v>0
in
\Omega
.

\Box

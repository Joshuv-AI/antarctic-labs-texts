Ergodic results for the stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with large damping
Zdzisław Brzeźniak, Benedetta Ferrario, Margherita Zanella

Abstract
We study the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with linear damping, i.e. a zero order dissipation, and additive noise. Working in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d = 2 d=2 or d = 3 d=3 , we prove the uniqueness of the invariant measure when the damping coefficient is sufficiently large.



1
Introduction

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation occurs as a basic model in many areas of physics: hydrodynamics, plasma physics, optics, molecular biology, chemical reaction, etc. It describes the propagation of waves in media with both nonlinear and dispersive responses.

In this article, we investigate the long time behavior of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
with a linear damping term and an additive noise. This is our model

(1.1)

\begin{cases}du(t)+\left[\mathrm{i}\Delta u(t)+\mathrm{i}\alpha|u(t)|^{2\sigma}u(t)+\lambda u(t)\right]\,{\rm d}t=\Phi\,{\rm d}W(t)\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

The unknown is
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
. We consider
\sigma>0
,
\lambda>0
and

\alpha\in\{-1,1\}
; for

\alpha=1
this is called the focusing equation and for
\alpha=-1
this is the defocusing one.

Many results are known about existence and uniqueness of solutions,
in different spatial domains and with different noises;
see
[
BRZ14
,
BRZ16
,
BRZ17
,
BHW19
,
CHS19
,
DBD99
,
DBD03
]
.
Basically these results are obtained without damping but can be easily extended
to the case with
\lambda>0
.

When there is no damping and no forcing term (
\lambda=0
and
\Phi=0
), the equation is
conservative. However, with a noise and a damping term, we expect that
the energy injected by the noise is dissipated by the damping term; because of this balance it is meaningful to look for stationary solutions or invariant measures.
Ekren, Kukavica and Ziane

[
EKZ17
]
and Kim
[
K06
]
provide the existence of invariant measures of the equation
(
1.1
) for any damping coefficient
\lambda>0
;
see also the more general setting of
[
BFZ21
]
for the two dimensional case
in a different spatial domain and with multiplicative noise and the book

[
HW19
]
for the numerical analysis approach.
Notice that the damping
\lambda u
is weaker than the dissipation given by a Laplacian

-\lambda\Delta u
;
for this reason we say that
\lambda u
is a zero-order dissipation.
This implies that the results of existence or uniqueness of invariant measures
for the damped Schrödinger equation are less easy than for the stochastic
parabolic equations (see, e.g.,
[
DPZ96
]
). A similar issue appears in the
stochastic damped 2D Euler equations, for which the existence of invariant measures has been recently proven in

[
BF20
]
; there again the difficulty comes from the absence of the strong dissipation, given by the Laplacian in the
Navier-Stokes equations.

The question of the uniqueness of invariant measures is quite challenging for the SPDE’s with a zero-order dissipation.
Debussche and Odasso
[
DO05
]
proved the
uniqueness of the invariant measure for the cubic focusing
Schrödinger equation (
1.1
), i.e.
\sigma=\alpha=1
, when the spatial domain is a bounded interval;
however no uniqueness results are known for larger
dimension. For the one-dimensional stochastic damped KdV equation there is a recent result
by Glatt-Holtz, Martinez and Richards
[
GHMR21
]
.
However for nonlinear SPDE’s of parabolic type, i.e. with the stronger dissipation term,
the uniqueness issue has been solved in many cases;
see, e.g., the book
[
DPZ96
]
by Da Prato and Zabczyk, and the many examples in the paper
[
GHMR17
]
by
Glatt-Holtz, Mattingly and Richards,
dealing with the coupling technique. Let us point out that the coupling technique allows for the
uniqueness result without restriction on the damping parameter
\lambda
but all the examples
solved so far are set in a bounded spatial domain and not in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The aim of our paper is to investigate the uniqueness of the invariant measures for equation
(
1.1
) in dimension
d=2
and
d=3
,
with some restrictions on the nonlinearity when
d=3
. However
our technique fails for larger dimension.
Notice that also the results for the attractor in the deterministic setting are known for
d\leq 3

(see
[
L95
]
). Our main result is Theorem
5.1
; it provides a sufficient condition
to get the uniqueness of the invariant measure, involving
\lambda
and the intensity of the noise.
To optimize this condition (
5.1
) we perform a
detailed analysis on how the solution depends on
\lambda
.

As far as the contents of this paper are concerned, in Section
2
we introduce the mathematical setting; in Section
3
by means of the Strichartz estimates
we prove a regularity result on the solutions; this will allow to prove in Section

4
that the support of any invariant measure is contained in

V\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and some estimates of the moments are given.
Finally Section
5
presents the uniqueness result.
The four appendices contain auxiliary results.

2
Assumptions and basic results

For
p\geq 1
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the classical Lebesgue space of complex valued functions, and the inner product in the real Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is denoted by

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(y)\overline{v}(y)dy.

We consider the Laplace operator
\Delta
as a linear operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; so

A_{0}=-\Delta,\qquad A_{1}=1-\Delta

are non-negative linear operators
and
\{e^{\mathrm{i}tA_{0}}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a unitary group in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover for
s\geq 0
we consider the power operator

A_{1}^{s/2}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with domain
H^{s}=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|A_{1}^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\}
.
Our two main spaces are
H:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
V:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We set
H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the dual space of
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and denote
again by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the duality bracket.

We define the generalized Sobolev spaces

H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with norm given by
\|u\|_{H^{s,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\|A_{1}^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
We recall the Sobolev embedding theorem, see e.g.
[
BL76
]
[Theorem 6.5.1]: if

1<q<p<\infty
with

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{r-s}{d},

then the following inclusion holds

H^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and there exists a constant
C
such that
\|u\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
u\in H^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.1
.

For
d=1
the space
V
is a subset of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and is a multiplicative algebra. This simplifies the analysis of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
). However for
d\geq 2

the analysis is more involved.

We write the nonlinearity as

(2.1)

F_{\alpha}(u):=\alpha|u|^{2\sigma}u.

Lemma
C.1
provides a priori estimates on it.

As far as the stochastic term is concerned, we consider a real Hilbert space
U
with an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and a complete probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
W
be a
U
-canonical cylindrical Wiener process adapted to a filtration
\mathbb{F}
satisfying the usual conditions. We can write it as a series

W(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}(t)e_{j},

with
\{W_{j}\}_{j}
a sequence of i.i.d. real Wiener processes. Hence

(2.2)

\Phi W(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}(t)\Phi e_{j}

for a given linear operator
\Phi:U\to V
.

Now we rewrite the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) in the abstract form as

(2.3)

\begin{cases}du(t)+\left[-\mathrm{i}A_{0}u(t)+\mathrm{i}F_{\alpha}(u(t))+\lambda u(t)\right]\,{\rm d}t=\Phi\,{\rm d}W(t)\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

We work under the following assumptions on the noise and the nonlinearity.
The initial data
u_{0}
is assumed to be
V
.

Assumption 2.2

(on the noise)
.

We assume that

\Phi:U\to V
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, i.e.

(2.4)

\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U,V)}:=\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\Phi e_{j}\|^{2}_{V}\big)^{1/2}<\infty.

This means that

\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|A_{1}^{1/2}\Phi e_{j}\|^{2}_{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\Phi e_{j}\|^{2}_{H}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\nabla\Phi e_{j}\|^{2}_{H}.

In order to compare our setting with the more general one of our previous paper
[
BFZ21
]

in the two-dimensional setting, we point out that

\Phi
is also a Hilbert-Schmidt operator from
U
to
H
(and we denote

\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;H)}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Phi e_{j}\|^{2}_{H}\right)^{1/2}
) and,
for
d=2
, a
\gamma
-radonifying operator from
U
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any finite
p
.

Assumption 2.3

(on the nonlinearity (
2.1
))
.

•

If
\alpha=1
(focusing), let
0\leq\sigma<\frac{2}{d}
.

•

If
\alpha=-1
(defocusing), let
\begin{cases}0\leq\sigma<\frac{2}{d-2},&\text{ for }d\geq 3\\
\sigma\geq 0,&\text{ for }d\leq 2\end{cases}

We recall the continuous embeddings

\begin{split}H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})&\qquad\forall\ p\in[2,\infty)\\
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})&\qquad\forall\ p\in[2,\tfrac{2d}{d-2}]\text{ for }d\geq 3\end{split}

Hence for
\sigma
chosen as in Assumption
2.3

there is the continuous embedding

(2.5)

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{2+2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover if
\sigma d<2(\sigma+1)
the following Gagliardo-Niremberg inequality holds

(2.6)

\|u\|_{L^{2+2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\frac{\sigma d}{2(1+\sigma)}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\sigma d}{2(1+\sigma)}}.

In particular this holds for the values of
\sigma
specified in the Assumption
2.3
.
In the focusing case, thanks to the
Young inequality for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

(2.7)

\|u\|_{L^{2+2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+2\sigma}\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+\frac{4\sigma}{2-\sigma d}}.

We recall the classical invariant quantities for the deterministic unforced Schrödinger equation
(
\lambda=0
,
\Phi=0
), the mass and the energy (see
[
C03
]
):

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(u)=\|u\|^{2}_{H},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{H}-\frac{\alpha}{2(1+\sigma)}\|u\|_{L^{2+2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+2\sigma}.

They are both well defined on
V
, thanks to (
2.5
).

Remark 2.4
.

In the defocusing case
\alpha=-1
, we have

(2.10)

\mathcal{H}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{H}\geq 0\qquad\forall u\in V.

In the focusing case
\alpha=1
, the energy has no positive sign but we can modify it by adding
a term and recover the sign property. We introduce the modified energy

(2.11)

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{2(1+\sigma)}\|u\|_{L^{2(1+\sigma)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+2\sigma}+G\|u\|_{H}^{2+\frac{4\sigma}{2-\sigma d}}

where
G
is the constant appearing in the following particular form of (
2.7
)

(2.12)

\frac{1}{2(1+\sigma)}\|u\|_{L^{2(1+\sigma)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+2\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{H}^{2}+G\|u\|_{H}^{2+\frac{4\sigma}{2-\sigma d}}.

Therefore

(2.13)

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{H}^{2}\geq 0\qquad\forall u\in V.

Above we denoted by
C
a generic positive constant which might vary from one line to the other,
except
G
which is the particular constant in (
2.12
)
coming from the Gagliardo-Niremberg inequality.
Moreover we shall use this notation:
if
a,b\geq 0
satisfy the inequality
a\leq C_{A}b
with a constant
C_{A}>0
depending on the expression
A
, we write
a\lesssim_{A}b
; for a generic constant we put no subscript.
If we have
a\lesssim_{A}b
and
b\lesssim_{A}a
, we write
a\simeq_{A}b
.

Now we recall the known results on solutions and invariant measures;
then we provide the improved estimates for the mass and the energy.

2.1
Basic results

We recall from
[
DBD03
]
the basic results on the solutions; for any
u_{0}\in V
there exists a unique global solution
u=\{u(t;u_{0})\}_{t\geq 0}
, which is a continuous
V
-valued process.
Here uniqueness is meant as pathwise uniqueness. Actually their result is given without damping but one can easily pass from

\lambda=0
to any
\lambda>0
. The difference consists in getting uniform in time estimates for
the damped equation over the time interval
[0,+\infty)
, whereas they hold on any finite time
interval
[0,T]
when
\lambda=0
. Let us state the result from De Bouard and Debussche

[
DBD03
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Under Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
, for every
u_{0}\in V

there exists a unique
V
-valued and continuous
solution of (
2.3
). This is a Markov process in
V
.
Moreover for any finite
T>0
and integer
m\geq 1
there exist positive constants

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
(depending on
T
,
m
and
\|u_{0}\|_{V}
) such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left[\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}\right]\leq C_{1}

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{H}(u(t))\right]\leq C_{2}.

We notice that the last estimate can be obtained for any power
m>1
for the energy in the
defocusing case as well as for the modified energy in the focusing case.
Similar computations
can be found in
[
EKZ17
]
for the defocusing case;
however their Lemma 5.1
has to be modified in the focusing case.
The strategy is the same as that given in the next Section
2.2
.

As soon as a unique solution in
V
is defined, we can introduce the Markov semigroup.
Let us denote by
u(t;x)
the solution evaluated at time
t>0
, with initial value
x
. We define

(2.14)

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}[f(u(t;x))]

for any Borelian and bounded function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
.

A probability measure
\mu
on the Borelian subsets of
V
is said to be an invariant measure for
(
2.3
) when

(2.15)

\int_{V}P_{t}f\ d\mu=\int_{V}f\ d\mu\qquad\forall t\geq 0,f\in B_{b}(V).

We recall Theorem 3.4 from
[
EKZ17
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Under Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
, there exists an invariant measure supported in
V
.

2.2
Mean estimates

In this section we revise some bounds on the moments of the mass, the energy and
the modified energy,
in order to see how these quantities depend on the damping coefficient
\lambda
.
This improves the results by

[
DBD03
]
[§3] and
[
EKZ17
]
[Lemma 5.1].

This is the result for the mass
\mathcal{M}(u)=\|u\|^{2}_{H}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u_{0}\in V
.
Then under assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
,
for every
m\geq 1
there exists a positive constant
C
(depending on
m
)
such that

(2.16)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}\right]\leq e^{-\lambda mt}\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m}+C\|\Phi\|^{2m}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}\lambda^{-m}

for any
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let us start by proving the estimate (
2.16
) for
m=1
.
We apply the Itô formula to
\mathcal{M}(u(t))

d\mathcal{M}(u(t))+2\lambda\mathcal{M}(u(t))dt=\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}dt+2\operatorname{Re}\langle u(t),\Phi dW(t)\rangle.

Taking the expected value and using the fact that the stochastic integral is a martingale by Theorem
2.5
,
we obtain, for any
t\geq 0
,

\frac{\rm d}{\rm dt}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(u(t))\right]=-2\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(u(t))\right]+\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)}.

Solving this ODE we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(u(t))\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-2\lambda t}\mathcal{M}(u_{0})+\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda t}\mathcal{M}(u_{0})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)},

which proves (
2.16
) for
m=1
.

For larger values of
m
, let us apply the Itô formula to
\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}
to obtain

(2.17)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}=&\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m}-2\lambda m\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{M}(u(s))^{m}\,{\rm d}s\\
&+2m\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{M}(u(s))^{m-1}\text{Re}\langle u(s),\Phi{\rm d}W(s)\rangle\\
&+m\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{M}(u(s))^{m-1}\,{\rm d}s\\
&+2(m-1)m\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{M}(u(s))^{m-2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\text{Re}\langle u(s),\Phi e_{j}\rangle]^{2}\,{\rm d}s.\end{split}

With the Young inequality we get

(2.18)

\begin{split}m\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)}&\mathcal{M}(u)^{m-1}+2(m-1)m\mathcal{M}(u)^{m-2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\text{Re}\langle u,\Phi e_{j}\rangle]^{2}\\
&\leq m(2m-1)\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U,H)}\mathcal{M}(u)^{m-1}\\
&\leq\lambda m\mathcal{M}(u)^{m}+\|\Phi\|^{2m}_{HS(U;H)}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{m-1}(2m-1)^{m}\lambda^{1-m}.\end{split}

By Theorem
2.5
we know that the stochastic integral in (
2.17
) is a martingale,
so taking the expected value on both sides of (
2.17
)
we obtain

\mathbb{E}\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}\leq\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m}-\lambda m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{M}(u(s))^{m}\,{\rm d}s+\|\Phi\|^{2m}_{HS(U;H)}C_{m}\lambda^{1-m}t.

By means of Gronwall inequality we get

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{M}(u(t))^{m}&\leq e^{-\lambda mt}\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m}+\|\Phi\|^{2m}_{HS(U;H)}C_{m}\lambda^{1-m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda m(t-s)}ds\\
&\leq e^{-\lambda mt}\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m}+\|\Phi\|^{2m}_{HS(U;H)}\frac{C_{m}}{m}\lambda^{-m}\end{split}

for any
t\geq 0
.
∎

Notice that the estimates on the mass do not depend on
\alpha
,
whereas this happens in the following result. Indeed,
consider the energy
\mathcal{H}(u)
given in (
2.9
) and the modified energy

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u)
given in (
2.11
). We deal with
d\geq 2
,
since the case
d=1
is easier and already analysed in
[
DO05
]
. Therefore the condition

\sigma<\frac{2}{d}
implies that
\sigma<1
when
d\geq 2
. We introduce the function

(2.19)

\phi_{1}(\sigma,\lambda,\Phi)=\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}^{2+2\sigma}\lambda^{-\sigma}.

Notice that the mapping

\lambda\mapsto\phi_{1}(\sigma,\lambda,\Phi)
is strictly decreasing.
The same property holds for the other function

\phi_{2}(d,\sigma,\lambda,m,\Phi)
appearing the next statement.
The expression of
\phi_{2}(d,\sigma,\lambda,1,\Phi)
is given in
(
2.34
); however for general
m
the expression is similar but
longer and we avoid to specify it.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
u_{0}\in V
.
Under Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
, we have the following estimates:

i)

When
\alpha=-1
, for every
m\geq 1
there exists
a positive constant
C=C(d,\sigma,m)

such that

(2.20)

\mathbb{E}\mathcal{H}(u(t))^{m}\leq e^{-\lambda mt}\mathcal{H}(u_{0})^{m}+C\phi_{1}^{m}\lambda^{-m}

for any
t\geq 0
.

ii)
When
\alpha=1
, for every
m\geq 1
there
exist a smooth positive function

\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}(d,\sigma,\lambda,m,\Phi)

and positive constants
a=a(d,\sigma)
,
C_{1}=C(d,\sigma,m)
and

C_{2}=C(d,\sigma,m)

such that

(2.21)

\mathbb{E}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u(t))^{m}\\
\leq e^{-ma\lambda t}\Big(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0})^{m}+C_{1}(\lambda^{-m}+\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{2}})\mathcal{M}(u_{0})^{m(1+\frac{2\sigma}{2-\sigma d})}\Big)+C_{2}\phi_{2}^{m}\lambda^{-m}

for any
t\geq 0
. Moreover the mapping

\lambda\mapsto\phi_{2}(d,\sigma,\lambda,m,\Phi)
is strictly decreasing.

Proof.

The Itô formula for
\mathcal{H}(u(t))
is

(2.22)

d\mathcal{H}(u(t))+2\lambda\mathcal{H}(u(t))dt=\alpha\lambda\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2\sigma+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\sigma+2}dt\\
-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Re}\langle\Delta u(t)+\alpha|u(t)|^{2\sigma}u(t),\Phi e_{j}\rangle dW_{j}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}dt\\
-\frac{\alpha}{2}\||u(t)|^{\sigma}\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}dt-\alpha\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle|u(t)|^{2\sigma-2},[\operatorname{Re}(\overline{u}(t)\Phi e_{j})]^{2}\rangle dt.

Below we repeatedly use the Hölder and Young inequalities. In particular

(2.23)

A^{m-1}B\leq\epsilon\lambda A^{m}+C_{\epsilon}\lambda^{1-m}B^{m}

and

(2.24)

A^{m-2}B\leq\epsilon\lambda A^{m}+C_{\epsilon}\lambda^{1-\frac{m}{2}}B^{\frac{m}{2}}

for positive
A,B,\lambda,\epsilon
.

\bullet
In the defocusing case
\alpha=-1
, we neglect the first term in the r.h.s. in (
2.22
), i.e.

(2.25)

d\mathcal{H}(u(t))+2\lambda\mathcal{H}(u(t))dt\leq-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Re}\langle\Delta u(t)-|u(t)|^{2\sigma}u(t),\Phi e_{j}\rangle dW_{j}(t)\\
+\Big[\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}+\frac{1}{2}\||u(t)|^{\sigma}\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}+\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle|u(t)|^{2\sigma-2},[\operatorname{Re}(\overline{u}(t)\Phi e_{j})]^{2}\rangle\Big]dt.

Moreover thanks to the Assumption
2.3
we use the
Hölder and Young inequalities to get

(2.26)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\||u|^{\sigma}\Phi\|^{2}_{L_{HS}(U;H)}+\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle|u|^{2\sigma-2},[\operatorname{Re}(\overline{u}(t)\Phi e_{j})]^{2}\rangle\\

there exists
q>2
and
\gamma^{\prime}

such that

(3.8)

\mathbb{E}\|I_{2}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;H^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{\gamma^{\prime}(2\sigma+1)}_{V}\ {\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{2\sigma}{\gamma^{\prime}}}.

Bearing in mind Corollary
2.9
, we get the second and third terms in the r.h.s. of
(
3.1
).
The details are given in the Appendix
D.1
.

It remains to estimate the term

. We choose

as in (

).
Using the stochastic Strichartz estimate (
A.3
), we get for
\sigma>1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|I_{3}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;H^{1,\frac{2\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\|A_{1}^{1/2}I_{3}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A_{1}^{1/2}\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;H)}^{2\sigma}=\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(H;V)}^{2\sigma}.

For smaller values of
\sigma
, we proceed as before for
I_{1}
.

Now consider
d=3
. The additional assumption on
\sigma
appears because of the
stronger conditions on the parameters given later on.

For
q\geq 1
we have
H^{\theta,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
when
\theta q>3
.
So for each
I_{i}
in (

)
we look for an estimate in the norm

for some
parameters
\theta q>3
.

We estimate

for any

. When

we consider the admissible Strichartz pair

.
By means of the homogeneous Strichartz estimate (

) we proceed as before

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;H^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle=\left\|e^{-\lambda\cdot}e^{iA_{0}\cdot}A_{1}^{1/2}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{iA_{0}\cdot}A_{1}^{1/2}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A_{1}^{1/2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u_{0}\|_{V}.

When
\sigma>1
we work with the admissible Strichartz pair

(2\sigma,\frac{6\sigma}{3\sigma-2})
and get

\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;H^{1,\frac{6\sigma}{3\sigma-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\lesssim\|A_{1}^{1/2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u_{0}\|_{V};

The estimate for

It remains to estimate the term

)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\|I_{3}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{2\sigma}(0,T;H^{1,6\frac{4+\sigma^{2}}{4+3\sigma^{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}&\lesssim_{T}\mathbb{E}\|I_{3}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{2+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}}(0,T;H^{1,6\frac{4+\sigma^{2}}{4+3\sigma^{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\\

∎

Notice that the restriction

on the power of the nonlinearity affects only the defocusing case.

We conclude this section by remarking that there is no similar result for

.

Remark 3.2
.

For larger dimension, there is no result similar to those in this section. Indeed, if one looks for

Already the estimate for

Since

for

,
the latter condition

and the condition

Let us notice that also in the deterministic setting the results on the attractors are known for

, see
[

]
.

4
The support of the invariant measures

Now we show some more properties on the invariant measures and their
support.
In dimension
d=2
and
d=3
, thanks to the regularity results of Section
3

we provide an estimate for the moments in the
V
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d=2
or
d=3
and the Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
hold.

Let
\mu
be an invariant measure for equation (
2.3
).
Then for any finite
m\geq 1
we have

(4.1)

\int\|x\|_{V}^{2m}\ d\mu(x)\leq\phi_{4}^{m}\lambda^{-m}

for some smooth function
\phi_{4}=\phi_{4}(d,\sigma,\lambda,m,\Phi)
which is strictly decreasing w.r.t.
\lambda
.

Moreover, supposing in addition that
\sigma<\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}{4}
when
d=3
, we have

(4.2)

\int\|x\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2\sigma}d\mu(x)\leq\phi_{5}(\lambda),

where
\phi_{5}(\lambda)
is a smooth decreasing function,
depending also on the parameters
d,\sigma
and on

\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}
,

Proof.

As far as (
4.1
) is concerned, we define the bounded mapping

\Psi_{k}
on
V
as

\Psi_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\|x\|_{V}^{2m},&\text{if}\ \|x\|_{V}\leq k\\
k^{2m},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

By the invariance of
\mu
and the boundedness of
\Psi_{k}
, we have

(4.3)

\int_{V}\Psi_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{V}P_{s}\Psi_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\qquad\forall s>0.

So

P_{s}\Psi_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{k}(u(s;x))]\leq\mathbb{E}\|u(s;x)\|_{V}^{2m}.

Moreover, from Corollary
2.9
we get an estimate for

\mathbb{E}\|u(s;x)\|_{V}^{2m}
,
and letting
s\to+\infty
the first terms in (
2.36
)
and (
2.37
) vanish so
we get

\lim_{s\to+\infty}P_{s}\Psi_{k}(x)\leq\phi_{4}^{m}\lambda^{-m}\qquad\forall x\in V

where

\phi_{4}=\begin{cases}\phi_{1}+\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;H)}^{2},&\text{ for }\alpha=-1\\
\phi_{2}+\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;H)}^{2},&\text{ for }\alpha=1\end{cases}

The same holds for the integral, that is

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{V}P_{s}\Psi_{k}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)\leq\phi_{4}^{m}\lambda^{-m}

by the Bounded Convergence Theorem.
From (
4.3
) we get

\int_{V}\Psi_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\leq\phi_{4}^{m}\lambda^{-m}

as well.
Since
\Psi_{k}
converges pointwise and monotonically from below to
\|\cdot\|_{V}^{2m}
,
the Monotone Convergence Theorem yields (
4.1
).

As far as (
4.2
) is concerned,
we consider the estimate (
3.1
) for
T=1
and set
\tilde{\Psi}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\sigma}
; this
defines a mapping

\tilde{\Psi}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
Its approximation
\tilde{\Psi}_{k}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, given by

\tilde{\Psi}_{k}(u)=\begin{cases}\|u\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},&\text{if}\ \|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq k\\
k^{2\sigma},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

defines a bounded mapping
\tilde{\Psi}_{k}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

It obviously holds

\int_{V}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{V}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\right)\,{\rm d}s.

By the invariance of
\mu
and the boundedness of
\tilde{\Psi}_{k}
, it also holds

\int_{V}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{V}P_{s}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\qquad\forall s>0.

Thus, by Fubini-Tonelli Theorem, since

\tilde{\Psi}_{k}(u)=\|u\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\wedge k^{2\sigma}\leq\|u\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we get by Proposition
3.1

\displaystyle\int_{V}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}

\displaystyle\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{V}P_{s}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}s=\int_{V}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\Psi}_{k}(u(s;x))\right]\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{1}\|u(s;x)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{V}\left(\|x\|_{V}^{2\sigma}+\lambda^{-b}\psi(x)^{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}+\phi_{3}^{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}\lambda^{-{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}}+\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}^{2\sigma}\right){\rm d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{\|x\|_{V}^{2\sigma}+\lambda^{-b}\psi(x)^{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}+\phi_{3}^{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}\lambda^{-{\sigma(2\sigma+1)}}}\quad\text{ from }\eqref{Goubet_est}.

Keeping in mind (
3.2
) and (
4.1
)
we obtain the existence of a smooth decreasing function

\phi_{5}(\lambda)
, depending also on the parameters
d,\sigma
and on

\|\Phi\|_{L_{HS}(U;V)}
, such that

\int_{V}\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\,{\rm d}\mu\leq\phi_{5}(\lambda)

for any
k
.

Since
\tilde{\Psi}_{k}
converges pointwise and monotonically from below to
\tilde{\Psi}
, the Monotone Convergence Theorem yields the same bound for

\int_{V}\|x\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,{\rm d}\mu(x)
. This proves (
4.2
).
∎

5
Uniqueness of the invariant measure for sufficiently large damping

We will prove that, if the damping coefficient
\lambda
is sufficiently large, then the invariant measure is unique.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
d=2
or
d=3
. In addition to the Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3

we suppose that
\sigma<\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}{4}
when
d=3
.

If

(5.1)

\lambda>2\phi_{5}(\lambda)

where
\phi_{5}
is the function appearing in Proposition
4.1
,
then there exists a unique invariant measure for equation (
2.3
).

Proof.

The existence of an invariant measure comes from Theorem
2.6
.
Now we prove uniqueness by means of a reductio ad absurdum.
Let us suppose that there exists more than one invariant measure. In particular there exist two different
ergodic invariant measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. For both of them Proposition
4.1
holds. Fix either
i=1
or
i=2
and consider
any
f\in L^{1}(\mu_{i})
. Then
by the Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
CFS82
]
)
for
\mu_{i}
-a.e.
x_{i}\in V
we have

(5.2)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(u(s;x_{i}))\ ds=\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{i}\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

Here
u(t;x)
is the solution at time
t
, with initial value
u(0)=x\in V
.

Now fix two initial data
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
belonging, respectively,
to the support of the measure
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
We have

\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{1}-\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{2}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}[f(u(s;x_{1}))-f(u(s;x_{2}))]\ ds

\mathbb{P}
-a.s..
Taking any arbitrary
f
in the set
\mathcal{G}_{0}
defined in (
B.2
), we get

\left|\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{1}-\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{2}\right|\leq L\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s;x_{1})-u(s;x_{2})\|_{H}ds.

If we prove that

(5.3)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|u(t;x_{1})-u(t;x_{2})\|_{H}=0\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.,

then we conclude that

\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{1}-\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{2}=0

so

\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
thanks to Lemma
B.1
.
So let us focus on the limit (
5.3
).

With a short notation we write
u_{i}(t)=u(t;x_{i})
.
Then consider the difference
w=u_{1}-u_{2}
fulfilling

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}w(t)-\mathrm{i}A_{0}w(t)+\mathrm{i}F_{\alpha}(u_{1}(t))-\mathrm{i}F_{\alpha}(u_{2}(t))+\gamma w(t)=0\\
w(0)=x_{1}-x_{2}\end{cases}

so

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{H}^{2}+\gamma\|w(t)\|_{H}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|[|u_{1}(t)|^{2\sigma}u_{1}(t)-|u_{2}(t)|^{2\sigma}u_{2}(t)]w(t)\Big|dy.

Using the elementary estimate

||u_{1}|^{2\sigma}u_{1}-|u_{2}|^{2\sigma}u_{2}|\leq C_{\sigma}[|u_{1}|^{2\sigma}+|u_{2}|^{2\sigma}]|u_{1}-u_{2}|,

we bound the nonlinear term in the r.h.s. as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|[|u_{1}|^{2\sigma}u_{1}-|u_{2}|^{2\sigma}u_{2}]w|\ dy\leq[\|u_{1}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{2}\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}]\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Therefore

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|w(t)\|^{2}_{H}+2\lambda\|w(t)\|^{2}_{H}\leq 2\left(\|u_{1}(t)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{2}(t)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\|w(t)\|_{H}^{2}.

Gronwall inequality gives

\|w(t)\|^{2}_{H}\leq\|w(0)\|^{2}_{H}e^{-2\lambda t+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u_{1}(s)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{2}(s)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)ds}

that is

(5.4)

\|w(t)\|^{2}_{H}\leq\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}_{H}e^{-2t\left[\lambda-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u_{1}(s)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{2}(s)\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)ds\right]}.

This is a pathwise estimate.

We know from Proposition
4.1
that
f(x)=\|x\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\sigma}\in L^{1}(\mu_{i})
; therefore (
5.2
) becomes

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s;x_{i})\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds=\int_{V}\|x\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\ d\mu_{i}(x)\leq\phi_{5}(\lambda)

\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for either
i=1
or
i=2
.
Therefore, if

\lambda>2\phi_{5}(\lambda),

the exponential term in the r.h.s. of (
5.4
) vanishes as
t\to+\infty
.
This proves (
5.3
) and concludes the proof.
∎

.

Appendix B
Determining sets

The set

(B.1)

\mathcal{G}_{1}=\left\{f\in C_{b}(V):\sup_{u\neq v}\frac{f(u)-f(v)}{\|u-v\|_{V}}<\infty\right\}

is a
determining set for measures on
V
(see, e.g.,
[
B13
]
Theorem 1.2). This means that given two probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on
V
we have

\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{1}=\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{2}\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{G}_{1}\quad\Longrightarrow\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}.

Following Remark 2.2 in
[
GHMR17
]
we can consider as a determining set for
measures on
V
the set

(B.2)

\mathcal{G}_{0}=\left\{f\in C_{b}(V):\sup_{u\neq v}\frac{f(u)-f(v)}{\|u-v\|_{H}}<\infty\right\}

involving the weaker
H
-norm instead of the
V
-norm.
Indeed we have

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
be two invariant measures.
If

\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{1}=\int_{V}f\ d\mu_{2}\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{G}_{0},

then
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

Proof.

We show the proof since we work in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whereas

[
GHMR17
]
deals with a bounded domain.

Set
P_{N}x
to be the element whose Fourier transform is
1_{|\xi|\leq N}\mathcal{F}(x)
; hence

\|P_{N}x\|_{V}\leq\sqrt{1+N^{2}}\|x\|_{H}
. Now we show that
any function
f\in\mathcal{G}_{1}

can be approximated by a function
f_{N}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
by setting
f_{N}(x)=f(P_{N}x)
. Indeed

\left|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}(y)\right|\leq L\|P_{N}x-P_{N}y\|_{V}\leq L\sqrt{1+N^{2}}\|x-y\|_{H}

By assumption we know that

\int_{V}f_{N}\ d\mu_{1}=\int_{V}f_{N}\ d\mu_{2}

Taking the limit as
N\to+\infty
, by the bounded convergence theorem we get the same identity for
f\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Hence
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.
∎

Appendix C
Estimate of the nonlinearity

We consider
F(u)=|u|^{2\sigma}u
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
d=2
. For any
\sigma>0
, if
p\in(1,2)
is defined as

(C.1)

p=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{2\sigma+1},&\qquad 0<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{4}{3},&\qquad\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

then

(C.2)

\|F(u)\|_{H^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2\sigma+1}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Let
d=3
. For any
\sigma\in(0,\frac{3}{2}]

we have

(C.3)

\|F(u)\|_{H^{1,\frac{6}{2\sigma+3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2\sigma+1}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

and for any
\sigma\in[1,\frac{3}{2}]
we have

(C.4)

\|F(u)\|_{H^{2-\sigma,\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2\sigma+1}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Proof.

We start with the case
d=2
. To estimate the
H^{1,p}
-norm of
F
it is enough to deal with

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and
\|\partial F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
We compute

(C.5)

\partial F(u)=\sigma|u|^{2\sigma-2}\left(\bar{u}\partial u+u\partial\bar{u}\right)u+|u|^{2\sigma}\partial u,\qquad\text{for an arbitrary}\ u\in V,

and thus
|\partial F(u)|\lesssim_{\sigma}|u|^{2\sigma}|\partial u|
.

We have

(C.6)

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{(2\sigma+1)p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\sigma+1}

and the Hölder inequality, for
1\leq p<2
, gives

(C.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\||u|^{2\sigma}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\partial u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\frac{4\sigma p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\sigma}\|u\|_{V}

We recall the Sobolev embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ for any }2\leq r<\infty.

Therefore, if

\begin{cases}2\leq(2\sigma+1)p\\
2\leq\dfrac{4\sigma p}{2-p}\end{cases}

then both the r.h.s. of (
C.6
) and (
C.7
) can be estimated by a quantity
involving the
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-norm. The two latter inequalities are the same as

p\geq\frac{2}{2\sigma+1}

so one easily sees that the choice (
C.1
)
allows to fulfil the two required estimates, i.e.

\|F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u\|_{V}^{2\sigma+1}

and
\|\partial F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u\|_{V}^{2\sigma+1}
.
This proves (
C.2
).

For
d=3
, first we show that for any
\sigma\in(0,\frac{3}{2}]

(C.8)

\|F(u)\|_{H^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2\sigma+1}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with
p=\frac{6}{2\sigma+3}\in[1,2)
.

To this end we notice that the r.h.s. of (
C.6
) and (
C.7
)
are estimated by a quantity involving the

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-norm if
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{(2\sigma+1)p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{\frac{4\sigma p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Recalling the Sobolev embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\text{ for any }2\leq r\leq 6

we get the conditions

(C.9)

\displaystyle 2\leq(2\sigma+1)p\leq 6\qquad\text{ (equivalent to }\frac{2}{2\sigma+1}\leq p\leq\frac{6}{2\sigma+1})

(C.10)

\displaystyle 2\leq\frac{4\sigma p}{2-p}\leq 6\qquad\text{ (equivalent to }\frac{2}{2\sigma+1}\leq p\leq\frac{6}{2\sigma+3})

Notice that (
C.10
) is stronger than (
C.9
); moreover (
C.10
) has a solution

p\in[1,2)
only if
\sigma\in(0,\frac{3}{2}]
.
Choosing

(C.11)

p=\frac{6}{2\sigma+3}\in[1,2)

we fulfil all the requirements and so we have proved (
C.3
).

Now for
1\leq\sigma\leq\frac{3}{2}
there is the continuous embedding

H^{1,\frac{6}{2\sigma+3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subseteq H^{2-\sigma,\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Hence from (
C.3
) we get (
C.4
).
∎

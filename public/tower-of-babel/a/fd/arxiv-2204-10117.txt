Hölder continuity of Oseledets subspaces for linear cocycles on Banach spaces Thanks: This work is partially supported by NSFC (11790274, 11871361).
Chiyi Luo, Yun Zhao

Abstract
. Let f : X → X f:X\to X be an invertible Lipschitz transformation on a compact metric space X X . Given a Hölder continuous invertible operator cocycles on a Banach space and an f f -invariant ergodic measure, this paper establishes the Hölder continuity of Oseledets subspaces over a compact set of arbitrarily large measure. This extends a result in [ 1 ] for invertible operator cocycles on a Banach space. Finally, this paper proves the Hölder continuity in the non-invertible case.

1.
Introduction

The multiplicative ergodic theorem plays a fundamental role in the modern theory of dynamical systems, which says that the Lyapunov exponents exist almost everywhere with respect to every given invariant measure, see
[
2
]
for more details of Lyapunov exponents. Before Oseledets’ work on multiplicative ergodic theorem
[
15
]
, there are previous work on random multiplication of matrices by Furstenberg and Kesten
[
9
]
.

Roughly speaking, the multiplicative ergodic theorem generalizes the notion of eigenvalues and eigenvectors for a single matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
to a product of matrices
A(f^{n-1}(x)\cdots A(f(x))A(x)
, where
A:X\rightarrow GL(d)
is an invertible matrix valued function on a probability space
(X,\mu)
and
f:X\rightarrow X
is a measure-preserving map with respect to
\mu
. Under some mild reasonable assumptions, there exists a finite set of numbers and subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are called Lyapunov exponents and Oseledets subspaces respectively, form a decomposition or a filtration of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(depending on whether
f
is invertible or not). These exponents define the corresponding subspaces of vectors having the same exponential growth rate under the action of the cocycle generated by
A
.

For infinite dimensional dynamical
systems, Ruelle
[
16
]
first proved the multiplicative ergodic theorem for
compact linear operators on a separable Hilbert space by using the operator theory of Hilbert
spaces, the main difficulty in this case are that the non-compactness of the phase space and the non-invertibility of the transformation. Mané
[
14
]
overcame the lack of inner product structure and extended the multiplicative ergodic theorem to compact operators on
Banach spaces. Later, Thieullen
[
19
]
obtained the multiplicative ergodic theorem for bounded linear operators on Banach spaces. Lian and Lu
[
12
]
established the multiplicative ergodic theorem for strong measurable operator cocycles on separable Banach spaces, see
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
for other versions of the multiplicative ergodic theorem for random dynamics in infinite dimensional spaces as mentioned in
[
12
]
.
Froyland
et al

[
7
]
established the multiplicative ergodic theorem in finite dimensional spaces for an arbitrary matrix cocycle over an invertible ergodic measure-preserving
transformation of a probability space. Later, the authors in
[
8
]
extended it to a continuous semi-invertible operator on Banach spaces, and Tokman
et al

[
10
]
set up the multiplicative ergodic theorem for a strong measurable semi-invertible operator on Banach spaces.

The dependence of the exponents and the corresponding Oseledets subspaces on the orbit is usually measurable. The stronger regularity of the dependence has been investigated. Brin
[
4
]
studied the special case of a partially hyperbolic
C^{1+\varepsilon}
diffeomorphism on a compact manifold. To be more precisely, for every ergodic measure he proved that the subspace given by the direct sum of the Oseledets subspaces corresponding to strictly negative Lyapunov exponents depend Hölder continuously on the chosen orbit (see also
[
2
, Chapter 5]
). If
f:X\rightarrow X
is a Lipschitz map on a compact metric space and
A:X\rightarrow GL(d)
is Hölder continuous, for every ergodic measure Araujo
et al

[
1
]
established the Hölder continuity of the Oseledets subspaces over a compact set of arbitrarily large measure. Recently, Dragičević
et al

[
6
]
proved the same result in the setting of possibly non-invertible cocycles, which, in addition, may take values in the space of compact operators on a Hilbert space.

Let
f:X\rightarrow X
be a Lipschitz map on a compact metric space
X
, for an invertible operator cocycles
\mathcal{A}(x,n)
on Banach spaces and an ergodic measure we show that the Oseledets subspaces depend Hölder continuously on a compact set of measure arbitrarily close to 1. In Section
2
, we recall some basic concepts and the multiplicative ergodic theorem for cocycles on Banach spaces which was proved by Thieullen
[
19
]
, we also present the statements of the main results here. In Section
3
, we give the detailed proofs of the main result. For an ergodic measure, using the Lyapunov norm to constructing a regular set which is compact and the measure arbitrarily close to 1, we estimate the distance of the Oseledets subspaces, which depend Hölder continuously on points of the regular set. Finally, we prove the Hölder continuity of Oseledets subspaces in the non-invertible case in Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries and statement of the main result

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise specified, let
f
be a homeomorphism on a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and let
\mu
be an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure, and
\mathcal{B}
denotes an infinite dimensional Banach space with norm
||\cdot||
.

2.1.
Linear cocycles

Let
GL(\mathcal{B})
denote the group of invertible bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{B}
, the metric
\rho
on
GL(\mathcal{B})
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\rho(A,B)=||A-B||+||A^{-1}-B^{-1}||,

(2.1)

then
(GL(\mathcal{B}),\rho)
is a complete metric space.

Let
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
be a continuous operator valued function, we have that
\|A\|:=\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||<\infty
since
X
is compact. Consequently, we have that
\log||A||\in L_{1}(\mu)
. A map
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
is called a
linear cocycle
over
f
generated by
A
, if

\mathcal{A}(x,n)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &A(f^{n-1}(x))\circ\cdots\circ A(f(x))\circ A(x),&n>0\\
&Id,&n=0\\
&A(f^{-n}(x))^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ A(f^{-1}(x))^{-1},&n<0\end{aligned}\right.

for every
x\in X
. Clearly,
\mathcal{A}(x,n+k)=\mathcal{A}(f^{k}(x),n)\circ\mathcal{A}(x,k)
. Let

\lambda(x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)||

whenever the limit exists.

Define the index of compactness (or Kuratowski measure of non-compactness) of a continuous linear operator
T:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
as the number

||T||_{\alpha}:=\inf\{k>0:T(S_{\mathcal{B}})\ \text{can be covered by finitely many balls of radius }k\},

where
S_{\mathcal{B}}
is the unit ball of
\mathcal{B}
. It is easy to show that
||T||_{\alpha}\leq||T||
and
\|T\circ S\|_{\alpha}\leq\|T\|_{\alpha}\|S\|_{\alpha}
for every
T,S\in GL(\mathcal{B})
.
Given a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
over
f
generated by
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
,
the index of compactness at point
x
is defined as

\alpha(x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)||_{\alpha}

whenever the limit exists.

By the subadditive ergodic theorem,
\alpha(x)
and
\lambda(x)
is well defined for
\mu
-almost every
x
, and the function
x\mapsto\alpha(x)
and
x\mapsto\lambda(x)
are measurable and
f
-invariant. Since
\mu
is an
f
-invariant ergodic measure,
\alpha(x)
and
\lambda(x)
are constants for
\mu
-almost every
x
, denote the constants by
\alpha(A,\mu)
and
\lambda(A,\mu)
respectively. Note that
\alpha(A,\mu)\leq\lambda(A,\mu)
, and we call
A
is
quasi-compact
if
\alpha(A,\mu)<\lambda(A,\mu)
.

The following Oseledets multiplicative ergodic theorem of continuous cocycles on Banach spaces was proved by P. Thieullen
[
19
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Multiplicative ergodic theorem)
.

Let
f
be a homeomorphism on a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and let
\mu
be an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure. Given a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}
over
f
generated by the quasi-compact and continuous operator valued function
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
, where
(\mathcal{B},||\cdot||)
is a Banach space. Then there exists a
f
-invariant subset
X_{0}\subset X
of full
\mu
-measure such that one of the following cases hold:

Case (1)
: There exist finite numbers

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}

with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda(A,\mu)
and
\lambda_{k}>\alpha(A,\mu)
, and for every
x\in X_{0}
there is a splitting

\mathcal{B}=E_{1}(x)\oplus E_{2}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}(x)\oplus F(x)

such that

(a)

for each
i=\{1,\cdots,k\}
,
\dim E_{i}(x)=m_{i}
is finite and constant. Moreover,
A(x)E_{i}(x)=E_{i}(f(x))
, and for any
v\in E_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
we have

\lambda_{i}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,-n)v||;

(b)

the distribution
F(x)
is closed and finite-codimensional, satisfies
A(x)F(x)=F(f(x))
and

\alpha(A,\mu)>\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)|_{F(x)}||;

(c)

the maps
x\mapsto E_{i}(x),x\mapsto F(x)
are measurable;

(d)

writing
\pi_{i}(x)
for the projection of
\mathcal{B}
onto
E_{i}(x)\,(i=1,2,\cdots,k)
via the splitting at
x
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\pi_{i}(f^{n}(x))||=0.

Case (2)
: There exist infinite numbers

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\alpha(A,\mu)

with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda(A,\mu)
such that the following properties hold: for each
x\in X_{0}
and each
positive integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a splitting

\mathcal{B}=E_{1}(x)\oplus E_{2}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}(x)\oplus F_{k+1}(x)

such that

(a)

for each
i=\{1,\cdots,k\}
,
\dim E_{i}(x)=m_{i}
is finite and constant for
\mu
-almost every
x
. Moreover,
A(x)E_{i}(x)=E_{i}(f(x))
and for every
v\in E_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
we have

\lambda_{i}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,-n)v||;

(b)

the distribution
F_{k+1}(x)
is closed, finite-codimensional and
A(x)F_{k+1}(x)=F_{k+1}(f(x))
and

\lambda_{k+1}>\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)|_{F_{k+1}(x)}||;

(c)

the maps
x\mapsto E_{i}(x),x\mapsto F_{k+1}(x)
are measurable;

(d)

writing
\pi_{i}(x)
for the projection of
\mathcal{B}
onto
E_{i}(x)
via the splitting at
x
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\pi_{i}(f^{n}(x))||=0.

The number
\lambda_{i}
in the above theorem is called the
i
-th Lyapunov exponents
of the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
\mu
and
m_{i}
are called multiplicities of

\lambda_{i}
for every
i
. Moreover, the splitting is called the
Oseledets splitting
and
E_{i}(x)
is called the
Oseledets subspaces.

2.2.
Gaps and distance between closed linear subspaces

We gather in this subsection some facts that are relevant to Banach spaces. The definitions of gap and distance are taken from Kato
[
11
]
(see also
[
12
]
and
[
3
]
).

Let
E
and
F
be two non-trivial closed linear subspaces of the Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, and let
S_{E}
denote the unit ball of
E
. Put

\displaystyle\delta(E,F)=\sup_{v\in S_{E}}\operatorname{dist}(v,F)=\inf\{a:\operatorname{dist}(v,F)\leq a||v||\ \text{for any}\ v\in E\setminus\{0\}\}

where
\operatorname{dist}(v,F)=\inf_{u\in F}||v-u||
,
and define the gap between
E
and
F
as follows

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}(E,F)=\max\{\delta(E,F),\delta(F,E)\}.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a Hilbert space, then
\hat{\delta}
is a metric and coincides with the operator norm of the difference between orthogonal projections. However, in the case that
\mathcal{B}
is a Banach space,
\hat{\delta}
is not a metric since it does not satisfies the triangle inequality in general
[
11
]
.

The topology on the set of closed linear subspaces of the Banach space
\mathcal{B}
is the metric topology defined by the Hausdorff distance
\hat{d}
between unit spheres

\displaystyle\hat{d}(E,F)=\max\{\operatorname{dist}(E,F),\operatorname{dist}(F,E)\}

where
\operatorname{dist}(E,F)=\sup_{v\in S_{E}}\operatorname{dist}(v,S_{F})
.
The gap
\hat{\delta}
and the distance
\hat{d}
are related by the following inequality (see
[
11
]
):

\hat{\delta}(E,F)\leq\hat{d}(E,F)\leq 2\hat{\delta}(E,F).

(2.2)

Hence, in the following we will work with
\hat{\delta}(E,F)
since it is more convenient.

The following lemma gives conditions under which complementation persists, see
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
for detailed proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
E
is a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}
,
F
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}=E\oplus F
. Let
\pi_{E//F}
be the projection operator
E\oplus F\rightarrow E
. If
{E}^{\prime}
is a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}
such that
\hat{d}(E,{E}^{\prime})\leq||\pi_{E//F}||^{-1}
, then
\mathcal{B}={E}^{\prime}\oplus F
.

2.3.
Statement of the main result

For a cocycle generated by an invertible bounded linear operator on the Banach space
\mathcal{B}
and an ergodic measure, this paper proves that the corresponding Oseledets subspaces varies continuously on a compact set of arbitrarily large measure. More precisely, if
f:X\rightarrow X
is bi-Lipschitz on a compact metric space and
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
is Hölder continuous, then the Oseledets subspaces
E_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)
and
F(x)
are Hölder continuous on a compact set of measure close to 1. This extends the main results in
[
1
]
for linear cocycles on a Banach space. Dragičević
et al

[
6
]
also proved the same result for possibly non-invertible cocycles on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as well as compact operator cocycles on Hilbert spaces.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow X
be a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism on a compact metric space
X
and
\mu
an ergodic Borel probability measure on
X
, and let
\mathcal{A}
be a linear cocycle over
f
generated by the
\nu
-Hölder continuous function
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
.
Let
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{k+1}
denote the distinct
k+1
Lyapunov exponents, corresponding to the splitting
\mathcal{B}=E_{1}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}(x)\oplus F_{k+1}(x)
defined for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
. Then, for every
\gamma>0
, there exist a compact subset
\Lambda_{\gamma}
of
X
with
\mu(\Lambda_{\gamma})>1-\gamma
, and constants
C=C(\Lambda_{\gamma})>0
,
\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1})<1,i=1,\cdots,k+1
and
\delta=\delta(\gamma,\Lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1})
such that for all
x,y\in\Lambda_{\gamma}
with
d(x,y)<\delta
, we have that for
i=1,\cdots,k

\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),E_{i}(y))\leq Cd(x,y)^{\nu\omega_{i}}\ \text{and}\ \ \hat{d}(F_{k+1}(x),F_{k+1}(y))\leq Cd(x,y)^{\nu\omega_{k+1}}.

Remark 2.1
.

In the above theorem,

k
is the number of all finite dimensional subspaces and
\lambda_{k+1}:=\alpha(A,\mu),F_{k+1}(x):=F(x)
in
Case (1)
, and
k
is any given positive integer in
Case (2)
.

3.
Proofs

We only prove Theorem
2.2
for the
Case (1)
, that is, there are finite Lyapunov exponents:

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{k+1}:=\alpha(A,\mu),

correspoding to the splitting
\mathcal{B}=E_{1}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}(x)\oplus F(x)
defined on an
f
-invariant subset
X_{0}
of full
\mu
-measure. The
Case (2)
can be proven in a similar fashion.

3.1.
Construction of the regular set

We first recall the definition of the Lyapunov norm. Given a sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
, for each
x\in X_{0}
and every
u=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{k}+u_{k+1}\in\mathcal{B},u_{i}\in E_{i}(x)\,(i=1,\cdots,k),u_{k+1}\in F(x)
, the Lyapunov norm is defined as

||u||_{x,\varepsilon}:=||u||_{x}=||u_{1}||_{x}+\cdots+||u_{k}||_{x}+||u_{k+1}||_{x}

where

||u_{i}||_{x}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-n\lambda_{i}-|n|\varepsilon}||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u_{i}||
for
i=1,\cdots,k
and

||u_{k+1}||_{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n(\lambda_{k+1}+\varepsilon)}||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u_{k+1}||.

For every
n>0
and every
u_{i}\in E_{i}(x)\,(i=1,\cdots,k)
, one can easily show that

e^{n(\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon)}||u_{i}||_{x}\leq||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u_{i}||_{f^{n}(x)}\leq e^{n(\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon)}||u_{i}||_{x},

(3.1)

and for every
u_{k+1}\in F(x)

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u_{k+1}||_{f^{n}(x)}\leq e^{n(\lambda_{k+1}+\varepsilon)}||u_{k+1}||_{x}.

(3.2)

The following lemma provides some fundamental properties of the above Lyapunov norm, see
[
5
, Theorem 7.2.3]
for detailed proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a small number
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a measurable function
D_{\varepsilon}:X_{0}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
such that for every
x\in X_{0}

||\cdot||\leq||\cdot||_{x}\leq D_{\varepsilon}(x)||\cdot||

(3.3)

and for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}

D_{\varepsilon}(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}D_{\varepsilon}(x).

In the setting of the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the Hilbert space, we have that the angels between two Oseledets subspaces decay sub-exponentially along orbits of
x
. However, in the case of Banach spaces, there is not such statement since the lack of reasonable definition of the “angels” between two closed linear subspaces of Banach space. The fact that the norm of each projection operator
\pi_{i}(x),i=1,\cdots,k
is temperate (see (d) of Theorem
2.1
) helps us to overcome this difficulty. For the multiplicative ergodic theorem of semi-invertible operator on Banach spaces (see
[
8
]
), it is also valid by the following lemma proved by Dragičević
et al

[
6
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be an
f
-invariant set and let
E(x)
and
F(x),x\in\Lambda
be
two families of closed subspaces of
\mathcal{B}
. Assume that there exist numbers
\chi_{2}<\chi_{1},\varepsilon>0
with
\chi_{2}+3\varepsilon\leq\chi_{1}-2\varepsilon
and measurable functions
C,\tilde{C}:\Lambda\rightarrow[1,\infty)
such that

(1)

A(x)E(x)\subset E(f(x)),A(x)F(x)\subset F(f(x))
and
E(x)\cap F(x)=\{0\}
for every
x\in\Lambda
;

(2)

for every
x\in\Lambda,v\in E(x)\oplus F(x)
and
n\geq 0
,

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||\leq\tilde{C}(x)e^{(\chi_{1}+\varepsilon)n}||v||;

(3)

for every
x\in\Lambda,v\in F(x)
and
n\geq 0
,

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||\geq\frac{1}{\tilde{C}(x)}e^{(\chi_{1}-\varepsilon)n}||v||;

(4)

for every
x\in\Lambda,v\in E(x)
and
n\geq 0
,

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||\leq C(x)e^{(\chi_{2}+\varepsilon)n}||v||;\ and

(5)

for every
x\in\Lambda
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

C(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}C(x)\ and\ \tilde{C}(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}\tilde{C}(x).

Then, there exists a measurable function
K:\Lambda\rightarrow[1,\infty)
satisfies

K(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}K(x)\ \text{for each}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\ and\ x\in\Lambda

and such that

||v_{1}||\leq K(x)||v_{1}+v_{2}||\ and\ ||v_{2}||\leq K(x)||v_{1}+v_{2}||

for
v_{1}\in E(x)
and
v_{2}\in F(x)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Dragičević et al
[
6
]
proved the above lemma for the Euclid space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is also valid for the case of Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, with a minor modification of the proof. Use the lemma above with some inductions, one can easily to show that (d) of Theorem
2.1
is valid for semi-invertible cocycle on Banach space.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cocycle over
f
with Lyapunov exponents as in Case (1), for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
. Let

E_{i}^{+}(x)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i}E_{j}(x)\ and\ E_{i}^{-}(x)=(\bigoplus_{j=i+1}^{k}E_{j}(x))\bigoplus F(x).

Then there exists a full
\mu
-measure,
f
-invariant subset
\Lambda
of
X
such that, for each
\varepsilon>0
small enough with
\varepsilon<\min_{i=1,\cdots,k}\{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})/100\}
, there are measurable functions
C,K:\Lambda\rightarrow[1,\infty)
with

C(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}C(x)\ and\ K(f^{n}(x))\leq e^{|n|\varepsilon}K(x).

so that for every
x\in\Lambda
:

(1)

for each
u\in E_{i}^{-}(x),v\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
and
n\geq 0
,

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\leq C(x)e^{(\lambda_{i+1}+\varepsilon)n}||u||\ and\ ||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||\geq\frac{1}{C(x)}e^{(\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon)n}||v||;

(2)

for each
u\in E_{i}^{-}(x)
and
v\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
,

\max\{||u||,||v||\}\leq K(x)||u+v||.

Proof.

Following the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
[
13
]
, we give the detailed proofs as follows.
By (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.1
, there exist a set
\Lambda
and a measurable function
C:\Lambda\rightarrow[1,\infty)
with
C(f^{\pm}(x))\leq e^{\varepsilon}C(x)
so that the first statement hold. To complete the proof of the theorem, it suffices to prove the second statement.

Let
\pi_{i}^{+}(x)
and
\pi_{i}^{-}(x)
denote the projections of
\mathcal{B}
onto
E_{i}^{+}(x)
and
E_{i}^{-}(x)
via the splitting
\mathcal{B}=E_{i}^{+}(x)\oplus E_{i}^{-}(x)
. By (d) of Theorem
2.1
, for every
x\in\Lambda
we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\pi_{i}^{+}(f^{n}(x))||=0,\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\pi_{i}^{-}(f^{n}(x))||=0.

Therefore, define the function
K:\Lambda\rightarrow[1,\infty)
as follows:

K(x)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{\max\{||\pi_{i}^{-}(f^{n}(x))||,||\pi_{i}^{+}(f^{n}(x))||\}}{e^{|n|\varepsilon}}.

One can easily to show that
K
is a well defined function on
\Lambda
. Moreover, one has that
K(x)\geq\max\{||\pi_{i}^{-}(x)||,||\pi_{i}^{+}(x)||\}
and
K(f^{\pm}(x))\leq e^{\varepsilon}K(x)
for every
x\in\Lambda
.

Finally, for each
u\in E_{i}^{-}(x)
and each
v\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
one has that

\displaystyle||u||=||\pi_{i}^{-}(x)(u+v)||\leq||\pi_{i}^{-}(x)||\cdot||u+v||\leq K(x)||u+v||

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle||v||=||\pi_{i}^{+}(x)(u+v)||\leq||\pi_{i}^{+}(x)||\cdot||u+v||\leq K(x)||u+v||.

(3.5)

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Fix a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the regular set
\Lambda_{\ell}
by

\Lambda_{\ell}=\{x\in\Lambda:C(x)\leq\ell\ and\ K(x)\leq\ell\}.

One can easily show that each
\Lambda_{\ell}
is compact,
\Lambda_{\ell}\subset\Lambda_{\ell+1}
and
\bigcup_{\ell>0}\Lambda_{\ell}=\Lambda
. Thus, for every
\gamma>0
, we may choose a subset
\Lambda_{\ell}
with
\mu(\Lambda_{\ell})>1-\gamma
.

3.2.
Hölder continuity of maps

and

Fix

, and let

and

be as in Theorem

, we now prove that the map

and

are (locally) Hölder continuous on

.

The following two lemmas is useful in the proof of the main result. See
[
4
]
for the original versions of the finite dimensional case, we also refer the reader to Lemmas 5.3.4 and 5.3.5 in
[

]
or Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 in
[

]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
is
\nu
-Hölder continuous with Hölder constant
a_{1}
and

f:X\rightarrow X
is bi-Lipschitz with constant
L\geq 1
, then there exists a constant
a>a_{1}
such that

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\mathcal{A}(y,n)||\leq a^{|n|}d(x,y)^{\nu}

(3.6)

for every
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Lemma 2.2 in
[
1
]
and argue by induction on
n
.

For
k=1
, by the Hölder continuity of the map
A:X\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{B})
and (
2.1
) we have

||A(x)-A(y)||\leq a_{1}d(x,y)^{\nu}.

Assume that there exists
a>a_{1}
so that (
3.6
) hold for
k=1,2,\cdots,n
. For
k=n+1
, we have that

\displaystyle||

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x,n+1)-\mathcal{A}(y,n+1)||

\displaystyle\leq||A(f^{n}(x))||\cdot||\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\mathcal{A}(y,n)||+||\mathcal{A}(y,n)||\cdot||A(f^{n}(x))-A(f^{n}(y))||

\displaystyle\leq(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x||)a^{n}d(x,y)^{\nu}+(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||)^{n}a_{1}d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq[(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x||)a^{n}+(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||)^{n}a_{1}L^{n\nu}]d(x,y)^{\nu}

where we use the fact that
f
is Lipschitz in the last inequality.
To find the number
a
, all we need is that

a^{n+1}>(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x||)a^{n}+(\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||)^{n}a_{1}L^{n\nu},

that is

a\geq a_{1}(\dfrac{\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||\cdot L^{\nu}}{a})^{n}+\sup_{x\in M}||A(x)||.

This can be easily achieved by taking a sufficiently large
a
such that

a>\max\{a_{1},\sup_{x\in M}||A(x)||\cdot L^{\nu}\}.

The case for
n<0
can be proven in a similar fashion, this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n\geq 1},\,\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be two sequences of operators in
GL(\mathcal{B})
, such that, for some
0<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{1}
and
\ell\geq 1
, there exist closed subspaces

and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
of
\mathcal{B}
satisfying
\mathcal{B}_{0}=E\oplus{E}^{\prime}=F\oplus{F}^{\prime}
such that for some fixed
n

(i)

||A_{n}u||\leq\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}||u||
and
||A_{n}v||\geq\ell^{-1}\alpha_{1}^{n}||v||
for every

,

;

(ii)

||B_{n}u||\leq\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}||u||
and
||B_{n}v||\geq\ell^{-1}\alpha_{1}^{n}||v||
for every

,

\max\{||v||,||w||\}\leq\ell||u||

.

Then for every

satisfying

(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a})^{n+1}\leq\delta<(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a})^{n}\ \text{and}\ ||A_{n}-B_{n}||\leq\delta a^{n},

we have that

\hat{d}(E,F)\leq(4+2\ell)\ell^{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\log(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})/\log(\alpha_{2}/a)}.

Proof.

With a minor modification of the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
, we give the proof of the result in the following.

Let us define the cone
Q=\{u\in\mathcal{B}_{0}:||A_{n}u||\leq 2\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}||u||\}
. For each
v\in F
, one has

\displaystyle||A_{n}v||

\displaystyle\leq||A_{n}-B_{n}||\cdot||v||+||B_{n}v||

\displaystyle\leq(\delta a^{n}+\ell\alpha_{2}^{n})||v||

\displaystyle\leq 2\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}||v||.

In consequence,

and this implies that
F\subset Q
.

For each

, write

where
v_{1}\in E
and
v_{2}\in{E}^{\prime}
. Recall that
\ell||v||\geq\max\{||v_{1}||,||v_{2}||\}
, one has

\displaystyle 2\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}||v||

\displaystyle\geq||A_{n}(v_{1}+v_{2})||

\displaystyle\geq||A_{n}v_{2}||-||A_{n}v_{1}||

\displaystyle\geq\ell^{-1}\alpha_{1}^{n}||v_{2}||-\ell\alpha_{2}^{n}\cdot\ell||v||.

This together with the fact that
(\alpha_{2}/a)^{n+1}<\delta<(\alpha_{2}/a)^{n}
imply that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(v,E)\leq||v_{2}||\leq(2+\ell)\ell^{2}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}\Big)^{n}||v||\leq(2+\ell)\ell^{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\log(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})/\log(\alpha_{2}/a)}||v||.

Since

, by the definition of
\delta(\cdot,\cdot)
one has

\delta(F,E)\leq(2+\ell)\ell^{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\log(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})/\log(\alpha_{2}/a)}.

Symmetrically, one can show that
\delta(E,F)\leq(2+\ell)\ell^{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\log(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})/\log(\alpha_{2}/a)}
. Hence, using
(
2.2
) we conclude that

\hat{d}(E,F)\leq(4+2\ell)\ell^{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\log(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})/\log(\alpha_{2}/a)}.

∎

Now, we estimate
\hat{d}(E_{i}^{-}(x),E_{i}^{-}(y))
and
\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))
for each

with

, where

is the same as in Theorem

.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each

Given

3.3.
Hölder continuity of the map
x\mapsto E_{i}(x)

In this section, we will give the proof of the main result of this paper.

For every
\gamma>0
, fix
\Lambda_{\ell}
so that
\mu(\Lambda_{\ell})>1-\gamma
.
Take a point
x\in\Lambda_{\ell}
, then
\mathcal{B}=E_{i}^{-}(x)\oplus E_{i}^{+}(x)
, here
E_{i}^{-}(x),E_{i}^{+}(x)
are the same as in Theorem
3.1
. By (
3.8
), choose a small number
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq 1/\ell

for each
x,y\in\Lambda_{\ell}
with
d(x,y)<\delta_{1}
.
By the definition of
\Lambda_{\ell}
and (
3.5
), the norm of the projection operator
\pi_{i}^{+}(x):E_{i}^{+}(x)\oplus E_{i}^{-}(x)\rightarrow E_{i}^{+}(x)
is no larger than
\ell
, thus

\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq 1/\ell<||\pi_{i}^{+}(x)||^{-1}.

By Lemma
2.1
, this yields that
\mathcal{B}=E_{i}^{+}(y)\oplus E_{i}^{-}(x)
. So, there exists a
linear operator
L_{x,y}:E_{i}^{+}(x)\rightarrow E_{i}^{-}(x)
such that the graph of
L_{x,y}

is equivalent to the subspace
E_{i}^{+}(y)
, that is,

E_{i}^{+}(y)=\{u+L_{x,y}(u):u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)\}.

Lemma 3.7
.

For each
x,y\in\Lambda_{\ell}
with
d(x,y)<\delta_{1}
, we have

\dfrac{||L_{x,y}||}{\ell(1+||L_{x,y}||)}\leq\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq 2\ell||L_{x,y}||.

(3.9)

Proof.

For simplicity of presentation, denote
L_{x,y}
by
L
. First, for each
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
, the fact that
u+Lu\in E_{i}^{+}(y)
implies

\operatorname{dist}(u,E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq||u-(u+Lu)||\leq||L||\cdot||u||.

Then, we have that
\delta(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq||L||
.
Next, given
v\in E_{i}^{+}(y)
, there exist
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
such that
v=u+Lu
. Since
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x),Lu\in E_{i}^{-}(x)
, it follows from (2) of Theorem
3.1
that
||u||\leq\ell||v||
. Thus, one has

\operatorname{dist}(v,E_{i}^{+}(x))\leq||v-u||\leq||L||\cdot||u||\leq\ell||L||\cdot||v||.

Therefore,
\delta(E_{i}^{+}(y),E_{i}^{+}(x))\leq\ell\cdot||L||
. This together with (
2.2
) yield that

\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(y),E_{i}^{+}(x))\leq 2\hat{\delta}(E_{i}^{+}(y),E_{i}^{+}(x))\leq 2\ell||L||.

To prove the other inequality in (
3.9
), note that for each
\beta>\delta(E_{i}^{+}(y),E_{i}^{+}(x))
and for every
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)\setminus\{0\}
, one has

\operatorname{dist}(u+Lu,E_{i}^{+}(x))<\beta\cdot||u+Lu||.

Fix such a
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
{u}^{\prime}\in E_{i}^{+}(x)

such that
||u+Lu-{u}^{\prime}||<\beta\cdot||u+Lu||
. Since
u-{u}^{\prime}\in E_{i}^{+}(x)
,
Lu\in E_{i}^{-}(x)
and

K(x)\leq\ell
, by (2) of Theorem
3.1
we have that

||Lu||<\ell||u-{u}^{\prime}+Lu||<\ell\beta\cdot||u+Lu||<\ell\beta(1+||L||)\cdot||u||.

Thus, we conclude that

\beta>\dfrac{||Lu||}{\ell(1+||L||)\cdot||u||}

for every
u\in E_{i}^{+}(x)\setminus\{0\}
. This implies that

\beta\geq\frac{||L||}{\ell(1+||L||)}.

By the arbitrariness of
\beta
and (
2.2
), one has

\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\geq\delta(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\geq\frac{||L||}{\ell(1+||L||)}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we shall prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

By Lemma

, we have that

||L_{x,y}||\rightarrow 0\ \text{whenever}\ d(x,y)\rightarrow 0.

Hence, there exists
\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{1})
with

||\Phi_{x,y}||\leq 1+||L_{x,y}||
. Since
||L_{x,y}||<1/2
, we obtain that

\Phi_{x,y}^{-1}=Id+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-L_{x,y})^{k}
. We write it as
\Phi_{x,y}^{-1}=Id+\hat{L}_{x,y}
, where
\hat{L}_{x,y}:E_{i}^{+}(y)\rightarrow E_{i}^{-}(x)
and one can show that
||\hat{L}_{x,y}||\leq||L_{x,y}||(1-||L_{x,y}||)^{-1}
.

For simplicity of notations, denote
L_{x,y},\hat{L}_{x,y}
and
\Phi_{x,y}^{\pm}

and

\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),E_{i}(y))\leq\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y))+\hat{d}(\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y),E_{i}(y)).

(3.10)

\hat{d}(\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y),E_{i}(y))

\hat{\delta}(\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y),E_{i}(y))\leq 2||L||

) and Lemma

\hat{d}(\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y),E_{i}(y))\leq 4||L||\leq 4\ell(1+||L||)\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq 6\ell\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))

.

(3.11)

Next we estimate
\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y))

\displaystyle\begin{split}||B_{n}u||=||\mathcal{A}(y,n)\Phi u||&\leq\ell e^{(\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon)n}\cdot||\Phi u||\\

&\leq 2\ell e^{(\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon)n}\cdot||u||\end{split}

(3.12)

where we use the fact that

in the last inequality.

and each

, by (1) of Theorem

\displaystyle\begin{split}||B_{n}u||&=||\mathcal{A}(y,n)\Phi u||\\
&\geq\ell^{-1}e^{(\lambda_{i-1}-\varepsilon)n}\cdot||\Phi u||\\

where the third inequality uses the fact that

For every

and, by (2) of Theorem

\ell||\Phi u+\Phi v||\geq\max\{||\Phi u||,||\Phi v||\}.

This yields that

\ell||\Phi||\cdot||u+v||\geq||\Phi^{-1}||^{-1}\cdot\max\{||u||,||v||\}.

Since

, we have that
||\Phi||\cdot||\Phi^{-1}||\leq(1+||L||)(1-||L||)^{-1}\leq 3
. Therefore, one has that

\displaystyle\max\{||u||,||v||\}\leq 3\ell||u+v||.

(3.14)

Consider
\alpha_{1}=e^{\lambda_{i-1}-\varepsilon},\alpha_{2}=e^{\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon}
and replace

by

. Clearly, the sequence

satisfies condition (i) of Lemma

), (

.

At last, consider a constant
a>\sup_{x\in X}||A(x)||
as in Lemma
3.3
, one can show that

\displaystyle||A_{n}-B_{n}||

\displaystyle\leq||\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\mathcal{A}(x,n)\Phi||+||(\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\mathcal{A}(y,n))\Phi||

\displaystyle\leq||\mathcal{A}(x,n)||\cdot||L||+||\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\mathcal{A}(y,n)||\cdot(1+||L||)

\displaystyle\leq a^{n}||L||+2a^{n}d(x,y)^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq a^{n}(||L||+2d(x,y)^{\nu}).

Let
\delta=||L||+2d(x,y)^{\nu}<1
, since
a>\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
there exist
\hat{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a})^{\hat{n}+1}\leq\delta<(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a})^{\hat{n}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.4
that

\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y))\leq\hat{C}_{i}(||L||+2d(x,y)^{\nu})^{\hat{\nu}_{i}}

(3.15)

where
\hat{C}_{i}=(4+6\ell)(3\ell)^{2}e^{\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}-2\varepsilon}
and
\hat{\nu}_{i}=(\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}-2\varepsilon)/(\log a-\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon)<1
. By Lemma
3.7
and (
3.8
), there exist constants
C_{i}^{+}>0
and
\nu_{i}^{+}\in(0,\nu)
so that

||L||\leq\ell(1+||L||)\hat{d}(E_{i}^{+}(x),E_{i}^{+}(y))\leq 2\ell C_{i}^{+}d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}^{+}}.

Since
\nu_{i}^{+}<\nu
implies
d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}^{+}}\geq d(x,y)^{\nu}
, this together with (
3.15
) one has

\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),\Phi^{-1}E_{i}(y))\leq 2\hat{C}_{i}(\ell C_{i}^{+}+1)d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}^{+}\hat{\nu}_{i}}.

(3.16)

Combining (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and (
3.16
), we have that

\displaystyle\hat{d}(E_{i}(x),E_{i}(y))

\displaystyle\leq 6\ell C_{i}^{+}d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}^{+}}+2\hat{C}_{i}(\ell C_{i}^{+}+1)d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}^{+}\hat{\nu}_{i}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}}

where
\nu_{i}=\nu_{i}^{+}\hat{\nu}_{i}
and
C_{i}=6\ell C_{i}^{+}+2\hat{C}_{i}(\ell C_{i}^{+}+1)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The non-invertible case

In this section, assume that
f
is a non-invertible Lipschitz map on a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and
\mu
is an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure on
X
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a separable Banach space, and let
L(\mathcal{B})
denote the space of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{B}
. Let
A:X\rightarrow L(\mathcal{B})
be a
\nu
-Hölder continuous operator valued function, i.e., there exist constants
C
and
\nu
such that
||A(x)-A(y)||\leq Cd(x,y)^{\nu}
. In addition, suppose that
\alpha(A,\mu)<\lambda(A,\mu)
, i.e.,
A
is quasi-compact (see the definitions in Section
2.1
).

The one-sided Oseledets theorem was obtained by Doan
[
5
, Theorem 7.1.7]
that we will recall in below, see
[
12
]
for the two-sided case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and let
\mu
be an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure. Given a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}
over
f
generated by a quasi-compact and strongly measurable operator valued function
A:X\rightarrow L(\mathcal{B})
. Then there exists a
f
-invariant subset
X_{0}\subset X
of full
\mu
-measure such that there exist
k
(
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\infty
) numbers
\lambda_{1}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}>\alpha(A,\mu)
, for every
x\in X_{0}
there is a filtration

\mathcal{B}=V_{1}(x)\supset V_{2}(x)\supset\cdots\supset V_{k+1}(x)

with the following properties:

(a)

if
k<\infty
, let
\lambda_{k+1}=\alpha(A,\mu)
, for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
,
V_{i}(x)
is a closed, finite-codimensional subspace and co-
\dim V_{i}(x)=m_{i}
is a finite constant. Moreover,
A(x)V_{i}(x)\subset V_{i}(f(x))
, and for every
u\in V_{i}(x)\setminus V_{i+1}(x)
we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||=\lambda_{i}\ \text{and}\ \limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)|_{V_{i+1}(x)}||\leq\lambda_{i+1};

(b)

if
k=\infty
then
\displaystyle{\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{i}=\alpha(A,\mu)}
, and
V_{i}(x)
is a closed, finite-codimensional and co-
\dim V_{i}(x)=m_{i}
is a finite constant for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
A(x)V_{i}(x)\subset V_{i}(f(x))
, and for every
u\in V_{i}(x)\setminus V_{i+1}(x)
we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||=\lambda_{i}\ \text{and}\ \limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)|_{V_{i+1}(x)}||\leq\lambda_{i+1}.

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that the continuity of
A:X\to L(\mathcal{B})
implies that
A
is strongly measurable, and the map
x\mapsto V_{i}(x)
is measurable (see
[
10
, Remark 2.9]
).

In the following, we will show that the map
x\mapsto V_{i}(x)
is (locally) Hölder continuous on a compact set of arbitrarily large measure provided that
f
is Lipschitz and
A:X\rightarrow L(\mathcal{B})
is Höder continuous.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f
be a Lipschitz map on a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and let
\mu
be an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure. Given a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}
over
f
generated by a quasi-compact and
\nu
-Höder continuous operator valued function
A:X\rightarrow L(\mathcal{B})
, where
(\mathcal{B},||\cdot||)
is a separable Banach space. Then, for every
\gamma>0
and every subspace
V_{i}(x)
as in Theorem
4.1
, there exists a compact subset
\Lambda_{\gamma}\subset X
with
\mu(\Lambda_{\gamma})>1-\gamma
so that the map
x\mapsto V_{i}(x)
is (locally) Hölder continuous on
\Lambda_{\gamma}
.

If
k
is a finite positive integer, we will show that the previous theorem for every
i=1,2,\cdots,k+1
. If
k
is infinite, we will prove the same result for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the following, we only prove the first case, the latter can be proven in a similar fashion.

The following lemma proved in
[
10
, Lemma 2.11]
allows us to choose a “good” complementary space for every space
V_{i}(x)
. In the setting of the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the Hilbert space, one may usually choose the orthogonal complement
V_{i}(x)^{\perp}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the filtration
\mathcal{B}=V_{1}(x)\supset\cdots\supset V_{k+1}(x)
be as in Theorem
4.1
. Then, for every
1\leq i\leq k
, there exists a finite-dimensional subspaces
\widetilde{U_{i}}(x)
such that

(1)

for
\mu
-almost every
x
,
V_{i+1}(x)\oplus\widetilde{U_{i}}(x)=V_{i}(x)
and the map
x\mapsto\widetilde{U_{i}}(x)
is measurable;

(2)

let
U_{i}(x)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{i-1}\widetilde{U_{k}}(x)
(where
\widetilde{U_{0}}(x)=\{0\}
), and let
\pi_{i}^{u}(x)
and
\pi_{i}^{v}(x)
denote the projections of
\mathcal{B}
onto
U_{i}(x)
and
V_{i}(x)
via the splitting
V_{i}(x)\oplus U_{i}(x)
respectively, then

||\pi_{i}^{v}(\cdot)||,||\pi_{i}^{u}(\cdot)||\in L^{\infty}(\mu).

Since
V_{1}(x)=\mathcal{B}
, the statement in Theorem
4.2
clearly holds. In the following, fix
i\in\{2\cdots,k+1\}
.
By (2) of Lemma
4.1
, on can choose
\ell>0
, such that

\max\{||\pi_{i}^{u}(x)||,||\pi_{i}^{v}(x)||\}\leq\ell

for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
.
Reducing
X_{0}
by a zero measure set such that
X_{0}
remains
f
-invariant and for every
x\in X_{0}
the above inequality holds for every
x\in X_{0}
. Then, for every
x\in X_{0}
, one has

\max\{||u||,||v||\}\leq\ell||u+v||

(4.1)

for every
u\in U_{i}(x)
and every
v\in V_{i}(x)
. By Theorem
4.1
, for every
x\in X_{0}
and every
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\geq\lambda_{i-1}.

(4.2)

Fix
x\in X_{0}
, let
B(x)=\pi^{u}_{i}(f(x))\circ A(x)\circ\pi^{u}_{i}(x)
,
C(x)=\pi^{v}_{i}(f(x))\circ A(x)\circ\pi^{u}_{i}(x)
and
D(x)=\pi^{v}_{i}(f(x))\circ A(x)\circ\pi^{v}_{i}(x)
. Since
\pi^{u}_{i}(f(x))\circ A(x)\circ\pi^{v}_{i}(x)=0
by the invariance of
V_{i}(x)
, we have that

A(x)=B(x)+C(x)+D(x).

Similarly, define
B_{n}(x)
,
C_{n}(x)
and
D_{n}(x)
as above, such that
\mathcal{A}(x,n)=B_{n}(x)+C_{n}(x)+D_{n}(x)
. As in
[
10
, Lemma 2.12]
( see also
[
20
, Sect. 4.2.5]
), One can show that

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)=B(f^{n-1}(x))\circ\cdots\circ B(x),\,D_{n}(x)=D(f^{n-1}(x))\circ\cdots\circ D(x)

and

C_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}D_{n-j-1}(f^{j+1}(x))C(f^{j}(x))B_{j}(x).

Note that
B(x)|_{U_{i}(x)}:U_{i}(x)\rightarrow U_{i}(f(x))
,
D(x)|_{V_{i}(x)}=A(x)|_{V_{i}(x)}
and
C(x)|_{U_{i}(x)}:U_{i}(x)\rightarrow V_{i}(f(x))
. For every
u\in U_{i}(x)
, by (
4.1
) we have that

\max\{||B(x)u||,||C(x)u||\}\leq\ell||B(x)u+C(x)u||=\ell||A(x)u||.

This implies that

\max\{||C(x)||,||B(x)||\}\leq\ell||A(x)||.

Moreover, if there exists
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
such that
B(x)u=0
, then

\mathcal{A}(x,n)u=\mathcal{A}(fx,n-1)A(x)u=\mathcal{A}(fx,n-1)C(x)u

for every
n>1
.
Since
C(x)u\in V_{i}(f(x))
, one has

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\leq\lambda_{i}.

This yields a contradiction with (
4.2
). Hence, one has
B(x)u\neq 0
for each
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore,
B(x)
is an isomorphism from
U_{i}(x)
to
U_{i}(fx)
. Note that
\dim U_{i}(x)=\dim U_{i}(fx)<\infty
, restricted on
\{U_{i}(x)\}
,
\{B_{n}(x)\}
is a cocycle on
X
with respect to
f
, applying the multiplicative ergodic theorem of the finite dimensional case (e.g., see
[
20
]
), one can obtain that for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X

the following limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||B_{n}(x)u||

exist for every
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover, by
[
10
, Sub-lemma 2.13]
or
[
20
, Proposition 4.14]
, we have that for every
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||B_{n}(x)u||=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\geq\lambda_{i-1}.

This implies that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||(B_{n}(x)|_{U_{i}(x)})^{-1}||^{-1}\geq\lambda_{i-1},\,\,\mu\text{-almost every }x.

(4.3)

Without loss of generality, assume that (
4.3
) holds for every
x\in X_{0}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
x\in X_{0}
and a small number
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
m>0
such that for each
v\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
with
||v||=1
, we have
||\mathcal{A}(x,n)v||\geq e^{n(\lambda_{i-1}-\varepsilon)}||v||
for each
n\geq m
.

Proof.

For every
u\in U_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
, write
\mathcal{A}(x,n)u=B_{n}(x)u+C_{n}(x)u
. Note that
B_{n}(x)u\in U_{i}(f^{n}x)
and
C_{n}(x)u\in V_{i}(f^{n}x)
, it follows from (
4.1
) that

||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\geq\ell^{-1}||B_{n}(x)u||\geq\ell^{-1}||(B_{n}(x)|_{U_{i}(x)})^{-1}||^{-1}||u||.

Therefore, we have that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U_{i}(x)\\
||u||=1\end{subarray}}\log||\mathcal{A}(x,n)u||\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||(B_{n}(x)|_{U_{i}(x)})^{-1}||^{-1}\geq\lambda_{i-1}.

This implies the desired result immediately.
∎

Next, we will show that the map
x\mapsto V_{i}(x)
is (locally) Hölder continuous on a compact subset of arbitrarily large measure.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Fix
i\in\{2,\cdots,k+1\}
and a sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle A^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}=\{x\in X_{0}:||\mathcal{A}(x,m)u||\leq e^{m(\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon)}||u||,\quad\forall u\in V_{i}(x),\ \forall m\geq n\}

and

\displaystyle B^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}=\{x\in X_{0}:||\mathcal{A}(x,m)v||\geq e^{m(\lambda_{i-1}-\varepsilon)}||v||,\quad\forall v\in U_{i}(x),\ \forall m\geq n\}.

Clearly, the sequences of sets
\{A^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}\}
and
\{B^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}\}
are nested, and by Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
we have that

\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}\Big)=\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}B^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}\Big)=1.

Therefore, for every
\gamma>0
there exists
n_{0}
such that
\mu(A^{i}_{n,\varepsilon}\cap B^{i}_{n,\varepsilon})>1-\gamma
for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

Let
\Lambda_{\gamma}=A^{i}_{n_{0},\varepsilon}\cap B^{i}_{n_{0},\varepsilon}
. We may assume further that
\Lambda_{\gamma}
is compact since otherwise we can
approximate it from within by a compact subset. For every
x,y\in\Lambda_{\gamma}
with
d(x,y)^{\nu}<(\frac{e^{\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon}}{a})^{n_{0}}<1
, where
a
is a sufficiently large constant as in Lemma
3.3
. Note that Lemma
3.3
is also valid for
n>0
in this case. Applying Lemma
3.4
with
A_{n}=\mathcal{A}(x,n),B_{n}=\mathcal{A}(y,n),\alpha_{2}=e^{\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon},\alpha_{1}=e^{\lambda_{i-1}-\varepsilon},E=V_{i}(x),F=V_{i}(y),{E}^{\prime}=U_{i}(x)
and
{F}^{\prime}=U_{i}(y)
. By the construction of the set
\Lambda_{\gamma}
, for every
n\geq n_{0}
, the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) of Lemma
3.4
hold. Set
\delta=d(x,y)^{\nu}
, there exists a number
{n}^{\prime}\geq n_{0}
such that

(\frac{e^{\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon}}{a})^{{n}^{\prime}+1}\leq\delta<(\frac{e^{\lambda_{i}+\varepsilon}}{a})^{{n}^{\prime}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.4
that

\hat{d}(V_{i}(x),V_{i}(y))\leq C_{i}d(x,y)^{\nu_{i}}

where
C_{i}=(4+2\ell)\ell^{2}e^{\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}-2\varepsilon}
and
\nu_{i}^{-}=\nu(\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}-2\varepsilon)/(\log a-\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon)<\nu<1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Convergence rates for identification of Robin coefficient from terminal observations
Subhankar Mondal

Abstract
. This paper deals with the problem of identification of a Robin coefficient (also known as impedance coefficient) in a parabolic PDE from terminal observations of the temperature distributions. The problem is ill-posed in the sense that small perturbation in the observation may lead to a large deviation in the solution. Thus, in order to obtain stable approximations, we employ the Tikhonov-regularization. We propose a weak source condition motivated by the work of Engl and Zou (2000) and obtain a convergence rate of O ⁡ ( δ 1 2 ) O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}) , the main goal of this paper, where δ \delta is the noise level of the observed data. The obtained rate is better than some of the previous known rates. Moreover, the advantage of the proposed source condition is that we are getting the above mentioned convergence rate without the need for characterizing the range space of modelling operator, which is in contrast to the general convergence theory of Tikhonov-regularization for non linear operators, where one obtain the same order of convergence by characterizing the range of the adjoint of the Fréchet derivative of modelling operator, a challenging task for many problems.



Keywords:
parameter identification, Robin coefficient, ill-posed, regularization, source condition

1.
Introduction

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Let
\tau>0
be fixed and we denote the sets
\Omega\times[0,\tau]
and
\partial\Omega\times[0,\tau]
by
\Omega_{\tau}
and
\partial\Omega_{\tau}
, respectively.
We consider the PDE

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=f\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\tau},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\gamma(x)u=g\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{\tau},\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega)),\,\,g\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega)),\,\,\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).
Here, for a Banach space
Y
, we used the notation
L^{2}(0,\tau;Y)
for the space of all
Y
-valued measurable functions
\phi
on
[0,\tau]
such that
\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\phi(t)\|_{Y}^{2}dt<\infty
. The Robin coefficient
\gamma
is the impedance coefficient that represents the heat exchange on the boundary
\partial\Omega,\,\,\nu
denotes the outward unit normal of the boundary
\partial\Omega.
The direct or forward problem for (
1.1
) is to find
u(x,t)
satisfying (
1.1
)(possibly in weak sense) for the known impedance coefficient
\gamma
and the input data
f,g
and
u_{0}
, and the existence of solution of the forward problem is well known.

The system (
1.1
) models heat conduction phenomenon where the impedance coefficient characterizes the thermal properties of the conductive material on the interface and certain physical processes, e.g. corrosion, on the boundary
[
2
,
20
]
. Thus, the value of the impedance coefficient
\gamma
is of significant interest in thermal imaging such as safety analysis of nuclear reactor and thermal protection of space shuttles
[
3
]
. In practice, the impedance coefficient
\gamma
cannot be specified from direct measurements since the domain
\Omega
may be embedded in an unknown region
[
30
]
. Therefore, one has to deal with the inverse problem of identifying the impedance coefficient from some available observations, for example, a final time observation on the whole spatial domain
[
30
]
, partial Dirichlet boundary observation for the full time period
[
2
,
19
]
, time integral observation on the full spatial boundary
[
14
,
23
]
, final or an intermediate time observation on the full spatial boundary
[
14
]
. Considering the amount of work that has been devoted for this type of parameter identification problem and various type of observations over the years, it is impossible to list all of them, however, the interested reader may refer to
[
9
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
31
,
32
]
.

In the applications of heat transport in the high temperature, it is not possible to measure the temperature distribution in the whole time interval
[0,\tau].
Therefore, following
[
12
,
5
]
(see also the recent work
[
6
]
), in this paper, we assume that the terminal status observations of the temperature distribution is known and with this knowledge we consider the inverse problem of identifying the spatially dependent impedance coefficient
\gamma
. More precisely, we assume that

(1.2)

u(x,t)=\phi(x,t)\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega\times[\tau-\sigma,\tau],

is known at hand, where
\sigma>0
is small so that
\tau-\sigma>0,
and then we consider the problem of identifying
\gamma
from the knowledge of
\phi
or its noisy approximations
\phi_{\delta}
satisfying

(1.3)

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\phi-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\leq\delta^{2}

for some noise level
\delta>0.

It can be observed that our inverse problem is non linear. Furthermore, as can be seen from next section, the inverse problem is also ill-posed, that is, a small perturbation in the observed data may lead to a large deviation in the corresponding solutions. Thus, some regularization scheme has to be employed in order to obtain stable approximations. We employ the standard Tikhonov regularization for obtaining the approximations for
\gamma
. It is well known that the convergence of the stable approximations obtained by regularization can be arbitrary slow
[
26
]
unless some apriori conditions, the so-called source conditions (cf.
[
10
,
24
]
) is assumed on the unknown that has to be identified. In Tikhonov regularization theory for non linear operators in Hilbert spaces, generally the source condition involves the adjoint of the Fréchet derivative of the non linear operator involved
[
10
]
. More precisely, if
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are Hilbert spaces,
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a (non linear) operator which is Fréchet differentiable, consider the problem of solving an ill-posed operator equation

F(x)=y

in the sense that small perturbation in
y
may lead to a large deviation in the solution of the operator equation. Let
y
be the exact data and
x^{\dagger}
be the unique solution (i.e.
F(x^{\dagger})=y
) to be identified from the knowledge of
y^{\text{obs}}\in\mathcal{Y}
, the observed data satisfying
\|y-y^{\text{obs}}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\delta.
The stable approximations are the minimizers of the functional

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\|F(x)-y^{\text{obs}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\alpha\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}},

for a fixed
\alpha>0
, the regularization parameter, and
x^{*}
is an initial guess for
x^{\dagger}
that incorporates some apriori smoothness assumptions. Let
F^{\prime}
denotes the Fréchet derivative of
F
. Moreover, we assume that the Fréchet derivative of
F
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
C_{\rm Lip}
. Then it is known that(cf.
[
10
,
11
,
25
]
) if

x^{\dagger}-x^{*}=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}\varphi

for some
\varphi
satisfying the smallness condition

(1.4)

C_{\rm Lip}\|\varphi\|\leq 1

then the rate of convergence of the regularized solution is
O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
, provided the regularization parameter
\alpha
is chosen as
\alpha\sim\delta
. In many problems it is extremely difficult to characterize these range spaces whereas in many problems these range spaces turns out to be certain Sobolev spaces with higher smoothness, see for e.g.
[
15
]
. In addition to these difficulties, verification of the smallness condition (
1.4
) is another challenging task. In order to overcome these challenges, a new type of source condition was proposed by Engl and Zou in
[
12
]
for a parameter identification problem in heat conduction, which is simple and verifiable (atleast for some reasonable regularity assumptions on
f,\,g
and
u_{0}
), does not require any smallness condition and also does not require much higher smoothness assumption on the unknowns. Motivated by the work in
[
12
]
and also the recent work in
[
6
]
, we consider a similar weak source condition and obtain the convergence rates, which is the main goal of this paper. Also, we show explicitly that our source condition is verifiable under certain regularity assumption(see Theorem
4.4
).

We now discuss the advantages and shortcomings associated with our considered observation (
1.2
) in comparison to some of the recent works where the observations are different from (
1.2
).

In
[
23
]
the authors have considered the problem of reconstruction of
\gamma(x)
associated with a system similar to (
1.1
) with
f=0,\,u_{0}=0
from the non-local measurement of the form

(1.5)

\int_{0}^{\tau}w(t)u(x,t)\,dt=h(x)\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,

for some weight function
w.
Since the governing PDE is a homogeneous heat equation, by using the fundamental solution of the heat equation, the authors could make use of their boundary observation (
1.5
) in the analysis of the inverse problem of reconstructing
\gamma
. In fact, the inverse problem of reconstructing
\gamma
is transformed into a problem of solving a system of ill-posed non linear integral equations, where at first one has to solve for a certain potential
q(x,t)
that arises from the fundamental solution (see
[
23
, pg.4]
) and then solve for the impedance coefficient. Moreover, for the stable reconstruction the authors have considered a semi-Tikhonov regularization scheme in which the penalty term involves the potential
q
(an auxiliary unknown) in contrast to our approach of traditional Tikhonov regularization functional where the penalty term is comprised of
\gamma
, the actual unknown to be identified. Although the observation (
1.5
) seems to be more realistic (as it deals with measurement only in the spatial boundary) than (
1.2
), but their analysis requires
f=0
and
u_{0}=0
, which is not the case in this paper. Moreover, the work in
[
23
]
does not provide any error estimate.

In
[
30
]
the authors have considered the the problem of simultaneous identification of
\gamma(x)
and the initial temperature
u_{0}(x)
associated with a system similar to (
1.1
) from the final time observation on the full spatial domain, that is, the data used for the inversion is of the form

(1.6)

u(x,\tau)=h(x)\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,

and in this work the authors could overcome the restriction of
u_{0}=0
that they have considered in their earlier work
[
23
]
. Because of the ill-posedness of the inverse problem, the authors in
[
30
]
have obtained a stable approximations for
\gamma
by a regularization scheme that involves the mollification of inversion input data
h
, and that is achieved by using higher regularity on
h
, namely
h\in W^{3,p}(\Omega)
for some
p>2.
Since
h
is an inversion input data, assuming such higher regularity of
h
is not that much realistic from application point of view. Moreover, for a noisy observation
h^{\delta}
of
h
the authors have obtained a Hölder rate of convergence
O(\delta^{\nu})
, for some
\nu\leq\frac{1}{5}
(see
[
30
, pg.603]
) under some source condition on
\gamma
and
u_{0}
which may be not feasible in applications, because it is assumed that the unknowns
\gamma
and
u_{0}
are sufficiently regular so that
h\in W^{3,p}(\Omega).
In contrast to these, as mentioned earlier, in this paper we will obtain stable approximations for
\gamma
using Tikhonov regularization and our regularity assumption on the inversion input data
\phi
(see (
1.2
)) is only that
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[\tau-\sigma,\tau]).
Moreover, under a verifiable source condition that only requires
\gamma\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
we obtain a better rate of convergence, namely,
O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Thus, again it is to be noted that although the observation (
1.6
) apparently seems more realistic than (
1.2
), but in terms of source condition and regularity assumption our work is more realistic than
[
30
]
with a better convergence rate.

This paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
we collect all the existing results related to existence and uniqueness of solutions for forward problem, precisely state the inverse problem that we consider and analyze the existence, uniqueness and ill-posedness of the inverse problem. In Section
3
we do the convergence analysis of the regularized approximations, propose the source condition and proof the convergence rate result, the main result of this paper. In Section
4
we discuss about the source condition, its compatibility, regularity and then show that it is indeed verifiable by a simple construction.

2.
The Inverse Problem

In this section we recall all the definitions, results related to the PDE (
1.1
) that will be used later and in addition we formulate the inverse problem more precisely and discuss about its uniqueness and ill-posedness. Throughout the paper whenever we come across a function defined on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, it is to be understood in the sense of trace
[
1
,
13
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Weak solution)

An element
u\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
is said to be a weak solution of (
1.1
) if

\begin{cases}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-u\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta]\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma u\eta\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}g\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\,u_{0}\eta(\cdot,0)\,dx\end{cases}

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.

\lozenge

We now state a result about existence and uniqueness of the forward problem.
Let

\mathcal{A}=\{\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega):0<\underline{\gamma}\leq\gamma\leq\overline{\gamma}\}

be the set of admissible parameters, for some constants
\underline{\gamma}
and
\overline{\gamma}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(cf.
[
29
]
)

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
,
f\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
g\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).
Then there exists a unique weak solution
u
of (
1.1
) satisfying the estimate

(2.1)

\max_{[0,\tau]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)});

where
C
is a constant depending only on
\underline{\gamma},\,\Omega
and
\tau.

Before proceeding further, let us first precisely state the inverse problem that is considered.

(IP)
Identify
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
from the observation
\phi\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
such that the unique weak solution
u
of (
1.1
) satisfies (
1.2
).

We now discuss about the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the inverse problem
(IP)
.
Let
g\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega))
,
\phi\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
be such that
\phi,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega))
and

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\phi\,dt\neq 0\quad\text{a.e. on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

Then from the Robin boundary condition in (
1.1
) and using (
1.2
), we have

(2.2)

\gamma=\frac{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,g\,dt-\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}\,dt}{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,\phi\,dt}.

Thus, the inverse problem to identify
\gamma
from the exact observation
\phi
satisfying (
1.2
) has a unique solution
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
given in (
2.2
).

Next, we observe that the inverse problem
(IP)
is non linear since the temperature distribution
u(x,t)
depends on the impedance coefficient
\gamma
. Also, it is ill-posed in the sense that small perturbation in the observation data
\phi
in (
1.2
) may lead to large deviation in the corresponding solution of the inverse problem. This is the case because the expression (
2.2
) for
\gamma
contains a derivative of the observation
\phi
. Thus, in order to obtain some stable approximations for
\gamma
some regularization method has to be employed. We will consider the Tikhonov regularization for obtaining stable approximations in the next section.

3.
Convergence rates with weak source condition

Let
u(\gamma)
denotes the unique weak solution of (
1.1
) for a fixed
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
. For
\delta>0
, let
\phi_{\delta}
be the noisy data corresponding to the exact data
\phi
satisfying (
1.3
). As discussed in the previous section, the inverse problem is ill-posed, and thus we shall use the Tikhonov-regularization in order to obtain stable approximations. Throughout we shall denote by
\gamma^{\dagger}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
the exact impedance coefficient to be identified for the corresponding exact data
\phi.
For a fixed
\alpha>0
, consider the output-least square Tikhonov functional

(3.1)

J(\gamma):=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma)-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma-\gamma^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where
\gamma^{*}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is an initial guess for
\gamma^{\dagger}
that incorporates some apriori smoothness assumption on
\gamma^{\dagger}.
By
\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}
we denote a minimizer of the optimization problem

(3.2)

\min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}}J(\gamma).

These minimizers are the Tikhonov-regularized solutions. Note that the initial guess
\gamma^{*}
may not belong to the admissible set
\mathcal{A}
. Our next two results are about the existence and stability of such minimizers, which in turn ensures that the minimizers are indeed regularized solutions. It is to be noted that by now the arguments for the proof of existence and stability is well established in the literature (cf.
[
12
]
), but since the context of this paper is different, in order to keep the paper self contained we include the proof also.

Theorem 3.1
.

The minimization problem (
3.2
) has a solution.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{A}
is a convex set. Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence in
\mathcal{A}.
Clearly,
\{\gamma_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega).
Thus, there exists a subsequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}
and a
\gamma^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
such that
\gamma_{m}\rightharpoonup\gamma^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega).
Now the closedness and convexity of
\mathcal{A}
implies that
\mathcal{A}
is weakly closed, thus,
\gamma^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}.
Since
u(\gamma_{m})
is the weak solution of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma_{m},
by the estimate (
2.1
), it follows that
\{u(\gamma_{m})\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega)).
Thus, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u(\gamma_{m})\}
, and
u^{*}\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
such that
u(\gamma_{m})\rightharpoonup u^{*}
in
L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
as
m\to\infty.
Now, for any
\eta\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
, writing

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma_{m}\,u(\gamma_{m})\eta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u(\gamma_{m})\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma_{m}-\gamma^{\alpha})\,u(\gamma_{m})\eta\,dx\,dt,

using the weak convergence of
\gamma_{m}
and the boundedness of
u(\gamma_{m})
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma_{m}\,u(\gamma_{m})\eta\,dx\,dt\to\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u^{*}\eta\,dx\,dt\quad\text{as}\,\,m\to\infty.

Therefore, using the fact that
u(\gamma_{m})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma_{m}
, and the weak convergence of
\{u(\gamma_{m})\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-u^{*}\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}+\nabla u^{*}\cdot\nabla\eta]\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u^{*}\eta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}g\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\eta(\cdot,0)\,dx

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.
Since
u(\gamma^{\alpha})
is the unique weak solution of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma^{\alpha}
, we have
u^{*}=u(\gamma^{\alpha}).

We now consider the identity

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\liminf_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[|u(\gamma_{m})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})|^{2}+|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}+2\big(u(\gamma_{m})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})\big)\big(u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\big)\Big]\,dx\,dt.

Since
u(\gamma_{m})
and
u(\gamma^{\alpha})
are the weak solutions of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma_{m}
and
\gamma^{\alpha}
, respectively, using the estimate (
2.1
), we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})|^{2}\,dx\,dt=0.

Therefore,

\liminf_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Thus, using the weak lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm and using the fact that
\{\gamma_{n}\}
is a minimizing sequence for the minimization problem (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\liminf_{m}\Big\{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{m}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad=\min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}}J(\gamma).

This shows that
\gamma^{\alpha}
is a minimizer of (
3.2
).
∎

We now prove the stability of the minimization problem (
3.2
) with respect to the observation data
\phi_{\delta}.
That is, we establish that the minimizers of
(
3.2
)
are indeed regularized solutions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{\phi_{n}\}
be a sequence such that
\phi_{n}
converges to
\phi_{\delta}
in
L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega)).
For a fixed
\alpha>0
, let
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
be the minimizer of

(3.3)

\min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma)-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Then there exists a subsequence
\{\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\}
that converges to a minimizer
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
is a minimizer of (
3.3
), we have

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma)-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}.
Thus,
\{\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega).
Therefore, there exists a subsequence, with abuse of notation, denoted by
\{\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\}
and a
\gamma^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
such that
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
converges weakly to
\gamma^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
as
n\to\infty.
Now, the closedness and convexity of
\mathcal{A}
implies that
\gamma^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}.

Since
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
is the unique weak solution of (
1.1
), by (
2.1
) it follows that
\{u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega)).
Thus, there exist a subsequence, still denoted by
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
and a
u_{*}\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
such that
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
converges weakly to
u_{*}
in
L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
as
n\to\infty.

Now, for any
\eta\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
, writing

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\,u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\eta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{\alpha})\,u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\eta\,dx\,dt,

using the weak convergence of
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
and the boundedness of
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\,u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\eta\,dx\,dt\to\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u_{*}\eta\,dx\,dt\quad\text{as}\,\,n\to\infty.

Therefore, using the fact that
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
is a weak solution, and the weak convergence of
\{u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-u_{*}\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}+\nabla u_{*}\cdot\nabla\eta]\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}\,u_{*}\eta\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}g\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\eta(\cdot,0)\,dx

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.
Since
u(\gamma^{\alpha})
is the unique weak solution of (
1.1
), we have
u_{*}=u(\gamma^{\alpha}).

Now the weak lower semi continuity of
L^{2}
-norm implies

\|\gamma^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{n}\,\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

and

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Since
\phi_{n}\to\phi_{\delta}
in
L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega)),
it follows that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}+|\phi_{n}-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}+2\big(u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\big)(\phi_{\delta}-\phi_{n})\Big]\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})|^{2}+|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}+2\big(u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})\big)\big(u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\big)\Big]\,dx\,dt.

Since
u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})
and
u(\gamma^{\alpha})
are the weak solutions of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
and
\gamma^{\alpha}
, respectively, using the estimate (
2.1
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\alpha})|^{2}\,dx\,dt=0.

Therefore,

\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\liminf_{n}\Big\{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\limsup_{n}\Big\{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\limsup_{n}\Big\{\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma)-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma)-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}.
This shows that
\gamma^{\alpha}
is a minimizer, and we denote this by
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}.
Also, taking
\gamma=\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
in the last equality, we have

(3.4)

\begin{cases}&\lim_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\\
&\quad=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\end{cases}

We now establish the convergence of
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
to
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
as
n\to\infty.
The proof is by contradiction. Suppose that
\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}
does not converge to
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega).
Then, clearly

\|\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}<\limsup_{n}\|\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}=:\epsilon.

Thus, there exists a subsequence of
\{\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\}
, say
\{\gamma_{m}^{\alpha}\}
such that
\gamma_{m}^{\alpha}
converges weakly to
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and

\|\gamma_{m}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\to\epsilon\quad\text{as}\,\,m\to\infty.

Now from (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m}^{\alpha})-\phi_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\Big(\|\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-\lim_{m}\|\gamma_{m}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\underbrace{\Big(\|\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-\epsilon\Big)}_{<0}.

Hence,

\lim_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m}^{\alpha})-\phi_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dt<\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{n}^{\alpha})-\phi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma_{m}^{\alpha})-\phi_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

a contradiction.
∎

Let
f\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega)),g\in L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then by Theorem
2.2
, we know that for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
, (
1.1
) has a unique weak solution in
L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
and we have used the notation
u(\gamma)
to denote this weak solution. For notational simplicity we denote this by a map
F
, that is,
F:\mathcal{A}\to L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
is defined as
\gamma\mapsto F(\gamma):=u(\gamma).

In order to avoid notational complexity, throughout
C
will denote a generic constant that may depend only on
\Omega,\tau,\sigma,\underline{\gamma},\overline{\gamma},u_{0},f,g,
the operator norm of the trace operator (and also on some function
\psi
considered later in Theorem
3.4
).

LEMMA 3.3
.

The mapping
F:\mathcal{A}\subset L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
is Fréchet differentiable.

Proof.

Since
u(\gamma)
denotes the unique weak solution of (
1.1
), we have

(3.5)

\begin{cases}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-u(\gamma)\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}+\nabla u(\gamma)\cdot\nabla\eta]\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma u(\gamma)\eta\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}g\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\,u_{0}\eta(\cdot,0)\,dx\end{cases}

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.

Let
h\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
be such that
\gamma+h\in\mathcal{A}.

Then, we have

(3.6)

\begin{cases}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-u(\gamma+h)\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}+\nabla u(\gamma+h)\cdot\nabla\eta]\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma u(\gamma+h)\eta\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}g\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\,u_{0}\eta(\cdot,0)\,dx\end{cases}

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.
Thus, from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

(3.7)

\begin{cases}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}-[u(\gamma+h)-u(\gamma)]\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\nabla u(\gamma+h)-\nabla u(\gamma)]\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma+h)[u(\gamma+h)-u(\gamma)]\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\,hu(\gamma)\eta\,dx\,dt=0\end{cases}

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0.

Now consider the PDE

(3.8)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\tau},\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\gamma v=-hu(\gamma)\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{\tau},\\
v(\cdot,0)=0\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{cases}

By Theorem
2.2
there exist a unique weak solution
v
of (
3.8
) satisfying

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}v\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma v\eta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}hu(\gamma)\eta\,dx\,dt

for all
\eta\in h^{1}(0;\tau,H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0
in
\Omega.
Moreover, from (
3.8
) and the estimate (
2.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|hu(\gamma)\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\|u(\gamma)\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\|u(\gamma)\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

Therefore, for a fixed
\gamma\in\mathcal{A},
the map
h\mapsto v,
where
v
is the unique weak solution of (
3.8
), is a bounded linear operator from
L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega)).

Let
w:=u(\gamma+h)-u(\gamma)-v
, then from (
3.9
) and (
3.7
), we have

(3.10)

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}-w\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma+h)w\eta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}hv\eta\,dx\,dt

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\eta(\cdot,\tau)=0
in
\Omega.
Therefore from (
3.10
), the definition of weak solution and the estimate (
2.1
), it follows that there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\underline{\gamma},\,\Omega
and
\tau
such that

(3.11)

\max_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\|hv\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}.

Now using the fact that
u(\gamma)
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), from Theorem
2.2
it follows that

\|u(\gamma)\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Therefore, from (
3.11
) we have

\begin{cases}&\max_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))}\\
&\quad\leq C\|h\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).\end{cases}

Thus, it follows that
F
is Fréchet differentiable, and

F^{\prime}(\gamma)h=v,

where
v
is the unique weak solution of (
3.8
) for a given
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}.

∎

We are now in a position to state and proof our main result about the convergence rate under a weak source condition. Let us recall that
\gamma^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{A}
is the exact Robin coefficient to be identified and
\gamma^{*}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is an apriori initial guess for
\gamma^{\dagger}
, and
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
denotes the unique weak solution of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=\gamma^{\dagger}.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Convergence rate)

Let
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap\,L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
be such that

(3.12)

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dt=\gamma^{\dagger}\,-\gamma^{*}\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

For fixed
\alpha,\delta>0,
let
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
be the minimizer of (
3.1
).
Then

\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\alpha}+\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\alpha,\,\,\delta,\gamma^{\dagger}
and
\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}
is a minimizer of (
3.1
) for a fixed
\alpha,
using (
1.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u({\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}-\gamma^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|u({\gamma^{\dagger}})-\phi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\alpha\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta^{2}+\alpha\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u({\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta^{2}+\alpha\big[\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\|^{2}_{l^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-\|\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big]

\displaystyle\quad=\delta^{2}+2\alpha\langle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta},\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

The source condition implies

\alpha\langle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta},\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}=\alpha\langle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}u({\gamma^{\dagger}})\psi\,dt,\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}=\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}u({\gamma^{\dagger}})\psi\,(\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})\,dx\,dt.

Since
F
is Fréchet differentiable, taking
h=\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger},

\eta=\psi,

u(\gamma)=u(\gamma^{\dagger})
and using the identity (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,[F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}-\nabla F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\cdot\nabla\psi]\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\dagger}F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dx\,dt.

Thus,

\displaystyle\alpha\langle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta},\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\Big(\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,\big[-F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\nabla F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\cdot\nabla\psi\big]\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\dagger}F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dx\,dt\Big).

Let
w_{\delta}^{\alpha}:=u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})-F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger}).
Then, we have

(3.13)

\begin{cases}&\alpha\langle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta},\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\\
&\quad=\alpha\Big[\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,\big(-[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\nabla[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\cdot\nabla\psi\big)\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\dagger}[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\psi\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big(w_{\delta}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}-\nabla w^{\alpha}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\big)\,dx\,dt-\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\dagger}w^{\alpha}_{\delta}\psi\,dx\,dt\Big]\end{cases}

Taking
w=w^{\alpha}_{\delta}
, from (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[-w_{\delta}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\nabla w_{\delta}^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\psi]\,dx\,dt+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}w^{\alpha}_{\delta}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})F^{\prime}(\gamma^{\dagger})(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dx\,dt=0.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[w_{\delta}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}-\nabla w_{\delta}^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\psi]\,dx\,dt-\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\dagger}w^{\alpha}_{\delta}\psi\,dx\,dt=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}-\gamma^{\dagger})(u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger}))\psi\,dx\,dt.

Therefore, from (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha\langle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta},\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad=\alpha\Big[\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,\big(-[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\nabla[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\cdot\nabla\psi\big)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}(u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger}))\psi\,dx\,dt\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\alpha\Big[\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,\big(-[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+[u(\gamma^{\dagger})-u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})]\Delta\psi\big)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\psi\,dx\,dt\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

We now estimate the integrals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}.
We will be using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality appropriately which will involve an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0.

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\,-[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\,dx\,dt|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\phi_{\delta}-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\phi_{\delta}-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\delta^{2}\varepsilon+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}[u(\gamma^{\dagger})-u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})]\Delta\psi\,dx\,dt|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\delta^{2}\varepsilon+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\Delta\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt.

In order to estimate
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
, additionally we will make use of the continuity of the trace map from
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(\partial\Omega).

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}[u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\,dx\,dt|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt+C\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\phi_{\delta}-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\delta^{2}\varepsilon+\frac{C^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}[u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})]\psi\,dx\,dt|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}\,C\alpha\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-u(\gamma^{\dagger})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+\delta^{2}\varepsilon+\frac{\overline{\gamma}^{2}\,C^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta^{2}+8\varepsilon\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+8\delta^{2}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big[\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\Big(\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C^{2}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\overline{\gamma}^{2}C^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)\,dt\Big].

Thus, taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{16},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+2\alpha\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 3\delta^{2}+32\alpha^{2}\Big[\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\Big(\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C^{2}\|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\overline{\gamma}^{2}C^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)\,dt\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\delta^{2}+\alpha^{2}).

Therefore,

\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\|u(\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha})-\phi_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\leq C(\delta^{2}+\alpha^{2})

and

\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\alpha}+\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

From the estimate obtained in the above theorem for
\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
under the source condition (
3.12
) it follows that if we choose the regularization parameter
\alpha
as
\alpha\sim\delta
, then we have
\|\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma_{\delta}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}=O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

\lozenge

Remark 3.6
.

As observed, our analysis could produce the rate
O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
. It would be interesting to analyse the convergence rates by proceeding along the recent line of research based on variational source conditions (see for e.g.,
[
7
,
16
,
17
,
28
]
) which to the best of our knowledge still remains to be explored in the context of impedance coefficient identification. In fact we are aware of only a very recent paper
[
8
]
that deals with Robin coefficient identification using variational source condition for elliptic PDE only.

\lozenge

4.
Discussion about source condition

We now discuss advantages of the source condition (
3.12
). From Theorem
3.4
it is clear that our source condition does not require any smallness condition to be verified unlike the smallness condition of the form (
1.4
), that is required in standard convergence theory for Tikhonov-regularization for non linear operators (cf.
[
10
,
11
]
). Moreover, in order to obtain a convergence rate of
O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
we do not need to assume that
\gamma^{\dagger}
belongs to the range of the adjoint of the Fréchet derivative of the parameter-to-solution operator. This is another big advantage since most often the range of such operators is nothing but some Sobolev spaces with higher smoothness (see for e.g.
[
15
]
), and thus our source condition being free from such apriori range condition means that we can obtain the said convergence rate without imposing higher smoothness assumption on
\gamma^{\dagger},
which is obviously not known to us.

We now discuss about the apriori regularity of
\gamma^{\dagger}
that is embedded in the source condition (
3.12
). Recall that for defining trace operator, the minimal assumption that we need is that
\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\,\psi\,dt\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, and in that case it follows that we must have
\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
. Thus, in order that the source condition (
3.12
) makes sense, the regularity assumption that we require is that
\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega).
We shall show that in our setting we do always have
\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dt\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, thanks to the following very recent result on multiplication of elements in Sobolev spaces
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 7.4]
)
Let
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,d\in\{1,2,3\},
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Let
s_{i}(i=1,2),\,s
be real numbers such that
s_{i}\geq s\geq 0
and
s_{1}+s_{2}-s>\frac{d}{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
s_{1},s_{2},s,d
and
\Omega
such that

\|UV\|_{H^{s}(D)}\leq C\,\|U\|_{H^{s_{1}}(D)}\|V\|_{H^{s_{2}}(D)},\quad\forall\,U\in H^{s_{1}}(D),\,\,V\in H^{s_{2}}(D).

Since
\psi\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
and
u(\gamma^{\dagger})\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{1}(\Omega))
, by Theorem
4.1
it follows that
\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dt\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is to be noted that one can also derive a sufficient condition for
\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dt\in H^{1}(\Omega)
based on the results related to Banach algebra properties of Sobolev space, but with this argument we need to assume
H^{2}
- spatial regularity of
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
, which of course is a drawback. Indeed, recall that for
d\in\{2,3\}
,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
H^{s}(\Omega),\,s\geq 2,
is a Banach algebra. Thus, if we assume that
u(\gamma^{\dagger})\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
(such a regularity indeed holds if the initial profile
u_{0}
and the source function
f
in (
1.1
) are assumed to be of appropriate spatial regularity) then
\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\,\psi\,dt\in H^{2}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and thus by the property of trace operator it follows that we must have
\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\subset H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega).

\lozenge

We now look into the compatibility issue related to the source condition. From the expression of the source condition (
3.12
), one may think that on some portion of the boundary
\partial\Omega
near the terminal time status, it may happen that
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
vanishes(in the sense of trace) but
\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}
does not, and this implies that on such portions one has to know
\gamma^{\dagger}.
But this is compatible with the fact that for terminal time status, if
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
vanishes (in the sense of trace) on some portion of the boundary
\partial\Omega
then it is impossible to recover
\gamma^{\dagger}
on such portions, as can be observed from (
2.2
).

Next, we give a procedure to verify the source condition explicitly, motivated from the construction given in
[
12
]
for the case of
1
-dimensional diffusion coefficient identification problem. However, our construction is valid for the dimension
d\in\{2,3\}
as considered in this paper, but provided we have
u(\gamma^{\dagger})\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
. Indeed, we construct a
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
that satisfies the source condition (
3.12
). Before proceeding further, we recall the following interesting result from
[
27
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

(cf.
[
27
, Theorem A.2.9]
)
Let
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary. Let
s>\frac{d}{2}
. If
U\in H^{s}(D)
and
U\geq c>0
for some constant
c,
then
\frac{1}{U}\in H^{s}(D).

Our next result is about verification of source condition under certain smoothness assumption.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\gamma^{\dagger},\gamma^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
and
u(\gamma^{\dagger})\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
. Let
\psi_{1}\in H^{1}(\tau-\sigma,\tau)
be an arbitrary function. Define

u_{1}(x):=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}(\tau-t)(\tau-\sigma-t)\psi_{1}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\,dt.

Assume that

(4.1)

u_{1}\neq 0\,\,\text{a.e. in}\,\,\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{u_{1}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Then there exists a
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega))
satisfying the source condition (
3.12
).

Proof.

Let
\psi_{2}\in H^{1}(\tau-\sigma,\tau)
and
\varphi\in H^{m}(\Omega)
,
m\geq 2,
be two arbitrary functions. We will write
u
to denote
u(\gamma^{\dagger}).
Define

u_{2}(x):=\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}(\tau-t)(\tau-\sigma-t)\psi_{2}\varphi\,u\,dt.

Let
\Upsilon^{\dagger},\Upsilon^{*}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
\gamma^{\dagger}
and
\gamma^{*}
are their traces, respectively. We define

\psi:=(\tau-t)(\tau-\sigma-t)\Big(\psi_{2}\varphi+\psi_{1}\frac{\Upsilon^{\dagger}-\Upsilon^{*}-u_{2}}{u_{1}}\Big).

We claim that this
\psi
satisfies the source condition. Observe that from the
L^{\infty}
-assumption in (
4.1
) and from Theorem
4.3
, it follows that
\frac{1}{u_{1}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and hence
\frac{\Upsilon^{\dagger}-\Upsilon^{*}-u_{2}}{u_{1}}\in L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega)).
Also, from the definition of
\psi
it clearly follows that
\psi(\cdot,\tau-\sigma)=0=\psi(\cdot,\tau)
in
\Omega
. Thus,
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\tau-\sigma,\tau;H^{2}(\Omega)).
Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}\,u(\gamma^{\dagger})\psi\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{\tau}(\tau-t)(\tau-\sigma-t)\Big(\psi_{2}\varphi\,u+\psi_{1}\,u\frac{\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}-u_{2}}{u_{1}}\Big)\quad\text{ on}\,\,\partial\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma^{*}\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that the non-zero assumption in (
4.1
) does make sense, because otherwise it would mean
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
vanishes on the boundary near the terminal time
\tau
(i.e.,
[\tau-\sigma,\tau]
), but then from (
2.2
) it follows that the impedance coefficient
\gamma^{\dagger}
is impossible to recover.

\lozenge

5.
Conclusion

We have considered an inverse problem of identifying a spatially dependent impedance coefficient in a parabolic PDE from a short-time observation of the temperature distribution. We have proposed a weak-type source condition that allowed to obtain a convergence rate of
O(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
provided we choose the regularization parameter
\alpha\sim\delta
, where
\delta
is the deterministic noise level. As compared to the standard convergence theory of Tikhonov regularization for non linear operators in Hilbert spaces, the above rate of convergence is obtained under a simple source condition which does not require any range condition of the adjoint of Fréchet derivative, no smallness condition to be verified and requires a regularity assumption that
\gamma^{\dagger}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega).
Moreover, if it is known that
u(\gamma^{\dagger})
vanishes(in the sense of trace) on some portion of the boundary
\partial\Omega,
then one has to know
\gamma^{\dagger}
apriori over those portions, as it is impossible to recover the impedance coefficient on those portions.

Robust Learning of Recurrent Neural Networks in Presence of Exogenous Noise
Arash Amini, Guangyi Liu, Nader Motee

Abstract
Recurrent Neural networks (RNN) have shown promising potential for learning dynamics of sequential data. However, artificial neural networks are known to exhibit poor robustness in presence of input noise, where the sequential architecture of RNNs exacerbates the problem. In this paper, we will use ideas from control and estimation theories to propose a tractable robustness analysis for RNN models that are subject to input noise. The variance of the output of the noisy system is adopted as a robustness measure to quantify the impact of noise on learning. It is shown that the robustness measure can be estimated efficiently using linearization techniques. Using these results, we proposed a learning method to enhance robustness of a RNN with respect to exogenous Gaussian noise with known statistics. Our extensive simulations on benchmark problems reveal that our proposed methodology significantly improves robustness of recurrent neural networks.

Despite recent advancements in the area of artificial neural networks, most of the proposed architectures are fragile in presence of input noise. Due to the iterative nature of the recurrent models, the effect of input noise propagates and magnifies as the input sequence becomes longer. The noise from prior inputs that are passing through nonlinear functions make the analysis more challenging. Throughout this paper, we aim to elucidate the problem of robustness for general recurrent neural networks with additive input noise. We begin by defining an effective metric for the RNN model’s robustness that can quantify the impact of input noise on the output and cost function. We proceed to introduce a novel approach that provides an estimation for the output statistics inspired by ideas from the Extended Kalman Filter. Finally, we propose two different algorithms to enhance robustness of recurrent neural networks. Our extensive simulation results confirm that our proposed methodology significantly improves robustness of RNNs in presence of Gaussian input noise.

Problem Statement

The state-of-the-art machine learning technique to handle sequential data is the class of recurrent neural networks (RNN), which has been successfully employed for commercial applications like Google’s voice
[

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf x}_{t}&={\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},{\bf u}_{t},{\theta}_{F}),\\
{\bf y}_{t}&={\bf G}({\bf x}_{t},\theta_{G})\end{split}

(1)

with initial condition
{\bf x}_{0}
, where
{\bf u}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},{\bf x}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},{\bf y}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
represent the input, hidden state, and the output of the recurrent network at time instant
t
. The vectors of all trainable parameters are denoted by
\theta_{F}
and
\theta_{G}
. Examples include basic RNN
[
7
]
, LSTM
[
10
]
, and GRU
[
11
]
. Throughout the paper,
\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert
stands for the Euclidean norm of vector
{\bf x}
.

Assumption 1

The vector-valued function
{\bf F}
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t the hidden state and input with constants
\lambda>0
and
\kappa_{u}>0
, respectively. Moreover function
{\bf G}
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t the hidden state with constant
\kappa_{G}>0
.

This assumption implies that

\left\lVert\,{\bf F}({\bf x},{\bf u},\theta_{F})-{\bf F}(\bar{{\bf x}},{\bf u},\theta_{F})\,\right\rVert\leq\lambda\left\lVert\,{\bf x}-\bar{{\bf x}}\,\right\rVert

(2)

for all
{\bf x},\bar{{\bf x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\left\lVert\,{\bf F}({\bf x},{\bf u},\theta_{F})-{\bf F}({\bf x},\bar{{\bf u}},\theta_{F})\,\right\rVert\leq\kappa_{u}\left\lVert\,{\bf u}-\bar{{\bf u}}\,\right\rVert,

(3)

for all
{\bf u},\bar{{\bf u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The learning process in a RNN model involves finding parameters
\theta_{F},\,\theta_{G}
using given training datasets
\left\{({\bf u}_{t},{\bf y}^{*}_{t})\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
by minimizing a loss function that measures closeness between the target output
\left\{{\bf y}^{*}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
and the output of system (
1
) w.r.t input
\left\{{\bf u}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}

\mathcal{E}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t},{{\bf y}}^{*}_{t}),

(4)

where
\mathcal{L}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}>0

Examples of some commonly used loss functions are the cross-entropy function for classification purposes, which is defined by

\mathcal{L}(\,{\bf y}_{t},{\bf y}_{t}^{*}\,)=-{\bf y}_{t}[{\bf i}^{*}]+\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\exp\left({{\bf y}_{t}\,[j]\,}\right)\right),

The

1
) such that system (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}&={\bf F}(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t},\theta_{F}),\\
\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}&={\bf G}(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t},\theta_{G}),\end{split}

(5)

The
objective
of this paper is to train network (
1
) using the given datasets
\left\{({\bf u}_{t},{\bf y}^{*}_{t})\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
such that the output of the perturbed system (
5
) minimizes the expected loss function

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t},{\bf y}_{t}^{*})\right\}.

(6)

Since the sequence of target outputs
\left\{{\bf y}_{t}^{*}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}

III
Robustness Measure for Learning

We can interpret the disturbed RNN model (
5
) as a control system with noisy input and employ ideas from robust control
[
20
]
to analyze this class of neural networks. We adopt the expected deviation of the output of the noisy RNN (
5
) from the output of the undisturbed RNN (
1
) as a robustness measure, i.e.,

\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G}):=\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{\bf y}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.

(7)

By denoting the expected value of the output by
\hat{{\bf y}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}]
and its covariance by

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left\{(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-\hat{{\bf y}}_{t})(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-\hat{{\bf y}}_{t})^{T}\right\},

one can show that

\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})=\mathrm{Tr}\left({\bf R}_{t}\right)+\mathrm{Bias}\left({\bf y}_{t}\right),

(8)

where the bias term is given by

\mathrm{Bias}({\bf y}_{t})=(\hat{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t})^{T}(\hat{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t}).

Our objective is to learn parameters
\theta_{F},\theta_{G}
using the original dataset such that the RNN will have a robust performance with respect to all noisy inputs
\left\{{\bf u}_{t}+{\bf w}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
with
{\bf w}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{t})
. Calculating the expected loss function (
6
) is very challenging in general as one should calculate statistics of the output of the noisy nonlinear system (
5
), which requires solving the corresponding Fokker–Planck equation
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
. Our following result quantifies a relationship that will help us solve the robust learning problem efficiently.

Theorem 1

The expected loss function for the noisy recurrent neural network (
5
) satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})\leq\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})}+\mathcal{E}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G}),

(9)

where
\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof:

At any time instance
t
one can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})-\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t})+\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t}),

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})-\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t,})\right\rVert+\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t}),

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t}\right\rVert+\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t}),

taking expected value with respect to the input noise from both side implies that

\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})\right\}\leq\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t})+\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t}\right\rVert\right\}.

(10)

For the second term by the Jensen’s inequality and the fact that square root is a concave function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t}\right\rVert\Big\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sqrt{(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t})^{T}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{{\bf y}}_{t})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{\bf y}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})}.

The above inequality combined with inequality (
10
) implies that

\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})\right\}\leq\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t})+\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})}.

Taking the summation over time from both sides of the inequality result in

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})\right\}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})}.

The expected value and summation are exchangeable. Therefore, from the definition of (
6
), the inequality (
9
) follows.
∎

The right-hand-side of the inequality (
10
) provides a meaningful
decomposition
that involves the learning accuracy, which can be quantified by the loss function (
4
), and the impact of noise on learning, which is quantified by the square root of the robustness measure. This result is important as it suggests that in order to achieve robust learning with respect to exogenous noise, one should train the RNN by minimizing a regularized cost function that represents a term for learning accuracy and an additional term for robustness. We will discuss this in detail in Section
VI
.

IV
Calculating Upper Bounds for the Robustness Measure

As we discussed earlier, finding explicit forms for the robustness measure (
7
) is a tedious task as one needs to calculate statistics of a stochastic process generated by a nonlinear dynamical system. In the following, we aim at formulating some explicit upper bounds under some assumptions.

Definition 1

The RNN model (
1
) is stable if there exists
\theta_{F}
such that the Lipschitz property (
2
) holds with
\lambda<1
.

We refer to
[
6
]
for a complete discussion on this class of networks.
Stable recurrent neural networks are more reliable due to their predictable behavior. In the following theorem, we show that if an RNN is stable, then the robustness measure is bounded for every data sequence.

Theorem 2

Let us consider the noiseless RNN (
1
) with initial condition
{\bf x}_{0}
, and the noisy RNN (
5
) with additive Gaussian noise
{\bf w}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{t})
and initial condition that is drawn from
\mathcal{N}({\bf x}_{0},\Gamma)
. Then,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})\leq\kappa_{G}^{2}\left((2\lambda^{2})^{t}\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)+\kappa_{u}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(2\lambda^{2})^{i}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{i})\right).

(11)

Moreover, if
\lambda<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and the covariance matrix of the input noise stays constant over time, i.e.,
\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma
for all
t\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})\leq\frac{2(\kappa_{u}\kappa_{G})^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)}{1-2\lambda^{2}}.

(12)

Proof:

At each time instant
t
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bf x}_{t}-\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},{\bf u}_{t}-{\bf F}(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=||{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},{\bf u}_{t})-{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})-{\bf F}(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})||

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},{\bf u}_{t})-{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\left\lVert{\bf F}({\bf x}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})-{\bf F}(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1},\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{u}\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf u}}_{t}-{\bf u}_{t}\right\rVert+\lambda\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{u}\left\lVert{\bf w}_{t}\right\rVert+\lambda\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1}-{\bf x}_{t-1}\right\rVert.

Squaring both sides results in

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\kappa_{u}\left\lVert{\bf w}_{t}\right\rVert+\lambda\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1}-{\bf x}_{t-1}\right\rVert\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(\kappa_{u}^{2}\left\lVert{\bf w}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1}-{\bf x}_{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\big),

(13)

where inequality
(x+y)^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2})
, for all
x,y\geq 0
, is utilized in the latter step.
The Lipschitz continuity of
{\bf G}
implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{\bf y}_{t}\right\rVert=\left\lVert{\bf G}({\bf x}_{t})-{\bf G}(\tilde{{\bf x}_{t}}))\right\rVert\leq\kappa_{G}\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert.

The robustness measure can be bounded by

\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t}-{\bf y}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}\leq\kappa_{G}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.

Since
{\bf w}_{t}
is Gaussian with zero mean and covariance
\Sigma_{t}
, it can be shown that

\mathbb{E}\{\left\lVert{\bf w}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\}=\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{t})
. Thus, inequality (
13
) can be rewritten as

\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}\leq 2\kappa_{u}^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{t})+2\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t-1}-{\bf x}_{t-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.

By inserting the previous error bounds recursively, one can obtain an explicit upper bound as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{t}-{\bf x}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(2\lambda^{2})^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0}-{\bf x}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(2\lambda^{2})^{i}\kappa_{u}^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{i}).

(14)

This inequality combined with the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0}-{\bf x}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}=\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)
leads to

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})\leq\kappa_{G}^{2}\left((2\lambda^{2})^{t}\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)+2\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(2\lambda^{2})^{i}\kappa_{u}^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{i})\right).

For stable recurrent networks, we have
\lambda<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. Thus,
\lambda^{t}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and the geometric series converges to

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})\leq 2\frac{(\kappa_{u}\kappa_{G})^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)}{1-2\lambda^{2}}.

∎

V
State and Output Covariance Estimation

Computing an exact and explicit expression for the robustness measure requires us to access the exact stochastic information of the disturbed RNN output, which is tremendously challenging, even for the basic RNN models.
The problem of estimating the statistics of the output of a noisy nonlinear control system has been studied comprehensively in the context of Kalman filtering
[
21
]
-
[
22
]
,
[
33
]
. The Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and its variants are powerful methods that have been successfully applied to various real-world applications
[
34
]
. In this section, we build upon ideas from the EKF and apply similar linearization techniques to estimate covariance matrices of the noisy RNN. In the following, we briefly state some of the known results on the transformation of uncertainty
[
35
]
.

Suppose that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and a random vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with expected value
\hat{{\bf x}}
and covariance matrix
{\bf P}_{xx}
are given. Then, one can expand right-hand-side of
{{\bf y}}=f({\bf x})
around
\hat{{\bf x}}=\mathbb{E}\left\{{\bf x}\right\}
to obtain

{\bf y}=f(\hat{{\bf x}})+\nabla_{x}f(\hat{{\bf x}})({\bf x}-\hat{{\bf x}})+{o}\big(({\bf x}-\hat{{\bf x}})^{2}\big).

(19)

Taking the expectation from both sides of the equality results in

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf y}}

\displaystyle=f(\hat{{\bf x}})+\nabla_{x}f(\hat{{\bf x}})\mathbb{E}\left\{{\bf x}-\hat{{\bf x}}\right\}+o\big(\mathbb{E}\left\{({\bf x}-\hat{{\bf x}})^{2}\right\}\big)

\displaystyle=f(\hat{{\bf x}})+\mathbb{E}\left\{o\big(({\bf x}-\hat{{\bf x}})^{2}\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\approx f(\hat{{\bf x}}),

(20)

where
\mathbb{E}[{\bf y}]=\hat{{\bf y}}
. Furthermore, the covariance matrix of the random variable
{\bf y}
can be approximated by

VI
Learning Robust RNN

In previous sections, we quantified the effect of noise input on the output of a RNN network. As we discussed in Section III, the expected value of the training cost in presence of noise is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bf y}}_{t})\right\}\leq\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})}+\mathcal{L}({{\bf y}}_{t}).

This inequality reveals that including the robustness measure
\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G})
as a regularization term to the loss function, i.e.,

\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\mathcal{L}({\bf y}_{t})+\mu\rho_{t}(\theta_{F},\theta_{G}),

(27)

where
\mu>0
is a design parameter to adjust robustness level, will significantly improve learning robustness. In view of (
27
) and the fact that calculating an explicit closed-form expression for the robustness measure is very challenging, we consider two remedies. The first approach is based on the upper bound in the right hand-side of inequality (
11
), which is obtained using Lipschitz properties of the RNN. The second method is based on approximation formula (
24
), which is derived by calculating propagation of noise in the output of the RNN throughout time.

(28)

Remark 4

The idea of training for a stable RNN may not necessarily improve robustness of RNN networks. One can observe from our derivations that the robustness measure depends on both weight matrices

VIII
Conclusion

We propose a formal analysis to study robustness properties of recurrent neural networks in presence of exogenous noise.
Obtaining dynamic equations for noise propagation throughout time and quantifying the effect of noise on the output of recurrent networks have allowed us to introduce and utilize a proper measure of robustness to achieve robust learning. We have discussed and compared four different methods and shown that one can achieve significant robustness by regularizing the learning loss function with proper robustness measures.

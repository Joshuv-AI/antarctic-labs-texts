Bohr inequalities for unimodular bounded functions on simply connected domains
Molla Basir Ahamed, Vasudevarao Allu, Himadri Halder

Abstract
. Let ℋ ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) \mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}) be the class of analytic functions in the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . The classical Bohr’s inequality states that if a power series f ⁡ ( z ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ a n ​ z n f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n} converges in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and | f ⁡ ( z ) | < 1 |f(z)|<1 for z ∈ 𝔻 z\in\mathbb{D} , then ∑ n = 0 ∞ | a n | ​ r n ≤ 1 ​ for ​ r ≤ 1 3 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{3} and the constant 1 / 3 1/3 cannot be improved. The constant 1 / 3 1/3 is known as Bohr radius. In this paper, we study Bohr phenomenon for analytic as well as harmonic mappings on simply connected domains. We prove several sharp results on improved Bohr radius for analytic functions as well as for harmonic mappings on simply connected domains.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
be the class of analytic functions
f
in
\Omega
such that
|f(z)|<1
for all
z\in\Omega
. The Bohr radius
B_{\Omega}
for the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
is defined by

B_{\Omega}:=\sup\bigg\{r\in(0,1):\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\text{for all}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega),\;z\in\mathbb{D}\bigg\}.

For
f\in B_{\Omega}
given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
, its associated majorant series defined by
M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}
.
For
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
, it is proved that
B_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3
, which is the classical Bohr radius. The classical result of Bohr
[
20
]
, which inspired a lot in the recent years is in the following form after subsequent improvements due to M. Riesz, I. Schur, and N. Wiener.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
20
]

If
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

(1.2)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

The radius
1/3
cannot be improved.

It is important to note that if
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z^{*})|=1
for some
z^{*}
in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f(z)
reduces to a unimodular constant. Thus, we restrict our attention to
|f(z)|<1
. It is worth to point out that if
|a_{0}|
in the equality (
1.2
) is replaced by
|a_{0}|^{2}
, then the radius
1/3
could be replaced by
1/2
. Furthermore, if
a_{0}=0
in Theorem
1.1
, then the sharp radius can be improved to
1/\sqrt{2}
. In a more convenient way, we can demonstrate these facts that a minor change in the coefficients gives different sharp radius, and thus, sharp coefficient estimates which play a vital role to obtain the sharp radius. Therefore, proofs of these results and the inequality (
1.2
) relied on the sharp coefficient inequalities which may be obtained as an application of Pick’s invariant form of Schwarz’s lemma for
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

In particular,
|f^{\prime}(0)|=|a_{1}|\leq 1-|f(0)|^{2}=1-|a_{0}|^{2}
and hence from this, the sharp inequality
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}
follows for
n\geq 1
.

Studying the Bohr inequalities in recent years become an interesting topic of research in functions of one as well as several complex variables. The notion of Bohr inequality has been generalized to several complex variables, to planar harmonic mappings, to polynomials, to solutions of elliptic partial differential equations, and more abstract settings. Let
\mathbb{D}(a,r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<1\}
. For
0\leq\gamma<1
, we consider the disk
\Omega_{\gamma}
defined by

\Omega_{\gamma}:=\bigg\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\bigg|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\bigg|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\bigg\}.

It is easy to see that
\Omega_{\gamma}
always contains the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. The following coefficient estimates for class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
are required to prove some of our results.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
23
]

For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let

\Omega_{\gamma}:=\bigg\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\bigg|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\bigg|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\bigg\},

and let
f
be an analytic function in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, bounded by
1
, with the series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}.
Then

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 1.

In

, the notion of classical Bohr inequality
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1
has been generalized by Fournier and Ruscheweyh
[

to the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
. More precisely,

Theorem 1.4
.

[
24

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
, with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq\rho:=\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma}.

Moreover,
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}=1
holds for a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
if, and only if,

In

Theorem 1.5

[

Let
\Omega

A complex-valued function

\mathbb{D}
has the following representation

(1.7)

f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Main Results

Before we state an improved version of Theorem
1.4
for class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
, we prove the following lemma. Let
Q(w)

.

Let

be an analytic function and
\gamma\in\mathbb{D}
be such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;\rho\leq\rho_{0}=\frac{1-|\gamma|^{2}}{3+|\gamma|},

where

under the mapping
g
and the non-negative real coefficients

In view of Lemma
2.2
, we obtain the following improved version of Theorem
1.4
. In deed, with an additional polynomial

with the majorant series of

for the radius

.

For

connected by the relations

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\gamma\in[0,1)
. Then it is easy to see that
z\in\mathbb{D}_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{D}(\gamma;1-\gamma)
if, and only if,
w=(z-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)\in\mathbb{D}.

Then we have

\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(1-\gamma)^{n}\phi^{n}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\phi^{n}(z):=G(\phi(z))

for
z\in\mathbb{D}_{\gamma},
where
b_{n}=\alpha_{n}(1-\gamma)^{n}
. A simple computation shows that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Area}\bigg(G(\mathbb{D}(0,\rho))\bigg)\leq(1-|b_{0}|^{2})^{2}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}=(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

Therefore,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\rho}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}=\frac{1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{\rho}{1-\gamma-\rho}\right).

In view of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\alpha_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=|\alpha_{0}|+\frac{(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})\rho}{(1+\gamma)(1-\gamma-\rho)}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{j}\bigg(\frac{(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})\rho}{(1-\rho^{2})}\bigg)^{2j}

\displaystyle=1+\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho),

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho)=\frac{(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})\rho}{(1+\gamma)(1-\gamma-\rho)}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{j}\bigg(\frac{(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})\rho}{(1-\rho^{2})}\bigg)^{2j}-(1-|\alpha_{0}|).

Let
G_{m}(\rho)
be defined by

\displaystyle G_{m}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{c_{m-1}}{c_{m}}\right)\frac{\rho^{2m-2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2m-2}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-2}}+\cdots+\left(\frac{c_{m-j}}{c_{m}}\right)\frac{\rho^{2m-2j}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2m-2j}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2j}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\cdots+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{m}}\right)\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-2}}+\frac{\rho^{2m}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2m}}.

Then we can write
\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho)
as

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2}}{2}\bigg(1+2c_{m}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-2}G_{m}(\rho)-\frac{2}{1+|\alpha_{0}|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2\lambda(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{3}}\left(\frac{2\rho}{(1+\gamma)(1-\gamma-\rho)}-1\right)\bigg).

We suppose that
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
. Then it is easy to see that
\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho)
is an increasing function and hence
\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho)\leq\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho_{0})
, where

\displaystyle\frac{2\rho_{0}}{(1+\gamma)(1-\gamma-\rho_{0})}=1

which is equivalent to

\rho_{0}=\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{3+\gamma}.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho_{0})=\frac{1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2}}{2}\left(1+2F_{m}(|\alpha_{0}|)-\frac{2}{1+|\alpha_{0}|}\right):=\frac{1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2}}{2}J(|\alpha_{0}|),

\displaystyle F_{m}(|\alpha_{0}|)

\displaystyle=c_{m}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-1}A^{2m}(\gamma)+c_{m-1}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-3}A^{2m-1}(\gamma)+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c_{m-j}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})^{2m-2j-1}A^{2j-1}(\gamma)+\cdots+c_{1}(1-|\alpha_{0}|^{2})A^{2}(\gamma),

\displaystyle J(x)

\displaystyle=1+2F_{m}(x)-\frac{2}{1+x}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\ x\in[0,1]\;\;\text{and}\;\;

\displaystyle=\frac{(3+\gamma)(1-\gamma^{2})}{(3+\gamma)^{2}-(1-\gamma^{2})^{2}}.

A(\gamma)>0
for

. In order to show that

for

and
\gamma\in[0,1)
so that
\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(\rho_{0})\leq 0
. Further, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle J(0)

\displaystyle=2c_{m}A^{2m}(\gamma)+2c_{m-1}A^{2m-1}(\gamma)+\cdots+c_{m-j}A^{2j-1}(\gamma)+\cdots+c_{1}A^{2}(\gamma)-1

and

A(\gamma)=(f_{1}\circ f_{2})(\gamma)
, where

and

in

\gamma

\gamma
in

, with

. It can be seen that each

, for

\displaystyle A^{2j}(\gamma)\leq A^{2j}(0)=\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{2j}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;j=1,2,\cdots,m.

, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle x(1+x)^{2}A^{2}(\gamma)

\displaystyle x(1+x)^{2}(1-x^{2})^{2}A^{4}(\gamma)\vskip 14.45377pt

. This completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem

From (

|a_{n}|\leq\lambda(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 1.

A simple calculation using (

\displaystyle\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\iint\limits_{|z|<r}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dxdy\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nr^{2n}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\left(\frac{r}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{2}.

For
r\leq 1/(1+2\lambda)
, it is easy to see that

(3.6)

\frac{r}{1-r^{2}}\leq\frac{1+2\lambda}{4(1+\lambda)\lambda}.

In view of (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+P\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1+\lambda}{1+2\lambda}\right)^{2j}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})}{16^{m}}\left(\frac{16^{m}}{1+|a_{0}|}-\frac{16^{m}}{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}16^{m-j}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2j-1}\right)

\displaystyle:\;=1-\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})}{16^{m}}J_{1}(|a_{0}|),

where

J_{1}(x)=\frac{16^{m}}{1+x}-\frac{16^{m}}{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}16^{m-j}(1-x^{2})^{2j-1}.

In particular, we have

J_{1}(0)=\frac{16^{m}}{2}-\frac{16^{m}-1}{15}=\frac{13}{30}16^{m}-1>0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;J_{1}(1)=0.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{16^{m}}{(1+x)^{2}}+2x\sum_{j=1}^{m}16^{m-j}(1-x^{2})^{2j-2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{16^{m}}{4}+2\frac{16^{m}-1}{16-1}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{7}{60}16^{m}+\frac{2}{15}\right)<0.

Therefore,
J_{1}(x)
is a decreasing function in
[0,1]
and hence
J_{1}(x)\geq J_{1}(1)=0
for
x\in[0,1].
Thus, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+P\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}.

It is easy to see that the equality

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\left(\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\right)^{n}+P\left(\frac{S_{\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}}}{\pi}\right)=1

holds for the function
f
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

if, and only if,
f\equiv c
with
|c|=1.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

From (
1.6
), we know that

|a_{n}|\leq\lambda(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 1.

Then for
r\leq 1/(1+2\lambda)
, a simple computation shows that

(3.7)

\displaystyle|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(|a_{n}|+\beta|a_{n}|^{2}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq|a_{0}|+\bigg(\lambda(1-|a_{0}|^{2})+\beta\lambda^{2}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}\bigg)\frac{r}{1-r}

\displaystyle\leq|a_{0}|+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{2}\left(1+\lambda\beta(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\right)

\displaystyle:=1-\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{2}F_{1}(x),

where
F_{1}(x)
is defined by

F_{1}(x)=\frac{2}{1+x}-1-\lambda\beta(1-x^{2}).

Since
0\leq\beta\leq 1/\lambda
, it follows that
F_{1}(0)=1-\lambda\beta\geq 0
and
F_{1}(1)=0
.
Our aim is to show that
F_{1}(x)\geq 0
for
x\in[0,1]
. It is easy to see that

F^{\prime}_{1}(x)=-\frac{2}{(1+x)^{2}}+2\beta\lambda x\leq-2+2\beta\lambda\leq 0\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq\beta\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Therefore,
F_{1}(x)
is a decreasing function on
[0,1]
and hence we have
F_{1}(x)\geq F_{1}(1)=0
for
x\in[0,1]
.
In view of (
3.7
), we obtain

|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(|a_{n}|+\beta|a_{n}|^{2}\bigg)r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}.

It is easy to see that the equality

|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(|a_{n}|+\beta|a_{n}|^{2}\bigg)\left(\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\right)^{n}=1

holds for the function
f
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

if, and only if,
f\equiv c
with
|c|=1.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
and
g(z)=f((z-\gamma)/(1-\gamma))
. Then it is easy to see that
g\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and

\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}(z-\gamma)^{n}.

Using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;\rho\leq\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{3+\gamma}

which is equivalent to

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{|a_{n}|}\left(\frac{\rho}{(1-\gamma)}\right)^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;\rho\leq\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{3+\gamma}.

Set
\rho=r(1-\gamma)
, then in view of (
3.8
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{|a_{n}|}r^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{r(1-\gamma)}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma}.

To show the sharpness of the result, we consider the following function

\displaystyle f_{a}(z)=\frac{a-\gamma-(1-\gamma)z}{1-a\gamma-a(1-\gamma)}\;\;\text{for}\;\;z\in\Omega_{\gamma}\;\;\text{and}\;\;a\in(0,1).

Define
\phi_{1}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
by
\phi_{1}(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
and
\phi_{2}(z):\Omega_{\gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
by
\phi_{2}(z)=(1-\gamma)z+\gamma
.
Then, the function
f_{a}=\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2}
maps
\Omega_{\gamma}
univalently onto
\mathbb{D}.
A simple computation shows that

f_{a}(z)=\frac{a-\gamma-(1-\gamma)z}{1-a\gamma-a(1-\gamma)}=C_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}z^{n}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a\in(0,1)
and

C_{0}=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\;\;\text{and}\;\;C_{n}=\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}.

A simple computation using (
3.9
) shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{|a_{n}|}r^{n}+Q\left(\frac{S_{r(1-\gamma)}}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}+\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{1-a\gamma}\right)\frac{(1-\gamma)r}{1-a\gamma-ar(1-\gamma)}+\frac{c_{1}r^{2}(1-a^{2})^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{c_{2}r^{4}(1-a^{2})^{4}(1-\gamma)^{8}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{4}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{m}r^{2m}(1-a^{2})^{2m}(1-\gamma)^{4m}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{2m}}

\displaystyle:\;=1-(1-a)\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r),

where

\displaystyle\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)r}{(1-a\gamma-ar(1-\gamma))(1-a\gamma)}-\frac{c_{1}r^{2}(1-a)(1+a)^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{c_{2}r^{4}(1-a)^{3}(1+a)^{4}(1-\gamma)^{8}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{4}}-\frac{c_{m}r^{2m}(1-a)^{2m-1}(1+a)^{2m}(1-\gamma)^{4m}}{((1-a\gamma)^{2}-a^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4})^{2m}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{1}{1-a}\left(\frac{a-\gamma}{1+a\gamma}-1\right).

It is not difficult to show that
\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r)
is strictly decreasing function of
r
in
(0,1)
. Therefore, for
r>r_{0}=(1+\gamma)/(3+\gamma)
, we have
\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r)<\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r_{0})
. An elementary calculation shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow 1}\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r_{0})=-\frac{2r_{0}}{(1-\gamma)(1-r_{0})}+\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}=0.

Therefore
\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r)<0
for
r>r_{0}
. Hence
1-(1-a)\Phi^{\gamma}_{1}(r)>1
for
r>r_{0},
which shows that
r_{0}
is the best possible.
∎

(3.10)

Proof of Theorem
2.9
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
given by (
1.7
) be harmonic in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|+|g(z)|\leq 1

\Omega_{\gamma}

(3.14)

\psi_{2}(z)=h(z)+\epsilon g(z),

\epsilon\;(|\epsilon|=1)

\psi_{2}
is analytic in
\Omega_{\gamma}

|\psi_{2}|\leq 1

\Omega_{\gamma}

3.10

\psi_{2}(z)=a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})z^{n}\quad\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

In view of Lemma
1.3

(3.15)

|a_{n}+\epsilon b_{n}|\leq\frac{1-|a_{0}+\epsilon b_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 1.

(3.16)

|a_{n}-\epsilon b_{n}|\leq\frac{1-|a_{0}-\epsilon b_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 1.

From (
3.15

3.16
), we obtain

(3.17)

It is easy to see that (
3.17
) is equivalent to

(3.18)

|a_{n}+\epsilon b_{n}|+|a_{n}-\epsilon b_{n}|\leq\frac{2\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2}\right)}{1+\gamma}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 1.

From (
3.18
), we obtain

\bigg(|a_{n}+\epsilon b_{n}|+|a_{n}-\epsilon b_{n}|\bigg)^{2}\leq\frac{4\bigg(1-|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{2}}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}

which is equivalent to

(3.19)

|a_{n}|^{2}+|b_{n}|^{2}+|a^{2}-e^{i2\theta}b_{n}^{2}|\leq\frac{2\bigg(1-|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{2}}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}.

A simple computation using (
3.20

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sqrt{|a_{0}|^{2}+|b_{0}|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{|a_{n}|^{2}+|a_{n}|^{2}}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq|a_{0}|+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{n}

Multiplicative topological indices: Analytical properties and application to random networks
R. Aguilar-Sánchez, J. A. Méndez-Bermúdez, José M. Rodríguez, José M. Sigarreta

Abstract
We make use of multiplicative degree-based topological indices X Π ​ ( G ) X_{\Pi}(G) to perform a detailed analytical and statistical study of random networks G = ( V ⁡ ( G ) , E ⁡ ( G ) ) G=(V(G),E(G)) . We consider two classes of indices: X Π ​ ( G ) = ∏ u ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) F V ​ ( d u ) X_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u}) and X Π ​ ( G ) = ∏ u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) F E ​ ( d u , d v ) X_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v}) , where u ​ v uv denotes the edge of G G connecting the vertices u u and v v , d u d_{u} is the degree of the vertex u u , and F V ​ ( x ) F_{V}(x) and F E ​ ( x , y ) F_{E}(x,y) are functions of the vertex degrees. Specifically, we find analytical inequalities involving these multiplicative indices. Also, we apply X Π ​ ( G ) X_{\Pi}(G) on three models of random networks: Erdös-Rényi networks, random geometric graphs, and bipartite random networks. We show that ⟨ ln ⁡ X Π ​ ( G ) ⟩ \left<\ln X_{\Pi}(G)\right> , normalized to the order of the network, scale with the corresponding average degree; here ⟨ ⋅ ⟩ \left<\cdot\right> denotes the average over an ensemble of random networks.

1
Introduction

We can identify two types of graph invariants which are currently been studied in
chemical graph theory, namely

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma}(G)=X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad

(1)

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma}(G)=X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})

and

\displaystyle X_{\Pi}(G)=X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)=\prod_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad

(2)

\displaystyle X_{\Pi}(G)=X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})\,.

Here
uv
denotes the edge of the graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
connecting the vertices
u
and
v
,

d_{u}
is the degree of the vertex
u
, and
F_{V}(x)
and
F_{V}(x,y)
are appropriate chosen functions,
see e.g.
G13
.
While both
X_{\Sigma}(G)
and
X_{\Pi}(G)
are referred as topological indices in the literature, to make
a distinction between them, here we name
X_{\Pi}(G)
as
multiplicative
topological indices (MTIs).

Among the vast amount of topological indices of the form given by Eq. (
1
) we can refer to
some prominent examples:
the first and second Zagreb indices
G72

M_{1}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}^{2}=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}+d_{v}

(3)

and

M_{2}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}d_{v},

(4)

respectively, the Randić connectivity index
R75

R(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}},

(5)

the harmonic index
F87

H(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{2}{d_{u}+d_{v}},

(6)

the sum-connectivity index
ZT46

\chi(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{u}+d_{v}}},

(7)

and the inverse degree index
F87

ID(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d_{u}}=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\left(\frac{1}{d_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{d_{v}^{2}}\right).

(8)

In fact, within a statistical approach on random networks, it has been recently shown that the average
values of indices of the type
X_{\Sigma}(G)
, normalized to the order of the network
n
, scale with the
average degree
\left<k\right>
; see e.g.
MMRS20
;
AHMS20
;
MMRS21
.
That is,
\left<X_{\Sigma}(G)\right>/n
is a function of
\left<k\right>
only.
Moreover, it was also found that
\left<X_{\Sigma}(G)\right>
, for indices like
R(G)
and
H(G)
,
is highly correlated with the Shannon entropy of the eigenvectors of the adjacency matrix of random
networks
AMRS20
. This is a notable result because it puts forward the application of topological
indices beyond mathematical chemistry. Specifically, given que equivalence of the Hamiltonian
of a tight-binding network (in the proper setup) and the corresponding network adjacency matrix,
either
\left<R(G)\right>
or
\left<H(G)\right>
could be used to determine the eigenvector
localization and delocalization regimes which in turn determine the insulator and metallic regimes
of quantum transport.

Therefore, motivated by their potential applications, in this work we study MTIs, see Eq. (
2
), on
random networks.

Indeed, several MTIs have been already deeply analyzed in the literature (see e.g. the work of V. R. Kulli),
among the most relevant we have to mention the Narumi-Katayama index
NK84

NK(G)=\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u},

(9)

and multiplicative versions of the Zagreb indices
TC1084
:

\Pi_{1}(G)=\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}^{2},

(10)

\Pi_{2}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}d_{v},

(11)

and

\Pi_{1}^{*}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}+d_{v}.

(12)

In addition to the MTIs of Eqs. (
9
-
12
), we also consider multiplicative versions of the
indices in Eqs. (
5
-
8
):
the multiplicative Randić connectivity index

R_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}},

(13)

the multiplicative harmonic index

H_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{2}{d_{u}+d_{v}},

(14)

the multiplicative sum-connectivity index

\chi_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{u}+d_{v}}},

(15)

and the multiplicative inverse degree index

ID_{\Pi}(G)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\left(\frac{1}{d_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{d_{v}^{2}}\right).

(16)

Below we apply the MTIs of Eqs. (
9
-
16
) on three models of random
networks:
Erdös-Rényi (ER) networks, random geometric (RG) graphs, and bipartite random (BR) networks.
ER networks
SR51
;
ER59
;
ER60

G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}}(n,p)
are formed by
n
vertices connected independently
with probability
p\in[0,1]
.
While RG graphs
DC02
;
P03

G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}}(n,r)
consist of
n
vertices uniformly and independently
distributed on the unit square, where two vertices are connected by an edge if their Euclidean distance is less
or equal than the connection radius
r\in[0,\sqrt{2}]
.
In addition we examine BR networks
G_{\mbox{\tiny BR}}(n_{1},n_{2},p)
composed by two disjoint sets, set 1 and set 2,
with
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
vertices each such that there are no adjacent vertices within the same set, being

n=n_{1}+n_{2}
the total number of vertices in the bipartite network. The vertices of the two sets are connected
randomly with probability
p\in[0,1]
.

The purpose of this work is threefold.
First, from an analytical viewpoint, we find several inequalities that relate multiplicative indices to their additive versions;
second, we want to establish the statistical
analysis of MTIs as a generic tool for studying average properties of random networks; and third, we
perform for the first time (to our knowledge), a scaling study of MTIs on random networks.

2
Some inequalities involving multiplicative topological indices

We obtain in this section several analytical inequalities involving general multiplicative topological indices
X_{\Pi}
.
There are many papers studying particular multiplicative topological indices (see, e.g.,
GutmanMilovanovic
,
RetiGutman
and the references therein).

The following equalities are direct:

\displaystyle X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)

\displaystyle=\prod_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})=e^{\sum_{u\in V(G)}\log F_{V}(d_{u})}=e^{X_{\Sigma,\log F_{V}}(G)},

\displaystyle X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)

\displaystyle=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})=e^{\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\log F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})}=e^{X_{\Sigma,\log F_{E}}(G)}.

Since the geometric mean is at most the arithmetic mean (by Jensen’s inequality), we have the following inequalities.

Proposition 1
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges.
Then

\displaystyle X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G),\qquad X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{1/m}\leq\frac{1}{m}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G).

In
BQRS
appears the following Jensen-type inequality.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on the space
X
and
a\leq b
real constants.
If
f:X\rightarrow[a,b]
is a measurable function and
\varphi
is a convex function on
[a,b]
, then

f
and
\varphi\circ f
are
\mu
-integrable functions and

\varphi\Big(a+b-\int_{X}f\,d\mu\Big)\leq\varphi(a)+\varphi(b)-\int_{X}\varphi\circ f\,d\mu.

Theorem
2
allows to find the following converse version of Proposition
1
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges,

a_{V}\leq b_{V}
,
a_{E}\leq b_{E}
be real constants and
F_{V}
,
F_{E}
be functions satisfying

a_{V}\leq F_{V}(d_{u})\leq b_{V}\quad\forall\,u\in V(G),\qquad a_{E}\leq F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})\leq b_{E}\quad\forall\,uv\in E(G).

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{V}}+e^{b_{V}}-\frac{e^{a_{V}+b_{V}}}{X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{1/n}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{E}}+e^{b_{E}}-\frac{e^{a_{E}+b_{E}}}{X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{1/m}}\,.

Proof.

Since
\varphi(x)=e^{x}
is a convex function on
\mathbb{R}
,
Theorem
2
gives

\displaystyle e^{a_{V}+b_{V}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V(G)}\log F_{V}(d_{u})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{V}}+e^{b_{V}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V(G)}e^{\log F_{V}(d_{u})},

\displaystyle e^{a_{V}+b_{V}}e^{-\log(\Pi_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u}))^{1/n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{V}}+e^{b_{V}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u}),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{a_{V}+b_{V}}}{X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{1/n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{V}}+e^{b_{V}}-\frac{1}{n}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G).

In a similar way,

\displaystyle e^{a_{E}+b_{E}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\log F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{E}}+e^{b_{E}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{uv\in E(G)}e^{\log F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})},

\displaystyle e^{a_{E}+b_{E}}e^{-\log(\Pi_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v}))^{1/n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{E}}+e^{b_{E}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v}),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{a_{E}+b_{E}}}{X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{1/m}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{a_{E}}+e^{b_{E}}-\frac{1}{m}\,X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G).

∎

The following Kober’s inequalities in
Kober
(see also
(
ZGA
, Lemma 1)
) are useful.

Lemma 1
.

If
a_{j}>0
for
1\leq j\leq k
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}+k(k-1)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{1/k}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{a_{j}}\Big)^{2}\leq(k-1)\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}+k\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{1/k}.

Lemma
1
has the following consequence.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges.
Then

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{V}^{2}}(G)+n(n-1)X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{2/n}

\displaystyle\leq X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)^{2}\leq(n-1)X_{\Sigma,F_{V}^{2}}(G)+nX_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{2/n},

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{E}^{2}}(G)+m(m-1)X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{2/m}

\displaystyle\leq X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)^{2}\leq(m-1)X_{\Sigma,F_{E}^{2}}(G)+mX_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{2/m}.

Proof.

Lemma
1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})^{2}+n(n-1)\Big(\prod_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})^{2}\Big)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})\Big)^{2},

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{V}^{2}}(G)+n(n-1)X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{2/n}

\displaystyle\leq X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)^{2},

and

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})^{2}+n\Big(\prod_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})^{2}\Big)^{1/n},

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)X_{\Sigma,F_{V}^{2}}(G)+nX_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)^{2/n}.

In a similar way,

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})^{2}+m(m-1)\Big(\prod_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})^{2}\Big)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})\Big)^{2},

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{E}^{2}}(G)+m(m-1)X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{2/m}

\displaystyle\leq X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)^{2},

and

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})^{2}+m\Big(\prod_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})^{2}\Big)^{1/m},

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)X_{\Sigma,F_{E}^{2}}(G)+mX_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)^{2/m}.

∎

Let us recall the following known Petrović inequality
Petrovic
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\varphi
be a convex function on
[0,a]
, and
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\geq 0
.
If
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in[0,a]
satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}w_{k}\in(0,a]
, and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}w_{k}\geq t_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi(t_{k})w_{k}\leq\varphi\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}w_{k}\Big)+\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}-1\Big)\varphi(0).

Theorem
5
has the following consequence.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\varphi
be a convex function on
[0,a]
.
If
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in[0,a]
satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\in(0,a]
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi(t_{k})\leq\varphi\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\Big)+(n-1)\varphi(0).

Proposition
6
allows to prove the following inequalities.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges.
Then

X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)\leq X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)+n-1,\quad X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)\leq X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)+m-1.

Proof.

Let us consider the convex function
\varphi(x)=e^{x}
.
Proposition
6
gives

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{V}}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})=\sum_{u\in V(G)}e^{\log F_{V}(d_{u})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sum_{u\in V(G)}\log F_{V}(d_{u})}+n-1=e^{\log\Pi_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})}+n-1

\displaystyle=\Pi_{u\in V(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})+n-1=X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)+n-1.

In a similar way,

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma,F_{E}}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}e^{\log F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\log F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})}+m-1=e^{\log\Pi_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})}+m-1

\displaystyle=\Pi_{uv\in E(G)}F_{E}(d_{u},d_{v})+m-1=X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)+m-1.

∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Then

X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)\geq X_{\Sigma,\log F_{V}}(G)+1,\qquad X_{\Pi,F_{E}}(G)\geq X_{\Sigma,\log F_{E}}(G)+1.

Proof.

The equality
e^{x}\geq x+1
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
This inequality gives

\displaystyle X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)

\displaystyle=e^{\log X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)}\geq\log X_{\Pi,F_{V}}(G)+1

\displaystyle=\log\Big(\Pi_{u\in E(G)}F_{V}(d_{u})\Big)+1=\sum_{u\in E(G)}\log F_{V}(d_{u})+1

\displaystyle=X_{\Sigma,\log F_{V}}(G)+1.

The same argument gives the second inequality.
∎

3
Statistical properties of multiplicative topological indices on random networks

3.1
Multiplicative topological indices on Erdös-Rényi random networks

Before computing MTIs on ER random networks we note that in the
dense limit, i.e. when
\left<d\right>\gg 1
, we can approximate
d_{u}\approx d_{v}\approx\left<d\right>

in Eqs. (
9
-
16
), with

\left<d\right>=(n-1)p.

(17)

Thus, for example, when
np\gg 1
, we can approximate
NK_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})
as

NK(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})=\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}\approx\prod_{u\in V(G)}\left<d\right>\approx\left<d\right>^{n},

which leads us to

\ln NK(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})\approx n\ln\left<d\right>

or

\frac{\ln NK(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx\ln\left<d\right>.

(18)

A similar approximation gives

\frac{\ln\Pi_{1}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx 2\ln\left<d\right>.

(19)

Also, for
\Pi_{2}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})
we have that

\Pi_{2}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}d_{v}\approx\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\left<d\right>\left<d\right>=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\left<d\right>^{2}\approx\left<d\right>^{n\left<d\right>},

where we have used
\mid E(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})\mid=n\left<d\right>/2
.
Therefore

\ln\Pi_{2}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})\approx n\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>

or

\frac{\ln\Pi_{2}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>.

(20)

Following this procedure, for the rest of the MTIs of Eqs. (
12
-
16
) we get:

\frac{\ln\Pi_{1}^{*}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx\frac{1}{2}\left<d\right>\ln(2\left<d\right>),

(21)

\frac{\ln R_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx-\frac{1}{2}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(22)

\frac{\ln H_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx-\frac{1}{2}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(23)

\frac{\ln\chi_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx-\frac{\ln 2}{4}\left<d\right>-\frac{1}{4}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(24)

and

\frac{\ln ID_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})}{n}\approx\frac{\ln 2}{2}\left<d\right>-\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>.

(25)

From the approximate expressions above we note that the logarithm of the MTIs
on ER random networks, normalized to the network size

to depend on

-

3.2
Multiplicative topological indices on random geometric graphs

As in the previous Subsection, here we start by exploring the dense limit.
Indeed, for RG graphs in the dense limit, i.e. when

d_{u}\approx d_{v}\approx\left<d\right>

\left<d\right>=(n-1)g(r)

\frac{1}{3}-2r^{2}\left[1-\arcsin(1/r)+\arccos(1/r)\right]\\
\qquad+\frac{4}{3}(2r^{2}+1)\sqrt{r^{2}-1}-\frac{1}{2}r^{4},&1\leq r\leq\sqrt{2}\,.\end{array}\right.

(27)

(30)

(31)

\frac{\ln R_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}})}{n}\approx-\frac{1}{2}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(32)

\frac{\ln H_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}})}{n}\approx-\frac{1}{2}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(33)

\frac{\ln\chi_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}})}{n}\approx-\frac{\ln 2}{4}\left<d\right>-\frac{1}{4}\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>,

(34)

and

\frac{\ln ID_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}})}{n}\approx\frac{\ln 2}{2}\left<d\right>-\left<d\right>\ln\left<d\right>.

(35)

4
Conclusions

In this work we have performed a thorough analytical and statistical study of multiplicative topological indices (MTIs)
on random networks.
As models of random networks we have used
Erdös-Rényi networks, random geometric graphs, and bipartite random networks.

From an analytical viewpoint, we find several inequalities that relate multiplicative indices to their additive versions.

Within a statistical approach, we show that the average logarithm of the MTIs,

\left<\ln X_{\Pi}(G)\right>
, normalized to the order of the network, scale with
the network average degree
\left<k\right>
.
Thus, we conclude that
\left<k\right>
is the parameter that fixes the average values of the
logarithm of the MTIs on random networks. Moreover, the equivalence among
Eqs. (
18
-
25
) for Erdös-Rényi networks,
Eqs. (
28
-
35
) for random geometric graphs, and
Eqs. (
37
-
42
) for bipartite random networks (when the subsets are equal in size)
allows us to state a scaling law that connects the three graph models.
That is, given the multiplicative topological index
X_{\Pi}
, the average of its logarithm divided by the
network size is the same function of the average degree regardless the network model:

\frac{\left<\ln X_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny ER}})\right>}{n}\approx\frac{\left<\ln X_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny RG}})\right>}{n}\approx\frac{\left<\ln X_{\Pi}(G_{\mbox{\tiny BR}})\right>}{n}\approx f(\left<k\right>).

(43)

To validate Eq. (
43
) we choose the Narumi-Katayama index, see Eq. (
9
),
apply it to Erdös-Rényi networks, random geometric graphs and bipartite random networks and plot

\left<\ln NK_{\Pi}(G)\right>/n
in Fig.
7
(a). There, we can clearly see that the curves corresponding
to the three network models fall one on top of the other, thus validating Eq. (
43
). Also notice that we are
using networks of different sizes to stress the scaling law in Eq. (
43
).
We observe the same scaling behavior in all the other MTIs of Eqs. (
10
-
16
), as can be seen
in Figs.

\left<\ln\chi_{\Pi}(G)\right>/n
and
\left<\ln ID_{\Pi}(G)\right>/n
,
respectively.

We hope that our work may motivate further analytical as well as computational studies of

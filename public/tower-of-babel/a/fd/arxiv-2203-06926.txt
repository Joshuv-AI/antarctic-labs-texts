Low c c -Differential Uniformity of the Swapped Inverse Function in Odd Characteristic
Jaeseong Jeong 1 , Namhun Koo 2 , Soonhak Kwon 1 Email: wotjd012321@naver.com, nhkoo@ewha.ac.kr, shkwon@skku.edu 1 Applied Algebra and Optimization Research Center, Sungkyunkwan University, Suwon, Korea 2 Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Ewha Womans University, Seoul, Korea

Abstract
The study of Boolean functions with low c c -differential uniformity has become recently an important topic of research. However, in odd characteristic case, there are not many results on the ( c c -)differential uniformity of functions that are not power functions. In this paper, we investigate the c c -differential uniformity of the swapped inverse functions in odd characteristic, and show that their c c -differential uniformities are at most 6 except for some special case. Keywords. c c -Differential Uniformity, Differential Uniformity, Permutation, Mathematics Subject Classification(2020) 94A60, 06E30

(

Proposition 1.2
.

We call an affine permutation of the form

a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}

and
A_{2}
are affine permutations of degree one. If two functions

\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

{}_{c}\Delta_{F}={}_{c}\Delta_{F^{\prime}}

Proof.

We write
F=A_{1}\circ F^{\prime}\circ A_{2}
and
F^{\prime\prime}=F^{\prime}\circ A_{2}
, where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are affine permutations of degree one.
F^{\prime}
and

c
-differential uniformity in even characteristic
[

[
7

F=A_{1}\circ F^{\prime\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime}
has the same
c
-differential uniformity. We denote
A_{1}(x)=a_{1}x+a_{0}
where
a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
and
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
implies that

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=F(x+a)-cF(x)=(A_{1}\circ F^{\prime\prime})(x+a)-c(A_{1}\circ F^{\prime\prime})(x)=A_{1}(F^{\prime\prime}(x+a))-cA_{1}(F^{\prime\prime}(x))

\displaystyle=a_{1}F^{\prime\prime}(x+a)+a_{0}-c(a_{1}F^{\prime\prime}(x)+a_{0})=a_{1}(F^{\prime\prime}(x+a)-cF^{\prime\prime}(x))+a_{0}(1-c).

So we have
{}_{c}D_{a}F^{\prime\prime}(x)=a_{1}^{-1}\left(b+a_{0}(c-1)\right)

{}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)={}_{c}\Delta_{F^{\prime\prime}}\left(a,a_{1}^{-1}\left(b+a_{0}(c-1)\right)\right)

∎

\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi(-1)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\

&\\

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}

D=a_{1}^{2}-4a_{0}a_{2}

\#\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}=0\}=\begin{cases}2&\text{ if }\chi(D)=1,\\
1&\text{ if }\chi(D)=0,\\
0&\text{ if }\chi(D)=-1.\\
\end{cases}

The following lemma is a generalization of Lemma 2.6 in
[
11
]
to the odd characteristic case. This lemma is very simple and useful to our results.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
c\neq 0
. Then
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)={}_{c^{-1}}\Delta_{F}(-a,-bc^{-1})
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, and hence
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}={}_{c^{-1}}\Delta_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then, it is clear that
x
is a solution of
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
if and only if

is a solution of
{}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}(x)=-bc^{-1}
. Hence we obtain
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)={}_{c^{-1}}\Delta_{F}(-a,-bc^{-1})
. Therefore, we get
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}={}_{c^{-1}}\Delta_{F}

∎

{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Under the same assumption as in (
1
) if
p^{n}>5
then
3\leq{}_{c}\Delta_{F}\leq 5
. Moreover,
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}=5
if and only if at least one of the followings are satisfied :

(i)
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
and
c\in\{-2,-2^{-1}\}

(ii)
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and one of the followings holds :

•

c\in\left\{\sqrt{-3},\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}\right\}
,
\sqrt{-3}\neq 5
, and
\frac{-3+5\sqrt{-3}}{6}

•

c\in\left\{-\sqrt{-3},-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}\right\}
,
\sqrt{-3}\neq-5
, and
\frac{-3-5\sqrt{-3}}{6}

(iii)
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}

•

c^{2}+4c-1=0
, and
7-30c
is a nonzero square.

•

c^{2}-4c-1=0
, and
7-30c^{-1}
is a nonzero square.

Proof.

The case when
a=\pm 1
is covered by Lemma
3.3
and we have (i). Hence it remains to show (ii) and (iii) when
a\neq\pm 1
. We can see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)=F(a)-cF(0)=a^{-1}-c,&{}_{c}D_{a}F(1)=F(1+a)-cF(1)=(1+a)^{-1},\\
{}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)=F(0)-cF(-a)=1+ca^{-1},&{}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)=F(1)-cF(1-a)=c(a-1)^{-1}.\end{array}

(2)

We investigate all possible cases that
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
has at least two solutions in
P_{a}
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1)\Leftrightarrow ca^{2}+ca-1=0,&{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)\Leftrightarrow a=\frac{1-c}{c+1}\text{ when }c\neq\pm 1,\\
{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)\Leftrightarrow ca^{2}-a+1=0,&{}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}+ca+c=0,\\
{}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)\Leftrightarrow a=\frac{c+1}{1-c}\text{ when }c\neq\pm 1,&{}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}-a-c=0.\end{array}

If
{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)
then
a=\frac{1-c}{c+1}=\frac{c+1}{1-c}
and hence we have
c=0
, a contradiction. Hence
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
has at most three solutions in
P_{a}
. We investigate all possible cases that
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
has three solutions in
P_{a}
.

•

If
{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)
then
a=\frac{1-c}{c+1}
is a solution of two equations
ca^{2}+ca-1=0
and
a^{2}+ca+c=0
. Then we have
0=ca^{2}+ca-1=c\left(\frac{1-c}{c+1}\right)^{2}+c\cdot\frac{1-c}{c+1}-1=-\frac{3c^{2}+1}{(c+1)^{2}}
and
0=a^{2}+ca+c=\left(\frac{1-c}{c+1}\right)^{2}+c\cdot\frac{1-c}{c+1}+c=\frac{3c^{2}+1}{(c+1)^{2}}
. Such
c
exists when
-3
is a nonzero square, if and only if
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, and then
c=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}
. Then
a=\frac{\sqrt{-3}\mp 1}{\sqrt{-3}\pm 1}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}
and
b={}_{c}D_{a}F(0)=a^{-1}-c=\frac{2}{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}=\frac{3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}
. Since

(ab+c-1)^{2}-4abc=\left(\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}\cdot\frac{3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}-1\right)^{2}\mp 4\cdot\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}\cdot\frac{3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}=\left(\frac{-3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}\right)^{2}+\frac{-2\pm 2\sqrt{-3}}{3}=\frac{-3\pm 5\sqrt{-3}}{6}
. Using
b+c=\frac{3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}\mp\frac{\sqrt{-3}}{3}=\frac{1\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}
, we have
a(b+c)+b+c+1=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}\cdot\frac{1\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}+\frac{1\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}+1=\frac{5\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}
and
(1-b)a+b+c-1=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{-3}}{6}\cdot\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}+\frac{1\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}-1=\frac{-3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}\neq 0
. So, we have
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)=5
if and only if
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,
\frac{5\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}\neq 0
, and
\frac{-3\pm 5\sqrt{-3}}{6}
is a square. Note that all double signs are in same order in above computations this case.

•

If
{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)
then
a=\frac{c+1}{1-c}
is a solution of two equations
ca^{2}+ca-1=0
and

. Then we have
0=c\cdot\left(\frac{c+1}{1-c}\right)^{2}+c\cdot\frac{c+1}{1-c}-1=\frac{c^{2}+4c-1}{(1-c)^{2}}
and

, and hence

. Such
c
exists if
(-4)^{2}-4\cdot(-1)=20=2^{2}\cdot 5
is a square, if and only if
p=5
or
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
. We set
b={}_{c}D_{a}F(0)=a^{-1}-c=\frac{1-c}{c+1}-c=\frac{1-2c-c^{2}}{c+1}
. Using the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we have
(ab+c-1)^{2}-4abc=c^{2}(a+1)^{2}-4c=c^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{1-c}\right)^{2}-4c=-\frac{4(c^{3}-3c^{2}+c)}{(c-1)^{2}}=-\frac{4((c-7)(c^{2}+4c-1)+30c-7)}{(c-1)^{2}}=-\frac{4(30c-7)}{(c-1)^{2}}
and
(b+c)a+b+c+1=a^{-1}\neq 0
and
(1-b)a+b+c-1=a^{-1}((c+1)a^{2}-2a+1)=a^{-1}\left((c+1)\cdot\left(\frac{c+1}{1-c}\right)^{2}-2\cdot\frac{c+1}{1-c}+1\right)=\frac{c(c^{2}+6c+1)}{a(c-1)^{2}}=\frac{2c(c+1)}{a(c-1)^{2}}\neq 0

. So, we have
{}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)=5
if and only if
p^{n}\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
and
7-30c
is a nonzero square. If

then we have

, and

is a square if and only if

and hence this case is already covered by (i).

•

If
{}_{c}D_{a}F(0)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)
then we have
{}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(0)={}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(1)={}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(-(-a))
by Lemma
2.2
. We set
c^{-1}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{-3}}
and
-a=\frac{1-c^{-1}}{c^{-1}+1}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}
or equivalently
c=\pm\sqrt{-3}
and
a=-\frac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}
, and
b=(-a)^{-1}-c^{-1}=\frac{3\mp\sqrt{-3}}{6}
. By the above observation and Lemma

,

if and only if
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,
\frac{5\mp\sqrt{-3}}{2}\neq 0
, and
\frac{-3\pm 5\sqrt{-3}}{6}
is a square.

•

If
{}_{c}D_{a}F(1)={}_{c}D_{a}F(-a)={}_{c}D_{a}F(1-a)
then we have
{}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(0)={}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(1)={}_{c^{-1}}D_{-a}F(1-(-a))
by Lemma
2.2
. We assume that
(c^{-1})^{2}+4c^{-1}-1=0
and
-a=\frac{c^{-1}+1}{1-c^{-1}}
or equivalently
c^{2}-4c-1=0
and
a=\frac{1+c}{1-c}
, and
b=-a^{-1}-c^{-1}=\frac{1-2c^{-1}-(c^{-1})^{2}}{1+c^{-1}}
. By the above observation and Lemma
2.2
,
5={}_{c^{-1}}\Delta_{F}(-a,b)={}_{c}\Delta_{F}(a,b)
if and only if
p\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
and
7-30c^{-1}
is a nonzero square. Similarly with the above case, the case
p=5
can be covered by (i).

Conversely, suppose each condition in (ii) and (iii) is satisfied. Using the same
a
and
b
in previous analysis, we can see that
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
has three solutions in

. By Lemma

, the given element is a nonzero square, then
{}_{c}D_{a}F(x)=b
has two solutions in

. Hence we have

∎

•

when

•

If

4.1
The differential uniformity

In this subsection we investigate the differential uniformity of
F
, that is, the case

. We first investigate the number of solutions of

in
P_{a}
when

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\{0,\pm 1,\pm\gamma,\pm(\gamma-1)\}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then,
D_{a}F(x)=b
has four solutions in
P_{a}
if and only if
\gamma=-1
and at least one of the followings is satisfied:

(i)
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
,
a^{4}-3a^{2}+1=0
and
b=a^{-1}
,

(ii)
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
,
a^{2}=2
and
b=a
.

Otherwise,
D_{a}F(x)=b
has at most three solutions.

Proof.

D_{a}F(x)
for each
x\in P_{a}
is as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{a}F(0)=F(a)-F(0)=a^{-1},&D_{a}F(-a)=F(0)-F(-a)=a^{-1},\\
D_{a}F(1)=F(1+a)-F(1)=(a+1)^{-1}-\gamma^{-1},&D_{a}F(1-a)=F(1)-F(1-a)=\gamma^{-1}-(1-a)^{-1},\\
D_{a}F(\gamma)=F(\gamma+a)-F(\gamma)=(a+\gamma)^{-1}-1,&D_{a}F(\gamma-a)=F(\gamma)-F(\gamma-a)=1+(a-\gamma)^{-1}.\end{array}

We first assume that
b=D_{a}F(0)=D_{a}F(-a)=a^{-1}
. We investigate
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has at least three solutions in
P_{a}
. First we investigate equivalent condition for each case that
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has at least two solutions in
P_{a}
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}a^{-1}=D_{a}F(1)\ \Leftrightarrow\ a^{2}+a+\gamma=0,&a^{-1}=D_{a}F(1-a)\ \Leftrightarrow\ a^{2}-a+\gamma=0,\\
a^{-1}=D_{a}F(\gamma)\ \Leftrightarrow\ a^{2}+\gamma a+\gamma=0,&a^{-1}=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)\ \Leftrightarrow\ a^{2}-\gamma a+\gamma=0.\end{array}

We can easily see that if
a^{-1}=D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(1-a)
or
a^{-1}=D_{a}F(\gamma)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)
then we have
a=0
, a contradiction. Hence
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has at most four solutions. If
a^{-1}=D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)
then we have
\gamma=-1
and hence
a^{2}+a-1=0
, such
a
exists if and only if
1^{2}-4\cdot(-1)=5
is a square which is equivalent to
p^{n}\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
. But if
p=5
then we have
a=2=1-\gamma
which contradicts to assumption, and hence we have
p\neq 5
. Similarly, if
a^{-1}=D_{a}F(\gamma)=D_{a}F(1-a)
we also have
\gamma=-1
and hence
a^{2}-a-1=0
, such
a
exists if and only if
(-1)^{2}-4\cdot(-1)=5
is a square, and we have
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
. Hence,
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has four solutions if and only if
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
,
\gamma=-1
and
0=(a^{2}+a-1)(a^{2}-a-1)=a^{4}-3a^{2}+1
. Otherwise,
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has at most three solutions. Conversely, if
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
then there exists
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with
a^{2}+a-1=0
or
a^{2}-a-1=0
and then by the above analysis we can see that
D_{a}F(x)=a^{-1}
has four solutions in
P_{a}
when
\gamma=-1
.

Next we investigate the case
b\neq a^{-1}
. In this case,
D_{a}F(x)=b
has four solutions in
P_{a}
if and only if
b=D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(\gamma)=D_{a}F(1-a)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)
. First we investigate equivalent condition for each case that
D_{a}F(x)=b
has at least two solutions in
\{1,\gamma,1-a,\gamma-a\}
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(\gamma)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}+(\gamma+1)a+2\gamma=0,&D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)\Leftrightarrow a(\gamma+1)(a-\gamma+1)a=0,\\
D_{a}F(1)=D_{a}F(1-a)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}+\gamma-1=0,&D_{a}F(\gamma)=D_{a}F(1-a)\Leftrightarrow a(\gamma+1)(a+\gamma-1)a=0,\\
D_{a}F(\gamma)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}+\gamma(1-\gamma)=0,&D_{a}F(1-a)=D_{a}F(\gamma-a)\Leftrightarrow a^{2}-(\gamma+1)a+2\gamma=0.\end{array}

From
a^{2}+(\gamma+1)a+2\gamma=0
and
a^{2}-(\gamma+1)a+2\gamma=0
we have
\gamma=-1
. Then we have
a^{2}=2
, such
a
exists if and only if
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
. Then we have
b=D_{a}F(1)=\frac{1}{a+1}+1=\frac{a+2}{a+1}=\frac{a^{2}+a-2}{a^{2}-1}=a
. Conversely, if
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{8}
then there exists
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with
a^{2}=2
and then by the above analysis we can see that
D_{a}F(x)=a
has four solutions in
P_{a}
, when
\gamma=-1
.
∎

Next we show the main theorem of this subsection.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
F=Inv\circ(1,\gamma)
with
\gamma\neq 0,1
. Then,

(i)
\Delta_{F}=7
if and only if
\gamma=-1
,
p=5
and
n
is even.

(ii)
\Delta_{F}=6
if and only if
\gamma=-1
, and
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{8}
or
p^{n}\equiv 1,4\pmod{15}
.

Otherwise,
\Delta_{F}\leq 5
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
we investigate all the cases that
D_{a}F(x)=b
has at least four solutions, which requires
\gamma=-1
. By Lemma
4.1
with
c=1
and
\gamma=-1
,
D_{a}F(x)=b
has two solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus P_{a}
if and only if
(ab)^{2}-4ab
is a nonzero square,
(b+1)a+b=ab+a+b\neq 0
and
(1-b)a+b=a+b-ab\neq 0
.

•

If
p=5
and
(a,b)\in\{(2,3),(3,2)\}
then

has five solutions in
P_{a}
. Then
(ab)^{2}-4ab=1^{2}-4\cdot 1=-3
is a square if and only if

which is equivalent to
n
is even. And, we have
ab+a+b=1\neq 0
and
a+b-ab=-1\neq 0
. Hence we have
\Delta_{F}(a,b)=7
if
n
is even,
\Delta_{F}(a,b)=5
if
n
is odd.

•

If
p=5
and
(a,b)\in\{(1,-1),(-1,1)\}
then
D_{a}F(x)=b
has four solutions in
P_{a}
. Then we have
(ab)^{2}-4ab=1^{2}+4\cdot 1=5=0

•

If
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
and
a^{4}-3a^{2}+1=(a^{2}+a-1)(a^{2}-a-1)=0
then
D_{a}(x)=a^{-1}
has four solutions in
P_{a}
. Then we require
(ab)^{2}-4ab=1^{2}-4\cdot 1=-3
is a nonzero square if and only if

. If
a^{2}+a-1=0
then

and
a+b-ab=a^{-1}(a^{2}-a+1)=2a^{-1}(1-a)\neq 0
since

. Similarly, if

then
ab+a+b\neq 0
and

. Hence we have
\Delta_{F}(a,a^{-1})=6

, when

and

, or equivalently

.

•

If
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
and
a^{2}=2
then
D_{a}(x)=a
has four solutions in
P_{a}
. Then we have
(ab)^{2}-4ab=a^{4}-4a^{2}=4-8=-4
is a square if and only if

. Since
a\neq 1
from

, we have

and

. Hence we have
\Delta_{F}(a,a)=6
where
a^{2}=2
, when
p^{n}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
and

, or equivalently
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{8}
.

Otherwise,
D_{a}F(x)=b
at most three solutions in
P_{a}
by Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, and hence we have

by Lemma

.
∎

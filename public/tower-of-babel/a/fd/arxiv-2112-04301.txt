On Generalized quasi-Einstein Manifolds
Antonio Airton Freitas Filho, Keti Tenenblat

Abstract
A rigidity result for a class of compact generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds with constant scalar curvature is obtained. Moreover, under some geometric assumptions, the rigidity for the noncompact case is also proved. Considering non constant scalar curvature, we characterize the generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds which are conformal to the Euclidean space and we show that there exist two classes of complete manifolds, which are obtained by considering potential functions and conformal factors either to be radial or invariant under the action of an (n-1) dimensional translation group. Explicit examples are given. Keywords: Generalized quasi-Einstein Manifolds, Einstein type manifolds, Rigidity results, Conformally flat complete manifolds. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: Primary: 53C25, 53C44, 53C24; Secondary: 53C21.

Introduction

The investigation of Einstein manifolds and their generalizations has been of great interest throughout recent years. In this paper, we study gradient generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds. This concept was introduced by Catino
[
5
]
as follow.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 3
. We denote by
Ric
and
S
its Ricci tensor and scalar curvature, respectively. We say that
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
is a
gradient generalized quasi-Einstein manifold
or
(M^{n},g)
supports a gradient generalized quasi-Einstein structure if there exist smooth real functions
f,\ \lambda
and
\nu
defined on
M
, such that

\displaystyle Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\nu df\otimes df=\lambda g,

(1.1)

where
\nabla^{2}f
stands for the Hessian of
f
. We refer to
f
as the
potential function
. We say that the generalized quasi-Einstein manifold is trivial when
f
is constant.

In
[
5
]
, Catino proved that a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold, with harmonic Weyl tensor and with vanishing radial Weyl curvature, is locally a warped product, with
(n-1)
-dimensional Einstein fibers.

Moreover, Catino et al.
[
6
]
also introduced the notion of an Einstein-type structure on a Riemannian manifold, unifying various cases studied recently, such as gradient Ricci solitons, Yamabe solitons and quasi-Einstein manifolds (see
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
20
]
). A Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is said to be a
gradient Einstein-type manifold
if there exist
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and constants

\alpha,\beta,\mu,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, with
(\alpha,\beta,\mu)\neq(0,0,0)
, such that

\displaystyle\alpha Ric+\beta\nabla^{2}f+\mu df\otimes df

\displaystyle=(\rho S+\lambda)g,

(1.2)

where
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

There is an intersection between those two notions. In fact, given a gradient
generalized quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
),
if
\nu
is a constant function
and
\lambda

is a linear function of the scalar curvature then, it is a gradient Einstein-type manifold. On the other hand, given a gradient Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.2
), if the constants
\alpha
and
\beta
are non zero, then dividing by
\alpha
and considering

\tilde{f}=\beta f/\alpha
,
\tilde{\nu}=-{\mu\alpha}/{\beta^{2}}
and
\tilde{\lambda}=(\rho S+\lambda)/\alpha
, we have a gradient generalized quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying
Ric+\nabla^{2}\tilde{f}-\tilde{\nu}d\tilde{f}\otimes d\tilde{f}=\tilde{\lambda}g
.

A Ricci-Hessian type manifold is another example of structure that generalizes an Einstein manifold and it is related to the construction of gradient Ricci (almost) soliton warped product (see
[
8
,
9
]
). For a complete characterization of gradient Ricci almost solitons on semi-Riemannian warped product see
[
3
]
.

Under certain conditions compact Riemannian manifolds of constant scalar curvature are isometric to a standard sphere, for example, Gomes
[
10
]
proved that a compact gradient Einstein-type manifold of constant scalar curvature is isometric to the standard sphere with a well defined potential function. The Yamabe problem assures that a compact smooth manifold admits a Riemannian metric of constant scalar curvature
[
21
]
.
Compact Einstein manifolds emerge as critical points of the total scalar curvature functional restricted to the Riemannian metrics of volume
1

[
1
]
.

This paper contributes in two directions. The first one provides rigidity results for complete quasi-Einstein type manifolds with constant scalar curvature
S
. The other one provides classes of complete quasi-Einstein type manifolds, conformal to the Euclidean space, with non constant curvature
S
.

We obtain rigidity results for compact (and noncompact) generalized quasi-Einstein type manifolds with constant scalar curvature, similar to those obtained in
[
10
]
for the generalized Einstein-type manifolds. assume an additional condition, which is motivated by a necessary property
satisfied by any generalized quasi-Einstein structure (see Proposition
2.1

\displaystyle d\|\nabla f\|^{2}\wedge d\nu\wedge df=0,

(1.3)

where
f
and
\nu
are the functions satisfying (
1.1
).

Considering (
1.3
), if
\nu
is not a constant function, we assume that
\nu
is a function
of
f
, i.e. ,
\nu=v\circ f
for some smooth function

where
I=f(M)
.
This same assumption was considered in
[
19

(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
, when the function

is a positive function of the potential

In order to state our rigidity results, Theorems

and
1.2
,
assuming that

\phi:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\phi(t)=c_{1}\int^{t}\left(e^{-\int^{s}v(r)dr}\right)ds+c_{2},

(1.4)

.

Our first rigidity result is a characterization of the standard sphere.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
be a compact generalized quasi-Einstein manifold of constant scalar curvature

and
\nu=v\circ f
for some smooth function

Then,

is isometric to the standard sphere

\phi
is given by (
1.4

is the height function on the unit sphere

For the noncompact complete case, we consider a geometric assumption motivated by Karp’s theorem (see Section

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)

Consider the geodesic ball

In a recent paper,
[
11

Gomes-Wang-Xia considered the

With this result in mind, we recall that if
(M^{n},g),

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature
S
. Assume that
\overline{g}=\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}g
is an Einstein metric on
M^{n}
where
\varphi
is a positive smooth function. Let
B(r)
be the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at some fixed point
x_{\circ}\in M
. In addition suppose that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\liminf_{r\to\infty}}\frac{1}{r}\int\limits_{B(2r)\backslash B(r)}\|\mathring{Ric}(\nabla\varphi)\|dvol_{g}=0.

(1.6)

Then,
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold with
S\leq 0
and
\varphi
has at most one stationary point. More precisely,
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to one the following manifolds:

(i)

a Riemannian Product
\mathbb{R}\times N^{n-1}.

(ii)

the Euclidean Space
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)

the hyperbolic Space
\mathbb{H}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n(n-1)}{|S|}}\right).

(iv)

a Warped Product
\left(\mathbb{R}\times N^{n-1},dt^{2}+e^{2\sqrt{\frac{|S|}{n(n-1)}}t}g_{N}\right),
where
(N^{n-1},g_{N})
is a Riemannian Einstein manifold.

The rigidity results stated above, require constant scalar curvature. If we release this requirement, then we obtain the following theorems, which prove the existence of classes of complete generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds, conformal to the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{\circ})
, where
g_{\circ}
is the standard metric. These manifolds are obtained by considering the conformal factor and the potential function to be either radial or invariant under the action of an
(n-1)
dimensional translation group.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
,
n\geq 3
, be a conformally flat Riemannian manifold, where
g=g_{\circ}/\varphi^{2}
.
Assume that
\varphi(r)
and
f(r)
are radial functions of
r=\|x\|^{2}
, such that
\varphi
does not vanish and
f
is strictly monotone. Then
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
is a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold if, and only if,
\lambda
and
\nu
are also functions of
r
which are given by

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}\left[(n-2)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi}+f^{\prime\prime}+2f^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\varphi}\right],

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lambda=4\ [(n-1)\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi-r\varphi^{\prime})+r\varphi(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime})]+2f^{\prime}\varphi^{2}.

(1.8)

In this case,

S=4(n-1)[2r\varphi\varphi^{\prime\prime}-nr(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+n\varphi\varphi^{\prime}].

(1.9)

Moreover, if
\varphi
is a bounded function then the manifold is complete.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
be a conformally flat Riemannian manifold, where

g=g_{\circ}/\varphi^{2}
and
\varphi(u)
is a non vanishing function of
u=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}x_{k}
, where
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are such that
a=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{2}\neq 0
.
Let
f(u)
be a strictly monotone function. Then there exist
\nu(u)
and

\lambda(u)
such that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
is a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold if, and only if,

\nu=\frac{1}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}\left[(n-2)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi}+f^{\prime\prime}+2f^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\varphi}\right],

(1.10)

\lambda=a\,[\varphi\varphi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}\varphi\varphi^{\prime}-(n-1)(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}].

(1.11)

Its scalar curvature is given by

S=a(n-1)[2\varphi\varphi^{\prime\prime}-n(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}].

(1.12)

Moreover, if
\varphi
is a bounded function then the manifold is complete.

In both theorems above, the generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds, given by Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
necessarily satisfy the property that
\nu
is a function of
f
, which was required in the rigidity results. This follows from the fact that we are considering the potential function
f
to be strictly monotone. In fact, one can obtain
r
in terms of
f
and hence also
\lambda
and
\varphi
are functions of
f

Preliminaries

In this section, we present the necessary results that will be used in
Section
3
, where we prove our rigidity theorems. We begin with the proposition that motivates one of our hypothesis.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
be a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold. Then

\displaystyle d\|\nabla f\|^{2}\wedge d\nu\wedge df=0.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{div}Ric=\frac{1}{2}dS,
we compute both sides of this equation.

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}Ric

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla^{2}f\right)+\mathrm{div}\left(\nu df\otimes df\right)+\mathrm{div}\left(\lambda g\right)

\displaystyle=-Ric(\nabla f,\cdot)-d\Delta f+\nu\Delta fdf+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\nu\rangle df+d\lambda,

and since

\displaystyle Ric(\nabla f,\cdot)=-\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\nu\|\nabla f\|^{2}df+\lambda df,

we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}Ric

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}-\nu\|\nabla f\|^{2}df-\lambda df-d\Delta f

\displaystyle\hskip 10.81218pt+\nu\Delta fdf+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\nu\rangle df+d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}-d\left(\lambda f\right)+fd\lambda-d\Delta f+d\lambda

\displaystyle\hskip 10.81218pt+\mathrm{div}_{f}\left(\nu\nabla f\right)df+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2},

(2.1)

where
\mathrm{div}_{f}(X):=\mathrm{div}(X)-\langle X,\nabla f\rangle,
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Moreover, it follows from (
1.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}dS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d\left(-\Delta f+\nu\|\nabla f\|^{2}+n\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}d\Delta f+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{2}d\nu+\frac{n}{2}d\lambda.

(2.2)

Therefore, (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) imply that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}d\Delta f+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{2}d\nu+\frac{n}{2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}-d\left(\lambda f\right)+fd\lambda-d\Delta f

\displaystyle\hskip 10.81218pt+d\lambda+\mathrm{div}_{f}\left(\nu\nabla f\right)df+\frac{\nu}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2},

which reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\frac{(n-2)}{2}d\lambda+d\left(\lambda f\right)+\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{2}d\nu=\mathrm{div}_{f}\left(\nu\nabla f\right)df+fd\lambda,

i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d\left[\Delta_{f}f+(n-2)\lambda+2\lambda f\right]+\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{2}d\nu=\mathrm{div}_{f}\left(\nu\nabla f\right)df+fd\lambda.

Taking exterior derivative, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d\|\nabla f\|^{2}\wedge d\nu=d\big[\mathrm{div}_{f}(\nu\nabla f)-\lambda\big]\wedge df,

and by considering the exterior product with
\ df
we conclude that

\displaystyle d\|\nabla f\|^{2}\wedge d\nu\wedge df=0.

∎

We recall that the traceless tensor associated to a tensor
T
on
(M^{n},g)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathring{T}=T-\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{g}T}{n}g.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda,\nu)
be a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold such that
\nu=v\circ f
for some smooth function
v:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},
where
I=f(M)
and let
\phi
be given by (
1.4
). Then

\displaystyle Ric_{x}+\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\nabla^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x}=\lambda(x)g_{x},

(2.3)

for all
x\in M
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathring{Ric}_{x}=-\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x},

(2.4)

for all
x\in M
and
t=f(x)\in I.

In particular,
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold if, and only if,
\nabla(\phi\circ f)
is a conformal vector field.

Proof.

By considering the hessian of
\phi\circ f,
for
x\in M
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{2}f\right)_{x}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}\nabla^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x}-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}df_{x}\otimes df_{x},

where
\phi^{\prime}(t)=d(\phi\circ f)_{x}\neq 0
and
t=f(x)\in I
. Since
\phi
is given by (
1.4
), it follows that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)+v(t)\phi^{\prime}(t)=0
. Therefore, substituting into (
1.1
), we conclude that (
2.3
) holds.

From (
2.3
), we can easily check the identity (
2.4
).
Moreover, it also follows from (
2.3
) that
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold if, and only if,
\nabla(\phi\circ f)
is a conformal vector field.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a connected Einstein manifold,
n\geqslant 3,
and let
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function whose gradient
\nabla u
is a conformal vector field. Then

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u=\left[-\frac{Su}{n(n-1)}+c\right]g,

for some constant
c.

Proof.

Since
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold, i.e.,
Ric=\frac{S}{n}g,
then
S
is constant and since
\nabla u
is a conformal vector field we get

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u=\frac{\Delta u}{n}g.

Recall two well known facts in the literature

\displaystyle Ric(\nabla u,\cdot)+d\Delta u=\mathrm{div}(\nabla^{2}u)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \mathrm{div}(wg)=dw,

for smooth functions
u
and
w
. Taking
w=\frac{\Delta u}{n}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{S}{n}du+d\Delta u=\frac{1}{n}d\Delta u.

Thus, dividing by
n-1,
we have

\displaystyle d\left[\frac{Su}{n(n-1)}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right]=0.

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta u}{n}=-\frac{Su}{n(n-1)}+c,

for some constant
c.

∎

We conclude this section by stating Karp’s theorem, which is an extension of Stokes theorem and it will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.4

(Karp’s theorem
[
15
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold. Consider the geodesic ball
B(r)
of radius
r
centered at some fixed
x\in M^{n}
and a vector field
X
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\liminf_{r\to\infty}}\frac{1}{r}\int\limits_{B(2r)\backslash B(r)}\|X\|dvol_{g}=0.

If
\mathrm{div}X
has an integral (i.e. if either
(\mathrm{div}X)^{+}
or
(\mathrm{div}X)^{-}
is integrable), then
\int_{M}\mathrm{div}Xdvol_{g}=0
. In particular, if
\mathrm{div}X
does not change sign outside some compact set, then
\int_{M}\mathrm{div}Xdvol_{g}=0
.

3
Proof of the rigidity results

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

We consider
\phi:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by (
1.4
). Then, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that (
1.1
) is equivalent to (
2.3
). Hence, by computing
\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{2n}\langle\nabla S_{x},\nabla(\phi\circ f)_{x}\rangle+\langle Ric_{x},\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda(x)g_{x}-\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\nabla^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x},\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)_{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)\|^{2}(x),

for all
x\in M
. Therefore, since
S
is constant, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)(x)=-\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)\|^{2}(x).

(3.1)

By integrating and using the divergence theorem, since
M
is compact we conclude that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)dvol_{g}=-\int_{M}\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)\|^{2}(x)dvol_{g}(x),

which implies that
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold and
\nabla(\phi\circ f)
is a conformal vector field. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(\phi\circ f)=\left[-\frac{S(\phi\circ f)}{n(n-1)}+c\right]g.

for some constant
c
.

Since
M
is compact, Obata’s theorem
[
17
]
implies that
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}(r)
and we can write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\nabla(\phi\circ f)}g=2\psi g,

where the conformal factor
\psi=\frac{\Delta(\phi\circ f)}{n}
satisfies the equation
\Delta\psi+\frac{S}{n-1}\psi=0
(see e.g. Yano
[
22
]
).

Rescaling the metric, we can assume that
S=n(n-1)
. Hence,
\Delta(\phi\circ f)
is the first eigenfunction of
the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Therefore, there is a fixed vector
w\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that
\Delta(\phi\circ f)=h_{w}=-\frac{\Delta h_{w}}{n}
(see
[
2
]
), where
h_{w}
is the height function on the unit sphere.
Therefore,
\phi\circ f=c-\frac{h_{w}}{n}
for some constant
c
. Since
\phi
is strictly monotone,
we conclude that
f=\phi^{-1}\left(c-\frac{h_{w}}{n}\right)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

As in the previous proof, by hypothesis we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{d(\phi\circ f)_{x}}\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)\|^{2}(x),

(3.2)

for all
x\in M
. The constant
c_{1}
of (
1.4
) is nonzero, therefore
\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)
does not change sign on

.
By integrating (

), it follows from Lemma

(Karp’s theorem) that

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}(\phi\circ f)\|^{2}(x)dvol_{g}(x)=\int_{M}\mathrm{div}\big(\mathring{Ric}(\nabla(\phi\circ f))\big)dvol_{g}=0.

Thus,
\nabla(\phi\circ f)
is a conformal vector field and hence

is an Einstein manifold. Therefore, Lemma

implies that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(\phi\circ f)=\left(-\frac{S(\phi\circ f)}{n(n-1)}+c\right)g,

(3.3)

and hence the classification of

depends on the scalar curvature

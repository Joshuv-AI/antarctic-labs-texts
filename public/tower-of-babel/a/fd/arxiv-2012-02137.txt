Derived equivalences of gerbey curves
Soumya Sankar, Libby Taylor

Abstract
We study derived equivalences of certain stacks over genus 1 1 curves, which arise as connected components of the Picard stack of a genus 1 1 curve. To this end, we develop a theory of integral transforms for these algebraic stacks. We use this theory to answer the question of when two gerbey genus 1 1 curves are derived equivalent. We use integral transforms and intersection theory on stacks to answer the following questions: if C ′ = P ​ i ​ c d ​ ( C ) C^{\prime}=Pic^{d}(C) , is C = P ​ i ​ c f ​ ( C ′ ) C=Pic^{f}(C^{\prime}) for some integer f f ? If C ′ = P ​ i ​ c d ​ ( C ) C^{\prime}=Pic^{d}(C) and C ′′ = P ​ i ​ c f ​ ( C ′ ) C^{\prime\prime}=Pic^{f}(C^{\prime}) , then is C ′′ = P ​ i ​ c g ​ ( C ) C^{\prime\prime}=Pic^{g}(C) for some integer g g ?

Introduction

The derived category of a variety is a well-studied invariant. Two projective varieties
X
and
Y
over a field
k
are said to be derived equivalent if there is an equivalence
D(X)\cong D(Y)
of
k
-linear triangulated categories. Finding non-isomorphic derived equivalent varieties has become an important problem in algebraic geometry. If
\mathcal{K}\in D(X\times Y)
is a complex on
X\times Y
, then we say that the
integral transform with kernel
\mathcal{K}
is the morphism

\Phi^{\mathcal{K}}:D(X)\to D(Y)

defined by

\mathcal{E}\mapsto R\pi_{Y*}(L\pi_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{K}).

Orlov’s representability theorem in
[
20
]
proves that any fully faithful exact functor
\Psi:D(X)\to D(Y)
is an integral functor, and that the kernel is uniquely determined as an element of the derived category by
\Psi
.
For an overview, see
[
22
]
.

Bondal and Orlov show in
[
5
]
that for a variety
X
, if
\omega_{X}
is ample or anti-ample, then one can recover
X
from
D(X)
. This implies that when
X
is either Fano or of general type,
X
has no non-isomorphic derived equivalent varieties. Furthermore, any two derived equivalent varieties have the same dimension. Therefore, in the case of curves, the only nontrivial case to study is that of derived equivalences of genus
1
curves. Antieau, Krashen and Ward study in
[
3
]
the question of when two genus
1
curves are derived equivalent. In particular, they prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 2.3.)
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be genus
1
curves over any field
k
such that
D(C)\cong D(C^{\prime})
. Then
C^{\prime}
is the moduli space of degree
d
line bundles on
C
for some integer
d
, and
C\times C^{\prime}
admits a universal sheaf.

Thus a necessary condition for the derived equivalence of two curves is the existence of a universal sheaf. It turns out that the existence of such a sheaf is also sufficient, and can be phrased via a cohomological criterion.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.5)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve, and let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be principal homogeneous spaces for it. Then
D(C)\cong D(C^{\prime})
if and only if there exists an automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(E)
and an integer
a
coprime to the order of
[C^{\prime}]\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\operatorname{Spec}k,E)
such that
[C]=\phi_{*}(a[C^{\prime}])
.

The cohomological criterion gives the existence of a universal sheaf on
C\times C^{\prime}
, which in turn can be used to construct the derived equivalence. In the event that
C\times C^{\prime}
does not admit a universal sheaf (see Example
6.1
), one has to look for derived equivalences at the level of stacks instead. We extend Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to this case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a genus
1
curve defined over a field
k
of characteristic 0. Let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
be the weight 1 substack of
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
(see Subsection
2.3
and Definition
10
) for some
d>0
such that
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
are derived equivalent. Then
\mathscr{C}
is the moduli space of rank
d
degree
1-d
vector bundles on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and the universal sheaf for this moduli problem is the dual of the universal degree
d
line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.

This theorem is a direct generalization of Theorem
1.1
. As a converse to this theorem, given two
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes, one of which is
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}
of the coarse space of the other, we use the universal line bundle at the level of gerbes to construct a derived equivalence.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a gerbey genus
1
curve over a field
k
of characteristic 0, and let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
be the fine moduli space of weight
1
degree
d
line bundles on
\mathscr{C}
for
d\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be the universal weight
1
degree
d
line bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. The integral functor

\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}:D(\mathscr{C})\to D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})

is an equivalence.

The techniques of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
allow us to go further and prove the following moduli-theoretic result.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a genus
1
curve,
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{f}_{1}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
, then
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{df-1}_{2}(\mathscr{C})
.

The stacks that appear in these theorems are all
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over genus
1
curves. To prove these theorems, we show that much of the machinery of derived categories and functors between them can be transferred to the level of stacks which are
\mathbb{G}_{m}
gerbes over proper schemes. We produce a theory of integral transforms and prove a strong simplicity criterion and a version of Verdier duality for these stacks. The theory of integral transforms of stacks is closely related to that of twisted derived equivalences of varieties, which is studied in detail in
[
8
]
. This relationship is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are two projective varieties with
\alpha\in H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
\beta\in H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m})
corresponding to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively, and suppose that
\Phi^{\mathcal{K}}:D(\mathcal{X})\cong D(\mathcal{Y})
is an equivalence, with
\mathcal{K}
of weight
1
. Then there is an equivalence of twisted derived categories
D(X,\alpha)\cong D(Y,\beta)
. Furthermore, the equivalence
D(X,\alpha)\cong D(Y,\beta)
is realized by
\Phi^{\mathbf{r}(\mathcal{K})}
where
\mathbf{r}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to X\times Y
is the rigidification map.

This theorem relates both the existence of these derived equivalences and the functors used to produce them.The converse of this theorem is not necessarily true. A twisted derived equivalence
D(X,\alpha)\cong D(Y,\beta)
does not imply
D(\mathcal{X})\cong D(\mathcal{Y})
on the corresponding gerbes. It does however, imply an equivalence of a special subcategory, which we prove in Section 4, Theorem
4.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we discuss background on twisted sheaves and sheaves on gerbes, and describe the relationship between the two. In Section
3
, we prove that integral transforms exist for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over proper schemes and that they satisfy many of the same properties as integral transforms of schemes. In particular, Verdier duality holds; there is a strong simplicity criterion for a kernel of an integral transform that guarantees that the integral transform is fully faithful; and when the canonical bundles are trivial, any fully faithful integral transform is an equivalence. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.6
and recover some results of Căldăraru on twisted derived categories of schemes. In Section
5
, we prove Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch for gerbes over proper schemes. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch will be used for intersection theory computations in Section
6
, where we apply the setup of Section
3
to gerbey curves and prove Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
1.5
.

Sheaves on gerbes and twisted sheaves on schemes

Our work in this paper is closely related to that of Căldăraru and Lieblich on twisted sheaves and twisted derived categories. In this section, we discuss the relationship between twisted sheaves on a scheme and sheaves on an appropriate stack. Sections
2.1
and
2.2
deal with standard background on twisted sheaves and gerbes, respectively. Sections
2.3
and
2.4
are less standard, and make explicit an identification between sheaves on a gerbe and twisted sheaves on the coarse space.

For more details on twisted sheaves and for proofs of the statements below, see
[
8
]
. Throughout this section
X
will denote a smooth, projective variety,
\Br(X)=H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
its étale cohomological Brauer group and
D(X)
its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves.

2.1
Brauer classes as twisting data for sheaves

A class
\alpha\in\Br(X)
can be interpreted as twisting data for sheaves on
X
, where the twisting data is described using Čech cocyles. Using the cocycle interpretation of étale cohomology,
\alpha
is given by data of a cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
, along with sections
\{\alpha_{ijk}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}(U_{ijk})\}
satisfying the 2-cocyle condition and defined up to a 1-coboundary.

Definition 1
.

Let
\alpha\in\Br(X)
, and let
\{\alpha_{ijk}\}
be a Čech 2-cocycle representing it. An
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is the data of a sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{i}
on each
U_{i}
in the cover, together with maps
\tau_{ij}:U_{i}\times_{X}U_{j}\rightarrow U_{i}\times_{X}U_{j}
satisfying
\tau_{ij}\circ\tau_{jk}\circ\tau_{ik}^{-1}=\alpha_{ijk}
.

Conversely, given a collection of sheaves
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
, where each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a sheaf on an open set
U_{i}
, one can construct a Brauer class by setting
\alpha_{ijk}=\tau_{ij}\circ\tau_{jk}\circ\tau_{ik}^{-1}
. Thus, Brauer classes represent the failure of local sheaves to glue together to give a global sheaf.

Given a variety
X
and an
\alpha\in\check{C}^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
, one can form the abelian category
\Mod(X,\alpha)
: the category of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
X
. The objects in this category are
\alpha
-twisted sheaves and the morphisms are morphisms of sheaves
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{i}\}
that preserve the classes
\alpha_{ijk}
.
Further, if two Čech cocycles
\{\alpha_{ijk}\}
and
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{ijk}\}
define the same Brauer class, then
\Mod(X,\alpha)\cong{\Mod}(X,\alpha^{\prime})
. Thus one can indeed make sense of the category
\Mod(X,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\Br(X)
. An object
\mathcal{F}
in this category is called
coherent
if each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a coherent sheaf. We will let
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
denote the subcategory of
\Mod(X,\alpha)
consisting of the coherent objects. The
\alpha
-twisted derived category of
X
is defined as

D(X,\alpha):=D^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)).

2.2
Brauer classes and gerbes

The second interpretation of Brauer classes that we will use is that of gerbes. For a detailed exposition about gerbes, see
[
21
, Chapter 12]
). We only recall some basic notions and properties associated with them.
For each
\alpha\in H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
there exists an algebraic stack
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow X
such that

1.

each point
x\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
has automorphism group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, and

2.

the rigidification of
\mathcal{X}
is
X
.

Étale locally on
X
,
\mathcal{X}
is of the form
X\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
. One may think of the class
\alpha
as determining the transition functions for the
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
-bundle structure. Indeed, by
[
23
, 06QB]
, there exists an algebraic space
U
, a group algebraic space
G
which is flat and locally of finite presentation over
U
, and a surjective, flat, locally finitely presented morphism
U\to X
such that
\mathcal{X}\times_{X}U\cong[U/G]
over
U
.

The derived category of an algebraic stack is defined as follows. Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack. Let
\Mod(\mathcal{X})
denote the category of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-modules on the lisse-étale site of
\mathcal{X}
.
Since
\mathcal{X}
is assumed to be algebraic, it admits a smooth cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
by a scheme
U
. A sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
is said to be coherent if its pullback to
U
is coherent. Let
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
denote the subcategory of
\Mod(\mathcal{X})
of coherent sheaves. For general algebraic stacks, the inclusion of
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
into
\Mod(\mathcal{X})
need not be exact (see e.g.
[
23
, 06WU]
). On the lisse-étale site, however, the inclusion
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})\hookrightarrow\Mod(\mathcal{X})
is exact (see
[
23
, 07B8]
). This means that the derived category
D(\mathcal{X})
has a description analogous to that for schemes: it is the bounded derived category of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-modules with coherent cohomology sheaves. This means that, for any Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br(X)
, we can consider the derived category
D(\mathcal{X})
for
\mathcal{X}
a stack over
X
associated to
\alpha
.

Remark 1
.

When working on the étale site of a gerbe, the derived category of
\mathcal{X}
is easy to define. When working on other sites (the fppf site, in particular), or on the étale site of a stack which is not smooth over its coarse space, defining the derived category of
\mathcal{X}
may not be so straightforward, since the inclusion of
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})\hookrightarrow{}\Mod(\mathcal{X})
is not necessarily exact.
Even with this complication, integral transforms can still be defined for any stack which is cohomologically proper over its good moduli space.

Thus from
\alpha\in\Br(X)
one can produce two
k
-linear triangulated categories:
D(X,\alpha)
and
D(\mathcal{X})
. There is a close relation between these two interpretations of Brauer classes:
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
X
are “almost” the same thing as sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
. More precisely, Lieblich has proved in
[
18
, Prop. 2.2.3.3]
that there is an equivalence of fibered categories between twisted sheaves on
X
and a certain subcategory of sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
. In what follows, we will reprove some of his results; the ideas are mostly the same as those in
[
18
]
, although the language used here is somewhat different.

2.3
Weight of a sheaf on a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe

A sheaf on the stack
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the data of a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-representation. This means that for a coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
, each fiber over a closed point of
X
comes equipped with a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action. Let
V
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-representation over
k
and let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
v\in V
, we say
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
v
by weight
t
if for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
\lambda\cdot v=\lambda^{t}v
. If
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts by weight
t
on every
v\in V
, then we say that
V
is of pure weight
t
(as opposed to mixed weight). Any mixed-weight representation can be decomposed into the direct sum of its weight spaces.
Let
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})
denote the subcategory of
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
consisting of those sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
whose stalks are weight
t

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-representations. We would like to understand the relationship between
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})
and
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
for each
t
, and understand how the
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})
relate to each other as
t
varies. As a first step, we discuss the relationship between
\operatorname{Coh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
.

Recall that
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a moduli space for line bundles, so that for a scheme
T
,
\operatorname{Hom}(T,B\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong\mathbf{Pic}(T)
. We will think of the
T
points of
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
as pairs
(T,L)
with
L\in\mathbf{Pic}(T)
. A coherent sheaf
F
on
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is determined by the following data: for every pair
(T,L)
with
L\in\mathbf{Pic}(T)
, a coherent sheaf
F_{L}
on
T
, satisfying the descent data for sheaves on a stack. In this case,
F_{L}
is the pullback of
F
along the morphism
L:T\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
. On the other hand, a coherent sheaf
V
on
\operatorname{Spec}k
can be thought of as the collection of pullbacks
V_{T}
, for each scheme
T\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}k
.

Let
f:B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\operatorname{Spec}k
be the structure map and let
s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
be a section.
There is a morphism
s^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
induced by the pullback along
s
. The pullback
s^{*}
has the effect of sending the collection
F_{L}
of sheaves to
F_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}
, the sheaf associated to the trivial line bundle on
T
. One may profitably think of
s^{*}
as a “forgetful map,” since it forgets the data of
\mathbf{Pic}(T)
for each test scheme
T
. Further, since
f\circ s
is the identity on
\operatorname{Spec}k
,
f^{*}
is a right inverse to
s^{*}
on
\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
. In general,
s^{*}
need not admit a left inverse. But we claim that for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
s^{*}
restricted to
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
does have a left inverse.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a fixed
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the weight
t
section map,

\displaystyle f_{t}^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\rightarrow\operatorname{Coh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})

\displaystyle F/T\mapsto((T,L)\mapsto F\otimes L^{\otimes t}),

with a weight 0 action on
F
and weight
1
action on
L
. Then
f_{t}^{*}
is the inverse of
s^{*}
restricted to
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
. In particular for each
t
,
s^{*}
induces an equivalence:

\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k).

Proof.

Note first that the image of
f_{t}^{*}
is indeed inside
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
. Since the weight of a representation is additive on tensor products,
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
L^{\otimes t}
with weight
t
. The fact that
f_{t}^{*}
is a right inverse for
s^{*}
follows from the interpretation of
s^{*}
as a forgetful map. In order to show that it is also a left inverse, it is enough to show that
f_{t}^{*}\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\to\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
is surjective. To see this, note that for some sheaf
F
on
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
on which
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts by weight
t
,
F
may be written as
(F\otimes L^{\otimes-t})\otimes L^{\otimes t}
, so that
F=f_{t}^{*}(F\otimes L^{\otimes-t})
.

∎

Remark 2
.

The weight
1
section map
f_{1}^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)\rightarrow\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
induces a map on the derived categories
Lf_{1}^{*}:D(X,\alpha)\rightarrow D(\mathcal{X})
via pullback, and by construction of
f_{1}^{*}
the image sits inside
D^{1}(\mathcal{X})
.
Let
F
be a sheaf on
X
, and identify
F
with an object in
D(X)
by placing it in degree
k
for some integer
k
. Then
Lf_{1}^{*}F
as an element of
D(\mathcal{X})
is the sheaf
f_{1}^{*}(F)
in degree
k
. This can be computed directly via resolutions. Note that it is important here that
F
is an honest sheaf rather than any complex of sheaves.

2.4
Preliminary results

By Lemma
2.1
and a base change argument, if
\mathcal{X}
is the trivial gerbe over a scheme
X
, then we have
\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X)
, where
\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
denotes the subcategory of
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
consisting of sheaves of pure weight
1
. We now show that this equivalence holds for non-trivial gerbes as well.

Proposition 2.2
.

There is a map of abelian categories
s^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
which is defined étale locally by pulling back along a section
U\to U\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
, that is an equivalence.

Proof.

We first show that there is an equivalence of fibered categories over
X
between
\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
and
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
. Since
X
is locally trivializable, there exists an étale cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
such that the structure morphism
\mathcal{X}\to X
is locally of the form
U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to U_{i}
. Each of the morphisms
U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to U_{i}
admits a section
s_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
. By the discussion above,
\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(U_{i})
. Therefore the desired equivalence of fibered categories holds over each étale open in a cover of
X
.

It remains to show that the local equivalences of fibered categories glue. We claim that the gluing data is provided by
\alpha
. If
F\in\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
, then one can define
s^{*}(F)
as the collection of sheaves
\{s_{i}^{*}(F|_{U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}})\}_{i}
. Since
F
is a sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
, by pulling back transition functions, one can see that the collection
\{s_{i}^{*}(F|_{U_{i}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}})\}_{i}
is an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
. A similar argument shows that
f_{1}^{*}
, which is a priori defined locally on
\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
, does indeed glue to give a global inverse to
s^{*}
. This gives the desired equivalence of fibered categories on
X
. To get the equivalence of abelian categories, one can take global sections of each of the fibered categories.

∎

Remark 3
.

One may define
\tilde{s}^{*}
, a “full” version of
s^{*}
, as a map

\tilde{s}^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})\to\underset{\beta\in\Br(X)}{\bigoplus}\operatorname{Coh}(X,\beta).

This map respects tensor products and
\operatorname{Hom}
s: if
F\in\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
has weight
t
and
H
has weight
t^{\prime}
, then
F\otimes H
has weight
t+t^{\prime}
and
\tilde{s}^{*}(F\otimes H)\in\operatorname{Coh}(X,(t-t^{\prime})\alpha)
. Similarly,
\operatorname{Hom}(F,H)
has weight
t^{\prime}-t
, and
\tilde{s}^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}(F,H))\in\operatorname{Coh}(X,(t^{\prime}-t)\alpha)
.

For later use, we prove that the map
s^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
commutes with flat base change.

Proposition 2.3
.

The map of coherent sheaves
s^{*}:\operatorname{Coh}^{1}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Coh}(X,\alpha)
is pullback along a flat map of stacks. In particular,
s^{*}
commutes with
\operatorname{Hom}
and with base change.

Proof.

Flatness is preserved by descent (see
[
13
, 2.2.11(iv))]
). Therefore it suffices to show that
s
is locally a flat morphism. Therefore by base change, it suffices to prove that a section map
s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is flat. Composing
\operatorname{Spec}k\overset{s}{\to}B\mathbb{G}_{m}\overset{f}{\to}\operatorname{Spec}k
, where
f
is the structure map, gives the identity map on
\operatorname{Spec}k
. Now,
f
is flat, since
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a smooth stack over its base
\operatorname{Spec}k
, and the identity map is flat. Therefore
s
is flat as well. The fact that a flat morphism of stacks commutes with base change and with
\operatorname{Hom}
is proved in
[
14
]
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Lieblich uses the language of characters in proving these equivalences, rather than weights of representations. His use of characters allows for
G
-gerbes for groups
G\neq\mathbb{G}_{m}
. In this paper, we only need the results for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes.

Remark 5
.

For general
t
, Lieblich shows that the rigidification map induces an equivalence of categories
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X,t\cdot\alpha)
. See for instance,
[
18
, Section 2.1.3.]
. Suppose
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is torsion of order

. Then
\operatorname{Coh}(X,t\cdot\alpha)\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X,(t+n)\cdot\alpha)
for all
t
. This means that
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
has infinitely many graded components which are, in a sense, cyclic of order
n
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The derived category of
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})
, which we call
D^{t}(\mathcal{X})
, exists as a faithful triangulated subcategory of
D(\mathcal{X})
. Furthermore, all cohomology sheaves in
D^{t}(\mathcal{X})
have weight
t
.

Proof.

First, note that since
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X,t\cdot\alpha)
, the fact that
\operatorname{Coh}(X,t\cdot\alpha)
is abelian (see
[
8
]
) implies that
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})
is abelian. Therefore its derived category exists. Since taking the derived category of an abelian category is covariant,
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})\subset\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{X})
implies that
D^{t}(\mathcal{X})
is a subcategory of
D(\mathcal{X})
. To see that all cohomology sheaves in
D^{t}(\mathcal{X})
have weight

, recall that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on a quotient representation

is inherited from the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
A
. Therefore, the cohomology sheaves of a complex of weight
t
sheaves themselves have weight
t
.

In order to see that the embedding
\iota:D^{t}(\mathcal{X})\hookrightarrow D(\mathcal{X})
is faithful, we note that for
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
two weight

complexes,
\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{t}(\mathcal{X})}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal{X})}(\iota(\mathcal{E}),\iota(\mathcal{F}))
.
∎

Since
D(\mathcal{X})
may be defined analogously to schemes on the lisse-étale site (by taking the homotopy category and inverting quasi-isomorphisms), the equivalence
\operatorname{Coh}^{t}(\mathcal{X})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(X,t\cdot\alpha)
of abelian categories induces an equivalence

of triangulated categories. The advantage of working with the larger category

rather than with

, is that the former is monoidal while the latter is not. In particular, tensor products in the former are well-defined.

3.1
Existence of integral transforms for gerbes

To study equivalences of derived categories of

-gerbes, we first recall that functors between the derived categories of schemes are produced via integral transforms:

Definition 2

For

\Psi

We will set up a theory of integral transforms for derived categories of

3.2
Adjoints, equivalences and duality

We would like to apply the following theorem of Bridgeland in
[
7
]
:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be triangulated categories and
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
a fully faithful exact functor. Suppose
\mathcal{B}
is indecomposable, and not every object of
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to 0. Then
F
is an equivalence of categories if and only if
F
has a left adjoint
G
and a right adjoint
H
such that for any object
b
of
\mathcal{B}
,

Hb\cong 0\implies Gb\cong 0.

Bridgeland uses this theorem to prove that any fully faithful integral functor
D(X)\to D(Y)
is an equivalence when
X
and
Y
are Calabi-Yau varieties. The result above is purely in the language of triangulated categories, and we may therefore apply it to prove that any fully faithful integral functor between the derived categories of two gerbes over Calabi-Yau varieties is an equivalence. To apply
Theorem
3.4
, we must show that any integral functor admits left and right adjoints and that those adjoints satisfy the hypotheses of the theorem. Producing these adjoints will require a gerbey version of Verdier duality to get a left adjoint to
\pi^{*}
and a right adjoint to
\pi_{*}
.

Recall the classical statement of local Verdier duality:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper map of schemes. For any sheaves
F
and
G
on
X
, there is an isomorphism

R\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_{*}F,G)\cong Rf_{*}R\operatorname{Hom}(F,f^{*}G\otimes\omega_{Y}[\dim Y])

of objects in the derived category of
Y
.

The object
\omega_{Y}[\dim Y]
in
D(Y)
is called the dualizing object on
Y
. It satisfies the property that
f^{*}(-)\otimes\omega_{Y}[\dim Y]
is a left adjoint to
f^{*}
. To state and prove Verdier duality for gerbes, we must first produce a candidate for the dualizing object.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}\to Y
a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe with
Y
a proper scheme. Then
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}:=f_{0}^{*}(\omega_{Y}[\dim Y])
will be called the dualizing object of
\mathcal{Y}
. That is,
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}
is the pullback of the dualizing object on
Y
under the weight
0
section map.
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}
is an object in
D(\mathcal{Y})
equipped with the trivial (i.e., weight zero)
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action.

There is an equivalence between
D^{0}(\mathcal{Y})
and
D(Y)
by Proposition
2.2
. One may think of
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}
as being the image of
\omega_{Y}[\dim Y]
under this equivalence. By a previous remark,
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}
is concentrated in a single degree, since it is the image under
f_{0}^{*}
of a sheaf concentrated in a single degree in
D(Y)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over proper schemes
X
and
Y
and let
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
the projection map. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{G}
a coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
. Then there is an isomorphism

R\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{F},\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}))\cong R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})

as elements of the derived category of
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

The idea is to reduce to the case of local Verdier duality on
X
using Proposition
2.2
.

First we must show that the left hand side and right hand side have the same
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, since to reduce to the case of local Verdier duality on
X
, we need that the images of both sides under
s^{*}
are twisted by the same Brauer class.

We first reduce to the case that the sheaves
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are of pure weight. If they are not, then as
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-representations (or equivalently, locally as sheaves) they may be decomposed into the direct sum of their weight spaces. The statement we are proving is local, and the tensor product and
\operatorname{Hom}
respect the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action. If each of the direct summands on the left hand and right hand sides are isomorphic, then the whole sheaves are isomorphic. Therefore we may consider each weight space separately in proving the statement.

Weight of representations is additive on tensor products, and
\omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is defined to be of weight zero. Therefore the weight of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is the same as the weight of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}
.

Consider the weight of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on

, since this will give the weight of the action on
R\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{F},\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}))
restricted to a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Now if
\mathcal{G}
has weight
t^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{X}
, then
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}|_{\{x\}\times\mathcal{Y}}
has weight
t^{\prime}
for any point
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Therefore if
\mathcal{F}\mid_{\{x\}\times\mathcal{Y}}
has weight
t
, then
R\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{F},\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet})
has weight
t^{\prime}-t
.

Similarly, we can consider the weight of the right hand side

, we see that the weight of
\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}\mathcal{F}|_{\{x\}}
is the same as the weight of
\mathcal{F}|_{\{x\}}\times\mathcal{Y}

t
and that of

is

, as above, then the weight of
R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
is
t^{\prime}-t
, as before.

Since the weights of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}

gives sheaves on

.

Let
F:=s^{*}(\mathcal{F})
and

be the images of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
on
X
. The map

is a flat base change by

, and
\operatorname{Hom}
commutes with flat base change on algebraic stacks by

R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(s^{*}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}\mathcal{F}),s^{*}(\mathcal{G}))\cong s^{*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))

s^{*}(R\pi_{\mathcal{X}*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{F},\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}))\cong R\pi_{X*}(R\operatorname{Hom}_{X\times\mathcal{Y}}(s^{*}(\mathcal{F}),s^{*}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\bullet}))).

An equivalence of categories sends isomorphisms to isomorphisms, so by Proposition

Definition 5
.

The canonical sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
is the pullback of
\omega_{X}
along the good moduli space map
\mathcal{X}\to X

Define
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}

\omega_{X}

\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{Y}

First we argue that it is enough to prove the claim when k is algebraically

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}[\dim\mathcal{X}]

We follow closely the proof of Theorem 5.4 in
[
7
]

G

By Lemma

Since

and

Conversely, suppose

for all

.

For later use, we record a result about how to compose integral transforms of gerbes.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{L}
be objects in the derived categories of
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
, respectively. Let

be the projection map, and define
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{Z}}

\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Z}}

is an element of

. Then
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}\circ\Phi^{\mathcal{K}}=\Phi^{\mathcal{K}*\mathcal{L}}
.

Proof.

Given
\mathcal{E}\in D(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})
, one has:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi^{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{E}))

\displaystyle=R\pi_{\mathcal{Z}*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}^{*}[R\pi_{\mathcal{Y}*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{K})]\otimes\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle\cong R\pi_{\mathcal{Z}*}(R\pi_{\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}^{*}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{K}))\otimes\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle\cong R\pi_{\mathcal{Z}*}(R\pi_{\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}^{*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{K})\otimes L\pi_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{Z}}^{*}\mathcal{L}))

\displaystyle\cong R\pi_{\mathcal{Z}*}R\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Z},*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Z}}^{*}L\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes L\pi_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}^{*}\mathcal{K}\otimes L\pi_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{Z}}^{*}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\cong R\pi_{\mathcal{Z}*}(L\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{K}))

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\mathcal{K}*\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{E}).

The isomorphism in the second line is cohomology and flat base change, which holds for these algebraic stacks by work of Hall in

]

3.3
Strong simplicity

Now that we know that fully faithful integral functors between Calabi-Yau stacks are equivalences, we want a criterion in terms of the kernel for determining when an integral functor is fully faithful. In the case of varieties, this is given by the following theorem of Bondal and Orlov in
[
6
]
. Throughout this section, we assume that
k
is algebraically closed or characteristic zero. In all applications, we will be able to deduce that the derived equivalence in question holds over
k
when
k\neq\overline{k}
, as explained in the proof of Proposition
3.9
.

Theorem 3.11

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective algebraic varieties of the same dimension, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a kernel in
D(X\times Y)
. The functor
\Phi^{\mathcal{K}}_{X\to Y}
is fully faithful if and only if
\mathcal{K}
satisfies

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{K}_{x},\mathcal{K}_{x})=k
for all closed points
x\in X
,

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{K}_{x},\mathcal{K}_{x})=0
for all
x\in X
when
i<0
or
i>\dim X
, and

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{K}_{x},\mathcal{K}_{y})=0
for all
i
and for all closed points
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
.

If these three criteria are satisfied, then
\mathcal{K}
is said to be strongly simple over
X
.

We will prove a similar “strong simplicity” criterion for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over such varieties. To do this, we first recall the definition of closed points on a stack.

Definition 6
.

Two morphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

is an equivalence class of field-valued points of

The set of closed points of a stack will be denoted

Let

See

Note that the set of closed points of

may be computed étale locally on

is a gerbe over

Therefore two morphisms

Definition 7

Let

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}})=k

and

with

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}})=0

.

This turns out to the the right condition to guarantee that the integral functor

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over proper schemes, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf over

. Then
\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}:D(\mathcal{X})\to D(\mathcal{Y})
is fully faithful if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is strongly simple over
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

If
\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}
is fully faithful and

denotes the inclusion of a closed point into
\mathcal{X}
, then

\begin{split}\operatorname{Hom}^{i}_{D(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathbf{L}j_{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\mathcal{F},\mathbf{L}j_{\mathbf{y}}^{*}\mathcal{F})&=\operatorname{Hom}^{i}_{D(\mathcal{Y})}(\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{x}},\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{y}}))\\
&=\operatorname{Hom}^{i}_{D(\mathcal{X})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{y}}),\end{split}

(1)

which implies that
\mathcal{F}
is strongly simple over
\mathcal{X}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is strongly simple over
\mathcal{X}
. We show that this implies that
s^{*}(\mathcal{F})
is strongly simple over
X
. To see this, we first show the orthogonality condition: let
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
denote geometric points of
\mathcal{X}
and let
x
and
y
denote the corresponding geometric points on
X
, i.e. the image of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
under the rigidification map.
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}
commutes with

by Proposition

, so

s^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{x},s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{y}).

The map
s^{*}
gives an equivalence of categories between

and sheaves on

twisted by the appropriate Brauer class, which means that
s^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}}))=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}})=0
. By the equality above,
s^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}}))=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{x},s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{y})=0
. Combining these statements, we get that
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}})=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{x},s^{*}(\mathcal{F})_{y})=0
.

Now we prove that if
\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}})=k

\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(s^{*}\mathcal{F}_{x},s^{*}\mathcal{F}_{y})=k
. This follows since rank
1

1

, so
s^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}}))=k
, and
s^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{y}}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(s^{*}\mathcal{F}_{x},s^{*}\mathcal{F}_{y})
. This shows that
s^{*}\mathcal{F}
is strongly simple if
\mathcal{F}
is.

We apply this fact to the following commutative diagram, where
t
is any integer and

is the weight of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
. The vertical arrows are given by

on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, and both of the vertical maps are equivalences.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{t}(\mathcal{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{t+a}(\mathcal{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(X,t\cdot\alpha)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(Y,(t+a)\cdot\beta)}}

This proves that

4
Derived equivalences of gerbes

We now consider the relationship between derived equivalences of gerbes and twisted derived equivalence of their coarse spaces, formalizing results in the proof of Proposition
3.13
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over
X
and
Y
respectively, corresponding to the Brauer classes
\alpha
and
\beta
. Suppose
D(\mathcal{X})\cong D(\mathcal{Y})
with kernel
\mathcal{K}\in D(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})
where
\mathcal{K}
has weight
1
. Then
D(X,\alpha)\cong D(Y,\beta)
.

Proof.

Consider the image

of the kernel
\mathcal{K}
under the forgetful map
s^{*}
on the coarse space
X\times Y
. Since
\mathcal{K}
has weight
1
,

is a twisted sheaf on

, with twisting given by
\pi_{X}^{*}(\alpha)\otimes\pi_{Y}^{*}(\beta)\in\Br(X\times Y)
. Since
\mathcal{K}
is strongly simple over
\mathcal{X}
,

is strongly simple over

. Therefore

induces a fully faithful integral functor
\Phi^{r(\mathcal{K})}:D(X,\alpha)\to D(Y,\beta)
. Since
X
and
Y
are of the same dimension and are Calabi-Yau, any fully faithful integral transform is an equivalence by Proposition
3.9
, proving the statement.
∎

Remark 6
.

. This means that

can be nonzero for all

, including

. Since the equivariant Chow groups

are defined as ordinary Chow groups of the variety

, they enjoy all the functoriality properties of ordinary Chow groups. One may also define

, which is the Grothendick group of

-equivariant vector bundles on

. The equivariant groups

and

satisfy Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, as stated in the following theorem, which is a consequence of Theorem 3.1 in
[

]
.

Notation 5.1
.

For a vector bundle

on
X
, we denote by

and

, the Chern character and Todd class of

respectively. If

and

]
for the precise definitions of the equivariant versions: essentially, these may be calculated by calculating the classical

.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists an equivariant homomorphism
\tau_{X}:K_{0}(G,X)\to\underset{i\geq 0}{\prod}\CH^{G}_{i}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
The map

is covariant for proper

-equivariant morphisms and when

is a smooth scheme and

\tau_{X}(\varepsilon)=\frac{ch(\varepsilon)Td(TX-\mathfrak{g})}{Td^{G}(V)}

where
\mathfrak{g}
is the adjoint representation of
G
and
V
is a choice of
G
-representation that is admissible for

. Furthermore, the map
\tau_{X}
is the map which makes Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch hold. In particular, the following diagram is commutative:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}^{G}(X\times Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}^{G}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{*}^{G}(X\times Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{*}^{G}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{X\times Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}

Indeed,

is typically defined on the larger group

Remark 7
.

In the case that

We now turn to defining Chow rings and Grothendieck rings on gerbes, and show that for quotient stacks, these groups coincide with their equivariant avatars on schemes.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
[X/G]
be a smooth quotient stack. Then
\CH^{*}([X/G])\cong\CH^{*}_{G}(X)
. Furthermore, the integral Chow groups of

are
\CH_{i}([X/G])=\CH^{G}_{i-g}(X)
for

.

For a proof of this proposition, see
[

]
. Next, we note that the Grothendieck group of vector bundles on a quotient stack has an explicit equivariant description.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group acting on the variety

. Then,
K_{0}([X/G])\cong K_{0}(G,X)
.

Proof.

This holds because a vector bundle on
[X/G]
is simply the data of a

-equivariant vector bundle on

.
∎

The above propositions give a clean description of
\CH_{*}(\mathcal{X})
and

in terms of their equivariant versions when

is a quotient stack. For an arbitrary stack, such relations need not hold. However, in the case that

is a

-gerbe over a genus

]
, Edidin, Hassett, Kresch and Vistoli give the following criterion for a

-gerbe to be a global quotient:

Proposition 5.5
.

[
[
10
]
, Theorem 3.6]
Let

(ii) the

-gerbe classifying

Combining Proposition

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a quasicompact separated scheme

, with
X
admitting an ample line bundle. Let
\mathcal{Y}
and
Y
be defined similarly. Then there is a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathcal{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{*}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{*}(\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{Q}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\mathcal{X}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}

When the input is a vector bundle,
\tau_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}(-)=ch(-)\cdot Td(T_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}})
, and similarly for

.

Proof.

By Proposition

\displaystyle K_{0}(\mathcal{X})\cong K_{0}(GL_{n_{1}},\widetilde{X})\cong K_{0}(G,\widetilde{X}),

Now,

It is worth noting that there has been a good deal of other work on Riemann-Roch type theorems for stacks. A version of Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch for

The following statement of Riemann-Roch for gerbey curves is a corollary of Theorem

obtained by setting the gerbes to by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over smooth proper curves and letting the vector bundles in the statement be line bundles.

Corollary 5.7

(Riemann-Roch for gerbey curves)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow C
be a gerbey curve over a smooth curve of genus

. Let

be a line bundle on

In particular, if

.

6
Applications to low dimensions

We now apply the ideas developed in the preceding sections to consider in detail the case of derived equivalences of gerbey genus 1 curves. Genus 1 curves are not Calabi-Yau, since
h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1
. Nevertheless, their canonical bundles are trivial, and hence by
[
5
]
, they may still admit nontrivial derived equivalences.

6.1
Gerbey genus
1
curves

The goal of this section is to generalize Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
proved by Antieau, Krashen and Ward. These generalizations are non-trivial, since even if
C^{\prime}=Pic^{n}(C)
for some non-zero integer
n
,
C\times C^{\prime}
need not admit a universal sheaf.

Example 6.1
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}
and let
E/k
be an elliptic curve with
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. In this case
\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let
C\in H^{1}(k,E)
be element of order
n=6
. For each
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, by (
[
3
]
, Lemma 2.7) we have that
dC
is represented by the homogeneous space
Pic^{d}(C)
of degree
d
line bundles on
C
. Let
C^{\prime}=Pic^{2}(C)
. Since
\phi
is of order 2, by Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to show that
\phi_{*}C^{\prime}=dC
where
d
is not coprime to 6. Now, the automorphism
\phi:E\rightarrow E
sends a point to its negative and thus acts as
[-1]
on
H^{1}(k,E)
. Thus
\phi_{*}(C^{\prime})=4C
, and in particular
C\times C^{\prime}
does not admit a derived equivalence.

Example 6.2
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors prove that over
\mathbb{R}
,
D(X)\cong D(Y)\implies X\cong Y
. Therefore, let
C/\mathbb{R}
be a curve without an
\mathbb{R}
-point and let
C^{\prime}
be the coarse space of its Jacobian. Now
C
and
C^{\prime}
are not isomorphic, and therefore cannot be derived equivalent. In particular, there cannot exist a universal sheaf, since Orlov’s theorem guarantees that if
D(C)\cong D(C^{\prime})
, then
C\times C^{\prime}
must admit a universal sheaf.

Remark 8
.

Antieau, Krashen and Ward say that a scheme
M
is a
fine moduli space
(of sheaves) for a moduli problem on a scheme
X
if it admits a universal sheaf. We use the term “fine moduli space” to mean the
stack

\mathcal{M}
admitting a universal sheaf for the moduli problem. That is, our “fine” moduli spaces admit a universal sheaf and encode all the automorphisms of the sheaves being parameterized. Therefore in this paper, all fine moduli spaces are
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes, whereas in
[
3
]
the fine moduli spaces being used are schemes.

By studying fine moduli spaces, we will obtain examples of derived equivalent gerbey curves which cannot be obtained using the results in
[
3
]
. We begin with some definitions.

Definition 9
.

A
gerbey genus 1 curve

\mathscr{C}
over
k
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a genus
1
curve
C
over
k
. Here,
C
is the coarse space for
\mathscr{C}
.

Note that while
\mathscr{C}
is called a gerbey
curve
, it is a (
1-1=0
) dimensional stack. The key example of a gerbey genus
1
curve is a connected component of the Picard stack
\mathbf{Pic}(C)
for
C
an ordinary genus
1
curve. Any line bundle on a curve has a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
’s worth of automorphisms, so any component of the Picard stack is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over the corresponding component of the Picard scheme.

Definition 10
.

Define
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
to be the moduli space of weight
1

d

\mathscr{C}
, where weight
1
refers to the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action in the sense of Section 2.

The substack
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
of
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
is a gerbey genus
1

.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\to C
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe with class

. Then
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})\cong\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(C,\alpha)

.

Proof.

The local section map

between weight
1
sheaves on
\mathscr{C}
and
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
C
preserves rank, since it is given by pullback along a flat base change. Therefore this map sends line bundles to line bundles. Therefore by Proposition
2.2
,
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})\cong\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(C,\alpha)
as abelian categories. Since

preserves families, this is additionally an isomorphism of moduli stacks.
∎

Note that if we had chosen to parameterize weight
t

line bundles on
\mathscr{C}
for some

, we could have played a similar game, using the fact that
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{t}(\mathscr{C})
is equivalent to
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(C,t\cdot\alpha)
.

Corollary 6.4
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a gerbey genus
1
curve, then for any

,
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
is also a gerbey genus
1

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem

and the fact that
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(C)
is a gerbey genus
1

∎

We make a statement for gerbey genus
1
curves analogous to Theorem

.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a gerbey genus
1

k
, and let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
be the fine moduli space of weight

\mathscr{C}
for

. Let
\mathcal{L}
be the universal weight
1
degree

line bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. The integral functor

\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}:D(\mathscr{C})\to D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})

is an equivalence.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.13
, it suffices to check that
\mathcal{L}
is strongly simple over
\mathscr{C}
. We will show that the universal weight
1
degree
d
line bundle
\mathcal{L}
is strongly simple if
d>0
.

To check strong simplicity, we need to check that

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{y}})=k
as sheaves on
\mathscr{C}
for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
with
\mathbf{x}\sim\mathbf{y},

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{y}})=0
when
i<0
or
i>1
, and
\mathbf{x}\sim\mathbf{y},
and

•

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{y}})=0
for all

if
\mathbf{x}\not\sim\mathbf{y}
.

By Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
, it suffices to check that these

sheaves satisfy the desired properties over the coarse space
\mathscr{C}\to C
. Recall that the étale and Zariski cohomology of quasicoherent sheaves coincides (see
[
23
, 03P2]
). Therefore Riemann-Roch applies in our setting. Let
x
and
y
denote images of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
respectively under the coarse space map
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
. Note that by definition, if

then

. By abuse of notation, we use

and

to mean the sheaves

and

on the coarse space

. We check first that

. We have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{x},\mathcal{L}_{x})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{x}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{x}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=k.

To show the other two statements, observe that

Thus when

which is 0 for

or

∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Let

be a genus 1 curve over

, then we get a derived equivalence of twisted categories,

in Theorem

. Now use the fact that sheaves on

∎

6.2
Some moduli-theoretic computations

The goal of this section to understand is how to make the derived equivalences in Section
6.1
more explicit. For instance, if
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is a Fourier-Mukai partner of a curve
\mathscr{C}
obtained by taking the moduli of line bundles of degree
d
, how do we produce
\mathscr{C}
as the moduli of vector bundles of some rank and degree on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
? Or, how do we compose such Fourier-Mukai transforms in an explicit moduli-theoretic way?

In answering these questions, we will frequently use the following result of Atiyah from
[
4
, Theorem 7]
.

Theorem 6.7
.

If
r,d
are coprime positive integers, and
J_{E}(r,d)
denotes the moduli of rank
r
degree
d
stable vector bundles on an elliptic curve
E
, then the determinant map
\det:J_{E}(r,d)\to J_{E}(1,d)
is an isomorphism.

Atiyah assumes that the base field is algebraically closed. Therefore we may assume only that whenever
r
and
d
are coprime, the determinant map
\det:J_{E}(r,d)\to J_{E}(1,d)
is
geometrically
an isomorphism. Before we prove Theorem
1.3
, we prove some intermediate lemmas.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\to C
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
be two gerbey genus 1 curves over smooth curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
. Then the maps

Td(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}):K_{0}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\rightarrow\CH_{*}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime})

and

Td(\mathscr{C}^{\prime}):K_{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\rightarrow\CH_{*}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})

from Theorem
5.6
are both trivial.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
denote the two projections
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
respectively. The tangent bundle
T_{\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}=\pi_{1}^{*}T_{\mathscr{C}}\oplus\pi_{2}^{*}T_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}
. Since
Td
is a homomorphism from
K_{0}(\mathcal{X})\to\CH_{*}(\mathcal{X})
for any
\mathcal{X}
, we have that
Td(T_{\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}})=Td(\pi_{1}^{*}T_{\mathscr{C}})\cdot Td(\pi_{2}^{*}T_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}})=\pi_{1}^{*}Td(T_{\mathscr{C}})\cdot\pi_{2}^{*}Td(T_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}})
. Thus it suffices to show that
Td(T_{\mathscr{C}})=Td(T_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}})=1
. Since
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
are interchangeable, we show below that
Td(T_{\mathscr{C}})=1.

Write
\mathscr{C}=[\widetilde{C}/GL_{n}]
as a quotient stack, for
\widetilde{C}
the
PGL_{n}
bundle on
C
arising from the interpretation of
\alpha
as a class in
H^{1}(C,PGL_{n})
. Then
T_{\mathscr{C}}=T\widetilde{C}-\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, where
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is the adjoint representation of the stabilizer
GL_{n}
. The morphism
\widetilde{C}\to C
given by the
PGL_{n}
bundle map gives a short exact sequence

0\to T_{\widetilde{C}/C}\to T\widetilde{C}\to TC\to 0.

Now
TC=\mathcal{O}_{C}
since
C
has genus
1
, so
T\widetilde{C}
is determined by the relative tangent bundle, which is
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n}
. Therefore
T\mathscr{C}=\mathfrak{pgl}_{n}-\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
Td:K_{0}(\mathscr{C})\to\CH_{*}(\mathscr{C})
is a homomorphism, so
Td(\mathscr{C})=Td(C)+Td(\mathfrak{pgl}_{n}-\mathfrak{gl}_{n})=Td(C)+Td(\mathfrak{g}_{m})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
is the adjoint representation of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Since
\mathbb{G}_{m}
is abelian,
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
is trivial, which means that
Td(\mathfrak{g}_{m})=1
. Since
TC
is trivial, this means that
Td(\mathscr{C})=1
.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be as in Lemma
6.8
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of degree
d>0
. Let
\pi:\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
denote the projection to the second component. Recall the commutative diagram given to us by Theorem
5.6
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{*}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime})}

Then,
ch(\pi_{*}\mathcal{L})=\pi_{*}(ch(\mathcal{L}))
.

Proof.

First, note that
\pi_{!}:K_{0}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\to K_{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is just the ordinary pushforward. This holds since
R^{1}
of any fiber is the first cohomology of a degree

line bundle on
\mathscr{C}
, which is Serre dual to the global sections of a degree
-d
line bundle on
\mathscr{C}
. Since
d>0
, a degree
-d
line bundle has no sections. The pushforwards
R^{i}
for
i\geq 2
vanish for dimension reasons. This allows us to replace the
\pi_{!}
in the diagram with
\pi_{*}
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.3
, which we recall here for convenience.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
for some
d>0
such that
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
are derived equivalent. Then
\mathscr{C}
is the moduli space of weight
1
, rank
d
degree
1-d
vector bundles on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and the universal sheaf for this moduli problem is the dual of the universal degree
d
line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, whose associated integral functor inverts the derived equivalence
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}:D(\mathscr{C})\to D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be the projection map to the second component. We begin by calculating the rank and degree of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}
on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. By gerbey Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch (Theorem
5.6
), we may compute the rank and degree using the commutative diagram given by GRR.

We have assumed that
d>0
. By Riemann-Roch on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}
on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
has
d
many sections. To see this, consider some point
x\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. The restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathscr{C}\times\{x\}
is a line bundle of degree
d
on
\mathscr{C}
, so by Riemann-Roch for gerbey curves (Corollary
5.7
), we have that

h^{0}(\mathscr{C},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathscr{C}\times\{x\}})=d.

This gives us the number of sections of the pushforward
\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}.

We will use Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch to show that the degree of the pushforward is
d-1
. By Lemma
6.9
, it suffices to compute the Chern character of
\mathcal{L}
itself. Further, since we are only interested in the degree of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{L},
we will only calculate the top part of the Chern character,
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{2}/2
. Consider the Weil divisor
D
corresponding to
\mathcal{L}
, i.e. the codimension 1 substack of
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
cut out by a section of
\mathcal{L}
. In what follows, by abuse of notation, we write the Weil divisors on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
corresponding to
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
as
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
as well.

Consider the morphism
\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
given by sending a point
p
in
\mathscr{C}
to
d\cdot p
in
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
. Let
\Delta
denote the graph of this morphism. Then
c_{1}(D)
is equal to
\Delta+(d-1)\mathscr{C}
, where
\mathscr{C}
denotes the fiber class of
\mathscr{C}
over
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. See Figure 1 for a diagram of this divisor, where the diagonal line represents
\Delta
and the horizontal lines represent the
d-1
copies of
\mathscr{C}
. Each copy of
\mathscr{C}
intersects
\Delta
in 1 point, and the copies of
\mathscr{C}
all intersect each other in zero points. Therefore
c_{1}^{2}=(\Delta+(d-1)\mathscr{C}).(\Delta+(d-1)\mathscr{C})=2d-2
. This means that the degree is
d-1
, so
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L})
has rank
d
and degree
d-1
. Therefore the dual of
\mathcal{L}
, which we denote
V
, induces an equivalence
\Phi^{V}:D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\to D(\mathscr{C})
, which inverts the derived equivalence
D(\mathscr{C})\to D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
.
V
has rank
d
and degree
1-d
over
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, which implies that
\mathscr{C}
is the moduli space of weight
1
rank
d
degree
1-d
vector bundles on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and
V
is the universal bundle for this moduli problem.

∎

When
d<0
, note that the dual of the universal line bundle produces a derived equivalence in this case, that is,
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}^{\vee}}:D(\mathscr{C})\to D(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is an equivalence. Applying the same argument as above shows that
\mathscr{C}
is the moduli space of weight
1
rank
d
degree
d+1
vector bundles on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

A geometric perspective on the kernel of the equivalence.

Since the rank and degree are coprime, the coarse moduli space of rank
d
degree
1-d
sheaves is geometrically isomorphic to the moduli space of degree
1-d
line bundles, as the determinant map is geometrically an isomorphism by Theorem
6.7
. This means that
\mathscr{C}
can alternatively be considered as being geometrically the moduli space of degree
1-d
line bundles on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. There are two distinct derived equivalences from
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
to
\mathscr{C}
which arise naturally from viewing
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
as a moduli space of vector bundles of specified rank and degree on
\mathscr{C}
. The first has kernel
V
, which is the universal vector bundle of rank
d
and degree
1-d
. The second has kernel the universal degree
1-d
line bundle, call it
\mathcal{L}
, obtained via the determinant map. Orlov’s representability theorem in
[
20
, Thm 2.2]
states that any fully faithful exact functor between derived categories is an integral functor and that any such functor corresponds to a unique kernel. Therefore the integral functors corresponding to these different kernels must give different derived equivalences, which implies that they should differ by pre- or post-composing with a derived autoequivalence of
\mathscr{C}
or
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. The resulting autoequivalence here admits a natural description: let
\mathcal{L}
be the universal line bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
,
V
be the universal rank
d
vector bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and
\det:J_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}(d,1-d)\to J_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}(1,d-1)
the determinant map as in Theorem
6.7
. Then
\Phi^{\mathcal{L}}=\det\circ\Phi^{V}
, i.e., we get the integral transform with kernel
\mathcal{L}
by composing the integral transform with kernel
V
with the determinant map, which is an isomorphism on
C
and therefore induces a derived autoequivalence of
C
. Since
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts by weight
1
on the universal rank
d
degree
1-d
vector bundle, it acts with weight
d
on the determinant of
V
, which means that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}_{d}^{1-d}(\mathscr{C})
.

Next, we show how to compose these equivalences in an explicit moduli-theoretic way. That is, if
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(C)
, and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{f}(C^{\prime})
, then what is the rank and degree of the convolution of the universal degree
d
and
f
line bundles after pushing forward to
C^{\prime\prime}
?

Theorem 6.11
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a gerbey genus 1 curve,
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{d}_{1}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathbf{Pic}^{f}_{1}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
, then
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{Pic}^{df-1}_{2}(\mathscr{C})
.

Proof.

The goal here is to compute the rank, degree, and weight of the convolution of the universal degree
d
and degree
f
line bundles. This can be done by running an intersection theory computation on the gerbey threefold
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
. If
\mathcal{L}
is the universal degree
d
line bundle on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is the universal degree
f
line bundle on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
, the kernel of the composition is
\pi_{02*}(\pi_{01}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi_{12}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
.

We use Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch as before. The tangent bundles of everything in sight are trivial, so the Todd classes of the tangent bundles vanish. Therefore we want to compute
c_{1}^{3}/6
, where
c_{1}
is the divisor of
\pi_{01}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi_{12}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

Since we understand the divisors on
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
, we can understand them on the threefold.
\mathcal{L}
pulls back to
(\Delta_{01}+(d-1)\mathscr{C})\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
pulls back to
(\Delta_{12}+(f-1)\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. Therefore the divisor of
\pi_{01}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi_{12}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is
(\Delta_{01}+(d-1)\mathscr{C})\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}+(\Delta_{12}+(f-1)\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\times\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. Computing the third power of this divisor shows that the pushforward of this kernel to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
has rank
df
and degree
df-1
. The weight of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action is the sum of the weights of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions on the universal degree
d
and degree
f
line bundles, which is
2
.

∎

As a sanity check, note that this means that if we take degree
d
line bundles on
\mathscr{C}
, and then degree 1 line bundles on
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
(whose universal line bundle induces the identity map on
D(\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C}))
, the convolution of the kernels gives a sheaf of rank
d
and degree
d-1
on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
, which is what we expect from the first computation.

Remark 9
.

We could also apply these techniques to study the derived categories of gerbey K3s, or gerbey moduli spaces of higher dimension. In particular, if
X
is a K3 and
\mathcal{M}
is a 1-dimensional component of the moduli stack of semistable sheaves such that
\mathcal{M}
does not parameterize any strictly semistable sheaves, then
D(X\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong D(\mathcal{M})
.

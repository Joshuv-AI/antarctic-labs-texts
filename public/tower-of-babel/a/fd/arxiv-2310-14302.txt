Highest Weight Varieties and Narayana Numbers
Boming Jia

Abstract
We compute the Hilbert series of the coordinate ring of some highest weight varieties. We also explain why Narayana numbers (and their generalizations) appear naturally in the numerator of the Hilbert series of the coordinate ring of complex grassmanian G ​ r ​ ( d , n + d + 1 ) Gr(d,n+d+1) and of the minimal nilpotent adjoint orbit in 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{\mathrm{n+1}}(\mathbb{C}) .



1
Catalan Numbers and Narayana Numbers

The Catalan numbers
1,2,5,14,42,429,1430,4862,\dots
and their generalizations are ubiquitous in mathematics. There are many equivalent definitions of the Catalan numbers, and we refer to the book
[
Sta15
]
for more than 200 interpretations of the Catalan numbers. For example, the
n
-th Catalan number

\mathrm{Cat}_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}

can be intepreted as the number of triangulations of a convex
(n+2)
-gon.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq n-1
. The Narayana numbers are defined by

N_{n,k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k+1}.

Note that the row sums in the triangle of Narayana numbers are the Catalan numbers:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}N_{n.k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k}\binom{n+1}{k+1}-\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k+1}=\binom{2n}{n}-\binom{2n}{n-1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}=\mathrm{Cat}_{n}.

There are also many combinatorial interpretations of the Narayana numbers, for example,
N_{n,k}
is the number of
231
-pattern avoiding permutations in
S_{n}
with
k
descents.

Definition 1.2
.

In fact, for any irreducible root system
\Phi
of type
X
and rank
n
:

\mathrm{Cat}_{X}(n)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e_{i}+h+1}{e_{i}+1},

where
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
are the exponents of
\Phi
, and
h
is the Coxeter number of
\Phi
, so that in type
A
, we have

\mathrm{Cat}_{A}(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{i+(n+1)+1}{i+1}=\frac{1}{n+2}\binom{2n+2}{n+1}=\mathrm{Cat}_{n+1}.

And in type
B
, we have

\mathrm{Cat}_{B}(n)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(2i-1)+2n+1}{(2i-1)+1}=\binom{2n}{n}.

Definition 1.3
.

There are numerous equivalent definitions of the (generalized) Narayana numbers, we refer to Theorem 5.9 in
[
FR07
]
and references within.
Narayana numbers of
A
-type are simply defined as

N_{A}(n,k)\coloneqq N_{n+1,k}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}\binom{n+1}{k+1}.

Narayana numbers of
B
-type are

N_{B}(n,k)\coloneqq\binom{n}{k}^{2}.

Then we can check

\sum_{k=0}^{n}N_{B}(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}=\binom{2n}{n}=\mathrm{Cat}_{B}(n).

On the otherhand, there are higher dimensional generalization of the classical Catalan numbers and Narayana numbers as well.

Definition 1.4
.

The
d
-dimensinal Catalan numbers
[
Sul04
]
are defined as

C_{d,n}\coloneqq(dn)!\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{i!}{(n+i)!},

so that the original Catalan number
C_{n}
becomes a special case
C_{2,n}
.

Definition 1.5
.

First, for
d\geq 2
, and
0\leq k\leq(d-1)(n-1)
the
d
-dimensional Narayana numbers
[
Sul04
]
can be equivalently defined as

N_{d,n,k}\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{dn+1}{k-j}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{n+i+j}{n}\binom{n+i}{n}^{-1}.

And as usual, we define
N_{A}(d,n,k)\coloneqq N_{d,n+1,k},
where
0\leq k\leq(d-1)n
.

2
Highest Weight Varieties.

All results in this section are well-known, we mostly follow Garfinkle’s thesis
[
Gar82
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra. Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
(\pi_{\lambda},V_{\lambda})
be an irreducible finite dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}
with nontrivial highest weight
\lambda
. Let
v_{\lambda}\in V_{\lambda}
be a highest weight vector. Then
G
acts on
V_{\lambda}
and we define the highest weight variety to be the Zariski closure

X_{\lambda}\coloneqq\overline{Gv_{\lambda}},

and we denote the corresponding ideal by
I\subset\mathbb{C}[V_{\lambda}]\simeq\mathrm{Sym}(V_{\lambda}^{*})
.

Let
v_{1}=v_{\lambda},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}
be a basis of
V_{\lambda}
consisting of weight vectors. Let
{\overline{\lambda}}
be the highest weight for
V^{*}_{\lambda}
. Let
v^{*}_{1}=v^{*}_{\lambda},\cdots,v^{*}_{n}
be the dual basis for
V_{\overline{\lambda}}\coloneqq V^{*}_{\lambda}
such that
v^{*}_{i}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for all
i,j
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define
V_{k\overline{\lambda}}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(V_{\lambda}^{*})
to be the simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module generated by
(v^{*}_{\lambda})^{k}
, and
C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
be the unique
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant complement of
V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
in
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(V_{\lambda}^{*})
, so

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(V_{\lambda}^{*})=V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}\oplus C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}.

Notice that
C_{0}=C_{\overline{\lambda}}=\{0\}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Proposition III.1.1 in
[
Gar82
]
)
.

The vanishing ideal
I\subset\mathrm{Sym}(V_{\lambda}^{*})
of the highest weight variety
X_{\lambda}
satisfies

I=\bigoplus_{k=2}^{\infty}\,C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}.\hskip 10.00002pt(C_{0}=C_{\overline{\lambda}}=\{0\}.)

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is nontrivial, the orbit
Gv_{\lambda}
is invariant under the scaling
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
V_{\lambda}
. So we have

I=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\,I\cap\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(V_{\overline{\lambda}}).

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we denote
I_{k}\coloneqq I\cap\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(V_{\overline{\lambda}})
, so it suffices to show that
I_{k}=C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
.

Since
(v^{*}_{1})^{k}(v_{\lambda})\neq 0
, we have
(v^{*}_{1})^{k}\notin I_{k}
. But
I_{k}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
is a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module containing the highest weight vector
(v^{*}_{1})^{k}
, so we have

V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}\cap I_{k}=\{0\}.

This proves

I_{k}\subseteq C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}.

Let
f\in C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
. Since
f
is a linear combination of monomials which all have some
v^{*}_{2},\cdots,v^{*}_{n}
factors, we have
f(v_{\lambda})=0
. Now we show that
f
vanishes on
G.v_{\lambda}
. Since
G
is simply connected, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}
is also a
G
-module, so for all
g\in G
,

f(gv_{\lambda})=(g^{-1}f)(v_{\lambda})=0.

So we have
f\in I_{k}
, and this proves

C_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}\subseteq I_{k}.\qed

And we get a useful corollary which will be applied again and again in this paper.

Corollary 2.3
.

The homogenous coordinate ring of the highest weight variety
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
is isomorphic to a graded algebra

\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}}

with a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant multiplication. So the Hilbert series for the graded algebra
\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]
is

h_{\mathbb{C}[X_{\lambda}]}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dim(V_{k{\overline{\lambda}}})t^{k}.

3
The Grassmanian
Gr(d,n+1+d)
and Higher Dimensional Narayana Numbers.

Take
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+d+1}(\mathbb{C})
. We fix the standard choice of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n+d}\}
, and let
\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n+d}
denote the corresponding fundamental weights.
Let
\rho
be the half sum of the positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Let
Gr(d,n+d+1)
be the Grassmanian variety consisting of
d
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1}
. Since the affine cone variety
X(d,n+d+1)
of
Gr(d,n+d+1)
is identified (under the Plücker embedding) with the decomposable elements in
\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\bigwedge$}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1})
. We have
X(d,n+d+1)
is isomorphic to the highest weight variety
X_{\omega_{d}}
for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+d+1}
-representation
V_{\omega_{d}}=\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\bigwedge$}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The irrducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+d+1}
-representation
V_{k\omega_{n+1}}
has dimension

\dim(V_{k\omega_{n+1}})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}N_{A}(d,n,i)\binom{d(n+1)+k-i}{d(n+1)}.

Proof.

We know
\rho=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n+d}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By Weyl’s dimension formula

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\dim(V_{k\omega_{n+1}})=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle k\omega_{n+1}+\rho,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha\rangle}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle k\omega_{n+1}+(\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n+d}),\alpha\rangle}{\langle\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n+d},\alpha\rangle}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{k+(n+1)+i-j}{(n+1)-j}\ =\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{k+(n+1)+i}{n+1}\binom{(n+1)+i}{n+1}^{-1}.

(3.2)

Now by Proposition 4 in
[
Sul04
]
, we have

\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{k+(n+1)+i}{n+1}\binom{(n+1)+i}{n+1}^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}N_{A}(d,n,i)\binom{d(n+1)+k-i}{d(n+1)}.\qed

Now we can give another proof of Braun’s result on Hilbert series of grassmanians.

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem 1 in
[
Bra19
]
)
.

Let
X(d,n+d+1)
be the affine cone variety of
Gr(d,n+d+1)
.
Then the Hilbert Series for the graded algebra
\mathbb{C}[X(d,n+d+1)]
is

h_{\mathbb{C}[X(d,n+d+1)]}(t)=\frac{\sum_{\ i=0}^{(d-1)n}N_{A}(d,n,i)\,t^{i}}{(1-t)^{d(n+1)+1}},

where
N_{A}(d,n,i)
are the
d
-dimensional Narayana numbers of type-
A
.

Proof.

Recall that
X(d,n+d+1)
is isomorphic to the highest weight variety
X_{\omega_{2}}
for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+d+1}
-representation
V_{\omega_{d}}=\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\bigwedge$}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1})
. Since
\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\bigwedge$}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1})^{*}\cong\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\bigwedge$}}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n+d+1})
we have
\overline{\omega_{d}}=\omega_{n+1}
. So by Corollary
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
we have

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{C}[X(d,n+d+1)]}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dim(V_{k\omega_{n+1}})t^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{k}N_{A}(d,n,i)\binom{d(n+1)+k-i}{k-i}t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{(d-1)n}N_{A}(d,n,i)\,t^{i}\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{d(n+1)+j}{j}t^{j}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{(d-1)n}N_{A}(d,n,i)t^{i}}{(1-t)^{d(n+1)+1}}.\qed

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X(2,n+3)
be the affine cone variety of
Gr(2,n+3)
.
Then the Hilbert Series for the graded algebra
\mathbb{C}[X(2,n+3)]
is

h_{\mathbb{C}[X(2,n+3)]}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{A}(n,i)\,t^{i}}{(1-t)^{2n+3}}

Remark 3.5
.

Since the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Grassmanian
Gr(2,n+3)
is well-known to have a cluster algebra structure of type
A
. And the number of clusters is always the Catalan numbers which is the sum of the Narayana numbers in the same row (as appeared in the numerator of the RHS of the above result). It is interesting to study further about the relations between the above theorem and the cluster algebra structure. A similar remark will show up again in the next section.

Next, inspired by the calculations and remarks in
[
Aga14
]
, we can acutally use highest weight varieties interpretation to prove the following result which completely avoids using any difficult combinatorial identities.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\partial_{t}:f(t)\mapstochar\rightarrow f^{\prime}(t)\in\End(\mathbb{C}[t])
and
T:f(t)\mapstochar\rightarrow tf(t)\in\End(\mathbb{C}[t])
. Then

h_{\mathbb{C}[X(d,n+d+1)]}(t)=\frac{(\partial_{t}^{d}\circ T^{d-1})^{n}\left[(1-t)^{-(d+1)}\right]}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(i+j)}.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction on
n
. For
n=0
, we have
Gr(d,d+1)\cong\mathbb{P}^{d}
, so

\mathrm{LHS}=h_{\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}]}(t)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-t)^{d+1}}=\mathrm{RHS}.

Suppose the statement holds for the
(n-1)
-case for some
n\geq 1
. By applying the induction hypothesis, the formula (
3
), and the same formula with
n
replaced by
(n-1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\partial_{t}^{d}\circ T^{d-1})h_{\mathbb{C}[X(d,n+d)]}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}(n+i)}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{k+n+i}{n}\binom{n+i}{n}^{-1}(\partial_{t}^{d}\circ T^{d-1})t^{k}}{\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(n+1+i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{k+n+i}{n}\binom{n+1+i}{n+1}^{\!\!-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(k+d-j)t^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{k+1+n+i}{n}\binom{n+1+i}{n+1}^{\!\!-1}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(k+1+j)t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{k+(n+1)+i}{n+1}\binom{(n+1)+i}{n+1}^{-1}t^{k}=\mathrm{LHS}.\qed

Remark 3.7
.

This theorem is proved without using any combinatoric identities such as Proposition 4 in
[
Sul04
]
. So we can prove Theorem
3.3
again with the help of some basic recurrence relations satisfied by the Narayana numbers.

4
The Minimal Nilpotent Orbit in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}

We first recall the well-known identity of Li Shan-lan, which has numerous proofs, and we present here its first known (but very difficult to find in the literature) proof due to Paul Turán
[
Tur54
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Li Shan-lan’s Identity)
.

For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\binom{m+n}{n}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\binom{2n+m-i}{2n}.

Proof.

Let

V_{n}(x)\coloneqq\left.-\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(\frac{z^{n}}{(z+x)^{n+1}}\right)\right|_{z=-1}.

(4.2)

Then

\displaystyle V_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n!}\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{z}\left(1+\frac{x}{z}\right)^{-n-1}\right)\right|_{z=-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n!}\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-n-1}{k}\frac{x^{k}}{z^{k+1}}\right|_{z=-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}x^{k}\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(z^{-k-1}\right)\right|_{z=-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}x^{k}.

On the other hand, we can differentiate the RHS of (5.2) by Leibniz’s rule directly and get

\displaystyle V_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-k}{k}(x-1)^{-2n+k-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{n}(x-1)^{n-k}\right)(1-x)^{-2n-1}

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n}P_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n+k}{2n}x^{k},

where
P_{n}(t)
is the
n
-th Legendre polynomial and the third equality is proved by checking

P_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{n}(-1)^{n-k}\left(\frac{t+1}{2}\right)^{k}

satisfies the characterizing ODE for Legendre polynomial

(1-t^{2})P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)-2tP_{n}^{\prime}(t)+n(n+1)P_{n}(t)=0

with conditions
P_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}(-1)=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}(-1)^{n+1}.

By Hurwitz formula for Legendre polynomials

(1-x)^{n}P_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)=\sum_{i=0}\binom{n}{i}^{2}x^{i}.

So

\displaystyle V_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}x^{i}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n+k}{2n}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\binom{2n+m-i}{2n}\right)x^{m}

Compare the coefficients of
x^{m}
in the first calculation of
V_{n}(x)
at the beginning of the proof, we have the desired result. (The upper limits of the sum in the RHS of Li Shan-lan’s identity can be chosen freely between
n
and
m
, as
\binom{n}{i}=0
for
i>n
and
\binom{2n+m-i}{2n}=0
for
i>m
.)
∎

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and take
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
. We fix the standard choice of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
, and let
\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n}
denote the corresponding fundamental weights.
Let
\rho
be the half sum of the positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{min}}
be the minimal (nonzero) nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. Let
\theta\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
be the highest root and
0\neq v_{\theta}\in\mathfrak{g}
be any highest root vector. Then
V_{\theta}=\mathfrak{g}
is the adjoint representation, and the closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}
is the highest weight variety
X_{\theta}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\dim(V_{k\theta})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\binom{k-i+2n-1}{2n-1}

Proof.

We know the highest root
\theta=\omega_{1}+\omega_{n}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By Weyl’s dimension formula,

\displaystyle\dim(V_{k\theta})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle k\theta+\rho,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha\rangle}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle k(\omega_{1}+\omega_{n})+\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n},\alpha\rangle}{\langle\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n},\alpha\rangle}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k+1}{1}\cdot\frac{k+2}{2}\cdots\frac{k+n-1}{n-1}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{2k+n}{n}

\displaystyle=\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}^{2}\cdot\frac{(n+k)^{2}-k^{2}}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=\binom{k+n}{n}^{2}-\binom{k-1+n}{n}^{2}.

Now we apply the well-known identity of Li Shan-lan (Propostion
4.1
) and get

\dim(V_{k\theta})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\left(\binom{k+2n-i}{2n}-\binom{k-1+2n-i}{2n}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\binom{k-i+2n-1}{2n-1}.\qed

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{min}}
be the minimal (nonzero) nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. Then the Hilbert series for the graded algebra
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}]
is

h_{\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}]}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{B}(n,i)\,t^{i}}{(1-t)^{2n}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}
is the highest weight variety
X_{\theta}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
under the Killing form, we have
\overline{\theta}=\theta
. So by Corollary
2.3
and Lemma
4.3
we have

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}]}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dim(V_{k\theta})t^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\binom{k-i+2n-1}{2n-1}t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}t^{i}\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+2n-1}{2n-1}t^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}^{2}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{2n}}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{B}(n,i)\,t^{i}}{(1-t)^{2n}}.\qed

Remark 4.5
.

Is it possible to formulate an analogous result of Theorem
4.4
by differentiating a suitable choice of rational function?
Does the coordinate ring of the minimal nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{sl_{\mathrm{n+1}}}
have a cluster algebra structure of type
C_{n}
?

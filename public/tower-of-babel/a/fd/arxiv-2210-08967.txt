Abstract
. This paper considers C 2 C^{2} random dynamical systems in a Banach space, and proves that under some mild conditions, SRB measures are characterized by invariant measures satisfying Pesin’s entropy formula, in which entropy is equal to the sum of positive Lyapunov exponents of the system. This can be regarded as a random version of the main result in Blumenthal and Young’s paper [ 8 ] .

1.
Introduction and setting

1.1.
Introduction

In the ergodic theory of smooth dynamical systems, SRB measures are the invariant measures most compatible with volume when the volume is not preserved by a system, see
[
35
]
for a detailed description.
Roughly speaking, they are invariant measures with smooth density along the expanding (or unstable) directions of the dynamical system.
A principal result concerning SRB measures is the relationship between entropy, volume growth and Lyapunov exponents.
The results show that, for a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism on a finite-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold, SRB measures are characterized by invariant measures satisfying Pesin’s entropy formula.
This formula characterizes the entropy to be the volume growth on unstable manifolds, or equivalently, the sum of positive Lyapunov exponents, see
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
for more details.

This kind of results has been generalized from two aspects: one is to consider relevant problems in random dynamical systems, the other is to consider similar phenomena in infinite dimensional dynamical systems.
It is natural and non-trivial to study related problems by combing these two aspects, i.e., random infinite dimensional dynamical systems.

The random dynamical systems consider the composition of sequences of random maps.
This is a more general set-up than the iteration of a certain map, and covers the most important examples of dynamical systems, such as products of random maps, stochastic ordinary and partial differential equations.
We would like to refer the reader to
[
2
]
and
[
28
]
for more details about random dynamical systems.
In the case of two-sided stationary random dynamical system of cocycles of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on a smooth compact Riemannian manifold over a Polish system, Bahnmüller and Liu
[
4
]
proved that an invariant measure has the SRB property if and only if it satisfies (random) Pesin’s entropy formula, which generalizes Ledrappier and Young’s result
[
18
]
for deterministic
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms. In the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) setting, Liu and Qian
[
28
, Chapter VI]
gave a detailed proof of the corresponding result, which had first been stated by Ledrappier and Young
[
19
]
with a brief proof. See also
[
28
, Chapter VI]
for the proof of the corresponding result in a one-sided stationary setting. In
[
27
]
, Liu established Pesin’s entropy formula in the setting of both i.i.d. and stationary compositions of random
C^{2}
endomorphisms of a compact manifold (not necessarily invertible and maybe with singularities), provided that the sample measures
are almost all SRB in the random diffeomorphisms case by the absolute continuity property of the unstable manifolds.

Although it is difficult to establish ergodic theorems in smooth infinite dimensional dynamical systems since the lack of inner product and compactness, there are still many important progresses made in this direction. For example, the multiplicative ergodic theorem
[
30
]
, which plays a fundamental role in the smooth ergodic theory and nonuniform hyperbolic theory (for more details, see
[
5
]
), has been extended to the infinite dimensional case. Ruelle
[
31
]
first proved the multiplicative ergodic theorem for compact linear operators on a separable Hilbert space. Mañé
[
29
]
overcame the lack of inner product structure and extended the multiplicative ergodic theorem to continuous compact operator cocycles on Banach spaces. Later, Thieullen
[
32
]
proved the multiplicative ergodic theorem for continuous bounded linear operator cocycles on Banach spaces. Lian and Lu
[
24
]
established the multiplicative ergodic theorem for strong measurable operator cocycles on a separable Banach space. Froyland
[
13
]
extended it to a continuous semi-invertible cocycle on Banach spaces, and Tokman
et al.

[
14
]
built the multiplicative ergodic theorem for a strong measurable cocycles on a separable Banach space.
In addition to the multiplicative ergodic theorem, the nonuniform hyperbolic theory in infinite dimensional dynamical systems has also been studied. Lian and Lu
[
24
]
described the dynamics of linear random dynamical systems on a separable Banach space, and obtained a splitting of the phase space related to the Lyapunov exponents and the essential exponent. Moreover, they obtained stable and unstable local random manifolds in this case.
Recently, Varzaneh and Riedel
[
33
]
obtained corresponding results for cocycles acting on measurable fields of Banach spaces, i.e., the operator is allowed to be non-invertible. See
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
25
,
26
]
and references therein for more results about smooth ergodic theory in infinite dimensional dynamical systems.

From then on, similar to the finite dimensional case, it is a natural problem to give a description of SRB measures in infinite dimensional dynamical systems. Li and Shu
[
20
]
proved the equivalence of SRB measures and invariant measures satisfying Pesin’s entropy formula in Hilbert space for random dynamical systems, and they only
[
21
]
proved Ruelle’s inequality in the case of infinite dimensional Banach space, because there is no clear definition of volume and determination in a Banach setting, which yield significant challenges.
Recently, Blumenthal and Young
[
8
]
overcame this difficulty and proved the equivalence of SRB measure and invariant measures satisfying Pesin’s entropy formula for a certain mapping on Banach spaces.
For more information on SRB measures for infinite dimensional dynamical systems, see
[
22
,
23
]
.

This paper focuses on the relationship between SRB measures and invariant measures satisfying Pesin’s entropy formula in random dynamical systems on a Banach space.
There are some new questions should be addressed in our case.
Firstly, because attractors are random and we only have weak measurability conditions, we need to revisit the measurability of certain mappings and sets.
Secondly, since the random maps are not assume to be diffeomorphisms, many problems cannot be dealt with using backward iterations.
This leads to regularity issues such as the continuity of the unstable subspaces
E^{u}
are not uniform under the random mappings.

The paper is organized as follows:
Section
1
presents the settings and the statements of the main results and introduces some notations.
Section
3
introduces the technical tools called Lyapunov norms and demonstrates the continuity of certain Oseledets splittings.
Section
4
discusses some properties of non-uniform hyperbolic theory, while Section
5
presents the proof of the main results.

The

A sequence of bounded linear operators

For a function

For each

A

can be represented as a measure-preserving skew product map.
Let

be the product measurable space.
A

over

\displaystyle\Phi:\Omega\times\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega\times\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle(\omega,x)

Note that

Let

\Phi
-invariant Borel probability measure projection to
\mathbb{P}
.
For every

and

f^{n}_{\omega}=\begin{cases}f_{\theta^{n-1}(\omega)}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega}&n>0;\\
Id&n=0.\end{cases}

The compositions of sequences of maps

is called the random

(\Phi,\mu)
.

We call a set

Basic assumptions

Throughout the paper, the following properties are assumed:

(H1)

(i)

for each
\omega\in\Omega
, the map

is

Fréchet differentiable and injective;

(ii)

for each
\omega\in\Omega

, is also injective;

(iii)

the mapping
\omega\mapsto f_{\omega}(x)
is measurable for every

.

(H2)

(i)

the subset

is random compact and satisfies

;

(ii)

\mu
is supported on
A
;

(iii)

there exists

such that

\int\log^{+}|(f_{\omega}|_{B(A_{\omega},r_{0})})|_{C^{2}}d\mathbb{P}(\omega)<\infty,

where
B(A_{\omega},r_{0})=\{y\in\mathcal{B}:d(y,A_{\omega})<r_{0}\}
.

(H3)

we assume for
\mu
-almost every
(\omega,x)
,

l_{\alpha}(\omega,x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}_{\omega}(x)|_{\alpha}<0.

Here

is the Kuratowski measure of the set

, where

is the closed unit ball in

.

Some

on the basic assumptions are given in order.
Condition (H2) implies that
f_{\omega}(A_{\omega})=A_{\theta(\omega)}
for every
\omega\in\Omega
.
By (H1), one can define
(f_{\omega}|_{A_{\omega}})^{-1}
on
A_{\theta(\omega)}
, which is also continuous.
Therefore, for every
n>0
, we define the map
f_{\omega}^{-n}
on
A_{\omega}
as follows:

f_{\omega}^{-n}:=(f_{\theta^{-1}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\theta^{-n}\omega}|A_{\theta^{-n}\omega})^{-1},

and we denote
\Phi^{-n}(\omega,x):=(\theta^{-n}\omega,f_{\omega}^{-n}(x))
for every
(\omega,x)\in A
.
Since
\Phi
is measurable, by Lusin’s theorem, there exists an increasing sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact subsets of
A

such that
\mu(\cup_{n\geq 0}A_{n})=1
and
\Phi|_{A_{n}}
is continuous for each
n
.
Let
B_{n}:=\Phi(A_{n}),n\geq 0
, which also is a compact subset of
A
, and satisfies
\mu(\cup_{n\geq 0}B_{n})=1
.
Note that
\Phi|_{A_{n}}
is a homeomorphism from
A_{n}
to
B_{n}
since
\Phi|_{A_{n}}
is injective and continuous.
This implies that
(\Phi|_{A_{n}})^{-1}
is continuous on
B_{n}
for each
n\geq 0
.
Hence, by applying Lusin’s theorem again, we conclude that
(\Phi|_{A})^{-1}
is also measurable on
A
.

The integrand in (iii) of (H2) is measurable by (iii) of (H1) and (i) of (H2).
To demonstrate this, let
\{x_{n}\}_{n>0}
be a countable dense subset of
\mathcal{B}
, we have

\sup_{x\in B(A_{\omega},r_{0})}|f_{\omega}(x)|=\sup_{n>0}|f_{\omega}(x_{n})|\cdot\chi_{B(A_{\omega},r_{0})}(x_{n}),

where
\chi_{E}
is the characteristic function of the set
E
.
Since

is random compact,
the set
\{\omega:d(x_{n},A_{\omega})<r_{0}\}
is measurable for each

.
This implies that
\omega\mapsto\chi_{B(A_{\omega},r_{0})}(x_{n})
is measurable.
By assumption (iii) of (H1),
\omega\mapsto|f_{\omega}(x_{n})|
is also measurable for each

.
Hence, the
C^{0}
norm is measurable.
For the

and

Df_{\omega}(x)u=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{f_{\omega}(x+t_{k}u)-{f_{\omega}(x)}}{t_{k}},

where

is a sequence of positive numbers converges to zero.
Then, by (iii) of (H1), the map
\omega\mapsto Df_{\omega}(x)u
is measurable for each
x\in\mathcal{B}
and each

.
Since

The measurability of the

2.1.
Conditional entropy

In this subsection, we recall the definition of conditional entropy, see
[
28
, Chapter 0]
for more details.
Let
(\Phi,\mu)
be given as in Section
1.2
.
The partition
\eta_{0}:=\{\{\omega\}\times\mathcal{B}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a measurable partition of
\Omega\times\mathcal{B}
since
\Omega
is a Polish space.

For every measurable partition
\eta
of
\Omega\times\mathcal{B}
with
H_{\mu}(\eta|\eta_{0})<\infty
, where
H_{\mu}(\eta_{1}|\eta_{2})
is the conditional entropy of
\eta_{1}
given by
\eta_{2}
.
The conditional entropy of
\eta
is given as follows:

\displaystyle h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\eta):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}\Big(\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}\Phi^{-i}\eta|\eta_{0}\Big).

The conditional entropy of
(\Phi,\mu)
is given by

h(\mu|\mathbb{P}):=\sup\Big\{h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\eta):\eta\ \text{is a
measurable partition of}\ \Omega\times\mathcal{B}\ \text{with}\ \ H_{\mu}(\eta|\eta_{0})<\infty\Big\}.

The definition of
h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\eta)
can be generalized for any measurable partition of
\Omega\times\mathcal{B}
as follows.
For a measurable partition
\eta
of
\Omega\times\mathcal{B}
, let

h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi,\eta):=H_{\mu}\Big(\eta|\bigvee_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi^{-i}\eta\vee\eta_{0}\Big).

If
H_{\mu}(\eta|\eta_{0})<\infty
, then
h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi,\eta)=h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\eta)
. Let

h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi):=\sup\Big\{h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi,\eta):\eta\ \text{is a measurable partition of}\ \Omega\times\mathcal{B}\Big\}.

By Theorem 5.1 in
[
28
]
, one has
h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi)=h(\mu|\mathbb{P})
.
Since
(\Phi|_{A})^{-1}
is measurable and
\mu
is supported on
A
,
by Theorem 4.3 in
[
28
]
, we have that

h(\mu|\mathbb{P},\Phi)=h(\mu|\mathbb{P})=h(\mu|\mathbb{P},(\Phi|_{A})^{-1}).

In this subsection, we recall the definitions of gap and distance in a Banach space, see

Let (

We consider the metric topology on

between unit spheres:
for non-trivial subspaces

\displaystyle d_{H}(E,F)=\max\Big\{\sup_{v\in S_{E}}d(v,S_{F}),\sup_{u\in S_{F}}d(u,S_{E})\Big\},

where

and

*=E,\,F
.
For every

, and denote by

Lemma 2.2

(
[

]

Suppose that (

For every two non-trivial subspaces

\displaystyle G(E,F):=\sup_{v\in S_{E}}d(v,F)

where

Note that

is not symmetric,
the relations between

and

given by the following result.

Lemma 2.3

(
[

]

(2.1)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=E\oplus F
for

If a sequence of closed subspaces

satisfy that

Then,
|\pi_{E//F}|_{E_{n}}|\rightarrow 1
and

.

Proof.

We first prove that
|(\pi_{F//E})|_{E_{n}}|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty

Fix a unit vector

By the definition of

, for every

arbitrarily close to 1, there exists a unit vector

Then,

|\pi_{F//E}v|=|\pi_{F//E}(v-v^{\prime})|\leq|\pi_{F//E}|\cdot|v-v^{\prime}|\leq c|\pi_{F//E}|\cdot d_{H}(E,E_{n}).

Therefore, we get

|\pi_{F//E}|_{E_{n}}|\leq|\pi_{F//E}|\cdot d_{H}(E,E_{n}).

This yields that

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Note that

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

For each

For two Banach spaces

depends on the choices of the norms
|\cdot|_{1}
,
|\cdot|_{2}
on

and

Therefore, we have that

|v^{u}|\leq(3\ell_{0})^{2}(1+\ell_{0})e^{-n(\lambda-\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{1})}+3\ell_{0}e^{-n\lambda}C(n,\ell_{0})|x-y|.

For each

,

we have

when

.
Symmetrically, replacing

by
(y,x)
one has

.
So, we get that

when
|x-y|\leq\delta
.

Note that we cannot iterate backwards to estimate

.
Fix a unit vector

, and decompose it as

with respect to the splitting

.
Denote by
x_{-n}
and

the points

and
f^{-n}_{\omega}(y)
respectively, and let

be the unique vector in

such that
Df^{n}_{\theta^{-n}\omega}(y_{-n})v_{-n}=v
.
We decompose

by

By using (
3.6
) and Lemma
3.4
, we have
|v_{-n}|\leq 3\ell_{0}e^{-n\lambda}
.
We can bound

as follows:

|v^{s}|\leq|v^{s}-Df^{n}_{\theta^{-n}\omega}(x_{-n})v^{s}_{-n}|+|Df^{n}_{\theta^{-n}\omega}(x_{-n})v^{s}_{-n}|.

The first term can be upper-bounded as

\leq|\pi^{cs}_{(\omega,x)}|\cdot|v-Df^{n}_{\theta^{-n}\omega}(x_{-n})v_{-n}|\leq 3\ell^{2}_{0}e^{-n\lambda}C(n,\ell_{0}e^{n\varepsilon_{1}})|x_{-n}-y_{-n}|.

The second term has the following estimation:

\displaystyle|Df^{n}_{\theta^{-n}\omega}(x_{-n})v^{s}_{-n}|

\displaystyle\leq 3\ell_{0}e^{n(\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})}|v^{s}_{-n}|

\displaystyle\leq 3\ell_{0}e^{n(\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1})}|\pi^{cs}_{\Phi^{-n}(\omega,x)}|\cdot|v_{-n}|

\displaystyle\leq 9\ell_{0}^{3}e^{-n(\lambda-\varepsilon_{0}-2\varepsilon_{1})}.

For each
\epsilon>0
, choose

large enough such that the second term is upper-bounded by
\epsilon/4
.
Therefore, by the definition of

we have

G(E^{u}(\omega,y),E^{u}(\omega,x))\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}+3\ell^{2}_{0}e^{-k\lambda}C(k,\ell_{0}e^{k\varepsilon_{1}})\cdot|x_{-k}-y_{-k}|.

Choose
{\delta}^{\prime\prime}:={\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\epsilon,\ell_{0})
to be sufficiently small such that
G(E^{u}(\omega,y),E^{u}(\omega,x))<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
when

.
Symmetrically, replacing
(x,y)
by
(y,x)
we have
G(E^{u}(\omega,x),E^{u}(\omega,y))<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.
Therefore, we obtain
d_{H}(E^{u}(\omega,x),E^{u}(\omega,y))<\epsilon
when
|x_{-k}-y_{-k}|<{\delta}^{\prime\prime}
.

To ensure that

, we use the fact that
f^{-k}_{\omega}
continuous on the compact subset

.
Therefore, we choose a real number

to be sufficiently small such that for every

then

.
This completes the proof.
∎

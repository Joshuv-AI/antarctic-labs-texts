Towards Efficient and Effective Adaptation of Large Language Models for Sequential Recommendation
Bo Peng, Ben Burns, Ziqi Chen, Srinivasan Parthasarathy, Xia Ning

Abstract
In recent years, with large language models (LLMs) achieving state-of-the-art performance in context understanding, increasing efforts have been dedicated to developing LLM-enhanced sequential recommendation (SR) methods. Considering that most existing LLMs are not specifically optimized for recommendation tasks, adapting them for SR becomes a critical step in LLM-enhanced SR methods. Though numerous adaptation methods have been developed, it still remains a significant challenge to adapt LLMs for SR both efficiently and effectively. To address this challenge, in this paper, we introduce a novel side sequential network adaptation method, denoted as 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits , for LLM-enhanced SR. 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits features three key designs to allow both efficient and effective LLM adaptation. First, 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits learns adapters separate from LLMs, while fixing all the pre-trained parameters within LLMs to allow efficient adaptation. In addition, 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits adapts the top- a a layers of LLMs jointly, and integrates adapters sequentially for enhanced effectiveness (i.e., recommendation performance). We compare 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits against five state-of-the-art baseline methods on five benchmark datasets using three LLMs. The experimental results demonstrate that 𝚂𝚂𝙽𝙰 \mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits significantly outperforms all the baseline methods in terms of recommendation performance, and achieves substantial improvement over the best-performing baseline methods at both run-time and memory efficiency during training. Our analysis shows the effectiveness of integrating adapters in a sequential manner. Our parameter study demonstrates the effectiveness of jointly adapting the top- a a layers of LLMs.



Introduction

Sequential recommendation (SR) aims to predict and recommend the next item of users’ interest using their historical interactions.
It has been
drawing increasing attention from the research community due
to its wide applications in online retail, video streaming and tourism planning, etc.
Recently, as large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in context understanding
(
OpenAI 2023
)
, numerous efforts have been dedicated to developing LLM-enhanced SR methods
(
Yuan et al. 2023
;
Hou et al. 2022
)
.
The key idea behind these methods is to leverage LLMs to generate expressive item embeddings from item texts (e.g., titles) for better recommendation.
Given that existing LLMs are generally recommendation-independent,
adapting LLMs for recommendation tasks becomes an important step in LLM-enhanced SR methods.

There are broadly two categories of methods in adapting LLMs for SR
(
Yuan et al. 2023
)
.
The first category of methods, referred to as top tuning (
\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
), adapts LLMs by learning an adapter on top of the LLM as illustrated in Figure
1(a)
.

\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
allows efficient adaptation as it does not require both forward and backward propagation across LLMs in training
if the outputs of LLMs (i.e.,
\mathbf{h}^{n}_{i}
) for each item text are pre-calculated.
However,
\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
could suffer from limited effectiveness (i.e., recommendation performance) as it is essentially equivalent to adapting only the top layer of LLMs.
As suggested in the literature
(
Shim, Choi, and Sung 2021
)
, different Transformer layers
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
in LLMs could capture diverse semantics in item texts.
Thus, adapting only the top layer could lose valuable information in other layers, leading to sub-optimal recommendation performance.
Conversely, the second category of methods, denoted as multi-layer tuning (
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
), enables effective adaptation by adapting potentially all the layers in LLMs via fine-tuning or parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)
(
He et al. 2021
)
.
Nevertheless, both fine-tuning and PEFT methods are notoriously computation-intensive
(
Liao, Tan, and Monz 2023
)
.
Consequently,
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
significantly underperforms
\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
in terms of efficiency
(
Yuan et al. 2023
)
.
Figure
1(b)
illustrates
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
in which LLMs are adapted using PEFT.

With the prosperity of LLM-enhanced SR methods, developing both efficient and effective LLM adaptation methods for SR has emerged as a critical research challenge.
To tackle this challenge, in this paper, we introduce a novel side sequential network adaptation method, denoted as
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
, for SR.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
features three key designs.
First,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns adapters separate from LLMs, and keeps all the pre-trained parameters within LLMs fixed.
As a result, similar to
\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
, with pre-calculated outputs from LLMs (e.g.,
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{n}
),

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
could
avoid forward and backward propagation across LLMs during training, and thus, enable efficient adaptation.
Second, distinct from
\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
that adapts only the top layer of LLMs,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
adapts the top-
a
layers jointly to allow effective adaptation.
Third,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
integrates adapters sequentially via a gated recurrent unit (
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
) network for better effectiveness.
As illustrated in Figure
1(b)
,
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
adds adapters within each layer of LLMs.
This design inherently integrates adapters in a sequential manner.
As will be shown in our analysis (Table
8
),
the sequential integration of adapters significantly contributes to the effectiveness.
Thus,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns an additional
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
network to sequentially integrate the adapters, thereby enhancing recommendation performance.

Related Work

Sequential Recommendation

Numerous SR methods have been developed in the last few years, particularly, leveraging neural networks and attention mechanisms.
For example, Hidasi
et al
.
(
Hidasi et al. 2015
)
utilizes
\mathop{\mathtt{GRUs}}\limits
to capture users’ latent intent from their historical interactions.
Tang
et al
.
(
Tang and Wang 2018
)
leverages a convolutional neural network (
\mathop{\mathtt{CNN}}\limits
) to capture the union-level patterns among items for better user intent modeling.
Besides neural networks, attention mechanisms have also been widely employed for SR.
Ma
et al
.
(
Ma, Kang, and Liu 2019
)
learns attention weights to differentiate the importance of items, enhancing the estimate of users’ intent.
Kang
et al
.
(
Kang and McAuley 2018
)
develops a self-attention-based SR method
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits
, in which self-attention mechanisms are utilized to capture the interactions between individual items for better user intent modeling.

Recently, increasing efforts have been dedicated to incorporating LLMs for SR.
For example, Hou
et al
.
(
Hou et al. 2022
)
adapts the top layer of LLMs for SR using the mixture-of-expert mechanism (
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits
), and learns transferable recommendation models based on the adapted item embeddings.
Yuan
et al
.
(
Yuan et al. 2023
)
shows that item embeddings generated from LLMs could be more expressive than those learned solely based on user interactions, and thus, lead to better recommendation performance.
Yuan
et al
.
(
Yuan et al. 2023
)
also shows that adapting the top layer of LLMs using multilayer perceptrons (
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
) underperforms
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
methods by a considerable margin.

Parameter-efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT)

As LLMs have demonstrated impressive performance in context understanding, numerous PEFT methods have been developed to adapt LLMs for specific tasks in a parameter-efficient manner.
Particularly, Hu
et al
.
(
Hu et al. 2021
)
develops a low-rank adaptation method
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
,
which learns rank decomposition matrices in each LLM layer, while fixing all the pre-trained parameters within LLMs for PEFT.
Zaken
et al
.
(
Zaken, Ravfogel, and Goldberg 2021
)
develops
\mathop{\mathtt{BitFit}}\limits
, in which only the bias-terms of LLMs are adapted for the task of interest.
Zhang
et al
.
(
Zhang et al. 2023
)
develops
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits
, which captures the importance of the pre-trained parameters within LLMs, and prioritizes the adaptation of parameters based on their importance.

Method

Figure
2
presents the overall architecture of
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
.
As shown in Figure
2
,

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
adapts LLMs by learning adapters for each of the top-
a
layers of LLMs.
Different from that in
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
, the adapters in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
are separate from LLMs to allow efficient adaptation.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
also learns a
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
network to sequentially integrate the adapters for enhanced recommendation performance.
In what follows, we present
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
in detail.

LLM Adaptation

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns an adaptation module (
\mathop{\mathtt{AM}}\limits
) to adapt LLMs for SR, while fixing all the pre-trained parameters within LLMs.
Particularly,
\mathop{\mathtt{AM}}\limits
comprises
a
adapters to adapt the top-
a
layers of LLMs.

\mathop{\mathtt{AM}}\limits
also includes a
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
network to integrate these adapters sequentially.

Adapter Design

Existing work
(
Yuan et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2022
)
typically implements adapters using multilayer perceptrons (MLPs).
However, as suggested in a recent work
(
Chen et al. 2022
)
, mixture-of-export mechanisms (MoEs)
(
Shazeer et al. 2017
)
could better capture the clustering structures within items compared to MLPs, enabling more expressive item embeddings.
Thus,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
implements adapters using MoEs.
Specifically,
each adapter in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
comprises a projection layer and a routing layer.
Within each projection layer,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
has
n_{p}
projection heads.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
implements each projection head using parametric whitening
(
Huang et al. 2021
)
as follows:

\mathbf{e}^{m}_{i}(k)=\left(\mathbf{h}_{i}^{n-m}-\mathbf{b}^{m}_{k}\right)W^{m}_{k},

(1)

where
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{n-m}
is the embedding of item text
v_{i}^{t}
generated from the
(n\text{-}m)
-th layer of
the LLM (
m\in\{0,1,\cdots,a\text{-}1\}
), and

\mathbf{e}^{m}_{i}(k)
is the projected embedding from the
k
-th projection head of the
m
-th adapter.

\mathbf{b}^{m}_{k}
is a learnable
shift parameter,
and
W_{k}^{m}
is a learnable projection parameter.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
utilizes a Gaussian routing to aggregate the projected embedding from each projection head as follows:

\mathbf{z}_{i}^{m}=\text{Softmax}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}^{m})[\mathbf{e}_{i}^{m}(1);\mathbf{e}_{i}^{m}(2);\cdots;\mathbf{e}_{i}^{m}(n_{p})],

(2)

where
\mathbf{z}_{i}^{m}
is the output from the
m
-th adapter, and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}^{m}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}^{m},\text{diag}((\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{m})^{2}))

is the weights learned to aggregate the projected embeddings.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
parameterizes the mean
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}^{m}
and standard deviation
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{m}
of the Gaussian distribution as follows:

\boldsymbol{\mu}^{m}_{i}=\mathbf{h}_{i}^{n-m}B^{m}\;\text{and}\;\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{m}=\text{Softplus}(\mathbf{h}_{i}^{n-m}U^{m}),

(3)

where
Softplus is the activation function to
enable a positive standard deviation;

B^{m}
and

U^{m}
are learnable parameters in the
m
-th adapter.

Sequential Integration of Adapters

As shown in Figure
2
,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns a
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
network to sequentially integrate the adapters as follows:

\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\star}=\mbox{$\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits$}(\mbox{$\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits$}(\mbox{$\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits$}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{z}_{i}^{0}),\mathbf{z}_{i}^{1})\cdots,\mathbf{z}_{i}^{a-1}),

(4)

where
\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\star}
is the adapted embedding for item
v_{i}
.
We initiate the memory cell in
\mathop{\mathtt{GRU}}\limits
with a zero vector
\mathbf{0}
following the literature
(
Hidasi et al. 2015
)
.
As illustrated in Figure
1(b)
,
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
stacks adapters inside LLMs, and thus, inherently integrates adapters in a sequential manner.
Our own analysis (Table
8
) demonstrates that this property could significantly contribute to the recommendation performance.
Thus, besides adapters,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns an additional GRU network to sequentially integrate the adapters for enhanced recommendation performance.

User Intent Modeling and Recommendation Generation

We utilize the widely used
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits
model
(
Kang and McAuley 2018
)
in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
to capture users’ intent and generate recommendations.
Specifically, given the interaction
sequence of user
u_{j}
:
S_{j}=\{v_{s_{1}}(j),v_{s_{2}}(j),\cdots\}
,
we generate adapted embeddings for each of the interacted items, and stack these embeddings to generate an embedding matrix for
S_{j}
as follows:

M_{j}=[\mathbf{v}_{s_{1}}^{\star}(j);\mathbf{v}_{s_{2}}^{\star}(j);\cdots],

(5)

where
\mathbf{v}_{s_{t}}^{\star}(j)
is the adapted embedding of
item
v_{s_{t}}(j)
.
Given
M_{j}
, we model users’ intent using
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits
:

\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mbox{$\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits$}(M_{j}),

(6)

where
\mathbf{u}_{j}
represents the intent of user
u_{j}
.
With
\mathbf{u}_{j}
, the recommendation score of item
v_{i}
is calculated as follows:

r_{ij}=\cos(\mathbf{u}_{j},{\mathbf{v}_{i}^{*}}),

(7)

where
\cos(\cdot)
is the cosine similarity and
r_{ij}
is the recommendation score of
u_{j}
on
v_{i}
.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
recommends the top-
k
items of the highest recommendation scores.

Network Training

We utilize the cross-entropy objective function
to minimize the negative log-likelihood of correctly recommending the ground truth next item as follows:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\sum_{u_{j}\in\mathbb{U}}\frac{\exp(\cos(\mathbf{u}_{j},\mathbf{v}_{g}^{*}(j))/\tau)}{\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{j}}\exp(\cos(\mathbf{u}_{j},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{*})/\tau)},

(8)

where
\mathbb{U}
is the set of all the users;

\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is the set of learnable parameters in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
(i.e., parameters in
\mathop{\mathtt{AM}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits
);

\exp(\cdot)
is the exponential function;

\mathbf{v}_{g}^{*}(j)
is the adapted embedding of the ground-truth next item of
u_{j}
;

\mathbb{N}_{j}
is the set of randomly sampled negative items of
u_{j}
;

\tau
is a hyper-parameter to scale the cosine similarities
(
Hou et al. 2022
)
.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
minimizes the objective function using batch optimizations.
For
u_{j}
within a particular batch,

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
regards the ground-truth next items from all other users in the same batch as the set of negative items (i.e.,
\mathbb{N}_{j}
) of
u_{j}
.
All the learnable parameters are randomly initialized,
and are optimized in an end-to-end manner.

Experimental Protocol

Training, Validation and Testing Set

For each user, following the literature
(
Fan et al. 2022
)
, we use her last and second last interaction for testing and validation, respectively.
The other interactions are used for training.
Following
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits

(
Kang and McAuley 2018
)
, we split each training sequence to sub-sequences and train
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
and all the baseline methods using all the sub-sequences.

Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate all the methods using the widely used evaluation metrics Recall@
k
(
\mathop{\mathtt{R@k}}\limits
) and NDCG@
k
(
\mathop{\mathtt{N@k}}\limits
).
We refer the audience of interest to Peng
et al
.
(
Peng et al. 2021
)
for the detailed definations.

Hyper-parameter Tuning

We tune hyper-parameters for
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
and all the baseline methods using
grid search.
For all the methods, we use the best-performing hyper-parameters in terms of
\mathop{\mathtt{R@10}}\limits
on the validation set for testing.
We use Adam optimizer with learning rate

\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{Full}}\limits

b

(
Bhattacharyya 2022
)
.

For
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits

b

\{32,64,128,256\}

\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits

p

\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}
.
For
\mathop{\mathtt{Full}}\limits

n_{t}
, in
\{2,4,6\}
.
For
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
, we search the number of MLP layers
n_{l}
in
\{1,2,3\}
.
For
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
, we search the number of top layers to adapt (
a
) in
\{2,3\}
.
We set the number of projection heads in each adapter (
n_{p}
) as 8.
We also set the scaling parameter
\tau
(Equation
8
) as 0.07.
Following the literature
(
Hou et al. 2022
)
,
we set both the number of self-attention layers and the
number of attention heads in
\mathop{\mathtt{SASRec}}\limits
as 2.
We also set the dimension for item embeddings as 256 for all the methods.
We report the best-performing hyper-parameters of
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
and all the baseline methods in Table

S2

S3

\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{Full}}\limits

We train
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{Full}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits

Comparison between
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits

As shown in Table
5
, overall,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
outperforms the best-performing
\mathop{\mathtt{MLT}}\limits
method
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
on the five datasets using the three LLMs.
For example, with
\mathop{\mathtt{DistilBERT}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
achieves an average improvement of 1.8%, 1.2%, 5.0% and 3.7% at
\mathop{\mathtt{R@10}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{R@50}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{N@10}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{N@50}}\limits
, respectively, when compared to
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
both integrate adapters in a sequential manner for effective adaptation.
However, while
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
stacks adapters over layers of LLMs,

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
learns adapters separate from LLMs and employs a GRU network for the integration.
Previous work
(
He et al. 2016
)
shows that gradient propagation across deep networks could be challenging.
Consequently, compared to
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
, the shallow architecture in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
could facilitate gradient-based optimization, leading to better performance.
In addition, while
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
adapts all the layers in LLMs,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
focuses on adapting only the top-
a
layers.
As will be shown in Figure
3
, we empirically find that adapting all the layers of LLMs may degrade the recommendation performance.

Comparing
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
to
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits

Table

\mathop{\mathtt{TT}}\limits
method
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
achieves significant average improvement over the five datasets with the three LLMs.
For example, with
\mathop{\mathtt{DistilBERT}}\limits
, in terms of
\mathop{\mathtt{R@10}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
outperforms both
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits
on all the five datasets, and achieves a significant average improvement of 8.0% and 7.3% over
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits
, respectively.

\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits
adapts only the top layer of LLMs, while
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
adapts the top-
a
layers jointly for enhanced recommendation performance.
The superior performance of
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
over
\mathop{\mathtt{TMLP}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{TMoE}}\limits
demonstrates the importance of adapting top-
a
layers of LLMs together in enabling effective recommendation.

We conduct an analysis to investigate the effectiveness of integrating adapters sequentially.
To this end, we introduce two
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
variants
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
.

\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
differ from
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
in the ways of integrating adapters.
Specifically,
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
aggregates the outputs of adapters using a mean pooling.

\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
learns weights on different adapters, and aggregates the outputs of adapters using a weighted sum.
We compare
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
against
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
to evaluate the effectiveness of integrating adapters sequentially.
Table
8
shows the recommendation performance of
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
on the five datasets using
\mathop{\mathtt{DistilBERT}}\limits
.
Due to the space limit,
we only present the results from
\mathop{\mathtt{DistilBERT}}\limits
.
But we observe a similar trend when using
\mathop{\mathtt{DistilRoBERTa}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{BERTMedium}}\limits
.

As presented in Table
8
,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
outperforms both
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
on all the five datasets.
Particularly, in terms of
\mathop{\mathtt{R@10}}\limits
,

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
achieves a remarkable average improvement of 9.7% and 11.3% compared to
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
, respectively, over the five datasets.
Similarly, in terms of
\mathop{\mathtt{N@10}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
substantially outperforms
\mathop{\mathtt{MEAN}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{WEIGHTED}}\limits
with an average improvement of 10.4% and 11.7%, respectively, across the five datasets.
These results show the effectiveness of integrating adapters in a sequential manner in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
.

We conduct a parameter study to evaluate how recommendation performance changes over the number of top LLM layers to be adapted in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
(i.e.,
a
).
Particularly, we utilize the widely used
\mathop{\mathtt{Sci}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{Pantry}}\limits
,
\mathop{\mathtt{Toys}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{Ins}}\limits
datasets for this study. On each dataset, we change
a

while fixing all the other hyper-parameters as the ones reported in Table
S1
(Appendix).
Figure
3
shows the performance at
\mathop{\mathtt{R@10}}\limits
on the four datasets.

As shown in Figure
3
, on all the four datasets, adapting multiple layers (
a\geq 2
) consistently outperforms that of adapting only the top layer of LLMs (
a=1
).
These results demonstrate the effectiveness of jointly adapting the top-
a
layers (
a\geq 2
) in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
.
It is also worth noting that,
as shown in Figure
3
,
adapting all the LLM layers (
a=6
) may not benefit the recommendation performance.
In fact, on all the datasets,
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
achieves the best performance when
a<6
.
A possible reason could be that recommendation datasets are generally sparse.
Adapting all the LLM layers on sparse recommendation datasets could induce overfitting, and thus, degrade the recommendation performance.

Conclusion

In this paper, we present a novel LLM adaptation method
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
for LLM-enhanced SR.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
allows efficient adaptation by learning adapters separate from LLMs and fixing all the pre-trained parameters within LLMs to avoid forward and backward propagation across LLMs in training.

\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
also enables effective adaptation by jointly adapting the top-
a
layers of LLMs,
and integrating adapters sequentially.
We evaluate
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
against five state-of-the-art baseline methods on five benchmark datasets using three different LLMs.
The experimental results demonstrate that
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
substantially outperforms all the baseline methods in terms of recommendation performance, and achieves remarkable improvement in terms of run-time and memory efficiency in training when compared to the best-performing baseline methods
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
and
\mathop{\mathtt{ADA}}\limits
(e.g., 30.8x average speedup over
\mathop{\mathtt{LoRA}}\limits
across the five datasets).
Our analysis also shows the effectiveness of integrating adapters sequentially in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
.
Our parameter study suggests that adapting top-
a
layers (
a\geq 2
) of LLMs together in
\mathop{\mathtt{SSNA}}\limits
substantially benefits the recommendation performance.
Our parameters study also shows that adapting all LLM layers for SR tasks may not benefit the recommendation performance.

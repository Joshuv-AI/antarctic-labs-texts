Bloch-Beilinson conjectures for Hecke characters and Eisenstein cohomology of Picard surfaces
Jitendra Bajpai, Mattia Cavicchi

Abstract
. We consider certain families of Hecke characters ϕ \phi over a quadratic imaginary field F F . According to the Bloch-Beilinson conjectures, the order of vanishing of the L L -function L ⁡ ( ϕ , s ) L(\phi,s) at the central point s = − 1 s=-1 should be equal to the dimension of the space of extensions of the Tate motive ℚ ⁡ ( 1 ) {\mathbb{Q}}(1) by the motive associated with ϕ \phi . In this article, we construct candidates for the corresponding extensions of Hodge structures, assuming that the sign of the functional equation of L ⁡ ( ϕ , s ) L(\phi,s) is − 1 -1 . This is accomplished through the cohomology of variations of Hodge structures over Picard modular surfaces associated with F F and Harder’s theory of Eisenstein cohomology. Furthermore, we demonstrate that these extensions are naturally realized within certain biextensions. We outline a program to compute the biextension height and utilize it to establish the non-triviality of these extensions.

Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and denote by
r
the rank of the finitely generated abelian group
E({\mathbb{Q}})
. Suppose that the sign
\epsilon(E)
of the functional equation of the (completed)
L
-function of
E
is
-1
. One of the striking consequences of the celebrated Gross-Zagier formula (
[
14
]
), in this case, is that if
L^{\prime}(E,1)\neq 0
, then
r\geq 1
. Since by the hypothesis on the sign,
L(E,s)
vanishes at the central point
s=1
, this agrees with the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, which predicts in particular that
r
should be precisely equal to the order of vanishing of
L(E,s)
at
s=1
.

In this article, we are interested in certain conjectural analogues of the latter results, when
L(E,s)
is replaced by the
L
-function
L(\phi,s)
of an algebraic Hecke character
\phi
of a quadratic imaginary field
F
, of odd weight
w
. A natural question arises: what kind of arithmetic information is encoded in the order of vanishing of
L(\phi,s)
at the central point
s=\frac{w+1}{2}
? The integer
w
is usually referred to as the motivic weight of the
L
-function.

One way to arrive at a reasonable prediction is by rephrasing the conjecture about elliptic curves in a form that lends itself to generalization. Given a rational point
P
on an elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, one can consider the open subvariety
U:=E\setminus\{O,P\}
, where
O
is the zero point. One looks at the localization exact sequence relating cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{Z}(E)
of
E
supported on
Z:=\{P,O\}
, cohomology with compact supports
H^{\bullet}_{c}(U)
of
U
, and cohomology
H^{\bullet}(E)
. Since the divisor
P-O
gives rise to a class in
H^{2}_{Z}(E)(1)
that maps to 0 in
H^{2}(E)(1)
, one gets a morphism
\mathds{1}\rightarrow H^{2}_{Z}(E)(1)
landing in the image of the connecting homomorphism from
H^{1}_{c}(U)(1)
. By pullback, this yields an exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(E)(1)\rightarrow E_{P}\rightarrow\mathds{1}\rightarrow 0

It turns out that the extension
E_{P}
is non-trivial if and only if
P
is of infinite order. This holds both in the sense of extensions of Galois representations (when taking étale cohomology) and of Hodge structures (when taking singular cohomology). Thus, one can reformulate the results of Gross-Zagier as saying that if
\epsilon(E)=-1
and
L^{\prime}(E,1)\neq 0
, then there exists a non-trivial extension of
\mathds{1}
by
H^{1}(E)(1)

of geometric origin
.

As explained in Section
3.3
, the Bloch-Beilinson conjectures imply a generalization of the previous statement to the case of an algebraic Hecke character
\phi

Let

Here,

We have chosen the formulation in the statement because it is the one more adapted to our approach using Eisenstein cohomology.

Work in progress of the two authors is aimed at showing that, at least when

1.1.
Ideas of construction and evidence for non-triviality

The strategy for proving our Theorem 1 is inspired by ideas of Harder (
[
18
]
) and makes use of his results in
[
17
]
. Starting from the Hecke character
\phi
, one can construct a subspace
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
in degree 2
boundary cohomology
, with coefficients in an appropriate local system, of a
Picard modular surface

\mathrm{S}_{K}
, that is, a non-projective Shimura variety, attached to a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebraic group
\mathrm{G}
such that
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})\simeq\mathop{\rm SU}\nolimits(2,1)
. Note that boundary cohomology of such a variety is computed in terms of the boundary of a compactification, but is independent of the choice of the latter. Then, one would like to lift elements of
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
to classes in the cohomology of
\mathrm{S}_{K}
itself. Through Langlands’ theory of Eisenstein operators, one sees that the desired lifts can be provided by classes represented by Eisenstein series, well defined as soon as their constant term converges. For Hecke characters whose infinity type and whose shape are as in our Theorem, this convergence is ensured precisely when
L(\phi,-1)=0
. Hence, in this case, we find that
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
is a subspace of
Eisenstein cohomology
, i.e. of the restriction map
r
from cohomology of
\mathrm{S}_{K}
to its boundary cohomology.

A natural way to enforce the vanishing
L(\phi,-1)=0
is to ask that
\epsilon(\phi)=-1
. Now, Rogawski’s study of the automorphic spectrum of unitary groups in three variables (
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
) shows that exactly under this sign hypothesis, a suitable subspace
H^{2}(\phi)_{!}
can be constructed inside the
interior cohomology
of Picard surfaces with coefficients in the local system as before, where interior cohomology is defined as the kernel of the restriction map
r
. The subspace
H^{2}(\phi)_{!}
is related to a
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
-representation induced from
\phi
, and is non-trivial when
\phi
has the shape prescribed by our Theorem.

To put the above two pieces together, one exploits the short exact sequence linking interior cohomology, cohomology, and Eisenstein cohomology, and gets an extension

(1)

0\rightarrow H^{2}(\phi)_{!}\rightarrow\bar{E}_{0}\rightarrow\partial H^{2}(\phi)\rightarrow 0

which is in fact an extension of Hodge structures. Classical results allow us to describe the Hodge structure on the piece on the left, while the one on the piece on the right can be computed thanks to the analysis of the degeneration of variations of Hodge structure to the boundary of the
Baily-Borel compactification

\mathrm{S}^{*}_{K}
of
\mathrm{S}_{K}
of
[
1
]
. It turns out that up to a suitable twist, the above extension is nothing but an extension

0\rightarrow H_{\phi}(-1)\rightarrow E_{0}\rightarrow\mathds{1}^{\oplus\dim\partial H^{2}(\phi)}\rightarrow 0

Any choice of a class in
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
produces then an extension
E_{\phi}
of geometric origin

(2)

0\rightarrow H_{\phi}(-1)\rightarrow E_{\phi}\rightarrow\mathds{1}\rightarrow 0

as required to prove our Theorem.

Note also that the two pieces in the above extension have been constructed from modules under the Hecke algebra which have repeating eigenvalues. The fact that the hypothesis
\epsilon(\phi)=-1
implies the existence of a sub-Hecke module of interior cohomology of Picard surfaces, isomorphic at all finite places either to a induced representation from an Hecke character, or to a subquotient of it, is indeed one of the striking consequences of Rogawski’s work that we crucially exploit. We see this feature of our construction as the main reason to hope that, at least for a careful choice of the class in
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
, such an extension may be non split. Note that by contrast, the mixed Hodge structure on the first cohomology of a modular curve is split precisely because interior and Eisenstein cohomology have different eigenvalues under the action of the Hecke algebra.

1.2.
Our program

With notation as in (
1
), let
\Psi
be a class in
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
, and denote by
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
the corresponding extension
E_{\phi}
as in (
2
). To confirm Conjecture 1, we need to provide a criterion on
\Psi
for the non-triviality of
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
, and to check that it is verified for a wise choice of
\Psi
.

As explained in Corollary
7.6
, there exists a subspace
\partial H^{1}(\theta\phi)
of degree-1 boundary cohomology of the Picard surface
\mathrm{S}_{K}
, such that choosing classes
\Psi^{\prime}
in
\partial H^{1}(\theta\phi)
yields extensions
E_{\phi}(\Psi^{\prime})
that are
dual
to the
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
’s in a precise sense. We aim to exploit this fact for establishing and verifying the desired criterion, through the following steps:

(1)

the support
\Theta
of the class
\Psi
, identified with a formal linear combination of cusps in the Baily-Borel compactification of the Picard surface
\mathrm{S}_{K}
, provides an algebraic cycle
\mathfrak{E}_{\phi}(\Psi)
inducing the extension
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
through an appropriate Abel-Jacobi map; similarly, there exist algebraic cycles
\mathfrak{E}_{\phi}(\Psi^{\prime})
inducing the extensions
E_{\phi}(\Psi^{\prime})
;

(2)

there is a well-defined
height pairing
which can be evaluated on the algebraic cycles
\mathfrak{E}_{\phi}(\Psi)
,
\mathfrak{E}_{\phi}(\Psi^{\prime})
, whose non-vanishing implies that the extension
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
is non-trivial;

(3)

the above height pairing is a (finite) sum of local contributions for each place of
F
, and the contribution from the place at infinity is expressed as the
biextension height
(
[
15
]
,
[
9
]
) of a biextension
B_{\phi}(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})
of Hodge structures having
E_{\phi}(\Psi)
and
E_{\phi}(\Psi)^{\prime}
as subquotients;

(4)

the computation of the biextension height of
B_{\phi}(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})
can be carried out explicitly and yields information about the non-vanishing of the global height pairing.

This program explains the presence of subsection
7.2
of the present paper, devoted to the construction of the biextensions
B_{\phi}(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})
. In order to make the computation of their biextension height accessible, one needs to provide explicit representatives of cohomology classes in
\partial H^{2}(\phi)
, by means of differential forms. The construction of such explicit representatives, using the language of Lie algebra cohomology, has recently been achieved in the work
[
2
]
by the two authors. The latter should hence be seen as providing one of the crucial ingredients required to carry out the above program. Note that it is precisely in the computation of the biextension height that the term
L^{\prime}(\phi,-1)
is expected to appear, making its non-vanishing necessary (and sufficient) for the non-vanishing of the pairing itself, and hence for the non-vanishing of our extension class.

1.3.
Related work and further future directions

When
\phi
is an algebraic Hecke character of odd weight
w
, the Bloch-Beilinson conjectures (
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
) are much more precise than their consequence studied in this paper, i.e. than Conjecture 1. Namely, they say that the order of vanishing of
L(\phi,s)
at the central point
s=\frac{w+1}{2}
should be equal to the (conjecturally finite) rank of a group of algebraic cycles, and they predict the value of the first non-trivial Taylor coefficient
L^{*}(\phi,\frac{w+1}{2})
of
L(\phi,s)
at the central point, up to an algebraic number.

Note that
s=\frac{w+1}{2}
is a
critical
point, in the sense of Deligne
[
12
]
, and hence there exists a canonical
period
associated to it as in
op. cit.
. Moreover, Deligne predicted that when non-zero, the value of
L(\phi,s)
at such a point should be an algebraic multiple of his period. Deligne’s conjecture was later proven for algebraic Hecke characters of
CM
-fields by Blasius
[
6
]
and the recent preprint
[
21
]
proposes a proof for general number fields.

When the order of vanishing of
L(\phi,s)
at the central point is greater than zero, the algebraic cycles whose existence is predicted by Bloch and Beilinson should give rise to non-trivial extensions, both as Hodge structures and Galois representations, through appropriate Abel-Jacobi maps. Moreover, the “transcendental part” of the value
L^{*}(\phi,\frac{w+1}{2})
should be given by the product of Deligne’s period and of the determinant of an
height pairing
, and related to the extension class of the associated extension of Hodge structures in a precise way. In this paper, we propose candidates for the latter extensions. The program that we have outlined in the previous section of this introduction should moreover make it possible to compute their (conjecturally non-zero) extension class. Our further goal is of course to show that this class, and the height pairing that we plan to employ for its calculation, have indeed the expected relationship with the value
L^{*}(\phi,\frac{w+1}{2})
.

Galois representation-theoretic extensions have been constructed in
[
3
]
, under the hypothesis of
odd
order of vanishing of
L(\phi,s)
at the central point, for essentially the same kind of Hecke characters
\phi
of
F
that we consider here (the case of
even
order of vanishing is studied in
[
19
]
). As here, one crucial input in
loc. cit.
is represented by Rogawski’s results, and the source of the Galois representations considered in these works is again the cohomology of Picard modular surfaces. On the other hand, in order to obtain the desired extensions, the authors use
p
-adic families of automorphic forms, without looking at the geometry of the boundary of the Baily-Borel compactification and at Eisenstein cohomology. Hence, their extensions are not of geometric origin in our sense. It would be interesting to study the Galois-theoretic counterpart of our constructions, via étale cohomology, and to put it in relation to their methods.

Structure of the article

In Section

3.
Algebraic Hecke characters and their motives

In this section, we define algebraic Hecke characters, survey the properties of their
L
-functions, and recall basic facts about the associated motives. We then discuss the Bloch-Beilinson conjectures for Hecke characters, and explain how they imply the existence of non-trivial extensions of the unit Hodge structure by a twist of the Hodge structure associated to an algebraic Hecke character.

3.1.
Algebraic Hecke characters

An algebraic Hecke character on a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-torus
\mathcal{T}
is a continuous homomorphism

\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{A}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\,.

We will often refer to a Hecke character on
\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits_{F|{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{G}}_{m,F}
as a Hecke character of
F
.

A Hecke character on
\mathcal{T}
is

if there exist a character

\chi:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

such that the component at infinity
\phi_{\infty}
of
\phi
is equal to the character on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{C}})
induced by
\chi^{-1}
. In this case, we say that
\phi
is of
type

\chi
. When
\mathcal{T}=\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits_{F|{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{G}}_{m,F}
,
\phi
being algebraic amounts to saying that there exist integers
(a,b)
such that
\phi_{\infty}
coincides with

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}&\rightarrow&{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\\
z&\mapsto&z^{-a}\bar{z}^{-b}\end{array}

In this case, we say that
(a,b)
is the
infinity type
of
\phi
, and
w(\phi):=a+b
is its
weight
. Note that the finite part
\phi_{f}
of an algebraic Hecke character takes values in
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{\times}
, see e.g.
[
16
, Sec. 2.5]
. More precisely, it takes values in a number field
F^{\prime}
, called its

.

In our case, algebraic Hecke characters on
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
will be identified to Hecke characters of
F
through the isomorphism (
10
). As at the end of subsection
2.4
, consider the natural embedding
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits_{F|{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{F}
. Fix a character
\chi:\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{F}\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m,F}
, and write it
\chi=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
in the parametrization of subsection
2.3
. Then
\chi
restricts, under the previous embedding, to the map given on
{\mathbb{R}}
-points by

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bar{z}&&\\
&\bar{z}^{-1}z&\\
&&z^{-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto z^{\kappa_{2}}\bar{z}^{\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}}\,,

With these conventions, the weight of an Hecke character
\phi
of
F
of type
\chi=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
is

w(\phi)=\kappa_{1}

and its infinity type is
(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2})
.

For later use, let us recall some basic properties of the
L
-function

of an algebraic Hecke character
\phi
of
F
. The region in which
L(\phi,s)
is expressed as an absolutely convergent Euler product is the half-plane
\mbox{Re}\ s>1+\frac{w(\phi)}{2}
. In this region, the
L
-function is therefore holomorphic, and moreover known to be non-zero. Multiplying the Euler product by appropriate
\Gamma
-factors, we obtain a

which admits a meromorphic continuation to the whole of

. The function
\Lambda(\phi,s)
and the meromorphically continued

are entire unless

; in the latter case,
\Lambda(\phi,s)
has first-order poles precisely in
s=-t
and

, and

has a unique first-order pole in
s=1-t
. Finally, there exists a holomorphic function
\epsilon(s)
such that

satisfies the functional equation

(24)

\Lambda(\phi,s)=\epsilon(s)\Lambda(\phi^{-1},1-s)\,.

To an algebraic Hecke character of

with field of values

, one associates a pure Hodge structure

with coefficients in

, and a compatible system of

, Deligne has used the comparison isomorphism between the Betti and de Rham realizations of the weight
-1
motive
M_{\phi}(\frac{w+1}{2})
to attach to it a canonical non-zero period
c^{+}(M_{\phi})\in{\mathbb{R}}
. He conjectured that if the value
L(M_{\phi},\frac{w+1}{2})
does not vanish, then it should be equal to an algebraic multiple of
c^{+}(M_{\phi})
. He offered no statement when
L(\phi,s)
has a zero at the central point. In order to express what is believed to happen in the latter case, consider the Chow group
CH^{n}(X)
of a smooth, projective variety
X
and its subgroup
CH^{n}(X)_{0}
formed by cycles homologically equivalent to 0, for
n\in\{0,\dots,\dim X\}
. Bloch and Beilinson conjectured the existence of a canonical, non-degenerate
height pairing

h^{n}_{X}:CH^{n}(X)_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\times CH^{\dim X+1-n}(X)_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

Using it, they formulated a conjecture, whose statement we want to express in the special case of
L(\phi,s)
.

In order to do so, we have to extend the conjecture to the Chow groups of our motive
M_{\phi}
. Recall that for
X,n
as above, there is a natural action of
CH^{\dim X}(X\times X)
on
CH^{n}(X)
, preserving
CH^{n}(X)_{0}
. Denote by
\Gamma_{*}
the action of an element
\Gamma\in CH^{\dim X}(X\times X)
. For such a
\Gamma
, the pairing
h^{n}_{X}
restricts to a pairing, also conjecturally non-degenerate

h^{n}_{X,\Gamma}:\Gamma_{*}CH^{n}(X)_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\times\Gamma_{*}CH^{\dim X+1-n}(X)_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

Then, writing
L^{*}(\phi,\frac{w+1}{2})
for the first non-trivial coefficient of
L(\phi,s)
at
s=\frac{w+1}{2}
, the conjecture reads as follows.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
M_{\phi}=(h^{w}(A^{w}_{\phi}),e_{\phi}e_{1,w})
be the Chow motive attached to an algebraic Hecke character
\phi
of
F
of positive odd weight
w
. Then

(1)

\mathop{\rm\tiny{ord}}\nolimits_{s=\frac{w+1}{2}}L(\phi,s)=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(e_{\phi}e_{1,w})_{*}CH^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(A^{w}_{\phi})_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}

(2)

Denoting by
\sim_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
the relation of coinciding up to multiplication by a non-zero algebraic number,

L^{*}(\phi,\frac{w+1}{2})\sim_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}c^{+}(M_{\phi})\det(h^{\frac{w+1}{2}}_{A_{\phi}^{w},e_{\phi}e_{1,w}})

The conjecture extends in the obvious way to the case of negative weight
w
.

Now consider the
Abel-Jacobi map

e_{\phi}e_{1,w}CH^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(A^{w}_{\phi})_{0}\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}}(\mathds{1},e_{\phi}e_{1,w}H^{w}(A^{w}_{\phi})(\frac{w+1}{2}))

Since
A_{\phi}^{w}
is defined over a number field, this map is conjectured to be
injective
(
[
5
, 5.6]
). Using the description (
25
) of
H_{\phi}
, it takes the form

(26)

e_{\phi}e_{1,w}CH^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(A^{w}_{\phi})_{0}\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}}(\mathds{1},H_{\phi}(\frac{w+1}{2}))

Let
\epsilon(\phi)
be the sign of the functional equation of
\phi
. The conjectural injectivity of the map (
26
), point (1) of Conjecture
3.2
, and the fact that
\epsilon(\phi)=-1
implies that
L(\phi,s)
vanishes at the central point, lead to the following (considerably weaker and less precise) conjecture.

Conjecture 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be an algebraic Hecke character of
F
of odd weight w. If
\epsilon(\phi)=-1
, then there exists a non-trivial extension

E_{\phi}\in\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}}(\mathds{1},H_{\phi}(\frac{w+1}{2}))

of geometric origin.

A way of making the above property of being
of geometric origin
precise is the following. There is a triangulated,
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-linear tensor category
\mathop{\rm DM}\nolimits_{c}(F)_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
of constructible motives over
F
, containing the category of Chow motives as a full subcategory. It is equipped with a realization functor

R_{H}:\mathop{\rm DM}\nolimits_{c}(F)_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}\rightarrow D^{b}(\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})

Then, we say that an object
E
of
\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
is of
geometric origin
if it belongs to the full Tannakian subcategory of
\mathop{\rm MHS}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
generated by the cohomology objects of objects belonging to the image of
R_{H}
.

4.
Boundary cohomology

In this section, we fix one of the Picard modular surfaces
\mathrm{S}_{K}
introduced in Section
2.7
, and let
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
be a connected component of
\mathrm{S}_{K}({\mathbb{C}})
. We aim to describe the boundary cohomology of
\mathrm{S}_{K}({\mathbb{C}})
with coefficients in local systems coming from representations of the underlying group
\mathrm{G}
, together with their mixed Hodge structures and
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
-actions. To do so, we will consider two different compactifications, the Baily-Borel compactification
\mathrm{S}^{*}_{\Gamma}
(a complex projective variety) and the Borel-Serre compactification
\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{\Gamma}
(a real analytic manifold with corners).

4.1.
Boundary of the Baily-Borel compactification

For facts recalled in this paragraph, we refer to
[
28
, Sects. 3.6-3.7]
. Any
{\mathbb{Q}}
-conjugacy class of parabolics in
\mathrm{G}
provides a set of strata of the boundary of the Baily-Borel compactification of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
. More precisely, given a representative
\mathrm{P}
of such a conjugacy class, there is a canonical reductive subgroup
\mathrm{M}
of the Levi component of
\mathrm{P}
, and a canonical conjugacy class of morphisms

(27)

h_{\mathrm{M}}:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{M}_{{\mathbb{R}}}

providing a Shimura datum attached to
\mathrm{M}
. Each of the strata of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}^{*}
corresponding to
\mathrm{P}
is then a connected component of a Shimura variety associated with this Shimura datum.

In our case, the only
{\mathbb{Q}}
-conjugacy class of parabolics in
\mathrm{G}
is the one of the Borel
\mathrm{B}
. The Levi component of
\mathrm{B}
is the torus
\mathrm{T}
, and hence, the positive dimensional, reductive subgroup
\mathrm{M}
is a torus as well. Thus, the Baily-Borel boundary is a disjoint union of 0-dimensional strata, called
cusps
.

4.2.
Boundary of the Borel-Serre compactification

For facts recalled in this paragraph, we refer to
[
17
, pp. 567-570]
. The Borel-Serre compactification
\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{\Gamma}
of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
admits a canonical projection
\pi
to the Baily-Borel one. Let us describe, in our situation, the fiber of
\pi
over each cusp
p
of
\mathrm{S}^{*}_{\Gamma}
. To do so, let us write

\mathrm{U}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{U}\cap\mathrm{G}^{\mathop{\rm\tiny{der}}\nolimits}

Denote by
A_{\mathrm{G}}
the identity component of the real points of the maximal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-split subtorus
\mathrm{T}^{s}
of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathop{\rm\tiny{der}}\nolimits}
. From equation (
10
), we see that there is an isomorphism of Lie groups
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
which by equation (
11
) restricts to
A_{\mathrm{G}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{>0}.

Define
{}^{0}T
as the Lie group
A_{\mathrm{G}}\backslash\mathrm{T}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
. It is isomorphic to
S^{1}
. Denote by
K^{T}_{\infty}
the intersection with
{}^{0}T
of a maximal compact subgroup of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathop{\rm\tiny{der}}\nolimits}({\mathbb{R}})
. By conjugating the maximal compact subgroup of equation (
18
) via the change of basis matrix given in (
3
), one sees that
K^{T}_{\infty}
coincides with
{}^{0}T
itself. On the other hand, denote
\Gamma_{\mathrm{U}}:=\Gamma\cap\mathrm{U}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
. The general theory tells us that the space
\pi^{-1}(p)
has the structure of a fibration, with base a quotient of
{}^{0}T\backslash K^{T}_{\infty}
(hence a point in our case), and with fiber diffeomorphic to the nilmanifold

\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{U}_{\Gamma}}:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{U}}\backslash\mathrm{U}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})

This means that in our case,
\pi^{-1}(p)\simeq\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{U}_{\Gamma}}
. Now
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}
is inserted into an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{W}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}^{\prime}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{U}^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0

where
\mathrm{W}
is the commutator subgroup of
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}
, isomorphic to
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
, and
\widetilde{\mathrm{U}^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits_{F|{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{G}}_{a,F}
. Since we are working with a torsion-free
\Gamma
, this yields a diffeomorphism of
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{U}_{\Gamma}}
with a
S^{1}
-fiber bundle over an elliptic curve.

4.3.
Boundary cohomology and its Hodge structure

Let
V_{\lambda}
be the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{G}
of highest weight
\lambda
. Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}
be a compactification of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
and let
j:\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}
be the corresponding open immersion, with boundary
i:\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}
. Boundary cohomology of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
with coefficients in
V_{\lambda}
is defined as hypercohomology of a complex of sheaves over
\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}
:

\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda}):=\mathbb{H}^{n}(\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}},i^{*}Rj_{*}V_{\lambda})

By proper base change, it does not depend on the choice of the compactification
\widetilde{\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}}
. There is a natural long exact sequence

(28)

\cdots\rightarrow H_{c}^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\rightarrow H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\rightarrow\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\rightarrow\cdots

By choosing as compactification the Baily-Borel compactification
S_{\Gamma}^{*}
, mixed Hodge theory endows the spaces
\partial H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})
with a canonical mixed Hodge structure, and the above long exact sequence becomes one of mixed Hodge structures.

The following Proposition gives a complete description of the mixed Hodge structure on
\partial H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})
, using the spaces
I_{\mu,\nu}
introduced in (
15

[
1
]
, but we have chosen to write it in detail using our parametrizations and notations and to provide an outline of its proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda=(k_{1},k_{2},c,r)
. Let
m
be the number of cusps of
\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma}
. Then, for any integer
n
, the mixed Hodge structures
\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})
are semisimple, and described as follows:

(1)

the space
\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})
is trivial for
n
outside the set
\{0,\dots,3\}
.

(2)

For
n=0
,

\partial H^{0}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\cong I_{k_{2},k_{1}-k_{2}}^{\oplus m}

\mbox{where}\ I_{k_{2},k_{1}-k_{2}}\ \mbox{is endowed with a 1-dimensional pure $F$-Hodge structure of}

\mbox{\emph{weight}:}\ r-k_{1}

\mbox{\emph{type}:}\ \left(-\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}+c-2r}{4},-\frac{3k_{1}-k_{2}-c-2r}{4}\right)

(3)

For
n=1

\partial H^{1}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\simeq I_{k_{1}+1,-k_{1}+k_{2}-2}^{\oplus m}\oplus I_{-k_{2}-2,k_{1}+1}^{\oplus m}

\mbox{\emph{weight}:}\ (r+1)-(k_{1}-k_{2})

\mbox{\emph{type}:}\ \left(-\frac{k_{1}-3k_{2}-4+c-2r}{4},-\frac{3k_{1}-k_{2}-c-2r}{4}\right)

(4)

For
n=2

\partial H^{2}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\simeq I_{k_{1}-k_{2},-k_{1}-3}^{\oplus m}\oplus I_{-k_{1}-3,k_{2}}^{\oplus m}

\mbox{where}\ I_{k_{1}-k_{2},-k_{1}-3}\ \mbox{is endowed with a 1-dimensional pure $F$-Hodge structure of}

\mbox{\emph{weight}:}\ (r+2)+k_{2}+1

\mbox{\emph{type}:}\ \left(-\frac{k_{1}-3k_{2}-4+c-2r}{4},-\frac{-k_{1}-k_{2}-8-c-2r}{4}\right)

\mbox{and}\ I_{-k_{1}-3,k_{2}}\ \mbox{is endowed with a 1-dimensional pure $F$-Hodge structure of}

\mbox{\emph{weight}:}\ (r+2)+(k_{1}-k_{2})+1

\mbox{\emph{type}:}\ \left(-\frac{-3k_{1}+k_{2}-8+c-2r}{4},-\frac{-k_{1}+3k_{2}-4-c-2r}{4}\right)

(5)

For
n=3

\partial H^{3}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\simeq I_{-k_{1}-4,-k_{2}-2}^{\oplus m}

\mbox{where}\ I_{-k_{1}-4,-k_{2}-2}\ \mbox{is endowed with a 1-dimensional pure $F$-Hodge structure of}

\mbox{\emph{weight}:}\ (r+3)+k_{1}+1

\mbox{\emph{type}:}\ \left(-\frac{-3k_{1}+k_{2}-8+c-2r}{4},-\frac{-k_{1}-k_{2}-8-c-2r}{4}\right)

Proof.

The assertion of point (1) follows from the vanishing discussed in paragraph
2.10

To compute boundary cohomology in degree

\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\simeq i^{*}R^{n}j_{*}V_{\lambda}

This is also an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures, once we consider the mixed Hodge structure on

\partial H^{n}(\mathrm{S}_{\Gamma},V_{\lambda})\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{c\in\partial\mathrm{S}^{*}_{\Gamma}}i_{c}^{*}R^{n}j_{*}V_{\lambda}

Now consider the subgroup
\mathrm{M}
of
\mathrm{T}

(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}z_{1}z_{2}&0&0\\
0&z_{1}&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),z_{1}z_{2})\,

Through

\mathrm{M}_{{\mathbb{R}}}

By
[

Since

4.4.
Automorphic structure of boundary cohomology

We end this section by describing the automorphic structure of boundary cohomology. For this purpose, we switch to the adelic setting and work with the spaces

\mathrm{S}_{K}({\mathbb{C}}):={\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash(X\times{\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/K)

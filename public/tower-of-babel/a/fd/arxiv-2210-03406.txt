Abstract
. The valuative criterion for proper maps of schemes has many applications in arithmetic, e.g. specializing ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} -points to 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -points. For algebraic stacks, the usual valuative criterion for proper maps is ill-suited for these kind of arguments, since it only gives a specialization point defined over an extension of the residue field, e.g. a ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} -point will specialize to an 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} -point for some n n . We give a new valuative criterion for proper maps of tame stacks which solves this problem and is well-suited for arithmetic applications. As a consequence, we prove that the Lang-Nishimura theorem holds for tame stacks.



1.
Introduction

The well known and extremely useful valuative criterion for properness says, in particular, that if
X\to Y
is a proper morphism of schemes,
R
is a DVR with quotient field
K
and residue field
k
, and we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}

there exists a unique lifting
\operatorname{Spec}R\to X
of
\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
extending
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
. This has many arithmetic applications: most of them use that the statement above ensures the existence of a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
of the composite
\operatorname{Spec}k\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
.

If
X
and
Y
are algebraic stack, and
X\to Y
is a proper morphism, then this fails, even in very simple examples, unless
X\to Y
is representable. The correct general statement is that there exists a local extension of DVR
R\to R^{\prime}
, such that if we denote by
K^{\prime}
the fraction field of
R^{\prime}
, the composite
\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
has a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}\to X
extending the composite
\operatorname{Spec}K^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
(see for example
[
Sta21
,
Tag 0CLY
]
). For arithmetic applications this is problematic, because the extension
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
will typically induce a nontrivial extension of residue fields, so it does not imply that
\operatorname{Spec}k\to Y
lifts to
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
, as in the case of schemes.

When
X
and
Y
are Deligne–Mumford stacks over a field of characteristic
0
a substitute was found by the first author in
[
Bre21
, Theorem 1]
. In this note we we extend this, in a somewhat more precise form, to positive and mixed characteristic. In this context the correct generality is that of
tame stacks
, in the sense of
[
AOV08
]
. Tame stacks are algebraic stacks with finite inertia, such that the automorphism group scheme of any object over a field is linearly reductive. In characteristic
0
they coincide with Deligne–Mumford stacks with finite inertia, but in positive and mixed characteristic there are Deligne–Mumford stacks with finite inertia that are not tame, and tame stacks that are not Deligne–Mumford.

In our version the role of the DVR
R^{\prime}
above is played by a
root stack

\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
; this is not a scheme, but a tame stack with a map
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
, which is an isomorphism above
\operatorname{Spec}K\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
(see the discussion at the beginning of §
3
). The statement of our main theorem
3.1
is that if
X\to Y
is a proper morphism of tame algebraic stacks and we have a commutative diagram as above, there exists a unique positive integer
n
and a unique representable lifting
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to X
of the composite
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
extending
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
. The key point for arithmetic applications is that the closed point
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}R
lifts to
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
. This statement is much harder to prove in arbitrary characteristic than in characteristic
0
.

Besides the original application to Grothendieck’s section conjecture in
[
Bre21
]
, this valuative criterion has been applied in
[
BVb
]
to give new proofs and stronger versions of the genericity theorem for essential dimension.

Recall that the Lang-Nishimura theorem states that the property of having a rational point is a birational invariant of smooth proper varieties. Another consequence of our version of the valuative criterion is that the Lang-Nishimura theorem generalizes to tame stacks, see
4.1
. Our version of the Lang-Nishimura theorem has an immediate corollary, which we find surprising: if
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth tame stack which is generically a scheme and
\overline{M}\to M
is a resolution of singularities of the coarse moduli space
\mathcal{M}\to M
, then a rational point of
M(k)
lifts to
\mathcal{M}
if and only if it lifts to
\overline{M}
. This gives a hint of the applications of the Lang–Nishimura theorem to fields of moduli, which will be the subject of the forthcoming papers
[
BVa
,
Bre
]
.

2.
Notations and conventions

We will follow the conventions of
[
Knu71
]
and
[
LMB00
]
; so the diagonals of algebraic spaces and algebraic stacks will be separated and of finite type. In particular, every algebraic space will be

, in the sense of
[
Sta21
, Definition 03I8]
.

We will follow the terminology of
[
AOV08
]
: a
tame stack
is an algebraic stack
X
with finite inertia, such that its geometric points have linearly reductive automorphism group. This is equivalent to requiring that
X
is étale locally over its moduli space a quotient by a finite, linearly reductive group scheme
[
AOV08
, Theorem 3.2]
.

More generally, a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of algebraic stacks is

if the relative inertia group stack
I_{X/Y}\to X
, defined as in
[
Sta21
,
Section 050P
]
, is finite and has linearly reductive geometric fibers. See
[
AOV11
, §3]
.

Using
[
Sta21
,
Lemma 0CPK
]
one can easily prove the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of algebraic stacks. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

f
is tame.

(2)

If
Z\to Y
is a morphism, and
Z
is a scheme, then
Z\times_{Y}X
is a tame stack.

(3)

If
Z\to Y
is a morphism, and
Z
a tame stack, then
Z\times_{Y}X
is also tame.

Furthermore, if
X
is tame, then the morphism
f
is also tame.

3.
The valuative criterion

A basic example of tame stacks is
root stacks
(see
[
AGV08
, Appendix B2]
). We will need this in the following situation. Let
R
be a DVR with uniformizing parameter
\pi
and residue field
k\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}R/(\pi)
. If
n
is a positive integer, we will denote by
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
the
n^{\text{th}}
root of the Cartier divisor
\operatorname{Spec}k\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
. It is a stack over
\operatorname{Spec}R
, such that given a morphism
\phi\colon T\to\operatorname{Spec}R
, the groupoid of liftings
T\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
is equivalent to the groupoid whose objects are triples
(L,s,\alpha)
, where
L
is an invertible sheaf on
T
,
s\in L(T)
is a global section of
L
, and
\alpha
is an isomorphism
L^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{T}
, such that
\alpha(s^{\otimes n})=\phi^{\sharp}(\pi)
. Alternatively,
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
can be described as the quotient stack
[\operatorname{Spec}R[t]/(t^{n}-\pi)/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}]
, where the action of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
on
\operatorname{Spec}R[t]/(t^{n}-\pi)
is by multiplication on
t
. The morphism
\rho\colon\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is an isomorphism outside of
\operatorname{Spec}k\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
, while the reduced fiber
\rho^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{red}}
is non-canonically isomorphic to the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{k}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
. In particular the embedding
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}R
lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
.

The following is our version of the valuative criterion for properness.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a tame, proper morphism of algebraic stacks,
R
a DVR with quotient field
K
. Suppose that we have a
2
-commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Then there exists a unique positive integer
n
and a representable lifting
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to X
of the given morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
, making the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}K\ }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}

2
-commutative. Furthermore, the lifting is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

Corollary 3.2
.

In the situation above, if
k
is the residue field of
R
, the composite
\operatorname{Spec}k\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y
has a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
.

As one would expect, these statements fail without the tameness hypothesis, even when
Y
is a scheme and
X
is a separated Deligne–Mumford stack.

Example 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime,
R
a DVR whose fraction field
K
has characteristic
0
and contains a
p
-th root of
1
, denoted by
\zeta_{p}
, while its residue field
k
has characteristic
p
and is not perfect. An example would be the localization of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}][t]
at a prime ideal of height 1 containing
p
.

Choose an element
a\in R^{*}
whose image in
k
is not a
p
-th power, and set
R^{\prime}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}R(\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{a})
. Then
R^{\prime}
is a DVR, since the
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}k=k(\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{\overline{a}})
is a field (here
\overline{a}
is the class of
a
in
k
). Write
K^{\prime}=K(\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{a})
for its fraction field and
k^{\prime}=k(\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{\overline{a}})
for its residue field.

Call
C_{p}
the cyclic group of order
p
generated by
\zeta_{p}\in K^{*}
. The extension
K^{\prime}/K
is Galois with cyclic Galois group
C_{p}
acting by
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{a}\mapsto\zeta_{p}\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[p]{a}
. The action of
C_{p}
on
K^{\prime}
naturally extends to
R^{\prime}
.

Let
X
be the quotient stack
[\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}/C_{p}]
; this is a separated Deligne–Mumford stack, but it is not tame. Since
(R^{\prime})^{C_{p}}=R
the moduli space of
X
is
\operatorname{Spec}R
, and we have we have a natural map
X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
, which is an isomorphism over
\operatorname{Spec}K\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
. Since
k^{\prime}/k
is purely inseparable, then
X_{k}(k)
is empty: such a
k
-rational point would correspond to a
C_{p}
-torsor
\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}k
with an equivariant morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}
and thus an embedding of
k^{\prime}
in the étale
k

, which is clearly absurd. In particular, there is no map
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to X
for any
n
.

Definition 3.4
.

In the situation of Theorem
3.1
, we call the integer
n
the
loop index
of the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
at the place associated with
R\subseteq K
. If the loop index is
1
, we say that
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
is
untangled
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
be an extension of DVRs with ramification index
e
, and let
K\subseteq K^{\prime}
be the fraction fields of
R
and
R^{\prime}
respectively. If
X
is a tame stack proper over
R
and
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
is a morphism with loop index

, the composite
\operatorname{Spec}K^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
has loop index equal to

.

Proof.

Write
m\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}n/\gcd(n,e)
; the statement follows from the fact that there is a natural representable morphism
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
inducing the given morphism
\operatorname{Spec}K^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}K
.
∎

We spend the rest of this section proving Theorem
3.1
. Given a DVR
R
and
\pi\in R
a uniformizing parameter, write
R^{(n)}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}R[t]/(t^{n}-\pi)

\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}=[\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}]

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a DVR,
m,n
integers. A morphism
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
over

exists if and only if

, and in this case it is unique up to equivalence.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that a section
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(m)}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}

, and in this case it is unique up to equivalence.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a DVR,
D\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
the divisor corresponding to the closed point,
m,n_{i},r_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
positive integers, with
n_{i}\geq 2
for every
i
. The fibered product

X=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n_{i}]{\operatorname{Spec}R,r_{i}D}.

is normal if and only if
m=1
and
r_{1}=1
.

Proof.

Let
V_{n,r}
be the scheme
\operatorname{Spec}R[t,s]_{s}/(t^{n}s-\pi^{r})
, there is an action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
V_{n,r}
given by
(\lambda,t,s)\mapsto(\lambda t,\lambda^{-n}s)
and
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R,rD}\simeq[V_{n,r}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
, see
[
AGV08
, Appendix B]
. Consider the fibered product

Y=\prod_{i}V_{n_{i},r_{i}}=R[t_{1},s_{1},\dots,t_{m},s_{m}]/(t_{i}^{n_{i}}s_{i}-\pi^{r_{i}}).

The prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m},\pi)
is the generic point of the special fiber and has height
1
. Since
V_{r,n}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R,rD}
is smooth, then
Y\to X
is smooth and hence
X
is normal if and only if
Y
is normal at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Now consider the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{0}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m},\pi)\subset R[t_{1},s_{1},\dots,t_{m},s_{m}]
,
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
have equal residue fields and there is a natural surjective linear map
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}\to\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}
. We have that
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}
has dimension
m+1
generated by the classes of
[t_{i}],[\pi]
. If
m=1
and
r_{1}=1
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}
has dimension

and
[\pi]
is in the kernel of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}\to\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}
, hence

is normal at
\mathfrak{p}
.

On the other hand, assume that
Y
is normal at
\mathfrak{p}
, so that
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}
has dimension
1
. Since
n_{i}\geq 2
for every
i
, the kernel of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}\to\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}
is generated by the classes
[\pi^{r_{i}}]
and hence has dimension
1
if

for some

, and dimension
0
otherwise. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{0}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}^{2}
has dimension
m+1
and
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}
has dimension
1
, this implies that

and

.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain with fraction field
K
,
D\subset\operatorname{Spec}A
an effective, reduced divisor. Let
f:X\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{(\operatorname{Spec}A,D)}
be a representable, proper morphism. Every generic section
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
of
f
extends uniquely to a global section
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{(\operatorname{Spec}A,D)}\to X
.

Proof.

Let
Y\subset X
be the schematic image of a generic section
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
, we want to prove that
Y\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{(\operatorname{Spec}A,D)}
is an isomorphism. Since the problem is local, we may assume that
A=R
is a DVR and
D
is either empty or the closed point. If
D
is empty, then
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R,D}=\operatorname{Spec}R
and this is simply the valuative criterion of properness. Suppose that
D
is the closed point. Consider the flat morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
and write
X^{\prime}=X\times_{\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}}\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}
,
Y^{\prime}=Y\times_{\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}}\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}
. Thanks to
[
Sta21
,
Lemma 0CMK
]
we have that
Y^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
is the schematic image of the induced generic section
\operatorname{Spec}K^{(n)}\to X^{\prime}
. By the valuative criterion of properness, there is a section
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\to X^{\prime}
which is a closed immersion since

is representable, this implies that
Y^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}
is an isomorphism. It follows that
Y\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
is an isomorphism, too.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
R
be a DVR,
n,m
positive integers. Assume that
n
is prime with the residue characteristic of
R
. Consider the
\mu_{n}
-torsor
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
. There exists a unique way of extending the action of

to
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}
, and the quotient
[\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}/\mu_{n}]

\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[mn]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
.

Proof.

We have a natural action

induced by the action on
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}
. The action

gives a structure of
\mu_{n}
-torsor to the natural morphism
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[mn]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
. Let
\eta:\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}\times_{R}\mu_{n}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}
be an action such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}\times_{R}\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\times_{R}\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

is
2
-commutative, we want to show that

are equivalent.

Let

be the pullback of the closed point of

, since

Corollary 3.10

Let

be a DVR,

. Let

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be a local, quasi-finite étale extension of DVRs and
X
a tame stack over
R
,
X^{\prime}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}X_{R^{\prime}}
. If
X^{\prime}\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}}
, then
X\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the residue field of
R
, clearly we have that
X_{K}\to\operatorname{Spec}K
is an isomorphism. Write
A=\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
, since

is quasi-finite étale over

is a product of Dedekind domains with a finite number of closed points. Let

Let

be the composite, and consider the two projections

is separated, then

is separated, too, and hence
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits(p_{1}^{*}\phi,p_{2}^{*}\phi)
is an algebraic stack with a proper, representable morphism to
S
. There is a generic section
S_{K}\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits(p_{1}^{*}\phi,p_{2}^{*}\phi)
which extends to a global section thanks to Lemma
3.8
, this gives descent data for a morphism
f:\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to X
(the cocycle condition can be checked on the generic point, where it is obvious). Since the base change to
R^{\prime}
of
f
is an isomorphism, we have that
f
is an isomorphism, too.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
X
be a normal, tame stack of finite type over a DVR
R
, and assume that there is a generic section
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
which is an open, scheme-theoretically dense embedding. Then
X\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
for some
n
.

Proof.

Since
X
is of finite type over
R
, there exists a DVR
R_{0}\subset R
which is the localization of a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra of finite type and a stack
X_{0}/R_{0}
such that
X\simeq X_{0,R}
. Furthermore, we may assume that the uniformizing parameter of
R_{0}
maps to a uniformizing parameter of
R
, so that
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R_{0}}\times_{R_{0}}\operatorname{Spec}R\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
. Up to replacing
R,X
with
R_{0},X_{0}
we may assume that
R
is Nagata. Let
k
be the residue field of
R
and
p
its characteristic.

By
[
AOV08
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a DVR
R^{\prime}
quasi-finite and étale over
R
and a finite, flat, linearly reductive group scheme
G/R^{\prime}
with an action on a scheme
U
finite over
R^{\prime}
such that
X_{R^{\prime}}\simeq[U/G]
. Up to enlarging
R^{\prime}
, by
[
AOV08
, Lemma 2.20]
there exists a diagonalizable flat, closed subgroup
\Delta\subset G
such that
H\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}G/\Delta
is constant and tame. We may furthermore assume that the degree of
\Delta
is a power of
p
. Thanks to Lemma
3.11
, we may assume
R^{\prime}=R
.

Case 1.

X
is tame and Deligne-Mumford. Since
\Delta_{k}
is connected and
X
is Deligne-Mumford and generically a scheme, the action of
\Delta
is free (because otherwise
X
would have ramified inertia), hence up to replacing
U
with
U/\Delta
we may assume that
G
is constant and tame. Since
X
is normal and
G
is constant and tame, then
U
is normal, too. If
u\in U
is a geometric point, the stabilizer
G_{u}
acts faithfully on the tangent space, hence the automorphism groups of the points of
X
are cyclic and tame. By
[
Ryd11
, Lemma 8.5]
and Lemma
3.7
, since
X
is normal we have
X\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
for some
n
.

Case 2.

X
is tame. Let
V\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}U/\Delta
and
Y_{0}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}[V/H]
, we have that
Y_{0}
is Deligne-Mumford and there is a natural birational morphism
X\to Y_{0}
whose relative inertia is diagonalizable. Let
Y\to Y_{0}
be the normalization, it is finite over
Y_{0}
since
R
is Nagata and since
X
is normal the morphism
X\to Y_{0}
lifts to a morphism
X\to Y
. By case
1
, there exists an
n
prime with
p
and an isomorphism
Y\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
. Consider the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\to\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
, it is a
\mu_{n}
-torsor and hence finite étale since
n
is prime with
p
, it follows that the base change
X\times_{\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}}\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}
is normal with diagonalizable inertia. By
[
Ryd11
, Lemma 8.5]
and Lemma
3.7
, we have
X\times_{\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}}\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R^{(n)}}
for some integer
m
, hence
X\simeq\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[mn]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
thanks to Corollary
3.10
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By base change, we may assume that
Y=\operatorname{Spec}R
and that
X
is a tame stack proper over
R
. With an argument similar to the one in the proof of Proposition
3.12
, we may reduce to the case in which
R
is Nagata.

By
[
Ryd11
, Theorem B]
, we may assume that
\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
is an open, scheme theoretically dense embedding. Since
R
is Nagata, the normalization
\overline{X}
is finite and representable over
X
. By Proposition
3.12
we have
\overline{X}=\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
, hence an extension exists. If
m
is another integer with a representable extension
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to X
, it factors through
\overline{X}=\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
since
\hskip-1.5pt\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
is normal by Lemma
3.7
. We conclude the proof of Theorem
3.1
by Lemma
3.6
.

∎

4.
The Lang–Nishimura theorem

Here is our version of the Lang–Nishimura theorem for tame stacks.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme and
X\dashrightarrow Y
a rational map of algebraic stacks over
S
, with
X
locally noetherian and integral and
Y
tame and proper over
S
. Let
k
be a field,
s\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to S
a morphism. Assume that
s
lifts to a regular point
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
; then it also lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to Y
.

In the standard version of the Lang–Nishimura theorem (see for example
[
Poo17
, Theorem 3.6.11]
), which is a standard tool in arithmetic geometry,
X
and
Y
are schemes, and
S=\operatorname{Spec}k
. In the applications that we have in mind, the additional flexibility of having a base scheme is important.

Proof.

According to
[
LMB00
, Théorème 6.3]
we can find a smooth morphism
U\to X
with a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}k\to U
of
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
; hence we can replace
X
by
U
, and assume that
X
is scheme. Furthermore, if
x
denotes the image of
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
and
k(x)
its residue field, we have a factorization
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}k(x)\to X
, and we may assume
k=k(x)
. If
x
has height
0
, then
\operatorname{Spec}k
dominates
X
, and the composite
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X\dashrightarrow Y
is well defined.

Otherwise, call
U\subseteq X
the open subset where
f
is defined. By
[
BVb
, Lemma 4.3]
there exists a DVR
R
with residue field
k=k(x)
and a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R\to X
that maps the generic point
\operatorname{Spec}K
of
\operatorname{Spec}R
into
U
. Thus we get a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}K\to U
, and we apply Corollary
3.2
to the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\ }
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

thus getting the desired morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to Y
.
∎

The Lang–Nishimura theorem fails for non-tame separated stacks. Let us give two examples, one in mixed characteristic, the other in positive characteristic.

Example 4.2
.

Let
X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be the stack constructed in
3.3
, it is a non-tame regular Deligne-Mumford stack. Let
k
be the residue field of
R
. There is a rational map
\operatorname{Spec}R\dashrightarrow X
and
\operatorname{Spec}R
has a
k
rational point, but
X
has no
k
-rational points.

Example 4.3
.

Let
C_{0}
be a smooth, projective curve of positive genus over a finite field
F
of characteristic
p
with
C_{0}(F)\neq\emptyset
. Let
a
be an indeterminate, write
k\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}F(a)
and
C\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}C_{0,k}
; since
C_{0}
has positive genus
C(k)=C_{0}(F)
is finite. Let
f\in k(C)
be a rational function such that each rational point is a zero of
f
(this can be easily found using Riemann-Roch). Consider the ramified cover
D\to C
given by the equation

t^{p}-f^{p-1}t=a\,;

in other words,
D
is the smooth projective curve associated with the field extension
k(C)[t]/(t^{p}-f^{p-1}t-a)
. Let
c\in C(k)
be a rational point, and write
R_{c}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\mathcal{O}_{C,c}[t]/(t^{p}-f^{p-1}t-a)
, it is a normal domain: if
\overline{R}_{c}
is the normalization, both
R_{c}\otimes k(C)\to\overline{R}_{c}\otimes k(C)
and
R_{c}\otimes k\to\overline{R}_{c}\otimes k
are isomorphisms for degree reasons since
R_{c}\otimes k=k[t]/(t^{p}-a)
is a field of degree
p
over
k
. Hence,
R_{c}
is a DVR with residue field
k^{\prime}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}k[t]/(t^{p}-a)
. It follows that
D
has no
k
-rational points.

The cyclic group
C_{p}
acts on
D
by
t\mapsto t+f
, the field extension
k(D)/k(C)
is a cyclic Galois cover and
C
is the quotient scheme
D/C_{p}
. Let
X
be the quotient stack
[D/C_{p}]
, there is a natural birational morphism
X\to C=D/C_{p}
. A rational point
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
corresponds to a
C_{p}
-torsor
\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}k
with an equivariant morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to D
: since the fibers of
D\to C
over rational points are isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}
, a rational point of
X
gives an embedding of
k^{\prime}
in an étale algebra
A
, which is clearly absurd. It follows that
X
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack over
k
with
X(k)=\emptyset
and a birational map
C\dashrightarrow X
.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, we can decide whether a residual gerbe of a tame stack is neutral or not by looking at a resolution of singularities of the coarse moduli space. We find this rather surprising.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a locally noetherian, regular and integral tame stack with coarse moduli space
X\to M
, and
\overline{M}\to M
a proper birational morphism, with
\overline{M}
integral and regular. Assume that there is a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}k(M)\to X
of the generic point
\operatorname{Spec}k(M)\to M
.

If
k
is a field and
m\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to M
a morphism, then
m
lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
if and only if it lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\overline{M}
.∎

So, for example, if
M
is regular all morphisms
\operatorname{Spec}k\to M
lift to
X
, and all residual gerbes are neutral.

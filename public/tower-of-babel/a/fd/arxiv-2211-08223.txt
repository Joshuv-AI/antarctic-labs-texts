Jump-preserving polynomial interpolation in non-manifold polyhedra
Martin Averseng

Abstract
We construct a piecewise-polynomial interpolant u ↦ Π ​ u u\mapsto\Pi u for functions u : Ω ∖ Γ → ℝ u:\Omega\setminus\Gamma\to\mathbb{R} , where Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} is a Lipschitz polyhedron and Γ ⊂ Ω \Gamma\subset\Omega is a possibly non-manifold ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -dimensional hypersurface. This interpolant enjoys approximation properties in relevant Sobolev norms, as well as a set of additional algebraic properties, namely, Π 2 = Π \Pi^{2}=\Pi , and Π \Pi preserves homogeneous boundary values and jumps of its argument on Γ \Gamma . As an application, we obtain a bounded discrete right-inverse of the “jump” operator across Γ \Gamma , and an error estimate for a Galerkin scheme to solve a second-order elliptic PDE in Ω \Omega with a prescribed jump across Γ \Gamma .

The motivation for this work is the discretization of partial differential equations (PDEs) in complex geometries, in particular, the complement in

This work is especially connected to the development, for such geometries, of boundary element methods (BEM)

To set a rigorous framework for this problem, we follow

Lemma 2.1

For each

Proof.

and the fact that two meshes of the same polyhedron admit a common subdivision, see

The equality

Let

Let

At least if

(Multi-trace interpolant)

(ii)

The operator

\left\|\mathcal{L}u\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}=\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\quad\textup{and}\quad\gamma_{\Gamma}\mathcal{L}u=\gamma_{\Gamma}u

We define

\Pi_{h}(U-U_{h})\in H^{1}_{0,\Gamma}(\Omega)

\|\Pi_{h}u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\leq C(\gamma_{0},p,1)\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\,.

\begin{split}&\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{h}^{*}}h_{F^{*}}^{2s-1}\left\|u-\Phi_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leq C_{\rm tr}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}^{2s-2}\left\|\mathcal{L}u-\Pi_{h}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{K}^{2s}\left\lvert\mathcal{L}u-\Pi_{h}\mathcal{L}u\right\rvert_{H^{1}}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leq C_{\rm tr}C(\gamma_{0},p,1)\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}^{2s}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\left\lvert\mathcal{L}u\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{1}(K)}\\
&\qquad\leq C_{\rm tr}C(\gamma_{0},p,1)\textup{diam}(\Omega)^{2s}\left\|\mathcal{L}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\\
&\qquad=C_{\rm tr}C(\gamma_{0},p,1)\textup{diam}(\Omega)^{2s}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\end{split}

where
\textup{diam}(\Omega)
is the diameter of
\Omega
. Summing these two estimates gives (iv).
∎

3.1
Bounded discrete right inverse for the jump operator

With the notation of Section
2
, define the discrete jump space

\widetilde{V}^{p}(\Gamma_{h}):=[{V}^{p}(\Omega_{h},\Gamma)]_{\Gamma}=\left\{[u_{h}]_{\Gamma}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}u_{h}\in V^{p}_{h}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)\right\}\,;

in other words,
\widetilde{V}^{p}(\Gamma_{h})
is the set of equivalence classes in
H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
of the elements of
V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
for the relation “differing by a function in
H^{1}(\Omega)
”.

Corollary 3.1
.

With the same assumptions as in
Theorem

1.2
, there exists a linear operator

E_{h}:\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\to V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)

such that

(i)

[E_{h}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}]_{\Gamma}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}
for all
\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}\in\widetilde{V}^{p}(\Gamma_{h})
,

(ii)

for all
\widetilde{\varphi}\in\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
,

\left\|E_{h}\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\leq C(\gamma_{0},p,1)\left\|\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\,.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}:\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\to{H}^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
be the linear isometry
such that

[\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}]_{\Gamma}=\widetilde{\varphi}\quad\textup{and}\quad\left\|\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|_{{H}^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}=\left\|\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\quad\forall\widetilde{\varphi}\in\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\,.

We put
E_{h}:=\Pi_{h}\circ\mathcal{E}
. Given
\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}\in\widetilde{V}^{p}(\Gamma_{h})
, let
w_{h}\in{V}^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
be such that
[w_{h}]_{\Gamma}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}
. Then, one has
[\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}-w_{h}]_{\Gamma}=0
, which exactly means that
\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}-w_{h}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the same is true of
\Pi_{h}(\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}-w_{h})
, hence

0=[\Pi_{h}(\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}-w_{h})]_{\Gamma}=[\Pi_{h}(\mathcal{E}\,\widetilde{\varphi}_{h})-\Pi_{h}w_{h}]_{\Gamma}=[E_{h}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}]_{\Gamma}-[w_{h}]_{\Gamma}=[E_{h}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}]_{\Gamma}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}\,.

This proves (i). The property (ii) is immediate.
∎

3.2
Boundary value problems with prescribed jumps

In
[
33
, Section 5]
, Scott and Zhang show that their interpolant provides a “systematical way for averaging [a prescribed] boundary data” in a boundary value problem. Here we translate this idea to solve an elliptic boundary value problem with a prescribed jump

\begin{gathered}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\alpha_{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0\quad\textup{in}\quad\Omega\setminus\Gamma\,,\\
[u]_{\Gamma}=[g]_{\Gamma}\,,\\[5.0pt]
u=0\quad\textup{ on }\partial\Omega\,,\end{gathered}

(3.1)

for some given
g\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
satisfying
g=0
on
\partial\Omega
. As in
[
33
, Section 5]
, we assume that
\alpha
is bounded and symmetric positive definite a.e. on
\Omega
. The week solution
u
of (
3.1
) is defined as the unique element of
H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
satisfying
u-g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
a(u-g,v)=0
for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, where

a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\alpha_{i,j}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx\,.

Define
V^{p}_{0}(\Omega_{h}):=V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and let

V^{p,g}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma):=\left\{v_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v_{h}-\Pi_{h}g\in V_{0}^{p}(\Omega_{h})\right\}

(3.2)

We can then define an approximation
u_{h}\in V^{p,g}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
by

a(u_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V^{p}_{0}(\Omega_{h})

and using the same method of proof as in
[
33
]
, we obtain

\left\|u-u_{h}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\lesssim\max\left\{\left\|\alpha_{i,j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}1\leq i,j\leq 3\right\}h^{l-1}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{l}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)}\,,\,\,1\leq l\leq p+1\,,

where
h=\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}
.

4
Outline of the construction of
\Pi_{h}

Replacing
\Gamma
by
\partial\Omega
and ignoring property (iii) of Theorem
1.1
, an operator meeting the remaining requirements is given by the Scott and Zhang interpolant
\mathscr{Z}_{h}

[
33
]
. This operator acts on a function

as

\mathscr{Z}_{h}u\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\sigma_{i}}\psi_{i}(x)u(x)\,dx\right)\phi_{i}=:\sum_{i=1}^{N}N_{i}(u)\phi_{i}\,.

(4.1)

Here,
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is the Lagrange nodal basis of
V^{p}(\Omega_{h})
, the space of continuous piecewise polynomial functions of degree
p
on
\Omega_{h}
. For Lagrange nodes
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
lying in the interior of some mesh element
K_{i}\in\Omega_{h}
, we take
\sigma_{i}:=K_{i}
. For
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\partial K
,
\sigma_{i}
is instead a
(d-1)
-simplex, freely chosen among the faces of
\Omega_{h}
incident to
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
, with the sole restriction that
\sigma_{i}\subset\partial\Omega
if
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\partial\Omega
. Finally,
\psi_{i}
is a “dual Lagrange polynomial” with the property that

\int_{\sigma_{i}}\psi_{i}(x)P(x)\,dx=P(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})

for any polynomial
P
of degree at most
p
.
It is immediate that
\mathscr{Z}_{h}u_{h}=u_{h}
for all
u_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h})
, and the restriction on
\sigma_{i}
also ensure
(\Pi_{h}u)_{|\partial\Omega}=0
when
u_{|\partial\Omega}=0
.

One may try to obtain a jump-aware interpolant by adapting the definition in (
4.1
). The first important change is that for
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\Gamma
, there generally needs to be more than just one basis function associated to
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
, to account for the fact that elements of
V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
can have several distinct limits at
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
depending on the considered “side” of
\Gamma
, see Figure
2

Figure 2:
A two-dimensional situation where
\Gamma
has
q_{i}=3
“sides” at
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
. Functions in
V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
may admit three distinct limits at
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
. The bridges
\{1,2\}
,
\{2,3\}
and
\{1,3\}
around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
are signaled by gray arrows.

Labeling the sides of
\Gamma
around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
from
1
to
q_{i}
, one can construct a nodal basis
\{\phi_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq q_{i}}
with the following properties. Any element
u\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
can be written as

u=\sum_{i,j}u(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j})\phi_{i,j}

where the coefficient
u(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j})
is equal to the limit of

as

approaches
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
from side

. Furthermore,

\phi_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}\phi_{i,j}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}\lambda_{j}\phi_{i,j}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\iff\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{j^{\prime}}\quad\forall 1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq q_{i}.

With these definitions, we set

\Pi_{h}u\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}\sum_{1\leq j\leq q_{i}}N_{i,j}(u)\phi_{i,j}\,,

where the

are suitably chosen linear form. For
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
away from
\Gamma
,

and

will be defined exactly as

in eq. (
4.1
); all the difficulty resides in the case where
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\Gamma
and
q_{i}>1
. In this case, one can attempt to define

N_{i,j}(u)\overset{?}{\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=}\int_{\sigma_{i,j}}\psi_{i,j}(x)u_{i,j}(x)\,dx\,.

(4.2)

Here
\sigma_{i,j}\subset\Gamma
is any face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{h})
which contains
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
and lies in the boundary of the
j
-th side of
\Gamma
around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
. Like above,
\psi_{i,j}
is the dual Lagrange polynomial associated to
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
on
\sigma_{i,j}
, and

is the restriction of (the polynomial function)

to
\sigma_{i,j}
.

The formula (
4.2
) comes close to satisfying the requirements. It guarantees the conditions
\Pi_{h}u_{h}=u_{h}
and
(\Pi_{h}u)_{|\Gamma}=0
when
u_{|\Gamma}=0
. To investigate the condition about jumps, suppose that

. To fulfill requirement (iii) of

1.2
, we need to ensure that
N_{i,j}(u)=N_{i,j^{\prime}}(u)
for all
1\leq i\leq N
,
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq q_{i}
. If

, i.e., if there are just two sides of

around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
(as for example when

in eq. (

). However, when there are more than two sides, there is always a pair

such that
\sigma_{i,j}\neq\sigma_{i,j^{\prime}}
. As soon as this is the case, one can easily find a smooth function

such that that

, showing that eq. (
4.2
) cannot meet the requirements.

A remedy is to define

N_{i,j}(u)=N_{i}(u)+\sum_{i,k,\ell}\mu_{i,k,\ell}N_{i,k,\ell}

(4.3)

where

is as in (
4.1

, are of the form

(4.4)

where
\sigma_{i,k,\ell}\subset\Gamma

\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
, and located at the interface between the sides

in this case. This ensures that

whenever

.

It remains to make sure that
\Pi_{h}u_{h}=u_{h}
for all

, and it turns out that one can always find a set of coefficients

such that this property holds; as we shall see, this is related to the fact that the graph with nodes the sides

and with edges the bridges

Simplices

An
n

called its vertices. A

k
-simplex,

. If

\sigma
is called a

of

the set of faces of

. We say that
\sigma

is incident to

if it is a subsimplex of
\sigma
, and that two
n
-simplices are

if they share a face.

Simplicial meshes

An
n
-dimensional simplicial mesh

\mathcal{M}
is a finite set of
n
-simplices, called
(mesh) elements
, such that if
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
, then
K\cap K^{\prime}
is either empty, or equal to a subsimplex of both
K
and
K^{\prime}
. The set of
faces
of
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M}):=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}(K)
. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is

if, for any elements

, there exists a sequence
K_{1},\ldots,K_{N}
of elements of
\mathcal{M}
such that
K_{1}=K
,
K_{N}=K^{\prime}
and for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, the elements
K_{i}
and
K_{i+1}
are adjacent. The
boundary

\partial\mathcal{M}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional mesh whose elements are the faces of
\mathcal{M}
incident to only one element of
\mathcal{M}
.

Conforming meshes

For a
n
-dimensional simplicial mesh
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\left\lvert\mathcal{M}\right\rvert
the closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\left\lvert\mathcal{M}\right\rvert:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}K\,.

Given a (closed) set

, we say that
\mathcal{M}
is a

\left\lvert\mathcal{M}\right\rvert=M
.

Regular meshes

An
n
-dimensional simplicial mesh
\mathcal{M}
is

if it is a conforming mesh of some
n
-dimensional submanifold of

(possibly with boundary). In other words,
\mathcal{M}
is regular if, for every point of

containing

such that

is homeomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Recall that for a positive real number
\gamma>0
, we say that
\mathcal{M}
is

-

if

\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{h_{K}}{\rho_{K}}\leq\gamma\,,

where
h_{K}
is the diameter of
K
and
\rho_{K}
is the radius of the largest
n
-dimensional closed ball contained in
K
.

We will use the following well-known facts about meshes. We haven’t found a complete proof of the first one it in the literature, so we give one for completeness. The second one is proven in
[
5
, Lemma 11.1.3]
for connected “

and the remark following it, since

is a regular mesh,

(or more precisely, the underlying simplicial complex) is indeed a

Proposition 5.1

(Solid angles in shape-regular meshes)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-simplex and
v
a vertex of

. The normalized solid angle
\Omega_{v}(K)
of the simplex
K
from

\Omega_{v}(K)\geq\frac{1}{d}\frac{\rho_{K_{i}}^{d}}{{h^{d}_{K_{i}}}}\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\,,

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Therefore, if
\mathcal{M}
is a
\gamma
-shape regular
d
-dimensional mesh in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the number
N
of mesh elements sharing a given vertex is bounded by

N\leq d\gamma^{d}\frac{\omega_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\,.

For the definition of the normalized solid angle, refer to
[
30

Proof.

To show the first inequality, we consider the solid
d
-dimensional cone
C
with apex
v
and base
S
, where

is a
(d-1)
-dimensional ball centered at the the circumenter

, and lying in the plane orthogonal to the line
(vc)
. This is illustrated in Figure
3
. The solid angle spanned by

from

is bounded from below by the solid angle spanned by

, for which we can readily prove the claimed estimate.
If

are mesh elements incident to

Proposition 5.2

(local face-connectedness in regular meshes)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular
n
-dimensional simplicial mesh with
n\geq 1
, let
v
be a vertex of some mesh element
K
and let
\textup{st}(v)
be the set of mesh elements incident to

\textup{st}(v)
is face-connected.

The sets
\Omega
and
\Gamma

For the remainder of this paper, we fix
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a
Lipschitz polyhedron
, that is, a bounded open set with Lipschitz regular boundary (see
[
20
, Definition 4.4]
) such that
\overline{\Omega}
admits a conforming, regular
d

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M}_{\Omega})\setminus\partial\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}\,,

.

Continuous function spaces

For an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

C^{\infty}(U)
(resp.

) is the set of infinitely differentiable functions on
U
(resp. which are furthermore compactly supported on

), and
C^{\infty}(\overline{U})
is the set of restrictions to

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For

, let

be the completion of

for the norm

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}(U)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq l}\left\|D^{\alpha}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U)}\,.

Here, we use the multi-index notation
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
,
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
and
D^{\alpha}=(\partial/\partial_{x_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}(\partial/\partial_{x_{2}})^{\alpha_{2}}\ldots(\partial/\partial_{x_{d}})^{\alpha_{d}}
. A semi-norm is defined by
\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{l}(U)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=l}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}f\right\rvert^{2}_{L^{2}(U)}
. If
U
is a Lipschitz domain, we will also consider the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(U)
for all
s>0
,
s\notin\mathbb{N}
, defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}(U)
for the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(U)}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\|f\right\|_{H^{l}(U)}^{2}+\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=l}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}f\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{\mu}(U)}\,,

(5.1)

where
s=l+\mu
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu\in(0,1)
, and
\left\lvert g\right\rvert_{H^{\mu}(U)}
is defined by

\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{\mu}(U)}=\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{\left\lvert g(x)-g(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{d+2\mu}}\,dx\,dy\,.

(5.2)

Finally, let
H^{s}_{0,\Gamma}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
in
H^{s}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
. If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
d
-simplex, we write
H^{s}(K)
in place of
H^{s}(\textup{int}(K))
for all
s\geq 0
.

Traces

If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
d
-simplex, the restriction operator
C^{\infty}(K)\to L^{2}(\partial K)
,
u\mapsto u_{|\partial K}
has a unique continuous linear extension
\gamma_{K}:H^{1}(K)\to L^{2}(\partial K)
, and for all

and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{3}
,

\int_{\Omega}u(x)\div\varphi(x)+\nabla u(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{\partial K}(\gamma_{K}u)(x)\varphi(x)\cdot n_{K}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\,,

(5.3)

where
n_{K}
is the unit outer normal vector and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
is the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, see
[
20
, Theorem 4.6]
.

We shall also require fractional Sobolev spaces defined over faces of
d
-simplices. Let
F
be a
n
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
n\geq 2
(but possibly
n<d
) we define
H^{s}(F)
for
s\in(0,1)
as the subset of

of functions satisfying

\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{s}(F)}:=\int_{F}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}(y)\,,

and let

\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}(F)}:=\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(F)}+\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}_{H^{s}(F)}\,.

If
K
is a
d
-simplex such that and
F\in\mathcal{F}(K)
is a face of
K
,
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, then there exists a constant
C
(depending on
K
and

) such that the restriction operator
\gamma_{[K,F]}:C^{\infty}(K)\to L^{2}(F)
defined by
\gamma_{[K,F]}u:=u_{|F}

6.1
Primal basis of

It is well-known that a piecewise-polynomial function

similarly involves the continuity of traces at faces of adjacent elements, except when this face is in
\Gamma
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
f:\Omega\setminus\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be such that for all
K\in\Omega_{h}
, there exists
f_{K}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
f_{|K}=f_{K}
. Then the following properties are equivalent

(i)

f\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
,

(ii)

For any pair of adjacent mesh elements
K,K^{\prime}\in\Omega_{h}
sharing a face
F\notin\Gamma_{h}
,

\gamma_{[K,F]}f_{K}=\gamma_{[K^{\prime},F]}f_{K^{\prime}}\,.

Proof.

Recall that by the
H=W
theorem
[
29
]
,
f\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
if and only if there exists a square-integrable vector field
g\in L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)^{d}
such that

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Gamma}f(x)\,\textup{div}(\varphi(x))\,dx=-\int_{\Omega\setminus\Gamma}g(x)\cdot\varphi(x)dx

for all
\varphi(x)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)^{d}
. The proof that this happens if and only if (ii) holds is classical.
∎

One sees from this result that elements of

\Gamma

and some Lagrange node

on

with

,

, such that

and

share a face

not in

containing

(see Figure

and

agree at

. Thus,

, hence the jump

vanishes at

, for all

.

The family

Proof.

Suppose that there exist coefficients

It is easy to show that

. Therefore, applying eq. (

) to

Next, we consider

6.2
Dual basis of

With the primal basis

at hand, we now set out to define a “dual basis”
\{N_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}\cup\{N_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}}

N_{i},N_{k,l}:H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
such that, among other properties,

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}N_{i}(\phi_{i^{\prime}})&=&\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\,,&&N_{i}(\psi_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}})&=&0\,,\\

As in Scott and Zhang’s original construction
[

]

Definition 6.4

(Lagrange dual polynomials)

For any
\gamma_{0}
shape-regular
n
-simplex

and any Lagrange node

for all

on the reference element, representing it under the form (
6.6
) (with
S
replaced by the reference element) via the Riesz representation theorem, and mapping back to

norm accordingly, see
[
33

and
[
9

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

(ii)

N_{i,j}(\psi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
for all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
.

(iii)

N_{i,j}
is a linear combination of terms of the form

w\mapsto\int_{F}\psi_{[F,\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}(x)\gamma_{[K,F]}w(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\,,

where
F
is a face incident to
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
and
K\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})
is such that
F\in\mathcal{F}(K)
. The number
N
of terms in this linear combination satisfies
N\lesssim 1
.

Each
N_{i,j}
will be constructed as a linear combination of “bridge functions” that we define now.

Definition 6.9

(Bridge functions)
.

Given
k,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,q_{i}\}
, we say that
\{k,\ell\}
is a
bridge
around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
if there exist two mesh elements
K_{k}\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};k)
,
K_{\ell}\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};\ell)
sharing a face
F_{k\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K_{k}\cap K_{\ell}\subset\Gamma
. The set of bridges around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(i)
.
Given a bridge
\{k,\ell\}\in\mathcal{B}(i)
with
k<\ell
, we define the
bridge function

N_{i,\{k,\ell\}}
by

N_{i,\{k,\ell\}}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{F_{k\ell}}\psi_{[F_{k\ell},\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}(x)\big(\gamma_{[K_{k},F_{k\ell}]}-\gamma_{[K_{\ell},F_{k\ell}]}\big)u(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\,.

Note that, by
Lemma

6.5
, for all
u_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
,

N_{i,\{k,\ell\}}(u_{h})=u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,k})-u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,\ell})\,.

We now fix
i
such that
q_{i}>1
and consider the vector space

F_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\textup{Span}(\{\psi_{i,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq q_{i}-1})\,.

Let
F_{i}^{*}
be the set of linear forms
L_{i}:F_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
, and let
n_{i,\{k,\ell\}}\in F_{i}^{*}
be the restriction of
N_{i,\{k,\ell\}}
to
F_{i}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

The bridge functions around
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
span
F_{i}^{*}
, i.e.,

F_{i}^{*}=\textup{Span}(\{n_{i,\{k,\ell\}}\}_{\{k,\ell\}\in\mathcal{B}(i)})\,.

Proof.

By a classical result on linear forms (see e.g.
[
31
, Lemma 3.9]
), it suffices to show that if
u_{h}\in F_{i}
satisfies

n_{i,\{k,\ell\}}(u_{h})=0\quad\forall\{k,\ell\}\in\mathcal{B}(i)\,,

(6.7)

then
u_{h}=0
. We first note that
u_{h}\in F_{i}
implies that
\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j})=0

since this property holds for every
\psi_{i,j}
. Hence to conclude it remains to show that
u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j})=u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j^{\prime}})
for
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq q_{i}
. For this, introduce the graph
{G}^{*}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})
defined by

•

Nodes
: the numbers
1,\ldots,q_{i}
,

•

Edges
: the bridges
\{k,k^{\prime}\}
.

The key point is that
{G}^{*}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})
is connected, because
\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})
is face-connected by
Proposition

5.2
. Eq. (
6.7
) immediately implies
u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j})=u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,j^{\prime}})
if
\{j,j^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{B}(i)
, and the same follows for a general pair
\{j,j^{\prime}\}\in\{1,\ldots,q_{i}\}
by using a path from
j
to
j^{\prime}
in
{G}^{*}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})
. This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

We denote by
\{{n}_{i,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq q_{i}-1}
the basis of
F_{i}^{*}
such that

n_{i,j}(\psi_{i,j^{\prime}})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

For every
(i,j)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
, there exists

\{k_{1},\ell_{1}\},\ldots,\{k_{q_{i}-1},\ell_{q_{i}-1}\}\in\mathcal{B}(i)\,,\qquad\lambda_{i,1}\,,\ldots\,,\lambda_{i,q_{i}-1}\lesssim 1

such that

n_{i,j}=\sum_{q=1}^{q_{i}-1}\lambda_{i,q}n_{i,\{k_{q},\ell_{q}\}}\,.

Proof.

Let
Q=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}(q_{i}-1)
. Since
\textup{dim}(F_{i}^{*})=\textup{dim}(F_{i})=q_{i}-1
, one can find a set of pairs
\{k_{q},\ell_{q}\}_{1\leq q\leq q_{i}-1}
such that
\{n_{i,\{k_{q},\ell_{q}\}}\}_{1\leq q\leq q_{i}-1}
is a basis of
F_{i}^{*}
. One can write
n_{i,j}
in a unique way as

n_{i,j}=\sum_{q=1}^{q_{i}-1}\lambda_{i,q}n_{i,\{k_{q},\ell_{q}\}}\,.

The coefficients
\lambda_{i,q}
can be found by solving the linear system

\mathbf{A}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{i,1}&\ldots&\lambda_{i,q_{i}-1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}=\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\,,

where
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}
is the
j
-th vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{q_{i}-1}
and the matrix coefficients

\mathbf{A}_{p,p^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\psi_{i,p^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,k_{q}})-\psi_{i,q^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i,\ell_{q}})

are integer between
-2
and
2
. The set
\mathcal{S}_{Q}
of invertible square matrices
\mathbf{A}
of size at most
Q
with coefficients in
\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}
is finite. Therefore, we can write

\left\lvert\lambda_{i,q}\right\rvert\leq\sup_{\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{Q}}|||\mathbf{A}^{-1}|||_{\infty}\,,\quad\forall q\in\{1\,,\ldots\,,q_{i}-1\}\,,

(6.8)

where, for an element
v
of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
and a square
r\times r
matrix
M
, we have denoted

\left\|v\right\|_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{1\leq q\leq r}\left\lvert v_{q}\right\rvert\,,\quad|||M|||_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{v\in R^{r}}\frac{\left\|Mv\right\|_{\infty}}{\left\|v\right\|_{\infty}}\,.

Since
Q\lesssim 1
, the right-hand side of the inequality (
6.8
) is
\lesssim 1
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

6.8
.

We define

N_{i,j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{q=1}^{q_{i}-1}\lambda_{i,q}N_{i,\{k_{q},\ell_{q}\}}\,.

The properties (i)-(iii) follow immediately by construction and the uniform bound on the number of elements in

.

Lemma 6.12

(Bramble-Hilbert lemma)
.

Let
p,d\geq 1
be integers,
\gamma_{0}>0
a real number. Then for any
\gamma_{0}
-shape-regular
d
-simplex

and
0<t\leq p+1
, and

, there exists
p_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}
such that

\left\|u-p_{K}\right\|_{H^{s}(K)}\leq C(\gamma_{0})h_{K}^{t-s}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}(K)}

(6.10)

Proof.

This is well-known, see the seminal work
[
6
]
(the proof for fractional Sobolev exponents is obtained by interpolation), and
[
18
]
. ∎

Lemma 6.13

(Scaled trace theorem)
.

For every
\gamma_{0}>0
,
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, there exists
C(\gamma_{0},t)>0
such that, if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
\gamma_{0}
-shape-regular
d

d\geq 2
) and
F\in\mathcal{F}(K)
, then for all

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}\leq C(\gamma_{0},t)\left(\textup{diam}(K)^{t-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}(K)}+\textup{diam}(K)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(K)}\right)

Proof.

This is shown by mapping to the reference element, using the trace inequality (
5.4

Fix a mesh element
K\in\Omega_{h}
and let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)\cap H^{t}_{\rm pw}(\omega_{K})
(as defined in

). For every

, let

be a polynomial such that

.

Lemma 6.14
.

For any
v_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
such that
(v_{h})_{|K}=p_{K}
, there holds

\left\|u-\Pi_{h}u\right\|_{H^{s}(K)}\lesssim h^{t-s}|u|^{2}_{H^{t}(K)}+h^{\frac{3}{2}-s}\sum_{\nu=1}^{N_{\nu}}\left\lvert\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu};F_{\nu}]}(u-v_{h})(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\right\rvert

where
N_{\nu}\lesssim 1
, and for all
1\leq\nu\leq N_{\nu}
,
\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(K)
,
K_{\nu}\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu})
and
F_{\nu}\in\mathcal{F}(K_{\nu})
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{N}_{i,j}
be the linear forms such that

\Pi_{h}z=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}}\widetilde{N}_{i,j}(z)\phi_{i,j}\,.

We can write

\begin{split}\left\|u-\Pi_{h}u\right\|_{H^{s}(K)}&\leq\left\|u-v_{h}\right\|_{H^{s}(K)}+\left\|\Pi_{h}(u-v_{h})\right\|\,,\\
&\lesssim h^{t-s}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}}+\sum_{(i,j)}|\widetilde{N}_{i,j}(u-v_{h})|\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|_{H^{s}(K)}\\
&\lesssim h^{t-s}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}}+h^{3/2-s}\sum_{(i,j)}|\widetilde{N}_{i,j}(u-v_{h})|\end{split}

where the sum runs over the pairs
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
such that
K\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};j)
. For all
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
and
w\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
,
\widetilde{N}_{i,j}(w)
is a linear combination of
N_{i}(w)
and
N_{i,j^{\prime}}(w)
for
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq q_{i}-1
, which in turn are both defined via linear combinations of terms of the form

\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{x}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}w(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

with
\boldsymbol{x}_{\nu}
,
F_{\nu}
and
K_{\nu}
as in the statement of the Lemma, or terms of the form

\int_{K}\psi_{[K;\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}w(x)\,dx\,.

when
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
is an interior Lagrange node. In the latter case, we can write

\begin{split}\left\lvert\int_{K}\psi_{[K;\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}\right\rvert(u(x)-v_{h}(x))dx&=\left\lvert\int_{K}\psi_{[K;\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}\right\rvert(u(x)-p_{K}(x))dx\\
&\leq\left\|\psi_{[K;\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|u-p_{K}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\\
&\leq h^{-3/2}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}}\,.\end{split}

We conclude the proof using again that
\textup{card}(\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}))\lesssim 1
.
∎

Define
v_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
by

v_{h}:=\sum_{(i,j)}p_{K_{i,j}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})\phi_{i,j}

where the sum ranges over the pairs
(i,j)
such that
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(K)
, and where the element
K_{i,j}\in\Omega_{h}
is chosen freely in
\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};j)
, with the only constraint that
K_{i,j}=K
whenever possible, i.e. whenever
\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};j)\ni K
. This constraint then ensures
(v_{h})|_{K}=p_{K}
by unisolvence, so that
Lemma

6.14
applies to

.

We now estimate a term of the form

\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-v_{h}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

as defined in that Lemma. Let
(i_{0},j_{0})
be the unique element of
\mathcal{I}
determined by

\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}=\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}}\quad\textup{and}\quad K_{\nu}\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}};j_{0})

Then for any
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
, one has

\begin{split}\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}\phi_{i,j}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)&=\begin{cases}\phi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}})&\textup{if }K_{\nu}\in\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i};j),\\
0&\textup{otherwise,}\end{cases}\\[10.00002pt]
&=\delta_{i,i_{0}}\delta_{j,j_{0}}\,.\end{split}

Therefore, for any
u_{h}\in V^{p}(\Omega_{h};\Gamma)
,

\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}u_{h}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)=u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0},j_{0}})\,.

Thus,

\begin{split}&\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-v_{h}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\\
&\qquad\quad=\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-p_{K_{i_{0},j_{0}}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\,.\end{split}

This quantity can be estimated using Veeser’s trick from
[
38
, Theorem 1]
(see Eq. (23)), stated as an independent result in
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
. In our notation, this reads

\begin{split}&\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-p_{K_{i_{0},j_{0}}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\\
&\quad\qquad=\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-p_{K_{\nu}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)+\,\,p_{K_{\nu}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}})-p_{K_{i_{0},j_{0}}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}})\\
&\quad\qquad=\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-p_{K_{\nu}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)+\sum_{l=1}^{M-1}\,\,p_{K_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}})-p_{K_{l+1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}})\end{split}

using a telescoping sum, where
K_{1},\ldots,K_{M}
is a sequence of mesh elements in
\textup{st}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i_{0}};j_{0})
such that
K_{\nu}=K_{1}
,
K_{i,j}=K_{M}
, and
K_{l}
and
K_{l+1}
share a face
F_{l}
not in
\Gamma
containing
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
for every
l=0\,,\ldots\,,M-1
. The first term is estimated via the scaled trace theorem (
Lemma

6.13
)

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\int_{F_{\nu}}\psi_{[F_{\nu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\nu}]}(x)\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u(x)-p_{K_{i,j}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\right\rvert\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left\|\psi_{[F_{\nu},\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}\right\|_{L^{2}(F_{\nu})}\left\|\gamma_{[K_{\nu},F_{\nu}]}(u-p_{K_{i,j}})\right\|_{L^{2}(F_{\nu})}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim h^{-3/2}\left\|u-p_{K_{i,j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K_{i,j})}+h^{-3/2+t}\left\lvert u-p_{K_{i,j}}\right\rvert_{H^{t}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{2}+t}|u|_{H^{t}(K_{i,j})}\,.\end{split}

Similarly, for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}
, we write

\begin{split}p_{K_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})-p_{K_{l+1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})&=\int_{F_{l}}\psi_{[F_{l},\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}(p_{K_{l}}(x)-p_{K_{l+1}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\\
&=\int_{F_{l}}\psi_{[F_{l},\boldsymbol{x}_{i}]}\gamma_{[K_{l+1},F_{l}]}(u-p_{K_{l+1}})(x)-\gamma_{[K_{l},F_{l}]}(u-p_{K_{l}})(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\end{split}

where we have used
Lemma

6.1
. Therefore,

\left\lvert p_{K_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})-p_{K_{l+1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})\right\rvert\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{2}+t}\left\lvert u\right\rvert_{H^{t}(K_{i,j})}

In view of
Lemma

6.14
, this establishes

1.2
.

An equivariant Hochster’s formula for 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -invariant monomial ideals
Satoshi Murai, Claudiu Raicu

Abstract
. Let R = 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] be a polynomial ring over a field 𝕜 \Bbbk and let I ⊂ R I\subset R be a monomial ideal preserved by the natural action of the symmetric group 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} on R R . We give a combinatorial method to determine the 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -module structure of Tor i ​ ( I , 𝕜 ) \mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk) . Our formula shows that Tor i ​ ( I , 𝕜 ) \mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk) is built from induced representations of tensor products of Specht modules associated to hook partitions, and their multiplicities are determined by topological Betti numbers of certain simplicial complexes. This result can be viewed as an 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -equivariant analogue of Hochster’s formula for Betti numbers of monomial ideals. We apply our results to determine extremal Betti numbers of 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -invariant monomial ideals, and in particular recover formulas for their Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and projective dimension. We also give a concrete recipe for how the Betti numbers change as we increase the number of variables, and in characteristic zero (or > n >n ) we compute the 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -invariant part of Tor i ​ ( I , 𝕜 ) \mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk) in terms of Tor \mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt groups of the unsymmetrization of I I .

Introduction

Let
R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\Bbbk
.
The study of the graded Betti numbers

\beta_{ij}(I)=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)_{j}

of a homogeneous ideal
I
is one of the central research topics in commutative algebra. When
I
is a monomial ideal, this is closely related to combinatorial topology, and is the subject of a vast literature
[
6
, Section 5]
,
[
15
, Part II]
,
[
20
, Part I]
,
[
23
, Part III]
. One of the most famous results on this topic is Hochster’s formula
[
6
, §5.5]
, which enables one to study Betti numbers of monomial ideals using combinatorics of simplicial complexes, and has seen numerous applications over the years. The goal of this paper is to develop an analogue of Hochster’s formula for monomial ideals
I
that are invariant under the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
by coordinate permutations.

In general, if
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\Bbbk)
is a subgroup and
I\subseteq R
is a
G
-invariant ideal (
g(I)=I
for all
g\in G
), then each
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)
acquires a
G
-module structure. The representation theory of
G
dictates the possible building blocks that make up
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)
, reducing the problem of understanding
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)
to that of identifying the multiplicity of each building block. When
G=\Bbbk^{\times n}
is the
n
-torus, the building blocks are
1
-dimensional, given by the torus characters, and the calculation of their multiplicity (the dimension of the multigraded components of
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)
) is the content of Hochster’s formula. When
G=\Bbbk^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
extends the torus action by that of the symmetric group, our work will show that a natural set of building blocks arises via induction from Specht modules associated to hook partitions for smaller symmetric groups. We then identify the multiplicities of each block with topological Betti numbers of associated simplicial complexes, which yields an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-equivariant Hochster’s formula
.

2.

In this section, we introduce some basic notation which will be used in the paper.

2.1.
Some remarks on partitions and multidegrees

Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal of
R
.
Recall that
P(I)=\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid x^{\lambda}\in I\}
.
Since
x^{\bm{a}}\in I
if and only if
{\mathrm{part}(\bm{a})}\in P(I)
,
the set
P(I)
determines the ideal
I
.
We regard
P_{n}
as a poset with the relation defined by
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\geq(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
if
a_{i}\geq b_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
Let
\Lambda(I)
be the set of minimal elements in
P(I)
. Identifying partitions with the corresponding monomials in
R
, we have that up to the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the set
\Lambda(I)
forms a minimal set of generators of
I
: we have
I=\langle\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda(I)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
and no proper subset of
\Lambda(I)
generates
I
. In particular,
\lambda\in P_{n}
is contained in
P(I)
if and only if there is
\mu\in\Lambda(I)
such that
\lambda\geq\mu
.

We sometimes regard a partition
\lambda
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
To avoid confusion,
for a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
,
when we denote the graded component of a module
M
of degree
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
we write it as
M_{\bm{\lambda}}
instead of
M_{\lambda}
.
Also, when we write partitions, we sometimes ignore “
0
” and identify
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},0,\dots,0)
. For any
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we write
|{\bm{a}}|=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}
for the
size
of
{\bm{a}}
.

We often consider both
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
.
We write
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{s}
for the standard vectors of
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
and write
\mathfrak{e}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{e}_{n}
for the standard vectors of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Also, for subsets
F\subset[s]
and
G\subset[n]
, we write
\mathbf{e}_{F}=\sum_{i\in F}\mathbf{e}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{G}=\sum_{i\in G}\mathfrak{e}_{i}
.

2.2.
Simplicial complexes and their homology groups

A simplicial complex on a finite set
V
is a collection
\Delta
of subsets of
V
satisfying the condition that
F\in\Delta
and
G\subset F
imply
G\in\Delta
.
Elements of
\Delta
are called
faces
and maximal elements of
\Delta
are called
facets
.
We distinguish the empty simplicial complex
\varnothing
from the simplicial complex
\{\varnothing\}
consisting only of the empty face.

If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex on
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, we write

\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}:0\longleftarrow C_{-1}(\Delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longleftarrow}}C_{0}(\Delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longleftarrow}}C_{1}(\Delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longleftarrow}}\cdots

for the (reduced) simplicial chain complex of
\Delta
over a field
\Bbbk
.
Here, each
C_{k}(\Delta)
is the
\Bbbk
-vector space spanned by the symbols
\{\alpha_{F}\mid F\in\Delta,\ |F|=k+1\}
. If we consider the total order
v_{1}<\cdots<v_{n}
on
V
then the boundary map is given by

\partial(\alpha_{F})=\sum_{v\in F}\epsilon_{v}(F)\cdot\alpha_{F\setminus\{v\}}

where
\epsilon_{v}(F)=(-1)^{|\{u\in F\mid u\leq v\}|}
.
The homology
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta)=H_{i}(\widetilde{C}_{\bullet}(\Delta))
is called the
i
-th
reduced homology group
of
\Delta
.
We note that
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\{\varnothing\})\cong\Bbbk
while
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\varnothing)=0
for all
i
.

2.3.

It is known that when

We quickly recall this fact.

.
For

we obtain the following formula.

2.5.
Multi-dimensional complexes

We define a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex
of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces to be a complex
(K_{\bullet},\partial)
where each term has a decomposition

(2.19)

K_{l}=\bigoplus_{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{s}=l}K_{(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})}

and the differential
\partial
sends

\partial(K_{(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})})\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}K_{(c_{1},\cdots,c_{i}-1,\cdots,c_{s})}.

All our complexes are finite, that is,
K_{{\bm{c}}}\neq 0
for finitely many
{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, and the spaces
K_{{\bm{c}}}
are finite dimensional. We have a decomposition
\partial=\bigoplus\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}
with
1\leq i\leq s
and
{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, where

\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}:K_{{\bm{c}}}\longrightarrow K_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}},

and the condition that
\partial
is a differential translates into

\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\circ\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}=0,\text{ and }\partial^{j}_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\circ\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}+\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\circ\partial^{j}_{{\bm{c}}}=0\text{ for }i\neq j.

We write
\partial^{K}
and
\partial^{K,i}_{{\bm{c}}}
when we want to emphasize the complex that
\partial
is a differential of. We define a morphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complexes
f:K\longrightarrow K^{\prime}
to be a morphism of complexes which is compatible with (
2.19
). Equivalently, for each
{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
we have a
\Bbbk
-linear map
f_{{\bm{c}}}:K_{{\bm{c}}}\longrightarrow K^{\prime}_{{\bm{c}}}
, satisfying

\partial^{K^{\prime},i}_{{\bm{c}}}\circ f_{{\bm{c}}}=f_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\circ\partial^{K,i}_{{\bm{c}}}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq s,\ {\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}.

We write
\mathfrak{com}_{s}
for the category of
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complexes, identify
\mathfrak{com}_{0}
with the category
\mathfrak{vec}
of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, and note that
\mathfrak{com}_{1}
is the usual category of complexes associated to
\mathfrak{vec}
. If we write
\mathfrak{E}=\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\partial^{1},\cdots,\partial^{s})
for the exterior algebra on
\partial^{1},\cdots,\partial^{s}
, then
\mathfrak{E}
has a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-grading with
\deg(\partial^{i})=-\mathbf{e}_{i}
, and the notion of a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex is equivalent to that of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-graded
\mathfrak{E}
-module. We will write
\mathfrak{grmod}_{\mathfrak{E}}
for the category of such modules, and use freely the equivalence between
\mathfrak{com}_{s}
and
\mathfrak{grmod}_{\mathfrak{E}}
.

We define the
support
of a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex to be the set

(2.20)

\operatorname{supp}(K)=\{{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}:K_{{\bm{c}}}\neq 0\}.

For
{\bm{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, we define the shifted complex
K[{\bm{d}}]
by
K_{{\bm{c}}}=K_{{\bm{c}}+{\bm{d}}}
, with differentials shifted accordingly. We can think of a vector space
W
as a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex supported at
(0^{s})
, and we write
W[{\bm{d}}]
for the corresponding shift (which is supported at
-{\bm{d}}
).

Example 2.2
.

For a vector space
W
, we define the
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex
E=E(W)
by

E_{{\bm{c}}}=\begin{cases}W&\text{if }{\bm{c}}\in\{0,1\}^{s};\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
{\bm{c}}\in\operatorname{supp}(E)
with
c_{i}=1
, we define
\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}:W\longrightarrow W
to be multiplication by
(-1)^{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{i}}
. It is easy to see that
E
is an exact complex (isomorphic up to shift to the tensor product of
W
with the reduced chain complex of a simplex). As an object of
\mathfrak{grmod}_{\mathfrak{E}}
,
E
can be identified with the free module
W\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{E}(-1^{s})
, with generators
W
in degree
(1^{s})
. As such,
E
is a projective object of
\mathfrak{com}_{s}
(it is also injective by
[
10
, Proposition 7.19]
).

We define a
Boolean
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex
to be a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex
K
which is isomorphic to
E[{\bm{d}}]
, where
{\bm{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
and
E
is as in Example
2.2
. This is equivalent to the fact that
\operatorname{supp}(K)=-{\bm{d}}+\{0,1\}^{\times n}
and
\partial^{i}_{{\bm{c}}}:K_{{\bm{c}}}\longrightarrow K_{{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}
is an isomorphism whenever
{\bm{c}},{\bm{c}}-\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\operatorname{supp}(K)
. Whenever we want to emphasize the relation between the Boolean complex
K
and the reduced chain complex of a simplex, we will write for each subset
F\subseteq[s]

(2.21)

K_{F}=K_{-{\bm{d}}+\mathbf{e}_{F}}.

It follows from Example
2.2
that Boolean complexes are projective (and injective) objects in
\mathfrak{com}_{s}
, which has the following useful consequence.

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose that
K
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complex with a filtration whose composition factors
E^{1},\cdots,E^{r}
are Boolean
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-complexes. We have that

K\simeq E^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E^{r}.

If
K\in\mathfrak{com}_{s}
and
L\in\mathfrak{com}_{t}
, then the (external) tensor product
K\boxtimes L
is defined to be the complex in
\mathfrak{com}_{s+t}
with

(K\boxtimes L)_{{\bm{c}}}=\bigoplus_{{\bm{d}}+{\bm{e}}={\bm{c}}}K_{{\bm{d}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}L_{{\bm{e}}},

and differential

\partial^{K\boxtimes L}_{{\bm{c}}}(u\otimes v)=\partial^{K}(u)\otimes v+(-1)^{|{\bm{d}}|}u\otimes\partial^{K}(v)\text{ for }u\in K_{{\bm{d}}},\ v\in L_{{\bm{e}}}.

We note that the complex in Example
2.2
is the tensor product of the
\mathbb{Z}
-complex
W\simeq W
with
(s-1)
copies of the
\mathbb{Z}
-complex
\Bbbk\simeq\Bbbk
. In general, the tensor product of a Boolean
s
-complex with a Boolean
t
-complex is a Boolean
(s+t)
-complex.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(K)
is an decreasing filtration of
K
by
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-subcomplexes, we write

\operatorname{gr}^{k}(K)=\mathcal{F}^{k}(K)/\mathcal{F}^{k+1}(K)\text{ and }\operatorname{gr}(K)=\bigoplus_{k}\operatorname{gr}^{k}(K).

Given filtrations
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(K)
,
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(L)
, we get an induced filtration on
K\boxtimes L
, with

\mathcal{F}^{k}(K\boxtimes L)=\sum_{i+j=k}\mathcal{F}^{i}(K)\boxtimes\mathcal{F}^{j}(L)\text{ and }\operatorname{gr}^{k}(K\boxtimes L)=\bigoplus_{i+j=k}\operatorname{gr}^{i}(K)\boxtimes\operatorname{gr}^{j}(L).

If
G
is a group, we will be interested more generally in the category
\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
of finite complexes of finite
\Bbbk[G]
-modules, or equivalently, the category
\mathfrak{grmod}_{\mathfrak{E}}^{G}
of finitely generated
G
-equivariant
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
-graded
\mathfrak{E}
-modules, where the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{E}
is trivial. For
s=0
,
\mathfrak{com}_{0}^{G}
is the category
\mathfrak{mod}_{G}
of finite
G
-modules. If
K\in\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
and
L\in\mathfrak{com}_{t}^{G^{\prime}}
then
K\boxtimes L\in\mathfrak{com}_{s+t}^{G\times G^{\prime}}
, and the discussion of filtrations is analogous in the equivariant setting.

Using the natural isomorphisms

\mathrm{Hom}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}}(E(W),K)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathfrak{grmod}_{\mathfrak{E}}^{G}}(W\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathfrak{E}(-1^{s}),K)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathfrak{mod}_{G}}(W,K_{(1^{s})})

we can interpet the construction of the Boolean complex
E(W)
in Example
2.2
as a functor
E:\mathfrak{mod}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
which is left-adjoint to the functor
P:\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{mod}_{G}
given by
P(K)=K_{(1^{s})}
. Since
P
is exact, we have that
E(W)
is projective whenever
W
is a projective
G
-module. When
G
is a finite group and
\Bbbk
has characteristic zero or coprime to
|G|
, we have that
\mathfrak{mod}_{G}
is semi-simple, and in particular
E(W)
is a projective object of
\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
for every
G
-module
W
. We get the following equivariant version of Corollary
2.3
:

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group,
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic zero or coprime to
|G|
, and
K\in\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
has a filtration with composition factors
E^{i}=E(W_{i})
, where
W_{i}\in\mathfrak{mod}_{G}
, for
i=1,\dots,r
. We have that

K\simeq E^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E^{r}.

We end this section by explaining how Corollaries
2.3
and
2.4
will be applied in our work. Suppose that
F\in\mathfrak{com}_{1}^{G}
is an exact complex supported in non-negative degrees:

0\longleftarrow F_{0}\overset{\partial_{1}}{\longleftarrow}F_{1}\longleftarrow\cdots\overset{\partial_{r}}{\longleftarrow}F_{r}\longleftarrow 0.

We let
U_{l}=\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{l+1})
and
D_{l}=F_{l}/U_{l}
, so that
\partial_{l}
establishes an isomorphism
D_{l}\simeq U_{l-1}
. We have a natural filtration on
F
given by the canonical truncations

\mathcal{F}^{l}(F):\quad 0\longleftarrow U_{l}\longleftarrow F_{l+1}\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow F_{r-1}\longleftarrow F_{r}\longleftarrow 0,

with
\operatorname{gr}^{l}(F)=(D_{l+1}\simeq U_{l})
a Boolean
\mathbb{Z}
-complex. By Corollary
2.3
, we have an isomorphism
F\simeq\operatorname{gr}(F)
in
\mathfrak{com}_{1}
, which by Corollary
2.4
can be taken to be
G
-equivariant (that is, in
\mathfrak{com}_{1}^{G}
) if
G
is finite and
\Bbbk
has characteristic zero or coprime to
|G|
. Choosing (not necessarily
G
-equivariant) sections of the quotient maps

, we can picture the complex

as:

\textstyle{U_{0}}
\textstyle{U_{1}}
\textstyle{U_{2}}
\textstyle{U_{3}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus}
\textstyle{\bigoplus}
\textstyle{\bigoplus}
\textstyle{\hskip-50.0pt\cdots}
\textstyle{D_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{1}}
\textstyle{D_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{2}}
\textstyle{D_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{3}}

More generally, if
F^{i}\in\mathfrak{com}_{1}^{G_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,s
, then the canonical filtrations on each

induce a filtration on the tensor product
F=F^{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes F^{s}\in\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
, where
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{s}
. We have an isomorphism
F\simeq\operatorname{gr}(F)
in
\mathfrak{com}_{s}
, and if
G
is finite and
\Bbbk
has characteristic zero or coprime to
|G|
, then
F\simeq\operatorname{gr}(F)
in
\mathfrak{com}_{s}^{G}
.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
. We first study the complex

(3.1)

K_{\bullet}^{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ \mathrm{part}(\bm{a})=\mu}(K_{\bullet}^{R})_{\bm{a}},

-equivariant Boolean complexes. Based on the discussion in Section

into a direct sum of Boolean complexes, which is
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-equivariant in characteristic zero or

4.
The
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant part of the Betti table

The goal of this section is to give a quick application of Theorem
1.3
and the Nerve Theorem, computing the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant part of
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)
when
I
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal, and
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic zero or
>n
. More precisely, we show the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\in P_{n}
.
Then, for any
\mu\in P_{n}
, one has

(4.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{\mu,\bm{0}}(\langle\lambda^{1},\cdots,\lambda^{r}\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}((x^{\lambda^{1}},\cdots,x^{\lambda^{r}}),\Bbbk)_{\bm{\mu}}\ \mbox{ for all }i.

In particular, if
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)=0
or
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)>n
, then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}\big(\langle\lambda^{1},\cdots,\lambda^{r}\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}},\Bbbk\big)^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cong\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}\big((x^{\lambda^{1}},\cdots,x^{\lambda^{r}}),\Bbbk\big)\ \mbox{ for all }i.

Proof.

Let
I=\langle\lambda^{1},\cdots,\lambda^{r}\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
and
J=(x^{\lambda^{1}},\cdots,x^{\lambda^{r}})
.
For a subset
\Lambda\subset P_{n}
we define the partition
\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(\Lambda)\in P_{n}
by

\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(\Lambda)_{i}=\max\{\lambda_{i}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

Also, for a subset
G\subseteq[r]
we write

\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(\{\lambda^{i}\mid i\in G\}).

Consider the simplicial complex

X_{<\mu}=\{G\subseteq[r]\mid\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(G)<\mu\}.

It follows from
[
3
, Theorem 1.11]
(see also the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
) that

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(J,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\mu}}\cong\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(X_{<\mu}).

Then it follows that in order to prove (
4.1
), it suffices to show that the complexes
\Delta^{\mu,\bm{0}}(I)
and
X_{<\mu}
are homotopy equivalent, which we do next.

If
\mu=(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}},0^{p_{s+1}})\in P_{n}
with
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{s}>0
, then we have

\lambda<\mu\Longleftrightarrow\lambda\leq\mu\setminus\mathbf{e}_{i}\text{ for some }i=1,\cdots,s.

It follows that the facets of
X_{<\mu}
are

G_{i}=\{j\in[r]\mid\lambda^{j}\leq\mu\setminus\mathbf{e}_{i}\}\text{ for }i=1,\cdots,s.

The Nerve Theorem (see for instance
[
5
, Theorem 10.6]
) implies that
X_{<\mu}
is homotopy equivalent to the
nerve
of
G_{1},\cdots,G_{s}
, which is the simplicial complex

\mathcal{N}(G_{1},\dots,G_{s})=\left\{F\subset[s]\mid\bigcap_{i\in F}G_{i}\neq\varnothing\right\}.

We note that
\bigcap_{i\in F}G_{i}\neq\varnothing
is equivalent to the fact that for some
\lambda^{j}
we have

\lambda^{j}\leq\mu\setminus\mathbf{e}_{i}\text{ for all }i\in F.

This is further equivalent to
\lambda^{j}\leq\mu\setminus\mathbf{e}_{F}
, which shows that

\bigcap_{i\in F}G_{i}\neq\varnothing\Longleftrightarrow\mu\setminus\mathbf{e}_{F}\in P(I).

It follows from (
1.6
) that
\mathcal{N}(G_{1},\dots,G_{s})=\Delta^{\mu,\bm{0}}(I)
, so
X_{<\mu}
is homotopy equivalent to
\Delta^{\mu,\bm{0}}(I)
, proving (
4.1
).

We now assume that
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)=0
or
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)>n
and prove (
4.2
).
Using the Taylor resolution of
J

[
15
, §7.1]
, we have that if
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(J,\Bbbk)_{{\bm{a}}}\neq 0
for some
{\bm{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
then
{\bm{a}}=\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(\Lambda)
for some subset
\Lambda\subseteq\{\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\}
, and in particular
{\bm{a}}\in P_{n}
.
Thus, to prove (
4.2
), it is then enough to show that

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\langle\mu\rangle}\cong\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(J,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\mu}}\text{ for all }\mu\in P_{n}.

It follows from the Littlewood–Richardson rule (see e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 4.9.14]
) that

\left(\mathcal{S}^{((p_{1},1^{q_{1}}),\dots,(p_{s},1^{q_{s}}))}\right)^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cong\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\mbox{ if }q_{1}=\cdots=q_{s}=0;\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus Theorem
1.3
and (
4.1
) imply the desired isomorphism

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I,\Bbbk)^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\langle\mu\rangle}\cong\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Delta^{\mu,\bm{0}}(I))\cong\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(J,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\mu}}.\qed

5.
Primary decomposition and extremal Betti numbers

The goal of this section is to describe a primary decomposition for any
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal
I
, and to study the extremal Betti numbers of
I
. As an application, we recover using Theorem
1.3
the formulas from
[
24
]
for the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, and for the projective dimension of
I
. To formulate our results, we consider the set of
extended partitions

P_{n}^{\infty}=\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})^{n}\mid\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0\}

where
\infty\geq a
for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
, for which a partial order is constructed in Section
5.3
. In Section
5.1
we determine a finite subset
\Lambda^{*}(I)\subset P_{n}^{\infty}
describing a natural primary decomposition of
I
, and refer to
\Lambda^{*}(I)
as the set of
dual generators
of
I
. If
\rho=(\infty^{p_{0}},d_{1}^{p_{1}},\cdots,d_{s}^{p_{s}})\in P_{n}^{\infty}
, with
\infty>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{s}\geq 0
, then we write

(5.1)

\ell(\rho)=p_{0}

for the number of
\infty
terms in
\rho
, and let

(5.2)

\widetilde{\rho}=((d_{1}+1)^{p_{0}+p_{1}},(d_{2}+1)^{p_{2}},\cdots,(d_{s}+1)^{p_{s}}).

In analogy with the multigraded version of extremal Betti numbers from
[
2
, p. 507]
, we say that a pair
(i,\lambda)\in[n]\times P_{n}
is
extremal
in the Betti table of
R/I
if

(a)

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\lambda\rangle}\neq 0
, and

(b)

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{j}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}=0
for all
j\geq i
and
\mu\gneq\lambda
with
|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i
.

If
(i,\lambda)
is extremal, the
extremal Betti number

\beta_{i,\lambda}(I)
is
\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\lambda}}
(which is equal to
\beta_{i,\sigma\cdot\lambda}(I)
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
). The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal
I
, we have

\displaystyle\{(i,\lambda)\in[n]\times P_{n}\mid(i,\lambda)\mbox{ is an extremal pair in the Betti table of $R/I$}\}

\displaystyle=\{\left(n-\ell(\rho),\widetilde{\rho}\right)\mid\rho\in\Lambda^{*}(I)\mbox{ is a maximal dual generator of }I\}.

Moreover, if
(i,\lambda)
is the extremal pair associated to
\rho=(\infty^{p_{0}},d_{1}^{p_{1}},\cdots,d_{s}^{p_{s}})
, then the corresponding extremal Betti number is
\beta_{i,\lambda}={p_{0}+p_{1}-1\choose p_{0}}
.

We prove Theorem
5.1
in Section
5.4
using a reformulation of Theorem
1.3
via Alexander duality, which is explained in Section
5.2
. In Section
5.5
we discuss the relationship between our results and the combinatorics used in
[
24
]
, and explain the relation of Theorem
5.1
to the study of
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
modules.

5.1.
Primary decompositions of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideals

We begin by recalling a canonical primary decomposition for a monomial ideal
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Every monomial ideal
I
of
R
has a presentation

(5.3)

\displaystyle I=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{r},

where each
Q_{i}
is an ideal of the form
(x_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}})
.
Moreover, such a presentation is unique if it is irredundant, i.e., if none of the ideals
Q_{i}
can be omitted from (
5.3
).

To describe the irredundant presentation (
5.3
) for an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal, we define for each
\mu=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n})\in P_{n}^{\infty}
with
\mu_{k}<\infty
, the ideal

Q_{\mu}=\bigcap_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma(x_{k}^{\mu_{k}+1},\dots,x_{n}^{\mu_{n}+1}).

If
I\subset R
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal, its irredundant presentation (
5.3
) is preserved by the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action. Therefore, if
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
then
\sigma(Q_{k})=Q_{l}
for some
1\leq l\leq r
.
This fact and Lemma
5.2
imply the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
I\subset R
be an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal.
Then there are unique elements
\mu^{1},\cdots,\mu^{t}\in P_{n}^{\infty}
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle I=Q_{\mu^{1}}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{\mu^{t}}

and none of the ideals
Q_{\mu^{k}}
can be omitted in the above presentation.

We call (
5.4
) the
irredundant decomposition
of
I
, and call
\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{t}
the
dual generators
of
I
. We write

(5.5)

\Lambda^{*}(I)=\{\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{t}\},

and note that the condition that (
5.4
) is irredundant implies that

(5.6)

\mu^{i}\not\leq\mu^{j}\text{ for }1\leq i\neq j\leq t.

Remark 5.4
.

If
\mu=(\infty^{p_{0}},d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{m}^{p_{m}})
with
\infty>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0
,
then

Q_{\mu}=\big\langle\big((d_{1}+1)^{p_{0}+1}\big),\big((d_{2}+1)^{p_{0}+p_{1}+1}\big),\dots,\big((d_{m}+1)^{p_{0}+p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m-1}+1}\big)\big\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}.

The ideals
Q_{\mu}
are therefore the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant ideals generated by a set of rectangular partitions. Combining the formula for
Q_{\mu}
with

\displaystyle\langle(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}})\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}=\langle(d_{1}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cap\langle(d_{2}^{p_{1}+p_{2}})\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cap\cdots\cap\langle(d_{s}^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{s}})\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}

provides a way to compute the presentation (
5.4
).
For example, we have

\displaystyle\langle(4,1,1),(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\langle(4,0,0),(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\cap\langle(1,1,1),(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\langle(4,0,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\cap\langle(1,1,1),(5,0,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\cap\langle(1,1,1),(2,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}

\displaystyle=Q_{(3,3,3)}\cap Q_{(4,4,0)}\cap Q_{(\infty,1,0)},

where for the first two equalities we used

\langle(4,1,1)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}=\langle(4,0,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\cap\langle(1,1,1)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\text{ and }\langle(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}=\langle(5,0,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}\cap\langle(2,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}.

We conclude that
\Lambda^{*}(\langle(4,1,1),(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}})=\{(3,3,3),(4,4,0),(\infty,1,0)\}
.

To shed more light on the set
\Lambda^{*}(I)
, we define

O(I)=P_{n}\setminus P(I)=\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid x^{\lambda}\not\in I\},

which is the set of all partitions that are not in
I
. The irredundant decomposition (
5.4
) is related to
O(I)
as follows. For
\mu=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n})\in P_{n}^{\infty}
, let

O_{\mu}=\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid\lambda\leq\mu\}.

One can check that

O_{\mu}=O(Q_{\mu}),

hence Lemma
5.3
implies that for any
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal
I\subset R
, one has

(5.7)

\displaystyle O(I)=\bigcup_{\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)}O_{\mu}.

Moreover, the decomposition (
5.7
) is irredundant (that is, no
O_{\mu}
can be omitted).

5.2.
A reformulation of Theorem
1.3
via Alexander duality

With the notation in Section
3
, we define for
\mu=(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}},0^{p_{s+1}})
and
\bm{c}\leq\bf p(\mu)
the simplicial complex

(5.8)

\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)=\{F\subseteq[s]\mid\mu\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus F})\in O(I)\}.

In other words,

\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)=\{F\subseteq[s]\mid[s]\setminus F\not\in\Delta^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)\}
, that is,
\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
is the
Alexander dual
of
\Delta^{\mu,\bm{c}}
. We have by
[
6
, Lemma 5.5.3]
that

\widetilde{H}_{i-2}(\Delta^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I))\cong\widetilde{H}_{s-i-1}(\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)).

Using this isomorphism, Theorem
1.3
can be rewritten as follows.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mu=(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}},0^{p_{s+1}})\in P_{n}
and let
I\subset R
be an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal.
We have an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{0}\leq{\bm{c}}\leq\bf p(\mu)}\left(\mathcal{S}^{((p_{1}-c_{1},1^{c_{1}}),\dots,(p_{s}-c_{s},1^{c_{s}}),(p_{s+1}))}\right)^{\dim_{\Bbbk}\widetilde{H}_{s-i-1+|\bm{c}|}(\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I))},

which is in addition an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules when
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)=0
or
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)>n
.

5.3.
Maximal dual generators

In what follows we introduce a partial order on the set of dual generators
\Lambda^{*}(I)
in (
5.5
), and explain how the maximal elements of
\Lambda^{*}(I)
contribute to the Betti numbers of
R/I
. For
\mu=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n})\in P_{n}^{\infty}
with
\mu_{k}\neq\infty
, we define
\ell(\mu)=k-1
as in (
5.1
), define
\widetilde{\mu}
as in (
5.2
), and let

\mu^{+}=(\mu_{k}+1,\dots,\mu_{k}+1,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n}).

We note that
\mu^{+}
is obtained from
\mu
by replacing
\infty
with
\mu_{k}+1
, and that

\widetilde{\mu}=\mu^{+}+\mathfrak{e}_{k}+\cdots+\mathfrak{e}_{n}.

We define the partial order
\preccurlyeq
on
\Lambda^{*}(I)
by
\mu\preccurlyeq\rho
if

(5.9)

\widetilde{\mu}\leq\widetilde{\rho}\text{ and }\ell(\mu)-\ell(\rho)\leq|\widetilde{\rho}|-|\widetilde{\mu}|.

Using the fact that

(5.10)

|\mu^{+}|=|\widetilde{\mu}|-(n-\ell(\mu)),

we can rewrite the conditions (
5.9
) as

(5.11)

\widetilde{\mu}\leq\widetilde{\rho}\text{ and }|\mu^{+}|\leq|\rho^{+}|.

We write
\mu\prec\rho
if
\mu\preccurlyeq\rho
and
\mu\neq\rho
. We let
\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)\subseteq\Lambda^{*}(I)
denote the subset of maximal elements with respect to
\preccurlyeq
, and call them
maximal dual generators
of
I
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal.

(i)

If
\mu,\rho\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
satisfy
\mu\prec\rho
then
\ell(\mu)>\ell(\rho)
.

(ii)

If
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
then
\rho^{+}\not\in O_{\mu}
for any
\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)\setminus\{\rho\}
.

Proof.

(i) Write
\mu=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n})\prec\rho=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\rho_{l},\dots,\rho_{n})
, and
suppose by contradiction that
\ell(\mu)\leq\ell(\rho)
, or equivalently, that
k\leq l
.
The condition
\widetilde{\mu}\leq\widetilde{\rho}
implies
\mu_{m}\leq\rho_{m}
for all
l\leq m\leq n
.
Since for
1\leq m<l
we have
\mu_{m}\leq\infty=\rho_{m}
, this shows that
\mu\leq\rho
,
contradicting (
5.6
).

(ii)
Let
\mu=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\mu_{k},\dots,\mu_{n})
,
\rho=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\rho_{l},\dots,\rho_{n})
, and suppose that
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
and
\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)\setminus\{\rho\}
. If
\mu_{m}<\rho_{m}
for some
l\leq m\leq n
, then
\rho^{+}\not\leq\mu
, hence
\rho^{+}\not\in O_{\mu}
, as desired. We may therefore assume that
\mu_{m}\geq\rho_{m}
for all
l\leq m\leq n
.
By (
5.6
) we have
\rho\not\leq\mu
, hence
\mu_{l-1}\neq\infty
and thus
k<l
. If
\mu_{l-1}\geq\rho_{l}+1
then we have
\mu^{+}\geq\rho^{+}
and
\widetilde{\mu}\geq\widetilde{\rho}
, which implies by (
5.11
) that
\mu\succ\rho
, contradicting the maximality of
\rho
. It follows that
\mu_{l-1}<\rho_{l}+1
, so
\rho^{+}\not\leq\mu
, concluding the proof.
∎

Maximal dual generators have the following contributions to Betti numbers.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\rho=(\infty^{p_{0}},d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}})\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
, with
\infty>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{s}\geq 0
,
and let
{\bm{c}}=(p_{1}-1,\dots,p_{s}-1)
. We have
\Gamma^{\widetilde{\rho},\bm{c}}=\{\varnothing\}
, and

\beta_{n-\ell(\rho),\widetilde{\rho}}(R/I)={p_{0}+p_{1}-1\choose p_{0}}.

Proof.

We observe that
\widetilde{\rho}
is as in (
5.2
), and

\widetilde{\rho}\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]})=\big((d_{1}+1)^{p_{0}},d_{1}^{p_{1}},d_{2}^{p_{2}}\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}}\big)=\rho^{+}.

Since
\rho^{+}\leq\rho
, it follows from (
5.7
) that
\rho^{+}\in O(I)
, so
\varnothing\in\Gamma^{\widetilde{\rho},\bm{c}}(I)
by (
5.8
). To prove that
\Gamma^{\widetilde{\rho},\bm{c}}=\{\varnothing\}
, it then suffices to check that
\{i\}\not\in\Gamma^{\widetilde{\rho},\bm{c}}(I)
for
i\in[s]
.

We fix
i\in[s]
and note that

\widetilde{\rho}\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus\{i\}})=\rho^{+}+\mathfrak{e}_{k}\text{ for some }k>p_{0},

hence
\widetilde{\rho}\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus\{i\}})\not\in O_{\rho}
. Since
\rho
is maximal, we have by Lemma
5.6
(ii) that
\rho^{+}\not\in O_{\mu}
for all
\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
with
\mu\neq\rho
. We get from (
5.7
) that
\widetilde{\rho}\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus\{i\}})\not\in O(I)
, hence
\{i\}\not\in\Gamma^{\widetilde{\rho},\bm{c}}(I)
by (
5.8
), as desired.

If we let
i=n-\ell(\rho)=n-p_{0}
and
\mu=\widetilde{\rho}
in Theorem
5.5
, then we have

s-i-1+|{\bm{c}}|=s-(n-p_{0})-1+(n-p_{0}-s)=-1,

and

\dim_{\Bbbk}\widetilde{H}_{s-i-1+|\bm{c}|}(\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I))=\dim_{\Bbbk}\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\{\varnothing\})=1.

It follows from Theorem
5.5
that

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{n-\ell(\rho)}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\widetilde{\rho}\rangle}=\mathcal{S}^{((p_{0}+1,1^{p_{1}-1}),(1^{p_{2}}),\cdots,(1^{p_{s}}))}

Restricting to the multidegree
\widetilde{\rho}
, and using the fact that each of the Specht modules
S^{(1^{p_{i}})}
has dimension
1
, it follows that

\beta_{n-\ell(\rho),\widetilde{\rho}}(R/I)=\dim_{\Bbbk}S^{(p_{0}+1,1^{p_{1}-1})}={p_{0}+p_{1}-1\choose p_{0}},

where the last equality follows from the Hook Length Formula
[
17
, § 20]
(or a direct count of the standard tableaux in
\mathrm{Tab}((p_{0}+1,1^{p_{1}-1}))
).
∎

Example 5.8
.

If we let
I=\langle(4,1,1),(5,2,0)\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{3}}
, then as seen in Remark
5.4
, we have

\Lambda^{*}(I)=\{(3,3,3),(4,4,0),(\infty,1,0)\}
.
The maximal dual generators of
I
are then
(3,3,3)
and
(4,4,0)
.
Since
\ell((3,3,3))=\ell((4,4,0))=0
, Lemma
5.7
implies that they contribute to
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle(4,4,4)\rangle}
and
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle(5,5,1)\rangle}
respectively. More precisely, we have

\beta_{3,(4,4,4)}(R/I)=\beta_{3,(5,5,1)}(R/I)=1,

and

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle(4,4,4)\rangle}=\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{(4,4,4)}

is
1
-dimensional, while

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle(5,5,1)\rangle}=\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{(5,5,1)}\oplus\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{(5,1,5)}\oplus\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{(1,5,5)}

is
3
-dimensional (see Example
1.4
). We note that
(3,(4,4,4))
and
(3,(5,5,1))
are all the extremal pairs in the Betti table of
R/I
.

5.4.
Extremal Betti numbers, regularity and projective dimension.

Our next goal is to give a proof of Theorem
5.1
, and to derive formulas for the regularity and projective dimension of an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal
I
. We begin by establishing some preliminary results.

We say that a simplicial complex
\Delta
is a
cone with apex
v
if for every face
F\in\Delta
, one has
F\cup\{v\}\in\Delta
.
If
\Delta
is a cone then it is contractible, and
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta)=0
for all
i
. We will need the following slight generalization of this fact.

Lemma 5.9
.

If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex on the set
[n]
, with
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Delta)\neq 0
, then there exists a facet
F
of
\Delta
with
1\not\in F
and
|F|\geq i
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be the simplicial complex whose facets are the facets of
\Delta
of dimension
\geq(i-1)
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta
have the same faces of dimension
\geq(i-1)
, it follows that
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Delta)\neq 0
, and in particular
\Delta^{\prime}
is not a cone with apex
1
. We get that
\Delta^{\prime}
contains a facet
F
with
1\not\in F
, which is then a facet of
\Delta
with
|F|\geq i
.
∎

We next record two more technical statements before proving Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mu=(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}},0^{p_{s+1}})
, and define
\bf p(\mu)
as in (
1.4
), and
\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
as in (
5.8
), for some
{\bm{c}}\leq\bf p(\mu)
. If
F
is a facet of
\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
with
1\not\in F
, we let

\mu^{\prime}=\mu\setminus({\bm{c}}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus F})=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{n}^{\prime}),

and define

h=\min\{i\mid\mu_{i}^{\prime}\neq\mu_{1}\}.

If
\rho=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\rho_{l},\dots,\rho_{n})\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
with
\rho_{l}\neq\infty
and
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
, then we have
h\geq l
and
\mu_{1}\leq\rho_{l}+1
.

Proof.

Since
1\not\in F
, the first entry of
\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus F}
is positive. By (
1.5
), we have

\mu_{h}^{\prime}+1=\mu_{h}=\mu_{1}.

If
h<l
then
\rho_{h}=\infty
, and using
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
we obtain

\mu\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus(F\cup\{1\})})=\mu\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus F}-\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mu^{\prime}+\mathfrak{e}_{h}\leq\rho.

This shows that
\mu\setminus(\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]\setminus(F\cup\{1\})})\in O_{\rho}\subset O(I)
, so
F\cup\{1\}\in\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
by (
5.8
), contradicting the fact that
F
was a facet. It follows that
h\geq l
, and since
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
, we have
\mu^{\prime}_{h}\leq\mu_{l}^{\prime}\leq\rho_{l}
. We get
\mu_{1}=\mu^{\prime}_{h}+1\leq\rho_{l}+1
, concluding the proof.
∎

For the next result we recall the definition of
s(\mu)
from (
1.4
).

Lemma 5.11
.

If
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Gamma^{\mu,{\bm{c}}}(I))\neq 0
for some
\mu\in P_{n}
and
{\bm{c}}\leq\bf p(\mu)
,
then there exists
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
such that

(i)

|{\bm{c}}|+s(\mu)-i\leq n-\ell(\rho)
,

(ii)

|\mu|-(|{\bm{c}}|+s(\mu)-i)\leq|\rho^{+}|
, and

(iii)

\mu\leq\widetilde{\rho}
.

Proof.

We note that each of
n-\ell(\rho)
,
|\rho^{+}|
,
\widetilde{\rho}
, increases as
\rho
increases with respect to the order
\preccurlyeq
by Lemma
5.6
(i). It follows that it is enough to find
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
satisfying (i)–(iii).

By Lemma
5.9
, there is a facet
F
of
\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
such that
1\not\in F
and
|F|\geq i
.
We let
\mu=(d_{1}^{p_{1}},\dots,d_{s}^{p_{s}},0^{p_{s+1}})
, so that
s=s(\mu)
, and define
\mu^{\prime}
and
h
as in Lemma
5.10
. Since
F\in\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I)
, we have
\mu^{\prime}\in O(I)
, so there exists an element
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
such that
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
. We prove that
\rho
satisfies conditions (i)–(iii).

We first note that
h=p_{1}-c_{1}
, and that
n=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{s}+p_{s+1}
. Combining these observations with the assumption
{\bm{c}}\leq\bf p(\mu)
, we get

n-|\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]}|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{s}(p_{j}-c_{j}-1)\geq p_{1}-c_{1}-1=h-1.

Since
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
, we know from Lemma
5.10
that
h\geq\ell(\rho)+1
, so

|\bm{c}|+s-i\leq|\bm{c}+\mathbf{e}_{[s]}|\leq n-(h-1)\leq n-\ell(\rho),

proving (i). The conclusion of Lemma
5.10
implies that
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho^{+}
, hence

|\mu|-(|\bm{c}|+s-|F|)=|\mu^{\prime}|\leq|\rho^{+}|,

which proves (ii). Finally, if we write
\rho=(\infty,\dots,\infty,\rho_{l},\dots,\rho_{n})
with
\rho_{l}\neq\infty
, then Lemma
5.10
implies

\mu_{l-1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{1}\leq\rho_{l}+1=\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=\cdots=\widetilde{\rho}_{l-1}.

Moreover, since
\mu^{\prime}\leq\rho
, we get

\mu_{m}\leq\mu^{\prime}_{m}+1\leq\rho_{m}+1=\widetilde{\rho}_{m}\text{ for }l\leq m\leq n.

This shows that
\mu\leq\widetilde{\rho}
, proving (iii) and concluding our argument.
∎

We are now in the position to prove the main result of the section.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We know from Lemma
5.7
that for each
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
we have

\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{n-\ell(\rho)}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\widetilde{\rho}\rangle}\neq 0.

To prove that every extremal pair for
R/I
is of the form
(n-\ell(\rho),\widetilde{\rho})
,
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
, it then suffices to check that for every pair
(j,\mu)
with
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{j}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}\neq 0
, there exists
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
such that

(5.12)

\ \ j\leq n-\ell(\rho),\ \mu\leq\widetilde{\rho}\mbox{ and }|\mu|-j\leq|\widetilde{\rho}|-(n-\ell(\rho)).

By Theorem
5.5
,
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{j}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}\neq 0
implies that there is a
{\bm{c}}\leq\bf p(\mu)
such that

\widetilde{H}_{s(\mu)-j-1+|{\bm{c}}|}(\Gamma^{\mu,\bm{c}}(I))\neq 0.

We apply Lemma
5.11
with
i=s(\mu)+|{\bm{c}}|-j
to find
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
satisfying

j=|{\bm{c}}|+s(\mu)-i\leq n-\ell(\rho),\ |\mu|-j=|\mu|-(|{\bm{c}}|+s(\mu)-i)\leq|\rho^{+}|,\text{ and }\mu\leq\widetilde{\rho}.

Using the identity (
5.10
), these conditions are precisely the ones from (
5.12
).

To conclude, we have to check that every pair
(n-\ell(\rho),\widetilde{\rho})
, with
\rho\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
, is extremal. Equivalently, we have to show that if (
5.12
) holds for
(j,\mu)=(n-\ell(\rho^{\prime}),\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime})
with
\rho^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
, then
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
. Indeed, in this case we can rewrite (
5.12
) as

l(\rho)\leq l(\rho^{\prime}),\ \widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}\leq\widetilde{\rho},\text{ and }l(\rho^{\prime})-l(\rho)\leq|\widetilde{\rho}|-|\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}|,

which implies
\rho^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\rho
. Since
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}_{\max}(I)
, we must have
\rho^{\prime}=\rho
, as desired.
∎

For a quick application of Theorem
5.1
, recall that for a homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
,
the
(Castelnuovo–Mumford) regularity
of
R/I
is

\mathrm{reg}(R/I)=\max\{j-i\mid\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{j}\neq 0\}

and the
projective dimension
of
R/I
is

\mathrm{pd}(R/I)=\max\{i\mid\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)\neq 0\}.

It follows that the extremal pairs in the Betti table of
R/I
determine regularity and projective dimension, and we get the following.

Corollary 5.12
.

If
I\subset R
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal then

(i)

\mathop{\mathrm{reg}}(R/I)=\max\{|\mu^{+}|\mid\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)\}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{pd}(R/I)=\max\{n-\ell(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(I)\}
.

Proof.

For
\mu,\rho\in\Lambda^{*}(I)
, the condition

\mu\preccurlyeq\rho
implies that
\ell(\mu)\geq\ell(\rho)
by Lemma
5.6
, and it implies
|\mu^{+}|\leq|\rho^{+}|
by (
5.11
).
It follows that the right side of the equations (i) and (ii) only depends on the maximal dual generators.
The conclusion then follows by combining Theorem
5.1
with (
5.10
).
∎

5.5.
Extremal Betti numbers via
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
modules

The goal of this section is to explain how the extremal pairs and the extremal Betti numbers for
R/I
can be recovered from the structure of the modules
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{\bullet}(R/I,R)
.
Using results from
[
24
]
,
which determine the structure of
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(I,R)
for any
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant monomial ideal,
we then give an alternative proof of Theorem
5.1
, and we show how Corollary
5.12
is equivalent to
[
24
, (1.3)]
.

In analogy with the notion of extremal pair from the beginning of Section
5
, we say that a pair
(i,\lambda)\in[n]\times P_{n}
is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal
if

(a)

\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{\langle-\lambda\rangle}\neq 0
, and

(b)

\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{j}(R/I,R)_{\langle-\mu\rangle}=0
for all
j\geq i
and
\mu\gneq\lambda
with
|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i
.

We first show that the
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal pairs coincide with the extremal pairs, and moreover that the extremal Betti numbers can be computed via
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
modules as follows.

Proposition 5.13
.

We have that
(i,\lambda)\in[n]\times P_{n}
is extremal if and only if it is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal. Moreover, for an extremal pair
(i,\lambda)
we have

(5.13)

\beta_{i,\lambda}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}.

Proposition
5.13
is a natural extension of the corresponding result in the standard-graded setting (see for instance
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
). When
I
is a square-free monomial ideal (not necessarily
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant), it follows from
[
22
, Theorem 3.3]
or
[
27
, Theorem 2.6]
that the multigraded components of
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)
can be computed as Betti numbers of the Alexander dual
I^{\vee}
, in which case the equality (
5.13
) is equivalent to the one proved in
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
.

To prove Proposition
5.13
, we first establish a preliminary result. We write
F_{\bullet}
for the minimal free resolution of
R/I
, so that

F_{i}=\bigoplus_{{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}R(-{\bm{c}})^{\beta_{i,{\bm{c}}}}.

We let
F_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}\hskip 1.0pt_{R}(F_{\bullet},R)
be the dual of
F_{\bullet}
, so that
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)
is the
i
-th cohomology module of
F_{\bullet}^{\vee}
. We write
\partial^{i}:F_{i}^{\vee}\longrightarrow F_{i+1}^{\vee}
for the differentials in
F_{\bullet}^{\vee}
.

Lemma 5.14
.

If
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}\neq 0
then there exist
k\geq 0
and
\mu\geq\lambda
, such that
|\mu|-|\lambda|\geq k
and
F_{i+k}^{\bm{\vee}}
has a minimal generator
m
of degree
-\mu
, with
\partial^{i+k}(m)=0
. In particular, we have
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i+k}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\mu}}\neq 0
, and if
(i,\lambda)
is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal, then every non-zero class in
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}
is represented by a minimal generator of
F_{i}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis, there exists
f_{i}\in\ker(\partial^{i})
with
\deg(f_{i})=-\lambda
, representing a non-zero element of
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}
. For
j\geq 0
and for as long as
0\neq f_{i+j}\in F_{i+j}^{\vee}
, we choose
m_{i+j}
to be a minimal generator of
F_{i+j}^{\vee}
of degree
-\mu^{j}\leq-\deg(f_{i+j})
, and set
f_{i+j+1}=\partial^{i+j}(m_{i+j})
. Since
F_{\bullet}^{\vee}
is a finite complex, this process ends after finitely many steps with a minimal generator
m_{i+k}
of
F_{i+k}^{\vee}
, satisfying
\partial^{i+k}(m_{i+k})=0
. We take
m=m_{i+k}
and show that
\mu=\mu^{k}
satisfies
\mu\geq\lambda
and
|\mu|-|\lambda|\geq k
.

We note that for each
j=0,\cdots,k-1
,
\deg(f_{i+j+1})=\deg(m_{i+j})=-\mu^{j}
, since the differential
\partial^{i+j}
is degree-preserving. Moreover, since
\partial^{i+j}
is minimal, it follows that
f_{i+j+1}
is not a minimal generator of
F_{i+j+1}^{\vee}
, hence
-\mu^{j+1}<-\mu^{j}
for
j=0,\cdots,k-1
. Starting with
\lambda=\deg(f_{i})
, we get

\lambda\leq\mu^{0}<\mu^{1}<\cdots<\mu^{k}=\mu,

which implies
\mu\geq\lambda
and
|\mu|-|\lambda|\geq k
, as desired.

Since
m
is a minimal generator of
F_{i+k}^{\vee}
,
m
is not a boundary, so it represents a non-zero element of
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i+k}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\mu}}
. If
(i,\lambda)
is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal, this is only possible if
\mu=\lambda
and
k=0
. If
f_{i}
was not a minimal generator of
F_{i}^{\vee}
, then one can choose
m_{i}
to be a minimal generator of
F_{i}^{\vee}
of degree
-\mu^{0}<-\lambda
, and the construction above yields a pair
(i+k,\mu)
with
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i+k}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\mu}}\neq 0
and
|\mu|-|\lambda|\geq k
, contradicting the fact that
(i,\lambda)
was
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal, and concluding our proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.13
.

Suppose first that
(i,\lambda)
is an extremal pair, so
F_{i}
has a minimal generator of degree
\lambda
, and there is no pair
(j,\mu)
with
|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i
such that
F_{j}
has a minimal generator of degree
\mu
. We show that every non-zero element
m\in F_{i}^{\vee}
with
\deg(m)=-\lambda
represents a non-zero class in
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}
. Indeed, we know that
m
is not a boundary, since
\partial^{i-1}
is minimal. Let
f=\partial^{i}(m)
, and suppose by contradiction that
f\neq 0
. Since
\deg(f)=\deg(m)=-\lambda
and
\partial^{i}
is minimal, there exists a minimal generator of
F_{i+1}^{\vee}
of degree
-\mu<-\lambda
. This corresponds to a generator of
F_{i+1}
of degree
\mu>\lambda
. This contradicts the fact that
(i,\lambda)
was extremal, since it implies
|\mu|-(i+1)\geq|\lambda|-i
. It follows that
\partial^{i}(m)=0
, so
m
represents a non-zero class in
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}
, as desired. By Lemma
5.14
, every non-zero class in
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\lambda}}
arises in this way, so (
5.13
) holds.

To show that
(i,\lambda)
is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal, suppose by contradiction that there exists a pair
(j,\mu)
with
\mu\gneq\lambda
,
|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i
, and
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{j}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\mu}}\neq 0
. Applying Lemma
5.14
to
(j,\lambda)
, we can find
k\geq 0
and
\delta\geq\mu
with
|\delta|\geq|\mu|+k
, and such that
F_{j+k}^{\vee}
has a minimal generator of degree
-\delta
. This shows that
F_{j+k}
has a minimal generator of degree
\delta
, where

|\delta|-(j+k)\geq|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i,

contradicting the fact that
(i,\lambda)
was extremal.

Suppose now that
(i,\lambda)
is
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal. By Lemma
5.14
,
F_{i}^{\vee}
has a minimal generator of degree
-\lambda
, so
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\lambda}}\neq 0
. Suppose by contradiction that there exists a pair
(j,\mu)
that satisfies
\mu\gneq\lambda
,
|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i
, and
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{j}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\mu}}\neq 0
. We consider one such pair for which
j
is maximal, and let
m
denote a minimal generator of
F_{j}^{\vee}
of degree
-\mu
. If
\partial^{j}(m)=0
then
m
represents a non-zero class in
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{j}(R/I,R)_{-\bm{\mu}}
(since it is not a boundary), contradicting the fact that
(i,\lambda)
was
\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt
-extremal. If
\partial^{j}(m)=f\neq 0
then there exists a minimal generator of
F_{j+1}^{\vee}
of degree
-\delta<-\mu
. It follows that
\delta>\mu\gneq\lambda
,

|\delta|-(j+1)\geq|\mu|-j\geq|\lambda|-i,

and
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{j+1}(R/I,\Bbbk)_{\bm{\delta}}\neq 0
, so the pair
(j+1,\delta)
contradicts the maximality of
(j,\mu)
, which concludes our proof.
∎

We define the module

(A)

In

(B)

In
[
24

there exists a filtration of

\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(R/I,R)\cong\bigoplus_{(\underline{z},l)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)}\mathrm{Ext}\hskip 1.0pt^{i}(J_{\underline{z},l},R).

We do not recall here the definition of

, nor do we recall the description of

[
24

. If we define

(5.15)

\mu(\underline{z},l)=(\infty^{l},z_{l+1},\cdots,z_{n})\in P_{n}^{\infty}

then we have

(C)

If
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
with
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{p}>z_{p+1}
for some

\lambda=\underline{z}+(1^{n})=(z_{1}+1,\cdots,z_{n}+1)=\widetilde{\mu(\underline{z},l)}

We explain a relation between the set
\mathcal{Z}(I)
and dual generators.
Let

\displaystyle\Lambda(J_{\underline{z},l})=\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid(J_{\underline{z},l})_{\lambda}\neq 0\}\overset{\eqref{eq:def-Jzl}}{=}\{\lambda\in P_{n}\mid\lambda\geq\underline{z},\ \lambda_{i}=z_{i}\text{ for }i\geq l+1\}.

O(I)=\biguplus_{(\underline{z},l)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)}\Lambda(\underline{z},l).

This allows us to write

\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)=\{(\underline{z},l)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)\mid\mu(\underline{z},l)\not\leq\mu(\underline{y},u)\text{ for }(\underline{z},l)\neq(\underline{y},u)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)\}.

We get an irredundant decomposition

Example 5.15
.

If

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(I)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}((0,0,0),1),((1,1,0),1),\\

((2,2,2),0),((3,2,2),0),((3,3,2),0),((3,3,3),0)\end{array}\right\}.

The (significantly smaller) subset of dual pairs is

\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)=\{((1,1,0),1),((4,4,0),0),((3,3,3),0)\},

corresponding to the dual generators of

\mu((1,1,0),1)=(\infty,1,0),\ \mu((4,4,0),0)=(4,4,0),\ \mu((3,3,3),0)=(3,3,3).

To give another perspective on
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)
, we introduce a partial order on
\mathcal{Z}(I)
by

(\underline{z},l)\leq(\underline{y},u)\Longleftrightarrow l=u\text{ and }\underline{z}\leq\underline{y}.

We have that
(\underline{z},l),(\underline{y},u)
are incomparable if

, and
(\underline{z},l)\leq(\underline{y},l)
if and only if
\mu(\underline{z},l)\leq\mu(\underline{y},l)
. For the proof of the next result, we assume that the reader has some familiarity with
[

and its implications, such as

]

Lemma 5.16
.

We have that

\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)=\{\text{maximal elements of }\mathcal{Z}(I)\text{ with respect to }\leq\}.

Proof.

For the inclusion “
\subseteq
”, let
(\underline{z},l)\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)
, and suppose by contradiction that there exists
(\underline{y},l)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
with
(\underline{y},l)>(\underline{z},l)
. This implies that
\mu(\underline{y},l)>\mu(\underline{z},l)
, contradicting (
5.16
).

For the reverse inclusion “
\supseteq
”, let
(\underline{z},l)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
be maximal with respect to
\leq
, and suppose by contradiction that
(\underline{z},l)\not\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}(I)
. By (
5.16
), there exists
(\underline{y},u)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
with
\mu(\underline{z},l)<\mu(\underline{y},u)
, which implies
l\leq u
. Moreover, if
l=u
then
\underline{z}<\underline{y}
, contradicting the maximality of
(\underline{z},l)
. We thus have

(5.17)

l<u\text{ and }z_{i}\leq y_{i}\text{ for }i\geq u+1.

We write
c=z_{1}
and
d=y_{1}
, and note that there exists
\underline{x}\in\mathcal{X}(I)
such that
\underline{x}(c)\leq\underline{z}
and
x^{\prime}_{c+1}\leq l+1
. In particular, we must have
x_{i}\leq c
for all
i>l+1
(hence for
i\geq u+1
). Suppose first that
c\leq d
. The condition
\underline{x}(c)\leq\underline{z}
implies that
x_{i}=\min(x_{i},c)\leq z_{i}
for
i\geq u+1
, which combined with (
5.17
) implies that
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for
i\geq u+1
. Since
y_{i}=d
for
i\leq u+1
, this shows that
\underline{x}(d)\leq\underline{y}
. Since
(\underline{y},u)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
, this forces
x^{\prime}_{d+1}\geq u+1
, which contradicts (
5.17
) since it implies

u+1\leq x^{\prime}_{d+1}\leq x^{\prime}_{c+1}\leq l+1.

Suppose now that
c>d
. We have that
z_{i}\leq y_{i}\leq d<c
for
i\geq u+1
, which combined with
\underline{x}(c)\leq\underline{z}
implies that
x_{i}\leq z_{i}
for
i\geq u+1
. Using (
5.17
), this shows that
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for
i\geq u+1
, hence
\underline{x}(d)\leq\underline{y}
. Since
(\underline{y},u)\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
, we must have
x^{\prime}_{d+1}\geq u+1
, hence

x_{u+1}\geq d+1>y_{u+1}\geq z_{u+1}.

Since
\underline{x}(c)\leq\underline{z}
, this forces
z_{u+1}=c
. The above inequality implies
d+1>z_{u+1}=c
, contradicting the fact that
d<c
and concluding the proof.

is equivalent to the formulas

Suppose that
(i,\lambda)
is an extremal pair with

If

is the unique interger satisfying

6.
Varying the number of variables

In this section, we study how the multigraded Betti numbers of the ideals
I_{m}
(defined in (
)) vary with
m
, when
f_{1},\cdots,f_{r}
are assumed to be monomials. This extends a result of the first author from
[
21
]
, that gives a simple recipe to determine for all
m\geq n
all the multidegrees
\mu\in P_{m}
for which
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{m},\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}
is non-zero. The recipe requires knowing the set

\{(i,\lambda)\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}\times P_{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{n},\Bbbk)_{\langle\lambda\rangle}\neq 0\},

and is summarized in Theorem
6.1
below. The goal of this section is to explain how using Theorem
1.3
we can determine not only which of the multigraded Betti numbers are non-zero, but to also compute them explicitly, and to describe the
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-module structure of
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{m},\Bbbk)_{\langle\mu\rangle}
for all
m\geq n
and all
\mu\in P_{m}
. This is explained in Theorem
6.2
, but before going into details we make some preliminary conventions.

Throughout this section, we identify
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in P_{n}
and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},0^{m-n})\in P_{m}
for
m\geq n
. By this identification, if
\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\in P_{n}
, we can regard them as elements of
P_{m}
with
m\geq n
, and consider the ideals

I_{m}=\langle\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\subset\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}].

The (non-)vanishing of the multigraded Betti numbers of
I_{m}
is characterized by the following theorem of the first author (see
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\in P_{n}
and let

I_{m}=\langle\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r}\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
for
m\in\{n,n+1\}
.
For any
0\leq i\leq n
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n},\mu_{n+1})\in P_{n+1}
, one has

(i)

if
\mu_{n+1}=0
then
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{n+1},\Bbbk)_{\mu}\neq 0
if and only if
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{n},\Bbbk)_{(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\neq 0
,

(ii)

if
0<\mu_{n+1}<\mu_{n}
then
\mathrm{Tor}\hskip 1.0pt_{i}(I_{n+1},\Bbbk)_{\mu}=0
,

(iii)

if
\mu_{n+1}=\mu_{n}
, then

Transformers Learn Nonlinear Features In Context: Nonconvex Mean-field Dynamics on the Attention Landscape
Juno Kim, Taiji Suzuki

Abstract
Large language models based on the Transformer architecture have demonstrated impressive capabilities to learn in context. However, existing theoretical studies on how this phenomenon arises are limited to the dynamics of a single layer of attention trained on linear regression tasks. In this paper, we study the optimization of a Transformer consisting of a fully connected layer followed by a linear attention layer. The MLP acts as a common nonlinear representation or feature map, greatly enhancing the power of in-context learning. We prove in the mean-field and two-timescale limit that the infinite-dimensional loss landscape for the distribution of parameters, while highly nonconvex, becomes quite benign. We also analyze the second-order stability of mean-field dynamics and show that Wasserstein gradient flow almost always avoids saddle points. Furthermore, we establish novel methods for obtaining concrete improvement rates both away from and near critical points. This represents the first saddle point analysis of mean-field dynamics in general and the techniques are of independent interest.

Introduction

Attention-based neural architectures such as Transformers have revolutionized modern machine learning, from tasks in natural language and computer vision to multi-modal learning and beyond. Recently, interest has surged in the remarkable ability of such large language models to learn
in context
, leading to a major paradigm shift in how intelligence arises in artificial systems. In-context learning (ICL) refers to the capacity of a pretrained model to solve previously unseen tasks based on demonstrative example prompts without further tuning its parameters.

A vigorous line of research initiated by
Garg et al. 2022
has sought to understand the mechanism behind ICL from a theoretical perspective, where prompts are real-valued input-output pairs
(x_{i},f(x_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}
generated from some function class
\TT
. Studies have shown that Transformers are capable of implementing various statistical learning algorithms such as gradient descent (GD) in context
(
von Oswald et al. 2023
;
Akyürek et al. 2023
;
Bai et al. 2023
)
. In particular,
Guo et al. 2023
consider the realistic setting of learning
with representations
where MLP layers act as transformations on top of which ICL is performed, and show that such models consistently achieve near-optimal performance.

While promising, these results are based on specific constructions which may not accurately reflect ICL in real models
(
Shen et al. 2023
)
. Other works have analyzed how ICL emerges from the training dynamics of Transformers
(
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Huang et al. 2023
;
Ahn et al. 2023a
)
. However, they are limited to models consisting of only a single attention layer due to the dynamical complexity and thus can only explain ICL of linear functions. Hence the following central question at the intersection of the two approaches remains unsolved:

How does in-context learning with nonlinear representations (features) arise in Transformers with

MLP layers, optimized via gradient descent?

In this paper, we investigate the optimization dynamics of a Transformer model consisting of a two-layer MLP followed by a linear attention layer, pretrained on linear transformations of feature representations. Contrary to existing approaches which attempt to solve for exact dynamics of the attention matrices, we factor out the attention layer via a two-timescale argument and study the geometric properties of the
loss landscape
faced by an overparametrized MLP. Our contributions are highlighted as follows.

•

We show that the MLP layer greatly increases the flexibility of ICL by extending the class of learnable functions to the Barron space and plays an essential role by encoding task-common features during pretraining.

•

Lifting to the mean-field regime, we show that this infinite-dimensional ‘attention landscape’ is benign (strictly saddle) via directional analysis: all critical points are either global minima or saddle points.

•

We formally prove that mean-field dynamics ‘almost always’ avoids saddles, explaining how the MLP learns globally optimal representations. •

2.1
Setup: In-Context Learning

The basic theoretical framework for studying ICL was first proposed by
Garg et al. 2022
and has since been widely embraced
(
Bai et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Ahn et al. 2023a
;
Huang et al. 2023
;
Lin et al. 2023
;
Wu et al. 2024
)
. Let
\DD_{\XX}
be a distribution over the input space
\XX\subseteq\RR^{d}
, and let
\TT
be a class of functions
\XX\to\RR
with a distribution
\DD_{\TT}
over functions. For each prompt, we generate a new task
f\sim\DD_{\TT}
and a batch of
n
example input-output pairs
(\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
where
\bm{x}_{i}\sim\DD_{\XX}
are i.i.d. and
y_{i}=f(\bm{x}_{i})
. We also independently generate a query token
\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}\sim\DD_{\XX}
. The prompt is gathered into an embedding matrix

\mathbf{E}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{E}^{\bm{x}}\\
\mathbf{E}^{y}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{x}_{n}&\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}\\
y_{1}&\cdots&y_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}\in\RR^{(d+1)(n+1)}.

In-context learning of a pretrained model
\mathbb{M}
refers to the ability to form predictions
\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}}=\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{E})
for
y_{\textup{qr}}=f(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})
without knowledge of the current task
f
and without updating its parameters.

2.2
MLP-Attention Transformer

We now formally define our Transformer model, which consists of a feedforward two-layer neural network (MLP) followed by a single linear self-attention (LSA) layer. This serves as a proxy of the original Transformer which consists of alternating feedforward and attention layers. As in
Guo et al. 2023
, we switch the conventional ordering of the two networks to view attention as a mechanism to exchange feature information encoded into the MLP layer (we may also consider the initial embedding as the first MLP layer).

MLP layer.

A vector-valued neuron with parameter
\theta=(\bm{a},\bm{w})^{\top}\in\Theta\subseteq\RR^{k}\times\RR^{d}
and activation
\sigma:\RR\to\RR
is defined as
h_{\theta}(\bm{x})=\bm{a}\sigma(\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x})
. While the original Transformer takes
k=d
, we allow any
k\leq d
representing the number of distinct features. The mean-field network corresponding to a measure
\mu\in\PP(\Theta)
is defined as
\textstyle h_{\mu}(\bm{x})=\int_{\Theta}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)
. We will also denote
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\nu}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\DD_{\XX}}[h_{\mu}(\bm{x})h_{\nu}(\bm{x})^{\top}]\in\RR^{k\times k}
. As we wish to extract features from the input tokens, the MLP is applied to only the covariates
\bm{x}_{i}
and
\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}
so that the prompt
\mathbf{E}
is transformed into

\MLP(\mathbf{E})=\begin{bmatrix}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{1})&\cdots&h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{n})&h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})\\
y_{1}&\cdots&y_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}.

LSA layer.

Linear attention is widely used to accelerate the quadratic complexity of standard attention
(
Katharopoulos et al. 2020
;
Yang et al. 2023
)
and can capture various aspects of softmax attention
(
Ahn et al. 2023b
)
while still being theoretically amenable. For query, key and value matrices
\mathbf{W}^{Q},\mathbf{W}^{K},\mathbf{W}^{V}\in\RR^{(k+1)(k+1)}
define

\textstyle\LSA(\mathbf{E})=\mathbf{W}^{V}\mathbf{E}\cdot\frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{W}^{K}\mathbf{E})^{\top}(\mathbf{W}^{Q}\mathbf{E}).

We impose a specific form, shown to achieve the global optimum in
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Mahankali et al. 2023
, where the query and key matrices are consolidated into
\mathbf{W}\in\RR^{k\times k}
and
\mathbf{W}^{V}
is reduced to a scalar multiplier
v
:

\mathbf{W}^{V}=\begin{bmatrix}*&*\\
0_{d}^{\top}&v\end{bmatrix},\quad(\mathbf{W}^{K})^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{Q}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{W}&0_{d}\\
0_{d}^{\top}&*\end{bmatrix}.

We further absorb
v
into
\mathbf{W}
and fix
v=1
in order to focus on the more complex dynamics of the MLP layer. Corresponding to the position of
y_{\textup{qr}}
, the
(k+1,n+1)
th element of the output
\LSA\circ\MLP(\mathbf{E})
is read out as the model prediction. Multiplying out the matrices yields

\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}).

Hence
\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}}
can be interpreted as a linear smoother with the kernel
k(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})=\frac{1}{n}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})
encoded by the MLP layer (cf.
Tsai et al. 2019
for softmax attention).

Regression over features.

In this paper, we study ICL of linear regression tasks over a common nonlinear transformation or feature map
f^{\circ}\in C(\XX,\RR^{k})
, that is
\TT=\{\bm{v}^{\top}f^{\circ}|\,\bm{v}\in\RR^{k}\}
with covariance
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{v}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{v}}[\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\top}]
. By replacing
f^{\circ}
by
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{v}}^{1/2}f^{\circ}
, we may assume
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{v}}=\mathbf{I}_{k}
.
We also take the
n\to\infty
(infinite prompt length) limit to disregard sampling error and let
\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[f(\bm{x})h_{\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}]\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})
for any task
f\in\TT
; but see Appendix
C.4
for a discussion on how to incorporate finite samples. Hence our Transformer is pretrained with the following mean squared risk,

\displaystyle\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W}):=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}},\bm{v}}\left[(y_{\textup{qr}}-\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}})^{2}\right]

(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}}\left[\left\lVert f^{\circ}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[f^{\circ}(\bm{x})h_{\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}]\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})\right\rVert^{2}\right].

Our goal is to show that gradient dynamics converges to a global minimum such that
\LL_{\textup{TF}}=0
. Then the MLP layer has successfully learned the true representations
f^{\circ}
, and even for a new or ‘unseen’ task
\bm{v}_{\text{new}}\in\RR^{k}
the Transformer is able to return the correct regression output
y_{\textup{qr}}
:

\widehat{y}_{\textup{qr}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{v}_{\textup{new}}^{\top}f^{\circ}(\bm{x})h_{\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}]\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})=\bm{v}_{\textup{new}}^{\top}f^{\circ}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}).

We call this behavior
in-context feature learning
(ICFL).

2.3
Expressivity of Representations

Before delving into the training dynamics of our Transformer, we show by extending classical analyses of two-layer neural networks that adding even a shallow MLP results in greatly increased in-context learning capabilities, justifying our feature-based approach.

Multivariate Barron class.

Barron-type spaces have been well established as the natural function classes for analyzing approximation and generalization of shallow neural networks
(
Barron 1994
;
Weinan et al. 2020
;
Weinan & Wojtowytsch 2022
)
. Here, we extend the theory to our vector-valued setting. We focus on the ReLU case for ease of presentation, but many results extend to more general activations
(
Klusowski & Barron 2016
;
Li et al. 2020
)
.

Set
\Theta=\RR^{k}\times\RR^{d}
,
\sigma(z)=\max\{0,z\}
and suppose
M_{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\DD_{\XX}}[\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{2}]<\infty
. The Barron space
\Brr_{p}
of order
p\in[1,\infty]
is defined as the set of functions
f=h_{\mu}
,
\mu\in\PP(\Theta)
with finite Barron norm

\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{p}}:=\inf_{\mu:f=h_{\mu}}\left(\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert^{p}\lVert\bm{w}\rVert^{p}\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{1/p}.

This turns out to not depend on
p
(Lemma
B.1
), so we refer to
the
Barron space and norm as
(\Brr,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\Brr})
. This space contains a rich variety of functions. The following is an application of the classical Fourier analysis
(
Barron 1993
)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
h_{\mu}
includes a bias term, i.e.
\XX\subseteq\XX_{0}\times\{1\}
. If
f=(f_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\in C(\XX_{0},\RR^{k})
such that each
f_{j}
satisfies
\textstyle\inf_{\widehat{f}_{j}}\int_{\RR^{d-1}}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1}^{2}|\widehat{f}_{j}(\omega)|<\infty
for
\widehat{f}_{j}
the Fourier transform of an extension of
f_{j}
to
\RR^{d-1}
, then
f\in\Brr
. In particular, the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\XX_{0})^{k}\subset\Brr
for
s>\frac{d+1}{2}
.

Furthermore,
\Brr
is exactly the class of representations that can be learned in context, demonstrating the expressive power gained by incorporating the MLP layer:

Lemma 2.2
.

\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W})=0
has a solution such that
\esssup_{\mu}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert<\infty
if and only if
f^{\circ}\in\Brr
.

In contrast,
Mahankali et al. 2023
show that the optimal LSA-only Transformer implements one step of GD for the
linear
regression problem
(\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
even when
y_{i}|\bm{x}_{i}
is nonlinear; thus we establish a clear gap in learning ability.

Generalization to unseen tasks.

If the Transformer has successfully learned
f^{\circ}
, it will achieve perfect accuracy on any new linear task
\bm{v}_{\textup{new}}^{\top}f^{\circ}
as discussed. On the other hand, if the test task is an arbitrary function
g\in C(\XX)
, we cannot hope to do better than the projection to the linear span of learned features
f_{1}^{\circ},\cdots,f_{k}^{\circ}
since (
2
) is a regression loss. We show this lower bound is optimal:

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W})\leq\epsilon
for
f^{\circ}\in\Brr
and
\lVert h_{\mu}\rVert_{\Brr},\lVert\mathbf{W}\rVert\lesssim 1
. Then for any new task
g\in C(\XX)
with
\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\DD_{\XX})}\lesssim 1
, the ICL test error satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}}}\left[\left\lVert g(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[g(\bm{x})h_{\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}]\mathbf{W}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{\textup{qr}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+\inf_{\bm{v}\in\RR^{k}}\lVert g-\bm{v}^{\top}f^{\circ}\rVert_{L^{2}(\DD_{\XX})}^{2}.

This extends the LSA-only case where the optimal output was shown to be the near-optimal linear model in
Zhang et al. 2023a
. This also raises an important question: if the task
g
depends nonlinearly on
h_{\mu^{\circ}}
, is it still beneficial to have learned the relevant features
\mu^{\circ}
? Clearly this depends on both
g
and the initialization
\mu_{0}
; however, we present experiments supporting this intuition in Section
6
.

2.4
From Finite to Infinite Width

Continuing the above discussion, elements of the Barron space are effectively approximated by finite-width networks, which can be seen as an adaptive kernel method. The proof of the following is essentially due to
Weinan et al. 2022
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any integer
N
and
f^{\circ}\in\Brr
, there exists a width
N
network
\widehat{h}_{N}
given by the discrete measure
\widehat{\mu}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\theta^{(j)}}
with path norm
\lVert\widehat{h}_{N}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lVert\bm{a}^{(j)}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}^{(j)}\rVert\leq 3\lVert f^{\circ}\rVert_{\Brr}
and

\inf_{\mathbf{W}}\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\widehat{\mu}_{N},\mathbf{W})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{h}_{N}-f^{\circ}\rVert_{L^{2}(\DD_{\XX})}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{2}\lVert f^{\circ}\rVert_{\Brr}^{2}}{N}.

Using the low Rademacher complexity of Barron spaces, we can also simultaneously bound the generalization gap for a finite number of tasks
T
as
\widetilde{O}(T^{-1/2})
which is nearly minimax optimal
(
Weinan et al. 2019
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Moreover from a dynamical perspective, a propagation of chaos argument
(
Sznitman 1991
)
shows that gradient descent indeed converges to (
1
) in the infinite-width limit. Let
F:\PP_{2}(\Omega)\to\RR
be any
C^{1}
functional such that
\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}\rVert\leq L_{1}
,
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)
is
L_{2}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\theta
and
L_{3}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\mu
in the
\WW_{1}
metric. Denote the initial measure as
\mu_{0}\in\PP_{2}(\Omega)
, let
\theta_{0}^{(1)},\cdots,\theta_{0}^{(N)}
be i.i.d. samples from
\mu_{0}
and consider the empirical GF trajectories

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\theta_{t}^{(j)}=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\widehat{\mu}_{t,N},\theta_{t}^{(j)}),\quad\widehat{\mu}_{t,N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\theta_{t}^{(j)}}.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
T\geq 0
, the
N
-particle empirical measure
\widehat{\mu}_{t,N}
converges to
\mu_{t}
as
\mathbb{E}[\WW_{1}(\widehat{\mu}_{t,N},\mu_{t})]\to 0
uniformly for all
t\in[0,T]
as
N\to\infty
.

See Remark
B.4
for the case of the ICFL objective. Hence it is natural to analyze optimization in the mean-field or extremely overparametrized regime.

3
Benign Attention Landscape

In this Section, we characterize the infinite-dimensional landscape of the ICFL objective. We will see that while highly nonconvex,
\LL_{\textup{TF}}
possesses various desirable properties that make global optimization feasible via first-order methods. We first state two mild assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

The nonlinearity is
C^{2}
and bounded as
|\sigma|\leq R_{1}
,
|\sigma^{\prime}|\leq R_{2}
,
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\leq R_{3}
. The parameter space is
\Theta=\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\RR^{d}
. The input distribution
\DD_{\XX}
has finite 4th moment,
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\DD_{\XX}}[\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{j}]=M_{j}<\infty
for
j=2,4
.

The smoothness of
\sigma
(which rules out ReLU activation) and restriction of the second layer to
\mathbb{D}^{k}
are technicalities to easily ensure regularity bounds. Alternatively, we may take the parameter space to be
\Theta=\RR^{k}\times\RR^{d}
without no-flux constraints and rely on the second moment bound of
\bm{a}
; see Lemma
E.2
and the preceding comments. Only the assumption
|\sigma|\leq R_{1}
is needed in this Section, which implies that
h_{\theta},h_{\mu}
lie within the ball of radius
R_{1}
in
\RR^{k}
.

Next, from the solvability condition of Lemma
2.2
we are naturally led to take
f^{\circ}=h_{\mu^{\circ}}
for some ‘true’ distribution
\mu^{\circ}
, which allows for a rich class of feature representations. We only require nondegeneracy of
f^{\circ}
:

Assumption 2
.

f^{\circ}=h_{\mu^{\circ}}
for some
\mu^{\circ}\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
satisfying
\underline{r}\mathbf{I}_{k}\preceq\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu^{\circ}}\preceq\overline{r}\mathbf{I}_{k}
for
\overline{r}\geq\underline{r}>0
.
4
4

4

Before task rescaling, this is equivalent to assuming
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{v}}
is also invertible and
f^{\circ}
is in a rescaled Barron class due to the pushforward argument in Section
3.2
.

In particular, we do not assume any Gaussianity as in
von Oswald et al. 2023
;
Akyürek et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2023a
nor orthonormality as in
Huang et al. 2023
. Note
\underline{r}\leq\frac{R_{1}^{2}}{k}
automatically since
R_{1}^{2}\geq\tr\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu^{\circ}}\geq k\underline{r}
, and also
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\nu}\preceq R_{1}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}
for all
\mu,\nu
. We will subsequently take
\underline{r},\overline{r}=\Theta(\frac{1}{k})
to extract the dependency on
k
.

One implicit assumption is that the number of true features
\dim h_{\mu^{\circ}}
is known and equal to
k
. When the dimensions do not match, attention can perform the regulatory function of selecting important features
(
Yasuda et al. 2023
)
; we leave a dynamical characterization to future work. Experiments on a misspecified model are also conducted in Section
6
.

3.1
Fast Convergence of Attention

In order to isolate the more nuanced dynamics of
\mu
, we first notice that minimizing
\LL_{\textup{TF}}
over
\mathbf{W}
is a least-squares regression problem. In particular,
\LL_{\textup{TF}}
is convex with respect to
\mathbf{W}
(strongly convex unless
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}
or
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}
are singular) and thus is optimized potentially much more quickly.

A possibility is that the MLP
\bm{x}\mapsto h_{\mu}(\bm{x})
degenerates to completely lie within a low-dimensional linear subspace of
\RR^{k}
. As the regression (
2
) is ill-conditioned in this case, we set
\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta):=\{\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta):\rank\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}<k\}
and restrict our attention to
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)=\PP_{2}(\Theta)\setminus\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta)
.
5
5

5

We show the singular set
\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta)
is sparse in a strong sense in Proposition
C.5
, justifying subsequent calculations.
Jiang et al. 2022
suggest that adaptive optimization methods can outperform SGD by biasing trajectories away from ill-conditioned regions.
Similarly to the asymptotic convergence of
\mathbf{W}
to
\Var(\bm{x})^{-1}
for the LSA-only model
(
Zhang et al. 2023a
)
, we then have:

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
\mu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
and any initialization
\mathbf{W}_{0}\in\RR^{k\times k}
, the flow
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{W}_{t}=-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W}_{t})
converges linearly to some
\mathbf{W}_{\mu}\in\argmin_{\mathbf{W}}\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W})
which satisfies
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\mathbf{W}_{\mu}=\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}
.

Therefore it is reasonable to suppose that
\mathbf{W}
is updated sufficiently quickly and has already converged to
\mathbf{W}_{\mu}
for each
\mu
– formally by modeling as two-timescale dynamics
(
Berglund & Gentz 2006
)
– leading us to study the objective

\LL(\mu):=\inf_{\mathbf{W}}\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,\mathbf{W})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\rVert^{2}\right]

(3)

where we denote
\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x}):=h_{\mu^{\circ}}(\bm{x})-\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})
. Note the constant bound
0\leq\LL(\mu)\leq\LL_{\textup{TF}}(\mu,0_{k\times k})\leq\frac{k\overline{r}}{2}
.

3.2
No Spurious Local Minima

For an orthogonal matrix
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{O}(k)
, define
\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu
as the pushforward of
\mu
along the rotation map
\mathbf{R}:(\bm{a},\bm{w})\mapsto(\mathbf{R}\bm{a},\bm{w})
so that
h_{\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu}(\bm{x})=\int_{\Theta}\mathbf{R}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)=\mathbf{R}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})
. Since the convex hull of
\mathcal{O}(k)\subset\RR^{k\times k}
is equal to
\BB_{1}(k)
, this can be extended to any
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
by decomposing
\mathbf{R}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
and defining
\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\sharp\mu
. See Lemma
C.6
for details of the construction. Achieving zero loss implies that we have learned the true representation
h_{\mu^{\circ}}
up to a linear transformation:

Lemma 3.2
.

The pushforwards
\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}
for any invertible
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
are global minima of
\LL
. Conversely, any global minimum
\mu
of
\LL
satisfies
h_{\mu}(\bm{x})=\mathbf{R}h_{\mu^{\circ}}(\bm{x}),\,\forall\bm{x}\in\XX
for some invertible matrix
\mathbf{R}
.

The following theorem is the main result of this Section. It states that for any
\mu
that is not a global minimum, it is either (1) possible to move in a direction where
\LL
is strictly decreasing, or (2)
\LL
possesses an unstable direction. In particular, all local minima must also be global minima.

Theorem 3.3

(no spurious local minima)
.

For any
\mu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
that is not a global minimum the following hold:

1.

There exists
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
depending on
\mu
such that along the linear homotopy
\bar{\mu}_{s}=(1-s)\mu+s\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}
we have
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{t=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq 0
.

2.

If
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})=0
for all
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
above, then
\LL(\mu)\geq\frac{\underline{r}}{2}
and
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{2}}|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\frac{4}{kR_{1}^{2}}\LL(\mu)^{2}
for some
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
.

The proof, deferred to Appendix
C.2
, exploits the linearity of the MLP output in
\mu
to analyze linearized perturbations.

As a corollary of
2
, critical points cannot exist in the band
0<\LL<\frac{\underline{r}}{2}
. The threshold, controlled by
\underline{r}
, corresponds to the minimum loss when the features
h_{\mu}
are uninformative in the sense that the regression coefficient
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}
against the true features is singular. This observation can be improved to the following quantitative guarantee:

Proposition 3.4

(accelerated convergence phase)
.

Let
\delta\in[0,\frac{\underline{r}^{2}}{4R_{1}^{2}}]
. For any
\mu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
such that

\frac{\underline{r}-\sqrt{\underline{r}^{2}-4R_{1}^{2}\delta}}{4}\leq\LL(\mu)\leq\frac{\underline{r}+\sqrt{\underline{r}^{2}-4R_{1}^{2}\delta}}{4},

there exists
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
such that along
\bar{\mu}_{s}=(1-s)\mu+s\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}
we have
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\delta
.

See Appendix
C.3
for the proof. In other words, once in the band
(0,\frac{\underline{r}}{2})
we are guaranteed a non-vanishing gradient which moreover becomes steeper closer to the center of the band, proportional to
\LL(\mu)(\frac{\underline{r}}{2}-\LL(\mu))
. We prove that for MFD this results in an acceleration-deceleration phase when converging to global minima in Theorem
5.3
.

4
Mean-field Dynamics Avoids Saddle Points

4.1
Local Geometry of Wasserstein Space

Strict saddle properties such as Theorem
3.3
have powerful implications for nonconvex optimization. In finite dimensions, a central result states that GD almost always avoids saddle points and converges to global optima
(
Lee et al. 2019
)
; see Appendix
D.1
for a recap. We develop the analogous general result for Wasserstein gradient flows (WGF) (
1
) by combining tools from functional analysis, optimal transport and metric geometry.

Let
F:\PP_{2}(\Omega)\to\RR
a general
C^{2}
functional with domain
\Omega\subseteq\RR^{m}
. We use the elegant formalism of Otto calculus
(
Otto 2001
)
to analyze local behavior of distributional flows. The reader is referred to Appendix
D
as well as
Ambrosio et al. 2005
;
Villani 2009
for expository details. There is a one-to-one equivalence between absolutely continuous curves
(\mu_{t})
in
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
and time-dependent gradient vector fields
(\bm{v}_{t})
on
\Omega
solving
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot(\bm{v}_{t}\mu_{t})
. This motivates the formal definition of the tangent space to
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
at
\mu
as

\Tan_{\mu}\PP_{2}(\Omega):=\overline{\{\bm{v}=\nabla\psi:\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\}}^{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)}

(4)

with the inherited inner product. We can view nearby measures as slight pushforwards of
\mu
along the optimal transport map
\id_{\Omega}+\epsilon\bm{v}
,
\epsilon>0
analogously to the exponential map.

4.2
Stability of Wasserstein Gradient Flow

With the above framework in mind, we derive a local transport characterization of MFD by lifting to the tangent space.

Lemma 4.1
.

The WGF
(\mu_{t})
in a neighborhood of a critical point
\mu^{\dagger}
of
F
can be written as
\mu_{t}=(\id_{\Omega}+\epsilon\bm{v}_{t})\sharp\mu^{\dagger}
where the velocity field
\bm{v}_{t}
changes as

\partial_{t}\bm{v}_{t}(\theta)=-\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\bm{v}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})+o(1).

(5)

Here,
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}:(\Omega\times\Omega,\mu\otimes\mu)\to\RR^{m\times m}
denotes the matrix-valued kernel
\textstyle\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime}):=\nabla_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})
.

The tangent field
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})
satisfies a similar dynamics (Lemma
5.4
); we posit
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
is the fundamental quantity governing second-order behavior of WGF. This facilitates stability analysis via the spectral theory of linear operators,

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose the kernel
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
is Hilbert-Schmidt for
\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Omega)
, that is
\iint\lVert\mathbf{H}_{\mu}\rVert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu<\infty
. Then the corresponding integral operator on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})
,

\HH_{\mu}:f\mapsto\HH_{\mu}\!f(\theta)=\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})f(\theta^{\prime})\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

(6)

is compact self-adjoint, hence there exists an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\ZZ}
for
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})
of eigenfunctions of
\HH_{\mu}
.

We are thus motivated to define the set of
strict saddle
points as
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}:=\{\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Omega):\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu)=0,\,\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\HH_{\mu})<0\}
. Near such points, we now apply the center-stable manifold theorem for Banach spaces (Theorem
D.3
). This tells us that
\Tan_{\mu^{\dagger}}\PP_{2}(\Omega)
can be decomposed into a direct sum of
\HH_{\mu^{\dagger}}
-invariant subspaces
\mathscr{E}^{s}\oplus\mathscr{E}^{u}
such that all flows (
5
) converging to
\mu^{\dagger}
must be eventually contained in the graph of a
C^{1}
map
h:\mathscr{E}^{s}\to\mathscr{E}^{u}
defined near the origin. Denoting the reversed WGF for time
t
as
\omega_{t}^{-}
whenever it is defined – which forms a bi-Lipschitz inverse for the forward flow
(
Ambrosio et al. 2005
, Theorem 11.1.4)
– we conclude:

Theorem 4.3
.

For any
C^{2}
functional
F:\PP_{2}(\Omega)\to\RR
with Hilbert-Schmidt kernel
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
, the set
\mathscr{G}_{0}^{\dagger}=\{\mu_{0}\in\PP_{2}(\Omega):\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}\in\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\}
of initializations which converge to strictly saddle points is contained in the countable union
\cup_{\ell\in\NN}\cup_{j\in\NN}\omega_{\ell}^{-}(\mathscr{V}_{j})
of images of submanifolds
\mathscr{V}_{j}
.

Remark 4.4
.

We point out that Otto calculus is only formal in the sense that existence and regularity issues are ignored, so it is difficult to rigorously turn the above into a meaningful measure-theoretic statement. This is compounded by the fact that there is no well-behaved canonical measure on
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
. A possible justification is to restrict to the subspace of measures with smooth positive Lebesgue density whose geometry is well-behaved
(
Lott 2008
;
Villani 2009
)
, but this is outside of the scope of our paper.

For the ICFL objective (
3
), Proposition
E.6
together with Theorem
3.3
2
will show that all critical points that are not global optima are strictly saddle in
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
. Hence Theorem
4.3
applies to
\LL
with the domain of interest replaced by
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
, and thus ‘almost all’ convergent flows in
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
must converge to global minima.

Application to three-layer networks.

The problem (
3
) can also be motivated by the training dynamics of a certain three-layer neural network in a teacher-student setting. We construct the first two layers identically to our MLP layer
h_{\mu}
and add a linear third layer given by the transformation
\mathbf{T}\in\RR^{k\times k}
. Then the
L^{2}
loss with respect to a teacher network
\bm{x}\mapsto\mathbf{T}^{\star}h_{\mu^{\star}}(\bm{x})
can be written as

\LL_{\textup{NN}}(\mu,\mathbf{T})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[\lVert\mathbf{T}^{\star}h_{\mu^{\star}}(\bm{x})-\mathbf{T}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})\rVert^{2}].

By setting
\mu^{\circ}=\mathbf{T}^{\star}\sharp\mu^{\star}
and taking the two-timescale limit where the last layer updates infinitely quickly, we see that
\mathbf{T}
must converge to
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}
and we end up with the regression objective (
3
), hence Sections
3
-
5
also directly apply to this problem. We remark that the two-timescale regime has been leveraged to show convergence of SGD for
two
-layer networks in
Marion & Berthier 2023
.

5
Convergence Rates for ICFL

Theorem
4.3
is encouraging but only qualitative. In this Section, we develop brand-new approaches to obtain quantitative improvement results for mean-field dynamics, in particular for the ICFL objective, (1) away from critical points; (2) near global minima; and (3) near saddle points.

5.1
MFD with Birth-Death

Consider the WGF (
1
) for
F=\LL
, where we have

\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\right].

To preserve the benign landscape, we do not add entropic regularization typically required in mean-field analyses. However, a different modification will be beneficial in obtaining concrete rates. For a fixed distribution
\pi\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
, if at any time
t\geq 0
the density ratio
\inf_{\Theta}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}
is no larger then a small threshold
\gamma
, we perform the discrete update
\mu_{t}\leftarrow(1-\gamma)\mu_{t}+\gamma\pi
. Forcing
\mu_{t}
slightly towards
\pi
ensures sufficient mass to decrease the objective at all times. This can be implemented by a birth-death process where a fraction
\gamma
of all neurons are randomly deleted and replaced with samples from
\pi
whenever
\LL
does not sufficiently decrease; see Algorithm
1
in the Appendix. For
\pi
, we require:

Assumption 3
.

\pi
is spherically symmetric in the
\bm{a}
component, that is
\pi(\bm{a},\bm{w})=\pi(\bm{a}^{\prime},\bm{w})
if
\lVert\bm{a}\rVert=\lVert\bm{a}^{\prime}\rVert
. Also,
\mu^{\circ}
has finite density w.r.t.
\pi
as
\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\circ}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\rVert_{\infty}\leq R_{4}
.

Remark 5.1
.

The continuous-time version of birth-death can be written as a PDE with discontinuous forcing,

\textstyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{t}\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\right)+\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\inf_{\Theta}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\leq\gamma\right\}}\gamma(\pi-\mu_{t}),

which also ensures an
\Omega(\gamma)
lower bound. A similar perturbation has been studied before for convex MFD in
Wei et al. 2019
. Birth-death mechanisms can also accelerate convergence of mean-field networks
(
Rotskoff et al. 2019
)
.

5.2
First-order Improvement

We first give a result which translates nonzero gradients along a direction of improvement into a first-order rate of decrease for the gradient flow. Unlike convex mean-field
Langevin
dynamics which relies on a log-Sobolev inequality to control dissipation
(
Nitanda et al. 2022
)
, our idea is to exploit the mobility of the second layer mass. The argument works for any objective built on top of the MLP layer
h_{\mu}
; see Proposition
E.1
in the Appendix for the general result.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose MFD with birth-death on
\LL
at time
t
satisfies Theorem
3.3
1
with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\delta
. Then
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\LL(\mu_{t})\leq-R_{4}^{-1}\gamma\delta^{2}
.

Since a steep gradient is guaranteed in the accelerated convergence phase by Proposition
3.4
, we further establish the following convergence rate.

Theorem 5.3

(accelerated convergence rate)
.

Once
\LL(\mu_{t})\leq 0.49\underline{r}
is satisfied, MFD with birth-death will converge in loss with
\LL(\mu_{t+\tau})\leq\epsilon
in at most
\tau=O(\frac{k^{2}}{\gamma\epsilon})
time.

The rate is quadratic in the
feature
dimension
k
and independent of
d=\dim\XX
. Hereafter, big
O
notation hides at most polynomial dependency on constants
R_{j},M_{j}
, while dependency on
k,d,\gamma
is made explicit and
\underline{r},\overline{r}=\Theta(\frac{1}{k})
.

5.3
Second-order Improvement

We now arrive at the main difficulty of our analysis: the behavior of mean-field dynamics near critical points. In the finite-dimensional case, local stability is determined by the Hessian matrix. We show that the mean-field analogue is

Lemma 5.4
.

For a smooth functional
F:\PP_{2}(\Theta)\to\RR
, the velocity field
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}
of (
1
) satisfies the evolution equation

\partial_{t}\left[\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\right]=-\HH_{\mu_{t}}\left[\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\right].

(7)

This is the non-perturbative or tangent curve version of Lemma
4.1
. For the specific objective
\LL
, we show that
\HH_{\mu}
is Hilbert-Schmidt and derive regularity properties in Lemma
E.3
and
E.5
. Next, the following lemma translates second-order instability into a spectral bound for
\HH_{\mu}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose MFD with birth-death at time
t
satisfies Theorem
3.3
2
with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{2}}\big|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\Lambda
. Then the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
\HH_{\mu_{t}}
satisfies
\lambda_{0}\leq-R_{4}^{-1}\gamma\Lambda
.

Therefore we expect that even if the dynamics is close to a saddle point and Proposition
5.2
is not useful, as long as the
L^{2}
-component along the eigenfunction
\psi_{0}
corresponding to
\lambda_{0}
is not exactly zero, it will blow up exponentially in time until
\mu_{t}
escapes and makes progress. In detail,

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose MFD with birth-death on
\LL
satisfies Theorem
3.3
1
with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\geq-O(k^{-1}\LL(\mu_{t})^{2})
at time
t
. Further suppose
\psi_{0}
satisfies

\bigg|\int\psi_{0}^{\top}\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\bigg|\geq\alpha

for some
\alpha>0
. Then for the time interval

\tau=\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{k}{\gamma\LL(\mu_{t})^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\alpha}\right),

MFD in the region
\{\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta):\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu})=\Omega(\frac{1}{k})\}
decreases
\LL
as

\LL(\mu_{t+\tau})\leq\LL(\mu_{t})-\widetilde{\Omega}\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}\alpha\LL(\mu_{t})^{4}}{k^{5}d}\right).

Simply put, we make
\widetilde{\Omega}(\alpha)
progress in
\widetilde{O}(\log\frac{1}{\alpha})
time. The proof idea is to find a
\WW_{2}
-ball where if
\mu_{t}
does not escape in time
\tau
, the exponential blowup guarantees improvement of
\LL
; if
\mu_{t}
does escape,
\LL
must have decreased enough to warrant such displacement (via the Benamou-Brenier formula). Again, we present general versions of Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
5.6
as Proposition
E.6
and Theorem
E.7
.

Dimensional dependency.

The rate is polynomial in the number of features
k
but only linear in
d
, mitigating the curse of dimensionality. Initially
\LL
decreases by
\widetilde{\Omega}(k^{-5}d^{-1})
in time
\widetilde{O}(k)
when
\LL=\Theta(1)
. As training progresses, the rate worsens to
\widetilde{\Omega}(k^{-9}d^{-1})
in time
\widetilde{O}(k^{3})
when
\LL=\Theta(\frac{1}{k})
due to the smaller curvature of
\LL
, until we enter the accelerated convergence phase and Theorem
5.3
takes over.

Remark 5.7
.

Since
\LL
becomes ill-conditioned if
h_{\mu}(\bm{x})
is nearly constrained on a subspace, we have assumed that
\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu})
is locally bounded below (on the same order as the upper bound
\frac{R_{1}^{2}}{k}
) to obtain regularity estimates. We expect this to not be a problem in practice since
\mathbf{W}
will not diverge without timescale separation. In our experiments,
\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu})
never changed by over 25% during each training phase.

5.4
Escaping from Saddle Points Efficiently

Theorem
5.6
on its own cannot ensure convergence rates. The flow might be initialized at or pass near multiple saddle points with very small
\alpha
values, taking longer to escape. This is an unavoidable problem of nonconvex gradient descent even in finite dimensions
(
Du et al. 2017
)
. In contrast, it has been shown that simply adding uniform noise allows GD to escape saddle points efficiently
(
Ge et al. 2015
;
Jin et al. 2017
)
. Here, we suggest without proof an adaptation to the Wasserstein gradient flow.

The main problem is how to apply ‘random’ perturbations in the infinite-dimensional space
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
. Motivated by the characterization of the tangent space (
4
), we propose the following scheme which constructs perturbations in the velocity space using vector-valued Gaussian processes. See Definition
E.8
and Algorithm
1
for details.

1.

Generate a random velocity field
\bm{\xi}:\Omega\to\RR^{m}
from a stationary Gaussian process
\GP(0,\mathbf{K})
with bounded kernel
\mathbf{K}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\RR^{m\times m}
.

2.

Run the pushforward dynamics
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot(\bm{\xi}\mu_{t})
from
\mu_{0}=\mu^{\dagger}
for fixed time
\Delta t
.

This can bypass the dimensional dependency in
Ge et al. 2015
and ensure a nonzero
\psi_{0}
-component for
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{\Delta t})
, which is approximately normally distributed with variance
O(\Delta t)
(Lemma
E.9
). Unfortunately this naive approach is not enough to ensure large
\alpha
, at least in polynomial time, since the eigenfunction
\psi_{0}
and base measure also change along the perturbation.
Jin et al. 2017
bypass this issue in finite dimensions via a geometric argument; we conjecture that our method also guarantees polynomial escape time. If this is true, we may combine Proposition
5.2
with
\delta=O(k^{-1}\LL(\mu_{t})^{2})
, yielding
O(\frac{k^{6}}{\gamma^{3}t^{3}})
convergence away from saddle points, and Theorem
5.6
to conclude that
perturbed
WGF enjoys polynomial convergence to global minima.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we explored the training dynamics of a Transformer with one MLP and one attention layer, enabling in-context feature learning of regression tasks on a rich class of representations. We showed that the loss landscape becomes benign in the two-timescale and mean-field limit and developed instability and improvement guarantees for the Wasserstein gradient flow. To our knowledge, this represents both the first work to theoretically study how features are learned in context, and the first analysis of nonconvex mean-field dynamics for strict saddle objectives. We hope our insights may be extended to more complex in-context learning behavior in deeper MLP-attention models.

Impact Statement

This paper aims to deepen our perception of how in-context learning ability arises in Transformer architectures, which is intimately connected to ethical issues such as AI privacy, fairness and accountability. We hope that our study will lead to a better understanding of the reasoning capabilities of large language models and facilitate the development of more transparent and socially beneficial AI systems.

Appendix A
Preliminaries

We begin by providing some necessary background for mean-field dynamics. Let
\Omega\subseteq\RR^{m}
be a Euclidean domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. For
p\geq 1
, let
\PP_{p}(\Omega)
be the
p
-Wasserstein space of probability measures on
\Omega
vanishing on
\partial\Omega
with finite
p
th moment. We will mostly be concerned with the space
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition A.1

(functional derivative)
.

The functional derivative
\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}
of a functional
F:\PP_{p}(\Omega)\to\RR
is defined (if one exists) as a functional
\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}:\PP_{p}(\Omega)\times\Omega\to\RR
satisfying for all
\nu\in\PP_{p}(\Omega)
,

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}F(\mu+\epsilon(\nu-\mu))=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)(\nu-\mu)(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta).

Note that the functional derivative is defined up to additive constants. We say a functional
F
is
C^{1}
if
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)
is well-defined and continuous, and
C^{2}
if
\nabla_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})
is well-defined and continuous. Furthermore, the functional
F
is convex if for all
\nu\in\PP_{p}(\Omega)
it holds that

F(\nu)\geq F(\mu)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)(\nu-\mu)(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta).

Definition A.2

(
p
-Wasserstein metric)
.

The
p
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu,\nu\in\PP_{p}(\Omega)
is defined as

\WW_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\RR^{m}}\lVert x-y\rVert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of joint distributions on
\Omega\times\Omega
whose first and second factors have marginal laws
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively.

We consider
\PP_{p}(\Omega)
as a metric space with respect to
\WW_{p}
, which metrizes weak convergence on
\PP_{p}(\Omega)

(
Villani 2009
, Theorem 6.9)
. By Hölder’s inequality it always holds that
\PP_{2}(\Omega)\subset\PP_{1}(\Omega)
and
\WW_{1}(\mu,\nu)\leq\WW_{2}(\mu,\nu)
. The
\WW_{1}
metric is also characterized via Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality as

\WW_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\Lip}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{\Omega}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu,

where the supremum runs over all 1-Lipschitz functions
f:\Omega\to\RR
, which makes it well-suited for perturbation analyses.

We develop more advanced theory concerning the local metric geometry and characterization of flows on
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
in Appendix
D
. As a consequence, one can show the following variational formulation of the
\WW_{2}
metric:

Proposition A.3

(Benamou-Brenier formula)
.

For
\mu,\nu\in\PP_{2}(\Omega)
it holds that

\WW_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\lVert\bm{v}_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{t};\RR^{m})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t:\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\bm{v}_{t}\mu_{t})=0,\;\mu_{0}=\mu,\,\mu_{1}=\nu\right\},

where the infimum runs over all unit time flows
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
from
\mu
to
\nu
.

Proof.

See e.g.
Ambrosio et al. 2005
, Chapter 8 or
Santambrogio 2015
, Section 6.1.
∎

The formula can be used to bound the movement of Wasserstein flows in relation to the magnitude of the gradient field. For convenience, we will use the following time-rescaled version which is easily checked:

\WW_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\inf\left\{\tau\int_{0}^{\tau}\lVert\bm{v}_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{t};\RR^{m})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t:\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\bm{v}_{t}\mu_{t})=0,\;\mu_{0}=\mu,\,\mu_{\tau}=\nu,\,\tau>0\right\}.

When the velocity field
\bm{v}_{t}=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})
is given as the functional derivative of a given functional
F
, the dynamics can be interpreted as the continuous-time limit of a discrete gradient descent process on
F
w.r.t. the
\WW_{2}
metric via the celebrated JKO scheme
(
Jordan et al. 1998
)
. Specifically, the implicit Euler scheme

\mu_{\eta}^{(k+1)}=\argmin_{\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{2}\WW_{2}(\mu,\mu^{(k)})^{2}+\eta F(\mu),\quad\mu^{(0)}=\mu_{0}

converges weakly in the limit
\eta\to 0
to the solution of the continuity or Fokker-Planck equation
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{t}\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\right)
in the sense that
\mu_{\eta}^{\lfloor t/\eta\rfloor}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
for all time
t\geq 0
. Hence we refer to this process as the Wasserstein gradient flow on
\PP_{2}(\Omega)
with respect to
F
.

B.1
Barron Class Analysis of Representations

Lemma B.1
.

For any
p>1
it holds that
\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{1}}=\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{p}}=\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{\infty}}
, where

\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{\infty}}:=\inf_{\mu:f=h_{\mu}}\esssup_{(\bm{a},\bm{w})\sim\mu}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert.

Proof.

Note
\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{1}}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{p}}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{\infty}}
trivially by Hölder’s inequality. For
f\in\Brr_{1}
, choose a measure
\mu
such that
f=h_{\mu}
and
\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{1}}+\epsilon
and define the nonnegative measure
\underline{\mu}
on
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
as

\underline{\mu}(A,B)=\int_{\underline{\bm{a}}\in A,\underline{\bm{w}}\in B}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{a},\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{w}),\quad\underline{\bm{a}}=\frac{\bm{a}}{\lVert\bm{a}\rVert},\quad\underline{\bm{w}}=\frac{\bm{w}}{\lVert\bm{w}\rVert}

for Borel sets

,
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then we can rewrite
f
via the ‘projected’ measure

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\int h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert\cdot\underline{\bm{a}}\sigma(\underline{\bm{w}}^{\top}\bm{x})\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{a},\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{w})=\int\underline{\bm{a}}\sigma(\underline{\bm{w}}^{\top}\bm{x})\underline{\mu}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{a},\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{w}).

Factoring out the total mass of
\underline{\mu}
to form a probability distribution on
\PP(\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, we obtain a representation of

such that the
\infty
-Barron norm becomes bounded as

\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{\infty}}\leq\underline{\mu}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1},\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\esssup_{(\bm{a},\bm{w})\sim\underline{\mu}}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert\lVert\bm{w}\rVert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\Brr_{1}}+\epsilon.

Taking
\epsilon\to 0
shows the reverse inequality.
∎

with the appropriate modification when

. Hence another application of Gronwall’s inequality yields

\mathbb{E}[\WW_{1}(\widehat{\mu}_{t,N},\mu_{t})]\leq C_{m}m_{2}(\mu_{0})^{1/2}N^{-1/m}\exp(L_{1}^{2}+L_{3}Te^{L_{2}T})\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. The convergence is uniform for any finite horizon
T
. ∎

Remark B.4
.

When
F=\LL
, we rely on the Lipschitz constants obtained in Lemma
E.4
to obtain the same statement, with the caveat that the flow must not reach the singular set
\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta)
in order to ensure existence and regularity of the flow; this will be a recurring issue. The result is clearly still valid for mean-field dynamics incorporating birth-death by the ordinary law of large numbers, assuming the update happens at the same instant for
\widehat{\mu}_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
. See also
Rotskoff et al. 2019
for a more involved study of birth-death dynamics.

Remark B.5
.

The above bounds are not optimized; compare for example
Berthier et al. 2023
. Explicit
uniform-in-time
propagation of chaos bounds have recently been proved for convex mean-field Langevin dynamics
(
Chen et al. 2022
;
Suzuki et al. 2023
)
and convex-concave descent-ascent dynamics
(
Kim et al. 2024
)
. It remains an open problem to prove such results for general nonconvex mean-field dynamics, with or without the entropic regularization framework.

Lemma C.1

The spectral norm of a block matrix

\mathbf{A}_{2,1}&\mathbf{A}_{2,2}\end{bmatrix}

Lemma C.2
.

The spectral and nuclear norms are dual:
\lVert\mathbf{A}\rVert_{*}=\max_{\lVert\mathbf{B}\rVert\leq 1}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle
and
\lVert\mathbf{A}\rVert=\max_{\lVert\mathbf{B}\rVert_{*}\leq 1}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle
for any
\mathbf{A}\in\RR^{m\times m}

m\geq 1
. In particular,
\tr(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\leq\lVert\mathbf{A}\rVert\lVert\mathbf{B}\rVert_{*}
for any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\RR^{m\times m}
.

Lemma C.3
.

For a positive semi-definite matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\RR^{k\times k}
it holds that
\frac{1}{k}(\tr\mathbf{A})^{2}\leq\tr\mathbf{A}^{2}\leq(\tr\mathbf{A})^{2}
.

The neural network output is continuous and well-behaved in the following sense:

Lemma C.4
.

The map
\theta\mapsto h_{\theta}(\bm{x})
on
\Theta
is
(R_{1}^{2}+R_{2}^{2}\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{2})^{1/2}
-Lipschitz for each
\bm{x}\in\XX

Proof.

For
\theta_{1}=(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{w}_{1}),\theta_{2}=(\bm{a}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})
we have

\displaystyle\lVert h_{\theta_{1}}(\bm{x})-h_{\theta_{2}}(\bm{x})\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert\bm{a}_{1}\sigma(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})-\bm{a}_{2}\sigma(\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x})\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bm{a}_{1}-\bm{a}_{2}\rVert\cdot|\sigma(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})|+\lVert\bm{a}_{2}\rVert\cdot|\sigma(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})-\sigma(\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x})|

|h_{\mu}(\bm{x})_{j}-h_{\nu}(\bm{x})_{j}|=\left\lvert\int_{\Theta}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})_{j}\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)-\int_{\Theta}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})_{j}\nu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right\rvert\leq(R_{1}^{2}+R_{2}^{2}\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{2})^{1/2}\WW_{1}(\mu,\nu)

Proof of Lemma

The gradient flow equation for

\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{D}_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}^{\top}

\mathbf{U}_{1},\mathbf{U}_{2},\mathbf{V}_{1}\in\mathcal{O}(k)

b_{2,i}>0
for all

\mathbf{Z}_{t}=\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_{t}\mathbf{U}_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{Z}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\top}(\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{D}_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}^{\top})(\mathbf{V}_{1}\mathbf{D}_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\top})(\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{Z}_{t}\mathbf{D}_{2}\mathbf{U}_{2}^{\top}-\mathbf{I}_{k})\mathbf{U}_{2}

\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{2}=(u_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

z_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)=-d_{1,i}^{2}(d_{2,j}z_{i,j}(t)-u_{i,j})

\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{i,j}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{cases}d_{2,j}^{-1}u_{i,j}&d_{1,i}\neq 0\\
z_{i,j}(0)&d_{1,i}=0\end{cases}\end{cases}=\mathbf{1}_{\{d_{1,i}\neq 0\}}d_{2,j}^{-1}u_{i,j}+\mathbf{1}_{\{d_{1,i}=0\}}z_{i,j}(0).

\lim_{t\to\infty}\!\mathbf{Z}_{t}=\mathbf{D}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{D}_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{2}\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}+(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\mathbf{D}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{D}_{1})\mathbf{Z}_{0}

Proposition C.5
.

For any
\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
,
\nu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
, there are at most
k
values
t\in[0,1]
such that
(1-t)\mu+t\nu\in\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta)
. Consequently,
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
is dense in
\PP_{2}(\Theta)
.

Note in particular that
\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
for any invertible
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
as
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ},\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}}\succeq\underline{r}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^{\top}
. This justifies the computations which appear in the statement and proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Proof.

Suppose there exist
k+1
distinct
t_{j}\in[0,1]
,
j=0,1,\cdots,k
such that
(1-t_{j})\mu+t_{j}\nu\in\PP_{2}^{0}(\Theta)
; note that
t_{j}\neq 1
since
\nu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
. Then there exist nonzero vectors
\bm{z}_{j}
such that
(1-t_{j})\bm{z}_{j}^{\top}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})+t_{j}\bm{z}_{j}^{\top}h_{\nu}(\bm{x})\equiv 0
, and which must be linearly dependent. Without loss of generality, let
\{\bm{z}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
be a minimally dependent subset of
\{\bm{z}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k}
so that
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}b_{j}\bm{z}_{j}=0
for constants
b_{j}
not all zero. Suppose
b_{0}\neq 0
. Then the equality

0\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}b_{j}\bm{z}_{j}^{\top}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})+\frac{t_{j}b_{j}}{1-t_{j}}\bm{z}_{j}^{\top}h_{\nu}(\bm{x})=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{t_{j}b_{j}}{1-t_{j}}\bm{z}_{j}^{\top}\bigg)h_{\nu}(\bm{x})

implies that

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\bigg(\frac{t_{j}}{1-t_{j}}-\frac{t_{\ell}}{1-t_{\ell}}\bigg)b_{j}\bm{z}_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{t_{j}b_{j}}{1-t_{j}}\bm{z}_{j}-\frac{t_{\ell}}{1-t_{\ell}}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}b_{j}\bm{z}_{j}=0,

which contradicts the minimality of
\{\bm{z}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
since the coefficient of
\bm{z}_{0}
is nonzero. This proves the first claim. Denseness of
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
immediately follows: for any
\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
, all but finitely many mixture distributions
(1-t)\mu+t\mu^{\circ}
lie in
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
, so there exists a subsequence weakly converging to
\mu
in
\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
.
∎

Theorem D.1

(

\lVert\bm{v}_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{t})}\leq\left\lvert\mu^{\prime}\right\rvert(t).

The field

-a.e. contained in the

This motivates the formal definition of the tangent space (
4

modulo divergence-free fields. Geometrically, this allows us to describe infinitesimal transport along curves

via their tangent vectors.

Proposition D.2

(

(11)

Proposition E.1
.

Let
F
be a functional depending on
\mu
only through the MLP layer
h_{\mu}
. Suppose MFD (
1
) at time
t
admits a distribution
\bar{\mu}\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
with
\chi^{2}(\bar{\mu},\mu_{t})\leq\bar{\chi}^{2}
such that along the linear homotopy
\bar{\mu}_{s}=(1-s)\mu_{t}+s\bar{\mu}
we have
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big|_{s=0}F(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\delta\leq 0
. Then
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}F(\mu_{t})\leq-\bar{\chi}^{-2}\delta^{2}
.

Proof.

We may express
F
as
F(\mu)=J(h_{\mu})
for an auxiliary functional
h\mapsto J(h)
defined on
C(\XX,\RR^{d})
, which implies that

\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)=\int\frac{\delta J}{\delta h}(h_{\mu},\bm{x})^{\top}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

In particular, since the dependency on the second layer
\bm{a}
is linear, it holds that
\bm{a}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}=\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}
. We can then directly lower bound the decrease rate of the objective under (
1
) by isolating the gradient provided by
\bm{a}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}F(\mu_{t})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\partial_{t}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq-\int\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq-\int\left(\bm{a}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta).

Starting from the first-order condition, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we can also bound

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\bigg|_{s=0}F(\bar{\mu}_{s})\right)^{2}=\left(\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{2}\leq\chi^{2}(\bar{\mu},\mu_{t})\int\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta).

Joining the two inequalities gives the desired bound.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

The functional derivative is computed as

\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\right],

(13)

where we have normalized the additive constant such that the integral with respect to the current measure
\mu
is zero, i.e.
\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=0
. This can be seen from

\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)\mu(d\theta)=-\int\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\right]\mu(d\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\mu}(\bm{x})\right]=0,

as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
. We remark that this differs from the usual normalization such that
\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}{\partial\mu}(\mu,\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta=0
. Due to the spherical symmetry of
\pi
in the first component, it is also immediate that

\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\pi}(\bm{x})\right]=0.

The chi-square divergence between
\bar{\mu}=\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}
and
\mu_{t}
can be bounded as

\int\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}-1\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\leq\left\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}\right\rVert_{\infty}-1\leq\gamma^{-1}\left\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\right\rVert_{\infty}

where the birth-death mechanism prevents the density ratio
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}
from falling below the threshold
\gamma
at any point. Writing the convex decomposition of
\mathbf{R}
in the sense of Lemma
C.6
as
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
with
\mathbf{R}_{j}\in\mathcal{O}(k)
, the density of
\bar{\mu}
relative to
\pi
is further bounded as

\left\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu^{\circ}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\left\lVert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\circ}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq R_{4}

by the spherical symmetry of
\pi
. Hence we may apply Proposition
E.1
with
\bar{\chi}^{2}=\gamma^{-1}R_{4}
, showing that the objective decreases along MFD by a rate of at least
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\LL(\mu_{t})\leq-R_{4}^{-1}\gamma\delta^{2}
.

Moreover, whenever the discrete linear update is performed, along the homotopy
\hat{\mu}_{s}:=(1-s\gamma)\mu_{t}+s\gamma\pi
we have

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\LL(\hat{\mu}_{s})=\gamma\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\hat{\mu}_{s},\theta)(\pi-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)=0.

Hence
t\mapsto\LL(\mu_{t})
is unaffected by the discrete updates, justifying the inequality for all time
t\geq 0
. ∎

We note that if the forcing term is applied continuously as in Remark
5.1
, almost the exact same proof for Proposition
E.1
applies by bounding

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\LL(\mu_{t})=-\int\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)+\gamma\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)(\pi-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq-\int\left(\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta).

As we mentioned briefly, the proof can also be easily modified to handle unbounded second layer
\bm{a}
by invoking the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to lower bound the gradient

\int\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\geq\left(\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{-1}\int\left(\bm{a}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

and bounding the second moment uniformly in time with the following result,

Lemma E.2
.

Denote the second moment of
\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
along the
\bm{a}
component as
m_{\bm{a}}(\mu)=\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert^{2}\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)
. Then the mean-field dynamics
\mu_{t}
for all time
t\geq 0
satisfies
m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{t})\leq m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{0})\vee m_{\bm{a}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

In fact,
m_{\bm{a}}(\cdot)
remains unchanged by gradient flow:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{t})

\displaystyle=\int\lVert\bm{a}\rVert^{2}\partial_{t}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-2\int(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-2\int\bm{a}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-2\int\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)=0.

Also if the discrete update is performed, the output satisfies
m_{\bm{a}}((1-\gamma)\mu_{t}+\gamma\pi)=(1-\gamma)m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{t})+\gamma m_{\bm{a}}(\pi)
by linearity of the moment functional
\mu\mapsto m_{\bm{a}}(\mu)
. Hence
m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{t})
always interpolates between
m_{\bm{a}}(\mu_{0})
and
m_{\bm{a}}(\pi)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Shifting the time index, suppose
\LL(\mu_{0})\leq 0.49\underline{r}
. Then
\LL(\mu_{t})\leq 0.49\underline{r}
for all
t\geq 0
and by Proposition
3.4
we are guaranteed a direction of improvement
\bar{\mu}_{s}=(1-s)\mu_{t}+s\bar{\mu}
with
\bar{\mu}=\mathbf{R}\sharp\mu
for some
\mathbf{R}\in\BB_{1}(k)
such that

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\bigg|_{s=0}\LL(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\frac{4}{R_{1}^{2}}\LL(\mu_{t})\left(\frac{\underline{r}}{2}-\LL(\mu_{t})\right).

Proposition
5.2
then ensures the objective decreases along the Wasserstein flow as

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\LL(\mu_{t})\leq-\frac{16\gamma}{R_{1}^{4}R_{4}}\LL(\mu_{t})^{2}\left(\frac{\underline{r}}{2}-\LL(\mu_{t})\right)^{2},\quad 0\leq\LL(\mu_{t})\leq\frac{\underline{r}}{2}.

We now divide the band into two halves.

1.

\frac{\underline{r}}{4}\leq\LL\leq\frac{\underline{r}}{2}
(acceleration band). By substituting
\LL(\mu_{t})^{2}\geq\frac{\underline{r}^{2}}{16}
above and solving the differential inequality, we obtain

\LL(\mu_{t})\leq\frac{\underline{r}}{2}-\left(\frac{100}{\underline{r}}-\frac{\underline{r}^{2}\gamma t}{R_{1}^{4}R_{4}}\right)^{-1}

and hence
\LL(\mu_{t})
decreases below
\frac{\underline{r}}{4}
after time
t_{1}\leq\frac{96R_{1}^{4}R_{4}}{\underline{r}^{3}\gamma}
.

2.

0\leq\LL\leq\frac{\underline{r}}{4}
(deceleration band). By substituting
(\frac{\underline{r}}{2}-\LL(\mu_{t}))^{2}\geq\frac{\underline{r}^{2}}{16}
we likewise obtain

\LL(\mu_{t})\leq\left(\frac{4}{\underline{r}}+\frac{\underline{r}^{2}\gamma(t-t_{1})}{R_{1}^{4}R_{4}}\right)^{-1}

and hence
\LL(\mu_{t})
achieves loss
\leq\epsilon
after time
t_{1}+\frac{R_{1}^{4}R_{4}}{\underline{r}^{2}\gamma}\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon}
.

Finally, note that the second term dominates the first since
\epsilon=O(\underline{r})
. ∎

Second-order Improvement

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

It is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\theta}\int\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu_{t},\theta,\theta^{\prime})(\partial_{t}\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\theta}\int\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu_{t},\theta,\theta^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu_{t}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}).

Each term is well-defined as soon as the kernel is assumed to be Hilbert-Schmidt, or due to Lemma
E.3
for the case
F=\LL
.
∎

Lemma E.3
.

The kernel
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
for the functional
\LL
is Hilbert-Schmidt for all
\mu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
. Moreover, the corresponding integral operator
\HH_{\mu}f(\theta)=\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})f(\theta^{\prime})\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})
is compact self-adjoint, hence there exists an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\ZZ}
for
L^{2}(\Theta,\mu;\RR^{k+d})
consisting of eigenfunctions of
\HH_{\mu}
.

Proof.

We extend our notation to write for example
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\theta}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\DD_{\XX}}[h_{\mu}(\bm{x})h_{\theta}(\bm{x})^{\top}]
. From (
13
) the second order functional derivative can be derived as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\!\!\LL}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta}{\delta\mu}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[(h_{\mu^{\circ}}(\bm{x})-\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\mu}(\bm{x}))^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}h_{\theta}(\bm{x})\right](\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\theta^{\prime}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu^{\circ}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta^{\prime},\mu}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\theta^{\prime}})\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu^{\circ}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta^{\prime},\mu}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\theta^{\prime}})\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta^{\prime},\mu^{\circ}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\theta^{\prime}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\tr\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta^{\prime},\mu}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\theta^{\prime}})\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\theta,\mu}\right).

It is tedious but straightforward to check that this expression is symmetric in
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
(which would otherwise follow directly if we had a priori second order regularity estimates for
\LL
). We then have that

[\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})]_{i,j}=\partial_{\theta_{i}}\partial_{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!\!\LL}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\partial_{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}\partial_{\theta_{i}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!\!\LL}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta^{\prime},\theta)=[\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta^{\prime},\theta)]_{j,i}

which implies
\HH_{\mu}
is self-adjoint as before. For the proof of the first claim, we refer to the uniform spectral bound for
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
obtained in Lemma
E.5
; this also shows that
\HH_{\mu}
is compact.
∎

In Lemma
E.4
and
E.5
, we derive various regularity bounds of the ICFL objective
\LL
. The constants
C_{1},\cdots,C_{5}
, numbered as to be consistent with Theorem
E.7
, are explicitly defined during the proofs and have at most polynomial dependency on all problem constants.

Lemma E.4
.

The gradients of the functional derivative of
\LL
at any
\mu\in\PP_{2}^{+}(\Theta)
such that
\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu})\geq\lambda
uniformly satisfy
\lVert\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}\rVert\leq C_{\bm{a}}
,
\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}\rVert\leq C_{\bm{w}}
and
\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}\rVert\leq C_{1}
. Moreover,
\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}
is
C_{2}
-Lipschitz on
\Theta
, where
C_{\bm{a}},C_{2}=O(\frac{1}{(k\lambda)^{1/2}})
and
C_{\bm{w}},C_{1}=O(\frac{1}{k\lambda})
.

Proof.

The gradient with respect to each component is given by

\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\sigma(\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x})\right]^{\top},\quad\nabla_{\bm{w}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{a}\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x})\bm{x}\right].

Hence we can bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\sigma(\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x})\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq R_{1}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-2}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{1}^{2}}{\lambda}\tr\left(\mathbf{L}_{\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{r}R_{1}^{2}}{\lambda}\LL(\mu)-\frac{2R_{1}^{2}}{\lambda}\tr\mathbf{L}_{\mu}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k\overline{r}^{2}R_{1}^{2}}{2\lambda}=:C_{\bm{a}}^{2},

and also

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{a}\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x})\bm{x}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\rVert^{2}\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{2}^{2}}{\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1/2}\rVert^{4}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{4}]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda}\left(\tr\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{r}R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda}\left(\tr\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda}\left(\tr\mathbf{L}_{\mu}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu^{\circ}}\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{r}R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda}\left(\overline{r}\tr\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\left(\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{2}\right]-2\tr\mathbf{L}_{\mu}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\left(\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{r}^{3/2}R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda}\textstyle\sup_{\bm{x}}\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\rVert(2\tr\mathbf{L}_{\mu})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k^{1/2}\overline{r}^{5/2}R_{1}^{3}R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2}}{\lambda^{2}}=:C_{\bm{w}}^{2},

where for the last line we have used the coarser bounds
\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})\rVert\leq R_{1}+R_{1}^{3}\lambda^{-1}
and
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{k}\tr\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu_{0},\mu_{0}}\leq\frac{R_{1}^{2}}{k}
. Combining the two bounds yields

\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta)\right\rVert\leq\left(\frac{\overline{r}^{2}R_{1}^{3}}{2\lambda^{2}}(R_{1}+4k^{1/2}\overline{r}^{1/2}R_{2}^{2}M_{4}^{1/2})\right)^{1/2}=:C_{1}.

Furthermore, for
\theta_{1}=(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{w}_{1})
,
\theta_{2}=(\bm{a}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta_{1})-\nabla_{\bm{a}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta_{2})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\sigma(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})-\sigma(\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x}))\right]\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x}-\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x}\rVert\right]

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}M_{2}^{1/2}\left(\frac{k\overline{r}^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\lVert\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\rVert,

and also

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta_{1})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\theta_{2})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\bm{a}_{1}\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})-\bm{a}_{2}\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x}))\bm{x}\right]\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}(\bm{a}_{1}-\bm{a}_{2})\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})\bm{x}\right]\right\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\bm{a}_{2}(\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x})-\sigma^{\prime}(\bm{w}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x}))\bm{x}\right]\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}M_{2}^{1/2}\left(\frac{k\overline{r}^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\lVert\bm{a}_{1}-\bm{a}_{2}\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+R_{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\lVert\zeta_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}(\bm{x})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu^{\circ},\mu}\bm{\Sigma}_{\mu,\mu}^{-1}\rVert\cdot\lVert\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\rVert\lVert\bm{x}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}M_{2}^{1/2}\left(\frac{k\overline{r}^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\lVert\bm{a}_{1}-\bm{a}_{2}\rVert+R_{3}M_{4}^{1/2}\left(\frac{k\overline{r}^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/2}\lVert\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\rVert.

Combining the two yields that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{a}}\nabla_{\bm{a}^{\prime}}t(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})

Proposition E.6
.

Let
F
be a functional depending on
\mu
only through the MLP layer
h_{\mu}
. Suppose MFD (
1
) at time
t
admits a distribution
\bar{\mu}\in\PP_{2}(\Theta)
with
\chi^{2}(\bar{\mu},\mu_{t})\leq\bar{\chi}^{2}
such that
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{2}}\big|_{s=0}F(\bar{\mu}_{s})\leq-\Lambda
. Then the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
\HH_{\mu_{t}}
satisfies
\lambda_{0}\leq-\bar{\chi}^{-2}\Lambda
.

Proof.

The second derivative along the linear homotopy
\bar{\mu}_{s}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{2}}\bigg|_{s=0}F(\bar{\mu}_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\bigg|_{s=0}\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu}_{s},\theta)(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\iint\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu_{t},\theta,\theta^{\prime})(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}).

Now similarly to the proof of Proposition
5.2
, denoting
\theta=(\bm{a},\bm{w}),\theta^{\prime}=(\bm{a}^{\prime},\bm{w}^{\prime})
we can exploit the fact that
\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})

\bm{a},\bm{a}^{\prime}
to relate it to the kernel
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\bm{a}^{\top}\left[\nabla_{\bm{a}}\nabla_{\bm{a}^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})\right]\bm{a}^{\prime}=(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu,\theta,\theta^{\prime})\right](\bm{a}^{\prime}\;\;0_{d})

\displaystyle=(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})(\bm{a}^{\prime}\;\;0_{d}).

Writing the eigenfunction decomposition of
\mathbf{H}_{\mu_{t}}
as (omitting the dependency on
t

\mathbf{H}_{\mu_{t}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\sum_{j\in\ZZ}\lambda_{j}\psi_{j}(\theta)\psi_{j}(\theta^{\prime})^{\top},\quad\int\lVert\psi_{j}\rVert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}=1\quad\forall j\in\ZZ,\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0,\quad\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{-1}\leq\cdots\leq 0,

\displaystyle-\Lambda\geq\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{2}}\bigg|_{s=0}F(\bar{\mu}_{s})

\displaystyle=\iint(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})(\bm{a}^{\prime}\;\;0_{d})(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq-\left\lvert\lambda_{0}\right\rvert\cdot\sum_{j\in\ZZ}\left(\int(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\psi_{j}(\theta)(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{t})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\lvert\lambda_{0}\right\rvert\cdot\sum_{j\in\ZZ}\left(\int\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}-1\right)(\bm{a}\;\;0_{d})^{\top}\psi_{j}(\theta)\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\lvert\lambda_{0}\right\rvert\int\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}-1\right)^{2}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\geq-\bar{\chi}^{2}|\lambda_{0}|,

where we have made use of Parseval’s identity. Hence the largest negative eigenvalue is bounded as
\lambda_{0}\leq-\bar{\chi}^{-2}\Lambda
.
∎

Theorem E.7
.

Assume
F:\PP_{2}(\Omega)\to\RR
,
\Omega\subseteq\RR^{m}
satisfies
\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}\rVert\leq C_{1}
,
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}
is
C_{2}
-Lipschitz,
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}
is Hilbert-Schmidt,
\lVert\mathbf{H}_{\mu}\rVert\leq C_{3}
,
\mathbf{H}_{\mu}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
is
C_{4}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
and
C_{5}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\mu
in
\WW_{1}
. Further suppose that
\lambda_{0}:=\lambda_{\textup{min}}(\HH_{\mu^{\dagger}})<0
and the corresponding eigenfunction
\psi_{0}
satisfies
|\int\psi_{0}^{\top}\nabla\frac{\delta\!\LL}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}|\geq\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
. Then WGF initialized at
\mu_{0}=\mu^{\dagger}
decreases
F
by at least
F(\mu_{\tau})\leq F(\mu_{0})-\Omega\left(\frac{|\lambda_{0}|\alpha}{\sqrt{m}\tau}\right)

\tau=O\left(\frac{1}{|\lambda_{0}|}\log\frac{|\lambda_{0}|}{\sqrt{m}\alpha}\right)
.

Unlike before,
F
can be completely general and does not need to depend on
\mu
through an MLP layer.

Proof.

First note that the function
\theta^{\prime}\mapsto\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})
is uniformly Lipschitz: for any
\theta_{1}^{\prime},\theta_{2}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta_{1}^{\prime})\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta_{1}^{\prime})-\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta_{2}^{\prime})\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta_{2}^{\prime})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta_{1}^{\prime})-\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta_{2}^{\prime})\rVert\cdot\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta_{1}^{\prime})\right\rVert+\lVert\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta_{2}^{\prime})\rVert\cdot\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta_{1}^{\prime})-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta_{2}^{\prime})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{4}\lVert\theta_{1}^{\prime}-\theta_{2}^{\prime}\rVert+C_{2}C_{3}\lVert\theta_{1}^{\prime}-\theta_{2}^{\prime}\rVert.

We re-expand the evolution equation (
7
) for the dynamics
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
around
\mu^{\dagger}
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right]

\displaystyle=-\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu_{t}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=:-\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})+e(t,\theta),

where the difference or error function
e(t,\theta)
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\lVert e(t,\theta)\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\int(\mathbf{H}_{\mu_{t}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})-\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime}))\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\int\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})(\mu_{t}-\mu^{\dagger})(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{1}C_{5}+(C_{1}C_{4}+C_{2}C_{3})m^{1/2}\right)\WW_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu^{\dagger}).

\displaystyle=:C_{6}\WW_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu^{\dagger}).

For the second term, we have used the Lipschitz constant derived above to bound each entry separately. Then the
\psi_{0}
-component
\alpha_{0}(t):=\int\psi_{0}^{\top}\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\dagger}
of the gradient evolves according to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\alpha_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=-\iint\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{\mu^{\dagger}}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime})\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)+\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}e(t,\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{0}\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)+\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}e(t,\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta),

and hence

\left\lvert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\alpha_{0}(t)+\lambda_{0}\alpha_{0}(t)\right\rvert\leq\left(\int\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\,\lVert e(t,\theta)\rVert\leq C_{8}\WW_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu^{\dagger}).

Without loss of generality, assume initially
\alpha_{0}(0)
is positive so that
\alpha_{0}(0)\geq\alpha
. We consider a 1-Wasserstein ball centered at
\mu^{\dagger}
with radius small enough so that the error term is negligible compared to the exponential growth,

\BB_{\WW}(\Delta)=\left\{\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Theta):\WW_{1}(\mu,\mu^{\dagger})\leq\Delta:=\frac{|\lambda_{0}|\alpha}{2C_{6}}\right\}.

Then for a set time interval
\tau>0
to be determined, either of the following must happen:

1.

(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau]}\subset\BB_{\WW}(\Delta)
. In this case,
\alpha_{0}(t)
grows exponentially during the entire interval
t\in[0,\tau]
as

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\alpha_{0}(t)\geq|\lambda_{0}|\alpha_{0}(t)-C_{6}\Delta=|\lambda_{0}|\left(\alpha_{0}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)>0,

showing that

\alpha_{0}(t)\geq e^{|\lambda_{0}|t}\left(\alpha_{0}(0)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\geq\frac{\alpha(e^{|\lambda_{0}|t}+1)}{2}.

Then the decrease of
F
after time
\tau
can be bounded below by retrieving the
\psi_{0}
-component as

\displaystyle F(\mu_{0})-F(\mu_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\int\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-2C_{1}C_{2}\WW_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu^{\dagger})\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-2C_{1}C_{2}\Delta\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\alpha_{0}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-2C_{1}C_{2}\Delta\tau

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{2|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\frac{2}{|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\tau\right)-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{C_{6}}|\lambda_{0}|\alpha\tau.

2.

\mu_{\tau_{e}}\notin\BB_{\WW}(\Delta)
for some
\tau_{e}\leq\tau
. If the mean-field flow has managed to escape the ball
\BB_{\WW}(\Delta)
in time
\tau_{e}
, the Benamou-Brenier formula (Proposition
A.3
) immediately guarantees that

F(\mu_{0})-F(\mu_{\tau})\geq F(\mu_{0})-F(\mu_{\tau_{e}})=\int_{0}^{\tau_{e}}\int\left\lVert\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right\rVert^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{\WW_{2}(\mu_{\tau_{e}},\mu^{\dagger})^{2}}{\tau_{e}}>\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\tau}.

Thus we have proved that:

F(\mu_{0})-F(\mu_{\tau})\geq\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{2|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\frac{2}{|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\tau\right)-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{C_{6}}|\lambda_{0}|\alpha\tau\right)\wedge\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4C_{6}^{2}\tau}.

(14)

Due to the exponential terms, we see
\tau\asymp\log\frac{1}{\alpha}
is enough to ensure that the two terms become roughly equal so that the guarantee is close to optimal. For the remainder of the proof, we derive the exact formula. Choose

\tau=\frac{1}{|\lambda_{0}|}\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}

for some
C_{7}>\alpha
. The first term in the right-hand side of (
14
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{2|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\frac{2}{|\lambda_{0}|}(e^{|\lambda_{0}|\tau}-1)+\tau\right)-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{C_{6}}|\lambda_{0}|\alpha\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8|\lambda_{0}|}\left(\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2|\lambda_{0}|}\left(\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}-1\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4|\lambda_{0}|}\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{C_{6}}\alpha\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}-\frac{5\alpha^{2}}{8|\lambda_{0}|}\right)+\left(\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{C_{6}}\alpha\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}\right)+\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}+\frac{C_{7}\alpha}{2|\lambda_{0}|}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4|\lambda_{0}|}\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}-\frac{5\alpha^{2}}{8|\lambda_{0}|}\right)+\left(\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}-\frac{C_{1}C_{2}C_{7}}{C_{6}e}\right)+\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|},

where we have used the fact that the function
x\mapsto x\log\frac{c}{x}
has maximum
\frac{c}{e}
. Then the first term of (

\frac{C_{7}^{2}}{24|\lambda_{0}|}\geq\frac{5\alpha^{2}}{8|\lambda_{0}|}\vee\frac{C_{1}C_{2}C_{7}}{C_{6}e}\vee\frac{|\lambda_{0}|^{3}\alpha^{2}}{4C_{6}^{2}\log\frac{C_{7}}{\alpha}}.

Manipulating terms shows that

C_{7}=\sqrt{15}\alpha\vee\frac{24C_{1}C_{2}|\lambda_{0}|}{C_{6}e}\vee\sqrt{\frac{12}{\log 15}}\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}\alpha}{C_{6}}

(15)

is sufficient. For the purposes of the general statement, we focus on asymptotic behavior w.r.t.
\alpha
and hide all regularity constants
C_{1},\cdots,C_{5}
, yielding

E.3
Escaping from Saddle Points

The usual theory of Gaussian processes can be readily extended to multivariable outputs.

Definition E.8

(vector-valued Gaussian process)
.

The random function
\bm{\xi}:\Omega\to\RR^{m}
is said to follow a Gaussian process if any finite collection of variables
\bm{\xi}(\theta_{1}),\cdots,\bm{\xi}(\theta_{j})
are jointly normally distributed. The process is determined by the mean function
\bm{m}:\Omega\to\RR^{m}
,
\bm{m}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\bm{\xi}(\theta)]
and matrix-valued covariance function

\mathbf{K}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\RR^{m\times m},\quad\mathbf{K}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[(\bm{\xi}(\theta)-\bm{m}(\theta))(\bm{\xi}(\theta^{\prime})-\bm{m}(\theta^{\prime}))^{\top}].

We denote this process as
\bm{\xi}\sim\GP(\bm{m},\mathbf{K})
. See
Álvarez et al. 2012
for further details.

Lemma E.9
.

For any
\mu\in\PP_{2}(\Omega)
, square-integrable test function
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})
and covariance function
\mathbf{K}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\RR^{m\times m}
satisfying
\int\lVert\mathbf{K}(\theta,\theta)\rVert\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
the inner product
\langle\psi,\bm{\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})}
for
\bm{\xi}\sim\GP(0,\mathbf{K})
is normally distributed.

Proof.

Note that the inner product is defined almost surely since

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})}^{2}\right]=\int\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\lVert\bm{\xi}(\theta)\rVert^{2}]\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int\tr\mathbf{K}(\theta,\theta)\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty.

We denote by
\mathscr{E}
the closed linear span of the set of square-integrable random variables
\{\psi(\theta)^{\top}\bm{\xi}(\theta):\theta\in\Omega\}
. For any
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{E}^{\perp}
it holds that
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\mathbf{Z}\psi(\theta)^{\top}\bm{\xi}(\theta)]=0
, so that by Fubini’s theorem

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\mathbf{Z}\langle\psi,\bm{\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\int\mathbf{Z}\psi(\theta)^{\top}\bm{\xi}(\theta)\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)\right]=0.

Hence
\langle\psi,\bm{\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mu;\RR^{m})}\in(\mathscr{E}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathscr{E}
, and so is normally distributed.
∎

For the proposed perturbation process, the change in the gradient field along the flow of
\bm{\xi}
can be quantified as

\displaystyle\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{\Delta t},\theta)-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu^{\dagger},\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\partial_{t}\left[\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{t},\theta)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\int\nabla_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{2}\!F}{\delta\mu^{2}}(\mu_{t},\theta,\theta^{\prime})\bm{\xi}(\theta^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\HH_{\mu_{t}}\left[\bm{\xi}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

The resulting
\psi_{0}
-component is

\displaystyle\alpha(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{\Delta t},\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\bigg(\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}(\mu^{\dagger},\theta)-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\HH_{\mu^{\dagger}}\left[\bm{\xi}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\Delta t}(\HH_{\mu^{\dagger}}-\HH_{\mu_{t}})\left[\bm{\xi}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{0}\Delta t\int\psi_{0}(\theta)^{\top}\bm{\xi}(\theta)\mu^{\dagger}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta)+\alpha+O(\Delta t^{2}),

Generalized transport inequalities and concentration bounds for Riesz-type gases
David García-Zelada, David Padilla-Garza

Abstract
This paper explores the connection between a generalized Riesz electric energy and norms on the set of probability measures defined in terms of duality. We derive functional inequalities linking these two notions, recovering and generalizing existing Coulomb transport inequalities. We then use them to prove concentration of measure around the equilibrium and thermal equilibrium measures. Finally, we leverage these concentration inequalities to obtain Moser-Trudinger-type inequalities, which may also be interpreted as bounds on the Laplace transform of fluctuations.



1
Introduction and motivation

We will study a many-particle system, in which the particles interact via a repulsive kernel, and are confined by an external potential. This is modelled by the Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})=\sum_{i\neq j}g(x_{i}-x_{j})+N\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i}),

(1)

where
g:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
is the repulsive kernel,
V:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
is the confining potential,
N
is the number of particles, and
X_{N}=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}
. We are interested in the behaviour for large but finite
N
. A system modeled by equation (
1
) will be called an interacting gas, or many-particle system.

At zero temperature, the particles will arrange themselves into the configuration that minimizes the Hamiltonian. However, we may also study many-particle systems at positive temperature. In this case the position of the particles is a random variable in
\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}
. The density of the random variable is given by the Gibbs measure,

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}(X_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(-\beta\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})\right)\,\mathrm{d}X_{N},

(2)

where

Z_{N,\beta}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}}\exp\left(-\beta\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})\right)\,\mathrm{d}X_{N}

(3)

is the partition function, and
\beta>0
is the inverse temperature which may depend on
N
.

The most frequent form of the repulsive interaction
g
is given by

\begin{cases}g(x)=\frac{c_{d}}{|x|^{d-2}}\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
g(x)=-c_{2}\log(|x|)\text{ if }d=2,\end{cases}

(4)

where
c_{d}
is such that

-\Delta g=\delta_{0}

(5)

for all
d\geq 2
. We will refer to this setting as the Coulomb case. Another frequent form of
g
is given by

g(x)=\frac{c_{d,s}}{|x|^{d-2s}},

(6)

with
0<s<\min\{\frac{d}{2},1\}
,
d\geq 1
, and
c_{d,s}
such that

(-\Delta)^{s}g=\delta_{0}.

(7)

We will refer to this setting as the Riesz case. This paper will deal with a generalization of Riesz interactions, which are very similar to ones first introduced in
[
31
,
35
]
.

Apart from the Riesz case, it is also possible to study a many-particle system with interactions given by a hypersingular (non-integrable) interaction, i.e.,
g
given by equation (
6
) with
s<0
, see for example
[
21
,
20
,
19
]
.

Coulomb and Riesz gases are a classical field with applications in spherical packing
[
43
,
13
,
14
,
45
,
34
]
, statistical mechanics
[
1
,
23
,
39
,
18
,
42
]
, random matrix theory
[
24
,
8
,
9
,
10
,

,

,

]
,and mathematical physics
[

]
, among other fields.

The study of a general interacting gas has recently begun to attract attention

2
Main definitions

In this section, we introduce the necessary objects and notation in order to state the main results of this paper.

2.1
Interacting gases

We start with notions related to interacting gases.

The most fundamental observable is the empirical measure.

Definition 2.1
.

The empirical measure
{\rm emp}_{N}
is defined as

{\rm emp}_{N}(X_{N})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}.

(8)

In order to ease notation, we will often write
{\rm emp}_{N}
instead of
{\rm emp}_{N}(X_{N})
.

We proceed to define quantities related to the electric energy and entropy.

Definition 2.2
.

We denote the electric self-interaction of a measure
\mu
by
\mathcal{E}(\mu)
:

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}g(x-y)\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\mu(x,y).

(9)

We denote the mean field limit of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
by
\mathcal{E}_{V}
, acting on a measure
\mu
by

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}V\mathrm{d}\mu.

(10)

We also introduce the free energy
\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}
, acting on a measure
\mu
by

\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)+\frac{1}{\theta}{\rm ent}[\mu],

(11)

with

{\rm ent}[\mu]=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\log({\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}})\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}\quad\mbox{ if }\mu\ll\mathcal{L},\ \log({\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}})\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\\
\infty\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(12)

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

We define
\mathcal{E}^{\neq}
for a measure
\mu
as

\mathcal{E}^{\neq}(\mu)=\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\Delta}g(x-y)\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\mu(x,y),

(13)

where

\Delta=\{(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\}.

(14)

We also define

{\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu)=\mathcal{E}^{\neq}(\mu-{\rm emp}_{N}(X_{N})).

(15)

The reason for excluding the diagonal in the integral is so that these quantities can be finite on atomic measures.
Lastly, given a measure
\mu
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we define

h^{\mu}=\mu\ast g.

(16)

We proceed to defining the equilibrium and thermal equilibrium measures, which will play a central role in the rest of the paper.

Notation 2.3
.

Given a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
the set of probability measures on
\Omega
.

Definition 2.4
.

We denote by
\mu_{\infty}
the minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{V}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
:

\mu_{\infty}:=\underset{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu).

(17)

We will refer to
\mu_{\infty}
as the equilibrium measure.

We denote by
\Sigma
the support of
\mu_{\infty}
.

We denote by
\mu_{\theta}
the minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
:

\mu_{\theta}:=\underset{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}(\mu).

(18)

We will refer to
\mu_{\theta}
as the thermal equilibrium measure.

We refer to Section
6
for existence, uniqueness and basic properties of
\mu_{\infty}
and
\mu_{\theta}
.

Remark 1
.

As long as the temperature is not too big (
\frac{1}{N}\ll\beta
), the equilibrium measure provides a good approximation to the empirical measure. A better approximation, however, is provided by
\mu_{N\beta}
, the thermal equilibrium measure with parameter
N\beta
. This difference becomes bigger as the temperature becomes bigger. In the endpoint case
\beta=\frac{1}{N}
,
\mu_{N\beta}
does not converge to
\mu_{\infty}
as
N
tends to
\infty
. In this case,
\mu_{N\beta}
still provides a good approximation to
{\rm emp}_{N}
, but
\mu_{\infty}
does not.

Remark 2
.

For the rest of the paper, we commit the abuse of notation of not distinguishing between a measure and its density.

As mentioned before, this paper deals with general interactions that qualitatively behave like Riesz interactions. We now specify this class of interactions.

Notation 2.5
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{F}(f)
, or alternatively by
\widehat{f}
the Fourier transform of
f.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
s\in(0,\min\{1,\frac{d}{2}\})
. A function
g:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
is called a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
if there exists an integer
m\geq 0
, a function
G:\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\to\mathbf{R}
and constants
C_{1},C_{2},C\geq 0
depending only on
d,s,m,
and
G
such that

1.

G(x,0)=g(x)
for every
(x,0)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+m}
.

2.

G(X)=G(-X)
for every
X\in\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\setminus\{0\}
.

3.

\lim_{X\to 0}G(X)=\infty
.

4.

There exists
r_{0}>0\text{ such that }\Delta G\leq 0\text{ in }B(0,r_{0})\subset\mathbf{R}^{d+m}
.

5.

\ \left|G(X)\right|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|X|^{d-2s}}\right)
for every
X\in\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\setminus\{0\}
.

6.

\ \left|\nabla G(X)\right|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|X|^{d-2s+1}}\right)
for every
X\in\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\setminus\{0\}
.

7.

\frac{C_{1}}{\left|\Xi\right|^{m+2s}}\leq\widehat{G}(\Xi)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\left|\Xi\right|^{m+2s}}
.

8.

\frac{C_{1}}{\left|\xi\right|^{2s}}\leq\widehat{g}(\xi)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\left|\xi\right|^{2s}}
.

9.

There exist
c_{s}<1
and
r_{0}>0

such that, for every
X,Y\in B(0,r_{0})\setminus\{0\}\text{ with }|Y|\geq 2|X|
,
the inequality

G(Y)<c_{s}G(X)
is satisfied.

10.

The function
h:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
, defined by
h=\mathfrak{F}\left({\frac{1}{\widehat{g}}}\right)
, satisfies
\frac{C_{1}}{\left|x\right|^{d+2s}}\leq h(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\left|x\right|^{d+2s}}
.

Remark 3
.

Items 1-9 are basically found in
[
31
,
35
]
, but unlike
[
31
,
35
]
, we only impose a growth condition on
G
and its first derivative. Item 10 is not found in
[
31
,
35
]
, but it is necessary to derive a monotonicity property, see Section
7
.

Remark 4
.

The most important example of a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
is, of course, the Riesz kernel (
6
) of order
s
. In this case, the function
G(X)
is given by
G(X)=\frac{c_{d,s}}{|X|^{d-2s}}
, which will satisfy Definition
2.6
for
m
big enough.

Apart from a general interaction, we will deal with a potential which is general except for growth and regularity conditions. We now specify the exact class of potentials that we will deal with.

Definition 2.7
.

We call a potential
V:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}

admissible
if

1.

V\in C^{2}
.

2.

\lim_{x\to\infty}V(x)=\infty
.

3.

\forall\beta>0
,

\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\exp(-\beta V(x))\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(19)

4.

\mu_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}
.

5.

If
d=2
and
g
is the Coulomb kernel, then

\lim_{x\to\infty}(V(x)-\log|x|)=\infty
.

2.2
Functional analysis

In this subsection, we define some norms and spaces used in the main results.

2.2.1
Hölder norms

We start by defining the Hölder norm and seminorm
as well as their duals.

Definition 2.8
.

For a continuous function
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
, we define the Hölder
\alpha
seminorm as

|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,x\neq y}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

(20)

We denote by
\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
the space

\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)=\{f\in C(\Omega):\,|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}<\infty\}.

(21)

For a continuous function
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
, we define the full Hölder
\alpha
norm as

\|f\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}}=|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}+\|f\|_{\infty},

(22)

where
\|f\|_{\infty}
is defined for a continuous function
f
as the supremum of
|f|
.

We now define the corresponding dual norms. For a measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, we define the dual Hölder
\alpha
norm as

\|\mu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}=\sup_{f\in\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}}.

(23)

For a measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, we define the dual full Hölder
\alpha
norm as

\|\mu\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}=\sup_{f\in{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\|f\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}}}.

(24)

We also define the
\dot{C}^{1,\alpha}
seminorm of
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
as

|f|_{\dot{C}^{1,\alpha}}=|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}+\max_{i}|\partial_{i}f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}.

(25)

Remark 5
.

Note that when
\alpha=1
, the dual Hölder
\alpha
norm is the Wasserstein
1-
distance, and the dual full Hölder
\alpha
norm is the bounded-Lipschitz norm.

Remark 6
.

Note that if
\mu(\Omega)\neq 0
, then
\|\mu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}=\infty
.

Remark 7
.

Whenever
\Omega
is bounded, the norms

\|\cdot\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}
and

\|\cdot\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}

are equivalent
on the set of measures of total mass zero.

2.2.2
On
H^{s}
norms

In this subsubsection, we deal with the
L^{2}
-based norm
H^{s}
.

We begin with the definition of this norm.

Definition 2.9
.

Given a tempered distribution
f
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

and
s\in(0,1)
, we define the (Fourier)
\dot{H}^{s}
seminorm of
f
as

|f|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2s}\mathrm{d}\xi.

(26)

We also define the (difference quotient)
\dot{H}^{s}
seminorm, for
s\in(0,1)
, as

|f|_{\dot{H}_{dq}^{s}}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

(27)

These two quantities are equivalent, and this is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

The norms
|\cdot|_{\dot{H}_{dq}^{s}}
and
|\cdot|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}
are equivalent.

Proof.

See
[
4
]
, chapter 1.
∎

Motivated by Lemma
2.10
, we will write
|\cdot|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
to denote either
|\cdot|_{\dot{H}_{dq}^{s}}
or
|\cdot|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}
, unless a distinction between them is necessary.

3
Main results

In this section, we state the main results of the paper, using the language introduced in the last section.

We start with a proposition about elementary properties of the thermal equilibrium measure.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
V
is admissible and let
\theta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{+}.

There exists a constant
C_{\theta_{0}}>0
such that,
for every
\theta>\theta_{0},

\mu_{\theta}(x)\leq C_{\theta_{0}}\mbox{ for every }x\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(28)

and outside of a bounded set
\Omega

(which depends only on
V
and
\theta_{0}
),

\mu_{\theta}(x)\leq\exp\left(C_{\theta_{0}}-\theta V(x)\right).

(29)

The proof is found in Section
7
.

We proceed to stating generalized transport inequalities, which are extensions of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 of
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, let
V
be an admissible potential, and let
\alpha>s
.

1.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a compact set, then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\Omega}
, which depends only on
g,\alpha
and
\Omega
such that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha,\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\mu-\nu).

(30)

2.

There exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\|\mu-\mu_{\infty}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{\infty})\right).

(31)

3.

Assume that

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{|x|^{2\alpha}}>0.

(32)

Then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),
we have

\|\mu-\mu_{\infty}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{\infty})\right).

(33)

The proof is found in Section
8
.

We then apply these results to obtain a quantitative understanding of the convergence of the thermal equilibrium measure to the equilibrium measure.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
V
be an admissible potential, let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, and let
\alpha>s
. Let
\theta_{n}
be a strictly positive sequence tending to infinity, and let

\underline{\theta}:=\min_{n}\theta_{n}.

(34)

Then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that

\|\mu_{\infty}-\mu_{\theta_{n}}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\theta_{n}}\left|{\rm ent}[\mu_{\infty}]-{\rm ent}[\mu_{\underline{\theta}}]\right|.

(35)

Additionally, if

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{|x|^{2\alpha}}>0

(36)

then there exits a constant
C_{\alpha}
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that

\|\mu_{\infty}-\mu_{\theta_{n}}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\theta_{n}}\left|{\rm ent}[\mu_{\infty}]-{\rm ent}[\mu_{\underline{\theta}}]\right|.

(37)

The proof is found in Section
8
.

We then use the previous results to prove concentration of measure around the equilibrium measure and thermal equilibrium measure. The next theorem is a generalization of
[
12
]
and
[
32
]
to general interactions.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume
V
is an admissible potential, let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, and let
\alpha>s
. Assume that
N\beta\to\infty
and let
r>0
. Then

1.

There exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that

\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\infty}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}>r\right)\leq\\
&\exp\Big(-N^{2}\beta\left(C[r-N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}]_{+}^{2}-C\|\mu_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}N^{-\frac{2s}{d}}\right)+\\
&\quad N\left(-\log\left|\Sigma\right|+\rm{ent}[\mu_{\infty}]+\beta\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\infty})\right)+o(N)\Big),\end{split}

(38)

where
(\cdot)_{+}
denotes the positive-part function.

2.

Set
\theta=N\beta
, then there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V,g
, and
\alpha
such that

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}>r\right)\leq\exp\left(-N^{2}\beta\left(C\left[r-CN^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\right]_{+}^{2}-CN^{-\frac{2s}{d}}\right)\right).

(39)

The proof is found in Section
9
.

The previous theorem implies, with high probability, an upper bound for the distances between the empirical measure and equilibrium and thermal equilibrium measures. We complement this with a lower bound for such distance.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mu_{N}
be a sequence of probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\sup_{N}\|\mu_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(40)

Let
\nu_{N}
be a sequence of probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\nu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}.

(41)

Then

\|\mu_{N}-\nu_{N}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\geq CN^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}},

(42)

where
C
depends on
d
and
\sup_{N}\|\mu_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

The proof is found in Section
10
.

Our final result is a series of Moser-Trudinger like inequalities, which may also be interpreted as bounds on the Laplace transform of fluctuations. This result is new also in the Coulomb case.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
V
be an admissible potential, and assume
N\beta\to\infty
. Let
f:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
be continuous, and define the random variable
{\rm Fluct}[f]
by

{\rm Fluct}[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}fd\left({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\right).

(43)

1.

If
g
is the Coulomb kernel, then there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V
such that

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq N^{2}\beta\left(Ct^{2}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{2}{d}}C\right).

(44)

2.

If
g
is a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
then for any
\alpha>s
there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V
,
g
, and
\alpha
such that

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq N^{2}\beta\left(Ct^{2}\|f\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{2\alpha}{d}}C\right).

(45)

3.

If
g
is the Coulomb kernel,
i\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
then there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V
such that

\begin{split}&\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq\\
&\beta N^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}+CN^{-\frac{2}{d}}\right)+\frac{d}{2}\left(\log(N^{2}\beta)+\log(1+t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}})\right).\end{split}

(46)

4.

If
g
is a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
,
i\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
V
and
g
such that

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq\beta N^{2}\left(Ct^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}+CN^{-\frac{2s}{d}}\right).

(47)

The proof is found in Section
11
.

4
Further work

1.

We expect that an analogue of the main results in this paper holds for compact manifolds. In this case, the Riesz kernel would be defined as the fundamental solution of the fractional Laplacian, and may be written in terms of the eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator.

2.

Theorems
3.4
and
3.2
hold for
\alpha>s
. The reason is in Remark
8
. Do Theorems
3.4
and
3.2
hold also in the endpoint case
\alpha=s
? We emphasise that this s true in the case
s=1
.

3.

Do transport inequalities hold for the thermal equilibrium measure as well? That is, is it true (in the Coulomb case) that

\begin{split}\|\mu-\mu_{\beta}\|_{\rm BL}&\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}^{V}_{\beta}(\mu_{\beta})\right),\\
W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{\beta})&\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}^{V}_{\beta}(\mu_{\beta})\right),\end{split}

(48)

for some constant
C
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\rm BL}
and
W_{1}
denote the bounded-Lipschitz norm and Wasserstein
1-
distance, respectively? These inequalities would prove a more intimate link between optimal transport and Coulomb gases than the ones available in the literature.

4.

Theorem
3.4
implies, with high probability, an upper bound for the bounded-Lipschitz distance between the empirical measure and thermal equilibrium measure. However, unlike the Coulomb case, this inequality is not geometrically optimal, i.e. it is not of order
N^{-\frac{1}{d}}
. Is it true that

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}(\limsup N^{\frac{1}{d}}\|{\rm emp}-\mu_{\beta}\|_{\rm BL}=\infty)=0?

(49)

In the case of Coulomb interactions, it is shown in
[
32
]
that equation (
49
) holds, and that it cannot hold for any exponent higher than
\frac{1}{d}
.

5.

We expect that generalized transport inequalities and concentration inequalities are also valid in the high temperature regime (
\beta=\frac{1}{N}
); and very high temperature regime (
\beta\ll\frac{1}{N}
). In the case of the very high temperature regime, it would be necessary to consider either a compact manifold, or a Hamiltonian in which the confining potential term has weight big enough to be comparable to the effect of the entropy.

6.

We expect that generalized transport inequalities (Theorem
3.2
) and concentration inequalities (Theorem
3.4
) also hold for subCoulomb interactions, i.e. interactions of the form
g(x)=|x|^{d-2s}
for
d\geq 3
and
s\in\left(1,\frac{d}{2}\right)
. In this case, the inequalities would be for the
C^{k,\alpha}_{*}
or
\dot{C}^{k,\alpha}_{*}
norms, for
k\in\left(0,\frac{d}{2}\right)\cap\mathbf{N}
.

The thermal equilibrium measure was introduced in this context in
[

[

[

Continues the ideas of

The type of interactions that we treat in this paper are, roughly speaking, interactions that qualitatively behave like Riesz potentials in both real space and Fourier space. Very similar kernels were introduced in
[

and
[

X_{N}\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}
satisfies the ODE

\begin{split}\dot{x}_{i}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\neq j}\nabla g(x_{i}-x_{j})\\
x_{i}(0)&=x_{i}^{0},\end{split}

(50)

then in the limit as
N
tends to infinity,
{\rm emp}_{N}(X_{N}(0))
converges to a measure
\mu_{0}
, and under additional conditions, then
{\rm emp}_{N}(X_{N}(t))
converges to a solution of the PDE

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mu&=-{\rm div}((\nabla g\ast\mu)\mu)\\
\mu(0)&=\mu_{0}.\end{split}

(51)

The exact result is more general, since it covers possible stochastic noise, among other things, see
[
31
,
35
]
for further details.

The Moser-Trudinger-type inequalities of Theorem
3.6
(which may also be interpreted as bounds on the Laplace transform of the fluctuations) may be compared to Theorem 1 of
[
41
]
, and Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 of
[
7

. Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 of
[

apply only to two-dimensional Coulomb gases, but they are sharp in that case. On the other hand the inequalities of Theorem

hold in arbitrary dimension and temperature regime, but we do not believe them to be sharp. Theorem 1 of
[
41
]
also holds in arbitrary dimension and temperature regime. It contains a bound in terms of the maximum absolute value of
f
and its higher order derivatives (or
\xi
in the notation of
[
41
]
). This bound requires higher regularity (namely,
C^{3}
regularity), and also that
Ct\max\{|f|_{C^{1}},|f|_{C^{2}}\}<1
for a given constant
C
, but is more accurate than Theorem
3.6
for small values of

. Unlike the bounds in both
[
41
]
and
[
7
]

3.6
holds for Riesz kernels and more general interactions.

[
38

obtain concentration inequalities for Coulomb gases as a consequence of an analysis of the renormalized energy.
[
29

obtains bounds on the Laplace transform of fluctuations for Coulomb gases in 2d, which imply concentration inequalities.
[

obtains bounds on the Laplace transform of fluctuations and also concentration bounds for log gases in 1d.

[

[

6.1
Splitting formulas and partition functions

We start by recalling some well known facts about the equilibrium and thermal equilibrium measures.

First, we recall existence and uniqueness.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
V
is admissible and
g
is either the Coulomb kernel or a Riesz-type kernel. Then the functional
\mathcal{E}_{V}
has a unique minimizer in the set of probability measures, which has compact support
\Sigma
and satisfies the First Order Condition

\begin{split}h^{\mu_{\infty}}+V-c_{\infty}&\geq 0\text{ in }\mathbf{R}^{d}\\
h^{\mu_{\infty}}+V-c_{\infty}&=0\text{ in }\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\infty}),\end{split}

(52)

for some constant
c_{\infty}\in\mathbf{R}.

Proof.

See
[
15
,
40
]
for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

The functional
\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}
has a unique minimizer in the set of probability measures, which is everywhere positive, bounded from above (with a bound that may depend on
\theta
), satisfies the First Order Condition

h^{\mu_{\theta}}+V+\frac{1}{\theta}\log(\mu_{\theta})=c_{\theta},

(53)

for some constant
c_{\theta}
, and also that

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}h^{\mu_{\theta}}(x)=0.

(54)

Proof.

See
[
2
]
for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.
∎

Next, we derive expansions of the Hamiltonian around the equilibrium and thermal equilibrium measures. These expansions are splitting formulas.

Proposition 6.3

(Splitting formula)
.

For every
X_{N}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}

such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
whenever
i\neq j
, we have that the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{N}
can be split into

\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})=N^{2}\left(\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{\infty})+{\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\infty})+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}\,{\rm emp}_{N}\right),

(55)

where

\zeta_{\infty}=V+c_{\infty}-2h^{\mu_{\infty}}.

(56)

Proof.

See
[
40
]
for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.
∎

Proposition 6.4

(Thermal splitting formula)
.

We introduce the notation

\zeta_{\theta}=-\frac{1}{\theta}\log(\mu_{\theta}).

(57)

For every
X_{N}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}

such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
whenever
i\neq j
, we have that the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{N}
can be split into

\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})=N^{2}\left(\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}(\mu_{\theta})+{\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\,{\rm emp}_{N}\right),

(58)

with

\theta=N\beta.

(59)

Proof.

See
[
3
]
for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.
∎

Motivated by splitting formulas, we can identify the leading order term
of the partition function, and also prove elementary results about the next order components.

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume that
V
is admissible, finite on a set of positive Lebesgue measure, and that
\rm{ent}[\mu_{\infty}]<\infty
. Then for any
N\geq 2
,

Z_{\beta}^{N}\geq\exp\left(-N^{2}\beta\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{\infty})+N\left(\beta\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\infty})+\rm{ent}[\mu_{\infty}]\right)\right).

(60)

Proof.

See
[
12
]
, Lemma 4.1 for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.
∎

Definition 6.6
.

Set
\theta=N\beta
. We define the next order partition function
K_{N,\beta}
as

K_{N,\beta}=\frac{Z_{N,\beta}}{\exp\left(N^{2}\mathcal{E}_{V}^{\theta}(\mu_{\theta})\right)}.

(61)

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
V
be an admissible potential, and let
g
be either a Riesz-type kernel or the Coulomb kernel. Then the next order partition function is greater that
1
, i.e.,

\log(K_{N,\beta})>0.

(62)

Proof.

See
[
32
,
3
]
for the Coulomb case, which extends to the Riesz-type case.

6.2
More on
H^{s}
norms

In this subsection, we introduce additional properties of the
H^{s}
norm.
We start by turning the
\dot{H}^{s}
semi-norm into a full norm.

Definition 6.8
.

We define the
H^{s}
norm of a function
f:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
by

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|f|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

(63)

We now extend the definition of
H^{s}
norms to negative indices.

Definition 6.9
.

Given a Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbf{R}^{d},
and
s>0
we define the
\dot{H}^{-s}
norm of
\mu
by

\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-2s}d\xi.

(64)

Now we introduce an analogue of the
H^{s}
norms for functions that are not defined on the whole space.

Definition 6.10
.

Given a domain
\Omega,
and a measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
we define the
\dot{H}^{s}
semi-norm restricted to
\Omega
by

|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

(65)

and the
{H}^{s}(\Omega)
norm by

\|f\|_{{H}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(66)

An alternative way to define an
H^{s}
space on a bounded set is to take restrictions of
H^{s}
functions on the whole space. These two approaches are equivalent, and this is the focus of the next lemma.

Lemma 6.11
.

Given a domain
\Omega
with a smooth boundary and
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R},
the following are equivalent:

1.

|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)}<\infty

(67)

2.

There exists
\overline{f}:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
such that
\overline{f}|_{\Omega}=f
and

|\overline{f}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\infty.

(68)

Proof.

See
[
44
]
.
∎

Next, we introduce the extension operator for
H^{s}
functions.

Lemma 6.12

(Extensions)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a compact domain with a smooth boundary. There exists a linear operator from
H^{s}(\Omega)
to
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, which takes a function
f\in H^{s}(\Omega)
to a function
\overline{f}\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
that satisfies

1.

\overline{f}|_{\Omega}=f\mbox{ and}

2.

\|\overline{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends on
\Omega
but does not depend on
f
.

Proof.

See
[
44
]
.
∎

We also introduce the
H^{-s}(\Omega)
norm, analogous to the
H^{-1}(\Omega)
norm introduced in
[
32
]
.

Definition 6.13
.

Given a bounded open set
\Omega,
a Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, we define the
H^{-s}(\Omega)
norm as

\|\mu\|_{H^{-s}(\Omega)}=\sup\frac{\int_{\Omega}\phi d\mu}{\|\phi\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}}.

(69)

A key point in this paper is that the
H^{s}
and
C^{0,\alpha}
norms can be easily compared in bounded sets.

Remark 8
.

Note that, for any
s<\alpha
and any bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
, there exists
C>0
such that for
any
f:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}^{d}

|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}},

(70)

which implies that for any Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
,

\|\mu\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\leq C\|\mu\|_{H^{-s}(\Omega)}.

(71)

We end this section with a lemma that relates the
H^{-s}
norm of a measure to its electric energy.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
g
be the Riesz kernel of order
s
(equation (
6
)) or the Coulomb kernel (in which case we take
s=1
), and let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Then

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}^{2}=\left(\sup_{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}}\frac{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d\mu}{|\phi|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}}\right)^{2}.

(72)

Proof.

It is clear that

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}^{2}.

(73)

By taking as a test functions a sequence of smooth functions with compact support that converge to
h^{\mu}
, we also have that

\sup_{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}}\frac{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d\mu}{|\phi|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}}\geq\frac{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}h^{\mu}\,d\mu}{|h^{\mu}|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}}=\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}.

(74)

Hence, we will prove that

\sup_{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}}\frac{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d\mu}{|\phi|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}}\leq\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}.

(75)

This is because, for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0},
we have that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d\mu&=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\mu}\,d\xi\\
&=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{s}\widehat{\phi}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{s}}\widehat{\mu}\,d\xi\\
&\leq\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}\|{|\xi|^{-s}}\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&=|\phi|_{\dot{H}_{F}^{s}}\|\mu\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}.\end{split}

(76)

From this, we can conclude.
∎

7
Proof of statements about the thermal equilibrium measure

This section is devoted to proving Proposition
3.1
. The proof will be a consequence of a series of lemmas.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
and let
\mathfrak{D}(f)
be the inverse of the operator

\mu\mapsto h^{\mu}.

(77)

Note that the inverse is a well defined map on from
H^{2s}
to
L^{2}
, since
\|h^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}}
is equivalent to
\|\mu\|_{H^{-2s}}
by item 8 of Definition
2.6
. Then
\mathfrak{D}(f)
can be written as

\mathfrak{D}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(-2f(x)+f(x+y)+f(x-y)\right)\left(h(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(78)

where
h
is given by

\widehat{h}=\frac{1}{\widehat{g}}.

(79)

Remark 9
.

Note that the integral in equation (
78
) is well defined for any
f\in H^{2s}
because of item 10 in the definition of Riesz-type kernel, Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

We begin by taking the Fourier transform in
x
:

\widehat{\mathfrak{D}(f)}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(-2+e^{i\xi\cdot y}+e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\right)h(y)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(80)

We now need to show that

\begin{split}F(\xi)&:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(-2+e^{i\xi\cdot y}+e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\right)h(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\frac{1}{\widehat{g}(\xi)}.\end{split}

(81)

To prove this, we apply Plancherel’s Theorem:

F(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\langle-2\delta_{0}+\delta_{\xi}+\delta_{-\xi},\widehat{h}\rangle.

(82)

Since
\widehat{h}
has a well-defined, finite value at
0
, we have that

\langle\delta_{0},\widehat{h}\rangle=0.

(83)

On the other hand, since
g
is even (item
2
of definition
2.6
),
\widehat{h}
is even, and therefore

\frac{1}{2}\langle\delta_{\xi}+\delta_{-\xi},\widehat{h}\rangle=\widehat{h}(\xi).

(84)

From this we can conclude.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

As a consequence of the previous lemma, the operator
\mathfrak{D}
satisfies a monotonicity property, i.e. if
x
is a maximum of
f
, then
\mathfrak{D}f(x)\leq 0
. Furthermore, if the inequality is not strict, then
f
is constant a.e.

After having access to a monotonicity property, we can now follow the same strategy as in
[
2
]
to proof Proposition
3.1
. We begin with a lemma, which is the analogue of Lemma 3.2 in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

m_{\theta}=\sup_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mu_{\theta}.

(85)

Then

-\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}\leq h^{\mu_{\theta}}-c_{\theta}-(h^{\mu_{\infty}}-c_{\infty}).

(86)

Proof.

We first show that we have

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\left(h^{\mu_{\theta}}+c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}\right)\geq 0,

(87)

which in virtue of equation (
54
) is equivalent to showing that

c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}\geq 0.

(88)

To prove this claim, we proceed by contradiction and assume that assume that

c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}<0.

(89)

We define
\psi
as the unique function satisfying that

\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}\psi=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\Sigma\\
\psi=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\Sigma\\
\lim_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)=c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}.\end{split}

(90)

Because
c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}<0
by hypothesis, we have
\psi-(c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta})
decays at infinity like the Green’s function associated to the operator
\mathfrak{D}
, i.e. for
x
large enough,
\psi(x)-(c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta})
is bounded above and below by a constant multiple of
|x|^{2s-d}
by item 5 of Definition
2.6
.

On the other hand, we define

\varphi:=h^{\mu_{\theta}}-h^{\mu_{\infty}}+c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}.

(91)

Note that
\mu_{\theta}
satisfies

h^{\mu_{\theta}}+V-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta}\geq 0,

(92)

and because of the First Order Condition for
\mu_{\infty}
-equation (
52
), we have that
\varphi
satisfies

\begin{split}\varphi\geq 0\quad{\rm in}\quad\Sigma\\
\mathfrak{D}(\varphi)\geq 0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\Sigma.\end{split}

(93)

We then have that
\mathfrak{D}(\varphi-\psi)\geq 0
in
\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\Sigma
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\varphi(x)-\psi(x)=0
, and
\varphi\geq\psi
in
\Sigma
. Then by monotonicity
\varphi-\psi\geq 0
in
\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\Sigma
. Furthermore, since

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{D}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mu_{\theta}-\mu_{\infty}=0,

(94)

we also have that
\varphi-(c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta})
satisfies that for
x
large enough, it is bounded above and below by a constant multiple of
x^{2s-d-1}
by item 6 of Definition
2.6
.

This last statement, the fact that
\varphi-(c_{\infty}-c_{\theta}+\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta})
decays at infinity like
|x|^{2s-d}
, and that fact that
\varphi\geq\psi
imply a contradiction. Therefore

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\varphi(x)\geq 0.

(95)

Hence, by equation (
93
) and monotonicity, we deduce that
\varphi\geq 0
in all of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. This implies the desired result.

∎

We can now prove Proposition
3.1
, restated here for convenience. The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
2
]
:

Proposition 7.4
.

Assume
V
is admissible and let
\theta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{+}.
For every
\theta>\theta_{0},
there exists a constant
C_{\theta_{0}}
such that

\mu_{\theta}(x)\leq C_{\theta_{0}},

(96)

and outside of a compact set
\Omega
(which depends only on
V
and
\theta_{0}
),

\mu_{\theta}(x)\leq\exp\left(C_{\theta_{0}}-\theta V(x)\right).

(97)

Proof.

We start with Lemma
7.3
, which along with the hypothesis that
\mu_{\infty}
is bounded implies that

h^{\mu_{\theta}}-c_{\theta}\geq-C-\frac{\log m_{\theta}}{\theta},

(98)

for some
C
which depends only on
V
.

Inserting into the First Order Condition for
\mu_{\theta}
-equation (
53
), we get that

\begin{split}\log\mu_{\theta}&=\theta(c_{\theta}-h^{\mu_{\theta}})-\theta V\\
&\leq\theta C+\log m_{\theta}-\theta V.\end{split}

(99)

Since
V(x)
tends to
\infty
as
x
tends to
\infty
, we have that for every
\theta_{0}
there exists
R_{0}
such that for every
\theta\geq\theta_{0}
and
x\notin B(0,R_{0})
we have

\log\mu_{\theta}(x)\leq\log m_{\theta}-1.

(100)

Therefore the supremum of
\mu_{\theta}
is achieved at
B(0,R_{0})
for every
\theta\geq\theta_{0}
.

Let
x_{\theta}\in B(0,R_{0})
be the maximizer of
\mu_{\theta}
. By monotonicity we have that
\mathfrak{D}(\log(\mu_{\theta}))\geq 0
, and therefore by the First Order Condition for
\mu_{\theta}
(equation (
53
)),

\mu_{\theta}(x_{\theta})+\mathfrak{D}V(x_{\theta})\leq 0.

(101)

Therefore

m_{\theta}\leq C_{\theta_{0}}:=\sup_{x\in B(0,R_{0})}|\mathfrak{D}V(x)|,

(102)

which implies that
\mu_{\theta}
is bounded independently of
\theta
, and therefore equation (
96
). Note that the hypothesis that

, along with item
10
of a Riesz-type kernel (Definition
2.6
) imply that
\sup_{x\in B(0,R)}|\mathfrak{D}V(x)|<\infty
. This uniform bound, together with equation (
99
) implies that

\log\mu_{\theta}\leq\theta C+\log C_{\theta_{0}}-\theta V,

(103)

which implies the uniform exponential decay (equation (
97
)).

∎

. The proof will rely on the following localization inequality, which is an extension of an analogous proposition in

to the

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\nu\in H^{-s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and assume that there exists a compact set
\Omega
such that
\nu
is nonpositive or nonnegative outside of
\Omega
. Then there exists a compact set
\Omega_{1}
which contains
\Omega,
and a constant
C
such that

\parallel\nu\parallel_{H^{-s}}\geq C\parallel\nu|_{\Omega_{1}}\parallel_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{1})}.

(104)

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, let
V
be an admissible potential, and let
\alpha>s
.

1.

Let

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}=\sup_{|\phi|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}\leq 1,\ \phi(0)=0}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d(\mu-\nu).

Since
\Omega

Hence,

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\leq\sup_{|\phi|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}\leq R,\ \|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq R}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\,d(\mu-\nu).

(111)

depends on
s,\alpha
, and
\Omega

Lastly, note that Lemma
6.12
implies that

\|\mu-\nu\|_{H^{-s}({\Omega})}\leq C\|\mu-\nu\|_{H^{-s}},

(112)

where
C
depends on
s
and
\Omega

Putting everything together, and noting that
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-s}}

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha,\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\mu-\nu),

(113)

where
C
depends on
s,\alpha
, and
\Omega
.

, and

\mu-\mu_{\infty}

Corollary 8.5
.

V
be an admissible potential, let
g
be a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, let
\alpha>s
. Let
\theta_{n}
be a strictly positive sequence tending to infinity, and let

C_{\alpha}
, which depends only on

, and

Lemma 9.2

(145)

\begin{split}\|(\mu-\nu)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}&\geq\left|\mu\left(\Omega\right)-\nu\left(\Omega\right)\right|\\

\|(\mu-\nu)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mu-\nu\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}.

(148)

10
Lower bound for the distance

In this section, we prove Proposition
3.5
, which we restate here.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
\mu_{N}
be a sequence of probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\sup_{N}\|\mu_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(163)

Let
\nu_{N}
be a sequence of probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\nu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}^{N}}.

(164)

Then

\|\mu_{N}-\nu_{N}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\geq CN^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}},

(165)

where
C
depends on
d
and
\sup_{N}\|\mu_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Let

\overline{X}_{N}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\{x_{i}^{N}\}.

(166)

For
\lambda>0
to be determined later, define the function
\varphi_{\lambda}:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
as

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\left(\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}-\left[{\rm dist}(x,\overline{X}_{N})\right]^{\alpha}\right)_{+},

(167)

where
(\cdot)_{+}
denotes the positive part of
(\cdot)
.

Note that for every
\lambda>0
, the function
\varphi_{\lambda}
satisfies

\begin{split}|\varphi_{\lambda}|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}&=1\\
{\rm supp}(\varphi_{\lambda})&=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B\left(x_{i}^{N},\lambda N^{-\frac{1}{d}}\right).\end{split}

(168)

Our aim is now to prove that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}d\left(\nu_{N}-\mu_{N}\right)\geq kN^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}},

(169)

for some
k\in\mathbf{R}^{+}
.

In order to do this, we introduce the notation

M:=\sup_{N}\|\mu_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(170)

and also the function

\widetilde{\mu}_{N}=M\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{i}^{N},\lambda N^{-\frac{1}{d}})}.

(171)

Note that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}d\left(\nu_{N}-\mu_{N}\right)\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}d\left(\nu_{N}-\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\right),

(172)

since

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}\,d\mu_{N}&\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{i}^{N},\lambda N^{-\frac{1}{d}})}\varphi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}\,d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}.\end{split}

(173)

We now compute

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}d\left(\nu_{N}-\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\right)&=\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}\,d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\\
&\geq\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}-M\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{i}^{N},\lambda N^{-\frac{1}{d}})}\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&=\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}-\lambda MN^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\left(Nk_{d}[\lambda N^{-\frac{1}{d}}]^{d}\right)\\
&=\lambda N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\left(1-Mk_{d}\lambda^{d}\right),\end{split}

(174)

where
k_{d}
is the volume of the
d-
dimensional unit sphere. Taking

\lambda=\frac{1}{\left(2Mk_{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}},

(175)

we have that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\lambda}d\left(\nu_{N}-\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\right)\geq N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d+1}{d}}\left(Mk_{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right),

(176)

which implies equation (
165
).

Bound on the Laplace transform

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
3.6
. The proof will rely on a concentration result from
[
32
]
:

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
, assume that
\frac{1}{N}\ll\beta
and that
V
is admissible. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
V
such that for any
r>0,
we have

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\parallel{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\parallel_{BL}\leq\frac{r}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)\geq 1-\exp\left(-N^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\left(C(r-C)_{+}^{2}-C\right)\right),

(177)

We will also use a simple concentration inequality, which is an immediate consequence of the splitting formula (Proposition
6.3
) and the fact that the log of the next-order partition function is positive (Lemma
6.7
). The proof is omitted.

Lemma 11.2
.

For any
r>0
,

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}({\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})>r)\leq\exp\left(-N^{2}\beta r\right).

(178)

We now prove Theorem
3.6
, restated here,

Theorem 11.3
.

Let
V
be an admissible potential, and assume
N\beta\to\infty
. Let
f:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
be continuous, and define the random variable
{\rm Fluct}[f]
by

{\rm Fluct}[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}fd\left({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\right).

(179)

1.

If
g
is the Coulomb kernel, then there exist a constant
C
, which depends only on
V
such that

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq N^{2}\beta\left(Ct^{2}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{2}{d}}C\right).

(180)

2.

If
g
is a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
then for any
\alpha>s
there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on

,

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq N^{2}\beta\left(Ct^{2}\|f\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{2\alpha}{d}}C\right).

(181)

3.

If
g
is the Coulomb kernel,
i\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
then there exists a constant
C
, which depends only on
V
such that

\begin{split}&\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq\\
&\beta N^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}+CN^{-\frac{2}{d}}\right)+\frac{d}{2}\left(\log(N^{2}\beta)+\log(1+t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}})\right).\end{split}

(182)

4.

If
g
is a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
,
i\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
V
and
g
such that

\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\leq\beta N^{2}\left(Ct^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}+CN^{-\frac{2s}{d}}\right).

(183)

Proof.

Step 1:
Proof of item
1.

We start with the case of
g
given by the Coulomb kernel. Since
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)
is a non-negative random variable, we can rewrite the expectation as

\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)>x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t{\rm Fluct}[f]>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{\rm BL}>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(184)

Note that if
x\leq 1
, then

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{\rm BL}>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)=1.

(185)

On the other hand, if
x>1
then by Theorem
11.1

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{\rm BL}>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)\leq\exp\left(-N^{2}\beta\left[C\left(\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta t\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}}\right)^{2}-CN^{-\frac{2}{d}}\right]\right).

(186)

Therefore we infer that

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\\
\leq&1+\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\left[C\frac{[\log x]^{2}}{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}\right]\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(187)

Performing the change of variables
y=\log x
we can transform the integral into

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\\
\leq&1+\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\left[C\frac{y^{2}}{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}-y\right]\right)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(188)

The last integral is a Gaussian density that can be computed exactly by completing squares:

\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\left[C\frac{y^{2}}{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}-y\right]\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{C}{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}\left(y-\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{2C}\right)^{2}+\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{4C}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\exp\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{4C}\right)\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{2C}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\end{split}

(189)

We conclude that

\begin{split}&\log\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\right)\\
\leq&\log\left(1+\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\exp\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{4C}\right)\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}}{2C}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\right)\\
\leq&N^{2}\beta\left(Ct^{2}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}+N^{-\frac{2}{d}}C\right).\end{split}

(190)

Step 2:
Proof of item
2.

The proof of the statement in the case of Riesz-type kernels is the same, after noting that if
g
is a Riesz-type kernel of order
s
, then for any
\alpha>s
there exist a constant
C
depending on
V,d,g
and
\alpha
such that

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\parallel{{\rm emp}}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\parallel_{C^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\geq r\right)\leq\exp\left(-N^{2}\beta\left(Cr^{2}-CN^{-\frac{2\alpha}{d}}\right)\right).

(191)

Step 3:
Proof of item
3.

We now turn to proving the third item.

We introduce the notation

{\rm emp}_{N}^{*}={\rm emp}_{N}\ast\delta_{N^{-\frac{1}{d}}},

(192)

where
\delta_{N^{-\frac{1}{d}}}
denotes the uniform probability measure on
B(0,N^{-\frac{1}{d}}),
and

{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f\,d({\rm emp}_{N}^{*}-\mu_{\theta}).

(193)

Note that if
i=0
, then

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f\,d({\rm emp}_{N}^{*}-{\rm emp}_{N})\right|&\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|f(x_{j})-\fint_{B(0,N^{-\frac{1}{d}})}f(x_{j})+(f(y)-f(x_{j}))\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\\
&\leq N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}|f|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}.\end{split}

(194)

On the other hand, for
i=1
, we have

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f\,d({\rm emp}_{N}^{*}-{\rm emp}_{N})\right|\leq\\
&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|f(x_{j})-\fint_{B(0,N^{-\frac{1}{d}})}f(x_{j})+\nabla f_{x_{j}}\cdot(y-x_{j})-(f(y)-f(x_{j})+\nabla f_{x_{j}}\cdot(y-x_{j}))\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\\
&N^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{d}}|f|_{\dot{C}^{1,\alpha}}.\end{split}

(195)

In either case, we get that

\left|{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]-{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\leq N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}.

(196)

We now proceed as in steps
1
and
2
, and write

\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)>x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t{\rm Fluct}[f]>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-1}}>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-1}}>\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(197)

Note that if
\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}<0
, i.e. if
x<\exp\left(N^{2-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}\beta t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)
, then

\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}_{N}^{*}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-1}}>\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)=1.

(198)

Otherwise, if
x\geq\exp\left(N^{2}\beta t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)
, then by Lemma
11.2
,

\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-1}}>\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)\\
=&\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}>\left[\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right]^{2}\right)\\
\leq&\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left({\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})>\left[\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right]^{2}-CN^{-\frac{2}{d}}\right)\\
\leq&\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\exp\left(\left[\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right]^{2}\right),\end{split}

(199)

for some
C
depending only on
V
.

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\\
\leq&\exp\left(N^{2-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}\beta t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)+\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\left[\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right]^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(200)

Proceeding as in the previous step, we can perform the change of variables
y=\log x
, then complete squares, and use the formula for the integral of a Gaussian density, and get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\left[\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right]^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\\
&\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left[\frac{t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}}{4}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right]\right).\end{split}

(201)

Note that

\begin{split}&\max\Bigg\{N^{2-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}\beta t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}},\\
&\quad\log\left(\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta\right)\left(\frac{N^{2}\beta t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left[\frac{t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}}{4}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right]\right)\right)\Bigg\}\leq\\
&CN^{2-\frac{2}{d}}\beta+\frac{d}{2}\left(\log(N^{2}\beta)+\log(1+t|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}})\right)+N^{2}\beta\left[\frac{t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}}{4}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right].\end{split}

(202)

From this we can conclude.

Step 4:
Proof of item
4.

We now turn to proving the last item of the Theorem. The procedure is similar to the proof of the last item, the difference is that the smearing procedure goes through higher dimensions.

Let
\overline{f}:\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\to\mathbf{R}
be such that
\overline{f}(x,0)=f(x)\forall x
and

\begin{split}|\overline{f}|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{m}{2}}}&\leq C|{f}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\\
|\overline{f}|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}&\leq C|{f}|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}},\end{split}

(203)

for some constant depending only on
d
and
m
. For example, take
\overline{f}(x,z)=\left(e^{|z|\Delta_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}}f\right)(x)
.

We introduce the notation
\delta_{x}^{(\eta)}
for the uniform probability measure on
\partial B(x,\eta)\subset\mathbf{R}^{d+m}
, and define

{\rm emp}_{N}^{*}={\rm emp}_{N}\ast\delta_{x}^{(N^{-\frac{1}{d}})},

(204)

and

{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d+m}}\overline{f}\,d(\mu_{\theta}-{\rm emp}_{N}^{*}).

(205)

Proceeding as in the proof of the last item, we have

\begin{split}\left|{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]-{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|&\leq N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}|\overline{f}|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\\
&\leq CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}|{f}|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}.\end{split}

(206)

Proceeding as in step 3, we have that

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)=\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)>x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t{\rm Fluct}[f]>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(t{\rm Fluct}^{*}[f]>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(Ct|\overline{f}|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{m}{2}}}\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}}>\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\|{\rm emp}^{*}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\|_{H^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}}>\frac{\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-CN^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}}{Ct|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(207)

Proceeding as in the previous step, we can split split the integral into the domains
\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}>0
and
\frac{\log x}{N^{2}\beta}-N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}<0
, then apply Lemma
13.1
with
\eta_{i}=N^{-\frac{1}{d}}
for each
i
, and Lemma
11.2
, then bound/compute the integrals, and get

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{N,\beta}}\exp\left(N^{2}\beta\left|t{\rm Fluct}[f]\right|\right)\leq\\
&\exp\left(N^{2-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}\beta t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right)+\\
&\quad\exp\left(CN^{2-\frac{2s}{d}}\beta\right)\left(N^{2}\beta t|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(CN^{2}\beta\left[{t^{2}|f|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}}+N^{-\frac{i+\alpha}{d}}t|f|_{\dot{C}^{i,\alpha}}\right]\right).\end{split}

(208)

From this we can conclude.

12
Appendix A: the
H^{-s}(\Omega)
norm

This section is devoted to proving Proposition
8.1
. The strategy is an extension of the proof
[
32
]
for an analogous result. The proof will rely on a few lemmas.

Lemma 12.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set with a
C^{2}
boundary, and let
f\in H^{s}({\Omega}).

Let

\epsilon_{*}=\sup\{\epsilon>0|x\mapsto x+\epsilon\widehat{n}(x)\text{ is a diffeomorphism for all }|\delta|<\epsilon\},

(209)

where
\widehat{n}(x)
is the unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
x.
Then for every
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{*}
there exists
f_{\epsilon}\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

1.

f_{\epsilon}|_{\Omega}=f.

2.

\mbox{supp}(f_{\epsilon})\subset\overline{\Omega}^{\epsilon},
where

\Omega^{\epsilon}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}|d(x,\Omega)<\epsilon\}.

(210)

3.

\begin{split}\parallel f_{\epsilon}\parallel_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}&\leq\frac{C}{{\epsilon}^{\frac{s}{2}}}\parallel f\parallel_{H^{s}}\\
\parallel f_{\epsilon}\parallel_{L^{2}}&\leq(1+C\sqrt{\epsilon})\parallel f\parallel_{L^{2}},\end{split}

(211)

where
C
depends only on
\Omega
.

In addition, if
f
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega,
then
f_{\epsilon}
is non negative in
\Omega^{\epsilon}\setminus\Omega.

Proof.

Step 1:
Flat case.

Assume for this step that for some
\delta>0

B(x,\delta)\bigcap\partial\Omega=\{y|y_{d}=0\}\bigcap B(x,\delta).

(212)

We will use the notation

x=(\underline{x},x_{d}),

(213)

where
\underline{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{d-1}
and
x_{d}\in\mathbf{R}.
Let
x\in\partial\Omega
and let

\underline{B}(x,\delta)=\{y|y_{d}=0\}\bigcap B(x,\delta).

(214)

Let
\alpha>0
be such that

\underline{B}(x,\delta)\times(-\alpha,0)\subset\Omega.

(215)

Define
\varphi:\underline{B}(x,\delta)\times(0,\alpha)\to\mathbf{R}
by odd reflection:

\varphi(\underline{y},y_{d})=f(\underline{y},-y_{d}).

(216)

Let
\mu\in C^{\infty}([0,\alpha],\mathbf{R}^{+})
be such that
\mu(0)=1,\ \mu(\alpha)=0,
and
\mu
is decreasing. Consider now
\widehat{\varphi}:\underline{B}(x,\delta)\times(0,\alpha)\to\mathbf{R}
defined as

\widehat{\varphi}(\underline{y},y_{d})=\varphi(\underline{y},y_{d})\mu(y_{d}).

(217)

Now we define the function
\varphi_{\epsilon}:\underline{B}(x,\delta)\times(0,\epsilon)\to\mathbf{R}
as

\varphi_{\epsilon}(\underline{y},y_{d})=\widehat{\varphi}\left(\underline{y},\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}y_{d}\right).

(218)

We immediately get the estimate

\parallel\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}}\parallel f\parallel_{L^{2}}.

(219)

We also have the estimate

\parallel\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C\max\left(\left(\frac{{\alpha}}{{\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}},1\right)\parallel f\parallel_{H^{1}},

(220)

where
C
depends only on
\Omega.

Lastly, if
f
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
consider the function

M\varphi_{\epsilon}=\max\left(\varphi_{\epsilon},0\right).

(221)

Then
M\varphi_{\epsilon}
is positive, and
M\varphi_{\epsilon}
agrees with
f
on
\{x_{d}=0\}
.

We also have that that

\begin{split}\parallel M\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{\dot{H}^{s}}&\leq\parallel\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{\dot{H}^{s}}\\
\parallel M\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{L^{2}}&\leq\parallel\varphi_{\epsilon}\parallel_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(222)

Step 2:
General case.

Now we turn to the general case, where
\partial\Omega
is not flat. Since by assumption
\partial\Omega
is
C^{2},
there exist finitely many balls
B(x_{i},\epsilon_{i})
and
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms
g_{i}:U_{i}\subset\mathbf{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

g_{i}\left(U_{i}\right)=B(x_{i},\epsilon_{i})\bigcap\partial\Omega.

(223)

For any
\delta<\epsilon_{*},
we can extend
g_{i}
to a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
\overline{g}_{i}:U_{i}\times(-\delta,\delta)\to V_{i}^{\delta},
where

V_{i}^{\delta}=\{x+s\widehat{n}(x)|x\in B(x_{i},\epsilon_{i})\bigcap\partial\Omega,s\in(-\delta,\delta)\}.

(224)

We define
\overline{g}_{i}
as

\overline{g}_{i}(\underline{x},s)=g_{i}(\underline{x})+s\nu(g_{i}(\underline{x})).

(225)

Now define for any
\epsilon<\epsilon_{*}
the function
\varphi_{\epsilon}^{i}:U_{i}\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
as in step 1, with
\alpha=\epsilon_{*}
. If
f
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, define
M\varphi_{\epsilon}^{i}
as in step 1.

Define the functions
\phi_{\epsilon}^{i}
as

\phi_{\epsilon}^{i}=\varphi_{\epsilon}^{i}\circ\overline{g}_{i}^{-1}.

(226)

If
f
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, define the functions
M\phi_{\epsilon}^{i}
as

M\phi_{\epsilon}^{i}=M\varphi_{\epsilon}^{i}\circ\overline{g}_{i}^{-1}.

(227)

Lastly, take a partition of unity
q_{i}
associated to
V_{i}^{\epsilon_{*}}.
Define the extension
f_{\epsilon}
as

f_{\epsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)\text{ if }x\in\Omega\\
\sum_{i}(q_{i}\phi_{i}^{\epsilon})\text{ if }x\in\bigcup V_{i}^{\delta}\\
0\text{ o.w.}\end{cases}

(228)

If
f
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, define the extension
Mf_{\epsilon}
as

Mf_{\epsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)\text{ if }x\in\Omega\\
\sum_{i}(q_{i}M\phi_{i}^{\epsilon})\text{ if }x\in\bigcup V_{i}^{\delta}\\
0\text{ o.w.}\end{cases}

(229)

It is easy to check that
f_{\epsilon},Mf_{\epsilon}
saitsfy the desired properties.

∎

We proceed to another lemma.

Lemma 12.2
.

Let
\nu\in H^{-s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Assume that there exists a compact set
\Omega
such that
\nu
is nonpositive or nonnegative outside of
\Omega.
Then there exists a compact set
\Omega_{2}
which contains
\Omega,
a constant
C,
and a function
\phi\in H^{s}(\Omega_{2})
such that

\int_{\Omega_{2}}\nu\phi\geq C\parallel\nu|_{\Omega_{2}}\parallel_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{2})},

(230)

\parallel\phi\parallel_{H^{s}}=1
, and
\phi
is non-negative a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega_{2}.
Furthermore,
C
and
\Omega_{2}
depends only on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We assume WLOG that
\nu
is positive outside of
\Omega
and that
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
. Otherwise we could find a compact set with
C^{2}
boundary containing
\Omega
. Let

\Omega^{\epsilon}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}|d(x,\Omega)\leq\epsilon\}

(231)

Take some
\epsilon>0
. Note that

\parallel\nu|_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}\parallel_{H^{-s}(\Omega^{\epsilon})}<\infty,

(232)

and hence, there exists some
\varphi\in H^{s}(\Omega^{\epsilon})
such that

\parallel\varphi\parallel_{H^{s}}=1

(233)

and

\int_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}\nu\varphi\geq\frac{1}{2}\parallel\nu|_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}\parallel_{H^{-s}(\Omega^{\epsilon})}.

(234)

Consider now
\overline{\varphi}=\varphi|_{\Omega}.
By the extension lemma (Lemma
12.1
), there exists an extension
\widetilde{\varphi}
of
\overline{\varphi}
such that

\text{supp}\left(\widetilde{\varphi}\right)\subset\Omega^{\epsilon}

(235)

and

\parallel\widetilde{\varphi}\parallel_{H^{s}}\leq C,

(236)

where
C_{\epsilon}
depends on
\Omega
and
\epsilon
. Note that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is
0
in a neighborhood of
\partial_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}
.

Consider now

\phi=\max\{\varphi,\widetilde{\varphi}\}.

(237)

Then since
\phi\geq\widetilde{\varphi},
we know that
\phi
is non-negative in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega^{\epsilon}
. We also know that

\begin{split}\varphi(x)&=\phi(x)\text{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
\varphi(x)&\leq\phi(x)\text{ for }x\in\Omega^{\epsilon}\setminus\Omega,\\
\end{split}

(238)

which implies

\int_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}\nu\varphi\leq\int_{\Omega^{\epsilon}}\nu\phi,

(239)

since
\nu
is positive outside of
\Omega
.

It can be easily shown that

\begin{split}\parallel\phi\parallel_{H^{s}}&\leq\parallel\varphi\parallel_{H^{s}}+\parallel\widetilde{\varphi}\parallel_{H^{s}}\\
&\leq 1+C_{\epsilon}.\end{split}

(240)

Taking
\widehat{\phi}=\frac{\phi}{\parallel\phi\parallel_{H^{s}}}
,
C=\frac{1}{2(1+C_{\epsilon})}
and
\Omega_{2}=\overline{\Omega}^{\epsilon}
we obtain the result.
∎

8.1
, restated here.

Proposition 12.3

\Omega,

Furthermore,

\Omega_{1}

\Omega.

Proof.

Again, WLOG we assume that

\Omega
. Take some fixed

there exists a
\varphi\in H^{s}(\Omega^{\epsilon})

1.

\parallel\varphi\parallel_{H^{s}}=1.

(242)

2.

\varphi
is positive in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega^{\epsilon}

3.

(243)

where

In this section, we prove Proposition

. In order to do so, we need a fundamental result about regularization for Riesz-type kernels.

Lemma 13.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
0<s<\min\{1,\frac{d}{2}\}
. Suppose that
X_{N}\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times N}
is a pairwise distinct configuration and that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
s,d,g
such that for every
\eta_{i}<\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{r_{0}}{2}\}
we have

\frac{1}{C}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}^{(\eta_{i})}-\widetilde{\mu}\right|\right|_{\dot{H}^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}}^{2}\leq{\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu)+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{\eta_{i}}(0)+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}}\left[\eta_{i}^{2s}+\eta_{i}^{2}\right]\right).

(247)

In this notation,
\delta_{x}^{(\eta)}
is the uniform probability measure on
\partial B(x,\eta)\subset\mathbf{R}^{d+m}
,
\widetilde{\mu}=\mu\delta_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\{0\}}
is a probability measure on
\mathbf{R}^{d+m}
, and
G_{\eta}=G\ast\delta^{\eta}_{0}
.

Proof.

See
[
31
]
, Proposition 2.2.
∎

We now prove Proposition
9.1
, restated here.

Proposition 13.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
have compact support
\Omega
and let
\alpha>s
. Then there exists a constant
C
, which depends on
V,g,\Omega
, and
\alpha
such that

\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\leq N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}+C\left({\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu)+C\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}}N^{-\frac{2s}{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(248)

Proof.

By Lemma
9.2
,

\|{\rm emp}_{N}-\mu\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}\leq 2\|({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}.

(249)

Note that

\|({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}_{*}}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\phi,

(250)

for some
\phi
with compact support and such that

\|\phi\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}}=1.

(251)

Arguing as in
[
31
]
, let
\overline{\phi}:\mathbf{R}^{d+m}\to\mathbf{R}
be such that
\overline{\phi}(x,0)={\phi}(x)\forall x
and

\begin{split}\|\overline{\phi}\|_{H^{s+\frac{m}{2}}}&\leq C\|{\phi}\|_{H^{s}}\\
|\overline{\phi}|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}}&\leq C|{\phi}|_{\dot{C}^{0,\alpha}},\end{split}

(252)

for some
C
depending only on
m
and
d
.

For example, take
\overline{\phi}(x,z)=\left(e^{|z|\Delta_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}}\phi\right)(x)
. We introduce the notation

{\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}={\rm emp}_{N}\ast\delta^{(\eta)}_{0}.

(253)

Now we can write

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\,d({\rm emp}_{N}-\mu)&\leq\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d+m}}\overline{\phi}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}({\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}-\overline{\mu})\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d+m}}\overline{\phi}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}\,d\left({\rm emp}_{N}-{\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\|\overline{\phi}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}\|_{H^{s+\frac{m}{2}}}\|\left({\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}-\overline{\mu}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}\|_{H^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}(\Omega\times B(0,1))}+\eta^{\alpha}.\end{split}

(254)

Note that, since
\phi
has compact support

\begin{split}\|\overline{\phi}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}\|_{H^{s+\frac{m}{2}}}&\leq\|{\phi}\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\leq C\|{\phi}\|_{{C}^{0,\alpha}},\end{split}

(255)

where
C
depends on
\Omega
.

On the other hand, using the localization inequality (Proposition
8.1
) and Lemma

\begin{split}&\|\left({\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}-\overline{\mu}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\times B(0,1)}\|_{H^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}(\Omega\times B(0,1))}\\
\leq&C\|{\rm emp}_{N}^{\eta}-\overline{\mu}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s-\frac{m}{2}}}\\
\leq&C\left({\rm F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu)+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{\eta_{i}}(0)+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}}\left[\eta_{i}^{2s}+\eta_{i}^{2}\right]\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(256)

where
C
depends on
V,g,\Omega
, and
\alpha
.

Taking

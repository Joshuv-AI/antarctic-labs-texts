Abstract
We consider the problem of solving robust Markov decision process (MDP), which involves a set of discounted, finite state, finite action space MDPs with uncertain transition kernels. The goal of planning is to find a robust policy that optimizes the worst-case values against the transition uncertainties, and thus encompasses the standard MDP planning as a special case. For ( 𝐬 , 𝐚 ) (\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}) -rectangular uncertainty sets, we establish several structural observations on the robust objective, which facilitates the development of a policy-based first-order method, namely the robust policy mirror descent (RPMD). An 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) ) \mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon)) iteration complexity for finding an ϵ \epsilon -optimal policy is established with linearly increasing stepsizes. We further develop a stochastic variant of the robust policy mirror descent method, named SRPMD, when the first-order information is only available through online interactions with the nominal environment. We show that the optimality gap converges linearly up to the noise level, and consequently establish an 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2}) sample complexity by developing a temporal difference learning method for policy evaluation. Both iteration and sample complexities are also discussed for RPMD with a constant stepsize. To the best of our knowledge, all the aforementioned results appear to be new for policy-based first-order methods applied to the robust MDP problem.

Introduction

We consider the problem of solving the robust Markov decision process (MDP) where the transition kernel is uncertain, and one seeks to learn a policy that behaves robustly against such uncertainties.
Specifically,
a robust MDP
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq\left\{\mathcal{M}_{u}=({\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{A},c,\mathbb{P}_{u},\gamma):u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}
consists of a set of MDPs, where
{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{A}
denote the state and action space, respectively;
\mathbb{P}_{u}:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
denotes the transition kernel, indexed by
u\in\mathcal{U}
;
c:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the cost function, which we assume with loss of generality that
0<c(s,a)\leq 1
for all
(s,a)
;
\gamma
denotes the discount factor.
The set of MDPs differ from each other only in their respective transition kernels.
The standard value function
V^{\pi}_{u}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
of a policy
\pi
with respect to MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u}
, is defined as

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{u}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(s_{t},a_{t})\big|s_{0}=s,a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t}),s_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}_{u}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})\right],~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Our end goal is to learn a policy
\pi^{*}
that is the solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\textstyle\min\left\{V^{\pi}_{r}(s)\coloneqq\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}V^{\pi}_{u}(s):~\pi\in\Pi\right\},

(1.1)

where
\Pi
denotes the set of all stationary and randomized policies.
That is, (
1.1
) aims to learn a policy that minimizes the worst-case value simultaneously for every state.
Clearly, when
\mathcal{U}
is a singleton, the problem of finding robust policy (
1.1
) reduces to solving a standard MDP planning problem.

Before any technical discussions, we first construct a simple example motivating our study of finding a robust policy in the sense of (
1.1
), when facing transition uncertainty.
Specifically, we will construct a pair of MDP
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{u}
, with the same
({\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{A},c,\gamma)
, and the transition kernels of these two MDPs are close to each other, yet the optimal policy for
\mathcal{M}
achieves highly suboptimal value in
\mathcal{M}_{u}
.
In contrast, we show that there exists a policy that achieves close-to-optimal performances in both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{u}
.

(a)

Nominal
\mathcal{M}
with transition kernel
\mathbb{P}
.

(b)

\mathcal{M}_{u}
with transition kernel
\mathbb{P}_{u}\approx\mathbb{P}
.

Example 1.1

(Tradeoff between planning efficiency and robustness)
.

Consider the nominal MDP
\mathcal{M}
illustrated in Figure
1(a)
.
Starting at
S_{0}
, the agent has two possible actions
\left\{L,R\right\}
, consisting of going either left or right.
Going right incurs a cost of
-1
, going left incurs a cost of
0
.
We assume the cost occurs immediately after the action is made.
Whenever the agent arrives at
S_{i,R}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
S_{k+1}
, the agent stays at the same place going forward.

Additionally, if the agent goes left from
S_{0}
, then in the ensuing rounds the agent has only one available action, which is to transit to the next state following the arrows.
No cost is incurred until the agent transits from
S_{k-1,L}
to
S_{k,L}
, which incurs a cost of
-(1+\epsilon)\gamma^{-k+1}
for some small positive number
\epsilon\ll 1
(e.g.,
\epsilon=0.01
).

Now consider another MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u}
that is exact the same as
\mathcal{M}
, except in the transition kernel, illustrated in Figure
1(b)
.
In particular, for any
1\leq m\leq k-1
, the transition changes to
\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{m+1,L}|S_{m,L})=p,~\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{m,R}|S_{m,L})=1-p
, for some
p\in(0,1)
close to
1
(e.g.,
p=0.99
).
Thus
\mathcal{M}_{u}
can be viewed as an approximate copy of
\mathcal{M}
.

It should be clear that for MDP
\mathcal{M}
, going left at
S_{0}
incurs a value of
-(1+\epsilon)
, which is close, but still strictly small than the value
-1
of going right.
Thus the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
for
\mathcal{M}
is to always go left.

However, when deploying
\pi^{*}
in the slightly changed environment
\mathcal{M}_{u}
, one can clearly see that going left at
S_{0}
now incurs a value of
-(1+\epsilon)p^{k-1}
, while going right still has the value of
-1
.
For
k
large enough, we obverse that the value of going left approaches
0
, which is significantly worse than the value of going right.

In conclusion, we see that going left at
S_{0}
serves as the optimal policy in
\mathcal{M}
, but its performance degrades significantly despite being deployed at a similar environment
\mathcal{M}_{u}
.
In fact, such a policy is even worse than the policy of randomly going left or right with equal probability.
In contrast, going right at
S_{0}
is an
\epsilon
-optimal policy in
\mathcal{M}
, and also the optimal policy in
\mathcal{M}_{u}
, thus being a much more desirable choice in terms of robustness.

The tensions between accuracy and robustness have been discussed extensively in the supervised learning literature
[
43
,
50
,
49
,
24
]
. Example
1.1
demonstrates that similar tensions between cost-minimization (planning) and robustness also exists in the control of uncertain Markov decision process.
It should be noted that the key ingredient for the lack of robustness in the MDP demonstrated in Figure
1
is the reward (cost) sparseness.
This feature has been widely observed for practical MDP applications
[
32
,
28
]
, and we believe the same mechanism can be one of the few important factors that lead to brittle robustness observed in existing reinforcement learning applications.

To proceed, we will focus on the case of
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
-rectangular uncertainty set, defined below.

Definition 1.1

(
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
-Rectangular Uncertainty)
.

We assume the transition kernel
\mathbb{P}_{u}
for the MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u}
takes the form of

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{u}(\cdot|s,a)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{N}}(\cdot|s,a)+u(\cdot|s,a),~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

(1.2)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{N}}(\cdot|s,a)
denotes the nominal transition kernel, and

u\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\times\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\times\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
denotes the perturbation to the nominal transition kernel.
The uncertainty is said to be rectangular if
\mathcal{U}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\Pi_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{U}_{s,a},~~\text{where}~~\mathcal{U}_{s,a}=\left\{u(\cdot|s,a):u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}.

We assume
\mathcal{U}
is compact.
In addition, we let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{s,a}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{N}}(\cdot|s,a)+\mathcal{U}_{s,a}

(1.3)

denote the set of possible transition probabilities at
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
, which we refer to as the ambiguity set.

Remark 1.1
.

While one can also consider uncertain cost function
c_{u}
when modeling, our motivation to focus on modeling uncertain transitions is due to the observation that cost function is mostly an endogenous user choice, and thus seems less suitable to be modeled as an uncertainty.

From Definition
1.1
, it should be also clear that Example
1.1
can be readily modeled into a robust MDP with a rectangular uncertainty set.
We will also define the nominal environment of the robust MDP
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{U}}
as follows, a useful notion for our discussions in the stochastic settings.

Definition 1.2

(Nominal Environment)
.

The nominal environment
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{N}}
for a robust MDP problem
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{U}}
, is the MDP with transition kernel
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Given a policy
\pi
, we define its robust value function
V^{\pi}_{r}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
as
V^{\pi}_{r}(s)=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
Consequently, solving (
1.1
) is equivalently to minimizing the robust value function:

\displaystyle\textstyle\pi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmin}}_{\pi\in\Pi}V^{\pi}_{r}(s).

(1.4)

The existence of an optimal policy
\pi^{*}
solving (
1.4
) is well known in the literature
[
30
,
12
]
,
and we denote the set of optimal policies as
\Pi^{*}\subseteq\Pi
.
Hence, we can succinctly reformulate (
1.4
) into a single objective optimization problem

\displaystyle\textstyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\left\{f_{\rho}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}V^{\pi}_{r}(s)\right\},

(1.5)

where
\rho
is a nonnegative measure defined over the state space
{\mathcal{S}}
.

For any
u\in\mathcal{U}
, we also define the state-action value function of policy
\pi
with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{u}
as

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}_{u}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(s_{t},a_{t})\big|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a,,a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t}),s_{t}\sim\mathbb{P}_{u}(\cdot|s_{t-1},a_{t-1}),t\geq 1\right],~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

Accordingly, the robust state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
as

Q^{\pi}_{r}(s,a)=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}Q^{\pi}_{u}(s,a)
for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
.
Note that from the definition of
Q^{\pi}_{u}
and
V^{\pi}_{u}
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{u}(s)=\langle Q^{\pi}_{u}(s,\cdot),\pi(\cdot|s)\rangle\coloneqq\langle Q_{u}^{\pi},\pi\rangle_{s}.

(1.6)

Given a policy
\pi
,
and an uncertainty
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
the discounted state visitation measure jointly induced by
(\pi,u)
is defined as

\displaystyle d^{\pi,u}_{s}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbb{P}_{u}^{\pi}(s_{t}=s^{\prime}|s_{0}=s),

(1.7)

where
\mathbb{P}_{u}^{\pi}(s_{t}=s^{\prime}|s_{0}=s)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left(s_{t}=s^{\prime}|s_{0}=s,a_{t}=\pi(\cdot|s_{t}),s_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}_{u}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})\right)
denotes the probability of reaching state
s^{\prime}
at timestep
t
, given initial state
s
, and following policy
\pi
within MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u}
.
Given any distribution
\rho
over
{\mathcal{S}}
, we define
distribution
d_{\rho}^{\pi,u}
over
{\mathcal{S}}
as

d_{\rho}^{\pi,u}(s^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}d_{s}^{\pi,u}(s^{\prime})
.
For a finite set
\mathcal{X}
, we will denote
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
as the
(\lvert\mathcal{X}\rvert-1)
-dimensional simplex.

Approximate dynamic programming (ADP) techniques

The application of ADP to policy-based methods also enables function approximation to be used to handle the curse of dimensionality

while incorporating approximate policy evaluation (e.g., robust TD learning

,

It should be noted there also exists another complementary line of research, on studying robust MDP with uncertainty set beyond

In addition to the prior developments in the context of dynamic programming, there has been a rising interests in developing first-order methods for solving the special case of (

being a singleton).
By using first-order information of objective (

Sublinear convergence of the optimality gap for constant stepsize PGMs have been established in

Moreover, stochastic PGMs, which utilize sample to estimate the first-order information, have also been proposed in

Complementary to the policy-based first-order methods,

In contrast to the aforementioned developments of PGMs for solving non-robust MDP,

Specifically,

Our contributions mainly exist in the the following aspects.
First, we establish some new structural results for the robust Markov decision process.
In particular, we discuss the differentiability of robust values, a robust version of performance difference lemma (c.f.,
[
13
]
), and a novel variational inequality perspective on solving robust MDP.
These results serve as a crucial component that facilitates our ensuing computational development, and might be of independent interests for other algorithmic studies (e.g., natural robust policy gradient, see Section
3
).

Second, we develop a first-order policy-based method, named robust policy mirror descent (RPMD), for solving the robust MDP problem (
1.5
).
Despite the non-convex and non-smooth structure of the objective (see
[
46
]
), RPMD finds an
\epsilon
-optimal policy in
\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon))
iterations.
The established convergence results hold for any Bregman divergence, as long as the policy space has a bounded distance to the initial policy measured in the same divergence.
To the best of our knowledge, no existing PGM can attain the obtained iteration complexity when solving (
1.5
).
In addition, we also establish the sublinear convergence of constant-stepsize RPMD, for both Euclidean Bregman divergence,
and a more general class of Bregman divergence applied to any relatively strongly convex uncertainty set.

Finally, we develop stochastic variants of the RPMD method, named SRPMD, when the first-order information is only available through online interactions with the nominal environment.
For general Bregman divergences, we show that SRPMD with linearly increasing stepsizes converges linearly up to the noise level, and consequently determine an
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
sample complexity.
For Euclidean Bregman divergence, we show an
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{3})
sample complexity with a properly chosen constant stepsize.
To the best of our knowledge, all the developed sample complexity results of RPMD appear to be new for PGM methods applied to the robust MDP problem.

2
Structural Properties of Robust MDP

In this section, we develop some important observations on the structural properties of robust MDP, which will prove to be useful in our ensuing algorithmic developments.

2.1
Structure of Robust Value Functions

We first characterize the robust value function of any stochastic policy, following similar arguments for deterministic policies in
[
30
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For robust MDP
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{U}}
with a compact rectangular uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}
, defined in Definition
1.1
,
the robust value function satisfies the following nonlinear Bellman equation

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{r}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\pi(a|s)+\gamma\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime}),~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

(2.1)

In addition, a worst-case transition kernel
\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}
for the policy
\pi
is given by

\displaystyle u_{\pi}(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmax}}_{u(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathcal{U}_{s,a}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime}),~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

(2.2)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{r}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\pi(a|s)+\gamma\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime}),~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

It is worth mentioning that the worst-case environment
u_{\pi}
defined in (
2.2
) can be non-unique.
In this case, one can choose any of them by an arbitrary deterministic rule.
Note that the last relation in Proposition
2.1
also shows that
V^{\pi}_{r}
is the solution of the standard Bellman equation for standard value function with uncertainty
u_{\pi}
, denoted by
V^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}
. Hence from the uniqueness of the solution for the Bellman equation, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{r}=V^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}.

(2.3)

Following similar lines as in Proposition
2.1
, we can establish the following properties of
Q^{\pi}_{r}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The robust state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}
satisfies

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}_{r}(s,a)=c(s,a)+\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}),~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

(2.4)

Moreover,
Q^{\pi}_{r}
and
V^{\pi}_{r}
satisfies the following relation

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}_{r}(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime}),~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

(2.5)

Finally, we also have

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle=\langle Q^{\pi}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi(\cdot|s)\rangle\coloneqq\langle Q^{\pi}_{r},\pi\rangle_{s},~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}_{r}

\displaystyle=Q^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}},

(2.7)

where
u_{\pi}
is defined as in (
2.2
).

The proof of Proposition
2.1
and
2.2
are deferred to Appendix
A
.
We next proceed to discuss the differentiability of robust values.

2.2
Differentiability of Robust Values

A seemingly natural concept to minimize
f_{\rho}
defined in (
1.5
) is to iteratively update the policy by following its negative gradient direction.
At the same time,
it should be noted that even for a fixed uncertainty (i.e., non-robust MDP),
for any state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, the value function
V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
is only well defined over the set of randomized policies
\Pi
, for which any
\pi\in\Pi
must satisfy
\mathbf{1}^{\top}\pi(\cdot|s)=1
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
Hence
\Pi=\mathrm{dom}(f_{\rho})
belongs to a lower-dimensional subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\times\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
with dimension
(\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert-1)\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert
.
Consequently,
the implicitly assumed gradient
\nabla f_{\rho}
, given by

\lim_{\delta\to\mathbf{0},\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\times\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}}\lvert f_{\rho}(\pi+\delta)-f_{\rho}(\pi)-\langle\nabla f_{\rho}(\pi),\delta\rangle\rvert/\lVert\delta\rVert\to 0
,
is not well defined.
Given this observation, we then adopt the following definition of policy gradient for objective (
1.5
) when considering direct policy parameterization.

Definition 2.1

(Policy Gradient with Direct Parameterization)
.

For any function of policy
f:\Pi\to\mathbb{R}
, the policy gradient of
f
with respect to
\pi
, denoted by
\nabla f(\pi)
, is the vector satisfying the following,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to\mathbf{0},\pi+\delta\in\Pi}\lvert f(\pi+\delta)-f(\pi)-\langle\nabla f(\pi),\delta\rangle\rvert/\lVert\delta\rVert_{2}\to 0.

(2.8)

Given Definition
2.1
, it should be clear that for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
, if
\nabla f(\pi)
exists, then it is unique.
Definition
2.1
slightly generalizes the notion of Fréchet derivative of objective (
1.5
) as
\Pi
is a closed set in its affine span.
We then proceed to derive the policy gradient of
V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
for a given uncertainty
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 2.1

(Policy Gradient for Fixed Uncertainty with Direct Parameterization)
.

Given
u\in\mathcal{U}
and a state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, then the policy gradient of
V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
with respect to
\pi
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla V^{\pi}_{u}(s)[s^{\prime},a]=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}d_{s}^{\pi,u}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{u}(s^{\prime},a),~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

where
\nabla V^{\pi}_{u}(s)[s^{\prime},a]
denotes the entry of
\nabla V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
corresponding to the
(s^{\prime},a)
state-action pair.

Proof.

For MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u}
, and any pair of policies
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
with
\pi^{\prime}=\pi+\delta
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)-V^{\pi}_{u}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{\prime},u}}\langle Q^{\pi}_{u}(s^{\prime},\cdot),\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}d_{s}^{\pi^{\prime},u}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{u}(s^{\prime},a)\left(\pi^{\prime}(a|s^{\prime})-\pi(a|s^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}d_{s}^{\pi,u}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{u}(s^{\prime},a)\left(\pi^{\prime}(a|s^{\prime})-\pi(a|s^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle d_{s}^{\pi,u}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}(\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u})^{t}e_{s}=(1-\gamma)\left(1-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}\right)^{-1}e_{s},

Given our prior discussions on the differentiability of objective (
1.5
), one can perhaps directly update the policy using the policy gradient specified in (
2.12
).
Moreover, the results developed in this subsection seems to be readily extensible for computing policy gradient when function approximation is adopted.

On the other hand, it should be noted that the task of calculating or estimating the gradient for the robust MDP seems difficult when one can only access information through interactions with the nominal environment, except for some special subclasses of uncertainty sets.
In particular, although one can sample directly from the worst-case environment
\mathcal{M}_{u_{\pi}}
to perform estimation, in practice we believe this is against the principle of pursuing robustness.
Instead, a much more desirable alternative is to train the policy in a fixed (nominal) environment, without actually deploying it in its worst-case environment.
More importantly, due to the nonconvex and potentially nonsmooth landscape, one typically could only get a sublinear convergence to a stationary point in the best-iterate sense, which would further require additional landscape analysis for showing approximate optimality of the learned policy
[
46
]
.

In the next subsection, we introduce an alternative viewpoint of solving the robust MDP based on variational inequalities.
As we will demonstrate in Section
3
, solving the variational inequality allows us to bypass the aforementioned difficulties,
and develop a policy mirror descent method with fast global convergence guarantees.

2.3
A Variational Inequality Perspective

To proceed, we introduce a characterization that upper bounds the difference of robust values for two policies, and serves as a keystone in the ensuing algorithmic developments.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any pair of policies
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d^{\pi^{\prime},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{s}}\langle Q^{\pi}_{r},\pi^{\prime}-\pi\rangle_{s^{\prime}}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Let
\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)\coloneqq\left\{(s_{t},a_{t})\right\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the trajectory generated from by
\pi^{\prime}
within MDP
\mathcal{M}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}
, with initial state set as
s
.
That is,
a_{t}\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s_{t})
,
s_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})
for all
t\geq 0
and
s_{0}=s
.
We know that

\displaystyle V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(s)-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(s_{t},a_{t})\right]-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t})-V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t})\right]\right]-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t+1})-V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t})\right]\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{0})\right]-V^{\pi}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t+1})-V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left[Q^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t},a_{t})-V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t})\right]\right]

(2.14)

\displaystyle\overset{(e)}{=}\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}d^{\pi^{\prime},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{s}(s^{\prime})\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi^{\prime}(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}d^{\pi^{\prime},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{s}(s^{\prime})\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})Q^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d^{\pi^{\prime},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{s}}\langle Q^{\pi}_{r},\pi^{\prime}-\pi\rangle_{s^{\prime}},

where
(a)
follows from (
2.3
);
(b)
follows from moving
V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{0})
outside the summation;

(c)
follows from definition that
s_{0}=s
;
(e)
follows from the definition of
d^{\pi,u_{\pi}}_{s}
in (
1.7
), and the relation between
V^{\pi}_{r}
and
Q^{\pi}_{r}
in (
2.6
).
It remains to show that
(d)
holds.

For any
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma V^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t+1})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a^{\prime})}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}|s_{t},a_{t})V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[c(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma\max_{u(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s_{t},a_{t})V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b^{\prime})}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)}\left[Q^{\pi}_{r}(s_{t},a_{t})\right],

where
(a^{\prime})
follows from the definition of
\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{u}(s)
, and
(b^{\prime})
follows from the property of
Q^{\pi}_{r}
(
2.5

Thus relation
(d)
follows immediately from the prior observation and the linearity of expectation.
∎

By applying Lemma
2.6
, we know that for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
and an optimal robust policy
\pi^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq V^{\pi}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi,u_{\pi}}}\langle Q^{\pi^{*}}_{r},\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle_{s}=\langle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi,\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle,

where
\mathcal{F}_{s}:\Pi\times\Pi\to\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})[s^{\prime},a]=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}d_{s}^{\pi,u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}(s^{\prime},a)~~\forall(s^{\prime},a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

Given the optimality of
\pi^{*}
, we then know that

\langle\mathcal{F}(\pi,\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0

for all
\pi\in\Pi
.
Now for any
\pi\in\Pi
, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we define
\pi_{\alpha}=\alpha\pi+(1-\alpha)\pi^{*}

Substituting
\pi_{\alpha}

\langle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi_{\alpha},\pi^{*}),\pi_{\alpha}-\pi^{*}\rangle=\alpha\langle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi_{\alpha},\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi_{\alpha},\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0

for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
It is straightforward to verify that the visitation measure

is a continuous function of
(\pi,u)
(see proof of Lemma
2.1

On the other hand, note that given (

For simplicity, let us assume that

\pi

Now let
\alpha\downarrow 0

\pi_{\alpha}\to\pi^{*}

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\langle\mathcal{F}_{s}(\pi_{\alpha},\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\langle\mathcal{G}_{s}(\pi^{*}),\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{s}(\pi^{*})[s^{\prime},a]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi^{*}}}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s^{\prime},a),~~\forall(s^{\prime},a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

(2.17)

In view of the discussions in the last two paragraphs, (

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}\left[\mathcal{G}_{s}(\pi^{*})\right],\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0,~~\forall\pi\in\Pi.

(2.18)

Interested readers might find that VI (

Specifically, for a non-robust, discounted finite MDP, the optimal policy satisfies the following VI,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\nu^{*}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{N}_{s}(\pi^{*})\right],

\displaystyle~\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle\geq 0,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{N}_{s}(\pi^{*})[s^{\prime},a]=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}d_{s}^{\pi^{*}}(s^{\prime})Q^{\pi^{*}}(s^{\prime},a),~~\forall(s^{\prime},a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

To solve the non-robust version of VI constructed therein,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\nu^{*}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{N}_{s}(\pi)\right],

\displaystyle\pi-\pi^{*}\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\nu^{*}}\left[V^{\pi}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}(s)\right]=f_{\nu^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\nu^{*}}(\pi^{*})>0,

(

Proof.

\displaystyle\pi(L|S_{a})=1,~\pi^{*}(R|S_{a})=1,~\pi(\cdot|S_{b})=\pi(\cdot|S_{c})=\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal{A}),~\pi^{*}(\cdot|S_{b})=\pi^{*}(\cdot|S_{c})=\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal{A}).

For any policy

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}(1-\gamma)\left(V^{\pi}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}(s)\right)

Before we end our discussion in this section, it is worth mentioning some similarities that robust state-action value function

shares with the (sub)gradient in the convex optimization literature, in the sense that with proper aggregation over states:

(with the quadratic term being identically

(2) its inner product with the direction to the optimal policy is lower bounded by the optimality gap, as shown in Lemma

[

,

.
These observations thus suggest using

.
In the following section, we develop the robust policy mirror descent method that formalizes this intuition, and establish its computational efficiency in finding an optimal policy.

3
Robust Policy Mirror Descent

Algorithm 1

The robust policy mirror descent (RPMD) method

Input:
Initial policy
\pi_{0}
and stepsizes
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
.

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

Update policy:

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{p(\cdot|s)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\eta_{k}\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot),p(\cdot|s)\rangle+D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

end

for

In this section, we introduce the deterministic robust policy mirror descent method (RPMD) for solving (
1.5
).
RPMD assumes access to an oracle that outputs the robust state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}
of a given policy
\pi
.
At each iteration, the RPMD method (Algorithm
1
) updates the policy according to

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{p(\cdot|s)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\eta_{k}\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot),p(\cdot|s)\rangle+D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

(3.1)

Here
D^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)
denotes the Bregman divergence between policy
\pi(\cdot|s)
and
\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)
, defined as

\displaystyle D^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)=w(\pi(\cdot|s))-w(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s))-\langle\partial w(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)),\pi(\cdot|s)-\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)\rangle,

(3.2)

where
w:\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}\to\mathbb{R}
is a strictly convex function, also known as the distance-generating function, and
\partial w(p)
denotes a subgradient of
w
at
p\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
.
Common distance-generating functions include
w(p)=\lVert p\rVert_{2}^{2}
, which induces
D^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)=\lVert\pi(\cdot|s)-\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)\rVert_{2}^{2}
;
and
w(p)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{a}\log p_{a}\coloneqq-\mathrm{Ent}(p)
, which induces

\displaystyle D^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\log\left(\pi(a|s)/\pi^{\prime}(a|s)\right)\coloneqq\mathrm{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)\|\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)).

We will also denote
D_{w}=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\max_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}D^{\pi}_{\pi_{0}}(s)
, which is finite for many practical divergences when
\pi_{0}
corresponds to the uniform policy, including the previously introduced KL-divergence and the squared
\ell_{2}
distance.

It is also worth mentioning that RPMD with KL-divergence yields an equivalent policy update for the natural policy gradient method
[
14
]
applied to the softmax parameterization for the robust MDP objective (
1.5
), by directly applying Lemma
2.5
.
The same equivalence has also been observed for solving the non-robust MDP problem in the literature.

We proceed to establish some general convergence properties of RPMD.
To begin with, the following lemma characterizes each policy update of RPMD.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
p\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
and any
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-p\rangle+D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{p}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s).

(3.3)

Proof.

From the optimality condition of the RPMD update (
3.1
), we have for any
p\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
,

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),p-\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)\rangle+\langle\nabla D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s),p-\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)\rangle\geq 0,

(3.4)

In addition, given the definition of Bregman divergence, we have the following identity

\displaystyle\langle\nabla D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s),p-\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)\rangle=D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{p}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s).

Combining the previous observation with (
3.4
), we immediately obtain the result.
∎

The next lemma then establishes the basic convergence properties of RPMD.

Lemma 3.2
.

At each iteration of RPMD, we have

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k+1})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s),

(3.5)

where
M\coloneqq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u}/\rho\rVert_{\infty}
is finite whenever
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)={\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

By plugging in
p=\pi_{k}
in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle\leq-D^{\pi_{k}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)-D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq 0,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

(3.6)

On the other hand, plugging in
p=\pi^{*}
in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\underbrace{\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle}_{(A)}+\underbrace{\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)-\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)\rangle}_{(B)}+D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s).

(3.7)

We let
u_{k}=u_{\pi_{k}}
denote the worst-case uncertainty of policy
\pi_{k}
for any
k\geq 0
.

To handle term
(A)
, note that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r},\pi_{k+1}-\pi_{k}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\tfrac{d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s)}{1-\gamma}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle\leq 0,

(3.8)

where
(a)
is due to Lemma
2.6
;
(b)
is due to (
3.6
);

(c)
is due to (
3.6
), and the observation that
d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s)\geq(1-\gamma)
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

To handle term
(B)
, we make use of Lemma
2.7
.
Specifically, we obtain from (
2.26
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}\left[\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r},\pi_{k}-\pi^{*}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}\right]\geq(1-\gamma)\left(V_{r}^{\pi}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)\geq 0.

(3.9)

Hence by aggregating (
3.7
) across different states with weights set as
d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}
, and making use of (
3.8
) and (
3.9
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}\left[\eta_{k}\left(V_{r}^{\pi_{k+1}}(s^{\prime})-V_{r}^{\pi_{k}}(s^{\prime})\right)\right]+(1-\gamma)\eta_{k}\left(V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s^{\prime}).

By further taking expectation with respect to
s\sim\rho
, and making use of
V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)\leq V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)
given (
3.8
), and the definition of
M\coloneqq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u}/\rho\rVert_{\infty}<\infty
,

\displaystyle M\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{k+1})-f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})\right]+(1-\gamma)\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(x^{*})\right]\leq\tfrac{1}{\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s),

which after simple arrangement, yields the desired claim.
∎

3.1
Convergence with Increasing Stepsizes

We proceed to show that by applying exponentially increasing stepsizes, RPMD achieves linear convergence in the optimality gap.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose the stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{k}\right\}
satisfy

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{k-1}\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{-1}M^{\prime},~~\forall k\geq 1,

(3.10)

where
M^{\prime}=\sup_{u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u}/d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u^{\prime}}\rVert_{\infty}
is finite whenever
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)={\mathcal{S}}
.
Then for any iteration
k
, RPMD produces policy
\pi_{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{D_{w}}{M\eta_{0}}.

Proof.

By recursively applying inequality (
3.5
) from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{0}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{0}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)

\displaystyle~~+\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left[\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-t-1}\tfrac{1}{\eta_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-t}\tfrac{1}{\eta_{t-1}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t-1}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\right]}_{(C)}.

Given the definition of stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{k}\right\}
in (
3.10
) and
M^{\prime}
, it should be clear that term
(C)\leq 0
.
In conclusion, we obtain that whenever (
3.10
) holds,

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{0}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{0}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{D_{w}}{M\eta_{0}}.

The proof is then completed.
∎

It should be noted that whenever
\mathcal{U}=\left\{u\right\}
is a singleton, solving the robust MDP
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{U}}
is equivalent to solving a non-robust MDP.
Then we have
M^{\prime}=1
, and

M=\lVert d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u}/\rho\rVert_{\infty}
reduces to the mismatch coefficient defined in the analysis of policy gradient methods for solving nominal MDP
[

,
1
,
13
]
.
In this case, RPMD admits a simple learning rate scaling rule given by
\eta_{t}\geq\gamma^{-1}\eta_{t-1}
, and the obtained convergence rate for RPMD matches exactly the fastest existing rate of convergence of first-order methods for solving the nominal MDP
[
48
,
25
,
21
]
.

The obtained linear convergence in Theorem
3.1
seems to be new among the existing literature of first-order policy-based methods applied to solving the robust MDPs.
In addition, although the constant
M^{\prime}
is in general unknown, one can simply provide a pessimistic upper bound of
M^{\prime}
.
In particular, for
\rho=\mathrm{Unif}({\mathcal{S}})
, it suffices to take
M^{\prime}=\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert/(1-\gamma)
.
Finally, the increasing-stepsize schemes are not the only ones that can certify the convergence of RPMD.
In the next subsection, we will establish the convergence of RPMD with a constant-stepsize scheme, which allows one to completely avoid the estimation of
M^{\prime}
.

3.2
Convergence with Constant Stepsizes

In this subsection, we establish the convergence of RPMD with constant stepsize.
In particular, for RPMD with Euclidean Bregman divergence and a constant stepsize of
\eta
, we establish an
\mathcal{O}(\max\left\{1/k,1/\sqrt{\eta k}\right\})
rate of convergence for arbitrary rectangular uncertainty set.
For RPMD with a general class of Bregman divergences, we also establish a similar convergence rate for rectangular uncertainty set that satisfies the so-called relative strong convexity, with details deferred to Appendix
B
.

To proceed, we first establish the following simple fact on the asymptotic stationarity of the RPMD iterates.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
k\geq 1
, the iterates in RPMD with constant stepsizes
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
satisfy

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)+D^{\pi_{t+1}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\right)\leq V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s).

(3.11)

Proof.

Given (
3.8
) and (
3.6
), we obtain that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)\leq-\tfrac{1}{\eta}D^{\pi_{k}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{\eta}D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq 0,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Summing up the prior relation from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)+D^{\pi_{t+1}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\right)\leq V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)\leq V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s).

The proof is then completed.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.2
, we are able to establish the following convergence characterization for RPMD with Euclidean Bregman divergence, when adopting any constant-stepsize scheme.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
w(\cdot)=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}^{2}
be the distance-generating function, and
\eta_{t}=\eta
for all
t\geq 0
and any
\eta>0
, then at each iteration, RPMD outputs policy
\pi_{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\leq\tfrac{M}{(1-\gamma)k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\sqrt{\tfrac{18\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}}{\eta k(1-\gamma)^{3}}},

(3.12)

where
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

By summing up inequality (
3.5
) from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)\right)

Note that
D^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\pi}(s)=\lVert\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)-\pi(\cdot|s)\rVert_{2}^{2}
, and hence

\displaystyle D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)\leq\sqrt{18D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)},

where we use the fact that for any
a,b,c\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(a_{i}-b_{i})^{2}-\sum_{i}(a_{i}-c_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert b+c-2a\rVert_{2}\lVert b-c\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{18}\lVert b-c\rVert_{2}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{\sqrt{18}}{M\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}\sqrt{D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)}.

To proceed, we will make use of Lemma
3.3
, which gives

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\sqrt{D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)}\right)^{2}\leq k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)\leq k\eta\left(V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)\leq\tfrac{k\eta}{1-\gamma},~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},

which combined with the previous inequality, gives

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}{M}\sqrt{\tfrac{18k}{\eta(1-\gamma)}}.

By (
3.8
), we know that
f_{\rho}(\pi_{t+1})\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})
for all
t\geq 0
.
The desired claim then follows immediately after combining this observation with the above inequality.
∎

It should be noted that the dependence on the size of the state space in the second term of (
3.12
) can be simply eliminated by using a large stepsize.
Theorem
3.2
states that to find an
\epsilon
-optimal policy using constant stepsizes RPMD with Euclidean divergence,
the iteration complexity is bounded by
\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{1/\epsilon,1/(\eta\epsilon^{2})\right\}\right)
, which reduces to
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)
when choosing
\eta=\Theta(1/\epsilon)
.
To the best of our knowledge, the fastest existing rate of convergence of Euclidean-divergence based first-order methods for solving robust MDP is
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{3})
with
\eta=\Theta(\epsilon)

[
46
]
, which studies a more restrictive subclass of polyhedral rectangular uncertainty set, and further requires a delicate smoothing technique and selecting the best policy among historical policy iterates.
In contrast, the RPMD comes with a much stronger convergence guarantee for general rectangular uncertainty sets, while at the same time enjoying greater algorithmic simplicity.

4
Stochastic Robust Policy Mirror Descent

In this section, we extend the deterministic RPMD method to the stochastic settings, where the exact information of the robust state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}
is not available.
The stochastic robust policy mirror descent (SRPMD) instead uses the stochastic estimator
Q^{\pi,\xi}_{r}
to update the policy, where
\xi
denotes the samples used for the construction of the stochastic estimator.

Algorithm 2

The stochastic robust policy mirror descent (SRPMD) method

Input:
Initial policy
\pi_{0}
and stepsizes
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
.

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

Update policy:

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{p(\cdot|s)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\eta_{k}\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}(s,\cdot),p(\cdot|s)\rangle+D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

end

for

At iteration
k
, given a stochastic estimator
Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}
, the SRPMD method (Algorithm
2
) updates the policy according to

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{p(\cdot|s)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\eta_{k}\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}(s,\cdot),p(\cdot|s)\rangle+D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

(4.1)

The convergence of SRPMD assumes the following noise condition on the noisy estimate
\{Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}\}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k}}\lVert Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}-Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}\rVert_{\infty}\leq e_{k}.

(4.2)

We will also define
\delta_{k}=Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}-Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Similar to Lemma
3.2
, we first establish the following generic convergence property of the SRPMD method.

Lemma 4.1
.

At each iteration of SRPMD, we have

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k+1})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty},

(4.3)

where
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

First, following the same lines as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-p\rangle+D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{p}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s).

(4.4)

Thus by letting
p=\pi_{k}
in (
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle\leq-D^{\pi_{k}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)-D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\leq 0.

(4.5)

On the other hand, by plugging in
p=\pi^{*}
in the above relation,
we obtain

\displaystyle\underbrace{\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle}_{(A)}+\underbrace{\eta_{k}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)-\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)\rangle}_{(B)}+D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s).

(4.6)

Let
u_{k}=u_{\pi_{k}}
denote the worst-case uncertainty of policy
\pi_{k}
.
To handle term
(A)
, note that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}}\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r},\pi_{k+1}-\pi_{k}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\tfrac{d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s^{\prime})}{1-\gamma}\left[\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}(s^{\prime},\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\rangle+\langle\delta_{k}(s^{\prime},\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s^{\prime})\left[\langle Q_{r}^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}(s^{\prime},\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\rangle+2\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\tfrac{d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s)}{1-\gamma}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle+\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}(s,\cdot),\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\rangle+\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty},

(4.7)

where
(a)
uses Lemma
2.6
,

(b)
uses (
4.5
), and
(c)
uses again (
4.5
) and the fact that
d^{\pi_{k+1},u_{k+1}}_{s}(s)\geq 1-\gamma
.
Hence we obtain from inequality
(c)
in the last relation that

\displaystyle(A)\geq\eta_{k}\left(V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)\right)-\tfrac{2\eta_{k}}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}.

(4.8)

For term
(B)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}\left[\langle Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r},\pi_{k}-\pi^{*}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}\left[\langle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{r},\pi_{k}-\pi^{*}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}+\langle\delta_{k},\pi_{k}-\pi^{*}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\left(V_{r}^{\pi_{k}}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)-2\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty},

(4.9)

where the inequality follows from (
3.9
).
Hence by combining (
4.6
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}\left(V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s^{\prime})-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s^{\prime})-\tfrac{2\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}}{1-\gamma}\right)+(1-\gamma)\left(V_{r}^{\pi}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)+\tfrac{1}{\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s^{\prime})-\tfrac{1}{\eta_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s^{\prime})+2\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}.

By using (
4.7
), the definition of
M
, and further taking
s\sim\rho
in the above relation, we conclude that

\displaystyle M\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}\left[V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)\right]+(1-\gamma)\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}\left(V_{r}^{\pi}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)+\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{k}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)+\tfrac{2(1-\gamma)+2M}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}.

The claim follows immediately after simple rearrangement to the above inequality.
∎

By specializing Lemma
4.1
with exponentially increasing stepsizes, we obtain the following linear convergence of SRPMD up to a noise level determined by the noise in the stochastic estimation
Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose the stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{k}\right\}
in SRPMD satisfy

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{k-1}\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{-1}M^{\prime},~~\forall k\geq 1,

(4.10)

where
M^{\prime}
is defined as in Theorem
3.1
.
Then for any iteration
k
, SRPMD produces policy
\pi_{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right]\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{D_{w}}{M\eta_{0}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-t-1}e_{t},

where
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
.
In particular, if we have
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k}}\lVert Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq e
for all
k\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right]\leq\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\left(1-\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\right)^{k-1}\tfrac{D_{w}}{M\eta_{0}}+\tfrac{4Me}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}.

(4.11)

Proof.

The proof follows from similar lines as the proof of Theorem
3.1
, except we will make use of Lemma
4.1
instead of Lemma
3.2
, and taking expectation with respect to
\left\{\xi_{t}\right\}
in the end.
∎

In view of (
4.11
) in Theorem
4.1
, the last-iterate of SRPMD converges linearly up to the noise-level of

\mathcal{O}(Me/(1-\gamma)^{2})
, where
e
characterizes the quality of the estimated robust state-action value function.

We then proceed to establish the convergence of SRPMD with a constant stepsize, by focusing on the Euclidean divergence considered in Section
3.2
.
Similar to Lemma
3.3
, we first make the following simple observations regarding the policies generated by SRPMD.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
k\geq 1
, the iterates in SRPMD with constant stepsizes
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
satisfy

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)+D^{\pi_{t+1}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\right)\leq V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)+\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Given (
4.5
) and inequality
(c)
in (
4.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\eta}\left(D^{\pi_{k}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)+D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\right)\leq V^{\pi_{k}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi_{k+1}}_{r}(s)+\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\lVert\delta_{k}\rVert_{\infty},~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Summing up the prior relation from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Combining Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
, we are able to establish the following convergence characterization for SRPMD with Euclidean Bregman divergence, when adopting any constant-stepsize scheme.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
w(\cdot)=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}^{2}
be the distance-generating function, and
\eta_{k}=\eta
for some fixed
\eta>0
and
k\geq 0
, then at any iteration
k\geq 1
, SRPMD produces policy
\pi_{R}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{R})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right]\leq\tfrac{M}{(1-\gamma)k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\tfrac{4M}{(1-\gamma)^{2}k}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}e_{t}+\sqrt{\tfrac{18\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}}{k\eta(1-\gamma)^{3}}}\sqrt{1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}e_{t}},

(4.13)

where
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
, and
R
is a random integer uniformly sampled from
\left\{1\ldots k\right\}
.
In particular, if we have
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k}}\lVert Q^{\pi_{k},\xi_{k}}_{r}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq e
for all
k\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{R})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right]\leq\tfrac{M}{(1-\gamma)k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\tfrac{4Me}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{18\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}}{\eta k(1-\gamma)^{3}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{36\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}e}{\eta(1-\gamma)^{3}}}.

(4.14)

Proof.

By summing up inequality (
4.3
) from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)\right)+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}.

Following the same lines as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we can obtain from the above relation that

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle~~~+\tfrac{\sqrt{18}}{M\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}\sqrt{D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)}.

(4.15)

From Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\sqrt{D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)}\right)^{2}\leq k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t+1}}(s)

\displaystyle\leq k\eta\left(V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)+\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{k\eta}{1-\gamma}\left(1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\right),~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},

which combined with (
4.15
), gives

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}+\tfrac{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}{M}\sqrt{\tfrac{18k}{\eta(1-\gamma)}}\sqrt{1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}}.

Finally, given the definition of
R\sim\mathrm{Unif}\left\{1\ldots k\right\}
, we take expectation with respect to
\left\{\xi_{t}\right\}
and
R
, and conclude that

\displaystyle\tfrac{(1-\gamma)k}{M}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\rho}(\pi_{R})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right]\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{4}{1-\gamma}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}e_{t}+\tfrac{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}{M}\sqrt{\tfrac{18k}{\eta(1-\gamma)}}\sqrt{1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}e_{t}},

where in the last inequality we also uses that fact that
\sqrt{1+x}
is concave and hence

\mathbb{E}\sqrt{1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\lVert\delta_{t}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{1+2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}e_{t}}
.
Hence the desired claim (
4.13
) follows immediately after simple rearrangement.
In addition, (
4.14
) follows from
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
.
∎

Given (
4.14
), Theorem
4.2
states that for large enough constant stepsize,
SRPMD with Euclidean divergence converges at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
, until the noise level of
\mathcal{O}(Me/(1-\gamma)^{2})
is reached.

Our discussions so far have assumed an oracle that outputs the stochastic estimation of the state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}
satisfying certain error condition (
4.2
).
In the next section, we present a stochastic policy evaluation method that can in turn certify this error condition, and consequently determine the total sample complexity of SRPMD.

5
Sample Complexity of Stochastic Robust Policy Mirror Descent

In this section, we discuss an online method of estimating the robust state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{r}
for a given policy
\pi
, by using samples
\xi
collected during the interaction with the nominal environment
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{N}}
.
By incorporating this online estimation method into the previously discussed SRPMD methods, we are able to learn a robust policy without the need of training policy within its worst-case environment.
Consequently, we will also establish the sample complexity of the SRPMD methods with different stepsize schemes discussed in Section
4
.

To facilitate our presentation, let us define operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}\to\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
by

\displaystyle F(x)={\rm diag}(\nu^{\pi})\left({\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(x)-x\right)+x,

(5.1)

where
\nu^{\pi}
denotes the stationary state-action pair distribution induced by policy
\pi
within the nominal environment
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{N}}
, and operator
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}\to\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
is defined by

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(x)\right](s,a)

\displaystyle=c(s,a)+\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})x(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=c(s,a)+\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{s^{\prime},a^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{u}^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)x(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}),

where in the second equality we denote

We will also write the previous definition in matrix form as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(x)=c+\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}x.

Clearly, given the rectangularity of uncertainty set in (

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(x)\right](s,a)

\displaystyle=c(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{N}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})x(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})+\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}u(s^{\prime}|s,a)\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})x(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=c(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{N}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})x(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})+\gamma\sigma_{\mathcal{U}_{s,a}}(M(\pi,x)),

(5.2)

where

is defined as
[M(\pi,x)](s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)x(s,a)
,
and
\sigma_{X}
denotes the support function of set

Given (

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(x)-{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(y)\rVert_{\infty}=\gamma\lVert\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}x-\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}y\rVert_{\infty}\leq\gamma\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}x-\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}y\rVert_{\infty}\leq\gamma\lVert x-y\rVert_{\infty},

{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}

[

In comparison, as shown in Proposition

6
Concluding Remarks

In this manuscript, we develop the robust policy mirror descent method and its stochastic variants for controlling Markov decision process with uncertain transition kernels.
Our established iteration and sample complexity seem to be new in the literature of policy-space first-order methods applied to this problem class.
We highlight a few future directions worthy of continuing explorations from our perspective.

First, the analysis of constant stepsize RPMD yields an additional dependence on the size of the state space.
Though this dependence can be bypassed with a large stepsize, removing this dependence completely remains not only as a theoretical interest, but can also potentially help improving the sample complexity of the SRPMD methods.

Second, the current analysis of SRPMD uses only a single characterization on the noise of the stochastic estimate (see (
4.2
)), which contrasts with more delicate approach of separating bias and variance for solving standard MDPs
[
21
]
.
As a result, it is unclear whether the dependence of obtained sample complexities on the effective horizon is optimal.
The reason for our simplified treatment is due to the fact that the robust TD method in Section
5
does not have a separate characterizations for the bias and variance in the obtained stochastic estimate given the nonlinearity of operator
F
in (
5.1
).
This hinder the application of techniques for standard MDPs adopted in
[
21
]
, where the author heavily exploits the fact that bias converges much faster than the variance, given the linearity of the TD operator.
It is even unclear that whether one can separate bias and variance in estimating the robust state-action value function, which by itself would be an highly interesting question.
Another question related to the robust TD method is to relax Assumption
1
, which requires handling the rarely visited state-action pair in evaluating the robust state-action value function.
Techniques for addressing this problem in solving standard MDPs have been recently discussed in
[
23
]
.

Lastly, it would also be rewarding to develop RPMD variants for solving robust MDP beyond the
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
-rectangular uncertainty sets considered in this manuscript.

\displaystyle\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}}\mathbf{e}_{s}^{\top}v~~\mathrm{s.t.}~~v\leq{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v,

(A.1)

where
\mathbf{e}_{s}
denotes the one-hot vector with the entry corresponding to

Moreover, we have

.
It is also useful to make note of the following properties of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}
:
(1)
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}
is monotone, in the sense that
v\leq v^{\prime}\Rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v\leq{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{\prime}

(2)
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}
is a
\gamma
-contraction in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
-norm, with the unique fixed-point being
V^{\pi}_{u}
.
Since both are trivial to verify, we omit their proofs here.

By varying the uncertainty
u\in\mathcal{U}
, for a fixed state

, we claim that the robust value
V^{\pi}_{r}(s)
satisfies
V^{\pi}_{r}(s)=v^{*}(s)
, where
(v^{*},u^{*})
is any solution of the following program

\displaystyle\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert},u\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbf{e}_{s}^{\top}v~\mathrm{s.t.}~v\leq{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v.

(A.2)

To see this, note that (1)
(V^{\pi}_{u},u)
is a feasible solution for (
A.2
) for any

;
(2) any optimal solution

must satisfy

\displaystyle v^{*}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmax}}_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}}~\mathbf{e}_{s}^{\top}v~\mathrm{s.t.}~v\leq{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u^{*}}v,

which implies
v^{*}(s)=V^{\pi}_{u^{*}}(s)
given (
A.1
).
Combining these two observations, it holds that

V^{\pi}_{u^{*}}(s)=v^{*}(s)\geq V^{\pi}_{u}(s)
for any
u\in\mathcal{U}
.
Consequently,

v^{*}(s)
is the robust value of
\pi
at state
s
, and

u^{*}
is the corresponding worst-case uncertainty when we start from state
s
.

We proceed to show that formulation (
A.2
) is equivalent to the following

\displaystyle\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}}\mathbf{e}_{s}^{\top}v~\mathrm{s.t.}~v\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v\right\}=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v\right\},

(A.3)

where operation
\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v\right\}
is the element-wise supremum, which is well-defined due to the rectangularity of
\mathcal{U}
. The equality holds since
\mathcal{U}
is compact and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v
is continuous in
u
.

To establish equivalence between (
A.2
) and (
A.3
).
Note that for any feasible solution
(v,u)
to (
A.2
),

must also be feasible to (
A.3
).
Hence we obtain
\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust_concise}\geq\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust}
.
On the other hand, suppose
v^{*}
is a solution of (
A.3
),
given the compactness and the rectangularity of
\mathcal{U}
,
we know that there exists
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}
, such that

v^{*}\leq{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u^{*}}v^{*}\equiv\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{*}\right\}
.
Thus
(v^{*},u^{*})
is a feasible solution to (
A.2
), and
\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust}\geq\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust_concise}
, which further implies
\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust_concise}=\mathrm{Opt}\eqref{opt_formulation_robust}
.
Moreover, in this case,

(v^{*},u^{*})
is an optimal solution of (
A.2
).
To summarize, we make the following observations:

•

Observation 1.
If
v^{*}
is a solution of (
A.3
), then
v^{*}(s)=V^{\pi}_{r}(s)
;

•

Observation 2.
If in addition,

{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u^{*}}v^{*}=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{*}\right\}
, then

u^{*}
is the corresponding worst-case uncertainty if we start from state
s
.

Now define operator
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}\to\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}
as
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}v=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v
, then
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
is monotone since
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}
is monotone for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
.
We proceed to show that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
is also a
\gamma
-contraction in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
-norm.

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}v-{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}v^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}=\lVert\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v-\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v-{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\gamma\lVert v-v^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty},

where the first inequality uses
\lVert\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}f(u,v)-\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}f(u,v^{\prime})\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lVert f(u,v)-f(u,v^{\prime})\rVert_{\infty}
for any vector-valued function
f
,
and the second inequality uses the contraction property of operator
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}
for any
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

Fixing the state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, given a solution
v^{*}
to (
A.3
), we claim that

, where
v^{\prime}

is the unique fixed point of operator

. That is,

v^{\prime}={\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}v^{\prime}\equiv\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u}v^{\prime}
.
To see this,
note that

\displaystyle v^{*}\overset{(a)}{\leq}\lim_{t\to\infty}\left({\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}\right)^{(t)}v^{*}\overset{(b)}{=}v^{\prime},

(A.4)

where
(a)
follows from applying the constraint of (
A.3
) repeatedly to
v^{*}
, together with the monotonicity of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
;

(b)
follows from
v^{\prime}
being the unique fixed point of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
.
In addition,
v^{\prime}
is clearly feasible to (
A.3
).
This in turn implies that if
v^{*}(s)<v^{\prime}(s)
, then
v^{*}
would not be an optimal solution to (
A.3
).
Combining this with (
A.4
), we must have
v^{*}(s)=v^{\prime}(s)
.
Note that from
Observation 1
, this in turn implies
V^{\pi}_{r}(s)=v^{\prime}(s)
.
Since the state
s
can be chosen arbitrarily, we obtain
V^{\pi}_{r}=v^{\prime}
, and (
2.1
) follows immediately.

It remains to show the existence of a single worst-case uncertainty
u^{\prime}
regardless of the initial state.
To this end, note that our previous discussion have shown that
v^{\prime}
is a solution of (
A.3
), regardless of the initial state
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
In addition, one can pick
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
v^{\prime}={\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}v^{\prime}={\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}_{u^{\prime}}v^{\prime}
, given the fact that
v^{\prime}
is the fixed point of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
and
\mathcal{U}
being compact and rectangular.
Then given
Observations 1 and 2
,
(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})
is an optimal solution of (
A.2
), regardless of the initial state.
Consequently,
\mu^{\prime}
is a worst-case uncertainty regardless of the initial state, and we obtain (
2.2
).
The proof is then completed.
∎

\displaystyle\liminf_{\pi^{\prime}_{e}\to\pi_{e},\pi_{e}^{\prime}\neq\pi_{e},\pi_{e}^{\prime}\in\Pi_{e}}\left(g(\pi^{\prime}_{e})-g(\pi_{e})-\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}d_{\rho}^{\pi,u_{\pi}}(s)Q^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}(s,a)\left(\pi^{\prime}_{e}(a|s)-\pi_{e}(a|s)\right)\right)\big/\lVert\pi_{e}^{\prime}-\pi_{e}\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\pi^{\prime}\to\pi,\pi^{\prime}\neq\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi^{\prime})-f_{\rho}(\pi)-\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}d_{\rho}^{\pi,u_{\pi}}(s)Q^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}(s,a)\left(\pi^{\prime}(a|s)-\pi(a|s)\right)\right)\big/\lVert\pi^{\prime}-\pi\rVert_{2}\geq 0,

where the equality follows from the construction of
g
and
\pi_{e}
, and the inequality follows from Lemma
2.2
.

By combining observations from the previous two paragraphs, it is clear that the set of non-Fréchet differentiable points of

form a zero-measure set in
\mathcal{U}
, when taking the
(\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert-1)\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
The Fréchet subgradient at any point
\pi_{e}
is given by
(
2.12
).
Moreover, at any Fréchet differentiable point
\pi_{e}
of
g
, we conclude that its Fréchet gradient

is the only element in its Fréchet subdifferential (Proposition 1.1,
[
18
]
), which is given by (
2.12
).

It remains to show that for any Fréchet differentiable point
\pi_{e}\in\mathrm{Int}_{\mathcal{U}}(\Pi_{e})
of
g
,
with its derivative denoted by
\nabla g(\pi)
,
the corresponding

is also a differentiable point of
f_{\rho}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
.
To see this, note that

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi^{\prime})-f_{\rho}(\pi)-\langle\nabla g(\pi_{e}),\pi^{\prime}-\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\pi^{\prime}_{e})-g(\pi_{e})-\langle\nabla g(\pi_{e}),\pi^{\prime}_{e}-\pi_{e}\rangle={{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}(\lVert\pi^{\prime}_{e}-\pi_{e}\rVert)={{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}(\lVert\pi^{\prime}-\pi\rVert_{2}),~~\forall\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ReInt}(\Pi).

Thus we conclude that

is also differentiable at
\pi

defined in Definition
2.1

at
\pi_{e}
, given by (
2.12

∎

\delta

Appendix B
RPMD with a General Class of Bregman Divergences

In this section, we show that for a general class of Bregman divergences, whenever the set of uncertain transitions
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
form a relatively strongly convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}
, then RPMD converges with any constant stepsize for a general class of divergences.
To proceed, we first recall the following definition of strongly convex sets.

Definition B.1

(Strongly Convex Set)
.

A set
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called strongly convex with respect to
R>0
, if

\mathcal{C}
is bounded, and for any
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
, any
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

\mathcal{B}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y),\delta)\subset\mathcal{C}
,
where
\delta=\tfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2R}\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
Here
\mathcal{B}(z,r)=\left\{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert z^{\prime}-z\rVert_{2}\leq r\right\}
denotes the ball centered around
z
with radius
r
.

It is well known that any level set of a strongly convex function is a strongly convex set
[
44
]
.
In particular, any full-dimensional ellipsoid is a strongly convex set.
A strongly convex convex set also satisfies the following useful property.

Lemma B.1

(Theorem 1,
[
44
]
)
.

For any set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is strongly convex with respect to
R>0
,
let
\mathrm{Bd}(C)
denote its boundary, and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
denote its normal cone at
x\in X
.
Then for any pair of vectors
(x_{i},p_{i})
,
i=1,2
, where
x_{i}\in\mathrm{Bd}(\mathcal{C})
and
p_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i})
with
\lVert p_{i}\rVert_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert x_{1}-x_{2}\rVert_{2}\leq R\lVert p_{1}-p_{2}\rVert.

(B.1)

Note that by definition, a strongly convex set must have full dimension, which can not be satisfied by
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
as
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
lies in a lower dimensional hyperplane.
Given this observation, we define the following strong convexity of a set
{\mathcal{S}}
restricted to its affine span.

Definition B.2

(Relatively Strongly Convex Set)
.

A set
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called relatively strongly convex with respect to
R>0
, if

\mathcal{C}
is bounded, and for any
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
, any
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

\mathcal{B}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y),\delta)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathcal{C}
,
where
\delta=\tfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2R}\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
Here
\mathcal{B}(z,r)=\left\{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert z^{\prime}-z\rVert_{2}\leq r\right\}
denotes the ball centered around
z
with radius
r
,
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{Aff}(\mathcal{C})
denotes the affine subspace spanned by
\mathcal{C}
.

Similar to Lemma
B.1
, the relatively strongly convex set also satisfies an analogy to (
B.1
).

Lemma B.2
.

For any set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is relatively strongly convex with respect to
R>0
,
let
\mathrm{ReBd}(C)
denote its relative boundary, and
\mathcal{N}^{r}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
denote its normal cone at
x\in X
restricted to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{r}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=\left\{z\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathcal{C}):(y-x)^{\top}z\leq 0,\forall y\in\mathcal{C}\right\},

where
\mathrm{Lin}(\mathcal{C})
denotes the unique linear subspace defining
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}
, which satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}=x+\mathrm{Lin}(C)
for any
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then for any pair of vectors
(x_{i},p_{i})
,
i=1,2
, where
x_{i}\in\mathrm{ReBd}(\mathcal{C})
and
p_{i}\in\mathcal{N}^{r}_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i})
with
\lVert p_{i}\rVert_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert x_{1}-x_{2}\rVert_{2}\leq R\lVert p_{1}-p_{2}\rVert.

(B.2)

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{N}^{r}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
is invariant to translation of set
C
.
Moreover, the both sides of statement (
B.2
) are also invariant to translation of set
C
, and hence we can without loss of generality assume
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{C}
, and hence
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{Lin}(\mathcal{C})
.
The rest of the claim follows directly from a change of coordinates and working in the lower-dimensional space
\mathrm{Lin}(\mathcal{C})
, in which we can apply Lemma
B.1
.
∎

We then show that if set of uncertain transitions
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
is a relatively strongly convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}
with dimension
\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert-1
, then the worst-case transition kernel
\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}
is
uniquely defined, and is
also Lipschitz continuous with respect to policy
\pi
.
En route, we will make use of the following simple fact.

Lemma B.3
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x,y\neq\mathbf{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\tfrac{x}{\lVert x\rVert_{2}}-\tfrac{y}{\lVert y\rVert_{2}}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}/\min\left\{\lVert x\rVert_{2},\lVert y\rVert_{2}\right\}.

(B.3)

Proof.

Since both side of claim (
B.3
) are symmetric with respect to
(x,y)
, we can without loss of generality assume
\lVert y\rVert_{2}\geq\lVert x\rVert_{2}
.
Note that
\lVert\tfrac{x}{\lVert x\rVert_{2}}-\tfrac{y}{\lVert y\rVert_{2}}\rVert_{2}=\tfrac{1}{\lVert x\rVert_{2}}\lVert x-\tfrac{y}{\lVert y\rVert_{2}}\lVert x\rVert_{2}\rVert_{2}
.
We make the following observations.

Denote
\mathrm{Proj}_{y}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the projection operator onto
\mathrm{span}(\left\{y\right\})
.
We know that

\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)=\delta_{x}y
for
\delta_{x}=\langle x,y\rangle/\lVert y\rVert_{2}^{2}
and
\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)\leq\lVert x\rVert_{2}
.
On the other hand, we have
x^{\prime}\coloneqq\tfrac{\lVert x\rVert_{2}}{\lVert y\rVert_{2}}y=\delta_{x}^{\prime}y
with
\lVert x^{\prime}\rVert_{2}=\lVert x\rVert_{2}
,
hence
\delta_{x}^{\prime}\geq\lvert\delta_{x}\rvert
.
Since
x^{\prime}\in\mathrm{span}(\left\{y\right\})
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert x-x^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert x-\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)-x^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert x-\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}+(\delta_{x}^{\prime}-\delta_{x})^{2}\lVert y\rVert_{2}^{2}.

On the other hand, since
\delta_{x}^{\prime}\leq 1
, we also have

\displaystyle\lVert x-\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}+(\delta_{x}^{\prime}-\delta_{x})^{2}\lVert y\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert x-\mathrm{Proj}_{y}(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1-\delta_{x})^{2}\lVert y\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Hence, we obtain
\lVert x-x^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
In conclusion, since we have assumed
\lVert x\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert y\rVert_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\tfrac{x}{\lVert x\rVert_{2}}-\tfrac{y}{\lVert y\rVert_{2}}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}/\min\left\{\lVert x\rVert_{2},\lVert y\rVert_{2}\right\}.

The proof is then completed.
∎

The next lemma provides a sufficient condition the uniqueness of the worst-case transition kernel.

Lemma B.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
is relatively strongly convex with respect to
R_{s,a}>0
for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})=\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert-1
.
Let
r_{*}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}
, where
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{Lin}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}:\mathbf{1}^{\top}x=0\right\}
.
Suppose
r_{*}>0
, then for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
, the worst-case environment
\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}
defined in (
2.2
) is unique, and

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(\cdot|s,a)\rVert_{2}\leq\tfrac{R_{s,a}}{r_{*}}\lVert V^{\pi}_{r}-V_{r}^{\pi^{\prime}}\rVert_{2},~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

(B.4)

The result of Lemma
B.4
clearly hinges upon the quantity
r_{*}>0
.
We will provide a verifiable necessary and sufficient condition that certifies this requirement.

Proof of Lemma
B.4
.

We first recall from (
2.2
) that for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
, the worse case transition
\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{u(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathcal{U}_{s,a}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V^{\pi}_{r}(s^{\prime})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{s,a}}p^{\top}V^{\pi}_{r},~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}.

(B.5)

It is worth mentioning that the solution to (
B.5
) remains unchanged when we shift
V^{\pi}_{r}
by
\delta\mathbf{1}
for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}
denotes the all-one vector.
To see this, note that

\displaystyle p^{\top}(V^{\pi}_{r}+\delta\mathbf{1})=p^{\top}V^{\pi}_{r}+\delta

(B.6)

due to
p\in\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
,
and hence shifting
V^{\pi}_{r}
only changes the objective by a constant
\delta
for every feasible
p
.
Let
\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}\to\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}
denote the projection operator onto
\mathcal{H}
, then
given observation (
B.6
), we must have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{s,a}}p^{\top}\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r}),~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},~\forall\pi\in\Pi.

(B.7)

Let
\mathrm{ReBd}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})
denote the relative boundary of
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
.
We claim that for any solution
p^{*}
of (
B.5
) (or equivalently (
B.7
)), we must have
p^{*}\in\mathrm{ReBd}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})
.
Suppose not, and
p^{*}
is in the relative interior of
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
, then there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathcal{B}(p^{*},\delta)\cap\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
.
Now it is immediate to see that
p^{*}+\delta\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})/\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}
is a strictly better solution than
p^{*}
unless

\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})=\mathbf{0}
, which can not happen since
r_{*}>0
.

We proceed to show that
p^{*}
is indeed unique.
Suppose not, and
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
are two solutions of (
B.5
).
From the relative strong convexity of set
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
, we know that for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
we have
p_{\lambda}=\lambda p_{1}+(1-\lambda)p_{2}
in the relative interior of
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
,
which contradicts with the previously established fact that any solution of (

) is in
\mathrm{ReBd}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})
.

By reading the optimality condition of the (
B.7
), we have

\displaystyle(p-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a))^{\top}\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\leq 0,~~\forall p\in\mathcal{P}_{s,a}.

(B.8)

Hence we immediately see that
\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)\in\mathrm{ReBd}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})
, and
\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{P}_{s,a}}(\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a))
.
Then we can apply Lemma
B.1
and obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(\cdot|s,a)\rVert_{2}\leq R_{s,a}\lVert\overline{V}^{\pi}_{r}-\overline{V}_{r}^{\pi^{\prime}}\rVert_{2},~~\forall(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A},

where
\overline{V}^{\pi}_{r}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})/\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2},~\overline{V}^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r})/\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r})\rVert_{2}
.
Now applying Lemma
B.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(\cdot|s,a)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq R_{s,a}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})-\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r})\rVert_{2}/r_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{R_{s,a}}{r_{*}}\lVert V^{\pi}_{r}-V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}\rVert_{2},

where

,
and the last inequality uses the non-expansiveness of the projection operator.
∎

It should be noted that the conditions on the relative strong convexity of
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
and
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})=\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert-1
are readily satisfied by many common uncertainty sets.
For instance, one can take
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
as the intersection of a full-dimensional ellipsoid with

(i.e., ellipsoidal uncertainty
[

]
).
On the other hand, Lemma

relies on the key condition that
r_{*}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}>0
.
We next provide a simple necessary and sufficient condition that certifies
r_{*}>0
.

Lemma B.5
.

Suppose the cost function

satisfies
\cap_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a),\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\right]=\emptyset
, then we must have

r_{*}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}>0
, where
\mathcal{H}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}:\mathbf{1}^{\top}x=0\right\}
.
The converse of the statement is also true.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
”:
Suppose the cost function satisfies
c
satisfies
\cap_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a),\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\right]=\emptyset
, but
r_{*}=0
.
Then we have
V^{\pi}_{r}=\lambda\mathbf{1}
for some
\lambda>0
(since
V^{\pi_{r}}>0
must hold) and
\pi\in\Pi
.
Let us define
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]
by
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}(s,s^{\prime})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(a|s)
,
and
c^{\pi}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
by
c^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)c(a|s)
,
then given observation (
2.3
) and the standard Bellman condition of

, we know that

V^{\pi}_{r}=\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}c^{\pi},

which gives

\displaystyle c^{\pi}=\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}\right)V^{\pi}_{r}=V^{\pi}_{r}-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}V^{\pi}_{r}\overset{(a)}{=}\lambda\mathbf{1}-\gamma\lambda\mathbf{1}=(1-\gamma)\lambda\mathbf{1},

(B.9)

where in term
(a)
we uses the fact that
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
.
However, (
B.9
) must contradict with the condition that

\cap_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a),\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\right]=\emptyset
,
since for each
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, we have
c^{\pi}(s)\in\left[\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a),\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\right]
.

“
\Leftarrow
”:
On the other hand,
suppose
r_{*}>0
but
\cap_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a),\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}c(s,a)\right]\neq\emptyset
, then one can readily find a policy
\pi
such that

c^{\pi}=\lambda\mathbf{1}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{r}=\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}c^{\pi}\overset{(b)}{=}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{t}c^{\pi}=\lambda\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbf{1}=\tfrac{\lambda}{1-\gamma}\mathbf{1},

where in term
(b)
we use the fact that
\rho(\gamma P^{\pi}_{u_{\pi}})\leq\gamma
, where
\rho(A)
denotes the spectral radius of matrix
A
.
Note that the previous observation then implies
r_{*}\leq\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}=0
.
∎

We proceed to show that
V^{\pi}_{r}(s)
is Lipschitz continuous in
\pi
.

Lemma B.6
.

For any policies
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert V^{\pi}_{r}-V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty},

(B.10)

where we define
\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\lVert\pi(\cdot|s)-\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)\rVert_{1}
,
i.e., the matrix
\ell_{\infty}
-norm when viewing
\pi
as a matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert\times\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}
.

Proof.

Note that for any
u\in\mathcal{U}
, from the performance difference lemma of standard MDPs
[
13
,
21
]
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert V^{\pi}_{u}(s)-V^{\pi}_{u}(s)\rvert=\lvert\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim d_{s}^{\pi,u}}\left[\langle Q^{\pi}_{u},\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}\right]\rvert\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty},

where in the last inequality we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the fact that
\lVert Q^{\pi}_{u}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert V^{\pi}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{\prime}}_{r}(s)\rvert=\lvert\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}V^{\pi}_{u}(s)-\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}V^{\pi}_{u}(s)\rvert\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lvert V^{\pi}_{u}(s)-V^{\pi}_{u}(s)\rvert\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}.

∎

By combining Lemma
B.6
and Lemma
B.4
, we are ready to establish the Lipschitz continuity of
d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi}}
with respect to policy
\pi
.

Lemma B.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
is relatively strongly convex with respect to
R_{s,a}>0
for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
with
R=\max_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in\mathcal{A}}R_{s,a}
, and
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})=\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert-1
.
Let
r_{*}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}
, where
\mathcal{H}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}:\mathbf{1}^{\top}x=0\right\}
.
If
r_{*}>0
,
then we have

\displaystyle\lVert d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi}}_{\rho}-d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{\rho}\rVert_{1}\leq\tfrac{\gamma\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert R}{(1-\gamma)^{2}r_{*}}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}.

(B.11)

Proof.

Let us define
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]
by
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}(s^{\prime},s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(a|s)
, then for any
\pi\in\Pi
, and
\rho\in\Delta_{{\mathcal{S}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{\rho}^{\pi,u}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}(\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u})^{t}\rho=(1-\gamma)\left(1-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u}\right)^{-1}\rho,

(B.12)

where in the last equality we use the fact that
\rho(\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{u})\leq\gamma<1
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi}}_{\rho}-d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\left(\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}-\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\right)\rho

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\gamma\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}\underbrace{\left(\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}-\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}\right)}_{\Delta^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}}\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\rho,

where the last equality uses the matrix identity

A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}
for any invertible matrix
A,B
.
Note that
\lVert\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}\rVert_{1}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
as we have shown in (
2.10
), it suffices to control the
\ell_{1}
-norm of
\Delta^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}=\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}-\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}
.
We now make the following observations

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\lvert\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime},s)-\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},s)\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\lvert\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\right)\pi^{*}(a|s)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\lvert\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\rvert\pi^{*}(a|s)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\lVert\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi}}(\cdot|s,a)-\mathbb{P}_{u_{\pi^{\prime}}}(\cdot|s,a)\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\tfrac{R\sqrt{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}}{r_{*}}\lVert V^{\pi}_{r}-V_{r}^{\pi^{\prime}}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\tfrac{R\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}{r_{*}(1-\gamma)}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}

where
(a)
uses Lemma
B.4
and the definition of
R
,
and
(b)
uses Lemma
B.6
,
From the prior inequality, we obtain
\lVert\Delta^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\leq\tfrac{R\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}{(1-\gamma)r_{*}}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}
.
Putting everything together, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lVert d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi}}_{\rho}-d^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi^{\prime}}}_{\rho}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)\gamma\lVert\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}}\right)^{-1}\rVert_{1}\lVert\Delta^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\lVert\left(I-\gamma\mathbb{P}^{\pi^{*}}_{u_{\pi}^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\rVert_{1}\lVert\rho\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\gamma\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert R}{(1-\gamma)^{2}r_{*}}\lVert\pi-\pi^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}.

The proof is then completed.
∎

Combining elements established above, we are now ready to establish the main result of this subsection.

Theorem B.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
is relatively strongly convex with respect to
R_{s,a}>0
for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
with
R=\max_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in\mathcal{A}}R_{s,a}
, and
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{P}_{s,a})=\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert-1
.
Let
r_{*}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\lVert\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{H}}(V^{\pi}_{r})\rVert_{2}
, where
\mathcal{H}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert}:\mathbf{1}^{\top}x=0\right\}
, and suppose
r_{*}>0
.
For any
\eta>0
, let
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
for all
k\geq 0
.
If

(1)

Distance-generating function
w
is
\mu
-strongly convex with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
-norm;

(2)

D_{w}^{*}=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi}(s)<\infty
.

Then RPMD outputs policy
\pi_{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\leq\tfrac{M}{(1-\gamma)k}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)+\tfrac{D_{w}}{(1-\gamma)\eta k}+\tfrac{2\gamma\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}RD_{w}^{*}}{(1-\gamma)^{7/2}r_{*}\sqrt{\eta\mu k}},

(B.13)

where
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

By summing up inequality (
3.5
) from
t=0
to
k-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{0}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{0}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)+\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-\tfrac{1}{M\eta_{t-1}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t-1}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\right)}_{(A)}.

(B.14)

We will make use of Lemma
B.7
to bound term
(A)
.
Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t-1}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{t}}(s)\rvert

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\lVert d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t}}-d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{t-1}}\rVert_{1}D_{w}^{*}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\tfrac{\gamma\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert RD_{w}^{*}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}r_{*}}\lVert\pi_{t}-\pi_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty},

(B.15)

where
(a)
uses Holder’s inequality, and
(b)
uses Lemma
B.7
, and the definition that
u_{t}=u_{\pi_{t}}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Since
w(\cdot)
is
\mu
-strongly convex with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\pi_{t}-\pi_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\lVert\pi_{t}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{t-1}(\cdot|s)\rVert_{1}\leq\sup_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sqrt{2D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t-1}}(s)/\mu},

which combined with (
B.14
) and (
B.15
), gives

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{0}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)+\tfrac{\gamma\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert RD_{w}^{*}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}r_{*}M\eta}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sqrt{\tfrac{2D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t-1}}(s)}{\mu}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\sqrt{D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t-1}}(s)}\right)^{2}\leq k\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}D^{\pi_{t}}_{\pi_{t-1}}(s)\leq k\eta\left(V^{\pi_{0}}_{r}(s)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{r}(s)\right)\leq\tfrac{k\eta}{1-\gamma},~~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

By combining two prior relations, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{k})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1-\gamma}{M}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\left(f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(\pi_{0})-f_{\rho}(\pi^{*})+\tfrac{1}{M\eta}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{0}}}D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)+\tfrac{2\gamma\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\rvert^{2}RD_{w}^{*}\sqrt{k}}{(1-\gamma)^{5/2}r_{*}M\sqrt{\eta\mu}}.

By (
3.8
), we know that
f_{\rho}(\pi_{t+1})\leq f_{\rho}(\pi_{t})
for all
t\geq 0
.
The desired claim then follows immediately after combining this observation with the above inequality.
∎

In view of Theorem
B.1
, the RPMD method with constant stepsize
\eta
outputs an
\epsilon
-optimal policy within

\mathcal{O}(\max\left\{1/\epsilon,1/(\eta\epsilon^{2})\right\})
iterations, for a general class of Bregman divergences.
Moreover, conditions (1) and (2) in Theorem
B.1
are both satisfied by
some common distance-generating functions, including the squared
\ell_{2}
-norm

w(p)=\lVert p\rVert_{2}^{2}
,
and the negative Tsallis entropy
w(p)=\tfrac{k}{q-1}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{q}-1\right)
for any entropic-index
q\in(1,2]
.

In addition, the second term of (
B.13
) also establishes a link on geometry of the set of uncertain probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{s,a}
to the convergence rate.
In addition, the dependence of convergence on the size of the state space is largely due to Lipschitz constant of
d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*},u_{\pi}}
with respect to policy
\pi
.
The current characterization of such Lipschitz constant seems improvable with additional efforts.
Nevertheless, it is worth mentioning that one can simply get rid of such a dependence by using a large stepsize.

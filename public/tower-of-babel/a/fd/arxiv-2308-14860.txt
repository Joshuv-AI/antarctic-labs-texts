1.
Introduction

Let
B_{d}
denote the open unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
,
and let
\sigma=\sigma_{d}
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
\partial B_{d}
.
For the unit disk
B_{1}
, the measure
\sigma_{1}
and the unit circle
\partial B_{1}
, we also use the
symbols
\mathbb{D}
,
m
and
\partial\mathbb{D}
, respectively.

Let
\mathcal{H}ol(B_{d})
denote the space of holomorphic functions in the ball
B_{d}
.
For
0<p<\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}=H^{p}(B_{d})

consists of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}ol(B_{d})
such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\partial B_{d}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}\,d\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

As usual, the Hardy space
H^{p}(B_{d})
,
p>0
,
is identified with the space

H^{p}(\partial B_{d})
, which consists of the corresponding boundary values.
Further details about the spaces
{H^{p}}(B_{d})
and related objects are contained,
for example, in monographs
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
.

1.1.
Carleson measures

Let
M_{+}(B_{d})
denote the space of all finite
positive Borel measures defined on
B_{d}
.
Firstly, assume that
d=1
and

\mu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{D})
.
The classical Carleson Theorem
[
3
]
states that

H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is embedded into
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)
, that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{p}}\quad\textrm{for all}\ f\in{H^{p}}

if and only if
\mu
satisfies the following Carleson condition:
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\mu(S_{I})\leq Cm(I)\quad\textrm{for all arcs}\ I\subset\partial\mathbb{D},

where

S_{I}=\left\{z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}:\ 1-m(I)\leq|z|\leq 1,\ \frac{z}{|z|}\in I\right\}.

For an arbitrary dimension
d\geq 1
, consider the sets

Q=Q(\zeta,\delta)=\{\xi\in\partial B_{d}:|1-\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle|\leq\delta\},\quad\zeta\in\partial B_{d},\ \delta>0.

Observe that
Q
is a closed ball with respect to the nonisotropic metric

\rho(\zeta,\xi)=|1-\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\zeta,\xi\in\partial B_{d}.

For
r>0
and
Q=Q(\zeta,\delta)
, put
rQ=Q(\zeta,r\delta)
by definition.

By the theorem on Carleson measures for the Hardy space in the unit ball
(see, for example,
[
10
, Ch. 5]
),
the estimate

(1.3)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{p}},\quad f\in{H^{p}},

is equivalent to the following condition on the measure
\mu
:

(1.4)

\mu(S_{Q})\leq C\sigma(Q)\quad\textrm{for all nonisotropic balls}\ Q\subset\partial B_{d},

where

S_{Q}=\left\{z\in B_{d}:\ 1-\sigma(Q)\leq|z|\leq 1,\ \frac{z}{|z|}\in Q\right\}

is a standard Carleson window.

1.2.
Reverse Carleson measures

Remark that Luecking
[
7
]
studied
Carleson measures for Bergman spaces in the unit disk
together with those measures for which the corresponding inverse inequality holds.
In the case of several complex variables,
we mention the recent related results by Green and Wagner
[
4
]

about the dominating sets for Bergman spaces on general domains in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

In the first part of the present paper, we investigate the reverse Carleson inequality

(1.5)

\|f\|_{H^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{B}_{d},\mu)},\quad f\in C(\overline{B}_{d})\cap H^{p}(B_{d}),\ 1<p<\infty,

for
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
.

Remark that the above problem is formulated
under assumption that the measure
\mu
is defined on the closed ball
\overline{B}_{d}
.
In fact, for
\mu\in M_{+}(B_{d})
this problem degenerates and has no solutions;
see Theorem
1
stated below.

Observe that the theorem about the Carleson measures for
{H^{p}}(B_{d})

also has a formulation under the assumption
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
.
Indeed, in this case, condition (
1.3
) makes sense for the functions
f
in the set

C(\overline{B}_{d})\cap H^{p}(B_{d})
, which is dense in
H^{p}(B_{d})
.
Condition (
1.4
) implies that the restriction
\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}

is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure
\sigma
,
and the Radon–Nikodym derivative
{d\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}}/{d\sigma}
is bounded.

For
d=1
and a measure
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
with property (
1.2
),
Lefèvre, Li et al.
[
6
]
proved that
property (
1.5
) is equivalent to the following condition:

\mu(S_{I})\geq Cm(I)\quad\textrm{for all arcs}\ I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}.

Further, this result was proved for all
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
in
[
5
]
,
where other equivalent properties have been also obtained.

The main result of the present paper about the reverse Carleson measures
for the Hardy space
H^{p}(B_{d})
with
d\geq 1

is Theorem
1
formulated in the next section.

1.3.
Reverse Carleson measures for Hardy spaces in the unit ball

Let
1<p<\infty
. For
w\in B_{d}
, consider the Cauchy kernels

k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{d}},\quad z\in B_{d},

and the corresponding normalized functions

K_{w}=\frac{k_{w}}{\|k_{w}\|_{{H^{p}}}}.

Proposition 1.4.10 from
[
8
]
guarantees that

\|k_{w}\|_{p}=(1-|w|^{2})^{-\frac{d}{q}},\quad\textrm{where}\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

For
d=1
, the following result was obtained in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
,
d\geq 1
.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(i)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\geq C\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\quad\textrm{for all}\ f\in C(\overline{B}_{d})\cap H^{p}.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}|K_{w}|^{p}\,d\mu\geq C\quad\textrm{for all}\ w\in B_{d}.

(iii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(Q)\geq C\sigma(Q)\quad\textrm{for all nonisotropic balls}\ Q\subset\partial B_{d}.

It is mentioned above that the problem on inverse Carleson measures for
{H^{p}}(B_{d})
,
1<p<\infty
,
has no solutions for the measures
\mu
defined on the open unit ball
B_{d}
.
Indeed, this fact follows immediately from property (iii) in Theorem
1
.
Further comments and the proof of Theorem
1

are given in Section
2
.

1.4.
Reverse Carleson measures for de Branges–Rovnyak spaces

The second type of spaces studied in this paper is that of
de Branges–Rovnyak spaces in the unit ball.

Let
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Direct inspection shows that the function

\mathcal{K}^{b}(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{d}},\quad z,w\in B_{d},

has the properties of a reproducing kernel.
The reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}(b)\subset H^{2}

with kernel
\mathcal{K}^{b}(z,w)

is called a de Branges–Rovnyak space.
Further details about these spaces
in the unit disk are given, for example, in monograph
[
9
]
.

On the one hand, if
\|b\|_{\infty}<1
, then the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
coincides with
H^{2}
,
up to an equivalent norm.
On the other hand, the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
has an explicit form for an inner function
b
.
Recall that a holomorphic function

I:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
is called
inner
if

|I(\zeta)|=1
for
\sigma_{d}
-almost all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Here and in what follows,

I(\zeta)
denotes the limit

\lim_{r\to 1-}I(r\zeta)
, as usual.
It is known that the corresponding limit exists
\sigma_{d}
-almost everywhere.
Direct computations of the reproducing kernel for the space

H^{2}\ominus IH^{2}
show that

\mathcal{H}(b)=H^{2}\ominus IH^{2}
.
If
I
is an inner function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
,
then
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus IH^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a classical model space.

A number of results on the reverse Carleson measures for the spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
in the unit disk
are obtained in
[
2
]
.
In that paper, for certain functions
b
, descriptions of the measures under consideration are obtained and
it is shown, in particular, that it suffices to check the reverse Carleson inequality
on the corresponding reproducing kernels.
For arbitrary spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)

in the unit disk, it is known that such a reproducing kernel thesis does not hold
(see
[
5
]
).

In the final Section
3
of the present paper, an analog
of property (ii) from Theorem
1

is used to deduce properties of the reverse Carleson
measures for the de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)

in the unit ball.
The results in this direction are obtained
for non-inner functions
b

and are contained in Theorem
2
and Corollary
1
,
see Section
3
.

Notation

As usual,
C>0
denotes a constant whose value can change from line to line.

2.
Reverse Carleson measures for Hardy spaces

In this section, Theorem
1
is proved
and one more description of the measures under investigation
is given in terms of the standard Carleson windows
S_{Q}
,
see Proposition
1
below.

2.1.
Auxiliary results

For
0<h\leq 1
and a nonisotropic ball
Q
, define the Carleson
h
-window
S_{Q,h}
by the following equality:

(2.1)

S_{Q,h}=\left\{z\in B_{d}:\ 1-h\leq|z|\leq 1,\ \frac{z}{|z|}\in Q\right\}.

Let
\nu=\nu_{d}
denote Lebesgue measures on the complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
Q\subset\partial B_{d}

be a nonisotropic ball. For
0<h\leq 1
, put

(2.2)

\Phi_{h}(z)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{pd-d}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{pd}}\,d\nu(w),\quad z\in\overline{B}_{d}.

Then

(a)

\Phi_{h}(z)\leq C
for all
z\in\overline{B}_{d}
and
0<h\leq 1;

(b)

\lim_{h\to 0}\Phi_{h}(z)=0
for all
z\in\overline{B}_{d}\setminus Q
.

Proof.

(a) It is clear that

\Phi_{h}(z)\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h\leq|w|\leq 1}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{pd-d}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{pd}}\,d\nu(w),\quad z\in B_{d}.

Next, integrating in polar coordinates, we have

\begin{split}\Phi_{h}(z)&\leq C(d)\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{pd-d}\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{d\sigma(\xi)}{|1-r\langle z,\xi\rangle|^{pd}}\,dr\\
&\leq C(d)\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{pd-d}(1-r|z|)^{d-pd}\,dr\\
&\leq C(d)\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\,dr\leq C(d)\end{split}

by
[
8
, Proposition 1.4.10]
.

(b) Assume that
{z}\notin Q
. Then there exists a
\delta=\delta(z)>0
such that

|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\geq\delta

for all
w\in S_{Q,h}
,

0<h\leq 1
. Hence,

\begin{split}0\leq\Phi_{h}(z)&\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{pd-d}}{\delta^{pd}}d\nu(w)\\
&\leq C\frac{1}{h}\frac{\sigma(Q)h^{pd-d+1}}{\delta^{pd}}\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\ h\to 0+.\end{split}

The proof of the lemma is finished.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1

(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{ii})

This implication is trivial, since
\|K_{w}\|_{H^{p}}=1
for
w\in B_{d}
.

(\mathrm{ii})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{iii})

Fix a nonisotropic ball
Q\subset\partial B_{d}
.
Integrating the estimate from condition (ii) over the set

\widetilde{S}_{Q,h}=\left\{z\in B_{d}:\ 1-h\leq|z|<1,\ \frac{z}{|z|}\in Q\right\},\quad 0<h\leq 1,

with respect to the measure
\nu
, we have

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{ii}}|Q|h

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{S}_{Q,h}}\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}|K_{w}|^{p}\,d\mu\,d\nu(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}\int_{\widetilde{S}_{Q,h}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{\frac{pd}{q}}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{pd}}\,d\nu(w)\,d\mu(z).

The last expression does not change after replacing the set

{\widetilde{S}_{Q,h}}
by

{S}_{Q,h}
in the inner integral.
Therefore, the above estimate has the following form in terms
of the function
\Phi_{h}
defined by identity (
2.2
):

(2.3)

\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}\Phi_{h}(z)\,d\mu(z)\geq C_{\mathrm{ii}}\sigma(Q).

On the one hand, properties (a) and (b) from Lemma
1
guarantee that

(2.4)

\lim_{h\to 0+}\int_{\overline{B}_{d}\setminus Q}\Phi_{h}(z)\,d\mu(z)=0.

On the other hand, by property (b) from Lemma
1
, we have

(2.5)

\int_{Q}\Phi_{h}(z)\,d\mu(z)\leq C\mu(Q)\quad\textrm{for all}\ h>0.

Combining properties (
2.3
)–(
2.5
), we obtain

C\mu(Q)\geq C_{\mathrm{ii}}\sigma(Q),

as required.

(\mathrm{iii})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{i})

Let

\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}=g\sigma+\mu^{s}_{|\partial B_{d}},

be the Lebesgue decomposition, where

\mu^{s}_{|\partial B_{d}}\bot\sigma

and
g\in L^{1}(\sigma)
denotes the corresponding Radon–Nikodym derivative.
Theorems 5.3.1 and 5.3.2 from monograph
[
8
]
guarantee that

g(\zeta)=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}(Q(\zeta,\delta))}{\sigma(Q(\zeta,\delta))}

for
\sigma
-almost all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Thus, condition (iii) implies that
g\geq C>0

\sigma
-almost everywhere.
Therefore,

\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\geq\int_{\partial B_{d}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\geq C\int_{\partial B_{d}}|f|^{p}\,d\sigma=C\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}

for all
f\in H^{p}\cap C(\overline{B}_{d})
.
The proof of Theorem
1
is finished.

2.3.
Comments and remarks

Remark 1
.

Equivalence of properties (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1
means that
to prove the reverse Carleson inequality, it suffices to verify this inequality
on the reproducing kernels
k_{w}
,
w\in B_{d}
.
In other words, the reproducing kernel thesis holds for the problem
on reverse Carleson measures for
{H^{p}}
,
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 2
.

On the one hand, property (iii) guarantees that Lebesgue measure
\sigma
, defined on the sphere
\partial B_{d}
,
is absolutely continuous with respect to the restriction
\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}
.
On the other hand, as it is shown in the proof of Theorem
1
,
the Radon–Nikodym derivative
d\mu^{a}_{|\partial B_{d}}/d\sigma
is separated
from zero by a positive constant.
Here
\mu^{a}_{|\partial B_{d}}
denotes
the absolutely continuous part (with respect to
\sigma
) of
\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}
.

The following assertion shows that condition (iii) from Theorem
1

is equivalent to its analog in terms of the sets
S_{Q}

used to characterize the classical Carleson measures.

Proposition 1
.

Equivalent properties from Theorem
1
hold if and only if
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.6)

\mu(S_{Q})\geq C\sigma(Q)\quad\textrm{for all nonisotropic balls}\ Q\subset\partial B_{d}.

Proof.

Clearly, property (iii) from Theorem
1
implies (
2.6
).
To prove the reverse implication, assume that property (
2.6
) holds.
Fix a
\delta>0
and a nonisotropic ball
Q=Q(\zeta,\delta)\subset\partial B_{d}
for some point
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
For
0<h<\delta
, choose a maximal family of pairwise disjoint nonisotropic balls

Q_{j}=Q_{j}(\zeta_{j},h)\subset 2Q
,
j=1,\dots,J
.
Observe that
Q\subset\cup_{1\leq j\leq J}2Q_{j}
.
Since the
h
-window
S_{Q_{j},h}
coincides with the set
S_{Q_{j}}
for
j=1,\dots,J
,
condition (iii) guarantees that

(2.7)

\begin{split}C_{0}(d)\mu(S_{Q,h})=\mu(S_{2Q,h})&\geq\mu\sum_{j=1}^{J}S_{Q_{j},h}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mu(S_{Q_{j},h})\\
&\overset{\mathrm{(iii)}}{\geq}C\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sigma(Q_{j})=C(d)\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sigma(2Q_{j})\\
&\geq C(d)\sigma(Q).\end{split}

If
Q\subset G\subset\overline{B}_{d}
and the set
G
is relatively open in
\overline{B}_{d}
,
then
G\supset S_{Q,h}
for a sufficiently small parameter
h>0
.
Thus, applying (
2.7
) and the outer regularity of the measure

\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
, we have

\begin{split}\mu(Q)&=\inf\{\mu(G):Q\subset G,\ G\ \textrm{is open in}\ \overline{B}_{d}\}\\
&\geq\inf_{h>0}\mu(S_{Q,h})\geq C(d)\sigma(Q),\end{split}

as required.
∎

3.
Reverse Carleson measures for de Branges–Rovnyak spaces

To define a reverse Carleson measure
\mu
for
H^{p}(B_{d})
,
we compute the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{B}_{d},\mu)}

only for
f\in H^{p}(B_{d})\cap C(\overline{B}_{d})
, see (
1.5
).
For the spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
, we apply a different approach based
on the following notion.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
. A function
f\in\mathcal{H}ol(B_{d})

is called
\mu
-admissible if
f
has radial limits
\mu
-almost everywhere
on the sphere
\partial B_{d}
.
Given a holomorphic function
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
,
the set of all
\mu
-admissible functions in the space

\mathcal{H}(b)
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(b)
.

Now, assume that
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(b)
.
On the support of the measure
\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}
, the function
f

is defined via its radial limits.
As a result, the quantity
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\overline{B}_{d},\mu)}
takes
value in the infinite interval
[0,+\infty]
.

Definition 2
.

Let
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
A measure
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
is called a reverse Carleson measure for the space
\mathcal{H}(b)

if

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}\leq C||f\|_{L^{2}(\overline{B}_{d},\mu)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(b)
.

The main result of the present section is the following theorem on properties of
the reverse Carleson measures.
For
d=1
, this result was obtained in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
and
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a
\mu
-admissible holomorphic function.
Put

g=\frac{d\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}}{dm}.

Assume that
\mu
is a reverse Carleson measure for the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
.
Then

(3.1)

1-|b(\zeta)|^{2}\leq C(1-|b(\zeta)|^{2})^{2}g(\zeta)

for
\sigma_{d}
-almost all points
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
and a universal constant
C>0
.

On the one hand, if
|b(\zeta)|=1
, then condition (
3.1
) imposes no restriction
at the point
\zeta
.
In particular, this condition degenerates completely for
an inner function
b
.
On the other hand, condition (
3.1
) naturally simplifies for
|b(\zeta)|<1

and provides the following explicit information.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
and
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a
\mu
-admissible non-inner holomorphic function.
Assume that
\mu
is a reverse Carleson measure for the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
.
Then

\frac{d\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}}{d\sigma}\neq\mathbf{0}

and

(3.2)

\int_{\{\zeta\in\partial B_{d}:\,|b(\zeta)|<1\}}\frac{1}{1-|b|}\,d\sigma<\infty.

Proof.

Firstly, by Theorem
2
, inequality (
3.1
) holds,
therefore,

g\neq\mathbf{0}
because
b
is not an inner function.
Secondly, property (
3.1
) implies that

\frac{1}{1-|b(\xi)|}\leq Cg(\xi)

for
\sigma
-almost all
\xi\in\{\zeta\in\partial B_{d}:\,|b(\zeta)|<1\}
.
Since
g\in L^{1}(\partial B_{d},\sigma)
, we obtain the required property (
3.2
).
∎

Remark that property (
3.2
) imposes a restriction on those functions
b
for which
the set of the reverse Carleson measures for
\mathcal{H}(b)
is not empty.

Corollary 2
.

There exists a holomorphic function
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
with the following property:
if
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
and

b
is
\mu
-admissible, then
\mu
is not a reverse Carleson measure for the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

Proof.

Choose a positive function
\psi\in C(\partial B_{d})
such that

\psi(\zeta_{0})=1
,
0<\psi(\zeta)<1
for
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}\setminus\{\zeta_{0}\}
and

\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,d\sigma=\infty.

By
[
1
, Corollary 2]
,
there exists a holomorphic function
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
such that

|b|=\psi

\sigma
-almost everywhere on the sphere
\partial B_{d}
.
Therefore, if
\mu\in M_{+}(\overline{B}_{d})
and

b
is
\mu
-admissible, then condition (
3.2
) does not hold.
Hence,
\mu
is not a reverse Carleson measure for
\mathcal{H}(b)
by Corollary
1
.
∎

Further, Corollary
1

provides negative information on the sampling sequences for
\mathcal{H}(b)
.
Recall that

\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset B_{d}
is called a sampling sequence for
\mathcal{H}(b)

provided that there exist constants

C_{2}\geq C_{1}>0
such that

C_{1}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f(w_{j})|^{2}}{\ \|\mathcal{K}_{w_{j}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}}\leq C_{2}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
.
For
d=1
, the following assertion is proved in
[
2
]
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
b:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a non-inner holomorphic function.
Then there is no sampling sequence for the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

Proof.

Assume that

\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset B_{d}
is a sampling sequence for
\mathcal{H}(b)
.
Then

\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{w_{j}}}{\|\mathcal{K}_{w_{j}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}}

is a reverse Carleson measure for
\mathcal{H}(b)
.
This contradicts Corollary
1
because
d\mu_{|\partial B_{d}}/d\sigma=\mathbf{0}
.
∎

Now, we proceed to the proof of Theorem
2
.

3.1.
Auxiliary results

Given a holomorphic function
F:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
, recall that
the boundary function
F(\xi)
is defined for
\sigma
-almost all
\xi\in\partial B_{d}

in terms of radial limits.
The following assertion refines Lemma
1
for
p=2
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
Q\subset\partial B_{d}
be a nonisotropic ball and
F:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the limit

\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{Q}|F(r\xi)|\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}d\sigma(\xi)

(a)

is equal to zero for
z\in\overline{B}_{d}\setminus Q
,

(b)

is equal
|F(z)|
for
\sigma
-almost all
z
in the interior of
Q
.

Proof.

(a) Since
F
is a bounded function,
it suffices to apply Lemma
1
(b) for
p=2
.

(b) Let
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
. Observe that

\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\partial B_{d}\setminus\widetilde{Q}(\zeta,\tau)}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle\zeta,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}d\sigma(\xi)=0,

where
\widetilde{Q}(\zeta,\tau)\subset\partial B_{d}
is a nonisotropic ball. Thus,
to prove property (b), it suffices to verify that

(3.3)

\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\partial B_{d}}|F(r\xi)|\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle\zeta,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}d\sigma(\xi)=|F(\zeta)|

for
\sigma
-almost all points
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.

Now, to prove property (
3.3
),
assume that
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
is a Lebesgue point for the function
F(\xi)
, i.e.,

(3.4)

\lim_{\tau\to 0+}\int_{\widetilde{Q}(\zeta,\tau)}|F(\xi)-F(\zeta)|\,d\sigma(\xi)=0.

Consider the following Poisson integral:

u(w)=\int_{\partial B_{d}}|F(\xi)-F(\zeta)|\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{2d}}d\sigma(\xi),\quad w\in B_{d}.

Remark that
\lim_{r\to 1-}u(r\zeta)=0
by (
3.4
) and Theorem 5.4.8 from
[
8
]
.

The function
|F(w)-F(\zeta)|
,
w\in B_{d}
, is plurisubharmonic, hence,

\begin{split}0&\leq\limsup_{r\to 1-}\int_{\partial B_{d}}|F(r\xi)-F(\zeta)|\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle\zeta,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}\,d\sigma(\xi)\\
&\leq\limsup_{r\to 1-}\int_{\partial B_{d}}u(r\xi)\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle r\zeta,\xi\rangle|^{2d}}\,d\sigma(\xi)=\limsup_{r\to 1-}u(r^{2}\zeta)=0.\end{split}

One has
\left||F(r\xi)|-|F(\zeta)|\right|\leq|F(r\xi)-F(\zeta)|
, thus,
the above estimate implies the required property
(
3.3
) at the point
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
under consideration.
To finish the argument, recall that property (
3.4
)
holds for
\sigma
-almost all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
2

The function
\mathcal{K}^{b}_{w}
,
w\in B_{d}
, is
\mu
-admissible,
since
b
is
\mu
-admissible by the hypothesis of the theorem.
Hence, by the definition of a reverse Carleson measure, we have

\|\mathcal{K}^{b}_{w}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(b)}\leq C||\mathcal{K}^{b}_{w}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

for all
w\in B_{d}
. Rewriting this inequality in explicit terms, we obtain

(3.5)

\frac{1-|b(w)|^{2}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{d}}\leq C\int_{\overline{B}_{d}}\left|\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{d}}\right|^{2}d\mu(z).

Let

\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}=g\sigma_{d}+\eta

be the Lebesgue decomposition, where

\eta\bot\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}
and
g
denotes the Radon–Nikodym derivative

\frac{d\mu_{\left|\partial B_{d}\right.}}{d\sigma_{d}}
.
Put

\rho:=\mu_{\left|B_{d}\right.}=\mu-g\sigma-\eta.

In these terms, estimate (
3.5
) has the following form:

(3.6)

\begin{split}C(1-|b(w)|^{2})&\leq\int_{B_{d}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}|^{2}\,d\rho(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}|^{2}\,d\eta(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}|^{2}\,g(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(z)\\
&\quad:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.\end{split}

Now, choose a point
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
and consider
a non-isotropic ball
Q=(\zeta,\tau)\subset\partial B_{d}
,
\tau>0
.
Recall that the Carleson
h
-window
S_{Q,h}
for
0<h\leq 1
is defined by the following equality:

S_{Q,h}=\left\{z\in B_{d}:\ 1-h\leq|z|\leq 1,\ \frac{z}{|z|}\in Q\right\}.

We integrate both parts of inequality (
3.6
) over the set
S_{Q,h}

with respect to the volume Lebesgue measure
\nu
,
divide by
h
and study the behavior of the resulting expressions as
h\to 0+
.

Firstly, for the integral of the left-hand side of inequality (
3.6
), after
integration in polar coordinates we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}C\int_{S_{Q,h}}&(1-|b(w)|^{2})\,d\nu(w)\\
&=\frac{C(d)}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}(1-|b(r\xi)|^{2})\,d\sigma(\xi)\,r^{2d-1}dr\\
&\to C(d)\int_{Q}(1-|b(\xi)|^{2})\,d\sigma(\xi)\end{split}

as
h\to 0
.

Secondly, applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{C(d)}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}J_{1}(w)\,d\nu(w)\\
&=\int_{B_{d}}\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(z)\overline{b(r\xi)}|^{2}\,d\sigma(\xi)\,r^{2d-1}dr\,d\rho(z).\end{split}

Remark that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}&|1-b(z)\overline{b(r\xi)}|^{2}\,d\sigma(\xi)\\
&\leq C\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}\,d\sigma(\xi)\leq C\end{split}

for all
z\in\overline{B}_{d}
, and

\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}\,d\sigma(\xi)\,r^{2d-1}dr\overset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\textrm{for all}\ z\in B_{d}

by Lemma
2
(i) with
F\equiv 1
.
Therefore,

(3.9)

\frac{1}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}J_{1}(w)\,d\nu(w)\overset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

Next,

\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}\,d\sigma(\xi)\,r^{2d-1}dr\overset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\textrm{for all}\ z\in\partial B_{d}\setminus Q

by Lemma
2
(i) with
F\equiv 1
.
Therefore, applying Fubini’s theorem and property (
3.8
), we obtain

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\frac{C(d)}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}J_{2}(w)\,d\nu(w)\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(z)\overline{b(r\xi)}|^{2}\,d\sigma(\xi)\,r^{2d-1}dr\,d\eta(z)\\
&\leq\int_{Q}d\eta+\varepsilon(h),\end{split}

where
\varepsilon(h)\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Finally, after application of Fubini’s theorem, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{C(d)}{h}\int_{S_{Q,h}}J_{3}(w)\,d\nu(w)\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{d}}\frac{1}{h}\int_{1-h}^{1}\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(r\xi)\overline{b(z)}|^{2}d\sigma(\xi)r^{2d-1}drg(\zeta)d\sigma(z).\end{split}

Applying Lemma
2
with
F(w)=(1-b(w)\overline{b(\zeta)})^{2}
, we obtain

C\geq\int_{Q}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{d}}{|1-\langle z,r\xi\rangle|^{2d}}|1-b(r\xi)\overline{b(z)}|^{2}\,d\sigma(\xi)\overset{r\to 1-}{\longrightarrow}(1-|b(z)|^{2})^{2}\chi_{Q}(z)

for
\sigma
-almost all
z\in\partial B_{d}
.
Therefore,

(3.11)

\frac{1}{h}\int_{S(Q,h)}J_{3}(w)\,d\nu(w)\to\int_{Q}(1-|b|^{2})^{2}g\,d\sigma

as
h\to 0
.

Recall that
Q=Q(\zeta,\tau)
.
So, combining properties (
3.7
) and (
3.9
)–(
3.11
), we obtain

\int_{Q(\zeta,\tau)}(1-|b|^{2})\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{Q(\zeta,\tau)}(1-|b|^{2})^{2}g\,d\sigma+C\int_{Q(\zeta,\tau)}d\eta

for all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.

Now, we divide both parts of the above estimate by
\sigma(Q(\zeta,\tau))

and let
\tau
tend to zero.
Since
\eta
is a singular measure, Theorem 5.3.2 from monograph
[
8
]
guarantees that

\frac{1}{\sigma(Q(\zeta,\tau))}\int_{Q(\zeta,\tau)}d\eta\overset{\tau\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0

for
\sigma
-almost all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Therefore, the required inequality (
3.1
) holds for
\sigma
-almost all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}

by the measure differentiation theorem on the sphere
\partial B_{d}
(see
[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
).
The proof of Theorem
2
is finished.

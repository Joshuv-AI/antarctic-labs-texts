The energy method for high-order invariants in shallow water wave equations
Qifeng Zhang, Tong Yan, Guang-hua Gao

Abstract
Third order dispersive evolution equations are widely adopted to model one-dimensional long waves and have extensive applications in fluid mechanics, plasma physics and nonlinear optics. Among them are the KdV equation, the Camassa–Holm equation and the Degasperis–Procesi equation. They share many common features such as complete integrability, Lax pairs and bi-Hamiltonian structure. In this paper we revisit high-order invariants for these three types of shallow water wave equations by the energy method in combination of a skew-adjoint operator ( 1 − ∂ x ​ x ) − 1 (1-\partial_{xx})^{-1} . Several applications to seek high-order invariants of the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation, the regularized long wave equation and the Rosenau equation are also presented.



1
Introduction

A family of third order dispersive evolution equations of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}-\alpha^{2}u_{xxt}+\gamma u_{xxx}+c_{0}u_{x}=(c_{1}u^{2}+c_{2}u_{x}^{2}+c_{3}uu_{xx})_{x},\quad x\in R,\;t>0

(1.1)

frequently appeared in the simulation of the shallow water waves, see e.g.,
ELY2006
, where
\alpha
,
\gamma
and
c_{i}

(i=0,1,2,3)
are real constants;
u
denotes a horizontal velocity field with the independent spatial variable
x
and temporal variable
t
.

A typical such equation (
1.1
) with
\alpha^{2}=c_{0}=c_{2}=c_{3}=0
,
c_{1}=2
,
\gamma=-2
is
the KdV equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-4uu_{x}-2u_{xxx}=0,\quad x\in R,\;t>0,

(1.2)

which describes the unidirectional propagation of waves at the free surface of shallow water under the influence of gravity.
The first four invariants of (
1.2
) are respectively as (see e.g.,
Tao2002
, although there is a
minor typo in the coefficient of the fourth invariant, it does not affect the reading of this classic review)

\displaystyle M_{1}=\int_{R}u\mathrm{d}x,\quad M_{2}=\int_{R}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad M_{3}=\int_{R}\Big(u_{x}^{2}-\frac{2}{3}u^{3}\Big)\mathrm{d}x,\quad M_{4}=\int_{R}\Big(u_{xx}^{2}-\frac{10}{3}uu_{x}^{2}+\frac{5}{9}u^{4}\Big)\mathrm{d}x.

Taking
\alpha^{2}=c_{3}=1
,
\gamma=c_{0}=0
,
c_{1}=-\frac{3}{2}
,
c_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
,
we have another example called
the Camassa–Holm equation

CH1993

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},\quad x\in R,\;t>0,

(1.3)

which models the unidirectional propagation of shallow water waves over a flat bottom.
The first three invariants are listed as follows

\displaystyle E_{1}=\int_{R}(u-u_{xx})\mathrm{d}x,\quad E_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{R}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})\mathrm{d}x,\quad E_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{R}u(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})\mathrm{d}x.

The third example by assigning
\alpha^{2}=c_{2}=c_{3}=1
,
\gamma=c_{0}=0
,
c_{1}=-2
is called
the Degasperis–Procesi equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+4uu_{x}=3u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},\quad x\in R,\;t>0,

(1.4)

which can be regarded as a model for nonlinear shallow water dynamics
DP1999
.
The frequently discussed invariants are

\displaystyle H_{1}=\int_{R}(u-u_{xx})\mathrm{d}x,\quad H_{2}=\int_{R}(u-u_{xx})v\mathrm{d}x,\quad H_{3}=\int_{R}u^{3}\mathrm{d}x,

where
4v-v_{xx}=u
.

Up to now, there have been thousands of papers focusing on the theoretical and numerical studies on these three equations.
It is worth mentioning that the invariant-preserving property is a key index of the success for numerical methods.
However, high-order invariants are usually difficult to preserve numerically. Liu
et al.
also pointed out
“it appears a rather difficult task to preserve all three conservation laws” in
LX2016
. In this work, higher-order invariants of these equations will be re-derived in view of the energy method, which may be possible to provide some thoughts for invariant-preserving numerical methods. Actually, the energy method originated from conservation laws in physics was first proposed in 1928 by Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy
CFL1928
. From then on, it has been widely applied to the mathematical and numerical analysis of nonlinear evolution equations. We trust the readers with
Sun2018
instead of a long list of references to relevant works.

The rest of the paper is arranged as follows. In Section
2
, combining the energy method and a skew-adjoint operator, we show the high-order invariants for the KdV equation, the Camassa–Holm equation and the Degasperis–Procesi equation, respectively. Then we list several applications for seeking some high-order invariants of other types of the shallow water wave equations in Section
3
.

and the corresponding high-order invariant

\displaystyle M(t)=\int_{R}(u_{xx}^{2}-Auu_{x}^{2}+Bu^{4})\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}5a=3Ab,\\

\displaystyle\frac{a}{b}=\frac{3A}{5}=\frac{12B}{A}.

\displaystyle A^{2}=20B.

For instance, when

, we have

,

,
which deduces to the KdV equation as

\displaystyle u_{t}+6uu_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,

\displaystyle=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot(3u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}-2(uu_{x})_{x})\mathrm{d}x

3
Applications to other periodic nonlinear dispersive waves

3.1
Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation

Consider the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation
MM1977
of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+u_{x}+\varepsilon uu_{x}=0,\quad x\in R.

(3.1)

It can be written as

\displaystyle u_{t}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}u^{2}\Big)=0,\quad x\in R.

Let
g=(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}u^{2}\Big)
, then the equation (
3.1
) turns out to be

\displaystyle u_{t}+g_{x}=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle g-g_{xx}=u+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}u^{2}.

(3.3)

Multiplying both sides of (
3.2
) by
u^{2}
and integrating the result, and then using (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{R}(u_{t}+g_{x})\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot(g-g_{xx}-u)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot u\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u\mathrm{d}x=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{R}\Big(\frac{1}{3}u^{3}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x,

which indicates

\displaystyle\int_{R}\frac{1}{3}\Big(u^{3}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x

is a three-order invariant for (
3.1
).

3.2
Regularized long wave equation

Consider the regularized long wave equation
SF1984
of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}-\mu u_{xxt}+u_{x}+u^{p}u_{x}=0,

(3.4)

where
\mu>0
is a positive constant. When

Similar to the foregoing argument, (

\displaystyle u_{t}+g_{x}=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle g-\mu g_{xx}=u+\frac{1}{p+1}u^{p+1}.

(3.6)

Multiplying both sides of (
3.5
) by
u^{p+1}

3.6
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{R}(u_{t}+g_{x})\cdot u^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot u^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x+({p+1})\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot(g-\mu g_{xx}-u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{R}u_{t}\cdot u^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x-({p+1})\int_{R}g_{x}\cdot u\mathrm{d}x

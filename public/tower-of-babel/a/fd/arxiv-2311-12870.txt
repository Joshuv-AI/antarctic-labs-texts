Self-adjointness of a simplified Dirac interaction operator without any cutoffs
Mads J. Damgaard

Abstract
We show that a simplified version of the Dirac interaction operator given by H ^ I ∝ ∫ d ​ 𝐤 ​ 𝑑 𝐩 ​ ( a ^ ​ ( 𝐤 ) + a ^ † ​ ( − 𝐤 ) ) ​ b ^ † ​ ( 𝐩 + 𝐤 ) ​ b ^ ​ ( 𝐩 ) / | 𝐤 | \hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\propto\int d\mathbf{k}d\mathbf{p}(\hat{a}(\mathbf{k})+\hat{a}^{\dagger}(-\mathbf{k}))\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})\hat{b}(\mathbf{p})/\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}|} is self-adjoint on a certain domain that is dense in the Hilbert space, even without any cutoffs. The technique that we use for showing this can potentially be extended to a much wider range of operators as well. This technique might therefore potentially lead to more mathematically well-defined theories of QFT in the future.



1
Introduction

In quantum field theory (QFT), one often encounters divergencies that need to be treated in some way. This sometimes means introducing certain cutoffs to the operators involved.
However, without the knowledge that there exist an underlying self-adjoint Hamiltonian for a given theory, it is not known whether the predictions of that theory will converge in the limit when these cutoffs are lifted.

In this paper, we will show that a certain interaction operator, which can be seen as a simplified version of the Dirac interaction operator, is self-adjoint on some domain of the Hilbert space. The hope is then that this technique can be extended to other operators as well, and in particular to ones that are more realistic as physical Hamiltonians.

2
A simplified Dirac interaction operator

The particular operator that we will investigate in this paper is a simplified version of the Dirac interaction operator, given formally by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}=\int\frac{d\mathbf{k}\,d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{6}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}|}}\big(\hat{a}(\mathbf{k})+\hat{a}^{\dagger}(-\mathbf{k})\big)\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})\hat{b}(\mathbf{p}).

(1)

\hat{a}(\mathbf{k})

\mathbf{p}

3
A domain on which
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}

We will now define a domain for
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
on which we wish to show that this operator is self-adjoint.
First of all, define

for all

,

\displaystyle p_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})=e^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{i}^{2}+1)^{2},

and define also

\displaystyle E_{n}=\big\{(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{3n}\;\big|\;e^{p_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})/2}<|\mathbf{k}_{n}|<e^{p_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})}\big\}.

(12)

Note that with this definition, we have
|\mathbf{k}_{n}|>\max(|\mathbf{k}_{1}|,\ldots,|\mathbf{k}_{n-1}|)
for all
n\geq 2

.
Then define

recursively for all

by

\displaystyle F_{n}=\mathcal{S}\big((F_{n-1}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap E_{n}\big),\quad F_{1}^{\complement}=\mathbb{R}^{3},

(13)

where we take
\mathcal{S}
to denote a symmetrizing operator for sets, such that for any set
X
that is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3N}
for some
N
, we take
\mathcal{S}X
to be the set of all
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}
for which a permutation of
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{N})
exists that is a member of
X
.
Note also that we take
X^{\complement}
to denote the complement of
X
.

Let then
T
and
T^{\complement}
be the two sequences of sets given by

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=(\emptyset,\,F_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,F_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,F_{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\ldots\,),

(14)

\displaystyle T^{\complement}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{R}^{3},\,F_{1}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,F_{2}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,F_{3}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\ldots\,).

And let furthermore
\hat{1}_{T}
and
\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}
denote the two operators given for all
\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{T}\psi

\displaystyle=(1_{\emptyset}\psi_{0},\,1_{F_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{1},\,1_{F_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{2},\,1_{F_{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{3},\,\ldots\,),

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\psi

\displaystyle=(1_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{0},\,1_{F_{1}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{1},\,1_{F_{2}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{2},\,1_{F_{3}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{3},\,\ldots\,),

where we for any set,
X
, we take
1_{X}
to denote the indicator function of
X
.
Note also that we take the product of any two functions,
f
and
g
(which could be e.g.

and
\psi_{n}
in this case), to denote the function given by
x\mapsto f(x)g(x)
,
x\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)\cap\mathrm{Dom}(g)
.

With these definitions, we are now ready to define two subsets of
\mathbf{H}
, the intersection of which we will choose as our domain for
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
.
Let first

be the set of all

\displaystyle\|\hat{1}_{T}\hat{A}^{+}\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\psi\|<\infty.

And

\displaystyle\ket{\hat{A}\psi}\in\mathbf{H},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})=V\cap U.

(18)

Proposition 1

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\mathbf{H}

In order to show this in the following sections, we will start by introducing a set of vectors,
W
, which we hope to show is a subset of both

U
. And we also hope to show that this

\mathbf{H}

1
. Then we want to show that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is symmetric on its domain. And finally we want to show that the domain of its adjoint,
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})

\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

.

Showing that

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{-}_{n,n}\chi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{-1_{D_{n-1}^{\complement}}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})}{\sqrt{n-1}\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{j}|}}\chi_{n-2}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{j}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k}_{j}).

Let us then compare this to

(33)

\displaystyle=\frac{1_{D_{n-1}}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})}{\sqrt{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{j}|}}\chi_{n-2}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{j}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k}_{j})

\displaystyle=(1_{D_{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{n-2}\chi_{n-2})(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p}),

where have used Eq. (

\displaystyle\chi_{n,j}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})

by
-\mathbf{k}_{j}-\mathbf{k}_{n}
for the integral. We can also note that both

\displaystyle\|\chi_{n,j}\|^{2}\leq\frac{e^{-2n}}{n}\|\chi_{n-2}\|^{2}\int\frac{1_{B_{1,3}^{\complement}}(\mathbf{k}_{n})}{|\mathbf{k}_{n}|^{5}}\,d\mathbf{k}_{n}\int\frac{1}{(\mathbf{k}_{j}^{2}+1)^{4}|\mathbf{k}_{j}|}\,d\mathbf{k}_{j}.

\displaystyle\int\frac{1_{B_{1,3}^{\complement}}(\mathbf{k})}{|\mathbf{k}|^{5}}\,d\mathbf{k}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{4\pi}{k^{3}}\,dk=\Big[\frac{-2\pi}{k^{2}}\Big]_{1}^{\infty}=2\pi,

\|v_{1}+\ldots+v_{n}\|^{2}\leq n^{2}\max(\|v_{1}\|^{2},\ldots,\|v_{n}\|^{2})

) and look at what we get in the cases when

(47)

Next we see that since

\displaystyle\int\frac{\big|f_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n})\big|^{2}}{(\mathbf{k}^{2}+1)^{4}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\,d\mathbf{k}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{4\pi}{(k^{2}+1)^{4}}\,dk=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{4\pi}{(k^{2}+1)^{4}}\,dk+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{4\pi}{(k^{2}+1)^{4}}\,dk

\displaystyle\bigg|\int\frac{\chi_{n-2}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{j}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p})}{f_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n})^{-1}(\mathbf{k}_{j}^{2}+1)^{2}|\mathbf{k}_{j}|}\,d\mathbf{k}_{j}\bigg|^{2}\leq 5\pi\int d\mathbf{k}_{j}\,\big|\chi_{n-2}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{j}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p})\big|^{2}

(49)

almost everywhere.

6
The density of
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
in
\mathbf{H}

In this section, we want to argue that the vectors in
W
span a dense subspace of
\mathbf{H}
, which means that we can get arbitrarily close to any vector in
\mathbf{H}
, i.e. in the norm topology of
\mathbf{H}
, by taking the sum of some finite set of vectors in
W
. And since
W\subset\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
, giving us that
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\mathbf{H}
.

To show that
\mathrm{span}(W)
is dense in
\mathbf{H}
, let us consider all
\ket{\chi}=\ket{\chi_{m}}+\ket{\chi_{m+2}}+\ldots\in W

\chi_{m}
is smooth and has compact support bounded by some
R>0
. Note that the set of all such
\chi_{m}

. For any such
\chi_{m}

(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{m+1};\mathbf{p})\in\mathrm{supp}(1_{F_{m+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{m}\chi_{m})

|\mathbf{k}_{1}|,\ldots,|\mathbf{k}_{m+1}|

\exp[p_{m+1}(R,\ldots,R)]=\exp[\exp(m+1)\prod_{i=1}^{m}(R^{2}+1)^{2}]
. This means that there exists a sufficiently large
R^{\prime}
such that
F_{m+1}\subset B_{R^{\prime},3}^{m+1}
. And we can therefore let
D_{m+1}\supset F_{m+1}
be equal to
B_{R^{\prime},3}^{m+1}
.

Let us then first confirm that such a
\ket{\chi}
is a member of
W
. We can first of all note that the fact that
\chi_{m}
is smooth and with compact support means that both
\|\chi_{m}\|
and
\|\hat{A}^{-}_{m}\chi_{m}\|
are finite. And since
D_{m+1}=B_{R^{\prime},3}^{m+1}
for some finite
R^{\prime}
, we also get that
\|1_{D_{m+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{m}\chi_{m}\|<\infty
. And the only remaining constraint on
W
that we need to check is then that
\ket{\chi}\in\mathbf{H}
in the first place, which requires that
\|\chi\|<\infty
. But from Eq. (
46
), we see that

\displaystyle\|\chi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\in\{m,m+2,m+4\ldots\}}^{\infty}\prod_{j=2,4,6,\ldots}^{n-m}\Big((2\pi)^{2}je^{-2(m+j)}\Big)\|\chi_{m}\|^{2}.

(60)

And it is easy to show that this expression is finite whenever
\|\chi_{m}\|
is. And in fact, we can also see, by a similar argument as we used to write up Eq. (
58
), that there exists a positive constant,
C_{2}
, such that

\displaystyle\|\chi\|^{2}\leq C_{2}\|\chi_{m}\|^{2}

(61)

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\ket{\chi}=\ket{\chi_{m}}+\ket{\chi_{m+2}}+\ldots\in W
.

If we then look at Eq. (
20
), we can see that
\chi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})
for all
n>m
is a sum of

(n-1)(n-3)\cdots(m+1)
terms.
And we see that all these include a factor of
1_{D_{m+1}^{\complement}}(\mathbf{k}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{m+1}^{\prime})
, where
(\mathbf{k}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1}^{\prime})
is some permutation of
(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1})
that differs for each term. Since we have that
D_{m+1}^{\complement}=(B_{R^{\prime},3}^{m+1})^{\complement}
, if we therefore let
R^{\prime}
tend towards infinity, which we are allowed to do, we then see that each of these terms will tend to zero pointwise. Moreover, if we regard each term as the formula for a function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3n+3})
, we see that all these functions will tend towards a zero vector in the norm topology of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3n+3})
. And we thus get that
\ket{\chi}\to\ket{\chi_{m}}
for all such vectors when
R^{\prime}\to\infty
.

Now, since the set of all such
\chi_{m}
functions spans
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3m+3})
, we can therefore get arbitrarily close to any particular
\ket{\psi_{m}}
this way,
\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
. And since we can do this for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we thus get that the vectors in
W
span a dense subspace of
\mathbf{H}
. Therefore, since
W\subset\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
, it follows that
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
is dense in
\mathbf{H}
.

7
The symmetry of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}

In this section, we will show that the operator
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
, which we recall is given by
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\hat{A}\psi}
for all
\ket{\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
, is
symmetric
in the sense that

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}\phi|\psi}=\braket{\phi|\hat{A}\psi}

(62)

for all
\ket{\phi}\!,\ket{\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
.

Let us recall, first of all, that

\displaystyle\hat{A}\psi=(\hat{A}^{-}_{1}\psi_{1},\;\hat{A}^{+}_{0}\psi_{0}+\hat{A}^{-}_{2}\psi_{2},\;\hat{A}^{+}_{1}\psi_{1}+\hat{A}^{-}_{3}\psi_{3},\,\ldots)

(63)

with

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{+}_{n-1}\psi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{i}|}}\psi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{i}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k}_{i}),

(64)

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{-}_{n}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{n}|}}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{i-1},\mathbf{k}_{n},\mathbf{k}_{i},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{k}_{n})\,d\mathbf{k}_{n}.

(65)

To show that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is symmetric, we thus want to show that the difference between the two series given by

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}\phi|\psi}

\displaystyle=\braket{\hat{A}^{-}_{1}\phi_{1}|\psi_{0}}+\braket{\hat{A}^{+}_{0}\phi_{0}+\hat{A}^{-}_{2}\phi_{2}|\psi_{1}}+\braket{\hat{A}^{+}_{1}\phi_{1}+\hat{A}^{-}_{3}\phi_{3}|\psi_{2}}+\ldots,

(66)

\displaystyle\braket{\phi|\hat{A}\psi}

\displaystyle=\braket{\phi_{0}|\hat{A}^{-}_{1}\psi_{1}}+\braket{\phi_{1}|\hat{A}^{+}_{0}\psi_{0}+\hat{A}^{-}_{2}\psi_{2}}+\braket{\phi_{2}|\hat{A}^{+}_{1}\psi_{1}+\hat{A}^{-}_{3}\psi_{3}}+\ldots

is zero.

Let us then consider the following cut-off versions of
\hat{A}^{+}_{n}
and
\hat{A}^{-}_{n}
, call them
\hat{A}^{R+}_{n}
and
\hat{A}^{R-}_{n}
, defined for all
\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
by

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{R+}_{n-1}\psi_{n-1}

\displaystyle=1_{(B_{R,3}\setminus B_{1/R,3})^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{n-1}\psi_{n-1},

(67)

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{R-}_{n}\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=1_{(B_{R,3}\setminus B_{1/R,3})^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{-}_{n}(1_{(B_{R,3}\setminus B_{1/R,3})^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{n}).

We see that this gives us

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{n-1}\phi_{n-1}|\psi_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(B_{R,3}\setminus B_{1/R,3})^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{k}_{n}\,d\mathbf{p}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{i}|}}

(68)

\displaystyle\phi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{i}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k}_{i})^{*}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle\braket{\phi_{n-1}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{n}\psi_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(B_{R,3}\setminus B_{1/R,3})^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{k}_{n}\,d\mathbf{p}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{n}|}}

\displaystyle\phi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p})^{*}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{i-1},\mathbf{k}_{n},\mathbf{k}_{i},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n-1};\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{k}_{i}).

And from a simple renaming of
\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n}
,
we can easily see that these two expression are equal, giving us

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{n-1}\phi_{n-1}|\psi_{n}}=\braket{\phi_{n-1}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{n}\psi_{n}}

(69)

for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and
R\geq 1
.
Let us therefore define

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R}\phi|\psi}_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\big(\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{n-1}\phi_{n-1}|\psi_{n}}+\braket{\hat{A}^{R-}_{n+1}\phi_{n+1}|\psi_{n}}\big),

(70)

\displaystyle\braket{\phi|\hat{A}^{R}\psi}_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\big(\braket{\phi_{n}|\hat{A}^{R+}_{n-1}\psi_{n-1}}+\braket{\phi_{n}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{n+1}\psi_{n+1}}\big),

where we let
\hat{A}^{R}
be defined similarly as in Eq. (
7
), and let
\hat{A}^{R+}_{-1}=\hat{A}^{R-}_{0}=0
. We then see that

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R}\phi|\psi}_{N}-\braket{\phi|\hat{A}^{R}\psi}_{N}

\displaystyle=\braket{\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\phi_{N+1}|\psi_{N}}+\braket{\phi_{N}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\psi_{N+1}},

(71)

namely since all other terms cancel each other. Thus, since the left-hand side of this equation converges to
\braket{\hat{A}\phi|\psi}-\braket{\phi|\hat{A}\psi}
when
N,R\to\infty
, we get that

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}\phi|\psi}-\braket{\phi|\hat{A}\psi}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\big(\braket{\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\phi_{N+1}|\psi_{N}}+\braket{\phi_{N}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\psi_{N+1}}\big).

(72)

So if we can only show that
\braket{\phi_{N}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\psi_{N+1}}\to 0
when
N,R\to\infty
for any
\ket{\phi}\!,\ket{\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
, we will get that both terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
72
) will tend to zero in this limit, which will thus give us that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is symmetric.

In Section
4
, we showed that

F_{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\subset F_{n}^{\complement}

for all

, which also implies that

\mathcal{S}(F_{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\mathcal{S}F_{n}^{\complement}=F_{n}^{\complement}.

(74)

And since
\hat{A}^{+}_{N}1_{F_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{N}

\hat{A}^{+}_{N}1_{F_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{N}
is zero everywhere on
F_{N+1}
. So if we split
\phi
and
\psi

\displaystyle\phi=\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\phi+\hat{1}_{T}\phi\equiv\phi^{\prime}+\phi^{\prime\prime},\quad\psi=\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\psi+\hat{1}_{T}\psi\equiv\psi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime\prime},

(75)

we therefore see that

\displaystyle\braket{\phi_{N}^{\prime\prime}|\hat{A}^{R-}_{N+1}\psi_{N+1}^{\prime\prime}}=\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N}\phi_{N}^{\prime\prime}|\psi_{N+1}^{\prime\prime}}=0

for all
N

This motivates us to analyze
\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}|\psi_{N}^{\prime}}
,
\psi^{\prime}=\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\psi

and

.
In order to do this, let us first split up

\displaystyle X_{n,\mathbf{i}}=(B_{i_{1}+1,3}\setminus B_{i_{1},3})\times\cdots\times(B_{i_{n}+1,3}\setminus B_{i_{n},3})

for all

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}=1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi^{\prime}_{N}.

for all

\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{1}=(1,0,0)
, in order to write this exponent more compactly.

Assuming that this lemma is true, let us see how this gives us that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is symmetric.
First we note that

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}|\psi_{N,\mathbf{i}}^{\prime}}=\braket{1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}|\psi_{N,\mathbf{i}}^{\prime}}\leq\|1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}\|\|\psi_{N,\mathbf{i}}^{\prime}\|.

(80)

Now, because
\ket{\psi}\in V
, we know that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|1_{F_{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{n}\psi^{\prime}_{n}\|^{2}
converges, which means that for any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
,
we have

\|1_{F_{N+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{N}\psi^{\prime}_{N}\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{1}

(81)

for any sufficiently large
N
.
Combining this with Eq. (
79
), we therefore get that

\|\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2\pi C_{3}}e^{-p^{\prime}_{N+1}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})}

(82)

for any
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}

Let us then also derive an upper bound for
\|1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}\|
. First let us define

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{+}_{n-1,l}\chi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}_{l}|}}\chi_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{l\,}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k}_{l})

\displaystyle\|1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1,l}\phi_{N-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{N}(2i_{l}+1)\|\phi_{N-1}\|^{2}.

converges, which means that for all

\|\phi_{N-1}\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{2}
for any sufficiently large

.
We therefore get that

\displaystyle\|1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1,l}\phi_{N-1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\pi}{N}(2i_{l}+1)\varepsilon_{2}.

(86)

And by using the fact again that
\|v_{1}+\ldots+v_{n}\|^{2}\leq n^{2}\max(\|v_{1}\|^{2},\ldots,\|v_{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\|1_{X_{N,\mathbf{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}\|^{2}\leq 2\pi N(2i_{l}+1)\varepsilon_{2}.

(87)

So from Eq. (
80

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}|\psi_{N,\mathbf{i}}^{\prime}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}}{C_{3}}}\sqrt{N(2i_{l}+1)}e^{-p^{\prime}_{N+1}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})/2}

for all

. Thus, since we can choose arbitrarily small

and
\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N-1}\phi_{N-1}|\psi_{N}^{\prime}}=0

,

\ket{\phi}\!,\ket{\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\braket{\hat{A}^{R+}_{N}\phi_{N}^{\prime\prime}|\psi_{N+1}^{\prime}}

In order to do this, let us first define

\displaystyle\infty>\lim_{R,M\to\infty}\lim_{R_{0},\ldots,R_{M},a\to\infty}\frac{\braket{\phi|\psi^{\prime\prime}}}{\|\psi\|}\geq\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\braket{(\hat{A}\phi)_{m}|(\hat{A}^{R}\phi)_{m}}}{\sqrt{C_{2}}\big\|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ket{\chi^{(m)}_{m}}\big\|}=\frac{\|\hat{A}\phi\|^{2}}{\sqrt{C_{2}}\|\hat{A}\phi\|}=\frac{\|\hat{A}\phi\|}{\sqrt{C_{2}}}

(124)

for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})
, where we have also used Eq. (
118
) to recognize
\chi^{(m)}_{m}
as
(\hat{A}^{R}\phi)_{m}
to get the third equality.
This shows us that
\|\hat{A}\phi\|<\infty
for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})
, and we therefore get that
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})\subset U
.

To then show that
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})\subset V
, we will use a similar strategy, but choose

and

\displaystyle\chi^{(m)}_{m}=1_{F_{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{m-1}1_{F_{m-1}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{m-1}

(125)

instead for all

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\hat{A}^{+}_{m}\chi^{(m)}_{m})\subset\big(\mathcal{S}(F_{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(126)

And since we have from Eq. (

(127)

(128)

So for our

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(1_{D^{(m)}_{m+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{m}\chi^{(m)}_{m})\subset(F_{m+1}\cap F_{m+1}^{\complement})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}=\emptyset.

(129)

\displaystyle\braket{\phi|\psi^{\prime}}=\sum_{m=2}^{M}\braket{\phi_{m-1}|\hat{A}^{-}_{m}\chi^{(m)}_{m}}=\sum_{m=2}^{M}\braket{\phi_{m-1}|\hat{A}^{-}_{m}1_{F_{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{R+}_{m-1}1_{F_{m-1}^{\complement}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{m-1}}.

\displaystyle\braket{\phi|\psi^{\prime}}

\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})\subset U\cap V=\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})=\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

for all
\psi\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is self-adjoint.
And since we have thus shown both that
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
is dense in
\mathbf{H}
and that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is self-adjoint, we hereby obtain Proposition
1
.

9
Conclusion

We have defined a simplified version of the Dirac interaction operator,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
, with no cutoffs on a certain domain. We have then shown, in Sections
4
–
6
, that this domain is dense in the Hilbert space. In Section
7
, we have furthermore shown that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is symmetric on the domain. And finally, in Section
8
, we have shown that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is also self-adjoint on the domain, thus obtaining Proposition
1
.

Appendix A
Symmetrizing the Hilbert space

In this appendix, we will briefly discuss how to extend the result of this paper to a restricted Hilbert space that only includes vectors,
\ket{\psi}
, where each
\psi_{n}
is symmetric with respect to the
\mathbf{k}
-coordinates.
For this, one can define a symmetrizing operator, call it
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
, given by
\hat{\mathcal{S}}\psi=(\psi_{0},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\psi_{1},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\psi_{2},\ldots)
, with each
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}
given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{n!}\big(\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n};\mathbf{p})+\text{all other permutations of $\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{n}$}\big).

(135)

It is easy to then show that
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
is bounded from above by 1, and that it commutes with
\hat{A}
. One can also see that
\ket{\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
implies
\ket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\psi}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
.

One way to proceed from there is then to show that
\braket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi|\hat{\mathcal{S}}\psi}=\braket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi|\psi}
for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
,
which will give us that
\braket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi|\hat{A}\psi}=\braket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi|\hat{\mathcal{S}}\hat{A}\psi}=\braket{\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi|\hat{A}\hat{\mathcal{S}}\psi}
. This tells us that
\hat{\mathcal{S}}\phi
is a member of the symmetrized version of
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})
if and only if it is also a member of the original
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*})
. And it follows that the symmetrized version of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
, (on the symmetrized version of
\mathbf{H}
) is also self-adjoint.

Appendix B
Adding a free energy to the operator

Let us here briefly discuss how to potentially make the technique of this paper work when we add a free energy,
\hat{H}_{0}
, to the operator. This
\hat{H}_{0}
should go asymptotically as
|\mathbf{k}_{1}|+\ldots+|\mathbf{k}_{n}|+|\mathbf{p}|
.
Being able to add this free energy is of course very important if we hope to extend this result to physically meaningful Hamiltonians.

Now,
\mathrm{Dom}(\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})
and
W
has actually been deliberately constructed in this paper such that
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\chi}
also has a finite norm for all
\ket{\chi}\in W
where the norm of
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\chi_{m}}
is finite. We should thus still be able to show that
W
is a subset of
U
when we alter
U
to be the subset of all
\ket{\psi}\in\mathbf{H}
for which
(\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}})\ket{\psi}\in\mathbf{H}
.

For the part of the proof in Section
8
, which aims to show that
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
is self-adjoint, we can then see that we also ought to redefine
V
in order to make this proof work, namely as the set of all
\ket{\psi}
for which

\displaystyle\|\hat{1}_{T}\hat{A}^{+}\hat{1}_{T^{\complement}}\psi+\hat{1}_{T}\hat{A}^{0}\psi\|<\infty,

(136)

where we define
\ket{\hat{A}^{0}\psi}=\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\psi}
.

This, however, also means that the proof in Section
7
, which aims to show that
\hat{H}
is symmetric, has to change, and in particular the part that shows the lemma of Eq. (
93
). This particular task is not so trivial, but is should not be too hard, still. Intuitively, one can see that in order for
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\psi}
to interfere with the lower bound on
\|1_{F_{N+1,n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{N,n}\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}\|
, so to speak,
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\psi_{N+1}}
then has to cancel some of
\ket{1_{F_{N+1,n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{N,n}\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}}

\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\psi_{N+1}}
to cancel any significant portion of
\ket{1_{F_{N+1,n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{A}^{+}_{N,n}\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}}

\psi_{N+1}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{N+1})
to go approximately as
1_{F_{N+1,n}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{N}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{k}_{n}},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{N+1})/|\mathbf{k}_{n}|^{3/2}

p^{\prime}_{N+1}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})\|\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}\|/2

p^{\prime}_{N+1}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
is a rapidly growing function, it will thus mean that
\|\psi_{N+1}\|

\|\psi^{\prime}_{N,\mathbf{i}}\|
in order to interfere with our bound, which should not be possible if
\|\psi\|
converges.

Thus, we should be able to change the proof of Eq. (
93
) to accommodate for the altered
V
when we add
\hat{H}_{0}
to the operator. And since this part seems to be the only one that is potentially somewhat tricky, at least at an overall glance, we can therefore see that we might very well be able to extend Proposition
1
to such
operators that include a free energy.

Appendix C
Handling the perturbed vacuum

A big hope is
that the technique
of this paper
can also at some point be extended to a theory such as the full theory of quantum electrodynamics (QED).
One of the key tasks for doing this, as can be seen e.g. in Damgaard
[
6
]
, is to find a way to handle the infinite vacuum fluctuations that seem to appear when negative-energy fermion solutions are reinterpreted as antiparticles. However, there might be a solution to handle this perturbed vacuum if one can only show that the vacuum-perturbing part of the Hamiltonian becomes self-adjoint if it is multiplied by factors of
1/\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}
, with
\mathcal{V}
being volume of the system, before going to the continuum limit. If this can be achieved, the idea is then to use this to show that the
\varepsilon
-almost eigenstates of the perturbed vacuum converge in the continuum limit. And from there it seems that one might be able to show that the transition amplitudes of the interaction between such a ‘‘vacuum state’’ and any ‘‘physical state’’ will vanish in this limit.
6
6

6

And even if the transition amplitudes do not vanish, perturbation theory still tells us that the growing energy of such vacuum solutions in the continuum limit,
which comes when we put back the factors of
\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}
,
ought to make the interaction vanish anyway between any physical state and any vacuum
state
that does not have a vanishing energy to begin with.

This would then tell us that the perturbed vacuum does not interact with the physical particles of the system, and that we can simply remove the vacuum-perturbing terms in the Hamiltonian.
Needless to say, this would be an incredible prospect.
However, it requires us to be able to prove the self-adjointness of such operators first.

Intermediate spaces, Gaussian probabilities and exponential tightness
Paolo Baldi

Abstract
We prove the existence of an intermediate Banach space between the space where the Gaussian measure lives and its RKHS, thus extending what happens with Wiener measure, where the intermediate space can be chosen as a space of Hölder paths. From this result it is very simple to deduce a result of exponential tightness for Gaussian probabilities.



AMS 2000 subject classification:
60F10, 60B12

Key words and phrases:
Gaussian probabilities, interpolation theory.

1
Introduction

Let
E={\mathcal{C}}_{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the space of continuous
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued paths starting at
0
and endowed with the sup norm and let
\mu
be the Wiener measure on it. It is well known that the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) of this Gaussian probability is the space
{\mathcal{H}}=H^{1}_{0}([0,T])
of the paths
\gamma
vanishing at
0
, that are absolutely continuous and have a square integrable derivative.

Let us denote by
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}_{\alpha}\subset E
the space of the paths that are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and whose modulus of continuity

\omega(\delta)=\sup_{{0\leq s<t\leq T\atop|t-s|\leq\delta}}|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)|

is such that

\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{\alpha}}=0\ .

This space (whose elements are sometimes called the “small”
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions) is separable and it is also well known (thanks to Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem) that, for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
,
\mu({\mathcal{C}}^{0}_{\alpha})=1
(whereas
\mu({\mathcal{H}})=0
). It is also well known that, still for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
,

E\hookleftarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{0}_{\alpha}\hookleftarrow{\mathcal{H}}

the embeddings being compact
.

We are concerned with the question whether the existence of such an “intermediate” space is a general fact, i.e. true for every centered Gaussian probability on a separable Banach space.

More precisely we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
E
be a separable Banach space,
\mu
a centered Gaussian probability on
E
and
{\mathcal{H}}
the corresponding RKHS. Then there exists a Banach space
\widetilde{E}
, separable and such that

a)
\mu(\widetilde{E})=1
and

b)
the embeddings

E\hookleftarrow\widetilde{E}\hookleftarrow{\mathcal{H}}

are compact.

We shall even prove that there are infinitely many such spaces. We shall call “intermediate space” any separable Banach space satisfying a) and b) of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is the object of §
3
. Of course Theorem
1.1
is obvious if
E
is finite dimensional, as then we can choose
E=\widetilde{E}={\mathcal{H}}
. Therefore in the sequel we implicitly assume that
E
is infinite dimensional.

In the proof we shall also assume that
E=\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)
. Otherwise just consider
\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)
instead of
E
.

This investigation was motivated by an application to the Large Deviations of the sequence of probabilities
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
,
\mu_{\varepsilon}
being the image of
\mu
through the map
x\mapsto\varepsilon x
. The property of exponential tightness is a key step in the proof of these estimates. One remarks that its proof in the case of Wiener measure is particularly simple and is based, besides Fernique’s theorem, on the existence of the spaces of Hölder continuous functions, which are intermediate spaces. Thanks to Theorem
1.1
the same, simple proof of exponential tightness for the Wiener measure works for a general Gaussian probability on a separable Banach space as developed in §
2
.

§
4
is devoted to comments and complements.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is largely inspired to the arguments of the fundamental papers of L.Gross
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.

2
Motivation: exponential tightness of Gaussian measures

Let
\mu
be a centered Gaussian probability on the separable Banach space
E
and let
\mu_{\varepsilon}
be its image through the map
x\mapsto\varepsilon x
as above.

The Large Deviations properties of the family
\mu_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
are well understood since a long time (see see
[
4
]
§5 e.g.). One of the main steps in this investigation is to prove that the family
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
exponentially tight
at speed
\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon^{2}
, i.e. that for every
R>0
there exists a compact set
K_{R}\subset E
such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\mu_{\varepsilon}(K_{R}^{c})\leq-R\ .

(2.1)

This fact follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
: let us denote
\|\kern 5.0pt\|_{i}
the norm of the intermediate space
\widetilde{E}
, as
\|\kern 5.0pt\|_{i}
is
\mu
-a.s. finite (this is a) of Theorem
1.1
), by Fernique’s theorem (
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
), for some
\rho>0
, we have

\int_{E}{\rm e}^{\rho\|x\|_{i}^{2}}\,d\mu(x):=C_{\rho}<+\infty\ .

Let
K_{R}
denote the ball of radius
\sqrt{R/\rho}
of
\widetilde{E}
, which is compact in
E
.
From

C_{\rho}\geq\int_{K_{R}^{c}/\varepsilon}{\rm e}^{\rho\|x\|_{i}^{2}}\,d\mu(x)\geq\mu({\textstyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}K_{R}^{c}){\rm e}^{R/\varepsilon^{2}}

we deduce

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\mu_{\varepsilon}(K_{R}^{c})=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{2}\log\mu({\textstyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}K_{R}^{c})\leq-R\ ,

i.e. (
2.1
).

3
Proof of the main result

From now on
\mu
will denote a Gaussian probability on the infinite dimensional separable Banach space
E
as in the introduction,
\|\kern 5.0pt\|
being the norm of the Banach space
E
.

If we denote by
E^{\prime}
the dual of
E
, then to every continuous functional
\xi\in E^{\prime}
we can associate the r.v.
(E,\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

x\mapsto\langle\xi,x\rangle
. Let
E^{\prime}_{\mu}
be the completion of
E^{\prime}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. This is a separable Hilbert space which is also a Gaussian space.

For every
g\in E^{\prime}_{\mu}
the vectors

h=\int_{E}xg(x)\,d\mu(x)

(3.2)

form a vector space
{\mathcal{H}}\subset E
which, endowed with the scalar product

\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\int_{E}g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)\,d\mu(x)

for

h_{1}=\int_{E}xg_{1}(x)\,d\mu(x),\qquad h_{2}=\int_{E}xg_{2}(x)\,d\mu(x)\ ,

is an Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
isometric to
E^{\prime}_{\mu}
. Remark that (
3.2
) can also be written
h={\rm E}[Xg(X)]
,
X
denoting an
E
-valued r.v. having law equal to
\mu
.
{\mathcal{H}}
is called the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) of
\mu
.

For more details on the structure of Gaussian probabilities see
[
9
]
or the very nice and very short presentation in §2 of
[
3
]
.

Proof
of Theorem
1.1
. Recall that we assume
E
to be infinite dimensional.

a)
First step: construction of
\widetilde{E}
.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\rm P})
be a probability space and
X:\Omega\to E
a Gaussian r.v. having distribution
\mu
. Let
(g_{n})_{n}
be an orthonormal system of the Hilbert space
E^{\prime}_{\mu}
. This forms also a sequence of independent
N(0,1)
-distributed r.v.’s. Let
e_{n}={\rm E}[Xg_{n}(X)]
.
e_{n}\in E
and
(e_{n})_{n}
is an orthonormal system of the RKHS
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Then it is well known (see Proposition 3.6 p. 64 in
[
9
]
) that the sequence

X_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}e_{j}

(3.3)

is a square integrable
E
-valued martingale converging a.s. and in
L^{2}
to
X
. Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm E}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}g_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)=0\ .

Let
\alpha>0
be fixed and let
(n_{k})_{k}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
n_{0}=0
and

{\rm E}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{\infty}g_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)\leq 2^{-k(3+2\alpha)}\ .

(3.4)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}=\mathop{\rm span}(e_{n_{k}+1},\dots,e_{n_{k+1}})
and let
Q_{k}
be the projector
{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. Let, for the vector
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\|x\|_{i}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k\alpha}\Big\|\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\Big\|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k\alpha}\|Q_{k}(x)\|\ .

(3.5)

\|\kern 5.0pt\|_{i}
is a norm on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Actually Lemma
3.2
below states that
\|x\|_{i}<+\infty
for every
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
while subadditivity and positive homogeneity are immediate.
Let

W_{k}:=\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}g_{j}e_{j}\ .

(3.6)

The
E
-valued r.v.’s
W_{k}
are Gaussian and independent. Remark that, with our choice of the numbers
n_{k}
, thanks to the Markov inequality we have

{\rm P}(2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|\geq 2^{-k})\leq 2^{2k(1+\alpha)}\,{\rm E}(\|W_{k}\|^{2})\leq 2^{2k(1+\alpha)}\,{\rm E}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{\infty}g_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)\leq 2^{-k}\ .

(3.7)

We can now define
\widetilde{E}=
the completion of
{\mathcal{H}}
with respect to the norm
\|\kern 5.0pt\|_{i}
. Remark that, as for
x\in{\mathcal{H}}

\|x\|_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k\alpha}\|Q_{k}(x)\|\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|Q_{k}(x)\|\geq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(x)\Big\|=\|x\|\ ,

we have
\widetilde{E}\subset E
. It is also obvious that
\widetilde{E}
is dense in
E
, as it contains
{\mathcal{H}}
which is itself dense in
E
.

b)
Second step:
\mu(\widetilde{E})=1
. Let

Y_{k}=X_{n_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}g_{j}e_{j}

and let us prove that
(Y_{k})_{k}
, as a sequence of
\widetilde{E}
-valued r.v.’s, converges in probability. We proceed quite similarly as in the proof of the subsequent Lemma
3.2
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. If
p_{0}
is such that
2^{-p_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, then for
p_{0}\leq\ell<r
,

\hfil\displaystyle{\rm P}(\|Y_{r}-Y_{\ell}\|_{i}>\varepsilon)\leq{\rm P}\Bigl(\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{r}2^{\alpha k}\|W_{k}\|>\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{r}2^{-k}\Bigr)\leq

\hfil\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{r}{\rm P}\bigl(2^{\alpha k}\|W_{k}\|>2^{-k}\bigr)\leq\sum_{k=p_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\leq 2\cdot 2^{-p_{0}}=\varepsilon\ .

Therefore
(Y_{k})_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence in probability in
\widetilde{E}
, hence it converges, in probability, to some
\widetilde{E}
-valued r.v.
\widetilde{X}
. As
\widetilde{E}\subset E
and its topology is stronger,
(Y_{k})_{k}
also converges in probability in
E
. But we know already that
(Y_{k})_{k}
in
E
converges to a r.v.
X
having law
\mu
. Hence
\widetilde{X}=X
a.s. and

\mu(\widetilde{E})={\rm P}(X\in\widetilde{E})={\rm P}(\widetilde{X}\in\widetilde{E})=1
.

c)
Step three: the embedding
E\hookleftarrow\widetilde{E}
is compact.

Let
(x_{p})_{p}
be a bounded sequence in
\widetilde{E}
and
(z_{p})_{p}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
another sequence such that
\|x_{p}-z_{p}\|_{i}<2^{-p}
, which is possible as
{\mathcal{H}}
is dense in
\widetilde{E}
. Let
M
be such that
\|z_{p}\|_{i}\leq M
for every
p
. As the projectors
Q_{k}
are finite dimensional, for every
k
there exists a subsequence
(p_{r}^{(k)})_{r}
such that
\|Q_{k}z_{p_{r}^{(k)}}-y^{(k)}\|_{i}\to 0
for some vector
y^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, i.e. of the form

y^{(k)}=\sum_{m=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\alpha_{m}e_{m}\ .

By the diagonal argument there exists a subsequence
(p^{\prime}_{r})_{r}
such that

\|Q_{k}z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-y^{(k)}\|_{i}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
for every
k
. Let now

\varepsilon>0
be fixed. We have, for every positive integer
k_{0}
,

\|z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}}\|=\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|+\Bigl\|\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\ .

(3.8)

We first choose
k_{0}
so that
M\,2^{-\alpha k_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
, so that

\hfil\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}\Bigl\|Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\leq

\hfil\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\alpha k_{0}}\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\alpha k}\Bigl\|Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\leq 2^{-\alpha k_{0}}\|z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}}\|_{i}\leq 2^{-\alpha k_{0}}\cdot 2M\leq\frac{2}{3}\,\varepsilon

and then
p_{0}
so that, for
r,\ell\geq p_{0}

\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}Q_{k}(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}}-z_{p^{\prime}_{\ell}})\Bigr\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\ \cdotp

Therefore
(z_{p^{\prime}_{r}})_{r}
is a Cauchy sequence in
E
, hence also
(x_{p^{\prime}_{r}})_{r}
which proves the compactness of the embedding
\widetilde{E}\hookrightarrow E
.

d)
Last step
\widetilde{E}\hookleftarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is compact. This is immediate as,
\widetilde{E}
being dense in
E
,
{\mathcal{H}}
is also the RKHS of the Gaussian probability
\mu
on
\widetilde{E}
. Such an embedding is always compact.

\blacksquare

Lemma 3.1

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite dimensional Hilbert space and
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{K}}
a subspace and let us denote by
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
the standard
N(0,I)
distributions on
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
respectively. Let
B\subset{\mathcal{K}}
be a convex, centrally symmetric, convex set. Then

\nu(B)\leq\nu^{\prime}({\mathcal{F}}\cap B)\ .

For the proof of Lemma
3.1
the reader is directed to
[
7
]
as this is a weaker version of Lemma 4.1 there.

Lemma 3.2

\|x\|_{i}<+\infty
for every
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof
Let us first prove that the sequence of real r.v.’s

Z_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|

converges in probability.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
p_{0}
such that
2^{-p_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Remark that this implies
\varepsilon>\sum_{k=p_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-k}
. For
p_{0}\leq n<m
, we have thanks to (
3.7
)

\hfil\displaystyle{\rm P}(|Z_{n}-Z_{m}|>\varepsilon)={\rm P}\Bigl(\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|>\varepsilon\Bigr)\leq{\rm P}\Bigl(\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|>\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}2^{-k}\Bigr)\leq

\hfil\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}{\rm P}\Bigl(2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|>2^{-k}\Bigr)\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}2^{-k}\leq\sum_{k=p_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-k}=2\cdot 2^{-p_{0}}<\varepsilon\ .

Hence
(Z_{n})_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in probability
and converges in probability to some real r.v.
Z
.

Let us prove that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we have
{\rm P}(Z<\varepsilon)>0
. Let

Z_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|,\qquad Z-Z_{N}=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|\ .

We have
{\rm P}(Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2})>0
, as
Z_{N}
depends only on the modulus of finitely many r.v.’s
W_{k}
each of them being Gaussian and with values in a finite dimensional vector space. Moreover let
N
be large enough so that

{\rm P}(Z-Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2})>0
. As the r.v.’s
Z-Z_{N}
and
Z_{N}
are independent (they depend on different
W_{k}
’s)

{\rm P}(Z<\varepsilon)\geq{\rm P}\Bigl(Z-Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)={\rm P}\Bigl(Z-Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr){\rm P}\Bigl(Z_{N}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)>0\ .

(3.9)

Finally, let us assume that there exists
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\|x\|_{i}=+\infty
and let us prove that this is absurd. Of course we can assume
\|x\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=1
. Let us consider the seminorms on
{\mathcal{H}}

\|z\|_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j\alpha}\|Q_{j}(z)\|

so that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z\|_{k}=\|z\|_{i}
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}_{k}=\mathop{\rm span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{k}})
so that the r.v.
X_{n_{k}}
of (
3.3
) takes values in
{\mathcal{K}}_{k}
. Let
x^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\alpha_{j}e_{j}
be the projection of
x
on
{\mathcal{K}}_{k}
. Remark that
\|x\|_{k}=\|x^{(k)}\|_{k}
. We apply Lemma
3.1
considering the convex set

B=\{z\in{\mathcal{K}}_{k},\|z\|_{k}\leq a\}

and
{\mathcal{F}}=\mathop{\rm span}(x^{(k)})
. Let
\xi_{k}=\sqrt{\alpha_{1}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{n_{k}}^{2}}
, so that the vector

\frac{x^{(k)}}{\xi_{k}}

has modulus
1
in
{\mathcal{H}}
and the r.v.

g:=\frac{1}{\xi_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}\alpha_{j}g_{j}

is
N(0,1)
-distributed. Hence the r.v.

\frac{1}{\xi_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}\alpha_{j}g_{j}\cdot\frac{x^{(k)}}{\xi_{k}}

is
N(0,1)
-distributed and
{\mathcal{F}}
-valued.

Let
a
be a continuity point of the partition function of
Z
. Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, as
\xi_{k}\to\|x\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=1
and we assume
\|x\|_{i}=+\infty
, we have

\hfil\displaystyle{\rm P}(Z\leq a)=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\rm P}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}g_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|_{i}\leq a\Bigr)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}{\rm P}\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{1}{\xi_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}\alpha_{j}g_{j}\Bigl|\frac{\|x\|_{k}}{\xi_{k}}\leq a\Bigr)=

\hfil\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\rm P}\Bigl(|g|\leq\frac{a\xi_{k}}{\|x\|_{k}}\Bigr)=0

which is in contradiction with (
3.9
) and completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

\blacksquare

4
Remarks and complements

Remark 4.1

In fact we have proved the existence on infinitely many intermediate spaces
\widetilde{E}
between
E
and
{\mathcal{H}}
(recall that we assume that
E
is infinite dimensional). Actually the argument above can be repeated in order to construct a subsequent intermediate space
\widetilde{E}_{1}
between
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\widetilde{E}
, which will be necessarily different of
\widetilde{E}
, the embedding

\widetilde{E}\hookleftarrow\widetilde{E}_{1}
being compact. And so on.

Remark 4.2

In a first attempt to prove Theorem
1.1
the author tried considering interpolation spaces. More precisely let, for
x\in E
,

K(t,x)=\inf_{a+b=x,a\in E,b\in{\mathcal{H}}}(\|a\|+t|b|_{{\mathcal{H}}})

and let, for
0<\theta<1
,

\|x\|_{\theta}=\sup_{t>0}t^{-\theta}K(t,x)\ .

(4.10)

Let us define the vector space
G_{\theta}
as the set of vectors
x\in E
such that
\|x\|_{\theta}<+\infty
, endowed with the norm
\|\kern 5.0pt\|_{\theta}
. See
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
or
[
12
]
for more details on this topic.

It is well known that
G_{\theta}
is a Banach space and also that the embeddings
E\hookleftarrow G_{\theta}\hookleftarrow{\mathcal{H}}
are compact (
[
10
]
, §V.2).

The question remains whether
\mu(G_{\theta})=1
.

In the case of the Wiener space,
E={\mathcal{C}}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,
{\mathcal{H}}=H^{1}_{0}
and
\mu=
the Wiener measure, it can be proved that

G_{\theta}
contains the space of small
\alpha
-Hölder paths for
\alpha>\theta
, which is a separable Banach space having Wiener measure
1
. This gives
\mu(G_{\theta})=1
for
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
, which however leaves open the question in the case
\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

The author does not know whether such a
Banach space
G_{\theta}
is also separable, but it can be proved that the closure of
H^{1}_{0}
in
G_{\theta}
,
\widetilde{G}_{\theta}
say, also contains the small
\alpha
-Hölder paths for
\alpha>\theta
. Hence
\widetilde{G}_{\theta}
, which is separable, is an intermediate space in the sense of Theorem
1.1
in this case. Note that the requirement
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
means that
G_{\theta}
should be “closer” to
E
than to
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Concerning interpolation spaces, hence, many questions, possibly of interest, remain open.
Is it true, in general, that the interpolated space
G_{\theta}
is an intermediate space? For every
0<\theta<1
or just for some values of the interpolating parameter
\theta
?

Remark 4.3

The construction of the intermediate space
\widetilde{E}
of §
3
is of course not unique, as other possibilities are available for the candidate norm (
3.5
). For instance, let us define the sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
so that

{\rm E}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{\infty}g_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)\leq 2^{-2k(\alpha+\eta)}\ .

(4.11)

for some
\eta>0
and then, for
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\|x\|^{\prime}=\sup_{k>0}2^{k\alpha}\Big\|\sum_{j=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\Big\|\ .

(4.12)

In this remark we prove that
also
\|x\|^{\prime}<+\infty
for
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
and that the completion of
{\mathcal{H}}
with respect to
\|\kern 5.0pt\|^{\prime}
is also an intermediate space.

This has some interest as it shows that there are (many) other possible ways of constructing intermediate spaces. Also we shall see, in the next remark, that for a suitable choice of the orthonormal system
(e_{n})_{n}
, in the case
E={\mathcal{C}}([0,1])
and Wiener measure the resulting intermediate spaces are the Hölder spaces.

The proof of
\|x\|^{\prime}<+\infty
for
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
is actually even simpler than the one of Lemma
3.2
: let
W_{k}
as in (
3.6
) and for
n>0

Z_{n}=\sup_{k\leq n}2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|\ .

Let us show that
Z_{n}\to Z
where
Z
is a r.v. such that
{\rm P}(Z<\varepsilon)>0
for every
\varepsilon>0
. The a.s. convergence of
(Z_{n})_{n}
is immediate being an increasing sequence. Denoting by
Z
its limit we have

\hfil\displaystyle{\rm P}(Z\leq\varepsilon)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm P}\bigl(2^{k\alpha}\|W_{k}\|<\varepsilon,k=1,\dots,n\bigr)=

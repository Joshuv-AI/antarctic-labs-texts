A discrete-ordinate weak Galerkin method for radiative transfer equation
Maneesh Kumar Singh

Abstract
This research article discusses a numerical solution of the radiative transfer equation based on the weak Galerkin finite element method. We discretize the angular variable by means of the discrete-ordinate method. Then the resulting semi-discrete hyperbolic system is approximated using the weak Galerkin method. The stability result for the proposed numerical method is devised. A priori error analysis is established under the suitable norm. In order to examine the theoretical results, numerical experiments are carried out.

The application of RTE takes place in numerous scientific fields, including atmospheric physics, biomedical optics, heat transfer, neutron transport, optical imaging, radiative transport, etc.
The RTE is an integro-differential equation that depends on a set of optical parameters (index of refraction, absorption, scattering, and scattering function) that describe the medium, further information in

1.1
The model problem

This research explores a stationary radiative transfer equation that is modulated by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})+\sigma_{t}(\mathbf{x})u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})-\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{x})\mathcal{K}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})=f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}),\quad(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\in\Omega\times\mathcal{S},\\[4.0pt]
u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})=u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}),\,\,\,(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\in\Gamma^{-},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u
is the radiative intensity for all
\mathbf{x}
in the domain
\Omega
, an open bounded polytopal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=1,2,3)
with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and for all directions
\mathbf{s}
of the unit sphere

\mathcal{S}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The incoming and outgoing boundaries are given by

\Gamma^{\pm}=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}):\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}}^{\pm},\,\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}\},

where the spatial boundaries
\Omega_{\mathbf{s}}^{\pm}
are

\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}}^{-}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})<0\},\quad\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}}^{+}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\},

where
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})

the unit outward normal for
\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega
.

The physical meaning of the medium parameters that determine the RTE is explained in the following few lines.

1.

Absorption:
The absolute amount of absorption is directly proportional to the magnitude of the incident energy and the distance the beam travels through the medium.

2.

Scattering:
or “out-scattering” (away from the direction
\mathbf{s}
under consideration), is very similar to absorption,
i.e.
, a part of the incoming intensity is removed from the direction of propagation,
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
.

3.

Total attenuation:
The total attenuation of the intensity in a pencil of rays is summed up by the contribution of both absorption and scattering. It is also known as “extinction”.

The equation,
\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{a}+\sigma_{s}
, describes how the overall attenuation
\sigma_{t}
is influenced by both absorption
\sigma_{a}
and scattering
\sigma_{s}
. In simpler terms, it breaks down the total impact on the medium into the portions caused by absorption and scattering.

The functions
f
and
u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}
are the source and external radiative intensity.
The integral operator
\mathcal{K}
in (
1.1
) is given by

\mathcal{K}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})d\sigma(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),\quad\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})d\sigma(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\equiv 1,\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\,\,\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S},

where
\Phi
is a non-negative normalized phase function, which describes the scattering property of the participating medium.
The precise form of the phase function
is usually unknown for applications.
In this study, we consider a benchmark choice the Henyey-Greenstein (H-G) phase function which is given by

\Phi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=\dfrac{1}{2^{d-1}\pi}\dfrac{1-\eta^{2}}{\left(1+\eta^{2}-2\eta(\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\right)^{3/2}},\,\,d=2,3.

(1.2)

where the anisotropy factor
\eta\in(-1,1)
.

We assume that data of the model problem (
1.1
) satisfies the following conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{t},\,\sigma_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\quad\sigma_{t}-\sigma_{s}\geq\sigma_{0}>0,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S}),\,\,f(\mathbf{x},\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}),\\
u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{-}),\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
is a continuous space over
\mathcal{S}
. Next, we denote the space
H^{1}_{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})
by

H^{1}_{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S}),\,\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla v\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})\}.

with the norm

\|v\|_{H^{1}_{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})}=\left(\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})}+\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})}\right)^{1/2}.

By using (
1.3
), it can be shown that the model problem has a unique solution
u\in H^{1}_{2}(\Omega\times\mathcal{S})
. For more details, one may see
[
13
, Theorem 1]

In literature, the numerical methods for RTE can be categorized into the stochastic and deterministic approaches. DOM reduces the original RTE into a semi-discrete first-order hyperbolic system. The detailed description of this numerical approach can be found in
[

Recently, the upwind DG method for scaled RTE is discussed in

Discrete-ordinate DG type finite element methods for RTE are developed in

One of the highlights of the WG method is that it has the advantage of the general mesh, which provides a high degree of versatility in numerical simulation, especially in the complex geometry domain. Unlike the DG method, the WG method is parameter-free while it has the same degrees of freedom as DG possesses. More details of this method can be found in

This is the first article that focuses on discretizing the spatial direction of RTE utilizing the WG methods. The proposed numerical method combines DOM for the angular variable and the WG method for the spatial variable. The resulting technique is called the discrete-ordinate weak Galerkin method or DOWG method for short. The stability result of the DOWG method is derived. A
priori
error estimate is obtained by combining the projection and discretization errors.

2
Discrete-ordinate WG finite element method

In this section, to solve the RTE, a discrete-ordinate weak Galerkin finite element method is presented (
1.1
). The numerical simulations are developed in two phases: First, the
discrete-ordinate approach is applied to discretize the angular variable, which deduces the model problem (
1.1
)
into a system of hyperbolic PDE.
Then, the resulting system of differential equations is further discretized by the weak Galerkin method.

2.1
Discrete-ordinate method

Before discussing the discrete-ordinate method, we briefly go through the quadrature rule, which is instrumental for the proposal of the discrete method. For any continuous function
F
, we can write due to the quadrature approximation

\int_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathbf{s})d\sigma(\mathbf{s})\approx\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}F(\mathbf{s}_{m}),\quad\omega_{m}>0,\,\,\ \mathbf{s}_{m}\in\mathcal{S},\,0\leq m\leq M.

(2.1)

For
\mathcal{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the quadrature rule (
2.1
) is expressed as

\int_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathbf{s})d\sigma(\mathbf{s})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{F}(\theta)d\theta\approx\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\overline{F}(\theta_{m}),\quad\omega_{m}>0,\,\,\ \theta_{m}\in[0,2\pi],\,1\leq m\leq M,

(2.2)

where the angular variable is written in the spherical coordinate form. Afterwards, the standard quadrature formula is used. More details can be found in any standard numerical analysis book, for example, see
[
46
]
. In this paper, we have applied the composite trapezoidal quadrature formula in (
2.2
). To achieve this, we adopted the angular spacing parameter
h_{\theta}=2\pi/M
with weights
\omega_{0}=\omega_{M}=\dfrac{h_{\theta}}{2}
with
\omega_{m}=h_{\theta}
and the discrete points
\theta_{m}=mh_{\theta}
, for all
1\leq m\leq M-1
.

Similarly, for
\mathcal{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the following quadrature rule is used in (
2.1
) to obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathbf{s})d\sigma(\mathbf{s})&\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\overline{F}(\theta,\psi)\sin\theta d\theta d\psi,\quad(\theta,\psi)\in[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi]\\[12.0pt]
&\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{\pi}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}\overline{F}(\theta_{i},\psi_{j}),\quad\omega_{i}>0,\end{array}

(2.3)

where
\{\theta_{i}\}
are chosen so that
\{\cos\theta_{i}\}
and
\{\omega_{i}\}
are the Gauss-Legendre nodes and weights on
[-1,1]
. The discrete points
\{\psi_{j}\}
are evenly spaced on
[0,2\pi]
with a spacing of
\pi/m
.
By following the quadrature error estimates
[
47
]
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathbf{s})d\sigma(\mathbf{s})-\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}F(\mathbf{s}_{m})\right|\leq C_{r}n^{-r}\|F\|_{r,\mathcal{S}},\,\,F\in H^{r}(\mathcal{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\,r\geq 1,

(2.4)

where
C_{r}
is a positive constant depending only on
r
, and
n
denotes the degree of precision of the quadrature. Here, norm
\|\cdot\|_{r,\mathcal{S}}
in (
2.4
)

\|v\|^{2}_{r,\mathcal{S}}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left|\nabla^{(k)}_{\mathbf{s}}v\right|^{2},\qquad\nabla^{(k)}_{\mathbf{s}}v:=\left\{\nabla^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbf{s}}v\big||\alpha|=k\right\},

(2.5)

where
\alpha
is a multi-index and
|\nabla^{(k)}_{\mathbf{s}}v|=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left(|\nabla^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbf{s}}v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.
By using the normalized property of the scattering phase function with the estimate (
2.4
), for any fixed
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
, we get

\left|1-\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\Phi(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})\right|\leq C_{r}n^{-r}\|\Phi(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot)\|_{r,\mathcal{S}},\quad r\geq 1.

(2.6)

Using the above numerical quadrature formulas, the integral operator
\mathcal{K}
is approximated in the following unified way:

\mathcal{K}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\approx\mathcal{K}_{d}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{m})u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}),\quad\omega_{m}>0,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\mathbf{s}_{m}\in\mathcal{S},\,\,0\leq m\leq M.

(2.7)

2.1.1
Semi-discrete problem

By employing the approximation (
2.7
), the continuous problem (
1.1
) is discretized in each angular direction
\mathbf{s}_{m}
as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla u^{m}(\mathbf{x})+\sigma_{t}(\mathbf{x})u^{m}(\mathbf{x})-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})u^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})=f^{m}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\\[12.0pt]
u^{m}(\mathbf{x})=u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}^{m}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega_{m}^{-}\equiv\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}_{m}}^{-},\qquad\qquad\qquad 0\leq m\leq M,\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

where
f^{m}(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}),\,u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}^{m}=u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m})
and the spatial boundaries
\Omega_{\mathbf{s}_{m}}^{\pm}
are

\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}_{m}}^{-}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})<0\},\quad\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}_{m}}^{+}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\},

and the incoming and outgoing boundaries are given by

\Gamma^{\pm}_{m}=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}):\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s}}^{\pm}\}
.

Note that, the exact solution
u^{m}=u^{m}(\mathbf{x})
of the semi-discrete system (
2.8
) is an approximation of the model solution
u
of the continuous problem (
1.1
) at points
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m})
for
m=0,2,\ldots,M
.

Before going into the details of the error estimate, consider the following regularity conditions for the solution of the discrete method (
2.8
):
In the
2D
setting, we assume that the exact solution
u
of the continuous problem (
1.1
) and the phase function satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{m}\in H^{p}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{for }\,\,p\geq 0,\quad 1\leq m\leq M,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{S}))\qquad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\in\Omega\times\mathcal{S}}\|\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}\cdot)\|_{0,\infty,\mathcal{S}}<C_{\Phi},\quad\mbox{for }\,\,r\geq 1,\quad\mbox{where}\,\,\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x},t)=\dfrac{d^{2}\Phi}{dt^{2}}.\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

And, in the
3D
setting

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{m}\in H^{p}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{for }\,\,p\geq 1,\quad 1\leq m\leq M,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle u\in L^{2}(\Omega,H^{r}(\mathcal{S}))\qquad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\in\Omega\times\mathcal{S}}\|\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}\cdot)\|_{r,\mathcal{S}}<C_{\Phi_{*}},\quad\mbox{for }\,\,r\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

where the Bochner spaces
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{S}))
and
L^{2}(\Omega,H^{r}(\mathcal{S}))
are given by

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{S}))=\left\{v:\Omega\to\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{S})):\int_{\Omega}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{S}))}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega,H^{r}(\mathcal{S})=\left\{v:\Omega\to H^{r}(\mathcal{S}):\int_{\Omega}\|v\|_{r,\mathcal{S}}<\infty\right\}.

For simplification of the presentation, we introduce

b(\mathbf{x})=\max_{1\leq\ell\leq M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{m}).

Then, from (
1.3
) and (
2.6
), one can deduce that

\sigma_{t}(\mathbf{x})-\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{x})b(\mathbf{x})\equiv(\sigma_{a}+\sigma_{s})(\mathbf{x})-\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{x})b(\mathbf{x})\geq C_{*}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\quad C_{*}\,\,\mbox{a positive constant}.

(2.11)

This condition will be utilized while deriving the stability and convergence result of the numerical approximation.

Let

and

where

and the phase function

By using the regularity properties (

)-(

), the proof of this theorem is followed from

2.2
Weak Galerkin finite element method

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a mesh partition of the spatial domain
\Omega
consisting of elements that are closed and simply connected polygons in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(polyhedrons in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
)
satisfying a set of shape-regular conditions
[
38
]
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}
is the set of all edges/faces in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega_{h}
is the set of all interior boundaries (faces for
d=3
or edges for
d=2
). And
\partial\Omega_{h}
be the subset of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
of all boundaries on
\partial\Omega
. For every element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote by
h_{T}
the diameter
of
T
and the mesh size
h=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
for
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

For a fixed angular direction
\mathbf{s}_{m}
, the incoming and outgoing boundaries of the mesh element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is given by

\partial^{-}T=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial T:\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})<0\},\qquad\partial^{+}T=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial T:\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\}.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the mesh elements sharing a common edge
e
. We define the average of the scalar valued
v
and vector valued functions
\mathbf{v}
on edge
e
as follows:

\{v\}_{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2}(v|_{T_{1}}+v|_{T_{2}}),&e\in\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h},\\[8.0pt]
v,&e\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.\quad\{\mathbf{v}\}_{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{v}|_{T_{1}}+\mathbf{v}|_{T_{2}}),&e\in\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h},\\[8.0pt]
\mathbf{v},&e\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

And, define the jump of
v
on
e
by
[v]=v^{+}-v^{-}
.

For convenience, we introduce the following notations:

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\phi\psi\mbox{d}\mathbf{x},\,\,\big\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\phi\psi\,\mbox{ds},\,\,\big\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T\cap\partial\Omega_{h}}\phi\psi\,\mbox{ds}.

Now, we introduce the finite element space
V_{h}
.
Given an integer
k\geq 1
, the finite element space is defined as follows

V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T),\,\,\forall\,T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)
is the space of polynomials on set
T
with degree no more than
k
.

Definition 2.2

For any function
v^{m}_{h}\in V_{h}
, on each element
T
, we define the weak gradient operator
\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{m}|_{T}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(T)]^{d}
as follows:

(\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{m},\mathbf{q}_{h})_{T}=-(v^{m}_{h},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q}_{h})_{T}+\big\langle\{v^{m}_{h}\},\mathbf{q}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\mathbf{q}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(T)]^{d}.

(2.14)

And we define the weak divergence
\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}v^{m}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)
associated with
\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m}
on
T
, as the unique polynomial satisfying

(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}v^{m}_{h},w_{h})=-(v^{m}_{h},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla w_{h})+\big\langle\{v^{m}_{h}\},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,w_{h}\big\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T).

(2.15)

Next, we introduce the projection operators, which will be used in the error analysis of the weak Galerkin method. For each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
\mathcal{Q}_{T}:L^{2}(T)\to\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)

and
\Pi_{T}:[L^{2}(T)]^{d}\to[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(T)]^{d}

be the
L^{2}
-projections on the associated local polynomial spaces respectively. For any mesh element
T
, we define the global projections
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
and
\Pi_{h}
element-wise by

\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}v)|_{T}=\mathcal{Q}_{T}(v|_{T}),&\forall\,v\in L^{2}(T),\\[4.0pt]
(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{q})|_{T}=\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q}|_{T}),&\forall\,\mathbf{q}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d}.\end{array}

The finite dimensional space
\mathbf{V}_{h}
is defined as
\mathbf{V}_{h}=(V_{h})^{M}
for the semi-discrete problem (
2.8
) and an element
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
is given by
\mathbf{v}_{h}:=\{v_{h}^{m}\}_{m=0}^{M}
.

The WG finite element method for the semi-discrete problem (
2.8
) reads:
Find
u_{h}^{m}\in V_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{c}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})=\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m}),\quad\forall\,v_{h}^{m}\in V_{h},

(2.16)

where
\mathcal{A}_{c}(\cdot,\cdot),\,\mathcal{A}_{st}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(\cdot)
are given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{c}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bigg(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h}^{m}+\sigma_{t}u_{h}^{m}-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})u_{h}^{\ell},v_{h}^{m}\bigg)_{T}-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}},\\[16.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{st}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(u^{m}_{h}-\{u^{m}_{h}\}\big),v^{m}_{h}-\{v^{m}_{h}\}\right\rangle_{\partial^{+}T},\\[16.0pt]
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m})=(f^{m},v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}},\end{array}

where, the incoming boundary

\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}=\left\{e\in\partial\Omega_{h}:\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})<0,\,\mathbf{x}\in e\right\}
. Note that, we have imposed the boundary condition in the weak sense.

2.3
Fully-discrete method

The global formulation of the DOWG method (
2.16
) is expressed as:
Find
\mathbf{u}_{h}=\{u_{h}^{m}\}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\,\mathcal{A}_{c}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})+\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\,\mathcal{A}_{st}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})=\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m}).\end{array}

In the simplified version, the DOWG method is presented as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{Find}\,\,\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\,\,\mbox{such that}\\[6.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h}),\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},\end{array}\right.

(2.17)

where the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the linear functional
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(\cdot)
are given by

\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\big(\mathcal{A}_{c}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})\big),\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m}).

For any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
, the proposed norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is defined as

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v^{m}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2},

(2.18)

where
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
is given by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}^{m}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|v^{m}_{h}\|^{2}_{T}+\left\||\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}|^{1/2}\big(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\big)\right\|^{2}_{\partial T}\right)+\||\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}|^{1/2}v_{h}^{m}\|^{2}_{\partial\Omega_{h}}.

3
Error analysis

In this section, we discuss the error estimate of the DOWG method. Firstly, we start with the stability result of the proposed numerical method (
2.17
). By using this stability result, we derive the error estimate of the numerical solution.

3.1
Stability result

Now, we discuss the coercivity of the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\cdot,\cdot)
in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

For sufficiently small
h
, we have

\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq\,\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}),\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

where
\alpha
is a positive constant.

Proof.

By using the definition of weak divergence (
2.15
) and integral by parts, it can be easily deduced that

\left(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}v^{m}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\big),v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}}.

Then, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}_{c}(v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})&=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\sigma_{t}v^{m}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{T}+I+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\\[16.0pt]
&\quad-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\big),v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\[16.0pt]
&\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(v^{m}_{h}-\{v^{m}_{h}\}\big),v^{m}_{h}-\{v^{m}_{h}\}\right\rangle_{\partial^{+}T},\end{array}

where
I
is given by

I=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})v^{\ell}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{T},

and it satisfies

|I|\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\sigma_{s}bv^{m}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{T}.

By using condition (
2.11
), we obtain that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}_{c}(v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})&\geq\displaystyle\left((\sigma_{t}-\sigma_{s}b)v^{m}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\\[14.0pt]
&\quad+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\big),v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\[14.0pt]
&\geq\left(C_{*}v^{m}_{h},v^{m}_{h}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,v_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\\[14.0pt]
&\quad+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\big(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\big),v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.\end{array}

Therefore, the bilinear form satisfies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})&\displaystyle\geq\alpha\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v^{m}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\\[12.0pt]
&=\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2},\qquad\alpha=\min\{C_{*},\frac{1}{2}\}.\end{array}

This completes the proof.

The direct consequence of the above lemma is the uniqueness result.

Theorem 3.2

There exists a unique solution of the DOWG method (
2.17
).

\phi\in H^{1}(T)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq C\left(h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{T}\right).

(3.1)

Error estimate

Next, we discuss the error estimate of the DOWG method. Firstly, we establish an error equation associated with the discrete problem (
2.17
).

Define the components
\mathcal{L}_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})

\displaystyle=(u^{m}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})

\displaystyle=-\big\langle u^{m}-\{\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}\},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\})\big\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{3}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle-\left(\sigma_{t}(u^{m}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m})-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})(u^{\ell}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{\ell}),v_{h}^{m}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{4}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})

\displaystyle=\big\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(u^{m}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}),v_{h}^{m}\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}}.

Lemma 3.3

The error term

\mathbf{e}_{\mathcal{Q}_{h}}=(\mathcal{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h})

satisfies:

\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathbf{e}_{\mathcal{Q}_{h}},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h}),

(3.3)

with

\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\mathcal{L}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m}),\qquad\mathcal{L}(u^{m},v^{m}_{h})=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{L}_{j}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m}),

where
\mathcal{L}_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
are given in (
3.2
).

Proof.

By using the integration by parts and definition (
2.15
), we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla u^{m},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}&=-(u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\[8.0pt]
&=-(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(u^{m}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\[8.0pt]
&\quad+\big\langle\{\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}\},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}^{m}\big\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\big\langle u^{m}-\{\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}\},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}(v_{h}^{m}-\{v_{h}^{m}\})\big\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\[8.0pt]
&=\left(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}),v^{m}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})-\mathcal{L}_{2}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m}).\end{array}

(3.4)

Next, one can obtain that

\left(\sigma_{t}u^{m}-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})u^{\ell},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{t}\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m}-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{\ell},v^{m}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\mathcal{L}_{3}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})

(3.5)

and

-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,u^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}}=-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}}-\mathcal{L}_{4}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m}).

(3.6)

Finally, by combining (
3.4
)-(
3.6
), we get

\mathcal{A}_{c}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m})-\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{L}_{j}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})=(f^{m},v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}}.

Adding
\mathcal{A}_{st}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m})
both side of the above equation to obtain

\mathcal{A}_{c}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m})+\mathcal{A}_{st}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},v_{h}^{m})=(f^{m},v_{h}^{m})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,u_{\tiny\mbox{in}}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{-}_{h}}+\mathbf{L}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m}).

(3.7)

The above equation into the global formulation is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}_{WG}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h})+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h}).\end{array}

(3.8)

By subtracting the discrete problem (
2.17
) from (
3.8
), we obtain the required error equation.

Proof.
By using

\begin{array}[]{ll}|\mathcal{L}_{1}(u^{m},v_{h}^{m})|=\displaystyle\left|\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(u^{m}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u^{m},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m}\right)_{T}\right|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|u^{m}\|_{k+1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}^{m}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.\end{array}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e^{m}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{1}h^{k+1/2}\|u^{m}\|_{k+1}+C_{2}\,n^{-r}\left(\int_{\Omega}\|u\|^{2}_{r,\mathcal{S}}\,\mbox{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/2}.

4.2.1
DODG method for RTE model (
1.1
)

DODG method for the model problem (
1.1
) is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{Find}\,\,\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\,\,\mbox{such that}\\[6.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{DG}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h}),\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where

\mathcal{A}_{DG}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\big(\mathcal{A}_{cdg}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{stdg}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})\big),\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m}),

with
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m})
is given as in (
2.16
) and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{cdg}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})=\bigg(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h}^{m}+\sigma_{t}u_{h}^{m}-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})u_{h}^{\ell},v_{h}^{m}\bigg)-\big\langle\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\hat{u}_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m}\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{h}},

\mathcal{A}_{stdg}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})=c_{p}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}}\big\langle[u_{h}^{m}],[v_{h}^{m}]\big\rangle_{e}.

Here,
c_{p}>0
is a penalty parameter and
\hat{u}_{h}^{m}
is the numerical trace, which is given by

\hat{u}_{h}^{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{in}^{m}&\mbox{in}\,\,\Gamma^{-}_{m}\\
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}u_{h}^{m}(\mathbf{x}-\epsilon\mathbf{s}_{m})&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

DODSD method for the model problem (
1.1

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{Find}\,\,\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\,\,\mbox{such that}\\[6.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{DSD}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h}),\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

where

\mathcal{A}_{DSD}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\big(\mathcal{A}_{cdsd}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{stdsd}(u_{h}^{m},v_{h}^{m})\big),\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\omega_{m}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}(v_{h}^{m}+\delta\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{m}),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{cdsd}(u_{h}^{m},v^{m}_{h})=\bigg(\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h}^{m}+\sigma_{t}u_{h}^{m}-\sigma_{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega_{\ell}\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\ell})u_{h}^{\ell},v_{h}^{m}+\delta\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{m}\bigg)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\langle[u^{m}_{h}],v^{m,+}_{h}|\mathbf{s}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\big\rangle_{\partial^{-}T},

5
Concluding remarks

In this article, we have proposed a weak Galerkin finite element method for the numerical solution of the radiative transfer equation. The discrete-ordinate weak Galerkin method has been used to approximate the angular and spatial variables. For the resulting numerical method, the stability result is established. In the specific norm, the error estimate is devised for the numerical solutions under the suitable regularity assumptions. In the end, the numerical experiments are presented for the justification of theoretical findings.

Deep ReLU Neural Network Emulation in High-Frequency Acoustic Scattering
Fernando Henríquez †, Christoph Schwab ‡

Abstract
. We obtain wavenumber-robust error bounds for the deep neural network (DNN) emulation of the solution to the time-harmonic, sound-soft acoustic scattering problem in the exterior of a smooth, convex obstacle in two physical dimensions. The error bounds are based on a boundary reduction of the scattering problem in the unbounded exterior region to its smooth, curved boundary Γ \Gamma using the so-called combined field integral equation (CFIE), a well-posed, second-kind boundary integral equation (BIE) for the field’s Neumann datum on Γ \Gamma . In this setting, the continuity and stability constants of this formulation are explicit in terms of the (non-dimensional) wavenumber κ \kappa . Using wavenumber-explicit asymptotics of the problem’s Neumann datum, we analyze the DNN approximation rate for this problem. We use fully connected NNs of the feed-forward type with Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation. Through a constructive argument we prove the existence of DNNs with an ϵ \epsilon -error bound in the L ∞ ​ ( Γ ) L^{\infty}(\Gamma) -norm having a small, fixed width and a depth that increases spectrally with the target accuracy ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 . We show that for fixed ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , the depth of these NNs should increase poly-logarithmically with respect to the wavenumber κ \kappa whereas the width of the NN remains fixed. Unlike current computational approaches such as wavenumber-adapted versions of the Galerkin Boundary Element Method (BEM) with shape- and wavenumber-tailored solution ansatz spaces, our DNN approximations do not require any prior analytic information about the scatterer’s shape.

1.
Introduction

In recent years, the use of DNNs for the numerical approximation of PDEs
has emerged as an alternative to well-established, “classical”
discretization techniques such as Finite Element-, Finite Difference- and
Finite Volume methods.
The success of DNNs in the field of computational mathematics
is arguably based on three main pillars: (i) universality results
(e.g.
[
25
]
and references therein), (ii)
a considerably stronger approximation properties
compared to classical linear approximation methods
or nonlinear (adaptive) approximation schemes, and (iii)
on the intense development of dedicated machine learning
algorithms and computer hardware for DNN-based computations.

Mounting computational evidence together with theoretical insights
unmistakably underlines the notable gains stemming
from the superior expressivity properties of DNNs.
These favorable properties come at the price of a
nonlinear dependence
of the DNNs upon their hidden parameters
.
This renders the solution approximation by DNNs of linear PDEs and BIEs a nonlinear, finite-parametric problem which must be solved numerically
in an iterative fashion during the so-called “DNN training” phase.
Therefore, the question arises if, in particular applications, the potential gain afforded by employing
DNNs for numerical approximation of solutions is so high as to justify the use of nonlinear optimization in the computation of the DNN approximation (as compared to a linear system solve in, e.g. Galerkin type
methods based on linear subspaces).

1.1.
Previous work on numerical high-frequency scattering

Due to its large importance in applications,
the numerical solution of exterior, time-harmonic
acoustic and electromagnetic scattering problems
for large wavenumber
\kappa
has received considerable
attention by engineers and by numerical analysts.
A large body of literature is available.
First, as
\kappa\to\infty
, it is classical that the
solution of the problem tends to the so-called
“geometric optics” limit, the solution of
which is governed by the Eikonal equation, see, eg.,
[
31
, Chap. X]
and
[
23
]

and the references there.
The Eikonal equation is a Hamilton-Jacobi PDE which,
in general, must be treated numerically.
For large, but finite
\kappa
, asymptotic expansion w.r. to

1/\kappa
of the solution have been obtained, and justified,
for smooth, convex scatterers
[
19
,
21
]
.
These works provide recursive expressions for the terms
appearing in the asymptotic expansions w.r. to
1/\kappa\downarrow 0
of the solution,
in terms of powers of
\kappa^{\frac{1}{3}}
,
for the solution of the exterior problem, in any space dimension
d\geq 2
.
These results indicate that in the case of convex scatterers, and
for large
\kappa
, the solution comprises a piecewise
smooth part plus oscillatory terms whose specific structure amounts
to a smooth modulation of oscillatory functions depending on a nontrivial
way on the scatterer’s boundary
\Gamma
.
This indicates the multiscale nature of the problem, and
immediately implies a
scale resolution
requirement for
the FEM and BEM. These are typically of the type
\kappa^{2}h<1
for low
order FE or of the type
\kappa h<1
for BE.
This kind of requirement is prohibitive for large values of
\kappa
,
especially in space dimension

d=3
.
High order methods of polynomial degree
p>1
have scale resolution
requirements of the type
\kappa h/p<1
. See, e.g.,
[
15
,
4
]

and the references there.

Early attempts to alleviate this scale resolution requirement
by incorporating information on the asymptotic expansions
into the numerical schemes include
[
6
]

for an approach in connection with Galerkin BEM.
We refer to
[
4
]
for a survey of these developments
and a comprehensive list of references.

The past decade has seen intense developments in the numerical
analysis of FE and BE methods for the Helmholtz equation at high wavenumber.
For further details, we refer to e.g.

[
16
,
17
]
, and also to
[
15
]
and
the references there. For boundary integral formulations,
several critical issues in FE discretizations
such as dispersion, and scale resolution
are either absent, or strongly mitigated.
We refer to
[
4
]
and the
references there for detailed discussion.
Boundary reduction and BIE reformulation
is also the basis for the present results.

L

w.r. to the wavenumber

.
In contrast to e.g.
[

]
,

2.
High-Frequency Acoustic Scattering Formulation

2.1.
Sound-Soft Scattering Problem

Let
{\text{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with
smooth boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial{\text{D}}
.
We denote by
{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{{\text{D}}}

the corresponding exterior domain.
Given
\kappa>0
and
\hat{{\bf d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
a unit vector,
we define

u^{\text{i}}({\bf x})\coloneqq\exp(\imath\kappa{\bf x}\cdot\hat{{\bf d}})
,
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and consider the following problem:
Find the scattered field
u^{\text{s}}\in H^{1}_{{\text{loc}}}({\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}})

such that the total field

u\coloneqq u^{\text{i}}+u^{\text{s}}
satisfies

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\Delta u+\kappa^{2}u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in }{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}},\quad\text{and}

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\gamma^{{\text{c}}}u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma,

In addition to (
2.1a
) and (
2.1b
), as it is customary,
we impose the Sommerfeld radiation condition on the scattered
field
u^{\text{s}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\text{s}}}{\partial r}({\bf x})-\imath\kappa u^{\text{s}}({\bf x})=o\left(\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

as
\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert\rightarrow\infty
, uniformly in
\hat{{\bf x}}\coloneqq{\bf x}/\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert
.

2.2.
Boundary Integral Formulation

We reduce the exterior acoustic scattering problem (
2.1
)
to an integral equation on the boundary
\Gamma=\partial{\text{D}}
.
In doing so, we satisfy exactly the radiation condition (
2.2
)
and the homogeneous PDE (
2.1a
).

Let
\text{G}_{\kappa}({\bf z})\coloneqq\frac{\imath}{4}H^{(1)}_{0}(\kappa\left\lVert{\bf z}\right\rVert)

for
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{{\bf 0}\}
denote the fundamental solution of
the Helmholtz equation in two spatial dimensions and of wavenumber
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Here
H^{(1)}_{0}
corresponds to the Hankel function of the first kind and of order zero.
We proceed to recast (
2.1
) using BIOs by means of boundary potentials.
To this end, we introduce

single layer potential

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}\,\psi\right)({\bf x})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\Gamma}\text{G}_{\kappa}({\bf x}-{\bf y})\psi({\bf y})\text{d}s_{{\bf y}},\quad{\bf x}\in{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}},

and the
double layer potential

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}\phi\right)({\bf x})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\Gamma}\mathbf{n}({\bf y})\cdot\nabla_{{\bf y}}\text{G}_{\kappa}({\bf x}-{\bf y})\phi({\bf y})\text{d}\text{s}_{\bf y},\quad{\bf x}\in{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}},

where
\psi,\phi\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
are referred to as
surface densities
,
which are defined on the boundary
\Gamma
.
The application of exterior Dirichlet and Neumann traces to
we obtain the single layer operator

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}_{\kappa}\coloneqq\gamma^{{\text{c}}}\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}\colon H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma),

the double layer operator

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{Id}+\mathsf{K}_{\kappa}\coloneqq\gamma^{{\text{c}}}\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}\colon H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma),

and the adjoint double layer operator

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{Id}-\mathsf{K}^{\prime}_{\kappa}\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}\colon H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma).

The double layer operator and the adjoint double layer operator
are mutually adjoint in the bilinear
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-duality pairing (e.g.
[
27
]
).

The starting point of the reformulation of (
2.1
) is Green’s representation formula,
which provides the following representation of the total field
u
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle u({\bf x})=u^{\text{i}}({\bf x})-\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\right)({\bf x}),\quad{\bf x}\in{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}},

where
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}
corresponds to the
Neumann trace of the scattered field

.
We remark that Sommerfeld radiation conditions are built-in in the single layer potential.
The application of both Dirichlet and Neumann traces to
(
2.8
) yields the BIEs

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}_{\kappa}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{{\text{c}}}u^{\text{i}},\quad\text{and},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{I}+\mathsf{K}^{\prime}_{\kappa}\right)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u^{\text{i}}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}.

Both (
2.9
) and (

in

A linear combination of (

) and (
2.10

leads to the resonance-free, well-posed BIE

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\varphi_{\kappa}=\frac{\partial u^{\text{i}}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}-\imath\eta\gamma^{{\text{c}}}u^{\text{i}}\eqcolon f_{\kappa,\eta}\in L^{2}(\Gamma),

where
\varphi_{\kappa}\coloneqq\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}
, and

\mathsf{A}_{\kappa,\eta}
and its adjoint are defined as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\kappa,\eta}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{I}+\mathsf{K}_{\kappa}-\imath\eta\mathsf{V}_{\kappa}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{I}+\mathsf{K}^{\prime}_{\kappa}-\imath\eta\mathsf{V}_{\kappa},

Here

It is well established that

.
However, if

is a domain of class

standard regularity results assert

Indeed, this also holds true even if

Therefore, as it is customary, we consider (

Well-posedness of the CFIE and

The following two propositions address the boundedness and coercivity

Proposition 2.1

(

It holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathsf{A}_{\kappa,\eta}\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\Gamma))}=\left\lVert\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\Gamma))}\lesssim 1+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left\lvert\eta\right\rvert\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}},

Proposition 2.2

(

2.4.
Galerkin vs DNN Approximations

In view of (
2.16
),
and as it is usual in the Galerkin BEM, the convergence analysis of (

)
boils down to the choices of the subspaces

and the regularity
of the solution

to the CFIE (

Concerning the latter aspect, in Section

relevant results addressing the asymptotic regularity of

in (

These results have been used in
[

and the references therein to design

affording wavenumber-robust spectral convergence
in the approximation of the discrete Galerkin BEM problem stated in (

The subspaces

depend on the wavenumber
\kappa

the incident angle

, and on

itself in a nontrivial way.

In the present work, we use wavenumber-explicit asymptotic regularity
for the solution
\varphi_{\kappa}
of the CFIE (

)
in our deep ReLU-NN emulation construction.
The NN architecture in the present paper is a deep feedforward NN
with strict ReLU activation and
fixed width
.

Contrary to the construction in
[
9
]
,
the presently obtained DNN emulation
results indicate no need to include
explicit asymptotic information of

into the DNN
architecture, while enjoying, as we shall show, similar convergence properties.
In practice, NN training would account via feature optimization
for the dependence of the approximation on the problem’s wavenumber and
on the scatterer’s geometry.
As usual in many DNN based PDE solvers,
this comes with the price of a nonlinear dependence of the
DNN approximation on its constitutive parameters.
In practice, for the computation of these parameters, iterative
training algorithms are required, as opposed to the solution of
the Galerkin approximation (
2.15
), which
merely requires
assembling and solution of a linear system of equations.

2.5.
Asymptotics of the normal derivative of the total field

We assume that the scatterer’s boundary

\Gamma\coloneqq\left\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:{\bf x}=\bm{\gamma}(s):\;s\in[0,2\pi]\right\},

where
\bm{\gamma}:[0,2\pi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

Following
[

\hat{\varphi}_{\kappa}(s)\coloneqq\left(\tau_{\bm{\gamma}}\varphi_{\kappa}\right)(s)=\kappa V_{\kappa}(s)\exp(\imath\kappa\bm{\gamma}(s)\cdot\hat{{\bf d}}),\quad s\in[0,2\pi],

where

Throughout this manuscript, we work under the assumption stated below.

Assumption 2.3

We require

simply connected and strictly convex, i.e. with non-vanishing curvature.

(a)

Geometrical setting.

(b)

Complex integration contour used in the definition of Fock’s integral.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
,
[

)
.

Let Assumption
2.3
be satisfied.
There exists
\delta>0

has the asymptotic expansion:

(2.18)

\displaystyle V_{\kappa}(s)\sim\sum_{\ell,m\geq 0}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3}\ell-m}b_{\ell,m}(s)\Psi^{(\ell)}\left(\kappa^{\frac{1}{3}}Z(s)\right)

valid for

s\in I_{\delta}\coloneqq(t_{1}-\delta,t_{1}+\delta)\cup(t_{2}-\delta,t_{2}+\delta)
,
where
\bm{\gamma}(t_{1})

and

The functions

(ii)

,
and
t_{2}

(iii)

\Psi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an entire function specified by

(2.19)

\displaystyle\Psi(\tau)\coloneqq\exp(-\imath\tau^{3}/3)\int\limits_{\mathfrak{C}}\frac{\exp(-\imath z\tau)}{\text{Ai}(\exp(2\pi\imath/3)z)}\text{d}z,

where

The asymptotics of

We have that

\displaystyle\Psi(\tau)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}\tau^{1-3j}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{1-3(n+1)}),\quad\text{as}\quad\tau\rightarrow\infty,

Furthermore, there exists

\displaystyle D^{n}_{\tau}\Psi(\tau)=c_{0}D^{n}_{\tau}\left\{\exp(-\imath\tau^{3}/3-\imath\tau\alpha_{1})\right\}(1+\mathcal{O}(\exp(-\beta\left\lvert\tau\right\rvert)),\quad\text{as}\quad\tau\rightarrow-\infty,

3.
Deep ReLU Neural Networks

We recall notation, terminology, and results concerning feedforward, fully connected
DNNs, as subsequently required, consistent with
[
11
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Deep Neural Network)
.

Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell_{0},\dots,\ell_{L}\in\mathbb{N}

and let

\varrho:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
non-linear function, referred to in the following as
the
activation function
.
Given
({\bf W}_{k},{\bf b}_{k})_{k=1}^{L}
,

{\bf W}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k}\times\ell_{k-1}}
,
{\bf b}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k}}
,
define the affine transformation

\mathcal{A}_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k-1}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k}}:{\bf x}\mapsto{\bf W}_{k}{\bf x}+{\bf b}_{k}

for
k\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. We define a
Deep Neural Network (DNN)

with activation function
\varrho:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as a map

\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}}:\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{0}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{L}}
with

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}}({\bf x})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{1}({\bf x}),&L=1,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{L}\circ\varrho\circ\mathcal{A}_{L-1}\circ\varrho\cdots\circ\varrho\circ\mathcal{A}_{1}\right)({\bf x}),&L\geq 2,\end{cases}

where the activation function
\varrho
is applied component-wise to vector inputs.
We define the depth and the width
of an DNN as

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}})\coloneqq\max\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{L}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}(\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}})\coloneqq L

together with the weight bounds

(3.3)

\mathcal{B}\left(\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}}\right)=\max_{\ell=1,\dots,L}\max\left\{\left\lVert{\bf W}_{k}\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert{\bf b}_{k}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}.

In the above definition,
\ell_{0}
is the dimension of the
input layer
,

\ell_{L}
denotes the dimension of the
output layer
.

In addition, we denote by
\mathcal{N\!N}^{\varrho}_{L,M,\ell_{0},\ell_{L}}

the set of all DNNs
\Phi_{\mathcal{N\!N}}:\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{0}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{L}}

with input dimension
\ell_{0}
, output dimension
\ell_{L}
, a depth of at most
L
layers,
maximum width
M
, and activation function
\varrho
.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume that
the activation is by the ReLU defined as

\varrho(x)\coloneqq\max\{x,0\}
, and we drop the explicit
dependence on the activation function in
\mathcal{N\!N}^{\varrho}_{L,M,\ell_{0},\ell_{L}}
.

We proceed to recall results concerning the
approximation properties of ReLU-NNs following
[
11
]
.
Firstly, we introduce the multiplication ReLU-NN.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
11
, Proposition III.3]
)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
D\in\mathbb{R_{+}}

and
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
there are networks

\Phi_{D,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,5,2,1}
with

(3.4)

L\leq C\left(\log\left(\lceil D\rceil\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)

such that

\left\lVert\Phi_{D,\epsilon}(x,y)-xy\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-D,D)^{2})}\leq\epsilon.

The weights of
\Phi_{D,\epsilon}

bounded according to
\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{D,\epsilon})\leq 1
.

Observe that
p_{0}(x)=1
and
p_{1}(x)=x

can be exactly represented using ReLU-NNs as follows

(3.5a)

\displaystyle p_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-1&1\end{pmatrix}\varrho\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
1\\
1\end{pmatrix}x+\begin{pmatrix}2\\
1\\
-1\\
-2\end{pmatrix}\right)\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{2,4,1,1},\quad\text{and}

(3.5b)

\displaystyle p_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}\varrho\begin{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}x\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{2,2,1,1}.

The next result addresses the ReLU-NN emulation
of polynomials of higher order.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
11
, Proposition III.5]
)
.

There exist a constant
C>0

such that for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
A\geq 1
,
D\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)

and

(3.6)

p_{m}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}x^{i}\quad\text{with }\displaystyle\max_{i=0,\dots,m}\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert\leq A,

there are ReLU-NNs
\Phi_{p_{m},D,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}
with

(3.7)

L\leq C\left(m\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)+m^{2}\log(\lceil D\rceil)+m\log(m)+m\log(A)\right)

such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi_{p_{m},D,\epsilon}(x)-p_{m}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-D,D))}\leq\epsilon.

The weights of
\Phi_{p_{m},D,\epsilon}
are bounded according to

\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{p_{m},D,\epsilon})\leq 1
.

Remark 3.1

(ReLU-NN Emulation of Polynomials with Complex Coefficients)
.

An inspection of the proof of Proposition
3.3

reveals that in order to accommodate for polynomials with complex coefficients
the architecture of the ReLU-NN should be modified as follows:
two outputs should be considered instead of one in order to account for the real and imaginary
parts separately, and the width of the ReLU-NN should be increased to
11
.
The depth of the ReLU-NN remains unchanged,
i.e. it behaves exactly as in (
3.7
).

Proposition 3.4

(
[
11
, Theorem III.8 and Corollary III.9]
)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such for each
a,D\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
there are ReLU-NNs

\Phi^{\cos}_{a,D,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}

and
\Phi^{\sin}_{a,D,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}

with

(3.9)

L\leq C\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+\log\left(\lceil aD\rceil\right)\right)

such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\cos(ax)-\Phi^{\cos}_{a,\epsilon}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-D,D))}\leq\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\sin(ax)-\Phi^{\sin}_{a,\epsilon}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-D,D))}\leq\epsilon.

The weights of
\Phi^{\cos}_{a,D,\epsilon}
and
\Phi^{\sin}_{a,D,\epsilon}

are bounded according to

\mathcal{B}(\Phi^{\cos}_{a,\epsilon})\leq 1
and
\mathcal{B}(\Phi^{\sin}_{a,\epsilon})\leq 1
.

3.1.
ReLU-NN Emulation of Decaying Exponentials

We approximate decaying exponentials,
i.e. maps of the form
x\mapsto\exp(-ax)
with
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
using ReLU-NNs within the interval
[0,D]
, where
D\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exist constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}>0

such that for each
a
,
D\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

there exist ReLU-NNs

\Phi^{\exp}_{a,D,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}

with

(3.11)

\displaystyle L\leq C_{1}\left(\lceil\log(aD)\rceil^{2}+\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right),\quad\text{as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0,

such that
\left\lVert\exp(-ax)-\Phi^{\exp}_{a,D,\epsilon}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq\epsilon
.
The weights of
\Phi^{\exp}_{a,D,\epsilon}
are bounded in absolute value by a constant
independent of
a
and
D
.

Proof.

We divide the proof in several steps.

1

ReLU-NN Emulation for
a\in(0,1]
and
D\in(0,1]
.

Firstly, let us consider the case
a\in(0,1]
and
D\in(0,1]
.
The MacLaurin series expansion of
\exp(-ax)
reads

(3.12)

\displaystyle\exp(-ax)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-ax)^{n}}{n!},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Taylor’s theorem with Lagrange remainder yields

(3.13)

\displaystyle\exp(-ax)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-ax)^{n}}{n!}+\frac{(-a)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\exp(-a\xi_{x})x^{N+1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

for some
\xi_{x}\in(0,x)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
N=\lceil(\log(2/\epsilon))\rceil
it holds

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\exp(-ax)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-ax)^{n}}{n!}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\frac{(-a)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\exp(-a\xi_{x})x^{N+1}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)!}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Thanks to Proposition
3.3
,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each
a\in(0,1]

and
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
there are
ReLU-NNs
\Phi_{a,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}

with

L\leq C\left(N\log\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}\right)+N\log(N)+N\right),

satisfying

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi_{a,\epsilon}(x)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-ax)^{n}}{n!}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Therefore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each
a\in(0,1]

there are ReLU-NNs
\Phi_{a,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}
with

L\leq C\left(\log(1/\epsilon)\right)^{2}
, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, satisfying

\left\lVert\exp(-ax)-\Phi_{a,\epsilon}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,1))}\leq\epsilon

with weights bounded according to
\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{a,\epsilon})\leq 1
.

2

ReLU-NN Emulation of the Squaring Operation.

Let us shortly recall Yarotsky’s result concerning
the approximation of the squaring operation using ReLU-NNs
[
33
]
.

We denote by
\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}
the linear piecewise
interpolation that approximates uniformly
x\mapsto x^{2}
up to
accuracy
\epsilon
in the interval
[0,1]
.
As stated in
[
11
, Proposition III.2]
,
we have that
\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,3,1,1}
,
with
L\leq C\log\left(1/\epsilon\right)
for any
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)

satisfying

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}(x)-x^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,1))}\leq\epsilon.

An inspection of Yarotsky’s construction reveals the following Lipschitz estimate

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}(x)-\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}(y)\right\rvert\leq 2\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

The weights are bounded according to
\mathcal{B}(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon})\leq 1
.

3

ReLU-NN Emulation for
a>1
and
D\in(0,1]
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists a
C_{a}\in(1/2,1]
such that
a=C_{a}2^{\lceil\log(a)\rceil}
.
Let us define
f_{n,a}(x)\coloneqq\exp(-2^{n-1}(C_{a}x))
.
Recalling
1
, there exists a constant

C>0
and ReLU-NNs
\Phi_{a,\zeta}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,9,1,1}

with
L\leq C\left(\log(1/\zeta)\right)^{2}
for any
\zeta\in(0,1/2)

satisfying

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{1,a}-\Phi_{a,\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq\zeta.

We define recursively
\Phi_{n+1,a,\zeta}\coloneqq\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}
,
where
\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,3,1,1}
, with
L\leq C\log\left(1/\zeta\right)

for any
\zeta\in(0,1/2)
is the ReLU-NN discussed in
2
.

Next, we prove inductively that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{n,a}-\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq 2^{2(n-1)}\zeta.

The statement for
n=1
has been proved in
1
.
Let us estimate

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\Phi_{n+1,a,\zeta}(x)-f_{n+1,a}(x)\right\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}\right)(x)-\left(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ f_{n,a}\right)(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\left(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ f_{n,a}\right)(x)-f_{n+1,a}(x)\right\rvert,\quad x\in[0,D].

Recalling (
3.17
), we have that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}\right)(x)-\left(\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ f_{n,a}\right)(x)\right\rvert\leq 2\left\lvert\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}(x)-f_{n,a}(x)\right\rvert,\quad x\in[0,D],

therefore, assuming that (
3.19
) holds (induction hypothesis),
we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi_{n,a,\zeta}-\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ f_{n,a}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq 2^{2n-1}\zeta.

Observe that
f_{n+1,a}=\left(f_{n,a}\right)^{2}
and that
f_{n,a}(x)\in[0,1]
, for
x\in[0,1]
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence, we obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ f_{n,a}-f_{n+1,a}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq\zeta\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\Phi_{n+1,a,\zeta}-f_{n+1,a}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq 2^{2n}\zeta,

thus proving the desired result.
Let us set
\epsilon=2^{2n}\zeta
and
n={\lceil\log(a)\rceil}-1
. Furthermore, we define

(3.24)

\displaystyle\omega_{a,\epsilon}\coloneqq\underbrace{\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\zeta}}_{{\lceil\log(a)\rceil}\text{ times}}\circ\Phi_{1,a,\zeta}.

Observe that
\omega_{a,\epsilon}\coloneqq\Phi_{n+1,a,\zeta}
and

\left\lVert\Phi_{n+1,a,\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,D))}\leq 1+2^{2n}\zeta
.
The depth of
\omega_{a,\epsilon}
is

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\omega_{a,\epsilon}\right)\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)\right)^{2}+{\lceil\log(a)\rceil}\log(1+2^{2n}\zeta)+{\lceil\log(a)\rceil}\log\left(\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left(2(\lceil\log(a)\rceil-1)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+{\lceil\log(a)\rceil}\log(1+\epsilon)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left(2(\lceil\log(a)\rceil-1)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right){\lceil\log(a)\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\lceil\log(a)\rceil^{2}+\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right)\quad\text{as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

The weight of
\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}
and
\Phi_{a,\zeta}

are bounded in absolute value by a constant (recall that
\epsilon=2^{2n}\zeta
and
n={\lceil\log(a)\rceil}-1
).
The weights of
\omega_{a,\epsilon}
are those of
\Phi^{\text{S}}_{\epsilon}
and
\Phi_{a,\zeta}
,
hence the weights are bounded according to
\mathcal{B}(\omega_{a,\epsilon})\leq 1
as well.

4

ReLU-NN Emulation for
a>1
and
D>1
.

Let
a
,
D>1
and let

4.3.
Wavenumber-Robust Approximation of the Far-Field

We are interested in the effect of the ReLU-NN approximation
of the Neumann trace on the far-field pattern of the scattered field.
An asymptotic expansion of (
2.8
) yields
the following expression for the far-field behaviour of
u^{\text{s}}

(4.59)

\displaystyle u^{\text{s}}({\bf x})\sim\frac{\exp(\imath\pi/4)}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\exp(\imath\kappa r)}{\sqrt{\kappa r}}F(\hat{\bf x})\quad\text{as }r\coloneqq\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert\rightarrow\infty,

where we have assumed that the origin of the cartesian system of coordinates
is contained in
D
,
\hat{\bf x}={\bf x}/\left\lVert{\bf x}\right\rVert\in\mathbb{S}_{1}
,
i.e. the unit circle, and

(4.60)

\displaystyle F(\hat{\bf x})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\int\limits_{\Gamma}\exp(-\imath\kappa\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}){\varphi}_{\kappa}({\bf x})\text{ds}_{{\bf y}}

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\exp(-\imath\kappa\hat{\bf x}\cdot\bm{\gamma}(s))\hat{\varphi}_{\kappa}(s)\left\lVert\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert\text{d}s,\quad\hat{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}.

Let
\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}
be
as in Theorem
4.6
and define

(4.61)

F_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}(\hat{\bf x})=-\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\exp(-\imath\kappa\hat{\bf x}\cdot\bm{\gamma}(s))\left(\left(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}\right)_{1}+\imath\left(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}\right)_{2}\right)\left\lVert\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert\text{d}s,\quad\hat{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}.

Thus, it holds

(4.62)

\left\lVert F-F_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq 2\pi\left\lVert\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,2\pi))}\left\lVert\hat{\varphi}_{\kappa}-\left(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}\right)_{1}-\imath\left(\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}\right)_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left((0,2\pi)\right)}.

Therefore, the constructed ReLU-NN
\hat{\varphi}^{\mathcal{N\!N}}_{\kappa,n,\epsilon}
once is
inserted in (
4.61

far-field pattern, of course, up to a constant which notably is independent of the wavenumber.

5.
Concluding Remarks

We proved wavenumber-robust ReLU-NN emulation rate bounds
for the sound-soft acoustic scattering by a model
smooth, strictly convex obstacle in two dimensions.
Due to these assumptions on the scatterers’ shapes
issues like conical diffraction,
trapping modes and
characteristic boundary behaviours were avoided.

We present a constructive approximation rate bound
that relied on two main tools.
Firstly, we adopt
a boundary reduction of the scattering problem using boundary potentials.
This yields an
equivalent BIE equation of the second kind

for unknown Neumann datum on the scatterers’ surface.
Second,
using the asymptotic expansion by Melrose and Taylor
[
21
]
,
we provide a detailed construction of
ReLU-NNs approximating the Neumann datum with NNs of
moderate, fixed width,
and of
depth that increases spectrally with the target accuracy,
whereas a bound for the NN depth
that depends poly-logarithmically on the wavenumber.

The present results pertain only to the
DNN approximation error
.
As it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
),
in a DNN-based solution algorithm,
also the training and the generalization errors need to be bounded.
In the presently considered, exterior acoustic scattering problem,
(
2.1
)-(
2.2
),
the boundary reduction to the BIE (
2.11
) on the scatterer’s
profile
\Gamma
, similar to
[
1
]
,
reduces controlling all these errors in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
,
rather than in the unbounded, exterior domain
{\text{D}}^{{\text{c}}}
.
In addition,
all quantities being periodic w.r. to the arclength parametrization of
\Gamma
,
for which efficient quadratures, such as the trapezoidal rule, are available.

We conclude this work by indicating extensions of the present results, and
comment on aspects of possible DL algorithms based on the BIE (
2.11
).
For
L,M\in\mathbb{N}
,
let us consider the map

(5.1)

\displaystyle L^{2}\left(\left(0,2\pi\right)\right)\supset\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,M,1,2}\ni\Phi_{\kappa}\mapsto\text{Loss}(\Phi_{\kappa})\coloneqq\left\lVert\hat{\varphi}_{\kappa}-(\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}-\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}((0,2\pi))}.

Given a target accuracy
\epsilon>0
, we aim to
find a ReLU-NN
\Phi^{\star}_{\kappa,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,M,1,2}\subset L^{2}((0,2\pi))
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\text{Loss}\left(\Phi^{\star}_{\kappa}\right)\leq\epsilon,

where the depth
L
and width
M
depend on
\kappa

and
\epsilon
.
Of course, the computation of the loss function defined in
(
5.1
) cannot be performed in practice.

Recalling Proposition
2.2
, there exists a constant
\eta_{0}>0

such that, given
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
\kappa_{0}
(depending on
\delta
)
such that for
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}\kappa
.
Choosing
\eta

\eta_{0}\kappa\leq\eta\lesssim\kappa

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\varphi}_{\kappa}-(\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}-\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}((0,2\pi))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,2\pi]}\left\lVert\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert^{-1}}{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\left\lVert\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\left(\varphi_{\kappa}-\tau^{-1}_{\bm{\gamma}}\left((\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}+\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,2\pi]}\left\lVert\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert^{-1}}{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\left\lVert f_{\kappa,\eta}-\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\left(\tau^{-1}_{\bm{\gamma}}(\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}+\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta,\bm{\gamma})\left\lVert\tau_{\bm{\gamma}}f_{\kappa,\eta}-\tau_{\bm{\gamma}}\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\tau^{-1}_{\bm{\gamma}}\left((\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}+\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}((0,2\pi)}

with

as in Section

(5.4)

does not depend on the wavenumber
\kappa

Set

(5.5)

\widetilde{\text{Loss}}\left(\Phi_{\kappa}\right)\coloneqq\left\lVert\tau_{\bm{\gamma}}f_{\kappa,\eta}-\tau_{\bm{\gamma}}\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{\kappa,\eta}\tau^{-1}_{\bm{\gamma}}\left((\Phi_{\kappa})_{1}+\imath(\Phi_{\kappa})_{2}\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}((0,2\pi)}

We conclude that a NN

computed by minimizing the loss function

within accuracy

\epsilon>0
approximates

with accuracy

in the

-norm
for any

The presently employed NN constructions

The present ReLU-based approximations do imply, however,

[

algorithm in

]

Appendix A
ReLU-NN Emulation of Smooth Functions

In this section, we establish ReLU-NN
spectral
emulation
rates of smooth functions using Chebyshev polynomials.

Let
T_{n}(x)\coloneqq\cos(n\arccos(x))\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
,
for
x\in[-1,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, denote the

n
-th Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind.
We have that
T_{0}(x)=1
,
T_{1}(x)=x
and
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

(A.1)

\displaystyle T_{n+1}(x)=2xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x),\quad x\in[-1,1].

Chebyshev polynomials satisfy the following orthogonality
property in the interval
[-1,1]

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x)T_{m}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\text{d}x=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0,&n\neq m,\\
\pi,&n=m=0,\\
\frac{\pi}{2},&n=m\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

For
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}([-1,1])
, the series

(A.5)

\displaystyle f(x)={\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}}^{\prime}a_{k}(f)T_{k}(x),\quad x\in[-1,1],

(the prime in the previous sum
means that the first term should be halved)
converges uniformly and absolutely in
[-1,1]
and in
L^{\infty}((-1,1))
,
respectively, where for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(A.6)

\displaystyle a_{k}\left(f\right)\coloneqq\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{k}(x)f(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\text{d}x.

According to
[
32
, Theorem 4.2]
for
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}([-1,1])

and for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(A.7)

\displaystyle\left\lvert a_{k}(f)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lVert f^{(\ell+1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-1,1))}}{k(k-1)\cdots(k-\ell)}\leq\left\lVert f^{(\ell+1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-1,1))},\quad k\geq\ell+1.

The following result quantifies how finite
linear combinations of Chebyshev polynomials
can be emulated by means of ReLU-NNs.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let the sequence
\bm{a}\coloneqq\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}\subset\mathbb{K}

be given, where
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
. Set

(A.8)

\displaystyle f_{\bm{a},n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}T_{k}(x),\quad x\in[-1,1].

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the following statements hold true.

(i)

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
. Then there exist ReLU-NNs

\Phi_{\bm{a},n,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N\!N}_{L,13,1,1}
with

(A.9)

\displaystyle L\leq C\left(n^{2}+n\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)+n\log\left(\max_{k=0,\dots,n}\left\lvert a_{k}\right\rvert\right)\right),\quad\text{as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0,

such that

(A.10)

\left\lVert f_{\bm{a},n}-\Phi_{\bm{a},n,\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((-1,1))}\leq\epsilon.

(ii)

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. Then there exist ReLU-NNs

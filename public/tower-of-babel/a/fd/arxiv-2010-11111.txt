Abstract
. We study ultradistributional boundary values of zero solutions of a hypoelliptic constant coefficient partial differential operator P ⁡ ( D ) = P ⁡ ( D x , D t ) P(D)=P(D_{x},D_{t}) on ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} . Our work unifies and considerably extends various classical results of Komatsu and Matsuzawa about boundary values of holomorphic functions, harmonic functions and zero solutions of the heat equation in ultradistribution spaces. We also give new proofs of several results of Langenbruch [ 23 ] about distributional boundary values of zero solutions of P ⁡ ( D ) P(D) . Keywords: Hypoelliptic and semi-elliptic differential operators; Boundary values; Ultradistributions. MSC 2020: 35G15; 35H10; 46F05; 46F20



1.
Introduction

The study of distributional and ultradistributional boundary values of holomorphic functions, which goes back to the seminal works of Köthe
[
16
]
and Tillmann
[
34
]
for distributions and of Komatsu
[
19
]
for ultradistributions, is an important subject in the theory of generalized functions. We refer to the survey article
[
28
]
and the books
[
4
,
5
]
and the references therein for an account of results on this topic. Similarly, the boundary value behavior of harmonic functions
[
1
,
8
,
22
,
35
]
and of zero solutions of the heat equation
[
6
,
18
,
26
,
27
]
in generalized function spaces have been thoroughly investigated.

In the distributional case, Langenbruch
[
23
]
generalized the above results by developing a theory of distributional boundary values for the zero solutions of a hypoelliptic partial differential operator
P(D)=P(D_{x},D_{t})
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with constant coefficients. For ultradistributions much less is known in this general setting: In
[
24
,
25
]
Langenbruch studied spaces of formal boundary values of zero solutions of
P(D)
, in the style of Bengel’s approach to hyperfunctions
[
2
]
. In
[
25
, Satz 2.4]
he characterized the zero solutions
f
of
P(D)
that admit a boundary value in a given ultradistribution space in terms of the ultradistributional extendability properties of
f
(see also
[
20
, Section 3]
). Furthermore, he proved that, for certain semi-elliptic operators
P(D)
, the space of formal boundary values of solutions of
P(D)
may be identified with the Cartesian product of some Gevrey ultradistribution spaces of Roumieu type
[
25
, Satz 4.9 and Satz 4.10]
. The aim of the present paper is to complement these results by extending Langenbruch’s results from
[
23
]
for distributions to the framework of ultradistributions, both of Beurling and Roumieu type, defined via weight sequences
[
19
]
.

We now describe the content of the paper and state a sample of our main results. For the sake of clarity, we consider here only Gevrey ultradistribution spaces. Let
P(D)
be a hypoelliptic partial differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. For
\sigma>1
we write
\mathscr{D}^{(\sigma)}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\{\sigma\}}(X)
for the spaces of compactly supported Gevrey ultradifferentiable functions of order
\sigma
of Beurling type and of Roumieu type, and endow these spaces with their natural locally convex topology. The spaces of Gevrey ultradistributions of order
\sigma
of Beurling type and of Roumieu type are defined as the strong dual spaces of
\mathscr{D}^{(\sigma)}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\{\sigma\}}(X)
. We denote them by
\mathscr{D}^{\prime(\sigma)}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime\{\sigma\}}(X)
. We use
\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
as a common notation for
\mathscr{D}^{\prime(\sigma)}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime\{\sigma\}}(X)
; a similar convention will be used for other spaces as well. Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. Set
C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(V\backslash X)\,|\,P(D)f=0\}
. The boundary value
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
of an element
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
is defined as

\langle\operatorname{bv}(f),\varphi\rangle:=\lim_{t,s\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}(f(x,t)-f(x,-s))\varphi(x)dx,\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{[\sigma]}(X),

provided that
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
exists. We have two main goals: Firstly, we wish to characterize the elements of
C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
that admit a boundary value in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
in terms of their local growth properties near
X
. Secondly, we aim to study the uniqueness and the solvability of the Cauchy type problem

\begin{cases}P(D)f=0&\mbox{ on $V\backslash X$},\\
\operatorname{bv}(D^{j}_{t}f)=T_{j}&\mbox{on $X$ for $j=0,\ldots,m-1$},\\
\end{cases}

where
f\in C^{\infty}(V\backslash X)
,
m=\deg_{t}P
and the initial data
T_{0},\ldots,T_{m-1}
belong to
\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
. This will lead to the representation of the
m
-fold Cartesian product of
\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
by means of boundary values of elements of
C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
.

Let us explicitly state the above two results for certain semi-elliptic partial differential operators. To this end, we introduce the following weighted spaces of zero solutions of
P(D)

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{P,(\sigma)}(V\backslash X):=\{

\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)\,|\,\forall K\Subset V\,\exists h>0\,:\,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{K,h}:=\sup_{(x,t)\in K\backslash K\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,t)|e^{-h|t|^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}}<\infty\},

C^{\infty}_{P,\{\sigma\}}(V\backslash X):=\{f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)\,|\,\forall K\Subset V\,\forall h>0\,:\,\|f\|_{K,h}<\infty\},

where
K\Subset V
denotes that
K
is a compact subset of
V
. We endow these spaces with their natural locally convex topology. We then have:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P(D)
be a semi-elliptic partial differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{1}}P=\cdots=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{d}}P=n
. Set
m=\deg_{t}P
and
a_{0}=n/m
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
.

(a)

Let
\sigma>\max\{1,1/a_{0}\}
. For
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f\in C^{\infty}_{P,[a_{0}\sigma]}(V\backslash X)
.

(ii)

\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
exists.

(iii)

For every relatively compact open subset
Y
of
X
there is
r>0
such that
\{f(\,\cdot\,,t)\,|\,0<|t|<r\}
is bounded in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(Y)
.

In such a case,
\operatorname{bv}(D^{l}_{t}f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)
exists for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Next, define

(1)

\operatorname{bv}^{m}:C^{\infty}_{P,[a_{0}\sigma]}(V\backslash X)\rightarrow\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X),\,f\mapsto(\operatorname{bv}(D^{j}_{t}f))_{0\leq j\leq m-1}.

Then, the sequence

0\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}C^{\infty}_{P}(V)\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}C^{\infty}_{P,[a_{0}\sigma]}(V\backslash X)\xrightarrow{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime[\sigma]}(X)\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}0

is exact and
\operatorname{bv}^{m}
is a topological homomorphism, provided that
V
is
P
-convex for supports. In particular, this holds for all open sets of the form
V=X\times I
, with
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an open interval containing
0
.

(b)

Let
\sigma=1/a_{0}>1
. We have that
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime\{\sigma\}}(X)
exists for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
. Define the mapping
\operatorname{bv}^{m}
similarly as in (
1
). Then, the sequence

0\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}C^{\infty}_{P}(V)\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)\xrightarrow{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime\{\sigma\}}(X)\xrightarrow{\phantom{\phantom{,}\operatorname{bv}^{m}\phantom{,}}}0

is exact and
\operatorname{bv}^{m}
is a topological homomorphism, provided that
V
is
P
-convex for supports. In particular, this holds for all open sets of the form
V=X\times I
, with
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an open interval containing
0
.

Since a partial differential operator
P(D)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is elliptic precisely when it is semi-elliptic with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{1}}P=\cdots=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{d}}P=\deg_{t}P
, Theorem
1.1
(a)
is applicable to every elliptic partial differential operator. Moreover, it is well-known that every open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
P
-convex for supports in such a case. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
(a)
comprises two classical results of Komatsu about the boundary values of holomorphic functions
[
19
, Section 11]
and harmonic functions
[
22
, Chapter 2]
in ultradistribution spaces. Also the heat operator and, more generally, the
k
-parabolic operators in the sense of Petrowsky
[
33
, Definition 7.11]
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
(a)
. In particular, this result extends Matsuzawa’s characterization
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
of compactly supported ultradistributions via boundary values of zero solutions of the heat equation to general ultradistributions. Theorem
1.1
(b)
states that the space
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime\{1/a_{0}\}}(X)
may be identified with the space
C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)/C^{\infty}_{P}(V)
of formal boundary values. This partially covers the above mentioned result of Langenbruch
[
25
, Satz 4.9 and Satz 4.10]
(the assumption
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{1}}P=\cdots=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{d}}P
is not needed there as Langenbruch considers anisotropic Gevrey ultradistribution spaces).

Next, we comment on the main new technique used in this article.
In
[
12
, p. 64]
Hörmander showed the existence of distributional boundary values of holomorphic functions by combining Stokes’ theorem with almost analytic extensions. Petzsche and Vogt
[
30
]
(see also
[
29
]
) extended this method to ultradistributions by using descriptions of ultradifferentiable classes via almost analytic extensions
[
7
,
10
,
29
,
30
]
. We develop here a similar technique to establish the existence of ultradistributional boundary values of zero solutions of a hypoelliptic partial differential operator
P(D)
. Namely, we combine a Stokes type theorem for
P(D)
(Lemma
5.4
) with the description of tuples
(\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1})
of compactly supported ultradifferentiable functions via functions
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
that are almost zero solutions of
P(D)
and satisfy
D_{t}^{j}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
(Proposition
4.1
).
Petzsche
[
29
]
constructed almost analytic extensions of ultradifferentiable functions by means of modified Taylor series. We use here the same basic idea, starting from a power series Ansatz
\Phi
that formally solves the Cauchy problem
P(D)\Phi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
D_{t}^{j}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
j=0,\ldots,m-1

[
14
, Section 4]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In the preliminary Section
2
we collect several results about partial differential operators and ultradistributions that will be used throughout this work. Next, in Section
3
, we construct a fundamental solution of a hypoelliptic partial differential operator
P(D)
satisfying precise regularity and growth properties. This fundamental solution will play an essential role in our work. We obtain it by a careful analysis of the construction of a fundamental solution of
P(D)
by Langenbruch
[
23
, p. 12–14]
. Section
4
is devoted to the description of ultradifferentiable classes via almost zero solutions of
P(D)
. By combining this description with a Stokes type theorem for
P(D)
, we show in Section
5
that zero solutions of
P(D)
satisfying certain growth estimates near
\mathbb{R}^{d}
have ultradistributional boundary values. We also establish the continuity of the boundary value mapping here.
Our main results are proven in Section
6
. We adapt several classical techniques used in the study of ultradistributional boundary values of holomorphic functions and harmonic functions
[
19
,
22
]
. In Sections
4
–
6
we also indicate how our methods may be adapted to the distributional case, thereby providing new proofs of several results of Langenbruch
[
23
]
. Most notably, we obtain an elementary proof of the continuity of the boundary value mapping (compare with
[
23
, Section 3]
). Finally, in Section
7
, we discuss our results for semi-elliptic operators and prove Theorem
1.1
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Partial differential operators

Elements of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
are denoted by
(x,t)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},t)
. We often identify
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the subspace
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. We write
D=(D_{x},D_{t})
, where
D_{x}=-i(\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{d}})
and
D_{t}=-i\partial_{t}
. Throughout this article
P\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},t]
always stands for a non-constant hypoelliptic polynomial. Moreover, we use the notation

P(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(x)t^{k}

for suitable
m\in\mathbb{N}
and polynomials
Q_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]
,
0\leq k\leq m
, with
Q_{m}\neq 0
. Then,
Q_{m}=c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}

[
13
, Example 11.2.8]
and we assume without loss of generality that
c=1
. As customary, we associate to
P
the constant coefficient partial differential operator
P(D)
given by

P(D)=P(D_{x},D_{t})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(D_{x})D_{t}^{k}.

Given
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
open, we define
C^{\infty}_{P}(V)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(V)\,|\,P(D)f=0\}
and endow this space with the relative topology induced by
C^{\infty}(V)
.

We now introduce various indices of
P
that play an important role in this article.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
(\gamma_{0},\mu_{0})=(\gamma_{0}(P),\mu_{0}(P))
as the largest pair of positive numbers satisfying the following property: There are
C,R>0
such that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(|x|^{\gamma_{0}}+|t|^{\mu_{0}})^{|\alpha|+l}|D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{l}_{t}P(x,t)|\leq C|P(x,t)|,\qquad|(x,t)|\geq R.

The existence of the pair
(\gamma_{0},\mu_{0})
follows from the fact that
P
is hypoelliptic; see
[
13
, Section 11.4]
and
[
33
, Section 7.4]
. Furthermore, it holds that
\gamma_{0},\mu_{0}\leq 1
.
Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open. For
\sigma,\tau\geq 1
we denote by
\Gamma^{\sigma,\tau}(V)
the space consisting of all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(V)
such that for all
K\Subset V
there is
h>0
such that

\sup_{(\alpha,l)\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}_{0}}\sup_{(x,t)\in K}\frac{|D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{l}_{t}\varphi(x,t)|}{h^{|\alpha|+l}|\alpha|!^{\sigma}l!^{\tau}}<\infty.

By
[
33
, Theorem 7.4]
, we have that
C^{\infty}_{P}(V)\subset\Gamma^{1/\gamma_{0},1/\mu_{0}}(V)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Set

d(x)=\min\{|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|\,|\,\zeta\in\mathbb{C},P(x,\zeta)=0\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
a>0
the following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

There are
C,R>0
such that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

|x|^{al}|D^{l}_{t}P(x,t)|\leq C|P(x,t)|,\qquad|x|\geq R,t\in\mathbb{R}.

(ii)

There are
C,R>0
such that

|x|^{a}\leq Cd(x),\qquad|x|\geq R.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of
[
13
, Lemma 11.1.4]
and therefore left to the reader.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

We define
a_{0}=a_{0}(P)
as the largest positive number
a
such that conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
from Lemma
2.2

The existence of
a_{0}
follows from the fact that

is hypoelliptic and
[

(cf. the proof of

.

Definition 2.4
.

We define

b_{0}=b_{0}(P):=\max\left\{\frac{\operatorname{deg}Q_{k}}{m-k}\,|\,k=0,\ldots,m-1\right\}.

Remark 2.5
.

By evaluating the inequalities from Lemma

(i)
for

2.2.
Weight sequences

Let
M=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of positive numbers. We write
M_{\alpha}=M_{|\alpha|}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. Furthermore, we set
m_{p}=M_{p}/M_{p-1}
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Given
a>0
, we define
M^{a}=(M^{a}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
M^{a,*}=(M^{a}_{p}/p!)_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

We consider the following conditions on a positive sequence
M
:

(M.1)

M^{2}_{p}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

(M.2)

M_{p+q}\leq CH^{p+q}M_{p}M_{q}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for some
C,H\geq 1
.

(M.2)^{*}

2m_{p}\leq m_{Np}
,
p\geq p_{0}
, for some
p_{0},N\in\mathbb{N}
.

(M.3)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M_{p}^{1/p}}<\infty
.

We refer to
[
19
]
for the meaning of the conditions
(M.1)
,
(M.2)
and
(M.3)^{\prime}
. Condition
(M.2)^{*}
was introduced in
[
3
]
without a name. Note that
(M.1)
means that the sequence
(m_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-decreasing. Hence, any positive sequence
M
with
M_{0}=M_{1}=1
satisfying
(M.1)
is non-decreasing. Furthermore, it holds that
M^{a}
,
a>0
, satisfies
(M.1)
,
(M.2)
and
(M.2)^{*}
, respectively, if
M
does so. On the other hand, condition
(M.3)^{\prime}
is not preserved under taking powers.

Definition 2.6
.

A sequence
M=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of positive numbers is called a
weight sequence
if

(i)

M_{0}=M_{1}=1
.

(ii)

M
satisfies
(M.1)
,
(M.2)
and
(M.3)^{\prime}
.

The most important examples of weight sequences are the
Gevrey sequences

p!^{\sigma}
with index
\sigma>1
.

Given two positive sequences
M
and
N
, the relation
M\subset N
means that there are
C,L>0
such that

M_{p}\leq CL^{p}N_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The stronger relation
M\prec N
means that the latter inequality is valid for every
L>0
and suitable
C>0
. We write
M\asymp N
if both
M\subset N
and
N\subset M
.

The next condition plays an important role in this article.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
a>0
. A positive sequence
M
is said to satisfy condition
(M.4)_{a}
if the sequence
(m^{a,*}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost non-decreasing, i.e.,
m^{a,*}_{p}\leq Cm_{q}^{a,*}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\leq q
, for some
C>0
.

The Gevrey sequence
p!^{\sigma}
satisfies
(M.4)_{a}
if and only if
\sigma\geq 1/a
. In the next lemma we state some consequences of the condition
(M.4)_{a}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
a>0
. Let
M
be a positive sequence satisfying
(M.4)_{a}
.

(i)

There exists a positive sequence
N
with
N\asymp M
such that
N^{a,*}
satisfies
(M.1)
.

(ii)

Either
p!^{1/a}\prec M
or
p!^{1/a}\asymp M
holds.

(iii)

M
satisfies
(M.2)^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)
This follows from
[
32
, Lemma 8]
.

(ii)
By
(i)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
M^{a,*}
satisfies
(M.1)
, or equivalently, that
(m^{a,*}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-decreasing. If
\lim_{p\to\infty}m^{a,*}_{p}=\infty
, we have that also
\lim_{p\to\infty}(M^{a,*}_{p})^{1/p}=\infty

and thus
p!^{1/a}\prec M
. If
\lim_{p\to\infty}m^{a,*}_{p}<\infty
, it is clear that
p!^{1/a}\asymp M
.

(iii)
This follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.11]
.
∎

Let
M
be a positive sequence with
\lim_{p\to\infty}M_{p}^{1/p}=\infty
. The
associated function
of
M
is defined as

\omega_{M}(\rho)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\log\frac{\rho^{p}M_{0}}{M_{p}},\qquad\rho\geq 0.

Note that
\log\rho=o(\omega_{M}(\rho))
as
\rho\to\infty
. If
M
satisfies
(M.1)
and
(M.2)
, then by
[
19
, Proposition 3.6]

2\omega_{M}(\rho)\leq\omega_{M}(H\rho)+\log C,\qquad\rho\geq 0,

where
C
and
H
are the constants occurring in
(M.2)
. Given two positive sequences
M
and
N
satisfying
(M.1)
with
\lim_{p\to\infty}M_{p}^{1/p}=\lim_{p\to\infty}N_{p}^{1/p}=\infty
, it holds that
N\subset M
if and only if

\omega_{M}(\rho)\leq\omega_{N}(L\rho)+\log C,\qquad\rho\geq 0,

for some
C,L>0

[
19

Remark 2.9

Let

Lemma 2.10

that are (almost) zero solutions of

and satisfy
D_{t}^{j}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
for

.
We start with the following result, which is essential for this article.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.4)_{b_{0}}
and let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
There is
L>0
such that for all
h>0
the following property holds: For all
\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1}\in\mathscr{D}^{M,h}_{K}
there exists
\Phi=\Phi(\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1})\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\Phi\subseteq K\times\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

D^{j}_{t}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P(D)\Phi(x,t)|e^{\omega_{M^{b_{0},*}}\left(\frac{1}{Lh^{b_{0}}|t|}\right)}<\infty
.

Remark 4.2
.

By Lemma
2.8
(ii)
,
(M.4)_{b_{0}}
implies that either
p!^{1/b_{0}}\prec M
or
p!^{1/b_{0}}\asymp M
. If
p!^{1/b_{0}}\prec M
, the meaning of condition
(ii)
in Proposition
4.1
is clear. If
p!^{1/b_{0}}\asymp M
, this condition means that
\Phi
satisfies
P(D)\Phi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-1/(Lh^{b_{0}}),1/(Lh^{b_{0}}))
(cf. Remark
2.9
). The same convention will be tacitly used in the rest of this article.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
requires some preparation. In
[
29
]
Petzsche constructed almost analytic extensions of ultradifferentiable functions by means of modified Taylor series. If
p!^{1/b_{0}}\prec M
, we use here a similar idea to prove Proposition
4.1
, starting from a power series Ansatz
\Phi
that formally solves the Cauchy problem
P(D)\Phi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
D_{t}^{j}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
j=0,\ldots,m-1

[
14
, Section 4]
(see also the proof of
[
24
, Satz 4.1]
). If
p!^{1/b_{0}}\asymp M
, we even show that
\Phi
converges. We now recall the definition and some basic properties of this formal power series solution; see
[
14
, Section 4]
for details. For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we recursively define the mapping
\mathscr{C}_{l}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\mathscr{C}_{l}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}0,&l=0,\ldots,m-2,\\
\varphi,&l=m-1,\\
-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}Q_{k}(D_{x})\mathscr{C}_{k+l-m}(\varphi),&l\geq m.\end{cases}

Since
Q_{m}=1
, we have that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(7)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(D_{x})\mathscr{C}_{k+l}(\varphi)=0,\qquad\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover, as
\mathscr{C}_{l}
is a constant coefficient partial differential operator,
\operatorname{supp}\,\mathscr{C}_{l}(\varphi)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi
and
D_{x}^{\alpha}\mathscr{C}_{l}(\varphi)=\mathscr{C}_{l}(D_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. If
\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are such that

S(\varphi_{j})(x,t)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{C}_{l}(\varphi_{j})(x)\frac{(it)^{l}}{l!},\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},

converges in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
for all
j=0,\ldots,m-1
, then

\Phi=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1-j}Q_{j+k+1}(D_{x})D_{t}^{k}S(\varphi_{j})

solves the Cauchy problem
P(D)\Phi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
D_{t}^{j}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,0)=\varphi_{j}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
j=0,\ldots,m-1

[
14
, Proposition 4.4]
.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence and let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. There is
L_{1}>0
such that for all
h>0
the following property holds: For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
there is

such that for all

\|\mathscr{C}_{p}(D^{\alpha}_{x}\varphi)\|_{0}\leq C(L_{1}h^{b_{0}})^{p}M^{b_{0}}_{p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)},\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{M,h}_{K}.

Proof.

By
(M.2)
, it suffices to consider the case
\alpha=0
. For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the auxiliary norm

|\varphi|_{l}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{|\alpha|=l}|D^{\alpha}_{x}\varphi(x)|,\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

There is
R\geq 1
(only depending on
K
) such that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\|\varphi\|_{l}\leq R^{l}|\varphi|_{l},\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}_{K}.

By Lemma
2.10
, there are
C,L\geq 1
such that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{M,h}_{K}

|\varphi|_{\lfloor b_{0}l\rfloor}\leq h^{\lfloor b_{0}l\rfloor}M_{{\lfloor b_{0}l\rfloor}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}\leq\frac{C}{\min\{1,h\}}(Lh^{b_{0}})^{l}M^{b_{0}}_{l}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}.

The recursively defined operators
\mathscr{C}_{m-1+l}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
have the following explicit representation
[
14
, Proposition 4.5]

\mathscr{C}_{m-1+l}(\varphi)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m},\sigma(\beta)=l}(-1)^{|\beta|}\binom{|\beta|}{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}Q_{m-k}^{\beta_{k}}(D_{x})\varphi,\;\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\sigma(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}j\beta_{j}
. Choose
L_{2}\geq 1
such that for all
k=0,\ldots,m-1

\|Q_{k}(D_{x})\varphi\|_{0}\leq L_{2}\|\varphi\|_{\operatorname{deg}Q_{k}},\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
h>0
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{M,h}_{K}
be arbitrary. For all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
with
\sigma(\beta)=l
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{k=1}^{m}Q_{m-k}^{\beta_{k}}(D_{x})\varphi\right\|_{0}\leq L^{|\beta|}_{2}\|\varphi\|_{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\beta_{k}\operatorname{deg}Q_{m-k}}\leq L_{2}^{l}\|\varphi\|_{\lfloor b_{0}l\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq(L_{2}R^{b_{0}})^{l}|\varphi|_{\lfloor b_{0}l\rfloor}\leq\frac{C}{\min\{1,h\}}(LL_{2}R^{b_{0}}h^{b_{0}})^{l}M^{b_{0}}_{l}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{C}_{m-1+l}(\varphi)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\min\{1,h\}}(LL_{2}R^{b_{0}}h^{b_{0}})^{l}M^{b_{0}}_{l}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m},|\beta|\leq l}\binom{|\beta|}{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\min\{1,h\}}((m+1)LL_{2}R^{b_{0}}h^{b_{0}})^{l}M^{b_{0}}_{l}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\min\{1,h^{1+b_{0}(m-1)}\}}((m+1)LL_{2}R^{b_{0}}h^{b_{0}})^{m-1+l}M^{b_{0}}_{m-1+l}\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{E}^{M,h}(K)}.

Since
\mathscr{C}_{l}=0
for
l<m-1
, this shows the result.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

and
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
open be such that
K\times[-r,r]\Subset V
.
Let

be such that
T_{|V\backslash V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}}\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(V\backslash V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d})
and suppose that there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

(14)

\sup_{(x,t)\in K\times[-r,r]\backslash K}|T(x,t)||t|^{N}<\infty.

Furthermore, let
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
C>0
be such that

(15)

|\langle T,\varphi\rangle|\leq C\|\varphi\|_{l},\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}_{K\times[-r,r]}.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}_{K\times[-r,r]}
be such that

(16)

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\varphi(x,t)|}{|t|^{\max\{N,l+1\}}}<\infty

Then,

\langle T,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x,t)\varphi(x,t)dxdt.

Proof.

In view of (
16
), Taylor’s theorem yields that
D^{n}_{t}\varphi(\,\cdot\,,0)=0
for all
n\leq l
. Hence, also
D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{n}_{t}\varphi(\,\cdot\,,0)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
n\leq l
. By another application of Taylor’s theorem, we obtain that

(17)

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{n-k}_{t}\varphi(x,t)|}{|t|^{k+1}}<\infty,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},k\leq n\leq l.

Choose
\psi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\psi=1
on a neighborhood of
0
. Set
\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\psi(t/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon>0
. Property (
17

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\varphi(x,t)\|_{l}=0.

Hence, by (

\displaystyle\langle T,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle T(x,t),(1-\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\varphi(x,t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x,t)(1-\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\varphi(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x,t)\varphi(x,t)dxdt,

where the last equality is justified by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the inequalities (

∎

5.
Boundary values of zero solutions of
P(D)

In this section we show that zero solutions of
P(D)
satisfying suitable growth estimates near
\mathbb{R}^{d}
have boundary values in a given ultradistribution space. We start with the following fundamental definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. The
boundary value

\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
of an element
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
is defined as

(20)

\langle\operatorname{bv}(f),\varphi\rangle:=\lim_{t,s\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}(f(x,t)-f(x,-s))\varphi(x)dx,\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{[M]}(X),

provided that
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
exists.

Since
\mathscr{D}^{[M]}(X)
is barrelled,
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
exists if and only if the limit in the right-hand side of (
20
) exists and is finite for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{[M]}(X)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
be such that
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
exists. Then,

(i)

\operatorname{bv}(D^{\alpha}_{x}f)=D^{\alpha}_{x}\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
exists for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
.

(ii)

Assume additionally that
p!^{1/\gamma_{0}}\prec M
(
p!^{1/\gamma_{0}}\subset M
). Let
G(D_{x})
be an ultradifferential operator of class
[M]
. Then,
G(D_{x})f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
and
\operatorname{bv}(G(D_{x})f)=G(D_{x})\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
exists.

Proof.

(i)
Obvious.

(ii)
The condition
p!^{1/\gamma_{0}}\subset M
(and thus also
p!^{1/\gamma_{0}}\prec M
) implies that
G(D_{x})
is an ultradifferential operator of class
\{p!^{1/\gamma_{0}}\}
. Since
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)\subset\Gamma^{1/\gamma_{0},1/\mu_{0}}(V\backslash X)
(see Subsection
2.1
), we have that
G(D_{x})f\in C^{\infty}(V\backslash X)
and
P(D)G(D_{x})f=G(D_{x})P(D)f=0
. Hence,
G(D_{x})f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
. The second statement is clear.
∎

We now introduce various weighted spaces of zero solutions of
P(D)
. Let
M
be a positive sequence. Suppose that
p!\prec M
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. For
h>0
we define
\mathscr{B}_{P,M,h}(V\backslash X)
as the Banach space consisting of all
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
such that

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{P,M,h}(V\backslash X)}:=\sup_{(x,t)\in V\backslash X}|f(x,t)|e^{-\omega_{M^{1,*}}\left(\frac{1}{h|t|}\right)}<\infty.

Let
b>0
and suppose that
p!^{1/b}\prec M
. Choose a sequence
(V_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of relatively compact open subsets of
V
such that
\overline{V}_{l}\Subset V_{l+1}
and
V=\bigcup_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}V_{l}
. Set
X_{l}=V_{l}\cap X
for
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We define

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{P,(M),b}(V\backslash X):=\varprojlim_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varinjlim_{h\to 0^{+}}\mathscr{B}_{P,M^{b},h}(V_{l}\backslash X_{l}),

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{P,\{M\},b}(V\backslash X):=\varprojlim_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varprojlim_{h\to\infty}\mathscr{B}_{P,M^{b},h}(V_{l}\backslash X_{l}).

This definition is independent of the chosen sequence
(V_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Obviously, it holds that
C^{\infty}_{P}(V)\subset C^{\infty}_{P,[M],b}(V\backslash X)
. If
p!^{1/b}\asymp M
, we set
C^{\infty}_{P,\{M\},b}(V\backslash X):=C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
.

Next, we define for
j=1,\ldots,m

P_{(j)}(x,t)=\sum_{k=j}^{m}Q_{k}(x)t^{k-j}.

Since
Q_{m}=1
, we have the recursion relations

(21)

t^{j}=\begin{cases}P_{(m)},&j=0,\\
P_{(m-j)}-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}Q_{k+m-j}t^{k},&j=1,\ldots,m-1.\\
\end{cases}

We are ready to discuss the existence of ultradistributional boundary values of zero solutions of
P(D)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.4)_{b_{0}}
(and
p!^{1/b_{0}}\prec M
in the Beurling case). Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. For all
j=0,\ldots,m-1
the mapping

C^{\infty}_{P,[M],b_{0}}(V\backslash X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X),\,f\mapsto\operatorname{bv}(P_{(j+1)}(D)f)

is well-defined and continuous.

We shall show Proposition
5.3
by combining the description of ultradifferentiable functions via (almost) zero solutions of
P(D)
(Proposition
4.1
) with the identity obtained in the next lemma. As mentioned in the introduction, this is a generalization of the method of establishing the existence of (ultra)distributional boundary values of holomorphic functions by combining almost analytic extensions with Stokes’ theorem (more precisely, the formula in
[
12
, Equation (3.1.9), p. 62]
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
. For all
f\in C^{\infty}(X\times[a,b])
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}(X\times\mathbb{R})
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\int_{X}f(x,s)\check{P}(D)\Phi(x,s)dxds=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{X}P(D)f(x,s)\,\Phi(x,s)dxds

\displaystyle+i\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}\int_{X}P_{(j+1)}(D)f(x,b)D_{t}^{j}\Phi(x,b)-P_{(j+1)}(D)f(x,a)D_{t}^{j}\Phi(x,a)dx,

where, as usual,
\check{P}(\xi)=P(-\xi)
.

Proof.

This follows by multiple partial integrations.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

We only show the Beurling case as the Roumieu case can be treated similarly. Let
0\leq j\leq m-1
be arbitrary. It suffices to show that for all relatively compact open subsets
Y
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r,h>0
and
K\Subset Y
the mapping

\mathscr{B}_{P,M^{b_{0}},h}(Y\times(-r,r)\backslash Y)\rightarrow(\mathscr{D}^{(M)}_{K})^{\prime}_{b},\,f\mapsto\operatorname{bv}(P_{(j+1)}(D)f)

is well-defined and continuous. Let
f\in\mathscr{B}_{P,M^{b_{0}},h}(Y\times(-r,r)\backslash Y)
be arbitrary. We set
f_{t}(x,s)=f(x,s+t)
for
0<t<r/2
. Then,
f_{t}\in C^{\infty}(Y\times[0,r/2])
. For
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}^{(M)}_{K}
we consider the function
\Phi=\Phi(0,\ldots,\varphi_{j}=\varphi,\ldots,0)
from Proposition
4.1
(with
\check{P}
and
(h/L)^{1/b_{0}}
instead of
P
and
h
). Choose
\psi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R})
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\psi\subseteq[-r/2,r/2]
and
\psi=1
on a neighborhood of
0
. By applying Lemma
5.4
to
f_{t}
and
\psi\Phi
, we obtain that for
0<t<r/2

\int_{Y}P_{(j+1)}(D)f(x,t)\varphi(x)dx=(-1)^{j}i\int_{0}^{r/2}\int_{Y}f(x,s+t)\check{P}(D)(\psi(s)\Phi(x,s))dxds.

Likewise, one may show that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P_{(j+1)}(D)f(x,-t)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j+1}i\int_{-r/2}^{0}\int_{Y}f(x,s-t)\check{P}(D)(\psi(s)\Phi(x,s))dxds.

Hence,

\langle\operatorname{bv}(P_{(j+1)}(D)f),\varphi\rangle=(-1)^{j}i\int_{-r/2}^{r/2}\int_{Y}f(x,s)\check{P}(D)(\psi(s)\Phi(x,s))dxds,

where the integral at the right hand-side is convergent by property
(ii)
of Proposition
4.1
. The result now follows from Remark
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.4)_{b_{0}}
(and
p!^{1/b_{0}}\prec M
in the Beurling case). Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. For all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the mapping

C^{\infty}_{P,[M],b_{0}}(V\backslash X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X),\,f\mapsto\operatorname{bv}(D^{l}_{t}f)

is well-defined and continuous.

Proof.

For
l=0,\ldots,m-1
this is a consequence of (
21
), Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
. As
Q_{m}=1
, the result for
l\geq m
then follows by using Lemma
5.2
recursively.
∎

Finally, we discuss the existence of distributional boundary values of zero solutions of
P(D)
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let

be open such that

\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)

\langle\operatorname{bv}(f),\varphi\rangle:=\lim_{t,s\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}(f(x,t)-f(x,-s))\varphi(x)dx,\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(X),

provided that
\operatorname{bv}(f)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
exists.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}=X
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define
\mathscr{B}_{P,N}(V\backslash X)
as the Banach space consisting of all
f\in C^{\infty}_{P}(V\backslash X)
such that

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{P,N}(V\backslash X)}:=\sup_{(x,t)\in V\backslash X}|f(x,t)||t|^{N}<\infty.

Choose a sequence
(V_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of relatively compact open subsets of
V
such that
\overline{V}_{l}\Subset V_{l+1}
and
V=\bigcup_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}V_{l}
. Set
X_{l}=V_{l}\cap X
for

. We define

C^{\infty}_{P,\operatorname{pol}}(V\backslash X):=\varprojlim_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varinjlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathscr{B}_{P,N}(V_{l}\backslash X_{l}).

We then have:

Proposition 5.7
.

(cf.
[
23

)

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be open such that

. For all

the mapping

C^{\infty}_{P,\operatorname{pol}}(V\backslash X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X),\,f\mapsto\operatorname{bv}(D^{l}_{t}f)

is well-defined and continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for all
j=0,\ldots,m-1

6.
Main results

This section is devoted to the two main results of this article. Firstly, we give various characterizations of zero solutions of

that admit a boundary value in a given ultradistribution space. Secondly, we represent the

Definition 6.1
.

Let

We start by showing that

Proposition 6.2

. We define the projective spectra

\mathscr{X}=(C^{\infty}_{P}(V_{l}))_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\;\mathscr{Y}=(C^{\infty}_{P,[M],a_{0}}(V_{l}\backslash X_{l}))_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\;\mathscr{Z}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X_{l})\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},

(with restriction as spectral mappings) and the morphism
(\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n,l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\mathscr{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
. We need to show that the mapping

\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n}=\operatorname{Proj}\,(\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n,l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}:C^{\infty}_{P,[M],a_{0}}(V\backslash X)=\operatorname{Proj}\,\mathscr{Y}\rightarrow\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)=\operatorname{Proj}\,\mathscr{Z}

is surjective. Proposition
6.2
yields that
\mathscr{X}=\ker(\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n,l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. By
[
36
, Proposition 3.1.8]
, it suffices to show:

(i)

For all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
T_{0},\ldots,T_{m-1}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X_{l+1})
there is
f\in C^{\infty}_{P,[M],a_{0}}(V_{l}\backslash X_{l})
such that
\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n,l}(f)=(T_{0|X_{l}},\ldots,T_{m-1|X_{l}})
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Proj}^{1}\,\mathscr{X}=0
.

Property
(i)
is a consequence of Proposition
6.4
(cf. the proof of
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
in Theorem
6.6
), whereas
(ii)
follows from the fact that
V
is
P
-convex for supports
[
36
, Section 3.4.4]
.
Finally, since
C^{\infty}_{P,[M],a_{0}}(V\backslash X)
is webbed and
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{D}^{\prime[M]}(X)
is ultrabornological,
\operatorname{bv}^{m}_{n}
is a topological homomorphism by the open mapping theorem of De Wilde.
∎

and Theorem

.

7.
Semi-elliptic polynomials

In this section we discuss the assumptions of our main results (Theorem
6.6
and Theorem
6.7
) for semi-elliptic polynomials. For
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
and
{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
we write
|\beta:{\bf{n}}|:=\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\frac{\beta_{j}}{n_{j}}
.

Definition 7.1
.

A polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d+1}]
is called
semi-elliptic
if there is
{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
such that
P
can be written as

P(\xi)=\sum_{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|\leq 1}c_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}

and

P^{0}(\xi):=\sum_{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|=1}c_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}\neq 0

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\{0\}
.

If
P
is semi-elliptic, the multi-index
{\bf{n}}
in the above definition is unique. More precisely,
n_{j}=\operatorname{deg}_{\xi_{j}}P
,
j=1,\ldots,d+1

[
33
, Lemma 7.14]
. Furthermore,
\deg P=\max\{\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}P\,|\,1\leq j\leq d+1\}

[
9
, Proposition 2]
. It is well-known that semi-elliptic polynomials are hypoelliptic; see
[
13
, Theorem 11.1.11]
and
[
33
, Theorem 7.7]
.

We now determine the values
a_{0}(P)
,
b_{0}(P)
and
\gamma_{0}(P)
for semi-elliptic polynomials
P
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
P(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(x)t^{k}
be semi-elliptic. Then,

\displaystyle a_{0}(P)=\frac{\min\{\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}P\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}{m},

\displaystyle b_{0}(P)=\frac{\max\{\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}P\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}{m}=\frac{\deg Q_{0}}{m},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(P)=\frac{\min\{\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}P\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}{\operatorname{deg}P}.

Proof.

The result for
\gamma_{0}
follows from
[
13
, Theorem 11.4.15]
(see also
[
33
, Theorem 7.7]
). Set
n_{j}=\deg_{x_{j}}P
,
j=1,\ldots,d
,
n_{d+1}=m
and
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d+1})
. Then,
P(\xi)=\sum_{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|\leq 1}c_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, for suitable
c_{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}
and

P^{0}(\xi)=\sum_{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|=1}c_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}\neq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\{0\}
. We define
|\xi|_{{\bf{n}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}|\xi_{j}|^{n_{j}},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Note that for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}

(24)

\,|\xi^{\beta}|\leq|\xi|_{{\bf{n}}}^{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}.

Furthermore, there are
C,R\geq 1
such that for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}

(25)

|\xi|_{{\bf{n}}}^{|\beta:{\bf{n}}|}|D^{\beta}P(\xi)|\leq C|P(\xi)|,\qquad|\xi|\geq R,

as essentially follows from (
24
) and the fact that
P
is semi-elliptic; see the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 7.7]
for details. We now determine
a_{0}
. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\geq R
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds that

\displaystyle|(x,t)|_{{\bf{n}}}^{|(0,\ldots,0,l):{\bf{n}}|}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{n_{j}}\right)^{\frac{l}{m}}\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{|x_{j}|}{|x|}\right)^{n_{j}}|x|^{\min\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}\right)^{\frac{l}{m}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},|y|=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|y_{j}|^{n_{j}}\right)^{\frac{l}{m}}|x|^{l\frac{\min\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}{m}},

which, by (
25
), shows that
a_{0}\geq\min\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}/m
. For the converse inequality, note that

(26)

Q_{0}(x)=\sum_{|(\alpha,0):{\bf{n}}|\leq 1}c_{(\alpha,0)}x^{\alpha}.

Hence, (
24
) implies that

|Q_{0}(x)|\leq C_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{n_{j}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},|x|\geq 1,

for some
C_{0}>0
. Inequality (
25
) particularly shows that

|\xi|_{{\bf{n}}}\leq C_{1}|P(\xi)|,\qquad|\xi|\geq R,

for some
C_{1}>0
. The above two inequalities together with (
2
) yield that for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|
sufficiently large

\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{n_{j}}+|x|^{a_{0}m}=|(x,|x|^{a_{0}})|_{{\bf{n}}}\leq C_{1}|P(x,|x|^{a_{0}})|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{n_{j}}

for some
C>0
, which implies that
a_{0}\leq\min\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}/m
. This proves the result for
a_{0}
. Next, we determine
b_{0}
. Let
0\leq k\leq m-1
with
Q_{k}\neq 0
be arbitrary. Then, there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|=\deg Q_{k}
such that
c_{(\alpha,k)}\neq 0
and, thus,
|(\alpha,k):{\bf{n}}|\leq 1
. Hence,

1\geq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{n_{j}}+\frac{k}{m}\geq\frac{\deg Q_{k}}{\max\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}}+\frac{k}{m}.

This implies that
\operatorname{deg}Q_{k}/(m-k)\leq\max\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}/m
and, thus,
b_{0}\leq\max\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}/m
. Let
1\leq j\leq d
be arbitrary. Consider
\alpha^{j}=(0,\ldots,\alpha^{j}_{j}=n_{j},\ldots,0)\ \in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Then,
c_{(\alpha^{j},0)}\neq 0
for otherwise
(\delta_{l,j})_{1\leq l\leq d+1}\neq 0
would be a zero of
P^{0}
. By (
26
), we obtain that
n_{j}=\deg_{x_{j}}Q_{0}
. Since
Q_{0}
is semi-elliptic (on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) as
P
is so, we have that
\operatorname{deg}Q_{0}=\max\{\deg_{x_{j}}Q_{0}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}
. Hence,
\max\{n_{j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq d\}/m=\deg Q_{0}/m
. Clearly,
\deg Q_{0}/m\leq b_{0}
. This proves the result for
b_{0}
.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Let
P(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(x)t^{k}
be semi-elliptic. By
[
13
, Theorem 11.4.15]
, we have that
\mu_{0}(P)=m/\operatorname{deg}P
. Since the exact value of
\mu_{0}(P)
is not important to us, we do not consider it in what follows.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
P(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}Q_{k}(x)t^{k}
be a semi-elliptic polynomial with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{1}}P=\cdots=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{d}}P=n
. Then,

a_{0}(P)=b_{0}(P)=\frac{n}{m}=\frac{\deg Q_{0}}{m}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{0}(P)=\min\left\{1,\frac{n}{m}\right\}.

Next, given a semi-elliptic polynomial
P
, we present a sufficient geometric condition on an open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
to be
P
-convex for supports
[
9
,
15
]
.
A function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to satisfy the minimum principle in a closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
if for all
K\Subset F\cap V
it holds that

\min_{x\in K}f(x)=\min_{x\in\partial_{F}K}f(x),

where
\partial_{F}K
denotes the boundary of
K
in
F
(in case of
F\cap V=\emptyset
the condition is vacuous).

Proposition 7.5
.

(
[
15
, Theorem 1]

and

[
9
, Proposition 2
(iii)
]
) Let
P
be a semi-elliptic polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Then, an open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is
P
-convex for supports if its boundary distance function

d_{V}\,:\,V\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\xi\mapsto d(\xi,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash V),

where
d(\xi,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash V)=\inf\{|\xi-\eta|\,|\,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash V\}
, satisfies the minimum principle in every affine subspace parallel to the subspace

\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}|\xi_{j}=0\mbox{ for all }1\leq j\leq d+1\mbox{ with }\operatorname{deg}_{\xi_{j}}P<\operatorname{deg}P\}.

Corollary
7.4
implies that Theorem
6.6
and Theorem
6.7
are applicable to every semi-elliptic polynomial with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{1}}P=\cdots=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{d}}P=n
. Moreover, Proposition
7.5
gives a sufficient condition on the open sets
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for which Theorem
6.7
is valid. We now discuss this class of polynomials in some more detail. We distinguish three cases:

(i)

m=n=\deg P
: Then,
P
is elliptic and, conversely, every elliptic polynomial is of this form. Corollary
7.4
implies that

a_{0}(P)=b_{0}(P)=\gamma_{0}(P)=1,

while Proposition
7.5
yields the well-known fact that every open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is
P
-convex for supports.

(ii)

m<n=\deg P
: The heat operator and, more generally, the
k
-parabolic operators in the sense of Petrowsky
[
33
, Definition 7.11]
are of this form.
Corollary
7.4
implies that

a_{0}(P)=b_{0}(P)=n/m\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{0}(P)=1.

By Proposition
7.5
, we have that an open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is
P
-convex for supports if
d_{V}
satisfies the minimum principle in every characteristic hyperplane, i.e., in every hyperplane of the form
\{(x,\tau)|\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\},\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. We mention that, by
[
15
, Corollary 5]
(see also
[
13
, Theorem 10.8.1]
), the converse holds true as well in this case. In particular, all open sets of the form
V=X\times I
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
open, are
P
-convex for supports.

(iii)

m=\deg P>n
: Corollary
7.4
implies that

a_{0}(P)=b_{0}(P)=\gamma_{0}(P)=n/m.

By Proposition
7.5
, we have that an open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is
P
-convex for supports if
d_{V}
satisfies the minimum principle in every line
\{(x,\tau)|\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Again, all open sets of the form
V=X\times I
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
open, are
P
-convex for supports.

Finally, note that Theorem
1.1
stated in the introduction follows from Theorem
6.6
, Theorem
6.7
and the above remarks.

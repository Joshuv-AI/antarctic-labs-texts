Motivated in particular by the extraordinary properties of graphene, which could make it possible to design revolutionary devices
[
12
,
11
,
44
]
, major efforts have been made to understand wave propagation phenomena in quantum waveguides. Mathematically, this leads to study the spectrum of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions in domains made of thin ligaments forming unbounded periodic lattices. In the literature, different kind of geometries have been considered and we refer the reader to
[
49
,
21
,
23
,
15
,
22
,
10
]
for review studies. In the present article, we work in a waveguide
\Pi^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
obtained by repeating periodically a pattern
\omega^{\varepsilon}
, itself constructed by shrinking an inner field geometry
\Omega
by a factor
\varepsilon>0
small (see Figure
1
). We denote by
A^{\varepsilon}
the corresponding Dirichlet Laplacian in
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
. From the Floquet-Bloch-Gelfand theory
[
13
,
25
,
50
,
26
]
, we know that the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
has a band-gap structure, the bands being generated by the eigenvalues of a spectral problem set on the periodicity cell
\omega^{\varepsilon}
with quasi-periodic boundary conditions involving the Floquet-Bloch parameter.

The first goal of this article is to recall how to study the features of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
as the ligaments become thinner and thinner, i.e. as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. To proceed, following D. Grieser in
[
14
]
(see also
[
47
,
29
,
5
,
30
]
), we use techniques of dimension reduction to derive 1D models for the spectral problem in
\omega^{\varepsilon}
. Then by exploiting these 1D models depending on the Floquet-Bloch parameter, for which explicit computations can be done, we get information on the spectral bands. The results that we obtain can be summarized as follows.

First, due to the Dirichlet boundary conditions, all the bands move to
+\infty
as
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Concerning the width of the bands, the results directly depend on the properties of the Dirichlet Laplacian
A^{\Omega}
in the near field geometry
\Omega
. Note that
\Omega
is an unbounded waveguide with two infinite outlets of height one. The first spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
are extremely short, of length
O(e^{-\delta/\varepsilon})
,
\delta>0
, and their number coincides with the number of eigenvalues of
A^{\Omega}
smaller or equal to the lower bound of the continuous spectrum (
\pi^{2}
in our case).

The width of the next spectral bands depends on the geometry of
\Omega
and more precisely on the dimension of some space
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}
of almost standing waves in
\Omega
. More precisely,
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}
is defined as the space of bounded functions which are not exponentially decaying at infinity and which satisfy the homogeneous Helmholtz equation in
\Omega
with a spectral parameter equal to the lower bound of the continuous spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
.

Generically, i.e. for most
\Omega
, we have
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=\{0\}
and in that situation, the length of the next spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
is
O(\varepsilon)
. As a consequence, for most spectral parameters, waves can not propagate in
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
. For certain particular inner field geometries
\Omega
however, there holds
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. In that case, the length of the next spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
in the corresponding periodic domains
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
is
O(1)
and so they are much more possibilities for waves to propagate.

The second goal of this work, which makes the main originality of this article, is to study the behaviour of the spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
when perturbing
\Omega
around a particular
\Omega_{\star}
where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. To precise the problem, assume that
\Omega
is defined via a geometrical parameter
\rho
such that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
for
\rho=\rho_{\star}
and
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=\{0\}
for
\rho-\rho_{\star}\neq 0
small. From these near field geometries, we can define a family of thin periodic waveguides that we denote by
\Pi^{\rho,\varepsilon}
. In
\Pi^{\rho_{\star},\varepsilon}
, the bands of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
are rather large whereas in
\Pi^{\rho,\varepsilon}
for
\rho\neq\rho_{\star}
, they are short. How to describe the transition? To answer this question, we will propose a 1D model for the spectrum incorporating the dependence with respect to the parameter
\rho
. It will allow us to highlight a phenomenon of breathing of the spectrum: when inflating
\Omega
around
\Omega_{\star}
, the spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
suddenly expand and then shrink. Additionally, in the process a band dives below the normalized threshold
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
, stops breathing, “dies” and becomes extremely extremely short as the near field geometry
\Omega
continues to inflate.

Let us mention that the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian was studied in different 3D periodic waveguides in
[
1
,
6
]
(see also
[
45
]
). More precisely, in
[
1
]
the authors deal with geometries for which the near field domain
\Omega
is a cruciform junction of two cylinders whose cross section coincides with the unit disc. In
[
6
]
,
\Omega
is the union of three bars which have a square cross section. In these works, it is shown that the corresponding
\Omega
are such that
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=\{0\}
. However by varying some parameters like the angle between the cylinders or the width of certain bars, or adding a resonator, one could find situations where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. Then perturbing
\Omega
around these particular configurations, one would observe the same phenomenon of breathing of the spectrum as described in the present article. Indeed what we describe below in Section
6
is generic and not specific to dimension two.

Finally, let us indicate that these phenomena do not appear when considering the spectrum of the Laplace operator with Neumann boundary conditions (BCs). The reason is that for this problem, the bottom of the continuous spectrum of the near field operator
A^{\Omega}
is zero and we always have
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
because the constants solve the homogeneous Laplace equation with Neumann BCs. As a consequence, independently of the geometry of
\Omega
, the good limit model is a system of ordinary differential equations on the 1D ligaments obtained when taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
supplemented by Kirchoff transmission conditions at the nodes of the graph (continuity of the field and zero outgoing flux, i.e. the sum of the derivatives of the field along the outgoing directions at the node vanishes). This is the so-called L. Pauling’s model
[
46
]
which is used in
[
23
]
and justified in
[
24
,
14
,
48
]
.

The outline is as follows. In Section
2
we start by defining the geometry and introduce the notation. Section
3
is dedicated to the presentation of the properties of the near field operator
A^{\Omega}
which plays a key role in the analysis. There we also state the first important result of this work, namely Theorem
3.5
, which describes the features of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon
tends to zero. In Sections
4
,
5
we prove the different items of Theorem
3.5
. Then in Section
6
we establish Theorem
6.1
, the second important result of this article, where we show the breathing phenomenon of the spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
when perturbing the inner field geometry around a particular
\Omega_{\star}
where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. In Section
7
, we explain how to construct examples of
\Omega_{\star}
where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. In Section
8
, we consider the particular case where
\Omega_{\star}
is such that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
and classical Kirchhoff transmissions conditions must be imposed in the first model describing the bands above
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
. Due to this latter property, we find that the spectrum of this model coincides with
[\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2};+\infty)
. By taking into account terms of higher order in
\varepsilon
, we show however that in general the spectrum of the exact operator
A^{\varepsilon}
has some short gaps. Finally in Section
9
, we give numerical illustrations of the different results.

2
Setting

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a waveguide which coincides with the strip
\mathbb{R}\times(-1/2;1/2)
outside of a bounded region (Figure
1
top left). For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the unit cell

\omega^{\varepsilon}:=\{z:=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,z/\varepsilon\in\Omega\mbox{ and }|x|<1/2\}

(1)

and set
\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}:=\{\pm 1/2\}\times(-1/2;1/2)
(Figure
1
top right). Finally we define the periodic waveguide

\Pi^{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,(x-m,y)\in\omega^{\varepsilon}\cup\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}_{+}\mbox{ for some }m\in\mathbb{Z}:=\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\}\}

(2)

(Figure
1
bottom). We assume that
\Omega
,
\omega^{\varepsilon}
and
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
are connected with Lipschitz boundaries. In
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
, we consider the spectral problem for the Dirichlet Laplacian

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta u^{\varepsilon}&=&\lambda^{\varepsilon}\,u^{\varepsilon}&\mbox{ in }\Pi^{\varepsilon}\\[3.0pt]
u^{\varepsilon}&=&0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Pi^{\varepsilon}.\end{array}

(3)

Note that since we work with Dirichlet boundary conditions, the assumption of Lipschitz regularity of the boundary could be relaxed. The variational form associated with this problem writes

\int_{\Pi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}\,dz=\lambda^{\varepsilon}\int_{\Pi^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\,dz,\qquad\forall v^{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Pi^{\varepsilon}).

(4)

Here
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})
stands for the Sobolev space of functions of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})
which vanish on the boundary
\partial\Pi^{\varepsilon}
. Classically (see e.g
[
3
, §10.1]
), the variational problem (
4
) gives rise to an unbounded, positive definite, selfadjoint operator
A^{\varepsilon}
in the Hilbert space
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(A^{\varepsilon})\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})
. Note that this operator is sometimes called the quantum graph Laplacian
[
2
,
48
]
. Since
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
is unbounded, the embedding
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Pi^{\varepsilon})
is not compact and
A^{\varepsilon}
has a non empty continuous component
\sigma_{c}(A^{\varepsilon})
(
[
3
, Thm. 10.1.5]
). Actually, due to the periodicity, we have
\sigma_{c}(A^{\varepsilon})=\sigma(A^{\varepsilon})
. The Gelfand transform (see the surveys
[
25
,
31
]
and books
[
26
,
42
]
)

u^{\varepsilon}(z)\mapsto U^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)=\cfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\eta j}u^{\varepsilon}(x+j,y),\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R},

converts Problem (
3
) into a spectral problem set in the periodicity cell
\omega^{\varepsilon}
(see Figure
1
top right). This problem, which involves quasi-periodicity boundary conditions at
\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
, writes

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta U^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)&=&\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\,U^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)&z\in\omega^{\varepsilon}\\[3.0pt]
U^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)&=&0&z\in\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Pi^{\varepsilon}\\[3.0pt]
U^{\varepsilon}(-1/2,y,\eta)&=&e^{i\eta}U^{\varepsilon}(+1/2,y,\eta)&y\in(-\varepsilon/2;\varepsilon/2)\\[3.0pt]
\partial_{x}U^{\varepsilon}(-1/2,y,\eta)&=&e^{i\eta}\partial_{x}U^{\varepsilon}(+1/2,y,\eta)&y\in(-\varepsilon/2;\varepsilon/2).\end{array}

(5)

Problem (
5
) is formally selfadjoint for any value of the Floquet parameter
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Additionally the transformation
\eta\mapsto\eta+2\pi
leaves invariant the quasiperiodicity conditions. Therefore it is sufficient to study (
5
) for
\eta\in[0;2\pi)
. For any
\eta\in[0;2\pi)
, the spectrum of (
5
) is discrete, made of the unbounded sequence of normal eigenvalues

0<\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)\leq\Lambda_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)\leq\dots\leq\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)\leq\dots

where the
\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
are counted according to their multiplicity. The functions

\eta\mapsto\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)

are continuous (
[
19
, Chap. 9]
) so that the sets

\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}=\{\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta),\,\eta\in[0;2\pi)\},

(6)

the so-called spectral bands, are connected compact in
[0;+\infty)
. Finally, according to the theory (see again
[
25
,
31
,
26
,
42
]
), the spectrum of the operator
A^{\varepsilon}
has the form

\sigma(A^{\varepsilon})=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}

where
\mathbb{N}^{\ast}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
. Thus, we see that to study the behaviour of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
with respect to
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we have to clarify the dependence of the
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
in
\varepsilon
.

3
Near field problem and first main result

As already mentioned in the introduction, the analysis developed for example in
[
14
,
33
]
shows that the asymptotic behaviour of the
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
with respect to
\varepsilon
depends on the features of the Dirichlet Laplacian in
\Omega
, the geometry obtained when zooming in the periodicity cell
\omega^{\varepsilon}
(see Figure
1
top left). This leads us to study the spectral problem

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta u&=&\lambda\,u&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\[3.0pt]
u&=&0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{array}

(7)

which is now independent of
\varepsilon
. We denote by
A^{\Omega}
the unbounded, positive definite, selfadjoint operator naturally associated with this problem defined in the Hilbert space
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(A^{\Omega})\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Its continuous spectrum
\sigma_{c}(A^{\Omega})
occupies the ray
[\pi^{2};+\infty)
and the threshold point
\lambda_{\dagger}:=\pi^{2}
is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian in the cross section of the unbounded branches of
\Omega
. According to the geometry of
\Omega
, the operator
A^{\Omega}
may have or not discrete spectrum. To set ideas, assume that
A^{\Omega}
has exactly
N_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) in its discrete spectrum, that we denote by

0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{N_{\bullet}}<\pi^{2}.

(8)

The fact that
\mu_{1}
is simple is a classical consequence of the Krein-Rutman theorem (see e.g.
[
17
, Thm. 1.2.5]
). We will see below that the properties of (
7
) with
\lambda
coinciding with the threshold of the continuous spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
plays an important role in the asymptotics of certain of the
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This leads us to study the problem

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}\Delta W+\pi^{2}W&=&0&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
W&=&0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}

(9)

Let us describe the features of Problem (
9
). To proceed, first define the waves
w_{0}
,
w_{1}
and the linear wave packets
w_{\pm}^{\mathrm{out}}
,
w_{\pm}^{\mathrm{in}}
such that

w_{0}(z)=\varphi(y),\qquad w_{1}=|x|\varphi(y),\qquad\begin{array}[]{|lcl}w^{\mathrm{out}}(z)&=&w_{1}(z)-iw_{0}(z)=(|x|-i)\varphi(y)\\[5.0pt]
w^{\mathrm{in}}(z)&=&w_{1}(z)+iw_{0}(z)=(|x|+i)\varphi(y)\end{array}

(10)

with

\varphi(y)=\sqrt{2}\cos(\pi y).

(11)

Here the notation “in/out” stands for “incoming/outgoing”. Introduce some cut-off functions
\psi_{\pm}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\psi_{\pm}(x)=1
for
\pm x>1
and
\psi_{\pm}(x)=0
for
\pm x\leq 1/2
. The theory presented for example in
[
42
, Chap. 5]
guarantees that Problem (
9
) admits solutions of the form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{+}&=&\psi_{-}s_{+-}w^{\mathrm{out}}+\psi_{+}(w^{\mathrm{in}}+s_{++}w^{\mathrm{out}})+\tilde{v}_{+}\\[5.0pt]
v_{-}&=&\psi_{-}(w^{\mathrm{in}}+s_{--}w^{\mathrm{out}})+\psi_{+}s_{-+}w^{\mathrm{out}}+\tilde{v}_{-}\end{array}

(12)

where
s_{\pm\pm}
,
s_{\pm\mp}
are complex numbers and the
\tilde{v}_{\pm}
decay exponentially as
|x|\to+\infty
. The coefficients
s_{\pm\pm}
,
s_{\pm\mp}
form the so-called threshold scattering matrix

\mathbb{S}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}s_{++}&s_{+-}\\[2.0pt]
s_{-+}&s_{--}\end{array}\right).

(13)

It is known that
\mathbb{S}
is symmetric (
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}^{\top}
) but not hermitian in general, and unitary (
\mathbb{S}\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\top}=\mathrm{Id}
). Here and below
\mathrm{Id}
is the identity matrix of
\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
. On the other hand, it may happen that (
9
) also admits solutions which are exponentially decaying at infinity (trapped modes). In this situation,
v_{\pm}
are not uniquely defined. However these trapped modes do not modify the threshold scattering matrix because of the decay and therefore
\mathbb{S}
is always uniquely defined. Following
[
30
,
34
,
20
,
38
]
, now we introduce some vocabulary. Define the vector spaces

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathrm{X}&\hskip-5.69046pt:=&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{U=\sum_{\pm}\psi_{\pm}(C^{1}_{\pm}w_{1}+C^{0}_{\pm}w_{0})+\tilde{U}\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\mbox{ with }C^{1}_{\pm},\,C^{0}_{\pm}\in\mathbb{C}\mbox{ and }\tilde{U}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\};\\[14.0pt]
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}&\hskip-5.69046pt:=&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{U=\sum_{\pm}\psi_{\pm}C^{0}_{\pm}w_{0}+\tilde{U}\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\mbox{ with }C^{0}_{\pm}\in\mathbb{C}\mbox{ and }\tilde{U}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\};\\[14.0pt]
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}&\hskip-5.69046pt:=&\hskip-5.69046pt\displaystyle\{U\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\mbox{ satisfying }(\ref{NearFieldPb})\}.\end{array}\hskip-8.5359pt

(14)

Here
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}
is the space of bounded solutions of (
9
) while
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}
stands for the space of trapped modes.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
A^{\Omega}
has a Threshold Resonance (TR) if
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}\neq\{0\}
, i.e. if (
9
) admits a non zero bounded solution.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
A^{\Omega}
has a proper TR if
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}/\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}\neq\{0\}
, i.e. if (
9
) admits a bounded solution with does not decay at infinity. If
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}\neq\{0\}
, we say that the TR is improper.

Definition 3.3
.

Set
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}:=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}/\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}
. This quotient space is sometimes called the space of almost standing waves of (
9
).

In the following, the dimension of
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}
will play an important role. The following proposition gives a characterization of this quantity. Its proof is similar to the one of
[
35
, Thm. 7.1]
(see also
[
33
]
).

Proposition 3.4
.

We have
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}\,(\mathbb{S}+\mathrm{Id}))
.

Let us describe the different situations that one can meet.

\bullet
The dimension of
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}
is independent of the other quantities. Moreover, the existence of trapped modes for (
9
) (hence at the threshold of the continuous spectrum of
A^{\Omega}
) is a rare phenomenon which appears only for specific geometries which are unstable with respect to small perturbations.

\bullet
From the definition of
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}
, clearly we have
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}\leq 2
. On the other hand, since
\mathbb{S}
is a unitary matrix, its two eigenvalues are located on the unit circle. Proposition
3.4
ensures that Problem (
9
) has a proper TR when one of these eigenvalues is located at
-1
. For this reason, the existence of TR is not the common situation. In other words, in general there holds
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}=\{0\}
. However let us mention that the reference strip
\Omega=\mathbb{R}\times(-1/2;1/2)
offers a very simple example of geometry where
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}\neq\{0\}
. More precisely, in that case there holds
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}=\mathrm{span}(w_{0})
,
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}=\{0\}
, and so
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
(see Remark
5.3
for more details). It is an open question to prove the existence of waveguides where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=2
but this does not seem impossible. For an example of geometry in 2D where this happens for the Neumann Laplacian at a positive threshold, we refer the reader to
[
39
]
.

Once this notation has been introduced, we can give the first important result of this article.

Theorem 3.5
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, let
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}=[a^{\varepsilon}_{p-};a^{\varepsilon}_{p+}]
, with
a^{\varepsilon}_{p-}\leq a^{\varepsilon}_{p+}
, be the spectral band in (
6
).
There are some (real) constants
c_{p-}<c_{p+}
,
C_{p}>0
,
\delta_{p}>0
and
\varepsilon_{p}>0
such that we have

\mbox{For }p=1,\dots,N_{\bullet}:

\Big|a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p}+\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-\sqrt{\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}/\varepsilon}c_{p\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{p}\,e^{-(1+\delta_{p})\sqrt{\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}/\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}];\\

(15)

\mbox{For }p=N_{\bullet}+1,\dots,N_{\bullet}+N_{\dagger}:

\Big|a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-\pi\sqrt{3}/\varepsilon}c_{p\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{p}\,e^{-(1+\delta_{p})\pi\sqrt{3}/\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}];\\

(16)

\mbox{For }p=N_{\bullet}+N_{\dagger}+m,\,m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phantom{ii}i)\mbox{ if }\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=\{0\},&\qquad\Big|a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+m^{2}\pi^{2}+\varepsilon c_{p\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{p}\,\varepsilon^{1+\delta_{p}},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}];\\[10.0pt]
\phantom{i}ii)\mbox{ if }\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1,&\qquad\Big|a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+c_{p\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{p}\,\varepsilon^{1+\delta_{p}},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}];\\[10.0pt]
iii)\mbox{ if }\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=2,&\qquad\Big|a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+(m-1)^{2}\pi^{2}+\varepsilon c_{p\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{p}\,\varepsilon^{1+\delta_{p}},\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{p}].\end{array}

(17)

Here the
\mu_{p}
are the ones introduced in (
8
).

Let us comment the different results provided by this theorem. First, when
\varepsilon
tends to zero, the whole spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
goes to
+\infty
as
\varepsilon^{-2}
. This is due to the Dirichlet condition imposed on the boundary combined with the fact that the elements of the lattice have diameter in
O(\varepsilon)
. Besides, the first
N_{\bullet}+N_{\dagger}
spectral bands of
A^{\varepsilon}
become extremely short, in
O(e^{-c/\varepsilon})
for some
c>0
which depends on the band. Concerning the next spectral bands
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
,
p=N_{\bullet}+N_{\dagger}+m
with
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, the behaviour depends on the dimension of
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}
. When the latter is zero or two (cases
i)
and
iii)
), the spectral bands are of length
O(\varepsilon)
. Moreover, between
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p+1}
, there is a gap, that is, a segment of spectral parameters
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
such that waves cannot propagate, whose length tends to
(2m+1)\pi^{2}
(resp.
(2m-1)\pi^{2}
) in case
i)
(resp.
iii)
). In other words, for these two cases, the propagation of waves in the thin lattice
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
is hampered and occurs only for very narrow (closed) intervals of frequencies. When the dimension of
\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}
is one (case
ii)
), the situation is very different. Indeed, asymptotically the spectral band
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
is of length
c_{p+}-c_{p-}
, with in general
c_{p+}>c_{p-}
. As a consequence, waves can propagate in
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
for much larger intervals of frequencies than in cases
i)
and
iii)
.

4
Asymptotic behaviour of the first
N_{\bullet}
spectral bands

In this section, we recall how to obtain a model leading to an expansion for the spectral band
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}
,
p\in\{1,\dots,N_{\bullet}\}
, appearing in (
6
). By definition, we have

\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}=\{\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta),\,\eta\in[0;2\pi)\}

(18)

where
\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
is the first eigenvalue of (
5
). Therefore our goal is to obtain an asymptotic expansion of
\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
with respect to
\varepsilon
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Pick
\eta\in[0;2\pi)
. Let
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\eta)
be an eigenfunction associated with
\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
. As a first approximation when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, it is natural to consider the expansions

\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)=\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p}+\dots,\qquad u^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)=v(z/\varepsilon)+\dots

(19)

where
\mu_{p}\in(0;\pi^{2})
stands for an eigenvalue of the discrete spectrum of the operator
A^{\Omega}
introduced in (
8
) and
v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a corresponding eigenfunction normalized in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Indeed, inserting the pair
(\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p},v(\cdot/\varepsilon))
in Problem (
5
) only leaves a small discrepancy on the faces
\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
defined after (
1
) because
v
is exponentially decaying at infinity. Let us write more precisely the decomposition of
v
at infinity for further usage. Using Fourier decomposition, we get

v(z)=K_{\pm}\,e^{-\beta^{p}_{1}|x|}\,\varphi(y)+O(e^{-\beta^{p}_{2}|x|})\qquad\mbox{ as }\pm x\to+\infty.

(20)

Here
K_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta^{p}_{1}:=\sqrt{\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}
,
\beta^{p}_{2}:=\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}
and
\varphi
appears in (
11
). The first model (
19
) is simple but does not comprise the dependence in
\eta
. To improve it, consider the more refined ansätze

\Lambda_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)=\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p}+\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-\beta^{p}_{1}/\varepsilon}M(\eta)+\dots,\qquad u^{\varepsilon}(z,\eta)=v(z/\varepsilon)+e^{-\beta^{p}_{1}/\varepsilon}V(z/\varepsilon,\eta)+\dots

(21)

where the quantities
M(\eta)
and
V(\cdot,\eta)
, which are complex valued, are to determine. Inserting (
21
) into (
5
), first we obtain that
V(\cdot,\eta)
must satisfy

\begin{array}[]{|rccl}-\Delta V(\cdot,\eta)-\mu_{p}V(\cdot,\eta)&=&M(\eta)v&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\[3.0pt]
V(\cdot,\eta)&=&0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}

(22)

Below, to simplify and because this is not the heart of the article, we shall assume that
\mu_{p}
is a simple eigenvalue and the constants
K_{\pm}
are non zero. When this is not the case, the analysis can be easily adapted (see Remark
4.1
below). In order to have a solution to (
22
), we must look for a
V(\cdot,\eta)
which is growing at infinity. Due to (
22
), the simplest growth that we can allow is

V(z,\eta)=B_{\pm}\,e^{\beta^{p}_{1}|x|}\,\varphi(y)+O(e^{-\beta^{p}_{1}|x|})\qquad\mbox{ as }\pm x\to+\infty

where the
B_{\pm}
are some constants. Then the quasi-periodic conditions satisfied by
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\eta)
at
\partial\omega^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
(see (
5
)) lead us to choose the
B_{j}
such that

\begin{array}[]{|l}K_{-}+B_{-}=e^{i\eta}(K_{+}+B_{+})\\[2.0pt]
K_{-}-B_{-}=e^{i\eta}(-K_{+}+B_{+})\end{array}

Solving this algebraic system, we obtain
B_{+}=K_{-}\,e^{-i\eta}
and
B_{-}=K_{+}\,e^{+i\eta}
. Now since
\mu_{p}
is a simple eigenvalue of
A^{\Omega}
, multiplying (
22
) by
v
, integrating by parts in

\Omega^{R}:=\{(x,y)\in\Omega\,|\,|x|<R\}

and taking the limit
R\to+\infty
, we find that there is a solution if and only if the following compatibility condition

M(\eta)\|v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=-2\beta^{p}_{1}K_{+}K_{-}(e^{i\eta}+e^{-i\eta})\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad M(\eta)=-4\beta^{p}_{1}K_{+}K_{-}\cos\eta

is satisfied. This defines the value of
M(\eta)
in the expansion (
21
). From (
18
), we deduce that as
\varepsilon
tends to zero, the bounds of
\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{p}=[a^{\varepsilon}_{p-};a^{\varepsilon}_{p+}]
admit the asymptotics

a^{\varepsilon}_{p\pm}=\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{p}\pm\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-\sqrt{\pi^{2}-\mu_{p}}/\varepsilon}c_{p}+\dots

with
c_{p}=4\beta^{p}_{1}K_{+}K_{-}
. This shows the result (
15
) of Theorem
3.5
. Note that we decided to focus on a rather formal presentation above for the sake of conciseness. We emphasize that all these results can be completely justified by proving rigorous error. This has been realized in detail in
[
36
,
37
,
40
]
for similar problems and can be repeated here with obvious modifications.

Remark 4.1
.

When
\mu_{p}
is a multiple eigenvalue of
A^{\Omega}
, one can always construct a basis of the corresponding eigenspace for which at most one vector admits the expansion (

) with

. Let us mention that the spectral bands generated by the other vectors of the basis will be shorter than the one studied above when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

5.2
Asymptotics of the spectral bands when
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1

In this section, we assume that the space of almost standing waves is of dimension one (in particular
A^{\Omega}
has a proper TR). From Proposition
3.4
, this is equivalent to have
\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}\,(\mathbb{S}+\mathrm{Id}))=1
. In accordance with the work of D. Grieser
[
14
]
, we will show that the conditions at zero to complement the system of ODEs (
26
) are different from the Dirichlet ones found in the previous section.

Denote by
\tau_{1}=-1
,
\tau_{2}\neq-1
the two eigenvalues of
\mathbb{S}
and introduce
\bm{b}_{1}
,
\bm{b}_{2}
two corresponding normalized eigenvector. Since
\mathbb{S}
is unitary, there holds
(\bm{b}_{1},\bm{b}_{2})=0
where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the standard inner product of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Since
\mathbb{S}
is unitary and symmetric, we can choose real valued
\bm{b}_{1}
,
\bm{b}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let us work for example with
\bm{b}_{1}
. Exploiting that
\mathbb{S}
is unitary and symmetric, we can write
\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}=\mathbb{S}\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\top}\,\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}=\mathbb{S}\overline{\mathbb{S}}\,\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}
. Inserting the identity
\mathbb{S}\bm{b}_{1}=\tau_{1}\,\bm{b}_{1}
in this relation, we obtain

\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}=\overline{\tau_{1}}\,\mathbb{S}\,\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}.

Using that
\tau_{1}=\overline{\tau_{1}}^{-1}
, because the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{S}
are located on the unit circle in the complex plane, we infer that
\overline{\bm{b}_{1}}
is also an eigenvector associated with
\tau_{1}
. This ensures that
\bm{b}_{1}
can be chosen in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If
\mathbb{S}
has an eigenvalue of multiplicity two, which is not the case considered in this section, one can also show that the corresponding eigenvectors can be chosen in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that since
\mathbb{S}
is unitary, it is diagonalizable, and therefore cannot have a Jordan block of size two.

Exploiting Lemma
5.1
, and changing
\bm{b}_{1}
in
-\bm{b}_{1}
if necessary, we can introduce
\theta\in[0;\pi)
such that

\bm{b}_{1}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\top}.

(44)

Then, due to the relation
(\bm{b}_{1},\bm{b}_{2})=0
, we deduce that we can take
\bm{b}_{2}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\top}
.

This said, let us exploit the identities (
29
), (
31
). Taking the inner product of (
31
) against
\bm{b}_{1}
, we find

\varepsilon(\partial_{x}\bm{\gamma}(0),\bm{b}_{1})=((\mathrm{Id}+\mathbb{S})\bm{a},\bm{b}_{1})=(\bm{a},(\mathrm{Id}+\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\top})\bm{b}_{1})=(\bm{a},(\mathrm{Id}+\overline{\mathbb{S}})\bm{b}_{1})=0.

(45)

Therefore we obtain

\cos\theta\,\partial_{x}\gamma^{+}(0)=\sin\theta\,\partial_{x}\gamma^{-}(0).

(46)

This is a first transmission condition to complement (
26
). On the other hand, (
31
) also implies that
(\mathrm{Id}+\mathbb{S})\bm{a}=0\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{S}\bm{a}=-\bm{a}
and so
\bm{a}\in\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{Id}+\mathbb{S})
, i.e.
\bm{a}=c\,\bm{b}_{1}
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
. From (
29
), this gives
\bm{\gamma}(0)=2i\bm{a}=2ic\,\bm{b}_{1}
, which implies
(\bm{\gamma}(0),\bm{b}_{2})=0
. This provides the second transmission condition at the origin

\sin\theta\,\gamma^{+}(0)=\cos\theta\,\gamma^{-}(0).

(47)

Let us summarize the results that we have obtained. Set
I:=(-1/2;1/2)
,
I_{+}:=(0;1/2)
and
I_{-}:=(-1/2;0)
. Gathering (
26
), (
46
), (
47
), finally we have found that
\nu
,
\gamma^{\pm}
satisfy the problem with weighted Kirchhoff transmissions conditions at the origin

\begin{array}[]{|rclrcl}\partial^{2}_{x}\gamma^{\pm}+\nu\gamma^{\pm}&=&0\quad\mbox{ in }I_{\pm}\\[3.0pt]
\gamma^{-}(-1/2)&=&e^{i\eta}\gamma^{+}(+1/2)&\sin\theta\,\gamma^{+}(0)&=&\cos\theta\,\gamma^{-}(0)\\[3.0pt]
\partial_{x}\gamma^{-}(-1/2)&=&e^{i\eta}\partial_{x}\gamma^{+}(+1/2)&\qquad\cos\theta\,\partial_{x}\gamma^{+}(0)&=&\sin\theta\,\partial_{x}\gamma^{-}(0).\end{array}

(48)

If
\psi
is a function defined on
I
, we note by
\psi_{\pm}
its restriction to
I_{\pm}
. Let us define the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\eta}(I):=\{\psi\,|\,\psi_{\pm}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{\pm}),\,\sin\theta\,\psi^{+}(0)=\cos\theta\,\psi^{-}(0)\mbox{ and }\psi^{-}(-1/2)=e^{i\eta}\psi^{+}(1/2)\}.

(49)

Classically, one shows that
(\nu,\gamma)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\eta}(I)\setminus\{0\}
is an eigenpair of (
48
) if and only if there holds

\int_{I_{-}\cup I_{+}}\partial_{x}\gamma\partial_{x}\overline{\psi}\,dx=\nu\,\int_{I_{-}\cup I_{+}}\gamma\overline{\psi}\,dx,\qquad\psi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\eta}(I).

(50)

Therefore, for all
\eta\in[0;2\pi)
, the spectrum of (

), (
46
), (
47
) is real, made of non negative eigenvalues

0\leq\nu_{1}(\eta)\leq\nu_{2}(\eta)\leq\dots.

Let us compute them explicitly. First, we consider the case where
\nu
is a non zero eigenvalue. Exploiting (

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\gamma^{+}(x)&=&\phantom{e^{i\eta}\big(}A\,e^{i\sqrt{\nu}(x-1/2)}+B\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}(x-1/2)}\\[3.0pt]
\gamma^{-}(x)&=&e^{i\eta}\big(A\,e^{i\sqrt{\nu}(x+1/2)}+B\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}(x+1/2)}\big).\end{array}

Writing the transmission conditions (

\nu\neq 0
is an eigenvalue if and only if the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sin\theta\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}/2}-\cos\theta\,e^{i\sqrt{\nu}/2}e^{i\eta}&\sin\theta\,e^{i\sqrt{\nu}/2}-\cos\theta\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}/2}e^{i\eta}\\[4.0pt]

has a non zero kernel. Computing its determinant, we obtain that this is equivalent to have

\sin(2\theta)\cos\eta=\cos\sqrt{\nu}.

(51)

A similar calculus shows that
\nu=0
is an eigenvalue if and only if there holds

\sin(2\theta)\cos\eta=1.

(52)

For

\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{p}(\eta)=\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+\nu_{m}(\eta)+\dots

(53)

This establishes the item

of (

Remark 5.3

Finally, we consider the hypothetical case where the geometry

Note that

6
A model problem for the breathing of spectral bands

In this section, our goal is to propose a model which describes the change of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
when perturbing the near field geometry around a particular
\Omega=\Omega_{\star}
where
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. Let us emphasize that this leads to make a periodic perturbation of
\Pi^{\varepsilon}
.

6.1
Setting and main result

Let us start from a geometry
\Omega_{\star}
such that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
(we will explain in Section
7
how to construct particular examples). To simplify the presentation, we assume that we also have
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{bo}}=\{0\}
. As in (
8
), we denote by
N_{\bullet}
the number of eigenvalues of
A^{\Omega_{\star}}
below the continuous spectrum. Now we perturb this waveguide. To proceed, consider
\Gamma
a smooth bounded and connected part of
\partial\Omega_{\star}
. Denote by
s
the arc length on
\Gamma
so that
\Gamma=\{P(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,s\in J\}
where
J
is a given interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
h\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{0}(J)
be a smooth profile function such that
h\geq 0
and
h\not\equiv 0
. For some given parameter
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
that we can choose as we wish, we define the geometry
\Omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}
such that outside of
\mathscr{V}
,
\partial\Omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega
and inside
\mathscr{V}
,
\partial\Omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}
coincides with

\Gamma^{\rho,\varepsilon}:=\{P(s)+\varepsilon\rho h(s)n(s)\,|\,s\in J\}.

(57)

Here
n(s)
is the outward unit normal vector to
\Gamma
at point
P(s)
. Note that here the inner field geometry also depends on
\varepsilon
. By (
1
), this defines a family of unit cells and periodic waveguides that we denote respectively
\omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}
and
\Pi^{\rho,\varepsilon}
to stress the dependence in
\rho
. Observe that with this notation, we have in particular
\omega^{0,\varepsilon}=\omega^{\varepsilon}
,
\Pi^{0,\varepsilon}=\Pi^{\varepsilon}
.
Our goal is to compute, for a fixed
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, the asymptotics of the spectral bands
\Upsilon^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{p}:=\{\Lambda_{p}^{\rho,\varepsilon}(\eta),\,\eta\in[0;2\pi)\}
of the Dirichlet Laplacian in
\Pi^{\rho,\varepsilon}
. The study of the first
N_{\bullet}
spectral bands has no particular interest compared to what has been done in Section
4
and so we focus our attention on the case
p>N_{\bullet}
. The next theorem constitutes the second important result of this article.

Theorem 6.1
.

Fix
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
and
p=N_{\bullet}+m
, let
\Upsilon^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{p}=[a^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{m-};a^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{m+}]
, with
a^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{m-}\leq a^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{m+}
, be the spectral band as defined above.
There are some (real) constants
c^{\rho}_{m-}<c^{\rho}_{m+}
,
C_{m}>0
,
\delta_{m}>0
and
\varepsilon_{m}>0
such that we have

\Big|a^{\rho,\varepsilon}_{m\pm}-\Big(\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+c^{\rho}_{m\pm}\Big)\Big|\leq C_{m}\,\varepsilon^{1+\delta_{m}},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{m}].

(58)

Here the constants
C_{m}
,
\varepsilon_{m}
can be chosen independently of
\rho
if we impose to
\rho
to belong to a compact set of
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we have

\lim_{\rho\to-\infty}c^{\rho}_{m\pm}=m^{2}\pi^{2},\qquad\qquad c^{\rho}_{1\pm}\underset{\rho\to+\infty}{\sim}-\cfrac{T^{2}}{4}\,\rho^{2},\qquad\qquad\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}c^{\rho}_{(m+1)\pm}=m^{2}\pi^{2}

(59)

with

c^{\rho}_{m+}-c^{\rho}_{m-}\underset{\rho\to-\infty}{=}O(1/\rho),\qquad c^{\rho}_{1+}-c^{\rho}_{1-}\underset{\rho\to+\infty}{=}O(e^{-\delta\rho}),\qquad c^{\rho}_{(m+1)+}-c^{\rho}_{(m+1)-}\underset{\rho\to+\infty}{=}O(1/\rho).

Here
T>0
which depends on
h
is defined in (
66
) and
\delta>0
is a constant.

Let us comment this statement. Note first that all the asymptotic intervals
[c^{\rho}_{m-};c^{\rho}_{m+}]
reduce to a singleton when
\pm\rho\to+\infty
. This describes the transition between cases
i)
and
ii)
in Theorem
3.5
. Some illustrations of the dependence of the
[c^{\rho}_{m-};c^{\rho}_{m+}]
, that we denote by
\Xi^{\rho}_{m}(\theta)
in the sequel, with respect to
\rho
are given in Figures
3
,
4
below. We can see that when
\rho
runs from
-\infty
to
+\infty
, which boils down to inflating the near field geometry around
\Omega_{\star}
, the
[c^{\rho}_{m-};c^{\rho}_{m+}]
expand and then shrink. This is what we call the breathing phenomenon of the spectrum of
A^{\varepsilon}
.
In the process, in
\sigma(A^{\varepsilon})
a band dives below
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
and then stops breathing. It is approximated when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
by the interval
[\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+c^{\rho}_{1-};\varepsilon^{-2}\pi^{2}+c^{\rho}_{1+}]
which becomes extremely short when
\rho\to+\infty
. We emphasize that in order these results to be valid for
\pm\rho
large,
\varepsilon
must be chosen small enough so that
\Omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}
remains a small perturbation of
\Omega_{\star}
. Typically, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, we can take
\rho\in(-\varepsilon^{-1/2};\varepsilon^{-1/2})
.

\hskip 19.91684pt\bm{\gamma}(0)=i(\mathrm{Id}-\mathbb{S}^{\rho,\varepsilon})\bm{a}

6.4
Behaviour of the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{S}
under small perturbation of the geometry

In this section, we show a result concerning the asymptotics of the eigenvalues of the threshold scattering matrix
\mathbb{S}
(see (
13
) for its definition) which has been used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
(for a close study, see also
[
43
]
). Let
\Omega
be a waveguide which coincides with the strip
\mathbb{R}\times(-1/2;1/2)
outside of a bounded region as described in Section
2
. Note that our result below is general and we do not assume here that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
. Consider
\tau
a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbb{S}
. We want to understand how this eigenvalue evolves when the geometry is slightly perturbed.

Define
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
from
\Omega
as
\Omega^{\rho,\varepsilon}
was constructed from
\Omega_{\star}
in (
77
) with
\rho=1
. In particular, outside of
\mathscr{V}
, we have
\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega
and inside
\mathscr{V}
,
\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}
coincides with

\Gamma^{\varepsilon}:=\{P(s)+\varepsilon h(s)n(s)\,|\,s\in J\}.

(77)

We denote by
\mathbb{S}^{\varepsilon}
the threshold scattering matrix in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
and by
\tau^{\varepsilon}
its eigenvalue corresponding to the perturbation of
\tau
.

Proposition 6.2
.

There are
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
C>0
such that we have

\Big|\tau^{\varepsilon}-\Big(\tau+\cfrac{i\tau\varepsilon}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h|\partial_{n}v|^{2}\,ds\Big)\Big|\leq C\,\varepsilon^{2},\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in(0;\varepsilon_{0}].

(78)

Here
v\not\equiv 0
on
\Gamma
is a function defined in (
88
).

Remark 6.3
.

This result also holds without sign assumption for
h
. However, we see that when we take
h\geq 0
with
h\not\equiv 0
, i.e. when the geometry inflates, the eigenvalues of the threshold scattering matrix rotates counter-clockwise on the unit circle. On the contrary, when
\Omega
deflates, the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{S}
rotate clockwise.

Proof.

To obtain an asymptotic expansion of
\tau^{\varepsilon}
when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we have to compute an expansion of the functions
v_{\pm}^{\varepsilon}
defined as
v_{\pm}
in (
12
) but in the geometry
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
. Let us focus our attention on
v_{+}^{\varepsilon}
. We consider the simplest ansatz

v_{+}^{\varepsilon}=v_{+}+\varepsilon v_{+}^{\prime}+\dots

(79)

where
v_{+}^{\prime}
is to determine and the dots stand for higher-order terms. Inserting (
79
) in the problem (
9
) posed in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
, taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and identifying the powers in
\varepsilon
, we find that
v_{+}
coincides with the function introduced in (
12
). Moreover, exploiting in particular the Taylor expansion, for
s\in J
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}0=v_{+}^{\varepsilon}(P(s)+\varepsilon h(s)n(s))&=&v_{+}^{\varepsilon}(P(s))+\varepsilon h(s)n(s)\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}_{+}(P(s))+O(\varepsilon^{2})\\[3.0pt]
&=&v_{+}(P(s))+\varepsilon\,(v^{\prime}_{+}(P(s))+h(s)n(s)\cdot\nabla v_{+}(P(s)))+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\end{array}

we obtain that
v^{\prime}_{+}
satisfies the problem

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}\Delta v^{\prime}_{+}+\pi^{2}v^{\prime}_{+}&=&0&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\[3.0pt]
v^{\prime}_{+}&=&0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma}\\[3.0pt]
v^{\prime}_{+}&=&-hn\cdot\nabla v_{+}&\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{array}

Note that above and below, we naturally define
h(z)
,
n(z)
for
z\in\Gamma
by taking the values of
h(s)
,
n(s)
for
s
such that
z=P(s)
. On the other hand, since the incident field in the problem defining
v_{+}^{\varepsilon}
is independent of
\varepsilon
, we find that
v^{\prime}_{+}
must admit the expansion

v^{\prime}_{+}=\psi_{-}s^{\prime}_{+-}w^{\mathrm{out}}+\psi_{+}s^{\prime}_{++}w^{\mathrm{out}}+\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{+}

(80)

where
s^{\prime}_{+\pm}
are complex numbers and
\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{+}
decays exponentially as
|x|\to+\infty
. Now we wish to obtain expressions for the
s^{\prime}_{+\pm}
. To proceed, we start from the identity

0=\int_{\Omega_{\kappa}}(\Delta v^{\prime}_{+}+\pi^{2}v^{\prime}_{+})v_{\pm}-v^{\prime}_{+}(\Delta v_{\pm}+\pi^{2}v_{\pm})\,dz

(81)

where for
\kappa>0
,
\Omega_{\kappa}:=\{z\in\Omega\,|\,|x|\leq\kappa\}
. Integrating by parts in (
81
), we obtain for large
\kappa

0=\int_{x=\kappa}\partial_{x}v^{\prime}_{+}v_{\pm}-v^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x}v_{\pm}\,dy-\int_{x=-\kappa}\partial_{x}v^{\prime}_{+}v_{\pm}-v^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x}v_{\pm}\,dy+\int_{\Gamma}h\partial_{n}v_{+}\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\,ds.

(82)

Taking the limit
\kappa\to+\infty
in (
82
) and working with the decompositions (
12
), (
80
), we find

0=2is^{\prime}_{+\pm}+\int_{\Gamma}h\partial_{n}v_{+}\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\,ds\qquad\mbox{ and so }\qquad s^{\prime}_{+\pm}=\cfrac{i}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h\partial_{n}v_{+}\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\,ds.

We get similar results when working with
v_{-}^{\varepsilon}
. Finally, this analysis shows that

\mathbb{S}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{S}+\varepsilon\mathbb{S}^{\prime}+O(\varepsilon^{2})

where
\mathbb{S}
is the threshold scattering matrix appearing in (
13
) and

\mathbb{S}^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}s^{\prime}_{++}&s^{\prime}_{+-}\\[3.0pt]
s^{\prime}_{-+}&s^{\prime}_{--}\\[3.0pt]
\end{array}\right)\qquad\mbox{ with }\qquad\begin{array}[]{|rcl}s^{\prime}_{\pm\pm}&=&\displaystyle\cfrac{i}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\,ds\\[6.0pt]
s^{\prime}_{\pm\mp}&=&\displaystyle\cfrac{i}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h\partial_{n}v_{\pm}\partial_{n}v_{\mp}\,ds.\end{array}

(83)

Now we come to the asymptotics of
\tau^{\varepsilon}
(which is by assumption a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbb{S}^{\varepsilon}
). Introduce
\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
a corresponding eigenvector. For
\tau^{\varepsilon}
,
\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}
, we work with the simplest ansatz

\tau^{\varepsilon}=\tau+\varepsilon\tau^{\prime}+\dots,\qquad\quad\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}=\bm{b}+\varepsilon\bm{b}^{\prime}+\dots,

(84)

where again the dots denote unessential higher order terms. Inserting the expansions (
84
) in the relation
\mathbb{S}^{\varepsilon}\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}=\tau^{\varepsilon}\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}
and identifying the powers in
\varepsilon
, we get

\mathbb{S}\bm{b}=\tau\bm{b},\qquad\qquad\mathbb{S}\bm{b}^{\prime}-\tau\bm{b}^{\prime}=-(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\bm{b}-\tau^{\prime}\bm{b}).

(85)

We deduce first that
(\tau,\bm{b})
is an eigenpair of
\mathbb{S}
. Besides, taking the inner product of the second equality of (
85
) with
\bm{b}
(which is real), we find

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\phantom{-}(\mathbb{S}\bm{b}^{\prime}-\tau\bm{b}^{\prime},\bm{b})=(\bm{b}^{\prime},\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\top}\bm{b}-\overline{\tau}\bm{b})=(\bm{b}^{\prime},\overline{\mathbb{S}}\bm{b}-\overline{\tau}\bm{b})=0\\[5.0pt]
=&-(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\bm{b}-\tau^{\prime}\bm{b},\bm{b}),\end{array}

which gives

\tau^{\prime}=\cfrac{(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\bm{b},\bm{b})}{(\bm{b},\bm{b})}\,.

(86)

Note that as expected,
\tau^{\prime}
is independent of the choice of the normalization made for
\bm{b}^{\varepsilon}
. Since
\tau
belongs to the unit circle in the complex plane, there is
\varsigma\in[0;2\pi)
such that
\tau=e^{i\varsigma}
. Introduce
b_{+}
,
b_{-}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\bm{b}=(b_{+},b_{-})^{\top}
. Additionally, we assume that we have
(\bm{b},\bm{b})=1
. Combining (
83
) and (
86
), we obtain

\tau^{\prime}=\cfrac{i}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h(\partial_{n}v)^{2}\,ds\qquad\mbox{ with }\qquad v:=b_{+}\,v_{+}+b_{-}\,v_{-}.

(87)

But the function
v
admits the expansion

\begin{array}[]{rcl}v&=&\psi_{-}(b_{-}w^{\mathrm{in}}+(b_{+}s_{+-}+b_{-}s_{--})w^{\mathrm{out}})+\psi_{+}(b_{+}w^{\mathrm{in}}+(b_{+}s_{++}+b_{-}s_{-+})w^{\mathrm{out}})+\tilde{v}\\[3.0pt]
&=&b_{-}\psi_{-}(w^{\mathrm{in}}+e^{i\varsigma}w^{\mathrm{out}})+b_{+}\psi_{+}(w^{\mathrm{in}}+e^{i\varsigma}w^{\mathrm{out}})+\tilde{v}\end{array}

(88)

with
\tilde{v}
which decays exponentially as
|x|\to+\infty
. From (
88
), we infer that
e^{-i\varsigma/2}v
is valued in
\mathbb{R}
because
e^{-i\varsigma/2}v-\overline{e^{-i\varsigma/2}v}
is exponentially decaying at infinity. Therefore we have

\tau^{\prime}=\cfrac{i\tau}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma}h|\partial_{n}v|^{2}\,ds.

On the other hand, the unique continuation principle ensures that
\partial_{n}v
can not vanish identically on
\Gamma
. With these two properties, together with (
87
), finally we find the desired result (
78
). Our approach above is rather formal but rigorous justification can be obtained classically by converting the perturbation of the geometry into a perturbation in an equation set in a fixed domain. For these techniques, we refer the reader for example to
[
9
,
18
]
and
[
19
, Chap. 7, §6.5]
.

In this section, we wish to come back to the possible situation where

Proposition 8.1
.

Assume that the domain
\Omega
is symmetric with respect to the
(Oy)
axis. Assume also that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{X}_{\dagger}=1
and
\theta=\pi/4
in (
44
). If additionally the constants

m_{\Omega}
,

introduced in (
100
), (
101
) are such that
M_{\Omega}+m_{\Omega}\neq 0
, then for
\varepsilon
small enough the operator
A^{\varepsilon}
has some gaps above
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
.

If
M_{\Omega}+m_{\Omega}=0
, we can not conclude from this study and it is necessary to compute higher order terms in the asymptotics. Roughly speaking, there is no gap above
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
in the spectrum of the exact operator
A^{\varepsilon}

if for any order of the model, the corresponding dispersion curves cross. This happens when
\Omega
is the straight strip (see the discussion of Remark
5.3
) but seems very rare.

Above we focused our attention on the case of classical Kirchhoff transmission conditions at the origin (
\theta=\pi/4
in (
48
)). The situation
\theta=3\pi/4
, which corresponds to anti-Kirchhoff transmission conditions, is also interesting. Then (

) writes

\begin{array}[]{|rclrcl}\partial^{2}_{x}\gamma^{\pm}+\nu\gamma^{\pm}&=&0\quad\mbox{ in }I_{\pm}\\[3.0pt]
\gamma^{-}(-1/2)&=&e^{i\eta}\gamma^{+}(+1/2)&\gamma^{+}(0)&=&-\gamma^{-}(0)\\[3.0pt]
\partial_{x}\gamma^{-}(-1/2)&=&e^{i\eta}\partial_{x}\gamma^{+}(+1/2)&\qquad\partial_{x}\gamma^{+}(0)&=&-\partial_{x}\gamma^{-}(0).\end{array}

Solving this problem, we get
\nu=(\eta+\pi(2k+1))^{2}
for some

with
\gamma_{\pm}(x)=\pm e^{i(-\eta-\pi(2k+1))x}
. As a consequence, the spectral bands of the model of order
\varepsilon^{0}
cover the interval
[\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2};+\infty)
(after the usual shift by
\pi^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
). However again one can show that in general there are some gaps in the spectrum of the exact operator
A^{\varepsilon}
. To proceed, one works as above when
\theta=\pi/4
. The first step consists in showing the existence of functions
V_{0}
,
V_{1}
solving (

\tilde{V}_{0}

V_{2}

\begin{array}[]{|rcll}-\Delta V_{2}-\pi^{2}V_{2}&=&V_{0}&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\[3.0pt]

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a ring. A map
f:R\rightarrow R
is said to be additive if

\displaystyle f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)

for all
a,b\in R
. In 1957, Herstein
[
5
]
introduced Jordan derivation over rings as an additive map
\tau:R\rightarrow R
satisfies

\displaystyle\tau(a^{2})=\tau(a)a+a\tau(a)

for all
a\in R
. He also proved that any Jordan derivation over a prime ring is a derivation with some torsion restrictions. In 2003, Jing and Lu
[
6
]
introduced generalized Jordan derivation and proved that every generalized Jordan derivation is a generalized derivation over a
2
-torsion-free prime ring. Recall that an additive map
\delta:R\rightarrow R
is said to be a generalized Jordan derivation if

\displaystyle\delta(a^{2})=\delta(a)a+a\tau(a)

for all
a\in R
and for some Jordan derivation
\tau:R\rightarrow R
. In 1952, Wendel
[
13
]
introduced the concept of a centralizer. An additive map
T:R\rightarrow R
is said to be a left centralizer if

\displaystyle T(ab)=T(a)b

for all
a,b\in R
. Similarly, we define the right centralizer over a ring. An additive map
T:R\rightarrow R
is said to be a two-sided centralizer if
T
is both left and right centralizer.
In 2014, Ali and Fošner
[
1
]
introduced the concept of
(m,n)
-derivation. Let
m,n\geq 0
be integers. Then an additive map
D:R\rightarrow R
is said to be an
(m,n)
-derivation if

\displaystyle(m+n)D(ab)=2mD(a)b+2naD(b),

for all
a,b\in R
. In fact, for
m=1
and
n=1
,
D
is a normal derivation on a
2
-torsion free ring
R
.

Let
n\geq 2
be any integer. Then a ring
R
is said to be
n
-torsion free if
na=0
for some
a\in R
implies
a=0
. It is an interesting question that when a map over a ring (satisfying some functional equations) is additive. The question was first raised by Rickart
[
9
]
in 1948. He showed that any bijective and multiplicative mapping of a Boolean ring
B
onto any arbitrary ring
S
is additive. Later, Johnson made an extensive study of these results in
[
7
]
. In 1969, Martindale III
[
8
]
proved that any multiplicative isomorphism of a ring
R
onto an arbitrary ring
S
is additive under the existence of a family of idempotent elements in
R
satisfying certain conditions. In 1991, by assuming Martindale’s conditions, Daif
[
2
]
proved that any multiplicative derivation of a ring
R
is additive.
Later, in 2009, Wang
[
11
]
proved that any
n
-multiplicative derivation
d
of
R
is additive by using Martindale’s conditions where
n>1
is an integer. He proved that any
n
-multiplicative isomorphism or
n
-multiplicative derivation of a standard operator algebra
A
over a Banach space
X
with
dim(X)\geq 2
, is additive.

Again, in 2011, Wang
[
12
]
proved that any
n
-multiplicative derivation
d
of a triangular ring
\mathcal{T}
is additive, with some assumptions on
\mathcal{T}
. In 2014, Ferreira
[
3
]
proved that every
m
-multiplicative isomorphism from a
n
-triangular matrix ring
\mathcal{T}
onto any ring
\mathcal{S}
is additive with some assumptions on
\mathcal{T}
where
n,m>1
are integers. He also showed that every
m
-multiplicative derivation of
\mathcal{T}
is additive. In 2015, Ferreira
[
4
]
again revisit and proved that a map
\tau
on a triangular ring
\mathcal{T}
satisfying

\displaystyle\tau(ab+ba)=\tau(a)b+a\tau(b)+\tau(b)a+b\tau(a)

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
, is additive with some assumptions on
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{T}
is
2
-torsion free, then
\tau
is a Jordan derivation. Ferreira’s result
[
4
]
motivated us to work to find out the conditions under which a multiplicative generalized Jordan derivation is additive on a triangular matrix ring. This paper provides an affirmative answer to the above question.

In 2014, El-Sayed et al.
[
10
]
proved that every map
T
on a prime ring with a non-trivial idempotent satisfying

(1.1)

T(ab)=T(a)b

for all
a,b\in R
, is additive. Note that a ring
R
is said to be a prime ring if
aRb=0
for some
a,b\in R
implies either
a=0
or
b=0
and is said to be a semi-prime ring if
aRa=0
for some
a\in R
implies that
a=0
. Thus, every prime ring is semi-prime, but the converse is not true.
Motivated by the work of El-Sayed et al.
[
10
]
, we prove that a map
T
on
\mathcal{T}
satisfying

\displaystyle T(ab)=T(a)b=aT(b),

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
, is additive.

Further, we prove that any map
D
on
\mathcal{T}
which satisfies

\displaystyle(m+n)D(ab)=2mD(a)b+2naD(b),

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
and for some integers
m,n\geq 1
, is additive.

Throughout the work, we use the definition of triangular algebra given by Wang
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be two rings, and
M
be an
(A,B)
-bimodule such that

(a)
M
is faithful as left
A
-module and right
B
-module;

(b) If
m\in M
is such that
AmB=0
implies
m=0
.

Then the ring

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=\text{Tri}(A,M,B)=\Biggl\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&m\\
&b\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0pta\in A,\,b\in B,\,m\in M\Biggl\},

under usual matrix addition and multiplication is said to be a triangular ring.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{11}=\Biggl\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&0\\
&0\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0pta\in A\Biggl\}
,
\mathcal{T}_{12}=\Biggl\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&m\\
&0\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0ptm\in M\Biggl\}
and

\mathcal{T}_{22}=\Biggl\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0\\
&b\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0ptb\in B\Biggl\}
.

Then
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{11}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{12}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Henceforth,
t_{ij}\in\mathcal{T}_{ij}
. Also,
t_{ij}t_{kl}=0
, if
j\neq k
where
j,~k\in\{1,2\}
.

2.
GENERALIZED JORDAN DERIVATIONS ON TRIANGULAR RINGS

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangular ring with
conditions:

(i)
aA=0
for some
a\in A
implies
a=0
;

(ii)
bB=0
for some
b\in B
implies
b=0
;

(iii)
A
and
B
are rings with identity.

If a function
\delta:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle\delta(ab+ba)=\delta(a)b+a\tau(b)+\delta(b)a+b\tau(a),

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
where
\tau:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle\tau(ab+ba)=\tau(a)b+a\tau(b)+\tau(b)a+b\tau(a),

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
, then
\delta
is additive. Moreover, if
\mathcal{T}
is
2
-torsion free, then
\delta
is a generalized Jordan derivation.

Since
\tau:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
satisfies all the conditions in Theorem
3.1
of
[
4
]
,
\tau
is additive.
In this section, we frequently use all three conditions for the triangular ring
\mathcal{T}
, the conditions on the maps
\delta
and
\tau
described in Theorem
2.1
, and the additivity of
\tau
without mentioning them. Note that
\delta(0)=0
. Before proving Theorem
2.1
, we first discuss several needful lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
,
b\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
and
m\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle(i)~~\delta(a+m)=\delta(a)+\delta(m),

\displaystyle(ii)~~\delta(b+m)=\delta(b)+\delta(m).

Proof.

Let
t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\delta[(a+m)t_{2}+t_{2}(a+m)]

\displaystyle=\delta(a+m)t_{2}+(a+m)\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})(a+m)+t_{2}\tau(a+m).

On the other hand,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\delta[(a+m)t_{2}+t_{2}(a+m)]

\displaystyle=\delta(mt_{2})=\delta(0)+\delta(mt_{2}+0)

\displaystyle=\delta(at_{2}+t_{2}a)+\delta(mt_{2}+t_{2}m)

\displaystyle=\delta(a)t_{2}+a\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})a+t_{2}\tau(a)

\displaystyle+\delta(m)t_{2}+m\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})m+t_{2}\tau(m).

Since
\tau
is additive, comparing (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{12}t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~~[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{22}t_{2}=0.

Using conditions (b) and (ii) of Definition
1.1
and Theorem
2.1
, respectively, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{12}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~~[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{22}=0.

Now, in order to prove
[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{11}=0
, let
n\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.6)

\displaystyle\delta[(a+m)n+n(a+m)]=\delta(an)

\displaystyle=\delta(an+na)+\delta(mn+nm)

\displaystyle=\delta(a)n+a\tau(n)+\delta(n)a+n\tau(a)+\delta(m)n+m\tau(n)+\delta(n)m+n\tau(m).

Also, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\delta[(a+m)n+n(a+m)]

\displaystyle=\delta(a+m)n+(a+m)\tau(n)+\delta(n)(a+m)+n\tau(a+m)

\displaystyle=\delta(a+m)n+(a+m)\tau(n)+\delta(n)(a+m)+n(\tau(a)+\tau(m))~(\text{By additivity of}~\tau).

Comparing the equalities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
),

(2.8)

\displaystyle[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{11}n=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)]_{11}=0~(\text{by condition (a) of Definition \ref{def1}}).

Hence,
\delta(a+m)=\delta(a)+\delta(m)
. Similarly, we can prove that
\delta(b+m)=\delta(b)+\delta(m)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
,
b\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
and
m,n\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.9)

\delta(am+nb)=\delta(am)+\delta(nb).

Proof.

Since
am+nb=(a+n)(m+b)+(m+b)(a+n)
, by Lemma
2.2
and the additivity of
\tau
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\delta(am+nb)

\displaystyle=\delta[(a+n)(m+b)+(m+b)(a+n)]

\displaystyle=\delta(a+n)(m+b)+(a+n)\tau(m+b)

\displaystyle+\delta(m+b)(a+n)+(m+b)\tau(a+n)

\displaystyle=(\delta(a)+\delta(n))(m+b)+(a+n)(\tau(m)+\tau(b))

\displaystyle+(\delta(m)+\delta(b))(a+n)+(m+b)(\tau(a)+\tau(n))

\displaystyle=\delta(am+ma)+\delta(nb+bn)+\delta(mn+nm)+\delta(ab+ba)

\displaystyle=\delta(am)+\delta(nb).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m,n\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.11)

\delta(m+n)=\delta(m)+\delta(n).

Proof.

Since we assume both
A
and
B
are rings with identity in Theorem
2.1
, putting
a=1
and
b=1
in Lemma
2.3
, we have the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
and
b_{1},b_{2},\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Then

(2.12)

\displaystyle(i)~\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})=\delta(a_{1})+\delta(a_{2}),

\displaystyle(ii)~\delta(b_{1}+b_{2})=\delta(b_{1})+\delta(b_{2}).

Proof.

Let
t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Then

(2.13)

\displaystyle\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})t_{2}+(a_{1}+a_{2})\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})(a_{1}+a_{2})+t_{2}\tau(a_{1}+a_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta[(a_{1}+a_{2})t_{2}+t_{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})]=\delta(0)=0

\displaystyle=\delta(a_{1}t_{2}+t_{2}a_{1})+\delta(a_{2}t_{2}+t_{2}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta(a_{1})t_{2}+a_{1}\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})a_{1}+t_{2}\tau(a_{1})

\displaystyle+\delta(a_{2})t_{2}+a_{2}\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})a_{2}+t_{2}\tau(a_{2}).

Since
\tau
is additive, from (
2.13
), we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{12}t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~~[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{22}t_{2}=0.

Using conditions
(b)
and
(ii)
of Definition
1.1
and Theorem
2.1
, respectively, we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{12}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~~[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{22}=0.

Now, to prove
[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{11}=0
, let
m\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.16)

\displaystyle\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})m+(a_{1}+a_{2})\tau(m)+\delta(m)(a_{1}+a_{2})+m\tau(a_{1}+a_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta[(a_{1}+a_{2})m+m(a_{1}+a_{2})]

\displaystyle=\delta[(a_{1}m+ma_{1})+(a_{2}m+ma_{2})]

\displaystyle=\delta(a_{1}m+ma_{1})+\delta(a_{2}m+ma_{2})~(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem3}})

\displaystyle=\delta(a_{1})m+a_{1}\tau(m)+\delta(m)a_{1}+m\tau(a_{1})+\delta(a_{2})m+a_{2}\tau(m)+\delta(m)a_{2}+m\tau(a_{2}).

By (
2.16
) and the additivity of
\tau
, we see that

(2.17)

\displaystyle[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]m=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{11}m=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})-\delta(a_{1})-\delta(a_{2})]_{11}=0~(\text{by condition (a) of Definition \ref{def1}}).

Hence,
\delta(a_{1}+a_{2})=\delta(a_{1})+\delta(a_{2})
. In a similar way, we can prove that
\delta(b_{1}+b_{2})=\delta(b_{1})+\delta(b_{2}).

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
,
b\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
and
m\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(2.18)

\delta(a+m+b)=\delta(a)+\delta(m)+\delta(b).

Proof.

Let
t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Then

(2.19)

\displaystyle\delta(a+m+b)t_{2}+(a+m+b)\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})(a+m+b)+t_{2}\tau(a+m+b)

\displaystyle=\delta[(a+m+b)t_{2}+t_{2}(a+m+b)]

\displaystyle=\delta(mt_{2}+bt_{2}+t_{2}b)

\displaystyle=\delta(bt_{2}+t_{2}b)+\delta(mt_{2})~(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem1}})

\displaystyle=\delta(bt_{2}+t_{2}b)+\delta(t_{2}m+mt_{2})+\delta(at_{2}+t_{2}a)

\displaystyle=\delta(b)t_{2}+b\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})b+t_{2}\tau(b)+\delta(t_{2})m+t_{2}\tau(m)

\displaystyle+\delta(m)t_{2}+m\tau(t_{2})+\delta(a)t_{2}+a\tau(t_{2})+\delta(t_{2})a+t_{2}\tau(a)

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]t_{2}=0~(\text{Using additivity of}~\tau)

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{12}t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{22}t_{2}=0.

Using conditions
(b)
and
(ii)
of Definition
1.1
and Theorem
2.1
, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{12}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{22}=0.

Let
t_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
. Then

(2.21)

\displaystyle\delta(a+m+b)t_{1}+(a+m+b)\tau(t_{1})+\delta(t_{1})(a+m+b)+t_{1}\tau(a+m+b)

\displaystyle=\delta[(a+m+b)t_{1}+t_{1}(a+m+b)]

\displaystyle=\delta(at_{1}+t_{1}a+t_{1}m)

\displaystyle=\delta(at_{1}+t_{1}a)+\delta(t_{1}m)~(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem1}})

\displaystyle=\delta(at_{1}+t_{1}a)+\delta(t_{1}m+mt_{1})+\delta(bt_{1}+t_{1}b)

\displaystyle=\delta(a)t_{1}+a\tau(t_{1})+\delta(t_{1})a+t_{1}\tau(a)+\delta(t_{1})m+t_{1}\tau(m)

\displaystyle+\delta(m)t_{1}+m\tau(t_{1})+\delta(b)t_{1}+b\tau(t_{1})+\delta(t_{1})b+t_{1}\tau(b)

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]t_{1}=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{11}t_{1}=0

\displaystyle\implies[\delta(a+m+b)-\delta(a)-\delta(m)-\delta(b)]_{11}=0

\displaystyle(\text{By condition (i) of Theorem \ref{der1}}).

Hence, by (
2.20
) and (
2.21
),
\delta(a+m+b)=\delta(a)+\delta(m)+\delta(b)
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{T}
and
u\in\mathcal{T}
. Then
t=t_{11}+t_{12}+t_{22}
and
u=u_{11}+u_{12}+u_{22}
where
t_{ij},u_{ij}\in\mathcal{T}_{ij}
and
i,~j\in\{1,2\}
. Now,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\delta(t+u)

\displaystyle=\delta((t_{11}+t_{12}+t_{22})+(u_{11}+u_{12}+u_{22}))

\displaystyle=\delta((t_{11}+u_{11})+(t_{12}++u_{12})+(t_{22}+u_{22}))

\displaystyle=\delta(t_{11}+u_{11})+\delta(t_{12}+u_{12})+\delta(t_{22}+u_{22})~(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem5}})

\displaystyle=\delta(t_{11})+\delta(u_{11})+\delta(t_{12})+\delta(u_{12})+\delta(t_{22})+\delta(u_{22})

\displaystyle(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem3} and \ref{lem4}})

\displaystyle=\delta(t_{11})+\delta(t_{12})+\delta(t_{22})+\delta(u_{11})+\delta(u_{12})+\delta(u_{22})

\displaystyle=\delta(t_{11}+t_{12}+t_{22})+\delta(u_{11}+u_{12}+u_{22})~(\text{By Lemma \ref{lem5}})

\displaystyle=\delta(t)+\delta(u).

Therefore,
\delta
is additive.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be
2
-torsion free. By Theorem
3.1
of
[
4
]
,
\tau
is a Jordan derivation. For any
t\in\mathcal{T}
,

(2.23)

\displaystyle 2\delta(t^{2})=\delta(2t^{2})=\delta(tt+tt)=2[\delta(t)t+t\tau(t)]

\displaystyle\implies\delta(t^{2})=\delta(t)t+t\tau(t).

Thus,
\delta
is a generalized Jordan derivation.
∎

The following example shows that dropping condition
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1

does not make any difference in the conclusion of this theorem.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\text{Tri}(2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z},3\mathbb{Z})=\Biggl\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&m\\
&b\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0pta\in 2\mathbb{Z},\,b\in 3\mathbb{Z},\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\Biggl\}
where
2\mathbb{Z}
is the ring of even integers,
3\mathbb{Z}
is the ring of integers multiplied by
3
and
\mathbb{Z}
is the
(2\mathbb{Z},3\mathbb{Z})
-bimodule of integers. Note that
2\mathbb{Z}
and
3\mathbb{Z}
are rings without identity. Define
\tau:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
as

\displaystyle\tau\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&m\\
&b\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\alpha a+\beta m-\alpha b\\
&0\end{array}\right),

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then it is easy to check that
\tau
satisfies all the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
of
[
4
]
. Hence,
\tau
is additive and also a Jordan derivation. Define
\delta:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
as

\displaystyle\delta\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&m\\
&b\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a\gamma&\alpha a+(\beta+\gamma)m+\mu b\\
&wb\end{array}\right),

where
\gamma,\mu
and

3.
TWO-SIDED CENTRALIZERS ON TRIANGULAR RINGS

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangular ring with
conditions:

(i)
Aa=0
for some
a\in A
implies
a=0
,

(ii)
bB=0
for some
b\in B
implies
b=0
,

(iii)
aA=0
for some
a\in A
implies
a=0
.

If a map
T:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle T(ab)=T(a)b=aT(b),

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
, then
T
is additive. Moreover,
T
is a two-sided centralizer.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}_{11}
,
b\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
and
m\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle(i)~T(a+m)=T(a)+T(m),

\displaystyle(ii)~T(b+m)=T(b)+T(m).

Proof.

Let
t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
. Now, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle T[(a+m)t_{2}]=T(a+m)t_{2}.

Also,

(3.3)

\displaystyle T[(a+m)t_{2}]=T(mt_{2})

\displaystyle=0+T(mt_{2})

\displaystyle=T(at_{2})+T(mt_{2})

\displaystyle=T(a)t_{2}+T(m)t_{2}.

Comparing identities (
3.2
) and (
3.3
),

(3.4)

\displaystyle[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\implies[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{12}t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\text{and}~[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{22}t_{2}=0

\displaystyle\implies[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{12}=0~(\text{By condition (b) of Definition \ref{def1}})

\displaystyle\text{and}~[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{22}=0~(\text{By condition (ii) of Theorem \ref{thm3.1}}).

Let
n\in\mathcal{T}_{12}
. We have

(3.5)

\displaystyle T[(a+m)n]=T(a+m)n.

Also,

(3.6)

\displaystyle T[(a+m)n]=T(an)+0=T(an)+T(mn)=T(a)n+T(m)n.

Comparing (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]n=0

\displaystyle\implies[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{11}n=0

\displaystyle\implies[T(a+m)-T(a)-T(m)]_{11}=0~(\text{By Condition (a) of Definition \ref{def1}}).

Hence, by using (
3.4
) and (
3.7
), we get
(i)
of Lemma
3.2
. Similarly, we have
(ii)
of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}_{11},b\in\mathcal{T}_{22}
and

. Then

\displaystyle=(a+n)T(m)+(a+n)T(b)

\displaystyle=(a+n)(T(m)+T(b)).

Comparing (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we have the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

(3.11)

(3.13)

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be integers and
\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle 2m[D(a+p)-D(a)-D(p)]c=0

\displaystyle(m+n)D((a+p)a_{1})

Comparing (

By (

) and (
4.7

(i)
of Lemma
4.2
. Similarly, we can prove
(ii)
of Lemma
4.2

Lemma 4.3
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Some remarks about the Link between the Fisher information and Landau or Landau-Fermi-Dirac entropy dissipation
L. Desvillettes

Abstract
. We present in this work variants of existing estimates for the Landau or Landau-Fermi-Dirac entropy dissipation, in terms of Fisher information, in the hard potential case. The specificity of those variants is that the entropy is never used in the estimates (in order to control possible concentrations on a zero measure set). The proofs are significantly simplified with respect to previous papers on the subject



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Description of the Landau operator and its entropy dissipation

We write down the Landau operator appearing in kinetic theory (cf.
[
9
,
21
]
),
defined by

(1)

Q_{\gamma}(f,f)(v)=\nabla_{v}\cdot\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\,\,|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\,\Pi(v-w)\,\bigg(f(w)\,\nabla{f}(v)-f(v)\,\nabla{f}(w)\bigg)\,dw\right\},

where

(2)

\Pi_{ij}(z)=\delta_{ij}-\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{2}}

is the
i,j
-component of the orthogonal projection
\Pi

onto
z^{\bot}:=\{y\,/\;y\cdot z=0\}.

In this paper, we focus on the case of so-called
Maxwell molecules, hard spheres or hard potentials, that is

(3)

\gamma\in[0,1].

The Landau operator satisfies (at the formal level) the conservation of
mass, momentum and kinetic energy, that is, for any
f:=f(v)\geq 0
:

(4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\gamma}(f,f)(v)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v_{i}\\
|v|^{2}/2\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right).

We also have the following property of nonnegativity of the so-called entropy dissipation associated to the Landau operator,
namely (at the formal level), for any
f:=f(v)>0
,

(5)

D_{\gamma}(f):=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\gamma}(f,f)(v)\ln f(v)\,dv

=\frac{1}{2}\,\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\,\Pi(v-w)\,\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}(v)-\frac{\nabla f}{f}(w)\right)

\cdot\,\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}(v)-\frac{\nabla f}{f}(w)\right)\,dvdw\geq 0.

In the last years, several works (cf.
[
18
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
) have been devoted to the proof of estimates from below of the quantity
D_{\gamma}(f)
in terms of
quantities like the Fisher information
F(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\nabla f(v)|^{2}}{f(v)}\,dv
, or the relative (with respect to a Maxwellian function
M:=M(v)
) Fisher information
F_{rel}(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f}{f}(v)-\frac{\nabla M}{M}(v)\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv
.

Those estimates (typically the ones related to
F(f)
) yield
results of regularity for the solutions to the Landau equation (cf.
[
11
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
15
]
). When they are complemented with the logarithmic Sobolev inequality of Gross (cf.
[
20
]
), they also
(typically the ones related to
F_{rel}(f)
) yield results for the large time behavior of the Landau equation (cf.
[
18
]
,
[
8
]
), following
the lines of the entropy-entropy dissipation method introduced in kinetic theory in the 90s by
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
23
]
(cf.
[
16
]
for more historical details and an explanation of the general context).

We write down a typical estimate, taken from Corollary 2.5 of
[
12
]
, which holds when
\gamma
satisfies (
3
), and
f>0
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
3\end{array}\right)

(and when the terms appearing in the inequality are defined):

(6)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\nabla f(v)|^{2}}{f(v)}\,dv\leq 3072\,\Delta(f)^{-2}\,\bigg\{8448+48\,\sqrt{1+\pi}\,D_{\gamma}(f)\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\bigg\},

where

(7)

\Delta(f):={\hbox{ Det}}\,\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,(1+|w|^{2})^{-1/2}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&w_{i}&w_{j}\\
w_{i}&w_{i}^{2}&w_{i}\,w_{j}\\
w_{j}&w_{i}\,w_{j}&w_{j}^{2}\end{array}\,\right]\,dw\,\bigg).

The purpose of this paper is to show that it is possible to obtain estimates like (
6
) with the following improvements:

•

No terms involving a determinant like (
7
) appears;

•

The numerical constants are much smaller than the ones appearing in (
6
);

•

The proof is short;

•

These new estimates extend somewhat to the case of the Landau-Fermi-Dirac entropy dissipation.

The price to pay in order to get those improvements is the following restriction: the estimates that we will write down hold only when
D_{\gamma}(f)
is not too large. We will see however that this is not a problem when one wishes to prove the exponential decay towards equilibrium with explicit rate
for the solutions to the Landau equation with hard potentials or hard spheres.

We notice that the statement and computations presented below have the same flavour as the material presented in chapter 5 of
[
18
]
(more generally, the methodology for getting estimates relating
D_{\gamma}
and
F
or
F_{rel}
was introduced in
[
18
]
, recalling some of the computations of
[
10
]
). The conditions on the cross section are however more general in the present paper, and the proofs are simplified a lot with respect to what is presented in
[
18
]
.

Our main result writes:

Theorem 1
.

We consider
\gamma
satisfying (
3
). Then
for all
f:=f(v)>0
(sufficiently smooth for the terms in the estimate to make sense) satisfying

(8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
3\end{array}\right),

and
such that

\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)\,D_{\gamma}(f)\leq\frac{27}{32},

the following estimate holds:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)}+v\,\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq\bigg[\frac{64}{9}\,\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)

(9)

+\,48+32\sqrt{\pi}\,||\,|\cdot|^{2}\,f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\bigg]\,D_{\gamma}(f).

Remark 1
.

Inequality (
9
) can be further simplified, and be transformed for example in

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)}+v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq 200\,||f||_{L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,D_{\gamma}(f),

as soon as

||f||_{L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,D_{\gamma}(f)\leq 0.062,

where we denote from now on (for any
p\in[1,\infty[
and
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
)

||f||_{L^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{p}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|v|^{2})^{pq/2}\,|f(v)|^{p}\,dv.

Indeed, one can check thanks to Hölder’s inequality that (when
f
satisfies (
8
))

\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)\leq\left(2\pi\,\sqrt{\pi}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\right)\,||f||_{L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}.

We see at this point that the numerical constant (that is, 200) is indeed much smaller than the ones appearing in inequality (
6
).

Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
. Then, we show in section
3
how this result can be used to obtain in a quick way the explicit exponential decay towards equilibrium for the solutions to the Landau equation with hard potentials or hard spheres. Finally, we show in section
4
that Theorem
1
can be extended somewhat in the case of the Landau-Fermi-Dirac equation.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

We start here the

Proof of Theorem
1
:
We denote in this proof, for
i=1,2,3
,

(10)

I_{i}(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,v_{i}^{2}\,dv,

so that
I_{1}(f)+I_{2}(f)+I_{3}(f)=3
.

Up to the use of a linear isometry of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we assume like in
[
18
]
without loss of generality that, for
i,j=1,2,3
such that
i\neq j
,

(11)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,v_{i}\,v_{j}\,dv=0.

Note that the Landau operator, like the Boltzmann operator (from which it is a singular limit: the so called grazing collisions limit) is indeed built to be Galilean-invariant.

We first observe that

D_{\gamma}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,[q_{i,i+1}^{f}(v,w)]^{2}\,dvdw,

where for
i,j=1,2,3
,

(12)

q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w):=(v_{i}-w_{i})\,\left(\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)-\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(w)\right)-(v_{j}-w_{j})\,\left(\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f}(v)-\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f}(w)\right),

and by definition
q_{i,4}^{f}:=q_{i,1}^{f}
.

Using the normalizations (
8
) and (
11
), we multiply (
12
) by
f(w)
and
w_{i}\,f(w)
and integrate on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in the
w
-variable.
We get for
i\neq j
the identities:

(13)

v_{i}\,\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)-v_{j}\,\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f}(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw,

(14)

I_{i}(f)\,\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)+v_{j}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}w_{i}\,f(w)\,q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw.

It is convenient to write (
14
) under the form

(15)

I_{i}(f)\,\left(\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)+v_{j}\right)=(I_{i}(f)-1)\,v_{j}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}w_{i}\,f(w)\,q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw.

As a consequence, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

I_{i}(f)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)+v_{j}\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq 2\,(I_{i}(f)-1)^{2}\,I_{j}(f)

+\,2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}w_{i}\,f(w)\,q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw\bigg|^{2}\,dv

\leq 2\,(I_{i}(f)-1)^{2}\,I_{j}(f)+\,2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dw\bigg]

\times\,\,\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,w_{i}^{2}\,dw\bigg]\,dv

(16)

\leq 2\,(I_{i}(f)-1)^{2}\,I_{j}(f)+\,2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dw\bigg]\,dv\,\sigma_{\gamma}(f),

where

(17)

\sigma_{\gamma}(f):=\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,|w|^{2}\,dw.

Multiplying identity (
13
) by
v_{i}\,v_{j}\,f(v)
and integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in the
v
-variable,
we see that

I_{i}(f)-I_{j}(f)=\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,v_{i}\,v_{j}\,q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dvdw

so that, using again the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

|I_{j}(f)-I_{i}(f)|\leq\bigg[\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dvdw\bigg]^{1/2}

\times\,\bigg[\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,v_{i}^{2}\,v_{j}^{2}\,dvdw\bigg]^{1/2}

\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,D_{\gamma}(f)^{1/2}\,s_{\gamma}(f)^{1/2},

where

(18)

s_{\gamma}(f):=\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,|v|^{4}\,dvdw.

As a consequence,

|I_{i}(f)-1|^{2}=\left(\frac{2}{3}I_{i}(f)-\frac{1}{3}I_{i+1}(f)-\frac{1}{3}I_{i+2}(f)\right)^{2}

\leq\frac{2}{9}(I_{i}(f)-I_{i+1}(f))^{2}+\frac{2}{9}(I_{i}(f)-I_{i+2}(f))^{2}\leq\frac{8}{27}\,D_{\gamma}(f)\,s_{\gamma}(f).

We deduce from this estimate that

I_{i}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}
as soon as

(19)

D_{\gamma}(f)\,s_{\gamma}(f)\leq\frac{27}{32},

since (
19
) implies that
|I_{i}(f)-1|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Under condition (
19
), we see therefore that (
16
) leads to

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f}(v)+v_{j}\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq 8\,(I_{i}(f)-1)^{2}\,I_{j}(f)

+\,8\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|q_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dw\bigg]\,\sigma_{\gamma}(f),

so that finally, summing from
j=1
to
3
(and choosing
i=j+1
)

(20)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f}{f}(v)+v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq\left(\frac{64}{9}\,s_{\gamma}(f)+16\,\sigma_{\gamma}(f)\right)\,D_{\gamma}(f).

Then we observe that

s_{\gamma}(f)\leq\int\int_{|v-w|\geq 1}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,f(w)\,dvdw+\int\int_{|v-w|\leq 1}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,dvdw

\leq\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{|v-w|\leq 1}f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,dw\right)

(21)

\leq\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right),

and that

\sigma_{\gamma}(f)\leq\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{|v-w|\geq 1}|w|^{2}\,f(w)\,dw+\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{|v-w|\leq 1}|w|^{2}\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,f(w)\,dw

(22)

\leq 3+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||\,|\cdot|^{2}\,f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

We conclude the proof of Theorem
1
by using estimates (
21
) and (
22
) in estimate (
20
).

\square

3.
Application to the large time behavior of the Landau equation

We show in this section that Theorem
1
quickly leads
to a result of exponential convergence towards equilibrium with explicit rate, for
the Landau equation in the case of hard potentials or hard spheres.

Note that in
[
18
]
, such a result is proven in the so-called overMaxwelian case, which includes the case of
Maxwell molecules, but not the case of hard potentials or hard spheres, because in those cases, the cross section cancels at some point. Those cases are treated as a special case (when the quantum parameter
\varepsilon
tends to
0
) of the results obtained in
[
2
]
for the Landau-Fermi-Dirac equation. What we now present is a new (somewhat simpler) proof.

We start with a simple Lemma which replaces here Proposition 6 and its consequences in
[
18
]
:

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
H\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and
D\in C({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
are such that

-H^{\prime}=D.

We also assume that for some real numbers
q,c_{0}>0

(23)

D\leq q\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad D\geq c_{0}\,H.

Then, for all
t\geq H(0)/q
, the following estimate holds:

(24)

H(t)\leq H(0)\,\exp(c_{0}\,H(0)/q)\,e^{-c_{0}\,t}.

In the sequel,
H
and
D
will be the respective relative entropy and entropy dissipation of the Landau
and the Landau-Fermi-Dirac equations.

Proof of Lemma
1
:
We first observe that if for some
{\bar{t}}\geq 0
, one has
H(\bar{t})\leq q/c_{0}
, then for all
t\geq{\bar{t}}
,

(25)

H(t)\leq q/c_{0}

since
H
is nonincreasing.

Then either
D(t)\leq q
and thanks to (
23
),
D(t)\geq c_{0}\,H(t)
; or
D(t)\geq q\geq c_{0}\,H(t)
thanks to (
25
).

In both cases,
-H^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{0}\,H(t)
for
t\geq\bar{t}
.
Consequently,

(26)

\forall t\geq\bar{t},\qquad H(t)\leq H(\bar{t})\,e^{-c_{0}\,(t-\bar{t})}.

We apply estimate (
26
) for

\bar{t}:=\inf\{\tau\geq 0,H(\tau)\leq q/c_{0}\}.

We see that
H(s)\geq q/c_{0}
for
s\in]0,\bar{t}]
(this interval is empty if
\bar{t}=0
, and might be of infinite length).
Then, for
s\in]0,\bar{t}]
, if
D(s)\leq q
we see that
D(s)\geq c_{0}\,H(s)\geq q
(because of (
23
)). Therefore
D(s)\geq q
on
[0,\bar{t}]
, so that
H^{\prime}(s)\leq-q
on the same interval, and finally

0\leq H(\bar{t})\leq H(0)-q\,\bar{t},

so that
\bar{t}\leq H(0)/q
.

Coming back to (
26
), we see that for
t\geq H(0)/q\,\,(\geq\bar{t})
,

H(t)\leq H(\bar{t})\,\exp(c_{0}\,H(0)/q)\,e^{-c_{0}\,t}.

We conclude that estimate (
24
) holds (using the fact that
H
is a nonincreasing function).

\square

We now explain how this Lemma (together with Theorem
1
) yields a result of exponential convergence towards equilibrium with explicit rate for
the Landau equation.

Using Theorem 5 of
[
17
]
, we see that when
\gamma\in]0,1]
(that is, in the case of hard potentials or hard spheres), and when the initial datum
f_{in}\geq 0
lies in

L^{2}_{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
(beware that the definition of
L^{p}_{q}
is different here and in
[
17
]
), then there exists a solution
f:=f(t,v)\geq 0
to the (spatially homogeneous) Landau equation

\partial_{t}f=Q_{\gamma}(f,f)
(with initial datum
f_{in}
) lying in
L^{\infty}([1,\infty[;L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
, and
satisfying the entropy identity in the strong sense (this is actually true if the initial datum is bounded below by a Maxwellian and has a sufficient number of moments; if the initial datum does not fulfill those requirements, one has to approximate it by initial data fulfilling it):

-\frac{d}{dt}H(f(t,\cdot)|M)=D_{\gamma}(f(t,\cdot)),

where

H(f|M):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\log f(v)dv-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}M(v)\,\log M(v)dv,

and
M(v)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}
.

At this point we assume the normalization
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{in}(v)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
3\end{array}\right)
, and apply Theorem
1
(more precisely Remark 1) with
f:=f(t,\cdot)
(the considered solution to the Landau equation).

It is then possible to use Lemma
1
(with a time translation), using

H:=H(f(t,\cdot)|M),\qquad D:=D_{\gamma}(f(t,\cdot)),

q:=0.22\,||f||_{L^{\infty}([1,\infty[;L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}^{-2},\qquad c_{0}:=c_{1}\,200^{-1}\,||f||_{L^{\infty}([1,\infty[;L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}^{-2},

where
c_{1}
is the constant in the logarithmic Sobolev inequality of Gross
[
20
]
.

We obtain thanks to Lemma
1

that this solution decays exponentially fast, with an explicit rate, towards the equilibrium
M
.

It is possible to obtain the same result without assuming the normalization (but with a different Maxwellian equilibrium
M
) thanks to the
change of unknown
f\to a\,f(b\,(\cdot-u))
, where
a,b>0
, and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Remark 2
.

A similar result can be obtained for the case of Maxwell molecules (
\gamma=0
), but in that case, direct explicit computations can be exploited in order
to get the exact rate of exponential convergence.

The case of soft potentials
\gamma\in[-3,0[
is somewhat different. Moments estimates still exist (cf.
[
11
]
and
[
8
]
), as well as smoothness estimates when
\gamma\in[-2,0[
(case of moderately soft potentials, cf. for example
[
25
]
and
[
11
]
), but they are not uniform in time. It is nevertheless possible (at least in the Coulomb case
\gamma=-3
) to obtain a stretched exponential estimate of convergence towards equilibrium (cf.
[
8
]
), using methods related to those of
[
24
]
. However, it is not obvious to know if such a result can be recovered with a simplified proof using
arguments similar to those used in this paper.

4.
Extension to the Landau-Fermi-Dirac equation

The Landau Fermi Dirac operator (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
) is
defined by

Q_{\gamma,LFD}(f,f)(v)=\nabla_{v}\cdot\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\,|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\,\Pi(v-w)\,\bigg(f(w)\,(1-\varepsilon\,f(w))\,\nabla{f}(v)\right.

(27)

\left.-f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon\,f(v))\nabla{f}(w)\bigg)\,dw\right\},

where
\Pi
is defined by (
2
), and
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter which mesures
the amount of quantum effects (due to the Pauli exclusion principle).

For this operator, we still have (at the formal level) the conservation of
mass, momentum and kinetic energy, that is, for any
f:=f(v)\geq 0
:

(28)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\gamma,LFD}(f,f)(v)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v_{i}\\
|v|^{2}/2\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right).

The entropy dissipation associated to the Landau-Fermi-Dirac operator also remains a nonnegative quantity, but it is not identical to the entropy dissipation associated to the Landau operator. Note that the entropy itself is different in the
classical and quantum case (cf. (
43
)). More precisely (at the formal level), for any
f:=f(v)>0
, it writes

(29)

D_{\gamma,LFD}(f):=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}Q_{\gamma,LFD}(f,f)(v)\,\left[\ln f(v)-\ln(1-\varepsilon\,f(v))\right]\,dv

=\frac{1}{2}\,\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\,(1-\varepsilon\,f(v))\,f(w)\,\,(1-\varepsilon\,f(w))|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\,\Pi(v-w)

\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon\,f)}(v)-\frac{\nabla f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon\,f)}(w)\right)\,\cdot\,\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon\,f)}(v)-\frac{\nabla f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(w)\right)\,dvdw\geq 0.

In
[
2
]
, it is shown that this quantity can be related to a quantity close to the Fisher information (more precisely, a quantum variant of
F
or
F_{rel}
). More precisely, the following estimate is presented (Proposition 2.12 of
[
2
]
):
for
\gamma
satisfying assumption (
3
) and for
f:=f(v)>0
such that (
8
) holds and

(30)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\qquad 1-\varepsilon\,f(v)\geq\kappa_{0},

one has (when the quantities below are well defined)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)(1-\varepsilon f(v))}-Kv\right|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq 510\,\,(\min_{i}I_{i}(f))^{-3}\,{\kappa_{0}^{-2}}\,\max(1,B_{\gamma}(f))

(31)

\times\,\max\left(1,\int f(v)\,(1+|v|^{2})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,dv\right)\,{J}_{\gamma}(f)\,{D}_{\gamma,LFD}(f),

where
K:=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(v))\,dv
, the directional temperature
I_{i}(f)
is defined in (
10
),

{B}_{\gamma}(f):=\bigg(\min_{i\neq j}\inf_{\sigma\in S^{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left|\sigma_{1}\frac{v_{i}}{\langle v\rangle}-\sigma_{2}\frac{v_{j}}{\langle v\rangle}\right|^{2}f(v)\,dv\bigg)^{-1},

and

{J}_{\gamma}(f):=\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|w-v|^{-\gamma}\,(1+|w|^{2})\,dw.

In the same spirit as in Section
2
, we propose an estimate which is close to (
29
), but somewhat simpler, and much easier to prove. It writes:

Proposition 2
.

We consider
\gamma
satisfying (
3
). Then
for all
f:=f(v)>0
(sufficiently smooth for the terms in the estimate to make sense) satisfying the normalization (
8
), such that

(32)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\qquad 1-\varepsilon\,f(v)\geq\kappa_{0},

and such that

\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\leq\frac{27}{32},

the following estimate holds:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))}-K\,v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq\bigg[\frac{32}{3}\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\,\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v|^{4}\,f(v)\,dv\bigg)\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)

(33)

+\,216\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)+24\,(3+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||\,|\cdot|^{2}\,f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})})\,\bigg]\,\kappa_{0}^{-2}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f),

where we recall that
K:=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(v))\,dv
.

Remark 3
.

An important feature of the above estimate is that it is uniform with respect to
\varepsilon
(for
\varepsilon>0
such that (
32
) holds, like all the estimates provided in
[
2
]
). One can check that when
\varepsilon\to 0
, Proposition
2
becomes Theorem
1
, up to the numerical constants. Remark 1 can also be adapted to the case of the Landau-Fermi-Dirac operator.

Proof of Proposition
2
:
We first observe that

D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))\,f(w)\,(1-\varepsilon f(w))

\times\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,[r_{i,i+1}^{f}(v,w)]^{2}\,dvdw,

where for
i,j=1,2,3
,

r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w):=(v_{i}-w_{i})\,\left(\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(w)\right)

(34)

-(v_{j}-w_{j})\,\left(\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(w)\right),

and, by definition,
r_{i,4}^{f}:=r_{i,1}^{f}
.

Using the normalization (
8
) and assuming without loss of generality that (
11
) holds,
we multiply (
34
) by
f(w)
and
w_{i}\,f(w)
and integrate on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in the
w
-variable.
We get the identities, when
i\neq j
,

(35)

v_{i}\,\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-v_{j}\,\frac{\partial_{i}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw,

(36)

I_{i}(f)\,\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-K\,v_{j}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(w))\,w_{j}\,dw=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}w_{i}\,f(w)\,r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw.

This last identity can be rewritten as

I_{i}(f)\,\bigg(\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-K\,v_{j}\bigg)=-(I_{i}(f)-1)\,K\,v_{j}

(37)

-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(w))\,w_{j}\,dw-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}w_{i}\,f(w)\,r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dw.

Multiplying identity (
35
) by
v_{i}\,v_{j}\,f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))
and integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in the
v
-variable,
we see that

I_{j}(f)-I_{i}(f)=\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))\,f(w)\,v_{i}\,v_{j}\,r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dvdw

so that, using again the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as in the case of the Landau equation,

|I_{j}(f)-I_{i}(f)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,\kappa_{0}^{-1/2}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)^{1/2}\,s_{\gamma}(f)^{1/2},

where
s_{\gamma}(f)
is defined by (
18
).
As a consequence, once again as in the case of the Landau equation,

|I_{i}(f)-1|^{2}\leq\frac{8}{27}\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\,s_{\gamma}(f).

We deduce from this estimate that

I_{i}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}
as soon as

(38)

D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\,s_{\gamma}(f)\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{27}{32}.

Then, multiplying (
36
) by
f(v)
and integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with respect to the variable
v
, we see that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(w))\,w_{j}\,dw=\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,w_{i}\,r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)\,dvdw,

so that

\bigg|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\ln(1-\varepsilon f(w))\,w_{j}\,dw\bigg|\leq\bigg(\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dvdw\bigg)^{1/2}

\times\,\bigg(\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,|w_{i}|^{2}\,dvdw\bigg)^{1/2}

\leq\sqrt{2}\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)^{1/2}\,\Sigma_{\gamma}(f)^{1/2},

where

\Sigma_{\gamma}(f):=\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,f(w)\,|v-w|^{-\gamma}\,|w|^{2}\,dvdw.

Under condition (
38
), we see that estimate (
37
) leads to

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\partial_{j}f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon f)}(v)-K\,v_{j}\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq 12\,(I_{i}(f)-1)^{2}\,K^{2}\,I_{j}(f)+24\,\kappa_{0}^{-2}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\,\Sigma_{\gamma}(f)

+\,12\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(w)\,|v-w|^{\gamma}\,|r_{i,j}^{f}(v,w)|^{2}\,dw\bigg]\,dv\,\,\sigma_{\gamma}(f),

so that finally, summing from
j=1
to
3
(and choosing
i=j+1
)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f}{f\,(1-\varepsilon\,f)}(v)-K\,v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq\bigg[\frac{32}{3}\,\kappa_{0}^{-1}\,K^{2}\,s_{\gamma}(f)+72\,\kappa_{0}^{-2}\,\Sigma_{\gamma}(f)

+\,24\,\kappa_{0}^{-2}\,\sigma_{\gamma}(f)\bigg]\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f).

At this level, we observe that (working as for establishing (
21
)),

(39)

\Sigma_{\gamma}(f)\leq 3\,\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi}\,||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right).

Observing that

|K|\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(v)}{1-\varepsilon\,f(v)}\,dv\leq\kappa_{0}^{-1},

and remembering (
21
), (
22
) and (
39
), we can
conclude the proof of Proposition
2
.

\square

We end up this section by briefly explaining how Proposition
2
leads to a result of exponential convergence with an explicit and
\varepsilon
-independent (for
\varepsilon
not too large) rate towards equilibrium for the solutions to the (spatially homogeneous) Landau-Fermi-Dirac equations with hard potentials or hard spheres (that is
\gamma\in]0,1]
). Since the proof closely follows the computations and estimates of
[
2
]
, we do not present all details.

First, observing that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(v)\,|v|^{4}\,dv
,
||f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
and
||\,|\cdot|^{2}\,f||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
can be estimated by

||f||_{L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
(see Remark 1), we see thanks to Proposition
2
that for some
A_{f},B_{f}>0
depending only on
||f||_{L^{2}_{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

and
\kappa_{0}
, the estimate

(40)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))}-K\,v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv\leq B_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)

holds for all normalized
f>0
such that (
32
) is satisfied, and such that

A_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\leq 1.

Estimate (
40
) can be rewritten (see
[
2
]
, subsection 3.1) as

(41)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))}+2b_{\varepsilon}\,v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv-3\,(K+2b_{\varepsilon})^{2}\leq B_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f),

where
b_{\varepsilon}
is defined (for
\varepsilon
not too large) in
[
2
]
, as the unique real number such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-b_{\varepsilon}\,|v|^{2}}}{1+\varepsilon\,a_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-b_{\varepsilon}\,|v|^{2}}}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)\,dv=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
3\end{array}\right).

Using the estimate (extracted from
[
2
]
, Lemma 3.1)

|K+2b_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\,\max(||f||_{\infty},||{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{\infty})\,||f-{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{a_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-b_{\varepsilon}\,|v|^{2}}}{1+\varepsilon\,a_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-b_{\varepsilon}\,|v|^{2}}},

we obtain the estimate

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg|\frac{\nabla f(v)}{f(v)\,(1-\varepsilon f(v))}+2b_{\varepsilon}\,v\bigg|^{2}\,f(v)\,dv-12\,\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\kappa_{0}^{4}}\,\max(||f||_{\infty}^{2},||{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{\infty}^{2})\,||f-{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

\leq B_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f).

Defining

(43)

H(f|{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}):=S_{\varepsilon}({\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon})-S_{\varepsilon}(f),

where

S_{\varepsilon}(f):=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bigg[\varepsilon\,f\,\log(\varepsilon f)+(1-\varepsilon f)\,\log(1-\varepsilon f)\bigg]\,dv,

and

22

||f-{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq 2\,H(f|{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}),

\bigg[\,2b_{\varepsilon}-24\,\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\kappa_{0}^{4}}\,\max(||f||_{\infty}^{2},||{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}||_{\infty}^{2})\,\bigg]\,H(f|{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon})\leq B_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f).

such that (
32

A_{f}\,D_{\gamma,LFD}(f)\leq 1

We now consider a normalized initial datum
f_{in}\geq 0
in

of the (spatially homogeneous) Landau-Fermi-Dirac

This ensures that for

At this level, it is possible to use Lemma

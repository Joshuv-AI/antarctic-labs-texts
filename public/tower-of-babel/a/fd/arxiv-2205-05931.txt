RH-Dependent Estimates of Remainder
in Modified Mertens Formula

Abstract




MSC-2010-class: 11N37 (Primary); 11M26 (Secondary)

Abstract:
Assuming the validity of Riemann Hypothesis
(RH)
,
we derive the explicit bilateral estimates (”narrow passage”) for the
remainder in the
modified
Mertens asymptotic formula
for the sums of primes’ reciprocals.

These results are reversable, thus yielding some new criteria for
RH
.

Keywords:
Mertens formula, Ingham method, Riemann Hypothesis.

2. Proof of the Theorem

We begin with some preliminary assertions.

Recall that
RH

may be reformulated in terms of the deviation of Chebyshev
function
\theta(x)
from

:

\Delta(x):=\theta(x)-x
, which is

unconditionally

O(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x})

Therefore (cf. also (1.2), (1.4)) for some

in between of

Q(x)=R(x)+\log\log x-\log\log\theta(x)=R(x)-\frac{\Delta(x)}{\tilde{x}\log\tilde{x}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right).

[formula]

The proposition below is well known classical result by H. von Koch (1901), cf., e.g., [5], th. 5.21.

Proposition 3.

The following assertions are equivalent:

{\bf(i)\hskip 11.49994ptRH}\hbox{ holds true};\quad{\bf(ii)}\quad|\Delta(x)|<x^{0.5+{\varepsilon}},\ \forall{\varepsilon}>0,\ \forall x>X_{{\varepsilon}};

{\bf(iii)}\quad|\Delta(x)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{x}\log^{2}x}{8\pi},\ x>X_{0}.

[formula]

In the Ingham’s monograph [6], S. V.10, form. (35), the helpful
conditional estimate for the primitive of
(\psi(x)-x)

Proposition 4
.
Assume
RH

\left|\int_{0}^{x}(\psi(t)-t)\,dt\right|<0.1x^{3/2},\ x<X_{0}

Whence, taking into account the relationship

\theta(x)=\psi(x)-\sqrt{x}+O(x^{1/3})

Proposition 5

The validity of
RH

(\theta(x)-x)

\Phi(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\Delta(t)\,dt=(-2/3+b(x))x^{3/2};\quad|b(x)|<0.1,\ x>X_{0}.

Assume

{\bf(i)}\ -2.5\leq\liminf H(x)\,\sqrt{x}\,\log x;\ {\bf(ii)}\ \limsup H(x)\,\sqrt{x}\,\log x\leq-1.5.

\triangleright
First let us note that the Taylor formula implies:

\log\log\theta(x)-\log\log x=\underline{\frac{\Delta(x)}{x\log x}}-\frac{\log\hat{x}+1}{2\hat{x}^{2}\log^{2}\hat{x}}\Delta^{2}(x),

[formula]

\theta(x)

On the other hand, integrating twice by parts, one obtains (cf. also (2.3)):

\sum_{x<p\leq y}\frac{1}{p}-\log\log y+\log\log x=\int\limits_{x}^{y^{+}}\frac{d\Delta(t)}{t\log t}=\frac{\Delta(t)}{t\log t}\Biggr|_{x}^{y^{+}}-\int\limits_{x}^{y^{+}}\Delta(x)\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)^{\prime}\,dt

=\frac{\Delta(y^{+})}{y\log y}\ \ \underline{-\ \frac{\Delta(x)}{x\log x}}-\Phi(t)\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)^{\prime}\Biggr|_{x}^{y}+\int\limits_{x}^{y}\Phi(t)\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)^{\prime\prime}dt.

D(x):=-\frac{\Phi(x)(\log x+1)}{x^{2}\log^{2}x},\ E(x):=\int\limits_{x}^{+\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{3}\log t}\left(2+\frac{3}{\log t}+\frac{2}{\log^{2}t}\right)\,dt,

[formula]

Summing this with (2.6) (the terms, involving

\frac{17}{30}\leq\liminf D(x)\,\sqrt{x}\,\log x\leq\limsup D(x)\,\sqrt{x}\,\log x\leq\frac{23}{30};\qquad\

3.  Some corollaries and conclusive remarks

The Theorem allows to deduce some new conditions equivalent to
RH

in terms of the function
\Phi(x)
and of the primitive of
(\psi(x)-x)^{2}
.

Corollary 1.

In order
RH
hold true it is necessary
and sufficient that at least one (and then all) of the three conditions
be fulfilled:

{\bf(i)}\quad\Phi(x)=O(x^{1.5+{\varepsilon}};\quad{\bf(ii)}\ \Phi(x)=O(x^{1.5});

{\bf(iii)}\ \int\limits_{0}^{x}(\psi(t)-t)^{2}dt=O(x^{2}).

[formula]

\triangleright

Necessity.
The implication
RH

\Rightarrow

(iii)
was established by H. Cramér (1921),

Now it remains to notice that in (3.1)
(iii)
\Rightarrow

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)

\Box.

\triangleright

Sufficiency.
Let (3.1)
(i)
be fulfilled,
i. e.
|\Phi(x)|\leq C({\varepsilon})x^{1.5+{\varepsilon}},\forall\ x,{\varepsilon}>0
.
then from (2.13), (2.11) and (2.9) one obtains for all
x>X_{{\varepsilon}}
:

-\ Q(x)=H(x)+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\leq D(x)+E(x)+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)

\leq C_{{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{x^{1.5+{\varepsilon}}}{x^{2}\log^{2}x}+2\int\limits_{x}^{\infty}\frac{t^{1.5+{\varepsilon}}}{t^{3}\log t}\,dt\right)\leq C_{{\varepsilon}}x^{-0.5+{\varepsilon}},

[formula]

whence

(ii)

RH

\Box.

The reasonings in the proofs of the Lemma and the Theorem show
that

\hbox{\it both conditions (2.5){\bf(i)(ii)}

[formula]

This allows to deduce the
RH

in terms of the consequences:

U_{k}:=\sum_{j>k}\left(\,\frac{1}{p_{j}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{j}}\right),\hbox{ where }\theta_{j}:=\theta(p_{j});\quad V_{k}:=\sum_{j>k}\left|\,\frac{1}{p_{j}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{j}}\,\right|.

[formula]

Corollary 2.

RH
is valid if and only if at least one (and then all) of the following six conditions is fulfilled for any

{\varepsilon}>0,\ k>K_{{\varepsilon}}
:

\qquad{\bf(i)}\ U_{k}<k^{-0.5+{\varepsilon}};\qquad\ {\bf(ii)}\ U_{k}>-k^{-0.5+{\varepsilon}};\

\quad{\bf(iii)}\ U_{k}\sqrt{p_{k}}\,\log p_{k}\,<-1.5+{\varepsilon};\qquad{\bf(iv)}\ U_{k}\sqrt{p_{k}}\,\log p_{k}\,>-2.5-{\varepsilon};

\qquad\quad{\bf(v)}\ V_{k}<k^{-0.5+{\varepsilon}};\qquad\ {\bf(vi)}\ \frac{V_{k}\,\sqrt{p_{k}}}{\log p_{k}}<\frac{1+{\varepsilon}}{4\pi}.

[formula]

\triangleright
First we note that (cp. (2.6)) for certain
\tau_{j}\in(\theta_{j-1},\theta_{j})

\log\log\theta_{j}-\log\log\theta_{j-1}=\frac{\log p_{j}}{\theta_{j}\log\theta_{j}}+\frac{\log\tau_{j}+1}{2\tau_{j}^{2}\log^{2}\tau_{j}}\log^{2}p_{j}

=\frac{1}{\theta_{j}}-\frac{\log\theta_{j}-\log p_{j}}{\theta_{j}\log\theta_{j}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p_{j}^{2}\log p_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{\theta_{j}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p_{j}^{2}\log p_{j}}\right).

[formula]

Here we have also taken into account that

\log\theta_{j}-\log p_{j}=(\theta_{j}-p_{j})/\tilde{x}^{*}_{j},

is some number in between of

and
\theta_{j}

\theta_{j}\approx p_{j}

Therefore, for

H_{j-1}-H_{j}=\frac{1}{p_{j}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{j}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p_{j}^{2}\log p_{j}}\right).

[formula]

Summing these relations from

to infinity, one obtains

H_{k}=U_{k}+O(1/k)=-Q(x)+O(1/k),\qquad x\in(p_{k-1},p_{k}].

[formula]

and thus (3.5)
(i)(ii)(iii)(iv)
are equivalent (resp.) to (1.5)
(i)(ii)

(1.8)
(i)(ii)
, each of which in turn (cf. Sect. 1)

At last, taking into account that

Remark 3.
If
RH
holds true, then Theorem shows not only
the fast, but also
quasi-monotonic
decrease of the remainder

Q(x)
in (1.4), in the sense that relations
y>ax,a>25/9,x>X_{a},

imply
Q(x)>Q(y)>0
.

The
oscilation properties
of the remainder
R(x)
in the original Mertens formula (1.2), studied by H. Diamond and J. Pintz [7], may be easily derived from the Theorem for the case,
when
RH
is valid.

Indeed, according to (2.1) one has
unconditionally
:

R(x)=Q(x)+\frac{\Delta(x)}{\tilde{x}\log\tilde{x}},\hbox{ where}\ \tilde{x}\approx x.

[formula]

Therefore
assuming
RH
, which according to the Theorem
(cf. (1.8)) implies
Q(x)=O(1/\sqrt{x}\log x)
, one obtains that

for any positive function

\eta(x)\to+\infty
:

{\bf(i)}\quad\Delta(x)=\Omega_{\pm}(\sqrt{x}\,\eta(x))\iff\ {\bf(ii)}\quad R(x)=\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{\eta(x)}{\sqrt{x}\,\log(x)}\right)\

[formula]

By virtue of the J. Littlewood result (1914), (cf., e. g. [5], th. 6.20),
this holds true for
\eta(x)=\log\log\log x
.

The same arguments allow to conclude that the estimate

R(x)=O(\log x/\sqrt{x})
, cf. (1.3),

is not only
necessary
, but
also
sufficient
for
RH
.

Introduction

This paper concerns periodic homogenization with localized defects in the sense of
[
2
]
. More exactly, we consider Dirichlet problems for semilinear second-order ODE systems in divergence form of the type

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\Big((A(x/\varepsilon)+B(x/\varepsilon))u^{\prime}(x)+c(x,u(x))\Big)^{\prime}=d(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in(0,1),\\
u(0)=u(1)=0.\end{array}\right\}

(1.1)

For small
\varepsilon>0
we look for solutions
u:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
to (
1.1
), and we suppose that

\displaystyle A\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{M}_{n})\mbox{ is 1-periodic,}

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!inf}\{A(y)u\cdot u:\;y\in\mathbb{R},u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\|u\|=1\}>0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto(c(\cdot,u),d(\cdot,u))\in L^{\infty}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2}\mbox{ is $C^{1}$-smooth.}

(1.4)

Here
\mathbb{M}_{n}
is the space of all real
n\times n
-matrices,
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto u\cdot v\in\mathbb{R}

is the Euclidean scalar product in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\|u\|:=\sqrt{u\cdot u}\mbox{ for }u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mbox{ and }\|M\|:=\max\{\|Mu\|:\,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\|u\|\leq 1\}

are the Euclidean norms in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{M}_{n}
, respectively.
Further,

\|u\|_{\infty}:=\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!sup}\{\|u(x)\|:\;x\in(0,1)\}

is the norm in the Lebesgue space
L^{\infty}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{n})
(which is used, for example, in (
1.4
)),
\|u\|_{\infty}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
is the norm in the Sobolev space
W^{1,\infty}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{n})
(which is used in Theorem
1.1
(ii) below), and

\|M\|_{\infty}:=\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!sup}\{\|M(x)\|:\;x\in\mathbb{R}\}\mbox{ and }\|M\|_{1}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|M(x)\|dx

are the norms in
L^{\infty}((0,1);\mathbb{M}_{n})
and
L^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{M}_{n})
, respectively.
Finally, for
r>1
we define

{\cal B}_{r}:=\left\{B\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{M}_{n})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{M}_{n}):\,\|M\|_{\infty}+\|M\|_{1}\leq r\right\}

and

{\cal M}_{r}:=\left\{B\in{\cal B}_{r}:\,(A(y)+B(y))u\cdot u\geq\frac{\|u\|}{r}\mbox{ for almost all }y\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and all }u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

(1.5)

We are going to prove existence and local uniqueness of
weak solutions
u
to (
1.1
) with
\varepsilon\approx 0
,
B\in{\cal M}_{r}
and
\|u-u_{0}\|_{\infty}\approx 0
, where
u_{0}
is a given non-degenerate weak solution to the homogenized boundary value problem

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\Big(A_{0}u^{\prime}(x)+c(x,u(x)\Big)^{\prime}=d(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in(0,1),\\
u(0)=u(1)=0\end{array}\right\}

(1.6)

with

A_{0}:=\left(\int_{0}^{1}A(y)^{-1}dy\right)^{-1}.

(1.7)

Remark that the assumption (
1.2
)
implies that the matrices
A(y)

and, for
B\in{\cal M}_{r}
, the matrices
A(y)+B(y)

are positive definite uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, these matrices are invertible, and their inverses also
are positive definite uniformly
with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!sup}_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\|A(y)^{-1}\|+\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!sup}_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{B\in{\cal M}_{r}}\|(A(y)+B(y))^{-1}\|<\infty.

(1.8)

Also the matrix
\int_{0}^{1}A(y)^{-1}dy

is positive definite and, hence, invertible. In particular,
the definition (
1.7
) is correct.

As usual, a vector function
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called weak solution to (
1.1
) if
for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{n})

it
satisfies
the variational equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\Big((A(x/\varepsilon)+B(x/\varepsilon))u^{\prime}(x)+c(x,u(x))\Big)\cdot\varphi^{\prime}(x)+d(x,u(x))\cdot\varphi(x)\Big)dx=0,

(1.9)

and similar for the homogenized boundary value problem (
1.6
) and for its linearization in

, which is

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\Big(A_{0}u^{\prime}(x)+\partial_{u}c(x,u_{0}(x))u(x)\Big)^{\prime}=\partial_{u}d(x,u_{0}(x))u(x)\mbox{ for }x\in(0,1),\\
u(0)=u(1)=0.\end{array}\right\}

Here

is the Jacobi matrix of the vector function

in the point

, and similarly

\partial_{u}d(x,u_{0}(x))\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
.

Theorem 2.1

Let be given Banach spaces

\displaystyle u_{0}=F_{0}(u_{0}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup\{\|F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u_{0})u\|_{V}:\;\varepsilon>0,\,\lambda\in\Lambda,\;u\in U,\;\|u\|_{U}\leq 1\}<\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|F_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u_{0})-F_{0}(u_{0})\|_{U}=0\mbox{ uniformly with respect to }\lambda\in\Lambda,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u_{0})-F^{\prime}_{0}(u_{0}))u\|_{U}=0\mbox{ for all }u\in U\mbox{ uniformly with respect to }\lambda\in\Lambda.

(2.13)

Stability, analytic stability for real reductive Lie groups Thanks: The first author was partially supported by PRIN 2017 “Real and Complex Manifolds: Topology, Geometry and holomorphic dynamics ” and “National Group for Algebraic and Geometric Structures, and their Applications” (GNSAGA - INDAM)
Leonardo Biliotti, Oluwagbenga Joshua Windare

Abstract
. We presented a systematic treatment of a Hilbert criterion for stability theory for an action of a real reductive group G G on a real submanifold X X of a Kähler manifold Z Z . More precisely, we suppose that the action of a compact Lie group with Lie algebra 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} extends holomorphically to an action of the complexified group U ℂ U^{\mathbb{C}} and that the U U -action on Z Z is Hamiltonian. If G ⊂ U ℂ G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}} is compatible, there is a corresponding gradient map μ 𝔭 : X → 𝔭 \mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:X\to\mathfrak{p} , where 𝔤 = 𝔨 ⊕ 𝔭 \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p} is a Cartan decomposition of the Lie algebra of G G . The concept of energy complete action of G G on X X is introduced. For such actions, one can characterize stability, semistability and polystability of a point by a numerical criteria using a G G -equivariant function called maximal weight. We also prove the classical Hilbert-Mumford criteria for semistability and polystability conditions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the Hilbert Mumford criterion for semistability and polystability points associated with the actions of real reductive groups on real submanifolds of Kähler manifolds. In the classical case of a group action on a Kähler manifold these characterization are given by Mundet i Riera
[
28
,
29
]
, Bruasse and Teleman
[
6
]
, Teleman
[
32
]
, Kapovich, Leeb
and Millson
[
22
]
and probably many others, indeed any of these ideas go
back as far as Mumford
[
30
, §2.2]
, where systematic presentation of notions of stability theory in the non-algebraic Kählerian geometry of complex reductive Lie groups and the relationships between these notions are given. Our aim is to investigate a class of actions of real reductive Lie groups on real submanifolds of a Kähler manifold using gradient map techniques. More precisely, we consider a Kähler manifold
(Z,\omega)
with an holomorphic action of a complex reductive Lie group
U^{\mathbb{C}}
, where
U^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of a compact Lie group
U
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}
. We also assume
\omega
is
U
-invariant and that there is a
U
-equivariant momentum map
\mu:Z\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}.
By definition, for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{u}
and
z\in Z,

d\mu^{\xi}=i_{\xi_{Z}}\omega,
where
\mu^{\xi}(z):=\mu(z)(\xi)
and
\xi_{Z}
denotes the fundamental vector field induced on
Z
by the action of
U,
i.e.,

\xi_{Z}(z):=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\exp(t\xi)z

(see, for example,
[
23
]
for more details on the momentum map).
Recently, the momentum map has been generalized to the following settings
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

We say that a subgroup
G
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
is compatible if
G
is closed and the map
K\times\mathfrak{p}\to G,

(k,\beta)\mapsto k\exp(\beta)

is a diffeomorpism where
K:=G\cap U
and
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{g}\cap\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u};

\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of
G
.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
is the direct sum
\mathfrak{u}\oplus\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}.
It follows that
G
is compatible with the
Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U\exp(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u})
,
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
and that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
(see Section
2.1
). The inclusion
\textbf{i}\mathfrak{p}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{u}
induces by restriction, a
K
-equivariant map
\mu_{\textbf{i}\mathfrak{p}}:Z\to(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{p})^{*}.
One can choose and fix
B
an
\mathrm{Ad}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
-invariant inner product of the Euclidian type on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}
, see
[
6
, Section 3.2]
,
[
25
, Definition 3.2.4]
and also
[
20
, Section 2.1]
for the analogue in the algebraic GIT. Such an inner product will automatically induce a well defined inner product on any maximal compact subgroup
U^{\prime}
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
. We thank the referee for pointing out these references.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the real part
B
. Then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is positive definite on
\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
, negative definite on
\mathfrak{u}
,
\langle\mathfrak{u},\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}\rangle=0
and finally the multiplication by
i
satisfies
\langle\textbf{i}\cdot,\textbf{i}\cdot\rangle=-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
(see Subsection
2.2
). In order to simplify the notation we replace consideration of
\mu_{\text{i}\mathfrak{p}}
by that of
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}
, where

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(x):=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x),\beta\rangle:=\langle\textbf{i}\mu(x),\beta\rangle=-\langle\mu(x),-\textbf{i}\beta\rangle=\mu^{-\textbf{i}\beta}(x).

The map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}
is
K
-equivariant and grad
\,\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{Z}
for any
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
. Here the grad is computed with respect to the Riemannian metric induced by the Kähler structure. The map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is called the
G
-gradient map associated with
\mu
(see Section
2.2
).
For a
G
-stable locally closed real submanifold
X
of
Z,
we consider
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
as a map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:X\to\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathrm{grad}\,\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}=\beta_{X}
, where the gradient is now computed with respect to the induced Riemannian metric on
X
. The norm square of
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined as

f(x):=\frac{1}{2}\parallel\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\parallel^{2};\quad x\in X.
A strategy for analyzing the
G
-action on
X
is to view
f
as a generalized Morse function. In
[
15
]
the authors proved that associated to critical points of
f
, we have
G
-invariant submanifolds of
X
called pre-strata and they studied its properties. If
X
is compact then the pre-strata are the strata of a Morse type stratification of
X
.
Semistable (polystable) points in
X
can be identified by taking into account the positions of their
G
-orbits with respect to

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0).
A point
x\in X
is polystable if it’s
G
-orbit intersects the level set
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)

(
G\cdot x\cap\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
) and semistable if the closure of it’s
G
-orbit intersects

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)
(
\overline{G\cdot x}\cap\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
).
The set of semistable points associated with the critical points of

is studied in great details
[
14
,
15
,
17
]
. The aim of these papers were to develop a Geometrical Invariant Theory for actions of real Lie groups on real submanifolds of a Kähler manifold, generalizing results of Heinzner-Huckleberry-Loose
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
and Sjamaar
[
31
]
.

On the other hand, the semistability (polystability) condition can be checked using a numerical criterion in term of a function called the maximal weight, which can be regarded as a Kählerian version of the Hilbert Criterion in Geometric Invariant Theory
[
29
,
32
]
. We refer to this numerical condition as analytic stability condition. Biliotti and Zedda
[
4
]
, see also
[
2
]
, give a systematic treatment of the stability theory for an action of a real non-compact reductive Lie group
G
on a compact connected real manifold

using the maximal weight function and apply this settings to the action of
G
on measures of
X.

In this paper, we introduce a large class of actions of
G
on
X
following Teleman
[
32
]
, called
energy complete action
. As Teleman pointed out, the energy completeness condition gives the natural framework for the stability theory in non-algebraic complex geometry. For such actions,
we prove that the semistability condition, respectively polystability condition, is equivalent to analytic semistability condition (Theorem
4.4
), respectively analytic polystability condition (Theorem
4.6
), extending the results due to Teleman
[
32
]
and Biliotti and Zedda
[
4
]
, respectively
[
9

. We also characterize the semistable and polystable points in terms of one-parameter subgroups (Corollary
4.4.1
and Corollary
4.6.1
).

The paper is organized as follows:

Section
2
contains a few basic facts concerning real reductive Lie groups and the gradient map. Many of the results in this section are well known but we include short proofs in some cases. We also recall the Kempf-Ness function associated to the
G
-action on
X
and some of its properties are given.

In Section
3
, we define the stability conditions and the maximal weight associated with the gradient map and some of its properties are given. We also introduce and discuss the class of energy complete actions and we prove the moment-weight inequality (Theorem
3.14
).

In Section
4
we introduce the notions of analytically semistability and polystability and we prove the main results.

In Section
5
we discuss examples which illustrate the general theory.

In Section
6
, following Teleman
[
32
]
, we introduce the notion of symplectization of the
U^{\mathbb{C}}
-action on
Z
with respect to
G
.
The notions of stability, polystability and semistability depend of the Cartan decomposition of
G
induced by the Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U\exp(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u})
and so these concepts depend on the triple
(U,\mathtt{g},\mu)
, where
\mathtt{g}
is the Riemannian metric induced by the Kähler form
\omega
. The norm square of the gradient map also depends on the triple
(U,\mathtt{g},\mu)
.

Let
g\in G
. Then
U^{\prime}=gUg^{-1}
is a maximal compact subgroup
U^{\mathbb{C}}
and
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U^{\prime}\exp(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime})
.
U^{\prime}
preserves the Kähler form
\omega_{g}=(g^{-1})^{\star}\omega
and there exists a momentum map
\mu:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, given by
\mu^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ad}(g)\circ\mu\circ g^{-1}
. Note that
(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g}
is the Riemannian metric associated to
\omega_{g}
. Now,
G
is also compatible with
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U^{\prime}\exp(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime})
. Indeed,
G=K^{\prime}\exp(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
, where
K^{\prime}=gKg^{-1}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\mathfrak{p})
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Ad}(g)\circ\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ g^{-1}
is the
G
-gradient map associated to
\mu^{\prime}
. This means that one may also chose the triple
(U^{\prime},(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g},\mu^{\prime})
for analyzing the
G
-action on
X
. We show that the polystable and semistable conditions and the stratification of
X
with respect to the norm square gradient map do not depend of the triple chosen.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Compatible subgroups and parabolic subgroups

Let
H
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and
E,F\subset\mathfrak{h}
. Then, we set

\displaystyle E^{F}:=\{\eta\in E:[\eta,\xi]=0,\forall\xi\in F\}

\displaystyle H^{F}=\{g\in H:\operatorname{Ad}g(\xi)=\xi,\forall\xi\in F\}.

If
F=\{\beta\}
we write simply
E^{\beta}
and
H^{\beta}
.

Let
U
be a compact Lie group and let
U^{\mathbb{C}}
be the corresponding complex linear algebraic group
[
7
]
. The group
U^{\mathbb{C}}
is reductive and is the universal complexification of
U
in the sense of
[
21
]
. On the Lie algebra level, we have the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
with a corresponding Cartan involution
\theta:\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\xi+\textbf{i}\nu\mapsto\xi-\textbf{i}\nu
. We also denote by
\theta
the corresponding involution on
U^{\mathbb{C}}
. The real analytic map
F:U\times\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}\longrightarrow U^{\mathbb{C}}
,
(u,\xi)\mapsto u\exp(\xi)
is a diffeomorphism. We refer to the composition
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U\exp(\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u})
as the Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
be a closed real
subgroup of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
. We say
that
G
is
compatible
with the Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
if
F(K\times\mathfrak{p})=G
where
K:=G\cap U
and
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{g}\cap\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
. The
restriction of
F
to
K\times\mathfrak{p}
is then a diffeomorphism onto

G
. It follows that
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
and
that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
. Since
K
is a retraction of
G
, it follows that
G
has only finitely many connected components and
G=KG^{o}
, where
G^{o}
denotes the connected component of
G
containing
e
. Since
U
can be embedded in
\operatorname{Gl}(N,\mathbb{C})
for
some
N
, and any such embedding induces a closed embedding of

U^{\mathbb{C}}
, any compatible subgroup is a closed linear group. By
[
24
, Proposition 1.59 p.57]
,

\mathfrak{g}
is a real reductive Lie algebra, and so
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, where
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\{v\in\mathfrak{g}:\,[v,\mathfrak{g}]=0\}
is the Lie algebra of the center of
G
. Denote by
G_{ss}
the analytic
subgroup tangent to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Then
G_{ss}
is closed and

G^{o}=Z(G^{o})^{o}\cdot G_{ss}

[
24
, p. 442]
, where
Z(G^{o})^{o}
denotes the connected component of the center of
G^{o}
containing
e
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
, Lemma 7]
)
.

a)

If
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
is a compatible
subgroup, and
H\subset G
is closed and
\theta
-invariant,
then
H
is compatible if and only if
H
has only finitely many connected components.

b)

If
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
is a connected
compatible subgroup, then
G_{ss}
is compatible.

c)

If
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
is a compatible
subgroup and
E\subset\mathfrak{p}
is any subset, then
G^{E}
is
compatible. Indeed,
G^{E}=K^{E}\exp(\mathfrak{p}^{E})
, where
K^{E}=K\cap G^{E}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{E}=\{x\in\mathfrak{p}:\,[x,E]=0\}
.

If
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
the endomorphism
\mathrm{ad}(\beta)\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
is diagonalizable over
\mathbb{R}
. Denote by
V_{\lambda}(\mathrm{ad}(\beta))
the eigenspace of
\mathrm{ad}(\beta)
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. We define,

\displaystyle G^{\beta+}

\displaystyle:=\{g\in G:\lim_{t\to-\infty}\exp(t\beta)\,g\exp(-t\beta)\text{ exists}\}

\displaystyle R^{\beta+}

\displaystyle:=\{g\in G:\lim_{t\to-\infty}\exp(t\beta)\,g\exp(-t\beta)=e\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}^{\beta+}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda>0}V_{\lambda}(\mathrm{ad}(\beta))

\displaystyle G^{\beta-}

\displaystyle:\{g\in G:\,\lim_{t\to+\infty}\exp(t\beta)\,g\exp(-t\beta)\text{ exists}\}

\displaystyle R^{\beta-}

\displaystyle:=\{g\in G:\lim_{t\to+\infty}\exp(t\beta)\,g\exp(-t\beta)=e\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}^{\beta-}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}V_{\lambda}(\mathrm{ad}(\beta)).

Note that
G^{\beta-}=G^{(-\beta)+}=\theta(G^{\beta+})
,
\theta(R^{\beta+})=R^{\beta-}
and
G^{\beta}=G^{\beta+}\cap G^{\beta-}
.
The group
G^{\beta+}
, respectively
G^{\beta-}
, is a parabolic subgroup of
G
with unipotent radical
R^{\beta+}
, respectively
R^{\beta-}
and Levi factor
G^{\beta}
.

R^{\beta+}
is connected with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}^{\beta+}
. Hence
R^{\beta-}
is connected with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}^{\beta-}
.
The parabolic subgroup
G^{\beta+}
is the semidirect product of
G^{\beta}
with
R^{\beta+}
and we have the projection
\pi^{\beta+}:G^{\beta+}\longrightarrow G^{\beta}
,

\pi^{\beta+}(g)=\lim_{t\mapsto-\infty}exp(t\beta)g\exp(-t\beta).

Analogously,
G^{\beta-}
is the semidirect product of
G^{\beta}
with
R^{\beta-}
and we have the projection
\pi^{\beta-}:G^{\beta-}\longrightarrow G^{\beta}
,
\pi^{\beta-}(g)=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}exp(t\beta)g\exp(-t\beta)
. In particular,
\mathfrak{g}^{\beta+}=\mathfrak{g}^{\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{r}^{\beta+}
, respectively
\mathfrak{g}^{\beta-}=\mathfrak{g}^{\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{r}^{\beta-}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
, we have
G=KG^{\beta+}
.

Proof.

If
G
is connected, the result is well-known, see for instance
[
1
, Lemma 9]
and
[
15
, Lemma 4.1]
. Since
G=KG^{o}
, it follows that

G=KG^{o}=K(G^{o})^{\beta+}=KG^{\beta+},

concluding the proof.

2.2.
Gradient map

Let
(Z,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold on which
U^{\mathbb{C}}
acts
holomorphically on
Z
. Assume that
U
preserves
\omega
and that there is a
U
-equivariant
momentum map
\mu:Z\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}^{*}
. By definition we then have
\mathrm{d}\mu^{\xi}=i_{\xi_{Z}}\omega
, where
\mu^{\xi}(z):=\mu(z)(\xi)
and
\displaystyle\xi_{Z}(z):=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\exp(t\xi)z
. We choose and fix an
\mathrm{Ad}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
inner product of Euclidian type on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}

[
6
, Section 3.2]
and
[
26
, Definition 3.2.4]
. One can use for instance an embedding of
U
in
\mathrm{U}(n)
and its extension
U^{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Then

B:\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad(X,Y)\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(XY)

is an
\mathrm{Ad}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
inner product of Euclidian type on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the real part of
B
. Then, keeping in mind that
\mathfrak{u}\subset\mathrm{u}(n)
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is positive-definite on
\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
, negative-define on
\mathfrak{u}
,
\langle\mathfrak{u},\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}\rangle=0
and
\langle\textbf{i}\cdot,\textbf{i}\cdot\rangle=-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. In particular we may identify
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{u}^{*}
by means of
-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and so we may of think the momentum map as
\mathfrak{u}
-valued map.

Let
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
be a compatible subgroup of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
. Then we have
\mathfrak{p}\subset\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
. If
z\in Z
, then the orthogonal projection of
\textbf{i}\mu(z)
onto
\mathfrak{p}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defines a
K
-equivariant map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}
. In other words, we define
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
requiring that for any
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
, we have

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(z):=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(z),\beta\rangle=\langle\textbf{i}\mu(z),\beta\rangle=-\langle\mu(z),-\textbf{i}\beta\rangle=\mu^{-\textbf{i}\beta}(z).

The map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}
is called the
G
-gradient map associated with
\mu
. The equation
\mathrm{d}\mu^{\xi}=i_{\xi_{Z}}\omega
is equivalent to
\mathrm{grad}\,\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{Z}
for any
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
. The gradient is computed with respect to the Riemannian structure given by the Kähler form
\omega
on
Z
, i.e.,
(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)
.
For the rest of this paper, we fix a
G
-invariant locally closed real submanifold
X
of
Z
and we also denote the restriction of
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
to
X
by
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}.
Then
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}:X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}
is a
K
-equivariant map such that
\text{grad}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{X}
. Here the gradient is now computed with respect to the induced Riemannian metric on
X
that we also denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Similarly,
\bot
denotes perpendicularity relative to the Riemannian metric on
X.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}
is compatible with respect to the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}
, then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}}

\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}=\{v\in\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{C}}:\,\langle v,\mathfrak{g}\rangle=0\}
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{p}
be an Abelian subalgebra. Then the
A=\exp(\mathfrak{a})
-gradient map on

is given by
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}=\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}\circ\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{p}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{a}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathfrak{p}
onto
\mathfrak{a}
. We recall some of the properties of the gradient map.

For any subspace
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}
and
x\in X,
let

\mathfrak{m}\cdot x:=\{\xi_{X}(x):\xi\in\mathfrak{m}\}

and let
\mathfrak{p}_{x}:=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{p}:\,\xi_{X}(x)=0\}
. We denote by
G_{x}
and
K_{x}
the stabilizer subgroup of
x\in X
with respect to the
G
-action and the

-action respectively and by
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
and
\mathfrak{k}_{x}
their respective Lie algebras.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x\in X
. Then
\text{Ker}\,\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=(\mathfrak{p}\cdot x)^{\bot}
.

Proof.

This follows from the basic fact that
\mathrm{grad}\,\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{X}
for any
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

As a corollary, one can prove the following result.

Corollary 2.4.1
.

Let
x\in X.
The following are equivalent:

a)

\mathrm{(}d\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{x}:T_{x}X\to\mathfrak{p}
is onto.

b)

(\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{x}:\mathfrak{p}\cdot x\to\mathfrak{p}

c)

\mathfrak{p}_{x}=\{0\}
.

Since
\mathrm{grad}\,\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(x)=\beta_{X}(x)
, the following result is easy to check.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
x\in X
and let
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}.
Then either
\beta_{X}(x)=0
or the function
t\mapsto\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(\exp(t\beta)x)
is strictly increasing.

Corollary 2.5.1
.

If
x\in X
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{p},
then
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(\beta)x)=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)
if and only if
\beta_{X}(x)=0.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(\beta)x)=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x).
Let
y(t)=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(\exp(t\beta)x)
. By the above Lemma,
y(0)=y(1)=0
if and only if
\beta_{X}(x)=0
, concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

2.3.
The Kempf-Ness Function

Given
G
a real reductive group which acts smoothly on
Z;

G=K\text{exp}(\mathfrak{p}),
where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G.
Let
X
be a
G
-invariant locally closed submanifold of
Z.
As Mundet pointed out in
[
27
]
, there exists a function
\Phi:X\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
such that

\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x),\xi\rangle=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{dt}}\bigg|_{t=0}\Phi(x,\exp(t\xi)),\qquad\xi\in\mathfrak{p},

and satisfying the following conditions:

a)

For any
x\in X,
the function
\Phi(x,.)
is smooth on
G.

b)

The function
\Phi(x,.)
is left-invariant with respect to
K,
i.e.,
\Phi(x,kg)=\Phi(x,g).

c)

For any
x\in X,

v\in\mathfrak{p}
and
t\in\mathbb{R};

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Phi(x,\exp(tv))\geq 0.

Moreover:

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Phi(x,\exp(tv))=0

if and only if
\exp(\mathbb{R}v)\subset G_{x}.

d)

For any
x\in X,
and any
g,h\in G;

\Phi(x,hg)=\Phi(x,g)+\Phi(gx,h).

This equation is called the cocycle condition. Finally, using the cocycle condition, we have

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{dt}}\Phi(x,\exp(t\beta))=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\xi)x),\beta\rangle.

The function

is called the Kempf-Ness function for
(X,G,K)
. It is just the restriction of the classical Kempf-Ness function

considered in
[

,
32
]
to

[
4
]
.

and
\pi:G\rightarrow M.

is a symmetric space of non-compact type
[

]
. Let

. By property

the function

descends to a function on

\Phi_{x}:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad\Phi_{x}(gK):=\Phi(x,g^{-1}).

.

[

]

This means that

Definition 3.2
.

\lambda(x,\beta)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda(x,\beta,t)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}.

From the proof of Lemma

\frac{d}{dt}\lambda(x,\beta,t)=\parallel\beta_{X}(\exp(t\beta)x)\parallel^{2},

and so,

\lambda(x,\beta,t)=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x),\beta\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\beta_{X}(\exp(s\beta)x)\parallel^{2}\mathrm{ds}.

For any
x\in X
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}

defined by

is given by

E(c_{x}^{\beta})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\parallel\beta_{X}(\exp(t\beta)x)\parallel^{2}\mathrm{dt}.

Thus,

\lambda(x,\beta)=\lambda(x,\beta,0)+E(c_{x}^{\beta}),

Let

Since

By the convexity property of

We get,

has a subsequence which converges to, say

Taking the limit of (

∎

The following result is the moment weight inequality which is an important ingredient to prove our results. We give a proof of this inequality applying the idea in

Theorem 3.14
.

(Moment-Weight Inequality). For every
x\in X,

\beta\in\mathfrak{p}\backslash\{0\}
and
g\in G,

\frac{-\lambda(x,\beta)}{\parallel\beta\parallel}\leq\parallel\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx)\parallel.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}\backslash\{0\}
, and
g\in G.
If
\lambda(x,\beta)=+\infty
, then the result follows. Assume that
\lambda(x,\beta)<+\infty
. By Proposition
3.9
,
\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\exp(t\beta)x
exists.

Define
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{p}
and
k:[0,\infty)\to K
such that

(5)

\exp(\alpha(t))k(t)=\exp(t\beta)g^{-1}.

We claim that

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}=\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}.

To prove this, observe that
\exp(-\alpha(t))\exp(t\beta)g^{-1}\in K.
Then from Lemma C.2 in
[
9
]
, there is a positive constant
c
such that
\parallel t\beta-\alpha(t)\parallel\leq c
for all
t\in[0,\infty).
Therefore, for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}-\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\frac{t\beta}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}-\frac{\alpha(t)}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}+\frac{\alpha(t)}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}-\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\parallel t\beta-\alpha(t)\parallel}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}+\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel\left|\frac{1}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}-\frac{1}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\parallel t\beta-\alpha(t)\parallel}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}+\frac{|\parallel t\beta\parallel-\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel|}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c}{t\parallel\beta\parallel}.

Therefore

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}=\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}.

For any
t\in(0,+\infty)
, applying Lemma
2.5
, the function

s\mapsto g(s)=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp\left(sk^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\right)gx),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle

is nondecreasing. In particular

g(0)=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle\leq g(1)=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(\exp(k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t))gx\right),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle.

Therefore, keeping in mind that
\exp(k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t))=k^{-1}(t)\exp(t\beta)
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
K
-equivariant, we have

\displaystyle-\parallel\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx)\parallel

\displaystyle\leq\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel^{-1}\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel^{-1}\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t))gx),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel^{-1}\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(k^{-1}(t)\exp(t\beta)x),k^{-1}(t)\alpha(t)k(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\beta)x),\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\beta)x),\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}-\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}+\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\parallel\beta\parallel^{-1}\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\beta)x),\beta\rangle+\left\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\beta)x),\frac{\alpha(t)}{\parallel\alpha(t)\parallel}-\frac{\beta}{\parallel\beta\parallel}\right\rangle.

Since
\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\exp(t\beta)x
exists,
taking the limit
t\to+\infty,
we have

-\parallel\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx)\parallel\leq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(\exp(t\beta)x),\beta\rangle}{\parallel\beta\parallel}=\frac{\lambda(x,\beta)}{\parallel\beta\parallel}.

Hence,

\frac{-\lambda(x,\beta)}{\parallel\beta\parallel}\leq\parallel\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(gx)\parallel

∎

Lemma 4.5

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\begin{split}\dot{f}(t)&=\omega\big((\xi_{\mathfrak{p}})_{X}(x(t)),J(\nu_{X}(x(t)))\big)+\omega\big((\xi_{\mathfrak{k}})_{X}(x(t)),J(\nu_{X}(x(t))\big)\\

Let

The maximal compact subgroup
\mathrm{U}(n)
of

[

n

\mathbb{C}^{n+m}

. If

is injective, then

is polystable. Indeed,

is stable. If

, p. 1043]

, where

\ \ \ \ \lambda<0\end{array}}V_{\lambda},

h\mathrm{Tr}(\beta)<0
. Then

.

\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{O}(n)\exp(\mathfrak{p})

\mu:Z\to\mathfrak{u}.
The stabilities conditions depend on the choice of a maximal compact subgroup

Let

be compatible. Let

. Then
\omega_{g}=(g^{-1})^{\star}\omega
is a Kähler form and

preserves

is
\mathrm{Ad}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
-invariant,

restricted to
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, respectively
\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, is negative-define, respectively positive-define.

Lemma 6.1
.

The

-action on
(Z,\omega_{g})
is Hamiltonian with momentum map
\mu^{\prime}:Z\longrightarrow\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, given by

\mu^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ad}(g)\circ\mu\circ g^{-1}.

Proof.

We prove that
\mu^{\prime}
is

-equivariant. Let

. Then

\begin{split}\mu^{\prime}(ghg^{-1}x)&=\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\mu(hg^{-1}x))\\
&=\mathrm{Ad}(g)\big(\mathrm{Ad}(h)(\mu(g^{-1}x)\big)\\
&=\mathrm{Ad}(ghg^{-1})\big(\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\mu(g^{-1}x))\big)\\
&=\mathrm{Ad}(ghg^{-1})(\mu^{\prime}(x))\end{split}

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{u}
. Then

\begin{split}(\mu^{\prime})^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)}(z)&=-\langle\mu^{\prime}(z),\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)\rangle\\
&=-\langle\mu(g^{-1}z),\xi\rangle,\end{split}

and so

\mathrm{d}(\mu^{\prime})^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi))}=\mathrm{d}\mu^{\xi}\circ\mathrm{d}g^{-1}
. Therefore

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}(\mu^{\prime})_{z}^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi))}&=\omega(\xi_{Z}(g^{-1}z),\mathrm{d}g^{-1}\cdot)\\
&=\omega(\mathrm{d}g^{-1}\big((\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)_{Z}(z)\big),\mathrm{d}g^{-1}\cdot)\\
&=\omega_{g}(\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)_{Z}(z),\cdot).\end{split}

∎

Let
X
be a
G
-invariant submanifold. Let
\mathtt{g}
denote the Riemannian metric induced by the Kähler form
\omega
.
For any
g\in G
, we have a triple
(U^{\prime},(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g},\mu^{\prime})
, where
U^{\prime}=gUg^{-1}
. Note that
G
is also compatible with respect to the Cartan decomposition
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U^{\prime}\exp(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
. Indeed,
G=gGg^{-1}=K^{\prime}\exp(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
, where
K^{\prime}=gKg^{-1}=G\cap U^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{g}\cap\textbf{i}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
. The associated gradient map
\mu^{\prime}:X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is the orthogonal projection of
i\mu^{\prime}
onto
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
with respect to
B
. One can check

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Ad}(g)\circ\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ g^{-1}.

Following Teleman
[
32
]
, we say that such triples define a
symplectization
of the
U^{\mathbb{C}}
-action on
Z
with respect to
G
. A priori the concept of energy completeness condition depends on the choice of a triple. The following result shows that the notion of energy completeness does not depend on the triple chosen.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
(U,\mathtt{g},\mu)
is energy complete then
(U^{\prime},(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g},\mu^{\prime})
is energy complete as well.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and let
x\in X
. We recall that
c_{x}^{\xi}
denotes the curve
c_{x}^{\xi}(t)=\exp(t\xi)x
. Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{p}
. Since

c_{x}^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)}=g\circ c_{g^{-1}x}^{\xi},

it follows that the energy of
c_{x}^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)}
with respect to
(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g}
coincides with the energy of
c_{g^{-1}x}^{\xi}
with respect to
\mathtt{g}
. Moreover, the limit

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}c_{x}^{\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)}(t)
exists if and only if the limit
\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}c_{g^{-1}x}^{\xi}(t)
does.
∎

We claim that the stable, polystable and semistable conditions do not depend on the triple chosen. It is a consequence of the following easy Lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
x\in X
. Then
x\in\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{-1}(0)
if and only if
g^{-1}x\in\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(x)=0
if and only if for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{p}
we have
\langle(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(x),\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\xi)\rangle=0
hence if and only if
\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(g^{-1}x),\xi\rangle=0
and so the result follows.
∎

Corollary 6.3.1
.

Let
x\in X
. Then

a)

G\cdot x\cap\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
G\cdot x\cap(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
;

b)

\overline{G\cdot x}\cap\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\overline{G\cdot x}\cap(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
.

The norm square gradient map depends on the choice of a triple
(U,\mathtt{g},\mu)
. Indeed,

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x),\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\rangle.

Let
g\in G
and let
(U^{\prime},(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g},\mu^{\prime})
another triple. We denote by
f^{\prime}
the norm square of
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
. Since
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant, it follows that

f^{\prime}(x)=f(g^{-1}x)
. Moreover,
x
is a critical point of
f^{\prime}
if and only if
g^{-1}x
is a critical point of
f
. This implies that
K\cdot\beta
is a critical orbit of
f
if and only if
K^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\beta)
is a critical orbit of
f^{\prime}
. From now on, we assume that
X
is compact and connected. Then the negative gradient flow line of the norm square is defined in all the real line. Moreover, if
x:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow X
denotes the negative gradient flow line, then
\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}x(t)
exists
[
3
]
. It is a straightforward computation that the gradient
\mathrm{grad}\,f^{\prime}(x)
with respect to
(g^{-1})^{\star}\mathtt{g}
is given by
(\mathrm{d}g)_{g^{-1}x}\big(\mathrm{grad}\,f(g^{-1}x)\big)
. Therefore, if
x(t)
is the negative gradient flow line of
f
, then
g(x(t))
is the negative gradient flow line of
f^{\prime}
.

Let
x_{\infty}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}x(t)
and let
\beta=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{\infty})
. Then
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(gx_{\infty})=\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\beta)
and
f(x_{\infty})=f^{\prime}(gx_{\infty})
. By
[
3
, Theorem 4.9]
, the stratum of associated to
K\cdot\beta
with respect to
f
coincides with the stratum associated to
K^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}(g)(\beta)
with respect to
f^{\prime}
. Summing up we have proved the following result.

Theorem 6.4
.

If
X
is connected and compact,
then the decomposition of
X
in strata associated to critical orbits of the norm square gradient map does not depend on the triple chosen.

Abstract
. In this paper we investigate the Prawitz’s area theorem and establish a new univalence criterion for the locally univalent analytic functions in the unit disk, which generalizes some related results of Aharonov [ Duke Math. J., 36(1969), 599-604 ].

Primary 30C55; Secondary 30C62.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
We denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
the extended complex plane. Let
\mathbb{D}_{e}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be the
exterior of
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}=\partial\mathbb{D}_{e}
be the unit circle.

We say
f
is a univalent analytic (meromorphic) function in an open domain
\Omega
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, if
f
is a one-to-one and analytic (meromorphic) function in
\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
be the class of all univalent analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
. We denote by
S
the class of all univalent analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.

We denote by
\Sigma
the class of all univalent meromorphic functions
g
in
\mathbb{D}_{e}
with the following expansion
at infinity as

g(z)=z+b_{0}+\frac{b_{1}}{z}+\frac{b_{2}}{z^{2}}+\cdots.

It is well known that the area theorems play an important role in the study of the theory of univalent analytic (meromorphic) functions. The first area theorem was proved by Grönwall in 1914, see
[
13
, page 18]
, which states that

Theorem 1.1
.

If
g\in\Sigma
, then we have

(1.1)

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{e}}|g^{\prime}(z)-1|^{2}dxdy=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|^{2}\leq 1.

Equality holds in (
1.1
) if and only if
g
is a full mapping.

Remark 1.2
.

Here we say
g
is a full mapping if the Lebesgue measure of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus g(\mathbb{D}_{e})
is zero.

If
f\in S
, then we see that
g(z)=\frac{1}{f(z^{-1})}
belongs to
\Sigma
. It follows from Theorem
1.1
that

Theorem 1.3
.

If
f\in S
, then we have

(1.2)

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{2}-1\right|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{|z|^{4}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq 1.

Where

\frac{1}{f(z)}=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

Equality holds in (
1.2
) if and only if
f
is a full mapping.

Remark 1.4
.

Here we say
f
is a full mapping if the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{C}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
is zero.

In 1927, Prawitz proved in
[
14
]
a generalized area theorem(see also
[
12
, page 13]
). Let
\lambda>0
. Let
g\in\Sigma
with
g(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}_{e}
. We see that

\left[\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]^{\lambda}=\exp\left[\lambda\ln\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]

is analytic in
\mathbb{D}_{e}
. Here the logarithm expression is determined uniquely by requiring that it is analytic in
\mathbb{D}_{e}
and takes the value zero at infinity.

Then we have the following Prawitz’s area theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Let
g\in\Sigma
with
g(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}_{e}
. Then we have

(1.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)|b_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}\leq\lambda.

Where

\left[\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]^{\lambda}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}(\lambda)z^{-n}.

Equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
g
is a full mapping.

If
f\in S
, then we get that
g(z)=\frac{1}{f(z^{-1})}\in\Sigma
with
g(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}_{e}
and

\frac{z}{f(z)}=\frac{g(z^{-1})}{z^{-1}},\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

From Theorem
1.5
, we obtain the following version of Prawitz’s area theorem for the class
S
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Let
f\in S
. Then we have

(1.4)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)|a_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}\leq\lambda.

Where

\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(\lambda)z^{n}.

Equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
f
is a full mapping.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.5
, when
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we first notice that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lambda\left[g^{\prime}(z)\left[\frac{z}{g(z)}\right]^{1-\lambda}-1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\frac{d}{dz}\left[\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]^{\lambda}+\lambda\left[\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]^{\lambda}-\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)b_{n}(\lambda)z^{-n}.

On the other hand, we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Delta^{*}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)b_{n}(\lambda)z^{-n}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{|z|^{2(1-\lambda)}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Delta^{*}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)^{2}|b_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}|z|^{-2n+2\lambda-2}dxdy

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)^{2}|b_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2n+2\lambda-2}rdr

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)|b_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}.

Therefore, we see from (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) that the Prawitz’s inequality (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

(1.7)

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Delta^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\left[\frac{z}{g(z)}\right]^{1-\lambda}-1\right|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{|z|^{2(1-\lambda)}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda},

for
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Note that (
1.7
) becomes (
1.1
) when
\lambda=1
.

Replacing
g
in (
1.7
) by
\frac{1}{f(z^{-1})}
for
f\in S
, we obtain that

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Delta^{*}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z^{-1})}{[zf(z^{-1}]^{2}}\left[zf(z^{-1})\right]^{1-\lambda}-1\right|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{|z|^{2(1-\lambda)}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda},

for
\lambda\in(0,1]
.
By the change of variables
z=\frac{1}{w}
, we get that

(1.8)

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(w)\left[\frac{w}{f(w)}\right]^{1+\lambda}-1\right|^{2}\frac{dudv}{|w|^{2(1+\lambda)}}dudv\leq\frac{1}{\lambda},

for
\lambda\in(0,1]
.

For
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we set

(1.9)

\sigma_{z}(\zeta):=\frac{\zeta+z}{1+\bar{z}\zeta},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}.

Now, let
f\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
, the Koebe transformation
\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta)
of
f
is defined as

(1.10)

\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta):=\frac{f(\sigma_{z}(\zeta))-f(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{f\left(\frac{\zeta+z}{1+\bar{z}\zeta}\right)-f(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}.

We see that
\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta)
belongs to
S
.
By using
\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta)
to be instead of
f
in (
1.8
), we get that

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(\sigma_{z}(\zeta))}{f^{\prime}(z)(1+\bar{z}\zeta)^{2}}\left[\frac{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\zeta}{f(\sigma_{z}(\zeta))-f(z)}\right]^{1+\lambda}-1\right|^{2}\frac{d\xi d\eta}{|\zeta|^{2(1+\lambda)}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Then, after the change of variables
\zeta=\frac{w-z}{1-\bar{z}w}
, we obtain that, for
\lambda\in(0,1]
,

(1.11)

\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2\lambda}}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left|P(f;z,w)\right|^{2}\frac{dudv}{|w-z|^{2(1+\lambda)}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Here

P(f;z,w)=\frac{f^{\prime}(w)(w-z)}{f(w)-f(z)}\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(w-z)}{f(w)-f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}-\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\bar{z}w}\right)^{1-\lambda},\,z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Remark 1.7
.

From the above arguments, we see that the Prawitz’s inequality (
1.11
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
. The inequaity (
1.11
) has appeared in
[
9
]
, where (
1.11
) was used to study the integral means spectrum of univalent analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 1.8
.

When
\lambda=1
, (
1.11
) reduces to

\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|U(f;z,w)|^{2}dudv\leq 1.

Here

U(f;z,w)=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(w)}{[f(w)-f(z)]^{2}}-\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}},\,\,z,w\in\mathbb{D},

is known as Grunsky kernel. We set

U_{f}(z):=\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|U(f;z,w)|^{2}dudv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is known that
U(f;z,w)
and
U_{f}(z)
also play an important role in the study of univalent analytic (meromorphic) functions and the universal Teichmüller space, see for example
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
.

In this note, motivated by the work of Aharonov
[
1
]
and by using the Prawitz’s area theorem, we establish a new univalence criterion for the locally univalent analytic functions in the unit disk. The univalence criterion
will be given in Section 2 and the proof of our main results will be presented in Section 3. In Section 4, we will give the final remarks and raise some questions.

2.
A univalence criterion for the locally univalent analytic functions in the unit disk

Astala and Gehring have studied in
[
2
]
the following

Problem 2.1
.

For a locally univalent analytic (or meromorphic ) function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, what additional conditions on
f
allow one to conclude that
f
is univalent?

In this note we continue to consider this problem. Motivated by the classical work of Aharonov
[
1
]
and by investigating the Prawitz’s area theorem, we establish a new univalence criterion for the locally univalent analytic functions in the unit disk. We first recall Aharonov’s results proved in
[
1
]
.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
the class of all locally univalent meromorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. We note that any pole of the function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
is of the first order.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
the class of all local univalent analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, i.e.,
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
is contained in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
with it is not the pole of
f
, we take a point
w
, which is close enough to
z
, and define the sequence
\{\phi_{n}(f;z)\}
by the expansion of the following generating function,

(2.1)

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(w)-f(z)}=\frac{1}{w-z}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(f;z)(w-z)^{n}.

The sequence
\{\phi_{n}(f;z)\}
is called as
Aharonov sequence
, which has some fine properties.

We have

(2.2)

\phi_{0}(f;z)=-\frac{1}{2}N_{f}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)};

(2.3)

\phi_{1}(f;z)=-\frac{1}{6}S_{f}(z)=-\frac{1}{6}\left[\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}\right];

(2.4)

\phi_{n+1}(f;z)=\frac{1}{n+3}\left[\phi^{\prime}_{n}(f;z)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\phi_{k}(f;z)\phi_{n-k}(f;z)\right],\,n\geq 1.

Here
S_{f}
is the Schwarzian derivative of
f
, and
N_{f}
is called Pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
. It is well known that the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
is Möbius invariant, see
[
10
, Chapter II]
. It follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that any
\phi_{n}(f;z)(n\geq 1)
is Möbius invariant in the sense that

\phi_{n}(\tau\circ f;z)=\phi_{n}(f;z)

for all Möbius transformation
\tau
.

Remark 2.2
.

When
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
has a first order pole
z
, we can define
\phi_{n}(f;z)
in view of (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) for all
n\geq 1
.

From
[
1
]
, we have

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent meromorphic in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds that

(2.5)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k+1}\phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|^{2}\leq 1.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent meromorphic and a full mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k+1}\phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|^{2}\equiv 1.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent meromorphic in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, there is a positive constant
C(\zeta)
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k+1}\phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|\leq C(\zeta).

Remark 2.6
.

In the above theorems, the combinatorial number
\binom{\alpha}{m}
is defined as

\binom{\alpha}{m}:=\frac{(\alpha)_{m}}{m!}=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-m+1)}{m!}.

Where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
m
is a non-negative integer number. In particular, we have

\binom{\alpha}{0}=1\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,\binom{-\alpha}{m}=(-1)^{m}\binom{\alpha+m-1}{m},

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. For
\lambda>0
, we next consider the following generating function and its expansion,

(2.6)

\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(w-z)}{f(w)-f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{\lambda,n}(f;z)(w-z)^{n}.

For the sake of simplicity, we will occasionally use
\Phi_{n}(f;z)
or
\Phi_{n}
to denote
\Phi_{\lambda,n}(f;z)
, and use
\phi_{n}
to denote
\phi_{n}(f;z)
.

From (
2.1
), we get that

\displaystyle\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(w-z)}{f(w)-f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(w-z)^{n+1}\right]^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{\lambda}{n}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(w-z)^{k+1}\right]^{n}.

Then we see that
\Phi_{0}\equiv 1
and

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(w-z)^{n}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{\lambda}{n}(w-z)^{n}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(w-z)^{k}\right]^{n}.

Note that

\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(w-z)^{k}\right]^{n}=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{n}=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{2}}\cdots\phi_{k_{n}}(w-z)^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}},\,n\geq 1.

It follows that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{\lambda}{j}\sum_{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{j})\in I(n,j)}\phi_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{2}}\cdots\phi_{k_{j}},\,n\geq 1.

Here
I(n,j)
is the set of all
j
-tuples
(k_{1},\cdots,k_{j})
of non-negative integers with
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=n-j
.

In particular, we have

\Phi_{1}(f;z)=\lambda\phi_{0}(f;z)=-\frac{\lambda}{2}N_{f}(z);

\Phi_{2}(f;z)=\lambda\phi_{1}(f;z)+\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{2}\phi_{0}^{2}(f;z)=-\frac{\lambda}{6}S_{f}(z)+\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{8}N_{f}^{2}(z).

Remark 2.7
.

We get from (
2.7
) that
\Phi_{n}(n\geq 0)
is affine invariant in the sense that

\Phi_{n}(\chi\circ f;z)=\Phi_{n}(f;z)

for all affine transformation
\chi(z)=az+b
since
\phi_{0}
is affine invariant and
\phi_{n}(n\geq 1)
is Möbius invariant.
From this fact, we see that we can’t define
\Phi_{n}(f;z)
if
f
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
and
z
is a pole of
f
.

We now state our main results of this paper.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent analytic in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds that

(2.8)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}\Phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|^{2}\leq\lambda.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent analytic and a full mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds that

(2.9)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}\Phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|^{2}\equiv\lambda.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f
is univalent analytic in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, there is a positive constant
C(\lambda,\zeta)
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

(2.10)

\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}\Phi_{k}(f;\zeta)\right|\leq C(\lambda,\zeta).

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.8
,
2.9
and
2.10

We need the following lemmas in our proof of Theorem
2.8
,
2.9
and
2.10
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
. Then we have

(3.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-\lambda)|\Phi_{n}(f;0)|^{2}\leq\lambda.

Equality holds in (
3.1
) if and only if
f
is a full mapping.

Proof.

We may assume that
f\in S
since
\Phi_{n}(n\geq 0)
is affine invariant. Noticing that

(3.2)

\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}=\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(0)(z-0)}{f(z)-f(0)}\right]^{\lambda}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;0)z^{n},\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then the lemma follows by using Theorem
1.6
. The proof of Lemma
3.1
is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. Let
w,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
be fixed and let
z=\sigma_{\zeta}(w)=\frac{w+\zeta}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}
. We set

\mathbf{F}(w)=f(z).

Then we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F};w)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}\frac{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n+k}}\Phi_{k}(f;z),\,n\geq 0.

Proof.

Choose
\Delta z
with
|\Delta z|
small enough, we define
\Delta w
by

z+\Delta z=\frac{(w+\Delta w)+\zeta}{1+\bar{\zeta}(w+\Delta w)}.

Then we get that

(3.4)

\Delta z=\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})\Delta w}{[1+\bar{\zeta}(w+\Delta w)](1+\bar{\zeta}w)}=\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{-\bar{\zeta}(1+\bar{\zeta}w)}\frac{d}{1-d},\,d=\frac{-\bar{\zeta}}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}\Delta w,

and

\frac{\Delta w}{\Delta z}=\left(1+\frac{\bar{\zeta}\Delta w}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}\right)\frac{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(w)}{\mathbf{F}(w+\Delta w)-\mathbf{F}(w)}=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z+\Delta z)-f(z)}\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(w)\Delta w}{\mathbf{F}(w+\Delta w)-\mathbf{F}(w)}\right]^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)\Delta z}{f(z+\Delta z)-f(z)}\frac{\Delta w}{\Delta z}\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{2}}\right]^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)\Delta z}{f(z+\Delta z)-f(z)}\right]^{\lambda}\left[1+\frac{\bar{\zeta}\Delta w}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}\right]^{\lambda}.

Thus we obtain that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F};w)(\Delta w)^{n}=\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)(\Delta z)^{n}\right]\left[1+\frac{\bar{\zeta}\Delta w}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}\right]^{\lambda}.

On the other hand, from (
3.4
), we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)(\Delta z)^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{n}}{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n}(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n}}\cdot d^{n}{(1-d)^{-n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{n}}{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n}(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n}}\cdot d^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{-n}{m}{(-d)}^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{n}}{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n}(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n}}\cdot d^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m-1}{m}{d}^{m}

Then we get that the coefficient of
(\Delta w)^{j}
in (
3.6
) is

\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}\frac{(-\bar{\zeta})^{j-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{j+k}}\Phi_{k}(f;z),\,j\geq 0.

Note that

\left[1+\frac{\bar{\zeta}\Delta w}{1+\bar{\zeta}w}\right]^{\lambda}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{\lambda}{n}\frac{{\bar{\zeta}}^{n}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n}}(\Delta w)^{n}.

It follows that the coefficient of
(\Delta w)^{n}
in the right side of (
3.5
) is

(3.7)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}\frac{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}w)^{n+k}}\Phi_{k}(f;z),\,n\geq 0.

Thus, from (
3.5
), we find that (
3.3
) holds. The lemma is proved.
∎

In the rest of this section, we present the proof of our main results of this paper. We will use the same notation as in Lemma

.

) and (

When

Meanwhile, it’s obvious that

Remarks and questions

Remark 4.1
.

We first remark that our results generalize some related ones of Aharonov in
[
1
]
. Let
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. When
\lambda=1
, from (
2.1
) and (
2.6
), we have

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(w-z)}{f(w)-f(z)}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}(f;z)(w-z)^{n}=1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(f;z)(w-z)^{n+1}.

It follows that
\Phi_{n}(f;z)=\phi_{n-1}(f;z)
for
n\geq 1
. Then we see from
[
1
, Theorem 2]
that the inequality (
2.5
) is equivalent to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\phi_{n}(\mathbf{F};0)|^{2}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F};0)|^{2}\leq 1.

Where
\mathbf{F}
is defined as in Lemma
3.2
.

On the other hand, when
\lambda=1
, we see from Lemma
3.2
that the inequality (
2.8
) is equivalent to

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F};0)|^{2}\leq 1.

From these, we can see Theorem
2.8
as a generalization of Theorem
2.3
if we assume that
f
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{D})
. By the same reason, Theorem
2.9
and
2.10
also can be seen as some kind of extensions of Theorem
2.4
and
2.5
, respectively.

Remark 4.2
.

In 1982, Harmelin proved the following property for the Aharonov sequence, see
[
8
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
. Then we have

(1-|z|^{2})^{n+1}|\phi_{n}(f;z)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{|z|^{n-k}}{\sqrt{k}},z\in\mathbb{D},\,n\geq 1.

We shall prove a similar result for the
\Phi_{n}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
f\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{D})
. Then we have

(4.1)

(1-|z|^{2})^{n}|\Phi_{n}(f;z)|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{|k-\lambda|}}|{z}|^{n-k},z\in\mathbb{D},\,n\geq 1.

Proof.

For fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. We set

\mathbf{F}_{1}(w)=f\left(\frac{w-\zeta}{1-\bar{\zeta}w}\right),

and
w=\sigma_{\zeta}(z)=\frac{z+\zeta}{1+\bar{\zeta}z}
.
Hence we see that
f(z)=F_{1}\circ\sigma_{\zeta}(z).
Then, by Lemma
3.2
, we get that, for
n\geq 0
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(f;z)=\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{1}\circ\sigma_{\zeta};z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}\frac{(-\bar{\zeta})^{n-k}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{k}}{(1+\bar{\zeta}z)^{n+k}}\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{1};w).

Taking
\zeta=-z
in (
4.2
), we obtain that, for
n\geq 0
,

(4.3)

\Phi_{n}(f;z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{\lambda}{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j-1}{j-k}(\bar{z})^{n-k}(1-|z|^{2})^{-n}\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{1};0).

On the other hand, from (
3.9
), we know that, for any
n\geq 1
,

\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{1};0)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{n-\lambda}}.

Also, noting that
\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{1};0)=1
, so we can write, for any
n\geq 0,

(4.4)

\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{1};0)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{|n-\lambda|}}.

Then (
4.1
) follows by (
4.4
) and (
4.3
). The proposition is proved.
∎

Finally, we consider the univalent analytic functions with a quasiconformal extension to the whole complex plane. We refer the reader to
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
for the introduction to the theory of quasiconformal mappings.

Let
f
be a univalent analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
, which can be extended to a quasiconformal mapping(still denoted by
f
) in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. We denote by
\mu_{f}(z)
the complex dilatation of
f
. Let
\mathbf{r}
be a Riemann mapping from
\mathbb{D}_{e}
to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-\overline{f(\mathbb{D})}
. We see that
\mathbf{r}
can be extended to a homeomorphism(still denoted by
\mathbf{r}
) from
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-\mathbb{D}
to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-f(\mathbb{D})
. Then
h=f^{-1}\circ\mathbf{r}|_{\mathbb{T}}
is a quasisymmetric homeomorphism from
\mathbb{T}
to itself. It follows that
\bar{f}=\mathbf{r}^{-1}\circ f|_{\mathbb{D}_{e}}
is a quasiconformal extension of
h^{-1}

to
\mathbb{D}_{e}
and
\bar{f}
has the same complex dilatation as
f
in
\mathbb{D}_{e}
. We let
\tilde{f}=\rho\circ\bar{f}\circ\rho|_{\mathbb{D}}
, here
\rho(z)={\bar{z}}^{-1}
.
Then we see that
\tilde{f}
is a quasiconformal extension of
h^{-1}
to
\mathbb{D}
. Denote by
\mu_{\tilde{f}}(z)
the complex dilatation of
\tilde{f}
, we get that

|\mu_{\tilde{f}}(z)|=|\mu_{f}({\bar{z}}^{-1})|,\,z\in\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, by Corollary 2.2 in
[
18
]
, we obtain that

(4.5)

U_{f}^{2}(z)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\mu_{f}({\bar{w}}^{-1})|^{2}}{1-|\mu_{f}({\bar{w}}^{-1})|^{2}}\frac{dudv}{|1-\bar{z}w|^{4}}.

On the other hand, for fixed
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we know that the Koebe transformation
\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta)
of
{f}
, defined as in (
1.10
), belongs to
S
. Then we see that
\mathbf{G}_{f}(z;\zeta):=\frac{1}{\mathbf{K}_{f}(z;\zeta^{-1})}\in\Sigma
and set

(4.6)

\mathbf{G}_{f}(z;\zeta)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2}){f}^{\prime}(z)}{f\left(\sigma_{z}(\zeta^{-1})\right)-{f}(z)}=\zeta+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Psi_{n}({f};z)\zeta^{-n},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}_{e}-\{\infty\}.

Then, by checking the arguments of the derivation of the Prawitz’s inequality (
1.11
) presented in the Introduction, we get that

(4.7)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\Psi_{n}(f;z)|^{2}=(1-|z|^{2})^{2}U_{f}^{2}(z).

Combining (
4.5
), (
4.7

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\Psi_{n}(f;z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}U_{f}^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\mu_{f}({\bar{w}}^{-1})|^{2}}{1-|\mu_{f}({\bar{w}}^{-1})|^{2}}\frac{dudv}{|1-\bar{z}w|^{4}}.

Also, we see from (
2.1
) that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f\left(\sigma_{z}({\zeta}^{-1})\right)-f(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{1+z\zeta}{\zeta+\bar{z}}-z}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(f;z)(\frac{1+z\zeta}{\zeta+\bar{z}}{-z)}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta+\bar{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(f;z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}\zeta^{-n}(1+\frac{\bar{z}}{\zeta})^{-n}.

Note that

(4.10)

(1+\frac{\bar{z}}{\zeta})^{-n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-n}{k}{\bar{z}}^{k}{\zeta}^{-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k-1}{k}{(-\bar{z})}^{k}{\zeta}^{-k}.

It follows from (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{f}(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta+\bar{z}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{n}(f;z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n+1}\zeta^{-n}\right]\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k-1}{k}{(-\bar{z})}^{k}{\zeta}^{-k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta+\bar{z}+(1-|z|^{2})\phi_{0}(f;z)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{n}(f;z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n+1}\zeta^{-n}\right]\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k-1}{k}{(-\bar{z})}^{k}{\zeta}^{-k}\right].

Then we get from (
4.6

\Psi_{0}(f;z)=\bar{z}+(1-|z|^{2})\phi_{0}(f;z)=\bar{z}-\frac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})N_{f}(z);

(4.11)

\Psi_{n}(f;z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{n-k}(-\bar{z})^{n-k}(1-|z|^{2})^{k+1}\phi_{k}(f;z),\,n\geq 1.

In particular,

\Psi_{1}(f;z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\phi_{1}(f;z)=-\frac{1}{6}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}S_{f}(z).

Therefore we know that any
\Psi_{n}(n\geq 1)
is Möbius invariant like the
\phi_{n}(n\geq 1)
.

Propagating terrace in a two-tubes model of gravitational fingering YP and ST have equal contribution
Yu. Petrova, S. Tikhomirov, Ya. Efendiev

Abstract
We study a semi-discrete model for the two-dimensional incompressible porous medium (IPM) equation describing gravitational fingering phenomenon. The model consists of a system of advection-reaction-diffusion equations on concentration, velocity and pressure, describing motion of miscible liquids under the Darcy’s law in two vertical tubes (real lines) and interflow between them. Our analysis reveals the structure of gravitational fingers in this simple setting – the mixing zone consists of space-time regions of constant intermediate concentrations and the profile of propagation is characterized by two consecutive traveling waves which we call a terrace. We prove the existence of such a propagating terrace for the parameters corresponding to small distances between the tubes. This solution shows the possible mechanism of slowing down the fingers’ growth due to convection in the transversal direction. The main tool in the proof is a reduction to pressure-free transverse flow equilibrium (TFE) model using geometrical singular perturbation theory and the persistence of stable and unstable manifolds under small perturbations. Keywords. propagating terrace, traveling waves, porous media, Darcy’s law, gravitational fingering, geometric singular perturbation theory, invariant manifolds. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification. 76S05, 35C07, 34D15, 37D10.

1.2
Informal description of the model and main results

In this paper, we introduce a new model for the gravitational fingering motivated by a finite-volume scheme with a simple upwind, that is widely used in multiphase flow simulations
[
25
]
. Our basic assumption is that the fluid displacement happens inside two vertical lines (“tubes”), and the transverse flow between the tubes is governed by the discrete Darcy’s law: the velocity is equal to the pressure difference divided by the distance between the tubes. This results in a system of nonlinear reaction-diffusion-convection equations that we call the

More precisely, consider the two tubes denoted by 1 and 2. The model we study reads (see Fig.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{1}+\partial_{y}(u_{1}c_{1})-\partial_{yy}c_{1}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{2}+\partial_{y}(u_{2}c_{2})-\partial_{yy}c_{2}

\displaystyle=f.

Here
c_{1,2}=c_{1,2}(t,y)
are the concentrations and
u_{1,2}=u_{1,2}(t,y)
are the velocities in tubes 1 and 2. Meanwhile the function
f
is responsible for the flow between the tubes and is defined by

\displaystyle f=\begin{cases}\cfrac{u_{T}}{l}\cdot c_{1},&u_{T}\geq 0,\quad\text{(fluid flows from tube 1 to 2)},\\
\cfrac{u_{T}}{l}\cdot c_{2},&u_{T}\leq 0,\quad\text{(fluid flows from tube 2 to 1)}.\end{cases}

Here
u_{T}=u_{T}(t,y)
is the velocity of the fluid in the transversal direction to the tubes (

can be interpreted as a distance between the tubes (or the cell size in
x

, analogous to equations (
2

3

for the complete description and Appendix

for the schematic derivation of the model from the finite-volume scheme.

3.1

3.1.1
Traveling wave dynamical system. Derivation

We are looking for a traveling wave solution
c_{1,2}(t,y)=\tilde{c}_{1,2}(y-vt)

of the system (
9
)–(
11
), (
14
), (
18
), connecting the states
(c_{1}^{-},c_{2}^{-})
and
(c_{1}^{+},c_{2}^{+})

(for simplicity we omit tildas)

\displaystyle c_{1,2}(t,y)=c_{1,2}(\xi),

\displaystyle\xi=y-vt,

\displaystyle c_{1,2}(\pm\infty)=c_{1,2}^{\pm}.

In the above notation the system (
9
)–(
10
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}c_{1}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}(u_{1}c_{1})-f+\partial_{\xi\xi}c_{1},

(19)

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}c_{2}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}(u_{2}c_{2})+f+\partial_{\xi\xi}c_{2}.

(20)

Let us rewrite these two second order ODEs as a system of four first order ODEs using new unknown variables
(a,b,r,s)
:

a:=c_{1}-c_{2};\quad b:=c_{1}+c_{2};\quad r:=\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}-c_{2});\quad s:=\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2}).

(21)

Lemma 1
.

The traveling wave dynamical system for the equations (
9
)–(
11
), (
14
), (
18
) takes the following form
\mathrm{(}
in variables (
21
)
\mathrm{)}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}a&=r,\\
\partial_{\xi}b&=s,\\
\partial_{\xi}r&=-vr-\frac{sa}{2}+\frac{a|r|}{2},\\
\partial_{\xi}s&=-vs-ra.\end{split}

(22)

Remark 2
.

Note that right hand side of system (
22
) is locally Lipschitz and hence it satisfies existence and uniqueness conditions. At the same time it is not differentiable (due to the presence of
|r|
) and hence invariant manifold theory cannot be directly applied. The analysis of (
22
) below is similar to description of stable and unstable manifolds at the same time it is adapted to nonsmoothness of the system.

Proof.

Clearly,
\partial_{\xi}a=r
and
\partial_{\xi}b=s
. Let’s show how to obtain the equation on
\partial_{\xi}s
. Summing up the equations (
19
)–(
20
), we get:

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}(u_{1}c_{1}+u_{2}c_{2})+\partial_{\xi\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2}).

Using the expressions for
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, see relation (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}(c_{2}-c_{1})\cdot(c_{1}-c_{2})+\partial_{\xi\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2}).

Substituting the new variables (
21
), we get the necessary equation on
\partial_{\xi}s
.

Now let’s show how to obtain the equation on

\partial_{\xi}r
. Subtracting the equation (
20
) from (
19
), we get:

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}-c_{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}(u_{1}c_{1}-u_{2}c_{2})+\partial_{\xi\xi}(c_{1}-c_{2})-2f,

\displaystyle-v\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}-c_{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\cdot(c_{1}+c_{2})-u_{1}\cdot\partial_{\xi}(c_{1}+c_{2})+\partial_{\xi\xi}(c_{1}-c_{2})-2f.

(23)

The sum
(-\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\cdot(c_{1}+c_{2})-2f)
can be simplified:

\displaystyle-\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\cdot(c_{1}+c_{2})-2f

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\cdot(c_{2}-c_{1}),&\partial_{\xi}u_{1}<0,\\
-\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\cdot(c_{1}-c_{2}),&\partial_{\xi}u_{1}\geq 0,\end{cases}=-\frac{a|r|}{2}.

Substituting variables (
21
) into equation (
23
), we get the necessary equation on
\partial_{\xi}r
.
∎

3.1.2
Traveling wave dynamical system. Analysis

In this section we make preliminary analysis of the traveling wave dynamical system and introduce invariant manifolds important for the proof of Theorem
2

The set of fixed points for the system (
22
) for any

coincides with the set

\displaystyle M:=\{(a,b,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:r=s=0\}.

(24)

Notice that the right hand side of the system (
22

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}a&=r,\\
\partial_{\xi}r&=-vr-\frac{sa}{2}+\frac{a|r|}{2},\\
\partial_{\xi}s&=-vs-ra.\end{split}

(25)

By a straightforward computation one can check that for

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}(r+s)

\displaystyle=\left(-v-\frac{a}{2}\right)(r+s);

(26)

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}(2r-s)

\displaystyle=(-v+a)(2r-s).

(27)

Similarly, for

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}(r-s)

\displaystyle=\left(-v+\frac{a}{2}\right)(r-s);

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}(2r+s)

\displaystyle=(-v-a)(2r+s).

As a consequence we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 1
.

The following two-dimensional manifolds are invariant for (
25

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;r>0\;\text{ and }\;s=2r\};

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;r>0\;\text{ and }\;s=-r\};

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;r<0\;\text{ and }\;s=r\};

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;r<0\;\text{ and }\;s=-2r\}.

By uniqueness of solutions, the following regions are also invariant for (
25

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:s\geq-2r\;\text{ and }\;s\geq 2r\};

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;s\leq 2r\;\text{ and }\;s\geq-r\};

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;s\leq-r\;\text{ and }\;s\leq r\};

\displaystyle R_{4}

\displaystyle=\{(a,r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;s\geq r\;\text{ and }\;s\leq-2r\}.

In what follows we will show that heteroclinic trajectories corresponding to traveling waves belong to the invariant manifolds

Proof.

Lemma 2

Appendix B
Connection between TFE and IPM models

In this Section we describe the connection between the Incompressible Porous Medium (IPM) equations (
1
)–(
3
) and the Transverse Flow Equilibrium (TFE) equations (
1
)–(
2
), (
7
). We want to show that TFE model can be seen as a first-order approximation to the IPM model in the regime when the flow “equilibrates much faster” in the transversal direction than in the main direction of the flow (e.g. an asymptotically thin domain). Notice that both models exhibit a fingering instability, but up to our knowledge, there is no rigorously proven connection between TFE and IPM. Nevertheless, for the viscous fingering the connection was formally derived in
[
85
]
; see also
[
2
, Section 3.3]
for numerical comparison of TFE and IPM.

Consider the domain
(x,y)\in(0,L)\times(0,H)
, and a system (
1
)–(
2
) under the assumption
\nu=0
, supplied with a more general form of the Darcy’s law (here
k^{x},k^{y}>0
are permeabilities in
x
and
y
-directions):

\displaystyle u=(u^{x},u^{y})=-\left(\begin{matrix}k^{x}&0\\
0&k^{y}\end{matrix}\right)\nabla p-(0,c).

(61)

First, we make the following change of variables:

\displaystyle\tilde{x}=\frac{x}{L}\in[0,1],\qquad\tilde{y}=\frac{y}{H}\in[0,1],\qquad\tilde{t}=\frac{t}{H},\qquad\tilde{p}=\frac{k^{y}}{H}\cdot p,\qquad\tilde{u}^{x}=\frac{H}{L}\cdot u^{x},

and the IPM equations (
1
)–(
2
), (
61
) become (here
\gamma:=\frac{L}{H}\sqrt{\frac{k^{y}}{k^{x}}}
; we omit tildas):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c+\partial_{x}(u^{x}c)+\partial_{y}(u^{y}c)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

(62)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u^{x}+\partial_{y}u^{y}

\displaystyle=0,

(63)

\displaystyle u^{x}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\partial_{x}p,

(64)

\displaystyle\quad u^{y}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{y}p-c.

(65)

This form of equations suggests to write a formal asymptotic expansion in
\gamma^{2}
as
\gamma\to 0
:

\displaystyle g(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=g^{0}(t,x,y)+\gamma^{2}\cdot g^{1}(t,x,y)+\ldots

for
g=\{c,p,u^{x},u^{y}\}
.
Using (
64
), we obtain
\partial_{x}p^{0}=0
, thus up to first approximation
p
doesn’t depend on
x
variable. Integrating (
65
) and using no-flow conditions on all boundaries, we get

\displaystyle 0=\int\limits_{0}^{1}u^{y,0}(t,x,y)\,dx=-\partial_{y}p^{0}(t,y)-\int\limits_{0}^{1}c^{0}(t,x,y)\,dx.

Putting this relation into (
65
), we immediately get (
7
) for velocity
u^{0}=(u^{x,0},u^{y,0})
. Note that first-order approximation of the equations (
62
)–(
63
) give exactly the equations (
1
)–(
2
) for
c^{0}
and
u^{0}
(under the assumption
\nu=0
). This finishes the derivation of the TFE equations (
1
)–(
2
), (
7
).

The parameter
\gamma=\frac{L}{H}\sqrt{\frac{k^{y}}{k^{x}}}
, responsible for the connection between IPM and TFE models, was originally introduced by Yortsos in
[
85
]
. It allows for the following two interpretations:

•

If
k^{x}=k^{y}
and
\gamma=\frac{L}{H}\to 0
, then the transverse length of the domain is much smaller compared to the length in the main direction of the flow (e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
).

•

If
L=H
and
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{k^{y}}{k^{x}}}\to 0
, then the permeability in the transverse direction is much higher than the permeability in the main direction of the flow.

In our context of the two-tubes models, the parameter
l
(distance between the tubes) plays the same role as parameter
\gamma
, thus the limit
l\to 0
corresponds to the two-tubes TFE model, as expected.

Appendix C
Slowdown of fingers due to intermediate concentration

In this Section we clarify what we mean by “slowdown of fingers due to intermediate concentration”. We give two examples: for 2D and two-tubes TFE models.

C.1
2D case

In
[
51
]
the authors prove pointwise upper and lower estimates on the speed of the growth of the mixing zone for 2D TFE model (
1
)–(
2
) and (
7
).
The key idea lies in comparison with a traveling wave solution connecting
-1
and
1
of the following 1D viscous conservation law of Burger’s type (for initial data (
4
)):

\displaystyle c_{t}+\left(c-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)_{y}=c_{yy}.

(66)

By Rankine-Hugoniot condition, the speed of the traveling wave connecting states
-1
and
1
, is equal to
v^{f}=1
, which corresponds to the mixing zone growth rate
h(t)\leq 2t
as was mentioned in the Introduction, see estimate (
6
).

If on the tip of the finger the concentration drops down from 1 to some intermediate concentration
c^{*}\in(-1,1)
(see numerical evidence
[
7
]
for viscous fingering), then the estimate (
6
) can be improved. Indeed, adhering to the proof strategy of
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, we compare the solution with the traveling wave connecting states
c^{*}
and
1
and get the following expression for its speed
v^{f}(c^{*})
due to Rankine-Hugoniot condition for the (inviscid version of) equation (
66
):

\displaystyle v^{f}(c^{*})=\frac{\left[c-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right]\biggr\rvert_{c^{*}}^{1}}{c\bigr\rvert_{c^{*}}^{1}}=\frac{1-c^{*}}{2}<1,\quad c^{*}\in(-1,1).

In particular, if
c^{*}=-1
we get
v^{f}(-1)=1
, which is in accordance with the estimate (
6
).
So the appearance of the intermediate concentration on the tip of the finger reduces its speed of propagation and gives a slowdown
(for 2D TFE model).

C.2
Two-tubes case

One of the most natural guesses for a possible propagating terrace consisting of two traveling waves for the two-tubes TFE model (
9
)–(
11
), (
14
), (
18
) is as follows:

\displaystyle(-1,-1)\xrightarrow{TW}(-1,1)\xrightarrow{TW}(1,1).

It can be interpreted as two counter-propagating fingers with concentrations
1
and
-1
.
If such propagating terrace exists, the speed of the traveling wave connecting
(-1,1)
and
(1,1)
can be calculated using the following formula (here we use
u_{1}=-u_{2}=(c_{2}-c_{1})/2
, see (

\displaystyle v^{f}=\frac{\left[u_{1}c_{1}+u_{2}c_{2}\right]\bigr\rvert_{(c_{1},c_{2})=(-1,+1)}^{(c_{1},c_{2})=(+1,+1)}}{\left[c_{1}+c_{2}\right]\bigr\rvert_{(c_{1},c_{2})=(-1,+1)}^{(c_{1},c_{2})=(+1,+1)}}=1.

(67)

This expression is an analogue of the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for the sum of equations (
9
) and (
10
). Indeed, this sum can be written in a conservative form:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(c_{1}+c_{2})+\partial_{y}(c_{1}u_{1}+c_{2}u_{2})=\partial_{yy}(c_{1}+c_{2}).

Putting traveling wave ansatz
(c_{1},c_{2})(t,y)=(c_{1},c_{2})(\xi)
,
\xi=y-vt
, integrating from
\xi=-\infty
to
\xi=+\infty
and assuming
\partial_{y}(c_{1,2})(\pm\infty)=0
, we obtain (
67
) for

.

Comparing the speed
v^{f}=1
from formula (
67
) with the speed
v^{f}=1/4
obtained in Theorem
2
, we observe that the selected propagating terrace for the two-tubes TFE model, indeed, moves slower. We interpret this propagating terrace as two counter-propagating fingers with concentrations
1/2
and
-1/2
, and thus conclude that we have a slowdown of fingers growth due to intermediate concentration.

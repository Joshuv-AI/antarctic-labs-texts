Variations of Ramanujan’s Euler Products
Shin-ya Koyama & Nobushige Kurokawa

Abstract
We study the meromorphy of various Euler products of degree two attached to holomorphic Hecke eigen cusp forms for the elliptic modular group, including Ramanujan’s Δ \Delta -function.



Key Words: Euler products; zeta functions; Ramanujan’s
\Delta
-function; cusp forms; modular group

AMS Subject Classifications: 11M06, 11M41,

Introduction

In 1916 Ramanujan
[
12
]
studied the Euler product associated to Ramanujan’s
\tau
-function defined as

\Delta(z)=e^{2\pi iz}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi inz})^{24}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)e^{2\pi inz}

for
\Im(z)>0
. This
\Delta(z)
is a Hecke-eigen cusp form of weight 12 for the elliptic modular group
SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Ramanujan’s Euler product is written in the normalized form as

L_{\Delta}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-a(p)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1},

where
a(n)=\tau(n)n^{-\frac{11}{2}}.

Ramanujan conjectured that
|a(p)|\leq 2
for all primes
p
and it was proved by Deligne
[
4
]
in 1974.
Hence there exists a unique
\theta(p)\in[0,\pi]
satisfying
a(p)=2\cos(\theta(p))
.

Thus

\displaystyle L_{\Delta}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-2\cos(\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\det(1-\alpha_{\Delta}^{0}(p)p^{-s})^{-1},

where

\alpha_{\Delta}^{0}(p)=\left[\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta(p)}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta(p)}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2)).

More general Euler products were constructed by Serre
[
13
]
, Langlands
[
9
]
and Tate
[
17
]
as

L_{\Delta}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\det(1-\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\alpha_{\Delta}^{0}(p))p^{-s})^{-1}

for the irreducible representation

\mathrm{Sym}^{m}:\ SU(2)\to U(m+1)
with
m=0,1,2,3,...
.
Quite recently, Newton-Thorne
[
11
,
10
]
proved that there exists an automorphic representation

\pi_{m}
of
GL(m+1,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfying

L(s,\pi_{m})=L_{\Delta}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})

for each
m=0,1,2,3,...
and that
\pi_{m}

(m\geq 1)
are cuspidal corresponding to the holomorphy of

L_{\Delta}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})
.

The situation is quite similar for each holomorphic Hecke eigen cusp form
\varphi
of general weight for the elliptic modular group.

In this paper we first study the Euler product

Z_{m}^{\pm}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1\pm 2\cos(m\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

for
m=0,1,2,3,...
and show the following result.

Theorem A
.

Z_{m}^{\pm}(s)
has an analytic continuation to all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
as a meromorphic function.

In fact, this is easily seen from Newton-Thorne
[
11
,
10
]
since each
Z_{m}^{\pm}(s)
can be written
explicitly in terms of
L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n})
.
In particular
Z_{m}^{-}(s)
has a simple expression as

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{-}(s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})}{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m-2})}&(m\geq 2)\\
L_{\varphi}(s)&(m=1).\end{cases}

Next, we show the converse in some sense.
Actually we determine all (non-constant) monic polynomials
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
such that

\displaystyle Z^{\pm}(s,f)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1\pm f(a(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1\pm f(2\cos(\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

are meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be a monic polynomial of degree
m\geq 1
.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

Z^{\pm}(s,f)
is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

|f(x)|\leq 2
for
-2\leq x\leq 2
.

(3)

f(x)=2T_{m}(\frac{x}{2})
. Here,
T_{m}(x)
is the Chebyshev polynomial defined by
T_{m}(\cos\theta)=\cos(m\theta)
.

(4)

Z^{\pm}(s,f)=Z_{m}^{\pm}(s)
.

Example 1
.

Let

Z^{m}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-(a(p^{2})-m)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then,

Z^{m}(s)
is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}

\Longleftrightarrow

m=1
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-(a(p^{2})-1)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-a(p^{2})p^{-s}+a(p^{2})p^{-2s}-p^{-3s})^{-1}.

This expression was used by Shimura
[
16
]
for the proof of the holomorphy of
L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2})
.

Example 2
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\prod\limits_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-(a(p)-m)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}
is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}

\Longleftrightarrow

m=0
.

Otherwise, each
Z^{\pm}(s,f)
has the natural boundary
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
as in the following result.

Theorem C
.

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be a non-constant monic polynomial.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

Z^{\pm}(s,f)
is not meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

Z^{\pm}(s,f)
has an analytic continuation in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
with the natural boundary
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
.

Our results come from a general meromorphy theorem for Euler products shown in
[
7
,
8
]
assuming

[
11
,
10
]
. We notice that the proof is similar to the case of meromorphy of Dirichlet series

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)^{m}n^{-s}
and

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n^{m})n^{-s}
treated in
[
7
,
8
]
.
For
m=1,2
they are meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
and for
m\geq 3
they are meromorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
with the
natural boundary
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
. We refer to
[
6
]
for an application to rigidity of Euler products.

1
Proof of Theorem A

(1)
The case
m=0

From

Z_{0}^{\pm}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1\pm 2p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1\pm p^{-s})^{-2}

we see that

Z_{0}^{-}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-p^{-s})^{-2}=\zeta(s)^{2}

and

Z_{0}^{+}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1+p^{-s})^{-2}=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\left(\frac{1-p^{-2s}}{1-p^{-s}}\right)^{-2}=\frac{\zeta(2s)^{2}}{\zeta(s)^{2}}

are meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)
The case
m=1

By identifying

Z_{1}^{-}(s)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1-2\cos(\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}=L_{\varphi}(s)

and

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{+}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}(1+2\cos(\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}[(1+e^{i\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1+e^{-i\theta(p)}p^{-s})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\left[\frac{(1-e^{2i\theta(p)}p^{-2s})(1-e^{-2i\theta(p)}p^{-2s})}{(1-e^{i\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1-e^{-i\theta(p)}p^{-s})}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\varphi}(2s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2})}{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{1})L_{\varphi}(2s,\mathrm{Sym}^{0})},

where

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{1})

\displaystyle=L_{\varphi}(s),

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{0})

\displaystyle=\zeta(s),

we know that both are meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

(3)
The case
m\geq 2

First

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{-}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}[(1-e^{im\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1-e^{-im\theta(p)}p^{-s})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\left[\frac{(1-e^{im\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1-e^{i(m-2)\theta(p)}p^{-s})\cdots(1-e^{-im\theta(p)}p^{-s})}{(1-e^{i(m-2)\theta(p)}p^{-s})\cdots(1-e^{-i(m-2)\theta(p)}p^{-s})}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})}{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m-2})}

is a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
by means of
[
1
,
11
,
10
]
.

Secondly

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{+}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}[(1+e^{im\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1+e^{-im\theta(p)}p^{-s})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}\left[\frac{(1-e^{2im\theta(p)}p^{-2s})(1-e^{-2im\theta(p)}p^{-2s})}{(1-e^{im\theta(p)}p^{-s})(1-e^{-im\theta(p)}p^{-s})}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\varphi}(2s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2m})}{L_{\varphi}(2s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2m-2})}\frac{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m-2})}{L_{\varphi}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m})}

is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
using
[
1
,
11
,
10
]
.
∎

2
Meromorphy of Euler products

We recall the meromorphy result of
[
8
, §3 Theorem 7]
needed in this paper.
Take the triple

E=(P({\mathbb{Z}}),\ SU(2),\ \alpha_{\varphi}^{0}),

where
P({\mathbb{Z}})
is the set of prime numbers, and the map

\alpha_{\varphi}^{0}:\ P({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2))

defined as

\alpha_{\varphi}^{0}(p)=\left[\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta(p)}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta(p)}\end{pmatrix}\right].

We identify
[0,\pi]
and
\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2))
via the following map

\begin{matrix}[0,\pi]&\longrightarrow&\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2)).\\
\rotatebox{90.0}{$\in$}&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\in$}\\
\theta&\longmapsto&\left[\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta}\end{pmatrix}\right]\end{matrix}

Notice that the normalized measure on
\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2))=[0,\pi]
coming from the normalized Haar measure of
SU(2)
is
given by
\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta
used in the Sato-Tate conjecture proved in
[
1
]
.

Main results of
[
7
,
8
]
give the criterion of the meromorphy of Euler product

L(s,E,H)=\prod_{p:\,\text{prime}}H_{\alpha_{\varphi}^{0}(p)}(p^{-s})^{-1}

for each polynomial

H(T)\in 1+TR(SU(2))[T]

when specialized to the present situation, where
R(SU(2))
is the ring of virtual characters of
SU(2)
and

H_{\alpha_{\varphi}^{0}(p)}(T)\in 1+T{\mathbb{C}}[T]

denotes the polynomial obtained by taking values of coefficients.

We first report Theorems 1 and 2.
The important assumption made in
[
7
,
8
]
for

E=(P({\mathbb{Z}}),SU(2),\alpha_{\varphi}^{0})
on the analytic properties of the symmetric power
L
-functions
follow from recently proved automorphy due to Newton-Thorne
[
11
, Theorem A]
.
Especially the boundedness in vertical strips of the symmetric power
L
-functions needed in

[
7
,
8
]
follow from Gelbart-Shahidi
[
5
]
(see Shahidi
[
15
]
and Cogdell-Piatetskii-Shapiro
[
3
]
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
H(T)\in 1+TR(SU(2))[T]
be a polynomial of degree
n
.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

H(T)
is unitary in the sense that there exist functions
\varphi_{j}:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

(j=1,2,...,n)
such that

H_{\theta}(T)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-e^{i\varphi_{j}(\theta)}T)

for
\theta\in[0,\pi]=\mathrm{Conj}(SU(2))
.

(2)

L(s,E,H)
is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
H(T)\in 1+TR(SU(2))[T]
.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

H(T)
is not unitary.

(2)

L(s,E,H)
is meromorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
with the natural boundary.

Here we prove a concrete example of degree two for applications in this paper.

Theorem 3
.

Let
H(T)=1\pm hT+T^{2}\in 1+TR(SU(2))[T]
.
Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

H(T)
is unitary.

(2)

|h(\theta)|\leq 2
for all
\theta\in[0,\pi]
.

(3)

L(s,E,H)
is meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

Since
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(3)
is implied in Theorem 1, it suffices to show

(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2)
.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
: From

H_{\theta}(T)=(1-e^{i\varphi(\theta)}T)(1-e^{-i\varphi(\theta)}T)

for a function
\varphi:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

|h(\theta)|=|2\cos(\varphi(\theta))|\leq 2

for
\theta\in[0,\pi]
.

(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
: From
|h(\theta)|\leq 2
we have

H_{\theta}(T)=\left(1\pm\frac{h(\theta)+i\sqrt{4-h(\theta)^{2}}}{2}T\right)\left(1\pm\frac{h(\theta)-i\sqrt{4-h(\theta)^{2}}}{2}T\right)

and

\left|\frac{h(\theta)\pm i\sqrt{4-h(\theta)^{2}}}{2}\right|=1.

∎

3
Proofs of Theorems B and C

From Theorems 1, 2 and 3 we see that Theorems B and C are proved by the following result.

Theorem 4
.

For a monic polynomial
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
of degree
m\geq 1
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

|f(x)|\leq 2
for
-2\leq x\leq 2
.

(2)

f(x)=2T_{m}(\frac{x}{2})
.

Actually, let

H(T)=1\pm f(2\cos\theta)T+T^{2}\in 1+TR(SU(2))[T],

then we see the following equivalence:

\displaystyle L(s,E,H)\text{ is meromorphic on }{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Th }1}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad H(T)\text{ is unitary}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Th }3}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad|f(2\cos\theta)|\leq 2\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,\pi]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad|f(x)|\leq 2\text{ for }-1\leq x\leq 1

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Th }4}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad f(x)=2T_{m}(\frac{x}{2})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad L(s,E,H)=\prod_{p}(1\pm 2\cos(m\theta(p))p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}.

We remark that this also gives a second proof of Theorem A.

Proof of Theorem 4.
Let

g(x)=\frac{1}{2^{m}}f(2x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x].

This is a monic polynomial of degree
m
.
Then, (1) is equivalent to the following condition:

|g(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\text{ for }-1\leq x\leq 1.

Such a monic polynomial
g(x)
is uniquely determined as

\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}T_{m}(x)
by the famous theorem of Chebyshev
[
2
]
; see Serre
[
14
]
.
Hence
f(x)=2T_{m}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

∎

Abstract
. We present two new constructions of satisfaction/truth classes over models of 𝖯𝖠 \mathsf{PA} (Peano Arithmetic) that provide a foil to the fact that the existence of a disjunctively correct full truth class over a model ℳ \mathcal{M} of 𝖯𝖠 \mathsf{PA} implies that ℳ \mathcal{M} satisfies 𝖢𝗈𝗇 ⁡ ( 𝖯𝖠 ) \mathsf{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) . In the first construction, given an arbitrary countable recursively saturated model ℳ \mathcal{M} of the relational version of 𝖯𝖠 \mathsf{PA} , we build an extensional satisfaction class S S over the family 𝖥𝗈𝗋𝗆 ℳ < ω \mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<\omega} of all arithmetical formulae coded in ℳ \mathcal{M} that have at most finitely-many free variables (from an external point of view) with the property that the truth class naturally associated with S S (using domain constants) is disjunctively correct for disjunctions in 𝖥𝗈𝗋𝗆 ℳ < ω \mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<\omega} (and S S has other ‘good’ properties). This construction lends itself to a generalization in which 𝖥𝗈𝗋𝗆 ℳ < ω \mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<\omega} is replaced with 𝖥𝗈𝗋𝗆 ℳ < I \mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<I} , where I I is an arbitrarily large cut I I of ℳ \mathcal{M} such that I ≺ ℳ I\prec\mathcal{M} and I ≅ ℳ I\cong\mathcal{M} . In the second construction, given an arbitrary countable recursively saturated model ℳ \mathcal{M} of the usual presentation of 𝖯𝖠 \mathsf{PA} (with function symbols for addition and multiplication, and constant symbols for 0 and 1), we show that there are arbitrarily large cuts I I of ℳ \mathcal{M} with the property that I ≺ ℳ I\prec\mathcal{M} , I ≅ ℳ I\cong\mathcal{M} , and ℳ \mathcal{M} carries a full truth class T T that is disjunctively correct for disjunctions whose code is in I I , and T T is also closed under first order proofs whose code is in I . 1. INTRODUCTION

By a theorem of Krajewski, Kotlarski, and Lachlan
[
KKL
]
,
every countable recursively saturated model
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathsf{PA}

(Peano Arithmetic) carries a ‘full satisfaction class’, i.e., there is a
subset
S
of the universe
M
of
\mathcal{M}
that ‘decides’ the
truth/falsity of each sentence of arithmetic in the sense of
\mathcal{M}

– even sentences of nonstandard length – while obeying the usual recursive
clauses guiding the behavior of a Tarskian satisfaction predicate. This
remarkable theorem implies that theory
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]}

(compositional truth over
\mathsf{PA}
with induction only for the language

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
of arithmetic) is
conservative
over
\mathsf{PA}
, i.e., if an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
-sentence
\varphi
is
provable in
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]}
, then
\varphi
is already
provable in
\mathsf{PA}
. New proofs of this conservativity result were
given by Visser and Enayat
[
EV-2
]
using basic model theoretic
ideas, and by Leigh
[
Lei
]
using proof theoretic tools; these new
proofs make it clear that in the Krajewski-Kotlarski-Lachlan theorem the
theory
\mathsf{PA}
can be replaced by any ‘base’ theory that supports a
modicum of coding machinery for handling elementary syntax.

On the other hand, it is well-known that the consistency of
\mathsf{PA}

(and much more) is readily provable in the stronger theory
\mathsf{CT[PA]}
, which is the result of strengthening
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]}
with
the scheme of induction over natural numbers for all
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA+T}}
-formulae, where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA+T}}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}\cup\{\mathsf{T}(x)\}.
Indeed, it is straightforward to demonstrate the
consistency of
\mathsf{PA}
within the subsystem
\mathsf{CT}_{1}\mathsf{[PA]}
of
\mathsf{CT[PA]}
, where
\mathsf{CT}_{n}\mathsf{[PA]}
is the
subtheory of
\mathsf{CT[PA]}
with the scheme of induction over natural
numbers limited to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA+T}}
-formulae that are at most
of complexity
\Sigma_{n}

[
WL
, Thm. 2.8]
.

The case of
\mathsf{CT}_{0}\mathsf{[PA]}
has taken a great deal of effort
to analyze. Kotlarski
[
Ko
]
established that
\mathsf{CT}_{0}\mathsf{[PA]}
is a subtheory of
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]+Ref(PA)}
,
where
\mathsf{Ref(PA)}
is the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA+T}}
-sentence
stating that “every first order consequence of
\mathsf{PA}

is true”. Łełyk
[
Lel
]
demonstrated that
the converse also holds, which immediately implies that
\mathsf{CT}_{0}\mathsf{[PA]}
is not conservative over
\mathsf{PA}
since
\mathsf{Con(PA)}
is readily provable in
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]+Ref(PA).}

1

[
Ko
]
, but his proof
outline of
\mathsf{Ref(PA)}
within
\mathsf{CT}_{0}\mathsf{[PA]}

. Łełyk’s work

over

by Wcisło and Łełyk

]
.
Kotlarski’s aforementioned theorem was
refined by Cieśliński
[

]
who proved that
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]+}
“
\mathsf{T}
is closed under
propositional proofs” and
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[PA]+Ref(PA)}

refined Cieśliński’s result by demonstrating that
\mathsf{CT}^{-}\mathsf{[I\Delta}_{0}+\mathsf{Exp]+DC}

and

is the

\mathsf{DC}
that says every true disjunction has a true
disjunct (the proof in
[

uses the version of
\mathsf{CT}^{-}
that includes the so-called regularity axiom; it is not clear if

[

In this paper we present two new constructions of satisfaction classes over
models of
\mathsf{PA}
(Peano Arithmetic) that provide a foil to the
aforementioned nonconservativity results concerning disjunctive correctness,
as described below.

In the first construction (presented as Theorem 4.1), given an arbitrary
countable recursively saturated model
\mathcal{M}
of the relational
version of
\mathsf{PA}
, we build an extensional satisfaction class
S

over the family
\mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<\omega}
of all arithmetical
formulae coded in
\mathcal{M}
that have at most finitely-many free
variables (from an external point of view) with the property that
S
is
disjunctively correct for disjunctions in
\mathsf{Form}_{\mathcal{M}}^{<\omega}
(
S
also has other ‘good’ properties). In Theorem 4.3 we give
a more general version of Theorem 4.1, and in Theorem 4.4 we combine this
construction with another construction due to Fedor Pakhomov
[
P
]
to show that there are arbitrarily large cuts
I
in
\mathcal{M}
such that there is
full
satisfaction class
S
on
\mathcal{M}

that is disjunctively correct for disjunctions whose code is in
I
.

In the second construction (presented as Theorem 5.1), given an arbitrary
countable recursively saturated model
\mathcal{M}
of the usual
presentation of
\mathsf{PA}
(with function symbols for addition and
multiplication, and constant symbols for 0 and 1), we show that there are
arbitrarily large cuts
I
in
\mathcal{M}
such that there is a full truth
class
T
on
\mathcal{M}
that is disjunctively correct for disjunctions
coded in
I
(and also
T
is closed under first order proofs whose code is
in
I).
This is the analogue of Theorem 4.4 for the usual presentation of
\mathsf{PA}
.

The main idea of the construction in Section 4 was discovered by the author
in 2009 (two years later, I found out that a similar construction was
proposed by Smoryński in unpublished work; see Section 6 for more
detail). The protoforms of the results in Sections 3 and 5 appeared in a
privately circulated manuscript
[
EV-1
]
written in
collaboration with Albert Visser, and appear here with his kind permission.

2. PRELIMINARIES

2.1. Definition.
In this paper we will use two versions
of Peano arithmetic, namely
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Rel}}}
and
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Func}}}
; the former is employed in the first
construction, and the latter in the second one.
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Rel}}}
refers to Peano arithmetic formulated in a
relational
language

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
using the logical constants
\left\{\lnot,\vee,\exists,=\right\}
.

Note that in this formulation
\mathsf{PA}
has no constant symbols; the arithmetical operations of
addition and multiplication are construed as ternary relations; and
conjunction, universal quantification, and other logical constants are taken
as defined notions in the usual way.
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Func}}}
is
the usual formulation of Peano arithmetic in a language that has function
symbols for addition and multiplication, constant symbols for 0 and 1, and
the logical constants
\left\{\lnot,\vee,\exists,=\right\}.
Of course
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Rel}}}
and
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Func}}}

are definitionally equivalent, but the difference between them is manifested
in the way in which a compositional truth theory is built upon them (see
Definitions 2.2 and 2.4 below).

It is well known that Peano Arithmetic has more than sufficient expressive
machinery to handle syntactic notions. The following list of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
-formulae will be useful here. Each of the formulae defined in
the following bullet items has two versions, depending on whether one is
using
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Rel}}}
or
\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Func}}}
(in the paper the context will make it clear which one is intended,
hence in the interest of keeping notation to a minimum we will not subscript
them with
Rel
and
Func).

•

\mathsf{Form}(x)
is the formula expressing “
x
is
the code of an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
-formula using variables
\{v_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\},
and the non-logical symbols available in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
”.

•

\mathsf{Sent}(x)
is the conjunction of
\mathsf{Form}(x)
and the
sentence expressing “
x
has no free variables”.

•

\mathsf{Asn}(x)
is the formula expressing “
x
is
the code of an assignment”, where an assignment here
simply refers to a function whose domain consists of a (finite) set of
variables. We use
\alpha
and its variants (
\alpha^{\prime}
,
\alpha_{0}
, etc.) to range over assignments.

•

y\in\mathsf{FV}(x)
is the formula expressing “
\mathsf{Form}(x)
and
y
is a free variable of
x
”.

•

y\in\mathsf{Dom}(\alpha)
is the formula expressing
“the domain of
\alpha
includes
y
”.

•

\mathsf{Asn}(\alpha,x)
is the following formula expressing
“
\alpha
is an assignment for
x
”:

\left(\mathsf{Form}(x)\wedge\mathsf{Asn}(\alpha)\wedge\forall y(y\in\mathsf{Dom}(\alpha)\leftrightarrow y\in\mathsf{FV}(x)\right).

The theory
\mathsf{CS}^{-}[\mathsf{PA}_{\text{{Rel}}}]
defined
below is formulated in an
expansion
of the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
by adding a new
binary
predicate
\mathsf{S}(x,y)
.
The binary/unary distinction is of course not an essential one since
\mathsf{PA}
has access to a definable pairing function. However, the
binary/unary distinction
at the conceptual level
marks the key
difference between satisfaction classes and truth classes
.

When reading the definition below it is helpful to bear in mind that
\mathsf{Tarski}(\mathsf{S,F})
expresses:

F
is a subset of

that is closed under immediate
subformulae; each member of
\mathsf{S}
is an ordered pair of the form

and
\mathsf{S}
satisfies Tarski’s compositional clauses.

Suppose

(a)

In this section
\mathsf{PA}:=\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{Func}},
and

5.1. Theorem.
(joint with Albert Visser)
Let
\mathcal{M}

be a countable recursively saturated model of

\mathsf{PA.}

There are arbitrarily large cuts

I

of

\mathcal{M}

for which the following properties hold
:

\mathbf{(a)}

I\prec\mathcal{M}

and

I\cong\mathcal{M}.

\mathbf{(b)\ }\mathcal{M}

carries a full truth class
T

that is

I
-deductively correct

(
i.e.,
T\
is closed under first order proofs coded in
I)

and is

I
-disjunctively correct
(
i.e., a disjunction whose code
is in

I

is deemed true by

T

iff one if its disjuncts
is true
)
.

Proof.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a countable recursively
saturated model of
\mathsf{PA}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be as in the proof of
the first construction; thus
\mathcal{N}
is an elementary extension of
\mathcal{M}
that is strongly interpreted in
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{N}).
Thanks to Corollary 3.3 there is an
elementary extension
\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
of
\mathcal{N}
that carries a
truth class
T
such that
(\mathcal{N},T_{N})\models\mathsf{CT}^{-}[\mathsf{PA}],
and
(\mathcal{N},T_{N})
is strongly interpretable in
\mathcal{M}
, which in turn will allow us to conclude that
\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
satisfies the following four properties:

(1)

T_{N}
is
\mathcal{M}
-deductively correct (i.e., the
conclusion of any from hypotheses in
T_{N}
is also in
T_{N}
; this is
handled the usual argument that shows that the classical Tarskian truth
predicate is deductively correct) and
\mathcal{M}
-disjunctively correct
(i.e., any disjunction in
\mathcal{M}
that is deemed true by
T_{N}
has a
true disjunct).

(2)
There is an
\mathcal{M}
-definable embedding of
\mathcal{M}
as an initial segment of
\mathcal{N}^{\ast}.

(3)

\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{N}^{\ast}).

(4)

\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
is recursively saturated.

Thanks to (2), (3), (4), and Theorem 2.5,
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
, which implies that we ‘copy over’
T_{N}
on
\mathcal{M}
, thus
\mathcal{M}
carries a full truth
T
class that is
I
-correct
for some cut
I
of
\mathcal{M}
. By using Theorem 2.8 and Remark 2.9 we
can readily conclude that such cuts can be arranged to be arbitrarily large
cuts
I
in
\mathcal{M}
(more specifically, if
I
is a strong cut of
\mathcal{M}
, then by Theorem 2.8 there is an isomorphism
f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
with
f(i)=i
for all
i
in
I
. This
makes it clear that
T:=f(T_{N})
is the desired full truth class).
\square

6. Historical Note

The main idea of the first construction was found by the author in 2009; it
was written up in a short note that was privately circulated among a few
colleagues. In 2011, I received an email from Jim Schmerl that shows that
over three decades earlier Craig Smoryński had come up with the same
idea, as indicated below. The email, as well as my response, are copied
below.

From James Schmerl to Ali Enayat

July 5, 2011

I moved out of my office over the weekend. I’m sure you’re not
interested in that tidbit, but what you might be interested in is that, in
the process, I uncovered the Smoryński letter that I had told you about
a while ago. I transcribed the relevant portion to the attached file [see
below]. What do you think of his proof?

The following is an exact transcription of a portion of a
handwritten letter dated 10 June ’80 sent to me by Craig Smoryński:

Kotlarski-Krajewski-Lachlan. If
M
is countable rec.
sat.,
M
has a full satisfaction class.

Pf: Let
M
define truth for

(
M
) by Orey’s Compactness Theorem. Since
M\equiv N
and
\mathrm{SSy}(M)=\mathrm{SSy}(N)
we have
M\cong N
. Now truth for
N
is defined for all nonstandard
formulae in
M
with parameters in
N
. Using isomorphism

F
define

S(\left\langle a_{0},...,a_{e-1}\right\rangle,\ulcorner\varphi v_{0},...,v_{e-1}\urcorner)\leftrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{N}(\ulcorner\varphi Fa_{0},...,Fa_{e-1}\urcorner)

[
e
possibly ns.].

**************

From Ali Enayat to James Schmerl

July 5, 2011

Thank you very much for sending me the note, it is VERY interesting
for me, because a couple of years ago I came up with an identical
construction (and even talked about it in a seminar in Sweden).

Later, when I tried to write a careful proof that the construction
works, I found a subtle serious obstacle, having to do with the case when
\varphi
has nonstandardly-many free variables. I recall spending
a great deal of effort dealing with this obstacle, and was finally convinced
that this “cheap way” cannot produce a full satisfaction
class. However, using the automorphism trick, one *can* obtain the
nontrivial result that when
M
is countable rec. sat., then for
any prescribed m in
M
, there is a satisfaction class on
M
that decides all the formulas at least up to
m
.

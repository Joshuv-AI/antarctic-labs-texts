Small-Time Local Controllability of the multi-input bilinear Schrödinger equation thanks to a quadratic term
Théo Gherdaoui

Abstract
The goal of this article is to contribute to a better understanding of the relations between the exact controllability of nonlinear PDEs and the control theory for ODEs based on Lie brackets, through a study of the Schrödinger PDE with bilinear control. We focus on the small-time local controllability (STLC) around an equilibrium, when the linearized system is not controllable. We study the second-order term in the Taylor expansion of the state, with respect to the control. For scalar-input ODEs, quadratic terms never recover controllability: they induce signed drifts in the dynamics (see [ 10 ] ). Thus proving STLC requires to go at least to the third order. Similar results were proved for the bilinear Schrödinger PDE with scalar-input controls in [ 16 ] . In this article, we study the case of multi-input systems. We clarify among the quadratic Lie brackets, those that allow to recover STLC: they are bilinear with respect to two different controls. For ODEs, our result is a consequence of Sussmann’s sufficient condition S ⁡ ( θ ) S(\theta) (when focused on quadratic terms), but we propose a new proof, designed to prepare an easier transfer to PDEs. This proof relies on a representation formula of the state inspired by the Magnus formula. By adapting it, we prove a new STLC result for the bilinear Schrödinger PDE. Keywords: Bilinear Schrödinger equation, infinite-dimensional systems, small-time local exact controllability, power series expansion.

Introduction

1.1
Model and problem

In this article, we study the following Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}i\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,x)-\left(u(t)\mu_{1}(x)+v(t)\mu_{2}(x)\right)\psi(t,x),&t\in(0,T),x\in(0,1),\\
\psi(t,0)=\psi(t,1)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
,
T>0
,
u,v:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
,
\psi:(0,T)\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
and
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=1
.
When well-defined, the solution is denoted
\psi
. When required, we will write
\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\psi_{0})
to refer to this solution to emphasize its dependence on the different parameters.

This equation describes the evolution of the wave function
\psi
of a quantum particle
in a
1
D infinite square potential well
(0,1)
, subjected to two electric fields with amplitudes
u(t)
and
v(t)
.
The functions
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, called "dipolar moments", model the interaction between the particle’s wave function
\psi
and the two electric fields
u,v
.

This is a multi-input nonlinear control system:

-

the state is the wave function
\psi:(0,T)\to\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the
L^{2}(0,1)
sphere,

-

the controls are
u,v:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, they act bilinearly on the state, through the term
(u(t)\mu_{1}(x)+v(t)\mu_{2}(x))\psi(t,x)
,
this is a reason why we speak of "bilinear Schrödinger equation".

The ground state is the particular trajectory
\psi_{1}(t,x):=\varphi_{1}(x)e^{-i\lambda_{1}t}
, with
(u,v)=0
where
\varphi_{1}(x):=\sqrt{2}\sin(\pi x)
and
\lambda_{1}:=\pi^{2}
.
We are interested in the local controllability of the nonlinear system (
1.1
) around the ground state,
i.e. realizing small motions around the ground state with small controls:
given a maximal size
\varepsilon>0
for the controls,
does there exist
T,\delta>0
such that,
for any target
\psi_{f}
close enough to the ground state, i.e.
\|\psi_{f}-\psi_{1}(T)\|<\delta
,
there exist controls
u,v:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|(u,v)\|\leqslant\varepsilon
and
\psi(T;(u,v),\varphi_{1})=\psi_{f}
.
The different norms used are of great importance and will be precised later.

More precisely, we are interested in the
small-time
local controllability around the ground state
i.e. realizing, in arbitrarily small times
T>0
, small motions around the ground state with small controls:
given
T,\varepsilon>0
,
does there exist
\delta>0
such that,
for any target
\psi_{f}
such that
\|\psi_{f}-\psi_{1}(T)\|<\delta
,
there exist controls
u,v:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|(u,v)\|\leqslant\varepsilon
and
\psi(T;(u,v),\varphi_{1})=\psi_{f}
.
This corresponds to the local surjectivity around
0
of the nonlinear end-point map

\Theta_{T}:(u,v)\mapsto\psi(T;(u,v),\varphi_{1})
.

Any positive answer to the STLC problem may be thought of as a nonlinear local open mapping theorem,
which underlines the deepness and intricacy of this problem,
when the inverse mapping theorem (or linear test, see
[
28
, Section
3.1
]
) cannot be used.

Small-time controllability has particularly relevant physical implications, both from a fundamental viewpoint and for technological applications.
Indeed, quantum systems, once engineered, suffer of very short lifespan before decaying (
e.g.
through spontaneous photon emissions) and losing their non-classical properties (such as superposition). Hence, the capability of controlling them in a minimal time is also an important challenge in physics.

The controllable linearized case: constrained functional framework

Under appropriate assumptions on the dipolar moments

One then obtains the following statement, that can easily be proved by adapting with two controls the strategy developed in

This statement requires the introduction of the following notations: we define the operator

A:=-\partial^{2}_{x}\text{ with domain }D(A):=H^{2}(0,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,1).

(1.2)

Except explicit precision, we will work with complex valued functions. We consider the space,

We consider, for

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Then, the bilinear Schrödinger equation (

H^{m}_{0}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
-small-time locally controllable in
H^{2(p+m)+3}_{(0)}(0,1)

\delta>0

In this statement, there are two parameters:

-

,

-

corresponds to the regularity expected on the controls:

The property

\forall j\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\qquad|\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\rangle|+|\langle\mu_{2}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\rangle|\neq 0

is necessary for the controllability of the linearized system around the ground state.
The stronger assumption (
1.5
) guarantees that the linearized system is
H^{m}_{0}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
-small-time controllable in

Then, a smoothing effect allows to prove that the end-point map is of class

between the following spaces

\Theta_{T}:(u,v)\in H^{m}_{0}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{2}\mapsto\psi(T;(u,v),\varphi_{1})\in H^{2(m+p)+3}_{(0)}(0,1)\cap\mathcal{S}.

Thus, by applying the inverse mapping theorem, we obtain the local controllability of the nonlinear system (
1.1
).

The choice of functional spaces is determined by the controllability of the linearized system:
once

are fixed and (
1.5
) is verified, then we must work with
\psi(T)\in H^{2(p+m)+3}_{(0)}(0,1)
.

Note that the assumption (

) prevents
\mu_{\ell}^{(2p+1)}
from vanishing at the boundary because, if

satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem

, then integrations by parts give: for all
k,j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, distinct, for all
\ell\in\{1,2\}
,

\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{k}\rangle=\frac{4(-1)^{p}(p+1)k}{j^{2p+3}\pi^{2p+2}}\left((-1)^{j+k}\mu_{\ell}^{(2p+1)}(1)-\mu_{\ell}^{(2p+1)}(0)\right)+\underset{j\to+\infty}{o}\left(\frac{1}{j^{2p+3}}\right).

(1.7)

1.3
Our case study and its interpretation in terms of Lie brackets

In this article, we study the case where the linearized system around the ground state is not controllable,
so we consider an integer
K\geqslant 2
and we assume

\mathbf{(H)_{lin,K,1}}:\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{K}\rangle=\langle\mu_{2}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{K}\rangle=0.

In this case, the linearized system is not controllable: it misses one complex direction
\langle\psi(t),\varphi_{K}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
. In other words, the first-order term in the Taylor expansion at
(0,0)
of the map
(u,v)\mapsto\langle\psi(t;(u,v),\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}\rangle
vanishes.
We assume that this is the only direction lost, i.e. all other directions are controllable on the linearized system, with the usual functional framework,
so we consider integers
p,m\in\mathbb{N}
and we assume

\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}:\mu_{\ell}\in H^{2(p+m)+3}((0,1),\mathbb{R}),\text{ with }\mathchoice{{\mu_{\ell}^{(2k+1)}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.50316pt,depth=5.57225pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\mu_{\ell}^{(2k+1)}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.10315pt,depth=5.57225pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\mu_{\ell}^{(2k+1)}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.41426pt,depth=6.45059pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\mu_{\ell}^{(2k+1)}\,\smash{\vrule height=4.43056pt,depth=7.03615pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}=0\text{ for }0\leqslant k\leqslant p-1,\ \ell\in\{1,2\}.

\mathbf{(H)_{lin,K,2}}:\text{there exists $C>0$ such that }\forall j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\left\{K\right\},\left\|\left(\displaystyle\left\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\right)_{1\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2}\right\|\geqslant\displaystyle\frac{C}{j^{2p+3}}.

To control the complex direction
\langle\psi(t),\varphi_{K}\rangle
of the nonlinear system (
1.1
),
we will use a power series expansion of the order
2
, i.e. the second-order term in the Taylor expansion at
(0,0)
of the map
(u,v)\mapsto\langle\psi(t;(u,v),\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}\rangle

At this step of the proof,
we consider an integer

and we assume that the following assumptions hold:

\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,1}}
:
\forall k\in\llbracket 1,\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor\rrbracket

\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,2}}
:

,

\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,3}}
:
\forall k\in\llbracket 1,n-1\rrbracket,

\gamma_{k}:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left((\lambda_{K}-\lambda_{j})^{\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\rfloor}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}d_{j}-(\lambda_{K}-\lambda_{j})^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\rfloor}\tilde{d_{j}}\right)=0,

\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,4}}

1.5.1

In this section, we recall a result from

Definition 1.5

(Concatenation)
.

Let
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
,

and
\tilde{u}:[0,T_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}

u\#\tilde{u}:=u\mathbb{1}_{(0,T_{1})}+\tilde{u}(\cdot-T_{1})\mathbb{1}_{(T_{1},T_{1}+T_{2})}.

Let
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
be a Banach space over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
(E_{T},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E_{T}})
be a family of normed vector spaces of functions defined on
[0,T]
for
T>0
. Assume that for all
T_{1},T_{2}>0
, for all
u\in E_{T_{1}}
,
\tilde{u}\in E_{T_{2}}
,

and the following inequality holds

\left\|u\#\tilde{u}\right\|_{E_{T_{1}+T_{2}}}\leqslant\left\|u\right\|_{E_{T_{1}}}+\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{E_{T_{2}}}.

Finally, let
(\mathscr{F}_{T})_{T>0}
be a family of functions from
X\times E_{T}
to
X
for
T>0
. The goal is to prove that the map
\mathscr{F}_{T}
in locally onto. To do this, we use the following definition; this is an equivalent of the notion of tangent vector introduced by Kawski in
[
36
]
, but in infinite-dimensional case.

Definition 1.6

(Small-time continuously approximately reachable vector)
.

A vector
\xi\in X
is called a small-time continuously approximately reachable vector if there exists a continuous map
\Xi:[0,+\infty[\to X
with
\Xi(0)=\xi
such that for all
T>0
, there exist
C,\rho,s>0
and a continuous map
z\in(-\rho,\rho)\mapsto u_{z}\in E_{T}
such that

\forall z\in(-\rho,\rho),\qquad\left\|\mathscr{F}_{T}(0,u_{z})-z\Xi(T)\right\|_{X}\leqslant C|z|^{1+s}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\left\|u_{z}\right\|_{E_{T}}\leqslant C|z|^{s}.

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume the following hypotheses hold.

(A_{1})

For all
T>0
,
\mathscr{F}_{T}:X\times E_{T}\to X
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
on a neighborhood of
(0,0)
with
\mathscr{F}_{T}(0,0)=0
.

(A_{2})

For all
x\in X
,
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F}_{T}(0,0)\cdot(x,0)\in X
can be continuously extended at zero with
\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F}_{0}(0,0)\cdot(x,0)=x
.

(A_{3})

For all
T_{1},T_{2}>0
, for all
x\in X
, for all
u\in E_{T_{1}}
and
v\in E_{T_{2}}
,

\mathscr{F}_{T_{1}+T_{2}}(x,u\#v)=\mathscr{F}_{T_{2}}(\mathscr{F}_{T_{1}}(x,u),v).

(A_{4})

The space
H:=\text{Im}(\mathrm{d}\mathscr{F}_{T}(0,0)\cdot(0,\cdot))
doesn’t depend on time, is closed and of finite codimension
n
.

(A_{5})

There exists
\mathcal{M}
, a supplementary of
H
that admits a basis
(\xi_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
of small-time continuously approximately reachable vectors.

Then, for all
T>0
,
\mathscr{F}_{T}
is locally onto from zero: for all
\eta>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all

Remark 1.8

Using a change of function argument, this theorem is also true around another equilibrium than

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let

,
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
be analytic vector fields on a neighborhood of

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
\underset{i\in\llbracket r+1,d\rrbracket}{\max}|b_{i}|=L

S_{1}(f)(0)\oplus\text{Span}(f_{b_{r+1}}(0),\cdots,f_{b_{d}}(0))=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.7)

Let

. Assume that

\text{for all }b\in\mathcal{B}_{2,bad}\text{ such that }|b|\leqslant|b_{j}|,\quad f_{b}(0)\in{S}_{1}(f)(0).

(2.8)

Then, for every

, the vector

is a small-time

continuously approximately reachable vector. Thus, if

\text{for all }b\in\mathcal{B}_{2,bad}\text{ such that }|b|\leqslant L,\quad f_{b}(0)\in{S}_{1}(f)(0),

(2.9)

the control-affine system (

Remark 2.16

The case

]

A very simple system for which the theorem applies is the following one

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{1}^{\prime}&=&u\\

Indeed, some computations on Lie brackets give:

and

. Thus, the LARC (

\{b\in\mathcal{B}_{2,bad},\ |b|\leqslant|C_{0}|\}=\emptyset
. Then, the condition (

Theorem
2.15
gives a sufficient condition for smooth-STLC; this condition is not necessary: indeed, consider the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{1}^{\prime}&=&u\\
x_{2}^{\prime}&=&v\\

and

. Then, the LARC (

) is verified. Moreover,
f_{W_{1}^{1}}(0)=2e_{3}\notin{S}_{1}(f)(0)
and

Then, the hypothesis (

Theorem
2.15
is a corollary of Sussmann’s

-condition (see Appendix

). We propose another proof of Theorem

relying on a representation formula of the state, presented in Propositions

Proposition 2.19

(Magnus formula)

2.5
A quadratic moment problem

To prove Theorem
2.15
, we solve quadratic moment problems.

Proposition 2.23

(Moment problems)
.

Let
B
be a finite subset of
\mathcal{B}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}_{2,\text{good}}
and
b_{0}\in B\cap\mathcal{B}_{2,good}
.
There exist
u,v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
such that,

\text{for every }b\in B,\qquad\eta_{b}(1,(u,v))=\delta_{b,b_{0}}.

(2.19)

Proof.

One may assume that

B=\left\{M_{\nu}^{1},\ M_{\nu}^{2},\ C_{\mu,\nu},\ (\mu,\nu)\in\llbracket 0,\mu^{*}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\nu^{*}\rrbracket\right\}.
One considers
N=\mu^{*}+\nu^{*}+1
. We are looking for
u
and
v
in the form of
u=\phi^{(N)}
and
v=\psi^{(N)}
, with
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
. One recalls that for
b\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\eta_{b}=\xi_{b}
. Then, for every
\mu\in\llbracket 0,\mu^{*}\rrbracket
,
\eta_{M_{\mu}^{1}}(1,(u,v))=u_{\mu+1}(1)=0
and
\eta_{M_{\mu}^{2}}(1,(u,v))=v_{\mu+1}(1)=0
, using (
2.11
). Thus, (
2.19
) is already verified for
b\in B\cap\mathcal{B}_{1}
.

Thanks to the choice of
N
, the equations (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) give: for all
(\mu,\nu)\in\llbracket 0,\mu^{*}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\nu^{*}\rrbracket
,

\eta_{C_{\mu,\nu}}(1,(u,v))=\xi_{C_{\mu,\nu}}(1,(u,v))=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{\nu}}{\nu!}u_{\lfloor\frac{\mu}{2}\rfloor+1}(t)v_{\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Using integrations by parts, one has

\eta_{C_{\mu,\nu}}(1,(u,v))=(-1)^{N-\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t)\left(\frac{(1-\cdot)^{\nu}}{\nu!}\phi^{(N-\lfloor\frac{\mu}{2}\rfloor-1)}\right)^{(N-\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor)}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Using the Leibniz formula, one gets:
\eta_{C_{\mu,\nu}}(1,(u,v))=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t)f_{\mu,\nu}(t)\mathrm{d}t
, with

f_{\mu,\nu}:t\in(0,1)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\min(N-\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor,\nu)}\binom{N-\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+N-\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}}{(\nu-k)!}(1-t)^{\nu-k}\phi^{(2N-1-\mu-k)}(t).

Step 1: There exists
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
s.t. the family
\mathcal{F}:=\left(f_{\mu,\nu}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant\mu\leqslant\mu^{*}\\
0\leqslant\nu\leqslant\nu^{*}\end{subarray}}
is linearly independent on
(0,1)
.
Let
m\geqslant\nu^{*}+2
and
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
. One defines
\phi:t\mapsto e^{t^{m}}\chi(t)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
. By induction, one can prove that: for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
P_{l}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
with
\deg(P_{l})=l(m-1)
such that, for all
t\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
,
\phi^{(l)}(t)=e^{t^{m}}P_{l}(t)
. Then,

\forall(\mu,\nu)\in\llbracket 0,\mu^{*}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\nu^{*}\rrbracket,\quad\deg\left(\mathchoice{{f_{\mu,\nu}(t)e^{-t^{m}}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.43962pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})}}{{f_{\mu,\nu}(t)e^{-t^{m}}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.03961pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})}}{{f_{\mu,\nu}(t)e^{-t^{m}}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.29202pt,depth=2.20056pt}}_{\,(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})}}{{f_{\mu,\nu}(t)e^{-t^{m}}\,\smash{\vrule height=4.03334pt,depth=2.78612pt}}_{\,(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})}}\right)=\nu+(m-1)(2N-1-\mu).

(2.20)

Thanks to the choice of
m
, all the degrees (
2.20
) are different. Then,
\left(t\mapsto f_{\mu,\nu}(t)e^{-t^{m}}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant\mu\leqslant\mu^{*}\\
0\leqslant\nu\leqslant\nu^{*}\end{subarray}}
is linearly independent on
(0,1)
. Consequently, one obtains the result for
\mathcal{F}
.

Step 2: Construction of
u
and
v
.
One chooses
u:=\phi^{(N)}
, with the function
\phi
obtained at step
1
. By construction of
\phi
, there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
, solution to the following moment problems

\text{for every }(\mu,\nu)\in\llbracket 0,\mu^{*}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\nu^{*}\rrbracket,\quad\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t)f_{\mu,\nu}(t)dt=\delta_{C_{\mu,\nu},b_{0}}.

We conclude with
v:=\psi^{(N)}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

Let

\mathchoice{{\varphi^{(2(l-j))}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.50316pt,depth=5.57225pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\varphi^{(2(l-j))}\,\smash{\vrule height=7.10315pt,depth=5.57225pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\varphi^{(2(l-j))}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.41426pt,depth=6.45059pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}{{\varphi^{(2(l-j))}\,\smash{\vrule height=4.43056pt,depth=7.03615pt}}_{\,\{0,1\}}}=0

\int_{0}^{T}v(t)\left(\int_{0}^{t}h_{1,2}(t,s)u(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(3.13)

The formulas (
3.2
) and (
3.8
) lead to

\left\langle\xi(T),\psi_{K}(T)\right\rangle=\mathcal{F}_{T}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{G}_{T}(u,v)+\mathcal{F}_{T}^{2}(v).

(3.14)

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
satisfy
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
. Then, as
\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
,

\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}\right).

(3.15)

Proof.

First step: linear remainder
. One defines
\Lambda:t\in[0,T]\mapsto\psi(t;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(t)
. The function
\Lambda
is solution to the following PDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}i\partial_{t}\Lambda=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Lambda-(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\Lambda-(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\psi_{1},&(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
\Lambda(\cdot,0)=\Lambda(\cdot,1)=0,&(0,T),\\
\Lambda(0,\cdot)=0,&(0,1).\end{array}\right.

As
\psi_{1}(t)\in H^{2p+3}_{(0)}(0,1)
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
verify
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
, Remark
3.3
ensures that
(u(t)\mu_{1}+v(t)\mu_{2})\psi_{1}(t)\in H^{2p+3}\cap H^{2p+1}_{(0)}(0,1)
.
Then, we can use the regularization inequality given by (
3.3
) with
m=0
to obtain

\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}\leqslant C\left\|(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}\right)}.

(3.16)

Note that

\left\|u\mu_{1}\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}\right)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}\left\|\mu_{1}\varphi_{1}e^{-i\lambda_{1}t}\right\|^{2}_{H^{2p+3}}\mathrm{d}t=\left\|\mu_{1}\varphi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2p+3}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}.

We can obtain the same estimate for
\left\|v\mu_{2}\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}\right)}
. Then, thanks to (
3.16
), one gets

\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right).

Second step: quadratic remainder
.
Using the same strategy, one defines
\Lambda:t\in[0,T]\mapsto\psi(t;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(t)-\Psi(t)
. The function
\Lambda
is solution to the following PDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}i\partial_{t}\Lambda=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Lambda-(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}\right),&(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
\Lambda(\cdot,0)=\Lambda(\cdot,1)=0,&(0,T),\\
\Lambda(0,\cdot)=0,&(0,1).\end{array}\right.

By the same way, one can use the estimate (
3.3
) with
m=0
in this equation to obtain

\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}\leqslant C\left\|(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T),H^{2p+3})}.

Computing this norm and using the algebra structure of the Sobolev space
H^{2p+3}(0,1)
,

\begin{gathered}\left\|u\mu_{1}\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}\right)}\\
\leqslant C\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}\left\|\mu_{1}\right\|_{H^{2p+3}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.\end{gathered}

(3.17)

Then, using the inequality proved in the first step in (
3.17
), we obtain

\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right).

Third step: cubic remainder
. Once again, one defines
\Lambda:t\in[0,T]\mapsto\psi(t;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(t)-\Psi(t)-\xi(t)
. The function
\Lambda
is solution to the following PDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}i\partial_{t}\Lambda=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Lambda-(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi\right),&(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
\Lambda(\cdot,0)=\Lambda(\cdot,1)=0,&(0,T),\\
\Lambda(0,\cdot)=0,&(0,1).\end{array}\right.

One uses the estimate (
3.3
) with
m=0
in this equation to obtain

\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}\leqslant C\left\|(u\mu_{1}+v\mu_{2})\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi\right)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T),H^{2p+3})}.

Using the algebra structure of the Sobolev space
H^{2p+3}(0,1)
,

\begin{gathered}\left\|u\mu_{1}\left(\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}\right)}\\
\leqslant C\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}\left\|\mu_{1}\right\|_{H^{2p+3}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.\end{gathered}

(3.18)

Then, using the inequality proved in the second step in (

\left\|\psi(\cdot;(u,v),\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}-\Psi-\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{2p+3}_{(0)}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}\right).

Let

) on

with initial data

in

and controls

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\psi(T;\Gamma_{T_{1},T}(\psi_{0},\psi_{f}),\psi_{0})\right)=\psi_{f},

with the following boundary conditions

u_{2}(T)=\cdots=u_{k+1}(T)=0,\quad\text{and}\quad v_{2}(T)\cdots=v_{k+1}(T)=0.

Finally, for all

(3.19)

Moreover, if
\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle\neq 0
, one can ensure
v_{1}(T)=0
.

Remark 3.9
.

The hypothesis
\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle\neq 0
allows us to solve the moment problems (given by the linear test) in the direction
\varphi_{1}
with the control

\langle\Psi(T),\psi_{1}(T)\rangle=i\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle u_{1}(T)+i\langle\mu_{2}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle v_{1}(T).

Thus, one can impose the border condition

4
Proof of the main theorem

The proof of the main theorem is divided in the following steps.

1.

In the first step, we use the quadratic expansion of the solution in the direction
\psi_{K}(T)
(given by Proposition
4.1
) to move along the real direction lost
i^{n}\varphi_{K}
.

2.

In the second step, we correct linearly the system in the other directions
\mathcal{H}
, thanks to Theorem
3.8
. Then, we examine the impact of this second step on the correction made in the first one. A priori, the second step can destroy the work of the first and we use weak estimates to prove that this is not the case.

3.

We use a trick introduced by Kawski in
[
35
]
and executed by Bournissou in
[
16
]
to move along the other real direction lost
i^{n+1}\varphi_{K}
.

4.

We apply Theorem
1.7
(based on the Brouwer fixed-point theorem) to obtain STLC.

In order to extract the leading terms of the dynamic of the system (as in the finite-dimensional case, with the Magnus-type representation formula), we manipulate the expression given by (
3.14
). This is the purpose of the following subsection.

4.1
Asymptotic estimates on the quadratic term of the solution

Notation
.

For
T>0
,
j\geqslant 1
and

We define
q:=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. This notation will be used throughout the rest of the article. As a remainder, the sequence
(c_{j})_{j\geqslant 1}
is defined in Section
1.3
as
c_{j}=\left\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{K}\right\rangle
. The goal of this subsection is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
be such that
u_{i}(T)=v_{i}(T)=0
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant q+1
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,1}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,2}}
and
\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,3}}
hold. Then,

\begin{gathered}\left\langle\xi(T),\psi_{K}(T)\right\rangle=-iA_{q+1}^{1}\int_{0}^{T}u_{q+1}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t+i^{n}\gamma_{n}\int_{0}^{T}u_{q+1}(t)v_{n-q}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t\\
-iA_{q+1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}v_{q+1}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t+R_{T,1}^{q+1}(u)+\rho_{T}^{q+1}(u,v)+R_{T,2}^{q+1}(v).\end{gathered}

(4.1)

with

R_{T,1}^{q+1}(u):=\displaystyle(-1)^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\omega_{j}^{q+1}\nu_{j}^{q+1}I^{j}_{T}(u_{q+1},u_{q+1}),

R_{T,2}^{q+1}(v):=\displaystyle(-1)^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{c_{j}}\omega_{j}^{q+1}\nu_{j}^{q+1}I^{j}_{T}(v_{q+1},v_{q+1}),

The proof is divided into several lemmas. The first one, strongly inspired by
[
18

5.1
]
, focuses on the first term on the right-hand side of the decomposition given in (
3.14
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T>0
,
0\leqslant l\leqslant q
and assume that
u\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
such that
u_{i}(T)=0
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant l+1
. If
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
holds, then

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{F}_{T}^{1}(u)=-u_{1}(T)\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}e^{i\nu_{j}T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}u(s)e^{i\omega_{j}s}\mathrm{d}s\right)+\frac{u_{1}(T)^{2}}{2}e^{i\omega_{K}T}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\\
-i\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}A_{k}^{1}\int_{0}^{T}u_{k}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t+R_{T,1}^{l+1}(u).\end{gathered}

(4.2)

Proof.

We prove the statement by a finite induction on
l\in\llbracket 0,q\rrbracket
.
If the formula is true for a fixed

, then, for all
u\in L^{2}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
such that
u_{i}(T)=0
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant l+2
, an integration by parts gives

(-1)^{l}R_{T,1}^{l+1}(u)=A+B,

(4.3)

with

A=-i\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\omega_{j}^{l+1}\nu_{j}^{l+2}I^{j}_{T}(u_{l+2},u_{l+1}),\hskip 28.45274ptB:=-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\omega_{j}^{l+1}\nu_{j}^{l+1}\mathcal{I}_{T}(u_{l+2},u_{l+1}).

Then, with one integration by parts, we obtain

A=-i\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\omega_{j}^{l+1}\nu_{j}^{l+2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{l+2}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t+(-1)^{l}R_{T,1}^{l+2}(u).

(4.4)

B=\frac{i\omega_{K}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\omega_{j}^{l+1}\nu_{j}^{l+1}\int_{0}^{T}u_{l+2}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega_{K}t}\mathrm{d}t.

(4.5)

Using (
4.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and the induction hypothesis, we conclude. The initialization is proved by the same manipulations, but there are boundary terms. The starting point is the equality given by (
3.11
) and (

\mathcal{F}_{T}^{1}(u)=-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}I^{j}_{T}(u,u).

All series converge thanks to the hypothesis
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\leqslant p
(see Remark
1.2
).
∎

One recalls that, for any integer

, the negative

Using the expression of Lemma
4.2

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
T>0
and assume that
u\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
such that
u_{i}(T)=0
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant q+1
. If
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
and
\mathbf{(H)_{quad,K,1}}

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.2
with

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{F}_{T}^{1}(u)=-u_{1}(T)\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}e^{i\nu_{j}T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}u(s)e^{i\omega_{j}s}\mathrm{d}s\right)+\frac{u_{1}(T)^{2}}{2}e^{i\omega_{K}T}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\\

\begin{gathered}\hskip-3.98337ptB=\sum_{k=0}^{l}(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{d_{j}}\omega_{j}^{k}\nu_{j}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{T}(u_{k+1},v_{k})+i\sum_{k=0}^{l}(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{d_{j}}\omega_{j}^{k+1}\nu_{j}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{T}(u_{k+1},v_{k+1})\\

(4.10)

Proof.

Proof.

Proposition A.10

,
j\geqslant 1
,

F_{M_{j}^{i}}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{M_{j-1}^{i}}(0)\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, for every
j\geqslant 2
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
,

F_{W_{j,l}^{i}}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{W_{j-1,l}^{i}}(0)\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad F_{C_{j,l}}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{C_{j-2,l}}(0)\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, the extended system satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.15
with dimension
d+2
.
By the induction hypothesis, the extended system is
W^{m,\infty}
-STLC.
By choosing trajectories of the extended system starting and finishing at
u,v=0
,
one concludes that the initial system
x^{\prime}=f_{0}(x)+uf_{1}(x)+vf_{2}(x)
is
W^{m+1,\infty}
-STLC.
∎

Definition A.14

(Support)

. For

, a Hall set, we denote by

.

Definition A.15
.

We consider the (unital associative) algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(X)
of formal power series generated by
\mathcal{A}(X)
. An element

a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

as the Lie algebra of formal power series

for each

.

We consider the formal differential equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{y}(t)&=y(t)(X_{0}+u(t)X_{1}+v(t)X_{2}),\\
y(0)&=1,\end{aligned}\right.

(A.9)

whose solution is a formal series valued function
y:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(X)
(see
[

, Section
2.2
]
).
By
[

, Section
2.4
, Theorem 41]
, for every
t>0

\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(t;X,(u,v))\in\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(X)
such that

y(t)=\exp(tX_{0})\exp\left(\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(t;X,(u,v))\right).

(A.10)

If
\mathcal{B}\subset\Br(X)
is a Hall set, there exists a unique family
(\eta_{b})_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
of maps
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
called coordinates of the pseudo-first kind associated with

).
Moreover, by

, Section 2.5]
, for every
t>0
and

y(t)=\underset{b\in\mathcal{B}}{\overset{\leftarrow}{\prod}}e^{\xi_{b}(t,(u,v))b}.

(A.11)

Let
\mathcal{B}

A.4
Some lemmas about ODEs

Lemma A.16
.

Let
\delta>0
and
z\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(B(0,{\delta}),\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\left\|z\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant\delta
. We note
x(\cdot;z,0)
the solution to the system:

\left\{\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}&=&z(x)\\
x(0)&=&0\end{matrix}\right.
. Then,

\left\|x(1;z,0)-z(0)\right\|\leqslant\left\|z(0)\right\|\left\|Dz\right\|_{\infty}e^{\left\|Dz\right\|_{\infty}}.

Proof.

This lemma is proved in
[
9
, Lemma 160]
.
∎

Lemma A.17
.

Let
\delta>0
and
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:B(0,2\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be real continuous functions, with
f_{0}(0)=0
. There exists
r(\delta)>0
, such that, for all
T_{1}<T<T_{1}+1
,
p\in B\left(0,r(\delta)\right)
and
u,v\in L^{\infty}(T_{1},T)
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<r(\delta)
, one has

\forall t\in[T_{1},T],\quad x(T;(u,v),p,T_{1})\in B(0,\delta).

(A.12)

Proof.

This result is linked to continuity with respect to the initial condition.
∎

Corollary A.18
.

Let
\delta>0
and
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:B(0,2\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be real continuous functions, with
f_{0}(0)=0
. There exists
r(\delta),C(\delta)>0
, such that, for all
T_{1}<T<T_{1}+1
,
p\in B\left(0,r(\delta)\right)
and
u,v\in L^{\infty}(T_{1},T)
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<r(\delta)
, one has

\left\|x(T;(u,v),p,T_{1})\right\|\leqslant C(\delta)\left(\left\|p\right\|+\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Proof.

This statement is based on the previous lemma and the Grönwall’s inequality.
∎

Lemma A.19
.

Let
f_{0}:B(0,2\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function, for
\delta>0
, with
f_{0}(0)=0
. There exist
C,\varepsilon>0
such that, for all
T_{1}<T<T_{1}+1
and
p\in B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)
,

\left\|x(T;(0,0),p,T_{1})-e^{(T-T_{1})Df_{0}(0)}p\right\|\leqslant C\left\|p\right\|^{2}.

Proof.

By definition, there exists
h>0
, s.t. for all
x\in B(0,h)
,

\left\|f_{0}(x)-f_{0}(0)-Df_{0}(0)x\right\|\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|D^{2}f_{0}(0)\right\|+1\right)\left\|x\right\|^{2}.

(A.13)

Let
p\in B(0,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon=\min\left(r(\delta),r(h)\right)
. Using the Duhamel’s principle,

\begin{gathered}\left\|x(T;(0,0),p,T_{1})-e^{(T-T_{1})Df_{0}(0)}p\right\|\\
\leqslant\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\left\|e^{(T-s)Df_{0}(0)}\right\|\left\|f_{0}(x(s,(0,0),p,T_{1}))-Df_{0}(0)x(s,(0,0),p,T_{1})\right\|\mathrm{d}s.\end{gathered}

Thank to Lemma
A.17
, one can use the inequality (
A.13
) and Corollary
A.18
gives the result.
∎

Lemma A.20
.

Let
\delta>0
and
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:B(0,2\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions, with
f_{0}(0)=0
. There exist
C,\varepsilon>0
, such that, for all
T_{1}<T<T_{1}+1
,
p\in B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)
and
u,v\in L^{\infty}(T_{1},T)
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<\varepsilon
, one has

\left\|x(T;(u,v),p,T_{1})-x(T;(u,v),0,T_{1})-x(T;(0,0),p,T_{1})\right\|\leqslant C\left(\left\|p\right\|\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|p\right\|^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
p\in B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)
,
u,v\in L^{\infty}(T_{1},T)
for
\varepsilon
small enough. For simplicity, we use the notations:
x:=x(\cdot;(u,v),p,T_{1})
,
x_{u,v}:=x(\cdot;(u,v),0,T_{1})
and
x_{p}:=x(\cdot;(0,0),p,T_{1})
. Let
z:=x-x_{u,v}-x_{p}
. By definition,
z
is solution to the following control-affine system

\begin{gathered}z^{\prime}=\left(f_{0}(z+x_{u,v}+x_{p})-f_{0}(x_{u,v}+x_{p})\right)+\left(f_{0}(x_{u,v}+x_{p})-f_{0}(x_{u,v})-f_{0}(x_{p})\right)\\
+u\left(f_{1}(z+x_{u,v}+x_{p})-f_{1}(x_{u,v})\right)+v\left(f_{2}(z+x_{u,v}+x_{p})-f_{2}(x_{u,v})\right),\end{gathered}

with
z(T_{1})=0
. We estimate each term of this decomposition:

1.

the first one scales like
\left\|z\right\|
,

2.

the second one is shaped like
\left\|p\right\|\left(\left\|p\right\|+\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{\infty}\right)
,

3.

the other terms are like
\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|z\right\|+\left\|p\right\|\right)
.

The Grönwall’s lemma gives the result.
∎

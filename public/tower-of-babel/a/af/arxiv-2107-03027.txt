Lemma 2.2

Let

(X({\mathfrak{W}}_{\infty})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})_{\Delta}\cong X(W_{\infty})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

If

{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K_{n})\to({\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{n}))^{\Delta}={\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{n})

has finite kernel and cokernel for all
n\geq 0
. But this follows easily from the fact that
\Delta
is of order 2, combined with a similar argument using Tate local duality for the abelian variety
B^{(R)}/K
, which has good reduction at the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
, as that given in the proof of Theorem 3.12 of
[
3
]
.
∎

For each
n\geq 0
, let
L(B^{(R)}/F_{n},s)
(resp.
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},s)
) be the complex
L
-series of
B^{(R)}/F_{n}
(resp.
B^{(R)}/K_{n}
). Let
\chi_{n}
be the non-trival
character of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(F_{n}/K_{n})
, and write
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},\chi_{n},s)
for the twist of
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},s)
by
\chi_{n}
. Then we have

(5.6)

L(B^{(R)}/F_{n},s)=L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},s)L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},\chi_{n},s).

Theorem 5.5
.

For all
R
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
satisfying the Twisting Hypothesis, and all
n\geq 0
, we have
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},\chi_{n},1)\neq 0
, and so

L(B^{(R)}/F_{n},1)\neq 0
if and only if
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},1)\neq 0
.

Proof.

This theorem, which is valid for all primes
q\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
is an equivalent form of Theorem 1.5 of
[
3
]
. Put
\beta=\sqrt{-q}
. Now

L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},\chi_{n},s)
is the complex
L
-series of the twist of
B^{(R)}
by the quadratic extension
F_{n}/K_{n}
. As
F_{n}=K_{n}(\sqrt{-\beta R})
, it follows
that the twist of
B^{(R)}
by
F_{n}/K_{n}
is just
B^{(-\beta)}/K_{n}
. Put
H_{n}=HK_{n}
, where we recall that
H
is the Hilbert class field of
K
. Then
the complex
L
-series of
B^{(-\beta)}/K_{n}
is the same as the complex
L
-series
L(A^{(-\beta)}/H_{n},s)
of
A^{(-\beta)}/H_{n}
, where
A/H
is the basic Gross
{\mathbb{Q}}
-curve defined over
H
. Since Theorem 1.5 of
[
3
]
asserts that
L(A^{(-\beta)}/H_{n},1)\neq 0
for all
n\geq 0
, the proof is complete.
∎

The following deep theorem is a consequence of the main conjecture of Iwasawa theory for the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension

F_{\infty}/F
. As above,
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
will denote a characteristic power series of the
\Gamma
-module
X(F_{\infty})
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
n\geq 0
be any non-negative integer, and let
R
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
satisfy the Twisting Hypothesis. Then
c_{F_{\infty}}(u\zeta-1)\neq 0
for all
2^{n}
-th roots of unity
\zeta
if and only if
L(B^{(R)}/F_{n},1)\neq 0
.

Combining this result with Theorem
5.5
and Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, we immediately obtain

Corollary 5.7
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K_{n})
is finite if and only if
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},1)\neq 0
.

Of course, this is not the place to give a detailed proof of this main conjecture. We simply say that similar methods, using the Euler system of elliptic units, to those used to prove Theorem 7.13 in §7 of
[
3
]
, can be generalized to prove the desired main conjecture for
B^{(R)}/F_{\infty}
, from which the above theorem follows as an immediate corollary.

Proof.

We now prove Theorem
5.2
. We first suppose we are in case (i), so that
q\equiv 15\ \mathrm{mod}\ 32
and
R=1
. By a theorem of Rohrlich
[
12
]
, we have
L(B/K,1)=L(A/H,1)\neq 0
. Thus, by Corollary
5.7
,
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B/K)
is finite, and, for all
n\geq 1
,
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B/K_{n})
is finite if and only if
L(B/K_{n},1)\neq 0
. Moreover, as
s_{\infty}(R)=2
, it follows from Theorem
4.3
that
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B/F_{\infty})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
as an abelian group, and so Lemma
5.4
implies that
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B/K_{n})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank at most 1 for all
n\geq 1
. This completes the proof of case (i). Next suppose we are in the case (ii), so that
q\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 16
and
R=r
, with
r\equiv 5\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
and
r
inert in
K
. Then Theorem 1.3 of
[
3
]
shows that
L(B^{(R)}/K,1)=L(A^{(R)}/H,1)\neq 0
. Again invoking Corollary
5.7
, it follows that
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K)
is finite, and, for all
n\geq 1
,
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K_{n})
is finite if and only if
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},1)\neq 0
. But
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{\infty})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
as an abelian group, whence, again by Lemma
5.4
,
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K_{n})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-crank at most
1
for all
n\geq 1
, and the proof of case (ii) is complete. Finally, suppose we are in case (iii), so that
q\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 16
and
R=-r
, with
r\equiv 3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
and
r
inert in
K
.
Now
B^{(R)}
is in fact defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and
L(B^{(R)}/K,s)=L(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s)^{2}
, where
L(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s)
is the complex
L
-series of
B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}}
(see §18 of
[
8
]
). Write

\Phi(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s))=(-qr/(2\pi))^{hs}\Gamma(s)^{h}L(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s),

where we recall that
h
is the class number of
K
. Now, since
h
is odd and
2
splits in
K
, Theorem 19.1.1 of
[
8
]
shows that
\Phi(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s)
satisfies
the functional equation

\Phi(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s)=-\Phi(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},2-s).

Hence
\Phi(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s))
must have a zero of odd order at
s=1
, and so
L(B^{(R)}/{\mathbb{Q}},1)=0
, whence also
L(B^{(R)}/K,1)=0
, which, in turn,
clearly implies that
L(B^{(R)}/F,1)=0
, and
L(B^{(R)}/K_{n},1)=0
for all
n\geq 0
. Now
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{\infty})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
as an abelian group, and

{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{\infty})^{\Gamma}
is infinite because
L(B^{(R)}/F,1)=0
. Hence, since the only infinite subgroup
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is the whole group, it follows that

(5.7)

{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{\infty})^{\Gamma}={\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{\infty})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}.

Thus, by Lemma
5.4
, for all
n\geq 0
, we have
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/F_{n})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, whence
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{P}}^{\infty}}(B^{(R)}/K_{n})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank 1. This completes
the proof of case (iii), and so of the whole theorem.
∎

We end by discussing one numerical example with
s_{\infty}(R)=4
, which arose in
[
1
]
. Let
q=7
, so that
B
is the elliptic curve

X_{0}(49)
, with equation

(5.8)

y^{2}+xy=x^{3}-x^{2}-2x-1.

Take
R=741=3.13.19
, and define
E=X_{0}(49)^{(R)}
, whence
s_{\infty}(R)=4
. By Theorem
4.4
, we then have
g_{F_{\infty}}(R)+e_{F_{\infty}}(R)=3
. It is shown in
[
1
]
that
E(K)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
has
dimension
2
over
K
and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/K)(2)=0
. We shall prove the following result. We recall that in this case
F_{\infty}=K(E_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}})
, where
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-7})
, and
F_{n}=K(E_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n+2}})
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
E=X_{0}(49)^{(R)}
, with
R=741
. Then
g_{F_{\infty}}=2
, and
e_{F_{\infty}}(R)=1
, so that
E(F_{\infty})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
has
K
-dimension equal to
2
, and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/F_{\infty})({\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})={\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Moreover, for all integers
n\geq 0
, we have that
E(F_{n})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
has
K
-dimension equal to
2
and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/F_{n})({\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})
is finite.

Proof.

There are two ingredients to the proof. The first is a MAGMA calculation. Let
C/K
be the twist of
E/K
by the quadratic extension
K_{1}/K
. Then a MAGMA computation shows that
C(K)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
has dimension
0
and
\hbox{\cyr X}(C/K)(2)=0
. It follows easily that
E(K_{1})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
has
K
-dimension
2
and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/K_{1})({\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})
is finite, whence
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
has corank
2
for
n=1
. We now recall that (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.13]
), for all
n\geq 0
both the
K
-dimensions of
E(K_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
and the corank of
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/K_{n})({\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})
do not change if we replace
K_{n}
with
F_{n}
. Thus the MAGMA calculation proves that
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/F_{1})
as well as
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/F_{1})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank equal to 2 by Lemma
5.4
. The second ingredient in the the proof is a simple remark on the characteristic power series
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
of
X(F_{\infty})
. By the Weierstrass Preparation Theorem, we may assume that
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
is a monic polynomial of degree 3, because
X(F_{\infty})
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-module of rank 3. The characteristic power series of the Pontrjagin dual of
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/F_{\infty})={\mathrm{Hom}}(X(F_{\infty}),E_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}})
is given by
c_{F_{\infty}}(u(1+T)-1)
. Moreover, since
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/F)
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank equal to 2, we conclude that
(T-(u-1))^{2}
must divide
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
. Suppose now that there exists an integer
n>0
such that
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}}(E/F_{n})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank at least 3, and choose the smallest integer
n
with this property. Then it follows from Lemma
5.4
that
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
must also be dvisible by
T-(u\zeta-1)
for some primitive
2^{n}
-th root of unity
\zeta
. But, as
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
is a polynomial with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
it would then be divisible by the product of all
T-(u\alpha-1)
, where
\alpha
runs over all primitive
2^{n}
-th roots of unity. Since this product is an irreducible polynomial of degree
2^{n-1}

with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, and
c_{F_{\infty}}(T)
has degree 3, we must have
2^{n-1}=1
, or equivalently
n=1
. But our MAGMA calculation has
already shown that
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}(E/F_{1})
has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-corank 2, and the proof is complete. ∎

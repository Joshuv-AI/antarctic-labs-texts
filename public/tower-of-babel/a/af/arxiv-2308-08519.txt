Auslander–Iyama correspondence for exact dg categories
Xiaofa Chen

Abstract
. We extend Auslander–Iyama correspondence to the setting of exact dg categories. By specializing it to exact dg categories concentrated in degree zero, we obtain a generalization of the higher Auslander correspondence for exact categories due to Ebrahimi–Nasr-Isfahani (in the case of exact categories with split retractions).

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative artinian ring.
An Artin
R
-algebra is an
R
-algebra which is finitely generated as an
R
-module.
Let
A
be an Artin
R
-algebra.
We denote by
\mathrm{mod}A
the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules.
Recall that the
dominant dimension
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is the largest
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that any projective object in
\mathcal{A}
has an injective coresolution whose first
n
terms are projective.
The dominant dimension of
A
is defined as the dominant dimension of
\mathrm{mod}A
.
An Artin
R
-algebra
A
is
representation-finite
if
\mathrm{mod}A
has finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects.
An Artin
R
-algebra
\Gamma
is a (1-)
Auslander algebra
if we have

\mathrm{gl.dim}\Gamma\leq 2\leq\mathrm{dom.dim}\Gamma.

Let us recall Auslander’s classical theorem
[
2
]
, which was the starting point and a key tool in developing Auslander–Reiten theory, see for example in
[
1
, Chapter VI, Proposition 5.11]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Auslander correspondence)
.

There is a bijection between the Morita classes of representation-finite Artin
R
-algebras
A
and the Morita classes of Auslander
R
-algebras
\Gamma
.
The bijection is induced by the map
M\mapsto\mathrm{End}_{A}(M)
, where
M
is an additive generator for
\mathrm{mod}A
.

It is natural to ask whether it is possible to replace the number
2
, which appears in the definition of Auslander algebras, by any integer
\geq 2
.
This was done by Iyama
[
13
, Theorem 0.2]
.
Let
d\geq 1
be any integer.
Recall that an
A
-module
M
is
d
-cluster-tilting

[
14
]
if

\displaystyle\mathrm{add}M

\displaystyle=\{X\in\mathrm{mod}A\mid\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(M,X)=0\text{ for $1\leq i\leq d-1$}\}

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\mathrm{mod}A\mid\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(Y,M)=0\text{ for $1\leq i\leq d-1$}\}.

Note that the notion of
1
-cluster-tilting module is equivalent to that of additive generator for
\mathrm{mod}A
.
An Artin
R
-algebra
\Gamma
is
d
-Auslander
if we have

\mathrm{gl.dim}\Gamma\leq d+1\leq\mathrm{dom.dim}\Gamma.

Two pairs
(M,A)
and
(N,B)
, where
A
and
B
are Artin
R
-algebras and
M
(resp.
N
) is a
d
-cluster-tilting
A
-module (resp.
B
-module), are
equivalent
, if there is an equivalence of
R
-categories
\mathrm{mod}A\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathrm{mod}B
which restricts to an equivalence
\mathrm{add}M\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathrm{add}N
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
13
]
, Auslander–Iyama correspondence)
.

There is a bijection between the equivalence classes of pairs (A,M), where
A
is an Artin
R
-algebras and
M
is a

d
-cluster-tilting module, and the Morita classes of
d
-Auslander
R
-algebras
\Gamma
.
The bijection is induced by the map
M\mapsto\mathrm{End}_{A}(M)
, where
M
is
d
-cluster-tilting
A
-module.

In this article, we extend Iyama’s higher Auslander correspondence to the setting of exact dg categories.
Let
k
be a commutative ring. We fix an integer
d\geq 1
.
We refer to
[
6
]
for the necessary definitions and properties of exact dg categories and to
[
19
]
for extriangulated categories.

An extriangulated category with enough projectives is
d
-Auslander
if it has positive global dimension at most
d+1
and dominant dimension at least
d+1
. It is
reduced
if moreover the only projective-injectives are
0
.
An exact dg category
\mathcal{A}
is
d
-Auslander
if the extriangulated category
H^{0}(\mathcal{A})
is
d
-Auslander.

Let
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
be a pair where
\mathcal{P}
is a connective additive dg category and
\mathcal{I}
is an additive dg subcategory of
\mathcal{P}
.
For
n\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}^{(n)}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}

the full dg subcategory of
\mathrm{pretr}(\mathcal{P})
consisting of the objects in

\mathcal{P}\ast\Sigma\mathcal{P}\ast\cdots\ast\Sigma^{n-1}\mathcal{P}\ast\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{P}\cap\mathrm{ker}\mathrm{Ext}^{\geq 1}(-,\mathcal{I}).

The pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
is called
d
-cluster tilting
if
H^{0}(\mathcal{I})
is covariantly finite in
H^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{(d)}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}})
.

Theorem 1.3

(=Theorem
3.7
)
.

There is a bijective correspondence between the following:

(1)

equivalence classes of connective exact dg categories
\mathcal{A}
which are
d
-Auslander;

(2)

equivalence classes of
d
-cluster tilting pairs
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
.

The bijection from
(1)
to
(2)
sends
\mathcal{A}
to the pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
formed by the full dg subcategory
\mathcal{P}
on the projectives of
\mathcal{A}
and its full dg subcategory
\mathcal{I}
of projective-injectives. The inverse bijection sends
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
to the
\tau_{\leq 0}
-truncation of the dg subcategory
\mathcal{A}^{(d+1)}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}

of
\mathrm{pretr}(\mathcal{P})
.

We refer to Definition
3.6
for the definition of the equivalence relations mentioned in Theorem

.
Theorem

.
In this case, the direction from (2) to (1) was known to Fang–Gorsky–Palu–Plamondon–Pressland, cf.
[

We show in Example

In

(
[

There is a bijection between the following:

(1)

d
-cluster tilting subcategories of exact categories with enough injectives.

(2)

\rm{(a)}

There is another generalization of the class of Auslander algebras, namely the class of Gorenstein algebras
\Gamma
which satisfy

(1.6.1)

\mathrm{dom.dim}(\Gamma)\geq 2\geq\mathrm{id}_{\Gamma}(\Gamma)

In

In this article, additive subcategories of additive categories are assumed to be closed under direct summands.

Theorem 3.7

(Auslander–Iyama correspondence for exact dg categories)
.

There is a bijective correspondence between the following:

(1)

equivalence classes of connective exact dg categories
\mathcal{A}
which are
d
-Auslander;

(2)

equivalence classes of
d
-cluster tilting pairs.

The bijection from
(1)
to
(2)
sends
\mathcal{A}
to the pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
formed by the full dg subcategory
\mathcal{P}
on the projectives of
\mathcal{A}
and its full dg subcategory
\mathcal{I}
of projective-injectives. The inverse bijection sends
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
to the
\tau_{\leq 0}
-truncation of the dg subcategory
\mathcal{A}^{(d+1)}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}

.

Clearly, under the correspondence of Theorem
3.7
,

the exact dg categories in
(1)
whose corresponding extriangulated category is moreover reduced correspond to the pairs in
(2)
where

the exact dg categories in

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

By Proposition
3.9
, the property of being
d
-Auslander is invariant under the equivalence relation introduced in Definition
3.6
.
Notice that for a
d
-Auslander exact dg category, the inclusion
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}
induces a quasi-equivalence
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\overline{\mathcal{A}})
and
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})
is quasi-equivalent to the pretriangulated hull of the full dg subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
on the projectives.
By the proof of Proposition
3.9
, we have that the full dg subcategory
\mathcal{P}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
on the projectives is quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}
. A similar argument shows that the full dg subcategory
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}
on the projective-injectives is quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}
.
Therefore the map sending the equivalence class of
\mathcal{A}
to the equivalence class of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
is well-defined.

For a
d
-cluster tilting pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
, the inclusion dg functor
\mathcal{P}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}
induces a quasi-equivalence
\mathrm{pretr}(\mathcal{P})\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathrm{pretr}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}})
.
It is not hard to check that this induces a quasi-equivalence
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}^{(d+1)}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}},\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}}^{(d+1)}
: we observe
\mathcal{P}\ast\Sigma\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}\ast\Sigma\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}
and then by induction we have
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}\ast\cdots\ast\Sigma^{n-1}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}\ast\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{P}\ast\cdots\ast\Sigma^{n-1}\mathcal{P}\ast\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{P}\ast\cdots\ast\Sigma^{n-1}\mathcal{P}\ast\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{P}
.
Therefore the map sending the equivalence class of a pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
to the equivalence class of
\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}^{(d+1)}
is well-defined.

Combining Proposition
3.12
and Proposition
3.13
, we see that the above maps are inverse to each other.
∎

4.

In this section we give necessary and sufficient conditions on the

-cluster tilting pair

when the connective

-Aulander exact dg category

We therefore obtain a generalization of the higher Auslander correspondence for exact categories (resp. abelian categories) in
[

in the case of exact categories with split retractions (resp. in

Corollary 4.4
.

There is a bijective correspondence between the following:

(1)

equivalence classes of exact categories
\mathcal{A}
which are
d
-Auslander as extriangulated categories;

(2)

equivalence classes of
d
-cluster tilting pairs
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
satisfying the conditions in Proposition

The bijection from
(1)
to

sends
\mathcal{A}
to the pair

where
\mathcal{P}
is the full subcategory on the projectives of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{I}
is the full subcategory of projective-injectives.
The inverse bijection sends
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
to the
\tau_{\leq 0}
-truncation of the dg subcategory
\mathcal{A}^{(d+1)}_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}}

of
\mathrm{pretr}(\mathcal{P})
.

Proposition 4.7
.

with

,

Proof.

Suppose that

Then for each morphism

, there is a kernel

Supercloseness of the DDG method for a singularly perturbed convection diffusion problem on Shishkin mesh
Xiaoqi Ma, Jin Zhang, Xinyi Feng, Chunxiao Zhang

Abstract
This paper investigates the supercloseness of a singularly perturbed convection diffusion problem using the direct discontinuous Galerkin (DDG) method on a Shishkin mesh. The main technical difficulties lie in controlling the diffusion term inside the layer, the convection term outside the layer, and the inter element jump term caused by the discontinuity of the numerical solution. The main idea is to design a new composite interpolation, in which a global projection is used outside the layer to satisfy the interface conditions determined by the selection of numerical flux, thereby eliminating or controlling the troublesome terms on the unit interface; and inside the layer, Gauß Lobatto projection is used to improve the convergence order of the diffusion term. On the basis of that, by selecting appropriate parameters in the numerical flux, we obtain the supercloseness result of almost k + 1 k+1 order under an energy norm. Numerical experiments support our main theoretical conclusion.

Introduction

In this paper, we study a direct discontinuous Galerkin (DDG) method to solve the following singularly perturbed two-point boundary value problem,

\displaystyle-\epsilon w^{\prime\prime}(x)+a(x)w^{\prime}(x)+b(x)w(x)=f(x),\quad x\in\Theta:=(0,1),

(1)

\displaystyle w(0)=0,\quad w(1)=0,

in which
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a perturbation parameter. Then we assume that
a
,
b
and
f
are sufficiently smooth functions with
a(x)\geq\alpha>0
on
\overline{\Theta}
and

b-\frac{1}{2}a^{\prime}\geq\gamma>0\quad\forall x\in\overline{\Theta},

(2)

where
\alpha
and
\gamma
are fixed positive constants. These assumptions ensure that the boundary value problem (
1
) has a unique solution
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)\cap H^{2}(\Theta)
for each
f\in L^{2}(\Theta)
; see
[
1
]
. Due to the parameter
\epsilon
can be small enough, the solution
u
usually produces a layer at
x=1
of width
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon\ln(1/\epsilon))
. At this point, numerical solutions obtained using the classical finite element method typically generate significant numerical oscillations in the whole region, see
[
3
, Section 6.1]
.
In order to correctly analyze these layers without increasing the scale of solving discrete problems, layer adapted meshes, which use a general mesh outside the layer and a refined mesh inside the layer, have emerged. On the other hand, due to the general interest in only global solutions, it is urgent to design stabilization methods to eliminate oscillations, regardless of the degree of mesh refinement.

The current popular stability methods include streamline upwind/Petrov Galerkin (SUPG) method
[
7
,
12
,
17
,
25
]
, least squares finite element method
[
24
]
, bubble function stabilization
[
4
,
9
,
10
]
, local projection stabilization
[
2
,
30
]
, continuous internal penalty method
[
11
,
13
]
, the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method and so on. Among them, DG method is a finite element method that utilizes a completely discontinuous piecewise polynomial space to solve numerical solutions and test functions.
One attractive feature of this method is the flexibility provided by the combination of local approximation space and appropriate numerical flux across cell interfaces. Given this unique advantage,
since it was first proposed by Reed and Hill in 1973 for the neutron transport equation, many authors have extended it to various partial differential equations, see
[
15
,
16
,
21
,
22
]
for more details.

However, most DG methods have a common feature of introducting auxiliary variables in their formulation, such as the local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method
[
21
]
, which greatly increases the computational cost in numerical methods. To avoid this drawback, Liu and Yan
[
27
]
proposed a direct discontinuous Galerkin (DDG) method in 2009, which is based on the direct weak formulations for solutions and on appropriate numerical fluxes.
Compared with the LDG method, this method does not introduce new variables, so it has the advantages of easier formulation and efficient computation of numerical solutions.
Furthermore, the authors in
[
27
]
presented a general numerical flux formula for the derivative of the solution at the element interface, and introduced the concept of compatibility to identify suitable numerical fluxes. Later, Liu et al. studied a more effective set of parameter selection in DDG numerical flux
[
23
]
, demonstrating the optimal
L^{2}
error estimate of the DDG method for solving convection diffusion problems in
[
26
]
.
It is worth noting that the DDG method degenerates into the classical interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method by using lower order piecewise constants and linear approximations
[
5
,
6
]
. And for higher order approximations (
k\geq 2
), the DDG method exhibits considerable advantages. For example, compared to the symmetric internal penalty discontinuous Galerkin method
[
18
]
, it does not require significant penalty parameters to maintain the stability of the scheme; Compared with the nonsymmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method
[
8
]
, its computational costs will be reduced and etc, see
[
14
]
for more details.

On the other hand, for the convective term, traditional DG methods usually use purely upwind numerical fluxes.
However, for complex systems, it is difficult to construct pure upwind fluxes because they require accurate eigenstructures of the flux Jacobian matrix.
In
[
31
]
, Meng et al. used a more general upwind-biased numerical flux, which may not always be monotonic, but can be more easily constructed, providing a good approximation for smooth solutions. On the basis of that, Ma and Stynes
[
29
]
analyzed a singularly perturbed problem using the DDG method and obtained an optimal error estimate of almost
k
order. Inspired by
[
29
]
, we study the supercloseness of a singularly perturbed convection diffusion problem by using the DDG method.
In this process, the numerical flux in
[
29
]
is corrected, and the concept of compatibility is also introduced as a criterion for selecting appropriate numerical flux to ensure the stability and corresponding error estimation of the DDG method.

The “supercloseness" here represent that the numerical solution is closer to the interpolation of the exact solution, rather than the exact solution itself.
The main technical difficulties of the DDG method for supercloseness analysis lie in the construction of special interpolation functions and the selection of parameters in numerical flux. In
[
31
]
, Meng et al. constructed a suitable global projection based on the definition of upwind-biased flux, which can eliminate boundary terms between elements, thereby obtaining the desired supercloseness results.
In this paper, we design a composite interpolation for the solution
u
. More specifically, Gauß Lobatto projection is used within the layer, and the global projection provided by
[
31
]
is used outside the layer. Then by choosing some specific values of the parameters in numerical flux, we obtain the supercloseness of almost
k+1
order in the corresponding norm.
Finally, a numerical experiment confirm the theoretical result.

The basic framework of this paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we provide
a
priori information of the solution, and introduce a layer-adapted mesh applicable to the continuous problem. In Section 3, the DDG method is presented and the concept of compatibility was given. Then, by defining a new composite interpolation, uniform convergence and supercloseness results are obtained in an energy norm in Section 4 and 5, respectively. Finally, our theoretical result can be validated through a numerical experiment.

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{\Delta h_{j}}{\epsilon\beta_{0,j}}}\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{N/2}\cup I_{N/2+1})}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\epsilon N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(N^{-k}+\epsilon^{-1}N^{-\sigma}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\left(\epsilon N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\epsilon N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

with
\beta_{0,j}=\beta_{1}^{2}
for
j=N/2
. Then using the same method, we also decompose
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}
as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[(S-P^{(\theta)}S)^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[(E-P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}+\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N-1}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j},

where from (
20
),
\epsilon\leq CN^{-1}
and (
10
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[(S-P^{(\theta)}S)^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\epsilon\beta_{1}^{2}(\Delta h_{j})^{3}}{\beta_{0,j}}\|(S-P^{(\theta)}S)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{j}\cup I_{j+1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\epsilon\beta_{0,j}}{\Delta h_{j}}[\xi]_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{1}h_{N/2+1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\beta_{0,j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(S-P^{(\theta)}S)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{N/2}\cup I_{N/2+1})}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\beta_{1}^{2}\epsilon N^{-3}}{\beta_{0,j}}h_{j}^{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}+C\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\epsilon^{2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}N^{1-k}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-k}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\epsilon^{2}N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\epsilon N^{\frac{1}{2}-k}+\epsilon^{2}N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon N^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\|\xi\|_{E}.

In addition, due to the fact that
E^{\prime\prime}\in C[0,1]
and (
10
), one can derive

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[(E-P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\epsilon(\Delta h_{j})^{3}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{0,j}}[(E-P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}]_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}h_{N/2+1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|(E-P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{N/2}\cup I_{N/2+1})}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\epsilon(\Delta h_{j})^{3}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{0,j}}(E^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}^{+})-(P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}^{+})-E^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}^{-})+(P^{(\theta)}E)^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}^{-}))\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}(\epsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\epsilon^{-2}N^{-\sigma}+N^{2-\sigma}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N\frac{\epsilon N^{-3}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{0,j}}N^{4-2\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}+C\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}N^{-(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma)}(\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{1-\sigma}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}N^{-(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma)}(\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\epsilon N^{\frac{3}{2}-\sigma}+N^{-(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma)}(\ln N)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|\xi\|_{E}

Then in the same way, from
\beta_{0,j}=\beta_{1}^{2}N
for
j=N/2+1,\cdots,N
, there is

\displaystyle\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N-1}\epsilon\beta_{1}\Delta h_{j}[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{j}[\xi]_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N-1}\frac{\epsilon(\Delta h_{j})^{3}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{0,j}}\|\eta^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{j}\cup I_{j+1})}\right)^{1/2}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N\frac{\epsilon(\epsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{3}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{0,j}}\epsilon^{-4}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{2(k-1)}\right)^{1/2}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\beta_{1}}{\sqrt{\beta_{0,j}}}N^{-k}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}.

In summary, by some simple calculations, it is straightforward to obtain that

\mathcal{M}_{2}\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{E}.

(39)

Next we can divide
\mathcal{M}_{3}
as

\mathcal{M}_{3}=-\epsilon\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}[\eta]_{j}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{j}-\epsilon\sum_{j=N/2}^{N}[\eta]_{j}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{j}.

From (
12
) and Remark

, we only analyze
-\epsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N/2-1}[\eta]_{j}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{j}
. Applying the inverse inequality and (

),

\begin{split}&|-\epsilon\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}[\eta]_{j}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{j}|\\
&\leq|\epsilon[\eta]_{0}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{0}|+|\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}[\eta]_{j}\{\xi^{\prime}\}_{j}|\\
&\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1})}\epsilon\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1})}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j}\cup{I_{j+1}})}\epsilon\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j}\cup{I_{j+1}})}\\
&\leq C\epsilon\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{1})}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{I_{1}}+C\epsilon N^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j}\cup{I_{j+1}})}\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{I_{j}\cup{I_{j+1}}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/2}N^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-(k+1)}\|\xi\|_{E}+C\epsilon^{1/2}N^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}\|\xi\|_{E}\\
&\leq C(\epsilon^{1/2}N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}+\epsilon^{1/2}N^{-k})\|\xi\|_{E}\\
&\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}\|\xi\|_{E}.\end{split}

(40)

We consider
\mathcal{M}_{4}
and
\mathcal{M}_{5}
, which are divided into
1\leq j\leq N/2
and
N/2+1\leq j\leq N
for analysis. For

, through (
14
), (

), (

), and (

), we derive
\eta(x_{N/2})=0
and

\begin{split}&-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}a\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{j=0}^{N/2}a(x_{j})\widetilde{\eta}(x_{j})[\xi]_{j}\\
&=-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}(a-a(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}))\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}a(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}})\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{j=0}^{N/2}a(x_{j})\widetilde{\eta}(x_{j})[\xi]_{j}\\
&=-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}(a-a(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}))\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where
a(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}})
is the value of
a(x)
at the point
x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}
on
[x_{j-1},x_{j}]
. Then from the Lagrange mean value theorem, there exists
\lambda
between

and

such that

Here

),

\begin{split}&|-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}(a(x)-a(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}))\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x|=|-\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\int_{I_{j}}a^{\prime}(\lambda)(x-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}})\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x|\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}h_{j}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}{(I_{j})}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}h_{j}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}\|\xi\|_{I_{j}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}\|\xi\|_{I_{j}}\\
&\leq CN^{-(k+1)}\|\xi\|_{E}.\end{split}

For
N/2+1\leq j\leq N

[\eta(x_{j})]=0
and
\eta(x_{j})=0

\begin{split}&|-\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N}\int_{I_{j}}a(x)\eta\xi^{\prime}\mathrm{d}x|\leq C\left(\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N}\|\eta\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N}\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}\left(\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N}\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{E}.\end{split}

Summing up, we obatin

\mathcal{M}_{4}+\mathcal{M}_{5}\leq C(N^{-1}\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{E}.

(41)

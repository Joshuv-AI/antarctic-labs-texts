1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over an algebraically closed field
K
of characteristic
0
and let
M_{H}=M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
be the moduli space of stable rank
r
vector bundles
E
with respect to an ample divisor
H
on
X
and with fixed Chern classes
c_{i}:=c_{i}(E)
for
i=1,\dots,s:=\min\{r,n\}
.

Moduli spaces of stable vector bundles were defined in 1970 by Murayama.
Since then, many authors have done a systematic study of these moduli spaces but in general, in spite of great contributions, a lot of questions remain open.

One way of studying the geometry of these moduli spaces is by studying their subvarieties.
A Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}=W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
is a subvariety of
M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
whose points correspond to stable vector bundles having at least
k
independent sections and, roughtly speaking, the Brill-Noether Theory describes the geometry of these subvarieties.

Classical Brill-Noether Theory deals with line bundles (respectively rank
r
stable bundles) on smooth curves. In the case of line bundles on projective curves
C
of genus
g
, Brill-Noether theory is concerned with the subvarieties of
\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
whose line bundles have at least
k
independent global sections. Basic questions concerning non-emptiness, connectedness, irreducibility, dimension, singularities, cohomological classes … have been answered when the curve
C
is generic on the moduli space of curves of genus
g
.

There are several natural ways to generalize the classical theory of Brill-Noether.
The first natural generalization concerns higher rank vector bundles on algebraic curves.
During the last two decades, a great amount of job has been made around the Brill-Noether stratification of the moduli space of stable rank
r
vector bundles of degree
d
on algebraic curves, giving rise to nice and interesting descriptions of these subvarieties. Nevertheless, it should be mentioned that in spite of big efforts, many questions concerning their geometry still remain open. The other way of generalizing this theory is considering line bundles on varieties of arbitrary dimension. Finally, we can go in both directions simultaneously.
The Brill-Noether theory for rank
r
bundles on a variety of arbitrary dimension was stablished in
[
2
]
. If fact, if
X
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
and
H
an ample divisor on
X
, the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
in
M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
is defined as the set of stable vector bundles in
M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
having at least
k
independent sections. It is proved in
[
2
]
that
W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
has a natural structure of
k
-determinantal variety and that these subvarieties give a filtration of the moduli space.

In the context of the Brill-Noether Theory, one can immediately raise the following natural problems:

Problem 1.1
.

(a)

Determine when the Brill-Noether locus

is non-empty.

(b)

Study the smoothness of the Brill-Noether locus.

(c)

and
\rho_{H}^{k}<0

.

(d)

Determine the irreducibility of

.

In this paper, we will focus the attention on moduli spaces of rank
2
stable vector bundles on ruled surfaces.
In

9

Qin developed a theory of walls and chambers and proved that moduli spaces

only depend on the chamber of

. In this paper we will analize how this phenomena affects the Brill-Noether loci and in addition using this structure we will obtain our first contribution to Problem

(a) (see Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to fix some notation and prove some results concerning the cohomology of line bundles on ruled surfaces. In addition, we will recall some definitions needed in the sequel.

A ruled surface is a surface
X
, together with a surjective map
\pi:X\rightarrow C
to a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g
such that, for every point
y\in C
, the fibre
X_{y}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and such that
\pi
admits a section. It is also defined as the projectivization
X=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
of a normalized rank
2
bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C
. Let
\mathfrak{e}
be the divisor on
C
corresponding to the invertible sheaf
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{E}
and let us define
e:=-\deg(\mathfrak{e})
.

Let
C_{0}\subseteq X
be a section and
f
be a fibre. We have that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\bigoplus\pi^{*}\operatorname{Pic}(C)
, where
\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
C_{0}
. Also
\operatorname{Num}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}
, generated by
C_{0}
and
f
satisfying
C_{0}^{2}=-e
,
C_{0}\cdot f=1
and
f^{2}=0
.
If
\mathfrak{b}
is a divisor on
C
, we will write
\mathfrak{b}f
instead of
\pi^{*}\mathfrak{b}
. Thus a divisor
D
on
X
can be written uniquely as
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
, being
\mathfrak{b}\in\operatorname{Pic}(C)
, and any element of
\operatorname{Num}(X)
can be written as
D\equiv aC_{0}+bf
.
The canonical divisor
K_{X}
on
X
is in the class
-2C_{0}+(\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{e})f
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the canonical divisor on
C
of degree
2g-2
.

If the genus of the curve
C
is zero then, for any integer
e\geq 0
, the ruled surface is known as a Hirzebruch surface
X_{e}
. In this case we have
X_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\bigoplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))
.
Since
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{e})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, dealing with divisors on Hirzebruch surfaces we will not make any distinction between
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
and its numerical class
D\equiv aC_{0}+bf
.

Going back to the general case, for any divisor
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
on
X
with
a\geq 0
, it follows from Lemma V.2.4, Exercises III.8.3 and
III.8.4 of
[
6
]

(1)

\operatorname{h}^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\operatorname{h}^{i}(C,(S^{a}\mathcal{E})(\mathfrak{b}))

where
S^{a}\mathcal{E}
stands for the
a
-th symmetric power of
\mathcal{E}
.
Moreover,

(2)

\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b}))\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,(S^{a}\mathcal{E})(\mathfrak{b}))\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a}\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b}+i\mathfrak{e})),

for each divisor
\mathfrak{b}
on
C
(see for instance
[
8
, Section 2]
).

Notation 2.1
.

Usually, we will write
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
to refer to
\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
.

From now on, we will assume that

. In this case, a divisor

is ample if and only if

and

(see

For effective divisors on

Lemma 2.2

Let

be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve

of genus

and

be a divisor on

with

. If

is effective then

.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
D
is effective.
If
D=0
there is nothing to prove. So, let
let us assume that
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f\neq 0
.

First of all we will see that
a\geq 0
.
Since
D
is an effective divisor,
D\cdot H>0
for any ample divisor

on

. In particular, taking the ample divisor on
X
,

H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
with
\beta>>0
, we have

0<D\cdot H=-ae+a\beta+b=a(\beta-e)+b

what implies that

.

Now, we will see that

.
Since

) and (

0<\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=\operatorname{h}^{0}S^{a}\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{b})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a}\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b}+i\mathfrak{e}).

In particular, since
\deg(\mathfrak{e})=-e\leq 0
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})\neq 0

.
∎

Lemma 2.3

Let

\\
(a+1)g&\mbox{for}&g\leq b\leq 2g-1,\thinspace g\geq 1\\

Let

If

By

(3)

In particular, we have

Since

{\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=b+1-g}

The following is a well-known result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
D
a divisor on
X
and
Z\subset X
a general 0-dimensional subscheme. If
|Z|\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(D)=0

Let
E
be a rank 2 vector bundle on a smooth projective surface
X
. We denote by

the Chern Classes of
E
,
c_{i}=c_{i}(E)\in\operatorname{H}^{2i}(X,E)
, for

Our aim is to study the moduli space of stable vector bundles on
X
by means of its Brill-Noether locus. To do so, we end the section by recalling basic facts concerning stable vector bundles and Brill Noether loci.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
H
an ample divisor on
X
and
E
a vector bundle on
X
with fixed Chern classes

for

. Then

\mu_{H}(F):=\frac{c_{1}(F)\cdot H}{\operatorname{rank}(F)}<\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{\operatorname{rank}(E)}:=\mu_{H}(E).

Notice that the notion of stability depends on the ample divisor. Nevertheless, when there is no confusion we will simply say that
E
is stable.

Given
H
an ample divisor on
X
,
c_{1}\in\operatorname{Num}(X)
and
c_{2}\in H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},c_{2})
the moduli space of
H
-stable rank
r
vector bundles

on
X
with fixed Chern classes
c_{i}
. When there is no confusion, we will denote it by

.

For big values of

, we have a general result concerning irreducibility, smoothness and dimension of these moduli spaces. In the particular case of smooth ruled surfaces, it can be stated as follows (see for instance
[

]
):

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus

,
H
an ample divisor on
X
and

{c_{1},c_{2}\in\operatorname{H}^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})}
Chern classes. For all

, the moduli space
M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},c_{2})
is a smooth, irreducible,
quasiprojective variety of dimension
2rc_{2}-(r-1)c_{1}^{2}-(r^{2}-1)(1-g)
.

One possible way to study the moduli space of stable vector bundles is by describing the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}:=W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},c_{2})
in

which parametrizes stable rank

vector bundles having at least

\operatorname{Supp}(W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},c_{2}))=\{E\in M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},c_{2})|\thinspace\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k\}.

The Brill-Noether locus has the structure of a

-determinantal variety

, and so we can talk about its expected dimension and singular locus. This is given by the following result (see
[

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and consider the

of rank

,
H
-stable vector bundles

on
X
with

. Assume that for any

,
\operatorname{h}^{i}E=0

.

such that

\operatorname{Supp}(W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},c_{2}))=\{E\in M_{H}|\thinspace\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k\}.

Moreover, each non-empty irreducible component of

has dimension at

\dim(M_{H})-k(k-\chi(r;c_{1},c_{2})),

and

.

Remark 2.8

By definition, the different Brill-Noether loci form a filtration of the moduli space

M_{H}\supseteq W_{H}^{1}(r;c_{1},c_{2})\supseteq W_{H}^{2}(r;c_{1},c_{2})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq W_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},c_{2})\supseteq W_{H}^{k+1}(r;c_{1},c_{2})\supseteq\cdots

Definition 2.9

The expected dimension of the Brill-Noether locus is defined as

\rho_{H}^{k}:=\rho_{H}^{k}(r;c_{1},c_{2})=\dim M_{X,H}(r;c_{1},c_{2})-k(k-\chi(r;c_{1},c_{2})).

By the Riemann-Roch theorem,

\chi(r;c_{1},c_{2})=r(1+p_{a}(X))-\frac{c_{1}K_{X}}{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}-c_{2}.

In particular, if

Let

In particular,

is effective and non zero, and thus
c_{1}\cdot H>0
for any ample divisor
H
. Furthermore, in the case of a rank
2
vector bundle
E
, we have that
E
is
H
-stable if and only if
E\otimes L
is
H
-stable, for any line bundle
L
on
X
, so it is natural to focus the attention on the cases
c_{1}\in\{C_{0},C_{0}+f,f\}
.

3.
Walls and chambers structure in Brill-Noether loci

We start the section recalling the theory of walls and chambers introduced by Qin, in the context of smooth surfaces (see for instance
[
9
]
). The main idea is that if
X
is a smooth surface, the ample cone of
X
,
C_{X}
, has a structure of walls and chambers such that the moduli space
M_{H}(2;c_{1},c_{2})
only depends on the chamber of
H
.

To introduce the main results of this theory, let us remember some basic definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

Let
\zeta\in\operatorname{Num}(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
. We define

W^{\zeta}=C_{X}\cap\{x\in\operatorname{Num}(X)|x\cdot\zeta=0\}.

(ii)

Define
\mathcal{W}(c_{1},c_{2})
as the set whose elements consist of
W^{\zeta}
, where
\zeta
is the numerical equivalence class of a divisor
D
on
X
such that
D+c_{1}
is divisible by
2
in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
,
-(4c_{2}-c_{1}^{2})\leq D^{2}<0
and
|Z|=c_{2}+\frac{D^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}{4}
for some locally complete intersection codimension-two cycle
Z
in
X
.

(iii)

A wall of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
is an element in
\mathcal{W}(c_{1},c_{2})
. A chamber of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
is a
connected component of
C_{X}\backslash W(c_{1},c_{2})
. A
Z
-chamber of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
is the intersection
of
\operatorname{Num}(X)
with some chamber of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
.

(iv)

A face of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
is
\mathcal{F}=W^{\zeta}\cap\mathcal{C}
, where
W^{\zeta}

is a wall of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
and
\mathcal{C}
is a chamber of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\zeta
be a numerical equivalence class defining a wall of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
. We define
E_{\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2})

as the set of rank
2
vector bundles
E
on
X
given by a non-trivial extension of type

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(c_{1}-D)\rightarrow 0

where
D
is a divisor with
2D-c_{1}\equiv\zeta
and
Z
is a locally complete intersection 0-cycle of
length
c_{2}+\frac{\zeta^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}{4}
.

Qin proved in
[
9
, Proposition 2.2.5]
and
[
9
, Remark 2.2.6]
that the moduli space
M_{H}(2;c_{1},c_{2})
only depends on the chamber of
H
, so he set
M_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})
to be
M_{H}(2;c_{1},c_{2})
for some
H\in\mathcal{C}
.

Once we know that
M_{H}(2;c_{1},c_{2})
only depends on the chamber of
H
, the natural question that arises is how the moduli space changes when the polarization
H
crosses a wall
W^{\zeta}
between two chambers. The phenomena that occurs is summarized in the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

(
[
9
, Proposition 1.3.1]
)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a chamber and
\mathcal{F}
be one of its faces. Then, as sets,

M_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})=M_{\mathcal{F}}(c_{1},c_{2})\amalg(\amalg_{\zeta}E^{k}_{\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))

where
\zeta
satisfies
\zeta\cdot H<0
for some
H\in\mathcal{C}
, and runs over all numerical equivalence classes which define the wall containing
\mathcal{F}
.

As a consequence

Theorem 3.4
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.3.3]
)

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be two adjancent chambers of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
sharing a common wall
W^{\zeta}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
lies above
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Then, as sets,

M_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(c_{1},c_{2})=(M_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(c_{1},c_{2})\backslash\amalg_{\zeta}E_{-\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))\amalg(\amalg_{\zeta}E_{\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))

where
\zeta
runs over all normalized numerical equivalence classes (that is
\zeta\cdot f>0
) which represents the wall
W^{\zeta}
.

It is natural to ask ourselves what happens at the level of Brill-Noether loci. To this end, let us define

W_{\mathcal{C}}^{k}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\{E\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})|h^{0}E\geq k\}

and

E_{\zeta}^{k}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\{E\in E_{\zeta}|h^{0}E\geq k\}.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
we can deduce

Proposition 3.5
.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a chamber and
\mathcal{F}
one of its faces. Then, as sets,

W^{k}_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})=W^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(c_{1},c_{2})\amalg(\amalg_{\zeta}E^{k}_{\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))

where
\zeta
satisfies
\zeta\cdot H<0
for some
H\in\mathcal{C}
, and runs over all numerical equivalence classes which define the wall containing
\mathcal{F}
.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be two adjancent chambers of type
(c_{1},c_{2})
sharing a common wall
W^{\zeta}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
lies above
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Then, as sets,

W^{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(c_{1},c_{2})=(W^{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(c_{1},c_{2})\backslash\amalg_{\zeta}E^{k}_{-\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))\amalg(\amalg_{\zeta}E^{k}_{\zeta}(c_{1},c_{2}))

We will use this wall crossing to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
and
c_{2}>>0
an integer.
Let us consider the family of numerical equivalence classes

\zeta_{b}\equiv(2b-m)f-C_{0}

with
0<b=k-1+g<c_{2}
and let
\mathcal{C}_{b}
be the chamber such that
W^{\zeta_{b}}\cap\text{Closure}(\mathcal{C}_{b})\neq\emptyset
and
\zeta_{b}\cdot H<0
for all
H\in\mathcal{C}_{b}
. Then, for any ample divisor
H\in\mathcal{C}_{b}
,
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
whenever
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

First of all, since we have the filtration
W_{H}^{l+1}\subseteq W_{H}^{l}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{H}^{1}\subseteq M_{H}
, we can assume
k\geq\max\{2,g\}
and thus
b\geq\max\{g+1,2g-1\}
.

Notice that
\zeta_{b}\equiv(2b-m)f-C_{0}
defines a non-empty wall of type
(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
. In fact,

(\zeta_{b})^{2}=((2b-m)f-C_{0})^{2}=-4b-e+2m.

Since
m\in\{0,1\}
and by the lower and upper bounds of
b
, we have

-4c_{2}-e\leq-4b-e+2m<0.

On the other hand,

\zeta_{b}+C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f=2\mathfrak{b}f
is divisible by
2
in
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
and

H\equiv C_{0}+(2b+e-m)f
is an ample divisor on
X
such that
\zeta_{b}\cdot H=0
.
Hence,
\zeta_{b}
defines a non-empty wall of type
(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
.

Let us consider
E_{\zeta_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
, which is by definition the family of rank 2 vector bundles given by non-trivial extensions of type

(4)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathfrak{b}f
is a divisor such that
\zeta_{b}\equiv(2b-m)f-C_{0}
with
b=\deg(\mathfrak{b})
and
Z
a 0-dimensional subscheme of length
|Z|=c_{2}-b
.

Claim
:
E_{\zeta_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
is non-empty.

Proof of the Claim
:
It is enough to see that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\neq 0
.
We have that
\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))=h^{1}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})
.
If we consider the short exact sequence

(5)

0\rightarrow I_{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightarrow 0,

we tensor it by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})
and we take cohomology,
we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X}))=0,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
2.2
.

On the other hand,
if we consider again the exact sequence (
5
)
and we take cohomology, we get

\operatorname{h}^{2}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})

and, by duality and Lemma
2.2
,

\operatorname{h}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f)=0.

Therefore,

\operatorname{h}^{1}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=-\chi I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X}).

Considering the exact sequence (
5
) and the Riemann-Roch Theorem,

-\chi I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=|Z|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=|Z|.

Putting altogether we have

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))=|Z|=c_{2}-b>0,

where the last inequality follows from the upper bound of
b
.

Therefore we can take a rank 2 vector bundle
E\in E_{\zeta_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
. It follows from Theorem
3.3
that
E_{\zeta_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f)\subseteq M_{\mathcal{C}_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
and thus
E
is
H
-stable for any
H\in\mathcal{C}_{b}
.

Finally, since
E
is given by the exact sequence (
4
), taking cohomology we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=\chi\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=b+1-g=k,

where the second equality follows from the fact that, since
k\geq\max\{1,g\}
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=0
.

Thus, we have seen that
\emptyset\neq E_{\zeta_{b}}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\subseteq W_{\mathcal{C}_{b}}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
and thus
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
for all
H\in\mathcal{C}_{b}
.
∎

Notice that with the above notations, if
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
the condition
\zeta_{b}\cdot H<0
is equivalent to

k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)].

Hence,

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
and
c_{2}>>0
an integer. Let us consider the family of numerical equivalence classes

\zeta_{b}\equiv(2b-m)f-C_{0}

with
0<b=k-1+g<c_{2}
.
Assume
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.
Then

W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
for

1\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta+\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]

and
H\in\mathcal{C}
being
\mathcal{C}
the chamber such that
W^{\zeta_{b}}\cap\text{Closure}(\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
.

4.
Emptiness and non-emptiness of Brill-Noether loci

The goal of this section is to determine lower bounds of
k
, depending on an ample divisor
H
, that garantize that the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}(2;c_{1},c_{2})
is non-empty. Moreover, we will see that these bounds are sharp. In contrast with the above section, we will obtain non-emptiness results by explicitly constructing families of
H
-stable rank 2 vector bundles for, in many cases, almost all ample divisors
H
and with values of the first Chern classes
c_{1}
in
\{C_{0},C_{0}+f,f\}
.

We will start with the case
c_{1}=C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f
with
\deg(\mathfrak{m})=m\in\{0,1\}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
with

\alpha(e+m)<\beta.

Then, for any
k
in the range

\max\{1,g\}\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)],

the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
whenever
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

First of all notice that one can extend
[
10
, Lemma 1.10]
to any ample divisor
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
and prove that if
M_{H}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
then
\beta<\alpha(2c_{2}+e-m)
. Hence we can assume
\beta<\alpha(2c_{2}+e-m)
.
Let us consider the family
\mathcal{G}
of rank two vector bundles
E
on
X
given by a non-trivial extension

(6)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\rightarrow 0,

where
Z
is a generic 0-dimensional subscheme of length
|Z|=c_{2}-b
with

b=\deg(\mathfrak{b})=k-1+g

and

\max\{1,g\}\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)].

Claim
:
\mathcal{G}
is non-empty.

Proof of the Claim
:
Notice that since
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})=0=\operatorname{h}^{2}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})
, using Riemann-Roch Theorem and the upper bound of
b
we deduce that

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))=|Z|=c_{2}-b>0,

what implies
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\neq 0
.

Hence any non-zero element in
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))
defines a non-trivial extension of type (
6
).
Notice that by the Cayley-Bacharach property
[
5
, Theorem 12]
,

E
is indeed a rank two vector bundle, with
c_{1}(E)=C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}>>0
.
Hence,
\mathcal{G}
is non-empty.

On the other hand, if we consider the exact sequence (
6
) we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})

and, since
k\geq\max\{1,g\}
we get
\operatorname{h}^{1}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=0
and thus
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=\chi\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=b+1-g=k
.

Now, let us prove that any rank two vector bundle
E
in
\mathcal{G}
is stable with respect to the polarization
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
with
\alpha(e+m)<\beta<\alpha(2c_{2}+e-m)
.

To this end, since
E
is a rank two vector bundle on
X
, we have to check that for any line subbundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow E
we have

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))=G\cdot H<\mu_{H}(E)=\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{2}.

By construction,
E
is given by a non-trivial extension of type (
6
). Therefore we have two possibilities:

\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)
or
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)
.

Case 1

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)
. In this case, since

k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)],

we get

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))\leq(\mathfrak{b}f)\cdot H=\alpha b=\alpha k+\alpha(-1+g)

<\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]+\alpha(-1+g)=\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha e+\alpha m)=\mu_{H}(E).

Case 2

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)
, where
G=sC_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
with
t=\deg(\mathfrak{t})
.
In this case,

C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f-G=(1-s)C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{t})f

is an effective divisor, which by Lemma
2.2
implies that
s\leq 1
and
t\leq m-b
.

Assume that
s\leq 0
.
Since
t\leq m-b
, we have

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))=s(\beta-\alpha e)+t\alpha\leq\alpha(m-b)<\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha e+\alpha m)=\mu_{H}(E),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
b\geq 0
and
\beta>\alpha(e+m)
.

Finally assume that
s=1
. In this case
G=C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
and since

\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),

we have
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f-G)=\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}((\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{t})f)\neq 0
. Since
Z
is a generic
0
-dimensional subscheme, we must have

|Z|<\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{t})f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{t})\leq m-b-t+1,

where the last inequality is given by (
3
).

Thus, since
|Z|=c_{2}-b
,

(7)

t\leq m-c_{2}.

Now, let us check that
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f))<\mu_{H}(E)
.
Notice that
{\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f))<\mu_{H}(E)}
if and only if

-\alpha e+\beta+t\alpha<\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha e+\alpha m)

what is equivalent to

-\alpha e+\beta-\alpha m<-2t\alpha.

This inequality holds since, by the upper bound of
\beta
, and according to (
7
),

-2t\alpha\geq-2\alpha(m-c_{2})>-\alpha e+\beta-\alpha m.

Therefore
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f))<\mu_{H}(E)
and putting altogether we get that
E
is
H
-stable.

We have proved that any
E
in
\mathcal{G}
is a rank two
H
-stable vector bundle with
{\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k}
, what implies that
\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
and hence
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Notice that the condition
\beta>\alpha(e+m)
in Theorem
4.1
is not restrictive. In fact, it is verified for almost all ample divisors, since
m\in\{0,1\}
and
\beta>\alpha e
for any ample divisor
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
with

\alpha(e+m)<\beta.

Then, for any

1\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)],

W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
whenever
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
\max\{1,g\}\leq k<\frac{1}{2}\alpha[\beta-\alpha(e+m+2g-2)]
, the result follows from Theorem
4.1
. Finally, since we have the filtration
W_{H}^{1}\supseteq W_{H}^{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq W_{H}^{q}
with
q=\max\{1,g\}
, the fact that
W_{H}^{q}\neq\emptyset
implies that
W_{H}^{l}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq l\leq q
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Notice that the expected dimension of the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})
is

\rho_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})=(4-k)c_{2}+3k-3+3(1-k)g+(1-k)e+2(k-1)m-k^{2}.

Hence, for
k\geq 4
we have
\rho_{H}^{k}<0
, but for

1\leq g\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]

we have seen that
W_{H}^{k}\neq\emptyset
. So, we have an example of a non-empty Brill-Noether locus with negative expected dimension and therefore it is clear that, in general,
\rho_{H}^{k}<0
does not imply that
W_{H}^{k}=\emptyset
.

Our next goal is to see that the above bound is sharp, in the sense that if

k\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)],

then
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})=\emptyset
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
be an ample divisor on
X
such that

\alpha(8+e-m+2g)<\beta.

Then
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})=\emptyset
for all
k\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\neq 0
, we can take a non-zero section
s
of
E
. We denote by
Y
its scheme of zeros and by
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
the maximal effective divisor contained in
Y
. Then
s
can be regarded as a section of
E(-D)
and its scheme of zeros has codimension greater or equal than two. Thus, we have a short exact sequence

(8)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f-D)\rightarrow 0

where
Z
is a locally complete intersection
0
-cycle of length
|Z|=c_{2}-D(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f-D)
.

Since
D
is effective, by Lemma
2.2
,
a\geq 0
and
b:=\deg(\mathfrak{b})\geq 0
.

If
D=0
, considering the exact sequence (
8
) and taking cohomology, we have that

k=\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}+\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f)=1+\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f).

By the short exact sequence

(9)

0\rightarrow I_{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightarrow 0

and by Lemma
2.3
, we have

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f)\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f)\leq 2(m+1)-e\leq 4.

This implies
k\leq 5
, but this contradicts the fact that
k\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]>5
. Hence
D\neq 0
.

Now let us see that if
D\neq 0
, then
D=\mathfrak{b}f
with
b>0
.

Since
E
is a rank two
H
-stable vector bundle,
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))<\mu_{H}(E)
and this is equivalent to

(10)

\alpha(2b-m)<(1-2a)(\beta-\alpha e).

Let us see that
a=0
. If
a>0
, since
\beta-\alpha e>\alpha(8+2g-m)
and considering the inequality in (
10
), we get
2b+m<-\alpha(8+2g-m)
which implies
b<0
, a contradiction. Thus
a=0
and hence
D=\mathfrak{b}f
.
In addition, since
D
is a non-zero effective divisor, we get
b>0
.
Therefore, by (
8
),
E
sits in the exact sequence

(11)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\rightarrow 0.

Considering the exact sequence (
11
) and taking cohomology, we get

k\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)+\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f).

On the one hand, following Lemma
2.3
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})\leq b+1
.
If we consider the exact sequence (
9
) and we take cohomology, we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f).

Since by Lemma
2.3
,

\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\leq 2(m-b+1)-e,

we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\leq 2(m-b+1)-e.

Putting altogether,

k\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq b+1+2(m-b+1)-e=2m+3-b-e,

what is equivalent to
b\leq 2m+3-e-k
. Since
k\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]
and

\beta>\alpha(e-m+2g+8)
, we have

b\leq 2m+3-e-k<2m+3-e-\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]<2m+3-e-5\leq 0,

what is a contradiction since
b>0
.
Hence
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})=\emptyset
for all
k\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)]
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

If we consider the particular case
g=1
in Theorem
4.5
, the result is also true if we expand the family of ample line bundles in
X
, to the one of
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
with

\alpha(6+e-m)<\beta.

As a consequence of the above results we have the following equivalence.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv\alpha C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
such that

\alpha(8+e-m+2g)<\beta.

Assume that
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.
Then,
W_{H}^{k}(2;C_{0}+\mathfrak{m}f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

1\leq k<\frac{1}{2\alpha}[\beta-\alpha(e-m+2g-2)].

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
4.3
and Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Let us now turn our attention to the case
c_{1}=f
and see under which conditions
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})
is non-empty.
The first result is about non-emptiness.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
.
Then
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
whenever
M_{H}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let us consider the family
\mathcal{G}
of rank two vector bundles
E
on
X
given by a non-trivial extension

(12)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(f)\rightarrow 0,

where
Z
is a 0-dimensional subscheme of length
|Z|=c_{2}
.

Claim
:
\mathcal{G}
is non-empty.

Proof of the Claim
:
First of all let us see that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(f),\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0
.
By Serre’s duality,
\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(f),\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{1}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})
.

From the short exact sequence (
9
),
by Lemma
2.2
and Serre’s duality,
we have

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(f+K_{X})=0

and also

\operatorname{h}^{2}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(f+K_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)=0.

Therefore,

\operatorname{h}^{1}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})=-\chi I_{Z}(C_{0}+(m-2b)f+K_{X})=|Z|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(f+K_{X})=|Z|-1+g.

Hence
\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(f),\mathcal{O}_{X})=|Z|-1+g=c_{2}-1+g\neq 0
.

Any non-zero element in
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(f),\mathcal{O}_{X})
defines a non-trivial extension of type (
12
).
Notice that by the Cayley-Bacharach property
[
5
, Theorem 12]
,

E
is indeed a rank two vector bundle, with
c_{1}(E)=f
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}>>0
.
Hence
\mathcal{G}
is non-empty and by construction, for any
E\in\mathcal{G}
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq 1
.

Now let us prove that any rank two vector bundle
E
in
\mathcal{G}
is stable with respect to
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
.
To this end, since
E
is a rank two vector bundle on
X
, we have to check that for any line subbundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow E
we have
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))=G\cdot H<\mu_{H}(E)=\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{2}.

By construction,
E
is given by a non-trivial extension of type (
12
). Therefore we have two possibilities:

\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
or
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow I_{Z}(f)
.

Case 1

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Then

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))\leq 0<\frac{1}{2}=\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{2}=\mu_{H}(E).

Case 2

Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(G)\hookrightarrow I_{Z}(f)
, where
G=sC_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
with
t=\deg(\mathfrak{t})
.
In this case

f-G=f-sC_{0}-\mathfrak{t}f

is an effective divisor, which by Lemma
2.2
implies that
s\leq 0
and
t\leq 1
.

Notice that since
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}=0
,
f\neq G
.
Since
f-G
is effective, we have
(f-G)\cdot H>0
, which is equivalent to
t\leq s(e-\beta)
,
and hence

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(G))=s(\beta-e)+t\leq 0<\mu_{H}(E).

Thus, putting altogether, we have already seen that
E
is
H
-stable.

We have proved that any
E
in
\mathcal{G}
is a rank two
H
-stable vector bundle with
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq 1
, what implies that
\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})
.Thus
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Let us see now what happens for values of
k\geq 2
.
It follows from
[
3
, Proposition 7.7]
that
W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
We will prove the case
k\geq 3
. To this end, we will use the following technical Lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
and
E
a rank
2
vector bundle with
c_{1}(E)=f
. If
E
is stable respect to the ample divisor
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
, then
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}E|_{f}
.

Proof.

First of all, let us see that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E(-f)=0
. If
\operatorname{h}^{0}E(-f)\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)\hookrightarrow E
. Since
E
is
H
-stable, we get

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(f))=1<\frac{1}{2}=\mu_{H}(E),

which is a contradiction. Hence
\operatorname{h}^{0}E(-f)=0
.

If we twist the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{f}\rightarrow 0

by
E
and we take cohomology, using the fact that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E(-f)=0
, we get
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}E|_{f}
.
∎

Now we are ready to study the Brill-Noether locus
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})
for values of
k\geq 3
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
. Then,
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})=\emptyset
for all
k\geq 3
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
and consider a vector bundle
E\in W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})
. Since
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k>0
, we can take a non-zero section
s
of
E
. We denote by
Y
its scheme of zeros and by
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
the maximal effective divisor contained in
Y
. Then
s
can be regarded as a section of
E(-D)
and its scheme of zeros has codimension greater or equal than two. Thus, we have a short exact sequence

(13)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(f-D)\rightarrow 0

where
Z
is a locally complete intersection
0
-cycle of length
|Z|=c_{2}-D(f-D)
.

Since
D
is effective, by Lemma
2.2
,
a\geq 0
and
b:=\deg(\mathfrak{b})\geq 0
.

Let us see that
D=0
.
Assume that
D\neq 0
.
Since
D
is effective,
D\cdot L>0
for any ample divisor
L
on
X
.
On the other hand, since
E
is stable respect to
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
, we have that
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))<\mu_{H}(E).

Putting altogether, we get

0<\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=D\cdot H<\frac{1}{2}=\mu_{H}(E),

which is a contradiction.
Hence
D=0
.

Therefore, by (
13
), any vector bundle
E\in W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})
sits in a short exact sequence

(14)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(f)\rightarrow 0.

If
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(f)=0
, then
\operatorname{h}^{0}E=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}=1
.
On the other hand, if
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(f)\neq 0
this would mean that the points of
Z
are contained in a line.

Let
l
be a line such that
Z\cap l=\emptyset
. Restricting the exact sequence (
14
) to
l
we get the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{l}\rightarrow E|_{l}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{l}\rightarrow 0,

and taking cohomology we obtain
\operatorname{h}^{0}E|_{l}\leq 2
.

By Lemma
4.9
, this implies that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\leq 2
and thus
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})=\emptyset
for all
k\geq 3
.
∎

Putting altogether:

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
. Then,
W_{H}^{k}(2;f,c_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
1\leq k\leq 2
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.8
,
[
3
, Proposition 7.7]
and Theorem
4.10
.
∎

See
[
3
, Proposition 7.7]
for an alternative proof of Theorems
4.8
and
4.10
.

Now we will analize in more detail the locus
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
,
c_{2}>>0
an integer and
H\equiv C_{0}+\beta f
an ample divisor on
X
. Then
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
is smooth and has the expected dimension, namely,

\rho_{H}^{1}=3c_{2}+g-1.

Proof.

Let us take a vector bundle
E\in W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
. Since
\operatorname{h}^{0}E=1
, we can take a non-zero section
s
of
E
. We denote by
Y
its scheme of zeros and by
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
the maximal effective divisor contained in
Y
. Then
s
can be regarded as a section of
E(-D)
and its scheme of zeros has codimension greater or equal than two. Thus, we have a short exact sequence

(15)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(f-D)\rightarrow 0

where
Z
is a locally complete intersection
0
-cycle of length
|Z|=c_{2}-D(f-D)
.

Following the same arguments as in proof of Theorem
4.10
we see that
D=0
.
Hence, any vector bundle
E
in
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
sits in a short exact sequence of the following type

(16)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(f)\rightarrow 0.

Let us now check that any vector bundle
E
in
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
is a smooth point of
M_{X,H}(2;f,c_{2})
. To this end, it is enough to see that
\operatorname{h}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes E)=0
.

First of all let us see that
\operatorname{h}^{2}(E^{*})=0
. If we tensor the short exact sequence (
16
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
and we take cohomology,
we get the long exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E(K_{X})\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})\rightarrow\cdots

From the fact that
\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})=\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(f+K_{X})=0
, we deduce that

0=\operatorname{h}^{0}E(K_{X})=\operatorname{h}^{2}E^{*},

where the last equality follows from Serre duality.
Now we will see that

\operatorname{h}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)\otimes I_{Z})=0.

Tensoring the short exact sequence (
16
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}-f)
and taking cohomology, we get the long exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}-f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E(K_{X}-f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(K_{X})\rightarrow\cdots

Since
\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}-f)=\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(K_{X})=0
, we get
\operatorname{H}^{0}E(K_{X}-f)=0
and by Serre’s duality
\operatorname{H}^{2}E^{*}(f)=0
.
Hence
\operatorname{h}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)\otimes I_{Z})=\operatorname{h}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f))=0
.

Finally, if we tensor the short exact sequence (
16
) by
E^{*}
and we take cohomology, we get

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*})\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes E)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)\otimes I_{Z})\rightarrow 0.

and since
\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*})=\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)\otimes I_{Z})=0
we deduce that
\operatorname{H}^{2}(E^{*}\otimes E)=0
. Hence
E
is a smooth point of
M_{H}(2;f,c_{2})
.

To end the proof, let us see that the map

\mu_{E}:\operatorname{H}^{0}(E)\otimes\operatorname{H}^{1}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(E\otimes E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))

is injective.
Since
E\in W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
,
\operatorname{H}^{0}(E)\cong K
and hence this is equivalent to prove that the map

\tilde{\mu}_{E}:\operatorname{H}^{1}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(E\otimes E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))

is injective.

If we tensor the exact sequence (
16
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)
and we take cohomology, we get the long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}E(-f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}\rightarrow 0.

Since by duality
\operatorname{h}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(f+K_{X})=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}I_{Z}=\operatorname{h}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}=0
, we deduce that
\operatorname{h}^{2}E(-f)=0
and this implies, again by Serre’s duality, that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E^{*}(K_{X}+f)=0
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{h}^{0}E^{*}(K_{X}+f)=0
we get
\operatorname{h}^{0}E^{*}\otimes I_{Z}(K_{X}+f)=0
.

Therefore, since
\operatorname{h}^{0}(E^{*}\otimes I_{Z}(K_{X}+f))=0
, if we tensor the exact sequence (
16
) by
E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
and we take cohomology, we get

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))\xrightarrow{\tilde{\mu}_{E}}\operatorname{H}^{1}(E\otimes E^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))\rightarrow\cdots

which implies that
\tilde{\mu}_{E}
is injective. Hence
\mu_{E}
is injective.

We have proved that, for any vector bundle
E\in W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
,
E
is a smooth point of
M_{X,H}(2;f,c_{2})
and
\mu_{E}
is injective.

Therefore, by
[
4
, Corollary 2.6]
,
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
is smooth and of the expected dimension at
E
, for all
E\in W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
. Hence
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
is smooth and of the expected dimension.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

All the irreducible components of
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})\backslash W_{H}^{2}(2;f,c_{2})
are smooth and of the expected dimension, namely,
\rho_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})=3c_{2}+g-1
.

Remark 4.14
.

The family of type (
16
) used in the proof of Proposition
4.12
parameterizes an irreducible component of
W_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})
of the expected dimension
\rho_{H}^{1}(2;f,c_{2})=3c_{2}+g-1

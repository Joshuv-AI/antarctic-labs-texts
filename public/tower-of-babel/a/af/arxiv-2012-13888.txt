Abstract
. We study the asymptotic stability of a planar rarefaction wave (in the x 1 x_{1} - direction) for the 3-d isentropic Navier-Stokes equations, where the initial perturbation is periodic on the torus 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} with zero average. To solve this Cauchy problem in which the initial data is periodic with respect to only x 2 x_{2} and x 3 x_{3} but not to x 1 , x_{1}, we construct a suitable ansatz carrying the oscillations of the solution in the x 1 x_{1} - direction, but remaining to be periodic in the transverse x 2 x_{2} - and x 3 x_{3} - directions. In such a way, the difference between the ansatz and the solution can be integrable on the region ℝ × 𝕋 2 , \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}, which allows us to utilize the energy method with the aid of a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality on ℝ × 𝕋 2 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2} to prove the result.



1.
Introduction

We consider a Cauchy problem of the three-dimensional (3-d) isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes (CNS) equations, which read in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\text{div}\left(\rho\mathbf{u}\right)=0,\\
\partial_{t}\left(\rho\mathbf{u}\right)+\text{div}\left(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}\right)+\nabla p(\rho)=\mu\triangle\mathbf{u}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\nabla\text{div}\mathbf{u},\end{cases}

where
t>0,x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

\rho(x,t)>0
is the density,
\mathbf{u}(x,t)=\left(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\right)(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity, the pressure
p(\rho)
satisfies
p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\geq 1,
and
\mu>0
and
\lambda+\frac{2}{3}\mu\geq 0
are the viscous coefficients.

When
\mu=\lambda=0,

Equation

1.1
turns to the 3-d isentropic compressible Euler equations. A planar centered rarefaction wave
\left(\rho^{r},\mathbf{u}^{r}\right)(x,t)=\left(\rho^{r},u_{1}^{r},0,0\right)(x_{1},t)
is a weak entropy solution to this hyperbolic system, where
\left(\rho^{r},u_{1}^{r}\right)
solves the following Riemann problem in one dimension,

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{1}\left(\rho u_{1}\right)=0,&\\
\partial_{t}\left(\rho u_{1}\right)+\partial_{1}\left(\rho u_{1}^{2}\right)+\partial_{1}p(\rho)=0,&\\
\left(\rho,u_{1}\right)(x_{1},0)=\begin{cases}\left(\overline{\rho}^{-},\overline{u}_{1}^{-}\right),\quad x_{1}<0,&\\
\left(\overline{\rho}^{+},\overline{u}_{1}^{+}\right),\quad x_{1}>0.&\end{cases}\end{cases}

In this paper, we consider only the 2-rarefaction wave, i.e., the constants of the initial data in
Equation

1.2
satisfy the relation

(1.3)

\overline{u}_{1}^{+}=\overline{u}_{1}^{-}+\int_{\overline{\rho}^{-}}^{\overline{\rho}^{+}}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(s)}}{s}ds\quad\text{ with }\quad\overline{\rho}^{-}<\overline{\rho}^{+}.

We remark that the cases for the 1-rarefaction wave and a combination of two families of rarefaction waves can be proved in a similar way.

The 2-rarefaction wave
\left(\rho^{r},u_{1}^{r}\right)(x_{1},t)
can be solved as follows.
For
\rho>0,
the system
Equation

1.2
is strictly hyperbolic with two distinct eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(\rho,u_{1})=u_{1}-\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)},\quad\lambda_{2}(\rho,u_{1})=u_{1}+\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)}.

One can normalize the corresponding right eigenvectors
r_{i}
as
\nabla\lambda_{i}\cdot r_{i}\equiv 1~(i=1,2).
And the i-Riemann invariant
(i=1,2)
is given by

Z_{i}(\rho,u_{1})=u_{1}+(-1)^{i+1}\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(s)}}{s}ds,

which satisfies
\nabla Z_{i}\cdot r_{i}\equiv 0.

Denote
\lambda_{2}^{\pm}:=\lambda_{2}\left(\overline{\rho}^{\pm},\overline{u}_{1}^{\pm}\right)
and
Z_{2}^{\pm}:=Z_{2}\left(\overline{\rho}^{\pm},\overline{u}_{1}^{\pm}\right).

Then the 2-centered rarefaction wave
\left(\rho^{r},u_{1}^{r}\right)
can be solved exactly by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\left(\rho^{r}(x_{1},t),u_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)\right)=u_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)+\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho^{r})}(x_{1},t)=\omega\left(\frac{x_{1}}{t}\right),

\displaystyle Z_{2}\left(\rho^{r}(x_{1},t),u_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)\right)=u_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)-\int_{1}^{\rho^{r}(x_{1},t)}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(s)}}{s}ds=Z_{2}^{-}\big(=Z_{2}^{+}\big),

where

\omega\left(\xi\right)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{2}^{-},\quad&\xi<\lambda_{2}^{-},\\
\xi,\quad&\lambda_{2}^{-}\leq\xi<\lambda_{2}^{+},\\
\lambda_{2}^{+},\quad&\xi\geq\lambda_{2}^{+}.\end{cases}

It is well-known that the compressible Euler equations have three important entropy solutions, the shock, the rarefaction wave and the contact discontinuity, which are called the Riemann solutions. For the 1-d case, it has been shown in many literatures that these Riemann solutions characterize the large time behaviors of the solutions, as long as the initial data tend to constant states at far field. In other words, the Riemann solutions are time-asymptotically stable if the initial perturbations are integrable on
\mathbb{R}
(at least
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
- integrable).
For the CNS equations, due to the effect of viscosity, the large time behaviors of the solutions are governed by the viscous versions of these three basic waves, i.e. the viscous shock wave, the rarefaction wave and the viscous contact discontinuity.
For instance, Goodman
[
1
]
and Matsumura-Nishihara
[
19
]
independently proved the stability of a single viscous shock wave with a zero-mass condition by using the anti-derivative method. And then there have been a lot of efforts
[
16
,
17
,
24
]
to remove the zero-mass condition, where the key is to introduce some diffusion waves propagating along the directions of other characteristic families in order to carry the excessive masses. For the rarefaction wave, Matsumura-Nishihara
[
21
]
was the first to show the stability for the isentropic CNS system, where the initial perturbations can be large but the density is away from the vacuum, and we also refer to
[
23
]
for the stability result of the full CNS system. For the contact discontinuity, Huang-Matsumura-Xin
[
6
]
and Huang-Xin-Yang
[
7
]
proved the stability with an algebraic decay rate by constructing a suitable ansatz and using the anti-derivative method.

For the multi-dimensional (m-d) wave patterns, Xin
[
25
]
showed in 1990 that the planar rarefaction waves are stable for the scalar viscous conservation laws through an
L^{2}
- energy method. Since then,
[
12
,
22
,
13
]
and the reference therein further improved the results of Xin
[
25
]
, where both the strength of the wave and the initial perturbation can be large and an optimal decay rate
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is also obtained.
However, for the case of the m-d Navier-Stokes equations, the stability of the planar rarefaction waves is still a challenging open problem.
For a 2
\times
2
system with an artificial viscosity matrix, Hokari-Matsumura
[
2
]
proved the stability of the planar rarefaction wave in two dimensions,
which crucially depends on the strict positivity of the viscosity matrix. Recently, Li-Wang
[
14
]
and Li-Wang-Wang
[
15
]
showed the stability of the planar rarefaction waves for the Navier-Stokes equations on the domain
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
and on an infinitely long nozzle domain
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2},
respectively, where the periodic boundary conditions are imposed in their settings.

In this paper, we consider a Cauchy problem of the 3-d isentropic CNS system, concerning the stability of the planar rarefaction waves under space-periodic perturbations.
It is important and interesting to study the stability of the Riemann solutions under periodic perturbations, where the initial data tend to different periodic functions at far fields. It was shown in
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
that for the 1-d scalar conservation laws, the periodic oscillations around the shocks and rarefaction waves can be canceled as time increases due to the genuine nonlinearity of the flux.
Recently, Huang-Yuan
[
10
]
further studied the m-d scalar viscous conservation laws to show the stability of the scalar planar rarefaction waves under m-d periodic perturbations. In particular, a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
was introduced, which plays an important role when doing energy estimates on this unbounded domain without zero boundary conditions. We also refer to
[
9
,
29
]
for the viscous shocks under periodic perturbations for the 1-d CNS equations.

Now we formulate the main result of this paper.
Since the centered rarefaction wave
\left(\rho^{r},u_{1}^{r}\right)(x_{1},t)
is only Lipschitz continuous, we need to construct a smooth approximation as in
[
20
]
.
Let
\tilde{\omega}(x_{1},t)
be the unique smooth solution to the problem,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\omega}+\partial_{1}\big(\frac{\tilde{\omega}^{2}}{2}\big)=0,\\
\tilde{\omega}(x_{1},0)=\frac{\lambda_{2}^{-}+\lambda_{2}^{+}}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}^{+}-\lambda_{2}^{-}}{2}\text{tanh}(x_{1}).\end{cases}

Same as
Equation

1.4
, we let
\left(\tilde{\rho}^{r},\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}\right)
be the smooth functions solved uniquely by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)+\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}^{r})}(x_{1},t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\omega}(x_{1},t),

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)-\int_{1}^{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},t)}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(s)}}{s}ds

\displaystyle=Z_{2}^{-}\big(=Z_{2}^{+}\big).

From this, one has that

(1.6)

\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)=\overline{u}_{1}^{-}+\int_{\overline{\rho}^{-}}^{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},t)}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(s)}}{s}ds.

To study the stability of the planar rarefaction waves under 3-d periodic perturbations, we prescribe the initial data for
Equation

1.1
as

(1.7)

\left(\rho,\rho\mathbf{u}\right)(x,0)=\left(\tilde{\rho}^{r},\tilde{\rho}^{r}\tilde{u}_{1}^{r},0,0\right)(x_{1})+\left(v_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0}\right)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
v_{0}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{w}_{0}(x)=\left(w_{1,0},w_{2,0},w_{3,0}\right)(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are periodic functions defined on the 3-d torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}:=[0,1]^{3},
satisfying

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(v_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0}\right)(x)dx=0.

Remark 1.1
.

The condition
Equation

1.8
indicates that the periodic perturbations of the conservative quantities, the density and the momentum, should have zero averages.
Otherwise, if the condition
Equation

1.8
does not hold, the problem
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.7
turns to be connected with other kinds of Riemann solutions, such like a shock or a combination of multiple waves, which is not the topic of this paper and will be studied in future works.

Remark 1.2
.

The solution
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
to the problem
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.7
is periodic with respect to only
x_{2}
and
x_{3},
but not to
x_{1}.
Thus, the problem cannot be studied on the bounded torus
\mathbb{T}^{3},
but on the unbounded domain
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
instead. However, the solution keeps oscillating as
\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert\rightarrow+\infty,
thus a suitable ansatz is needed if we want to use the energy method.

Before stating the main theorem, we first introduce two periodic solutions to
Equation

1.1
and construct the ansatz of the solution to
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.7
.

Let
\left(\rho^{\pm},\mathbf{u}^{\pm}\right)(x,t)=\left(\rho^{\pm},u_{1}^{\pm},u_{2}^{\pm},u_{3}^{\pm}\right)(x,t)
denote the unique periodic solutions to
Equation

1.1
with the periodic initial data

(1.9)

\left(\rho^{\pm},\rho^{\pm}\mathbf{u}^{\pm}\right)(x,0)=\left(\overline{\rho}^{\pm},\overline{\rho}^{\pm}\overline{u}_{1}^{\pm},0,0\right)+\left(v_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0}\right)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{3},

respectively.
The global existence and uniqueness of the periodic solutions to
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.9
±
are standard; see
2.3
below.
Comparing
Equation

1.7
and
Equation

1.9
{}^{\pm},
one can see that the periodic solutions

share same behaviors as
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
at far fields, i.e., it holds that

\displaystyle(\rho,\rho\mathbf{u})(x,0)-(\rho^{\pm},\rho^{\pm}\mathbf{u}^{\pm})(x,0)=(\tilde{\rho}^{r},\tilde{\rho}^{r}\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{r})(x_{1},0)-(\overline{\rho}^{\pm},\overline{\rho}^{\pm}\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm})\quad\forall(x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

which tends to zero as
x_{1}\rightarrow\pm\infty,
respectively. And similarly, if
\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is small enough, the difference of the velocity satisfies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(x,0)-\mathbf{u}^{\pm}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\tilde{\rho}^{r}\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{r})(x_{1},0)+\mathbf{w}_{0}(x)}{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)+v_{0}(x)}-\frac{\overline{\rho}^{\pm}\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm}+\mathbf{w}_{0}(x)}{\overline{\rho}^{\pm}+v_{0}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)-\overline{\rho}^{\pm}\right)\left(v_{0}(x)\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm}-\mathbf{w}_{0}(x)\right)}{(\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)+v_{0}(x))(\overline{\rho}^{\pm}+v_{0}(x))}+\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{r}(x_{1},0)-\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm})}{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)+v_{0}(x)},

which yields that

\displaystyle\sup_{(x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\lvert\mathbf{u}(x,0)-\mathbf{u}^{\pm}(x,0)\right\rvert\leq C\left(\left\lvert\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},0)-\overline{\rho}^{\pm}\right\rvert+\left\lvert u_{1}^{r}(x_{1},0)-\overline{u}_{1}^{\pm}\right\rvert\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }x_{1}\rightarrow\pm\infty.

Observing from this, it is plausible that, when

the solution
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
to
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.7
tends to the periodic solutions

as

Ansatz.

Inspired by the formulas of the background smooth rarefaction wave,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\rho}^{-}\left(1-\sigma(x_{1},t)\right)+\overline{\rho}^{+}\sigma(x_{1},t),

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)

\displaystyle=\overline{u}_{1}^{-}\left(1-\eta(x_{1},t)\right)+\overline{u}_{1}^{+}\eta(x_{1},t),

where

(1.12)

\sigma(x_{1},t):=\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},t)-\overline{\rho}^{-}}{\overline{\rho}^{+}-\overline{\rho}^{-}},\quad\eta(x_{1},t):=\frac{\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)-\overline{u}_{1}^{-}}{\overline{u}_{1}^{+}-\overline{u}_{1}^{-}},

we set the ansatz

as

(1.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\rho^{-}(x,t)\left(1-\sigma(x_{1},t)\right)+\rho^{+}(x,t)\sigma(x_{1},t),

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}^{r}(x_{1},t)+v^{-}(x,t)(1-\sigma(x_{1},t))+v^{+}(x,t)\sigma(x_{1},t),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{u}^{-}(x,t)\left(1-\eta(x_{1},t)\right)+\mathbf{u}^{+}(x,t)\eta(x_{1},t),

\displaystyle=\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}(x_{1},t)\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{z}^{-}(x,t)(1-\eta(x_{1},t))+\mathbf{z}^{+}(x,t)\eta(x_{1},t),

where

Note that if

.

,

\displaystyle\in C\left(0,+\infty;H^{2}(\Omega)\right),

Lemma 2.2
.

The functions

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\lVert\nabla_{t,x_{1}}^{m}\left(\sigma,\eta\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

Proof.

Let
C
denote a constant independent of either
\delta
or
t.

From
2.1
, it is direct to prove i). And for ii), we prove only

and
\sigma-\eta,
since the proof of others is similar and the proof of the derivatives is straightforward.

It follows from
Equation

1.3
that
C^{-1}\delta\leq\left\lvert\overline{u}_{1}^{+}-\overline{u}_{1}^{-}\right\rvert\leq C\delta.
Then by
Equation

1.5
, one has that

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}=\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}^{r})}}{\tilde{\rho}^{r}}\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}^{r}\geq C\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}^{r},

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}^{r}=\left(1+\frac{p^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{\rho}^{r})\tilde{\rho}^{r}}{2p^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}^{r})}\right)^{-1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\omega}\leq C\partial_{1}\tilde{\omega},

which yields that

0<\tilde{\rho}^{r}-\overline{\rho}^{-}=\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}\partial_{y}\tilde{\rho}^{r}(y,t)dy\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}\partial_{y}\tilde{\omega}(y,t)dy\leq C\left(\tilde{\omega}-\lambda_{2}^{-}\right),

and similarly,

0<\overline{\rho}^{+}-\tilde{\rho}^{r}\leq C\left(\lambda_{2}^{+}-\tilde{\omega}\right).

Thus, one has that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(1-\eta)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

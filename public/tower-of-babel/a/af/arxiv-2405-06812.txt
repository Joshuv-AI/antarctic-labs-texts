1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

In this paper we study the well-posedness of the evolution equation of the form

(1.1)

u^{\prime}(t)=Au(t)+Cu(t),\quad t\geq 0,

where
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup and
C
is a (possibly unbounded) linear operator in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
. To be precise, we will find conditions on
A
and
C
so that
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of linear operators in
\mathbb{X}
. We then go further studying the “size” of (possibly unbounded) perturbation
C
under which an asymptotic behavior (exponential dichotomy) of the unperturbed equation persists.

Perturbation of strongly continuous semigroups is one of the central topics in the semigroup theory. We refer the reader to some classic results in
[
3
]
,
[
6
, p. 631–641]
,
[
7
, Chapter III]
,
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
]

and their references for more information. The case of unbounded perturbation is of particular interest as it is very hard to measure the size of the perturbation. In this paper we will go further in this direction to study some conditions for the operator
A+C
to generate a strongly continuous semigroup. Our approach is to use the Hille-Yosida’s Theorem for the perturbed semigroups rather than using the Variation-of-Parameters Formula to prove the perturbed operator generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Given an operator
A
that generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup we propose a norm for a linear space of (possibly unbounded) linear operators associated with
A
(see Definition
3.2
and Condition (ii) of Theorem
2.4
) that can be used to measure the size of the perturbation for which the operator
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup and that can be used to study the persistence of exponential dichotomy under perturbation. We then discuss this “size” with the so call Yosida distance between linear operators that was introduced recently to study the perturbation of asymptotic behavior of
C_{0}
-semigroups. Our main result is Theorem
2.4
that seems to be new.

In the paper we use some standard notations:
\mathbb{R},\,\mathbb{C}
stand for the fields of real and complex numbers, respectively. By
\mathbb{X}
we often denote a Banach space over
\mathbb{C}
with norm
\|\cdot\|
. The space
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X})
of all bounded linear operators on
\mathbb{X}
with norm
\|\cdot\|
, by abuse of notation for our convenience if this does not cause any confusion. For a linear operator
A
in
\mathbb{X}
we denote by
D(A)
its domain, and
\sigma(A)
and
\rho(A)
its spectrum and resolvent set, respectively. If
\mu\in\rho(A)
, then
R(\mu,A):=(\mu-A)^{-1}
. We recall the Hille-Yosida’s Theorem for the generation of strongly continuous semigroups that we will use later on (see

Theorem 1.1

(Hille-Yosida’s Theorem)
.

A linear operator
A
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
satisfying
\|T_{A}(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
, if and only if

i)

A
is closed and
D(A)
is dense in
\mathbb{X}
;

ii)

the resolvent set
\rho(A)
of
A
contains the ray
(\omega,\infty)
and

(1.2)

\|R(\lambda,A)^{n}\|\leq\frac{M}{(\lambda-\omega)^{n}},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda>\omega,\qquad n=1,2,\dots.

Note that when
M=1
the inequality (
1.2
) can be simplified to be

(1.3)

\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda-\omega},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda>\omega.

2.
Main Results

To prove the main result (Theorem
2.4
) we need the following lemmas that deal with several special cases.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
such that for all
t\geq 0

\|T_{A}(t)\|\leq e^{\omega t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
\omega
is a given real number. Assume further that
C
is a linear operator in
\mathbb{X}
such that

i)

D(A)\subset D(C)
;

ii)

There exist a constant
K>0
such that if
\mu>\omega
, then

(2.1)

\|CR(\mu,A)\|\leq\frac{K}{\mu-\omega}.

Then,
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup, denoted by
\left(T_{A+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
that satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|\leq e^{\left(\omega+K\right)t},\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

First, we will prove that

(2.3)

\rho(A+C)\supset(K+\omega,\infty).

For each
\mu>K+\omega
, as
K>0
we have
\mu\in\rho(A)
, and

\displaystyle(\mu-(A+C))R(\mu,A)

\displaystyle=\mu R(\mu,A)-AR(\mu,A)-CR(\mu,A)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=I-CR(\mu,A)

so,

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\mu-(A+C))

\displaystyle=\left[I-CR(\mu,A)\right](\mu-A).

But, for
\mu>K+\omega
we have
K/(\mu-\omega)<1
, so
\|CR(\mu,A)\|<1
, and then, the operator
I-CR(\mu,A)
is invertible. Thus, the operator
(\mu-(A+C))
is invertible as well, and (
2.3
) follows. In particular, this also yields that
A+C
is a closed operator (as
\rho(A+C)\not=\emptyset
).

Next, for each
\mu>K+\omega
, it is easy to verify the identity

(2.6)

R(\mu,A+C)-R(\mu,A)=R(\mu,A+C)CR(\mu,A).

Hence,

(2.7)

R(\mu,A+C)\left[I-CR(\mu,A)\right]=R(\mu,A).

By assumption (
2.1
), for
\mu>K+\omega
as
\|CR(\mu,A)\|\leq K/(\mu-\omega)<1
, the operator
\left[I-CR(\mu,A)\right]
is invertible and

(2.8)

\|\left[I-CR(\mu,A)\right]^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-K/(\mu-\omega)}.

Consequently, for all
\mu>K+\omega

\displaystyle\|R(\mu,A+C)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|R(\mu,A)\left[I-CR(\mu,A)\right]^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu-\omega}\cdot\frac{1}{1-K/(\mu-\omega)}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu-(\omega+K)}.

By the Hille-Yosida’s generation theorem, since
D(A)\subset D(A+C)
is densely everywhere in
\mathbb{X}
, (
2.9
) yields that the closed linear operator
A+C
generate a
C_{0}
-semigroup, denoted by
\left(T_{A+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
that satisfies (
2.2
). And,
the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
such that

\|T_{A}(t)\|\leq M,\quad t\geq 0,

where
M\geq 1
is a given real number. Assume further that
C
is a linear operator in
\mathbb{X}
such that

i)

D(A)\subset D(C)
;

ii)

there exists a constant
K>0
such that if
\mu>0
, then

(2.10)

\|CR(\mu,A)\|\leq\frac{K}{\mu}.

Then,
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup, denoted by
\left(T_{A+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
that satisfies

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|\leq Me^{MKt},\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

We re-norm
\mathbb{X}
by using

|x|:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|T_{A}(t)x\|,\quad x\in\mathbb{X}.

Then, as is well known, for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

(2.12)

\|x\|\leq|x|\leq M\|x\|

and

\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions in
(\mathbb{X},|\cdot|)
. Next, for each
\mu>0
we have

\displaystyle|CR(\mu,A)x|

\displaystyle\leq M\|CR(\mu,A)x\|

\displaystyle\leq M\cdot\frac{K}{\mu}\|x\|

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{MK}{\mu}|x|.

By Lemma
2.1
, the closed operator
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup in
(\mathbb{X},|\cdot|)
that satisfies

|T_{A+C}(t)|\leq e^{MKt},\quad t\geq 0.

Therefore, by using (
2.12
)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)x\|\leq|T_{A+C}(t)x|

\displaystyle\leq e^{MKt}|x|\leq Me^{MKt}\|x\|

for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
. And, the lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
C
is a bounded linear operator, the estimate (
2.11
) can be improved a little bit. In fact, in this case
K
can be replaced with
M\|C\|
. Next, in (
2.13
) we can improve the estimate as follows: Since the semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
(\mathbb{X},|\cdot|)
is an contraction semigroup,
|R(\mu,A)|\leq 1/\mu
, so

(2.15)

\displaystyle|CR(\mu,A)x|

\displaystyle\leq|C|\cdot|R(\mu,A)x|\leq\frac{|C|}{\mu}|x|.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)x\|\leq|T_{A+C}(t)x|\leq e^{|C|t}|x|\leq Me^{|C|t}\|x\|.

We have

\displaystyle|Cx|:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|T_{A}(t)Cx\|

\displaystyle=M\|Cx\|

\displaystyle\leq M\|C\||x|.

That means,
|C|\leq M\|C\|
, and thus,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|\leq Me^{M\|C\|t}.

For the general
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
we have the following theorem that is the main result of the paper:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
such that for all
t\geq 0

\|T_{A}(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
\omega
is a given real number. Assume further that
C
is a linear operator in
\mathbb{X}
such that

i)

D(A)\subset D(C)
;

ii)

There exists a constant
K>0
such that if
\mu>\omega
, then

(2.18)

\|CR(\mu,A)\|\leq\frac{K}{\mu-\omega}.

Then,
A+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup, denoted by
\left(T_{A+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
that satisfies

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|\leq Me^{(\omega+MK)t},\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Set
S(t):=e^{-\omega t}T_{A}(t)
. The generator of
\left(S(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
is
B:=A-\omega,
and
\|S(t)\|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
. We now apply
Lemma
2.2
to the semigroup
\left(S(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
. In fact, for
\mu>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|CR(\mu,B)\|

\displaystyle=\|CR(\mu+\omega,A)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{\mu+\omega-\omega}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{\mu}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, the operator
B+C
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{B+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
. It is easy to see that the semigroup
\left(e^{\omega t}T_{B+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
has
B+C+\omega=A+C
as its generator. That is, the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(e^{\omega t}T_{B+C}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
is generated by
A+C
, so
(e^{\omega t}T_{B+C}(t))_{t\geq 0}=(T_{A+C}(t))_{t\geq 0}
which satisfies [(
2.2
) applies to
B+C
]:

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|

\displaystyle=e^{\omega t}T_{B+C}(t)\|

\displaystyle=e^{\omega}\|T_{B+C}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\omega t}Me^{MKt},\quad t\geq 0.

And, the theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

If
C
is a bounded linear operator, the estimate (
2.19
) can be improved a little bit. In fact, in this case
K
can be replaced with
M\|C\|
, therefore, by Remark
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{A+C}(t)\|

\displaystyle=e^{\omega t}\|T_{B+C}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\omega t}Me^{M\|C\|t}

\displaystyle=Me^{(\omega+M\|C\|)t},\quad t\geq 0.

3.
About the Space
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})

In this section, given an operator
A
as the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup we will define a normed space
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
associated with
A
, that is equipped with a norm determined by Theorem
2.4
. We will study the persistence of some behavior of solutions of the equation
u^{\prime}(t)=Au(t)
,
t\geq 0,
like exponential dichotomy under small perturbation of
A
in
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
.

Below we always assume that
A
generates a
C_{0}
semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
with
\left\|T_{A}(t)\right\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
and for certain fixed constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
to be the linear space of all linear operators
C
in
\mathbb{X}
with
D(C)=D(A)
and

(3.1)

\sup_{\mu>\omega}(\mu-\omega)\|CR(\mu,A)\|<\infty.

For each
C\in\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
we define

(3.2)

\|C\|_{A}:=\frac{1}{M}\sup_{\mu>\omega}\|(\mu-\omega)CR(\mu,A)\|<\infty.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given an operator
A
in
\mathbb{X}
as the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Then,
(\mathcal{GL}_{A}(X),\|\cdot\|_{A})
is a normed space.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
is a linear space, and that
\|\cdot\|
satisfies the following conditions:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|C\|_{A}\geq 0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\mu C\|_{A}=|\mu|\|C\|_{A},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|C+B\|_{A}\leq\|C\|_{A}+\|B\|_{A},

for all
B,\,C\in\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
, and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. We just need to show that
\|C\|_{A}=0
implies that
C=0
(on
D(A)
). In fact, if
\|C\|_{A}=0
, then for every
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
\mu>\omega
we have
CR(\mu,A)x=0
. Since
R(\mu,A)
is a bijective map from
\mathbb{X}
on
D(A)
, for every given
y\in D(A)
there exists an
x\in\mathbb{X}
such that
y=R(\mu,A)x
. Therefore, for each
y\in D(A)
,
Cy=CR(\mu,A)x=0
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that if
C\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X})
, then,
\|C\|_{A}\leq\|C\|
. In fact, by the Hille-Yosida’s theorem (applied to
A
and the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
),

\frac{1}{M}\sup_{\mu>\omega}\|(\mu-\omega)R(\mu,A)\|\leq 1,

so we have

\displaystyle\|C\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sup_{\mu>\omega}\|(\mu-\omega)CR(\mu,A)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|\frac{1}{M}\sup_{\mu>\omega}\|(\mu-\omega)R(\mu,A)\|

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|.

This means that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X})\subset\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
.

Below we will apply Theorem
2.4
to study the persistence of an asymptotic behavior of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
when its generator
A
is under small perturbation
C
in the sense of the norm in
\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
. This can be done via the concept of
Yosida distance
.

Recall that given two linear operators
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{X}
, the
Yosida distance

d_{Y}(A,B)
, assuming that
\rho(A)
and
\rho(B)
contain the ray
[\omega,\infty)
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, is defined as,
see
[
1
,
2
]
,

(3.7)

d_{Y}(A,B):=\limsup_{\lambda\to+\infty}\lambda^{2}\|R(\lambda,A)-R(\lambda,B)\|.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Then, for any
C_{1},\,C_{2}\in\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
the following assertion is true:

(3.8)

d_{Y}(A+C_{1},A+C_{2})\leq\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
, both
A+C_{1}
and
A+C_{2}
are the generators of
C_{0}
-semigroups, so, for sufficiently large
\mu_{0}>0
,
[\mu_{0},\infty)\subset\rho(A+C_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Then, for
\mu>\mu_{0}
,
by (
2.6
) we have

\displaystyle R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})

\displaystyle=R(\mu,A+C_{1})C_{1}R(\mu,A)-R(\mu,A+C_{2})C_{2}R(\mu,A).

Therefore, by part (i) and the Hille-Yosida’s Theorem for
C_{0}
-semigroups,

\displaystyle\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})\|\cdot\|C_{1}R(\mu,A)-C_{2}R(\mu,A)\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C+C_{2})\|\cdot\|C_{2}R(\mu,A)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{\mu-(\omega+M^{2}\|C_{1}\|_{A})}\|(C_{1}-C_{2})R(\mu,A)\|

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|\cdot\|C_{2}R(\mu,A)\|.

Therefore, for large
\mu
, by assertion (i),

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|(1-\|C_{2}R(\mu,A)\|)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu^{2}M}{\mu-(\omega+M^{2}\|C_{1}\|_{A})}\|(C_{1}-C_{2})R(\mu,A)\|.

Consequently, by the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{A}
, for
\mu>\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\|C_{2}R(\mu,A)\|\leq\frac{1}{M}\cdot\frac{\|C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega},

\displaystyle\|(C_{1}-C_{2})R(\mu,A)\|\leq\frac{1}{M}\cdot\frac{\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega},

so, for sufficiently large
\mu>0
,

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|\left(1-\frac{\|C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega}\right)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu-(\omega+M^{2}\|C_{1}\|_{A})}\cdot\frac{\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega}.

Finally,

\displaystyle d_{Y}(A+C_{1},A+C_{2})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\mu\to\infty}\mu^{2}\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\mu\to\infty}\left[\mu^{2}\|R(\mu,A+C_{1})-R(\mu,A+C_{2})\|\left(1-\frac{\|C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\mu\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu-(\omega+M+\|C_{1}\|_{A})}\cdot\frac{\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}}{\mu-\omega}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

Definition 3.5

(Exponential dichotomy)
.

A linear
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
is said to have an
exponential dichotomy
or to be
hyperbolic
if there exist a bounded projection
P
on
\mathbb{X}
and positive constants
N
and
\alpha
satisfying

i)

T(t)P=PT(t)
, for
t\geq 0
;

ii)

T(t)\big|_{\ker(P)}
is an isomorphism from
\ker(P)
onto
\ker(P)
, for all
t\geq 0
,
and its inverse on
\ker(P)
is defined by
T(-t):=\left(T(t)|_{\ker(P)}\right)^{-1}
;

iii)

the following estimates hold

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|T(t)x\|\leq Ne^{-\alpha t}\|x\|,\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0,\quad x\in\mathrm{Im}(P),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|T(-t)x\|\leq Ne^{-\alpha t}\|x\|,\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0,\quad x\in\ker(P).

When the projection
P=I
the semigroup
\left(T(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
exponentially stable
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
. Then,

i)

It has an exponential dichotomy if and only if

\sigma(T(1))\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}=\emptyset.

ii)

It is exponentially stable if and only if
r_{\sigma}(T(1))<1.

Proof.

For the proof of Part (i), see
[
7
, Theorem 1.17]
. For Part (ii) the proof is straightforward via the Spectral Radius Theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
A
be the generator of a

-semigroup
\left(T_{A}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}

\mathbb{X}

\|T_{A}(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
. Assume further that for a given
C_{1}\in\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})
, the semigroup
\left(T_{A+C_{1}}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}

C_{2}\in\mathcal{GL}_{A}(\mathbb{X})

\left(T_{A+C_{2}}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
generated by
A+C_{2}
has also an exponential dichotomy provided that
\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}
is sufficiently small. In particular, if
\left(T_{A+C_{1}}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable, then
(T_{A+C_{2}}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is also exponentially stable provided that
\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}

Proof.

By (
3.12
) in the proof of Theorem
3.4
the Yosida distance

\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}
is sufficiently small. On the other hand, by the proof of
[
2

, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|T_{A+C_{1}}(t)-T_{A+C_{2}}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq tM^{2}e^{4\omega_{0}t}d_{Y}(A+C_{1},A+C_{2})

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq tM^{2}e^{4\omega_{0}t}\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A},

where

\omega_{0}:=\omega+M^{2}(\|C_{1}\|+\epsilon_{0}),

with
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|<\epsilon_{0}.
Suppose that
\left(T_{A+C_{1}}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}

n_{0}
such that
\|T_{A+C_{1}}(n_{0})\|<1

, by (
3.15
) we have
\|T_{A+C_{2}}(n_{0})\|<1

(T_{A+C_{2}}(t))_{t\geq 0}

Now suppose that
(T_{A+C_{2}}(t))_{t\geq 0}
has an exponential dichotomy.
By (
3.15
), we have

(3.16)

\|T_{A+C_{1}}(1)-T_{A+C_{2}}(1)\|\leq M^{2}e^{4\omega_{0}}\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{A}.

Therefore, if
\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|
is sufficiently small,
\|T_{A+C_{1}}(1)-T_{A+C_{2}}(1)\|
is also sufficiently small. By

, as the set

for sufficiently small

Rosenthal compacta that are premetric of finite degree
Antonio Avilés, Alejandro Poveda and Stevo Todorcevic

Abstract
We show that if a separable Rosenthal compactum K K is a continuous n n -to-one preimage of a metric compactum, but it is not a continuous n − 1 n-1 -to-one preimage, then K K contains a closed subset homeomorphic to either the n − n- Split interval S n ​ ( I ) S_{n}(I) or the Alexandroff n − n- plicate D n ​ ( 2 ℕ ) D_{n}(2^{\mathbb{N}}) . This generalizes a result of the third author that corresponds to the case n = 2 n=2 .

Given two nonempty sets

Recall that every product

Remember that a topological space is said to be Polish if it is separable and completely metrizable; that is, there exists a complete metric compatible with its topology. If

Definition 1

(Cantor Scheme)

Given a set

1.

\mathcal{A}_{s\frown 0}\cap\mathcal{A}_{s\frown 1}=\emptyset
.

2.

for all

.

Given any totally ordered set

with the topology generated by its
\prec-
rays

. Recall that given two partially ordered sets

(x_{1},y_{1})\leq_{lex}(x_{2},y_{2})\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\text{if}\;\;(x_{1}<x_{2})\;\;\text{or}\;\;(x_{1}=x_{2},\;\;\text{and}\;\;y_{1}\preceq y_{2}).

A special property shared by every space whose topology arises from an order is the following

Theorem 1

For a topological space

endowed with an order topology it is equivalent to be separable and hereditarily separable.

If

is a countable dense subset of

.∎

A real-valued function
f
is of the
first Baire class
on a Polish space
X
, if it is the pointwise limit of real-valued continuous functions on
X
. That is,
f
is a first Baire class function if there are continuous functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
such that
f(x)=\lim_{n}f_{n}(x)
for every
x\in X
. This class of functions is usually denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(X)
.

Definition 2

(Rosenthal compactum)

We say that a topological space
K
is a Rosenthal compactum if it is homeomorphic to a pointwise compact subspace of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}})
.

If
X
is an arbitrary Polish space, then every pointwise compact subset of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(X)
is a Rosenthal compactum, because

is a continuous image of
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
.
The class of Rosenthal compact spaces constitutes a generalization of the class of compact metrizable spaces and many of the properties of metrizable compacta hold true as well for Rosenthal compacta. One example is Bourgain-Fremlin-Talagrand’s result on the Fréchet-Uryshon property of Rosenthal compacta
[
BFT78
]
, meaning that every point in the closure of a set is the limit of a convergent sequence from that set. Another remarkable result on the same line states that every Rosenthal compactum contains a dense metrizable subspace
[
Tod99
]
. We are specially interested in the separable Rosenthal compacta. If we have countably many Borel functions on a Polish space
X
, we can add countably many open sets to the topology of

to get a larger Polish topology where all those functions are continuous. From this observation one gets that:

Proposition 1

Every separable Rosenthal compactum is homeomorphic to the pointwise closure in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}})
of a sequence of continuous functions.

Recall that a Rosenthal compactum
K
is premetric of degree at most

onto a metric compactum

such that
|f^{-1}(x)|\leq n
for all

. Some classical examples of premetric compacta of degree at most

are the compact metric spaces (

) as well as the Split interval

and the Alexandroff duplicate of the Cantor space

(

). In this regard, in this paper we present the

and

of

the definition of

. There are also classical examples of Rosenthal compacta which are not premetric compacta of any degree. One of theses examples is the Alexandroff compactification of a discrete space

There is a nice topological characterization of those compact spaces (not necessarily Rosenthal) that are of certain degree depending on projections over countable products.

Proposition 2

Given a set

If there exists a

such that

is at most

continuous and at most

of

such that if

Definition 3

(Split Interval)

The Split Interval

is the space

endowed with the topology induced by the lexicographical order.

It is easy to see that a neighbourhood basis for points

,
(x,1)

\displaystyle](y,0),(x,1)[\;\;\text{with}\;\;y\in I,\,y<x.

\displaystyle](x,0),(y,1)[\;\;\text{with}\;\;y\in I,\,x<y.

It is convenient to make some comments about the convergence on

It is easy to see that

is a Rosenthal compactum. Indeed, the map given by

is separable and thus hereditarily separable by virtue of Theorem

are the countable ones. We shall see later that the space

is not separable, so

does not contain copies of

precisely because

is hereditarily separable. We summarize in the following theorem some of the most important properties of

that will be necessary later:

Theorem 2

(Properties of

)

The Split Interval

has the following properties:

1.

It is Rosenthal compactum of degree
2
.

2.

It is hereditarily separable.

3.

It is non-metrizable so it is not of degree
1
.

4.

It does not contain copies of

.

The present section will be devoted to the proof of our main result. First of all, it is convenient to point out that the assumption of separability is essential. Indeed, let us consider the Rosenthal compact space

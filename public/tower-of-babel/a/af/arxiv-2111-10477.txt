1.
Introduction

Let
M_{k}(T)
denote the
2k^{\text{th}}
moment of the Riemann zeta-function. Namely, we let

M_{k}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|^{2k}dt.

Hardy and Littlewood
[
HL16
]
showed that
M_{1}(T)\sim T\log T
, and Ingham
[
Ing26
]
showed that
M_{2}(T)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}T(\log T)^{4}
. It is conjectured that

M_{k}(T)\sim A_{k}T(\log T)^{k^{2}},

for some constant
A_{k}
, whose precise value was predicted by Keating and Snaith
[
KS00b
]
, using analogies with random matrix theory. No moment higher than
4
has been rigorously computed so far. Soundararajan
[
Sou09
]
obtained almost sharp upper bounds, conditional on the Riemann hypothesis. More precisely, he showed that
M_{k}(T)\ll T(\log T)^{k^{2}+\epsilon},
for any
\epsilon>0
. Refining Soundararajan’s method, Harper
[
Har
]
obtained upper bounds of the correct order of magnitude for moments of the Riemann zeta-function, by removing the
\epsilon
on the power of
\log T
.

Focusing on the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions, Jutila
[
Jut81
]
obtained asymptotics for the first and second moment of this family. He showed that

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq D}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)\sim C_{1}D\log D,

where the sum above is over fundamental discriminants, and that

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq D}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)^{2}\sim C_{2}D(\log D)^{3},

for some explicit constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
Soundararajan
[
Sou00
]
obtained an asymptotic formula for the second moment with a power savings error term, and also obtained an asymptotic for the third moment. The cubic moment was independently computed using multiple Dirichlet series in
[
DGH03
]
. More recently, a lower order term of size
D^{3/4}
was explicitly computed for the cubic moment by Diaconu and Whitehead
[
DW21
]
and by Diaconu in the function field setting
[
Dia19
]
. Conditional on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, Shen
[
She21
]
obtained an asymptotic with the leading order term for the fourth moment.
Generally, it is conjectured that

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq D}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)^{k}\sim C_{k}D(\log D)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}},

(1)

and the precise value of
C_{k}
follows from work of Keating and Snaith
[
KS00a
]
, again using random matrix theory. Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
CFK+05
]
further refined this conjecture, including lower order terms in the asymptotic formula (
1
).
The approach used by Soundararajan and Harper in obtaining upper bounds for moments of
\zeta(s)
yields upper bounds of the right order of magnitude for the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions conditional on GRH, while work of Soundararajan and Rudnick
[
RS05
]
provides unconditional lower bounds of the right order of magnitude. Over function fields, the first moment was computed by Andrade and Keating
[
AK12
]
, and a lower order term identified in
[
Flo17c
]
. Higher moments, up to the fourth, were obtained in
[
Flo17b
,
Flo17a
]
, as well as almost sharp upper bounds on all the positive moments.

While all the results mentioned above hold for positive moments in families of
L
–functions, much less is known about negative moments, even at a conjectural level. In the case of the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
, a conjecture due to Gonek
[
Gon89
]
states the following.

Conjecture 1.1

(Gonek)
.

Let
k>0
be fixed. Uniformly for
1\leq\delta\leq\log T
,

\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\Big|\zeta\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{\log T}+it\Big)\Big|^{-2k}\,dt\asymp\Big(\frac{\log T}{\delta}\Big)^{k^{2}},

and uniformly for
0<\delta\leq 1
,

\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\Big|\zeta\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{\log T}+it\Big)\Big|^{-2k}\,dt\asymp\begin{cases}(\log T)^{k^{2}}&\mbox{ if }k<1/2,\\
(\log\frac{e}{\delta})(\log T)^{k^{2}}&\mbox{ if }k=1/2,\\
\delta^{1-2k}(\log T)^{k^{2}}&\mbox{ if }k>1/2.\end{cases}

Random matrix theory inspired ideas (see
[
BK02
,
FK04
]
) seem to suggest certain transition regimes in the formulas above when
k=(2n+1)/2
, for
n
a positive integer.
While obtaining lower bounds for the negative moments is a more tractable problem (Gonek
[
Gon89
]
proved lower bounds of the conjectural correct order of magnitude for
1\leq\delta\leq\log T
and all
k>0
and for
0<\delta\leq 1
for
k<1/2
conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis), obtaining upper bounds is a more difficult problem, and no progress has been made so far on the problem in any family of
L
–functions (recent work in progress of the author and H. Bui addresses the question of obtaining upper bounds in some ranges of
\delta
).

In the case of quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions, when studying the
k^{\text{th}}
negative moment, random matrix theory computations due to Forrester and Keating
[
FK04
]
seem to suggest certain transition regimes for small shifts (i.e. shifts smaller than
1/\log X
, where
X
is roughly the size of the family.) More precisely, the computations in
[
FK04
]
suggest certain jumps in the asymptotic formulas when
k=2j+1/2
, and
j
a positive integer.

Very recently, almost sharp upper bounds were obtained for negative moments of quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions over function fields
[
BFK
]
when the shift in the
L
–function is big enough. Specifically, if
\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}
denotes the ensemble of monic, square-free polynomials of degree
2g+1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, let
L(s,\chi_{D})
denote the
L
–function associated to the quadratic character
\chi_{D}
. Then it is shown in
[
BFK
]
that for
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\ll\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}(\log g)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}.

(2)

Note that it is expected that the upper bound above is sharp, up to the logarithmic factor.

In this paper, we treat the range when
\beta\ll g^{-\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}
, which is more difficult. The closer we are to the critical line, the more difficult the problem becomes, due to the closer proximity of zeros. Here, we obtain non-trivial upper bounds for small shifts
\beta
with
\log(1/\beta)\ll\log g
. In certain ranges where
\beta
is big enough (i.e.,
\beta\gg g^{-1/k+\epsilon}
), we prove a more precise analogue of Gonek’s conjecture, obtaining an asymptotic formula. We remark that asymptotic formulas for negative moments of
L(1,\chi_{d})
were obtained by Granville and Soundararajan
[
GS03
]
in the number field setting and by Lumley
[
Lum19
]
in the function field setting. The techniques used in those papers are different, as one considers moments far from the critical point
1/2
, and the
L
–functions in those cases can be modeled by random Euler products. In our work, we obtain asymptotic formulas or upper bounds when the shift goes to zero with the size of the family. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k>0
and
\beta>0
such that
\log(1/\beta)\ll\log g
.
Then

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{\Big|L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta+it,\chi_{D}\Big)\Big|^{k}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\log g)^{k(k+2)/2}g^{k(k+1)/2}

if
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon},\,k\geq 3/2
,

(3)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\log g)^{7k/4}g^{5k/4}

if
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon},\,k<3/2,

(4)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle q^{g\big(k\frac{\log(1/\beta)}{\log g}-1+\epsilon\big)}

if
\beta=o(g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon})
.

(5)

Note that Theorem
1.2
above holds for any
t
(the
L
–function is periodic as a function of
t
).

We also refine Theorem
1.2
to obtain an asymptotic formula in the following case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k>0
and
\epsilon>0
. Then for
\Re\beta\gg\max\{g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon},\log g/g\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}A(1;\beta)+O\Big(q^{-g\Re(\beta)(1-\epsilon)}g^{1+\frac{k}{2}\Big(1+\max\{k,3/2\}\Big)}

\displaystyle\times(\log g)^{\frac{k}{2}(2+\max\{k,3/2\})}\Big),

with
A(1;\beta)
given in equation (
59
).

We note that the theorem above provides an asymptotic formula when
k\geq 1
and
\Re\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
. If
k<1
, then one needs
\Re\beta\geq c_{k}\log g/g
, for
c_{k}
a specific constant depending on
k
. We record this in the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
k>0
,
\epsilon>0
and
C>0
. Then for
\Re\beta\geq\max\{Cg^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon},(1+\epsilon)\frac{(\frac{7k}{4}-\frac{k^{2}}{2}+1)\log_{q}g}{g}
, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}A(1;\beta)+o(1).

Note that Corollary
1.4
allows one to obtain an asymptotic formula for the negative moments when
\beta
is as small as roughly
\log g/g
, as long as
k<1
. We note that the term
1+7k/4-k^{2}/2
could be slightly improved in the corollary above, but we have decided not to focus on that. It would be of interest to be able to obtain asymptotic formulas in the range
\beta\gg\log g/g
for all values of
k
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In section
2
we provide some background and the preliminary lemmas we will use throughout the paper. We prove Theorem
1.2
in section
4
and Theorem
1.3
in section
5
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
starts in a similar way as the proof of Theorem
1.3
in
[
BFK
]
, and uses sieve theoretic inspired ideas. This circle of ideas has recently been used successfully in a variety of settings, as in
[
Sou09
,
Har
,
RlS15
,
LR21
]
.

The difference from
[
BFK
]
which allows one to double the range of
\beta
(in Theorem
1.2
one obtains almost sharp bounds for
\beta\gg g^{-1/k+\epsilon}
as opposed to
\beta\gg g^{-1/(2k)+\epsilon}
in
[
BFK
]
) is the use of the large sieve for quadratic characters rather than simple orthogonality of characters. However, the quadratic large sieve introduces a factor of
q^{\epsilon g}
in the upper bound, hence one needs to use more care to refine the initial bound of size
q^{\epsilon g}
to a bound of the form
g^{O(1)}
. When performing the first step of the argument, one has to use an priori bound for negative moments coming from a pointwise bound for the inverse
L
–function. Once we obtain the upper bound of size
q^{\epsilon g}
(and keep track on the dependence on
k
in the bound), we do the second step of the argument, but use as an a priori bound for the negative moments the bound obtained in the previous step. When the shift is bigger than
g^{-1/k+\epsilon}
, the argument described above gives an almost sharp upper bound, up to some logarithmic factors. This allows us to further refine the result and obtain the asymptotic formula in Theorem
1.3
in that range.

2.
Background in function fields

Here we gather some basic facts about
L
–functions in function fields. Many of the proofs can be found in
[
Ros02
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of monic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,
\mathcal{M}_{n}
the set of monic polynomials of degree
n
,
\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}
the set of monic polynomials of degree at most
n
and
\mathcal{M}_{\geq n}
the set of monic polynomials with degree at least
n
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
denote the set of monic, square-free polynomials of degree
n
, and
\mathcal{P}
the ensemble of monic, irreducible polynomials. The symbol
P
will stand for a monic, irreducible polynomial.
Note that

|\mathcal{M}_{n}|=q^{n}
, and for
n\geq 1
,
|\mathcal{H}_{n}|=q^{n-1}(q-1)
.

For a polynomial
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, let
|f|:=q^{\deg(f)}
denote the norm of
f
. For
\Re(s)>1
, the zeta-function of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is defined by

\zeta_{q}(s)=\sum_{f\text{ monic}}\frac{1}{|f|^{s}}=\prod_{P}(1-|P|^{-s})^{-1}.

Since
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|=q^{n}
, we see that

\zeta_{q}(s)=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}.

It is sometimes convenient to make the change of variable
u=q^{-s}
, and then write
\mathcal{Z}(u)=\zeta_{q}(s)
, so that

\mathcal{Z}(u)=\frac{1}{1-qu}.

The Möbius function
\mu
is defined as usual by
\mu(f)=(-1)^{\omega(f)}
if
f
is a square-free polynomial and where
\omega(f)=\sum_{P|f}1
, and
0
otherwise.

The Prime Polynomial Theorem states that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
\deg(P)=n\end{subarray}}1=\frac{q^{n}}{n}+O\Big(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\Big).

(6)

We will also use the Prime Polynomial Theorem in the less precise form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
\deg(P)=n\end{subarray}}1\leq\frac{q^{n}}{n}.

(7)

(See, for example, formula
2.1
in
[
DFL21
]
.)

For
P
a monic irreducible polynomial, the quadratic residue symbol
\big(\frac{f}{P}\big)\in\{0,\pm 1\}
is defined by

\Big(\frac{f}{P}\Big)\equiv f^{(|P|-1)/2}(\textrm{mod}\ P).

If
Q=P_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}P_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\ldots P_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
, then the Jacobi symbol is defined by

\Big(\frac{f}{Q}\Big)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Big(\frac{f}{P_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha_{j}}.

The Jacobi symbol satisfies the quadratic reciprocity law. Namely, if
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are relatively prime, monic polynomials, then

\Big(\frac{A}{B}\Big)=(-1)^{(q-1)\deg(A)\deg(B)/2}\Big(\frac{B}{A}\Big).

For
D
monic, we define the character

\chi_{D}(g)=\Big(\frac{D}{g}\Big),

and consider the
L
-function attached to
\chi_{D}
,

L(s,\chi_{D}):=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\chi_{D}(f)}{|f|^{s}}.

With the change of variable
u=q^{-s}
we have

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{D}):=L(s,\chi_{D})=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\chi_{D}(f)u^{d(f)}=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\big(1-\chi_{D}(P)u^{d(P)}\big)^{-1}.

(8)

For
D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}
,
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{D})
is a polynomial in
u
of degree
2g
satisfying the functional equation

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{D})=(qu^{2})^{g}\mathcal{L}\Big(\frac{1}{qu},\chi_{D}\Big).

(9)

The Riemann Hypothesis for curves over function fields was proven by Weil
[
Wei48
]
, so all the zeros of
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{D})
are on the circle
|u|=q^{-1/2}
.

We will use the following pointwise upper bound for the inverse of the
L
–function.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
0<\beta\ll\frac{1}{\log g}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|}\leq\exp\Bigg(\frac{(1+\epsilon)g}{\log_{q}g}\log\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}\Big)\Bigg).

Proof.

See Lemma
5.3
and Remark
5.1
in
[
BFK
]
.
∎

We also need the following estimates.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f\in\mathcal{M}
we have

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\chi_{D}(f^{2})=|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|\prod_{P|f}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{|P|}\bigg)^{-1}+O_{\varepsilon}(|f|^{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

See, for example, Lemma 3.4 in
[
BF18
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For
f
not a square polynomial, we have

\Big|\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\chi_{D}(f)\Big|\ll q^{g}|f|^{\epsilon}.

Proof.

See Lemma
3.5
in
[
BF18
]
.
∎

Throughout the paper, we will frequently use the following analogue of Perron’s formula in function fields. If the power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)u^{n}
is absolutely convergent in
|z|\leq r<1
, then

\sum_{n\leq N}a(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|u|=r}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)u^{n})\Big)\frac{du}{(1-u)u^{N+1}}.

(10)

Now let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\ell
be an even integer. Let

E_{\ell}(t)=\sum_{s\leq\ell}\frac{t^{s}}{s!}.

Note that we have
E_{\ell}(t)>0
for any
t
since
\ell
is even. We will use the fact that for
t\leq\ell/e^{2}
, we have

e^{t}\leq(1+e^{-\ell/2})E_{\ell}(t).

(11)

For a proof, see, for example,
[
RlS15
]
.

Let
\nu(f)
be the multiplicative function given by

\nu(P^{a})=\frac{1}{a!}.

Let
\Omega(f)
denote the number of prime factors of
f
, counting multiplicity. We will use the following result (see Lemma
3.2
in
[
DFL21
]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a(f)
be a completely multiplicative function. Then for any interval
I
and any
s\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\bigg(\sum_{\deg(P)\in I}a(P)\bigg)^{s}=s!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P|f\Rightarrow\deg(P)\in I\\
\Omega(f)=s\end{subarray}}a(f)\nu(f).

We will also need the following form of the quadratic large sieve over function fields
[
FS
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a(f)
be arbitrary complex numbers supported on monic polynomials, and let
n=O(g)
. We have

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\Big|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}a(f)\chi_{D}(f)\Big|^{2}\leq(q^{2g}+q^{n})q^{Ag/(\log g)^{1/4}}\sum_{f_{1}f_{2}=\square}|a(f_{1})a(f_{2})|,

for some absolute constant
A>0
.

We note that the result in
[
FS
]
could be improved to obtain a better bound than
q^{Ag/(\log g)^{1/4}}
, but for the purpose of this paper, Lemma
2.5
above is enough.

Setup of the proof and initial lemmas

We will first introduce some of the ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, and will state some key lemmas. We will return to the proof of Theorem
1.2

.

Let

I_{0}=(0,N_{0}],I_{1}=(N_{0},N_{1}],\ldots,I_{K}=(N_{K-1},N_{K}],

where
N_{j}
are parameters we will choose later. Also let
s_{j}
and

and
\sum_{h=0}^{K}\ell_{h}N_{h}\ll g

Let

\displaystyle a_{\beta}(P;N)

\displaystyle=-\cos(t\deg(P)\log q)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{(j+1)\deg(P)q^{-j(N+1)\beta}}{\deg(P)+j(N+1)}

\displaystyle-\frac{(j+1)\deg(P)q^{-(j+2)(N+1)\beta}|P|^{2\beta}}{(j+2)(N+1)-\deg(P)}\Big).

We extend

As in
[
BFK
]
, for
\deg(P)\leq N
, we have

|a_{\beta}(P;N)|\leq 1+\frac{1}{q^{(N+1)\beta}-1},

(12)

if

, and

|a_{\beta}(P;N)|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\Big)\Big(\log\frac{1}{N\beta}\Big)

(13)

if

. Note that in
[

|a_{\beta}(P;N)|\leq B(N)\Big(\log\frac{1}{N\beta}\Big)^{\gamma(N)},

(14)

where
\gamma(N)=1

and

(16)

Lemma 3.1
.

We either have

or

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\leq\exp(O(k))(S_{1}(D)+S_{2}(D)),

(17)

\displaystyle S_{2}(D)=(\log g)^{k/2}

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq K-1}\sum_{j<u\leq K}\Big(1-q^{-(N_{j}+1)\beta}\Big)^{-\frac{2gk}{N_{j}+1}}N_{j}^{k/2}

Proof.

\exp\Big(\frac{2gk}{N_{K}+1}\log\frac{1}{1-q^{-(N_{K}+1)\beta}}\Big)=O(1),

and then

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}S_{1}(D)\leq q^{2g+1}\exp\Big(O(k^{2})\Big)(\log g)^{k/2}g^{k(k+1)/2}.

(45)

Now we proceed as before to deal with the term
S_{2}(D)
, but use Lemma
3.5
instead of Lemma
3.4
. Similarly to the bound (
35
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}

\displaystyle S_{2}(D)\leq q^{2g+1}(\log g)^{k/2}\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}(K-j)\exp\Big(\frac{g\log g}{N_{j}}\Big(2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}\Big)

(46)

\displaystyle+\frac{g\log N_{j}}{N_{j}}\Big(\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}-2k\Big)+\frac{ag}{rN_{j}}\log\Big(e^{3/2}ckB(N_{j+1})\Big(\log\frac{1}{N_{j+1}\beta}\Big)^{\gamma(N_{j+1})}\Big)

\displaystyle+(\log N_{j})\Big(B(N_{j})^{2}k^{2}(\log\frac{1}{N_{j}\beta})^{2\gamma(N_{j};\beta)}/2+k/2\Big)\Big)\exp\Big(O\Big(k^{2}\Big(\log\frac{1}{N_{j}\beta}\Big)^{2\gamma(N_{j})}\Big),

for some constant
c>0
.
Because
\beta\ll g^{-\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}
, we have
2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}\geq k\epsilon,
in light of (
40
). We also have that
\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}-2k<0
, since
k\geq 1
.
Now note the function in the sum over
j
above is decreasing as a function of
N_{j}
for
N_{j}\leq\delta g^{(2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})/(2k-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})}
and is increasing for
N_{j}>\delta g^{(2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})/(2k-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})}
for some constant
\delta>0
. If
2k(2\alpha-1)\leq\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}
, then since
N_{0}\asymp\sqrt{g}(\log g)^{1/8}/\sqrt{\log\log g}
, we have that
N_{j}>\delta g^{(2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})/(2k-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})}
for all
j\leq K-1
, and hence the function of
N_{j}
in (
46
) is increasing.Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}

\displaystyle S_{2}(D)\leq q^{2g+1}K(\log g)^{k/2}\exp\Big(\frac{g\log g(\alpha-1)}{N_{K-1}}+O(1)+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\log g\Big).

Using the fact that
\alpha-1\leq-\epsilon
(recall that
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
and
k\geq 1
), that
N_{K-1}\asymp g
and that
K\asymp\log g
, it follows that

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}S_{2}(D)=o\Big(q^{2g}g^{k(k+1)/2}\Big).

(47)

Now if
k\geq 3/2
, then since
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
, it follows that
2k(2\alpha-1)\leq\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r}
, so the bound (
47
) holds in this case.

Putting things together (equations (
44
), (
45
), (
47
)) it follows that for
k\geq 3/2
and
\beta\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
, then

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\leq q^{2g+1}\exp\Big(O(k^{2})\Big)(\log g)^{k(k+2)/2}g^{k(k+1)/2}.

(48)

This proves the bound (
3
) in Theorem
1.2
.

If
k<3/2
, let
m
be such that
mk\geq 3/2
. Using Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\leq\Big(\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{mk}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m}}\Big(\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}1\Big)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}.

Since
mk\geq 3/2
, we use (
48
), and it follows that

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\ll q^{2g}(\log g)^{k(mk+2)/2}g^{\frac{k}{2}(mk+1)}.

Picking
m=3/(2k)
, we get that

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\ll q^{2g}(\log g)^{7k/4}g^{\frac{5k}{4}}.

This finishes the proof of the bound (
4
) in Theorem
1.2
.

4.3.
The range
\beta\ll g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}

Here, we choose the parameters as follows:

N_{0}=\Big[\frac{\log_{q}g(4(d-1/2)+2k\alpha-\frac{a(d-1/2)}{r})}{k\alpha(1+\epsilon)}\Big],\,s_{0}=2[2g/N_{0}],\,\ell_{0}=2\lceil s_{0}^{d}/2\rceil,

(49)

where we choose

so that

(50)

For

, we choose
N_{j},s_{j}
and
\ell_{j}
as in (

). We choose
N_{K}
to be the greatest integer of the form given in (
28
) such that

N_{K}\leq c_{2}g,

(51)

where

\displaystyle\frac{4c_{2}^{1-d}a^{d}r^{1-d}}{r^{1-d}-1}g+\frac{8rc_{2}}{r-1}g+2(4^{d}g^{d})N_{0}^{1-d}\leq(4-a)g.

The third term above is of size
o(g)

3.2
,
3.4
,
3.6

We now proceed similarly as in the previous case.

If
D\notin\mathcal{T}_{0}
, then for some
0\leq u\leq K
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{D\notin\mathcal{T}_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\leq\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\Big(\frac{ke^{2}}{\ell_{0}}\Big)^{s_{0}}(P_{I_{0}}(D;N_{u}))^{s_{0}}.

(52)

Now using the pointwise bound in Lemma
2.1
for the

\displaystyle\sum_{D\notin\mathcal{T}_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\leq q^{2g+gk\alpha(1+\epsilon)+Ag/(\log g)^{1/4}}\exp\Big(-s_{0}(d-1/2)\log s_{0}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(s_{0}\log\Big(2^{1/2}e^{5/2}kB(N_{0})\Big(\log\frac{1}{N_{0}\beta}\Big)^{\gamma(N_{0})}\sqrt{\log N_{0}}\Big)\Big).

Note that with the choice (

), it follows that (after a relabeling of the

):

\sum_{D\notin\mathcal{T}_{0}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\ll q^{g(k\alpha+1+2k\epsilon)}.

(53)

Now we consider the contribution from
D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
Using Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}\frac{1}{|L(1/2+\beta+it,\chi_{D})|^{k}}\ll\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}S_{1}(D)+\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}S_{2}(D).

Now using Lemma
3.6
and similarly to the previous case, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}S_{1}(D)\ll q^{2g}\exp\Big(O\Big(k^{2}\Big(\log\frac{1}{N_{K}\beta}\Big)^{2\gamma(N_{K})}\Big)(\log g)^{k/2}N_{K}^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{k^{2}B(N_{K})^{2}}{2}\Big(\log\frac{1}{N_{K}\beta}\Big)^{2\gamma(N_{K})}},

5.
The asymptotic formula

Here, we will prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\chi_{D}(h)\tau_{-k}(h)}{|h|^{1/2+\beta}},

(55)

where
\tau_{-k}
is the generalized divisor function (i.e., the multiplicative function which is given by

\tau_{-k}(P^{r})=(-1)^{r}\binom{k}{r},

for
P
a prime and
r
a positive integer, and where
\binom{k}{r}
is the generalized binomial coefficient.)
In equation (
55
), we truncate the sum over
h
and we write

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{<X}}\frac{\chi_{D}(h)\tau_{-k}(h)}{|h|^{1/2+\beta}}+\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{\geq X}}\frac{\chi_{D}(h)\tau_{-k}(h)}{|h|^{1/2+\beta}},

for some parameter
X
which we will choose later. For now, we can think of
X\asymp g
. Let
M_{1}
denote the first term above, and
M_{2}
the second. For
M_{2}
, we use Perron’s formula (
10
) for the sum over
h
and we have

M_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}\Big(\frac{z}{q^{1/2+\beta}},\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}z^{X}(z-1)}\,dz,

where we are integrating over a circle of radius
|z|>1
. We can pick for example
|z|=q^{\Re(\beta)/2}
, and then using Theorem
1.2
it follows that

M_{2}\ll q^{2g-X\Re(\beta)/2}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}\Big)g^{\frac{k}{2}\Big(1+\max\{k,3/2\}\Big)}(\log g)^{\frac{k}{2}(2+\max\{k,3/2\})}.

Since
\Re(\beta)\gg\max\{g^{-1/k+\epsilon},\log g/g\}
, we have that
1/\Re(\beta)\ll g
, so

M_{2}\ll q^{2g-X\Re(\beta)/2}g^{1+\frac{k}{2}\Big(1+\max\{k,3/2\}\Big)}(\log g)^{\frac{k}{2}(2+\max\{k,3/2\})}.

(56)

We now focus on
M_{1}
. We write
M_{1}=M(\square)+M(\neq\square),
corresponding to whether
h
is a square or not in the expression for
M_{1}
.
When
h\neq\square
, we use Lemma
2.3
, and we get that

\displaystyle M(\neq\square)\ll q^{g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\mathcal{M}_{<X}\\
h\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{|\tau_{-k}(h)|}{|h|^{1/2+\Re(\beta)}}|h|^{\epsilon}.

Trivially bounding the sum over
h
yields

M(\neq\square)\ll q^{\frac{X}{2}+g+\epsilon g}.

(57)

Now for the term
M(\square)
, we use Lemma
2.2
and we rewrite

\displaystyle M(\square)=\frac{q^{2g+1}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{<X/2}}\frac{\tau_{-k}(h^{2})}{|h|^{1+2\beta}}\prod_{P|h}\Big(1+\frac{1}{|P|}\Big)^{-1}+O(q^{\epsilon g}).

(58)

We look at the generating series of the sum over
h
, and we have

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\tau_{-h}(h^{2})}{|h|^{1+2\beta}}

\displaystyle\prod_{P|h}\Big(1+\frac{1}{|P|}\Big)^{-1}u^{\deg(h)}=\prod_{P}\Big(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{k}{2j}u^{j\deg(P)}}{|P|^{j(1+2\beta)}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{|P|}\Big)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}\Big(\frac{u}{q^{1+2\beta}}\Big)^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathcal{A}(u;\beta),

(59)

where
\mathcal{A}(u;\beta)
is given by an Euler product which converges in a wider region (for example, for
|u|<\sqrt{q}
.) Using Perron’s formula (
10
) in (
58
), we get that

\displaystyle M(\square)=\frac{q^{2g+1}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{\mathcal{Z}\Big(\frac{u}{q^{1+2\beta}}\Big)^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathcal{A}(u;\beta)}{(1-u)u^{[X/2]+1}}\,du,

where we are integrating along a small circle around the origin. We can shift the contour of integration to
|u|=q^{1/2-\epsilon}
, and encounter the poles at
u=1
and
u=q^{2\beta}
. We evaluate the residue of the pole at
u=1
and bound the contribution from the residue at
u=q^{2\beta}
. We get that

M(\square)=\frac{q^{2g+1}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathcal{A}(1;\beta)+O\Big(q^{2g-X\Re(\beta)}\frac{X^{k(k-1)/2-1}}{\beta}\Big).

Since
X\asymp g
and
1/\Re(\beta)\ll g
, we have that

M(\square)=\frac{q^{2g+1}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathcal{A}(1;\beta)+O\Big(q^{2g-X\Re(\beta)}g^{k(k-1)/2}\Big).

(60)

Now we combine the bounds (
56
), (
57
) and (
60
), and pick
X=2g(1-2\epsilon)
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}A(1;\beta)+O\Big(q^{-g\Re(\beta)(1-2\epsilon)}g^{1+\frac{k}{2}\Big(1+\max\{k,3/2\}\Big)}

\displaystyle\times(\log g)^{\frac{k}{2}(2+\max\{k,3/2\})}\Big).

The conclusion now follows after a relabeling of the
\epsilon
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

This easily follows from Theorem
1.3
. Indeed, if
k\geq 1
and
\Re(\beta)\gg g^{-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
then Theorem
1.3
provides an asymptotic formula. If
k<1
, then we rewrite Theorem
1.3
as

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{1}{L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{D}\Big)^{k}}=\zeta_{q}(1+2\beta)^{\binom{k}{2}}A(1;\beta)+O\Big(q^{-g\Re\beta(1-\epsilon)}g^{1+5k/4}(\log g)^{7k/4}\Big).

Note that the main term above is of size
(1/\beta)^{k(k-1)/2}
. If
g\Re(\beta)(1-\epsilon)\log q>(1+5k/4+k(1-k)/2+\epsilon)\log g
, then indeed the expression above indeed provides an asymptotic formula. Corollary (
1.4
) follows after a relabeling of
\epsilon
.
∎

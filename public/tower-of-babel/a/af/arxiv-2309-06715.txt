On correlation distribution of Niho-type decimation d = 3 ​ ( p m − 1 ) + 1 d=3(p^{m}-1)+1
Maosheng Xiong, Haode Yan

Abstract
The cross-correlation problem is a classic problem in sequence design. In this paper we compute the cross-correlation distribution of the Niho-type decimation d = 3 ​ ( p m − 1 ) + 1 d=3(p^{m}-1)+1 over GF ⁡ ( p 2 ​ m ) {\mathrm{GF}}(p^{2m}) for any prime p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 . Previously this problem was solved by Xia et al. only for p = 2 p=2 and p = 3 p=3 in a series of papers. The main difficulty of this problem for p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , as pointed out by Xia et al., is to count the number of codewords of “pure weight” 5 in p p -ary Zetterberg codes. It turns out this counting problem can be transformed by the MacWilliams identity into counting codewords of weight at most 5 in p p -ary Melas codes, the most difficult of which is related to a K3 surface well studied in the literature and can be computed. When p ≥ 7 p\geq 7 , the theory of elliptic curves over finite fields also plays an important role in the resolution of this problem.

II-A

Character sums and elliptic curves

Assume here that
p
is odd. Denote by
\eta

{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})^{*}:={\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})\setminus\{0\}

\eta
to
{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})
by setting
\eta(0)=0
. For any element
\beta\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})

\eta(\beta)=1

in
{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})

\pm\sqrt{\beta}

For a polynomial
f(x)\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})[x]

\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})}\eta(f(x)).

This character sum is trivial to evaluate when

is linear. When

is quadratic, we have

Lemma 2
.

[
9
, Theorem 5.48]
Let

with

. Put

and let
\eta
be the quadratic character of
{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})
. Then

\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})}\eta(f(x))=\begin{cases}-\eta(a_{2}),&\text{ if }d\neq 0,\\
(p^{m}-1)\eta(a_{2}),&\text{ if }d=0.\end{cases}

As it will be seen later, to compute the correlation distribution for
d=3(p^{m}-1)+1
, we need to evaluate a specific character sum

\displaystyle\lambda_{p^{m}}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})}\eta\left((x^{2}-4)(2x+1)(2x+5)\right).

(7)

The value
\lambda_{5^{m}}
can be computed easily. When
p\geq 7
, the polynomial involved in
\lambda_{p^{m}}
is of degree 4, so the character sum corresponds to the elliptic curve

over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{m})
. The evaluation of
\lambda_{p^{m}}
is equivalent to the computation of the Hasse zeta function of the corresponding elliptic curve over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
. Generally speaking, there is no explicit and simple formula for such character sums, except in rare situation when the elliptic curves are very special
[
16
]
. Based on the theory of elliptic curves, we can employ an efficient algorithm to evaluate
\lambda_{p^{m}}
for any

. Interested readers may also review
[
21
, Section II]
for a more detailed description as the theory of elliptic curves was used in the same way to evaluate a similar character sum for

II-B

Linear codes, the complete weight distribution and MacWilliams identity

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,k]_{q}
code, that is,
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bot}
be the dual code of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to the usual inner product on
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}
.

To define the complete weight distribution of
{\mathcal{C}}
, we first order elements of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
explicitly as
u_{0}=0,u_{1},\cdots,u_{q-1}
. Next, for any
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}
, the
complete weight
of
\mathbf{c}
is defined to be

\mathrm{Comp}(\mathbf{c}):=(w_{0},w_{1},\cdots,w_{q-1}),

where
w_{i}:=w_{i}(\mathbf{c})=\#\{1\leq j\leq n:c_{j}=u_{i}\}
. It is clear that
w_{i}
is a non-negative integer for each
i
and
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}w_{i}=n
.

Denote by
\Sigma^{n,q}
the set of all integer vectors
\mathbf{t}=(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots,t_{q-1})
such that
t_{i}\geq 0\,\forall\,i
and
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}t_{i}=n
. The
complete weight enumerator
of the code
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as the polynomial in variables
z_{0},\ldots,z_{q-1}
:

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{q-1}):=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}z_{0}^{w_{0}}z_{1}^{w_{1}}\cdots z_{q-1}^{w_{q-1}}=\sum_{\mathbf{t}}A_{\mathbf{t}}\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{t}}.

Here the subscript
\mathbf{t}=(t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{q-1})
runs over all
\mathbf{t}\in\Sigma^{n,q}
,
\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{t}}:=z_{0}^{t_{0}}z_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots z_{q-1}^{t_{q-1}}
, and

A_{\mathbf{t}}:=\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in{\mathcal{C}}:\mathrm{Comp}(\mathbf{c})=\mathbf{t}\right\},\quad\forall\mathbf{t}\in\Sigma^{n,q}.

The sequence
(A_{\mathbf{t}})_{\mathbf{t}}
is called the
complete weight distribution
of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Setting
z_{0}=1,z_{1}=\cdots=z_{q-1}=z
in the complete weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}
, we obtain the
weight enumerator
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, which can be written as

W_{C}(1,z,\cdots,z)=\sum_{t=0}^{n}A_{t}z^{t},

where

A_{t}:=\#\{\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}:\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})=t\}.

Here
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})
is the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{c}
. So the complete weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}
provides much more detailed information than the weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}
if
q>2
.

For any
\mathbf{t}\in\Sigma^{n,q}
, let

B_{\mathbf{t}}:=\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\bot}:\mathrm{Comp}(\mathbf{c})=\mathbf{t}\right\}.

The complete weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bot}
is defined similarly as

W_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\bot}}(z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{q-1})=\sum_{\mathbf{t}}B_{\mathbf{t}}\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{t}}.

Denote by
\psi:{\mathrm{GF}}(q)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
the standard additive character. The fundamental relation between the weight enumerators of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bot}
is given by MacWilliams identity
[
11

Let

Lemma 5
.

The value

is given by

\displaystyle b_{3}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}q&:&q\not\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
q+2&:&q\equiv 2\pmod{3}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We need to solve the equation
(y+1)^{d}-y^{d}-1=0
for
y\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2})
, where
d=3q-2
. Obviously
y=0,-1
satisfy this equation. If
y\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2})\setminus\{0,-1\}
, denoting

we have

0=(y+1)^{d}-y^{d}-1=\frac{(\bar{y}+1)^{3}}{(y+1)^{2}}-\frac{\bar{y}^{3}}{y^{2}}-1.

We can clear the denominators to have

\displaystyle y^{2}(\bar{y}+1)^{3}-(y+1)^{2}\bar{y}^{3}-y^{2}(y+1)^{2}=0.

Expanding the left hand side of the above equation and collecting terms, we can rewrite it as

\displaystyle(y-\bar{y})^{2}(y^{2}+2y\bar{y}+2y+\bar{y})=0.

(13)

Because
y=0,-1
also satisfy the equation (

), the quantity
b_{3}
is also the number of

of the equation (

). Now we can compute

easily.

If
y-\bar{y}=0
, this is equivalent to
y\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
, such

’s are solutions of (
13
) and the total number of such

’s is
q
.

Now suppose

. Then we have

\displaystyle y^{2}+2y\bar{y}+2y+\bar{y}=0.

(14)

Taking

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{2}+2y\bar{y}+2\bar{y}+y=0.

Adding the left hand sides of (

15

(y-\bar{y})(y+\bar{y}+1)=0.

Since

, we have

y+\bar{y}+1=0.

Plugging

back into the left hand side of (

\displaystyle y^{2}+y+1=0.

(16)

Now it is clear that if

, two distinct roots of the equation (

of the above equation are all in

, which shall be excluded when calculating

∎

V-A

Melas codes and Zetterberg codes

Let

be a prime and
q=p^{m}
for some positive integer

. Melas codes and Zetterberg codes can be defined over any finite field
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. However, for the case that is most interesting to us, we only consider such codes over the prime field

Denote by
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}:{\mathrm{GF}}(q)\to{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
the standard trace function. The unit circle of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2})
is defined by

U_{q+1}=\{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2}):x^{q+1}=x\bar{x}=1\}.

Here
\bar{x}:=x^{q}
for any

. The
p
-ary Zetterberg code
Z(q)
is a linear code of length

over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
defined as

\displaystyle Z(q):=\left\{(c_{x})_{x\in U_{q+1}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p)^{q+1}:\sum_{x\in U_{q+1}}c_{x}\cdot x=0\right\}.

By Delsarte’s theorem
[
11
]
, the dual of the Zetterberg code is

\displaystyle Z(q)^{\bot}:=\left\{c(a)=\left({\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{2}/p}(ax)\right)_{x\in U_{q+1}}:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2})\right\}.

The
p
-ary Melas code
M(q)
is a linear code of length
q-1
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
defined as

\displaystyle M(q):=\left\{(c_{x})_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(p)^{q-1}:\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}}c_{x}\cdot x&=&0\\
\sum_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}}c_{x}\cdot x^{-1}&=&0\end{array}\right\}.

By Delsarte’s theorem
[
11
]
again, the dual of the Melas code
M(q)
is

\displaystyle M(q)^{\bot}:=\left\{d(a,b)=\left({\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}(ax+bx^{-1})\right)_{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}}:a,b\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)\right\}.

For simplicity by ordering the elements of
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
as
u_{i}=i
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
, we can define the complete weight distribution of linear codes

and
M(q)^{\bot}
as describe in Section
II
. It turns out that they are closely related with each other:

Lemma 11
.

For
a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{2})^{*}
, let
b:=a\bar{a}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Comp}(c(a))=\frac{2q}{p}\cdot\mathbf{1}-\mathrm{Comp}(d(b,1)).

(21)

Here

is the vector with all entries being 1.

Proof.

For
0\leq i\leq p-1
, let

\displaystyle t_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{u\in U_{q+1}:{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{2}/p}(au)=u_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{u\in U_{q+1}:{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}(au+b(au)^{-1})=u_{i}\right\},

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}:{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}(bx+x^{-1})=u_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}:{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}(x+bx^{-1})=u_{i}\right\}.

\displaystyle W_{M(q)^{\bot}}({\mathbf{z}})=z_{0}^{q-1}+2(q-1)z_{0}^{-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot{\mathbf{1}}}+(q-1)\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathrm{Comp}(d(\beta^{j},1))}.

V-B

The pure weight distribution of Zetterberg codes

Recall that we have ordered elements of
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
as
u_{i}=i
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Denote by
W(z)
the function in variable
z
obtained by setting

in the complete weight enumerator
W_{Z(q)}({\mathbf{z}})
,
that is,

\displaystyle W(z)=W_{Z(q)}(1,0,\ldots,0,-z)=\sum_{t=0}^{q+1}(-1)^{t}B_{t}z^{t}.

We may call

as a “pure weight enumerator” of

because

\displaystyle B_{i}=\#\left\{{\mathbf{c}}\in Z(q):\mathrm{Comp}({\mathbf{c}})=(q+1-i,0,\ldots,0,i)\right\},

(28)

that is,
B_{i}
counts the number of “pure weight”

codewords of
Z(q)
(with respect to the symbol
p-1
). Then we have

Theorem 14
.

\displaystyle q^{2}W(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-z)^{q+1}-2(q+1)(1-z)\left(1-z^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}-\frac{q+1}{q-1}\left(1-z^{p}\right)^{\frac{2q}{p}}(1-z)^{1-q}

\displaystyle+\,\frac{q^{2}(q+1)}{q-1}\left(1-z^{p}\right)^{\frac{2q}{p}-q+1}W_{M(q)}(\mathbf{v}),

where
W_{M(q)}(\mathbf{v})
is the complete weight enumerator of
M(q)
and
\mathbf{v}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{p-1})
is given by

\displaystyle v_{i}=z^{i},\quad 0\leq i\leq p-1.

Proof.

We simply use
u_{i}=i
(
0\leq i\leq p-1
),
z_{0}=1,z_{1}=\cdots=z_{p-2}=0,z_{p-1}=-z
and simplify the right hand side of (
25
) in Theorem
13
. First, from (
26
) we have

x_{i}=1-\psi(-i)z,\quad y_{i}=\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}\frac{\psi(is)}{1-\psi(-s)z}\quad\mbox{ for }0\leq i\leq p-1.

We may expand
y_{i}
as

\displaystyle y_{i}=\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}\psi(is)\sum_{t\geq 0}\left(\psi(-s)z\right)^{t}=\sum_{t\geq 0}z^{t}\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}\psi(s(i-t)).

Since the subscript

runs over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
and
\psi:{\mathrm{GF}}(p)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the standard additive character, the inner sum is

unless

, thus we obtain

\displaystyle y_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\geq 0\\
t\equiv i\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}z^{t}\cdot p=p\cdot z^{i}\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{kp}=\frac{pv_{i}}{1-z^{p}},\quad 0\leq i\leq p-1.

Using the above relation between
y_{i}
and

W_{M(q)}({\mathbf{y}})=\left(\frac{p}{1-z^{p}}\right)^{q-1}W_{M(q)}(\mathbf{v}).

Finally, using
x_{0}=1-z
and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}x_{i}=\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\left(1-\psi(-i)z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\left(1-\zeta_{p}^{-i}z\right)=1-z^{p},

we can simplify the right hand side of Equation (
25
) to yield the desired result in Theorem
14
.
∎

VII
concluding remarks

In this paper we computed the cross-correlation distribution of the Niho-type decimation
d=3(p^{m}-1)+1
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(p^{2m})
for any prime
p\geq 5
where
\gcd(d,p^{2m}-1)=1
. The main difficulty of this problem for
p\geq 5
is to count the number of codewords of “pure weight” 5 in
p
-ary Zetterberg codes. We solve this problem by using the complete weight distribution of Zetterberg codes and MacWilliams identity to convert it into counting the number of codewords of small weight of various patterns in
p
-ary Melas codes, the most difficult of which is related to a K3 surface well studied in the literature and hence can be computed.

VIII-A

Codewords of weight at most two in Melas codes

Lemma 18
.

For any
p\geq 2
we have

(i)

,

(ii)

(iii)

(i) This is obvious. For example, the value

counts the number of weight

, the number of which is obviously

.

(ii) The value

counts the number of “pure weight” 2 codewords of

, or equivalently,

is half the number of solutions

(iii) The existence of the value

9
. Define

\displaystyle Y:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\{(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\left({\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\right)^{3}:\sum_{i}y_{i}+2=0,\sum_{i}y_{i}^{-1}+2=0\bigg\},

\displaystyle\triangle:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\neq j}Y\cap\{y_{i}=y_{j}\},\quad Y^{\prime}=Y\setminus\triangle,

\displaystyle H:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j}Y^{\prime}\cap\{y_{j}=1\}.

Thus

Y^{\prime\prime}=Y^{\prime}\setminus H.

We will compute
\#Y,\#\triangle
and
\#H
respectively to obtain

\#Y^{\prime\prime}=\#Y-\#\triangle-\#H.

We first compute
\#Y
, that is, we consider
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\left({\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\right)^{3}
such that

\displaystyle y_{1}+y_{2}=-2-y_{3},\quad y_{1}^{-1}+y_{2}^{-1}=-2-y_{3}^{-1}.

(43)

If
y_{1}+y_{2}=0
, then
y_{1}^{-1}+y_{2}^{-1}=\frac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{y_{1}y_{2}}=0
, hence
-2-y_{3}=-2-y_{3}^{-1}=0
, that is,
y_{3}=-2=-\frac{1}{2}
, which is impossible since
p\geq 5
. So
y_{1}+y_{2}\neq 0
and
y_{1}^{-1}+y_{2}^{-1}\neq 0
, so we have
y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\setminus\{-2,-\frac{1}{2}\}
.
Equation (
43
) implies that

y_{1}+y_{2}=-y_{3}-2,\quad y_{1}y_{2}=\frac{y_{3}+2}{y^{-1}_{3}+2}.

So
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}
are the two roots of the quadratic equation

t^{2}+(y_{3}+2)t+\frac{y_{3}+2}{y^{-1}_{3}+2}=0.

(44)

Since
\frac{y_{3}+2}{y^{-1}_{3}+2}\neq 0
as
y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\setminus\{-2,-\frac{1}{2}\}
,
t=0
is not a root of Equation (
44
). The discriminant is

D=(y_{3}+2)^{2}-\frac{4(y_{3}+2)}{y^{-1}_{3}+2}=\frac{\left(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+5\right)\cdot\left(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+1\right)}{(y_{3}^{-1}+2)^{2}}.

So for a given
y_{3}
, the number of
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}
satisfying (
43
), which is the number of roots of Equation (
44
) in
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}
, is given by
1+\eta(D)
, so we have

\displaystyle\#Y

\displaystyle=\sum_{y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\setminus\{-2,-\frac{1}{2}\}}\left(1+\eta(D)\right)

\displaystyle=q-3+\sum_{y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\setminus\{-2,-\frac{1}{2}\}}\eta\left(\big(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+5\big)\cdot\big(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+1\big)\right)

\displaystyle=q-3+\sum_{y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}}\eta\left(\big(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+5\big)\cdot\big(2(y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1})+1\big)\right).

Let
y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1}=a
, that is,
y_{3}^{2}-ay_{3}+1=0
. Easy to see that for any given
a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
, the number of
y_{3}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
satisfying the relation
y_{3}+y_{3}^{-1}=a
is given by
1+\eta(a^{2}-4)
, so we have

\displaystyle\#Y

\displaystyle=q-3+\sum_{a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)}\left(1+\eta(a^{2}-4)\right)\cdot\eta\left((2a+5)(2a+1)\right)

\displaystyle=q-3+\sum_{a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)}\eta\left((2a+5)(2a+1)\right)+\sum_{a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)}\eta\left((a^{2}-4)(2a+5)(2a+1)\right).

The second character sum is
-\eta(4)=-1
by Lemma
2
, and the third character sum is
\lambda_{p^{m}}
, after substituting
2a
by
a
. Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\#Y=q-4+\lambda_{p^{m}}.

As for
\triangle
, denoting

\triangle_{i,j}=Y\cap\{y_{i}=y_{j}\},\quad i<j.

Very similar to the proof of Lemma
9
, we can obtain

\#\triangle_{i,j}=1+\eta(-15),\quad\forall i<j,

\#\left(\triangle_{i,j}\cap\triangle_{i,k}\right)=\#\left\{y\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}:3y+2=0,3y^{-1}+2=0\right\}=\delta_{p,5},\quad i,j,k~\mbox{distinct}.

Noting

\triangle=\triangle_{1,2}\cup\triangle_{1,3}\cup\triangle_{2,3},

by a simple application of the inclusion-exclusion principle, we can obtain

\displaystyle\#\triangle=3+3\eta(-15)-2\delta_{p,5}.

Finally for
H
, using similar argument in the proof of Lemma
9
, by considering

H_{i}=Y^{\prime}\cap\{y_{i}=1\},\quad\forall i

for
i=1,2,3
individually and by a simple application of the inclusion-exclusion principle again, we can obtain

\displaystyle\#H=3\left(1+\eta(5)-\delta_{p,5}\right).

We conclude that

\displaystyle\#Y^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#Y-\#\triangle-\#H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q-10-3\eta(5)-3\eta(-15)+5\delta_{p,5}+\lambda_{p^{m}}.

Noting (
41
), we completes the proof of Lemma
21
.
∎

VIII-D

Codewords of “pure weight” 5 in the Melas codes and a K3 surface

Denote by
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
the number of solution
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\in({\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*})^{5}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}&=&0,\\
\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}}+\frac{1}{x_{4}}+\frac{1}{x_{5}}&=&0,\end{array}\right.\quad x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\emph{\mbox{ distinct}}.

The quantity
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
is closely related to the number of codewords of “pure weight” 5 in the Melas codes. It turns out
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
can be computed as a consequence of the main results in
[
13
]
for any
p
, though in the paper only the case
p=2
was treated explicitly (see
[
13
, Section 1. Melas codes]
). Here we follow the presentation of
[
13
]
closely and give a detailed account of how to compute
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
for any
p
. Interested readers may review
[
10
,
13
]
for other aspects of the deep theories of arithmetic geometry and number theory that were involved in the computation.

Let

\displaystyle X:=\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{4}:\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}=0\\
x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{4}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}=0\end{array}\right\}.

X
is a surface. It was known that the ten points which form the
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
-orbit of
(1,-1,0,0,0)
are all singular on
X
; in fact they are ordinary double points which become smooth after one blow up. The resulting surface
\tilde{X}
is smooth, that is, we let

\tilde{X}:=\mbox{the blow up of }X\mbox{ in the }\mathfrak{S}_{5}\mbox{-orbit of }(1,-1,0,0,0).

This surface is considered to be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is non-singular and reduces to a non-singular surface
\tilde{X}(p)
whenever
p\neq 3,5
.

Let
q=p^{m}
. We denote by
N_{q}(Y)
the number of points of a variety
Y
with all coordinates in
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. Since every singular point is defined over the ground field
{\mathrm{GF}}(p)
and is replaced by a projective line upon blowing up, we derive

\displaystyle N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)=N_{q}\left(X\right)+10q.

(47)

It turns out that the K3-surface
\tilde{X}
has maximal Picard number whose Hasse zeta function over
\mathbb{{Q}}
can be explicitly computed, from which the quantity
N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)
can be derived easily. The result of
N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)
can be summarized below (see (0.1) Theorem, (4.12) Proposition and (4.12) Proposition (iii) in
[
13
]
):

Theorem 22
.

Let
q=p^{m}
where
p
is a prime. Then

\displaystyle N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)=1+q^{2}+q\left(16+4\left(\frac{q}{3}\right)\right)+A_{q},

where
(\frac{\cdot}{\cdot})
is the Jacobi symbol and

(1)

If
p=3
, then
A_{q}=(-1)^{m}\cdot q
;

(2)

If
p=5
, then
A_{q}=q
;

If
p\neq 3,5
then

A_{q}=\beta_{1}^{m}+\beta_{2}^{m},

where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are reciprocal of the two complex roots of the polynomial

\displaystyle Q_{2}(t)=1-A_{p}t+\left(\frac{p}{15}\right)p^{2}t^{2},

and the quantity
A_{p}
is the coefficient of
z^{p}
in the
z
-expansion of

\left(\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{{Z}}}z^{m^{2}+mn+4n^{2}}\right)\cdot z\cdot\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}(1-z^{r})(1-z^{3r})(1-z^{5r})(1-z^{15r}).

Or in this case
A_{p}
can be computed more precisely as follows:

(3)

If
p\equiv 7,11,13,14\pmod{15}~\Longleftrightarrow~\left(\frac{p}{15}\right)=-1
, then
A_{p}=0
, so
\beta_{1,2}=\pm p
and

A_{q}=q\left(1+(-1)^{m}\right).

(4)

If
p\equiv 1,4\pmod{15}
, let
a,b
be integers satisfying
a^{2}+ab+4b^{2}=p
, then
A_{p}=2a^{2}-7b^{2}+2ab
.

(5)

If
p\equiv 2,8\pmod{15}
, let
a,b
be integers satisfying
2a^{2}+ab+2b^{2}=p
, then
A_{p}=a^{2}+8ab+b^{2}
.

Here we compute
A_{q}
for several small
p
.

Example 8
.

When
p=7,11,13
, then
A_{q}=q\left(1+(-1)^{m}\right)
.

Example 9
.

When
p=17
, since
p\equiv 2\pmod{15}
and since
17=2a^{2}+ab+2b^{2}
for
a=-1,b=3
, we have
A_{17}=a^{2}+8ab+b^{2}=-14
, the reciprocal of the roots of
Q_{2}(t)=1+14t+17^{2}t^{2}
are

\beta_{1,2}=-7\pm 4\sqrt{-15},

so

A_{q}=\left(-7+4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}+\left(-7-4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}.

Example 10
.

When
p=19
, since
p\equiv 4\pmod{15}
and since
19=a^{2}+ab+4b^{2}
for
a=1,b=2
, we have
A_{17}=2a^{2}-7b^{2}+2ab=-22
, the reciprocal of the roots of
Q_{2}(t)=1+22t+19^{2}t^{2}
are

\beta_{1,2}=-11\pm 4\sqrt{-15},

so

A_{q}=\left(-11+4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}+\left(-11-4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}.

Example 11
.

When
p=23
, since
p\equiv 8\pmod{15}
and since
23=2a^{2}+ab+2b^{2}
for
a=1,b=3
, we have
A_{23}=a^{2}+8ab+b^{2}=34
, the reciprocal of the roots of
Q_{2}(t)=1-34t+23^{2}t^{2}
are

\beta_{1,2}=17\pm 4\sqrt{-15},

so

A_{q}=\left(17+4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}+\left(17-4\sqrt{-15}\right)^{m}.

Armed with Theorem
22
, we can compute
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
for any prime
p
. First, let

\displaystyle H:=\bigcup_{j}X\cap\left\{x_{j}=0\right\},\quad X^{\prime}=X\setminus H.

(48)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle N_{q}(H)=10q-10,

(49)

accounting for the ten points which form the
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
-orbit of
(1,-1,0,0,0)
, and the
10(q-2)
points which form the
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
-orbit of
(x,-x-1,1,0,0)
for any
x\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)\setminus\{0,-1\}
.

Next define

\displaystyle\triangle:=\bigcup_{i\neq j}X^{\prime}\cap\{x_{i}=x_{j}\},\quad X^{\prime\prime}:=X^{\prime}\setminus\triangle.

(50)

Clearly by (
47
) and (
48
)–(
50
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}}{q-1}=N_{q}\left(X^{\prime\prime}\right)=N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)-N_{q}(\triangle)-20q+10.

(51)

The quantity
N_{q}\left(\triangle\right)
can be also treated easily: partitioning the projective variety
\triangle
into disjoint subsets according to the “pattern” of indices
i,j
such that
x_{i}=x_{j}
(for example, the pattern
\mathbf{2^{1}3^{1}}
means that among the variables
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{5}
, exactly two variables are equal, the other three are equal, and these two are distinct, one example is
x_{1}=x_{2},x_{3}=x_{4}=x_{5}
and
x_{1}\neq x_{3}
), we have

\displaystyle N_{q}\left(\triangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\left\{N_{\mathbf{5^{1}}}+\binom{5}{1}N_{\mathbf{1^{1}4^{1}}}+\binom{5}{2}N_{\mathbf{2^{1}3^{1}}}+\binom{5}{3}N_{\mathbf{1^{2}3^{1}}}+\binom{5}{2}N_{\mathbf{1^{3}2^{1}}}+\binom{5}{1}\cdot 3N_{\mathbf{1^{1}2^{2}}}\right\}.

Here the quantities such as
N_{\mathbf{5^{1}}},N_{\mathbf{1^{1}4^{1}}},N_{\mathbf{1^{2}3^{1}}}
etc are all given in Lemmas
18
-
21
. Now returning to (
51
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}}{q-1}=N_{q}\left(\tilde{X}\right)-20q+10-\delta_{p,5}-5\delta_{p,3}-\frac{10N_{\mathbf{1^{2}3^{1}}}}{q-1}-\frac{10N_{\mathbf{1^{3}2^{1}}}}{q-1}-\frac{15N_{\mathbf{1^{1}2^{2}}}}{q-1},

(52)

Finally employing Theorem
22
and Lemmas
18
-
21
, we can summarize the result of
N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
and
A_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}
below.

Lemma 23
.

For any prime
p
, we have

\frac{N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}}{q-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{2}+q\left(-14+(-1)^{m}\right)+36&\mbox{ if }p=3\\
q^{2}+q\left(-13+4(-1)^{m}\right)+10\left(7+(-1)^{m}\right)&\mbox{ if }p=5\\
q^{2}+q\left(-14+4\left(\frac{q}{3}\right)\right)+86+20\left(\frac{5}{q}\right)+15\left(\frac{-15}{q}\right)-10\lambda_{q}+A_{q}&\mbox{ if }p\geq 7\end{array}\right.

A_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}=\frac{1}{5!}N_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}.

Here
\lambda_{q}
is given by Theorem
3
and
A_{q}
is given by Theorem
22
.

Now we can prove Lemma
16
.

Proof of Lemma
16

Since
\Gamma_{2}=A_{\mathbf{1^{2}}}+A_{\mathbf{2^{1}}}
, quoting Lemma
18
, we obtain
\Gamma_{2}=\frac{q-1}{2}
. On the other hand,

\Gamma_{5}:=A_{\mathbf{5^{1}}}+A_{\mathbf{1^{1}4^{1}}}+A_{\mathbf{2^{1}3^{1}}}+A_{\mathbf{1^{2}3^{1}}}+A_{\mathbf{1^{3}2^{1}}}+A_{\mathbf{1^{1}2^{2}}}+A_{\mathbf{1^{5}}}.

Quoting Lemmas
18
-
21
together with Lemma
23
, we can obtain the value of
\Gamma_{5}
as described in (
16
). This completes the proof of Lemma
16
.
\hfill\square

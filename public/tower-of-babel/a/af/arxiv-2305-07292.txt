Abstract
We consider the problem of authenticated communication over a discrete arbitrarily varying channel where the legitimate parties are unaware of whether or not an adversary is present. When there is no adversary, the channel state always takes a default value ∅ \emptyset . When the adversary is present, they may choose the channel state sequence based on a non-causal noisy view of the transmitted codewords and the encoding and decoding scheme. We require that the decoder output the correct message with a high probability when there is no adversary, and either output the correct message or reject the transmission when the adversary is present. Further, we allow the transmitter to employ private randomness during encoding that is known neither to the receiver nor the adversary. Our first result proves a dichotomy property for the capacity for this problem – the capacity either equals zero or it equals the non-adversarial capacity of the channel. Next, we give a sufficient condition for the capacity for this problem to be positive even when the non-adversarial channel to the receiver is stochastically degraded with respect to the channel to the adversary. Our proofs rely on a connection to a standalone authentication problem, where the goal is to accept or reject a candidate message that is already available to the decoder. Finally, we give examples and compare our sufficient condition with other related conditions known in the literature.

Introduction

Consider the problem of communication over a channel where an adversary may or may not be present. Neither the transmitter nor the receiver know
a priori
whether or not the adversary is present. The goal for the transmission is to decode the message with an authentication guarantee. In particular, when the adversary is not present, it is desirable that the message is decoded correctly with a high probability. On the other hand, when the adversary is present, the decoding goal is relaxed – the decoder may either output the correct message, or it may declare that the adversary is present.

Model and Notation

Notation

We follow standard notation for information theoretic quantities (

[

Channel

We consider
myopic arbitrarily varying channels (AVCs)
that consist of pairs of channels
({{W}}_{Y|X,S},{U}_{Z|X})
. The transmitter and the receiver are connected through the
main channel

{{W}}_{Y|X,S}

\mathscr{X}

with input alphabet

and output alphabet

are independent,
i.e.
, the Markov chain
Y-(X,S)-Z
holds. All channel alphabets and the state set are finite sets.

Codes

We consider stochastic codes for two problems in this paper. The first problem we consider is that of communicating a message in an authenticated manner over the channel
({{W}}_{Y|X,S},{U}_{Z|X})
using

(

Definition 2

The adversary (if present) first independently and non-causally observes

Adversary Present

III
Equivalence between Authentication Code and Authentication Tag capacities

In this section, we prove Theorem
1

\displaystyle=\max_{m,\hat{m}\neq m}\sup_{\hat{Q}_{S^{t_{n}}|Z^{t_{n}}}}\Pr_{X^{t_{n}},Y^{t_{n}},Z^{t_{n}},S^{t_{n}}}(\phi_{\hat{m}}=\mathsf{ACCEPT}|m\mbox{ sent})\leq\epsilon/2.\qed

(20)

Remark 2
.

The proof of Lemma
2
suggests a natural two-phase architecture for authentication. The the first phase may be thought of as the “payload” which can be transmitted using any reliable code for the channel
\mathsf{W}^{(\emptyset)}
without adversary. The second phase is a short authentication phase. Our analysis shows that the length of the second phase need be no larger than logarithmic in the overall block length to achieve vanishing probability of error at rates achieving the capacity. This aspect of communication with authentication has been noted in different adversarial models in prior work
[
15
]
.

Remark 3
.

In the proof of Lemma
2
, the no-adversary case proceeds identically to the setting with an oblivious adversary
[
15
]
. However, unlike in the oblivious setting, when adversary is present, they can correlate the attack strategy in the first and the second phases. Thus, it is not
a priori
clear if the authentication tag phase can be analyzed separately from the communication phase. Our proof shows that even though the adversary may choose a strategy that depends on the entire transmission
Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}
, such a strategy is not more powerful than strategies
\hat{Q}_{S^{t_{n}}|Z^{t_{n}}}
that depend only on the authentication tag phase and the knowledge of the message and the first phase reconstruction.

\displaystyle\min_{Q_{S|Z}}\mathbb{V}\left(\sum_{x,z,s}P_{X}(x){U}_{Z|X}(z|x)Q_{S|Z}(s|z){{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x,s),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right).

(48)

Lemma 5
.

Let
(X^{n},Y^{n},Z^{n},S^{n})
be drawn from
\prod_{i}P_{X}(\cdot)\prod_{i}{U}_{Z|X}(\cdot|\cdot)Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}\prod_{i}{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|\cdot,\cdot)
. Then, there exists
\gamma>0
, such that for
n
large enough and for all
Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}
,

\Pr(\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\kappa(P_{X},x^{\prime})/2)<2^{-n\gamma}.

Notation:
In the following, we borrow notation from
[
20
, Chapter 1]
. We let
\mathscr{P}^{(n)}=\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})
be the set of all types on
\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S}
. For a joint type
P\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}
, let
\mathsf{T}_{P}
denotes the corresponding type class,
i.e.
, all sequences whose empirical distribution is
P
. Similarly, we define the set of types and type classes for all other combinations random variables.

Suppose that
(z^{n},s^{n})\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}
for some
Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})
. Let
V_{Y|ZS}
be the conditional probability of
Y
given
(Z,S)
when
X
is drawn as per
P_{X}
and
S
is drawn as per
Q_{S|Z}
,
i.e.
,

V_{Y|ZS}(y|zs)=\frac{\sum_{x}P_{X}(x){U}_{Z|X}(z|x)Q_{S|Z}(s|z){{W}}_{Y|X,S}(y|x,s)}{\sum_{x}P_{X}(x){U}_{Z|X}(z|x)Q_{S|Z}(s|z)}.

Let
Q_{YZS}=V_{Y|ZS}Q_{ZS}
and let
Q_{Y}(=Q_{Y}^{(z^{n},s^{n})})
be the marginal distribution induced by
Q_{YZS}
on
\mathscr{Y}
. Note that this distribution is completely determined by the joint type of
(z^{n},s^{n})
, the given channel conditional probabilities, and the given input distribution.

Proof overview:
The proof of this lemma proceeds by first showing in (
52
) that, for any
(z^{n},s^{n})
, the joint type of
(Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n})
is close to
Q_{YZS}=V_{Y|ZS}Q_{SZ}
, with high probability over the generation of
Y^{n}
. Next, we show that, with a high probability over
Z^{n}
, the joint type of
(Y^{n},Z^{n},S^{n})
is close to the single letter distribution
V_{Y|ZS}P_{Z}Q_{S|Z}
, where
P_{Z}
is the probability distribution of the random variable
Z
when
X
is drawn from
P_{X}
and
Z
is the output of the channel
{U}_{Z|X}
. This allows us to bound the probability that the variational distance in the lemma statement exceeds
\kappa(P_{X},x^{\prime})/2
by a similar expression in terms of the type
Q_{Y}
of
Y
(Eq. (
67
)). Finally, we note that
Q_{Y}
approximately satisfies the form required in the definition (
48
) to conclude that the lemma statement holds.

Proof details:
Let
\kappa=\kappa(P_{X},x^{\prime})
. Let
(z^{n},s^{n})\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}
for some
Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})
and let
Y^{n}\sim\prod_{i}V_{Y|Z,S}(\cdot|z_{i},s_{i})
. Following
[
20
, Lemma 2.6]
, we first note that, for all
Q^{\prime}_{YZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})
such that
Q^{\prime}_{ZS}=Q_{ZS}
,

\Pr((Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n})\in\mathsf{T}_{Q^{\prime}_{YZS}})\leq 2^{-nD(P^{\prime}_{Y|ZS}||V_{Y|ZS})|P_{ZS})}.

Thus, for the given
(z^{n},s^{n})
, and
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\Pr(D({\hat{P}}_{Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n}}||V_{Y|ZS}Q_{ZS})>\eta|z^{n},s^{n})

(49)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q^{\prime}_{YZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})\\
s.t.\ Q^{\prime}_{ZS}=Q_{ZS}\\
D(Q^{\prime}_{YZS}||V_{Y|ZS}Q_{ZS})>\eta\end{subarray}}\Pr((Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n})\in\mathsf{T}_{P^{\prime}_{YZS}})

(50)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q^{\prime}_{YZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}(\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Z}\times\mathscr{S})\\
s.t.\ Q^{\prime}_{ZS}=Q_{ZS}\\
D(Q^{\prime}_{YZS}||V_{Y|ZS}Q_{ZS})>\eta\end{subarray}}e^{-nD(Q^{\prime}_{Y|ZS}||V_{Y|ZS}|Q_{ZS})}

(51)

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)^{|\mathscr{Y}|}2^{-n\eta}<2^{-n\eta/2}.

(52)

Now, by Pinsker’s inequality, whenever
D(\hat{P}_{Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n}}||Q_{YZS})<\eta
, we have
\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n}},Q_{YZS}))<\sqrt{2\eta}
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},Q_{Y})

(53)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))

(54)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n},z^{n},s^{n}},Q_{YZS})

(55)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\sqrt{2\eta}.

(56)

Let
P_{Z}
be the distribution of each
Z_{i}
under the conditions of the lemma,
i.e.

P_{Z}(\cdot)=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{X}}P_{X}(x){U}_{Z|X}(\cdot|x)
. We have,

\displaystyle\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg)

(57)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z^{n},s^{n}}\bigg[\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg|z^{n},s^{n}\bigg)

(58)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{i}P_{Z}(z_{i})Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}(s^{n}|z^{n})\bigg]

(59)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}}\sum_{(z^{n},s^{n})\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}}\bigg[\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg|z^{n},s^{n}\bigg)

(60)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{i}P_{Z}(z_{i})Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}(s^{n}|z^{n})\bigg]

(61)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z^{n},s^{n})\\
\;\;\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}\end{subarray}}\bigg[\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\sqrt{2\eta}<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg|z^{n},s^{n}\bigg)

(62)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{i}P_{Z}(z_{i})Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}(s^{n}|z^{n})\bigg]+2^{-n\eta/2}

(63)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z^{n},s^{n})\\
\;\;\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}\end{subarray}}\bigg[\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\sqrt{2\eta}<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg|z^{n},s^{n}\bigg)

(64)

\displaystyle\qquad\times 2^{-nD(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})-nH(Q_{Z})}Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}(s^{n}|z^{n})\bigg]+2^{-n\eta/2}

(65)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{ZS}\in\mathscr{P}^{(n)}\\
D(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})<\xi\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z^{n},s^{n})\\
\;\;\in\mathsf{T}_{Q_{ZS}}\end{subarray}}\bigg[\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\sqrt{2\eta}<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg|z^{n},s^{n}\bigg)

(66)

\displaystyle\qquad\times 2^{-nD(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})-nH(Q_{Z})}Q_{S^{n}|Z^{n}}(s^{n}|z^{n})\bigg]+2^{-n\xi/2}+2^{-n\eta/2}.

(67)

In the above,
(a)
follows from (
56
).
(b)
follows from the method of types. To obtain
(c)
, we bound the probability contribution from all
z^{n}
in type classes with
D(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})>\xi
by
2^{-n\xi/2}
. Let
\tilde{Q}_{Y}
be the marginal distribution on
\mathscr{Y}
induced by the joint distribution
P_{Z}Q_{S|Z}V_{Y|ZS}
. Note that
\tilde{Q}_{Y}
is of the form

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{Y}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z,s}P_{Z}(z)Q_{S|Z}(s|z)V_{Y|ZS}(y|z,s)

(68)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,z,s}P_{X}(x){U}_{Z|X}(z|x)Q_{S|Z}(s|z)W_{Y|XS}(y|x,s).

(69)

Now, note that for all
Q_{S|Z}
,
D(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})<\xi
implies that
\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},\tilde{Q}_{Y})<\sqrt{2\xi}
, since

D(Q_{Y}||\tilde{Q}_{Y})\leq D(Q_{Z}Q_{S|Z}V_{Y|ZS}||P_{Z}Q_{S|Z}V_{Y|ZS})=D(Q_{Z}||P_{Z}),

and Pinsker’s inequality gives us
\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},\tilde{Q}_{Y})\leq\sqrt{2D(Q_{Y}||\tilde{Q}_{Y})}
. As a consequence, we have, for all
Q_{Y}
that obtained from a joint distribution
Q_{Z}Q_{S|Z}V_{Y|ZS}
with
D(Q_{Z}||P_{Z})<\xi
, we have,

\mathbb{V}(Q_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))\geq\mathbb{V}(\tilde{Q}_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))-\sqrt{2\xi}.

Applying this bound to (
67
) gives

\displaystyle\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(\hat{P}_{Y^{n}},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg)

(70)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\bigg(\mathbb{V}(\tilde{Q}_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))<\sqrt{2\xi}+\sqrt{2\eta}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg)+2^{-n\xi/2}+2^{-n\eta/2}.

(71)

Finally, comparing (
69
) with (
48
), we note that
\tilde{Q}_{Y}
satisfies
\mathbb{V}(\tilde{Q}_{Y},{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset))\geq\kappa
. Thus, as long as
\sqrt{2\xi}+\sqrt{2\mu}<\mu/2
, the conclusion of the lemma follows with
\gamma=\min\left\{\xi,\eta\right\}/2
.

\blacksquare

Lemma 6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
. Let
R<\min\left\{\beta^{2}\alpha^{2}/6,\beta^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)/4\right\}
. Then, there exists a family
\mathfrak{B}
of subsets of
[t]
satisfying:

1.

\alpha(1-\beta)n\leq|\mathscr{B}|\leq\alpha(1+\beta)n
for all
\mathscr{B}\in\mathfrak{B}
,

2.

|\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|<\alpha^{2}(1+\beta)n
for all
\mathscr{B},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}
,

3.

|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}|>\alpha(1-\alpha)(1+\beta)n
for all
\mathscr{B},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}
, and

4.

|\mathfrak{B}|\geq 2^{Rn}
.

5.

For every
y^{n}\in\mathscr{Y}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)

(72)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}{1+\beta}\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)

(73)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\frac{\alpha(1+\beta)}{(1-\beta)}.

(74)

Choose
\mathfrak{B}
by picking
N
subsets of
[t]
such that each
\mathscr{B}\in\mathfrak{B}
by independently including every element of
[t]
with probability
\alpha
each. Then, we have,

\displaystyle{\textsf{\bf E}}\left[|\mathscr{B}|\right]=\alpha n,

(75)

\displaystyle{\textsf{\bf E}}\left[|\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|\right]=\alpha^{2}n,\ \mathrm{and}

(76)

\displaystyle{\textsf{\bf E}}\left[|\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|\right]=\alpha(1-\alpha)n

(77)

for all
\mathscr{B},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}
. Next, we apply Chernoff bound to conclude that

\displaystyle\Pr(\left||\mathscr{B}|-\alpha n\right|>\alpha\beta n)\leq 2e^{-\beta^{2}\alpha n/3},

(78)

\displaystyle\Pr(|\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|>\alpha^{2}(1+\beta)n)\leq e^{-\beta^{2}\alpha^{2}n/3},\ \mathrm{and}

(79)

\displaystyle\Pr(|\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|<\alpha(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)n)\leq e^{-\beta^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)n/2}

(80)

for all
\mathscr{B},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}
. Taking a union bound over all
N
sets in the first inequality and
N^{2}
pairs of sets in the second and third, we see that this random choice of
\mathfrak{B}
satisfies Properties 1) to 3) with probability at least

1-Ne^{-\beta^{2}\alpha n/2}-N^{2}e^{-\beta^{2}\alpha^{2}n/3}-N^{2}e^{-\beta^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)n/2}
. Thus, as long as
N<e^{Rn}
, our procedure outputs a set
\mathfrak{B}
satisfying Properties 1) through 3) with high probability. Further, we may let
N=2^{Rn}
, thus also satisfying Property 4).

Next, given
\mathscr{B},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
such that
|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}|>0
, for every
y^{n}\in\mathscr{Y}^{n}
and
y\in\mathscr{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(y|\mathscr{B}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\{i\in\mathscr{B}^{\prime}:y_{i}=y\}}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}

(81)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\{i\in\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}:y_{i}=y\}|+|\{i\in\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}:y_{i}=y\}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}

(82)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(y|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B})+\frac{|\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(y|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}).

(83)

Thus, by the triangle inequality for variational distance, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)

(84)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)

(85)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{|\mathscr{B}\cap\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)

(86)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}\mathbb{V}\left(\hat{P}_{y^{n}}(\cdot|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{B}),{{W}}_{Y|X,S}(\cdot|x^{\prime},\emptyset)\right)-2\frac{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}|}{|\mathscr{B}^{\prime}|}.

(87)

Property 5) now follows from applying properties 1) to 3) to
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
.

\blacksquare

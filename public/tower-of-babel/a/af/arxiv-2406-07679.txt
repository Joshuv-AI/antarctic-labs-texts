Nerve Models of Subdivision Bifiltrations
Michael Lesnick and Ken McCabe

Abstract
. We study the size of Sheehy’s subdivision bifiltrations, up to homotopy. We focus in particular on the subdivision-Rips bifiltration 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ ( X ) \mathcal{SR}(X) of a metric space X X , the only density-sensitive bifiltration on metric spaces known to satisfy a strong robustness property. Given a simplicial filtration ℱ \mathcal{F} with a total of m m maximal simplices across all indices, we introduce a nerve-based simplicial model for its subdivision bifiltration 𝒮 ​ ℱ \mathcal{SF} whose k k -skeleton has size O ⁡ ( m k + 1 ) O(m^{k+1}) . We also show that the 0 0 -skeleton of any simplicial model of 𝒮 ​ ℱ \mathcal{SF} has size at least m m . We give several applications: For an arbitrary metric space X X , we introduce a 2 \sqrt{2} -approximation to 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ ( X ) \mathcal{SR}(X) , denoted 𝒥 ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{J}(X) , whose k k -skeleton has size O ⁡ ( | X | k + 2 ) O(|X|^{k+2}) . This improves on the previous best approximation bound of 3 \sqrt{3} , achieved by the degree-Rips bifiltration, which implies that 𝒥 ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{J}(X) is more robust than degree-Rips. Moreover, we show that the approximation factor of 2 \sqrt{2} is tight; in particular, there exists no exact model of 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ ( X ) \mathcal{SR}(X) with poly-size skeleta. On the other hand, we show that for X X in a fixed-dimensional Euclidean space with the ℓ p \ell_{p} -metric, there exists an exact model of 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ ( X ) \mathcal{SR}(X) with poly-size skeleta for p ∈ { 1 , ∞ } p\in\{1,\infty\} , as well as a ( 1 + ϵ ) (1+\epsilon) -approximation to 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ ( X ) \mathcal{SR}(X) with poly-size skeleta for any p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) and fixed ϵ > 0 {\epsilon>0} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Topological data analysis (TDA)
[
28
,
52
,
82
,
21
]
studies the shape of a data set by constructing a diagram of topological spaces and computing invariants of this diagram. In particular, persistent homology
[
39
,
67
,
46
]
, the mostly widely studied and applied TDA technique, begins by constructing a 1-parameter filtration of spaces, e.g., the
Čech filtration
of a Euclidean point cloud or the
(Vietoris-)Rips filtration
of a finite metric space
[
38
]
. Applying the homology functor with field coefficients to a filtration yields a diagram of vector spaces called a persistence module, whose isomorphism type is fully described by a multiset of intervals called a
barcode
.

The barcodes of Čech and Rips filtrations are well-known to be stable with respect to small perturbations of the data
[
31
,
29
,
27
]
. However, these barcodes can be highly unstable with respect to outliers, and also insensitive to variations in density
[
12
, Section
4
]
. Several strategies have been proposed to address these limitations within the framework of 1-parameter persistence; see
[
13
, Section 1.7]
. However, as noted in
[
13
]
, these strategies share a common disadvantage, which is that they depend on a choice of one or more parameters. It can be unclear how to best choose these parameters, and in some settings, it can be that no single choice allows us to fully capture the topological structure of interest in our data. This motivates us to analyze the topology of point cloud or metric data by constructing a
bifiltration
, treating density and spatial scale as separate parameters
[
22
]
.

The branch of TDA that studies data via the homology of multiparameter filtrations is known as
multiparameter persistent homology (MPH)

[
22
,
17
]
. Though there are difficulties with extending the notion of a barcode to the multiparameter setting, several simple, albeit incomplete, invariants of multiparameter persistence modules have been proposed and studied. These can be used as surrogates for a barcode in applications; see
[
17
, Sections 4 and 9]
for an overview.

The problem of working with 2-parameter persistent homology in a practical, computationally efficient way has been actively studied in recent years, leading to significant advances in algorithms and software
[
45
,
57
,
56
,
7
,
4
,
3
,
23
,
79
,
86
,
58
,
70
]
, as well as several interesting applications
[
80
,
34
,
8
,
30
,
1
,
85
,
58
]
. We expect such applications will become more common as multiparameter persistence software continues to improve and is more fully integrated into standard ecosystems for TDA and machine learning. However, the applicability of some of the prevailing 2-parameter approaches is limited by relatively high computational cost, and much current research in this area centers on limiting this cost.

1.2.
Density-Sensitive Bifiltrations

There are multiple ways of constructing a density-sensitive bifiltration from point cloud or metric data, which vary in their robustness to outliers, computability, and generality
[
12
]
. We now review several of these constructions, focusing on those that do not depend on an additional parameter choice; see
[
22
,
26
,
3
,
10
]
for other constructions.

We denote the Rips filtration of a finite metric space
X
as
\mathcal{R}(X)
. For
X
equipped with an embedding into an ambient metric space
Y
(e.g.,
Y=\mathbb{R}^{n}
), we denote the Čech filtration of
X
as
\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, suppressing the dependence on
Y
in the notation; see
Section

2.1.1
for the definitions.
Sheehy
[
74
]
introduced the
subdivision-Rips
and
subdivision-Čech bifiltrations

\mathcal{SR}(X)
and
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, which refine
\mathcal{R}(X)
and
\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, respectively. In fact, Sheehy’s construction provides a bifiltered refinement
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{F}
of any simplicial filtration
\mathcal{F}
. The definition of
\mathcal{SF}
amounts to the observation that there is a natural filtration on the barycentric subdivision of any simplicial complex. Unfortunately, the sizes of both
\mathcal{SR}(X)
and
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
are exponential in
|X|
, so in practice we cannot directly compute them.

Sheehy, Cavanna, and Gardners’
multicover nerve theorem

[
24
,
74
,
13
]
, a 2-parameter extension of the standard topological equivalence between the Čech and union-of-balls filtrations, motivates and illuminates the subdivision-Čech bifiltration. The theorem says that if finite intersections of balls are contractible in the ambient metric space
Y
, then
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homotopically equivalent (i.e.,
weakly equivalent
, see
Section

2.1
) to the
multicover bifiltration

\mathcal{M}(X)
. The bifiltration
\mathcal{M}(X)
is an extension of the union-of-balls filtration, where one filters both by radius and by
cover multiplicity
, i.e., the minimum number of balls covering a point.

In the case that
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is in general position, Corbet et al.
[
32
]
showed that the
rhomboid bifiltration

\mathcal{T}(X)
, a polyhedral bifiltration of size
\Theta(|X|^{d+1})
introduced by Edelsbrunner and Osang
[
41
]
, is homotopically equivalent to
\mathcal{M}(X)
, and hence also to
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. This improves on earlier results from
[
41
]
concerning 1-parameter slices of
\mathcal{M}(X)
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{T}(X)
can be computed in polynomial time
[
40
]
, and an implementation is available for the cases
d=2,3

[
63
]
. Even so, the size of
\mathcal{T}(X)
makes its full computation impractical for
d=3
.
Recent work by Buchet et al.
[
19
]
gives a linear-size approximation of the restriction of
\mathcal{M}(X)
to cover multiplicities at most some constant
\mu
. However, the size of this construction depends exponentially on
\mu
.

Lesnick and Wright
[
56
]
introduced another simple density-sensitive bifiltration on a finite metric space
X
, the
degree-Rips bifiltration

\mathcal{DR}(X)
, whose
k
-skeleton has size at most
O(|X|^{k+2})
. Restricting
\mathcal{DR}(X)
to a grid of constant size yields a bifiltration whose skeleta have size
O(|X|^{k+1})
, which asymptotically matches the size of the 1-parameter Rips filtration. This is small enough that
H_{0}(\mathcal{DR}(X))
and invariants thereof can readily be computed for real data sets with thousands of points. The software packages RIVET
[
77
]
and Persistable
[
73
]
support such computations.

Furthermore, Blumberg and Lesnick
[
13
]
showed that
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(-)
and
\mathcal{SR}(-)
both satisfy strong robustness properties, closely paralleling the standard stability theorems of single-parameter persistence.
In the case of
\mathcal{SR}(-)
, the result is the following:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
, Theorem 1.6 (iii)]
)
.

For any finite, non-empty metric spaces
X
and
X^{\prime}
, the homotopy interleaving distance between
\mathcal{SR}(X)
and
\mathcal{SR}(X^{\prime})
is at most the Gromov-Prohorov distance between the uniform probability measures on
X
and
X^{\prime}
.

In addition,
[
13
, Theorem
1.7
(ii)]
gives a robustness result for
\mathcal{DR}(-)
analogous to
Theorem

1.1
, formulated in terms of
affine homotopy interleavings
, where the shifts between indices in the interleaving are affine maps rather than translations. This a far weaker robustness property than that of
\mathcal{SR}(-)
, but is tight, in a sense.

Rolle and Scoccola
[
72
]
give a Lipschitz stability result for
\mathcal{DR}(-)
with respect to the
Gromov-Hausdorff-Prohorov distance
. However, since that distance is not robust to outliers, Rolle and Scoccola’s result does not speak to the robustness of
\mathcal{DR}(-)
.

1.3.
Contributions

Together, the above results reveal a tension between between robustness, computability, and generality for density-sensitive bifiltrations: The more general and more robust a bifiltration is, the larger and less computable it is, at least with current technology. Ideally, one would like a bifiltration that is robust (say, in the sense of
Theorem

1.1
), computable, and defined for arbitrary finite metric spaces. No such construction is known at present, and the problem of understanding what can and cannot be done in this direction is largely open.

In this paper, we approach this problem by studying the size (up to homotopy) of
\mathcal{SR}(-)
, the only density-sensitive bifiltration on metric spaces known to satisfy a strong robustness property. Specifically, we ask if and when there exists a simplicial construction weakly equivalent to
\mathcal{SR}(-)
with a
poly-size
(i.e., polynomial-size)
k
-skeleton for each fixed
k
. We consider both exact and approximate formulations of this question. For context, note that the multicover nerve theorem and the main result of
[
32
]
together imply that for data in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d
fixed, there exists a poly-size cellular bifiltration weakly equivalent to
\mathcal{S}\check{\mathcal{C}}(-)
, namely the rhomboid bifiltration
\mathcal{T}(-)
; our aim here is to develop analogous results for
\mathcal{SR}(-)
.

In what follows, we state our main results, deferring some key definitions to later sections.

Let
\mathbf{Top}
and
\mathbf{Simp}
denote the categories of topological spaces and finite simplicial complexes, respectively. We regard
\mathbf{Simp}
as a subcategory of
\mathbf{Top}
via geometric realization.

The next definition uses the notion of weak equivalence of
\mathbf{Top}
-valued functors, which we review in
Section

2.1.2
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a poset
P
, a
(simplicial) model
of a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon P\to\mathbf{Top}
is a functor
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\colon P\to\mathbf{Simp}
such that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are weakly equivalent.

Notation 1.3
.

Let
T
be a totally ordered set, e.g.,
T=[0,\infty)
. For
\mathcal{F}\colon T\to\mathbf{Simp}
a filtration and
k\geq 0
, let
m_{k}=m_{k}(\mathcal{F})
denote the number of sets
S
of simplices in
\colim\mathcal{F}=\bigcup_{t\in T}\mathcal{F}_{t}
such that

•

|S|\leq(k+1)
and

•

for some
t\in T
, each
\sigma\in S
is a maximal simplex (i.e., has no cofacet) in
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.

Note that
m_{0}
is simply the number of simplices in
\colim\mathcal{F}
that are maximal in some
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Therefore,
m_{k}=O(m_{0}^{k+1})
.

In
Section

3
, we construct a certain functor
\mathcal{NF}\colon\mathbb{N}^{\op}\times T\to\mathbf{Simp}
from a simplicial filtration
\mathcal{F}\colon T\to\mathbf{Simp}
, and prove the following, which is our main result about general subdivision bifiltrations:

Theorem 1.4
.

For any finitely presented filtration
\mathcal{F}\colon T\to\mathbf{Simp}
,

(i)

\mathcal{NF}
is a simplicial model of
\mathcal{SF}
whose
k
-skeleton has size
O(m_{k})
,

(ii)

the
0
-skeleton of any simplicial model of
\mathcal{SF}
has size at least
m_{0}
.

See
Section

2.3.1
for the definition a finitely presented filtration and
Definition

2.3
for the definition of size used here.

In brief, to construct
\mathcal{NF}
, we cover each simplicial complex
\mathcal{F}_{t}
of
\mathcal{F}
by its maximal simplices. These covers induce a
functorial cover
of
\mathcal{SF}
; we define
\mathcal{NF}
to be the nerve of this functorial cover. To show that
\mathcal{NF}
is a model of
\mathcal{SF}
, we use a functorial version of the nerve theorem
[
6
]
. See
Sections

3
and
2.6
for the details.

We give several applications of
Theorem

1.4
.
We use a notion of
\delta
-approximation
of bifiltrations, whose definition, given in
Section

2.5
, is formulated in terms of
(multiplicative) homotopy interleavings

[
11
]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

(i)

For any finite metric space
X
, there exists a simplicial
\sqrt{2}
-approximation
\mathcal{J}(X)
to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
whose
k
-skeleton has size
O(|X|^{k+2})
.

(ii)

There exists an infinite family of finite metric spaces
X
such that for any
\delta\in[1,\sqrt{2})
, there is no simplicial
\delta
-approximation to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
with size polynomial in
|X|
.

Remark 1.6
.

(i)

We define
\mathcal{J}(X)
as a linear rescaling of
\mathcal{NI}(X)
, where
\mathcal{I}(X)
is the
intrinsic Čech filtration
of
X

[
27
, Section 4.2.2]
,

(ii)

Hellmer and Spaliński
[
51
]
have independently introduced a bifiltration which is weakly equivalent to
\mathcal{NI}(X)
and has the same worst-case asymptotic size.

(iii)

It follows from
[
13
, Proposition
3.4
]
that a linear rescaling of
\mathcal{DR}(X)
is a
\sqrt{3}
-approximation of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
.
Corollary

1.5
thus gives an improved (optimal) approximation factor of
\sqrt{2}
, via a construction of the same asymptotic size. Using this approximation bound, arguments in
[
13
]
extend immediately to show that
\mathcal{J}(X)
satisfies the same robustness property as the one given for
\mathcal{DR}(X)
in
[
13
, Theorem
1.7
(ii)]
, but with an improved constant of 2 replacing the constant 3 in that result. Hence, as the constant 3 in the robustness result for
\mathcal{DR}(X)
is tight
[
13
, Proposition 3.7]
,
\mathcal{J}(X)
is more robust to outliers than
\mathcal{DR}(X)
.

When
\delta=1
,
Corollary

1.5
(ii) says that there exists no poly-sized model of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
for arbitrary finite metric spaces. In fact, this is true even for
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with the usual
\ell_{2}
metric:

Corollary 1.7
.

(i)

For
X
a finite set of uniformly spaced points on the unit circle, any simplicial model of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
has exponential size in
|X|
.

(ii)

Let
X
be a finite i.i.d. sample of the uniform distribution on the unit square. There exists a constant
C>0
such that
for any simplicial model
\mathcal{F}(X)
of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
,

\operatorname{Pr}[\textup{size of }\mathcal{F}(X)\textup{ is at least }\exp(C|X|^{1/3})]\rightarrow 1

as
|X|\rightarrow\infty
.

Informally,
Corollary

1.7
(ii) tells us that for random data sets
X\subset[0,1]^{2}
, the size of any model of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
is exponential in
|X|
with high probability. The proof uses a bound on the number of maximal cliques in random geometric graphs, due to Yamaji
[
87
]
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, let
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{p}
denote
d
-dimensional Euclidean space, endowed with the
\ell_{p}
metric.

Corollary 1.8
.

Fixing
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{p}
.

(i)

If
d=1
or
p\in\{1,\infty\}
, then
\mathcal{NR}(X)
is a simplicial model of
\mathcal{SR}(X)
whose
k
-skeleton has size
O(|X|^{\alpha(k+1)+2})
, where

\alpha=\alpha(d,p)=\begin{cases}d&\text{ for $p=\infty$ or $d=1$ or $(d,p)=(2,1)$},\\
d2^{d^{2}-1}&\text{ for $p=1$ and $d>2$}.\end{cases}

(ii)

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a
(1+\epsilon)
-approximation to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
whose
k
-skeleton has size
O(|X|^{\beta(k+1)+2}),
where

\beta=c_{d}\epsilon^{-(d^{2}-1)/2}
for some constant
c_{d}
depending
d
.

The main takeaway here is that for fixed
d
,
\epsilon
, and
k
, the
k
-skeleta considered in
Corollary

1.8
have size polynomial in
|X|
. However, the quantities
\alpha(d,1)
and
\beta
appearing in the exponents of the bounds are large and grow very quickly with
d
. It might be possible to improve these bounds.

We conjecture that
Corollary

1.8
(ii) extends to finite metric spaces of bounded doubling dimension
[
47
, Section
1.3
]
, as follows:

Conjecture 1.9
.

For
X
a finite metric space of bounded doubling dimension and fixed
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
(1+\epsilon)
-approximation to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
whose fixed-dimensional skeleta have size polynomial in
|X|
.

We remark that the functor
\mathcal{NF}
of
Theorem

1.4
(i), which underlies all of our main results, is generally not a bifiltration, but is rather a
semifiltration
, meaning that the structure maps in only one of the two parameter directions are required to be inclusions. In
Appendix

A
, we observe that by extending a construction of Kerber and Schreiber
[
53
]
in the 1-parameter setting, we can convert a simplicial semifiltration of bounded pointwise dimension to a bifiltration, with only logarithmic increase in size. One can use this construction to give variants of
Theorem

1.4
(i),
Corollary

1.5
(i), and
Corollary

1.8
where the functors are bifiltrations. For example, we have the following corollary of
Theorem

1.4
(i):

Corollary 1.10
.

For a finitely presented filtration
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbf{Simp}
and fixed
k\geq 0
, there exists a
bifiltered
simplicial model of the
k
-skeleton of
\mathcal{SF}
whose size is
O(m_{k}\log m_{0})
.

The fixed-dimensional skeleta of all of our constructions can be computed in time polynomial in the total size of the input and output. We briefly explain this in
Section

6
, deferring a more thorough study of computation to future work.

2.1.1.
Clique and Čech Filtrations

A

is a simplicial complex of dimension at most one. A
graph filtration
is a graph-valued filtration
\mathcal{G}\colon P\to\mathbf{Simp}
. Any such graph filtration induces a simplicial filtration

of

, where each
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}

of
\mathcal{G}_{p}
, i.e.,
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}
is the largest simplicial complex with 1-skeleton
\mathcal{G}_{p}
. For a key example, let
X
be a metric space with metric
d_{X}
and let

. The
r

\mathcal{G}(X)_{r}
is the graph with vertex set
X
and an edge
[x,y]
if and only
d_{X}(x,y)\leq 2r
. The graphs
\mathcal{G}(X)_{r}
assemble into a graph filtration
\mathcal{G}(X):[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{Simp}
. We call
\mathcal{R}(X)\coloneqq\overline{\mathcal{G}(X)}
the
(Vietoris)-Rips filtration of
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a collection of sets

, the
nerve of
U
is the simplicial complex

\Ner(U)\coloneqq\{S\subset U\mid S\textup{ is finite and }\bigcap_{\sigma\in S}\sigma\neq\emptyset\}.

Given a metric space

, a point

, and

, let

B(y)_{r}=\{z\in Y\mid d_{Y}(z,y)\leq r\}.

For a metric space
X
equipped with an embedding into a metric space

, let

\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)_{r}=\Ner(\{B(x)_{r}\mid x\in X\}),

where each

. Allowing

In TDA, one most often considers Čech filtrations where

. However, in this paper we will primarily consider the setting

, in which case we call
\check{\mathcal{C}}(X)

of

. The stability of

M

.

2.3.1.
Size of Simplicial Functors

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
for totally ordered sets
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
. We define the
size
of a finitely presented functor
\mathcal{F}\colon P\to\mathbf{Simp}
to be

(2.1)

\beta_{1}(C_{0}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\beta_{0}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}}).

Remark 2.4
.

We now give some context and motivation for
Definition

2.3
. Outside of the familiar case of 1-critical filtrations, we are aware of only two TDA papers that quantify the size of a
\mathbf{Simp}
-valued functor: First,
[
56
, Section
1.2
]
defines the size of a finitely presented bifiltration
\mathcal{F}
as

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{0}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}}),

which differs from
Eq.

2.1
by at most a factor of
2
. Second, for
T
a totally ordered set, the main results of
[
53
]
are formulated using several measures of the size of a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon T\to\mathbf{Simp}
; see
Appendix

A
.

Our motivation for
Definition

2.3
is the following: Assuming that the simplicial complexes of
\mathcal{F}
have bounded dimension and that storing an element of
\mathbb{K}
requires constant memory, (
2.1
) is an asymptotic upper bound on the amount of memory needed to store
\mathcal{F}
. To explain, the 0-skeleton of
\mathcal{F}
can be encoded up to isomorphism using

O(\beta_{0}(C_{0}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})+\beta_{1}(C_{0}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}}))

memory, e.g., as a minimal presentation of
C_{0}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}}
. Since the structure maps of
\mathcal{F}
are simplicial, they are determined by the 0-skeleton of
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore, to store the rest of
\mathcal{F}
, we need only to record all births of higher-dimensional simplices in the filtration, which requires

O\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{0}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})\right)

memory.

For practical computations of homology, it is natural to begin with explicit presentations of the chain modules. Thus, one might prefer to instead define the size of
\mathcal{F}
to be

(2.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{1}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{0}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}}).

However, we are primarily interested in the case where
\mathcal{F}
is a semifiltration; in this case,
Proposition

3.5
below gives that
\beta_{1}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})\leq\beta_{0}(C_{i}\mkern 2.0mu{\mathcal{F}})
for all
i\geq 0
, implying that the measures of size (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) differ by at most a factor of
2
.

2.4.
Subdivision Bifiltrations

A sequence of nested simplices

\sigma_{1}\subset\sigma_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\sigma_{m}

of an abstract simplicial complex
W
is called a
flag
of
W
. The set
{W}^{+}
of all flags of
W
is itself a simplicial complex, called the
barycentric subdivision
of
W
.

Definition 2.5
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots\}
, define
\mathcal{S}(W)_{k}
to be the subcomplex of
{W}^{+}
spanned by all flags whose minimum element has dimension at least
k-1
. Then in particular,
\mathcal{S}(W)_{1}={W}^{+}
. Varying
k
yields a filtration

\mathcal{S}(W)\colon\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Simp};

see
Figure

1
.

Figure 1
.
The subdivision filtration of a
2
-simplex
\Delta
.

For any poset
P
and filtration
{\mathcal{F}:P\to\mathbf{Simp}}
, the family of filtrations
(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{p}))_{p\in P}
assembles into a filtration

\mathcal{SF}\colon\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}\times P\to\mathbf{Simp},

which we call the
subdivision filtration of
\mathcal{F}

[
74
]
. In this paper, we are primarily interested in the case
P=[0,\infty)
. For
X
a metric space, we call
\mathcal{SR}(X)
and
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{I}(X)
the
subdivision-Rips
and
intrinsic subdivision-Čech bifiltrations of

, respectively.

Note that if
\mathcal{F}
is 1-critical, then so is
\mathcal{SF}
, and that if
\mathcal{F}
is finitely presented, then so is
\mathcal{SF}
.

2.5.
Homotopy Interleavings

A category is said to be
thin
if for any objects
x
and
y
, there is at most one morphism from
x
to
y
. For
\epsilon\geq 0
, let
I^{1+\epsilon}
be the thin category with object set
[0,\infty)\times\{0,1\}
and a morphism
(r,i)\to(s,j)
if and only if either

(i)

r(1+\epsilon)\leq s
, or

(ii)

i=j
and
r\leq s
.

We then have functors
E^{0},E^{1}:[0,\infty)\to I^{1+\epsilon}
mapping
r\in[0,\infty)
to
(r,0)
and
(r,1)
, respectively.

Definition 2.6
.

For any category
\mathbf{C}
and functors
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{C}
, a (multiplicative)
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaving between
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a functor

\mathcal{Z}:I^{1+\epsilon}\to\mathbf{C}

such that

\mathcal{Z}\circ E^{0}=\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}\circ E^{1}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
If such a
\mathcal{Z}
exists, we say
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaved
.

We now extend this definition to the
2
-parameter setting: For
\epsilon\geq 0
, let the
I^{(1,1+\epsilon)}
, be the thin category with object set
{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[0,\infty)\times\{0,1\}
and a morphism
(k,r,i)\to(l,s,j)
if and only if either

(i)

(k,r(1+\epsilon))\leq(l,s)
, or

(ii)

i=j
and
(k,r)\leq(l,s)
.

As above, we have functors
E^{0},E^{1}:{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[0,\infty)\to I^{(1,1+\epsilon)}
sending
(k,r)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[0,\infty)
to
(k,r,0)
and
(k,r,1)
, respectively.

Definition 2.7
.

For functors
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{C}
, a
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaving between
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a functor

\mathcal{Z}:I^{(1,1+\epsilon)}\to\mathbf{C}

such that
\mathcal{Z}\circ E^{0}=\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}\circ E^{1}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Definition

2.7
is essentially the same as the definition of 2-parameter interleaving used in
[
19
]
to formulate sparse approximation results for the multicover bifiltration. It is somewhat different than the definition of interleavings used in
[
13
]
to formulate
Theorem

1.1
; the definition of
[
13
]
also allows for shifts in the first coordinate, and the shifts in each coordinate are additive rather than multiplicative.

Definition 2.9

(
[
11
]
)
.

For functors
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{Top}
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are
(1+\epsilon)
-homotopy interleaved
if there exist
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaved functors
\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

If
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are
(1+\epsilon)
-homotopy interleaved, we say that
\mathcal{G}
is a
(1+\epsilon)
-approximation
to
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

For any
\epsilon\geq 0
, if filtrations
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{Simp}
are
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaved, then so are
\mathcal{SF}
and
\mathcal{SF^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

For
\epsilon=0
, the result holds because isomorphic filtrations have isomorphic subdivision filtrations. For the case
\epsilon>0
, consider a
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaving
\mathcal{Z}
between
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Note that since the structure maps of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are inclusions, the structure maps of
\mathcal{Z}
are monomorphisms (i.e., injections on vertex sets). Hence,
\mathcal{Z}
is naturally isomorphic to a filtration
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
, i.e., a functor whose structure maps are inclusions; concretely, we can take each
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}_{r}
to be the image of
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
under the cocone map
\mathcal{Z}_{r}\hookrightarrow\colim\mathcal{Z}
. The functor
\mathcal{SZ^{\prime}}
is a
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaving between filtrations isomorphic to
\mathcal{SF}
and
\mathcal{SF^{\prime}}
. It follows that
\mathcal{SF}
and
\mathcal{SF^{\prime}}
are themselves
(1+\epsilon)
-interleaved.
∎

2.6.
Functorial Nerves

Following
[
6
]
, we give a functorial version of the nerve theorem for simplicial covers. We begin with the requisite definitions.

For
X
a topological space, a
cover of
X
is a collection
U=(U_{a})_{a\in A}
of subspaces of
X
indexed by a set
A
such that
\bigcup_{a\in A}U_{a}=X
. We say
U
is
good
if the intersection of finitely many elements of
U
is always either empty or contractible.

Let
X
and
Y
be topological spaces,
U=(U_{a})_{a\in A}
a cover of
X
, and
V=(V_{b})_{b\in B}
a cover of
Y
. We define a
map of cover indices

\varphi\colon U\to V
to be a function
\varphi\colon A\to B
. We say that
\varphi

carries
a continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
if for all
a\in A
we have
f(U_{a})\subset V_{\varphi(b)}
.

Definition 2.11
.

The objects of the
category of covered spaces

\mathbf{Cov}
are pairs
(X,U)
where
X
is a topological space and
U
is a cover of
X
. A morphism
(f,\varphi):(X,U)\to(Y,V)
in
\mathbf{Cov}
consists of a continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
and a map of cover indices
\varphi\colon U\to V
such that
\varphi
carries
f
.

The nerve construction (
Definition

2.1
) induces a functor
\Ner\colon\mathbf{Cov}\to\mathbf{Simp}
. We also have a forgetful functor
\mathcal{Y}\colon\mathbf{Cov}\to\mathbf{Top}
that maps an object
(X,U)
to
X
.

Using these functors, we can extend the formalism of covers and nerves to diagrams of spaces, as follows:

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon P\to\mathbf{Top}
be a functor.

(i)

A
cover
of
\mathcal{F}
is a functor
\mathcal{U}\colon P\to\mathbf{Cov}
such that
\mathcal{Y}\circ\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

Writing
\mathcal{U}_{p}=(X_{p},U_{p})
, we say that
\mathcal{U}
is
good
if
U_{p}
is good for each
p\in P
.

(iii)

The
nerve of
\mathcal{U}
is the composite
\Ner{}\!\circ\mathcal{U}
, which we will denote as
\Ner_{\mathcal{U}}
.

The following is the version of functorial nerve theorem we will use in this paper. See
[
6
]
for a lucid treatment of variants and generalizations.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
)
.

If
\mathcal{U}
is a good simplicial cover of a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon P\to\mathbf{Simp}
, then
\Ner_{\mathcal{U}}\simeq\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 3.2

Lemma 3.3

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbf{Simp}
be a finitely presented semifiltration. For any
j\geq 0
, we have
\beta_{1}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})\leq\beta_{0}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})
, and
\beta_{i}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
for
i>1
.

Proof.

Since
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}
is finitely generated, Hilbert’s syzygy theorem implies that
\beta_{i}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
for
i>2

[
65
, Theorem 15.2]
. To see that
\beta_{2}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
, we use the well-known characterization of Betti numbers in terms of Koszul homology; see, e.g.,
[
42
, Proposition 2.7]
for the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded case. For a finitely generated persistence module
M\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbf{Vec}
, the Koszul homology formula for
2^{\mathrm{nd}}
Betti numbers implies that

\beta_{2}(M)=\sum_{y}\sum_{z}\Nullity(M_{y-1,z-1}\xrightarrow{f_{y,z}}M_{y,z-1}\oplus M_{y-1,z}),

where the map
f_{y,z}
is induced by the structure maps of
M
. Since all of the horizontal structure maps of
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}
are injections, this formula yields
\beta_{2}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
.

According to a standard inclusion-exclusion formula relating Betti numbers and the Hilbert function
[
65
, Theorem 16.2]
, for
y,z
sufficiently large we have

\dim(M_{y,z})=\beta_{0}(M)-\beta_{1}(M)+\beta_{2}(M).

Since
\dim M_{y,z}\geq 0
, this implies that
\beta_{0}(M)\geq\beta_{1}(M)-\beta_{2}(M)
. Since
\beta_{2}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
, we have
\beta_{1}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})\leq\beta_{0}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})
.
∎

To bound the size of
\mathcal{NF}
in

(i), we will use the

:

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
T
be a totally ordered set and let
\mathcal{A}\colon\mathbb{N}^{\op}\times T\to\mathbf{Simp}
be a finitely presented semifiltration. For any
j\geq 0
, we have
\beta_{1}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})\leq\beta_{0}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})
, and
\beta_{i}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=0
for
i>1
.

Proof.

Our argument uses ideas similar to those in
[
9
, Section 2.6]
, wherein the reader may find additional details. Define a
grid function
to be a poset map
g\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}^{\op}\times T
of the form
g(y,z)=(g_{1}(y),g_{2}(z))
, for maps
g_{1}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}^{\op}
and
g_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\to T
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is finitely presented,
\mathcal{A}
is the left Kan extension of a functor
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbf{Simp}
along a grid function.

Left Kan extension along
g
is functorial, i.e., defines a functor

\mathcal{E}_{g}(-)\colon\mathbf{C}^{\mathbb{N}^{2}}\to\mathbf{C}^{\mathbb{N}^{\op}\times T}

for any category
\mathbf{C}
. The functor
\mathcal{E}_{g}(-)
commutes with the construction of simplicial chain complexes, so that in particular, we have

C\mathcal{A}=C(\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}))=\mathcal{E}_{g}(C\mathcal{A}^{\prime}).

Moreover,
\mathcal{E}_{g}(-)
is exact when
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{Vec}

[
9
, Lemma 2.23]
. Thus, for any persistence module

M\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbf{Vec}
, the functor
\mathcal{E}_{g}(-)
maps a resolution of
M
to a resolution of
\mathcal{E}_{g}(M)
of the same size.
In particular, taking
M=C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, this implies that
\mathcal{E}_{g}(-)
maps a resolution of
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A^{\prime}}
to a resolution of
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}
of the same size. Conversely, the restriction functor along
g
is also exact, and so maps a resolution of
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}
to a resolution of
C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A^{\prime}}
of the same size. Therefore, for each
i,j\geq 0
we have
\beta_{i}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A})=\beta_{i}(C_{j}\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. This, together with
Proposition

3.5
, yields the result.
∎

Proof of

Proposition

tells us that

is a simplicial model of

. Recalling our definition of size of a finitely presented

Recall the definitions of

from the beginning of this subsection. For

For

3.2.
Zeroth Homology

The key to our proof of

Proposition 3.7
.

For any totally ordered set
T
and finitely presented filtration
\mathcal{F}:T\to\mathbf{Simp}
, we have
\beta_{0}(H_{0}(\mathcal{SF}))=m_{0}

We prepare for the proof of the proposition with a lemma. For a simplex

denote the vertex corresponding to

{\mathcal{F}_{t}}^{+}

Lemma 3.8
.

If

\hat{\sigma}
is an isolated component of
\mathcal{SF}_{\mathbf{b}(\hat{\sigma})}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{b}(\hat{\sigma})=(k,z)
. Note that
k=\dim\sigma+1
and that
z

\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{z}
. Seeking a contradiction, suppose that
\hat{\sigma}
belongs to some

[\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau}]\in\mathcal{SF}_{k,z}
. We have
\mathcal{SF}_{k,z}\subset\mathcal{F}^{+}_{z}
, so by the definition of barycentric subdivision, simplices in

correspond to flags in
\mathcal{F}_{z}
. Thus, either
\tau\subset\sigma
or vice-versa. If
\tau\subset\sigma
, then
\dim\tau<\dim\sigma=k-1
, implying that
\hat{\tau}\not\in\mathcal{SF}_{k,z}
, a contradiction. If
\sigma\subset\tau
, then
\sigma
is not maximal in
\mathcal{F}_{z}
, contradicting the assumption that
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.7
.

Let
\hat{\Sigma}=\{\hat{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\Sigma\}
. Identifying each
\hat{\sigma}\in\hat{\Sigma}
with its corresponding element in

or there exists a 1-simplex

. Suppose that

4.

We next prove
Corollary

1.5
, which concerns poly-size approximation of the subdivision-Rips bifiltration of an arbitrary finite metric space. Recall the statement:

Corollary
1.5
.

(i)

For any finite metric space

, there exists a simplicial
\sqrt{2}
-approximation
\mathcal{J}(X)
to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
whose

-skeleton has size
O(|X|^{k+2})
.

(ii)

There exists an infinite family of finite metric spaces

such that for any
\delta\in[1,\sqrt{2})
, there is no
\delta
-approximation to
\mathcal{SR}(X)
with size polynomial in

.

Define
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(X)\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{Simp}
by

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(X)_{r}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}(X)_{\sqrt{2}r},

where
\mathcal{I}(X)
is the intrinsic Čech filtration of

. In

1.5
(i), we take
\mathcal{J}(X)\coloneqq\mathcal{NI}^{\prime}(X)
.

For the next lemma, recall the definition of

from
Notation

1.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any finite metric space
X
and

,
m_{k}(\mathcal{I}(X))\leq|X|^{k+2}
.

Proof.

A simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}(X)_{r}
is maximal in
\mathcal{I}(X)_{r}
only if there is some point
x\in X
such that
\sigma=B(x)_{r}
, i.e.,
\sigma
is the closed ball of radius
r
in
X
centered at
x
.
We call
x
a
witness
of
\sigma
. For
S=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\}
a set of maximal simplices in
\mathcal{I}(X)_{r}
, we say that

\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}

witnesses

S
if each
x_{i}
witnesses
\sigma_{i}
. Call any set with
j

j

.

For each

, there are at most
|X|
balls in
X
centered at
x
. Thus, as

varies, each
(k+1)
-set in

witnesses a total of at most

{{|X|}\choose{k+1}}\leq\frac{|X|^{k+1}}{k+1},

it follows that
m_{k}(\mathcal{I}(X))\leq|X|^{k+2}
.
∎

Since the metric on

\mathcal{I}(X)_{r}\subset\mathcal{R}(X)_{r}\subset\mathcal{I}(X)_{2r},

Hence

Since

,

For

We conclude this section with two general remarks giving context for our use of cocktail party graphs.

Remark 4.3
.

Cocktail party graphs are examples of
Turán graphs
, which occur frequently in extremal graph theory
[
2
]
. In brief, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{1\leq r\leq n}
, the
Turán graph

T(n,r)
is the complete
r
-partite graph whose partite sets are as close to equal cardinality as possible. For example,
T(2n,n)
is the cocktail party graph
C_{n}
, while
T(n,1)
is the graph with
n
vertices and no edges, and
T(n,n)
is the complete graph
K_{n}
. The
n
-vertex graph with the largest possible number of maximal cliques is given by the Turán graph
T(n,\lceil n/3\rceil)

[
62
, Theorem
1
]
.

Remark 4.4
.

A class of graphs
\Omega
is said to have
few cliques
if the number of maximal cliques of any graph
G=(V,E)
in
\Omega
is polynomial in
|V|

[
66
,
71
]
.
In view of
Theorem

1.4
(i), to understand when the subdivision bifiltrations of a class of clique filtrations have poly-sized skeleta, one wants to identify conditions under which a class of graphs has few cliques. This is well studied. Graph classes known to have few cliques include trees, chordal graphs, planar graphs
[
84
, Corollary
2
]
, graphs with bounded degree
[
84
, Theorem 3]
or bounded degeneracy
[
43
, Theorem 3]
, and intersection graphs of convex polytopes with a bounded number of facets
[
18
, Theorem 15]
. This last class of graphs will be of particular relevance to us in
Section

5
.

We have seen above that the class of cocktail party graphs does not have few cliques. Moreover, it is shown in
[
44
]
that this observation has the following satisfying converse: Let
\Omega
be a class of graphs for which there exists a constant
n_{0}
such that for all
n>n_{0}
, no graph
G\in\Omega
has a vertex-induced subgraph isomorphic to
C_{n}
. Then
\Omega
has few cliques.

For the proof, we will need the following definition:

Definition 5.2

(
[
61
]
)
.

For a collection of non-empty sets
U
, the
intersection graph of
U
, denoted
\Gamma_{U}
, is the 1-skeleton of the nerve of
U
. That is,
\Gamma_{U}
is the graph with vertex set
U
and an edge
[A,B]
if and only if
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
,

, and

Lemma 5.3
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{p}
, we have
\mathcal{G}(X)_{r}=\Gamma_{U_{r}}
, where

U_{r}=\{B(x)_{r}^{p}\mid x\in X\}.

Proof.

Consider
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
a=\frac{1}{2}(x+y)
. Then since
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a norm,

\|x-a\|_{p}=\|y-a\|_{p}=\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{p}.

By the triangle inequality for the
\ell_{p}
-metric, there exists no
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\max(\|x-z\|_{p},\|y-a\|_{p})<\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{p}.

Thus, if
r\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{p}
, then
[x,y]
is contained in both
\mathcal{G}(X)_{r}
and
\Gamma_{U_{r}}
, while if

r>\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{p}
, then
[x,y]
is contained in neither
\mathcal{G}(X)_{r}
nor
\Gamma_{U_{r}}
.
∎

Our main tool for proving

If

The next result strengthens

5.4
(ii) in the special case that each polytope is a

of positive length. It was stated without proof in

For completeness, we include a proof here.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
76
,
55
]
)
.

If
G
is the intersection graph of a set

of boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the number of maximal cliques of

is at most
|V|^{d}
.

Proof.

We call

an

if each

is the minimum

coordinate of some box in

Boxes have the

(also known as the

-Helly property

are exactly the simplices in

[

may be chosen as an anchor. Thus, the number of maximal cliques of

∎

6.
Computation

We now briefly discuss computation. Specifically, we address the problem of computing the fixed-dimensional skeleta of the nerve-based semifiltrations appearing in the statements of our main theorems. We concern ourselves only with the modest goal of computing these skeleta in time polynomial in the total size of input and output (i.e.,
output-sensitive polynomial time
), leaving a more nuanced study of computation to future work.

Observe that for a finitely presented filtration
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbf{Simp}
, to compute the
0
-skeleton of
\mathcal{NF}
it suffices to compute the set
\Sigma_{t}
of maximal simplices of each
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Once we have done so, to compute the
k
-skeleton, it suffices to test all
j
-sets of each
\Sigma_{t}
for non-empty intersections, where
j\leq k+1
. For fixed
k
, this can be done naively in polynomial time.

When
\mathcal{F}
is the clique filtration of a graph filtration
\mathcal{G}
(e.g., a Rips filtration), we can compute the maximal simplices of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
in output-sensitive polynomial time using well-known algorithms for enumerating all maximal cliques in a graph
[
78
,
59
,
25
]
.

To compute the 0-skeleton of the semifiltration
\mathcal{J}(X)
of
Corollary

1.5

(ii) for a larger value of the constant

(ii).

Appendix A
Constructing a Bifiltration from a Semifiltration

The simplicial models in this paper are generally not bifiltrations, but rather semifiltrations. However, computation of 2-parameter persistent homology is simplest and most closely aligned with existing software
[
45
,
57

56

7

Extending work of Dey et al.
[

, Kerber and Schreiber

have given a nice solution to the 1-parameter version of this problem. Namely,

\mathcal{F}^{\,0}

.

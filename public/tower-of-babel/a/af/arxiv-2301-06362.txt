On the density of strongly minimal algebraic vector fields
Rémi Jaoui

Abstract
. Two theorems witnessing the abundance of geometrically trivial strongly minimal autonomous differential equations of arbitrary order are shown. The first one states that a generic algebraic vector field of degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 on the affine space of dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 is strongly minimal and geometrically trivial. The second one states that if X 0 X_{0} is the complement of a smooth hyperplane section H X H_{X} of a smooth projective variety X X of dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 then for d ≫ 0 d\gg 0 , the system of differential equations associated with a generic vector field on X 0 X_{0} with poles of order at most d d along H X H_{X} is also strongly minimal and geometrically trivial.

1.1.
The model-theoretic strategy

Given a (definable) family of algebraic (non linear) differential equations

E(\overline{\alpha}):F(y,y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime},\ldots,y^{(n)},\overline{\alpha})=0

depending on some complex parameters
\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the core of the model-theoretic strategy is to first establish a “
rough classification”
of the algebraic relations among solutions of the equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
. This rough classification — called the
semi-minimal analysis
of the family — is phrased in the language of geometric stability theory
[
33
]
and describes the structures of the solution sets of the equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
for fixed
\overline{\alpha}
as well as the (qualitative) behavior of the definable relations across several equations in the family.

Among all the possible semi-minimal analysis appearing in geometric stability theory, a key feature of (non linear) differential algebra is that — after discarding a “small subset” of parameters — one of them appears to have an
ubiquitous character
: for instance, all the classical families of equations discussed above share the same semi-minimal analysis outside of a small subset of parameters. This ubiquitous semi-minimal analysis can be summarized as follows:

(1)

strong minimality
: outside of an exceptional subset
\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of parameters formed by an (at most) countable union of proper Zariski-closed subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
are
strongly minimal
: for every solution
y
of
E(\overline{\alpha})
and every differential field
L
containing the parameters of the equation
E(\overline{\alpha})

\mathrm{trdeg}(L\langle y\rangle/L)=0\text{ or }n

where
n
denotes the (common) order of the equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
and
L\langle y\rangle
denotes the differential field generated by
L
and
y
.

(2)

geometric triviality:
for every
r\geq 2
, every
\overline{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\alpha_{r}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}
, every solution
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
of
E(\overline{\alpha_{1}}),\ldots,E(\overline{\alpha_{r}})
respectively and every differential field
L
containing the parameters of all the
E(\overline{\alpha_{i}})
,

\textit{ if }\mathrm{trdeg}(L\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\rangle/L)<r\cdot n\textit{ then}\text{ for some }i\neq j,\mathrm{trdeg}(L\langle y_{i},y_{j}\rangle/L)=0\text{ or }n.

(3)

multidimensionality:
outside of an exceptional set of pairs of parameters
\mathcal{E}_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, for every
(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})\notin\mathcal{E}_{2}
, the equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
and
E(\overline{\beta})
are
orthogonal
:

\mathrm{Corr}(E(\overline{\alpha}),E(\overline{\beta}))=\emptyset

where
\mathrm{Corr}(E(\overline{\alpha}),E(\overline{\beta}))
denotes the set of generically finite-to-finite correspondences between the solution sets of the differential equations
E(\overline{\alpha})
and
E(\overline{\beta})
.

The problem addressed in this article is to confirm the ubiquitous character of this semi-minimal analysis by showing that the families of algebraic differential equations satisfying the properties (1) and (2) are indeed abundant in the universe of
autonomous
algebraic differential equations of arbitrary order.

1.4.
Description of proof methods and organization of the paper

The proof of these results uses a combination of
model-theoretic techniques
concerning the structure of strongly minimal sets in the theory of differentially closed fields of characteristic zero and of
geometric techniques
describing the dynamical properties of a generic foliation on a smooth projective algebraic variety. While Section 2 is a preliminary section introducing the relevant vocabulary needed later on in the text, we quickly describe below the articulation between these techniques in Section 3 to Section 5.

1.5.
Open questions

We left several open questions about the structure of the family of strongly minimal equations that we have constructed. We describe some of them below.

Strong minimality and specialization

The first of these questions concerns the structure of the exceptional set of parameters. In all the classical examples of families of algebraic differential equations mentioned above

E(\overline{\alpha}):F(y,y^{\prime},\ldots,y^{(n)},\overline{\alpha})=0

the exceptional set of parameters where the equation fails to be strongly minimal is an at most countable union of proper Zariski-closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. On the other hand, the results of this article (Theorem A and Theorem B) only ensures that the exceptional set is
contained in an at most countable union of proper Zariski-closed subsets
. It seems reasonable to expect that the exceptional set is in fact always of the form observed for the classical families. This would follow from the following specialization (or generalization) result:

Question 1.5.1

(Under some reasonable smoothness and irreducibility assumptions)
.

Is it true that for a family of differential equations as above, if for some value of the parameter the differential equation is strongly minimal then for generic values of the parameter the equation is strongly minimal?

If one replaces strong minimality by orthogonality to the constants in the statement, the corresponding specialization result has been established in
[
24
]
. Furthermore, the results of
[
6
]
concerning the specialization of the Malgrange pseudogroup of a vector field strongly suggest that a positive answer to this question is expected.

Multidimensionality and trivial internal structure

As described in the first part of the introduction, all the classical families mentioned in the first part of the introduction also satisfy the property (3) of multidimensionality. Furthermore, while certain Schwarzian equations give rise to beautiful strongly minimal structures with trivial forking geometry but with a rich (non
\aleph_{0}
-categorical) internal structure, one expects that the opposite happens in the generic case considered in this article:

(4)

trivial internal structure
: for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
generic,

\mathrm{Corr}(E(\overline{\alpha}),E(\overline{\alpha}))=\{\Delta_{E(\overline{\alpha})}\}

where
\Delta_{E(\overline{\alpha})}
denotes the diagonal of the solution set of
E(\overline{\alpha})
.
A natural extension of the results of this article which seems outside of the scope of the methods presented in this article is to show that the properties (3) and (4) are also abundant for families of autonomous differential equations:

Question 1.5.2
.

Is it true that the families
\Xi(n,d)
for
n,d\geq 2
and
\Xi(X_{0},d)
for
d
large enough of Theorem A and Theorem B are multidimensional with trivial internal structure?

Abundance relatively to a foliation

The articulation between the notions of foliation and vector field plays a important role in the proofs of this article. As a byproduct of the results of this article, we showed that a “generic one-dimensional foliation” in the sense of
[
9
]
supports a vector field with a strongly minimal solution set. The Poizat equation (and related examples) also shows that certain foliations on surfaces with a Liouvillian (non-algebraic) first integral also support similar vector fields (see
[
13
, Section 4]
).

A natural question is therefore
which foliation support a vector field with a (strongly) minimal solution set?
In its simplest form (i.e. in dimension two), the only known obstruction is the existence of a nontrivial rational first integral:

Question 1.5.3
.

Is it true that every non algebraically-integrable foliation on a projective surface supports (on a Zariski open set) a strongly minimal algebraic vector field?

2.3.
Closed invariant subvarieties

If

is a closed invariant subvariety of

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightarrow 0

which after tensoring by an invertible sheaf

identifying
\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{L}
with the subsheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z}.\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{L}
with
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{I}_{Z}.\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{L},v)
be a twisted algebraic vector field. A closed subvariety

of

is called a

of

(we also say that

where

.

Obviously, this definition extends the (untwisted) notion of invariance of differential algebra. It also coincides with the notion of invariance of
[

.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{L},v)
be a twisted algebraic vector field. A closed subvariety

of

if and only if we have a factorization:

\textstyle{\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{i_{v}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}}
\textstyle{\Omega^{1}_{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}}

where

Let

associated with a sheaf of ideals

. By

The following properties of closed invariant subvarieties are classical and follow for the corresponding statements for (untwisted) algebraic vector fields.

2.4.
Singular points

The singular points are a particular kind of closed invariant subvarieties, namely they are the irreducible closed subvarieties of dimension zero.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{L},v)
be a twisted algebraic vector field and let
x\in X(k)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\{x\}
is a closed invariant subvariety of
(X,\mathcal{L},v)
,

(ii)

the morphism
i_{v}:\Omega^{1}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
is not surjective at
x
.

(iii)

the regular section of
v:X\rightarrow T_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}
vanishes at
x
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
: Denote by
m_{x}
the maximal ideal corresponding to
x
. Since
\Omega^{1}_{\{x\}}
is trivial,
\{x\}
is a closed invariant subvariety if and only if the morphism of
k
-vector space

\Omega^{1}_{X,x}/m_{x}\Omega^{1}_{X,x}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{X,x}/m_{x}\mathcal{L}_{X,x}

is identically zero if and only if

i_{v}(\Omega^{1}_{X,x})\subset m_{x}.\mathcal{L}_{X,x}

if and only if
i_{v}
is not surjective at
x
(as
m_{x}
is the unique maximal ideal).

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
: we can consider an affine open set endowed with a system of étale coordinates
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
around
x
(see subsection 3.1) and write

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\otimes s

where
s
is a non-vanishing section of
\mathcal{L}
on
U
.
It follows that
i_{v}(dx_{i})=f_{i}\otimes s
and hence that
i_{v}
is not surjective if and only
f_{1}(x)=\ldots=f_{n}(x)=0
.
∎

Definition 2.4.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{L},v)
be a twisted algebraic vector field. We say that
x\in X(k)
is
a singular point (or a zero of
v
)
if the equivalent previous properties of Lemma
2.4.1
hold. We denote by

\mathrm{Sing}(v)=\mathrm{Sing}(X,\mathcal{L},v)

the closed (obviously invariant under
v
) subvariety of

formed by the singular points of

.

Through the text, we will say that a (twisted) algebraic vector field has reduced singularities if the singular locus
\mathrm{Sing}(v)
has codimension
\geq 2
in
X
.

Construction 2.4.3
.

Let
(X,v)
be an algebraic vector field and let

be a singular point of

. The vector field

defines a derivation

2.5.
Some classical terminology on distributions and foliations

Definition 2.5.1
.

A
predistribution

\mathcal{D}
of rank
r
on
X
is a coherent subsheaf
\Theta_{\mathcal{D}}
of the tangent sheaf
\Theta_{X}
of
X
of rank
r
called
the tangent sheaf of the distribution
\mathcal{D}
. We say that a predistribution
\mathcal{D}
is

(1)

a
distribution
if the subcoherent sheaf
\Theta_{D}
of
\Theta_{X}
is saturated that is if
\Theta_{X}/\Theta_{D}
does not have torsion.

(2)

a
foliation
if it is a distribution which is also involutive:

[v,w]\in\Theta_{\mathcal{D}}(U)\text{ for every local sections }v,w\in\Theta_{D}(U)

where the Lie bracket is the Lie bracket of vector fields.

A predistribution
\mathcal{D}
defines an exact sequence

(2.7)

0\rightarrow\Theta_{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow\Theta_{X}\rightarrow\Theta_{X}/\Theta_{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow 0

The
singular locus

\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{D})
of the predistribution is the closed subset of
X
formed by the points
x\in X
such that this exact sequence is not an exact sequence of locally free sheaves at
x
.

It is well-known that the condition (1) of saturation is equivalent to:

(1)’

the singular locus
\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{D})
of
\mathcal{D}
is a closed subvariety of
X
of codimension
\geq 2
.

See for example
[
25
, Lemma 2.1.3]
.

Notation 2.5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a distribution of rank
r
on
X
and set
U=U(\mathcal{D})=X\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{D})
for the complement of the singular locus. By definition, the exact sequence (
2.7
) induces an exact sequence of vector bundles over
U

(2.8)

0\rightarrow T\mathcal{D}\rightarrow TU\rightarrow N\mathcal{D}\rightarrow 0

and the vector bundle
N\mathcal{D}
over
U
(of rank

) is called the

of the distribution
\mathcal{D}
. The dual vector bundle
N\mathcal{D}^{\ast}
can be identified with the vector subbundle of
T^{\ast}U
whose fiber
N\mathcal{D}_{x}
over
x\in X
is given by:

N\mathcal{D}^{\ast}_{x}=\{\omega\in T^{\ast}X_{x}\mid\omega(v)=0\text{ for all }v\in T\mathcal{D}_{x}\}

The vector bundle

is called the

\mathcal{D}
.

Although the conormal bundle of the distribution is only defined outside of the singular locus of the distribution
\mathcal{D}
, it completely determines
\mathcal{D}
because of the following fact.

Fact 2.5.3

(Extension of a distribution)
.

Let
U
be a dense open set of
X
. The restriction map

\mathcal{D}\mapsto\mathcal{D}_{\mid U}

sending a distribution on
X
to a distribution on

is one-to-one and sends foliations to foliations. In other words, every distribution (resp. foliation) on

.

See for example the proof of Proposition 2.3.1 of
[

]
. ∎

Example 2.5.4

(Foliations of rank one)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{L})
be an algebraic variety endowed with a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. We have a natural isomorphism

\Xi(X,\mathcal{L})=\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Theta_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}^{\vee},\Theta_{X})

which to a twisted algebraic vector field

associates the morphim given on affine open sets

of

and satisfying the following properties:

(i)

If

is not the trivial twisted vector field then

is injective. In particular, the image
\mathcal{D}(v)
of

is a distribution of rank one with tangent sheaf isomorphic to

.

Indeed,

and

(ii)

is a foliation of rank one.

is locally free at

if and only if

has constant rank around

if and only if

does not vanish at

(iii)

If

\mathcal{D}(v)=\mathcal{D}(w)\text{ if and only if there is }f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\ast}(X)\text{ with }v=f\cdot w.

Indeed, two injective morphisms define the same subobject if and only they differ by precomposition by an element of

2.6.
Invariant distributions

Definition 2.6.1
.

Remark 2.6.2

(See Section 3.2 of
[
25
]
)
.

The analytic interpretation of this definition for

goes as follows: a foliation
\mathcal{F}
of rank
r
defines a partition of (or, equivalently, an equivalence relation on)
X^{an}
into a singular set
\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
and a partition of

into analytic immersed subvarieties of dimension

called the leaves of the foliation.
The fact that this foliation is invariant under

means that this partition is preserved by the integral curves of the algebraic vector field
(X,v)
namely that

•

the singular set
\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
is a closed invariant subvariety of
(X,v)
.

•

the partition of

into leaves is preserved: namely that if

are two solutions such that

lie in the same leaf then the same holds true for

for every

.

If

is only a distribution then no such interpretation is possible.

.

Let

(ii)

3.1.
Systems of étale coordinates

We will often need to work locally on

Definition 3.1.1
.

An

of

where

is an affine open set of

and
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}

Systems of étale coordinates correspond to uniformizing parameters in the sense of
[
28
]
. We refer to
[
28
, Chapter III, Section 6]
for more details and an (easy) proof of the following fact:

Fact 3.1.2
.

Every smooth algebraic variety can be covered by affine open sets endowed with étale coordinates.

Let
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an affine open set with étale coordinates of
X
. By definition of étale morphisms,
\Omega^{1}_{X}(U)
is the free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module generated by
dx_{1},\ldots,dx_{n}
. We will always denote by
\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}
the dual basis of
\Theta_{X}(U)
so that a vector field
v
on

can always be uniquely written as

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}

where the
a_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
.

Example 3.1.3

(Lift by an étale morphism)
.

Let
\phi:X\rightarrow Y
be an étale morphism of smooth algebraic varieties. Every vector field
w
on
Y
has a unique lift to a vector field
v=\phi^{\ast}w
on
X
such that

\phi:(X,v)\rightarrow(Y,w)

is a morphism of algebraic vector fields. If
(V;y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
is an affine open set of
Y
endowed with étale coordinates, after setting

U=\phi^{-1}(V)\text{ and }x_{i}=y_{i}\circ\phi\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n

then
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is an affine open set of
X
endowed with étale coordinates and the lift of

w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\cdot\partial_{y_{i}}
is

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}\circ\phi)\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}

Example 3.1.4

(Presentation of the cotangent bundle)
.

Let
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an affine open set with étale coordinates of
X
.
For every
i\leq n
,
\partial_{x_{i}}
defines a function

on
\pi^{-1}(U)
given by

y_{i}:(\omega,x)\mapsto\omega(\partial_{x_{i}}(x))

The open set

is affine and we have a natural identification:

\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}X}(\pi^{-1}(U))=\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

where the left-hand side is the (free) polynomial ring generated by the

. This ring is naturally equipped with a graduation given by the total degree in

and we have that:

\pi^{-1}(U)\simeq\mathrm{Spec}\text{ }\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]\text{ and }p^{-1}(U)\simeq\mathrm{Proj}\text{ }\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

In particular, the
(\pi^{-1}(U);x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
where
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
runs through a covering of

by affine open sets equipped with étale coordinates gives a covering of

by affine open sets equipped with étale coordinates.

3.2.
Partial connection along an algebraic vector field

We fix
(X,v)
an algebraic vector field and we always denote by

\delta_{v}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}

the derivation defined by
v
on the structure sheaf of
X
. For coherence with the rest of the text, this section has been written for algebraic vector fields but works equally well for (non necessarily autonomous) smooth finite-dimensional
D
-varieties in the sense of Buium
[
5
]
.

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. A
partial connection
\nabla_{v}
on
\mathcal{E}
along
v
is an additive morphism of sheaves

\nabla_{v}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}

satisfying the Leibniz rule:

\nabla_{v}(f\cdot\sigma)=\delta_{v}(f)\cdot\sigma+f\cdot\nabla_{v}(\sigma)

for every local section
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\sigma\in\mathcal{E}(U)
.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Assume that
\nabla_{v}
is a partial connection on
\mathcal{E}
along
v
. There is a unique partial connection
\nabla_{v}^{\vee}
along
v
on the dual
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}=Hom(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{X})
such that

(3.1)

\delta_{v}(\omega(\sigma))=\nabla_{v}^{\vee}(\omega)(\sigma)+\omega(\nabla_{v}(\sigma))

for every local sections
\omega\in\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(U)
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}(U)
.

Proof.

Since a section of
\omega\in\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(U)
is determined by the
\omega(\sigma)
where
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}(U)
, the previous formula determines at most one partial connection on
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
along
v
. Therefore it suffices to check that

\omega\mapsto\nabla_{v}^{\vee}\omega:\sigma\mapsto\omega(\nabla_{v}(\sigma))-\delta_{v}(\omega(\sigma))

is a well-defined connection. This follows from a direct computation.
∎

Our main example is the natural connection induced by a vector field
v
on the tangent sheaf
\Theta_{X}
and its dual the sheaf of
1
-forms
\Omega^{1}_{X}
on
X
.

Example 3.2.3
.

The connection

\nabla^{st}_{v}:\xi\mapsto[v,\xi]

where the Lie bracket on the right-hand side is the Lie bracket of vector fields is a partial connection on
\Theta_{X}
along
v
. This connection and its dual on
\Omega^{1}_{X}
will be called the
partial standard connections associated to
(X,v)
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
, we will denote by
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})
the linear space over
X
whose local sections are the sections of
\mathcal{E}
and by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
its projectivization. If
\mathcal{E}
is locally free then
\pi:\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow X
and
p:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow X
are respectively a vector bundle and a projective bundle over
X
. A local section
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(U)
can be identified with a function on
\pi^{-1}(U)
given by

\overline{\sigma}:(x,e)\mapsto\sigma_{x}(e)

and the correspondence
\sigma\mapsto\overline{\sigma}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-linear.

Proposition 3.2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\nabla_{v})
be a locally free sheaf endowed with a partial connection along
v
. There are unique algebraic vector fields
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
on
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v})
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
respectively such that the following diagram commutes in the category of algebraic vector fields:

\textstyle{(\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})\setminus\{0-\text{section}\},\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{(X,v)}

and such that for every local section
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(U)
,

\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\overline{\sigma})=\overline{\nabla_{v}^{\vee}(\sigma)}

where the vector field
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
has been identified with the derivation it defines on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})}
.

Proof.

We work locally on
X
: consider
U
an affine open set of
X
, a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
of
\mathcal{E}(U)
and denote by
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
the dual basis of
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(U)
. By construction of
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})
, the coordinate ring on
\pi^{-1}(U)
is the free polynomial ring

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})}(\pi^{-1}(U))=\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[\overline{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\sigma_{n}}]

generated by
\overline{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\sigma_{n}}
. The description of derivations on free polynomial rings implies that there is a unique derivation
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})}(\pi^{-1}(U))
extending
v
and such that

\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\overline{\sigma_{i}})=\overline{\nabla_{v}^{\vee}(\sigma_{i})}.

which already implies uniqueness of the algebraic vector field structure on
\pi^{-1}(U)
. If
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}(U)
is any other section, then after writing
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\sigma_{i}
, we see that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\overline{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\delta_{v}(f_{i})\overline{\sigma_{i}}+f_{i}\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\overline{\sigma_{i}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\delta_{v}(f_{i})\overline{\sigma_{i}}+f_{i}\overline{\nabla_{v}^{\vee}(\sigma_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla_{v}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\sigma_{i})}=\overline{\nabla_{v}(\sigma)}

so that the construction produces a unique algebraic vector field structure on
\pi^{-1}(U)
with the desired properties.

To define the algebraic vector field structure on
p^{-1}(U)
, note that by construction the derivation

\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v}):\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[\overline{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\sigma_{n}}]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)[\overline{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\sigma_{n}}]

is homogeneous of degree zero. It follows that
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
defines a unique algebraic vector field structure
\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v})
on
p^{-1}(U)
such that

(\pi^{-1}(U)\setminus\{0-\text{section}\},\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v}))\rightarrow(p^{-1}(U)),\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v}))

is a morphism of algebraic vector fields. Indeed on the open set
U_{i}
defined by
\sigma_{i}\neq 0
, we just take:

\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v})(\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i})=\frac{\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\sigma_{i})\sigma_{j}-\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})(\sigma_{j})\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\text{ for }j\neq i

which can be written as a function of the
\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i}
for
j\neq i
by homogeneity of
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
.

Finally, the uniqueness of the constructions implies that this local constructions glue together to define global algebraic vector fields
\mathbb{E}(\nabla_{v})
on
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathbb{P}(\nabla_{v})
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
respectively.
∎

Remark 3.2.6

(Local expression)

Let
(U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an affine open set with étale coordinates of

Setting

3.3.
Invariant horizontal subvarieties of the first prolongation

For now on,
p:\mathbb{P}(T^{\ast}X)\rightarrow X

Definition 3.3.1
.

A subvariety of

is called

6

If
(X,v)
is an algebraic vector field,
an invariant horizontal subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(T^{\ast}X)
is a horizontal subvariety invariant under the vector field
\mathbb{P}(v^{[1]})

By definition, in the rest of the text,
a horizontal subvariety of

Lemma 3.3.2

Let

The inverse is given by

where

Linear connection along a smooth foliation

Let

Definition 4.1.1

Example 4.1.2

(Bott’s partial connection)

If

(a)

Definition 4.3.3
.

Let

Lemma 4.3.4
.

Let

be a smooth morphism of algebraic vector fields and

projecting surjectively on

is a closed invariant subvariety.

Proof.

First note that it is indeed a closed subvariety of
Z
as on the complement of the singular locus (which is closed), it is defined by the vanishing of the determinant of

. Since the singular locus of an invariant subvariety is always invariant (see for example

[

), we can assume that

Consider

and consider pieces of analytic flow

and

associated with

and

around

and

respectively. Since

Since

and

are locally invertible, it follows that

Moreover, since

It follows from the previous equalities that

4.4.
A minimality criterion based on a singular point

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4.1

(Theorem D)
.

Let
(X,v)
be a complex algebraic vector field with reduced singularities of dimension
n\geq 2
. Assume that
(X,v)
admits a singularity
x_{0}\in X(\mathbb{C})

such that

(a)

the singularity
x_{0}
is non-resonant,

(b)

the singularity
x_{0}
is not contained in any invariant proper positive-dimensional irreducible closed algebraic subvariety of
(X,v)
.

Then the generic type of
(X,v)
is minimal.

Remark 4.4.2
.

•

In arbitrary dimension, an easy geometric argument shows that the presence of a singular point of
(X,v)
rules out the possibility of (ii) in Fact
1.4.1
.

Indeed, assume that
(X,v)
admits a singular point

and consider a rational generically finite dominant morphism

\phi:(X,v)\dashrightarrow(A,v_{A})

as in (ii) of Fact
1.4.1
. Because

is an abelian variety,
\phi
can be extended into a regular morphism
\overline{\phi}:X\rightarrow A
and since

is a global vector field on

, we obtain a regular morphism of algebraic vector fields

\overline{\phi}:(X,v)\rightarrow(A,v_{A})

It follows that
\overline{\phi}(x_{0})
must be a singular point of

which is a contradiction since
v_{A}
is nowhere singular.

•

Clearly, the presence of a singular point alone does not rule out (i) in Fact
1.4.1
.

Indeed, consider an example of the form
(X,v)=(Y,w)\times(\mathbb{A}^{1},x\frac{d}{dx})

where
(Y,w)
admits a singular point

. Then
x_{0}=(y_{0},0)
is a singular point of
(X,v)
but the generic type of
(X,v)
is not minimal. Note however that
this singular point is contained both in an invariant hypersurface and an invariant curve
so that the hypothesis (b) is violated in two different ways.

•

It follows that (b) is a necessary assumption for the conclusion of the theorem to hold. On the other hand, in dimension two
9
9

9

Even in dimension three, applying the methods of section 3 of
[
32
]
together with arguments from this paper, one can get rid of the assumption (a) (see Remark
4.2.5
).
, the hypothesis (a) is not necessary. This follows from Theorem C together with the results of
[
26
]
. In dimension

, this assumption will be needed in our analysis in the form of Corollary

.

We start the proof of the theorem by the following lemma:

Lemma 4.4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an invariant distribution of codimension one and let

be the foliation tangent to

. Assume that
\mathcal{D}
and

are generically transverse: on some nonempty open set

We may work locally on

so we assume that

is affine and we may assume that

(or equivalently

. Since

and since the distribution

. We claim that the function

generates an invariant hypersurface. Indeed:

since

and

are transverse on

•

since

So that the equation

defines a subvariety of

of pure codimension one going through all singular points of

. It remains to see that this codimension one subvariety is invariant:

•

By Lemma

Proof of Theorem

.

We first show that the first prolongation of

Case 2:

5.
Abundance of strongly minimal autonomous differential equations

5.1.
From minimality to strong minimality

The following easy lemma makes the connection between minimality and strong minimality that will be suitable to the study of generic autonomous differential equations.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Let
(X,v)
be a algebraic vector field of dimension
n\geq 2
over some algebraically closed field
k
. Assume that

(\ast)
:
(X,v)
does not admit any proper positive-dimensional invariant algebraic subvariety

and denote by
p
the generic type of
(X,v)
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{Sing}(v)
is finite,

(ii)

the solution set
(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}
can be decomposed as

(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}=\mathrm{Sing}(v)\cup p(\mathcal{U})

(iii)

the solution set
(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}
is strongly minimal if and only if
p
is a minimal type.

Proof.

(i) follows from
(\ast)
and the fact that
\mathrm{Sing}(v)
is a proper closed invariant subvariety. To show (ii), note that by elimination of quantifiers in
\mathrm{DCF}_{0}
, the set
\mathcal{Q}
of types
q\in S(k)
living on
(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}
is in bijection with the set of invariant irreducible subvarieties of
(X,v)
. It follows from
(\ast)
that

(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q(\mathcal{U})=p(\mathcal{U})\cup\mathrm{Sing}(v)

since the proper irreducible invariant subvarieties are all
0
-dimensional.
To prove (iii), assume that
p
is a minimal type and, for the sake of a contradiction, consider
D
a definable subset of
(X,v)^{\mathcal{U}}
defined over a differential extension
K
of
k
which is both finite and cofinite. Since
\mathrm{Sing}(v)
is finite, (ii) implies that both

p(x)\cup\{x\in D\}\text{ and }p(x)\cup\{x\notin D\}

are infinite and contain non-algebraic complete types
p_{+}\in S(K)
and
p_{-}\in S(K)
respectively. Since
p
is minimal both are non-forking extension of
p
which leads to a contradiction as
p
is a stationary type.
∎

5.2.
Case of the affine space

Theorem 5.2.1
.

Let

and consider a system of autonomous differential equations:

(S):\begin{cases}x_{1}^{\prime}&=f_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\\

.

Note that even when

improves on the main theorem of

]
which assumes that the degree

•

The assumption

Corollary 5.5.5
.

Let

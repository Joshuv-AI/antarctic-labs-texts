Symmetries of the Space of Linear Symplectic Connections
Daniel J.F. FOX

Abstract
There is constructed a family of Lie algebras that act in a Hamiltonian way on the symplectic affine space of linear symplectic connections on a symplectic manifold. The associated equivariant moment map is a formal sum of the Cahen–Gutt moment map, the Ricci tensor, and a translational term. The critical points of a functional constructed from it interpolate between the equations for preferred symplectic connections and the equations for critical symplectic connections. The commutative algebra of formal sums of symmetric tensors on a symplectic manifold carries a pair of compatible Poisson structures, one induced from the canonical Poisson bracket on the space of functions on the cotangent bundle polynomial in the fibers, and the other induced from the algebraic fiberwise Schouten bracket on the symmetric algebra of each fiber of the cotangent bundle. These structures are shown to be compatible, and the required Lie algebras are constructed as central extensions of their linear combinations restricted to formal sums of symmetric tensors whose first order term is a multiple of the differential of its zeroth order term.

Introduction

A (linear)
symplectic connection
on a symplectic manifold
(M,\Omega)
is a torsion-free affine connection preserving the symplectic form
\Omega
.
The space
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
of symplectic connections is a symplectic affine space; the difference of two symplectic connections is identified with a section of the bundle
S^{3}(T^{\ast}M)
of completely symmetric covariant three tensors, and the symplectic form
\mathbb{\Omega}
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is given by integrating over
M
the pairing induced on such sections by
\Omega
. The geometry of the symplectic affine space
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
is the focus of this note.

Efforts have been made to identify geometrically interesting classes of symplectic connections analogous to classes of connections studied in the Riemannian setting, such as the Levi-Civita connections of Einstein or constant scalar curvature metrics. Such classes are defined in terms of the zeros and critical points of functionals on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
equivariant or invariant with respect to some action of some group of symplectomorphisms or Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms.

The geometry of any symplectic space is reflected in its Poisson algebra of functions. Interesting functionals on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
are constructed from the curvature of
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. The simplest interesting classes of symplectic connections that have been studied are those of Ricci type, the preferred symplectic connections (these include the Ricci flat symplectic connections), and the critical symplectic connections. The focus here is on preferred and critical symplectic connections.

The critical points of the integral of the trace of the square of the Ricci endomorphism are the
preferred
symplectic connections first studied by F. Bourgeois and M. Cahen
[
5
,
6
]
. A symplectic connection is preferred if and only if there vanishes the complete symmetrization of the covariant derivative of its Ricci tensor
\mathsf{Ric}_{ij}=R_{ij}
, that is, it solves
(\delta^{\ast}\mathsf{Ric})_{ijk}=-\nabla_{(i}R_{jk)}=0
. The functionals given by integrating the trace of higher even powers of the Ricci endomorphism play a role in the averaging procedure used by Fedosov in
[
9
]
, and, as is explained briefly in Section
4.3
, their critical points generalize preferred symplectic connections. However, because the first Pontryagin class of a symplectic manifold equals the integral of an expression quadratic in the curvature of the connection, the class of preferred symplectic connections is the only interesting class of symplectic connections obtained as the critical points of a functional given by integrating expressions quadratic in the curvature tensor (this observation is due to Proposition 2.4 of
[
6
]
; see also the introduction of
[
10
]
).

In
[
7
]
, M. Cahen and S. Gutt showed that the action of the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is Hamiltonian, with a moment map denoted here by
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)
(see (
4.3
) for its definition). In
[
10
]
, the author began the study of the
critical
symplectic connections, that are defined to be the critical points of the integral
\mathcal{E}(\nabla)=\int_{M}\mathscr{K}(\nabla)^{2}\Omega_{n}
(where
\Omega_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n!}\Omega^{n}
) with respect to arbitrary variations of
\nabla
. A symplectic connection
\nabla
is critical if and only if the Hamiltonian vector field
\mathsf{H}_{\mathscr{K}(\nabla)}
generated by
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)
is an infinitesimal automorphism of
\nabla
. Explicitly this means that
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{K}(\nabla))=0
where
\mathscr{H}(f)_{ijk}=(\mathfrak{L}_{\mathsf{H}_{f}}\nabla)_{ij}\,^{p}\Omega_{pk}
,
\mathsf{H}_{f}
is the Hamiltonian vector field generated by
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathsf{H}_{f}}
is the Lie derivative along
\mathsf{H}_{f}
. The moment map
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)
is the sum of a multiple of the twofold divergence of the Ricci tensor of
\nabla
and a multiple of the complete contraction of the first Pontryagin form of
\nabla
with
\Omega\wedge\Omega
. By the main theorem of D. Tamarkin
[
19
]
, a functional on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
given by integrating against
\Omega_{n}
polynomials in the curvature tensor and its covariant derivatives contracted with the symplectic form is independent of the choice of connection if and only if its integrand is, modulo divergences, a polynomial in Pontryagin forms contracted with the symplectic form. It follows that
\mathscr{K}
is in some sense, not made precise here, the simplest symplectomorphism equivariant map from
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
to
C^{\infty}(M)
.

Suppose
(M,\Omega)
is compact, and fix a reference symplectic connection
\nabla_{0}
. Here there are constructed, for each
(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, a Lie bracket
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{s,t}
on

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}=C^{\infty}(M)\oplus\Gamma\big(S^{2}(T^{\ast}M)\big)\oplus\Gamma\big(S^{3}(T^{\ast}M)\big)

(1.1)

and a Hamiltonian action of the Lie algebra
(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}},(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{s,t})
on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
. The associated moment map is essentially a linear combination of
\mathscr{K}
and the Ricci tensor (see (
6.16
) for the precise expression when
(s,t)=(1,1)
; it requires some preliminary discussion to make sense of it). The choice of
\nabla_{0}
is unimportant in the sense that the Lie algebras associated with different choices of
\nabla_{0}
are isomorphic. In fact, the brackets
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{s,t}
(and corresponding Hamiltonian actions) for which neither
s
nor
t
is
0
are all pairwise isomorphic, so isomorphic to
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)=(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{1,1}
; the reason for considering the full family is that it interpolates between the degenerate cases
(s,t)=(1,0)
and
(s,t)=(0,1)
(this is discussed further below). In (
1.1
), the factor
C^{\infty}(M)
should be seen as the central extension of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
of Hamiltonian vector fields, while
\Gamma(S^{2}(T^{\ast}M))
should be seen as the Lie algebra of infinitesimal gauge transformations of the symplectic frame bundle. The Lie bracket
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)
combines and extends these actions.

The somewhat complicated definition of the bracket
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)
is summarized now. The precise definition is (
6.14
) of Section
6.1
; see also Theorem
6.4
. The algebra
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
whose elements are formal linear sums of completely symmetric tensors on
(M,\Omega)
(see Section
3
for the precise meaning) carries two Lie brackets, a differential Lie bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
induced from the Schouten bracket on symmetric contravariant tensors on
M
and an algebraic Lie bracket
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
induced fiberwise by regarding the symmetric tensor algebra as the associated graded algebra of the Weyl algebra. The definition of
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)
requires Lemma
3.5
, showing that these Lie brackets are compatible in the sense of bi-Hamiltonian systems; precisely, the differential, in the Lie algebra cohomology of
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
, of the symmetrized covariant derivative along
\nabla
equals the Schouten bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
(see (
2
) of Lemma
3.5
). (Although it seems that this claim about the Schouten bracket must be known in some form to experts, I do not know a reference.) This means that any linear combination
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{s,t}=s(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)+t\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
is again a Lie bracket.

The space
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
is too big because its
1
-graded part comprises all one-forms on
M
, rather than the closed one-forms, that are dual to symplectic vector fields. Because the
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]=[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{1,1}
bracket of closed one forms in
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
need not be closed, it is inadequate to restrict to the subspace of
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
the
1
-graded part of which comprises closed one-forms (although something can be done in this directions; see Lemma
3.11
). However, the subspace
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
, comprising formal sums
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\cdots
such that
\alpha_{k}\in\Gamma(S^{k}(T^{\ast}M))
and
\alpha_{1}=-d\alpha_{0}
is a subalgebra of
(\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
, and this subalgebra plays a prominent role in the discussion. Lemmas
3.7
and
3.11
describe its structure.

Although
(\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
cannot be made to act on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
in any obvious way, the subalgebra
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
admits a symplectic affine action on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
such that the ideal
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{4}(M)=\prod_{k\geq 4}\Gamma(S^{k}(T^{\ast}M))
acts trivially. This action is weakly Hamiltonian, but the cocycle obstructing equivariance can be computed explicitly. This yields a central extension that acts in a Hamiltonian manner on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
, with moment map
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}
. Since the Lie ideal
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{4}(M)
acts trivially, quotienting by it yields an action of the Lie algebra
(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}},(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!))
.

The action of the group of gauge transformations of the symplectic frame bundle of
M
on the space of affine connections, possibly having torsion, preserving
\Omega
, is Hamiltonian with a moment map constructed in the manner of Atiyah and Bott in
[
1
]
. See Theorem
5.1
for the precise statement. Using the symplectic form the Lie algebra of infinitesimal gauge transformations can be identified with the space
\Gamma(S^{2}(T^{\ast}M))
equipped with a constant multiple of the algebraic Lie bracket mentioned above. The obstruction to this Lie algebra acting on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is given by the Schouten bracket. However, an extended bracket on
\Gamma(S^{2}(T^{\ast}M))\oplus\Gamma(S^{3}(T^{\ast}M))
built from the Poisson bracket and the algebraic bracket does act on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
, by symplectic affine transformations. This extended action can be combined in a coherent way with the action of
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. While the resulting action is only weakly Hamiltonian, the cocycle measuring nonequivariance of the moment map can be computed explicitly and from it there is constructed the Hamiltonian action of
(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}},(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{s,t})
. This action combines, at the infinitesimal level, the actions of
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\Omega)
and the group of gauge transformations to yield a Hamiltonian action with a moment map
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}
that simultaneously extends the moment map
\mathscr{K}
for the action of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
of Hamiltonian vector fields and the Atiyah–Bott moment map.

When
M
is compact, there is constructed (see (
6.17
) for the precise definition) from
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}
a functional
\mathbb{N}\colon\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
. There is a graded symmetric bilinear pairing on
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
that descends to
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
. Pairing the moment map
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}
with itself with respect to this pairing yields
\mathbb{N}
. The critical points of
\mathbb{N}
are the solutions of

\displaystyle t^{2}\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{K}(\nabla))+s^{2}\delta^{\ast}\mathsf{Ric}=0.

(1.2)

The equations (
1.2
) interpolate between the equations for preferred and critical symplectic connections. The case
(s,t)=(1,0)
yields the equations for the preferred symplectic connections introduced by Bourgeois and Cahen
[
6
]
, and the case
(s,t)=(0,1)
recovers the equations for the critical symplectic connections introduced by the author in
[
10
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
10
]
it is shown that on a
2
-manifold a preferred symplectic connection is critical. Consequently, on a
2
-dimensional symplectic manifold a preferred symplectic connection solves (
1.2
) for any
(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Section
3
gives the background needed to formulate and prove Lemma
3.5
. Section
4
recalls the definitions and basic characterizations of critical and preferred symplectic connections. Section
5
discusses the failure of the symplectomorphism group to act in a Hamiltonian manner on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. Finally, the construction of
(\!(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\!)_{s,t}
and its action on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is given in Section
6
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1.

Smooth means
C^{\infty}
, and all manifolds, bundles, sections, etc. are smooth, unless otherwise indicated. Throughout,
(M,\Omega)
is a connected
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold oriented by the Liouville volume form
\Omega_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n!}\Omega^{n}
. For simplicity,
M
is often assumed compact, although most claims generalize straightforwardly to the noncompact setting.

For a finite-dimensional real vector space
\mathbb{V}
,
S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
and
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{V})
denote the
k
th symmetric and antisymmetric powers of
\mathbb{V}
. Purely algebraic constructions on vector spaces extend straightforwardly to vector bundles and their spaces of sections, and the same notations will be used in both contexts. For a smooth vector bundle
E\to M
,
\Gamma(E)
denotes the vector space of smooth sections of
E
, and
\Lambda^{k}(E)
and
S^{k}(E)
denote the
k
th antisymmetric and symmetric powers of
E
. By definition
\Lambda^{0}(E)=S^{0}(E)=C^{\infty}(M)
.

Tensors are usually indicated using the abstract index conventions (see
[
17
]
or
[
20
]
). Indices are labels and do not indicate a choice of frame. An index is in either
up
or
down
position. Up (down) indices label contravariant (covariant) tensors. For example,
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X^{k}
(which could also be written
(\nabla^{2}X)_{ij}\,^{k}
) indicates the second covariant derivative of the vector field
X^{k}
. Were a frame
E_{i}
chosen,
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X^{k}
would correspond to
(\nabla^{2}X)(E_{i},E_{j})=\nabla_{E_{i}}\nabla_{E_{j}}X-\nabla_{\nabla_{E_{i}}E_{j}}X
, and not to
\nabla_{E_{i}}\nabla_{E_{j}}X
. The up index
k
on
X^{k}
is simply a label that indicates that
X
is a contravariant vector field. The summation convention is used in the following form: repetition of a label in up and down position indicates the trace pairing. Enclosure of indices in square brackets (parentheses) indicates complete antisymmetrization (symmetrization) over the enclosed indices, so that, for example,
a^{ij}=a^{(ij)}+a^{[ij]}
indicates the decomposition of a contravariant two-tensor into its symmetric and skew-symmetric parts. An index included between vertical bars
|\,|
is omitted from an indicated (anti)symmetrization; for example
2a_{[i|jk|l]}=a_{ijkl}-a_{ljki}
.

The conventions are illustrated by the following definitions. The curvature
R_{ijk}\,^{l}
and torsion
\tau_{ij}\,^{k}
of an affine connection
\nabla
are defined by
2\nabla_{[i}\nabla_{j]}X^{k}=R_{ijp}\,^{k}X^{p}-\tau_{ij}\,^{p}\nabla_{p}X^{k}
. The
Ricci curvature
of
\nabla
is
R_{ij}=R_{pij}\,^{p}
. (Sometimes, for readability,
\mathsf{Ric}
is written instead of
R_{ij}
.)

The antisymmetric bivector
\Omega^{ij}
inverse to
\Omega_{ij}
is defined by
\Omega^{ip}\Omega_{pj}=-\delta_{j}\,^{i}
. Indices are raised and lowered using
\Omega_{ij}
and
\Omega^{ij}
by contracting with these tensors consistently with the conventions
X_{i}=X^{p}\Omega_{pi}
and
X^{i}=\Omega^{ip}X_{p}
. When it is raised or lowered, an index’s horizontal position is maintained. The symplectic
sharp
and
flat
operators on a vector field
X^{i}
and a one-form
\alpha_{i}
are defined by
X^{\curlyvee}_{i}=X^{p}\Omega_{pi}
and
\alpha^{\curlywedge\,i}=\Omega^{ip}\alpha_{p}
. These are inverses, so that
(X^{\curlyvee})^{\curlywedge}=X
. For a vector field
X
,
X_{i}
and
X^{\curlyvee}
are synonyms and both notations will be used.

2.2.

Let
\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
be the group of compactly supported symplectomorphisms of
(M,\Omega)
. Its Lie algebra
\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
comprises compactly supported vector fields
X
that are locally Hamiltonian, meaning
\mathfrak{L}_{X}\Omega=0
; equivalently
X^{\curlyvee}=\Omega(X,\,\cdot\,)
is closed. Define the Hamiltonian vector field
\mathsf{H}_{f}
of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
by
\mathsf{H}_{f}^{i}=-df^{i}=\Omega^{pi}df_{p}
. Since
\mathsf{H}_{\{f,g\}}=[\mathsf{H}_{f},\mathsf{H}_{g}]
for the Poisson bracket
\{f,g\}=\Omega(\mathsf{H}_{f},\mathsf{H}_{g})
of
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the compactly supported Hamiltonian vector fields constitute a subalgebra
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)\subset\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
. For a
2k
-form
\sigma
and
\Omega_{k}=\tfrac{1}{k!}\Omega^{k}
,

\displaystyle\sigma\wedge\Omega_{n-k}=\tfrac{1}{k!2^{k}}\Omega^{i_{1}i_{2}}\vdots\Omega^{i_{2k-1}i_{2k}}\sigma_{i_{1}\dots i_{2k}}\Omega_{n}=\tfrac{1}{k!2^{k}}\sigma_{i_{1}}\,^{i_{1}}\,{}_{i_{2}}\,^{i_{2}}\,{}_{\dots}\,^{\dots}\,{}_{i_{k}}\,^{i_{k}}\Omega_{n}.

(2.1)

By (
2.1
),
\{f,g\}\Omega_{n}=df\wedge dg\wedge\Omega_{n-1}=d(fdg\wedge\Omega_{n-1})
. Since, if
M
is compact,
\int_{M}\{f,g\}\,\Omega_{n}=0
for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the subspace
C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\subset C^{\infty}(M)
comprising mean zero functions is a Lie subalgebra of
C^{\infty}(M)
, isomorphic to
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
via the map
f\to\mathsf{H}_{f}
. If
M
is noncompact, then
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
is identified with the Lie algebra
(C^{\infty}_{c}(M),\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\})
of compactly supported smooth functions.
The subgroup
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\Omega)\subset\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)_{0}
of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of
(M,\Omega)
comprises symplectomorphisms of
(M,\Omega)
that can be realized as the time one flow of a normalized time-dependent Hamiltonian on
M\times[-1,1]
, where
normalized
means mean-zero if
M
is compact and compactly supported if
M
is noncompact. The infinitesimal generator of a flow by Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms is a Hamiltonian vector field, so that the Lie algebra of
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\Omega)
is
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
.

2.3.

An action of a Lie group
G
on a symplectic manifold
(M,\Omega)
is
symplectic
if
G
acts by symplectic diffeomorphisms. The associated Lie algebra homomorphism from the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
to
\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
, defined by
x\to\mathscr{X}^{x}_{p}=\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\exp(-tx)\cdot p
, is
weakly Hamiltonian
if there is a map
\mu\colon M\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
such that for each
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the Hamiltonian vector field
\mathsf{H}_{\mu(x)}
equals the vector field
\mathscr{X}^{x}
determined by the action. If
\mu
is equivariant with respect to the given action of
G
on
M
and the coadjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, then the action is
Hamiltonian
and
\mu
is a
moment map
. When
G
is simply connected, this equivariance is equivalent to the requirement that when viewed as a map from
\mathfrak{g}
to
C^{\infty}(M)
, the map
\mu
be a Lie algebra homomorphism,
\{\mu(x),\mu(y)\}=\mu([x,y])
, and the induced action of
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be Hamiltonian if it satisfies this last condition. For a weakly Hamiltonian action that is not necessarily Hamiltonian, the associated
\mu
is called a
nonequivariant moment map
. Since some authors do not require equivariance in the definition of a moment map, the redundant expression
equivariant moment map
is sometimes used for clarity. These definitions are applied in the infinite-dimensional context, in which case they are to be understood formally.

2.4.

This subsection fixes terminology related to affine actions of a Lie algebra. This is needed mainly in Section
6.1
.
A map
f\colon A\to B
between affine spaces
A
and
B
is affine if there is a linear map
\mathsf{L}(f)\colon V\to W
, between the vector spaces
V
and
W
of translations of
A
and
B
, such that
f(q)-f(p)=\mathsf{L}(f)(q-p)
for all
p,q\in A
. Under composition the bijective affine maps of
A
to itself form the group
\mathbb{Aff}(A)
of affine automorphisms of
A
, and
\mathsf{L}\colon\mathbb{Aff}(A)\to{\rm GL}(V)
is a surjective homomorphism. A one-parameter subgroup through the identity in
\mathbb{Aff}(A)
has the form
\operatorname{Id}_{A}+t\phi+O(t^{2})
for some affine map
\phi\colon A\to V
. Consequently, the Lie algebra
\mathbb{aff}(A)
of
\mathbb{Aff}(A)
is the vector space of affine maps from
A
to
V
equipped with the bracket
[f,g]=\mathsf{L}(f)\circ g-\mathsf{L}(g)\circ f
.

An
affine representation
of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
on the affine space
A
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{aff}(A)
, that is, a linear map satisfying
\rho([a,b])=\mathsf{L}(\rho(a))\rho(b)-\mathsf{L}(\rho(b))\rho(a)
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
. An
affine action
of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
on the affine space
A
is a biaffine map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{g}\times A\to A
such that
\pi(0,p)=p
for all
p\in A
and
a\cdot(b\cdot p)-b\cdot(a\cdot p)=[a,b]\cdot p-p
for all
p\in A
and all
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
. That this identity is an equality of vectors in
V
explains the need for the
p
on its right-hand side. That
\pi
be biaffine means that the maps
p\to a\cdot p=\pi(a,p)
and
a\to a\cdot p=\pi(a,p)
are affine maps from
A
to
A
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathcal{A}
for all
p\in\mathcal{A}
. (Note that a biaffine map need not be affine with respect to the product affine structure on
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathcal{A}
.)

Lemma 2.1
.

For an affine space
A
and a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, a map
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{aff}(A)
is an affine representation if and only if
\pi\colon\mathfrak{g}\times A\to A
defined by
\pi(a,p)=p+\rho(a)p
is an affine action.

First one shows that
\rho
is linear if and only if
\pi
is biaffine and
\pi(0,p)=0
for all
p\in A
. Then one checks directly that, in this case,
\left((a\cdot(b\cdot p)-b\cdot(a\cdot p)\right)-([a,b]\cdot p-p)=(\mathsf{L}(\rho(a))\rho(b)-\mathsf{L}(\rho(b)\rho(a)-\rho([a,b]))p
. The representation
\rho
is said to be
associated
with the affine action
\pi
. The
stabilizer
of
p_{0}\in A
under the affine action of
\mathfrak{g}
means the subalgebra
\{a\in\mathfrak{g}\colon a\cdot p_{0}=p_{0}\}=\{a\in\mathfrak{g}\colon\rho(a)p_{0}=0\}
.

A typical example is the affine action
X\cdot\nabla=\nabla+\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla
of the Lie algebra of vector fields on
M
on the space of affine connections on
M
. The associated affine representation is
\rho(X)=\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla
.

A
symplectic affine space
means an affine space
A
equipped with a symplectic form
\Omega
invariant under the action of the group of translations of
A
. An affine representation
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{aff}(A)
on a symplectic affine space
(A,\Omega)
is
symplectic affine
if each
\rho(a)
is symplectic, meaning the linear part
\mathsf{L}(\rho(a))
satisfies
\Omega(\mathsf{L}(\rho(a))u,v)+\Omega(u,\mathsf{L}(\rho(a))v)=0
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v\in V
. Similarly, an affine action is symplectic affine if the corresponding affine representation is symplectic.

2.5.

An affine connection is
symplectic
if
\nabla_{i}\Omega_{jk}=0
. For background about symplectic connections see
[
2
]
or
[
8
]
and the references therein. The space
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
of symplectic affine connections on
(M,\Omega)
is an affine space modeled on the vector space
\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes S^{2}(T^{\ast}M))
of covariant tensors
\Pi_{ijk}=\Pi_{i(jk)}
symmetric in the last two entries. Precisely, if
\nabla\in\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
and
\bar{\nabla}=\nabla+\Pi_{ij}\,^{k}
then
\bar{\nabla}_{i}\Omega_{jk}=-2\Pi_{i[jk]}
, so that
\bar{\nabla}\in\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
if and only if
\Pi_{i[jk]}=0
. Note that
\Pi_{ip}\,^{p}=0
, reflecting that a symplectic affine connection preserves the associated volume form
\Omega_{n}
. The affine subspace
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\subset\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
comprising torsion-free symplectic affine connections is modeled on the vector space
\mathsf{S}^{3}(M)
, for the difference tensor of torsion-free connections satisfies
\Pi_{[ij]}\,^{k}=0
, and with
\Pi_{i[jk]}=0
, this implies
\Pi_{ijk}=\Pi_{(ijk)}
. The space
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is nonempty, for if
\bar{\nabla}
is any torsion-free affine connection then
\nabla=\bar{\nabla}+\tfrac{2}{3}\Omega^{kp}\bar{\nabla}_{(i}\Omega_{j)p}
is symplectic. It is convenient to write
T_{\nabla}\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)=\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes S^{2}(T^{\ast}M))
or
T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)=\mathsf{S}^{3}(M)
for the tangent space to the affine spaces
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
or
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
at
\nabla
. The focus here is on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
, and it is mainly in Section
5.1
that reference will be made to
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
. Throughout this paper
symplectic connection
means a torsion-free symplectic connection, while
symplectic affine connection
means a connection preserving
\Omega
but possibly having torsion
.

Since, for
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,
\Omega_{n}
is
\nabla
-parallel, the curvature
-R_{ijp}\,^{p}
of the connection induced on
\Lambda^{2n}(T^{\ast}M)
by
\nabla
vanishes. Hence
\nabla
has symmetric Ricci tensor, for
2R_{[ij]}=-R_{ijp}\,^{p}=0
. By the Ricci identity,
0=2\nabla_{[i}\nabla_{j]}\Omega_{kl}=-2R_{ij[kl]}
, where
R_{ijkl}=R_{ijk}\,^{p}\Omega_{pl}
. The algebraic Bianchi identity and
R_{ij[kl]}=0
yield
R_{p}\,^{p}\,{}_{ij}=-2R_{ip}\,^{p}\,{}_{j}=2R_{pij}\,^{p}=2R_{ij}
, from which it follows that every nontrivial trace of
R_{ijkl}
is a constant multiple of
R_{ij}
.

2.6.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
be a symplectic vector space. On
\operatorname{W}^{p,q}(\mathbb{V})=\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{V})\otimes S^{q}(\mathbb{V})
define

\displaystyle\Omega(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{p}\tfrac{1}{p!}\alpha_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}j_{1}\dots j_{q}}\beta^{i_{1}\dots i_{p}j_{1}\dots j_{q}}.

(2.2)

This pairing is graded symmetric in the sense that
\Omega(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{|\alpha||\beta|}\Omega(\beta,\alpha)
for homogeneous elements
\alpha
and
\beta
, where
|\alpha|=p+q
is the
degree
of
\alpha\in\operatorname{W}^{p,q}(\mathbb{V})
. Integrating (
2.2
) yields a
\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
-invariant graded symmetric pairing on
\Gamma(\operatorname{W}^{p,q}(T^{\ast}M))
defined by

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle=\int_{M}\Omega(\alpha,\beta)\,\Omega_{n}=(-1)^{p}\tfrac{1}{p!}\int_{M}\alpha_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}j_{1}\dots j_{q}}\beta^{i_{1}\dots i_{p}j_{1}\dots j_{q}}\,\Omega_{n}.

(2.3)

Using an almost complex structure compatible with
\Omega
it is straightforward to show that the pairing
\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle
is weakly nondegenerate in the sense that if
\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle=0
for all compactly supported
\beta\in\Gamma(\operatorname{W}^{p,q}(T^{\ast}M))
then
\alpha=0
.

2.7.

Fix a
2n
-dimensional symplectic vector space
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
. The
(linear) symplectic frame bundle

\pi\colon\mathscr{F}\to M
is the
G=Sp(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
principal bundle whose fiber over
p\in M
comprises symplectic linear maps
u\colon(\mathbb{V},\Omega)\to(T_{p}M,\Omega)
. A symplectic affine connection
\nabla
determines and is determined by a principal connection on
\mathscr{F}
. The space
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
of symplectic affine connections on
M
is an affine space modeled on the vector space of
1
-forms taking values in the adjoint bundle
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathscr{F})=\mathscr{F}\times_{\operatorname{Ad}}\mathfrak{g}
associated with
\mathscr{F}
by the adjoint action of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=^{(}\mathbb{V},\Omega)
.

2.8.

Given
\alpha,\beta\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
, composing the bundle-valued two-form
(\alpha\wedge\beta)_{ijk}\,^{l}=2\alpha_{[i|p|}\,^{l}\beta_{j]k}\,^{p}

=-2\alpha_{[i}\,^{pl}\beta_{j]kp}
with the trace on endomorphisms yields the two-form
\operatorname{\mathsf{tr}}(\alpha\wedge\beta)_{ij}=2\alpha_{[i|p|}\,^{q}\beta_{j]q}\,^{p}
. By (
2.1
),
\operatorname{\mathsf{tr}}(\alpha\wedge\beta)\wedge\Omega_{n-1}=-\alpha_{ijk}\beta^{ijk}\Omega_{n}
. By (
2.3
) the two-form
\mathbb{\Omega}
defined on
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}(\alpha,\beta)=-\int_{M}\operatorname{\mathsf{tr}}(\alpha\wedge\beta)\wedge\Omega_{n-1}=\int_{M}\alpha_{ijk}\beta^{ijk}\,\Omega_{n}=\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle,\qquad\alpha,\beta\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{T},

is weakly nondegenerate. The translation invariance,
\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla+\Pi}=\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}
, for
\Pi\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
means
\mathbb{\Omega}
is parallel, so closed, and so
\mathbb{\Omega}
is a symplectic form. The subspace
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\subset\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
is symplectic.

The group of diffeomorphisms acts on the space of affine connections on the right, by pullback. Precisely
\phi^{\ast}(\nabla)
is defined by
\phi^{\ast}(\nabla)_{X}Y=T\phi^{-1}(\nabla_{T\phi(X)}T\phi(Y))
. This action preserves the torsion-free connections. Consequently,
\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
acts on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
and
\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
by pullback, and this action preserves
\mathbb{\Omega}
. Integration by parts yields the corresponding infinitesimal statement,
\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\alpha,\beta)+\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}(\alpha,\mathfrak{L}_{X}\beta)=0
, for
X\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
.

3
Symmetric tensors on symplectic manifolds

3.1.

The symmetric tensor algebra
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{V})
, which is, by definition, a quotient of the tensor algebra, can be identified, as a graded vector space, with the graded vector space
\oplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
equipped with the symmetric product
(\alpha\odot\beta)^{i_{1}\dots i_{k+l}}=\alpha^{(i_{1}\dots i_{k}}\beta^{i_{k+1}\dots i_{k+l})}
, for
\alpha\in S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
and
\beta\in S^{l}(\mathbb{V})
. The subspace
S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
is identified with the space
\mathsf{Pol}^{k}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast})
of homogeneous degree
k
polynomials on the dual vector space
\mathbb{V}^{\ast}
, and the product
\odot
corresponds to multiplication of polynomials. Elements of the formally completed symmetric tensor algebra
\hat{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{V})=\prod_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
are identified with formal infinite sums of symmetric tensors, and correspond with formal power series on
\mathbb{V}^{\ast}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(T^{\ast}M)=\oplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(T^{\ast}M)
and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}(T^{\ast}M)=\prod_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(T^{\ast}M)
. Set
\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)=\Gamma(S^{k}(T^{\ast}M))
. It is more convenient to work with the direct product
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)=\prod_{k\geq 0}\!\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
than with
\Gamma(\mathcal{S}(T^{\ast}M))
or the direct sum
\mathsf{S}(M)=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
. Because a section of
\mathcal{S}(T^{\ast}M)
need not have bounded degree, the space
\Gamma(\mathcal{S}(T^{\ast}M))
can be larger than
\mathsf{S}(M)
. On the other hand, the direct product
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
equals the space of sections of
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}(T^{\ast}M)
. An element of
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
is regarded as a formal infinite sum of symmetric tensors on
M
. There are inclusions,
\mathsf{S}(M)\subset\Gamma(\mathcal{S}(M))\subset\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
, as associative algebras. In general, the algebraic constructions considered here are valid on any of these spaces, and, although they are usually formulated in terms of
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
, their reformulations for the smaller spaces are generally trivial or straightforward.

An element
\alpha\in S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
or
\alpha\in\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
is said to have degree
|\alpha|=k
. For all
i\geq 1
, the subspaces
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\mathbb{V})=\oplus_{i\geq k}S^{i}(\mathbb{V})
and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\mathbb{V})=\prod_{i\geq k}S^{i}(\mathbb{V})
are ideals with respect to the commutative algebra structure determined by
\odot
.
The
p=0
case of (
2.2
) extends to a graded symmetric pairing on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{V})
in such a way that
S^{k}(\mathbb{V})
and
S^{l}(\mathbb{V})
are orthogonal if
k\neq l
. Likewise, as commutative algebras,
\mathsf{S}(M)
and
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
are filtered by the ideals
\mathsf{S}_{k}(M)
and
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}_{k}(M)
comprising sums of tensors of degree at least
k
, and the pairing (
2.3
) extends to a graded symmetric pairing on
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
.

3.2.

If
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
is a symplectic vector space, then
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast})
carries a Lie bracket, determining with
\odot
a Poisson algebra, that can be defined most simply as the unique Poisson bracket

on

such that

for all

has degree
-2
. The bracket

can be described explicitly as follows. The space of functions on

carries the Poisson structure determined by
\Omega
. Transporting this Poisson structure to

via the identification of

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)_{i_{1}\dots i_{k+l-2}}

\displaystyle=kl\alpha_{p(i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}\beta_{i_{k}\dots i_{k+l-2})}\,^{p}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{kl}{k+l-2}\left((k-1)\alpha_{pi_{1}(i_{2}\dots i_{k-1}}\beta_{i_{k}\dots i_{k+l-2})}\,^{p}-(l-1)\beta_{pi_{1}(i_{2}\dots i_{l-1}}\alpha_{i_{l}\dots i_{k+l-2})}\,^{p}\right).

In the case

\beta^{i}
in
\alpha

3.1
) extends to
\hat{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast})
with the same definition.

Although it is true by construction that

is a Lie bracket that forms with the symmetric product

is a Lie bracket follows by summing cyclic permutations of the identity

\displaystyle((\alpha,\beta),\gamma)_{i_{1}\dots i_{k+l+m-4}}=klm\left((k-1)\alpha_{pq(i_{1}\dots i_{k-2}}\beta_{i_{k-1}\dots i_{k+l-3}}\,^{p}\gamma_{i_{k+l-2}\dots i_{k+l+m-4})}\,^{q}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hphantom{((\alpha,\beta),\gamma)_{i_{1}\dots i_{k+l+m-4}}=}{}-(l-1)\beta_{pq(i_{1}\dots i_{l-2}}\alpha_{i_{k-1}\dots i_{k+l-3}}\,^{p}\gamma_{i_{k+l-2}\dots i_{k+l+m-4})}\,^{q}\right)

(obtained by using both equalities in (
3.1
)). Similarly, verifying

is straightforward using (

is a Poisson bracket.

Note that
(S^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast}),(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,))
is a Lie subalgebra of
(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast}),(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,))
. Using the easily checked invariance
\Omega((\alpha,\beta),\gamma)+\Omega(\beta,(\alpha,\gamma))=0
for

and

defined by

is a Lie algebra isomorphism onto the symplectic Lie algebra

(see Section 2 of
[

Remark 3.1
.

The Weyl algebra of the symplectic vector space
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
is the quotient of the tensor algebra of
\mathbb{V}
by the ideal generated by elements of the form

for

\mathbb{V}

and carries a Poisson bracket defined by projecting the commutator of elements of level

and

Remark 3.2
.

Another construction of (

Remark 3.3
.

A horizontal distribution on a symplectic fibration such that the corresponding parallel transport preserves the fiberwise symplectic structure is also called a
symplectic connection
(see for example
[
16
]
). The symplectic affine connections considered here are symplectic connections in this more general sense, although the converse is clearly false. For this reason (and also to honor their role in Fedosov’s deformation quantization) what are here called
symplectic connections
are sometimes called
Fedosov connections
; here the notions are distinguished terminologically as
linear
and
nonlinear
symplectic connections.

The two notions encapsulate different points of view. Each is equivalent to the data of a principal connection on what might be called the symplectic frame bundle of
M
, the point being that
symplectic frame bundle
has two possible meanings, depending on whether
symplectic
refers to the symplectic linear group or the symplectomorphism group of the reference symplectic vector space
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
. The linear symplectic frame bundle is the reduction of the usual linear frame bundle determined by restricting to symplectic frames. A much flabbier notion is obtained by instead considering as frames at
p\in M
all symplectomorphisms from
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
to
(T_{p}M,\Omega)
mapping
0\in\mathbb{V}
to
0\in T_{p}M
. Although to define such a nonlinear symplectic frame bundle rigorously requires restricting the class of symplectomorphisms considered, or working formally, e.g., with infinite jets, morally there results a principal bundle with structure group an infinite-dimensional group of (perhaps formal) symplectomorphisms of
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
, and, by construction,
\mathscr{F}
is a reduction of this bundle. Correspondingly, there are two quite distinct notions of symmetries of
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. The usual one is to consider principal bundle automorphisms of the linear symplectic frame bundle
\mathscr{F}
, regarded as a principal bundle for the finite-dimensional group
G=\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
of linear symplectic transformations. On the other hand, the symplectic frame bundle
\mathscr{F}
can be regarded as a subbundle of a principal bundle with structure group some infinite-dimensional Lie group
\widehat{G}
of symplectomorphisms of
(\mathbb{V},\Omega)
fixing the origin.

The automorphism group of
\mathscr{F}
comprises the
G
-equivariant bundle maps from
\mathscr{F}
to itself. The
group
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})
of gauge transformations
of
\mathscr{F}
is its subgroup comprising bundle automorphisms of
\mathscr{F}
covering the identity. A gauge transformation is naturally identified with a map from
\mathscr{F}
to
G
, equivariant with respect to the action of
G
on itself by conjugation, or, equivalently, with a section of the bundle
\mathscr{F}\times_{G}G
associated with
\mathscr{F}
by this action. Via this identification the group structure corresponds to fiberwise composition. An
infinitesimal gauge transformation

3.5.

The Schouten bracket of contravariant symmetric tensors on a smooth manifold

is defined by regarding such tensors as functions on the cotangent bundle

polynomial in the fibers and pairing them via the canonical Poisson bracket. When
M
is symplectic the resulting Poisson algebra structure can be transferred via the symplectic form to the algebra of covariant symmetric tensors. The resulting degree
-1

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket_{i_{1}\dots i_{k+l-1}}=-k\alpha_{p(i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}\nabla^{p}\beta_{i_{k}\dots i_{k+l-1})}+l\beta_{p(i_{1}\dots i_{l-1}}\nabla^{p}\alpha_{i_{l}\dots i_{k+l-1})}.

(3.7)

By the definition (
3.7
), the Schouten bracket of functions is trivial. In (
3.7
) the signs are chosen so that
\llbracket X^{\curlyvee},Y^{\curlyvee}\rrbracket=[X,Y]^{\curlyvee}
for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. More generally, if
X\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
then
\llbracket X^{\curlyvee},\alpha\rrbracket=\mathfrak{L}_{X}\alpha
. In particular,
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathsf{H}_{f}}\alpha=\llbracket\alpha,df\rrbracket=\llbracket\delta^{\ast}f,\alpha\rrbracket
for

. Since

is a Lie algebra, the Schouten bracket of closed one-forms is a closed one-form.
The operator

is a homomorphism from

to

\displaystyle\llbracket\delta^{\ast}f,\delta^{\ast}g\rrbracket=\llbracket df,dg\rrbracket=-d\{f,g\}=\delta^{\ast}\{f,g\},\qquad\text{for}\quad f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

(3.8)

Since the pairing (

) does not depend on the choice of

\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
-equivariant. The corresponding infinitesimal statement, the infinitesimal symplectomorphism equivariance of the Schouten bracket, is equivalent to the statement that the Lie derivative
\mathfrak{L}_{X}
along

is a derivation of

\displaystyle=-(\delta^{\ast}f,\delta^{\ast}g)+\delta^{\ast}\{f,g\}=-\{f,g\}+\delta^{\ast}\{f,g\}=\iota(\{f,g\}),

showing that
\mathsf{h}(M)=\iota(C^{\infty}(M))
is a Lie subalgebra of
(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
that is isomorphic to
(C^{\infty}(M),\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\})
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. For a symplectic vector field

,

1)

the Lie derivative
\mathfrak{L}_{X}
along
X
is a derivation of the algebraic bracket
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
;

2)

the operator

defined by
I(X)\alpha=k\iota(X)\alpha=-(X^{\curlyvee},\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
is a derivation of the Schouten bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=X^{\curlyvee}
,
\beta\in\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
, and
\gamma\in\mathsf{S}^{l}(M)
. By (
3.10
) and the definition of

,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{X}(\beta,\gamma)-(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\beta,\gamma)-(\beta,\mathfrak{L}_{X}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\alpha,(\beta,\gamma)\rrbracket-(\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket,\gamma)-(\beta,\llbracket\alpha,\gamma\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=-(\alpha,\llbracket\beta,\gamma\rrbracket)+\llbracket(\alpha,\beta),\gamma\rrbracket+\llbracket\beta,(\alpha,\gamma)\rrbracket

\displaystyle=I(X)\llbracket\beta,\gamma\rrbracket-\llbracket I(X)\beta,\gamma\rrbracket-\llbracket\beta,I(X)\gamma\rrbracket.

(3.20)

By (

), (

) and (

\displaystyle(\delta^{\ast}f,\llbracket\beta,\gamma\rrbracket)-\llbracket(\delta^{\ast}f,\beta),\gamma\rrbracket-\llbracket\beta,(\delta^{\ast}f,\gamma)\rrbracket=\llbracket f,\llbracket\beta,\gamma\rrbracket\rrbracket-\llbracket\llbracket f,\beta\rrbracket,\gamma\rrbracket-\llbracket\beta,\llbracket f,\gamma\rrbracket\rrbracket=0.

∎

Let
\pi_{k}\colon\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)\to\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
be the projection with image

. Define

by

is a Lie bracket on
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)

\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(k)}
and

.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. For

, the Lie brackets
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(k)}
and
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{(k+1)}
on

are compatible.

Proof.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\widehat{\mathsf{S}}_{k+1}(M)
, by the definitions of

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket_{(k)}

\displaystyle=\llbracket\pi_{k}\alpha,\pi_{k}\beta\rrbracket=(\delta^{\ast}\pi_{k}\alpha,\pi_{k}\beta)+(\pi_{k}\alpha,\delta^{\ast}\pi_{k}\beta)-\delta^{\ast}(\pi_{k}\alpha,\pi_{k}\beta)

∎

That an arbitrary vector field need not preserve
\Omega

of
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)

(the symplectic dual
\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)^{\curlyvee}

\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{B}}(M)=\big\{\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)\colon\alpha_{1}\,\,\text{is exact}\big\}\subset\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}(M)=\big\{\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)\colon d\alpha_{1}=0\big\}.

(3.21)

If
\alpha,\beta\in\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}(M)

\widehat{\mathsf{S}}(M)
with respect to the bracket
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{(2)}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. The Lie brackets
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(1)}
and
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{(2)}
on
\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}(M)
are compatible and
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}(M)
is an ideal in
\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}(M)
with respect to each of
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(1)}
and
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{(2)}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}(M)
. Then, using
\pi_{1}\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\pi_{2}\alpha
,

\displaystyle\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\big((\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket_{(1)},\gamma)_{(2)}+\llbracket(\alpha,\beta)_{(2)},\gamma\rrbracket_{(1)}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\big((\pi_{2}\llbracket\pi_{1}\alpha,\pi_{1}\beta\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+\llbracket\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta),\pi_{1}\gamma\rrbracket\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\left((\pi_{2}\llbracket\alpha_{1}+\pi_{2}\alpha,\beta_{1}+\pi_{2}\beta\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+\llbracket\pi_{2}(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta),\gamma_{1}+\pi_{2}\gamma\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\big((\llbracket\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+\llbracket(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta),\pi_{2}\gamma\rrbracket+(\pi_{2}\llbracket\alpha_{1},\pi_{2}\beta\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+(\pi_{2}\llbracket\pi_{2}\alpha,\beta_{1}\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+(\pi_{2}\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+\llbracket\pi_{2}(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta),\gamma_{1}\rrbracket\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\left(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\pi_{2}\beta\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+(\llbracket\pi_{2}\alpha,\beta_{1}\rrbracket,\pi_{2}\gamma)+\llbracket(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta),\gamma_{1}\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\underset{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{\operatorname{Cycle}}\big((\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\pi_{2}\beta,\pi_{2}\gamma)+(\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\pi_{2}\beta,\pi_{2}\gamma)-\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}(\pi_{2}\beta,\pi_{2}\gamma)\big)=0,

the last equality because, by Lemma

, the Lie derivative along a symplectic vector field is a derivation of the algebraic bracket
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
. This shows
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(1)}
and

are compatible. If

and

such that

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket_{(1)\,1}=\llbracket\pi_{1}\alpha,\pi_{1}\beta\rrbracket_{1}=\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket=\llbracket\beta_{1},df\rrbracket=\mathfrak{L}_{\beta_{1}^{\curlywedge}}df=d\mathfrak{L}_{\beta_{1}^{\curlywedge}}f.

Hence
\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket\in\widehat{\mathsf{B}}(M)
.
∎

By Lemma

\displaystyle[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\operatorname{\top}}=(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{(2)}+\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{(1)}

(3.22)

(the subscript

is meant to suggest

and
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}(M)
is an ideal of

. Because

for all

.

shows that the Lie algebra

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. The sequence

\displaystyle\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{B}}_{1}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\operatorname{\top}}\big)\longrightarrow\{0\}

(3.23)

of Lie algebras is exact, where
\iota(c)
is the constant function on
M
equal to
-c
.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
,
\pi_{1}\alpha=0
if and only if
\alpha_{i}=0
for
i\geq 2
and
\alpha_{0}
is constant. As
\pi_{1}
maps
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
surjectively onto
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}_{1}(M)
, the content of the claim is that
\pi_{1}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
. The identity (
3.19
) can be restated as

\displaystyle\pi_{2}[\alpha,\beta]=(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta)+\pi_{2}\llbracket\pi_{1}\alpha,\pi_{1}\beta\rrbracket=\pi_{2}[\alpha,\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}}.

The identity (
3.18
) shows
[\alpha,\beta]_{1}=\delta^{\ast}\{\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}\,1}

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2}\alpha,\pi_{2}\beta)_{1}+\llbracket\pi_{1}\alpha,\pi_{1}\beta\rrbracket_{1}=\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\llbracket\delta^{\ast}\alpha_{0},\delta^{\ast}\beta_{0}\rrbracket=\delta^{\ast}\{\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\}=[\alpha,\beta]_{1}.

Hence
\pi_{1}[\alpha,\beta]=\pi_{1}[\alpha,\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}}=[\alpha,\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}}=[\pi_{1}\alpha,\pi_{1}\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}}
.
∎

The subspace
\widehat{\mathsf{S}}_{2}(M)
is a Lie ideal of each of

and

of the brackets

4
Functionals on the symplectic affine space
\boldsymbol{(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})}

of symplectic connections

This section reviews the definitions and characterizations of the preferred and critical symplectic connections.

4.1.

The Lie derivative of the affine connection
\nabla
along
X
,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{ij}\,^{k}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X^{k}+X^{p}R_{pij}\,^{k}

(4.1)

is defined to be the derivative at
t=0
of the pullback
\phi_{t}^{\ast}\nabla
along the flow
\phi_{t}
of
X
. Given
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
, define
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M)\to T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
by
\mathscr{L}(X^{\curlyvee})_{ijk}=(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{(ijk)}
for
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. Since
2(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{i[jk]}=\nabla_{i}dX^{\curlyvee}_{jk}
,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{ijk}=(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{(ijk)}+\tfrac{2}{3}\nabla_{(i}dX^{\curlyvee}_{j)k}=\mathscr{L}(X^{\curlyvee})_{ijk}+\tfrac{2}{3}\nabla_{(i}dX^{\curlyvee}_{j)k}.

If
X\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
, then
(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{ijk}
is completely symmetric, so, in this case,
\mathscr{L}(X^{\curlyvee})_{ijk}=(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{ijk}
.
Define a linear operator
\mathscr{S}\colon\mathsf{S}^{3}(M)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M)
by
\mathscr{S}(\beta)_{i}=\beta^{abc}R_{iabc}
. The formal adjoint
\mathscr{S}^{\ast}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M)\to\mathsf{S}^{3}(M)
is
\mathscr{S}^{\ast}(\alpha)_{ijk}=-\alpha^{p}R_{p(ijk)}
. For
\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M)
,
\mathscr{L}(\alpha)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\alpha)_{ijk}=\nabla_{(i}\nabla_{j}\alpha_{k)}+\alpha^{p}R_{p(ijk)}=\big(\delta^{\ast\,2}\alpha\big)_{ijk}-\mathscr{S}^{\ast}(\alpha)_{ijk}.

The induced action of
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is given by
\mathscr{H}\colon C^{\infty}(M)\to T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(f)=\mathfrak{L}_{\mathsf{H}_{f}}\nabla=\mathscr{L}(-df)=\mathscr{L}(\delta^{\ast}f)=\big(\delta^{\ast\,3}f\big)-\mathscr{S}^{\ast}(\delta^{\ast}f).

Taking formal adjoints shows
\mathscr{L}^{\ast}=-\delta^{2}-\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}=(\mathscr{L}\delta^{\ast})^{\ast}=\delta\mathscr{L}^{\ast}=-\delta^{3}-\delta\mathscr{S}
. Tracing (
4.1
) and using the Ricci identity twice yields that, for
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\delta\mathscr{L}(X^{\curlyvee})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{p}(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\nabla)_{ij}\,^{p}=\nabla_{p}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X^{p}+X^{q}\nabla_{p}R_{qij}\,^{p}+R_{qij}\,^{p}\nabla_{p}X^{q}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{j}X^{p}+R_{iq}\nabla_{j}X^{q}+2X^{q}\nabla_{[q}R_{i]j}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{p}X^{p}+R_{jq}\nabla_{i}X^{q}+R_{iq}\nabla_{j}X^{q}+X^{q}\nabla_{q}R_{ij}+2X^{q}\nabla_{i}R_{[jq]}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{L}_{X}\mathsf{Ric})_{ij}.

(4.2)

Taking
X=\mathsf{H}_{f}
in (
4.2
) yields
\delta\mathscr{H}(f)=\mathfrak{L}_{\mathsf{H}_{f}}\mathsf{Ric}
.

4.2.

The
Cahen–Gutt moment map

\mathscr{K}\colon\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\to C^{\infty}(M)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(\nabla)=-\delta^{2}\mathsf{Ric}-\tfrac{1}{2}R^{ij}R_{ij}+\tfrac{1}{4}R^{ijkl}R_{ijkl}=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}R_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{2}R^{ij}R_{ij}+\tfrac{1}{4}R^{ijkl}R_{ijkl}.

(4.3)

The map
\mathscr{K}
was defined by M. Cahen and S. Gutt in
[
7
]
(see also
[
2
]
or
[
13
]
), where it was shown that
\mathscr{K}
is a moment map for the action of
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\Omega)
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. The map
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)
has the form required by Tamarkin’s theorem; its part quadratic in the curvature is a constant multiple of the contraction of the first Pontryagin form
\mathsf{p}_{1}(\nabla)
of
\nabla
with
\Omega_{2}
:
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)=-\delta^{2}\mathsf{Ric}-(\pi^{2}/2)\mathsf{p}_{1}(\nabla)_{p}\,^{p}\,{}_{q}\,^{q}
. As a consequence, when
M
is compact the integral of
\mathscr{K}(\nabla)
depends only on the cohomology class
[\Omega_{n-2}]
of
\Omega_{n-2}
and the first Pontryagin class
[\mathsf{p}_{1}]
of
M
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathscr{K}(\nabla)\,\Omega_{n}=-4\pi^{2}\int_{M}\mathsf{p}_{1}\wedge\Omega_{n-2}=-4\pi^{2}\langle[\mathsf{p}_{1}]\cup[\Omega_{n-2}],[M]\rangle.

(4.4)

(See
[
10
]
for details.) Since integration against
f\Omega_{n}
defines a linear functional on
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, whatever is the precise meaning of the dual space
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)^{\ast}
, this space contains
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)
as a subspace. Hence
\mathscr{K}
can be regarded as a map
\mathscr{K}\colon\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\to\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)^{\ast}
.

The first variation
\updelta_{\Pi}\mathcal{F}(\nabla)
of a functional
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
at
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
in the direction of
\Pi\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is defined by
\updelta_{\Pi}\mathcal{F}(\nabla)=\tfrac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\mathcal{F}(\nabla+t\Pi)
. The essential content of Theorem
4.1
is that
\updelta_{\Pi}\mathscr{K}_{\nabla}=\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{\nabla}(\Pi)
.

Theorem 4.1

(M. Cahen and S. Gutt,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. The map
\mathscr{K}\colon\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\to\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)^{\ast}
is a moment map for the action of
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\Omega)
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
, equivariant with respect to the actions of
\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
.

Indication of proof.

For a complete proof see
[
10
]
. Let
\bar{R}_{ijk}\,^{l}
be the curvature of
\bar{\nabla}=\nabla+t\Pi_{ij}\,^{k}
and label with a
\bar{\,}
the tensors derived from it; for example
\bar{R}_{ij}
is the Ricci curvature of
\bar{\nabla}
. Let
B(\Pi)_{ij}=\Pi_{ip}\,^{q}\Pi_{jq}\,^{p}
and
(\Pi^{\ast}\Pi)_{i}=3\delta B(\Pi)_{i}-\Pi^{abc}\nabla_{i}\Pi_{abc}
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}+2t\nabla_{[i}\Pi_{j]kl}+2t^{2}\Pi_{pl[i}\Pi_{j]k}\,^{p}=R_{ijkl}+td_{\nabla}\Pi_{ijkl}+t^{2}(\Pi\wedge\Pi)_{ijkl},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{ij}=R_{ij}+t\nabla_{p}\Pi_{ij}\,^{p}-t^{2}\Pi_{ip}\,^{q}\Pi_{jq}\,^{p}=R_{ij}+t\delta\Pi_{ij}-t^{2}B(\Pi)_{ij},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\nabla}}^{\ast}\overline{\mathsf{Ric}}=\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\mathsf{Ric}+t\big(\delta^{\ast}\delta\Pi+\tfrac{1}{3}(\mathsf{Ric},\Pi)\big)+t^{2}\big({-}\delta^{\ast}B(\Pi)+\tfrac{1}{3}(\delta\Pi,\Pi)\big)-\tfrac{1}{3}t^{3}(B(\Pi),\Pi),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\delta_{\nabla+t\Pi}\mathsf{Ric}(\nabla+t\Pi)_{i}=\delta_{\nabla}\mathsf{Ric}_{i}-t(\mathscr{L}^{\ast}(\Pi)_{i}+\mathcal{T}(\Pi)_{i})-t^{2}\left(\delta B(\Pi)_{i}+\Pi_{i}\,^{pq}\delta\Pi_{pq}\right)+O\big(t^{3}\big),\!\!\!\!

(4.8)

where
\mathcal{T}(\beta)_{i}=\beta^{abc}(R_{i(abc)}-\Omega_{i(a}R_{bc)})=\beta_{i}\,^{pq}R_{pq}+\mathscr{S}(\beta)_{i}
. Combining (
4.5
)–(
4.8
) yields

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(\nabla+t\Pi)=\mathscr{K}(\nabla)+t\mathscr{H}^{\ast}(\Pi)+\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\delta(\Pi^{\ast}\Pi)+O\big(t^{3}\big),

(4.9)

For
\phi\in\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
,
\mathscr{K}(\phi^{\ast}\nabla)=\mathscr{K}(\nabla)\circ\phi
, so
\langle\!\langle f\circ\phi,\mathscr{K}(\phi^{\ast}\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle f,\mathscr{K}(\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle
. This shows
\mathscr{K}
is equivariant with respect to the action of
\operatorname{Symp}(M,\Omega)
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
and its action on
\operatorname{ham}(M,\Omega)^{\ast}
induced by its action on
C^{\infty}(M)
. For
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,
\Pi\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,
X\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
, and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
it follows from (
4.9
) that
\updelta_{\Pi}\mathscr{K}_{\nabla}=\mathscr{H}_{\nabla}^{\ast}(\Pi)
. Hence
\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}(\mathscr{H}(f),\Pi)=\langle\!\langle f,\mathscr{H}^{\ast}(\Pi)\rangle\!\rangle=\updelta_{\Pi}\langle\!\langle f,\mathscr{K}(\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle
, showing that
\mathscr{K}
is a moment map.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
f\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R})
, the Hamiltonian vector field on
(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})
generated by
\mathscr{E}_{f}(\nabla)=\int_{M}f(\mathscr{K}(\nabla))\,\Omega_{n}
is
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{E}_{f}}=-\mathscr{H}(f^{\prime}(\mathscr{K}(\nabla)))
.

Proof.

Calculating the first variation
\updelta_{\Pi}\mathscr{E}_{f}(\nabla)
along
\Pi\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
using (
4.9
) yields

\displaystyle\updelta_{\Pi}\mathscr{E}_{f}=\langle\!\langle f^{\prime}(\mathscr{K}(\nabla)),\mathscr{H}^{\ast}(\Pi)\rangle\!\rangle=\mathbb{\Omega}_{\nabla}(\mathscr{H}(f^{\prime}(\mathscr{K}(\nabla))),\Pi),

(4.10)

from which the claim follows.
∎

A symplectic connection
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
is

5.1.

Recall from Remark

that
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})
is the group of gauge transformations of the linear symplectic frame bundle

\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})

\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)\times\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})\to\mathbb{T}(M,\Omega)
, on the associated covariant derivatives given by

\displaystyle g\cdot\nabla=\nabla+(g^{-1})_{p}\,^{k}\nabla_{i}g_{j}\,^{p}=\nabla-g^{kp}\nabla_{i}g_{jp},

(5.1)

where
g_{iq}g_{j}\,^{q}=g_{i}\,^{p}g_{j}\,^{q}\Omega_{pq}=\Omega_{ij}
and

preserve the symplectic structure, but need not be torsion-free, and the action of
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})
does not preserve the torsion, for, by (

is torsion-free, then

is torsion-free if and only if

) is affine (as in Section

). Its linear part

, and there follows

, showing that the action of
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})
on

is symplectic affine. However, there is no obvious way to modify the action of
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{F})
on

to obtain an action on

Theorem 5.1

\displaystyle\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla+\Pi}\alpha_{2}=\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\alpha_{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}(\alpha_{2},\Pi),

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\nabla+\Pi}(\alpha_{1})=\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}(\nabla+\Pi)=\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\nabla+\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\Pi=\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{1})+\llbracket\alpha_{1},\Pi\rrbracket,

(the first is (
3.11
)) it follows that the map
\alpha\to\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))
associating with
\alpha
the linear endomorphism

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\Pi=q\llbracket\alpha_{1},\Pi\rrbracket+\tfrac{r}{3}(\alpha_{2},\Pi),

of
\mathsf{S}^{3}(M)
, satisfies
\rho(\alpha)(\nabla+\Pi)-\rho(\alpha)\nabla=\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\Pi
. This shows that
\rho(\alpha)
is an affine transformation of
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
with linear part
\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))
, and so, by Lemma
2.1
,
\alpha\cdot
is an affine action with linear part
\mathsf{L}(\alpha\cdot)=\operatorname{Id}+\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))
. By the
\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
-equivariance of
\mathbb{\Omega}
and (
3.4
), for
\Pi,\Upsilon\in T_{\nabla}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{\Omega}_{\rho(\alpha)\nabla}(\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\Pi,\Upsilon)+\mathbb{\Omega}_{\rho(\alpha)\nabla}(\Pi,\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\Upsilon)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=q\left(\langle\!\langle\llbracket\alpha_{1},\Pi\rrbracket,\Upsilon\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\Pi,\llbracket\alpha_{1},\Upsilon\rrbracket\rangle\!\rangle\right)+\tfrac{r}{3}\left(\langle\!\langle(\alpha_{2},\Pi),\Upsilon\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\Pi,(\alpha_{2},\Upsilon)\rangle\!\rangle\right)=0.

This shows that
\rho(\alpha)
is symplectic affine for all
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{B}}_{1}(M)
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\widehat{\mathsf{B}}_{1}(M)
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla=q\llbracket\alpha_{1},\rho(\beta)\nabla\rrbracket+\tfrac{r}{3}(\alpha_{2},\rho(\beta)\nabla)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla}{}=q\llbracket\alpha_{1},q\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1})+r\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2}+s\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\tfrac{r}{3}(\alpha_{2},q\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1})+r\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2}+s\beta_{3})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla}{}=q^{2}\llbracket\alpha_{1},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1})\rrbracket+qr\left(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\tfrac{1}{3}(\alpha_{2},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1}))\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla=}{}+qs\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\tfrac{r^{2}}{3}(\alpha_{2},\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2})+\tfrac{rs}{3}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{3}).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla-\mathsf{L}(\rho(\beta))\rho(\alpha)\nabla=q^{2}\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1})\rrbracket+\llbracket\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{1}),\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)

\displaystyle{}+qr\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\rrbracket+\tfrac{1}{3}(\alpha_{2},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1}))+\tfrac{1}{3}(\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{1}),\beta_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle{}+qs\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)+\tfrac{r^{2}}{3}\big((\alpha_{2},\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2})+(\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\big)+\tfrac{rs}{3}\big((\alpha_{2},\beta_{3})+(\alpha_{3},\beta_{2})\big).

(5.12)

Since
d\alpha_{1}=0=d\beta_{1}
,
\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge},\beta_{1}^{\curlywedge}\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
, and

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha_{1},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1})\rrbracket+\llbracket\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{1}),\beta_{1}\rrbracket=[\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}},\mathfrak{L}_{\beta_{1}^{\curlywedge}}]\nabla=\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket).

(5.13)

Using (
5.13
) and (
3.9
) to simplify (
5.12
) yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla-\mathsf{L}(\rho(\beta))\rho(\alpha)\nabla

\displaystyle{}=q^{2}\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket)+qr\big(\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\beta_{2}-\tfrac{1}{3}(\beta_{2},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{1}))-\mathfrak{L}_{\beta_{1}^{\curlywedge}}\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\alpha_{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}(\alpha_{2},\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\beta_{1}))\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+qs\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)+\tfrac{r^{2}}{3}\big(\llbracket\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\big)+\tfrac{rs}{3}\big((\alpha_{2},\beta_{3})+(\alpha_{3},\beta_{2})\big).

(5.14)

By (
3.11
), there holds
\delta^{\ast}_{\phi_{t}^{\ast}(\nabla)}\gamma-\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\gamma=\tfrac{1}{3}(\gamma,\phi_{t}^{\ast}\nabla-\nabla)
for
\gamma\in\mathsf{S}^{k}(M)
and the local flow
\phi_{t}
generated by
Z\in\operatorname{symp}(M,\Omega)
. Since
\delta^{\ast}_{\phi_{t}^{\ast}(\nabla)}\gamma=\phi_{t}^{\ast}(\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\phi_{-t}^{\ast}(\gamma))
, differentiating at
t=0
yields

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{L}_{Z},\delta^{\ast}]\gamma=(\mathfrak{L}_{Z}\delta^{\ast})\gamma=\tfrac{1}{3}(\gamma,\mathscr{L}(Z^{\curlyvee})).

(5.15)

Applying (
5.15
) to simplify (
5.14
) yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)\nabla-\mathsf{L}(\rho(\beta))\rho(\alpha)\nabla=q^{2}\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket)+r\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\big(q\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)+\tfrac{r}{3}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\big)\!

\displaystyle\qquad{}+qs\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)+\tfrac{r^{2}}{3}\llbracket\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\tfrac{rs}{3}\big((\alpha_{2},\beta_{3})+(\alpha_{3},\beta_{2})\big).

Hence

\displaystyle\big(\mathsf{L}(\rho(\alpha))\rho(\beta)-\mathsf{L}(\rho(\beta))\rho(\alpha)-\rho([\alpha,\beta]_{\operatorname{\top}}\big)\nabla

\displaystyle{}=q(q-1)\mathscr{L}_{\nabla}(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\rrbracket)+r\delta^{\ast}_{\nabla}\big((q-1)\left(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\right)+\tfrac{r-3}{3}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+(q-1)s\big(\llbracket\alpha_{1},\beta_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\rrbracket\big)+(\tfrac{r^{2}}{3}-s)\llbracket\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\rrbracket+\tfrac{s(r-3)}{3}\big((\alpha_{2},\beta_{3})+(\alpha_{3},\beta_{2})\big).

This vanishes if
q=1
and
r=3=s
. This yields (
5.9
) and shows that (
5.9
) defines a symplectic affine action. That the stabilizer of
\nabla\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
has the form (
5.10
) is straightforward.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic manifold. Let
(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be the Lie algebra defined by (
3.12
) and (

). The map

for

and

). By Lemmas
3.11

∎

Remark 5.6
.

Consider the more general map

6
Hamiltonian action of the extended Lie algebra

on
\boldsymbol{(\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega),\mathbb{\Omega})}

In this section
M
is supposed compact. This is needed only to guarantee convergence of the integrals that appear. With appropriate qualifications, everything extends to noncompact

, but this requires fussing that would be distracting here.

6.1.

A reference connection
\nabla_{0}\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
and a fixed
\Pi\in T_{\nabla_{0}}\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
determine a function
\mathbb{\Theta}_{\nabla_{0},\Pi}(\nabla)=\langle\!\langle\Pi,\nabla-\nabla_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
. Since

is that generating the flow on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
given by translation by
-t\Pi
.

Given

,
\nabla_{0}\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
, and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}(M)
define
\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}(\nabla)=p\langle\!\langle\alpha_{0},\mathscr{K}(\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle+q\mathscr{R}_{\alpha_{2}}(\nabla)+r\mathbb{\Theta}_{\nabla_{0},\alpha_{3}}(\nabla).

The dependence of
\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}
on the reference connection
\nabla_{0}
is not indicated so that the notation does not become too cluttered.
By Theorem
4.1
, Lemma
5.2
, and the preceding paragraph, the Hamiltonian vector field
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}}
generated on
\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
by
\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{M}^{p,q,r}_{\alpha}}=-p\mathscr{H}(\alpha_{0})-q\delta^{\ast}\alpha_{2}-r\alpha_{3}.

In the case
p=-1
,
q=3
, and
r=3
, there is written simply
\mathscr{M}
for
\mathscr{M}^{-1,3,3}
. Lemma
6.2
computes the Poisson brackets
\{\!\!\{\mathscr{M}_{\alpha},\mathscr{M}_{\beta}\}\!\!\}
. The following preliminary lemma is needed in its proof.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(M,\Omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold. For
\nabla=\nabla_{0}+\Pi_{ij}\,^{k},\nabla_{0}\in\mathbb{S}(M,\Omega)
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{S}^{2}(M)
, and

as defined in Lemma

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\mathcal{C}(\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle-2\langle\!\langle\delta_{\nabla}\alpha,\delta_{\nabla}\beta\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\mathcal{C}(\nabla_{0})\rangle\!\rangle-2\langle\!\langle\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\beta\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket,\Pi\rangle\!\rangle.

(6.1)

Proof.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{S}^{2}(M)
,
\mathcal{C}(\nabla)=\mathcal{C}(\nabla_{0})+d_{\nabla_{0}}\Pi_{ijkl}+2\Pi_{pl[i}\Pi_{j]k}\,^{p}
, by (
4.5
), so

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\mathcal{C}(\nabla)\rangle\!\rangle-\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\mathcal{C}(\nabla_{0})\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,d_{\nabla_{0}}\Pi+\Pi\wedge\Pi\rangle\!\rangle.

Integrating by parts and using
(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\Pi)=-3(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha)^{p}\Pi_{ijp}
and (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,d_{\nabla_{0}}\Pi\rangle\!\rangle=-\tfrac{2}{3}\langle\!\langle(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\Pi),\beta\rangle\!\rangle+\tfrac{2}{3}\langle\!\langle\alpha,(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\beta,\Pi)\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket,\Pi\rangle\!\rangle.

(6.2)

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{S}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle\alpha^{ab}\beta^{cd}\Pi_{acp}\Pi_{bd}\,^{p}=\alpha^{ba}\beta_{dc}\Pi_{acp}\Pi_{bd}\,^{p}=\alpha^{ab}\beta_{cd}\Pi_{bdp}\Pi_{ac}\,^{p}=-\alpha^{ab}\beta^{cd}\Pi_{acp}\Pi_{bd}\,^{p}

(6.3)

so that
\alpha^{ab}\beta^{cd}\Pi_{acp}\Pi_{bd}\,^{p}=0
. By (
6.3
),

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\Pi\wedge\Pi\rangle\!\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{M}(\alpha\star\beta)_{ijkl}(\Pi\wedge\Pi)^{ijkl}\,\Omega_{n}=2\int\alpha^{ab}\Pi_{abp}\beta^{cd}\Pi_{cd}\,^{p}\,\Omega_{n}.

(6.4)

From

\displaystyle d_{\nabla}\alpha_{[ij]i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}=d_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha_{[ij]i_{1}\dots,i_{k-1}}-(k-1)\big(\Pi_{i(i_{1}}\,^{p}\alpha_{i_{2}\dots i_{k-1})jp}-\Pi_{j(i_{1}}\,^{p}\alpha_{i_{2}\dots i_{k-1})ip}\big),

there follows

\displaystyle\delta_{\nabla}\alpha_{i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}=\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha_{i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}+(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)\Pi^{pq}\,_{(i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}\dots i_{k-1})pq}.

(6.5)

Computing using (
6.5
),
(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\Pi)=-3(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha)^{p}\Pi_{ijp}
, and (
6.4
) yields

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\delta_{\nabla}\alpha,\delta_{\nabla}\beta\rangle\!\rangle-\langle\!\langle\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\beta\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=-\tfrac{1}{3}\langle\!\langle(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\alpha,\Pi),\beta\rangle\!\rangle+\tfrac{1}{3}\langle\!\langle\alpha,(\delta_{\nabla_{0}}\beta,\Pi)\rangle\!\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\!\langle\alpha\star\beta,\Pi\wedge\Pi\rangle\!\rangle.

(6.6)

Combining (
6.2
) and (
6.6
) yields (
6.1
).
∎

Lemma 6.2

\phi_{t}

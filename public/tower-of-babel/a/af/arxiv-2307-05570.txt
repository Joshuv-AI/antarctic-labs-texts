Large deviations for 2D stochastic Navier-Stokes Equations driven by a periodic force and a degenerate noise
Rongchang Liu, Kening Lu

Abstract
. We consider the incompressible 2D Navier-Stokes equations on the torus, driven by a deterministic time periodic force and a noise that is white in time and degenerate in Fourier space. The main result is twofold. Firstly, we establish a Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type theorem for the time inhomogeneous Feynman-Kac evolution operators with regular potentials associated with the stochastic Navier-Stokes system. The theorem characterizes asymptotic behaviors of the Feynman-Kac operators in terms of the periodic family of principal eigenvalues and corresponding unique eigenvectors. The proof involves a time inhomogeneous version of Ruelle’s lower bound technique. Secondly, utilizing this Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type theorem and a Kifer’s criterion, we establish a Donsker-Varadhan type large deviation principle with a nontrivial good rate function for the occupation measures of the time inhomogeneous solution processes.

1.
Introduction

The 2D Navier-Stokes equations subject to a time periodic force has been extensively studied over the years, dating back to Serrin
[
36
]
, Yudovich
[
38
]
and many others, see Galdi and Kyed
[
16
]
for recent progress. It was proved by Da Prato and Debussche
[
10
]
that the periodically forced 2D Navier-Stokes system with a non-degenerate noise has an exponentially mixing periodic invariant measure. Furthermore, it was proved recently
[
28
]
that when the noise is degenerate, the strong law of large numbers and central limit theorem hold. In particular, the central limit theorem describes the small deviations from the periodic invariant measure.

The aim of the current paper is to investigate the probabilities of large deviations from the periodic invariant measure, for the periodically forced 2D Navier-Stokes equations with a degenerate white in time noise. To the best of our knowledge, this work seems to be the first one that establishes Donsker-Varadhan type large deviation results for the occupation measures of the time inhomogeneous solution processes solving a given SPDE.
In what follows, we will briefly describe our results (Theorems
A
and
B
below), while leave the technical preliminaries to the next section.

\displaystyle A_{1}=\{g_{l}:1\leq l\leq d\},A_{k+1}=A_{k}\cup\{\widetilde{B}(u,v):u,v\in A_{k}\},A_{\infty}=\overline{\mathrm{span}(\cup_{k\geq 1}A_{k})},

Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type theorem for the Feynman-Kac operators

In the study of an infinite one dimensional lattice gas model, Ruelle
[
35
]
proved his Perron-Frobenius type theorem for the corresponding transfer operator with Hölder regular potentials. This theorem is now known as Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius (RPF) theorem and has been widely used and extended in the study of dynamical systems and thermodynamic formalism, see Baladi
[
3
]
, Bowen
[
6
]
, Climenhaga and Pesin
[
7
]
and references therein. Ruelle’s ideas
in
[
35
]
were then used by Kuksin and Shirikyan
[
25
]
to show unique ergodicity and asymptotic stability of Markov operators for a class of dissipative PDEs.
Later the same ideas were applied by Jakšić et al.
[
22
]
to the discrete time Feynman-Kac semigroups associated with the models considered in
[
25
]
for which an RPF type theorem was proved in a compact setting. An extension to the non-compact setting was obtained by the same authors in
[
23
]
, which was further extended to continuous time by Martirosyan and Nersesyan
[
29
]
. All the existing results work with time homogeneous Feynman-Kac semigroups.

Our Theorem
A
below gives a version of the RPF type theorem for the time inhomogeneous Feynman-Kac evolution operators of (
1.1
), which is effective in establishing LDP for the time inhomogeneous solution processes. Here the effectiveness means that the eigen-elements are continuous on the time symbols which enables us to establish
the LDP for the original system (
1.1
) from Theorem
A
. Below
\mathcal{P}(H)
is the space of probability measures on
H
.

Theorem A

(RPF Type Theorem)
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R},H_{2})
is
2\pi
-periodic and Lipschitz. For each potential
V\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
there exists a unique family of eigen-triple of the Feynman-Kac operators (
1.3
) associated with the Navier-Stokes family (
1.2
),

\displaystyle(\Gamma^{V},\lambda^{V},F^{V})\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))\times C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})\times C(H\times\mathbb{T}),

with
F^{V}
strictly positive and normalized by
\Gamma^{V}
such that the following properties hold:

(1)

Eigenmeasure/eigenfunction property with positive eigenvalue
\lambda_{t,h,V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V}(\beta_{r}h)dr\right)
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)=\lambda_{t,h,V}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{t}h),\qquad\text{(eigenmeasure property)}\\
&P_{0,t,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w)=\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}(\cdot,h),\quad\text{(eigenfunction property)}\end{split}

for all
h\in\mathbb{T}
and
t\geq 0
.

(2)

Forward and pullback stability: for any
h\in\mathbb{T}
,
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
and
w\in H
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda^{-1}_{t,h,V}P_{0,t,h}^{V}\phi(w)-\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{t}h),\phi\right\rangle F^{V}(w,h)\right|=0,\\
&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda^{-1}_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}\phi(w)-\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle F^{V}(w,\beta_{-t}h)\right|=0.\end{split}

Theorem
A
is a simplified version of Theorem
2.1
, which will be proved in section
3
. Note that when
h=0
the theorem gives the RPF type theorem for the Feynman-Kac operators associated with (
1.1
). Theorem
A
is proved by first showing a weak version (without continuous dependence on time symbols) for the discrete time restrictions and then passing to the whole continuous time.

A major step in proving the weak version is to show the forward
L^{1}
stability after one obtains the existence of the eigen-elements. In
[
25
,
23
]
, this was proved by combining the uniform Feller property and irreducibility with Ruelle’s lower bound technique, which relies on an
L^{1}
non-expansive property. Due to the time inhomogeneity, such non-expansiveness is not available in the current setting. The approach we take in the proof provides a way to tailor the core of Ruelle’s lower bound technique in this situation, to obtain the desired forward
L^{1}
stability. The impact of time inhomogeneity is also reflected throughout the process.

The passage from the weak version to the whole continuous time is accomplished through the eigen-properties and uniqueness. Then we establish the continuity of eigen-triple and the representation of the eigenvalues through a common
\lambda^{V}
.
The continuity of the eigenmeasure (and eigenvalue) is obtained by controlling the lower bound of the evaluation of eigenmeasure along the eigenfunction.
The continuity of the eigenfunction is proved by combining the eigenfunction property (
1.5
) and the time continuity of the Feynman-Kac family, as well as the dynamical structure of the time inhomogeneity.

Donsker-Varadhan type large deviation principle

For time homogeneous Markov processes, asymptotic statistical behaviors can be described by
the strong law of large numbers and central limit theorem when there is a mixing invariant measure, see Kuksin and Shirikyan
[
26
]
for example.
In particular, the central limit theorem characterizes fluctuations of the occupation measures around the invariant measure. A more detailed analysis enables one to derive a LDP that quantifies the exponentially decaying rates of the probabilities of large deviations from the invariant measure,
see for example the pioneering works of Donsker and Varadhan
[
12
,
13
]
.

For time inhomogeneous Markov processes, the asymptotic statistical profile depends on the time inhomogeneity.
It was proved recently
[
28
]
that the statistical dynamics of the time inhomogeneous solution processes for the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) is characterized by an exponential mixing periodic invariant measure
\Gamma\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))
. In addition, one has the following strong law of large numbers and central limit theorem:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\left\langle\Gamma(\beta_{r}0),\phi\right\rangle\big)dr\to 0,\,\text{ almost surely, }

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\left\langle\Gamma(\beta_{r}0),\phi\right\rangle\big)dr=N(0,\sigma^{2}),\,\text{ in distribution, }

where recall that
\beta_{r}
is the circle rotation (
1.2
) and
\Phi_{0,r}
is the solution to (
1.1
). Here
N(0,\sigma^{2})
is a centered normal random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
. The central limit theorem describes the distribution of the fluctuations around the periodic invariant measure.

Our Theorem
B
below gives quantitative information on the probabilities of large deviations from the periodic invariant measure. Denote the occupation measure by

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{(\Phi_{0,r},\beta_{r}0)}dr,

which is a family of random probability measures on
H\times\mathbb{T}
. In the following theorem
\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
is the space of probability measures on
H\times\mathbb{T}
with the topology of weak convergence.

Theorem B

(Large Deviation Principle)
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R},H_{2})
is
2\pi
-periodic and Lipschitz. Then there is a non-trivial good rate function
I:\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})\to[0,\infty]
such that for every initial vorticity
w\in H
, we have

\displaystyle-\inf_{\mu\in\mathring{B}}I(\mu)\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{P}_{w}\{\zeta_{t}\in B\}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{P}_{w}\{\zeta_{t}\in B\}\leq-\inf_{\mu\in\overline{B}}I(\mu),

for any Borel subset
B
in
\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, with
\mathring{B}
and
\overline{B}
its interior and closure.

Theorem
B
is a simplified version of Theorem
2.2
, which will be proved in section
4
. It immediately implies probability bounds for large deviations from the unique periodic invariant measure
\Gamma
. Indeed, for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
and observable function
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
, define the Borel subset
B
of
\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,

B:=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T}):\left\langle\mu,\phi_{\Gamma}\right\rangle\in E\right\},

where
\phi_{\Gamma}(w,h)=\phi(w)-\left\langle\Gamma(h),\phi\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\mu,\phi\right\rangle
denotes the integral of a function
\phi
with respect to a measure
\mu
. Note that

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}\in B\text{ is equivalent to }\left\langle\zeta_{t},\phi_{\Gamma}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\left\langle\Gamma(\beta_{r}0),\phi\right\rangle\big)dr\in E.

Thus by Theorem
B
one obtains when
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle e^{-tL^{-}}\lesssim\mathbf{P}_{w}\left\{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\left\langle\Gamma(\beta_{r}0),\phi\right\rangle\big)dr\in E\right\}\lesssim e^{-tL^{+}},

where
L^{\pm}=L^{\pm}(\phi_{\Gamma},E)\geq 0
are constants determined by the rate function.

The theorem is proved through a combination of the RPF type Theorem
A
and a Kifer’s criterion established by Jakšić et al.
[
23
]
. It is known that existence of the pressure function
and exponential tightness imply the large deviation upper bound with its Legendre transform as the rate function. Such existence in the time homogeneous case is a direct consequence of the stability of Feynman-Kac semigroups but more effort is needed here. The existence here follows by a combination of the forward stability (
1.6
), integral representation for
\lambda_{t,h,V}
in (
1.5
), and the following Birkhoff ergodic theorem for circle rotation with unique invariant Lebesgue measure
m
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V}(\beta_{r}0)dr=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V}(h)m(dh),

which is ensured by the continuity of
\lambda^{V}
established in Theorem
A
. We emphasize that if there is no continuity then Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem is not necessarily true for the time symbol
0
and therefore the existence of the pressure function for the given system (
1.1
) would become an issue.

The large deviation lower bound requires additionally the uniqueness of equilibrium states for a rich class of regular potentials. This is usually done by connecting the equilibrium state with the unique invariant measure of an auxiliary Markov semigroup obtained from the Feynman-Kac semigroup by Doob’s
h
-transform.
We follow this approach but additional issues arise due to time inhomogeneity. Firstly, the Doob’s
h
-transform of the inhomogeneous Feynman-Kac family is still inhomogeneous. To establish the uniqueness of invariant measure for its homogenization
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
, we make a correspondence between their invariant measures, so that we can apply the uniqueness of eigen-triple in Theorem
A
.
Secondly, to utilize the Donsker-Varadhan functional of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
to establish an effective connection between equilibrium states and invariant measures, we show that the domain of the infinitesimal generator of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
is rich enough to be determining. This involves a verification of the Feller property of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
, where the continuity on time symbols of the eigen-triple plays a crucial role.

We end the introduction with a brief review on the Donsker-Varadhan type large deviations from a stationary distribution for 2D Navier-Stokes equations. It is worth mentioning that the first such LDP for SPDEs was given by Gourcy
[
17
,
18
]
. By assuming that the white in time noise is sufficiently irregular in space, he established the LDP for occupation measures of the stochastic Burgers and Navier-Stokes equations, using a general result of Wu
[
37
]
that requires the strong Feller property. Utilizing the approach that combines the asymptotic behavior of Feynman-Kac semigroups with a Kifer type criterion, Jakšić et al.
[
22
]
established the first result on LDP for dissipative SPDEs when the strong Feller property does not hold, including stochastic Navier-Stokes equations perturbed by a smooth bounded random kick force.
The authors in
[
22
]
extended their work to the case of smooth unbounded random kick forces in
[
23
]
,
which was extended by Martirosyan and Nersesyan
[
29
,
30
]
to the continuous time case, giving a proof of the LDP for the 2D Navier-Stokes equation with a non-degenerate white in time noise that is smooth in space. Combining this approach with Malliavin calculus, Nersesyan, Peng and Xu
[
31
]
proved the LDP in the case when there is no deterministic force and the white noise is degenerate in Fourier space. The LDP for 2D Navier-Stokes equations with a space-time localised noise was also considered in Peng and Xu
[
34
]
by using the same approach. The solution processes considered by these works are time homogeneous.

,

(2.2)

\displaystyle dw(t,x)+B(\mathcal{K}w,w)(t,x)dt=\nu\mathrm{\mathrm{\Delta}}w(t,x)dt+f(\beta_{t}h,x)dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}g_{i}dW_{i}(t),\,t>s,\,w(s)=w_{0},

with corresponding solution
\Phi_{s,t,h}(w_{0})
. Note that the symbol
h=0
corresponds to the original equation (
2.1
). For any
s\in\mathbb{R},t\geq 0,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
, It follows from the uniqueness of solution that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,s+t,h}(\omega,w)=\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{s}h}(\theta_{s}\omega,w),\,\mathbf{P}\text{-}\mathrm{a.s.},

where
\theta
is the Wiener shift. The coupled process

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}(w,h)=(\Phi_{0,t,h}(w),\beta_{t}h)

is a homogeneous Markov process on
H\times\mathbb{T}
.

For every symbol
h\in\mathbb{T}
and potential
V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, the associated Feynman-Kac operator
P_{s,t,h}^{V}:C_{b}(H)\to C_{b}(H)
corresponding to (
2.2
) is defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle P_{s,t,h}^{V}\phi(w)=\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}V(\Phi_{s,r,h}(w),\beta_{r}h)dr\right)\phi(\Phi_{s,t,h}(w))\right],\,\phi\in C_{b}(H),w\in H.

It naturally induces by duality an action
P_{s,t,h}^{V*}
on the space
\mathcal{M}_{+}(H)
of non-negative finite Borel measures on
H
,

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{s,t,h}^{V*}\mu,\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle\mu,P_{s,t,h}^{V}\phi\right\rangle,\,\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(H),\phi\in C_{b}(H).

It also satisfies the following evolution relation by the Markov property

\displaystyle P_{u,t,h}^{V}=P_{u,s,h}^{V}P_{s,t,h}^{V},\,\text{ for all }u\leq s\leq t,\,h\in\mathbb{T}.

The translation relation (
2.3
) of solutions yields the following translation identity of
P_{s,t,h}^{V}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle P_{s,s+t,h}^{V}=P_{0,t,\beta_{s}h}^{V},\,s\in\mathbb{R},t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}.

Combining this with the evolution relation we obtain the following cocycle property

(2.7)

\displaystyle P_{0,s+t,h}^{V}=P_{0,s,h}^{V}P_{0,t,\beta_{s}h}^{V},\,s,t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}.

It is also convenient to use the homogenized Feynman-Kac semigroup
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}
acting on
C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
as

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}\phi(w,h)=P_{0,t,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w),\,t\geq 0,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

The Feynman-Kac operators with vanishing potential are the usual Markov operators, which we denote by
P_{s,t,h}:=P^{0}_{s,t,h}
, as well as
\mathcal{P}_{t}:=\mathcal{P}_{t}^{0}
.

2.2.
Main theorems

The following two theorems are our main results under the standing assumption:

(2.9)

\displaystyle f\in C(\mathbb{R},H_{2})\text{ is }2\pi\text{-periodic and Lipschitz};g_{i}\in H_{\infty},\forall 1\leq i\leq d;\text{ and }A_{\infty}=H,

where the Hörmander bracket condition
A_{\infty}=H
is defined as in (
1.4
).

The first result is the following time inhomogeneous Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. Then there is a unique family of eigen-triple

(\Gamma^{V},\lambda^{V},F^{V})
for the Feynman-Kac evolution operators
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
, such that

(\Gamma^{V},\lambda^{V},F^{V})\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))\times C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})\times C(H\times\mathbb{T}),

with
F^{V}
strictly positive everywhere. Moreover, the following properties hold:

(1)

Normalization property:
\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right\rangle=1
for all
h\in\mathbb{T}
.

Eigenmeasure/eigenfunction property,

\displaystyle P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)=\lambda_{t,h,V}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{t}h),\qquad\text{(eigenmeasure property)}

\displaystyle P_{0,t,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w)=\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}(\cdot,h),\quad\text{(eigenfunction property)}

with positive eigenvalue

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lambda_{t,h,V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V}(\beta_{r}h)dr\right),\,\text{ and }\lambda^{V}(h)=\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle,

for all
h\in\mathbb{T}
and
t\geq 0
.

(2)

Stability property: for any
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
\phi\in C_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and
M,R>0
, denoting

\widetilde{\phi}(w,h)=\phi(w,h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle F^{V}(w,h),\quad(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},

then we have for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
and
R>0
,

(a)

Pullback stability:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,h)\right|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,h)\right|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-t}h)}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta^{\prime},M}(H)}\left|\left\langle\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,h),\mu\right\rangle\right|=0,\,\forall\eta^{\prime}\in(\eta,\eta_{0});

(b)

Forward stability:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}=0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta^{\prime},M}(H)}\left|\left\langle\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V}\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h),\mu\right\rangle\right|=0,\,\forall\eta^{\prime}\in(\eta,\eta_{0}).

Remark 2.1
.

Here are some remarks about the theorem.

(1)

In fact, we have

(\Gamma^{V},F^{V})\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}(H))\times C_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}(H\times\mathbb{T}),

for
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and some
q=q(V)\geq 1
, where
\eta_{0}
is from Proposition
B.1
. See Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
.

(2)

If the potential
V=0
then
(\Gamma^{V},\lambda^{V},F^{V})=(\Gamma,0,1)
, where
\Gamma
is the unique periodic invariant measure of the Markov operators
P_{0,t,h}
of system (
2.1
). In this vein, Theorem
2.1
extends the unique ergodicity obtained in
[
28
]
to general Feynman-Kac evolution operators.

The second theorem is a Donsker-Varadhan type large deviation principle for occupation measures of the time inhomogeneous solution processes. Denote the occupation measure by

(2.12)

\displaystyle\zeta_{t,h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{(\Phi_{0,r,h},\beta_{r}h)}dr,

which is a family of random probability measures on
H\times\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
M>0
, the family of random probability measures
\zeta_{t,h}
satisfies the LDP for any
h\in\mathbb{T}
, uniformly with respect to initial measures
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)
, i.e., there is a non-trivial good rate function
I:\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})\to[0,\infty]
that does not depend on
\eta,M
such that for any
h\in\mathbb{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)}\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\{\zeta_{t,h}\in F\}\leq-\inf_{\mu\in F}I(\mu),

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)}\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\{\zeta_{t,h}\in G\}\geq-\inf_{\mu\in G}I(\mu),

for any closed set
F
and open set
G
in
\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Here are some remarks about the theorem.

(1)

The rate function
I
is the Legendre transformation of the pressure function
Q
,

\displaystyle I(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\sup_{V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})}\left(\left\langle V,\mu\right\rangle-Q(V)\right),&\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T}),\\
\qquad+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Here the pressure function
Q:C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{R}
is given by the limit

(2.15)

\displaystyle Q(V)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)}\log\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV,\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle,\text{ for }V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}),

where the limit
Q(V)
does not depend on initial data
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)
and
h\in\mathbb{T}
. The function
Q
is
1
-Lipschitz and convex. In particular,

Q(V)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V}(h)m(dh),\text{ for }V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T}),

where
\lambda^{V}\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
is the eigenvalue obtained in Theorem
2.1
. See Theorem
4.1
.

(2)

For any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
, define the set

K(\phi_{\Gamma},E):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T}):\left\langle\phi_{\Gamma},\mu\right\rangle\in E\right\},

where
\Gamma\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}(H))
is the unique periodic invariant measure of the Navier-Stokes system (
2.1
) obtained in
[
28
]
and
\phi_{\Gamma}(w,h)=\phi(w)-\left\langle\phi,\Gamma(h)\right\rangle
. Note that

\displaystyle\zeta_{t,0}\in K(\phi_{\Gamma},E)\text{ is equivalent to }\left\langle\phi_{\Gamma},\zeta_{t,0}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\int_{H}\phi(w)\Gamma(\beta_{r}0)\right)dr\in E.

Thus by Theorem
2.2
one obtains a version of the LDP involving the unique periodic invariant measure for solution processes of (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\left\{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\int_{H}\phi(w)\Gamma(\beta_{r}0)\right)dr\in E\right\}\leq-L^{+}(\phi_{\Gamma},E),

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\left\{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\phi(\Phi_{0,r})-\int_{H}\phi(w)\Gamma(\beta_{r}0)\right)dr\in E\right\}\geq-L^{-}(\phi_{\Gamma},E),

for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)
, where

\displaystyle L^{-}(\phi_{\Gamma},E)=\inf_{\mu\in K(\phi_{\Gamma},\mathring{E})}I(\mu),\,\text{ and }L^{+}(\phi_{\Gamma},E)=\inf_{\mu\in K(\phi_{\Gamma},\overline{E})}I(\mu).

The central limit theorem proved in
[
28
]
implies that
\sqrt{t}\left\langle\phi_{\Gamma},\zeta_{t,0}\right\rangle
is asymptotically Gaussian, which describes small deviations. Here the LDP gives quantitative information on the probabilities of large deviations.

3.
A Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type theorem

The aim of this section is to prove the Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius type Theorem
2.1
on the asymptotic behavior of the Feynman-Kac evolution operators
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
in terms of the corresponding eigen-triple. We first show the existence of eigen-triple of the discrete time operators
P_{0,k,h}^{V},k\in\mathbb{N}
in the first subsection
3.1
. Then in subsection
3.2
we prove the forward and pullback stability of the eigen-triple for
P_{0,k,h}^{V}
. Subsection
3.3
is devoted to the proof of uniquenesses and certain regularity properties of the eigen-triple. The proof of Theorem
2.1
will be given in subsection
3.4
.

3.1.
Existence of eigen-triple

In this subsection we establish the existence of eigen-triple for the discrete time operators
P_{0,k,h}^{V},k\in\mathbb{N}
. In Proposition
3.1
we prove the existence of eigenmeasure and eigenvalue by combining the idea of pullback and a Schauder-Tychonoff’s fixed point theorem. Then in Proposition
3.2
we construct the corresponding eigenfunction by utilizing the eigenmeasure and eigenvalue, as well as a Krylov–Bogolyubov averaging procedure.

For
\eta,A>0
, let
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
be the closure of
C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))
with respect to the topology of point-wise convergence. Using the idea of pull-back we define the following operator family

(3.1)

\displaystyle S_{t}^{V}(\gamma)(h):=\frac{P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-t}h)}{\big(P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-t}h)\big)(H)},\quad(t,h)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T},\gamma\in\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}.

It is straightforward to verify that this family of operators has the semigroup property, whose fixed point gives the desired eigenmeasure.

Proposition 3.1

(Existence of Eigenmeasure and eigenvalue)
.

Assume
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
. Then there is a constant
A>0
such that the operator
S_{1}^{V}
maps
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
into itself, in which it has a fixed point
\Gamma^{V}
. Denote
\lambda_{k,h,V}=P_{0,k,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H)
. Then
\Gamma^{V}
has the following properties:

(1)

Eigenmeasure property:

P_{0,k,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)=\lambda_{k,h,V}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h),\,(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}.

Cocycle property of eigenvalues
\lambda_{k,h,V}
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{m+\ell,h,V}=\lambda_{m,\beta_{\ell}h,V}\lambda_{\ell,h,V},\text{ and }\lambda_{m,h,V}=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{1,\beta_{k}h,V}.

(2)

Support property: for each
h\in\mathbb{T}
, the support of
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is
H
. In addition, for any
\rho,r>0
, there is a number
p=p(\rho,r,V)>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w,h)\in B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}\times\mathbb{T}}\Gamma^{V}(h)(B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w))\geq p.

Moreover, there is a large
L_{0}
such that for any
L\geq L_{0}
, there holds

(3.4)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w,h)\in B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}\times\mathbb{T}}\Gamma^{V}(h)(B_{r}^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(w))\geq p/2.

(3)

Concentration property: for each
h\in\mathbb{T}
, the measure
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
, i.e.
\Gamma^{V}(h)(H_{2})=1
. There is a constant
C
such that for any
R>0
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}})\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is divided into the following four steps.

Step 1: Show that
S_{1}^{V}
maps
C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))
into itself for appropriately chosen
A
. Let
\gamma\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))
and recall from the notation that
m_{V},M_{V}
the infimum and supremum of
V
. We first observe that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0<e^{m_{V}}\leq&\big(P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)\big)(H)\leq e^{M_{V}},\forall h\in\mathbb{T}.\end{split}

By estimate (
B.1
) and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}\left(S_{1}^{V}(\gamma)(h)\right)(dw)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{H}\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}V\big(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,\beta_{-1}h)\big)dr+\eta\|\Phi_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}(w)\|^{2}\right)\right]\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)}{\big(P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)\big)(H)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{M_{V}-m_{V}}\int_{H}\mathbf{E}e^{\eta\|\Phi_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}(w)\|^{2}}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{M_{V}-m_{V}}\int_{H}e^{\eta e^{-\nu}\|w\|^{2}}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{M_{V}-m_{V}}\left(\int_{H}e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)\right)^{e^{-\nu}}\leq CA^{e^{-\nu}}e^{(M_{V}-m_{V})},

therefore if we choose
A\geq\exp\left(\frac{\ln C+M_{V}-m_{V}}{1-e^{-\nu}}\right)
then
S_{1}^{V}(\gamma)(h)\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
.

It remains to show the continuity, that is
S_{1}^{V}(\gamma)(h_{n})\to S_{1}^{V}(\gamma)(h)
weakly if
h_{n}\to h
. Let
\phi
be any bounded Lipschitz function on
H
. Since
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and
|\beta_{r}h_{n}-\beta_{r}h|=|h_{n}-h|
, one has

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|P_{0,1,h_{n}}^{V}\phi(w)-P_{0,1,h}^{V}\phi(w)\right|\\
&\begin{aligned} \leq&\mathbf{E}\left|e^{\int_{0}^{1}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h_{n}))dr}\left(\phi(\Phi_{0,1,h_{n}}(w))-\phi(\Phi_{0,1,h}(w))\right)\right|\\
&\qquad+\mathbf{E}\left|\left(e^{\int_{0}^{1}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h_{n}))dr}-e^{\int_{0}^{1}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))dr}\right)\phi(\Phi_{0,1,h}(w))\right|\end{aligned}\\
&\leq C(V,\phi)\mathbf{E}\left(\|\Phi_{0,1,h_{n}}(w)-\Phi_{0,1,h}(w)\|+\int_{0}^{1}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h_{n}))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))\right|dr\right)\\
&\leq C(V,\phi)\left(\mathbf{E}\|\Phi_{0,r,h_{n}}(w)-\Phi_{0,r,h}(w)\|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h|)\right)\leq C(V,\phi)e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}(|h_{n}-h|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h|)),\end{split}

for some modulus of continuity
\kappa
, where we used estimate (
B.4
) and Lipschitz continuity of
f
in the last step. Hence

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\Big\langle\gamma(h_{n}),P_{0,1,h_{n}}^{V}\phi-P_{0,1,h}^{V}\phi\Big\rangle\right|&\leq C\int_{H}e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}\gamma(h_{n})(dw)(|h_{n}-h|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h|))\\
&\leq C(|h_{n}-h|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h|)).\end{split}

Since
P_{0,1,h}^{V}
is Feller,
P_{0,1,h}^{V}\phi\in C_{b}(H)
. By continuity of
\gamma
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left|\Big\langle\gamma(h_{n})-\gamma(h),P_{0,1,h}^{V}\phi\Big\rangle\right|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore by (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

\left|\Big\langle\gamma(h_{n}),P_{0,1,h_{n}}^{V}\phi\Big\rangle-\Big\langle\gamma(h),P_{0,1,h}^{V}\phi\Big\rangle\right|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty,

which means
P_{0,1,h_{n}}^{V*}\gamma(h_{n})
converges weakly to
P_{0,1,h}^{V}\gamma(h)
since
\phi
was chosen arbitrarily. Hence

h\to P_{0,1,h}^{V}\gamma(h)

is continuous. As a result,
S_{1}^{V}(\gamma)(h)
is continuous in
h\in\mathbb{T}
. So
S_{1}^{V}
maps
C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))
into itself as desired.

Step 2: Prove that
S_{1}^{V}
maps
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
into itself
. We claim that
S_{1}^{V}
is a continuous map from
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)^{\mathbb{T}}
to itself with respect to the topology of point-wise convergence. Then it will follow that
S_{1}^{V}
maps
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
into itself. Foy any
h\in\mathbb{T}
, let
\Pi_{h}
be the canonical projection of
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)^{\mathbb{T}}
to the
h
component, which is
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
.

Note that
P_{0,t,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}
is a continuous map from
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
to itself, and

F_{1}:\quad\gamma\to P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)=P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h_{0}}^{V*}\Pi_{\beta_{-1}h}\gamma

is the composition of
P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}
with the projection
\Pi_{\beta_{-1}h}
. Hence it is a continuous map from

\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)^{\mathbb{T}}
to
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
. Similarly, we see that

F_{2}:\quad\gamma\to\frac{1}{P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(H)}

is a continuous map from
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)^{\mathbb{T}}
to
\mathbb{R}
since
P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(H)
is bounded away from zero by (
3.6
). Observe that the product map

(a,\mu)\to a\mu

is continuous, hence
\Pi_{h}S_{1}^{V}
as the composition of the product map with
(F_{2},F_{1})
is continuous. Since
h
is arbitrary, we have that
S_{1}^{V}
from
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)^{\mathbb{T}}
to itself is continuous.

Step 3: Existence of a fixed point
. We show that the image of
S_{1}^{V}
is precompact and conclude the existence of a fixed point by Schauder-Tychonoff’s fixed point theorem. In view of the well-known Tychonoff’s compactness theorem, to show precompactness, we only need to show that
\left\{(S_{1}^{V}\gamma)(h):\gamma\in\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}\right\}
is tight for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
. From estimate (
B.6
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{H}\|w\|_{2}^{2}\left(S_{1}^{V}\gamma(h)\right)(dw)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}^{V*}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(H)}\int_{H}\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,\beta_{-1}h))dr\right)\|\Phi_{0,1,\beta_{-1}h}(w)\|_{2}^{2}\right]\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)

\displaystyle\leq e^{M_{V}-m_{V}}\int_{H}Ce^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}\gamma(\beta_{-1}h)(dw)\leq C.

Hence

(3.10)

\displaystyle S_{1}^{V}\gamma(h)\{\|w\|_{2}>R\}\leq R^{-2}\int_{H}\|w\|_{2}^{2}\left(S_{1}^{V}\gamma(h)\right)(dw)\leq R^{-2}C.

Since
\{\|w\|_{2}\leq R\}
is compactly embedded in
H
, we know that
\left\{(S_{1}^{V}\gamma)(h):\gamma\in\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}\right\}
is tight.

Now observe that
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
is a nonempty closed convex subset of the locally convex Hausdorff topological vector space of the maps from
\mathbb{T}
to the space of finite signed measures on
H
, and
S_{1}^{V}
is a continuous mapping of
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
into a compact subset. Hence the Schauder-Tychonoff’s theorem (See Appendix of
[
5
]
or Theorem 10.1 in
[
33
]
) implies the existence of a fixed point
\Gamma^{V}
of
S_{1}^{V}
in
\overline{C_{p}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))}
.

Step 4: Prove that the fixed point has the desired properties in items (1)-(3)
. The eigenmeasure property and cocycle property directly follow from the definition of
S_{k}^{V}
. Indeed, Since
S_{k}^{V}
has the semigroup property,
\Gamma^{V}
is also a fixed point of
S_{m}^{V}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)=P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)(H)\Gamma^{V}(h),\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{T}.

Replacing
h
by
\beta_{m}h
, one obtains

\displaystyle P_{0,m,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)=P_{0,m,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H)\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{m}h),\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{T}.

Combining this with the cocycle property of the inhomogeneous Feynman-Kac evolution operators, we have

\displaystyle P_{0,m,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)

\displaystyle=P_{0,m-1,\beta_{1}h}^{V*}P_{0,1,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)=P_{0,m-2,\beta_{2}h}^{V*}P_{0,1,\beta_{1}h}^{V*}P_{0,1,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H)\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{1}h)

\displaystyle=P_{0,m-3,\beta_{3}h}^{V*}P_{0,1,\beta_{2}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{2}h)P_{0,1,\beta_{1}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{1}h)(H)P_{0,1,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}P_{0,1,\beta_{k}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)(H)\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{m}h).

The desired cocycle property then follows since
\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{m}h)(H)=1
.

The concentration property follows from (
3.10
). To show (
3.3
), we use the uniform irreducibility from Proposition
C.3
. From the concentration inequality (
3.5
), we can choose an
R
large such that

\inf_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\Gamma^{V}(h)(B_{R}^{H})\geq 1/2.

Choose
m
large such that
m>2\ln R
. Then it follows from (
3.6
) and Proposition
C.3
that

\displaystyle\Gamma^{V}(h)(B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w))

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathbb{1}_{B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w)}(u)(S_{m}^{V}\Gamma^{V})(h)(dw)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathbb{1}_{B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w)}(u)\frac{P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)(du)}{\big(P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)\big)(H)}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-mM_{V}}\int_{H}P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V}(u,B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w))\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)(du)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-mM_{V}}\int_{B_{R}^{H}}P_{0,m,\beta_{-m}h}^{V}(u,B_{r}^{H,H_{2}}(w))\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-m}h)(du)\geq\frac{p}{2}e^{-mM_{V}},

where
p=p(r,\rho)
is from Proposition
C.3
. Hence (
3.3
) is proved. The proof of (
3.4
) is similar by combining (
C.18
). In particular we have shown that
\Gamma^{V}(h)
has a positive measure for any nondegenerate ball in
H
, so its support is
H
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now prove the existence of an eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue and eigenmeasure from the previous proposition. The scheme of the proof by using a Krylov–Bogolyubov averaging procedure is a time inhomogeneous version of the arguments developed in
[
23
]
.

Proposition 3.2

(Existence of Eigenfunction)
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H
and
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. Then the family of functions

\displaystyle F_{k}(w,h):=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}},\quad(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}

is uniformly equicontinuous on
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
. So there is a subsequence converges to a function
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
for
q=q(V)
as in Proposition
C.1
. Moreover,
F^{V}
satisfies the following

(1)

Strict positivity: for every
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle F^{V}(w,h)>0.

(2)

Eigenfunction property:

(3.12)

\displaystyle P_{0,m,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{m}h)(w)=\lambda_{m,h,V}F^{V}(w,h),\quad(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},m\in\mathbb{N}.

(3)

Normalization property:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\rangle=1,\quad h\in\mathbb{T}.

(4)

Equicontinuity: for any
R>0
the restriction of the family
\left\{F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{T}}
to
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
is equicontinuous.

Proof.

The proof is divided into the following three steps.

Step 1: Existence of
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H_{2}\times\mathbb{T})
satisfying items (3) and (4)
. By uniform Feller property from Proposition
C.4
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}=\frac{\quad\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}\quad}{\quad\frac{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}\quad}

is uniformly equicontinuous on
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
. If one can show the existence of a constant
C=C(R_{0})
such that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell,h}\frac{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}\leq C,

then
\{F_{k}\}
will be uniformly equicontinuous.

Let
g(w)=\displaystyle\inf_{\ell,h}\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}},w\in H_{2}
. Since the infimum of a uniform equicontinuous and bounded family is still continuous,
g\in C(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
. Note that
g
is non-negative and each
\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}}{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}
is on the unit sphere in
C(B_{R_{0}}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
, hence there must be a point
w_{0}\in B_{R_{0}}^{H_{2}}
and a constant
a>0
such that
g(w_{0})>2a
. Then for each
R\geq R_{0}
,
by the fact that
g\in C(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
, there is
r>0
such that
g(w)>a
for any
w\in B_{r}^{H,B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(w_{0})
. By choosing
R
large, the support property (
3.4
) implies the existence of a constant
c>0
such that

\inf_{h}\Gamma^{V}(h)(B_{r}^{H,B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(w_{0}))\geq c.

Since
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
, one has

\displaystyle\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell,h,V}

\displaystyle=\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}^{-1}\int_{H}P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)\Gamma^{V}(h)(dw)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{H_{2}}g(w)\Gamma^{V}(h)(dw)\geq\int_{B_{r}^{H,B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(w_{0})}g(w)\Gamma^{V}(h)(dw)

\displaystyle\geq ca.

This verifies the uniform bound (
3.14
). Hence
\left\{F_{k}(\cdot,h)\right\}
is uniformly equicontinuous on
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
. It is also uniformly bounded on any ball
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
by the growth condition from Proposition
C.1
.

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, there is a subsequence
\{F_{k_{j}}(\cdot,h)\}
and a function
F^{V}(\cdot,h):H_{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
that is continuous when restricted on
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
, such that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{w\in B_{R}^{H_{2}}}|F_{k_{j}}(w,h)-F^{V}(w,h)|=0,\quad\forall R>0.

The growth condition from Proposition
C.1
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\qquad\frac{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}}}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}\qquad}{\frac{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}}\leq\frac{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}}}{\|P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}\leq M_{q}.

Together with boundedness (
3.14
), this shows that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}\leq C_{R_{0}}M_{q}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\quad w\in H,

where the right-hand side is
\Gamma^{V}(h)
integrable. Hence Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and eigenmeasure property from Proposition
3.1
yield

\displaystyle\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\rangle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle F_{k_{j}}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\rangle=1,\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{T},

which verifies the normalization property (
3.13
). It also follows from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) that
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H_{2}\times\mathbb{T})
. Since for each
R>0
, the family
\{F_{k}(\cdot,h)\}_{h\in\mathbb{T}}
is uniformly equicontinuous on the ball
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
, one deduces item (4) about equicontinuity of
\{F^{V}(\cdot,h)\}_{h\in\mathbb{T}}
.

Step 2: Proof of
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and item (2) on the eigenfunction property
. We first show that the eigenfunction property is true for
w\in H_{2}
. By cocycle property (
3.2
),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{1}F_{k_{j}})(w,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}P_{0,1,h}^{V}\left(\frac{P_{0,\ell,\beta_{1}h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(\cdot)}{\lambda_{\ell,\beta_{1}h,V}}\right)(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1,h,V}}{k_{j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{P_{0,\ell+1,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{1,h,V}\lambda_{\ell,\beta_{1}h,V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta_{1}h,V}}{k_{j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{\ell,h,V}}-\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta_{1}h,V}}{k_{j}}\left(1+\frac{P_{0,k_{j},h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{k_{j},h,V}}\right)

It follows from the construction of
F^{V}(w,h)
and the boundedness from (
3.16
) that the right-hand side converges to
\lambda_{1,\beta_{1}h,V}F^{V}(w,h)
as
j\to\infty
. The left-hand side converges to

\mathcal{P}_{1}F^{V}(w,h)=P_{0,1,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{1}h)(w)

by dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
w_{0,1,h}\in H_{2}
. This proves the eigenfunction property for
m=1
and
(w,h)\in H_{2}\times\mathbb{T}
. The general case for
m\geq 2
and
(w,h)\in H_{2}\times\mathbb{T}
follows from cocycle property (
3.2
). By the regularizing property of the Navier-Stokes solution process, for any
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
, the function
\frac{P_{0,1,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{1}h)(w)}{\lambda_{1,h,V}}
is well defined and satisfies (similar to the proof of (
3.16
))

\displaystyle\frac{P_{0,1,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{1}h)(w)}{\lambda_{1,h,V}}\leq C(F^{V},R_{0})M_{q}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\quad w\in H.

Define
F^{V}(w,h)=\frac{P_{0,1,h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{1}h)(w)}{\lambda_{1,h,V}}
for those
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}\backslash H_{2}\times\mathbb{T}
. Then
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
is a nonnegative function satisfying the eigenfunction property for any
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
. Since
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
, the definition on
H\setminus H_{2}
does not affect the normalization identity (
3.13
).

Step 3: Proof of item (1) on the strict positivity
. Let
R\geq 1
, and
(w_{0},h_{0})\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}
. Since
\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=1
, and
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
, for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
there is a point
w_{h}\in H_{2}
and a number
c=c(w_{h})>0
such that
F^{V}(w_{h},h)\geq c
. To apply the irreducibility from Proposition
C.3
, we choose an
R>0
such that
w_{0}\in B_{R}^{H}
, and integer
\ell>2\ln R
. Then we choose
L>L_{0}(\ell,R)
as in (
C.18
) large such that
w_{\beta_{\ell}h_{0}}\in B_{L}^{H_{2}}
. Since
F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{\ell}h_{0})
is continuous on
(B_{L}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
, there is a radius
r>0
such that

F^{V}(w,\beta_{\ell}h_{0})\geq c/2\text{ for any }w\in B_{r}^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(w_{\beta_{\ell}h_{0}}).

By the eigenfunciton property (
3.12
) and boundedness of
V
, we have,

\displaystyle F^{V}(w_{0},h_{0})=\frac{P^{V}_{0,\ell,h_{0}}F^{V}(w_{0},\beta_{\ell}h_{0})}{\lambda_{\ell,h_{0},V}}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\ell M_{V}}\int_{B_{r}^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(w_{\beta_{\ell}h_{0}})}F^{V}(w,\beta_{\ell}h_{0})P^{V}_{0,\ell,h_{0}}(w_{0},du)

\displaystyle\geq ce^{-\ell M_{V}}P^{V}_{0,\ell,h_{0}}(w_{0},B_{r}^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(w_{\beta_{\ell}h_{0}}))>0

by (
C.18
).

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is complete.
∎

3.2.
Forward and pullback stability for discrete time

This subsection is devoted to the proof of the forward stability in the following Theorem
3.3
for the eigen-triple of the discrete time Feynman-Kac evolution operators
P_{0,k,h}^{V}
. The pullback stability is stated in Theorem
3.6
at the end of the section where the proof is omitted since it is similar to the proof of forward stability by combining the eigenfunction/eigenmeasure property in the pullback sense.

Theorem 3.3

(Forward stability the eigen-triple)
.

Let
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. For any
\phi\in C_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,
M,R>0,h\in\mathbb{T}
, and
\eta^{\prime}\in(\eta,\eta_{0})
we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{k,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}=0,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{k,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}=0,\,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta^{\prime},M}(H)}\left|\left\langle\lambda_{k,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,h}^{V*}\mu,\varphi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle-\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h),\mu\rangle\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h),\varphi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle\right|=0,

This theorem is proved in subsection
3.2.2
, after we establish the
L^{1}
-forward stability for differentiable observable functions in subsection
3.2.1
through a version of Ruelle’s lower bound technique. Before proceeding, let’s introduce some notations and a lemma.

Fix
h\in\mathbb{T}
for which the dependence on
h
will be dropped unless stated throughout the proof. Set

(3.20)

\displaystyle g(w,h)=\phi(w,h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle F^{V}(w,h)

and

(3.21)

\displaystyle g_{k}(w,h)=\frac{(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{V}g)(w,h)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}.

It follows from the eigenfunction property (
3.12
) that for every
h\in\mathbb{T}
,

(3.22)

\displaystyle g_{k}(w,h)=\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)(w)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle F^{V}(w,h).

and

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{k}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=0,\text{ and }\left\langle g_{k}^{+}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=\left\langle g_{k}^{-}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}.

by combining the eigenmeasure property from Proposition
3.1
. The following observation is useful.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and
q
be the number from Proposition
3.2
. Then the family
g_{k}(\cdot,h)
satisfies

\displaystyle\{g_{k}(\cdot,h)\}_{(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}}\subset L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T}),

and is uniformly equicontinuous on
C_{b}(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
.

Proof.

In view of (
3.16
) and the fact that
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)(w)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),

where the constant

does not depend on

. In addition,
\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle
is bounded uniformly for
(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}
. It then follows from Proposition
3.2
that the family

\left\{\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right\}_{(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}}

is uniformly equicontinuous on

and

\left|\left\langle\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle F^{V}(w,h)\right|\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w).

As a consequence (note that the following (

) is still true if

),

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sup_{(k,h)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{T}}|g_{k}(w,h)|\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w).

) and the uniform Feller property in Proposition

∎

3.2.1.
Forward stability in
L^{1}
norm

This subsection is devoted to prove the following proposition on forward stability in
L^{1}
norm for differentiable observable functions.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and
h\in\mathbb{T}
, we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left|\lambda_{k,h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi\left(\cdot,\beta_{k}h\right)-\left\langle\phi\left(\cdot,\beta_{k}h\right),\Gamma^{V}\left(\beta_{k}h\right)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\right|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}=0.

Proof.

We prove the convergence by contradiction. The proof is divided into four steps.

Step 1: Lower bounds from contradiction assumption
. Assume (
3.25
) does not hold. Then there is a constant
a>0
and a subsequence
k_{j}\to\infty
such that

(3.26)

\displaystyle|g_{k_{j}}(\cdot,h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}\geq a.

Due to the time inhomogeneity, here we do not have non-expansiveness of the sequence
g_{k}(\cdot,h)
under
L^{1}(\Gamma^{V}(h))
norm. Instead, the cocycle property (
3.2
) implies

(3.27)

\displaystyle g_{k}(w,h)=\frac{P^{V}_{0,l,h}g_{k-l}(\cdot,\beta_{l}h)(w)}{\lambda_{l,h,V}},\,\text{ for all }0\leq l\leq k,

which together with the eigenmeasure property from Proposition
3.1
yields

(3.28)

\displaystyle|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(h)}\leq|g_{k-l}(\cdot,\beta_{l}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{l}h)},\,\text{ for all }0\leq l\leq k,h\in\mathbb{T}.

This “local” monotonicity along the line
x+y=k
and (
3.26
) imply that

\displaystyle|g_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{n}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{n}h)}\geq a,\,\text{ for all }(m,n)\in K,

where
K=\bigcup_{j\geq 1}K_{j}
with

\displaystyle K_{j}=\left\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:m,n\geq 0\text{ and }m+n=k_{j}\right\}.

Combining with (
3.23
), we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle|g_{m}^{\pm}(\cdot,\beta_{n}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{n}h)}\geq a/2,\,\text{ for all }(m,n)\in K.

Since
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
for every
h
, we have from (
3.5
) and Lemma
3.4
that

\displaystyle|g_{m}^{\pm}(\cdot,\beta_{n}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{n}h)}

\displaystyle=\int_{H_{2}}g_{m}^{\pm}(w,\beta_{n}h)\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{n}h)(dw)

\displaystyle\left\langle|\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\phi_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)|,\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{k}h)\right\rangle<\varepsilon/3.

Let

. By the uniform bounds (

), (

) and the growth condition for the function

in Proposition

\displaystyle\frac{|P_{0,k,h}^{V}(\phi-\phi_{m})(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)(w)|}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(\phi-\phi_{m})(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)(w)|}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}+\frac{|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}}}(\phi-\phi_{m})(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)(w)|}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}

\displaystyle\leq|\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\phi_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)|_{C_{b}(B_{L}^{H_{2}})}\frac{\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{r}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}+C\frac{\left\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}}}\right\|_{r}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\phi_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)|_{C_{b}(B_{L}^{H_{2}})}+L^{-2}\frac{\left\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}N_{2}\right\|_{r}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\phi_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)|_{C_{b}(B_{L}^{H_{2}})}+L^{-2}\frac{\left\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\right\|_{R_{0}}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(|\phi(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)-\phi_{m}(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)|_{C_{b}(B_{L}^{H_{2}})}+L^{-2}),

where the constant
C
does not depend on
L,k,m
. Hence by first choosing

large and then choosing

large, we can find

such that for all
m\geq M_{2}
and

(3.43)

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in B_{r}^{H}}\frac{\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}(\phi-\phi_{m})(\cdot,\beta_{k}h)\|_{r}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}<\varepsilon/3.

The result from
Step 2
implies that for

, one can find

,

\|g_{k,m}(\cdot,h)\|_{r}<\varepsilon/3.

Combining this with (

) and (

) we obtain the desired convergence (
3.17
) for
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. The convergence (
3.18
) follows from (
3.17
) and the concentration property (
3.5
) and the fact that
\Gamma^{V}(h)\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
from Proposition
3.1
.

The proof of

is complete.

Step 4: Proof of convergence (
3.19
)
. Let
\phi\in C_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and
\eta^{\prime}\in(\eta,\eta_{0})
. Then by the uniform bound (
3.14
) and growth condition from Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(w,h)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}

\displaystyle=C\frac{P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)}{\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}\frac{\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}{\lambda_{k,h,V}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w).

Since
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, one has
g_{k}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, where
g_{k}
is the function obtained from (

. Hence by Hölder’s inequality and Markov inequality, one has

(3.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|,\mu\right\rangle&=\left\langle|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|\mathbb{1}_{B_{R}^{H}},\mu\right\rangle+\left\langle|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H}},\mu\right\rangle\\
&\leq|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}+C\left\langle\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H}},\mu\right\rangle\\
&\leq|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}+C\left\langle\mathfrak{m}_{\eta^{\prime}},\mu\right\rangle^{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}\left\langle\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H}},\mu\right\rangle^{1-\eta/\eta^{\prime}}\\
&\leq|g_{k}(\cdot,h)|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}+CR^{-1}\end{split}

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta^{\prime},M}(H)
, where

is a constant independent of
k,h,R
. Therefore by first taking

and then

, we obtain the desired convergence (
3.19
).

The proof of Theorem
3.3
is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.6

(Pullback stability of the eigen-triple)
.

Let

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h)\right|_{C_{b}(B_{R}^{H})}=0,

(3.46)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,h)-\left\langle\phi(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h)\right|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)}=0,

(3.47)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta^{\prime},M}(H)}\left|\left\langle\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu,\phi(\cdot,h)\right\rangle-\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\mu\rangle\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi(\cdot,h)\right\rangle\right|=0.

Proof.

Replacing

by
\beta_{-k}h
in (

\displaystyle|g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)}\leq|g_{k-1}(\cdot,\beta_{-(k-1)}h)|_{\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-(k-1)}h)},\,\text{ for all }k,h\in\mathbb{T}.

This means for each fixed

is non-increasing. This global non-expansive structure implies that if the convergence (

∎

The following corollary states that uniformly for initial measures in

Corollary 3.7
.

For each

, we have the following convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\overline{\Gamma}}\left|\frac{\left\langle P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu,\phi\right\rangle}{P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu(H)}-\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle\right|=0,

.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
. Convergence (

) implies that

(3.48)

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\left|\left\langle\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu,\phi\right\rangle-\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\mu\rangle\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle\right|\to 0

uniformly for

as

, where

is from Proposition
3.1
. In particular, taking

, then

(3.49)

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\left|\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu(H)-\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\mu\right\rangle\right|\to 0

uniformly for

as well. Combining (
3.48
) and (
3.49
) with triangle inequality, we have

(3.50)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k,h,\mu}:=\left|\frac{\left\langle P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu,\phi\right\rangle}{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}-\frac{P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu(H)}{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle\right|\leq I_{1}+I_{2}\left|\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle\right|\to 0

uniformly for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
as

.

We now claim that
\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\mu(H)
can be bounded away from 0 for large

through the convergence (
3.49
) and equicontinuity of the eigenfunction family

. Since
\Gamma^{V}(h)
is concentrated on

for every

\displaystyle 1=\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\mathbb{1}_{B_{L}^{H_{2}}},\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle+\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\mathbb{1}_{H_{2}\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}}},\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h)\mathbb{1}_{B_{L}^{H_{2}}},\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathfrak{m}_{q}\mathbb{1}_{H_{2}\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}}},\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle

for all

. Combining this with (

3.3.
Uniqueness and regularity of the eigen-triple

We now prove uniqueness of eigen-triple and corresponding regularity. The uniqueness of eigenmeasure and eigenvalue follows from the pullback convergence in Theorem
3.6
, while the uniqueness of eigenfunction follows from the forward convergence in Theorem
3.3
. The continuous dependence of eigenmeassure on time symbols
h\in\mathbb{T}
follows from Corollary
3.7
, which implies continuity of eigenvalue
\lambda_{k,h,V}
. The regularity of eigenfunction
F^{V}
on
w\in H
follows from the forward convergence (
3.17
) and uniform Lipschitz property from Proposition
C.4
. Continuity of eigenfunction on time symbols
h\in\mathbb{T}
will be proved in subsection
3.4
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, the eigen-triple
(\Gamma^{V},\lambda_{k,h,V},F^{V})
of the Feynman-Kac evolution operator
P_{0,k,h}^{V}
is unique. In addition,

h\to\Gamma^{V}(h),\,\text{ and }h\to\lambda_{k,h,V},

are continuous for every
k
. Moreover, the family
\left\{F^{V}(\cdot,h),h\in\mathbb{T}\right\}
is uniformly Lipschitz on
B_{R}^{H}
for any
R>0
with a uniform bound
L=L(R,V)
on the Lipschitz constants independent of
h\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into three steps. We first show uniqueness at the first step. Then we prove continuity of eigenmeasure and eigenvalue at the second step. The continuity of eigenfunction is given at the last step.

Step 1: Uniqueness
. Suppose there is another eigen-triple
(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V},\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,h,V},\widetilde{F}^{V})
. We show the uniqueness by first proving
\Gamma^{V}=\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}
.

Noting
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}
must have the same support property as
\Gamma^{V}
in Proposition
3.1
. This fact together with the convergence (
3.45
) imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h)|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)}=0,

where
g_{k}
is given as in (
3.21
). Since
\left|\left\langle g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle\right|\leq|g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h)|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)}
, it follows that

(3.53)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle=0.

Taking in particular
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
in
g_{k}
, using the facts
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(H)=\Gamma^{V}(H)=1
for all
h\in\mathbb{T}
and
P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h}^{V*}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)=\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(h)
, we have

(3.54)

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle=\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}-\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle\to 0,\quad k\to\infty,

by (
3.53
). Similarly by exchanging the role of eigen-triple we have

(3.55)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}{\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}-\left\langle\widetilde{F}^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle\to 0,\quad k\to\infty,

Observe that
F^{V}
is strictly positive everywhere by Proposition
3.2
. We claim that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle>0.

Indeed if this is not true, then by (
3.54
) we have

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}=0.

This contradicts with the limit (
3.55
), since as an eigenfunction
\widetilde{F}^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
implies that
\left\langle\widetilde{F}^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle
is uniformly bounded. Hence the claim is proved.

Combining the claim with (
3.54
), one has

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}:=\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}{\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}}\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle^{-1}\to 1,\quad k\to\infty,

which together with (
3.53
) imply that for every
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\phi,\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(h)\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi,\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\Lambda_{k}\left\langle\phi,\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(h)\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi,\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle^{-1}\left|\left\langle g_{k}(\cdot,\beta_{-k}h),\widetilde{\Gamma}^{V}(\beta_{-k}h)\right\rangle\right|=0.

Hence the eigenmeasure, as the fixed point of the semigroup
S_{k}^{V}
in Proposition
3.1
, is unique. The corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{k,h,V}
is uniquely determined. Furthermore, the eigenfunction
F^{V}
satisfying the normalization condition
\left\langle F^{V}(h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=1
is also uniquely determined by the forward convergence in Theorem
3.3
by taking
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
in (
3.17
).

Step 2: Continuity of eigenmeasure and eigenvalue
. Let
\phi
be any bounded Lipschitz function on
H
and
h_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
. We would like to show

\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{n}),\phi\right\rangle\to\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{0}),\phi\right\rangle,\,\text{ as long as }h_{n}\to h,

by combining Corollary
3.7
and Lipschitz continuity of
h\to P_{0,k,h}^{V}\phi(w)
modulo the Lyapunov function
\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}
and growth in
k
.

To be specific, define

\gamma_{k}^{\varphi}(h_{1},h_{2})=\left\langle P_{0,k,\beta_{-k}h_{1}}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-k}h_{2}),\varphi\right\rangle,\,\varphi\in C_{b}(H),h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{T}.

Note that by Proposition
3.1
, one has

\gamma_{k}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h,h)=\lambda_{k,\beta_{-k}h,V}\,\text{ and }\,\gamma_{k}^{\phi}(h,h)=\gamma_{k}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h,h)\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h),\phi\right\rangle.

Also observe that

(3.56)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{n}),\phi\right\rangle\ -\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{0}),\phi\right\rangle\right|\\
&\leq\left|\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{n}),\phi\right\rangle\ -\frac{\gamma_{k}^{\phi}(h_{0},h_{n})}{\gamma_{k}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{0},h_{n})}\right|+\left|\frac{\gamma_{k}^{\phi}(h_{0},h_{n})}{\gamma_{k}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{0},h_{n})}-\left\langle\Gamma^{V}(h_{0}),\phi\right\rangle\right|:=I_{1,k}+I_{2,k}.\end{split}

It follows from Corollary
3.7
that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a large
K=K(h_{0})
independent of
h_{n}
such that for all
k\geq K
,

(3.57)

\displaystyle I_{2,k}<\varepsilon/2.

Since
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, estimate as in (
3.7
) and the fact
\Gamma^{V}(h)\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H)
yield the existence of some modulus of continuity
\kappa
and a constant
C=C(K,\phi,V)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\gamma_{K}^{\phi}(h_{n},h_{n})-\gamma_{K}^{\phi}(h_{0},h_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle P_{0,K,\beta_{-K}h_{n}}^{V}\phi(w)-P_{0,K,\beta_{-K}h_{0}}^{V}\phi(w),\Gamma^{V}({\beta_{-K}h_{n}})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\langle\mathfrak{m}_{\eta},\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-K}h_{n})\right\rangle(|h_{n}-h_{0}|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h_{0}|))

\displaystyle\leq C(|h_{n}-h_{0}|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h_{0}|)),

for any Lipschitz
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
, where we used the fact that
|\beta_{-K}h_{n}-\beta_{-K}h|=|h_{n}-h|
. Therefore by noting that
e^{km_{V}}\leq\gamma_{k}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{1},h_{2})\leq e^{kM_{V}}
and
|\gamma_{k}^{\phi}(h_{1},h_{2})|\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{kM_{V}}
for any
h_{1},h_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1,K}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\big|\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{0},h_{n})-\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{n},h_{n})\big|\big|\gamma_{K}^{\phi}(h_{n},h_{n})\big|}{\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{0},h_{n})\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{n},h_{n})}+\frac{\left|\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{n},h_{n})\right|\left|\gamma_{K}^{\phi}(h_{n},h_{n})-\gamma_{K}^{\phi}(h_{0},h_{n})\right|}{\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{0},h_{n})\gamma_{K}^{\mathbb{1}_{H}}(h_{n},h_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C(K,\phi,V)(|h_{n}-h_{0}|+\kappa(|h_{n}-h_{0}|)).

As a result, there is an
N=N(K,\varepsilon,\phi,V)
such that for all
n\geq N
,

(3.58)

\displaystyle I_{1,K}<\varepsilon/2.

Hence
\Gamma^{V}\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}_{\eta,A}(H))
by combining (
3.56
)-(
3.58
) and arbitrariness of bounded Lipschitz function
\phi
. The continuity of
h\to\lambda_{k,h,V}
follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,h,V}=\left\langle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H},\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle

and continuity of
h\to\Gamma^{V}(h)
.

Step 3: Continuity of eigenfunction
.
The uniform Lipschitz continuity of the eigenfunction family follows from the convergence (
3.17
) with
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
, the uniform Lipschitz property from Proposition
C.4
with
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
, and the uniform boundedness (
3.14
).

The proof of Proposition
3.8
is complete.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The proof is divided into three steps. We first show the uniqueness of eigen-triple and then prove their continuity. Finally we prove the forward and pullback stability properties.

Step 1: Uniqueness of eigen-triple for the continuous time family
P^{V}_{0,t,h}
. Let
\lambda_{t,h,V}=P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)(H)
. We first show that
\Gamma^{V}
is the unique eigenmeasureof the continuous time Feynman-Kac evolution operators
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{t,h,V}
. For any
t_{0}>0
, we apply the process in the previous three subsections to the discrete time Feynman-Kac evolution operators
P_{0,kt_{0},h}^{V},k\geq 0
, which yields the existence of unique eigen-triple
(\Gamma_{t_{0}}^{V},\widetilde{\lambda}_{kt_{0},h,V},F_{t_{0}}^{V})
. Note that
\Gamma_{t_{0}}^{V}
is the unique fixed point of the operator
S_{t_{0}}^{V}
as in (
3.1
), i.e.,

S_{t_{0}}^{V}\Gamma_{t_{0}}^{V}=\Gamma_{t_{0}}^{V}.

By the semigroup property of
S_{t}^{V}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}^{V}(S_{t_{0}}^{V}\Gamma^{V})=S_{t_{0}}^{V}(S_{1}^{V}\Gamma^{V})=S_{t_{0}}^{V}\Gamma^{V},

implying that
S_{t_{0}}^{V}\Gamma^{V}
is a fixed point of
S_{1}^{V}
, which is unique by Proposition
3.8
. Hence
S_{t_{0}}^{V}\Gamma^{V}=\Gamma^{V}
, which in turn shows that
\Gamma^{V}
is a fixed point of
S_{t_{0}}^{V}
. Therefore by uniqueness

\Gamma_{t_{0}}^{V}=\Gamma^{V},\quad\text{and }\widetilde{\lambda}_{t_{0},h,V}=\lambda_{t_{0},h,V}.

Since
t_{0}
is arbitrary, we obtain that
\Gamma^{V}
, as the unique fixed point of the semigroup
S_{t}^{V}
, is the unique eigenmeasure of
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{t,h,V}
.

The uniqueness of eigenfunction is similar. Indeed, let
\widetilde{F}(w,h)=\lambda_{t_{0},h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t_{0},h}^{V}F^{V}(w,\beta_{t_{0}}h)
. Then by using eigenfunction property of
F^{V}
and cocycle property of
\lambda_{t,h,V}
, one can verify in a straightforward manner that

\left\langle\widetilde{F}(h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=1,\text{ and }P_{0,k,h}^{V}\widetilde{F}(w,\beta_{k}h)=\lambda_{k,h,V}\widetilde{F}(w,h),\quad(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}.

By uniqueness of eigenfunction from Proposition
3.8
, one has
\widetilde{F}=F^{V}
. Hence

P_{0,t_{0},h}^{V}F^{V}(w,\beta_{t_{0}}h)=\lambda_{t_{0},h,V}F^{V}(w,h),

which means that
F^{V}
is an eigenfunction of the family
\{P_{0,kt_{0},h}^{V}\}
. Therefore
F^{V}=F_{t_{0}}^{V}
by uniqueness.

With the invariance (eigenmeasure property) of
\Gamma^{V}
in the whole time horizon, we are now able to show the integral representation (
2.10
) for the eigenvalue through a differentiation procedure. Indeed, since
r\to H_{r}
is continuous almost surely, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lambda_{t,h,V}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{H}\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr}\right]\Gamma^{V}(h)(dw)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr}V(H_{t})\right]\Gamma^{V}(h)(dw)

\displaystyle=\left\langle V(\cdot,\beta_{t}h),P^{V*}_{0,t,h}\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=\lambda_{t,h,V}\left\langle V(\cdot,\beta_{t}h),\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle.

Since
\lambda_{0,h,V}=1
, by integrating the above equation we obtain the desired representation

\displaystyle\lambda_{t,h,V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V}(\beta_{r}h)dr\right),\,\lambda^{V}(h)=\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle.

The proof of
Step 1
is complete.

Step 2: Continuity of the eigen-triple
. It follows from Proposition
3.8
that

\Gamma^{V}\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))
. The continuity of eigenvalue follows from the fact that
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and the continuity of eigenmeasure once we note that

\displaystyle\left|\lambda^{V}(h_{n})-\lambda^{V}(h)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle V(\cdot,h_{n}),\Gamma^{V}(h_{n})\right\rangle-\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle V(\cdot,h_{n}),\Gamma^{V}(h_{n})\right\rangle-\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h_{n})\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h_{n})\right\rangle-\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa(|h_{n}-h|)+\left|\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h_{n})\right\rangle-\left\langle V(\cdot,h),\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle\right|\to 0,

as long as
h_{n}\to h
, where
\kappa
is some modulus of continuity coming from the fact
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
.

Since
\left\{F^{V}(\cdot,h),h\in\mathbb{T}\right\}
is uniformly Lipschitz on any bounded ball in
H
by Proposition
3.8
, we only need to show that
h\to F^{V}(w,h)
is continuous for every
w\in H
, which will imply that
F^{V}\in C(H\times\mathbb{T})
. To that end, it suffices to show that

(3.59)

\displaystyle t\in\mathbb{R}\to F^{V}(w,\beta_{t}h)\,\text{ is continuous for every }(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}.

Indeed, since
\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)
and
\beta_{t}h=h+t\mod 2\pi
, we have
h=\beta_{h}0
for
h\in[0,2\pi)
. Hence

\displaystyle h\to F^{V}(w,h)=F^{V}(w,\beta_{h}0),

which is continuous by time continuity (
3.59
).

Therefore it remains to show (
3.59
). By the eigenfunction property, we have

(3.60)

\displaystyle P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w)=\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}F^{V}(w,\beta_{-t}h),\,\forall h\in\mathbb{T},t\geq 0.

By the integral representation (
2.10
), we see that
\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}
is continuous in time. Hence we only need to show that
P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w)
is time continuous.

Let
\tau\geq 0
, for notational simplicity denoting
F(w)=F^{V}(w,h),w\in H
and

\displaystyle\Xi_{t,h}^{V}:=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))dr\right)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(\Phi_{0,r,h}(w),\beta_{r}h)dr\right).

Note that with
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{w}
,

(3.61)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w)-P_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w)\\
&=\mathbf{E}\Xi_{t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-\mathbf{E}\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}^{V}F(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h})\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[(\Xi_{t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}-\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}^{V})F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}^{V}(F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-F(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}))\right]\\
&:=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Since
F^{V}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and is strictly positive everywhere by Proposition
3.2
, one has

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}\left[\left|\Xi_{t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}-\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}\right|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\left|\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}-\Xi_{\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h}^{V}\right|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left|\int_{\tau}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,\beta_{-t}h))dr\right|\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,\beta_{-t}h))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,\beta_{-\tau}h))\right|dr\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|t-\tau|\mathbf{E}\mathfrak{m}_{q}+\kappa(|\beta_{-t}h-\beta_{-\tau}h|)\mathbf{E}\mathfrak{m}_{q}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbf{E}\left[\mathfrak{m}_{q}\|\Phi_{0,r,\beta_{-t}h}-\Phi_{0,r,\beta_{-\tau}h}\|\right]dr\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|t-\tau|+\kappa(|\beta_{-t}h-\beta_{-\tau}h|)+|\beta_{-t}h-\beta_{-\tau}h|\right)

for some modulus of continuity
\kappa
coming from the fact
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and the constant
C=C(t,\tau,V)>0
. Here in the second inequality we used the boundedness of
V,t,\tau
and the Lipschitz continuity of the exponential function
e^{x}
on some bounded interval determined by
m_{V},M_{V},t,\tau
. In the third inequality we used the fact
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. In the last step we used Hölder’s inequality and (
B.6
), and (
B.4
) together with the Lipschitz continuity of
f
. Hence
I_{1}\to 0
as
t\to\tau
.

Now observe that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\tau\|V\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{E}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-F(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|+\mathbf{E}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})-F(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|\right)

\displaystyle:=C(I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}).

Let
\Omega_{R}=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h},\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h}\in B_{R}^{H}\right\}
. It follows from Proposition
3.8
that
F
is Lipschitz continuous on
B_{R}^{H}
with some Lipschitz constant
L(R)
. Therefore by
F^{V}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and (
B.4
) together with the Lipschitz continuity of
f
, and noting that

1\leq R^{-2q}\left(\|\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}\|^{2q}+\|\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h}\|^{2q}\right)\leq R^{-2q}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})+\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right),\text{ on }\Omega_{R}^{c},

one obtains

\displaystyle I_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{R}}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{R}^{c}}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})-F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq L(R)\mathbf{E}\left\|\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}-\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h}\right\|+C\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\Omega_{R}^{c}}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})+\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(L(R)|\beta_{-t}h-\beta_{-\tau}h|+R^{-2q}\mathbf{E}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h})+\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(L(R)|\beta_{-t}h-\beta_{-\tau}h|+R^{-2q}\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(w)\Big),

where we used (
B.6
) at the last step. Therefore by first choosing
R
large and then letting
t\to\tau
, we have
I_{2,1}\to 0
.

Since each sample path of the solution
\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h},t\geq 0
is continuous in
t
, and
F^{V}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}(H\times\mathbb{T})
implies

\mathbf{E}\left|F(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})-F(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right|\leq\mathbf{E}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,\beta_{-\tau}h})+\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,\tau,\beta_{-\tau}h})\right)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\,t\geq 0,

therefore by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have
I_{2,2}\to 0
as
t\to\tau
. As a result
I_{2}\to 0
as
t\to\tau
.

In view of (
3.61
) we obtain the time continuity of
P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w)
. Therefore by (
3.60
), the function

(3.62)

\displaystyle t\to F^{V}(w,\beta_{-t}h)=\lambda_{t,\beta_{-t}h,V}^{-1}P_{0,t,\beta_{-t}h}^{V}F^{V}(\cdot,h)(w),\,t\geq 0

is continuous for every
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
. The continuity (
3.59
) for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
follows by shifting (replacing
h
by
\beta_{s}h
for any
s>0
in (
3.62
)).

The proof of
Step 2
is complete.

Step 3: Proof of the stability properties of Theorem
2.1
. Let
g_{t}(w,h)
be the function obtained from (
3.21
) by replacing
k
with
t
. The proof of the forward convergence in this case is exactly the same as the proof of Theorem
3.3
. Namely, we first show as in Proposition
3.5
the convergence of
g_{t}
for
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
in
L^{1}(\Gamma^{V}(h))
by utilizing the unique eigen-triple and the local monotonicity (
3.28
) which is also valid for continuous time. Then we proceed as in subsection
3.2.2
.

The pullback convergence in
L^{1}(\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-t}h))
follows from the convergence (
3.46
) in discrete time and the global monotonicity of
t\to\left\langle|g_{t}(\cdot,\beta_{-t}h)|,\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-t}h)\right\rangle
, where the later follows by the semigroup property of
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}
, and cocycle property of the eigenvalue, as well as the eigenmeasure property. The other pullback stability properties in Theorem
2.1
then follow from convergence in
L^{1}(\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{-t}h))
and similar arguments as in subsection
3.2.2
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.

4.
A Donsker-Varadhan type large deviation principle

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.2
. We prove this theorem in the first subsection
4.1
. Then in subsection
4.2
we prove several lemmas that are used in the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

In this subsection, we prove the main result on the large deviation bounds in Theorem
2.2
by combining the results of Theorem
2.1
and the Kifer type criterion in Theorem
A.1
from the Appendix, where the later was established in
[
23
]
.

More specifically, we first give a proof of Theorem
2.2
in subsection
4.1.1
by assuming the following Theorem
4.1
, which gives a verification of the conditions in Kifer’s Theorem
A.1
. The proof of Theorem
4.1
is given in subsection
4.1.2
, which is based on Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H
and
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},H_{2})
is Lipschitz. Let
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
M>0
.

(1)

For any
V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, the following limit called the pressure function exists

(4.1)

\displaystyle Q(V):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)}\log\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV,\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle,

and independent of
h,\eta,M
. Also
Q(V)
is a convex
1
-Lipschitz function such that
Q(C)=C
for any
C\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular for
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle Q(V)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V}(h)m(dh),

where
\lambda^{V}\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
is the eigenvalue obtained in Theorem
2.1
.

(2)

There is a function
R:H\times\mathbb{T}\to[0,\infty]
with compact level sets
\{(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}:R(w,h)\leq\alpha\}
for any
\alpha\geq 0
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tR,\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle\leq Ce^{ct},\quad\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H),

for some positive constants
c,C
.

(3)

For each
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, there is a unique equilibrium state
\mu_{V}\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, i.e.,

(4.4)

\displaystyle Q(V)=\langle V,\mu_{V}\rangle-I(\mu_{V}).

Here
I
is the Legendre transform of
Q
as in (
1
).

4.1.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

Proof.

The proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1: Link Theorem
4.1
to Theorem
A.1
. Note that the three conditions of Kifer’s Theorem
A.1
are verified by Theorem
4.1
, where we take the directed set

\Theta=(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H),

equipped with an order
\prec
defined by
(t_{1},\sigma_{1})\prec(t_{2},\sigma_{2})
if
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
. And for each fixed
h\in\mathbb{T}
, we set

\displaystyle\zeta_{\theta}=\zeta_{t,h},\quad r(\theta)=t,\,\text{ for every }\theta=(t,\sigma)\in\Theta,

where
\zeta_{t,h}
is the occupation measure defined as (
2.12
), which is a family of random probability measures defined on the probability space
(\Omega_{\theta},\mathcal{F}_{\theta},\mathbf{P}_{\theta})=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P}_{\sigma})
. Hence Theorem
A.1
implies the desired large deviation bounds with the good rate function

\displaystyle I(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\sup_{V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})}\left(\left\langle V,\mu\right\rangle-Q(V)\right),&\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T}),\\
\qquad+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It remains to show that the good rate function is nontrivial.

Step 2: Prove the non-triviality of the rate function
. Assume by contradiction that
I(\gamma)=0
and
I(\mu)=+\infty
for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(H\times\mathbb{T})\setminus\{\gamma\}
, where
\gamma=\Gamma(h)m(dh)
is the unique invariant measure of the homogenized process
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, and
\Gamma\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))
is the continuous periodic invariant measure of
\Phi_{0,t,h}
[
28
]
. Note that
\Gamma
is also the unique eigenmeasure of
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
in the case when the potential
V=0
. Since
I
is the Legendre transform of
Q
, it follows from (
4.1.1
) that

\displaystyle Q(V)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})}\left(\left\langle V,\mu\right\rangle-I(\mu)\right),\quad V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

The assumption of triviality of
I
implies that

(4.7)

\displaystyle Q(V)=\left\langle V,\gamma\right\rangle\text{ for any }V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

To each
V\in C_{b}^{1}(H)
we associate

V_{\gamma}(w,h):=V(w)-\left\langle V,\Gamma(h)\right\rangle.

It has been shown in
[
28
]
that
\Gamma\in C^{r}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{P}(H))
for some
r\in(0,1)
, where
\mathcal{P}(H)
is equipped with a Wasserstein metric weighted by the Lyapunov function
\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}
. Hence
V_{\gamma}\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
for each
V\in C_{b}^{1}(H)
. Since
\left\langle V_{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle=0
, by (
4.2
) and (
4.7
), we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V_{\gamma}}(h)m(dh)=0,\,V\in C_{b}^{1}(H).

Combining this with the integral representation (
2.10
) and Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\lambda_{t,h,V_{\gamma}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V_{\gamma}}(\beta_{rh})dr=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V_{\gamma}}(h)m(dh)=0,

which implies that
\lambda_{t,h,V_{\gamma}}
has a subexponential growth in
t
.

The forward convergence (
2.11
) in Theorem
2.1
with
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
and
\mu=\delta_{w}
implies that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{t,h,V_{\gamma}}^{-1}P^{V_{\gamma}}_{0,t,h}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)=F^{V_{\gamma}}(w,h),\,w\in H,

where we note that

(4.11)

\displaystyle P^{V_{\gamma}}_{0,t,h}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)=\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\big(V(\Phi_{0,r,h})-\left\langle V,\Gamma(\beta_{r}h)\right\rangle\big)dr\right).

On the other hand, the central limit theorem proved in
[
28
]
shows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\big(V(\Phi_{0,r,h})-\left\langle V,\Gamma(\beta_{r}h)\right\rangle\big)dr\text{ is asymptotically Gaussian with variance }\sigma_{V}^{2}.

Hence if one can show the existence of
V\in C_{b}^{1}(H)
such that
\sigma_{V}^{2}\neq 0
, then (
4.11
) will imply that
P^{V_{\gamma}}_{0,t,h}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)
grows exponentially fast in
t
, which will contradicts with the subexponential growth (
4.9
) and the convergence (
4.10
). This contradiction then shows that the rate function
I
is nontrivial.

Step 3: Prove the existence of an observable function with nonzero asymptotic variance.
The rest of the proof is devoted to show the existence of such a function
V
that makes
\sigma_{V}^{2}\neq 0
through a contradiction argument as in
[
26
]
Proposition 4.1.4. For any
R>0
, we consider a
C_{b}^{1}
function
V_{R}:H\to\mathbb{R}
defined as follows:
0\leq V_{R}\leq 1
and

(4.12)

\displaystyle V_{R}(w)=1,\,\text{ when }w\in B_{R}^{H},\quad\text{ and }V_{R}(w)=0,\,\text{ when }w\in H\setminus B_{R+1}^{H}.

Since
\Gamma
is the unique eigenmeasure corresponding to
V=0
, it also has the support property as in Proposition
3.1
. Hence there is a
0<p=p(R)<1
does not depend on
h\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\displaystyle\Gamma(h)(B_{R+1}^{H})\leq p<1,\,h\in\mathbb{T},

which implies
\left\langle V_{R},\Gamma(h)\right\rangle\leq p
. As a result, one has

(4.13)

\displaystyle V_{\gamma,R}(w,h):=V_{R}(w)-\left\langle V_{R},\Gamma(h)\right\rangle\geq 1-p>0,\,\forall w\in B_{R}^{H},h\in\mathbb{T}.

Assume by contradiction that
\sigma_{V_{\gamma,R}}^{2}=0
. Recall the martingale approximation as in
[
28
]
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr=M_{N}-\chi(\mathcal{H}_{N})+\chi(\mathcal{H}_{0}),

where
M_{N}=\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr+\chi(\mathcal{H}_{N})-\chi(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is the Dynkin martingale and the corrector
\chi
is defined as (Note that
\mathcal{P}_{t}=\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}
with

)

\chi(w,h)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{t}V_{\gamma,R}(w,h)dr,\,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},

which is bounded modulo
e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}
, see
[
28
]
. We claim that the assumption
\sigma_{V_{\gamma,R}}^{2}=0
implies

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\gamma}\left\{M_{N}=0,\,\forall N\geq 0\right\}=1.

Indeed, by boundedness of
\chi
and integrability of
e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}M_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\right)^{2}+3\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}|\chi(\mathcal{H}_{N})|+3\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}|\chi(\mathcal{H}_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\right)^{2}+1\right).

Therefore by Proposition 4.12 in
[
28
]
on the asymptotic variance
\sigma_{V_{\gamma,R}}^{2}
, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}M_{N}^{2}=0,\text{ since }\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\right)^{2}=\sigma_{V_{\gamma,R}}^{2}=0.

Since
\gamma
is the invariant measure of the homogenized process, for the martingale difference sequence
Z_{k}=M_{k}-M_{k-1}
, one has
\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}Z_{k}^{2}=\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}Z_{1}^{2}
for all
k\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}M_{N}^{2}=\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}\right)^{2}=\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}^{2}=N\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}Z_{1}^{2}.

Combining this with (
4.16
) we obtain
\mathbf{E}_{\gamma}Z_{k}^{2}=0
for all
k
. Hence
M_{N}=0
almost surely for all
N
and the claim (
4.15
) is proved.

We are now ready to derive a contradiction. Note that by deterministic version of the estimate (
B.6
), there is a constant
C>1
independent of
h\in\mathbb{T}
such that the deterministic solution
\Phi_{0,t,h}^{d}(w)
to equation (
2.2
) without noise satisfies (see also
[
8
]
)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{0,t,h}^{d}(w)\|^{2}\leq e^{-\nu t}\|w\|^{2}+C,\,\forall w\in H,h\in\mathbb{T}.

Hence by choosing
R=2C
in the definition (
4.12
) of
V_{R}
, we have that for any initial condition
(w,h)\in B_{1}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}
, the deterministic solution
\Phi_{0,t,h}^{d}(w)\in B_{R}^{H}
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore for any initial
(w,h)\in B_{1}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}
and
N\geq 0
, there is a small positive probability (corresponding to those Brownian sample paths in a small neighborhood of the origin), such that the solution
\Phi_{0,t,h}(w)
to the noisy system (
2.2
) always stays in
B_{R}^{H}
for
t\in[0,N]
. Namely, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{(w,h)}\left\{\mathcal{H}_{r}\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T},\text{ for }0\leq r\leq N\right\}>0,\,(w,h)\in B_{1}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}.

Since
\gamma(B_{1}^{H}\times\mathbb{T})>0
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\gamma}\left\{\mathcal{H}_{r}\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T},\text{ for }0\leq r\leq N\right\}>0.

In view of (
4.13
), we obtain for any
N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\gamma}\left\{\int_{0}^{N}V_{\gamma,R}(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\geq N(1-p)\right\}>0.

This combined with (
4.15
), and boundedness of the corrector
\chi
over
B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}
, imply that (
4.14
) cannot hold with
\mathbf{P}_{\gamma}
probability
1
. This contradiction shows that
\sigma_{V_{\gamma,R}}^{2}\neq 0
.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1

We now supply a proof of Theorem
4.1
based on the results of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Items (1) and (2) in Theorem
4.1
.
Note that Item (2) in Theorem
4.1
directly follows from (
B.2
) with
R(w,h)=\eta\nu\|w\|_{1}^{2}
. The level set
\{(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}:R(w,h)\leq\alpha\}
for any
\alpha\geq 0
is compact since
\mathbb{T}
is compact and the embedding
H_{1}\to H
is compact.

Item (1) of Theorem
4.1
follows from the forward convergence in Theorem
2.1
, together with Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem and a
buc
-approximation argument. Indeed, taking
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{H}
in the forward convergence (
2.11
) in Theorem
2.1
, and noting the strict positivity of eigenfunction
F^{V}
, we have for
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t}\log\lambda_{t,h,V}-\frac{1}{t}\log\left\langle P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H},\mu\right\rangle\right|=0,

uniformly for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)
. The integral representation (
2.10
) and Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, as well as the continuity of
h\to\lambda^{V}(h)
imply that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\lambda_{t,h,V}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V}(\beta_{r}h)dr=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V}(h)m(dh).

A combination of (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) verifies (
4.1
) for
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and (
4.2
) in Item (1) of Theorem
4.1
. The existence of the limit in (
4.1
) for general
V\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
follows from a
buc
-approximation argument as in
[
23
]
, where the required exponential tightness has been verified in Item (2). The Lipschitz continuity is derived from the limit definition of
Q
. Indeed, note that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta,M}(H)
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV_{1},\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\big(\left\langle t(V_{1}-V_{2}),\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle+\left\langle tV_{2},\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle\big)

\displaystyle\leq e^{t\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV_{2},\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle.

By exchanging the role of
V_{1},V_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV_{1},\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\exp\left\langle tV_{2},\zeta_{t,h}\right\rangle\right|\leq\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\infty},

which by taking limit, implies the Lipschitz continuity of
Q
. The convexity follows from Hölder’s inequality. The proof of Item (1) is then complete.

Proof of Item (3) in Theorem
4.1
. The remainder of this subsection is then devoted to prove Item (3) on uniqueness of equilibrium state in Theorem
4.1
, which is accomplished through the following three steps. Fix any
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
.

Step 1: The auxiliary Markov evolution operators defined through Doob’s
h
-transform is uniquely ergodic
.
Define the following auxiliary evolution family of Markov operators
T^{V}_{0,t,h}
acting on
C_{b}(H)
,

(4.19)

\displaystyle T^{V}_{0,t,h}\phi(w)=\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}F^{V}(w,h)^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V}(\phi F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h))(w),\,\text{ for }(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},\phi\in C_{b}(H).

This is an analogue of the Doob’s
h
-transform in our current setting. See
[
4
]
and references therein for an introduction about the Doob’s
h
-transform, and
[
23
,
30
]
for the application in proving the uniqueness of equilibrium state in the time homogeneous case.

The operator
T^{V}_{0,t,h}
is Markov since
T^{V}_{0,t,h}\mathbb{1}_{H}=\mathbb{1}_{H}
by the eigenfunction property of
F^{V}
. It follows from the integral representation (
2.10
) that the eigenvalue satisfies the cocycle property

\lambda_{t+s,h,V}=\lambda_{t,\beta_{s}h,V}\lambda_{s,h,V},\,\text{ for }s,t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}.

Base on this and the cocycle property (
2.7
) of the Feynman-Kac operators, it is straightforward to check that the Markov operators
T^{V}_{0,t,h}
satisfy the cocycle property as well,

(4.20)

\displaystyle T_{0,s+t,h}^{V}=T_{0,s,h}^{V}T_{0,t,\beta_{s}h}^{V},\,s,t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}.

In addition, one directly verifies that the induced operator
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
acting on
C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
by

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}\phi(w,h)=T^{V}_{0,t,h}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w),\,\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}),

is a Markov semigroup, whose restriction on
C_{b}(H)
yields
T^{V}_{0,t,h}
.

Note that the action of
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
on
C_{b}(\mathbb{T})
is just the Koopman operator for the circle rotation. Hence any invariant measure
\mu
of
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
must have marginal
m
on
\mathbb{T}
. By disintegration theorem, the disintegration
\mu(h),h\in\mathbb{T}
of
\mu
is uniquely determined up to an
m
-null set and
\mu=\mu(h)m(dh)
. One can check that
\mu(h)
is an invariant measure of the evolution family
T_{0,t,h}^{V*}
, where
T_{0,t,h}^{V*}
is the dual of
T_{0,t,h}^{V}
. Namely, for every
t\geq 0
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle T_{0,t,h}^{V*}\mu(h)=\mu(\beta_{t}h),\quad m\text{-a.e }h\in\mathbb{T}.

Conversely, any measurable family of probability measures
\mu(h),h\in\mathbb{T}
satisfying (
4.22
) gives an invariant measure
\mu(h)m(dh)
of
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
. In this vein, unique ergodicity of Markov evolution family (
4.19
) is equivalent to the unique ergodicity of its homogenization (
4.21
) and hence we will not distinguish them.

The following lemma says that the semigroup
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
F^{V}(h)\Gamma^{V}(h)m(dh)\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, which is a consequence of the uniqueness of eigenmeasure from Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma_{V}(h)=F^{V}(\cdot,h)\Gamma^{V}(h)
. Then
\gamma_{V}(h)m(dh)
is the unique invariant measure of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
.

Proof.

We first verify that
\gamma_{V}(h)m(dh)
is indeed invariant under
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
. Since
\Gamma^{V}
is the eigenmeasure of
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
, one has

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{0,t,h}^{V}g,\gamma_{V}(h)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}F^{V}(\cdot,h)^{-1}(P_{0,t,h}^{V}g(\cdot)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)),F^{V}(\cdot,h)\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}g(\cdot)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h),P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\Gamma^{V}(h)\right\rangle=\left\langle g,F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\Gamma^{V}(\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle,

for every
g\in C_{b}(H)
. Hence
\gamma_{V}
is an invariant measure for
T_{0,t,h}^{V*}
, i.e.,

T_{0,t,h}^{V*}\gamma_{V}(h)=\gamma_{V}(\beta_{t}h).

Therefore
\gamma_{V}(h)m(dh)
is an invariant measure for the semigroup
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
.

We next show that this invariant measure is unique. Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
is a different invariant measure of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}^{V}_{t}
, then it must have marginal
m
on
\mathbb{T}
and its unique disintegration which we denote by
\gamma(h)
satisfies (
4.22
), i,e.,

T_{0,t,h}^{V*}\gamma(h)=\gamma(\beta_{t}h),\quad m\text{-a.e }h\in\mathbb{T}.

We claim that
\gamma
is necessarily equal to
\gamma_{V}
up to a null set. Let
\gamma_{0}(h)=F^{V}(\cdot,h)^{-1}\gamma(h)
and
\phi\in C_{b}(H)
. It follows from the invariance of
\gamma
that

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma_{0}(\beta_{t}h),\phi F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle=\left\langle\gamma(\beta_{t}h),\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle T_{0,t,h}^{V*}\gamma(h),\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}\left\langle F^{V}(\cdot,h)^{-1}\gamma(h),P_{0,t,h}^{V}\left(\phi(\cdot)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}\left\langle P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\gamma_{0}(h),\phi(\cdot)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle.

Since
\phi
is arbitrary and
F^{V}
is strictly positive, we deduce

P_{0,t,h}^{V*}\gamma_{0}(h)=\lambda_{t,h,V}\gamma_{0}(\beta_{t}h),\quad m\text{-a.e }h\in\mathbb{T}.

The uniqueness of eigenmeasure then implies
\gamma_{0}=\Gamma^{V}
up to a null set, which in turn shows that
\gamma=\gamma_{V}
for almost every
h\in\mathbb{T}
. Hence
\gamma_{V}(h)m(dh)
is the unique invariant measure of the semigroup
T_{t}^{V}
.

The proof of Lemma
4.2
is complete.
∎

Step 2: Correspondence between equilibrium states and zeros of the auxiliary rate function obtained from the auxiliary Markov evolution operators (
4.19
)
.
To show the uniqueness of equilibrium state associated with
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
as in (
4.4
), we shall establish a correspondence between equilibrium states and the zeros of an auxiliary rate function, where the later is connected with the invariant measure of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
. The uniqueness of the invariant measure proved in Lemma
4.2
thus will imply the uniqueness of equilibrium state.

More precisely, consider the auxiliary Feynman-Kac operators
R_{0,t,h}^{V,U}
associated with
T_{0,t,h}^{V}
and
U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
given by

R_{0,t,h}^{V,U}g(w)=\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}F^{V}(w,h)^{-1}(P_{0,t,h}^{V+U}g(\cdot)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h))(w),\quad g\in C_{b}(H),(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},

which induces as in (
4.21
) its homogenization
\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U}
acting on
C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
:

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U}g(w,h)=\lambda_{t,h,V}^{-1}F^{V}(w,h)^{-1}(P_{0,t,h}^{V+U}g(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h))(w),\quad g\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

We claim that the following limit is well defined, which gives the corresponding auxiliary pressure function,

(4.24)

\displaystyle Q^{V}(U):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log(R_{0,t,h}^{V,U}\mathbb{1}_{H})(w),\quad U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}),w\in H,h\in\mathbb{T},

which is independent of
w,h
.

Indeed, for
U\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, the forward convergence (
2.11
) in Theorem
2.1
implies that

(4.25)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,h,V+U}^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V+U}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w)-F^{V+U}(w,h)\left\langle\Gamma^{V+U}(\beta_{t}h),F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle\right|=0.

Estimating as in (
3.51
) and using the properties of eigenfunction
F^{V}
and eigenmeasure
\Gamma^{V+U}
as in Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.1
, one can show that

\displaystyle p_{1}\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\Gamma^{V+U}(\beta_{t}h),F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\Gamma^{V+U}(\beta_{t}h),F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle\leq p_{2},

for some constants
p_{1},p_{2}>0
. Therefore convergence (
4.25
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\lambda_{t,h,V+U}^{-1}\left\langle\Gamma^{V+U}(\beta_{t}h),F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)\right\rangle^{-1}P_{0,t,h}^{V+U}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w)-F^{V+U}(w,h)\right|=0.

Combining this with Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem and continuity of
h\to\lambda^{V+U}(h)
and integral representation (
2.10
) for potential
V+U
, we have

(4.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log P_{0,t,h}^{V+U}F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)(w)&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\lambda_{t,h,V+U}\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{V+U}(\beta_{r}h)dr=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{V+U}(h)m(dh)=Q(U+V).\end{split}

In view of (
4.24
) and the definition of
R_{0,t,h}^{V,U}
, limits (
4.18
) and (
4.26
) yield

(4.27)

\displaystyle Q^{V}(U)=Q(U+V)-Q(V),\,\text{ for }U\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

Therefore the limit (
4.24
) is well defined for
U\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. The existence of the limit (
4.24
) for general
U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
follows from a
buc
-approximation argument as in the proof of Item (1). The relation (
4.27
) extends to
U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
as well by Lipschitz continuity of the pressure functions.

Denote by
I^{V}(\mu):=\displaystyle\sup_{U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})}\big(\left\langle U,\mu\right\rangle-Q^{V}(U)\big)
the Legendre transformation of
Q^{V}
, which is the auxiliary rate function. Then relation (
4.27
) implies

I^{V}(\mu)=I(\mu)+Q(V)-\langle V,\mu\rangle.

Hence a measure
\mu_{V}
is an equilibrium state of the potential
V
, i.e,

Q(V)=\langle V,\mu_{V}\rangle-I(\mu_{V})

if and only if
I^{V}(\mu_{V})=0
.

Step 3: The zeros of the auxiliary rate function are invariant measures of the Markov evolution operators (
4.19
)
.
Following the scheme as in
[
29
]
, one can prove the following lemma that connects the zeros of the auxiliary rate function
I^{V}
with the invariant measure of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
. The major difference is in proving the time continuity and Feller property of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
since we are working with a semigroup obtained by coupling the evolution operator (
4.19
) with the circle rotation. The proof is postponed in the next subsection
4.2
for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
I^{V}(\mu)=0
then
\mu
is an invariant measure of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
.

Now since
I
is a good rate function, there is at least one equilibrium state for
V
. Hence the zeros of
I^{V}
is non-empty. In view of Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we know that the equilibrium state for
V
is unique, which is the unique invariant measure
F^{V}(h)\Gamma^{V}(h)m(dh)
of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
. This proves Item (3) in Theorem
4.1
.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is complete. ∎

in subsection

, which is the part that is different from the time homogeneous setting. Then in subsection

we prove Lemma
4.3
, by combining the time continuity and Feller property, following the scheme
as in
[

. Recall that
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})

4.2.1.
Time continuity and Feller property of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}

The following lemma establishes the time continuity and Feller property of the semigroup.

Lemma 4.4
.

The semigroup
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
has the following two properties:

(1)

For any
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
, the function
t\to\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(w,h)
is continuous.

(2)

For any
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
maps
C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
to itself.

Proof.

The proof is divided into the following two steps.

Step 1: Proof of time continuity
. It follows from the integral representation (
2.10
) for the eigenvalue that
\lambda_{t,h,V}
is continuous in time. In view of definition (
4.19
) and (
4.21
), we only need to show the time continuity of the function

(4.28)

\displaystyle t\in[0,\infty)\to P^{V}_{0,t,h}(\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h))(w).

Let’s write for convenience
F_{\phi}=\phi F^{V}
. Since
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and
F^{V}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, we know
F_{\phi}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
as well. In addition, since
F^{V}\in C(H\times\mathbb{T})
by Theorem
2.1
, we know that

(w,\tau)\to F^{V}(w,\beta_{\tau}h)\,\text{ is jointly continuous everywhere}.

Therefore so is
F_{\phi}
.

By triangle inequality, denoting
\Xi_{t}^{V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\right)
,

(4.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big|P^{V}_{0,t,h}(\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{t}h))(w)-P^{V}_{0,\tau,h}(\phi(\cdot,\beta_{\tau}h)F^{V}(\cdot,\beta_{\tau}h))(w)\Big|\\
&\leq\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\Big|\Xi_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t})-\Xi_{\tau}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t})\Big|+\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\Big|\Xi_{\tau}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t})-\Xi_{\tau}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})\Big|\\
&:=J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

Since
F_{\phi}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, we obtain

(4.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{1}&\leq C\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\Big|\Xi_{t}^{V}-\Xi_{\tau}^{V}\Big|\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,h})\right]\\
&\leq C\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\Big|\int_{\tau}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{r})dr\Big|\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,h})\right]\\
&\leq C|t-\tau|\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\end{split}

where in the second step we used the fact
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, and the Lipschitz continuity of
e^{x}
on some bounded interval determined by
m_{V},M_{V},t,\tau
and in the last inequality we used (
B.6
). Hence
J_{1}\to 0
as
t\to\tau
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq e^{\tau\|V\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{E}_{w}\Big|F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h},\beta_{t}h)-F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,\tau,h},\beta_{\tau}h)\Big|

By time continuity of sample paths of the solution
\Phi_{0,t,h}(w)
and joint continuity of
(w,t)\to F_{\phi}(w,\beta_{t}h)
, we know that

\displaystyle|F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h},\beta_{t}h)-F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,\tau,h},\beta_{\tau}h)\Big|\to 0

almost surely as
t\to\tau
. In addition, the fact
F_{\phi}\in L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H\times\mathbb{T})
implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{w}\Big|F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h},\beta_{t}h)-F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,\tau,h},\beta_{\tau}h)\Big|\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),

by (

). Hence by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have

as

.

In view of (
4.29
), we obtain the desired continuity of (

). The proof of
Step 1
is complete.

Step 2: Proof of Feller property
. Fix

. We first show that for any Lipschitz
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
,

(4.31)

\displaystyle(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(w,h)\,\text{ is continuous. }

Since

is continuous by Theorem

, in view of definitions (

) and (

\displaystyle(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(w,h),

where

. The main difficulties here are possible unboundedness of

and coupling of the Navier-Stokes family (

) with the circle rotation.

Denoting

, we write

(4.33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(w_{n},h_{n})-\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(w,h)&=\mathbf{E}_{(w_{n},h_{n})}\Xi_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t})-\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\Xi_{t}^{V}F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t})\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\Xi_{t}^{V}(w_{n},h_{n})-\Xi_{t}^{V}(w,h)\right)F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(w_{n},h_{n}))\right]\\
&\qquad+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}(w,h)\big(F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(w_{n},h_{n}))-F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(w,h))\big)\right]\\
&:=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

We can assume
w_{n},w\in B_{R_{0}}^{H}
for some
R_{0}>0
and all

. Note that since

, by the same reasoning as in (
4.30
), and the fact

as well as (

), we have

(4.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq C\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w_{n},h_{n}))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))\right|dr\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n}))\right]\\
&\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w_{n},h_{n}))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))\right|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n}))\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w_{n},h_{n}))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))\right|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

By

and triangle inequality, we have the existence of some modulus of continuity

such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w_{n},h_{n}))-V(\mathcal{H}_{r}(w,h))\right|^{2}dr

where we used (
B.4
) with Lipschitz continuity of
f
and (
B.5
) in the last inequity. Combining this with (
4.34
), we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\left(\kappa(|h_{n}-h|)+\|w_{n}-w\|+|h_{n}-h|\right).

We now estimate
I_{2}
. The boundedness of
V
yields

(4.36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-t\|V\|_{\infty}}|I_{2}|\leq&\mathbf{E}\left|F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(w_{n},h_{n}))-F_{\phi}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(w,h))\right|\\
&\leq\mathbf{E}\left|F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n}),\beta_{t}h_{n})-F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h}(w),\beta_{t}h_{n})\right|\\
&\qquad+\mathbf{E}\left|F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h}(w),\beta_{t}h_{n})-F_{\phi}(\Phi_{0,t,h}(w),\beta_{t}h)\right|\\
&:=I_{21}+I_{22}.\end{split}

Note that
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
is Lipschitz, and by Proposition
3.8
, for any
R>0
, the family
F^{V}(\cdot,h),h\in\mathbb{T}
is uniformly Lipschitz on

with a uniform bound

on the Lipschitz constants, therefore so is the the family

. In addition, (
B.4
) with Lipschitz continuity of
f
and (

) imply that

(4.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{E}\|\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n})-\Phi_{0,t,h}(w)\|&\leq\mathbf{E}\|\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n})-\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w)\|+\mathbf{E}\|\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w)-\Phi_{0,t,h}(w)\|\\
&\leq C\left(\|w_{n}-w\|+|h_{n}-h|\right),\end{split}

with constant
C=C(R_{0})
, where we used the fact that
w_{n},w\in B_{R_{0}}^{H}
for all
n\geq 1
. Since

(4.38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{21}&\leq\mathbf{E}\left|(\mathbb{1}_{B_{R}}F_{\phi})(\Phi_{0,t,h_{n}}(w_{n}),\beta_{t}h_{n})-(\mathbb{1}_{B_{R}}F_{\phi})(\Phi_{0,t,h}(w),\beta_{t}h_{n})\right|\\

By Hölder’s inequality and Markov inequality, as well as (

(4.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{0,t,\mathfrak{h}}\left(\mathbb{1}_{B_{R}^{c}}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right)(u)&\leq\left(P_{0,t,\mathfrak{h}}\mathbb{1}_{B_{R}^{c}}(u)\right)^{1/2}\left(P_{0,t,\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(u)\right)^{1/2}\\

for all

.

We now look at

\displaystyle F_{\phi}(u,\beta_{t}h_{n})\to F_{\phi}(u,\beta_{t}h),\,\text{ as }h_{n}\to h.

integrable by (

\displaystyle I_{22}=\int_{H}\left|F_{\phi}(u,\beta_{t}h_{n})-F_{\phi}(u,\beta_{t}h)\right|P_{0,t,h}(w,du)\to 0\,\text{ as }h_{n}\to h.

In view of (

.

Combining this with (

Now for any
\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, we can find a sequence

of bounded Lipschitz functions such that

(4.40)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(z_{n})-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(z_{0})\right|\\

We can assume the existence of

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(z)-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi_{m}(z)\right|\\

\displaystyle P_{0,t,\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{1}_{K_{R}^{c}}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(u))\leq CR^{-1},

. Since

Combining this with (

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(z)-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi_{m}(z)\right|\leq\sup_{(w,h)\in K_{R}\times T}|\phi(w,h)-\phi_{m}(w,h)|+CR^{-1}

In this subsection we prove Lemma

. Consider the infinitesimal generator

of the Markov semigroup
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}
. The domain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi=\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\mathbb{L}_{V}\phi,\,\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{L}_{V}).

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

The proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1: Proof of Item (1)
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and assume

(4.46)

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi,\mu_{1}\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi,\mu_{2}\right\rangle,\,\text{ for all }\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{+}.

It is known that the following set

\displaystyle C_{b}^{+}(H\times\mathbb{T}):=\left\{U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T}):\inf_{(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}}U(w,h)>0\right\}

is determining. We will show that
\mathcal{D}_{+}
is rich enough to approximate any function in
C_{b}^{+}(H\times\mathbb{T})
.

Take any
U\in C_{b}^{+}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. We claim that
\overline{U}_{s}:=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}Udr\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
for any

. Indeed, since
\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}
is conservative, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}\mathbb{1}=\mathbb{1}
, we have

\displaystyle 0<m_{U}\leq\overline{U}_{s}(w,h)\leq M_{U},\,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},

where
m_{U},M_{U}
are the infimum and supremum of
U
. On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\overline{U}_{s}-\overline{U}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{t+r}^{V}Udr-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}Udr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\int_{s}^{s+t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}Udr-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}Udr=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}\widehat{U}^{s}dr,

where
\widehat{U}_{s}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{s}^{V}U-U}{s}
. Since
U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that
\widehat{U}_{s}\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. Hence
\overline{U}_{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
and the claim is proved.

It then follows from (
4.46
) that

(4.47)

\displaystyle\left\langle\overline{U}_{s},\mu_{1}\right\rangle=\left\langle\overline{U}_{s},\mu_{2}\right\rangle,\,\text{ for all }s>0.

Again the fact
U\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and Lemma
4.4
yield

\lim_{s\to 0}\overline{U}_{s}(w,h)=U(w,h),\,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}.

Therefore by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and (
4.47
), one has

\displaystyle\left\langle U,\mu_{1}\right\rangle=\left\langle U,\mu_{2}\right\rangle.

Since
U\in C_{b}^{+}(H\times\mathbb{T})
is arbitrary, we conclude
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

Step 2: Proof of Item (2)
. We first claim that for any
U,\phi\in C_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})

of the semigroup

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}(U\phi_{t-r})dr.

Note that by (
4.19
), (
4.21
) and (
4.23
), denoting
\Xi_{s,t}^{V}=\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}V(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})d\tau\right)
, and
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U}\phi-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,t}^{V}(\Xi_{0,t}^{U}-1)(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,t}^{V}\left(\int_{0}^{t}-\frac{d}{dr}\Xi_{r,t}^{U}dr\right)(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,t}^{V}U(H_{r})\Xi_{r,t}^{U}(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{t})\right]dr.

Therefore by the Markov property, and time homogeneity of
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V+U}
, as well as cocycle property of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{t,h,V}
from the integral representation (

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,r}^{V}U(H_{r})\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{r,t}^{V+U}(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{r}\right]\right]dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{t,h,V}F^{V}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,r}^{V}U(H_{r})\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{r,t}^{V+U}(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{r}\right]\right]dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\lambda_{r,h,V}F^{V}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,r}^{V}U(H_{r})F^{V}(H_{r})\frac{1}{\lambda_{t-r,\beta_{r}h,V}F^{V}(H_{r})}\mathcal{P}_{t-r}^{V+U}(\phi F^{V})(\mathcal{H}_{r})\right]dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\lambda_{r,h,V}F^{V}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{0,r}^{V}U(H_{r})F^{V}(H_{r})\mathcal{R}_{t-r}^{V,U}(\mathcal{H}_{r})\right]dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}(U\phi_{t-r})dr

which completes the proof of the claim.

Now for any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi(w,h)=\phi(w,h)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}\mathbb{L}_{V}\phi(w,h)dr.

Combining this and with
U_{\phi}=-\frac{\mathbb{L}_{V}\phi}{\phi}
it is straightforward to verify that
\phi_{t}\equiv\phi
solves the equation

\phi_{t}=\mathcal{T}_{t}^{V}\phi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{r}^{V}(U_{\phi}\phi_{t-r})dr.

Hence we have
\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U_{\phi}}\phi=\phi
for all
t\geq 0
. Since
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{+}
, in view of (
4.23
), there are constants
m_{\phi},M_{\phi}>0
such that

\displaystyle m_{\phi}R_{0,t,h}^{V,U_{\phi}}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)\leq\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U_{\phi}}\phi(w,h)\leq M_{\phi}R_{0,t,h}^{V,U_{\phi}}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w).

Also note

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{R}_{t}^{V,U_{\phi}}\phi(w,h)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\phi(w,h)=0.

Hence we obtain the desired Item (2)

\displaystyle Q^{V}(U_{\phi})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log R_{0,t,h}^{V,U_{\phi}}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)=0.

The proof is complete.

∎

Appendix

A.
A Kifer type criterion

The following Kifer type theorem was established in
[
23
]
, which is based on the work of Kifer
[
24
]
in the compact setting. Suppose that
X
is a polish space,
\Theta
is a directed set and
\{\zeta_{\theta},\theta\in\Theta\}
is a family of random probability measures on
X
that are defined on some probability spaces
(\Omega_{\theta},\mathcal{F}_{\theta},\mathbf{P}_{\theta})
.
r:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
is a given positive function such that
\lim_{\theta\in\Theta}r(\theta)=+\infty
.

Theorem A.1

(Kifer Type Criterion
[
23
]
)
.

Assume that the family
\{\zeta_{\theta},\theta\in\Theta\}
satisfies the following two properties:

(1)

The following limit exists for any
V\in C_{b}(X)
:

Q(V)=\lim_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{r(\theta)}\log\int_{\Omega_{\theta}}\exp(r(\theta)\left\langle V,\zeta_{\theta}\right\rangle)\mathbf{P}_{\theta}(d\omega).

(2)

There is a function
R:X\to[0,\infty]
with compact level sets
\{x\in X:R(x)\leq a\}
for all
a\geq 0
such that

\int_{\Omega_{\theta}}\exp(r(\theta)\left\langle R,\zeta_{\theta}\right\rangle)\mathbf{P}_{\theta}(d\omega)\leq Ce^{cr(\theta)},\,\theta\in\Theta

where
C
and
c
are positive constants.

Then
Q
is
1
-Lipschitz and convex, and its Legendre transform
I
given by

I(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\sup_{V\in C_{b}(X)}\left(\left\langle V,\mu\right\rangle-V(Q)\right),&\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X),\\
\qquad+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

is a good rate function, for which the following upper bound holds for any closed subset
F\in\mathcal{P}(X)
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{r(\theta)}\log\mathbf{P}_{\theta}\{\zeta_{\theta}\in F\}\leq-\inf_{\mu\in F}I(\mu).

If in addition, the following property is satisfied:

(3)

Suppose there exists a vector space
\mathcal{V}\subset C_{b}(X)
such that the restrictions of its functions to any compact set
K\subset X
form a dense subspace in
C(K)
and for any
V\in\mathcal{V}
there is a unique equilibrium state, i.e., a unique
\mu_{V}\in\mathcal{P}(H)
such that

Q(V)=\left\langle V,\mu_{V}\right\rangle-I(\mu_{V}).

Then the following lower bound holds for any open subset
G\in\mathcal{P}(X)
:

\displaystyle\liminf_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{r(\theta)}\log\mathbf{P}_{\theta}\{\zeta_{\theta}\in G\}\geq-\inf_{\mu\in G}I(\mu).

C.
Growth condition, uniform irreducibility and uniform Feller property

C.1.
The Growth Condition

The following growth condition is useful when working with the non-conservative Feynman-Kac operator
P^{V}_{0,t,h}
in a non-compact phase space, which was first introduced in
[
23
]
in the time homogeneous setting.

Proposition C.1
.

Assume
V\in B_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. Recall that
\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)=e^{\eta\|w\|^{2}}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)=1+\|w\|^{2q}
. Then there are
R_{0}>0
and
q=q(V)\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle M_{\eta}:=\sup_{t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}}\frac{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}^{\infty}(H)}}{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}<\infty.

\displaystyle M_{q}:=\sup_{t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}}\frac{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H)}}{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}<\infty.

\displaystyle M_{H_{2}}:=\sup_{t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}}\frac{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}N_{2}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}}^{\infty}(H)}}{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}<\infty,\quad N_{2}(w)=\|w\|_{2}^{2},

for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}]
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{R_{0}}
is the
L^{\infty}
norm on
B_{R_{0}}^{H_{2}}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\leq M_{\eta}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w),\,\text{ for all }t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T},w\in H.

The proof of Proposition
C.1
is based on
[
30
]
where the time homogeneous case was proved, which is based on the following hyper-exponential recurrence property of the solutions to the Navier-Stokes system, see also
[
23
,
26
]
.

Proposition C.2
.

For any
R>0,h\in\mathbb{T}
, let
\tau_{h}(R)
be the first hitting time of the solution
\Phi_{0,t,h}
to the set
B_{R}^{H_{2}}
:

\displaystyle\tau_{h}(R)=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\Phi_{0,t,h}\in B_{R}^{H_{2}}\right\}.

Then for any
\gamma>0
, there is
q_{0}=q_{0}(\gamma)>\gamma/\nu
so that for any
q\geq q_{0}
, there are positive numbers
R,C
depending on
q
such that

(C.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp\left(\gamma\tau_{h}(R)\right)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\,w\in H.

Here
\nu>0
is the viscosity.

For the sake of completeness, we provide proofs for both propositions in the current time inhomogeneous setting. The translation identity (
2.6
) and uniform in
h
bounds from Proposition
B.1
are the crucial ingredients when dealing with time inhomogeneity throughout the proof. We first give the proof of Proposition
C.1
based on Proposition
C.2
, whose proof is given at the end of this subsection.

Proof of Proposition
C.1
.

Since we are estimating the ration of
P_{0,t,h}^{V}
, we can assume

m_{V}=\inf_{(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}}V(w,h)\geq 0.

Otherwise we can replace it by
V-m_{V}
. The proof is divided into four steps.

Step 1: Proof of
M_{q}<\infty
when
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
is replaced by
\mathbb{1}_{H}
. Here we aim to show the existence of
q_{0},R_{0}\geq 1
such that

(C.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}}\frac{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{L_{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}^{\infty}(H)}}{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}}<\infty.

In view of Proposition
C.2
, we pick any
q,R_{0}
the numbers corresponding to
\gamma=\|V\|_{\infty}
, with
\tau_{h}(R_{0})
be the first hitting time of the ball
B_{R_{0}}^{H_{2}}
. Now we write

(C.3)

\displaystyle P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\Xi_{t}^{V}=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\mathbb{1}_{G_{t,h}}\Xi_{t}^{V}+\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\mathbb{1}_{G_{t,h}^{c}}\Xi_{t}^{V}:=I_{1}+I_{2},

where
\Xi_{t}^{V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(H_{r})dr\right)
and
G_{t,h}=\left\{\tau_{h}(R_{0})>t\right\}
. Since
V\geq 0
and
\tau_{h}(R_{0})>t
on
G_{t,h}
, we obtain

(C.4)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\Xi_{\tau_{h}(R_{0})}^{V}\leq\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp\left(\gamma\tau_{h}(R_{0})\right)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}},

by Proposition
C.2
. By the strong Markov inequality and the fact that
\Phi_{0,\tau_{h}(R_{0}),h}\in B_{R_{0}}^{H_{2}}
, we have

(C.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\mathbb{1}_{G_{t,h}^{c}}\Xi_{\tau_{h}(R_{0})}^{V}\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\exp\left(\int_{\tau_{h}(R_{0})}^{t}V(H_{r})dr\right)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{h}(R_{0})}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\mathbb{1}_{G_{t,h}^{c}}\Xi_{\tau_{h}(R_{0})}^{V}P_{\tau_{h}(R_{0}),t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{h}(R_{0}),h})\right]\\
&\leq\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{G_{t,h}^{c}}\exp(\gamma\tau_{h}(R_{0}))P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{h}(R_{0}),h})\right]\\
&\leq\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\exp(\gamma\tau_{h}(R_{0}))\right]\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}},\end{split}

by Proposition
C.2
again. By combining (
C.3
)-(
C.5
) we obtain the desired (
C.2
).

The proof of
Step 1
is complete.

Step 2: Proof of
M_{q}<\infty
. We first show the estimate for discrete time and then pass to the continuous time case by evolution property. Noting that
\delta:=e^{\gamma-\nu q}<1
by Proposition
C.2
, hence by translation identity (
2.6
) one has

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)

\displaystyle=P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\left(P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right)(w)

\displaystyle\leq P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\left(e^{\gamma}P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right)(w)\leq e^{\gamma}\left(e^{-\nu q}P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)+C(q)P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)+C(q,\gamma)P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)

where we used estimate (
B.6
) in the penultimate step and monotonicity of
P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}
in
k
in the last step. Iterating this inequality we obtain

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\leq\delta^{k}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)+\frac{C}{1-\delta}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w),

where the constant
C
does not depend on
w,h,k
. Combining this with (
C.2
), we obtain
M_{q}<\infty
in the discrete time case.

For the continuous time case, denoting
[t]
the integer part of
t
, by (
B.6
) and (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle P^{V}_{[t],t,h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)=P^{V}_{0,t-[t],\beta_{[t]}h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\leq e^{\gamma}P_{0,t-[t],\beta_{[t]}h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\leq e^{\gamma}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)+C,\,w\in H,h\in\mathbb{T}.

Hence

\displaystyle P^{V}_{0,t,h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)

\displaystyle=P^{V}_{0,[t],h}\left(P^{V}_{[t],t,h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\right)(w)\leq e^{\gamma}P^{V}_{0,[t],h}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)+CP^{V}_{0,[t],h}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,[t],h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}

by the proved discrete time case, estimate (
C.2
), and monotonicity of
t\to\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}
. Therefore
M_{q}<\infty
in the continuous time as well.

The proof of
Step 2
is complete.

Step 3: Proof of
M_{\eta}<\infty
. As in the proof of the previous step, we only need to show the discrete time case. The passage to the continuous time is similar by invoking (
B.1
).

Let
A>0
and note that

(C.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)&=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\|\Phi_{0,k,h}\|^{2}\leq A}\Xi_{k}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(\Phi_{0,k,h})\right]+\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\|\Phi_{0,k,h}\|^{2}\geq A}\Xi_{k}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(\Phi_{0,k,h})\right]\\
&:=I_{k,h}+J_{k,h}.\end{split}

It follow from (
C.2
) that

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}},\,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}.

Therefore

(C.7)

\displaystyle I_{k,h}\leq e^{\eta A}P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}},

where
C
is independent of
k,h,w
. By (
2.6
), (
B.3
) and denoting
F(w)=\|w\|^{2}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)
for
w\in H
, we have

(C.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{k,h}\leq A^{-1}P_{0,k,h}^{V}F(w)&=A^{-1}P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\left(P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}^{V}F\right)(w)\\
&\leq e^{\gamma}A^{-1}P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\left(P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}F\right)(w)\\
&\leq A^{-1}CP_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w).\end{split}

Combining (
C.6
)-(
C.8
) and choosing
A
large such that
\delta:=A^{-1}C<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}+\delta P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w).

By iteration, one has

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\leq\frac{C}{1-\delta}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}+\delta^{q}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w).

Hence
M_{\eta}<\infty
.

The proof of
Step 3
is complete.

Step 4: Proof of
M_{H_{2}}<\infty
. This follows from (
B.6
) and

proved in the previous step. Indeed, since
\mathfrak{m}_{q}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}
, one has

\displaystyle P_{0,k,h}^{V}N_{2}(w)

\displaystyle=P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}(P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}^{V}N_{2})(w)\leq e^{\gamma}P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}(P_{0,1,\beta_{k-1}h}N_{2})(w)

\displaystyle\leq CP_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k-1,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\|P_{0,k,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}.

Hence

, which finishes the proof of
Step 4
.

The proof of Proposition
C.1
is complete.
∎

Proof of Proposition
C.2
.

The proof is divided into two steps. First we prove hyper-exponential recurrence in
H
and then we show the desired recurrence in
H_{2}
.

Step 1: Hyper-exponential recurrence in
H
.
For any
r>0
, denote
\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)
the first hitting time

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\Phi_{0,t,h}\in B_{r}^{H}\right\}.

We aim to show that for any
\gamma>0
, there is
q_{0}=q_{0}(\gamma)
so that for any
q\geq q_{0}
, there are positive numbers
r,\overline{C}
depending on
q
such that

(C.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp\left(\gamma\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)\right)\leq\overline{C}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\,w\in H.

Denote by
N_{0}^{2q}(w)=\|w\|^{2q},\,w\in H
. It follows from (
B.6
) that

(C.10)

\displaystyle P_{0,1,h}N_{0}^{2q}(w)=\mathbf{E}_{w}\|\Phi_{0,1,h}\|^{2q}\leq\delta(\|w\|^{2q}\vee r),\,(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T},

where
\delta=e^{-\nu q}<1
and
r=e^{\nu q}C>1
, with the constant
C=C(q)
from (
B.6
). Let

\displaystyle R_{k,h}(w)=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k\}}\|\Phi_{0,k,h}\|^{2q}\right).

Note that
\mathbb{1}_{\{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k\}}\geq\mathbb{1}_{\{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k+1\}}
and on the event
\{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k\}
one has
\|\Phi_{0,k,h}\|^{2q}>r^{2q}>r
. Therefore by Markov inequality, (
2.6
) and (
C.11
), we have

\displaystyle R_{k+1,h}(w)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k}\|\Phi_{0,k+1,h}\|^{2q}\right)=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\|\Phi_{0,k+1,h}\|^{2q}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k}P_{k,k+1,h}N_{0}^{2q}(\Phi_{0,k,h})\right)=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k}P_{0,1,\beta_{k}h}N_{0}^{2q}(\Phi_{0,k,h})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathbf{E}_{w}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k}\|\Phi_{0,k,h}\|^{2q}\right)=\delta R_{k,h}(w).

By iteration, one has

\displaystyle R_{k,h}(w)\leq\delta^{k}\|w\|^{2q},

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k)\leq r^{-2q}R_{k,h}(w)\leq\delta^{k}r^{-2q}\|w\|^{2q},\,k\geq 0,w\in H.

Hence for any
\gamma>0
, by choosing

, we obtain the desired (

):

(C.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp\left(\gamma\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)\right)&\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\gamma k}\mathbf{P}_{w}(\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)>k-1)\\
&\leq 1+r^{-2q}\|w\|^{2q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\gamma k}\delta^{k-1}\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\end{split}

for some constant
C
independent of
h\in\mathbb{T}
.

Step 2: Hyper-exponential recurrence in
H_{2}
. First it follows from (
B.6
) and Markov inequality that for any
p\in(0,1)
, there is a large
R>0
such that

(C.12)

\displaystyle P_{0,1,h}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(w)=\mathbf{P}_{w}\left(\|\Phi_{0,1,h}\|_{2}\geq R\right)\leq R^{-2}\mathbf{E}_{w}\|\Phi_{0,1,h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq p,

for all
h\in\mathbb{T}
and
w\in B_{r}^{H}
, where

is the radius from (
C.9
) with corresponding
\overline{\tau}_{h}(r)
. Define recursively the following sequence of stopping times

\displaystyle\tau_{0,h}^{\prime}=\overline{\tau}_{h}(r),\,\tau_{n,h}^{\prime}=\inf\left\{t\geq\tau_{n-1,h}^{\prime}+1:\Phi_{0,t,h}\in B_{r}^{H}\right\},\,\text{ and }\tau_{n,h}=\tau_{n,h}^{\prime}+1,\,\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Note that
\tau_{0,h}^{\prime}
is the first hitting time of the ball
B_{r}^{H}
, and
\tau_{n,h}^{\prime}
is the first recurrence time after
\tau_{n-1,h}^{\prime}+1
. Also let

\displaystyle\hat{n}=\min\left\{n\geq 0:\Phi_{0,\tau_{n,h},h}\in B_{R}^{H_{2}}\right\}

be the first time the solution entering the ball
B_{R}^{H_{2}}
among the sequence
\tau_{n,h}
. Note that for any
k,M\geq 1
, since
\left\{\tau_{h}(R)>\tau_{k,h}\right\}\subset\left\{\hat{n}>k\right\}
, we have

(C.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)\geq M)&=\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)\geq M,\tau_{k,h}<M)+\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)\geq M,\tau_{k,h}\geq M)\\
&\leq\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)>\tau_{k,h})+\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{k,h}\geq M)\\
&\leq\mathbf{P}_{w}(\hat{n}>k)+\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{k,h}\geq M).\end{split}

In what follows we estimate the probabilities on the right hand side.

It follows from translation identity (
2.6
), estimate (
C.12
), the fact
\Phi_{0,\tau_{k,h}^{\prime},h}\in B_{r}^{H}
and the strong Markov property that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\hat{n}>k)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\hat{n}>k-1\right\}}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k,h},h})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\hat{n}>k-1\right\}}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k,h},h})|\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{k,h}^{\prime}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\hat{n}>k-1\right\}}P_{\tau_{k,h}^{\prime},\tau_{k,h},h}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k,h}^{\prime},h})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\hat{n}>k-1\right\}}P_{0,1,\beta_{\tau_{k,h}^{\prime}}h}\mathbb{1}_{H\setminus B_{R}^{H_{2}}}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k,h}^{\prime},h})\right]\leq p\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\hat{n}>k-1\right\}}\right]=p\mathbf{P}_{w}(\hat{n}>k-1),

which, by iteratively using the strong Markov property, implies

(C.14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\hat{n}>k)\leq p^{k},\,\text{ for }w\in H,k\geq 0.

In a similar fashion, by Markov’s inequality, the recurrence (
C.9
) with constant
\overline{C}
, and the strong Markov property, we have for any
M\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{k,h}\geq M)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma M}\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp(\gamma\tau_{k,h})=e^{-\gamma M}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\exp(\gamma(\tau_{k,h}^{\prime}+1))|\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{k-1,h}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma M}e^{\gamma}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\exp(\gamma\tau_{k-1,h})\mathbf{E}_{\Phi_{0,\tau_{k-1,h},h}}\exp(\gamma\overline{\tau}_{\beta_{\tau_{k-1,h}}h}(r))\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma M}\overline{C}e^{\gamma}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\exp(\gamma\tau_{k-1,h})\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k-1,h},h})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma M}\overline{C}e^{\gamma}\mathbf{E}_{w}\left[\exp(\gamma\tau_{k-1,h})\mathbf{E}_{\Phi_{0,\tau_{k-1,h}^{\prime},h}}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,\tau_{k-1,h},h})\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma M}\overline{C}e^{\gamma}(e^{-\nu q}r^{2q}+C(q))\mathbf{E}_{w}\exp(\gamma\tau_{k-1,h}),

where we used the fact that
\tau_{k-1,h}
is
\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{k-1,h}^{\prime}}
measurable in the last equality, and
(
B.6
) with
\Phi_{0,\tau_{k-1,h}^{\prime},h}\in B_{r}^{H}
in the last inequality. Hence by iteratively using the strong Markov inequality, we obtain

(C.15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{k,h}\geq M)\leq e^{-\gamma M}C^{k}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w),\,w\in H,h\in\mathbb{T},

with
C=\overline{C}e^{\gamma}(e^{-\nu q}r^{2q}+C(q))
independent of
w,M,h
.

Now combining (
C.13
)-(
C.15
), one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)\geq M)\leq p^{k}+e^{-\gamma M}C^{k}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w).

Choosing
k=\frac{\gamma M}{2\ln C}
and
R
large such that
p=1/C
by (
C.12
), then we have

(C.16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{w}(\tau_{h}(R)\geq M)\leq 2e^{-\gamma M/2}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)

for any
\gamma>0,M\geq 1
and
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
. This implies the desired hyper-exponential recurrence (
C.1
) by following the same reasoning as in (
C.11
). Note that to obtain (
C.1
) with any
\gamma>0
we can replace the
\gamma
in (
C.16
) by
4\gamma
.

The proof of Proposition
C.2
is complete.
∎

C.2.
Uniform Irreducibility

In this subsection we prove the uniform irreducibility, which follows from the well-known controllability results of Agrachev and Sarychev
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
, the cocycle property (
2.7
) and standard compactness arguments.

Proposition C.3

(Uniform Irreducibility )
.

Let
V\in B_{b}(H\times\mathbb{T})
. Given any
\rho,r>0,R\geq 1
, as long as

\ell\geq 2\ln R
, then there is
p=p(V,\rho,r,\ell)>0
such that

(C.17)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w_{0},h,w)\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}\times B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}}P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq p

Moreover, there is a large
L_{0}=L_{0}(\ell,R)
such that for any
L\geq L_{0}
, there holds

(C.18)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w_{0},h,w)\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}\times B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}}P_{0,\ell,h}^{V}(w_{0},B^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}_{r}(w))\geq p/2.

If
\displaystyle m_{V}=\inf_{w\in H}V(w)\geq 0
, then one can choose
p=p(\rho,r)
that is independent of
V,\ell
.

Proof.

First of all, we claim the existence a large time
t_{0}
depending on
R
and a large number
r_{0}>0
independent of
w_{0},R,h,t_{0}
, such that

(C.19)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w_{0},h)\in B^{H}_{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{0,t_{0},h}(w_{0},B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}})\geq 1/2.

Let
N(w)=\|w\|^{2}
and
N_{2}(w)=\|w\|_{2}^{2}
. By estimate (
B.6
), we have for any
t\geq 1
, and
w_{0}\in B^{H}_{R}
,

\displaystyle P_{0,t,h}N_{2}(w_{0})

\displaystyle=P_{0,t-1,h}(P_{0,1,\beta_{t-1}h}N_{2})(w_{0})\leq C\left(P_{0,t-1,h}N^{6}(w_{0})+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(e^{-6(t-1)}\|w_{0}\|^{12}+1)\leq C(e^{-6(t+1)}R^{12}+1)

where
C
is a constant independent of
t,h,R
. Since
P_{0,t,h}(w_{0},\cdot)
is concentrated on
H_{2}
, by choosing
t_{0}
large so that
e^{-6(t_{0}+1)}R^{12}\leq 1
and choosing a constant
r_{0}>2\sqrt{C}
, we have by Markov inequality that

(C.20)

\displaystyle P_{0,t_{0},h}(w_{0},B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}})

\displaystyle=1-P_{0,t_{0},h}(w_{0},H_{2}\setminus B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}})\geq 1-\frac{(P_{0,t_{0},h}N_{2}(w_{0}))^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}\geq 1/2.

Next we show the existence of
p_{0}>0
, depending only on
r_{0},r,\rho
such that

(C.21)

\displaystyle\inf_{(w_{0},h,w)\in B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}}\times\mathbb{T}\times B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}}P_{0,1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq p_{0}.

Let

\Omega_{1}=\{X\in C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d}):X(0)=0\}

be the restriction of
\Omega
on
[0,1]
. In view of the controllability results of Agrachev and Sarychev
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
(see
[
27
]
for a verification in the time inhomogeneous case), the Navier-Stokes system (
2.1
) is approximately controllable. That is, for any
(w_{0},v,h)\in H^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
and
r>0
, if we replace
f(t,x)
by
f(\beta_{t}h,x)
in
(
2.1
)
, then there is a piecewise smooth map
X=X_{w_{0},v,h,r}\in\Omega_{1}
such that
\Phi_{0,1,h}(w_{0},X)\in\mathring{B}_{r/4}^{H}(v),

where
\Phi_{0,t,h}(w_{0},X)
is the solution of (
2.1
) with symbol
h
and
W(t)
replaced by
X(t)
.
Since
\Phi_{0,1,h}(w_{0},\cdot):\Omega_{1}\to H
is continuous, there is an
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(w_{0},v,h,r)>0
such that for any
Y\in\mathring{B}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega_{1}}(X)
one has

\|\Phi_{0,1,h}(w_{0},Y)-\Phi_{0,1,h}(w_{0},X)\|<r/4.

Hence by triangle inequality and regularizing property of the Navier-Stokes systems, we obtain that
Y\in\mathring{B}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega_{1}}(X)
implies
\Phi_{0,1,h}(w_{0},Y)\in\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v)
.

Since
B^{H_{2}}_{\rho}
is compact in
H
, its open cover
\left\{\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v)\right\}_{v\in B^{H_{2}}_{\rho}}
has a finite subcover
\left\{\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v_{k})\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
where the positive integer
K=K(r,\rho)
. Let
\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon(w_{0},v_{k},h,r)
and
X_{k}
be the path from the controllability result corresponding to
v_{k}
. Note that
\mathbf{P}(\mathring{B}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\Omega_{1}}(X_{k}))>0
for each
1\leq k\leq K
since the Wiener measure has a full support. Hence

p(w_{0},h,r,\rho):=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\mathbf{P}(\mathring{B}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\Omega_{1}}(X_{k}))>0.

Now for any
w\in B^{H_{2}}_{\rho}
, there is some
v_{k}
such that
w\in\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v_{k})
. Therefore
\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v_{k})\subset B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w)
. It then follows that

\displaystyle P_{0,1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq P_{0,1,h}(w_{0},\mathring{B}_{r/2}^{H,H_{2}}(v_{k}))\geq\mathbf{P}(\mathring{B}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\Omega_{1}}(X_{k}))\geq p(w_{0},h,r,\rho),\quad(w_{0},h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}.

This combined with a continuity and compactness argument similar to the proof of Lemma 4.4 in
[
27
]
implies the existence of a number
p_{0}=p_{0}(r_{0},r,\rho)>0
such that

\inf_{(w_{0},h)\in B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{0,1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq p_{0},

where
B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}}
is from (
C.20
).

Lastly, it follows from the Chapman-Kolmogorov relation and inequalities (
C.19
) and (
C.21
) that

(C.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))&=\int_{H}P_{0,1,\beta_{t_{0}}h}(u,B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))P_{0,t_{0},h}(w_{0},du)\\
&\geq\int_{B_{r_{0}}^{H_{2}}}P_{0,1,\beta_{t_{0}}h}(u,B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))P_{0,t_{0},h}(w_{0},du)\geq p_{0}/2\end{split}

for any
(w_{0},h,w)\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}\times B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}
.
Hence

P^{V}_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq e^{(t_{0}+1)m_{V}}P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))\geq\frac{p_{0}e^{(t_{0}+1)m_{V}}}{2}.

The inequality (
C.17
) then follows with
\ell=t_{0}+1\geq 2\ln R
and
p=\frac{p_{0}e^{(t_{0}+1)m_{V}}}{2}
.

For any
L>0
, we have by estimate (
B.6
)

\displaystyle P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},H\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}})\leq L^{-2}\mathbf{E}_{w_{0}}\|\Phi_{0,t_{0}+1,h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq CL^{-2},

where the constant
C=C(R,t_{0})
does not depend on
h
. There is
L_{0}=L_{0}(R)
such that for all
L\geq L_{0}
,

\sup_{(w_{0},h)\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},H\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}})\leq p_{0}/4.

Hence by (
C.22
), we deduce

\displaystyle P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,B_{L}^{H_{2}}}_{r}(w))

\displaystyle=P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}}_{r}(w))-P_{0,t_{0}+1,h}(w_{0},B^{H,H_{2}\setminus B_{L}^{H_{2}}}_{r}(w))

\displaystyle\geq p_{0}/2-p_{0}/4=p_{0}/4,\quad\text{ for all }(w_{0},h,w)\in B_{R}^{H}\times\mathbb{T}\times B_{\rho}^{H_{2}}.

Inequality (
C.18
) then follows. The proof is complete.
∎

C.3.
Uniform Feller Property

In this subsection we prove the uniform Feller property. We follow the arguments presented in
[
31
]
and use the cocycle property of the inhomogeneous Feynman-Kac family.

Proposition C.4

(Uniform Feller Property)
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H,f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},H_{2})
in (
2.2
). For any
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
there is an
R_{0}=R_{0}(V)>0
such that for any
R>0
the family

\left\{\frac{P_{0,t,h}^{V}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t}h)}{\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}},t\geq 0,h\in\mathbb{T}\right\}

is uniformly Lipschitz on
B_{R}^{H}
with a uniform bound on the Lipschitz constants depending on

R,\|\phi\|_{\infty},\|V\|_{\infty},\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\|\nabla\phi(\cdot,h)\|_{\infty},\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\|\nabla V(\cdot,h)\|_{\infty}.

In particular, the family is uniformly equicontinuous on
(B_{R}^{H_{2}},\|\cdot\|)
for any
R>0
.

Here
\nabla
is the gradient in the
H
component. For convenience we will denote

\|\nabla V\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{h\in\mathbb{T}}\|\nabla V(\cdot,h)\|_{\infty},\,\text{ for }V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T}).

Proposition
C.4
is a direct consequence of the following estimate.

Proposition C.5
.

Assume
A_{\infty}=H,f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},H_{2})
in (
2.2
). Then for any
V\in C_{b}^{1,0}(H\times\mathbb{T}),\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(H\times\mathbb{T})
and
c,\eta>0
, there are
R_{0}=R_{0}(V)
and a constant
C
independent of
h
such that

(C.23)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{P}^{V}_{t}\phi(w,h)\|\leq C\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}\|_{R_{0}}\left(\|\phi\|_{\infty}+e^{-ct}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\right),

for any
(w,h)\in H\times\mathbb{T}
and
t\geq 0
.

This estimate is proved through Malliavin calculus. In the time homogeneous case this type of gradient estimate was proved in
[
31
]
recently. Since the current work is in a time inhomogeneous setting, we include a detailed proof below, which basically follows from the scheme in
[
31
]
. The time inhomogeneity is overcome by the translation identity (
2.6
) and the cocycle property (
2.7
). The proof of Proposition
C.5
is giving in subsection
C.3.2
, after we give some basic facts about Malliavin calculus in subsection
C.3.1
.

C.3.1.
Malliavin calculus preliminaries

Recall that
\Phi_{0,t,h}(w_{0},W)=\Phi_{0,t,h}(w_{0},W\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]})
is the solution of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equation at time
t
, where
W
is the Wiener process. The Malliavin derivative of this random variable in the direction of
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
is

\displaystyle D^{v}\Phi_{0,t,h}(w_{0}):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{\Phi}_{0,t,h}(w_{0},W+\varepsilon V)-\mathrm{\Phi}_{0,t,h}(w_{0},W)}{\varepsilon},

where
V(t)=\int_{0}^{t}v(r)dr
. The above limit exists almost surely and one has

\displaystyle D^{v}\Phi_{0,t,h}(w_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}J_{r,t,h}Gv(r)dr,

where
J_{r,t,h}
is the Jacobian that solves the linearized equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J_{r,t,h}\xi=\nu\mathrm{\Delta}J_{r,t,h}\xi+\widetilde{B}(\Phi_{0,t,h},J_{r,t,h}\xi),\quad t>r,\quad J_{r,r,h}\xi=\xi,

for
\xi\in H
. See
[
21
,
32
]
and references therein for more about Malliavin calculus.

For any
0\leq\tau\leq t
, the Malliavin operator is defined as
M_{\tau,t,h}:=A_{\tau,t,h}A_{\tau,t,h}^{*}
, where the random operator
A_{\tau,t,h}:\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H
is given by

\displaystyle A_{\tau,t,h}v:=\int_{\tau}^{t}J_{r,t,h}Gv(r)dr,\,v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and
A_{\tau,t,h}^{*}
is its adjoint by the relation

\left\langle A_{\tau,t,h}v,u\right\rangle_{H}=\left\langle v,A_{\tau,t,h}^{*}u\right\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,u\in H,v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d}).

It is known that the Tikhonov regularization
\widetilde{M}_{\tau,t,h}:=M_{\tau,t,h}+\beta
, is invertible for any
\beta>0
.

Denote
\Xi_{t}^{V}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(H_{r})dr\right)
, and
v_{0,t}=v\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}
, and set

\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}=J_{0,t,h}\xi-A_{0,t,h}v_{0,t},

which is the error between the infinitesimal variation on the Wiener path
W
by
v
and the variation on the initial condition by
\xi
of the solution process. Applying the integration by parts formula, chain rule and product rule for the Malliavin derivative
[
32
]
, for any
w,\xi\in H
with
\|\xi\|=1
, and
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{(w,h)}
, we have

(C.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\langle\nabla&\mathcal{P}_{t}^{V}\phi(w,h),\xi\big\rangle=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})J_{0,r,h}\xi dr+\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})J_{0,t,h}\xi\right]\\
&\begin{aligned} &=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})D^{v}\Phi_{0,r,h}dr+\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})D^{v}\Phi_{0,t,h}\right]\\
&\qquad+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr+\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}\right]\end{aligned}\\
&\begin{aligned} &=\mathbf{E}\left[D^{v}\left(\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\right)\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}\right]\end{aligned}\\
&\begin{aligned} &=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}\right]\end{aligned}\\
&:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},\end{split}

where we used the fact that
D^{v}\Phi_{0,t,h}=A_{0,t}v_{0,t}
. The gradient inequality (
C.23
) will be established if one can choose an appropriate control
v
to have desired bounds on
I_{j},j=1,2,3
. Here we choose essentially the same control
v
as in
[
21
]
,

\displaystyle v(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{2n}^{*}\widetilde{M}_{2n}^{-1}J_{2n}\mathfrak{R}_{2n,h}&\text{ for }r\in[2n,2n+1),n\geq 0,\\
0&\text{ for }r\in[2n+1,2n+2),n\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

where
J_{n}=J_{n,n+1,h}
,
A_{n}=A_{n,n+1,h}
,
M_{n}=A_{n}A_{n}^{*}
,
\widetilde{M}_{n}=\beta+M_{n}
. Note that the control is not always adapted, hence the integral in
I_{1}
is understood as a Skorokhod integral.

The following result follows from the framework developed in
[
21
]
. We refer interested readers to
[
27
]
for a verification in the current setting.

Proposition C.6
.

For any
\eta>0
and
a>0
there are constants
\beta>0
and
C=C(\eta,a)
, so that

(C.25)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathfrak{R}_{t}\|^{2}\leq C\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})e^{-2at},\,\forall t\geq 0,

(C.26)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left|\int_{2n}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right|^{2}\leq C\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})e^{-2an},\,\forall t\in[2n,2n+2],

for all
n\geq 0
,
h\in\mathbb{T},w\in H
.

C.3.2.
Proof of Proposition
C.5

We now prove Proposition
C.5
by combining inequality (
C.24
), Proposition
C.6
and the growth estimates from Proposition
C.1
, through giving desired estimates on
I_{j},j=1,2,3
in inequality (
C.24
). Let
R_{0}
and
q
be the numbers as in Proposition
C.1
and assume
V\geq 0
without loss of generality. The proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1: Estimate on
I_{1}
. The idea is to use the decay in (
C.26
) to control the growth resulted by the potential
V
on each single interval. Let
N_{t}
be the largest integer such that
2N_{t}\leq t
. We can rewrite
I_{1}
as follows,

\displaystyle I_{1}=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{t}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{2N_{t}}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right]

\displaystyle:=I_{1,1}+I_{1,2}.

By the construction of
v
, we know that for each
1\leq n\leq N_{t}
, the integral
\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)
is
\mathcal{F}_{2n}
measurable. Therefore by the Markov property, we have

(C.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\right]&=\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{2n}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{2n}^{V}\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\mathcal{P}_{t-2n}^{V}\phi(\mathcal{H}_{2n})\right].\end{split}

By Proposition
C.1
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{P}_{t-2n}^{V}\phi(w,h)\right|=\left|P^{V}_{0,t-2n,h}\phi(\cdot,\beta_{t-2n}h)(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}P^{V}_{0,t-2n,h}\mathbb{1}_{H}(w)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(w)\|P_{0,t-2n,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}.

Therefore by
V\geq 0
and translation identity (
2.6
),

(C.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{P}_{t-2n}^{V}\phi(\mathcal{H}_{2n})\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,2n,h})\|P_{0,t-2n,\beta_{2n}h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}&=C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,2n,h})\|P_{2n,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\\
&\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,2n,h})\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
C.27
), estimate (
B.6
), Hölder’s inequality, and (
C.26
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg|\mathbf{E}\Big[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)

\displaystyle dW(r)\Big]\Bigg|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{2\|V\|_{\infty}n}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,2n,h})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{2\|V\|_{\infty}n}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left|\int_{2n-2}^{2n}v(r)dW(r)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(\Phi_{0,2n,h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)n}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w).

As a result,

\displaystyle\left|I_{1,1}\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\sum_{n=1}^{N_{t}}e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)n}.

On the other hand, again by
V\geq 0
and estimate (
C.26
), one has

\displaystyle\left|I_{1,2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{2N_{t}}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}t}\mathbf{E}\left|\int_{2N_{t}}^{t}v(r)dW(r)\right|\leq Ce^{(\|V\|_{\infty}-a)n}.

Therefore we obtain

(C.29)

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|\leq\left|I_{1,1}\right|+\left|I_{1,2}\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\mathfrak{m}_{\eta}(w)\sum_{n=1}^{N_{t}}e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)n}.

The proof of
Step 1
is complete.

Step 2: Estimate on
I_{2}
. The calculation of
I_{2}
is similar to that of
I_{1}
. The difference is that now we will use (
C.25
) and
\mathcal{F}_{r*}
measurability of
\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}
, where
r^{*}=r
if
\lceil r\rceil
, the smallest integer greater than or equal to
r
, is even, and
r^{*}=\lceil r\rceil
if
\lceil r\rceil
is odd. We first rewrite

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{0}^{N_{t}}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\int_{N_{t}}^{t}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}dr\right]

\displaystyle:=I_{2,1}+I_{2,2},

where
N_{t}
is the largest even integer such that
N_{t}\leq t
.
By the Markov property, we have

\displaystyle I_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\int_{0}^{N_{t}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\phi(H_{t})\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}|\mathcal{F}_{r^{*}}\right]dr

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\int_{0}^{N_{t}}\Xi_{r^{*}}^{V}\nabla V(H_{r})\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}\mathcal{P}^{V}_{t-r^{*}}\phi(\mathcal{H}_{r^{*}})dr

Similar to the arguments in deriving (
C.28
), by Proposition
C.1
,
V\geq 0
and translation identity (
2.6
), and boundedness of
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{P}^{V}_{t-r^{*}}\phi(\mathcal{H}_{r^{*}})\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,r^{*},h})\|P_{0,t-r^{*},\beta_{r^{*}}h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}

\displaystyle=C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,r^{*},h})\|P_{r^{*},t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,r^{*},h})\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}.

Hence by estimate (
B.6
), Hölder’s inequality and (
C.25
), one has

\displaystyle\left|I_{2,1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla V\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\int_{0}^{N_{t}}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}r^{*}}\mathbf{E}\left[\|\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}\|\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\Phi_{0,r^{*},h})\right]dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\int_{0}^{N_{t}}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}r^{*}}\left(\mathbf{E}\|\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\mathfrak{m}_{2q}(\Phi_{0,r^{*},h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})\int_{0}^{N_{t}}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}r^{*}-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})\int_{0}^{N_{t}}e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)r}dr,

since
r^{*}\leq r+1
.

Now we look at
I_{2,2}
. Noting that by (
C.25
) and
\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2,2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}t}\int_{N_{t}}^{t}\mathbf{E}\|\mathfrak{R}_{r,h}\|dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})e^{\|V\|_{\infty}t}\int_{N_{t}}^{\infty}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)t}

Combining estimates for
I_{2,1}
and
I_{2,2}
we obtain

(C.30)

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{N_{t}}e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)r}dr+e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)t}\right).

Step 3: Estimate on
I_{3}
. The calculation for
I_{3}
follows directly from (
C.25
). Indeed,

(C.31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|I_{3}\right|&=\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{V}\nabla\phi(H_{t})\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}\right]\right|\leq C\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}e^{\|V\|_{\infty}t}\mathbf{E}\|\mathfrak{R}_{t,h}\|\\
&\leq C\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}_{H}\|_{R_{0}}\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})e^{(2\|V\|_{\infty}-a)t}\end{split}

Now combining (
C.24
) and (
C.29
)-(
C.31
), for any
c>0
, we can choose
a>0
such that
2\|V\|_{\infty}-a=-c
, then we obtain the desired gradient estimate (
C.23
)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{P}^{V}_{t}\phi(w,h)\|\leq C\exp(\eta\|w\|^{2})\|P_{0,t,h}^{V}\mathbb{1}\|_{R_{0}}\left(\|\phi\|_{\infty}+e^{-ct}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\right),

where
C=C(c,\eta,\|V\|_{\infty},\|\nabla V\|_{\infty})>0
is independent of
h\in\mathbb{T}
.

The proof of Proposition
C.5
is complete.
∎

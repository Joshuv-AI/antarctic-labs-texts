A uniqueness result for the two vortex travelling wave in the Nonlinear Schrödinger equation
David Chiron, Eliot Pacherie

Abstract
For the Nonlinear Schrödinger equation in dimension 2, the existence of a global minimizer of the energy at fixed momentum has been established by Bethuel-Gravejat-Saut [ 7 ] (see also [ 13 ] ). This minimizer is a travelling wave for the Nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For large momentums, the propagation speed is small and the minimizer behaves like two well separated vortices. In that limit, we show the uniqueness of this minimizer, up to the invariances of the problem, hence proving the orbital stability of this travelling wave. This work is a follow up to two previous papers [ 15 ] , [ 14 ] , where we constructed and studied a particular travelling wave of the equation. We show a uniqueness result on this travelling wave in a class of functions that contains in particular all possible minimizers of the energy.

We consider the Nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\mathcal{E}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbbm{R}^{2},\mathbbm{C}),E(u)\char 60\relax+\infty\}.

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{2}}\mathfrak{Re}(i\nabla u\bar{u})\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}

In addition to the translation invariance, the

Let us explain schematically the proof of Proposition

in such a way that

the position of the vortices, is smooth. We then decompose

This can not be done near the zeros of

The equation satisfied by

(\operatorname{TW}_{c})(u)=0=(\operatorname{TW}_{c})(Q)+\it{L}(\psi)+\it{NL}(\psi)
,
where we regroup the linear terms in

because

. We then take the scalar product of this equation with

Now, the coercivity of

has been studied in

14
]

)
up to three orthogonality conditions, that can be satisfied by changing slightly the modulation parameters

. We deduce that
B_{Q}(\psi)\geqslant K\|\psi\|^{2}_{1}
for some norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
.

There are two main difficulties at this point. First, since the hypothesis on

in Proposition
1.8

are weak, we simply have
||\psi||_{1}\char 60\relax+\infty
, but not the fact that it is small. Therefore, an estimate
of the form
\lvert\langle\it{NL}(\psi),\psi\rangle\rvert\leqslant K\|\psi\|^{3}_{1}
would not be enough to conclude.
Secondly, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is rather weak, and in fact
\langle\it{NL}(\psi),\psi\rangle
can not be controlled
by powers of
\|\psi\|_{1}
.

Concerning the term
\langle(TW_{c})(Q),\psi\rangle
, we may show that we always have
|c-c^{\prime}|\leqslant o(1)\|\psi\|_{1}
,

\lvert\langle(TW_{c})(Q),\psi\rangle\rvert\leqslant o(1)\|\psi\|^{2}_{1}

(K/2)\|\psi\|^{2}_{1}\leqslant\langle(\operatorname{TW}_{c})(Q),\psi\rangle+B_{Q}(\psi)=-\langle\it{NL}(\psi),\psi\rangle

Then, even if
\|\psi\|_{1}
is not small, by the hypothesis of Proposition

\psi

Another consequence of this fact is that
\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
possesses at most two (simple) zeroes

\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}
.

We then define
d=d_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that the unique zero
\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+}

of
\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in the right half-plane is

\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+}=\frac{d_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\mathfrak{p}}\overrightarrow{e_{1}}\to(1/(2\pi),0)\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}.

We deduce from Step 2 of subsection
3.2.4
that

d_{\mathfrak{p}}\sim\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{2\pi}\sim\frac{1}{c_{\mathfrak{p}}},

so that hypothesis 4 of Proposition
1.8
is satisfied for
\mathfrak{p}
large enough
(still for
\lambda=\max(R_{0},\lambda_{*})
).
Furthermore, hypothesis 2 of Proposition
1.8
is satisfied by taking

\mathfrak{p}
large enough, associated with the choice
\lambda=\max(R_{0},\lambda_{*})
.

Step 2: conclusion.
Applying Proposition
1.8
to
{\sf e}^{-i\beta}u_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
we infer that there exists
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathbbm{R}
such that (for large
\mathfrak{p}
)

u_{\mathfrak{p}}={\sf e}^{i\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}Q_{c_{\mathfrak{p}}}

(no translation is needed in the
x_{2}
direction at this stage since the zeros of
\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are
on the
x_{1}
-axis).

3.3
Decay slightly away from the vortices

In this section, we provide some estimates for
\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in the region

B(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+},2R_{0})\cup B(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},-},2R_{0})
.
For the Ginzburg-Landau (stationary) model, such estimates have been first given in
[
37
]

for minimizing solutions and later generalized in
[
18
]
to non-minimizing solutions.
However, the paper
[
37
]
being difficult to find, we give here
a proof of these estimates that includes the transport term.
They improve some estimates in
[
15
]

and are not specific to the way we construct the solutions.

Proposition 3.6

We have, for
\lvert\hat{y}\lvert\leqslant\frac{3}{20}
,

\big\lvert\lvert\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}+\hat{y})\rvert-1\big\rvert\leqslant\frac{C}{\mathfrak{p}^{2}\lvert\hat{y}\rvert^{2}},\quad\quad\big\lvert\nabla\lvert\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rvert(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}+\hat{y})\big\rvert\leqslant\frac{C}{\mathfrak{p}^{2}\lvert\hat{y}\rvert^{3}},\quad\quad\big\lvert\nabla\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}+\hat{y})\big\rvert\leqslant\frac{C}{\lvert\hat{y}\rvert}.

Proof.

We work near
\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+}
(the minus sign is similar), say in the annulus

B(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+},1/10)\setminus B(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+},1/\mathfrak{p})
and set

\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+}+\hat{y})=\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{y}){\sf e}^{i\theta+i\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{y})}

with
\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}
real-valued and smooth in the annulus (
\theta

is the polar angle centered at
\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},+}
). Then, we obtain the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\lvert\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rvert^{2}+\mathfrak{p}^{2}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\lvert V_{1}\rvert^{2}(1-\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})-2\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{\partial_{\theta}\varphi}{r^{2}}-c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\partial_{2}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}-c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\frac{\cos\theta}{r}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\\
\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Delta\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}+2\nabla\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}+2\frac{\partial_{\theta}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}}{r^{2}}+c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\partial_{2}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}=0.\end{array}\right.

The second equation may be recast as

\nabla(\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}})+\frac{\partial_{\theta}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}}{r^{2}}=-\frac{c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}}{2}\partial_{2}(\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}-1).

(3.20)

Multiplying by
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and integrating over
DB(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p})}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\lvert\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rvert^{2}\,d\hat{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p})}(1-\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\frac{\partial_{\theta}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}}{r^{2}}+\frac{c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}}{2}(1-\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\partial_{2}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,d\hat{y}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\partial B(0,3/20)}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\partial\nu}+\frac{c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}}{2}(\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}-1)\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\nu_{2}\,d\ell.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
3.3
) and Step 1 of subsection
3.2.3
, we infer

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p}))}^{2}\leqslant C(1+c_{\mathfrak{p}})\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p}))}+C

where, for the contribution of the integral over
\partial B(0,3/20)
, we have used (
3.16
) and
(
3.15
) (see Step 2 of subsection
3.2.2
). This implies

\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p}))}\leqslant C.

(3.21)

We fix
\hat{y}\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}
such that
2R_{0}/\mathfrak{p}\leqslant\lvert\hat{y}\lvert\leqslant\frac{3}{20}
.
Then, since
\lvert\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rvert\geqslant 1/2
in the annulus

B(0,3/20)\setminus B(0,R_{0}/\mathfrak{p})\supset B(\hat{y},\lvert\hat{y}\rvert/2)
,
we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{B(\hat{y},\lvert\hat{y}\rvert/2)}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\lvert\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}+\vec{e}_{\theta}/r\rvert^{2}\,d\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{B(\hat{y},\lvert\hat{y}\rvert/2)}\lvert\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,d\hat{x}\leqslant C

by (
3.21
) and the fact that
r=\lvert\hat{x}\rvert\geqslant\lvert\hat{y}\rvert/2
.
By Step 1 of subsection
3.2.3
, we then infer the upper bound (also shown in
[
37
]
)

E_{1/\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}},B(\hat{y},\lvert\hat{y}\rvert/2))\leqslant C.

(3.22)

We now make some rescaling and consider

v(X)\overset{\rm def}{=}\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big(\hat{y}+\frac{\lvert\hat{y}\rvert}{2}X\Big)

in
B(0,1)
(
v
depends on
\hat{y}
and
\mathfrak{p}
), which solves

\Delta v+i\frac{c_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\delta}\partial_{2}v+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}v(1-\lvert v\rvert^{2})=0

in
B(0,1)
, with
\delta:=2/(\mathfrak{p}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert)
. This equation is of the type
(
3.1
) with "
\epsilon=\delta
" and "
\mathfrak{c}=c_{\mathfrak{p}}/\delta
". Let us check that the
assumption
\lvert\mathfrak{c}\rvert\leqslant M_{0}\lvert\ln\epsilon\rvert
is satisfied with
M_{0}=1
. As a matter of
fact, we have
\delta=2/(\mathfrak{p}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert)\in]40/(3\mathfrak{p}),1/2]
, thus

M_{0}\delta\lvert\ln\delta\rvert\geqslant\frac{40}{3\mathfrak{p}}\ln 2\geqslant c_{\mathfrak{p}}=\frac{2\pi}{\mathfrak{p}}+o(1)

by Step 2 of subsection
3.2.4
(note
40(\ln 2)/3\approx 9.24(1)\char 62\relax 2\pi
).
Furthermore, the upper bound (
3.22
) reads now

E_{\delta}(v,B(0,1))\leqslant C.

It then follows from the proof of Step 7 (p. 48) of Theorem 1 in
[
8
]
that, for

\delta
sufficiently small,

\|2\delta^{-2}(1-\lvert v\rvert)-c_{\mathfrak{p}}\delta^{-1}\partial_{2}\arg(v)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(B(0,1/2))}\leqslant C,\quad\quad\quad\|\nabla\arg(v)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(B(0,1/2))}\leqslant C.

Therefore, by Step 2 of subsection
3.2.3
,

\big\lvert 1-\lvert v(0)\rvert\big\rvert+\big\lvert\nabla\lvert v\rvert(0)\big\rvert\leqslant Cc_{\mathfrak{p}}\delta+C\delta^{2}\leqslant\frac{C}{\mathfrak{p}^{2}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert^{2}},\quad\quad\big\lvert\nabla\arg(v)(0)\big\rvert\leqslant C,

and scaling back this yields the conclusion, at least for
\delta=2/(\mathfrak{p}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert)

sufficiently small, say
\mathfrak{p}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert\geqslant\delta_{0}/2
, but the estimate
is easy to show if
\mathfrak{p}\lvert\hat{y}\lvert\leqslant\delta_{0}/2
.
∎

3.4
Some remarks on the non symmetrical case

In the case where we do not assume the
x_{1}
symmetry for
u_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the location of
the vortices
\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}
is more delicate. Indeed, we can no longer assume
(
3.14
), that is

(\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-})_{2}=(\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+})_{2}=0\quad\quad\quad{\rm and}\quad\quad\quad-(\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-})_{1}=(\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+})_{1}\to\frac{1}{2\pi}.

Up to a translation, we may assume
\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+}+\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-}=0
,
and it remains true that
\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+,1}-\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-,1}\to 1/\pi
,
but we may have
\lvert\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+}-\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-}\rvert\gg 1
.
By following carefully the proof in
[
43
]
, one could show that

\lvert\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},+}-\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},-}\rvert\leqslant C.

Then, the location of the limiting vortices
\hat{y}_{\infty,\pm}=\lim_{\mathfrak{p}\to+\infty}\hat{y}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}

can be obtained through the use of the Hopf differential as in
[
4
]
(chapter VII), and would lead as before
to
\hat{y}_{\infty,\pm}=(\pm 1/(2\pi),0)
. This is of course related to the fact that the only critical point
of the action functional

\mathcal{F}(\hat{y}_{\infty,+},\hat{y}_{\infty,-}):=2\pi\Big(2\ln\lvert\hat{y}_{\infty,+}-\hat{y}_{\infty,-}\rvert-2\pi\big[(\hat{y}_{\infty,+})_{1}-(\hat{y}_{\infty,-})_{1}\big]\Big)

associated with the action of the Kirchhoff energy is
(\hat{y}_{\infty,+},\hat{y}_{\infty,-})=(1/(2\pi),-1/(2\pi))\in\mathbbm{C}^{2}

(up to translation).

Next, Step 1 of subsection
3.2.4
becomes, for any
p\in[1,2[
, and in
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbbm{R}^{2})
,

\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightharpoonup{\sf e}^{i\Theta}\hat{u}_{\infty}.

The term
\Theta
is somewhat the phase at infinity, even though we do not claim
some uniformity at infinity in space. Next, for the local convergences, there are two
phases
\beta_{\pm}\in\mathbbm{R}
such that

\hat{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\hat{z}_{\mathfrak{p},\pm}+\mathfrak{p}\cdot)\to{\sf e}^{i\beta_{\pm}}V_{\pm}

(3.23)

in
C^{k}_{\rm loc}(\mathbbm{R}^{2})
for any
k\in\mathbbm{N}
. We are then simply able to show that
\beta_{\pm}=\Theta
,
but this is not enough for the uniqueness result. This follows from the arguments given
in
[
44
]
, as we explain.

We work for the
+
sign. Integrating (
3.20
) over the disk
B(0,R)
yields

\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\partial\nu}\,d\ell+c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\nu_{2}(\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}-1)\,d\ell=0.

We now consider the average

\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r):=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,d\ell

which satisfies, for
1/\mathfrak{p}\leqslant r_{0}\leqslant r_{1}\leqslant 3/20
,

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{0})-\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\partial_{r}\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r)\,dr=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\partial_{r}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,d\ell dr

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}(1-\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\partial_{r}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,d\ell dr+c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\nu_{2}(\hat{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}-1)\,d\ell dr.

Therefore, by Step 5,

\displaystyle\lvert\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{0})-\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{1})\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{dr}{\mathfrak{p}^{2}r^{3}}+C\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{dr}{\mathfrak{p}^{2}r^{2}}\leqslant\frac{C}{(r_{0}\mathfrak{p})^{2}}+\frac{C}{\mathfrak{p}}.

We now fix
\eta\in]0,1]
. Taking
r_{0}=1/(\sqrt{\eta}\mathfrak{p})
and
r_{1}=3/20
, we infer

\lvert\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{0})-\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{1})\rvert\leqslant C\eta+\frac{C}{\mathfrak{p}}.

Moreover, by (
3.23
), we have

\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{0})=\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/(\sqrt{\eta}\mathfrak{p}))\to\beta_{+}

as
\mathfrak{p}\to+\infty
, and by Step 1 of subsection
3.2.4
, we deduce

\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{1})\to\Theta.

As a consequence,

\lvert\beta_{+}-\Theta\rvert\leqslant C\eta,

and the conclusion follows by letting
\eta\to 0
.

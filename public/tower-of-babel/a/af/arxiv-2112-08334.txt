Ideals in enveloping algebras of affine Kac-Moody algebras
Rekha Biswal, Susan J. Sierra

Abstract
. Let L L be an affine Kac-Moody algebra, with central element c c , and let λ ∈ ℂ \lambda\in\mathbb{C} . We study two-sided ideals in the central quotient U λ ​ ( L ) := U ⁡ ( L ) / ( c − λ ) U_{\lambda}(L):=U(L)/(c-\lambda) of the universal enveloping algebra of L L , and prove: Theorem 1. If λ ≠ 0 \lambda\neq 0 then U λ ​ ( L ) U_{\lambda}(L) is simple. Theorem 2. The algebra U 0 ​ ( L ) U_{0}(L) has just-infinite growth , in the sense that any proper quotient has polynomial growth. As an immediate corollary, we show that the annihilator of any nontrivial integrable highest weight representation of L L is centrally generated, extending a result of Chari for Verma modules. We also show that universal enveloping algebras of loop algebras and current algebras of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras have just-infinite growth, and prove similar results to Theorems 1 and 2 for quotients of symmetric algebras of these Lie algebras by Poisson ideals.

1.
Introduction

Fix an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic 0.
Let
L
be an affine Kac-Moody algebra over
\Bbbk
.
This paper is concerned with the structure, and particularly the
size
, of two-sided ideals of the universal enveloping algebra
U(L)
: our main theorem shows that such ideals are extremely large, in a sense that we make precise below.

In the introduction, to simplify discussion, we assume that
L
is untwisted: that is, that there is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
so that, as a vector space,

L=\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\Bbbk c\oplus\Bbbk d,

where
c
is central,
d
is the derivation measuring degree, and
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
is the loop algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
(We consider general affine algebras in the body of the paper.)
The derived subalgebra
L^{\prime}
of
L
is

L^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\Bbbk c

and is the unique (up to isomorphism) nontrivial central extension of the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
.
To emphasise the relationship between
L
and
\mathfrak{g}
, we sometimes write
L=\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

If
\lambda\in\Bbbk
, define
U_{\lambda}(L)=U(L)/(c-\lambda)
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})=U(L^{\prime})/(c-\lambda)
, so
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}])\cong U_{0}(L^{\prime})
.
We study two-sided ideals in
U(L)
and in the central quotients
U_{\lambda}(L)
.
We will see that they are very big, in a sense we make precise below.
Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Bbbk
.

(0)

The algebras
U_{\lambda}(L)
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
have just-infinite growth.
That is, let
J
be a nonzero ideal of
U_{\lambda}(L)
, and let
J^{\prime}
be a nonzero ideal of
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
.
Then
U_{\lambda}(L)/J
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})/J^{\prime}
have polynomial growth.

(1)

In fact, if
\lambda\neq 0
then
U_{\lambda}(L)
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
are simple rings.

(2)

Any nonzero ideal of
U(L)
or of
U(L^{\prime})
contains a nonzero element of
\Bbbk[c]
; equivalently, the algebras
U(L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
and
U(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
are simple.

Here recall that a
\Bbbk
-algebra
R
has
polynomial growth
if there is a polynomial
p(t)\in\mathbb{R}[t]
so that, for every finite-dimensional subspace
V
of
R
,
\dim V^{n}\leq p(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
.
(For example, enveloping algebras of finite-dimensional Lie algebras have polynomial growth.)
Further,
R
has
exponential growth
if there exists
V\subset R
so that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\dim V^{n})^{1/n}>1
and (strictly)
subexponential growth
if
R
has neither exponential nor polynomial growth.
It is well-known that
U_{\lambda}(L)
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
have subexponential growth.
Thus Theorem
1.1
(0) tells us that two-sided ideals in these algebras are extremely large: large enough to cut these very big algebras down to a reasonable size.

We also prove a similar theorem for Poisson ideals in the symmetric algebras of
L
and
L^{\prime}
.
Recall that the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{k})
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
is a Poisson algebra under the Kostant-Kirillov Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
induced by defining
\{x,y\}=[x,y]
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{k}
.
A
Poisson ideal
of a Poisson algebra
R
is an ideal of
R
which is also a Lie ideal for the Poisson bracket of
R
.
We prove:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\Bbbk
and consider the Poisson algebras
S_{\lambda}(L)=S(L)/(c-\lambda)
and
S_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})=S(L^{\prime})/(c-\lambda)
.

(0)

Let
I
be a nonzero Poisson ideal of
S_{\lambda}(L)
and let
I^{\prime}
be a nonzero Poisson ideal of
S_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
.
Then
S_{\lambda}(L)/I
and
S_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})/I^{\prime}
have polynomial growth.

(1)

In fact, if
\lambda\neq 0
then
S_{\lambda}(L)
and
S_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
are Poisson simple in the sense that they have no nontrivial Poisson ideals.

(2)

Any nonzero Poisson ideal of
S(L)
or of
S(L^{\prime})
contains a nonzero element of
\Bbbk[c]
; equivalently,
S(L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
and
S(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
are Poisson simple.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
we compute the annihilators of a large class of representations of
L
, and in particular for
all
nontrivial integrable highest weight representations of
L
: we show these annilators are all centrally generated.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N
be a nonzero representation of
L
so that
(c-\lambda)N=0
for some
\lambda\neq 0
. Then
\ann_{U(L)}(N)=(c-\lambda)
.
In particular, if

M
is a nontrivial integrable highest weight represention of
L
with central character
\lambda
,
then
\ann_{U(L)}(M)=(c-\lambda)
.

Proof.

Theorem
1.1
(1) shows that
(c-\lambda)
is a maximal ideal of
U(L)
. The second statement is an immediate consequence, since nontrivial highest weight representations of
L
have nontrivial central character.
∎

Theorem
1.3
extends Vyjayanthi Chari’s
well-known calculation of the annihilators of Verma modules for infinite-dimensional symmetrisable Kac-Moody algebras
[
Cha85
]
.
To our knowledge, no annihilator of a (nontrivial) integrable highest weight representation of an affine algebra has been known until now.

Our investigation was partially motivated by work of Natalia Iyudu and the second author
[
IS20
]
, showing that the analogue of Theorem
1.1
(0) holds for the Virasoro Lie algebra: central quotients of the enveloping algebra of the Virasoro algebra have just-infinite growth.
(See
[
IS20
, Theorem 1.2]
.)
Affine Kac-Moody algebras (more precisely, their derived subalgebras) and the Virasoro algebra are both central extensions of a graded simple Lie algebra of linear growth, and their representation theory is related through the Sugawara construction.
It is thus natural to ask whether results for the Virasoro algebra can be extended to cover affine algebras. However, affine algebras are much more commutative than the Virasoro algebra, where the centralisers of elements are in general finite-dimensional (in fact two-dimensional); thus, naively, two-sided ideals in their enveloping algebras are smaller.
It is surprising to find that the two-sided structure of their enveloping algebras is similarly constrained.

The phrasing of Theorem
1.1

appears redundant: of course, since
U_{\lambda}(L)
is simple for
\lambda\neq 0
, it has just-infinite growth. However, it reflects the structure of the paper. We focus first on proving Theorems
1.1
(0) and
1.2
(0).
Our proof strategy here is broadly similar to the methods of
[
IS20
]
, but significantly more delicate because of the commutativity problem: centralisers in
L
are large in general and so the adjoint action of
L
is more difficult to control.
Note that to prove just-infinite growth for a (left and right) non-noetherian algebra like
U_{\lambda}(L)
, it is helpful for as many commutators as possible to be nonzero in order to ensure that two-sided ideals are big.

To prove Theorems
1.1
(0) and
1.2
(0), we
construct an ordered PBW basis for
U_{\lambda}(L)
and then show, through analysing the adjoint action of
L
on
U_{\lambda}(L)
, that if
B
is a sufficiently large element of this basis, then there is an element of
J
with leading term
B
.
(See Lemma
5.4
.)
This allows us to reduce almost all basis elements, modulo
J
, to smaller elements and thus to bound the growth of
U_{\lambda}(L)/J
.

We work first with the symmetric algebra of the positive current algebra
t\mathfrak{g}[t]
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
This is the associated graded ring of
U(t\mathfrak{g}[t])
under the natural (length) filtration and so has a Poisson algebra structure; further it is finitely graded under the grading induced by giving elements of
\mathfrak{g}
degree 0 and
t
degree 1.
We show that if
I
is a nontrivial Poisson ideal of
S(t\mathfrak{g}[t])
, then we can reduce almost all monomials
B
in an ordered PBW basis of
S(t\mathfrak{g}[t])
to smaller monomials, modulo
I
.
This reduction result allows us to prove (Theorem
3.9
) that
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
has just-infinite growth.
We then extend our analysis to
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}])
, to general
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
, and to
U_{\lambda}(L)
.

A corollary of Theorem
1.1
(0) is the following somewhat counterintuitive result:

Proposition 1.4
.

[Proposition
4.9
, Corollary
7.2
]
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra, and let
J
be a nontrivial ideal of the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}])
of the loop algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is any infinite set (for example,
X
consists of all the primes), and
g\in\mathfrak{g}\setminus\{0\}
, then
J
contains a (nonzero) element involving only Lie algebra elements of the form
gt^{x}
for
x\in X
.

In particular, if
L
is the (untwisted) affine algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
and
J
is a nonzero ideal of
U(L)
, then
J\cap U(\mathfrak{g}[t])\neq(0)
.

Proposition
1.4
allows us to conclude that if
e
is an element of the nilradical of the positive Borel of
\mathfrak{g}
, then any nontrivial Poisson ideal of
U_{\lambda}(L)
contains a nonzero element
E\in\Bbbk[\dots,et^{-1},e,et,\dots]
.
By considering the adjoint action of the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
on
E
, we are able to reduce the length of
E
and thus, by induction prove Theorem
1.1
(1). Similar techniques give Theorem
1.1
(2) and parts
(1)
,
(2)
of Theorem
1.2
.

To conclude the introduction, we briefly describe the structure of the paper.
Section
2
establishes notation and basic facts about affine Lie algebras and loop algebras, as well as the basics of growth and GK-dimension.
Key results on Poisson ideals of symmetric algebras of positive current algebras are proved in Section
3
.
In Section
4
we prove preparatory results needed to extend our methods from
U(t\mathfrak{g}[t])
to
U_{\lambda}(L)
before proving Theorems
1.1
(0) and
1.2
(0) in Section
5
.
In Section
6
we prove the remaining parts of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
Finally, in Section
7
we give several other applications of Theorem
1.1
, including Proposition
1.4
.

2.
Bases and basic facts

If
L
is an affine Kac-Moody algebra with central element
c
then there is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
so that
L^{\prime}/(c)
is isomorphic to the (possibly twisted) loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.
In this section we establish notation and basic facts about loop algebras, and further establish ordered bases for loop algebras and for their enveloping/symmetric algebras, which we will use in the next section.
We also recall some background on growth and Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of algebras and modules.

We adopt the following notation:
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra.
Let
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
which (without loss of generality) we assume comes from an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We also let
\sigma
denote the diagram automorphism and the associated automorphism of the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
r
be the order of
\sigma
.
Let
\eta
be a primitive
r
’th root of unity.
We extend the action of
\sigma
to
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
and denote the corresponding twisted current algebra by
\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma}
and the twisted loop algebra by
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.

2.1.
Basic facts about
\mathfrak{g}

In this subsection we establish notation and basic facts about
\mathfrak{g}
.
Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we denote the
\alpha
-eigenspace of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.

If
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\overline{s}
denote the congruence class of
s
in
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
.
The automorphism
\sigma
induces a
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{s=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}=\{g\in\mathfrak{g}:\sigma(g)=\eta^{s}g\}
.
If
g\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
, we write
|g|=\overline{s}
.
The
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
for
0\leq s\leq r-1
are irreducible
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-modules under the adjoint action.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\cap\mathfrak{h}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
.
As in
[
Kac90
, p. 130]
, for
\overline{s}\in\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
let
\Delta_{\overline{s}}
be the set of nonzero weights of
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
and define the weight space decomposition

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\overline{s}}\cup\{0\}}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}.

We say an element
g\in\mathfrak{g}
is
weight-homogeneous
if
g
is in some
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}
.
Any weight-homogeneous element is by definition
\sigma
-equivariant.
It is clear that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{p},\beta}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}+\overline{p},\alpha+\beta}
.

We observe the following basic facts. As these are standard, most are stated without proof.

(0)

We have
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}t^{s}
.

(1)

The automorphism
\sigma
preserves the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
and so the height of elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In particular, the positive Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and the nilpotent radical
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{b}^{+}
are
\sigma
-invariant and thus decompose as sums

\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{s=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\overline{s}}

and

\mathfrak{b}^{+}=\bigoplus_{s=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{\overline{s}}

of
\sigma
-weight spaces.

(2)

For each
\overline{s}\in\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
and each
\alpha\in\Delta_{\overline{s}}
we either have
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{+}

or
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{-}
.
Thus we can define

\Delta^{+}_{\overline{s}}=\{\alpha\in\Delta_{\overline{s}}:\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{+}\}.

Let
\Delta^{+}=\{(\overline{s},\alpha):\alpha\in\Delta_{\overline{s}}^{+}\}
.
Similarly, define
\Delta^{-}_{\overline{s}}
and
\Delta^{-}
.
Let
\Delta=\Delta^{+}\cup\Delta^{-}=\{(\overline{s},\alpha):\alpha\in\Delta_{\overline{s}}\}
.

(3)

Let
\theta
be the longest root of
\mathfrak{g}
. There are
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}
so that
\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}
.

(4)

For
\alpha\neq 0
the spaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}
are one-dimensional, so we may choose a generator
g_{\overline{s},\alpha}
.
Also, if
\gamma
is a positive roof of
\mathfrak{g}
, let
e_{\gamma},f_{\gamma}
be the Chevalley generators, respectively, of
\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-\gamma}
.
Let
h_{\gamma}=[e_{\gamma},f_{\gamma}]
.

(5)

If
(\overline{p},\beta)\in\Delta^{\pm}
, then there are
(\overline{s}_{1},\gamma_{1}),\cdots,(\overline{s}_{k},\gamma_{k})\in\Delta^{\mp}
so that

[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}_{k},\gamma_{k}},[\cdots,[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}_{1},\gamma_{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\theta}]\cdots]]=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{p},\beta}.

(6)

The centralizer of
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and is thus equal to
\mathfrak{h}
, and so

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0},0}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{r-1},0},

and
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0},0}
.

(7)

If
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}

(\overline{-s},-\alpha)\in\Delta^{-}

and
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{-s},-\alpha}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}
.
Let
\kappa
be the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}

\nu:\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}\to\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}^{*}

Then
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\overline{s},-\alpha}]=\Bbbk\nu^{-1}(\alpha)
.
Further, there is a positive root

so that

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}\subseteq\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma^{i}(\gamma)}.

Let

be the

-order of

(e_{\gamma}-e_{\sigma(\gamma)},f_{\gamma}-f_{\sigma(\gamma)})\text{ if }r^{\prime}=2\text{ and }\overline{s}=\overline{1},\\

Also,

2.2.
Two monomial orders on
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})

In this section, we define PBW bases of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
and
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.
We will refer to elements of these bases as
monomials
, and we will define two different orders on monomials.
The interplay between these monomial orders is key to proving our reduction results.

We first extend the
\sigma
-equivariant basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathfrak{g}
to a basis of
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}=\{gt^{rn+|g|}:g\in\mathcal{B},n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, which is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}[t]^{\sigma}=\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}\cap\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma}
.
We will need an order on
\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
, which we define as follows.

We first give an order on the roots of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
R
be the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
R^{+},R^{-}
be respectively the sets of positive and negative roots.
Let
\{\alpha_{i}:i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\}
be the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
\alpha=\sum b_{i}\alpha_{i}
is a positive root, then we define the height of
\alpha
to be
\height\alpha=\sum b_{i}
.
Recall that we denote the highest root of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\theta
.

We will define a total order
<
on
R
by:

•

\alpha<\beta\text{ if }\height\alpha<\height\beta
.

•

If
\alpha=\sum b_{i}\alpha_{i}
,
\beta=\sum c_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\height\alpha=\height\beta
, then
\alpha<\beta
if the tuple
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
is less than the tuple
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
in lexicographic order.

For each
\beta\in R

let
g_{\beta}=e_{\beta}
if
\beta\in R^{+}
and
g_{\beta}=f_{-\beta}
if
\beta\in R^{-}
.
Then

\mathcal{C}=\{g_{\beta}:\beta\in R\}\cup\{h_{\alpha_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\}

is a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{\ell})
be an enumeration of
R^{+}
written in increasing order w.r.t. the above total order (so
\beta_{\ell}=\theta
).
We define the following total order on
\mathcal{C}
:

g_{-\beta_{\ell}}<\cdots<{g}_{-\beta_{1}}<{h}_{\alpha_{1}}<\cdots<{h}_{\alpha_{n}}<{g}_{\beta_{1}}<\cdots<{g}_{\beta_{\ell}}.

Given
g\in\mathfrak{g}
, define the
\mathcal{C}
-leading term
of
g
, which we denote by
LT_{\mathcal{C}}(g)
, to be the largest element of
\mathcal{C}
occurring in
g
with nonzero coefficient.
(The reason for the subscript
\mathcal{C}
in the notation is to distinguish this from other uses of the terminology “leading term” in this paper.)

The ordering defined above on
\mathcal{C}
has the property that:

(2.1)

\mbox{ if $x,y,z\in\mathcal{C}$ with $[x,y]\neq 0\neq[x,z]$, then }y<z\iff LT_{\mathcal{C}}[x,y]<LT_{\mathcal{C}}[x,z].

We now induce orderings on each basis
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
from our order on
\mathcal{C}
.
Let
0\leq s\leq r-1
.
If
x,y\in\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}
, define

x<y\quad\iff\quad LT_{\mathcal{C}}(x)<LT_{\mathcal{C}}(y).

(We note here that it can be seen by inspection that, since every element of
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}
is associated to a unique
\sigma
-orbit in
\mathcal{C}
, if
LT_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=LT_{\mathcal{C}}(y)
then
x=y
.)
For
g\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
, we define
LT_{\mathcal{B}}(g)
to be the largest element of
\mathcal{B}
occurring in
g
with nonzero coefficient.

It follows from (
2.1
) that

(2.2)

\begin{split}\mbox{if $x\in\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}$, $y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}$ with $LT_{\mathcal{B}}(y)<x$, and $z$ is weight-homogeneous with $[z,x]\neq 0\neq[z,y]$,}\\
\mbox{ then $LT_{\mathcal{B}}[z,y]<LT_{\mathcal{B}}[z,x]$.}\end{split}

As an example, we give the orderings on the
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}
in the case
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
,
|\sigma|=2
.
We have

\mathcal{B}_{\overline{0}}:g_{-\alpha_{1}}<g_{-\alpha_{2}}<h_{1}+h_{2}<g_{\alpha_{2}}<g_{\alpha_{1}},

\mathcal{B}_{\overline{1}}:g_{-\theta}<h_{1}-h_{2}<g_{\theta}.

Using the total order
<
on the
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{s}}
, we define a total order on the basis
\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
of
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
as follows:
If
xt^{a},yt^{b}\in\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
, then
xt^{a}<yt^{b}
iff

•

a<b
; or

•

a=b
, and
x<y
in the total order defined on
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{a}}
.

We now define a basis of normal words or standard monomials for
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
and
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.
We will refer to elements of
\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
as
letters
, as “words” (or monomials) in these letters will span the algebras of interest.
A
standard monomial
or
normally ordered monomial
in the letters
\mathcal{B}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
is an expression of the form:

(2.3)

M=g_{1}t^{rm_{1}+|g_{1}|}\dots g_{k}t^{rm_{k}+|g_{k}|},

where the
g_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
, and
g_{1}t^{rm_{1}+|g_{1}|}\leq\dots\leq g_{k}t^{rm_{k}+|g_{k}|}
.
We sometimes write a standard monomial as

M=g_{1}t^{n_{1}}\dots g_{k}t^{n_{k}},

and when we do so we assume that
\overline{n_{a}}=|g_{a}|
for all
a
: in other words, that each
g_{i}t^{n_{i}}\in\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
, so
M\in S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.
We say that
k
is the
length
of
M
, which we denote
\len M
, and that the total
t
-power
\sum_{a}n_{a}
is the
degree
of
M
, which we denote
\deg(M)
.

We introduce two total orderings on the set of standard monomials.
For two standard monomials
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, we write
M_{1}<M_{2}
if

•

\len M_{1}<\len M_{2}
or

•

\len M_{1}=\len M_{2}
and
\deg M_{1}<\deg M_{2}
or

•

\len M_{1}=\len M_{2}
,
\deg M_{1}=\deg M_{2}
, and
M_{1}
is less than
M_{2}
with respect to the
left to right
lexicographic order when both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are written in increasing (that is, normal) order.

Similarly, we write
M_{1}\prec M_{2}
if

•

\len M_{1}<\len M_{2}
or

•

\len M_{1}=\len M_{2}
and
\deg M_{1}<\deg M_{2}
or

•

\len M_{1}=\len M_{2}
,
\deg M_{1}=\deg M_{2}
, and
M_{1}
is less than
M_{2}
with respect to the
right to left
lexicographic order when both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are written in increasing order.

By the PBW theorem, both
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
and
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
have a basis of standard monomials.
Given nonzero
F\in U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
or
F\in S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
, we define
LT_{<}F
, respectively
LT_{\prec}F
, to be the
<
-largest, respectively
\prec
-largest, standard monomial occurring in
F
with nonzero coefficient when
F
is written as a linear combination of standard monomials.

2.3.
Growth

In this short subsection we recall some definitions and basic facts about the growth of algebras and modules.
Let
R
be a finitely generated associative
\Bbbk
-algebra and let
M
be a finitely generated representation of
R
.
Then
R
has
polynomial growth
if there is a polynomial
p(t)\in\mathbb{R}[t]
so that, for every finite-dimensional subspace
V
of
R
,
\dim V^{n}\leq p(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
.
Further,
R
has
exponential growth
if there exists
V\subset R
so that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\dim V^{n})^{1/n}>1
(note that this limit always exists) and (strictly)
subexponential growth
if
R
has neither exponential or polynomial growth.
The growth of
M
is defined similarly; here we let
V\subset R
and
W\subset M
be finite-dimensional vector spaces and consider
\dim V^{n}W
as
n\to\infty
.
If
\mathfrak{k}
is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra of polynomial growth (for example, an affine or loop algebra) then
U(\mathfrak{k})
has subexponential growth by
[
Smi76
]
.

The idea of growth may be refined through considering the
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
or
\GK
of
R
and of
M
.
Here we define

\GK R=\sup_{V}\varlimsup\log_{n}\dim V^{n},

where the supremum is taken over all finite-dimensional subspaces
V
of
R
.
Likewise,

\GK M=\sup_{V,W}\varlimsup\log_{n}\dim V^{n}W,

where the supremum is taken over all finite-dimensional subspaces
V
of
R
and
W
of
M
.
If
R
is a finitely generated
\Bbbk
-algebra and
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module, let
V
be any finite-dimensional generating subspace of
R
that contains 1, and let
W
be any finite-dimensional generating subspace of
M
.
Then

\GK R=\varlimsup\log_{n}\dim V^{n},\quad\GK M=\varlimsup\log_{n}\dim V^{n}W,

and does not depend on the choice of
V
or
W
.

Note that
R
(respectively,
M
) has polynomial growth if and only if
\GK R<\infty
(respectively,
\GK M<\infty
).
We say that
R
has
just-infinite growth
(equivalently,
just-infinite GK-dimension
) if
\GK R=\infty
but for any nontrivial ideal
J
of
R
, then
\GK R/J<\infty
.
For more background about growth and GK-dimension, we refer the reader to
[
KL00
]
.

If
\mathfrak{k}
is a Lie algebra, then
\GK U(\mathfrak{k})=\dim\mathfrak{k}
.
Further, by
[
Smi76
]
, if
L
is an affine Kac-Moody algebra, then the central quotients
U_{\lambda}(L)
and
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
have intermediate growth.

We will repeatedly use the following basic fact about growth of algebras:
.

Scholium 2.4
.

Let
K
be a field and let
A\subseteq B
be
K
-algebras.
Suppose that
\GK A=\infty
and that
J
is an ideal of
B
such that
B/J
has polynomial growth.
Then
J\cap A\neq(0)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the fact that GK-dimension does not increase on subalgebras
[
KL00
, Lemma 3.1]
, so we cannot have
A\hookrightarrow B/J
.
∎

3.
A reduction result for symmetric algebras of current algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and let
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
.
We adopt the notation of Section
2
; in particular, let
r
be the order of
\sigma
.
The
positive twisted current algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the subalgebra
(tg[t])^{\sigma}
of the (twisted) loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.
In this section, we consider the symmetric algebra of the positive twisted current algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and show that it has just-infinite growth as a Poisson algebra: any factor by a proper Poisson ideal has polynomial growth.
We will generalise this result in later sections to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

The symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is a Lie algebra under the Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
induced from the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
.
That is, for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
we have
\{x,y\}=[x,y]
, and
\{-,-\}
is anticommutative and a derivation in each input.

A
Poisson ideal
of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is an ideal of the underlying commutative algebra which is also a Lie ideal for the Poisson bracket.
Note that a Lie ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is also a
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
-subrepresentation, where
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
acts on
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
by the adjoint action
\ad gt^{a}=\{gt^{a},-\}
.
Each
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is a
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
-subrepresentation of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
, although of course not a Poisson ideal.

Thus if
I
is a Poisson ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
, each
I\cap S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is a
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}
-subrepresentation of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.
We will spend the bulk of this section analyzing the structure of such subrepresentations.
In fact, because we want our results to apply to Poisson ideals of current algebras, we will consider the structure of
(t\mathfrak{g}[t])^{\sigma}
-subrepresentations of the
m
’th symmetric power
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.

Our key technical result is the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
I
be any nonzero
(t\mathfrak{g}[t])^{\sigma}
sub-representation of
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
.
There are
n,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}

such that for any standard monomial
M=g_{1}t^{i_{1}}\dots g_{m}t^{i_{m}}
with
i_{1}\geq n
, there is
H_{M}\in I
such that
LT_{<}H_{M}=M
and all
t
-powers in
H_{M}
are
\geq\ell
.

This result allows us to reduce “big” monomials modulo
I
to a linear combination of smaller monomials.
The proof is based on the interplay between the
<
and
\prec
-orders on standard monomials, generalising the key arguments of
[
IS20
]
to work in our substantially more commutative context.

3.1.
Preparatory results

Before proving the main reduction result Proposition
3.1
, we will need several preparatory lemmata which will help us control the
\prec
-leading terms of elements of
I
.
If
M=g_{1}t^{i_{1}}\dots g_{m}t^{i_{m}}
is a standard monomial, we say that
(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})
is the
congruence class
of
M
and write
M\equiv(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})
.
We will construct, for any congruence class
\underline{g}\in(\mathcal{B}_{\Delta})^{m}
, an element
H_{\underline{g}}\in I
with
LT_{\prec}H_{\underline{g}}\equiv\underline{g}
.
We will see that the existence of these elements is sufficient to prove Proposition
3.1
.

Constructing the
H_{\underline{g}}
will take several steps.
We first show that
H_{\underline{g}}
exists for
\underline{g}=(g_{\theta},\dots,g_{\theta})
or
(g_{-\theta},\dots,g_{-\theta})
; and further, we can assume, in this case, that all monomials of
H_{\underline{g}}
have the same congruence class.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\neq F\in S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
, and let

I
be the
(t\mathfrak{g}[t])^{\sigma}
-subrepresentation of
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
generated by
F
.

(1)

If
\{g_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{rp+s},F\}=0

for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and all
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}

then

F\in S^{m}(g_{\theta}[t,t^{-1}])
: that is,

F
is a linear combination of monomials of the form
{g}_{\theta}t^{k_{1}}\cdots{g}_{\theta}t^{k_{m}}
for some
k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{m}
.

(2)

If
\{g_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{rp+s},F\}=0

for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and all
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{-}

then

F\in S^{m}(g_{-\theta}[t,t^{-1}])
: that is,

F
is a linear combination of monomials of the form
{g}_{-\theta}t^{k_{1}}\cdots{g}_{-\theta}t^{k_{m}}
for some
k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{m}
.

(3)

There are nonzero
G\in I\cap S^{m}(g_{\theta}[t,t^{-1}])
and
G^{\prime}\in I\cap S^{m}(g_{-\theta}[t,t^{-1}])
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}
.
Let us take
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
\overline{q}=\overline{s}
and so that
if
g_{1}t^{j_{1}}\cdots g_{m}t^{j_{m}}
is a monomial in
F
, then
q>|j_{1}|+\cdots+|j_{m}|
.
Thus, if
M=g_{1}t^{j_{1}}\cdots g_{m}t^{j_{m}}
is a monomial appearing in
F
, then

\{{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{rq+s},M\}=g_{2}t^{j_{2}}\cdots g_{m}t^{j_{m}}[{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},g_{1}]t^{j_{1}+q}+g_{1}t^{j_{1}}g_{3}t^{j_{3}}\cdots g_{m}t^{j_{m}}[{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},g_{2}]t^{j_{2}+q}+\cdots\\
+g_{1}t^{j_{1}}\cdots g_{v-1}t^{j_{v-1}}[{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},g_{m}]t^{j_{m}+q}.

Note that each of the monomials above are written in normal order.
Since we have taken
q
to be large enough, none of those monomials will appear in expressions coming from the action of
{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{q}
on any monomial
M^{\prime}\neq M
occurring in
F
.
Hence by hypothesis the Lie brackets
[{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha},g_{i}]
are all zero.
This is true for all
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}
which implies that
[\mathfrak{n}^{+},g_{i}]=0
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Hence
g_{1}=\cdots=g_{m}=g_{\theta}
, which spans the centre of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.
The proof of
(2)
is similar.

To prove
(3)
, we first construct
0\neq G\in I
so that

(3.3)

\{g_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{rp+s},G\}=0\mbox{ for all $p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}$ and $(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{+}$.}

If
(
3.3
)
holds for
G=F
, we are done.
Otherwise there exist
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
(\overline{u},\beta)\in\Delta^{+}
such that
F_{1}=\{g_{\overline{u},\beta}t^{ra+u},F\}\neq 0
.
If (
3.3
) holds for
G=F_{1}
, we are done; else
there exist
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
(\overline{v},\gamma)\in\Delta^{+}
such that
F_{2}=\{g_{\overline{v},\gamma}t^{rb+v},F_{1}\}\neq 0
.
As
\{g_{\overline{s},\alpha},-\}
increases the height of monomials and the height of a monomial in
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
is at most
m\height\theta
, this process must terminate.
Thus, continuing this procedure repeatedly, we will reach a nonzero element
G
of
I
such that
(
3.3
) holds.

By symmetry there is
0\neq G^{\prime}\in I
such that

\{g_{\overline{s},\alpha}t^{rp+s},G^{\prime}\}=0
for all

p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
(\overline{s},\alpha)\in\Delta^{-}
.
The two parts of
(3)
then follow by applying
(1)
and
(2)
.
∎

Enumerate
\Delta^{+}=\{(\overline{\xi}_{1},\beta_{1}),\cdots,(\overline{\xi}_{\ell},\beta_{\ell})\}
where
0\leq\xi_{i}\leq r-1
.
Then the set
\{{g}_{\overline{\xi}_{1},\beta_{1}},\dots,{g}_{\overline{\xi}_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}}\}
forms a basis of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and the set
\{{g}_{\overline{-\xi_{1}},-\beta_{1}},\dots,{g}_{\overline{-\xi_{\ell}},-\beta_{\ell}}\}
forms a basis of
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
.
Recall that if
g\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s}}
, then we write
\overline{s}=|g|
.

We now prove the existence of
H_{\underline{g}}
with
LT_{\prec}H_{\underline{g}}\equiv\underline{g}
for any
\underline{g}\in(\mathcal{B}_{\Delta})^{m}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
I
be the
(t\mathfrak{g}[t])^{\sigma}
-subrepresentation of
S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma})
generated by an element
F\neq 0
.
Let
(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})\in(\mathcal{B}_{\Delta})^{m}
.
Then there exists
G
in
I
such that
LT_{\prec}(G)\equiv(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})
.

Proof.

Let
d=\height(\theta)>0
. Write
\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{s},\alpha}
, which implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{-\theta}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{-s},-\alpha}
i.e.
|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|=-|\mathfrak{g}_{-\theta}|
(mod
r
).
We prove by induction that for all
n\in\{0,\dots,m\}
there is
G(n)\in I
with the following properties:

(1)

LT_{\prec}(G(n))=g_{1}t^{k_{1}}\dots g_{n}t^{k_{n}}g_{\theta}t^{k_{n+1}}\dots g_{\theta}t^{k_{m}}
for some
k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(2)

k_{i}+2rd\leq k_{i+1}
for all
i>n
;

(3)

k_{i}<k_{i+1}
for all
i\leq n
.

Then
G=G(m)
is the element we seek.

To construct
G(0)
we use Lemma
3.2
.
By that result, there is a nonzero
H\in I\cap S^{m}(\mathfrak{g}_{-\theta}[t,t^{-1}])
.
Write
LT_{\prec}H=g_{-\theta}t^{j_{1}}\dots g_{-\theta}t^{j_{m}}
where
\overline{j}_{1}=\cdots=\overline{j}_{m}=|\mathfrak{g}_{-\theta}|
.

Let
G(0)=(\ad g_{\theta}t^{rd+|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|})^{2}(\ad g_{\theta}t^{{2rd+|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|}})^{2}\dots(\ad g_{\theta}t^{mrd+|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|})^{2}(H)
.
The monomials in
G(0)
are obtained from monomials in
H
by applying exactly two
g_{\theta}t^{i}
to each
g_{-\theta}t^{j_{k}}
.
(This is because
(\ad g_{\theta})^{3}=0
.)
The
\prec
-leading term of
G(0)
is obtained from
LT_{\prec}(H)
by applying the highest powers of
t
to the rightmost letters.
Thus

LT_{\prec}(G(0))=g_{\theta}t^{k_{1}}\dots g_{\theta}t^{k_{m}}

where
k_{i}=j_{i}+2(ird+|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|)
.
It is immediate that this has the claimed properties.

Now assume we have constructed
G(n)
as claimed for some
1\leq n\leq m-1
; we construct
G(n+1)
.
There are two cases, depending on whether
g_{n+1}
lies in a positive or negative root space.

Negative case:

g_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{n}^{-}
.

Step
1^{-}
:
let
H_{1}^{-}=(\ad g_{-\theta}t^{r-|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|})^{2(m-n)}(G(n))
and let

N_{1}^{-}=g_{1}t^{k_{1}}\dots g_{n}t^{k_{n}}g_{-\theta}t^{k_{n+1}+2(r-|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|)}\dots g_{-\theta}t^{k_{m}+2(r-|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|)}.

We claim that
N_{1}^{-}=LT_{\prec}(H_{1}^{-})
.
This is because the
\prec
-leading term of
H_{1}^{-}
is obtained from
LT_{\prec}(G(n))
by increasing the right-most powers of
t
as much as possible, and this clearly gives
N_{1}^{-}
.

Step
2^{-}
:
Let
H_{2}^{-}=(\ad g_{\theta}t^{r+|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|})^{2(m-n-1)}(H_{1}^{-})
and let

N_{2}^{-}=g_{1}t^{k_{1}}\dots g_{n}t^{k_{n}}g_{-\theta}t^{k_{n+1}+2(r-|\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}|)}g_{\theta}t^{k_{n+2}+4r}\dots g_{\theta}t^{k_{m}+4r}.

We claim that
N_{2}^{-}=LT_{\prec}(H_{2}^{-})

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and let
\sigma\in\Aut\mathfrak{g}
.

(1)

The enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
has just-infinite growth. That is, if
J
is a nonzero ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
, then
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})/J
has polynomial growth.

(2)

Let
I
be a nonzero Poisson ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
.
Then
S(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})/I
has polynomial growth.

Proof.

If
M
is a standard monomial in the letters
\mathcal{B}[t]^{\sigma}
,
we define the
modified degree
of
M
to be

\md M=\deg M+\len M.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{j}
(respectively,
\mathcal{S}^{j}
) be the subspace of
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
(respectively,
S(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
) spanned by standard monomials of modified degree
\leq j
.
As
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
is degree-graded, and, by the PBW theorem, is filtered by length, thus
\mathcal{U}^{\bullet}
defines a filtration on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
: that is,
\mathcal{U}^{i}\mathcal{U}^{j}\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Likewise,
\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}
defines a filtration on
S(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
.
It suffices to prove that

\dim\frac{\mathcal{U}^{j}+J}{J}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dim\frac{\mathcal{S}^{j}+I}{I}

have polynomial growth.

Let

\gr_{\md}(U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma}))=\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{U}^{j}/\mathcal{U}^{j-1}.

This is a (commutative) Poisson algebra:
if
F\in\mathcal{U}^{i}
and
G\in\mathcal{U}^{j}
then
FG-GF\in\mathcal{U}^{i+j-1}
so
\gr_{\md}U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})

Define

is isomorphic to the infinite-dimensional Heisenberg algebra.)
We thus have an adjoint
H
-action and induced
U(H)
-action on
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
; but the adjoint action of
c
is trivial, so this descends to an action of
U(H)/(c)\cong\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
.
This proves
(1)
.
Further, an ideal of a ring is closed under commutation with any element, establishing
(4)
.

It remains to prove
(3)
.
The case
k=1
is given by (
6.3
): set
\theta_{n}=h_{n}/2
.
Now let
\vec{r}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{k},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{k+1}
.
Let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
and denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
by
\{1_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0),1_{2},\dots,1_{k}\}
.
Then for
F\in\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
we have

\displaystyle\theta_{q}\theta_{\vec{p}}(F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{j+q}\frac{\partial}{\partial j}\Bigl(\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}}\frac{\partial^{k}F}{\partial I}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},I\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}e_{j+q}\Bigl(e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}}\frac{\partial^{k+1}F}{\partial j\partial I}+\frac{\partial^{k}F}{\partial I}\frac{\partial}{\partial j}(e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\theta_{\vec{r}}(F)+\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\frac{\partial^{k}F}{\partial I}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{j+q}\delta_{j,i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}(e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots\widehat{e_{i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}}),

where the notation
\widehat{e_{i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}}
means to omit this term.
But

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{j+q}\delta_{j,i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}(e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots\widehat{e_{i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}})=e_{i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}+q}e_{i_{1}+p_{1}}\dots\widehat{e_{i_{\ell}+p_{\ell}}}\dots e_{i_{k}+p_{k}},

and so

\theta_{q}\theta_{\vec{p}}=\theta_{\vec{r}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\theta_{\vec{p}+q1_{\ell}}.

By induction on
k
we have that
\theta_{\vec{r}}\in\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
, as needed.
∎

Lemma
6.2
gives strong constraints on the structure of
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
-subrepresentations of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
.
To see some of the implications, we will use the length grading on
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
, given by setting
\deg e_{i}=1
for all
i
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]_{k}
denote the vector space of homogeneous elements of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
of length
k
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
J^{\prime}
be any
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
-subrepresentation of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
, where the action of
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
is given by Lemma
6.2
.
Then:

(1)

J^{\prime}
is length-graded;

(2)

J^{\prime}
is closed under the translation automorphism

T:e_{n}\mapsto e_{n+1}

of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
, as well as under
T^{-1}
.

Before proving Corollary
6.4
, we give an elementary result on the
\theta
-operators of Lemma
6.2
.

Sublemma 6.5
.

Let
T_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\theta_{1^{k}}\in\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
, where
1^{k}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k})
.
Then the adjoint action of
T_{k}
applies the translation operator
T
to all elements of
S[e_{\bullet}]_{k}
.

Proof.

For
I\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, let
e_{I}=e_{i_{1}}\dots e_{i_{k}}
.
Then

T^{-1}(\theta_{1^{k}}(e_{I}))=\sum_{J\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}e_{J}\frac{\partial^{k}e_{I}}{\partial J}=\sum_{J\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}\cdot I}e_{J}\frac{\partial^{k}e_{I}}{\partial J}=|\mathfrak{S}_{k}\cdot I|e_{I}\frac{\partial^{k}e_{I}}{\partial I},

as
e_{I}
and
\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial I}
depend only on the
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
-orbit of
I
.
But
\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial I}e_{I}=|\Stab_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}(I)|
so
T^{-1}(\theta_{1^{k}}(e_{I}))=k!e_{I}
by the orbit-stabilizer theorem.
∎

Proof of Corollary
6.4
.

(1)
. A nonzero element of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]_{k}
is an eigenvector for the adjoint action of
\theta_{0}=h_{0}/2
with eigenvalue
k
.
As
J^{\prime}
is closed under
\theta_{0}
it decomposes as a sum of
\theta_{0}
-eigenspaces, which gives the grading.

For
(2)
, let
E\in J^{\prime}
; by
(1)
we may assume that
E
is homogeneous of length
k
.
Define
T_{k}
as in Sublemma
6.5
.
Likewise, let
T^{-1}_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\theta_{(-1)^{k}}
.
By the sublemma,
(\ad T_{k})(E)=T(E)
and likewise,
(\ad T^{-1}_{k})(E)=T^{-1}(E)
.
Thus these are are in
J^{\prime}
, as needed.
∎

It is useful to observe that the translation
T
extends to an automorphism of the loop algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

The linear map
T:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]\to\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
defined by

T(e_{n})=e_{n+1},\quad T(h_{n})=h_{n},\quad T(f_{n})=f_{n-1}

is a Lie algebra automorphism of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
. ∎

We will need two more computations in
U(L^{\prime})
.

Lemma 6.7
.

The following statements hold in
U(L^{\prime})
.

(1)

We have:

[f_{-a},e_{a}^{m}]=-me_{a}^{m-1}(h_{0}+ac)-2\binom{m}{2}e_{a}^{m-1}

for all
a\in\mathbb{Z},m\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

For any
E\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 0}]_{k}
we have

[f_{0},E]\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 0}]_{k-1}\cdot(\Bbbk\cdot(h_{\geq 0})\oplus\Bbbk).

Proof.

(1)
. The statement is immediate for
m=1
.
(Note that our convention is that
[f_{-a},e_{a}]=-h_{0}-ac
in
L^{\prime}
.)
Then by induction we have

\displaystyle[f_{-a},e_{a}^{m+1}]

\displaystyle=[f_{-a},e_{a}^{m}]e_{a}+e_{a}^{m}[f_{-a},e_{a}]

\displaystyle=\bigl(-me_{a}^{m-1}(h_{0}+ac)-2\binom{m}{2}e_{a}^{m-1}\bigr)e_{a}-e_{a}^{m}(h_{0}+ac)

\displaystyle=-me_{a}^{m-1}\bigl(e_{a}(h_{0}+ac)+2e_{a}\bigr)-2\binom{m}{2}e_{a}^{m}-e_{a}^{m}(h_{0}+ac)

\displaystyle=-(m+1)e_{a}^{m}(h_{0}+ac)-2\binom{m+1}{2}e_{a}^{m},

proving the statement.

(2)
is proved by a similar induction.
For
k=1
it is immediate, since
[f_{0},e_{a}]=-h_{a}
for any
a\geq 0
.

Now let
M
be a length
k
monomial in
e_{\geq 0}
and let
a\geq 0
.
By induction, write
[f_{0},M]=\sum_{i\geq 0}N_{i}h_{i}+N
where
N_{i},N\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 0}]_{k-1}
.
Then

[f_{0},Me_{a}]=[f_{0},M]e_{a}+M[f_{0},e_{a}]=(\sum N_{i}h_{i}+N)e_{a}-Mh_{a}=\sum(N_{i}e_{a}h_{i}+2N_{i}e_{a+i})-Mh_{a}.

This is in
\Bbbk[e_{\geq 0}]_{k}\cdot(\Bbbk\cdot(h_{\geq 0})\oplus\Bbbk)
, as needed.
∎

Let
B
be the subalgbra of
U(L^{\prime})
generated by the
e_{n}
and the
h_{n}
(so
c\in B
).
Observe that if we set
T(c)=c
then the formulae in Lemma
6.6
also give an automorphism of
B
.
Further, if
E\in\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]\subset B
, then, by an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
6.7
(2),
[f_{a},E]\in B
for any
a
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
E\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 0}]
, which we think of as a subalgebra of
U(L^{\prime})
.
Write
E=e_{0}^{m}E^{\prime}+E^{\prime\prime}
, where
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 1}]
.
Then

T([f_{0},E])-[f_{-1},T(E)]=cme_{1}^{m-1}T(E^{\prime}).

Proof.

By Lemma
6.7
we have

(6.9)

T([f_{0},E])-[f_{-1},T(E)]=\\
T\bigl((-me_{0}^{m-1}h_{0}-2\binom{m}{2}e_{0}^{m-1})E^{\prime}+e_{0}^{m}[f_{0},E^{\prime}]+[f_{0},E^{\prime\prime}]\bigl)-\\
(-me_{1}^{m-1}(h_{0}+c)-2\binom{m}{2}e_{1}^{m-1})T(E^{\prime})-e_{1}^{m}[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime})]-[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime\prime})]\\
=cme_{1}^{m-1}T(E^{\prime})+T(e_{0}^{m}[f_{0},E^{\prime}])-e_{1}^{m}[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime})]+T([f_{0},E^{\prime\prime}])-[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime\prime})].

Now, if
n\geq 0
then
[f_{0},e_{n}]=-h_{n}
in
U(L^{\prime})
.
We thus observe that, as
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 1}]
, the commutators
[f_{0},E^{\prime}]
and
[f_{0},E^{\prime\prime}]
agree with the commutators in
U_{0}(L^{\prime})=U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}])
, and likewise for the commutators
[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime})]
and
[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime\prime})]
.
It follows that, as
T
is an automorphism of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
and thus extends to an automorphism of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}])
, we have

T(e_{0}^{m}[f_{0},E^{\prime}])=e_{1}^{m}[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime})]\quad\mbox{and}\quad T([f_{0},E^{\prime\prime}])=[f_{-1},T(E^{\prime\prime})].

Thus (
6.9
) simplifies to

cme_{1}^{m-1}T(E^{\prime}),

as claimed.
∎

We now prove a version of Theorem
1.1
(1,2) for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
L
be an affine Lie algebra of type
A_{1}^{(1)}
, so
L\cong\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.

(1)

For any
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{*}
, the algebra
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
is simple.

(2)

Any nonzero ideal of
U(L^{\prime})
contains a nonzero polynomial in
c
; equivalently,
U(L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
is simple.

Proof.

(1)
. Let
J
be a nonzero ideal of
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
.
By Proposition
6.1
,
J^{\prime}=J\cap\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]\neq(0)
.
By Lemma
6.6
(4),
J^{\prime}
is a
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
-subrepresentation (under the adjoint action) of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
.
Thus,
J^{\prime}
is length-graded, by Corollary
6.4
(1).
Let
E
be a nonzero homogeneous element of
J^{\prime}
of minimal length, say
k
.
We show that we must have
k=0
; note that if
k=0
then
1\in J
.

Suppose that
k>0
.
Let
e_{m}
be the smallest letter in
E
: that is,
m
is the minimum
i
so that
e_{i}
appears in
E
.
Let
E(i):=T^{i-m}(E)
, which is a translate of
E
with smallest letter
e_{i}
.
By Corollary
6.4
(2), the
E(i)
are in
J^{\prime}
.
Thus
[f_{0},E(0)],[f^{-1},E(1)]\in J
.
By Lemma
6.7

[f_{0},E(0)]\in J\cap(\Bbbk\cdot(h_{\geq 0})\oplus\Bbbk)\cdot\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]_{k-1}.

Now, consider the element
T_{k-1}
defined in the proof of Corollary
6.4
(2), which lies in
\Bbbk[h_{\geq 0}]
, considered as a (commutative) subalgebra of
U_{\lambda}(L^{\prime})
.
As
\Bbbk[h_{\geq 0}]
is commutative, the adjoint action of
T_{k-1}
on
[f_{0},E(0)]
does not affect the
h_{i}
and so by the proof of Corollary
6.4
(2) it applies the translation operator
T
.
Thus

(\ad T_{k-1})([f_{0},E_{0}])=T([f_{0},E_{0}]).

This element is in
J
, which is closed under the adjoint action of
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
.

Write
E(0)=e_{0}^{m}E^{\prime}+E^{\prime\prime}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}\in\Bbbk[e_{\geq 1}]
; as
e_{0}
appears in
E(0)
we have
m\geq 1
and
E^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Lemma
6.8
shows (as
\lambda\neq 0
) that
e_{1}^{m-1}T(E^{\prime})\in J^{\prime}
.
But this is a nonzero homogeneous element of length
k-1
, contradicting the minimality of
k
.

(2)
. Suppose that
J
is a nonzero ideal of
U(L^{\prime})
with
J\cap k[c]=(0)
.
Then
J(c):=J\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
is a nonzero ideal of
U(L^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
.
Combining Scholium
2.4
and Proposition
5.5
we see that
J(c)\cap\Bbbk(c)[e_{\bullet}]\neq(0)
and clearing denominators we obtain a nonzero
E\in J\cap\Bbbk[c,e_{\bullet}]
.

As in the proof of
(1)
we can apply Lemma
6.8
to reduce the length of
E
. Thus we reduce to the case that
E
is homogeneous of length
0
in the
e_{i}
: that is,
E\in\Bbbk[c]
, giving a contradiction.
∎

6.2.
The general case

We now let
L
be an arbitrary affine Lie algebra.
To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we note that
L
contains (in fact, many choices of) a subalgebra isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
L
be an affine Kac-Moody algebra, with Chevalley generators
e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}
, central element
c
, and derivation
d
.
For any
f_{i}
, there is a subalgebra
\overline{L}
of
L
which contains
f_{i}
,
c
and
d
and is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
via an isomorphism which sends the positive Borel of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
into the positive Borel of
L
.

This is proved in
[
CRR+21
]
, but we give the computation explicitly here.

Proof.

There is a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and an automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
so that

L^{\prime}/(c)\cong\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]^{\sigma}.

Let
r
be the order of
\sigma
, so
r\in\{1,2,3\}
.
There are
e,f\in\mathfrak{g}
, forming part of an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
e_{i}=et^{k},f_{i}=ft^{-k}
.
Let
h=[e,f]
, and let

\overline{L}=\Bbbk\cdot(\{e_{i}t^{rn}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{f_{i}t^{rn}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{ht^{rn}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},c,d).

From the definition of the Lie bracket in
L
we have

[e_{i}t^{rn},f_{i}t^{rm}]=ht^{r(n+m)}+(k+rn)\delta_{n,-m}\kappa(e,f)c,

where
\kappa
is the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
, so
\overline{L}
is a Lie subalgebra of
L
.
It is not hard to see that
\overline{L}^{\prime}/(c)\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
via an isomorphism which sends the positive Borel to the positive Borel, so
\overline{L}^{\prime}
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
.
To show that
\overline{L}\cong\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
it is enough to show this central extension is nontrivial; in other words, that
\kappa(e,f)\neq 0
.

If
r=1
then
e,f
span root spaces of
\mathfrak{g}
.
By
[
Hum78
, Proposition 8.1]
,
e
is
\kappa
-orthogonal to all root spaces of
\mathfrak{g}
except for
\Bbbk f
.
As
\kappa
is nondegenerate,
\kappa(e,f)\neq 0
.

If
r>1
, then from the tables in
[
Kac90
, pp. 128-9]
we see that there are root vectors
e^{\prime},f^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
so that
\{e^{\prime},f^{\prime},[e^{\prime},f^{\prime}]\}
form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and an
r
’th root of unity
\eta
so that

e=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\eta^{i}\sigma^{i}(e^{\prime}),\quad\quad f=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\eta^{r-i}\sigma^{i}(f^{\prime}).

Applying
[
Hum78
, Proposition 8.1]
again we have

\kappa(e,f)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\kappa(\sigma^{i}(e^{\prime}),\sigma^{i}(f^{\prime}))=r\kappa(e^{\prime},f^{\prime})\neq 0,

where we have used that
\sigma
preserves the Killing form
\kappa
.
Thus
\overline{L}^{\prime}
is a nontrivial central extension of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}[t,t^{-1}]
and thus
\overline{L}\cong\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

Write the standard basis of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
as
\{et^{n}\}\cup\{ft^{n}\}\cup\{ht^{n}\}\cup\{C,d\}
, where

is central.
We caution that the isomorphism
\overline{L}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
constructed above may not send
c
to
C
; it does, however, send
c
to a nonzero scalar multiple of
C
.

We now complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by proving parts
(1)
and
(2)
, which are, respectively, parts (1) and (2) of the next result.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
L
be an affine Lie algebra.

(1)

For any
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{*}
, the algebras
U_{\lambda}(L)
and

are simple.

(2)

Any nonzero ideal of

or of

contains a nonzero element of

; equivalently, the algebras

Proof.

By Lemma
6.11
, let
\overline{L}
be a Lie subalgebra of
L
which is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.

(1)
. Let

be a nonzero ideal of

or of

.
By Proposition
6.1
,
J\cap U_{\lambda}(\overline{L}^{\prime})\neq(0)
and thus

by Theorem

(1).

(2)
. This proof is similar, applying Scholium

, Proposition

, and Theorem

(2) to the localised ideal

∎

We next finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
by proving parts (1) and (2).

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
L
be an affine Lie algebra.

(1)

For any
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{*}
, the algebras
S_{\lambda}(L)
and

are Poisson simple.

(2)

Any nonzero Poisson ideal of

or of

contains a nonzero element of

S(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)
and

Again, let
\overline{L}
be a Lie subalgebra of

which is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
Define

as in Subsection

.

(1)
.
Let

be a nonzero Poisson ideal of

or

.
By Theorem

(0) and Scholium

,

.

Since

is closed under Poisson brackets with any element of

, again

is a
\Bbbk[h_{\bullet}]
-subrepresentation of
\Bbbk[e_{\bullet}]
.
Thus Corollary
6.4
applies to

, and again

is graded and invariant under the translation operator

.

7.
Other applications

In this section, we give other applications of our growth results.

In Proposition
4.9
we used growth to show that nontrivial ideals of
U_{\lambda}(L)
must meet
U(L_{+})
.
However, we can use Theorem
1.1
(0) to obtain much stronger results of a similar flavour.

For simplicity, we state this next result only for untwisted loop algebras, although a similar result clearly holds in the twisted case.
Corollary
7.2
, which is an immediate consequence, seems rather surprising without the growth context.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. Let
J
be a nonzero ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}])
and let
\mathfrak{k}
be any infinite-dimensional Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
. Then
J\cap U(\mathfrak{k})
is nonzero.

Proof.

This is a direct application of Proposition
6.1
with
\lambda=0
.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

In the situation of Proposition
7.1
, if
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
is any infinite set, for example
X
consists of all of the powers of 7 or all of the primes, and
g
is any element of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
J
contains an element involving only
gt^{x}
for
x\in X
.

Proof.

The vector space spanned by
\{gt^{x}:x\in X\}
is an infinite-dimensional (abelian) Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
, so this follows directly from Proposition
7.1
.
∎

It is well-known that the enveloping algebras
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
,
U(L)
, etc., are not left or right noetherian.
However, the results of this paper, as well as
[
LSS20
, Corollary 5.14]
, raise the natural question of whether they satisfy the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals.
We close with two results related to this question.

In the next two results, let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with diagram automorphism
\sigma
, and let
L
be the affine Kac-Moody algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
and
\sigma
.

Proposition 7.3
.

The algebras

U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
,
U(L)
, and
U(L^{\prime})
satisfy the ascending chain condition (ACC) on completely prime ideals.

Proof.

The proof of
[
IS20
, Proposition 6.4]
works in our setting, appealing to Proposition
5.5
, Theorem
3.9
, and Theorem
1.1
as necessary. We omit the details.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

Let
W_{+}=t^{2}\Bbbk[t]\frac{d}{dt}
be the
positive Witt algebra
.
It is shown in
[
IS20
, Theorem 1.2]
that
U(W_{+})
has just-infinite growth.
Further, by
[
PS20
, Theorem 1.5]
, the symmetric algebra
S(W_{+})
satisfies the ascending chain condition on radical Poisson ideals.
These results are supporting evidence for the conjecture
[
PS20
, Conjecture 1.3]
that the enveloping algebra
U(W_{+})
satisfies the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals.

It is now known
[
LSS20
]
that symmetric algebras of (twisted) loop algebras satisfy the ascending chain condition on radical Poisson ideals, and this can easily be extended to symmetric algebras of affine Kac-Moody algebras.
Combining this result with Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
7.3
, it is natural to ask if enveloping algebras of affine Kac-Moody algebras satisfy the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals.
(It is easy to see that these enveloping algebras are not left or right noetherian.)
This is the subject of ongoing research. Note that this question is only really interesting for
U_{0}(L^{\prime})
.

A ring
R
is
Hopfian
if
R
is not isomorphic to any proper quotient
R/J
(equivalently, any epimorphism from
R\to R
is an isomorphism).
If
R
satisfies the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals, then
R
must be Hopfian.
We do not know if enveloping algebras of affine Lie algebras satisfy this ACC, but it is a consequence of our growth results that they are Hopfian.
Further, enveloping algebras of current algebras and central quotients of enveloping algebras of affine algebras satisfy the stronger Bassian property, where a ring
R

is
Bassian
if there is no injection of
R
into any proper quotient
R/J
.

Proposition 7.5
.

The algebras
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
,
U_{0}(L^{\prime})
, and
U_{0}(L)
are Bassian and Hopfian. Further,
U(L)
and
U(L^{\prime})
are Hopfian.

Proof.

This proof is similar to the proof of
[
IS20
, Proposition 6.5]
, but as it is fairly brief we give it here in full.

If
R
has just infinite growth, then
\GK R/J<\GK R
for any proper ideal
J
of
R
, so
R
cannot inject in
R/J
. Thus the Bassian (and thus Hopfian) property for
U(\mathfrak{g}[t]^{\sigma})
follows from Theorem
3.9
, and for
U_{0}(L^{\prime})
and
U_{0}(L)
it follows from Theorem
1.1
(0).

Let
U
be either
U(L)
or
U(L^{\prime})
.
To show that
U
is Hopfian, let
f
be a surjective endomorphism of
U
, with kernel
J
. As
U/J\cong\operatorname{Im}(f)=U
is torsionfree as a module over
\Bbbk[c]
,
the complex

0\to J\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)\to U\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)\to U\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)\to 0

is exact. Now by Proposition
5.5
, we must have
J\otimes_{\Bbbk[c]}\Bbbk(c)=0
, as otherwise a
\Bbbk(c)
-algebra of finite GK-dimension would surject onto one of infinite GK-dimension.
As
U
is
\Bbbk[c]
-torsionfree (or by Theorem
1.1
(2)),
J=0
.
∎

Frame set for Gabor systems with Haar window
Xin-Rong Dai, Meng Zhu

Abstract
. We show the full structure of the frame set for the Gabor system 𝒢 ( g ; α , β ) := { e − 2 π i m β ⋅ g ( ⋅ − n α ) : m , n ∈ ℤ } \mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta):=\{e^{-2\pi im\beta\cdot}g(\cdot-n\alpha):m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\} with the window being the Haar function g = − χ [ − 1 / 2 , 0 ) + χ [ 0 , 1 / 2 ) g=-\chi_{[-1/2,0)}+\chi_{[0,1/2)} . The strategy of this paper is to introduce the piecewise linear transformation ℳ \mathcal{M} on the unit circle, and to provide a complete characterization of structures for its (symmetric) maximal invariant sets. This transformation is related to the famous three gap theorem of Steinhaus which may be of independent interest. Furthermore, a classical criterion on Gabor frames is improved, which allows us to establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the Gabor system 𝒢 ⁡ ( g , α , β ) \mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta) to be a frame, i.e., the symmetric invariant set of the transformation ℳ \mathcal{M} is empty. Compared with the previous studies, the present paper provides a self-contained environment to study Gabor frames by a new perspective, which includes that the techniques developed here are new and all the proofs could be understood thoroughly by the readers without reference to the known results in the previous literature.

Introduction

Let
g\in L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\alpha,\beta>0
. The
Gabor system

\mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
with the

(or

and

and

, is defined by

\mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta):=\{e^{-2\pi im\beta x}g(x-n\alpha):m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

and we call that the Gabor system

if it forms a frame for

, i.e., there exist positive constants

such that

A\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mid\langle f,e^{-2\pi im\beta\cdot}g(\cdot-n\alpha)\rangle\mid^{2}\leq B\|f\|_{2}^{2}\ \ \mbox{for all }f\in L^{2}.

By
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and

we denote the set of all natural numbers, positive rational numbers and positive real numbers. For

, we use

to denote the greatest positive rational number that divides both
x
and
y
. The characteristic function on a set

is denoted by

. In this paper, we give the following full description of the frame set for the Haar function.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
and

\mathcal{G}(H_{1/2};\alpha,\beta)
be the Gabor system (
1.1
) generated by the Haar function

. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\mathcal{G}(H_{1/2};\alpha,\beta)
is a frame for

, then

(1.2)

\alpha\beta\leq 1\ \mbox{ and }\ \alpha\leq 1.

(ii)

If
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfies (

) and
\alpha\beta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then

\mathcal{G}(H_{1/2};\alpha,\beta)

if and only if

.

(iii)

If
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfies (

1.1.
Background and reformulation of Theorem
1.1

The conception of the Gabor frame was originated with two historical problems on the Gabor system with Gaussian window discussed by von Neumann
[
32
]
in 1932 and Gabor
[
15
]
in 1946, and the frame theory introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
11
]
in 1952.
The Gabor system can usually be viewed as a Schrödinger representation on the Weyl-Heisenberg group equipped with the Haar measure, and therefore Gabor frame is also known as Weyl-Heisenberg frame, see
[
10
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
23
]
and references therein.
A landmark paper
[
10
]
was published in 1986, where Daubechies, Grossmann and Meyer proved that for each pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
with
\alpha\beta<1
, there exists a compactly supported smooth function
g
such that
\mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a Gabor frame. Since then, Gabor theory has been received great attentions.
The readers may refer to the surveys by Casazza
[
2
]
, Gröchenig
[
17
]
and Heil
[
22
]
for historical remarks and its interaction to a wide range of mathematical fields, such as operator algebra and complex analysis, and many engineering applications involving time-dependent frequency content.

A fundamental problem in Gabor analysis is to determine the
frame set

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\ \mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)\ \ \mbox{is a Gabor frame}\ \}

for a given window function
g\in L^{2}
. Well-known results on the frame set include its openness for window functions in the Feichtinger algebra
[
12
,
13
]
, and the following density requirement

\mathcal{F}(g)\subset\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\}

for arbitrary window function
g\in L^{2}

[
22
,
16
,
8
]
.
The first breakthrough on the frame set is that
\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\alpha\beta<1\}
for the Gaussian window
g=e^{-x^{2}}
, which was conjectured by Daubechies and Grossmann
[
9
]
and
confirmed by Lyubarskii
[
30
]
and Seip and Wallstén
[
34
,
35
]
independently. Later on, Janssen and Strohmer
[
27
,
24
,
26
]
proved that the window functions
(e^{x}+e^{-x})^{-1},(1+x^{2})^{-1}
and
e^{-|x|}
share the same frame set with the Gaussian window, and the frame sets for
e^{-x}\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(x)
and
(1+ix)^{-1}
are
\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\}
.
A recent significant progress was made by Gröchenig, Romero and Stöckler
[
19
,
18
]
, where they proved that the frame sets for a class of totally positive functions are
\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\alpha\beta<1\}
and indicated that all the window functions mentioned above, with the exception of the hyperbolic secant window
(e^{x}+e^{-x})^{-1}
, are either totally positive functions or their slight modifications. In 2021, Belov, Kulikov and Lyubarskii
[
1
]
obtained some deep results on the frame sets for Herglotz functions and rational functions.

Gabor frames with compact windows (i.e., window functions are compactly supported) occupy important positions in Gabor theory due to their natural time-frequency localizations
[
7
]
, and they are widely studied in recent years, see
[
5
,
4
,
29
,
16
]
and references therein.
Among them the known necessary condition for the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
with a compact window
g
being a frame for
L^{2}
is that the support of its
\alpha-
shifts should cover the whole line, and hence

(1.3)

\mathcal{F}(g)\subset\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\mbox{ and }\alpha\leq|\mbox{supp }g|\},

where
\mbox{supp }g
denotes the closed interval with minimal length that contains the support of the window function
g
.
The only compact window that the frame set was totally determined is the characteristic function on an interval.
It was first considered by Janssen
[
25
]
, and then, after the contribution of Gu and Han
[
20
]
, Sun and the first author
[
6
]
provided a complete characterization to the frame set by 14 subfamilies.

As stated in our main result Theorem
1.1
, the main purpose of this paper is to explore the frame set of the Haar function
g(x)=H_{1/2}(x)
. Throughout this paper, we always denote
H(x)=-\chi_{[-1,0)}(x)+\chi_{[0,1)}(x)
and

(1.4)

H_{c}(x)=-\chi_{[-c,0)}(x)+\chi_{[0,c)}(x)\ \ \mbox{for all }\ c>0.

It is well known that the Haar function plays special roles in scientific fields, and the Haar wavelet basis generated by the Haar function has been widely used in many engineering fields related to information science. Therefore, we naturally expect that the Gabor system generated by the Haar window function should have considerable impact on basic and applied sciences.

For
g\in L^{2}
and scale

, define

and the Gabor system with normalized shift

Observe that for any

. Thus,
by taking

and

,
the full description of the frame set of the Haar window function

is reduced to finding parameter pairs

such that the normalized Gabor system

.
Furthermore, according to (

holds, and hence it suffices to consider the parameter pair

satisfying

0<a\leq\min(1,2c).

In view of the facts mentioned above, one can formulate the following statement that is equivalent to the assertions (ii) and (iii) of Theorem

For simplicity, we denote a non-negative rational number

Let

1.2.
Piecewise linear transformation on the unit circle

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the unit circle. The famous “Steinhaus conjecture” on the mod 1 distribution said that, for given
\alpha>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there are at most three arcs of the various lengths on the circle
\mathbb{T}
partitioned by
\{\langle n\alpha\rangle:n=0,1,\ldots,N\}
, where
\langle x\rangle
denotes the fractional part of number
x
. This conjecture was proved independently by P. Erdös, G. Hajós, V. T. Sós, J. Surányi, S. Świerczkowski and P. Szüsz, and was well known as the three gap theorem, see
[
36
,
37
]
for details, and also see
[
21
]
and references therein for its recent progresses and extensions.

Throughout this paper, we will use
[0,1)
and also
(0,1]
to represent the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, and denote by
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
and
\mathbb{T}_{(0,1]}
the topological space retaining the topology of
\mathbb{T}
, in the usual manner.
For
0\leq\alpha<1
and
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}\leq 1
, we define the
piecewise linear transformation

\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,x_{1},x_{2}}
from
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
to itself as follows,

(1.9)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,x_{1},x_{2}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle&{\rm if}\ t\in[0,x_{1})=:U\\
t&{\rm if}\ t\in[x_{1},x_{2})=:\textbf{H}\\
\langle t+\alpha\rangle&{\rm if}\ t\in[x_{2},1)=:V\end{array}\right.;

and the
maximal invariant set
of the map
\mathcal{M}
is defined to be the maximal set
\mathcal{S}
for which

(1.10)

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus\textbf{H}.

Obviously,
\mathcal{S}
consists of the points
t\in\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
such that the orbit
\{\mathcal{M}^{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will not be absorbed in
H
when
n
tends to infinity.
We shall point out that the asymptotic behavior of the points in
\mathcal{S}
under the map
\mathcal{M}
behave like that in the Steinhaus’ problem. For instance, if
\mathcal{S}\subset U
(or
\mathcal{S}\subset V
), it is clear that
\mathcal{M}^{n}(t)=\langle t+n(\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})\rangle
(or
\mathcal{M}^{n}(t)=\langle t+n\alpha\rangle
) for all
t\in\mathcal{S}
, and we will further show that if

(1.11)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap U\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathcal{S}}\cap V\neq\emptyset,

there will have a bijective map
Y
from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
such that
Y(\mathcal{M}^{n}(t))=\langle Y(t)+nY(\alpha)\rangle
holds for all
t\in\mathcal{S}
, see Theorem
1.4
below.

An extended topic for the Steinhaus’ problem
is the interval exchange transformations, for which considering the transformations on
{[0,1)}
by exchanging the order of the partition
I_{j},j=1,2,\ldots,n
, of
{[0,1)}
via a substitution over
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.
This topic links to vast intrinsic problems in many subjects of pure mathematics such as number theory, dynamical system, harmonic analysis and so on, see
[
28
,
14
,
3
,
38
,
31
]
and references therein for details. It is worth noting that the map
\mathcal{M}
defined in (
1.9
) is a 4–intervals exchange transformation by redefining
\mathcal{M}(\textbf{H})=\textbf{H}+\alpha-x_{1}
and the appropriate division on
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
. In the topic of the interval exchange transformation, the studies mainly focus on the transformations with the property “unique ergodicity”, which leads to
\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
.
However, in this paper we shall characterize the condition for
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, as well as its structure, and the property of its symmetric maximal invariant subset, and so on.

The following theorem gives us a characterization on the structure of the maximal invariant set
\mathcal{S}
of the transformation

.

.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the map defined in (

3.
The Ron-Shen’s characterization and its improvements

Our journey to the preparatory works for full description of the frame set for Haar windows in Theorem
1.2
starts from
the Ron-Shen’s characterization in
[
33
]
, which can be recast
as follows in our setting.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
g\in L^{2}
and
a>0
. Then
\mathcal{G}(g;a)
is a Gabor frame if and only if
there exist
A,B>0
such that for each
\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}

(3.1)

A\|\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{j}g(x+j-na)\Big|^{2}\leq B\|\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\ \mbox{ a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
W(\mathcal{C},\ell^{1}):=\{g\in\mathcal{C}:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in[0,1)}|g(x+k)|<\infty\}
be the Wiener amalgam space. Using a technique combining
the Ron-Shen’s characterization,
Gröchenig, Romero and Stöckler
[
18
]
combined some tricks in functional analysis, operator theory and complex analysis, and they
presented a very strong criterion that the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(g;a)
generated by a window function
g
in the Wiener amalgam space
W(\mathcal{C},\ell^{1})
is not a Gabor frame if and only if there exist
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and a nonzero bounded sequence
\textbf{0}\neq\{d_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
such that

(3.2)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{j}g(t_{0}+j-na)=0\mbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Unfortunately, the Haar function
H
does not belong to the Wiener amalgam space
W(\mathcal{C},\ell^{1})
and then the above bounded linear dependence does not apply directly. Observe that the Haar function is piecewise continuous. To describe the frame set of Haar function, we make a slight modification to the criterion (
3.2
), through replacing the window function
g
by its left/right limits
g^{\pm}(t):=\lim_{\tilde{t}\rightarrow t^{\pm}}g(\tilde{t})
in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
a>0
, and
g
be a compactly supported function such that its left/right limits
g^{\pm}
are well defined. Then
\mathcal{G}(g;a)
is not a Gabor frame if and only if
there exist
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and a nonzero bounded sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
such that

(3.3)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{j}h(t_{0}+j-na)=0\ \mbox{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{Z},

for either
h=g^{+}
or
h=g^{-}
.

As the requirement (
3.3
) is
a
-shift invariance, we may replace
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
by
t_{0}\in[0,a)
in Theorem
3.2
. Observe that
\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}^{+}}H(x)=H(x_{0})
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}^{-}}H(x)=-H(-x_{0})
. Therefore we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

\mathcal{G}(H_{c};a)
is not a frame for
L^{2}
, if and only if
there exist
t_{0}\in[0,a)
and a nonzero bounded sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
such that

(3.4)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{j}H_{c}(t_{0}+j-na)=0\ \mbox{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

To prove Theorem

, we need some notations and the following technical lemma.
Given a sequence

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Q=\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}
and
\Lambda_{N},N\geq 1
be as in (
3.5
). Then

(3.6)

\|Q_{\Lambda_{N}}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{3}{32N}\|Q\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Proof
.

For each

, let

and

be the nearest elements in

|q_{k}|\leq\max\{2^{-\ell(k)+1}|q_{L(k)}|,2^{-r(k)+1}|q_{R(k)}|\},k\in\mathbb{Z},

{k_{j_{1}}}<R(k)\leq k_{j_{1}+1}.

\displaystyle\hskip-5.78172pt\leq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{0}-na\in N_{0}+(-M_{0},M_{0})}\Big|\sum_{|j+t_{0}-na|<M_{0}}b_{j}g^{+}(t_{0}+j-na)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{0}-na\in-N_{0}+(-M_{0},M_{0})}\Big|\sum_{|j+t_{0}-na|<M_{0}}b_{j}g^{+}(t_{0}+j-na)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g^{+}\|^{2}_{\infty}\sum_{t_{0}-na\in N_{0}+(-M_{0},M_{0})}\Big(\sum_{|j-N_{0}|<2M_{0}}|b_{j}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|g^{+}\|^{2}_{\infty}\sum_{t_{0}-na\in-N_{0}+(-M_{0},M_{0})}\Big(\sum_{|j+N_{0}|<2M_{0}}|b_{j}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\big(\frac{2M_{0}}{a}+1\big)M_{0}^{3}\sum_{N_{0}-2M_{0}<|j|\leq N_{0}}|b_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle A\|\{b_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{+}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{j}g(t+j-na)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{+}}\sum_{|n|<(N_{0}+M_{0}+|t_{0}|)/a}\Big|\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}b_{j}g(t+j-na)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{j}g^{+}(t_{0}+j-na)\Big|^{2}.

This together with (

)
in Proposition
3.1
. This completes the proof of the sufficiency.

Necessity
. Suppose that

is not a Gabor frame. Observe that for sequence

. Therefore for all

and Proposition
3.1
,
we can find a nonzero sequence

with positive Lebesgue measure

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|\{\|(T_{t}(Q_{k}))_{(\lfloor{n}/a\rfloor-k/a,\lfloor{n}/a\rfloor+k/a)}\|_{\ell^{2}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

and define

Then

(3.17)

p_{k,0}=1

by the definition of the sequence

(3.18)

by the assumption

for the sequence

,
and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|<k/a}\Big|\sum_{|j|\leq 2k}p_{k,j}g(t^{\prime}+j-na)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|q_{k,n_{0}}|^{-2}\sum_{|n|<k/a}\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{k,n_{0}+j}g(t+n_{0}+j-(n+\lfloor{n_{0}}/a\rfloor)a)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle|q_{k,n_{0}}|^{-2}\|(T_{t}(Q_{k}))_{(\lfloor{n_{0}}/a\rfloor-k/a,\lfloor{n_{0}}/a\rfloor+k/a)}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq k^{-2},\ t^{\prime}\in E_{k,n_{0}}+n_{0}-a\lfloor{n_{0}}/a\rfloor,

where the first equality follows from (

4.
Piecewise linear transformation I: maximal invariant sets

This section will be devoted to investigating the structure of the maximal invariant set
\mathcal{S}
of the map
\mathcal{M}
defined as in (
1.9
), which plays an important role in the next section for analyzing the symmetric maximal invariant set.
In Subsection
4.1
, we provide some descriptions on
\mathcal{S}
in Proposition
4.1
, and then we use it to prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.
The structures of
\mathcal{S}
for the cases
\mathcal{S}\subset U
and
\mathcal{S}\subset V
are presented in Subsection
4.2
.
In Subsection
4.3
, we consider the case that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap U\neq\emptyset
and
{\mathcal{S}}\cap V\neq\emptyset
, and show in Theorem
4.4
that there are three types of the structures for such
\mathcal{S}
. At last, we build the relationship between the different types of the structures via the disturbance of the map
\mathcal{M}
in Proposition
4.6
.

At first, we indicate that both the maximal invariant set
\mathcal{S}
and the symmetric maximal invariant set
\mathcal{E}
are well defined. This is because we may verify that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\{t\in\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}:\mathcal{M}^{n}(t)\not\in\textbf{H}\ \mbox{ for all }\ n\geq 0\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\{t\in\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}:\mathcal{M}^{m}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{n}(1-t))\not\in\textbf{H}\ \mbox{ and }\ \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{m}(1-{\mathcal{M}}^{n}(t))\not\in\widetilde{\textbf{H}}\ \mbox{ for all }\ m,n\geq 0\}.

We shall introduce some notations which will be used in the sequel. Two sets
E_{1},E_{2}
in the unit circle
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
are called
separated
if they have positive distance. Given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we use the symbol
\langle I\rangle
to denote an arc in
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
, that is,
\langle I\rangle:=\{\langle x\rangle:x\in I\}
. In particular, if an arc
\langle I\rangle
in
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
contains
0
, we might regard it as the union of two intervals in
[0,1)
, such as
(a,1)\cup[0,b)
for some
0<b<a<1
. Moreover, an arc
J
is said to be a
gap
(resp.
open gap
) of
\mathcal{M}
if
J
is the maximal arc (resp. open arc) such that
J\subset\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. We will see, from the proof of Theorem
1.3
, that all gaps are left-closed and right-open intervals in
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
, therefore they are pairwise separated.

4.1.
Proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

Proposition 4.1
.

For
0\leq\alpha<1
and
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}\leq 1
, we let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,x_{1},x_{2}}
be the transformation defined as in (
1.9
), and
{\mathcal{S}}
be its maximal invariant set defined in (
1.10
). If
{\mathcal{S}}\neq\emptyset
, then for each
0<\varepsilon<1
,

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset
or
{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon)\rangle\neq\emptyset
;

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon,1)\neq\emptyset
or
{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{1}-\varepsilon,x_{1})\rangle\neq\emptyset
;

(iii)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset
or
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon,1)\neq\emptyset
;

(iv)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{1}-\varepsilon,x_{1})\rangle\neq\emptyset
or
{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon)\rangle\neq\emptyset
.

Proof
.

Let
U,V
and
\mathcal{S}
be as in (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).

(i). Suppose on the contrary that there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)={\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon)\rangle=\emptyset
.
Set
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{S}}-\varepsilon
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap U={\mathcal{S}}\cap U-\varepsilon
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap V={\mathcal{S}}\cap V-\varepsilon
. The linearity of
\mathcal{M}
on
U
and
V
will yield that

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap U)=\langle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}\cap U)-\varepsilon\rangle
and
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap V)=\langle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}\cap V)-\varepsilon\rangle
. Consequently,

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})=\langle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}})-\varepsilon\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{S}}-\varepsilon\rangle={\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}.

This implies
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}}\cup{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})={\mathcal{S}}\cup{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, contradicting to the maximality of
\mathcal{S}
, we obtain (i).

(ii). The idea of the proof is the same to that of (i). We omit the details.

(iii). On the contrary, we may let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
be the largest numbers such that

(4.1)

\mathcal{S}\cap[0,\varepsilon_{1})={\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon_{2},1)=\emptyset.

Now it follows from the dichotomy in (i) and (ii) that
0<x_{1}<x_{2}<1
and

(4.2)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[x_{1}-\varepsilon,x_{1})\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathcal{S}}\cap[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset

hold for all
0<\varepsilon<\min{(x_{1},1-x_{2})}
, and therefore
[0,\varepsilon_{1})\subset U
and
[1-\varepsilon_{2},1)\subset V
. This means
\varepsilon_{1}<x_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}<1-x_{2}
.
Combining with (
4.1
) and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}
, one has that

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{M}([\varepsilon_{1},x_{1}))\cup\mathcal{M}([x_{2},1-\varepsilon_{2}))\subset\langle[\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\varepsilon_{1},1+\alpha-\varepsilon_{2})\rangle.

Let
K=\langle[\alpha-\varepsilon_{2},\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\varepsilon_{1})\rangle
. Then
K
is an arc of the length
x_{2}-x_{1}+\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}
and
K\cap\mathcal{S}=\emptyset.
This, together with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), yields that
K\cap\textbf{H}=\emptyset
and
K\cap\big([1-\varepsilon_{2},1)\cup[0,\varepsilon_{1})\big)=\emptyset
. Therefore,
K\subset U
or
K\subset V
, and hence
|{\mathcal{M}}(K)|=|K|
and

\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{M}(K)\subset\mathcal{M}\Big(\mathcal{S}\cap\big(K\cup\textbf{H}\big)\Big)=\emptyset.

Applying the above procedure to the set
\mathcal{M}(K)
instead of
K
, and then by induction, we will get a sequence of arcs
\mathcal{M}^{n}(K),n\geq 0
, with the same length
|{\mathcal{M}}^{n}(K)|=|K|
, such that
\mathcal{M}^{n}(K)\subset\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus\mathcal{S}
satisfying that
\mathcal{M}^{n}(K)\cap\textbf{H}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{M}^{n}(K)\cap\big([1-\varepsilon_{2},1)\cup[0,\varepsilon_{1})\big)=\emptyset
.
Thus, for any two integers
1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}
,

\mathcal{M}^{n_{1}}(K)\cap\mathcal{M}^{n_{2}}(K)=\mathcal{M}^{n_{1}}(K\cap\mathcal{M}^{n_{2}-n_{1}}(K))=\mathcal{M}^{n_{1}+1}\Big(\big([1-\varepsilon_{2},1)\cup[0,\varepsilon_{1})\big)\cap\mathcal{M}^{n_{2}-n_{1}-1}(K)\Big)=\emptyset.

This means
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}(K),n\in\mathbb{N}
, is a sequence of pairwise disjoint sets with the same length in
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
, this contradiction means that (iii) holds.

(iv). The proof is similar to that of (iii). In fact, since
\mathcal{S}\cap
H
=\emptyset,
one can suppose on the contrary that there exists
an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[x_{1}-\varepsilon_{0},x_{2}+\varepsilon_{0})\rangle=\emptyset
.
This together with (i) and (ii) implies that

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon,1)\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset

for all
0<\varepsilon<1
,
which yields, from the definition of
\mathcal{M}
, that

{\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[\alpha-\varepsilon,\alpha)\rangle\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathcal{S}}\cap\langle[\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1},\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\varepsilon)\rangle\neq\emptyset.

Let

K
is an open gap, and therefore

and

. Thus, it follows that
K\subset U
or
K\subset V
, and hence
{\mathcal{M}}(K)
is an open gap with
|{\mathcal{M}}(K)|=|K|
. By induction, we will get a sequence of open gaps

, with the same length
|{\mathcal{M}}^{n}(K)|=|K|
, and that, for any

It is impossible, (iv) follows. The proof of Proposition

\square

{\mathcal{S}}
. This together with (
4.6
) leads to
{\mathcal{M}}^{N}(t)\in\textbf{H}
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus\mathcal{S}
.
We complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First, by Theorem
1.3
,
{\mathcal{S}}
can be written as the union of finitely many pairwise disjoint left-closed and right-open intervals in [0,1).
It is obvious that the map
Y
is bijective from
{\mathcal{S}}
to
\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}
.

If
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<1
are two points in one interval of
\mathcal{S}
, then

\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t_{2}))-Y(\mathcal{M}(t_{1}))\rangle=\frac{|{\mathcal{S}}\cap\mathcal{M}([t_{1},t_{2}))|}{|\mathcal{S}|}=\frac{|{\mathcal{S}}\cap[t_{1},t_{2})|}{|\mathcal{S}|}=\langle Y(t_{2})-Y(t_{1})\rangle,

which implies
\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t_{2}))-Y(t_{2})\rangle=\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}))-Y(t_{1})\rangle
.

If
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<1
are two points in two adjacent intervals of
{\mathcal{S}}
, assume that
t_{1}\in[a,b)\subset{\mathcal{S}}
,
t_{2}\in[c,d)\subset{\mathcal{S}}
and
[b,c)\subset\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}
, then

\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t_{2}))-Y(t_{2})\rangle=\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(c))-Y(c)\rangle=\lim_{t\rightarrow b^{+}}\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t))-Y(t)\rangle=\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}))-Y(t_{1})\rangle.

So, by recursion,
\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t))-Y(t)\rangle
is a constant for all

t\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and therefore

\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(t))-Y(t)\rangle=\langle Y({\mathcal{M}}(0))-Y(0)\rangle=\langle Y(\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})\rangle=\langle Y(\alpha)\rangle,

where the first equality follows from Proposition
4.1
(iii) and take
t\to 0
or
t\to 1
for desired situations. We obtain (
1.14
).

The other conclusion in Theorem
1.4
is an immediate consequence of Proposition
4.3
below.

\square

4.2.
Maximal invariant set, Part I

In this subsection, we study the structure of the maximal invariant set
{\mathcal{S}}
when
{\mathcal{S}}\subset U
and
{\mathcal{S}}\subset V
respectively.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,x_{1},x_{2}}
be the map defined in (
1.9
). Then:

(i)

\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\subset U
if and only if
\langle\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle=\frac{p}{q}
and
x_{1}>\frac{q-1}{q}
, where
\frac{p}{q}
is a simple fraction for some integers
0\leq p<q
. In this case,
Y(\alpha)=\frac{p}{q}
and

(4.8)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[\frac{k}{q},\frac{k-q+1}{q}+x_{1}\big).

(ii)

\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\subset V
if and only if
\alpha=\frac{p}{q}
and

x_{2}<\frac{1}{q}
, where
\frac{p}{q}
is a simple fraction for some integers
0\leq p<q
. In this case,
Y(\alpha)=\frac{p}{q}
and

(4.9)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[x_{2}+\frac{k}{q},\frac{k+1}{q}\big).

Proof
.

(i).
Necessity
. Note that
\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\subset U
implies that
\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
by Corollary
4.2
and the observation that

(4.10)

{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})=\langle\mathcal{S}+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle.

If
\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}=1
, then
\mathcal{S}=U
, which obviously leads to that
Y(\alpha)=Y(\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})=0
and (
4.8
) holds. Write

\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}=\frac{p}{q}

for some co-prime positive integers
p<q
. Then it follows from (
4.10
) that

\langle{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{1}{q}\rangle={\mathcal{S}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\cap[\frac{q-1}{q},1)\subset[0,x_{1})\cap[\frac{q-1}{q},1),

which implies that
x_{1}>\frac{q-1}{q}
and

(4.11)

{\mathcal{S}}\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[\frac{k}{q},\frac{k-q+1}{q}+x_{1}\big).

Observing that the right hand side of (
4.11
) is invariant under the map
{\mathcal{M}}
. Thus, (
4.8
) is valid and

Y(\alpha)=Y(\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})=\frac{|\mathcal{S}\cap[0,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})|}{|\mathcal{S}|}=\frac{|\mathcal{S}\cap[0,p/q)|}{|\mathcal{S}|}=\frac{p}{q},

as desired.

Sufficiency
. Notice that the set
{\mathcal{S}_{U}}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[\frac{k}{q},\frac{k-q+1}{q}+x_{1}\big)\subset U
is an invariant set of
{\mathcal{M}}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{S}}_{U}\subset\mathcal{S}
. Therefore it suffices to show that
\mathcal{S}\cap V=\emptyset
.

In fact, if
\mathcal{S}\cap V\neq\emptyset
, it is reasonable to define

t=\min\{x:x\in\mathcal{S}\cap V\}

since
\mathcal{S}
can be rewritten as the union of finitely many pairwise disjoint left-closed and right open intervals in
[0,1)
by Theorem
1.3
. Thus,
t-(x_{2}-x_{1})\not\in\mathcal{S}
.

However, since each
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}\cap[0,\frac{q-1}{q})
corresponds to a
k^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,q-1\}
such that
\mathcal{M}^{k^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})=\langle t^{\prime}+\frac{k^{\prime}p}{q}\rangle\in[\frac{q-1}{q},1)
, it follows that

\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(t)=\mathcal{M}^{k}(\langle t+\alpha\rangle)=\langle t+\alpha+\frac{kp}{q}\rangle\in[\frac{q-1}{q},1)

for some
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}
. Notice that
t-(x_{2}-x_{1})\in[\frac{q-1}{q},1)
and
(t-(x_{2}-x_{1}))-\langle t+\alpha+\frac{kp}{q}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}/q
. Then
t-(x_{2}-x_{1})=\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(t)\in\mathcal{S}
. It is a contradiction, and thus
\mathcal{S}\cap V=\emptyset
, as desired.

(ii). The proof is similar to that of (i), we will give the detailed proof for the sake of completeness.

Necessity
. If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset V
, it follows from
{\mathcal{S}}=\langle\mathcal{S}+\alpha\rangle
that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, for the case that
\alpha=0
, one has
p=0
and
q=1
, and
\mathcal{S}=V
by the assumption that
\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\subset V
. Therefore
Y(\alpha)=0
and (
4.9
) is valid. For the case that
\alpha\not=0
, we write
\alpha=\frac{p}{q}
for some co-prime positive integers
p<q
, then
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
\frac{1}{q}-
periodic set, and

\emptyset\neq{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\frac{1}{q})\subset[x_{2},1)\cap[0,\frac{1}{q}).

Therefore, we have
x_{2}<\frac{1}{q}
,
{\mathcal{S}}=\cup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[x_{2}+\frac{k}{q},\frac{k+1}{q}\big)
, and obviously
Y(\alpha)=\frac{p}{q}
.

Sufficiency
. Since the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{V}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}\big[x_{2}+\frac{k}{q},\frac{k+1}{q}\big)\subset V
is an invariant set of
{\mathcal{M}}
, so it suffices to prove
\mathcal{S}\cap U=\emptyset
.

Assume on the contrary that
\mathcal{S}\cap U\neq\emptyset
. One can choose
t\in\mathcal{S}\cap U
such that

t>\sup\{x:x\in\mathcal{S}\cap U\}-(x_{2}-x_{1}).

This means that
t+x_{2}-x_{1}\not\in\mathcal{S}
. We will show that it is impossible in the following:

(a) If
\mathcal{M}(t)=\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle\in[0,\frac{1}{q})
, then
\mathcal{M}(t)=t+x_{2}-x_{1}
by the observation that
t+x_{2}-x_{1}\in[0,\frac{1}{q})
and
\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle-(t+x_{2}-x_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}/q
. Therefore
t+x_{2}-x_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
.

(b) If
\mathcal{M}(t)=\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle\not\in[0,\frac{1}{q})
, then there exists
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,q-1\}
such that

\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(t)=\mathcal{M}^{k}(\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle)=\langle t+\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\frac{kp}{q}\rangle\in[0,\frac{1}{q}).

Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(t)=t+x_{2}-x_{1}
, and
t+x_{2}-x_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Thus
\mathcal{S}\cap U=\emptyset
. This finishes the proof of Proposition
4.3
.

\square

4.3.
Maximal invariant set, Part II

In this subsection, we consider the topological structure of the maximal invariant set
{\mathcal{S}}
when
\mathcal{S}
satisfies

(4.12)

{\mathcal{S}}\cap U\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{and }\ {\mathcal{S}}\cap V\neq\emptyset.

In this case, we have
0<x_{1}<x_{2}<1
. Recall that
\alpha=0
and
\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}=1
will lead to
\mathcal{S}=V
and
\mathcal{S}=U
respectively by Proposition
4.3
. Therefore, by Corollary
4.2
, we have

0<\alpha<\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}<1.

The fine structure of the maximal invariant set
\mathcal{S}
as in (

, in which we denote these three types of structures for

by

,
II

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,x_{1},x_{2}}
be the map defined in (
1.9
) and
{\mathcal{S}}
be its maximal invariant set defined in (
1.10
). Assume that
{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies (
4.12

(i)

(
Type I
) There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}

{\mathcal{M}}^{N-1}([\alpha,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}))=[x_{1},x_{2})
and

(4.13)

\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}([\alpha,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})).

(ii)

(
Type II
) There exist
0<\delta<\min(x_{1},1-\alpha-x_{2}+x_{1})

(4.14)

\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}([\alpha,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\delta))\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{M-N}{\mathcal{M}}^{j-1}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1})).

(iii)

(
Type III
) There exist
0<\delta<\min(1-x_{2},\alpha)
and integers
0<N<M
such that

{\mathcal{M}}^{N-1}([\alpha-\delta,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}))=[x_{1},x_{2}+\delta),\quad{\mathcal{M}}^{M-N}([x_{2},x_{2}+\delta))=[1-\delta,1)

(4.15)

\mathbb{T}_{[0,1)}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}([\alpha-\delta,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}))\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{M-N}{\mathcal{M}}^{j-1}([x_{2},x_{2}+\delta)).

Furthermore, the unions of (
4.13

4.14
) and (
4.15
) are separated respectively.

Proof
.

According to Proposition
4.1
(iii), we can divide the whole proof into the following three cases corresponding to (i), (ii) and (iii), respectively.

Case 1:

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset
and
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon,1)\neq\emptyset
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

In this case, it follows from
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{S}}
that

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[\alpha-\varepsilon,\alpha)\neq\emptyset\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{S}}\cap[\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1},\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1}+\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset

for all
0<\varepsilon<\min(\alpha,1-\alpha-x_{2}+x_{1})
. Then the set
T=[\alpha,\alpha+x_{2}-x_{1})
is a gap of
\mathcal{M}
and it is separated from
0
.

By Theorem
1.3
, there is the smallest number
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{M}^{N-1}(T)\cap\textbf{H}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, to each
0\leq k<N-1
, if
\mathcal{M}^{k}(T)
is a gap of
\mathcal{M}
,
then either
\mathcal{M}^{k}(T)\subset U
or
\mathcal{M}^{k}(T)\subset V
, and thus it follows from
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}
that
\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(T)
is also a gap with length
|\mathcal{M}^{k+1}(T)|=|\mathcal{M}^{k}(T)|
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{N-1}(T)
is a gap with
|\mathcal{M}^{N-1}(T)|=|T|=|\textbf{H}|
, which implies
\mathcal{M}^{N-1}(T)=\textbf{H}
.

We next claim that
\mathcal{M}^{k}(T)
,
0\leq k<N
, are pairwise disjoint. In fact, if there exist
0\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<N
such that
\mathcal{M}^{k_{1}}(T)\cap\mathcal{M}^{k_{2}}(T)\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{M}^{k_{1}}(T)=\mathcal{M}^{k_{2}}(T)

Let

and
{\mathcal{M}}(S^{\ast})=S^{\ast}
. So we have
{\mathcal{S}}=S^{\ast}
by the maximality of
\mathcal{S}

Case 2:
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\varepsilon,1)\neq\emptyset
for all
\varepsilon>0

{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\delta)=\emptyset
for some
\delta>0

Without loss of generality assume

and

\mathcal{M}
, and

H
.

By Theorem
1.3

N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.16)

Thus, by the same argument as in the
Case 1

{\mathcal{M}}^{k}(T_{\delta}),0\leq k\leq N-1

[0,\delta)

4.1
(i) implies that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset
for all small
\varepsilon>0
. This, together with (
4.16

{\mathcal{M}}^{N-1}(T_{\delta})=[x_{1}-\delta,x_{2}),

and hence
{\mathcal{M}}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))
is a gap with length
\delta
.

By Theorem
1.3
again, there is the smallest number
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}^{K}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))
intersects
\bigcup_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathcal{M}}^{k}(T_{\delta})\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{K-1}{\mathcal{M}}^{j}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))\cup[0,\delta)
,
then
{\mathcal{M}}^{j}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))
,
1\leq j\leq K

\delta
, and

{\mathcal{M}}^{K}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))=[0,\delta)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
be the complementary set of
\bigcup_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathcal{M}}^{k}(T_{\delta})\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{K-1}{\mathcal{M}}^{j}([x_{1}-\delta,x_{1}))\cup[0,\delta)

{\mathcal{S}}\subset S^{\ast}
and
{\mathcal{M}}(S^{\ast})=S^{\ast}
, (ii) follows.

Case 3:
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[0,\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\delta,1)=\emptyset
for some
\delta>0
.

As in
Case 2
, we may assume
\delta<1-x_{2}
is the largest number such that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap[1-\delta,1)=\emptyset
. One can check that
\delta<\alpha

and

H

.

\square

\mathcal{M}

(i). The definition of

j\in\mathbb{Z}
by the argument above. Hence
\mathcal{G}(H_{c};a)
is a Gabor frame by Corollary
3.3
.
We have proved (iii).

(iv). If
1-\frac{1}{2q}<c<1
, then
\mathcal{G}(H_{c};a)
is not a Gabor frame by Proposition
7.2
(3) and the observation that
\mathcal{G}(H;a)
is not a Gabor frame by (i).

Now assume
c\leq 1-\frac{1}{2q}
. By the same argument in (iii), we only need to find an integer
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q_{j_{0}}=0
when (
7.5
) is valid for some
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}
.
Let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer such that
x-n_{0}a\in[-\frac{1}{2q},\frac{1}{2q})+\mathbb{Z}
, and
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the number such that
x+j_{0}-n_{0}a\in[-\frac{1}{2q},\frac{1}{2q})
. Then
j_{0}
is the only integer satisfying
x+j_{0}-n_{0}a\in[-c,c)
, and
q_{j_{0}}=0
by (
7.5
).
This proves (iv), and the proof of Theorem
2.1
is completed.

\square

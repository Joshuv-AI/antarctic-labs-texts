A fusion category

\mathcal{C}

2.1
Fusion categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category, the cardinality of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is called rank of
\mathcal{C}
, and denoted by
\text{rank}(\mathcal{C})
. Let
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
be the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
. Then there is a unique ring homomorphism FPdim(-) from the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathds{k}
such that
\text{FPdim}(X)\geq 1
is an algebraic integer for all objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})

[
11
, Theorem 8.6]
, and
\text{FPdim}(X)
is called the Frobenius-Perron dimension of object
X
. The Frobenius-Perron dimension
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
of fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

A simple object
X
of fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is invertible if
\text{FPdim}(X)=1
. Fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a pointed fusion category, if simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
are all invertible. In this case,
\mathcal{C}\cong\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
, the category of finite-dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces of
\mathds{k}
, where
G=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is a finite group induced by fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}
and
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
is a
3
-cocycle. In the following, we use
G(\mathcal{C})
to denote the group of the isomorphism classes of invertible objects of fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is weakly integral if
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})\in\mathbb{Z}
;
\mathcal{C}
is integral if
\text{FPdim}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\forall X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
be the adjoint fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
is generated by simple objects
Y
such that
Y\subseteq X\otimes X^{*}
for some simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is weakly integral then
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
is integral
[
11
, Proposition 8.27]
.

Let
G
be a finite group, for a
G
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, we use the following notation to denote the Frobenius-Perron dimension of component
\mathcal{C}_{g}

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{g}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

If the
G
-grading of
\mathcal{C}
is faithful, that is, for any
g\in G
, the component
\mathcal{C}_{g}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=|G|\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{g})

by
[
11
, Proposition 8.20]
. Any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
has a faithful grading with
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
being its trivial component, the grading is called the universal grading of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, for any faithful
G
-grading of
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{e}

[
14
, Corollary 3.7]
.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be nilpotent if there exists a natural number
n
such that
\mathcal{C}^{(n)}=\text{Vec}

[
14
]
, where
Vec
is the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathds{k}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(0)}:=\mathcal{C},~\mathcal{C}^{(1)}:=\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}},~\mathcal{C}^{(j)}:=(\mathcal{C}^{(j-1)})_{\text{ad}},~j\geq 1.

Obviously, pointed fusion categories are nilpotent, and fusion categories of prime power Frobenius-Perron dimensions are also nilpotent
[
11
, Theorem 8.28]
, for example. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is weakly group-theoretical if it is Morita equivalent to a nilpotent
fusion category
\mathcal{D}
;
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical if it is Morita equivalent to a pointed fusion category, see
[
13
]
for details.

2.2
Braided fusion categories

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category with a braiding

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\overset{\sim}{\to}Y\otimes X,~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{C}.

Two objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
are said to centralize each other, if

\displaystyle c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\text{id}_{X\otimes Y}.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be a fusion subcategory. Then the centralizer of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
is the fusion subcategory generated by objects of
\mathcal{C}
that centralize every object of
\mathcal{D}
.
We call
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
the Müger center of
\mathcal{C}

[
18
]
. Moreover, braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a pre-modular fusion category if
\mathcal{C}
is a spherical fusion category
[
10
]
.

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{E}
is symmetric if
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. For any symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{E}
, there exists a finite group
G
and a central element
u\in G
such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\text{Rep}(G,u)

[
5
]
, where
\text{Rep}(G,u)
is the category of finite-dimensional representation of
G
,
u^{2}=e
and
u
acts as parity automorphism for any
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E})
. A symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{E}
is a Tannakian fusion category if
\mathcal{E}\cong\text{Rep}(G)
, the braiding of
\text{Rep}(G)
is given by interchange of vector spaces. A Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is maximal, if
\mathcal{E}
is not contained in any other Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
if there exists a braided tensor functor
F:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\mathcal{E}
is mapped faithfully to
\mathcal{C}
via functor
\text{Forg}\circ F
, where
\text{Forg}:\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is the forgetful functor
[
9
, Definition 4.16]
. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a braided fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
, if
\mathcal{C}
is braided and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}
is a non-degenerate fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}
.

Given a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be a non-trivial Tannakian subcategory, then there exists a
G
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{G}
, which is called the de-equivariantization of
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathcal{E}
, such that
\mathcal{C}\cong(\mathcal{C}_{G})^{G}
as braided fusion categories. In general,
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is a braided
G
-crossed fusion category, but the trivial component
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\cong(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}})_{G}
is a braided fusion category, where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is the centralizer of
\mathcal{E}
in
\mathcal{C}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is a braided fusion category, and
(\mathcal{C}_{G})^{\prime}\cong(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})_{G}
, see
[
9
, section 4]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category. Then
\mathcal{C}
is said to be non-degenerate if its Müger center
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{Vec}
. A pre-modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a modular fusion category if
\mathcal{C}
is non-degenerate.
It is known that pointed non-degenerate fusion categories are in bijective correspondence with metric groups
(G,q)
, where
G
is an abelian group,
q:G\to\mathds{k}^{*}
is a non-degenerate quadratic form, see
[
9
,
10
]
for details. We use
\mathcal{C}(G,q)
to denote the pointed non-degenerate fusion category determined by the metric group
(G,q)
below.

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is slightly degenerate if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{sVec}
, where
sVec
is the symmetric category of finite-dimensional super vector spaces over
\mathds{k}
. We say a slightly degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
splits, if there exists a braided equivalence
\mathcal{C}\cong\text{sVec}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a non-degenerate fusion category. Slightly degenerate pointed fusion categories split
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
, for example.
\mathcal{C}
is a super-modular fusion category if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{sVec}
and
\mathcal{C}
is spherical. In particular, for any slightly degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, if
\mathcal{C}
contains a Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is also slightly degenerate
[
9
, Propositon 4.30]
.

2.3
Witt equivalence and Witt group

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category,
(A,m)\in\mathcal{C}
a commutative algebra, where
m:A\otimes A\to A
is the multiplication such that
m=m\circ c
.
A
is a connected étale algebra if
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I,A)\cong\mathds{k}
and the category of right
A
-modules
\mathcal{C}_{A}
in
\mathcal{C}
is a semisimple category
[
3
, Proposition 2.7, Definition 3.1]
. In addition, we say two étale algebras
A,B\in\mathcal{C}
are centralizing each other if
c_{B,A}\circ c_{A,B}=\text{id}_{A\otimes B}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a symmetric fusion category. By
[
3
, Proposition 3.22]
, the symmetric category
\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}
contains a connected étale algebra
A
such that
(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E})_{A}\cong\mathcal{E}
as braided fusion category. In fact, the étale algebra

\displaystyle A=\mathcal{I}(I):=\oplus_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E})}X\boxtimes X^{*},

where
\mathcal{I}
is the right adjoint functor of the surjective braided tensor functor

\displaystyle\otimes:\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},~X\boxtimes Y\mapsto X\otimes Y,~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{E}.

Then for any braided fusion categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D}:=(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})_{A},

is also a braided fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
, where
(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})_{A}
is the category of right
A
-modules of
A
in
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
.

Recall that two non-degenerate fusion categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{E}
are Witt equivalent if and only if there exists a fusion category
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathcal{E}
such that there is a braided equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E}),

where
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E})
is the centralizer of
\mathcal{E}
in Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}:=\mathcal{D}
as fusion category, the braiding

c^{\text{rev}}
of
\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}
is given by

\displaystyle c^{\text{rev}}_{X,Y}:=c_{Y,X}^{-1},~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}.

The Witt equivalence class of
\mathcal{C}
will be denoted by
[\mathcal{C}]
, and the Witt equivalence classes of non-degenerate fusion categories over
\mathcal{E}
form the Witt group
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
, we denote
\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{W}(\text{Vec})
and
s\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{W}(\text{sVec})
, see
[
3
,
4
]
for detail.

Given a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{E}
, M. Müger conjectured that there is a non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
as braided fusion subcategory and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{E}

[
18
, Conjecture 5.2]
, and
\mathcal{A}
is called a minimal extension of
\mathcal{C}
. Obviously, if a slightly degenerate category
\mathcal{C}
splits, then it has a minimal extension. This fails for some non-trivial Tannakian fusion category
\mathcal{E}
by V. Drinfeld
[
7
]
, however.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a super-modular fusion category, assume that
\mathcal{C}
admits a minimal modular extension
\mathcal{A}
, i.e., the minimal extension
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a modular fusion category, then it was showed in
[

,
17
]
that
\mathcal{A}
has exactly 16 modular equivalence classes, and these minimal modular extensions are also Witt inequivalent
[
4
]

On the minimal extension conjecture

In this section, we study the minimal extensions for braided fusion categories, and we show that every slightly degenerate weakly group-theoretical fusion category admits a minimal extension.

To begin with, we need the following lemma, which is a direct result of

[
3

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{D}
a braided fusion category. Then we have a braided equivalence
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\cong\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

Notice that we have an injective braided tensor functor

\displaystyle\iota:\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}\boxtimes(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}),~X\boxtimes Y\mapsto X\boxtimes I\boxtimes Y,

and a surjective braided tensor functor
F:\mathcal{C}\boxtimes(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})
. Then the composition
F\circ\iota:\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})
is a braided tensor functor. By definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})=(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})_{A}

where
A=\mathcal{I}(I)=\oplus_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E})}X\boxtimes X^{*}\boxtimes I
, and
\mathcal{I}
is the right adjoint functor of tensor functor
\otimes:\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
. Since
(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})\cap A\cong I
,
F\circ\iota
is injective by
[
17
, Proposition 3.4]
. Hence,

is a braided equivalence by
[

, as we have the following equations

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}))=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}),

this finishes the proof of the lemma.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}

Let
\mathcal{C}

Proof.

, it suffices to show that

On the one hand, if

or

4
Structure of certain braided weakly group-theoretical fusion categories

In this section, we study the structure of non-degenerate and slightly degenerate weakly group-theoretical fusion categories of particular Frobenius-Perron dimensions.

Let
G
be a finite group. Given a faithful
G
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, assume
\mathcal{C}_{e}\cong\text{Vec}_{N}^{\omega}
, where
N
is a finite group and
\omega\in Z^{3}(N,\mathds{k}^{*})
is a
3
-cocycle. By definition, the
G
-grading structure of
\mathcal{C}
induces an action of group
N
on sets
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
for all
g\in G
.

The following lemma is trivial, we include it for the reader’s interest.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
be a faithful
G
-graded fusion category, and
\mathcal{C}_{e}\cong\text{Vec}_{N}^{\omega}
is pointed, where
G,N
are finite groups and
\omega\in Z^{3}(N,\mathds{k}^{*})
is a
3
-cocycle. Then for any element
g\in G
, N acts transitively on
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
.

Proof.

By definition, for any
g\in G
, let
X\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
be an arbitrary simple object, there exists a subgroup
H
of
N
such that
X\otimes X^{*}=\oplus_{h\in H}h
, then
\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}=|H|
. Note that the orbit of
X
has a size of
[N:H]
, by
[
11
, Proposition 8.20]

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{g})=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{e})=|N|=[N:H]\text{FPdim}(X)^{2},

which then shows the orbit of
X
is exactly
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
.
∎

Now we are ready to give our first classification theorem of non-degenerate fusion categories.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let d be a square-free positive integer. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is an integral non-degenerate weakly group-theoretical fusion category such that
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=nd
and
(n,d)=1
. Then
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is not pointed, otherwise the result is trivial. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a weakly group-theoretical integral fusion category,
\mathcal{C}
contains a non-trivial Tannakian subcategory by
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
. Let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is a
G
-crossed braided fusion category whose trivial component is braided equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
; moreover, the
G
-grading of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is faithful and
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a non-degenerate fusion category by
[
9
, Proposition 4.56]
. Meanwhile,
[
9
, Corollary 5.19]
says
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is weakly anisotropic. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a pointed fusion category
by
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
. So
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
contains a pointed non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
, since
|G|^{2}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{C}_{G}
and
\mathcal{B}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{B}_{g}
be the
G
-grading of
\mathcal{B}
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{e}
by universal property of
\mathcal{B}_{\text{ad}}

[
14
, Corollary 3.7]
, so
\mathcal{B}
is nilpotent. Hence, for any
g\in G
and any object

, we have that
\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{B}_{e})

[
14
, Corollary 5.3]
, so
(\text{FPdim}(X),d)=1
. By Lemma
4.1

\text{rank}(\mathcal{B}_{g})=\frac{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{B}_{e})}{\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}}
, in particular
d|\text{rank}(\mathcal{B}_{g})
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{e}=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a non-degenerate fusion category, by
[
16
, Lemma 10.7]

\text{rank}(\mathcal{B}_{g})
is equal to the cardinality of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}^{0}_{G})^{g}
, the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{G}
that are fixed by
g
. Note that
g
acts as a braided tensor equivalence on
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
, thus
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0})^{g}
is an abelian group and contains
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
as a subgroup, which then implies that
G
acts trivially on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
. For
(d,|G|)=1
and
H^{i}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},G)=0
for all non-negative integer
i
, fusion category
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0})^{G}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
contains a fusion subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)^{G}\cong\text{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
.

Therefore,
\mathcal{C}
contains a non-degenerate fusion subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
.
By
[
9
, Theorem 3.13]
we have a braided tensor equivalence
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the centralizer of
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
, and
\mathcal{D}
is also a non-degenerate fusion category.
∎

Next we will generalize Theorem
4.2
to strictly weakly integral non-degenerate fusion categories. Assume that a braided weakly group-theoretical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is strictly weakly integral. However, if
\mathcal{C}
is weakly anisotropic, it was shown in

Let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a Tannakian fusion category. Assume that

\displaystyle B:=\mathcal{I}(I)=\oplus_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E})}X\boxtimes X^{*}

is the connected étale algebra such that
(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E})_{B}\cong\mathcal{E}
as braided fusion categories
[

.
Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
be braided fusion categories, which contain
\mathcal{E}
as a fusion subcategory. Since
\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{B}

[

Given two

and
\mathcal{D}_{G}

Proposition 4.3

Let

be two connected étale algebras in a non-degenerate fusion category

. If

For any braided fusion category

Theorem 4.5

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate fusion category with
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=nd
, where

is a square-free integer such that

. Assume that
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}

\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{A}

[

\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)

\mathcal{A}

(|G|,d)=1

Consider the natural action of

G
on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
and
\mathcal{A}
give rise to
G-
crossed extensions
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
of these categories,
i.e. the corresponding obstructions (see
[
12
, Theorem 7.12]
) ] in
H^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
vanish.

(2) The action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
is trivial, so
the action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to the product of trivial action
on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
and the action above on
\mathcal{A}
.

Assume that (1) and (2) are proved. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G}
be the equivariantization of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
; this
is a non-degenerate braided fusion category, see
[
9
, Proposition 4.56]
. The category

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G}
is also a non-degenerate braided fusion category containing
Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
in
{\bf 1}\boxtimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G}\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G}
;
by (2) above we have a
G-
equivariant braided equivalence
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
. Thus by Lemma

4.4
we will have

\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{D}\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})\cong(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G})\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{G}\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})),

which immediately implies Theorem
4.5
. Thus it remains to prove (1) and (2).

Let us prove (1). For the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
the obstruction is annihilated by multiplication
d^{4}
by
[
12
, Theorem 8.16]
. On the other hand, any element of
H^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
is annihilated by
multiplication by
|G|
. Since
(|G|,d)=1
, we get that the obstruction is zero.

Let us construct
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Pick a
G-
crossed extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}
and
let
\mathcal{B}
be the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}^{G}
with reversed braiding. Then
\mathcal{B}
is non-degenerate,
it contains
\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{G}^{0}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q^{-1})
where
q^{-1}
is the inverse quadratic form of
q
on
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, that is,
q^{-1}(v):=q(v)^{-1}
, for all
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
. Now consider the product
of
G-
crossed categories
\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G}
(the category
\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes\mathcal{B}_{G}
is naturally
graded by
G\times G
and
\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G}
is the part supported on the diagonal subgroup

\Delta(G)\subset G\times G
). Clearly

(\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0}=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{B}_{G}^{0}=\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q^{-1})=\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d},q\oplus q^{-1})\boxtimes\mathcal{A}.

Note that the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d},q\oplus q^{-1})
labelled by the diagonal subgroup

\Delta(\mathbb{Z}_{d})\subset\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d}
generate a Tannakian subcategory
{\mathcal{H}}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d},q\oplus q^{-1})
, which is invariant under
G-
action. Then
G-
equivariantization
{\mathcal{H}}^{G}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Tannakian category
equivalent to
\text{Rep}(\widetilde{G})
for some group
\widetilde{G}
which fits into exact sequence

1\to\mathbb{Z}_{d}\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1
. Since
(|G|,d)=1
this sequence splits, and
\widetilde{G}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}\rtimes G
. Thus
{\mathcal{H}}^{G}=\text{Rep}(\widetilde{G})
contains a connected étale algebra
A=\text{Fun}(\widetilde{G}/G)
(
\mathds{k}-
valued functions on finite set
\widetilde{G}/G
). In other words, the direct sum of all simple
objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d},q\oplus q^{-1})
labelled by the diagonal subgroup

\Delta(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
admits a structure of
G-
equivariant connected étale algebra.

Now we would like to define
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})_{A}^{0}
. However, this requires a generalization
of the construction of dyslectic modules to the setting of
G-
crossed categories which we could not find in existing
literature. Thus we consider the algebra
A
as an object of non-degenerate braided fusion category
(\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G}\supset((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0})^{G}
. Finally we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}:=(((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G})_{A}^{0})_{G}

(note that
((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G})_{A}^{0}
contains
({\mathcal{H}}^{G})_{A}^{0}=({\mathcal{H}}^{G})_{A}=\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
, so de-equivariantization by
G
makes sense). Let
B=\text{Fun}(G)\in\mathcal{E}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{G}
be the regular
algebra of
G
, see
[
3
, Example 2.8]
. Then the algebras
A
and
B
satisfy the assumptions
of Proposition
4.3
and we get a braided equivalence

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{0}=(((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G})_{A}^{0})_{G}^{0}=(((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G})_{G}^{0})_{A}^{0}=((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0})_{A}^{0}=\mathcal{A}

(we use that operations
(?)_{G}^{0}
and
(?)_{B}^{0}
are identified, see
[
3
, Example 3.14]
).
Moreover it is clear that the canonical
G-
action on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{0}=\mathcal{A}
coincides with the action defined
in the beginning of the proof (since we can describe the identification
\mathcal{A}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{0}
as follows:
start with an obvious embedding
\mathcal{A}\subset(\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G}
and
promote it to
\mathcal{A}^{G}\subset(\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{G}\to((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0})_{A}
; since
\mathcal{A}

and
A
centralize each other, the image is contained in
((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0})_{A}^{0}
, so we get
a
G-
equivariant braided tensor functor
\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}^{G})_{G}\to(((\mathcal{C}_{G}\boxtimes_{G}\mathcal{B}_{G})^{0})_{A}^{0})_{G}
).
Thus the action of
G
on
\mathcal{A}
can be upgraded to
G-
crossed extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
and (1) is proved.

Let us prove (2), i.e. that
G
acts on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
trivially. If not, then by
[
16
, Lemma 10.7]
, there exists a non-trivial component of the
G
-crossed grading of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}
which has rank less than
d
. Since
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
is the trivial component of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}
, and the
G
-grading of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}
is faithful, there exists non-invertible simple objects of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}
with Frobenius-Perron dimension
\sqrt{t}
by
[
14
, Corollary 5.3]
, where
t>1
is an integer and
t|d
. Then the
G-
crossed category

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)}\boxtimes_{G}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
contains a simple object
W
with

\text{FPdim}(W)^{2}=st
for some integer
s
. By Lemma
4.4
the same is true for the category
\mathcal{C}_{G}
;
hence the category
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{G})^{G}
contains a simple object
V
such that
\text{FPdim}(V)^{2}
is divisible by
st
.
This contradicts the assumption
(\text{FPdim}(V)^{2},d)=1
for all simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
and (2) is proved.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In Theorem
4.5
, the condition that
(\text{FPdim}(V)^{2},d)=1
for all simple objects
V\in\mathcal{C}
can not be dropped. For example, let
d>1
be a square-free odd integer, there exists a non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
of Frobenius-Perron dimension
4d
, which is braided equivalent to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariantization of a Tambara-Yamagami fusion category
\mathcal{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},\tau,\mu)
. It was proved that
\mathcal{C}
contain a simple object of Frobenius-Perron dimension
\sqrt{d}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}}=\text{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, see
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
for details. Obviously,
\mathcal{C}\ncong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a weakly group-theoretical non-degenerate fusion category, and
d
be a square-free integer. Assume that
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=nd
and
(n,d)=1
. If
(\text{FPdim}(X)^{2},d)=1
for all
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{C}
is not pointed, otherwise the result is trivial. If
\mathcal{C}
is a weakly anisotropic fusion category, it follows from
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
that
\mathcal{C}
is braided equivalent to a Deligne tensor product of an Ising category and a pointed fusion category. If not, let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. By
[
9
, Corollary 5.19]

\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a weakly anisotropic fusion category, as
|G|^{2}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
, thus
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
says that
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.5
, hence
\mathcal{C}
contains a non-degenerate fusion subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
.
∎

Moreover, by using Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
4.5
, we have the following direct result.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
n
be an integer,
d
an odd square-free integer such that
(n,d)=1
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a slightly degenerate weakly group-theoretical fusion category with
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=nd
. If there exists a minimal extension
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
(\text{FPdim}(V)^{2},d)=1
for all
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category.

Recall that a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
of Frobenius-Perron dimension
p^{a}r^{b}d
is weakly group-theoretical
[
21
, Proposition 3.14]
, where
p,r
are primes,
a,b
are non-negative integers,
d
is a square-free integer such that
(pr,d)=1
; for non-degenerate fusion categories, this conclusion was first proved in
[
19
, Corollary 5.4]
. Therefore, if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\text{sVec}
and
\mathcal{C}
is integral, then for all simple objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
,
\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
by
[
13
, Theorem 2.11]
and
[
21
, Corollary 3.4]
, hence
(\text{FPdim}(X),d)=1
. Thus, Theorem
4.2
shows that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{m},q)\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a braided fusion category with Müger center
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\text{sVec}
, and
m=d
or
\frac{d}{2}
.

In particular, we have the following corollary, which generalizes the conclusions of
[
6
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
21
, Proposition 3.8, Corollary 3.12]
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an integral braided fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension
p^{n}d
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\text{sVec}
, where
p
is a prime,
n
is a nonnegative integer and
d
is a square-free integer such that
(p,d)=1
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent and group-theoretical.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{Vec}
, then
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
as braided fusion category by Theorem
4.5
, and
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D})=p^{n}
. If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{sVec}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent by
[
21
, Corollary 3.12]
when
p
is odd; if
p=2
, then previous argument shows that
\mathcal{C}
contains
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
as a fusion subcategory, consequently
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{d},q)
, where Müger center
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\text{sVec}
and
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D})=2^{n}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is braided equivalent to a Deligne tensor product fusion subcategories of prime power Frobenius-Perron dimensions, it is nilpotent by
[
11
, Theorem 8.28]
, and
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical
[
8
, Corollary 6.8]
.
∎

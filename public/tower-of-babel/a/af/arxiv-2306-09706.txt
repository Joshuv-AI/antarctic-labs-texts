Krasovskii Passivity for Sampled-data Stabilization and Output Consensus
Yu Kawano, Alessio Moreschini, Michele Cucuzzella

Abstract
In this paper, we establish the novel concept of Krasovskii passivity for sampled discrete-time nonlinear systems, enabling Krasovskii-passivity-based control design under sampling. We consider two separate control objectives: stabilization and output consensus, where the latter is studied under the presence of an unknown constant disturbance. Inspired by methodologies in the continuous-time case, we develop sampled-data control schemes for each control objective based on Krasovskii passivity. The proposed sampled discrete-time controllers are respectively validated through simulations on a DC microgrid of boost converters and a DC microgrid of buck converters whose continuous-time models and their implicit midpoint discretizations are Krasovskii passive in each time scale.

Passivity provides an input-output approach to analysis and control of nonlinear dynamical systems. As leveraged by books

Along this line of research, the concept of Krasovskii passivity

(also called

]

, to acieve for example current sharing in DC microgrids under unknown power consumption. In practice, these control methodologies for continuous-time systems are implemented after temporal discretization of the considered plant. Hence, we need additional performance analysis for the sampled closed-loop system.

Literature review

Temporal discretization of a passive system has been studied in the context of numerical integration. Notably, the standard discretization of continuous-time dynamics by means of zero-order hold and ideal sampler does not yield in general discrete-time dynamics satisfying the standard passivity inequality with respect to the same continuous-time output
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
. Accordingly, if a given passive system is expressed as a port-Hamiltonian system, the underlying geometric structure is generally destroyed
[
21
,
22
]
. To overcome this issue, geometric and symplectic integration schemes
[
23
]
are employed with the aim of preserving the invariants of motion, and thus ensuring faithful performances of the long-time behavior while retaining passivity at the sampling instants. Also in
[

22
,
25

[

26

27

for linear port-Hamiltonian systems (PHSs).

Contributions

To develop sampled-data control schemes based on Krasovskii passivity, we introduce the novel concept of Krasovskii passivity for sampled discrete-time nonlinear systems. Inspired by the continuous-time property, we define Krasovskii passivity by taking the temporal discretization of the time derivative of the actual input as the new input
involved in the passivity analysis. To get a better understanding of the proposed Krasovskii passivity concept, we also investigate its connection with relevant passivity concepts originally defined for continuous-time systems: incremental passivity and shifted passivity
[
7
,
10
,
9
]
. In particular, we show the following implications: incremental passivity
\implies
Krasovskii passivity
\implies
shifted passivity with respect to a suitable output function. The latter can be expected by a similar implication in the continuous-time case
[
11
]
. However, the first comes from discrete-time nature. In this paper, we study nonlinear systems while linear PHSs are used for illustrating our results.

Based on the proposed discrete-time Krasovskii passivity concept, we study two design problems: stabilization and output consensus. Understanding the continuous-time stabilizing controllers in terms of passivity, we first characterize structures of sampled stabilizing controllers and derive conditions for the closed-loop stability under detectability assumptions. Next, moving to systems under unknown constant disturbances, we present sampled-data control methodologies for output consensus by interpreting continuous-time output consensus controllers from the passivity perspective.

The contribution of this paper is summarized as follows:

•

We provide a novel notion of Krasovskii passivity for sampled discrete-time systems;

•

We establish the following implications for the relevant passivity concepts: incremental passivity
\implies
Krasovskii passivity
\implies
shifted passivity with respect to a suitable output function;

•

We develop sampled-data stabilizing control schemes for Krasovskii passive sampled discrete-time systems;

•

We develop sampled-data control methodologies for output consensus of Krasovskii passive sampled discrete-time systems under unknown constant disturbances.

II
Motivation and Review on Krasovskii passivity for continuous-time systems

In this section we first provide a motivating example to show the usefulness of Krasovskii passivity for control design and its potential for sampled-data control. Then, we recall the definition of Krasovskii passivity for continuous-time systems and the main control techniques based on it.

II-A

Motivation

Consider the average governing dynamic equations of a boost converter
[
28
]
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}L_{s}\dot{I}_{s}&{}=-(1-u)V+V^{*}_{s}\\[2.15277pt]
C\dot{V}&{}=(1-u)I_{s}-G^{*}_{l}V\end{array}\right.

with the states
I_{s}(t)
,
V(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
denoting the average current and voltage, respectively, and the input
u(t)\in[0,1]
denoting the duty ratio, where
L_{s}
,
C
,
V^{*}_{s}
, and
G^{*}_{l}
are positive constants. Note that this is a bilinear system.

First, we verify its standard passivity by selecting the total energy as a storage function,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle S(I_{s},V):=\frac{1}{2}(L_{s}I_{s}^{2}+CV^{2}).

(3)

Its time derivative along the system trajectory satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{S}(I_{s},V)=-G^{*}_{l}V^{2}+V^{*}_{s}I_{s}\leq V^{*}_{s}I_{s},

which implies passivity with respect to the current
I_{s}
and the constant voltage source
V^{*}_{s}
. However,
V^{*}_{s}
is not a control input. Thus, this passivity property is not directly helpful for control design.

Now, we consider replacing
(I_{s},V)
in (
3
) with its time derivative
(\dot{I}_{s},\dot{V})
.
Then, we employ the following storage function:

\displaystyle S_{K}(\dot{I}_{s},\dot{V}):=\frac{1}{2}(L_{s}\dot{I}_{s}^{2}+C\dot{V}^{2}),

(4)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{S}_{K}(\dot{I}_{s},\dot{V})\leq\dot{u}(\dot{I}_{s}V-I_{s}\dot{V}).

Based on this property, the integral-type controller

\displaystyle\dot{u}=K(u-u^{*})-(\dot{I}_{s}V-I_{s}\dot{V})

(5)

with
K>0
stabilizes the unique equilibrium
((I_{s}^{*},V^{*}),u^{*})
(see
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
for further details). This approach is later formalized by introducing the notion of Krasovskii passivity as a tool for passivity-based integral-type control design
[
11
]
.

In practice, continuous-time controllers are implemented after discretized approximation. However, their performance analyses are often neglected. In this paper, we are interested in studying this problem from the Krasovskii passivity perspective. In fact, it is reasonable to expect that a suitable discretization method will preserve the Krasovskii passivity of the boost converter (
II-A
). To confirm this, we calculate the implicit midpoint discretization of (
II-A
),
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}L_{s}\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k}&{}=-(1-u_{k})\sigma_{\delta}V_{k}+V^{*}_{s}\\[2.15277pt]
C\Delta_{\delta}V_{k}&{}=(1-u_{k})\sigma_{\delta}I_{s,k}-G^{*}_{l}\sigma_{\delta}V_{k},\end{array}\right.

with

\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k}:=\frac{I_{s,k+1}-I_{s,k}}{\delta},\quad\sigma_{\delta}I_{s,k}:=\frac{I_{s,k+1}+I_{s,k}}{2}

and
\Delta_{\delta}V_{k}
and
\sigma_{\delta}V_{k}
similarly defined, while
\delta>0
and the subscript
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
denote the sampling time and the time instant, respectively.
Based on the storage function (
4
) for Krasovskii passivity, we consider the following storage function:

\displaystyle S_{K}(\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k},\Delta_{\delta}V_{k}):=\frac{1}{2}(L_{s}(\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k})^{2}+C(\Delta_{\delta}V_{k})^{2}),

which satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}S_{K}(\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k},\Delta_{\delta}V_{k})\leq\Delta_{\delta}u_{k}(\Delta_{\delta}I_{s,k}\sigma_{\delta}V_{k}-\sigma_{\delta}I_{k}\Delta_{\delta}V_{s,k}).

The inequality above can be understood as Krasovskii passivity for the sampled boost converter (
II-A
).
Therefore, one can expect that it can be stabilized by the implicit midpoint discretization of the continuous-time stabilizing controller (
5
), which is further investigated in Section
VI-A
for a network of boost converters.

Motivated by this example, the objective of this paper is to develop Krasovskii passivity theory for sampled discrete-time systems. To make the paper self-contained, the rest of this section is dedicated to reviewing results on Krasovskii passivity in the continuous-time case
[
11
,
15
]
. We first show its definition and applications to integral-type control design for stabilization and output consensus.

II-B

Definition

Consider a continuous-time nonlinear system:

\displaystyle\dot{x}=f(x,u),

(8)

with state
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, input
u(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and class
C^{1}
mapping
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Krasovskii passivity is introduced under the existence of an equilibrium point.

Assumption II.1

For the system (
8
), the set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}:=\{(x^{*},u^{*})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:f(x^{*},u^{*})=0\}

is not empty.
\lhd

Krasovskii passivity is defined as passivity of the so-called extended system
[
29
]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\dot{x}&{}=f(x,u)\\[2.15277pt]
\dot{u}&{}=v\\[2.15277pt]
z&{}=\hat{q}(x,u):=q(x,u,\dot{x}),\end{array}\right.

where
q:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is continuous, and
\hat{q}(x,u)
is defined by substituting
\dot{x}=f(x,u)
into
q(x,u,\dot{x})
.
This yields a system with the extended state
(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, input
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and output
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Now, we are ready to state the definition of Krasovskii passivity.

Definition II.2

Under Assumption
II.1
, the system (
8
) is said to be
Krasovskii passive
at
(x^{*},u^{*})\in{\mathcal{E}}
if for its extended system (
II-B
), there exist a class
C^{1}
function
S_{K}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and a continuous function
W_{K}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that
S_{K}(x^{*},u^{*},0)=0
, and
\hat{S}_{K}(x,u):=S_{K}(x,u,\dot{x})
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{S}_{K}(x,u)}{\partial x}f(x,u)+\frac{\partial\hat{S}_{K}(x,u)}{\partial u}v\leq-W_{K}(x,u,\dot{x})+v^{\top}z

for all
(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Moreover, the system is said to be
strictly Krasovskii passive
if there exists a class
C^{1}
function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that

for
y:=h(x)
, and
W_{K}(x,u,\dot{x})=0

\dot{x}=0
for all
(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

\lhd

Strict Krasovskii passivity is stronger than Krasovskii passivity in two aspects. First, the Krasovskii passive output is the time derivative of
y
. Second,
W_{K}
is positive definite with respect to
\dot{x}
. In contrast, an equilibrium point is not necessarily to exist. Below, we respectively employ Krasovskii passivity and strict Krasovskii passivity for stabilizing control and output consensus control, where the existence of an equilibrium point is not required for output consensus.

II-C

Stabilizing Control

In this subsection, we recall results in
[
11

for Krasovskii-passivity-based stabilizing control, studied under the following detectability assumption for the extended system (

Definition II.3

Under Assumption
II.1

\displaystyle v(\cdot)=0\mbox{\hskip 10.22217ptand\hskip 10.22217pt}z(\cdot)=0

\lhd

Krasovskii passivity can be understood as passivity of the extended system (

\displaystyle K_{1}\dot{u}=-K_{2}(u-u^{*})-z,

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|u-u^{*}|_{K_{2}}^{2}=-|\dot{u}|_{K_{1}}^{2}-\dot{u}^{\top}z.

[

Consider the closed-loop system consisting of a Krasovskii passive system (

(a)

, then any trajectory starting from

\displaystyle\{(x,u)\in\Omega:\hat{z}=0,W_{K}=0\}

\displaystyle\ \ \,\hat{z}:=K_{2}(u-u^{*})+\hat{q}(x,u)

(b)

If the extended system (
II-B
) with the output
(\hat{z},W_{K})
is detectable at
(x^{*},u^{*})
, then
(x^{*},u^{*})
is asymptotically stable.

\lhd

For the controller (
12
), we only specify

, and information of

is not required. In general,

In the context of network control, strict Krasovskii passivity has been applied to output consensus control under external unknown constant disturbance

Consider the following system under unknown constant disturbance:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\dot{x}&{}=g(x,u,d)\\[2.15277pt]

For the forward Euler method, we have

\displaystyle f_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k})

\displaystyle=f(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}

\displaystyle=x_{k}.

For the implicit midpoint method, we have

\displaystyle f_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k})

\displaystyle=f(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k}),

(20a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k}+x_{k+1}}{2}.

(20b)

Both methods satisfy (
19
).

Next, we apply
\Delta_{\delta}
to the quadratic function
|x_{k}|^{2}/2
. It follows that

\displaystyle{\Delta_{\delta}\left(\frac{|x_{k}|^{2}}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|x_{k+1}|^{2}-|x_{k}|^{2}}{2\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x_{k+1}-x_{k})^{\top}}{\delta}\frac{(x_{k}+x_{k+1})}{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\delta}x_{k})^{\top}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}.

Similar calculations are later used for the analysis of linear PHSs.

III-B

Definition

In this paper, we investigate Krasovskii passivity for a sampled discrete-time system (
17
) by assuming that it has an equilibrium point in the discrete-time sense.

Assumption III.4

For the system (
17
), the set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}:=\{(x^{*},u^{*})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:x^{*}=f_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x^{*},u^{*})\}

is not empty.

\lhd

In the continuous-time case, Krasovskii passivity is defined as passivity of the extended system (
II-B
) by viewing
\dot{u}
as the new input. Mimicking this procedure, we introduce an extended system of the discrete-time system (
17
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\Delta_{\delta}x_{k}&{}=f_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k})\\[2.15277pt]
\Delta_{\delta}u_{k}&{}=v_{k}\\[2.15277pt]
z_{k}&{}=q_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k},\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}),\end{array}\right.

where
q_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a continuous function at each
\delta
.
This is a system with the extended state
(x_{k},u_{k})
, input
v_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and output
z_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Under Standing Assumption
III.2
, this extended system is well defined also.

Now, we are ready to introduce the concept of Krasovskii passivity for sampled discrete-time systems.

Definition III.5

Under Assumption
III.4
, the discrete-time system (
17
) is said to be
Krasovskii passive
at
(x^{*},u^{*})\in{\mathcal{E}}
if for its extended system (
III-B
), there exist continuous functions
S_{K},W_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that
S_{K}(x^{*},u^{*},0)=0
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}S_{K}(x_{k},u_{k},\Delta_{\delta}x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq-W_{K}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k},\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k})+v_{k}^{\top}z_{k}

(24a)

for all
(x_{k},u_{k})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
v_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Moreover, the system is said to be
strictly Krasovskii passive
if there exists a continuous function
h_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
z_{k}=\Delta_{\delta}y_{k}
for
y_{k}:=h_{\delta}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k})
, and

\displaystyle W_{K}(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},u_{k},\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k})=0\quad\implies\quad\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}=0

(24b)

for all
(x_{k},u_{k})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
The function
S_{K}
is referred to as a
storage function
.

\lhd

Similarly to the continuous-time case, strict Krasovskii passivity is stronger than Krasovskii passivity in two aspects. First, the Krasovskii passive output is the discretization of the time derivative of
y_{k}
. Second,
W_{K}
is positive definite with respect to
\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}
. In contrast, an equilibrium point is not necessarily to exist. As for the continuous-time case, we develop sampled stabilizing controllers based on Krasovskii passivity and sampled output consensus controllers based on strict Krasovskii passivity, where the latter control design does not require the existence of an equilibrium point.

Remark III.6

In sampled discretization considered in this paper,
u(\delta k)
is replaced with
u_{k}
by viewing
u(t)
as the control input. Accordingly,
\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}(u(\delta k+\tau)-u(\delta k))/\tau
is replaced with
\Delta_{\delta}u_{k}
. In contrast, a function of the state
x(\delta k)
(resp.
\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}(x(\delta k+\tau)-x(\delta k))/\tau
) is replaced with some function of
\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}
(resp.
\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}
). In the continuous-time case, the extended system (
II-B
) can be viewed as a system with the states
x
and
u
. Thus, one may consider another sampled discretization by replacing a function of
(x,u)
with some function of
(\sigma_{\delta}x_{k},\sigma_{\delta}u_{k})
. Our results can be extended to such a case; see the preliminary version for linear PHSs
[
27
, Section 3.3]
.
\lhd

A discrete-time PHS (

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}S_{K}(\Delta_{\delta}x_{k})

\displaystyle=-|H\Delta_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}x_{k}|_{R}^{2}+v_{k}^{\top}z_{k}.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced the concept of Krasovskii passivity for sampled discrete-time nonlinear systems, inspired by the counterpart concept to continuous-time systems. The proposed concept has been investigated by relating it with widely known passivity concepts: incremental passivity and shifted passivity. In particular, we have established the following implications: incremental passivity
\implies
Krasovskii passivity
\implies
shifted passivity with respect to a suitable output function. Then, we have developed sampled-data control frameworks for stabilization and output consensus based on Krasovskii passivity. Their effectiveness has been illustrated by a network of boost converters and an islanded DC microgrid, respectively. As exemplified by these power systems and linear PHSs, suitable temporal discretization preserves Krasovskii passivity,
i.e.
, suitable temporal discretizations of Krasovskii passive continuous-time systems are Krasovskii passive sampled discrete-time systems. Future work includes investigating if this is true for all Krasovskii passive continuous-time systems.

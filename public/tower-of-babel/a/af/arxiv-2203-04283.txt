Radiated Angular Momentum and Dissipative Effects in Classical Scattering
Aneesh V. Manohar, Alexander K. Ridgway, Chia-Hsien Shen

Abstract
We present a new formula for the angular momentum J μ ​ ν J^{\mu\nu} carried away by gravitational radiation in classical scattering. This formula, combined with the known expression for the radiated linear momentum P μ P^{\mu} , completes the set of radiated Poincare charges due to scattering. We parametrize P μ P^{\mu} and J μ ​ ν J^{\mu\nu} by non-perturbative form factors and derive exact relations using the Poincare algebra. There is a contribution to J μ ​ ν J^{\mu\nu} due to static (zero-frequency) modes, which can be derived from Weinberg’s soft theorem. Using tools from scattering amplitudes and effective field theory, we calculate the radiated J μ ​ ν J^{\mu\nu} due to the scattering of two spinless particles to third order in Newton’s constant G G , but to all orders in velocity. Our form-factor analysis elucidates a novel relation found by Bini, Damour, and Geralico between energy and angular momentum loss at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 3 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{3}) . Our new results have several nontrivial implications for binary scattering at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 4 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{4}) . We give a procedure to bootstrap an effective radiation reaction force from the loss of Poincare charges due to scattering.

Radiated Linear and Angular Momentum.

Consider a scattering process where the initial state consists of massive particles (referred to as matter), and the final state consists of matter and outgoing gravitational radiation.
Poincare symmetry implies that the loss of linear and angular momentum of matter is equal to that carried away by radiation. The radiated linear and angular momentum in the final state are given by

\displaystyle P^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{3}x\,T^{\mu 0},

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{3}x\,x^{[\mu}T^{\nu]0},\vskip-10.0pt

(1)

where
T^{\mu\nu}
is the stress-energy tensor of the radiation,
a^{[\mu}b^{\nu]}\equiv a^{\mu}b^{\nu}-a^{\nu}b^{\mu}
, and the integrals are over all space at a fixed time.
The global conserved charges are invariant under improvement terms in
T^{\mu\nu}

Callan et al. 1970
.

Gravitational radiation is defined as the fluctuation around flat space
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\sqrt{32\pi G}\,h_{\mu\nu}
. In what follows, we use the mostly-minus convention. Asymptotically, the radiation can be decomposed into on-shell plane waves labeled by
k^{\mu}=(\omega,\bm{k})
, where
\omega
is the energy, boldface
\bm{k}
denotes spatial momentum, and
k^{2}=0
.
After gauge fixing, the radiation field of any frequency can be solved in terms of the stress-energy pseudotensor
\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)
, which is analogous to the current in EM.

\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)
contains both matter and radiation contributions, unlike the usual stress-tensor which does not contain radiation.
\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)
is conserved on shell, i.e.
k_{\rho}\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)=0
. (One can always find such a
\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)

Kosmopoulos 2020
.) There is an invariance under residual gauge transformations
\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)+k^{\mu}\epsilon^{\nu}(k)+k^{\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}(k)
where
\epsilon(k)\cdot k=0
.
Crucially, the formula for the radiation field and
J^{\mu\nu}
are written in terms of
\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)
, rather than the transverse-traceless components of the radiation field used in the standard formula
Maggiore 2007
, which means our results are also applicable to the static mode that contributes to the angular momentum. This is similar to the Coulomb field in EM, which is not in the transverse projection of the vector potential.
The radiation field in terms of
\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)
reads

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{8\pi G}\int\widetilde{{\rm d}k}\left(P_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\mathcal{T}^{\rho\sigma}(k)\,e^{-ik\cdot x}+\textrm{c.c.}\right),

(2)

where
P_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the gauge-dependent projection and
\widetilde{{\rm d}k}=\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega}
is the Lorentz invariant phase space measure.

Using Einstein’s equations, it is straightforward to relate
T^{\mu\nu}
to the radiation field. Combining Eqs. (
1
), (
2

T^{\mu\nu}
in terms of

\displaystyle P^{\mu}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\int\widetilde{{\rm d}k}\,k^{\mu}\left(\mathcal{T}^{*\rho\sigma}(k)\mathcal{T}_{\rho\sigma}(k)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}^{*\rho}_{\rho}(k)\mathcal{T}^{\sigma}_{\sigma}(k)\right),

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\int\widetilde{{\rm d}k}\Big(\mathcal{T}^{*\rho\sigma}(k)\mathcal{L}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}_{\rho\sigma}(k)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}^{*\rho}_{\rho}(k)\mathcal{L}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}^{\sigma}_{\sigma}(k)

\displaystyle\;\qquad\qquad\qquad+2i\,\mathcal{T}^{*\rho[\mu}(k)\mathcal{T}^{\nu]}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\rho}(k)\Big),

(3)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\mu\nu}\equiv ik^{[\mu}\partial^{\nu]}
. Note the absence of any explicit time dependence. This completes the set of expressions for the radiated Poincare charges. Since the stress-energy pseudotensor can be derived directly from on-shell amplitudes using the KMOC framework
Kosower et al. 2019
;
Cristofoli et al. 2021

Analogous formulæ for
P^{\mu}
and
J^{\mu\nu}
in EM are given in Appendix
A

The expressions in Eq. (
3

J^{\mu\nu}
integrand is fixed by invariance under residual gauge transformations. These terms are sometimes referred to as the orbital and spin contributions. However, only their combination is gauge invariant, implying that individually, they have no physical meaning

\displaystyle P^{\mu}

\displaystyle\rightarrow P^{\mu},

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\rightarrow J^{\mu\nu}+a^{[\mu}P^{\nu]}.

(4)

Since
\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)e^{ik\cdot a}
under translations, the expressions in Eq. (
3

4

Radiation Reaction Force.

Dissipative effects on a binary system in a generic orbit
can be described by the RR force
{\bm{F}}_{RR}
.
Let the spinless binary motion lie on the
x-y
plane.
In polar coordinates,

\bm{F}_{RR}=F_{r}\bm{e}_{r}+\frac{F_{\phi}}{r}\bm{e}_{\phi}
,
where
\bm{e}_{r}
and
\bm{e}_{\phi}
are the radial and angular unit vectors,

r
is the relative distance, and
\phi
is the polar angle.
The energy
E
and angular momentum
J
of the binary are not conserved in the presence of
{\bm{F}}_{RR}
.
Using the formulation in
Bini and Damour 2012
;
Bini et al. 2021
, the fluxes of
E
and
J
are

\displaystyle\dot{J}

\displaystyle=F_{\phi},\quad\dot{E}=\dot{r}F_{r}+\dot{\phi}F_{\phi}.

(20)

One can boostrap the RR force using the loss of energy and angular momentum due to scattering in the CM frame.
Assuming
{\bm{F}}_{RR}
is a vector under spatial parity and odd under time reversal,

\displaystyle{\bm{F}}_{RR}

\displaystyle=c_{r}p_{r}\,\bm{e}_{r}+c_{p}\,\bm{p}=(c_{r}+c_{p})p_{r}\bm{e}_{r}+\frac{c_{p}J}{r}\bm{e}_{\phi}

(21)

where
\bm{p}
is the relative momentum,
p_{r}=\bm{p}\cdot\bm{e}_{r}
, and
c_{r}
and
c_{p}
are unknown coefficients that are even under time reversal.
We also assume that
c_{r}
and
c_{p}
can be expressed in isotropic gauge, i.e. they only depend on
r
and
\bm{p}^{2}
. Classical power counting yields the ansatze

\displaystyle c_{r}=\frac{G^{2}}{r^{3}}c_{r,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}\right)+\dots,\quad c_{p}=\frac{G^{2}}{r^{3}}c_{p,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}\right)+\dots,

(22)

where the dots denote higher orders in
G
.

Plugging Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
) into Eq. (
20
),
and integrating over the conservative trajectories in the CM frame, which to leading order are straight lines, yields the change in
E
and
J
after scattering

\displaystyle\Delta{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{2G^{2}}{b}\frac{E_{1}E_{2}}{E_{12}}\,c_{p,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right)+\dots,

(23)

\displaystyle\Delta{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{2G^{2}\mathsf{J}_{\textrm{CM}}}{3b^{3}}(c_{r,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right)+3c_{p,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right))+\dots\,,

Conservation of energy and angular momentum implies,

\displaystyle\Delta{J}

\displaystyle=-J^{12}_{\rm CM},\quad\Delta{E}=-P^{0}_{\rm CM}\,.

(24)

Matching this to Eq. (
12
) and
P^{0}_{\rm CM,2}=0
at
\mathcal{O}(G^{2})
fixes the ansatze entirely:

\displaystyle c_{r,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=-3c_{p,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right),

(25)

\displaystyle c_{p,2}\left(\bm{p}^{2}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\nu^{2}M^{4}}{E_{1}E_{2}}(2\sigma^{2}-1)\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma).

This extends the RR force at
\mathcal{O}(G^{2})
to all orders in velocity, which was only derived previously to the first three orders in the velocity expansion
Gopakumar et al. 1997
.
The equations of motion for the CM recoil,
and the extension to
\mathcal{O}(G^{3})
,
can be studied similarly.

This analysis, however, assumes that
{\bm{F}}_{RR}
can be expressed in the isotropic gauge.
It would be important to check the agreement between Eq. (
25
) with the known RR force in the overlapping region by including Schott terms
Schott 1915
, i.e. total time derivatives that leave
\Delta J
and
\Delta E
invariant
Saketh et al. 2022
.
We leave this to future work.

Conclusions.

In this Letter, we build a new framework to calculate the radiated angular momentum due to scattering that meshes well with QFT-based methods.
Our work opens up many avenues for future work. Some obvious generalizations include dissipative effects in scattering with spin
Jakobsen et al. 2022
;
Jakobsen and Mogull 2022
, and in gauge
Saketh et al. 2022
;
Bern et al. 2021c
and supersymmetric theories
Amati et al. 1990
;
Di Vecchia et al. 2020
.
It would also be interesting to compare our method with other approaches using soft theorems
Di Vecchia et al. 2021b
;
Heissenberg 2021
.
A crucial next step is to calculate
P^{\mu}
and
J^{\mu\nu}
to
\mathcal{O}(G^{4})
.
For the bounded binaries,
it would be interesting to compare with the flux from analytic continuation
Cho et al. 2021
, and study its impact on waveform models
Antonelli et al. 2019
.
Last but not least, it would be interesting to extend our framework beyond gravitational-wave science, perhaps along the lines of jet observables
Basham et al. 1978
.

Appendix A
Radiated Linear and Angular Momenta in Electromagnetism

In this section, we derive the analog of Eq. (3) of the main paper in electromagnetism (EM).
The EM stress-energy tensor associated with the gauge field
A_{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\mu}\,_{\rho}F^{\nu\rho}+\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}}{4}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma},

(26)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}A_{\nu]}
is the field strength.
Once a gauge is chosen,
A^{\mu}
can be expressed in terms of the source current using the classical Green’s function.
In momentum space, the gauge field is given by

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\widetilde{dk}\left(P_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal{J}^{\nu}(k)\,e^{-ik\cdot x}+\textrm{c.c.}\right),

(27)

where
\mathcal{J}^{\nu}(k)
is the conserved current satisfying
k_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}(k)=0
,
and
P_{\mu\nu}
is the gauge-dependent transverse projector.
In Lorentz gauge, one replaces
P_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{J}^{\nu}(k)
with
\mathcal{J}_{\mu}(k)
in Eq. (
27
).
Like the stress-energy pseudotensor in gravity, the EM source current exhibits a residual gauge freedom:
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}(k)\rightarrow\mathcal{J}^{\mu}(k)+\alpha k^{\mu}
.

Combining Eq. (1) of the main text and Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
) above, the EM expressions for
P^{\mu}
and
J^{\mu\nu}
are

\displaystyle P^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\int\widetilde{dk}\,k^{\mu}\left(-\mathcal{J}^{*\rho}(k)\mathcal{J}_{\rho}(k)\right),

(28)

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\int\widetilde{dk}\,\big(-\mathcal{J}^{*\rho}(k)\,\mathcal{L}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{J}_{\rho}(k)-i\mathcal{J}^{*[\mu}(k)\mathcal{J}^{\nu]}(k)\big).

Like in gravity, the explicit time dependence and gauge choice drop out and we are left with expressions that only depend on
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}(k)
.
This radiated angular momentum formula is valid for radiation with arbitrary frequency.
As a consistency check, the formulæ Eq. (
28
) are also invariant under the residual gauge transformation and obey the Poincare algebra.

Appendix E
Stress-energy Pseudotensor at
\mathcal{O}(G)

The full stress-energy pseudotensor at
\mathcal{O}(G)
sourced by binary scattering has been calculated using various methods,
including the worldline and the KMOC framework.
It can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)

\displaystyle=i\int\hat{d}\ell\ \hat{\delta}(2p_{1}\cdot l)\hat{\delta}(2p_{2}\cdot\ell)e^{il\cdot b_{1}-i\ell\cdot b_{2}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)\,,

(40)

where
\hat{d}\ell=\frac{d^{D}\ell}{(2\pi)^{D}}
and
l=k+\ell
.
The kernel
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)
is closely related to the tree-level amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{5}(\ell,k)
of
five-particle scattering depicted by the diagrams in the bottom row of Fig. 2 of the main text.

We use the KMOC formalism to extract
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)
.
First, we strip off the graviton polarization
e_{\mu\nu}
from
\mathcal{M}_{5}(\ell,k)

such that
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)
is a symmetric tensor and
reproduces the correct amplitude,

e_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)=\mathcal{M}_{5}(\ell,k)
.
Next, we impose that the Ward identity is satisfied even when one index is free,
i.e.
k_{\mu}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)=0
, to
ensure the conservation of the stress-energy pseudotensor,
k_{\mu}\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(k)=0
.
This is made possible by using the pure gauge freedom in
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)
.
Finally,
we isolate the classical contribution to
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)
by rescaling
(\ell,k)\rightarrow(\lambda\ell,\lambda k)
and expanding in small
\lambda
.
For this purpose, it is sufficient to truncate to
\mathcal{O}(1/\lambda^{2})
.
In contrast to the conservative case,
there is actually an iteration contribution if one keeps the Feynman
i\varepsilon
in the matter propagators.
As expected, this iteration is precisely canceled by the cut contribution in the KMOC approach.
Therefore, we can effectively ignore the Feynman
i\varepsilon
when taking the classical limit.
This procedure yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{5}(\ell,k)

\displaystyle=8\pi G\left(D^{\mu\nu}_{1}+D^{\mu\nu}_{2}+D^{\mu\nu}_{3}\right),

(41)

where

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}(2\sigma^{2}-1)}{(p_{1}\cdot k)^{2}\ell^{2}}\left(2(p_{1}\cdot k)p_{1}^{(\mu}\ell^{\nu)}-(\ell\cdot k)p_{1}^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{8m_{1}m_{2}\sigma}{(p_{1}\cdot k)\ell^{2}}\left(2(p_{1}\cdot k)p_{1}^{(\mu}p_{2}^{\nu)}-(p_{2}\cdot k)p_{1}^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}\right)\,,

(42)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}(2\sigma^{2}-1)}{(p_{2}\cdot k)^{2}l^{2}}\left((\ell\cdot k)p_{2}^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}-2(p_{2}\cdot k)p_{2}^{(\mu}\ell^{\nu)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{8m_{1}m_{2}\sigma}{(p_{2}\cdot k)l^{2}}\left(2(p_{2}\cdot k)p_{1}^{(\mu}p_{2}^{\nu)}-(p_{1}\cdot k)p_{2}^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}\right)\,,

(43)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{16m_{1}m_{2}\sigma}{\ell^{2}l^{2}}\left((p_{2}\cdot k)p_{1}^{(\mu}\ell^{\nu)}-(p_{1}\cdot k)p_{2}^{(\mu}\ell^{\nu)}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{8}{\ell^{2}l^{2}}\left((p_{1}\cdot k)^{2}\,p_{2}^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}+(p_{2}\cdot k)^{2}\,p_{1}^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{8p_{1}^{(\mu}p_{2}^{\nu)}}{\ell^{2}l^{2}}\left(2(p_{1}\cdot k)(p_{2}\cdot k)-m_{1}m_{2}\sigma\left(\ell^{2}+l^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}(2\sigma^{2}-1)}{\ell^{2}l^{2}}\ell^{\mu}\ell^{\nu}\,.

Rigidification of Connective Comodules
Maximilien Péroux

Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a commutative ring with global dimension zero. We show that we can rigidify homotopy coherent comodules in connective modules over the Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum of 𝕜 \Bbbk . That is, the ∞ \infty -category of homotopy coherent comodules is represented by a model category of strict comodules in non-negative chain complexes over 𝕜 \Bbbk . These comodules are over a coalgebra that is strictly coassociative and simply connected. The rigidification result allows us to derive the notion of cotensor product of comodules and endows the ∞ \infty -category of comodules with a symmetric monoidal structure via the two-sided cobar resolution.

1.
Introduction

Rigidifying multiplications

It has long been understood that one can derive the usual tensor product of modules over a commutative ring
R
to obtain a monoidal structure in the derived category of
R
-modules.
The two-sided bar resolution
B(M,R,N)
provides an explicit model for the derived tensor product of
R
-modules
M
and
N
. The homology of
B(M,R,N)
can be computed as
\mathsf{Tor}_{*}^{R}(M,N)
.
More generally, symmetric monoidal model categories provide a symmetric monoidal structure on their associated homotopy categories given by the derived tensor product, see
[
Hov99
, 4.3.2]
.
Thanks to the monoidal structure on the homotopy categories, one can consider algebraic structures such as rings, algebras or modules that are associative and unital up to homotopy. However, higher homotopy coherences capture more interesting data. For instance a grouplike
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebra in spaces is an
n
-fold loop space, see
[
May72
]
.

In this realization comes an obstacle: how does one define such an algebraic structure? One would need not only to specify a multiplication and unit, but also all the homotopy coherences.
Therefore there has been a growing interest to describe all the data of homotopy coherences.
The work of
[
EKMM97
,
HSS00
,
MMSS01
,
MM02
,
Shi04
]
shows that, in stable homotopy theory, we can conveniently choose a model category so that the homotopy coherence can be represented by a strictly associative, commutative and unital algebraic object.
In other words one can rigidify the multiplication so that specifying the data amounts only to define the multiplication and unit.
Higher categories expand on this approach: any presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category is the Dwyer-Kan localization of a combinatorial symmetric monoidal model category, see
[
NS17
]
. Moreover, given some assumptions on the combinatorial symmetric monoidal model category, homotopy coherent associative or commutative algebras can be rigidified and modeled by strictly associative and unital algebras. See for instance
[
Lur17
, 4.1.8.4, 4.3.3.17, 4.5.4.7]
or
[
Hin15
]
.

The rise of coalgebraic structures

Valuable geometric information can be stored by considering homotopy coherent coalgebraic structures.
For instance, it was shown in
[
KSV97
, 1.3]
that a
2
-connected space is equivalent to a suspension if and only if it is an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-cogroup in pointed spaces. Extending the results in
[
Man01
]
, the work of
[
Yua23
]
uses homotopy coherent coalgebras and Frobenius actions to show that the homotopy of a simply connected
p
-complete finite space is entirely determined by some
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebra on the chain complexes. Moreover,
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-ring spectra are enriched in
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebras
[
Pér22a
]
.

As in algebras, we need to construct homotopy coherent coalgebraic structures. In particular, we would want to rigidify homotopy coherent comultiplications and coactions.
However, we face multiple obstacles. Firstly, a common assumption for algebras and modules is that the monoidal product is closed and preserves colimits. So in order to dualize the results for coalgebras and comodules, we would need a monoidal product that preserves limits, which in practice is never the case.
Moreover, it is difficult to endow a model structure on the categories for coalgebras and comodules, see
[
BHK+15
,
HKRS17
,
GKR20
]
. In the cases where a model structure does exist, the author has shown that they may not model the correct
\infty
-category. For instance, strictly coassociative coalgebras in symmetric spectra cannot model
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebras in spectra, see
[
PS19
]
and
[
Pér22b
]
. This raises an issue as we would want to give a monoidal structure on homotopy coherent comodules. If one wanted to mimic the monoidal structure on modules given by the derived relative tensor product, one could be tempted to dualize the result of
[
Lur17
, 4.5.2.1]
but it would require the underlying tensor product to preserve totalizations. In the underived setting, the cotensor product of comodules only provides a monoidal structure if considered in a flat setting. Attempts to consider a derived cotensor product as a two-sided cobar construction was considered in
[
Tor20
, 4.3]
. In
[
FG12
]
, the authors forced the tensor product to commute with totalizations but the setting is limiting in practice.

Results

Our main result here shows that we can rigidify certain homotopy coherent coactions of comodules over a coalgebra. Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring of global dimension zero, i.e. a finite product of fields, and denote
H\Bbbk
its Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum. We consider
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category of
connective
modules over
H\Bbbk
. It is represented by the symmetric monoidal model category of non-negative chain complexes over
\Bbbk
, denoted
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, in which weak equivalences are quasi-isomorphisms. In other words, the Dwyer-Kan localization of
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
.
If
C
is a
simply connected
coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, then we show that the
\infty
-category of (homotopy coherent) comodules in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
over
C
is represented by a model category of (strictly coassociative) differential graded comodules over
C
in which weak equivalences are quasi-isomorphisms of
C
-comodules.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let
C
be a simply connected differential graded coalgebra over
\Bbbk
. Then right
C
-comodules in the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
of connective
H\Bbbk
-modules are quasi-isomorphic to strictly coassociative differential graded connective comodules over
C
.

One of the main application of the above result is that it allows us to define a symmetric monoidal structure on the
\infty
-category of
C
-comodules in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
. It is called the
derived cotensor product
and is given by a two-sided cobar resolution.
As recalled above, simply dualizing the results for modules seen in
[
Lur17
, 4.5.2.1]
is not possible, as it would require totalizations to commute with the monoidal product, which is
not
the case in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
.
Theorem
1.1
allows us to bypass this difficulty and use an explicit model for the derived cotensor product.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
C
be a simply connected cocommutative differential graded coalgebra over
\Bbbk
. The
\infty
-category
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))
of
C
-comodules in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
is endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure induced by
\widehat{\square}_{C}
, the derived cotensor of comodules which is given by the two-sided cobar resolution:
M\widehat{\square}_{C}N\simeq\Omega(M,C,N)
. Moreover:

•

Theorem
4.8
:
there is an Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence computing
H_{*}(X\widehat{\square}_{C}Y)
with an
E^{2}
-page given by
\mathsf{CoTor}_{H_{*}(C)}(H_{*}(X),H_{*}(Y))
;

•

Proposition
4.10
:
if
C
and
D
are quasi-isomorphic simply connected cocommutative differential graded coalgebras, then
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))
and
\mathcal{CoMod}_{D}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))
are equivalent as symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.

In
[
KP23
]
, the above results are applied to provide a bicategorical trace on coHochschild homology. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are crucial to define a bicategory on derived connective bicomodules in chain complexes.
Although (homotopy) colimits of modules are well understood, understanding (homotopy) limits of comodules is much more challenging. A key observation in our proof is that we can determine homotopy limits of certain explicit cosimplicial objects in comodules.

Simplicial comodules

The
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
of connective
H\Bbbk
-modules is also represented by the symmetric monoidal category of simplicial
\Bbbk
-modules
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
in which the weak equivalences are weak homotopy equivalences.
All arguments in this paper remain valid if we replace
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
by
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
. In particular Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
have a simplicial version. However
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
and
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
are not equivalent as monoidal categories, but they are after we derive their tensor products. We show in
[
Pér24
]
there is a Dold-Kan correspondence between simplicial comodules and differential graded connective comodules which induces an equivalence of their derived cotensor products.
Therefore the induced derived cotensor product of comodules in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
in Theorem
1.2
is independent from choosing between
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
or
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

A rigidification for connective bicomodules

For the sake of simplicity, we focus in this paper on right comodules over a coalgebra, but the above theorems remain true for left comodules. In fact, given
C
and
D
simply connected differential graded coalgebras, we can obtain a rigidification result as in Theorem
1.1
for
(C,D)
-bicomodules, as they are equivalent to left
(C\otimes D^{\mathsf{op}})
-comodules. In particular, we can drop the cocommutativity requirement of Theorem
1.2
and obtain a derived cotensor product for bicomodules over
C
which provides a monoidal (but not symmetric) structure. We provide further details in
[
KP23
]
.

On the global dimension zero condition

Throughout this paper, we work exclusively with
\Bbbk
a commutative ring of global dimension zero, i.e. a finite product of fields. There are several reasons this condition is imposed. First, it induces that every object is cofibrant and fibrant in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, and the projective and injective model structures are equal. In particular, the model structure on comodules is left-induced from a nice monoidal model category. In
[
HKRS17
]
, it was shown that the model structures for comodules are left-induced from injective model structures which are in general not monoidal model categories. This creates several issues to understand the induced homotopy theory on comodules. Moreover, as every module is flat, the tensor product preserves finite limits. This allows us to understand finite limits of comodules, which is essential for Theorem
1.1
. Finally, the cotensor product of comodules is
not
a comodule without the flat setting, hence Theorem
1.2
cannot be true. Notice though that in
[
Pér20
, 6.4.7]
, we proved an unbounded version of Theorem
1.1
, where
\Bbbk
is replaced by any commutative ring and
C
is a differential graded coalgebra that is perfect as a chain complex.

On the simply connected condition

A reader familiar with the notion of Koszul duality might not be surprised of the requirement of our coalgebra
C
to be simply connected, as there is an equivalence between comodules over
C
with certain modules over the cobar construction
\Omega(\Bbbk,C,\Bbbk)
, see
[
Pos11
]
. However our main results would not follow through this duality as the relation is not well established yet in the
\infty
-categorical setting, and even so, it is in general not a monoidal equivalence.

Outline

We introduce the model structure on the category of comodules in Section
2
, and then provide an explicit fibrant replacement given by the two-sided cobar resolution in Theorem
2.5
.
We prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
3
, and we apply this result to define the derived cotensor product of Theorem

in Section

.

(2)

(X\otimes Y)_{n}=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}X_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}Y_{j},

(3)

Given

Elements in the summands are denoted

, where

Let

2.
The two-sided cobar resolution

We begin by first introducing some elementary coalgebraic notions. By a
coalgebra

(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
we mean a chain complex
C
together with chain maps
\Delta\colon C\rightarrow C\otimes C
and
\varepsilon\colon C\rightarrow\Bbbk
such that it is coassociative, i.e.
(\mathsf{id}_{C}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta=(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C})\circ\Delta)
, and counital, i.e.
(\mathsf{id}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\mathsf{id}_{C}=(\varepsilon\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C})\circ\Delta
. It is
cocommutative
if in addition
\tau\circ\Delta=\Delta
where
\tau
is the symmetric isomorphism in the monoidal structure of
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. We say the coalgebra
C
is
simply connected
if
C_{0}=\Bbbk
and
C_{1}=0
.
We shall occasionally use the Sweedler notation for the comultiplication and write simply
c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
for
\Delta(c)=\sum_{i}{c_{(1)}}_{i}\otimes{c_{(2)}}_{i}\in(C\otimes C)_{n}
for any
c\in C_{n}
.

Given a coalgebra
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, a
right
C
-comodule

(M,\rho)
is a chain complex
M
together with a chain map
\rho\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes C
that is coassociative, i.e.
(\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\Delta)\circ\rho=(\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C})\circ\rho
, and counital, i.e.
(\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\rho=\mathsf{id}_{M}
.
A (right)
C
-colinear map
f\colon(M,\rho)\rightarrow(M^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is a chain map
f\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
such that
\rho^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C})\circ\rho
.
We denote by
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
the induced category of right
C
-comodules. Left
C
-comodules are defined completely analogously. If
C
is cocommutative, then left and right
C
-comodules are equivalent and we will simply refer to them as
C
-comodules. We shall occasionally use the Sweedler notation for the coaction and write simply
x_{(0)}\otimes x_{(1)}
for
\rho(x)=\sum_{i}{x_{(0)}}_{i}\otimes{x_{(1)}}_{i}\in(M\otimes C)_{n}
for any
x\in M_{n}
.

One can view
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
as the category of coalgebras over the comonad
-\otimes C\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. Thus by
[
AR94
, 2.78, 2.j]
, the category
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
is locally presentable.
Moreover, the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is left adjoint to the cofree functor
-\otimes C\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
. Therefore colimits in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
are computed in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. Since
C
is automatically flat in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, we get that the comonad
-\otimes C\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
preserves finite limits, and thus finite limits in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
are also determined in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, and the category
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
is abelian.
If
\mathsf{D}
is a small category (with infinitely many objects) and
F
is a
\mathsf{D}
-diagram in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
, we denote by
\mathsf{lim}^{C}_{\mathsf{D}}F
the limit in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
and by
\mathsf{lim}_{\mathsf{D}}F
the limit of
U\circ F
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Definition 2.4

\delta(x\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes y)=dx\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes y\pm x_{(0)}\otimes s^{-1}x_{(1)}|s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes y\pm x\otimes d_{\Omega}\Big(s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\Big)\otimes y

\pm x\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes dy\pm x\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}|s^{-1}y_{(1)}\otimes y_{(0)},

where

denotes either the differential on

or
N
, and

denotes the coaction of

applied to an element

, and
y\mapsto y_{(1)}\otimes y_{(0)}
denotes the coaction of
N\rightarrow C\otimes N
applied to an element

. The differential

is defined as:

d_{\Omega}(s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pm s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}(dc_{j})|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pm s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}{c_{j}}_{(1)}|s^{-1}{c_{j}}_{(2)}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}

When
N=C
, we can give a
C
-comodule structure on the chain complex
\Omega(M,C,C)
induced by the graded cofree structure
x\otimes c\mapsto x\otimes c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a right comodule over a simply connected coalgebra
C
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
The coaction
\rho\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes C
factors in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(M,C,C)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\rho}}

where
j
is a trivial cofibration and
\widehat{\rho}
is a fibration in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
specified by

and
\widehat{\rho}(x\otimes 1\otimes c)=x\otimes c
, while
\widehat{\rho}(x\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\dots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes c)=0
for all

.
Here

is a homogeneous element of
M
and
c
and
c_{i}
are homogeneous elements of
C
.
Additionally, we have a quasi-isomorphism
\Omega(M,C,C)\simeq\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}\Omega^{\bullet}(M,C,C)
.
Moreover, both
\Omega(M,C,C)
and
M\otimes C
are fibrant in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
, hence
\Omega(M,C,C)
is a fibrant replacement of

in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that
\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}(M,C,C)
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is quasi-isomorphic to the (direct-product) totalization of the double chain complex
\overline{\Omega}^{\bullet}(M,C,C)_{\bullet}
(see for instance
[
Bun12
, 4.23]
). It is elementary to check that the resulting quasi-isomorphism
M\rightarrow\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}(M,C,C)\simeq\Omega(M,C,C)
is precisely the map
j
, which is a monomorphism.
It is also an elementary proof to show that
j
and
\widehat{\rho}
are
C
-colinear maps.
We are only left to show that
\widehat{\rho}
is a fibration and that
\Omega(M,C,C)
is the homotopy limit in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
of
\Omega^{\bullet}(M,C,C)
.

We shall omit the signs from the Koszul rule for simplicity. Given a positive chain complex
(X,d)
, i.e.
X_{0}=0
, denote
s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(X)=(X\oplus s^{-1}X,D)
where
D(x)=d(x)+s^{-1}x
and
D(s^{-1}x)=-s^{-1}d(x)
, as in
[
Wei94
, 1.5.1]
.
Notice that if
N
is a right
C
-comodule, then so is
s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(N)
. Denote
e_{N}\colon s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(N)\rightarrow N
the projection, which is
C
-colinear.
Observe that if
f\colon M\rightarrow N
is a
C
-colinear map between right
C
-comodules, where
N_{0}=0
, then the pullback of
f
along
e_{N}
is the
C
-comodule
M\oplus s^{-1}N
with differential
D_{f}
where
D_{f}(x)=dx+s^{-1}f(x)
and
D_{f}(s^{-1}y)=-s^{-1}dy
.

We introduce now, for all
n\geq 0
the positive chain complex
B(n)=s^{-n}(M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes n+1})
.
As the quotient
e_{n}\colon s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(B(n))\rightarrow B(n)
is a positive epimorphism, it is a fibration in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
Therefore the induced cofree map
e_{n}^{C}\colon s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(B(n))\otimes C\cong s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(B(n)\otimes C)\rightarrow B(n)\otimes C
is a fibration in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
.
Define the cofree
C
-comodule
E(0)=M\otimes C
and define
f^{(1)}\colon E(0)\rightarrow B(0)\otimes C
as
\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\Delta
where we implicitly composed with the quotient
C\rightarrow\overline{C}
.
The map
f^{(1)}
is
C
-colinear as it is the sum of colinear maps. Let
E(1)
be the following pullback in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(B(0))\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B(0)\otimes C.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\rho}^{(1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e_{0}^{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{(1)}}

Explicitly
E(1)=(M\otimes C)\oplus s^{-1}(M\otimes\overline{C}\otimes C)
with differential denoted
d^{(1)}_{\Omega}
defined on
M\otimes C
as

d^{(1)}_{\Omega}=d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes d+s^{-1}(\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\Delta)

while on
M\otimes\overline{C}\otimes C
as

d^{(1)}_{\Omega}s^{-1}=-s^{-1}(d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}\otimes C}+\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}}\otimes d).

The resulting map
\widehat{\rho}^{(1)}\colon E(1)\rightarrow E(0)
is a fibration in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
.

By induction, suppose we have the
C
-comodule
E(n)=(M\otimes C)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}s^{-k}(M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes k}\otimes C)

for all
1\leq n<N
for some
N>1
, with differential

defined so that on the pieces

where
0\leq k<n
we have:

\displaystyle d^{(n)}_{\Omega}s^{-k}=(-1)^{k}s^{-k}\big(d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes k}\otimes C}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes i}}\otimes d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{k-i-1}\otimes C}+\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes k}}\otimes d\big)

\displaystyle+s^{-k-1}\big(\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes k}\otimes C}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes i}}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{k-i-1}\otimes C}+\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes k}}\otimes\Delta\big),

while on the piece
M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes n}\otimes C
:

\displaystyle d^{(n)}_{\Omega}s^{-n}=(-1)^{n}s^{-n}\big(d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes n}\otimes C}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes i}}\otimes d\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{n-i-1}\otimes C}+\mathsf{id}_{M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes n}}\otimes d\big).

Define
f^{(N)}\colon E(N-1)\rightarrow B(N-1)\otimes C
as trivial on all terms of the sum of

except on

for which is defined as:

f^{(N)}s^{-N+1}=s^{-N+1}(\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{N-1}}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes k}}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes N-k-2}}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}+\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{\overline{C}^{\otimes N-1}}\otimes\Delta)

for which we are again using implicitly the quotient
C\rightarrow\overline{C}
.
As before, we can check that
f^{(N)}
is a chain map that is
C
-colinear.
Let
E(N)
be the following pullback in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s^{-1}\mathsf{Cone}(B(N-1))\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(N-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B(N-1)\otimes C.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\rho}^{(N)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e_{N-1}^{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{(N)}}

As above, the
C
-comodule is explicitly given by
E(N)=(M\otimes C)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}s^{-k}(M\otimes\overline{C}^{\otimes k}\otimes C)
with differential

defined similarly as

.

We have therefore constructed a tower of fibrations in

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(n-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(0)=M\otimes C.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\rho}^{(n)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\rho}^{(1)}}

The composition of the tower
\mathsf{lim}_{n}^{C}E(n)\rightarrow M\otimes C
is a fibration in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
, and we argue now that it is precisely the map
\widehat{\rho}
.
For this we notice that
\mathsf{lim}_{n}^{C}E(n)\cong\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n)
even though it is an infinite limit.
Indeed, we can give a right
C
-comodule structure on
\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n)
as we have:

U(E(n))\cong(M\otimes C)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}M\otimes(s^{-1}\overline{C})^{\otimes k}\otimes C.

Notice that the maps in the tower induce

as
(M\otimes(s^{-1}\overline{C})^{\otimes n}\otimes C)_{i}=0
for all

since
(s^{-1}\overline{C})_{0}=0
as

is simply connected.
Therefore the coaction on
\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n)
is a sequence of maps
(\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n))_{i}\rightarrow((\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n))\otimes C)_{i}
that are entirely determined by the coactions
E(n)_{i}\rightarrow(E(n)\otimes C)_{i}
for
n
large enough. It is easy to then verify that the natural maps
\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n)\rightarrow E(n)
are
C
-colinear therefore enforcing that
\mathsf{lim}_{n}^{C}E(n)\cong\mathsf{lim}_{n}E(n)
. Thus
\Omega(M,C,C)\cong\mathsf{lim}^{C}_{n}E(n)\simeq\mathsf{holim}^{C}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}(M,C,C)
and
\widehat{\rho}
must be a fibration.
∎

3.
Rigidifying the coaction

We first make the notion of homotopy coherent coassociative comodules precise using an
\infty
-categorical approach.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, as in
[
Lur17
, 2.0.0.7]
. Let
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
be the associative
\infty
-operad as in
[
Lur17
, 4.1.1.6]
.
Let
A
be an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, as in
[
Lur17
, 4.1.1.6]
. We denote
\mathcal{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})
the
\infty
-category of right
A
-modules, instead of
\mathcal{RMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})
as in
[
Lur17
, 4.2.1.13]
.
By an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebra
in
\mathcal{C}
we mean an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-algebra in the opposite category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{op}}
.
If
C
is an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
, we define the category of right
C
-comodules in
\mathcal{C}
as:

\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{C}):={\left(\mathcal{Mod}_{C}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{op}})\right)}^{\mathsf{op}}.

Given a symmetric monoidal model category
\mathsf{M}
, any coalgebra
C
in
\mathsf{M}
that is cofibrant as an object in
\mathsf{M}
can be regarded as an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebra in the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M}_{c})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}]
of
\mathsf{M}
. See more details in
[
Pér22b
]
. Essentially, a strictly coassociative coalgebra is naturally coassociative up to coherent homotopies. Let
\mathsf{M}^{\otimes}
be the operator category of
\mathsf{M}
. Let
C
be a coalgebra in
\mathsf{M}
. It can be regarded as an
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebra in
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})
and there is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories:

\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M}))\simeq\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})).

Suppose now that
\mathsf{M}
is a combinatorial symmetric monoidal model category in which each object is cofibrant.
By
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.6]
, the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]
is symmetric monoidal via the derived tensor product of
\mathsf{M}
. Moreover, the localization functor
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M}^{\otimes})\rightarrow\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}]^{\otimes}
is symmetric monoidal. In particular, we obtain a functor:

\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}\left(\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]\right).

By construction, the functor sends weak equivalences to equivalences, and thus by the universal property of the localization, we obtain a functor:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{C}:\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M}))\left[\mathsf{W}_{C}^{-1}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{CoMod}_{{C}}\left(\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]\right).}

Here
\mathsf{W}_{C}
denotes the class of weak equivalences between right
C
-comodules in
\mathsf{M}
.

Objects in
\mathcal{CoMod}_{{C}}\left(\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]\right)
are
C
-comodules in
\mathsf{M}
with a coaction that is coassociative only up to higher homotopies. Objects in
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M}))\left[\mathsf{W}_{C}^{-1}\right]
are
C
-comodules in
\mathsf{M}
with a coaction that is strictly coassociative. We have just shown that comodules with a strictly coassociative coaction are naturally endowed with a coaction that is coassociative up to coherent homotopies.

If the functor
\gamma_{C}
above is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories, then this shows that any
C
-comodule in
\mathsf{M}
with a homotopy coherent coassociative coaction is weakly equivalent to a
C
-comodule with a strictly coassociative coaction. We say that we can
rigidify the coaction
.

We apply our above discussion to
\mathsf{M}={\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, the category of non-negative chain complexes over
\Bbbk
. By
[
Lur17
, 7.1.3.10]
, the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}]
is equivalent as a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category to connective
H\Bbbk
-modules in spectra, which we denote
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
. Here
H\Bbbk
denotes the Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum of
\Bbbk
. In this situation, we show we can always rigidify the coaction of a comodule.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
C
is a simply connected coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, then there is a natural equivalence of
\infty
-categories:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{C}:\mathscr{N}\left(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}})\right)\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{C}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}\left(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)\right),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where
\mathsf{W}_{C}
denotes the class of
C
-colinear chain maps that are quasi-isomorphisms in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Proof.

We shall use a (dual) version of the Barr-Beck-Lurie monadicity theorem
[
Lur17
, 4.7.3.16]
. More precisely, we show that the forgetful functors
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}))[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{C}]\rightarrow\mathscr{N}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
and
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}\left(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
are both comonadic over
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
via the comonad
-\otimes C\colon\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
.
By comonadic, we mean the dual version of monadic as in
[
Lur17
, 4.7.3.4]
.
Since
C
is automatically cofibrant in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, we have that the functor
-\otimes C\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is already left derived and thus
X\otimes C
is equivalent to the derived tensor product of
X
and
C
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
for any chain complex
X
.
The forgetful functor
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}\left(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
is comonadic by the dual version of
[
Lur17
, 4.7.2.5]
.

We are only left to check that the functor
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{C}]\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
is comonadic.
By
[
Lur17
, 4.7.3.5]
, this is equivalent to show that it is conservative and preserves split coaugmented cosimplicial (in the dual sense of
[
Lur17
, 4.7.2.2]
).
Since every object is cofibrant in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
, the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is already left derived.
Since
U
preserves and reflects weak equivalences by definition of the model structures, we obtain that its left derived version
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{C}]\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
is conservative.
By
[
Lur17
, 1.3.4.16, 1.3.4.23, 1.3.4.25]
, since
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
is a combinatorial model category, homotopy limits of cosimplicial diagrams in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
are equivalent to limits in the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}((\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})_{f})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{C}]
.
So let
M^{-1}\rightarrow M^{\bullet}
be a
U
-split cosimplicial diagram of fibrant objects in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
and let us show that
M^{-1}
is the homotopy limit
\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}(M^{\bullet})
in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
. The natural map
M^{-1}\rightarrow\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}(M^{\bullet})
fits in a commutative diagram in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}(M^{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}\Omega^{\bullet}(M^{-1},C,C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta\times\Delta}^{C}\Omega^{\bullet}(M^{\bullet},C,C).}

The left vertical map is a quasi-isomorphisms by Theorem
2.5
. The right vertical map is also a quasi-isomorphism by
[
Hir03
, 19.4.2]
as it is a map of cosimplicial diagrams of fibrant objects (using that both
M^{n}
and
\Omega(M^{n},C,C)
are fibrant by Theorem
2.5
) which is a quasi-isomorphism levelwise.
The bottom horizontal map is a quasi-isomorphism as
M^{-1}\rightarrow M^{\bullet}
is
U
-split. Indeed, we have that
U(M^{-1})\rightarrow\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}M^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
Since the cofree functor
-\otimes C\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
preserves limits and quasi-isomorphisms, we obtain a quasi-isomorphism
U(M^{-1})\otimes C\rightarrow\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}(M^{\bullet})\otimes C\simeq\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}^{C}(U(M^{\bullet})\otimes C)
and
U(M^{-1})\otimes C\rightarrow U(M^{\bullet})\otimes C
remains
U
-split.
Reiterating this argument shows that the bottom horizontal map is a quasi-isomorphism in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
, thus we can conclude that the top horizontal arrow is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

4.
The derived cotensor product

We assume
C
is a
cocommutative
simply connected coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
throughout this section. The cotensor product over
C
results in a symmetric monoidal structure
(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C},\square_{C},C)
.
We wish to induce a similar symmetric monoidal structure on the
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))
. We cannot reproduce the techniques on modules as in
[
Lur17
, 4.5.2.1]
as the derived tensor product in
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
does not commute with totalizations. Instead, we use our previous rigidification result to derive the cotensor product on
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
.

Given a symmetric monoidal model category
(\mathsf{M},\otimes)
, we can Quillen left derive the tensor product
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}
to obtain a symmetric monoidal structure on the homotopy category of
\mathsf{M}
(see
[
Hov99
, 4.3.2]
). It also endows its underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{M}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]
with a symmetric monoidal structure (see
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.6]
). In the case of modules over a commutative algebra
A
, the derived tensor product
M\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}N
of
A
-modules
M
and
N
is given by a two-sided bar construction
B(M,A,N)
(see
[
Lur17
, 4.4.2.8]
). However, we come across an obstacle: the monoidal structure in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
is not a monoidal model structure. The bifunctor
-{{\square}_{C}}-:\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\times\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
is not a Quillen bifunctor in the sense of
[
Hov99
, 4.2.1]
. It is not even part of a closed monoidal structure.
It is not either a “co-Quillen bifunctor” as
M{{\square}_{C}}-:\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
does not preserve fibrations, even if
M
is fibrant.
Nevertheless, we can still derive the cotensor product using the following general method in
\infty
-categories from
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.4]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
be a symmetric monoidal category endowed with a model structure. Denote by
\mathsf{M}_{f}
the full subcategory spanned by fibrant objects.
Suppose

(i)

for any fibrant object
M
in
\mathsf{M}
, the functors

4.2.
An Eilenberg-Moore Spectral Sequence

We show here that if
M
is a fibrant
C
-comodule (i.e. coflat), then the functor
M{{\square}_{C}}-:\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
preserves quasi-isomorphisms. We shall use classical methods from
[
EM66
]
.
As noticed in
[
Rav86
, A1.2.12]
, it is useful to observe that for
i\geq 0
and all
C
-comodules
M
and
N
, we have an isomorphism of
C
-comodules:

\mathsf{CoTor}_{C}^{i}(M,N)\cong H^{i}(\overline{\Omega}^{\bullet}({M},C,{N})).

Recall that to any chain complex
X
, we can regard its homology
H_{*}(X)
as a chain complex with trivial differentials. As
C
is a simply connected coalgebra, then
H_{*}(C)
is also a simply connected coalgebra (with trivial differentials). Moreover, for any
C
-comodule
M
, we can check that
H_{*}(M)
is a
H_{*}(C)
-comodule.

Theorem 4.8

(Eilenberg-Moore Spectral Sequence)
.

Let
M
be a fibrant
C
-comodule, i.e. a coflat
C
-comodule.
Let
N
be any
C
-comodule.
Then there is a convergent spectral sequence:

E^{2}_{\bullet,q}=\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(C)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N))\Rightarrow E^{\infty}_{\bullet,0}=H_{*}(M{{\square}_{C}}N).

Proof.

As previously observed, the second quadrant double chain complex
\overline{\Omega}^{\bullet}(M,C,N)_{\bullet}
of Definition
2.4
is bounded since
C
is simply connected.
Thus the two associated spectral sequences to the double complex converge, see
[
McC01
, 2.15]
.
The first spectral sequence has its
E^{1}
-page induced by the cohomology of the columns, and therefore:

E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=H^{q}(\overline{\Omega}^{\bullet}(M,C,N))\cong\mathsf{CoTor}_{C}^{q}(M,N).

Since
M
is a coflat
C
-comodule, then
E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=0
for all
q\geq 1
, and we have
E^{1}_{\bullet,0}=M{{\square}_{C}}N
. Thus the spectral sequence collapses onto its second page
E^{2}_{\bullet,0}=H_{*}(M{{\square}_{C}}N)
.
The second spectral sequence has its
E^{1}
-page induced by the homology of the rows, and therefore:

E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=H_{*}(\overline{\Omega}^{q}(M,C,N)))=\overline{\Omega}^{q}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(C),H_{*}(N)).

Thus, as its
E^{2}
-page is given by the cohomology of the induced cochain complex, we obtain:

E^{2}_{\bullet,q}=\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(C)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N)).

It converges to the page with trivial rows except its
0
-th column which is given by the homology
H_{*}(M{{\square}_{C}}N)
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
C
be a simply connected coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
C
-comodule. (i.e. a coflat
C
-comodule). Then the functor
M{{\square}_{C}}-:\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
preserves quasi-isomorphisms.

Proof.

Let
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y^{\prime}
be a quasi-isomorphism of
C
-comodules.
It induces an isomorphism
H_{*}(Y)\cong H_{*}(Y^{\prime})
of
H_{*}(C)
-comodules.
Therefore we obtain:

\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(C)}(H_{*}(X),H_{*}(Y))\cong\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(C)}(H_{*}(X),H_{*}(Y^{\prime})),

for all
q\geq 0
. By Theorem
4.8
, we obtain
H_{*}(X{{\square}_{C}}Y)\cong H_{*}(X{{\square}_{C}}Y^{\prime})
via the map
Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
. Then
X{{\square}_{C}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}X{{\square}_{C}}Y^{\prime}
is a quasi-isomorphism of
C
-comodules.
∎

4.3.
Change of Coalgebras

We observe here a direct consequence from Corollary
4.9
.
Let
f\colon C\rightarrow D
be a map of simply connected cocommutative coalgebras in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. The map endows the coalgebra
C
with a right
D
-comodule structure:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes D,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{C}\otimes f}

such that
f\colon C\rightarrow D
is a map of
D
-comodules.
We obtain a functor
f^{*}\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{D},
where each
C
-comodule
(M,\rho)
is sent to the
D
-comodule
(M,(\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes f)\circ\rho)
. We shall often write
f^{*}(M)
simply as
M
.

Given any
D
-comodule
M
, we can form the cotensor of
D
-comodules
M\square_{D}C
, which can be endowed with the structure of
C
-comodule
M\square_{D}C\rightarrow M\square_{D}(C\otimes C)\cong(M\square_{D}C)\otimes C
via the comultiplication on
C
.
Therefore, we obtain a functor
-\square_{D}C\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}
which is right adjoint to
f^{*}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
f\colon C\rightarrow D
be a map of simply connected cocommutative coalgebras in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
Then the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\square_{D}C}

is a Quillen pair. The adjunction is a Quillen equivalence if and only if the map
f
is a quasi-isomorphism.
Moreover, when
f
is a quasi-isomorphism, we obtain an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories

\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))\simeq\mathcal{CoMod}_{D}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)),

with respect to their derived cotensor product.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from the fact that the functor
f^{*}
preserves monomorphisms and weak equivalences.
For the second statement, we shall apply
[
Hov99
, 1.3.16]
. Notice that
f^{*}
reflects weak equivalences. Suppose first that
f
is a weak equivalence.
Now let
M
be any fibrant
D
-comodule, the counit of the adjunction
M\square_{D}C\xrightarrow{\simeq}M\square_{D}D\cong M

is a weak equivalence by Corollary
4.9
. Conversely, if we suppose the adjunction to be a Quillen equivalence, then the map

f\colon C\cong D\square_{D}C\longrightarrow D\square_{D}D\cong D

must be a weak equivalence, as
D
is always fibrant as a
D
-comodule.

The third statement, it follows from the universal property of the symmetric Dwyer-Kan localization
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.4]
.
The functor
f^{*}\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}
is colax monoidal with respect to the cotensor products.
The induced lax monoidal map on the right adjoint:

(M\square_{D}C){{\square}_{C}}(N\square_{D}C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}(M\square_{D}N)\square_{D}C,

is actually strong monoidal by associativity of the cotensor product, for any
D
-comodules
M
and
N
. Thus we get a symmetric monoidal functor of
\infty
-categories:

-\square_{D}C\colon\mathscr{N}((\mathsf{CoMod}_{D})_{f}^{\otimes})\longrightarrow\mathscr{N}((\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})_{f}^{\otimes}).

If we suppose
f
to be a weak equivalence, then if we post-compose the functor with the symmetric monoidal Dwyer-Kan localization:

\mathscr{N}((\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})_{f}^{\otimes})\longrightarrow\mathscr{N}((\mathsf{CoMod}_{C})_{f}^{\otimes})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}_{\mathsf{CoMod}}]\simeq\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)),

which is strong monoidal, it sends weak equivalence in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}
to equivalences in
\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))
.
Thus we obtain the desired equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.
∎

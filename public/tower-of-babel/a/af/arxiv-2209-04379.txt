Minimizing Information Leakage under Padding Constraints
Sebastian Simon, Cezara Petrui, Carlos Pinzón, Catuscia Palamidessi

Abstract
An attacker can gain information of a user by analyzing its network traffic. The size of transferred data leaks information about the file being transferred or the service being used, and this is particularly revealing when the attacker has background knowledge about the files or services available for transfer. To prevent this, servers may pad their files using a padding scheme , changing the file sizes and preventing anyone from guessing their identity uniquely. This work focuses on finding optimal padding schemes that keep a balance between privacy and the costs of bandwidth increase. We consider Rényi-min leakage as our main measure for privacy, since it is directly related with the success of a simple attacker, and compare our algorithms with an existing solution that minimizes Shannon leakage. We provide improvements to our algorithms in order to optimize average total padding and Shannon leakage while minimizing Rényi-min leakage. Moreover, our algorithms are designed to handle a more general and important scenario in which multiple servers wish to compute padding schemes in a way that protects the servers’ identity in addition to the identity of the files.



I
Introduction

This paper focuses on the problem of minimizing the information that an adversary can obtain when he analyzes the network traffic of a user.

Network traffic analysis is a powerful tool that is needed for optimizing the routing and transmission speed of data in telecommunications networks
[
Wel05
,
PLB11
]
, as well as for detecting anomalies
[
BBK17
]
and possibly some types of attacks.
In addition to this, traffic analysis is, on its own, a subject which sparks various research topics. For instance, there has been discovered evidence of self-similar patterns in network traffic
[
PW00
]
.
Nonetheless, it can also be used to infer users’ demographics
[
LXZ+16
]
and the types of web services they are using
[
HKDV15
,
CKS+09
]
.
Therefore, network traffic analysis is an active and important research topic in telecommunications, networking, privacy, computer security, as well as in mathematics.

Since the vast majority of modern traffic is encrypted, the research on traffic analysis has increasingly been focused towards the analysis of encrypted traffic, in which the adversary willing to gain information can not observe the content of the messages, but can make inferences based on their timing and sizes.
Although data-encryption provides a strong limitation for the attacker, it is possible, with a substantial degree of accuracy, to detect information about the activity of the users, such as what site is being browsed
[
SJVR+18
,
CHJ17
]
or the format of a streamed video
[
WUB15
]
.

Moreover, as pointed out in
[
WCM09
]
, packet sizes and timing are essential tools that can reveal the language of a VoIP call
[
WBMM07
]
, passwords in secure shell logins
[
Son01
]
, or even web browsing habits
[
SSW+02
,
LL06
]
.
The danger of these inferences is highly increased when the adversary combines several of them, possibly also with additional background knowledge about the user or about application standards, e.g. if Google Meet and Skype use different video formats by default, the attacker would be able to identify which application is being used.

Padding messages is a well-known technique for reducing the quality of the inferences that the traffic observer can do.
It increases the sizes of the files being transferred across a network in order to make them harder to recognize.
More precisely, padding reduces the probability that the attacker infers correctly the file being transferred.
But it also increases the bandwidth use of the network.
Therefore, it is necessary to design padding-schemes that achieve an optimal trade-off between reducing bandwidth overhead and its costs while maintaining a high level of privacy.

We propose different algorithms that minimize Rényi-min leakage subject to three different types of bandwidth constraints that occur naturally in practice.
We prove the correctness of all the algorithms described in the paper, test them against brute-force implementations over small datasets, and compare them to other methods in the literature over a large dataset.

II
Related Works

The need for padding has been identified already in
[
SJVR+18
]
and solved for specific applications in
[
WCM09
]
and
[
RR21
]
.

This paper is strongly related with the work of Reed and Reiter
[
RR21
]
, in which the authors propose three padding algorithms, PRP_Shannon, POP_Shannon (referred to as
PopSh
in this document) and PwoD (padding without a distribution), for finding padding schemes that minimize Shannon leakage under different bandwidth constraints.
They formalize the padding problem as a server that pads its files before or while serving them to clients.

We extend
[
RR21
]
in two ways.
First, we take also into consideration the problem of
server identity protection
, in which several servers coordinate themselves before deploying their optimal padding-schemes such as to protect their identities in addition to the individual identities of the files.
We adequate their POP algorithm to minimize Shannon leakage for this new scenario.
Secondly, we redesign all their algorithms to find padding functions that minimize Rényi-min leakage
[
Smi09a
]
instead of Shannon leakage, and we provide some heuristics for Rényi-then-Shannon and Rényi-then-bandwidth minimization, i.e. finding among all schemes that minimize Rényi-min leakage, the one that minimize Shannon leakage or bandwidth respectively.

We believe that Rényi-min leakage is a better metric for privacy than Shannon leakage for the the file padding problem.
We justify this claim in this paper by pointing out that Rényi-min leakage is directly related via a bijective function with the probability of an attacker’s best inference to be true. This correspondence can also be interpreted as an instance (to network traffic analysis) of the general operational interpretation of Rényi-min leakage in terms of one-try attacks
[
Smi09a
]
.
For Shannon, which is more related to information theoretic applications like compression, the attackers’ success and the leakage are highly correlated, however, they are not directly related via a linear function.
This fact is corroborated experimentally in
[
RR21
]
, as well as in our paper.

In general, in the privacy community, metrics are better described in terms of their associated attacker rather than their information-theoretic properties
[
ACPS12
,
Rom20
]
.
For this particular application, the direct pragmatic connection between Rényi-min leakage and a simple adversary success makes it very appealing.
The same argument is used in
[
Che17
]
, whose privacy measure is closely related with ours.

III
Problem formalization

Generally speaking, the objective is to determine how much to pad each of the files stored in a server to minimize the information gained by a potential adversary analyzing network traffic.

The files in the server store are denoted as
E=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
.
We assume the files in
E
to be sorted non-decreasingly by their sizes
{|e_{i}|}
, and to be accessed with frequency
p_{i}\in[0,1]
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1
.
To denote a random file from the server, we use the random variable
X
that takes values in
E
with probabilities
\mathbb{P}{(X{\mkern 0.0mu\mathchar 61\relax\mkern 1.0mu}e_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}p_{i}
.
We define the set of file sizes
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}{\{|e||e\in E\}}
and enumerate its unique elements in increasing order as
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{m}\}
.
Note that
m\leq n
, since there may be different files with the same size.

Given a fixed multiplicative constraint
c\geq 1
, a
padding function
, or padding scheme, is a (possibly randomized) function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
that satisfies, for all
i\in[1..n]
,
{|e_{i}|}\leq f(e_{i})\leq c\cdot{|e_{i}|}
(with probability
1
).
The padding function will determine the size of the files in the server after padding them, and the double inequality represents, on the one hand, the fact that files can only be padded to a size greater than the initial one:

\mathbb{P}{(f(e)\geq{|e|})}\mathchar 61\relax 1,

(III.1)

and on the other hand, the constraint of avoiding unexpectedly large paddings:

\mathbb{P}{(f(e)\leq c\cdot{|e|})}\mathchar 61\relax 1.

(III.2)

The latter constraint guarantees both for the server and the client that the bandwidth used is not excessive.
It is multiplicative instead of additive because we consider typical cases in which the largest files are accessed less frequently than the smallest ones, hence, it is desirable to limit more severely the padding added to smaller files.
Also, it might be randomized in case the server has the infrastructure to pad files independently for each transfer request.
Moreover, in the favor of clients, the constraint is enforced for each individual file instead of a single global expectation of bandwidth increase because if a client accesses just one or two files, it is of his interest that the bandwidth is bounded for each of those two files.

The objective is to find a padding function
f:E\to\mathbb{N}
that minimizes a given metric of information leakage
\mathbb{I}({|X|},f(X))
, while respecting the constraints
{|e_{i}|}\leq f(e_{i})\leq c\cdot{|e_{i}|}
, for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

In the rest of this section, we describe all the variations of the problem by considering two different leakage functions from the literature, as well as several additional types of constraints that may arise in real-world applications.

III-A

Leakage functions

In the literature there are multiple options for quantifying the leakage
\mathbb{I}{({|X|},f(X))}
of a padding function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.
We will further use the notation
\mathbb{I}(f)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}\mathbb{I}{({|X|},f(X))}
to reduce verbosity since the random variable
X
is fixed.

In
[
RR21
]
, they use Shannon mutual information as a leakage measure.
Shannon leakage is based on Shannon entropy
\mathbb{H}
and is given by:

\mathbb{I}_{1}{({|X|},f(X))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}\mathbb{H}{({|X|})}-\mathbb{H}{({|X|}{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}f(X))}

(III.3)

Shannon leakage is a particular case (
\alpha=1
) of a family of leakages
\mathbb{I}_{\alpha}
based on
\alpha
-Rényi entropy.
A particular case of interest for privacy applications is what we call
Rényi-min leakage
(
\alpha=\infty
), whose derivation and importance is highlighted in
[
PR18
]
and
[
Smi09b
]
:

\mathbb{I}_{\infty}({|X|},f(X))=\mathbb{H}_{\infty}({|X|})-\mathbb{H}_{\infty}({|X|}\mid f(X)).

(III.4)

Both Shannon leakage and Rényi-min leakage are directly related (in one-to-one relation) to the probability of success of an attacker, but the specific assumptions about the attacker and his objective are different.
In the case of Shannon leakage, the attacker is assumed to have the power to perform set-queries of the type ”is the secret in
this
set?”, and his objective is to guess the secret using the minimal number of queries.
For Rényi-min leakage, the attackers’ power is reduced to a one-try attack, i.e. he can only make one query of the type ”is
this
the secret?”, and his objective is to maximize the probability of being correct.
In both cases, to protect against worst-case scenarios, the attacker is assumed to know the server’s files’ sizes and frequencies, as well as the padding-scheme used by the server.
This knowledge may arise in practice if the server hosts public files and the attacker has had time to request each of the files, possibly multiple times in case of a randomized padding scheme.
Notice that the fact that the server files are public, does not reduce at all the need for privacy.
For instance, although the files of an adult video site are public, the users are very interested that no adversarial third party knows exactly which video is being watched.
Therefore, it is clear from the point of view of attackers, that Rényi-min leakage is superior to Shannon leakage as a measure of privacy for this particular application.

We conclude this section by proving an important property of padding functions that minimize Rényi-min leakage.
The same holds true for paddings that minimize Shannon leakage, as shown in
[
RR21
]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathbb{I}
denote Rényi-min leakage,
S
the set of sizes of the files and
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
a padding-scheme. Then, there is a padding-scheme
f^{*}:E\rightarrow S
such that
\mathbb{I}(f^{*})\leq\mathbb{I}(f)
and
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}(f^{*})\leq\mathbb{I}_{\infty}(f)
.

Proof.

We consider that the files are sorted in non-decreasing order with respect to their sizes, denoted by the set
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{m}\}
.
We have
X
the random variable associated to
E
and denote the random variable
Y=f(X)
.
We define
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow S
to be the function satisfying
g(f(e_{i}))=\max\{s\in S|s\leq f(e_{i})\}
.
Denote by
Z
the random variable
(g\circ f)(X)
equipped with the probability space of
E
.
We use the Data Processing Inequality for
{|X|},Y,Z
as stated in Theorem 6.2 of
[
MCPS12
]
and Theorem 8 of
[
MMS+14
]
, and remarked also in Theorem 5.1 of
[
Smi15
]
.

Z
only depends on
Y
and is conditionally independent of
{|X|}
because
Z
is a deterministic function of
Y
.
Since this holds for any
g
-leakage function, we have
\mathbb{I}({|X|},f(X))\geq\mathbb{I}({|X|},(g\circ f)(X))
and
\mathbb{I_{\infty}}({|X|},f(X))\geq\mathbb{I_{\infty}}({|X|},(g\circ f)(X))
.
Hence, we proved that there exists a function
f^{*}=g\circ f:E\rightarrow S
such that it has a lower leakage than
f
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

A padding function that has minimal leakage must pad each file to the size of another file in the initial set.

III-B

Variations of the padding problem

The padding problem can have additional restrictions that arise commonly in practice, giving rise to the following variations, of which the first two are also considered in
[
RR21
]
.

1.

POP (Per-object-padding)

In this variation, the server plans to pad the files only once and forever.
This saves the server from costs and complexity associated to repeated padding.
Hence, our objective in this variation is to compute a deterministic padding function
f
.

2.

PRP (Per-request-padding)

In this variation, we assume no additional constraints to the padding function others than Equations
III.1
and
III.2
.
This means that the store can pad the files before every transaction.

3.

Server identity protection

Note that although the generic problem has no restriction on the output sizes
f(X)
, Corollary
2
implies that the optimal padding is achieved when
f(X)=S
, meaning that the files of the store can only be padded to sizes of other files in the set
E
.
This fact has a critical implication regarding the protection of server identities.
For instance, assume that an attacker who knows that server
A
and
B
have file sizes
20,22,24
and
21,23,25
respectively, and he observes a (padded) file of size
22
being transferred to a user; then he does not know if the file is that of size
20
or
22
, due to padding, but he knows certainly that the user accessed server
A
.
In order to protect the server identity, we suggest a standardized protocol among the servers.
This protocol involves having all the servers padding the files to the same pre-determined set of sizes
Z
such that
f(X)\subset Z
.
In practice,
Z
can be a set of rounded sizes such as
\{5\texttt{Mb},10\texttt{Mb},20\texttt{Mb},30\texttt{Mb},\dots\}
.
We keep the padding constant
c
and conditions (
III.1
) and (
III.2
).
Additionally, we add the constraint that,
\forall{i\in[1..n]}
:

{\{z\in Z{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}z\geq{|e_{i}|},\;z\leq c\cdot{|e_{i}|}\}}\neq\emptyset

(III.5)

meaning that the set
Z
has enough possible padding sizes to be consistent with (
III.2
).
Moreover, the protocol aforementioned for protecting the servers holds not only for Per-Object-Padding, but also for Per-Request-Padding, i.e. it does not influence in any way if the padding function
f
is deterministic or probabilistic, since we can apply the same procedure for both cases.

III-C

Generalization

All the aforementioned variations of the padding problem can be written as instances of a single input-output problem specification.
The specification requires to standardize the bandwidth constraints as pairs
[l_{i},r_{i}]
, meaning that file
e_{i}
can only be padded to sizes
{\{z_{l_{i}},...,z_{r_{i}}\}}
, for some fixed increasing sequence of output values
Z={\{z_{1},...,z_{|Z|}\}}
.
Naturally, these pairs must satisfy some minimal properties to reflect a possible instance of the padding problem.
Namely, we say a sequence
{\{{[l_{i},r_{i}]}\}}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{n}
is
a valid sequence of constraints
for
Z
if
l_{i}\leq r_{i}
holds for all
i\in[1..n]
, and the sequences
{\{l_{i}\}}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{n}
and
{\{r_{i}\}}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{n}
are non-decreasing and satisfy
l_{1}=1
and
l_{n}=r_{n}=|Z|
.

We now state the problem specification and proceed to explain how it captures all variations of the padding problem.
All the algorithms in following sections follow this specification.

Problem specification 3
.

Input
:

A set
E
of
n
files
{\{e_{i}{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}i\in[1..n]\}}
with sizes
{|e_{i}|}
and frequencies
p_{i}
;
a set
Z
of possible output sizes (
Z=S
unless otherwise specified) with a valid sequence of constraints
{\{[l_{i},r_{i}]\}}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{n}
;
and a leakage function
\mathbb{I}
that measures the informational leakage of padding-schemes.

Desired output
:

A padding function
f:E\to Z
(deterministic for POP, possibly randomized for PRP) that minimizes the information leakage
\mathbb{I}({|X|},f(X))
, where
\mathbb{P}{(X{\mkern 0.0mu\mathchar 61\relax\mkern 1.0mu}e_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}p_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

If
f
is deterministic, it can be encoded as a list
{(f(e_{i}))}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{n}
, and in the general probabilistic case as the channel-matrix
(p_{ij})
of size
n\times|Z|
between the secrets
X
and the observables
f(X)
.

Firstly, we argue that the specification generalizes the variations POP and PRP.
In both cases, we invoke Corollary
2
which mandates
Z=S
, hence the the padding function will have signature
f:E\rightarrow S
.
Condition (
III.1
) is attained by choosing
l_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}\min{\{j\in[1..m]{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}{|e_{j}|}\geq{|e_{i}|}\}}
and
r_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}\max{\{j\in[1..m]{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}s_{j}\leq c\cdot{|e_{i}|}\}}
, thus specifying the padding restriction individually for each file.
Note that this also generalizes POP and PRP, allowing the store to have special padding restrictions for each specific file rather than a universal constant
c
, e.g. a list
c_{i}
for
i\in[1..n]
.

Secondly, to deal with the server identity protection case, we start with a padding-scheme
f:E\rightarrow Z
, the probability distribution
\mathbb{P}
, and the set
S
, with the purpose of adapting these three to the generalized problem.
For each
i\in[1..n]
, let
L^{*}_{i}=\min{\{z\in Z{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}z\geq{|e_{i}|}\}}
, i.e. the smallest file size in
Z
that file
e_{i}
can be padded to.
Define
e^{*}_{i}
to be a file with size
L^{*}_{i}
.
Due to (
III.5
),
L^{*}_{i}
exists,
\forall{i\in[1..n]}
, and consider the multiset
S^{*}:={\{L^{*}_{i}{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}i\in[1..n]\}}
.
Because each file is mapped injectively to an element of
S^{*}
, we consider that we pad each element to the closest one in
Z
and then apply the padding problem for the new files.
Using this and Corollary
2
, we reduce the problem to finding a padding-scheme
f^{*}:E^{*}\rightarrow S^{*}
, and a sequence of restrictions
[l_{i},r_{i}],i\in[1..n]
that satisfy the relation
r_{i}:=\max{\{j{\mkern 2.0mu\mid\mkern 1.5mu}L^{*}_{j}<c\cdot L^{*}_{i}\}}
, where
L^{*}_{j}
is the size of the file in
E^{*}
.
Note that the sizes of the files are given in the three variations of the problem, but not in the generalization, as these can be reduced to the restrictions sequence.

By convention, we consider

IV-B

PrpRe

In this section, we treat the case of Per-Request-Padding and provide an algorithm for finding the probabilistic channel-matrix
P
which minimizes the Rényi-min leakage.
We will look at the joint distribution matrix
I
with entries
I_{ij}=p_{i}\cdot p_{ij},\forall i\leq n,j\leq m
, for which the row-wise sum
\sum_{j=1}^{m}I_{ij}
is equal to
p_{i},\forall i\in[1..n]
.

We proceed by finding iteratively, for each of the
m
columns, starting from the last one, the Rényi-min optimal manner of setting the entries of
I
given the padding constraints. We define the
optimal distribution of
p_{i}
across the
i
-th row,
1\leq i\leq n
to be the way we fill in the entries
p_{i1},\dots,p_{im}
such as to obtain the minimum sum of the type (
IV.1
) and preserve the relation
p_{i1}+...+p_{im}=p_{i}
.

The proof of our algorithm requires us to consider sub-problems in which the sequence
(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is updated at each step of the algorithm, thus being different from the initial set of frequencies associated to each file. Hence, we rewrite the problem as a more general one in terms of a
budget
sequence
{(b_{i})}_{1\leq i\leq n}
of length
n
(initialized as
{(p_{i})}_{1\leq i\leq n}
), which dictates the remaining value to be distributed across each row
i,\text{ for }i\in[1..n]
.
The general problem is stated below:

Given a non-negative budget sequence
{(b_{i})}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{k}
of length
k\in[1..n]
, find a solution matrix
I_{k\times m}
that minimizes Equation (
IV.1
), under the padding constraints for rows
i\in[1..k]
, namely the set
\{[l_{1},r_{1}],\dots,[l_{k},r_{k}]\}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}I_{ij}=b_{i}
.

We will design the algorithm to solve the general problem recursively by returning the matrix
I
for the budget sequence
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
with
n
terms. The recurrence relationship can be described using the following observation that will be used when we create the probabilistic channel-matrix for the padding-scheme
f
:

Observation 5
.

The solution
I_{k\times m}
for a given
{(b_{i})}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{k}
that minimizes Rényi leakage satisfies the recurrence relationship

I_{ij}=\begin{cases}b_{i}&\text{ if }j=m\text{ and }i\in[1..k],{|e_{i}|}\mathchar 61\relax s_{m}\\
b_{i}-b_{i}^{^{\prime}}&\text{ if }j=m\text{ and }i\in[1..k-1],{|e_{i}|}\neq s_{m},\\
&m\in[l_{i}..r_{i}]\\
I^{^{\prime}}_{ij}&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

where
I^{^{\prime}}_{(k-t)\times(m-1)}
is the solution to the same minimization problem for the sequence
{(b^{^{\prime}}_{i})}_{i\mathchar 61\relax 1}^{k-t}
of length
k-t
,
t=
number of files from
E
which can be padded to
s_{m}
, such that for any
i\in[1..k-t]
, it is defined as:

b_{i}^{^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\max(b_{i}-b_{t_{max}},0)&\text{ if }m\in[l_{i}..r_{i}]\text{ and }\\
&b_{t_{max}}=\max\{b_{i}|{|e_{i}|}\mathchar 61\relax s_{m}\}\\
b_{i}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

If there are no files among
{\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}}
which can be padded to
s_{m}
, we set
t=0
and solve the minimization problem for the same budget sequence and for the set of
m-1
sizes
{\{s_{1},\dots,s_{m-1}\}}
.

If there are files that can be padded to
s_{m}
, then due to the padding constraints, the element
e_{i}
can only be padded to
s_{m}
, so the entry
I_{im}
must necessarily be equal to
b_{i},\text{ for all }i
such that
{|e_{i}|}\mathchar 61\relax s_{m}
. Let us denote by
T={\{k-t+1,\dots,k\}}
the set of indices satisfying
{|e_{i}|}\mathchar 61\relax s_{m},\forall i\in T
and
b_{t_{max}}=\max\{b_{i}|i\in T\}
. Clearly, for every
i\in T
,
I_{ij}=0,\forall j\in{\{1,\dots,k-1\}}
. On the
m
-th column of the matrix
I
, we have
\max_{i\in[1..k]}I_{im}\geq b_{t_{max}}
.

In order to minimize the sum (
IV.1
) and taking into consideration that the maximum entry on column
m
is at least
b_{t_{max}}
, we aim to distribute, for every
i
such that
e_{i}
can be padded to
s_{m}
but
{|e_{i}|}\neq s_{m}
, a quantity equal to
b_{t_{max}}
(or, if
b_{i}<b_{t_{max}}
, then we distribute the whole
b_{i}
) on the entry
I_{im}
, such that we preserve the maximum on this last column to be
b_{t_{max}}
. This way, we can assure that, among the other columns, we’ll have to distribute a smaller fraction of
b_{i}
, which means that the maximum on each column between
1
and
m-1
will decrease, and so will (
IV.1
).

The problem reduces to find the optimal sub-matrix
I^{^{\prime}}_{(k-t)\times(m-1)}
to complete the first
k-t
rows of
I
, and with the aforementioned observation, we can actually consider
I^{^{\prime}}
to be the solution given the updated sequence
(b^{^{\prime}}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k-t}
which is defined, for every
i
such that file
e_{i}
that can be padded to
s_{m}
, as either
0,\text{ if }b_{i}\leq b_{t_{max}}
, or as
b_{i}-b_{t_{max}},\text{ if }b_{i}\geq b_{t_{max}}
. When we reconstruct the matrix
I
, on the
m
-th column we will have the value
I^{^{\prime}}_{im}+b_{t_{max}}
or
I^{^{\prime}}_{im}+b_{i}
(depending whether

is smaller, respectively larger, than
b_{t_{max}}
).

Now, let us show that, for the sub-matrix
I^{^{\prime}}
, we have
0
’s on every entry of the
m
-th column. By definition,
I^{^{\prime}}
must be a Rényi-min optimal solution for the updated sequence of
b^{^{\prime}}_{i}
’s. Using Proposition
1
, there exists a Rényi-min optimal padding-scheme
f^{^{\prime}}
which maps
e_{i},i\in[1..k-t]\rightarrow\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k-t}\}
, for any set of files
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k-t}\}
with the associated frequencies
\{b^{^{\prime}}_{1},\dots,b^{^{\prime}}_{k-t}\}

i\in[1..k-t],\mathbb{P}(f^{^{\prime}}(e_{i})=s_{m})=0\Rightarrow I^{^{\prime}}_{im}=0
.
∎

Therefore, we have proved that the matrix
I
can be recursively expressed using the sub-matrices obtained when we update the budget sequence accordingly, at each step decreasing by
1
the number of columns and by at least
1
the number of rows of the matrix returned from the algorithm, until we reduce a problem to finding the Rényi-min optimal scheme for a budget sequence with a single element. Since we want to minimize (
IV.1
) in the case of
n
files with frequencies
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
and the associated set of sizes
{\{s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\}}
, we proceed the induction on the number of rows and columns as described in Observation
5
and eventually fill in all the entries of the solution
I_{n\times m}
. If we want to recover the channel-matrix
P
of conditional probabilities, we return the matrix with entries:

P_{ij}=I_{ij}/p_{i},\forall i\in[1..n],j\in[1..m].

This approach also handles a problem tackled in
[

]

V
Algorithms for further minimizations

We observe that there might be numerous padding-schemes that achieve minimal Rényi-min leakage.
In this section we provide improvements to our algorithms in order to optimize average total padding and Shannon leakage after minimizing Rényi-min leakage.
That is, we start with a Rényi-min optimal padding scheme found with the algorithms of the previous section and apply several heuristics to reduce the average padding or the Shannon leakage, while preserving minimal Rényi-min leakage.

V-A

Average padding minimization

In this section, we show improvements for the Per-Object-Padding and Per-Request-Padding cases in order to reduce the average padding while keeping the Rényi-min leakage at its minimum.

1.

POP(Per-Object-Padding)

The heuristic for reducing bandwidth is already part of the design of algorithm
PopRe
.
The heuristic consists of resolving ties by always choosing to pad the file with maximal frequency to the file with the least possible size.

Technically, this heuristic guarantees we find the padding-scheme with minimal bandwidth among those that are reachable using zero or more
moves
(see Section

We verified this experimentally, with more than
10{,}000
random generated tests using the code in
[
PPS22
]
, as well as the fact that this heuristic is limited and does not find the minimal bandwidth among all padding functions with optimal Rényi-min leakage.

2.

Shannon leakage minimization

We aim to improve Shannon leakage while keeping the Rényi-min leakage minimal.
For instance, in Figure

In this section, instead of working with the channel-matrix
P=(p_{ab})_{a\in[1..n],b\in[1..m]}
of dimension
n\times m
, we will talk about the matrix
I=(p_{ab}\cdot p_{a})_{a\in[1..n],b\in[1..m]}
. This is intuitive since minimizing the Rényi-min leakage is equivalent to minimizing (
IV.1
). However, the sum in (
IV.1
) is equal to
\sum_{j\in[1..m]}\max_{i\in[1..n]}(I_{ij})
, i.e. the sum of the maximal entries of the columns of matrix
I
.

We consider a
move
to be a change of a padding-scheme
f
to a different padding-scheme
f^{*}
such that the two functions differ in one element only, and
f^{*}
respects the padding constraints inherent to
f
. Our goal is to start from matrix
I
and apply a series of
moves
in order to eventually reach a matrix that preserves Rényi-min leakage but has lower Shannon leakage.

We focus on keeping the Rényi-min leakage constant after each single
move
. This does not find the universal Rényi-min optimal padding with the least Shannon leakage, a fact that was tested experimentally
[
PPS22
]
, but in practice it performs very well (refer to Section
VI
for details).

Starting from the initial matrix
I
, we want to get the matrix
I^{*}
such that, at any step during the sequence of
moves
applied, the following property holds for all columns
j\in[1..m]
:

\max_{i\in[1..n]}I_{ij}=\max_{i\in[1..n]}I^{*}_{ij}

This property ensures that the Rényi-min leakage stays the same due to the fact that the quantity in
IV.1
remains constant. By restricting our algorithm to only perform
moves
that respect the above condition, we define a list of positions where the file
e_{i}
can be padded to, named
poss[i]
, for each
i
in
[1..n]
:

\texttt{poss[i]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}}{{\coloneqq}}\{j\in[1..m]|j\in[l_{i}..r_{i}]\text{ and }\max_{a\in[1..n]}I_{aj}\geq p_{i}\}.

Allowing to pad file
e_{i}
only to files with index in

will preserve the minimal Rényi-min leakage. Now, the method to minimize the Shannon mutual information is rooted in the following underlying observation:

Observation 6
.

Consider
f
to be a Rényi-min optimal padding-scheme with
P=(p_{ab})_{a\leq n,b\leq m}
its channel-matrix and let

be the index of the column of the channel-matrix

with the highest associated probability
\mathbb{P}(f(X)=s_{j})={\sum_{a=1}^{n}p_{a}\cdot p_{aj}}
. Then, any
move
of
1

j
-th column gives a padding-scheme with less Shannon leakage.

Proof.

The Shannon mutual information is
\mathbb{I}({|X|},f(X))=\mathbb{H}({|X|})-\mathbb{H}({|X|}\mid f(X))=\mathbb{H}(f(X))-\mathbb{H}(f(X)\mid{|X|})

f(X)
is a deterministic function of
{|X|}
, so
\mathbb{H}(f(X)\mid{|X|})=0
. Hence, in order to minimize Shannon mutual information, it is sufficient to minimize:

\mathbb{H}(f(X))=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}(f(X)=s_{j})\cdot\log_{2}\mathbb{P}(f(X)=s_{j})

(V.1)

Suppose there exists
i
an index such that
p_{ij}=0
, and

. Then,
\exists k\neq j
with

. We want to show that

the

from position

P_{ij}

f^{*}

\sum_{b=1}^{m}\tilde{p}(g(b))\text{ where for every }b\in[1..m],\text{ we define }g(b):=\mathbb{P}(f(X)=s_{b})\text{ and }g^{*}(b):=\mathbb{P}(f^{*}(X)=s_{b})

\displaystyle D=\sum_{b=1}^{m}\tilde{p}(g(b))-\sum_{b=1}^{m}\tilde{p}(g^{*}(b))=\tilde{p}(g(j))+\tilde{p}(g(k))-

Experiments and Comparison

Several experiments were carried out for three distinct purposes, namely, (1) to test the correctness of the implementations against brute-force algorithms for small sized problems, (2) to corroborate the direct link between Rényi-min leakage and the success rate of an attacker and (3) to compare the runtime, bandwidth and leakages of all the algorithms on a large real-world dataset.
The code of all the experiments is available in
[
PPS22
]
.

VI-A

Brute-force tests

All the algorithms developed, including our re-implementation of
PopSh
to handle the server identity problem, were tested against brute-force implementations for small datasets (with at most
10
elements).
That is, for each randomly generated test case of file sizes and frequencies, we explored (exhaustively) all the POP padding schemes satisfying the constraints, and chose among them, the ones that minimized Rényi-min leakage, Shannon leakage or bandwidth, with the purpose of comparing them with the solutions returned by our algorithms.

We ran tens of thousands of experiments (code available in
[
PPS22
]
), all corroborating that:
(a) among all POP schemes,
PopRe
achieves minimal Rényi-min leakage and
PopSh
achieves minimal Shannon leakage;
(b)

is always better than
PopRe
;
(c)

leaks at most the Rényi-min leakage of
PopRe
.

VII
Future Work

One natural improvement in our work would be to make our extended algorithms, namely
PopReSh
and
PrpReBa
, find the padding-scheme with optimal Shannon mutual information, respectively minimal average padding among all the channels with minimal Rényi-min leakage.
In order to better delve into the problem, let us consider a graph
G
for each algorithm, such as the one in Figure
VII.1
, where the nodes represent all the Rényi-min optimal padding-schemes.
We draw a
dotted edge
between two vertices if we can apply exactly one
move
(each algorithm has a different type of
move
) to one of the padding-schemes and reach the other one.
On the other hand, we draw a
straight edge
between two padding-schemes if we can apply a single
move
to one function in order to reach the other one, and, furthermore, preserve the set of maximal elements on the columns, as in the solution for
PopReSh
, or
PrpReBa
.
Clearly, every two vertices united by a straight edge are also united by a dotted one.
Denote by
G_{\emph{\text{dotted edges}}}
and
G_{\emph{\text{straight edges}}}
the sub-graphs of
G
containing solely dotted, respectively straight edges.
Our algorithms find the optimal vertex in the connected component of
G_{\emph{\text{dotted edges}}}
which includes the node returned by
PopRe
.
A future upgrade would be to find the optimal padding-scheme in the connected component of
G_{\emph{\text{straight edges}}}
, or in the whole graph
G
.

For instance, if
PopRe
returns one of the blue nodes in Figure
VII.1
, then
PopReSh
will find the blue bold node, as it minimizes Shannon leakage in its connected component, thus it will fail to find the bold green node, which achieves the actual global minimum.

Secondly, we prioritized the minimization of Rényi-min leakage over the measure of average padding. Future work can be directed in finding an optimal trade-off between the two, not necessarily giving absolute priority to the bandwidth of each costumer using the constant
c
.

Lastly, our paper and
[
RR21
]
provided algorithms to minimize Rényi-min and Shannon leakage.
As mentioned in the subsection Problem formalization, we could also try to minimize
\mathbb{I}_{\alpha}
, the Rényi-min leakage.
However, thanks to the applicability of the notion of Rényi-min leakage to measure the success of a real adversary, we believe that Rényi-min leakage is the most adequate measure of leakage for this problem.

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper we used Rényi-min leakage as the main measure for privacy, arguing that it is the most suitable for simulating a real-life attacker.
We built two types of algorithms that minimize Rényi-min leakage, treating not only the per-object-padding case in which the files are padded in a deterministic way, but also the per-request-padding, in which each single file is padded to every available size with a given probability.

We provided several improvements that lowered the average padding and the Shannon leakage.
We want to highlight the algorithms
PrpRe
and
PrpReBa
, which have proven a better running time compared to all of their competitors.
Nonetheless, the latter one also has an impressive bandwidth advantage.
We analyzed all of our algorithms numerically, using many artificial examples and also the NodeJS dataset.

Furthermore, we described a method for designing padding-schemes for multiple servers that protect the identities of both the files and the servers.
The servers should simply agree on a fixed standard set of possible output sizes and run our algorithms.

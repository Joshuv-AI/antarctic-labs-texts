Information in probability: Another information-theoretic proof of a finite de Finetti theorem
Lampros Gavalakis and Ioannis Kontoyiannis

Abstract
We recall some of the history of the information-theoretic approach to deriving core results in probability theory and indicate parts of the recent resurgence of interest in this area with current progress along several interesting directions. Then we give a new information-theoretic proof of a finite version of de Finetti’s classical representation theorem for finite-valued random variables. We derive an upper bound on the relative entropy between the distribution of the first k k in a sequence of n n exchangeable random variables, and an appropriate mixture over product distributions. The mixing measure is characterised as the law of the empirical measure of the original sequence, and de Finetti’s result is recovered as a corollary. The proof is nicely motivated by the Gibbs conditioning principle in connection with statistical mechanics, and it follows along an appealing sequence of steps. The technical estimates required for these steps are obtained via the use of a collection of combinatorial tools known within information theory as ‘the method of types.’

Entropy and information in probability

Shannon’s landmark 1948 paper
shannon:48
founded the field
of information theory and ignited the fuse that led to much of the
subsequent explosive development of communications theory and
engineering in the 20th century. At the same time, it also led to a
wave of applications of information theory to numerous other
branches of science. Of those, some, e.g. those in bioinformatics
and neuroscience, were successful, while some others,
despite Shannon’s “bandwagon” warning

,
much less so.

Within mathematics, information-theoretic ideas have had a major
impact along several directions, perhaps most notably (although certainly
not exclusively) in connection with probability theory. For our
present purposes, the most relevant line of work is based on the
idea of utilising information-theoretic tools and ideas in order
to prove core probabilistic results. Over the past 55 years,
a great number of such proofs have appeared.
These are often accompanied by new interpretations
and rich intuition, thus providing new ways of understanding
why fundamental probabilistic theorems are true,
and sometimes also giving stronger versions of the original results.

In the rest of this introduction we describe some of the
main landmarks along this path, and we indicate
directions of current and likely near-future activity.
This brief survey is necessarily incomplete and
biased, due to our own subjective taste and bounded knowledge.
Then in Section
2

a new finite version of de Finetti’s classical
representation theorem for finite-valued
exchangeable random variables.

D(P_{k}\|M_{\mu,k})\leq\epsilon(n,k):=2\delta+ke^{-\frac{n}{k}\delta}\Bigl(\frac{n}{k}+1\Bigr)^{2m^{k}}\log n,

(9)

with

\alpha=\alpha_{n,k}=\big[\frac{2k}{\sqrt{n}}\big(\frac{1+2k}{\sqrt{n}}+1\big)\big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big((X_{1},X_{2})=(a_{1},a_{2})\big|\hat{P}_{X_{1}^{2\ell}}=Q_{2\ell}\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(Z_{1}=(a_{1},a_{2})\big|\hat{P}_{Z_{1}^{\ell}}\in E(Q_{2\ell})\big),

If we write

.
At this point, we would wish to apply the conditional limit
theorem

to the i.i.d. process

,
to obtain that,

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(Z_{1}=(a_{1},a_{2})\big|\hat{P}_{Z_{1}^{\ell}}\in E(Q_{2\ell})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}W_{\ell}^{*}(a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}Q_{2\ell}(a_{1})Q_{2\ell}(a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(a_{1})Q(a_{2}),

and combining this with (

\mathbb{P}\big((X_{1},X_{2})=(a_{1},a_{2})\big|\hat{P}_{X_{1}^{2\ell}}=Q_{2\ell}\big)\to Q(a_{1})Q(a_{2}),\;\;\;\ell\to\infty.

The same argument can be used without difficulty
to show that
for any

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{1}^{k}=a_{1}^{k}|\hat{P}_{X_{1}^{k\ell}}=Q_{k\ell})=Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k}).

(13)

Step 4
:
Since (

every sequence
\{Q_{n}\}
, letting
\ell\to\infty

bounded converge theorem we have,

\mathbb{P}(X_{1}^{k}=a_{1}^{k})={\mathbb{E}}\Big(\mathbb{P}(X_{1}^{k}=a_{1}^{k}|\hat{P}_{X_{1}^{k\ell}})\Big)\to\int_{\cal P}Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})d\bar{\mu}(Q),

as required.

\Box

The only problem with the above argument

Lemma 1

For any
\ell>k\geq 1
and
any
n
-type
Q
, there is a
W\in E_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}
with:

\max_{a_{1}^{k}}|W(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|\leq M:=\Bigg[\frac{2}{\ell}+\frac{4k}{\ell}+2\sqrt{\frac{k}{\ell}}\Bigg]^{1/2}.

Moreover, for
2\leq k\leq\sqrt{\ell}/10,

|H(W)-H(Q^{k})|\leq-M\log\frac{M}{m^{k}}.

Proof

Let
x_{1}^{k\ell}\in A^{k\ell}
have type
Q
,
let
V_{1}^{k\ell}
be a random permutation of
x_{1}^{k\ell}
,
and let
\widehat{W}
denote its (random)
\ell
-type.
Obviously we have that

\widehat{W}\in E_{k}(Q)
by construction,
and we will also show that
\widehat{W}
satisfies the
statement of the lemma with positive
probability.
Taking any
k\leq\ell
and
\gamma>0
arbitrary,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{a_{1}^{k}}

\displaystyle\,|\widehat{W}(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|>\gamma\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}\mathbb{P}\Big(|\widehat{W}(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|>\gamma\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}\gamma^{-2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\widehat{W}(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{-2}\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}\Big[\rho_{2}(a_{1}^{k})-2Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})+Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})^{2}\Big],

(15)

where

\rho_{2}(a_{1}^{k})=\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{W}(a_{1}^{k})^{2}\big]
and

\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})=\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{W}(a_{1}^{k})\big]
.

Now we find appropriate bounds
so that the above probability is
<1
. To get an
upper bound on
\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})
for some fixed
a_{1}^{k}

note that,

\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})=\mathbb{P}(V_{1}^{k}=a_{1}^{k})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{n(a_{i})}{\ell k-i+1},

where
n(a_{i})
is the number of appearances of
a_{i}

in
x_{1}^{k\ell}
, and hence,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{n(a_{i})}{\ell k}\frac{\ell k}{\ell k-i+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big(\frac{\ell k}{\ell k-k}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\Big)^{-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})(1+k/\ell),

since
(1-x)^{-k}\leq 1+kx
for
x\in[0,1)
.
Similarly, writing
a_{1}^{k}*a_{1}^{k}
for the concatenation
of
a_{1}^{k}
with itself, we can estimate,

\mathbb{P}(V_{1}^{2k}=a_{1}^{k}*a_{1}^{k})\leq Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})^{2}(1+4k/\ell),

so that,

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(a_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})+\ell(\ell-1)\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\mathbb{P}(V_{1}^{2k}=a_{1}^{k}*a_{1}^{k})

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1/\ell)(1+k/\ell)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})+(1+4k/\ell)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2/\ell)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})+(1+4k/\ell)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})^{2}.

Substituting the bound
(
16
) in (
15
) we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{a_{1}^{k}}|W(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|>\gamma\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-2}\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\Big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\Big)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})-2\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-2}\max_{a_{1}^{k}}\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\Big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\Big)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})-2\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})\Big].

(17)

Finally, we get a lower bound on

\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})
.
In the case where for some
\beta>0

(to be chosen later),

Q(a_{i})>\beta
for all
a_{i}
,
we have,

\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{n(a_{i})-i+1}{\ell k-i+1}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{n(a_{i})-k+1}{\ell k}\geq Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell Q(a_{i})}\Big),

so that,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})>Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell\beta}\Big)^{k}\geq Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big(1-\frac{k}{\ell\beta}\Big),

(18)

assuming
\beta\geq 1/2\ell,
since
(1-x)^{k}\geq 1-kx
for
all
k\geq 1
and all
x\in[0,2]
.
For all such
a_{1}^{k}
,
using (
18
)
we can bound the expression
in the maximum in (
17
) by

\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\Big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\Big)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})-2\rho_{1}(a_{1}^{k})\Big]<\frac{2}{\ell}+\frac{4k}{\ell}+\frac{2k}{\ell\beta}.

And in the case when at least one
a_{i}
has
Q(a_{i})\leq\beta
,
simply omitting the negative term and noting that
Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\leq\beta

we bound the same term above by,

\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\Big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\Big)Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\Big]\leq\frac{2}{\ell}+\Big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\Big)\beta.

Combining the last three bounds,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{a_{1}^{k}}|W(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|>\gamma\Big)\leq\gamma^{-2}\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\max\Big\{\frac{2k}{\ell}\big(2+\frac{1}{\beta}\big),\;\big(2+\frac{4k}{\ell}\big)\beta\Big\}\Big],

where the inequality is strict when the first
term dominates the maximum.
To obtain a good bound we
take for
\beta
a value approximately equal to
the minimiser of the above expression: We
set
\beta^{*}=\sqrt{k/\ell}
.
Note that for this
\beta^{*}
it can be easily
verified that the first term strictly dominates
the maximum, giving,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{a_{1}^{k}}|W(a_{1}^{k})-Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})|>\gamma\Big)<\gamma^{-2}\Big[\frac{2}{\ell}+\frac{4k}{\ell}+2\sqrt{\frac{k}{\ell}}\Big],

and taking
\gamma=M
as in the lemma,
completes the proof of the first statement.

For the second part, noting that for
2\leq k\leq\sqrt{\ell}/10

we have
M<1/2
, the result follows
from
(
csiszar:book
, Lemma 2.7)
.

Next we obtain an upper bound on the conditional

in (

Lemma 2

Suppose
n=\ell k
, with
2\leq k\leq\sqrt{\ell}/10

For any
n

\mathbb{E}\Big(D(\hat{P}_{Z_{1}^{\ell}}\|Q^{k})\,\Big|\hat{P}_{Z_{1}^{\ell}}\in E_{k}(Q)\Big)\leq\epsilon(n,k).

Proof

We follow the same steps as in the proof
of the conditional limit theorem in
cover:book2
.
Recall that if we write
U_{k}
for the uniform distribution
on
A^{k}
, then the
W_{k}^{*}
that uniquely achieves

is
W_{k}^{*}=Q^{k}

We partition

into
B_{2\delta}
and
C=E_{k}(Q)-B_{2\delta}
,
where
B_{2\delta}=\{W\in E_{k}(Q)\,:\,D(W\|U_{k})\leq D^{*}+2\delta\}
, with
\delta=\delta_{n,k}
.
Then, writing
\nu_{\ell}
for the distribution of

\hat{P}_{Z_{1}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}(C|E_{k}(Q))=\frac{\nu_{\ell}(C\cap E_{k}(Q))}{\nu_{\ell}(E_{k}(Q))}\leq\frac{\nu_{\ell}(C)}{\nu_{\ell}(B_{2\delta})}.

Next we bound the above numerator and denominator.
For the numerator, writing again

{\cal P}_{\ell}
for the set of
\ell
-types on
A^{k}
,

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}(C)

\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\;

\displaystyle\sum_{W\in C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}}U_{k}^{\ell}(T(W))

\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\;

\displaystyle\sum_{W\in C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}}e^{-\ell D(W\|U_{k})}

\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\;

\displaystyle|E_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}|e^{-\ell(D^{*}+2\delta)}

\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{\leq}}\;

\displaystyle(\ell+1)^{m^{k}}e^{-\ell(D^{*}+2\delta)}

where

denotes the type class of all
strings of length
\ell

with type

,

is a standard property

,
(c)
follows from the

,
and
(d)
follows from the

|E_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}|\leq|\mathcal{P}_{\ell}|\leq(\ell+1)^{m^{k}}
.
Similarly, letting
W_{0}
denote the type from Lemma

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}(B_{2\delta})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}(B_{\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{W\in B_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell}}U_{k}^{\ell}(T(W))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle U_{k}^{\ell}(T(W_{0}))

\displaystyle(\ell+1)^{-m^{k}}e^{-\ell D(W_{0}\|U)}

\displaystyle(\ell+1)^{-m^{k}}e^{-\ell(D^{*}+\delta)}.

Lemma 3

For any
n
-type
Q
,
\max_{W\in E_{k}(Q)}D(W\|Q^{k})\leq k\log{n}
.

Proof

If
a_{1}^{k}\in A^{k}
is such that
Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{Q(a_{i})}=0
,
then
Q(a_{i_{0}})=0
for some
i_{0}
. Since

Q(a_{i_{0}})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{W_{j}(a_{i_{0}})}
, we must
have
W_{1}(a_{i_{0}})=\cdots=W_{k}(a_{i_{0}})=0
,
which implies that
W(a_{1}^{k})=0.

On the other hand, if
Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})>0
then
Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{n^{k}}
.
Thus, for any
W\in E_{k}(Q)
,

\displaystyle D(W\|Q^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}^{k}\in A^{k}}W(a_{1}^{k})\log\frac{W(a_{1}^{k})}{Q^{k}(a_{1}^{k})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}^{k}\in A^{k}}W(a_{1}^{k})\log\frac{W(a_{1}^{k})}{({\frac{1}{n}})^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\log n-H(W)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\log n,

as required.

Theorem
2.1
follows from (

)
combined with Lemma
2
.

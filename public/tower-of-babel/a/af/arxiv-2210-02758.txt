Frobenius integrability of certain p p -forms on singular spaces
Junyan Cao, Andreas Höring

Abstract
. Demailly proved that on a smooth compact Kähler manifold the distribution defined by a holomorphic p p -form with values in an anti-pseudoeffective line bundle is always integrable. We generalise his result to compact Kähler spaces with klt singularities.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
u\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p})
be a holomorphic
p
-form on
X
. As a consequence of the Kähler identity for the Laplacians
\Delta_{d}=2\Delta_{\partial}
one obtains that the holomorphic form is
d
-closed, i.e.
du=0
. Twenty years ago Jean-Pierre Demailly used a very clever “integration by parts” to generalise this statement to forms with values in certain line bundles:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Dem02
, Main thm]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. Let
(L,h)
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
where
h
is a possibly singular metric such that
i\Theta_{h}(L)\geqslant 0
on
X
in the sense of currents.
Let

u\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes L^{\star})

be a non-zero holomorphic section, and let

S_{u}\subset T_{X}
be the saturated subsheaf given by vector fields
\xi
such that the contraction
i_{\xi}u
vanishes.

Then one has
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u=0
. Hence
S_{u}
is integrable, i.e. it defines a (possibly singular) foliation on
X
, and
(L,h)
has flat curvature along the leaves.

Demailly’s main motivation for this result was to prove that if a compact Kähler manifold admits a contact structure, then the canonical bundle
K_{X}
is never pseudoeffective
[
Dem02
, Cor.2]
. Moreover Theorem
1.1
has turned out to be a very efficient tool for the study of foliations with vanishing first Chern class
[
PT13
,
LPT18
,
GKP21
]
. In view of the increased interest in foliations on singular spaces (cf. e.g.
[
CS21
,
Dru21
]
) it seems worthwhile to look at Demailly’s arguments in this setting. Our main result is:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be a normal compact Kähler space with klt singularities.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a rank one reflexive sheaf such that the reflexive power
{\mathcal{A}}^{[m]}
is locally free and pseudoeffective for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

u\in H^{0}(Y,(\Omega_{Y}^{p}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\star})^{\star\star})

be a non-zero holomorphic section. Let
S_{u}\subset T_{Y}
be the saturated subsheaf given by vector field
\xi
such that the contraction
i_{\xi}u
vanishes.
Then
S_{u}
is integrable, i.e. it defines a (possibly singular) foliation on
Y
.

For applications in foliation theory it is interesting to verify if
{\mathcal{A}}
has flat curvature
along the leaves of
S_{u}
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is not locally free the precise formulation would be a bit awkward, but flatness holds for the corresponding line bundle
(L,h)

on a resolution of singularities (cf. Proposition
3.3
and Proposition

Our basic strategy is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1

{\mathcal{A}}
is not locally free this leads to some well-known difficulties, for example the saturation of

in
\Omega^{p}_{X}
is not always pseudoeffective
[
GKP14
,
Ou14
]
. Therefore we consider forms with logarithmic poles along the exceptional divisor
E
of the resolution
\pi
, in particular we obtain that the saturation in
\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log E)
is pseudoeffective, cf. Corollary
4.3
.

2.
Notation and terminology

For general definitions in complex and algebraic geometry we refer to
[
Har77
,
Dem12
]
,
for the terminology of singularities of the MMP we refer to
[
KM98
]
.
Manifolds and normal complex spaces will always be supposed to be irreducible.

Given a normal complex space

, we denote by
\Omega_{Y}^{[p]}:=(\Omega_{Y}^{p})^{\star\star}
the sheaf of holomorphic reflexive
p
-forms. If
Y
has klt singularities we know by
[

, Thm.1.1]
that this coincides with the sheaf of holomorphic
p
-forms that extend to a resolution of singularities

.

For a reflexive sheaf

on

3.
Twisted logarithmic forms

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
E=\sum E_{i}
be a snc divisor. Let
(L,h)
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
where
h
is a smooth metric such that
i\Theta_{h}(L)\geqslant 0
. Let
u\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log E)\otimes L^{\star})
and
(L^{\star},h^{\star})
be the dual metric on
(L,h)
. Then
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u=0
on
X
and
i\Theta_{h}(L)\wedge u\wedge\overline{u}=0
.

Proof.

If
L
is a trivial line bundle, it is done by
[
Nog95
]
. We generalize it to the twisted setting by the following argument.

Step 1:
Since
h
is a smooth metric, we know that
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\in C^{\infty}(X,\Omega^{p+1}_{X}(\log E)\otimes L^{\star})
. We show in this step that

D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\in C^{\infty}(X,\Omega^{p+1}_{X}\otimes L^{\star})
.

We consider the residue of
u
and
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u
on
E_{i}
.
First of all, by a direct calculation, we have

(3.1)

\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u)=-D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(u)\qquad\text{ on }E_{i}.

In fact, let
\Omega
be a neighborhood of a generic point of
E_{i}
. We suppose that
E_{i}
is defined by
z_{1}=0
and
h=e^{-\varphi}
on
\Omega
.
Then we can write

u=\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\wedge f+g

for two smooth forms
f,g
on
\Omega
.

For the RHS of (
3.1
), since
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(u)=f
and we obtain

-D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(u)=-(\partial f+\partial\varphi\wedge f)|_{E_{i}}.

For the LHS of (
3.1
), we have

\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u)=\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}(\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\wedge f))=\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(-\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\wedge\partial f+\partial\varphi\wedge\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\wedge f)=-(\partial f+\partial\varphi\wedge f)|_{E_{i}}.

Then we obtain (
3.1
).

Note that
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(u)\in H^{0}(E_{i},\Omega_{E_{i}}^{p-1}(\log(E-E_{i}))\otimes L^{\star})
. By induction on dimension, we know that
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(u)
is
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}
-closed on
E_{i}
. Then (
3.1
) implies that
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u)=0
. Therefore the form
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u
is a smooth form on the total space
X
.

Step 2:
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and let
\Xi_{N}(x)
be a smooth function which equals to
1
on
[0,N]
, equals to
0
on
[N+1,\infty]
and
0\leqslant\Xi^{\prime}_{N}(x)\leqslant 1
. Let
s_{E}
be the canonical section of
E
. We consider the integration

(3.2)

\int_{X}\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|))\{D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u,D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}.

Here
|s_{E}|
denotes the norm of
s_{E}
with respect to a fixed smooth metric on
E
.

By integration by parts, (
3.2
) equals to

=\int_{X}\{D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}(\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|))u),D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}-\int_{X}\{\partial(\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|)))\wedge u,D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}

=-\int_{X}(-1)^{p}\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|))\{u,\bar{\partial}(D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u)\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}-\int_{X}\{\partial(\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|)))\wedge u,D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}

(3.3)

=-\int_{X}i\Theta_{h}(L)\Xi_{N}\cdot\{u,u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}-\int_{X}\{\frac{\Xi^{\prime}_{N}\cdot\partial\log|s_{E}|\wedge u}{\log|s_{E}|},D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}.

Since
i\Theta_{h}(L)\geqslant 0
, the first term of (
3.3
) is semi-negative.
For the second term of (
3.3
), by step 1, we know that
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u
is smooth on
X
. Together with
\frac{ds_{E_{i}}}{s_{E_{i}}\log|s_{E_{i}}|}\wedge\frac{ds_{E_{i}}}{s_{E_{i}}}=0
, we know that the second term of (
3.3
) is controlled by

\int_{N\leqslant\log(-\log|s_{E}|)\leqslant N+1}\frac{1}{\prod_{i}|s_{E_{i}}|}\omega_{X}^{n},

which converges to zero when
N\rightarrow 0
.

As a consequence, when
N\rightarrow+\infty
, the upper limit of (
3.3
) will not be strictly positive. Since (
3.2
) is always positive, we obtain

(3.4)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}\Xi_{N}(\log(-\log|s_{E}|))\{D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u,D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u\}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p}=0.

Therefore
D^{\prime}_{h^{\star}}u=0
on
X
.
∎

by a Stein neighborhood

\tilde{Z}

We denote by

\textstyle{\tilde{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Denote the exceptional locus of

of

) gives a natural map

Since

and

we have an inclusion

By assumption

\pi_{Z}^{\star}\gamma^{\star}A^{[m]}\rightarrow S^{[m]}\Omega_{\tilde{Z}}^{[p]}(\log E_{Z}).

We interrupt the proof of the lemma for the

Thus we can replace the pull-back with logarithmic poles (

As above the inclusion

then gives the inclusion

This proves Remark
4.2

4.1

Since

is smooth, the saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{C}\subset\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log E)
is locally free and a subbundle in codimension one. Thus

\mathcal{C}^{\otimes m}\subset S^{m}\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log E)

\tilde{\gamma}
is étale in the complement

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

be a normal compact complex space with lc singularities, and let
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega_{Y}^{[p]}
be a reflexive subsheaf of rank one that is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, i.e. there exists a

such that
\mathcal{A}^{[m]}
is locally free. Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\Omega^{p}_{X}(\log E)
be the saturation of

Proof.

Since pseudoeffectivity of a line bundle is invariant under taking

is pseudoeffective. Yet this follows from the non-zero morphism

\pi^{*}\mathcal{A}^{[m]}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\otimes m}

∎

We need the following proposition.

Lemma 4.4

summarized in the commutative diagram (

1.2

, and denote by
E
the exceptional locus. Since

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

.

\square

Proof of Proposition
1.4
:

Since

\square

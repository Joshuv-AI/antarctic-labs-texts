Tight Bounds for γ \gamma -Regret via the Decision-Estimation Coefficient
Margalit Glasgow, Alexander Rakhlin

Abstract
In this work, we give a statistical characterization of the γ \gamma -regret for arbitrary structured bandit problems, the regret which arises when comparing against a benchmark that is γ \gamma times the optimal solution. The γ \gamma -regret emerges in structured bandit problems over a function class ℱ \mathcal{F} where finding an exact optimum of f ∈ ℱ f\in\mathcal{F} is intractable. Our characterization is given in terms of the γ \gamma -DEC, a statistical complexity parameter for the class ℱ \mathcal{F} , which is a modification of the constrained Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) of Foster et al. 2023 (and closely related to the original offset DEC of Foster et al. 2021 ). Our lower bound shows that the γ \gamma -DEC is a fundamental limit for any model class ℱ \mathcal{F} : for any algorithm, there exists some f ∈ ℱ f\in\mathcal{F} for which the γ \gamma -regret of that algorithm scales (nearly) with the γ \gamma -DEC of ℱ \mathcal{F} . We provide an upper bound showing that there exists an algorithm attaining a nearly matching γ \gamma -regret. Due to significant challenges in applying the prior results on the DEC to the γ \gamma -regret case, both our lower and upper bounds require novel techniques and a new algorithm.

Introduction

In this work, we study the problem of structured bandits. Formally, given a known class of functions
\mathcal{F}
on some domain
\Pi
, at each round
t=1,\ldots T
, the algorithm queries some
\pi_{t}\in\Pi
and achieves a random reward
r(\pi_{t})
, where
\mathbb{E}[r(\pi_{t})]=f^{*}(\pi_{t})
for some unknown ground truth function
f^{*}\in\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{f:\Pi\to[0,1]\}
. The traditional goal in structured bandit problems is to minimize the regret

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}(T):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{\pi\in\Pi}f^{*}(\pi)-f^{*}(\pi_{t}),

(1.2)

which compares the behaviour of the algorithm to the best action for
f^{*}
. In settings where maximizing
f^{*}(\pi)
by better that a
\gamma
ratio is intractable, we consider the notion of
\gamma
-regret, originally introduced in
Kakade et al. 2007
;
Streeter and Golovin 2008

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\gamma f^{*}(\pi)-f^{*}(\pi_{t}).

This definition of regret compares the behavior of the algorithm to a benchmark which is a

. Yet, it is often feasible to find approximately optimal solutions, which attain a
\gamma

Assuming for a minute that the rewards
r(\pi_{t})
follow a normal distribution with mean
f^{*}(\pi_{t})
, each problem instance is fully described by the class
\cal F
of possible truths
f^{*}
. The determination of minimax optimal

then amounts to finding (nearly) matching upper and lower bounds
\psi,\Psi
with

\displaystyle\psi(\mathcal{F},T)~\leq~\min_{\text{Alg}}\max_{f^{*}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\right]~\leq~\Psi(\mathcal{F},T)

(1.4)

where the minimum is over all regret-minimization algorithms. To put this question in perspective, tight upper and lower bounds on minimax performance have been developed for a variety of learning problems, such as
statistical and PAC learning
—where
\psi,\Psi
are formulated in terms of the VC dimension or entropy numbers of
\mathcal{F}
—and
online learning
—where the analogous sequential complexities of
\mathcal{F}
can be shown to be necessary and sufficient.

Recently, a line of work initiated by
Foster et al. 2021
has yielded a general framework for determining the minimax regret of bandit problems (and more generally, online decision making problems such as reinforcement learning) in terms of a complexity measure called the
Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC), described in Section
2.1
. The DEC is shown in
Foster et al. 2021
;
Foster et al. 2022
;
Foster et al. 2023
;
Foster and Rakhlin 2022
to be a fundamental limit for the regret of online decision making problems, in the sense that it yields the exact optimal regret up to logarithmic factors in
T
and in the size of the model class
\mathcal{F}
. The DEC has proved to be a powerful framework for obtaining both upper and lower bounds for online decision making problems, for instance, unifying many existing results in reinforcement learning, and leading to new bounds and algorithms for contextual bandit problems. Our aim in this paper is to extend this framework to the case of
\gamma
-regret with
\gamma<1
.

In general, our understanding of
\gamma
-regret is much poorer than our understanding of the traditional regret, and to our knowledge, there are no existing lower bounds on the
\gamma
-regret. Thus while many of the above works have given algorithms upper bounding the
\gamma
-regret of various structured bandit problems, we do not know whether they achieve the optimal
\gamma
-regret. For instance, for the setting of linear optimization, the works of
Kakade et al. 2007
;
Garber 2017
;
Hazan et al. 2018
develop an online-to-offline reduction which achieves
T^{2/3}

\gamma
-regret (for minimization) in bandit settings using an offline approximate optimization oracle. However, it is possible that a
\sqrt{T}

\gamma
-regret is achievable, because in the analogous
1
-regret linear bandit setting, where an exact offline optimization oracle is available, the optimal regret is known to scale with
\sqrt{T}

(
Ito et al. 2019
)
. One reason proving lower bounds in the
\gamma
-regret settings is challenging is because at each round
t
, it is possible to achieve negative
\gamma
-regret if
f^{*}(\pi_{t})\geq\gamma\max_{\pi\in\Pi}f^{*}(\pi)
. Thus to prove a lower bound of, say,
\Delta T
on the
\gamma
-regret, it no longer suffices to show that for, say,
T/2
rounds, we have
\gamma
-regret greater than
2\Delta
, which is a standard approach in bandit lower bounds (see eg.
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
).

While the DEC appears to be a promising candidate for understanding the
\gamma
-regret, the existing results in
Foster et al. 2021
and
Foster et al. 2023
fall short of characterizing the
\gamma
-regret. Briefly, the reason for this is that the lower bounds in both works, and the upper bound in
Foster et al. 2023
, is only tight up to constant factors at best.
Thus, if we desire to show that the
\gamma
-regret is on the order of
\Delta T
– which is similar to the traditional regret being
\left(1-\gamma+\Theta(\Delta)\right)T
– any upper or lower bound which loses constant factors will only be able to show a traditional regret of
\Theta((1-\gamma+\Delta)T)
, which does not translate to any meaningful bound on the
\gamma
-regret if
\Delta=o(1)
.

Our main result is showing that a variant of the DEC, which we term the
\gamma
-DEC, does in fact characterize the
\gamma
-regret, in the sense of providing nearly matching upper and lower bounds in (
1.4
). We formally define the
\gamma
-DEC, parameterized by
\epsilon
and denoted
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}
, in Definition
1
. In the case that
\gamma=1
, the
\gamma
-DEC essentially generalizes the constrained DEC of
Foster et al. 2023
.

Specifically, our first result, Theorem
1
, lower bounds the regret by
T\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}
(for
\epsilon\approx\sqrt{1/T}
) with exact multiplicative constant
1
up to a small additive term, that depends on a certain localization parameter of the model class
\mathcal{F}
. Even in the absence of localization, for the traditional exact regret setting, our result yields an improvement upon the lower bound of
\mathsf{dec}^{1}_{\epsilon}/\Theta(\log(T))
of
Foster et al. 2023
by a logarithmic factor. More importantly, the sharp nature of the
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}
lower bound allows us to establish the corresponding lower bounds on
\gamma
-regret. The proof of Theorem
1
involves a new technique of lower bounding the regret by considering the behavior of the algorithm up to various stopping times
\tau
.

Our second result, Theorem
3
, provides an algorithm which obtains a regret of at most
T\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}
(for
\epsilon\approx\sqrt{1/T}
), matching our lower bound. This algorithm is based on the estimation-to-decision principle from prior work on the DEC, which employs a reduction from algorithms for interactive decision making (such as bandits) to online estimation algorithms, which provide an estimator of
f^{*}
based on past observations. Thus Theorem
3
assumes access to such an online estimation oracle, described in Assumption
1
. While our algorithm is similar to those in
Foster et al. 2021
;
Foster et al. 2023
, it differs by carefully tuning a certain parameter which governs the amount of exploration at each round, allowing us achieve a meaningful result in the
\gamma
-regret setting.

We emphasize that the matching upper and lower bounds (
1.4
) are obtained for any set of models (satisfying the mild assumptions stated in Section
2
) and thus constitute a complete solution to determining the minimax sample complexity for a function class
\mathcal{F}
, at least from the statistical point of view. In Section
2.4
, we mention some of the computational implications of our results, and discuss some exciting directions at the intersection of statistics and computation. Finally in Section
6
, we show how to bound the
\gamma
-DEC in several examples. As one example, we consider a bandit problem over the action space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which finding an exact optimum in
T\leq\exp(\Theta(d))
steps is impossible, but finding a
\gamma
-approximate-maximum is possible. We show how to derive a tight bound on the
\gamma
-DEC, and show that Theorem
1
can yield a tight lower bound for the
\gamma
-regret of this bandit problem. We believe that bounding the
\gamma
-DEC is a promising framework for understanding the
\gamma
-regret in more natural approximate optimization settings in the references above, and we leave to future work the question of finding the
\gamma
-DEC in such applications.

Preliminaries and Theorem Statements

2.1

Structured Bandits and the Decision-Estimation Coefficient.

We define our notation to keep with the prior line of work on the DEC. We study structured bandit problems with action space
\Pi
and a set of models
\mathcal{M}
. Each model
M\in\mathcal{M}
is a probability kernel
M:\Pi\to\Delta(\mathbb{R})
where
\Delta(\mathbb{R})
is a set of distributions on
\mathbb{R}
. The set of models induces a class of mean reward functions
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{M}\}_{M\in\mathcal{M}}
where
f_{M}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}^{M}[r|\pi]=\mathbb{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle r\sim M(\pi)}}[r]
and
\mathbb{E}^{M}
denotes the expectation under the model
M
. We also use
\mathbb{P}^{M}
to denote the probability operator under the model
M
. For a model
M\in\mathcal{M}
, we define
\pi_{M}:=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}f_{M}(\pi)
. We let
M^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
denote the true model, and the corresponding mean reward function by
f^{*}=f_{M^{*}}
. Throughout, we assume
f_{M}(\pi)\in[0,1]
for all
\pi\in\Pi,M\in\mathcal{M}
. We use
\mathsf{co}(\mathcal{M})
to denote the convex hull of a model class
\mathcal{M}
.

At each round
t=1,\ldots,T
, the algorithm chooses an action
\pi_{t}\in\Pi
. The algorithm then receives a reward
r_{t}
, where
\mathbb{E}[r_{t}|\pi_{t}]=f^{*}(\pi_{t})
, and the variance of
r_{t}
is at most
1
. Formally, we define the history
\mathcal{H}_{t}:=\{(\pi_{i},r_{i})\}_{i=1}^{t}
, and we let
\{p_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be any randomized algorithm which maps histories to distributions over
\Pi
, that is,
\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1})
.

We now define the
\gamma
-Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC), which generalizes
2
2

2

5
; since we use a squared error to
f_{\widehat{M}}
constraint instead of a squared Hellinger distance constraint, the definition stated here generalizes theirs only for the bandit setting with Gaussian noise.

the constrained DEC, recently introduced in
Foster et al. 2023
.

Definition 1

(
\gamma
-DEC)
.

For a model class
\mathcal{M}
and a reference model
\widehat{M}
, for any
\gamma\in(0,1]
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, define

\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M},\widehat{M}):=\min_{p\in\Delta(\Pi)}\max_{M\in\mathcal{M}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}\left[\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\pi)\right]\mid M\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})\right\},

(2.5)

where
\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M}):=\{M\in\mathcal{M}\cup\widehat{M}:\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}(f_{M}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\}
is set of models with mean rewards within an
L_{2}(p)
ball of radius
\epsilon
around
\widehat{M}
.
Define

\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}):=\sup_{\widehat{M}}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\cup\{\widehat{M}\},\widehat{M}),

(2.6)

where the supremum is over any probability kernel
\widehat{M}:\Pi\rightarrow\Delta(\mathbb{R})
, not necessarily in
\mathcal{M}
.

The
\gamma
-DEC quantifies the best possible instantaneous
\gamma
-regret attained by a distribution
p
over
\Pi
under the worst model
M
that is close in the sense of expected squared error to
f_{\widehat{M}}
. This quantity captures the exploration-exploitation trade-off of
\mathcal{M}
: if there exists some distribution
p
under which for each
M\in\mathcal{M}\cup\{\widehat{M}\}
either the information gain (
\mathbb{E}[(f_{M}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi))^{2}]
) is large, or the
\gamma
-regret (
\mathbb{E}[\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\pi)]
) is small, then
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}
will be small.
The work of
Foster et al. 2023
showed that the quantity
\mathsf{dec}^{1}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon=\sqrt{1/T}
characterizes the
1
-regret of interactive decision making, in the sense that
\frac{T\mathsf{dec}^{1}_{\epsilon}}{\Theta(\log(T))}\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{Reg}(T)]\leq\Theta\left(T\mathsf{dec}^{1}_{\epsilon}\log(|\mathcal{M}|)\right)
, under mild conditions. In this work, we extend these results on traditional regret to the setting of
\gamma
-regret. We refer additionally to
Foster et al. 2021
;
Foster and Rakhlin 2022
for a detailed discussion on the relationship between DEC and notions such as Posterior Sampling and the Information Ratio, Feel-Good Thompson Sampling, the modulus of continuity in statistical estimation, and further connections to the literature.

2.2

Lower Bound

Our first result, Theorem
1
, yields a constant probability lower bound on the regret in terms of the
\gamma
-DEC. In order for this theorem to be meaningful in the
\gamma
-regret setting, we will leverage the following localization property, which states that all models in
\mathcal{M}
have similar maxima.

Definition 2
.

A model class
\mathcal{M}
is
\rho
-localized if
\max_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})-\min_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})\leq\rho
.

We note that the
\gamma
-regret is monotonically decreasing with respect to taking smaller subsets of a model class. Thus, for the purposes of achieving a better lower bound for a model class with a poor localization parameter, one may wish to consider the
\gamma
-DEC of a more localized subset of the model class.

Without loss of generality, we will assume throughout that in our model class
\mathcal{M}
, we have
\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})=1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
C\geq 2
be any integer. Let
\frac{4}{T}\leq\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{3C\sqrt{T}}
.
Let
\Delta:=\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is
\rho
-localized for some
\rho\geq 6C^{2}\epsilon
and that
\Delta\geq 6C^{2}\epsilon
. Then for any algorithm, for some
M\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\geq\Delta-\frac{3\min(\rho,\Delta+1-\gamma)}{C}\right]\geq\frac{1}{3C}.

Here the probability is over the randomness of the algorithm and variance-1 Gaussian noise in the rewards.

Remark 1
.

If we invoke Theorem
1
with
\rho=1
and
\gamma=1
(that is, without any localization assumption on
\mathcal{M}
, and with the constrained DEC as defined in
Foster et al. 2023
), and choose
C=6
, we achieve that for some
M\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathsf{Reg}(T)\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}\right]\geq\frac{1}{18}
, implying that
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{Reg}(T)\right]\geq\frac{\Delta}{36}
. This improves upon the lower bound in
Foster et al. 2023
, which yields
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{Reg}(T)\right]\geq\frac{\Delta}{\Theta(\log(T))}
, by removing the log factors. Remarkably, for model classes
\mathcal{M}
with the same value
f_{M}(\pi_{M})
for all
M\in\mathcal{M}
, the lower bound has a sharp factor of
1
in front of the DEC, up to additive terms that scale with
\epsilon
.

Upper Bounds

Having established a sharp lower bound for (
1.4
) in terms of
\gamma
-DEC, we now turn to the question of upper bounds. We prove two upper bounds on the regret in terms of the
\gamma
-DEC. Our first upper bound, Theorem
2

Similarly to the algorithms in

2.4

Computational Perspective

While our results thus far have been statistical — yielding (nearly) matching upper and lower bounds on the minimax optimal
\gamma
-regret — it is natural to consider whether there is a computational analog of our results. In particular, we can ask the following two questions.

Question 1
.

In settings where it is computationally tractable to obtain a
\gamma
-regret of
\Delta T
, is it always possible to efficiently find a distribution
p
that upper bounds the
\gamma
-DEC by nearly
\Delta
?

Question 2
.

Conversely, if it is possible to efficiently construct a distribution
p
which upper bounds the
\gamma
-DEC by
\Delta
, can we provide an efficient algorithm which bounds the
\gamma
-Regret by nearly
\Delta T
?

These questions are particularly relevant in the
\gamma
-regret setting, where often times the barrier to obtaining sublinear traditional regret is computational, not statistical. If the answers were affirmative, it would suggest that upper bounding the
\gamma
-DEC efficiently is (nearly) equivalent to efficiently minimizing
\gamma
-regret.

While the current tools in this work are not yet sufficient to answer these questions in a completely satisfying way, we state the following computational corollaries to our results, which begin to give a picture of computational significance of the DEC. To state these results, we define a computational analog of the
\gamma
-DEC.

Definition 3
.

We say an algorithm
A
upper bounds
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}(\mathcal{M})
by
\Delta
in time
R
, if with query access to
f_{\widehat{M}}
for any model
\widehat{M}\in\mathsf{co}(\mathcal{M})
, in time
R
, with probability
1-\delta
,
A
can provide a random sample from a distribution
p
for which

\displaystyle\max_{M\in\mathcal{M}\cup\{\widehat{M}\}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}\left[\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\pi)\right]\mid M\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})\right\}\leq\Delta.

(2.9)

We abuse notation and define
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}(\mathcal{M},(R,\delta))
to be the best upper bound on
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}
by any algorithm
A
that runs in time
R
and succeeds with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Definition 4
.

We say a bandit algorithm
\{p_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
, where
p_{t}:(\Pi\times\mathbb{R})^{t-1}\rightarrow\Delta(\Pi)
maps histories
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
to distributions over
\Pi
, runs in time
R
if for all
t
, on any history
\mathcal{H}_{T}\in(\Pi\times\mathbb{R})^{T}
, the total runtime of
p_{t}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1})
for all
t\leq T
is at most
R
. Here
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is defined to be the first
t
pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{T}
.

We prove the following corollary of our lower bound. It requires the following condition.

Assumption 4

(Efficient Optimization)
.

With query access to any
f_{\widehat{M}}
for
\widehat{M}\in\mathsf{co}(\mathcal{M})
, there is an algorithm which runs in time
R
which outputs some
\hat{\pi}
with
f_{\widehat{M}}(\hat{\pi})\geq f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}})-\Delta/10
.

Corollary 1

(Computational Corollary to Theorem
1
)
.

Let
C\geq 2
be any integer, and let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{3C\sqrt{T}}
and
\delta>0
.
Suppose
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M},(2R,\delta))\geq\frac{11}{10}\Delta
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is
\rho
-localized for some
\rho\geq 6C^{2}\epsilon
. Assume also that
\Delta\geq 6C^{2}\epsilon
. Then if Assumption
4
holds, for any algorithm
\{p_{t}\}
which runs in time
\frac{R}{50C^{2}\log(C/\delta)}
, for some
M\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\geq\Delta-\frac{3\min(\rho,\Delta+1-\gamma)}{C}\right]\geq\frac{1}{6C}.

Here the probabilities are over the randomness of the algorithm and variance-1 Gaussian noise in the rewards.

Corollary
1
gives a lower bound on the regret of any efficient algorithm in terms of the computation lower bound on the
\gamma
-DEC. The contrapositive of this corollary provides a conditional answer to Question
1
: if there is an efficient
\gamma
-regret minimizing algorithm, then conditional on it being efficient to find a near-optimal maximizer of
f_{\widehat{M}}
for any
\widehat{M}\in\mathsf{co}(\mathcal{M})
(Assumption
4
), it is efficient to find a distribution
p
upper bounding the
\gamma
-DEC with query access to
f_{\widehat{M}}
. This answer is somewhat unsatisfying in the
\gamma\neq 1
case, because it would be impractical to assume Assumption
4
. However, in the case that
\gamma=1
, Assumption
4
is indeed necessary for efficiently upper bounding the DEC by
\Delta/10
, because we always have
\widehat{M}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})
. Thus, under Assumption
4
, the answer to Question
1
is yes; otherwise, it is no. Corollary
1
is proved in Section
7
.

We now turn to the converse, Question
2
. We can prove the following corollary to our upper bound in Theorem
2
. We focus on our attention on Theorem
2
, which only yields a matching bound when
\gamma=1
, because the result is simpler, and as previously discussed for the lower bound, our understanding of the computational landscape is limited for
\gamma\neq 1
.

Corollary 2

(Computational Corollary to Theorem
2
)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and Assumption
2
holds for some function
\Delta_{\epsilon}\geq\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M},(R,\delta))
. With probability
1-\delta(T+1)
, Algorithm
1
runs in time
RT
and makes
T
oracle calls to the online estimation oracle (Assumption
1
), and attains regret

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\leq(1-\gamma)T+2T\Delta_{\epsilon}+9\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)},

(2.10)

where
\epsilon=\sqrt{24\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)/T}
.

Corollary
2
provides a conditional answer to Question
2
in the case that
\gamma=1
. It answers affirmatively whenever there is an efficient online optimization oracle satisfying Assumption
1
. (More precisely, the online algorithm implementing Assumption
1
should efficiently output query access to
f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}
). Corollary
2
is immediate from examining Algorithm
1
and Theorem
2
, so we omit its proof.

We leave it as an exciting open direction to provide satisfying answers to Questions
1
and
2
for the
\gamma\neq 1
case.

3

Technical Overview of Lower Bound (Theorem
1
)

We present here a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1
. For simplicity, in this section we consider the case when
\gamma=1
, since the main idea of our technique is clearer in this setting.

Fix an algorithm
\{p_{t}\}
, and for any model
M
, define
g_{M}(\pi):=f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\pi)
. Let
\widehat{M}
be the model attaining the constrained DEC in (
2.6
), that is, for any distribution
p
on
\Pi
, for some model
M\in\mathcal{M}\cup\{\widehat{M}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}[g_{M}(\pi)]\geq\Delta,

(3.11)

and
\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}[(f_{M}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi))^{2}]\leq\epsilon^{2}.

A typical approach (see eg.
Foster et al. 2021
;
Foster et al. 2023
) is to invoke the lower bound (
3.11
) on the distribution
p=p_{\widehat{M}}
, defined to be the average distribution of actions played over all
T
steps by the algorithm
\{p_{t}\}
under the ground truth
\widehat{M}
. However, this approach results in the loss of constant factors, and only suffices if
\widehat{M}
is contained in the model class
\mathcal{M}
. To mitigate these issues, we will find a different distribution to invoke the DEC lower bound on, one which we know will result in
M
not being
\widehat{M}
(and thus necessarily
M\in\mathcal{M}
). To construct such a distribution, we consider the algorithm
\{\overline{p}_{t}\}
, which plays algorithm
\{p_{t}\}
until the stopping time
\tau
, and then plays
\pi_{\widehat{M}}
for the rest of the
T-\tau
steps. Assuming the stopping time
\tau
occurs before
\widehat{M}
has accrued
\Delta T
regret, it will be impossible for
\widehat{M}
to attain the DEC. Thus our goal will be to show that when we invoke the DEC on
\overline{p}_{\widehat{M}}
(the distribution of
\overline{p}_{t}
averaged over all
T
rounds), the model
M
maximizing the DEC attains, with constant probability, a regret close to
\Delta T
.

For any model
\tilde{M}
, recall that
\mathbb{P}^{\tilde{M}}
denotes the probability operator under the ground truth
\tilde{M}
, and algorithm
\{p_{t}\}
. Similarly, define
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\tilde{M}}
to be the probability operator under the ground truth
\tilde{M}
, and algorithm
\{\overline{p}_{t}\}
. Define
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{M}}
and
\overline{\mathbb{E}}^{\tilde{M}}
to be the respective expectation operators. Since the regret is strictly increasing, if for some
\mu
close to
\Delta
we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\geq\mu\right]\geq\Omega(1),

(3.12)

then it will follow that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Reg}(T)\geq\mu\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\geq\mu\right]

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\geq\mu\right]-D_{TV}(\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}},\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{M}).

(3.14)

Crucially, observe that it suffices to bound the total variation distance between
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}
and
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
(as opposed to between
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}
and
\mathbb{P}^{M}
), because the events in question are only determined by events up to time
\tau
, and
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
and
\mathbb{P}^{M}
are identical up to time
\tau
. By standard argument (eg. using Pinkser’s inequality), one can use the square error constraint of the DEC to bound the total variation distance
D_{TV}(\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}},\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{M})
by an arbitrarily small constant. Thus it suffices to show Equation (
3.12
) for the desired value
\mu
.

To prove Equation (
3.12
), we will define the stopping time
\tau
to be the first step
t
at which
\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{s})\geq aT
for some
a<\Delta
. Let
M
be the model in (
2.5
) that maximizes the DEC for
p=\overline{p}_{\widehat{M}}
. The key observation is that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\approx\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})+f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}}).

(3.15)

Such an equivalence holds (with high probability) because the square error constraint in the definition of DEC ensures that most of the time,
\sum_{t}|f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})-f_{M}(\pi_{t})|
is small.
We obtain two observations from this equation. First, on the event that
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in[a^{\prime},a)
, we have
\tau=T
, and thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\gtrapprox a^{\prime}+f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}}).

(3.16)

Secondly, we can show that the gap
f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}})
is large, in particular, nearly as large as
\Delta-a
. This holds because, taking the expectation of Equation (
3.15
) under
\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\lessapprox\overline{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\right]+f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}})

(3.17)

\displaystyle\lessapprox a+f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}}).

(3.18)

Indeed, by definition of the algorithm
\overline{p}
,
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})
never exceeds
a
(by more than a smidge). Thus since by definition of the DEC, we have
\overline{\mathbb{E}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\overline{p}_{\widehat{M}}}[g_{M}(\pi)]\geq\Delta
, it follows that the gap
f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}})\gtrapprox\Delta-a
.

Combining these observations, we have that
for any
0\leq a^{\prime}<a<\Delta
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\gtrapprox\Delta-a+a^{\prime}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in[a^{\prime},a)\right],

(3.19)

It remains to choose the values of
a
and
a^{\prime}
to apply this approach to. If
a-a^{\prime}
is small, then the regret guarantee will be good, but the probability on the right hand side may be small. Fortunately however, since
\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in[0,1]\right]=1
, we can divide up the interval
[0,1]
into a constant number of shorter intervals
I_{i}
, and claim that the probability
\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in I_{i}\right]
is
\Omega(1)
for one of them. Formally, for some integer
C
, let

1.

I_{i}=[a_{i}^{\prime},a_{i}):=\left[\frac{i-1}{C}\min(\Delta,\rho),\frac{i}{C}\min(\Delta,\rho)\right)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,C-1
.

2.

I_{C}:=\left[\frac{i-1}{C}\min(\Delta,\rho),1\right]

For any of the first
i-1
intervals, we have
\Delta+a_{i}^{\prime}-a_{i}\geq\Delta-\frac{\min(\Delta,\rho)}{C}
; thus Equation (
3.19
) yields the desired regret with probability
\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in I_{i}\right]
for some model
M
(which may depend on
a_{i}
).
To handle the final interval, we will need to prove a modification of Equation (
3.19
), which shows that
for any
0\leq a^{\prime}<a<\Delta
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g_{M}(\pi_{t})\gtrapprox\min\left(\Delta+a^{\prime}-\min(\rho,\Delta),a\right)\right]\geq\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\geq a^{\prime}\right].

(3.20)

Thus by choosing
a\approx\Delta
, and
a^{\prime}=\frac{C-1}{C}\min(\rho,\Delta)
, this shows that we achieve the desired regret with probability
\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{M}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})\in I_{C}\right]
for some model
M
.

This modification is again derived from Equation (
3.15
), but it requires an additional argument using the localization assumption that
f_{M}(\pi_{M})\geq 1-\rho
, to obtain that
f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}})\gtrapprox\Delta-\rho
. We omit the details.

The full proof of Theorem
1
appears in Section
7
.

4

Technical Overview of Upper Bounds (Theorems
2
and
3
)

Before giving an overview of the key ideas in the proofs of our upper bounds, we note the following observation which will simplify our discussion.

Observation 1
.

If
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)
, then we can bound the regret as:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{t})+\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}|f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{t})-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{t})|

(4.21)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{t})+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(4.22)

Here the second inequality follow from applying Jensen’s inequality on the distribution
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}
.

Since our estimation oracle (Assumption
1
) guarantees that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)
with probability
1-\delta
, this observation shows that it suffices to bound
\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{t})
.

4.1

Proof Overview of Theorem
2

Theorem
2
provides a regret guarantee for Algorithm
1
. Recall that at each round
t
, Algorithm
1
queries the estimation oracle to gain a model
\widehat{M}_{t}
, and then plays
\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}
, where
p_{t}
minimizes
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
. Recall that the guarantee of the
\gamma
-DEC is that if we choose an action from the distribution
p_{t}
which minimizes
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}
, then if
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{t})\geq\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{t}}^{\gamma}.

(4.23)

Thus, if we knew that at each round
t
, we had
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
, we would immediately get a bound of
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{t}}^{\gamma}
on the regret, which would remove the
(1-\gamma)T
term in Theorem
2
.

Unfortunately, it is not always the case that
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
; for the schedule of the
\epsilon_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{8\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{t}}
given in Algorithm
1
, we may have
M^{*}\notin\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
for a constant fraction of the rounds
t
. This issue is unavoidable, because the estimator
\widehat{M}_{t}
can be arbitrarily bad at some rounds without violating Assumption
1
. Thus we need some way of bounding the regret in the rounds where
M^{*}\notin\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
. The main step of our proof is showing that for all
t
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})\geq\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{\lfloor{t/4}\rfloor}}^{\gamma}-O\left(\sqrt{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)/t}\right).

(4.24)

Since
\widehat{M}_{t}
is always in the ball
\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
, the
\gamma
-DEC guarantees that we have good regret relative to
\widehat{M}_{t}
at each round:

\displaystyle\gamma f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{t})\leq\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}.

(4.25)

Combining Equations (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) and Observation
1
essentially yields Theorem
2
. The growth condition on the DEC (Assumption
2
) helps to bound the sum of the
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}
terms.
These ideas suffice to prove Theorem
2
, which is proved formally in Section
8
.

Our technique in proving Equation (
4.24
) is similar to the approach in
Foster et al. 2023
, where we leverage the fact Assumption
1
guarantees that
\widehat{M}_{t}
is in the convex hull of a certain refined model class,
\mathcal{M}_{t-1}
. Indeed, Assumption
1
guarantees that
M_{t-1}
only includes models on which the estimation error thus far has been small, that is,
\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{s}}(f_{\widehat{M}_{s}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)
.

Now we can argue that any model in the convex hull of
\mathcal{M}_{t-1}
has a large optimum. By the estimation error guarantee, there must exist a large set
S
of rounds in the range
s\in[t/2,t-1]
on which
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{s},\epsilon_{s}}(\widehat{M}_{s})
for
s\in S
. Construct the distribution

\hat{p}:=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}p_{s}.

We can write any
\widehat{M}_{t}\in\mathsf{co}(\mathcal{M}_{t-1})
as a distribution
\nu
over models in
\mathcal{M}_{t-1}
, that is,
\widehat{M}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{M\sim\nu}M
. We have

\displaystyle f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}\left[f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)\right]

(4.26)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{M\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}\left[f_{M}(\pi)\right]

(4.27)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}\left[f_{M^{*}}(\pi)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{M\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}|f_{M}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi)|.

(4.28)

First, because for all
s\in S
, we have
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{s},\epsilon_{s}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
, the
\gamma
-DEC guarantees that for the first term, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}\left[f_{M^{*}}(\pi)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\min_{s\in S}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{s}}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t/2}}.

(4.30)

Second, because all the
M
in the support of
\widehat{M}_{t}
are in
\mathcal{M}_{t-1}
, their estimation error during all rounds in
S
must be less than
\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)
, and thus we are able to prove that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim\hat{p}}|f_{M}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi)|\leq O\left(\sqrt{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)/t}\right).

(4.31)

Putting these two steps together yields Equation (
4.24
).

We formally prove Theorem
2
in Section
8
.

4.2

Proof Overview of Theorem
3

In this section, we sketch some of the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
3
, which analyzes the
\gamma
-regret of Algorithm
3
.
Unfortunately, this reduction to calling the guarantee of the DEC on
\widehat{M}_{t}
instead of
M^{*}
leads to an additional
(1-\gamma)T
term in the regret, which is problematic in the case when
\gamma<1
. Thus we need some additional ideas to prove Theorem
3
.
The key difference between Algorithm
3
and Algorithm
1
is that in Algorithm
3
, the value
\epsilon_{t}
is chosen in a very careful manner.

The motivation for Algorithm
3
comes from the idea that we should predict if the estimator
\widehat{M}_{t}
is “bad”, and if so, we should choose a distribution which explores more by choosing a large
\epsilon_{t}
, rather than exploiting by choosing a small
\epsilon_{t}
. Exploring more corresponds to choosing a larger value for
\epsilon_{t}
, because if
M^{*}\notin\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}(\widehat{M}_{t})
, we will have learned a lot about
M^{*}
.

It turns out that for a given
\epsilon
, if we know the value of the optimum
f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
, we can predict whether
M^{*}
will be excluded from the ball
\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})
, where
p
is the distribution that minimizes
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}(\widehat{M})
.
With a further continuity assumption (Assumption
3
), we can use this knowledge to choose a good value for
\epsilon_{t}
at each round, and attain an ideal trade-off between exploration and exploitation. To understand this more quantitatively, we define the following function
g
which will be used in Algorithm
3
to choose the value of
\epsilon_{t}
.

Definition 5
.

For any
\widehat{M}
and
\epsilon
, define
p(\epsilon,\widehat{M})
to be the distribution guaranteed by Assumption
3
. Define the function

\displaystyle g(\epsilon,\widehat{M},v):=\gamma v-\mathbb{E}_{p(\epsilon,\widehat{M})}f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-2\epsilon-\Delta_{\epsilon},

(4.32)

We can prove the following lemma, which is the key idea in the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose Assumption
3
holds. For any
\widehat{M}
and
v\in[0,1]
, there exists some
\epsilon\geq 0
such that
g(\epsilon,\widehat{M},v)\leq 0
. Let
\epsilon^{*}:=\arg\min_{\epsilon\geq 0}g(\epsilon,\widehat{M},v)\leq 0
, and let
p^{*}:=p(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M})
as in Assumption
3
. Then:

1.

Exploitation:
We have
\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p*}[f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)]\geq\gamma v-2\epsilon-\Delta_{\epsilon^{*}}
.

2.

Exploration:
For any
M\in\mathcal{M}
with
f_{M}(\pi_{M})\geq v
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{p*}(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}\geq(\epsilon^{*})^{2}
.

Proof.
First observe that if
\epsilon\geq 1/2
, then we will have
g(\epsilon,\mathcal{M},v)\leq 0
. Second observe that by Assumption
3
, the function
g(\epsilon,\widehat{M},v)
is continuous in
\epsilon
, and thus this argmin
\epsilon^{*}
exists. In particular, either
\epsilon^{*}=0
, or
g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v)=0
.

Now the first item of the Lemma follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p*}[f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)]

\displaystyle=\gamma v-2\epsilon^{*}-\Delta_{\epsilon^{*}}-g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v),

(4.33)

and
g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v)\leq 0
. For the second item, observe that if
M\in\mathcal{B}_{p^{*},\epsilon^{*}}
, then since
p^{*}
bounds the
\gamma
-DEC by
\Delta_{\epsilon}
, we must have

\displaystyle\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-\mathbb{E}_{p^{*}}f_{M}(\pi)\leq\Delta_{\epsilon^{*}},

(4.34)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p^{*}}|f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi)|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{p*}[(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}]}\leq\epsilon^{*}.

(4.35)

These two together imply that

\displaystyle g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v)

\displaystyle=\gamma v-\mathbb{E}_{p^{*}}f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-2\epsilon^{*}-\Delta_{\epsilon^{*}}

(4.36)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma v-\mathbb{E}_{p^{*}}f_{M}(\pi)-\epsilon^{*}-\Delta_{\epsilon^{*}}

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(v-f_{M}(\pi_{M}))-\epsilon^{*}

(4.38)

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon^{*}.

(4.39)

If
\epsilon^{*}>0
, then this would imply that
g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v)<0
, which contradicts that fact that
g(\epsilon^{*},\widehat{M},v)=0
exactly. If
\epsilon^{*}=0
, it is immediate that
\mathbb{E}_{p*}(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}\geq(\epsilon^{*})^{2}
. Thus in either case we have
\mathbb{E}_{p*}(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}\geq(\epsilon^{*})^{2}
as desired.
∎

Algorithm 2

Simplified version of Algorithm
3

input:
Parameter
\gamma\in[0,1]
, the optimum value
f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
.

for

t=1,\ldots,T

do

Let
\widehat{M}_{t}=\mathsf{AlgEst}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1})
.

Let
\epsilon_{t}:=\min_{\epsilon}:g(\epsilon,\widehat{M}_{t},f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}}))\leq 0
, where

is defined in Definition
5
.

Play
\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}
where
p_{t}:=p(\epsilon_{t},\widehat{M}_{t})
is the distribution guaranteed by Assumption
3
.

To understand how to leverage Lemma
1
, we will analyze in this section a simplified setting where we assume the algorithm has knowledge of
f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
. Of course in Algorithm
3
, we do not know this maximum, so we will need to use some proxy for it. We state the simplification of Algorithm
3
in Algorithm
2
. In Algorithm
2
, at each round
t
, we obtain from the estimation oracle an estimate
\widehat{M}_{t}
of the model. We then choose
\epsilon_{t}
as in Lemma
1
to be the minimum value of
\epsilon
such that
g(\epsilon,\widehat{M}_{t},f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}}))\leq 0
. Finally, we play

to be the distribution from Assumption

that upper bounds the

-DEC.

By playing this

, Lemma
1
(with

, where we define the open ball
\mathcal{B}^{o}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M}):=\{M:\mathbb{E}_{p}(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M}(\pi))^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\}
. While it may seem counter intuitive that we want to choose
(\epsilon_{t},p_{t})
such that we have
M^{*}\notin\mathcal{B}^{o}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}
the first bullet of Lemma
1
guarantees that we get as good of a regret guarantee for this
\epsilon_{t}
as we would if we have
M^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{t},\epsilon_{t}}
. Because
M^{*}
is excluded from the
L_{2}
ball, by the guarantee of our estimation oracle, the
\epsilon_{t}
cannot be too large at each round, that is, we must have with probability
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\epsilon_{t}^{2}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}}(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{t})-f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{t}))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta).

(4.40)

Putting together Lemma
1
and this bound on
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\epsilon_{t}^{2}
, we can prove the following bound on the regret of Algorithm
2
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
hold, such that
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}\leq\Delta_{\epsilon}
. Suppose additionally that the value
f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
is known. Then with probability
1-\delta
, Algorithm
2
attains the regret

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\leq T\Delta_{\sqrt{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)/T}}+3\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(4.41)

Proof.
Condition on the event that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta),

(4.42)

which occurs with probability
1-\delta

T

, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{M^{*}}(\pi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(4.43)

By item 1 of Lemma

, applied with

\displaystyle\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)\leq 2\epsilon_{t}-\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}.

(4.44)

Now, by item 2 of Lemma

\mathbb{E}_{p(\epsilon_{t},\widehat{M}_{t})}[(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi))^{2}]\geq\epsilon_{t}^{2}

. Thus our final regret is at most:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}2\epsilon_{t}+\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{T},\|x\|^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}\sum_{i}\left(2x_{i}+\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\right)+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}

(4.45)

(4.46)

Applying Holder’s inequality (see Lemma
3

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{T},\|x\|^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}\sum_{i}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\leq T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{T}}},

(4.47)

which yields the proposition.
∎

5

Comparison to other works on the DEC.

Other definitions of the DEC.

The work of
Foster et al. 2023
, which defined the “constrained” DEC (which the
\gamma
-DEC is based on) follows the earlier work of
Foster et al. 2021
, which originally introduced the notion of a decision-estimation coefficient. This original work defined the DEC (or offset DEC) as
\mathsf{dec}_{\lambda}:=\min_{p}\max_{M}f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\pi)-\lambda\mathbb{E}[D_{H}^{2}(M(\pi),\widehat{M}(\pi))]
, where
D_{H}
is the Hellinger distance. This offset DEC yielded bounds of the form
\Theta\left(\inf_{\lambda}(T\mathsf{dec}_{\lambda}+\lambda)\right)\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{Reg}(T)]\leq\Theta\left(\inf_{\lambda}(T\mathsf{dec}_{\lambda}+\lambda\log(|\mathcal{M}|))\right)
, though under much more stringent conditions on the class
\mathcal{M}
for the lower bound.

Extension to Decision Making with Structured Observations

For simplicity, we stated Theorem
1
for bandits with Gaussian noise, but our lower bound can be extended to the more general setting of Decision Making with Structured Observations (see
Foster et al. 2021
;
Foster et al. 2023
; this also encompasses reinforcement learning and MDPs). If the density ratios
\frac{M(\pi)}{\widehat{M}(\pi)}
are bounded, then we achieve the same result as Theorem
1
if we define the
\gamma
-DEC using a constraint on the squared Hellinger Distance,
\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}D_{H}^{2}(\widehat{M}(\pi),M(\pi))\leq\epsilon^{2}
instead of the squared error to
f_{\widehat{M}}
constraint. If the density ratios are unbounded, the same result as Theorem
1
holds, using the
\gamma
-DEC with the squared Hellinger distance constraint, under a slightly stronger condition that
\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}\log(T)}
. (See
Foster et al. 2021
). Our upper bounds (Theorems
2
and
3
) can be similarly extended to the general Decision Making with Structured Observations setting by using a definition of the
\gamma
-DEC with a squared Hellinger constraint.

We remark that with this Hellinger constraint modification, for the case that
\gamma=1
, our Theorem
2
recovers the same result as the regret upper bound in
Foster et al. 2023
. To simplify the exposition in our paper, we have strengthened two of the assumptions in their paper (the regularity condition in Assumption
2
is stronger than the regularity assumption they assume), and we have assumed a slightly different estimation oracle (as per Assumption
1
) instead of their constrained estimation oracle assumption. However, our Algorithm
1
can be (very minorly) modified to be run in epochs of doubling size to work with their oracle, and our proof can be (very slightly) modified to hold under their regularity condition. Making these changes recovers their result with a much simpler algorithm.

Examples

6.1

Example of
1
-DEC for the Multi-armed Bandit (MAB).

In the following proposition, we construct a distribution
p
that near-optimally upper bounds
1
-DEC in the setting where
\mathcal{M}
is the class of MAB problems with
K
-arms and mean rewards in
[0,1]
. We provide this example to show that in this simple setting, it is possible to satisfy Assumption
3
by upper bounding the
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}
by a distribution
p
that is continuous in
\epsilon
.

Proposition 2
.

Fix any
\epsilon>0
and reference model
\widehat{M}
on
k
-arms with mean reward
f_{\widehat{M}}(i)
for
i\in[K]
. Define the distribution
p
on
[K]
as

\displaystyle p(i)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\lambda+\max_{j}f_{\widehat{M}}(j)-f_{\widehat{M}}(i))^{2}},

(6.48)

where
\lambda
is chosen such that
\sum_{i}p(i)=1
.
Then for any
M\in\mathcal{M}\cup\{\widehat{M}\}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}(f_{\widehat{M}}(i)-f_{M}(i))^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(i)]\leq(\sqrt{K}+1)\epsilon.

(6.49)

This distribution near-optimally bounds the DEC for the
K
-armed MAB. Indeed, it is straightforward to check that the
\mathsf{dec}^{1}(\mathcal{M})
grows roughly like
\epsilon\sqrt{K}
:

Lemma 2
.

For the
K
-armed MAB model class
\mathcal{M}
, if
K\geq 2
and
\epsilon\leq 1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{1}(\mathcal{M})\geq\min\left(\epsilon\sqrt{K-1},1/4\right).

(6.50)

Proof of Proposition
2
.

First observe that if
\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}(f_{\widehat{M}}(i)-f_{M}(i))^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}
, we have by Jenson’s inequality
\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}|f_{\widehat{M}}(i)-f_{M}(i)|\leq\epsilon
, and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(i)]\leq\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)]+\epsilon.

(6.51)

Further, letting
i^{*}:=\arg\max_{i}f_{\widehat{M}}(i)
, since
\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}(f_{\widehat{M}}(i)-f_{M}(i))^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}
, for all
i
, we must have

\displaystyle|f_{M}(i)-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{p(i)}}=\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i),

(6.52)

and so

\displaystyle f_{M}(\pi_{M})\leq f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{M})+\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi_{M})=\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*}).

(6.53)

Now we can compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p(i)\left(f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)\right)

(6.54)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}p(i)\left(\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)\right)

(6.55)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)}

(6.56)

Now we know
\lambda
has to be large enough such that
\sum_{i}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i))^{2}}\leq 1
.

Reparameterize such that
b_{i}:=\lambda+f_{\widehat{M}}(i^{*})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)
. Then we need to solve the following optimization problem over
\{b_{i}\geq 0\}_{i\in[K]}
:

\displaystyle\max\sum_{i}\frac{1}{b_{i}},\text{ such that }\>\sum_{i}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{b_{i}^{2}}\leq 1.

(6.57)

The maximum is attained when all
b_{i}=\epsilon\sqrt{K}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{\widehat{M}}(i)]\leq\epsilon^{2}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{b_{i}}=\epsilon\sqrt{K}.

(6.58)

Combining with Equation (
6.51
) yields the proposition.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Let
\widehat{M}
be the model with
f_{\widehat{M}}(i)=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}}
for all
i\in[K]
. Fix any distribution
p
on
[K]
, and denote
p_{i}:=p(i)
for
i\in[K]
. Let
j:=\arg\min_{i\in[K]}p_{i}
, such that
p_{j}\leq 1/K
. Let
M
be the model with

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f_{M}(i)=\min\left(1,\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}}+\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}}\right)&i=j\\
f_{M}(i)=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}}-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}}&i\neq j,\end{cases}

(6.59)

such that
f_{M}(i)\in[0,1]
for all
i
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}(f_{\widehat{M}}(i)-f_{M}(i))^{2}=(1-p_{j})\epsilon^{2}\frac{p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}+p_{j}\epsilon^{2}\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}=\epsilon^{2},

(6.60)

and

\displaystyle f_{M}(j)-\mathbb{E}_{p}f_{M}(j)=(1-p_{j})\left(\min\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}},\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}}\right)+\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}}\right).

(6.61)

If
\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}}+\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}}\leq 1
, then the regret under
p
equals

\displaystyle(1-p_{j})\epsilon\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}}+\sqrt{\frac{p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}}\right)=\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{j}}{p_{j}}}\geq\epsilon\sqrt{K-1}.

(6.62)

Otherwise the regret under
p
is at least
(1-p_{j})(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{K-1}})\geq 1/4
since
\epsilon\leq 1/2
.

∎

6.2

Example of
\gamma
-Regret bounds via the
\gamma
-DEC

In this section, we give an example of a bandit problem for which whenever
T
is sub-exponential in the dimension of the action space, we can prove nearly matching upper and lower bounds on the
\gamma
-regret. This example is constructed by linearly combining two problems in the proportions
\gamma
and
1-\gamma
: a standard K-armed MAB, and a bandit problem where the feedback is given by a shifted ReLU function of
w^{T}\pi
, where
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the ground truth, and
\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the action. While the
K
-MAB problem is easy to solve with
\sqrt{KT}
regret, the shifted ReLU problem cannot be solved with less than exponentially in
d
many steps, and thus the regret is
\exp(\Theta(d))\sqrt{T}
. If we constrain
T
to be less than
\exp(\Theta(d))
, then we will see that the
\gamma
-regret of the example is equal to the regret of the
K
-MAB problem.

While this example is artificial, there may be more natural settings where a similar phenomena occur where some part of the function is easy to maximize, and another part is not. For example,
Rajaraman et al. 2023
studies a setting where there is an initial “burn-in” phase where the regret is large, and then a second learning phase where the regret grows less quickly as a function of
T
. This parallels our setting where initially we can only achieve a sublinear regret against a
\gamma
-benchmark, but when
T
becomes exponentially large, we can achieve sublinear regret.

Example 1
.

For any
d,K
, consider the following model class
\mathcal{M}
with the action set
\Pi:=\{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\in\{-1,1\}^{d}\cup\bot\times[K]\}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be parameterized by
\{(w,v)\in\{-1,1\}^{d}\times[0,1]^{K}\}
, and define

\displaystyle f_{(w,v)}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)=(1-\gamma)h_{w}(\pi^{\prime})+\gamma g_{v}(\pi),

(6.63)

where
g_{v}(\pi)=v_{\pi}
and

\displaystyle h_{w}(\pi^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{d}\max\left(0,\langle\pi^{\prime},w\rangle-\frac{d}{2}\right)&\pi^{\prime}\neq\bot\\
0&\pi^{\prime}=\bot.\end{cases}

(6.64)

It is straightforward to see that the function
h_{w}
is hard to maximize in less that
\exp(\Theta(d))
queries, since finding any
\pi^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
with
\langle\pi^{\prime},w\rangle>\frac{d}{2}
with high probability requires
\exp(\Theta(d))
queries. However, it is possible to maximize
g_{v}
by any standard MAB algorithm. Thus if we find
\text{argmax}\>v
but not
w
, we can achieve
\gamma
of the optimum, which makes
\gamma
a natural approximation ratio for this model class.

We proceed to prove upper and lower bounds on the
\gamma
-regret for this model class. The bounds are matching up to
\log(K)
factors.

Proposition 3

(Upper bound for Example
1
)
.

We have
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}\leq\gamma(\sqrt{K}+1)\epsilon
.
Further, for some algorithm, for any
T\geq 1
, we have
\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\leq 6\sqrt{KT\log(K)}
with probability at least
1-\frac{1}{k}
.

Proof.
We first bound the
\gamma
-DEC. For any reference model
\widehat{M}
, let
\widehat{M}_{1}
be a model over the action space
[K]
given by
\widehat{M}_{1}(\pi)=\widehat{M}(\perp,\pi)
.

For any
\epsilon
, let
p=p(\epsilon,\widehat{M})
be the distribution on
\Pi
which returns a sample
(\perp,\pi)
, where
\pi\sim q(\epsilon,\widehat{M}_{1})
, where
q(\epsilon,\widehat{M}_{1})
is any distribution upper bounding the
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{1}
by
(\sqrt{K}+1)\epsilon
for the
K
-MAB problem. Such a distribution exists by Proposition
2
.

Then for any
M\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})
with mean
f_{w,v}
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma f_{w,v}((w,v))-\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\prime},\pi\sim p}[f_{w,v}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(1-\gamma)h_{w}(w)+\gamma^{2}g_{v}(v)-\gamma\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim q}[g_{v}(\pi)]

(6.65)

\displaystyle=\gamma(1-\gamma)\left(f_{w,v}((w,v))-g_{v}(v)\right)+\gamma\left(g_{v}(v)-\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim q}[g_{v}(\pi)]\right)

(6.66)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(g_{v}(v)-\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim q}[g_{v}(\pi)]\right)

(6.67)

\displaystyle\gamma(\sqrt{K}+1)\epsilon,

(6.68)

where the last line follows because the MAB model with mean
g_{v}
must be inside
\mathcal{B}_{q,\epsilon}(\widehat{M}_{1})
, otherwise it would be impossible to have
M\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})
.

To bound
\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)
, we could apply Theorem
3
with an estimation oracle satisfying Assumption
1
with
\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)=\tilde{O}(K)
(see
Foster et al. 2021
, which guarantees such an oracle), which would yield a regret guarantee of
\tilde{O}(K\sqrt{T})
.

However, since this is not tight, instead let
\{p_{t}\}
be any MAB algorithm which achieves a regret of at most
6\sqrt{KT\log(K)}
with probability at least
1-\frac{1}{K}
. Such an algorithm exists, see eg. the EX3.P algorithm in
Bubeck et al. 2012
.

Consider the following algorithm, which at round
t
, chooses
\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}
, and
\pi^{\prime}_{t}=\bot
. Since there is no feedback from the
h_{w}
part of the function since the algorithm always chooses
\bot
, the
\gamma
-regret at each step will be the same as the
\gamma
times the regret of the MAB algorithm the on function class given by
\{g_{v}\}_{v\in[0,1]^{k}}
. This yields the proposition.
∎

Proposition 4

(Lower Bound for Example
1
)
.

For any
T\leq\exp(d/8)

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}
, such that
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}})\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{20}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{T}}
for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{T}}
.

Further, for
K\geq 6000/\gamma^{2}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is
6\epsilon
-localized, and thus by Theorem

, for some
M\in\mathcal{M}
, with probability at least
\frac{1}{3}
, we have
\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\geq\frac{\gamma}{10}\sqrt{KT}

Proof.
First we define the subset
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}
to be the set of all models indexed by
w,v
such that
v
has exactly one coordinate equal to
1
, and the remaining coordinates all equal
1-\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}
. For

to denote the function
f_{w,v}
, where

is the vector with a

Define
\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime},\pi):=\gamma\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\right)

\widehat{M}
be the corresponding model
\widehat{M}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)=\mathcal{N}(\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime},\pi),1)

\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)
. Fix any distribution
p
on
\Pi
. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{w}:=\text{argmin}_{w\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}\left(\langle w,\pi^{\prime}\rangle\geq\frac{d}{2}\right)\right],

(6.69)

and let

\displaystyle\tilde{k}:=\text{argmin}_{k\in[K]}\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\pi=k)\right].

(6.70)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\pi=\tilde{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k\sim\text{Uniform}[K])}\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\pi=k)\right]

(6.71)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\mathbb{E}_{k\sim\text{Uniform}([K])}\left[\mathbf{1}(\pi=k)\right]

(6.72)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}.

(6.73)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\langle\tilde{w},\pi^{\prime}>d/2\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{w\sim\text{Uniform}(\{-1,1\}^{d})}\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\langle w,\pi^{\prime}>d/2\rangle)\right]

(6.74)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\mathbb{E}_{w\sim\text{Uniform}(\{-1,1\}^{d})}\left[\mathbf{1}(\langle w,\pi^{\prime}>d/2\rangle)\right]

(6.75)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\text{Bin}\left(d,\frac{1}{2}\right)\geq\frac{3}{4}d\right]

(6.76)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-d/8),

(6.77)

where the last line follows from Hoeffding’s inequality.

Let
M
be the model with mean
f_{\tilde{w},\tilde{k}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\left(f_{M}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}\left(\langle\tilde{w}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}\rangle\geq\frac{d}{2}\right)\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\right)^{2}\mathbf{1}\left(\pi=\tilde{k}\right)\right]

(6.78)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp(-d/8)+\frac{2}{20T}\leq\epsilon^{2}.

(6.79)

Now we consider the
\gamma
-regret of
f_{M}
under
p
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\gamma f_{M}^{*}-f_{M}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\gamma-\mathbb{E}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[f_{M}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\right]

(6.80)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma-\mathbb{P}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\langle\tilde{w},\pi^{\prime}>d/2\rangle)\right]-\gamma\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\right)-\gamma\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\mathbb{P}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\tilde{k}=\pi)\right]

(6.81)

\displaystyle\geq-\exp(-d/8)+\gamma\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\left(1-\mathbb{P}_{(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\sim p}\left[\mathbf{1}(\tilde{k}=\pi)\right]\right)

(6.82)

\displaystyle\geq-\exp(-d/8)+\gamma\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\right)

(6.83)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}},

(6.84)

where in the final line we used the fact that
T\leq\exp(d/8)
.

It follows that
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}
.

Finally, we check that we can apply Theorem
1
with
\epsilon=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{T}}
and
C=1
. We observe that
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is
6\epsilon
-localized (indeed, all of its maxima are identical), and that
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\sqrt{\frac{K}{20T}}\geq 6\epsilon
for
K\geq 6000/\gamma^{2}
. Thus it follows that for some
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{20}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{T}}-18\epsilon\right]\geq\frac{1}{3}
, so for
K\geq\frac{6000}{\gamma^{2}}
, we have
\mathbb{P}^{M}\left[\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\geq\frac{\gamma}{10}\sqrt{KT}\right]\geq\frac{1}{3}
.
∎

6.3

Example of
\gamma
-DEC with Approximate Linear Optimization Oracles.

Inspired by the works
Kakade et al. 2007
;
Garber 2017
;
Hazan et al. 2018
, which study a linear minimization setting with access to an
\alpha
-approximate minimization oracle over the space
\Pi
, we give an example here of a comparable linear maximization setting where we have an access to an oracle which attains a
\gamma
-approximate maximum over the space
\Pi
.

We use this oracle and a barycentric spanner of
\Pi
(which is used in
Kakade et al. 2007
;
Garber 2017
;
Hazan et al. 2018
) to provide an upper bound scaling with
\epsilon^{2/3}
on
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}
. Assuming a regression oracle for the linear model class satisfying Assumption
1
, Theorem
3
yields a
\gamma
-regret scaling with
T^{2/3}
, which mirrors the
T^{2/3}
regret bound in
Kakade et al. 2007
;
Garber 2017
;
Hazan et al. 2018
.

Formally, let
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and suppose the mean reward function is a linear function of
\pi
, that is, for all
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
f_{M}(\pi):=w_{M}^{T}\pi
for some vector
w_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose we have access to an approximate optimization oracle
\mathcal{O}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\Pi
, which satisfies for some
\gamma<1
:

\displaystyle w^{T}\mathcal{O}(w)\geq\gamma\max_{\pi\in\Pi}w^{T}\pi.

(6.85)

Suppose also that the space
\Pi
has some
\beta
-barycentric spanner of linearly independent vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\Pi
such that for any
\pi\in\Pi
, we can write
\pi=\sum_{i}c_{i}v_{i}
for
|c_{i}|\leq\beta
.

Proposition 5
.

For any such
\Pi,\mathcal{M}
, we have
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}(\widehat{M})\leq\epsilon^{2/3}d
. Further, given query access to
f_{\widehat{M}}
, and a
\beta
-barycentric spanner
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
, and one call to the oracle
\mathcal{O}
, we can compute the distribution
p=p(\epsilon,\widehat{M})
bounding
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(\widehat{M})\leq 3d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}
. Further, this distribution is continuous in
\epsilon
.

Proof.
For
i\in[d]
, let
y_{i}:=f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i})
, and let
w
be the unique vector for which
w^{T}v_{i}=y_{i}
for all
y
. Let
\hat{\pi}:=\mathcal{O}(w)
be the value returned by the oracle, such that

\displaystyle w^{T}\hat{\pi}\geq\gamma\max_{\pi\in\Pi}w^{T}\pi.

(6.86)

Let
p
be the distribution which for each
i
, chooses
v_{i}
with probability
\epsilon^{2/3}\beta^{2/3}
, and chooses
\hat{\pi}
with probability
1-d\epsilon^{2/3}\beta^{2/3}
. Note that this distribution is continuous in
\epsilon
.

We claim that this
p
bounds
\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon}^{\gamma}(\widehat{M})\leq 3d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}
. Fix any
M\in\mathcal{B}_{p,\epsilon}(\widehat{M})
. Then it must be the case that for all
i\in[d]
, we have

\displaystyle\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}(f_{M}(v_{i})-f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i}))^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},

(6.87)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle|f_{M}(v_{i})-f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i})|\leq\epsilon^{2/3}\beta^{-1/3}.

(6.88)

Let
\hat{\pi}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}v_{i}
, where
|c_{i}|\leq\beta
.
Then

\displaystyle f_{M}(\hat{\pi})

\displaystyle=w_{M}^{T}\hat{\pi}=\sum_{i\in[d]}c_{i}w_{M}^{T}v_{i}=\sum_{i\in d}c_{i}f_{M}(v_{i})

(6.89)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in d}c_{i}f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i})-|c_{i}|\epsilon^{2/3}\beta^{-1/3}

(6.90)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in d}c_{i}f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i})-d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.91)

\displaystyle=w^{T}\hat{\pi}-d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.92)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma w^{T}\pi_{M}-d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}.

(6.93)

Similarly, with
\pi_{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}v_{i}
, where
|a_{i}|\leq\beta
, we have

\displaystyle f_{M}(\pi_{M})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in d}a_{i}f_{M}(v_{i})

(6.94)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in d}a_{i}f_{\widehat{M}}(v_{i})+|a_{i}|\beta^{-1/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.95)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[d]}w_{\widehat{M}}^{T}v_{i}+d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.96)

\displaystyle=w^{T}\pi_{M}+d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}.

(6.97)

Thus we have

\displaystyle\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}f_{M}(\pi_{M})

\displaystyle=\left(1-d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}\right)\left(\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\hat{\pi})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}\left(\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p}f_{M}(v_{i})\right)

(6.98)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma f_{M}(\pi_{M})-f_{M}(\hat{\pi})+d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.99)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left(w^{T}\pi_{M}+d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}\right)-\left(\gamma w^{T}\pi_{M}-d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}\right)+d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}

(6.100)

\displaystyle\leq 3d\beta^{2/3}\epsilon^{2/3}.

(6.101)

∎

Claim 1

Proof.

\displaystyle D_{\text{TV}}\left(\overline{\mathbb{P}}_{a}^{\widehat{M}},\overline{\mathbb{P}}^{M_{a}}_{a}\right)

7
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{\lceil{t/2}\rceil}}^{\gamma}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{t}}\sqrt{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}\right)\leq\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{\lceil{t/2}\rceil}}^{\gamma}\right)+8\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}

(8.189)

For the second term in Equation (
8.185
), by definition of the fact that the distribution
p_{t}
minimizes the
\gamma
-DEC, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}(1-\gamma)f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})+\mathsf{dec}_{\epsilon_{t}}^{\gamma}.

(8.190)

Finally for the third term in Equation (
8.185
), we have by Jensen’s inequality (applied to the distribution
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}|f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi)|\leq\sqrt{T\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}(f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi))^{2}}\leq\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(8.191)

Putting these three bounds together, and using the fact that
f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi_{\widehat{M}_{t}})\leq\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})\leq 1
, we achieve

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\leq(1-\gamma)T+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{\lceil{t/2}\rceil}}+9\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(8.192)

Under Assumption
2
, using Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{\lceil{t/2}\rceil}}\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{T/2}\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{T/2}\Delta_{\epsilon_{t}}\leq 2T\Delta_{\epsilon_{T/2}}.

(8.193)

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Reg}_{\gamma}(T)\leq(1-\gamma)T+2T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{24\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{T}}}+9\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(8.194)

∎

Proof of Theorem

We restate and prove Theorem
3
.
See
3

Algorithm 3

input:
Parameter
\gamma\in[0,1]
.

Initialize epoch index
i=1
. Let
T_{0}:=0
.

Initialize an online estimation oracle for the model class
\mathcal{M}
.

Initialize
m_{1}=\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})
.

for

t=1,\ldots,T

do

/*
Obtain estimator
\widehat{M}_{t}
from the Estimation oracle. */

Let
\widehat{M}_{t}=\mathsf{AlgEst}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1})
.

/*
Choose
\epsilon_{t}
. */

Approximate the maximum as
v_{t}=m_{i}-\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{t-T_{i-1}}
, where
\beta_{s}:=\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1}{s}}}
.

Let
\epsilon_{t}:=\min_{\epsilon}:g(\epsilon,\widehat{M}_{t},v_{t})\leq 0
where
g
is defined in Definition
5
.

/*
Play an action from the distribution that minimizes
\mathsf{dec}^{\gamma}_{\epsilon_{t}}
. */

Play
\pi_{t}\sim p_{t}
where
p_{t}:=p(\epsilon_{t},\widehat{M}_{t})
is the distribution guaranteed by Assumption
3
.

/*
Refine the feasible model class to exclude models that cannot be
M^{*}
. */

Let
\mathcal{M}_{t}:=\{M\in\mathcal{M}:\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{s}}(f_{M}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{s}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)\}

,
/*
End the epoch if the feasible class doesn’t contain a large enough maximum. */

if

\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})<m_{i}-\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{t+1-T_{i-1}}

then

Start a new epoch:
i\leftarrow i+1

Record the epoch end time as
T_{i}:=t
, and the epoch length as
L_{i}=T_{i}-T_{i-1}
.

Let
m_{i}=\sup_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}f_{M}(\pi_{M})
.

We use the following lemma in the proofs of Proposition
1
and Theorem
3
.

Lemma 3
.

If
\Delta_{\epsilon}:=C\epsilon^{c}
for some
0\leq c\leq 1
, then for any integer
T
and any
Q>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{T},\|x\|^{2}\leq Q}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_{x_{t}}\leq T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{Q}{T}}}.

(8.195)

Further, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{Q}{t}}}\leq 2T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{Q}{T}}}.

(8.196)

Proof.
The first inequality follows from applying Holder’s inequality, which states that for
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, we have
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}X_{t}Y_{t}\leq\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}X_{t}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}Y_{t}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
. Apply Holder’s with
X_{t}=\Delta_{x_{t}}=x_{i}^{c}
,
Y_{t}=1
, and
p=\frac{2}{c}
.

For the second inequality, we can bound

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{Q}{t}}}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\frac{Q}{t}\right)^{c/2}

(8.197)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{x=0}^{T}\left(\frac{Q}{x}\right)^{c/2}dx

(8.198)

\displaystyle=Q^{c/2}\frac{1}{1-c/2}T^{1-c/2}

(8.199)

\displaystyle\leq 2T\left(\frac{Q}{T}\right)^{c/2}

(8.200)

\displaystyle=2T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{Q}{T}}}.

(8.201)

∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Throughout the proof, we assume we have conditioned on the event that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta),

(8.202)

which occurs with probability
1-\delta
.

Now we sum up the regret over the
T
rounds. At each epoch
i
, by the first item of Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\gamma v_{t}-2\epsilon_{t}-\Delta_{\epsilon_{t}}

(8.203)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\gamma\left(m_{i}-\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{T_{i}-T_{i-1}}\right)-2\epsilon_{t}-\Delta_{\epsilon_{t}}

(8.204)

\displaystyle=\gamma L_{i}m_{i}-\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\left(10\beta_{t-T_{i-1}}+2\epsilon_{t}+\Delta_{\epsilon_{t}}\right).

(8.205)

Similar to the proof of Proposition
1
, we next want to show that the
\epsilon_{t}
cannot be too large. Let
M_{i}
be any model such that
f_{M_{i}}(\pi_{M_{i}})\geq v_{T_{i}}
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T_{i}-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M_{i}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta).

(8.206)

Such a model necessarily exists since the epoch ended at time
T_{i}
, meaning that at time
T_{i}-1
, there was a some model satisfying the above. Thus by item 2 of Lemma
1
, at each step
t
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{p(\epsilon_{t},\widehat{M}_{t})}[(f_{\widehat{M}}(\pi)-f_{M_{i}}(\pi))^{2}]\geq\epsilon_{t}^{2}
. Thus by Equation (
8.206
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{i-1}}^{T_{i}}\epsilon_{t}^{2}\leq\sum_{t=T_{i-1}}^{T_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{p(\epsilon_{t},\widehat{M}_{t})}[(f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)-f_{M_{i}}(\pi))^{2}]\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+(f_{\widehat{M}_{T_{i}}}(\pi)-f_{M_{i}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1

(8.207)

Thus by Assumption
2
and Lemma
3
, with
L_{i}=T_{i}-T_{i-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\Delta_{\epsilon_{t}}\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{i}},\|x\|^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1}\sum_{i}\Delta_{x_{i}}\leq L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}},

(8.208)

and similarly by Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\beta_{t-T_{i-1}}\leq 2L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}.

(8.209)

Thus in total, since
\epsilon\leq\Delta_{\epsilon}
(see Assumption
2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{i-1}+1}^{T_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma L_{i}m_{i}-(10*2+3)L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}=\gamma L_{i}m_{i}-23L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}.

(8.210)

Now consider the total
\gamma
-regret we accumulate after all
T
rounds. Let
I
be the total number of epochs. Then we have

\displaystyle\gamma Tf_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{M^{*}}(\pi)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma Tf_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}|f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)-f_{M^{*}}(\pi)|

(8.211)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma Tf_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}}f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi)+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}

(8.212)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{I}L_{i}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-m_{i})+23L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}+\sqrt{T\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}.

(8.213)

Here in the second line we applied Equation (
8.202
) and Jenson’s inequality to the distribution
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}p_{t}
, and in the third line we plugged in Equation (
8.210
).

Observe crucially that for all
i\leq I-1
, we must have
m_{i+1}\leq m_{i}-\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{i}+1}
because we ended the epoch precisely because there was no model left in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{t}
with small estimation error with a maximum above
m_{i}-\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{i}+1}
. Using Assumption
2
, we can simplify to get
m_{i+1}\leq m_{i}-\frac{9}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{i}}
. Indeed, the epoch must last at least 99 rounds since
\frac{10}{\gamma}\beta_{100}\geq 1
,
\beta_{L_{i}+1}\geq 0.99\beta_{L_{i}}
for
L_{i}\geq 99
.

Further, we must have
m_{I}\geq f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
because we have conditioned on the event that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim p_{t}}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi)-f_{\widehat{M}_{t}}(\pi))^{2}\leq\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)
.

If
I
is a constant, it is easy to check that this expression is sufficiently large to guarantee a
\gamma
-regret of
O(\beta_{T})
. For instance, if
I=1
, then this follows immediately. In the rest of the proof, our goal will be to show that we can essentially reduce to the case when
I\leq O(\log(T))
.

Consider maximizing the expression

\displaystyle\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{I}L_{i}(f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})-m_{i})+23L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}

(8.214)

over any choice of feasible integer
I
, integers
\{L_{i}\}_{i\in[I]}
and
\{m_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in[0,1]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{I}L_{i}=T
, and
m_{I}\geq f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
and
m_{i+1}\leq m_{i}-\frac{9}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{i}}
.

We make the follow two claims about the optimal solution.

Claim 8
.

In any optimal solution, we will have
m_{i+1}=m_{i}-\frac{9}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{i}}
exactly, and
m_{I}=f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})
. Thus in any optimal solution, we have
m_{i}-f_{M^{*}}(\pi_{M^{*}})=\sum_{j=i}^{I-1}\frac{9}{\gamma}\beta_{L_{j}}
.

Proof.
Working backwards from
i=I
to
1
, we can see that decreasing the
m_{i}
as much as possible clearly increases the objective.
∎

Thus we now care about upper bounding the objective

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}L_{i}\sum_{j=i}^{I-1}9\beta_{L_{j}}+23L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{I-1}\left(9L_{i}\sum_{j=i+1}^{I-1}\beta_{L_{j}}+14L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}\right)+23L_{I}\beta_{L_{I}}

(8.215)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{I-1}\left(9L_{i}\sum_{j=i+1}^{I-1}\beta_{L_{j}}+14L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}\right)+23T\beta_{T},

(8.216)

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
2
.

Claim 9
.

If for some
k\in[I-2]
, we have
L_{k+1}\leq 1.1L_{k}
, then we can increase the value of the above upper bound on the objective by combining epochs
k
and
k+1
into a single epoch of length
L_{k}+L_{k+1}
.

Proof.
For convenience, let
S_{i}:=\sum_{j=i+1}^{I-1}\beta_{L_{j}}
.

We have two terms that depend on the
L_{i}
in the above objective,
-9\sum_{i}L_{i}S_{i}
and
14\sum_{i}L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}
.

Consider doing this change where we combine
L_{k}
and
L_{k+1}
.

For the first term involving the
S_{i}
, for all the terms with
i\leq k-1
, the value
L_{i}S_{i}
will only decrease from combining, since trivially,
\beta_{L_{k}}+\beta_{L_{k+1}}\geq\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}
. For all the terms with
i\geq k+1
, the value of
L_{i}S_{i}
will be unaffected.

In combining
L_{k}
and
L_{k+1}
, we will lose the term
L_{k}S_{k}+L_{k+1}S_{k+1}
and gain
(L_{k}+L_{k+1})S_{k+1}
.

Thus by combining the two epochs, the total objective increases by at least

\displaystyle 9\left(L_{k}S_{k}+L_{k+1}S_{k+1}\right)-9(L_{k}+L_{k+1})S_{k+1}-14L_{k}\beta_{L_{k}}-14L_{k+1}\beta_{L_{k+1}}+14(L_{k}+L_{k+1})\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}.

(8.217)

Here the final 3 terms arise from the change to the
14\sum_{i}L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}
term in the objective. Simplifying, this increase equals

\displaystyle 9L_{k}

\displaystyle(S_{k}-S_{k+1})-14L_{k}\beta_{L_{k}}-14L_{k+1}\beta_{L_{k+1}}+14(L_{k}+L_{k+1})\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}

(8.218)

\displaystyle=9L_{k}\beta_{L_{k+1}}-14L_{k}\beta_{L_{k}}-14L_{k+1}\beta_{L_{k+1}}+14(L_{k}+L_{k+1})\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}

(8.219)

\displaystyle=9\left(L_{k}\beta_{L_{k+1}}-\alpha L_{k}\beta_{L_{k}}-\alpha L_{k+1}\beta_{L_{k+1}}+\alpha(L_{k}+L_{k+1})\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}\right),

(8.220)

where
\alpha=14/9
.

Claim 10
.

if
L_{k+1}/L_{k}\leq 1.1
, then

9\left(L_{k}\beta_{L_{k+1}}-\alpha L_{k}\beta_{L_{k}}-\alpha L_{k+1}\beta_{L_{k+1}}+\alpha(L_{k}+L_{k+1})\beta_{L_{k}+L_{k+1}}\right)\geq 0.

Proof.
We will analyze this by plugging Assumption
2
, which states that
\Delta_{\epsilon}=C\epsilon^{c}
for some
c\leq 1
. Recall that
\beta_{\ell}=\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{\ell}}}=C\ell^{-c/2}(\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta))^{c/2}
.

To simplify, let
L=L_{k}
, and let
x={L_{k+1}}/L_{k}
. So we can lower bound the increase as

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=9L\left(\beta_{Lx}-\alpha\beta_{L}-\alpha x\beta_{Lx}+\alpha(1+x)\beta_{L(1+x)}\right)

(8.221)

\displaystyle=9LC\left(\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{L}\right)^{c/2}\left(x^{-c/2}-\alpha-\alpha xx^{-c/2}+\alpha(1+x)(1+x)^{-c/2}\right)

(8.222)

\displaystyle=9LC\left(\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{L}\right)^{c/2}\left(x^{-c/2}-\alpha-\alpha x^{1-c/2}+\alpha(1+x)^{1-c/2}\right).

(8.223)

Applying Holder’s inequality as in Lemma
3
, since
c\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle 1+x^{1-c/2}=1^{1-c/2}+x^{1-c/2}\leq(1+x)\left(\frac{1+x}{2}\right)^{-c/2},

(8.224)

and thus, we have

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\geq 9LC\left(\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{L}\right)^{c/2}\left(x^{-c/2}-\alpha(1+x)\left(\frac{1+x}{2}\right)^{1-c/2}+\alpha(1+x)^{1-c/2}\right)

(8.225)

\displaystyle=9LC\left(\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{L}\right)^{c/2}\left(x^{-c/2}+\alpha\left(-2^{c/2}+1\right)(1+x)^{1-c/2})\right)

(8.226)

\displaystyle=9LC\left(\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{L}\right)^{c/2}x^{-c/2}\left(1+\alpha\left(-2^{c/2}+1\right)(1+x)\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)^{c/2}\right)

(8.227)

Now we want to show that this is positive for
x\leq 1.1
. Observe that for
c\geq 0
, the expression

\displaystyle 1+\alpha\left(-2^{c/2}+1\right)(1+x)\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)^{c/2}

(8.228)

is non-increasing in
x
for
x\geq 0
. Thus it suffices to show that when we plug in
x=1.1
, that expression is positive. Plugging in
x=1.1
, this evaluates to

\displaystyle 1+\alpha\left(-2^{c/2}+1\right)2.1\left(\frac{1.1}{2.1}\right)^{c/2}\geq 1+3.3\left(-2^{c/2}+1\right)\left(\frac{1.1}{2.1}\right)^{c/2}

(8.229)

One can check that the above expression is decreasing in
c
, and thus since
c\leq 1
, the expression is at least
1+3.3\left(-2^{1/2}+1\right)\left(\frac{1.1}{2.1}\right)^{1/2}>0
.
∎

It follows from this claim that if
x=L_{k+1}/L_{k}<1.1
, it will increase the objective to combine the two epochs. This yields the claim.

∎

It follows from Claim
9
that the maximum of the objective in
8.215
is achieved when
I\leq\log_{1.1}(T)\leq 10.5\log(T)
.

Now recalling that
\beta_{s}=\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1}{s}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I-1}14L_{i}\beta_{L_{i}}+23T\beta_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I-1}\sum_{t=T_{i-1}}^{T_{i}}\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1}{L_{i}}}}+23T\beta_{T}

(8.230)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{T},\|x\|^{2}\leq I(\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}14\Delta_{x_{t}}+23T\beta_{T}

(8.231)

\displaystyle\leq 14T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{I\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)+1}{T}}}+23T\beta_{T}

(8.232)

\displaystyle\leq 37T\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{11\log(T)\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{T}}}

(8.233)

\displaystyle\leq 500\log(T)\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{T}}}.

(8.234)

The second inequality and the final inequality follows from Assumption
2
. Thus we have that the objective in Equation (
8.215
) and thus also the objective in Equation (
8.214
) is upper bounded by
500\log(T)\Delta_{\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{Est}(T,\delta)}{T}}}
.

Combining with Equation (
8.211
) yields the theorem.

∎

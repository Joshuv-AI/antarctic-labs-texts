Realistic tight-binding model for monolayer transition metal dichalcogenides in 1T’ structure
Mengli HU, Guofu MA, Chun Yu WAN, Junwei LIU

Abstract
Monolayer transition metal dichalcogenides M ​ X 2 MX_{2} ( M M = Mo,W and X X = Te, Se, S) in 1T’ structure were predicted to be quantum spin Hall insulators based on first-principles calculations, which were quickly confirmed by multiple experimental groups. For a better understanding of their properties, in particular their responses to external fields, we construct a realistic four-band tight-binding (TB) model by combining the symmetry analysis and first-principles calculations. Our TB model respects all the symmetries and can accurately reproduce the band structure in a large energy window from -0.3 eV to 0.8 eV. With the inclusion of spin-orbital coupling (SOC), our TB model can characterize the nontrivial topology and the corresponding edge states. Our TB model can also capture the anisotropic strain effects on the band structure and the strain-induced metal-insulator transition. Moreover, we found that although M ​ X 2 MX_{2} share the same crystal structures and have the same crystal symmetries, while the orbital composition of states around the Fermi level are qualitatively different and their lower-energy properties cannot fully described by a single 𝒌 ⋅ 𝒑 \bm{k\cdot p} model. Thus, we construct two different types of 𝒌 ⋅ 𝒑 \bm{k\cdot p} model for M M S 2 , M M Se 2 and M M Te 2 , respectively. Benefiting from the high accuracy and simplicity, our TB and 𝒌 ⋅ 𝒑 \bm{k\cdot p} models can serve as a solid and concrete starting point for future studies of transport, superconductivity, strong correlation effects and twistronics in 1T’-transition metal dichalcogenides.



I
INTRODUCTION

Since proposed in 2005,quantum spin Hall insulators (QSHIs)
Kane and Mele 2005
have attracted tremendous attention due to its unexpected nontrivial topology and potential applications in next-generation information technologies. There are many theoretical predictions of QSHIs in various material systems
Hasan and Kane 2010
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
;
Bansil et al. 2016
. To date, however, only three material systems have exhibited quantized edge conductance in transport experiments: HgTe quantum wells
Bernevig et al. 2006
;
König et al. 2007
, InAs/GaSb quantum wells
Liu et al. 2008
;
Knez et al. 2011
and monolayer WTe
2
-type transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs)
Qian et al. 2014
;
Fei et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2018
. Among them, WTe
2
-type TMDCs are the most promising for potential applications of QSHIs, as they are 2D van der Waals materials.

WTe
2
-type TMDCs were first predicted to be QSHIs in 2014
Qian et al. 2014
and was experimentally confirmed in WTe
2
in 2017 by several independent experiments including transport
Fei et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2018
, angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES)
Tang et al. 2017a
and scanning tunneling microscopy (STM)
Tang et al. 2017a
;
Peng et al. 2017
;
Jia et al. 2017
. Remarkably, the quantized conductance persists at a high temperature of around 100 K
Wu et al. 2018
. Besides the nontrivial topology, the thin films of WTe
2
also possess many other interesting properties including gate-induced superconductivity
Palomaki et al. 2018
;
Fatemi et al. 2018
;
Hsu et al. 2020
;
Xie et al. 2020
and ferroelectricity
Fei et al. 2018
;
Sharma et al. 2019
.
Although inversion symmetry is preserved in monolayer 1T’-WTe
2
, it is broken in the bulk and multilayer thin films, which leads to the existence of Weyl points
Soluyanov et al. 2015
, Berry curvature dipole
Xu et al. 2018a
, and spin-orbit torques
MacNeill et al. 2017
. Besides, WTe
2
also exhibits a temperature-induced Lifshitz transition
Wu et al. 2015
and pressure-driven superconductivity
Pan et al. 2015
;
Kang et al. 2015
.

In addition to the extensive studies of WTe
2
, the exploration has also been extended to other related materials. For example, monolayer 1T’-WSe
2
Chen et al. 2018
;
Pedramrazi et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2021
, 1T’-MoSe
2
Cheng et al. 2019
, 1T’-MoTe
2
Naylor et al. 2016
and 1T’-MoS
2
Xu et al. 2018b
are successfully fabricated; plus WSe
2
and MoS
2
show a gapped bulk and conducting edge states in experiments. Observations of topological Fermi arcs
Deng et al. 2016
and superconductivity
Qi et al. 2016
in bulk MoTe
2
are also reported.

So far, all the experiments about monolayer WTe
2
are consistent with each other on the nontrivial topology, and transport experiments clearly show that the bulk states of monolayer WTe
2
are insulating
Fei et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2018
. However, on the origin of such an insulating behavior, ARPES and STM experiments give contrasting results. ARPES experiments show a non-zero single-particle gap of monolayer WTe
2

Tang et al. 2017b
, while the STM experiments show that monolayer WTe
2
is gapless in the single-particle level, and the gap is from electron-electron correlation pinned at the Fermi level
Song et al. 2018
. Meanwhile, there are some other strange transport behaviors varying with the gating voltage and temperature, which cannot be explained by a single-particle gap
Fei et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2018
. A general theoretical picture of all these experimental results is crucial for future studies and applications. Although first-principles calculations can characterize the band structure of 1T’-
MX_{2}

Qian et al. 2014
, the huge computational cost makes it incapable to study correlation effects and related properties under various doping/gating, strains and electric fields.

Here, we build a realistic tight-binding model that can capture the low-energy physics of 1T’-
MX_{2}
well at the level of first-principles calculations. Different from the previous works
Muechler et al. 2016
;
Ok et al. 2019
that can only describe the dispersion along some high symmetry lines, our model can accurately characterize the band structure in the whole Brillouin zone in a large energy window around the Fermi level from -0.3 eV to 0.8 eV for all the monolayer
MX_{2}
in 1T’ structure. The SOC effects which open the band gaps of Dirac cones are precisely captured in our method.
The SOC drives the system into a QSHI as it is in the Kane-Mele model
Kane and Mele 2005
. Moreover, our TB model can describe the anisotropic effects of strain on the band structure, and can correctly show the strain-induced metal-insulator phase transition. Based on the different orbital components around
\Gamma

\bm{k\cdot p}
model with fewer parameters. Our results here provide a solid basis for future theoretical studies of transport properties and twistronics in 1T’-
MX_{2}

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II

In Sec.
III
, we introduced two types of
\bm{k\cdot p}
model at
\Gamma

In Sec.
IV

V
.

As shown in Fig.

\in

II.2
SOC effects

In a fermion system, SOC is necessary to get the nontrivial topology. To describe the effects of SOC, we first extend the spinless bases to the spinful bases
\left|\phi_{l,\alpha}\right\rangle(l=A,B,a,b;\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow)

H^{soc}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=i\sum_{mn}(\bm{E}_{mn}\times\bm{r}_{mn})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\left|\phi_{lm,\alpha}\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{l^{\prime}n,\alpha^{\prime}}\right|

\bm{r}_{mn}
is the vector connecting two atoms at
\bm{r}_{m}
, and
\bm{r}_{n}
,
\bm{E}_{mn}
is the electric field between them, and
\bm{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices for spin.

\Lambda

II.3
Non-trivial topology in
MX_{2}

The little group of different momenta are always distinct from the space group of the given material. For 1T’-
MX_{2}
, four time-reversal-invariant momenta
\Gamma,\ X,\ Y,
and

belong to
C_{2}
, the momenta along
\Gamma-Y
belong to

, and all the other momenta including those along
\Gamma-M
belong to a trivial symmetry group with only an identity operation.

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{2x}(k_{x})

\displaystyle=s_{0}\left(\begin{matrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{x}(k_{x})

\displaystyle=s_{z}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\bar{C}_{2x}(0)\bar{M}_{x}(0).

V
SUMMARY

In this paper, we constructed four-band TB models based on the orbital components of monolayer 1T’-
MX_{2}
. By analyzing the nonsymmorphic space group of 1T’-
MX_{2}
structure, bases from
A_{g}
and
A_{u}
IRs in
C_{2h}
were selected. Our TB models match well with the first-principles band structures along all high symmetric lines.

I
-TB model with fewer parameters fits well within the energy window from -0.3 eV to 0.3 eV;
II
-TB model with more parameters fits well within the energy window from -0.3 eV to 0.8 eV.
With the inclusion of SOC effects, both TB models can capture the nontrivial topological properties of 1T’-
MX_{2}
.
Specifically, we find that the momenta belong to the same little group have different degeneracies due to features of the nonsymmorphic group. Plus, the spin of edge states along the
\bm{R_{x}}
direction always lies in the
y-z
plane, which is guaranteed by the time-reversal symmetry and the mirror symmetry
M_{x}
.
Moreover, for the first time, we found that
MX_{2}
have different parities at
\Gamma
point, and then we built two different types of
\bm{k\cdot p}
model to fully describe the low-energy properties of
MX_{2}
.
In the last section, we built the strained TB model to capture the effects of uniaxial strain along
\bm{R_{x}}
and
\bm{R_{y}}
. The small tensile strain along the
x
direction or compressive strain along the
y
direction induces a metal-insulator transition. To conclude, our TB and
\bm{k\cdot p}
models are simple and efficient to describe the 1T’-
MX_{2}
band structure and its topological properties, which can serve as a solid foundation for future studies of superconductivity and other many-body physics in 1T’-
MX_{2}
.

On log-concave approximations of high-dimensional posterior measures and stability properties in non-linear inverse problems
Jan Bohr Richard Nickl University of Cambridge August 7, 2026

Abstract
. The problem of efficiently generating random samples from high-dimensional and non-log-concave posterior measures arising in nonlinear regression problems is considered. Extending investigations from [ 48 ] , local and global stability properties of the model are identified under which such posterior distributions can be approximated in Wasserstein distance by suitable log-concave measures. This allows the use of fast gradient based sampling algorithms, for which convergence guarantees are established that scale polynomially in all relevant quantities (assuming ‘warm’ initialisation). The scope of the general theory is illustrated in a non-linear inverse problem from integral geometry for which new stability results are derived.



1.
Introduction

The circle of problems we study here evolves around the question of how to efficiently generate random samples from high-dimensional Gibbs-type probability measures

(1.1)

\mathrm{d}\Pi(\theta|Z^{(N)})\propto e^{\ell(\theta|Z^{(N)})}\mathrm{d}\Pi(\theta),\penalty\ \penalty\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{D},\quad D\in\mathbb{N},

with
non-concave
‘energies’
\theta\mapsto\ell_{N}(\theta)\equiv\ell(\theta|Z^{(N)})
. The maps
\ell_{N}
can be thought of as log-likelihood functions from a non-linear regression equation generating a random data vector
Z^{(N)}
of length
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and the reference (or
prior
) measure
\Pi
is assumed to be Gaussian. Sampling algorithms play a fundamental role in scientific applications such as Bayesian inference and inverse problems
[
57
,
61
,
12
,
15
]
, data assimilation
[
9
,
34
,
56
]
and probabilistic numerics
[
16
,
8
]
and have close connections to computational complexity theory and statistical physics. Since direct computation of the ‘partition function’ (normalising factor) in (
1.1
) is typically not viable, the sampling task can be computationally hard if not impossible when
D
and
N
are large. See, for instance,
[
55
]
for the discussion of a prototypical ‘curse of dimensionality’ in a filtering problem, or
[
6
]
in a sampling problem involving sparse PCA.

In applications, the non-concavity of
\theta\mapsto\ell_{N}(\theta)
may arise from a partial differential equation (PDE) underlying the data generating process for
Z^{(N)}
, specifically from the non-linearity of the ‘parameter to solution’ map. In the present article we continue investigations from
[
48
]
and demonstrate that if the PDE has certain local and global ‘stability’ properties, then ‘polynomial time’ sampling from
\Pi(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
is possible even in high-dimensional (
D\to\infty
), spiked (
N\to\infty
), non-log-concave situations. More precisely, we devise a general set of analytical hypotheses on the underlying PDE model such that the measure (
1.1
) can be sampled from within precision
\epsilon
after
O(N^{b_{1}}D^{b_{2}}\epsilon^{-b_{3}})
algorithmic iterations (each iteration requiring one evaluation of
\ell_{N}
, and assuming appropriate initialisation). Our theory applies to the elliptic example with a Schrödinger equation considered in
[
48
]
, but we further demonstrate its applicability in a more demanding transport PDE setting arising with non-Abelian X-ray transforms which have been studied recently in
[
52
,
39
,
51
]
(see
[
26
,
58
,
49
]
for applications and
[
65
,
50
,
19
]
for earlier references). Our main contribution, next to novel stability estimates for X-ray transforms described below, is a
log-concave approximation theorem
which ensures that
\Pi(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
from (
1.1
) is very close to a log-concave measure in Wasserstein distance, with high probability under the law of the data
Z^{(N)}
.

The most widely used methods to generate samples for (
1.1
) are Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms, where one runs a Markov chain
(\vartheta_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N})
on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
that has
\Pi(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
as invariant measure. Recent years have seen great progress on computational performance guarantees of such algorithms, of which we want to highlight two advances that are relevant in our PDE setting: The first is the work of Hairer, Stuart and Vollmer
[
23
]
, who prove a
D
-independent spectral gap for the so called
preconditioned Crank-Nicolson
(pCN) algorithm in settings where
\ell_{N}
is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz in
\theta
. While their results imply
D
-independent convergence guarantees, they remain non-quantitative in the magnitude (or ‘temperature’) of the energy
\ell_{N}
and hence in
N
(cf. the discussion in §1.1.2 in
[
48
]
). The second line of advances concerns
log-concave
Gibbs-measures (
1.1
) and was initiated by Dalalyan
[
14
]
and Durmus and Moulines
[
17
,
18
]
. Their work on gradient based
(unadjusted) Langevin algorithms
(ULA) implies mixing time bounds of the law of the iterates
\vartheta_{k}
towards
\Pi(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
which scale
polynomially
in both
D,N
. This exploits fundamental ideas of hyper-contractivity of Langevin diffusions and Wasserstein gradient flows going back to Bakry and Émery
[
2
]
and Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto
[
28
]
, see also the monographs
[
66
,
3
]
. While these techniques do not apply directly to our non-log-concave setting, the general log-concave approximation theorems proved below provide the crucial link to transfer the fast mixing properties of such gradient based methods to the more complex PDE-driven models considered here, at least if a reasonable initialiser is available. We discuss the difference between our results and traditional Laplace approximations of posterior measures after Theorem
2.6
below. Note that in general, without exploiting log-concavity, the runtime of sampling algorithms for high-dimensional (even unimodal) posterior distributions scales exponentially in dimension, see the very recent
[
4
]
.

Non-linear inverse problems and PDE stability.
We now discuss informally the notions of ‘local and global stability’ of the underlying model that we will employ. It is common (e.g., in statistical inverse problems) to encode PDE structure in a ‘forward map’

(1.2)

\mathscr{G}:\mathbb{R}^{D}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{X},V),\quad\theta\mapsto\mathscr{G}(\theta),

describing vector space
V
-valued regression fields
\mathscr{G}(\theta):\mathcal{X}\to V
defined on some probability space
(\mathcal{X},\lambda)
. The data vector
Z^{(N)}=((Y_{i},X_{i})\in V\times\mathcal{X},i=1,\dots,N)
is generated via the regression equation
Y_{i}=\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i},
with
\varepsilon_{i}
standard i.i.d. Gaussian noise in
V
and
X_{i}\sim^{\mathrm{iid}}\lambda
independently of the
\varepsilon_{i}
’s. This induces an ‘energy’ (log-likelihood) associated to
\mathscr{G}
by

(1.3)

\ell_{N}(\theta)=\ell_{N}(\theta|Z^{(N)})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|Y_{i}-\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_{i})\right|_{V}^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{D}.

The non-concavity of
\ell_{N}(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
is due to the map
\mathscr{G}
which we think of here as the composition of a non-linear ‘parameter to solution map’ of some PDE (such as (
1.5
) below) with a discretisation step that represents an infinite-dimensional parameter
\Phi
by its approximation
\Phi_{\theta}
from a finite-dimensional approximation space
E_{D}
with (‘Fourier-’) coefficients
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
.

With
\ell_{N}
from (
1.3
), the Gibbs-type measure (
1.1
) is the Bayesian
posterior distribution
for a Gaussian prior
\Pi
arising from the data. Suppose
Z^{(N)}
is generated from ‘ground truth’
\Phi_{\star}
and let
\Phi_{\theta_{\star,D}}
with
\theta_{\star,D}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
denote the best approximation of
\Phi_{\star}
from
E_{D}\cong\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Then properties of
\mathscr{G}
near
\theta_{\star,D}
will play a crucial role in our analysis. Very informally speaking our conditions on
\mathscr{G}
will comprise the following:

(a)

(Global stability)
\mathscr{G}
is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and satisfies a stability estimate
\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\lesssim\|\mathscr{G}(\theta)-\mathscr{G}(\theta^{\prime})\|^{\gamma},\gamma>0,
on bounded sets in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
.

(b)

(Local regularity)
\mathscr{G}
is
C^{3}
in a neighbourhood of
\theta_{\star,D}
, with
C^{3}
-norms of
\mathscr{G}
growing at most polynomially in
D
.

(c)

(Local gradient stability) For all
\theta
near
\theta_{\star,D}
, the gradient
\nabla\mathscr{G}(\theta)
of
\mathscr{G}
satisfies

(1.4)

\|\nabla\mathscr{G}(\theta)[h]\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}\gtrsim D^{-\kappa_{0}}\|h\|_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ h\in\mathbb{R}^{D},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for some }\kappa_{0}\geq 0.

The global requirements in
(a)
pose strong restrictions on
\mathscr{G}
but fall far short of assuming global log-concavity of the Gibbs measure (
1.1
). Such conditions can often be verified in concrete PDE settings, in particular global inverse continuity (or ‘stability’) estimates for
\mathscr{G}
have recently emerged as drivers of posterior consistency theorems in non-linear inverse problems see
[
67
,
45
,
41
,
47
,
1
,
39
,
22
,
31
]
, §
5.1
below, and also
[
42
]
for a general account of the theory. The regularity condition
(b)
provides sufficient smoothness of the local parameterisation of
\theta
and is typically satisfied in PDE settings too.

Assumption
(c)
ensures that the energy
\theta\mapsto\ell_{N}(\theta|Z^{(N)})
is locally concave near
\theta_{\star,D}

on average
under the distribution of
Z^{(N)}
(see Condition
2.4
below). While there is no general recipe to obtain such a stability estimate, it was demonstrated in
[
48
]
how to use elliptic PDE theory to verify (
1.4
) in a representative example with a Schrödinger equation. Our main example to be introduced below shares some conceptual similarities and we explain these now in more detail, thereby also clarifying the role of the approximation spaces
E_{D}\cong\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Consider PDEs of the form

(1.5)

(\mathscr{D}+\Phi_{\theta})u=0\text{ on }\mathcal{M},

where
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth manifold,
\mathscr{D}
is a
known linear
differential operator and
\Phi_{\theta}
is a (possibly matrix valued) potential defined on
\mathcal{M}
, indexed by the parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
to be determined. Subject to suitable boundary conditions under which (
1.5
) admits a unique solution
u=u_{\theta}
, it is assumed that one can measure
u_{\theta}
on a subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, giving the forward map

\mathscr{G}(\theta)=u_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{X}}
. For instance we can consider

•

A
Schrödinger equation
: Here
\mathcal{M}
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathscr{D}=\Delta
is the Laplace-operator, say with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

•

A
transport equation
: Here
\mathcal{M}=\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq|v|=1\}
(the unit sphere bundle over a disk) and
\mathscr{D}=v\cdot\nabla_{x}
is the
geodesic vector field
.

Now, if we consider a variation
\theta+th
(
t\in\mathbb{R}
) in direction
h\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and denote
\dot{u}_{\theta}=\left(\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t|_{t=0}\right)u_{\theta+th}
the resulting variation of the solution to (
1.5
), then
formally

(1.6)

\nabla\mathscr{G}(\theta)[h]=\dot{u}_{\theta}=-(\mathscr{D}+\Phi_{\theta})^{-1}\big[\dot{\Phi}_{\theta}[h]u_{\theta}\big],

and a stability estimate as in (
1.4
) can be expected to follow from
isomorphim properties
of
(\mathscr{D}+\Phi_{\theta})^{-1}
between suitable function spaces.

In case of the Schrödinger equation the powerful machinery of elliptic PDE’s implies that
(\Delta+\Phi_{\theta})^{-1}
is an isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
onto the Sobolev space
H_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{M})=\{u\in H^{2}(\mathcal{M}):u|_{\partial\mathcal{M}}=0\}
for (non-positive) potentials
\Phi_{\theta}
. Choosing the approximation space
E_{D}
to equal the span of the first
D
eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet-Laplacian (i.e. the eigen-basis corresponding to
\Phi_{\theta}=0
), the usual Weyl asymptotics imply that on
E_{D}
the
H_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
-norm is equivalent to
D^{-2/d}\|\cdot\|_{E_{D}}
. A duality argument then yields a stability estimate as in (
1.4
) with
\kappa_{0}=4/d
; see Lemma 4.7 in
[
48
]
for more details.

For the transport equation the operator
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}\equiv(\mathscr{D}+\Phi)^{-1}
can be interpreted as a linear X-ray transform on the disk
\mathbb{D}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|<1\}
that is ‘attenuated’ by a potential
\Phi=\Phi_{\theta}
. Transforms of this kind appear frequently as linearisations for non-linear geometric inverse problems
[
27
,
53
]
and are subject of current research. Mapping properties of
\mathbb{I}_{0}
acting on compactly supported functions are well known (see
[
40
, Thm. 5.1]
) and generally the operator
\mathbb{I}_{0}
is understood to be
1/2
-regularising. Taking into account the behaviour near
\partial\mathbb{D}
, it was proved in
[
36
]
that
\mathbb{I}_{0}
is an isomorphism from a Sobolev-type space
\tilde{H}^{-1/2}(\mathbb{D})
, defined in (
4.6
) below, onto
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{X})
. Extending recent developments in
[
38
]
, we will prove here that this isomorphism property extends to
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}
for a large class of
\Phi\neq 0
(see Theorem
4.1
). The natural discretisation spaces
E_{D}
for which we can expect curvature as in (
1.4
) are defined in terms of the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
\mathbb{I}_{0}
, and we obtain in Theorem
3.1
a stability estimate as in (
1.4
) with
\kappa_{0}=1/2
.

While the precise analysis in the transport case is very different from the elliptic techniques used for Schrödinger type equations, we want to emphasise the unifying aspects: The discretisation spaces
E_{D}
are based on the SVD of the linearisation of
\mathscr{G}
at
\Phi_{\theta}=0
; then one uses an adequate isomorphism theorem for
\Phi_{\theta}\neq 0
to prove the linear stability estimate in (
1.4
) with
\kappa_{0}
related to the ‘local’ ill-posedness of the underlying inverse problem.

In Section
2
we state our general log-concave approximation theorem, while Section
3
contains the application to
X
-ray transforms. Proofs and further technical developments are given in later sections.

2.
A general log-concave approximation theorem

2.1.
Functional setting, spaces, and forward map

Consider a measurable space
\mathcal{Z}=(\mathcal{Z},\mathscr{Z})
equipped with a measure
\zeta
on
\mathscr{Z}
, and a finite-dimensional vector space
W\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d_{W}}
of dimension
d_{W}
. We denote the resulting
L^{p}
spaces of
W
-valued (Borel) vector fields
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{d_{W}}),f_{i}\in L_{\zeta}^{p}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})
, by
L_{\zeta}^{p}(\mathcal{Z},W)\equiv\times_{i=1}^{d_{W}}L_{\zeta}^{p}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R}),1\leq p<\infty,
while
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z},W)
denote the bounded measurable maps on
\mathcal{Z}
equipped with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. On each coordinate space
L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})
, we consider an
L^{2}_{\zeta}
-orthonormal system

\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\subset L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

On the product space
L_{\zeta}^{2}(\mathcal{Z},W)
we obtain a corresponding system
\{(e_{n,1},\dots,e_{n,d_{W}}),\penalty\ e_{n,i}=e_{n}\penalty\ \forall i,\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
that is orthonormal for the Hilbert space inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},W)}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\langle\cdot_{i},\cdot_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})},

where
\cdot_{i}
selects the
i
-th coordinate of the vector field. Next, for a sequence of positive scalars
(\lambda_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
such that
0<\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda_{n+1}\to_{N\to\infty}\infty
, Sobolev-type spaces of real-valued functions for the system
(e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
are defined as

(2.1)

\tilde{H}^{s}=\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})=\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R}):\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})}^{2}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\lambda_{n}^{s}\langle f,e_{n}\rangle_{L_{\zeta}^{2}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})}^{2}<\infty\Big\},\penalty\ s\geq 0.

Clearly the imbedding
\tilde{H}^{s}\subset L^{2}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})
is continuous for any
s\geq 0
. The spaces
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},W)=\times_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})
of vector fields whose coordinate functions lie in
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},\mathbb{R})
are equipped with the natural (Hilbert-space) norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},W)}
arising from inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\tilde{H}^{s}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{W}}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{s}\langle f_{i},e_{n}\rangle_{L_{\zeta}^{2}}\langle g_{i},e_{n}\rangle_{L_{\zeta}^{2}}.

We will sometimes just use the notation
\tilde{H}^{s}
when the range
W
or
\mathbb{R}
is clear from the context. We note that when
\mathcal{Z}
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, these Sobolev ‘type’ spaces can be ‘non-standard’ depending on the boundary behaviour of the basis
e_{n}
. The theory that follows encodes all results in terms of regularity of the
\tilde{H}^{s}
scale.

Finite-dimensional subspaces
E_{D}\subset\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},W)
arise as the linear span of vector fields

(2.2)

E_{D}=\spn\{(e_{n,1},\dots,e_{n,d_{W}}):n\leq D_{W}\equiv D/d_{W}\},

isomorphic (by Parseval’s theorem) to an Euclidean space of dimension
D=d_{W}\sum_{n\leq D/d_{W}}1
. In what follows we assume for notational simplicity that
D
is a positive integer multiple of
d_{W}
. The ‘Euclidean norm’ on
E_{D}
, equal to the
L^{2}_{\zeta}
-norm, will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{E_{D}}
.

Condition 2.1
.

Consider an orthonormal system
(e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
in
L_{\zeta}^{2}(\mathcal{Z})
such that

(2.3)

\mathrm{max}_{0\leq n\leq D_{W}}\|e_{n}\|_{\infty}\lesssim D^{\tau},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ some }\tau\geq 0,\penalty\ D\in\mathbb{N},

and for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
and constants
b_{1},b_{2}
,

(2.4)

b_{1}n^{2/d}\leq\lambda_{n}\leq b_{2}n^{2/d},\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\Theta
be a (measurable) linear subspace of
L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},W)
such that
\cup_{D\in\mathbb{N}}E_{D}\subset\Theta\subset L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z},W)
.

While in principle the constant
d
is arbitrary in the preceding condition, in applications here it will model the dimension of
\mathcal{Z}
and (
2.4
) then corresponds to the asymptotic eigenvalue distribution of an elliptic second order differential operator on
\mathcal{Z}
. Alternatively the ‘eigen-pairs’
(e_{n},\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
could arise from the Karhunen-Loéve expansion of an infinite-dimensional Gaussian process prior over
\mathcal{Z}
(cf. also before (
2.9
) below).

When a suitable Sobolev imbedding is available we can take
\Theta=\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathcal{Z},W)
for appropriate
s>0
. On
\Theta
we consider a measurable ‘forward’ map

(2.5)

\mathscr{G}:\Theta\to L_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathcal{X},V)

into another
L^{2}
-type space of vector fields; specifically, for an arbitrary
probability
space
(\mathcal{X},\mathscr{X},\lambda)
and a finite-dimensional normed vector space
(V,|\cdot|_{V})
with norm
|\cdot|_{V}
arising from an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
, we define as before by
L_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathcal{X},V)\equiv\times_{i=1}^{d_{V}}L_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
the resulting Hilbert spaces of
V
-valued vector fields, where
d_{V}=\dim(V)
.

2.2.
Gaussian regression model and Gaussian process priors

We now consider a Gaussian regression model with regression maps
\{\mathscr{G}(\theta):\theta\in\Theta\}
, specifically we observe
(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
arising from the equation,

(2.6)

Y_{i}=\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \varepsilon_{i}\sim^{\mathrm{iid}}\mathcal{N}(0,I_{V}),\penalty\ i=1,\dots,N,

where the
X_{i}
are i.i.d. drawn from
\lambda
, and with random noise vectors
\varepsilon_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{V})
where
I_{V}
is the identity matrix on
V
. The joint product law of
(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
on
(V\times\mathcal{X})^{N}
will be denoted by
P_{\theta}^{N}=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}P_{\theta}
where
P_{\theta}
is the law of a generic observation
(Y,X)=(Y_{1},X_{1})
. The corresponding expectation operators are denoted by
E_{\theta}^{N}
and
E_{\theta}
, respectively. The log-likelihood function for given data vector is then (up to additive constants) given

(2.7)

\ell_{N}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R},\penalty\ \penalty\ \ell_{N}(\theta)=\ell_{N}(\theta,(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y_{i}-\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_{i})|_{V}^{2},

and we also define the log-likelihood ‘per observation’

(2.8)

\ell:\Theta\to\mathbb{R},\penalty\ \penalty\ \ell(\theta)=\ell(\theta,(Y,X))=-\frac{1}{2}|Y-\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X)|_{V}^{2}.

To construct an ‘
\alpha
-regular’ high-dimensional Bayesian model, we consider random Gaussian series

\theta=N^{-d/(4\alpha+2d)}\theta^{\prime},\penalty\ \text{ where }\penalty\ \theta^{\prime}=\sum_{i\leq d_{W}}\sum_{n\leq D_{W}}\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha/2}g_{n,i}e_{n,i},\penalty\ \penalty\ g_{n,i}\sim^{\mathrm{iid}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),\penalty\ \alpha>0,

and with
d
from (
2.4
). We denote by
\Pi
the prior law
\mathcal{L}(\theta)
on
\Theta
, i.e., the normal distribution

(2.9)

\Pi=\mathcal{N}\big(0,(N\delta_{N}^{2})^{-1}\Lambda_{\alpha}\big)\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{D}\simeq E_{D},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where }\delta_{N}=N^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+d)},

with covariance matrix
\Lambda_{\alpha}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha})
and probability density
\pi=\mathrm{d}\Pi
. Assuming joint measurability of
(x,\theta)\to\mathscr{G}(\theta)(x)
(implied by Condition
2.2
b) below), the posterior distribution on
\Theta
that arises from this prior is then given by

(2.10)

\Pi(B|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})=\frac{\int_{B}e^{\ell_{N}(\theta)}d\Pi(\theta)}{\int_{E_{D}}e^{\ell_{N}(\theta)}d\Pi(\theta)},\penalty\ \penalty\ B\subseteq E_{D}\penalty\ \text{ Borel},

and has a Lebesgue-probability density function on
\mathbb{R}^{D}\simeq E_{D}
equal to

\pi(\theta|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})\propto\exp\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y_{i}-\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_{i})|_{V}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}N\delta_{N}^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\tilde{H}^{\alpha}}\Big\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \theta\in E_{D}.

2.3.
Analytic hypotheses

We now formulate some general conditions on uniformly bounded forward maps
\mathscr{G}
that will allow us to formulate our main theorems. These conditions are split into ‘global’ and ‘local’ types and we start with the global ones.

Condition 2.2
.

For
\mathscr{G}:\Theta\to L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{X},V)
from (
2.5
), assume the following:

a) [uniform boundedness] There exists a constant
U_{\mathscr{G}}\geq 1
such that

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta,x\in\mathcal{X}}|\mathscr{G}(\theta)(x)|_{V}\leq U_{\mathscr{G}}.

b) [global Lipschitz] There exists a fixed constant
C_{\mathscr{G}}\geq 1
s.t. for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta

\|\mathscr{G}(\theta)-\mathscr{G}(\theta^{\prime})\|_{*}\leq C_{\mathscr{G}}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{+},

for norms
\|\cdot\|_{*}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
,

2.4.
The log-concave approximation theorem

Broadly speaking the results in this section are modelled after the proof of Theorem 4.14 in
[
48
]
where the forward map
\mathscr{G}
arises from a steady state Schrödinger equation. The main additional feature accounted for here is to deal with the quantitative interaction of the ‘local’ and ‘global’ ill-posedness of a general map
\mathscr{G}
described by the parameters
\kappa_{0},\gamma
from (
2.5
) and the radius
\eta
of the region where average curvature holds. In the Schrödinger model the ‘local’ ill-posedness dominates which simplifies the proof, but for other inverse problems the various high-dimensional regimes occurring can lead to different outcomes. We note that while
[
48
]
do provide a concentration result for the empirical Hessian of

for general
\mathscr{G}
, their log-concave approximation theorem is restricted to the particular Schrödinger forward map they consider – Theorem

below is thus the first result of this kind for general
\mathscr{G}
.

2.4.1.
The surrogate posterior measure

Let
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
be any fixed vector in
E_{D}
such that

(2.18)

\|\theta_{\mathrm{init}}-\theta_{\star,D}\|_{E_{D}}\leq\eta/8

for some
\eta>0
from Condition
2.5
. For the purposes of log-concave approximations one may well take
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}=\theta_{\star,D}
but for applications to bounds on mixing times of ‘feasible’ MCMC type algorithms, an explicit initialiser

has to be constructed.

We follow Definition 3.5 in
[
48
]
and require two auxiliary functions,
g_{\eta}
(globally convex) and
\alpha_{\eta}
(cut-off function): For some smooth and symmetric (about
0
) function

\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
\text{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq[-1,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)dx=1
, let us define the mollifiers
\varphi_{h}(x):=h^{-1}\varphi(x/h),h>0
. Then, we define the functions
\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta},\gamma_{\eta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.19)

\begin{split}\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}(t)\penalty\ &:=\begin{cases}0\penalty\ \penalty\ &\text{if}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ t<5\eta/8,\\
(t-5\eta/8)^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ &\text{if}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ t\geq 5\eta/8,\end{cases}\\
\gamma_{\eta}(t)\penalty\ &:=\big[\varphi_{\eta/8}\ast\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}\big](t),\end{split}

where
\ast
denotes convolution. Further, let
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be smooth and satisfy
\alpha(t)=1
for
t\in[0,3/4]
and
\alpha(t)=0
for
t\in[7/8,\infty)
. We then define functions

and
\alpha_{\eta}:E_{D}\to[0,1]
as

(2.20)

g_{\eta}(\theta):=\gamma_{\eta}(\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\|_{E_{D}}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \alpha_{\eta}(\theta)=\alpha\big(\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\|_{E_{D}}/\eta\big).

Now for

as in (

) and

\tilde{\ell}_{N}(\theta)=\alpha_{\eta}(\theta)\ell_{N}(\theta)-Kg_{\eta}(\theta),\penalty\ \theta\in E_{D},

which ‘convexifies’

in the ‘tails’. The resulting renormalised Gibbs-type probability measure
\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
then has probability density

(2.22)

\tilde{\pi}(\theta|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})=\frac{e^{\tilde{\ell}_{N}(\theta)}\pi(\theta)}{\int_{E_{D}}e^{\tilde{\ell}_{N}(\theta)}\pi(\theta)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \theta\in E_{D}.

2.4.2.
Main result

The following theorem establishes that
\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
is strongly log-concave with high probability, and that moreover the posterior measure
\Pi(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
is very well approximated in Wasserstein distance from (
2.24
) by
\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
. ‘In probability’ here means for
(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\sim P_{\theta_{\star}}^{N}
where
\theta_{\star}\in\Theta
is well approximated by its
L^{2}_{\zeta}
-projection
\theta_{\star,D}
onto
E_{D}
;

(2.23)

\|\mathscr{G}(\theta_{\star})-\mathscr{G}(\theta_{\star,D})\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq\delta_{N}/2u,

where
u=2(U_{\mathscr{G}}+1)
. The last condition is a natural ‘bias requirement’ and for ground truths
\theta_{\star}\in\tilde{H}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{Z})
will require ‘high dimensions’, i.e., that
D\simeq N^{\beta}
diverges, where
\beta=\beta(\alpha,d)>0
. Also, as the nature of the following theorem is ‘local’ it is natural to assume
\eta\leq 1
, which also simplifies several technical bounds in the proofs.

The Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
between probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
is

(2.24)

W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D}}|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta,\theta^{\prime}),

where the infimum is taken over all couplings
\pi
of
\mu
and
\nu
.

Theorem 2.6

(Log-concave Wasserstein-approximation)
.

Let
(e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),\tau,d
and
\Theta
be as in Condition
2.1
. Let the posterior distribution
\Pi(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
from (
2.10
) arise from the Gaussian process prior
\Pi
in (
2.9
) and data
(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\sim P_{\theta}^{N}
from (
2.6
). Suppose
\mathscr{G}
from (
2.5
) satisfies Condition
2.2
for some
0<\gamma\leq 1
as well as Condition
2.5
for some
\eta\leq 1
. Assume further that
D\leq A_{1}N^{d/(2\alpha+d)}
for some
A_{1}<\infty
and that
\theta_{\star}\in\tilde{H}^{\alpha}\cap\Theta
satisfies (
2.23
), for

(2.25)

\alpha>d,\penalty\ \alpha\geq d\big(\tau+\frac{1}{2}\big).

Let the surrogate posterior measure
\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
be as in (

) for the given value of
\eta
and any

A) For all
\theta\in\{\theta\in E_{D}:\|\theta-\theta_{\star,D}\|_{E_{D}}\leq 3\eta/8\}
we have
\tilde{\ell}_{N}(\theta)=\ell_{N}(\theta)
and hence also

\nabla\log\tilde{\pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})(\theta)=\nabla\log\pi(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})(\theta).

B) On an event

of

is strongly log-concave on

\inf_{\theta\in E_{D}}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(-\nabla^{2}\log\tilde{\pi}(\theta|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}))\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}ND^{-\kappa_{0}},

and moreover

.

C) On an event

of

W^{2}_{2}\big(\Pi(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}),\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})\big)\leq e^{-N^{d/(2\alpha+d)}}.

The theorem is ‘non-asymptotic’ in the sense that whenever the hypotheses hold for pairs

, then so do the conclusions (which are informative only when

).

Some main ideas underpinning the proof of this theorem are as follows: By Condition
2.4
and tools from concentration of measure, the function
\ell_{N}
and hence also the posterior distribution are
locally
log-concave near
\theta_{\star,D}
, with high probability. Then our proof shows and exploits the fact that the surrogate posterior density
\tilde{\pi}(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
has as unique maximiser the
maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MAP}}
, again with high probability – see (
5.35
). Next we use that under Condition
2.2
the posterior measure is statistically consistent under
P_{\theta_{\star}}^{N}
(see §
5.1
) and hence charges most of its mass precisely to the neighbourhood of
\theta_{\star,D}
where
\Pi(\cdot|(Y_{i},X_{i})_{i=1}^{N})
is log-concave. Making the last statement quantitative in Part C) is the main challenge in the proof.

The log-concave approximations provided by the previous theorem are qualitatively different from commonly used Gaussian ‘Laplace approximations’ for numerical integration of Gibbs measures (
1.1
) (see
[
68
]
and also
[
5
,
33
,
59
,
25
]
for recent references), which are related to Bernstein-von Mises theorems (
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
54
,
41
,
46
,
44
,
38
]
and references therein). In high-dimensional situations these hold only under restrictive analytical conditions (e.g.,
[
43
]
) and in particular with slower convergence rates than the approximations obtained in Theorem
2.6
. The added flexibility of being able to approximate from the (infinite-dimensional) class of
log-concave measures
instead of just from normal distributions is essential in our context. See also Remark 5.1.4 in
[
42
]
for more discussion.

While the surrogate posterior
\tilde{\Pi}(\cdot|D_{N})
proposed here is constructive (up to selection of
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
), it involves choices of constants that may not be practical. However, for the key applications of Langevin-type MCMC methods, this is immaterial, as the gradients of the log-posterior and the surrogate log-posterior coincide near
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
by Part A) of the preceding theorem. If a Markov chain such as the ones to be discussed below is started in this region, it will rarely (if ever) exit the region where the bulk of the posterior mass lies and hence not enter the region where the surrogate construction and the choice of its constants become relevant. While the last argument can be made rigorous via Markov chain hitting times, we abstain from giving the details here.

3.
Main results for non-Abelian X-ray transforms

We now describe here the concrete results we obtain when the map
\mathscr{G}
arises from a
non-Abelian X-ray transform
. While we review the essential details, we refer to
[
52
,
39
]
for general background and references, and adopt their general geometric notation.

Throughout, for
m\geq 2
we denote by
SO(m)=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}:A^{T}A=\mathrm{id},\det A=1\}
the special orthogonal group, with Lie-algebra

\mathfrak{so}(m)=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}:A^{T}=-A\}
consisting of skew-symmetric
m\times m
-matrices. We equip the vector spaces
\mathfrak{so}(m)
and
\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
with the Frobenius-norm
|A|_{F}=\tr(A^TA)^{1/2}
.

Let
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the open unit disk with boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
and so called ‘influx and outflux’ boundaries
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
and
\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
, defined by

(3.1)

\partial_{\pm}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}=\{(x,v)\in\partial\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}:\mp x\cdot v\geq 0\},

where
S^{1}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|v|=1\}
is the unit circle.
Then
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
parametrises the set of line segments
\gamma_{x,v}(t)=x+tv
in
\mathbb{D}
,
0\leq t\leq\tau(x,v)
, where
\tau(x,v)=-2x\cdot v
is the hitting time with
\partial\mathbb{D}
. The space
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
is diffeomorphic to a cylinder and will be equipped with its normalised area measure
\lambda
, which is given in fan-beam coordinates
(\alpha,\beta)\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
(described in §
4.3.1
) by

(3.2)

\lambda=(2\pi^{2})^{-1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta.

Let
C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))
be the space of
\mathfrak{so}(m)
-valued maps on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
that are continuous up to the boundary. The
non-Abelian X-ray transform
is the non-linear map

(3.3)

\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}),

which associates to a matrix field
\Phi
its
scattering data

C_{\Phi}
, defined as follows: for every
(x,v)\in\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
we can solve a matrix ODE along the line segment
\gamma_{x,v}
,

(3.4)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u(t)+\Phi(\gamma_{x,v}(t))u(t)=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u(\tau(x,v))=\mathrm{id},

formulated as end-value problem by convention; we then define

(3.5)

C_{\Phi}(x,v):=u(0)\in SO(m)

as the resulting initial value. By standard ODE theory,
C_{\Phi}
is a continuous function of
(x,v)\in\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
and thus can be viewed as element in the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{R}^{m\times m})
.

Finite dimensional approximation spaces
E_{D}\subset C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))
are spanned by the classical
Zernike polynomials
, which are part of an SVD of the derivative of
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}
at
\Phi=0

[
30
]
. Postponing a full definition of
E_{D}
to (
6.6
), we mention here that if
D\in\mathbb{N}
is of the form
D=\dim\mathfrak{so}(m)\cdot(D^{\prime}+1)(D^{\prime}+2)/2
for some
D^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

(3.6)

E_{D}=E_{D}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{so}(m))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(m):\text{Each matrix entry }\Phi_{ij}(x_{1},x_{2})\\
\text{is a polynomial in }x_{1},x_{2}\text{ of order }\leq D^{\prime}\end{array}\right\}.

The space
E_{D}
is a
D
-dimensional subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R}^{m\times m})
and (after choosing an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{so}(m)
) can be naturally identified with
\mathbb{R}^{D}
; we sometimes make this identification explicit via the map

(3.7)

\mathbb{R}^{D}\rightarrow E_{D},\quad\theta\mapsto\Phi_{\theta}

and refer to
\theta
as the ‘Fourier coefficients’ of
\Phi_{\theta}
.

What precedes fits into the framework of the §
2
by making the following choices:

Local gradient stability for the X-ray transform.
In order to apply the general theory from §

need to be verified. In the present setting, the main non-trivial properties are a
global stability estimate
(Condition

The Gateaux derivative of the non-Abelian X-ray transform

in (

\Phi\in C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))
and in direction

\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}h:=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}C_{\Phi+th}\in L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m)).

It can be shown to extend as bounded linear map between

.

Suppose

for a constant

For

from

The proof of the theorem (see Remark

.

Computation time bounds and approximation of the Gibbs measure.
We now state our main results on log-concave approximation and polynomial time sampling of posterior measures in the context of the non-Abelian X-ray transform.

Analogous to §

and with the choices made surrounding (

where

\mathbb{D}

, where this noise model for the non-Abelian X-ray transform is discussed in detail. The law of

is now denoted

The prior

\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\dots

The bias condition (

\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\frac{\delta_{N}}{2\sqrt{m}+1},\quad\delta_{N}=N^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+2)}.

The general Wasserstein approximation result from Theorem

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>5
and suppose that
D\leq A_{1}N^{1/(\alpha+1)}
for some
A_{1}>0
. Let
\Pi(\cdot|Z^{(N)})
be the posterior distribution on

described in §

following model (

.

Suppose the hypotheses of the previous theorem hold and that there exists an initialiser

4.
Mapping properties of X-ray transforms in Zernike scales

The results on the non-Abelian X-ray transform from §
3
rely on novel forward- and stability estimates for its linearisation
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}
. These are of independent interest and will be discussed and proved in this section; here we pass to a complex valued setting, noting that the group
SO(m)
in Section
3
can at any point be replaced by the unitary group
U(m)
which has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(m)=\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}:A^{*}=-A\}
; moreover, within this section, function spaces with suppressed codomain are understood to contain
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions (that is,
L^{2}(\mathbb{D})=L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
etc.).

The linearisation
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}
from (
3.10
) can be expressed in terms of the
attenuated X-ray transform
, which is a linear map

(4.1)

I_{\Phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{C}^{m}),

defined for
\Phi\in C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{u}(m))
in the following manner: Consider the
phase space

S\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}=\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:|v|=1\}
, whose elements are tuples
(x,v)
of points and directions. Its closure
S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
has as boundary
\partial S\bar{\mathbb{D}}=\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\cup\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.
Given a continuous function
f:\bar{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
we define

(4.2)

I_{\Phi}f=u_{\Phi}|_{\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}},

where
u=u_{\Phi}:S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the unique continuous solution of the transport problem

(4.3)

(X+\Phi)u=-f\text{ on }S\mathbb{D}\quad\text{and }\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}.

Here
X=v\cdot\nabla_{x}
is the
geodesic vector field
and
\Phi
and
f
are viewed as functions on
S\mathbb{D}
(constant in the
v
-variable). Note that (
4.3
) is equivalent to solving an ODE along every line
\gamma_{x,v}(t)=x+tv
and existence of
u
follows from standard arguments. On
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
the transform
I_{\Phi}
is defined by continuous extension, using well known forward estimates (e.g., Theorem
4.2
for
s=0
).

The non-Abelian X-ray transform
C_{\Phi}
from (
3.3
) is related to this linear transform via a
pseudolinearisation identity
from
[
52
]
, see also
[
39
, Lem. 5.5]
. This takes the form

(4.4)

C_{\Phi}C_{\Psi}^{-1}-\mathrm{id}=I_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}(\Phi-\Psi),\quad\Phi,\Psi\in C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{u}(m))

where
\Xi(\Phi,\Psi):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}(m^{2})
is defined as follows: For
x\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)(x):\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be the linear map
\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)(x)A=\Phi(x)A-A\Psi(x)
. Then
\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)(x)
is skew-hermitian with respect to the Frobenius inner product and thus may be viewed as element in
\mathfrak{u}(m^{2})
; the attenuated X-ray transform in the previous display is then to be understood ‘one level higher’, acting on
\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}\cong\mathbb{C}^{m^{2}}
-valued functions. By virtue of (
4.4
), injectivity and stability questions for
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}
reduce to linear problems; moreover one can show (cf. Lemma
4.5
below) that the Gateaux derivative
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}
can be expressed as

(4.5)

\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}h\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}C_{\Phi+th}=\big(I_{\Xi(\Phi,\Phi)}h\big)C_{\Phi}\in L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{u}(m)),\quad\Phi,h\in C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{u}(m)).

Whenever it is convenient, we will think of
I_{\Phi}
as the linearisation of
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}
, bearing in mind that eventually one has to pass to
\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}=I_{\Xi}\cdot C_{\Phi}
.

4.1.
Analytical results

Our results are formulated in a non-standard scale
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
of Sobolev-type spaces studied systematically in
[
36
]
. This so called
Zernike scale
is defined in terms of the (normalised)
Zernike-polynomials

\hat{Z}_{nk}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
(
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},0\leq k\leq n
), defined in (
4.7
) below. Precisely, for
s\geq 0
we have

(4.6)

\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m}):\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},0\leq k\leq n}(1+n)^{2s}|f_{n,k}|^{2}<\infty\right\},

where
f_{n,k}=\langle f,\hat{Z}_{n,k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
are the coefficients of
f
in the Zernike basis.
The definition can be extended to
s<0
by duality, or equivalently by relaxing the requirement that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
to
f
being a
supported distribution
in
\dot{C}^{-\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{C}^{m})\supset L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, as described in
[
38
, §6.4.2]
. Further, we write

C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})

(k\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
for the Banach space of functions on
\mathbb{D}
, which are
k
-times continuously differentiable up to the boundary.

Theorem 4.1

(Stability estimate)
.

Suppose
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(m)
is of regularity
C^{4}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
and its support is contained in a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{D}
. Then

\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{-1/2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\leq C(\Phi,K)\cdot\|I_{\Phi}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
and a constant
C(\Phi,K)>0
which (for fixed
K
) depends continuously on
\Phi
in the
C^{4}
-topology.

The uniform constant
c>0
in Theorem
3.1
is obtained from the continuity statement in Theorem
4.1
and the compact embedding
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset C^{4}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
(
\alpha>5
, see Remark
6.5
).

The preceding Theorem is sharp in that it captures the
\frac{1}{2}
-ill-posedness of the attenuated X-ray transform. For smooth fields
\Phi
the stability estimate can be seen to follow from the
normal operator

N_{\Phi}=I_{\Phi}^{*}I_{\Phi}
being a Hilbert space isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
onto
\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, which is the content of
[
38
, Thm. 6.18]
.
Our contribution here lies in extending the result to lower regularity
\Phi
, and in the associated continuity statement. The challenge is to bypass the (smooth) microlocal analysis methods that underpin the results in
[
38
]
to obtain mapping properties of
N_{\Phi}
for
\Phi
being of
finite
regularity as is relevant in the statistical results from §
3
. We achieve this by analysing certain singular integral operators ‘by hand’ (requiring
four derivatives
on
\Phi
in order to regularise) and establish Lipschitz type bounds for the maps
\Phi\mapsto N_{\Phi}
. This allows to pass to a finite regularity setting by means of an approximation argument.

We emphasise here that the codomain of
I_{\Phi}
(relevant to define
N_{\Phi}
) is the
L^{2}
-space with respect to the canonical area form
\lambda
, (
3.2
). Mapping properties of
I_{\Phi}
are also frequently studied with respect to the ‘symplectic measure’ on
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
(e.g. in
[
37
]
) and we refer to
[
38
, Remark 2.3]
for a discussion of the choice of measure in the present setting.

The stability result is complemented by a fitting forward estimate in the Zernike scale over
\mathbb{D}
. Note that
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
is a compact manifold with boundary, hence there is a standard scale of Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
(
s\in\mathbb{R}
, see e.g.
[
62
, §4.4]
).

Theorem 4.2

(Forward estimate)
.

Let
s\geq 0
and suppose
\Phi
is of regularity
C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
, where
k=2\lceil s/2\rceil
(the smallest even integer
\geq s
). Then for all
f\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
we have

\|I_{\Phi}f\|_{H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq C\cdot\big(1+\|\Phi\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}\big)^{k}\cdot\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}

for a constant
C=C(s)>0
. For
s=1
one may also take
k=1
.

For zero attenuation this can be strenghtened to the mapping property
I_{0}:\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{s+1/2}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})

[
36
, Prop. 16]
. At present it is not clear how to obtain
\frac{1}{2}
-smoothing forward estimates for general potentials
\Phi
.

4.2.
Preliminaries

Below we use the usual notation
A\lesssim B

as shorthand for ‘
A\leq CB
, with a constant
C>0
independent of the relevant quantities’. If we want to emphasise a certain dependence
C=C(a,b,\dots)
, we write
A\lesssim_{a,b,\dots}B
.

4.2.1.
Zernike scale and the operator
\mathcal{L}

The Zernike polynomials (and their normalisation) are defined in a complex coordinate
z\in\mathbb{D}
by

(4.7)

Z_{nk}(z)=\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{z}^{k}\left(z^{n}(\frac{1}{z}-\bar{z})^{k}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{Z}_{nk}(z)=\frac{Z_{nk}(z)}{\|Z_{nk}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq k\leq n
and we follow the labelling convention from
[
30
]
. They form a complete orthogonal system of
L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and are part of a well known SVD of the unattenuated X-ray transform
I_{0}

[
30
]
. More recently it was observed that Zernike polynomials form an eigenbasis of a certain
2
nd order differential operator
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathbb{D}

[
36
]
, given by

(4.8)

\mathcal{L}=(4\pi^{2})^{-1}\left(\xi^{*}\xi+\eta^{*}\eta+1\right)

in terms of the complex vector fields

\xi=\sqrt{|z|^{-2}-1}\cdot(z\partial_{z}+\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})
and
\eta=i|z|^{-1}\cdot(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})

as well as their
L^{2}
-adjoints
\xi^{*}
and
\eta^{*}
. We use the same prefactor as in
[
38
]
and refer to there for a description of
\mathcal{L}
in polar coordinates. A computation shows that indeed
\mathcal{L}Z_{nk}=\left(\frac{1+n}{4\pi}\right)^{2}Z_{nk}
, such that alternatively the Zernike scale is defined by

(4.9)

\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})=\dom\mathcal{L}^{s/2},\quad s\geq 0

where
\mathcal{L}
is viewed as self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
with domain
\dom\mathcal{L}=\tilde{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{L}^{s/2}
, together with its natural domain, are defined via the spectral theorem.

The operator
\mathcal{L}
is elliptic in
\mathbb{D}
, but degenerates at
\partial\mathbb{D}
as
\xi
vanishes at the boundary. As consequence the Zernike scale is non-standard near
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Nevertheless it shares many features with usual Sobolev spaces. We refer to
[
38
, Lem. 6.14]
for a Rellich compactness theorem and interpolation properties. For the comparison with the standard Sobolev scale see
[
38
, Lem. 6.15]
(stating that
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})
and
H^{s}(\mathbb{D})
are equivalent on compactly supported functions) and
[
36
, Lem. 14]
, which implies the embeddings (strict for
s>0
)

(4.10)

\tilde{H}^{2s+\epsilon}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})\subset\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}),\quad s\geq 0,\epsilon>0.

For future reference we record that

(4.11)

\|Z_{nk}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=\left(\pi/(n+1)\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|Z_{nk}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}=1,

(see e.g.
[
36
, Appendix A.2]
) and, as can easily be observed from (
4.7
),

(4.12)

\overline{Z_{nk}}=(-1)^{n}Z_{n,n-k}\quad\text{ and }\quad Z_{nk}(uz)=u^{n-2k}Z_{nk}(z)\penalty\ (u\in S^{1},z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
u
is viewed as complex number to make sense of its exponents.

4.2.2.
Bounds on integrating factors

Given an attenuation
\Phi\in C(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{u}(m))
, we write
R_{\Phi}:S\mathbb{D}\rightarrow U(m)
for the unique continuous solution (differentiable along
X
) of

(4.13)

(X+\Phi)R_{\Phi}=0\text{ on }S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\quad\text{ and }\quad R_{\Phi}=\mathrm{id}\text{ on }\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}.

This is an example of an
integrating factor
for
\Phi
and enters several computations below; we thus collect here some useful bounds. Using a similar notation for a second attenuation
\Psi
and writing
R^{\pm 1}
for
R
or its inverse
R^{-1}
, we have

(4.14)

\|R^{\pm 1}_{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{m}1\quad\text{ and }\quad\|R^{\pm 1}_{\Phi}-R^{\pm 1}_{\Psi}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{m}\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}.

Further, assuming that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are of regularity
C^{k}
(
k\geq 1
) and have their support contained in a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{D}
, also
R_{\Phi},R_{\Psi}
are of regularity
C^{k}
and obey

(4.15)

\begin{split}\|R^{\pm 1}_{\Phi}\|_{C^{k}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}&\lesssim_{m,k,K}(1+\|\Phi\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})})^{k}\\

Remark 4.3
.

In general, one can avoid compact support conditions on

and

Further, any pair of maps

Remark 4.4
.

The estimates above will often be used ‘one level higher’ without further mention. This means that

is replaced by an attenuation

4.2.3.

Lemma 4.5

Let

Proof.

The pseudolinearisation formula (

4.3.
Proof of forward estimate

The attenuated X-ray transform can be written as

(4.26)

I_{\Phi}f=R_{\Phi}I(R_{\Phi}^{-1}f),\quad\text{ on }\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}

where
I
here denotes the general, unattenuated X-ray transform on
S\mathbb{D}

(i.e.
Iu(x,v)=\int_{0}^{\tau(x,v)}u(x+tv,v)\mathrm{d}t
for
u\in C(S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{C}^{m})
)
and
R_{\Phi}:S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow U(m)
is an integrating factor for
\Phi
, that is, a solution to
(X+\Phi)R_{\Phi}=0
on
S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
. In our context, when
\Phi
has regularity

, the integrating factor can be chosen of regularity

(cf. Remark
4.3
).

On
S\mathbb{D}
we define a scale, which captures ‘Zernike-regularity’ in the spatial variable and standard Sobolev regularity in the phase variable. Precisely, for
s\geq 0
let

\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(S\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}):\|u\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\ell\rangle^{2s}\|u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|u_{\ell}\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}<\infty\Big\}.

where
u_{\ell}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the
\ell
th vertical Fourier mode, given by

u_{\ell}(x)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-i\ell t}u\left(x,(\cos t,\sin t)\right)\mathrm{d}t,\quad u\in L^{2}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{C})

and
\langle\ell\rangle=\left(1+\ell^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. Here
\tilde{H}^{0}(S\mathbb{D})=L^{2}(S\mathbb{D})
is the
L^{2}
-space with respect to the natural Lebesgue measure on

. As usual, one defines a version of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
-valued spaces.

Theorem
4.2
follows from forward estimates of the general transform
I
on
\tilde{H}^{s}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
, together with operator norm bounds for
R_{\Phi}^{\pm 1}
, acting via multiplication. These assertions are the content of the next two lemmas, formulated in the scalar case for convenience.

Lemma 4.6
.

For all
s\geq 0
and
u\in\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})
we have

\|Iu\|_{H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim_{s}\|u\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})}.

Note that the transform
I:L^{2}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
, acting on functions on
S\mathbb{D}
(rather than
\mathbb{D}
),
is
not

1/2
-regularising, resulting in the mapping property in Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s\geq 0
, and
k=2\lceil s/2\rceil\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if
s=1
also
k=1
is allowed). Then multiplication is continuous in the following settings:

(4.27)

\displaystyle C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})\times\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})\qquad

(4.28)

\displaystyle C^{k}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})\times\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})\qquad

The lemmata are proved below, first we complete the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let
f\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
(
s\geq 0
) and
k
as in Lemma
4.7
. Then

(4.29)

\begin{split}\|I_{\Phi}f\|_{H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}&\leq\|R_{\Phi}\|_{C^{k}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\|I(R_{\Phi}^{-1}f)\|_{H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\\
&\lesssim\|R_{\Phi}\|_{C^{k}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|R_{\Phi}^{-1}f\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})}\\
&\lesssim\|R_{\Phi}\|_{C^{k}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|R_{\Phi}^{-1}\|_{C^{k}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})},\end{split}

where we used that
C^{k}
-functions act continuously (via multiplication) on

and then applied Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
. Now, as

is a function on
\mathbb{D}
, its Fourier modes

for

, such that the Zernike-norm on

turns into

. Finally, we have

\|R_{\Phi}^{\pm 1}\|_{C^{k}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\|R_{\Phi}^{\pm 1}\|_{C^{k}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim(1+\|\Phi\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})})^{k},

4.3.2.
Estimate on products

The proof of Lemma
4.7
relies on the following three identities for Zernike polynomials:

(4.42)

\displaystyle zZ_{nk}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\nicefrac{{k}}{{n+1}}\right)Z_{n+1,k}-(\nicefrac{{k}}{{n+1}})Z_{n-1,k-1}

(4.43)

\displaystyle(1-z\bar{z})\partial_{\bar{z}}Z_{nk}

\displaystyle=(n-k+1)(zZ_{nk}-Z_{n+1,k})

(4.44)

\displaystyle(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})Z_{nk}

\displaystyle=i(n-2k)Z_{nk},

where we set
Z_{nk}=0
if
n<0
or
|n-2k|>n
. The second and third identity were kindly communicated to us by François Monard; all three can be verified by a straightforward computation that we omit here.

The virtue of the identities is that they immediately give mapping properties for certain model operators in the Zernike scale. For example, (
4.42
) implies that multiplication by
z
gives a continuous map
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})
. Using this together with the remaining two identities, we see that all of the operators

(4.45)

(1-|z|^{2})\partial_{\bar{z}},\quad(1-|z|^{2})\partial_{z},\quad\text{ and }\quad z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}

map
\tilde{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})
(
s\geq 0
) continuously. Here, the assertion for the second operator, which is the conjugate of the first one, follows from symmetry property (
4.12
).

Proof of Lemma
4.7
.

The proof of (
4.27
) will be explained in detail, the one for (
4.28
) is very similar and the necessary changes are discussed at the very end.
Recall from (
4.8
) the representation of the operator
\mathcal{L}
in terms of the first order operators

\xi=\sqrt{|z|^{-2}-1}\cdot(z\partial_{z}+\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta=i|z|^{-1}\cdot(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})

and their

-adjoints
\xi^{*},\eta^{*}
. Let
u,v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and assume for now that
u
vanishes for

. Then
\xi^{*}(uv)=(\xi^{*}u)v-u\xi v
(similarly for

(4.46)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(uv)=&\penalty\ (\mathcal{L}u)v+u\mathcal{L}v-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left[uv+2\xi u\cdot\xi v+2\eta u\cdot\eta v\right].\end{split}

We want to show by induction over
s\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}
, that

(4.47)

\|uv\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{s}\|u\|_{C^{s}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}.

This is trivially true for

and prove it for

\begin{split}\|uv\|_{\tilde{H}^{s+2}(\mathbb{D})}=\|\mathcal{L}(uv)\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\leq&\penalty\ \|(\mathcal{L}u)v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}+\|u(\mathcal{L}v)\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}+\|uv\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\\
&+\|\xi u\cdot\xi v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}+\|\eta u\cdot\eta v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\end{split}

and by the induction hypothesis the first three terms on the right hand side can be bounded by (a constant times)

, as desired. To bound the remaining terms note that

(4.48)

\begin{split}\xi u\cdot\xi v&=(|z|^{-2}-1)\cdot\left(z\partial_{z}u+\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}u\right)\left(z\partial_{z}v+\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}v\right)\\
&=\left[\frac{z}{\bar{z}}\partial_{z}u+\partial_{\bar{z}}u\right]\cdot(1-|z|^{2})\partial_{z}v+\left[\partial_{z}u+\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}u\right]\cdot(1-|z|^{2})\partial_{\bar{z}}v,\end{split}

whence the mapping properties discussed after (

) (together with the induction hypothesis) can be used to obtain

(4.49)

\|\xi u\cdot\xi v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{s,\delta}\|u\|_{C^{s+1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|v\|_{\tilde{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{D})}.

Finally,
\eta u\cdot\eta v=-|z|^{-2}(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})u\cdot(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})v
and since we have also derived mapping properties for
(z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}})
, (
4.49
) remains true with
\xi
replaced by
\eta
. We have thus established (
4.47
) for
u
vanishing in a disk
|z|\leq\delta
.

The restriction that
u
shall vanish in a neighbourhood of zero is easily removed by a cutoff procedure, as the scales
\tilde{H}^{s}
and
H^{s}
are equivalent on compactly supported functions
[
38
, Lemma 6.15]
and the analogue of (
4.47
) for the standard scale is well known.

Mapping property (
4.27
) for
s\in[0,\infty)
follows from an interpolation argument, noting that the Zernike scale satisfies the expected interpolation property
[
38
, Lemma 6.14]
. The remark about the special case
s=1
easily follows from

(4.50)

\|uv\|_{\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\|\xi(uv)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|\eta(uv)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|uv\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}

together with the Leibniz rule and the obvious estimates, noting that both
\xi
and
\eta
have coefficients which are bounded away from a neighbourhood of zero.

Finally, we discuss how the argument can be adapted to phase space
S\mathbb{D}
. The scale
\tilde{H}^{s}(S\mathbb{D})
is generated by the operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}=\mathcal{L}-V^{2}+1
, where
V\equiv\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\theta
is the vertical derivative (i.e.
\tilde{H}^{s}(S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
is the natural domain of
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s/2}
, defined via the spectral theorem). The argument from above can thus be applied almost verbatim, with the only difference that expansion (
4.46
) also contains vertical derivatives which are easily bounded.
∎

4.4.
Proof of stability estimate

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is based on a careful analysis of the normal operator associated to
I_{\Phi}
. Recall that this is defined by

(4.51)

N_{\Phi}=I_{\Phi}^{*}I_{\Phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m}),

where
I_{\Phi}^{*}
is the
L^{2}
-adjoint of
I_{\Phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.

We introduce the following notation: For
K\subset\mathbb{D}
compact and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we write
C_{K}^{k}(\mathbb{D})
for the space of
k
-times continuously differentiable functions on
\mathbb{D}
with support contained in
K
and equip it with the obvious Banach space norm. For two Hilbert spaces
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
we write
\mathcal{B}(H_{1},H_{2})
for the space of continuous linear operators between them, equipped with the operator norm;
\mathcal{B}^{\times}(H_{1},H_{2})
denotes the open subset of invertible operators.

Theorem 4.8

(Mapping properties of
N_{\Phi}
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{D}
be compact.

(i)

For
\Phi\in C^{2}_{K}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))
the normal operator
N_{\Phi}
maps
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
into
\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
. The following map is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets:

(4.52)

\Phi\mapsto N_{\Phi},\text{ as map }C_{K}^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{D}),\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D}))

(ii)

For
\Phi\in C_{K}^{4}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))
the normal operator
N_{\Phi}
is an isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
onto
\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
. The following map is continuous:

(4.53)

\Phi\mapsto N_{\Phi}^{-1}\text{ as map }C_{K}^{4}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}^{\times}(\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D}),L^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

The preceding theorem is proved in three steps:

(A)

For smooth
\Phi
, the operators
N_{\Phi}
and
K_{\Phi}=N_{\Phi}-N_{0}
are pseudodifferential operators of order
-1
and
-2
, respectively, admitting singular integral representation in terms of a smooth kernel
A(x,v,t)
. For operators of this type we derive norm bounds in terms of the kernel
A(x,v,t)
. Using a Calderón-Zygmund type argument, we only require bounds of the
2
nd and
4
th order derivatives of
A
.

(B)

Using these norm bounds, we follow the localisation procedure from
[
38
, Lem. 6.17]
to obtain (local) Lipschitz estimates for

\Phi\mapsto N_{\Phi}
and
\Phi\mapsto K_{\Phi}
, where the domain is equipped with
C^{2}
-norm and
C^{4}
-norm, respectively.

(C)

By means of the local Lipschitz estimates and an approximation argument, we obtain an analogue of
[
38
, Lem. 6.17]
for
\Phi
of regularity
C^{4}
. Additionally using stability estimate from
[
39
, Thm. 5.3]
to get injectivity of
N_{\Phi}
and
N_{\Phi}^{*}
for
\Phi\in C_{K}^{4}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))
, we can then imitate the proof of
[
38
, Thm. 6.18]
.

The next ingredient in the proof of Theorem
4.1
is a classical result of Heinz and Löwner on the ‘operator monotonicity’ of the square root
T\mapsto\sqrt{T}
(cf.
[
24
, Satz 3]
or
[
29
, §V.4, Thm. 4.12]
), combined with a duality argument. The result is purely functional analytic, and we formulate it on an arbitrary Hilbert space
H
and with respect to a scale

(4.54)

H_{L}^{s}=\dom(L^{s/2}),\quad(s\geq 0)

where
L
is a non-negative, self-adjoint operator in
H
and
L^{s/2}
is defined via the spectral theorem. We further assume that
L
is injective, such that

\|x\|_{H_{L}^{s}}=\|L^{s/2}x\|_{H}
(
x\in H^{s}_{L}
)
defines a norm that turns
H^{s}_{L}
into a Hilbert space in its own right.

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
N\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is a self-adjoint, non-negative operator. Assume additionally that
N\in\mathcal{B}^{\times}(H,H^{1}_{L})
and set
\underline{n}=\|N^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H^{1}_{L},H)}
.

(i)

Then
\sqrt{N}\in\mathcal{B}^{\times}(H,H^{1/2}_{L})
and
\|\sqrt{N}^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H^{1/2}_{L},H)}=\sqrt{\underline{n}}
.

(ii)

There is a stability estimate of the form

(4.55)

\|x\|^{2}_{(H^{1/2}_{L})^{*}}\leq\underline{n}\cdot\langle Nx,x\rangle_{H}\quad\text{ for all }x\in H.

Proof.

Given non-negative, self-adjoint operators
S,T
in
H
, let us write
S\leq T
if
\dom T^{1/2}\subset\dom S^{1/2}
and
\|S^{1/2}x\|_{H}\leq\|T^{1/2}x\|_{H}
for all
x\in\dom T^{1/2}
. Operator monotonicity of the square root is then the assertion that

(4.56)

0\leq S\leq T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\leq\sqrt{S}\leq\sqrt{T}.

We use this in the operator inequalities
0\leq\underline{n}^{-2}N^{-2}\leq L
and
0\leq L\leq\bar{n}^{2}N^{-2}
(where
\bar{n}=\|N\|_{\mathcal{B}(H,H_{L}^{1})}
), both of which follow from the asserted isomorphism property. Taking square roots we get
0\leq\underline{n}^{-1}N^{-1}\leq L^{1/2}\leq\bar{n}N^{-1}
. In particular
\range N^{1/2}=\dom N^{-1/2}=H_{L}^{1/2}
, which implies
N^{1/2}\in\mathcal{B}^{\times}(H,H_{A}^{1/2})
(injectivity is clear); further
\|N^{-1/2}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H_{A}^{1/2},H)}\leq\underline{n}^{1/2}
, which proves part
(i)
.
For
(ii)
write
B
for the unit ball in
H_{L}^{1/2}
, then by Cauchy-Schwarz

\|x\|_{(H_{L}^{1/2})^{*}}=\sup_{y\in B}\langle x,y\rangle_{H}=\sup_{y\in B}\langle N^{1/2}x,N^{-1/2}y\rangle_{H}\leq\sup_{y\in B}\|N^{-1/2}y\|_{H}\cdot\langle Nx,x\rangle_{H}^{1/2}

for all
x\in H
. By part
(i)
, we have
\sup_{y\in B}\|N^{-1/2}y\|_{H}\leq\underline{n}
, as desired.
∎

Combining the previous two results we can prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Put
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
,
L=\mathcal{L}
and let
\Phi\in C_{K}^{4}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))
. Then by Theorem
4.8
, the normal operator
N_{\Phi}
satisfies the requirements of Lemma
4.9
and
\underline{n}(\Phi)=\|N^{-1}_{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D}),L^{2}(\mathbb{D}))}
depends continuously on
\Phi
in the
C^{4}
-topology. As
\tilde{H}^{-1/2}(\mathbb{D})
is the dual space of
\tilde{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{D})
, we have

(4.57)

\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{-1/2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\leq\underline{n}(\Phi)\langle N_{\Phi}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=\underline{n}(\Phi)\|I_{\Phi}f\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, which is the desired stability estimate.
∎

In the remaining section we carry out the program outlined above, ultimately leading to the proof of Theorem
4.8
.

(Part A) Norm bounds for certain integral operators.
For a smooth function
A:\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
we denote with
T_{A}
the singular integral operator defined by

(4.58)

T_{A}f(x)=\int_{S^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(x,v,t)f(x+tv)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v,\quad x\in\mathbb{D}.

Here it is understood that
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(a smooth function with compact support in
\mathbb{D}
) is extended by zero outside of
\mathbb{D}
. From
[
13
, Lemma B.1]
we know that
T_{A}
is a classical pseudodifferential operator on
\mathbb{D}
of order
-1
with principal symbol

(4.59)

\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)=2\pi(A(x,\xi^{\perp},0)+A(x,-\xi^{\perp},0)),\quad x\in\mathbb{D},\xi\in S^{1}.

The present section provides bounds on the operator norm of
T_{A}
in different functional analytic settings in terms of the norms

(4.60)

\|A\|_{C^{k}}=\sup\{\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{3}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{4}}A\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{R})}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4},|\alpha|\leq k\}.

Precisely, we establish (writing
\ord
for the order of a differential operator):

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
P,Q
be differential operators on
\mathbb{D}
with coefficients in
C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
and put
m=\ord P+\ord Q
. Suppose that either
0\leq m\leq 1
or that
\sigma_{A}\equiv 0
and
0\leq m\leq 2
. Then

(4.61)

\|PT_{A}Qf\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\leq C\|A\|_{C^{2m}}\cdot\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})},\quad 0\leq s\leq m,f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

for a constant
C>0
which only depends on
P
and
Q
.

The proposition remains true if instead
A
takes values in
\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
; we confine ourselves to the scalar case for notational simplicity.

The strategy to prove Proposition
4.10
is to rewrite expressions like
\partial_{x_{j}}(T_{A}f)
and
T_{A}(\partial_{x_{j}}f)
in a form where all derivatives fall on
A
. To this end we
introduce a type of Hilbert transform that features in the next Lemma and is witness to the singular nature of
T_{A}
.

Definition 4.11

(Hilbert transform)
.

For a function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
we define

(4.62)

Hf(x,v)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|t|>\epsilon}f(x+tv)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\equiv\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{\epsilon}f(x,v),\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}.

The transform
Hf(x,v)
corresponds to the standard Hilbert transform (up to a sign in the argument) along rays parallel to the line
\{sv:s\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The following facts are thus derived as in the standard case and will be given without proof: For
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D})
the limit in (
4.62
) exists pointwise; further the transform
Hf(x,v)
is continuous and satisfies

(4.63)

\sup_{v\in S^{1}}\|Hf(\cdot,v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
A:\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be smooth and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. Then

(4.64)

T_{A}(\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x)=\pi\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)Hf(x,v)\mathrm{d}v+T_{\tilde{A}_{1}}f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{D}

where
\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)
and
\tilde{A}_{1}(x,v,t)
are derived from a Taylor expansion
A(x,v,t)=A_{0}(x,v)+tA_{1}(x,v,t)
via the formulas

(4.65)

\tilde{A}_{0}=\partial_{v}\left(v_{2}A_{0}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{A}_{1}=\partial_{v}(v_{2}A_{1})-\partial_{t}(tv_{1}A_{1}).

In particular we have
\|\tilde{A}_{0}\|_{C^{k}}\lesssim_{k}\|A\|_{C^{k+1}}
and
\|\tilde{A}_{1}\|_{C^{k}}\lesssim_{k}\|A\|_{C^{k+2}}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
).

Let us remark on a few aspects of the lemma. First, one has
A_{0}(x,v)=A(x,v,0)
and
A_{1}(x,v,t)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}A(x,v,st)\mathrm{d}s
and thus all of
A_{0},A_{1},\tilde{A}_{0},\tilde{A}_{1}
are smooth functions. Further, if the principal symbol
\sigma_{A}\equiv 0
, then
\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)
is an even function in the sense that
\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)=\tilde{A}_{0}(x,-v)
. As
Hf(x,v)
is odd, the first term in (
4.64
) vanishes. This reflects the fact that the operator
T_{A}
is then a pseudodifferential operator of order
-2
and implies that the Lemma may be applied twice.
We tacitly use the Lemma also for
x_{2}
-derivatives, where it is proved analogously.

Proof of Lemma
4.12
.

Using the expansion
A(x,v,t)=A_{0}(x,v)+tA_{1}(x,v,t)
, decompose

(4.66)

T_{A}(\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x)=T_{A_{0}}(\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x)+T_{A_{1}}(t\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x).

We investigate the two terms separately, starting with the second one. Writing
\tilde{f}(x,v,t)=f(x+tv)
, a straightforward calculation reveals that

(4.67)

t\partial_{x_{1}}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)=tv_{1}\partial_{t}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)-v_{2}\partial_{v}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)

and thus

\begin{split}T_{A_{1}}(t\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x)=\int_{S^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}tv_{1}A_{1}(x,v,t)\partial_{t}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v-\int_{S^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{2}A_{1}(x,v,t)\partial_{v}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v.\end{split}

We can now partially integrate with respect to
t
and
v
respectively without collecting boundary terms, as the integrand is compactly supported. This yields

=\int_{S^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\partial_{v}(v_{2}A_{1})-\partial_{t}(tv_{1}A_{1})\right](x,v,t)f(x+tv)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v=T_{\tilde{A}_{1}}f(x),

i.e. the second term in (
4.64
). To examine the first term in (
4.66
), we define

I_{\epsilon}(x)=\int_{S^{1}}\int_{|t|>\epsilon}A_{0}(x,v)\partial_{x_{1}}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v,\quad x\in\mathbb{D},\epsilon>0

and note that
I_{\epsilon}(x)\rightarrow T_{A_{0}}(\partial_{x_{1}}f)(x)

(\epsilon\rightarrow 0
) for all
x\in\mathbb{D}
due to the dominated convergence theorem. Use (
4.67
) once more to write

\begin{split}I_{\epsilon}(x)=\int_{S^{1}}v_{1}A_{0}(x,v)\int_{|t|>\epsilon}\partial_{t}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v+\int_{S^{1}}\int_{|t|>\epsilon}\left[-v_{2}A_{0}(x,v)\right]\partial_{v}\tilde{f}(x,v,t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\mathrm{d}v\end{split}

and call the two summands
I_{\epsilon}^{1}(x)
and
I^{2}_{\epsilon}(x)
respectively. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, the first summand also equals

I_{\epsilon}^{1}(x)=\int_{S^{1}}v_{1}A_{0}(x,v)[f(x-\epsilon v)-f(x+\epsilon v)]\mathrm{d}v

which vanishes in the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
by the dominated convergence theorem. To rewrite the second summand, we integrate by parts in
v
to obtain

I^{2}_{\epsilon}(x)=\int_{S^{1}}\partial_{v}\left(v_{2}A_{0}(x,v)\right)\left(\int_{|t|>\epsilon}\frac{f(x+tv)}{t}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}v=\pi\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)H_{\epsilon}(x,v)\mathrm{d}v,

which approaches the first term in (
4.64
) as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. The Lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. By Minkowski’s integral inequality

\|T_{A}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\int_{S^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|A(\cdot,v,t)f(\cdot+tv)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x})}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v.

For
|t|>2
(the diameter of
\mathbb{D}
), the integrand vanishes. For
|t|\leq 2
we bound it from above by
\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|f(\cdot+tv)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x})}\leq\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x})}
. This yields

(4.68)

\|T_{A}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 8\pi\cdot\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

and thus proves the Proposition for the case
0=s=m
. By means of the preceding Lemma we will bootstrap this inequality to prove

(4.69)

\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}T_{A}\partial^{\gamma}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\|A\|_{C^{2m}}\cdot\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})}

for multi-indices
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
|\alpha|\leq s
and
|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq m
. To prove (
4.69
), we use the Leibniz rule to write

(4.70)

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}T_{A}\partial^{\gamma}_{x}f(x)=\sum{\alpha+\beta\choose\mu}T_{(\partial^{\mu}_{x}A)}(\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f)(x),

where the sum runs over multi-indices
\mu,\nu
with
\mu+\nu=\alpha+\beta
. We will estimate the summands separately, distinguishing the cases
m\leq|\mu|\leq 2m
and
0\leq|\mu|\leq m-1
. In the first case we necessarily have
|\nu+\gamma|\leq s
and thus, using (
4.68
), the respective term can be estimated as

(4.71)

\|T_{(\partial^{\mu}_{x}A)}\partial_{x}^{(\nu+\gamma)}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 8\pi\|\partial_{x}^{\mu}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 8\pi\|A\|_{C^{2m}}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})}.

From now on we assume that
0\leq|\mu|\leq m-1
; the remaining proof consists of a case study
m=1
vs.
m=2
(note that
m=0
is already proved).

Suppose that
m=1
, then
|\mu|=0
and
|\nu+\gamma|\leq s+1
. We may assume that
|\nu|=1
or
|\gamma|=1
(if both are zero, then (
4.68
) suffices to estimate the respective term). If
|\nu|=1
, we can use Lemma
4.12
to write

(4.72)

T_{A}\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f(x)=\pi\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{A}_{0}(x,v)H(\partial^{\gamma}_{x}f)(x,v)\mathrm{d}v+T_{(\tilde{A}_{1})}\partial_{x}^{\gamma}f(x)

and (
4.68
) as well as (
4.63
) to estimate

(4.73)

\begin{split}\|T_{A}\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}&\leq 2\pi^{2}\|\tilde{A}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+8\pi\|\tilde{A}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\\
&\lesssim\|A\|_{C^{2}}\cdot\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})}.\end{split}

If
|\gamma|=1
one can use the same method thus the case
m=1
is proved.

Next suppose that
m=2
and
\sigma_{A}\equiv 0
. If
|\mu|=1
, then again we have
|\nu+\gamma|\leq s+1
and, similar to (
4.71
) we obtain

(4.74)

\|T_{(\partial_{x}^{\mu}A)}\partial^{\nu+\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{\mu}A\|_{C^{2}}\cdot\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})},

which gives the desired bound as
\|\partial_{x}^{\mu}A\|_{C^{2}}\leq\|A\|_{C^{4}}
. It remains to deal with the case
|\mu|=0
, where we merely have
|\nu+\gamma|\leq s+2
. Now
two
derivatives have to be shuffled over to
A
, but this is possible as
\sigma_{A}\equiv 0
and thus Lemma
4.12
may be applied twice. Indeed, writing
\nu+\gamma=\nu^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}+\delta
with
|\delta|\leq 2
and
|\nu^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}|\leq s
we have

(4.75)

T_{A}\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f(x)=\pi\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{B}_{0}(x,v)H(\partial_{x}^{\nu^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}}f)(x,v)\mathrm{d}v+T_{\tilde{B}_{1}}(\partial_{x}^{\nu^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}}f)(x),

where now
\|\tilde{B}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|A\|_{C^{3}}
and
\|\tilde{B}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|A\|_{C^{4}}
. Similar to above we find

(4.76)

\|T_{A}\partial_{x}^{\nu+\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|A\|_{C^{4}}\cdot\|\partial_{x}^{\nu^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\|A\|_{C^{4}}\cdot\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})}.

Combining the preceding estimates, inequality (
4.69
) follows. As the coefficients of
P
and
Q
as well as their derivatives are easily bounded, we get

(4.77)

\|\partial^{\alpha}PT_{A}Qf\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{P,Q}\|A\|_{C^{2m}}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{D})},\quad 0\leq|\alpha|\leq s\leq m

which proves the Proposition for integral
s
. General values for
0\leq s\leq m
can be obtained by an interpolation argument and the proof is complete.
∎

(Part B) Local Lipschitz estimates.
Recall that we had defined
N_{\Phi}=I_{\Phi}^{*}I_{\Phi}
and
K_{\Phi}=N_{\Phi}-N_{0}
. We bound their operator norms as follows:

Proposition 4.13

(Local Lipschitz estimates)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{D}
be compact. Then for all
\Phi,\Psi\in C_{K}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{u}(m))
and
0\leq s\leq 2
we have

(4.78)

5.3.3.
Metric entropy estimates

The following standard result controls metric entropies of balls in the spaces
\tilde{H}^{\alpha}
, relevant in our proofs. We include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,B)
be a norm-ball of radius
B
in
\tilde{H}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{Z},W),\alpha>0
. Suppose that (
2.4
) holds for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the
\epsilon
-log-covering numbers of
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,B)
for the
L^{2}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{Z},W)
-distance satisfy

(5.45)

\log N(\mathcal{H}(\alpha,B),\|\cdot\|_{L_{\zeta}^{2}},\epsilon)\leq\Big(\frac{AB}{\epsilon}\Big)^{d/\alpha},\penalty\ \penalty\ 0<\epsilon<AB,

where
A=A(d,\alpha,b_{1},d_{W})<\infty
is a fixed constant. Moreover, assuming also (
2.3
), we have for all
D\in\mathbb{N},\bar{\tau}=d(\tau+(1/2))/\alpha
and some
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}(A)<\infty

(5.46)

\log N(\mathcal{H}(\alpha,B)\cap E_{D},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\epsilon)\leq D^{\bar{\tau}}\Big(\frac{A^{\prime}B}{\epsilon}\Big)^{d/\alpha},\penalty\ \penalty\ 0<\epsilon<A^{\prime}B.

Proof.

We set
d_{W}=1
, the general case is the same up to adjusting constants. Also a standard scaling argument for norms allows to restrict to
B=1
. Let us write
f_{n}=\langle f,e_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\zeta}}
for the ‘Fourier’ coefficients in the basis
e_{n}
throughout the proof. We first prove (
5.45
): For any
f
contained in
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,1)=\big\{f:\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}f_{n}^{2}\leq 1\big\},
using (
2.4
) and Parseval’s identity, we can estimate the error of
L^{2}_{\zeta}
-approximation
P_{2^{\ell_{0}}}f
of
f
from all frequencies up to the largest integer
n<2^{\ell_{0}}
, as

(5.47)

\displaystyle\|f-P_{2^{\ell_{0}}}f\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2^{\ell_{0}}}f_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}\leq b_{1}^{-\alpha}2^{-2\alpha\ell_{0}/d}\leq(\epsilon/4)^{2}

where we have chosen
\ell_{0}
as

(5.48)

\ell_{0}=\frac{d}{\alpha}\log_{2}\Big(\frac{4\cdot 2^{(\alpha/d)+1}b_{1}^{-\alpha/2}}{\epsilon}\Big)

The remaining indices
n:0\leq n<2^{\ell_{0}}
are now decomposed into dyadic brackets

N_{l}=\{n:2^{l-1}\leq n\leq 2^{l}-1\},\penalty\ l=1,\dots,;\penalty\ |N_{l}|=2^{l-1},

where by convention we set
N_{0}=\{0\}
. Thus using Parseval’s identity, (
2.4
) and the preceding estimate we can bound, for any
f,g\in\mathcal{H}(\alpha,1)
,

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}\sum_{n\in N_{l}}\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}(f_{n}-g_{n})^{2}}+2\epsilon/4

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha/d}b_{1}^{-\alpha/2}\sum_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}2^{-l\alpha/d}\sqrt{\sum_{n\in N_{l}}(\lambda_{n}^{\alpha/2}f_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{\alpha/2}g_{n})^{2}}+\epsilon/2.

Now by definition of
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,1)
, for every
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
the vectors
\{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha/2}f_{n}:n\in N_{l}\},\penalty\ \{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha/2}g_{n}:n\in N_{l}\}
lie in the unit ball of a Euclidean space of dimension
|N_{l}|\leq 2^{l}
. The Euclidean
\epsilon^{\prime}
-covering numbers of such a unit ball

for the standard Euclidean norm are

N(l)\equiv N(B_{l},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{R}^{|N_{l}|}},\epsilon^{\prime})\leq(3/\epsilon^{\prime})^{2^{l}},\penalty\ 0<\epsilon^{\prime}<1,

(see, e.g., Proposition 4.3.34 in
[
21
]
). By choosing
\epsilon^{\prime}_{l}=2^{l((\alpha/d)+1)}2^{-\ell_{0}((\alpha/d)+1)}
-coverings of
B_{l}
for each
l
centred at points
(f_{n,l,i}:n\in N_{l},l\leq\ell_{0}+1)
, and setting
f_{n,l,i}=0
for
n\geq 2^{\ell_{0}+1}
, we obtain a covering

\Big(\bar{f}^{(i)}=\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha/2}f_{n,l,i}:i=1,\dots,\prod_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}N(l)\Big)

of
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,1)
of radius bounded by

\displaystyle\|f-\bar{f}^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+2^{\alpha/d}b_{1}^{-\alpha/2}\sum_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}2^{-l\alpha/d}2^{l((\alpha/d)+1)}2^{-\ell_{0}((\alpha/d)+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+2^{(\alpha/d)+2}b_{1}^{-\alpha/2}2^{-\alpha\ell_{0}/d}\leq\epsilon,

by choice of
\ell_{0}
. We thus obtain, for some
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\alpha,d)>0

\displaystyle\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H}(\alpha,1),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\zeta}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}\log_{2}N(l)\leq\sum_{0\leq l\leq\ell_{0}+1}2^{l}\Big[\log_{2}3+(\ell_{0}-l)\big(\frac{\alpha}{d}+1\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}2^{\ell_{0}}\leq(A/\epsilon)^{d/\alpha}

so that (
5.45

5.46

\|f-g\|_{\infty}=\sup_{z}\left|\sum_{n\leq D}(f_{n}-g_{n})e_{n}(z)\right|\leq\|f-g\|_{L_{\zeta}^{2}}\|e_{n}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\sum_{n\leq D}1}\lesssim D^{\tau+1/2}\|f-g\|_{L_{\zeta}^{2}},

so that the result follows from (
5.45
) after adjusting the constant
A
.

5.3.4.
A concentration inequality for empirical processes

The following is Lemma 3.12 from
[
48
]
. The result is formulated for countable
\Theta

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\Theta
be countable. Suppose a class of real-valued measurable functions

\mathcal{H}=\{h_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R},\theta\in\Theta\}

defined on a probability space
(\mathcal{X},\mathscr{X},P^{X})
is uniformly bounded by

and has variance envelope
\sigma^{2}\geq\sup_{\theta}E^{X}h_{\theta}^{2}(X)
where

J_{2}(\mathcal{H})=\int_{0}^{4\sigma}\sqrt{\log N(\mathcal{H},d_{2},\rho)}d\rho,\penalty\ \penalty\ d_{2}(\theta,\theta^{\prime}):=\sqrt{E^{X}[h_{\theta}(X)-h_{\theta^{\prime}}(X)]^{2}},

For

We also record here the ‘pointwise’ bound for singleton

\mathrm{Pr}\left(|Z_{N}|\geq L\sigma\sqrt{x}+LUx/\sqrt{N}\right)\leq 2e^{-x}.

6.1.
Real-valued setting and approximation spaces

An orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})
can be obtained by taking real and imaginary parts of Zernike polynomials, noting that (
4.12
) reveals redundant polynomials that can be dropped; precisely we put

(6.3)

W_{nk}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2}\real Z_{nk}&n-2k>0\\
Z_{nk}&n-2k=0\\
\sqrt{2}\imaginary Z_{n,n-k}&n-2k<0\end{cases},\quad\hat{W}_{nk}=\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{\pi}}\cdot W_{nk},

for indices
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq k\leq n
.
Ordering the double indices as
(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),\dots,(n_{\ell},k_{\ell}),\dots
(i.e. rearranged in a pyramid scheme) we can pass to a single index
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for the basis. Then the pair

(6.4)

(6.5)

\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq D}\|e_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim D^{1/4}\quad\text{ and }\quad\ell\lesssim\lambda_{\ell}\lesssim\ell,

such that the asymptotic conditions (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) in Conditions
2.1
are satisfied with parameters
\tau=1/4
and

respectively. Indeed, (
6.5
) follows from (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and the asymptotic equality
n_{\ell}\sim\ell^{1/2}
.

In order to define the finite dimensional approximation spaces

, we write
d_{m}=\dim\mathfrak{so}(m)

\equiv m(m-1)/2
and

and fix a (Frobenius-)orthonormal basis
(A_{i}:1\leq i\leq d_{m})

E_{D}=E_{D}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{so}(m))=\left\{\Phi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{D_{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{m}}\phi_{\ell,i}e_{\ell}A_{i}:\phi_{\ell,i}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))

is a subspace of dimension

as in (

).
If

for some
D^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\mathrm{span}\{e_{\ell}:\ell\leq D_{m}\}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})
consists of polynomials of degree
\leq D^{\prime}

Note that the scale
\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})
from (

On

the norms of all function spaces are comparable and we record here some inequalities that are frequently used below. Recall from §3.2 that
\|\cdot\|_{E_{D}}
is just the

-norm, restricted to

.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
h\in E_{D}
(
D\in\mathbb{N}
) and
\alpha\geq 0
we have:

(6.7)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{3/4}\cdot\|h\|_{E_{D}}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\tilde{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\alpha/2}\cdot\|h\|_{E_{D}}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{E_{D}}

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\alpha/2}\cdot\|h\|_{\tilde{H}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}

Proof.

Let
h_{\ell,i}:=\langle h,e_{\ell}A_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\in\mathbb{R}
be the coefficients of
h
and write
h_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{m}}h_{i\ell}A_{i}\in\mathfrak{so}(m)
. Then for
the first inequality we use Cauchy-Schwarz and (

\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq D_{m}}|h_{\ell}|_{F}\cdot\|e_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{D_{m}}\|e_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\|h\|_{E_{D}}\lesssim D^{3/4}\|h\|_{E_{D}},

noting that
D_{m}\lesssim D
.
For the second inequality use the upper bound on the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
in (

(6.10)

\|h\|_{\tilde{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{D_{m}}\lambda_{\ell}^{\alpha}|h_{\ell}|_{F}^{2}\lesssim D^{\alpha}\|h\|_{E_{D}}^{2},

6.2.
Global conditions for log-concave approximation

We now show that the non-Abelian X-ray transform
C:\Theta\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
(given in (
3.3
), with
\Theta
as in (

2.2
.

a)

The
uniform boundedness property
is a direct consequence of

taking values in the compact group

(6.11)

\sup_{\Phi\in\Theta}\|C_{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\sqrt{m}.

b)

The

is a special case of Theorem 2.2 in
[

]

.

Combining the stability estimate in

]
with our forward estimate in the Zernike scale (Theorem

c)

The

from Condition

holds on

for any

and for any pair

with

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose

\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\omega_{s}(\|\Phi\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})}\vee\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{H}^{s}(\mathbb{D})})\cdot\|C_{\Phi}-C_{\Psi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{1-1/s}

and

Proof.

Starting with the stability estimate from
[

, Thm. 5.3]
we have

\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\leq C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)\cdot\|I_{\Xi}(\Phi-\Psi)\|_{H^{1}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})},

and

\lesssim C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)\cdot\|I_{\Xi}(\Phi-\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{1-1/s}\cdot\|I_{\Xi}(\Phi-\Psi)\|_{H^{s}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{1/s},

6.3.

Next, we consider the non-Abelian X-ray transform on finite-dimensional Euclidean balls

-orthogonal projection of

The first result concerns local regularity on

and follows from our computations in Lemma

and standard forward estimates. As a matter of fact, the bounds below are uniform in

for the space of all twice differentiable maps

with finite norm

\begin{split}\|g\|_{C^{2,1}(E_{D},\mathbb{R}^{m\times m})}=&\sup_{i=1,\dots,m^{2}}\Big\{\|g^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(E_{D},\mathbb{R})}+\|\nabla g^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(E_{D},\mathbb{R}^{D},|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}^{D}})}\\

where

denote gradient and Hessian on

and

in an orthonormal basis of

(cf. (

Lemma 6.3

(Local regularity)
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For fixed

\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}(x,v)\in SO(m)

(6.13)

Proof.

From Lemma
4.5
we already know that the map
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}(x,v)
is twice differentiable on

; we will prove for
\Phi,\Psi,h\in E_{D}
and

(6.14)

\displaystyle|C_{\Phi}(x,v)|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1

(6.15)

\displaystyle|\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}

(6.16)

\displaystyle|\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}

(6.17)

\displaystyle|\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)-\ddot{C}_{\Psi}[h](x,v)|_{F}

with implicit constants only depending on

6.13
): Fix an orthonormal basis
A^{1},\dots,A^{m^{2}}
of
(\mathbb{R}^{m\times m},|\cdot|_{F})
and write
g^{i}(\Phi)=\langle C_{\Phi}(x,v),A^{i}\rangle_{F}
, then

(6.18)

|g^{i}(\Phi)|\leq|C_{\Phi}(x,v)|_{F},\quad|\nabla g^{i}(\Phi)|_{F}\leq\sup_{\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1}|\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)|_{F}.

Further, noting that the operator norm of a symmetric matrix equals the absolute value of its largest eigenvalue, we have

(6.19)

|\nabla^{2}g^{i}(\Phi)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sup_{\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1}|\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)|_{F},

with similar bounds for the difference
\nabla^{2}g^{i}(\Phi)-\nabla^{2}g^{i}(\Psi)
. Overall this implies

(6.20)

\|C_{\bullet}(x,v)\|_{C^{2,1}(E_{D},\mathbb{R}^{m\times m})}\lesssim\sup_{\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1}\left(1+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}+2\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\right)\lesssim D^{3/2},

where the
L^{\infty}
-norms of
h\in E_{D}
have been bounded by means of Lemma
6.1
.

On to the proof of (
6.14
) - (
6.16
). The first inequality was already stated in (
6.11
) and the second follows from noting that
|\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h](x,v)|_{F}=|I_{\Xi}h(x,v)|_{F}
(with
\Xi
as in Lemma
4.5
) and the fact that
I_{\Xi}
is
L^{\infty}
-
L^{\infty}
bounded (with operator norm uniform in
\Xi
, see e.g.
[
39
, Lem. 5.2]
). To prove (
6.16
) and (
6.17
), note that

(6.21)

\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]=I_{\Xi}(hV_{h})\cdot C_{\Phi}=I(R_{\Xi}^{-1}hV_{h})\cdot C_{\Phi},

where
\Xi
and
V_{h}
are as in Lemma
4.5
and
R_{\Xi}:S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow SO(m)
satisfies

(X+\Xi)R_{\Xi}=0
on
S\mathbb{D}
and
R_{\Xi}=\mathrm{id}
on
\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
. Using
L^{\infty}
-
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of
I_{\Xi}
we obtain

(6.22)

\|\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim\|hV_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\cdot\|V_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}

where the last inequality follows from (
4.17
) – this proves (
6.16
). For (
6.17
) we estimate, using the notation
\Theta
and
W_{h}
for
\Psi
in place of
\Xi
and
V_{h}
for
\Phi
,

(6.23)

\begin{split}\|\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]-\ddot{C}_{\Psi}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq&\penalty\ \penalty\ \|I_{\Xi}(hV_{h})\cdot\left(C_{\Phi}-C_{\Psi}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\\
&+\|I_{\Xi}(hV_{h}-hW_{h})\cdot C_{\Psi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\\
&+\|I\left((R_{\Xi}^{-1}-R_{\Theta}^{-1})(hW_{h})\right)\cdot C_{\Psi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\\
=&(i)+(ii)+(iii)\end{split}

The terms
(i)
-
(iii)
are bounded seperately; for the first one note that

(6.24)

(i)\leq\|I_{\Xi}(hV_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|C_{\Phi}-C_{\Psi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\cdot\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})},

where the first factor has been estimates as in the argument leading to (
6.16
) and the second one by the
L^{\infty}
-
L^{\infty}
-Lipschitz estimate from
[
39
, Thm. 2.2]
. Similarly,

(6.25)

(ii)\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\cdot\|V_{h}-W_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\cdot\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}

by estimate (
4.17
) above. Finally we have

(6.26)

(iii)\leq\|R_{\Xi}^{-1}-R_{\Theta}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\cdot\|hW_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\cdot\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}

by (
4.14
) and (
4.17
). The proof is complete.
∎

Next we verify ‘local curvature’ Condition
2.4
, which is stated in terms of the generic log-likelihood function (cf. (
2.8
))

(6.27)

\ell(\Phi)=\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))=-\frac{1}{2}|Y-C_{\Phi}(X,V)|_{F}^{2}\quad\text{where }(X,V,Y)\in\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}

and its expectation
E_{\Phi_{\star}}
under the assumption that
(X,V)
is drawn uniformly at random from
\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
and
Y
follows the regression model
Y=C_{\Phi_{\star}}(X,V)+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is independent Gaussian noise (cf. (
3.12
)). Recalling the discussion preceding Condition
2.4
(and the identification of
\Phi\in E_{D}
with its coefficients
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
) we use the notation

(6.28)

\nabla\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\quad\text{ and }\quad\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}

for gradient and Hessian of the map
\Phi\mapsto\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))
on
E_{D}
(with dimension
D
suppressed, by abuse of notation.)

Lemma 6.4

(Local curvature)
.

Given a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{D}
and numbers
M>0,\alpha>5
there are constants

\epsilon,c_{0},c_{1}>0
(depending on
M,K,\alpha
)
with the following property: Suppose
\Phi_{\star}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(m)
is of regularity
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
and obeys

(6.29)

\supp\Phi_{\star}\subset K,\quad\|\Phi_{\star}\|_{H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\epsilon D^{-2}

for all
D\in\mathbb{N}
. Then on balls
\mathcal{B}(\eta,D)=\{\Phi\in E_{D}:\|\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\|_{E_{D}}<\eta\}
of radius

0<\eta\leq\epsilon D^{-2}

the averaged log-likelihood satisfies

(6.30)

\displaystyle\inf_{\Phi\in\mathcal{B}(\eta,D)}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}\left(-E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{0}D^{-1/2}

(6.31)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Phi\in\mathcal{B}(\eta,D)}\left[|E_{\Phi_{\star}}\ell(\Phi)|+|E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla\ell(\Phi)|_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}+|E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}.

Remark 6.5
.

The requirement that
\Phi_{\star}
has Sobolev-regularity
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
can be replaced by
\Phi_{\star}\in F
for any normed function space
F
that embeds compactly into
C^{4}(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))
, another valid choice being Hölder-space
F=C^{4+\epsilon}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
(
\epsilon>0
). Due to compactness, the constant
C(\Phi_{\star},K)
from Theorem
4.1
achieves its infimum over
\{\Phi_{\star}^{\prime}\in F:\supp\Phi_{\star}^{\prime}\subset K,\|\Phi_{\star}^{\prime}\|_{F}\leq M\}
; in particular Theorem
3.1
follows from Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
6.1
.

Remark 6.6

(Non-sharp version of Theorem
3.1
)
.

We briefly sketch a proof of the non-sharp version of Theorem
3.1
, as discussed in Remark
3.2
.
Let
\eta>0
, then for
h\in E_{D}
and
\Phi\in C^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m))
with
C^{1}
-norm
\leq M
we have

(6.32)

\|\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}h\|_{L^{2}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\gtrsim_{\eta}\frac{\|\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}h\|^{1+1/\eta}_{H^{1}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}}{\|\mathbb{I}_{\Phi}h\|^{1/\eta}_{H^{1+\eta}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}}\gtrsim_{M,\eta}\left[\frac{\|h\|_{E_{D}}}{\|h\|_{\tilde{H}^{1+\eta}(\mathbb{D})}}\right]^{1/\eta}\cdot\|h\|_{E_{D}},

where we first interpolated
H^{1}
between
L^{2}
and
H^{1+\eta}
and then used the stability estimate

[
39
, Theorem 5.3]
and the forward estimate from Theorem 4.2. Now, using Lemma
6.1
, the bracket on the right hand side can be lower bounded by
\gtrsim_{\eta}D^{-\frac{1}{2}[1+1/\eta]}
and the result follows by taking
\eta
sufficiently large.

Remark 6.7
.

Given
K
and
M,\alpha
as in the preceding Lemma, as well as some
A_{1}>0
, there is a threshold
N_{\mathrm{min}}(M,K,\alpha,A_{1})\in\mathbb{N}
as follows: Suppose a pair of integers
(D,N)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
satisfies
D\leq A_{1}N^{1/(\alpha+1)}
and
N\geq N_{\mathrm{min}}
; then, setting

(6.33)

\delta_{N}=N^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\delta}_{N}=\log N\cdot\mathrm{max}\left(\delta_{N}^{\gamma},\delta_{N}D^{\kappa_{0}/2}\right)

as in Condition
2.5
, where
\gamma=1-1/\alpha
and
\kappa_{0}=1/2
, the prerequisites of Theorem
2.6
are satisfied for

\eta=D^{-2}/\log N

and
every
\Phi_{\star}\in H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{so}(m))
, obeying

(6.34)

\supp\Phi_{\star}\subset K,\quad\|\Phi_{\star}\|_{H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}\leq M,\quad\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\frac{\delta_{N}}{2\sqrt{m}+1}.

Specifically, on the ball
\mathcal{B}=\{\Phi\in E_{D}:\|\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\|_{E_{D}}<\eta\}
Condition
2.4
is satisfied for exponents
\kappa_{0}=1/2
and
\kappa_{1}=0
and constants
c_{0},c_{1}
only depending on
K
and
M,\alpha
.
To see this, let
\epsilon=\epsilon(M,K,\alpha)
be as in Lemma
6.4
.
Since
\alpha>5
, choosing
N
sufficiently large implies

\displaystyle D^{2}\delta_{N}\leq A_{1}^{2}N^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}\cdot N^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2}}\leq(2\sqrt{m}+1)\epsilon,

\displaystyle D^{2}(\log N)^{3}\delta_{N}^{\gamma}\leq A_{1}^{2}N^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}\cdot(\log N)^{3}N^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha+2}}=A_{1}^{2}(\log N)^{3}N^{-\frac{\alpha-5}{2\alpha+2}}\leq 1

\displaystyle D^{2}(\log N)^{3}\delta_{N}{D^{\kappa_{0}/2}}\leq A_{1}^{\frac{9}{4}}\cdot(\log N)^{3}N^{-\frac{2\alpha-9}{4\alpha+4}}\leq 1

Hence, for
N_{\mathrm{min}}(M,K,A_{1},\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we have

(6.35)

\frac{\delta_{N}}{2\sqrt{m}+1}\leq\epsilon D^{-2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\log N\cdot\tilde{\delta}_{N}\leq\eta\leq\epsilon D^{-2}\quad(N\geq N_{\mathrm{min}}),

so that Lemma
6.4
applies. In summary we have verified the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
.

Proof of Lemma
6.4
.

We start with computing the expected values of the log-likelihood and its derivatives. For a fixed direction
h\in E_{D}
and in view of the regularity stated in Lemma
4.5
, we have

(6.36)

\displaystyle\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))

\displaystyle=-|C_{\Phi}(X,V)-Y|_{F}^{2}/2

(6.37)

\displaystyle h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))

\displaystyle=-\langle C_{\Phi}(X,V)-Y,\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h](X,V)\rangle_{F}

(6.38)

\displaystyle h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi,(X,V,Y))h

\displaystyle=-|\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h](X,V)|_{F}^{2}+\langle C_{\Phi}(X,V)-Y,\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h](X,V)\rangle_{F}.

Now
C_{\Phi}(X,V)-Y=C_{\Phi}(X,V)-C_{\Phi_{\star}}(X,V)-\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
and
(X,V)
are independent; in particular
E_{\Phi_{\star}}\langle\varepsilon,\varphi(X,V)\rangle_{F}=0
for any measurable function
\varphi(X,V)
and thus we have the following expected values:

(6.39)

\displaystyle-E_{\Phi_{\star}}\ell(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|C_{\Phi}-C_{\Phi_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}+1\right)

(6.40)

\displaystyle-E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\langle C_{\Phi}-C_{\Phi_{\star}},\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}

(6.41)

\displaystyle-E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h

\displaystyle=\|\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}-\langle C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi},\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}\bar{\mathbb{D}})}.

Curvature bound.
Our first goal is to prove the curvature bound in (
6.30
). To this end note that the first derivative is given by
\dot{C}_{\Phi}[h]=I_{\Xi}h\cdot C_{\Phi}
(Lemma
4.5
) and admits a lower bound, at least at the compactly supported
\Phi_{\star}
, due to Theorem
3.1
.
To make use of this, we apply standard inequalities (and that
C_{\Phi}^{T}C_{\Phi}=\mathrm{id}
) to the effect that

(6.42)

\begin{split}-E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h\geq&\penalty\ \frac{1}{2}\|I_{\Xi_{\star}}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}\\
&-\|I_{\Xi_{\star}}h-I_{\Xi}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}-\langle C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi},\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\end{split}

By Theorem
3.1
, the first term satisfies
\frac{1}{2}\|I_{\Xi_{\star}}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}\geq\delta D^{-1/2}\|h\|_{E_{D}}^{2}
for some
\delta=\delta(M,K,\alpha)>0
.
Assuming w.l.o.g. that
\|h\|_{E_{D}}=1
and decreasing
\delta
if necessary, we have

(6.43)

\begin{split}-E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h\gtrsim_{M,K}&\penalty\ \delta D^{-1/2}\cdot\|h\|_{E_{D}}^{2}\\
&-\left[\underbrace{\|\Phi_{\star}-\Phi_{\star,D}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}}_{(i)}+\underbrace{\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}}_{(ii)}\right]\\
&-\left[\underbrace{\|\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}}_{(iii)}+\underbrace{\|\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}}_{(iv)}\right],\end{split}

where the terms
(i)
-
(iv)
arise from bounding the error terms in (
6.42
) by means of standard forward estimates; we will give a detailed proof below, but first continue with the main line of argument.

In order to bound
(i)
, we use the chain of embeddings
\tilde{H}^{t}(\mathbb{D})\subset H^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{D})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(valid for
t>2
and some
\gamma=\gamma(t)>0
, see (
4.10
)) and an interpolation argument to estimate the difference
\Psi=\Phi_{\star}-\Phi_{\star,D}
as follows:

(6.44)

\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{t}\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{H}^{t}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{t}\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{1-t/5}\cdot\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{H}^{5}(\mathbb{D})}^{t/5},\quad 2<t\leq 5.

The
\tilde{H}^{5}
-norm can be estimated by
\|\Psi\|_{\tilde{H}^{5}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\|\Phi_{\star}\|_{\tilde{H}^{5}(\mathbb{D})}+\|\Phi_{\star,D}\|_{\tilde{H}^{5}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 2\|\Phi_{\star}\|_{\tilde{H}^{5}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 2M
. To bound the
L^{2}
-norm we use Lemma
6.2
for
s=5
. Choosing
t
such that
(1-t/5)(1-1/5)=1/4
, we get

(6.45)

(i)\lesssim_{M}\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{1/2}\leq\epsilon^{1/2}D^{-1},

where we used the bias estimate in (
6.29
), for
\epsilon>0
to be chosen.

To bound
(ii)
we use (
6.29
) and the fact
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\lesssim D^{3/2}
(Lemma
6.1
); then

(6.46)

(ii)\leq\epsilon D^{-1/2}

Similarly, for
\eta>0
to be chosen, we have

(6.47)

(iii)\lesssim\eta^{2}D^{3/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad(iv)\lesssim\eta D^{3/2},

where we used Lemma
6.1
both for
h
and for
h^{\prime}=\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\in E_{D}
.

Combining the previous displays, we obtain (again for
\delta>0
decreased if necessary to compensate for all implicit constants) the following inequality:

(6.48)

-E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h\gtrsim_{M,K}\delta D^{-1/2}-\left(\epsilon^{1/2}D^{-1}+\epsilon D^{-1/2}+\eta^{2}D^{3/2}+\eta D^{3/2}\right),

valid for
h\in E_{D}
with
\|h\|_{E_{D}}=1
. Taking the infimum over
h
, we obtain the lowest eigenvalue of
-E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)
; overall we have

(6.49)

\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}\left(-E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)\right)\geq CD^{-1/2}\cdot\left[\delta-\left(\epsilon^{1/2}D^{-1/2}+\epsilon+\eta^{2}D^{2}+\eta D^{2}\right)\right]

for constants
C(M,K,\alpha)>0
,
0<\delta(M,K,\alpha)<1
and with
\epsilon,\eta>0
still free to choose. Under the assumption that

(6.50)

\epsilon\leq\epsilon^{1/2}\leq\delta/5\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta^{2}\leq\eta\leq D^{-2}\delta/5

we have

(6.51)

\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}\left(-E_{\Phi_{\star}}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)\right)\geq\frac{C\delta}{5}D^{-1/2}.

In particular (
6.30
) follows for
c_{0}(M,K,\alpha)=C(M,K,\alpha)\delta(M,K,\alpha)/5
and
\epsilon(M,K,\alpha)=(\delta(M,K,\alpha)/5)^{2}
.

Finally, we demonstrate how to derive (
6.43
) from (
6.42
); this consists of proving

(6.52)

\displaystyle\|I_{\Xi_{\star}}h-I_{\Xi}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,K}(i)+(iii)

(6.53)

\displaystyle|\langle C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi},\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,K}(ii)+(iv).

For the first inequality we note that
I_{\Xi}h=I(R_{\Xi}^{-1}h)
, and
R_{\Xi}\in C(S\bar{\mathbb{D}},\mathfrak{so}(m)))
is an integrating factor for
\Xi
, i.e. a solution to
(X+\Xi)R_{\Xi}=0
with
S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
and
R_{\Xi}=\mathrm{id}
on
\partial_{-}S\bar{\mathbb{D}}
. Using a similar notation for
\Xi_{\star}
, we have

\begin{split}\|I_{\Xi_{\star}}h-I_{\Xi}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}&=\|I\left((R_{\Xi_{\star}}^{-1}-R_{\Xi}^{-1})h)\right)\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}\\
&\leq\|R^{-1}_{\Xi_{\star}}-R_{\Xi}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}^{2},\end{split}

where we used boundedness of
I:L^{2}(S\mathbb{D})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})

[
60
, Thm. 4.2.1]
. By (
4.14
) we have
\|R^{-1}_{\Xi_{\star}}-R_{\Xi}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|\Xi_{\star}-\Xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|\Phi_{\star}-\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}
, such that (
6.53
) follows from the triangle inequality.

Next, for any
g\in L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
we have (using Hölder’s inequality)

|\langle g,\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}|=|\langle g\cdot C_{\Phi},I_{\Xi}[hV_{h}]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}|\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|I_{\Xi}[hV_{h}]\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})},

where
V_{h}
is as in Lemma
4.5
and we made use of the fact that
C_{\Phi}
takes values in
SO(m)
. Using the representation of
I_{\Xi}
via integrating factors from above, one sees that it is bounded
L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})
with operator norm independent of
\Xi
. The previous display can thus be continued with

(6.54)

\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|hV_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2},

where we made use of (
4.17
). Setting
g=C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi}
we get

(6.55)

|\langle C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi},\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}|\lesssim_{m}\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}.

Now the
L^{2}
-norm on the right hand side can be estimated with the triangle inequality in terms of
C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}
, which gives term
(ii)
, and
C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}-C_{\Phi}
, which gives term
(iv)
after applying the global
L^{2}
-Lipschitz estimate for
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}
. Together, we have also proved (
6.53
).

Upper bounds.
Finally, we prove the upper bounds (
6.31
). Using the triangle inequality and the
L^{2}
-Lipschitz estimate for
\Phi\mapsto C_{\Phi}
we obtain

(6.56)

\|C_{\Phi}-C_{\Phi_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim\|\Phi-\Phi_{\star,D}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}+\|C_{\Phi_{\star},D}-C_{\Phi_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\leq 2\epsilon D^{-2}

for
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}(\eta,D)
with
\eta\leq\epsilon D^{-2}
. In particular, assuming without loss of generality that
\epsilon<1
, we have

(6.57)

|E_{\Phi_{\star}}\ell(\Phi)|\lesssim 1+\epsilon^{2}D^{-4}\lesssim 1.

Next, for
h\in E_{D}
with
\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1
we have

(6.58)

|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi)|\leq\|C_{\Phi}-C_{\Phi_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\cdot\|I_{\Xi}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim\epsilon D^{-2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim 1,

where we again used (
6.56
), as well as
L^{2}
-boundedness of
I_{\Xi}
(with operator norm bounded uniformly in
\Xi
). Finally, using additionally (
6.53
), we have

\begin{split}|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h|&\leq\|I_{\Theta}h\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}^{2}+|\langle C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi},\ddot{C}_{\Phi}[h]\rangle_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\\
&\lesssim_{M,K}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|C_{\Phi_{\star}}-C_{\Phi_{\star,D}}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\partial_{+}S\bar{\mathbb{D}})}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|\Phi_{\star,D}-\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim 1+\epsilon D^{-2}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}.\end{split}

As above, we have
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\lesssim D^{3/2}
, which is tempered by
D^{-2}
. Combining the previous displays we obtain, for
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}(\eta,D)
and
h\in E_{D}
with
\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1

|E_{\Phi_{\star}}\ell(\Phi)|+|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi)|+|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h|\leq c_{2},

where
c_{2}=c_{2}(M,K)
incorporates all implicit constants from above. Finally,

\sup_{\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1}|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi)|=|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla\ell(\Phi)|_{\mathbb{R}^{D}},\quad\sup_{\|h\|_{E_{D}}\leq 1}|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h|=|E_{\Phi_{\star}}h^{T}\nabla^{2}\ell(\Phi)h|_{\mathrm{op}}

and the proof is complete.

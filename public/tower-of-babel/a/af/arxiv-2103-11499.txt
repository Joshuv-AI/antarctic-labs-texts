Abstract
In polynomial optimization problems, nonnegativity constraints are typically handled using the sum of squares condition. This can be efficiently enforced using semidefinite programming formulations, or as more recently proposed by Papp and Yildiz 2019 , using the sum of squares cone directly in a nonsymmetric interior point algorithm. Beyond nonnegativity, more complicated polynomial constraints (in particular, generalizations of the positive semidefinite, second order and ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm cones) can also be modeled through structured sum of squares programs. We take a different approach and propose using more specialized polynomial cones instead. This can result in lower dimensional formulations, more efficient oracles for interior point methods, or self-concordant barriers with smaller parameters. In most cases, these algorithmic advantages also translate to faster solve times in practice.

Introduction

The
sum of squares
(
SOS
) condition is commonly used as a tractable restriction of polynomial nonnegativity.
While SOS programs have traditionally been formulated and solved using semidefinite programming (SDP),
Papp and Yildiz 2019
recently demonstrated the effectiveness of a nonsymmetric interior point algorithm in solving SOS programs without SDP formulations.
In this note, we focus on structured SOS constraints that can be modeled using more specialized cones.
We describe and give barrier functions for three related cones useful for modeling functions of dense polynomials, which we hope will become useful modeling primitives.

The first is the cone of
SOS matrices
, which was described by
Coey et al. 2022
without derivation.
We show that this cone can be computationally favorable to equally low-dimensional SOS formulations.
Characterizations of univariate SOS matrix cones in the context of optimization algorithms have previously been given by
Genin et al. 2003
.
However, their use of monomial or Chebyshev bases complicates computations of oracles in an interior point algorithm
(
Papp and Yildiz 2019
, Section 3.1)
and prevents effective generalizations to the multivariate case.

The second is an
SOS
\ell_{2}
-norm
(
SOS-L2
) cone, which can be used to certify pointwise membership in the second order cone for a vector with polynomial components.
The third is an
SOS
\ell_{1}
-norm
(
SOS-L1
) cone, which can be used to certify pointwise membership in the epigraph set of the
\ell_{1}
-norm function.
Although it is straightforward to use SOS representations to approximate these sets, such formulations introduce cones of higher dimension than the constrained polynomial vector.
We believe we are first to describe how to handle these sets in an interior point algorithm without introducing auxiliary conic variables or constraints.
We suggest new barriers, with lower barrier parameters than SOS formulations allow.

1.1
The SOS polynomials cone and generic interior point algorithms

A polynomial
p(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}
is SOS if it can be expressed in the form

We also say that

represents a vector of coefficients of an SOS polynomial under a given basis.

To construct a vectorized definition for

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{s})_{i,j}=\langle\lambda(i,j),\mathbf{s}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{U}}\quad\forall i,j\in\llbracket 1..L\rrbracket,

(1)

\displaystyle{K}_{\SOS}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}:\exists\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},\mathbf{s}=\mathsf{\Lambda}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})\},

(2a)

\displaystyle{K}_{\SOS}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}:\mathsf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{s})\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L}\}.

(2b)

Equation

2

\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}

\lambda(\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{j})
returns the vector of coefficients of the product of the polynomials.
Then
{K}_{\SOS}
is equal to the SOS cone of
(\mathbb{R}^{L},\mathbb{R}^{U},\lambda)
.

As we describe in
Section

3.1
,
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
also show how to build lifting operators for general algebras.
This allows us to construct membership checks and easily computable LHSCBs for
{K}_{\SOSpsd}^{\ast}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}
once we represent them as SOS cones of
tensor products
of algebras.

The tensor product of two algebras
(A_{1},B_{1},\diamond_{1})
and
(A_{2},B_{2},\diamond_{2})
is a new algebra
(A_{1}\otimes A_{2},B_{1}\otimes B_{2},\diamond_{1}\otimes\diamond_{2})
, where
\diamond_{1}\otimes\diamond_{2}
is defined via its action on
elementary tensors
.
For
\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1}\in A_{1}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{2}\in A_{2}
:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{2})\diamond_{1}\otimes\diamond_{2}(\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{v}_{2})=(\mathbf{u}_{1}\diamond_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1})\otimes(\mathbf{u}_{2}\diamond_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}).

(15)

The algebra we are interested in for a functional representation of
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
is the tensor product of
(\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d},\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d},\cdot)
with
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{S}^{m},\bar{\diamond})
.
We can think of elements in
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}
as polynomial vectors in
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}^{m}
, and
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{m}
as the symmetric polynomial matrices in
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}^{m\times m}
.
The SOS cone of
(\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{m},\cdot\otimes\bar{\diamond})
corresponds to the polynomial matrices that can be written as
{\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1..N\rrbracket}}\mathbf{m}_{i}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{m}_{i}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}
with
\mathbf{m}_{i}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]^{m}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1..N\rrbracket

(
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
, Section 4.3)
, which is exactly
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
.
Equivalently, a vectorized representation of
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
can be characterized as the SOS cone of
(\mathbb{R}^{L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{U}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{m},\lambda\otimes\bar{\diamond})
.
We can think of
\mathbb{R}^{L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
\mathbb{R}^{L\times m}
and we can think of
\mathbb{R}^{U}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{m}
as a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{U\times m\times m}
that represents the coefficients of symmetric polynomial matrices.

Likewise, the algebra we are interested in for a functional representation of
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
is the tensor product of
(\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d},\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d},\cdot)
with
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m},\circ)
.
We can think of
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,d}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]_{n,2d}^{m}
respectively.
The SOS cone of the tensor product of these algebras then corresponds to
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
due to
Equation

10
.
A vectorized representation of
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
may be characterized as the SOS cone of
(\mathbb{R}^{L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{U}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\lambda\otimes\circ)
.
We can think of
\mathbb{R}^{U}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}
as the coefficients of polynomial vectors, represented in
\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}
.

3.1
Lifting operators for SOS-PSD and SOS-L2

The lifting operator of
(A,B,\diamond)
, when
A
and
B
are finite dimensional, is defined by
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
as the function
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}:B\to\mathbb{S}^{|A|}
satisfying
\langle a_{1},\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}(b)a_{2}\rangle_{A}=\langle b,a_{1}\diamond a_{2}\rangle_{B}
for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,
b\in B
.
This leads to the following descriptions of
{K}_{\diamond}
and
{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}

(
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
, Theorem 3.2)
:

\displaystyle{K}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{s}\in B:\exists\mathbf{S}\succeq 0,\mathbf{s}=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})\},

(16a)

\displaystyle{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{s}\in B:\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}(\mathbf{s})\succeq 0\}.

(16b)

Recall that in order to use either
{K}_{\diamond}
or
{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}
in a generic interior point algorithm, we require efficient oracles for a membership check and derivatives of an LHSCB of
{K}_{\diamond}
or
{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}
.
If
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}(\mathbf{s})
is efficiently computable,
Equation

16b
provides a membership check for
{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}
.
Furthermore, an LHSCB for
{K}_{\diamond}^{\ast}
is given by
\mathbf{s}\mapsto-\logdet({\mathsf{\Lambda}}_{\diamond}(\mathbf{s}))
with barrier parameter
|A|
due to the linearity of
{\mathsf{\Lambda}}_{\diamond}

(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Proposition 5.1.1)
.
The following lemma describes how to compute
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond}(\mathbf{s})
for a tensor product algebra.

Lemma 1

(
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
, Lemma 5.1)
: If
\mathbf{w}_{1}\in B_{1}
and
\mathbf{w}_{2}\in B_{2}
, then:

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond_{1}\otimes\diamond_{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{w}_{2})=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond_{1}}(\mathbf{w}_{1})\otimes_{K}\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\diamond_{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{2}).

(17)

Let us define
\otimes:\mathbb{R}^{U}\times\mathbb{S}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{U\times m\times m}
such that
(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{V})_{i,j,k}=u_{i}V_{j,k}
and let us represent the coefficients of a polynomial matrix by a tensor
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m\times m}
.
Then we may write
\mathbf{S}={\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket,j\in\llbracket 1..i\rrbracket}}\mathbf{S}_{i,j}\otimes\mathbf{E}_{i,j}
, where
\mathbf{E}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is a matrix of zeros and ones with
E_{i,j}=E_{j,i}=1
and
\mathbf{S}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}
are the coefficients of the polynomial in row
i
and column
j
.
Applying
Lemma

1
, the lifting operator for
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
,
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOSpsd}:\mathbb{R}^{U\times m\times m}\to\mathbb{S}^{Lm}
is:

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOSpsd}(\mathbf{S})=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\lambda\otimes\bar{\diamond}}(\mathbf{S})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\lambda\otimes\bar{\diamond}}({\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket,j\in\llbracket 1..i\rrbracket}}\mathbf{S}_{i,j}\otimes\mathbf{E}_{i,j})

(18a)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket,j\in\llbracket 1..i\rrbracket}}\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{S}_{i,j})\otimes_{K}\mathbf{E}_{i,j}.

(18b)

The output is a block matrix, where each
L\times L
submatrix in the
i
th group of rows and
j
th group of columns is
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOSpsd}(\mathbf{S})_{i,j}=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{S}_{i,j})
for all
i,j\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket
.
The adjoint operator
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOSpsd}^{\ast}:\mathbb{S}^{Lm}\to\mathbb{R}^{U\times m\times m}
may also be defined blockwise,

\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOSpsd}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})_{i,j}=\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S}_{i,j})
for all
i,j\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket
where
\mathbf{S}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}
is the
(i,j)
th submatrix in
\mathbf{S}
.

Likewise, we use a tensor
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}
to describe the coefficients of a polynomial vector, and write
\mathbf{s}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}
to denote the vector of coefficients of the polynomial in component
i
.
Applying
Lemma

1
again, we obtain the (blockwise) definition of the lifting operator for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
,
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}:\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}\to\mathbb{S}^{Lm}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{1})&i=j\\
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{j})&i=1,j\neq 1\\
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{i})&i\neq 1,j=1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad\forall i,j\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket,

(19)

where
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})_{i,j}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}
is the
(i,j)
th submatrix of
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})
.
Thus
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})
has a block arrowhead structure.
The output of the adjoint operator
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}:\mathbb{S}^{Lm}\to\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}
may be defined as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})_{i}=\begin{cases}{\textstyle\sum_{j\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket}}\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S}_{j,j})&i=1\\
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S}_{1,i})+\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S}_{i,1})&i\neq 1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad\forall i\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket,

(20)

where
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}
is the
i
th slice of
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}(\mathbf{S})
and
\mathbf{S}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}
is the
(i,j)
th block in
\mathbf{S}
for all
i,j\in\llbracket 1..m\rrbracket
.

4
Efficient barriers for SOS-L2 and SOS-L1

As for
{K}_{\SOSpsd}^{\ast}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}
, we show that a barrier for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}^{\ast}
can be obtained by composing a linear lifting operator with the
\logdet
barrier.
This is sufficient to optimize over
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
,
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}
without high dimensional SDP formulations.
However, for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}^{\ast}
we can derive improved barriers by composing nonlinear functions with the
\logdet
barrier instead.
We show that these compositions are indeed LHSCBs.

4.1
SOS-L2

Recall
Equation

16b
suggests that checking membership in
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}
amounts to checking positive definiteness of
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})
with side dimension

.
This membership check corresponds to a
straightforward
LHSCB with parameter
Lm
given by
\mathbf{s}\mapsto-\logdet(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s}))
.
We now show that by working with a Schur complement of
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})
, we obtain a membership check for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}
that requires factorizations of only two matrices with side dimension
L
and implies an LHSCB with parameter
2L
.

Let
\mathsf{\Pi}:\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}\to\mathbb{S}^{L}
return the Schur complement:

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Pi}(\mathbf{s})={\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{1})-{\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket}}{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{i}){\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{1})^{-1}{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{i}).

(21)

By
Equations

16b
and
21

\displaystyle{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}:\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})\succeq 0\}

(22a)

\displaystyle=\cl\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}:\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s})\succ 0\}

(22b)

\displaystyle=\cl\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}:{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{1})\succ 0,\mathsf{\Pi}(\mathbf{s})\succ 0\}.

(22c)

Equation

22c
describes a simple membership check.
Furthermore, the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=-\logdet(\mathsf{\Pi}(\mathbf{s}))-\logdet({\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}}(\mathbf{s}_{1}))

(23a)

\displaystyle=-\logdet(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}(\mathbf{s}))+(m-2)\logdet(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{1})),

(23b)

is a
2L
-LHSCB barrier for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
.

Theorem 4.1

The function

2L
-LHSCB for

Proof

It is easy to verify that

We first show that
\hat{F}:\mathbb{S}_{++}^{L}\times(\mathbb{R}^{L\times L})^{m-1}\to\mathbb{R}

(24)

We then argue that

is an inverse image of

under the same map.
Then by
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994

is self-concordant.

Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{S}^{L}_{+}\times(\mathbb{R}^{L\times L})^{m-1}
and
\mathbf{G}:\intr(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{S}^{L}
be defined as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{m})=\mathbf{X}_{1}-{\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket}}\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\top}.

(25)

Let us check that
\mathbf{G}
is
(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},1)
-compatible with the domain
\Gamma
in the sense of
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994

.
This requires that

\mathbf{G}
is
C^{3}
-smooth on
\intr(\Gamma)
,

\mathbf{G}
is concave with respect to
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L}

and at each point
\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{m})\in\intr(\Gamma)

\mathbf{V}=(\mathbf{V}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{V}_{m})\in\mathbb{S}^{L}\times(\mathbb{R}^{L\times L})^{m-1}

\displaystyle\tfrac{d^{3}\mathbf{G}}{d\mathbf{X}^{3}}[\mathbf{V},\mathbf{V},\mathbf{V}]\preceq-3\tfrac{d^{2}\mathbf{G}}{d\mathbf{X}^{2}}[\mathbf{V},\mathbf{V}].

\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}\times(\mathbb{R}^{L\times L})^{m-1}

\tfrac{d^{3}\mathbf{G}}{d\mathbf{X}^{3}}
is continuous on the domain of
\mathbf{G}

\displaystyle\tfrac{d^{2}\mathbf{G}}{d\mathbf{X}^{2}}[\mathbf{V},\mathbf{V}]

\displaystyle=-2{\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}-\mathbf{V}_{i})\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}-\mathbf{V}_{i})^{\top},

(27)

\displaystyle=6{\textstyle\sum_{i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}-\mathbf{V}_{i})\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}-\mathbf{V}_{i})^{\top}.

(28)

Since
\mathbf{X}_{1}\succ 0

\intr(\Gamma)

Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994

is concave with respect to

26
) is satisfied.

.

\displaystyle 6\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{1}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1}\preceq-3\times-2\mathbf{X}_{1}^{-1},

(29)

for all

\intr(\Gamma)

(

2L

by composing

∎

SOS-L1

Lemma 2

The set
\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:-\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{Y}\preceq\mathbf{X}\}
is equal to
{K}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}=\cl\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}\succ 0\}

Proof

For inclusion in one direction:

\displaystyle\cl\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}\succ 0\}

(32a)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{X}&\mathbf{Y}\\
\mathbf{Y}&\mathbf{X}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0,\ \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{X}&-\mathbf{Y}\\
-\mathbf{Y}&\mathbf{X}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0\bigr\}

(32b)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigl\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:2\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{v}\pm 2\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{v}\geq 0,\ \forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}\bigr\}

(32c)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{L}:\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Y}\succeq 0,\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\succeq 0\}.

(32d)

For the other direction, suppose
-\mathbf{X}\prec\mathbf{Y}\prec\mathbf{X}
.
Then
\mathbf{X}\succ 0
,
\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{X}\succ 0
,
\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\succ 0
.
Note that
(\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{X})\mathbf{X}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y})=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}
is symmetric.
Due to
Subramanian and Bhagwat 1979
, this product of three matrices also has nonnegative eigenvalues.
We conclude that
-\mathbf{X}\prec\mathbf{Y}\prec\mathbf{X}
implies
\mathbf{X}\succ 0
and
\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}\succeq 0
.
Since
-\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{Y}\preceq\mathbf{X}=\cl\{-\mathbf{X}\prec\mathbf{Y}\prec\mathbf{X}\}
, taking closures gives the result.
∎

By

\displaystyle{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}^{\ast}=\cl\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{U\times m}:\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{1})\succ 0,\\
\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{1})-\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{i})\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{1})^{-1}\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\SOS}(\mathbf{s}_{i})\succ 0,\forall i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket\end{aligned}\right\}.

(33)

Theorem 4.2

The function

is an
Lm
-LHSCB for
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}^{\ast}

Proof

It is easy to verify that

is a logarithmically homogeneous barrier, and we show it is an

-self-concordant barrier.
As in
Theorem

4.1
, we define an auxiliary cone:

\displaystyle{K}_{\ell_{\infty}}^{m}

\displaystyle=\{(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{m})\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{L}\times(\mathbb{R}^{L\times L})^{m-1}:(\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{i})\in{K}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\forall i\in\llbracket 2..m\rrbracket\}.

(35)

Let

Numerical example

For each cone
({K}_{\SOSpsd},{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}},{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}})

We use an example analogous to the polynomial envelope problem from
(

be randomly generated polynomials in

We seek a polynomial that gives the tightest approximation to the

or
\ell_{2}
norm of

\displaystyle\min_{q_{1}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}_{n,2d}[\mathbf{x}]}\int_{[-1,1]^{n}}q_{1}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle:

(47a)

\displaystyle q_{1}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq||(q_{2}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,q_{m}(\mathbf{x}))||_{p}

7
Conclusions

SOS generalizations of PSD,
\ell_{2}
-norm and
\ell_{1}
-norm constraints can be modeled using specialized cones that are simple to use in a generic interior point algorithm.
The characterizations of
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
rely on ideas from
Papp and Alizadeh 2013
as well as the use of a Lagrange polynomial basis for efficient oracles in the multivariate case.
For the
{K}_{\SOSpsd}
barrier, the complexity of evaluating the Hessian is reduced by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(m^{2})
from the SOS formulation barrier.
This does not result in significant speed improvements since Hessian evaluations are not the bottleneck in an interior point algorithm.
In contrast, the dimension and barrier parameter of the
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}
cones are lower compared to their SOS formulations, and the complexity of evaluating the Hessian of the
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
barrier is reduced by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(m^{3})
from its SOS formulation.
For both
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{2}}
and
{K}_{\SOS\ell_{1}}
, the total solve time was generally lower compared to their SOS formulations.
While there is no penalty in using lower dimensional representations of SOS-L1 constraints, SOS-L2 formulations give rise to more conservative restrictions than higher dimensional SOS formulations, which is observable in practice.

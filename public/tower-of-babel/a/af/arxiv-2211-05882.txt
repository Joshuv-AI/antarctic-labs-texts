Field line subdiffusion and cosmic ray perpendicular transport in isotropic turbulence Preprint: TTK-22-36
Marco Kuhlen, Philipp Mertsch, Vo Hong Minh Phan

Abstract
The transport of high-energy particles in the presence of small-scale, turbulent magnetic fields is a long-standing issue in astrophysics. Analytical theories disagree with numerical simulations at rigidities where the particles’ gyroradii are slightly smaller than the correlation length of turbulence. At the same time, extending the numerical simulations to lower rigidities has proven computationally prohibitive. In this letter , we provide a solution to the problem of perpendicular transport in isotropic turbulence at both, high and low rigidities. We also clarify the relation between the perpendicular diffusion of particles and the transport of magnetic field lines. To this end, we have run a large suite of test particle simulations at unprecedentedly low rigidities, making extensive use of graphical processing units (GPUs). We have also developed an analytical model, based on (1) initial particle transport along field lines, (2) the transport of field lines and (3) the eventual decorrelation of particles from field lines. Our numerical results exhibit a non-standard rigidity-dependence for the perpendicular diffusion coefficient at intermediate rigidities. At the lowest rigidities, the standard rigidity-dependence is recovered. The simulated diffusion coefficients are nicely reproduced by our analytical model. We have traced the non-standard rigidity-dependence to a subdiffusive phase in the field line transport. Our study has important implications for the transport of Galactic cosmic rays, acceleration at perpendicular shocks and for high-energy particles in the heliosphere.



Much of the universe is pervaded by magnetic fields, oftentimes turbulent, and high-energy charged particles, cosmic rays (CRs). The transport of charged particles in the presence of turbulent magnetic fields is a fundamental question in astrophysics. The presence of a large-scale, coherent magnetic field breaks the isotropy and leads to the differentiation of particle transport along and across this large-scale field. Transport along the field has been understood a while ago to be the result of initially ballistic gyrations, followed by pitch-angle scattering due to resonant interactions
Jokipii 1966
;
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
;
Hall and Sturrock 1967
. Eventually, pitch-angle scattering results in spatial diffusion along the large-scale magnetic field
Hasselmann and Wibberenz 1970
. Perpendicular transport has proven much more difficult to model, but is generally believed to be the combination of transport along field lines and transport of the field lines
Shalchi 2009
;
Shalchi 2020
.

Analytical models can be compared to so-called test particle simulations. Those are based on solving the equations of motion of individual particles in a turbulent magnetic field simulated on a computer. (See Ref.
Mertsch 2020
for a review.) At low reduced rigidities
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}
(where
r_{\text{g}}
is the gyroradius and
L_{\text{c}}
is the correlation length of turbulence
Harari et al. 2002
), particle trajectories have to be followed for very long times before particle motion becomes diffusive. Previously, simulations have only been possible at relatively large
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\gtrsim 10^{-2}

Giacalone and Jokipii 1999
;
Casse et al. 2001
;
Qin et al. 2002a
;
Qin et al. 2002b
;
DeMarco et al. 2007
;
Giacinti et al. 2012
;
Snodin et al. 2016
;
Pucci et al. 2016
;
Subedi et al. 2017
;
Giacinti et al. 2018
;
Schlegel et al. 2020
;
Reichherzer et al. 2020
;
Reichherzer et al. 2022
. For parameters typical of the interstellar medium, e.g. an rms field strength of
4\,\mu\text{G}
and
L_{\text{c}}=30\,\text{pc}
, this corresponds to rigidities
\gtrsim 1\,\text{PV}
.

Recently, it has become apparent that none of the analytical theories developed over the last decades, e.g.
Matthaeus et al. 2003
;
Shalchi 2010
, can describe the results of test particle simulations for isotropic turbulence
Dundovic et al. 2020
. For instance, these theories typically predict a rather weak, but falling dependence of the ratio of perpendicular and parallel mean free paths,
\lambda_{\perp}/\lambda_{\parallel}
on
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}
. Instead, in simulations, the ratio increases for
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\lesssim 1
and drops sharply for
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\gg 1

DeMarco et al. 2007
;
Snodin et al. 2016
;
Dundovic et al. 2020
. This failure of analytical theories is most worrisome and casts doubt on our general understanding of perpendicular transport. Furthermore, if the difference in the rigidity-scaling of
\lambda_{\parallel}
and
\lambda_{\perp}
were confirmed, this would have important consequences for phenomenological applications of such models, for instance in modelling of Galactic cosmic rays and diffuse emission
Evoli et al. 2012
;
Cerri et al. 2017
;
Giacinti et al. 2018
;
Reichherzer et al. 2022
.

We have run a large suite of test particle simulations with a field composed of a large-scale, coherent magnetic field, taken to point in the
z
-direction and a small-scale, static, turbulent magnetic field
\bm{\delta B}(\bm{r})
. (The magneto-static approximation is valid for particle velocities exceeding the typical speed of the turbulent waves, e.g. the Alfvén speed.) The turbulent modes are isotropically distributed and follow a Kolmogorov
Kolmogorov 1941
power spectrum
P(k)\propto k^{-5/3}
for
k>(2\pi)/L_{\text{max}}
with an outer scale
L_{\text{max}}
, where
\langle\bm{\delta\tilde{B}}(\bm{k})\bm{\delta\tilde{B}}^{*}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}P(k)\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})
. Here, angled brackets denote averages over an ensemble of turbulent magnetic field. The correlation length
L_{\text{c}}\simeq L_{\text{max}}/5
and the overall turbulent strength is parametrised by
\eta\equiv\delta B^{2}/(B_{0}^{2}+\delta B^{2})
with
\delta B^{2}\equiv 8\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}P(k)
.

From the particle trajectories,
\{x(t),y(t),z(t)\}
, we have computed running parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients
Shalchi 2009
,

\displaystyle d_{\parallel}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle(\Delta z(t))^{2}\rangle\,,

(1)

\displaystyle d_{\perp}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\langle(\Delta x(t))^{2}\rangle+\langle(\Delta y(t))^{2}\rangle\right)\,,

(2)

with
\Delta x(t)=x(t)-x(0)
etc.
Here, the angled brackets denote the average over initial particle directions and realisations of the turbulent magnetic field.

We have also computed the magnetic field lines
\{x(s),y(s),z(s)\}
, parametrised by their arc-length
s
, by solving the field line equations for individual realisations of
\bm{\delta B}
. From this, we have computed the running field line (FL) diffusion coefficient
Shalchi 2009
,

d_{\text{FL}}(s)\equiv\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\langle(\Delta x(s))^{2}\rangle+\langle(\Delta y(s))^{2}\rangle\right)\,.

(3)

Note that in axisymmetric turbulence,
\langle(\Delta x(s))^{2}\rangle=\langle(\Delta y(s))^{2}\rangle
.

To cover the large dynamical range of
L_{\text{max}}/L_{\text{min}}\simeq 10^{-6}
required for resonant scattering at low rigidities, we have employed the nested grid method
Giacinti et al. 2012
;
Giacinti et al. 2018
;
Mertsch 2020
for setting up a large enough number of individual realisations of
\bm{\delta B}
on a computer. We employ 16 levels of subgrids and the largest grid extends over
40\,L_{\text{c}}
so as to minimise periodicity artefacts. We have made use of graphical processing units (GPUs) that allow for a significant speed up
Tautz 2016
;
Kuhlen et al. 2022
. In a companion paper
Kuhlen et al. 2022
, we provide a detailed discussion of the method and possible systematic errors as well as the time-dependent running diffusion coefficients.

Our analytical model is based on the same principles as earlier works
Bieber and Matthaeus 1997
;
Matthaeus et al. 2003
;
Qin et al. 2002b
;
Shalchi et al. 2004
;
Shalchi 2019
, but with a crucial difference. (1) Initially, particles follow magnetic field lines, at first ballistically, later diffusively. (2) The transport of field lines is also transitioning from an early ballistic phase to an eventual diffusive regime. At variance with earlier studies, however, we allow for the possibility of a subdiffusive episode in field line transport where
\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\propto s^{\alpha}
with
\alpha<1
. (3) Eventually, as the particle displacement in the perpendicular direction exceeds about a correlation length, particles can be considered decoupled from individual field lines and start to diffuse
Shalchi 2019
.
Based on these principles, we can express the running perpendicular diffusion coefficient
d_{\perp}(t)
in terms of the field line diffusion coefficient,
d_{\text{FL}}(z)
, the particle running parallel diffusion coefficient
d_{\parallel}(t)
and the particle mean-square displacement,
\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle
,

\displaystyle d_{\perp}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}d_{\text{FL}}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\text{FL}}(\sqrt{\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle})}{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle}}d_{\parallel}(t).

(4)

Note that it follows from eq. (
1
) that

\langle(\Delta z)^{2}(t)\rangle=2\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}d_{\parallel}(t)\,.

(5)

Eq. (
4
) represents the main result of this
letter
. In the supplemental material, we provide three derivations of this relation, a heuristic one, one based on a microscopic treatment and one based on compound transport.

It is easy to see that eq. (
4
) will result in subdiffusive behaviour for the running perpendicular diffusion coefficient
d_{\perp}(t)\propto t^{-1/2}
for times
t\gg\tau_{\text{d}}
where particle transport in the parallel direction is diffusive,
\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle\propto t\Leftrightarrow d_{\parallel}=\kappa_{\parallel}
and
z>L_{\text{c}}
such that field line transport is also diffusive, that is
d_{\text{FL}}(z)=\kappa_{\text{FL}}
. Here,
\kappa_{\parallel}
and
\kappa_{\text{FL}}
denote the
t\to\infty
and
z\to\infty
limits of
d_{\parallel}
and
d_{\text{FL}}
, respectively. However, once particles have travelled more than
\sim L_{\text{c}}
in the perpendicular direction they experience the transverse complexity, decouple from individual field lines and their perpendicular motion becomes diffusive
Shalchi 2019
, with the asymptotic diffusion coefficient
d_{\perp}(\tau_{c})=\kappa_{\perp}=\text{const.}
The time
\tau_{\text{c}}
is implicitly defined through

d_{\perp}(\tau_{\text{c}})\!=\!\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle(\Delta x)^{2}(t)\rangle\Big|_{t\to\tau_{\text{c}}}\sim\frac{\langle(\Delta x)^{2}(\tau_{\text{c}})\rangle}{2\tau_{\text{c}}}\sim\frac{L_{\text{c}}^{2}}{2\tau_{\text{c}}}.

(6)

In order to evaluate
\kappa_{\perp}=d_{\perp}(\tau_{c})
with eqs. (
4
) and (
6
), we need to specify the functional forms for the running parallel and field line diffusion coefficients,
d_{\parallel}
and
d_{\text{FL}}
. We parametrise the time-dependence of
d_{\parallel}
with a broken power law,

d_{\parallel}(t)=\kappa_{\parallel}\left(1+\left(t/\tau_{\text{s}}\right)^{-1/s_{d}}\right)^{-s_{d}}

(7)

where
\tau_{\text{s}}=3\kappa_{\parallel}/v^{2}
and
s_{d}=1/2
. At early times,
t\ll\tau_{\text{s}}
, parallel particle transport is ballistic,
d_{\parallel}(t)\simeq\kappa_{\parallel}t/\tau_{\text{s}}=v^{2}t/3
; at late times,
t\gg\tau_{\text{s}}
, it turns diffusive,
d_{\parallel}(t)\simeq\kappa_{\parallel}
.

We model the asymptotic diffusion coefficient
\kappa_{\parallel}
as a broken power law in
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}

Shalchi 2009
,

\kappa_{\parallel}(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})\!=\!A(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/3}\left(1+\left(\frac{r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}}{\rho_{*}}\right)^{5/(3s_{\kappa})}\right)^{s_{\kappa}}.

(8)

At small rigidities,
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\ll\rho_{*}
, CRs diffuse due to resonant interactions with turbulent fields and the rigidity-scaling
\kappa_{\parallel}\propto(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/3}
is characteristic for the Kolmogorov turbulence assumed. At large rigidities,
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\gg\rho_{*}
, no resonant interactions are possible; instead, particles diffuse in the small-angle scattering limit
\kappa_{\parallel}\propto(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{2}

Gruzinov 2018
. For each turbulence level
\eta
, there are thus three free parameters for
d_{\parallel}
:
A
,
\rho_{*}
, and
s_{\kappa}
which are determined by fitting eq. (
8
) to the simulated
\kappa_{\parallel}=(3/v)\lambda_{\parallel}
.

As far as
d_{\text{FL}}
is concerned, our simulations have shown that field-line transport does not transition directly from ballistic to diffusive behaviour
Kuhlen et al. 2022
.
Instead, there is a subdiffusive phase where
d_{\text{FL}}
decreases with time.
As the field-line transport is inherently non-linear, it is rather difficult to develop intuition for the intermediate times where the field line transport is neither ballistic nor diffusive. One can however investigate this period semi-analytically, by solving a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) for the mean-square displacement
\langle(\Delta x(s))^{2}\rangle
. We provide a discussion of this approach in the supplemental material.

We model the transition from ballistic to diffusive field line transport including an intermediate subdiffusive phase with a double-broken power law:

d_{\text{FL}}(z)=C\,z\frac{\delta B_{x}^{2}}{B_{0}^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{z}{z_{1}}\right)^{(1-\gamma)/s_{1}}\right)^{-s_{1}}\left(1+\left(\frac{z}{z_{2}}\right)^{-\gamma/s_{2}}\right)^{s_{2}}\,.

(9)

Here,
C
is a normalisation constant,
z_{1,2}
are the distances along the field-line where the power law scaling of
d_{\text{FL}}
changes and
s_{1,2}
are the softnesses of these breaks.
For small distances,
z\ll z_{1}
, field-line transport is ballistic,
d_{\text{FL}}(z)\simeq z(\delta B_{x}^{2}/B_{0}^{2})
in line with analytical models of ballistic field line transport
Shalchi 2020
.
For
z_{1}\ll z\ll z_{2}
, our parametrisation gives
d_{\text{FL}}(z)\propto z^{\gamma}
, thus allowing field line transport to be sub-diffusive, if
\gamma<0
.
For
z\gg z_{2}
,
d_{\text{FL}}(z)=\text{const.}

By fitting to the simulated running field line diffusion coefficient for the three turbulence levels
\eta=0.2,0.5
and
0.8
, we can fix all, but one of the parameters of eq. (
9
).
Specifically, we find that
C=0.8,z_{1}=1.5\,L_{\text{c}}
and
z_{2}=8\,L_{\text{c}}
reproduce the simulations well for
\eta=0.2
.
For
\eta=0.5
, we use
C=0.55,z_{1}=1.5\,L_{\text{c}}
and
z_{2}=5\,L_{\text{c}}
and for
\eta=0.8
we adopt
C=0.2,z_{1}=2.5\,L_{\text{c}}
and
z_{2}=5.5\,L_{\text{c}}
.
In all cases,
s_{1}=1.5
and
s_{2}=0.2
.

In contrast, the decorrelation time
\tau_{c}
is rather sensitive to the choice of
\gamma
and so we have determined

by fitting to the simulated asymptotic perpendicular mean free path
\lambda_{\perp}

Similarly, the above requirement for transverse complexity is only approximate. Therefore we replace

In summary, our model has two free parameters which cannot be fixed by fitting to the simulated running field line diffusion coefficient

The cause of the non-standard rigidity-dependence for intermediate rigidities is the sub-diffusive phase in the running field-line diffusion coefficient. The fact that without such a subdiffusive phase, the non-standard rigidity-dependence for intermediate rigidities could not be reproduced is illustrated by the dotted lines in Figs.

To summarise, we have shown the results of a large suite of simulations of the transport of high-energy particles and field lines in isotropic Kolmogorov-like turbulence. The perpendicular diffusion coefficient in particular scales like
(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/3}
for low rigidities,
(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/2}
at intermediate rigidities and becomes constant for
r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}}\gg 1
. This behaviour is nicely reproduced in our analytical model and is ultimately due to a subdiffusive phase in the running field line diffusion coefficient.

Our study has provided a number of crucial insights: First, we have confirmed the non-standard rigidity-scaling of the perpendicular mean free path at intermediate rigidities, but also found that it transitions back to the standard
(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/3}
-dependence at the lowest rigidities. Second, we have for the first time provided a consistent analytical model that reproduces the non-standard behaviour. Our parametrised running and asymptotic diffusion coefficients are easy to implement in phenomenological studies or for comparison with future test particle simulations. Third, our model largely confirms the interpretation of perpendicular transport as due to particle transport along field lines and field line transport in the perpendicular direction, but with the need for a subdiffusive phase in the running field line diffusion coefficient. Finally, this understanding has given us renewed confidence in the standard scaling
\lambda_{\perp}\propto(r_{\text{g}}/L_{\text{c}})^{1/3}
of the perpendicular diffusion coefficient at low rigidities. We provide a discussion of applications to a number of astrophysical problems in the companion paper
Kuhlen et al. 2022
.

Appendix B
Derivation of the running perpendicular diffusion coefficient

In the following, we provide three derivations for the running perpendicular diffusion coefficient, eq. (
4
): a heuristic one, one based on a microscopic treatment and one based on compound transport.

B.1
Heuristic derivation

We employ the definition of the running perpendicular diffusion coefficient, eq. (
2
), and the running FL diffusion coefficient, eq. (
3
). Integrating eq. (
3
),

\langle(\Delta x^{\text{FL}})^{2}(z)\rangle=2\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\text{FL}}(z^{\prime})\equiv\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\,,

(10)

identifying
\Delta x(t)=\Delta x^{\text{FL}}(\langle z^{2}(t)\rangle)
,

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}(t)\rangle=2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\text{FL}}(z^{\prime})\,,

(11)

Substituting eq. (
11
) into eq. (
2
) leads to

\displaystyle d_{\perp}(t)\!=\!\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\!\!\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}d_{\text{FL}}(z^{\prime})\!=\!\frac{d_{\text{FL}}(\!\sqrt{\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle})}{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta z)^{2}\rangle}}d_{\parallel}(t).

which is eq. (
4
).

B.2
Microscopic derivation

In the following, we provide a microscopic derivation for eq. (
4
), which bears some resemblance with time-dependent unified non-linear theory (UNLT)
Shalchi 2017
;
Lasuik and Shalchi 2017
.
Note however, that time-dependent UNLT has not been applied to isotropic turbulence before.
We start from the assumption that up to the time where transverse complexity becomes important, particles follow field lines,

v_{x}=\frac{\delta B_{x}}{B_{0}}v_{z}\,.

(12)

The velocity autocorrelation therefore evaluates to

\displaystyle\langle v_{x}(t)v_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\langle\delta B_{x}(t)\delta B_{x}(0)v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\rangle

Usually, this is evaluated by first assuming that the four point function factorises, e.g.
Matthaeus et al. 2003
;
Shalchi 2020
,

\langle\delta B_{x}(t)\delta B_{x}(0)v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\rangle\simeq\langle\delta B_{x}(t)\delta B_{x}(0)\rangle\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\rangle\,,

(13)

and then using the Fourier representation for the magnetic field two-point function,

\displaystyle\langle\delta B_{x}(t)\delta B_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k^{\prime}\,\mathrm{e}^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}(t)-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{x}(0))}\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\Big\rangle

(14)

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{x}(t)-\bm{x}(0))}\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\,.

Applying Corrsin’s independence hypothesis
Corrsin 1959
, this results in

\displaystyle\langle\delta B_{x}(t)\delta B_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{x}(t)-\bm{x}(0))}\rangle\langle\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\,,

which can be evaluated by making an assumption for the spatial distribution of particles, e.g. assuming it to be Gaussian. However, the approximation of eq. (
13
) ignores correlations between particle positions and velocities and it has been shown
Shalchi 2020
that this loss of correlations leads to erroneous results. For instance in slab turbulence, perpendicular particle transport is known to be subdiffusive,
\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\propto t^{1/2}
while the result following from the assumption of eq. (
13
) is diffusive,
\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\propto t
. Allowing for correlations between particle positions and velocities, however, perpendicular subdiffusion is recovered
Shalchi 2020
.

Here, we factorise the correlation function into terms which depend on the parallel transport, on the magentic field and on the perpendicular position,

\displaystyle\langle v_{x}(t)v_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle\langle\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\,.

(15)

B.2.1
Cumulant expansion

In the following, we evaluate the first term in the integrand,
\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle
. With
v_{z}(t)=\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}t
we find

\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle=\frac{1}{ik_{\parallel}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle\,,

(16)

and we expand the complex exponential into a power series,

\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}(ik_{\parallel}z)^{n}\,.

(17)

We assume Gaussian statistics, that is only correlation functions of even order (in
v_{z}
and
z
) contribute and higher order correlation functions can be expressed in terms of products of two-point functions. Therefore, in the exponential series of eq. (
17
) only the odd terms contribute,

\displaystyle\langle v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}(ik_{\parallel})^{n}\langle v_{z}(0)z^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2p+1)!}(ik_{\parallel})^{2p+1}\langle v_{z}(0)z^{2p+1}\rangle\,.

(18)

A cumulant expansion
Kubo 1962
gives,

\langle v_{z}(0)z^{2p+1}\rangle=(2p+1)\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle\langle z^{2p}\rangle=(2p+1)\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle(2p-1)!!\langle z^{2}\rangle^{p}\,.

We can now exploit the fact that
(2p+1)!!/(2p+1)!=1/2^{p}/(p!)
and perform the sum in eq. (
18
),

\displaystyle\langle v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2p+1)!}(ik_{\parallel})^{2p+1}(2p+1)\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle(2p-1)!!\langle z^{2}\rangle^{p}

\displaystyle=ik_{\parallel}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2p+1)!!}{(2p+1)!}(ik_{\parallel})^{2p}\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle\langle z^{2}\rangle^{p}

\displaystyle=ik_{\parallel}\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!}\left(-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=ik_{\parallel}\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}

\displaystyle=ik_{\parallel}d_{\parallel}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\,.

(19)

In the last line we have used the fact that
\langle v_{z}(0)z\rangle=d_{\parallel}
which follows from

d_{ij}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\langle v_{i}(t^{\prime})v_{j}(0)\rangle=\left\langle\int_{0}^{x_{i}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}_{i}v_{j}(0)\right\rangle=\langle x_{i}(t)v_{j}(0)\rangle\,.

Combining eqs. (
16
) and (
19
) we thus find,

\displaystyle\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{\parallel}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(d_{\parallel}(t)\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}-k_{\parallel}^{2}\left(d_{\parallel}(t)\right)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\,.

(21)

Note how this differs from the result obtained if the correlations between
v_{z}
and
z
were ignored,

\displaystyle\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(d_{\parallel}(t)\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\,.

(22)

In this case, the diffusivity is usually overestimated. The last term in eq. (
21
) can be considered a correction due to correlations between parallel velocities and parallel positions. It reduces the velocity autocorrelation function, thus allowing for instance for sub-diffusive behaviour for the case of slab turbulence.

Eq. (
20
) can be further simplified by again using that
d_{\parallel}(t)=(1/2)\mathrm{d}\langle z^{2}\rangle/\mathrm{d}t
,

\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle=\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{\parallel}^{2}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\Big)\,.

(23)

B.2.2
Perpendicular mean-square displacement

We substitute eq. (
23
) into the perpendicular velocity autocorrelation, eq. (
15
),

\displaystyle\langle v_{x}(t)v_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\langle v_{z}(t)v_{z}(0)\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\rangle\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{\parallel}^{2}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\Big)\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\,.

(24)

Both in the ballistic and diffusive regime, the displacement in the perpendicular direction is much slower than in the parallel one. We therefore consider
\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\simeq\text{const.}
over the time-scales where the integral has support and so, we can pull the double time-derivative out of the integral,

\displaystyle\langle v_{x}(t)v_{x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{\parallel}^{2}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\,.

(25)

Using the fact that

\langle v_{x}(t)v_{x}(0)\rangle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\,,

(26)

we can integrate eq. (
25
),

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\parallel}\langle z^{2}\rangle}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{1}{(ik_{\parallel})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}

(27)

\displaystyle=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\int\mathrm{d}z\int\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\,.

(28)

Again, for small turbulence level, we evaluate the field line essentially along the
z
-axis and so we can approximate the Fourier transform

\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\mathrm{e}^{ik_{\parallel}z}\langle\delta B_{x}(\bm{k})\delta B^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{i\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{x}_{\perp}}\rangle\simeq\langle\delta B_{x}(z)\delta B_{x}(0)\rangle\,.

(29)

With this, eq. (
28
) gives

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}z\int\mathrm{d}z\langle\delta B_{x}(z)\delta B_{x}(0)\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}

(30)

\displaystyle=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\text{FL}}(z^{\prime})\,.

(31)

Eq. (
31
) can be evaluated in two ways.

B.2.3
Estimating the integral

This expression can be evaluated by again switching the order of integrations,

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\int_{z^{\prime}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/(2\langle z^{2}\rangle)}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\right)\,,

(32)

where
\mathrm{erfc}(\cdot)
denotes the complementary error function,

\mathrm{erfc}(\zeta)\equiv\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\zeta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta^{2}}\,.

(33)

The factor
\mathrm{erfc}(z^{\prime}/\sqrt{2\langle z^{2}\rangle})
in the integrand of eq. (
32
) is suppressing contributions to the integral for
z^{\prime}\gtrsim\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle/2}
. Thus we can approximate the integral of eq. (
32
) as

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\simeq 2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle/2}}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\,.

(34)

Especially for the oftentimes found broken power law forms for
d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})
this is a good approximation for eq. (
32
). Up to order one factors, this agrees with eq. (
11
) and so with eq. (
2
), again, we recover eq. (
4
).

B.2.4
Saddle point method

Alternatively, the integral of eq. (
31
) can be evaluated with a saddle point method. To this end, we first rewrite eq. (
31
) in dimensionless quantitites,
\tilde{\zeta}\equiv\zeta/\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle}
etc.,

\frac{\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle}{\langle z^{2}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{z}^{2}/2}\underbrace{2\int_{0}^{|\tilde{z}|}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}^{\prime}\,\tilde{d}_{\mathrm{FL}}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}_{=\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{z}^{2}/2}\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})\,.

(35)

We also define

f(\tilde{z})\equiv\frac{\tilde{z}^{2}}{2}-\ln\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})\,.

Generally speaking,
\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})\propto\tilde{z}^{2}
for
\tilde{z}\ll\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{FL}}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})\propto\tilde{z}\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{FL}}
for
\tilde{z}\gg\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{FL}}
. In the former case,
f(\tilde{z})
has a minimum at
\tilde{z}_{0}=\sqrt{2}
irrespective of what the constant of proportionality is; in the latter case the minimum is at
\tilde{z}_{0}=1
, again independent of the constant of proportionality. As
\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\tilde{z})
interpolates between the two limits, the minimum of
f(\tilde{z})
will be between
\tilde{z}_{0}=1
and
\sqrt{2}
.
The second derviative of
f(\tilde{z})
evaluated at the minimum of
f
,
f^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{z}_{0})
, evaluates to
2
in both limits and so we assume
f^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{z}_{0})=2
to hold always. Hence the saddle point approximation of the integral is

\displaystyle\frac{\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle}{\langle z^{2}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-f(\tilde{z})}

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{-f(\tilde{z}_{0})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-f^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{z}_{0})(\tilde{z}-\tilde{z}_{0})^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{-f(\tilde{z}_{0})}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{f^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{z}_{0})}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\mathrm{e}^{-f(\tilde{z}_{0})}\,.

In the limit of
\tilde{z}\ll\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{FL}}
, this evaluates to
\sqrt{2}\mathrm{e}^{-1/2}\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(1)
and in the limit of
\tilde{z}\gg\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{FL}}
to
\sqrt{2}\mathrm{e}^{-1}\tilde{\sigma}_{x}^{2}(\sqrt{2})
. So, up to order one factors, this results in the same functional dependence that was motivated by heuristic arguments,

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle\simeq\sigma_{x}^{2}(\sqrt{\langle z^{2}\rangle})\,.

This is identical with eq. (
11
) and with eq. (
2
), again, we recover eq. (
4
).

B.3
Compound transport

We model the perpendicular transport of particles as compound transport
Lingenfelter et al. 1971
;
Webb et al. 2006
, that is the combination of particle transport along the field line and transport of the field line.
This description should apply up until the time that particles decorrelate from the field line.
We work under the assumption that the turbulent field strength is small, such that the field line direction approximately coincides with the background field direction, which we take to be the
z
-direction.
Specifically, we will consider the conditional probability
p_{\mathrm{FL}}(x|z)
for finding the field line at a distance
x
perpendicular to the background field direction for a given position
z
along the background field and the conditional probability
p_{z}(z|t)
for finding the particle at the position
z
at a given time
t
.
The probability density density
p_{x}(x|t)
for the
x
-position of the particle at time
t
is then the product of
p_{\mathrm{FL}}(x|z)
and
p_{z}(z|t)
integrated over
z
,

p_{x}(x|t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,p_{\mathrm{FL}}(x|z)p_{z}(z|t)\,.

From this, we compute the mean square displacement of the particle in the perpendicular direction,

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{2}\,p_{x}(x|t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{2}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,p_{\mathrm{FL}}(x|z)p_{z}(z|t)\,.

We can now switch the order of integrations and make use of the definition of the variance of the
x
-position of the field line,

\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{2}\,p_{\mathrm{FL}}(x|z)\,,

(36)

to find

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\,p_{z}(z|t)\,,

(37)

The variance of eq. (
36
) can also be expressed in terms of the field line diffusion coefficient,

\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)=2\int_{0}^{|z|}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\,,

and so with eq. (
37
),

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,p_{z}(z|t)\,2\int_{0}^{|z|}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\,.

(38)

Next, we model the probability density
p_{z}(z|t)
for the particle position along the field line as a Gaussian,

p_{z}(z|t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/(2\langle z^{2}\rangle)}\,,

where
\sigma_{z}=\sigma_{z}(t)
. Note that we have not made any assumption about the time-dependence of
\sigma_{z}(t)
yet. We thus find for the perpendicular mean-square displacement of the particle,

\langle(\Delta x)^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle z^{2}\rangle}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/(2\langle z^{2}\rangle)}2\int_{0}^{|z|}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,d_{\mathrm{FL}}(z^{\prime})\,.

(39)

This is again eq. (
31
) and in the same way as before, ultimately eq. (
4
) can be recovered.

B.4
ODE approach for running field line diffusion coefficient

The running field line diffusion coefficient
d_{\text{FL}}
can be approximated as the solution of a set of coupled ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
Sonsrettee et al. 2016
.
Here, we consider a simplified setup where the dispersion
\sigma_{z}^{2}
in the field line direction is ignored.
In addition, we approximate the arc-length along the field line with the coordinate
z
along the background field direction which should be a good approximation for small turbulence levels
\eta
.

We start by integrating the field line equation for the
x
-component,

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta x(z))^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime\prime}\,\langle\delta B_{x}(z^{\prime})\delta B_{x}(z^{\prime\prime})\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}(z-z^{\prime})\,\langle\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\langle\mathrm{e}^{\imath\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\Delta x}}\rangle

(41)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{B_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}(z-z^{\prime})\,\langle\delta\tilde{B}_{x}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{x}(\bm{k})\rangle\cos\left(k_{\parallel}\Delta z\right)\langle\mathrm{e}^{\imath\bm{k}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\Delta x}_{\perp}}\rangle\,,

(42)

where we have made use of Corrsin’s independence hypothesis
Corrsin 1959
and assumed independence of the characteristic functions in the parallel and perpendicular directions. For an isotropic Kolmogorov spectrum,

\langle\delta\tilde{B}_{i}(\bm{k})\delta\tilde{B}^{*}_{j}(\bm{k})\rangle\propto\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{-11/3}\,.

We differentiate eq. (
42
) twice with respect to
z
and together with the definition of
d_{\text{FL}}
, the system of coupled ODEs reads,

\displaystyle d_{\text{FL}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}z}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\text{FL}}}{\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{N}}\frac{\delta B^{2}}{B_{0}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}(\cos\theta)\,\int_{k_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{-11/3}(1+\cos^{2}\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{k^{2}}{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\sin^{2}\theta}\cos(k\cos\theta z)\,,

(44)

where
\sigma_{x}^{2}(z)\equiv\langle(\Delta x(z))^{2}\rangle
. Here, the normalisation constant
\mathcal{N}
is defined as

\mathcal{N}=8\pi\int_{k_{0}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{-11/3}

(45)

Figure 5:
Running field line diffusion coefficient
d_{\text{FL}}
. For turbulence levels of
\eta=0.2
and
0.5
we compare the result of the numerically integration of eqs. (
43
) and (
44
) with the results of the numerical simulations
Kuhlen et al. 2022
.

We have solved this system numerically for
\eta=0.2
and
0.5
and we show the resulting
d_{\text{FL}}(z)
in Fig.
5
.
Note that we have rescaled the results from the numerical solution of eqs. (
43
) and (
44
) by a factor
0.8
and
0.56
for
\eta=0.2
and
0.5
, respectively, to achieve agreement for small
z
.
Overall, the agreement between simulations and this semi-analytical approach is rather good.
At
z\ll L_{\text{c}}
, field lines move ballistically and so
d_{\text{FL}}
increases linearly with
z
.
At
z\gg L_{\text{c}}
, field lines travel diffusively such that
d_{\text{FL}}=\text{const.}

In between, there is no direct transition; instead,
d_{\text{FL}}
seems to overshoot and then oscillates before approaching the asymptotic value.
These oscillation are seen both for the simulations and the semi-analytical approach, even though the period and phase do not match perfectly.

Given the non-linear nature of the system of ODEs, it is difficult to understand the origin of the subdiffusive phase analytically.
However, in very broad terms, the three different phases can be understood qualitatively based on eqs. (
43
) and (
44
):
For small
z
,
\sigma_{x}^{2}
is very small, such that both the exponential factor and the cosine in the integrand of eq. (
44
) evaluate to
1
.
The right hand side of eq. (
44
) is then independent of
z
, such that
d_{\text{FL}}\propto z
.
For very large
z
, the exponential term in eq. (
44
) drives the the integrand to zero, such that
d_{\text{FL}}
becomes constant.
The overshooting at intermediate times is due to the cosine term which changes sign.

Description of tempered exponential dichotomies by admissibility with no Lyapunov norms Thanks: Corresponding author: Linfeng Zhou (zlf63math@163.com, zlf63math@scu.edu.cn)
Davor Dragičević, Weinian Zhang, Linfeng Zhou

Abstract
. Tempered exponential dichotomy formulates the nonuniform hyperbolicity for random dynamical systems. It was described by admissibility of a pair of function classes defined with Lyapunov norms, For MET-systems (systems satisfying the assumptions of multiplicative ergodic theorem (abbreviated as MET)), it can be described by admissibility of a pair without a Lyapunov norm. However, it is not known how to choose a suitable Lyapunov norms before a tempered exponential dichotomy is given, and there are examples of random systems which are not MET-systems but have a tempered exponential dichotomy. In this paper we give a description of tempered exponential dichotomy for general random systems, which may not be MET-systems, purely by measurable admissibility of three pairs of function classes with no Lyapunov norms. Further, restricting to the MET-systems, we obtain a simpler description of only one pair with no Lyapunov norms. Finally, we use our results to prove the roughness of tempered exponential dichotomies for parametric random systems and give a Hölder continuous dependence of the associated projections on the parameter.

Such an investigation on description of dichotomy by admissibility
was also extended to

For random dynamical systems,

Later,

,

In this paper we purely use admissibility of function classes with no Lyapunov norms
to give a complete description of tempered exponential dichotomies

, which may not be MET-systems.
In section
2
, we recall the concept of tempered exponential dichotomy,
define several classes of weighted-bounded sequences, which
are constructed in terms of the original norm of

In section

In section

More precisely, one of the three pairs

In comparison with
[

]
,

and the weighted-unstable subspaces,

purely uses three admissibility pairs but does not need the assumption
even if we ignore ‘random’.
In section
5

restricting to MET-systems,

Using an induced system to estimate
the upper (resp. lower) bound of the Lyapunov exponents for the random system restricted on the stable (resp. unstable) subspace in Section

is partially inspired by those developed in
[
DSS
]
for uniform exponential dichotomy.
However, the induced system constructed in
[
DSS
]

can not be used in our case because of the nonuniformity.
Section

is devoted to application.
First, we apply our result obtained in section 4
to giving the roughness of tempered exponential dichotomies
for parametric random systems
and give a Hölder continuous dependence of the associated projections on the parameter.
Then, we apply our approach made in section
4
to
giving a corresponding description of
dichotomy
for the deterministic difference equation (
1.2

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the concept of tempered exponential dichotomy,
define several classes of weighted-bounded sequences, and introduce the concept of measurable admissibility.

2.1.
Tempered exponential dichotomy

Throughout this paper,

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{-}
)
denotes the set of nonnegative (resp. nonpositive) integers,

\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of positive integers,

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
denotes a measure space,
X=(X,|\cdot|)
is a separable
Banach space,
and
\mathcal{L}(X)
denotes the Banach space of all bounded linear
operators on
X
.
Furthermore, let
\theta\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an invertible measurable transformation that preserves
\mathbb{P}
.
This means that
\mathbb{P}(\theta^{-1}(E))=\mathbb{P}(E)
for each
E\in\mathcal{F}
.
A map
\Phi\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\Omega\to\mathcal{L}(X)
is said to be a
(strongly) measurable linear cocycle

over
\theta
(
[
Ar
]
)
if

•

\Phi(0,\omega)=\text{\rm Id}
for
\omega\in\Omega
, where
Id
denotes the identity operator on
X
;

•

\Phi(n+m,\omega)=\Phi(m,\theta^{n}(\omega))\Phi(n,\omega)
for
\omega\in\Omega
and
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;

•

the map
\omega\mapsto\Phi(n,\omega)
is strongly measurable for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, i.e.
\omega\mapsto\Phi(n,\omega)x
is measurable for each
x\in X
.

A
linear random dynamical system
(
[
Ar
]
) on
X
over
\theta
is a measurable map

\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\Omega\times X\to X,\quad(n,\omega,x)\mapsto\phi(n,\omega,x),

such that the map
(n,\omega)\mapsto\phi(n,\omega,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}(X)

forms a linear cocycle over
\theta
.
The measurability of
\phi
implies that the induced linear cocycle is strongly measurable.
Conversely, it is clear that for a given measurable linear cocycle
\Phi
over
\theta
, the map

\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\Omega\times X\ni(n,\omega,x)\mapsto\Phi(n,\omega)x

defines a linear random dynamical system on
X
over
\theta
. Consequently,
from now on we will use “linear random dynamical system” and “measurable linear cocycle” synonymously.
Let
\Phi
be a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta
. Set

A(\omega):=\Phi(1,\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

Then

\omega\mapsto A(\omega)x
is measurable for each fixed
x\in X
.
We say that
A
is the
generator
of the cocycle
\Phi
.
A random variable

K\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
is said to be
tempered

with respect to
\theta
(
[
Ar
]
)
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{\ln(K(\theta^{n}\omega))}{n}=0,\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$.}

(2.1)

We now recall the concept of tempered exponential dichotomy (see
[
Ar
,
LL
]
).
Let
\Phi
be a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta
. We say that
\Phi
has a
tempered exponential dichotomy

if there exists a strongly measurable map
\Pi^{s}:\widetilde{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X)

defined on a
\theta
-invariant subset
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
of full measure such that:

(i)

\Pi^{s}(\omega)
is a projection on
X
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
;

(ii)

for all
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\Pi^{s}(\theta^{n}\omega)\Phi(n,\omega)=\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{s}(\omega);

(2.2)

(iii)

the restriction

\Phi(n,\omega)|_{\mathcal{R}(\Pi^{u}(\omega))}

to the range
\mathcal{R}(\Pi^{u}(\omega))
of
\Pi^{u}(\omega)
is
an isomorphism from
\mathcal{R}(\Pi^{u}(\omega))
onto

\mathcal{R}(\Pi^{u}(\theta^{n}\omega))

for each
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
and
\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{u}(\omega)
is strongly measurable on
\omega

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}
,
where
\Pi^{u}(\omega):=\text{\rm Id}-\Pi^{s}(\omega)
and

\Phi(n,\omega):=(\Phi(\theta^{n}\omega,-n)|_{\mathcal{R}(\Pi^{u}(\theta^{n}\omega))})^{-1}\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}$;}

(iv)

there is a
\theta
-invariant random variable
\alpha:\Omega\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
(i.e.,
\alpha(\theta\omega)=\alpha(\omega)
for
\omega\in\Omega
) and
a tempered (with respect to
\theta
) random variable

K:\Omega\rightarrow(0,+\infty)

such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{s}(\omega)\|\leq K(\omega)e^{-\alpha(\omega)n},\quad\forall~\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{u}(\omega)\|\leq K(\omega)e^{\alpha(\omega)n},\quad\ \forall~\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}.

(2.4)

For convenience, we call
\alpha\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
and
K\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)

the exponent and the bound of the tempered exponential dichotomy respectively.

2.2.
Some classes of weight-bounded sequences

We introduce some classes of weighted-bounded sequences, which are two-sided sequences in
X
.
Let
\beta,K\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
be random variables. For
\omega\in\Omega
, let

\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z}):=\bigg\{f=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X:\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(K(\theta^{n}\omega)e^{-\beta(\omega)n}|f(n)|\big)<+\infty\bigg\}.

Then,
\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
is a Banach space equipped with
the norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(K(\theta^{n}\omega)e^{-\beta(\omega)n}|f(n)|\big),\quad f\in\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z}).

Similarly, let

\ell^{\infty}_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z}):=\bigg\{f=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X:\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(K(\theta^{n}\omega)e^{-\beta(\omega)|n|}|f(n)|\big)<+\infty\bigg\},

which is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(K(\theta^{n}\omega)e^{-\beta(\omega)|n|}|f(n)|\big),\quad f\in\ell^{\infty}_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z}).

In the particular case
K\equiv 1
, we use
\ell^{\infty}_{1,\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\ell^{\infty}_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z})

to denote
\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})

and
\ell^{\infty}_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z})
respectively.
Furthermore, for a fixed
\theta
-invariant measurable set
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
,
we consider

\displaystyle\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}):=\{\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!f:\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow X:f(\omega,\cdot)\in\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z}),\forall\ \omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}\},

\displaystyle\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}):=\{\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!f:\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow X:f(\omega,\cdot)\in\ell^{\infty}_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}(\mathbb{Z}),\forall\ \omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}\}.

2.3.
Measurable admissibility

Let
\Phi
be a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta

with the generator
A

and
let
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
be a
\theta
-invariant measurable set. We say that the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is
properly admissible
for
\Phi
if
for each
f\in\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})

there exists a unique
x\in\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})

such that

\displaystyle x(\omega,n+1)=A(\theta^{n}\omega)x(\omega,n)+f(\omega,n+1),\quad\forall\ n\in\mathbb{Z},\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}.

(2.5)

By the definitions of
\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})
and
\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})
,
the proper admissibility of
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
for
\Phi
is equivalent to saying that
for every
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}

and every
sequence
f=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})

there exists a unique
x=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}_{1,\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

\displaystyle x(n+1)=A(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n)+f(n+1),\quad\forall\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.6)

In addition, we say that the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is
measurably properly admissible
for
\Phi
if
the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

is properly admissible and
for each
f\in\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})

measurable on
\omega
(i.e.,
\omega\mapsto f(\omega,n)
is measurable for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
)
the unique
x\in\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z})
given by the proper admissibility is also measurable on
\omega
.
In an analogous manner, one can introduce the notion of (measurable) proper admissibility of the pair
(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
.

3.
Tempered dichotomy implies measurably admissibility

In this section, we show that the tempered exponential dichotomy implies measurable proper admissibility of several pairs.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\Phi

is a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta
with the generator
A

and
has a tempered exponential dichotomy on
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega

with the exponent
\alpha\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)

and bound
K\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
.
Then for any
\theta
-invariant random variable
\beta\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

with the property
\beta(\omega)\in(-\alpha(\omega),\alpha(\omega))
for
\omega\in\Omega
,
the followings hold:

(i)

the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

is measurably properly admissible
for
\Phi
,
and additionally
there is a
\theta
-invariant positive random variable
\gamma\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
such that
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
f\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\|x\|_{\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\gamma(\omega)\|f\|_{\ell_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})},

(3.1)

where

x\in\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
is the (unique) solution of (
2.6
);

(ii)

the pair
(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

is measurably properly admissible for
\Phi
, and additionally
there is a
\theta
-invariant positive random variable
\widetilde{\gamma}\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
such that for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
f\in\ell_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\|x\|_{\ell_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\widetilde{\gamma}(\omega)\|f\|_{\ell_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})},

(3.2)

where
x\in\ell_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
is the (unique) solution of (
2.6
).

Proof.

Without any loss of generality, we may assume that (
2.1
)
holds for each
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
. Indeed, otherwise we can replace
\widetilde{\Omega}
with
\widetilde{\Omega}\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta^{n}(\Omega^{\prime})\big)
, where
\Omega^{\prime}
is a full measure set such that (
2.1
) holds for each
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
.

For
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

G(n,\omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{s}(\omega),&n\geq 0;\\
-\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{u}(\omega),&n<0,\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

where
\Pi^{s}(\omega)
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
is the projection associated to the tempered exponential dichotomy for
\Phi

and
\Pi^{u}(\omega):=\text{\rm Id}-\Pi^{s}(\omega)
.
For convenience, we also call
G(n,\omega)
the Green function
corresponding to the tempered exponential dichotomy as the one corresponding to the uniform exponential dichotomy for deterministic systems
in
[
DK
, p.167]
.

By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have

\|G(n,\omega)\|\leq K(\omega)e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n|},\ \forall\ (\omega,n)\in\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}.

(3.4)

For
f:=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have (using (
3.4
)) that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{\beta(\omega)k}\|f\|_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)n}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{-\beta(\omega)n}e^{\beta(\omega)k}\|f\|_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))(n-k)}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{+\infty}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))(k-n)}\bigg\}\|f\|_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)n}\bigg\{\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}+\frac{e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}\bigg\}\|f\|_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)},\end{split}

(3.5)

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We conclude that
x=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,
where

\displaystyle x(n):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(k),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.6)

We claim that
x
is the solution of (
2.6
).
Indeed,
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that

\begin{split}A(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n)&=A(\theta^{n}\omega)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&=A(\theta^{n}\omega)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}\Phi(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{s}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&\phantom{=}-A(\theta^{n}\omega)\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\Phi(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{u}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}\Phi(n+1-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{s}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&\phantom{=}-\Pi^{u}(\theta^{n+1}\omega)f(n+1)-\sum_{k=n+2}^{\infty}\Phi(n+1-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{u}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n+1}\Phi(n+1-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{s}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)-\Pi^{s}(\theta^{n+1}\omega)f(n+1)\\
&\phantom{=}-\Pi^{u}(\theta^{n+1}\omega)f(n+1)-\sum_{k=n+2}^{\infty}\Phi(n+1-k,\theta^{k}\omega)\Pi^{u}(\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\\
&=x(n+1)-f(n+1),\end{split}

which implies that

\displaystyle x(n+1)=A(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n)+f(n+1),\quad\forall\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence,
x
is the solution of (
2.6
). Moreover, observe that it follows from (
3.5
) that (
3.1
) holds with
the
\theta
-invariant positive random variable

\gamma(\omega):=\frac{1+e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-|\beta(\omega)|)}}.

In order to show the uniqueness, it is sufficient to prove that for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, the only
solution
x=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
of

x(n+1)=A(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.7)

in

\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
is the trivial solution

x=0
.
Let
x:=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a solution of (
3.7
) in
\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

x^{s}(n):=\Pi^{s}(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n)\quad\text{and}\quad x^{u}(n):=\Pi^{u}(\theta^{n}\omega)x(n).

Then, we have that

\displaystyle x^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi^{s}(\theta^{n}\omega)\Phi(n-m,\theta^{m}\omega)x(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(n-m,\theta^{m}\omega)\Pi^{s}(\theta^{m}\omega)x(m),\quad\forall\ n\geq m,

and similarly

\displaystyle x^{u}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(n-m,\theta^{m}\omega)\Pi^{u}(\theta^{m}\omega)x(m),\quad\forall\ n\leq m.

By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have that

\displaystyle|x^{s}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(\theta^{m}\omega)e^{-\alpha(\omega)(n-m)}|x(m)|~~\mbox{ for }m\leq n,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|x^{u}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(\theta^{m}\omega)e^{-\alpha(\omega)(m-n)}|x(m)|~\,\mbox{ for }m\geq n.

(3.9)

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows from (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle|x^{s}(n)|\leq K(\theta^{m}\omega)e^{-\alpha(\omega)n}e^{(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))m}\|x\|_{\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})},

(3.10)

for
m\leq n
.
Since

\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega)>0
, it follows from (
2.1
) that

\lim_{m\rightarrow-\infty}K(\theta^{m}\omega)e^{(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))m}=0.

Consequently, by letting
m\to-\infty
in (
3.8
) we conclude that
x^{s}(n)=0
.
Similarly, we obtain that
x^{u}(n)=0
and thus
x(n)=x^{s}(n)+x^{u}(n)=0
. Since
n\in\mathbb{Z}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
x=0
.
Therefore, the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is properly admissible.

In order to show that this pair is also measurably properly admissible,
let
f\colon\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}\to X

be a measurable map such that
(f(\omega,n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
.
From the preceding discussion, we have that the unique solution of (
2.5
) is given by

\displaystyle x(\omega,n):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(\omega,k),\quad(\omega,n)\in\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}.

Hence, since
\omega\mapsto f(\omega,n)
is measurable for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
the map

\omega\mapsto\sum_{k=-m}^{m}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(\omega,k)

is measurable for any fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Indeed, this follows directly from the
strong measurability of maps
\omega\mapsto\Phi(n,\omega)
,

\omega\mapsto\Pi^{s}(\omega)
and
\omega\mapsto\Phi(n,\omega)\Pi^{u}(\omega)
.
This implies that for arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\omega\mapsto x(\omega,n)
is measurable
since it is the pointwise limit of measurable maps

\omega\mapsto\sum_{k=-m}^{m}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(\omega,k)
. We conclude that the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is
measurably properly admissible.

We now prove that the pair

(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

is measurably properly admissible. Similarly to (
3.5
), in the case when
\beta(\omega)\geq 0
, for
f:=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
we have that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{\beta(\omega)|k|}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{-\beta(\omega)|n|}e^{\beta(\omega)|k|}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&\leq e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\bigg\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))(n-k)}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{+\infty}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))(k-n)}\bigg\}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\bigg\{\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}+\frac{e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}\bigg\}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|},\end{split}

for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where we used that

-\beta(\omega)|n|+\beta(\omega)|k|\leq\beta(\omega)|n-k|
.
Analogously, if
\beta(\omega)<0
we have that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(n-k,\theta^{k}\omega)f(k)\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{\beta(\omega)|k|}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(\omega)|n-k|}e^{-\beta(\omega)|n|}e^{\beta(\omega)|k|}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&\leq e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\bigg\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))(n-k)}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{+\infty}e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))(k-n)}\bigg\}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|}\\
&=e^{\beta(\omega)|n|}\bigg\{\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}+\frac{e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}\bigg\}\|f\|_{|1/K(\omega),\beta(\omega)|},\end{split}

for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
-\beta(\omega)|n|+\beta(\omega)|k|\leq-\beta(\omega)|n-k|
.
Hence, (
3.2
) holds with the
\theta
-invariant positive random variable

\widetilde{\gamma}(\omega):=\begin{cases}\frac{1+e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)-\beta(\omega))}}&\text{if $\beta(\omega)\geq 0$}\\
\frac{1+e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}{1-e^{-(\alpha(\omega)+\beta(\omega))}}&\text{if $\beta(\omega)<0$.}\end{cases}

For the uniqueness and measurability, one can argue in the same manner as
we did above for the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

\square

In next section,
we will conversely
use the three pairs in Theorem
1

to prove the existence of tempered exponential dichotomy
for measurable cocycles which may not satisfy the assumptions of MET.

4.
Converse without MET

The main result on the converse reads:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\Phi

is a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta

with the generator
A
. Furthermore,
assume that there exist a
\theta
-invariant measurable subset
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
of full measure,
a
\theta
-invariant random variable
\beta\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)

and a tempered (with respect to
\theta
) random variable
K\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
such that

(S1)

for
\lambda=\pm\beta
,
the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\lambda}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\lambda}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

is measurably properly admissible,
and additionally there is a
\theta
-invariant random variable
\gamma_{\lambda}\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
such that for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
f=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\|x\|_{\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(\omega)\|f\|_{\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})},

(4.1)

where
x=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the unique solution of (
2.6
) in

\ell^{\infty}_{1,\lambda(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
;

(S2)

the pair
(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is properly admissible.

Then,
\Phi
has a tempered exponential dichotomy.

Note that the theorem needs three admissible pairs

(\ell_{1/K,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
,

(\ell_{1/K,-\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,-\beta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))

and

(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
,
as mentioned in the Introduction.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The proof will be divided into several steps.
For any fixed
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
\lambda=\pm\beta
,
let

L_{\lambda,\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}(L_{\lambda,\omega})\subset\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
be the linear operator
defined by

\displaystyle(L_{\lambda,\omega}x)(n):=x(n)-A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)x(n-1),\quad\ n\in\mathbb{Z},

for each
x\in\mathcal{D}(L_{\lambda,\omega})
,
where
D(L_{\lambda,\omega})
denotes the domain of
L_{\lambda,\omega}
,
that is,

\mathcal{D}(L_{\lambda,\omega}):=\{x\in\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}):L_{\lambda,\omega}x\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

L_{\lambda,\omega}
is a closed linear operator.

Proof.

Let
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{D}(L_{\lambda,\omega})
such that sequences
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(L_{\lambda,\omega}x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converge in
\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
respectively. Let

x:=\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad y:=\lim_{k\to\infty}L_{\lambda,\omega}x_{k}.

Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\begin{split}x(n)-A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)x(n-1)&=\lim_{k\to\infty}(x_{k}(n)-A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)x_{k}(n-1))\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}(L_{\lambda,\omega}x_{k})(n)\\
&=y(n).\end{split}

Hence,
x\in\mathcal{D}(L_{\lambda,\omega})
and
L_{\lambda,\omega}x=y
. The proof is completed.
∎

Observe that the assumptions of the theorem imply that
L_{\lambda,\omega}
is invertible.
By Lemma
4.1
,
L_{\lambda,\omega}^{-1}\colon\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

is closed and thus bounded.

For any
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, we define a linear operator
G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)\colon X\to X
in the following way. For
z\in X
, let
\tilde{f}_{z,k}:=(\tilde{f}_{z,k}(m))_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence
given by

\tilde{f}_{z,k}(m):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z,&m=k,\\
0,&m\neq k.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

We now let

G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)z:=(L_{\lambda,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k})(n).

Observe that
G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)
is a bounded linear operator. Indeed, using (
4.1
), for any
z\in X
we have

\begin{split}e^{-\lambda(\omega)n}|G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)z|&=e^{-\lambda(\omega)n}|(L_{\lambda,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k})(n)|\\
&\leq\lVert L_{\lambda,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k}\rVert_{\ell_{1,\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\\
&\leq\lVert L_{\lambda,\omega}^{-1}\rVert\cdot\lVert\tilde{f}_{z,k}\rVert_{\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\\
&\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(\omega)K(\theta^{k}(\omega))e^{-\lambda(\omega)k}|z|,\end{split}

and thus

\lVert G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)\rVert\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(\omega)K(\theta^{k}(\omega))e^{-\lambda(\omega)k}e^{\lambda(\omega)n}.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.2
.

For each
(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, the map
\omega\mapsto G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)
is strongly measurable.

Proof.

Fix
(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
.
For any fixed
z\in X
, define
f\colon\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}\to X
as

f(\omega,m)=\begin{cases}z&\text{for $m=k$;}\\
0&\text{for $m\neq k$,}\end{cases}

for
(\omega,m)\in\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}
. As we have observed,
(f(\omega,m))_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{1/K(\omega),\lambda(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Since the pair
(\ell_{1/K,\lambda}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{1,\lambda}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is measurably properly admissible, there exists a unique measurable map
x\colon\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}\to X
such that

(x(\omega,m))_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}_{1,\lambda(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and

x(\omega,m+1)=A(\theta^{m}\omega)x(\omega,m)+f(\omega,m+1),\quad\text{for $m\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

It follows that

G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)z=x(\omega,n).

Since
\omega\mapsto x(\omega,n)
is measurable, we conclude that
\omega\mapsto G_{\lambda,\omega}(n,k)z
is also measurable and the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,

G_{\beta,\omega}(n,k)=G_{-\beta,\omega}(n,k)
.

Proof.

For any fixed
z\in X
, let
\tilde{f}_{z,k}:=(\tilde{f}_{z,k}(m))_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence given by (
4.2
). Let
x:=(x(m))_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
be given by

x(m):=(L_{\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k}-L_{-\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k})(m),\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then,

x(m)-A(\theta^{m-1}\omega)x(m-1)=0,\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(4.4)

Moreover, since
\ell_{1,\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

and
\ell_{1,-\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have that

x\in\ell_{|1,\beta(\omega)|}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.
From our assumption that the pair
(\ell_{|1/K,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}),\ell_{|1,\beta|}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}))
is properly admissible,
we conclude from (
4.4
) that
x=0
.
Consequently, we have that
x(n)=0
and thus

G_{\beta,\omega}(n,k)z=L_{\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k}(n)=L_{-\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{z,k}(n)=G_{-\beta,\omega}(n,k)z.

∎

For
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
n\geq m
, let

U_{\omega}(n,m):=\begin{cases}A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)\cdot\cdot\cdot A(\theta^{m}\omega)&\text{for $n>m$;}\\
\text{\rm Id}&\text{for $n=m$.}\end{cases}

Observe that
U_{\omega}(n,m)=\Phi(n-m,\theta^{m}\omega)
. In addition, for
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m):=\bigg\{v\in X:\sup_{n\geq m}|U_{\omega}(n,m)v|<+\infty\bigg\}.

Finally, let
\mathcal{U}_{\omega}(m)
consist of all
v\in X
for which there exists a sequence
(x(n))_{n\leq m}
such that
x(m)=v
,
\sup_{n\leq m}|x(n)|<+\infty
and

x(n)=A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)x(n-1),\quad\text{for $n\leq m$.}

Clearly,
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m)
and
\mathcal{U}_{\omega}(m)
are subspaces of
X
. We will write
G_{\omega}(n,k)
instead of
G_{\beta,\omega}(n,k)=G_{-\beta,\omega}(n,k)
. Furthermore, let

P_{\omega}^{s}(m):=G_{\omega}(m,m),\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
P_{\omega}^{s}(m)
is a projection on
X
such that

\mathcal{R}(P_{\omega}^{s}(m))=\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m)
and

\mathcal{N}(P_{\omega}^{s}(m))=\mathcal{U}_{\omega}(m)
.

Proof.

For any fixed
v\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m)
, define
x=(x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
by

x(n)=\begin{cases}U_{\omega}(n,m)v&\text{for $n\geq m$;}\\
0&\text{for $n<m$.}\end{cases}

Note that

x(n)=A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)x(n-1)+\tilde{f}_{v,m}(n),\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

In addition,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(|x(n)|e^{-\beta(\omega)n})=\sup_{n\geq m}(|x(n)|e^{-\beta(\omega)n})\leq e^{\beta(\omega)|m|}\sup_{n\geq m}|x(n)|<+\infty.

Hence,
x\in\ell^{\infty}_{1,\beta(\omega)}(\mathbb{Z})
. We conclude that
L_{\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{v,m}=x

and therefore

P_{\omega}^{s}(m)v=v
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m)\subset\mathcal{R}(P_{\omega}^{s}(m))
.

Now, choose an arbitrary
v\in X
and let
y:=L_{-\beta,\omega}^{-1}\tilde{f}_{v,m}
. Then,
y(n)=U_{\omega}(n,m)y(m)
for
n\geq m
. Moreover,

\sup_{n\geq m}|y(n)|\leq e^{\beta(\omega)|m|}\sup_{n\geq m}(|y(n)|e^{\beta(\omega)n})\leq e^{\beta(\omega)|m|}\|y\|_{\ell_{1,-\beta(\omega)}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}<+\infty.

Thus,
P_{\omega}^{s}(m)v=y(m)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(m)
. We conclude that
P_{\omega}^{s}(m)
is a projection onto
S_{\omega}(m)
.

In addition, if we assume

. We define

Lemma 4.5
.

For

For any fixed

(x(n))_{n\leq m}\subset X

Lemma 4.7

For

5.

Throughout this section, we continue to assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)
is an invertible
\mathbb{P}
-preserving dynamical system. In addition, we assume that

•

\Omega
is a compact metric space and
\mathcal{F}
is the associated Borel
\sigma
-algebra;

•

\mathbb{P}
is ergodic, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(E)\in\{0,1\}

In order to recall the version of MET established in
[

]
,
we introduce several concepts.
For a fixed

, the constant

\displaystyle\kappa_{ic}(A):=\inf\{r:A(B_{X}(0,1))~{\rm has~a~finite~cover~by~balls~of~radius~r}\}

is called the
index of compactness
of
A
, where
B_{X}(0,1)
denotes the unit ball in
X
centered at

.
Let
\Phi
be a measurable linear cocycle over
\theta
with the generator
A
such that

\int_{M}\ln^{+}\lVert A(\omega)\rVert\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)<\infty.

(5.1)

It follows essentially from the subadditive ergodic theorem (see Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5 of
[

]

that there exist

such that

\displaystyle\kappa^{*}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\kappa_{ic}(\Phi(n,\omega))}{n}~~~{\rm and}~~~\lambda^{*}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|\Phi(n,\omega)\|}{n}

\mathbb{P}
-a.e.

and

of
\Phi
(with respect to
\mathbb{P}
) respectively.
If

, we say that

\Phi
is

. Observe that when
\Phi
is compact, i.e. when

is a compact operator for each

, we have that

for each

and

. Consequently, a compact cocycle is quasi-compact if and only if

.

Then, we are ready to state the version of MET established in
[

]
.

]
)

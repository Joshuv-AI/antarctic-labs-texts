Palindromic concatenations of two distinct repdigits in Narayana’s cows sequence †
Mahadi Ddamulira, Paul Emong, Geoffrey Ismail Mirumbe

Abstract
Let ( N n ) n ≥ 0 (N_{n})_{n\geq 0} be Narayana’s cows sequence given by a recurrence relation N n + 3 = N n + 2 + N n N_{n+3}=N_{n+2}+N_{n} for all n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 , with initial conditions N 0 = 0 N_{0}=0 , and N 1 = N 2 = 1 N_{1}=N_{2}=1 . In this paper, we find all members in Narayana’s cow sequence that are palindromic concatenations of two distinct repdigits. Our proofs use techniques on Diophantine approximation which include the theory of linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers and Baker’s reduction method. We show that 595 595 is the only member in Narayana’s cow sequence that is a palindromic concatenation of two distinct repdigits in base 10 10 .



Keywords and phrases
: Narayana’s cow sequence; Repdigit; Linear forms in logarithms; Baker’s method.

Introduction.

Narayana’s cows sequence
(N_{n})_{n\geq 0}
was named after the Indian mathematician Narayana
[
2
]
, who proposed the problem of a herd of cows and calves. He described the sequence as the number of cows present each year, starting from one cow in the first year, where every cow has one calf every year starting in its fourth year.

Narayana’s cow sequence is a sequence of positive integers and its first few terms are

1,1,1,2,3,4,6,9,13,19,28,41,60,88,129,189,277,406,595,872,1278,1873,\ldots.

In this sequence, the number of cows in each year is equal to the number of cows in the previous year, plus the number of cows three years ago.
Translating this description into recurrence sequences, we have that in the
n^{\text{th}}
year, Narayana’s cows sequence problem is given by the following recurrence relation:

N_{n+3}=N_{n+2}+N_{n},

for all
n\geq 0
, with the initial conditions
N_{0}=0
, and
N_{1}=N_{2}=1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer. We say that a positive integer
R
is called a base
q-repdigit
if all its digits are equal in base
q
. If
q=10
, then we omit the base and simply say that
R
is a repdigit. That is,
R
is of the form

\displaystyle R=\overline{\underbrace{b\cdots b}_{\text{$k$ times}}}=b\left(\frac{10^{k}-1}{9}\right),

for some non negative integers
b
and
k
with
1\leq b\leq 9
and
k\geq 1
. Given
s\geq 1
, we say that
R
is a concatenation of
s
repdigits if
R
can be written in the form

\overline{\underbrace{b_{1}\cdots b_{1}}_{\text{$k_{1}$ times}}\underbrace{b_{2}\cdots b_{2}}_{\text{$k_{2}$ times}}\cdots\underbrace{b_{s}\cdots b_{s}}_{\text{$k_{s}$ times}}}.

Diophantine equations involving repdigits have been studied in several papers by different authors. For instance, the problem of writing repdigits as sum of three Fibonacci numbers has been studied by Luca
[
15
]
. Alvarado and Luca
[
3
]
solved the Diophantine equation which involved expressing Fibonacci numbers as sum of two repdigits. In
[
9
,
10
]
, Ddamulira studied the problem of searching for Padovan and tribonacci numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits, respectively. In
[
1
]
, the authors proved that the only Fibonacci numbers that are concatenations of two repdigits are
\{13,21,34,55,89,144,233,377\}
. Erduvan and Keskin, in their paper obtained all Lucas numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits, see
[
13
]
. The same authors determined Lucas numbers which are concatenations of three repdigits
[
14
]
. Other results on concatenation of repdigits appear in
[
5
,
6
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
. Furthermore, the authors in
[
11
]
showed that
\{13,19,28,41,60,88,277\}
are the only members of Narayana’s cow sequence that are concatenation of two repdigits.

In this paper, we study a related problem and find all members in Narayana’s cow sequence which are palindromic concatenations of two distinct repdigits. To be more specific, we solve the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle N_{n}=\overline{\underbrace{f_{1}\cdots f_{1}}_{\text{$u_{1}$ times}}\underbrace{f_{2}\cdots f_{2}}_{\text{$u_{2}$ times}}\underbrace{f_{1}\cdots f_{1}}_{\text{$u_{1}$ times}}},

(1)

with
f_{1}\neq f_{2}\in\{0,1,2,3,\ldots,9\},\ f_{1}>0
, and
u_{1},\ u_{2}\geq 1
.

Therefore, we have the following result:

Theorem 1
.

The only member in Narayana’s cows sequence which is a palindromic concatenation of two distinct repdigits is
595
.

\displaystyle a_{1}=\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3})},~a_{2}=\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3})},~~\text{and}~~a_{3}=\dfrac{\alpha_{3}}{(\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{1})(\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2})}=\overline{a_{2}}.

Numerically, we have the following:

\displaystyle 1.46<

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}<1.47,

\displaystyle 0.82<|\alpha_{2}|=

\displaystyle|\alpha_{3}|<0.83,

\displaystyle 0.41<

\displaystyle a_{1}<0.42,

\displaystyle 0.27<|a_{2}|=

\displaystyle|a_{3}|<0.28.

Considering the estimates for

\displaystyle r(n):=N_{n}-a_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}=a_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n}+a_{3}\alpha_{3}^{n},~\text{we have that}~|r(n)|<\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{n/2}}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 1.

Furthermore, the minimal polynomial of

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{n-2}\leq N_{n}\leq\alpha_{1}^{n-1},

(3)

2.2
Linear forms in logarithms.

Consider the algebraic number
\lambda
of degree
d
. Let
c>0
be the leading coefficient of its minimal polynomial over integers, and
\lambda=\lambda^{(1)},\cdots,\lambda^{(d)}
denote its conjugates.
The logarithmic height of
\lambda
is defined by

\displaystyle h(\lambda):=\dfrac{1}{d}\left(\log c+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\left(\max\{|\lambda^{(i)}|,1\}\right)\right).

If
\lambda=\dfrac{p}{q}
is a rational number with
\gcd(p,q)=1
, and
q>0
, then
h(\lambda)=\log\max\{|p|,q\}.

The logarithmic height has the following properties:

(i)

h(\lambda_{1}\pm\lambda_{2})\leq h(\lambda_{1})+h(\lambda_{2})+\log 2,

(ii)

h(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\pm})\leq h(\lambda_{1})+h(\lambda_{2}),

(iii)

h(\lambda^{v})=|v|h(\lambda),~\text{for}~v\in\mathbb{Z}.

The following is a result of Matveev
[
16
]
; also see Bugeaud, Mignotte, and Siksek (
[
8
]
, Theorem 9.4).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\lambda,\ldots,\lambda_{s}
be positive real algebraic numbers in a real algebraic number field
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{R}
of degree
d
. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}
be nonzero integers such that

\displaystyle\varGamma:=\lambda_{1}^{b_{1}}\ldots\lambda_{s}^{b_{s}}-1\neq 0.

Then

\displaystyle\log|\varGamma|>-1.4\times 30^{s+3}\times s^{4.5}\times d^{2}(1+\log d)(1+\log D)A_{1}\ldots A_{s},

where

\displaystyle D\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{s}|\},

and

\displaystyle A_{j}\geq\max\{dh(\lambda_{j}),|\log\lambda_{j}|,0.16\},\quad\text{for all}\quad j=1,\ldots,s.

We recall the following result by Guzmán and Luca that appears in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 1

Let
m\geq 1
and
T>0
be such that
T>(4m^{2})^{m}
and

. Then

\displaystyle x<2^{m}T(\log T)^{m}.

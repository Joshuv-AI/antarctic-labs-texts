Higher Jacobian matrix of weighted homogeneous polynomials and derivation algebras
Wágner Badilla-Céspedes, Abel Castorena, Daniel Duarte

Abstract
. We prove that the ideal generated by the maximal minors of the higher-order Jacobian matrix of a weighted homogeneous polynomial is also weighted homogeneous. As an application, we give a partial answer to a conjecture concerning the non-existence of negative weight derivations on the higher Nash blowup local algebra of a hypersurface.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14B05, 32S05, 13N15.

Introduction

The Jacobian matrix of order
n
is a higher-order version of the classical Jacobian matrix. It was introduced as a tool for computing the higher Nash blowup of a hypersurface
[
Dua17
]
. Higher-order Jacobian matrices were later rediscovered and further developed by several authors
[
BJNB19
,
BD20
,
CMDGF21
]
. Ever since, this matrix has seen a wide variety of applications: in the study of higher Nash blowups
[
Dua17
,
CMDGF21
]
, the study of invariants of rings in positive characteristic
[
BJNB19
]
, the study of homological properties of the module of higher Kähler differentials
[
BD20
,
dAD21
]
, relations with Hasse-Schmidt derivations and jet schemes
[
LDS
,
Bar23
]
, invariants of germs of hypersurface singularities
[
HMYZ23
,
LY23
]
, motivic zeta functions
[
LY23
]
. In addition to these applications, new questions has been raised regarding general properties of higher Jacobian matrices.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
, where
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
denotes the ring of convergent power series at the origin. Let
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
be the higher Jacobian matrix of
f
and
\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)
the ideal generated by the maximal minors of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
(see Definition
1.3
). N. Hussain, G. Ma, S. T. S. Yau, and H. Zuo defined the higher Nash blowup local algebra as the quotient
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)=\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}/\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle

[
HMYZ23
]
. For
n=1
, this is the classical Tjurina algebra. The authors proposed several conjectures regarding these algebras: invariance under contact equivalence, homogeneity properties, and bounds for their dimensions. They also provided some evidence that supports the conjectures. Very recently, the conjecture regarding the invariance under contact equivalence was proved by Q. T. Lê and T. Yasuda
[
LY23
]
. In this paper we are interested in exploring the questions regarding the homogeneity properties of the higher Jacobian matrix. The following is our first main theorem.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
1.6
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial. Then
\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)
is a weighted homogeneous ideal for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Another object introduced by N. Hussain, G. Ma, S. T. S. Yau, and H. Zuo is the algebra of derivations
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)=\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{T}_{n}(f))
. For
n=1
, this is known as the Yau algebra of
f
. A classical result in singularity theory states that the Yau algebra is a solvable Lie algebra for isolated hypersurface singularities
[
Yau86
,
Yau91
]
. Moreover, the Yau algebra has been used to distinguish complex analytic structures of isolated hypersurface singularities
[
SY90
]
.

By Theorem
A
,
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
is a graded algebra whenever
f
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial. In this case, the algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
is also graded. There is a general conjecture on algebras of derivations of local Artinian graded algebras, known as Halperin conjecture, stating the non-existence of negative weight derivations over those algebras
[
Che99
]
. This conjecture has been intensively studied
[
Mei82
,
FHT01
,
CYZ19
,
CCYZ20
,
CHYZ20
]
. Inspired by Halperin’s question, N. Hussain, G. Ma, S. T. S. Yau, and H. Zuo conjectured that there are no negative weight derivations on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
. They proved their conjecture in the case
n=s=2
. Our second main result shows that the conjecture is true for
n\geq 3
and
s=2
.

Theorem B

(see Theorem
2.6
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and degree
d
that defines an isolated hypersurface singularity. Suppose that
d\geq 2w_{1}\geq 2w_{2}>0
. Let
n\geq 3
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
is non-negatively graded.

This paper is divided as follows. In the first section we prove Theorem
A
. We also establish some facts regarding the weighted degree of the maximal minors of the higher Jacobian matrix. The second section contains the proof of Theorem
B
.

1.
Higher-order Jacobian matrix of a weighted homogeneous polynomial

The following notation will be constantly used throughout this paper.

Notation 1.1
.

Let
n,s\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. Given
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, denote
|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{s}
. For
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
we denote
\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}
the usual Euclidean inner product. In addition, consider

\displaystyle B:=\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{s}|0\leq|\beta|\leq n-1\},

\displaystyle B_{1}:=\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{s}|1\leq|\beta|\leq n-1\},

\displaystyle A:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}|0\leq|\alpha|\leq n\},

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}|1\leq|\alpha|\leq n\},

\displaystyle C_{n}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}||\alpha|=n\}.

Remark 1.2
.

It is known that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Card}(B)=\binom{s+n-1}{s}=:M,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Card}(A)=\binom{s+n}{s}=:N,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Card}(C_{n})=\binom{n+s-1}{s-1}=N-M=:l.

Definition 1.3

(
[
Dua17
,
BJNB19
,
BD20
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Denote

\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f):=\left(\frac{1}{(\alpha-\beta)!}\frac{\partial^{\alpha-\beta}(f)}{\partial x^{\alpha-\beta}}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in B\\
\alpha\in A_{1}\end{subarray}},

where we define
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{(\alpha-\beta)!}\frac{\partial^{\alpha-\beta}(f)}{\partial x^{\alpha-\beta}}=0},
whenever
\alpha_{i}<\beta_{i}
for some
i
. It is a
(M\times N-1)
-matrix. We order this matrix increasingly using a graded lexicographical order and taking
x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{s}
. Moreover, denote as
\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)
the ideal generated by all maximal minors of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
f=x^{3}-y^{2}\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
. Then

\operatorname{Jac}_{2}(f)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}3x^{2}&-2y&3x&0&-1\\
f&0&3x^{2}&-2y&0\\
0&f&0&3x^{2}&-2y\\
\end{array}\right).

N. Hussain, G. Ma, S. T. S. Yau, and H. Zuo proposed the following conjecture regarding homogeneity properties of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
.

Conjecture 1.5

(
[
HMYZ23
, Conjecture 1.7]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial. Then
\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)
is a weighted homogeneous ideal.

The aforementioned authors verified Conjecture
1.5
in the case
s=n=2

[
HMYZ23
, Lemma 4.1]
. The following theorem gives a positive answer to Conjecture
1.5
for arbitrary
n
and
s
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
f
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
of weight
w\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and degree
d
. Then
\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)
is a weighted homogeneous ideal for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a matrix formed by taking
M
columns of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
. Let
B=\{\beta(1),\ldots,\beta(M)\}
and
\{\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(M)\}\subset A_{1}
be the sets of vectors indexing the rows and columns of
L
, respectively. Denote the entries of
L
as
L_{\beta(i)\alpha(j)}
with
1\leq i,j\leq M
. Recall that

L_{\beta(i)\alpha(j)}=\frac{1}{(\alpha(j)-\beta(i))!}\frac{\partial^{\alpha(j)-\beta(i)}(f)}{\partial x^{\alpha(j)-\beta(i)}}.

Set
I=\left\{\theta\in S_{M}\;|\;L_{\beta(i)\alpha(\theta(i))}\not=0\;\mathrm{for\,every}\;1\leq i\leq M\right\}
, where
S_{M}
denotes the symmetric group of
M
elements. We have that

\det(L)=\sum_{\theta\in I}\operatorname{sgn}(\theta)L_{\beta(1)\alpha(\theta(1))}\cdots L_{\beta(M)\alpha(\theta(M))}.

Notice that
f
being weighted homogeneous of degree
d
implies that
L_{\beta(i)\alpha(\theta(i))}
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of degree
d-(\alpha(\theta(i))-\beta(i))\cdot w
. Hence,
L_{\beta(1)\alpha(\theta(1))}\cdots L_{\beta(M)\alpha(\theta(M))}
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of degree
dM-\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(\theta(i))-\beta(i))\cdot w
.

We claim that
\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(\sigma(i))-\beta(i))\cdot w=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w
for every
\sigma\in I
. Indeed,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(\sigma(i))-\alpha(i))\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(\sigma(i))-\beta(i)+\beta(i)-\alpha(i))\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(\sigma(i))-\beta(i))\cdot w-\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w.

Taking
c=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w
, we conclude
L_{\beta(1)\alpha(\theta(1))}\cdots L_{\beta(M)\alpha(\theta(M))}
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
w
and degree
dM-c
for every
\theta\in I
. Therefore,
\det(L)
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
w
and degree
dM-c
.
∎

Notice that the proof of the previous theorem also exhibited the degree of the maximal minors of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
. Our next goal is to give lower bounds for those degrees, in some special cases.

1.1.
Homogeneous case

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
of degree
d
, and let
n\geq 2
,
s\geq 2
. Let
g
be the determinant of the submatrix of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
formed by the columns indexed by
\{\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(M)\}\subset A_{1}
and the rows indexed by
B=\{\beta(1),\ldots,\beta(M)\}
. From the proof of Theorem
1.6
,
g
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
dM-c
, where

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\alpha(i)-\beta(i)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\alpha(i)|-\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\beta(i)|.

Lemma 1.7
.

With the previous notation we have
sM\geq c+s
.

Proof.

We can assume that
1\leq|\alpha(1)|\leq\cdots\leq|\alpha(M)|\leq n
. Recall the notation from Remark
1.2
and denote
\mathcal{A}:=\{\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(M-l)\}
. Notice that
\mathcal{A}\subset B_{1}
, thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\alpha(i)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|+\sum_{\alpha\in C_{n}}|\alpha|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|+ln

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|+sM.

On the other hand,
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})=(M-1)-(M-l)=l-1\geq s
. This implies

\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\beta(i)|=\sum_{\beta\in B_{1}}|\beta|\geq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|+s.

From the above,

\displaystyle c+s

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\alpha(i)|-\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\beta(i)|+s

\displaystyle\leq sM+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|-\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\beta(i)|+s

\displaystyle\leq sM.

∎

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq s
and let
n\geq 2
. Then
d
is a lower bound for the degrees of the maximal minors of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
. In other words,
d\geq s
implies
dM-c\geq d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.7
,
d(M-1)-c\geq s(M-1)-c\geq 0
.
∎

1.2.
Weighted homogeneous case

In this subsection we work with weighted homogeneous polynomials assuming
s=2
and
n\geq 3
. Recall Notation
1.1
. In this case we have
C_{n}=\{(n-i,i)\;|\;0\leq i\leq n\}
. Notice that
\operatorname{Card}(C_{n})=n+1
and
M=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
.

As in the previous subsection, we want to give a lower bound for the weighted degrees of the maximal minors of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
. Because we are working with an arbitrary weight, the strategy for finding such bound is not as straightforward as the ones of Lemma
1.7
and Corollary
1.8
.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. Then
\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in C_{n}}\alpha\cdot w=|w|M
.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in C_{n}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(n-i,i)\cdot(w_{1},w_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(n-i)w_{1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}iw_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}w_{1}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle=|w|M.

∎

Before going any further, we present an example that illustrates the notation and the key idea we develop to find the desired bound.

Example 1.10
.

Let
n=3
. Thus
M=6
. In this case,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\},

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\},

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(0,2),(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3)\},

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3)\}.

Consider the submatrix of
\operatorname{Jac}_{3}(f)
defined by the columns

\mathfrak{A}=\{(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(3,0),(1,2)\}.

Let us show how we bound the sums
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}}\alpha\cdot w
and
\sum_{\beta\in B}\beta\cdot w
.

Let
C=\mathfrak{A}\setminus C_{3}=\{(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1)\}
. Let
\theta=(1,0)
and
\tau=(2,1)
. Notice that
\theta\in C
,
\tau\in C_{3}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
, and
\theta\cdot w<\tau\cdot w
. This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in C}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{3}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w+\theta\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{3}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle<\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w+\tau\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{3}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle<\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in C_{3}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w+|w|M.

On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in B}\beta\cdot w=\sum_{\beta\in B_{1}}\beta\cdot w

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in B_{1}\\
\beta\neq(1,0),(0,2)\end{subarray}}\beta\cdot w\Big)+(1,0)\cdot w+(0,2)\cdot w

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w\Big)+(1,0)\cdot w+(0,2)\cdot w

\displaystyle>\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\{\theta\}}\alpha\cdot w+w_{1}+w_{2}.

The following lemma generalizes the process of the previous example.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. Assume
w_{1}\geq w_{2}>0
. Let
\mathfrak{A}=\{\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(M)\}\subset A_{1}
,
B=\{\beta(1),\ldots,\beta(M)\}
,
and
C=\mathfrak{A}\setminus C_{n}
. Define subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset C
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{A}=C
,
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
and there exists
\theta\in B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A}
such that
\theta_{1}\geq 1
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta\}
, where
\theta\in C
is such that
\theta_{1}\geq 1
,
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
, and there exists
\tau\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
such that
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
.

(3)

\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta,\theta^{\prime}\}
, where
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in C
are such that
\theta_{1}\geq 1
and there exist
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
such that
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
and
\theta^{\prime}\cdot w\leq\tau^{\prime}\cdot w
.

Then
|w|M\geq c+|w|
, where
c=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w
.

Proof.

In each of the cases we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha(i)\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in C}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{n}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in C\setminus\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{n}}\alpha\cdot w

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w+\sum_{\alpha\in C_{n}}\alpha\cdot w=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w+|w|M,

where the last equality follows by Lemma
1.9
.

On the other hand, assuming that
\beta(1)=(0,0)
, since
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
we also have:

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta(i)\cdot w=\sum_{i=2}^{M}\beta(i)\cdot w\geq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w+(w_{1}+w_{2}).

Finally,

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha(i)\cdot w-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta(i)\cdot w

\displaystyle\leq|w|M+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta(i)\cdot w

\displaystyle\leq|w|M+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha\cdot w-(w_{1}+w_{2})=|w|M-|w|.

∎

Remark 1.12
.

Let
f
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2}]
of weight
w\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and degree
d
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
w_{1}\geq w_{2}>0
. Let
n\geq 3
and

g
be the determinant of the submatrix of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
formed by the columns indexed by
\mathfrak{A}=\{\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(M)\}\subset A_{1}
and the rows indexed by
B=\{\beta(1),\ldots,\beta(M)\}
. From Theorem
1.6
,
g
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
w
and degree
dM-c
, where

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha(i)\cdot w-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta(i)\cdot w.

We take
C=\mathfrak{A}\setminus C_{n}\subset B_{1}
. We note that
\mathfrak{A}\cap C_{n}\not=\emptyset
since
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1})=M-1
. Hence
M-(n+1)\leq\operatorname{Card}(C)\leq M-1
. Furthermore, denote
I_{j}=\{(0,1),(0,2),\ldots,(0,j)\}
for
1\leq j\leq n
, and
C_{i}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{2}||\beta|=i\}
, for
0\leq i\leq n
.

Proposition 1.13
.

Consider the assumptions of Remark
1.12
. If
\operatorname{Card}(C)=M-n
, then the following cases hold.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{A}=(B_{1}\cup C_{n})\setminus I_{n}
, then
c=w_{1}M
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{A}\not=(B_{1}\cup C_{n})\setminus I_{n}
, then
|w|M\geq c+|w|
.

Proof.

In order to show (1), we first notice

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\beta\in C_{i}}\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{i(i+1)}{2},\frac{i(i+1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(i-1)i}{2},\frac{(i-1)i}{2}\right).

Besides, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in C_{i}\\
\alpha\not=(0,i)\end{subarray}}\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{i(i+1)}{2},\frac{i(i-1)}{2}\right).

Therefore,
c=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\alpha(i)-\beta(i))\cdot w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i,0)\cdot w=w_{1}M
.

For (2) we consider two cases:

•

C\not=B_{1}\setminus I_{n-1}
. In this case, there exists
\theta\in B_{1}\setminus C
such that
\theta_{1}\geq 1
. Set
\mathcal{A}=C
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. Then
|w|M\geq c+|w|
by Lemma
1.11
(1).

•

C=B_{1}\setminus I_{n-1}
. In this case
\theta=(1,0)\in C
, and
C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
has a unique element
\tau\not=(0,n)
. Then,
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
. Set
\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta\}
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. Then
|w|M\geq c+|w|
by Lemma
1.11
(2).

∎

Proposition 1.14
.

Consider the assumptions of Remark
1.12
. If
\operatorname{Card}(C)\not=M-n
, then
|w|M\geq c+|w|
.

Proof.

We proceed by analyzing all possible cases we can have. Assume
B_{1}=B\setminus\{\beta(1)\}
.

(1)

\text{Card}(C)=M-1
. In this case we have
C=B_{1}
. We have that
\theta=(1,0),\theta^{\prime}=(0,1)\in C
. Since
\operatorname{Card}(C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A})=n\geq 3
, there exist two vectors
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
,

\tau\neq\tau^{\prime}
such that
\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2}\geq 1
, so that
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
and
\theta^{\prime}\cdot w\leq\tau^{\prime}\cdot w
. In this case we take
\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta,\theta^{\prime}\}
and the result follows by Lemma
1.11
(3).

(2)

\text{Card}(C)=M-2
. In this case
C=\mathfrak{A}\setminus C_{n}=B_{1}\setminus\{\beta(j)\}
for some
j\geq 2
. We have two subcases:

(a)

\theta=(1,0)\in C
. In this case, since
\operatorname{Card}(C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A})=n-1\geq 2
, there exists
\tau\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
, such that
\tau_{1}\geq 1
and
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
. We can take
\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta\}
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. The result follows by Lemma
1.11
(2).

(b)

(1,0)\not\in C
. We have that
\{(2,0),(1,1)\}\subset C
. As
\operatorname{Card}(C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A})=n-1\geq 2
, there exists
\tau\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
such that
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 1
or
\tau=(n,0)
. If
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 1
let
\theta=(1,1)
. If
\tau=(n,0)
let
\theta=(2,0)
. In any of the cases
\theta\in C
and
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
. We can take
\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta\}
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. The result follows by Lemma
1.11
(2).

(3)

\text{Card}(C)=M-j
for
3\leq j\leq n-1
. We have two subcases:

(a)

C=B_{1}\setminus\{(0,b_{1}),...,(0,b_{j-1})\}
. Then
\theta=(1,0)\in C
and
\operatorname{Card}(C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A})=n+1-j\geq 2
. Then there exists
\tau\in C_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{A}
such that
\theta\cdot w\leq\tau\cdot w
. We can take
\mathcal{A}=C\setminus\{\theta\}
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. The result follows by Lemma
1.11
(2).

(b)

C=B_{1}\setminus\{(a_{1},b_{1}),...,(a_{j-1},b_{j-1})\}
with at least one subindex
i
,
1\leq i\leq j-1
such that
\theta=(a_{i},b_{i})
satisfies that
a_{i}\geq 1
. In this case,
\theta\in B_{1}\setminus C
. We can take
\mathcal{A}=C
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. The result follows by Lemma
1.11
(1).

(4)

\operatorname{Card}(C)=M-(n+1)
. In this situation there exists
\theta\in B_{1}\setminus C
with
\theta_{1}\geq 1
. In this case we can take
\mathcal{A}=C
and so
\operatorname{Card}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{A})\geq 2
. The result follows by Lemma
1.11
(1).

∎

Corollary 1.15
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
w\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and degree
d
. Suppose that
d\geq 2w_{1}\geq 2w_{2}>0
. Let
n\geq 3
and
g
be a maximal minor of
\operatorname{Jac}_{n}(f)
. Then
g
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of degree greater or equal than
d
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.6
,
g
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of degree
dM-c
. Propositions
1.13
and
1.14
imply
d(M-1)\geq 2w_{1}(M-1)\geq c.

∎

2.
Derivations of the higher Nash blowup local algebra

In this section we study the Lie algebra of derivations of the higher Nash blowup local algebra of a hypersurface defining an isolated singularity.

Definition 2.1
.

[
HMYZ23
, Definition 1.3]
.
Let
f\in\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Denote

\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)=\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}/\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle.

\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
is called the higher Nash blowup local algebra of
f
. For
n=1
this is also known as the Tjurina algebra of
f
. Moreover, denote as
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)=\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{T}_{n}(f))
, i.e., the Lie algebra of derivations of
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The higher Nash blowup local algebra is denoted as
\mathcal{M}_{n}
in
[
HMYZ23
]
. In this paper we change the notation in order to be consistent with the usual notation for the Tjurina algebra. In addition, it is also customary to use
\mathcal{M}_{1}
(or, more precisely,
\mathcal{M}
) for the Milnor algebra.

Let
f
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial defining an isolated hypersurface singularity. Theorem
1.6
implies that
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
is graded. There is an induced grading on
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
, as we explain next.

Remark 2.3
.

Suppose that the hypersurface defined by
f
has an isolated singularity. Then
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
is Artinian. This follows from the fact that the zero locus of
\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle
coincides with the singular locus of the hypersurface
[
Dua17
, Corollary 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
XY96
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t}A_{i}
be a graded commutative Artinian local algebra. Let
L(A)
be the derivation algebra of
A
. Then
L(A)
can be graded as follows:
L(A)=\bigoplus_{k=-t}^{t}L_{k}
, where
L_{k}=\{D\in L(A)|D(A_{i})\subset A_{i+k}\mbox{ for all }i\}
.

N. Hussain, G. Ma, S. T. S. Yau, and H. Zuo proposed the following conjecture regarding the non-existence of negative weight derivations on
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
.

Conjecture 2.5

(
[
HMYZ23
, Conjecture 1.7]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
w\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and degree
d
that defines an isolated hypersurface singularity. Suppose that
d\geq 2w_{1}\geq\cdots\geq 2w_{s}>0
. Then there is no non-zero negative weight derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
is non-negatively graded.

The same authors verified Conjecture
2.5
in the case
s=n=2

[
HMYZ23
, Theorem B]
. The following theorem gives a positive answer to Conjecture
2.5
for
s=2
and
n\geq 3
. Together, these results settle the conjecture for two variables.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2}]
be a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weight
(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and degree
d
that defines an isolated hypersurface singularity. Suppose that
d\geq 2w_{1}\geq 2w_{2}>0
. Let
n\geq 3
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
is non-negatively graded.

Proof.

Let
\overline{D}
be a derivation of negative degree
-k
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
. Then
\overline{D}
corresponds to a homogeneous derivation
D
of
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
such that
D(\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle)\subset\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle

[
YZ16a
, Theorem 2.2]
. Since
D
is homogeneous, there are weighted homogeneous polynomials
h_{1},h_{2}
of degree
d_{i}=-k+w_{i}
,
i=1,2
, such that
D=h_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+h_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}
. It is known that
D(\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle)\subset\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle

[
XY96
, Lemma 2.5]
. This fact, together with
D
being of negative degree and
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
, implies that
h_{2}=0
and
h_{1}=cx_{2}^{b}
, for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
b\geq 1
. Hence,
D=cx_{2}^{b}\partial_{x_{1}}
.

By the assumptions on the weight and degree of
f
, and the fact that
f
defines an isolated singularity, we have that
f
must be one of the following cases

[
CXY95
, Lemma 2.1]
:

(1)

f=x_{1}^{m}+a_{1}(x_{2})x_{1}^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{m}(x_{2})
.

(2)

f=x_{1}^{m}x_{2}+a_{1}(x_{2})x_{1}^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{m}(x_{2})
.

By Corollary
1.15
,
f
is a generator of smallest degree among the generators of
\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle
. Since
D(f)\in\langle f,\mathcal{J}_{n}(f)\rangle
and
D
is of negative degree, it follows that
D(f)=0
, i.e.,
cx_{2}^{b}\partial_{x_{1}}(f)=0
. Using (1) and (2) we obtain
\partial_{x_{1}}(f)\neq 0
. We conclude that
c=0
and so
D=0
.
∎

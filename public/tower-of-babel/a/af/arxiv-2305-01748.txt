A note on asymmetric hypergraphs
Dominik Bohnert, Christian Winter

Abstract
A k k -graph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is asymmetric if there does not exist an automorphism on 𝒢 \mathcal{G} other than the identity, and 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is called minimal asymmetric if it is asymmetric but every non-trivial induced sub-hypergraph of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is non-asymmetric. Extending a result of Jiang and Nešetřil [ 8 ] , we show that for every k k -graph, k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , there exist infinitely many minimal asymmetric k k -graphs which have maximum degree 2 2 and are linear. Further, we show that there are infinitely many 2 2 -regular asymmetric k k -graphs for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 .



1
Introduction

For
k\geq 2
, a
k
-uniform hypergraph
, or
k
-graph
, is a pair
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}),\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}))
such that the edge set
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
consists of
k
-element subsets of the vertex set
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
. Note that
2
-graphs are commonly known as graphs.
An
automorphism
on a
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is a bijection
\phi\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
such that for every
E\in\mathcal{E}
,
\{\phi(v):~v\in E\}\in\mathcal{E}
.
An automorphism which is not the identity is called
non-trivial
.
We say that a
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}
is
symmetric
if there exists a non-trivial automorphism on
\mathcal{G}
and
asymmetric
otherwise.

\mathcal{G}
is
minimal asymmetric
if it is asymmetric and every induced sub-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
with
2\leq|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H})|<|\mathcal{V}(G)|
is symmetric.

Asymmetry of graphs was first considered by Frucht
[
6
]
in 1949. It was famously observed by Erdős and Rényi
[
5
]
that almost all graphs are asymmetric.
In 1988, Nešetřil conjectured that the number of minimal asymmetric graphs is finite, see
[
1
]
.
After several partial results
[
10
,
12
,
11
]
, this conjecture was recently confirmed by Schweitzer and Schweitzer
[
13
]
who showed that there are exactly
18
minimal asymmetric graphs.
In the hypergraph setting, Ellingham and Schroeder
[
4
]
studied a related variant of asymmetry. The notion of asymmetry used in this paper was introduced by Jiang and Nešetřil
[
8
]
, who showed that the natural generalization of Nešetřil’s conjecture to
k
-graphs does not hold.

Theorem 1

(Jiang, Nešetřil
[
8
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
be a positive integer. Then there exist infinitely many minimal asymmetric
k
-graphs.

They provide an explicit construction in their proof where each
k
-graph has maximum degree
3
.
In this paper, we extend their result by requiring even stricter structural properties.
In a
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
for any two distinct vertices
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
the
codegree
of
u
and
v
is the number of edges in
\mathcal{E}
which contain both
u
and
v
. The
maximum codegree
of
\mathcal{G}
is the maximum over the codegrees of all vertex pairs
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
,
u\neq v
.
A
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}
with maximum codegree
1
is called
linear
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is linear if any two edges
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
intersect in at most one vertex. Here we prove the following strengthening of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\geq 3
be a positive integer. There exist infinitely many minimal asymmetric
k
-graphs which have maximum degree
2
and maximum codegree
1
.

Note that every
k
-graph with maximum degree
1
or maximum codegree
0
is symmetric, so our result is best possible in this sense.
In our construction for Theorem
2
, most vertices have degree
2
, but crucially some vertices have degree
1
.
This raises the question whether there exist (minimal) asymmetric
k
-graphs where every vertex has the same degree.
We say that a
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}
is
r
-regular
if every vertex has degree
r
.

Theorem 3
.

There are infinitely many
2
-regular, asymmetric
k
-graphs for every
k\geq 3
.

It remains open if this result extends to minimal asymmetric
k
-graphs. We raise the following unanswered question.

Question 4
.

For
k\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
, is there an
r
-regular, minimal asymmetric
k
-graph?

Note that this question can be answered negatively for
k=2
and arbitrary
r
: None of the
18
minimal asymmetric graphs characterized by Schweitzer and Schweitzer
[
13
]
is regular.

In this paper we use standard graph theoretic notions, for formal definitions we refer the reader to Diestel
[
3
]
.
We denote by
[n]
the set of the first
n
integers
\{1,\dots,n\}
. For consistency, let
[0]=\varnothing
.
Given a function
\phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
and a subset
W\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, we denote image of
W
by
\phi(W):=\{\phi(v):~v\in W\}
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2.1
we present the constructions needed for the proof of Theorem
2
, in Sections
2.2
and
2.3
we show some properties of these constructions.
Subsequently, in Section
2.4
we prove Theorem
2
and in Section
3
we give a proof of Theorem
3
.

2.2
Properties of
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}

First we state slight reformulations of two properties shown by Jiang and Nešetřil
[
8
]
.

Lemma 8

(Jiang, Nešetřil
[
8
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be an induced sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
on at least two vertices.

(i)

There is a non-trivial automorphism on
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, i.e.
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is symmetric.

(ii)

If
E_{1}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
and
|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})|<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}_{k,t})|
, then there is a non-trivial automorphism
\phi
on
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
such that
\phi(E_{1})=E_{1}
and
\phi(u_{1})=u_{1}
.

A stronger version of Lemma
8
(i) is given in Lemma 4(2) of
[
8
]
, where
weak
sub-hypergraphs are considered.
Lemma
8
(ii) follows from the proof of Lemma 3(3) of
[
8
]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
. Let
\phi
be an automorphism on
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
.

(i)

Then
\{\phi(u_{i}):~i\in[tk]\}=\{u_{i}:~i\in[tk]\}
. Furthermore,
\phi(E)\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
for every
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
and
\{\phi(v_{i}):~i\in[tk]\}=\{v_{i}:~i\in[tk]\}
.

(ii)

There is a
j\in[tk]
such that either
\phi(E_{i})=E_{i+j-1}
for every
i\in[tk]
or
\phi(E_{i})=E_{j-i+1}
for every
i\in[tk]
, where the indexes are considered modulo
tk
.

We remark that the statement of Lemma
9
(i) is given implicitly in
[
8
]
.
Statements similar to Lemma
9
(ii) appear as Lemma 3(1) in
[
8
]
and as Lemma 9(1) in
[
9
]
.
Unfortunately, both versions only cover one of the two characterizations of
\phi
accurately.

Proof of Lemma
9
.

Note that the
u_{i}
’s are exactly the vertices of degree
1
in
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
. Observe that, since
\phi
is an automorphism,
v
and
\phi(v)
have the same degree for every vertex
v
, thus
\phi(\{u_{i}:~i\in[tk]\})=\{u_{i}:~i\in[tk]\}
. This implies (i).

A consequence of (i) is that
\phi(E_{1})=E_{j}
for some
j\in[tk]
, so
\{\phi(v_{1}),\phi(v_{2})\}=\{v_{j},v_{j+1}\}
.
If
\phi(v_{1})=v_{j}
and
\phi(v_{2})=v_{j+1}
, then
\phi(E_{2})=E_{j+1}
and thus
\phi(v_{3})=v_{j+2}
.
Iteratively, we find that
\phi(E_{i})=E_{i+j-1}
for every
i\in[tk]
.
Now suppose that
\phi(v_{1})=v_{j+1}
and
\phi(v_{2})=v_{j}
. Then
\phi(E_{2})=E_{j-1}
and iteratively
\phi(E_{i})=E_{j-i+1}
for every
i\in[tk]
.
This completes the proof of (ii).
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
k=4
or
k\geq 6
and
t\geq 2
. Let
\phi
be an automorphism on
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
.
If
\phi(E_{1})=E_{1}
, then
\phi
is the identity.

A result closely related to Lemma
10
is given in Lemma 9(2) of
[
9
]
without a proof.
Note that Lemma 3(2) of
[
8
]
almost corresponds to our Lemma
10
, but it does not hold for

and

, see for example the non-trivial automorphism illustrated in Figure

Proof of Lemma
10
.

Assume that
\phi
is not the identity. Then Lemma
9
provides that
\phi(E_{i})=E_{2-i}
for every
i\in[tk]
.

If
k=4
, then Lemma
9
(i) implies that
\{\phi(w_{i,1}):~i\in[tk]\}=\{w_{i,1}:~i\in[tk]\}
.
Since
\phi(E_{1})=E_{1}
and
\phi(E_{2})=E_{tk}
, we find that
\phi(w_{1,1})=w_{1,1}
and
\phi(w_{2,1})=w_{tk,1}
, respectively.
Observe that the edge
E_{1,1}
contains vertices
w_{1,1}
and
w_{2,1}
.
However, there is no edge in
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
containing
\phi(w_{1,1})=w_{1,1}
and
\phi(w_{2,1})=w_{tk,1}

Now suppose that
k\geq 6
. Then consider the edge
E_{3,3}=\{w_{3,3},\dots,w_{k+2,3}\}
. It intersects each of the edges
E_{3},\dots,E_{k+2}
.
Because
\phi
is an automorphism,
\phi(E_{3,3})
has a non-empty intersection with each of the edges
\{\phi(E_{3}),\dots,\phi(E_{2+k})\}=\{E_{(t-1)k},\dots,E_{tk-1}\}
.
However, it is easy to see from our construction that in
\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
such an edge does not exist.

2.3
Connectivity

Next we introduce a notion of
connectivity
between two vertices of a
k
-graph.
In a
k
-graph
\mathcal{G}
, a
v_{1}-v_{r+1}
-path
is an alternating sequence
(v_{1},E_{1},v_{2},E_{2},\dots,E_{r},v_{r+1})
of
r+1
distinct vertices
v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
and
r
distinct edges
E_{i}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
such that both
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are contained in
E_{i}
for any
i\in[r]
.
Such paths are commonly known as
Berge paths
.
Two
u-v
-paths are
edge-disjoint
if the underlying edge sets of the paths are disjoint.
We say that
u
and
v
are
t
-connected
if there are
t
pairwise edge-disjoint
u-v
-paths.
It is a simple observation that an automorphism leaves the connectivity invariant:

Proposition 11
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
k
-graph and
u,v\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
. Let
\phi
be an automorphism on
\mathcal{G}
. Then
u
and
v
are
t
-connected if and only if
\phi(u)
and
\phi(v)
are
t
-connected.

Lemma 12
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
and consider
\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}_{k,t}
.
Let
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
and let
u,v\in E
be distinct vertices of degree
2
, i.e.
u,v\notin\{u_{1},\dots,u_{tk}\}
.
Then
u
and
v
are
2
-connected.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, the
k
-graph obtained from
\mathcal{G}
by deleting the edge
E
. We shall show that
u
and
v
are
1
-connected in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
Recall that the edges set
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{E}_{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{cyc}
consists of L-edges
E_{i}
and cyclic edges
E_{i,j}
.

If
E\in\mathcal{E}_{cyc}
, then
u
and
v
are contained in distinct L-edges of
\mathcal{G}
, say without loss of generality
u\in E_{1}
and
v\in E_{j}
.
Then
(u,E_{1},v_{2},E_{2},v_{3},\dots,E_{j},v)
is a
u-v
-path in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

If
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
, let
i
such that
E=E_{i}
. Note that the L-edges of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
form a
v_{i+1}-v_{i}
-path containing all
v_{j}
,
j\in[tk]
.
Therefore, if there is a
u-v_{j}
-path and a
v-v_{j^{\prime}}
-path in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
for any
j,j^{\prime}\in[tk]
, then we also find a
u-v
-path in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
If
u\in\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
, there is a trivial
u-v_{i}
-path. Otherwise,
u\in E^{\prime}
for some cyclic edge
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{cyc}
.
Let
w
be an arbitrary vertex in
E^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}
. Then
w
is also contained in some L-edge
E_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
where
j\in[tk]
.
Then
(u,E^{\prime},w,E_{j},v_{j})
is a
u-v_{j}
-path. Similarly, we find a
v-v_{j^{\prime}}
-path, which completes the proof.
∎

2.4
Proof of the main result

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For the first part of the proof, let
k=4
or
k\geq 6
. Let
t_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for
i\in[k-1]
such that
2\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{k-1}
.
We shall show that
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1})
is minimal asymmetric.
In order to verify that
\mathcal{H}
is asymmetric, let
\phi
be an arbitrary automorphism on
\mathcal{H}
.
Recall that
E_{0}
is an edge of
\mathcal{H}
which connects otherwise disjoint copies of
\mathcal{G}_{k,t_{i}}
,
i\in[k-1]
.

First, we show that
\phi(E_{0})=E_{0}
.
We know that
\phi(E_{0})\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
, so assume that
\phi(E_{0})=E
for some
E\neq E_{0}
.
Then
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
for some
\ell\in[k-1]
.
Consider two distinct vertices
u,v\in E_{0}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. Both vertices have degree
2
in
\mathcal{H}
.
Since
\phi
is an automorphism,
\phi(u)
and
\phi(v)
are distinct vertices in
E
with degree
2
.
By Lemma
12
,
\phi(u)
and
\phi(v)
are
2
-connected in
\mathcal{G}^{\ell}
, so in particular
2
-connected in
\mathcal{H}
.
However, in our construction the vertices
u
and
v
are not
2
-connected, because the only path between them is
(u,E_{0},v)
.
This contradicts Proposition
11
. Therefore, we conclude
\phi(E_{0})=E_{0}
, so in particular
\phi(x_{0})=x_{0}
.

Since
\phi(E_{0})=E_{0}
,
\phi
is also an automorphism on the
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}-E_{0}
, which is the disjoint union of an isolated vertex
x_{0}
and
k
-graphs
\mathcal{G}^{\ell}
.
Recall that the
t_{\ell}
’s are pairwise disjoint, therefore the
\mathcal{G}^{\ell}
’s are pairwise non-isomorphic.
This implies that
\{\phi(E):~E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})\}=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
for every
\ell\in[k-1]
, i.e.
\phi
maps each
\mathcal{G}^{\ell}
to itself.

Now we show that
\phi
is the identity, thus
\mathcal{H}
is asymmetric.
Fix an arbitrary
\ell\in[k-1]
.
Note that
\phi(u^{\ell}_{1})\in\phi(E_{0})\cap\phi(E^{\ell}_{1})
, thus
\phi(u^{\ell}_{1})\in E_{0}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
.
This implies that
\phi(u^{\ell}_{1})=u^{\ell}_{1}
and therefore
\phi(E^{\ell}_{1})=E^{\ell}_{1}
.
Now Lemma
10
provides that
\phi
restricted to
\mathcal{G}^{\ell}
is the identity.
Since
\ell
was chosen arbitrarily, the entire automorphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is the identity.
We conclude that
\mathcal{H}
is asymmetric.

Now let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary induced sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
with
2\leq|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H})|
.
We shall show that that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is symmetric. We can assume that
|\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|\geq 2
, otherwise
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is trivally symmetric.

Case 1:

E_{0}\notin\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
with
E\neq E_{0}
, i.e.
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
for some fixed
\ell\in[k-1]
.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
be the sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
induced by the vertex set
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
.
Because
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
has at least two vertices.
Now Lemma
8
(i) provides a non-trivial automorphism
\psi
on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
.
We extend this automorphism to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
as follows. Let
\phi:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
with
\phi(w)=\psi(w)
for every
w\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
and
\phi(w)=w
for every
w\notin\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
.
Then
\phi
is a non-trivial automorphism on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, so
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is symmetric.

Case 2:

E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.

If there is some
\ell\in[k-1]
such that
E^{\ell}_{1}\notin\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, then consider the function
\phi:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
with

\phi(u^{\ell}_{1})=x_{0}
,
\phi(x_{0})=u^{\ell}_{1}
and
\phi(w)=w
for every
w\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\setminus\{u^{\ell}_{1},x_{0}\}
.
Observe that this is a non-trivial automorphism on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

If
E^{\ell^{\prime}}_{1}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
for every
\ell^{\prime}\in[k-1]
, fix
\ell
such that there is a vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})\setminus\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
and let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
be the sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
induced by
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})
.
Then
2<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})|<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\ell})|
, thus Lemma
8
(ii) yields a non-trivial automorphism
\psi
on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
with
\psi(u^{\ell}_{1})=u^{\ell}_{1}
.
Similarly to Case 1, we extend
\psi
to a non-trivial automorphism on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

This completes the proof for
k=4
and
k\geq 6
. For
k=3
and
k=5
, let
t
and
t^{\prime}
be arbitrary integers with
2\leq t<t^{\prime}
.
We show that
\mathcal{H}^{3}:=\mathcal{H}^{3}(t,t^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{H}^{5}:=\mathcal{H}^{5}(t,t^{\prime})
are minimal asymmetric. The proof is similar to the argumentation presented above, so we only provide a sketch. A detailed proof is given in the first author’s thesis
[
2
]
.

If
k=3
, let
\phi
be an arbitrary automorphism on
\mathcal{H}^{3}
. Then
\phi(E_{0})=E_{0}
, and thus

\{\phi(E):~E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})\}=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
as well as
\{\phi(E):~E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\}=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
.
Therefore,
\phi(u_{1})=u_{1}
and
\phi(u^{\prime}_{1})=u^{\prime}_{1}
. There are six edges in which every vertex has degree
2
, namely
E_{i}
and
E^{\prime}_{i}
for
i\in[3]
.
It is easy to see that each of them is invariant under
\phi
, which then implies that
\phi
is the identity.
Thus,
\mathcal{H}^{3}
is asymmetric.

Now consider an induced sub-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{H}^{3}
with
2\leq|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{3})|
.
We can suppose that there is no edge which contains two vertices of degree
1
, otherwise
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is clearly symmetric.
If
E_{0}\notin\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, then there is a non-trivial automorphism
\phi
on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
with
\phi(u_{2})=u_{3}
and
\phi(u_{3})=u_{2}
.
If
E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, then
E_{1},E^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. Let
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{3})\setminus\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
and say that
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
.
Now if
E_{y}\notin\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, we can apply Lemma
8
(ii) as in Case 2, so suppose that
E_{y}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.
Since no edge contains two vertices of degree
1
, we find that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})=\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{y}\}
.
Then there is an automorphism
\phi
on
\mathcal{G}
with
\phi(E_{y})=E_{3}
and
\phi(E_{3})=E_{y}
.

If
k=5
, given an automorphism
\phi
on
\mathcal{H}^{5}
, we see that
\phi(E_{0})=E_{0}
, thus
\{\phi(u_{1}),\phi(u_{2})\}=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
.
If
\phi(u_{1})=u_{2}
and
\phi(u_{2})=u_{1}
, then
\phi(E_{1})=E_{2}
,
\phi(E_{2})=E_{1}
and
\phi(E_{3})=E_{tk}
. Note that the edge
E_{1,1}
intersects each of
E_{1},E_{2}
and
E_{3}
,
but there does not exist an edge in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{5})
which intersects
\phi(E_{1})=E_{2},\phi(E_{2})=E_{1},\phi(E_{3})=E_{tk}
, a contradiction.
Thus
\phi(u_{1})=u_{1}
and
\phi(u_{2})=u_{2}
, and similarly,
\phi(u^{\prime}_{1})=u^{\prime}_{1}
and
\phi(u^{\prime}_{2})=u^{\prime}_{2}
.
By Lemma
9
(ii),
\phi
is the identity, so
\mathcal{H}^{5}
is asymmetric.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be an induced sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}^{5}
such that
2\leq|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|<|\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H}^{5})|
.
If
E_{0}\notin\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, we proceed as in Case 1.
Otherwise, we can suppose that
E_{1},E_{2},E^{\prime}_{1},E^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
by a similar argument as in Case 2.
Then a variant of Lemma
8
(ii), see Lemma 2.9(i) of

, provides a non-trivial automorphism on

.
∎

3
Regular asymmetric
k
-graphs

In order to prove Theorem
3
, we need a result by Izbicki
[
7
]
.

Theorem 13

(Izbicki
[
7
]
)
.

For every
k\geq 3
, there exist infinitely many
k
-regular asymmetric
2
-graphs.

Given a
k
-regular
2
-graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, the
(hypergraph) dual
of
G
is the
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{E},\{A(v):~v\in\mathcal{V}\})

where
A(v)=\{E\in\mathcal{E}:v\in E\}
is the
adjacency set
of
v
.
Note that
|A(v)|=k
for every
v
, thus
\mathcal{H}
is a well-defined
k
-graph. An example for a hypergraph dual is provided in Figure
5
.
Observe that adjacency sets are unique in regular
2
-graphs:

Proposition 14
.

Let
G
be an
r
-regular
2
-graph,
r\geq 2
. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{V}(G)
be two distinct vertices of
G
. Then
A(u)\neq A(v)
.

Figure 5:
The
3
-regular Frucht graph (left) and its hypergraph dual (right).

Lemma 15
.

Let
G
be an
r
-regular asymmetric graph for some
r\geq 3
. Then the hypergraph dual of
G
is also asymmetric.

Proof.

Let
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
.
Let
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}
be an arbitrary automorphism on the dual
\mathcal{H}
of
G
. By the definition of a dual we know that
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is a bijection such that for any
v\in\mathcal{V}
,
the edges in its adjacency set
A(v)
are mapped to
\big\{\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E):~E\in A(v)\big\}=A(w_{v})
for some vertex
w_{v}\in\mathcal{V}
.
By Proposition
14
,
w_{v}
is uniquely determined.

We define the function
\phi_{G}\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
,
\phi_{G}(v)=w_{v}
. Observe that
\phi_{G}
is a bijection. Now we show that
\phi_{G}
is an automorphism on
G
.
Consider an arbitrary edge
E_{uv}=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}
.
Then
E_{uv}\in A(v)
, thus
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E_{uv})\in\big\{\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E):~E\in A(v)\big\}=A(w_{v})=A(\phi_{G}(v))
.
Similarly, we obtain
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E_{uv})\in A(\phi_{G}(u))
.
Therefore,

\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E_{uv})=\{\phi_{G}(u),\phi_{G}(v)\}.

(1)

This implies that
\{\phi_{G}(u),\phi_{G}(v)\}\in\mathcal{E}
, so
\phi_{G}
is an automorphism on
G
.
Since
G
is asymmetric,
\phi_{G}
is the identity.
Then for every edge
E_{uv}=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}
, (1) implies that
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(E_{uv})=\{u,v\}=E_{uv}
, i.e.
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity.
Consequently,
\mathcal{H}
is asymmetric.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
k\geq 3
. Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots
be an infinite family of pairwise distinct
k
-regular, asymmetric graphs as provided by Theorem
13
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{i}
be the hypergraph dual of
G_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
’s are pairwise distinct
2
-regular
k
-graphs.
Lemma
15
provides that the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
’s are asymmetric.∎

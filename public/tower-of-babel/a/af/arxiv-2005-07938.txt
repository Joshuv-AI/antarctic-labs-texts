Efficient quantization and weak covering of high dimensional cubes
Jack Noonan, Anatoly Zhigljavsky

Abstract
Let ℤ n = { Z 1 , … , Z n } \mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\} be a design; that is, a collection of n n points Z j ∈ [ − 1 , 1 ] d Z_{j}\in[-1,1]^{d} . We study the quality of quantization of [ − 1 , 1 ] d [-1,1]^{d} by the points of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} and the problem of quality of coverage of [ − 1 , 1 ] d [-1,1]^{d} by ℬ d ​ ( ℤ n , r ) {\cal B}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r) , the union of balls centred at Z j ∈ ℤ n Z_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n} . We concentrate on the cases where the dimension d d is not small ( d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 ) and n n is not too large, n ≤ 2 d n\leq 2^{d} . We define the design 𝔻 n , δ {\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta}} as a 2 d − 1 2^{d-1} design defined on vertices of the cube [ − δ , δ ] d [-\delta,\delta]^{d} , 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1 0\leq\delta\leq 1 . For this design, we derive a closed-form expression for the quantization error and very accurate approximations for the coverage area vol ( [ − 1 , 1 ] d ∩ ℬ d ​ ( ℤ n , r ) ) ([-1,1]^{d}\cap{\cal B}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)) . We provide results of a large-scale numerical investigation confirming the accuracy of the developed approximations and the efficiency of the designs 𝔻 n , δ {\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta}} .



∎

1.3
Relation between quantization and weak coverage

The two characteristics,
C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)
and
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
, are related:
C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)
, as a function of

, is the c.d.f. of the r.v.

while
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is the second moment of the distribution with this c.d.f.:

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\int_{r\geq 0}r^{2}dC_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)\,.

(3)

In particular, this yields that if an
n
-point design
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
maximizes, in the set of all
n
-point designs,
C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)
for all
r>0
, then it also minimizes

\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
. Moreover, if r.v.
\varrho(X,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
stochastically
dominates
\varrho(X,\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime})
, so that

for all

and the inequality is strict for at least one

, then
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})<\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime})
.

The relation (
3
) can alternatively be written as

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\int_{r\geq 0}r\,dC_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\sqrt{r})\,,

(4)

where
C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\sqrt{r})
, considered as a function of
r
, is the c.d.f. of the r.v.
\varrho^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
and hence
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is the mean of this r.v. Relation (
4
) is simply another form of (
1
).

1.5
Thickness of covering

Let

of the whole space. In our case of

\displaystyle\Theta({\cal B}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{(n)},r))=\frac{n\,\mbox{vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(0,r)\right)}{\mbox{vol}([-1,1]^{d})}=\frac{n\,r^{d}\,\mbox{vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(0,1)\right)}{2^{d}}\,.

(

\displaystyle\theta({\cal B}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{(n)},r))=\frac{n\,r^{d}}{2^{d}}=d^{d/2}\,\left[R_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{(n)})\right]^{d}\,\,,

where we have recalled that

and

for any

.

We can thus define the normalised thickness of the covering of the cube by the same formula and extend it to any

Definition 3

-coverage of the cube

where

In view of (

2.1
Reformulation in terms of the Voronoi cells

Consider any
n
-point design
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
. The Voronoi cell
V(Z_{i})
for
Z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle V(Z_{i})=\{x\in[-1,1]^{d}:\left\lVert Z_{i}-x\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert Z_{j}-x\right\rVert\text{ for }j\neq i\}\,.

The mean squared quantization error
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
introduced in (
1
) can be written in terms of the Voronoi cells as follows:

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{X}\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\|X-Z_{i}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{{\rm vol([-1,1]^{d})}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{V(Z_{i})}\left\lVert X-Z_{i}\right\rVert^{2}dX\,,

(10)

where
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
dX=dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{d}
.

This reformulation has significant benefit when the design
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
has certain structure.
In particular, if all of the Voronoi cells
V(Z_{i}),i=1,\ldots,n,
are congruent, then we can simplify (
10
) to

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(V(Z_{1}))}\int_{V(Z_{1})}\left\lVert X-Z_{1}\right\rVert^{2}dX\,.

(11)

In Section
2.4
, this formula will be the starting point for derivation of the closed-form expression for
\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
for the design
\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta}
.

2.2
Re-normalization of the quantization error

To compare efficiency of
n
-point designs
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with different values of
n
, one must suitably normalise

with respect to

. Specialising a classical characteristic for quantization in space, as formulated in
(

\displaystyle Q_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{1}{d}\frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{V(Z_{i})}\left\lVert X-Z_{i}\right\rVert^{2}dX}{\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm vol}(V(Z_{i}))\right]^{1+\frac{2}{d}}}\,.

(12)

Note that

is re-normalised with respect to dimension

too, not only with respect to

Using (

\displaystyle Q_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{n^{2/d}\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})}{d\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm vol}(V(Z_{i}))\right]^{2/d}}=\frac{n^{2/d}\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})}{d\cdot{\rm vol}([-1,1]^{d})^{2/d}}=\frac{n^{2/d}}{4d}\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\,.

(13)

2.4
Explicit formulae for the quantization error

Theorem 2.2

For the design
\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta}
with

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(\delta^{2}-\delta+\frac{1}{3}\right)+\frac{2\delta}{d+1}\,,

\displaystyle Q_{d}(\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-2/d}\left(\delta^{2}-\delta+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2\delta}{d(d+1)}\right)\,.

(19)

\mathbf{Proof}
.
To compute

, we use (

), where, in view of Theorem

,
{{\rm vol}(V(Z_{1}))}=2
.
Using the expression (

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V(Z_{1})}\left\lVert X-Z_{1}\right\rVert^{2}dX=\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{C_{0}}\left\lVert X-Z_{1}\right\rVert^{2}dX+d\int_{U_{1}}\left\lVert X-Z_{1}\right\rVert^{2}dX\right]\,.

(20)

Consider the two terms in (

For the second term we have:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{U_{1}}\left\lVert X-Z_{1}\right\rVert^{2}dX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}\left[\int_{|x_{1}|}^{1}\ldots\int_{|x_{1}|}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-\delta)^{2}dx_{2}\ldots dx_{d}\right]dx_{1}

3.1
Reduction to Voronoi cells

For an

covered by the ball

\displaystyle V_{d,Z_{i},{r}}:={\rm vol}(V(Z_{i})\cap{\cal B}_{d}(Z_{i},{r}))/{\rm vol}(V(Z_{i}))\,.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 1

Consider a design
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
such that all Voronoi cells

are congruent. Then for any

C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)=V_{d,Z_{i},{r}}
.

In view of Theorem
2.1
, for design
\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta}
all Voronoi cells

are congruent and
{\rm vol}(V(Z_{i}))=2
; recall that

.
By then applying Lemma
1
and without loss of generality we have choosen

, we have for any
r>0

\displaystyle V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm vol}(V(\boldsymbol{\delta})\cap{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}))=C_{d}(\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta},r)\,.

(26)

In order to formulate explicit expressions for

, we need the important quantity, proportion of intersection of
[-1,1]^{d}
with one ball.
Take the cube
[-1,1]^{d}
and a ball

.
The fraction of the cube

Appendix A: Proof of Theorem
3.1

In view of (
26
),
C_{d}(\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta},r)=V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}
for all
0\leq\delta\leq 1
and
r\geq 0
and we shall derive expressions for

V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}
rather than
C_{d}(\mathbb{D}_{n,\delta},r)
.

\mathbf{Case(a):}

r\leq\delta
.

To prove this case, we observe i) for this range of
r
,
{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\subset[0,1]^{d}
; ii) the fraction of a cube covered by a ball is
preserved under invertible affine transformations; iii) the affine transformation
x\rightarrow 2x-\boldsymbol{1}

maps the ball
{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})
and cube
[0,1]^{d}
to
{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r})
and
[-1,1]^{d}
, respectively. This
leads to

\displaystyle V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}=\frac{{\rm vol}({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}))}{2\,{\rm vol}([0,1]^{d})}=\frac{{\rm vol}({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}))}{2\,{\rm vol}([-1,1]^{d})}=\frac{1}{2}C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}\,.

\mathbf{Case(b)}
:
\delta\leq r\leq 1+\delta

Using (
15
) we obtain

\displaystyle V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg[{\rm vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap C_{0}\right)+d\cdot{\rm vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap U_{1}\right)\bigg].

The first quantity in the brackets has been considered in case (a) and it is simply
C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}
.
Therefore we aim to reformulate the second quantity within the brackets,
{\rm vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap U_{1}\right)
.
Denote by
{\cal P}(t)=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}):x_{1}=t\}
, the
(d-1)
-dimensional hyperplane. Then

\displaystyle{\rm vol}\left({\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap U_{1}\right)=\int_{\delta-r}^{0}{\rm vol}_{d-1}({\cal P}(t)\cap{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap U_{1})dt\,.

Notice further that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} U_{1}\cap{\cal P}(t)=\{t\}\times[|t|,1]^{d-1},&&\text{ for }-1\leq t\leq 0\\
{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap{\cal P}(t)=\{t\}\times{\cal B}_{d-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta},{\sqrt{r^{2}-(t-\delta)^{2}}}\right)&&\text{ for }\delta-r\leq t\leq 0,r\geq\delta,\end{aligned}

(32)

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta,\ldots,\delta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and the natural identification of
{\cal P}(t)
with
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
is used. The r.h.s. in (
32
) are a
(d-1)-
dimensional cube and ball respectively. Since covered fraction is preserved under affine transformations in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, it suffices to construct one, denote by
\phi
, for which
\phi([|t|,1]^{d-1})=[-1,1]^{d}
. In
{\cal P}(t)
, such
\phi
maps the cube from (
32
) to the standard cube
[-1,1]^{d}
. Clearly,
\phi
can be taken as

\displaystyle\phi:x\rightarrow\frac{x-(\boldsymbol{1+|t|})/2}{({1-|t|})/2}=\frac{2x-(\boldsymbol{1+|t|})}{{1-|t|}}\,,

where
{\boldsymbol{1}}=(1,\ldots,1)
and
\boldsymbol{|t|}=(|t|,\ldots,|t|)
are constant vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Note that,

\displaystyle\phi\left({\cal B}_{d-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta},{\sqrt{r^{2}-(t-\delta)^{2}}}\right)\right)={\cal B}_{d-1}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{2\delta-(1+|t|)}}{\boldsymbol{1-|t|}},\frac{2\sqrt{r^{2}-(t-\delta)^{2}}}{1-|t|}\right)\,.

Finally, by the preservation of covered fraction, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm vol}_{d-1}({\cal P}(t)\cap{\cal B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\delta},{r})\cap U_{1})={\rm vol}_{d-1}([|t|,1]^{d-1})\cdot C_{d-1,\boldsymbol{\frac{2\delta-1-|{t}|}{1-|{t}|}},{\frac{2\sqrt{r^{2}-(t-\delta)^{2}}}{1-|t|}}}\,.

As a result,

\displaystyle V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}+d\int_{\delta-r}^{0}C_{d-1,\boldsymbol{\frac{2\delta-1-|{t}|}{1-|{t}|}},{\frac{2\sqrt{r^{2}-(t-\delta)^{2}}}{1-|t|}}}(1-|t|)^{d-1}\,dt\right]

(33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}+d\int_{0}^{r-\delta}C_{d-1,\boldsymbol{\frac{2\delta-1-{t}}{1-{t}}},{\frac{2\sqrt{r^{2}-(t+\delta)^{2}}}{1-t}}}(1-t)^{d-1}\,dt\right]\,.

\mathbf{Case(c)}
:
r\geq 1+\delta
:

Case (c) is almost identical to Case (b), with the only change occurring within the lower limit of integration in (
33
); the lower limit of the integral remains at
-1
for all
r\geq 1+\delta
. Since the steps are almost identical to Case (b), they are omitted and we simply conclude:

\displaystyle V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}+d\int_{0}^{1}C_{d-1,\boldsymbol{\frac{2\delta-1-{t}}{1-{t}}},{\frac{2\sqrt{r^{2}-(t+\delta)^{2}}}{1-t}}}(1-t)^{d-1}\,dt\right]\,.

\Box

defined in (

Appendix C: Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Before proving Theorem
3.2
, we prove three auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 3

Let
r=r_{\alpha,d}=\alpha\sqrt{d}
with
\alpha\geq 0
and
Z_{a,b;d}=(a,b,b,\ldots,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the limit
\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d,{Z_{a,b;d}},{2r}}
exists and

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d,Z_{a,b;d},{2r}}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }\alpha<\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}\\
1/2\text{ if }\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}\\
1\text{ if }\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}\,\end{cases}

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}=\frac{\sqrt{3}(d(4\alpha^{2}-b^{2}-1/3)+b^{2}-a^{2})}{2\sqrt{a^{2}+(d-1)b^{2}+d/15}}\,.

As the r.v.
\eta_{z}
introduced in Appendix D are concentrated on a finite interval, for finite
a
and
b
the quantities of
\rho_{a}:=\mathbb{E}(|\eta_{a}-a^{2}-\frac{1}{3}|^{3})
and
\rho_{b}:=\mathbb{E}(|\eta_{b}-b^{2}-\frac{1}{3}|^{3})
are bounded.
By applying Berry-Esseen theorem (see §2, Chapter 5 in
petrov2012sums
) to
C_{d,Z_{a,b},{2r}}
, there exists some constant
C
such that

\displaystyle-\frac{C\cdot\max\{\rho_{a}/\sigma_{a}^{2},\rho_{b}/\sigma_{b}^{2}\}}{\left(\sigma_{a}^{2}+(d-1)\sigma_{b}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}+\Phi\left(t_{\alpha}\right)\leq C_{d,Z_{a,b},{2r}}\leq\Phi\left(t_{\alpha}\right)+\frac{C\cdot\max\{\rho_{a}/\sigma_{a}^{2},\rho_{b}/\sigma_{b}^{2}\}}{\left(\sigma_{a}^{2}+(d-1)\sigma_{b}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\,,

where
\sigma_{a}^{2}={\rm var}(\eta_{a})
and
\sigma_{b}^{2}={\rm var}(\eta_{b})
.
By the squeeze theorem, it is clear that if
4\alpha^{2}-b^{2}-1/3>0
and hence
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}
, then
C_{d,Z_{a,b},{2r}}\rightarrow 1
as
d\rightarrow\infty
. If
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}
, then
C_{d,Z_{a,b},{2r}}\rightarrow 0
as
d\rightarrow\infty
. If
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+b^{2}}
, then
C_{d,Z_{a,b},{2r}}\rightarrow 1/2
as
d\rightarrow\infty
.

\Box

Lemma 4

Let
r=\alpha\sqrt{d}
. Then for
\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta,\delta,\ldots,\delta)
, we have:

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}=\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }\alpha<\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\\
1/2\text{ if }\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\\
1\text{ if }\alpha>\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\,\end{cases}

Proof.

Using Lemma
3
with
Z_{a,b}=A=\left(2\delta+1,2\delta-1,\ldots,2\delta-1\right)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d,A,{2r}}=\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d,\boldsymbol{2\delta-1},{2r}}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }\alpha<\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\\
1/2\text{ if }\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\\
1\text{ if }\alpha>\frac{\sqrt{1/3+(2\delta-1)^{2}}}{2}\,\end{cases}

By then applying the squeeze theorem to the bounds in Lemma
2
using the fact from Lemma
1
we have
V_{d,\boldsymbol{\delta},{r}}=C_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},r)
, we obtain the result.

Appendix D: Derivation of approximation (
30
)

Let
U=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})
be a random vector with uniform distribution on
[-1,1]^{d}
so that
u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}
are i.i.d.r.v. uniformly distributed on
[-1,1]
. Then for given
Z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
r>0
,

\displaystyle C_{d,Z,{r}}\!=\!\mathbb{P}\left\{\|U\!-\!Z\|\!\leq\!{r}\right\}\!=\!\mathbb{P}\left\{\|U\!-\!Z\|^{2}\leq{r^{2}}\right\}\!=\!\mathbb{P}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}(u_{j}\!-\!z_{j})^{2}\leq{r}^{2}\right\}.\;\;

That is,
C_{d,Z,{r}}
, as a function of
{r}
, is the c.d.f. of the r.v.
\|U-Z\|
.

Let
u
have the uniform distribution on
[-1,1]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
The first three central moments of the r.v.
\eta_{z}=(u-z)^{2}
can be easily computed:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\eta_{z}=z^{2}+\frac{1}{3},\;\;{\rm var}(\eta_{z})=\frac{4}{3}\left(z^{2}+\frac{1}{15}\right)\,,\;\;\mu_{z}^{(3)}=E\left[\eta_{z}-E\eta_{z}\right]^{3}=\frac{16}{15}\left(z^{2}+\frac{1}{63}\right)\,.

(36)

Consider the r.v.

\|U-Z\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{z_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(u_{j}-z_{j})^{2}\,.

From (
36
) and independence of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{d,Z}=\mathbb{E}\|U-Z\|^{2}=\|Z\|^{2}+\frac{d}{3}\,\;\;{\sigma}_{d,Z}^{2}={\rm var}(\|U-Z\|^{2})=\frac{4}{3}\left(\|Z\|^{2}+\frac{d}{15}\right)\,\;\;\;\;\;\;

and

\displaystyle{\mu}_{d,Z}^{(3)}=\mathbb{E}\left[\|U-Z\|^{2}-\mu_{d,Z}\right]^{3}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{z_{j}}^{(3)}=\frac{16}{15}\left(\|Z\|^{2}+\frac{d}{63}\right)\,.\;\;\;\;\;\;

If
d
is large enough then the conditions of the CLT for
\|U-Z\|^{2}
are approximately met and the distribution of
\|U-Z\|^{2}

is approximately normal with mean
\mu_{d,Z}
and variance
{\sigma}_{d,Z}^{2}
. That is, we can approximate

C_{d,Z,{r}}

by

\displaystyle C_{d,Z,{r}}\cong\Phi\left(\frac{{r}^{2}-\mu_{d,Z}}{{\sigma}_{d,Z}}\right)\,,

(37)

where
\Phi(\cdot)
is the c.d.f. of the standard normal distribution:

\Phi(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\varphi(v)dv\;\;{\rm with}\;\;\varphi(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-v^{2}/2}\,.

The approximation (
37
) can be improved by
using an Edgeworth-type expansion in the CLT for sums of independent non-identically distributed r.v.

General expansion in the central limit theorem for sums of independent non-identical r.v. has been derived
by V.Petrov, see
Theorem 7 in Chapter 6 in
petrov2012sums
; the first three terms of this expansion have been specialized in
Section 5.6 in
petrov
.
By using only the first term in this expansion,
we obtain the following approximation for the distribution function of
\|U-Z\|^{2}
:

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{\|U-Z\|^{2}-\mu_{d,Z}}{\sigma_{d,Z}}\leq x\right)\cong\Phi(x)+\frac{{\mu}_{d,Z}^{(3)}}{6({\sigma}_{d,Z}^{2})^{3/2}}(1-x^{2})\varphi(x),

leading to the following improved form of (
37
):

\displaystyle C_{d,Z,{r}}\cong\Phi(t)+\frac{\|Z\|^{2}+d/63}{5\sqrt{3}(\|Z\|^{2}+d/15)^{3/2}}(1-t^{2})\varphi(t)\,,

where

\displaystyle t=t_{d,\|Z\|,{r}}=\frac{{r}^{2}-\mu_{d,Z}}{{\sigma}_{d,Z}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}(r^{2}-\|Z\|^{2}-d/3)}{2\sqrt{\|Z\|^{2}+{d}/{15}}}\,.

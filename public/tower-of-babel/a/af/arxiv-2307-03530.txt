A basepoint free theorem for algebraically integrable foliations
Priyankur Chaudhuri, Omprokash Das

Abstract
. We show that if ℱ \mathcal{F} is an algebraically integrable foliation on a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial normal projective variety X X , A , B ≥ 0 A,B\geq 0 are ℚ \mathbb{Q} -divisors on X X with A A ample such that ( ℱ , B ) (\mathcal{F},B) is foliated dlt, ( X , B ) (X,B) is klt and K ℱ + A + B K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B is nef, then K ℱ + A + B K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B is semiample. We also provide some applications of this result such as contraction theorem for F-dlt pairs and a special case of the b-semiampleness conjecture.



1.
Introduction

The study of birational geometry of algebraically integrable foliations was initiated by Ambro, Cascini, Shokurov and Spicer in
[
ACSS21
]
, where the authors used foliated MMP to study the positivity of the moduli part of lc-trivial (and more general) fibrations. They observe that the moduli divisor of a ‘nice’ fibration can be related to a log canonical divisor of the foliation induced by it. In case the fibration is lc-trivial, the moduli b-divisor is known to be b-nef and b-good (
[
Amb05
]
,
[
Hu20
]
) and conjectured to be b-semiample
[
PS09
]
. Hence abundance-type theorems for foliated log canonical pairs are expected to have applications to the b-semiampleness conjecture.

In
[
CS23
]
, Cascini and Spicer stated a version of the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
CS23
, Conjecture 4.1]

Let
(\mathcal{F},B)
be an F-dlt pair (see Definition
4.2
) on a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety
X
,
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable and
A
an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Moreover, assume
(X,B)
is klt. If
K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B
is nef, then it is semiample.

This can be thought of as a basepoint free theorem for foliated dlt pairs. Indeed, the above statement can be phrased in the following equivalent form:

Let
(\mathcal{F},B)
be a foliated dlt pair on
X
where
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable and
L
a nef divisor on
X
such that
L-(K_{\mathcal{F}}+B)
is ample. Then
L
is semiample.

This conjecture was shown to be true for all corank one foliations on threefolds by Cascini and Spicer
[
CS21
, Theorem 9.4]
. In this article, we prove this conjecture.

Theorem 1.2

(=Theorem
4.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety
X
which is induced by a dominant rational map
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
. Suppose
(\mathcal{F},B)
is F-dlt,
(X,B)
is klt and
A
an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. If
K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B
is nef, then it is semiample.

A direct consequence of this result is the contraction theorem for F-dlt pairs (sse Corollary
5.2
). We now come to some extensions of this theorem. Essentially the same arguments used in proving Theorem
4.3
show that in the given setting, if
K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B
is not assumed to be nef, then
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B))
is semiample for some birational morphism
\mu:X^{\prime}\to X
. At present, we do not know how to prove Theorem
4.3
when
(\mathcal{F},B)
is only lc. However, in case
\mathcal{F}
is induced by an equidimensional morphism
f:X\to Y
such that if
B_{f}
and
G
denote the reduced branch and ramification divisors of
f
respectively,
(X,B+G-\epsilon f^{*}B_{f})
is klt for some
\epsilon>0
(for example, if
f
is toroidal), then
K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B
is semiample. This result (see Lemma
4.1
) is a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
4.3
. Proving it requires working in the category of foliated generalized lc pairs. We briefly mention the motivation behind using this larger category. One is the failure of Bertini-type results: if
(\mathcal{F},B)
is lc, then
(\mathcal{F},B+A)
need not be lc even if
A
is general in its linear system. But since
(\mathcal{F},B,\mathbf{A})
is generalized lc, this provides an easy way of working with
K_{\mathcal{F}}+A+B
. The other reason is that the proof of Lemma
4.1
involves a canonical bundle formula where the output is a foliated glc pair. Therefore, along the way, we extend several results for foliated lc pairs (proved in
[
ACSS21
]
,
[
CS21
]
,
[
LLM23
]
) namely adjunction, cone theorem and canonical bundle formula to the setting of foliated glc pairs.

We close this section with an interesting application of our results to the moduli part of a nice lc-trivial fibration:

Theorem 1.3
.

(=Corollary
5.5
)
Let
(X,B)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pair,
A
an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Suppose
f:(X,\Sigma_{X})\to(Y,\Sigma_{Y})
is an equidimensional toroidal morphism to a smooth projective variety such that
K_{X}+A+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q},f}0
and components of
B
are components of the
f
-horizontal part of the toroidal boundary
\Sigma_{X}

Definition 2.1

(Basics on Foliations; see
[
Spi20
]
,
[
Dru21
]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety. A
foliation

\mathcal{F}
on
X
is a coherent subsheaf of the tangent sheaf
T_{X}
which is closed under Lie brackets and such that
T_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion free. The
singular locus
of
\mathcal{F}
is the locus where
\mathcal{F}
fails to be a sub-bundle of
T_{X}
. It is a large open subset of
X
whose complement has codimension at least
2
. In particular, there exists a large open
U\subseteq X
where
X
and
\mathcal{F}
are both smooth. The
canonical divisor
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is then the Zariski closure of det
(\mathcal{F}|_{U})^{*}
. A subvariety
W\subseteq X
is called
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
if for any local section
\partial
of
\mathcal{F}
over some
U\subseteq X
open,
\partial(I_{W\cap U})\subseteq I_{W\cap U}
. If
P\subseteq X
is a prime divisor, then we define
\epsilon(P)=0
if
P
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\epsilon(P)=1
otherwise.

Let
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
be a dominant rational map between normal varieties and
\mathcal{F}
a foliation on
Y
. Then as in
[
Dru21
, Section 3.2]
, we can define the pullback foliation
f^{-1}\mathcal{F}
. The pullback of the zero foliation on
Y
is known as the foliation induced by
f
. Such foliations are called
algebraically integrable
. If
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation induced by
f
, then one can check that a prime divisor
D\subseteq X
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant if and only if it is
f
-vertical, i.e.
f(D)
does not dominate
Y
. If
f
is a morphism, note that the foliation induced by
f
is nothing but the relative tangent sheaf
T_{X/Y}
. If
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
is birational and
\mathcal{F}
a foliation on
X
, then we have an induced foliation on
Y
defined by
g^{-1}\mathcal{F}
where
g:=f^{-1}
.

If
f:X\to Y
is an equidimensional morphism of normal varieties and
\mathcal{F}
the foliation induced by
f
, then
K_{\mathcal{F}}\sim K_{X/Y}-R_{f}
, where
R_{f}:=\sum_{D}(f^{*}D-f^{-1}D)
is the ramification divisor of
f
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation on
X
induced by a dominant rational map
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
. We say that a subvariety
V\subseteq X
is
tangent
to
\mathcal{F}
if there exist

(1)

a birational morphism
\mu:X^{\prime}\to X
and an equidimensional contraction
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
which induces
\mu^{-1}\mathcal{F}
; and

(2)

a subvariety
V^{\prime}
contained in a fiber of
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
such that
V=\mu(V^{\prime})
.

3.
Properties of generalized foliated pairs

3.1.
Adjunction

In this subsection we will prove an adjunction theorem for generalized foliated pair. This is a generalization of
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 3.2]
and our arguments are similar to theirs.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a normal projective variety
X
induced by a contraction
f:X\rightarrow Z
. Suppose that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta,\mathbf{M})
is a foliated generalized log canonical pair on
X

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Let

be a prime Weil divisor on

with normalization
\nu:S\to T

\operatorname{mult}_{T}(\Delta)=\epsilon(T)

(1)

there exists a generalized foliated pair

on

such that

\nu^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M})=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}+\Delta_{S}+\mathbf{N}

(2)

if
(\mathcal{F},\Delta,\mathbf{M})
is generalized log canonical, then so is
(\mathcal{F}_{S},\Delta_{S},\mathbf{N})

Proof.

First replacing

-factorization we may assume that

-Cartier. Let

descends to

Let

\Delta_{S}-\tilde{\Delta}_{S}+\mathbf{N}_{S}=\nu^{*}\mathbf{M}_{X}.

. By

. But then

\beta^{*}((K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M})|_{S})=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}}}+(\Delta^{\prime}-S^{\prime}+F_{1})|_{S^{\prime}}+F_{0}|_{S^{\prime}}+\mathbf{N}_{S^{\prime}}

3.2.
Cone theorem

We need the following simple variation of the bend and break lemma of Spicer

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta,\mathbf{M})
be a generalized foliated pair on a normal projective variety

be any nef divisor on

. Suppose there exist nef
\mathbb{R}
-divisors

Proof.

The arguments of

still work if we replace

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta,\mathbf{M})
be a generalized foliated log canonical pair on a normal projective variety
X
, where
\mathcal{F}
is induced by a dominant map
f:X\dashrightarrow Z
of normal varieties. Then there are at most countably many rational curves
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that
\xi_{i}
is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
,
0<-(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot\xi_{i}\leq 2n
for all
i\in I
and

\overline{NE}(X)=\overline{NE}(X)_{K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X}\geq 0}+\sum\mathbb{R}_{+}[\xi_{i}].

Proof.

Our proof uses some ideas of the proof of
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 3.9]
.
We will proceed by induction on the dimension of
X
. Assume that the above theorem holds for varieties of dimension
\leq n-1
. Let
R\subseteq\overline{NE}(X)
be a
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X}
-negative exposed extremal ray supported by the nef (but not ample) Cartier divisor
H_{R}
, i.e.
H_{R}^{\bot}\cap\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)=R
. We make the following claim:

Claim 3.4
.

R
is generated by a rational curve
\xi\subseteq X
tangent to
\mathcal{F}
such that
0<-(K_{X}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot\xi\leq 2\operatorname{dim}X
.

Proof of Claim
3.4
.

From a standard argument it follows that there is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
A
such that
H_{R}\equiv K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X}+A
. We divide our proof into two cases depending on whether
H_{R}
is big or not.

Case I:
Suppose that
H_{R}
is not big. In this case we argue as in the proof of
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 3.9]
. Let
\nu:=\nu(H_{R})
be the numerical dimension of
H_{R}
,
D_{i}:=H_{R}
for
1\leq i\leq\nu+1
and
D_{i}:=A
for
\nu+1<i\leq n
. Then
D_{1}\cdots D_{n}=H_{R}^{\nu+1}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}=0
and thus
-(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot D_{2}\cdots D_{n}=(A-H_{R})\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}>0
. Fix

3.2

there is a rational curve

\mathcal{F}

\begin{split}-(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot\xi=N\cdot\xi&\leq 2n\left(\frac{N\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}}{-K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}}\right)\\
&=2n\left(\frac{-(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}}{-K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}}\right)\\
&\leq 2n,\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
(\Delta+\mathbf{M}_{X})\cdot H_{R}^{\nu}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}\geq 0
. Indeed, to see that
\mathbf{M}_{X}\cdot H^{\nu}_{R}\cdot A^{n-\nu-1}\geq 0

descends on

Suppose that

-divisor

.

Now let
\Lambda

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\lambda\geq 0

(2)

(3)

.

Note that

Let

\tilde{X}

over

. By

ACSS21

or

3.5
.

Let
H_{Z^{\prime}}
be an ample Cartier divisor on

\Theta^{\prime}:=\beta_{*}^{-1}(\Delta+\mu S)+\sum\epsilon(E_{i})E_{i}

3.5
. We can write

Claim 3.7

There exists a

Proof.

The arguments are standard; see for example
[
BCHM10

,
[
CS21
, Lemma 9.2]
. We give the details for the reader’s convenience. The number of

Corollary 5.5

(Semiampleness of the moduli part)
.

Let
(X,B)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pair,
A
an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Suppose
f:(X,\Sigma_{X})\to(Y,\Sigma_{Y})
is an equidimensional toroidal morphism to a smooth projective variety such that
K_{X}+A+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q},f}0
and components of
B
are components of the
f
-horizontal part of the toroidal boundary
\Sigma_{X}
. . Then the moduli part
M_{X}^{A+B}
is semiample.

Proof.

Our arguments are similar to those of
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 1.3]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the foliation on
X
induced by
f
. Let
\Delta:=A+B
. Let
G
denote the inverse image of the branch divisor and
B_{f}
the reduced branch divisor of
f
. Note that
(X,B+G)
has property (
*
) by
[
ACSS21
, Prop 2.16]
. Replacing
A
by a general member in its linear system (note that the moduli part is only determined up to linear equivalence, so is not affected by this), we have
B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}=B_{Y}^{B+G}
(Clearly,
B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}\geq B_{Y}^{B+G}
. Since
(X,B+G+a_{P}^{B+G}f^{*}P)
is lc over
\eta_{P}
, so is
(X,B+A+G+a_{P}^{B+G}f^{*}P)
for any
P
which is a component of
B_{Y}^{B+G}
. Thus
a_{P}^{B+G}\leq a_{P}^{\Delta+G}
and hence
B_{Y}^{B+G}\geq B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}
). Next we observe that
B_{Y}^{B+G}=B_{f}
(Indeed, if
P\subseteq Y
is a prime divisor not contained in
B_{f}
, then due to Property (
*
), we have coeff
{}_{P}(B_{Y}^{B+G})=0
. If
P\subseteq B_{f}
, then coeff
{}_{P}(B_{Y}^{B+G})=1
). Let
\overline{\Delta}:=\Delta+f^{*}(B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}-B_{Y}^{\Delta})
. Clearly,
M_{X}^{\Delta}=M_{X}^{\overline{\Delta}}
. Note that
E:=\Delta+G-\overline{\Delta}\geq 0
and is
f
-very exceptional (i.e.
E
does not support the fiber over any codimension
1
point of
Y
). Indeed, for
P\subseteq B_{f}
, let
f^{*}P=n_{1}Q_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}Q_{k}
with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{k}
, then
Q_{1}\not\subseteq
Supp
\Delta+G-\overline{\Delta}
. This can be seen as follows: as a consequence of Property (
*
), it follows that
a_{P}^{\Delta}=\frac{1}{n_{1}}
, and thus coeff
{}_{P}B_{Y}^{\Delta}=1-\frac{1}{n_{1}}
. Since
B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}=B_{f}
, it follows that coeff
{}_{P}(B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}-B_{Y}^{\Delta})=\frac{1}{n_{1}}
and thus coeff
{}_{Q_{1}}f^{*}(B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}-B_{Y}^{\Delta})=1
. Since
E=G-f^{*}(B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}-B_{Y}^{\Delta})
, it follows that coeff
{}_{Q_{1}}E=0
. If
P\not\subseteq B_{f}
, then coeff
{}_{P}B_{Y}^{\Delta+G}=
coeff
{}_{P}B_{Y}^{\Delta}=0
.

Since
K_{X}+\overline{\Delta}\sim_{Y}0
, it follows that
K_{X}+\Delta+G\sim_{Y}E
. Note that

K_{\mathcal{F}}+B+A\sim_{Y}K_{X}+B+G-\epsilon f^{*}B_{f}+A

for all
\epsilon>0
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{F},B,\mathbf{A})
is F-glc and
(X,B+G-\epsilon f^{*}B_{f},\mathbf{A})
is gklt for some
\epsilon>0
since
f
is toroidal. Now, by
[
Bir12
, Theorem 3.4]
, we can run a
K_{\mathcal{F}}+B+\mathbf{A}
-MMP
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
which is a
K_{X}+B+G-\epsilon f^{*}B_{f}+\mathbf{A}
-MMP over
Y
with scaling of
A
such that
\phi_{*}E=0
(see the proof of Claim
3.5
for details). This is also a
K_{\mathcal{F}}+B
-MMP and thus Property (
*
) and equidimensionality are preserved by it
[
ACSS21
, Prop 2.18]
. We have
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}\sim_{Y}0
and
K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
is nef, where
\Delta^{\prime}:=\phi_{*}\Delta
and
G^{\prime}:=\phi_{*}G
. By the arguments used in Corollary
5.3
, we can write
K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}\sim K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{H}
, where
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime},\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{H})
is F-glc and
H
is ample. Then
K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
is semiample by Corollary
5.3
.

We claim that
M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}\sim K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
. Indeed, as observed above, the induced morphism
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
is equidimensional and
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime})
has property (
*
). By
[
ACSS21
, Prop 3.6]
, we have
M_{X^{\prime}}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}\sim K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}
. Now, by definition,

M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}=K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}+A^{\prime}-f^{\prime*}(K_{Y^{\prime}}+B_{Y^{\prime}}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}+A^{\prime}})
.

For
P\subseteq Y
a prime divisor, we have
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}+a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}f^{\prime*}P)
is lc over
\eta_{P}
and has some lc center. Now,
\phi
is a
K_{X}+B+G+a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}f^{*}P
-MMP over
\eta_{P}
. Thus any lcc of
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}+a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}f^{\prime*}P)
over
\eta_{P}
arises as image of some lcc of
(X,B+G+a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}f^{*}P)
over
\eta_{P}
(note the last entity is lc by property (
*
)).
A
being a general ample divisor, does not contain any such lcc for any such
P
. Thus, neither does
A^{\prime}
. We conclude that
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}+A^{\prime}+a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}f^{\prime*}P)
is lc over
\eta_{P}
and hence
a_{P}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}\geq a_{P}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}
for all prime divisors
P\subseteq Y
. It follows that
B_{Y^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}=B_{Y^{\prime}}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}
. From this, we can infer that
M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}=M_{X^{\prime}}^{B^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}+A^{\prime}\sim K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
. This proves the claim. We also note that
M_{X}^{\Delta+G}=M_{X}^{B+G}+A\sim K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
.

Now
M_{X^{\prime}}^{\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}}=M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}\sim K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
is semiample. Let
X\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}X^{\prime}
be a resolution of indeterminacy of
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
. Then
p^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)=q^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})+F
for some
F\geq 0
which is exceptional over
X^{\prime}
. Now
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta\sim K_{X}+\Delta+G-f^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}^{\Delta+G})=K_{X}+\Delta-f^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}^{\Delta})+E=M_{X}^{\Delta}+E
. It follows that

(5.2)

p^{*}M_{X}^{\Delta}=q^{*}M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}+F-p^{*}E.

By
[
ACSS21
, Prop 2.21]
,
F-p^{*}E\sim D\geq 0
. Now we have
F-p^{*}E\equiv_{X^{\prime}}p^{*}M_{X}^{\Delta}
. Since
M_{X}^{\Delta}\equiv_{Y}0
, it follows that
F-p^{*}E\equiv_{X^{\prime}}0
. Since
F
and
p^{*}E
are both exceptional over
X^{\prime}
,
p^{*}E\geq F
by negativity lemma over
X^{\prime}
. Combining this with the above observation, it follows that
p^{*}E=F
. By equation
5.2
, it follows that
p^{*}M_{X}^{\Delta}=q^{*}M_{X^{\prime}}^{\Delta^{\prime}+G^{\prime}}
is semiample.

∎

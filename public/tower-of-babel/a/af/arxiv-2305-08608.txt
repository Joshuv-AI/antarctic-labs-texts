Schur rings over infinite dihedral group
Gang chen, Jiawei He, Zhiman Wu

Abstract
. Schur rings over the infinite dihedral group 𝒵 ⋊ 𝒵 2 \mathcal{Z}\rtimes\mathcal{Z}_{2} are studied according to properties of Schur rings over infinite groups and the classification of Schur rings over infinite cyclic groups. Schur rings over 𝒵 ⋊ 𝒵 2 \mathcal{Z}\rtimes{\mathcal{Z}}_{2} are classified under the assumption that 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} is an 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -subgroup. Those Schur rings are proved to be traditional.



1.
Introduction

Schur rings are a type of subrings of group rings which are determined by a partition of the underline group, and they were originally developed by Schur
[
8
]
and Wielandt
[
9
]
in order to study permutation groups as an alternative to character theory. In addition, theory of Schur rings has been used in many other applications. For the applications of Schur rings to association
schemes, see for example, subsections 2.4 and 4.4 of
[
2
]
.

Classification of Schur rings over a given group
G
has been a subject of much research interest.
Most papers have studied Schur rings over finite groups.
Leung and Man have classified Schur rings over finite cyclic groups. There are four types of Schur rings over finite cyclic groups which are: partitions induced by direct product decomposition of groups (Schur rings of direct produt type); partitions induced by orbits of automorphism subgroups (orbit Schur rings); partitions induced by cosets of normal subgroups (Schur rings of wedge product type); trivial Schur ring. We say that a Schur ring over a group is a
traditional Schur ring
if it is one of these four types.

Some scholars have extended some properties of Schur rings over finite groups to Schur rings over infinite groups and have classified Schur rings over infinite cyclic groups, the group
\mathcal{Z}\times{\mathcal{Z}_{2}}

(\mathcal{Z}
is an infinite cyclic group,
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
is a cyclic group of order 2) and torsion-free locally cyclic groups. In particular, the classification of Schur rings over infinite cyclic groups and the main
results of Leung and Man tell us that all Schur rings over cyclic groups are traditional.

Although general structure theorems are available for Schur
rings over finite cyclic groups, the problem of classification of Schur rings over infinite groups is proved to be a difficult problem. Some special cases have been considered, for example, Bastian et al.
[
1
]
study the Schur rings over some types of infinite groups. According to their results, there are nontraditional Schur
rings over free groups and free products. Recently, Chen et al.
[
3
]
classify
Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}_{3}
. In particular, all these Schur rings are proved to be traditional. In this paper we consider the classification of Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}\rtimes{\mathcal{Z}_{2}}
.

Throughout let
G
denote an arbitrary group with identity element
1
, and
\mathbb{F}
an arbitrary field of characteristic
0
and we assume that it contains
\mathbb{Z}
as a subring. The group algebra over
G
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
is denoted
\mathbb{F}[G]
. The infinite dihedral group
\langle z,s~:~s^{2}=1,s^{-1}zs=z^{-1}\rangle
is denoted by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}
.

As we shall see, the main results of the paper tells us that when
\langle z\rangle
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup for a Schur ring over
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}
. Then can get the exact classification of
\mathcal{A}
, which are prove to be traditional. The following results implies that under some condition,
\langle z\rangle
is really an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup.

.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over

,

The following are the main results of the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over

2.
Preliminary

For any finite non-empty subset
C\subseteq G
, denote
\underline{C}=\sum_{g\in C}g
and call
\underline{C}
a
simple quantity
. In addition, we define
C^{*}=\{g^{-1}:g\in C\}
. A partition
{\mathcal{D}}
of
G
is said to be
finite support
provided any
C\in{\mathcal{D}}
is a finite subset of
G
. Our notation for Schur ring is taken from
[
7
]
.

2.1.
Schur Rings

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a finite support partition of
G
and
\mathcal{A}
a subspace of
\mathbb{F}[G]
spanned by
\{\underline{C}:C\in{\mathcal{D}}\}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is a
Schur ring
(or
S
-ring) over
G
if

(1)

\{1\}\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

(2)

for any
C\in{\mathcal{D}}
,
C^{*}\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

(3)

for all
C,D\in{\mathcal{D}}
,
\underline{C}\cdot\underline{D}=\sum_{E\in{\mathcal{D}}}\lambda_{CDE}\underline{E}
, where all but finitely many
\lambda_{CDE}
equal
0
.

For a Schur ring
\mathcal{A}
over
G
, the associated partition
{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})
and each element in
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})
is called a
basic set

of
\mathcal{A}
. Each
\lambda_{CDE}
is called a
structure constant
. We say that a subset
C
of
G
is an
\mathcal{A}
-set
if
C
is a union of some basic sets of
\mathcal{A}
. An
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup
is simultaneously an
\mathcal{A}
-set and a subgroup.

Suppose
C=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}g
and
D=\sum_{g\in G}\beta_{g}g\in\mathbb{F}[G]
(note that here only finitely many nonzero coefficients
\alpha_{g}
and
\beta_{g}
). Then define

C^{*}=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}g^{-1}
and
\supp(C)=\{g:\alpha_{g}\neq 0\}
. Additionally, the
Hadamard product
is defined as
C\circ D=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}\beta_{g}g
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over a group
G
. Suppose
C=\{g\}
is a basic set. Then for any basic set
D
,
gD
is a basic set.

The following theorem was proved by Wielandt in
[
10
]
when
G
is finite. The general case appeared as
[
1
, Corollary 2.12]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{A}
a subring of
\mathbb{F}[G]
which is spanned by
\{\underline{C}:C\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})\}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space. Then
\mathcal{A}
is a Schur ring over
G
with basic set
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is closed under
\circ
and
*
,
1\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
, and for all
g\in G
there exists some
C\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
such that
g\in\supp(C)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be a function such that
f(0)=0
. For any
\alpha=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}g
, we set

f(\alpha)=\sum_{g\in G}f(\alpha_{g})g.

The following proposition was proved by Wielandt in
[
10
, Proposition 22.3]
. The general case appeared as
[
1
, Propostion 2.4]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over a group
G
and the function be as above. Then

f(\alpha)\in\mathcal{A}
whenever
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
f
take value
1
at one non-zero number and
0
at other numbers. We get the following statement, which is known as the Schur-Wielandt principle; see
[
7
, Corollary 1.10]
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over
G
. For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, suppose
g\in\supp({\alpha})
with
\alpha_{g}=c\neq 0
. The set

\{g\in G:\alpha_{g}=c\}

is an
\mathcal{A}
-set.

The following two propositions, which were first proved by Wielandt in
[
10
]
and were generalized in
[
1
]
, tell us how to generate
\mathcal{A}
-subgroups in any Schur ring
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over
G
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and

{\rm Stab}(\alpha)=\{g\in G:g\alpha=\alpha\}.

Then
{\rm Stab}(\alpha)
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over an infinite group
G
. Then for any basic set
C

{\rm Stab}(C)=\{g\in G:gC=C\}

is a finite
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup.

Proof
. Observe that
gC=C
if and only if
g\underline{C}=\underline{C}
. It then follows from Proposition
2.3
that
{\rm Stab}(C)
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup. Since we are assuming that
C
is a finite set,
{\rm Stab}(C)
is a finite subgroup of
G
.

\square

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over
G
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and
H=\langle\supp(\alpha)\rangle
. Then
H
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup.

Suppose that
H
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup, then
\{C:C\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}),C\subseteq H\}

consists of a basic set of a Schur ring over
H
. It is denoted
\mathcal{A}_{H}
.

When we have normal
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup, we can construct Schur ring over the factor group as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.2]
) Let
\varphi:G\rightarrow H
be a group homomorphism with
{\rm Ker}(\varphi)=K
and
\mathcal{A}
be a Schur ring over
G
. Suppose that
K
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup. Then the image
\varphi(\mathcal{A})
is a Schur ring over
\varphi(G)
where
{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi(\mathcal{A}))=\{\varphi(C):C\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})\}
.

In particular, if
H=G/K
, the factor group of
G
over
K
, and
\varphi
is the natural homomorphism, the corresponding
S
-ring is denoted
\mathcal{A}_{G/K}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
) The only Schur rings over the infinite cyclic group
\langle z\rangle
are either discrete or symmetric Schur ring. Here each basic set of the discrete Schur ring is a singleton set and each basic set of the the symmetric Schur rings has the form
\{z^{i},z^{-i}\}
for some nonnegative integer
i
.

2.2.
Traditional Schur rings

Discrete and trivial Schur rings
.
The group ring
\mathbb{F}G
itself forms a Schur ring over
G
, which is called
the
discrete Schur ring
over
G
. When
G
is a finite group, the trivial partition
\{1,G\}
produces
a Schur ring which is known as the
trivial Schur ring
over
G
.

Tensor products
. Assume that
G=H\times K
,
\mathcal{A}_{H}
and
\mathcal{A}_{K}
are Schur rings over
H
and
K
, respectively. Moreover, the
set

\{CD:C\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{H}),D\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{H})\}

consists of the basic sets of a Schur ring over
G
, which is first proved by Wielandt in
[
9
]
. The corresponding Schur ring is called the
tensor product
of
\mathcal{A}_{H}
and
\mathcal{A}_{K}
.

Orbit Schur rings
. If
\mathcal{K}
is a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, the set of elements of
\mathbb{F}[G]
fixed by
\mathcal{K}
is a Schur ring over G, denoted
\mathbb{F}[G]^{\mathcal{K}}
and called the
orbit Schur ring
associated with
\mathcal{K}
. Obviously, the group ring
\mathbb{F}G
itself also forms an orbit Schur ring.

Wedge products
. A Schur ring
\mathcal{A}
over
G
is a
wedge product
if there exist nontrivial proper
\mathcal{A}
-subgroups
H,K
such that
K\leq H,K\unlhd G
, and every basic set outside
H
is a union of
K
-cosets. In this case, the series

1<K\leq H<G

is called a wedge-decomposition of
\mathcal{A}
. Furthermore, we write
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{A}_{H}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{G/K}
.

A Schur ring over a group
G
is called
traditional
if it is either a trivial Schur ring (when
G
is finite), or an orbit Schur ring, or a tensor product of Schur rings over smaller subgroups, or a wedge product. A group
G
is said to be
traditional
if each Schur ring over it is traditional.

3.
Proof of the main Results

Proof of Proposition
1.1

If
z^{i}s\notin C
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
\mathcal{Z}=\langle C\rangle
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup. Then statement
(i)
follows.

Next, assume that
z^{i}s\in C
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. If there is only a unique
0\neq i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
z^{i}s
is contained in
C
. Observe that
z^{i}s\in C\cap C^{*}
, and we conclude that
C=C^{*}
. This yields that

\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}\cdot z^{i}s=z^{i}s\cdot\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}.

Also, for any odd prime number
p
,

\begin{split}\underline{C^{p}}&={(\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}+z^{i}s)}{(\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}+z^{i}s)}...{(\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}+z^{i}s)}\\
&\equiv({\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}})^{p}+(z^{i}s)^{p}\\
&\equiv({\underline{C\backslash\{z^{i}s\}}})^{(p)}+(z^{i}s)^{p}\\
&={\underline{C}^{(p)}}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(mod~~p),\end{split}

and so
C^{(p)}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-set. This means that
C\subseteq C^{(p)}
as

{(z^{i}s)}^{p}=z^{i}s
, and hence
C=C^{(p)}
because

|C|\leq|C^{(p)}|\leq|C|<\infty.

This implies that
z^{p}\in C
for any odd prime number
p
, which contradicts the finiteness of
C
.
Thus there exists a positive integer
k
greater than or equal to 2 such that

z^{m_{1}}s,z^{m_{2}}s,...,z^{m_{k}}s\in C,

where
m_{1},m_{2},...,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m_{1}<m_{2}<...<m_{k}.
Thus, statements
(ii)
follows.

Now, we will prove conclusion
(iii)
.
Note that

\begin{split}&\underline{C\backslash\{z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s\}}\cdot(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)\\
=&(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)\cdot\underline{C\backslash\{z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s\}}.\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}{\underline{C}}^{2}&=\{\underline{C\backslash\{z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s\}}+(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)\}^{2}\\
&\equiv(\underline{C\backslash\{z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s\}})^{2}+(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)^{2}\\
&\equiv({\underline{C\backslash\{z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s\}}})^{(2)}+(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)^{2}~~~~~~~(mod~~~2).\end{split}

Let
K
denote the set of all elements of
{\underline{C}}^{2}
with odd coefficients. Clearly,
K\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
and is an
\mathcal{A}
-set.
Observe that

\begin{split}&(z^{m_{1}}s+z^{m_{2}}s+...+z^{m_{k}}s)^{2}\\
=&(1+z^{m_{1}-m_{2}}+z^{m_{1}-m_{3}}+...+z^{m_{1}-m_{k}})+\\
&(1+z^{m_{2}-m_{1}}+z^{m_{2}-m_{3}}+...+z^{m_{2}-m_{k}})+...+\\
&(1+z^{m_{k}-m_{1}}+z^{m_{k}-m_{2}}+...+z^{m_{k}-m_{k-1}}),\end{split}

by the assumption in statement
(iii)
, one can see that
m_{k}-m_{1}
is odd and

m_{i+1}-m_{i}>2,i=1,\ldots,k-1.

These facts yield that

\{z^{m_{1}-m_{k}},z^{m_{k}-m_{1}}\}\subseteq K,~\{z^{2},z^{-2}\}\subseteq K.

Therefore
\mathcal{Z}=\langle K\rangle
is an
\mathcal{A}
-set, and hence we complete the proof of Proposition
1.1
.
\square

Proof of Theorem
1.1
In the sequel, we fix the following notation:

G=\mathcal{Z}\rtimes\mathcal{Z}_{2}=\langle z\rangle\rtimes\langle s\rangle,

and
\mathcal{A}
is a Schur ring over
G
such that
\mathcal{Z}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup.

By Theorem
2.2
, we consider the following two:

Case 1.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{Z}]
is a discrete Schur ring.

We claim that each basic set in
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})
is a singleton set. Otherwise there exists
i\neq j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
z^{i}s,z^{j}s\in C
for some basic set
C
. It then follows that
z^{i-j}C
is a basic set by Lemma
2.1
. Observe that

z^{i}s=z^{i-j}\cdot z^{j}s\in z^{i-j}C,

and so
z^{i-j}C=C
. This yields that
z^{i-j}
belongs to
{\rm Stab}(C)
and which would contain the infinite subgroup
\langle z^{i-j}\rangle
. This is a contradiction to Lemma
2.2
.

As a consequence,
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}[G]
.

Case 2.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{Z}]}^{\pm}
is the symmetric Schur ring. Let
D
be the basic set containing
s
.

Subcase 2.1.
Suppose
|D|=1
. It then follows that

{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})=\{\{z^{j}s,z^{-j}s\}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\{z^{j},z^{-j}\}|j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Thus case (ii) in Theorem
1.1
occurs with

.

Subcase 2.2.
Suppose
|D|=2
. Then there exists
i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
such that

.

Claim 1.
There exists a basic set of the form
\{z^{i_{0}}s\}
for some

if and only if
i
is even. Furthermore, if this happens then

\{z^{\frac{i}{2}}s\}
is the unique basic set of size one and

{\mathcal{D}}=\{\{z^{j+\frac{i}{2}}s,z^{\frac{i}{2}-j}s\}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\{z^{j},z^{-j}\}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof
. If
\{z^{i_{0}}s\}
is a basic set for some

, applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain that

\{s,z^{2i_{0}}s\}=\{z^{i_{0}}s\}\{z^{-i_{0}},z^{i_{0}}\}

is a basic set. This yields that

D=\{s,z^{2i_{0}}s\},

and hence
2i_{0}=i
. This yields that
i
must be even and
\{z^{\frac{i}{2}}s\}
is the unique basic set of size one consisting of nonidentity.

Conversely, assume that
i
is even. Then

(s+z^{i}s)(z^{\frac{i}{2}}+z^{-\frac{i}{2}})=z^{\frac{3i}{2}}s+z^{\frac{i}{2}}s+2z^{\frac{i}{2}}s

belongs to
\mathcal{A}
. Since
i\neq 0
, we obtain that
\{z^{\frac{i}{2}}s\}
is a basic set by Corollary

. Applying Lemma

, one can see in this case that

{\mathcal{D}}=\{\{z^{j+\frac{i}{2}}s,z^{\frac{i}{2}-j}s\}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\}\cup\{\{z^{j},z^{-j}\}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\}.

This completes the proof of Claim

.

\square

Now we may assume that
i
is odd. Then every basic set of nonidentity elements has size at least
2
by Claim 1. For any

, then both

\{z^{j}s,z^{i+j}s,z^{-j}s,z^{i-j}s\}=\{z^{j},z^{-j}\}\{s,z^{i}s\},

Subcase 2.3.
Suppose
|D|\geq 3
. Then there are
i\neq j\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
such that
z^{i}s
and
z^{j}s
belong to
D
.
Observe that
C=\{z^{i},z^{-i}\}
is a basic set by the assumption. Since
s\in CD\cap D
, one can see that
D\subseteq CD
.

Claim 2.
The structure constant
\lambda_{CDD}=1
.

Proof of Theorem

Now let

Let
i_{1},i_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
2\nmid i_{1},2\nmid i_{2}

2|(i_{1}-i_{2})
. So,

&2(z^{i_{1}-i_{2}}+z^{i_{2}-i_{1}})+2(z^{i_{1}-i_{2}}s+z^{i_{2}-i_{1}}s)\in\mathcal{A}.\end{split}

Choose
i_{3},i_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
2\nmid i_{3},2\mid i_{4}
, then
2\nmid(i_{3}-i_{4})
. One can easily see that

\begin{split}&(z^{i_{3}}+z^{-i_{3}}+z^{i_{3}}s+z^{-i_{3}}s)(z^{i_{4}}+z^{-i_{4}})\\

Abstract
. We consider inductive systems of C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras with completely positive contractive connecting maps. We define a condition, called C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -encoding, which is sufficient for the limit of the system to be completely order isomorphic to a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra and hence guarantees a unique C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra associated to the limit. When the system consists of finite-dimensional C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras, this condition is also necessary and thus characterizes when the limit is completely order isomorphic to a (nuclear) C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra. C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -encoding systems generalize the NF systems of Blackadar and Kirchberg and the CPC ∗ \mathrm{CPC}^{*} -systems of the author and Winter. Moreover, any system of completely positive approximations of a nuclear C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra gives rise to a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -encoding system. Consequently a separable C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra is nuclear if and only if it is completely order isomorphic to the limit of a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -encoding system. This gives an inductive limit description of all separable nuclear C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras equivalent to the recent construction of the author and Winter but without the additional structure of order zero maps. Without these extra structural requirements, one can easily construct examples of our systems, which we demonstrate for all amenable group C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras.

Introduction

Inductive limit constructions are ubiquitous in operator algebras because they enable the transfer of structural information from relatively well-behaved building blocks to more general algebras. This transfer is particularly well utilized in the classification of von Neumann and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (see
[
9
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
to name a few). To do so, one must first discern whether a given algebra is isomorphic to one arising from an inductive limit.
The definitive result in this direction for von Neumann algebras was established in
[
7
]
,
where Connes showed that all injective, all amenable, and all semi-discrete von Neumann algebras contain an ultraweak
∗
-dense nested sequence of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras, i.e., they are all approximately finite-dimensional (called AFD or hyperfinite). The direct analogue in the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-setting fails:
Any
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra which arises as the inductive limit of finite-dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (called an AF algebra) is nuclear, where nuclearity is considered the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-analogue to amenability and semi-discreteness, but most nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are not AF.

Nonetheless, generalizations of inductive systems of finite-dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
- algebras have proven to be a powerful tool in the study of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. One way to generalize is to weaken the finite-dimensional assumption on the algebras,
giving rise to systems of (sub)homogeneous
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
Another route is to instead weaken the assumptions on the connecting maps of the system, giving rise to the NF systems of Blackadar and Kirchberg in
[
2
]
and more recently the
\mathrm{CPC}^{*}
-systems introduced by the author and Winter in
[
8
]

It is this route we pursue here.

Classically, an inductive system consists of a sequence of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
(A_{n})_{n}
together with connecting
∗
-homomorphisms
\rho_{n+1,n}\colon A_{n}\to A_{n+1}
. These induce
∗
-homomorphisms
\rho_{n}\colon A_{n}\to\prod_{m}A_{m}/\bigoplus_{m}A_{m}
into the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of norm bounded sequences modulo null convergent sequences, and the inductive limit of the system is simply the norm closure of these images
\overline{\bigcup_{n}\rho_{n}(A_{n})}
. To generalize this construction,
we relax the assumptions on the connecting maps from
∗
-homomorphisms to completely positive and contractive (c.p.c.) maps.
Though not necessarily multiplicative, c.p.c. maps are
∗
-linear and positivity preserving (even up to matrix amplifications) and hence still preserve much of the structure of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. We call a system
(A_{n},\rho_{n+1,n})_{n}
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with c.p.c. connecting maps
a
c.p.c. system
,
and define the limit exactly as in the classical setting,
except now the induced maps
\rho_{n}\colon A_{n}\to\prod_{m}A_{m}/\bigoplus_{m}A_{m}
are only c.p.c., and the limit
\overline{\bigcup_{n}\rho_{n}(A_{n})}
is only a closed self-adjoint subspace.

A key source of examples of c.p.c. systems come from c.p.c. approximations of (separable) nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. From
[
6
,
11
]
, we know that a (separable)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is nuclear precisely when it admits a system of c.p.c. approximations consisting of a sequence
(F_{n})_{n}
of finite-dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and c.p.c. maps
A\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}F_{n}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{n}}A
such that
\varphi_{n}\circ\psi_{n}
converges pointwise in norm to
\mathrm{id}_{A}
. The sequence
(F_{n})_{n}
together with the maps
\rho_{n+1,n}\coloneqq\psi_{n+1}\circ\varphi_{n}\colon F_{n}\to F_{n+1}
form a c.p.c. system, which somehow encodes our original
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
. In particular, the limit of the system is completely order isomorphic to
A
. A complete order isomorphism between closed self-adjoint subspaces of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras is a completely positive, completely isometric map with completely positive inverse. These are extremely robust identifications. For example, a complete order isomorphism between
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras is automatically a
∗
-isomorphism, and so the complete order isomorphism class of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra determines its
∗
-isomorphism class.

The question now is when does a given c.p.c. system actually encode a (nuclear)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra? In this article, we answer this question by establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for the limit of a c.p.c. system to be completely order isomorphic to a (nuclear)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. These are built around the following definition.

\displaystyle u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{11}&u_{12}\\

\displaystyle=\rho_{m,j}(x_{j}a_{j})-\rho_{m,n}\big(\rho_{n,j}(x_{j})\rho_{n,j}(a_{j})\big)

in (

) for this

\displaystyle=\|u_{12}\|

Definition 4.1
.

Let

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and

(A\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}F_{n}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{n}}A)_{n}

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}\circ\psi_{m}\circ\varphi_{n}\|<\varepsilon_{m}.

Remarks 4.2
.

(i)
In essence, summability guarantees that the maps

\psi_{m}\circ\varphi_{n}\colon F_{n}\to F_{m}

sufficiently large.
Indeed, since the unit ball of each

is compact,
we may choose for any

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{m,n}-\psi_{m}\circ\varphi_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{m-1}\circ\rho_{m-1,n}-\varphi_{n}\|<\textstyle{\sum}_{j=n+1}^{m-1}\varepsilon_{j}<\varepsilon.

A key upshot is that for any

and

\displaystyle a_{x}\coloneqq\lim_{n}\varphi_{n}(\rho_{n,k}(x)).

Another upshot is that

. Indeed, using (

\displaystyle<\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon.

-algebra

5.
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding systems for amenable group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

One advantage of our construction is how readily we can construct examples of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding systems. In this section we show how to build a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding system for the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra for any countable discrete amenable group using classic c.p.c. approximations via Følner sequences (as in
[
4
, Theorem 2.6.8]
).
We shall also see that, at least for any non-torsion group, the resulting c.p.c. system is never NF or
\mathrm{CPC}^{*}
.

Let
G
be a countable amenable discrete group with reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)\subset B(\ell^{2}(G))
induced by the left regular representation
\lambda:G\to B(\ell^{2}(G))
with
\lambda(g)=\lambda_{g}\in\mathcal{U}(\ell^{2}(G))
given by
\lambda_{g}\delta_{h}=\delta_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
. Recall that a
Følner sequence
for
G
is a sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
of finite subsets of
G
so that for any
s\in G

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{n}\Delta s\mathcal{F}_{n}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{n}|}=2-2\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{n}|}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

We will call a Følner sequence
summable
if there exists a decreasing sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})^{1}_{+}
so that for all
m>n\geq 0

(5.1)

\displaystyle\max_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}\left(1-\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap gh^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{m}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{m}|}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{n}|<\varepsilon_{m}.

Given any Følner sequence we can find a summable subsequence.

Now, from a summable Følner sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
for
G
with respect to a decreasing sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})_{+}^{1}
, we construct a summable system of c.p.c. approximations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
following
[
4
, Theorem 2.6.8]
: For each
n\geq 1
, let
P_{n}\colon\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(G)
be the projection onto the span of
\{\delta_{g}\mid g\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
and identify
P_{n}B(\ell^{2}(G))P_{n}
with
M_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
with canonical matrix units
\{e_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}
. Define
\psi_{n}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)\to M_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
on
\lambda(G)\subset\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
by

(5.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\lambda_{s})=P_{n}\lambda_{s}P_{n}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}}e_{r,s^{-1}r},

for
s\in G
, and
\varphi_{n}:M_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
on matrix units by

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(e_{g,h})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{n}|}\lambda_{gh^{-1}}

for
g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
These maps are u.c.p., and the Følner condition guarantees that this is a system of c.p.c. approximations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2.6.8]
for a proof). To see that the system is summable, we note that for each
s\in G
and
m\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}(\psi_{m}(\lambda_{s}))=\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{m}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{m}|}\lambda_{s},

and we approximate for
m>n\geq 0

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}\circ\psi_{m}\circ\varphi_{n}\|

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq: sum Fol}}}{\leq}|\mathcal{F}_{n}|^{2}\max_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}\|\varphi_{n}(e_{g,h})-\varphi_{m}\circ\psi_{m}\circ\varphi_{n}(e_{g,h})\|

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq: sum Fol}}}{=}|\mathcal{F}_{n}|^{2}\max_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}\left\|\left(1-\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap gh^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{m}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{m}|}\right)\frac{\lambda_{gh^{-1}}}{|\mathcal{F}_{n}|}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: sum Fol}}{<}\varepsilon_{m}.

It follows that the system of approximations is summable and so by
Theorem

4.5
the associated system
(\mathrm{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}},\psi_{n+1}\circ\varphi_{n})_{n}
is
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding, and the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-limit is isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
.

We indicated in the introduction that such systems are generally not NF or
\mathrm{CPC}^{*}
, and it remains to justify this assertion. Since our maps are all u.c.p., these two notions coincide, and so we focus our attention on NF systems and begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
A\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}F_{n}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{n}}A
is a system of summable c.p.c. approximations of a separable nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. The associated system is asymptotically multiplicative if and only if the sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n}
is approximately multiplicative in the sense that
\lim_{n}\|\psi_{n}(ab)-\psi_{n}(a)\psi_{n}(b)\|=0
for all
a,b\in A
.

Proof.

First we assume the sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n}
is approximately multiplicative. Let
k\geq 0
,
x,y\in F_{k}
, and
\varepsilon>0
, and let
a_{x},a_{y}\in A
as in (
4.2
).
Section

4
guarantees an
M>k
so that (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) hold for
\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}
for all
m>n>M
. By approximate multiplicativity, we can choose
N>M
so that
\|\psi_{m}(a_{x}a_{y})-\psi_{m}(a_{x})\psi_{m}(a_{y})\|<\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}
for all
m>N
. Then for all
m>n>N
, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{m,n}(\rho_{n,k}(x)\rho_{n,k}(y))-\rho_{m,k}(x)\rho_{m,k}(y)\|

\displaystyle<\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}+\|\psi_{m}(a_{x}a_{y})-\psi_{m}(a_{x})\psi_{m}(a_{y})\|<\varepsilon.

Hence the associated system
(F_{n},\rho_{n+1,n})_{n}
is asymptotically multiplicative.

Now assume
(\psi_{n})_{n}
is not approximately multiplicative.
By Remark
4
(i) there exist
k\geq 0
,
x,y\in F_{k}
that witness this, i.e., there exist
k\geq 0
,
x,y\in F_{k}
, and
\varepsilon>0
so that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an
m>n
with
\|\psi_{m}(a_{x}a_{y})-\psi_{m}(a_{x})\psi_{m}(a_{y})\|>\varepsilon
.

Section

4
guarantees an
M>k
so that (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) hold for
\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}
for all
m>n>M
, which gives

\displaystyle\|\rho_{m,n}(\rho_{n,k}(x)\rho_{n,k}(y))-\rho_{m,k}(x)\rho_{m,k}(y)\|<\|\psi_{m}(a_{x}a_{y})-\psi_{m}(a_{x})\psi_{m}(a_{y})\|+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}.

for all
m>n>M
. Now for any
n>M
, there exists an
m>n
such that
\|\psi_{m}(a_{x}a_{y})-\psi_{m}(a_{x})\psi_{m}(a_{y})\|>\varepsilon
, and so

\|\rho_{m,n}(\rho_{n,k}(x)\rho_{n,k}(y))-\rho_{m,k}(x)\rho_{m,k}(y)\|>\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}.

It follows that the system
(F_{n},\rho_{n+1,n})_{n}
is not asymptotically multiplicative.
∎

Of course some amenable groups, such as finite groups, will admit a summable system of c.p.c. approximations from a Følner sequence as above with
(\psi_{n})_{n}
approximately multiplicative, but it turns out this will never hold for many groups.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete amenable group with summable Følner sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
, and let
\big(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}\mathrm{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{n}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)\big)_{n}
be the summable approximation for
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)
derived above. If
G
contains an element with infinite order, then the maps
(\psi_{n})_{n}
are not approximately multiplicative, and the associated
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding system is neither NF nor
\mathrm{CPC}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
s\in G
with infinite order. It follows in particular that
|\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}|<|\mathcal{F}_{n}|
for all
n\geq 0
.
Then using (
5.2
) we compute for any
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}(\lambda_{s}^{*}\lambda_{s})-\psi_{n}(\lambda_{s}^{*})\psi_{n}(\lambda_{s})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|1_{\mathrm{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}}-\left(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}}e_{s^{-1}r,r}\right)\left(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}}e_{r,s^{-1}r}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|1_{\mathrm{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}}-\sum_{r\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap s\mathcal{F}_{n}}e_{s^{-1}r,s^{-1}r}\right\|

\displaystyle=1.

It follows from
Section

5
that the associated
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-encoding system

(\mathrm{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}},\psi_{n+1}\circ\varphi_{n})_{n}
is not NF, and since the maps
\rho_{n+1,n}=\psi_{n+1}\circ\varphi_{n}
are all u.c.p., the system is also not
\mathrm{CPC}^{*}
.
∎

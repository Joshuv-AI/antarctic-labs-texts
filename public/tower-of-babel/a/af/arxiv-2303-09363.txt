Asymptotic formulas involving Cohen-Ramanujan expansions
Arya Chandran, K Vishnu Namboothiri

Abstract
. Some necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of Cohen-Ramanujan expansions for arithmetical functions were provided by these authors in [ arXive preprint arXive:2205.08466 , 2022]. Given two arithmetical functions f f and g g with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions, we derive an asymptotic formula for ∑ n ≤ N f ⁡ ( n ) ​ g ​ ( n + h ) \sum_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h) where h h is a fixed positive integer. We also provide Cohen-Ramanujan expansions for certain functions to illustrate some of the results we prove consequently.

1.
Introduction

Srinivasa Ramanujan introduced the following trigonometric sum

(1)

\displaystyle c_{r}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{r}}

in
[
18
]
and used this sum to derive infinite Fourier series like expansions for several arithmetical functions in the form
\sum\limits_{r}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
. The trigonometric sum (
1
) now known as the Ramanujan sum has several interesting properties. For example it is multiplicative in
r
, but not so in
n
. To see the other properties in detail, please see
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
or
[
20
]
. When such a Fourier series like expansion exists for an arithmetical function (mostly only pointwise convergent), the function is said to possess a Ramanujan-Fourier series expansion or simply a Ramanujan expansion. Ramanujan expansions were computed for various functions by many authors. Various conditions were provided for the existence of Ramanujan expansions in papers like
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
15
]
.

Ramanujan sum has been generalized in many directions. For example, in
[
4
]
, E. Cohen defined the sum

(2)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=1\\
{(h,r^{s})_{s}=1}\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi inh}{r^{s}}},

generalizing the Ramanujan sum. For a positive integer
s
, integers
m,n
, not both zero, the generalized GCD of
m
and
n
denoted by
(m,n)_{s}
is the largest
l^{s}
(where
l\in\mathbb{N}
) dividing both
m
and
n
. If
(m,n)_{s}=1
,
m
and
n
are said to be relatively
s
-prime to each other. When
s=1
, generalized GCD becomes the usual GCD and so the sum (
2
) reduces to the usual Ramanujan sum in that case.
We will refer to the sum (
2
) by the name Cohen-Ramanujan sum. Just like the usual Ramanujan sum, for certain specific values of
s
and
n
, this sum transforms into various well known arithmetical functions. For example,
c_{r}^{s}(0)=\Phi_{s}(r^{s})=J_{s}(r)
, where
\Phi_{s}
is the Klee’s function and
J_{s}
is the Jordan totient function. Please see the next section for the definitions of these two functions. Cohen derived various properties of this sum and proved several results using this generalization in a series of papers
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

E. Cohen
[
7
]
himself gave another generalization of the Ramanujan sum using
k
-vectors. A
k
-vector is an ordered set
\{x_{i}\}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
of
k
integers. Two
k
-vectors
\{x_{i}\}
and
\{y_{i}\}
are congruent (mod
k,r
) if
x_{i}\equiv y_{i}
(mod
r
),
i=1,\ldots,k
. For
k\geq 1
, Cohen defined

(3)

\displaystyle c^{k}(n,r)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{(\{x_{i}\},r)=1}e^{\frac{2\pi in(x_{1}+x_{2}+\ldots x_{k})}{r}},

where
\{x_{i}\}
ranges over a residue system (mod
k,r
).

Based on this sum, Sivaramakrishnan in
[
19
]
gave the infinite series expansion

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{J_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\zeta(s+k)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(r)c^{k}(n,r)}{J_{s+k}(r)},

where
s
and
k
are positive integers with
s+k\geq 2
.

Another generalization of the Ramanujan sum, and a list of references to some more generalizations can be found in
[
21
]
. Later, more generalizations were derived in
[
17
]
. Recently, Haukkanen and McCarthy gave another generalization in
[
11
]
.

An arithmetical function
f
is said to admit a Cohen-Ramanujan expansion

\displaystyle f(n)=\sum\limits_{r}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n),

if the series on the right hand side converges for suitable complex numbers
\widehat{f}(r)
. A study on the Cohen-Ramanujan expansions of some arithmetical functions were conducted by these authors in
[
3
]
. Some necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of such expansions were also given there. To the best of our knowledge, such series expansions are not available for any other generalization of the Ramanujan sum as of now.

In this paper, we use the Cohen-Ramanujan expansion in the context initiated by the attempts of H. Gadiyar and R. Padma in
[
8
]
. Hardy derived a Ramanujan expansion
[
8
, Identity 2.23]
for the function
\Lambda_{1}(n)=(\phi(n)/n)\Lambda(n)
where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function defined as

(5)

\displaystyle\Lambda(n)=\begin{cases}\log p,\text{ if $n=p^{k}$ , $p$ is prime and $k$ any positive integer}\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
a
is an arithmetical function with the Ramanujan expansion

(6)

\displaystyle a(n)=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}a_{r}c_{r}(n)

then the Wiener–Khintchine theorem for an arithmetical function
a

[
8
, Section 3.2]
can be stated as

(7)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n\leq qN}a(n)a(n+h)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}a_{r}^{2}c_{r}(h)

provided that certain convergence conditions are satisfied.

H. Gadiyar and R. Padma showed that Hardy-Littlewood conjecture on twin primes can be proved if the Wiener–Khintchine theorem can be applied to the Ramanujan expansion of the arithmetical function
\Lambda_{1}
provided that the expansion is uniformly and absolutely convergent. They derived the formula

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\phi(r)}c_{r}(h),

which agrees with the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture. But note that this identity is valid only if the Ramanujan expansion derived by Hardy is absolutely convergent, which is not actually the case. Taking forward these discussions, H. Gadiyar, M. Ram Murthy and R. Padma in
[
9
]
derived an asymptotic formula for the sum

\sum\limits_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h),

where
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions.

Here, we try to derive an asymptotic formula for the sum
\sum\limits_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h)
,
where
f
and
g
are arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions and satisfy some additional conditions. Therefore, our results extend the results in
[
9
]
. We also provide some examples to demonstrate our results. The main results we propose in this paper are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{(s)}(n)
and
g(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(k)c_{k}^{(s)}(n)
respectively. Suppose that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(k)\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Then as
N
tends to infinity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)\Phi_{s}(r^{s})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{(s)}(n)
and
g(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(k)c_{k}^{(s)}(n)
respectively. Suppose that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Then as
N
tends to infinity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)
.

Given an arithmetical function
f
, if we write
f_{h}(n)=f(n+h)
, we answer a natural question that if
f
has a Cohen-Ramanujan expansion, then does
f_{h}
also have one?

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
f
has an absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansion with coefficients
\widehat{f}(q)
satisfying
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(q)|\tau(q)<\infty
. Then
f_{h}
has an absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansion with coefficients
\frac{\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}
.

Notations and basic results

Most of the notations, functions and identities we use in this paper are standard and their definitions can be found in

As usual
[m,n]

m,n

(m,n)[m,n]=mn

(m^{s},n^{s})_{s}[m^{s},n^{s}]=m^{s}n^{s}

The Jordan totient function

s

The Klee’s function
\Phi_{s}
is defined
to give the cardinality of the set
\{m\in\mathbb{N}:1\leq m\leq n,(m,n)_{s}=1\}
.
Note that
\Phi_{1}=\varphi
, the Euler totient function. It is known that
[
19
, Section V.3]

n^{s}=\sum\limits_{d|n}J_{s}(d)
and
J_{s}(n)=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})
. Hence

(8)

\displaystyle n^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|n}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})=\sum\limits_{d^{s}|n^{s}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s}).

By
\tau_{s}(n)
where
s,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we mean the number of
l^{s}
dividing
n
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
.
\zeta(s)
denotes the Riemann zeta function which is defined by the equation
\zeta(s)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}
for

.

\zeta(z)=\prod\limits_{p}\frac{1}{1-p^{-z}}

Let

The following lemma is essential to prove one of the main results that we propose in this paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

[

For any

For an arithmetical function

, its mean value is defined by
M(f)=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\leq x}\end{subarray}}f(n)

.
We prove a lemma using elementary techniques.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
h>0
,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d\mid n\\
e\mid n\end{subarray}}1=\llbracket\frac{N}{[d,e]}\rrbracket

and
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d\mid n+h\\
e\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1\leq\llbracket\frac{N+h}{[d,e]}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

The first sum is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d\mid n\\
e\mid n\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
[d,e]\mid n\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
k\mid n\end{subarray}}1,\text{ where }k=[d,e]

\displaystyle=\llbracket\frac{N}{k}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\llbracket\frac{N}{[d,e]}\rrbracket

Now
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d\mid n+h\\
e\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
[d,e]\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
k\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1
, where
k=[d,e]
. Here
k
runs through the divisors
k,2k,3k,\ldots,mk
where
mk\leq N+h
. Therefore number of such
k
’s
=m\leq\frac{N+h}{k}
. Since
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\leq\llbracket\frac{N+h}{k}\rrbracket
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For
n\in N
, the number of divisors
d
of
n
with a fixed
\delta
as a factor of

is
\tau(\frac{n}{\delta})
.

Proof.

We have to find the number of

such that

and
\delta\mid d
. That is

for some integer

. Which implies,

.
Thus we get number of such

\frac{n}{\delta}=tk
, which is exactly
\tau(\frac{n}{\delta}).

∎

3.
Proofs of the Main Results

We start by proving three easy lemmas essential in the proof of our main results.

Lemma 3.1
.

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)\leq N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
d^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{r}{d})d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid k\\
e^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{e})e^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{r}{d})\mu(\frac{k}{e})d^{s}e^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d^{s}\mid k\\
e^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{r}{d})\mu(\frac{k}{e})d^{s}e^{s}\llbracket\frac{N}{[d^{s},e^{s}]}\rrbracket\text{\hskip 28.45274pt(by Lemma \ref{l3}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}d^{s}e^{s}\frac{N}{[d^{s},e^{s}]}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}\hskip 5.69046pt(\text{since }(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}[d^{s},e^{s}]=d^{s}e^{s})

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\hskip 28.45274pt\text{(by equation }(\ref{phi-reltn}))

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\\
e^{s}\mid k^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid d^{s}\\
\delta^{s}\mid e^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s}).

When we take the sum
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid d^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})
, each
\delta
is repeated
\tau(\frac{r^{s}}{\delta^{s}})
times.
Now

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\\
e^{s}\mid k^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\tau_{s}(\frac{r^{s}}{\delta^{s}})\tau_{s}(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}})

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})

\displaystyle=N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})

\displaystyle=N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{(by equation }(\ref{phi-reltn})).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)=\delta_{rk}Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}k^{s}\log r^{s}k^{s})
, where
\delta_{rk}
is the Kronecker delta.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ib(n+h)}{k^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ibh}{k^{s}}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}})n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ibh}{k^{s}}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}.

Now we have two cases.

Case 1 :
\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In this case

\displaystyle\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow r^{s}k^{s}\mid ak^{s}+br^{s}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow ak^{s}+br^{s}=tr^{s}k^{s},\text{ for some }t\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow br^{s}=tr^{s}k^{s}-ak^{s}.

Since
k^{s}\mid RHS
and
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1
,
k\mid r
. Using similar arguments, we get
r\mid k
, which implies
r=k
. Now we have
r^{s}\mid a+b
. Since
1\leq a<r^{s}
and
1\leq b<r^{s}
,
r^{s}=a+b
.
Then
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}1=N.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=Nc_{r}^{s}(h).

Case 2 :
\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}}\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

In this case
e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}\neq 1
. Let us write
N=(r^{s}k^{s})Q+R
, where
0\leq R<r^{s}k^{s}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|

\displaystyle=|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq(r^{s}k^{s})Q\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|

\displaystyle=|0+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|.

Now

\displaystyle|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|

\displaystyle=|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\|\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\|}\text{\hskip 28.45274pt(by Lemma \ref{l2}}).

This is because,
e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{r^{s}k^{s}}}
is a proper non-trivial root of unity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{r^{s}k^{s}}}=0
if
l\neq 0
.

\displaystyle|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}=1}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}=1}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ibh}{ks}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}=1}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}rk>U\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{(s)}(n)c_{k}^{(s)}(n+h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}rk>U\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|N^{\frac{1}{2}}(N+|h|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})\text{( by Lemma \ref{lem3})}

\displaystyle\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}(N+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}rk>U\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s}).

Given that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}rk>U\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Thus we get
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}rk>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(k)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{(s)}(n)c_{k}^{(s)}(n+h)=O(N)
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{2}\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(U^{s}\log U^{s})+O(N)

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{2}\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(U^{s}\log U^{s})

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)-N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{2}>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(U^{s}\log U^{s}).

Now we have
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Therefore
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(r)|r^{s}\tau_{s}(r^{s})^{2}<\infty
. Now

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)|

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)r^{s}\tau_{s}(r^{s})|

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)r^{s}\tau_{s}(r^{s})^{2}|.

By comparison test,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(k)c_{r}^{s}(h)
converges absolutely.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(U^{s}\log U^{s})+O(N)

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(U^{s}\log U^{s}).

Thus we get
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h))
.
∎

Theorem 3.5

p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}})

\displaystyle=N\prod\limits_{p\mid m}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})+\frac{p^{s}-1}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big)\prod\limits_{p\nmid m}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})-\frac{1}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big).

Lemma 3.7
.

For
h\leq N
,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k)
.

Proof.

Cosider the sum

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{d})d^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{d})d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{d})d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+h\leq N+h\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{d})d^{s}\left[\left[\frac{N+h}{d^{s}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{k}{d})d^{s}\frac{N+h}{d^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq(N+h)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}1\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq(N+h)\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k)

\displaystyle\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k).

∎

If
d\mid r
and
t\mid r
, then by the orthogonality relation,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}c_{d}^{s}(m)c_{t}^{s}(m)=\begin{cases}\Phi_{s}(d^{s}),\text{ if }d=t\\
0,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

This orthogonality relation allows us to write down the possible candidates for the Cohen-Ramanujan coefficients of any given arithmetical function. Indeed if
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)\text{ then }f(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n).

So we get,

\displaystyle\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}f(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(r)\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\Phi_{s}(r^{s}).

\displaystyle\text{ Therefore, }\widehat{f}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{M(fc_{r}^{s})}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})},

where by
M(f)
we mean the limit
\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}f(n)
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h).

Now consider the first term in this summation.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\left[\delta_{r,q}Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}q^{s}\log(r^{s}q^{s}))\right]\hskip 14.22636pt\text{(by Lemma }\ref{lem2})

\displaystyle=Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s})).

Second sum is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\tau(q)\hskip 14.22636pt({\text{by Lemma \ref{lem4}}}).

Since
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(q)|\tau(q)<\infty
,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)=O(N).

Therefore
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)=N\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s}))+O(N)
. Now

\displaystyle M(f_{h}c_{r}^{s})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f_{h}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left[N\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s}))+O(N)\right]

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h).

Now by orthogonality relation, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{h}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{M(f_{h}c_{r}^{s})}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}.

∎

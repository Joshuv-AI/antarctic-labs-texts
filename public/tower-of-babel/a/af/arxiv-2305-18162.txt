Diffusion enhancement and Taylor dispersion for rotationally symmetric flows in discs and pipes
Michele Coti Zelati, Michele Dolce, Chia-Chun Lo

Abstract
. In this note, we study the long-time dynamics of passive scalars driven by rotationally symmetric flows. We focus on identifying precise conditions on the velocity field in order to prove enhanced dissipation and Taylor dispersion in three-dimensional infinite pipes. As a byproduct of our analysis, we obtain an enhanced decay for circular flows on a disc of arbitrary radius.

35Q35, 47B44, 76F25

1.
Introduction

This note considers the evolution of a passive scalar
f
in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
,
that is advected by an external velocity field
{\boldsymbol{v}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is undergoing molecular diffusion.
Our interest is to study quantitatively how the combined effect of diffusion and advection leads to faster time-scales of
homogenization for
f
compared to the case when only diffusion is present. The passive scalar satisfies the advection-diffusion equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\nabla f=\nu\Delta f,\qquad{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
f|_{t=0}=f^{in},\qquad\partial_{{\boldsymbol{n}}}f|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

where
\nu>0
is the diffusion coefficient and
{\boldsymbol{n}}
is the outward unit normal to
{\partial}\Omega
. We are interested in the regime
where
\nu\ll 1
, in which dissipative effects are observed on large time-scales of order
O(\nu^{-1})
. Since the average over
the domain is conserved, we always assume that
\fint_{\Omega}f\,{}\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=0
. The domain
\Omega
will either be a
disc of radius
R>0
, denoted by
D
, or the infinite pipe
D\times\mathbb{R}
, and the velocity field is respectively

(1.2)

{\boldsymbol{v}}=rv(r)\hat{{\mathsf{e}}}_{\theta},\quad\text{in }D,\qquad{\boldsymbol{v}}=v(r)\hat{{\mathsf{e}}}_{z}\quad\text{in }D\times\mathbb{R}.

Here
\hat{{\mathsf{e}}}_{\theta}
and
\hat{{\mathsf{e}}}_{z}
are the unit vectors in the angular direction in
D
and in the vertical direction in
D\times\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
This situation and similar have been recently studied in
[
8
,
7
,
6
]
, in analogy with the case of passive scalars advected by shear flows
[
4
,
1
]
.

The purpose of this short note is twofold: one the one hand, we identify precise conditions on the velocity field in the pipe
setting that guarantee the
enhanced dissipation

[
5
]
and
Taylor dispersion

[
10
,
11
,
2
]
mechanisms. On the other hand, we derive
a
dissipation enhancement
result in the disc for a general class of radial velocity fields. In particular, we will assume the following
for the profile
v(r)
of the velocity field in (
1.2
).

Assumption 1
.

The first
m
derivatives of
v:[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}
do not vanish
simultaneously; that is,

(1.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{m}\left\lvert v^{(n)}(r)\right\rvert\neq 0

for every
0\leq r\leq R
.

We now state our main results, considering differently the pipe and the disc cases.

1.1.
Pipe parallel flows

When
\Omega=D\times\mathbb{R}
, the equation (
1.1
) can be written in cylindrical coordinates as

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v(r)\partial_{z}f=\nu\left(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r})+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2}\right)f,&(r,\theta,z)\in[0,R]\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},\\
f|_{t=0}=f^{in},\qquad\partial_{{\boldsymbol{n}}}f|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

Taking the partial Fourier transform along the
axial coordinate
z
on both sides of the equation, we see then that
for each
k\in\mathbb{R}
the Fourier component

(1.5)

\hat{f}_{k}(t,r,\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t,r,\theta,z){\rm e}^{-ikz}\,{}\mathrm{d}z

satisfies the equation

(1.6)

\partial_{t}\hat{f}_{k}+ikv(r)\hat{f}_{k}=\nu\big(\Delta_{r,\theta}-k^{2}\big)\hat{f}_{k},

where we denote

(1.7)

\Delta_{r,\theta}:=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r})+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}.

In particular,
each Fourier mode
\hat{f}_{k}
evolves independently from all the others,
so in the following, it suffices to consider the
equation (
1.6
) for a fixed parameter
k
. A further reduction can be made by considering the function

(1.8)

g_{k}={\rm e}^{\nu k^{2}t}\hat{f}_{k}

that satisfies

(1.9)

\partial_{t}g_{k}+ikv(r)g_{k}=\nu\Delta_{r,\theta}g_{k}.

Notice that
g_{k}
already incorporates the diffusion along the channel. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
v:[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
Assumption
1
and let
k\neq 0
.
Then, there exist constants
c_{1},C_{1}>0
, independent of
\nu,k
, such that for all initial data

g_{k}^{in}\in L^{2}(D)
the solution to (
1.9
) satisfies

(1.10)

\left\lVert g_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}{\Lambda_{\nu,k}}t}\left\lVert g^{in}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(D)}\quad\text{where}\quad\Lambda_{\nu,k}=\begin{cases}\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{m+2}},&\text{if $0<\nu\leq\left\lvert k\right\rvert$,}\\
\frac{k^{2}}{\nu},&\text{if $0<\left\lvert k\right\rvert\leq{\nu}$},\end{cases}

for every
t\geq 0
.
For the solution to
(
1.6
) with initial data
g_{k}^{in}=\hat{f}^{in}_{k}
, we have the estimate

(1.11)

\|\hat{f}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{1}{\rm e}^{-(\nu k^{2}+c_{1}{\Lambda_{\nu,k}})t}\|\hat{f}_{k}^{in}\|_{L^{2}(D)},

for every
t\geq 0
.

The bounds in the theorem above are analogous to the ones obtained in
[
7
]

for multi-dimensional shear flows. In fact, they also proved the same result as in Theorem

1
for the velocity field
v(r)=1-r^{m}
in the disc of radius
1
. However, a general
condition analogous to that required in Assumption
1
was not identified. The proof of Theorem
1
, given in Section
2
,
is inspired by the arguments in
[
7
]
. In particular, we obtain (
1.10
) as a consequence of a
pseudospectral lower bound and the application of a result of Wei
[
12
]
*Theorem 1.3, see also Theorem
3
below.

Having at hand the
k
by
k
estimate (
1.10
), we are also able to quantify precisely the time-decay for the solution to the original problem (
1.4
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
f^{in}\in L^{1}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}
, and let

v:[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy Assumption
1
. Then, there exist constants
c_{2},C_{2}>0
, independent of

\nu
, such that the solution to (
1.4
) satisfies

(1.12)

\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}\leq C_{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{t}}+\frac{{\rm e}^{-c_{2}\nu t}}{t}\right)\left\lVert f^{in}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}.

for every
t\geq 0
.

With (
1.12
) we have a precise quantification of the Taylor dispersion mechanism for the problem at hand, see also
[
3
]
where these types of bounds were obtained in another context. The polynomial decay rate is analogous to the standard heat equation with diffusivity coefficient
\nu^{-1}
. In particular, the presence of the advection allows us to prove the polynomial decay on a time-scale
O(1)
, which is much faster than
O(\nu^{-1})
we would get without advection.

The choice of the norms on which to quantify the decay is rather natural from the available
k
by
k
bounds. From a physical point of view, the flow is stretching the concentration towards spatial infinity in the
z
-direction. Combining this with enough integrability in
z
, the stretching generated by the flow makes the concentration intersect smaller sets in the discs orthogonal to
z
, so that a decay can be effectively quantified even if the diffusion is still not efficient on a time-scale of order
O(1)
. Notice that the order of the critical points of
v
does not enter at all in the physical space estimates, contrary to the disc setting, as we show below.

1.2.
Circular flows in a disc

When we consider the equation (
1.1
) with
\Omega=D
and
{\boldsymbol{v}}=rv(r)\hat{{\mathsf{e}}}_{\theta}
, the problem we have at hand is

(1.13)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v(r)\partial_{\theta}f=\nu\Delta_{r,\theta}f,\qquad(r,\theta)\in[0,R]\times\mathbb{T},\\
f|_{t=0}=f^{in},\qquad\partial_{{\boldsymbol{n}}}f|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}

where we recall that
\Delta_{r,\theta}
is defined in (
1.7
).
If we now take a partial Fourier transform in the angular direction, namely

(1.14)

\hat{f}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t,r,\theta){\rm e}^{-i\ell\theta}\,{}\mathrm{d}\theta

the Fourier coefficients

\hat{f}_{\ell}
of a solution
f
to equation (
1.13
) satisfy

(1.15)

\partial_{t}\hat{f}_{\ell}+i\ell v(r)\hat{f}_{\ell}=\nu\left(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial r)-\frac{\ell^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\hat{f}_{\ell}

for each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. The analogy with (
1.9
) is the following: if we take the angular Fourier transform in (
1.9
),
we get

(1.16)

\partial_{t}\hat{g}_{k,\ell}+ikv(r)\hat{g}_{k,\ell}=\nu\left(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r})-\frac{\ell^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\hat{g}_{k,\ell}.

Hence,
(
1.15
) is the equation
(
1.16
) for the choice of parameters

\ell=k
. We can therefore recover the bounds on
\hat{f}_{\ell}
from the ones we have for
g_{\ell,\ell}
in Theorem
1
. Observing that
|\ell|\geq 1>\nu
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
v:[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy Assumption
1
and
\ell\neq 0
. Then, there exist constants
c_{3},C_{3}>0
, independent of
\nu,\ell
, such that for all initial data
\hat{f}_{\ell}^{in}\in L^{2}(0,R)
the solution to (
1.15
) satisfy

(1.17)

\|\hat{f}_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,R)}\leq C_{3}{\rm e}^{-c_{3}\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{m+2}}t}\|\hat{f}^{in}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,R)},

for every
t\geq 0
.

The bound in the physical space for
f
now it directly follows by Parseval’s identity. Namely, if

(1.18)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{in}(r,\theta)\,{}\mathrm{d}\theta=0,

we obtain that

(1.19)

\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{4}{\rm e}^{-c_{3}\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}t}\|{f^{in}}\|_{L^{2}(D)},

for a suitable constant
C_{4}>0
. Therefore we capture the enhanced dissipation mechanism, telling us that the solution is decaying on a time-scale
O(\nu^{-\frac{m}{m+2}})
, which is always faster than
O(\nu^{-1})
. When the angular average, corresponding to
\hat{f}_{0}
, is not zero, we would obtain that
f
is converging towards its angular average on the fast time-scale. Notice that
\hat{f}_{0}
is not conserved but it satisfies a standard
1d
heat equation, therefore we cannot expect to have decay on a faster time-scale for it.

2.
Semigroup decay via resolvent estimates

The main tool we will employ in the proof of Theorem
1
is a quantitative version of the Gearhart-Prüss obtained by Wei in
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
9
]
), which we reproduce below for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
H:{\mathcal{D}}(H)\to X
be an
m
-accretive operator on
X
. Then

(2.1)

\left\lVert{\rm e}^{-tH}\right\rVert_{X\to X}\leq{\rm e}^{-t\Psi(H)+\pi/2}

in which the quantity
\Psi(H)
is the pseudospectral abscissa of
H
, defined as

(2.2)

\Psi(H)=\inf\{\left\lVert(H-z)f\right\rVert_{X}\mid z\in i\mathbb{R},f\in{\mathcal{D}}(H),\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}=1\}.

We rewrite the equation (
1.9
) as

(2.3)

\partial_{t}g_{k}+Hg_{k}=0,

where the operator
H:{\mathcal{D}}(H)\to L^{2}(D)
is defined as

(2.4)

H=-\nu\Delta_{r,\theta}+ikv(r),\qquad{\mathcal{D}}(H)=H^{2}(D)

which is indeed
m
-accretive
[
8
]
.
Thus, by the Lumer-Phillips theorem, the
unique mild solution to equation (
1.9
) is given by
a strongly continuous semigroup in
L^{2}(D)
, namely, for the
initial datum
g_{k}^{in}\in L^{2}(D)
,

(2.5)

g_{k}(t)={\rm e}^{-tH}g_{k}^{in}

solves equation (
1.9
). Moreover, by Theorem

3
, the operator
{\rm e}^{-tH}
satisfies the estimate
(
2.1
). Hence, the proof of Theorem
1
is reduced in proving a pseudospectral bound for the operator
H
defined in (
2.4
).

2.1.
Pseudospectral bounds

Being
k
a fixed parameter from now on, let us write

(2.6)

H_{\lambda}=H-ik\lambda=-\nu\Delta_{r,\theta}+ik(v(r)-\lambda).

To prove Theorem
1
, it suffices to show that

(2.7)

\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(D)}\geq c_{1}\Lambda_{\nu,k}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(D)},

for every
g\in{\mathcal{D}}(H)
, where the constant
c_{1}
needs to be chosen
uniformly in
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

In the computations that follow, we frequently omit the subscripts on
the notation for norms and inner products in
L^{2}(D)
, where no ambiguity can occur
as to the relevant function space.

The strategy for proving (
2.7
) is as follows: we choose,
for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a neighbourhood

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\subseteq[0,R]
of the level set

E_{\lambda}=v^{-1}(\lambda)
, and split the domain of integration

(2.8)

\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in[0,R]\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x.

in order to get upper bounds for each of the two integrals on the
right-hand side. The motivation behind this is that, away from the
annulus
\{x\in D:\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\}
, the convection term
v-\lambda
in (
2.6
) is
bounded away from zero, which allows us to recover a bound on the
L^{2}
norm in terms of
H_{\lambda}
thanks to the invertibility of
v-\lambda
. On the other hand, in the integral over the
region where
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
, we exploit some Poincaré-type inequality where we can gain smallness parameters from the measure of the set
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. That the latter set is indeed small is consequence of Assumption

1
.
We thus choose the sets
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be the one in Assumption
1
. Define

•

E_{\lambda,\delta}
to be the preimage under
v
of the interval

(\lambda-\delta^{m},\lambda+\delta^{m})
, that is,

E_{\lambda,\delta}=\{r\in[0,R]\mid\left\lvert v(r)-\lambda\right\rvert<\delta^{m}\}
.

•

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
to be the neighbourhood of the set
E_{\lambda,\delta}
with
thickness
\delta^{m}
, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}=\{r\in[0,R]\mid\mathrm{dist}(x,E_{\lambda,\delta})<\delta^{m}\}
.

We collect in the next two propositions the bounds we have for the two integrals on the right-hand side of (
2.8
). Away from the level sets we have the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
be the set defined in Definition
2.1
. Then, for any
g\in{\mathcal{D}}(H)
the following holds true

(2.9)

\int_{|x|\in[0,R]\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}}|g|^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{4}\|g\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{|k|\delta^{m}}+\frac{\nu}{|k|^{2}\delta^{2m+2}}\right)\|H_{\lambda}g\|\|g\|.

Near the level sets, we can prove the result below.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
be the set defined in Definition
2.1
. Then there exists a constant
\tilde{C}>0
such that, for any
g\in{\mathcal{D}}(H)
, the following holds true

(2.10)

\int_{|x|\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}}|g|^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g\|^{2}+\frac{\tilde{C}\delta^{2}}{\nu}\|H_{\lambda}g\|\|g\|.

We postpone the proof of Propositions
2.2
–
2.3
to the end of this section. With the bounds (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) at hand, we are ready to present the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Summing together (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) and
rearranging, we have for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
that

(2.11)

\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}\leq 4\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\delta^{m}}+\frac{\nu}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}\delta^{2m+2}}+\frac{\tilde{C}\delta^{2}}{\nu}\right)\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert.

We now make a choice of
\delta
depending on the parameters
\nu
and
k
:

•

If
0<\nu\leq\left\lvert k\right\rvert
, then the sharpest bound we can recover is by
choosing

\delta=\tilde{\delta}\nu^{\frac{1}{m+2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{m+2}}
,
resulting in
\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}\leq c_{1}\nu^{-\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-\frac{2}{m+2}}\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert
with the constant
c_{1}=4(\tilde{\delta}^{-m}+\tilde{\delta}^{-(2m+2)}+\tilde{C}\tilde{\delta}^{2})
.

•

If instead
0<\left\lvert k\right\rvert\leq\nu
, observing that

(2.12)

\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\delta^{m}}+\frac{\nu}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}\delta^{2m+2}}+\frac{\tilde{C}\delta^{2}}{\nu}=\frac{\nu}{k^{2}}\Bigg(\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{\nu}\frac{1}{\delta^{m}}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2m+2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\tilde{C}\delta^{2}\Bigg).

Since
|k|/\nu\leq 1
, we choose
\delta=\tilde{\delta}
, and find that
\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}\leq c_{1}\frac{\nu}{k^{2}}\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert
, with
the same constant
c_{1}
as in the previous case.

Altogether we recover inequality (
2.7
), thanks to which we can apply Theorem
3
and conclude
the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

It thus remains to show the proofs of Proposition
2.2
-
2.3
, which we present in the next two sections.

2.2.
Bounds away from level sets

In this section, we aim at proving Proposition
2.2
. To this end, we follow the strategy in
[
7
]
, and we introduce the function

(2.13)

\chi(r)=\varphi(\mathrm{sign}(v(r)-\lambda)\mathrm{dist}(r,E_{\lambda,\delta})/\delta^{m}),

in which

(2.14)

\varphi(s)=\begin{cases}s,&\text{if $\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq 1$},\\
\mathrm{sign}(s),\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

By the definition of
\chi
, we know that
\chi(v-\lambda)\geq 0
. Moreover, in the set
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
we have
|v-\lambda|\geq\delta^{m}
. Therefore

(2.15)

\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in[0,R]\setminus{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{(v(r)-\lambda)\chi(r)}{\delta^{m}}\left\lvert g(r,\theta)\right\rvert^{2}r\,{}\mathrm{d}r\,{}\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{1}{\delta^{m}}\langle(v-\lambda)\chi g,g\rangle.

To estimate the term
\langle(v-\lambda)\chi g,g\rangle
, observe
that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Im}{\langle H_{\lambda}g,\chi g\rangle}

\displaystyle=\nu\mathrm{Im}{\langle\Delta_{r,\theta}g,\chi g\rangle}+\mathrm{Im}{\langle ik(v-\lambda)g,\chi g\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\nu\mathrm{Im}{\langle\partial_{r}g,(\partial_{r}\chi)g\rangle}+k\langle(v-\lambda)g,\chi g\rangle,

in which we recognise the relevant term on the final line. Noting that

\left\lvert\partial_{r}\chi\right\rvert<\delta^{-1}
, it then
follows from (
2.16
) and the triangle inequality that

(2.17)

\langle(v-\lambda)\chi g,g\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}\left(\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert+\frac{\nu}{\delta}\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert\right).

Observe also that

(2.18)

\nu\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert^{2}=\mathrm{Re}\langle H_{\lambda}g,g\rangle\leq\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert.

Thus, combining (
2.15
) with (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in[0,R]\setminus{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{m}}\langle(v-\lambda)\chi g,g\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\delta^{m}}\left(\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert+\frac{\nu}{\delta}\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\delta^{m}}\left(\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\delta}\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\delta^{m}}+\frac{\nu}{\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}\delta^{2m+2}}\right)\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2},

where we also applied the Young’s inequality on the product

\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-(m+1)}\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}
on the penultimate line.
∎

2.3.
Bounds near level sets

To prove Proposition
2.3
, we use two results from

[
7
]
. The first of these is a Poincaré-type bound which
appears in
[
7
, Lemma B.1]
:

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
g\in H^{1}(D)
and all
R\geq R_{2}\geq R_{1}\geq 0
, we have

(2.20)

\int_{R_{1}\leq\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R_{2}}|g|^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2(R_{2}-R_{1})\left\lVert g\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert.

The second result is that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}(v)
is covered by a finite union of
intervals whose total length is in
O(\delta)
as
\delta\longrightarrow 0
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
v\in C^{m}([0,R])
satisfy
Assumption
1
. Then there exist constants

C_{0},\delta_{0}>0
and, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
, a
choice of a finite family
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}
of intervals such that

(2.21)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}(v)\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}\subseteq[0,R]

and such that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
we
have that

(2.22)

\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}}\left\lvert V\right\rvert<C_{0}\delta.

Proof.

This Lemma can be extracted from the proof of

[
7
, Lemma 2.6]
, where such coverings by intervals are constructed in
order to bound the measure
\left\lvert\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}\right\rvert
of the level set
neighbourhoods.

Observe first that it suffices to prove this result with
E_{\lambda,\delta}
in
place of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
, since one may enlarge by
\delta
each interval in
a covering of
E_{\lambda,\delta}
to produce one for
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\delta}
that still satisfies
(
2.22
) but with a worse constant

C_{0}
. Moreover, since
E_{\lambda,\delta}
is empty for
\lambda
outside of a
compact neighbourhood of
v([0,R])\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, it suffices to be
able to choose
C_{0}
locally constant near each
\lambda_{0}
in this
neighbourhood.

Fix now
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. The idea is to use the fact that
E_{\lambda,\delta}
is
a union of level sets
E_{\lambda}
with
\lambda
close to
\lambda_{0}
:

(2.23)

E_{\lambda,\delta}=\bigcup_{\left\lvert\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right\rvert<\delta^{m}}E_{\lambda}

and by the continuity of the function
v
, we expect the level set

E_{\lambda}
not too change to much when
\lambda
is perturbed away
from
\lambda_{0}
. Indeed, using Assumption
1
,

E_{\lambda_{0}}=v^{-1}(\lambda_{0})
consists of finitely many elements

r_{1},\dots,r_{N_{\lambda_{0}}}
. Near each
r_{i}
, the function
v
is
approximated by its Taylor series

(2.24)

v(r)\approx\lambda+a_{i}(r-r_{i})^{n_{i}}\quad\text{where}\quad a_{i}=\frac{v^{(n_{1})}(r_{i})}{n_{i}!},

in which
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is the order of the lowest-order derivative of

v
which does not vanish at
r_{i}
; again by Assumption
1
we have that
1\leq n_{i}\leq m
. For small

\delta>0
, the function
v
then approximately maps the interval

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(r_{i})\subseteq[0,R]
to the interval

\mathcal{B}_{a_{i}\delta^{n_{i}}}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Conversely, one is
able to choose
R_{0}>0
such that

(2.25)

v^{-1}(\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{m}}(\lambda))\subseteq\bigcup_{r_{i}\in E_{\lambda}}\mathcal{B}_{R_{0}\delta}(r_{i})

for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Once again we refer to the
reference
[
7
]
for the details of this computation.

Choose now
V_{i}=\mathcal{B}_{R_{0}\delta}(r_{i})
for

i=1,\dots,N_{\lambda_{0}}
. Then (
2.25
) is
precisely the statement that the collection

\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\lambda_{0}}}
covers
E_{\lambda,\delta}
for all
\lambda
in
a small neighbourhood around near
\lambda_{0}
. Finally, choosing

C_{0}=2N_{\lambda_{0}}R_{0}
, we find that (
2.22
) is
satisfied:

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\lambda_{0}}}\left\lvert V_{i}\right\rvert=2N_{\lambda_{0}}R_{0}\delta=C_{0}\delta
.
∎

With the covering by intervals
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}
just obtained in Lemma
2.5
, we are to prove Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

First, we observe that for any
V\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}
, thanks to Lemma
2.4
we have

(2.26)

\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in V}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\left\lvert V\right\rvert\left\lVert g\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert.

Therefore,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in\mathcal{E}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}}\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in V}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\lambda,\delta}}2\left\lvert V\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}\delta\left\lVert g\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+2{C_{0}}^{2}\delta^{2}\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert^{2},

where we have used Lemma
2.5
,
followed by Young’s inequality on the final line. Combining this
with (
2.18
) we find that

(2.28)

\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\in\mathcal{E}}\left\lvert g\right\rvert^{2}\,{}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2C_{0}^{2}\delta^{2}}{\nu}\left\lVert H_{\lambda}g\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert.

∎

3.
Estimates in physical space

In this Section we prove Theorem
2
, which
gives a decay estimate on the
L^{\infty}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}
norm of a solution to
(
1.4
) when the initial datum belongs to
L^{1}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Using that the Fourier transform is a continuous map between
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
together with Hölder’s inequality, thanks to Theorem
1
we have
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{f}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{k}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\Lambda_{\nu,k}t}\|\hat{f}^{in}_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{r,\theta}}\,{}\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{f}^{in}\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\Lambda_{\nu,k}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f^{in}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{z}L^{2}_{r,\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\Lambda_{\nu,k}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k.

Then, we control the integral above by splitting the domain of integration in two regions, namely
\left\lvert k\right\rvert\leq\nu
and
|k|>\nu
which is where the definition of
\Lambda_{\nu,k}
changes. In particular, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\Lambda_{\nu,k}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\leq\nu}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\nu^{-1}k^{2}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k+\int_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert>\nu}{\rm e}^{-c_{1}\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{m+2}}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k:=\mathcal{I}_{\leq\nu}+\mathcal{I}_{>\nu}.

For the low-frequency region, a change of variables shows that

(3.3)

\mathcal{I}_{\leq\nu}=\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{c_{1}t}}\int_{\left\lvert\eta\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{c_{1}\nu t}}{\rm e}^{-\eta^{2}}\,{}\mathrm{d}\eta\\
\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{t}}.

For the second integral, since
\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert\nu\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{m+2}}=\nu
, we estimate as follows:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{>\nu}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t}\int_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert>\nu}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu^{\frac{m}{m+2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{m+2}}t}\,{}\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t}\nu^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}c_{1}t\right)^{-\frac{m+2}{2}}\int_{\left\lvert\eta\right\rvert>(\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}}{\rm e}^{-\eta^{\frac{2}{m+2}}}\,{}\mathrm{d}\eta.

We then have the approximation

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lvert\eta\right\rvert>(\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}}{\rm e}^{-\eta^{\frac{2}{m+2}}}\,{}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{n(\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}}^{(n+1)(\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}}{\rm e}^{-\eta^{\frac{2}{m+2}}}\,{}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim(\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}n\nu t}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tfrac{1}{2}c\nu t)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\eta c_{1}\nu t}\,{}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim(\nu t)^{\frac{m}{2}}.

Inserting this into (
3.4
) yields

(3.6)

\mathcal{I}_{>\nu}\lesssim t^{-1}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}c\nu t},

which combined with (
3.3
) proves the desired
result.
∎

Stackelberg exact controllability of a class of nonlocal parabolic equations
Landry Djomegne Cyrille Kenne

Abstract
This paper deals with a multi-objective control problem for a class of nonlocal parabolic equations, where the non-locality is expressed through an integral kernel. We present the Stackelberg strategy that combines the concepts of controllability to trajectories with optimal control. The strategy involves two controls: a main control (the leader) and a secondary control (the follower). The leader solves a controllability to trajectories problem which consists to drive the state of the system to a prescribed target at a final time while the follower solves an optimal control problem which consists to minimize a given cost functional. The paper considers two cases: in the first case, both the leader and the follower act in the interior of the domain, and in the second case, the leader acts in the interior of the domain and the follower acts on a small part of the boundary. These results are applied to both linear and nonlinear nonlocal parabolic systems.

2.1
Existence, uniqueness and characterization of the optimal control

In this section, we are interested in the classical optimal control problem (
1.4
). To this end, we first prove the existence and the uniqueness of a minimizer and secondly, we characterize it through a suitable optimality system.

Due to the fact that the functional
J
given by (
2.3
) is quadratic and the system (
2.2
) is linear, it is not difficult to prove that
J
is continuous and strictly convex. Moreover, we have

J(f;v)\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O})}.

The above inequality allow us to say that
J
is coercive. These three properties guarantee the existence of a unique solution
\hat{v}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O})
to the optimal control problem (
1.4
)-(
2.3
).

In order to characterize the optimal control
\hat{v}
, we use the Euler-Lagrange optimality condition (
1.5
), i.e.

\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{J(f;\hat{v}+\lambda w)-J(f;\hat{v})}{\lambda}=0,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ w\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O}).

(2.7)

After some calculations, (
2.7
) gives

\begin{array}[]{rll}&&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(z-z_{d})h\ dx\,dt+\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\hat{v}w\ dx\,dt=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O}),\end{array}

(2.8)

where
h
is the solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle h_{t}-\Delta h+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)h(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&w\chi_{\mathcal{O}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle h&=&0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle h(0,\cdot)&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

Now, we introduce the adjoint state to system (
2.9
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle-p_{t}-\Delta p+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)p(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&(z-z_{d})\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle p&=&0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle p(T,\cdot)&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

If we multiply the first equation of this latter system by
h
solution of (
2.9
) and we integrate by parts over

, one obtain using the Fubini’s theorem

\begin{array}[]{rll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(z-z_{d})h\ dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}wp\ dx\,dt.\end{array}

Combining this above equality with (
2.8

\begin{array}[]{rll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}w\left(p+\mu\hat{v}\right)\ dx\,dt=0\,\,\mbox{for all}\ w\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O}),\end{array}

from which we deduce
p+\mu\hat{v}=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
.

We have proved the following result giving the optimality system that characterizes the optimal control for the cost functional

Proposition 2.3

Proof.
We proceed in two steps.

Step 1.
We prove that for

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}(\psi)\leq\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)\left(s^{3}\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{-2s\eta}\varphi^{3}\left(|\rho|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\right)dx\,dt\right).\end{array}

(2.28)

We apply Carleman inequality (

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}(\psi)&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)s^{3}\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{-2s\eta}\varphi^{3}\left(|\rho|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\right)dx\,dt\\

&+&\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left|\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\psi(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|^{2}dx\,dt.\end{array}

Taking the parameter

large enough, we can absorb the second expression in the right-hand side of above expression and we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}(\psi)&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)s^{3}\lambda^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{-2s\eta}\varphi^{3}\left(|\rho|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\right)dx\,dt\\
&+&\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left|\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\rho(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|^{2}dx\,dt\\
&+&\displaystyle C(\Omega,\omega)\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left|\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\psi(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|^{2}dx\,dt.\end{array}

(2.29)

Now, arguing as in
[
4
]

sufficiently large. We have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\rho(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|&=&\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}e^{\frac{\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}K(t,x,\theta)e^{-\frac{\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}\rho(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|\\

(2.30)

We proceed to estimate the term in the right-hand side of the above inequality. We use Fubini’s theorem and we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{-\frac{2\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\ d\theta\right)\ dxdt=\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-\frac{2\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{-2s\eta}\ dx\right)\ d\theta dt.\end{array}

Using the definition of
\sigma^{-}
given by (
2.20b
), we get

\int_{\Omega}e^{-2s\eta}\ dx\leq|\Omega|e^{-\frac{2s\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}},

(2.31)

where
|\Omega|
denotes the measure of
\Omega
. Due to Proposition
2.2
, the definition of
\sigma^{+}
given by (

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{-\frac{2\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\ d\theta\right)\ dxdt&\leq&C\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-\frac{2(1+s)\sigma^{-}}{l^{4}(t)}}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\ d\theta\ dt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-\frac{2s\sigma^{+}}{l^{4}(t)}}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\ d\theta\ dt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C\displaystyle\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}|\rho(t,\theta)|^{2}\ d\theta\ dt.\end{array}

Putting it all together, the expression (

\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}\left|\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\rho(t,\theta)\ d\theta\right|^{2}dx\,dt\leq C\mathcal{K}^{2}\int_{Q}e^{-2s\eta}|\rho|^{2}\ dxdt.

Proceeding as above, we prove the following estimate

(2.33)

Combining (
2.32
) and (
2.33

2.29
), then taking
s
sufficiently large, we deduce estimate (
2.28

Step 2.
Now, we want to eliminate the local term corresponding to
\psi
on the right hand side of the estimate (

). We argue as the same as in
[
7
]
. Consider an open set
\omega_{0}
such that

(2.34a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\xi}{\xi^{1/2}}\in L^{\infty}(\omega_{0}),\,\,\frac{\nabla\xi}{\xi^{1/2}}\in[L^{\infty}(\omega_{0})]^{N}.

(2.34b)

\\

Proposition 2.4

Proof.

2.4
The semilinear case

In this section, we apply the Stackelberg strategy for the following nonlocal semilinear system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle y_{t}-\Delta y+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)y(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&G(y)+f\chi_{\omega}+v\chi_{\mathcal{O}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\

In the semilinear case, the convexity of the functional

Proposition 2.6

Let us assume that

,
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)
are fixed and
\mu
is sufficiently large. Suppose that

,

,

and there exists a constant

such that

Then, if
\hat{v}
satisfies (

), there exists a constant

In particular, the functional

Proof.
The technique of the proof is inspired by
[

, Proposition 1.4]
.

Let

be given and let

be the associated optimal control solution of (

and

, we denote by

the solution of the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle y^{s}_{t}-\Delta y^{s}+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)y^{s}(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&G(y^{s})+f\chi_{\omega}+(\hat{v}+sw^{1})\chi_{\mathcal{O}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle y^{s}&=&0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle y^{s}(0,\cdot)&=&y^{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.62)

and we set
y:=y^{s}|_{s=0}
.

Then, we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}&&\displaystyle DJ(f;\hat{v}+sw^{1})\cdot w^{2}-DJ(f;\hat{v})\cdot w^{2}=s\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}w^{1}w^{2}\ dxdt\\
&&\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(y^{s}-y_{d})z^{s}\ dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(y-y_{d})z\ dxdt,\\
\end{array}

(2.63)

where
z^{s}
is the derivative of
y^{s}
with respect to
\hat{v}
in the direction
w^{2}
, that is
z^{s}
is the solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle z_{t}^{s}-\Delta z^{s}+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)z^{s}(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&G^{\prime}(y^{s})+w^{2}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle z^{s}&=&0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle z^{s}(0,\cdot)&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.64)

We will also use the notation
z:=z^{s}|_{s=0}
.

Let us introduce the adjoint system of (
2.64
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle-p_{t}^{s}-\Delta p^{s}+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)p^{s}(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&G^{\prime}(y^{s})p^{s}+\left(y^{s}-y_{d}\right)\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle p^{s}&=&0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle p^{s}(T,\cdot)&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\end{array}\right.

(2.65)

and let us use the notation

.

Multiplying the first equation of (
2.64
) by
p^{s}
solution of (
2.65
) and integrating by parts over

, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(y^{s}-y_{d})z^{s}\ dxdt=\int_{Q}w^{2}p^{s}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}\ dxdt.

(2.66)

From (
2.63
) and (
2.66
), we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle DJ(f;\hat{v}+sw^{1})\cdot w^{2}-DJ(f;\hat{v})\cdot w^{2}&=&\displaystyle s\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}w^{1}w^{2}\ dxdt\\
&+&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}(p^{s}-p)w^{2}\ dxdt.\end{array}

(2.67)

Notice that

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(p^{s}-p)-\Delta(p^{s}-p)+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)(p^{s}(t,\theta)-p^{s}(t,\theta))\ d\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left[G^{\prime}(y^{s})-G^{\prime}(y)\right]p^{s}+G^{\prime}(y)(p^{s}-p)+(y^{s}-y)\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(y^{s}-y)-\Delta(y^{s}-y)+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)(y^{s}(t,\theta)-y^{s}(t,\theta))\ d\theta=\left[G(y^{s})-G(y)\right]+sw^{1}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}.

Consequently, the following limits exist

\eta=\lim\limits_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}(p^{s}-p)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \phi=\lim\limits_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}(y^{s}-y)

and verify the following systems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\Delta\phi+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\phi(t,\theta)\ d\theta&=&G^{\prime}(y)\phi+w^{1}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\

\displaystyle\phi(0,\cdot)&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\end{array}\right.

(2.68)

Thus, from (
2.67

2.69

Indeed, given

and since

(2.72)

Using systems (
2.68
)-(
2.69

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta w^{1}\ dxdt&=&\displaystyle\int_{Q}\eta\left(\phi_{t}-\Delta\phi+\int_{\Omega}K(t,x,\theta)\phi(t,\theta)\ d\theta-G^{\prime}(y)\phi\right)\ dxdt\\

Using Hölder inequality in the last expression of (

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta w^{1}\ dxdt\right|\leq\displaystyle\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2r^{\prime}}((0,T);L^{2s^{\prime}}(\Omega))}\|p\|_{L^{r}((0,T);L^{s}(\Omega))}\displaystyle+\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{d})},\end{array}

where

and

First, we have that

Using interpolation results, we deduce that

From Sobolev embedding results, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(\Omega),

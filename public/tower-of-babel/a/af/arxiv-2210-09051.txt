Unipotent Elements and Twisting in Link Homology
Minh-Tâm Quang Trinh

Abstract
. Let 𝒰 \mathcal{U} be the unipotent variety of a complex reductive group G G . Fix opposed Borel subgroups B ± ⊆ G B_{\pm}\subseteq G with unipotent radicals U ± U_{\pm} . The map that sends x + ​ x − ↦ x + ​ x − ​ x + − 1 x_{+}x_{-}\mapsto x_{+}x_{-}x_{+}^{-1} for all x ± ∈ U ± x_{\pm}\in U_{\pm} restricts to a map from U + ​ U − ∩ g ​ B + U_{+}U_{-}\cap gB_{+} into 𝒰 ∩ g ​ B + \mathcal{U}\cap gB_{+} , for any g g . We conjecture that the restricted map forms half of a homotopy equivalence between these varieties, and thus, induces a weight-preserving isomorphism between their compactly-supported cohomologies. Noting that the map is equivariant with respect to certain actions of B + ∩ g ​ B + ​ g − 1 B_{+}\cap gB_{+}g^{-1} , we prove for type A A that an equivariant analogue of this isomorphism exists. Curiously, this follows from a certain duality in Khovanov–Rozansky homology, a tool from knot theory.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
G
be a complex, connected, reductive algebraic group.
Let
B_{+}
and
B_{-}
be opposed Borel subgroups of
G
, and let
U_{\pm}
be the unipotent radical of
B_{\pm}
.
For instance, if
G
is the group of invertible
n\times n
matrices
\GL_{n}
, then we can choose
U_{+}
,
resp.

U_{-}
, to be the subgroup of upper-triangular,
resp.
lower-triangular, matrices with
1
’s along the diagonal.
Every element of
U_{+}U_{-}
can be written uniquely in the form
x_{+}x_{-}
, where
x_{\pm}\in U_{\pm}
.

Let
\cal{U}\subseteq G
be the closed subvariety of unipotent elements.
With the notation above, there is a map
\Phi:U_{+}U_{-}\to\cal{U}
defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{+}x_{-})=x_{+}x_{-}x_{+}^{-1}.

Note that
U_{+}U_{-}
is an affine space, whereas
\cal{U}
is usually singular.
Nonetheless, in the analytic topology, the sets
U_{+}U_{-}(\mathbf{C})
and
\cal{U}(\mathbf{C})
are homotopy equivalent, as they are both contractible.

For any
g\in G(\mathbf{C})
, the map
\Phi
restricts to a map
\Phi_{g}:\cal{V}_{g}\to\cal{U}_{g}
, where

(1.2)

\displaystyle\cal{U}_{g}

\displaystyle=\cal{U}\cap gB_{+},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\cal{V}_{g}

\displaystyle=U_{+}U_{-}\cap gB_{+}.

Note that
\cal{U}_{g},\cal{V}_{g},\Phi_{g}
only depend on the coset
gB_{+}
.
We endow
\cal{U}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
and
\cal{V}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
with the analytic topology.
The new idea proposed in this note is:

Conjecture 1
.

The map
\Phi_{g}
defines half of a homotopy equivalence between
\cal{U}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
and
\cal{V}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
.

The simplest case is
g\in B_{+}(\mathbf{C})
.
Here,
\cal{U}_{g}(\mathbf{C})=U_{+}(\mathbf{C})=\cal{V}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
and
\Phi_{g}
is a retract from the affine space
U_{+}(\mathbf{C})
onto the point corresponding to the identity of
G(\mathbf{C})
.
In general, neither
\cal{U}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
nor
\cal{V}_{g}(\mathbf{C})
will be contractible, as we will show in
Section

3
through examples.

We emphasize that for an arbitrary map of varieties
\Phi:Y\to X
that induces a homotopy equivalence on
\mathbf{C}
-points, there may not exist a nonconstant map of varieties
X\to S
such that
\Phi
induces a homotopy equivalence of fibers
Y_{s}(\mathbf{C})\to X_{s}(\mathbf{C})
for every
s\in S(\mathbf{C})
.
For instance, take
\Phi
to be the projection from a quadric cone onto its axis of symmetry.
Both total spaces are contractible, so
\Phi
is automatically a homotopy equivalence.
For the map of fibers over
s
to be a homotopy equivalence as well,
X_{s}(\mathbf{C})
must contract onto the origin of
X(\mathbf{C})=\mathbf{C}
.
Since
X\to S
is nonconstant, some

must violate this condition.

1.2.

Some motivation for

comes from classical results about finite groups of Lie type.
To state them, let us implicitly replace

with its split form over a finite field

of good characteristic.

In
[

, Steinberg showed the identity
|\cal{U}(\mathbf{F})|=|U_{+}U_{-}(\mathbf{F})|
.
In
[

\displaystyle|\cal{U}_{g}(\mathbf{F})|=|\cal{V}_{g}(\mathbf{F})|;

see

.
More recently, Lusztig has given a new proof of (

Indeed, once one checks that

and

-point counts.

Our main result is evidence for an equivariant analogue of

Let

The map

-equivariance of

.
Though we do not have a general theorem about

\displaystyle\gr_{\ast}^{\mathsf{W}}\mathrm{H}_{c,H_{g}}^{\ast}(\cal{U}_{g},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\simeq\gr_{\ast}^{\mathsf{W}}\mathrm{H}_{c,H_{g}}^{\ast}(\cal{V}_{g},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}),

The variety

By definition,

\displaystyle\sigma_{ww^{\prime}}

Note that there is a central element

In

When

At the same time, when

Just as

For

This proof suggests that we regard

Moreover, it suggests an extension of

The isomorphism of

Lemma 4

5.
Khovanov–Rozansky Homology

5.1.

In this section, we prove

Let
\mathit{Br}_{W}^{+}
be the
positive braid monoid
of
W

It is the monoid freely generated by a set of symbols

\sigma_{ww^{\prime}}=\sigma_{w}\sigma_{w^{\prime}}

\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})

The
full twist
is the element
\pi=\sigma_{w_{0}}^{2}\in\mathit{Br}_{W}^{+}

For all
\beta=\sigma_{w_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{w_{k}}\in\mathit{Br}_{W}^{+}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\cal{U}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{(u,B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})\in\cal{U}\times\cal{B}^{k}\mid u^{-1}B_{k}u\xrightarrow{w_{1}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{w_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{w_{k}}B_{k}\},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\cal{X}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})\in\cal{B}^{k}\mid B_{k}\xrightarrow{w_{1}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{w_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{w_{k}}B_{k}\}.

Let
G
act on these varieties by left conjugation.
We regard
\cal{U}(\beta)
and
\cal{X}(\beta)
as varieties over
\cal{O}_{w}
, where

and
(B_{i})_{i}\mapsto(B_{k},B_{1})

Deligne showed that up to isomorphism over

\beta
, not on the sequence of elements

.
His full result describes the extent to which the isomorphism can be pinned down uniquely; see
[

]
for details.

In particular, we have equivariant identifications

(5.3)

\displaystyle\cal{U}_{w}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\cal{U}(\sigma_{w}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle\cal{X}_{w}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\cal{X}(\sigma_{w}\pi)

via

5.2.

If

is the symmetric group on

Any braid can be closed up end-to-end to form a link: that is, an embedding of a disjoint union of circles into

In

Its graded dimension can be written as a formal series in variables

In
[

When we set

The positive braid monoid

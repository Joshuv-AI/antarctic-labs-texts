On log differentials of local fields
Nicola Mazzari

Abstract
. We prove a logarithmic version of Fontaine’s classic result on differentials of 𝒪 K ¯ \mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}} over 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} .



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field,
\bar{K}
an algebraic closure and
\mathbb{C}_{p}=\hat{\bar{K}}
its completion. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}}
their rings of integers. The module of Kähler differentials
\Omega=\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}
is well-known.
In particular Fontaine
[
3

gives the following identification

\mathbb{C}_{p}(1)\cong V_{p}(\Omega)

where
V_{p}(\Omega)
is the rational Tate module of
\Omega

\dlog_{F}:\bar{K}(1)=\bar{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)\rightarrow\Omega\ ,\ \dlog_{F}(p^{-n}\otimes\underline{\epsilon})=\frac{d\epsilon_{n}}{\epsilon_{n}}\ ,

where
\underline{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a compatible sequence of
p
-powers roots of
1

\epsilon_{1}\neq 1

In this article we slightly modify the source of the previous map in order to compute a logarithmic version of
\Omega
and its Tate module.

Fix
q\in p\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
. We denote by

the Tate elliptic curve of parameter

: its group of
\bar{K}
-points is isomorphic to
{\bar{K}}^{*}/\langle q\rangle
and its Tate module, denoted
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{\log}
, is an extension of the trivial Galois module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)
. Its divisible group is denoted by
\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\log}
.
Fix a sequence
\underline{q}=(q_{n})_{n}
of compatible
p
-power roots of
q
. Then,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{\log}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\tilde{\epsilon}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}\tilde{q}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module, where
\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{q}
are the logarithms of
\underline{\epsilon},\underline{q}
. The Galois action is given by
\begin{pmatrix}\chi&c\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

\sigma(q_{n})=\epsilon_{n}^{c_{n}(\sigma)}q_{n}
(cf.
[
1

The inclusion
\{q_{n}^{s}:\ s,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}}

\bar{N}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}}

\Omega^{\log}=\Omega_{(\mathcal{O}_{\bar{K}},\bar{N})/(\mathcal{O}_{K},\text{triv})}

For any

-module

We aim to prove the following result (cf.
[

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a natural surjection

\dlog:\bar{K}(1)^{\log}\rightarrow\Omega^{\log}

compatible with
\dlog_{F}
. Moreover it induces an isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}(1)^{\log}\cong T_{p}(\Omega^{\log})\ .

In particular
V_{p}(\Omega^{\log})
can be seen as a submodule of
\mathbb{B}_{\rm st}
sitting in the following exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{C}_{p}\cdot t\to V_{p}(\Omega^{\log})\to\mathbb{C}_{p}\cdot u\to 0

where

and
u=\log([\underline{q}^{\flat}])
(we write
\underline{a}^{\flat}
to denote an element of
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}
given by a fixed compatible system
\underline{a}
of
p
-powers roots of
a
).

The result of Fontaine is used to compute the
p
-adic periods of abelian varieties with good reduction. We believe that the logarithmic version can be used to address the case of semistable abelian varieties in a direct way (see remark
2.1
).

Remark 2.1
.

It is now easy to define a Galois equivariant pairing

(2)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:T_{p}E_{q}\times\mathrm{Fil}^{1}H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(E_{q}/K)\rightarrow V_{p}\Omega^{\log}

analogous to the Fontaine paring for abelian varieties with good reduction. We will discuss the details of this construction in a forthcoming work: we will develop the
p
-adic Hodge theory for
1
-motives, and construct a perfect pairing between the Tate module and the full de Rham cohomology of a
1
-motive over
K
. The theory in the good reduction (or crystalline) case is already explained in
[
5

, we will treat also the (potentially) semi-stable case.

For the sake of the reader we give a sketch of the construction of (
2
) in the following paragraphs.

Let
M_{q}=[\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{1\mapsto q}\mathbb{G}_{m}]
be the strict 1-motive over
K
such that
T_{p}(M_{q})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{\log}

[
6
, § 4.2]
(See also
[
2

There is a short exact sequence

0\to T_{p}\Omega^{\log}\to A_{2}^{\log}\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}\langle X^{\pm 1/p^{\infty}}\rangle\to 0

where

is simply the

This induces a short exact sequence (of complexes)

By taking the cones of the multiplication by

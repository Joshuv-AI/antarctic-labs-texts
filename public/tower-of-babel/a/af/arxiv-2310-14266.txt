On Universal Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of Hypermatrices
Daizhan Cheng \aref amss, Zhengping Ji \aref amss,ucas

Abstract
A cubic hypermatrix of order d d can be considered as a structure matrix of a tensor with covariant order r r and contra-variant order s = d − r s=d-r . Corresponding to this matrix expression of the hypermatrix, an eigenvector x x with respect to an eigenvalue λ \lambda is proposed, called the universal eigenvector and eigenvalue of the hypermatrix. According to the action of tensors, if x x is decomposable, it is called a universal hyper-(UH-)eigenvector. Particularly, if all decomposed components are the same, x x is called a universal diagonal hyper (UDH-)eigenvector, which covers most of existing definitions of eigenvalue/eigenvector of hypermatrices. Using Semi-tensor product (STP) of matrices, the properties of universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors are investigated. Algorithms are developed to calculate universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors for hypermatrices. Particular efforts have been put on UDH- eigenvalues/eigenvectors, because they cover most of the existing eigenvalues/eigenvectors for hypermatrices. Some numerical examples are presented to illustrate that the proposed technique is universal and efficient.

A=\{a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}}\in{\mathbb{R}}\;|\;i_{s}\in[1,n],s\in[1,d]\}

\displaystyle[M_{A}x^{d-1}]_{i}:=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i_{2}=1}^{n}\cdots\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i_{d}=1}^{n}a_{i,i_{2},\cdots,i_{d}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}},\quad i\in[1,n].

(1)

H-eigenvalue and eigenvector

(2)

(ii)

E- (or Z-) eigenvalue and eigenvector

(3)

(iii)

(4)

(iv)

Markov chain-based eigenvalue and eigenvector

(5)

\displaystyle M_{A}x^{d-1}=\lambda Bx^{d-1}.

(6)

The eigenvalues and eigenvectors defined by the above definitions have been proved to be useful or have potential uses. But the above definitions may not be general enough. In the above definitions the eigenvectors are essentially of the type as

\displaystyle\xi=x^{d-1}.

(7)

If we take the tensor action into consideration, this kind of
\xi
is limited in some sense. To understand this, we observe the following.

A square matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
can be considered as a quadratic form over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. That is,

\displaystyle t_{A}(x):=x^{T}Ax\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(8)

It is very natural to consider it as a tensor of covariant order
1
and contra-variant
1
. That is,
t\in{\mathcal{T}}^{1}_{1}(V)
, where the basis of the vector space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is fixed. Of course, it can also be considered as a linear mapping
\pi_{A}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as:

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x):=Ax\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(9)

Now if a particular
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(or in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
) is
A
-invariant, (precisely speaking, the subspace
V_{0}=\Span\{x_{0}\}
is
A
-invariant,) then we have

\displaystyle Ax_{0}=\lambda x_{0}.

(10)

We therefore have eigenvector
x_{0}
w.r.t. eigenvalue
\lambda
.

A hypermatrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
is a set of data with order
d
and dimension
n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}

[
31
]
. When
n_{i}=n
,
i\in[1,d]
, it is called a cubic hypermatrix.
As a generalization of square matrix, a cubic hypermatrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{d}}
can naturally be considered as a
multi-linear mapping
\pi_{A}:\underbrace{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
as

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i_{1}=1}^{n}\cdots\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i_{d}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{i_{d}}.

(11)

Let
I\bigcup J
be a partition of
[1,d]
, (precisely speaking, let
i_{I}\bigcup i_{J}
be a partition of index set
\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\}
), and
|I|=s:=d-r
,
|J|=r
. Using
I
and
J
as the row index set and column index set respectively,
A
can be expressed by alphabetic order into a matrix form as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}M_{A}^{I\times J}:=\\
\begin{bmatrix}a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=1}_{i_{J_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=1}&a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=1}_{i_{J_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=2}&\cdots&a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=1}_{i_{J_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=n}\\
a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}=2}}_{i_{J_{1}=1},\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=1}&a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}=2}}_{i_{J_{1}=1},\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=2}&\cdots&a^{i_{I_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}=2}}_{i_{J_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=n}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
a^{i_{I_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=n}_{i_{J_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=1}&a^{i_{I_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=n}_{i_{J_{1}}=1,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=2}&\cdots&a^{i_{I_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{I_{d-s}}=n}_{i_{J_{1}}=n,\cdots,i_{J_{s}}=n}\\
\end{bmatrix}.\end{array}

We refer to Section 3 for more details, and to
[
21
]
for matrix expression of hypermatrices and the formulas to convert a hypermatrix from one matrix expression to another. In addition,
[
21
]
also shows that different matrix expressions of a hypermatrix can be used for different contraction products of the hypermatrix.

Using matrix expression (
1
) of hypermatrices, it is clear that a cubic hypermatrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{d}}
can also be considered as a multi-linear mapping
\pi_{A}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{r}},{\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{s}})
.

From this perspective, in definitions (
2
)-(
6
),
M_{A}=M_{A}^{\{1\}\times\{2,\cdots,d\}}
. That is, they consider only the mapping

\pi_{A}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{d-1}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This seems to be an unnecessarily restriction.

Another counter example is: when
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\overbrace{n\times\cdots\times n}^{2r}}
, the classical eigenvalues/eigenvectors for
M_{A}^{n^{r}\times n^{r}}
do not meet the existing definitions of eigenvalue/eigenvector for hypermatrices. Hence, current definitions of eigenvalue/eigenvector for hypermatrices do not include the classical eigenvalues/eigenvectors of hypermatrices.

We prefer to give a definition of hypermatrices, which is consistent with tensor structure. To this end, we consider a tensor
t\in{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}(V)
, which has its structure constants as a hypermatrix of order
d=r+s
. Then it is acting on vectors of the form
\xi=\otimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
.
Comparing it with the
\xi
in (
7
), it is different at two aspects: (i) its degree can be arbitrary
r<d
; (ii) the components of
\xi
, i.e.,
x_{i}
,
i\in[1,r]
, may not be the same.

Based on the above observations, we propose a more general eigenvalue/eigenvector for hypermatrices, called the universal (U-) eigenvalue/eigenvector, as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}\xi:=\lambda{\mathcal{B}}\xi,

(14)

where
\xi=\otimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
,
{\mathcal{A}}
is the action of hypermatrix
A
on
\xi
, and
{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(V^{r},V^{s})
is a pre-assigned linear mapping.

This kind of eigenvalue/eigenvector is mimic to the definition of
[
24
]
, but more general. In other words, the general eigenvalue/eigenvector of hypermatrices may be considered as the motivation of U-eigenvalue/eigenvector.

In this paper we first show that the definition (
14
) is more general. It covers (
2
)-(
6
). In addition, more general types of eigenvalues/eigenvectors can be converted into the form of (
14
). Say, if
M
is a structure matrix of a tensor
t\in{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}
, then an eigenvalue/eigemvector problem is

\displaystyle Mx^{r}=\lambda x^{s}.

(15)

In the last section an example is given to show how to convert it into (
14
), and then this problem can be completely solved.

Of course, the most important issue is to find (all) universal eigenvalue/eigenvector for (
14
). A general algorithm need to be developed.

The basic tool used in this paper is the semi-tensor product (STP) of matrices. Using it, we solved two fundamental problems:

(i)

Converting non-homogeneous type of eigenvalue/eigenvector equations into the universal homogeneous form (

(ii)

Solving the universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors for (

The process for using STP to solve the eigenvalue/eigenvector problem for hypermatrices is described as follows.

Observing definitions (
2
)-(
6
), there are some (degree) homogeneous (say, (
2
), (
5
), and (
2
)); and some non-homogeneous ((
3
), (
4
)). Homogeneity is very important for eigenvectors, because it makes each eigenvector a one dimensional eigenvector space. Otherwise, an additional norm requirement, such as
x^{T}X=1
, has been assumed.

Roughly, speaking, our approach is based on subspace analysis. Observing that even if the definition is not homogeneous, if
x
is an eigenvector w.r.t. eigenvalue
\lambda
,
kx
is also an eigenvector w.r.t.
k^{s}\lambda
, where
s
is the difference of degrees of both sides,( where
s
might be negative.) One eigenvector
x
still provides a one-dimensional eigenvectors. Based on this observation, the norm restriction such as
x^{T}x=1
seems not necessary.

Using STP, we propose a new concept, called “hypervector”. In the light of the properties of hypervector, a technique is developed to convert non-homogeneous eigenvalue/eigenvector equation into a homogeneous one.

Finding eigenvalues/eigenvectors of a hypermatrix is a real challenge.
The basic idea used in this paper is as follows: First, using certain properties of STP, the eigenvalue/eigenvector relations, such as in (
2
)-(
6
) and many more, are converted into the universal form (
14
). (see the examples in the last section.) Then try to solve
\xi
via subspace analysis. Thirdly, verify if
\xi=x^{r}
, if “yes”, retrieve
x
from
\xi
. Finally, if the original relation is non-homogeneous, adjust the corresponding eigenvalue for corresponding
x
. Our algorithms assure that theoretically, all the eigenvalues/eigenvectors can be founded.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 is a brief review on STP and the STP-based matrix description of tensors. Section 3 provides a matrix expression of hypermatrices. In Section 4 the hypervectors are defined and its properties are investigated. Particularly, the decomposition formula is presented, which is the key for solving eigenvalue/eigenvector problem of hypermatrices by subspace analysis. The universal eigenvalue/eigenvector of hypermatrices is defined in Section 5. Two special kinds of universal eigenvectors, called hyper (UH-)eigenvectors and diagonal hyper (UDH-)eigenvectors, are also introduces. Section 6 discusses fundamental properties of universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors of hypermatrices. Section 7 devoted to the calculation of universal (H-, DH-)eigenvectors. Several numerical examples, which represent different kinds of eigenvalues/eigenvectors of hypermatrices, are presented in Section 8. Finally, Section 9 is a brief conclusion.

2.1
Semi-tensor Product of Matrices

This subsection gives a brief review on STP of matrices
[
9
,
10
]
.

Definition 2.1

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
,
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p\times q}
, and
t=\lcm(n,p)
. The STP of
A
and
B
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle A\ltimes B=\left(A\otimes I_{t/n}\right)\left(B\otimes I_{t/p}\right).

(16)

It is easy to see that STP is a generalization of classical matrix product. That is, when
n=p
, it degenerates to the classical matrix product, i.e.,
A\ltimes B=AB
. Because of this, we omit the symbol
\ltimes
in most cases.

One of the most important advantages of STP is that it keeps most properties of classical matrix product available. In the following, some basic properties are reviewed, which will be used in the sequel.

Proposition 2.2

(i)

(Associativity) Let
A,B,C
be three matrices of arbitrary dimensions. Then

\displaystyle(A\ltimes B)\ltimes C=A\ltimes(B\ltimes C).

(17)

(ii)

(Distributivity) Let
A,B
be two matrices of same dimension,
C
is of arbitrary dimension. Then

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(A+B)\ltimes C=A\ltimes C+B\ltimes C,\\
C\ltimes(A+B)=C\ltimes A+C\ltimes B.\end{array}

(iii)

Let
A,B
be two matrices of arbitrary dimensions. Then

\displaystyle(A\ltimes B)^{T}=B^{T}\ltimes A^{T}.

(20)

(iv)

Let
A,B
be two invertible matrices of arbitrary dimensions. Then

\displaystyle(A\ltimes B)^{-1}=B^{-1}\ltimes A^{-1}.

(21)

Proposition 2.3

(i)

Let
x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be a column vector. Then

\displaystyle xA=(I_{n}\otimes A)x.

(22)

(ii)

Let
\omega\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be a row vector. Then

\displaystyle A\omega=\omega(I_{n}\otimes A).

(23)

It is worth noticing that if
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{i}

i\in[1,r]
are column vectors, then by definition we have

\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=\otimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}.

Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4

Let
T_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m_{i}\times n_{i}}
,
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n_{i}}
,
i\in[1,r]
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\otimes_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}\right)\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}(M_{i}x_{i}).

(24)

Note that the product of hypermatrices, say, contracted product
[
31
]
, etc. is essentially a multi-linear mapping. STP is very convenient in expressing multi-linear mappings. For instance, the product (
1
), which was firstly proposed by Qi
[
35
]
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle Mx^{d-1}=M\ltimes\underbrace{x\ltimes\cdots\ltimes x}_{d-1},

(25)

where
M
is understood as the matrix expression of a hypermatrix.
If we omit the symbol
\ltimes
, then the right hand side (RHS) of (
25
) becomes left hand side (LHS) of (
25
). It is worth noting that hereafter,
Mx^{r}
is always considered as the STP of
M
with
r

x
’s.

Throughout this paper the default matrix product is assumed to be STP, and the symbol
\ltimes
is omitted if there is no possible confusion.

2.2
Matrix Expression of Tensors

Definition 2.5

[
2
]
Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
n
with its dual space
V^{*}
. A tensor
t
of covariant order
r
and contra-variant order
s
is a multilinear mapping

t:\underbrace{V\times\cdots\times V}_{r}\times\underbrace{V^{*}\times\cdots\times V^{*}}_{s}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
. The set of tensors of covariant order
r
and contra-variant order

is denoted by
{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}
.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
V
, and
\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{n}\}
be its dual basis, i.e.,

d_{i}(e_{j})=\begin{cases}1,\quad i=j,\\
0,\quad\mbox{Otherwise}.\end{cases}

The vector expression of
x\in V
is expressed as

x=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}e_{i}\sim(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})^{T}.

Similarly,
\omega\in V^{*}
is expressed as

\omega=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}d_{j}\sim(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}).

Proposition 2.6

[
10
]
Assume
t\in{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}
and

\begin{array}[]{l}t(e_{i_{1}},\cdots,e_{i_{r}},d_{j_{1}},\cdots,d_{j_{s}})=\mu^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}},\\
~~i_{p},j_{q}\in[1,n],\quad p\in[1,r],\;q\in[1,s].\end{array}

Then
\left\{\mu^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}},~i_{p},j_{q}\in[1,n],\quad p\in[1,r],\;q\in[1,s]\right\}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}t(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r};\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{s})=\omega_{s}\ltimes\cdots\ltimes\omega_{1}\ltimes\\
M_{t}\ltimes x_{1}\ltimes\cdots\ltimes x_{r},\end{array}

where

\displaystyle M_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{1,\cdots,1}_{1,\cdots,1}&\mu^{1,\cdots,2}_{1,\cdots,1}&\cdots&\mu^{r,\cdots,r}_{1,\cdots,1}\\
\mu^{1,\cdots,1}_{1,\cdots,2}&\mu^{1,\cdots,2}_{1,\cdots,2}&\cdots&\mu^{r,\cdots,r}_{1,\cdots,2}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
\mu^{1,\cdots,1}_{s,\cdots,s}&\mu^{1,\cdots,2}_{s,\cdots,s}&\cdots&\mu^{r,\cdots,r}_{s,\cdots,s}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(28)

is called the structure matrix of

.

Let

\displaystyle(e^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,e^{\prime}_{n})=(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})E

(29)

be a basis transformation, and
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}
and

be two vector forms of

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=E^{-1}x.

(30)

Let

and
\{d^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,d^{\prime}_{n}\}
be the dual bases of
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})

(e^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,e^{\prime}_{n})

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}d^{\prime}_{1}\\
d^{\prime}_{2}\\
\vdots\\

d_{2}\\
\vdots\\
d_{n}\end{bmatrix}

(31)

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})
and
\sigma^{\prime}=(\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})

\sigma\in V^{*}
with respect to

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma E.

(32)

Proposition 2.7

Let
t\in{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}
be a tensor and its structure matrix with respect to
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is
M_{t}
. Then its structure matrix with respect to

(e^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,e^{\prime}_{n})=(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})E,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}M^{\prime}_{t}&=&E^{-1}(I_{n}\otimes E^{-1})\cdots(I_{n^{s-1}}\otimes E^{-1})M_{t}\\
&&(I_{n^{r-1}}\otimes E)(I_{n^{r-2}}\otimes E)\cdots(I_{n}\otimes E)E.\end{array}

Proof.
Note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s-1}\cdots\sigma_{1}M_{t}x_{1}\cdots x_{r}=\sigma^{\prime}_{s}\sigma^{\prime}_{s-1}\cdots\sigma^{\prime}_{1}M^{\prime}_{t}x^{\prime}_{1}\cdots x^{\prime}_{r}.

Next, we extend the definition of eigenvalues/eigenvectors for hypermatrices to these of tensors.

Definition 5.14

Assume

under type

.

To show that Definition

is meaningful for tensors, we have to show that the eigenvalue and eigenvector defined by Definition

are independent of the basis of

. It is also said that the definition of eigenvalue and eigenvector is independent of the choice of coordinates.

Theorem 5.15

The eigenvalue and eigenvector defined by Definition
5.14
are independent of the basis of
V
.

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})
be the original basis of
V
, which leads to the structure matrix
M_{t}
in (
66
), and

(e_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,e_{n}^{\prime})=(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})E.

Define

\Phi^{E}_{k}:=\left(I_{n^{k-1}}\otimes E\right)\left(I_{n^{k-2}}\otimes E\right)\cdots\left(I_{n}\otimes E\right)E,\quad k\geq 1;

and

\Phi^{E^{-1}}_{k}:=E^{-1}\left(I_{n}\otimes E^{-1}\right)\left(I_{n^{k-2}}\otimes E^{-1}\right)\left(I_{n^{k-1}}\otimes E^{-1}\right).

It follows that

\left[\Phi^{E}_{k}\right]^{-1}=\Phi^{E^{-1}}_{k}.

Using this expression, (
2.7
) becomes

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{t}=\Phi^{E^{-1}}_{s}M\Phi^{E}_{r}.

(73)

Suppose under basis
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})
the matrix expression of type
{\mathcal{B}}:V^{r}\rightarrow V^{s}
is
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n^{s}\times n^{r}}
. That is,

\displaystyle\ltimes_{j=1}^{s}y_{j}=B\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}.

(74)

Now assume under basis
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})

\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}
is the H-eigenvector of
M_{t}
w.r.t.
\lambda
.
That is,

\displaystyle M_{t}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=\lambda B\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}.

(75)

We have to show that

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{t}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda B^{\prime}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i},

(76)

where
B^{\prime}
is the matrix expression of
{\mathcal{B}}
under basis
(e^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,e^{\prime}_{n})
.

First, we calculate
B^{\prime}
. Since

\ltimes_{j=1}^{s}y^{\prime}_{j}={\mathcal{B}}(\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime})=B^{\prime}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{\prime},

which leads to

\Phi^{E^{-1}_{s}}\ltimes_{j=1}^{s}y_{s}=B^{\prime}\Phi^{E^{-1}_{r}}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}.

Using (
74
), we have

\Phi^{E^{-1}_{s}}B\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=B^{\prime}\Phi^{E^{-1}_{r}}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}.

Since

,

are arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle B^{\prime}=\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}B\Phi^{E}_{r}.

(77)

Finally, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}M^{\prime}_{t}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i}=\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}M\Phi_{r}^{E}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i}\\
~~=\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}M\Phi_{r}^{E}\Phi^{E^{-1}}_{r}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\\
~~=\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}M\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\\
~~=\lambda\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}B\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\\
~~=\lambda\Phi_{s}^{E^{-1}}B\Phi^{E}_{r}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i}\\
~~=\lambda B^{\prime}\ltimes_{i=1}^{r}x^{\prime}_{i},\end{array}

which proves (

How to verify if a vector

9
Conclusion

The universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors (including UH- and UDH- eigenvectors) of hypermatrices have been introduced. Using STP, their properties and are discussed. It was shown that universal eigenvalues/eigenvectors are more general than most currently existing ones. Based on STP, a general method has been constructed to calculate the U-, UH-, and UDH- eigenvalues/eigenvectors. The solving procedure can be described as follows: First, convert the eigenvalue/eigenvector equation into a homogeneous equation. Then by solving set of linear equation (with eigenvalue as a parameter) to find the universal solution (for both eigenvalues and eigenvectors). Finally, check whether a U-eigenvector is decomposable to get UH-eigenvectors; and furthermore, check whether a a UH-eigenvector is diagonalizable to get UHD-eigenvectors. Some numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the proposed technique.

Some related challenging problems remain for further study. For instance,

(i)

When the eigenvalue/eigenvector problem has solution? Precisely speaking, for a given eigenvalue/eigenvector equation, when it has solution? Furthermore, how many solutions do we have?

(ii)

To solve higher degree multi-variable algebraic equations appeared in solving eigenvalue/eigenvector equation, some numerical methods have to be developed.

(iii)

The computational complexity in solving eigenvalue/eigenvector equation.

Canonial Analysis of General Relativity Formulated with the New Metric f a ​ b = ( − g ) α ​ g a ​ b f^{ab}=(-g)^{\alpha}g^{ab}


Abstract
In this short note we investigate canonical formalism for General Relativity which is formulated with the metric f a ​ b = ( − g ) α ​ g a ​ b f^{ab}=(-g)^{\alpha}g^{ab} . We find corresponding Hamiltonian and we show that constraint structure is the same as in the standard formulation.

Introduction and Summary

Dynamical variable of General Relativity is metric with components
g_{ab}
. They are natural variables for formulation of Riemann geometry and corresponding quantities as for example scalar curvature that is fundamental part of Einstein-Hilbert action. Properties of this action were carefully examined by T. Padmanabhan in

An importance of these variables was already stressed in

[

of General Relativity

Further, it was nicely shown in

that these variables have nice thermodynamic interpretation in the emergent gravity paradigm that claims that gravity is emergent from some unknown more fundamental theory. Then we could conjecture that
f^{ab}
variables are more fundamental than
g_{ab}
and study consequence of this hypothesis. In particular, it would be interesting to formulate canonical formalism
for these variables. By canonical formalism we mean conventional formalism based on
D+1
splitting of space time

[

]
, for review see
[

. The goal of this paper is investigate this question.

More precisely, we consider theory with the metric
f^{ab}
that is related to
g^{ab}
by point transformation
f^{ab}=(-g)^{\alpha}g^{ab}
, where parameter
\alpha
can be arbitrary number and our goal is to study dependence of the theory on
\alpha
. Then in order to find canonical formulation of theory for
f^{ab}
variable we should again perform
D+1
splitting of
f^{ab}
when we introduce variables
M,a^{ij},M^{i}
whose precise definitions will be given in the next section and which are related to similar splitting of
g^{ab}
metric in terms of
N,h_{ij},N^{i}
. With the help of these relations we will be able to find corresponding conjugate momenta. During this procedure we also find primary constraint that relates
a^{ij}
with
M
and which is a consequence of the fact that
M
is dynamical variable as opposite to the lapse
N
whose conjugate momentum is primary constraint of the theory in the original formulation. On the other hand performing standard manipulation we obtain Hamiltonian that has similar form as the standard one. Then the requirement of the preservation of the primary constraints leads to emergence of
D+1
secondary constraints which are Hamiltonian constraint together with
D
spatial diffeomorphism constraints. Finally we study stability of these constraints. It turns out that it is useful to express them in terms of the original variables as composite objects from
a^{ij},M
and conjugate momenta. Then it turns out that the constraints and Poisson brackets between them have the same form as in General Relativity.

We also consider the case when we use new set of variables for spatial metric
h_{ij}
only. In this case the situation is simpler than in the more general case since lapse function does not change. We determine corresponding Hamiltonian and constraint structure that has again the same form as in General Relativity. Finally we argue that this Hamiltonian can be derived by gauge fixing of the primary constraint in the model with dynamical
M
.

Let us outline our results. We investigate General Theory formulated in terms of new variable
f^{ab}
and study their constraint structure. We show that compared to the original case the Hamiltonian is more complicated and introducing new variables does not bring new benefits for the theory. In other words while
f^{ab}
variable has significant meaning in the thermodynamics interpretation of the theory and in the covariant canonical formalism standard Hamiltonian formalism is naturally formulated in terms of original metric
g_{ab}
and conjugate momenta.

This paper is organized as follows. In the next section (

2
Hamiltonian Formalism for
f^{ab}
metric

As we wrote in the introduction the goal of this paper is to study canonical structure of General Relativity action
which is expressed in terms of variable
f^{ab}=(-g)^{\alpha}g^{ab}
in
D+1
dimensions, where
a,b=0,1,\dots,D
. This form of the metric is generalization of the relation

f^{ab}=\sqrt{-g}g^{ab}
that was introduced long time ago by Einstein and whose
importance in the covariant canonical formalism was stressed recently by Padmanabhan in

[
1
]
. An unanswered question remains what is the form of the canonical structure for the General Relativity formulated with the variable
f^{ab}
. The goal of this paper is to find Hamiltonian for
f^{ab}
and conjugate momenta.

In the first step of our analysis we find inverse relations between
g^{ab}
and
f^{ab}
. From definition above we get

-f=(-g)^{\alpha(D+1)}(-g)^{-1}\ ,f\equiv\det f^{ab}\ ,g\equiv\det g_{ab}\ .

(1)

With the help of these results we find inverse relation

(-g)=(-f)^{\frac{1}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ ,\quad g^{ab}=f^{ab}(-f)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ .

(2)

In order to find canonical formulation of theory we again presume
D+1
decomposition
of metric
g_{ab}
. Explicitly, in case of metric
g_{ab}
we introduce
lapse function
N=1/\sqrt{-g^{00}}
and the shift function

N^{i}=-g^{0i}/g^{00}
. In terms of these variables we
write the components of the metric
g_{ab}
as

\displaystyle g_{00}=-N^{2}+N_{i}h^{ij}N_{j}\ ,\quad g_{0i}=N_{i}\ ,\quad g_{ij}=h_{ij}\ ,

\displaystyle g^{00}=-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\ ,\quad g^{0i}=\frac{N^{i}}{N^{2}}\ ,\quad g^{ij}=h^{ij}-\frac{N^{i}N^{j}}{N^{2}}\ ,

where
h_{ij}
is non-singular spatial
D-
dimensional metric with inverse

h^{jk}
so that
h_{ij}h^{jk}=\delta_{i}^{k}
.

Let us presume the same decomposition of
f^{ab}

and its inverse
f_{ab}

\displaystyle f_{00}=-M^{2}+M_{i}a^{ij}M_{j}\ ,\quad f_{0i}=M_{i}\ ,\quad f_{ij}=a_{ij}\ ,

\displaystyle f^{00}=-\frac{1}{M^{2}}\ ,\quad f^{0i}=\frac{M^{i}}{M^{2}}\ ,\quad f^{ij}=a^{ij}-\frac{M^{i}M^{j}}{M^{2}}\ ,

where
a_{ij}a^{jk}=\delta_{i}^{k}
and where
M^{i}=a^{ij}M_{j}
.
Then if we firstly compare
g^{00}
with
f^{00}
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}=\frac{1}{M^{2}}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ ,\quad a\equiv\det a_{ij}\ .

As the next step we consider relation between
g^{0i}
and
f^{0i}
and we get

\displaystyle N^{i}=M^{i}\frac{N^{2}}{M^{2}}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}=M^{i}\ ,

that implies equality of the shift functions

N^{i}=M^{i}\ .

(7)

Finally we proceed to the relation between
g^{ij}
and
f^{ij}
that allows us to find relation between
h^{ij}
and
a^{ij}
in the form

\displaystyle h^{ij}=a^{ij}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ .

In summary, we have following relations between original and new variables

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}=\frac{1}{M^{2}}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ ,\quad N^{i}=M^{i}\ ,\quad h^{ij}=a^{ij}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\ .

Now we are ready to proceed to the definition of the momenta conjugate to
M,M^{i}
and
a_{ij}
.
Note that the action for gravity in
D+1
dimensions has the form

S=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int d^{D+1}x\sqrt{-g}R\ ,\quad\kappa=16\pi G\ ,

(10)

where
R(g)
is scalar curvature. In order to find canonical action
we use
D+1
decomposition of
R

\displaystyle R=K_{ij}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}+r(h)+\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}[\sqrt{-g}n^{\mu}K]-\frac{2}{\sqrt{h}N}\partial_{i}[\sqrt{h}h^{ij}\partial_{j}N]\ ,

\displaystyle K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2N}(\partial_{t}h_{ij}-\nabla_{i}N_{j}-\nabla_{j}N_{i})\ ,

\displaystyle n^{0}=\sqrt{-g^{00}}\ ,\quad n^{i}=-\frac{g^{0i}}{\sqrt{-g^{00}}}\ ,

and where
r
is scalar curvature defined with
h_{ij}
and
\nabla_{i}
is covariant
derivative compatible with the metric
h_{ij}
. Note that the divergence terms in (
2
) can be ignored in the action (
10
).
Finally we introduced de Witt metric
\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}
and its inverse
\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}
that have the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}(h^{ik}h^{jl}+h^{il}h^{jk})-h^{ij}h^{kl}\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}(h_{ik}h_{jl}+h_{il}h_{jk})-\frac{1}{D-2}h_{ij}h_{kl}\ .

From their definition we obtain useful relations

h_{ij}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}=-(D-2)h^{kl}\ ,\quad h^{ij}\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}=-\frac{1}{D-2}h_{kl}\ .

(13)

As the first step we proceed with the momentum conjugate to
M
and we get

\displaystyle p_{M}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}M)}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}N)}\frac{\partial(\partial_{0}N)}{\partial(\partial_{0}M)}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{ij})}\frac{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{ij})}{\partial(\partial_{0}M)}=

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}N)}\frac{\partial N}{\partial M}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{ij})}\frac{\partial h_{ij}}{\partial M}=

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha(D-2)}{\kappa M(\alpha(D+1)-1)}\sqrt{h}K\ ,

where we used the fact that
f^{ab}
and
g^{ab}
are related by point transformations so that

\frac{\partial(\partial_{0}g^{ab})}{\partial(\partial_{0}f^{cd})}=\frac{\partial g^{ab}}{\partial f^{cd}}

(15)

and also the fact
h^{ij}h_{jk}=\delta^{i}_{k}
so that

\frac{\partial h_{ij}}{\partial M}=-h_{im}\frac{\delta h^{mn}}{\delta M}h_{nj}\ .

(16)

In case of the momenta conjugate to
M^{i}
we also get that they are primary constraints

\displaystyle\pi_{i}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}M^{i})}\approx 0

(17)

as follows from the fact that
M^{i}=N^{i}
and from the fact that the action (
10
) does not depend on time derivative of
N^{i}
.

Finally we proceed to the momentum conjugate to
a^{ij}
and we get

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}a_{ij})}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}N)}\frac{\partial(\partial_{0}N)}{\partial(\partial_{0}a^{ij})}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}\frac{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}{\partial(\partial_{0}a^{ij})}=

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}\frac{\partial h_{kl}}{\partial a^{ij}}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}h_{km}\frac{\delta h^{mn}}{\delta a^{ij}}h_{nl}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{G}^{klpr}h_{ki}h_{lj}K_{pr}(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}-\frac{p_{M}M}{2}a_{ij}\ .

Taking the trace of this equation we get

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}a^{ij}=\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha}p_{M}M

that implies primary constraint of the theory

\mathcal{G}\equiv\pi_{ij}a^{ij}-\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha}p_{M}M\approx 0\ .

(20)

Now we are ready to proceed to the definition of the canonical Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\pi_{ij}\partial_{0}a^{ij}+\pi_{M}\partial_{0}M-\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{ij})}\partial_{0}h_{ij}-\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}K_{ij}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}-\frac{1}{\kappa}N\sqrt{h}r+\frac{2}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}\nabla_{i}N_{j}\equiv

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{H}_{T}+\frac{2}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}\nabla_{i}N_{j}\ ,

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}\partial_{0}a^{ij}+\pi_{M}\partial_{0}M=

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}\frac{\partial h_{kl}(a,M)}{\partial a^{ij}}\partial_{0}a^{ij}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{kl})}\frac{\partial h_{kl}(a,M)}{\partial M}\partial_{0}M=

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}h_{ij})}\partial_{0}h_{ij}\ .

Then using previous relations we obtain explicit form of Hamiltonian in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}=\kappa M^{\frac{2\alpha D-1}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}a^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2(\alpha(D+1)-1)}}[\Pi_{mn}a^{mp}a^{nr}\Pi_{pr}-\frac{1}{D-2}\Pi_{mn}a^{mn}\Pi_{pr}a^{pr}]-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa}(M^{2}a)^{-\frac{1}{2[\alpha(D+1)-1]}}r(h)

where

\Pi_{ij}=\pi_{ij}+\frac{p_{M}M}{2}a_{ij}\ .

(24)

Finally we consider last part of the Hamiltonian and after some calculations we get

\displaystyle\int d^{D}\mathbf{x}\frac{2}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}\nabla_{i}N_{j}=\int d^{D}\mathbf{x}N^{m}\mathcal{H}_{m}\ ,

where
\mathcal{H}_{m}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{m}=2\partial_{i}[a^{ip}\pi_{pm}]+\partial_{m}[p_{M}M]-2\Gamma^{k}_{im}a^{ip}\pi_{pk}-\Gamma^{k}_{km}p_{M}M\ .

(26)

In summary we get Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{T}+M^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}

and set of primary constraints
\pi_{i}\approx 0,\mathcal{G}\approx 0
. In the next section we will study stability of these constraints.

3
Stability of Primary Constraints

In this section we study stability of the primary constraints.
We start with the constraints
\pi_{m}\approx 0
where the requirement of their preservations implies secondary constraints

\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbf{x})\approx 0\ .

(27)

In case of the constraint
\mathcal{G}\approx 0
the situation is more involved.
Recall that
\mathcal{G}\approx 0
has explicit form

\mathcal{G}\equiv\pi_{ij}a^{ij}-\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha}p_{M}M\approx 0\ .

(28)

This constraint has following Poisson brackets with canonical variables

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),a^{ij}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=-a^{ij}(\mathbf{x})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\quad\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),\pi_{ij}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\pi_{ij}(\mathbf{x})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),M(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}M\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\quad\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),p_{M}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}p_{M}(\mathbf{x})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\

that implies

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),M^{2}a(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\frac{\alpha(D+1)-1}{\alpha}M^{2}a(\mathbf{y})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\quad\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),h^{ij}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=0\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{x})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ .

We further have

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),M^{\frac{2\alpha D-1}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}a^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2(\alpha(D+1)-1)}}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}M^{\frac{2\alpha D-1}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}a^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2(\alpha(D+1)-1)}}(\mathbf{y})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),(M^{2}a)^{-\frac{1}{2(\alpha(D+1)-1)}}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}(M^{2}a)^{-\frac{1}{2(\alpha(D+1)-1)}}(\mathbf{y})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ .

Collecting these terms together we finally obtain

\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),\mathcal{H}_{T}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{T}(\mathbf{y})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ .

(32)

Further, since
\left\{\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}),\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=0

we immediately find that

\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}=\left\{\mathcal{G},H\right\}=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{T}

(33)

so that requirement of the preservation of the primary constraint
\mathcal{G}\approx 0
implies
secondary constraint

\mathcal{H}_{T}\approx 0\ .

(34)

At this stage we identified
D+1
secondary constraints
\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0\ ,\mathcal{H}_{T}\approx 0
together with
D+1
primary constraints
\pi_{i}\approx 0\ ,\mathcal{G}\approx 0
.
Now we should check stability of secondary constraints. To proceed to these calculations it is convenient to express
\mathcal{H}_{i}
in different way.

We start with following Poisson bracket

\left\{\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{x}),h^{kl}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{j}^{l}+\delta_{i}^{l}\delta_{j}^{k})\Sigma\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\Sigma=(M^{2}a)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}\

(35)

which has almost canonical form. In order to have Poisson brackets in the canonical form
we introduce
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}
defined as

\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}=\Sigma^{-1}h^{ik}\Pi_{kl}h^{lj}\

(36)

that has following Poisson brackets

\left\{\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}(\mathbf{x}),h_{kl}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{i}_{k}\delta^{j}_{l}+\delta^{i}_{l}\delta^{j}_{k})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ .

(37)

Note that
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}
is related to
K_{ij}
by following formula

\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}=-\frac{1}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}\ .

(38)

Then it is easy to see that the spatial diffeomorphism constraint can be written as

\mathcal{H}_{i}=2h_{il}\nabla_{k}\tilde{\Pi}^{lk}\ .

(39)

Clearly this diffeomorphism constraint has the same form as spatial diffeomorphism constraint derived in General Relativity.
Further, since the Poisson bracket between
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}
and
h_{kl}
(
37
) has the same form as in case of General Relativity(up to sign) we find that the Poisson brackets between two smeared forms of diffeomorphism constrains has the form

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{T}_{S}(X^{i}),\mathbf{T}_{S}(Y^{j})\right\}=\mathbf{T}_{S}(X^{j}\partial_{j}Y^{i}-Y^{j}\partial_{j}X^{i})\ .

(40)

Let us now turn our attention to the Hamiltonian constraint
\mathcal{H}_{T}
. In fact, using
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}
we can write Hamiltonian constraint in the form

\mathcal{H}_{T}=\frac{N\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}\tilde{\Pi}^{kl}-\frac{1}{\kappa}N\sqrt{h}r(h)\ ,

(41)

where
N
and
h
are composed from canonical variables
a^{ij}
and
M
.
Then it is clear that the Poisson brackets between smeared form of diffeomorphism constraints and Hamiltonian constraint has the same form as in general relativity.
Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{T}_{T}(X),\mathbf{T}_{T}(Y)\right\}=\mathbf{T}_{S}((NX\partial_{i}(NY)-NY\partial_{i}(XN))^{ij})\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{T}_{S}(X^{i}),\mathbf{T}_{T}(X)\right\}=\mathbf{T}_{T}(X^{i}\partial_{i}(NX)N^{-1})\ .

This result makes an analysis of General Relativity with
a^{ij}
and
\pi_{ij}
as canonical variables complete. We see that introducing these new variables
leads only to an emergence of new constraint
\mathcal{G}\approx 0
which replaces original constraint
p_{N}\approx 0
. Then the remaining constraint structure is completely the same. Further, from the form of the Hamiltonian and diffeomorphism constraint it is hardly to see that they would simplify resulting Hamiltonian. For that reason we mean that introducing new variables does not bring new benefits for theory.

4
New Set of Alternative Variables

In this section we derive canonical formulation for gravity when we introduce
spatial metric
a^{ij}
related to
h^{ij}
in the form

a^{ij}=h^{\beta}h^{ij}\ ,\quad h=\det h_{ij}

(43)

while
N
and
N^{i}
remain the same. As in the second section
we derive inverse relation between
h^{ij}
and
a^{ij}
in the form

h^{ij}=a^{ij}a^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}\

(44)

so that momentum conjugate to
a^{ij}
is equal to

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\partial_{0}a^{ij}}=-\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\partial_{0}h_{kl}}h_{kp}\frac{\delta h^{pr}}{\delta a^{ij}}h_{rl}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\kappa}\mathcal{G}^{klmn}K_{mn}h_{kp}h_{pr}[\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{p}_{i}\delta^{r}_{j}+\delta^{p}_{j}\delta^{r}_{i})-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}a^{pr}a_{ij}]a^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}\ .

As in previous section we find Hamiltonian in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\pi_{ij}\partial_{0}a^{ij}-\mathcal{L}=N\sqrt{h}K_{ij}\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}K_{kl}+\frac{N\sqrt{h}}{\kappa}\mathcal{G}^{klmn}K_{mn}\nabla_{k}N_{l}\ .

Taking the trace of the relation for
\pi_{ij}
we obtain

\displaystyle\pi=\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\kappa}\frac{1-D}{\beta D-1}K\

and finally we get

\displaystyle(\pi_{ij}-\beta\pi a_{ij})h^{im}h^{jn}=-\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\kappa}\mathcal{G}^{mnkl}K_{kl}a^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}\ .

Then the Hamiltonian constrain has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\Pi^{mn}\mathcal{G}_{mnkl}\Pi^{kl}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\sqrt{h}r(h)\ ,

where

\Pi^{ij}=h^{ik}(\pi_{kl}-\beta\pi a_{kl})h^{lj}a^{\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}\ .

(50)

Then using the fact that

\left\{\pi_{ij}-\beta\pi a_{ij},h^{kl}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{k}_{i}\delta^{l}_{j}+\delta^{k}_{j}\delta^{l}_{i})a^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}\

(51)

it is easy to see that the Poisson bracket between
\Pi^{ij}
and
h_{ij}=a_{ij}a^{\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}
has the canonical form (up to sign)

\left\{\Pi^{ij}(\mathbf{x}),h_{kl}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{i}_{k}\delta^{j}_{l}+\delta^{i}_{l}\delta^{j}_{k})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ .

(52)

Note that the Hamiltonian can be written in the form

\mathcal{H}=N\mathcal{H}_{T}-2\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}\nabla_{j}N_{i}\equiv N\mathcal{H}_{T}+N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\ ,\\

where

\mathcal{H}_{i}=h_{ik}\nabla_{j}\tilde{\Pi}^{jk}\ .

(53)

Now as in the case of General Relativity
\pi_{N},\pi_{i}
which are momenta conjugate to
N
and
N^{i}
are primary constraints of the theory. Then the requirement of their preservation implies that
\mathcal{H}_{T}\approx 0\ ,\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0
are secondary constraints that
have the same form as in the case of General Relativity and also thanks to the Poisson brackets
(
52
) we get that the Poisson brackets between these constraints are the same as in General Relativity. In other words
\mathcal{H}_{T}\approx 0,\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0
are first class constraints.

Now we show that this theory can be derived from the theory studied in section (
2
) when we fix the gauge symmetry
\mathcal{G}=\pi_{ij}a^{ij}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}p_{M}M\approx 0
. Let us fix this gauge symmetry by introducing gauge fixing function

\mathcal{F}\equiv M-K\approx 0\ ,K=\mathrm{const}
. Then the Poisson bracket between

\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
is non-zero and they are second class constraints that can be explicitly solved. Solving
\mathcal{G}
for
p_{M}
and
M
we get

p_{M}M=\frac{2\alpha\pi}{\alpha-1}\ .

(54)

Inserting this result into
\tilde{\Pi}^{ij}
defined in (
36
) we get
that we should identify
\beta
with
\alpha
as

\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}=-\beta\ .

(55)

For
K=1
we find that

h^{ij}=a^{ij}a^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha(D+1)-1}}=a^{ij}a^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta D-1}}
and the correspondence is exact.

As the final point of this section we express Hamiltonian constraint in terms of physical variables
a^{ij}
and
\pi_{ij}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}=\kappa a^{\frac{1}{2(\beta D-1)}}\left(\pi_{mn}a^{mk}a^{nl}\pi_{kl}+\frac{1}{D-2}(\pi_{ij}a^{ij})^{2}(-1+4\beta-2\beta^{2}D)\right)-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa}a^{-\frac{1}{2(\beta D-1)}}r(h)\ .

We see that generally this Hamiltonian constraint has similar form as in case of the original variables.
On the other hand we hardly see any simplification introducing new variables

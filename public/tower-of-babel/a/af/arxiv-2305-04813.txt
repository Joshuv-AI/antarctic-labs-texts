A fully conservative and shift-invariant formulation for Galerkin discretizations of incompressible variable density flow Journal: Journal of Computational Physics
Lukas Lundgren, Murtazo Nazarov

Abstract
This paper introduces a formulation of the variable density incompressible Navier-Stokes equations by modifying the nonlinear terms in a consistent way. For Galerkin discretizations, the formulation leads to favorable discrete conservation properties without the divergence-free constraint being strongly enforced. In addition, the formulation is shown to make the density field invariant to global shifts. The effect of viscous regularizations on conservation properties is also investigated. Numerical tests validate the theory developed in this work. The new formulation shows superior performance compared to other formulations from the literature, both in terms of accuracy for smooth problems and in terms of robustness.



1
Introduction.

The simulation of incompressible flow is important for industrial and scientific applications when modeling subsonic flow. In this work, we consider incompressible flow when the density field is variable, which
arises when multiple fluids interact. A pair of fluids can remain immiscible as seen in oil/water, gas/liquids and so on. Alternatively, fluids can diffuse and mix, as observed in pairs of gases and certain pairs of liquids such as salt water/fresh water, alcohol/water and more. These phenomena can be modeled using the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with variable density, along with an advection equation for the density field. Complexity is often added to the model by introducing additional terms to account for factors such as gravity, chemical reactions and surface tension. At a continuous level, when viscosity effects are neglected,
these equations can be shown to conserve mass, squared density, momentum, angular momentum and kinetic energy. The density field can also be shown to be shift-invariant which means that density remains invariant if it is shifted by a constant. These properties are fundamental to the flow model and are closely linked to the so-called divergence-free constraint which is pointwise satisfied. It is favorable for numerical approximations of variable density flow to fulfill as many of these physical properties as possible.

Unfortunately, numerical approximations of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations are rarely pointwise divergence-free
[
20
]
which leads to issues with conservation. In particular, most Galerkin methods are known to satisfy the divergence-free condition only weakly and much work has been performed to improve the conservation properties of these methods. Recently,
Charnyi et al. 2017
showed that, by consistently modifying the nonlinear term in the constant density Navier-Stokes equations, a fully conservative Galerkin method could be derived. The novelty of the formulation is that a modified pressure is solved for and the formulation has been named the energy, momentum and angular momentum conserving (EMAC) formulation. The EMAC formulation has later been extended to be more efficient by
Charnyi et al. 2019
, further analyzed by
Olshanskii and Rebholz 2020
and extended to projection methods by
Ingimarson et al. 2023
.

Unlike constant density flow, variable density flow has additional conserved properties and no fully conservative formulation is currently known in the literature. The current state-of-the-art formulations for variable density flow are based on skew-symmetric formulations that can be shown to conserve squared density and kinetic energy
[
13
]
or a formulation that can be shown to conserve mass, kinetic energy, momentum and angular momentum
[
30
,
22
]
. In this work, we extend the modified pressure technique
[
5
]
to variable density flow which leads to a new formulation that, when discretized by a Galerkin method,
is shift-invariant and also conserves mass, squared density, momentum and angular momentum. We show that the leading error term for discrete kinetic energy conservation is independent of the divergence-free constraint. The modified Galerkin method by
[
8
]
is also shift-invariant but instead leads to conservation of mass, squared density and kinetic energy with a leading error term for discrete momentum and angular momentum conservation independent of the divergence-free constraint.
The analysis is first performed on the inviscid problem, i.e., the incompressible Euler equations with variable density. Later, since viscous regularizations affect the conservation properties of the model, we also investigate this.

The theoretical findings are summarized in Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
, and these are verified using numerical experiments. The results show that the new formulation leads to improved accuracy and robustness compared to existing formulations in the variable density literature. Furthermore, the new formulation can easily be implemented into existing Galerkin-based variable density Navier-Stokes solvers.

The paper is organized the following way: In Section
2
we give the governing equations, present our notation and explain the properties of the governing equations that we later want our proposed numerical method to mimic. Then, in Section
3
we introduce an alternative formulation of the governing equations which, when discretized using a Galerkin method, leads to improved conservation properties in the semi-discrete form. In Section
4
we present a family of viscous regularizations that lead to various conserved properties of the model. In Section
5
a conservative second-order accurate time discretization is presented which leads to a fully discrete method. In Section
6
we perform numerical validations. In Section
7
we give concluding remarks.

2.3
Properties of the governing equations.

The governing equations can be shown to conserve mass, squared density, kinetic energy, momentum and angular momentum. These are defined as

\displaystyle\text{Mass}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}};\quad

\displaystyle\text{Kinetic energy}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}};\quad

\displaystyle\text{Angular momentum}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}};

(2.13)

\displaystyle\text{Momentum}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}};\quad

\displaystyle\text{Squared density}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

Another property that is rarely (if at all) mentioned in the variable density flow literature is that the mass equation is invariant to shifts in the density field.
To be more precise, we say that the advection equation is shift-invariant since

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\partial}_{t}(\rho+c)+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla(\rho+c)=0,\quad\forall c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}.}

(2.14)

On the other hand, the advection equation in conservative form is not shift-invariant since

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}\rho+\nabla{\cdot\,}(\rho{\boldsymbol{u}})=0\quad\nLeftrightarrow\quad{\partial}_{t}(\rho+c)+\nabla{\cdot\,}((\rho+c){\boldsymbol{u}})=0,\quad\forall c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}.}

(2.15)

3
Properties when
\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}\neq 0.

For most numerical methods,
\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}=0
does not hold pointwise at the discrete level. This is also true for most Galerkin methods where the numerical solution usually satisfies

(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},q)=0,\quad\forall q\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathcal{Q}}}.

(3.1)

We note that there are some exceptions to this. In particular, some exotic elements are pointwise divergence-free such as iso-geometric B-splines
[
7
]
, Raviart-Thomas elements
[
27
,
8
]
, Scott-Vogelius elements
[
28
]
and so on, but some of these are not included in all FEM software libraries and some introduce constraints on the computational mesh and polynomial degree. Additionally, not all divergence-free elements, such as
[
27
,
8
]

In this work, we analyze many variations of the nonlinear terms. Depending on how these are chosen, favorable conservation properties can be obtained which we investigate thoroughly in this work. To this end, we write the governing equations (

Remark 3.1
.

In this work, we provide strong numerical evidence supporting that shift invariance is a valuable property that enhances both the robustness and accuracy of variable density flow simulations. Ideally, we would expect a numerical method solving the advection equation to produce the same error for the initial conditions
\rho_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})
and
\rho_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+c
. However, if a numerical method for the advection equation is not shift-invariant this is not the case, i.e., the method behaves artificially differently when the initial condition is shifted. In simpler terms, such a method would produce larger discretization errors in its results for the initial condition
\rho_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+100
compared to
\rho_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The properties of Table
1
hold provided that the conditions inside it are met.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several parts. To simplify the proof we assume a periodic domain and set
{\boldsymbol{f}}=0
. Extending the proofs to other boundary conditions such as no-slip (
{\boldsymbol{u}}=0|_{{\partial}\Omega}
) can be done by using the proof technique in
[
5
, Sec 3.1.2]
or
[
16
, Sec 3.1.2]
.

Mass:

By using the definition
of mass
we have that (
3.5
) satisfies

{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{\Omega}{\partial}_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=-({\boldsymbol{u}},\nabla\rho)-\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho}).

(3.7)

Since
\nabla\bar{\rho}=0
, (
3.7
) is equivalent to

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&-({\boldsymbol{u}},\nabla(\rho-\bar{\rho}))-\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho})\\
=\ &(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho})-\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho})\\
=\ &\ (1-\alpha_{\rho})(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho}),\\
\end{split}

(3.8)

where integration by parts was used on the first term. Therefore mass is conserved if
\alpha_{\rho}=1
. If
\bar{\rho}=\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
then

\int_{\Omega}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}\rho-\bar{\rho}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=0,

(3.9)

which means that
(\rho-\bar{\rho})\in{\mathcal{Q}}
provided that
k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
. Invoking the weak divergence-free condition (
3.5
) shows that

(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\rho-\bar{\rho})=0,

(3.10)

which means that mass is conserved if
k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
.

Shift-invariance:

If
\alpha_{\rho}=0
then the proof is evident from (
2.15
). Otherwise, if
\bar{\rho}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
, the following is obtained by shifting
\rho
by a constant
c
in (
3.4
)

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}(\rho+c)+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla(\rho+c)+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})\left(\rho+c-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}(\rho+c)\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)&=0,\\
{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})\left(\rho+c-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}(\rho+c)\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)&=0,\\
{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})\left(\rho-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)&=0,\\
{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})\left(\rho-\bar{\rho}\right)&=0,\\
\end{split}

(3.11)

which shows that the formulation is shift-invariant.

Squared density:

By using that
\nabla\bar{\rho}=0
, one can show that (
3.5
) satisfies

\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}\rho,\rho-\bar{\rho})=-({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\rho-\bar{\rho})-\alpha_{\rho}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\rho-\bar{\rho}).

(3.12)

Using (
2.8
) inside (
3.12
) yields

\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\rho}\right)((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\rho-\bar{\rho}).

(3.13)

If
\alpha_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}
then
\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=0
. This shows that line 4 in Table
1
is true.

If
\bar{\rho}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
, then we can perform the following simplification

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}&=\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\bar{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
&=\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
&=\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)\\
&=\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}\right),\end{split}

(3.14)

to show that line 5 in Table
1
is true.

Lastly, we consider line 6 in Table
1
. By recalling that
\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho=0
if
k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
, (
3.14
) simplifies to

\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}},

(3.15)

which shows that squared density is conserved if
\alpha_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}
and
k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
.
Next, for
A\in{\mathbb{R}}
we also have

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Omega}A\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}&=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}\rho,\rho-A)=-({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla(\rho-A),\rho-A)-\alpha_{\rho}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\rho-A)\\
&=\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},\frac{1}{2}(\rho-A)^{2}-\alpha_{\rho}(\rho-\bar{\rho})(\rho-A)\right).\\
\end{split}

(3.16)

There exist two roots of
A
that satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\rho-A)^{2}-\alpha_{\rho}(\rho-\bar{\rho})(\rho-A)\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=0.

(3.17)

We only need one
A
to satisfy (
3.17
) and one of the roots (found symbolically using Matlab) are

\begin{split}A=|\Omega|^{-1}\left(\alpha_{\rho}|\Omega|\bar{\rho}+(1-\alpha_{\rho})\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}+\left(\alpha_{\rho}^{2}|\Omega|^{2}\bar{\rho}^{2}-2\alpha_{\rho}^{2}|\Omega|\bar{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}+\alpha_{\rho}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}+2\alpha_{\rho}|\Omega|\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.-2\alpha_{\rho}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}-|\Omega|\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).\end{split}

(3.18)

This means that, if
2k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
then there exists an
A
such that
\left(\frac{1}{2}(\rho-A)^{2}-\alpha_{\rho}(\rho-\bar{\rho})(\rho-A)\right)\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Because of this, we can invoke the weak divergence-free condition (
3.5
) to conclude that squared density is conserved.

Kinetic energy:

Using the definition of kinetic energy, the definition of the
L^{2}
-projection (
3.6
) and setting
w=\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}
,
{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{u}}
inside the density and momentum updates (
3.5
) yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\ \frac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{u}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}\rho},{\boldsymbol{u}})+(\rho{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})=({\boldsymbol{u}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}\rho,{\boldsymbol{u}})+(\rho{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{u}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}\rho},{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
=&\ ({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}\rho,{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}\rho,\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-(\nabla P,{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla({\boldsymbol{m}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}),{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)-\alpha_{\rho}\left(\nabla\left((\rho-\bar{\rho}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)}\right),{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right).\end{split}

(3.19)

Integration by parts on the pressure term and invoking the weak divergence-free constraint inside (
3.5
), using (
2.5
) on
\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})
and integration by parts on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{\rho}\left(\nabla\left((\rho-\bar{\rho})\left(\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)\right),{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)}
yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})+\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}\right)((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&+\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla({\boldsymbol{m}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}),{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)+\alpha_{\rho}\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}},(\rho-\bar{\rho}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)}\right)\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
\\
=&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})+\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}\right)((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla({\boldsymbol{m}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}),{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)}\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right).}\end{split}

(3.20)

Next,
using (
2.5
) on
b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})
and integration by parts on
\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla({\boldsymbol{m}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}),{\boldsymbol{u}})
yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}\right)((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}}),\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)}\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right).}\end{split}

(3.21)

which means that kinetic energy is conserved if
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}-\alpha_{P}-\alpha_{\rho}/2=1/2

and if
\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}=1
.

Momentum:

We set
{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}
inside the momentum equations (
3.5
) and obtain

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\ ({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})=-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-(\nabla P,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})\\
&-\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)+\alpha_{\rho}\left((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\right)\\
\\
=&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-(\nabla P,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})\\
&-\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right),\end{split}

(3.22)

since
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}
is a constant. Using (
2.5
) on
\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})
and
b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})
and integration by parts on the pressure term yield

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\ b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}})+((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})+(P,\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})\\
&+\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ (1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-b({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ (1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})-b(\rho{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ (1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}((\nabla{\cdot\,}(\rho{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})){\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ (1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}-(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right),\end{split}

(3.23)

where (
2.6
) was used in the third equality
and (
2.4
) was used in the second equality
. Thus, momentum is conserved if
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}=1

and if
\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}=0
.

Angular momentum:

Conservation of angular momentum is derived by setting
{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}
inside (
3.5
) to obtain

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}({\boldsymbol{m}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{x}})_{i}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\ ({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\partial}_{t}}{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})=-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-(\nabla P,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})\\
&-\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)+\alpha_{\rho}\left((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})(\rho-\bar{\rho}),{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\right)\\
\\
=&-b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-(\nabla P,{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})\\
&-\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right),\end{split}

(3.24)

since
\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}=0
. Using (
2.5
) on
\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{u}})
and
b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})
and integration by parts on the pressure term yield

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\int_{\Omega}({\boldsymbol{m}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{x}})_{i}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=&\ b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}})+((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})+(P,\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})\\
&+\left(\alpha_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\left(\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\right)b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}})+(1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-b({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{m}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}})+(1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})-b(\rho{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\
=&\ b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{m}})+(1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}})((\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}((\nabla{\cdot\,}(\rho{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i})){\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right)\\

Discussion.

One implication of Theorem
4.1
is that there is no viscous regularization
(\kappa>0)
that simultaneously dissipates kinetic energy and conserves angular momentum. Another implication is that a unique viscous regularization dissipates kinetic energy and conserves momentum. This can be seen by combining the kinetic energy and the momentum conditions in Theorem
4.1
to arrive at
A_{5}=A_{4}=1
,
A_{2}=0
and
A_{1}=A_{3}
. Setting these conditions inside (
4.2
) and performing some simplifications lead to the following viscous flux

{\boldsymbol{f}}_{GP}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}})^{\top}{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}+(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}){\boldsymbol{u}}+A_{1}(\nabla{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}){\boldsymbol{u}}+A_{1}(\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}=\nabla{\cdot\,}({\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}\otimes{\boldsymbol{u}})+A_{1}\nabla({\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}),

(4.19)

where the second term does not change the model since it only results that a modified pressure is solved for, i.e.,
P=p-A_{1}{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\rho}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}
, and does not affect the behavior of density and velocity. Since that term is not important, we are left with the so-called Guermond-Popov viscous flux
[
12
]
. Compared to (
4.19
), the Guermond-Popov flux
[
12
]
is simplified since it now is in the incompressible setting instead of the compressible setting. This means that, if we only consider the density and momentum contribution of
[
12
]
, it is exactly the same as (
4.19
) and (
4.1
).

Combining the last two conditions of Theorem
4.1

\left(\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n},\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)=\Delta t_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\rho}\right)\left(\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right)\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right),\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right).

(5.3)

Simplifying the left-hand side finally yields

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n+1}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n+1},\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n},\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)\\
&+\Delta t_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\rho}\right)\left(\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right)\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right),\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right),\end{split}

(5.4)

which is a fully discrete equivalent of (
3.13
). If
\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{n+1/2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
then

\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n+1}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n+1},\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n},\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)\right),

(5.5)

simplifies to

\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\rho^{n+1}\|^{2}-\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{n+1}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\rho^{n}\|^{2}-\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{n}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)^{2}\right),

(5.6)

which is a fully discrete equivalent of (
3.14
).

Lastly, similar steps can be performed to show that for
A\in{\mathbb{R}}

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n+1}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n+1},A\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{n}\|^{2}-\left(\rho^{n},A\right)\\
&+\Delta t_{n}\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2},\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-A\right)^{2}-\alpha_{\rho}\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-A\right)\right).\end{split}

(5.7)

There exists an
A
such that
\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-A\right)^{2}-\alpha_{\rho}\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}\right)\left(\rho^{n+1/2}-A\right)\right)\in{\mathcal{Q}}
provided that
2k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
. This means that squared density is conserved if
2k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
by using the weak divergence-free condition (
5.1
) and that mass is conserved.

Shift invariance:

Substituting
\rho^{n+j}
with
\rho^{n+j}+c
and repeating the steps of the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows that the density update (
5.1
) is invariant if
\alpha_{\rho}=0
or if
\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{n+1/2}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
.

Kinetic energy:

We begin by using the identity from
[
8
, Sec 3]
and the definition of the
L^{2}
-projection (
3.6
)
to obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho^{n+1}}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho^{n}}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}\|^{2}&=\left(\rho^{n+1}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}-\rho^{n}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}\right)\\
&=\left(\rho^{n+1}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}-\rho^{n}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n},\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}}\right).\end{split}

(5.8)

Next, repeating the steps of the proof of Theorem
3.1
on (
5.1
) leads to

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\sqrt{\rho^{n+1}}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\sqrt{\rho^{n}}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}\|^{2}\right)=\Delta t_{n}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{P}+\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}}\right)\left(\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right){\boldsymbol{m}}^{n+1/2},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right)\\
\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}{\cdot}\nabla\rho^{n+1/2}+\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2})(\rho^{n+1/2}-\bar{\rho}^{n+1/2}),{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}}+(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}-\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}}}\right)}\\
\end{split}

(5.9)

which shows that kinetic energy is conserved if
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}-\alpha_{P}-\alpha_{\rho}/2=1/2

and if
\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}=1
.

Momentum:

Setting
{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}
inside (
5.1
) and repeating the steps of the proof of Theorem
3.1

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{i}^{n+1}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{i}^{n}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta t_{n}}\left(1-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}\right)\left(\left(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}\right){\boldsymbol{m}}^{n+1/2},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta t_{n}}\left({\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla\rho^{n+1/2},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}\overline{{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1}}-(1-\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}}){\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n+1/2}}\right).\end{split}

(5.10)

Momentum is conserved if

(5.11)

6.1
Accuracy test.

In this section, we verify the accuracy of the proposed formulations using a manufactured solution on a unit disk. The time step is computed using CFL = 0.025 and the initial time step is used throughout the whole simulation. A small time step is chosen so that the error in time is negligible. Since the error will be dominated by spatial errors, the expected convergence rate is then 4 for velocity and 3 for pressure. The expected rate for density is 4 for all formulations except SI-MEDMAC where we expect a rate of 3. We follow the setup from
[
14
]
where the forcing function
{\boldsymbol{f}}
is chosen to obtain the following exact solution

\begin{split}&\rho({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=2+x\cos(\sin(t))+y\sin(\sin(t)),\\
&{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=\begin{bmatrix}-y\cos(t)\\
x\cos(t)\end{bmatrix},\\
&p({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=\sin(x)\sin(y)\sin(t),\\
&P({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=p-\alpha_{\rho}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}\bar{\rho}{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\left(p-\alpha_{\rho}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rho}\bar{\rho}{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.\end{split}

(6.1)

Note that we have included the modified pressure
P
above which is the one that is solved for in (
5.1
).
Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed for the velocity. The boundary conditions are imposed strongly through the linear system using the routine implemented in FEniCS
[
2
]
.
We perform a convergence study using a series of unstructured meshes. The dynamic viscosity is set to

and we set

The convergence results are presented in Figure

Overall, the velocity errors converge with the expected convergence rate 4 and all formulations have similar errors. Similarly, the pressure errors converge with the expected rate of 3 with similar errors for all formulations. However, for density, the convergence rate drops to around 3.5 for all formulations except the LSI-EMAC formulation. The reason for this is that the added term in the density update
\alpha_{\rho}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho})
pollutes the accuracy of

Another important observation is that the shift-invariant formulations are more accurate than their counterpart, i.e., SI-EDMAC is more accurate than EDMAC, and SI-MEMAC is more accurate than MEMAC. More specifically, the error for density is roughly 1 order of magnitude lower for SI compared to the non-SI formulations.

7
Conclusion.

By consistently modifying the governing equations, we introduce a new formulation for the incompressible variable density Navier-Stokes equations. When discretized by a Galerkin method, the formulation allows the method to be shift-invariant and mass, squared density, kinetic energy, momentum and angular momentum conserving without the divergence-free constraint being strongly enforced.
The formulation we propose for the inviscid case (
2.1
) is

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}})(\rho-\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle=0,

(7.1)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}{\boldsymbol{m}}+{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}\nabla{\boldsymbol{m}}+(\nabla{\cdot\,}{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}}+\nabla P+(\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{m}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\rho({\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}})-\frac{1}{2}\nabla((\rho-\bar{\rho}){\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}})\right)

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{f}},

where
P=p-\frac{1}{4}\rho{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\rho}{\boldsymbol{u}}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{u}}
is a modified pressure and
\bar{\rho}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
. If
\rho=1
, the formulation simplifies to the EMAC formulation by
Charnyi et al. 2017
.
One important finding is that by ensuring that the polynomial degree of the mixed FEM formulation satisfies
k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
, mass conservation is automatically achieved. Similarly, by setting
2k_{\rho}\leq k_{P}
, squared density conservation can be automatically achieved.
We numerically compare the new formulation to some previously considered in the literature (see Table
2
). The new formulation performs the best on the benchmarks considered in this manuscript, both in terms of accuracy for smooth problems and in terms of robustness.

Lastly, we consider the effect of viscous regularizations and our theoretical findings are summarized in Theorem
4.1
. One implication of Theorem
4.1
is that, if mass diffusion is included in the model, there is no viscous regularization that simultaneously dissipates kinetic energy and conserves angular momentum. Another implication is that a unique viscous regularization dissipates kinetic energy and conserves momentum.

As additional work, one could extend the formulation to more realistic multiphase flow models where surface tension is included such as Cahn-Hilliard or similar. One interesting research direction would be to consider positivity-preserving discretizations that are also shift-invariant. It would also be interesting to investigate which viscous regularization in Section
4
gives the best match with experimental data from real-world experiments. For underresolved flows, i.e.,
\kappa\ll|{\boldsymbol{u}}|h
or
\mu\ll\rho|{\boldsymbol{u}}|h
,
we recommend that a stabilization technique
[
29
,
21
,
23
,
24
]
is used.

Appendix B
Lock-exchange 3D.

In this section, we consider the lock-exchange problem in 3D. Even though the theory is developed for the Crank-Nicolson scheme, one can use the viscous regularization and formulations developed in this work with different time-integration schemes such as high-order (backward differentiation formula) BDF methods. To this end, we consider the variable time-step BDF4 method from
[
21
]
which we modify to be fully implicit.

We follow the setup of
Bartholomew and Laizet 2019
. The setup is similar to the 2D case with a few exceptions. No-slip boundary conditions are used on the top and bottom boundaries and slip is used on the remaining boundaries.
These boundary conditions are imposed strongly through the linear systems. A Neumann condition
{\boldsymbol{n}}{\cdot}\nabla\rho|_{\partial\Omega}=0,
is automatically imposed weakly on all boundaries through the weak form (
5.1
).
The Reynolds number is computed in the same as in 2D, but now it is set to
Re=2236
instead. The domain is set to
\Omega=\{(x,y{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},z})\in(-1L,17L){\times}(0,2L){\times}(0,2L)\}
.

A random perturbation is added to the initial velocity

{\boldsymbol{u}}(0,{\boldsymbol{x}})=X\cdot 0.05\cdot\exp{(-25x^{2})}+(0,0,0)^{\top},

(B.1)

where
X\sim U(-1,1)
, a uniform distribution of random numbers in
(-1,1)
. The authors of
[
3
]
added this perturbation ‘to simulate that a physical barrier is removed’.

We compute the solution using 15,696,120
{\mathbb{P}}_{3}
nodes on a uniform mesh which corresponds to a similar mesh resolution as
Bartholomew and Laizet 2019
. We also set
\text{CFL}=0.1
and use the LSI-EMAC formulation using
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbb{P}}_{3}{\times}{\mathbb{P}}_{3}{\times}{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}
elements. The nonlinear system is solved using the relative tolerance
10^{-7}
. The results are presented at
T=20
in Figure
8
. In the figure, we compare the Guermond-Popov viscous flux (
{\boldsymbol{f}}_{GP}
) and the momentum and angular momentum conserving flux that
Bartholomew and Laizet 2019
use (
{\boldsymbol{f}}_{0}
). The simulations closely match
Bartholomew and Laizet 2019
, but there is no pointwise match due to the random perturbations in the initial condition and differences in the numerical method.

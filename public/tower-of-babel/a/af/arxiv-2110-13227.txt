Abstract
. A recent question of Gabriel Navarro asks whether it is true that the derived length of a defect group is less than or equal to the number of degrees of irreducible characters in a block. In this article, we bring new evidence towards the validity of this statement.



1.
Introduction

One of the main themes in the representation theory of finite groups is to relate global and local invariants. For example, for a prime
p
, Brauer’s Problem 12 asks what can be said about the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
from the makeup of the set of irreducible characters
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. More generally, the problem of determining structural properties of a defect group of a
p
-block
B
of
G
based on the set
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
of irreducible characters of
B
has been an important topic of study in the area.
The proof of one direction of the Brauer Height Zero Conjecture in
[
KM13
]
and the recent proof for principal blocks
[
MN21
]
have been major breakthroughs in this line of investigation. Other recent results studying the
p
-structural properties
of the defect groups in terms of properties of the irreducible characters in the block
can be found in
[
FLLMZ19
]
,
[
GRSS20
]
and
[
NRSV21
]
. The present paper is a contribution to this lively area of research.

Let
B
be a Brauer
p
-block of a finite group
G
, with defect group
D
. We write

\mathrm{cd}(B)=\{\chi(1)\mid\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)\}
for the set of the degrees
of the complex irreducible characters in
B
and
\mathrm{dl}(D)
for the derived length of the solvable group
D
. This article’s main motivation is the following question, recently appeared in
[
Mar21
]
.

Question A

(Navarro)
.

Is it true that
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq|\mathrm{cd}(B)|
?

Question
A
has a positive answer
if
|\mathrm{cd}(B)|=1
. In fact, a theorem of Okuyama and Tsushima
[
OT83
]
shows that in this case the defect group
D
is abelian. Similarly, Navarro’s prediction is confirmed whenever
D
is normal in
G
by
[
Rey63
, Theorem 8]
.
On the other hand, at the time of this writing,
the case where
|\mathrm{cd}(B)|=2
remains an open problem, although it would follow from an affirmative answer to the Brauer Height Zero Conjecture, along with a conjecture of Malle and Navarro
[
MN11
]
.
Specializing the question to the principal block
B=B_{0}(G)
, it was recently shown by the second author in
[
Mar21
]
that it is true that if
|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))|=2
then the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
have derived length at most
2
.
In this article we extend the above mentioned result by proving that the following holds.

Theorem B
.

Let
p

be a prime, let
G
be a finite group with Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
, and let
B_{0}(G)
be the principal
p
-block of
G
.
If
|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))|\leq 3
, then
\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))|
.

As mentioned before, Theorem B extends the results of
[
Mar21
]
by confirming Question
A
for principal blocks with at most three distinct character degrees.
The strategy we use to prove this statement is to reduce it to a few technical statements about the principal blocks of non-abelian simple groups (see Section
2
).
Checking these statements led us to the discovery of some interesting features of several important classes of finite groups. For instance, we show that Question
A
holds for symmetric and alternating groups, as well as for general linear groups in defining characteristic (respectively denoted by
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
) as a consequence of the following stronger statement.

Theorem C
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
B
be a
p
-block of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
with
q
a power of
p
. Let
D
be a defect group of
B
. Then
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq|\mathrm{ht}(B)|.

Here
\mathrm{ht}(B)
denotes the set of heights of the irreducible characters in
B
.

We care to remark that it is not possible in general to bound the derived length of the defect groups by the number of distinct heights in the corresponding block. In fact, in Section
5
we construct a solvable group that does not satisfy the conclusion of Theorem
C
.
It is worth pointing out that this construction also provides a counterexample to the main conjecture of the recent article
[
FLZ20
]
.

2.
Reduction to simple groups

2.1.
Auxiliary lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
N\triangleleft\,G
, and
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
. Assume
\theta
extends to
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. Then
\{\mu\chi\mid\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))\}\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G|\theta)\cap\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
.

Proof.

By Gallagher’s correspondence,
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\theta)=\{\mu\chi\mid\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(G/N)\}
. We have
\chi=1_{G/N}\chi
and then by
[
Riz18
, Lemma 2.4]
, if
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))
then
\mu\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
.∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t}\triangleleft\,G
, where the
S_{i}
’s are subgroups permuted by
G
. Let
S=S_{1}
. Assume there exists
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}({\bf N}_{G}(S)/{\bf C}_{G}(S))
in the principal block and such that
S
is not contained in
{\rm ker}(\alpha)
. Then
\alpha^{G}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
.

Proof.

Write
H={\bf N}_{G}(S)
and
C={\bf C}_{G}(S)
. Let
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(S)
be under
\alpha
. Now let
\psi=\eta\times 1_{S_{2}}\times\dots\times 1_{S_{t}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t})
. Notice that
G_{\psi}=H_{\eta}
. Since
S_{2}\cdots S_{t}\subseteq C\subseteq{\rm ker}(\alpha)
we have that
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(H|\psi)
. Hence
\alpha^{G}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
by Clifford’s theorem. Now by
[
N98
, Corollary 6.2]
,
B_{0}(H)^{G}
is defined and contains
\alpha^{G}
, and by Brauer’s Third Main Theorem
[
N98
, Theorem 6.7]
,
B_{0}(H)^{G}=B_{0}(G)
so we are done.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t}\triangleleft\,G
, where the
S_{i}
’s are subgroups permuted transitively by
G
. Let
S=S_{1}
,
S_{i}=S^{x_{i}}
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(S)
. If
\alpha
is
{\bf N}_{G}(S)
-invariant then
\eta=\alpha^{x_{1}}\times\dots\times\alpha^{x_{t}}
is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
s\in S
,
x\in G
and
H={\bf N}_{G}(S)
. Notice that
G=\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}Hx_{j}
is a disjoint union, so
x^{-1}=hx_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,t\}
and some
h\in H
. Then

\eta^{x}(s)=\eta(s^{x^{-1}})=\eta(s^{hx_{j}})=\alpha^{x_{j}}(s^{hx_{j}})\prod_{i\neq j}\alpha^{x_{i}}(1).

Now
s^{h}\in S
and then
\alpha^{x_{j}}(s^{hx_{j}})=\alpha(s^{h})
. Since
\alpha
is
H
-invariant we have

\eta^{x}(s)=\alpha(s^{h})\prod_{i\neq j}\alpha^{x_{i}}(1)=\alpha(s)\prod_{i\neq 1}\alpha^{x_{i}}(1)=\eta(s),

so
\eta^{x}(s)=\eta(s)
.

Now
\eta^{x}(s^{x_{j}})=\eta^{xx_{j}^{-1}}(s)=\eta(s)=\eta^{x_{j}^{-1}}(s)=\eta(s^{x_{j}})
applying the equality from the previous paragraph twice. In particular, for any
y\in S_{i}
,
\eta^{x}(y)=\eta(y)
.

Let
s_{i}\in S_{i}
and notice that

\eta(s_{1}\cdots s_{t})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\eta(s_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\alpha_{i}(1)\right)}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\eta(s_{i})}{\alpha(1)^{t^{2}-t}}

then if
x\in G
we have

\eta^{x}(s_{1}\cdots s_{t})=\eta(s_{1}^{x^{-1}}\cdots s_{t}^{x^{-1}})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\eta(s_{i}^{x^{-1}})}{\alpha(1)^{t^{2}-t}}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\eta(s_{i})}{\alpha(1)^{t^{2}-t}}

which equals
\eta(s_{1}\cdots s_{t})
, so we are done.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
N=S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t}\triangleleft\,G
, where the
S_{i}
’s are subgroups transitively permuted by
G
. Let
S=S_{1}
. Assume that there exists
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(S))
with
\alpha(1)
not divisible by
p
and such that it extends to
\hat{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(S)))
. Then the tensor induced character
\alpha^{\otimes G}
is in the principal block of
G
.

Proof.

Write
H={\bf N}_{G}(S)
. Let
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\}
be a transversal for
H
in
G
such that
S^{x_{i}}=S_{i}
and notice that
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{i})=H^{x_{i}}
. Also, let
\alpha_{i}=\alpha^{x_{i}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(S_{i})
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\alpha^{x_{1}}\times\dots\times\alpha^{x_{t}}
is
G
-invariant. Let
M=\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}H^{x_{i}}\triangleleft\,G
. Since
B_{0}(H)
covers
B_{0}(M)
then
\hat{\alpha}_{M}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(M))
. By
[
N18
, Corollary 10.5]
, the tensor induced character
\chi=\hat{\alpha}^{\otimes G}
is an irreducible character of
G
. Also, by
[
N18
, Lemma 10.4]
we have that for
g\in M
,

\chi(g)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\hat{\alpha}^{x_{i}}(g).

Then
\chi_{N}=\alpha_{1}\times\dots\times\alpha_{t}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
and
\chi_{M}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\hat{\alpha}^{x_{i}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(M)
. Moreover,
\hat{\alpha}^{x_{i}}(1)=\alpha(1)
is not divisible by
p
, so
\chi_{M}
is in the principal block of
M
by
[
NT12
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Now let
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(M)
. We have that
Q\cap S_{i}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(S_{i})
. Then
{\bf C}_{G}(Q)\subseteq{\bf C}_{G}(Q\cap S_{i})\subseteq H^{x_{i}}
, and then
{\bf C}_{G}(Q)\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}H^{x_{i}}=M
.
By
[
NT12
, Lemma 3.1]

B_{0}(M)^{G}
is defined and is the unique block of
G
covering
B_{0}(M)
. Now by Brauer’s Third Main Theorem
[
N98
, Theorem 6.7]
,
B_{0}(M)^{G}=B_{0}(G)
and we conclude that
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and assume
{\bf C}_{G}({\bf O}_{p}(G))\subseteq{\bf O}_{p}(G)
. Then
G
has a unique
p
-block.

Proof.

Let
P={\bf O}_{p}(G)
. Let
b
be the unique block of
P
. Since
{\bf C}_{G}(P)P\subseteq P
, by
[
N98
, Theorem 4.14]

b^{G}
is defined. By
[
N98
, Theorem 4.8]
,
P
is contained in every defect group of every block of
G
. Applying the second part of
[
N98
, Theorem 4.14]
, we conclude that
b^{G}
is the unique block of
G
.
∎

We shall need the following results on simple groups.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(3^{k})
for
k\geq 3
and let
p=3
. Then for any
S\leq T\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
there is a character
\gamma\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(T))
such that
\gamma_{S}=a\alpha
for some
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(S))
,
a\in\{1,2\}
and
\alpha(1)\geq 13
is not divisible by

.

Proof.

This can be found in the proof of

Mar21
, Proposition 3.8]
.

The proof of the next three theorems is done in sections 3 and 4.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

and let

be a finite nonabelian simple group of order divisible by

. Let

be an almost simple group with socle

and assume

Then there is some

and some

Let

.
If

Let

2.3.
The reduction theorem

Next we prove our main result assuming Theorems
2.7
,
2.8
, and
2.9
, whose proofs will be delayed to sections 3 and 4. For this reduction theorem, we make use of the following fact: the unique integral solution for
xy=x^{y}
with
x,y>1
is
x=y=2
. Furthermore if
a,x,y
are positive integers and
a,x>1
then
xy<ax^{y}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime and assume
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))=\{1,m,n\}
. Then the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
have derived length at most 3.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|G|
.

Step 1: We may assume
p=2,3
,
p
divides
m
but not
n
and that
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

If
p
divides
n
and
m
then by
[
IS76
, Corollary 3]
we have
G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is a
p
-group. By
[
N98
, Theorem 10.20]
,
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))=\operatorname{Irr}(G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G))
, so
G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
has 3 character degrees, and then the result holds by
[
I06
, Theorem 12.15]
. If
p
does not divide
n
and
m
then the result follows by the main theorem of
[
MN21
]
. Hence we assume
p
divides
m
but not
n
. If
p>3
then by the main result of
[
GRSS20
]
we have
G
is
p
-solvable. Again, by
[
N98
, Theorem 10.20]
,

\operatorname{Irr}(G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G))=\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
and then
G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
has at most 3 degrees and we are done by
[
I06
, Theorem 12.15]
. Hence we assume
p=2,3
. Also, using
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)))=\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
(by
[
N98
, Theorem 9.9(c)]
) and arguing by induction we may assume
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

Step 2:
G
has a nontrivial component
K
with
|K/{\bf Z}(K)|
divisible by
p
.

Let
{\bf{E}}(G)
be the layer of
G
. If
{\bf{E}}(G)=1
then
{\bf{F}}^{*}(G)={\bf{F}}({G})={\bf O}_{p}(G)
and then
{\bf C}_{G}({\bf O}_{p}(G))\subseteq{\bf O}_{p}(G)
by
[
A86
, (31.13)]
. Then
G
has a unique block by Lemma
2.5
, and we are done by
[
I06
, Theorem 12.15]
. We may assume that
{\bf{E}}(G)>1
. Let
K
be a component of
G
and let
N
be the normal closure of
K
in
G
. By
[
KS03
, (6.5.3)]
,
\prod_{g\in G}K^{g}\leq G
, so
N=\prod_{g\in G}K^{g}
. If
|K|
is not divisible by
p
then
|N|
is not divisible by
p
, contradicting
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
from Step 1, so
K
has order divisible by
p
. By
[
A86
, (33.12)]
we have that
K/{\bf Z}(K)
is also divisible by
p
.

Step 3: If
N
is the normal closure of
K
in
G
then
N/{\bf Z}(N)
is a direct product of isomorphic copies of
K/{\bf Z}(K)
and
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/{\bf Z}(N)))=\{1,m,n\}
.

By
[
KS03
, (6.5.3)]
,
N
is a central product of
G
-conjugates of
K
. Write
\bar{G}=G/{\bf Z}(N)
. Then by
[
KS03
, (1.6.7)]
,
N/{\bf Z}(N)=\bar{N}=\prod\bar{K^{g}}=\bar{K}_{1}\times\dots\times\bar{K}_{t}
is a direct product of non-abelian simple groups
\bar{K_{i}}\cong K/{\bf Z}(K)
, and the
\bar{K}_{i}
’s are transitively permuted by
\bar{G}
. We write
\bar{K_{1}}=\bar{K}
. If
|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))|<3
then by the main result of
[
Mar21
]
we have
\bar{G}
is
p
-solvable, which is impossible, so
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))=\{1,n,m\}
.

Step 4: If
p=3
then
\bar{K}\ncong\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(9)

Let
\bar{H}/\bar{C}={\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{K})/{\bf C}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{K})
. There is a natural isomorphism
\bar{K}\cong\bar{K}\bar{C}/\bar{C}\subseteq\bar{H}/\bar{C}
and we view
\bar{H}/\bar{K}\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{K})
. If
\bar{K}\cong\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(9)
and
p=3
then it is easy to check in
[
GAP
]
that for any
\bar{K}\leq\bar{T}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{K})
there are two characters
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{T}))
that do not contain
\bar{K}
in their kernel with
\alpha(1)\neq\beta(1)
and both
\alpha(1)
and
\beta(1)
are not divisible by
p
. If
\bar{T}=\bar{H}/\bar{C}
, then by Lemma
2.2
we have that
\alpha^{\bar{G}},\beta^{\bar{G}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
and
\alpha^{\bar{G}}(1)=t\alpha(1)
,
\beta^{\bar{G}}(1)=t\beta(1)
, (both
p^{\prime}
or both divisible by
p
, depending on
t
). This is a contradiction, so
\bar{K}
can not be isomorphic to
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(9)
.

Step 5: If
p=3
and
\bar{K}\cong\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(3^{k})
for
k\geq 3
then we may assume
\bar{K}\triangleleft\,\bar{G}
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma
and
a\in\{1,2\}
be as in Lemma
2.6
. Since
\bar{K}
has abelian Sylow 3-subgroups, we know that there exists
\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{H}))
with
\beta(1)\neq\gamma(1)
and
\bar{K}\not\subseteq{\rm ker}(\beta)
by
[
Mar21
, Theorem 2.2]
. Then we can argue as before with Lemma
2.2
, and we get that
\gamma^{\bar{G}},\beta^{\bar{G}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
, so
t\gamma(1),t\beta(1)\in\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
. Then necessarily
n=\gamma^{\bar{G}}(1)=t\gamma(1)=ta\alpha(1)
, and
m=t\beta(1)
. Also by
[
Mar21
, Lemma 4.4]
there exists some
1\leq e\leq a^{t}\leq 2^{t}
not divisible by 3 such that
n=e\alpha(1)^{t}
. Thus
ta\alpha(1)=e\alpha(1)^{t}
. It is easy to see that the above equality has no integral solutions verifying
\alpha(1)\geq 13
and
t>1
.

Step 6: In the remaining cases, we may also assume
\bar{K}\triangleleft\,\bar{G}
.

Assume by way of contradiction that
t>1
. Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{K}))
with
\alpha(1)>2
, and
\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{H}/\bar{C}))
be as in Theorem
2.7
. Let
\hat{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{H}/\bar{C}))
be an extension of
\alpha
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\hat{\alpha}^{\bar{G}},\hat{\beta}^{\bar{G}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
. Hence
t\alpha(1),t\beta(1)\in\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))
. Since
\alpha(1)\neq\beta(1)
we have that
t
is not divisible by
p
(because
n
is not divisible by
p
).

If
\alpha(1)
is not divisible by
p
, then by Lemma
2.4
,
\hat{\alpha}^{\otimes\bar{G}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
, so
\hat{\alpha}^{\otimes\bar{G}}(1)=\alpha(1)^{t}\in\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G))
. Since
\beta(1)
is necessarily divisible by
p
, this forces
\alpha(1)^{t}=t\alpha(1)=n
and if
t>1
the only possibilty is
\alpha(1)=2=t
, a contradiction with
\alpha(1)>2
.

We are left with the case that
p
divides
\alpha(1)
, so
\beta(1)
is
p^{\prime}
. By
[
N18
, Corollary 10.5]

\chi=\hat{\alpha}^{\otimes\bar{G}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\bar{G})
extends
\eta=\alpha^{x_{1}}\times\dots\times\alpha^{x_{t}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{N}))
, where
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\}
is a transversal of
\bar{H}
in
\bar{G}
(recall that
\eta
is
G
-invariant by Lemma
2.3
). By
[
N98
, Theorem 9.4]
there exists some
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
over
\eta
. By Gallagher’s theorem,
\psi=\mu\chi
for some
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(\bar{G}/\bar{N})
. Since
p
divides
\alpha(1)
then
p
divides
\psi(1)
and
\psi(1)=m
. Then
\psi(1)=\mu(1)\chi(1)=\mu(1)\alpha(1)^{t}=m=t\alpha(1)
, but this forces
\mu(1)=1
and
\alpha(1)=2=t
, which contradicts the fact that
\alpha(1)>2
. This proves the claim.

Step 7:
G/{\bf C}_{G}(K)
is almost simple with socle
K{\bf C}_{G}(K)/{\bf C}_{G}(K)\cong K/{\bf Z}(K)
.

By Steps 5 and 6 any component
K
of
G
is normal in
G
. Write
C={\bf C}_{G}(K)
and
Z={\bf Z}(K)
. Since
K/Z\cong KC/C\triangleleft\,G/C
, it suffices to prove that
{\bf C}_{G/Z}(K/Z)=C/Z
. Let
xZ\in{\bf C}_{G/Z}(K/Z)
. We have that for all
k\in K
there exists a unique
z_{k}\in Z
such that
k^{x}=kz_{k}
. Notice that the map
k\mapsto z_{k}
is a homomorphism
K\rightarrow Z
. Since
K
is perfect and
Z
is abelian,

for all

, so

. The reverse inclusion is immediate.

Final Step.

If
|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/C))|>3
then we are done because
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/C))\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. Hence we assume
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/C))=\{1,m,n\}
(if
|\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/C))|<3
then
G/C
is
p
-solvable by the main result of
[
Mar21
]
). Since
m
is divisible by
p
, by the main result of
[
KM13
]
the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G/C
are nonabelian. Then by Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
we have
p=2
and that the Sylow 2-subgroups of
K
are metabelian. By Theorem
2.7
there is some nontrivial
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(K))
that extends to
\hat{\eta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/K))\subseteq\{1,m/n\}
and since

we have
\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(G/K))=\{1\}
. In particular, if

then

is abelian by the main result of
[
MN21
]
, and

is metabelian, so

.
∎

The primary goal of this section is to prove that Theorems

, and

Proposition 3.1
.

Theorems

,
2.8
, and
2.9
hold if
S
is a sporadic simple group,
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
, or a group of Lie type with exceptional Schur multiplier.

Proof.

This can be seen using GAP and the GAP Character Table Library
[

]
. For the list of groups of Lie type with exceptional Schur multipliers, see
[
GLS94

. ∎

Let
q
be a power of some prime
r
. By a simple group of Lie type defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we mean a finite simple group that can be written
S=H/{\bf Z}(H)
, where

is the set of fixed points of a simple, simply connected algebraic group

under a Frobenius morphism

endowing
{\mathbf{H}}
with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational structure.

Let
\widetilde{S}
be the group of inner-diagonal automorphisms of

. Then
\operatorname{Aut}(S)=\widetilde{S}\rtimes D
, where

is an appropriate group of graph and field automorphisms. (See
[
GLS94
, Section 2.5]
.) Further, we may let
{\mathbf{H}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{{\mathbf{H}}}
be a regular embedding as in
[
CE04
, (5.1)]
and
\widetilde{H}=\widetilde{{\mathbf{H}}}^{F}
, such that
{\bf Z}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{H}}})
is connected,

, and
\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{H}/{\bf Z}(\widetilde{H})
.

It is useful to note that the characters of

are partitioned into so-called Lusztig series indexed by semisimple characters

of an appropriate dual group

. When

3.1.
Some Notes on Unipotent Characters and Classical Groups

Keeping the notation from before, unipotent characters of
\widetilde{H}
are irreducible on restriction to
H
and trivial on
{\bf Z}(\widetilde{H})
. So, we may consider unipotent characters as characters of
S,\widetilde{S},H
, or
\widetilde{H}
. Often, unipotent characters also extend to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. (See
[
Mal08
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.5]
.) One such unipotent character, the Steinberg character, has degree
|\widetilde{H}|_{r}
. For
X\in\{S,\widetilde{S},H,\widetilde{H}\}
, we will denote the Steinberg character of
X
by
\mathrm{St}_{X}
.

The explicit list of unipotent characters and their degrees can be found in
[
Car85
, Section 13.9]
for groups of exceptional type. Hence in this section, we primarily focus on the case of classical groups.
The following will be useful for finding a rough lower bound on the number of character degrees in the principal block in this case. Throughout, we will use the notation
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, to denote
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=1
and
\operatorname{PSU}_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=-1
, and these are the simple groups of type
\operatorname{A}_{n-1}(q)
and
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{A}_{n-1}(q)
, respectively. Similarly,
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n}^{\epsilon}(q)
will denote the simple group of type
\operatorname{D}_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=1
and type
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{D}_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=-1
. For completeness, we recall that
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
denotes the simple group of type
\operatorname{B}_{n}(q)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
q
be a power of some prime
r
. Let
S=\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
with
n\geq 2
,
{\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
with
n\geq 2
,
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
with
n\geq 3
, or
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)
with
n\geq 4
. Then there are more than
\frac{n+1}{2}
unipotent characters of
\widetilde{S}
(and of
S
) with distinct degrees that extend to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.

Proof.

The unipotent characters of
S
or of
\widetilde{S}
are in bijection with partitions of
n
in the case of
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
and in bijection with certain “symbols” in the other cases. (See, e.g.
[
Car85
, Section 13.8]
for details.)

If
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, consider the characters corresponding to the partitions
(n-j,j)
for
1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
. If
S={\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
or
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
, consider the symbols
{j,n-j+1}\choose{0}
for

. In the case of
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n}(q)
and
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n}^{-}(q)
, consider the symbols
{j}\choose{n-j}
and
{j,n-j}\choose{\emptyset}
, respectively, for

. Using
[
Ols86
, (21), (22)]
, the
r
-part of the degree of the corresponding unipotent characters is
q^{j}
, except in the case
r=2
and
S={\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
or
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
, in which case it is
q^{j}/2
. Taking into account the Steinberg and trivial characters, this shows that there are strictly more than
\frac{n-1}{2}+1
distinct unipotent character degrees. Further, using
[
Mal08
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.5]
, we see that each of these characters extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. (Note that the exception of
{\mathrm{PSp}}_{4}(q)
when

and

does not actually occur in our list here, as
\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor=0<1
in this case.)
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type and let
\widetilde{\chi}
be a unipotent character of
\widetilde{S}
that lies in
B_{0}(\widetilde{S})
and extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. Then
B_{0}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))
contains an extension of
\widetilde{\chi}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\operatorname{Aut}(S)=\widetilde{S}\rtimes D
. First, suppose that
S\neq\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
, so that the group
D
generated by graph and field automorphisms is abelian. (See, e.g.
[
GLS94
, Theorem 2.5.12]
.) Since
\widetilde{\chi}
extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, we have by Gallagher’s theorem that every member of
\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))
above
\widetilde{\chi}
is an extension since
D
is abelian. In particular, using
[
N98
, Theorem 9.4]
, there exists an extension of
\widetilde{\chi}
in
B_{0}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))
, as stated.

Now suppose for the remainder of the proof that
S=\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)={\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{8}^{+}(q)
. In this case,
D\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}\times D_{1}
, where
D_{1}
is a cyclic group of field automorphisms and
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
is the symmetric group on 3 letters, generated by graph automorphisms. Write
X
for the group such that
X/\widetilde{S}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
and let
Y\lhd X
such that
Y/\widetilde{S}
is cyclic of size 3. Since
\widetilde{\chi}
extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, we have
\widetilde{\chi}
extends to three characters
\hat{\chi},\beta\hat{\chi},\beta^{-1}\hat{\chi}
of
Y
, where
\beta
and
\beta^{-1}
are the characters of
\operatorname{Irr}(Y/\widetilde{S})
of order 3, using Gallagher’s theorem. Now, since
Y/\widetilde{S}
is abelian, any block of
Y
lying above
B_{0}(\widetilde{S})
is of the form
B_{0}(Y)\otimes\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\operatorname{Irr}(Y/\widetilde{S})
. In particular,
B_{0}(Y)
either contains all three of
\hat{\chi},\beta\hat{\chi},\beta^{-1}\hat{\chi}
or exactly one of these characters. Say
\hat{\chi}\in B_{0}(Y)
.

Note that for every
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/|Y|})/{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have
\hat{\chi}^{\sigma}
also lies in
B_{0}(Y)
. Further,
\widetilde{\chi}
is rational-valued (see
[
Lus02
, Corollary 1.12]
). Then since
\chi^{\sigma}=\chi
, we have
\hat{\chi}^{\sigma}\in\{\hat{\chi},\beta\hat{\chi},\beta^{-1}\hat{\chi}\}
. So, if
B_{0}(Y)
contains just one of the three characters, we know
\hat{\chi}^{\sigma}=\hat{\chi}
. Since
\beta
is not rational-valued,
\hat{\chi}
is then the unique character of
Y
above
\widetilde{\chi}
that is rational-valued. Let
\alpha\in X
. Then since
\widetilde{\chi}
is
\alpha
-invariant, we see
\hat{\chi}^{\alpha}\in\{\hat{\chi},\beta\hat{\chi},\beta^{-1}\hat{\chi}\}
. But note that
\hat{\chi}^{\alpha}
is also rational-valued, and hence
\hat{\chi}^{\alpha}=\hat{\chi}
. Then
\hat{\chi}
must be the extension of
\chi
to
Y
that extends all the way to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. On the other hand, if
B_{0}(Y)
contains all three of
\{\hat{\chi},\beta\hat{\chi},\beta^{-1}\hat{\chi}\}
, we may assume without loss that
\hat{\chi}
is the one that extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.

In either case, since
D/Y
is abelian, we know every character of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
above
\hat{\chi}
is an extension. In particular, there is an extension of
\hat{\chi}
(and hence of
\widetilde{\chi}
) to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
that lies in
B_{0}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))
, again using
[
N98
, Theorem 9.4]
.
∎

For
p
an odd prime and
q
an integer not divisible by
p
, we write
d_{p}(q)
for the order of

modulo
p
. In what follows, if

is an odd prime not dividing

, we will write

to denote

in the case

and

in the case

,
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
, or
{\operatorname{P\Omega}}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
q
be a power of some prime. Let
S
be a simple group such that

with

,
{\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
with

,

, or

denote the principal

(i)

.

(ii)

If

[

.
If

to see that if

contains the Steinberg character along with all of the unipotent characters constructed in Proposition

, except possibly in the case of type

or

when

. In the latter case, either the character corresponding to the symbol

in Proposition

or the character corresponding to the symbol

lies in

, and the latter still satisfies that

and

extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.
In the case of (3), using

]
, we see

contains at least the unipotent characters constructed in Proposition

such that

is even, along with the trivial and Steinberg character. But,

, which has

as the

. This gives more than

Hence in each case,

Proposition 3.6
.

holds if

is not

and

T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j})=\begin{cases}((a-1),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)&\mathrm{if}\ T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})=((a),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset),\ \exists\ a\in[1,p-1]\\
(\gamma_{a-1},\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)&\mathrm{if}\ T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})=(\gamma_{a},\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset),\ \exists\ a\in[2,p-1]\\
((p-1),(1),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)&\mathrm{if}\ T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})=(\gamma_{1},\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset),\\
((p-1),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)&\mathrm{if}\ T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})=((p-1),(1),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset).\end{cases}

We will now verify that for every
j\in[0,k-1]
the partition
\lambda_{j}
is well-defined and labels a character lying in
B=B(\gamma,w)
. Afterwards we will show that
h_{p}(\lambda_{j})=h_{p}(\lambda_{i})
if and only if
i=j
. This will allow us to conclude that
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq|\mathrm{ht}(B)|
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Given
j\in[0,k-1]
we have that
\lambda_{j}
is well-defined and labels a character lying in
B=B(\gamma,w)
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
j\in[0,k-1]
. The base case is immediate. The partition
\lambda_{0}
is well defined,
\gamma=T_{0}(\lambda_{0})
is the
p
-core of
\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}
is a partition of
n
since

|\lambda_{0}|=\sum_{i=0}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{0})|p^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}p^{i}=n.

Let now
j\in[1,k-1]
. Let us start by showing that the definition of its
p
-core tower is consistent. In order to do this we need to show that each row
T_{i}(\lambda_{j})
is well-defined. If
i\notin\{k-j,k-j+1\}
then no problems arise, as
T_{i}(\lambda_{j})=T_{i}(\lambda_{j-1})
. For
i\in\{k-j,k-j+1\}
, we need to verify that

T_{k-j}(\lambda_{j-1})\in\{((a),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)\ |\ a\in[0,p-1]\},

and that

T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})\in\{((a),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset),(\gamma_{a},\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset),((p-1),(1),\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)\ |\ a\in[1,p-1]\}.

Both statements are verified again by induction (on
j-1
). They are clearly satisfied by
\lambda_{0}
, and for
j-1\geq 1
they remain true by direct verification (using the recursive definition of the partitions
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1}
given in Definition
4.5
).
Now, we want to show that
\lambda_{j}
is a partition of
n
for all
j\in[0,k-1]
. The base case
j=0
has been already verified above. Let
j\geq 1
and let us suppose that
|\lambda_{j-1}|=n
. Then we observe that
T_{i}(\lambda_{j})=T_{i}(\lambda_{j-1})
, for all
i\in[0,k]\smallsetminus\{k-j,k-j+1\}
.
Moreover, we have that
|T_{k-j}(\lambda_{j})|=|T_{k-j}(\lambda_{j-1})|+p
and that
|T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j})|=|T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})|-1
. It follows that

|\lambda_{j}|=\sum_{i=0}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{j})|p^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{j-1})|p^{i}+p\cdot p^{k-j}-1\cdot p^{k-j+1}=|\lambda_{j-1}|=n.

Since
c_{p}(\lambda_{j})=T_{0}(\lambda_{j})=\gamma
, we deduce that
\chi^{\lambda_{j}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)
, for all
j\in[0,k-1]
.
∎

We conclude by showing that the irreducible characters labelled by the partitions
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1}
have distinct character degrees. In the following statement we keep the notation introduced in Definition
4.5
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
j\in[0,k-1]
. Then
h_{p}(\chi^{\lambda_{j}})=j
.

Proof.

To simplify the notation, given a partition
\mu
we will denote by
h_{p}(\mu)
the integer
h_{p}(\chi^{\mu})
. We also recall that
w
denotes the
p
-weight of the block
B(\gamma,w)
and that
wp
has
p
-adic expansion given by

wp=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}p^{i}.

We proceed by induction on
j\in[0,k-1]
. Using Theorem
4.3
we see that

h_{p}(\lambda_{0})=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{0})|-a_{i}\big)/(p-1)=0.

The second equality follows from the definition of
\lambda_{0}
having
|T_{i}(\lambda_{0})|=a_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
.
Let now
j\geq 1
and let us assume that
h_{p}(\lambda_{j-1})=j-1
. As already explained in the proof of Lemma
4.6
we have that
T_{i}(\lambda_{j})=T_{i}(\lambda_{j-1})
, for all
i\in[0,k]\smallsetminus\{k-j,k-j+1\}
.
Moreover, we have that
|T_{k-j}(\lambda_{j})|=|T_{k-j}(\lambda_{j-1})|+p
and that
|T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j})|=|T_{k-j+1}(\lambda_{j-1})|-1
.
Using Theorem
4.3
, it follows that

(p-1)h_{p}(\lambda_{j})=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{j})|-a_{i}\big)=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}(\lambda_{j-1})|-a_{i}\big)+p-1=(p-1)h_{p}(\lambda_{j-1})+p-1.

Using the inductive hypothesis we get
h_{p}(\lambda_{j})=h_{p}(\lambda_{j-1})+1=(j-1)+1=j
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
4.1
and, as a consequence, to verify Theorems
2.7
,
2.8
, and
2.9
for alternating groups
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
. We recall that, given a partition
\lambda
of
n
, the restriction
(\chi^{\lambda})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}
is irreducible whenever
\lambda
is not equal to its conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
\chi^{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n}))
then every irreducible constituent of
(\chi^{\lambda})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}
lies in
\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{n}))
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
G\in\{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n},{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}\}
and let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
with defect group
D
. Then
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq|\mathrm{ht}(B)|
.

Proof.

If
n\leq 4
the statement holds by direct verification. Hence, let us assume that
n\geq 5
.
We first deal with the case where
G={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
Let
B=B(\gamma,w)
for some
p
-core
\gamma
and some integer
w
such that
n=|\gamma|+wp
. Let
wp=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}p^{i}
be its
p
-adic expansion. Then by Lemma
4.2
we have that
\mathrm{dl}(D)=k
. From the discussion started in Definition
4.5
and ended with Proposition
4.7
we know that the
p
-block
B
admits
k
irreducible characters whose
p
-heigths are pairwise distinct. It follows that
|\mathrm{ht}(B)|\geq k=\mathrm{dl}(D)
, as desired.

Let now
G={\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
. Let
B
be a
p
-block of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
, covered by the
p
-block
\tilde{B}
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
Let
\gamma
and
w
be such that
\tilde{B}=B(\gamma,w)
and let
wp=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}p^{i}
be its
p
-adic expansion. Let
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1}
be the partitions of
n
described in Definition
4.5
.
Let
Q
be a defect group of
B
and
D
a defect group of
\tilde{B}
chosen so that
Q\leq D
(if
p\neq 2
then
Q=D
).
Since
n\geq 5

[
Ols94
, Proposition 3.5]
shows that
(\lambda_{i})^{\prime}\neq\lambda_{j}
for all
i,j\in[0,k-1]
. It follows that
\{\mathrm{h}_{p}((\chi^{\lambda_{i}})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}})\ |\ i\in\ [0,k-1]\}
is a subset of size
k
of
\mathrm{ht}(B)
. We conclude that
\mathrm{dl}(Q)\leq\mathrm{dl}(D)=k\leq|\mathrm{ht}(B)|
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Theorem
2.7
holds for alternating groups
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
, for
n\geq 5
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\{2,3\}
and
n
be a natural number.
The cases
n\leq 7
for
p=2
and
n\leq 26
for
p=3
can be easily checked in GAP.
Let us now suppose to be in one of the remaining cases. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{k}\leq n<p^{k+1}
. Let
B=B_{0}({\mathfrak{S}}_{n})
. Since
k\geq 3
, recycling the notation introduced in Definition
4.5
setting
\gamma=c_{p}((n))
, it follows that
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are well-defined partitions of
n
,
labelling irreducible characters lying in
B
. Using
[
Ols94
, Proposition 3.5]
we observe that

(\lambda_{i})^{\prime}\neq\lambda_{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,2\}
. It follows that
\alpha:=(\chi^{\lambda_{2}})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{n}))
and
\alpha(1)>2
because
p^{2}
divides
\alpha(1)
by Proposition
4.7
. Similarly
\beta:=\chi^{\lambda_{1}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)
. By Proposition
4.7
we know that
p
divides
\beta(1)
and that
p^{2}
does not. It follows that
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
is not a subgroup of
\mathrm{Ker}(\beta)
and that
\alpha(1)\neq\beta(1)
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
hold for alternating groups
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
, for
n\geq 5
.

Proof.

We start with Theorem
2.8
.
Let
B
be the principal
2
-block of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. If
5\leq n\leq 15
the statement can be checked in GAP. Let us now assume that
n\geq 16
. Setting
\gamma=c_{p}((n))
we have that the partitions
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
are well defined partitions of
n
and label irreducible characters of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
lying in
B
. By
[
Ols94
, Proposition 3.5]
we know that
(\lambda_{i})^{\prime}\neq\lambda_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq 3
. It follows that
\{(\chi^{\lambda_{i}})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}\ |\ 0\leq i\leq 3\}
is a subset of size
4
of
\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{n}))
.

To conclude we now verify Theorem
2.9
. Clearly
n\geq 9
as a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
is nonabelian. In this case we consider
\lambda=(n)
,
\mu=(n-3,1^{3})
and
\eta=(n-3,3)
.
Clearly
(\chi^{\lambda})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}},(\chi^{\mu})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}},(\chi^{\eta})_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{n}))
and have pairwise distinct character degrees.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

We conclude by mentioning that Theorem
4.1
was already known to hold in the case of symmetric groups for primes
p\geq 5

[
Mor04
]
. Moreto’s proof relies on the important result of Granville and Ono
[
GO96
]
, showing that for every natural number
n
there exists a
p
-core partition of size
n
, provided that
p
is at least
5
. Our proof does not use this fact; it is uniform for all prime numbers and allows us to deduce the needed results on alternating groups.

5.
More on Question
A

In this section, we discuss certain cases of Question
A
and related questions. We denote by
{\rm{ht}}(B)
the set of heights of characters in a block
B
.

5.1.
Blocks of the general linear group in defining characteristic

Using results of A. Moretó, we show that Question
A
holds for blocks of the general linear group in defining characteristic.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
B
is a
p
-block of positive defect of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
, where
q
is a power of
p
, then
|{\rm{ht}}(B)|\geq n-1
.

Proof.

This is
[
Mor04
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The Sylow
p
-subgroups of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
, where
q
is a power of
p
, have nilpotency class
n-1
.

Proof.

This is
[
Hup67
, Satz III.16.3]
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Question
A
has an affirmative answer for the blocks of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
in defining characteristic.

Proof.

The result follows from Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
using that the derived length is bounded by the nilpotency class and
|{\rm{ht}}(B)|\leq|\mathrm{cd}(B)|
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
N\triangleleft\,{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
for a power
q
of a prime
p
, and assume
|{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q):N|
is not divisible by
p
. Then Question
A
has an affirmative answer for the
p
-blocks of
N
. In particular, Question
A
holds for the blocks of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(q)
in the defining characteristic.

Proof.

Let
b
be a block of
N
and let
B
be a block of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
covering
b
. By
[
N98
, Theorem 9.26]
, the defect groups of
B
are defect groups of
b
. Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(b)
and let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)
be over
\psi
(see
[
N98
, Theorem 9.4]
). It follows from
[
N18
, Theorem 5.12]
that
\psi(1)_{p}=\chi(1)_{p}
so we see that
{\rm{ht}}(B)={\rm{ht}}{(b)}
, so this result follows from the argument of the proof of Corollary
5.3
.
∎

5.2.
Checking GAP libraries

Let
p
be a prime and let
D
be a
p
-group of size
p^{k}
. Since groups of order
p^{2}
are abelian, it is easy to see that
\operatorname{dl}(D)\leq\frac{k+1}{2}
. Using this bound, we have checked in
[
GAP
]
that Question
A
holds for all sporadic groups. Furthermore, Question
A
has been checked for all perfect groups and the primitive groups of degree up to
1500
and size up to
10^{6}
. Whenever the bound above does not work, in most cases it suffices to check that
|\mathrm{cd}(B)|\leq\operatorname{dl}(P)
for a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
, since
\operatorname{dl}(D)\leq\operatorname{dl}(P)
for any defect group
D
. Otherwise, the fact that if
D
is a defect group of some block, then
D={\bf O}_{p}({\bf N}_{G}(D))
(see
[
N98
, Corollary 4.18]
) helps locating possible defect groups of a block if the defect is known.

5.3.
Related questions

If
D
is a
p
-group, Taketa’s Theorem
[
I06
, Theorem 5.12]
states that
|\mathrm{cd}(D)|\geq\operatorname{dl}(D)
, so questions regarding the derived length of
D
are closely related to its set of character degrees. The following conjecture was recently asked in
[
FLZ20
]
.

Conjecture 5.5

(Feng–Liu–Zhang)
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of a finite group
G
with defect group
D
. Let
p^{a}
be the maximal element in
\mathrm{cd}(D)
and let
b
be the maximal height of the characters in
B
. Then
a\leq b
.

Using
[
GAP
]
notation, let
G=\verb+SmallGroup(729, 955)+
. Let
A=\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{13}(A)
. Let
D\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}({\bf N}_{A}(Q))
and
R=\langle D,Q\rangle\leq A
. We consider the semidirect product
H=G\rtimes R
. Then
H
is a solvable group of size
28431=3^{7}\cdot 13
, with
{\bf O}_{3^{\prime}}(H)=1
. Then
\operatorname{Irr}(H)=\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(H))
and
\mathrm{cd}(H)=\mathrm{cd}(B_{0}(H))=\{1,3,13,39\}
. However if
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(H)
we have
\mathrm{cd}(P)=\{1,3,9\}
, so
H
is a counterexample to Conjecture
5.5
. This example is based on an unpublished note of I. M. Isaacs regarding a similar question. In fact, this provides a counterexample to Conjecture
5.5
for all odd primes, as asked by G. Malle after G. Navarro found the counterexample
\verb+SmallGroup(192,955)+
for
p=2
.

In Corollaries
5.3
and
5.4
, as well as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we have used the bound
\operatorname{dl}(D)\leq|{\rm ht}(B)|
taking advantage of the fact that
|{\rm ht}(B)|\leq|\mathrm{cd}(B)|
. It is a natural question to ask if we can improve the bound in Question
A
by
\operatorname{dl}(D)\leq|{\rm ht}(B)|
in general. However this bound does not hold even in solvable groups; the group
H
constructed above is a counterexample, as its Sylow
3
-subgroups have derived length 3. We mention that for
p=2
, the group
PerfectGroup(17280,1)
is a counterexample for this bound. No solvable counterexamples have been found for
p=2
.

Finally, we would like to remark that if
G
is solvable and
B
is the principal block, then an affirmative answer to Question
A
would follow from assuming the Isaacs–Seitz conjecture (see
[
N10
, (8.2)]
).

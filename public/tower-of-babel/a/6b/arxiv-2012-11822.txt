Supplementary Note 1: Dynamical phase transitions for other parameters and initial/final states

Here, we describe in detail dynamical phases and their transitions of the stroboscopic Ising model for situations different from that presented in the main text.

F_{\mathrm{\infty,T}}^{\mathrm{Tr}}

T=8

b

Supplementary Note 2: Derivation of spacetime-dual operators

Here, we describe in detail the derivation of the spacetime-dual operators to calculate the dynamical free energies, following Refs.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
We first seek for the representation for
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[U^{T}]}{2^{L}}=\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}^{L}]

(1)

or equivalently

\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L,T}=-\frac{\log|\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}^{L}]|}{L}.

(2)

For this purpose, we notice

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[U^{T}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\right\}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{T}\braket{\mathbf{s}_{\tau+1}|e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{L}b\sigma_{j}^{x}}e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{L}J\sigma_{j}^{z}\sigma_{j+1}^{z}-i\sum_{j=1}^{L}h\sigma_{j}^{z}}|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sin 2b}{2i}\right)^{LT/2}\sum_{\left\{s_{\tau,j}\right\}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(Js_{\tau,j}s_{\tau,j+1}+J^{\prime}s_{\tau,j}s_{\tau+1,j}+hs_{\tau,j})},

(3)

where
\ket{\mathbf{s}_{\tau}}
are the computational basis,
s_{\tau,j}
are classical spin variables taking
\pm 1
, and
J^{\prime}=-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{i}{2}\log\tan b
.
On the other hand, we can consider

\displaystyle\tilde{U}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{Tr}}=e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma^{x}_{\tau}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}(\tilde{J}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}+{h}\sigma_{\tau}^{z})},

(4)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{U}^{\prime L}_{\mathrm{Tr}}]=\left(\frac{\sin 2\tilde{b}}{2i}\right)^{LT/2}\sum_{\left\{s_{\tau,j}\right\}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(\tilde{J}^{\prime}s_{\tau,j}s_{\tau,j+1}+\tilde{J}s_{\tau,j}s_{\tau+1,j}+hs_{\tau,j})}

(5)

with
\tilde{J}^{\prime}=-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{i}{2}\log\tan\tilde{b}
.
Then, introducing a normalization constant

\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sin 2b}{\sin 2\tilde{b}}\right)^{\frac{LT}{2}}

(6)

and setting
\tilde{b}=\mathrm{arctan}[e^{2i(J+\pi/4)}]=\frac{i}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+e^{2iJ}}{1-e^{2iJ}}\right)=-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{i}{2}\log\tan J
(to satisfy
\tilde{J}^{\prime}=J
) with
\tilde{J}=J^{\prime}
,
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}=C\tilde{U}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{Tr}}=Ce^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma^{x}_{\tau}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}(\tilde{J}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}+{h}\sigma_{\tau}^{z})},

(7)

which satisfies Supplementary Equation (
1
)
as desired.

Next,
\tilde{U}_{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}
can be calculated similarly.
We show that they are represented as

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}=C^{\prime}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\tilde{b}\sigma_{\tau}^{x}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-2}\tilde{J}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}h\sigma_{\tau}^{z}-i\tilde{J}(\sigma_{1}^{z}+I\sigma_{T-1}^{z})}

(8)

with the open boundary condition, where

C^{\prime}=(\sin 2b/2i)^{1/2}(\sin 2b/\sin 2\tilde{b})^{(T-1)/2}e^{-i(h+J)}

, and
I=1\>(-1)
for
\tilde{U}_{\uparrow\uparrow}\>(\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow})
.
To see this, we notice (for
\ket{\psi_{f}}=\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}/\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
)

\displaystyle\braket{\psi_{f}|U^{T}|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\left\{\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\right\}}}\braket{\psi_{f}|U|\mathbf{s}_{T-1}}\cdots\braket{\mathbf{s}_{1}|U|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sin 2b}{2i}\right)^{LT/2}\sum_{{\left\{s_{\tau,j}\right\}}}

\displaystyle e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\sum_{j=1}^{L}Js_{\tau,j}s_{\tau,j+1}+hs_{\tau,j}-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}J^{\prime}s_{\tau,j}s_{\tau+1,j}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{L}{\left\{(J+h)+J^{\prime}s_{1,j}+J^{\prime}Is_{T-1,j}\right\}}},

(9)

where
I=1
for
\ket{\psi_{f}}=\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}
and
I=-1
for
\ket{\psi_{f}}=\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
.
To construct dual operators, we consider
(T-1)
-spins along time with the open boundary condition.
In fact, if we assume Supplementary Equation (
8
), we find

\displaystyle F^{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}_{L,T}=-\frac{\log|\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{U}_{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}^{L}]|}{L}.

(10)

Supplementary Note 3: Existence of antiunitary symmetry

Here, we describe in detail the antiunitary symmetry (AUS) of the spacetime-dual operator.
First, we show that the spacetime-dual operator
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}
with
J=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
satisfies

\displaystyle V\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}^{*}V^{\dagger}=e^{i\phi}\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}},

(11)

where

\displaystyle V=\prod_{\tau=1}^{T}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\sigma_{\tau}^{y}}

(12)

and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
J=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
,
\tilde{b}=\pm\pi/4
becomes real.
Noticing
\tilde{J}=-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{i}{2}\log\tan b=-\tilde{J}^{*}-\frac{\pi}{2}
, the left-hand side of Supplementary Equation (
11
) becomes

\displaystyle V\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}^{*}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=CVe^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma^{x}_{\tau}}V^{\dagger}Ve^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}(\tilde{J}^{*}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}+{h}\sigma_{\tau}^{z})}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma^{x}_{\tau}}e^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}(\tilde{J}^{*}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}-{h}\sigma_{\tau}^{z})}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma^{x}_{\tau}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}(\tilde{J}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}+{h}\sigma_{\tau}^{z})}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\sigma^{z}_{\tau}\sigma^{z}_{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{T}\left(-i\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}\right)=(-i)^{T}\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}},

(13)

which is the right-hand side.
Since
VV^{*}=\mathbb{I}
for even
T
and
-\mathbb{I}
for odd
T
,
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}
belongs to Class AI for even
T
and Class AII for odd
T
.

Next, we show that
\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}
with
J=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
satisfies

\displaystyle V\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}^{*}V^{\dagger}=e^{i\phi}\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow},

(14)

where

\displaystyle V=\mathcal{P}\prod_{\tau=1}^{T-1}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\sigma_{\tau}^{y}}

(15)

and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\mathcal{P}
is the parity operator exchanging the site
\tau
and
T-\tau
.
In fact, the left-hand side of Supplementary Equation (
14
) becomes

\displaystyle V\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}^{*}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime*}Ve^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\tilde{b}\sigma_{\tau}^{x}}V^{\dagger}Ve^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-2}\tilde{J}^{*}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}+i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}h\sigma_{\tau}^{z}+i\tilde{J}^{*}(\sigma_{1}^{z}-\sigma_{T-1}^{z})}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{i\zeta}C^{\prime}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\tilde{b}\sigma_{\tau}^{x}}e^{i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-2}\tilde{J}^{*}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}h\sigma_{\tau}^{z}-i\tilde{J}^{*}(-\sigma_{1}^{z}+\sigma_{T-1}^{z})}

\displaystyle=e^{i\zeta}\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}+i\frac{\pi}{2}(-\sigma_{1}^{z}+\sigma_{T-1}^{z})}

\displaystyle=e^{i\zeta}\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}\sigma_{1}^{z}\sigma_{T-1}^{z}\prod_{\tau=1}^{T-1}\left(-i\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i\zeta}(-i)^{T-1}\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow},

(16)

which is the right-hand side.
Here, we have used
\tilde{b}\in\mathbb{R}
for
J=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
,
\tilde{J}^{*}=-(\tilde{J}+\pi/2)
,

C^{\prime*}/C^{\prime}=e^{i\zeta}\>(\zeta\in\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathcal{P}\sigma_{1}\mathcal{P}=\sigma_{T-1}
.
Since
VV^{*}=\mathbb{I}
for odd
T
and
-\mathbb{I}
for even
T
,
\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}
belongs to Class AI for odd
T
and Class AII for even
T
.

We note that the minus sign associated with the exchange between
\sigma_{1}^{z}-\sigma_{T-1}^{z}
under parity operation is essential for this antiunitary symmetry.
This is not possible for
\tilde{U}_{\uparrow\uparrow}
, where

\sigma_{1}^{z}+\sigma_{T-1}^{z}
is invariant under the parity operation.

Supplementary Note 4: Thermalization of the expectation values of local observables averaged over a long time

As mentioned in the main text,
DQPTs in our model do not appear as an infinite-time average of expectation values of local observables because of the Floquet eigenstate thermalization hypothesis
[
4
]
.
To demonstrate this, here we numerically show that the time-averaged expectation values of local observables become the thermalized values.

We particularly consider a time-averaged expectation value of local magnetization

\displaystyle\overline{m^{z}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\braket{\psi_{i}|U^{-t}\sigma_{i=1}^{z}U^{t}|\psi_{i}},

(17)

and that of local correlation

\displaystyle\overline{C^{zz}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\braket{\psi_{i}|U^{-t}\sigma_{i=1}^{z}\sigma_{i=2}^{z}U^{t}|\psi_{i}},

(18)

where
T
is sufficiently large.
If the system thermalizes,
\overline{m^{z}}
and
\overline{C^{zz}}
will be equal to the expectation value at the infinite temperature, i.e.,
\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i=1}^{z}]/2^{L}=\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i=1}^{z}\sigma_{i=2}^{z}]/2^{L}=0
.
In the following, we take an initial state as
\ket{\psi_{i}}=\ket{\uparrow\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}
.
We remind that the DQPTs (including the exceptional DQPT) occur for several
b
for
F_{\infty,T}^{{\left\{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow\right\}}}
with finite
T
starting from this initial state (see Supplementary Note 1).

Supplementary Figure
4
shows the values of
\overline{m^{z}}
and
\overline{C^{zz}}
as a function of
b
for different system sizes
L
.
We find that
\overline{m^{z}}
and
\overline{C^{zz}}
approach zero as increasing the system size especially for
b
far from the integrable point (
b/\pi=0,0.5,1
), which indicates that they become zero in the thermodynamic limit.
This implies that our DQPTs are unique to finite-time regimes, in which time serves as an important parameter in stark contrast with conventional phase transitions.

.

Supplementary Note 5: Dynamical phase transition at the self-dual point

In this section, we discuss the details for the DQPT occurring at the self-dual points, i.e.,
J=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}
and
b=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{m\pi}{2}\>(n,m\in\mathbb{Z})

[
2
,
3
]
.
We especially show that crossing self-dual points entails DQPTs universally for
F^{\mathrm{Tr}/\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}_{\infty,T}
with any
T
and
h
, whose singularity is analogous to that for the conventional DQPT.
Moreover, the dynamical free energy density takes a universal value as
F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\infty,T}=\log 2
or
F^{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}_{\infty,T}=\log 2/2
there.

We especially focus on the case for
F_{\mathrm{Tr}}
(other dynamical free energies are discussed in a similar manner).
As discussed in the main text,
we can exactly write

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}=Ce^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{b}\sigma_{\tau}^{x}}e^{-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\tilde{J}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}\sigma_{\tau+1}^{z}-i\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}h\sigma_{\tau}^{z}}

(19)

using the spacetime duality
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
Here,
\tilde{b}=-\pi/4-i\log(\tan J)/2
,
\tilde{J}=-\pi/4-i\log(\tan b)/2
and
C=(\sin 2b/\sin 2\tilde{b})^{T/2}/2
.
Importantly,
\tilde{U}
is unitary (up to a constant) only at the self-dual points.

As noted in the main text, for typical cases, DQPTs occur when maximum of two eigenvalues with different
\theta_{\alpha}
switches accidentally, where
n_{\mathrm{deg}}=1
for each phase and
n_{\mathrm{deg}}=2
at transition (Supplementary Figure
5
a
).
On the other hand, DQPT occurs more universally if the self-dual point is crossed:
all of the modulus of the eigenvalues of
\tilde{U}
are equal at this point (
n_{\mathrm{deg}}=2^{T}
),
and crossing this point typically switches the largest eigenvalue (Supplementary Figure
5
b
).
At transition, dynamical free energies are determined only by the modulus of the eigenvalues, which leads to universal values
F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\infty,T}=\log 2
(similarly, we have
F^{\uparrow\uparrow/\downarrow\uparrow}_{\infty,T}=\log 2/2
).
To our best knowledge, this is the first evidence that the self-dual point is a critical point of different dynamical phases.

Supplementary Note 6: Dynamical phase transitions in finite systems

Here, we show several results concerning the DQPTs for finite system size
L
.

Degeneracy and finite-size effect

While the number of eigenvalues with maximum modulus
n_{\mathrm{deg}}
does not contribute to the free energy density for
L\rightarrow\infty
,
n_{\mathrm{deg}}
can characterize each phase via a finite-size correction
\Delta F_{L,T}=F_{L,T}-F_{\infty,T}
.
Indeed, the second term in

\displaystyle F_{L,T}\simeq-\log|\lambda_{\mathrm{M}}|-\frac{1}{L}\log\left|\sum_{\alpha}e^{i\theta_{\alpha}L}\right|.

(20)

shows that

\Delta F_{L,T}
is upper bounded by
\log n_{\mathrm{deg}}/L
, where the bound is achieved when
e^{i\theta_{\alpha}L}=e^{i\theta_{\beta}L}
for every
\alpha\neq\beta
.

Supplementary Figure
6
a
shows the finite-size scaling of
\Delta F_{L,T}^{\mathrm{Tr}}
near the exceptional DQPT.
While
\Delta F_{L,T}^{\mathrm{Tr}}
exponentially decays with
L
for
b<b_{c}
since
n_{\mathrm{deg}}=1
, it exhibits polynomially decaying oscillations for
b>b_{c}
, where the decay is upper bounded by
\log n_{\mathrm{deg}}/L=\log 2/L
for sufficiently large
L
.
At the transition point, oscillation-free polynomial decay is observed.
Another example is the behavior at the self-dual point, where the decay is upper bounded by
\log 2^{T}/L
(Supplementary Figure
6
b
).

The number
n_{\mathrm{deg}}
thus provides universal information through
\Delta F_{L,T}
on dynamical phases, which is deeply related to the symmetries hidden in the space-time dual operator
\tilde{U}
.
If
\tilde{U}
is in Class AI (
F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L,T}
with even

and
F^{\downarrow\uparrow}_{L,T}
with odd
T
under the condition
J=-\pi/4+n\pi/2\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
), phases with

can naturally appear as well as phases with

.
If
\tilde{U}
is in Class AII (
F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L,T}
with odd
T
and
F^{\downarrow\uparrow}_{L,T}
with even
T
under the condition
J=-\pi/4+n\pi/2\>(n\in\mathbb{Z})
), all phases satisfy
n_{\mathrm{deg}}\geq 2
.
We note that other symmetries are found to exist that enhance the value of
n_{\mathrm{deg}}
, such as translation invariance for
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{Tr}}
or integrability at
h=0
, which may also be interesting to study systematically.

for

and

for

Supplementary Note 7: Explicit construction of an order parameter

We here show that we can explicitly construct an order parameter using different-time generalized observables.
For this purpose, we especially focus on
F^{\downarrow\uparrow}_{L,T}=-\frac{1}{L}\log|\braket{\psi_{f}|U^{T}|\psi_{i}}|
with
\ket{\psi_{i}}=\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}
and
\ket{\psi_{f}}=\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
, instead of
F^{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L,T}
, since its operational meaning in experimental situations is more direct.
We note that
F^{\downarrow\uparrow}_{\infty,T}
shows the exceptional DQPT for
b=b_{c}\simeq 0.446\pi
with
h=1.3
,
T=5

For our discussion, we first note that, for a usual symmetry breaking, such as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry breaking of an Ising model, magnetization

becomes an order parameter.
In this case,
m^{z}
is odd under symmetry operation (

), so
\braket{m^{z}}=-\braket{m^{z}}=0
when symmetry of the state is unbroken.

Similarly, our antiunitary symmetry operation in the space-time dual space (

for
\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}
)
is found to correspond to a combined symmetry of time exchange
\tau\leftrightarrow T-\tau
, complex conjugation, and certain spin reversal in the original space.
For example, using the spacetime-dual transformation and the spectral decomposition
\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\ket{\phi_{\alpha}}\bra{\chi_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\braket{\psi_{f}|U^{T-\tau}\sigma_{i}^{z}U^{\tau}|\psi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{U}_{\downarrow\uparrow}^{L}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}]

(21)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\lambda_{0}^{L}\braket{\chi_{0}|\sigma_{\tau}^{z}|\phi_{0}}

(22)

\displaystyle V\ket{\phi_{0}}^{*}=\ket{\phi_{0}},\quad V\ket{\chi_{0}}^{*}=\ket{\chi_{0}},

(23)

\displaystyle\braket{\chi_{0}|\sigma_{\tau}^{z}|\phi_{0}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\chi_{0}^{*}|V^{\dagger}\sigma_{\tau}^{z}V|\phi_{0}^{*}}=-\braket{\chi_{0}|\sigma_{T-\tau}^{z}|\phi_{0}}^{*}

\mathrm{Re}[\braket{\psi_{f}|U^{T-\tau}\sigma_{i}^{z}U^{\tau}|\psi_{i}}]=-\mathrm{Re}[\braket{\psi_{f}|U^{\tau}\sigma_{i}^{z}U^{T-\tau}|\psi_{i}}]

On the other hand, we can also find

Monodromy and mapping class groups of 3-dimensional hypersurfaces
Oscar Randal-Williams

Abstract
. We describe the subgroup of the mapping class group of a hypersurface in ℂ ​ ℙ 4 \mathbb{CP}^{4} consisting of those diffeomorphisms which can be realised by monodromy.

1.
Introduction

Let
d
be a positive integer, and
X_{d}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
denote the degree
d
Fermat hypersurface. It resides in the universal family
\mathcal{X}_{d}\to\mathcal{U}_{d}
of smooth 3-dimensional degree
d
hypersurfaces, whose base is the open subspace
\mathcal{U}_{d}\subset\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4};\mathcal{O}(d))
of degree
d
homogeneous polynomials in 5 variables whose zero locus is smooth. We write

\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{d},X_{d})

for the monodromy group of this family.

On the other hand
X_{d}
can be considered as an oriented 6-manifold, and we write

\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{0}\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(X_{d})

for the oriented mapping class group of this manifold: the group of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Fibre transport for the universal family yields a group homomorphism

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{MCG}_{d}.

A presentation for the group
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
has been given by Lönne
[
L0̈9
]
, who suggests
[
L0̈9
, p. 362]
studying the map
\alpha
. Furthermore, a quite complete description of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
has been given by Kreck and Su
[
KS24
]
. Using the latter, Hain
[
Hai23
]
has explained how the methods of Sullivan
[
Sul77
]
show that the image of
\alpha
has infinite index in
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
. Our goal is to completely describe the image of
\alpha
, in terms of Kreck and Su’s description of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
.

Most of the answer will be described in terms of automorphisms of
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
respecting certain structures. There is a natural extension

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/d\longrightarrow\pi_{3}(X_{d})\overset{h}{\longrightarrow}H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 0,

where
h
is the Hurewicz map, and the
\mathbb{Z}
-valued intersection form of
X_{d}
induces an antisymmetric form
\lambda
on
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
, whose radical is
\mathbb{Z}/d\leq\pi_{3}(X_{d})
. We write
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
for the group of automorphisms of
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
which preserve
\lambda

and which induce the identity on the radical
\mathbb{Z}/d\leq\pi_{3}(X_{d})
of
\lambda
. We will explain that
\lambda
has a quadratic refinement

\mu\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{3}(X_{d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2,

induced by the embedding
X_{d}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
, and that this quadratic refinement is
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
-invariant. By a quadratic refinement of
\lambda
we mean that
\mu
is a function satisfying

\mu(a+b)=\mu(a)+\mu(b)+\lambda(a,b)\mod 2.

We write
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda,\mu)
for the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
consisting of those automorphisms which preserve
\mu
.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
.

(i)

The map
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda,\mu)
is surjective.

(ii)

The kernel of the surjection
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda,\mu)
consists precisely of those isotopy classes of diffeomorphisms of
X_{d}
which may be represented by diffeomorphisms supported in an embedded 6-disc.

The latter is a quotient of
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{6}))=\Theta_{7}=\mathbb{Z}/28
, and may be extracted from the work of Kreck and Su (see Lemma
2.2
). It is given by

\frac{\Theta_{7}}{\mathrm{Ker}(\Theta_{7}\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d})}\cong\begin{cases}0&d\equiv 2,4,6,10,12,14\mod 16\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&d\equiv 3,5,8,11,13\mod 16\\
\mathbb{Z}/4&d\equiv 0,1,7,9,15\mod 16\end{cases}\oplus\begin{cases}0&d\not\equiv 0\mod 7\\
\mathbb{Z}/7&d\equiv 0\mod 7.\end{cases}

There is therefore a central extension

0\longrightarrow\Theta_{7}/\mathrm{Ker}(\Theta_{7}\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda,\mu)\longrightarrow 0.

In Section
6
we shall combine Theorem
A
with existing work on diffeomorphism groups of the manifolds
W_{g,1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\#^{g}S^{3}\times S^{3})\setminus\mathrm{int}(D^{6})
to partially understand this extension, and in particular obtain the following, where we recall that the
finite residual
of a group is the intersection of all of its finite-index subgroups.

Theorem B
.

The finite residual of
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)
is the subgroup
\Theta_{7}/\mathrm{Ker}(\Theta_{7}\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d})
.

It follows that
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)
is often not residually finite, so neither is
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
.

Remark 1.1
.

We use the assumption
d\geq 3
to guarantee that
X_{d}
contains a modest number of
S^{3}\times S^{3}
connect-summands (
d\geq 3
implies that it contains at least 5). If
d=1
or
2
then
X_{d}
contains no
S^{3}\times S^{3}
connect summands. The work of Kreck and Su shows that
\mathrm{MCG}_{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/4
(see also
[
Bru71
, Remark II.11]
) and
\mathrm{MCG}_{2}=0
. On the other hand
\mathrm{Mon}_{1}=0
because
all
degree 1 hypersurfaces are smooth, so
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)
is trivial if
d\leq 2
.

The map
\alpha
naturally factors through the symplectic mapping class group

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\longrightarrow\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})\longrightarrow\mathrm{MCG}_{d}.

This work began in discussions with Ailsa Keating and Ivan Smith, exploring the possibility of using this factorisation to investigate
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})
. While
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)
clearly gives a lower bound for
\mathrm{Im}(\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d})
, trying to go any further seemed to produce more questions than answers on the symplectic side:

(i)

Is the quadratic refinement
\mu
invariant under the action of
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})
?

(ii)

Does the “distortion of the first Pontrjagin class” vanish for symplectomorphisms? (See Section
3.3
for this notion.)

(iii)

Do
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
and
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})
have the same image in
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
?

(iv)

Is
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\to\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})
surjective?

If the answer to (iv) is “yes” then it is for the other questions too, but I am told it is not currently accessible. With regards to (i),
\mu
is not
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
-invariant, and neither is it invariant for the subgroup of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
which preserves the almost complex structure of
X_{d}
(because this is the whole of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
, see Remark
3.3
).

Strategy.
The strategy for identifying the image of
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Mod}_{d}\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
is fairly clear: one must produce constraints on mapping classes—which are satisfied by monodromy—to cut down the image, and then one must then show that enough mapping classes are realised by monodromy to see that the constraints are sharp. This is broadly what we will do.

The first constraint, developed in Section
3
, is based on the observation that the universal family of smooth hypersurfaces over
\mathcal{U}_{d}
is equipped with a certain (stable) tangential structure
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}
, so that
\alpha
lands in the stabiliser
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})
of this tangential structure. This stabiliser can be analysed following Krannich
[
Kra20
]
or Kupers and the author
[
KRW21
]
, and in particular it is shown to preserve a quadratic refinement
\mu
on
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
, which with the work of Kreck and Su
[
KS24
]
leads to a central extension

0\longrightarrow\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{diffeomorphisms supported}\\
\text{near $S^{2}\subset X_{d}$}\end{subarray}\}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda,\mu)\longrightarrow 0.

To explain the left-hand term, we choose once and for all an embedding
S^{2}\hookrightarrow X_{d}
whose homotopy class represents a generator of
\pi_{2}(X_{d})\cong\mathbb{Z}
: the left-hand term then consists of those isotopy classes of diffeomorphisms which may be represented by diffeomorphisms supported in a tubular neighbourhood of this embedded
S^{2}
. This subgroup
has been completely determined by Kreck and Su. In particular it is finite and abelian. Choosing an embedded 6-disc inside the tubular neighbourhood of
S^{2}\subset X_{d}

gives a homomorphism

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{6}))=\Theta_{7}\longrightarrow\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{diffeomorphisms supported}\\
\text{near $S^{2}\subset X_{d}$}\end{subarray}\}.

The second constraint, developed in Section
5
, is the construction of a surjective map
\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})\to\mathrm{Coker}(\Phi)
such that
\kappa\circ\alpha
is trivial. This map is obtained indirectly, essentially by calculating the abelianisation of
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})
. This is done by relating this stabiliser to a certain moduli space of manifolds (diffeomorphic to
X_{d}
and equipped with a certain tangential structure), describing the first homology of the latter in terms of Thom spectra using the work of Galatius and the author
[
GRW17
]
, and then calculating using various techniques from stable homotopy theory.

We get lower bounds for the image of
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Mon}_{d}\to\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
from three sources. Firstly, work of Krylov
[
Kry03
]
quickly shows that the subgroup
\Phi(\Theta_{7})
can be realised by monodromy. Secondly, work of Beauville
[
Bea86
]
(for which we supply a missing detail) fully describes the automorphisms of
H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
that can be realised by monodromy. Finally, in Section
4
we explain how work of Pham
[
Pha65
]
and of Looijenga
[
Loo10
]
concerning the action of groups of
d
th roots of unity on
X_{d}
can be used to extend Beauville’s work to fully describe the automorphisms of
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
which can be realised by monodromy.

Some recollections

2.1.
Algebraic topology of hypersurfaces

Let us explain our notation for the cohomology of a degree
d
smooth hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Writing
x\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for the restriction from
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
of the hyperplane class, by Poincaré duality there is a unique class
y\in H^{4}(X;\mathbb{Z})
such that
\int_{X}x\cdot y=1
. As
\int_{X}x^{3}=d
, it follows that
x^{2}=dy
. Then the even cohomology of
X
is

H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{1\}\quad H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{x\}\quad H^{4}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{y\}\quad H^{6}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{xy\}.

The only odd cohomology is
H^{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, which is equipped with the nondegenerate antisymmetric form
(a,b)\mapsto\int_{X}a\cdot b
.

The inclusion
X\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
is 3-connected by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem. Combined with the fact that
\pi_{3}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4})=\pi_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4})=0
and the Hurewicz theorem, this gives isomorphisms

\pi_{3}(X)\overset{\sim}{\longleftarrow}\pi_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4},X)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4},X;\mathbb{Z}).

The long exact sequence on homology for this pair takes the form

\cdots\longrightarrow H_{4}(X;\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4},X;\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H_{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 0

and so gives an extension

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/d\{\eta\}\longrightarrow\pi_{3}(X)\overset{h}{\longrightarrow}H_{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 0.

The subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/d
is generated by the Hopf map
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{3}\to S^{2}
composed with the generator of
\pi_{2}(X)\cong H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
dual to
x\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. The intersection form of
X
induces an antisymmetric form
\lambda
on
\pi_{3}(X)
, whose radical is precisely the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/d\{\eta\}
.

The definition of
X
as a hypersurface gives an isomorphism of complex vector bundles
TX\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)|_{X}\oplus\underline{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 5}|_{X}
, and so its total Chern class is

\displaystyle c(TX)=\tfrac{(1+x)^{5}}{(1+dx)}

\displaystyle=1+(5-d)x+(d^{2}-5d+10)x^{2}+(-d^{3}+5d^{2}-10d+10)x^{3}

\displaystyle=1+(5-d)x+(d^{2}-5d+10)dy+(-d^{3}+5d^{2}-10d+10)dxy.

As the third Chern class of
TX
is also the Euler class of this vector bundle, we see that
\chi(X)=(-d^{3}+5d^{2}-10d+10)d
and so

\mathrm{rank}\,H^{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})=4-(-d^{3}+5d^{2}-10d+10)d=d^{4}-5d^{3}+10d^{2}-10d+4.

We write
2g
for this number: by a theorem of Wall
[
Wal66
, Theorem 1]
there is a decomposition
X\cong X^{\prime}\#g(S^{3}\times S^{3})
for some 6-manifold
X^{\prime}
; it will be useful to know that
g\geq 5
as long as
d\geq 3
. The class
c_{1}(TX)=(5-d)x
reduces modulo 2 to
w_{2}(TX)
, so
X
is Spin if and only if
d
is odd. Its first Pontrjagin class is

\displaystyle p_{1}(TX)

\displaystyle=-c_{2}(TX\otimes\mathbb{C})=-c_{2}(TX\oplus\overline{TX})=-(2c_{2}(TX)-c_{1}(TX)^{2})

\displaystyle=(5-d^{2})dy.

3.1.
Tangential structures

A
stable tangential structure
is a Serre fibration
\bar{\theta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\to B\mathrm{O}
, and a
\bar{\theta}
-structure
on a manifold
M

\ell\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B
of the map
\tau^{s}_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B\mathrm{O}
classifying the stable tangent bundle of
M
. Familiar examples are
\bar{\theta}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\mathrm{SO}\to B\mathrm{O}
, for which
\bar{\theta}^{+}
-structures are orientations, or
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{sfr}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\mathrm{O}\to B\mathrm{O}
, for which
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{sfr}}
-structures are stable framings.

As the map
\tau_{M}
is not literally unique—it is only unique up to coherent homotopies—in order to study the action of
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
on
\bar{\theta}
-structures it is best to take another model. If
M
is
d

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{d}}

-action.

Choosing a map
\tau_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to B\mathrm{O}(d)
classifying the tangent bundle of
M
, it is easy to show (using that
\mathrm{Bun}(TM,\gamma_{d})\simeq*
, which is the defining property of the universal bundle
\gamma_{d}\to B\mathrm{O}(d)
) that
\Theta(M)
is homotopy equivalent to the space of lifts in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{O}(d),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{M}}

and so by cartesianness of (
3.1
) it is equivalent to the space of lifts of the map
\tau^{s}_{M}
along
\bar{\theta}
, as we originally defined a
\bar{\theta}
-structure to be. By the cartesianness of (
3.1
) we therefore blur the distinction between
\bar{\theta}
- and
\theta
-structures.

Remark 3.1
.

In the discussion above it is clear that it does not matter that
\theta
is obtained by pulling back from a fibration over
B\mathrm{O}
, and it is common to refer to a fibration over
B\mathrm{O}(d)
as a tangential structure.

3.2.
The tangential structure
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}

There are two natural tangential structures in the situation we are considering. The tangent bundle of a degree
d
hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4}
satisfies
TX\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)|_{X}=T\mathbb{CP}^{4}|_{X}
and so using that
T\mathbb{CP}^{4}\oplus\underline{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 5}
we have

(3.2)

TX\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)|_{X}\oplus\underline{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 5}|_{X}.

If we define maps

\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 5}-\mathcal{O}(d)-\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}B\mathrm{U}\overset{\bar{\theta}^{\mathbb{C}}}{\longrightarrow}B\mathrm{O},

which we implicitly convert into Serre fibrations, then the identity (
3.2
) shows that
X
has a canonical
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
-structure
\ell_{X}^{\mathrm{hyp}}
, and so also a canonical
\theta^{\mathbb{C}}
-structure
\ell_{X}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

More generally, the universal family of smooth hypersurfaces
\mathcal{X}_{d}\to\mathcal{U}_{d}
comes with a fibrewise embedding
\mathcal{X}_{d}\subset\mathcal{U}_{d}\times\mathbb{CP}^{4}
, and the vertical tangent bundle of this family satisfies
T_{v}\mathcal{X}_{d}\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}\oplus\underline{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 5}|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}
, giving a lift of the map
\tau_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{X}_{d}\to B\mathrm{O}
classifying
T_{v}\mathcal{X}_{d}
along
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}
, i.e. a
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
-structure on the whole family. This implies that
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
-structure
\ell_{X_{d}}^{\mathrm{hyp}}
is preserved by
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
. Thus the
\theta^{\mathbb{C}}
-structure
\ell_{X_{d}}^{\mathbb{C}}
is also preserved by
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
, which is unsurprising as it is even preserved by the symplectic mapping class group
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Symp}(X_{d})
. Somewhat surprisingly we have the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

The
\theta^{\mathbb{C}}
-structure
\ell_{X_{d}}^{\mathbb{C}}
is preserved by
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
.

Proof.

As
\bar{\theta}^{\mathbb{C}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\mathrm{U}\to B\mathrm{O}
is a fibration of
H
-spaces (in fact of infinite loop spaces) with fibre the
H
-space
\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}
, the set
\theta^{\mathbb{C}}(X_{d})
is a torsor for the abelian group
[X_{d},O/U]
, with action

-\cdot-\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\theta^{\mathbb{C}}(X_{d})\times[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]\longrightarrow\theta^{\mathbb{C}}(X_{d}).

An orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{d}\to X_{d}
then has
\ell^{\mathbb{C}}_{X_{d}}\circ D\varphi=\ell^{\mathbb{C}}_{X_{d}}\cdot\delta^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi)
for a unique
\delta^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi)\in[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]
, and this defines a function

\delta^{\mathbb{C}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\longrightarrow[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]

which is a (right) crossed homomorphism with respect to the right action of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
on
[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]
by precomposition.

Using the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence to calculate
[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]
, we see it has a filtration with filtration quotients

H^{0}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2,\quad H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{x\},\quad H^{6}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\{xy\}.

The projection of
\delta^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi)
to the first quotient
H^{0}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)
is always trivial, as this records whether
\varphi
preserves orientation. Neglecting this first quotient we see that the remainder
[X_{d},\mathrm{SO}/\mathrm{U}]
of
[X_{d},\mathrm{O}/\mathrm{U}]
is torsion-free, and the Chern–Dold character gives an isomorphism

\mathrm{ch}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[X_{d},\mathrm{SO}/\mathrm{U}]\otimes\mathbb{Q}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H^{4*+2}(X_{d},\mathbb{Q}).

In other words
\delta^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi)
is trivial if and only if
\varphi
fixes
c_{1}(X_{d})
and
c_{3}(X_{d})
. But all orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms act trivially on
H^{even}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
, so they all preserve
c_{1}(X_{d})
and
c_{3}(X_{d})
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

There is an unstable tangential structure
\theta^{ac}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\mathrm{U}(3)\to B\mathrm{O}(6)
, classifying almost complex structures on 6-manifolds:
\bar{\theta}^{\mathbb{C}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\mathrm{U}\to B\mathrm{O}
classifies
stable
almost complex structures. Although we will not need it, we record the fact that
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
also preserves any almost complex structure. The following argument, along lines suggested by the referee, replaces an earlier more complicated one.

First, we choose a lift
\theta^{ac,+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\mathrm{U}(3)\to B\mathrm{SO}(6)
: as
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
preserves orientations by definition, we may as well study its action on lifts along this map. There is a homeomorphism
\mathrm{SO}(6)/\mathrm{U}(3)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}
(arising from
\mathrm{Spin}(6)\cong\mathrm{SU}(4)
, and identifying the preimage of
\mathrm{U}(3)\leq\mathrm{SO}(6)
in
\mathrm{Spin}(6)
with
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))\leq\mathrm{SU}(4)
). The maps classifying the the first Chern class and second Stiefel–Whitney class give the bottom homotopy commutative square in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{CP}^{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\mathbb{Z},2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{U}(3)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\mathbb{Z},2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{SO}(6)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\mathbb{Z}/2,2),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{ac,+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{2}}

and choosing a homotopy making this square commute yields the top dashed map. By considering the induced map of Serre spectral sequences we see that the top map is an isomorphism on
H_{2}(-;\mathbb{Z})
and so on
\pi_{2}(-)
, and so is 7-connected. It follows that the map from
\Theta^{ac,+}(X_{d})
to the space of lifts

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\mathbb{Z},2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\mathbb{Z}/2,2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{2}(X_{d})}

is
(7-\dim(X_{d})=1)
-connected. In particular
\theta^{ac,+}(X_{d})=\pi_{0}\Theta^{ac,+}(X_{d})
is canonically identified with the set of lifts of
w_{2}(X_{d})\in H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)
to an integral cohomology class. As
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
acts trivially on the second integral cohomology of
X_{d}
, it acts trivially on this set of lifts.

It is not the case that
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
preserves
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}
, and our next goal is to determine
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})
. The methods we will use are related to those of
[
KRW21
,
Kra20
]
. We begin by an analysis similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2
. The map
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\to B\mathrm{O}
is no longer a map of
H
-spaces, but we can proceed as follows. We have explained that
\Theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d})
is homotopy equivalent to the space of lifts of
\tau^{s}_{X_{d}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{d}\to B\mathrm{O}
along
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}
, which shows that it fits into a homotopy cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{map}(X_{d},\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\tau_{X_{d}}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{map}(X_{d},B\mathrm{O}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}})_{*}}

On homotopy groups this gives a sequence

0=H^{1}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow KO^{-1}(X_{d})\overset{\curvearrowright}{\longrightarrow}\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d})\overset{\hat{x}}{\longrightarrow}H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\overset{(\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}})_{*}}{\longrightarrow}KO^{0}(X_{d})

which is exact exact in the sense of groups and pointed sets: the two leftmost terms are groups, the map marked
\curvearrowright
is an action, and the orbits of this action are in bijection with
((\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}})_{*})^{-1}(0)
. The map
\hat{x}
assigns to a lift
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{d}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}
the class
(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}})^{*}(\iota_{2})\in H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
.

As an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{d}\to X_{d}
acts trivially on
H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
it follows that
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}\circ D\varphi
and
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}
lie in the same fibre of
\hat{x}
: thus there is a unique
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\varphi)\in KO^{-1}(X_{d})
such that
\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}\circ D\varphi=\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}\cdot\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\varphi)
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
this defines a function

\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\longrightarrow KO^{-1}(X_{d})

which is a crossed homomorphism with respect to the right action of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
on
KO^{-1}(X_{d})
by contravariant functoriality. Using the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence to calculate
KO^{-1}(X_{d})
, we see it has a filtration with filtration quotients

H^{0}(X_{d};\pi_{0}(\mathrm{O}))=\mathbb{Z}^{\times},\quad H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O})).

As in the lemma, projection of
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\varphi)
to the first quotient detects whether
\varphi
preserves orientation, which it does, so
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
refines to a crossed homomorphism

\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\longrightarrow H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))\subset KO^{-1}(X_{d}).

We then have

\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})=\mathrm{Ker}(\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\to H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))).

Remark 3.4
.

The stable tangential structure
\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\to B\mathrm{O}
is not the most sophisticated structure with which we can endow a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{CP}^{4}
. The equation
TX\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)|_{X}=T\mathbb{CP}^{4}|_{X}
shows that it may be endowed with the (unstable) tangential structure given by the homotopy equaliser

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathbb{CP}^{4}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{U}(4)}

of the maps classifying
\gamma_{3}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathcal{O}(d)
and
T\mathbb{CP}^{4}
respectively, made into a tangential structure via the natural maps
B\to B\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathbb{CP}^{4}\to B\mathrm{U}(3)\to B\mathrm{O}(6)
. It is more complicated to analyse the set of such structures on
X_{d}
, and it seems likely that for reasons similar to Remark
3.3
there is no advantage in doing so.

3.3.
Distortion of the first Pontrjagin class

We wish to relate
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
to Kreck and Su’s map
v_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}\to H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
from (
2.3
), which is described in terms of Sullivan’s “distortion of the first Pontrjagin class”, see
[
Sul77
, §13]
. As explained by Hain
[
Hai23
, §5.2]
, in the case at hand this may be implemented as follows.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
act trivially on cohomology, i.e. lie in the
Torelli subgroup

\mathrm{TMCG}_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\to\mathrm{Aut}(H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})))
, and let
T_{\varphi}
denote its mapping torus. The long exact sequence on cohomology for the pair
(T_{\varphi},X_{d})
, using excision, takes the form

\cdots\longrightarrow H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\overset{\mathrm{Id}-\varphi^{*}=0}{\longrightarrow}H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}H^{4}(T_{\varphi};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{4}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\cdots.

There is a unique
\bar{x}\in H^{2}(T_{\varphi};\mathbb{Z})
which restricts to
x\in H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
. On
X_{d}
we have
p_{1}(TX_{d})=(5-d^{2})x^{2}
, so the class
p_{1}(T(T_{\varphi}))-(5-d^{2})\bar{x}^{2}\in H^{4}(T_{\varphi};\mathbb{Z})
vanishes on
X_{d}
and so comes from a unique class
\Delta_{p_{1}}(\varphi)\in H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
. This defines a function

\Delta_{p_{1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{TMCG}_{d}\longrightarrow H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}),

which is easily checked to be a homomorphism.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\Delta_{p_{1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{TMCG}_{d}\overset{\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}|_{\mathrm{TMCG}_{d}}}{\longrightarrow}H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))\overset{(p_{1})_{*}}{\longrightarrow}H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}).

Under the usual identification
\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O})=\mathbb{Z}
, the latter map is multiplication by 2.

Proof.

For
\varphi
in the Torelli group, we describe
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\varphi)\in KO^{-1}(X_{d})
in a similar way to
\Delta_{p_{1}}
. The long exact sequence on
KO
-theory for the pair
(T_{\varphi},X_{d})
, using excision, takes the form

\cdots\longrightarrow KO^{-1}(X_{d})\overset{Id-\varphi^{*}}{\longrightarrow}KO^{-1}(X_{d})\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}KO^{0}(T_{\varphi})\longrightarrow KO^{0}(X_{d})\longrightarrow\cdots.

The short exact sequence
0\to H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\to KO^{-1}(X_{d})\to H^{0}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)\to 0
is functorially split by
*\to X_{d}\to*
, so the fact that
\varphi
acts as the identity on cohomology shows that the left-hand map in the above sequence is zero. Using the map
\bar{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{\varphi}\to K(\mathbb{Z},2)=\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
we can form the class

(T(T_{\varphi})-\varepsilon^{7})-\bar{x}^{*}\bar{\theta}^{\mathrm{hyp}}\in KO^{0}(T_{\varphi}).

This vanishes on
X_{d}
, and so comes from a unique class in
KO^{-1}(X_{d})
: this is
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\varphi)
.

The first Pontrjagin class gives a homomorphism
p_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}KO^{0}(T_{\varphi})\to H^{4}(T_{\varphi};\mathbb{Z})
, which extends to a map between the two exact sequences above. The composition
H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))\subset KO^{-1}(X_{d})\overset{p_{1}}{\to}H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
is the map induced by
p_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O})\to\mathbb{Z}
, which is well-known to be an isomorphism onto the subgroup
2\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

We now relate this to the work of Kreck and Su, namely to the map
v_{p}
in the central extension

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{K}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}\overset{v_{p}}{\longrightarrow}H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathrm{K}_{d}
is a certain finite abelian group, and
\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}\leq\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
is the subgroup of those diffeomorphisms which act trivially on
\pi_{3}(X_{d})
. This map is made explicit by the diagram before the statement of
[
KS24
, Theorem 2.6]
and the definition of the map
v_{x,p}
just before
[
KS24
, Proposition 4.5]
. Together those show that:

Lemma 3.6
.

We have
\Delta_{p_{1}}|_{\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}}=4\cdot v_{p}
.∎

Restricted to
\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}
the crossed homomorphism
\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\to H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
is a homomorphism, so it annihilates the finite group
\mathrm{K}_{d}
and hence descends to a homomorphism

\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}/\mathrm{K}_{d}\longrightarrow H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O})).

Corollary 3.7
.

This is an isomorphism onto
2\cdot H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))
.

Proof.

Postcomposing with the map
(p_{1})_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))\to H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
, which is an isomorphism onto
2\cdot H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
, gives the map
\Delta_{p_{1}}
by Lemma
3.5
, which is the map
4\cdot v_{p}
by Lemma
3.6
, so is an isomorphism onto
4\cdot H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
as
v_{p}
is surjective.
∎

In particular
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})=\mathrm{Ker}(\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{MCG}_{d}\to H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O})))
intersects
\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}
precisely in
\mathrm{K}_{d}
, giving a half-exact sequence

(3.3)

1\longrightarrow\mathrm{K}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda).

3.4.
The surgery kernel

The action of
\mathrm{MCG}_{d}
on the set
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d})
preserves the subset
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d};x,+)
of those
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
-structures which map to
x\in H^{2}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
under
\hat{x}
and which induce the standard orientation of
X_{d}
. This action descends to an action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
on the set

\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d};x,+)/\mathrm{SMCG}_{d},

and the discussion of the previous section shows that this set is a torsor for
H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)=H^{3}(X_{d};\pi_{3}(\mathrm{O}))/\mathrm{Im}(\delta^{\mathrm{hyp}}|_{\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}})
. We wish to identify this set with something more meaningful. Associated to a
\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}
-structure
\ell
there is a quadratic form
\mu_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{3}(X_{d})\to\mathbb{Z}/2
on

\pi_{3}(X_{d})\overset{\sim}{\longleftarrow}\pi_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty},X_{d})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H_{4}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty},X_{d};\mathbb{Z})

obtained by considering this as the surgery kernel for the normal map
\ell\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{d}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}
covered by the corresponding bundle map, see
[
Kre99
, p. 728]
. This
\mu_{\ell}
is a quadratic refinement of the intersection form
\lambda
in the sense that

\mu_{\ell}(a+b)=\mu_{\ell}(a)+\mu_{\ell}(b)+\lambda(a,b)\mod 2.

In terms of the extension (
2.1
), as the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/d\{\eta\}\leq\pi_{3}(X_{d})
is radical with respect to
\lambda
, the restriction
\mu_{\ell}|_{\mathbb{Z}/d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}/d\to\mathbb{Z}/2
is a homomorphism. In particular if
d
is odd then this homomorphism must be trivial, and so
\mu_{\ell}
descends to a quadratic refinement of the intersection form on
H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 3.8
.

The quadratic refinement
\mu_{\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}}}
is
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
-invariant, and when
d
is odd the induced quadratic refinement on
H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
supplies the missing ingredient in Beauville’s Theorem
2.1
. The argument of
[
Woo75
, Section 2]
applies to this quadratic refinement, showing that it has Arf invariant
0
if
d\equiv\pm 1\mod 8
and Arf invariant
1
if
d\equiv\pm 3\mod 8
.

If
d
is even then this is not the case:

Lemma 3.9
.

If
d
is even then the homomorphism
\mu_{\ell}|_{\mathbb{Z}/d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}/d\to\mathbb{Z}/2
is surjective.

Proof.

If not then
\mu_{\ell}
would descend to a quadratic refinement of the intersection form on
H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})
, which would be
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
-invariant, contradicting Beauville’s Theorem
2.1
in the case
d
even.
∎

Let us write
\mathrm{Quad}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
for the set of quadratic refinements
\mu
of
(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
whose restriction to
\mathbb{Z}/d\leq\pi_{3}(X_{d})
is zero if
d
is odd, and non-zero if
d
is even. If
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
are such quadratic refinements then
\mu-\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{3}(X_{d})\to\mathbb{Z}/2
vanishes on
\mathbb{Z}/d\leq\pi_{3}(X_{d})
so descends to a homomorphism
H_{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}/2
, and hence
\mathrm{Quad}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
forms a
H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)
-torsor.

The construction above defines a function

\displaystyle\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d};x,+)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Quad}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mu_{\ell}.

This is equivariant when the right-hand side is equipped with the right
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
-action by precomposition, so it descends to a
\mathrm{Aut}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda)
-equivariant function

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\theta^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X_{d};x,+)/\mathrm{SMCG}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Quad}(\pi_{3}(X_{d}),\lambda).

This is easily checked to be a map of
H^{3}(X_{d};\mathbb{Z}/2)
-torsors, so is a bijection.

Corollary 3.10
.

The image of
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{MCG}_{d}}(\ell^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{X_{d}})
in

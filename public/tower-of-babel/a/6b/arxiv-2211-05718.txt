Abstract
. We consider a Markov chain on non-negative integer arrays of a given shape (and satisfying certain constraints) which is closely related to fundamental S ​ L ​ ( r + 1 , ℝ ) SL(r+1,{\mathbb{R}}) Whittaker functions and the Toda lattice. In the index zero case the arrays are reverse plane partitions. We show that this Markov chain has non-trivial Markovian projections and a unique entrance law starting from the array with all entries equal to + ∞ +\infty . We also discuss connections with imaginary exponential functionals of Brownian motion, a semi-discrete polymer model with purely imaginary disorder, interacting corner growth processes and discrete δ \delta -Bose gas, extensions to other root systems, and hitting probabilities for some low rank examples.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Fundamental Whittaker functions

Fundamental
SL(r+1,{\mathbb{R}})
Whittaker functions are series solutions to the eigenvalue equation

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}^{r}\phi=-\lambda^{2}\phi,

where

(2)

{\mathcal{H}}^{r}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}e^{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}

is the Hamiltonian of the
(r+1)
-particle quantum Toda chain. They were first introduced by
Hashizume
[
18
]
in a more general context.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots
be a fixed sequence of complex numbers (independent of
r
)
and denote
\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}
for
i\leq j
.
Let
r\geq 1
and define
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r+1}
by
\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}\nu_{i}=0

and
\alpha_{i}=\nu_{i}-\nu_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Then

(3)

\phi_{r}(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}}a_{r}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{r}e^{n_{i}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})+\nu_{i}x_{i}}

satisfies (
1
) with
\lambda^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}\nu_{i}^{2}/2

provided the coefficients
a_{r}(n)
satisfy

(4)

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i}n_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}n_{i}\right]a_{r}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{r}(n-e_{i}),

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
denote the standard basis vectors in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
and
with the convention
a_{r}(n)=0
for
n\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
.
Ishii and Stade
[
20
]
obtained the following recursive formula for the coefficients
a_{r}(n)
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r-1}
, define

(5)

q_{r}(n,k)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(n_{i}-k_{i})!(n_{i}-k_{i-1}+\alpha_{ir})!},

with the convention
k_{0}=k_{r}=0
.
Define

a_{r}(n)
,
r\geq 1
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, recursively by

(6)

a_{1}(n)=\frac{1}{n!(n+\alpha_{1})!}

and, for
r\geq 2
,

(7)

a_{r}(n)=\sum_{k}q_{r}(n,k)a_{r-1}(k).

where the sum is over
0\leq k_{i}\leq n_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,r-1
.
Then, for each
r\geq 1
,
a_{r}(n)
satisfies the difference equation (
4
) with

(8)

a_{r}(0)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq r}\frac{1}{\alpha_{ij}!}.

For example,

\phi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=I_{\alpha_{1}}\left(2e^{(x_{1}-x_{2})/2}\right),

where
I_{a}(z)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind

I_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!(n+a)!}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2n+a}.

For
r=2
, the coefficients are given by

(9)

a_{2}(n,m)=\frac{(n+m+a+b)!}{n!(n+a)!(n+a+b)!m!(m+b)!(m+a+b)!},

where
a=\alpha_{1}
and
b=\alpha_{2}
. This formula is due to Bump
[
8
]
.

We remark that, in the case
\alpha\equiv 0
, the recursive formula (
7
)
agrees with a special case (for complete flag manifolds) of a formula given in Batyrev,
Ciocan-Fontanine, Kim and van Straten
[
3
, Theorem 5.1.6]
for the coefficients of hypergeometric
series of partial flag manifolds (see also Remark
5.2
below).

The formula (
7
) is quite similar to Givental’s integral formula
[
15
,
21
,
13
]
for
another family of eigenfunctions of the quantum Toda lattice known as class one Whittaker functions.
It is this similarity which motivated the present work.
Gerasimov, Kharchev, Lebedev and Oblezin
[
13
]

showed that Givental’s formula may be understood in terms of some intertwining relations
between the Hamiltonians of quantum Toda chains with different numbers of particles.
These intertwining relations were extended and given a probabilistic interpretation,
in terms of Brownian motion, in
[
26
]
. In this paper we will show that Ishii and Stade’s formula (
7
)
admits a similar development, in a discrete setting.

1.3.
Discrete Whittaker processes

Let
{\rm RPP}(\lambda)
denote the set of reverse plane partitions of shape
\lambda
.
Fix
\lambda
, and consider the (continuous time) Markov chain on
{\rm RPP}(\lambda)
,
defined as follows: for each
(i,j)\in\lambda
, subtract one from
\pi_{ij}
at rate

b_{ij}(\pi)=(\pi_{ij}-\pi_{i,j-1})(\pi_{ij}-\pi_{i-1,j}),

with the convention
\pi_{i,0}=\pi_{0,j}=0
.
The infinitesimal generator of this Markov chain is given by the difference operator

G^{\lambda}=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}b_{ij}(\pi)D_{\pi_{ij}},

where
D_{n}
denotes the backward difference operator
D_{n}f(n)=f(n-1)-f(n)
.

For example, if
\lambda=(2,1)
and the current state is
the reverse plane partition
\pi_{11}=2
,
\pi_{12}=4
,
\pi_{21}=3

1.4.
Dualities and imaginary exponential functionals of Brownian motion

When
\alpha=0
, the difference equation (
4
) may be written as
h^{r}a_{r}=0
, where

(12)

h^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_{n_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i}n_{i+1}.

The generator
L^{r}
defined by (
11
) is the corresponding Doob transform

L^{r}=a_{r}(n)^{-1}\circ h^{r}\circ a_{r}(n).

The difference operator
h^{r}
is related to the quantum Toda Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}^{r}
, defined by (
2
),
via the duality relation

h_{n}^{r}f(n,x)={\mathcal{H}}^{r}_{x}f(n,x),\qquad f(n,x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}e^{n_{i}(x_{i+1}-x_{i})}.

Equivalently,
L^{r}_{n}h(n,x)={\mathcal{H}}^{r}_{x}h(n,x)
, where
h(n,x)=a_{r}(n)^{-1}f(n,x)
.
Changing variables to
x_{k}=i\xi_{k}
, this becomes a
Markov
duality relation, namely

L^{r}_{n}H(n,\xi)={\mathcal{I}}^{r}_{\xi}H(n,\xi)

where
H(n,\xi)=h(n,i\xi)
and

{\mathcal{I}}^{r}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}e^{i(\xi_{k}-\xi_{k+1})}.

This Markov duality provides a relation between the transition probabilities of the Markov
chain with generator
L^{r}
and certain imaginary exponential functionals of Brownian motion,
as follows.
Denote by
p_{t}(n,m)
the transition kernel of the Markov chain on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
with generator
L^{r}
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the law of this process started from
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
,
and by
T_{0}
the first hitting time of
0\equiv(0,0,\ldots,0)
.
Let
B=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{r+1})
be a standard Brownian motion in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r+1}
,
started at the origin, and set

Y_{k}(t)=e^{i(B_{k}(t)-B_{k+1}(t))},\qquad Z_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Y_{k}(s)ds,\qquad k=1,\ldots,r.

For
0\neq y,z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, write

y^{-n}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}y_{k}^{-n_{k}},\quad z^{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}z_{k}^{n_{k}},\quad n!=\prod_{k=1}^{r}n_{i}!.

Then (see Proposition
4.13
below) for
n,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
with
n\geq m
,

p_{t}(n,m)=\frac{a_{r}(m)}{a_{r}(n)}\frac{1}{(n-m)!}{\mathbb{E}}\left[Y(t)^{-n}Z(t)^{n-m}\right].

In particular, since
Y(t)^{-1}Z(t)
has the same law as
Z(t)
, this implies

{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(T_{0}\leq t)\equiv p_{t}(n,0)=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}Z(t)^{n}}{a_{r}(n)n!}.

Letting
t\to\infty
, this also yields a probabilistic interpretation of the (index zero) fundamental

SL(r+1,{\mathbb{R}})
Whittaker function.
When
\alpha\equiv 0
, the fundamental Whittaker function

\phi_{r}(x)
defined by (
3
) may be written as
\phi_{r}(x)=\Phi_{r}(y)
where

y_{i}=e^{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
, and

\Phi_{r}(y)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}}a_{r}(n)y^{n}.

This series is well defined for any
y\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, and satisfies
(see Corollary
4.16
below):

\Phi_{r}(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{r}y_{k}Z_{k}(t)\right).

This complements a similar representation for class one Whittaker functions in
terms of exponential functionals of Brownian motion given in
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
.

On the other hand, recall that the first row
n_{i}=\pi_{1,r-i+1}
,
i=1,\ldots,r

of the Markov chain with generator
G^{\delta_{r+1}}
, evolves as a Markov
chain on

E_{r}=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}:\ n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 0\}

with generator

M^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i}(n_{i}-n_{i+1})D_{n_{i}}+n_{r}^{2}D_{n_{r}}.

Denote the transition kernel of this Markov chain by
q_{t}(n,m)
.
In this case we have the Markov duality relation

M_{n}^{r}K(n,\xi)={\mathcal{J}}_{\xi}^{r}K(n,\xi),

where, writing
\xi=a+ib
with
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r+1}
,

{\mathcal{J}}^{r}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\Re\left[e^{i(\xi_{k}-\xi_{k+1})}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{k+1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\Im\left[e^{i(\xi_{k}-\xi_{k+1})}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{k+1}}

and

K(n,\xi)=\prod_{k=1}^{r}n_{k}!e^{in_{k}(\xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k})}.

It follows (see Proposition
4.18
below) that if we define

U^{r}(t)=\int_{0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{r}<t}Y_{1}(s_{1})\ldots Y_{r}(s_{r})ds_{1}\ldots ds_{r},

then

q_{t}(p^{r},0)=\frac{1}{p!^{r}}{\mathbb{E}}U^{r}(t)^{p},

where
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
p^{r}=(p,p,\ldots,p)\in E_{r}
.
In particular, we deduce the following (see Corollary
4.19
below):
if
(N_{1}(t),\ldots,N_{r}(t)),\ t>0
is a Markov chain with generator
L^{r}
started from
+\infty

and
\zeta=\inf\{t>0:\ N_{1}(t)=0\}
, then

{\mathbb{P}}(\zeta\leq t)=\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p!^{r}}{\mathbb{E}}U^{r}(t)^{p}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-k_{i})(n_{i}-k_{i-1}+\alpha_{ir})-P_{r}(n)

More general parameters and symmetries

The assumption

is not necessary for
some version of Theorem

to hold.
For example, if
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
then the statement may be
modified as follows.
Denote by
\Pi^{r,\alpha}
the set of non-negative integer arrays
(\pi_{ij},\ 1\leq i+j\leq r+1)
satisfying

\pi_{ij}\geq\omega_{ij}\vee\pi_{i,j-1}\vee(\pi_{i-1,j}-\beta_{ij}),\qquad 1\leq i+j\leq r+1,

where

(18)

\omega_{ij}=\omega_{i,j-1}\vee(\omega_{i-1,j}-\beta_{ij}),\qquad 1\leq i+j\leq r+1,

(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
,
since

in that case.
Similarly, let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r,\alpha}
denote the set of
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
satisfying

,

. Then the statement and proof of Theorem
2.2

remain valid, as written, with
\Pi^{r}
and

replaced by
\Pi^{r,\alpha}

The array

is an absorbing state
for the Markov chain with generator
G^{r}
.
It is given explicitly as follows. Consider the triangular array

(\nu_{i}^{k},\ 1\leq i\leq k\leq r+1)

\Pi^{r,-\alpha}
with generator

.
If
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}^{r}

{\mathbb{Z}}^{r,\alpha}

L^{r}=L^{r,\alpha}
, where

4.
Transition probabilities and hitting times

Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
and consider the Markov chain
(X(t),\ t\geq 0)
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}

with generator
L^{r}
. Denote by
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the law of this process started from
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{n}

the corresponding expectation. By Theorem
2.3
, this law is also well defined for
n=+\infty
,
in this case we will write
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{+\infty}
and
{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{E}}_{+\infty}
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, let

T_{n}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\ X(t)=n\}

denote the first hitting time of
n
,
with the usual convention
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
.

The transition probabilities are given by

p^{r}_{t}(n,m)=\frac{a_{r}(m)}{a_{r}(n)}\tilde{p}^{r}_{t}(n,m),

where
\tilde{p}^{r}_{t}(n,m)
is the heat kernel associated with
h^{r}
.
Let

g_{r}(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{r}_{t}(n,m)dt,

and note that, since the rate at which the chain leaves a state

is
P_{r}(m)
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(T_{m}<\infty)=P_{r}(m)g_{r}(n,m).

By Theorem
2.3
, we may define

p^{r}_{t}(m)=\lim_{n\to\infty}p^{r}_{t}(n,m),\qquad h^{r}_{m}={\mathbb{P}}(T_{m}<\infty)

and, for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}\backslash\{0\}
,

g_{r}(m)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{r}(n,m),\qquad a_{r}^{*}(m)=g_{r}(m)/a_{r}(m).

Note that
h^{r}_{0}=1
and, for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}\backslash\{0\}
,

(19)

h^{r}_{n}=P_{r}(n)a_{r}(n)a_{r}^{*}(n).

Proposition 4.1
.

For
r\geq 2
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r-1}\backslash\{0\}
,

a_{r-1}^{*}(k)=\sum_{n}q_{r}(n,k)a_{r}^{*}(n),

where the sum is over
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
such that
n_{i}\geq k_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
,

p^{r-1}_{t}(k)=\sum_{n}a_{r}(n)^{-1}q_{r}(n,k)a_{r-1}(k)p^{r}_{t}(n).

Integrating over
t>0
gives, by monotone convergence,

g_{r-1}(k)=\sum_{n}a_{r}(n)^{-1}q_{r}(n,k)a_{r-1}(k)g_{r}(n).

The statement of the proposition follows.
∎

When

Proposition 4.2

Proof.

The first claim is immediate from the fact that, under

is distributed

j(j+a)
,

For the second claim, define

\varphi_{j}(n)=\frac{1}{(n-j)!(n+a+j)!},\qquad\varphi_{j}^{*}(n)=(-1)^{j}\left[(1+j)_{a}+(j)_{a}\right](-j)_{n}(a+j)_{n},

and note that

5.
More general shapes

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of non-negative integers and denote

\beta_{ij}=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i+j-1}.

Let
\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
denote the set of non-negative integer arrays
(\pi_{ij},\ (i,j)\in\lambda/\mu)

which satisfy
\pi_{ij}\geq\pi_{i,j-1}\vee(\pi_{i-1,j}-\beta_{ij}),\quad(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu
,
with the convention
\pi_{ij}=0
for
(i,j)\notin\lambda/\mu
. We will write
\Pi^{\lambda}=\Pi^{\lambda/\phi}
.
Note that if
\alpha\equiv 0
then
\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
is the set of reverse plane
partitions of shape
\lambda/\mu
.

Recall that for a partition
\lambda
we denote by
\lambda^{\circ}
the
set of
(i,j)\in\lambda
, such that
(i+1,j)\in\lambda
and
(i,j+1)\in\lambda
.
Fix
\lambda
and
\mu\subset\lambda^{\circ}
.
Denote by
\tilde{\mu}
the extension of
\mu
to include
(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu

such that either
(i-1,j)\in\mu
or
(i,j-1)\in\mu
.
For
\sigma\in\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
, let
\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}
denote the set of
\pi\in\Pi^{\lambda}
with
\pi|_{\lambda/\mu}=\sigma
.
For
\pi\in\Pi^{\lambda}
, let

W_{\lambda,\mu}(\pi)=\prod_{(i,j)\in\tilde{\mu}}{\pi_{ij}\choose\pi_{i,j-1}}{\pi_{ij}+\beta_{ij}\choose\pi_{i-1,j}}\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}\frac{\pi_{ij}!}{(\pi_{ij}+\beta_{ij})!}.

For
\sigma\in\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
, let
K^{\lambda,\mu}_{\sigma}
denote the probability distribution on
\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}

defined by
K^{\lambda,\mu}_{\sigma}(\pi)=W_{\lambda,\mu}(\pi)/A_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma)
, where

A_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}}W_{\lambda,\mu}(\pi).

For
\sigma\in\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
and
(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu
, set

b_{ij}(\sigma)=(\sigma_{ij}-\sigma_{i,j-1})(\sigma_{ij}-\sigma_{i-1,j}+\beta_{ij}),

with the convention
\sigma_{ij}=0
for
(i,j)\notin\lambda/\mu
.
Let
C(\mu)
denote the set of external corners of
\mu
, that is, the set of

(i,j)\in\mu
such that
(i,j+1)\notin\mu
and
(i+1,j)\notin\mu
.
Define

G^{\lambda,\mu}=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu}b_{ij}(\sigma)D_{\sigma_{ij}},\qquad G^{\lambda}=G^{\lambda,\phi}

H^{\lambda,\mu}=G^{\lambda,\mu}+V_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma),\quad V_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma)=\sum_{(i,j)\in C(\mu)}\sigma_{i+1,j}\sigma_{i,j+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}\beta_{i+1,\mu_{i}}\sigma_{i,\mu_{i}+1}.

In proving the following theorem, we will also show that
H^{\lambda,\mu}A_{\lambda,\mu}=0
,
so that the corresponding Doob transform

L^{\lambda,\mu}=A_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma)^{-1}\circ H^{\lambda,\mu}\circ A_{\lambda,\mu}(\sigma)

generates a Markov chain on
\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\pi(t),\ t\geq 0
be a Markov chain on
\Pi^{\lambda}
with generator
G^{\lambda}

and initial law
K^{\lambda,\mu}_{\sigma}
, for some
\mu\subset\lambda^{\circ}
and
\sigma\in\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
.
Then
\sigma(t)=\pi(t)|_{\lambda/\mu}
is a Markov chain on
\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
with generator

L^{\lambda,\mu}
and, for
t>0
, the conditional law of
\pi(t)
given
\{\sigma(s),\ s\leq t\}

is
K^{\lambda,\mu}_{\sigma(t)}
.

Proof.

For notational convenience, let us fix
\lambda
and
\mu\subset\lambda^{\circ}
and write

W=W_{\lambda,\mu}
,
H=H^{\lambda,\mu}
and
V=V_{\lambda,\mu}
.
For functions
f
on
\Pi^{\lambda}
, define

(\Lambda f)(\sigma)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}}W(\pi)f(\pi).

We will show that

(26)

H\circ\Lambda=\Lambda\circ G^{\lambda},

which implies both
HA_{\lambda,\mu}=0
and the statement of the theorem.

For
\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}
and
v\in\lambda/\mu
,

[L_{\sigma_{v}}W(\pi)]b_{v}(\sigma)=W(\pi)b_{v}(\pi).

Thus,

\displaystyle[H(\Lambda f)](\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{\sigma}(\lambda)}\left([HW(\pi)]f(\pi)+\sum_{v\in\lambda/\mu}[L_{\sigma_{v}}W(\pi)]b_{v}(\sigma)D_{\sigma_{v}}f(\pi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{\sigma}(\lambda)}\left([HW(\pi)]f(\pi)+\sum_{v\in\lambda/\mu}W(\pi)b_{v}(\pi)D_{\pi_{v}}f(\pi)\right).

To complete the proof, we will show that, for
\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}
,

(27)

HW(\pi)={}^{*}\!G^{\mu}W(\pi),

where

{}^{*}\!G^{\mu}=\sum_{u\in\mu}B_{\pi_{u}}\circ b_{u}(\pi),

and
B_{n}
denotes the forward difference operator
B_{n}f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n)
.

For
u=(i,j)\in\mu
, define

b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)=(\pi_{i,j+1}-\pi_{ij})(\pi_{i+1,j}-\pi_{ij}+\beta_{i+1,j}),

and note that

B_{\pi_{u}}(b_{u}(\pi)W(\pi))=[b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{u}(\pi)]W(\pi).

On the other hand, for
\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}
and
v\in\lambda/\mu
,

b_{v}(\sigma)D_{\sigma_{v}}W(\pi)=[b_{v}(\pi)-b_{v}(\sigma)]W(\pi).

Thus (
27
) reduces to the identity, for
\pi\in\Pi_{\sigma}^{\lambda}
,

(28)

\sum_{v\in\lambda/\mu}[b_{v}(\pi)-b_{v}(\sigma)]+V(\sigma)=\sum_{u\in\mu}[b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{u}(\pi)].

For
u=(i,j)
, let us write
u\to v
is
v
is either
(i+1,j)
or
(i,j+1)
.
For
(i,j)\in\mu
,

\displaystyle b_{ij}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{ij}(\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{i+1,j}\pi_{i,j+1}-\pi_{ij}\pi_{i+1,j}-\pi_{ij}\pi_{i,j+1}

\displaystyle+\pi_{ij}\pi_{i-1,j}+\pi_{ij}\pi_{i,j-1}-\pi_{i-1,j}\pi_{i,j-1}

\displaystyle-\beta_{ij}(\pi_{ij}-\pi_{i,j-1})+\beta_{i+1,j}(\pi_{i,j+1}-\pi_{ij}).

Summing over
(i,j)\in\mu
gives

\sum_{u\in\mu}[b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{u}(\pi)]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\mu\\
(i+1,j+1)\notin\mu\end{subarray}}\pi_{i+1,j}\pi_{i,j+1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mu,v\in\lambda/\mu\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\pi_{u}\pi_{v}-T(\pi)+U(\pi),

where

T(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}\beta_{i,\mu_{i}+1}\pi_{i,\mu_{i}}\qquad U(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}\beta_{i+1,\mu_{i}}\pi_{i,\mu_{i}+1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\lambda/\mu}[b_{v}(\pi)-b_{v}(\sigma)]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu\\
(i-1,j-1)\in\mu\backslash C(\mu)\end{subarray}}\pi_{i-1,j}\pi_{i,j-1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mu,v\in\lambda/\mu\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\pi_{u}\pi_{v}-T(\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\mu\\
(i+1,j+1)\notin\mu\end{subarray}}\pi_{i+1,j}\pi_{i,j+1}-\sum_{(i,j)\in C(\mu)}\sigma_{i+1,j}\sigma_{i,j+1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mu,v\in\lambda/\mu\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\pi_{u}\pi_{v}-T(\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\mu\\
(i+1,j+1)\notin\mu\end{subarray}}\pi_{i+1,j}\pi_{i,j+1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mu,v\in\lambda/\mu\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\pi_{u}\pi_{v}-T(\pi)+U(\pi)-V(\sigma),

as required.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\pi
be distributed according to

, where
\sigma\in\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
.
Suppose that in the limit as
N\to\infty
,
\sigma/N\to a
, where
a=(a_{ij})\in\left({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\right)^{\lambda/\mu}
.
Then, in the same limit,
\pi/N\to x^{a}
in probability, where
x^{a}=(x^{a}_{ij})\in\left({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\right)^{\lambda}
is the unique solution
to the equations

(29)

(x_{i+1,j}-x_{ij})(x_{i,j+1}-x_{ij})=(x_{ij}-x_{i-1,j})(x_{ij}-x_{i,j-1}),\qquad(i,j)\in\mu

satisfying
x_{ij}=a_{ij}
for
(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu
,
0\leq x_{ij}\leq x_{i+1,j}\wedge x_{i,j+1}
for
(i,j)\in\mu
,
and with the convention
x_{i,0}=x_{0,j}=0
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
34
, Theorem 10.2]
,
see also
[
2
, Lemma 5.4]
.
Let
X_{a}
be the set of
x=(x_{ij})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{\lambda}
satisfying
x_{ij}=a_{ij}
for
(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu

and
x_{ij}\leq x_{i+1,j}\wedge x_{i,j+1}
for
(i,j)\in\mu
.
By Stirling’s formula, for
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{yN+b\choose xN}=-y\;h\left(\frac{x}{y}\right),

uniformly on any compact set
0\leq x\leq y\leq K
, where

h(p)=p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p).

Thus,

(30)

\lim_{\pi/N\to x}\frac{1}{N}\log W_{\lambda,\mu}(\pi)=-F(x),

uniformly for
x\in X_{a}
, where

F(x)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mu}\left[x_{i+1,j}h\left(\frac{x_{ij}}{x_{i+1,j}}\right)+x_{i,j+1}h\left(\frac{x_{ij}}{x_{i,j+1}}\right)\right].

We will show that
F
is strictly convex on
X_{a}^{\circ}
.
Let us write
u\to v
if
u=(i,j)
and
v
is either
(i+1,j)
or
(i,j+1)
.
In this notation,

F(x)=\sum_{u\in\mu}\sum_{v\in\lambda:u\to v}x_{v}h\left(\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\right).

Let
X_{a}^{\circ}
denote the set of
x=(x_{ij})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{\lambda}
satisfying
x_{ij}=a_{ij}
for
(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu

and
0<x_{ij}<x_{i+1,j}\wedge x_{i,j+1}
for
(i,j)\in\mu
.
Suppose that
x\in X_{a}^{\circ}
so that
x_{u}>0
for all
u\in\lambda

and
x_{v}>x_{u}
for all
u,v\in\lambda
with
u\to v
.
For
u\in\mu
, we compute

\partial_{x_{u}}F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\lambda\\
t\to u\end{subarray}}\log\left(1-\frac{x_{t}}{x_{u}}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\lambda\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\log\left(\frac{x_{v}}{x_{u}}-1\right),

\partial_{x_{u}}^{2}F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\lambda\\
t\to u\end{subarray}}\frac{x_{t}}{x_{u}}\frac{1}{x_{u}-x_{t}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\lambda\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\frac{x_{v}}{x_{u}}\frac{1}{x_{v}-x_{u}}.

For
u,v\in\mu
with
u\to v
,

\partial_{x_{u}}\partial_{x_{v}}F(x)=-\frac{1}{x_{v}-x_{u}}.

If
u,v\in\mu
and neither
u\to v
nor
v\to u
, then
\partial_{x_{u}}\partial_{x_{v}}F(x)=0
.
The associated quadratic form is thus given by

\sum_{u,v\in\mu}\xi_{u}\xi_{v}\partial_{x_{u}}\partial_{x_{v}}F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in\lambda\\
u\to v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x_{v}-x_{u}}\left(\frac{x_{v}}{x_{u}}\xi_{u}^{2}+\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\xi_{v}^{2}-2\xi_{u}\xi_{v}\right),

with the convention
\xi_{u}=0
for
u\in\lambda/\mu
. This is clearly non-negative
and, moreover, vanishes if, and only if,

\left(\frac{x_{v}}{x_{u}}\xi_{u}^{2}+\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\xi_{v}^{2}-2\xi_{u}\xi_{v}\right)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{x_{v}}{x_{u}}}\xi_{u}-\sqrt{\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}}\xi_{v}\right)^{2}=0,

or, equivalently,
\xi_{u}/x_{u}=\xi_{v}/x_{v}
, for all
u,v\in\lambda
with
u\to v
.
Recalling that
\xi_{v}=0
for
v\in\lambda/\mu
, this implies that

\xi_{u}=0
for all
u\in\mu
. The Hessian is therefore positive
definite on
X_{a}^{\circ}
.

Now,
F
is continuous on the compact set
X_{a}

and therefore has at least one global minimiser
x^{a}\in X_{a}
.
If
x^{a}\in\partial X_{a}
then, since
a_{v}>0
for
v\in\lambda/\mu
,
there exists

such that either

x^{a}_{i-1,j}\vee x^{a}_{i,j+1}=x^{a}_{ij}<x^{a}_{i+1,j}\wedge x^{a}_{i,j+1}

or

x^{a}_{i-1,j}\vee x^{a}_{i,j+1}<x^{a}_{ij}=x^{a}_{i+1,j}\wedge x^{a}_{i,j+1},

with the convention
x^{a}_{ij}=0
for
(i,j)\notin{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
.
In the first case,
\partial_{x_{u}}F(x^{a})=-\infty
and in the second case

\partial_{x_{u}}F(x^{a})=+\infty
, both contradicting the minimising
property of
x^{a}
. Thus

and,
by the strict convexity of

on
X_{a}^{\circ}
, it is the unique

to the critical point equations (

for

Since

is continuous and the limit (

satisfies a large deviation

Theorem 5.5
.

The Markov chain on
\Pi^{\lambda}
with generator

has a unique

for each
\mu\subset\lambda^{\circ}

\Pi^{\lambda/\mu}
with

and, for all

\pi(t)

Proof.

The proof of the first claim is in two steps.
First we note the following monotonicity property.
Given two different starting positions
\rho(0),\pi(0)\in\Pi^{\lambda}
with

\rho(0)\leq\pi(0)
, it is clear from the definition of
G^{\lambda}
that
we may construct a coupling between two realisations
\rho(t)
and

of the Markov chain with generator

and these starting positions,
such that, almost surely,
\rho(t)\leq\pi(t)
for all

. Indeed, we simply allow
the jumps at
(i,j)
to occur independently unless
\rho_{ij}=\pi_{ij}

and couple the next jump so that

decreases by one or both

and
\pi_{ij}

decrease by one, thus preserving the order
\rho\leq\pi

that the law of the process

is stochastically increasing in
the initial position
\pi(0)
.
We can therefore let
\pi(0)\to+\infty
in
\Pi^{\lambda}
to obtain a unique (in law) limiting process

in
\Pi^{\lambda,*}
.
It only remains to show that
\pi(t)\in\Pi^{\lambda}
for all

, almost surely.
This is straightforward, by induction on
\lambda
.

For the second claim, let

\tau\leq\sigma

By Theorem

, we can construct random starting positions

taking values in

for a fixed time

being
\tau
at time

. By Theorem

, this construction
has the required properties. The monotonicity property therefore

the law of the process
\pi(t),\ t\geq 0

,
is stochastically increasing in
\sigma
.
By uniqueness of the entrance law, it remains to show that

\pi

\pi\to+\infty

yields the formula

(31)

B_{r}(n)=\sum_{\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta^{\prime}_{r})}W_{B_{r}}(\pi).

Since
H^{r}B_{r}=0
and
B_{r}>0
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, we may consider the Doob transform

L^{B_{r}}=B_{r}(n)^{-1}\circ H^{B_{r}}\circ B_{r}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{B_{r}(n-e_{i})}{B_{r}(n)}n_{i}^{2}D_{n_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{B_{r}(n-e_{r})}{B_{r}(n)}n_{r}^{2}D_{n_{r}}.

For
\pi\in{\rm RPP}(\delta^{\prime}_{r})
, we define

b_{ij}(\pi)=\begin{cases}(\pi_{ij}-\pi_{i-1,j})(\pi_{ij}-\pi_{i,j-1})&i\neq j\\
(\pi_{ii}-\pi_{i-1,i})(\pi_{ii}-\pi_{i-1,i-1})/2&i=j\end{cases}

with the convention
\pi_{i,0}=\pi_{0,j}=0
,
and set

G^{B_{r}}=\sum_{(i,j)\in\delta_{r}^{\prime}}b_{ij}(\pi)D_{\pi_{ij}}.

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, let
K^{B_{r}}_{n}
be the probability distribution on

{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta^{\prime}_{r})
defined by
K^{B_{r}}_{n}(\pi)=W_{B_{r}}(\pi)/B_{r}(n)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\pi(t),\ t\geq 0
be a Markov chain on
{\rm RPP}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})
with generator
G^{B_{r}}

and initial distribution
K^{B_{r}}_{n}
, for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
.
Then

N(t)=(\pi_{1,2r-1}(t),\ldots,\pi_{r,r}(t))

is a Markov chain on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
with generator

L^{B_{r}}

and, for all
t>0
, the conditional law of
\pi(t)
given
\{N(s),\ s\leq t\}

is
K^{B_{r}}_{N(t)}
.

Proof.

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
and functions
f
on
{\rm RPP}(\delta^{\prime}_{r})
, define

(\Lambda_{B_{r}}f)(n)=\sum_{\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta^{\prime}_{r})}W_{B_{r}}(\pi)f(\pi).

We will show that
H^{B_{r}}\circ\Lambda_{B_{r}}=\Lambda_{B_{r}}\circ G^{B_{r}}
,
from which the statement of the theorem follows.
Note that this intertwining relation also implies
H^{B_{r}}B_{r}=0
,
for
B_{r}
defined by (
31
).
The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, let
b_{i,2r-i}(n)=n_{i}^{2}
,
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and
b_{rr}(n)=n_{r}^{2}/2
.
Note that

H^{B_{r}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i,2r-i}(n)D_{n_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i}n_{i+1}.

For
\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})
and
i=1,\ldots,r
, we have

[L_{n_{i}}W_{B_{r}}(\pi)]b_{i,2r-i}(n)=W_{B_{r}}(\pi)b_{i,2r-i}(\pi).

Thus, as in the type
A
case, it suffices to show that,
for
\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})
,

(32)

H^{B_{r}}W_{B_{r}}(\pi)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j<2r-i}B_{\pi_{ij}}\left(b_{ij}(\pi)W_{B_{r}}(\pi)\right).

For
u=(i,j)
, with
1\leq i\leq j<2r-i
, define

b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)=\begin{cases}(\pi_{i+1,j}-\pi_{ij})(\pi_{i,j+1}-\pi_{ij})&i\neq j\\
(\pi_{i+1,i}-\pi_{ii})(\pi_{i,i+1}-\pi_{ii})/2&i=j\end{cases}

and note that

B_{\pi_{u}}(b_{u}(\pi)W_{B_{r}}(\pi))=[b_{u}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{u}(\pi)]W_{B_{r}}(\pi).

On the other hand, for
\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})
and
i=1,\ldots,r
,

b_{i,2r-i}(n)D_{n_{i}}W_{B_{r}}(\pi)=[b_{i,2r-i}(\pi)-b_{i,2r-i}(n)]W_{B_{r}}(\pi).

Thus (
32
) reduces to the identity, for
\pi\in{\rm RPP}_{n}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})
,

\sum_{i=1}^{r}[b_{i,2r-i}(\pi)-b_{i,2r-i}(n)]+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i}n_{i+1}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j<2r-i}[b_{ij}^{\prime}(\pi)-b_{ij}(\pi)].

This is readily verified, as in the type
A
case.

Remark 6.2

6.2.
Type

There is a more refined structure incorporating types
BC_{r}
which
naturally interpolate, via intertwining relations, between the root systems of type
B_{r}
.
The intertwining relations we discuss here are analogous to those
presented in
[
14

in the context of class one Whittaker functions.

For example, if we let

H_{k}^{BC_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}kD_{k}+\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)D^{(2)}_{k}

D^{(2)}_{k}f(k)=f(k-2)-f(k)

Q^{B_{1}}_{BC_{1}}(n,k)=2^{-n}{n\choose k},

then one can easily check that

H^{B_{1}}\circ Q^{B_{1}}_{BC_{1}}=Q^{B_{1}}_{BC_{1}}\circ H^{BC_{1}}.

This intertwining relation yields the following. Let
E=\{n\geq k\geq 0\}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(X_{t},Y_{t})
be a Markov chain on
E
with generator

G=H_{k}^{BC_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}n(n-k)D_{n}.

Suppose
X_{0}=n
and
Y_{0}\sim\mbox{\rm Binomial}(n,1/2)
.
Then
X_{t}
is a Markov chain on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with generator

H^{B_{1}}=n^{2}D_{n}/2
and, for all
t>0
, the conditional law of
Y_{t}

given
X_{s},\ 0\leq s\leq t
, is
\mbox{\rm Binomial}(X_{t},1/2)
.

For
r=2
, we define

H^{BC_{2}}=n^{2}D_{n}+\frac{1}{2}mD_{m}+\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)D^{(2)}_{m}+nm,

and denote by
BC_{2}(n,m)
the unique solution to
H^{BC_{2}}BC_{2}=0
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2}
with
BC_{2}(0,0)=1
.
The numbers

are positive integers and given by

BC_{2}(n,m)=\sum_{k}{n\choose k}{m\choose k}2^{k}.

These are the Delannoy numbers.
They also satisfy

\tilde{H}^{BC_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}D_{n}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}D_{m}+\frac{1}{2}nmD_{n,m}+nm,

These claims follow from the intertwining relations

H^{BC_{2}}\circ Q=Q\circ H^{B_{1}},\quad\tilde{H}^{BC_{2}}\circ Q=Q\circ H^{B_{1}},\quad Q((n,m),k)={n\choose k}{m\choose k}2^{k}.

Denote the corresponding Doob transforms by

L^{BC_{2}}=BC_{2}(n,m)^{-1}\circ H^{BC_{2}}\circ BC_{2}(n,m),

\tilde{L}^{BC_{2}}=BC_{2}(n,m)^{-1}\circ\tilde{H}^{BC_{2}}\circ BC_{2}(n,m).

P

A penalty-free Shifted Boundary Method of arbitrary order Journal: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering
J. Haydel Collins, Alexei Lozinski, Guglielmo Scovazzi

Abstract
We introduce and analyze a penalty-free formulation of the Shifted Boundary Method (SBM), inspired by the asymmetric version of the Nitsche method. We prove its stability and convergence for arbitrary order finite element interpolation spaces and we test its performance with a number of numerical experiments. Moreover, while the SBM was previously believed to be only asymptotically consistent (in the sense of Galerkin orthogonality), we prove here that it is indeed exactly consistent. This contribution is dedicated to Thomas J.R. Hughes, in honor of his lifetime achievements.

Introduction

Recently, immersed/embedded/unfitted computational methods have received great attention for problems involving complex geometrical features, described in standard formats (i.e., CAD) and non-standard formats (i.e., STL, level sets, etc.).
In fact, immersed/embedded/unfitted have the potential to drastically reduce the pre-processing time involved in the acquisition of the geometry and the generation of the computational grid.
An (incomplete) list of recent developments include the Immersed Boundary Finite Element Method (IB-FEM)
boffi2003finite
, the cutFEM
burman2010ghost
,
hansbo2002unfitted
,
schott2015face
,
burman2018cut
,
burman2019dirichlet
,
burman2017cut
,
burman2010fictitious
,
burman2012fictitious
,
burman2014unfitted
,
burman2018shape
,
massing2015nitsche
,
burman2015cutfem
,
kamensky2017immersogeometric
,
xu2016tetrahedral
,
badia2018aggregated
, the Finite Cell Method
parvizian2007finite
,
duster2008finite
, and similar earlier methods
hollig2003finite
,
hollig2001weighted
,
ruberg2012subdivision
,
ruberg2014fixed
,
lozinski2016new
.

Most of these approaches require the geometric construction of the partial elements cut by the embedded boundary, which can be both algorithmically complicated and computationally intensive, due to data structures that are considerably more complex with respect to corresponding fitted finite element methods.
Furthermore, integrating the variational forms on the characteristically irregular cut cells may also be difficult and advanced quadrature formulas might need to be employed
parvizian2007finite
,
duster2008finite
.

The Shifted Boundary Method (SBM) was proposed in
main2018shifted0
as an alternative to existing embedded/unfitted boundary methods and belongs to the more specific class of approximate domain methods
bramble1972projection
,
bramble1996finite
,
bramble1994robust
,
cockburn2012solving
,
cockburn2014priori
,
cockburn2014solving
,
bertoluzza2005fat
,
bertoluzza2011analysis
,
glowinski1994fictitious
,
lozinski2016new
.
The
\phi
-FEM
duprez2020phi
,
duprez2022immersed
,
cotin2022varphi
,
duprez2023phi
,
duprez2023phi2
,
duprez2023new
also belongs to this class, although with some key differences.
The SBM was proposed in
main2018shifted0
for the Poisson and Stokes flow problems and generalized in
main2018shifted
to the advection-diffusion and Navier-Stokes equations, and in
song2018shifted
to hyperbolic conservation laws.
An analysis of the stability and accuracy of the SBM for the Poisson, advection-diffusion, and Stokes operators was also included in
main2018shifted0
,
main2018shifted
,
atallah2020analysis
, respectively. A high-order version of the SBM was proposed in
atallah2022high
, applications to solid and fracture mechanics problems were presented in
liu2020shift
,
atallah2021shifted
,
li2021shifted
,
li2023blended
,
li2021shiftedsimple
and simulations of static and moving interfaces were developed in
li2020shifted
,
colomes2021weighted
.

The SBM is built for minimal computational complexity and does not contain any cut cell by design.
Specifically, the location where boundary conditions are applied is
shifted
from the true to an approximate (surrogate) boundary, and, at the same time, modified (
shifted
) boundary conditions are applied in order to avoid a reduction in the convergence rates of the overall formulation.
In fact, if the boundary conditions associated to the true domain are not appropriately modified on the surrogate domain, only first-order convergence is to be expected. The shifted boundary conditions are imposed by means of Taylor expansions and are applied weakly, using Nitsche’s method, leading to a simple, robust, accurate and efficient algorithm.

In the original and subsequent works on the SBM, boundary conditions were enforced by means of a Nitsche-like approach, inspired by the symmetric penalty Galerkin formulation.
This strategy requires the selection of a penalty parameter, which might be tedious to estimate in practical engineering computations.
In the context of immersed methods, there has been recent interest in developing penalty-free methods
oyarzua2019high
, and we propose here an alternative SBM inspired by the work of Burman
burman12
.
We prove stability, consistency and convergence of this new, parameter-free, SBM formulation in the context of the Poisson equation, and we test it in a number of preliminary numerical experiments.
We also extend these ideas to the case of compressible linear elasticity.

In the derivation of the mathematical proofs, we also introduce an interpretation of the SBM that allow us to prove its
exact
Galerkin consistency, while before the method was only thought to be asymptotically consistent.

This article is organized as follows: Section
2
introduces the general SBM notation; the analysis of the penalty-free SBM variational formulation of the Poisson problem and its numerical analysis of stability and convergence is discussed in Section
3
; the extension of the method to the equations of compressible linear elasticity is discussed in Section
4
; a series of numerical tests is presented in Section
5
; and, finally, conclusions are summarized in Section
6
.

2.2
General strategy

In the SBM, the governing equations are discretized in
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
rather than in
\Omega
, with the challenge of accurately imposing boundary conditions on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
.
To this end, boundary conditions are
shifted
from
\Gamma
to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
, by performing the
m
th-order Taylor expansion of the variable of interest at the surrogate boundary.

Under the assumption that
u
is sufficiently smooth in the strip between
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
and
\Gamma
, so as to admit a
m
th-order Taylor expansion point-wise, let
{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
denote the
i
th-order directional derivative along
\boldsymbol{\delta}
:

{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}u=\displaystyle{\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=i}\frac{i!}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}!}\frac{\partial^{i}u}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\;.

Then, we can write

\displaystyle u(\boldsymbol{x})=u(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}+\boldsymbol{\delta}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))=u({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\,u(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})}{i!}+({\mbox{R}}^{m}(u,\boldsymbol{\delta}))({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})\,,

(4)

where the remainder
{\mbox{R}}^{m}(u,\boldsymbol{\delta})
satisfies
|{\mbox{R}}^{m}(u,\boldsymbol{\delta})|=o(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{m})
as
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|\to 0
.
Assume that the Dirichlet condition
u(\boldsymbol{x})=u_{D}(\boldsymbol{x})
needs to be imposed on the true boundary
\Gamma
.
Using the map
\boldsymbol{M}_{h}
, one can extend
u_{D}
from
\Gamma
to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
as
\bar{u}_{D}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})=u_{D}(\boldsymbol{M}_{h}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}}))
. Then, the Taylor expansion can be used to enforce the Dirichlet condition on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
rather than
\Gamma
, as

{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{m}u-\bar{u}_{D}+{\mbox{R}}^{m}(u,\boldsymbol{\delta})=0\;,\qquad\mbox{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;,

(5)

where we have introduced the boundary
shift
operator for every
\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
, namely:

{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{m}u(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}):=u(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\,u(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})}{i!}\;.

(6)

Neglecting the remainder
{\mbox{R}}^{m}(u,\boldsymbol{\delta})
, we obtain the final expression of the
shifted
approximation of order
m
of the boundary condition

{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{m}u\approx\bar{u}_{D}\,,\quad\mbox{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;.

(7)

This shifted boundary condition will be enforced weakly in what follows, and whenever there is no source of confusion, the bar symbol will be removed from the extended quantities, and we would write
u_{D}
in place of
\bar{u}_{D}
.

2.3
General notation

Throughout the paper, we will denote the space of square integrable functions on
\Omega
as
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
We will use the Sobolev spaces
H^{m}(\Omega)=W^{m,2}(\Omega)
of index of regularity
m\geq 0
and index of summability 2, equipped with the (scaled) norm

\|v\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}=\left(\|\,v\,\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\,l(\Omega)^{k}{\mbox{D}}^{k}v\,\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}\;,

(8)

where
{\mbox{D}}^{k}
is the
k
th-order spatial derivative operator and
l(A)=\mathrm{meas}_{d}(A)^{1/d}
is a characteristic length of the domain
A
(
d=2,3
indicates the number of spatial dimensions). Note that
H^{0}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega)
. As usual, we use a simplified notation for norms and semi-norms, i.e., we set
\|\,v\,\|_{m,\Omega}=\|\,v\,\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}
and
|\,v\,|_{k,\Omega}=\|\,{\mbox{D}}^{k}v\,\|_{0,\Omega}=\|\,{\mbox{D}}^{k}v\,\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

We also introduce the definition of the
L^{2}
-inner product over
\omega\in\Omega
, namely
(\,u\,,\,v\,)_{\omega}=\int_{\omega}u\,v
, and an analogous inner product on the portion of the boundary
\gamma\subset\partial\Omega
, namely
\langle\,u\,,\,w\,\rangle_{\gamma}=\int_{\gamma}u\,w
.
We can also restrict to
\omega
and
\gamma
the norms and seminorms initially defined on
\Omega
and
\Gamma
, that is
\|\cdot\|_{k,\omega}
,
|\cdot|_{k,\omega}
and
\|\cdot\|_{0,\gamma}
, for example.

3
A penalty-free Shifted Boundary Method for the Poisson equation

Consider the Poisson problem with non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=\;f\qquad\text{\ \ in \ }\Omega\;,

(9a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\;u_{D}\qquad\text{on \ }\Gamma=\partial\Omega\;,

(9b)

where
u
is the primary variable,
u_{D}
its value on the boundary
\Gamma
and
f
a body force (i.e., non-homogeneous boundary conditions are enforced on the entire boundary
\Gamma
).

Let us introduce the discrete space

\displaystyle V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})=\;\left\{v_{h}\in C^{0}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\ |\ {v_{h}}_{|\tilde{T}}\in\mathscr{P}^{k}(\tilde{T})\,,\,\forall\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}\right\}\,,

(10)

where
\mathscr{P}^{k}(\tilde{T})
is the the space of polynomials of at most order
k
over the triangle
\tilde{T}
.
We propose a penalty-free Shifted Boundary Method, inspired by the antisymmetric version of the Nitsche’s method
nitscheweak
,
arnold2002unified
that was analyzed by Burman
burman12
.
Hence, the penalty-free variational statement of (
9
) can be cast as

Find
u_{h}\in V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
such that,
\forall w_{h}\in V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})

\displaystyle a^{k}_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,w_{h})

\displaystyle=\;l_{h}(w_{h})\;,

(11a)

where

\displaystyle a^{k}_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,w_{h})

\displaystyle=\;(\,\nabla u_{h}\,,\,\nabla w_{h}\,)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,w_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\,{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,,\,\nabla w_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;,

(11b)

\displaystyle l_{h}(w_{h})

\displaystyle=\;(\,f\,,\,w_{h}\,)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\langle\,\bar{u}_{D}\,,\,\nabla w_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(11c)

3.1
Main theoretical results

We introduce the natural
h
-dependent norm

\|u\|_{h}=\left(|u|^{2}_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\|h^{-1/2}\,u\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and prove stability by way of an inf-sup condition, under the following assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

Denoting
\delta(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})=|\boldsymbol{\delta}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})|
the Euclidean norm of
\boldsymbol{\delta}
at
\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
, we assume that

\displaystyle\max_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\delta(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})\leqslant C_{\max}h\;.

(12)

Assumption 2
.

Let
C_{I,k}
be the constant in the trace inverse inequality
\|{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\leqslant C_{I,k}\,|h_{\tilde{T}}^{1/2}\,u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{T}}
, for any
u_{h}\in V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
and for any side
\tilde{e}\subset\partial\tilde{T}
of the element
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}
(i.e., an edge/face in two/three dimensions).
We assume that
\exists\beta\in[0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant\frac{\beta}{C_{I,k}}\,|h^{1/2}\,u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

(13)

Assumption 3
.

In two dimensions, let
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}
be a triangle of the mesh with a side
\tilde{e}\subset\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
.
We call
\tilde{T}
a “normal boundary triangle” if one of its vertices lies in
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}\setminus\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
.
Otherwise (i.e., if all the 3 vertices of the triangle are on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
) we call
\tilde{T}
an “abnormal boundary triangle.”
We shall suppose that every abnormal boundary triangle lies at a distance
d\leqslant c_{\tilde{T}}h
from a normal boundary triangle, where the constant
c_{\tilde{T}}
is not large.
The three-dimensional case is analogous.

Assumption 4
.

The Neumann boundary is body-fitted, that is
\Gamma_{N}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{N,h}
.

Remark 4

(validity of Assumption
1
and
2
)
.

Assumption
1
is normally satisfied with a constant
C_{\max}
of order 1.
Assumption
2
is inspired by a similar assumption made in
atallah2021analysis
but is more difficult to satisfy in practice and should be considered as a technical condition for the proofs.
In fact, this condition may not be satisfied by the computational grids used in the numerical experiments in Section
5
, despite obtaining optimal convergence rates in all simulations.
Note that the
h^{1/2}
factor appears naturally in Assumption
2
, since on each side
\tilde{e}\subset\partial\tilde{T}
we have

\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\sim h_{\tilde{T}}\,|\nabla u_{h}|\sqrt{|\tilde{e}|}\sim\sqrt{h_{\tilde{T}}}\,|\nabla u_{h}|\sqrt{|\tilde{T}|}\sim\sqrt{h_{\tilde{T}}}\,\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{T}}\;.

The practical difficulty in this assumption consists in requiring that
\beta<1
.

Remark 5

(validity of Assumption
3
)
.

For practical purposes, Assumption
3
can be safely accepted.
While Assumption
3
could be violated in principle, this happens very rarely in practice.

In fact, the way elements are connected in regular grids prevents the edges/faces associated to several abnormal elements to form a chain along the surrogate boundary.
Even in the most complex three-dimensional geometries, a chain of abnormal boundary tetrahedral elements (each of which with all four nodes on the surrogate boundary) is highly unlikely. Isolated abnormal boundary tetrahedrons are possible, but intermixed with many normal boundary tetrahedrons, and Assumption
3
would then hold.

Remark 6
.

Although we restrict the numerical analysis to a pure Dirichlet problem, we will present numerical examples with mixed Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions.

Lemma 1
.

Assume Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold. Then there exists
\gamma>0
depending only on
C_{\max}
,
\beta
, and the regularity of the mesh such that

\inf_{u_{h}\in V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\,\sup_{v_{h}\in V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\,\frac{a^{k}_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,w_{h})}{\|u_{h}\|_{h}\|v_{h}\|_{h}}\geq\gamma\;.

Proof

Take any
u_{h}\in V^{1}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
. We construct first a test function
w_{h}\in V^{1}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\subset V^{k}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
such that

\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\geq c_{1}\,\|h^{-1}\,u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{2}-c_{2}|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}

(14a)

and

\|w_{h}\|_{h}\leq c_{3}\|u_{h}\|_{h}\;.

(14b)

This is achieved by taking
w_{h}\in V^{1}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
such that
w_{h}
vanishes at all the interior nodes and

w_{h}={\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}\;,\qquad\text{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;,

where
{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}
the nodal linear interpolation operator to
V^{1}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
.
We will restrict the proof of (
14a
) to the two-dimensional case, since the three-dimensional case would follow very similar steps, although with more tedious derivations.
Observe first that

\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}=\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},u_{h}-{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;,

and consider now an edge
\tilde{e}\subset\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\cap\partial\tilde{T}
, for a corresponding element
\tilde{T}
.
Applying Assumption
1
, appropriate inverse and trace inequalities (which also rely on the equivalency of discrete norms), we obtain

\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\lesssim h_{\tilde{e}}^{3/2}\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{e})}\lesssim h_{\tilde{e}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\leqslant Ch_{\tilde{T}}^{1/2}|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{T}}\;,

where the symbol
\lesssim
indicates inequality up to a constant.
By analogous inverse inequalities, since
w_{h}
is fully determined by
u_{h}
on the boundary
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
, we have

\|{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\leqslant\frac{C}{h_{\tilde{T}}}\|u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}}\;.

Hence

\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\geqslant\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\frac{C}{\sqrt{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

(15)

and to prove (
14a
) it is sufficient to bound from below
\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}
, which can be done edge by edge.
First, consider any boundary edge
{\tilde{e}}
associated with a
normal
boundary triangle
{\tilde{T}}
.
Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be the endpoints of
{\tilde{e}}
and
x_{3}
the remaining vertex of
{\tilde{T}}
.
Since
{\tilde{T}}
is a normal boundary triangle, we are sure that
x_{3}
is not on the boundary, hence
w_{h}(x_{3})=0
.
Let
x_{\tilde{T}}
be the point on the segment
x_{1}x_{2}
such that the segments
x_{\tilde{T}}x_{3}
and
x_{1}x_{2}
are perpendicular and let
h_{\tilde{T}}
be the distance between
x_{\tilde{T}}
and
x_{3}
(i.e., the height of
{\tilde{T}}
).
Recalling that
w_{h}(x_{3})=0
we observe

{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h}=\frac{w_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})}{h_{\tilde{T}}}=\frac{{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})}{h_{\tilde{T}}}

with
\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}
the outward unit normal on the edge
{\tilde{e}}
. We have thus

\int_{\tilde{e}}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h})\,u_{h}=\int_{\tilde{e}}\frac{{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})}{h_{\tilde{T}}}\,u_{h}=\int_{\tilde{e}}\frac{u_{h}^{2}}{h_{\tilde{T}}}-\int_{\tilde{e}}\frac{u_{h}-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})}{h_{\tilde{T}}}\,u_{h}\;.

As

lies on the straight line passing through

and
x_{2}

with

satisfying

with some

depending only on the mesh regularity (in fact, in the most typical case of an acute triangle

, one actually has

, but we also allow for obtuse mesh cells). Since
{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}
is an affine function on

taking the value

at points

{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}u_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})=au_{h}(x_{1})+(1-a)u_{h}(x_{2})

Using the inverse inequality

\max_{x\in{\tilde{T}}}|\nabla u_{h}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{I}}{h_{\tilde{e}}}|u_{h}|_{1,{\tilde{T}}}\;,

it follows that

\|u_{h}-u_{h}(x_{\tilde{T}})\|_{0,{\tilde{e}}}\leq C\sqrt{h_{\tilde{T}}}|u_{h}|_{1,{\tilde{T}}}\;,

which can be substituted inside (

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{e}}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h})\,u_{h}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{h_{\tilde{T}}}\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,{\tilde{e}}}-\frac{C}{\sqrt{h_{\tilde{T}}}}|u_{h}|_{1,{\tilde{T}}}\|u_{h}\|_{0,{\tilde{e}}}\;.

Summing this over all the boundary edges belonging to normal boundary cells
and combining with (
15

\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\geqslant\|h^{-1/2}u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{n}}-{C}\,\|h^{-1/2}u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\,{\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}}\cdot\nabla w_{h},u_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{a}}

(17)

where
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{n}
regroups the boundary edges from normal boundary cells,

\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{a}
regroups the boundary edges from abnormal boundary cells. This would immediately lead to (

) in the case

However,
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{a}\not=\emptyset
in general and given any abnormal boundary cell
{\tilde{T}}
with all three vertices on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}

Hence,

In view of Assumption
3

\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{a}
to that on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}^{n}

\tilde{e}^{a}
from an abnormal cell sharing a vertex

\displaystyle\|u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{e}^{a}}^{2}\leqslant C(h_{\tilde{e}^{a}}|u_{h}(v)|^{2}+h_{\tilde{e}^{a}}^{2}\|\nabla u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,{\tilde{e}^{a}}})\leqslant C(\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{e}^{n}}+h_{{\tilde{e}^{n}}}^{2}\|\nabla u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,{\tilde{e}^{n}}}+h_{\tilde{e}^{a}}^{2}\|\nabla u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,{\tilde{e}^{a}}})\;.

Note in particular that this length is uniformly bounded, under Assumptions

Summing (

\displaystyle a^{k}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\geq\;(1-\beta)|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}+\alpha c_{1}\|h^{-1/2}u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{2}-\alpha c_{2}|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}-\alpha|u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}|w_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
1

In addition, assume that the mesh
\mathscr{T}_{h}

\mathfrak{h}
(i.e.

) and that

\mathscr{C}^{2}
.

-error bound holds:

\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\;.

Proof

Introduce
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, the solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta w

\displaystyle=\;(u-u_{h})\mathbbm{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\quad\;\text{ on }\Omega\;,

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\;0\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ on }\partial\Omega\;,

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}
is the indicator function of the set
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
.
Then, we have
\|w\|_{2,\Omega}\leqslant C\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}
and, integrating by parts,

\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}=(u-u_{h},-\Delta w)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}=-\langle u-u_{h},\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla w)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\\
=a^{k}_{h}(u-u_{h},w)-\langle u-u_{h},\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h}),\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}},w\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

Thanks to the Galerkin orthogonality property (
23
),

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\;a^{k}_{h}(u-u_{h},w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w)-\langle u-u_{h}+\langle{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h}),\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}},w\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\underbrace{|u-u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}|w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}}_{I}+\underbrace{\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\left\|\sqrt{h}\nabla(w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w)\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}}_{II}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leqslant}\;+\underbrace{\left\|\sqrt{h}\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}(w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w)\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}}_{III}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leqslant}\;+\underbrace{(\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}})\|\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}}_{IV}+\underbrace{\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\|w\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}}_{V}\;.

(26)

We recall that the proof of Theorem
1
gives
\|u-u_{h}\|_{h}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|\,u|_{k+1,\Omega}
. This enables us to obtain optimal bounds for the first three terms above:

Term I:

Recalling that
|u-u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leqslant\|u-u_{h}\|_{h}
and using standard interpolation estimates, we have

|u-u_{h}|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}|w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\,|w|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

Term II:

By definition of
{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}(u-u_{h})\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\hskip 1.69998pt(u-u_{h})}{i!}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\|u-u_{h}\|_{h}+C\,\|h^{1/2}\,\nabla(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

The last bound is obtained using Assumption
1
and combining Theorem
1
with discrete trace inequalities, inverse inequalities, and interpolation estimates.
Indeed, for any
i
between 1 and
k
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\frac{{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\hskip 1.69998pt(u-u_{h})}{i!}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;C\,\|h^{i-1/2}\,\nabla^{i}(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;C\,\left(\|h^{i-1/2}\,\nabla^{i}(u-{\mbox{I}}^{i}_{h}u)\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|h^{i-1/2}\,\nabla^{i}({\mbox{I}}^{i}_{h}u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\;C\,\left(\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}+\|h^{-1/2}\,({\mbox{I}}^{i}_{h}u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\;C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\;,

where
\nabla^{i}
is the
i
th-order gradient.
Hence,

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\;{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\left(\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}+\|h^{-1/2}(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\right)\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}

and

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\;{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\left\|\sqrt{h}\;\nabla(w-{\mbox{I}}^{1}_{h}w)\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\;\mathfrak{h}^{k+1}\;|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\;|w|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

Term III:

Because this term is similar to Term II, it can be bounded in the same manner.

The remaining two terms in (
3.1
) are responsible for the loss of
\sqrt{h}
in our error estimate.

Term IV:

Again, by inverse estimates (similar to Term II),

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;2\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{{\mbox{D}}^{i}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\hskip 1.69998pt(u-u_{h})}{i!}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;2\,\mathfrak{h}^{1/2}\,\|u-u_{h}\|_{h}+C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega})\;.

This gives already the error of order
\mathfrak{h}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}
.
Unfortunately, one cannot gain another
\mathfrak{h}^{1/2}
, since the norm
\|\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}
can only be bounded by
\|\nabla w\|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}
, using the trace theorem.
Thus,

(\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}(u-u_{h})\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}})\,\|\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\,\|w\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

Term V:

By calculations similar to those above, we have

\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k-1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\;.

To bound
\|w\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}
, we introduce the distance function
\phi
on

\Omega
, i.e.
\phi(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\Gamma)
for any
x\in\Omega
and
recall the Hardy-type inequality from
duprez2020phi
(recall that
w
vanishes on

\Gamma
)

\left\|\frac{w}{\phi}\right\|_{1,\Omega}\leqslant C\,\|w\|_{2,\Omega}\;.

By Assumption
1
and the trace theorem, we obtain

\|w\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant\max_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}|\phi(x)|\left\|\frac{w}{\phi}\right\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}\,\left\|\frac{w}{\phi}\right\|_{1,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}\,\|w\|_{2,\Omega}\;.

Hence

\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\|w\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leqslant C\,\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\,\|w\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

Assembling the bounds for all the terms in (
3.1
) yields

\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{2}\leqslant C\;\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\,\|w\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leqslant C\;\mathfrak{h}^{k+1/2}\,|u|_{k+1,\Omega}\,\|u-u_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;,

which concludes the proof.

3.2
On condition numbers

it is easy to see that the condition number of the matrix associated to the bilinear form
a_{h}^{k}
in (
11b
) scales like
\mathfrak{h}^{-2}
on a quasi-uniform mesh of size
\mathfrak{h}
, similar to the case of a standard FEM on a fitted mesh.
More precisely, denoting by
\mathbf{A}
the matrix representing
a_{h}^{k}
in the standard basis of
V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
, the condition number
\kappa(\mathbf{A}):=\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\|_{2}
satisfies

\kappa(\mathbf{A})\lesssim\mathfrak{h}^{-2}\;.

(27)

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{2}
stands for the matrix (operator) norm associated to the vector
2
-norm (or, Euclidean norm)
|\cdot|_{2}
, namely:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{\mathbf{Av}\cdot\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{v}|_{2}|\mathbf{w}|_{2}}\;.

We give here a sketch of the proof. Associating any
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
to a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(of
N
degrees of freedom), we observe
\|v_{h}\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\sim\mathfrak{h}^{d/2}|\mathbf{v}|_{2}
by the equivalence of norms on the quasi-uniform mesh. The continuity of the form
a_{h}^{k}
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
leads to

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\;\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{\mathbf{Av}\cdot\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{v}|_{2}|\mathbf{w}|_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\mathfrak{h}^{d}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\dfrac{a_{h}^{k}(v_{h},w_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\|w_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\mathfrak{h}^{d}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\dfrac{\|v_{h}\|_{h}\|w_{h}\|_{h}}{\|v_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\|w_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\mathfrak{h}^{d-2}

since
\|w_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\mathfrak{h}^{-1}\,\|w_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}
.
Similarly, the inf-sup condition of Lemma
1
implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\|_{2}}=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{\mathbf{Av}\cdot\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{v}|_{2}|\mathbf{w}|_{2}}\gtrsim\,\mathfrak{h}^{d}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\;\dfrac{a_{h}^{k}(v_{h},w_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\|w_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}}\gtrsim\,\mathfrak{h}^{d}

since
\|w_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim\|w_{h}\|_{h}
(a form of the Poincaré inequality). These two estimates give (
27
).

4
A penalty-free Shifted Boundary Method for compressible linear elasticity

In the numerical tests that follow, we also consider the equations of (compressible) linear elasticity. Their strong form is given as

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma(\boldsymbol{u})}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{b}\qquad\text{\ \ in \ }\Omega\;,

(28a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{u}_{D}\qquad\!\text{on \ }\Gamma_{D}\;,

(28b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{t}_{N}\qquad\text{on \ }\Gamma_{N}\;,

(28c)

where
\boldsymbol{u}
is the displacement field,
\boldsymbol{u}_{D}
its value on the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
,
\boldsymbol{t}_{N}
the normal traction along the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
, and
\boldsymbol{b}
a body force.
We assume that
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}}
and
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
.
The stress
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a linear function of
\boldsymbol{u}
, according to the constitutive model

\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})={\mbox{C}}\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\;,

where
C
is the fourth-order elasticity tensor. For isotropic materials, the previous definition of the stress reduces to

\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})=2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{I}\;.

We then consider the following variational formulation of (
28
), also inspired by
atallah2021shifted
:

Find
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
, where

\boldsymbol{V}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h}):=\;\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in(C^{0}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h}))^{n_{d}}\ |\ {\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}_{|T}\in(\mathscr{P}^{1}(T))^{n_{d}}\,,\,\forall T\in\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}\right\}\;,

such that, for any
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
, it holds

\displaystyle(2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\,,\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}))_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+(\lambda\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\,,\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\,\,2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle+\langle\,{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{D}\,,\,2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{D,h}}

\displaystyle+\langle\,\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\,(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}})\,(2\mu\,{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k-1}(\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))\boldsymbol{n}+\lambda\,{\mbox{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{k-1}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\boldsymbol{n})-\bar{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N}\,\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{N,h}}

\displaystyle\;=\;(\boldsymbol{b}\,,\,\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,.

(29)

This variational formulation can be analyzed with very similar strategies to the Poisson equation, and similar results on convergence and stability can be derived in the case of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions.
In the case when Neumann boundary conditions are applied, numerical tests indicate that this formulation is sub-optimal by one order of accuracy, because the stress can only be extrapolated with Taylor expansions of order
k-1
, since it contains the gradients of
\boldsymbol{u}
.
We recall that a high-order body-fitted finite element method requires the fitting of the mesh to the geometry at the same order of accuracy of the polynomial interpolation space utilized.
This represents a strong practical limitation of high-order discretizations, and the tradeoff offered by the SBM with Neumann boundary conditions is that no body-fitted meshing is required, at the price of the loss of one order of accuracy with respect to optimal rates.

Obviously, if only Dirichlet boundary conditions are applied, the SBM is optimal, although this situation is less common in structural mechanics applications, and more typical in fluid mechanics applications.

6
Summary

We have proposed a penalty-free version of the SBM, which avoids the tedious selection/estimation of a penalty parameter.
The analysis of stability and convergence demonstrates that the method is stable and convergent for any order of accuracy, and these theoretical results are confirmed by a series of numerical experiments. In the course of the numerical analysis, we also discovered an important interpretation of the SBM as a Galerkin consistent method, a new and somewhat unexpected result on the SBM.

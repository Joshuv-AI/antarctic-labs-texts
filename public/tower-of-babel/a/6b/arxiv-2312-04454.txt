Notation and prerequisites

We will use the asymptotic notation
f=O(g)
or
f\ll g
if there is a constant
C

|f(n)|\leqslant Cg(n)
for all

for all

, and we write

.

[

.
Let

The unimodular roots of a reciprocal polynomial

(7)

\displaystyle P(e^{i\theta})e^{-i\theta N/2}

\displaystyle=a_{N/2}+2\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}a_{N/2+n}\cos n\theta\;

for

\displaystyle P(e^{2i\theta})e^{-iN\theta}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}a_{(N-1)/2+n}\cos((2n-1)\theta)

\displaystyle\text{for $N$ odd}.

The polynomials

with

are reciprocal Littlewood polynomials by assumption so we can write

P_{N}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a^{(N)}_{n}z^{n}

a corresponding polynomial

\displaystyle Q_{N/2}(z)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=0}^{N/2}a^{(N)}_{N/2+n}z^{n}\;

if

(10)

\displaystyle Q_{N}(z)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}a^{(N)}_{(N-1)/2+n}z^{2n-1}

Note that

is itself a Littlewood polynomial when

for even degrees

as from (

(11)

\displaystyle P_{N}(e^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta N/2}\Re\left(2Q_{N/2}(e^{i\theta})-a_{N/2}\right).

The situation for

\displaystyle P_{N}(e^{2i\theta})

A structural result

As a first step, we prove the following lemma which shows that a sequence of polynomials with a bounded number of roots contains a subsequence of polynomials that all have the same structure.

Lemma 1
.

Let
d\in\mathbf{N}
and let

be finite. Then there exists a constant

The following lemma will be useful at several stages in our argument. It allows us to show that a sequence of trigonometric functions with a certain common structure contains a subsequence of functions that all behave, on an appropriate scale, like the same fixed function.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbf{N}
be an infinite set, let
R\subset\mathbf{C}
be finite, let
0\leqslant\rho_{0}<\dots<\rho_{l}\leqslant 1
and let
l,D\in\mathbf{N}
. Let
\{H_{N}(\theta):[0,1]\to\mathbf{C}\}_{N\in\mathcal{N}}
be a set of functions. Suppose that for each
N\in\mathcal{N}
there are
l+1
trigonometric polynomials
B^{(N)}_{j}(\theta)
with coefficients in
R
, with at most
D
non-zero coefficients, and of degree
\deg B^{(N)}_{j}=o(N)
so that for each
N\in\mathcal{N}
we can write

(19)

H_{N}(\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}B^{(N)}_{j}(\theta)e(r^{(N)}_{j}\theta),

where the
r^{(N)}_{j}
are real numbers satisfying
r^{(N)}_{j}=\rho_{j}N+o(N)
. If
(\gamma_{N})_{N\in\mathcal{N}}
is a sequence of points
\gamma_{N}\in[0,1]
, then there exists an infinite subset
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{N}
and constants
b_{j}\in\mathbf{C}
such that the following holds. Let
C>0
, then for all
N\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and
u\in(-C,C)
we have

(20)

H_{N}\left(\gamma_{N}+\frac{u}{N}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}b_{j}e(\rho_{j}u)+o_{C;N\to\infty}(1).

Moreover, for each
j
we have

(21)

\lim_{N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N})e(r^{(N)}_{j}\gamma_{N})=b_{j}.

Remark
.

Here, we use the notation
o_{C;N\to\infty}(1)
for a function
f(N,u)
that satisfies
\sup_{u\in(-C,C)}|f(N,u)|\to 0
as
N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This follows from the following elementary compactness argument. Note that each trigonometric polynomial
B^{(N)}_{j}
has at most
D
non-zero terms and coefficients in a finite set
R
so that the vectors
(B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N})e(r^{(N)}_{j}\gamma_{N}))_{j=0}^{l}
with
N\in\mathcal{N}
form a bounded sequence in
\mathbf{C}^{l+1}
. By compactness, there exists an infinite set
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{N}
and constants
b_{j}\in\mathbf{C}
such that
B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N})e(r^{(N)}_{j}\gamma_{N})\to b_{j}
as
N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Let
C>0
be a fixed constant. Since each
B^{(N)}_{j}
has degree
o(N)
and at most
D
terms with coefficients in
R
, we can use the basic bound
|e(\alpha)-e(\beta)|\ll|\alpha-\beta|
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{R}
to deduce that for all
u\in(-C,C)
:

\displaystyle\left|B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N}+u/N)e(r^{(N)}_{j}\gamma_{N})-B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N})e(r^{(N)}_{j}\gamma_{N})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N}+u/N)-B^{(N)}_{j}(\gamma_{N})\right|\ll_{R,D,C}\deg B^{(N)}_{j}/N=o_{C;N\to\infty}(1)

and hence that
H_{N}(\gamma_{N}+u/N)=\sum_{j}b_{j}e(ur^{(N)}_{j}/N)+o_{C}(1)
uniformly for all
u\in(-C,C)
. Using now that
|e(ur^{(N)}_{j}/N)-e(u\rho_{j})|\ll_{C}o(1)
when
|u|<C
by the assumption that
r^{(N)}_{j}=\rho_{j}N+o(N)
, it follows immediately that
H_{N}(\gamma_{N}+u/N)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}b_{j}e(\rho_{j}u)+o_{C;N\to\infty}(1)
.
∎

We state one more lemma which provides us with a useful criterion for when a trigonometric function with real frequencies, like those appearing in the right hand side of (

Lemma 3
.

Let

be a function of the form

with coefficients

,
a_{j}\in\mathbf{C}
and
0<\rho_{1}<\dots<\rho_{l-1}<\rho_{l}=1
. Suppose that either
2|a_{l}|>|a_{0}|
, or else that
2|a_{l}|\geqslant|a_{0}|
and
a_{j}\neq 0
for some
j
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant l-1
. Then

has infinitely many sign changes on
\mathbf{R}

To prove this lemma, we use the following classical theorem of Fejér
[
9
]
and Riesz
[
15
]
, see also
[
17

for a more modern discussion.

Theorem 5

(Fejér-Riesz Theorem)
.

Let
w(t)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}c_{n}e^{itn}
be a real-valued trigonometric polynomial that is non-negative for all real
t
. Then
w
is expressible in the form

w(t)=|p(e^{it})|^{2}
for some polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}d_{n}z^{n}
with
d_{n}\in\mathbf{C}
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
w(t)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}c_{n}e^{itn}
be a real-valued trigonometric polynomial. Suppose that either
2|c_{N}|>|c_{0}|
, or else that
2|c_{N}|=|c_{0}|
and that
c_{n}\neq 0
for some
1\leqslant n\leqslant N-1
. Then
w
has a sign change in
[0,2\pi]
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, assuming that there is a choice of sign
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that function
\epsilon w(t)
is non-negative. By Fejér-Riesz, there then exist
d_{j}\in\mathbf{C}
such that

(23)

\epsilon c_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-n}d_{j}\overline{d_{n+j}}.

Hence,
|c_{N}|=|d_{0}d_{N}|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}|d_{j}|^{2}=\frac{|c_{0}|}{2}
so the inequality
|c_{N}|>\frac{|c_{0}|}{2}
is false and the assumptions of the lemma then imply that
c_{n}\neq 0
for some
n\in[N-1]
. Since
|c_{N}|\geqslant\frac{|c_{0}|}{2}
by assumption, this chain of inequalities also shows that
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|d_{j}|^{2}=0
. Hence, we deduce from (
23
) that
c_{n}=0
for all
1\leqslant n\leqslant N-1
, a contradiction.
∎

Problem 2

Proof of Proposition

Our task consists of finding a constant

As the sets

Throughout the proof, the limit

but we will not explicitly state this every time. We define the following truncated version of the functions in (
59

\displaystyle s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\Lambda_{1}^{(N)}\cap\Lambda^{(N)}}\delta_{m}\left(\sin mt-\sin((m-p_{m}^{(N)}D)t)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\Lambda_{2}^{(N)}\cap\Lambda^{(N)}}\delta_{m}\left(\sin mt-\sin((m-p_{m}^{(N)}D)t)\right)

Lemma 5

Let
s^{(N)}_{1},s^{(N)}_{2}

be their truncations that we defined in (

sufficiently small such that for all

\sup_{t\in I_{N}(c)}\left|s_{j}^{(N)}\left(t\right)\right|<\rho,

(69)

\displaystyle s_{j}^{(N)}\left(t\right)=s_{j,\Lambda}^{(N)}\left(t\right)+o_{c;N\to\infty}(1).

We use the bound

We prove this under the assumption that

\Im(C_{1}^{(N)}(t))>\kappa.

, where we remind the reader that the
n_{j}(N)
are integers such that the decomposition (
73
) of
s^{(N)}_{\Lambda}
holds. First, if
n_{1}(N)=O(p(N))
then we note that we could have simply defined
n_{1}(N)=0
and
C^{(N)}_{1}(t)=\sum_{m\in\Lambda^{(N,1)}}\delta_{m}\left(e^{imt}-e^{i(m-p^{(N)}_{m}D)t}\right)
in the decomposition (
73
) of
s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}
and we check that all of the desired properties in items (i),(ii) and (iii) of Lemma
6
still hold: for item (i) note that
\Lambda^{(N,1)}=\{m\in\Lambda:|m-n_{1}(N)|=O(p)\}=\{m\in\Lambda:|m|=O(p)\}
as we are assuming that
n_{1}(N)=O(p)
, item (ii) is clear as the function
C^{(N)}_{1}
above has degree
O(p)
since we just noted that
m=O(p)
for all
m\in\Lambda^{(N,1)}
, and item (iii) is trivial as could only have decreased
n_{1}(N)
. Hence, integrating the expression (
73
) over
J=J(N)
gives

\displaystyle\int_{J}s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\Im(C_{1}^{(N)}(t))\,dt+\sum_{j=2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\int_{J}\Im(C_{j}^{(N)}(t)e^{in_{j}(N)t})\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\Im(C_{1}^{(N)}(t))\,dt+O\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(n_{j}(N)+O(p))^{-1}\right)

where the bound in the second line follows from items (i) and (ii) in Lemma
6
which show that the frequency of any term in
C_{j}^{(N)}(t)e^{in_{j}(N)t}
is of size
n_{j}(N)+O(p)
. Recall now (
77
) which certainly implies that
p(N)=o(n_{j}(N))
for
j\geqslant 2
so that
n_{j}(N)^{-1}=o(p^{-1})
. Hence, as
J
was chosen so that
|J|\gg p^{-1}
and (
81
) holds, the integral above is
>\kappa|J|+o(p(N)^{-1})\gg\kappa|J|
as
N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
. This shows that for
N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
sufficiently large, there exists a
t\in J\subset(-\gamma_{r}-\frac{c}{p},\gamma_{r}+\frac{c}{p})
such that
s_{\Lambda}(t)^{(N)}\gg\kappa
which is the desired conclusion of Lemma
7
.

Finally, in the second case we may assume that
\frac{n_{1}(N)}{p(N)}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
. As we showed that there exists an interval
J
satisfying (
81
), there certainly exists a
\gamma\in J\subset(-\gamma_{r}-\frac{c}{p},\gamma_{r}+\frac{c}{p})
such that

(82)

|\Im(C_{1}^{(N)}(\gamma))|>\kappa.

Again recall that
p(N)=o(n_{j+1}(N)-n_{j}(N))
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant\lambda^{\prime}-1
by (
77
), and as we are assuming that
p(N)=o(n_{1}(N))
now, it is possible to define a variable
\omega=\omega(N)
such that
p(N)=o(\omega)
and

(83)

\displaystyle\omega(N)=o\big(\min\{n_{1}(N),n_{2}(N)-n_{1}(N),\dots,n_{\lambda}(N)-n_{\lambda-1}(N)\}\big).

As the trigonometric polynomials
C_{j}^{(N)}
consist of
O(1)
terms of degree
O(p(N))
by item (ii) in Lemma
6
, it follows that
\left|C_{j}^{(N)}(t)-C_{j}^{(N)}(\gamma)\right|\ll p(N)/\omega(N)=o_{N\to\infty}(1)
holds uniformly for all
t=\gamma+\frac{u}{\omega(N)}
where
u
ranges over
(-1,1)
. Hence, for
u\in(-1,1)
and
t=\gamma+\frac{u}{\omega(N)}
we can rewrite (
73
) as

(84)

s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)=\Im\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}}C_{j}^{(N)}(\gamma)e^{in_{j}t}\right)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

where
o_{N\to\infty}(1)
denotes a function that tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
uniformly for all
u\in(-1,1)
. Note now that the
C_{j}^{(N)}(\gamma)
are fixed coefficients independent of
t
. Hence, if we integrate this expression over the interval
I=I(N)\vcentcolon=(\gamma-\frac{1}{\omega(N)},\gamma+\frac{1}{\omega(N)})
, we get

(85)

\displaystyle\int_{I}s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=O\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}}n_{j}^{-1}\right)+o(|I|)=o(\omega^{-1})+o(|I|)=o(|I|)

as
N\to\infty
, and a similar calculation yields

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left(s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=|I|\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}}|C_{j}^{(N)}(\gamma)|^{2}\right)+O(\max_{j>j^{\prime}}(n_{j}-n_{j^{\prime}})^{-1})+o(|I|)

(86)

\displaystyle\gg|I|

as
N\to\infty
, where we used that
|I|=2\omega^{-1}
so that
\max_{j>j^{\prime}}(n_{j}-n_{j^{\prime}})^{-1})=o(\omega^{-1})=o(|I|)
by the definition (
83
) of
\omega
, and for the final inequality that
|\Im(C_{1}^{(N)}(\gamma))|>\kappa
as we chose
\gamma
to satisfy (
82
). The following straightforward second moment then shows that there exists a
\kappa^{\prime}\gg 1
, independent of
N
, such that
s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}
takes both the values
\pm\kappa^{\prime}
in
I(N)\subset(\gamma_{r}-\frac{c}{p(N)},\gamma_{r}+\frac{c}{p(N)})
when
N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
is sufficiently large, which implies the desired result of Lemma
7
. Indeed, note that if such a
\kappa^{\prime}
did not exist, then we could find functions
g^{(N)}(t)
satisfying
\sup_{I}|g^{(N)}(t)|=o_{N\to\infty}(1)
such that
f^{(N)}(t)\vcentcolon=\epsilon s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)+g^{(N)}(t)
is non-negative for all
t\in I
, for some
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then, combined with the fact that
\sup_{I}|g^{(N)}(t)|=o_{N\to\infty}(1)
by definition, (
85
) shows that
\int_{I}f^{(N)}=o(|I|)
while (
86
) gives
\int_{I}\left(f^{(N)}\right)^{2}\gg|I|
. However, note that the
f^{(N)}
are uniformly bounded on
I
for
N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
because the
g^{(N)}
are uniformly bounded by definition and because
s_{\Lambda}^{(N)}
is a trigonometric polynomial with
\lambda^{\prime}=O(1)
terms by (
84
) and all these terms are
O(1)
by item (ii) in Lemma
6
. As
f^{(N)}
is also non-negative by definition, we deduce that
\int_{I}\left(f^{(N)}\right)^{2}\ll\int_{I}f^{(N)}
as
N\to\infty,N\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
. This contradicts our estimates above and therefore finishes the proof of Lemma
7
;

∎

This completes the proof of Proposition
2
.

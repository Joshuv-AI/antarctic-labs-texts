Constructions of superabundant tropical curves in higher genus
Sae Koyama

Abstract
. We construct qualitatively new examples of superabundant tropical curves which are non-realizable in genus 3 3 and 4 4 . These curves are in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} and ℝ 4 {\mathbb{R}}^{4} respectively, and have properties resembling canonical embeddings of genus 3 3 and 4 4 algebraic curves. In particular, the genus 3 3 example is a degree 4 4 planar tropical curve, and the genus 4 4 example is contained in the product of a tropical line and a tropical conic. They have excess dimension of deformation space equal to 1 1 . Non-realizability follows by combining this with a dimension calculation for the corresponding space of logarithmic curves.

Motivation and Prior Work

Tropical realizability is a fundamental question when using tropical techniques to study algebraic objects. Mikhalkin first studied realizability in the case of smooth (in particular, non-superabundant), nodal, planar curves and used this to obtain results in curve counting
[
13
]
. Tropical analogues of divisors have led to a new proof of the Brill–Noether theorem by showing that certain algebraic objects cannot exist because of the non-existence of the corresponding tropical object
[
8
]
. In the other direction, realizability results can be used to transfer tropical constructions to algebraic ones and this approach has led to new results
[
10
]
. Another variant of the realizability problem is that of tropical double ramification cycles
[
22
]
.

One approach to the realizability problem is logarithmic deformation theory, which was laid out by
Nishinou–Siebert
[
16
]
. The realizability results of Cheung–Fantini–Park–Ulirsch expanded on this setup
[
7
]
. In genus
0
, all tropical curves are non-superabundant, hence realizable. In genus
1
, much work has been done to create a full picture, including other approaches such as non-archimedean geometry
[
5
]
. The superabundant curves are planar superabundant, and realizable if and only if they satisfy the
well-spacedness
condition
[
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

Extensions of these techniques to some higher genus cases have been studied
[
15
]
. A promising direction is via Hurwitz covers
[
4
,
6
]
, which reduces certain cases to tropical modifications of realizable ones.
Superabundancy phenomena propagate to higher genera so lower genus cases can give families of realizable or non-realizable curves. However, our results suggest that there are new types of superabundacy in every genus. For higher genera, even the combinatorial conditions for superabundancy become more complicated and are not very well understood.

We speculate that the “canonical” superabundant curves identified up to genus
4
will have realizability conditions analagous to the well-spacedness condition. In particular, the excess dimension of the deformation space is
1
, so we expect these to have a single condition on edge lengths determining realizability. There may be an interesting connection with the realizability of tropical
canonical
divisors which have been studied by Möeller–Ulirsch–Werner
[
14
]
.

Definition 1.1.1

An

The curve

\Gamma^{\text{met}}=(G^{\text{met}}\amalg\coprod_{i\in\{1,\ldots n\}}{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})/\sim

0

\Gamma^{\text{met}}

As a metric space, the tropical curve

does not remember the underlying graph structure, in particular the existence of valence two vertices. However, every point on

2

The identification of the edges of
\Gamma

on
\Gamma
as continuous maps
\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
which restrict to affine linear functions on the edges.

Definition 1.1.2
.

A

(\Gamma,\varphi)
where

is an abstract tropical curve, and

Note that the balancing conditions implies that in the neighbourhood of a
2

Sometimes one imposes that each edge

Let

(i)

The finite graph (with one-valence vertices removed)

(ii)

For each directed edge of

1.2.
The abundancy matrix

Given a combinatorial type
\Theta

{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
acts on the set of all parametrised tropical curves with this combinatorial type by translation. The quotient set is a rational polyhedral cone
\sigma_{\Theta}

[
7

Definition 1.2.1
.

[
7

The
abundancy map
associated to a tropical curve

\Phi_{\Gamma}:{\mathbb{R}}^{E}\to\operatorname{Hom}(H_{1}(\Gamma),{\mathbb{R}}^{r})

sending a tuple of real numbers associated to edges to their weighted displacement in

given by the formula:

(\ell_{e})\mapsto\left(\sum_{e\in E(G)}a_{e}[e]\mapsto\sum_{e\in E(G)}\ell_{e}a_{e}\vec{e}\right)

where

is the direction vector of the edge

The abundancy map captures a failure of cycles to close up when arbitrary edge lengths are chosen. If the abundancy map is surjective, then

is non-superabundant, and the dimension of the moduli space can be calculated as the dimension of the kernel of this map

11

13
]

Proposition 1.2.2
.

Fix a trivalent combinatorial type

\dim\sigma_{\Theta}\geq n+(r-3)(1-g)-r

Proof.

Suppose
\Gamma
has
b

e_{1},\ldots,e_{b}
. The map

is a vector valued linear function on each edge, and fixing

\varphi(x)=\mathbf{w_{e}}x+\mathbf{c}

on

\eta_{g,i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $e_{i}$ in cycle $g$ and direction preserved}\\
-1&\text{ if $e_{i}$ in cycle $g$ and direction reversed}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

We define the
abundancy matrix
as the following block matrix:

K=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1,1}\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}&\ldots&\eta_{1,b}\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{g,1}\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}&\ldots&\eta_{g,b}\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}}\end{pmatrix}

We wish to determine whether a given point

lies is an assignment of edge lengths for which the continuous map

\mathbf{k}_{j}

\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{l}
must vanish. Since we started with a basis of simple cycles, this is a necessary and sufficient condition.

There are

∎

If

Superabundancy is determined only by information on the edges which form cycles. To simplify the picture, it will be useful to pass to subgraphs.

Suppose

Definition 1.2.4

A subcurve of a tropical curve in

The superabundancy condition can be seen geometrically by considering a simplification procedure of the underlying graph

Edges that are not in any cycle of the basis cycles correspond to columns of the matrix

(1)

K=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1,1}^{\prime}A_{1}&\ldots&\eta_{1,t}^{\prime}A_{t}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{g,1}^{\prime}A_{1}&\ldots&\eta_{g,t}^{\prime}A_{t}\end{pmatrix}

The columns of

1.3.
Rational Transformations

The group
GL_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
naturally acts by post-composition on the set of parameterized tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
. More generally, we consider
rational
transformations. Given a matrix
Q
in
GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
and a tropical curve
(\Gamma,\varphi)
, the composite map

\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\xrightarrow{Q}{\mathbb{R}}^{r}

need not be a parameterized tropical curve, since it may not have integral slopes. However, by changing the integral structure on
\Gamma
, it can be made into one.

Given
\Gamma
and an edge
e
in it, a
rational dilation of
e
changes the metric on
e
by multiplying the length by a positive rational number. A rational dilation of a leg of
\Gamma
is defined analogously. A
rational dilation of
\Gamma
is a sequence of rational dilations of edges and legs. It produces a homeomorphic graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, with a different integral/metric structure.

Proposition 1.3.1
.

Consider the linear transformation
T:{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
, given by
\mathbf{x}\mapsto Q\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}
where
Q\in M_{s,r}({\mathbb{Q}}),\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
. For a parameterized tropical curve
(\Gamma,\varphi)
, let
\varphi^{*}=T\circ\varphi
. Then there exists a parametrized tropical curve
(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a rational dilation of
\Gamma
and
\varphi^{\prime}
is obtained by composing with the resulting homeomorphism. Moreover, if
Q\in GL_{r}({\mathbb{Q}})
, the deformation space of
(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
coincides with that of
(\Gamma,\varphi)
as a real cone, i.e. ignoring integral structures.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is continuous and linear on the edges of
\Gamma
, and
T
is linear (hence continuous), the map
\varphi^{*}
is also a continuous, piecewise linear function.
On edge
e
, the map
\varphi^{*}
has the form
Q\mathbf{w}x+Q\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{b}
. For this to be a tropical parametrization, we require
Q\mathbf{w}
to have integer elements. Let
m
be the lowest common multiple of the denominators of the entries of
Q\mathbf{w}
. We can multiply
Q\mathbf{w}
by
m
and divide the lengths of the edges of
\Gamma
by
m
to preserve the image. Let the resulting piecewise linear function and abstract tropical curve be
\varphi^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

It now suffices to check the balancing condition. Consider a vertex
v
with edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
adjacent to it, with outgoing slopes
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{t}
. The new slopes are
mQ\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,mQ\mathbf{w}_{t}
. Have

mQ\mathbf{w}_{1}+\ldots+mQ\mathbf{w}_{t}=mQ(\mathbf{w}_{1}+\ldots+\mathbf{w}_{t})=0

since
\mathbf{w}_{1}+\ldots+\mathbf{w}_{t}=0
, as required.

Note that
\Gamma^{\prime}
has the same graph structure as
\Gamma
. Suppose
\ell
lies in the deformation space for
(\Gamma,\varphi)
. We obtain the superabundancy matrix for
(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
by multiplying all slopes by
mQ
:

K=\begin{pmatrix}m\eta_{1,1}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}&\ldots&m\eta_{1,b}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
m\eta_{g,1}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}&\ldots&m\eta_{g,b}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}}\end{pmatrix}

By factorising, we see that

\ell_{1}m\eta_{i,1}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{b}m\eta_{i,b}Q\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}}=mQ(\ell_{1}\eta_{i,1}\mathbf{w}_{e_{1}}\ldots+\ell_{b}\eta_{i,b}\mathbf{w}_{e_{b}})=0

for
i=1,\ldots,g
, and so
\ell\in\mathcal{M}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}
, the kernel of the associated abundancy map.
Hence
\mathcal{M}_{(\Gamma,\varphi)}\subset\mathcal{M}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}
. If
Q\in GL_{r}({\mathbb{Q}})
then the process is reversible; the kernels of the two matrices coincide, so we ensure that the two deformation spaces coincide.
∎

To understand genus
g

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
where

Proposition 1.3.2
.

Given any superabundant genus
g
parametrised tropical curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for
d>g
, we can find a projection to
{\mathbb{R}}^{g}
whose image (perhaps after applying rational dilation) is superabundant.

Proof.

The idea is to “project onto the obstruction”. Suppose we have a parametrised tropical curve
(\Gamma,\varphi)
of genus
g
which is superabundant. Superabundancy is equivalent to the existence of
\lambda^{(1)}
, …,
\lambda^{(g)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
with segments
i=1,\ldots,t
contained in

\langle\sum_{i=1}^{g}\eta_{i,j}^{\prime}\lambda^{(i)}\rangle^{\perp}+\mathbf{c}

We can consider an orthogonal projection
\pi
on to the span of
\lambda^{(i)}
, which is a space with dimension at most
g
. The affine subspaces

\langle\sum_{i=1}^{g}\eta_{i,j}^{\prime}\lambda^{(i)}\rangle^{\perp}+\mathbf{c}

are mapped to

\langle\sum_{i=1}^{g}\eta_{i,j}^{\prime}\lambda^{(i)}\rangle^{\perp}+\pi(\mathbf{c})

So the resulting curves satisfy the conditions for superabundancy.
∎

1.4.
Defining new phenomena

We clarify what we mean by “new” types of superabundancy. There are two ways in which superabundant curves may easily be constructed from other superabundant curves. Firstly, if a genus
g
curve contains a genus
g^{\prime}<g
subcurve which is superabundant, then it is also superabundant. Secondly, given a superabundant curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
with segments contained in affine hyperplanes with normals
\lambda^{(i)}
as in the discussion above, we can consider the product
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
, and extend the affine hyperplanes by taking the preimage of the projection. Any curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r+s}
with segments contained in these extended affine hyperplanes are also superabundant. In this case, we have a projection onto a superabundant curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
.

Definition 1.4.1
.

A superabundant tropical curve
(\Gamma,\varphi)
has
irreducible superabundacy
if it does not contain a subcurve of lower genus that is superabundant. Otherwise, say
(\Gamma,\varphi)
has
reducible superabundancy
.

Definition 1.4.2
.

A superabundant tropical curve
(\Gamma,\varphi)
for
\varphi:\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
has
decomposable superabundancy
if there exists a linear map
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r^{\prime}}
with
r^{\prime}<r
, such that a rational dilation of
(\Gamma,A\circ\varphi)
is superabundant. Otherwise, say it has
indecomposable superabundancy
.

Superabundancy is determined by the basis of cycles, and so a parametrized tropical curve has irreducible or indecomposable superabundancy if and only if its core neighbourhood does.

Remark 1.4.3
.

These two notions are not equivalent, nor does one imply the other. For example, we can consider a modification of the triple tuning fork in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(Figure
1
), which is a triple tuning fork in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, were we have “pulled apart” each fork (see Figure

). No cycle is contained in a plane so it has irreducible superabundancy, but the projection to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is the triple tuning fork in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, so is still superabundant. Thus it does not have indecomposable superabundancy.

Figure 4.

has irreducible superabundancy but not indecomposable superabundancy.

Conversely, if a tropical curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
contains a triple tuning fork in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and a full-rank cycle (see Figure

However, we can obtain the notion of a “minimal” superabundant curve.

Proposition 1.4.4
.

If
\varphi:\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
has irreducible superabundancy, then any projection that is superabundant also has irreducible superabundancy.

Proof.

Suppose that for a projection
\pi
, the resulting curve
\varphi^{\prime}:\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r^{\prime}}
is superabundant of reducible type. Then there is a subcurve of strictly lower genus whose segments are contained in affine hyperplanes with configurations giving rise to the superabundancy, say with normals
\lambda^{(i)}

\varphi:\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
must be contained in affine hyperplanes with normals
\tilde{\lambda}^{(i)}

\tilde{\lambda}^{(i)}
are obtained from
\lambda^{(i)}
under the inclusion
{\mathbb{R}}^{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
into the first
r^{\prime}
coordinates. But the
\tilde{\lambda}^{(i)}
satisfy the same relations that the
\lambda^{(i)}
do, and hence give rise to superabundancy in a subcurve with strictly lower genus.
∎

4.

4.1.
Moduli spaces with larger than expected dimension

Superabundancy is not sufficient for non-realizability, even generically for curves of the given combinatorial type, since the moduli space of the corresponding algebraic curves may also have dimension larger than expected.

For example, the triple tuning fork
\varphi_{2}:\Gamma_{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is superabundant with
\dim\operatorname{Def}(\Gamma_{2})=3

2
curves with
3
markings mapping to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, such that the markings map to the different coordinate lines, and the tangency along each coordinate line is
2
. Since
C
is smooth, such a map

corresponds to a rational map from
C
to the dense open torus
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, defined away from the marked points. Equivalently, this is given by the two principal divisors
\operatorname{div}(x)
and
\operatorname{div}(y)
, where
x
and
y
are the two coordinates on the first standard affine patch of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Any such divisor pair gives
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
worth of rational maps. Let
{\cal M}^{\circ}
be the locus of maps
(C,p,q,r)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
with the tangency conditions above, and let
D_{2}(A)
be the set of

for which such a map exists. Then the natural map
{\cal M}^{\circ}\rightarrow D_{2}(A)
is a
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
-bundle.

Remark 4.1.1
.

For the deformation space of tropical curves, we took the quotient by the action given by translation, which corresponds algebraically to scaling the rational maps, i.e. taking the quotient corresponds to going from

to

{\cal M}_{g}(A)\rightarrow X=\bar{{\cal M}}_{g,n}
. Its image, say
Y
, is closed in
X
, and the fibers over the image are
r
dimensional (the group
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
acts on them by translation). So by Lemma
4.2.2
,

\dim Y=\dim{\cal M}_{g}(A)-r=(r+1)d+(r-3)(1-g)-r

Suppose we have a parametrized tropical curve
\varphi
of degree
d
(so
d
rays in each of the directions
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
and
-(e_{1}+\ldots+e_{r})
) which contains
\varphi_{3}
(resp.
\varphi_{4}
) as a core neighbourhood and has degree at least 5 (resp. 7). Then
\varphi
is superabundant. For these values of
d
, we have that
\dim Y
is the expected dimension, which is now strictly less than the dimension of the deformation space of
\varphi
. By
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
we have

\dim\mathrm{trop}_{X}(Y)\leq\dim Y<\dim\mathsf{Def}(\varphi)

and so the realizable curves form a cone with dimension strictly less than that of the ambient space. Hence a generic deformation of
\varphi
is not realizable, as required.

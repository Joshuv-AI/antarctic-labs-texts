Abstract
. We construct a free group of continuum many generators among those autobijections of ℝ \mathbb{R} which are also Hamel bases of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , with identity function included. We also observe two new cases when a real function is a composition of two real functions which are Hamel bases of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} .

1.
Introduction

Hamel bases are very basic algebraic notion, firstly used to obtain non-continuous solutions to Cauchy’s functional equation. Our note concerns some special Hamel bases of the real plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}

[

, lattice structure of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}

[

and their relations to other families of real functions
[

.

For the present paper, article
[
3
]
is of particular importance. Its Authors constructed in
[
3

a bijection

which is a Hamel basis of the plane. The Authors also noted that
[

an inverse of a Hamel bijection is also a Hamel bijection and
[

each real function is a composition of three Hamel functions. Those results were the motivations for our research on behaviour of Hamel functions in the context of function composition. The aim of this note is to prove that there exists a free group of

many generators within the family of all Hamel autobijetions of
\mathbb{R}

2.
Preliminaries

For any set
X
by
|X|
we denote its cardinality. By Hamel basis we mean a set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is a basis of the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We say that
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Hamel function (
f\in\operatorname{HF}
in short) if
f
treated as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Hamel basis. Now we recall the definition of free group.

Let
(G,\circ)
be a group. For a given
g\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we will denote
g\circ\ldots\circ g
by
g^{n}
and the inverse element of
g
by
g^{-1}
. For any
z\in\mathbb{Z},z<0
we define
g^{z}\coloneqq(g^{-1})^{-z}
,
g^{0}=e
where
e
is the neutral element of group
(G,\circ)
. For any
z\in\mathbb{Z},g^{z}
will be called the
z
-th power of
g
. We will say that
G
is a
free group
if there exists a subset
S\subset G
such that for every
g\in G
there exists a unique (disregarding trivial variations such as
a\circ b=a\circ c\circ c^{-1}\circ b
or
a^{5}=a^{2}\circ a^{3}
) representation of
g
as a product of finitely many powers of elements of
S
. Then the set
S
will be called the
set of generators
, its elements - generators and the elements of
G
- words.

For sets
X,Y
,
Z\subset X
and
f\colon Z\to Y
we say that
f
is a partial function from
X
to
Y
and write
f:X\rightharpoonup Y
. In such a case we denote domain of
f
by
\operatorname{dom}(f)
and its range by
\operatorname{rng}(f)
.

We say that a function
f\colon X\rightharpoonup\mathbb{R}
, where
X\subset\mathbb{R}
, is a
partially linearly independent function
(
f\in\operatorname{PLIF}
in short) if it is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If moreover
X=\mathbb{R}
, then we simply say
f
is a
linearly independent function
(
f\in\operatorname{LIF}
in short). For
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
\operatorname{LIN}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
we will denote the linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
A
.

The following Proposition is very useful for working with Hamel functions.

Proposition 1
.

[
3
, Fact 2.2.]
Let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function,
f\in\operatorname{LIF}
. Then
f\in\operatorname{HF}
if and only if
\langle 0,x\rangle\in\operatorname{LIN}_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Main result

Our goal is to prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

There exists a family
\{f_{\beta}:\beta<\mathfrak{c}\}
of Hamel bijections such that they are free generators of a group with respect to the composition and all elements of this group but identity are also Hamel bijections.

Proof.

The general idea of the proof is to construct functions
f_{\beta}
, for
\beta<\mathfrak{c}
, inductively. Those functions will be free generators of the desired group. By
{{}_{\kappa}}f_{\beta}
we will denote the state of function

at
\kappa

We define

A\coloneqq\bigcup_{m\geq 1}(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})^{m}\times\mathfrak{c}^{m},

B\coloneqq\{(n_{0},\ldots,n_{m-1},\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{m-1})\in A:\exists i<m-1\ \ \gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i+1},\ \ m\geq 1\}

and enumerate

The set

is identified with all possible words written in a reduced form.

Let

be a well-ordering of

For

{}_{\kappa}f_{\gamma_{m_{\alpha}-1}^{\alpha}}^{n_{m_{\alpha}-1}^{\alpha}}\circ\ldots\circ_{\kappa}f_{\gamma_{0}^{\alpha}}^{n_{0}^{\alpha}}.

Similarly by

More precisely, for
\beta<\mathfrak{c}
we construct a sequence of partial functions
{}_{\kappa}f_{\beta}
,

such that the following conditions hold.

(I)

{}_{\kappa}f_{\beta_{n}}^{m_{n}}\circ\ldots\circ_{\kappa}f_{\beta_{1}}^{m_{1}}\in\operatorname{PLIF}
for all

(II)

{}_{\kappa}f_{\beta}
is one-to-one.

(III)

|\bigcup_{\beta<\mathfrak{c}}{}_{\kappa}f_{\beta}|\leq\omega+|\kappa|
.

(IV)

{}_{\kappa}f_{\beta}\subset\ _{\gamma}f_{\beta}

.

(V)

(VI)

(VII)

Then for

.
In condition (I) we consider the composition just at those points at which it is already defined.

Note that once we are done, conditions (II), (VI) and (VII) guarantee that for every
\beta<\mathfrak{c}
,

is an autobijection of
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore every word constructed from the set

is an autobijection.

The condition (I) assures that every word is LIF. Using Proposition
1
and the condition (V) we get that every word is a Hamel function.

Observe now, that condition (I) also assures that the set
\{f_{\beta}:\beta<\mathfrak{c}\}
generates a free group.

Indeed, let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n},l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\beta_{1}\neq\ldots\neq\beta_{n},\gamma_{1}\neq\ldots\neq\gamma_{m}\in\mathfrak{c}
and suppose that

g\coloneqq f_{\beta_{n}}^{k_{n}}\circ\ldots\circ f_{\beta_{1}}^{k_{1}}=f_{\gamma_{m}}^{l_{m}}\circ\ldots\circ f_{\gamma_{1}}^{l_{1}}\eqqcolon h

but

(1)

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\neq(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m})

i.e., the representation of the word

is not unique.

Since

we get that
g\circ h^{-1}=\id_{\mathbb{R}}
. However (
1
) implies that word

written in reduced form remains nontrivial. Thus there exists

,

such that

, which contradicts

, as
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}\notin\operatorname{LIF}

As a consequence we get that for

and thus the cardinality of

.

To sum up, we showed that

many generators.

[
3
, Problem 2.1]
. As stated in
[
3
, Corollary 2.3]
, each real function is a composition of three Hamel functions, and it is an open problem if there is a real function for which two Hamel functions won’t be enough. By
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
every
\operatorname{LIF}
function is a composition of two Hamel functions. We will observe that similar result holds under different assumptions.

Corollary 1
.

If
g
is a constant function, then
g
is a composition of two Hamel functions.

Proof.

Let
g
be a constant function
g\equiv c
. By
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
, there is a Hamel function
f
whose range is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then let us set
h_{0}:\operatorname{rng}(f)\to\mathbb{R}
to be constantly equal to
c
, and then let
h
be a Hamel function extending the
h_{0}
obtained by
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
. Note that
g=h\circ f
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

If
g:\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
and there exists a Hamel basis
H
such that
g|_{H\cup\{0\}}
is constant then
g
is a composition of two Hamel functions.

Proof.

Let
g:\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
and
H
be a Hamel basis. Assume that
g|_{H\cup\{0\}}
is constant. Let
H^{\prime}
be any Hamel basis. Let
f|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus H}
be a bijection onto
H^{\prime}
and
f|_{H}\equiv f(0)
. By the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
,
f
is a Hamel function. Clearly,
\operatorname{rng}(f)=H^{\prime}
.

Now, let
h_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
h_{0}=g\circ f^{-1}
. There is one point in
H^{\prime}
, namely
f(0)
, on which
f^{-1}
is multi-valued. Nevertheless, the composition still makes sense as
g
is constant on the respective set. By
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
,
h_{0}
can be extended to a Hamel function
h:\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
g=h\circ f
.
∎

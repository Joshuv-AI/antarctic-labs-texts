On the connectivity of the disjointness graph of segments of point sets in general position in the plane
J. Leaños, Christophe Ndjatchi, L. M. Ríos-Castro

Abstract
Let P P be a set of n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 points in general position in the plane. The edge disjointness graph D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) of P P is the graph whose vertices are all the closed straight line segments with endpoints in P P , two of which are adjacent in D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) if and only if they are disjoint. We show that the connectivity of D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) is at least ( ⌊ n − 2 2 ⌋ 2 ) + ( ⌈ n − 2 2 ⌉ 2 ) \binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2} , and that this bound is tight for each n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 .

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
P
is a set of
n\geq 3
points in general position in the plane, i.e., no three points in
P
are collinear. The
edge disjointness graph

D(P)
of
P
is the graph whose vertices correspond to the
closed straight line segments with endpoints in
P
and in which two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding segments are disjoint. Figure
1
depicts a point set
P
,
\mathcal{P}
and
D(P)
.

The edge disjointness graph and other similar graphs were introduced by Araujo, Dumitrescu, Hurtado, Noy and Urrutia in
Araujo et al. 2005
, as geometric analogs of the
Kneser graphs. We recall that if
m
and
k
are positive integers with
k\leq m/2
, then the
Kneser graph

KG(m;k)
is the graph whose vertices are all the
k
–subsets of

\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
and in which two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding
k
-subsets are disjoint. Kneser conjectured
Kneser 1956
in 1956 that the chromatic number
\chi(KG(m;k))
of
KG(m;k)

is equal to
m-2k+2
. This conjecture was proved by Lovász
Lovász 1978
in 1978 using topological methods, and (independently) by Bárány
Bárány 1978
in the same year. For more about the
study of several other aspects and combinatorial properties of Kneser graphs, see for instance
Albertson and Boutin 2007
;
Matousěk 2003
;
Chen 2000
;
Ekinci and Gauci 2019
;
Valencia-Pabon and Vera 2005
.

The study of the graph invariants of the edge disjointness graph
D(P)
began in
Araujo et al. 2005
with the estimation of a general lower bound for the chromatic number
\chi(D(P))
of
D(P)
.
Up to now the problem of determining the exact value of
\chi(D(P))
remains open in general. As far as we know, the exact
value of
\chi(D(P))
is known only for two particular cases: when
P

is in convex position
Fabila-Monroy and Wood 2011
;
Jonsson 2011
, and when
P
is the double chain
Fabila-Monroy et al. 2020
. In 2017 Pach, Tardos, and Tóth
Pach et al. 2017

studied the chromatic number and the clique number of
D(P)
in the more general setting of
\reals^{d}
for
d\geq 2
, i.e., when
P
is a subset of
\reals^{d}
.
More precisely, in
Pach et al. 2017
it was shown that the chromatic number of
D(P)
is bounded by above by a polynomial function that depends on its clique number
\omega(D(P))
,
and that the problem of determining any of
\chi(D(P))
or
\omega(D(P))
is NP-hard. Two years later, Pach and Tomon
Pach and Tomon 2019

showed that if
G
is the disjointness graph of a set of grounded
x
-monotone curves in
\reals^{2}
and
\omega(G)=k
, then
\chi(G)\leq k+1
. We remark that the set of grounded
x
-monotone curves play the role of our closed straight line segments.

The basic notations that we will use in this work are the following. If
x
and
y
are distinct points of
P
, then we shall use
xy
to
denote the closed straight line segment whose endpoints are
x
and
y
. Similarly, we will use
{\mathcal{P}}
to denote the set of segments
\{xy~:~x,y\in P\mbox{ and }x\neq y\}
,
and we shall refer to the elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
as the
segments
of
{\mathcal{P}}
. Then,
{\mathcal{P}}
is the vertex set of
D(P)
. We often make no distinction between
an element of
{\mathcal{P}}
and its corresponding vertex in
D(P)
. We also note that
{\mathcal{P}}
naturally defines a rectilinear drawing of
K_{n}
in the plane.
Let
x_{1}y_{1}
and
x_{2}y_{2}
be two distinct elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
, and suppose that
x_{1}y_{1}\cap x_{2}y_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Then
x_{1}y_{1}\cap x_{2}y_{2}

consists precisely of one point
o\in\reals^{2}
, because
P
is in general position. If
o
is an interior point
of both
x_{1}y_{1}
and
x_{2}y_{2}
, then we say that they
cross
at
o
.

We will denote by
CH(P)
the boundary of the convex hull of
P
, and by
\overline{P}
the set
P\cap CH(P)
, as depicted in Figure
1
.
In particular, note that if
P
is in convex position, then
P=\overline{P}
.

The set
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}\}
of points in general position on the left is
P
. We note that
\overline{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\}
. In the middle we have
{\mathcal{P}}
, which can be seen as the rectilinear drawing of
K_{5}
induced by

. Note that

is the convex quadrilateral formed by the union of the segments
p_{1}p_{2},\>p_{2}p_{3},\>p_{3}p_{4},
and

. The graph on the right is the edge disjointness graph

corresponding to

.

Let
H=(V(H),E(H))
be a (non-empty) simple connected graph. If
u
and
v
are distinct vertices of
H
,
then the distance between
u
and
v
in
H
will be denoted by
d_{H}(u,v)
, and we write
uv
to mean that
u
and
v
are adjacent in
H
.
We emphasize that this last notation is similar to that used to denote the straight line segment
xy
defined by the points
x,y\in\reals^{2}
.
However, none of these notations should be a source of confusion, because the former objects are vertices of a graph, and the
latter are points of the plane.

The
neighborhood
of
v
in
H
is the set
\{u\in V(H):uv\in E(H)\}
and is denoted by
N_{H}(v)
. The
degree

\deg_{H}(v)
of
v
is the number
|N_{H}(v)|
.
The number
\delta(H):=\min\{\deg_{H}(v):v\in V(H)\}
is the
minimum degree
of
H
, and
\Delta(H):=\max\{\deg_{H}(v):v\in V(H)\}
is its
maximum degree
.
A
u-v
path of

H
is a path of
H
having an endpoint in
u
and the other endpoint in
v
. Similarly, if
U\subset V(H)
, then

H\setminus U
is the subgraph of
H
that results by removing the vertices of
U
from
H
.

We recall that if
k
is a nonnegative integer, then
H
is
k
–connected
if
|V(H)|>k
and
H\setminus W
is connected for every set
W\subset V(H)
with
|W|<k
. The
connectivity

\kappa(H)
of
H
is the greatest integer
k
such that
H
is
k
-connected.
We follow the usual convention that
\kappa(H)=0
if and only if
H
is disconnected or
|V(H)|=1
.

Throughout this paper, if
m
is a nonnegative integer, then
[m]:=\emptyset
if
m=0
,
[m]:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
if
m>0
, and
by convention
\binom{m}{2}:=0
if
m<2
.

Our aim in this paper is to study the connectivity of
D(P)
. As far as we know, this parameter of
D(P)
has not been investigated previously, which is somehow surprising for us. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

If
P
is any set of
n\geq 3
points in general position in the plane, then

\kappa(D(P))\geq\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2}.

Our next statement is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
and the well-known fact that the connectivity of
a graph is bounded by above by its minimum degree.

Corollary 1
.

If
P
is any set of
n\geq 3
points in general position in the plane and
\delta(D(P))=\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2},

then

\kappa(D(P))=\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2}.

As we will see in Proposition
1
, the lower bound of
\kappa(D(P))
given in Theorem
1
is in fact a lower bound for the minimum degree of
D(P)
.
Moreover, in that proposition we also show that each point set in the collection
\{C_{n}\}_{n=3}^{\infty}
, where
C_{n}
denotes the set of
n
points in
general and convex position, satisfy the hypothesis of Corollary
1
, and hence
\kappa(D(C_{n}))
is equal to the lower bound in Theorem
1
for each
n\geq 3
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce additional terminology and give some auxiliary results which will be used in the proof Theorem
1
. Finally, in Section
3
we give the proof of our main result.

2
Preliminaries

For the rest of the paper,
P
is a fixed set of
n\geq 3
points in general position in the plane, and (for brevity)

\kappa(n):=\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2}.

Similarly, we will use
\eta(P;a,b)
to denote the maximum number of pairwise internally
disjoint
a-b
paths of
D(P)
.

Proposition 1
.

If
P
and
n
are as above, then
\delta(D(P))\geq\kappa(n)
and
\Delta(D(P))=\binom{n-2}{2}
.
If additionally
P
is in convex position, then
\delta(D(P))=\kappa(n)
.

Proof.

Let
f=uv
be a vertex of
D(P)
, and let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be the subsets of
P\setminus\{u,v\}
separated by the line spanned by
f
.
Let
n_{1}:=|P_{1}|
and
n_{2}:=|P_{2}|
. Then,
n_{1}+n_{2}=n-2
.
Since each segment of
{\mathcal{P}}
having both endpoints in
P_{i}
(
i=1,2
) is disjoint from
f
, then each of these segments is adjacent to
f
in
D(P)
. Hence
\deg(f)\geq\binom{n_{1}}{2}+\binom{n_{2}}{2}
.
On the other hand, it is well known that the sum
\binom{n_{1}}{2}+\binom{n_{2}}{2}
attains its minimum when

\{n_{1},n_{2}\}=\{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor,\>\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil\}
. From the last two assertions it follows that

\deg(f)\geq\kappa(n)
. Since
f
is an arbitrary vertex of
D(P)
, then

\delta(D(P))\geq\kappa(n)
.

It follows from
n\geq 3
and the fact that
P
is in general position that
CH(P)
is a polygon of at least three sides. Note that if
g=xy
is a segment (side) of
CH(P)
, then
g
is disjoint from
any segment joining two points of
P\setminus\{x,y\}
. This implies that
\deg(g)\geq\binom{n-2}{2}
, and so
\Delta(D(P))\geq\binom{n-2}{2}
. On the other hand, note that
for
f=uv
there are exactly
2(n-2)
segments of
{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{f\}
that share an endpoint with
f
, (namely, those incident with exactly one of
u
or
v
). Since
f
cannot be adjacent to any of these
segments, then
\deg(f)\leq\binom{n}{2}-1-2(n-2)=\binom{n-2}{2}
. Again, since
f
is an arbitrary vertex of
D(P)
, then
\Delta(D(P))\leq\binom{n-2}{2}
, as required.

Finally, suppose that
P
is in convex position. Let us label the points of
P
by
x_{1},\>x_{2},\>\ldots,\>x_{n}
in clockwise order. Let
h
be the segment of
{\mathcal{P}}
joining
x_{1}
with
x_{j}
, where
j:=\lfloor(n+2)/2\rfloor
.
Then the line spanned by
h
separates
S_{1}:=\{x_{2},\ldots,x_{j-1}\}
from
S_{2}:=\{x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Then
|S_{1}|=\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor
and
|S_{2}|=\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil
. Since
P
is in convex position, then any segment of
{\mathcal{P}}

with an endpoint in
S_{1}
and the other in
S_{2}
crosses
h
, and hence the only neighbours of
h
in
D(P)
are those segments that have both endpoints in exactly one of
S_{1}
or
S_{2}
. This implies that

\deg(h)\leq\kappa(n)
, showing the last assertion of Proposition
1
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
H
be a connected graph. Then
H
is
k
-connected if and only if
H
has

k
pairwise internally disjoint
a-b
paths, for any two vertices
a
and
b
of
H
such that
d_{H}(a,b)=2
.

Proof.

The forward implication follows directly from Menger’s Theorem. Conversely, let
U
be a vertex cut of
H
of minimum order.
Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two distinct components of
H\setminus U
, and let
u\in U
. Since
U
is a minimum cut, then
u
has at least a neighbor

v_{i}
in
H_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
d_{H}(v_{1},v_{2})=2
. By hypothesis,
H
has
k
pairwise internally disjoint
v_{1}-v_{2}
paths. Since each of these

k
paths intersects
U
, then we have that
|U|\geq k
, as required.
∎

.

Let

In view of Remark

and

are two fixed vertices of

such that

.
Then

and

are not adjacent in

, and hence

imply that

consists precisely of one point of

and

, or

and

.

or

. Thus, if

and

, then each endpoint of

and

and

, and each of

and

(respectively,

By translating

, if necessary, from now on we will assume that

. Let
\ell_{a}
and

be the straight lines spanned by

and

, respectively. Additionally, by rotating

around

consists of four open connected regions

and
R_{x^{+}}
, where

denotes the region containing the negative

-axis, and so on. For brevity, in the rest of the paper, we use
Y^{-}(P):=P\cap R_{y^{-}},\>Y^{+}(P):=P\cap R_{y^{+}},\>X^{-}(P):=P\cap R_{x^{-}}
, and

. Then,

without the endpoints of

and

is the disjoint union of

and
X^{+}(P)
. Let
r:=|Y^{-}(P)|,\>s:=|Y^{+}(P)|,\>p-1:=|X^{-}(P)|,
and

, as depicted in Figure

. If there is no danger of confusion, we often omit the argument

We recall that the set
{\mathcal{P}}
of segments with endpoints in

is the vertex set of

.
We now split the set of neighbours of
a
and
b
into three sets as follows.

\begin{split}{A}&:=\{e\in{\mathcal{P}}~|~e\cap a=\emptyset\mbox{ and }e\cap b\neq\emptyset\},\\

Clearly,
{A},\>{B},\>{M},
and

are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, note that
N_{D(P)}(a)={A}\cup{M}
and

.
Let
\delta_{2}:=|{M}|
and
\delta_{3}:=\min\{|{A}|,|{B}|\}
. Let us denote by
\delta(P;a,b)
the minimum of

and
|B\cup M|
.
Then,
\delta(P;a,b)=\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}
.

Let

be the subgraph of

induced by

. In particular, note that

N_{G}(a)=N_{D(P)}(a)
and

. We shall see later that the subgraph

contains (almost all) the
\kappa(n)

a-b
paths mentioned in Remark

. The following observation is easy to check.

Observation 1
.

Let

. Then

is an

path of

of length 3 if and only if

satisfies the following conditions:
(i)

, (ii)

, and (iii)

and

are disjoint.

The next proposition provides a useful collection
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
of pairwise internally disjoint

paths of

.

Proposition 3
.

Let
a,\>b,M,
and
G
be as above, and let
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}:=\{aeb~|~e\in{M}\}.
If
\eta_{2}(a,b)
denotes the maximum number of pairwise internally disjoint

paths of

of length

, then
\eta_{2}(a,b)=\delta_{2}=|{\mathbb{P}}_{0}|
.

Proof.

From the definitions of
\delta_{2}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
, it is clear that
\delta_{2}=|{\mathbb{P}}_{0}|
. Similarly, from the definition of
{M}

it follows that each element of
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
is an

path of

of length 2. Conversely, if

is an

path of

of length 2, then
the definition of

implies that the inner vertex of

must be a segment of

, and so
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
consists precisely of all the

of length 2.
Since the paths in

Then
\eta_{2}(a,b)=|{\mathbb{P}}_{0}|
, as claimed.
∎

3
The proof of Theorem
1

We start by noting that
\kappa(3)=0,\>\kappa(4)=0,
and
\kappa(5)=1
. On the other hand, it is straightforward to check that

D(P)
is a connected graph for all
n\geq 5
. From these facts and the definition of
\kappa(D(P))
it follows that Theorem
1

holds for each
n\in\{3,\>4,\>5\}
. Thus we may assume that
n\geq 6
and, according to Remark
1
, all we need to show is that

\eta(P;a,b)\geq\kappa(n)
.

Our proof of Theorem
1
is mostly constructive, and the main steps are the following. First we prove that if
a
and
b
cross each other and have their four leaves in
\overline{P}
(namely, Case 1), then there is a collection of pairwise internally disjoint

a-b
paths with cardinality
\eta(P;a,b)\geq\kappa(n)
. We remark that such a collection of paths will be constructed in several ways,
depending on the values of
p,\>q,\>r
, and
s
. Then, we observe that many of the paths constructed in Case 1 remain well defined and useful even if
a
and
b
do not satisfy the conditions of Case 1. Finally, in each case distinct from Case 1, we take the useful paths given in Case 1 and complete the required collection of
a-b
paths in certain way (which depends on the specific case).

Case 1.

Suppose that
a
and
b
cross at
o
and have their four leaves in
\overline{P}
.

By performing a suitable rotation of all the points of

around

, if necessary, we may assume that

|Y^{+}|\geq\max\{|Y^{-}|,\>|X^{-}|,\>|X^{+}|\}
. Additionally, by reflecting

along the

-axis, if necessary, we also can assume that
|X^{+}|\geq|X^{-}|
.

Our approach in this case is to give an explicit collection of pairwise internally disjoint

paths with cardinality

. Roughly speaking, we start by showing that
agb
defines an
a-b
path in
D(P)
of length two for each

. These will be the
a-b
paths provided by
M
. Then, in each arising subcase, we will construct (explicitly) a bijective function with domain
A
or almost all

and codomain

, such that if

and
h\in B
are matched by that bijection, then
adhb
defines an
a-b
path in
D(P)
of length
3
. As we will see, the
a-b
paths provided by that bijection together with those provided by
M
give all (or almost all) the required collection.

Remark 2
.

Hence

4
Concluding remarks

A trivial upper bound for the connectivity
\kappa(H)
of a graph
H
is its minimum degree
\delta(H)
. As we have observed in Proposition
1
, if
P
is a set of
n\geq 3
points in general position in the plane, then its disjointness graph of segments
D(P)
has minimum degree
\delta(D(P))\geq\kappa(n):=\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2}.

From Corollary
1
it follows that if
\delta(D(P))=\kappa(n)
, then
\kappa(D(P))=\delta(D(P))
, and hence Theorem
1
is best possible for such point sets. We remark that not only the points in convex position satisfy the hypothesis of Corollary
1
, but also any point set
P
containing two points in
\overline{P}
such that the line spanned by them separates
P
into two sets of sizes as equal as possible. We believe that it would be interesting to determine a the exact value of

\delta(D(P))-\kappa(D(P))
for the case in which
\delta(D(P))>\kappa(n)
.

Finally, we recall that the basic idea of the proof of Theorem
1
is to construct, for each pair of nonadjacent vertices
a
and
b
in

D(P)
, a collection with at least
\kappa(n)
pairwise internally disjoint
a-b
paths. Surprisingly, we were able to form all these collections with
paths of length at most
4
, which implies that the diameter of
D(P)
is between
2
and
4
whenever
n\geq 5
.

Commutativity and spectral properties for a general class of Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators
Ulrich Abel, Ana Maria Acu, Margareta Heilmann, Ioan Raşa

Abstract
In this paper we present commutativity results for a general class of Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer type operators and associated differential operators and investigate their eigenfunctions.

Introduction

For fixed
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we consider sequences of positive linear
operators
S_{n,j}
which can be viewed as a generalization of the
Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators
[
11
]
, Phillips operators

[
12
]
and corresponding Kantorovich modifications of higher order.

For
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, these operators possess the exceptional
property to preserve constants and the monomial
x^{j}
.

It turned out (see
[
1
]
), that an extension of this family covers certain well-known
operators studied before, so that the outcoming results could be unified.
Continuous functions of exponential growth on the positive half-axis are approximated
by the operators
S_{n,j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
in each point of
continuity (see
[
1
, Corollary 12]
.

In this paper we investigate commutativity properties of the operators and their
commutativity with certain differential operators as well as their spectral properties.

For
A\geq 0
, we denote by
E_{A}
the space of functions
f:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
f
locally integrable, satisfying the growth
condition
\left|f\left(t\right)\right|\leq Ke^{At}
,
t\geq 0

for some positive constant
K
. Furthermore, define
E:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{A\geq 0}E_{A}
.

Throughout the paper we use the convention that a sum is zero if the upper
limit is smaller than the lower one.

Definition 1

Let
f\in E_{A}
,
n>A
and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then the operators
S_{n,j}

are defined by

(S_{n,j}f)(x)=f(0)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}s_{n,k}(x)+\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x)n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k-j}(t)f(t)dt

where

s_{n,k}(x)=\dfrac{(nx)^{k}}{k!}e^{-nx},\,k\geq 0.

For the sake of simplicity we define
s_{n,k}(x)=0
, if
k<0
.

For
j\leq 0
we have

(S_{n,j}f)(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x)n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k-j}(t)f(t)dt.

For the special case
j=0
we get the Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators
first defined in
[
11
]
, for
j=1
the Phillips operators, also
called genuine Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators,
[
12
]
. For
j\leq-1
the operators coincide with certain auxiliary operators, see, e.g.,

[
8
, (1.4)]
for
c=0
, named
M_{n,r}
there,
r=-j
or
[
6
, (3.5)]
for
c=0
, named
M_{n,s}
there,
s=-j
. They can also be
considered as corresponding Kantorovich modifications of higher order.

In
[
1
, Lemma 2]
it was proved that
S_{n,j}f\in E_{2A}
for
n>2A
,
A\geq 0
,
f\in E_{A}
.

For
l=0,1,\ldots
we denote
e_{l}(t)=t^{l}
,
t\geq 0
.

All these operators preserve
e_{0}
and for
j\geq 1
also
e_{j}
since

\displaystyle(S_{n,j}e_{j})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x){n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k-j}(t)t^{j}dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}s_{n,k-j}(x)=x^{j}.

Furthermore, for
j\geq 1
it is easy to see that
S_{n,j}(f;0)=f(0)
.

Throughout the paper we use the following formulas

\displaystyle s_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n(s_{n,k-1}(x)-s_{n,k}(x)),

(1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,l}(t)t^{r}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(l+r)!}{l!}n^{-r-1},

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{m,r}(t)s_{n,l}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{r}n^{l}}{(m+n)^{r+l+1}}\frac{(r+l)!}{r!l!}.

(3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,0}(t)f^{\prime\prime}(t)dt=n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,0}(t)f^{\prime}(t)dt
.

Define

\mathcal{D}^{2l}_{j}:=e_{j}D^{l}(e_{l-j}D^{l}),\,l\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(9)

For
l=1
this is in agreement with (
7
). Moreover, define
D^{0}_{j}

to be the identity operator.

Proposition 9

For
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
the differential operator
\mathcal{D}^{2l}_{j}
can be written as
l
-th iterate of
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{j}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2l}=(\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2})^{l}.

(10)

Proof.
For
j\leq 0
see
[
7
, (13)]
. We prove the relation (
10
)
by induction on
l
.

For
l=1
it is sufficient to use (
7
).

Suppose (
10
) is true for
l
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2l}_{j}\circ\mathcal{D}^{2}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2l}_{j}[(1-j)D+e_{1}D^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}D^{l}\left\{e_{l-j}D^{l}[(1-j)D+e_{1}D^{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}D^{l}\left\{e_{l-j}[(l+1-j)D^{l+1}+e_{1}D^{l+2}]\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2l+2}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}D^{l+1}[(l+1-j)e_{l-j}D^{l+1}+e_{l+1-j}D^{l+2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}D^{l}\left\{e_{l-j}[(l+1-j)D^{l+1}+e_{1}D^{l+2}]\right\}.

Corollary 10

For
l\in\mathbb{N}
we have

{\mathcal{D}}^{2l}_{j}=({\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{j})^{l}=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\binom{l}{i}\left(l-j\right)^{\underline{l-i}}e_{i}D^{l+i}.

Proof.
According to (

), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2l}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}D^{l}(e_{l-j}D^{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}\binom{l}{i}\left(l-j\right)^{\underline{l-i}}e_{i}D^{l+i}.

Observe that
{\mathcal{D}}^{2l}_{j}=\sum_{i=j}^{l}\binom{l}{i}\left(l-j\right)^{\underline{l-i}}e_{i}D^{l+i}

4
Eigenstructure of
S_{n,j}

In this section we study the eigenstructure of the operators
S_{n,j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As we already know,
e_{0}
is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
1
for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Now we present a family of eigenfunctions
g_{j,p}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
, of
S_{n,j}
which are independent of
n
and can be expressed as
modified Bessel functions
I_{j}

of the first kind.

Theorem 11

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. For each
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the function

\displaystyle g_{j,p}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=\max{\{0,-j\}}}^{\infty}\frac{p^{m}x^{m+j}}{m!(m+j)!}

is an eigenfunction of
S_{n,j}
with eigenvalue
e^{p/n}
.

Proof.
For
p=0
and
j\geq 0
we get
g_{j,0}(x)=\frac{x^{j}}{j!}=\frac{1}{j!}S_{n,j}(e_{j};x)
by
the preservation of
e_{j}
.

Now let
p\not=0
.

By using (
2
) we get for
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle(S_{n,j}g_{j,p})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(x)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m}}{m!(m+j)!}\cdot\frac{(k+m)!}{(k-j)!}n^{-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-nx}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m}}{m!(m+j)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}x^{k+j}}{(k+j)!}\cdot\frac{(k+m+j)!}{k!}.

As

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}x^{k+j}}{(k+j)!}\cdot\frac{(k+m+j)!}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{k!}\left(x^{k+m+j}\right)^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}\left(x^{m+j}e^{nx}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\frac{(m+j)!}{(l+j)!}x^{l+j}n^{l}e^{nx},

we derive

\displaystyle(S_{n,j}g_{j,p})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{n^{l}x^{l+j}}{l!(l+j)!(m-l)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l+j}p^{l}}{l!(l+j)!}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m-l}}{(m-l)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{p}{n}}g_{j,p}(x).

For
-j\in\mathbb{N}
we get in a similar way

\displaystyle(S_{n,j}g_{j,p})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-nx}\sum_{m=-j}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m}n^{-j}x^{j}}{m!(m+j)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}x^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}\cdot\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}.

As

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}x^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}\cdot\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{k!}\left(x^{k+m}\right)^{(m+j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{m+j}}{dx^{m+j}}\left(x^{m}e^{nx}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m+j}\binom{m+j}{l}\frac{m!}{(l-j)!}x^{l-j}n^{l}e^{nx},

we conclude

\displaystyle S_{n,j}(g_{j,p};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-j}^{\infty}\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{m+j}\frac{n^{l-j}x^{l}}{l!(l-j)!(m+j-l)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l}p^{l-j}}{l!(l-j)!}\sum_{m=l-j}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{n}\right)^{m+j-l}}{(m+j-l)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{p}{n}}g_{j,p}(x).

Remark 12

For
p\not=0
the eigenfunctions can be written in terms of modified Bessel functions of the first kind,
i.e.,

g_{j,p}(x)=(px)^{\frac{j}{2}}I_{j}(2\sqrt{px}).

5
Eigenstructure of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}

The Bessel differential equation is given by

z^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+zy^{\prime}+\left(z^{2}-\nu^{2}\right)y=0.

(12)

Solutions are the Bessel functions of the first kind
J_{\nu}\left(z\right)
and of the second kind
Y_{\nu}\left(z\right)
(also called
Weber’s function). Each is a holomorphic function of
z
throughout the
z
-plane cut along the negative real axis.
J_{\nu}\left(z\right)
and
J_{-\nu}\left(z\right)
are linearly independent except when
\nu
is an
integer. In our case we consider the integer
\nu=j
. Therefore, we take as
a fundamental system the functions
J_{\nu}\left(z\right)
and
Y_{\nu}\left(z\right)
which are linearly independent for all values of
\nu
.
For integer
\nu
,
J_{\nu}
is an entire function with series expansion

J_{\nu}\left(z\right)=\left(-1\right)^{\nu}J_{-\nu}\left(z\right)=\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\right)^{k}}{k!\Gamma\left(\nu+k+1\right)}\text{ \qquad}\left(z\in C,\text{ }\nu=0,1,2,\ldots\right).

Y_{\nu}\left(z\right)
has a more complicated representation.

We show that the solutions of (
12
) are closely connected to
the eigenfunctions of the differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}=(1-j)D+e_{1}D^{2}
as defined in (
7
). Since the second
order linear ODE
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}y=0
has the both independent solutions

e_{0},e_{j}
, the kernel of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
is the span of
\left\{e_{0},e_{j}\right\}
. The case
p\neq 0
will be considered in the next
theorem.

Theorem 13

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\in\mathbb{R\smallsetminus}\left\{0\right\}
. If
y\left(x\right)
is a solution
of the Bessel differential equation

x^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+xy^{\prime}+\left(x^{2}-j^{2}\right)y=0

(13)

on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, then the function
h\left(x\right)=g\left(px\right)
, where

g\left(x\right)=x^{j/2}y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
to the eigenvalue
p
.

Remark 14

If we take
y\left(x\right)=J_{j}\left(x\right)

with
j,x\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle h\left(x\right)=\left(px\right)^{j/2}J_{j}\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(px\right)^{j/2}\left(i\sqrt{px}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(px\right)^{k}}{k!\Gamma\left(j+k+1\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{j}\left(px\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(px\right)^{k}}{k!\Gamma\left(j+k+1\right)}.

For
p\geq 0
, we essentially have the modified Bessel function
I_{j}
. To
avoid this, we can replace
p
with
-p
. For negative integers
j
we can
take advantage of the relation
J_{j}\left(x\right)=\left(-1\right)^{j}J_{-j}\left(x\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
13
.

Direct calculation yields

\displaystyle g^{\prime}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}x^{j/2-1}y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+ix^{\left(j-1\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right),

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\frac{j-2}{2}x^{j/2-2}y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+\frac{j}{2}x^{j/2-1}ix^{-1/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

\displaystyle+i\frac{j-1}{2}x^{\left(j-3\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+i^{2}x^{\left(j-2\right)/2}y^{\prime\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\frac{j-2}{2}x^{j/2-2}y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+i\frac{2j-1}{2}x^{\left(j-3\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)-x^{\left(j-2\right)/2}y^{\prime\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right).

y^{\prime\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)=-\frac{1}{2i\sqrt{x}}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)-\left(1+\frac{j^{2}}{4x}\right)y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

and it follows that

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{j^{2}-2j}{4}x^{j/2-2}y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+i\frac{2j-1}{2}x^{\left(j-3\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}x^{\left(j-3\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+\left(x^{j/2-1}+\frac{j^{2}}{4}x^{j/2-2}\right)y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x^{j/2-1}+\frac{j^{2}-j}{2}x^{j/2-2}\right)y\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right)+i\left(j-1\right)x^{\left(j-3\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{x}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle p^{-1}\left(p^{2}xg^{\prime\prime}\left(px\right)+\left(1-j\right)pg^{\prime}\left(px\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(px\right)^{j/2}+\frac{j^{2}-j}{2}\left(px\right)^{j/2-1}\right)y\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)+i\left(j-1\right)\left(px\right)^{\left(j-1\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{j\left(1-j\right)}{2}\left(px\right)^{j/2-1}y\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)+i\left(1-j\right)\left(px\right)^{\left(j-1\right)/2}y^{\prime}\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(px\right)^{j/2}+\frac{j^{2}-j}{2}\left(px\right)^{j/2-1}\right)y\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)+\frac{j\left(1-j\right)}{2}\left(px\right)^{j/2-1}y\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(px\right)^{j/2}y\left(2i\sqrt{px}\right)=g\left(px\right),

i.e., the function
h\left(x\right)=g\left(px\right)
is a solution of
the ODE

xh^{\prime\prime}+\left(1-j\right)h^{\prime}=ph.

The next theorem shows a connection between eigenfunctions of
S_{n,j}
and
eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
.

Theorem 15

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
. If a function
h\in C^{2}\left(0,\infty\right)
is an eigenfunction of
S_{n,j}
to the eigenvalue
e^{p/n}
for infinitely many integers
n
, then
h
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
to the eigenvalue
p
.

Proof.
Since
h\in C^{2}\left(0,\infty\right)
, we have for each
x>0
the Voronovskaja
relation

\left(S_{n,j}h\right)\left(x\right)=h\left(x\right)+n^{-1}\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}h\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(1/n\right)\text{
\qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

On the other hand, we have

\left(S_{n,j}h\right)\left(x\right)=e^{p/n}h\left(x\right)=\left(1+\frac{p}{n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)h\left(x\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Here
n\rightarrow\infty
means that we take only such
n
for which
S_{n,j}h=e^{p/n}h
. Comparing both relations we infer that

\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}h\right)\left(x\right)=ph\left(x\right)+o\left(1\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right),

which completes the proof.

The converse problem is more complicated. It is easy to show the following
result.

Theorem 16

If a function
h\in C^{2}\left(0,\infty\right)\cap E
is an eigenfunction
of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
to the eigenvalue
p
on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, it follows, for each
x>0
and for each constant
c>0
, the asymptotic
relation

\left(S_{n,j}h\right)\left(x\right)=e^{p/n}h\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-c}\right)\text{ \hskip 20.44434pt}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Proof.
The fact, that
h
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}
implies that

h
is infinitely often differentiable. Let
q
be a positive integer. By
[
1
, Theorem 25]

the operators
S_{n,j}
possess the asymptotic
expansion

\left(S_{n,j}h\right)\left(x\right)=h\left(x\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}h\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Observing that
{\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}h=\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}\right)^{k}h=p^{k}h
(see Proposition
9
) and

\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{p^{k}}{k!n^{k}}=e^{p/n}+O\left(n^{-q-1}\right)\text{
\qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right)

completes the proof.

Cohomology and Relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖸𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieYRep} pairs Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 17B38; Secondary 17B60,17A99
Jia Zhao, Yu Qiao*

Abstract
. A 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖸𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieYRep} pair consists of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and its representation. In this paper, we establish the cohomology theory of 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖸𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieYRep} pairs and characterize their linear deformations by the second cohomology group. Then we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖸𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieYRep} pairs, investigate their properties, and prove that a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure gives rise to a pair of compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators under a certain condition. Finally, we show the equivalence between r r -matrix-Nijenhuis structures and Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Outline of the paper.
After recalling some basic definitions in Section 2, we establish the cohomology theory of
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pairs and utilize it to characterize linear deformations in Section 3. The corresponding complex is exactly a subcomplex of that of the associated semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra with coefficients in the adjoint representation. Then we investigate the linear deformations of
\mathsf{LieYRep}

(N,S)
, where
N\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})

S\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)

(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]{{}_{\ltimes}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes})

(\mathfrak{g},\rho,\mu)
.

In Section 3, the notion of relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pairs is introduced and some properties are investigated. Given linear maps
N\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
S\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, and let

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Then there exists a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
on
V
, which is called the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and is denoted by
V^{T}
. Consequently, we obtain the deformed brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{S}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{S})
; meanwhile, we introduce linear maps
\hat{\rho}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and
\hat{\mu}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
associated with linear maps
N
and
S
to obtain the brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{\hat{\rho}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{\hat{\mu}})
.
It is
not
true that
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{S}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{S})
or
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{\hat{\rho}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{\hat{\mu}})
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
V
. In order to tackle this problem, we introduce the main object of this paper:
relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures
. We find that deformed brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{S}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{S})
and
([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{\hat{\rho}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{\hat{\mu}})
are equal under the condition that the triple
(T,S,N)
is a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure. Besides, the linear map
S
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})

([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{S}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{S})

([\cdot,\cdot]{{}^{T}_{\hat{\rho}}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T}_{\hat{\mu}})
are Lie-Yamaguti algebra structures on
V
. Furthermore, we show that the composition
N\circ T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)

\textstyle{(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{S}^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{S}

\scriptstyle{T}

where each arrows is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism, i.e., each

Unlike those on Lie algebras or associative algebras, a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}

This phenomenon is similar to the context of

[

In fact, an
r

2.
Preliminaries

All vector spaces in the article are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite-dimensional.
In this section, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theory.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
17
]
)
A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a trilinear operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, such that the following conditions hold

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket=0,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w]+[z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket,

for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
. We denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Note that a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with
[x,y]=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie triple system, while a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with
\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=0
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie algebra.
The following example is taken from
[
24
]
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold
1
1

1

a smooth compact manifold without boundary
with an affine connection, and denote by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, set

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T(x,y),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R(x,y)z,

where
T
and
R
are torsion tensor and curvature tensor respectively. It turns out that the triple

(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms an (infinite-dimensional) Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Definition 2.3
.

[
28
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A
homomorphism
from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
preserving the Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure, i.e., for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},
the following equalities hold

\displaystyle\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle~\phi(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

The notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was given in
[
32
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space
V
endowed with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and a bilinear map
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, which satisfies the following conditions for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

\displaystyle\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

\displaystyle\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],

where
D_{\rho,\mu}
is given by

(1)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is easy to see that
D_{\rho,\mu}
is skew-symmetric. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
(V;\rho,\mu)
. In the sequel, we write
D_{\rho,\mu}
as
D
for short without confusion.

The notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras is also a natural generalization of Lie algebras and Lie triple systems.

By a direct computation, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Then we have the following equalities

\displaystyle D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0;

\displaystyle D(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+D(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D(x,y),D(z,w)];

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)=\mu(x,w)\mu(z,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(z,x)-\mu(z,w)D(x,y),

for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We define linear maps
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{R}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
to be
x\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{x}
and
(x,y)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
respectively, where
\mathrm{ad}_{x}z=[x,z]
and
\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket
for all
z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
forms a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, where
\mathfrak{L}:=D_{\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R}}
is given by

\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{g}.

The representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is called the
adjoint representation
.

Representations of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra can be characterized by the semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
34
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
V
a vector space. Suppose that
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are linear maps. Then
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
if and only if there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes})
on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
which is defined to be

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u,

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+D(x,y)w+\mu(y,z)u-\mu(x,z)v,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in V
. This Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes})
is called the
semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra
, and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V
.

Cohomology theory on Lie-Yamaguti algebras was founded in
[
32
]
. Let
(V;\rho,\mu)
be a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, and denote the set of
p
-cochains by
C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)~(p\geqslant 1)

\displaystyle C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\triangleq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n},V)\times\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V),&\forall n\geqslant 1,\\
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V),&n=0.\end{cases}

The coboundary map
\delta:C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow C^{p+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is given as follows:

•

If
n\geqslant 1
, for any
(f,g)\in C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary map

\delta=(\delta_{\rm I},\delta_{\rm II}):C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{n+2}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(f,g)\mapsto\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big),

is given by

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\rho(x_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})-\rho(y_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle-g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{n+1},y_{n+1}])\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X}_{k})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1}),

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\mu(y_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})-\mu(x_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k+1}D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X}_{k})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

where
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}~(i=1,\cdots,n+1),~z\in\mathfrak{g}
, and the notation
\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l}
means that

\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l}:=\left\llbracket x_{k},y_{k},x_{l}\right\rrbracket\wedge y_{l}+x_{l}\wedge\left\llbracket x_{k},y_{k},y_{l}\right\rrbracket.

•

If
n=0
, for any element
f\in C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary map

\delta:C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{2}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),

\qquad\qquad\qquad f\mapsto\Big(\delta_{I}(f),\delta_{II}(f)\Big),

is given by

(4)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)\Big)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)f(y)-\rho(y)f(x)-f([x,y]),

(5)

\displaystyle~\Big(\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)f(z)+\mu(y,z)f(x)-\mu(x,z)f(y)-f(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 2.8
.

(
[
32
]
)

With the notations above, for any
f\in C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm I}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)),\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta_{\rm II}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)),\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)=0.

Moreover, for all
(f,g)\in C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)~(p\geqslant 2)
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm I}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta_{\rm II}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)=0.

In some literature, this conclusion is denote by
\delta\circ\delta=0.

Theorem 3.1
.

With the above notations, we have
\Delta\circ\Delta=0
. Thus we obtain a well-defined complex
({\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,\mu),\Delta)
.

Sketch of Proof.

Consider the semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes})
, and take its corresponding cohomology
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{LieY}}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\delta)
with coefficients in the adjoint representation. It is easy to see that
({\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,\mu),\Delta)
is a subcomplex of
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{LieY}}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\delta)
. Thus for any
(f,g)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,\mu)
, we have that
(\Delta\circ\Delta)(f,g)_{1}=(\Delta\circ\Delta)(f,g)_{2}=(\Delta\circ\Delta)(f,g)_{3}=0
. The conclusion thus follows.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left\llbracket Nx,Ny,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Nx,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ny,Nz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle~~-N\big(\left\llbracket Nx,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ny,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,y,Nz\right\rrbracket\big)+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(D(Nx,Ny)w+D(Nx,y)(Sw)+D(x,Ny)(Sw)

\displaystyle~~-S\big(D(Nx,y)w+D(x,Ny)w+D(x,y)(Sw)\big)+S^{2}(D(x,y)w)\Big)

\displaystyle~~+\Big(\mu(Ny,z)(Su)+\mu(y,Nz)(Su)+\mu(Ny,Nz)u

\displaystyle~~-S\big(\mu(y,z)(Su)+\mu(Ny,z)u+\mu(y,Nz)u\big)+S^{2}(\mu(y,z)u)\Big)

\displaystyle~~-\Big(\mu(Nx,z)(Sv)+\mu(Nx,Nz)v+\mu(x,Nz)(Sv)

\displaystyle~~+S\big(\mu(Nx,z)v+\mu(x,z)(Sv)+\mu(x,Nz)v\big)-S^{3}(\mu(x,z)v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{N}+\hat{D}(x,y)w+\hat{\mu}(y,z)u-\hat{\mu}(x,z)v,

which implies that
(V;\hat{\rho},\hat{\mu})
is a representation on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{N})
.
∎

4.
Relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures and compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators

4.1.
Relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pairs

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures and discuss several properties of relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures. First, let us recall some notions in
[
27
]
about relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
27
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
and
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
a linear map. If
T
satisfies

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V,

then we call
T
a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

(
[
27
]
)

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
.
Then
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, where

(23)

\displaystyle[u,v]^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u,

(24)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v,\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Thus
T
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism from
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.
Furthermore,
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
is the
sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
of the pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,*^{T},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}^{T})
defined by

(25)

\displaystyle u*^{T}v=\rho(Tu)v,\quad\{u,v,w\}^{T}=\mu(Tv,Tw)u,\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

With the above notations, let
S\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a linear map on
V
, then the deformed bracket
([\cdot,\cdot]_{S}^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T})
of the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
is given as follows:

\displaystyle[u,v]_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Su,v]^{T}+[u,Sv]^{T}-S[u,v]^{T},

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Su,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket Su,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,Sv,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle-S\Big(\left\llbracket Su,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)+S^{2}\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T},\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Moreover, define a pair of brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T})
to be

\displaystyle[u,v]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(Tu)v-\hat{\rho}(Tv)u,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{D}(Tu,Tv)w+\hat{\mu}(Tv,Tw)u-\hat{\mu}(Tu,Tw)v,

respectively, where
\hat{\rho},~\hat{\mu}
and
\hat{D}
are given by (
20
)-(
22
). Note that in general
([\cdot,\cdot]_{S}^{T},~\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T})
, and
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T},~\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T})
are not necessarily Lie-Yamaguti algebra structures.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(N,S)
be a Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
and let
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
.
If for all
u,v,w\in V
, the following conditions are satisfied:

(26)

\displaystyle N\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\circ S,

(27)

\displaystyle~[u,v]_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,v]^{N\circ T},

(28)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{N\circ T}.

Then we call the triple
(T,S,N)
a
relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure
on the
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
. Then we have

\displaystyle~[u,v]_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,v]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T},

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T}.

Proof.

It follows from (
26
) and a direct computation.
∎

Thus if
(T,S,N)
is a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),\\
(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
, then the brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]_{S}^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T})
and
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T})
are equal. Moreover, we have the following conclusion.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
. Then
S
is a Nijenhuis operator on the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
. Thus the brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]_{S}^{T},~\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T})
and
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T},~\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T})
are the Lie-Yamaguti algebra structures on
V
.

Proof.

Since
(T,S,N)
is a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure, then we have

\displaystyle[Su,Sv]^{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{induce1}}}{{=}}\rho(TSu)(Sv)-\rho(TSv)(Su)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ON1},\eqref{Nijstru1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S\Big(\rho(TSu)v+\rho(Tu)(Sv)\Big)-S^{2}\Big(\rho(Tu)v\Big)

\displaystyle-S\Big(\rho(TSv)u+\rho(Tv)(Su)\Big)+S^{2}\Big(\rho(Tv)u\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{induce1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S\Big([Su,v]^{T}+[u,Sv]^{T}\Big)-S^{2}[u,v]^{T},

and

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Su,Sv,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{induce2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle D(TSu,TSv)(Sw)+\mu(TSv,TSw)(Su)-\mu(TSu,TSw)(Sv)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ON1},\eqref{Nijstru2},\eqref{Nijstru3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S\Big(D(TSu,Tv)(Sw)+D(Tu,TSv)(Sw)+D(TSu,TSv)w\Big)

\displaystyle-S^{2}\Big(D(TSu,Tv)w+D(Tu,TSv)w+D(Tu,Tv)(Sw)\Big)+S^{3}(D(Tu,Tv)w)

\displaystyle+S\Big(\mu(TSv,Tw)(Su)+\mu(Tv,TSw)(Su)+\mu(TSv,TSw)u\Big)

\displaystyle-S^{2}\Big(\mu(TSv,Tw)u+\mu(Tv,TSw)u+\mu(Tv,Tw)(Su)\Big)+S^{3}(\mu(Tv,Tw)u)

\displaystyle-S\Big(\mu(TSu,Tw)(Sv)+\mu(Tu,TSw)(Sv)+\mu(TSu,TSw)v\Big)

\displaystyle+S^{2}\Big(\mu(TSu,Tw)v+\mu(Tu,TSw)v+\mu(Tu,Tw)(Sv)\Big)-S^{3}(\mu(Tu,Tw)v)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{induce2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S\Big(\left\llbracket Su,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket Su,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,Sv,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle-S^{2}\Big(\left\llbracket Su,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket Su,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)+S^{3}\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}.

Thus,
S
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket^{T})
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
. Then we have

(i)

T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the deformed Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{N})
with respect to a representation
(V;\hat{\rho},\hat{\mu})
;

(ii)

N\circ T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
and
N\circ T=T\circ S
, we have

\displaystyle~T[u,v]_{\hat{\rho}}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T[u,v]_{S}^{T}=T\Big([Su,v]^{T}+[u,Sv]^{T}-S[u,v]^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[TSu,Tv]+[Tu,TSv]-T\circ S[u,v]^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[NTu,Tv]+[Tu,NTv]-N\circ T[u,v]^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[NTu,Tv]+[Tu,NTv]-N[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]_{N},

and

\displaystyle T\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\hat{\mu}}^{T}=T\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\left\llbracket Su,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket Su,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket u,Sv,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}

\displaystyle-S\left\llbracket Su,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}-S\left\llbracket u,Sv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}-S\left\llbracket u,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}+S^{2}\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket TSu,TSv,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket TSu,Tv,TSw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,TSv,TSw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-T\circ S\left\llbracket Su,TSv,w\right\rrbracket^{T}-T\circ S\left\llbracket Su,Sv,w\right\rrbracket-T\circ S\left\llbracket u,v,Sw\right\rrbracket^{T}+T\circ S^{2}\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket NTu,NTv,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket NTu,Tv,NTw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,NTv,NTw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-N\Big(\left\llbracket NTu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket^{T}+\left\llbracket NTu,Tv,NTw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,NTw\right\rrbracket^{T}\Big)+N^{2}\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{N}.

This proves (i).
By (
27
) and (
28
), we have that

N\circ T[u,v]^{N\circ T}=N\circ T[u,v]_{S}^{T}=N[Tu,Tv]_{N}=[NTu,NTv],

and

N\circ T\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{N\circ T}=N\circ T\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{S}^{T}=N\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{N}=\left\llbracket NTu,NTv,NT.w\right\rrbracket

This proves (ii).
∎

4.2.
Compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and we show that a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure gives rise to a pair of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras under a certain condition.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
T_{1},~T_{2}:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If for all
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
,
k_{1}T_{1}+k_{2}T_{2}
is still a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
, then we say that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are
compatible
.

By a direct computation, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
T_{1},~T_{2}:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Then
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are compatible if and only if the following equations are satisfied:

\displaystyle[T_{i}u,T_{j}v]+[T_{j}u,T_{i}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{i}\Big(\rho(T_{j}u)v-\rho(T_{j}v)u\Big)+T_{j}\Big(\rho(T_{i}u)v-\rho(T_{i}v)u\Big),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket T_{i}u,T_{i}v,T_{j}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{i}u,T_{j}v,T_{i}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{j}u,T_{i}v,T_{i}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{i}\Big(D(T_{i}u,T_{j}v)w+\mu(T_{i}v,T_{j}w)u-\mu(T_{i}u,T_{j}w)v

\displaystyle+D(T_{j}u,T_{i}v)w+\mu(T_{j}v,T_{i}w)u-\mu(T_{j}u,T_{i}w)v\Big)

\displaystyle+T_{j}\Big(D(T_{i}u,T_{i}v)w+\mu(T_{i}v,T_{i}w)u-\mu(T_{i}u,T_{i}w)v\Big),\quad(i,j=1,2).

The following proposition shows that a pair of compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators with an invertible condition yields a Nijenhuis operator.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
T_{1},~T_{2}:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be two compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If
T_{2}
is invertible, then
N=T_{1}\circ T_{2}^{-1}
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Proof.

Since
T_{2}
is invertible, for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exist
u,v,w\in V
, such that
x=T_{2}u,~y=T_{2}v,~z=T_{2}w
. Thus it is sufficient to show that

(29)

\displaystyle~[NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\Big([NT_{2}u,T_{2}v]+[T_{2}u,NT_{2}v]\Big)-N^{2}[T_{2}u,T_{2}v],

(30)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\Big(\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,T_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket\Big)

\displaystyle-N^{2}\Big(\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,T_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,NT_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,T_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket\Big)

\displaystyle+N^{3}\left\llbracket T_{2}u,T_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket.

Since
T_{1}=N\circ T_{2}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator, the left hand side of Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
) are

NT_{2}\Big(\rho(NT_{2}u)v-\rho(NT_{2}v)u\Big)

and

NT_{2}\Big(D(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v)w+\mu(NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w)u-\mu(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}w)v\Big)

respectively.
Since
T_{1}=N\circ T_{2}
and
T_{2}
are compatible, by Proposition
4.8
, we have

\displaystyle[NT_{2}u,T_{2}v]+[T_{2}u,NT_{2}v]

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{2}\Big(\rho(NT_{2}u)v-\rho(NT_{2}v)u\Big)+NT_{2}\Big(\rho(T_{2}u)v-\rho(T_{2}v)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{2}\Big(\rho(NT_{2}u)v-\rho(NT_{2}v)u\Big)+N[T_{2}u,T_{2}v].

The action of
N
on both sides of (
31
) yields Eq (
29
). On the other hand, as for (
30
) we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,T_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{2}\Big(D(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v)w+\mu(NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w)u-\mu(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}w)v\Big)

\displaystyle+NT_{2}\Big(D(NT_{2}u,T_{2}v)w+\mu(NT_{2}v,T_{2}w)u-\mu(NT_{2}u,T_{2}w)v

\displaystyle+D(T_{2}u,NT_{2}v)w+\mu(T_{2}v,NT_{2}w)u-\mu(T_{2}u,NT_{2}w)v\Big),

and

\displaystyle\left\llbracket NT_{2}u,T_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,NT_{2}v,T_{2}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket T_{2}u,T_{2}v,NT_{2}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle NT_{2}\Big(D(T_{2}u,T_{2}v)w+\mu(T_{2}v,T_{2}w)u-\mu(T_{2}u,T_{2}w)v\Big)

\displaystyle+T_{2}\Big(D(NT_{2}u,T_{2}v)w+\mu(NT_{2}v,T_{2}w)u-\mu(NT_{2}u,T_{2}w)v

\displaystyle+D(T_{2}u,NT_{2}v)w+\mu(T_{2}v,NT_{2}w)u-\mu(T_{2}u,NT_{2}w)v\Big).

Thus the right hand side of Eq (
30
) is

NT_{2}\Big(D(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}v)w+\mu(NT_{2}v,NT_{2}w)u-\mu(NT_{2}u,NT_{2}w)v\Big),

which is exactly the left hand side of (
30
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Given a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure
(T,S,N)
, by Theorem
4.6
,
T\circ S=N\circ T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator. However, in general,
T
and
T\circ S
are not compatible spontaneously. Indeed, we can show that they are compatible under a certain condition.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
satisfying

(32)

D(Tu,Tv)(Sw)+\mu(Tv,Tw)(Su)-\mu(Tu,Tw)(Sv)=S\big(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket^{T}\big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
T
and
T\circ S
are compatible relative Rota-Baxter operators.

Proof.

Since
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator, we have

\displaystyle[TSu,Tv]+[Tu,TSv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(TSu)v-\rho(Tv)(Su)+\rho(Tu)(Sv)-\rho(TSv)u\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ON2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(TSu)v-\rho(TSv)u\Big)+TS\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big).

Moreover, Eqs. (
28
) and (
32
) imply that

(33)

\displaystyle D(TSu,Tv)(Sw)+\mu(TSv,Tw)(Su)-\mu(TSu,Tw)(Sv)

\displaystyle+D(Tu,TSv)(Sw)+\mu(Tv,TSw)(Su)-\mu(Tu,TSw)(Sv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\Big(D(TSu,Tv)w+\mu(TSv,Tw)u-\mu(TSu,Tw)v

\displaystyle+D(Tu,TSv)w+\mu(Tv,TSw)u-\mu(Tu,TSw)v\Big).

By the fact that
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator again, we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket TSu,TSv,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket TSu,Tv,TSw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,TSv,TSw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(TSu,TSv)w+\mu(TSv,Tw)(Su)-\mu(TSu,Tw)(Sv)

\displaystyle+D(TSu,Tv)(Sw)+\mu(Tv,TSw)(Su)-\mu(TSu,TSw)v

\displaystyle+D(Tu,TSv)(Sw)+\mu(TSv,TSw)u-\mu(Tu,TSw)(Sv)\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{strong1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle T\Big(D(TSu,TSv)w+\mu(TSv,TSw)u-\mu(TSu,TSw)v\Big)

\displaystyle+T\circ S\Big(D(TSu,Tv)w+\mu(TSv,Tw)u-\mu(TSu,Tw)v

\displaystyle+D(Tu,TSv)w+\mu(Tv,TSw)u-\mu(Tu,TSw)v\Big).

Similarly, we deduce from (
32
) that

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,TSv,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,TSw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket TSu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\circ S\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big)

\displaystyle+T\Big(\rho(TSu,Tv)w+\mu(TSv,Tw)u-\mu(TSu,Tw)v+D(Tu,TSv)w

\displaystyle+\mu(Tv,TSw)u-\mu(Tu,TSw)v\Big).

By Proposition
4.8
, the conclusion thus follows.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

In the context of Lie algebras or associative algebras, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
T_{k}=N^{k}\circ T
are relative Rota-Baxter operators and what is more,
T_{k}
and
T_{l}
are compatible. However, this property fails in the context of
3
-Lie algebras (
[

]
) and Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which leads to a different content of the relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on ternary case.

5.
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures and Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures

In this section, we introduce the notions of
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures and Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras and investigate the relationship between them.
If
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then the dual representation of
(V;\rho,\mu)
is given by

\Big(V^{*};\rho^{*},-\mu^{*}\tau\Big),

where
\rho^{*},~\mu^{*}
are dual to
-\rho,~-\mu
respectively, and
\tau:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is the switching map, i.e.,

\tau(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x,\quad\forall x\otimes y\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}.

See
[
27
]
for ore details about dual representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Example 5.1
.

(
[
27
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
be the adjoint representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
given in Example
2.6
, then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*},-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\tau)
is the dual representation of
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
, which is called the
coadjoint representation
.

First, we introduce the notions of dual Nijenhuis structures and relative Rota-Baxter-dual-Nijenhuis structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
be a
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair,
N\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
,
S\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, and
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

(i)

A pair
(N,S)
is called a
dual Nijenhuis structure
if
(N,S^{*})
is a Nijenhuis structure on the
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V^{*};\rho^{*},-\mu^{*}\tau)\Big)
.

(ii)

If moreover,
T
and dual Nijenhuis structure
(N,S)
satifies Eqs. (
26
)-(

(T,S,N)
is called a

on
\mathsf{LieYRep}
pair
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),(V;\rho,\mu)\Big)
.

Note that a pair
(N,S)
is a dual Nijenhuis structure is equivalent to that

is a Nijenhuis operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
and for all

\displaystyle\rho(Nx)(Sv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\big(\rho(Nx)v-\rho(x)(Sv)\Big)+\rho(x)S^{2}v,

\displaystyle\mu(Nx,Ny)(Sv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\Big(\mu(Nx,Ny)v-\mu(Nx,y)(Sv)-\mu(x,Ny)(Sv)+\mu(x,y)(S^{2}v)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mu(Nx,y)(S^{2}v)+\mu(x,Ny)(S^{2}v)-\mu(x,y)(S^{3}v).

Remark 5.4
.

Note that in the context of Lie algebras or associative algebras, a relative Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure

is also a relative Rota-Baxter-dual-Nijenhuis structure under the condition that

is invertible. However, this property fails in the the context of
3

Definition 5.5

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}},

\pi^{\sharp}

The following theorem illustrates the relationship between
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures and Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structures.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\Big((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket),B\Big)
be a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra,
N:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a Nijenhuis operator on
\mathfrak{g}
and
R:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a skew-symmetric endomorphism of
(\mathfrak{g},B)
. Set
\pi^{\sharp}=R\circ B^{\sharp}
and assume that
B
and
N
are compatible, i.e.,

(44)

\displaystyle B^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}=N\circ B^{\sharp}.

Then we have

(i)

If
(R,N)
is a Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, then
(\pi,N)
is an
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

If
(\pi,N)
is an
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then the pair
(R=\pi^{\sharp}\circ(B^{\sharp})^{-1},N)
is a Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

First, by (
38
) and (
44
), it is sasy to see that
N\circ\pi^{\sharp}=\pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
.
Next, we show that
\pi^{\sharp}:=R\circ B^{\sharp}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation, which is equivalent to that
\pi\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is an
r
-matrix. Indeed, by the invariance of
B
, we have

\displaystyle\pi^{\sharp}\Big(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}\beta-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)}\alpha\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\circ B^{\sharp}\Big(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}\beta-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)}\alpha\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{inva1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle R\Big(\mathrm{ad}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}B^{\sharp}(\beta)-\mathrm{ad}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)}B^{\sharp}(\alpha)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big([R\circ B^{\sharp}(\alpha),B^{\sharp}(\beta)]+[B^{\sharp}(\alpha),R\circ B^{\sharp}(\beta)]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[R\circ B^{\sharp}(\alpha),R\circ B^{\sharp}(\beta)]=[\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)],

and similarly

\displaystyle\pi^{\sharp}\Big(\mathfrak{L}^{*}(\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta))\gamma-\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta))\alpha+\mathfrak{R}^{*}(\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma),\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha))\beta\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big(\left\llbracket\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta),B^{\sharp}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket B^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta),\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket B^{\sharp}(\beta),\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket R\circ B^{\sharp}(\alpha),R\circ B^{\sharp}(\beta),R\circ B^{\sharp}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha),\pi^{\sharp}(\beta),\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket.

What is left is to show
[\alpha,\beta]^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}=[\alpha,\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}
and
\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}=\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}
.

Let
\alpha=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),~\beta=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),~\gamma=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)
, by (
41
)-(
43
), we have

\displaystyle~[\alpha,\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x))}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\pi^{\sharp}((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y))}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{inva1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{R(x)}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{R(y)}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([R(x),y]-[R(y),x]\Big)=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}[x,y]^{R},

and

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}^{*}\Big(\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\Big)-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\Big(\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z),\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{R}^{*}\Big((\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z),\pi^{\sharp}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}^{*}\Big(R(x),R(y)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\Big)-\mathfrak{R}^{*}\Big(R(z),R(y)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)\Big)+\mathfrak{R}^{*}\Big(R(z),R(x)\Big)\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{inva2},\eqref{inva3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big(\left\llbracket R(x),R(y),z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,R(y),R(z)\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket y,R(x),R(z)\right\rrbracket\Big)=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}.

Thus we have

(45)

\displaystyle~[(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}[x,y]^{R},

(46)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}.

Hence, by (
44
), (
45
), and (
46
), we have

\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}-[\alpha,\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}=[\alpha,\beta]^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}-\Big([N^{*}(\alpha),\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}+[\alpha,N^{*}(\beta)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}-N^{*}[\alpha,\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{con1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([x,y]^{N\circ R}\Big)-\Big([N^{*}((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x)),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}+[(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),N^{*}((B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y))]^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle-N^{*}[(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{app}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([x,y]^{N\circ R}\Big)-\Big([(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}+[(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle-N^{*}[(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y)]^{\pi^{\sharp}}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{con1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([x,y]^{N\circ R}\Big)-\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}[Nx,y]^{R}+(B^{\sharp})^{-1}[x,Ny]^{R}-N^{*}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}[x,y]^{R}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([x,y]^{N\circ R}\Big)-(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([Nx,y]^{R}+[x,Ny]^{R}-N[x,y]^{R}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big([x,y]^{N\circ R}-[x,y]^{R}_{N}\Big)=0,

and

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}-\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}-\Big(\left\llbracket N^{*}(\alpha),N^{*}(\beta),\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket N^{*}(\alpha),\beta,N^{*}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket\alpha,N^{*}(\beta),N^{*}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle-N^{*}\big(\left\llbracket N^{*}(\alpha),\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket\alpha,N^{*}(\beta),\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,N^{*}(\gamma)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}\big)+(N^{*})^{2}\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{con2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{N\circ R}-\Big(\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle-N^{*}\big(\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}+\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}N(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}\big)+(N^{*})^{2}\left\llbracket(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(y),(B^{\sharp})^{-1}(z)\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{con2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{N\circ R}-\Big((B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket N(x),N(y),z\right\rrbracket^{R}+(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket N(x),y,N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}+(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,N(y),N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}

\displaystyle-N^{*}\circ(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\big(\left\llbracket N(x),y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket x,N(y),z\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket x,y,N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}\big)+(N^{*})^{2}(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{N\circ R}-(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big(\left\llbracket N(x),N(y),z\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket N(x),y,N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket x,N(y),N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}

\displaystyle-N\big(\left\llbracket N(x),y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket x,N(y),z\right\rrbracket^{R}+\left\llbracket x,y,N(z)\right\rrbracket^{R}\big)+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{N\circ R}-(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}_{N}=(B^{\sharp})^{-1}\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{N\circ R}-\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket^{R}_{N}\Big)=0,

which implies that

[\alpha,\beta]^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}=[\alpha,\beta]^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{N\circ\pi^{\sharp}}=\left\llbracket\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right\rrbracket^{\pi^{\sharp}}_{N^{*}}.

Thus
(\pi,N)
is an
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure. This proves (i).

(ii) can be proved similarly.

Example 5.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=C^{\infty}([0,1])
endowed with the following operations

\displaystyle[f,g](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)g^{\prime}(x)-g(x)f^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket f,g,h\right\rrbracket(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)g^{\prime}(x)h^{\prime}(x)-g(x)f^{\prime}(x)h^{\prime}(x)-h(x)\Big(f(x)g^{\prime\prime}(x)-g(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)\Big),\quad\forall x\in[0,1],

for all
f,g,h\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. For all
f\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
, the integral operator
R:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
defined to be

R(f)(x):=\int_{0}^{x}f(t){\mathrm{d}}t,\quad\forall x\in[0,1]

is a Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
, and
N:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
given by

N(f)(x):=\lambda f(x),\quad\forall x\in[0,1]

is a Nijenhuis operator for
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.
Then
(R,N)
is a Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 5.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a
2
-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, where
([\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is defined to be

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1},\quad\left\llbracket e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}\right\rrbracket=e_{1}.

Then
(R,N)
is a Rota-Baxter-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, where

\displaystyle R=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad N=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the projective space over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
The celebrated Alexander-Hirschowitz Theorem (see e.g.
[
1
,
7
,
19
,
20
]
) classifies all linear systems of hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which are singular at a given number of general points and do not have the expected dimension. More explicitly:

Theorem 1.1

(Alexander-Hirschowitz)
.

Given
s
points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position, the linear system of degree
d
hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which are singular at these points has not the expected dimension

\max\left\{\binom{n+d}{n}-s(n+1)-1,-1\right\}

if and only if

•

either
(n,d,x)=(n,2,x)
with
2\leq x\leq n
,

•

or
(n,d,x)\in\{(2,4,5),(3,4,9),(4,3,7),(4,4,14)\}
.

Linear systems whose dimension is greater than the expected one are called
special
. The speciality of linear systems of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is related to the defectivity of higher secant varieties of the Veronese varieties, see for instance
[
6
]
for more details, and, consequently, is very important for many applications.

A new object of study in this setting is the Terracini locus of a projective variety, see
[
5
]
.
Since then, it has been
investigated by many authors, see in particular
[
3
]
,
[
15
]
and the references therein.
Roughly speaking, the Terracini locus parametrizes the set of all points of a projective variety such that the linear systems of hypersurfaces singular at them are special.

In this paper we focus on the case of Veronese embeddings of projective spaces.

For any positive integers
x
,
let
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
denote the set of all
A\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of cardinality
x
, endowed with the Zariski topology.
Then the
Terracini locus

\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
is the set of all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)

such that

\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n},\quad h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{2S}(d))>0,\quad h^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{2S}(d))>0.

Assume
n\geq 2
. By
[
4
]
, Theorem 1.1, we recall that the Terracini locus

\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
is empty if
d=2
and if
(n,d)=(2,3)
.
On the other hand, if
d\geq 3
and
(n,d)\neq(2,3)
, then
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\neq\emptyset
if and only if

x\geq n+\lceil d/2\rceil
.

By an obvious parameter count we have
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\leq xn
.
The Alexander-Hirschowitz Theorem
1.1
can be rephrased as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Alexander-Hirschowitz)
.

We have
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)=xn
if and only if

•

either
(n,d,x)=(n,2,x)
with
2\leq x\leq n
,

•

or
(n,d,x)\in\{(2,4,5),(3,4,9),(4,3,7),(4,4,14)\}
.

It seems natural to try and find out which are the triples of integers

(n,d,x)
for which we have at least one irreducible component

V\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
of fixed dimension
\dim(V)<nx
.

Already in the first case of dimension
xn-1
the question above turns out to be widely open. More precisely, we pose the following:

Problem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 3
,
(n,d)\neq(2,3)
, and
x\geq n+\lceil d/2\rceil
, so that

\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\neq\emptyset
.
Determine all triples
(n,d,x)
such that
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
has at least one irreducible component of dimension
nx-1
.

Indeed, this task seems to be nontrivial even in the case
n=2
. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix integers
x>0
and
d\geq 4
. The locus
\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)
has at least one irreducible component of dimension
2x-1
if and only if

•

either
(d,x)\in\{(4,4),(5,6),(5,7),(6,9),(6,10)\}

•

or
d\geq 7
,
d\equiv 1,2\pmod{3}
, and
x=(d+2)(d+1)/6
.

Moreover, for
d\geq 7
, such a component is unique with the only exception of
(d,x)=(8,15)
, where there are exactly two components.

Note that, for
n=2
, in all the cases which are not listed in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
, we have that
\dim(\mathbb{T}(2,d;x))\leq 2x-2
.

For many reasons (see for instance
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
),
divisors on parameter spaces are very important. Anyway, they should be defined on a projective parameters space, not on a Zariski open subset of it. Indeed divisors should have an intrinsic definition, it is not sufficient to say:
Take the Zariski closure of the divisor D of
U
. In the set-up of Terracini loci, the natural compactification of
S(X_{\reg},x)
is the smoothable component of the Hilbert scheme of
x
points of
X
. If
X
is a smooth surface, then this is the full Hilbert scheme of
x
points of
X

(see
[
13
]
).

Finally, we would like to point out that one of the main tools we used in order to prove our result is the
spread

\eta
. More precisely, for any locally closed irreducible set
K\subseteq S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
let
\eta(K)
be the maximal integer
y
such that for a general
S^{\prime}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},y)
there exists
S\in K
containing
S^{\prime}
(see Notation
3.1
for the precise definition).
We think that it is interesting and useful to study the spread
\eta(K)
for the irreducible components
K
of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.
In Section
3
we start this investigation, while collecting the ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
.

We thank the referees for several useful comments that helped us to improve our manuscript.

2.
Preliminaries

If
X
is a reducible projective variety, then we denote by
\dim(X)
the dimension of a maximal irreducible component of
X
.

Given a scheme
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we denote by
|\cal{I}_{Z}(d)|:=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\cal{I}_{Z}\otimes\cal{O}(d)))
the linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
d
containing
Z
.

Remark 2.1
.

Take
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
,
n\geq 2
, and assume
(n+1)x\geq\binom{n+d}{n}
.
Taking the cohomology of the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{2S}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

we
have that

S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
if and only if
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Fix positive integers
c
and
x
such that
(n+1)(x+1)\leq\binom{n+d}{n}
. Assume the existence of an irreducible family
K\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
such that
\dim K=nx-c
.
The set of all
S\cup\{p\}
,
S\in K
,
p\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus S
, is an irreducible family
F\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x+1)
of dimension
n(x+1)-c
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Fix integers
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
such that
(n,d)\notin\{(2,4),(4,3),

(4,4)\}
. Set

(1)

\sigma:=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+d}{n}\right\rfloor.

Fix an integer
y<\sigma
and assume
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;y)\geq ny-1
. Then
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)=nx-1
for all
y\leq x\leq\sigma
.

Proof.

By the Alexander-Hirschowitz Theorem
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)<nx
for all
x\leq\sigma
. Thus it is sufficient to prove that
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\geq nx-1
.
We prove it by induction on
x\geq y
.
If
x=y
we know that
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;y)\geq ny-1

by hypothesis.
Assume that
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x-1)\geq n(x-1)-1
, then by Remark
2.2
, with
c=1
, we conclude.
∎

Notation 2.4
.

For any positive integer
x
, let
\mathbb{P}^{n}[x]
denote the set of all
(p_{1},\dots,p_{x})\in(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{x}
such that
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.
Let

u_{x}:\mathbb{P}^{n}[x]\xrightarrow{\ \ }S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)

denote the map
(p_{1},\dots,p_{x})\mapsto\{p_{1},\dots,p_{x}\}
.
For every
y
with
1\leq y\leq x
, let

\eta_{y}:\mathbb{P}^{n}[x]\xrightarrow{\ \ }(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{y}

denote the projection onto the first
y
factors of
(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{x}
.

Observe that
u_{x}
is a finite and unramified map with fibers of cardinality
x!
. Thus for any locally closed irreducible set
K\subseteq S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
the
set
u_{x}^{-1}(K)
has pure dimension
\dim(K)
.

Notation 2.5
.

Given integers
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
we set

\rho(n,d):=\left\lceil\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+d}{n}\right\rceil

if
(n,d)\notin\{(2,4),(3,4),(4,3),(4,4)\}

and

\rho(n,d):=\left\lceil\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+d}{n}\right\rceil+1

if
(n,d)\in\{(2,4),(3,4),(4,3),(4,4)\}
.

The following proposition gives a first easy bound on the dimension of the Terracini locus when the number of points is high.

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix integers
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 3
and
c>0
such that
(n,d)\neq(2,3)
.
Set
\rho=\rho(n,d)
as in Notation
2.5
and
x_{0}:=c\rho
. Fix an integer
x\geq x_{0}
. Then we have
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\leq nx-c
.

Proof.

Let
K
be an irreducible component of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
. By the Alexander-Hirschowitz Theorem, we have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))=0
for a general

A\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\rho)
. Hence

\dim\eta_{\rho}(u_{x}^{-1}(K))\leq n\rho-1
.
Fix a general
S\in K
and label the
x
points of
S
as
p_{i,j}
,
1\leq i\leq\rho
,
1\leq j{\leq c}
,
and call
q_{\alpha}
the other points (if
x>x_{0}
).
Varying
S
in
K
for each fixed
j
each set
\{p_{i,j},1\leq i\leq\rho\}
depends on at most
n\rho-1
parameters. The set of all
q_{\alpha}
’s depends on at most

n(x-x_{0})
parameters. Thus
\dim K\leq{c(n\rho-1)+n(x-x_{0})}=nx-c
.
∎

3.
The general case

Definition 3.1
.

For any locally closed irreducible non-empty set
K\subseteq S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
, let
\eta(K)
be the maximal integer
y
such that
\eta_{y}(u_{x}^{-1}(K))
contains a non-empty open subset of
(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{y}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The integer
\eta(K)
, which we will call
spread of
K
,
is the maximal integer
y
such that for general
S_{1}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},y)

there is
S\in K
containing
S_{1}
. Hence
for any general
S_{2}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},y+1)
there is no
S\in K
containing
S_{2}
. Equivalently
\eta(K)
is the maximal integer such that the map
\eta_{y}
is dominant on
K
.

Remark 3.3
.

Take an irreducible family
K\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
and set
\eta:=\eta(K)
.
By definition of
\eta(K)
we have

(2)

n\eta\leq\dim K\leq n\eta+(n-1)(x-\eta){=(n-1)x+\eta}.

Indeed, by Remark
3.2
, we can choose
\eta
general points in
K
, but we cannot generically choose the other
x-\eta
points.

Let
K
be an irreducible component of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.
If
y=\dim K-(n-1)x>0
,
then by (
2
) we have
\eta(K)\geq y
.

Therefore, if
\dim(K)=nx-1
, we have that the spread
\eta(K)=x-1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Given integers
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
,
set
\rho=\rho(n,d)
as in Notation
2.5
and
x\geq\rho
.
Then
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
has no irreducible component
K
such that
\eta(K)\geq x
.

Proof.

By (
2
) we have

\dim(\mathbb{T}(n,d;x))\geq\dim(K)\geq n\eta(K).

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.6
, with
c=1
, we have

\dim(\mathbb{T}(n,d;x))\leq nx-1.

Hence we conclude that the spread satisfies
\eta(K)\leq x-1.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Given integers
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
,
set
\rho=\rho(n,d)
as in Notation
2.5
and
x\geq\rho+1
.
Then we have
\dim\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\leq nx-2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, with
c=1
, we have

\dim(\mathbb{T}(n,d;x))\leq nx-1.

Assume by contradiction
that
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
has an irreducible component
K
of dimension
nx-1
.
Therefore, by Remark
3.3
, we have
\eta(K)=x-1
.
Since
x-1\geq\rho
, by Theorem
3.4
we have that there are no irreducible component
K
of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
such that
\eta(K)\geq x-1
, and so we have a contradiction.
∎

The next result uses our assumption that each element of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
spans
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
K
be an irreducible component of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
. Then
\eta(K)\geq n+1
.
Moreover,

\eta(K)\geq n+2
if and only if there is
S\in K
containing
n+2
points in linear general position.

Proof.

We have
h(\mathbb{T}(n,d;x))=\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
for all
h\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
. Since
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is irreducible,
h(K)=K
for all
h\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
.
Since each
S\in K
spans
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then
S
contains
S^{\prime}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},n+1)
such that
\langle S^{\prime}\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Since all elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
act transitively
on the open subset of
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},n+1)
formed by linearly independent points, we conclude that
\eta(K)\geq n+1
.

Recall that
n+2
general points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
are in linear general position, if any subset of
n+1
of these points spans
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. The group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
acts transitively on the subset of
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},n+2)
formed by points in linear general position. Thus arguing as above, we can prove that
\eta(K)\geq n+2
if and only if there is
S\in K
containing
S^{\prime\prime}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},n+2)
in linear general position.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Take
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 3
and
x
such that

x\geq n+\lceil d/2\rceil
and
(n+1)x\leq\binom{n+d}{n}
. Then there is an irreducible family
K

of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
such that
\eta(K)=x-\lceil d/2\rceil+1
and

\dim K=\lceil d/2\rceil+n-1+n(x-\lceil d/2\rceil)
.

Proof.

Fix any
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
. Since
\deg(2S)=(n+1)x\leq\binom{n+d}{n}
, we have that
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
if and only if
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.

We will first define an irreducible quasi-projective variety
K_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)

and then we will take as
K
the closure of
K_{1}
in
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

Let
G(2,n+1)
be the Grassmannian of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
Consider the set

E\subset S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)\times G(2,n+1)

formed by all pairs
(A,L)
with
A\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)
,
L\in G(2,n+1)
such that
A\subset L
.
Since
\dim G(2,n+1)=2n-2
, the projection

S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)\times G(2,n+1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }G(2,n+1)

shows that
E
is an irreducible quasi-projective variety of dimension
(2n-2)+(\lceil d/2\rceil+1).

Let
K_{2}\subset S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)

be the image of
E
by the projection of the second factor. Since
\lceil d/2\rceil+1>2
and any two points are contained in a unique line,
K_{2}
is an irreducible constructible subset of
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)
and
\dim K_{2}=2n-2+\lceil d/2\rceil+1
. The set
K_{2}
is the the set of all collinear elements of
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},\lceil d/2\rceil+1)
. Now, let
K_{1}
be the set of all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
containing an element of
K_{2}
and such that
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
Notice that, since
\deg(2S\cap L)\geq 2(\lceil d/2\rceil+1)\geq d+2
, then we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap L}(d))>0
. Therefore
K_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

Let
K
be the closure of
K_{1}
.
We have
\dim(K)=(2n-2+\lceil d/2\rceil+1)+n(x-1-\lceil d/2\rceil)
.
It is easy to check that
\eta(K)=(x-\lceil d/2\rceil-1)+2=x-\lceil d/2\rceil+1
.
∎

The following result gives a complete description of
\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)
.

Proposition 3.10
.

\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)
is irreducible of dimension
14
, formed by all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
such that
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
4
of the points of
S
are coplanar. The action of
PGL(4)
on
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
sends
\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)
into itself with two orbits: an orbit
\Phi
of dimension
13
formed by all
S\in\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)
containing
3
collinear points and the open orbit

\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)\setminus\Phi
.

Proof.

The group
PGL(4)
acts on
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},4)
and on
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
and in both cases it has an open orbit in the Zariski topology. The open orbit of
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},4)
is formed by the linearly independent subsets. The open orbit of
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
is formed by all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
in linearly general position, i.e. the set
S
such that all proper subsets of

are linearly independent.

The action of
PGL(4)
on the subset of
S(\mathbb{P}^{3},5)
given by the sets which span
\mathbb{P}^{3}

Notation 4.2

)

The crucial result in this section is the following:

Proposition 4.13
.

Fix a positive integer
d\geq 7
.

(i)
If

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\binom{d+2}{2}-1=\frac{(d+4)(d-1)}{6},\mbox{ if }d\equiv 1,2\pmod{3};

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{1}{3}\binom{d+2}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{d(d+3)}{6},\mbox{ if }d\equiv 0\pmod{3},

then we have
\dim\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)\leq 2x-2
.

(ii) If
d\equiv 0\pmod{3}

y=\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}\binom{d+2}{2}\right\rceil=\frac{d(d+3)}{6}+1,

then we have
\dim\mathbb{T}(2,d;y)\leq 2x-2
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists a component
K
of
\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)
of dimension
2x-1
.
By Remark
3.3
, we have
\eta(K)=x-1
.

For any
S\in K
, by definition of Terracini locus, we have

{\varepsilon:=}h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
hence

(3)

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))=\binom{d+2}{2}-3x+\varepsilon\geq 2+\frac{d^{2}+3d}{2}-3x,

which implies
\dim|\cal{I}_{2S}(d)|\geq 1+\frac{d^{2}+3d}{2}-3x
.

Consider the set

\Gamma_{K}=\{(S,C):S\in K,C\in|\cal{I}_{2S}(d)|\}\subset S(\mathbb{P}^{2},x)\times|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d)|

and take an irreducible family

A_{K}\subseteq\Gamma_{K}
such that
the projection
{\pi_{1}}:A_{K}\xrightarrow{\ \ }K
is dominant. Note that
A_{K}
is non-empty because any element of
K
is in the Terracini locus.

Let
{\pi_{2}}:A_{K}\xrightarrow{\ \ }|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d)|
denote the restriction to
A_{K}

of the projection to the second factor. Since each fiber of
{\pi_{1}}
has dimension greater than or equal to

1+(d^{2}+3d)/2-3x
and
\dim K=2x-1
, we have

(4)

\dim A_{K}\geq\frac{d^{2}+3d}{2}-x=\dim V_{x,d}.

Take a general
(S,C)\in A_{K}
. Since
\eta(K)=x-1
, then there is a general
S^{\prime}\in S(\mathbb{P}^{2},x-1)
contained in
S
.
Fix such
S^{\prime}
.

In the sequel of the proof (steps (b) and (c)), we will use the following two claims.

Claim 1:
We have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d-1))=0
, by the Alexander-Hirschowitz Theorem.

Claim 2:

Every irreducible component
D
of
C
contains at least one point of
S^{\prime}
.
Indeed, from
Claim 1 we easily deduce that
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}\cap D}(d))
.

We consider now the following three cases:
C
irreducible and reduced,
C
reducible and reduced, and
C
non-reduced.

(a)
Assume first that
C
is irreducible and reduced.
Since
C
has no multiple components, then
\mathrm{Sing}(C)
is finite. Thus a general fiber of
{\pi_{2}}
is finite, hence
\dim{\pi_{2}}(A_{K})=\dim A_{K}
.
Therefore by (
4
) we have
\dim\pi_{2}(A_{K})\geq\dim V_{x,d}
.
Hence
{\pi_{2}}(A_{K})
contains a non-empty open subset of
V_{x,d}
, by Remark
4.12
.
By Lemma
4.10
we get
\dim\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)=2x
, which is false.

(b)
Assume now
C
reduced and reducible.
Since
C
has no multiple components, we have

\dim{\pi_{2}}(A_{K})=\dim A_{K}{=\binom{d+2}{2}-x-1+\varepsilon},

where

\varepsilon:=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.

Write
C=C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{s}
,
s\geq 2
,
with
C_{i}
an irreducible curve of degree
d_{i}
and geometric genus
g_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

Restricting to a non-empty Zariski open subset
U_{K}
of
K
, we may assume that the integers
s
,
d_{i}
and
g_{i}
are the same for all
C\in U_{K}
. We have
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=d
.

By
[
16
, Introduction]
, the family

\cal{A}_{i}

of all
irreducible plane curves of degree
d_{i}
and geometric genus

is irreducible of dimension

\dim\cal{A}_{i}=(d_{i}^{2}+3d_{i})/2-(d_{i}-1)(d_{i}-2)/2+g_{i}=3d_{i}-1+g_{i}.

Obviously,
\dim\pi_{2}(A_{K})
is at most

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim\cal{A}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(3d_{i}-1+g_{i})=3d-s+g_{1}+\cdots+g_{s}
.

By
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
,
we have

g_{1}+\cdots+g_{s}\leq\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-x+1,

hence, we get

\dim\pi_{2}(A_{K})=\binom{d+2}{2}-x-1+\varepsilon\leq 3d-s+\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-x+1

from which we have

, which contradicts our assumption.

(c)
Now assume that
C
has at least one multiple component.

First, note that each irreducible component of
C_{\red}
contains at least one point
of
S^{\prime}
, by Claim 2. Moreover,
by Claims 1 and 2,
each irreducible component of

has multiplicity at most

.

Hence, we can write

,
where

, is the union of the irreducible components of

appearing with multiplicity exactly

in

.
Set

, so that

, and

,

and

.
Note that

and

.

Claim 3:

Note that

. Indeed if

, then we would have

cannot have singular points. Hence

would be in

.

Since

and

is general in

(5)

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{B_{2}}(a_{2}))=\binom{a_{2}+2}{2}-b_{2}>0.

Now, let

denote the base locus of

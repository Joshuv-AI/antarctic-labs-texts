Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty, open set in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
\lambda(\Omega)
denote the bottom of the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian acting in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. That is

\lambda(\Omega)=\inf\Big\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}:u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),u\not\equiv 0\Big\}.

If the measure
|\Omega|
of
\Omega
is finite, then
\lambda(\Omega)
is the fundamental eigenvalue.

We define the torsion function for arbitrary non-empty open sets
\Omega
(with either finite or infinite measure) by a limiting procedure. Let
B_{r}
denote the ball of radius
r
centred at the origin, and
define
w_{\Omega}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
by
w_{\Omega}(x)=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}w_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}(x)
, where
w_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}(x)
is the unique solution of

-\Delta w=1,\,\,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\cap B_{r}).

If
\Omega
has finite measure, then
w_{\Omega}
is the classical torsion function, and the convergence
w_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}\to w_{\Omega}
is strong in
H^{1}
. If
\Omega
has infinite measure, the function
w_{\Omega}
is a well-defined, non-negative Borel function, and possibly infinite valued.
If
w_{\Omega}
is finitely valued on
\Omega
, then
w_{\Omega}(x)
is the expected lifetime of Brownian motion starting at
x\in\Omega
, and
w_{\Omega}
is a weak solution, in the sense of distributions, of
-\Delta w=1
on
\Omega
, which satisfies
w|_{\partial\Omega}=0
at all regular points of
\partial\Omega
. Moreover, if
G_{\Omega}(x,y),\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in\Omega
is the kernel of the resolvent of the Dirichlet Laplacian, then

w_{\Omega}(x)=\int_{\Omega}dy\,G_{\Omega}(x,y).

(1)

It is known that
\lambda(\Omega)>0
is equivalent to
w_{\Omega}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see
[
6
]
). Moreover, as soon as
\lambda(\Omega)>0
, the following inequality holds

1\leq\|w_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}\lambda(\Omega)\leq c_{m}

(2)

and the constant
c_{m}
satisfies, see
[
6
, Theorem 1]
,

c_{m}\leq 4+3m\log 2.

A proof of the lower bound in (
2
) can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 5.3]
. It was shown in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
that if
m\geq 2
the lower bound
1
in (
2
) is sharp:
for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a non-empty open set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\|w_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\infty}\lambda(\Omega_{\varepsilon})<1+\varepsilon.

(3)

An example of such an open set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is an open cube with side-length
1
punctured with
N^{m}
balls positioned in a periodic order and with suitable chosen small radii. See Figure 1.

Figure 1:
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
N=9,\,m=2

In 1994, R. Bañuelos and T. Carroll
[
2
, (4.4),(4.6)]
conjectured that the lower bound in the left-hand side of (
2
) remains sharp for
\Omega
in the class of simply connected sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since the open sets constructed in
[
8
]
(Figure
1
) are not simply connected, the answer to the conjecture of Bañuelos and Carroll remained open.

In Theorem
1
below we show that if
\Omega
is simply connected in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
then the constant
1
in the left hand side of (
2
) can be improved by a positive number, thereby disproving the Bañuelos and Carroll conjecture. We shall give an estimate of this number, without any claim to be optimal.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be non-empty, open and simply connected. If
\lambda(\Omega)>0
, then

\|w_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}\lambda(\Omega)>1+\frac{3^{6}e^{9}}{2^{43}13^{13}j_{0}},

(4)

where
j_{0}
is the first positive zero of the Bessel function
J_{0}
.

In
[
2
, Theorem 1]
it was shown that if
\Omega
is simply connected in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\|w_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{7\zeta(3)}{16c_{0}^{2}}r(\Omega)^{2},

(5)

where
\zeta(.)
is the Riemann zeta function,
c_{0}
is the schlicht Bloch-Landau constant, and

r(\Omega)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}d_{\Omega}(x)

is the inradius of
\Omega
with
the distance to the boundary
\partial\Omega
given by

d_{\Omega}(x)=\inf\{|x-y|:y\notin\Omega\}.

Combining Theorem
1
with (
5
) yields

\lambda(\Omega)\geq\frac{16c_{0}^{2}}{7\zeta(3)}\Big(1+\frac{3^{6}e^{9}}{2^{43}13^{13}j_{0}}\Big)r(\Omega)^{-2}.

This improves the constant in
[
2
, (0.7)]
, see also
[
10
, p.2473]
, by a factor given by the right-hand side of (
4
). We believe this is, as far as we know, the best result at present.
However, its importance is not so much its small improvement rather the non-trivial contribution of the right-hand side of (
4
).

We recall
[
3
, Theorem 1]
where it was shown that

\sup\big\{\|w_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}r(\Omega)^{-2}:\Omega\,\,\textup{simply connected, non-empty, open in}\,\mathbb{R}^{2},\,r(\Omega)<\infty\}

has a maximiser. We point out that this maximiser is not necessarily a minimiser of

\inf\big\{\lambda(\Omega)r(\Omega)^{2}:\Omega\,\,\textup{simply connected, non-empty, open in}\,\mathbb{R}^{2},\,r(\Omega)<\infty\big\}.

We shall see below that it suffices to prove Theorem

for non-empty, open, simply connected sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with finite measure.
The proof of Theorem

relies on some properties of the torsion function

and of the first Dirichlet eigenfunction

In Proposition

, while in (ii) we assume that the torsion function has a maximum. In fact (ii) is not needed in the proof of Theorem

In Proposition

In order to introduce the second result of the paper, we recall a classical inequality for open sets
\Omega
with finite measure involving the torsional rigidity

of

It asserts that the function

(10)

F(\Omega)<1.

(11)

The constant

in the right-hand side of (

) is sharp, as shown in

. That is, given

(12)

The open set

is yet again an open cube with side-length

in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The question arises once more whether the constant

in (

with finite measure. Our second main result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is non-empty, open and simply connected with finite measure, then

F(\Omega)<1-\frac{3^{4}}{12801\cdot 2^{35}\Big(1+\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{16}\Big)^{4}}.

(14)

Note that for simply connected open sets (

) cannot give a bound of the form (

with

be the union of

connected by

disjoint thin tubes having total measure

.
It is easily seen that

.

and

with possibly different correction to

\Phi_{1,\infty}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\frac{\|w_{\Omega}\|_{1}}{\|w_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}},\hskip 28.45274pt\Phi_{1,2}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\|w_{\Omega}\|_{1}}{\|w_{\Omega}\|_{2}}.

Proposition 4
.

If

We also obtain an estimate of the mean to max ratio.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\Phi_{1,\infty}(\Omega)<\Bigg(1-\frac{3^{4}}{12801\cdot 2^{35}\Big(1+\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{16}\Big)^{4}}\Bigg)\Big(1+\frac{3^{6}e^{9}}{2^{43}13^{13}j_{0}}\Big)^{-1}.

Throughout the paper we denote an open ball centred at

We first prove Proposition

, and begin with a few basic lemmas. Let
p_{\Omega}(x,y;t),\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in\Omega,\,t>0
be the Dirichlet heat kernel for
\Omega
.
Then

G_{\Omega}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}dt\,p_{\Omega}(x,y;t)

is the kernel of the resolvent of the Dirichlet Laplacian acting in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 6
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is non-empty, open and simply connected with finite measure, then

(i)

G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{(d_{\Omega}(y)+|x-y|)^{1/2}+d_{\Omega}(y)^{1/2}}{(d_{\Omega}(y)+|x-y|)^{1/2}-d_{\Omega}(y)^{1/2}},\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in\Omega,

(21)

and

(ii)

G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\Big(\frac{\min\{d_{\Omega}(x),d_{\Omega}(y)\}}{|x-y|}\Big)^{1/2},\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in\Omega.

We recall the proof on
[
5
, p.618]
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the conformal map from the unit disc onto
\Omega
with
H(0)=x
. Then by the results of
[
1
]
,

G_{\Omega}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\coth(\rho_{\Omega}(x,y)),

(22)

where

\rho_{\Omega}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}dt\,\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|}{|H^{\prime}(0)|},

(23)

and where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable curves
\gamma
in
\Omega
with
\gamma(0)=x,\,\gamma(1)=y
and where
H^{\prime}(0)
is evaluated at
\gamma(t)
.
By Koebe’s

Theorem,

d_{\Omega}(\gamma(t))\leq|H^{\prime}(0)|\leq 4d_{\Omega}(\gamma(t)).

(24)

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\gamma
has a parametrisation with constant speed
c
. Then for any such
\gamma
,

d_{\Omega}(\gamma(t))\leq d_{\Omega}(x)+ct.

(25)

By (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
),

\rho_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}dt\,\frac{c}{d_{\Omega}(x)+ct}=\frac{1}{4}\log\Big(1+\frac{c}{d_{\Omega}(x)}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{4}\log\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{d_{\Omega}(x)}\Big),

(26)

since
c\geq|x-y|
. By (
22
) and (
26
) we get that

G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{(d_{\Omega}(x)+|x-y|)^{1/2}+d_{\Omega}(x)^{1/2}}{(d_{\Omega}(x)+|x-y|)^{1/2}-d_{\Omega}(x)^{1/2}},\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in\Omega.

(27)

By symmetry we may reverse the roles of
x
and of

in (

). This proves (i).

By (

) we have

\displaystyle G_{\Omega}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\log\Big(\Big(1+\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{1/2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\Big(\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{1/2},

and the assertion under (ii) follows by symmetry.
∎

Below we state and prove an upper bound for
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
which decays faster than
|x-y|^{-1/2}
for large

.

Lemma 7
.

If
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian is discrete, then

G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{2^{1/2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}-\frac{t\lambda(\Omega)}{4}}.

Proof.

The following inequality is well known. By the semigroup property of the Dirichlet heat kernel and Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle p_{\Omega}(x,y;t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}dz\,p_{\Omega}(x,z;t/2)p_{\Omega}(z,y;t/2)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega}dz\,p_{\Omega}(x,z;t/2)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}dz\,p_{\Omega}(z,y;t/2)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(p_{\Omega}(x,x;t)p_{\Omega}(y,y;t)\Big)^{1/2}.

(28)

Furthermore by the spectral representation of the Dirichlet heat kernel and monotonicity

\displaystyle p_{\Omega}(x,x;t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t\lambda(\Omega)/2}p_{\Omega}(x,x;t/2)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t\lambda(\Omega)/2}p_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,x;t/2)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-t\lambda(\Omega)/2}}{2\pi t}.

(29)

We obtain, by (
2
) and (
2
),

\displaystyle p_{\Omega}(x,y;t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(p_{\Omega}(x,x;t)p_{\Omega}(y,y;t)\Big)^{1/4}p_{\Omega}(x,y;t)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(p_{\Omega}(x,x;t)p_{\Omega}(y,y;t)\Big)^{1/4}p_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,y;t)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{1/2}}{4\pi t}e^{-\frac{t\lambda(\Omega)}{4}-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}}.

∎

To prove Proposition

Proof of Theorem

The key result to prove Theorem

Before proving Proposition

, we give a technical result.

Lemma 9
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a non-empty, open, simply connected set, let
x_{0}\in\partial U
and let
R_{0}>0
. Assume that
v\in H^{1}(U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}))
satisfies the following

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=1&\hbox{in }U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}),\\
v=0&\hbox{on }\partial U,\\
v\leq 1&\hbox{on }\partial B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

Then
\forall x\in B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\cap U
we have that

v(x)\leq\Big(1+\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{2R_{0}^{2}}\Big)\frac{8}{3}\pi^{-\frac{3}{4}}(4\pi R_{0}^{2})^{\frac{3}{4}}|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(49)

Proof.

Note that
U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})
may not be connected. However its connected components are simply connected sets. It is enough to prove the inequality for such an arbitrary component, and for a point
x_{0}
on its boundary. Collecting all inequalities on all components, (
49
) will hold on the full set
B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\cap U
with a point
x_{0}\in\partial U
. Indeed, if
x_{0}
is not on the boundary of some component, the inequality still holds since the right hand side in (
49
) is larger. Let us introduce the radial function

\varphi:B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})\to[0,1]
such that

\varphi(x)=0
on
B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})
, and

\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cos\Big(\pi\frac{|x|-R_{0}}{R_{0}}\Big),\,x\in B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0}).

Note that
\varphi\in H^{2}(B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}))
and, by direct computation, that

\forall x\in B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}),\;\;|\Delta\varphi(x)|\leq\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{2R_{0}^{2}}.

Now,
v-\varphi\leq 0
on
\partial(U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}))
and

-\Delta(v-\varphi)\leq 1+\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{2R_{0}^{2}}\mbox{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})).

This implies that

\forall x\in U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}),\;v(x)-\varphi(x)\leq\Big(1+\frac{\pi+\pi^{2}}{2R_{0}^{2}}\Big)w_{U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})}.

But
U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})
is a union of simply connected sets and the estimate of Corollary
8
(see also
[
5
, Corollary 5.2]
) holds

w_{U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})}(x)\leq\frac{8}{3}\pi^{-\frac{3}{4}}|B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0})|^{\frac{3}{4}}\Big(d_{\partial(U\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x_{0}))}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
x_{0}\in\partial U
and
\varphi(x)=0
on
B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})
, we get (
49
).

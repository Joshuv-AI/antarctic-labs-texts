Abstract
. In the first part of the paper, we define the concept of a G G -table of a G G -(co)algebra and we compute the G G -table of some G G -(co)algebras (here a G G -algebra is an algebra on which G G acts, semisimply, by algebra automorphisms). The G G -table of a G G -(co)algebra 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} is a set of scalars that provides very precise and concise information about both the algebra structure and the G G -module structure of 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} . In particular, the ordinary multiplication table of 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} can be derived from the G G -table of 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} . From the G G -table of a G G -algebra 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} we define a plain algebra P ⁡ ( 𝔄 ) P(\mathfrak{A}) associated to it and we present some basic functoriality results about P P . Obtaining the G G -table of a given G G -algebra 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} requires a considerable amount of work but, the result, is a very powerful tool as shown in the second part of the paper. Here we compute the SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝕂 ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K}) -tables of the Poisson algebra structure of the even-degree part of the cohomology associated to the cotangent bundle of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie group with Lie algebra 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} , that is H E ∙ , ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 ) = H E ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 , ⋀ ∙ 𝔥 ) H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})=H_{E}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{h}) . This Poisson SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝕂 ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K}) -algebra has dimension 18. From these SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝕂 ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K}) -tables we deduce that the underlying Lie algebra of H E ∙ , ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 ) H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}) is isomorphic to 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 , 𝕂 ) ⋉ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 , 𝕂 ) a ​ b \mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb{K})\ltimes\mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb{K})_{ab} with the first factor acting on the second (abelian) one by the adjoint representation. We find it remarkable that the Lie algebra structure on H E ∙ , ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 ) H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}) contains a semisimple Lie subalgebra (in this case 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 , 𝕂 ) \mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{K}) ) strictly larger than the Levi factor of Der ​ ( 𝔥 ) \text{Der}(\mathfrak{h}) , which in this case is 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , 𝕂 ) ⊂ H 1 ​ ( 𝔥 , 𝔥 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{K})\subset H^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}) . This means that the Levi factor of the Lie algebra H E ∙ , ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 ) H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}) has nontrivial elements outside H 1 ​ ( 𝔥 , 𝔥 ) H^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}) . Finally, this leads us to find a family of commutative Poisson algebras whose underlying Lie structure is 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , 𝕂 ) ⋉ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , 𝕂 ) a ​ b \mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{K})\ltimes\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{K})_{ab} (arbitrary n n ) such that, for n = 3 n=3 , is isomorphic to H E ∙ , ∙ ​ ( 𝔥 ) H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}) .

1.
Introduction

This work is part of a project originally intended to investigate in detail the multiplicative structures that appear in various cohomology groups, particularly the Gerstenhaber and Poisson (super-)algebras associated with certain (co)homology spaces of associative or Lie algebras.
It is widely acknowledged that obtaining a description of these types of algebras is a very challenging problem, even if we were satisfied
with some superficial information about the product in a small portion of the entire cohomology space.

In this context, the purpose of this article is twofold.
Firstly, we introduce the concept of the
G
-table of a given
G
-algebra (an algebra on which
G
acts semisimply by algebra automorphisms, see
[
Tur95
,
The95
]
).
If
\mathfrak{A}
is a
G
-algebra, then, roughly speaking, we have a
G
-module decomposition into irreducible

G
-submodules

\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{r\in R}\mathfrak{A}_{r},

and, in very vague terms, the

G
-table of
\mathfrak{A}
is certain set of scalars

c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}\in\mathbb{K}
satisfying, in some sense,

(1.1)

\mathfrak{A}_{r_{1}}\cdot\mathfrak{A}_{r_{2}}=\sum_{s\in R}c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}\mathfrak{A}_{s}

resembling the ordinary multiplication table of
\mathfrak{A}

with respect to a given basis.
In §
2
we formalize this concept by providing a precise definition of a
G
-table of a given
G
-(co)algebra.
For instance, if
\mathfrak{A}=M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
then
\mathfrak{A}
is a

SO(3)
-algebra and

\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{A}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{A}_{2}
as a
SO(3)
-module, where
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
are, respectively, the skew-symmetric and symmetric traceless matrices, and
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
are the multiples of the identity.
The
SO(3)
-table of
\mathfrak{A}
turns out to be

\mathfrak{A}_{0}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}

\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{0}

\mathfrak{A}_{0}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}

\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}

\mathfrak{A}_{0}+\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{1}-\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\mathfrak{A}_{0}+\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}

The
SO(3)
-table of the associative algebra
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.

When computing a
G
-table, there are some choices that allow certain normalizations, this is why we were able to have, in this particular case, all the coefficients equal to
\pm 1
in the previous
SO(3)
-table.

The
G
-table of
\mathfrak{A}
provides very precise information about both, the algebra structure and the
G
-module structure of
\mathfrak{A}
.
If
G=\{1\}
is the trivial group, the
G
-table of an algebra is (almost) the same as the ordinary multiplication table of
\mathfrak{A}
.
On the other hand, when
G
is large, the
G
-table is much more concise than
an ordinary multiplication table and, still,
the ordinary table can be deduced from the
G
-table.
When
G
is ‘sufficiently’ large, many fine details about the algebra structure of
\mathfrak{A}
are revealed by
G
-table. For instance, the above table shows us immediately that
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
does not have any proper
SO(3)
-subalgebra (subalgebra and
SO(3)
-submodule).
We can also observe that
[\mathfrak{A}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{2}]=2\mathfrak{A}_{2}
(which is the same as saying that
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
is a
\mathfrak{so}(3)
-submodule, note that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
is the vector space
\mathfrak{so}(3)
).
The first part of the paper includes many examples of
G
-tables and concludes by associating a plain algebra to a given
G
-algebra and analyzing some basic functoriality properties of this assignment.

Obtaining the actual
G
-table of a given
G
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
requires a considerable amount of work, in addition to a very precise set up in advance,
that we refer to as the labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}_{G}
.
But, it is worth it since it becomes a very powerful tool as it
shown in the second part of the paper.
In §
3
we describe the
Poisson algebra structure of
the even-degree part of the cohomology associated to the cotangent bundle of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie group.
Recall that, by a theorem of Nomizu
[
Nom54
]
, the de Rham cohomology of
the compact quotients of this cotangent bundle
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}

where
\mathfrak{h}
is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.
We know that
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-algebra and thus
the even cohomology

H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})=\bigoplus_{k\text{ even}}\;\bigoplus_{i+j=k}H^{i}(\mathfrak{h},\mbox{\normalsize$\bigwedge$}^{j}\mathfrak{h})

is a Poisson
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-algebra (of dimension 18).
First, we give the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-tables of the cup product and
of the Poisson bracket in
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
.
This requires three steps: (i) choosing a labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})}
,
(ii) obtaining the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-module decomposition of

H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
and matching it with the labeling,
and (iii) a lengthy and very careful work to compute the actual coefficients of the
G
-tables (cup product and Lie bracket).

From these
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-tables we derive that the underlying Lie algebra of
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}
is isomorphic to

\mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb{K})\ltimes\mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb{K})_{ab}
with the
first factor acting on the second (abelian) one by the adjoint representation.
It turns out that, for arbitrary
n
,
the semidirect product (Lie algebra)
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{K})\ltimes\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{K})_{ab}
, with
associative commutative product

\displaystyle(a_{0}I_{n}+A_{0},\;

\displaystyle a_{1}I_{n}+A_{1})\cdot(b_{0}I_{n}+B_{0},\;b_{1}I_{n}+B_{1})

\displaystyle=\Big(

\displaystyle a_{0}b_{0}I_{n}\;\;+\;\;a_{0}B_{0}+b_{0}A_{0}\;,\;

\displaystyle\big(a_{0}b_{1}+a_{1}b_{0}+\text{tr}(A_{0}B_{1}+A_{1}B_{0})-\tfrac{2}{n}\text{tr}(A_{0}B_{0})\big)I_{n}\;\;+\;\;a_{0}B_{1}+b_{0}A_{1}+A_{0}B_{0}+B_{0}A_{0}\Big)

(here
A_{0},A_{1},B_{0},B_{1}\in\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{K})
)
is a Poisson algebra, and
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})

is isomorphic to the member of this family corresponding to
n=3
.
The proof of this automorphism follows, in an almost straight forward way,
by comparing the corresponding
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-tables.

It is remarkable for us that the Lie algebra structure on

H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
contains a semisimple
Lie subalgebra (in this case
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{K})
)
strictly larger than the Levi factor
of
\text{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, which in this case is
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{K})\subset H^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
. This means that the Levi factor of the
Lie algebra
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
has nontrivial elements outside
H^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
.

2.
G
-tables

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an algebra (not necessarily associative) over a field of characteristic zero
\mathbb{K}
.
If
G
is a group and
G
acts on
\mathfrak{A}
by algebra automorphisms in such a way that
\mathfrak{A}
is a direct sum of finite dimensional irreducible
G
-submodules, we will say that
\mathfrak{A}
is a
G
-algebra
.
If
\mathfrak{A}
is a coalgebra, with comultiplication
\Delta
,
such that its dual
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
(with multiplication
\Delta^{*}
) is a
G
-algebra, we will say that
\mathfrak{A}
is a
G
-coalgebra
.

Recall that the

multiplication table
of
\mathfrak{A}
, with respect to a given
basis
B=\left\{e_{r}:r\in R\right\}
, is the set of scalars
c_{r_{1}r,_{2}}^{s}\in\mathbb{K}
satisfying

e_{r_{1}}e_{r_{2}}=\sum_{s\in R}c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}e_{s}

(for each pair
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
, the scalar
c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}
is zero for all but a finite number of
s\in R
).

For a
G
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, we will introduce
the concept of the
G
-table of
\mathfrak{A}
, which will be
a sort of multiplication table of
\mathfrak{A}
with respect to a given

G
-module decomposition of
\mathfrak{A}
in irreducible
G
-submodules.
Very roughly speaking, assume that

(2.1)

\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{r\in R}\mathfrak{A}_{r}

is a
G
-module decomposition of
\mathfrak{A}
with
\mathfrak{A}_{r}
an irreducible
G
-submodule of
\mathfrak{A}
. We want to define
the multiplication
G
-table of
\mathfrak{A}
, with respect to (
2.1
), as a set of scalars

c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}\in\mathbb{K}
satisfying (in some sense to be explained below)

(2.2)

\mathfrak{A}_{r_{1}}\cdot\mathfrak{A}_{r_{2}}=\sum_{s\in R}c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}\mathfrak{A}_{s}

(as earlier, for each pair
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
, the scalar
c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s}
will be zero for all but a finite number of
s\in R
).
In order to formalize this idea we need to introduce some preliminaries.

2.1.
Labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)

In what follows,
G
will be a group
such that every finite dimensional representation of
G
over

\mathbb{K}
is completely reducible. We are mainly thinking in a semisimple
algebraic group
G
over
\mathbb{K}
. We will denote by

\operatorname{Rep}(G)
the (tensor) category of finite dimensional representations of
G
.

In this context, we fix a set of representatives

\hat{G}=\left\{(\pi_{i},V_{i}):i\in\hat{I}\right\}

of the isomorphism classes of irreducible representations in
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
.
In addition, given
i_{1},i_{2},j\in\hat{I}
, we also fix a basis

\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}
of
\Hom_{G}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes V_{i_{2}},V_{j}\right)
, that is we choose
G
-morphisms,

m^{i_{1},i_{2},j}_{q}:V_{i_{1}}\otimes V_{i_{2}}\to V_{j},\qquad q=1,\dots,d^{i_{1},i_{2},j},

where

d^{i_{1},i_{2},j}=\dim\big(\Hom_{G}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes V_{i_{2}},V_{j}\right)\big)
,
and we set

\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}=\left\{m^{i_{1},i_{2},j}_{q}:q=1,\dots,d^{i_{1},i_{2},j}\right\}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the choice of the set
\hat{G}
of representatives and the choice of each basis

\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}
for
i_{1},i_{2},j\in\hat{I}
,
constitute a
labeling
of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
.
Occasionally, it might be enough, for our proposes, to consider only a subset

\hat{G}_{0}
of
\hat{G}
(corresponding to
\hat{I}_{0}\subset\hat{I}
) and to choose
\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}

only for some
i_{1},i_{2},j\in\hat{I}_{0}
.
We will say that this choice constitute a
partial labeling
of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
.

Note that giving a labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)

is almost the same as fixing a set of representatives
of
\hat{G}
and fixing a particular choice of the

3j
-symbols for the tensor category
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
.

Remark 2.2
.

In what follows we will frequently abuse
the notation identifying the index set
\hat{I}

(resp.
\hat{I}_{0}
) with
the set
\hat{G}
(resp.
\hat{G}_{0}
).

Remark 2.3
.

For some groups
G
it may happen that

\Hom_{G}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes V_{i_{2}},V_{j}\right)
is 1-dimensional for all
i_{1},i_{2},j\in\hat{I}_{0}
;
in these cases we will omit the subindex
q

running from
1
to
d^{i_{1},i_{2},j}=1
.
A particular instance of this situation is when
G

is a simply reducible group, see for instance
[
KY08
]
or
[
Ham12
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Although it is not mandatory, in many instances it
is convenient to choose the bases

\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}
so that, for all
q
, the
G
-morphisms

{m}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}_{q}
and

{m}^{i_{2},i_{1},j}_{q}
,
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
, coincide up to the canonical braiding

V_{i_{1}}\otimes V_{i_{2}}\to V_{i_{2}}\otimes V_{i_{1}}
.
Also, assuming that
i=0
corresponds to the trivial representation of
G
, it is also convenient to choose the bases

\mathcal{B}^{0,i,i}=\{{m}^{0,i,i}\}
and

\mathcal{B}^{i,0,i}=\{{m}^{i,0,i}\}
with

{m}^{0,i,i}(x\otimes v)={m}^{i,0,i}(v\otimes x)=xv

for all
x\in V_{0}=\mathbb{K}
and
v\in V_{i}
.
Note that, here, we have omitted the index
q=1
since

d^{0,i,i}=d^{i,0,i}=1
for all
i\in\hat{I}
.

Example 2.6
.

A partial labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}(GL(k,\mathbb{K}))
.

2.2.
G
-tables of algebras (and coalgebras)

Once
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
has been (partially) labeled, we are
almost ready to define (or compute) the

G
-table of
any
G
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
.
First, from the definition of a
G
-algebra, we can fix a decomposition

\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{r\in R}\mathfrak{A}_{r}.

into irreducible
G
-submodules.
Here
\mathfrak{A}_{r}
is finite dimensional and isomorphic to some
V_{i}\in\hat{G}
for some
i=\varepsilon(r)\in\hat{I}
.
Thus, we may choose a
G
-morphism

\tau_{r}:V_{\varepsilon(r)}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}

whose image is
\mathfrak{A}_{r}
. We denote by
\bar{\tau}_{r}:V_{\varepsilon(r)}\to\mathfrak{A}_{r}
the corresponding
G
-isomorphism.

Now, for each pair
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
, we consider the following basis of
\Hom_{G}\left(\mathfrak{A}_{r_{1}}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{r_{2}},\mathfrak{A}_{s}\right)
obtained from the labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}^{r_{1},r_{2},s}=\left\{\tilde{m}^{r_{1},r_{2},s}_{q}:q=1,\dots,d^{\varepsilon(r_{1}),\varepsilon(r_{2}),\varepsilon(s)}\right\},

where, for each
q=1,\dots,d^{\varepsilon(r_{1}),\varepsilon(r_{2}),\varepsilon(s)}
,

\tilde{m}^{r_{1},r_{2},s}_{q}=\bar{\tau}_{s}\circ m^{\varepsilon\left(r_{1}\right),\varepsilon\left(r_{2}\right),\varepsilon\left(s\right)}_{q}\circ\left(\bar{\tau}_{r_{1}}\otimes\bar{\tau}_{r_{2}}\right)^{-1}.

Let
\mu:\mathfrak{A}\otimes\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
denote the multiplication of
\mathfrak{A}
.
Since, for each pair
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
, the restriction

\mu_{r_{1},r_{2}}:\mathfrak{A}_{r_{1}}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{r_{2}}\to\mathfrak{A}

is a
G
-morphism,
we can express it as a linear combination of the elements in

\bigcup_{s\in R}\tilde{\mathcal{B}}^{r_{1},r_{2},s}
, that is

(2.7)

\mu_{r_{1},r_{2}}=\sum_{s\in R}\sum_{q=1}^{d^{\varepsilon(r_{1}),\varepsilon(r_{2}),\varepsilon(s)}}c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s,q}\;\tilde{m}^{r_{1},r_{2},s}_{q}

for some uniquely determined scalars

c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s,q}\in\mathbb{K}
.
Since
\mathfrak{A}_{r_{1}}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{r_{2}}
is finite dimensional,
it follows that, for each pair
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
,
only for a finite number of
s\in R
there are some

q\geq 1
such that
c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s,q}\neq 0
.

Summarizing, we give the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that the set
of scalars
c_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{s,q}\in\mathbb{K}
, defined by equation (
2.7
), is the
G
-table
of
\mathfrak{A}
.
We point out that a
G
-table depends on

(1)

The labeling of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
, that is the choice of
\hat{G}
and the choice of each basis

\mathcal{B}^{i_{1},i_{2},j}
for
i_{1},i_{2},j\in\hat{I}
.

(2)

The choice of the
G
-morphisms

\tau_{r}:V_{\varepsilon(r)}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}
,
r\in R
, so that

\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{r\in R}\tau_{r}(V_{\varepsilon(r)})
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a
G
-coalgebra, its
G
-cotable
is the

G
-table of
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
.

The
G
-table
(
2.7
) of
\mathfrak{A}

is a very synthetic way to explicitly
describe the multiplicative structure of
\mathfrak{A}
in combination with
its
G
-module structure.
It contains much more information than an ordinary
multiplication table. Indeed, since we labeled

\operatorname{Rep}(G)
, we have a an explicit description of
each
m^{i,j,k}_{q}
, and thus we
have the multiplication table of
each
m^{i,j,k}_{q}
, therefore it is straightforward to give the
ordinary multiplication table of
\mathfrak{A}
from its
G
-table
(
2.7
).

Remark 2.10

When some

Remark 3.2
.

Before proceeding with the proof, we point out that, even though the cup product is commutative in
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
, there are some entries that may look as indicating the opposite.
They are

\displaystyle H_{1}^{2,0}\vee H_{1}^{0,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}H_{0}^{2,2}-\frac{1}{2}H_{2}^{2,2}

while

\displaystyle H_{1}^{0,2}\vee H_{1}^{2,0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}H_{0}^{2,2}-\frac{1}{2}H_{2}^{2,2}

\displaystyle H_{1}^{2,0}\vee H_{1}^{1,3}

\displaystyle=-H_{0}^{3,3}

while

\displaystyle H_{1}^{1,3}\vee H_{1}^{2,0}

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{3,3}

\displaystyle H_{1}^{0,2}\vee H_{1}^{3,1}

\displaystyle=-H_{0}^{3,3}

while

\displaystyle H_{1}^{3,1}\vee H_{1}^{0,2}

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{3,3}.

All of these are due to the fact that the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{K})
-morphism

m^{1,1,0}:(\pi_{1},\mathbb{K}^{2})\otimes(\pi_{1},\mathbb{K}^{2})\to(\pi_{0},\mathbb{K}_{0})
is skew-symmetric (see Example
2.7
).

Proof.

In all the following computations it is important to recall the choice of the maps
\tau
that we have already made.
It is also useful to recall that
F.x_{1}=x_{-1}
and
F.x^{1}=-x^{-1}
.

We will organize the proof following the rows of Table
1
. Since the cup product in
H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})

is commutative, it is enough to consider the upper right triangle
of the table.
Also, since the zero products are easier to check than the non-zero ones (many of them are straightforward because there is no room for the potential result) we will concentrate in the non-zero entries.

Proof for the row corresponding to
H_{0}^{0,0}

Since

Proof for the row corresponding to
H_{0}^{1,1}
.
Here, the most subtle computation corresponds to the entry is

H_{0}^{1,1}\vee H_{0}^{1,1}=-6\,H_{0}^{2,2}

\big(x^{1}\otimes x_{-1}+x^{-1}\otimes x_{1}+2h^{0}\otimes h_{0}\big)\vee\big(x^{1}\otimes x_{-1}+x^{-1}\otimes x_{1}+2h^{0}\otimes h_{0}\big)=-6\,x^{-1}x^{1}\otimes x_{1}x_{-1}+4u

On other hand, since
{m}^{0,0,0}(1_{\mathbb{K}_{0}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{K}_{0}})=1_{\mathbb{K}_{0}}

\tilde{m}^{0,0,0}\big(x^{1}\otimes x_{-1}+x^{-1}\otimes x_{1}+2h^{0}\otimes h_{0}\,,\,x^{1}\otimes x_{-1}+x^{-1}\otimes x_{1}+2h^{0}\otimes h_{0}\big)=x^{-1}x^{1}\otimes x_{1}x_{-1}

-6\,\tilde{m}^{0,0,0}

Proof of

\displaystyle(x^{1}\otimes x_{1})\vee F^{2}.(x^{1}\otimes x_{1})

\displaystyle=-2(x^{1}\otimes x_{1})\vee(x^{-1}\otimes x_{-1})

\displaystyle=2(x^{1}x^{-1}\otimes x_{1}x_{-1})\qquad\text{(note the Koszul sign)}

\displaystyle=-2(x^{-1}x^{1}\otimes x_{1}x_{-1}).

On the other hand, we know that (see Example
2.7

m^{2,2,0}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\otimes F^{2}.\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=m^{2,2,0}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\otimes\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\

Remark 3.5
.

It is remarkable for us that the Lie algebra structure on

H_{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})
contains a semisimple
Lie subalgebra (in this case
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{K})
)
strictly larger than the Levi factor
of
\text{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, which in this case is
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{K})\subset H^{1,1}
.

As a by product of this computation, we discovered that

is a member of

(3.7)

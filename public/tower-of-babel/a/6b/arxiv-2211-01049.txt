In the current paper, we consider, for each natural number

c_{k}a_{n+k}+c_{k-1}a_{n+k-1}+\cdots+c_{1}a_{n+1}+c_{0}a_{n}=0

We now introduce a sequence of polynomials

(1)

(1)

(2)

(3)

p_{3}(x)=x^{8}-4x^{7}+4x^{6}+2x^{5}-5x^{4}+2x^{3}+x^{2}-x-1.

Initial values of the sequences

This section contains several computations related to the initial values of the sequences we are considering.

Lemma 3
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 2
and for each
n\in\{1,\cdots,2^{k}-2\}
we have
r_{k,n}^{0}=0
. Moreover,

r_{k,2^{k}-1}^{0}=1,\ \ \ \ r_{k,2^{k}}^{0}=2^{k-1}-1,\ \ \ r_{k,2^{k}+1}^{0}=\binom{2^{k-1}}{2}+2^{k-2}.

Proof.

Since
\{1,\cdots,2^{k}-1\}
is a maximal
k
-Schreier set, no proper subset can be a maximal
k
-Schreier set. This proves that
for each
n\in\{1,\cdots,2^{k}-2\}
, we have
r_{k,n}^{0}=0
and
r_{k,2^{k}-1}^{0}=1
. The equality
r_{k,2^{k}}^{0}=2^{k-1}-1
follows from the fact that
\{1,\cdots,2^{k}\}\setminus\{n\}
is a maximal
k
-Schreier set for each
n\in\{2^{k-1}+1,\cdots,2^{k}-1\}
, and every such set is of this form. To compute
r_{k,2^{k}+1}^{0}
, one easily checks that any set
F\in\mathcal{R}_{k,2^{k}+1}^{0}
has initial segment
\{1,\ldots,2^{k-2}\}
and is made by either deleting two numbers from the set
\{2^{k-1}+1,\cdots,2^{k}\}
or deleting one number from
\{2^{k-2}+1,\ldots,2^{k-1}\}
.
∎

For all

Lemma 5
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and for each
n\in\{1,\cdots,2^{k}-1\}
, we have
s_{k,n}=2^{n-1}
. Moreover,

s_{k,2^{k}}^{0}=2^{2^{k}-1}-1,\ \ \ \ s_{k,2^{k}+1}=2^{2^{k}}-(2^{k-1}+1).

Proof.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The set
\{1,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
is a maximal
k
-Schreier set. Therefore, every subset of
\{1,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
is
k
-Schreier set. Consequently, for each
n\in\{1,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
, we have
s_{k,n}=2^{n-1}
. On the other hand,
\{1,\ldots,2^{k}\}
is not a
k
-Schreier set, but every of its proper subset is a
k
-Schreier. Thus,
s_{k,2^{k}}=2^{2^{k}-1}-1

We compute
s_{k,2^{k}+1}
. We will show there are exactly
2^{k}+1
many subsets of
\{1,\ldots,2^{k}+1\}
with maximum element
2^{k}+1
that are not in
k\mathcal{S}
. Let
F
be such a set and assume the case that
F\not=\{1,\ldots,2^{k}+1\}
.

First observe that
\{1,\ldots,2^{k-1}\}\subseteq F
. Indeed, if this were not the case, then
\min E_{k}(F)>2^{k-1}
, which implies that
\max E_{k}(F)\geqslant 2^{k}+1
. Hence, since
F\not\in k\mathcal{S}
,
\max F>2^{k}+1
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\min E_{k}(F)=2^{k-1}
, and since
2^{k}-1\leqslant\max E_{k}(F)<2^{k}+1
, it follows that
F=\{1,\ldots,2^{k}+1\}\setminus\{n\}
for each
n\in\{2^{k-1}+1,\ldots,2^{k}\}
. Including the case that
F=\{1,\ldots,2^{k}+1\}
. We have a total of
2^{k}+1
many such sets. This is the desired result.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 2
. Then

d_{2^{k}-2}^{k}=\binom{2^{k-1}}{2}-2^{k-2},\ \ \ \ d^{k}_{2^{k}-1}=-2^{k-1}\ \ ,d^{k}_{2^{k}}=1

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. Since
p_{2}(x)=x^{4}-2x^{3}+x-1
, the base case
k=2
holds. Fix some integer
k
with
k\geqslant 3
and assume the identities hold for
k-1
. Let
m=2^{k-1}
. Then

p_{k}(x)=p_{k-1}(x(x-1))=x^{m}(x-1)^{m}-\frac{m}{2}x^{m-1}(x-1)^{m-1}+\cdots.

By inspection of the leading coefficients and using the binomial theorem, we have

p_{k}(x)=x^{2m}-mx^{2m-1}+\bigg(\binom{m}{2}-\frac{m}{2}\bigg)x^{2m-2}+\cdots.

This completes the inductive step of the proof.
∎

4.
Pascal-like sets and recursions

A set
\{r^{j}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}
is
Pascal-like
if for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\geqslant 3
and every
j\in\{0,\cdots,i-3\}
we have

r_{i}^{j}=r_{i-1}^{j}+r_{i-1}^{j+1}.

By definition, the Pascal-like set is determined by the values of the two main diagonals
(r^{i-1}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
(r^{i-1}_{i+1})_{i=1}^{\infty}
.
See
Figure

1
for an illustration.
In the next lemma, we express each element of the main diagonal in terms of the first column.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\{r_{i}^{j}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}
be Pascal-like set. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(5)

r_{n}^{n-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-j}\binom{n-1}{j}r^{0}_{n+j}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. We shall prove that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, any Pascal-like set satisfies (
5
). For
n=1
, the assertion is clearly satisfied. Fix
n\geqslant 2
and assume that (
5
) holds replacing
n
with
n-1
for any Pascal-like set. Fix a Pascal-like set
\{r_{i}^{j}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{1,\cdots,i-1\}\}
and define
b_{i}^{j}=r_{i+1}^{j+1}
. Then
\{b_{i}^{j}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}
is also a Pascal-like set. By the inductive assumption, we have

\displaystyle r_{n}^{n-1}=b_{n-1}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-2-j}\binom{n-2}{j}b_{n-1+j}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-2-j}\binom{n-2}{j}r^{1}_{n+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-2-j}\binom{n-2}{j}(r^{0}_{n+j+1}-r^{0}_{n+j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-j}\binom{n-2}{j-1}r^{0}_{n+j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-1-j}\binom{n-2}{j}r^{0}_{n+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-j}\binom{n-1}{j}r^{0}_{n+j}.

Notice that, in the fourth equality, we used the definition of a Pascal-like set.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\{r_{i}^{j}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}
be a Pascal-like set. If
(r_{i}^{i-1})_{i=1}^{\infty}

(r_{i+1}^{i-1})_{i=1}^{\infty}

Proof.

We sketch the easy proof.
The Pascal-like property yields that

(r^{i-1}_{i+2})_{i=1}^{\infty}
must satisfy the same recursions as both

and

Lemma 10
.

Suppose that
\{r^{j}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}

Proof.

Let

Fix some non-negative integer

so that
\sum_{i=1}^{2k+1}d^{\prime}_{i-1}r^{0}_{\ell+i}=0
(for each non-negative integer
\ell
). We wish to show that
d^{\prime}_{i}=d_{i}
. Rather than a messy calculation, a simple way to see this is by converting to the characteristic polynomial and matching the coefficients. That is, replace
r^{0}_{\ell+\alpha}
by
x^{\alpha-1}
in (
7
) and apply the binomial theorem to obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{i}{j}x^{i+j}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}x^{i}(x-1)^{i}=p(x(x-1))=\sum_{i=0}^{2k}d_{i}x^{i}.

Therefore
d^{\prime}_{i}=d_{i}
as desired.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let

\{r^{j}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots,i-1\}\}
be Pascal-like so that
(r_{i}^{i-1})_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
(r_{i+1}^{i-1})_{i=1}^{\infty}
both satisfy a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
p(x)
. Let
(d_{i})_{i=0}^{2k}
be the coefficients of the polynomial
p(x(x-1))
and
t_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}r^{0}_{i}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose

(8)

\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}t_{j}=0.

Then
(t_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a linear recurrence relation with characteristic polynomial
p(x(x-1))
.

Proof.

Set all the notation as in the statement of the lemma. We proceed by induction to show that for each non-negative integer
\ell
.

(9)

\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}t_{\ell+j}=0

The base case of the induction
\ell=0
is assumed – (
8
).

Suppose that for some
\ell\geqslant 0
, (
9
) holds. We will show that (
9
) holds for
\ell+1
.
By
Lemma

10
,
(r^{0}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the recurrence relation with characteristic polynomial
p(x(x-1))
. Using this fact and the induction hypothesis, we have the following

(10)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}t_{\ell+1+j}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}(t_{\ell+1+j}-t_{\ell+j})+\sum_{i=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}t_{\ell+j}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}r^{0}_{\ell+1+j}+\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}d_{j-1}t_{\ell+j}=0+0=0\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

r_{k+1,n+1}^{n}=t_{k,n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{k,i}^{0}.

Proof.

First, recall that the upper index
0
in the expression
r_{k,n}^{0}
indicates counting maximal
k\mathcal{S}
sets. Observe that every element of
\mathcal{R}_{k+1,n+1}^{n}
is the union of two sets: The first is a maximal
k\mathcal{S}
set with a maximum at most
n-1
. The second is
\{n+1\}
. Consider the following map

\mathcal{R}_{k+1,n+1}^{n}\ni F\mapsto F\backslash\{n+1\}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{R}_{k,i}^{0}.

The map is clearly bijective ("
i
" can be interpreted as the second largest element of
F
) and the families under the union in the co-domain are pairwise disjoint.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geqslant 3
, and
d\in\{0,\cdots,n-3\}
. Then

r_{k,n}^{d}=r_{k,n-1}^{d}+r_{k,n-1}^{d+1}.

Hence, the set
\{r_{k,i}^{j}:i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{0,\cdots i-1\}\}
is a Pascal-like. Moreover,
r^{j-1}_{k,j+1}=0
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}:=\{F\in\mathcal{R}_{k,n}^{d}\ :\ n-1\in F\},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{T}_{2}:=\{F\in\mathcal{R}_{k,n}^{d}\ :\ n-1\notin F\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}:=\{F\backslash\{n\}\ :\ F\in\mathcal{T}_{1}\},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}:=\{F\backslash\{n\}\cup\{n-1\}\ :\ F\in\mathcal{T}_{2}\}.

Clearly, we have
|\mathcal{T}_{1}|=|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}|
and
|\mathcal{T}_{2}|=|\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}|
. Hence,
r_{k,n}^{d}=|\mathcal{T}_{1}|+|\mathcal{T}_{2}|=|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}|
. Consider some
F\in\mathcal{R}_{k,n}^{d}
, we claim that
|E_{k}(F)|>1
. If
E_{k}(F)=\{n\}
, then
d_{k}(F)=n-1
, but we assumed that
d<n-1
, which is a contradiction. Comparing the sets
E_{k}(F)
and
E_{k}(F^{\prime})
, where
F^{\prime}
is the set
F
modified in one of two presented ways, it can be easily checked that
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}_{k,n-1}^{d+1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}_{k,n-1}^{d}
. This concludes the proof of the first part.

We will show that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{k,n+1}^{n-1}=\emptyset
. We know that if
F\in\mathcal{R}_{k,n+1}^{n-1}
we have
n+1\in E_{k}(F)
and, by definition,
d_{k}(F)=n-1
. We obtain contradictions for all possible values of
\min E_{k}(F)
. If
\min E_{k}(F)\leqslant n
, using that
n+1\in E_{k}(F)
, we have
d_{k}(F)\leqslant n-2
. If
n+1=\min E_{k}(F)
, then
d_{k}(F)=n
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geqslant 2
. We have

s_{k,n}=2s_{k,n-1}-r_{k,n-1}^{0}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{F\cup\{n\}\ :\ F\in(k\mathcal{S})^{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{k,n-1}^{0}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{F\backslash\{n-1\}\cup\{n\}\ :\ F\in(k\mathcal{S})^{n-1}\}.

We claim that
(k\mathcal{S})^{n}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}
. Then, by the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}=\emptyset
, we obtain

s_{k,n}=|(k\mathcal{S})^{n}|=|\mathcal{T}_{1}|+|\mathcal{T}_{2}|=(s_{k,n-1}-r_{k,n-1}^{0})+s_{k,n-1}.

For each
G\in(k\mathcal{S})^{n}
we have
F=G\backslash\{n\}\cup\{n-1\}\in(k\mathcal{S})^{n-1}
.
If
n-1\in G
,
F=G\setminus\{n\}
. Since
G\in(k\mathcal{S})^{n}
,
F
is not a maximal
k
-Schreier set. Hence,
G\in T_{1}
. If
n-1\not\in G
,
G\in T_{2}
. It follows that
(k\mathcal{S})^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}
. The inclusion
\mathcal{T}_{2}\subseteq(k\mathcal{S})^{n}
is obvious. It suffices to prove that
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq(k\mathcal{S})^{n}
, however, this is almost obvious – adding one element larger than the maximum to a non-maximal
k
-Schreier produces a
k
-Schreier set.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We claim that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
(s_{k,n})
satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
p_{k}(x)
. This claim has already been established in previous work for
k=1
; however, we will not use this statement directly. Instead, we prove the following statement holds for each natural number
k
.

(P
k

The sequences
(r^{0}_{k,i})_{i=1}^{\infty},(t_{k,i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfy a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
p_{k}(x)
.

Consider the base case
k=1
. Note that
r_{1,n}^{n-1}=1
for each

since
F\in\mathcal{R}_{1,n}^{n-1}
if and only if

. Therefore
(r_{1,n}^{n-1})
satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial

and the same holds for
(r_{1,n+1}^{n-1})_{n=1}^{\infty}
since this sequence is identically 0 (Lemma
13
). Therefore by Lemma
10
,
(r^{0}_{1,i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial
p_{0}(x(x-1))=x(x-1)-1=p_{1}(x)
. Notice that (
8
) is satisfied by Lemma
7
for
k=1
. Therefore we may apply Lemma
11
to see that
(t_{1,i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial

. Therefore (P
1
) holds.

Fix a positive integer
k
and assume that (P
k
)
holds; we will prove that (P
k+1
) holds.
By Lemma
12
, we have that

t_{k,i}=r_{k+1,i+1}^{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
(r_{k+1,i+1}^{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial

. We want to show that

satisfies a linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial

By Lemma

Fix

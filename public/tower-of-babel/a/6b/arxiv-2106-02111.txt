Efficient ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} synchronization on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} under symmetry-preserving side information
Ahmed El Alaoui

Abstract
We consider ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -synchronization on the Euclidean lattice. Every vertex of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} is assigned an independent symmetric random sign θ u \theta_{u} , and for every edge ( u , v ) (u,v) of the lattice, one observes the product θ u ​ θ v \theta_{u}\theta_{v} flipped independently with probability p p . The task is to reconstruct products θ u ​ θ v \theta_{u}\theta_{v} for pairs of vertices u u and v v which are arbitrarily far apart. Abbé, Massoulié, Montanari, Sly and Srivastava (2018) showed that synchronization is possible if and only if p p is below a critical threshold p ~ c ​ ( d ) \tilde{p}_{c}(d) , and efficiently so for p p small enough. We augment this synchronization setting with a model of side information preserving the sign symmetry of θ \theta , and propose an efficient algorithm which synchronizes a randomly chosen pair of far away vertices on average, up to a differently defined critical threshold p c ​ ( d ) p_{c}(d) . We conjecture that p c ​ ( d ) = p ~ c ​ ( d ) p_{c}(d)=\tilde{p}_{c}(d) for all d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . Our strategy is to renormalize the synchronization model in order to reduce the effective noise parameter, and then apply a variant of the multiscale algorithm of AMMSS. The success of the renormalization procedure is conditional on a plausible but unproved assumption about the regularity of the free energy of an Ising spin glass model on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} .



1
Introduction and main results

In this paper we study a
group synchronization
problem. Formulated generally, this is the task of estimating a sequence of group–valued random variables up to a global shift from noisy measurements of their differences. This problem arises in several applications such as community detection, where individuals belong to well-defined communities and observations come in the form of pairwise affinities between individuals
[
KN11
]
, time synchronization in sensor networks where the goal is to adjust the local clock of each sensor given noisy clock differences
[
HCMC10
]
, computer vision and microscopy, where the task is to estimate the shape of a moving object from multiple noisy snapshots of it
[
SS11
]
.

This problem has mostly been studied in settings where the set of compared pairs forms a “unstructured” graph such as the complete graph or a random graph from the Erdös-Rényi model. The analysis and the algorithms proposed in such cases rely on the high degree of exchangeability of the variables involved in the problem. A prime example being
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
synchronization on the complete graph, where the observations can be put together in a matrix having the form of a rank-one-perturbed Wigner matrix; a well studied object for which powerful tools from random matrix theory and mean-field methods originating in statistical physics are readily available. These methods will typically fail on more structured graphs having geometric features such as finite-dimensional lattices.
Furthermore, studying signal recovery problems on structured graphs can potentially be relevant to applications such as the ones mentioned above where, due to resource constraints or simply the problem’s nature, only a limited number of comparisons can be measured.

To our knowledge, Abbé, Massoulié, Montanari, Sly and Srivastava
[
AMM+18
]
were the first to consider the synchronization problem on the Euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for compact groups. They proved for
d\geq 3
that synchronization is possible with an efficient algorithm if the noise is weak enough and impossible in the opposite regime where the noise is strong. (They also treat the case
d=2
in their paper, however this case is special, as the structure of the group plays an important role in the results.) By monotonicity with respect to the value of the noise, this establishes the existence of a critical value where recovery is possible below it and impossible above. We call this threshold the
information-theoretic
threshold, or limit, of synchronization. In this paper we ask the question

Is group synchronization on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
possible with an
efficient
algorithm up the information-theoretic limit?

We answer this question in the affirmative in the case of the simplest non trivial group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{-1,+1\}
, for all dimensions
d\geq 2
, assuming one has access to a vanishing amount of ‘side information’ providing extra help in the synchronization task. We should mention that contrary to ‘mean-field models’ as discussed above, no ‘explicit’ characterization of this information-theoretic threshold is known on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Therefore whatever strategy aiming to answer the above question has to be content with the implicit definition of the threshold, as we do in this paper, and build an algorithm based on it.

We now set up the mathematical problem.
Let
\theta=(\theta_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be i.i.d. symmetric
\pm 1
random variables assigned to the vertices of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The edges of the lattice
\mathbb{E}^{d}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|u-v|=1\}
(
|\cdot|
is the
\ell_{2}
distance in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
are assigned random variables

\displaystyle Y^{\delta}_{uv}=\begin{cases}+\theta_{u}\theta_{v}&\mbox{with probability }1-p,\\
-\theta_{u}\theta_{v}&\mbox{with probability }p,\end{cases}

(1.1)

independently for every
(u,v)\in\mathbb{E}^{d}
conditional on
\theta
, where
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
is a fixed parameter. We let
\delta=1-2p
so that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{uv}^{\delta}|\theta_{u},\theta_{v}]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{uv}^{\delta}|\theta_{u}\theta_{v}]=\delta\theta_{u}\theta_{v}
.

Let
\Lambda_{n}=[-n,n]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite box of side length
2n+1
and let
E_{n}=\mathbb{E}^{d}\cap\Lambda_{n}^{2}
be its set of edges. We will informally denote the graph
(\Lambda_{n},E_{n})
by its vertex set
\Lambda_{n}
.
Let us also denote the set of observations in the box
\Lambda_{n}
by
Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta}=\{Y_{uv}^{\delta}\,:\,u,v\in\Lambda_{n}\}
.

Due to sign symmetry, it is not possible to distinguish
\theta
from
-\theta
from the knowledge of the edge observations
Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta}
. The
synchronization problem
is as follows: given
Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta}
and possibly a small amount of side information as clarified below, we want to produce estimates
T_{uv}
that approximate the product
\theta_{u}\theta_{v}
with non-trivial error for ‘most’ pairs of vertices
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
.

We assume that side information comes in the form of pairwise measurements according to the
‘spiked GOE’
model

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T):=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[T_{uv}(Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L})\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\Big],

The presence of side information (

\theta_{\Lambda_{n}}=(\theta_{u})_{u\in\Lambda_{n}}
. Our results will be stated in the regime
L\to\infty

Before considering the algorithmic question, we first define the information-theoretic limits of synchronization. For the purpose of establishing some key definitions, we consider the situation where side information of the form (
1.2
) is available for every pair
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
; this is equivalent to
L=L_{n}=2n+1
.
We now define the average pair correlations

\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}:=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L_{n}}\big]^{2}\Big].

(1.4)

Note that
\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}=\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T^{\mbox{\rm\tiny Bayes}})
where
T^{\mbox{\rm\tiny Bayes}}_{uv}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L_{n}}\big]
is the Bayes-optimal estimator.

Proposition 1.1
.

The sequence
(\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}})_{n\geq 1}
has a limit
q_{\star}^{2}(\delta,\eta)
for all
\delta\in[0,1]
and all except countably many
\eta\geq 0
. Moreover, the maps
\delta\mapsto q_{\star}(\delta,\eta)
and
\eta\mapsto q_{\star}(\delta,\eta)
are non-decreasing.

The proof of Proposition
1.1
can be found in Section
6.2
. Let us denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}

\eta
(for a given
\delta

(\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}})_{n}
fails to converge, as per Proposition
1.1
. Then the following limit exists for all
\delta\in[0,1]
and is non-decreasing in
\delta
:

q_{\star\star}(\delta):=\lim_{\underset{\eta\notin\mathcal{D}_{\delta}}{\eta\to 0^{+}}}q_{\star}(\delta,\eta).

(1.5)

We now define
the synchronization threshold

\delta_{c}=\inf\{\delta>0~:~q_{\star\star}(\delta)>0\}.

(1.6)

The next two results show that synchronization is efficiently possible above
\delta_{c}
and impossible below (efficiently or otherwise). The synchronization result is conditional on an unproved but highly plausible assumption which we state in Section
6

1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Conditional on Assumption
1
, for all
d\geq 2
and all
\delta>\delta_{c}
there exists
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(d,\delta)>0
such that for almost all
\eta\leq\eta_{0}
, there exists
L_{0}=L_{0}(\delta,d,\eta)\geq 1
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(\delta,d,\eta)
such that the following holds. For all
n
there exists a randomized estimator
T^{(n)}=(T^{(n)}_{uv})_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}
where
T^{(n)}_{uv}=T^{(n)}_{uv}(Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L})\in\{-1,+1\}
with runtime
\mathcal{O}(n^{2d})
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
L\geq L_{0}
,

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}\big(T^{(n)}\big)\geq\frac{9}{10}q_{\star\star}^{2}(\delta).

The above result establishes the existence of an algorithm whose running time is quadratic in the size of the problem and which succeeds at synchronizing a randomly chosen pair of variables on average, immediately above the synchronization threshold
\delta_{c}
, when given an arbitrarily small amount of side information as per Eq. (
1.2
). (Note that the interaction range
L
in the side information does not depend on
n

Moreover the synchronization accuracy is near optimal (the constant
\frac{9}{10}
is arbitrary and can be replaced by any other constant
c_{0}<1
.)
We next answer the natural question of whether synchronization is possible below
\delta_{c}
. We show that this is not the case in the following sense.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\delta<\delta_{c}
and
L=L_{n}\geq 1
be any sequence of integers depending on
n
. There exists a set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
of full Lebesgue measure such that the following holds. For any sequence of estimators
T^{(n)}=(T^{(n)}_{uv})_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}
where
T^{(n)}_{uv}=T^{(n)}_{uv}(Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L_{n}})\in\{-1,+1\}
, we have

\lim_{\underset{\eta\in\mathcal{A}}{\eta\to 0^{+}}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big|\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}\big(T^{(n)}\big)\big|=0.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section

1.3

2
Discussion and related work

As mentioned earlier, there is a wealth of results for reconstruction problems on the complete graph, sparse and dense regular and Erdös-Rényi random graphs and trees. See for instance
[
Sin11
,
SW12
,
Bou16
,
BSS13
,
BBS17
,
CC18
,
Abb17
]
and references therein for work using various methods for group synchronization and its applications. However, the study of reconstruction on structured graph models with a lesser degree of exchangeability is fairly limited. A few pointers to the latter category are
[
GRSY15
,
AMM+18
,
SB18
,
ABRS18
,
ABA20
,
PW20
]
.
The former class of models, in addition to being analytically tractable, is also known to exhibit an information-computation gap where in a certain regime of parameters, reconstruction (in our case, synchronization) is information-theoretically possible but all known efficient (polynomial-time) algorithms fail at it.

Local algorithm with BEC.
In contrast, Montanari and the author showed in
[
AM19
]
that on amenable graphs, and under a stronger model of side information where one receives the value of
\theta_{u}
with small probability independently for each vertex
u
—this is called the binary erasure channel (BEC)—optimal reconstruction is possible in a certain asymptotic sense by means of an efficient local algorithm. Therefore, reconstruction on amenable graphs with BEC side information does not exhibit a statistical-computational gap. The local algorithm uses the side information from the BEC in a crucial way to induce, asymptotically, a decoupling among the variables
\theta_{u}
. Amenability then implies that observations beyond a receding boundary around a given vertex
u
carries vanishing information about its spin
\theta_{u}
. Therefore, under BEC, it is possible to estimate each
\theta_{u}
by only using information in a ball of bounded radius around
u
. (Synchronization is then trivial as one can return an estimate for
\theta_{u}\theta_{v}
by multiplying the estimates corresponding to
\theta_{u}
and
\theta_{v}
respectively.)
The present model of side information (
1.2
) is weaker than BEC. In particular the sign symmetry of the variables
\theta_{u}
is not broken given the observations
Y^{\delta,\eta,L}_{\Lambda_{n}}
, so one cannot estimate individual spins better than random and the strategy employed in
[
AM19
]
fails. One can instead focus on the task of synchronizing two faraway vertices.

Synchronization via a multiscale algorithm.
Our algorithm is based on a multiscale procedure similar to the one used in
[
AMM+18
]
. Our main innovation concerns the base layer of the multiscale hierarchy: we partition the lattice into overlapping patches of constant size (see Figure
1
) and construct a
renormalized
instance of the synchronization problem where patches play the role of vertices, i.e., we assign a global spin to each patch and construct a new synchronization variables between every two neighboring patches. We exploit properties of the posterior measure of
\theta
when augmented with the side information to show that the effective noise parameter for the renormalized instance can be made as small as one desires by making the patches large enough.
We then adapt the multiscale algorithm of
[
AMM+18
]
and apply it to this renormalized instance, therefore synchronizing any two vertices by traveling the path connecting them in tree encoding the multiscale hierarchy. The analysis of this second stage also requires significant effort as the renormalized instance is a more complicated graphical model than the original synchronization instance.

The synchronization threshold.
The authors of
[
AMM+18
]
consider a definition of synchronization slightly different from ours. The latter is considered possible if one can synchronize an
arbitrary
pair of far away vertices, and the synchronization threshold is defined as

\tilde{\delta}_{c}:=\inf\Big\{\delta>0~:~\liminf_{|u-v|\to\infty}\,\sup_{T_{uv}}\,d_{\mbox{\rm\tiny TV}}\Big(\text{Law}\big(T_{uv}(Y^{\delta}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\big),\bar{\nu}\Big)>0\Big\},

where
Y^{\delta}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}=\{Y^{\delta}_{uv}:(u,v)\in\mathbb{E}^{d}\}
,
T_{uv}:\{-1,+1\}^{\mathbb{E}^{d}}\to\{-1,+1\}
measurable,
\bar{\nu}
is the uniform distribution over
\{-1,+1\}
, and
d_{\mbox{\rm\tiny TV}}
is the total variation distance.
This definition relies only on the lattice observations
Y^{\delta}_{uv},(u,v)\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and not on any side information. We find it more convenient to work with our definition of
\delta_{c}
, Eq. (
1.6
), which is more suitable to algorithmic reasoning. Nevertheless, in light of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
we find it plausible to conjecture that the equality
\tilde{\delta}_{c}=\delta_{c}
holds for all
d\geq 2
.

The Ising spin glass.

In the absence of measurements on the edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, i.e.,
\delta=0
, the quantity
q_{\star}(0,\eta)
in Proposition
1.1
has a well understood, rather explicit expression
[
LKZ15
,
LM17
]
. In particular it is known that
q_{\star}(0,\eta)=0
for all
\eta\leq 1
(therefore
q_{\star\star}(0)=0
) and
q_{\star}(0,\eta)>0
for
\eta>1
.
When
\delta>0
, little is known about
q_{\star}(\delta,\eta)
. For instance, the pure lattice case
\eta=0
is of interest to statistical physics, since the posterior measure of
\theta
is equivalent to the Ising spin glass measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, on the so-called Nishimori line: A simple application of the Bayes rule reveals

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\theta|Y^{\delta}_{\Lambda_{n}})\propto e^{\beta\sum_{(u,v)\in E_{n}}Y^{\delta}_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}},~~~\beta=\frac{1}{2}\log\big(\frac{1-p}{p}\big).

Let us write
Y^{\delta}_{uv}=Z_{uv}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}
where
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{uv}=+1)=1-p=1-\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{uv}=-1)
as per Eq. (
1.1
) (the added subscript in
\theta_{0}
is to distinguish it from a generic vector
\theta
in the above formula). Let us also define
\sigma_{u}=\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}
for all
u\in\Lambda_{n}
. Then the push-forward of
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|Y^{\delta}_{\Lambda_{n}})
by
\theta\to\sigma
is of the form

\mu_{\Lambda_{n}}(\sigma)\propto e^{\beta\sum_{(u,v)\in E_{n}}Z_{uv}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}}.

The relation
\beta=\frac{1}{2}\log\big(\frac{1-p}{p}\big)
between the inverse temperature and the bias of the disorder variables
Z_{uv}
defines a curve in the plane
(p,\beta)
called the Nishimori line; see also
[
Nis01
, Chapter 4]
. Furthermore,
\delta_{c}=1-2p_{c}
as defined in (
1.6
) corresponds to
the tri-critical point

(p_{c},\beta_{c})
conjecturally separating three distinct phases of the model.
A few predictions are available for its location in
d=2
: Nonrigorous analytical considerations based on the replica method and special symmetries of the model have lead the authors of
[
MNN03
]
to put forward the conjecture that
p_{c}
solves the equation
-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)=\frac{\log 2}{2}
with
p<\frac{1}{2}
, which leads to
p_{c}\simeq 0.110028
, i.e.,
\delta_{c}^{2}\simeq 0.608312
. However Ohzeki
[
Ohz09
]
suggests that this is only an approximation which can be further improved via a renormalization group analysis, and they advance the value
p_{c}\simeq 0.109168
(
\delta_{c}^{2}\simeq 0.610939
). On the other hand, Monte-Carlo simulations for
d=2
made by Toldin, Pelissetto and Vicari
[
TPV09
]
seem to agree with the theoretical predictions to a good extent and indicate that
p_{c}\simeq 0.10917
(
\delta_{c}^{2}\simeq 0.61099
.)

On the rigorous mathematical side, explicit bounds on
\tilde{\delta}_{c}
are known in the recent literature: It was shown in
[
AMM+18
]
that
p_{\mbox{\rm\tiny perc}}(d)\leq\tilde{\delta}_{c}<1
, where
p_{\mbox{\rm\tiny perc}}(d)
be the critical threshold of Bernoulli bond percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Abbé and Boix
[
ABA20
]
, and Polyanskiy and Wu
[
PW20
]
independently introduced a more refined
information–percolation
argument implying that synchronization is impossible if
\delta^{2}\leq p_{\mbox{\rm\tiny perc}}(d)
, i.e.,
\tilde{\delta}_{c}^{2}\geq p_{\mbox{\rm\tiny perc}}(d)
. The iquadratic mprovement comes from a comparison with a more accurate, potentially inhomogeneous, percolation model where an edge
(u,v)\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
is open with probability
I_{2}(Y^{\delta}_{uv};\theta_{u},\theta_{v})
,
I_{2}
being the mutual information in
\chi^{2}
divergence. In the two-dimensional case
d=2
, this latter bound reads
\tilde{\delta}_{c}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, to be compared with the above-mentioned predictions.

At the opposite end of the spectrum, one would expect mean-field behavior when
d
is large, and the critical point of the model should approach its analogue on the infinite regular tree of the same degree, which this given by the Kesten-Stigum threshold for reconstruction on the
2d
-regular tree with binary alphabet
[
EKPS00
]
. This leads to a natural conjecture:
\tilde{\delta}_{c}^{2}\sim 1/(2d)
as
d\to\infty
.

3
The synchronization algorithm

In this section we describe the synchronization algorithm which underlies Theorem
1.2
.

1. Partitioning.

We first cover the lattice
\Lambda_{n}
with pairwise interlocking blocks:
We consider a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with translates of the cube
C=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
along the coordinate directions.
We put translates of the cube
\frac{1}{3}C
at the midpoint of every edge of the integer lattice: these are all sets
\frac{1}{3}C+(k+\frac{1}{2})e_{i}
where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{d})
is the standard unit basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We call these translates
joints
. Note that each joint overlaps with two adjacent elements of the tiling.
We define a
block
to be the union of an element of the tiling with its
2d
surrounding joints; see Figure
1
(left). Hence two adjacent blocks have a non empty intersection which is precisely the joint between them; see Figure
2
. We also define the
core
of a block
A
as
A^{\bullet}=A\setminus\cup J
where the union is over all joints surrounding
A
; see Figure
1
(right).
Now we dilate all distances by a factor
L^{\circlearrowleft}
and then intersect the dilated sets with
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

\mathbb{B}=\Big\{(L^{\circlearrowleft}\cdot A)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}~:~A\mbox{ is a block}\Big\},\quad\mathbb{J}=\Big\{(L^{\circlearrowleft}\cdot J)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}~:~J\mbox{ is a joint}\Big\}.

The parameter
L^{\circlearrowleft}\geq 1
is an integer representing the scale of the renormalization. We retain the terminology of blocks, joints and cores for the elements of
\mathbb{B}
, the elements of
\mathbb{J}
and blocks deprived of their surrounding joints, respectively.
We define the
intersection graph

\mathbb{L}=(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{J})
to have blocks as its vertices and joints as its edges;
\mathbb{L}
is clearly isomorphic to the integer lattice. We write
B\sim B^{\prime}
for adjacency in
\mathbb{L}
.
Finally, we let
\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{B\in\mathbb{B}:B\cap\Lambda_{n}\neq\emptyset\}
. For convenience we ignore the blocks
B
that fall on the boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
, as their contribution to
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}
, Eq. (
1.3
), is negligible.

We set the range of the GOE interaction (
1.2
) to be
L=2L^{\circlearrowleft}
. This insures that an observation
Y^{\eta,L}_{xy}
is available for every two vertices
x,y\in B
for every block
B\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

2. Processing side information.

Without loss of generality we can assume we have access to an arbitrary finite number of independent copies of the GOE side information
Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L}
, Eq.(
1.2
) with arbitrary SNR
\eta

4.1
An overview of the main ideas of the proof

The proof of Theorem

3.1

7

\eta
) of the

associated to a block

and to the union of two adjacent blocks

, in the limit where the volume of

and
B^{\prime}
tends to infinity. These free energies are the (properly normalized) expected logarithms of the partition functions of the posterior measures of

given

, and
(\theta_{u})_{u\in B\cup B^{\prime}}
given

respectively, see e.g., Eq. (

). We will show that these free energies converge to certain variational formulas involving the limiting free energy of the posterior measure of
\theta_{\Lambda_{n}}
given the lattice measurements
Y^{\delta}_{\Lambda_{n}}
together with a side information where one observes the spin
\theta_{u}
of every vertex
u
corrupted by an
independent
Gaussian noise

. These results are stated and discussed in Section
6
, and proved in Section

. The convexity of the free energies with respect to
\eta
allows to deduce convergence and concentration of the overlap

in (

As for
M_{B^{\bullet}}
and

overlap locking property
by varying the parameter

. For the sake of this discussion, suppose we have a sequence of graphs

of growing size, and let
(A_{n},B_{n})
be a partition of

into two subsets such that

. Each vertex

drawn independently at random form a distribution

, and we receive an observation

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{t\lambda}{|V_{n}|}}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+Z_{uv}~~~\mbox{for all}~u,v\in V_{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{uv}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{|A_{n}|}}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+Z_{uv}^{\prime}~~~\mbox{for all}~u,v\in A_{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{uv}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{|B_{n}|}}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+Z_{uv}^{\prime\prime}~~~\mbox{for all}~u,v\in B_{n},

\phi_{n}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{|V_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\int\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{n}}Q(Y_{uv}|\theta_{u},\theta_{v})\cdot e^{H_{n}(\theta)}\prod_{u\in V_{n}}\mathrm{d}p_{0}(\theta_{u}),

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in V_{n}}\sqrt{\frac{t\lambda}{|V_{n}|}}\tilde{Y}_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}-\frac{t\lambda}{2|V_{n}|}\theta_{u}^{2}\theta_{v}^{2},

\displaystyle H_{A_{n}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in A_{n}}\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{|A_{n}|}}\tilde{Y}_{uv}^{\prime}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}-\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{2|A_{n}|}\theta_{u}^{2}\theta_{v}^{2},

\displaystyle H_{B_{n}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in B_{n}}\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{|B_{n}|}}\tilde{Y}_{uv}^{\prime\prime}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}-\frac{(1-t)\lambda}{2|B_{n}|}\theta_{u}^{2}\theta_{v}^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2|V_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big\langle|V_{n}|R_{1,2}(V_{n})^{2}-|A_{n}|R_{1,2}(A_{n})^{2}-|B_{n}|R_{1,2}(B_{n})^{2}\Big\rangle

Next, showing
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]\longrightarrow q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta)
requires one more ingredient, of an operator-theoretic flavor. Let us first expand the expression at hand:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}||B||B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B,z\in B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}^{B}\theta_{x}^{B^{\prime}}\theta_{y}^{B}\theta_{y}\theta_{z}^{B^{\prime}}\theta_{z}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}||B||B^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B,z\in B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{3}}\sum_{x,y,z\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

We observe that this is equal to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{3})
, where

is the

\chi_{xy}=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big].

On the other hand we know that, say, from Proposition

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{2})=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]\longrightarrow q_{\star\star}^{2}(\delta).

\var(|M_{B}|)=o_{L^{\circlearrowleft},\eta}(1)

5
The multiscale scheme

We now present the multiscale algorithm and prove Theorem
4.2
.

5.1
The algorithm

1. Hierarchical partioning.

We construct a hierarchy of partitions
(\mathcal{B}^{(k)})_{k\geq 0}
of the rescaled lattice
\mathbb{L}
which we define recursively.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{(0)}=\mathbb{B}
and
\mathbb{L}^{(0)}=\mathbb{L}
. Let
\mathbf{0}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{B}
is the block centered at the origin.
Given
\mathbb{L}^{(k)}
which we assume is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{d})
(this is already the case at level
k=0
), we construct
\mathbb{L}^{(k+1)}
satisfying the same property. Let
d_{k}
be the
\ell_{\infty}
distance in
\mathbb{L}^{(k)}
, which is well defined by isomorphism to
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{d})
.
Now we fix a integer constant
\kappa\geq 1
and define the origin of
\mathcal{B}^{(k+1)}
as

\mathbf{0}^{(k+1)}=\Big\{B\in\mathcal{B}^{(k)}:d_{k}\big(B^{(k)},\mathbf{0}^{(k)}\big)\leq\kappa(k+1)^{2}\Big\},

and let

\mathcal{B}^{(k+1)}=\Big\{\mathbf{0}^{(k+1)}+m\ell_{k}e_{i}~:~m\in\mathbb{Z},~i=1,\cdots,d\Big\},

where
e_{i}
are the coordinate directions of unit length in
\mathbb{L}^{(k)}
. Two elements
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of
\mathcal{B}^{(k+1)}
are adjacent, and we write
B_{1}\sim B_{2}
, if one is a translate of the other by
\ell_{k}e_{i}
for some
i
. This completely defines
\mathbb{L}^{(k+1)}
.
We refer to the elements of
\mathcal{B}^{(k)}
as the level-
k
blocks. The original lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be thought of as being at level
k=-1
. We also define
\ell_{k}=2\kappa(k+1)^{2}+1
be the side length of a block in
\mathcal{B}^{(k+1)}
. We require the following three conditions on the sequence of scales:

\begin{split}\mathbf{A1}:~~~\sup_{k\geq 0}\Big\{k^{2d}\ell_{k}^{2d}&(2\kappa)^{-(d-1)(k+5)}\Big\}\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\mathbf{A2}:~~~\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1+3^{d-1}}{\ell_{k}^{d-1}}\leq\frac{1}{20},\\
\mathbf{A3}:&~~~(1+3^{d})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{2d}(2\kappa)^{-(d-1)(k+6)}\leq\frac{1}{42}.\end{split}

(5.1)

All three conditions can be satisfied simultaneously if
\kappa=\kappa_{0}(d)
is a sufficiently large constant depending on
d
.
Indeed, since
\ell_{k}\sim(2\kappa)(k+1)^{2}
for
k
or
\kappa
large, the supremum in
\mathbf{A1}
is finite and can be made arbitrarily small if
\kappa
is large enough. Next, the two series in
\mathbf{A2}
and
\mathbf{A3}
are convergent for all
\kappa>\frac{1}{2}
and all
d\geq 2
, and their values are decreasing to zero as a function of
\kappa
. We fix
\kappa
such that
\mathbf{A1,A2,A3}
are satisfied.

2. Block variables 1.

We assign recursively to every level-
k
block
B\in\mathcal{B}^{(k)}
a random variable
W^{(k)}_{B}\in\{-1,+1\}
which is measurable with respect to the new synchronization variables
\{\widetilde{Y}_{B_{1},B_{2}}:B_{1}\sim B_{2}\in\mathbb{B}\}
defined in (
3.3
). Our estimates
\widetilde{T}_{B_{1},B_{2}}
for two level-0 blocks
B_{1},B_{2}
are defined as

\widetilde{T}_{B_{1},B_{2}}=\prod_{k\geq 0}W^{(k)}_{B_{1,k}}W^{(k)}_{B_{2,k}},

(5.2)

where
B_{1,k}
is the unique level-
k
block containing
B_{2}
, and similarly for
B_{2,k}
. Note that the above product is finite and extends up to the most recent ancestor of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, that is the lowest level block which contains both
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. It will be convenient to define the product of the variables
W^{(k)}_{B}
up to a given high in the hierarchy: For a level-
0
block
B
we let

\widetilde{W}^{(k)}_{B}=\prod_{j=0}^{k}W^{(j)}_{B_{j}},

were
B_{j}
is the unique level-
j
block containing
B
. (In this case
\widetilde{T}_{B_{1},B_{2}}=\widetilde{W}^{(k)}_{B_{1}}\widetilde{W}^{(k)}_{B_{2}}
where
k
is the level of the lowest common ancestor of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.)

3. Synchronization variables.

We recursively compute synchronization variables
Y^{(k)}_{B,B^{\prime}}
for every pair of adjacent level-
k
blocks. For
k=0
we let
Y^{(0)}_{B,B^{\prime}}=\widetilde{Y}_{B,B^{\prime}}
. For general
k
we proceed as follows. For two adjacent level-
k
blocks
B_{1},B_{2}
, we define
\partial^{(0)}(B_{1},B_{2})
to be the set of pairs of level-
0
adjacent blocks
B\sim B^{\prime}
such that
B\in B_{1}
and
B^{\prime}\in B_{2}
.
The set
\partial^{(0)}(B_{1})
of level-
0
blocks
B\in B_{1}
which have an adjacent block
B^{\prime}\in B_{2}
, seen as graph, is isomorphic to a box in
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
, and is thus bipartite, therefore 2-colorable.
Let
\mathsf{col}:\partial^{(0)}(B_{1})\mapsto\{0,1\}
be such a coloring (which can be found in linear time in a recursive way). We now define the subset of
\partial^{(0)}(B_{1},B_{2})
consisting of those edges whose end in
B_{1}
are colored
1
:

\Xi(B_{1},B_{2})=\big\{(B,B^{\prime})\in\partial^{(0)}(B_{1},B_{2})~:~\mathsf{col}(B)=1\big\}.

(5.3)

Importantly, two edges in
\Xi
have their endpoints non-adjacent. This property will be important in the analysis due to the fact that two non-adjacent level-
0
blocks do
not
overlap. Finally, we define

Y^{(k)}_{B_{1},B_{2}}=\sign\Big(\sum_{(B,B^{\prime})\in\Xi(B_{1},B_{2})}\widetilde{Y}_{B,B^{\prime}}\widetilde{W}^{(k-1)}_{B}\widetilde{W}^{(k-1)}_{B^{\prime}}\Big).

(5.4)

4. Block variables 2.

It remains to describe how
W^{(k)}_{B}
is constructed from the knowledge of the level-
k
synchronization variables
\big\{Y^{(k)}_{B_{1},B_{2}}:B_{1}\sim B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{(k)}\big\}
. Let
B^{*}
a level-
(k+1)
block. We call a
quartet
a collection of 4 level-
k
blocks
B_{1}\sim B_{2}\sim B_{3}\sim B_{4}\sim B_{5}=B_{1}
which form a square inside
B^{*}
. We say that a quartet is
incoherent
if
\prod_{i=1}^{4}Y^{(k)}_{B_{i},B_{i+1}}=-1
, and coherent otherwise. Let
\mathcal{I}_{B^{*}}
be the largest connected component of level-
k
sub-blocks
B\in B^{*}
which belong to no incoherent quartet. Now we assign, whenever possible, a random variable
W^{(k)}_{B}
to every
B\in B^{*}
such that

Y^{(k)}_{B_{1},B_{2}}=W^{(k)}_{B_{1}}W^{(k)}_{B_{2}}~~\mbox{for all}~~B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{B^{*}}~\mbox{with}~B_{1}\sim B_{2}.

(5.5)

If this is not possible then all blocks are assigned the value
+1
. Next, the blocks which do not belong to
\mathcal{I}_{B^{*}}
are all assigned the value
+1
.
This completes the description of the multiscale algorithm.

5.2
Proof of Theorem
4.2

We claim that for an appropriate choice of the renormalization length
L^{\circlearrowleft}
, the construction satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
4.2
.
We follow the argument in
[
AMM+18
]
which used a similar construction directly on the original lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for

. There are two novel aspects in our setting: the construction is generalized to all dimensions, and the initial renormalization step, in particular the fact that
p^{\circlearrowleft}\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
is used to ‘jump-start’ the argument.

Let
\delta^{\circlearrowleft}=1-2p^{\circlearrowleft}
. Let us call an edge

for two level-
k
blocks
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{(k)}

\sum_{(B,B^{\prime})\in\Xi(B_{1},B_{2})}\widetilde{Y}_{B,B^{\prime}}\tilde{\theta}_{B}\tilde{\theta}_{B^{\prime}}\geq\frac{9}{10}\delta^{\circlearrowleft}\big|\Xi(B_{1},B_{2})\big|.

(5.6)

Recall

was defined in Eq. (

).
Now we recursively define the notion of a

•

All adjacent level-
(k-1)

•

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists

and all

We proceed by induction.
The base case

being true, let us assume that for some

each level-
k

. Fix a level-
(k+1)

, and two of its level-

for two level-

Thus the sum on the left-hand side of (

Further,

Moreover, have

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{4d}\ell_{k}^{2d}}{(k+1)^{2d}}(2\kappa)^{-(d-1)(k+5)}u_{k}^{2}+d\frac{\ell_{k}^{d}}{(k+1)^{2d}}\,c_{0}(p^{\circlearrowleft})^{-1}(2\kappa)^{7(d-1)}.

\displaystyle\leq k^{2d}\ell_{k}^{2d}(2\kappa)^{-(d-1)(k+5)}u_{k}^{2}+\frac{2d(2\kappa+1)^{d}(2\kappa)^{7(d-1)}}{c_{0}(p^{\circlearrowleft})}

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{B}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}\Big(\sum_{\underset{\mathsf{col}(B)=1}{\bar{B}_{1}\ni B\sim B^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{2}}}\tilde{\theta}_{B}\widetilde{W}^{(k-1)}_{B}\Big)S_{B_{1}^{*}}^{(k+1)}W_{\bar{B}_{1}}^{(k)}-(1+3^{d-1})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{B}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}\Big(\sum_{\underset{\mathsf{col}(B)=1}{\bar{B}_{1}\ni B\sim B^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{2}}}\tilde{\theta}_{B}\widetilde{W}^{(k-1)}_{B}S^{(k)}_{\bar{B}_{1}}\Big)-(1+3^{d-1})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|.

We use item 4 of the induction hypothesis:
\underset{\underset{\mathsf{col}(B)=1}{\bar{B}_{1}\ni B\sim B^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{2}}}{\sum}\tilde{\theta}_{B}\widetilde{W}^{(k-1)}_{B}S^{(k)}_{\bar{B}_{1}}\geq(1-\epsilon_{k})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|
, whence the above is lower bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{B}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}(1-\epsilon_{k})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|-(1+3^{d-1})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\underset{\mathsf{col}(B)=1}{B_{1}^{*}\ni\bar{B}_{1}\sim\bar{B}_{2}\in B_{2}^{*}}}(1-\epsilon_{k})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|-(2-\epsilon_{k})(1+3^{d-1})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon_{k})|\Xi(B_{1}^{*},B_{2}^{*})|-(2-\epsilon_{k})(1+3^{d-1})|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|.

Since
|\Xi(B_{1}^{*},B_{2}^{*})|/|\Xi(\bar{B}_{1},\bar{B}_{2})|=\ell_{k}^{d-1}
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{\mathsf{col}(B)=1}{B\in B_{1}^{*}\cap\partial B_{2}^{*}}}\tilde{\theta}_{B}S_{B_{1}^{*}}^{(k+1)}\widetilde{W}^{(k)}_{B}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(1-\epsilon_{k}-\frac{2(1+3^{d-1})}{\ell_{k}^{d-1}}\Big)|\Xi(B_{1}^{*},B_{2}^{*})|

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon_{k+1})|\Xi(B_{1}^{*},B_{2}^{*})|.

This concludes the inductive argument.

\blacksquare

We now finish the proof of Theorem
4.2
. Let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be two level-
0

be their most recent common ancestor in the hierarchical partitioning; we let

be its level. Item 2 in Lemma

and
B_{2}

\widetilde{T}_{B_{1},B_{2}}=\tilde{\theta}_{B_{1}}\tilde{\theta}_{B_{2}}

B_{1}
and
B_{2}
along with their ancestors

and
B^{(j)}_{2}
for

are all good and agreeable. (Here we have

and

and similarly for

1-\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(J_{B_{1},B_{2}}^{(k)}\big)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{a=1}^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad or non-agreeable}\big)+\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{*}~\mbox{is bad}\big).

A non-agreeable block must belong to at least one incoherent quartet. Since a quartet of good sub-blocks belonging to a good block is necessarily coherent, a non-agreeable block inside a good block is the center of a cube of side-length 3 in which at least one block is bad. Hence for
0\leq j\leq k-1
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad or non-agreeable}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad or non-agreeable}~\big|B^{(j+1)}_{a}~\mbox{is good}\big)

\displaystyle~~~+\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j+1)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{d}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad}\big)+\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j+1)}_{a}~\mbox{is bad}\big).

Therefore,

\displaystyle 1-\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(J_{B_{1},B_{2}}^{(k)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 3^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{1}~\mbox{is bad}\big)+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{1}~\mbox{is bad}\big)+\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{*}~\mbox{is bad}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+3^{d})\sum_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(B^{(j)}_{1}~\mbox{is bad}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+3^{d})\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}j^{2d}(2\kappa)^{-(d-1)(j+6)}\leq\frac{1}{21}.

The third inequality follows from Lemma

in Eq. (

Finally, concerning the running time of the algorithm,
B_{1}
and

6
Posterior measures

The analysis of the renormalization procedure relies on understanding the concentration properties of the posterior measure of
\theta_{A}=(\theta_{x})_{x\in A}
given information associated to
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for various subsets
A
. Concretely we will investigate the asymptotic properties of the ‘free energies’ which are the normalized log-partition functions of the posterior measures and then extract convergence and concentration properties of certain overlaps. These free energies will converge to variational formulas given in terms of a simpler system where the side information is decoupled across vertices. We begin by describing this setting.

6.1
Scalar side information

Let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of finite connected subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which is increasing:
B_{n}\subseteq B_{n+1}
, invades the entire lattice:
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and has vanishing isoperimetry:
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}|\partial B_{n}|/|B_{n}|=0
. We call such a sequence a
van Hove
sequence
[
FV17
]
.
Consider the scalar additive Gaussian noise channel with SNR
\lambda\geq 0
on
B_{n}
:

y_{u}=\sqrt{\lambda}\theta_{u}+z_{u},~~~\forall u\in B_{n},

(6.1)

where
(z_{u})_{u\in B_{n}}
are i.i.d. Gaussian random variables independent of everything else.
Consider the posterior measure of
\theta_{B_{n}}
given the lattice information
Y^{\delta}_{B_{n}}=\{Y^{\delta}_{uv}:(u,v)\in B_{n}^{2}\cap\mathbb{E}^{d}\}
and
Y^{\lambda}_{B_{n}}=\{y_{u}:u\in B_{n}\}
:

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big(\theta_{x}\big)_{x\in B_{n}}\big|Y^{\delta}_{B_{n}},Y^{\lambda}_{B_{n}}\Big)=\frac{1}{Z_{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+H_{B_{n}}^{\lambda}(\theta)},

(6.2)

where

H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta):=\beta\sum_{\underset{|u-v|=1}{u,v\in B_{n}}}Y^{\delta}_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v},

(6.3)

with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{1-p}{p})
is the Hamiltonian encoding the lattice interaction and

H_{B_{n}}^{\lambda}(\theta)=\sum_{u\in B_{n}}\big(\sqrt{\lambda}y_{u}\theta_{u}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\big),

(6.4)

encodes the side information
Y^{\lambda}_{B_{n}}
.
We define the free energy associated to this posterior by

f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}(\delta,\lambda):=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+H_{B_{n}}^{\lambda}(\theta)}\Big\},

(6.5)

where the expectation is with respect to
Y^{\delta}_{B_{n}}
and
Y^{\lambda}_{B_{n}}
.
We also define a more general function with two parameters

and
r\geq 0

\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,s,r):=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+{\widetilde{H}}^{r,s}_{B_{n}}(\theta)}\Big\},

(6.6)

where

{\widetilde{H}_{B_{n}}}^{r,s}(\theta):=\sum_{u\in B_{n}}\big(\sqrt{r}z_{u}\theta_{u}+s\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{r}{2}\big),

where
(z_{u})_{u\in B_{n}}
are i.i.d.
N(0,1)
r.v.’s and
(\theta_{0u})_{u\in B_{n}}
are independent symmetric signs. Note that
\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,r,r)=f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}(\delta,r)
for all
r\geq 0
3
3

3

When
s\neq r
, the Gibbs measure proportional to
e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+{\widetilde{H}}^{r,s}_{B_{n}}(\theta)}
doe not have a Bayesian interpretation as the posterior distribution of a set of random variables given some sort of observations.

Proposition 6.1

For all

We let

We do not know of an ‘explicit’ expression for either

(6.7)

6.2
GOE side information

The side information we are ultimately interested in is the spiked GOE
Y^{\eta}_{B_{n}}=\{Y^{\eta}_{uv}:u,v\in B_{n}\}
where
Y^{\eta}_{uv}=\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{|B_{n}|}}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+Z_{uv}
,
Z_{uv}\sim N(0,1)
independent of everything else. The posterior measure is

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big(\theta_{x}\big)_{x\in B_{n}}\big|Y^{\delta}_{B_{n}},Y^{\eta}_{B_{n}}\Big)=\frac{1}{Z_{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+H_{B_{n}}^{\eta}(\theta)},

(6.8)

where

H_{B_{n}}^{\eta}(\theta):=\sum_{u,v\in B_{n}}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{|B_{n}|}}Y_{uv}^{\eta}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}-\frac{\eta}{2|B_{n}|}\Big).

(6.9)

We similarly define the free energy

f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta):=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+H_{B_{n}}^{\eta}(\theta)}\Big\}.

(6.10)

Proposition 6.3
.

For all
\delta\in[0,1]
,
\eta\geq 0
,
f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)
has a limit
\phi(\delta,\eta)
as
n\to\infty
which does not depends on the van Hove sequence
(B_{n})
, and we have the relation

\phi(\delta,\eta)=\sup_{q\geq 0}\Big\{f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,\eta q)-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}\Big\}.

(6.11)

Similarly to the scalar side information case,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B_{n}}
converges to
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi
everywhere except on countably many points. On the one hand, the derivative of
f_{B_{n}}
is

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B_{n}}=\frac{1}{4|B_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta}_{B_{n}},Y^{\eta}_{B_{n}}\big]^{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{4}\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}_{B_{n}}.

(6.12)

On the other hand, the derivative of
\phi
can be linked to the maximizer in the variational formula (
6.11
). This proves Proposition
1.1
and provides a another characterization of
q_{\star}
:

Lemma 6.4
.

For all
\delta\in[0,1]
and all
\eta
where
\phi(\delta,\cdot)
is differentiable, the above maximization problem has a unique maximizer
q_{\star}\in[0,1]
, and
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi(\delta,\lambda)=q_{\star}^{2}/4
. Therefore, for all
\delta
and all except countably many
\eta
, we obtain the relation

\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}_{B_{n}}\to q_{\star}^{2}
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

This argument appears in
[
LM17
]
but we reproduce it here for the sake of completeness. First, it is easy to see that the supremum in (
6.11
) is achieved. Indeed
f^{\textup{{sc}}}
being convex, it is also continuous, and one has the elementary bound
f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,\lambda)\leq 2\beta d+\lambda
. Therefore, large values of
q
cannot achieve the supremum. Next, we reparametrize the variational formula as
\phi(\delta,\eta)=\sup_{\bar{q}\geq 0}\{f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,\bar{q})-\frac{\bar{q}^{2}}{4\eta}\}
with
\bar{q}=\eta q
.
The envelope theorem implies that on points of differentiability of
\phi
, we have
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi=\bar{q}^{2}/4\eta^{2}
where
\bar{q}
is any point achieving the maximum. Therefore the maximum is achieved at a unique point
q_{\star}
and we have
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi(\delta,\lambda)=q_{\star}^{2}/4
.
Moreover from (
6.12
), the fact that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B_{n}}
converges to
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi
whenever the derivative is defined implies the last claim
\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}_{B_{n}}\to q_{\star}^{2}
.

\blacksquare

6.3
One-block and two-block posteriors

In this subsection we analyze the

(\theta_{x})_{x\in B}

The one-block posterior is given by

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big(\theta_{x}\big)_{x\in B}\big|Y^{\delta}_{B},Y^{\eta}_{B}\Big)=\frac{1}{Z_{B}}\omega^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}_{B}(\theta)\cdot e^{H_{B}^{\delta}(\theta)},

V_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(B^{\prime},B^{\prime\prime})\to 0
for all
B^{\prime\prime}\sim B^{\prime}
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
.

Proof.

Convexity of
f_{B}
and
f_{B\cup B^{\prime}}
w.r.t.
t
implies that their derivatives converge to the derivatives of the limits
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
everywhere except on countably many points.
A short computation of the finite-volume derivatives reveals

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f_{B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}V_{B}(B;B^{\prime}),\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f_{B\cup B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}V_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(B,B^{\prime\prime})-\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B^{\prime}}V_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(B^{\prime};B^{\prime\prime}).

Since by Proposition
6.5
, the limits do not depend on

for
\eta\leq\eta_{0}
, the above right-hand sides converge to zero.

\blacksquare

Now that overlaps of sub-blocks of a given block are locked together, they are all asymptotically equal to the overlap on the entire block (
B
in the case of the one-block posterior and
B\cup B^{\prime}
in the case of the two-block posterior). The limits of these global overlaps are again available by differentiating w.r.t.
\eta
. Similarly to
V_{A}(B;B^{\prime})
, we define

W_{A}(B;B^{\prime}):=t\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\right\rangle_{A}+(1-t)\Big(\alpha\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{A}+(1-\alpha)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\setminus B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{A}\Big).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}W_{B}(B;B^{\prime}),\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B\cup B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}W_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(B;B^{\prime\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B^{\prime}}W_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(B^{\prime};B^{\prime\prime}).

We deduce from Proposition
6.6
that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B}=\frac{d}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\right\rangle_{B}+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),~~\mbox{and}~~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{B\cup B^{\prime}}=d\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),

where
o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1)\to 0
for almost all
t
and all
\eta\leq\eta_{0}
. Hence the following

Corollary 6.7
.

Under Assumption
1
, let
q^{\bullet}_{1}
and
q^{\bullet}_{2}
be the unique maximizers in the variational problems (
6.19
) and (
6.20
) respectively. For almost all
t\in[0,1]
and all except countably many
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\right\rangle_{B}\to{q^{\bullet}_{1}}^{2}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\to{q^{\bullet}_{2}}^{2}
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
.

Proof.

From (

) and Lemma

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi_{1}=d{q^{\bullet}_{1}}^{2}/2
.
Second, note that the same argument used to obtain Lemma

applies to the formula (
6.20

q^{\bullet}_{2}

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\phi_{2}=d{q^{\bullet}_{2}}^{2}

f_{B}
and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi_{1}
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\phi_{2}

\blacksquare

Next, we show that the maximizers

Lemma 6.8
.

Under Assumption
1
, and for
\delta>\delta_{c}
, there exist a constant

such that for all except countably many
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]

|q^{\bullet}_{2}-q^{\bullet}_{1}|\vee|q^{\bullet}_{1}-q_{\star}|\leq c_{0}\eta

Proof.

Since

{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta;\eta q_{\star})=\frac{q_{\star}}{2},~~{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta,2d\eta q^{\bullet}_{1})=\frac{q^{\bullet}_{1}}{2},~~(1-\alpha){f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta,2d\eta q^{\bullet}_{2})+\alpha{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta,4d\eta q^{\bullet}_{2})=\frac{q^{\bullet}_{2}}{2}.

\blacksquare

Next, we exploit information contained in the second derivatives of the free energies.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
A
be
B
or
B\cup B^{\prime}
. For almost all
\lambda\geq 0
,

\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in A}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

Call the quantity in the above display

. Taking two derivatives of the free energy
f_{A}
with respect to

, we obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{2}}f_{A}=\frac{1}{4|A|}\sum_{x,y\in A}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]=\frac{|A|}{4}R.

Moreover, the first derivative is uniformly bounded:
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}f_{A}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, so
\int_{0}^{\lambda}\big(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{2}}f_{A}\big)\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}
. Therefore
\int_{0}^{\lambda}R\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}\leq 2/|A|\to 0
as

. Since

is non-negative, it must converge to

for almost all values of
\lambda
.

\blacksquare

Lemmas
6.10
and
6.11
provide averaged forms of decoupling of the variables at different vertices. Next, we show a form of continuity in

at zero for the pair correlations:

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
A
be
B
or
B\cup B^{\prime}
. For almost all
\eta\geq 0
,

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in A}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{A}\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

Proof.

Call
R
the quantity whose double limit is taken in the above display. Since
Y_{A}^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}
contains more information than
Y_{A}^{\delta,\eta}
, we have

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in A}\Big\{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{A}\big]^{2}\Big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{A}\big]^{2}\Big]\Big\}

Since
\varphi_{A}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}=4\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}f_{A}
, we have

\int_{0}^{\eta}R\mathrm{d}\eta^{\prime}\leq 4\big|f_{A}(\delta,\eta,t,\lambda)-f_{A}(\delta,\eta,t,0)\big|+4\big|f_{A}(\delta,0,t,\lambda)-f_{A}(\delta,0,t,0)\big|.

Since

is

uniformly in the other variables (recall that the derivative is uniformly bounded by

), we arrive at
\int_{0}^{\eta}R\mathrm{d}\eta^{\prime}\leq 4\lambda
. Since

is non-negative and has a limit as

, Fatou’s lemma implies

\int_{0}^{\eta}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}R~\mathrm{d}\eta^{\prime}\leq 4\lambda.

Sending

\blacksquare

.

Let
A
be

or

This time we compute the second derivative of

with respect to

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B}\big]^{2}\Big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{x\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B}\big]^{2}\Big]^{2}+\mbox{error}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{B}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)-\varphi_{B}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny v}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)^{2}+\mbox{error}.

Lemma
6.12
implies
\mbox{error}\to 0
for almost every
\eta>0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\lambda\to 0
. Moreover, from Lemma
6.10
, we have
\varphi_{B}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)-\varphi_{B}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny v}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)^{2}\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
. Thus, we have shown that for almost every
\eta>0
,

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\var(X^{2})=0.

Now, since Lemma
6.11
implies

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{x\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B}\big]^{2}\Big)^{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B}\big]^{2}\Big]+\mbox{error},

the above tends to

as

then
\lambda\to 0
, and then

. We conclude that
\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\var(M_{B}^{2})=0
.

7.2
Proof of item 2: analysis of
W_{B,B^{\prime}}

We proceed analogously. Recall that

W_{B,B^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\theta^{B}_{x}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{x},

where
\big(\theta^{B}_{x}\big)_{x\in B}\sim\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|Y_{B})
and
\big(\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{x}\big)_{x\in B^{\prime}}\sim\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|Y_{B^{\prime}})
independently. Therefore

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\theta^{B}_{x}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{x}\Big)^{2}\Big|Y_{B},Y_{B^{\prime}}\Big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

We will use overlap locking (Proposition
6.6
) to replace the conditional expectations
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}]
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B^{\prime}}]
in the above expression by
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}]
where

is the union of
Y_{B}
and
Y_{B^{\prime}}
.
Now, let
(\theta^{B\cup B^{\prime}}_{x})_{x\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\sim\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}})
be drawn from the two-block posterior conditionally independently from everything else, and let

First we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{M}_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}]=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}.

(7.1)

Second, we since all variables are bounded, a simple triangle inequality implies

\displaystyle\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{2}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{M}_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\Big\{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B^{\prime}}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big|\Big]\Big\}.

We use Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
B
and
B^{\prime}
play symmetric roles to write

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{4}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big)^{2}\Big]\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
Y_{B}\subset Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}
, using iterated expectations, we see that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}]\big)^{2}\big]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}]^{2}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}]^{2}\big].

It follows that

\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{2}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{M}_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}]\Big|\leq 2\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B}\Big)^{1/2}.

(7.2)

By virtue of Proposition
6.6
, we have on the one hand,
R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})
and
R_{1,2}(B)
lock together under the one-block posterior on
B
,
and on the other hand,
R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})
and
R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})
lock together under the two-block posterior. More precisely, for almost all
t\in[0,1]
and all
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]
, it holds

\displaystyle\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\big\rangle_{B}\Big|\xrightarrow[L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty]{}0,~~~~\mbox{and}~~

\displaystyle\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\Big|\xrightarrow[L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty]{}0.

Furthermore, we know that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\big\rangle_{B}\to{q^{\bullet}_{1}}^{2}(\delta,\eta)
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\to{q^{\bullet}_{2}}^{2}(\delta,\eta)
from Corollary
6.7
for all except countably many
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]
. Now from Lemma
6.8
,
q^{\bullet}_{1}-q^{\bullet}_{2}\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
. We deduce from this and the bound (
7.2
) that for almost every
t\in[0,1]
,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\Big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{2}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{M}_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}]\Big|=0,

and Eq.(
7.1
) implies

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{M}_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}]=\lim_{\eta\to 0}{q^{\bullet}_{2}}^{2}(\delta,\eta)=q_{\star\star}^{2}(\delta).

This establishes the first claim of item 2.

As for the variance, it suffices to consider the fourth moment of
W_{B,B^{\prime}}
. We proceed similarly to the case of
M_{B}
:

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{4}]=\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{4}}\sum_{x,y,z,w\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B}\big]\cdot\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

We use Lemma
6.13
(applied with
A=B
then
A=B^{\prime}
) to decouple the pairs
x,y
and
w,z
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{4}]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|^{4}}\sum_{x,y,z,w\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B^{\prime}}\big]\Big]+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),

where

as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
for almost every
\eta
.
Now we repeat the argument used in the analysis of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}^{2}]
to argue that the above is

\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{4}}\sum_{x,y,z,w\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]+\mbox{error},

where
\mbox{error}\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\eta\to 0
, and
Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}=\{Y_{B},Y_{B^{\prime}}\}
is as defined above.
We use Proposition
6.13
to merge the pairs
x,y
and
w,z
. The above becomes

\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{4}}\sum_{x,y,z,w\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}\theta_{z}\theta_{w}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]+\mbox{error}.

The main term in the above display is equal to

. Here the same analysis used for

in the previous subsection applies as well. Therefore

converges to

.

7.3
Proof of item 3: analysis of
W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}

We first perform a preliminary computation and then sketch the argument, which we then execute in three steps.
Let
Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}=\{Y_{B},Y_{B^{\prime}}\}
where as in the analysis of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[M_{B}^{2}]
, we use the short-hand
Y_{B}=\{Y^{\delta}_{B},Y^{\eta}_{B}\}
. Then

\displaystyle{|B|\cdot|B^{\prime}|\cdot|B\cap B^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\cdot\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\theta^{B}_{x}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{x}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{y\in B}\theta^{B}_{y}\theta_{y}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{z\in B^{\prime}}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{z}\theta_{z}\Big)~\Big|~Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\Big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\sum_{y\in B}\sum_{z\in B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta^{B}_{x}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{x}\theta^{B}_{y}\theta_{y}\theta^{B^{\prime}}_{z}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\sum_{y\in B}\sum_{z\in B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

(7.3)

In this case the strategy is to replace the conditional expectations
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B^{\prime}}\big]
in the right-hand side with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]
respectively.
Once we succeed at this operation,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]
is approximated by the quantity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|\cdot|B^{\prime}|\cdot|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B,z\in B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

Notice that the above sum is over different subsets of
B\cup B^{\prime}
. We then exploit the fact that local overlaps lock under the two-block posterior to replace the above with a homogenous sum over all vertices of
B\cup B^{\prime}
:

\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{3}}\sum_{x,y,z\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big].

We remark that this is the trace of the third power of a certain matrix
\chi
, which we call
susceptibility matrix
of the block
B\cup B^{\prime}
, defined by

\chi_{x,y}:=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big],~~~x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}.

(7.4)

We will then argue that
\chi
is approximately a
rank-one
matrix, in the sense that its Frobenius and operator norms are equal in the limit
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\eta\to 0
, with common asymptotic value
q_{\star\star}(\delta)
. Since
\trace(\chi^{3})^{1/3}
is sandwiched between the operator and Frobenius norms, a concentration argument finally yields that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]
converges to
q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta)
.

Step 1: replacing the conditional expectations.

The first step is to replace
Y_{B}
and
Y_{B^{\prime}}
by
Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}
in the conditional expectations in (
7.3
). Since
Y_{B}\subset Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|\cdot|B\cap B^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|\cdot|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B}\big]^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})\big\rangle_{B}.

Overlap locking under both the one-block and two-block posteriors, as given in Proposition
6.6
, implies that the above is equal to

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\big\rangle_{B}+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),

where
o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1)\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
for almost all
t\in[0,1]
and all
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]
.
Now we use Corollary
6.7
and Lemma
6.8
to deduce that for almost all
t
,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to 0}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)^{2}\big\rangle_{B}=0.

The same result obviously holds if
B
is replaced by
B^{\prime}
in the above argument. Therefore we can substitute
B
and
B^{\prime}
with
B\cup B^{\prime}
in the conditional expectations in the expression of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]
, Eq.(
7.3
): as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to 0
then
\eta\to 0
we have

\displaystyle\Big|

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[W_{B,B^{\prime}}M_{B}M_{B^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|B|\cdot|B^{\prime}|\cdot|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cap B^{\prime},y\in B,z\in B^{\prime}}\hskip-14.22636pt\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big]\Big|\longrightarrow 0.

Step 2: replacing the domain of summation.

The next step is to replace the domains of summation in the above sum to
B\cup B^{\prime}
for the three indices. The above sum can be written as

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B)R_{2,3}(B\cap B^{\prime})R_{3,1}(B^{\prime})\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}.

Since each of the three overlaps above is locked to
R_{a,b}(B\cup B^{\prime})
for the appropriate replica pair
(a,b)
(this is again a straightforward application of Proposition
6.6
, the above expression is equal to

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})R_{2,3}(B\cup B^{\prime})R_{3,1}(B\cup B^{\prime})\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),

where again
o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1)\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
for almost all
t\in[0,1]
and all
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0}]
.
As explained in the sketch of the proof, the above triangular product can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})

\displaystyle R_{2,3}(B\cup B^{\prime})R_{3,1}(B\cup B^{\prime})\big\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{3}}\sum_{x,y,z\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}\theta_{z}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{3}),

where
\chi
is the susceptibility matrix (
7.4
).

Step 3: structure of the susceptibility matrix.

Next, we prove that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{3})\to q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta)
for almost all
t\in[0,1]
.
We proceed by showing upper and lower bounds separately. For the upper bound, we observe that as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\eta\to 0
, we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{2})=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle R_{1,2}(B\cup B^{\prime})^{2}\right\rangle_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\longrightarrow q_{\star\star}^{2}(\delta).

This is obtained through previously employed arguments: Corollary
6.7
and Lemma
6.8
.
Moreover, by monotonicity of the
\ell_{p}
norm and the fact that
\chi
is a symmetric and positive semi-definite (PSD) matrix,

\trace(\chi^{3})^{1/3}\leq\trace(\chi^{2})^{1/2}.

Thus,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\trace(\chi^{3})]\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\trace(\chi^{2})^{3/2}]
.
Now, since

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\trace(\chi^{2})^{2}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\trace(\chi^{2})\big]^{2}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[M_{B}^{4}\big]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[M_{B}^{2}\big]^{2}=\var(M_{B}^{2}),

which, according to Theorem
4.3
, item 1, converges to zero as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to 0
then
\eta\to 0
, we have

\limsup_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\trace(\chi^{2})^{3/2}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\trace(\chi^{2})]^{3/2}\Big)=0.

Further, since
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\trace(\chi^{2})]^{3/2}\to q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta)
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\eta\to 0
, this establishes the upper bound

\limsup_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\trace(\chi^{3})\big]\leq q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta).

As for the lower bound, we have
\trace(\chi^{3})\geq\|\chi\|_{\textup{op}}^{3}
since
\chi
is PSD. We will prove that

\liminf_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\chi\|_{\textup{op}}\geq q_{\star\star}(\delta).

(7.5)

This implies a matching lower bound through Jensen’s inequality.
We have the following characterization of the operator norm for a PSD matrix:

\|\chi\|_{\textup{op}}=\sup_{|u|=1}u^{\intercal}\chi u,

where
|\cdot|
is the
\ell_{2}
norm of a vector.
Now it suffices to exhibit a candidate unit vector
u
that (approximately) achieves the required bound.
Our candidate is
u=\hat{u}/|\hat{u}|
, where

\hat{u}_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|B\cup B^{\prime}|}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}\big],~~~x\in B\cup B^{\prime},

(7.6)

where we have added additional side information from the scalar Gaussian noise channel (
6.1
) with SNR
\lambda>0
on
B\cup B^{\prime}
. We show next that as
\lambda\to 0
, the (sequence of) vector(s)
u
achieves the supremum.
We use the decoupling Lemma
6.11
(with
A=B\cup B^{\prime}
) to obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}\big]\Big]+o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1),

for almost every
\lambda>0
.
Moreover, according to Lemma
6.12
we can remove the scalar side information and incur a small error:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]+\mbox{error}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{2})+\mbox{error},

where
\mbox{error}\to 0
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
and then
\lambda\to 0
for almost every
\eta>0
. We have already established that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{2})\to q_{\star\star}^{2}(\delta)
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
and
\eta\to 0
. Now it remains to analyze
|\hat{u}|
.
We have

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\hat{u}|^{2}\big]=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|}\sum_{x\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]=\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny v}}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda).

Now we examine the fourth moment of
|\hat{u}|
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\hat{u}|^{4}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B\cup B^{\prime}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{x}\theta_{y}|Y^{\delta,\eta,\lambda}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}\big]^{2}\Big]+\mbox{error}_{1}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)+\mbox{error}_{1},

where, similarly to previous arguments,
\mbox{error}_{1}\to 0
for almost every
\lambda>0
and all
\eta>0
.
Now observe that by virtue of Lemma
6.10
,

\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)-\varphi^{\mbox{\rm\tiny v}}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(\delta,\eta,\lambda)^{2}\to 0,~~\mbox{as}~~L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty.

Thus
\var(|\hat{u}|^{2})\to 0
.
As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[u^{\intercal}\chi u\big]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\frac{\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}}{|\hat{u}|^{2}}\Big]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\frac{\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]}\Big]+\mbox{error},

where

\displaystyle|\mbox{error}|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{|\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}|\cdot|\Delta|}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]\cdot|\hat{u}|^{2}}\right],~~~\Delta=|\hat{u}|^{2}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\Delta|\mathds{1}\{|\hat{u}|<\epsilon\}]}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]}+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{|\hat{u}^{\intercal}\chi\hat{u}|\cdot|\Delta|}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]\cdot|\hat{u}|^{2}}\mathds{1}\{|\hat{u}|\geq\epsilon\}\right],~~~(\mbox{for }\epsilon>0)

\displaystyle\leq 2\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(|\hat{u}|<\epsilon)+\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\Delta|]}{\epsilon^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]}.

By Chebychev’s inequality,
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(|\hat{u}|<\epsilon)\leq\var(|\hat{u}|^{2})/(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]-\epsilon^{2})
for
\epsilon<\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]^{1/2}
. On the other hand
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\Delta|]\leq\var(|\hat{u}|^{2})^{1/2}
.
When
\delta>\delta_{c}
,
q_{\star\star}(\delta)>0
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|\hat{u}|^{2}]>0
for
L^{\circlearrowleft}
large enough, and the above tends to zero.
Therefore, we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[u^{\intercal}\chi u]\to q_{\star\star}(\delta)
when
\delta>\delta_{c}
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
then
\eta\to 0
. This implies the desired lower bound (
7.5
).

Putting the upper and lower bounds together we deduce that

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\trace(\chi^{3})=q_{\star\star}^{3}(\delta).

7.4
Proof of Theorem
1.3

For any
T
with output values in
\{\pm 1\}
and
L_{n}\geq 1
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\left|\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[T_{uv}(Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta,\eta,L_{n}})\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L_{n}}\big]\Big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L_{n}}\big]^{2}\Big]\Big)^{1/2}.

We add auxiliary GOE side information with SNR parameter
\eta_{2}
and interaction range
L=2n+1
, (i.e., there is a measurement
Y_{uv}^{\eta_{2}}
available for any pair
(u,v)
in
\Lambda_{n}
) and let

\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}:=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L_{n}},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta_{2}}\big]^{2}\Big].

We clearly have
|\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T)|\leq\big(\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}\big)^{1/2}
. Now recall the definition of
\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}
:

\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2}):=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\Lambda_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta_{2}}\big]^{2}\Big].

The crucial point is that
\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2})
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\eta_{2})
are close when
\eta
is small:

Lemma 7.1
.

For almost all
\eta_{2}>0
,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}-\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}})=0.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
6.12
. Let
\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},\eta_{2})=\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log Z_{\Lambda_{n}}
where
Z_{\Lambda_{n}}
is the partition function of
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot\,|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L_{n}},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta_{2}})
. Then
\eta\mapsto\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},\eta_{2})
is
\frac{1}{2}
-Lipschitz as seen by taking the
\eta
-derivative of
\tilde{f}_{n}
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\tilde{f}_{n}=\frac{1}{2|\Lambda_{n}|L_{n}^{d}}\sum_{u\in\Lambda_{n}}\sum_{\underset{|u-v|\leq L_{n}}{v\in\Lambda_{n}}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\theta_{u}\theta_{v}|Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\delta},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta,L_{n}},Y_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\eta_{2}}\big]^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{1}{2}.

On the other hand, the
\eta_{2}
-derivative of
\tilde{f}_{n}
is
\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\eta_{2})
. Therefore, for all
\eta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta_{2}}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\eta_{2}^{\prime})-\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}\eta_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},\eta_{2})-\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},0))

\displaystyle~~~-2(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,0,L_{n},\eta_{2})-\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,0,L_{n},0))

\displaystyle=2(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},\eta_{2})-\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,0,L_{n},\eta_{2}))

\displaystyle~~~-2(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,\eta,L_{n},0)-\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta,0,L_{n},0))

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta.

We used the Lipschitz property of
\tilde{f}_{n}
in
\eta
to obtain the last line. Since the integrand is nonnegative, Fatou’s lemma implies

\int_{0}^{\eta_{2}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\tilde{\varphi}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta,\eta_{2}^{\prime})-\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}\eta_{2}^{\prime}\leq 2\eta.

Now, letting
\eta\to 0
implies the result.

\blacksquare

Given the above result, we have for almost all
\eta_{2}>0
,

\limsup_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}|\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T)|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2})\big)^{1/2}.

Since
\underset{\eta_{2}\to 0}{\lim}\,\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\varphi_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny e}}(\delta,\eta_{2})=0
when
\delta<\delta_{c}
we have

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}|\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda_{n}}(T)|=0.

8
Limits of the free energies

In this section we prove convergence of the various free energies defined in Section
6
, i.e., we prove Propositions
6.1
,
6.3
,
6.5
and
6.9
.

8.1
Proof of Proposition
6.1

We start with the free energy
f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}(\delta,\lambda)
in Eq. (
6.5
). The argument is standard and we follow Friedli and Velenik’s exposition
[
FV17
, Chapter 3]
. Minor changes need to be made in order to accommodate our setting with disorder and the additional Hamiltonian
H^{\lambda}_{B_{n}}
encoding the scalar side information.
We start with the special case where
B_{n}=D_{n}=\{0,1,\cdots,2^{n}\}^{d}
, we then extend the convergence to a general van Hove sequence.

Observe that
D_{n+1}
can be partitioned disjointly into
2^{d}
translates of
D_{n}
; call them
D_{n}^{(1)},\cdots,D_{n}^{(2^{d})}
. Moreover, the Hamiltonian (
6.3
) corresponding to the information
Y^{\delta}_{D_{n}}
observed in the edges of the lattice can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle H_{D_{n+1}}^{\delta}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{\underset{|u-v|=1}{u,v\in D_{n+1}}}Y^{\delta}_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}H_{D_{n}^{(i)}}^{\delta}(\theta)+R,

where
H_{D_{n}^{(i)}}^{\delta}(\theta)
are defined exactly as in (
6.3
) (observe that they are identical in law), and
R
collects the terms corresponding to the edges whose endpoints belong to two different sub-blocks
D_{n}^{(i)}
. Since there are at most
|\partial D_{n+1}|=\beta d2^{(n+1)(d-1)}
such edges, and all individual terms in the above Hamiltonian are bounded by one in absolute value, we have
|R|\leq\beta d2^{(n+1)(d-1)}
. Therefore, denoting by
Z_{D_{n+1}}
and
Z_{D^{(i)}_{n}}
the partition functions we have

\prod_{i=1}^{2^{d}}Z_{D_{n}^{(i)}}\cdot e^{-\beta d2^{(n+1)(d-1)}}\leq Z_{D_{n+1}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{2^{d}}Z_{D_{n}^{(i)}}\cdot e^{\beta d2^{(n+1)(d-1)}}.

Since
\{Z_{D_{n}^{(i)}}:i=1,\cdots,2^{d}\}
are all equal in distribution, taking logarithms and then expectations, we get

\frac{2^{d}|D_{n}|}{|D_{n+1}|}f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n}}-\beta d\frac{2^{(n+1)(d-1)}}{|D_{n+1}|}\leq f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n+1}}\leq\frac{2^{d}|D_{n}|}{|D_{n+1}|}f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n}}+\beta d\frac{2^{(n+1)(d-1)}}{|D_{n+1}|}.

Since
|D_{n}|=2^{nd}
for all
n
, this yields

|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n+1}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n}}|\leq\frac{\beta d}{2^{n+1}}.

We deduce from this that the sequence
(f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n}})_{n\geq 0}
is Cauchy:
|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{m}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{n}}|\leq\beta d2^{-n}
for all
m\geq n
. Hence it converges to a limit
f^{\textup{{sc}}}
as
n\to\infty
, and the convergence is uniform on any interval
[0,\beta_{0}]
with
\beta_{0}<\infty
.

Consider now an arbitrary van Hove sequence
(B_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. Fix an integer
k
and partition
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
into disjoint translates of
D_{k}=\{0,1,\cdots,2^{k}\}^{d}
. For each
n
let
[B]_{n}
be the minimal cover of
B_{n}
by translates of
D_{k}
. We have

|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}|\leq|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{[B]_{n}}|+|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{[B]_{n}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{k}}|+|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{k}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}|.

Now we bound each one of these three terms. For the first term, we let
\Delta_{n}=[B]_{n}\setminus B_{n}
. We then have

Z_{B_{n}}\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{\Delta_{n}}\cdot e^{-2d\beta|\Delta_{n}|}\leq Z_{[B]_{n}}\leq Z_{B_{n}}\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{\Delta_{n}}\cdot e^{2d\beta|\Delta_{n}|},

where

\widetilde{Z}_{\Delta_{n}}=2^{-|\Delta_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{\Delta_{n}}}e^{H^{\lambda}_{\Delta_{n}}(\theta)}.

Therefore, letting
\vartheta=\frac{1}{|\Delta_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\widetilde{Z}_{\Delta_{n}}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\cosh(\sqrt{\lambda}z+\lambda)
with
z\sim N(0,1)
, we have

\Big|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{[B]_{n}}-\frac{|B_{n}|}{|[B]_{n}|}f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}-\frac{|\Delta_{n}|}{|[B]_{n}|}\vartheta\Big|\leq\frac{2d\beta|\Delta_{n}|}{|[B]_{n}|}.

On the one hand,
[B]_{n}
is a minimal cover of
B_{n}
by translates of
D_{k}
, so we have
|\Delta_{n}|\leq|D_{k}|\cdot|\partial B_{n}|
. On the other hand
|[B]_{n}|\geq|B_{n}|
, and
f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}
is uniformly bounded by
2\beta d+\vartheta
. Thus

|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{[B]_{n}}|\leq C\frac{|\partial B_{n}|}{|B_{n}|}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0

for some constant
C=C(\beta,d,k,\lambda)>0
. Convergence is again uniform on
[0,\beta_{0}]\times[0,\lambda_{0}]
for any
\beta_{0},\lambda_{0}>0
.
Next, we address the second term. The same estimates previously conducted show that

\displaystyle|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{[B]_{n}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{k}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta|\partial[B]_{n}|}{|[B]_{n}|}\leq\frac{\beta|\partial D_{k}|}{|D_{k}|}\leq\beta d2^{-k}.

Lastly, we already know that
|f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{D_{k}}-f^{\textup{{sc}}}|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Now we take
n\to\infty
then
k\to\infty
to conclude the proof.

8.2
Proof of Proposition
6.3

Here we prove convergence of the free energy
f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)
with GOE side information with SNR
\eta>0
. The argument relies on Guerra’s interpolation method
[
GT02
]
and concentration of measure arguments. It is almost identical to the proof in the spiked GOE case with no lattice information (
\delta=0
). We follow the exposition in
[
AK18
]
.
The lower and upper bounds are proved separately.
We start with the lower bound. We use the interpolating Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{s}(\theta)

\displaystyle=H^{\delta}_{B_{n}}(\theta)+\sum_{u,v\in B_{n}}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{s\eta}{|B_{n}|}}Y_{uv}^{s\eta}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}-\frac{s\eta}{2|B_{n}|}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{u\in B_{n}}\Big(\sqrt{(1-s)\eta q}\,y^{\lambda}_{u}\theta_{u}-\frac{(1-s)\eta q}{2}\Big),

where
q\geq 0
is a fixed constant to be chosen later,
Y_{uv}^{s\eta}=\sqrt{\frac{s\eta}{|B_{n}|}}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}+Z_{uv}
and
y^{\lambda}=\sqrt{\lambda}\theta_{0u}+z_{u}
with
\lambda=(1-s)\eta q
and
Z_{uv}
and
z_{u}
are independent
N(0,1)
r.v.’s. From now on we denote the hidden assignment by
(\theta_{0u})_{u\in B_{n}}
in order to distinguish it from a generic vector
(\theta_{u})_{u\in B_{n}}
. The interpolating free energy is

\varphi(s)=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H_{s}(\theta)}\Big\}.

Note that
\varphi(1)=f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)
. On the other end of the interpolation,

\displaystyle\varphi(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H^{\delta}_{B_{n}}(\theta)+\underset{u\in B_{n}}{\sum}(\sqrt{\eta q}y^{\lambda}_{u}\theta_{u}-\frac{\eta q}{2})}\Big\}

\displaystyle=f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta q).

We compute the derivative of
\varphi
using gaussian integration by parts to obtain

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=-\frac{\eta}{4}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle(R_{1,2}(B_{n})-q)^{2}\big\rangle_{s}+\frac{\eta}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle(R_{1,0}(B_{n})-q)^{2}\big\rangle_{s}-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{s}
is the Gibbs measure associated to the Hamiltonian
H_{s}
.
(Here and from now on,
R_{1,0}(B_{n}):=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\sum_{u\in B_{n}}\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}
.)
By Bayes’ rule
R_{1,2}(B_{n})
and
R_{1,0}(B_{n})
are identical in law, and we obtain

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=\frac{\eta}{4}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle(R_{1,2}(B_{n})-q)^{2}\big\rangle_{s}-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}.

Since the first in the right-hand side in the above display is non-negative, we obtain the lower bound
\varphi^{\prime}(s)\geq-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}
for all
s
. Integrating w.r.t.
s
yields the lower bound

f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)\geq f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta q)-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4},

valid for all
n
and all
q
.
From Proposition
6.1
,
f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}
converges to
f^{\textup{{sc}}}
as
n\to\infty
, hence the lower bound

\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)\geq\sup_{q\geq 0}\Big\{f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,\eta q)-\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}\Big\}.

(8.1)

As for the upper, we use the same argument while fixing the value of the overlap
R_{1,0}(B_{n})
to a prescribed value
m
along the interpolation. Define the set
O_{n}:=\{k/|B_{n}|:k\in\mathbb{Z},|k|\leq|B_{n}|\}
and let
m\in O_{n}
. Consider the constrained free energy

\varphi(s;m)=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{\tilde{H}_{s}(\theta)}\mathbf{1}_{R_{1,0}(B_{n})=m}\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{s}(\theta)

\displaystyle=H^{\delta}_{B_{n}}(\theta)+\sum_{u,v\in B_{n}}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{s\eta}{|B_{n}|}}Z_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+\frac{s\eta}{|B_{n}|}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}-\frac{s\eta}{2|B_{n}|}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{u\in B_{n}}\Big(\sqrt{(1-s)\eta q}\,z_{u}\theta_{u}+(1-s)\eta m\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{(1-s)\eta q}{2}\Big).

(Note that this Hamiltonian no longer corresponds to an inference problem as before.)
The derivative reads

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=-\frac{\eta}{4}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle(R_{1,2}(B_{n})-q)^{2}\big\rangle_{s}+\frac{\eta}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle(R_{1,0}(B_{n})-m)^{2}\big\rangle_{s}-\frac{\eta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}.

The middle term vanishes due the constraint on
R_{1,0}(B_{n})
and we obtain the upper bound
\varphi^{\prime}(s)\leq-\frac{\eta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}
. Therefore

\varphi(1;m)\leq\inf_{q\geq 0}\Big\{\varphi(0;m)-\frac{\eta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}\Big\}.

(8.2)

On the one hand we have
\varphi(0;m)\leq\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q)
, where

\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q):=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H^{\delta}_{B_{n}}(\theta)+\underset{u\in B_{n}}{\sum}(\sqrt{\eta q}z_{u}\theta_{u}+\eta m\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{\eta q}{2})}\Big\}.

(8.3)

We can follow the exact same argument leading to Proposition
6.1
to prove that
\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q)
converges to a limit
f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q)
as
n\to\infty
; the only difference being
m
and
q
are not necessarily equal, but this does not alter the argument in any significant way. Moreover, the convergence is uniform on any compact set
K\subset[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

On the other hand, let
Z_{n}(m)=2^{-|B_{n}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B_{n}}}e^{H_{B_{n}}^{\delta}(\theta)+H_{B_{n}}^{\eta}(\theta)}\mathbf{1}_{R_{1,0}(B_{n})=m}
. We have

\displaystyle f_{B_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\sum_{m\in O_{n}}Z_{n}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\max_{m\in O_{n}}Z_{n}(m)+\frac{\log(2|B_{n}|+1)}{|B_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max_{m\in O_{n}}\log Z_{n}(m)+\frac{\log(2|B_{n}|+1)}{|B_{n}|}.

(8.4)

At this point we want to show concentration of the random variables
F_{n}(m):=\log Z_{n}(m)
so that we can compare
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max_{m}F_{n}(m)
with
\max_{m}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}F_{n}(m)
. First, the random variables
\theta_{0u}
are irrelevant as they can be absorbed in
\theta_{u}
through the change of variables
\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}\mapsto\theta_{u}
without changing the distribution of
\{F_{n}(m):m\in O_{n}\}
. So without loss of generality we set
\theta_{0u}=1
for all
u\in B_{n}
.
Now we deal with the randomness of
Z_{uv}
and
Y^{\delta}_{uv}
. We use the so-called entropy method. We see
F_{n}(m)
as a measurable Lipschitz function of the random variables
(Z_{uv})_{u,v\in B_{n}}
and
(Y^{\delta}_{uv})_{u,v\in B_{n},|u-v|=1}
. Since we are only interested in an upper tail bound, it is enough to prove a logarithmic Sobolev inequality of the form

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F_{n}}\big)\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e^{\gamma F_{n}}\big]~~~\mbox{for all}~~\gamma\geq 0,

(8.5)

for some
\sigma_{n}>0
, where we define the ‘entropy’ functional of a positive random variable
Y
by

\mathds{H}(Y):=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}Y\log Y-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}Y\log\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}Y.

In our particular case, we will show that (
8.5
) holds with
\sigma_{n}^{2}=(\frac{\eta}{2}+2dc(\beta))|B_{n}|
for some finite constant
c(\beta)>0
. We momentarily assume this to be true in order to finish the general argument, and then return to its proof later on. Herbst’s argument; see
[
BLM13
]
, then implies a sub-Gaussian bound on the moment generating function:

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}e^{\gamma(F_{n}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}F_{n})}\leq e^{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}/2}~~~\mbox{for all}~~\gamma\geq 0.

This in turn implies a bound on the expected maximum deviation
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max(F_{n}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}F_{n})
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\max_{m\in O_{n}}\{F_{n}(m)-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}F_{n}(m)\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\Big\{\sum_{m\in O_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}e^{\gamma(F_{n}(m)-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}F_{n}(m))}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log(2|B_{n}|+1)}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma\sigma_{n}^{2}}{2}.

We let
\gamma=1/\sqrt{|B_{n}|}
and obtain that the above is bounded by
\mathcal{O}\big(\sqrt{|B_{n}|}\log|B_{n}|\big)
.
Now, coming back to (
8.2
), we obtain

f_{B_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{|B_{n}|}\max_{m\in O_{n}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log Z_{n}(m)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\log|B_{n}|}{\sqrt{|B_{n}|}}\Big).

We notice that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log Z_{n}(m)=\varphi(1;m)
, thus the interpolation upper bound (
8.2
) and (
8.3
) imply

f_{B_{n}}\leq\sup_{m\in[-1,1]}\inf_{q\geq 0}\Big\{\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q)-\frac{\eta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}\Big\}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\log|B_{n}|}{\sqrt{|B_{n}|}}\Big).

(8.6)

Since
\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}
converges uniformly to
\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}
on any compact set, this implies the upper bound

\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{B_{n}}\leq\sup_{m\in[-1,1]}\inf_{q\geq 0}\Big\{\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}(\delta,\eta m,\eta q)-\frac{\eta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{\eta q^{2}}{4}\Big\}.

(8.7)

Next, we show that the upper and lower bounds (
8.7
) and (
8.1
) match. For any
m\in[-1,1]
we obtain an upper bound on (
8.7
) by letting
q=|m|
. Now observe that the map
r\mapsto\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}(\delta,r,s)
is even since
r\mapsto\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}_{B_{n}}}(\delta,r,s)
is even. This implies the bound

\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{B_{n}}\leq\sup_{m\in[-1,1]}\Big\{\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}(\delta,\eta|m|,\eta|m|)-\frac{\eta|m|^{2}}{4}\Big\},

which is identical to the lower bound since
\widetilde{f^{\textup{{sc}}}}(\delta,r,r)=f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta,r)
for
r\geq 0
, and thus we conclude that
f_{B_{n}}
has a limit given by the above variational formula.

Now it remains to prove the log-Sobolev inequality (
8.5
).

Proof of the bound (
8.5
).

We lighten the notation and denote by
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{N})
the random variables
\{Z_{uv}:u,v\in B_{n}\}\cup\{Y^{\delta}_{uv}:u,v\in B_{n},|u-v|=1\}
: we have
X_{i}=Z_{uv}
for some one-to-one mapping
(u,v)\mapsto i
for
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
, and
X_{i}=Y^{\delta}_{uv}
for
N_{1}+1\leq i\leq N
. Denote
F=F(X_{1},\cdots,X_{N})
the random variable
F_{n}(m)=\log Z_{n}(m)
. Denote
X^{\setminus k}=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k-1},X_{k+1},\cdots,X_{N})
. The following tensorization formula for the entropy
\mathds{H}
is well known; see e.g.
[
BLM13
]
: for all
\gamma\geq 0
,

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big)\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)\Big].

(8.8)

Therefore is suffices to bound the entropies
\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)
in which
F
is seen as a univariate function of
X_{k}
.
For
1\leq k\leq N_{1}
the variable
X_{k}
is Gaussian
N(0,1)
. We use the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.4 in
[
BLM13
]
)

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[(\partial_{k}F)^{2}e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big],

valid for all
\gamma\geq 0
. Since
\partial_{k}F=\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{|B_{n}|}}\langle\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\rangle
, we have
|\partial_{k}F|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{|B_{n}|}}
, and therefore

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2|B_{n}|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big].

(8.9)

For
N_{1}+1\leq k\leq N
the variable
X_{k}
is Rademacher with probability
1-p
for taking the value
1
and probability
p
for
-1
. We use the following logarithmic Sobolev inequality for Rademacher random variables (Theorem 5.2 in
[
BLM13
]
) :

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)\leq\frac{c(p)}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(e^{\gamma F/2}-e^{\gamma\tilde{F}^{(k)}/2}\big)^{2}\big|X^{\setminus k}\Big],

where
c(p)=\frac{1}{1-2p}\log(\frac{1-p}{p})=2\beta/\delta
and
\tilde{F}^{(k)}=F(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}^{\prime},\cdots,X_{N})
where
X_{k}^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
X_{k}
.
By convexity of the exponential,
e^{t}\leq e^{s}+(t-s)e^{t}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, so

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(e^{\gamma F/2}-e^{\gamma\tilde{F}^{(k)}/2}\big)^{2}\big|X^{\setminus k}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[(F-\tilde{F}^{(k)})^{2}e^{\gamma F}\mathbf{1}_{F\geq\tilde{F}^{(k)}}\big|X^{\setminus k}\Big]

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[(\tilde{F}^{(k)}-F)^{2}e^{\gamma\tilde{F}^{(k)}}\mathbf{1}_{F\leq\tilde{F}^{(k)}}\big|X^{\setminus k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Big[(F-\tilde{F}^{(k)})^{2}e^{\gamma F}\mathbf{1}_{F\geq\tilde{F}^{(k)}}\big|X^{\setminus k}\Big].

The last line follows since the two terms in the first inequality are identical. Now since
\partial_{k}F=\beta\langle\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\rangle
which is
\leq\beta
in absolute value, we have
|F-\tilde{F}^{(k)}|\leq\beta|X_{k}-X_{k}^{\prime}|\leq 2\beta
. Plugging this estimate into the above bounds we obtain

\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big)\leq c(p)\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e^{\gamma F}\big|X^{\setminus k}\big].

(8.10)

Now we combine the two bounds (
8.9
) and (
8.10
) into (
8.8
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{H}\big(e^{\gamma F}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2|B_{n}|}{|B_{n}|\choose 2}+c(p)\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}d|B_{n}|\Big)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e^{\gamma F}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}}{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e^{\gamma F}\big],

with
\sigma_{n}^{2}=(\frac{\eta}{2}+2c(p)\beta^{2}d)|B_{n}|
.

\blacksquare

8.3
Proof of Propositions
6.5
and
6.9

In this subsection we prove convergence of the free energies of the one-block and two-block posteriors respectively. The difference with the setting of Proposition
6.3
is the inhomogeneity of the SNR in the GOE side information, e.g., pairs of vertices in
B\setminus B^{\prime}
have a different SNR than the ones in
B\cap B^{\prime}
. Although we apply the same approach, the inhomogeneity will lead to technical complications which prevent us from proving equality of the upper and lower bounds achieved via the interpolation method. Nevertheless, Assumption
1
allows us to salvage this situation for small
\eta
.

We only write a detailed argument for Proposition
6.5
; the proof extends verbatim to Proposition
6.9
.

Recall that for a given block
B
, the one-block posterior is given by

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big(\theta_{x}\big)_{x\in B}\big|Y^{\delta}_{B},Y^{\eta}_{B}\Big)=\frac{1}{Z_{B}}\omega^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}_{B}(\theta)\cdot e^{H_{B}^{\delta}(\theta)},

and for two adjacent blocks
B
and
B^{\prime}
, the two-block posterior is

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big(\theta_{x}\big)_{x\in B\cup B^{\prime}}\big|\bigcup_{A\in\{B,B^{\prime}\}}\{Y^{\delta}_{A},Y^{\eta}_{A}\}\Big)=\frac{1}{Z_{B\cup B^{\prime}}}\omega_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}(\theta)\cdot e^{H_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\theta)},

where

H_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\theta):=\beta\sum_{\underset{|u-v|=1}{u,v\in B\cup B^{\prime}}}Y^{\delta}_{uv}\theta_{u}\theta_{v},

and for
\theta=(\theta_{B},\theta_{B^{\prime}})\in\{\pm 1\}^{B\cup B^{\prime}}
,

\displaystyle\omega_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}e^{H^{\eta}_{B^{\prime\prime}}(\theta_{B})}\cdot\prod_{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B^{\prime}}e^{H^{\eta}_{B^{\prime\prime}}(\theta_{B^{\prime}})}.

The free energies of the above posteriors are

\displaystyle f_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B}}\omega^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}_{B}(\theta)\cdot e^{H_{B}^{\delta}(\theta)}\Big\}~~~\mbox{and}

\displaystyle f_{B\cup B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B\cup B^{\prime}|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B\cup B^{\prime}}}\omega^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}_{B\cup B^{\prime}}(\theta)\cdot e^{H_{B\cup B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\theta)}\Big\}.

8.4
Limit of
f_{B}

The same arguments already used can be applied: rather than having one parameter in the interpolation argument, we will have several, each one assigned to a specific spatial region. As before, we prove the lower and upper bounds separately.
The overlap replacement in the interpolation proceeds according to the following table with
2d+1
free parameters:

\displaystyle R_{1,2}\big(B^{\bullet}\big)~

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow~q^{\bullet}

\displaystyle R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})~

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow~q_{B\cap B^{\prime}},~~~\forall~B^{\prime}\sim B,

In the above, we denote
B^{\bullet}=B\setminus\underset{B^{\prime}\sim B}{\cup}B^{\prime}
.
Overlaps in other regions are then obtained as convex combinations of
\{q^{\bullet},q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}:B^{\prime}\sim B\}
. For instance, with
\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{|B\cap B^{\prime}|}{|B|}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{1,2}(B)~

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow~q_{B}:=(1-2d\alpha^{\prime})q^{\bullet}+\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\alpha^{\prime}q_{B\cap B^{\prime}},

\displaystyle R_{1,2}(B\setminus B^{\prime})~

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow~q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}:=\frac{1-2d\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}q^{\bullet}+\sum_{\underset{B^{\prime\prime}\neq B^{\prime}}{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}}\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}q_{B\cap B^{\prime\prime}}.

For all the arguments to come, we will be neglecting a
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}$}}_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1)
error stemming from replacing
\alpha^{\prime}
which a priori varies with
L^{\circlearrowleft}
with its limit
\alpha
. This can be safely done because all involved quantities are Lipschitz in
\alpha^{\prime}
. So tacitly assume
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
with no further comment.

We let
h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B};\lambda):=\sum_{x\in B}(\sqrt{\lambda}y_{x}\theta_{x}-\lambda/2)
, where
y_{x}=\sqrt{\lambda}\theta_{x}+z_{x}
.
With this notation, we interpolate between the two Hamiltonians

\displaystyle H^{\eta}_{B^{\prime}}(\theta_{B})~

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow~h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B};t\eta q_{B})+h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B\cap B^{\prime}};(1-t)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}})+h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B\setminus B^{\prime}};(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}).

Interpolating à la Guerra, the derivative along the interpolation path reads

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\Big\{\frac{t\eta}{4}\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle\big(R_{1,2}(B)-q_{B}\big)^{2}\big\rangle-q_{B}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\alpha\frac{(1-t)\eta}{4}\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle\big(R_{1,2}(B\cap B^{\prime})-q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\big\rangle-q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+(1-\alpha)\frac{(1-t)\eta}{4}\Big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big\langle\big(R_{1,2}(B\setminus B^{\prime})-q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\big\rangle-q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)\Big\}.

Dropping the squared differences which are nonnegative we obtain a lower bound

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\Big(tq_{B}^{2}+\alpha(1-t)q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}+(1-\alpha)(1-t)q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big).

At the decoupled end of the interpolation, the free energy reads

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\frac{1}{|B|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B}}e^{H_{B}^{\delta}(\theta)}\omega(\theta)\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\omega(\theta)

\displaystyle=\prod_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\exp\Big\{h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B};t\eta q_{B})+h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B\cap B^{\prime}};(1-t)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}})+h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B\setminus B^{\prime}};(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}})\Big\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\exp\big\{h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B^{\bullet}};r^{\bullet})\}\cdot\prod_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\exp\big\{h^{\textup{{sc}}}(\theta_{B\cap B^{\prime}};r_{B\cap B^{\prime}})\big\},

where

\displaystyle r^{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}(t\eta q_{B}+(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}),

(8.11)

\displaystyle r_{B\cap B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=2dt\eta q_{B}+(1-t)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}+\sum_{\underset{B^{\prime\prime}\neq B^{\prime}}{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}}(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime\prime}}.

(8.12)

From this expression we can obtain an asymptotic formula for
\varphi(0)
as
L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
. We partition
B
into
B^{\bullet}\cup_{B^{\prime}\sim B}(B\cap B^{\prime})
, and observe that omitting the interactions corresponding to the edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
crossing the boundaries of this partition in the Hamiltonian
H^{\delta}_{B}(\theta)
contributes an error term
o_{L^{\circlearrowleft}}(1)
:

\lim_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}\varphi(0)=(1-2d\alpha)f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta;r^{\bullet})+\alpha\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta;r_{B^{\prime}}).

Thus we obtain a lower bound on the free energy
f_{B}
:

\liminf_{L^{\circlearrowleft}\to\infty}f_{B}\geq\phi_{1},

(8.13)

where, letting
\underline{q}=\{q^{\bullet},q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}:B^{\prime}\sim B\}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\underline{q}\in[0,1]^{2d+1}}\Big\{(1-2d\alpha)f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta;r^{\bullet})+\alpha\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}f^{\textup{{sc}}}(\delta;r_{B\cap B^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\Big(t\eta q_{B}^{2}+\alpha(1-t)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}+(1-\alpha)(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)\Big\}.

(8.14)

Now we prove an upper bound by restricting to configurations of fixed overlaps
\underline{m}=\{m^{\bullet},m_{B\cap B^{\prime}}:B^{\prime}\sim B\}
, exactly in the same way as done for
f_{B_{n}}(\delta,\eta)
.
We consider the restricted free energy

\varphi(s;\underline{m})=\frac{1}{|B|}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\log\Big\{2^{-|B|}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B}}\omega_{B}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny SK}}(\theta;s)e^{H^{\delta}_{B}(\theta)}\mathbf{1}_{\underset{R_{1,0}(B^{\bullet})=m^{\bullet}}{R_{1,0}(B\cap B^{\prime})=m_{B\cap B^{\prime}}\forall B^{\prime}\sim B,}}\Big\}

where

and for each
B^{\prime}\sim B
,

H^{\eta}_{B^{\prime}}(\theta;s)=I+II+III,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in B}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{ts\eta}{|B|}}Z_{uv}^{(a,\bullet)}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+\frac{ts\eta}{|B|}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}-\frac{ts\eta}{2|B|}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{u\in B}\Big(\sqrt{(1-s)t\eta q_{B}}\,z^{(a,\bullet)}_{u}\theta_{u}+(1-s)t\eta m_{B}\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{(1-s)t\eta q_{B}}{2}\Big),

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|}}Z_{uv}^{(a,\cap)}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}-\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{2|B\cap B^{\prime}|}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{u\in B\cap B^{\prime}}\Big(\sqrt{(1-t)(1-s)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}}\,z_{u}^{(a,\cap)}\theta_{u}+(1-s)(1-t)\eta m_{B\cap B^{\prime}}\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{(1-t)(1-s)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in B\setminus B^{\prime}}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{|B\setminus B^{\prime}|}}Z_{uv}^{(a,\setminus)}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}+\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{|B\setminus B^{\prime}|}\theta_{u}\theta_{v}\theta_{0u}\theta_{0v}-\frac{(1-t)s\eta}{2|B\setminus B^{\prime}|}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{u\in B\setminus B^{\prime}}\Big(\sqrt{(1-t)(1-s)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}}\,z_{u}^{(a,\setminus)}\theta_{u}+(1-s)(1-t)\eta m_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}\theta_{u}\theta_{0u}-\frac{(1-t)(1-s)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}}{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle m_{B}

\displaystyle=(1-2d\alpha)m^{\bullet}+\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\alpha m_{B\cap B^{\prime}}~~~\mbox{and}

\displaystyle m_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-2d\alpha}{1-\alpha}m^{\bullet}+\sum_{\underset{B^{\prime\prime}\neq B^{\prime}}{B^{\prime\prime}\sim B}}\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}m_{B\cap B^{\prime\prime}}.

Observe that at

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\Big(t\eta m_{B}^{2}+\alpha(1-t)\eta m_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}+(1-\alpha)(1-t)\eta m_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\Big(t\eta q_{B}^{2}+\alpha(1-t)\eta q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}^{2}+(1-\alpha)(1-t)\eta q_{B\setminus B^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)\Big\},

(8.16)

where

Next, if we could argue that the supremum in

8.5
Reducing the number of parameters

We show that under Assumption
1
, the variational formulas defining
\phi_{1}
Eq. (

\tilde{\phi}_{1}
Eq. (

) are achieved when all the overlap parameters become equal when

\phi_{1}=\phi(\delta,2d\eta)

).

We start by writing the first-order stationarity conditions for the optimality of
\underline{q}
. Let
F=F(\underline{q})
be the potential being maximized in (

). Via straightforward computations we can write the derivatives of the potential

in a condensed form.
We fix an ordering of the variables

as a vector of size
2d+1

For all

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}\underline{q}_{i}}

\displaystyle=(1-2\alpha d)\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\bullet}}{\mathrm{d}\underline{q}_{i}}\cdot\big({f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta;r^{\bullet})-\frac{q^{\bullet}}{2}\big)+\alpha\sum_{B^{\prime}\sim B}\frac{\mathrm{d}r_{B\cap B^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{d}\underline{q}_{i}}\cdot\big({f^{\textup{{sc}}}}^{\prime}(\delta;r_{B\cap B^{\prime}})-\frac{q_{B\cap B^{\prime}}}{2}\big).

Observe from the above formula that optimizers

of (

Define the

.
With this notation the gradient of the potential

\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}\underline{q}_{i}}\Big)_{i\in[2d+1]}=J\underline{x},

Proof.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\beta

Number-Rigidity and β \beta -Circular Riesz gas
David Dereudre, Thibaut Vasseur

Abstract
For an inverse temperature β > 0 \beta>0 , we define the β \beta -circular Riesz gas on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} as any microscopic thermodynamic limit of Gibbs particle systems on the torus interacting via the Riesz potential g ⁡ ( x ) = ‖ x ‖ − s g(x)=\|x\|^{-s} . We focus on the non integrable case d − 1 < s < d d-1<s<d . Our main result ensures, for any dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and inverse temperature β > 0 \beta>0 , the existence of a β \beta -circular Riesz gas which is not number-rigid. Recall that a point process is said number rigid if the number of points in a bounded Borel set Δ \Delta is a function of the point configuration outside Δ \Delta . It is the first time that the non number-rigidity is proved for a Gibbs point process interacting via a non integrable potential. We follow a statistical physics approach based on the canonical DLR equations. It is inspired by the recent paper [ 4 ] where the authors prove the number-rigidity of the Sine β \text{Sine}_{\beta} process.

Introduction

The pairwise Riesz potential
g(x)=\|x\|^{-s}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is abundantly studied in several domains of mathematics as statistical mechanics, potential theory, optimization, etc. The particular case
s=d-2
corresponds to the the Coulomb pair potential coming from the electrostatic theory. The general case
s\leq d
is particularly interesting and challenging since the potential is not integrable at infinity.

The thermodynamic limits of associated canonical Gibbs measures at inverse temperature
\beta>0
are the natural microscopic equilibrium states appearing in the bulk of systems with a large number of particles. Their studies are old topics in physics and mathematical physics literature
[
12
,
20
]
. Recently a general large deviation principle at the microscopic level has been established
[
18
]
with the rate function equals to the entropy plus the renormalized energy times
\beta
. The general description of thermodynamic limits or minimizers of the rate function is mainly open and only few results have been proved. The study of the special case ”
s=0
” (
g(x)=-\log\|x\|
by convention), corresponding to the so-called
\log
-gas potential, is more advanced. For instance in the case
d=1
, the thermodynamic limit or the minimizer is unique and called
\text{Sine}_{\beta}
[
25
,
15
,
6
]
. It is associated to the eigenvalues of random matrices
[
5
,
7
]
and it is determinantal for
\beta=2
. In the present paper we study some stochastic properties of thermodynamic limits with periodic boundary conditions in the case
d-1<s<d
.

The definition of the Riesz interaction on the torus is not obvious. An useful variant of the Coulomb energy is the periodic Jellium which can also be defined for the Riesz potential, as explained in
[
22
]
. The construction of the periodic energy for general long range potential and periodic discrete configurations has been also given in
[
13
]
. The equivalence between the ground state energy of such periodic versions has been recently achieved in
[
19
]
. We follow these ideas to define the periodic energy
H_{n}
of a configuration of
n
points lying on a torus
\mathbb{T}_{n}
of volume
n
. It is the limit of the mean energy (with neutralizing background) of copies of the configuration, when the number of copies tends to infinity. It corresponds to the natural energy of an infinite periodic crystal. Then, for an inverse temperature parameter
\beta>0
, the finite volume canonical Gibbs measures
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
of energy
H_{n}
is defined on
\mathbb{T}_{n}
and the microscopic thermodynamic limit is obtained by letting
n
going to infinity. Based on the compactness of entropy level-set, the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
admits accumulation points which are called
\beta
-circular Riesz gases. Unfortunately the uniqueness of accumulation points is not guaranteed in general and probably a phase transition (non uniqueness) could occur for special values of
\beta
.

We are mainly interested in the number-rigidity of such
\beta
-circular Riesz gases. A point process is said number rigid if the number of points in a bounded Borel set
\Delta
is a function of the point configuration outside
\Delta
. This property has been introduced in
[
14
]
and studied for a large variety of point processes as for instance the Gaussian zeros and Ginibre process
[
11
]
, perturbed lattices
[
21
]
, stable matchings
[
16
]
or Pfaffian point processes
[
2
]
. The number rigidity of the
\text{Sine}_{\beta}
process for any inverse temperature
\beta>0
has been proved independently in
[
4
]
and
[
23
]
with two drastically different approaches. In
[
23
]
the authors follow the strategy of
[
11
]
which consists of controlling the variance of linear statistics whereas in
[
4
]
the authors lean on a statistical physics approach which has inspired the present work.
Our main result claims that for
d-1<s<d
and any inverse temperature
\beta>0
, there exists a
\beta
-circular Riesz gas
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
which is not number rigid. More precisely, in Theorem
1.11
, we show that, conditionally on the configuration outside a compact set
\Delta
, the number of points in
\Delta
can take any value with positive probability under
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
. Since the
\text{Sine}_{\beta}
process corresponds to the Riesz interaction with
d=1
and
s=0
, our result and
[
4
]
ensures that in dimension
d=1
the number-rigidity appears for
s\leq d-1
. We believe that this statement occurs in any dimension
d\geq 1
but presently it is a conjecture.

An important tool used in the present paper are the so-called canonical Dobrushin-Lanford-Ruelle (DLR) equations. They give a local conditional description of the
\beta
-circular Riesz gases as stated in Theorem
1.8
. This formalism has a long history since the sixties where rigorous results in statistical mechanics have been developed
[
24
]
. Recently they have been used successfully for the
\text{Sine}_{\beta}
process since they are the main ingredient of the proof of its number rigidity
[
4
]
and, coupled with optimal transport theory, to obtain a central limit theorem for the fluctuations of linear statistics
[
17
]
. Our main contribution is to use the canonical DLR equations to prove the non number-rigidity of
\beta
-circular Riesz gases in the opposite way of
[
4
]
where they proved number-rigidity of the
\text{Sine}_{\beta}
process. In particular, we formalise the idea to move points toward infinity (and so to remove them) and therefore we show that the number of points in a compact set conditionally to the outside is arbitrary. Moreover this allows to go further in the Gibbsian description of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
. We show in Theorem
1.14
that the distribution of points in a compact
\Delta
conditionally to the outside admits a density with respect to the Poisson point process in
\Delta
with a standard expression
e^{-\beta H_{\Delta}}
where
H_{\Delta}
is the energy of points in
\Delta
given the outside of
\Delta
. In particular we give a sense to the energy of a point inside an infinite configuration. More technically, we pass from a canonical description of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
to a grand canonical one, and our theorem is then a reminiscent of an equivalence of ensembles result, as described in
[
8
]
. The full DLR description, obtained in the case
d-1<s<d
, is also expected for
s<d-1
(in particular for the Coulomb case
s=d-2
as mentioned in
[
1
]
) but so far nothing has been proved for the moment.

The plan of the paper is the following. In a first section we present the model of
\beta
-circular Riesz gas and give the results. The second part is devoted to the proofs.

1
Model and Results

1.0
Notations

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|x\|
is the usual Euclidean norm of
x
and
\|x\|_{\infty}
its sup-norm. We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of the Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and by
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the bounded Borel subsets. The Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted
\lambda^{d}{}
. For an integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\Lambda_{n}=[-\sqrt[d]{n}/2,\sqrt[d]{n}/2]
the box of volume
n
.

The space of
point configurations

\mathcal{C}
is the set of all locally finite subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{C}:=\{\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}:\forall\Delta\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|\gamma\cap\Delta|<+\infty\},

where we use the notation
|\cdot|
for the cardinality of a set. We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{f}
the set of all finite point configurations, and, for
\Lambda
a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
the subset of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of point configurations in
\Lambda
. Configurations can also be seen as locally finite counting measures, in particular we use the convenient notation

\int f(x)\gamma(dx)=\sum_{x\in\gamma}f(x)
.

For
\Delta
a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we introduce the counting function
N_{\Delta}
defined for
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
as
N_{\Delta}(\gamma)=|\gamma\cap\Delta|
. We equip the set
\mathcal{C}
with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by these counting functions
\mathcal{F}:=\sigma(N_{\Delta},\Delta\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. For
\Lambda
a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we equip
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
with the
\sigma
-agebra
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}:=\sigma(N_{\Delta},\Delta\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\Lambda))
.

We study probability measures on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
and refer to them as
point processes
in place of their distribution.
If
\mathbb{P}
is a point process, its projection to the space
(\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda},\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda})
is denoted
\mathbb{P}_{|\Lambda}
or
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
, depending on possible ambiguities.

For
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\geq 1
an integer,
\tau_{u}
denotes the translation of vector
u
, and
\tau_{u}^{n}
the translation of vector
u
in
\Lambda_{n}
viewed as a torus. A point process
\mathbb{P}
is said
stationary
if for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the identity
\mathbb{P}\circ\tau_{u}^{-1}=\mathbb{P}
holds, and we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\text{sta}}(\mathcal{C})
the set of all stationary point processes.

If
\mathbb{P}
is a stationary point process, there exists a constant
i(\mathbb{P})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
, called intensity of
\mathbb{P}
, such that for every bounded Borel subset
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
E_{\mathbb{P}}(N_{\Delta})=i(\mathbb{P})\lambda^{d}{}(\Delta)
. If
i(\mathbb{P})
is finite, we say that
\mathbb{P}
has
finite intensity
. More generally, if we only have the existence of a constant
\kappa>0
such that for any Borel subset
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
E_{\mathbb{P}}(N_{\Delta})\leq\kappa\lambda^{d}{}(\Delta)
, we said that
\mathbb{P}
has
intensity bounded
by
\kappa
.

We denote by
\Pi
the stationary Poisson point process of intensity equal to
1
. Recall briefly that
\Pi
is the unique point process of intensity
1
such that two disjoint regions of space are independent under
\Pi
.

A function
f:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is said
local
if there exists a bounded Borel set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
f
is
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}
-mesurable (i.e. for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
,
f(\gamma)=f(\gamma_{\Delta})
). The function
f
is called
tame
if for every configuration
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
,
\lvert f(\gamma)\rvert\leq b(1+N_{\Delta}(\gamma))
, for
b
a positive constant. The
local topology
is the smallest topology on
\mathcal{C}
such that the mappings
\mathbb{P}\mapsto\int fd\mathbb{P}
are continuous for every local tame function
f
.

1.1
Circular-Riesz interaction

We are interested in continuum particle system interacting via the Riesz potential
g
defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
g(x)=\|x\|^{-s}
, with
s\in(d-1,d)
(and the convention
1/0=+\infty
). The natural pairwise energy for a finite point configuration
\gamma
is given by

H(\gamma)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma}g(x-y).

Problems coming from the non-integrability at infinity of the potential can be solved by introducing a natural compensation inspired from physics (Jellium model). Charged particles interact together and with an uniform background of opposite charge. It corresponds to

\widetilde{H}_{n}(\gamma)=\iint_{\Lambda_{n}^{2}\setminus\text{Diag}}g(x-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx).(\gamma(dy)-dy),

for
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
. The diagonal of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
defined by
\text{Diag}=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}:x\neq y\}
has been removed to avoid the singularity. This new energy is no longer pairwise, depends on the size of the box where the configuration is lying and does not have translation invariance property. We correct these difficulties by building the energy on the torus
\Lambda_{n}
where the particles interact with infinitely many copies of themselves. For
\gamma
a point configuration in
\Lambda_{n}
, we define
[k]=\{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\|r\|_{\infty}\leq k\}
and

\gamma+n^{1/d}[k]=\{x+n^{1/d}u:x\in\gamma,u\in[k]\},

consisting of
(2k+1)^{d}
copies of
\gamma
in order to form a point configuration in
\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}
. The next proposition gives a sense to the mean energy per copy when
k
goes to infinity.

Proposition 1.1
.

For
\gamma
a point configuration in
\Lambda_{n}
such that
N_{\Lambda_{n}}(\gamma)=n
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}(\gamma+n^{1/d}[k])}{(2k+1)^{d}}=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma}g_{n}(x-y)+n\varepsilon_{n},

where
\varepsilon_{n}
goes to zero as
n
goes to infinity and
g_{n}
is the periodized version of the Riesz interaction defined as

g_{n}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(g(x+kn^{1/d})-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}g(y+kn^{1/d})dy\right).

We recover a pairwise interaction up to a constant which will disappear in the partition function since it does not depend on the configuration. Finally the definition of the circular-Riesz energy on the torus
\Lambda_{n}
is

H_{n}(\gamma)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma}g_{n}(x-y),

for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

The energy function
H_{n}
and the periodized potential
g_{n}
satisfy the following properties:

(i)

There exists a constant
A\leq 0
such that for every integer
n\geq 1
and every point configuration
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
such that
|\gamma|=n
, we have
H_{n}(\gamma)\geq An
.

(ii)

For every
u\in\Lambda_{n}
and every configuration
\gamma
in
\Lambda_{n}
we have
H_{n}(\tau_{u}^{n}(\gamma))=H_{n}(\gamma)
.

(iii)

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for every integer
n\geq 1
and every point
x\in\Lambda_{n}
we have

|g_{n}(x)-g(x)|\leq cn^{-s/d}.

Property
(i)
corresponds to the so-called
stability property
and is inherited from
\widetilde{H}_{n}
. It is important to notice that it occurs only under the
charge balancing condition

|\gamma|=\lambda^{d}{}(\Lambda_{n})
. Property (ii) is the
invariance under torus translations
of
H_{n}
, which also holds for
g_{n}
. Finally, Property (iii) expresses that for large
n
, the periodized potential
g_{n}
looks like
g
.

1.2
From finite volume Gibbs measures to
\beta
-Circular Riesz gases

For
\Lambda
a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lambda^{d}{}(\Lambda)>0
and
N
an integer, let
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda,N}
be the
binomial point process
on
\mathcal{C}
which consists of throwing
N
independent and uniformly distributed points in
\Lambda
. For an integer
n\geq 1
and
\beta>0
a parameter called the
inverse temperature
, we define the canonical partition function as

Z^{\beta}_{n}=\int e^{-\beta H_{n}(\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(d\gamma).

Lemma 1.3
.

For all
\beta>0
, there exists two constants
0<a_{\beta}<b_{\beta}
such that for all integer
n\geq 1

a_{\beta}^{n}\leq Z^{\beta}_{n}\leq b_{\beta}^{n}.

The lemma claims that the logarithm of the partition function behaves as the volume of the system which is usual and expected for stable system in statical physics. We are now able to define the finite volume canonical Gibbs measures.

Definition 1.4
.

For an integer
n\geq 1
and a real number
\beta>0
, the canonical Gibbs measure in the finite volume
\Lambda_{n}
with energy
H_{n}
and inverse temperature
\beta
is the probability measure on
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
defined by

\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}=\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{n}}e^{-\beta H_{n}}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}.

The term canonical, coming from statistical physics, refers to the fact that for a finite system, the number of particles is fixed. The next step involves the thermodynamic limit when
n
goes to infinity.

Proposition 1.5
.

The set
\{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is sequentially compact for the local topology. Any accumulation point is a stationary probability measure on
\mathcal{C}
with intensity equal to 1, called
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas
.

We do not know if the convergence occurs for the whole sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. The existence of different accumulation points would correspond to a phase transition phenomenon ensuring the coexistence of different equilibrium states at the microscopic level. This phenomenon is expected for
d\geq 2
and some special values of
\beta
. In particular, the marginal laws of
\beta
-Circular Riesz gases could be different and they are mainly intractable. Nevertheless the conditional marginal laws are explicit via the DLR equations.

Remark 1.6

(
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas with arbitrary intensity)
.

To construct point processes with a given intensity
\rho>0
, the finite volume Gibbs point process
\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\rho}_{n}
has to be built from the reference measure
\mathbb{B}_{\rho^{-1/d}\Lambda_{n},n}
. According to the scaling property of
g
, the energy becomes
H_{n}^{\rho}(\gamma)=\rho^{s/d}H_{n}(\rho^{1/d}\gamma)

for
\gamma\subset\rho^{-1}\Lambda_{n}
with
|\gamma|=n
.
Therefore, a
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas of intensity
\rho
is obtained as a dilatation of factor
\rho^{-1/d}
of a
(\beta\rho^{s/d})
-Circular Riesz gas of intensity 1.

1.3
Canonical DLR equations

In this section, we give a Gibbsian description of
\beta
-Circular Riesz gases via the so-called canonical DLR equations. For an integer
p\geq 1
, a bounded Borel set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and two configurations
\eta
and
\gamma
, we introduce the cost of moving the points of
\eta
in
\Delta
from the origin into the field created by
\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}

M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)=\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}\setminus\Delta}}\left[g(x-y)-g(y)\right].

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a point process with bounded intensity and
\Delta
be a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every point configuration
\gamma
and for every point configuration
\eta
the following limit exists and is finite

M^{\vphantom{(p)}}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma):=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma).

The quantity
M^{\vphantom{(p)}}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)
represents the cost to the points of
\eta
in
\Delta
from the origin into the field created by the full configuration
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}
be a
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas,
\Delta
be a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f
be a bounded measurable function. Then

\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}(d\gamma)=\iint\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{\Delta}(\gamma)}f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}(d\gamma),

with the normalisation constant

Z^{\beta}_{\Delta}(\gamma)=\int e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta).

The DLR equations claim that the behaviour of the points in a bounded set given an exterior configuration is gibbsian, with an energy coming from the interaction between the points and the cost of moving them from the origin to their positions in the field created by the exterior.

Given a bounded measurable function
f
and a bounded Borel set
\Delta
, it is useful to use the notation

f_{\Delta}(\gamma)=\int\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{\Delta}(\gamma)}f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta).

The DLR equations can be reformulated as follow

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}}(f)=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}}(f_{\Delta}).

Remark 1.9

(canonical DLR equations as conditional expectations)
.

Introducing the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}=\sigma(N_{\Delta},\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})
, canonical DLR equations can be reformulated in terms of conditional expectations. Let
f
be a bounded measurable function and
g
be a bounded
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}
-mesurable function. We have
(f\cdot g)_{\Delta}=f_{\Delta}\cdot g
and Theorem
1.8
gives
E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}}(f\cdot g)=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}}(f_{\Delta}\cdot g)
, which means that
f_{\Delta}=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}}(f\mid\mathcal{E}_{\Delta})
.

At this point, it seems natural to ask if we can have a conditional description of the marginal law based only on
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}}
, or in other words, to ask how the number of points in
\Delta
depends on the exterior. The number rigidity corresponds to an extreme case.

1.4
Non Number-Rigidity

Definition 1.10
.

A point process
\mathbb{P}
is said
number-rigid
if for any bounded Borel set
\Delta
, there exists a measurable function
F_{\Delta}
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every point configuration
\gamma
we have
N_{\Delta}(\gamma)=F_{\Delta}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})
.

In particular, for a number-rigid point process, the canonical DLR equation on the set
\Delta
would express the conditional law with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}
in place of
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}
(see remark
1.9
). In the range
s\in(d-1,d)
we exhibit a
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas which is not number-rigid.

Theorem 1.11
.

There exists a
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
such that for any compact subset
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\lambda^{d}{}(\Delta)>0
, then for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-a.e.
\gamma
and every integer
k

\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)>0.

In particular,
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
is not Number-Rigid.

Proof.

The main result is proved Section 3.3, we only provide here the non-number rigidity. If we assume that
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
is number-rigid then
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost surely, the function
N_{\Delta}
is equal to a
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}}
-mesurable function
F_{\Delta}
. Hence for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
and for every
k\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}\left[1_{F_{\Delta}=k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}}\right](\gamma)=1_{F_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)=1_{N_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)>0,

which is impossible.
∎

Remark 1.12

(Deletion tolerance of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
)
.

The previous result is related to the notion of
deletion tolerance
for point processes, especially investigated in
[
14
]
. A point process
\Gamma
(here viewed as a random variable) is said deletion tolerant if for every random variable
X
such that
X\in\Gamma
almost surely, the law of the new point process
\Gamma\setminus X
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of the point process
\Gamma
. Applying Theorem 1 of
[
14
]
and Theorem
1.11
shows that the point process
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
is deletion tolerant.

In
[
4
]
they give a sense to the canonical DLR equations in the case
d=1
and
s=d-1=0
(which correspond to the logarithmic potential) and show that a point processes which satisfies the canonical DLR equations has to be number-rigid. Our result seems to suggest that all point processes (not only
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
) satisfying the DLR equations for
d-1<s<d
are not number rigid. We conjecture that the number-rigidity should occur for
s\leq d-1
at any dimension
d
(in particular for the Coulomb case
s=d-2
). But define DLR equations and exhibit a point process that satisfies them for
s\leq d-1
and
d\geq 2
looks challenging and has not been achieved yet.

1.5
Grand canonical DLR equations

We investigate more precisely the point process
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
introduced in Theorem
1.11
. The next proposition provides a way to define the local energy of a point in a configuration.

Proposition 1.13
.

There exists a sequence
(C^{\beta}_{\star,\Lambda_{p}})_{p\geq 1}
of measurable functions from
{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathcal{C}
to
{\mathbb{R}}
such that for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every configuration
\gamma
the following limit exists and is finite

h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\gamma):=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}}g(y-x)+C^{\beta}_{\star,\Lambda_{p}}(N_{\Lambda_{p}}(\gamma),\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}^{c}}).

The natural candidate to define the energy of the point
x
inside the configuration
\gamma
is
\sum_{y\in\gamma}g(x-y)
, but it is always equal to infinity as soon as the point process is stationary with finite intensity. The sequence of functions
(C^{\beta}_{\star,\Lambda_{p}})_{p\geq 1}
solve this problem by compensating the partial sums
\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}}g(x-y)
in order to obtain a limit. We call such a sequence a
compensator
.

Proposition
1.13
allows to define the energy of a point in
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every configuration. Given a bounded Borel set
\Delta
and
\eta=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
a configuration in
\Delta
, we can define the local energy of
\eta
in
\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}
as

H^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)=h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{1},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})+h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{2},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{x_{1}\})+\dots+h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{n},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{x_{2},\dots x_{n-1}\}).

The fact that the previous formula is well-defined is discussed in Section
2.5.3
. The next theorem states the grand canonical DLR equations satisfied by
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
\Delta
be a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f
be a bounded measurable function, we have

\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)=\iint\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\gamma)}f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta H_{\star,\Delta}^{\beta}(\eta,\gamma)}\Pi_{\Delta}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)

with the finite and nonzero normalisation constant

Z^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\gamma)=\int e^{-\beta H_{\star,\Delta}^{\beta}(\eta,\gamma)}\Pi_{\Delta}(d\eta).

As in Remark
1.9
, we can see that the previous Theorem gives a description of conditional expectations with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}}

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)=\int\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\gamma)}f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta H_{\star,\Delta}^{\beta}(\eta,\gamma)}\Pi_{\Delta}(d\eta).

More precisely, the grand canonical DLR equations claim that that the law of the point process
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
in any bounded Borel set
\Delta
conditionally to the exterior has a density with respect to the Poisson point process in
\Delta
. This is the natural continuation of Theorem
1.11
, which claims that the number of points in
\Delta
conditionally to the outside can be any integer.

Remark 1.15

(Integral compensator and fluctuations)
.

At this point a remaining question is the nature of the compensator introduced in Proposition
1.13
. A natural candidate would be the integral compensator given by
C^{\beta}_{\star,\Lambda_{p}}=-\int_{\Lambda_{p}}g(y)dy
which is just the expectation of the partial sum. But obtain a convergence as in the proposition is difficult. It could be achieved with a good control of the fluctuation of the number of points in bounded sets. For instance, if we can find
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for every integer
k\geq 1
we have

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(|N_{\Lambda_{k}}-k|)\leq Ck^{s/d-\varepsilon},

(1.1)

then it is easy to show that the partial sum with the integral compensator converges. The discrepancy estimate obtained using the electrical energy in
[
18
]
gives for (
1.1
) an upper bound of order
k^{1/2+s/d}
, which is not enough here.
Such property recall the notion of
hyperuniform
point process, for which
\text{Var}(N_{\Lambda})=o(\lambda^{d}(\Lambda))
. In particular, in dimension 1, if
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
is hyperuniform, then (
1.1
) is true as soon as
s>1/2
.

Unfortunately we were unable to prove such properties on the fluctuations. The nature of the compensator remains an open question although the integral compensator is the main natural candidate.

Properties of the periodized potential

The following lemma contains two useful upper-bounds for

. The first property corresponds to the third one in Proposition

Lemma 2.1
.

The periodized Riesz potential
g_{n}
statisfies both inequalities:

(i)

there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every point
x\in\Lambda_{n}

\lvert g_{n}(x)-g(x)\rvert\leq cn^{-s/d},

(ii)

for a given bounded Borel set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a constant
c_{\Delta}>0
such that for
n\geq 1
large enough and for all
x\in\Delta
and
y\in\Lambda_{n}

\lvert(g_{n}(y-x)-g_{n}(y))-(g(y-x)-g(y))\rvert\leq c_{\Delta}n^{-(s+1)/d}.

Proof.

(i)
Let
k
be an integer such that
k\geq\sqrt{d}/2
. For
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the inequality

\lVert n^{1/d}u+x\rVert\geq\lVert n^{1/d}u\rVert-n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}/2

implies

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus[k]}\left[\lVert x+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\lVert y+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}dy\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus[k]}\left[(\lVert n^{1/d}u\rVert-n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}/2)^{-s}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\lVert y+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}dy\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq n^{-s/d}\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus[k]}\left[(\lVert u\rVert-\sqrt{d}/2)^{-s}-\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\lVert y+u\rVert^{-s}dy\right].

For
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\|x+n^{1/d}u\|\geq\|n^{1/d}u/2\|
and so

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in[k]\setminus\{0\}}\left[\lVert x+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\lVert y+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}dy\right]\leq n^{-s/d}\sum_{u\in[k]\setminus\{0\}}(\|u\|/2)^{-s}.

The upper-bound follows from both previous inequalities

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)-g(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\|y\|^{-s}dy+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left[\lVert x+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\lVert y+n^{1/d}u\rVert^{-s}dy\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{-s/d}\Big(\sum_{u\in[k]\setminus\{0\}}(\|u\|/2)^{-s}+\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus[k]}\Big[(\lVert u\rVert-1/2)^{-s}-\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\lVert y+u\rVert^{-s}dy\Big]\Big).

For the lower bound we use the inequality

\lVert x+n^{1/d}u\rVert\leq\lVert n^{1/d}u\rVert+n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}/2

to obtain for
x\in\Lambda_{n}

g_{n}(x)-g(x)\geq n^{-s/d}\Big(-\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\|y\|^{-s}dy+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\Big[(\lVert u\rVert+\sqrt{d}/2)^{-s}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\lVert y+u\rVert^{-s}dy\Big]\Big).

(ii)
By definition of
g_{n}

(g_{n}(y-x)-g_{n}(y))-(g(y-x)-g(y))=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left[\lVert y-x+un^{1/d}\rVert^{-s}-\lVert y+un^{1/d}\rVert^{-s}\right].

Using the gradient of the potential
g
,

\big\lvert\lVert y-x+un^{1/d}\rVert^{-s}-\lVert y+un^{1/d}\rVert^{-s}\big\rvert\leq s\lVert x\rVert\int_{0}^{1}\lVert y-tx+un^{1/d}\rVert^{-(s+1)}dt.

We introduce the quantity
\rho_{\Delta}:=\sup_{x\in\Delta}\|x\|
. For
x\in\Delta
,
y\in\Lambda_{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\|x\|\leq\rho_{\Delta}
and
\|y+n^{1/d}u\|\geq\|un^{1/d}\|/2
, and so for
n\geq 1
such that
n^{1/d}/4\geq\rho_{\Delta}

\lVert y-tx+un^{1/d}\rVert\geq n^{1/d}\lVert u\rVert/4.

Therefore we obtain the upper-bound

\big\lvert[g_{n}(y-x)-g_{n}(y)]-[g(y-x)-g(y)]\big\rvert\leq s4^{s+1}\rho_{\Delta}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\lVert u\rVert^{-(s+1)}n^{-(s+1)/d}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every integers
0\leq j\leq k
and every

\int_{\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}}g(y+u)dy\leq\begin{cases}C((k-j)^{d+1-s}+k^{d+1-s})&\text{ if }0\leq j<k,\\
C(1+k^{d+1-s})&\text{ if }j=k.\end{cases}

Proof.

We introduce
u_{j}=(j,...,j)
, one of the
2^{d}
extremal points of
[j\setminus j-1]
. By convexity of

, for every
u\in[j\setminus j-1]
we have

\int_{\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}}g(y+u)dy\leq\int_{\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}}g(y+u_{j})dy.

The domain
\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}
can be partitioned into
V_{2k+2}-V_{2k}
disjoint cubes (which are translations of
\Lambda_{1}
). The distance between the point
u_{j}
and the domain
\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}
is equal to
k-j
. To provide an upper-bound of the integral, we count the number of cubes which are at distance
k-i
from
u_{j}
(for the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
) with
0\leq i\leq j
. there exists a constant
A>0
such that

•

the number of cubes at distance
k-j
is less than
A(k-j)^{d-1}
(or
A
if
j=k
),

•

the number of cubes at distance
k-i
with
0<i<j
is less than
A(k-i)^{d-2}
,

•

the number of cubes at distance greater than
k
is less than
Ak^{d-1}
.

If a cube is at distance
k-i
from
u_{j}
, then its interaction with
u_{j}
is less than
(k-i)^{-s}
if
i\neq k
, and less than a constant
b>0
if
i=k
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for
j\neq k

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}}g(y+u_{j})dy

\displaystyle\leq Ak^{d-1}k^{-s}+A\sum_{i=1}^{j}(k-i)^{d-2}(k-i)^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq C((k-j)^{d-1-s}+k^{d-1-s}),

and for
j=k

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda_{(2k+2)^{d}}\setminus\Lambda_{(2k)^{d}}}g(y+u_{j})dy

\displaystyle\leq Ak^{d-1}k^{-s}+A\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(k-i)^{d-2}(k-i)^{-s}+Ab

\displaystyle\leq C(1+k^{d-1-s}).

∎

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v\in[k]}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}g(x-y+n^{1/d}(u-v))dy-\sum_{u,v\in[k]}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}g(y+n^{1/d}(u-v))dy.

2.1.3
Stability of
H_{n}
.

We start by proving the stability of
\widetilde{H}_{n}
(a similar proof is given in Appendix A of
[
19
]
). The Riesz potential can be decomposed in two parts
g=g^{(1)}+g^{(2)}
with

g^{(1)}(x)=\frac{1}{(1+\|x\|^{2})^{s/2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad g^{(2)}(x)=\frac{1}{\|x\|^{s}}-\frac{1}{(1+\|x\|^{2})^{s/2}}.

The potential
g^{(2)}
is non-negative and integrable, and
g^{(1)}
is of
positive type
, which means that there exists a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

g^{(1)}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{it\cdot x}\nu(dt).

Indeed
g^{(1)}
is the Fourier transform of the Bessel kernel given by

G_{s}(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2d}\Gamma(s/2)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-u-\|y\|^{2}}/(4u)}{u^{1+(d-s)/2}}du.

So the energy
\widetilde{H}_{n}
is decomposed in two terms
\widetilde{H}_{n}=\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(1)}+\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(2)}
studied separately. The contribution of
g^{(1)}
is lower bounded using the positive type property

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(1)}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Lambda_{n}^{2}\setminus\text{Diag}}g^{(1)}(x-y)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dx)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dy)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{(1)}(0)|\gamma|+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Lambda_{n}^{2}}g^{(1)}(x-y)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dx)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dy)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}|\gamma|+\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{\Lambda_{n}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{it\cdot(x-y)}\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dx)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dy)\nu(dt)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}|\gamma|+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\Lambda_{n}}e^{it\cdot x}\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dx)\right|^{2}\nu(dt)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}|\gamma|.

The contribution of
g^{(2)}
is lower-bounded using successively its non-negativity and integrability

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(2)}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\text{Diag}}g^{(2)}(x-y)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dx)\big(\gamma-\lambda^{d}{}\big)(dy)

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{x\in\gamma}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}g^{(2)}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{x\in\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{(2)}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\geq-|\gamma|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{(2)}(y)dy.

In particular, for a point configuration
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
,

\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}(\gamma+n^{1/d}[k])\geq-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{(2)}(y)dy\right)(2k+1)^{d}|\gamma|,

and if
|\gamma|=n
, Proposition
1.1
implies that

H_{n}(\gamma)+n\varepsilon_{n}\geq-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{(2)}(y)dy\right)n.

Since
\varepsilon_{n}
goes to
0
as
n
goes to infinity, we can conclude that
H_{n}
satisfies the property
(i)
of Proposition
1.2
.

2.2
\beta
-Circular Riesz gases

In this section we provide the proofs related to the existence of the
\beta
-Circular Riesz gases. First we introduce a family of configurations, the perturbed lattices, which are used to obtain the lower bound of the partition function. This control is crucial to verify that the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies our compactness tool described in the last section.

2.2.1
The lattice perturbed configurations

For
n\geq 1
we use the notation
r_{n}=\lfloor n^{1/d}\rfloor
and introduce the translated boxes
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}=\Lambda_{r_{n}}-(n^{1/d}-r_{n})u
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}+1}=\Lambda_{r_{n}+1}-(n^{1/d}-r_{n})u
where
u=(1,\dots,1)
. They satisfy
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}\subset\Lambda_{n}\subset\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}+1}
and there exist disjoint cubes
(C_{j})_{1\leq j\leq(r_{n}+1)^{d}}
(i.e. translations of
\Lambda_{1}
) such that

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq r_{n}^{d}}C_{j}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}+1}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq(r_{n}+1)^{d}}C_{j}.

(2.2)

Then we define
\widetilde{C}_{j}=C_{j}\cap\Lambda_{n}
, which is a box whose center is denoted
u_{j}
, and for
\delta\in]0,1[
,
\Delta_{j}=\{x\in\widetilde{C}_{j}:\|x-u_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta/2\}
which is a neighbourhood of the center
u_{j}
. A point configuration
\gamma=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}\subset\Lambda_{n}
is a
perturbed lattice
if it satisfies

-

for every
j\in\{1,\dots,r_{n}^{d}\}
,
|\gamma\cap\Delta_{j}|=1
,

-

for every
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
|\gamma\cap\Delta_{j}|\in\{0,1\}
.

In other words,
\gamma
has one point in the neighbourhood of each center of the cubes in
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}
and its other points are in the neighbourhood of centers of the boxes in
\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}
. We denote
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,n}
the set of perturbed lattices defined above.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
\delta\in]0,1/2[
and
n\geq 1
, if
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,n}
then
|H_{n}(\gamma)|\leq cn
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
1.1
, we only have to prove that for every perturbed lattice
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,n}
and every integer
k

\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}(\gamma_{k})\leq c(2k+1)^{d}n.

where
\gamma_{k}=\gamma+n^{1/d}[k]
. As in the proof of the stability property in Section
2.1.3
, we use the decompositions
g=g^{(1)}+g^{(2)}
and
\widetilde{H}_{n}=\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(1)}+\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(2)}
. The two contributions are studied separately.

Contribution of
\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(1)}
:
we need to control

\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(1)}(\gamma_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}\times\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)(\gamma_{k}(dy)-dy).

First we look at the interaction of a point
y\in\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}
with the lattice part
\gamma_{\Lambda_{r_{n}}}+n^{1/d}[k]
and his background

\int_{\Lambda_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}[k]}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)=\sum_{u\in[k]}\int_{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx).

According to the definition of a perturbed lattice configuration and the decomposition given in (
2.2
)

\int_{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx)=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}^{d}}\int_{C_{j}}(g^{(1)}(x_{j}+n^{1/d}u-y)-g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y))dx,

where
\gamma_{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r_{n}^{d}}\}
. Again using the gradient of
g^{(1)}
,

g^{(1)}(x_{j}+n^{1/d}u-y)-g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)=\int_{0}^{1}\langle x_{j}-x,\nabla g^{(1)}\left(x+n^{1/d}u-y+t(x_{j}-x)\right)\rangle dt.

From the upper-bound
\|\nabla g^{(1)}(x)|\leq(1+\|x\|^{2})^{(s+1)/2}
and the inequality

\|x+n^{1/d}u-y+t(x_{j}-x)\|\leq\|u_{j}+n^{1/d}u-y\|+\sqrt{d}/2+\delta,

we obtain that for
v_{y}\in\{u_{j}+u:j\in\{1,\dots,r_{n}^{d}\},u\in[k]\}
such that
\|v_{y}-y\|\leq\sqrt{d}

\left|\int_{\Lambda_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}[k]}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)\right|\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}\sum_{u\in[k]}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}^{d}}(1+(\|u_{j}+n^{1/d}u-v_{y}\|+2\sqrt{d})^{2})^{(s+1)/2}.

At the end we find an upper bound independent of
y

\left|\int_{\Lambda_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}[k]}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}\leq\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+(\|u\|+2\sqrt{d})^{2})^{(s+1)/2}=:C_{1}.

It leads to

\left|\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(1)}(\gamma_{k})\right|\leq 3(2k+1)^{d}nC_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{B_{k,n}\times B_{k,n}}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)(\gamma_{k}(dy)-dy),

where
B_{k,n}=\bigcup_{u\in[k]}(\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}u)
, which corresponds to the peripheral points. For
y\in\Lambda_{n}
we look at

\int_{B_{k,n}}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)=\sum_{u\in[k]}\int_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx).

(2.3)

There exist Borel sets
(D_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n-r_{n}^{d}}
of volume equal to one forming a partition of
\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}
and so

\int_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-r_{n}^{d}}\int_{D_{j}}(g^{(1)}(x_{j}+n^{1/d}u-y)-g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y))dx.

Again using the gradient of
g^{(1)}
,

g^{(1)}(x_{j}+n^{1/d}u-y)-g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)=\int_{0}^{1}\langle x_{j}-x,\nabla g^{(1)}\left(x+n^{1/d}u-y+t(x_{j}-x)\right)\rangle dt.

Since
\|x_{j}-x\|\leq n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}
and
y\in\Lambda_{n}
we have the lower bound

\|x+n^{1/d}u-y+t(x_{j}-x)\|\geq n^{1/d}(\|u\|-3/2),

and using it for each
u\in[k]\setminus[1]
in (
2.3
),

\left|\int_{B_{k,n}}g^{(1)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}[1]}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx)\right|\\
+(n-r_{n}^{d})n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus[1]}(1+n^{2/d}(\|u\|-3/2)^{2})^{-(s+1)/2}.

Using that
n-r_{n}\leq dn^{(d-1)/d}
, the second term of the right hand side is upper-bounded

(n-r_{n}^{d})n^{1/d}\sqrt{d}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus[1]}(1+n^{2/d}(\|u\|-3/2)^{2})^{-(s+1)/2}\\
\leq n^{(d-1-s)/d}d^{3/2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus[1]}(\|u\|-3/2)^{-(s+1)}\leq C_{2},

where
C_{2}
is a constant independent of
n
. Moreover, for
C_{2}>0
chosen large enough, with a argument similar to the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we can show that for all
n\geq 1
and
y\in\Lambda_{n}

\left|\int_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\widetilde{\Lambda}_{r_{n}}+n^{1/d}[1]}g^{(1)}(x+n^{1/d}u-y)(\gamma(dx)-dx)\right|\leq C_{2}(1+n^{(d-1-s)/d})\leq 2C_{2}.

To conclude, we have obtained

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(1)}(\gamma_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3(2k+1)^{d}nC_{1}+2(2k+1)^{d}(n-r_{n}^{d})C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2k+1)^{d}n(3C_{1}+2C_{2}).

Contribution of
\widetilde{H}_{n}^{(2)}
:
it remains to control

\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(2)}(\gamma_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}\times\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}}g^{(2)}(x-y)(\gamma_{k}(dx)-dx)(\gamma_{k}(dy)-dy),

which is simpler, thanks to the integrability of
g^{(2)}
. For instance, for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left|\int_{(2k+1)^{d}n}g^{(2)}(x-y)dx\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{(2)}|(x-y)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{(2)}|(x)dx.

Thus we obtain

\left|\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(2)}(\gamma_{k})\right|\leq\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma_{k}}|g^{(2)}|(x-y)+\frac{3}{2}(2k+1)^{d}n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{(2)}|(x)dx.

The points of a perturbed lattice lie in different boxes and the same goes for the points of
\gamma_{k}
. For
\delta<1/2
, the distance between two adjacent boxes is greater than
1/4
. Using the fact that that
g^{(2)}
is decreasing and positive, it implies that

\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma_{k}}|g^{(2)}|(x-y)\leq\frac{1}{2}(2k+1)^{d}n\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}4^{s}|g^{(2)}|(u),

which concludes the expected control for
\widetilde{H}_{(2k+1)^{d}n}^{(2)}(\gamma_{k})
and the proof of the proposition.
∎

2.2.2
Control of the partition function

Proof of Lemma
1.3
.

For the upper-bound, the stability property of the energy gives

Z^{\beta}_{n}\leq\int e^{-\beta An}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(d\gamma)=(e^{-\beta A})^{n}.

For the lower-bound, introducing the subset of perturbed lattice configurations defined in the previous section, that leads to

Z^{\beta}_{n}\geq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\delta,n}}e^{-\beta H_{n}(\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(d\gamma).

According to Proposition
2.4
, we have
Z^{\beta}_{n}\geq e^{-\beta cn}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(\mathcal{C}_{\delta,n})
. The next lemma, whose proof is postponed below, gives a lower-bound of the volume of the perturbed lattice configurations under the law of the binomial point process.

Lemma 2.5
.

We can choose
\delta>0
small enough such that for every
n\geq 1

\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(\mathcal{C}_{n,\delta})\geq n!\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n}.

Applying the lemma for
\delta
chosen small enough, and using the inequality
n!/n^{n}\geq e^{-n}
, that gives

Z_{n}^{\beta}\geq\left(\delta e^{-(\beta c+1)}\right)^{n}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

To construct a perturbed lattice configuration, we start by spread
r_{n}^{d}
points in the boxes
\Delta_{j}
with
j=1,...,r_{n}^{d}
. Since they have a volume of
\delta^{d}
, it corresponds to an event of probability

p_{1}=(r_{n}^{d})!\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{r_{n}^{d}}.

Then we have to spread the
n-r_{n}^{d}
remaining points between the boxes
\Delta_{j}
where
{j=r_{n}^{d}+1,...,(r_{n}+2)^{d}}
, which is an event of probability
p_{2}
. We consider two cases.

1.

n^{1/d}-r_{n}\geq\delta
, all the boxes have a volume equal to
\delta^{d}
and we have the lower-bound

p_{2}\geq(n-r_{n}^{d})!\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n-r_{n}^{d}}.

2.

n^{1/d}-r_{n}\leq\delta
, the volume of the boxes is then smaller than
\delta^{d}
, we try to put the points in the ”good boxes”, those with a volume equal to
\delta^{d-1}(n^{1/d}-r_{n})
. There are
dr_{n}^{d-1}
such boxes, and if
\delta\leq d2^{-d}

n-r_{n}^{d}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta^{k}r_{n}^{d-k}\binom{d}{k}\leq\delta r_{n}^{d-1}2^{d}\leq dr_{n}^{d-1},

hence we can spread the remaining points among the good boxes. This gives a lower-bound of the probability
p_{2}

p_{2}\geq(n-r_{n}^{d})!\binom{dr_{n}^{d-1}}{n-r_{n}^{d}}\left(\frac{(n^{1/d}-r_{n})\delta^{d-1}}{n}\right)^{n-r_{n}^{d}}.

Using successively the inequalities

\binom{N}{p}\geq(N/p)^{p}
and
b^{d}-a^{d}\leq d(b-a)b^{d-1}
(for
a<b
), that leads to

p_{2}\geq(n-r_{n}^{d})!\left(\left(\frac{r_{n}}{n^{1/d}}\right)^{d-1}\frac{\delta^{d-1}}{n}\right)^{n-r_{n}^{d}}

Since
r_{n}/n^{1/d}\geq 1/2
, for
\delta\leq(1/2)^{d-1}
we have

p_{2}\geq(n-r_{n}^{d})!\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n-r_{n}^{d}}.

At the end, if
\delta
is chosen small enough

\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(\mathcal{C}_{n,\delta})=\binom{n}{r_{n}^{d}}p_{1}p_{2}\geq n!\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n}.

∎

2.2.3
Existence of an accumulation point

Before starting the proof of Proposition
1.5
, we introduce the specific entropy criterion which is the compactness tool we use. We recall that for two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on the same measurable space, the relative entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
is defined as

I(\mu\mid\nu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int\log\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\right)d\mu&\text{if }\mu\ll\nu,\\
\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a stationary probability measure on
\mathcal{C}
, the specific entropy of
\mathbb{P}
(with respect to
\Pi
) is defined by

I(\mathbb{P})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})}I(\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{n}}\mid\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}}).

The proof of the existence of the limit in the previous definition and other properties of the specific entropy can be found in the Chapter 15 of
[
9
]
. The following result, stated as Proposition 2.6 in
[
10
]
, is the compactness criterion.

Proposition 2.7
.

For any
c>0
, the set of probability measures

\{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{sta}}(\mathcal{C}):I(\mathbb{P})\leq c\}

is compact and sequentially compact for the local topology.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

The specific entropy criterion is adapted for stationary point processes, so we need to construct a stationarized version of
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Let
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the point process consisting of independent copies of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
in the cubes
\Lambda_{n}+n^{1/d}u
with
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Formally we can define it as follow, if
A
is a local event and
k
an integer such that
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda_{(2k+1)^{d}n}}
then

\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\beta,/}(A)=\int 1_{A}\Big(\bigcup_{u\in[k]}\tau_{u}(\gamma_{u})\Big)\prod_{u\in[k]}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma_{u}).

The probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n}
is an infinite volume point process, but not a stationary one yet. The stationarized version of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
is defined by

\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n}\circ\tau_{u}du.

To show that the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
admits a convergent subsequence, we need an uniform bound of the specific entropy. According to the definition of the specific entropy and the properties of the relative entropy we have

I(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}I(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}\mid\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}}).

We compute the relative entropy of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
with respect to
\pi_{\Lambda_{n}}
as follows

\displaystyle I_{\Lambda_{n}}(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}\mid\pi)

\displaystyle=\int\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}{d\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}}}\right)d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}{d\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}}\right)d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}+\int\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}}{d\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}}}\right)d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\log(Z^{\beta}_{n})-\beta\int H_{n}d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}+\log\left(e^{n}\frac{n!}{n^{n}}\right).

Using the stability property of
H_{n}
and the lower-bound on the partition function given by Lemma
1.3
we obtain

\frac{1}{n}I(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}\mid\Pi_{\Lambda_{n}})\leq b_{\beta}-\beta A+1.

According to Proposition
2.7
, the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
admits a convergent subsequence for the local topology, denoted by
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
. Let
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}
be the limit of this subsequence, it is a stationary probability measure on
\mathcal{C}
. Since all probability measures
\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n}
have intensity equal to
1
, the converge for the local topology ensures that
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}
has also an intensity equal to
1
. To conclude the proof, it remains to show that
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}
is the limit of
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
as well. Without loss of generality, we assume that
n_{j}=j
, or in other words that
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}
is the limit of
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\text{sta}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Let
f
be a local tame function, and
\Delta
a Borel bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for all point configurations
\gamma
we have
f(\gamma)=f(\gamma_{\Delta})
. By definition of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}

\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}(d\gamma)=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\int f(\tau_{u}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n}(d\gamma)du.

If we take
n
large enough to have
\Delta\subset\Lambda_{n}
and introduce
\Lambda_{n}^{\star}=\{u\in\Lambda_{n}:\tau_{u}(\Delta)\subset\Lambda_{n}\}
, then for all
u\in\Lambda_{n}^{\star}
we have

\int f(\tau_{u}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n}(d\gamma)=\int f(\tau_{u}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma).

The key remark here is that
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}\circ\tau_{-u}^{n}
is equal to
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}
, where
\tau_{u}^{n}
is the translation of vector
u
on
\Lambda_{n}
seen as a torus. Since
H_{n}
is invariant by such torus translations, for every
u\in\Lambda_{n}^{\star}

\int f(\tau_{u}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma)=\int f(\tau_{u}^{n}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma)=\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma).

Finally, since
\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n}^{\star})/\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})
goes to
1
as
n
goes to infinity, we obtain successively

\displaystyle\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})}\int_{\Lambda^{*}_{n}}\int f(\tau_{u}(\gamma))\mathbb{P}^{\beta,/}_{n}(d\gamma)du

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n}^{\star})}{\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})}\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma).

That proves that any accumulation point of the sequence
(\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\beta,\text{sta}})_{n\geq 1}
is also an accumulation point of the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
∎

2.3
Canonical DLR equations

In this section we provide the proof of Theorem
1.8
inspired by part (C) of Theorem 1.1 in
[
4
]
. We start by proving the existence of the Move functions for point processes with bounded intensity. Then the theorem is proved using truncations of these Move functions, local convergence and canonical DLR equations in finite volume.

2.3.1
Move functions and their truncations in infinite volume

We prove Proposition
1.1
and derive a control lemma which is needed for the proof the canonical DLR equations in infinite volume.
Let
\Delta
be a bounded Borel set,
p\geq 1
an integer and
\delta>0
a real. We introduce the event

A_{\Delta}^{(p)}(\delta)=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}:\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{\Delta}\\
|\eta|=|\gamma_{\Delta}|\end{subarray}}\left|M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)-M_{\Delta}^{(p)}(\eta,\gamma)\right|\leq\delta\right\}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\kappa>0
be a fixed parameter, for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
\delta>0
,
there exists
p>0
such that for every point process
\mathbb{P}
with intensity bounded by
\kappa
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(A_{\Delta}^{(p)}(\delta)\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
and Lemma
2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a point process of intensity bounded by
\kappa
. For
p\geq 1
,
M_{\Delta}^{(p)}(\eta,\gamma)=+\infty
if and only if
0\in\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}
, which is an event of zero probability under
\mathbb{P}
. Therefore, the sequence
(M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))_{p\geq 1}
contains
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely finite elements. For integers
q>p>0
we have

\displaystyle\left|M^{(q)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)-M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)\right|\leq\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{q}\setminus\Lambda_{p}}}|g(y-x)-g(y)|

Using the gradient of the potential

and

\|y-tx\|\geq\|y\|-\|x\|\geq\|y\|/2.

Thus we obtain successively

\displaystyle\left|M^{(q)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)-M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle\leq s2^{s+1}\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{q}\setminus\Lambda_{p}}}\|x\|\|y\|^{-(s+1)}

\displaystyle\leq s2^{s+1}\rho_{\Delta}N_{\Delta}(\eta)\sum_{k=p}^{q-1}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{k+1}\setminus\Lambda_{k}}}\|y\|^{-(s+1)}

\displaystyle\leq s4^{s+1}\rho_{\Delta}N_{\Delta}(\eta)\sum_{k=p}^{+\infty}N_{\Lambda_{k+1}\setminus\Lambda_{k}}(\gamma)k^{-(s+1)/d}.

Since

\mathbb{P}
has the intensity bounded by
\kappa

(2.4)

It implies that for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\gamma

M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma).

Proposition
1.1
is proved, and it remains to prove Lemma
2.8
. The previous computation shows that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{\Delta}\\
N_{\Delta}(\eta)=N_{\Delta}(\gamma)\end{subarray}}\left|M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)-M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)\right|\leq s4^{s+1}\rho_{\Delta}N_{\Delta}(\gamma)\sum_{k=p}^{+\infty}N_{\Lambda_{k+1}\setminus\Lambda_{k}}(\gamma)k^{-(s+1)/d}.

Since
\mathbb{P}
has intensity bounded by
\kappa
, we can find a positive constant
K
(depending only on
\kappa
and
\Delta

. According to (

) and Markov’s inequality, we can find

For that choice of

∎

The sequence

allows to approximate

This partition function is positive since we integrate a

Proposition 2.9
.

For every

\left|E_{\mathbb{P}}\big(f^{\vphantom{(p)}}_{\Delta}-f^{(p)}_{\Delta}\big)\right|\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

We write the difference as

\displaystyle f_{\Delta}^{(p)}(\gamma)-f_{\Delta}(\gamma)=

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\frac{1}{Z^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\gamma)}\int f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\left(e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}-e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}\right)\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left(\frac{1}{Z^{\vphantom{(p)}}_{\Delta}(\gamma)}-\frac{1}{Z^{(p)}_{\Delta}(\gamma)}\right)\int f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma))}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta).

(2.5)

For

2.3.2
DLR equations in finite volume

In finite volume the canonical DLR equations are simpler to obtained. We will state them using the local energy. For
\Delta\subset\Lambda_{n}
a bounded Borel set and
\eta
,
\gamma
two point configurations in
\Lambda_{n}
, the local energy of the points of
\eta
in
\Delta
into the field created by
\gamma
is given by

H_{n,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)=H_{n}(\eta_{\Delta}\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})-H_{n}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})=H_{n}(\eta_{\Delta})+\sum_{x\in\eta}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}}g_{n}(x-y).

It corresponds to the sum of the energy of
\eta_{\Delta}
and the interaction between
\eta_{\Delta}
and
\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}
. As before, for a measurable bounded function
f
we introduce the notation

f_{n,\Delta}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{Z_{n,\Delta}(\gamma)}\int f(\eta\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})e^{-\beta H_{n,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{N_{\Delta}(\gamma),\Delta}(d\eta),

with the partition function

Z_{n,\Delta}(\gamma)=\int e^{-\beta H_{n,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\gamma)}(d\eta).

We can now state the canonical DLR equations in the finite volume case.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
f
be a bounded measurable function on
\mathcal{C}
and
\Delta
a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
n\geq 1
is such that
\Delta\subset\Lambda_{n}
then

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f)=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f_{n,\Delta}).

Proof.

Introducing the number of points in
\Delta
, the expectation can be written as

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{\beta}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int 1_{N_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)f(\gamma)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n}(d\gamma).

We do the change of variables
\gamma=\eta\cup\xi
with
(\eta,\xi)\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta}\times\mathcal{C}_{\Delta}
and use the notation
p_{\Delta}=\lambda^{d}(\Delta)/\lambda^{d}(\Lambda_{n})
in order to obtain

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f)=\frac{1}{Z^{\beta}_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}p_{\Delta}^{k}(1-p_{\Delta})^{n-k}\iint f(\eta\cup\xi)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\eta\cup\xi)}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta)\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta,n-k}(d\xi).

(2.6)

Introducing the partition function
Z_{n,\Delta}(\eta\cup\xi)
in the integral, that leads to

\iint f(\eta\cup\xi)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\eta\cup\xi)}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta)\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta,n-k}(d\xi)\\
=\iiint\frac{1}{Z_{n,\Delta}(\eta\cup\xi)}f(\eta\cup\xi)e^{-\beta(H_{n}(\eta\cup\xi)+H_{n,\Delta}(\zeta\cup\xi))}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,N_{\Delta}(\eta)}(d\zeta)\mathcal{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta)\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta,n-k}(d\xi).

Since
H_{n}(\eta\cup\xi)+H_{n,\Delta}(\zeta,\xi)=H_{n,\Delta}(\eta,\xi)+H_{n}(\zeta\cup\xi)
and
Z_{n\Delta}(\eta\cup\xi)=Z_{n,\Delta}(\zeta\cup\xi)
the integral can be written using the function
f_{n,\Delta}
as

\iint f(\eta\cup\xi)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\eta\cup\xi)}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta)\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta,n-k}(d\xi)\\
=\iint f_{n,\Delta}(\zeta\cup\xi)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\zeta\cup\xi)}\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\zeta)\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Delta,n-k}(d\xi).

Inserting the previous equality in (
2.6
), that gives that
E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f)=E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(f_{n,\Delta})
.
∎

2.4
Existence of a non number-rigid Riesz gas

In this section we prove Theorem
1.11
. The
\beta
-Circular Riesz Gas
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
is constructed as an accumulation point of a particular subsequence along which the expectation of local energy is uniformly bounded. It allows to control the cost of exchanging the point configuration in a compact
\Delta
with the one in the translated
\Delta+u
, where
\|u\|
can be as large as we want. Then the idea is to let
u
goes to infinity in order to prove that the number of points in
\Delta
can be as we want.

2.4.1
Construction of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}

We use a subsequence for which we control uniformly the expectation of the local energy, defined for a configuration
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{n}}
and
x\in\Lambda_{n}
by

h_{n}(x,\gamma)=\sum_{y\in\gamma}g_{n}(x-y).

The existence of a such subsequence is assured by the following result.

Proposition 2.13
.

For every
\beta>0
, there exists a constant
K>0
and a an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
such that for all
j\geq 1

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n_{j}}}(|h_{n_{j}}(0,.)|)\leq K.

According to Proposition
1.5
, the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
admits an accumulation point, denoted
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
, which is our non number-rigid candidate.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
is invariant by torus translations on
\Lambda_{n}
, for every bounded Borel subset
\Delta\subset\Lambda_{n}
,
E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(N_{\Delta})=\lambda^{d}{}(\Delta)
and

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}}(h_{n}(0,\cdot))=\int\sum_{x\in\gamma}g_{n}(x)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma)=\int g_{n}(x)\lambda^{d}_{\Lambda_{n}}(dx)=0.

Therefore, we only need to find a subsequence such that

E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n_{j}}}(h^{-}_{n}(0,\cdot))\geq-K

where we use the notation
h^{-}_{n}(0,\gamma)=\min(h^{-}_{n}(0,\gamma),0)
. The following lemma is useful to manage computations under
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}
. Its proof is given at the end of the section.

Lemma 2.14
.

For any measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

\int\sum_{x\in\gamma}f(x,\gamma\setminus\{x\})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n}(d\gamma)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{\beta}}\iint f(x,\gamma)e^{-\beta H_{n}(\gamma\cup\{x\})}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n-1}(d\gamma)\lambda^{d}_{\Lambda_{n}}(dx).

We introduce a modified inverse temperature
\beta_{n}=\beta(n/(n-1))^{s/d}
. The stability property of
H_{n}
(Proposition
1.2
) and Lemma
2.14
give

\displaystyle 2A

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta_{n}}_{n}}(2H_{n})

, we have
h_{n}(x,\gamma)=h_{n}(0,\tau^{n}_{-x}(\gamma))
and
H_{n}(\gamma\cup\{x\})=H_{n}(\tau^{n}_{-x}\gamma\cup\{0\})
. Since
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n-1}

\tau^{n}_{-x}

disappears and

2A\leq\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{\beta_{n}}}\iint h_{n}(0,\gamma)e^{-\beta_{n}H_{n}(\gamma\cup\{0\})}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n},n-1}(d\gamma).

To pass from a binomial point process on
\Lambda_{n}
to one on
\Lambda_{n-1}

r_{n}=(n/(n-1))^{1/d}
, which leads to

2A\leq\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{\beta_{n}}}\int h_{n}(0,r_{n}\gamma)e^{-\beta_{n}h_{n}(0,r_{n}\gamma)}e^{-\beta_{n}H_{n}(r_{n}\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n-1},n-1}(d\gamma),

where
r_{n}\gamma=\{r_{n}x,\;x\in\gamma\}
.
According the scaling property of the periodic potential
g_{n}
we obtain

2A\leq r_{n}^{-s}\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{\beta_{n}}}\int h_{n-1}(0,\gamma)e^{-\beta h_{n-1}(0,\gamma)}e^{-\beta H_{n-1}(\gamma)}\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{n-1},n-1}(d\gamma),

and recover the finite volume Gibbs measure on

with inverse temperature
\beta

2A\leq r_{n}^{-s}\frac{Z^{\beta}_{n-1}}{Z^{\beta_{n}}_{n}}E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n-1}}\left(h_{n-1}(0,\cdot)e^{-\beta h_{n-1}(0,\cdot)}\right).

Since
xe^{-\beta x}\leq x
if
x\leq 0
and

, we have

2A\leq r_{n}^{-s}\frac{Z^{\beta}_{n-1}}{Z^{\beta_{n}}_{n}}\left(E_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{n-1}}\left(h^{-}_{n-1}(0,.)\right)+\frac{e^{-1}}{\beta}\right).

The choice of a good subsequence
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
allows to bound from below the quotient of partition functions, as explained in the following result whose the proof is postponed as well.

Lemma 2.15
.

There are infinitely many indices

such that

The lemma implies that we can find a subsequence

.

According to the definition of

2.4.2
A key inequality

Proposition 2.16

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a point process and
n
a positive integer, the Campbell measure of order
n
of
\mathbb{P}
is the measure
\Camp^{(n)}_{\mathbb{P}}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\Omega
defined for any non-negative test function
f:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\mathcal{C}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

\Camp^{(n)}_{\mathbb{P}}(f)=\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\gamma\\
\neq\end{subarray}}f(x_{1},...,x_{n},\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\})\mathbb{P}(d\gamma),

where the summation is performed over all
n
-tuples of distinct points in
\gamma
.

Using Theorem
1.11
, we show that the Campbell measure of order one has a density with respect to
\lambda^{d}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
. Then we obtain a description of Campbell measures for other order and finally we construct the compensator mentioned in Proposition
1.13
.

2.5.1
Structure of the one point Campbell measure

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma
a point configuration, the cost of moving a point from the origin to the position
x
in the field created by
\gamma
is defined by

V(x,\gamma)=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}}\left[g(y-x)-g(y)\right].

According to Lemma
1.7
, if
\mathbb{P}
is a point process with bounded intensity, then for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\gamma
, the previous limit exists and is finite. This cost function plays a crucial role in the structure of the Campbell measures for canonical Gibbs point processes.
We start by the properties of the Campbell measure of order one.

Proposition 2.23
.

There exists a measure
\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}
on
\mathcal{C}
such that
\Camp^{(1)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda^{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}
and has density

\frac{d\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}}{d\lambda^{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}}(x,\gamma)=e^{-\beta V(x,\gamma)}.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the canonical DLR equations, and the proof is the same as the one of Theorem 3.7 in
[
4
]
.
∎

The previous result is valid has soon as the point process satisfies the canonical DLR equations, and in particular, it is true for every
\beta
-Circular Riesz gas. The next result is specific to the point process
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
, and rely on the property highlighted in Theorem
1.11
.

Theorem 2.24
.

The measures
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
and
\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with
\lambda^{d}(\Delta)>0
and
E\in\mathcal{F}
an event. Introducing the number of points in
\Delta
we have

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\int\sum_{x\in\gamma}1_{\Delta}(x)1_{E}(\gamma\setminus x)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\int\sum_{x\in\gamma}1_{\Delta}(x)1_{E}(\gamma\setminus x)1_{N_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

The canonical DLR equations lead to

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\iint\sum_{x\in\eta}1_{E}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\eta\setminus x)1_{N_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)G_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma_{\Delta^{c}},k)\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma),

where

G_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma,k)=\frac{1}{Z_{\Delta}(\gamma,k)}\exp(-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)))1_{N_{\Delta}(\eta)=k},

with the partition function

Z_{\Delta}(\gamma,k)=\int\exp(-\beta(H(\eta)+M_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)))\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k}(d\eta).

These expressions depend only on
\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}
(i.e. they are
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}
-measurable with respect to
\gamma
). Using Lemma
2.14
for the binomial process (
\beta=0
),

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\iiint 1_{E}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\eta)1_{N_{\Delta}=k}(\gamma)G_{\Delta}(\eta\cup x,\gamma_{\Delta^{c}},k)\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx)\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k-1}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

According to the properties of the conditional expectation and using Theorem
1.11
, we obtain

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\iiint 1_{E}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\eta)\frac{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k-1\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}G_{\Delta}(\eta\cup x,\gamma_{\Delta^{c}},k)\\
1_{N_{\Delta}=k-1}(\gamma)\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx)\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k-1}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

Since for
\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k-1}
-almost every
\eta
and
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
,
G_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma,k-1)>0
and so it can be introduced in the integral

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\iiint 1_{E}(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\eta)\frac{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k-1\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}\frac{G_{\Delta}(\eta\cup x,\gamma,k)}{G_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma,k-1)}\\
1_{N_{\Delta}=k-1}(\gamma)G_{\Delta}(\eta,\gamma,k-1)\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx)\mathbb{B}_{\Delta,k-1}(d\eta)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

Using again the canonical DLR equations,

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\int\frac{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k-1\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}\left(\int_{\Delta}\frac{G_{\Delta}(\gamma\cup x,\gamma,k)}{G_{\Delta}(\gamma,\gamma,k-1)}dx\right)\\
1_{E}(\gamma)1_{N_{\Delta}=k-1}(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

We deduce from the previous equation that if
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(E)=0
then
\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\Delta\times E)=0
, since it holds for every compact set
\Delta
, we have
\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}(E)=0
. Observing that for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
the function

\gamma\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=k-1\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)}\left(\int_{\Delta}\frac{G_{\Delta}(\gamma\cup x,\gamma,k)}{G_{\Delta}(\gamma,\gamma,k-1)}dx\right)1_{N_{\Delta}=k-1}(\gamma)

is strictly positive, we also deduce that
\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}(E)=0
implies
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(E)=0
. At the end, that proves that
\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
are equivalent.
∎

In particular, the previous result ensures the existence of a function
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{\beta}_{\star}}{d\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(\gamma)=\exp\big(-\beta\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)\big).

(2.11)

2.5.2
Structure of the
n
-point Campbell measure

The fonction
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}
introduced in (
2.11
) can be used to describe the Campbell measure of order
n
for every
n\geq 1
.

Proposition 2.25
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
and every positive measurable function
f

\Camp^{(n)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f)=\int f(x_{1},...,x_{n},\gamma)\exp\left\{-\beta H(\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\})-\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(x_{j},\gamma)\right.\\
\left.-\beta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{j}\})-\sum_{i=1}^{j}g(x_{i})\right]\right\}dx_{1}...dx_{n}\,\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
n
. For
n=1
the previous expression becomes

\Camp_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}^{(1)}(f)=\iint f(x,\gamma)\exp\left(-\beta(V(x,\gamma)+\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma))\right)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)dx,

which is true from the definition of
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}
introduced in (
2.11
). Let
n\geq 1
be given, and assume that the result of Proposition
2.25
is true for
n
. The Campbell measure of order
(n+1)
can be written as

\Camp^{(n+1)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f)=\int\sum_{x_{n+1}\in\gamma}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\gamma\setminus x_{n+1}\\
\neq\end{subarray}}f(x_{1},...,x_{n+1},\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},...,x_{n+1}\})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma).

The result for the Campbell measure of order one gives

\Camp^{(n+1)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f)=\iint\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}\subset\gamma\\
\neq\end{subarray}}f(x_{1},...,x_{n+1},\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\})e^{-\beta(V(x_{n+1},\gamma)+\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma))}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)dx_{n+1}.

Applying the result for the Campbell measure of order
n
and use the identity

H(\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\})+V(x_{n+1},\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\})=H(\{x_{1},...,x_{n+1}\})+V(x_{n+1},\gamma)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(x_{j}),

that leads to the expected expression for the Campbell measure of order
(n+1)
.
∎

It is interesting to point out the three terms appearing in the exponential in Proposition
2.25
. The term
H(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})
is the energy of the interaction between the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(x_{j},\gamma)
is the cost for moving the points from the origin to their positions in the field created by
\gamma
. The third one is a little bit more complicated and partially analysed in the next corollary.

Corollary 2.26
.

For every integer
n
, for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
and
(\lambda^{d})^{\otimes k}
-almost every
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{k}

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(x_{j})=\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(y_{j}).

Proof.

It is obvious for
n=0
. For
n\geq 1
, let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a test function, and introduce the function
g
defined by
g(x_{1},...,x_{n+1},\gamma)=f(x_{1},...,x_{n+1},x_{n},\gamma)
. According to the definition of the Campbell measure,
\Camp^{(n)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f)=\Camp^{(n)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(g)
and comparing the expressions obtained with Proposition
2.25
, we deduce that for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
and
\lambda^{d}
-almost every
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{n-1},x_{n}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(x_{j})=\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{n-1},y_{n}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}g(x_{j})-g(y_{n}).

Applying that property
(n-1)
times, that gives the result.

∎

The quantities
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{j}\})-\sum_{i=1}^{j}g(x_{i})
appearing in Proposition
2.25
do not depend on the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and can be interpreted as the cost of adding a point at the origin in the configuration
\gamma
when
j
points has been previously added to
\gamma
. Then the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{j}\})-\sum_{i=1}^{j}g(x_{i})\right]
is the cost of adding
n
points at the origin. This sum is the equivalent, in our case, of the function
\textsf{create}_{n}
introduced in Theorem 3.15 of
[
4
]
.

In particular, for
n=1
,
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)
corresponds to the cost of adding one point at the origin. We must be careful, nothing ensures that the cost of adding two points is twice the cost of adding one point, and we cannot say that the previous sum is equal to
n\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)
. It would be true if we had for instance
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{x\})=\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)+g(x)
.

2.5.3
Construction of the compensator

We start by describing the effect on
\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}
of simultaneously moving
k
points in a point configuration.

Proposition 2.27
.

For all integers
m,n
and
k
, for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
, for
\lambda^{d}
-almost every
y_{1},...,y_{n+k}
and every
\{x_{1},...,x_{m+k}\}\subset\gamma
we have

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},...,x_{m+k}\}\cup\{y_{1},...,y_{n+k}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(y_{n+j})\\
=\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}\cup\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(x_{m+j}).

Proof.

We denote the vectors
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+k})
and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{n+k})
respectively by
X_{m+k}
and
Y_{n+k}
. Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{m+k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\times\mathcal{C}\to[0,+\infty)
be a test function and, for
Y_{n+k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, we introduce the partial function

h^{(1)}_{Y_{n+k}}:(X_{m+k},\gamma)\mapsto h(X_{m+k},Y_{n+k},\gamma)e^{-\Psi(\gamma\cup\{y_{1},...,y_{n},x_{m+1},\dots,x_{m+k}\})}.

By definition of the Campbell measure of order
(m+k)

\Camp^{(m+k)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(h^{(1)}_{Y_{n+k}})=\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m+k}\}\subset\gamma\\
\neq\end{subarray}}h(X_{m+k},Y_{n+k},\gamma)e^{-\Psi(\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}\cup\{y_{1},\dots y_{n}\})}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)dY_{n+k}.

(2.12)

But according to Corollary
2.26
and Proposition
2.25
, for
(\lambda^{d})^{\otimes(n+k)}
-almost every
Y_{n+k}

\Camp^{(m+k)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(h^{(1)}_{Y_{n+k}})=\Camp^{(m+k)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(h^{(2)}_{Y_{n+k}})

where we have introduced a second partial function

h^{(2)}_{Y_{n+k}}:(X_{m+k},\gamma)\mapsto h(X_{m+k},Y_{n+k},\gamma)e^{-\beta\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma\cup\{y_{1},...,y_{n+k}\})+\beta\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(y_{n+j})-g(x_{m+j})}.

Again, if we use the definition of the Campbell measure

\Camp^{(m+k)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(h^{(2)}_{Y_{n+k}})=\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m+k}\}\subset\gamma\\
\neq\end{subarray}}h(X_{m+k},Y_{n+k},\gamma)e^{-\Psi(\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m+k}\}\cup\{y_{1},\dots y_{n+k}\})}\\
e^{\beta\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(y_{n+j})-g(x_{m+j})}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)dY_{n+k}.

(2.13)

Since expressions (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) are equal for every test function
h
, the result is proved.
∎

Let
\mathcal{N}_{m,n,k}
be a negligible subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\mathcal{C}
for
(\lambda^{d})^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
such that the result of Proposition
2.27
is true for every
(y_{1},\dots,y_{n},\gamma)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{m,n,k}
and
\lambda^{d}
-almost every
y_{n+1},\dots,y_{n+k}
. We introduce a set of
admissible
point configurations

E=\bigcup_{n,m\geq 0}\big\{\gamma\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}\cup\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\},(y_{1},\dots,y_{n},\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{m,n},\\
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}\subset\gamma\big\},

(2.14)

where
\mathcal{N}_{m,n}=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{N}_{m,n,k}
. According to Proposition
2.27
, if
\gamma\in E
and
\Lambda
is a bounded Borel set, then for almost every
y_{1},\dots,y_{N_{\Lambda}(\gamma)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the quantity

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}}\cup\{{y}_{1},\dots{y}_{N_{\Lambda}(\gamma)}\})-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\Lambda}(\gamma)}g(y_{j})

only depends on
\gamma
and
k
, and defines a function
C^{\beta}_{\star}
on
{\mathbb{N}}\times E
. We extend the function
C^{\beta}_{\star}
to
{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathcal{C}
by taking it equals to
0
on
{\mathbb{N}}\times(\mathcal{C}\setminus E)
. Moreover, if we use Proposition
2.27
with
\gamma\in E
,
m=n=0
,
k=N_{\Lambda}(\gamma)
and
\{{y}_{1},\dots{y}_{k}\}=\gamma_{\Lambda}
we have

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)=\sum_{x\in\gamma_{\Lambda}}g(x)+C^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Lambda}(\gamma),\gamma_{\Lambda}).

In particular the following limit exists

\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{x\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}}g(x)+C^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Lambda_{p}}(\gamma),\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}).

If the local energy of a point is defined as
h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\gamma)=V(x,\gamma)+\Psi^{\beta}_{\star}(\gamma)
then for every
\gamma\in E
we have

h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\gamma)=\lim_{p\to+\infty}\sum_{y\in\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}}g(x-y)+C^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Lambda_{p}}(\gamma),\gamma_{\Lambda_{p}}),

which proves Proposition
1.13
. We can remark that the compensator constructed here is in fact a constant sequence of functions.

Given a bounded Borel set
\Delta
with
\lambda^{d}(\Delta)>0
, the local energy of a configuration
\eta=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is defined as

H^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)=h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{1},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})+h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{2},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{x_{1}\})+\dots+h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{n},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{x_{1},\dots x_{n-1}\}).

Since for almost every
x_{1},\dots,x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{{x}_{1},\dots{x}_{j}\}\in E
(according to
2.14
) the local energy
h^{\beta}_{\star}(x_{j},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}\cup\{{x}_{1},\dots{x}_{j}\})
is well defined. We also need to check that
H^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma)
does not depend on the implicit order of the points of
\eta
when we write
\eta=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. This is the case since, according to Proposition
2.27
, for
\gamma\in E
and almost every
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle h(x,\gamma)+h(y,\gamma\cup\{x\})

\displaystyle=V(x,\gamma)+\Psi(\gamma)+V(y,\gamma\cup\{x\})+\Psi(\gamma\cup\{x\})

\displaystyle=g(x-y)+V(x,\gamma)+\Psi(\gamma)+V(y,\gamma\cup)+\Psi(\gamma\cup\{x\})-g(x)

\displaystyle=g(x-y)+V(x,\gamma)+\Psi(\gamma)+V(y,\gamma\cup)+\Psi(\gamma\cup\{y\})-g(y)

\displaystyle=V(y,\gamma)+\Psi(\gamma)+V(x,\gamma\cup\{y\})+\Psi(\gamma\cup\{y\})

\displaystyle=h(y,\gamma)+h(x,\gamma\cup\{y\}).

To conclude, it remains to prove the grand canonical DLR equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.14
.

According to Proposition
2.25
and our previous definition of the local energy of a point, we have

C^{(1)}_{\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}}(f)=\iint f(x,\gamma)e^{-\beta h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\gamma)}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)dx.

(2.15)

Equation (
2.15
), is called a Georgii-Nguyen-Zessin (GNZ) equation. Usually, DLR equations and GNZ equations are equivalent. To obtain that equivalence in our case, we follow the steps of the proof of Theorem 2 of
[
3
]
. Let
\Delta
be a bounded Borel set with
\lambda^{d}(\Delta)>0
, we denote by
\gamma\mapsto\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(\cdot\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})
a regular version of the conditional expectation (then for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
the application
A\in\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(A\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})
is a probability measure). Applying (
2.15
) to the function

(x,\gamma)\mapsto g(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})1_{\Delta}(x)f(x,\gamma_{\Delta})
where
g
is a positive measurable function and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{C}\to[0,+\infty)
a test function, leads to

\int g(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\int\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}f(x,\eta_{\Delta}\setminus\{x\})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)\\
=\int g(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\iint\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}f(x,\eta_{\Delta})e^{-\beta h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\eta_{\Delta}\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)

It implies that for
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}
-almost every
\gamma
, the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(\cdot\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})
satisfies the GNZ equation

\int\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}f(x,\eta_{\Delta}\setminus\{x\})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)\\
=\iint\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}f(x,\eta_{\Delta})e^{-\beta h^{\beta}_{\star}(x,\eta_{\Delta}\cup\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx).

Now if we consider the measure

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})=e^{\beta H^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\eta_{\Delta},\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}),

a similar computation to the one done in the proof of Proposition 5 in
[
3
]
gives

\int\sum_{x\in\eta_{\Delta}}f(x,\eta_{\Delta}\setminus\{x\})\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})=\iint f(x,\gamma)\lambda^{d}_{\Delta}(dx)\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}).

The previous equation shows that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star\mid\Delta}(\cdot\mid\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})
satisfies the so-called Slivnyak-Mecke formula, which implies that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star\mid\Delta}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})=c(\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})\Pi_{\Delta}(d\eta)
. The restriction of
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(\cdot\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})
is proportional to the Poisson point process in
\Delta
(with a factor depending on
\gamma_{\Delta^{c}}
). Since we have
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\star\mid\Delta}(\varnothing\mid\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})=\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(N_{\Delta}=0\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{c}})(\gamma)
,
we deduce from Theorem
1.11
that
c(\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\in(0,+\infty)
and

\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star\mid\Delta}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})=c(\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})e^{-\beta H^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\eta,\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})}\Pi_{\Delta}(d\eta).

It proves that the normalisation constant introduced in Theorem
1.14
is finite and non-zero since
Z^{\beta}_{\star,\Delta}(\gamma)=1/c(\gamma_{\Lambda^{c}})
. Finally, if
f
is a measurable function, from

\int f(\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma)=\iint f(\eta\cup\gamma)\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star\mid\Delta}(d\eta\mid\gamma_{\Delta^{c}})\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{\star}(d\gamma),

we obtain the grand canonical DLR equations.
∎

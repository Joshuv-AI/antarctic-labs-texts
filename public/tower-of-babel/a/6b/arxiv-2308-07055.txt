On solutions of an ill-posed Stefan problem
Evgeny Yu. Panov

Abstract
We study multi-phase Stefan problem with increasing Riemann initial data and with generally negative latent specific heats for the phase transitions. We propose the variational formulation of self-similar solutions, which allows to find precise conditions for existence and uniqueness of the solution. Bibliography : : 2 2 titles.

Introduction

In a half-plane
\Pi=\{\ (t,x)\ |\ t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\ \}
we consider the multi-phase Stefan problem for the heat equation

u_{t}=a_{i}^{2}u_{xx},\quad u_{i}<u<u_{i+1},

(1)

where
u_{-}=u_{0}<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n}<u_{n+1}=u_{+}
,
u_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
being the temperatures of phase transitions,
a_{i}>0
,

i=0,\dots,n
, are the diffusivity constants. On the unknown lines
x=x_{i}(t)
of phase transitions where
u=u_{i}
the following Stefan condition

d_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}(t)+k_{i}u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)+)-k_{i-1}u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)-)=0

(2)

is postulated, where
k_{i}>0
are the thermal conductivity of the
i
-th phase, while
d_{i}
is the Stefan number (the latent specific heat) for the
i
-th phase transition. In the case when the temperature
u(t,x)
non-decreases with respect to the spatial variable
x
the Stefan numbers
d_{i}
should be nonnegative by physical reasons. In this case the problem (
1
), (
2
) is well-posed and reduces to a degenerate nonlinear diffusion equation, see
[
3
, Chapter 5]
. In the present paper we consider the case of arbitrary
d_{i}
when the problem is generally ill-posed. This was firstly demonstrated in
[
4
]
for a one-phase Stefan problem with a negative Stefan number. Our aim is to find general conditions which guarantees the correctness of our problem.
We will study the Cauchy problem with the Riemann initial data

u(0,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u_{-},&x<0,\\
u_{+},&x>0.\end{array}\right.

(3)

By the invariance of our problem under the transformation group

(t,x)\to(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lambda\not=0
, it is natural to seek a self-similar solution of problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), which has the form
u(t,x)=v(\xi)
,
\xi=x/\sqrt{t}
. For the heat equation

u_{t}=a^{2}u_{xx}
a self-similar solution must satisfy the linear ODE
a^{2}v^{\prime\prime}=-\xi v^{\prime}/2
, the general solution of which is

v=C_{1}F(\xi/a)+C_{2},\ C_{1},C_{2}={\rm const},\mbox{ where }F(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds.

This allows to write our solution in the form

\displaystyle v(\xi)=u_{i}+\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i}}{F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})}(F(\xi/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})),

(4)

\displaystyle\xi_{i}<\xi<\xi_{i+1},\ i=0,\ldots,n,

where
-\infty=\xi_{0}<\xi_{1}<\cdots<\xi_{n}<\xi_{n+1}=+\infty
and we agree that
F(-\infty)=0
,
F(+\infty)=1
.
The parabolas
\xi=\xi_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, where
u=u_{i}
, are free boundaries. They must be determined by conditions (
2
).
In the variable
\xi
these conditions have the form

d_{i}\xi_{i}/2+\frac{k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i})}{a_{i}(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))}-\frac{k_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})}{a_{i-1}(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})-F(\xi_{i-1}/a_{i-1}))}=0,\ i=1,\ldots,n.

(5)

To investigate this nonlinear system, we notice that (
5
) coincides with the condition

\nabla E(\bar{\xi})=0
, where the function

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4,

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Omega,

the open convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is given by the inequalities
\xi_{1}<\cdots<\xi_{n}
.
Observe that
E(\bar{\xi})\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

2
Coercivity of
E

Let us introduce the sub-level sets

\Omega_{c}=\{\ \bar{\xi}\in\Omega\ |\ E(\bar{\xi})\leq c\ \},\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We are going to prove that under some exact conditions on the parameters these sets are compact. This means the coercivity of function
E(\bar{\xi})
. First, we investigate when the sets
\Omega_{c}
are bounded.

Lemma 1
.

The sets
\Omega_{c}
are bounded if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle S^{j}\doteq\sum_{i=1}^{j}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,n;

(7)

\displaystyle S_{j}\doteq\sum_{i=j}^{n}[\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}]\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,n,

(8)

where
\kappa_{i}=k_{i}/a_{i}^{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that conditions (
7
), (
8
) hold. We have to prove that the set
\Omega_{c}
is bounded. Assuming the contrary, we can find the sequence
\bar{\xi}_{m}\in\Omega_{c}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
|\bar{\xi}_{m}|\to+\infty
as
m\to\infty
(here and in the sequel we denote by
|v|
the Euclidean norm of a finite-dimensional vector
v
). Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that the coordinates
\xi_{mi}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, of the points
\bar{\xi}_{m}
have finite or infinite limits in
[-\infty,+\infty]
. Let
l
be the minimal
i
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\xi_{mi}=+\infty
. If such
i
does not exist we set
l=n+1
. Similarly, we define
r
as the maximal
i
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\xi_{mi}=-\infty
. In the case when there is no such
i
we set
r=0
. By the condition
|\bar{\xi}_{m}|\to+\infty
we can claim that either
l\leq n
or
r\geq 1
. Since the coordinates

\xi_{mi}
increase with respect to
i
then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\xi_{mi}=+\infty\ \forall i,l\leq i\leq n;\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\xi_{mi}=-\infty\ \forall i,1\leq i\leq r

and, in particular,
r<l
.
Now we observe that by L’Hôpital’s rule and the identities
F^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\xi^{2}/4}
,
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\xi F^{\prime}(\xi)/2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\frac{1-F(\xi)}{2F^{\prime}(\xi)/\xi}=\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\frac{F^{\prime}(\xi)\xi^{2}}{2(F^{\prime}(\xi)-\xi F^{\prime\prime}(\xi))}=\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\frac{\xi^{2}}{\xi^{2}+2}=1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{-F(\xi)}{2F^{\prime}(\xi)/\xi}=\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{F^{\prime}(\xi)\xi^{2}}{2(F^{\prime}(\xi)-\xi F^{\prime\prime}(\xi))}=\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\frac{\xi^{2}}{\xi^{2}+2}=1.

By these relations

F(-\xi)=1-F(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}|\xi|}e^{-\xi^{2}/4}(1+o(1))\ \mbox{ as }\xi\to+\infty.\\

(9)

Since

-\ln(F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))\geq\max(-\ln(1-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i})),-\ln F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i}))\geq 0,

we have

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi}_{m})=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}[-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))+d_{i+1}\xi_{m(i+1)}^{2}/4]+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l}^{n}[-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))+d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4]-

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r}^{l-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=r+1}^{l-1}d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}[-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln F(\xi_{m(i+1)}/a_{i})+d_{i+1}\xi_{m(i+1)}^{2}/4]+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l}^{n}[-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(1-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))+d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4]+p_{m}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}[-k_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\ln F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i-1})+d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4]+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l}^{n}[-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(1-F(\xi_{mi}/a_{i}))+d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4]+p_{m},

(10)

where

p_{m}=\sum_{i=r+1}^{l-1}d_{i}\xi_{mi}^{2}/4

is a bounded sequence, because the sequences
\xi_{mi}
have finite limits as
m\to\infty
for all
i=r+1,\ldots l-1
. Remark that the set of such
i
may be empty, in this case
p_{m}=0
. In view of (
9
), it follows from (
2
) that

\displaystyle c\geq E(\bar{\xi}_{m})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4+\sum_{i=l}^{n}[\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4+q_{m},

(11)

where, up to a vanishing sequence,

q_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\ln(\sqrt{\pi}|\xi_{mi}|/a_{i-1})+\sum_{i=l}^{n}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(\sqrt{\pi}|\xi_{mi}|/a_{i})+p_{m}.

In particular, the sequence
q_{m}\to+\infty
as
m\to\infty
and it follows from (
11
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4+\sum_{i=l}^{n}[\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4\leq c-q_{m}<0

(12)

for all sufficiently large
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. On the other hand,
\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}=S^{i}-S^{i-1}
,
\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}=S_{i}-S_{i+1}
and applying the summation by parts formula, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4=S^{r}\xi_{mr}^{2}/4-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}S^{i}(\xi_{m(i+1)}^{2}-\xi_{mi}^{2})/4\geq

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}S^{i}(\xi_{m(i+1)}-\xi_{mi})(\xi_{m(i+1)}+\xi_{mi})/4\geq 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l}^{n}[\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}]\xi_{mi}^{2}/4=S_{l}\xi_{ml}^{2}/4+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}S_{i}(\xi_{mi}^{2}-\xi_{m(i-1)}^{2})/4\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}S_{i}(\xi_{mi}-\xi_{m(i-1)})(\xi_{mi}+\xi_{m(i-1)})/4\geq 0

for sufficiently large
m
because
S_{i},S^{i}\geq 0
by assumptions (
7
), (
8
) while
\xi_{mi}<0
for
i=1,\ldots,r
,
\xi_{mi}>0
for
i=l,\ldots,n
, if
m
is sufficiently large. But this contradicts to (
12
). Hence, the set
\Omega_{c}
is bounded for every real
c
.

Conversely, assume that at least one of conditions (
7
), (
8
) fails. For definiteness we suppose that
(
7
) fails. This means that for some
1\leq r\leq n

S^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]<0.

(13)

For
\sigma>0
we define the point
\bar{\xi}(\sigma)\in\Omega
with coordinates
\xi_{i}=-\sigma+i-r
,
i=1,\ldots,r
;
\xi_{i}=i-r
,
i=r+1,\ldots,n
. Since for
i=1,\ldots,r-1

F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\xi_{i}/a_{i}}^{\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds\geq\frac{\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}}{2\sqrt{\pi}a_{i}}e^{-\xi_{i}^{2}/4a_{i}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}a_{i}}e^{-(\sigma+r-i)^{2}/4a_{i}^{2}},

and, in view of (
9
),

F(\xi_{1}/a_{0})=\frac{a_{0}}{\sqrt{\pi}|\sigma+r-1|}e^{-(\sigma+r-1)^{2}/4a_{0}^{2}}(1+o(1))\ \mbox{ as }\sigma\to+\infty

then for some constant
C

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi}(\sigma))\leq-\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4+C\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}]\sigma^{2}/4+O(\sigma).

(14)

By (
13
) it follows from (
2
) that

E(\bar{\xi}(\sigma))\to-\infty\ \mbox{ as }\sigma\to+\infty.

Therefore,
\bar{\xi}(\sigma)\in\Omega_{c}
for all sufficiently large
\sigma
and the set
\Omega_{c}
is unbounded.
Thus, if the set
\Omega_{c}
is bounded (for some
c
) then both conditions (
7
), (
8
) are satisfied.
This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we are going to demonstrate that these conditions are necessary and sufficient for coercivity of the function
E
.

Theorem 1
.

The sets
\Omega_{c}
are compact for each
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if conditions (
7
), (
8
) are satisfied.
In particular, under these conditions the function
E(\bar{\xi})
reaches its minimal value.

Proof.

If the set
\Omega_{c}
is compact for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
then it is bounded and by Lemma
1
conditions (
7
), (
8
) are satisfied.

Conversely, if conditions (
7
), (
8
) hold then the set
\Omega_{c}
is bounded (for every fixed
c
). Therefore, there exists
R>0
such that
|\bar{\xi}|\leq R
for all
\bar{\xi}\in\Omega_{c}
. This implies the estimate

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))\leq E(\bar{\xi})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\doteq c+\frac{R^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max(-d_{i},0).

Since
-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))>0
, we claim that for all
i=0,\ldots,n

-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))\leq c_{1}.

It follows that

F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})\geq\delta\doteq\exp(-c_{1}/\alpha)>0,

(15)

where
\displaystyle\alpha=\min_{i=0,\ldots,n}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})>0
. Since
F^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\xi^{2}/4}<1
, the function
F(\xi)
is Lipschitz with constant
1
, and it follows from (
15
) that

(\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i})/a_{i}\geq F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})\geq\delta,\quad i=1,\ldots,n-1,

and we obtain the estimates
\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}\geq\delta_{1}=\delta\min a_{i}
. Thus, the set
\Omega_{c}
is contained in a compact

K=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ |\ |\bar{\xi}|\leq R,\ \xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}\geq\delta_{1}\ \forall i=1,\ldots,n-1\ \}.

Since
E(\bar{\xi})
is continuous on
K
, the set
\Omega_{c}
where
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
is a closed subset of
K
and therefore is compact. For
c>N\doteq\inf E(\bar{\xi})
, this set is not empty and the entropy
E(\bar{\xi})
reaches on it a minimal value, which is evidently equal
N
.
∎

We have established the existence of minimal value
E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\min E(\bar{\xi})
. At the point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
the required condition
\nabla E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=0
is satisfied, and
\bar{\xi}_{0}
is a solution of system (
5
). The coordinates
of
\bar{\xi}_{0}
determine the solution (
4
) of our Stefan-Riemann problem. Thus, we establish the following existence result.

Theorem 2
.

Under conditions (
7
), (
8
) there exists a self-similar increasing solution of problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
).

3
Convexity of the Function
E
and Uniqueness of the Solution

In this section we find a condition of strict convexity of the function
E(\bar{\xi})
. Since a strictly convex function can have at most one critical point (and it is necessarily a global minimum) the system (
5
) has at most one solution, that is, a self-similar solution (
4
) of problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) is unique. We introduce the functions

E_{i}(\bar{\xi})=-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})),\quad i=0,\ldots,n,

which are exactly summands of the first sum in expression (
6
). They depend only on two coordinates
\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1}
if

i=1,\ldots,n-1
or on one coordinate
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{n}
if
i=0,n
, respectively. We compute the second derivatives

\frac{\partial^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}^{2}}=\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}+\frac{(F^{\prime}(y_{i}))^{2}}{(F(x_{i})-F(y_{i}))^{2}}\right),

where we denote
x_{i}=\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}
,
y_{i}=\xi_{i}/a_{i}
.
Since
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{x}{2}F^{\prime}(x)
we can transform the above relation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}^{2}}=\frac{\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\left(-\frac{y_{i}}{2}-\frac{F^{\prime}(x_{i})-F^{\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\right)+

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(y_{i})F^{\prime}(x_{i})}{(F(x_{i})-F(y_{i}))^{2}}=\beta_{i}^{-}+\gamma_{i},

(16)

where

\displaystyle\beta_{i}^{-}=\frac{\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\left(-\frac{y_{i}}{2}-\frac{F^{\prime}(x_{i})-F^{\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\right),\ i=1,\ldots,n;

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}=\frac{\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(y_{i})F^{\prime}(x_{i})}{(F(x_{i})-F(y_{i}))^{2}},\ i=0,\ldots,n.

Notice that
x_{n}=+\infty
,
y_{0}=-\infty
and therefore
F(x_{n})=1
,
F(y_{0})=0
,
F^{\prime}(y_{0})=F^{\prime}(x_{n})=0
. In particular,

\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{n}=0
. By the similar computations we find

\frac{\partial^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i+1}^{2}}=\beta_{i}^{+}+\gamma_{i},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{i+1}}=-\gamma_{i},

(17)

\mbox{ where }\ \beta_{i}^{+}=\frac{\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(x_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{2}+\frac{F^{\prime}(x_{i})-F^{\prime}(y_{i})}{F(x_{i})-F(y_{i})}\right),\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

To estimate the values
\beta_{i}^{\pm}
, we use the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For each
x>y
there exist such
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(y,x)
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(x)-F(y)}\left(-\frac{y}{2}-\frac{F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)}{F(x)-F(y)}\right)=\frac{1}{4F^{\prime}(\theta_{1})},

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(x)-F(y)}\left(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)}{F(x)-F(y)}\right)=\frac{1}{4F^{\prime}(\theta_{2})}.

(19)

Proof.

Observe that the left hand side of (
18
) can be written in the form

\frac{-\frac{y}{2}(F(x)-F(y))-(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}}=\frac{g(x)-g(y)}{h(x)-h(y)},

where

g(y)=-\frac{y}{2}(F(x)-F(y))-(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)),\quad h(y)=(F(x)-F(y))^{2}.

By the Cauchy mean value theorem there exists such a value
\theta_{1}\in(y,x)
that

\frac{g(x)-g(y)}{h(x)-h(y)}=\frac{g^{\prime}(\theta_{1})}{h^{\prime}(\theta_{1})}.

Using again the identity
F^{\prime\prime}(y)=-y/2F^{\prime}(y)
, we find that

g^{\prime}(y)=-(F(x)-F(y))/2+yF^{\prime}(y)/2+F^{\prime\prime}(y)=-(F(x)-F(y))/2,\quad h^{\prime}(y)=-2(F(x)-F(y))F^{\prime}(y).

Therefore,
g^{\prime}(\theta_{1})/h^{\prime}(\theta_{1})=1/(4F^{\prime}(\theta_{1}))
and (
18
) follows.
Similarly, the left hand side of (
19
) can be represented as the ratio
(g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y))/(h(x)-h(y))
, where

g_{1}(x)=\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-F(y))+(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)),\quad h(x)=(F(x)-F(y))^{2}.

Applying again the Cauchy mean value theorem, we arrive at (
19
). Notice also that (
19
) reduce to (
18
) after the change
x^{\prime}=-y
,
y^{\prime}=-x
.
∎

Remark 1
.

As is easy to realize from the proof, relations (
18
), (
19
) remain valid, respectively, for
x=+\infty
,
y=-\infty
.

Corollary 1
.

There exist values
\theta_{1i},\theta_{2i}\in(y_{i},x_{i})
such that

\beta_{i}^{-}=\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(y_{i})/(4F^{\prime}(\theta_{1i})),\quad\beta_{i}^{+}=\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(x_{i})/(4F^{\prime}(\theta_{2i})).

(20)

In particular,
\beta_{i}^{\pm}>0
and for
i=1,\ldots,n

\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{i-1}^{+}>\frac{1}{4}\min(\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i}),\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})).

(21)

Proof.

Inequalities (
20
) directly follows from Lemma
1
and the definitions of
\beta_{i}^{\pm}
.
Further, if
\xi_{i}\geq 0
then
\theta_{1i}>y_{i}=\xi_{i}/a_{i}\geq 0
, which implies that
F^{\prime}(y_{i})/F^{\prime}(\theta_{1i})>1
. Therefore,

\beta_{i}^{-}>\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})/4
and (
21
) follows. Similarly, in the case
\xi_{i}\leq 0
we have
\theta_{2(i-1)}<x_{i-1}=\xi_{i}/a_{i-1}\leq 0
and therefore
F^{\prime}(x_{i-1})/F^{\prime}(\theta_{2(i-1)})>1
. Hence

\beta_{i-1}^{+}>\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})/4
and (
21
) is again fulfilled.
∎

Now we are ready to formulate a sufficient condition for convexity of the function
E
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that for all
i=1,\ldots,n

\min(\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i}),\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1}))+2d_{i}\geq 0.

(22)

Then the function
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We fix a point
\bar{\xi}_{0}=(\xi_{01},\ldots,\xi_{0n})\in\Omega
and define values
d_{i}^{-}
,
d_{i}^{+}
such that
d_{i}^{-}+d_{i}^{+}=d_{i}
and
that

\beta_{i}^{-}+d_{i}^{-}/2>0,\quad\beta_{i-1}^{+}+d_{i}^{+}/2>0,

(23)

where
\beta_{i}^{\pm}
correspond to
\bar{\xi}_{0}
. Since, in view of (
21
), (
22
)

\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{i-1}^{+}+d_{i}/2>0,

such values really exist, for instance we can take

d_{i}^{-}=\frac{d_{i}\beta_{i}^{-}}{\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{i-1}^{+}},\quad d_{i}^{+}=\frac{d_{i}\beta_{i-1}^{+}}{\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{i-1}^{+}}.

We introduce the functions

\displaystyle P_{i}(\bar{\xi})=E_{i}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1})+d_{i}^{-}\xi_{i}^{2}/4+d_{i+1}^{+}\xi_{i+1}^{2}/4,\ i=1,\ldots,n-1;

\displaystyle P_{0}(\bar{\xi})=E_{0}(\xi_{1})+d_{1}^{+}\xi_{1}^{2}/4,\quad P_{n}(\bar{\xi})=E_{n}(\xi_{n})+d_{n}^{-}\xi_{n}^{2}/4.

Observe that

\sum_{i=0}^{n}P_{i}(\bar{\xi})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}^{-}+d_{i}^{+})\xi_{i}^{2}/4=\sum_{i=0}^{n}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4=E(\bar{\xi}).

First, we observe that by relations (
3
), (
17
), (
23
)

\frac{\partial^{2}P_{0}(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{1}^{2}}=P_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{01})=\beta_{0}^{+}+d_{1}^{+}/2>0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}P_{n}(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{n}^{2}}=P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0n})=\beta_{n}^{-}+d_{n}/2>0.

(24)

For
i=1,\ldots,n-1
the function
P_{i}
depend only on two variables
\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1}
,
P_{i}(\bar{\xi})=P_{i}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1})
. Denote by
D_{2}^{2}P_{i}
the Hessian of
P_{i}
as a function of two variables. In view of (
3
), (
17
) we find

\frac{\partial^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{i}^{2}}=\beta_{i}^{-}+d_{i}^{-}/2+\gamma_{i},\ \frac{\partial^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{i+1}^{2}}=\beta_{i}^{+}+d_{i+1}^{+}/2+\gamma_{i},\ \frac{\partial^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{i+1}}=-\gamma_{i},

and the matrix
D_{2}^{2}P_{i}
can be represented as the sum
R_{1}+\gamma_{i}R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a diagonal matrix with the positive (in view of (
23
)) diagonal elements

\beta_{i}^{-}+d_{i}^{-}/2
,
\beta_{i}^{+}+d_{i+1}^{+}/2
while
R_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
R_{1}>0
,
R_{2}\geq 0
, then the matrix
D_{2}^{2}P_{i}>0
(strictly positive definite).
This, together with (
24
), implies that the “full“ Hessian
D^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})\geq 0
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
and therefore

D^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}D^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})\geq 0.

Let us prove that
D^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})>0
. Assume that for some
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

D^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})\zeta\cdot\zeta=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=0.

(25)

Since
D^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})
is a sum of nonnegative matrices
D^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})
, we find that

D^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})\zeta\cdot\zeta=0\quad\forall i=0,\ldots,n.

(26)

Taking
i=0,n
we derive with the help of (
24
) that
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{n}=0
. In the case
i=1,\ldots,n-1

(
26
) reduces to the equality

D_{2}^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})v_{i}\cdot v_{i}=0,\quad v_{i}\doteq(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{i+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Since
D_{2}^{2}P_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{0})>0
, this implies that
v_{i}=0
. In particular,
\zeta_{i}=0
and we claim that
\zeta=0
.
Thus, (
26
) may hold only for
\zeta=0
, that is, the Hessian
D^{2}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})>0
. Taking into account that a point

\bar{\xi}_{0}\in\Omega
is arbitrary, we conclude that the function
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Under requirement (
22
) there exists a unique increasing self-similar solution (
4
) of problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
).

Proof.

Obviously, condition (
22
) implies that

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+d_{i}=\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})/2+(\kappa_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})+2d_{i})/2>0,

\displaystyle\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+d_{i}=\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})/2+(\kappa_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})+2d_{i})/2>0

and, in particular, conditions (
7
), (
8
) are satisfied. By Theorem
2
solution (
4
) of problem (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) exists. Uniqueness of this solution follows from strict convexity of the function
E(\bar{\xi})
claimed in Theorem
3
.
∎

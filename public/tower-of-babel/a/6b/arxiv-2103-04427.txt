Laguerre expansions on conic domains
Yuan Xu

Abstract
. We study the Fourier orthogonal expansions with respect to the Laguerre type weigh functions on the conic surface of revolution and the domain bounded by such a surface. The main results include a closed form formula for the reproducing kernels of the orthogonal projection operator and a pseudo convolution structure on the conic domain; the latter is shown to be bounded in an appropriate L p L^{p} space and used to study mean convergence of the Cesàro means of the Laguerre expansions on conic domains.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
33C50, 35C10, 42C05, 42C10

1.
Introduction

The Laguerre expansions usually mean the Fourier orthogonal expansions in the Laguerre polynomials
on the half-line
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=[0,\infty)
or expansions in product Laguerre polynomials on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
for

d\geq 2
(cf.
[
18
]
). In the present paper, we study the Fourier orthogonal expansions on the conic surface

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}=\left\{(x,t):\|x\|=t,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\right\}

with respect to the polynomials that are orthogonal with respect to the weight function

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(x,t)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)t^{-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}\quad\hbox{with}\quad h_{\kappa}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{{\kappa}_{i}},\qquad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},

as well as the expansions on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
bounded by the surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
.
This is the first study for the Laguerre type weight on conic domains; our results are new even for

{\mathsf{w}}_{0}(x,t)=t^{-1}e^{-t}
.

If
f(x,t)=f_{0}(t)
with
f_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
, then the orthogonal expansions of
f
on the conic
domain becomes the classical Laguerre expansions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, which has been studied extensively
in the literature; see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
17
,
18
]
and, for some more recent works, see

[
2
,
11
,
12
,
15
]
and the references therein. As in the study of classical Laguerre expansions,
our study relies on intrinsic properties of orthogonal polynomials that hold only for particular weight
functions and special domains. The study of orthogonal structure on the conic domains is initiated
recently in
[
22
,
23
]
. A basis of orthogonal polynomials with respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}
, called the
Laguerre type polynomials below, can be given in terms of the classical Laguerre polynomials and
spherical
h
-harmonics, the latter are orthogonal polynomials with respect to
h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)
on the unit
sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and are the simplest examples in the Dunkl’s theory of
h
-harmonics associated with
reflection groups. This basis is briefly treated in
[
22
]
, where the main results are established for
the Jacobi type polynomials on compact conic domains, for which
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is replace by
t\in[0,1]

and
{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
is replace by
(1-t)^{\gamma}
. Our starting point is to show that the Laguerre type polynomials with
respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}
on the conic surface can be regarded as limits of the Jacobi type polynomials on
the compact conic surface, which allows us to derive properties of the former from those of the latter.
In particular, starting from the closed formula for the reproducing kernels of the Jacobi type polynomials
in
[
22
]
, we are able to deduce closed form formulas for the Poisson kernel and for the reproducing
kernels of the Laguerre type polynomials on the conic domains. Since the reproducing kernels are kernels
of the orthogonal projection operators, the closed form formula provides an essential tool for our analysis.
It leads to the definition of a pseudo convolution structure on the conic surface, defined via a generalized
translation operator, which is shown to be bounded in an appropriate
L^{p}
space. As an application,
we use the convolution structure to study the mean convergence of the Cesàro
(C,\delta)
means of
the Laguerre expansions on the conic surface and determine the critical index for the convergence. We
will also establish analogues results on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
with respect to the weight function

W_{{\kappa},\mu}(x,t)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
, which can be deduced from a closed relation
between the orthogonal structures on the two domains, akin to the relation between the unit ball and the
unit sphere
[
6
]
.

The above narrative of our main results on the conic surface is rooted in the study of the Laguerre
expansions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
. The difference in the domain, however, means different geometry and different
obstacles. The closed form formula for the reproducing kernel, hence the generalized translation operator,
is substantially more involved on the conic domains. Because of the complexity of the closed form formula,
proving the boundedness of the pseudo convolution becomes far more elaborate. There is also a hidden
subtle dependence on the parameters, which shows, in the case of
{\mathsf{w}}_{0}
, that the convolution has a
weaker upper bound for
d=2
than that for
d\geq 3
. The analysis on the conic surface is not a
straightforward extension of the ordinary Laguerre expansions.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we collect results on classical orthogonal polynomials
and special functions that will be needed. The Laguerre type polynomials and the closed form formulas for
the Poisson and reproducing kernels will be studied in the the third section. The generalized translation
operator and the pseudo convolution will be defined in the fourth section, where they are shown to be
bounded and used to study the Cesàro means. Finally, the results on the conic surface are extended to
solid cone in the fifth section.

2.
Preliminary

We recall several families of orthogonal polynomials that will be needed in this section. These are
the Laguerre polynomials, the Gegenbauer and Jacobi polynomials, and spherical
h
-harmonics
on the unit sphere and orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball.

2.1.
Laguerre polynomials

For
{\alpha}>-1
, the Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}^{\alpha}
are given by

L_{n}^{\alpha}(t)=\frac{({\alpha}+1)_{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}}{k!({\alpha}+1)_{k}}t^{k},

and they can also be defined by their generating function

(2.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{\alpha}(t)r^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-r)^{{\alpha}+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{tr}{1-r}}.

The Laguerre polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)g(t)w_{\alpha}(t){\mathrm{d}}t,\quad w_{\alpha}(t):=t^{\alpha}e^{-t},\quad b_{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma({\alpha}+1)},

which is normalized so that
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{\alpha}=1
. More precisely, for
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

{\langle}L_{n}^{\alpha},L_{m}^{\alpha}{\rangle}_{\alpha}=h_{n}^{\alpha}\delta_{n,m},\quad\hbox{where}\quad h_{n}^{\alpha}:=\frac{({\alpha}+1)_{n}}{n!}=L_{n}^{\alpha}(0).

For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\alpha})
, its Fourier-Laguerre expansion is defined by

(2.2)

f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}_{n}^{\alpha}\frac{L_{n}^{\alpha}(t)}{\sqrt{h_{n}^{\alpha}}},\quad\hbox{where}\quad\widehat{f}_{n}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{n}^{\alpha}}}{\langle}f,L_{n}^{\alpha}{\rangle}_{\alpha}.

One of the important tool for studying the Laguerre expansions is a convolution structure motivated by
the product formula of the Laguerre polynomials. Let
J_{\alpha}

be the usual Bessel function and let
j_{{\alpha}}(z):=z^{-{\alpha}}J_{\alpha}(z)
. For
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, the product formula is
classical
[
19
]
; see also
[
18
, p. 142]
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{L_{n}^{\alpha}(t)L_{n}^{\alpha}(s)}{L_{n}^{\alpha}(0)}=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{{\alpha}}\Gamma({\alpha}+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{{\alpha}}\left(t+s+2\sqrt{ts}\cos{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\times{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sqrt{ts}\cos{\theta}}j_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{ts}\sin{\theta}\right)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta},

whereas for
{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}
, the formula is given in
[
8
]
and attributed to J. Boersma,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s)}{L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sqrt{ts}}L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t+s+2\sqrt{ts})+{\mathrm{e}}^{\sqrt{ts}}L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t+s-2\sqrt{ts})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-ts\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(t+s+2\sqrt{ts}\cos{\theta}\right){\mathrm{e}}^{-\sqrt{ts}\cos{\theta}}j_{1}\left(\sqrt{ts}\sin{\theta}\right)\sin{\theta}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

The convolution is defined as an integral operator with the kernel
T_{t}g(s)
, where
T_{t}
is defined by

T_{t}L_{n}^{\alpha}(s)=L_{n}^{\alpha}(t)L_{n}^{\alpha}(s)/L_{n}^{\alpha}(0)
for all
n\geq 0
. The convolution is bounded in the space

L^{p}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
that has the norm defined by
[
7
]

\|g\|_{p,u({\alpha})}=\bigg(b_{\alpha}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|g(t){\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\right|^{p}t^{{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

where
b_{\alpha}=1/\Gamma({\alpha}+1)
and, for
p=\infty
,

\|g\|_{\infty}=\|g\|_{\infty,u({\alpha})}:=\esssup\left\{|g(t)|{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}:t>0\right\}.

For
p=2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,u({\alpha})}
coincides with the usual norm of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\alpha})
.

For the summability of the Laguerre expansions, one often considers the Cesàro mans.
For
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the Cesàro means of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are defeind by

s_{n}^{\delta}=\frac{1}{A_{n}^{\delta}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n-k}^{\delta}a_{k},\qquad A_{n}^{\delta}=\binom{n+\delta}{n},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

which can also be defined by the relation

(2.5)

\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\delta+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}r^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}^{\delta}s_{n}^{\delta}r^{n}.

We denote the
(C,\delta)
means of the Fourier-Laguerre series by
s_{n}^{\delta}(w_{\alpha};f)
. The case

\delta=0
is the partial sum operator, which we denote by
s_{n}(\varpi_{\alpha};f)
; that is,

s_{n}(w_{\alpha};f)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{n}^{\alpha}L_{n}^{\alpha}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad s_{n}^{\delta}(w_{\alpha};f)=\frac{1}{A_{n}^{\delta}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n-k}^{\delta}f_{k}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}.

For
{\alpha}>-1
, the Poisson kernel of the Laguerre polynomials satisfies

(2.6)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)L_{n}^{\alpha}(y)}{\binom{n+{\alpha}}{n}}r^{n}=\frac{\Gamma({\alpha}+1)}{(1-r)^{{\alpha}+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(x+y)r}{1-r}}(xyr)^{-\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}I_{\alpha}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{xyr}}{1-r}\right),

where
I_{\alpha}(z)=e^{-{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\pi/2}J_{\alpha}(ze^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/2})
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind

[
3
, (10.27.6)]
. For
{\alpha}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
I_{\alpha}
has an integral representation
[
3
, (10.32.4)]
,
which leads to the following formula for the Poisson kernel: for
{\alpha}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,

(2.7)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)L_{n}^{\alpha}(y)}{\binom{n+{\alpha}}{n}}r^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-r)^{{\alpha}+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(x+y)r}{1-r}}c_{\alpha}\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{2\sqrt{xyr}}{1-r}v}(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v,

where the constant
c_{\alpha}
is given by

(2.8)

c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma({\alpha}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})},\qquad{\alpha}>-\tfrac{1}{2},

and the formula holds under the limit for
{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}
using

(2.9)

\lim_{a\to\frac{1}{2}+}c_{\alpha}\int_{-1}^{1}f(t)(1-t^{2})^{a-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}t=\frac{f(1)+f(-1)}{2}.

Finally, we will need the following lemma
[
8
, Lemma 1]
or
[
18
, Lemma 1.5.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\alpha}+{\beta}>-1
. Then

b_{{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|L_{n}^{{\alpha}+{\beta}}(t)\right|t^{{\alpha}/2}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}{\mathrm{d}}t\sim\begin{cases}n^{\frac{{\alpha}+1}{2}},&\hbox{if $\beta<\frac{3}{2}$}\\
n^{\frac{{\alpha}+1}{2}}\log n,&\hbox{if $\beta=\frac{3}{2}$}\\
n^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}+{\beta}-1},&\hbox{if $\beta>\frac{3}{2}$}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Jacobi and Gegenbauer Jacobi polynomials

We will also need the Jacobi polynomials, denoted by
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
, that satisfy the orthogonal relation

c^{\prime}_{{\alpha},{\beta}}\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)P_{m}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta}{\mathrm{d}}t=h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}\delta_{n,m},\quad{\alpha},{\beta}>-1,

where the normalization constant
c^{\prime}_{{\alpha},{\beta}}=2^{-{\alpha}-{\beta}-1}c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}
with

(2.10)

c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}=\frac{\Gamma({\alpha}+{\beta}+2)}{\Gamma({\alpha}+1)\Gamma({\beta}+1)}

and
h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
, the square of the norm of
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
, is given by

h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}=\frac{({\alpha}+1)_{n}({\beta}+1)_{n}({\alpha}+{\beta}+n+1)}{n!({\alpha}+{\beta}+2)_{n}({\alpha}+{\beta}+2n+1)}.

The Gegenbauer polynomials, denoted by
C_{n}^{\lambda}
, satisfy the orthogonal relation

c_{\lambda}\int_{-1}^{1}C_{n}^{\lambda}(t)C_{m}^{\lambda}(t)(1-t^{2})^{{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}t=\frac{{\lambda}}{n+{\lambda}}C_{n}^{(}1)\delta_{n,m},\quad{\lambda}>\tfrac{1}{2},

and normalized by
C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)=(2{\lambda})_{n}/n!
, where the constant
c_{\lambda}
is as in (
2.8
). When
{\lambda}=0
, the Gegenbauer polynomial reduces to the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}
of the first kind,
defined by
T_{n}(\cos{\theta})=\cos n{\theta}
. For convenience, we define

(2.11)

Z_{n}^{\lambda}(t):=\frac{n+{\lambda}}{{\lambda}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(t),\quad{\lambda}>0,\quad\hbox{and}\quad Z_{n}^{0}(t):=\begin{cases}2T_{n}(t)&n\geq 1\\
1&n=0\end{cases},

which will be used multiple times in our discussion below.

2.3.
Orthogonal polynomials on the unit sphere and the unit ball

For
{\kappa}=({\kappa}_{1},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define the weight function

(2.12)

h_{\kappa}(x)=\prod_{k=0}^{d}|x_{i}|^{{\kappa}_{i}},\qquad{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

which is invariant under the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{d}
of sign changes. On the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we consider orthogonal polynomials with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\mathbb{S}}}=c_{\kappa}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(\xi)g(\xi)h_{\kappa}^{2}(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\xi),\qquad c_{\kappa}^{h}:=\frac{\Gamma(|{\kappa}|+\frac{d}{2})}{2\Gamma({\kappa}_{1}+\frac{1}{2})\cdots\Gamma({\kappa}_{d}+\frac{1}{2})},

where
{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}
denotes the surface measure of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
|{\kappa}|={\kappa}_{1}+\cdots+{\kappa}_{d}
, and it is
normalized so that
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{\mathbb{S}}=1
. The orthogonal polynomials for this inner product are a special
case of Dunkl’s
h
-harmonics for weight functions invariant under reflection groups. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(h_{\kappa}^{2})

be the of
h
-harmonics of degree
n
in
d
variables. It consists of homogeneous polynomials
Y

of degree
n
in
d
-variables that satisfy
\Delta_{h}Y=0
, where
\Delta_{h}
is the Dunkl Laplacian, a
second order differential-difference operator
[
5
]
, which we shall not specify. If
Y\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
,
then
Y(x)=r^{n}Y(\xi)
,
x=r\xi
with
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We shall also use
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
to denote the
space of
Y(\xi)
, the restriction of
h
-harmonics on the unit sphere or spherical
h
-harmonics.
When
{\kappa}=0
, or
h_{\kappa}(x)=1
,
\Delta_{h}
becomes the ordinary Laplace operator
\Delta
and the
space
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{0}^{2})
becomes the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}
of ordinary spherical harmonics of degree
n

in
d
variables. It is known that

a_{n}^{d}:=\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}=\binom{n+d-1}{n}-\binom{n+d-3}{n-2}.

The spherical
h
-harmonics are orthogonal polynomials in the sense that
{\langle}Y_{n},Y_{m}{\rangle}_{\mathbb{S}}=0

if
Y_{n}
and
Y_{m}
are spherical
h
-harmonics of degree
n
and
m
with
n\neq m
. An explicit
orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{k}^{2})
can be given in terms of the Jacobi polynomials (cf.
[
6
, p. 229]
).
The reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
satisfies a closed form formula, which is a generalization
of the addition formula for the ordinary spherical harmonics. For
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, let

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}(h_{\kappa}^{2};\cdot,\cdot)
be the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
. Then
[
20
]

(2.13)

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}(h_{\kappa}^{2};\xi,\eta)=V_{\kappa}\left[Z_{n}^{|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}}{\langle}\xi,\cdot{\rangle}\right](\eta),\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

where
V_{\kappa}
is the integral operator, called intertwining operator, defined by

(2.14)

V_{\kappa}f(x)=c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}\int_{[1,1]^{d}}f(x_{1}u_{1},\cdots,x_{d}u_{d})\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u,

where
c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}=c_{{\kappa}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots c_{k_{d}-\frac{1}{2}}
with
c_{\lambda}
as defined
in (
2.8
) and

(2.15)

\Phi_{\kappa}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+u_{i})(1-u_{i}^{2})^{{\kappa}_{i}-1}.

If
{\kappa}_{i}=0
for some
{\kappa}_{i}
, then the identity (
2.14
) is defined under the limit (
2.9
).
In particular, if
{\kappa}_{i}=0
for all
i
, then
h_{\kappa}(x)=1
and the right-hand side of (
2.13
)
reduces to
Z_{n}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}({\langle}x,y{\rangle})
, in which case (
2.13
) degenerates to the addition
formula of the ordinary spherical harmonics. We need the following lemma proved in
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a function such that both integrals below are defined. Let

{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|k|+\frac{d-2}{2}
. Then, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(2.16)

c_{\kappa}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}V_{\kappa}[f({\langle}x,\cdot{\rangle})](\eta)h_{\kappa}^{2}(\eta)d\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\eta)=c_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}}\int_{-1}^{1}f(\|x\|v)(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v.

The closed formula (
2.13
) has been instrumental for recent progress on analysis on the
sphere equipped with
h_{\kappa}^{2}
weight; see, for example,
[
4
,
6
]
.

2.4.
Orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball

It is known
[
6
, Section 4.4]
that orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball is closely related to orthogonal
polynomials on the unit sphere. Let
h_{\kappa}
be defined in (
2.12
). For the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we let

\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}(x)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)(1-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mu>-\tfrac{1}{2},\quad x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d},

and consider orthogonal polynomials with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\mathbb{B}}}=b_{{\kappa},\mu}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}f(x)g(x)\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}(x){\mathrm{d}}x,

where
b_{{\kappa},\mu}
is a normalization constant such that
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{{\mathbb{B}}}=1
. For
{\kappa}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we define
{\kappa}_{d+1}=\mu
and
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=({\kappa},{\kappa}_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. For
x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
, we define

x_{d+1}=\sqrt{1-\|x\|^{2}}
and
X=(x,x_{d+1})
as well as
X^{-}=(x,x_{d+1})
; then

X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
and
X^{-}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. It is easy to verify that the measure
\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}{\mathrm{d}}x
on
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}

satisfies

\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}(x){\mathrm{d}}x=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}|x_{i}|^{2{\kappa}_{i}}d\sigma(X)=h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}(X){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}(X),

where
{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}
is the surface measure of
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. By symmetry, it follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}f(x)\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}(x){\mathrm{d}}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}\left[f(X)+f(X^{-})\right]h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}(X){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}(X).

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu})
be the space of orthogonal polynomials with respect to the inner
product
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{\mathbb{B}}
. We then have the decomposition

{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d+1}(h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})={\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu})\bigoplus x_{d+1}{\mathcal{V}}_{n-1}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu+1}).

In particular, a basis for
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu})
can be derived from those spherical
h
-harmonics
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d+1}(h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
that are even in the
x_{d+1}
variable. Moreover, we can also derive a close
formula for the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu})
through this correspondence.

3.
Laguerre expansions on the conic surface

We work on the conic surface that is standardized by

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}=\left\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}:\|x\|=t,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\right\},\quad d\geq 2.

For
{\kappa}\geq 0
, we define the Laguerre type weight function
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}
on the conic surface by

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(x,t)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)t^{-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t},\qquad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},

where
h_{\kappa}
is given in (
2.12
). In the first subsection, we discuss orthogonal polynomials with
respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}
. In the second subsection we derive closed form formulas for the Poisson kernel and
the reproducing kernels of these polynomials.

3.1.
Orthogonal polynomials

For
d\geq 2
, we define the inner product with respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}
on the polynomial space

{\mathbb{R}}[x,t]/{\langle}\|x\|^{2}-t^{2}{\rangle}
by

\displaystyle{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\kappa}}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t){\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t),

where
{\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t)
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the conic surface and
{\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa}}
is the normalization
constant chosen so that
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{{\kappa}}=1
. The value of
{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}
is given by

{\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t)}=\frac{c_{\kappa}^{h}}{\Gamma(2|{\kappa}|+d-1)}=b_{2|{\kappa}|+d-2}c_{\kappa}^{h},

evaluated by writing the integral over
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as, using the spherical-polar coordinates,

(3.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(x,t)d\sigma(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(t\xi,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\xi){\mathrm{d}}t.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})
denote the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n
with
respect to this inner product. The space has the same dimension as that of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d+1}
, so that

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})=\binom{n+d}{n}-\binom{n+d-2}{n-2}.

Using (
3.1
), we see that a family of orthogonal polynomials on the conic surface can be
given in terms of
h
-spherical harmonics and the Laguerre polynomials.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{Y_{\ell}^{m}:1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
. Define

(3.2)

{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t)=L_{n-m}^{2m+2|{\kappa}|+d-2}(t)Y_{\ell}^{m}(x),\quad 0\leq m\leq n,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d}.

Then
\{{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}:0\leq m\leq n,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d}\}
is an orthogonal basis of

{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})
. Moreover, the norm square of
{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}
is given by

{\mathsf{h}}_{m,n}:={\langle}{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n},{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}{\rangle}_{{\kappa}}=\frac{(2|{\kappa}|+d-1)_{n+m}}{(n-m)!}.

Proof.

Let
{\alpha}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}
. Then, using (
3.1
), we obatin

\displaystyle{\langle}{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n},{\mathsf{L}}_{m^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}{\rangle}_{{\kappa},-1}\,

\displaystyle=b_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{n-m}^{2m+{\alpha}}(t)L_{n^{\prime}-m^{\prime}}^{2m+{\alpha}}(t)t^{2m+2{\alpha}}e^{-t}{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times c_{\kappa}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\xi)Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}(\xi)h_{\kappa}^{2}(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(2m+2{\alpha}+1)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+1)}h_{n-m}^{2m+2{\alpha}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime\prime}},

where
h_{n-m}^{\alpha}
is the norm square of the Laguerre polynomial
L_{n-m}^{\alpha}
.
∎

We could consider orthogonal polynomials with respect to the weight function
h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)t^{\beta}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
for

{\beta}\neq-1
. However, the case
{\beta}=-1
appears to be the most natural setting for several reasons
[
22
]
.
In particular, the closed form formula of the reproducing kernels is of a relative simple form for
{\beta}=-1
;
see Theorem
3.7
below.

We call the polynomials in (
3.2
) Laguerre type polynomials when
{\kappa}\neq 0
, and Laguerre
polynomials when
{\kappa}=0
, on the conic surface.
For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})
, the Fourier-Laguerre expansion of
f
is defined by

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{m}^{d}}\widehat{f}_{m,\ell}^{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}\qquad\hbox{with}\qquad\widehat{f}_{m,\ell}^{n}=\frac{{\langle}f,{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}{\rangle}_{{\kappa}}}{{\mathsf{h}}_{m,n}}

The projection operator
\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}):L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})

and the
n
-th partial sum operator
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})
of this expansion are defined by

\operatorname{proj}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{m}^{d}}\widehat{f}_{m,\ell}^{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathsf{S}}_{n}f\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\operatorname{proj}_{m}f\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f\right).

If
f(x,t)
depends only on
t
, then
\widehat{f}_{m,\ell}^{n}=0
for all
m>0
and
\widehat{f}_{0,\ell}^{n}
reduces to the
classical Fourier-Laguerre coefficients of one variable. In particular, the following
proposition follows readily.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f(x,t)=f_{0}(t)
and
f_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};w_{2|{\kappa}|+d-2})
. Then the Fourier-Laguerre expansion of
f

on the conic surface becomes the Laguerre expansion of
f_{0}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};w_{2|{\kappa}|+d-2})
. In particular,

{\mathsf{S}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f,(x,t)\right)=s_{n}(w_{2|{\kappa}|+d-2};f_{0},t),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

The projection operator
\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})
of the Fourier-Laguerre series can be written as an
integral operator

\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})f={\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{P}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\right){\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s),

where
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};\cdot,\cdot)
is the reproducing kernel of the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})
,
which can be written in terms of the orthogonal basis
{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}
as

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{m}^{d}}\frac{{\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t){\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(y,s)}{{\mathsf{h}}_{m,n}}.

The Laguerre type polynomials on the conic surface can be derived from the Jacobi type polynomials on
the conic surface via a limit process. The latter polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the inner product

{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}}
defined by

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}={\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t){\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}(x,t)h_{\kappa}^{2}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t),

where
{\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}=c_{\kappa}^{h}c_{2|{\kappa}|+d-1,{\gamma}}
, with
c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}
defined in (
2.10
), and the weight
function
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}
is given by

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}(t)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)t^{-1}(1-t)_{+}^{\gamma},\qquad{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0,\quad{\gamma}>-1,

where
(1-t)_{+}^{\gamma}=(1-t)^{\gamma}
if
t<1
and
0
if
t\geq 0
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}})

be the corresponding space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n
. An orthogonal basis of this space
can be given by the Jacobi polynomials and spherical
h
-harmonics
[
22
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\{Y_{\ell}^{m}:1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{d}(h_{\kappa}^{2})
. Define

(3.3)

{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t)=P_{n-m}^{(2m+2|{\kappa}|+d-2,{\gamma})}(1-2t)Y_{\ell}^{m}(x),\quad 1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d},\,\,0\leq m\leq n.

Then
\{{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}:0\leq m\leq n,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq a_{m}^{d}\}
is an orthogonal basis of

{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}})
. Moreover, the norm square of
{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}
is given by

{\mathsf{h}}^{\mathsf{J}}_{m,n}={\langle}{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n},{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}{\rangle}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}}=\frac{(2|{\kappa}|+d-1)_{2m}}{(2|{\kappa}|+{\gamma}+d)_{2m}}h_{n-m}^{(2m+2|{\kappa}|+d-2,{\gamma})},

where
h_{n}^{({\alpha},b)}
is the norm square of the Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
.

The proof follows exactly as that of Proposition
3.1
using the Jacobi polynomials.
We denote the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}\left({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}\right)
by

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};\cdot,\cdot)
. Then

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{m}^{d}}\frac{{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t){\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(y,s)}{{\mathsf{h}}^{\mathsf{J}}_{m,n}}.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
d\geq 2
,

(3.4)

\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}{\gamma}^{m}{\mathsf{J}}_{m,\ell}^{n}\left(\frac{x}{{\gamma}},\frac{t}{{\gamma}}\right)={\mathsf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}{\gamma}^{2m}{\mathsf{h}}^{\mathsf{J}}_{m,n}={\mathsf{h}}_{m,n}.

Furthermore, for the reproducing kernels,

(3.5)

\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};\left(\frac{x}{{\gamma}},\frac{t}{{\gamma}}\right),\left(\frac{y}{{\gamma}},\frac{s}{{\gamma}}\right)\right)={\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)).

Proof.

Since the Laguerre polynomials are limits of the Jacobi polynomials
[
16
, (5.3.4)]
,

\lim_{{\beta}\to\infty}P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(1-2{\beta}^{-1}t)=L_{n}^{\alpha}(t),

the limits in (
3.4
) follow accordingly using
Y_{\ell}^{m}({\gamma}^{-1}x)={\gamma}^{-m}Y_{\ell}^{m}(x)
and
the explicit formula of
h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
. The limit (
3.5
) follows as a consequence using
the expression of both kernels written in terms of their corresponding orthogonal bases.
∎

The reproducing kernel of the Jacobi polynomials on the conic surface satisfies a closed form formula,
discovered recently in
[
22
]
, given via
Z_{n}^{\lambda}
in (

).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
and
{\gamma}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
. Let
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}
. Then, for
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\,

\displaystyle c_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+2}}Z_{2n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+{\gamma}+1}\big(\zeta(x,t,y,s;u,v)\big)

\displaystyle\times(1-v_{1}^{2})^{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u,

where
c_{{\kappa},{\gamma}}=c_{\gamma}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
and
\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=v_{1}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)+v_{2}\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}
with

(3.7)

\rho(x,t,y,s;u):=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(ts+x_{1}y_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}y_{d}u_{d})};

moreover, the identity holds under the limit (
2.9
) if either
{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}
, or
d=2
, or
{\kappa}_{i}=0
for
one or more
i
.

The formula (
3.6
) is stated in
[
22
, Theorem 10.2]
where, however,
|{\kappa}|
should be
replaced by
2|{\kappa}|
. There is also a more general formula when the weight function is
h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)t^{\beta}(1-t)^{\gamma}
,
which however is more involved for our purpose.

3.2.
Poisson kernel and reproducing kernels

Our main results in this subsection are the closed form formulas for the Poisson kernels and the reproducing
kernels of orthogonal polynomials on the conic surface. We start with the Poisson kernels

{\mathsf{P}}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};r,\cdot,\cdot)
defined by

(3.8)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};x,t,y,s)r^{n},\qquad 0\leq r<1.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
. If
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}>0
, then

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s))=\,

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(t+s)r}{1-r}}}{(1-r)^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}}c_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\exp\bigg\{\frac{2\sqrt{r}v}{1-r}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\times\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}{\mathrm{d}}v,

where
\Phi_{\kappa}
is in (
2.15
); moreover, if
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=0
or, equivalently,
{\kappa}=0
and
d=2
, then

{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{0};(x,t),(y,s))=\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(t+s)r}{1-r}}}{1-r}\cosh\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1-r}\rho(x,t,y,s)\bigg),

where
\rho(x,t,y,s)=\rho(x,t,y,s;1)=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(ts+{\langle}x,y{\rangle})}
.

Proof.

We start with (
3.6
) and change variable
v_{2}\mapsto r=\zeta(x,t,y,s;u,v_{1},v_{2})
in the
integral against
v_{2}
, so that

v_{2}=\frac{r-v_{1}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)}{\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{d}}v_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}}{\mathrm{d}}r.

This leads to a reformulation of
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};\cot,\cdot\big)
as

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\,

\displaystyle c_{\gamma}\int_{-1}^{1}Z_{2n}^{{\lambda}}(r)G_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u;r)(1-r^{2})^{{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}r,

where
{\lambda}={\gamma}+2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1={\gamma}+2|{\kappa}|+d-1
and

G_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u;r)=c_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}F_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s,r;u,v)(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}{\mathrm{d}}v\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u

in terms of the the function
F_{\gamma}
defined by

F_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s,r;u,v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}(1-r^{2})^{{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1-\frac{(r-v\rho(x,t,y,s;u))^{2}}{(1-t)(1-s)}\right)^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}

if
|r-v\rho(x,t,y,s;u)|\leq\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}
and
F_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s,r;u,v)=0
otherwise. Let us define

\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u,r)=\frac{1}{2}\left[G_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u,r)+G_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u,-r)\right],

which is even in
r
. By symmetry of the integral, it follows from (
3.9
) that

c_{\gamma}\int_{-1}^{1}Z_{m}^{{\lambda}}(r)\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;u,r)(1-r^{2})^{{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}r=\begin{cases}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big(w_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big),&m=2n,\\
0,&m=2n+1.\end{cases}

The integral in the left-hand side gives the Fourier coefficient, up to the normalization constant, of the function

r\mapsto\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;r)
of the Fourier-Gegenbauer series in
L^{2}([-1,1],(1-\{\cdot\}^{2})^{{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Consequently, using

h_{n}^{\lambda}=\frac{n+{\lambda}}{{\lambda}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)
, it follows that

\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;r)=\frac{c_{\gamma}}{c_{\lambda}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}^{\mathsf{J}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa},{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)\frac{C_{2n}^{\lambda}(r)}{C_{2n}^{\lambda}(1)}.

Since
{\lambda}={\gamma}+2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1
,
c_{\gamma}/c_{\lambda}\to 1
as
{\gamma}\to\infty
and, by the
{}_{2}F_{1}
expression of
C_{n}^{\lambda}
, it is easy
to see that
C_{2n}^{\lambda}(r)/C_{2n}^{\lambda}(1)\mapsto r^{2n}
as
{\gamma}\to\infty
, it follows from (
3.4
) that

(3.10)

\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}(x,t,y,s;r)={\mathsf{Q}}_{r^{2}}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\big).

Since
\rho(x/{\gamma},t{\gamma},y{\gamma},s{\gamma};u)=\frac{1}{{\gamma}}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)
, we see that
F_{\gamma}
satisfies

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{{\gamma}},\frac{t}{{\gamma}},\frac{y}{{\gamma}},\frac{s}{{\gamma}},r;u,v\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}(1-\frac{t}{{\gamma}})^{\gamma}(1-\frac{s}{{\gamma}})^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{t+s-2v\rho(x,t,y,s;u)}{{\gamma}(1-r^{2})}-\frac{ts+v^{2}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)^{2}}{{\gamma}^{2}(1-r^{2})}\right)^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}

if
r-\frac{v}{{\gamma}}\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\leq\sqrt{1-\frac{t}{{\gamma}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{s}{{\gamma}}}
. Taking the limit
{\gamma}\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}F_{\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{{\gamma}},\frac{t}{{\gamma}},\frac{y}{{\gamma}},\frac{s}{{\gamma}},r;u,v\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t-s}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t+s-2vr\rho(x,t,y,s;u)}{1-r^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(t+s)r^{2}-2vr\rho(x,t,y,s;u)}{1-r^{2}}\right\}.

Hence, taking the limit of
G_{\gamma}
accordingly, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{{\gamma}\to\infty}G_{\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{{\gamma}},\frac{t}{{\gamma}},\frac{y}{{\gamma}},\frac{s}{{\gamma}},r;v\right)=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(t+s)r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d+1}}\exp\left\{\frac{2r\rho(x,t,y,s;u)}{1-r^{2}}v\right\}

\displaystyle\times c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}v\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u.

Changing variable
v\to-v
shows that the right-hand side is an even function in
r
. Hence, the
limit of
\widetilde{G}_{\gamma}
is the sam as the limit of
G_{\gamma}
. Thus, comparing the above limit with
(
3.10
), we complete the proof.
∎

The Poisson kernel can be regarded as the generating function of the reproducing kernels. We derive a
closed formula of the latter from that of the former.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
. If
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}>0
, then for
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=C_{{\kappa}}\int_{[1,1]^{d}}\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}}j_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}\big(\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\sin{\theta}\big)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u,

where
C_{{\kappa}}=\frac{2^{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}\Gamma({\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
and
\rho(\cdot)

is given in (
3.7
). If
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=0
or equivalently,
d=2
and
|{\kappa}|=0
, then

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[{\mathrm{e}}^{-\rho(x,t,y,s)}L_{n}^{0}\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s)\big)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+{\mathrm{e}}^{\rho(x,t,y,s)}L_{n}^{0}\big(t+s-2\rho(x,t,y,s)\big)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\rho(x,t,y,s)^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{0}\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s)\cos{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s)\cos{\theta}}j_{1}\big(\rho(x,t,y,s)\sin{\theta}\big)\sin{\theta}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

In particular, for
d\geq 2
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq 0
,

(3.13)

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(0,0)\big)=L_{n}^{2|{\kappa}|+d-2}(t),\qquad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}.

Proof.

We can compare the Poisson formula for
{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};\cdot,\cdot)
with (
2.7
)
of the Laguerre polynomials by setting

\displaystyle t^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\left(t+s-\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}-2(x_{1}y_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}y_{d}u_{d})}\right),

\displaystyle s^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\left(t+s+\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}-2(x_{1}y_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}y_{d}u_{d})}\right),

which are well defined, since the expression under the square root is nonnegative for

\|x\|=t
and
\|y\|=s
by the Cauchy inequality, and they satisfy

(3.14)

t^{*}+s^{*}=t+s\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sqrt{t^{*}s^{*}}=\rho(x,t,y,s;u).

Comparing the formula in Theorem
3.6
with (
2.7
), we obtain with

{\alpha}={\alpha}_{\kappa}
,

{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};x,t,y,s)=\frac{1}{(1-r)^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(t^{*})L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(s^{*})}{L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}r^{n}\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u.

If
{\alpha}>0
, we apply the product formula (
2.3
) on
L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(t^{*})L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(s^{*})

which gives an integral representation that contains only
t^{*}+s^{*}
and
t^{*}s^{*}
, so that we obtain,
by (
3.14
),

\displaystyle\frac{L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(t^{*})L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(s^{*})}{L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}=\,

\displaystyle\frac{2^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}}j_{{\alpha}-1}\left(\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\sin{\theta}\right)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.\,

From the generating function (
2.1
) it follows readily that

\frac{1}{(1-r)^{{\beta}+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)r^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{{\alpha}+{\beta}+1}(x)r^{n}.

Consequently, putting the last three displayed formula together, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{r}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};x,t,y,s)\,

\displaystyle=C_{\kappa}\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\int_{0}^{\pi}L_{n}^{2{\alpha}}\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}\big)r^{n}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}}j_{{\alpha}-1}\left(\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\sin{\theta}\right)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u.

By (
3.8
), the coefficient of
r^{n}
is the reproducing kernel
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};\cdot,\cdot)
,
which gives the stated formula for
{\alpha}>0
. The proof for
{\alpha}=0
follows the same procedure but
using (
2.4
) instead. Finally, using
\rho(x,t,0,0)=0
and
j_{\alpha}(0)=1/(2^{\alpha}\Gamma({\alpha}+1))
,
(
3.13
) follows readily from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).
∎

4.
Convolution structure on conic surface with Laguerre weight

The closed form of the reproducing kernel suggests a pseudo convolution structure on the conic surface,
which is bounded in the
L^{p}
space defined on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as follows:
f\in L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})

if
\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}
is finite, where

\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}:=\left({\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|f(x,t){\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\right|^{p}{\mathsf{u}}_{\kappa}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad{\mathsf{u}}_{k}(x,t)=t^{-1}h_{\kappa}^{2}(x),

for
1\leq p<\infty
, and

\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}:=\esssup\left\{|f(x,t)|{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}:(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}\right\}.

For
p=2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,{\kappa}}
coincides with the usual norm of
L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})
.

The pseudo convolution is defined via a generalized translation operator, which is defined and shown to be
bounded in the first subsection. The convolution structure is studied in the second subsection, which is used
to analyze the Cesàro summability of the generalized Laguerre series on the conic surface in the third
subsection.

4.1.
Translation operator

For
d\geq 2
,
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
, let
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}
as before. For
g:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
we define a
generalized translation operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
g\in L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. If
{\alpha}_{k}>0
, define

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)=\,

\displaystyle C_{\kappa}\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\int_{0}^{\pi}g(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\cos{\theta}}j_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}\big(\rho(x,t,y,s;u)\sin{\theta}\big)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u,

where
C_{\kappa}
is given in (
3.11
); if
d=2
and
{\kappa}=0
, define

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[{\mathrm{e}}^{-\rho(x,t,y,s)}g\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s)\big)+{\mathrm{e}}^{\rho(x,t,y,s)}g\big(t+s-2\rho(x,t,y,s)\big)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\rho(x,t,y,s)^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}g\big(t+s+2\rho(x,t,y,s)\cos{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times e^{-\rho(x,t,y,s)\cos{\theta}}j_{1}\big(\rho(x,t,y,s)\sin{\theta}\big)\sin{\theta}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

The definition is motivated by the closed form formula of the reproducing kernel. Indeed, it
follows readily that

(4.15)

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),\cdot)={\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}L_{n}^{2|{\kappa}|+d-2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

The generalized translation operator will be used to define a pseudo convolution structure on the conic surface
in the next subsection. We first show that this operator is bounded. It turns out that we need to consider
two separated cases.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Assume
{\alpha}_{k}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. If
g\in L^{p}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
then

\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g\|_{p,{\kappa}}\leq e^{t/2}\|g\|_{p,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Proof.

Setting
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we can write

\rho(x,t,y,s;u)=\sqrt{ts}v(\xi,\eta;u),\qquad v(\xi,\eta;u)=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\xi_{1}\eta_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{d}\eta_{d}u_{d})}.

To simplify the notation, we write
{\alpha}={\alpha}_{\kappa}
throughout the proof and define

G(t,s;z)=g\left(t+s+2\sqrt{ts}\,z\right){\mathrm{e}}^{-\sqrt{ts}\,z}.

The integral representation of the Bessel function
[
3
, (10.9.4)]
states that

(4.16)

j_{{\alpha}}(z)=\frac{2^{-{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{{\mathrm{i}}zu}(1-u^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u,\quad{\alpha}>-\tfrac{1}{2},

and the formula holds under the limit when
{\alpha}\to-\frac{1}{2}+
, so that
j_{-\frac{1}{2}}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cos z
. It
implies, in particular, that
|j_{{\alpha}-1}(z)|\leq 1/(2^{{\alpha}-1}\Gamma({\alpha}))
for all
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
With the explicit formula of
C_{\kappa}
, it follows that
C_{\kappa}|j_{{\alpha}-1}(z)|\leq c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}}
. This last inequality
leads to an upper bound of
|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g|
,

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)|\,

\displaystyle\leq c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\big|G\big(t,s;v(\xi,\eta;u)\cos{\theta}\big)\big|(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}\Phi_{\kappa}(u){\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle=:F(t,\xi,s,\eta).

By the definition of
\|g\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}
, it follows readily that

|G(t,s;u)|=\left|g\left(t+s+2\sqrt{ts}\,u\right)\right|{\mathrm{e}}^{-(t+s+2\sqrt{ts}\,u)/2}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t+s)/2}\leq{\mathrm{e}}^{(t+s)/2}\|g\|_{\infty,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})},

which leads immediately to

\left\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g\right\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}=\sup_{(y,s)}\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}\big(t,s,v(\xi,\eta)\big)\right|{\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}\leq{\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}\|g\|_{\infty,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}.

This establishes the stated result for
p=\infty
.

Next we consider
p=1
. Using the intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
, we can write

\displaystyle F(t,\xi,s,\eta)=c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}V_{\kappa}\left[\left|G\left(t,s;\sqrt{\frac{1+{\langle}\xi,\cdot{\rangle}}{2}}\cos{\theta}\right)\right|\right](\eta)(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

Hence, using the identity (
2.16
), we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{(x,t)}(s):=\,

\displaystyle c_{\kappa}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}F(t,\xi,s,\eta)h_{\kappa}^{2}(\eta){\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|G\left(t,s,\sqrt{\frac{1+u}{2}}\cos{\theta}\right)\right|(1-u^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle 2^{2{\alpha}+1}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\left|G(t,s,v\cos{\theta})\right|v^{2{\alpha}}(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta},

where the second step follows from changing variable
v\to\sqrt{(1+u)/2}
. Changing variable

{\theta}\mapsto u=v\cos{\theta}
in the integral with respect to
{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}
, we further obtain that

(4.17)

\displaystyle I_{(x,t)}(s)\,

\displaystyle=2^{2{\alpha}+1}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-v}^{v}|G(t,s;u)|(v^{2}-u^{2})^{{\alpha}-1}{\mathrm{d}}u\,v(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v

\displaystyle=2^{2{\alpha}+1}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{-1}^{1}|G(t,s;u)|\int_{|u|}^{1}v(v^{2}-u^{2})^{{\alpha}-1}(1-v^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}v\,{\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle=2^{2{\alpha}}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{-1}^{1}|G(t,s;u)|(1-u^{2})^{2{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u\int_{0}^{1}z^{{\alpha}-1}(1-z)^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=c_{2{\alpha}}\int_{-1}^{1}|G(t,s;u)|(1-u^{2})^{2{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u,

where we have verified in the last step, using
\Gamma(2a)=2^{2a-1}\Gamma(a)\Gamma(a+\frac{1}{2})/\sqrt{\pi}
, that

2^{2{\alpha}}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}z^{{\alpha}-1}(1-z)^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}z=2^{2{\alpha}}c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma({\alpha})\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})}=c_{2{\alpha}}.

Changing one more variable
u\to z=t+s-2\sqrt{ts}u
in the last integral of (
4.17
), we further
obtain

(4.18)

\displaystyle I_{(x,t)}(s)\,

\displaystyle=c_{2{\alpha}}\int_{z_{-}}^{z_{+}}|g(z)|{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{z-t-s}{2}}\left(1-\frac{(z-t-s)^{2}}{4ts}\right)^{2{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\sqrt{ts}}

\displaystyle=c_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)z^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z}{\mathrm{d}}z,

where
z_{\pm}=t+s\pm 2\sqrt{ts}=(\sqrt{t}\pm\sqrt{s})^{2}
and

H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)=\frac{(t^{2}+s^{2}+z^{2}-2ts-2tz-2sz)^{2{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}}{(4tsz)^{2{\alpha}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{t+s+z}{2}},

if
z\in[z_{-},z_{+}]
and
H_{d}(t,s;z)=0
otherwise. In particular, it follows that
H_{\alpha}(t,s,z)=0
if

2ts+2tz+2sz\leq t^{2}+s^{2}+z^{2}
, so that
H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)
is symmetric in
t,s
and
z
and
it is nonnegative. Moreover, choosing
g(z)={\mathrm{e}}^{(-z+t+s)/2}
in the (
4.18
), it follows readily that

(4.19)

\displaystyle c_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)z^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(z+t+s)}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}z=c_{2{\alpha}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-u^{2})^{2{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u=1.

Consequently, exchanging the order of integrals and, by the symmetry of
H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)
, integrating

H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)
with respect to
s
first, we obtain by (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) that

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g\right\|_{1,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}\right|s^{-1}h_{\kappa}^{2}(y){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq b_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{(x,t)}(s)s^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=c_{2{\alpha}}b_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)s^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-(s+z)/2}{\mathrm{d}}sz^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z/2}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}b_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|z^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z/2}{\mathrm{d}}z={\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha})},

which completes the proof for
p=1
. The case
1<p<\infty
follows from the Riesz-Thorin theorem
by interpolating between the estimates for
p=1
and
p=\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

The operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}
is not a positive operator because of the presence of the Bessel function, but its
boundedness in the
L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}
norm is as strong as that of a positive operator in the sense that
it is bounded with a constant 1 in the right-hand side of the inequality in the Proposition
4.2
.

The above proposition holds under the assumption that
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, which comes from the upper
bound of
j_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}(t)
deduced from (
4.16
). For
{\alpha}_{\kappa}<\frac{1}{2}
, we need to a different upper
bound of
j_{{\alpha}_{\kappa}-1}
given in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
{\alpha}>0
and
t\in\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,

\left|j_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{{\alpha}-1}\Gamma({\alpha})}+\frac{t^{-1}}{2^{{\alpha}}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{3}{2})}.

Proof.

We start from the three-term relation
J_{{\alpha}-1}(t)+J_{{\alpha}+1}(t)=2{\alpha}t^{-1}J_{\alpha}(t)
of the Bessel function

[
3
, (10.6.1)]
, which gives immediately

j_{{\alpha}-1}(t)=2{\alpha}j_{\alpha}(t)-t^{2}j_{{\alpha}+1}(t),\qquad{\alpha}>0.

We use (
4.16
) for
j_{{\alpha}+1}
and integrate by parts once to obtain

\displaystyle j_{{\alpha}+1}(t)=\frac{2^{-{\alpha}-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{3}{2})}\frac{2{\alpha}+1}{it}\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}tu}u(1-u^{2})^{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}u

which implies that
t\left|j_{{\alpha}+1}(t)\right|\leq\frac{2^{-{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\alpha}+\frac{3}{2})}
. Hence,
using
2{\alpha}|j_{\alpha}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2^{{\alpha}-1}\Gamma({\alpha})}
, the desired estimate follows from the three term relation.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. For
g\in L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\cap L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g\|_{1,{\kappa}}\leq e^{t/2}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{k})}+\frac{1}{(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1)\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}.

Proof.

Let
{\alpha}={\alpha}_{k}
. We need to consider two cases,
{\alpha}=0
and
{\alpha}>0
. Assume first
{\alpha}>0
. We adopt
the same notation as in the proof of the previous proposition for
{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Using Lemma
4.3
,
we obtain
C_{\kappa}|j_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}(z)|\leq c_{{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}+\frac{z}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi}c_{{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{2}}
.
Applying this inequality with
z=\rho(x,t,y,s;u)=\sqrt{ts}v(\xi,\eta;u)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)\right|\leq F(t,\xi,s,\eta)+F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta),

where
F(t,\xi,s,\eta)
is the same as before and
F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta)
is defined by

\displaystyle F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta)=\frac{\sqrt{ts}}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}V_{\kappa}

\displaystyle\left[\sqrt{\frac{1+{\langle}\xi,\cdot{\rangle}}{2}}\left|G\left(t,s;\sqrt{\frac{1+{\langle}\xi,\cdot{\rangle}}{2}}\cos{\theta}\right)\right|\right](\eta)

(4.20)

\displaystyle\times(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}

in terms of the intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
. The estimate of
F(t,\xi,s,\eta)
for
{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{2}
remains valid
for
{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
, which gives the first term in the right-hand side of the desired estimate. The estimate of

F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta)
can be carried out by the similar approach. In particular, following the proof of Proposition

4.2
and checking the constant carefully, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{(x,t)}(s):=\,

\displaystyle c_{\kappa}^{h}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta)h_{\kappa}^{2}(\eta)d\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{ts}}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}|G(t,s;u)|(1-u^{2})^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}u.

Changing variable
u\to z=t+s-2\sqrt{ts}u
as in the case of
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we further obtain

\displaystyle I_{(x,t)}(s)=\frac{1}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|H_{\alpha}^{(1)}(t,s;z)z^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z}{\mathrm{d}}z,

where
H_{\alpha}^{(1)}
is defined by

H_{\alpha}^{(1)}(t,s;z)=\frac{(t^{2}+s^{2}+z^{2}-2ts-2tz-2sz)^{2{\alpha}}}{2(4tsz)^{2{\alpha}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{t+s+z}{2}},

if
z\in[z_{-},z_{+}]
and
H_{d}(t,s;z)=0
otherwise. Again,
H_{\alpha}^{(1)}(t,s;z)
is symmetric in
t,s
and
z
and
it is nonnegative. Furthermore, we also have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\alpha}(t,s;z)^{(1)}z^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(z+t+s)}{2}}{\mathrm{d}}z=\sqrt{ts}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-u^{2})^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}u=\frac{\sqrt{ts}}{c_{2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Consequently, continuing as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|F_{1}(t,\xi,\cdot)\|_{1,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle=b_{2{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{(x,t)}(s)s^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{2{\alpha}}}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\alpha}^{(1)}(t,s;z)s^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-(s+z)/2}{\mathrm{d}}sz^{2{\alpha}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z/2}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}\frac{b_{2{\alpha}}}{(2{\alpha}+1)\pi c_{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(z)|z^{2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-z/2}{\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2{\alpha}+1)\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{t/2}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})},

where we have used
b_{2{\alpha}}=1/\Gamma(2{\alpha}+1)
and
1/c_{2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}=b_{2{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}/b_{2{\alpha}}

in the last step. This completes the proof for
{\alpha}>0
.

Next we consider
{\alpha}=0
, which is equivalent to
{\kappa}=0
and
d=2
since
|{\kappa}|\geq 0
and
d\geq 2
. This
corresponds to the Laguerre weight
{\mathsf{w}}_{0}(x,t)=t^{-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
on the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{3}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
In this case, we write the operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}
as a sum of two parts,

{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}={\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(1)}+{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(2)},

where, using the notation
G(t,s;u)
and
v(\xi,\eta)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(1+{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle})}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(1)}g(y,s)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[G(t,s;v(\xi,\eta))+G(t,s;-v(\xi,\eta))\right];

\displaystyle{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(2)}g(y,s)\,

\displaystyle=-\frac{ts}{2}[v(\xi,\eta)]^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}G(t,s;v(\xi,\eta)\cos{\theta})j_{1}\big(\sqrt{ts}v(\xi,\eta)\sin{\theta}\big)\sin{\theta}{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

For
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(1)}g
, we first integrate over
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, which is the unit circle and
{\mathsf{b}}_{2}=2\pi
.
Let
{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle}=\cos\phi
. Then
v(\xi,\eta)=\cos\frac{\phi}{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(1)}g(y,s)\right|d\sigma\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|G\left(t,s;\cos\frac{\phi}{2}\right)+G\left(t,s;-\cos\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\right|{\mathrm{d}}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|G(t,s;u)+G(t,s;-u)\right|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}|G(t,s;u)|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}.

Sine
c_{0}=1/\pi
, we see that the integral in the right-hand side is the integral in (
4.17
)
when
{\alpha}_{k}=0
. The proof below (
4.17
) for
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
remains valid for
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=0
,
which proves the bound for
\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(1)}g\|_{1,0}\leq e^{t/2}\|g\|_{1,u(0)}
. For
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(2)}
,
we use the bound
|zj_{1}(z)|\leq\frac{2}{\pi}
in the proof of Lemma
4.3
to obtain

\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(2)}g(y,s)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{ts}|v(\xi,\eta)|\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|G(t,s;v(\xi,\eta)\cos{\theta})\right|{\mathrm{d}}{\theta}.

Since
V_{\kappa}
becomes identity operator when

, the right-hand side of the above expression
coincides with
F_{1}(t,\xi,s,\eta)
with
{\alpha}=0
. The evaluation of the norm of

in the case of

remains valid for

, so that the norm
\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}^{(2)}g\|_{1,0}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\|g\|_{1,u(\frac{1}{2})}

follows right away. This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Pseudo convolution structure on the conic surface

We define a pseudo convolution structure on the conic surface.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}
. For
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
and

g\in L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, define

f*g(x,t)={\mathsf{b}}_{{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s){\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}(y,s)d\sigma(y,s),\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}.

From (
4.15
) it follows readily that, for
f\in L^{2}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,

(4.21)

\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f)=f*L_{n}^{2|{\kappa}|+d-2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

The following proposition justifies calling it a convolution.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
. For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})
and
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}})
,

(4.22)

\operatorname{proj}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f*g\big)=\widehat{g}_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}\operatorname{proj}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f\big),

where
\widehat{g}_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}
is the Fourier-Laguerre coefficient as defined in (
2.2
).
In particular,

(4.23)

\|f*g\|_{2,k}\leq\|f\|_{2,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{2,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}.

Proof.

Writing
g
in terms of its Laguerre expansion in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}})
as in (
2.2
),
it follows by (
4.15
) that

{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{g}_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}((x,t),\cdot)\quad\hbox{and}\quad f*g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{g}_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}),

where the second identity follows from the first one by the definition of
f*g
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{2,{\kappa}}

coincides with the norm of
L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})
and
\|\cdot\|_{2,u({\alpha})}
coincides with the
norm of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\alpha})
, we have the Parseval identities

\|f*g\|_{2,k}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\|\operatorname{proj}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f*g\big)\right\|_{2,{\kappa}}^{2}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\|g\|_{2,u({\alpha}_{\kappa})}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\widehat{g}_{n}^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}\right|^{2}.

Hence, (
4.23
) follows from (
4.22
) and the Cauchy inequality.
∎

We now show that the pseudo convolution operator is bounded in
L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
for
p\neq 2
.
We again need to consider two cases:
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
{\alpha}_{k}<\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Assume
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. For
f\in L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
and
g\in L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

\|f*g\|_{p,{\kappa}}\leq\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Proof.

If
p=1
, then using the Proposition
4.2
and the obvious symmetry of
{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}(y,s)
in
(x,t)
and
(y,s)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{1,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s){\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\right|{\mathsf{u}}_{\kappa}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|\cdot\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(y,s)}g\|_{{\kappa},1}{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|e^{s/2}{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})},

which proves the stated inequality for
p=1
. Furthermore, for
p=\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle=\sup_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|f*g(x,t){\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}\sup_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)\right|{\mathrm{e}}^{s/2}{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}\sup_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g\|_{{\kappa},1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\leq\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})},

which is the stated inequality for
p=\infty
. The case
1<p<\infty
follows from the Riesz-Thorin
interpolation theorem.
∎

A straight forward generalization of the theorem is the Young’s inequality.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. For
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
with
r^{-1}=p^{-1}+q^{-1}-1
, if

f\in L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
and
g\in L^{q}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, then
f*g\in L^{r}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
and

\|f*g\|_{r,{\kappa}}\leq\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{q,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}.

For the case
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, the boundedness of
f*g
is more restricted. We need to introduce another
space
L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa}),*}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
that has the norm

\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}^{*}=\left({\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)e^{-t/2}|^{p}\sqrt{t}\,{\mathsf{u}}_{\kappa}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x,t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

which has an additional
\sqrt{t}
in the integral in comparing with
\|f\|_{p,{\kappa}}
, and also

\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}^{*}=\esssup\left\{|f(x,t)|\sqrt{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}:(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}\right\}.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. For
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})\cap L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa}),*}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})

and
g\in L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\cap L^{1}_{u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

\|f*g\|_{1,{\kappa}}\leq\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}+\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}^{*}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}.

Proof.

The proof follows along the line of that Theorem
4.7
using Proposition
4.2
instead.
It follows then that

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{1,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|\cdot\|{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}g(y,s)\|_{1,{\kappa}}{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|{\mathsf{u}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}{\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{(2{\alpha}_{k}+1)\pi}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|\sqrt{s}{\mathsf{u}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{-s/2}{\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{k})}+\frac{1}{(2{\alpha}_{k}+1)\sqrt{\pi}}\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}^{*}\|g\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{k}+\frac{1}{2})}.

Since
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq 0
, we can replace the constant by 1. This proves the stated inequality.
∎

We restrict to the case
p=1
in the above theorem. For
p=\infty
, we need to modify the the norm in
the left-hand side. For example, the following inequality

\left|f*g(x,t)\min\{1,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}\right|\leq\|f\|_{\infty,{\kappa}}\left(\|g\|_{\infty,u(2{\alpha}_{k})}+\|g\|_{\infty,u(2{\alpha}_{k}+1)}\right).

follows readily. We omit the details.

4.3.
Cesàro means on the conic surface

For
\delta>-1
, the Cesàro means of the Fourier orthogonal expansions with respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n-k+\delta}{n-k}\operatorname{proj}_{k}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(x,t),(y,s)){\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s),

where
{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})
denote the
(C,\delta)
kernel

{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(x,t),(y,s))=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n-k+\delta}{n-k}{\mathsf{P}}_{k}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(x,t),(y,s)).

By the closed formula of the projection operator in (
4.21
), we can express the means in terms of the
puedo convolution.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\delta>-1
. Then

(4.24)

{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}{\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}\left(L_{n}^{\delta+2|{\kappa}|+d-1}\right)(y,s),

and

(4.25)

{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f)=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}f*L_{n}^{\delta+2|{\kappa}|+d-1}.

Proof.

By (
4.15
), we see that

{\mathsf{K}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(x,t),\cdot)={\mathsf{T}}_{(x,t)}\tau_{n}^{\delta}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\tau_{n}^{\delta}=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n-k+\delta}{n-k}L_{k}^{2|{\kappa}|+d-2}.

From the identity (
2.5
) and the generating function (
2.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+\delta}{n}\tau_{n}^{\delta}(z)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\delta+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{2|{\kappa}|+d-2}(z)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\delta+2|{\kappa}|+d}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{zr}{1-r}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{\delta+2|{\kappa}|+d-1}(z)r^{n}.

Hence, comparing the coefficients of
r^{n}
, we obtain

\tau_{n}^{\delta}=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}L_{n}^{\delta+2|{\kappa}|+d-1},

which proves (
4.24
) and, consequently, (
4.25
).
∎

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\kappa}}
denote the operator norm of
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}
defined by

\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\kappa}}=\sup\left\{\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};f)\|_{p,{\kappa}}:f\in L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})\right\}.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\delta>-1
. If
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then for
p=1
and
p=\infty
,

(4.26)

\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{k}+1}\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}\sim\begin{cases}n^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}-\delta}&-1<\delta<2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}\\
\log n&\delta=2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}\\
1&\delta>2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}f
converse to
f
in
L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if

\delta>2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}=2|{\kappa}|+d-\frac{3}{2}
and the condition is sharp if
p=1
and
p=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-2}{2}
. We need to consider two cases
{\alpha}_{\kappa}>0
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}=0
separately.
Since
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};f)
is an integral operator, a standard argument shows that,

(4.27)

\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})\|_{p,{\kappa}}=\sup_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(x,t),(y,s)\big)\right|{\mathrm{e}}^{s/2}{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

for
p=1
and
p=\infty
. By the estimate in Proposition
4.2
or Theorem
4.7
, we
have

\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\kappa}}\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{k}+1}\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}.

Furthermore, by (
3.13
), we see that
{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}};(0,0),(y,s)\big)=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{k}+1}(t)
,
so that

\displaystyle\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\kappa}}\,

\displaystyle\geq{\mathsf{b}}_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|{\mathsf{K}}_{n}^{\delta}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa};(0,0),(y,s)\big)\right|{\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa}(y,s){\mathrm{e}}^{s/2}{\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{k}+1}(t)\right|t^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t/2}{\mathrm{d}}t.

Together, we have proved the equality in (
4.26
), whereas the asymptotic in (
4.26
)
follows form Lemma
2.1
with
{\alpha}=4{\alpha}_{k}
and
{\beta}=\delta-2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1
and

\binom{n+\delta}{n}\sim n^{\delta}
. Consequently, it follows that
\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}f({\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}})\|_{p,{\kappa}}

is bounded if
\delta>2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}
and it is unbounded if
\delta\leq 2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

It is evident that the lower bound of
\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{\kappa})\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{{\kappa}}}
in (
4.27
) remains
valid when
{\alpha}_{\kappa}<\frac{1}{2}
. However, the bound in Propostion
4.4
or Theorem
4.9

does not lead to a matching upper bound.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{0};f)
converse to
f
in
L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})\cap L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa}),*}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
if
\delta>2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1
. Moreover,
{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{0};f)
does
not converge for all
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
if
\delta\leq 2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
4.9
, we obtain that

\|{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{\delta}({\mathsf{w}}_{0};f)\|_{1,{\kappa}}\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\left(\left\|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}\right\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}+\left\|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}\right\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{1,{\kappa}}^{*}\right).

The term
\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\|L_{n}^{\delta+2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1}\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{\kappa})}
is bounded as shown in the
case of
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. By Lemma
2.1
with
{\alpha}=4{\alpha}_{k}+1
and
{\beta}=\delta-2{\alpha}_{k}1
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\binom{n+\delta}{n}}\|L_{n}^{\delta+1}\|_{1,u(2{\alpha}_{k}+\frac{1}{2})}\sim\begin{cases}n^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1-\delta}&\delta<2{\alpha}_{k}+\frac{3}{2}\\
n^{2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+1-\delta}\log n&\delta=2{\alpha}_{k}+\frac{3}{2}\\
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\delta>2{\alpha}_{k}+\frac{3}{2},\end{cases}

which is bounded if
\delta>2{\alpha}_{k}+1
. This proves the convergence part.
∎

If
{\kappa}=0
, then our result holds for
{\mathsf{w}}_{0}(x,t)=t^{-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
. In this case, our result shows that
the
(C,\delta)
means converge in
L^{p}_{{\mathsf{u}}({\kappa})}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,
p=1
and
\infty
if and only if

\delta>d-\frac{3}{2}
when
d\geq 3
. For
d=2
, the condition
\delta>\frac{1}{2}
remains necessary
but our sufficient condition
\delta>1
is weaker. We believe that the condition
\delta>\frac{1}{2}

should be sufficient as well, for which one may need to find a way to estimate the Cesàro kernel
without using the closed form formula.

More generally, we believe that Theorem
4.11
should hold for all
{\alpha}_{\kappa}\geq 0
, which
means that
\delta>2{\alpha}_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{2}
is necessary and sufficient. This is non-trivial even for the classical
Laguerre expansions in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, where the convergence of the
(C,\delta)
means for
w_{\alpha}
is
determined via the convolution structure when
{\alpha}\geq 0
, but requires delicate hard estimate when

{\alpha}<0
. The same obstacle appears in the Laguerre expansions in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
with the weight

w_{{\alpha}_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots w_{{\alpha}_{d}}(x_{d})
, for which the convergence of the
(C,\delta)
means is
determined under the restriction
{\alpha}_{i}\geq 0

[
18
,
21
]
and, as far as we are aware, the case
of negative
{\alpha}_{i}
remains open.

The product Laguerre expansions of
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
is particularly pertinent to our
{\mathsf{w}}_{0}
weight when

d=2
. Indeed, as we shall see in the next section, the orthogonal expansion for
{\mathsf{w}}_{0}
and
d=2

is closely related to the orthogonal expansion on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{2}=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:|x|\leq t\}
with
respect to the weight function
(t^{2}-x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and the latter is equivalent to the product Laguerre
expansions with respect to the weight function
w_{-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})w_{-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
; see
Remark
5.1
.

5.
Laguerre expansions on the solid cone

In this section we consider the Laguerre expansion on the solid cone

{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}=\left\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}:\|x\|\leq t,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\right\},\qquad d\geq 1

with respect to the weight function
W_{{\kappa},\mu}
defined by

W_{{\kappa},\mu}(x,t)=h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t},\quad{\kappa}_{i}\geq 0,\quad\mu>-\tfrac{1}{2}.

5.1.
Orthogonal polynomials

We define the inner product on
L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\kappa},\mu})
with respect to
W_{{\kappa},\mu}
by

\displaystyle{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\kappa},\mu}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{b}}_{k,\mu}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t)W_{{\kappa},\mu}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t,

where
{\mathbf{b}}_{{\kappa},\mu}=b_{2|{\kappa}|+2\mu+d-1}b_{{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
with
b_{\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
being the normalization
constant of the weight
h_{\kappa}^{2}(x)(1-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}
on
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\kappa},\mu})
denote
the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n
with respect to this inner product. Then

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\mu})=\binom{n+d}{n}.

An orthogonal basis for this space can be given in terms of orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball
and the Laguerre polynomials.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\{P_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{m}(\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu}):|{\mathbf{k}}|=n\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu})
. Define

(5.1)

{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t)=L_{n-m}^{2m+2|{\kappa}|+2\mu+d-1}(t)t^{m}P_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{m}\left(\varpi_{{\kappa},\mu};\frac{x}{t}\right),\quad|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,\quad 0\leq m\leq n.

Then
\{{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}:|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,\quad 0\leq m\leq n\}
is an orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\kappa},\mu})
.
Moreover, the norm square of
L_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}
is given by

{\mathbf{h}}^{\mathbf{L}}_{m,n}:={\langle}{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n},{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}{\rangle}_{{\kappa},\mu}=\frac{(2|{\kappa}|+2\mu+d)_{n+m}}{(n-m)!}.

The orthogonality is stated in
[
22
, Proposition 3.3]
and the norm can be verified directly as in the proof of
Proposition
5.1
. When
{\kappa}=0
, the polynomials
{\mathbf{L}}_{m,\ell}^{n}
are called the Laguerre polynomials
on the cone.

Remark 5.1
.

When
d=1
, the cone
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{2}
is the wedge domain bounded by the two lines
x=\pm t
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

and the weight function is
W_{{\kappa},\mu}(x,t)=|x|^{2{\kappa}}(t^{2}-x^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-t}
, where we have written

{\kappa}={\kappa}_{1}\geq 0
. If we rotate the domain by
90^{\circ}
by setting
x_{1}=\frac{t+x}{2}
and
x_{2}=\frac{t-x}{2}
,
then the wedge domain becomes
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
and the weight function
W_{{\kappa},\mu}
becomes

(5.2)

U_{{\kappa},\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2{\kappa}}|x_{1}x_{2}|^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x_{1}-x_{2}},\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}.

In particular, for
{\kappa}=0
,
U_{{\kappa},\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})=w_{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})w_{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})
is the product Laguerre
weight.

For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\kappa},\mu})
, the Fourier-Laguerre expansion on the cone is defined by

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{|{\mathbf{k}}|=m}\widehat{f}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}\qquad\hbox{with}\qquad\widehat{f}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}=\frac{{\langle}f,{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}{\rangle}_{\mu}}{{\mathbf{h}}_{m,n}^{\mathbf{L}}}

For
{\kappa}=0
, this orthogonal expansion can be used to derive an explicit solution for the non-homogeneous
wave equation; see
[
13
]
. The projection operator

the

\operatorname{proj}_{n}\left(W_{{\kappa},\mu};f\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{|{\mathbf{k}}|=m}\widehat{f}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathbf{S}}_{n}f\left(W_{{\kappa},\mu};f\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\operatorname{proj}_{k}\left(W_{{\kappa},\mu};f\right).

If

depends only on

\widehat{f}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}=0
for all

to the classical Fourier-Laguerre series.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
{\kappa}\geq 0
and
\mu\geq-\frac{1}{2}
. Define
{\alpha}=\mu+|{\kappa}|+\frac{d-1}{2}
. If

,

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};w_{2{\alpha}})
. In particular,

{\mathbf{S}}_{n}\left(W_{{\kappa},\mu};f,(x,t)\right)=s_{n}(w_{2{\alpha}};f_{0},t),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

As in the case of conic surface, the polynomials
{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}
can also be deduced from taking the
limit of the Jacobi type orthogonal polynomials defined in
[
22
]
on the solid cone. A more direct
way, however, is to relate them to the orthogonal polynomials on the conic surface that we have already
encountered. The relation is modeled after, in fact uses, the relation between orthogonal polynomials on
the unit ball and those on the unit sphere.

For
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
, we introduce the notation
X=(x,\sqrt{t^{2}-\|x\|^{2}})
, so that
\|X\|=t
and

(X,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
. Similarly, for
(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}

{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=({\kappa},\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+2}

x=tx^{\prime}
,
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d}

(5.3)

W_{{\kappa},\mu}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t=h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}(X)t^{-1}e^{-t}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}x^{\prime}{\mathrm{d}}t}{\sqrt{1-\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}}}={\mathsf{w}}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(X){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(X,t),

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
in the right-hand side.
Let

{\mathbb{V}}_{0,+}^{d+2}=\left\{(x,x_{d+1},t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}:x_{d+1}\geq 0\right\},

which is half of the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
. Likewise, we define
{\mathbb{V}}_{0,-}^{d+2}
as the other half
with

. Let

. Then
(x,t)\mapsto(X,t)
maps
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
onto

{\mathbb{V}}_{0,+}^{d+2}
and
(x,t)\mapsto(X^{-},t)

{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
onto
{\mathbb{V}}_{0,-}^{d+2}
. By symmetry,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}}f(y,s){\mathsf{w}}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}(y,s){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y,s)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}\frac{1}{2}\left[f(X,t)+f(X^{-},t)\right]W_{{\kappa},\mu}(X,t){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t.

As a consequence of this relation, we obtain a relation between orthogonal polynomials on the cone
and the conic surface. To emphasis the dependence on the weight function, we denote the orthogonal
polynomial given in (

) by
{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(W_{{\kappa},\mu})
.
Define

Y_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n,1}(X,t)={\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(W_{{\kappa},\mu};(x,t)),\qquad Y_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n,2}(X,t)=X_{d+1}{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n-1}(W_{{\kappa},\mu+1};(x,t))

for

and

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=({\kappa},\mu)

Proof.

Since

is even when

is even and odd when

is odd, it follows that

n

(X,t)

variable. Moreover, the same argument

is a polynomial of degree
n
in
(X,t)
variables, odd in
X_{d+1}
variable.
By (

), it follows that
Y_{{\mathbf{k}},m}^{n,1}
and

are orthogonal with
respect to
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
by parity and, moreover, by (

)
and
W_{{\kappa},\mu+1}(x,t){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}t=X_{d+1}^{2}{\mathsf{w}}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(X){\mathrm{d}}\sigma(X,t)
followed by (

\displaystyle\big\langle Y_{{\mathbf{k}},m}^{n,2},Y_{{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime},2}\big\rangle_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\,

\displaystyle=\big\langle{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(W_{{\kappa},\mu}),{\mathbf{L}}_{m^{\prime},{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}(W_{{\kappa},\mu})\big\rangle_{{\kappa},\mu}

\displaystyle=\big\langle{\mathbf{L}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n-1}(W_{{\kappa},\mu+1}),{\mathbf{L}}_{m^{\prime},{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}-1}(W_{{\kappa},\mu+1})\big\rangle_{{\kappa},\mu+1},

. Furthermore, it is easy to verify that

This completes the proof.
∎

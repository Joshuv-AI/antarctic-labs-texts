Deformations of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -instantons on nearly G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} manifolds
Ragini Singhal

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the deformation theory of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons on nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds. There is a one-to-one correspondence between nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures and real Killing spinors, thus the deformation theory can be formulated in terms of spinors and Dirac operators. We prove that the space of infinitesimal deformations of an instanton is isomorphic to the kernel of an elliptic operator. Using this formulation we prove that abelian instantons are rigid. Then we apply our results to describe the deformation space of the canonical connection on the four normal homogeneous nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds.

1 Introduction

Nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures on a 7-manifold
M
are defined by a so-called positive 3-form
\varphi
. Such a
3
-form induces a metric
g
, an orientation and a spin structure on
M
(see §
2
). We denote by
\nabla^{g}
the Levi-Civita connection and its lift on the spinor bundle. The
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is nearly parallel if for some
\tau_{0}\neq 0

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}*_{\varphi}\varphi,

or equivalently if there exists a real Killing spinor
\eta
such that

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{X}\eta=-\frac{\tau_{0}}{8}X\cdot\eta.

Nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds were introduced as manifolds with weak holonomy
\mathrm{G}_{2}
by Gray in
[
28
]
. Some examples of such manifolds are the round and squashed
7
-spheres, the Aloff–Wallach spaces, and the Berger space
\rm{SO}(5)/\rm{SO}(3)
. The inclusion of the exceptional Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
as a possible holonomy group for Riemannian manifolds in Berger’s list
[
12
]
led mathematicians to look for examples of manifolds with holonomy
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. In
[
43
]
Wang established the first correspondence between parallel spinors and integrable geometries. Later the classification of manifolds with real Killing spinors in
[
31
,
22
,
25
,
29
,
10
]
established a link between manifolds with weak holonomy and manifolds with real Killing spinors. These manifolds are Einstein with positive scalar curvature. Except for the round
7
-sphere, the dimension of the space of Killing spinors on a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold is 1,2 or 3 (see
[
24
]
) giving rise to three types: proper, Sasaki–Einstein and 3-Sasakian respectively. The cones over these manifolds have holonomy contained in
\textup{Spin}(7)
which makes these spaces particularly important in the construction and understanding of manifolds with torsion free
\textup{Spin}(7)
-structures.

The correspondence between nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures and Killing spinors has been extensively used to produce many results on nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds. The infinitesimal deformation space of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures was explicitly described as an eigenspace of a Dirac operator in
[
7
]
. In the homogeneous setting, non-trivial deformations were only found for the Aloff–Wallach space and which in
[
21
]
were proved to be obstructed.

The spinorial approach can also be used to study gauge theory on manifolds with weak holonomy. A connection
A
on
M
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton
if its curvature
F
satisfies the algebraic condition

\displaystyle F\wedge\varphi=*_{\varphi}F,

or equivalently
F\cdot\eta=0
. In this article we describe the infinitesimal deformation space of instantons on nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds as the eigenspaces of the Dirac operators associated to the one parameter family of connections with skew-symmetric torsion

\displaystyle\nabla^{t}_{X}Y=\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+\frac{t}{3}\varphi(X,Y,\cdot),

described in
[
4
,
1
,
3
,
2
,
5
]
. At
t=-1
, the connection
\nabla^{-1}
is the
characteristic connection
which is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton. We explicitly describe the infinitesimal deformation space of the characteristic connections for the normal homogeneous nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds classified in
[
24
]
. In
[
16
]
an analogous description for the infinitesimal deformation space of instantons on nearly Kähler 6-manifolds is given. On an oriented manifold with real Killing spinor
\eta
the volume form
\vol
defines a Killing spinor
\vol\cdot\eta
. On a nearly Kähler
6
-manifold
\{\eta,\vol\cdot\eta\}
defines a 2 dimensional space of Killing spinors whereas on a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold
\eta
and
\vol\cdot\eta
are linearly dependent. This prevents us from having a relation like in
[
16
]
*Proposition 4(iii) which makes the computation of the infinitesimal deformation space much more convenient (See
4.3
). In fact we show in §
4
that such a relation does not exist in the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
case by explicitly computing the kernel of the elliptic operator for the homogeneous nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds. In
[
20
]
the author uses the spinorial approach to describe the deformation space of instantons on asymptotically conical
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds.

Let
M^{7}
be a manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
and let
\eta
be the Killing spinor associated to
\varphi
. A connection
A
on
M
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton
if its curvature
F_{A}
satisfies the algebraic condition

\displaystyle F_{A}\wedge\varphi=*_{\varphi}F_{A}.

The above condition is equivalent to
F_{A}\cdot\eta=0
as shown in §
3
. When the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure is parallel (the case when the constant
\tau_{0}=0
) these instantons clearly solve the Yang–Mills equation
d_{\nabla}^{*}F=0
. The analogous result was proved in the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
case by Harland–Nölle
[
30
]
. They showed that the instantons on manifolds with real Killing spinors solve the Yang–Mills equation which makes the study of instantons on nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds important from the point of view of gauge theory in higher dimensions. However
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons in the parallel case are the minimizers of the Yang–Mills functional which is not necessarily true for the nearly parallel case, as proved by Ball–Oliveira in
[
9
]
. The first examples of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons on parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds were constructed in
[
17
]
,
[
42
]
and
[
40
]
. In
[
9
]
the authors proved the existence of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons on certain Aloff–Wallach spaces and classified invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons on these spaces with gauge group U(1) and SO(3). Recently, Waldron
[
41
]
proved that the pullback of the standard instanton on
S^{7}
obtained from ASD instantons on the 4-sphere via the quaternionic Hopf fibration lies in a smooth, complete, 15-dimensional family of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons.

In §
2
we describe a
1
-parameter family of connections on the spinor bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}
over nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds and the associated Dirac operators. In
[
21
]
the authors introduced a Dirac type operator and used it to completely describe the cohomology of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds and proved the obstructedness of infinitesimal deformations of the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure on the Aloff–Wallach space. We remark that the Dirac type operator introduced there is not associated to any connection in the
1
-parameter family.

In §
3
we describe the deformation space of a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
instanton
A
as an eigenspace of a Dirac operator associated to
A
and the characteristic connection (Theorem
3.2
). Using this description, we show that on a compact nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton
A
is rigid if the structure group is abelian (cf. Theorem
3.7
(i)) or if all the eigenvalues of a linear operator
L_{A}
are greater than
-\frac{28}{5}
(Theorem
3.7
(ii)) . The instanton
A
is also rigid if all the eigenvalues of
L_{A}
are less than
6
, as shown in
[
9
]
*Proposition 8 where authors used a Weitzenböck formula, while the proof of Theorem
3.7
(ii) uses the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula for the family of Dirac operators associated to
\nabla^{t}
and
A
.

In §
4
we describe the infinitesimal deformation space of the characteristic connection on all the homogeneous nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds whose nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
metric is normal. By considering the actions of the Lie groups
H
and
\mathrm{G}_{2}
on
G/H
we can view the characteristic connection as an
H
-connection or a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-connection. We compute its infinitesimal deformation spaces in both of these cases. The results are recorded in Theorem
4.6
. The deformations are shown to be genuine in all cases except that of the Aloff–Wallach space
\frac{\rm{SU}(3)\times\rm{SU}(2)}{\rm{SU}(2)\times\rm{U}(1)}
. In the latter case the author is currently unaware of any known family of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons for which the infinitesimal deformations are the ones found in Theorem
4.6
.

2 Preliminaries

2.1 Nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures

Let
M
be a
7
-dimensional Riemannian manifold equipped with a positive
3
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)
. The
3
-form
\varphi
induces an orientation and a metric on
M
and thus a Hodge star operator
*_{\varphi}
on the space of differential forms (see
[
14
]
). The
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
is called a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure on
M
if it satisfies the following differential equation for some non-zero
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle d\varphi=\tau_{0}*_{\varphi}\varphi.

We denote the
4
-form
*_{\varphi}\ \varphi
by
\psi
in the remainder of this article. The condition
d\varphi=\tau_{0}\psi
implies
\mathrm{d}\psi=0
, thus the nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
is co-closed.

Every manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure is orientable and spin, and thus admits a spinor bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}
. Let
\nabla^{LC}
be the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric on
M
. A spinor
\eta\in\Gamma(\not{\mathcal{S}})
is a real Killing spinor if for some non-zero
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{LC}\eta=\delta X\cdot\eta\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ X\in\Gamma(TM).

There is a one-to-one correspondence between nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures and real Killing spinors on
M
. Given a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
that satisfies (
2.1
) there exists a real Killing spinor
\eta
that satisfies (
2.2
) with
\delta=-\frac{1}{8}\tau_{0}
and vice-versa. Switching
-\frac{\tau_{0}}{8}
to
\frac{\tau_{0}}{8}
corresponds to changing the orientation of the cone
M\times_{r^{2}}\mathbb{R}^{+}
. See
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
for more details.

The constant
\tau_{0}
can be altered by rescaling the metric and readjusting the orientation. In this article we use
\tau_{0}=4
. With this choice of
\tau_{0}
our nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
and Killing spinor
\eta
satisfies the following equations respectively

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=4\psi,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{LC}\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\eta.

Manifolds with nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures have several nice properties which can be found in detail in
[
11
]
. In particular they are positive Einstein. Let
g
be the metric induced by
\varphi
, then the Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=\frac{3}{8}\tau_{0}^{2}g
and the scalar curvature
\textup{Scal}_{g}=7\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=\frac{21}{8}\tau_{0}^{2}
. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure on
M
induces a splitting of the spaces of differential forms on
M
into irreducible
\mathrm{G}_{2}
representations. The space of
2
-forms
\Lambda^{2}(M)
decomposes as

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}(M)

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}_{7}(M)\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14}(M),

where
\Lambda^{2}_{l}
has pointwise dimension
l
. More precisely, we have the following description of the space of forms :

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{7}(M)

\displaystyle=\{X\lrcorner\varphi\mid X\in\Gamma(TM)\}=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(M)\mid*(\varphi\wedge\beta)=-2\beta\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{14}(M)

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(M)\mid\beta\wedge\psi=0\}=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(M)\mid*(\varphi\wedge\beta)=\beta\}.

Note that we are using the convention of
[
34
]
which is opposite to that of
[
33
]
and
[
15
]
.

The space
\Lambda^{2}_{14}
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Since the group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
preserves the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
, it preserves the real Killing spinor
\eta
induced by
\varphi
. The space
\Lambda^{2}_{14}
can be equivalently defined as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{14}

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\ |\ \omega\cdot\eta=0\}.

We make use of this identification when defining the instanton condition on
M
in §
3
.

2.2 The spinor bundle

For a
7
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
with a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
, the spinor bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}
is a rank-8 real vector bundle over
M
and is isomorphic to the bundle
\underline{\mathbb{R}}\oplus TM=\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1}
. At each point
p\in M
, we can identify the fiber of
\not{\mathcal{S}}
with
\mathbb{R}\oplus T_{p}M\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{7}\cong\textup{Re}(\mathbb{O})\oplus\textup{Im}(\mathbb{O})=\mathbb{O}
. If
\eta
is the real Killing spinor on
M
induced by
\varphi
then we have the isomorphism

\displaystyle\not{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=(\Lambda^{0}TM\cdot\eta)\oplus(\Lambda^{1}TM\cdot\eta)\cong\Lambda^{0}TM\oplus\Lambda^{1}TM.

Under this isomorphism any spinor
s=(f\cdot\eta,\alpha\cdot\eta)\in\not{\mathcal{S}}
can be written as
s=(f,\alpha)\in\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1}
.

The
3
-form
\varphi
induces a cross product
\times_{\varphi}
on vector fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Throughout this article we use
e_{i}
to denote both tangent vectors and
1
-forms, identified using the metric. All the computations are done in a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{7}\}
and any
repeated indices are summed over all possible values. With respect to this local orthonormal frame, we have
(X\times_{\varphi}Y)_{l}=X_{i}Y_{j}\varphi_{ijl}
. The octonionic product of two octonions
(f_{1},X_{1})
and
(f_{2},X_{2})
is given by,

\displaystyle(f_{1},X_{1})\cdot(f_{2},X_{2})

\displaystyle=(f_{1}f_{2}-\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle,f_{1}X_{2}+f_{2}X_{1}+X_{1}\times X_{2}).

As shown in
[
35
]
the Clifford multiplication of a 1-form
Y
and a spinor
(f,Z)
is the octonionic product of an imaginary octonion and an octonion and is thus given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle Y\cdot(f,Z)

\displaystyle=-(-\langle Y,Z\rangle,fY+Y\times Z).

Note that the product defined above differs from
[
35
]
by a negative sign due to our choice of the representation of
Cl_{7}
on
\not{\mathcal{S}}

[
38
]
*Chapter 1.8.
We define the Clifford multiplication of any
p
-form
\beta=\beta_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{p}}
with a spinor by,

\displaystyle\beta\cdot(f,X)

\displaystyle=\beta_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}(e_{i_{1}}\cdot(e_{i_{2}}\cdot\ldots\cdot(e_{i_{p}}\cdot(f,X))\ldots)).

We record an identity for Clifford algebras for later use and refer the reader to
[
38
]
*Proposition 3.8, Ch1 for the proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}e_{j}\cdot\alpha\cdot e_{j}=(-1)^{p+1}(n-2p)\ \alpha.

The Clifford multiplication between a
p
-form
\alpha
and a
1
-form
v
can be written as
[
38
]
*Proposition 3.9

\displaystyle v\cdot\alpha=v\wedge\alpha-v\lrcorner\alpha.

The vector bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-representation and since
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is the isotropy group of the 3-form
\varphi
the map
\mu\mapsto\varphi\cdot\mu
from the bundle of spinors

to itself is an isomorphism. The same argument holds for the 4-form
\psi
. The following formulae described in
[

23
]
will prove useful in later computations.

.

The subbundles of
\not{\mathcal{S}}
isomorphic to
\Lambda^{0}
and
\Lambda^{1}

and

\Lambda^{1}

is a

respectively. By Schur’s Lemma there exist real constants

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}f,\quad\varphi\cdot\alpha=\lambda_{1}\alpha,

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}f,\quad\psi\cdot\alpha=\mu_{1}\alpha.

A common feature between nearly Kähler
6
-manifolds and manifolds with nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures is the presence of a unique canonical connection
\nabla^{can}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion defined below. The Killing spinor
\eta
is parallel with respect to this connection and thus we have
\textup{Hol}(\nabla^{can})\subset\mathrm{G}_{2}
. It was proved by Cleyton–Swann in
[
18
]
*Theorem 6.3 that a
G
-irreducible Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with an invariant skew-symmetric non-vanishing intrinsic torsion falls in one of the following categories:

1.

it is locally isometric to a non-symmetric isotropy irreducible homogeneous space, or,

2.

it is a nearly Kähler
6
-manifold, or,

3.

it admits a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure.

For the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold
(M,\varphi)
we define a
1
-parameter family of connections on
TM
that include the canonical connection
\nabla^{can}
. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\nabla^{t}
be the
1
-parameter family of connections on
TM
defined for all
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM)
by

(2.6)

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{t}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{LC}_{X}Y,Z)+\frac{t}{3}\varphi(X,Y,Z).

Let
T^{t}
be the torsion
(1,2)
-tensor of
\nabla^{t}
. Since the connection
\nabla^{LC}
is torsion free

\displaystyle g(X,T^{t}(Y,Z))

\displaystyle=g(X,\nabla^{t}_{Y}Z)-g(X,\nabla^{t}_{Z}Y)-g(X,[Y,Z])

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{LC}_{Y}Z,X)+\frac{t}{3}\varphi(Y,Z,X)-g(\nabla^{LC}_{Z}Y,X)-\frac{t}{3}\varphi(Z,Y,X)

\displaystyle\quad-g(X,[Y,Z])

\displaystyle=\frac{2t}{3}\varphi(X,Y,Z).

Therefore the torsion tensor
T^{t}
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle T^{t}(X,Y)=\frac{2t}{3}\varphi(X,Y,\cdot)

which is proportional to
\varphi
and is thus totally skew-symmetric.

By
[
38
]
*Theorem 4.14 the lift of the connection
\nabla^{t}
on the spinor bundle which is also denoted by
\nabla^{t}
acts on sections
\mu
of
\not{\mathcal{S}}
as

(2.8)

\displaystyle\nabla^{t}_{X}\mu

\displaystyle=\nabla^{LC}_{X}\mu+\frac{t}{6}(i_{X}\varphi)\cdot\mu.

The space of real Killing spinors is isomorphic to
\Lambda^{0}
thus for a Killing spinor
\eta
it follows from (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.2
that for any vector field
X
since
X\cdot\varphi+\varphi\cdot X=-2\ i_{X}\varphi
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{t}_{X}\eta

\displaystyle=\nabla^{0}_{X}\eta+\frac{t}{6}(i_{X}\varphi)\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\eta-\frac{t}{12}(X\cdot\varphi+\varphi\cdot X)\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\eta-\frac{t}{12}(7X\cdot\eta-X\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{t+1}{2}X\cdot\eta.

Therefore
\eta
is parallel with respect to the connection
\nabla^{-1}
. The connection
\nabla^{-1}
thus has holonomy group contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion and is therefore the canonical connection on the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold
M
described in
[
18
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}^{t}
of the connection
\nabla^{t}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{t}

\displaystyle=(6-\frac{2t^{2}}{3})g.

Proof.

By using the expression of the Ricci tensor for a connection with a totally skew-symmetric torsion from
[
23
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{t}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ric}^{0}(X,Y)-\frac{t}{3}\mathrm{d}^{*}\varphi(X,Y)-\frac{2t^{2}}{9}g(i_{X}\varphi,i_{Y}\varphi)

The Ricci tensor for the Levi-Civita connection is given by
\mathrm{Ric}^{0}=6g
. Since
d\psi=0
,
\varphi
is co-closed and the second term in the above expression vanishes. The third term can be calculated in a local orthonormal frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}
using the contraction identity
\varphi_{ijk}\varphi_{ijl}=6\delta_{kl}
as follows

\displaystyle g(i_{X}\varphi,i_{Y}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,k,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}X_{k}Y_{\gamma}\varphi_{ijk}\varphi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}g(e_{i}\wedge e_{j},e_{\alpha}\wedge e_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,k,\gamma}X_{k}Y_{\gamma}(\varphi_{ijk}\varphi_{ij\gamma}-\varphi_{ijk}\varphi_{ji\gamma})

\displaystyle=3\sum_{k,\gamma}X_{k}Y_{\gamma}\delta_{k\gamma}=3g(X,Y).

Summing up all the terms together give the desired identity for
\mathrm{Ric}^{t}
.
∎

3 Deformation theory of instantons

Let
\mathcal{P}\to M
be a principal
K
-bundle. We denote by
\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}
the adjoint bundle associated to
\mathcal{P}
. Let
A
be a connection
1
-form on
\mathcal{P}
and
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
be the curvature
2
-form associated to
A
given by

\displaystyle F_{A}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}A+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A].

There are many ways to define the instanton condition on
A
. If
(M,g)
is equipped with a
G
-structure such that
G\subset\mathrm{O}(n)
, there is a subbundle
\mathfrak{g}(T^{*}M)\subset\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M
whose fibre is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}=\textup{Lie}(G)
. The connection
A
is an instanton if the 2-form part of
F_{A}
belongs to
\mathfrak{g}(T^{*}M)
. In global terms,
A
is an instanton if

\displaystyle F_{A}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}(T^{*}M)\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}).

Note that in dimension
7
if
M
is equipped with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure then this condition implies that
A
is an instanton if the
2
-form part of
F_{A}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}(T^{*}M)=\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{14})
.

The second definition of an instanton is a special case of the first when the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is simple. Its quadratic Casimir is a
G
-invariant element of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
which may be identified with a section of
\Lambda^{2}\otimes\Lambda^{2}
and thus to a
4
-form
Q
by taking a wedge product. Since this
Q
is
G
-invariant the operator
u\to*(*Q\wedge u)
acting on
2
-forms commutes with the action of
G
and hence by Schur’s Lemma the irreducible representations of
G
in
\Lambda^{2}
are eigenspaces of the operator. Then
F_{A}
is an instanton if

\displaystyle*(*Q\wedge F_{A})

\displaystyle=\nu F_{A}.

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. In dimension
7
it turns out that
Q=\psi
(see
[
30
]
) and the above condition is equivalent to
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{14})
when
\nu=1
.

Furthermore if
M
is a spin manifold, and the spinor bundle admits one or more non-vanishing spinors
\eta
, then
A
is an instanton if

\displaystyle F_{A}\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=0.

When
M
has a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure and
\eta
is the corresponding spinor, (
2.4
) implies that a the above condition is satisfied if and only if
A
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton. An interested reader can read further on these definitions and their relations in
[
30
]
.

We remark that for an instanton
A
on a manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure
\varphi
all the above definitions are equivalent. They all imply that the curvature
F_{A}
associated to
A
lies in
\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{14})
and thus satisfies
all
of these equivalent conditions:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{A}\cdot\eta&=0,\\
F_{A}\wedge\varphi&=*F,\\
F_{A}\wedge\psi&=0,\\
F_{A}\intprod\varphi&=0.\end{split}

From now on in this article we use these instanton conditions interchangeably according to the context without further specification. Note that the above definitions are valid for any general
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure and not only for nearly parallel ones.

On a manifold with real Killing spinors it was shown in
[
30
]
that instantons solve the Yang–Mills equation. In the case of a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton we can prove this fact by direct computation. For an instanton
A
, (
3.1
) and the second Bianchi identity imply

\displaystyle(d^{A})^{*}F_{A}

\displaystyle=*d^{A}*F_{A}

\displaystyle=*d^{A}(\varphi\wedge F_{A})

\displaystyle=4*(\psi\wedge F_{A})=0.

3.1 Infinitesimal deformation of instantons

Let
M^{7}
be a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold. We are interested in studying the infinitesimal deformations of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons on

. An infinitesimal deformation of a connection

represents an infinitesimal change in

and thus, is a section of
T^{*}M\otimes Ad_{\mathcal{P}}
. If

is an infinitesimal deformation of

, the corresponding change in the curvature

up to first order is given by
\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon
. A standard gauge fixing condition on this perturbation is given by
(\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon=0
. So in total the pair of equations whose solutions define an infinitesimal deformation of an instanton

is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon)\cdot\eta=0,\ \ (\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon=0.

On a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold we can define a
1
-parameter family of Dirac operators

\displaystyle D^{t,A}

\displaystyle=D^{A}+\frac{t}{2}\varphi\cdot

The 1-parameter family of connections on the spinor bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}
defined in (

) and the connection
A
on
\mathcal{P}
can be used to construct a 1-parameter family of connections on the associated vector bundle
\not{\mathcal{S}}\otimes Ad_{\mathcal{P}}
. We denote by
\nabla^{t,A}
, the connection induced by
\nabla^{t}
and
A
for all

respectively. We denote by
D^{t,A}
the Dirac operator associated to
\nabla^{t,A}
. The following proposition associates the solutions to (
3.2
) to a particular eigenspace of
D^{t,A}
for each

. The proposition was proved in
[
26
]
for

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\epsilon
be a section of

, and let

be the Dirac operator constructed from the connections
\nabla^{t,A}
for

solves (
3.2
) if and only if

(3.3)

\displaystyle D^{t,A}(\epsilon\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{t+5}{2}\epsilon\cdot\eta.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{a},a=1\dots 7\}
be a local orthonormal frame for
T^{*}M
. Then

\displaystyle D^{0,A}(\epsilon\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=e_{a}\cdot\nabla^{0}_{a}(\epsilon\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=(e_{a}\cdot\nabla^{0}_{a}\epsilon)\cdot\eta+e_{a}\cdot\epsilon\cdot\nabla^{0}_{a}\eta

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon+(\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon)\cdot\eta+e_{a}\cdot\epsilon\cdot\nabla^{0}_{a}\eta.

Applying Proposition
2.1

\epsilon

e_{a}\cdot\epsilon\cdot e_{a}\cdot\eta=5\epsilon\cdot\eta
. So if
\eta
is a real Killing spinor then (

\displaystyle D^{0,A}(\epsilon\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon+(\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon)\cdot\eta-\frac{1}{2}e_{a}\cdot\epsilon\cdot e_{a}\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon+(\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon-\frac{5}{2}\epsilon)\cdot\eta.

It follows from (
2.8
) and the identity
\sum_{a}e_{a}\cdot i_{a}\varphi=3\varphi
that

\displaystyle D^{t,A}

\displaystyle=D^{0,A}+\frac{t}{2}\varphi\cdot

Since

, by Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle D^{t,A}(\epsilon\cdot\eta)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathrm{d}^{A}\epsilon+(\mathrm{d}^{A})^{*}\epsilon+\frac{-t-5}{2}\epsilon\Big)\cdot\eta.

The equation

since these two components live in complementary subspaces.

Since

Theorem 3.2

The space of infinitesimal deformations of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton

on a principal bundle

over a nearly

\displaystyle\Big(D^{-1,A}+2\ \mathrm{Id}\Big)\colon\Gamma(\Lambda^{1}\otimes Ad_{\mathcal{P}})\to\Gamma(\Lambda^{1}\otimes Ad_{\mathcal{P}}).

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{5t^{2}}{12}+\frac{3t}{2}-\frac{17}{4}\Big)\epsilon\cdot\eta-L_{A}(\epsilon)\cdot\eta.

then

is the only solution. Thus we get the following result.

Theorem 3.7
.

Any
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton

on a principal
G
-bundle over a compact nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold M is rigid if

(i)

the structure group

is abelian, or

(ii)

the eigenvalues of the operator

are either all greater than
-\frac{28}{5}
or all smaller than

.

Some immediate consequences of Theorem

are that the flat instantons are rigid. Also if all the eigenvalues of

are equal then

has to be rigid.

4.2 Infinitesimal deformations of the canonical connection

Let

be a homogeneous manifold. Consider the principal
H
-bundle
G\to M

(V,\rho)
is an
H
-representation then the space of smooth sections
\Gamma(G\times_{\rho}V)
of the associated vector bundle

is isomorphic to the space

of

defined by

For any connection

is denoted by

and

be the
G
-equivariant function given by
s(gH)=[g,f_{s}(g)]

\displaystyle(\nabla^{A}_{X}s)(gH)

\displaystyle=(g,X_{h}(f_{s})(g)).

For the canonical connection on

for every vector field. Thus the covariant derivative
\nabla^{can}
is given by

\displaystyle(\nabla^{can}_{X}s)(gH)

\displaystyle=(g,X(f_{s})(g)).

By the Peter–Weyl Theorem
[
36
]
*Theorem 1.12 the space of sections can also be formulated as follows. If we denote by
G_{irr}
the set of equivalence classes of irreducible
H
-representations then

\displaystyle\Gamma(G\times_{\rho}V)

\displaystyle=\overline{\underset{W\in{G_{irr}}}{\bigoplus}\mathrm{Hom}(W,V)_{H}\otimes W}.

The embedding
\textup{Hom}(W,V)_{H}\otimes W
into
C^{\infty}(G,V)_{H}=\Gamma(G\times_{\rho}V)
is given by sending
(\phi,w)
to the function

defined by

. Thus
(\phi,w)
defines a section
s_{(\phi,w)}(gH)=[g,f_{(\phi,w)}(g)]
which we denote by
(\phi,w)
as well.

Claim:
The left
G
-action is given by
g.f_{(\phi,w)}=f_{(\phi,\tau(g)w)}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in G
. Then since
g.f=f\circ l_{g^{-1}}
and
f_{(\phi,w)}(g)=\phi(\tau(g^{-1})w)
we have

\displaystyle(g.f_{(\phi,w)})(k)

\displaystyle=f_{(\phi,w)}(g^{-1}k)=\phi(\tau((g^{-1}k)^{-1})w)

\displaystyle=\phi(\tau(k^{-1})\tau(g)w)=f_{(\phi,\tau(g)w)}(k).

The proof of the claim is now complete.∎

We can compute the covariant derivative on
s_{(\phi,w)}\in\textup{Hom}(W,V)_{H}\otimes W\subset\Gamma(G\times_{\rho}V)
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{can}_{X}s_{(\phi,w)}(gH)

\displaystyle=X(f_{(\phi,w)})(g)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f(e^{tX}g)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(f_{(\phi,w)}\circ l_{e^{tX}})(g)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(e^{-tX}.f)(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f_{(\phi,\tau(e^{-tX})w)}=-f_{(\phi,\tau_{*}(X)w)}(gH).

The above can be written as

(4.1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{can}_{X}(\phi,w)=-(\phi,\tau_{*}(X)w).

Thus we get that for the canonical connection the covariant derivative of a section

with respect to

translates into the derivative

, which is minus the differential of the left-regular representation

In the following sections we implement the above procedure on each of the four homogeneous spaces.

Remark 4.3
.

In a nearly Kähler
6
-manifold whose structure is defined by a real Killing spinor
\eta
, the spinor
\textup{vol}\cdot\eta
is another independent real Killing spinor. Any Dirac operator
\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}
anti-commutes with the Clifford multiplication by
\vol
that is
\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}\textup{vol}=-\mathbf{\vol\cdot\mathcal{\not{D}}}
, hence for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}-\lambda\textup{id})\cong\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}+\lambda\textup{id})
. Therefore
\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}^{2}-\lambda^{2}\textup{id})\cong 2\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}\pm\lambda\textup{id})
and one can compute the
\lambda

\not{D}
by computing the
\lambda^{2}
eigenspace of
\not{D}^{2}
as done in
[
16
]
*Proposition 4. In the case of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifolds
\mathcal{\not{D}}
and the
7
-dimensional

commute and thus we do not have such an isomorphism between the
\pm\lambda
eigenspaces of the Dirac operator. In fact there is no such automatic relation between
\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}^{2}-\lambda^{2}\textup{id})
and
\ker(\mathbf{\mathcal{\not{D}}}+\lambda\textup{id})
as §
4.4
reveals.

Remark 4.4
.

The Dirac operator is always self-adjoint therefore the above method of finding a particular eigenspace of a Dirac operator
D
can be used more generally in any bundle associated to the spinor bundle over a homogeneous spin manifold. Often times it is easier to find the eigenspaces of the square of the Dirac operator

similar to the case in hand. Once we know the
\lambda^{2}
-eigenspace of
D^{2}
we can apply
D
on them to see which of them lie in the
\lambda
or
-\lambda
-eigenspace of
D

4.5 Integrability of the deformation spaces

We now describe some of the deformation spaces obtained in Theorem
4.6
.

Let
M
be a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold. We first observe that for the structure group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
the space of non-trivial deformations in Theorem
4.6
are either isomorphic to or contains as a subrepresentation one or multiple copies of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of the automorphism group
G
. A vector field
X
on
M
preserves the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
if
\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi=0
. We denote by
\mathcal{X}
the space of vector fields on
M
preserving the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.
Since the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
G/H
is
G
invariant, the space
\mathfrak{g}
is contained in
\mathcal{X}
. Note that if
X\in\mathcal{X}
then
\mathcal{L}_{X}\psi=\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0
.

Given a parallel section in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}(T^{*}M)\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
, one can define an operator that associates to each vector field in
\mathcal{X}
an infinitesimal deformation of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
instanton on
M
. Such an operator was defined in
[
16
]
where a similar situation arises when one computes the deformation space of the canonical connection on the homogeneous
6
-dimensional nearly Kähler manifolds.

The next proposition asserts that if we fix a section
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}(T^{*}M)\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
, then for any vector field
X\in\mathcal{X}
on
M
the
\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}
valued
1
-form
\epsilon_{X}=i_{X}\xi\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
defines an infinitesimal deformation of the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
instanton
A
in the sense of (
3.2
). The proof of the proposition follows verbatim from the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 9]
and is hence omitted.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
A
be an instanton on a principal
G
-bundle
\mathcal{P}
over a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
manifold
M
. Let
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}(T^{*}M)\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
such that
\nabla^{-1,A}\xi=0
. Then for any
X\in\mathcal{X}
,
\epsilon_{X}=i_{X}\xi\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\textup{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
satisfies the linearised instanton condition

\displaystyle d^{A}\epsilon_{X}\cdot\eta=0.

The above proposition implies that for each
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{2}(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
such that
\nabla^{-1,A}\xi=0
, there is a copy of
\mathfrak{g}
in the deformation space of
A
. Thus the multiplicity of
\mathfrak{g}
in the deformation space can be found by identifying the parallel sections of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}
. On
G/H
, when we see
\mathcal{P}
as a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-bundle, every parallel section of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}
corresponds to an
H
-invariant element of the
H
-representation
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}
(since
\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{2}
) and vice-versa. The number of linearly independent
H
-invariant elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is equal to the multiplicity of the trivial
H
-representation in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

Observe that since
A
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
instanton, the curvature
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{2}(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{P}})
. When
A=\nabla^{can}
is the canonical connection on
G/H
and
F
is the curvature,
\nabla^{-1,can}F=0
since
\mathrm{Hol}(\nabla^{can})\subseteq\mathrm{G}_{2}
. Hence by Proposition
4.7
for every
X\in\mathcal{X}
,
\epsilon_{X}=i_{X}F
defines an infinitesimal deformation of
A=\nabla^{can}
.
Using the Bianchi identity and the definition of
\epsilon_{X}
we have that

\displaystyle d^{A}\epsilon_{X}

\displaystyle=d\epsilon_{X}+[A,\epsilon_{X}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}F-i_{X}dF+[A,\epsilon_{X}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}F+i_{X}[A,F]+[A,\epsilon_{X}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}F+[i_{X}A,F].

Since under the action of a gauge transformation
\phi
, the curvature
F
transforms by
\phi F\phi^{-1}
, for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
there exists an infinitesimal gauge transformation
\phi_{X}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}F

\displaystyle=[\phi_{X},F].

Also
i_{X}A
defines an infinitesimal gauge transformation, hence
[\phi_{X}+i_{X}A,F]
is an action of an infinitesimal gauge transformation on
F
.
Thus for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
the deformations

arise from gauge transformations and hence do not descend to the moduli space.

Thus for finding the multiplicity of
\mathfrak{g}
in the deformation space (modulo gauge transformations) of the canonical connection on

apart from the one that corresponds to

of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}

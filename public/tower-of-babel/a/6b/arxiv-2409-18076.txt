On the existence and properties of solutions of the generalized Jang equation with respect to asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data
Benjamin Meco

Abstract
We provide a rigorous analysis of the generalized Jang equation in the asymptotically anti-de Sitter setting modelled on constant time slices of anti-de Sitter spacetimes in dimensions 3 ≤ n ≤ 7 3\leq n\leq 7 for a very general class of asymptotics. Potential applications to spacetime positive mass theorems for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets are discussed.

Introduction

In Mathematical General Relativity, initial data sets are triples
(M,g,K)
where
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold and
K
is a symmetric
2
-tensor. An initial data set
(M,g,K)
models a slice of a spacetime
(N,h)
such that the induced metric is
g
and the second fundamental is
K
. Roughly speaking, an initial data set can be described as

•

asymptotically Euclidean
if it is asymptotic to the
\{t=0\}
slice of Minkowski spacetime which may be seen as the initial data set
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\delta,0)
,

•

asymptotically hyperbolic
or
hyperboloidal
if it is asymptotic to the upper unit hyperboloid in Minkowski spacetime which may be seen as the initial data set
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b,b)
, where
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is hyperbolic space and
b
is the standard hyperbolic metric,

•

asymptotically anti-de Sitter
if it is asymptotic to the
\{t=0\}
slice of anti-de Sitter spacetime which may be seen as the initial data set
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b,0)
.

The mass of an initial data set
(M,g,K)
is defined by comparing it with a model initial data set
(M_{0},g_{0},K_{0})
seen as a slice of some model spacetime
(N_{0},h_{0})
. The expressions that have been derived using Hamiltonian formalism usually take form of a flux integral at infinity that is well-behaved under isometries of
(N_{0},h_{0})
. In all physically reasonable matter models, the so called
dominant energy condition
is satisfied and so it is standard to assume it when studying initial data sets. A general positive mass theorem may be stated as follows: ”If an initial data set
(M,g,K)
has well-defined mass and satisfies the dominant energy condition, then its mass is non-negative and is equal to zero if and only if
M
embeds in the model spacetime as a spacelike slice with the induced metric
g
and the second fundamental form of the embedding equal to
K
”.

The first result in this direction was obtained by Schoen and Yau
[
SY79
]
for asymptotically Euclidean
3
-dimensional initial data sets with
K\equiv 0
, the so called Riemannian case. Subsequently, this result was extended by Schoen and Yau in
[
SY81
]
to the case of general
3
-dimensional asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets. Around the same time, Witten
[
Wit81
]
proved a positive mass theorem for asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets in all dimensions under the assumption that the manifold be spin, which imposes a topological restriction in dimensions
n>3
. Many new results have recently been proven in the asymptotically Euclidean setting. In particular, the result of
[
SY81
]
has been extended to hold in dimensions
3<n\leq 7
, see Eichmair
[
Eic13
]
and Eichmair, Huang, Lee and Schoen
[
EHLS16
]
, using in particular methods from geometric measure theory. In addition, a positive mass theorem has been shown to hold for a more general class of asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets which may, in addition to the distinguished asymptotically Euclidean end, have other asymptotic ends, see Lesourd, Unger and Yau
[
LUY21
]
. There are also recent proofs by so-called level set methods. See for instance Bray, Khuri, Kazaras, and Stern
[
BKKS22
]
and Hirch, Kazaras, and Khuri
[
HKK22
]
using level sets of linearly growing harmonic functions and generalizations thereof. See also Agostiniani, Mazzieri and Oronzio
[
AMO24
]
for a proof in the Riemannian case using level sets of the Green function of the
manifold
(M,g)
.

The first definitions of mass in the asymptotically hyperbolic setting were given by Wang
[
Wan01
]
, for a class of conformally compact Riemannian manifolds satisfying rather stringent asymptotic conditions, and by Chruściel and Nagy
[
CN01
]
for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets. Some key properties of these definitions, in particular coordinate invariance, were established by Chruściel and Herzlich
[
CH03
]
. Proofs of related positive mass theorems were obtained, under spin assumption, by Wang
[
Wan01
]
, Chruściel and Herzlich
[
CH03
]
, and Zhang
[
Zha04
]
. The first non-spinor proof in the asymptotically hyperbolic setting was given by Andersson, Cai, and Galloway
[
ACG08
]
for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with asymptotics as in Wang
[
Wan01
]
satisfying an additional assumption that the so called
mass aspect function
has sign. This assumption has been recently removed by Chruściel, Galloway, Nguyen, and Paetz in
[
CGNP18
]
, yielding a non-spinor proof of the positive mass theorem for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds in dimensions less than
8
. We also note a reduction argument of Chruściel and Delay
[
CD19
]
that allows one to deduce the positive mass theorem for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds from the positive mass theorem for asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets in any dimension, provided that the later holds.

Positivity of mass of hyperboloidal initial data sets has also been actively studied. The definition of mass in this setting and the first spinor proof of the positive mass theorem can be found in Chruściel, Jezierski, and Łȩski
[
CJL04
]
. A non-spinor proof adapting the original argument of Schoen and Yau
[
SY81
]
to the hyperboloidal case was given in dimension
3
by Sakovich
[
Sak21
]
. This proof was extended to higher dimensions
3<n<8
by Lundberg
[
Lun23
]
using methods similar to those in Eichmair
[
Eic13
]
.

The proofs of the positive mass theorem for asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets in
[
SY81
]
and
[
Eic13
]
and for hyperboloidal initial data sets in
[
Sak21
]
and
[
Lun23
]
are based on the so-called
Jang equation reduction
argument. The Jang equation is a prescribed mean curvature equation for a graph in a product Riemannian manifold that first appeared in the work of Jang
[
Jan78
]
. Jang was the first to notice the relevance of this equation in the context of positive mass theorems, especially analysing the case of having zero mass, pointing out that the equation can be used to characterize initial data sets arising as slices of Minkowski spacetime. The Jang equation was rediscovered by Schoen and Yau in
[
SY81
]
, where it was observed that it can be used to deform initial data sets satisfying the dominant energy condition, to initial data sets with the same mass and ”almost nonnegative” scalar curvature. Further, it turns out that performing a conformal change to zero scalar curvature decreases the mass and yields a Riemannian asymptotically Euclidean manifold to which the Riemannian case of the positive mass theorem for asymptotically Euclidean manifolds can be applied, see
[
SY79
]
and
[
SY81
]
, yielding positivity of mass for the initial data that may be chosen to be asymptotically Euclidean as in
[
SY81
]
and
[
Eic13
]
or asymptotically hyperbolic as in
[
Sak21
]
and
[
Lun23
]
. When the mass is zero, the constructed solution to the Jang equation provides the desired embedding into the Minkowski spacetime.

It has been discussed (see e.g. Malec and O’Murchadha
[
MOM04
]
) whether a Jang equation reduction argument can be used to prove the so-called spacetime Penrose inequality, a refinement of the positive mass theorem for spacetimes with black holes. The version of this conjectured result for asymptotically Euclidean initial data sets argues that there is a certain lower bound for their mass that becomes an equality only for spacelike slices of Schwarzschild spacetime. As pointed out by Bray and Khuri in
[
BK10
]
, Schwarzschild spacetime is a warped product and so it appears more natural to modify the Jang equation so that the reduction is carried out in a warped product setting. One of the proposals of Bray and Khuri reduces the proof of the spacetime Penrose inequality to solving a coupled system consisting of the so-called generalized Jang equation and an equation for the warping factor designed so that initial data sets satisfying the dominant energy condition are deformed into asymptotically Euclidean manifolds with “almost nonnegative” scalar curvature. We note that in spherical symmetry the coupled system reduces to a single ODE yielding a proof of the spacetime Penrose inequality in the spherically symmetric case, see
[
BK10
]
for details.

Similar issues arise when attempting to apply the Jang equation reduction to anti-de Sitter initial data sets. In the case of zero mass the initial data set is expected to be embedable as a slice in anti-de Sitter spacetime, which is a warped product. Thus, it appears natural that reduction arguments allowing to prove the positive mass theorem for this class of initial data should involve a generalized Jang equation as well. One such argument, proposed by Cha and Khuri
[
CK18
]
in dimension 3, involves deforming the asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set to an asymptotically Euclidean initial data set in a several step process using both a coupled system involving a generalized Jang equation as in
[
BK10
]
and a classical Jang equation as studied in
[
Sak21
]
. Cha and Khuri
[
CK18
]
also study the behaviour of solutions of the generalized Jang equation at infinity in dimension
3
, for a fixed warping factor when the asymptotics of initial data are similar to those in
[
Wan01
]
.

This article may be seen as a complement to the work of Cha and Khuri
[
CK18
]
. Our main result is the proof of the existence of solutions to the generalized Jang equation for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data in dimensions
3\leq n\leq 7
for very general asymptotics, given a warping factor satisfying a general asymptotic condition. We make use of geometric measure theory tools as in Eichmair
[
Eic13
]
and a barrier method, different from the one used in Cha and Khuri
[
CK18
]
, based on the observation that the metric is ”almost conformal” to the Euclidean metric. We also propose an alternative reduction argument that can be used to deduce a positive mass theorem for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data from the positive mass theorem for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds provided that a coupled system involving the same generalized Jang equation as in
[
CK18
]
and
[
BK10
]
but a different equation for a warping factor, has a solution. The reduction thereby proceeds in one step and the proposed coupled system appears to be simpler and arguably more natural from a geometric point of view.

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
contains necessary background and definitions that we will use. In Section
3
we construct barriers for the generalized Jang equation for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets with the most general possible asymptotics and in any dimension
n\geq 3
. In Section
4
we apply standard methods (similar to those used in
[
Eic13
, Section
2
]
and
[
Sak21
, Section
4
]
) to solve a regularized boundary value problem associated to the generalized Jang equation. In Section
5
we apply the results of Sections
3
and
4
and methods of geometric measure theory developed in
[
Eic09
]
to construct a so-called
geometric
solution to the generalized Jang equation. This is the only part of our work where the dimension restrictions
3\leq n\leq 7
are required. In Section
6
we present our proposed coupled system and discuss its relevance in the context of positive mass theorems. We prove
section 6
, that the positive mass theorem is valid for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets, assuming the coupled system has a solution with certain properties.

(2.15)

where

q_{i}:=\frac{uf^{j}(A^{\Sigma}_{ij}-(\overline{K}|_{\Sigma})_{ij})}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}},\quad w:=\frac{uf^{i}\partial_{i}}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}},\quad f^{i}=f_{j}g^{ij}.

(2.16)

The difference between (
2.15
) and
[
BK10
, Equation
2.3
]
is that the definition of
\mu
in (
2.8
) is different, to account for the cosmological constant.

We will often assume that the warping factor
u
is strictly positive and that
u-\rho^{-1}\in C^{2,0}_{0}(M\setminus K_{0})
. In this case we have the following estimate.

{lemma}

Suppose that
k\geq 1
, that
(M,g,K)
is a
C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set and that
(u-\rho^{-1})\in C^{2,0}_{0}(M)
. Then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
(M,g,K)
,
|u-\rho^{-1}|_{C^{2,0}_{0}(M)}
and
\min_{M}u>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla u|_{g}+|\nabla^{2}u|_{g}\leq Cu.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{b}\rho^{-1}\right|_{b}=\frac{1+O(\rho)}{\rho},\quad|(\nabla^{b})^{2}\rho^{-1}|_{b}=\frac{2+O(\rho)}{\rho}.

The claim now follows from the assumptions on
(M,g,K)
,
u
and
u-\rho^{-1}
.
∎

3
Barriers at infinity for the Jang equation

In this section,
(M,g,K)
is a
C^{1,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\tau>n/2
. The barriers are defined as follows (cf. Schoen and Yau
[
SY81
]
).

{definition}

A function
f_{+}:\{\rho<\rho_{0}\}\to\mathbb{R}
is an upper barrier for the Jang equation
\mathcal{J}(f)=0
, if
f_{+}\in C^{2}(\{\rho<\rho_{0}\})\cap C^{0}(\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\})
and

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\rho_{0}-}\partial_{\rho}f_{+}=\infty,\quad\mathcal{J}(f_{+})<0.

(3.1)

Similarly, a function
f_{-}:\{\rho<\rho_{0}\}\to\mathbb{R}
is a lower barrier for the Jang equation if
f_{-}\in C^{2}(\{\rho<\rho_{0}\})\cap C^{0}(\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\})
and

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\rho_{0}-}\partial_{\rho}f_{-}=-\infty,\quad\mathcal{J}(f_{-})>0.

(3.2)

The following comparison principle is well known (cf. Schoen and Yau
[
SY81
]
and Sakovich
[
Sak21
, Proposition
4.4
]
).

{lemma}

Let
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{0}
be constants, suppose that
f_{+}
is an upper barrier and that
f_{-}
is a lower barrier for the Jang equation on
\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
such that

f_{-}|_{\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}}\leq\phi|_{\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}}\leq f_{+}|_{\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}}

and let
\epsilon>0
be such that
\mathcal{J}(f_{+})\leq\epsilon f_{+}
and
\mathcal{J}(f_{-})\geq\epsilon f_{-}
on
\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. Further suppose that
f
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{J}(f)=\epsilon f,&\text{on }\{\rho\geq\rho_{1}\}\\
f=\phi,&\text{on }\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}\end{cases}.

Then

f_{-}\leq f\leq f_{+}\text{ on }\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}.

Proof.

Since
\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
is compact it follows that
f_{+}-f
attains a minimum at some point
p\in\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. If
p\in\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}
then since
f_{+}\geq\phi
on
\{\rho=\rho_{1}\}
we find

\displaystyle\inf_{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}}(f_{+}-f)=f_{+}(p)-f(p)=f_{+}(p)-\phi(p)\geq 0.

If
p\in\{\rho_{1}<\rho<\rho_{0}\}
then
|d(f-f_{+})|_{g}(p)=0
and
\Hess(f_{+}-f)
is positive definite at
p
. Combining this with (
2.13
) we find that at
p

\displaystyle\epsilon(f_{+}-f)\geq\mathcal{J}(f_{+})-\mathcal{J}(f)=u\Delta^{g}(f_{+}-f)\geq 0.

Finally if
p\in\{\rho=\rho_{0}\}
, then since
\lim_{\rho\to\rho_{0}^{-}}\partial_{\rho}f_{+}=\infty
, it follows that
\lim_{\rho\to\rho_{0}^{-}}\partial_{\rho}(f_{+}-f)=\infty
and hence there is some point
p^{\prime}
with
\rho_{1}<\rho(p^{\prime})<\rho_{0}
such that
(f_{+}-f)(p^{\prime})<(f_{+}-f)(p)
, which contradicts
p
being a global minimum. Thus
f_{+}-f\geq 0
on all of
\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. A similar argument shows that
f-f_{-}\geq 0
on
\{\rho_{1}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
.
∎

{proposition}

[Existence of barriers for the Jang equation]

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tau\in(n/2,n)
and that
(M,g,K)
is a
C^{1,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set and let
U:=\{\rho<\rho_{0}\}
. If the warping factor
u
satisfies

u-\rho^{-1}\in C^{1,0}_{0}(U),

(3.3)

then there exists a positive function
f_{+}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(U)
such that
f_{+}
is an upper barrier in the sense of
section 3
and
f_{-}:=-f_{+}
is a lower barrier in the sense of
section 3
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we let
C
denote a constant that may differ from line to line but that may depend only on the initial data
(M,g,K)
and
u
. The bound
|K|_{g}\leq C\rho^{\tau}
gives

\mathcal{J}(f)=\left(g^{ij}-\frac{u^{2}f^{i}f^{j}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)\frac{u\Hess_{ij}f+f_{i}u_{j}+u_{i}f_{j}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{1/2}}+O(\rho^{\tau}).

Note that when
f=f(\rho)
, we have
f^{\alpha}=g^{\alpha\rho}f_{\rho}
,
f^{\rho}=g^{\rho\rho}f_{\rho}
,
|df|_{g}^{2}=f^{\rho}f_{\rho},
and hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}g^{\rho\rho}-\frac{u^{2}f^{\rho}f^{\rho}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}&=\frac{g^{\rho\rho}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}},\\
g^{\rho\alpha}-\frac{u^{2}f^{\rho}f^{\alpha}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}&=\frac{g^{\rho\alpha}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}},\\
g^{\alpha\beta}-\frac{u^{2}f^{\alpha}f^{\beta}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}&=g^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\rho\beta}(g^{\rho\rho})^{-1}\frac{u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly, for
f=f(\rho)
we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}u\Hess_{\rho\rho}f+f_{\rho}u_{\rho}+u_{\rho}f_{\rho}&=u\left(f^{\prime\prime}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\rho}f^{\prime}+\frac{2u_{\rho}}{u}f^{\prime}\right),\\
u\Hess_{\rho\alpha}f+f_{\rho}u_{\alpha}+u_{\rho}f_{\alpha}&=u\left(-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\alpha}+\frac{u_{\alpha}}{u}\right)f^{\prime},\\
u\Hess_{\alpha\beta}f+f_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+u_{\alpha}f_{\beta}&=-u\Gamma^{\rho}_{\alpha\beta}f^{\prime}.\end{split}

In summary, in the case when
f=f(\rho)
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(f)&=\frac{ug^{\rho\rho}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}}\left(f^{\prime\prime}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\rho}f^{\prime}+\frac{2u_{\rho}}{u}f^{\prime}\right)+\frac{2ug^{\rho\alpha}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}}\left(-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\alpha}+\frac{u_{\alpha}}{u}\right)f^{\prime}\\
&\quad-\frac{u\Gamma^{\rho}_{\alpha\beta}f^{\prime}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{1/2}}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\rho\beta}(g^{\rho\rho})^{-1}\frac{u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)+O(\rho^{\tau}).\end{split}

(3.4)

As observed by Malec and Murchadha
[
MOM04
]
, in the presence of spherical symmetry there is a substitution that transforms the Jang equation into an ordinary differential equation. We employ this fact here and set

f_{+}:=f_{+}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\xi_{+}(s)}{\sqrt{1-\xi_{+}(s)^{2}}}ds,

(3.5)

for some
\xi_{+}:[0,\rho_{0}]\to[0,1]
to be determined. We require that
\xi_{+}(\rho_{0})=1
, so that

\partial_{\rho}f_{+}(\rho)=f_{+}^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{\xi_{+}(\rho)}{\sqrt{1-\xi_{+}(\rho)^{2}}},

(3.6)

implies that the first condition of (
3.1
) is fulfilled by
f_{+}
. From (
3.5
) it follows that

f_{+}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\xi_{+}^{\prime}}{(1-\xi_{+}^{2})^{3/2}}.

(3.7)

In the view of the identities (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) we obtain from (
3.4
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathcal{J}(f_{+})(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})^{3/2}}{ug^{\rho\rho}}\\
&<\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\rho}\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})+\frac{2u_{\rho}}{u}\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})+\frac{2g^{\alpha\rho}}{g^{\rho\rho}}\left(-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\rho\alpha}+\frac{u_{\alpha}}{u}\right)\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})\\
&\quad-\frac{\Gamma^{\rho}_{\alpha\beta}(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})(1-\xi_{+}^{2})\xi_{+}}{g^{\rho\rho}}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\rho\beta}(g^{\rho\rho})^{-1}\frac{u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{C\rho^{\tau}(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})^{3/2}}{ug^{\rho\rho}}.\end{split}

(3.8)

Furthermore, the fact that
(M,g,K)
is
C^{1,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter, together with equations (
2.7
) and (
3.3
) implies the estimates

\displaystyle g^{\rho\rho}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}(1-2\rho)+O(\rho^{\tau+2}),

\displaystyle g^{\rho\alpha}

\displaystyle=O(\rho^{\tau+2}),

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho^{2}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{1-2\rho}+O(\rho^{\tau+2}),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho\rho}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\rho}\frac{1-3\rho}{1-2\rho}+O(\rho^{\tau-1}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho\alpha}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=O(\rho^{\tau-1}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\alpha\beta}(\rho^{-1}+O(1)),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}+O(1),

\displaystyle\frac{u_{\rho}}{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\rho}+O(1),

\displaystyle\frac{u_{\alpha}}{u}

\displaystyle=O(\rho).

As a consequence of (
3.9
) and (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\rho\beta}(g^{\rho\rho})^{-1}\frac{u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho^{2}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{1-2\rho}+O(\rho^{\tau+2})

(3.10)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})(1-\xi_{+}^{2})&=\left(1+\frac{u^{2}g^{\rho\rho}\xi_{+}^{2}}{1-\xi_{+}^{2}}\right)(1-\xi_{+}^{2})\\
&=1-\xi_{+}^{2}+\left(\rho^{-1}+O(1)\right)^{2}\left(\rho^{2}(1-2\rho)+O(\rho^{\tau+2})\right)\xi_{+}^{2}\\
&=1-\xi_{+}^{2}+(1+O(\rho))\xi_{+}^{2}\\
&=1+O(\rho).\end{split}

(3.11)

Combining (
3.8
) with the estimates (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{J}(f_{+})(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})^{3/2}}{ug^{\rho\rho}}

\displaystyle<\xi_{+}^{\prime}+\frac{1+O(\rho)}{\rho}\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})-\frac{2+O(\rho)}{\rho}\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})+2\left(O(\rho^{\tau-1})+O(\rho)\right)\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}(\rho^{-1}+O(1))(1+O(\rho))\xi_{+}}{\rho^{2}(1+O(\rho))}\bigg(\frac{\rho^{2}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{1+O(\rho)}+O(\rho^{\tau+2})\bigg)+O\left(\rho^{\tau-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{1+O(\rho)}{\rho}\xi_{+}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})-\frac{(1+O(\rho))\xi_{+}(n-1+O(\rho))}{\rho}+O(\rho^{\tau-1}),

which in turn yields the inequality

\frac{\mathcal{J}(f_{+})(1+u^{2}|df_{+}|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}(1-\xi_{+}^{2})^{3/2}}{ug^{\rho\rho}}<\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{+}^{2}-C_{0}\rho)+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1},

for some fixed constant
C_{0}>0
that only depends on
(M,g,K)
and
u
.

We now seek a function
\xi_{+}:[0,\rho_{0}]\to[0,1]
such that
\xi_{+}(\rho_{0})=1
and

\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{+}^{2}-C_{0}\rho)+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}<0,

(3.12)

so that
f_{+}
defined by (
3.5
) is an upper barrier in the sense of
section 3
. For some
\lambda\in(0,1)
to be specified later, we will look for
\xi_{+}
in the form

\xi_{+}(\rho)=\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{n/2}+\lambda(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{\tau}}.

(3.13)

Since
\rho_{0}/\rho\geq 1
and
n/2<\tau
we have
(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{n/2}\leq(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{\tau}
and hence for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
we have
(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{n/2}\leq(1-\lambda)(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{n/2}+\lambda(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{\tau}\leq(\rho_{0}/\rho)^{\tau}
. Consequently

(\rho/\rho_{0})^{\tau}\leq\xi_{+}\leq(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n/2}.

(3.14)

Defining

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle:=\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\lambda(\tau-n/2)}{\lambda+(1-\lambda)(\rho/\rho_{0})^{\tau-n/2}},

(3.15)

we obtain
\xi_{+}^{\prime}=\gamma\xi_{+}\rho^{-1}
. We conclude that for
\xi_{+}
as in (
3.13
)

\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{+}^{2}-C_{0}\rho)+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}<-(n-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}-C_{0}\rho)\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}.

(3.16)

Next let
\rho_{0}>0
be such that
C_{0}\rho_{0}+C_{0}\rho_{0}^{\tau}<(n-\tau)/4
, then we have

\displaystyle n-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}-C_{0}\rho_{0}-C_{0}\rho_{0}^{\tau}>\frac{3n+\tau}{4}-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}.

(3.17)

Let
F:[0,1]\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

F(x,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda(\tau-n/2)}{\lambda+(1-\lambda)x^{\tau-n/2}},

(3.18)

so that
0\leq F(x,\lambda)\leq\tau-n/2
. Let
x_{0}:=(3(n-\tau)/8)^{1/n}>0
and note that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}F(x_{0},\lambda)=0
. Thus we may choose
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
F(x_{0},\lambda)<1/8
. Now for any
x\in[0,x_{0}]

\displaystyle\frac{n+\tau}{4}-F(x,\lambda)-x^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n+\tau}{4}-(\tau-n/2)-x_{0}^{n}=\frac{3(n-\tau)}{4}-\frac{3(n-\tau)}{8}>0.

Since
\tau>n/2
, the function
F(x,\lambda)
is decreasing in
x
, hence for
x\in[x_{0},1]
we have

\displaystyle F(x,\lambda)+x^{n}\leq F(x_{0},\lambda)+1<\frac{9}{8}=\frac{3+3/2}{4}\leq\frac{n+\tau}{4}.

In summary, for this choice of
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

F(x,\lambda)+x^{n}<\frac{n+\tau}{4},

(3.19)

for all
x\in[0,1]
. Moreover in view of (
3.15
) and (
3.18
) we find
\gamma=n/2+F(\rho/\rho_{0},\lambda)
, which combined with (
3.17
) and (
3.19
) implies

\displaystyle n-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}-C_{0}\rho_{0}-C_{0}\rho_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle>\frac{3n+\tau}{4}-\frac{n}{2}-\bigg(F(\rho/\rho_{0},\lambda)+(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}\bigg)

(3.20)

\displaystyle>\frac{3n+\tau}{4}-\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n+\tau}{4}=0.

Equation (
3.20
) implies
n-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}-C_{0}\rho>C_{0}\rho_{0}^{\tau}
which combined with (
3.14
) and (
3.16
) yields

\displaystyle\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{+}^{2}-C_{0}\rho)+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}

\displaystyle<-(n-\gamma-(\rho/\rho_{0})^{n}-C_{0}\rho)\frac{\rho^{\tau-1}}{\rho_{0}^{\tau}}+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}

\displaystyle<C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}-C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}=0,

which we wanted to show. In conclusion, the function
f_{+}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\xi_{+}(s)}{\sqrt{1-\xi_{+}(s)^{2}}}ds
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\rho_{0}^{-}}\partial_{\rho}f_{+}=\infty,\quad\mathcal{J}(f_{+})<0,\quad f_{+}>0,

and due to the bound

\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{0}/2}^{\rho_{0}}|f_{+}^{\prime}(s)|ds<\int_{\rho_{0}/2}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\xi_{+}(s)^{2}}}ds<C\int_{0}^{C}x^{-1/2}dx=2C^{3/2}<\infty,

it follows that
f_{+}
extends continuously to the boundary
\{\rho=\rho_{0}\}
of
\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. Hence
f_{+}
is indeed an upper barrier of the generalized Jang equation in the sense of
section 3
. Finally a computation shows that
\xi_{+}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
and hence
f_{+}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau+1}
.

If we now let
\xi_{-}:=-\xi_{+}
, then
f_{-}=-f_{+}
. By construction,
f_{-}<0
, it satisfies the first condition of (
3.2
) and we have
f_{-}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau+1}
. The second condition of (
3.2
) is also satisfied since

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{J}(f_{-})(1+u^{2}|df_{-}|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}(1-\xi_{-}^{2})^{3/2}}{ug^{\rho\rho}}

\displaystyle>\xi_{-}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{-}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{-}^{2}-\epsilon)-C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}

\displaystyle=-\left(\xi_{+}^{\prime}-\frac{\xi_{+}}{\rho}(n-\xi_{+}^{2}-\epsilon)+C_{0}\rho^{\tau-1}\right)>0,

where in the last line we have used that
\xi_{+}
satisfies (
3.12
).
∎

4
A regularized boundary value problem

In this section we will prove the following proposition.

{proposition}

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\tau>0
are real numbers, that
(M,g,K)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set and that
u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(M)
satisfies

\displaystyle u-\rho^{-1}\in C^{2,0}_{0}(M).

Suppose furthermore that
\Omega\subset M
is a bounded domain such that
\partial\Omega
is
C^{3,\alpha}
and that the mean curvature
H_{\partial\Omega}
of
\partial\Omega
computed as the tangential divergence of the outward pointing unit normal satisfies

H_{\partial\Omega}-|\tr_{\partial\Omega}K|>0.

Lastly suppose that
\epsilon>0
and
\phi\in C^{3,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, if either
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small or
\phi\equiv 0
then there exists
f\in C^{3,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(g^{ij}-\frac{u^{2}f^{i}f^{j}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{u\Hess_{ij}(f)+u_{i}f_{j}+f_{i}u_{j}}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}}-K_{ij}\right)}_{=\mathcal{J}(f)}

\displaystyle=\epsilon f,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,

(4.1)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\phi,

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega.

(4.2)

We note that similar results have been obtained in Eichmair
[
Eic09
, Lemma
2.2
]
, Eichmair
[
Eic13
, Proposition
5
]
, Andersson, Eichmair and Metzger
[
AEM11
, Theorem
3.1
]
and Sakovich
[
Sak21
, Lemma
4.2
]
, see also references therein. To prove Proposition
4
we will apply the continuity method to the related boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left(g^{ij}-\frac{u^{2}f^{i}f^{j}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{u\Hess_{ij}(f)+u_{i}f_{j}+f_{i}u_{j}}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}}-sK_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon f,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,

(4.3)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=s\phi,

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega,

(4.4)

for
s\in[0,1]
.

4.1
A-priori estimates

{proposition}

Suppose that
f_{s}\in C^{3,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (
4.3
)-(
4.4
). Then there is a constant
C
depending on
(M,g,K)
,
\epsilon>0
,
\Omega
,
\phi
,
|u|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}
,
\inf_{M}u
and
\alpha
, but independent of
s\in[0,1]
, such that

|f_{s}|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, the constant
C>0
may change from line to line but depends only on
(M,g,K)
,
\epsilon>0
,
\Omega
,
\phi
,
|u|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}
,
\inf_{M}u
and
\alpha
, and is independent of
s\in[0,1]
. For simplicity of notation, we will write
f
instead of
f_{s}
. The proof proceeds in the following steps.

Global
C^{0}
estimate.
Suppose that
f
attains its maximum at an interior point
p\in\Omega
. Then
\nabla f(p)=0
and
\Hess f(p)
is negative semi-definite. Hence (
4.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\epsilon f(p)=g^{ij}\left(u\Hess_{ij}(f)-sK_{ij}\right)=u\Delta^{g}f-s\tr_{g}K\leq|\tr_{g}K|.

We get a similar inequality at an interior minimum point. Consequently,

\displaystyle|f_{s}|\leq\max\left\{\epsilon^{-1}\sup_{\Omega}|\tr_{g}(K)|,\sup_{\partial\Omega}|\phi|\right\}\leq C\quad\text{on $\overline{\Omega}$},

(4.5)

where
C
depends only on
(M,g,K)
,
\epsilon>0
and
\phi
.

Interior
C^{1}
estimate.
We set
v:=|df|_{g}^{2}
. Let
p\in\Omega
be an interior maximum point for
v
. Then

\displaystyle v_{i}=2f^{j}\Hess_{ij}f=0,

(4.6)

at
p
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Further, we note that

&=\nu^{i}F_{p_{j}}\nabla_{i}\nu^{j}+\nu^{i}(F_{x^{i}}-F_{p_{j}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{im}^{j}\nu^{m})+F_{t}|df|_{g}^{2}\langle\partial_{t},-\nu\rangle.\end{split}

(5.4)

The last two terms on the right hand side of (

and

. As a consequence of these inequalities, (

A computation using the identity

shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(uw)_{i}&=\frac{u_{i}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{u^{2}u_{i}|df|_{g}^{2}+u^{3}f^{k}\nabla_{i}f_{k}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}}\\
&=\frac{u_{i}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{1/2}}\left(1-\frac{u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)-\frac{u^{3}f^{k}\nabla_{i}f_{k}}{(1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2})^{3/2}}\\
&=w^{3}u_{i}-u^{3}w^{3}f^{k}\nabla_{i}f_{k}.\end{split}

Further, (
5.3

5.6

\displaystyle\quad uw\nu^{i}\nabla_{i}\nu^{j}

\displaystyle=u^{2}w^{2}f^{i}\left(uw\nabla_{i}f^{j}-(uw)^{3}f^{j}f^{k}\nabla_{i}f_{k}+w^{3}f^{j}u_{i}\right)

by (
5.3
) and (
5.6

\displaystyle=u^{3}w^{3}f^{i}\left(g^{jk}-u^{2}w^{2}f^{j}f^{k}\right)\nabla_{i}f_{k}+u^{2}w^{5}f^{i}f^{j}u_{i}

\displaystyle=u^{3}w^{3}f^{i}\overline{g}^{jk}+u^{2}w^{5}f^{i}f^{j}u_{i}

by
\overline{g}^{ij}=g^{ij}-u^{2}w^{2}f^{i}f^{j}

\displaystyle=\overline{g}^{jk}(-(uw)_{k}+w^{3}u_{k})+u^{2}w^{5}f^{i}f^{j}u_{i}

by (
5.6
)

\displaystyle=-\overline{g}^{ij}(uw)_{i}+\overline{g}^{ij}w^{3}u_{i}+u^{2}w^{2}f^{i}f^{j}w^{3}u_{i}

\displaystyle=-\overline{g}^{ij}(uw)_{i}+g^{jk}w^{3}u_{k}

by
\overline{g}^{ij}=g^{ij}-u^{2}w^{2}f^{i}f^{j}
.

After multiplying the equality resulting from the above calculation by
F_{p_{j}}
we have

uw\nu^{i}F_{p_{j}}\nabla_{i}\nu^{j}=-\overline{g}^{ij}(uw)_{i}F_{p_{j}}+g^{jk}w^{3}F_{p_{j}}u_{k}.

(5.7)

Due to

we have
|du|_{g},|\Hess u|_{g}\leq Cu
for some constant
C
depending on
(M,g,K)
and
u

. These bounds imply

and combining this with (
5.5

5.7

-uw\nu(H)\leq\overline{g}^{ij}(uw)_{i}F_{p_{j}}+Cuw.

(5.8)

Consequently, (
5.1

\Delta^{\overline{g}}(uw)=(\underbrace{-\widetilde{\Ric}(\nu,\nu)}_{\leq C}\underbrace{-|A|_{\overline{g}}^{2}}_{\leq 0}-\nu(H))uw\leq Cuw-uw\nu(H),

which combined with (
5.8
) yields, after rearrangement,

\Delta^{\overline{g}}w\leq\left(C+\overline{g}^{ij}F_{p_{j}}\frac{u_{i}}{u}-\frac{\Delta^{\overline{g}}u}{u}\right)w+\overline{g}^{ji}\left(F_{p_{j}}-\frac{2u_{j}}{u}\right)w_{i}.

(5.9)

Recalling that

, equation (

\left|\frac{\Delta^{\overline{g}}u}{u}\right|=\left|\frac{\Delta u}{u}-\frac{u(\Hess u)(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}+\frac{|du|_{g}^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}-\frac{\langle df,du\rangle H_{\Gamma(f)}}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}}\right|\leq C,

hence

We may now conclude by (

where

. Note that

which together with the bound

yields

This in turn implies

and (
5.2

|f|_{C^{k,\alpha}(B_{r}(x))}\leq C\rho^{\tau+1}
, for all
B_{r}(x)\subset M\setminus K_{0}
with
r<R
and
C=C(M,g,K,R)
, establishing (
5.20
).

Since
\Gamma(f)=\partial E
is boundaryless it follows that
|f(x)|\to\infty
as
x\to\partial U
. Thus we have

\partial U=\partial^{+}U\cup\partial^{-}U\quad\text{ where }\quad\partial U^{\pm}:=\{x_{0}\in\partial U:\lim_{x\to x_{0}}f(x)=\pm\infty\}.

Clearly, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the mean curvature of the graph
\Gamma(f-t)\subset M\times_{u}\mathbb{R}
is independent of
t
and is given by

\displaystyle F(x,X):=\tr_{\widetilde{g}}K-K(X,X)+\frac{\langle X,\nabla u\rangle}{u}\left(1-\langle-X,u^{-1}\partial_{t}\rangle^{2}\right).

Letting
t\to\infty
and applying
Appendix B
again, up to passing to a subsequence, we obtain a
C^{3,\alpha}
surface
G=\partial W
in the limit, where
W\subset M\times_{u}\mathbb{R}
is open. The convergence of the indicator functions in
Appendix B
implies
W=\pi_{M}(W)\times\mathbb{R}
. By construction we must have
\partial^{+}U=\partial(\pi_{M}(W))
and hence
G=\partial^{+}U\times\mathbb{R}
. The cylinder
G
is
C^{3,\alpha}
and hence so is
\partial^{+}U
. Letting
t\to-\infty
, we conclude that
\partial^{-}U
is
C^{3,\alpha}
as well.

We will now show that
\Gamma(f)\cap\{|t|>T\}
is a graph over
\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}
for
T
large enough. To this end, let
N
be a
C^{1}
unit normal vector field defined in a neighborhood
\Omega
of
\partial^{+}U\subset M
and extend
N
to all of
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
by requiring that
N_{(x,t)}=N_{(x,t+s)}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose on the contrary that for any
T>0
, the set
\Gamma(f)\cap\{t>T\}
cannot be written as a graph over
\partial^{+}U\times\mathbb{R}
. In this case, there is a sequence of points
\{(x_{l},f(x_{l}))\}\subset\Gamma
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\text{dist}(x_{l},\partial^{+}U)=0,\quad\lim_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})=\infty,\quad\langle\nu,N\rangle_{g}|_{(x_{l},f(x_{l}))}=0.

Passing to a subsequence we can assume that
x_{l}\to x_{0}\in\partial^{+}U
and that the submanifolds
\Gamma(f_{l}-f(x_{l}))\cap\{-1<t<1\}

C^{3,\alpha}
converge to
\partial^{+}U\times(-1,1)
. Let
\nu^{+}
denote the unit normal of
\partial^{+}U
pointing out of
W
, we then find

\displaystyle\pm 1=\langle\nu^{+},N\rangle|_{(x_{0},0)}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle\nu,N\rangle|_{(x_{l},f(x_{l}))}=\lim_{l\to\infty}0=0,

a contradiction. Repeating the same argument for
t\to-\infty
, we conclude that for some
T>0
,
\Gamma(f)\cap\{t>|T|\}
is a graph over
\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lastly we compute the mean curvature of
\partial^{\pm}U\subset M
, the tangential divergence of the normal
\nu
pointing out of
U
. In the view of the above argument,
Appendix B
implies
H^{\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}}=\tr_{\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}}\overline{K}
. At the same time, we note that (
2.12
) and (
2.2
) yield

\displaystyle\tr_{\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}}\overline{K}

\displaystyle=\tr_{\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}}K+\frac{\langle\nu,\nabla u\rangle}{u}\left(1-\langle-\nu,u^{-1}\partial_{t}\rangle^{2}\right)=\tr_{\partial^{\pm}U}K+\frac{\langle\nu,\nabla u\rangle}{u},

\displaystyle H^{\partial^{\pm}U\times\mathbb{R}}-(\pm H^{\partial^{\pm}U})

\displaystyle=-g^{tt}\langle\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\nu,\partial_{t}\rangle=u^{-2}\langle\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{t}\rangle=u^{-2}\langle\nu,u\nabla u\rangle=\frac{\langle\nu,\nabla u\rangle}{u},

and hence
\pm H^{\partial^{\pm}U}=\tr_{\partial^{\pm}U}K
.
∎

{remark}

In the proof of the above proposition we have not discussed the asymptotics of the asymptotically cylindrical ends. These asymptotics remain the same no matter which initial data we use, be it asymptotically hyperboloidal, asymptotically Euclidean or asymptotically anti-de Sitter. The reader is referred to Metzger
[
Met10
]
, Han and Khuri
[
HK13
]
and Yu
[
Yu19
]
for results in this direction that all apply in our case, under some additional assumptions on the set
\partial^{\pm}U
and/or the warping factor
u
in a neighborhood of
\partial^{\pm}U
.

6
A Jang equation reduction argument for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets

In this section we discuss applications of the generalized Jang equation in the context of the positive mass theorem for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets. We start by recalling the definition of mass that is relevant here, see Michel
[
Mic11
, Section
4.3
]
and Chruściel and Herzlich
[
CH03
]
.

{definition}

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tau>n/2
, that
(M,g)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic manifold (possibly with boundary) as in
section 2
such that

\frac{\Scal^{g}+n(n-1)}{\rho}\in L^{1}(M,g).

(6.1)

Letting
\{x^{i}\}
be the restrictions of Cartesian coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
S^{n-1}
we define

\mathcal{N}:=\operatorname{span}\left\{V_{0}:=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho},\quad V_{1}:=\frac{x^{1}}{\rho},\quad\dots\quad V_{n}:=\frac{x^{n}}{\rho}\right\}.

(6.2)

Defining
e:=g-b
and
c_{n}^{-1}=2\omega_{n-1}(n-1)
, then for
V\in\mathcal{N}
we have

\M_{g}(V)=c_{n}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}[V(\div^{b}e-d(\tr_{b}e))-e(\nabla^{b}V,\cdot)+(\tr_{b}e)dV](\nu)d\mu^{b},

(6.3)

is a well defined linear map called the
mass functional
of
(M,g)
.

Recall that in Section
5
we constructed a geometric solution to the Jang equation as a submanifold of the warped product
M\times_{u}\mathbb{R}
with prescribed mean curvature. We also noted that this geometric solution has a
C^{3,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic component
\Gamma(f)
, which is given as the graph of a function
f\in C^{3,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(U)
defined outside of a compact subset of
M
.

{theorem}

[Mass change and Jang reduction]

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tau>n/2
, that
(M,g,K)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set with
g
satisfying (
6.1
) and that
u-\rho^{-1}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{0}(M)
. Suppose further that
f\in C^{3,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(U)
is a solution to the Jang equation on an open set
U\subset M
such that
M\setminus U
is compact and
\partial U
is
C^{3,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}
. Then the mass functional of
(\Gamma(f),\overline{g}=g+u^{2}df^{2})
is well defined and coincides with the one of
(M,g)
.

Proof.

The identity (
2.15
) implies

\Scal^{\overline{g}}+n(n-1)=2(\mu-J(w))+|A-\overline{K}|_{\Gamma(f)}|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+2|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}-\frac{2}{u}\div_{\overline{g}}(uq).

That
(\Scal^{g}+n(n-1))\rho^{-1}\in L^{1}(M,g)
,
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(U)
,
K\in C^{k-1,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
and the identities (
2.8
), (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and (
2.16
) combined with the above imply

\frac{\Scal^{\overline{g}}+n(n-1)}{\rho}\in L^{1}(M,\overline{g}).

Moreover we note that
\overline{g}-b=u^{2}df^{2}+g-b
where
u^{2}df^{2}\in C^{k,\alpha}_{2\tau}(U)
and
g-b\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(U)
. It follows that
(\Gamma(f),g+u^{2}df^{2})
is a
C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic manifold satisfying (
6.1
) and so its mass functional is well defined. That the mass functionals of
(\Gamma(f),g+u^{2}df^{2})
and
(M,g)
agree follows from a straightforward computation using (
6.3
).
∎

Next, we study how the mass functional changes under conformal changes of the metric. In what follows we let

\kappa:=\frac{4}{n-2}.

(6.4)

.

{proposition}

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tau>n/2
, that
(M,g)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic manifold satisfying (
6.1
). Let
\theta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
and
\widehat{g}:=e^{\kappa\theta}g
be such that

(\Scal^{\widehat{g}}+n(n+1))\rho^{-1}\in L^{1}(M,\widehat{g}).

Then

\M_{\widehat{g}}(V)-\M_{g}(V)=\frac{\kappa}{2\omega_{n-1}}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}\bigg(\theta dV-Vd\theta\bigg)(\nu)d\mu^{b}.

(6.5)

Proof.

Noting that

\widehat{e}:=\widehat{g}-b=\widehat{g}-g+g-b=(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)g+g-b,

we see that
(M,\widehat{g})
is a
C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic manifold as well and so the mass functional of
(M,\widehat{g})
is well defined. From (
6.3
) and the definition of
\widehat{g}
it follows that

\M_{\widehat{g}}(V)-\M_{g}(V)=c_{n}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}[V(\div^{b}\widetilde{e}-d\tr^{b}\widetilde{e})+(\tr^{b}\widetilde{e})dV-\widetilde{e}(\nabla^{b}V,\cdot)](\nu)d\mu^{b},

(6.6)

for
V\in\mathcal{N}
and
\widetilde{e}:=(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)g
. Recalling that
e=g-b
, we can also check that

\displaystyle V(\div^{b}\widetilde{e})(\nu)

\displaystyle=\kappa e^{\kappa\theta}Ve(\nabla^{b}\theta,\nu)+\kappa e^{\kappa\theta}Vd\theta(\nu)+(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)V\div^{b}e(\nu),

\displaystyle-V(d\tr^{b}\widetilde{e})(\nu)

\displaystyle=-V(n+\tr^{b}e)\kappa e^{\kappa\theta}d\theta(\nu)-V(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)(d\tr^{b}e)(\nu),

\displaystyle(\tr^{b}\widetilde{e})dV(\nu)

\displaystyle=(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)\tr^{b}(e)dV(\nu)+n(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)dV(\nu),

\displaystyle-\widetilde{e}(\nabla^{b}V,\nu)

\displaystyle=-(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)e(\nabla^{b}V,\nu)-(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)dV(\nu).

These identities together with (
6.6
) imply

\displaystyle\M_{\widehat{g}}(V)-\M_{g}(V)

\displaystyle=c_{n}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}\bigg((e^{\kappa\theta}-1)[V(\div^{b}e-d\tr^{b}e)+(\tr^{b}e)dV-e(\nabla^{b}V,\cdot)](\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa e^{\kappa\theta}V[e(\nabla^{b}\theta,\nu)-(\tr^{b}e)d\theta(\nu)]+(n-1)[(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)dV-\kappa Ve^{\kappa\theta}d\theta](\nu)\bigg)d\mu^{b}.

Recalling that
|\theta|+|\nabla^{b}\theta|_{b}\leq C\rho^{\tau}
,
|e|_{b}+|\nabla^{b}e|_{b}\leq C\rho^{\tau}
, we obtain

\M_{\widehat{g}}(V)-\M_{g}(V)=(n-1)c_{n}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}\bigg((e^{\kappa\theta}-1)dV-\kappa Ve^{\kappa\theta}d\theta\bigg)(\nu)d\mu^{b}.

The estimates
e^{\kappa\theta}-1=\kappa\theta+O(\rho^{2\tau})=O(\rho^{\tau})
and
d\theta(\nu)=O(\rho^{\tau})
now imply (
6.5
).
∎

We conclude this section with a theorem that highlights a potential application of the results of this paper in the context of the positive mass conjecture for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets.

{theorem}

[]

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tau>n/2
, that
(M,g,K)
is a complete
C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition and that
(M,g)
satisfies (
6.1
). Suppose furthermore that there exists functions
f\in C^{3,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(M)
and
u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(M)
such that
u
is positive,
u-V_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\tau-1}(M)
and
(f,u)
is an entire solution (i.e. defined on all of
M
) to the coupled system

\begin{cases}\mathcal{J}(f)&=0,\\
\Delta^{\overline{g}}u&=nu,\end{cases}

(6.7)

where
\overline{g}=g+u^{2}df^{2}
. Then the energy momentum vector of
(M,g,K)
is future causal,

\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})\geq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{i})^{2}}.

If
\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})=0
, then
(M,g)
embeds as a spacelike graphical slice into the anti-de Sitter spacetime with second fundamental form
K
.

Proof.

Suppose the assumptions of the theorem are satisfied. By Gicquaud
[
Gic10
, Theorem
3.3
]
there is a function
\theta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
satisfying the Yamabe equation

-\kappa(n-1)\left(|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+\Delta^{\overline{g}}\theta\right)+\Scal^{\overline{g}}=-n(n-1)e^{\kappa\theta},

(6.8)

so that
(M,\widehat{g}=e^{\kappa\theta}\overline{g})
is a
C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}
asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with
\Scal^{\widehat{g}}\equiv-n(n-1)
. Thus
(M,\widehat{g})
has a well defined mass functional.
section 6
applied with
\overline{g}
in the place of
g
combined with the estimates
|\theta dV_{0}-V_{0}d\theta|_{b}=O(\rho^{\tau-1})
and
|d\mu^{b}-d\mu^{\overline{g}}|=O(\rho^{\tau})
implies:

\delta\mathcal{M}:=\frac{2\omega_{n-1}}{\kappa}[\M_{\widehat{g}}(V_{0})-\M_{\overline{g}}(V_{0})]=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}\bigg(\theta dV_{0}-V_{0}d\theta\bigg)(\overline{\nu})d\mu^{\overline{g}}.

Let
\eta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(M)
be any function such that
\eta-1\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
. Since
u-V_{0}\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau-1}(M)
and
\theta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
we have

\displaystyle\theta dV_{0}-V_{0}d\theta

\displaystyle=\theta\underbrace{d(V_{0}-u)}_{\in C^{k-1,\alpha}_{\tau-1}(M)}-\underbrace{(V_{0}-u)}_{\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau-1}(M)}\theta+(1-\eta^{2})\cdot\underbrace{(\theta du-ud\theta)}_{\in C^{k-1,\alpha}_{\tau-1}(M)}+\eta^{2}(\theta du-ud\theta)

\displaystyle=(\eta\theta)d(\eta u)-(\eta u)d(\eta\theta)+O(\rho^{2\tau-1}).

As a consequence, using the divergence theorem, we can rewrite
\delta\mathcal{M}
as

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}[(\eta\theta)d(\eta u)-(\eta u)d(\eta\theta)](\overline{\nu})d\mu^{\overline{g}}=\int_{M}\eta\theta\Delta^{\overline{g}}(\eta u)-\eta u\Delta^{\overline{g}}(\eta\theta)dV^{\overline{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\bigg(\eta^{2}(\theta\Delta^{\overline{g}}u-u\Delta^{\overline{g}}\theta)+\langle d(\eta^{2}),\theta du-ud\theta\rangle\bigg)dV^{\overline{g}}.

Next we note the following inequality which follows from the generalized Schoen and Yau identity and the dominant energy condition, see (
2.15
) and
section 2
:

-\Scal^{\overline{g}}\leq n(n-1)-2|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+\frac{2}{u}\div_{\overline{g}}(uq).

Combining the above with (
6.8
) we obtain

-\Delta^{\overline{g}}\theta=|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}-\frac{ne^{\kappa\theta}}{\kappa}-\frac{\Scal^{\overline{g}}}{\kappa(n-1)}\leq|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}-\frac{n}{\kappa}(e^{\kappa\theta}-1)-\frac{2\left(|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}-\frac{1}{u}\div_{\overline{g}}(uq)\right)}{\kappa(n-1)}.

(6.9)

In the view of the assumption
\Delta^{\overline{g}}u=nu
and the inequality (
6.9
) we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}n\eta^{2}u\left(\theta-\frac{e^{\kappa\theta}-1}{\kappa}\right)dV^{\overline{g}}-\frac{2}{\kappa(n-1)}\int_{M}\eta^{2}(u|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}-\div_{\overline{g}}(uq))dV^{\overline{g}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\bigg(\eta^{2}u|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+\langle d(\eta^{2}),\theta du-ud\theta\rangle_{\overline{g}}\bigg)dV^{\overline{g}}.

Since
1+x\leq e^{x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the first integral above is non-positive. Furthermore, the divergence theorem implies

\int_{M}\eta^{2}\div_{\overline{g}}(uq)dV^{\overline{g}}=-\int_{M}uq(\overline{\nabla}\eta^{2})dV^{\overline{g}}.

Note that there are no boundary terms in the above formula since (
2.16
), (
2.4
) and the assumptions
u-\rho^{-1}\in C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(M)
,
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}_{\tau+1}(M)
and
K\in C^{k-1,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
together imply
|u\eta^{2}q(\overline{\nu})|=O(\rho^{2\tau-1})=o(\rho^{n-1})
. In conclusion, we have the upper bound

\delta\mathcal{M}\leq\int_{M}\bigg(\eta^{2}u|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+\langle d(\eta^{2}),\theta du-ud\theta\rangle_{\overline{g}}\bigg)-\frac{2}{\kappa(n-1)}\bigg(\eta^{2}u|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+uq(\overline{\nabla}\eta^{2})\bigg)dV^{\overline{g}}.

We now let
\eta=e^{2\theta}
, then

\delta\mathcal{M}\leq\int_{M}\bigg(ue^{4\theta}|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+4e^{4\theta}\langle d\theta,\theta du-ud\theta\rangle_{\overline{g}}\bigg)-\frac{2ue^{4\theta}}{\kappa(n-1)}\bigg(|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+4q(\overline{\nabla}\theta)\bigg)dV^{\overline{g}}.

(6.10)

In turn, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies:
|4q(\overline{\nabla}\theta)|\leq|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+4|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}
, hence

\int_{M}\frac{-2ue^{4\theta}}{\kappa(n-1)}\bigg(u|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+4q(\overline{\nabla}\theta)\bigg)dV^{\overline{g}}\leq\int_{M}\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}ue^{4\theta}|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}dV^{\overline{g}}.

(6.11)

Moreover, we have

4e^{4\theta}\langle d\theta,\theta du-ud\theta\rangle_{\overline{g}}=4\theta e^{4\theta}\langle d\theta,du\rangle_{\overline{g}}-4ue^{4\theta}|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}.

(6.12)

Combining (
6.10
), (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) we obtain

\delta\mathcal{M}\leq\int_{M}ue^{4\theta}|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}\underbrace{\left(1-4+\frac{2(n-2)}{(n-1)}\right)}_{<0}dV^{\overline{g}}+\int_{M}4\theta e^{4\theta}\langle d\theta,du\rangle_{\overline{g}}dV^{\overline{g}}.

(6.13)

The first integral on the right hand side is non-positive. As for the second integral, we note that the function
\gamma(x):=xe^{4x}-e^{4x}/4+1/4
satisfies

\lim_{x\to-\infty}\gamma(x)=\frac{1}{4},\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\gamma(x)=\infty,\quad\gamma^{\prime}(x)=4xe^{4x},\quad\gamma(0)=0.

It is easy to check that this implies that
\gamma(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and that
\gamma(x)=O(x^{2})
for small
x
. Hence
\gamma(\theta)=O(\rho^{2\tau})
and consequently, the divergence theorem implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\theta e^{4\theta}\langle d\theta,du\rangle_{\overline{g}}dV^{\overline{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle d(\gamma(\theta)),du\rangle_{\overline{g}}dV^{\overline{g}}-\int_{M}\gamma(\theta)(\Delta^{\overline{g}}u)dV^{\overline{g}}

(6.14)

\displaystyle=-n\int_{M}u\gamma(\theta)dV^{\overline{g}}\leq 0.

Again, there are no boundary terms appearing when applying the divergence theorem due to the estimate
|\gamma(\theta)du|_{\overline{g}}=O(\rho^{2\tau-1})=o(\rho^{n-1})
. It follows that
\delta\mathcal{M}\leq 0
and hence
\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{g}}(V_{0})\leq\mathcal{M}_{\overline{g}}(V_{0})=\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})
. An application of the standard positive mass theorem for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, see e.g.
[
CG21
, Theorem
1
]
, to
(M,\widehat{g})
gives
\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{g}}(V_{0})\geq 0
and hence
\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})\geq 0
as well.

Next suppose that
E:=\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})^{2}<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{i})^{2}
and define the vector

P:=(\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{n})),

so that
E^{2}<|P|^{2}
. Since every Lorentz boost (see
[
CDG16
, Section 2]
for details)
q:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
is an isometry, we find that for any chart at infinity
\Psi:M\setminus K_{0}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{R_{0}}
, the map
\Psi^{\prime}=q\circ\Psi
is another a chart at infinity, after replacing
K_{0}
with a larger compact set if necessary. If
q
is a Lorentz boost by the angle
\eta
in the direction
P
, then since the mass functional changes equivariantly under isometries of the background manifold
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
(see e.g.
[
Mic11
]
), we have

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{g}(V_{0})=\frac{E-\eta|P|}{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}},

where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{g}
is the mass functional with respect to the chart
\Psi^{\prime}
. In particular, choosing
\eta\in(|E|/|P|,1)
gives
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{g}(V_{0})<0
, a contradiction. Thus we have
\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{i})^{2}
as desired.

Lastly suppose that
\mathcal{M}_{g}(V_{0})=0
, in which case we must have
\delta\mathcal{M}=0
. By the calculation in
(
6.14
)
, the second term in the right hand side of (
6.13
) is non-positive and so

0=\delta\mathcal{M}\leq\int_{M}ue^{4\theta}|d\theta|_{\overline{g}}^{2}\underbrace{\left(1-4+\frac{2(n-2)}{(n-1)}\right)}_{<0}dV^{\overline{g}}\leq 0.

This combined with
\theta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}(M)
implies that
d\theta\equiv 0
so that
\theta\equiv 0
and
\Scal^{\overline{g}}\equiv-n(n-1)
, which combined with
[
HJM20
, Theorem
3
]
implies that
(M,\overline{g})
is isometric to the hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
.

The equality
\Scal^{\overline{g}}\equiv-n(n-1)
combined with the generalized Schoen-Yau identity (see (
2.15
)) and the dominant energy condition implies

u|A-\overline{K}|_{\overline{g}}^{2}\leq 2u(\mu-J(w))+u|A-\overline{K}|_{\overline{g}}^{2}=-2u|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+2\div_{\overline{g}}(uq).

(6.15)

Integrating over
\{|x|\leq\rho\}
and letting
\rho\to 0
, we may argue as before to find

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{M}u|A-\overline{K}|_{\overline{g}}^{2}dV^{\overline{g}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}-u|q|_{\overline{g}}^{2}+\div_{\overline{g}}(uq)dV^{\overline{g}}\leq\int_{M}\div_{\overline{g}}(uq)dV^{\overline{g}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\{|x|=\rho\}}uq(\overline{\nu})d\mu^{\overline{g}}=0.

Since

. The calculations in

embeds into the warped product space-time

with second fundamental form

it follows that

is the anti-de Sitter spacetime. In summary,

can be embedded as a space-like graphical slice of anti de-Sitter space-time with second fundamental form

∎

{remark}

{proposition}

Let

\overline{f},\underline{f}\in C^{2}(U\cap\Omega)\cap C^{1}(U\cap\overline{\Omega})
be such that

\displaystyle H_{g}(\overline{f})-s\tr_{g}(K)(\overline{f})-\epsilon\overline{f}

\displaystyle<0,

\displaystyle H_{g}(\underline{f})-s\tr_{g}(K)(\underline{f})-\epsilon\underline{f}

\displaystyle>0,

in

and
\underline{f}=\overline{f}=s\phi
on
\partial\Omega
. If

is a solution to (
4.3
)-(
4.4
) and satisfies

\underline{f}\leq f\leq\overline{f}\quad\text{ on }\quad\partial U\cap\Omega,

then we have the bound

\sup_{\partial\Omega}|df|_{g}\leq\max(|d\underline{f}|_{g},|d\overline{f}|_{g}).

Proof.

Since
\overline{\Omega\cap U}
is compact,
\overline{f}-f
attains its minimum at some point
p
in
\overline{\Omega\cap U}
. If

f=\overline{f},\text{ on $\partial\Omega$}\quad\text{ and }\quad f\leq\overline{f},\text{ on $\partial U\cap\Omega$},

it follows that
f(p)\leq\overline{f}(p)
. If
p
lies in the interior of
\Omega\cap U
, then at

\nabla(\overline{f}-f)=0,\quad\Hess(\overline{f}-f)\text{ is non-negative definite at $p$}.

Hence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle>H_{g}(\overline{f})-s\tr_{g}(K)(\overline{f})-\epsilon\overline{f}-H_{g}(f)+s\tr_{g}(K)(f)+\epsilon f

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(g^{ij}-\frac{u^{2}f^{i}f^{j}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{u\Hess_{ij}\overline{f}-u\Hess_{ij}f}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}}\right)}_{\geq 0}+\underbrace{s\left(g^{ij}-\frac{u^{2}f^{i}f^{j}}{1+u^{2}|df|_{g}^{2}}\right)(K_{ij}-K_{ij})}_{=0}

\displaystyle+\epsilon(f-\overline{f})\geq\epsilon(f-\overline{f}),

at
p
. It follows that:
\overline{f}(p)\geq f(p)
. Hence
\overline{f}\geq f
in

{definition}

Suppose that
n\geq 1
and

are integers,
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+l}
is an open set,
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+l}
is an oriented and embedded

-dimensional

and

for some
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}
measurable subset

of locally finite perimeter in

.

We say that

is

-almost minimizing in

if for any open set

and any integer multiplicity current

M_{W}(T)\leq M_{W}(T+\partial X)+CM_{W}(X).

(B.1)

In the next lemma we show that a graph
\Gamma=\{(x,f(x)),x\in\Omega\}\subset(M\times\mathbb{R},h)
of bounded mean curvature

has the

-almost minimizing property whenever the coordinate vector field

along the

factor is a Killing vector field of the metric

. This holds in particular for the aforementioned warped product metric

, but also for metrics of the form:

where

is a function and

[

and
[

]
and Cha, Khuri and Sakovich

{lemma}

Let
(M\times\mathbb{R},h)
be an oriented Riemannian manifold with

and let

be the coordinate on
\mathbb{R}

\partial_{t}
is a Killing vector field for the metric

\Omega\subset M
is an open set and that

is a

function whose graph
\Gamma(f)=\{(x,f(x)):x\in\Omega\}\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
has uniformly bounded mean curvature
|H_{\Gamma(f)}|\leq C
.

For any
C^{2}
isometry
\psi:(M\times\mathbb{R},h)\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{n+l},\delta)
and any open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+l}
such that
\psi(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})=\psi(M\times\mathbb{R})\cap U
, the current
[\![\psi(\Gamma(f))]\!]\in D_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n+l})
is

-almost minimizing in
\psi(M\times\mathbb{R})\cap U

Proof.

We begin by defining a one parameter family of maps
\varphi:(M\times\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(x,t,s)=(x,t+s)\quad\text{for $x\in M$ and $t,s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

The map
\varphi
is the flow generated by the vector field
\partial_{t}
and since
\partial_{t}
is a Killing vector field of

we conclude that each map
\varphi_{s}(x,t)=\varphi(x,t,s)
is an isometry of
(M\times\mathbb{R},h)
. Letting

be the downward pointing unit normal to
\Gamma(f)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}

defined by

\nu(x,t+s)=d\varphi_{s}(\nu(x,t))\quad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $t,s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(B.2)

This extension implies that
\nu(x,f(x)+s)
is the downward pointing unit normal of the vertically shifted graph
\Gamma(f+s)
. To see this, let
\gamma:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to M\times\mathbb{R}
be a curve satisfying
\gamma(\tau)\in\Gamma(f+s)
for all
\tau\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. We want to show that
\langle\gamma^{\prime}(0),\nu\rangle_{h}=0
. By definition we have

\varphi_{s}^{-1}(\gamma(\tau))\in\Gamma(f)\quad\text{for all $\tau\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)$}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{\prime}(0),\nu(x,f(x)+s)\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle d\varphi_{s}^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime}(0)),d\varphi_{s}^{-1}(\nu(x,f(x)+s))\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle(\varphi_{s}^{-1}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(0),\nu(x,f(x))\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=0,

where we used the fact that
\varphi_{s}
is an isometry in the first line and (
B.2
) and the chain rule in the second line. The last line follows from the fact that
\nu
is normal to
\Gamma(f)
and that
(\varphi_{s}^{-1}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(0)\in T_{(x,f(x))}\Gamma(f)
. A similar computation shows that

\langle\partial_{t},\nu\rangle_{h}\bigg|_{(x,f(x)+s)}=\langle\partial_{t},\nu\rangle_{h}\bigg|_{(x,f(x))}<0\quad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}$},

which shows that
\nu(x,t)
always points downwards. Furthermore, we note that the mean curvature of each shifted graph
\Gamma(f+s)
is the same as that of
\Gamma(f)
, namely

H_{\Gamma(f+s)}(x,f(x)+s)=H_{\Gamma(f)}(x,f(x))\quad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

To see this, fix

and let

be an orthonormal frame of
\Gamma(f)
at

. We extend each

to

by setting

(B.3)

and we set

. Denoting

and

\displaystyle H_{\Gamma(f+s)}(p_{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}(p_{s}),(\nabla^{h}_{e_{i}(p_{s})}\nu)(p_{s})\rangle_{h}

by definition

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle d\varphi_{s}(e_{i}(p)),(\nabla^{h}_{d\varphi_{s}(e_{i}(p))}d\varphi_{s}(\nu))(p_{s})\rangle_{h}

by (

) and (

)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle d\varphi_{s}(e_{i}(p)),d\varphi_{s}(\nabla^{h}_{e_{i}(p)}\nu)(p_{s})\rangle_{h}

since
\varphi_{s}
is an isometry

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}(p),(\nabla^{h}_{e_{i}(p)}\nu)(p)\rangle_{h}

since
\varphi_{s}
is an isometry

\displaystyle=H_{\Gamma(f)}(p)

by definition.

We can thus extend

to a function
H:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

H(x,f(x)+s)=H_{\Gamma(f+s)}(x,f(x)+s)=H_{\Gamma(f)}(x,f(x))\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

The proof of the fact that

. For all

,

\displaystyle\leq 1

\displaystyle d\sigma=(\div\nu)dV^{h}

\displaystyle=HdV^{h}

We now set

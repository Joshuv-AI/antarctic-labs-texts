Scattering For three waves Nonlinear Schrödinger System with mass-resonance in 5D
Fanfei Meng, Sheng Wang, Chengbin Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the dynamics behavior of the ( NLS system ) with three waves interaction in the energy space H 1 ​ ( ℝ 5 ) × H 1 ​ ( ℝ 5 ) × H 1 ​ ( ℝ 5 ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5}) . Inspired by B. Dodson and J. Murphy in [ 9 ] , we establish an interaction Morawetz estimate for the ( NLS system ), together with the criterion which proved by Tao-Dodson–Murphy we can get the scattering under the ground state in energy space with mass-resonance. Under the radial assumption, we can remove the mass-resonance condition. Key words: nonlinear Schrödinger system, scattering theory, interaction Morawetz estimate.



1.
Introduction

Considering the Cauchy problem for the quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\rm u}+A\boldsymbol{\rm u}=\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\boldsymbol{\rm u}(0,x)=\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(
NLS system
)

where
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
,
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}
, and
\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
are all vector-valued complex functions with three components defined as follow:

\boldsymbol{\rm u}:=\left(\begin{aligned} u^{1}\\
u^{2}\\
u^{3}\end{aligned}\right),~\boldsymbol{\rm u_{0}}:=\left(\begin{aligned} u^{1}_{0}\\
u^{2}_{0}\\
u^{3}_{0}\end{aligned}\right),

(1.1)

and
A
is a
3\times 3
matrix,
\boldsymbol{\rm f}:\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
as follow

A=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{1}\Delta&0&0\\
0&\kappa_{2}\Delta&0\\
0&0&\kappa_{3}\Delta\end{pmatrix},~\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):=\left(\begin{aligned} -\overline{u^{2}}u^{3}\\
-\overline{u^{1}}u^{3}\\
-u^{1}u^{2}\end{aligned}\right),

(1.2)

where
u^{i}
:
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are unknown functions,
\kappa_{i}\in\left(0,\infty\right)
are real number
\left(i=1,2,3\right)
.

In terms of physics, the quadratic nonlinearity of (
NLS system
) arises from a model in the nonlinear optics describing the second harmonic generation and Raman amplification phenomenon in plasma, this process is a nonlinear instability phenomenon (see
[
4
]
for more detail).
In fact, (
NLS system
) is the form of infinite dimensional Hamilton if we treat
\mathbb{C}\sim\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\rm u}=S_{6}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\overline{\boldsymbol{\rm u}}},

(
IDH
)

where
S_{6}
is the standard
6\times 6
symplectic matrix and
H=H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
is the Hamiltion with

H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2}\|u^{i}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2}-\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{u^{1}}\overline{u^{2}}u^{3}{\rm d}x.

Thus, by Emmy Noether’s theorem, the solutions to (
NLS system
) conserve the
mass
,
energy
and
momentum
, corresponding to the transfomations of rotation of phase, translation of time and translation of space respectively, defined as follows

\displaystyle M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u^{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\equiv M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=K(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-V(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\equiv E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{u^{1}}\nabla u^{1}+\overline{u^{2}}\nabla u^{2}+\overline{u^{3}}\nabla u^{3}\right){\rm d}x\equiv P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

where

\displaystyle(\text{kinetic energy})\quad

\displaystyle K(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}\|u^{1}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2}\|u^{2}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{3}}{2}\|u^{3}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle(\text{potential energy})\quad

\displaystyle V(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{u^{1}}\overline{u^{2}}u^{3}{\rm d}x.

The equation (
NLS system
) also shares the scaling invariance

\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{\rm u}\left(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x\right)

for
\lambda>0
.
If we set
{{\dot{\rm H}}^{s}}:={\dot{H}}^{s}\times{\dot{H}}^{s}\times{\dot{H}}^{s}
, then the critical regularity of Sobolev space is
{\rm\dot{H}}^{s_{c}}
,
i.e.
\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\lambda}\|_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}\|_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
where
s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-2
.
Therefore, the equation (
NLS system
) is called mass-subcritical if
d\leqslant 3
, mass-critical if
d=4
, energy-subcritical if
d=5
, and energy-critical if
d=6
.

Now, we review some basic facts about the nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\mu|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

The case
\mu=1
is the defocusing case, while
\mu=-1
gives the focusing case.
For the defoucsing case, the potential energy has the same sign as the kinetic energy; while for the focusing case, the contray is the case. In contrast to the classical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1
), the (
NLS system
) do not have classification of the so-called focusing and defocusing, since we can not figure out the sign of potential energy.

Both defocusing case (
\lambda>0
) and focusing case (
\lambda<0
) of (
1
) have been studied in a large amount of literature,
such as
[

,

,

,

by J.Bourgain, T. Tao, B. Dodson, T. Duyckaerts, R. Killip, C. Miao, M. Visan and so on.

Similarly to the classical (
1
), we can define the Galilean transformation to the (
NLS system
) as follows:

\left(\begin{aligned} u^{1}(t,x)\\
u^{2}(t,x)\\
u^{3}(t,x)\end{aligned}\right)\to\left(\begin{aligned} e^{i\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\kappa_{1}}}e^{-it\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\kappa_{1}}}u^{1}(t,x-2t\xi)\\
e^{i\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\kappa_{2}}}e^{-it\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\kappa_{2}}}u^{2}(t,x-2t\xi)\\
e^{i\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\kappa_{3}}}e^{-it\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\kappa_{3}}}u^{3}(t,x-2t\xi)\end{aligned}\right).

It is easy to check that the (
NLS system
) is invariant under Galiean transformation only if the coefficient satisfies

\frac{1}{\kappa_{3}}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}+\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}.

(1.3)

So we call the (
1.3
) is the mass-resonance condition.

In this article, we consider energy-subcritical case (d=5) of (
NLS system
) under the mass-resonance condition (
1.3
).
By solution, we mean a function
\boldsymbol{\rm u}\in C_{t}(I,{\rm H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))
on an interval
I\ni 0
satisfying the Duhamel formula

\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(s)){\rm d}s

for
t\in I
,
where we denote Schrödinger flow
\mathcal{S}(t)\boldsymbol{\rm w}=:\left(e^{\kappa_{1}it\Delta}w^{1},e^{\kappa_{2}it\Delta}w^{2},e^{\kappa_{3}it\Delta}w^{3}\right)^{T}
for any
\boldsymbol{\rm w}=:(w^{1},w^{2},w^{3})^{T}
.
In order to study the well-posedness of (
NLS system
), we need Strichartz estimates.
By using the standard contraction mapping theorem, we can show:
\exists~\delta_{0}>0
such that, if

\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{6}(I,{\rm L}_{x}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}<\delta_{0},

then there exists a unique global solution
\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)=(u^{1}(t),u^{2}(t),u^{3}(t))^{T}
to (
NLS system
).
For the large initial data, we have solution
\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)
with a maximal interval of existence
I_{\max}=(T_{-}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}),T_{+}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}))
.
A global solution
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
“scatters”, i.e.
there exists
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\pm}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\pm}\right\|_{{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}=0.

Definition 1.1

(Trivial scattering solution,
[
19
]
)
.

We say a solution to a nonlinear equation is a
trivial scattering solution
if it also solves the corresponding linear equation.
(In other words, the solution which does not have nonlinear interaction.)

Remark 1.2
.

For (
1
), zero is the only trivial scattering solution.
While for (
NLS system
),
(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{T}=(0,0,e^{\kappa_{3}it\Delta}u^{3}_{0})
is a trivial scattering solution for any
u^{3}_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
.

The (
NLS system
) admits a global but nonscattering solution

(u^{1}(t,x),u^{2}(t,x),u^{3}(t,x))^{T}=(e^{it}\phi_{1}(x),e^{it}\phi_{2}(x),e^{2it}\phi_{3}(x))^{T},

where
\boldsymbol{\rm Q}:=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}\in\Xi
is a non-negative radial solution to the elliptic system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi_{1}-\kappa_{1}\Delta\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}\phi_{3},\\
&\phi_{2}-\kappa_{2}\Delta\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}\phi_{3},\\
&2\phi_{3}-\kappa_{3}\Delta\phi_{3}=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\end{aligned}\right.\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{5},

(1.4)

where
\Xi:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\rm G}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})~\big|~V(\boldsymbol{\rm G})\neq 0\right\}
.

We call
\boldsymbol{\rm Q}:=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}
the “
ground state
”.
In
[
13
]
, M. Hamano determined the global behavior of the solutions to the system with data below the ground state and proved a blowing-up result if the data had finite variance or was radial.

Our main result in this paper is follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

For the (
NLS system
) under the mass-resonance condition (
1.3
).
If the initial data
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
satisfies

M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})<M(\boldsymbol{\rm Q})E(\boldsymbol{\rm Q})
and
M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})K(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})\leqslant M(\boldsymbol{\rm Q})K(\boldsymbol{\rm Q})
,
then the solution of (
NLS system
) is global and scatters in
{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
.

M. Hamano in
[
13
]
originally studied the (
NLS system
) with two waves interaction under mass-resonance, through the use of concentration compactness by Kenig-Merle in
[

]
. Later, using concentration compactness again, M. Hamano, T. Inui and K. Nishimura in

studied the scattering for (

Remark 1.4
.

In fact, the results of Theorem
1.3
essentially holds for any
\kappa_{i}>0,(i=1,2,3)
if the initial data

is radial. Without mass-resonance (

Under this circumstance, if the (

Thus, we only need to use the simpler Morawetz estimate instead of Proposition

Our proof of Theorem

consists of two steps:
firstly, we establish a scattering criterion as follows, using the method from B. Dodson, J. Murphy
[
8
]
and T. Tao
[
26
]
.
Secondly, in order to verify the condition of the above criterion,

).

Proposition 1.5

(Interaction Morawetz estimate)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0},\boldsymbol{\rm Q},I
be as in Theorem
4.1
, and suppose further that

M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})=E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})=E_{0}.

(1.5)

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm u}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{5}\to\mathbb{C}
be the corresponding global solution to (
NLS system
).
Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for
R_{0}=R_{0}(\delta,M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}),\boldsymbol{\rm Q})
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{JT_{0}}\int_{I}\int_{R_{0}}^{R_{0}e^{J}}\frac{1}{R^{5}}\iiint L^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\chi^{2}\left(\frac{x-s}{R}\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{y-s}{R}\right)N_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\frac{{\rm d}R}{R}{\rm d}t\lesssim\nu,

with
\nu=\frac{R_{0}e^{J}}{JT_{0}}+{\varepsilon}
, where
0<{\varepsilon}<1,J\geqslant{\varepsilon}^{-1}
are both constant and

L^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}=\kappa_{1}|\nabla u^{1,\xi}(x)|^{2}+\kappa_{2}|\nabla u^{2,\xi}(x)|^{2}+\kappa_{3}|\nabla u^{3,\xi}|^{2}
,
N_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}=\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}|u^{1}(y)|^{2}+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}|u^{2}(y)|^{2}

+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}|u^{3}(y)|^{2}
,

\chi\in C^{\infty}
is a radial decreasing function satisfying

\chi(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1&\quad\quad|x|\leqslant 1-{\varepsilon},\\
0&\quad\quad|x|>1,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

and
\boldsymbol{\rm u}^{\xi}=(e^{\frac{ix\cdot xi}{\kappa_{1}}}u^{1},e^{\frac{ix\cdot xi}{\kappa_{2}}}u^{2},e^{\frac{ix\cdot xi}{\kappa_{3}}}u^{3})
with

\xi=-\frac{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\Im\left(\overline{u^{1}(x)}\nabla u^{1}+\overline{u^{2}(x)}\nabla u^{2}+\overline{u^{3}(x)}\nabla u^{3}\right)\chi^{2}\left(\frac{x-s}{R}\right){\rm d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\left(\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}|u^{1}(x)|^{2}+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}|u^{2}(x)|^{2}+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}|u^{3}|^{2}\right)\chi^{2}\left(\frac{x-s}{R}\right){\rm d}x}

(1.7)

(unless the denominator is zero, in which case
\xi=\xi(t,s,R)=\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
).

Combining the two steps, that are respectively formulated as above, we can obtain Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.6
.

For

3.
Variational characterization

In this section, we are in the position to prove the variational characterization for the sharp Gargliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
Firstly, we will show the existence of the ground state.
As a consequence, we can obtain the sharp Gargliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
And we use the properties of the ground state to establish the coercivity condition which will be used in the proof of Morawetz estimate in the following section.

First, we study the existence of ground state following the argument of Weinstein in
[

[

Proposition 3.1

(Existence of ground state)
.

The minimal
J_{\min}
of the nonnegative funtional

J(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):=(M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}))^{\frac{1}{2}}(K(\boldsymbol{\rm u}))^{\frac{5}{2}}(V(\boldsymbol{\rm u}))^{-2},\qquad\boldsymbol{\rm u}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}

are attained at
{\boldsymbol{\rm u}}=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{T}

whose expression has to be in the form of
(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{T}\\
=(e^{i\theta_{1}}m\phi_{1}(nx),e^{i\theta_{2}}m\phi_{2}(nx),e^{i\theta_{3}}m\phi_{3}(nx))^{T}
,
where
m,n>0
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\boldsymbol{\rm Q}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}\neq{\boldsymbol{0}}
is the non-negative radial solution of the elliptic equation system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi_{1}-\kappa_{1}\Delta\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}\phi_{3},\\
&\phi_{2}-\kappa_{2}\Delta\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}\phi_{3},\\
&2\phi_{3}-\kappa_{3}\Delta\phi_{3}=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\end{aligned}\right.\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{5}.

where the
{\boldsymbol{\rm Q}}
is the
ground state
with
J({\boldsymbol{\rm Q}})=J_{\min}
. The sets of all ground states are denoted as
\mathcal{G}
.
All ground states share the same mass, denoted as
M_{gs}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the non-zero function sequence
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}
is the minimal sequence of the functional
J
, i.e.

\lim_{n\to\infty}J(\mathbf{u}_{n})=\inf\{J(\mathbf{u}):\mathbf{u}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\}.

By the Schwartz symmetrical rearrangement lemma, without loss of generality, we assume
\mathbf{u}_{n}
are non-negative radial.

Step.1
Note that for any
\mathbf{u}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\},\mu,\nu>0
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &M(\mu\mathbf{u}(\nu\cdot))=\mu^{2}\nu^{-5}M(\mathbf{u}),\\
&K(\mu\mathbf{u}(\nu\cdot))=\mu^{2}\nu^{2-5}K(\mathbf{u}),\\
&V(\mu\mathbf{u}(\nu\cdot))=\mu^{3}\nu^{-5}V(\mathbf{u}).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

By the defination of
J(\mathbf{u})
, it is easy to see
J(\mu\mathbf{u}(\nu\cdot))=J(\mathbf{u})
. Denote

\mathbf{v}_{n}(x)=\frac{[M(\mathbf{u}_{n})]^{\frac{5-2}{4}}}{[K(\mathbf{u}_{n})]^{\frac{5}{4}}}\mathbf{u}_{n}\left(\left(\frac{M(\mathbf{u}_{n})}{K(\mathbf{u}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}x\right).

On one hand, the mass and kinetic energy of
\mathbf{v}_{n}
have been unitized, i.e.

M(\mathbf{v}_{n})=K(\mathbf{v}_{n})\equiv 1.

On the other hand,
\mathbf{v}_{n}
also retain non-negative radial at the same tine and satisfies

J(\mathbf{v}_{n})=J(\mathbf{u}_{n})
, which implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}J(\mathbf{v}_{n})=\inf\{J(\mathbf{u}):\mathbf{u}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\}.

We note that
\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
is bounded and the embedding
H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\to L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
is compact,
then there exists a subsequence
\{\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\star}\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm rad}
, such that as
k\to\infty
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{v}^{\star}
in
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
and
\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}}\to\mathbf{v}^{\star}
in
{\rm L}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
.

By thw weak low semi-continuity of the functional
M
and
K
, we have

M(\mathbf{v}^{\star}),K(\mathbf{v}^{\star})\leqslant 1.

Then using triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}}\|_{{\rm L}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}^{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\|\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}}-\mathbf{v}^{\star}\|_{{\rm L}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}+\|\mathbf{v}^{\star}\|_{{\rm L}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\|\mathbf{v}^{\star}\|_{{\rm L}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}^{3}=V(\mathbf{v}^{\star}).

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle J(\mathbf{v}^{\star})=

\displaystyle[M(\mathbf{v}^{\star})][K(\mathbf{v}^{\star})]^{5}[V(\mathbf{v}^{\star})]^{-4}\leqslant\lim_{k\to\infty}[V(\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}})]^{-4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}J(\mathbf{v}_{n_{k}})=\inf\{J(\mathbf{u}):\mathbf{u}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\},

which shows that the minimal of
J
attains at
\mathbf{v}^{\star}
.

Step 2.
We consider the variational derivatives of
M,K,V
: fix
\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{T}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
, for any
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{T}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\right|_{h=0}M(\mathbf{u}+h\mathbf{w})=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}u^{1}\overline{w_{1}}{\rm d}x+\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}u^{2}\overline{w_{2}}{\rm d}x+\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}2u^{3}\overline{w_{3}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(u^{1},u^{2},2u^{3})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\right|_{h=0}K(\mathbf{u}+h\mathbf{w})=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}-\kappa_{1}\Delta u^{1}\overline{w_{1}}{\rm d}x+\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}-\kappa_{2}\Delta u^{2}\overline{w_{2}}{\rm d}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}-\kappa_{3}\Delta u^{3}\overline{w_{3}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(-\kappa_{1}\Delta u^{1},-\kappa_{2}\Delta u^{2},-\kappa_{3}\Delta u^{3})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\right|_{h=0}V(\mathbf{u}+h\mathbf{w})=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\bar{u^{2}}u^{3}\overline{w_{1}}{\rm d}x+\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\bar{u}^{1}u^{3}\overline{w_{2}}{\rm d}x+Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}u^{1}u^{2}\overline{w_{3}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(\bar{u}^{2}u^{3},\bar{u}^{1}u^{3},u^{1}u^{2})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x.

(3.5)

If the functional
J
attains the minimum at
\mathbf{W}=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})^{T}
, then we have for any
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{T}\in{\rm H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
,

\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\right|_{h=0}J(\mathbf{W}+h\mathbf{w})=0.

It means that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle[M(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},2\Phi_{3})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+5[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(-\kappa_{1}\Delta\Phi_{1},-\kappa 2\Delta\Phi_{2},-\kappa_{3}\Delta\Phi_{3})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-4[V(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}(\Phi_{2}\Phi_{3},\Phi_{1}\Phi_{3},\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2})\cdot\overline{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}{\rm d}x.

By the arbitrariness of
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{T}
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[M(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Phi_{1}-5[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\kappa_{1}\Delta\Phi_{1}-4[V(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Phi_{2}\Phi_{3}=0,\\
&[M(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Phi_{2}-5[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\kappa_{2}\Delta\Phi_{2}-4[V(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Phi_{1}\Phi_{3}=0,\\
&[M(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}2\Phi_{3}-5[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\kappa_{3}\Delta\Phi_{3}-4[V(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]^{-1}\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The above is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha\Phi_{1}-\kappa_{1}\Delta\Phi_{1}=\beta\Phi_{2}\Phi_{3},\\
&\alpha\Phi_{2}-\kappa_{2}\Delta\Phi_{2}=\beta\Phi_{1}\Phi_{3},\\
&\alpha\Phi_{3}-\kappa_{3}\Delta\Phi_{3}=\beta\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}\end{aligned}\right.

where

\alpha=\frac{[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]}{5[M(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]},\beta=\frac{4[K(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]}{5[V(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})]}.

Set
\mathbf{W}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha}x)
i.e.
(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))=(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\phi_{1}(\sqrt{\alpha}x),\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\phi_{2}(\sqrt{\alpha}x),\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\phi_{3}(\sqrt{\alpha}x))
, we obtain that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi_{1}-\kappa_{1}\Delta\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}\phi_{3},\\
&\phi_{2}-\kappa_{2}\Delta\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}\phi_{3},\\
&2\phi_{3}-\kappa_{3}\Delta\phi_{3}=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

The non-negative radial solutions of the above equation are the ground states. And let
\mathcal{G}
denote the set of all ground states.

Step 3.
We show that the ground state has to be radial.
We note the fact that the radiality of
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{W}
are equivalent.
If
\mathbf{W}
is non-radial, then by the Schwartz symmetrical rearrangement lemma,
M(\mathbf{W}^{\star})=M(\mathbf{W})
,
K(\mathbf{W}^{\star})<K(\mathbf{W})
, and
V(\mathbf{W}^{\star})\geqslant V(\mathbf{W})
, so we have
J(\mathbf{W}^{\star})<J(\mathbf{W})
, which is contradict to the minimality of
\mathbf{W}
.
Thus
\mathbf{W}
is radial and so is
\mathbf{Q}
.

Step 4.
We show that if
\mathbf{W}=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3})^{T}
is the minimal element, then there exists three constants
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))^{T}=(e^{i\theta_{1}}m\phi_{1}(nx),e^{i\theta_{2}}m\phi_{2}(nx),

e^{i\theta_{3}}m\phi_{3}(nx))^{T}
with
m=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},n=\sqrt{\alpha}
.

Since
J(m\phi_{1}(nx),m\phi_{2}(nx),m\phi_{3}(nx))\leqslant J(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))
, it is easy to find
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}
is also a minimal element. Suppose that
(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))=(e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}m\phi_{1}(nx),e^{i\theta_{2}(x)}m\phi_{2}(nx)
,
e^{i\theta_{3}(x)}m\phi_{3}(nx))
, where
\theta_{1}(x),\theta_{2}(x),\theta_{3}(x)
are real-valued functions, then

\displaystyle|\nabla\Phi_{1}(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\nabla e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}m\phi_{1}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{1}(x)e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}m\phi_{1}(nx)+e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}mn\nabla\phi_{1}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{1}(x)m\phi_{1}(nx)+mn\nabla\phi_{1}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|mn\nabla\phi_{1}(nx)|^{2}+|\nabla\theta_{1}(x)m\phi_{1}(nx)|^{2},

\displaystyle|\nabla\Phi_{2}(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\nabla e^{i\theta_{2}(x)}m\phi_{2}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{2}(x)e^{i\theta_{2}(x)}m\phi_{2}(nx)+e^{i\theta_{2}(x)}mn\nabla\phi_{2}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{2}(x)m\phi_{2}(nx)+mn\nabla\phi_{2}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|mn\nabla\phi_{2}(nx)|^{2}+|\nabla\theta_{2}(x)m\phi_{2}(nx)|^{2},

and

\displaystyle|\nabla\Phi_{3}(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\nabla e^{i\theta_{3}(x)}m\phi_{3}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{3}(x)e^{i\theta_{3}(x)}m\phi_{3}(nx)+e^{i\theta_{3}(x)}mn\nabla\phi_{3}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\nabla\theta_{3}(x)m\phi_{3}(nx)+mn\nabla\phi_{3}(nx)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|mn\nabla\phi_{3}(nx)|^{2}+|\nabla\theta_{3}(x)m\phi_{3}(nx)|^{2}.

By the minimality of
J(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x)),J(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))=J(m\phi_{1}(nx)
,
m\phi_{2}(nx),m\phi_{3}(nx))
. But by
M(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3})=M(m\phi_{1}(nx),m\phi_{2}(nx),m\phi_{3}(nx))
and
V(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))=V(m\phi_{1}(nx),m\phi_{2}(nx),m\phi_{3}(nx))
, so we have
K(\Phi_{1}(x)
,

\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))
=
K(m\phi_{1}(nx),m\phi_{2}(nx),m\phi_{3}(nx))
. Then
\nabla\theta_{1}(x)=\nabla\theta_{2}(x)=\nabla\theta_{3}(x)=0
, thus
\theta_{1}(x),\theta_{2}(x),\theta_{3}(x)
are both constants.
Therefore,

(\Phi_{1}(x),\Phi_{2}(x),\Phi_{3}(x))=(e^{i\theta_{1}}m\phi_{1}(nx),e^{i\theta_{2}}m\phi_{2}(nx),e^{i\theta_{3}}m\phi_{3}(nx)),

(3.6)

where
m,n>0,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}\neq\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
is the non-negative non-zero radial solution to (

).
∎

Remark 3.2

\displaystyle 0=

(3.7)

Lemma 3.7

(Coercivity on balls)
.

There exists

Proposition 4.1

(Scattering criterion)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0},\boldsymbol{\rm Q},I

Before showing the proof of Proposition

Lemma 4.2

Let

Let

Using Dumahel formula, Strichartz estimates, and Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle~\left\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\rm u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([t_{0}-l,t_{0}),L_{x}^{\frac{10}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}

Thus, by Strichartz estimates, for any

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{0}-l}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(s)){\rm d}s\right\|_{L_{t}^{6}([t_{0},\infty),L_{x}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}\leqslant

), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{JT_{0}}\int_{I}\int_{R_{0}}^{R_{0}e^{J}}\frac{1}{R^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}L^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\chi^{2}\left(\frac{x-s}{R}\right)\chi^{2}\left(\frac{y-s}{R}\right)N_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\frac{{\rm d}R}{R}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\left(\frac{R_{0}e^{J}}{JT_{0}}+\frac{1}{JR_{0}}+\frac{1}{J}+{\varepsilon}\right)E_{0}^{2},

which completes the proof of Proposition
1.5
.
∎

6.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we combine the results in Section 4 and Section 5 to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
:

First of all, using the scaling

\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{\rm u}\left(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x\right),

we can always fix
\lambda>0
such that (
1.5
) holds.
In order to establish (
4.1
), we change (
1.5
) into

\frac{\delta}{JT_{0}}\int_{I}\int_{R_{0}}^{R_{0}e^{J}}\frac{1}{R^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\mathcal{L}^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\mathcal{N}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\frac{{\rm d}R}{R}{\rm d}t\lesssim_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\left(\frac{R_{0}e^{J}}{JT_{0}}+{\varepsilon}\right)E_{0}^{2},

where

\mathcal{L}^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\kappa_{i}\left|\nabla\left(\chi\left(\frac{x-s}{R}\right)u^{i,\xi}(x)\right)\right|^{2},

and

\mathcal{N}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}=\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\left|\chi^{2}\left(\frac{y-s}{R}\right)u^{i}(y)\right|^{2}}{\kappa_{i}}.

If we choose
J={\varepsilon}^{-1}R_{0},T_{0}=e^{J}
, then

\frac{\delta}{JT_{0}}\int_{I}\int_{R_{0}}^{R_{0}e^{J}}\frac{1}{R^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\mathcal{L}^{\xi}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\mathcal{N}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\frac{{\rm d}R}{R}{\rm d}t\lesssim_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3},E_{0}}{\varepsilon}.

Considering the support of
\chi
in (
1.6
) and using the integral mean value theorem, we can omit writing some constants and get

\frac{1}{T_{0}}\int_{I}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{2}_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{2}_{{\rm L}_{y}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}{\rm d}t\lesssim_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3},E_{0}}{\varepsilon}.

The interval
I
is divided into many sub-intervals of the same length
l={\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{4}}
.
By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
I_{k}
such that

\int_{I_{k}}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{2}_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{2}_{{\rm L}_{y}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}{\rm d}t\lesssim_{E_{0}}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{4}},

which means

\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{4}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}^{4}=\int_{I_{k}}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{4}_{{\rm L}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}{\rm d}t\lesssim_{E_{0}}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Thanks to Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{2}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{4}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}\left\|\boldsymbol{\rm 1}\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{4}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{}_{E_{0}}~{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{16}}{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{16}}={\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

At last, by interpolation,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|_{{\rm L}_{t}^{6}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{\frac{1}{3}}_{{\rm L}_{t}^{2}(I_{k},{\rm L}_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}\left\|\left(\chi u^{1},\chi u^{2},\chi u^{3}\right)\right\|^{\frac{2}{3}}_{{\rm L}_{t}^{\infty}(I_{k},{\rm H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))}\lesssim_{E_{0}}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{24}},

which implies (
4.1
) holds.

So the scattering criterion, Proposition
4.1
, tells us that
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
scatters, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

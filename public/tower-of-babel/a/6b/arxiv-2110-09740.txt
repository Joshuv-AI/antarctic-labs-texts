Product systems and their representations: an approach using Fock spaces and Fell bundles Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 46L08, 47L55, 46L05 Thanks: Key words and phrases: Co-actions, Fock space, Fell bundles, product systems.
Evgenios T.A. Kakariadis

Abstract
. In this exposition we highlight product systems as the semigroup analogue of Fell bundles. Motivated by Fock creation operators we extend the definition of Fowler’s product systems over unital discrete left-cancellative semigroups, via both a concrete and an abstract characterization. We next underline the importance of Fock spaces and Fell bundle methods for obtaining covariant representation results. First, the strongly covariant representations of Sehnem, for product systems over group embeddable semigroups, find their analogue here. Secondly, we give two solutions for the existence of the co-universal C*-algebra with respect to equivariant injective Fock-covariant representations in this context. The first one uses an intertwining isometry technique to realize it as the C*-envelope of the normal cosystem on the Fock tensor algebra. The second uses a Fell bundle quotient technique to realize it as the reduced C*-algebra of Sehnem’s strong covariance relations. This C*-algebra is boundary for a larger class of equivariant injective representations.



1.
Introduction

Framework.

A boundary quotient is a terminal object for a class of representations.
In most of the cases boundary quotients are manifestations of corona sets and further our understanding beyond the “classical” universe of finite dimensional algebras and their norm-limits.

They have been a continuous source of inspiration in particular in the operator algebras theory.
First of all, they provide key C*-constructions in the study of geometric and topological objects, e.g., semigroups, graphs, dynamical systems etc.
Dating as back as the classification of factors by Murray and von Neumann in the 1930’s and 1940’s, they have been used successfully for detecting phase transitions of C*-measurements, such as K-theory, equilibrium states, finite dimensional approximations, or quasi-diagonal representations, and help reveal properties of the structures that may not be apparent intrinsically.
A second manifestation of boundary representations arises through the notions of the Šilov and the Choquet boundaries.
Motivated by the interaction with convexity and approximation theory
[
10
]
on (possibly nonselfadjoint) function algebras, Arveson foresaw a noncommutative analogue in his ground-breaking work
[
3
]
, later established by Hamana
[
29
]
.
A multi-faceted combination of the selfadjoint and nonselfadjoint viewpoints has been recently confirmed, with fruitful applications in group theory
[
7
,
35
]
, noncommutative geometry
[
12
]
, and noncommutative convexity
[
22
]
.

Herein we study product systems over a unital discrete group-embeddable semigroup
P\subseteq G
, and their representations.
Explored for more than 30 years, this construct models a great number of operator algebras, including those associated with graphs (of rank 1 or higher), dynamical systems of several flavours (reversible and irreversible), semigroups, and Nica-covariant product systems.
The identification of the co-universal C*-object has been of major importance and is at the base of many other developments, e.g., C*-correspondences

.
The

case has been quite accommodating in this respect, e.g., see

for a discussion that we will avoid repeating here.
Applications beyond

include results on the C*-structure, Takai duality, and the Hao-Ng problem, e.g.,

A long-standing question has been the identification of the boundary quotient with the (plain) C*-envelope of the Fock tensor algebra.
The breakthrough connection with co-universality was established by Dor-On–Katsoulis
[
18
]
, where they show that all objects (the boundary quotient, the C*-envelope and the co-universal object) coincide in the abelian lattice case.
These ideas were further explored by Dor-On–Kakariadis–Katsoulis–Laca–Li
[
19

for the right LCM case and by Kakariadis–Katsoulis–Laca–Li
[

for the semigroup algebras case, by introducing the C*-envelope of a cosystem, where it was noted that further advancements would require building a conditional expectation on the C*-envelope.
Recently, Sehnem

Our main goal here has been to amplify the Fock space and the Fell bundle techniques of

Motivated by a concrete picture, we consider product systems over any unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup, and use the Fock space as a model of covariance.
We also provide an equivalent definition of abstract product systems.
We show that the equivariant injective Fock-covariant representations of product systems admit a terminal object via two incarnations: as the C*-envelope of the cosystem on the Fock tensor algebra from
[
19
]
, and as the reduced C*-algebra of the strong covariance Fell bundle from
[
68
]
.
Working backwards we identify a super-class of covariant representations that admits the same terminal object and argue why this is the furthest one goes.

Fell bundles.

Hilbertian representations play an important role in the study of algebraic structures.
One of the cornerstone examples is the Gel’fand–Raikov Theorem that “identifies” a locally compact topological group with its unitary representations.
The idea of
quantized/quantum functional analysis
(as it was termed in the beginning) has been the driving force for constructing C*-algebras of groups, dynamical systems, graphs etc.
Similar to the group case, at one end there is a maximal/universal C*-algebra that emits to all possible representations, and at the other end there is a minimal/reduced C*-algebra that receives from all possible representations that admit a grading and are injective on the initial generators.
Exel
[
23
]
provided a great generalization at the level of Fell bundles over a discrete group
G
, showing that the left regular representation is terminal in this class.
His seminal work unifies and covers a great number of constructions, including that of graphs and crossed products of (partial) dynamics.

Product systems.

Product systems aim in modelling a semigroup analogue of Fell bundles.
They appeared first in the work of Arveson
[
4
]
disguised under duality, and considered later by Dinh
[
17
]
for discrete semigroups of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Fowler
[
26
,
27
]
formally defined product systems
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
and proceeded to an in-depth study when
(G,P)
is a quasi-lattice, that inspired a great number of subsequent works.
They have been used extensively for encoding C*-constructs with the list of examples being rather long to include here.
The interested reader may consult Michael Frank’s detailed Literature List on Hilbert modules (available online).
It may be instructive, however, to give the analogies with the prototypical example of graphs
[
14
]
.
Given a finite directed graph
{\mathcal{G}}
there are two C*-models for the reconstruction of vertices:
the Toeplitz–Cuntz–Krieger C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{G}}
(from emitting edges) and the Cuntz–Krieger C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
(from emitting and receiving edges).
They both admit a coaction of
\mathbb{Z}
by measuring the length of paths (with adjoints contributing with a negative length).
A third operator algebra, nonselfadjoint, arises by considering the
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-semigroup: we write
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{G}}
for the algebra generated by
{\mathcal{G}}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
We thus have the following analogies with the notions we are going to use:

(i)

The C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{G}}
represents the Fock-covariant representations.

(ii)

The operator algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{G}}
represents the Fock tensor algebra.

(iii)

The C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
represents the strong covariance relations.

The C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
is co-universal in two ways.
If
\Phi
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{G}}
that is injective on the vertices, then there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism from
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Phi)
onto
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
On the other hand, if
\phi
is completely isometric representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{G}}
(that inherits the coaction of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), then there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism from
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\phi)
onto
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Hence
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
is both a C*-boundary quotient and an
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{G}}
-boundary representation in the sense of Arveson.

Nica-covariance.

Fowler was greatly inspired by Nica’s work
[
57
]
on quasi-lattices.
It had been known that, unlike to the group case, taking plain isometric representations leads to intractable objects.
For example, the universal isometric C*-algebra of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
is not even nuclear
[
56
]
.
A richer structure is at hand when one considers the relations reflected in the Fock representation of a quasi-lattice order
(G,P)
, known as Nica-covariance.
In order to deal with this, Fowler imposed several axioms on the product system such as being compactly aligned and that the “product”
X_{p}\cdot X_{q}
is dense in
X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
.
A major consequence of this setup is a Wick ordering, i.e., the “monomials”
X_{p}\cdot X_{q}^{*}
densely span the generated C*-algebra.
The same picture is acquired by Kwaśniewski–Larsen
[
47
]
when
P
is a right LCM-semigroup (see also
[
19
]
).
In practice, compact alignment on right LCM-semigroups is the furthest one can go and still admit a Wick ordering.

Fock covariance.

Nica-covariance may fail for general semigroups, but one can still use the Fock representation as the prototype for covariant-type relations.
This has been noticed by Cuntz–Deninger–Laca
[
13
]
for
P=R\rtimes R^{\times}
.
In his ground-breaking work Li
[
52
]
introduced and studied semigroup representations that, apart from the semigroup structure, they remember the principal ideals and their intersections, i.e., the
constructible
ideals.
This model was further examined by Kakariadis–Katsoulis–Laca–Li
[
33
]
in connection also with inverse semigroup realizations of Norling
[
58
]
.
Recently Laca–Sehnem
[
49
]
identified the correct covariant relations that place the Fock representation of a semigroup as the reduced C*-algebra of a natural Fell bundle.
The constructible and the Fock model coincide only under an independence condition
[
33
,
49
]
.
In the case of product systems it has been observed in
[
19
, Proposition 4.3]
that the Nica-Toeplitz C*-algebra is maximal for the Fock bundle in the case of right LCM-semigroups.
Interestingly the Nica-covariance and compact alignment for right LCM-semigroups are connected in a solid way as shown in
[
37
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
34
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Strong covariance.

The graph C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
provides a corona context for the graph by moding out appropriate compact operators.
Following a series of works, Fowler–Laca–Raeburn
[
28
]
defined
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{G}}
for general graphs.
Later Katsura
[
42
]
established the covariant relations that give the correct analogue of a Cuntz-type C*-algebra for single C*-correspondences.
An extensive effort for establishing the strong covariance relations for general product systems has been put since then.
The goal had been to identify a C*-algebra that:

(a)

admits an injective copy of the co-efficient C*-algebra
A
;

(b)

inherits a coaction of
G
; and

(c)

any representation that is injective on
A
is faithful on its fixed point algebra.

For
regular
product systems this was met with much success
[
2
,
16
,
27
,
48
]
.
Moving forwards, Sims–Yeend
[
72
]
introduced the augmented Fock representation as a candidate for quasi-lattices, and Carlsen–Larsen–Sims–Vittadello
[
9
]
showed that it is an effective model in many non-regular cases.
At the same time they showed that this setup has its limitations and there are examples of semigroups that are not encountered, e.g., for
P=\left((\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{Z}\right)\bigcup\left(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
with the lexicographical order
[
72
, Example 3.16]
, or when
P
is not directed
[
9
, Example 3.9]
.
Recently, Sehnmen
[
68
]
provided the universal construction
A\times_{X}P
of strong covariance relations for general product systems.
This breakthrough step encompasses previous results and works for Fowler’s product systems with no restrictions on the semigroup whatsoever.
Sehnem terms
A\times_{X}P
as the “covariance C*-algebra of
X
”.
However in view of the different covariant relations we treat here and since the representations of
A\times_{X}P
are termed by Sehnem as strongly covariant, we will refer to
A\times_{X}P
as the
strong covariance C*-algebra of
X
.

Boundary quotients.

Strongly covariant quotients sometimes are referred to as boundary quotients, since by construction they produce a non-commutative boundary in a corona universe.
They have been shown to give a boundary quotient in several constructions in two ways: as the Šilov boundary of the nonselfadjoint Fock tensor algebra (and thus its C*-envelope in the sense of Arveson), and as the co-universal representation of
X
with respect to equivariant Fock-covariant representations that are injective on
A
.
At the level of C*-correspondences the C*-envelope question was answered by Katsoulis–Kribs
[
39
]
, while co-universality of the Cuntz-Pimsner algebra was proven by Katsura
[
43
]
by a parametrization of the equivariant ideals of the Fock representation.
Concerning compactly aligned product systems, Carlsen–Larsen–Sims–Vittadello
[
9
]
considered the co-universality question for quasi-lattices, with additional assumptions on
X
.
The C*-envelope question for C*-dynamics over
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
was answered by Davidson–Fuller–Kakariadis
[
15
]
, who used the C*-envelope machinery to acquire the form of the co-universal C*-algebra.
Recently, Dor-On–Katsoulis
[
20
]
used the C*-envelope to answer the co-universality question for abelian lattices, providing solid evidence of a novel connection between the two problems.
Lately, Dor-On–Kakariadis–Katsoulis–Laca–Li
[
19
]
resolved the issue at the generality of right LCM-semigroups, which has been the latest to date context for product systems theory.
A pivotal step in their work is the initiation of a C*-envelope theory for cosystems on possibly nonselfadjoint operator algebras.
In parallel, Kakariadis–Katsoulis–Laca–Li
[
33
]
have attained boundary quotient results for trivial fibers over any group-embeddable semigroup.
The essence of
[
19
,
33
]
is that they establish the compatibility between the two uses of the “boundary” term, as in the C*-theory and as in the nonselfajoint theory.
The methods of
[
19
,
33
]
rely on a blend of Fock space and Fell bundle techniques, and form a cornerstone for the current work.

Gauge-invariant uniqueness theorems.

A gauge-invariant-uniqueness-theorem (GIUT) provides necessary and sufficient conditions for an equivariant representation of
A\times_{X}P
to be faithful.
This can be derived for example by property (c) of strong covariance for amenable groups
G
.
Sometimes co-universality results are termed as GIUT’s (or the other way around).
However, the GIUT for strong covariance is weaker than co-universality, as it refers to a subclass of representations of
X
.
Even more, any relative Cuntz-Pimsner algebra for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
satisfies an appropriate GIUT
[
31
]
, while one cannot hope for a GIUT for
A\times_{X}P
without imposing amenability.

Main results.

We therefore arrive to the following questions:

(I)

Fowler’s product systems impose that
X_{p}\cdot X_{q}
be dense in
X_{pq}
.
Although the inclusion
X_{p}\cdot X_{q}
in
X_{pq}
is easy to check, the density requirement adds an extra step for associating a C*-construct with a product system.
Can this be relaxed, and how?

(II)

The (unconditional) universal C*-algebra of a product system may lead to intractable objects, even for abelian orders.
A rich structure is prominent for the universal C*-algebra that is related to the Fell bundle arising in the Fock representation has been observed in
[
19
,
34
]
for right LCM-semigroups and in
[
49
]
for semigroups.
Different covariant models give in principle non-isomorphic universal algebras.
However, substantial progress can be made without those completely being identified, as for example in
[
13
]
and
[
52
,
58
]
for semigroups.
What are the key algebraic properties from the Fock-covariant model?

(III)

The co-universal object for equivariant injective Fock-covariant representations of
X
has been computed for compactly aligned product systems over a right LCM-semigroup
[
19
]
and for trivial product systems over any group-embeddable semigroup
[
33
]
, in direct connection to
[
9
,
20
,
68
]
.
In some cases, the same strongly covariant object is co-universal for a wider class of representations
[
33
]
.
What is the largest covariant model that accommodates such a result?

Taking motivation from
[
19
,
33
,
34
,
49
]
, we consider an expanded notion of product systems, which includes that of Fowler
[
27
]
, by relaxing the condition in item (I).
We show that this class is still accommodating for key results concerning their covariant representations in relation to item (II).
Towards this end we show that the techniques of
[
9
,
19
,
23
,
33
,
68
]
find their analogue here under the appropriate adjustments.
As a consequence, we obtain the solution to the co-universality problem of item (III) without assuming extra structure on
X
or
P
, gathering previous co-universality results, for example those that appear in
[
2
,
9
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
27
,
33
,
38
,
39
,
42
,
46
,
48
,
52
,
55
,
60
,
65
,
66
,
68
,
72
]
.

Discussion of results.

Section 2 is devoted to preliminaries.
We describe the main results from
[
19
,
23
]
that we are going to use here.

In Section 3 we revisit the definition of a product system, and we first consider concrete families
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of operator spaces in a common
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
We require that the inclusions
X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq X_{pq}
are satisfied, as in the super-product systems setting of Margetts–Srinivasan
[
54
]
and Shalit–Skeide
[
71
]
.
We next make use of the left-cancellation of
P
to induce an “adjoint behaviour” and obtain
adjointable
left creation operators on the Fock space, by declaring that
A:=X_{e}
is a C*-algebra and that
X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq X_{q}
.
These alterations still make each
X_{p}
a C*-correspondence over
A
with the structure endowed by
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
At the same time, we provide an abstract characterization of a product system, which is useful for algebraic constructs.
We show that there is no distinction between “concrete” and “abstract” in terms of the related operator algebras.
Next we study a generalized version of cores and we give some preliminary results on the fixed point algebra of equivariant representations.

In Section 4 we use the Fock representation to locate the key properties for a rich algebraic structure.
First we use the coaction of
G
on the Fock representation to identify a universal
Fock-covariant
C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
, whose Fell bundle has
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
as its reduced C*-algebra.
We also identify the injective representations of
X
that descend to injective Fock-covariant representations, which we refer to simply as
covariant
.
In particular, we show that if an equivariant injective representation factors through a Fock-covariant one then it needs to be covariant.
Secondly we extend the results of Sehnem
[
68
]
and show that there is a direct analogue of the universal
strong covariance
C*-algebra
A\times_{X}P
in this context.
Most of the arguments of
[
68
]
pass through here, with the difference that we unconditionally show that there are canonical
*
-epimorphisms

{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\longrightarrow A\times_{X}P,

that fix
X
.
We have included plenty of details from
[
68
]
and
[
19
]
here in order to make clear how the arguments extend to our setting.
The
G
-Fell bundle in
A\times_{X}P
defines also the
reduced
strong covariance C*-algebra
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
that will be of great importance and was first considered in
[
19
]
.
The C*-algebras of this section are independent of the group embedding of
P
(and this why
G
is omitted from their notation).

In Section 5 we show that the class of equivariant injective covariant representations of
X
admits a terminal object.
One key point is that we can reduce to the case of injective Fock-covariant representations.
In the first approach we give a nonselfadjoint proof through the C*-envelope
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)
of the cosystem on the algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
generated by
A
and
X
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, first introduced in
[
19
]
.
Towards this end we prove a Fell’s absorption principle for
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
by any equivariant representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that is injective on
A
.
Thus such representations generate C*-covers for
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
, and the strategy of
[
19
]
applies to give that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)
is co-universal for the equivariant injective covariant representations of
X
.
Due to the normality of the coaction we deduce that it coincides with
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
Alternatively, we give a C*-algebraic approach of this result from Carlsen–Larsen–Sims–Vittadello
[
9
]
, inspired by
[
36
,
63
]
.
We note that the algebraic structure of strong covariance combines with the general Fell bundle arguments of
[
9
]
, and gives that
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
is the required co-universal object.
As the C*-envelope of the cosystem is in the class of representations and inherits a normal coaction, it then coincides with
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
We close with some immediate consequences of the co-universality results, including a GIUT for
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
They encompass a number of results in the literature, highlighting in the most emphatic way the ground-breaking significance of Sehnem’s strong covariance relations
[
68
]
, and the angles taken in
[
19
,
33
,
34
]
.

2.
Operator algebras and their coactions

2.1.
Operator algebras

If
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are subspaces of a normed algebra, then we write
[{\mathcal{X}}\cdot{\mathcal{Y}}]
for the norm-closure of the linear span of the elements
x\cdot y
with
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
and
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.
Our main references for operator spaces theory are the monographs
[
6
,
59
]
.
For this paper an operator algebra is a subalgebra of some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
for a Hilbert space
H
.
We will write
\otimes
for the spatial tensor product between operator spaces.

We say that
(C,\iota)
is a
C*-cover
of
{\mathfrak{A}}
if
\iota\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to C
is a completely isometric representation with
C=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\iota({\mathfrak{A}}))
.
The
C*-envelope

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
of
{\mathfrak{A}}
is a C*-cover
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}}),\iota)
with the following co-universal property:
if
(C^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
is a C*-cover of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, then there exists a (necessarily unique)
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon C^{\prime}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
such that
\Phi(\iota^{\prime}(a))=\iota(a)
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Arveson predicted the existence of the C*-envelope in
[
3
]
which he computed for a variety of operator algebras.
Later Hamana
[
29
]
removed all assumptions and proved the unconditional existence of injective envelopes.
The C*-envelope is the C*-algebra generated by
{\mathfrak{A}}
in its injective envelope once this is endowed with the Choi–Effros C*-structure.

The basic example of a C*-envelope arises in the case of uniform algebras: the C*-envelope of a uniform algebra is formed by the continuous functions on its Šilov boundary.
For the non-commutative analogue of this result consider
{\mathfrak{A}}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})
.
An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})
is called
boundary
if the quotient map
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})/{\mathcal{I}}
is completely isometric on
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
The
Šilov ideal

{\mathcal{I}}_{s}
is a boundary ideal that contains all the boundary ideals of
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
The existence of the C*-envelope implies the existence of the Šilov ideal; in particular, it follows that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})/{\mathcal{I}}_{s}
.

2.2.
Coactions on operator algebras

All groups we consider are discrete.
For a discrete group
G
we write
u_{g}
for the generators of the universal group C*-algebras
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
, and
\lambda_{g}
for the generators of the left regular representation
\lambda\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
We write
\chi
for the character on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
.

The notion of a cosystem for a (possibly nonselfajoint) operator algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
was introduced in
[
19
]
.
A
coaction of
G
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
is a completely isometric representation
\delta\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
such that the induced subspaces

{\mathfrak{A}}_{g}:=\{a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\mid\delta(a)=a\otimes u_{g}\}

densely span
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Therefore
\delta
satisfies the coaction identity

(\delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)})\delta=({\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}}\otimes\Delta)\delta.

If, in addition, the map
({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\delta
is injective, then the coaction
\delta
is called
normal
.
The triple
({\mathfrak{A}},G,\delta)
is called a
cosystem
.
A map
\Phi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime}
between two cosystems
({\mathfrak{A}},G,\delta)
and
({\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime},G,\delta^{\prime})
is said to be
G
-equivariant
if
\delta^{\prime}\Phi=(\Phi\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\delta
; equivalently
\Phi({\mathfrak{A}}_{g})\subseteq{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime}_{g}
for all
g\in G
.
We will be using the term
equivariant
when the group
G
is understood.

This definition covers that of Quigg for full coactions on C*-algebras
[
62
]
.
In
[
62
]
the coactions are assumed to be non-degenerate instead of the fibers densely spanning the C*-algebra, but for discrete groups these two properties are equivalent.

Remark 2.1
.

Suppose that
({\mathfrak{A}},G,\delta)
is a cosystem and that
\delta
extends to a
*
-homomorphism
\delta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
that satisfies the coaction identity

(\delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\delta(c)=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Delta)\delta(c)\text{ for all }c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}}).

By
[
19
, Remark 3.2]
then
\delta
is automatically non-degenerate on
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})
in the sense that

\overline{\delta(\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}}))\left[\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\right]}=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G).

In particular, the coactions on operator algebras arise as restrictions of coactions on C*-algebras.
Recall (for example from
[
6
, Proposition 2.4.2]
) that
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}}),j)
is the C*-cover of
{\mathfrak{A}}
with the following universal property: every completely contractive representation

extends to a
*
-representation
\widetilde{\Phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Therefore, if
({\mathfrak{A}},G,\delta)
is a cosystem, then we may consider the
*
-representation extension
\widetilde{\delta}
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})
by using the completely isometric homomorphism:

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})\supseteq j({\mathfrak{A}})\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{A}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\longrightarrow j({\mathfrak{A}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G).

Since
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\chi
is a left inverse for
\widetilde{\delta}
, we get that
\widetilde{\delta}
is faithful.
Moreover, we have that each fibre

[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})]_{g}:=\{x\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})\mid\widetilde{\delta}(x)=x\otimes u_{g}\}

contains all elements from
{\mathfrak{A}}_{g}
and
({\mathfrak{A}}_{g^{-1}})^{*}
, and all elements of the form

a_{g_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}a_{g_{2}}^{*}a_{g_{3}}\cdots a_{g_{n-1}}a_{g_{n}}^{*}a_{g_{n+1}}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad g=g_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}\cdots g_{n-1}g_{n}^{-1}g_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{2}};n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\{0,1\};

with the understanding that, if
\varepsilon_{i}=0
, then we omit that element from the product.
By construction
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathfrak{A}})
is densely spanned by these monomials and therefore
\widetilde{\delta}
is a coaction.

A
reduced
coaction on
{\mathfrak{A}}
consists of a family of spaces
\{{\mathfrak{A}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
that densely span
{\mathfrak{A}}
and a completely isometric homomorphism
\delta_{\lambda}\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
such that

\delta_{\lambda}(a)=a\otimes\lambda_{g}\text{ for all }a\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{g}.

By using the Fell absorption principle, it is shown in
[
19
, Proposition 3.4]
that in this case there is a coaction
\delta
such that
({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\delta=\delta_{\lambda}
, i.e., reduced coactions lift to normal coactions.

In
[
19
]
the authors introduce the notion of the C*-cover and of the C*-envelope for a cosystem.
A triple
(C,\iota,\delta_{C})
is called a
C*-cover
for a cosystem
({\mathfrak{A}},G,\delta)
if
(C,\iota)
is a C*-cover of
{\mathfrak{A}}
and

such that the following diagram

commutes.
The

of

is a C*-cover

.

One of the main results of
[

]
is that the C*-envelope of a cosystem

exists and that it links to the usual C*-envelope in the following way.
Fix the embedding

Then the C*-algebra

We can then endow it with the coaction

If

is normal as well

If

.

of the dual group

.
Since every

It is unknown if this is the case for amenable groups in general.

2.3.
Semigroups

For a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup
P
we write

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\mid p\in P)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}(P):=\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{V_{p}\mid p\in P\},

for the left-creation (isometric) operators given by

V_{p}\colon\ell^{2}(P)\to\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(P);\delta_{s}\mapsto\delta_{ps}.

The restriction to the diagonal
\ell^{\infty}(P)
induces a faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.

We will require some facts about the right ideals of
P
from
[
52
]
.
For a set
X\subseteq P
and
p\in P
we write

pX:=\{px\mid x\in X\}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{-1}X:=\{y\in P\mid py\in X\}.

Note here that by definition
p^{-1}P=P
.
We write
{\mathcal{J}}
for the smallest family of right ideals of
P
containing
P
and
\emptyset
that is closed under left multiplication, taking pre-images under left multiplication (as in the sense above) and finite intersections, i.e.,

{\mathcal{J}}:=\left\{\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{N}q_{j,n_{j}}^{-1}p_{j,n_{j}}\dots q_{j,1}^{-1}p_{j,1}P\mid N,n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};p_{j,k},q_{j,k}\in P\right\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

The right ideals of
P
in
{\mathcal{J}}
are called
constructible
.
It follows from
[
52
, Lemma 3.3]
that

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}X=(q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P)\cap X

for every
p_{i},q_{i}\in P
and every subset
X
of
P
.
Thus the set of constructible ideals is automatically closed under finite intersections, i.e.,

{\mathcal{J}}=\left\{q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};p_{i},q_{i}\in P\right\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

We will write
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}},{\mathbf{z}}
etc. for the elements in
{\mathcal{J}}
.
For a set
X\subseteq P
we will write
E_{[X]}
for the projection on
\left\langle\delta_{p}\mid p\in X\right\rangle
.
It follows that
E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}E_{[{\mathbf{y}}]}=E_{[{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}]}
for all
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
.

Suppose that
P
is in addition a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
.
Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
admits a normal coaction of
G
such that

[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)]_{g}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{p_{n}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N};p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P;p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=g\}.

Indeed, if
U\colon\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)
is the unitary operator given by

U(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{g})=\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{sg}\text{ for all }s\in P,g\in G,

then a routine calculation shows that the
*
-homomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes I\mid p\in P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes\lambda_{p}\mid p\in P)

is a reduced coaction, and thus it lifts to a normal coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
induced by
G
coincides with the compression to the diagonal.
Lemma 3.1 of
[
52
]
asserts that, if
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
are such that
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
in
G
, then

E_{[q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P]}=V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}.

Note here that for such
p_{i},q_{i}\in P
we have

V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}e_{r}=e_{r}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad p_{j}^{-1}q_{j}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r\in P\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,n,

otherwise it returns a zero value.
Since
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
in
G
it follows that

q_{j-1}^{-1}p_{j-1}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}r=p_{j}^{-1}q_{j}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r\in P\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,n.

This shows that

V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\neq 0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P\neq\emptyset.

Moreover, recall that

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P=(q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P)\cap P=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P.

Hence, without loss of generality, we will always assume that we write
{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P
with
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
.
We can also use these relations to deduce computations for ideals in
{\mathcal{J}}
.
For example, let
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
with
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
and
r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{m},s_{m}\in P
with
r_{1}^{-1}s_{1}\cdots r_{m}^{-1}s_{m}=e_{G}
.
Then for the ideals

{\mathbf{x}}:=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P,\;\ {\mathbf{y}}:=s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P\;\ \text{and}\;\ {\mathbf{z}}:=s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P,

we have that

E_{[{\mathbf{z}}]}=V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}V_{r_{1}}^{*}V_{s_{1}}\cdots V_{r_{m}}^{*}V_{s_{m}}=E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\cdot E_{[{\mathbf{y}}]}=E_{[{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}]}.

We deduce that

s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}P={\mathbf{z}}={\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}=s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P\cap p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}\dots p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}P.

2.4.
Gradings on C*-algebras

A reduced coaction on a C*-algebra defines a topological grading over a discrete group
G
in the sense of Exel
[
23
]
.
Recall that a
topological grading

{\mathcal{B}}:=\{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of a C*-algebra consists of linearly independent subspaces that span a dense subspace of the C*-algebra they generate and are compatible with the structure of
G
, namely

{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{*}={\mathcal{B}}_{g^{-1}}\text{ and }{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{gh}\text{ for all }g,h\in G.

By
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
the independence condition can be substituted by the existence of a conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
.
A
representation

\Psi
of
{\mathcal{B}}
is a continuous linear map on
\bigcup_{g\in G}{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
such that

\Psi(b_{g})\cdot\Psi(b_{h})=\Psi(b_{g}\cdot b_{h})\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi(b_{g})^{*}=\Psi(b_{g}^{*})

for all
b_{g}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
and
b_{h}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
.
We will say that
\Psi
is
G
-equivariant
(or simply
equivariant
) if
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)
admits a coaction such that

\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G);\Psi(b_{g})\mapsto\Psi(b_{g})\otimes u_{g}.

The maximal C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})
of a topological grading
{\mathcal{B}}
is the universal C*-algebra generated by
{\mathcal{B}}
with respect to the representations of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Due to universality, the maximal representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
is equivariant.
The reduced C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})
of
{\mathcal{B}}
is defined by the left regular representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})
given by

\lambda(b_{g})b_{h}=b_{g}b_{h}\text{ for all }g,h\in G.

It follows that the left regular representation is equivariant with a normal coaction of
G
.
Indeed, for the unitary operator

U\colon\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\longrightarrow\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\ell^{2}(G);b_{g}\otimes\delta_{h}\mapsto b_{g}\otimes\delta_{gh},

we have that

U\cdot(\lambda(b_{g})\otimes I)=(\lambda(b_{g})\otimes\lambda_{g})\cdot U\text{ for all }g\in G.

Hence we deduce the faithful
*
-homomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G);\lambda(b_{g})\mapsto\lambda(b_{g})\otimes\lambda_{g}.

A topological grading defines a
Fell bundle
, and once a representation of a Fell bundle is established the two notions are the same.
In a loose sense, a Fell bundle
{\mathcal{B}}
over a discrete group
G
is a collection of Banach spaces
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
, often called its fibers, that satisfy canonical algebraic and C*-norm properties; for full details we see that
[
24
, Definition 16.1]
.
So we will alternate between these two notions.

If
\delta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
is a coaction on a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
, then we write
\{[{\mathcal{C}}]_{g}\}_{g\in G}
for the induced topological grading.
It follows that
\delta
is normal if and only if the conditional expectation induced by
G
is faithful.
An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd{\mathcal{C}}
is called
induced
if
{\mathcal{I}}=\left\langle{\mathcal{I}}\cap[{\mathcal{C}}]_{e}\right\rangle
.
If
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd C
is an induced ideal, then
\delta
descends to a coaction on
C/{\mathcal{I}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We will be using the following facts from
[
23
,
24
]
.
Let
\Psi
be an equivariant representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})
and let

By

there are equivariant

, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Psi({\mathcal{B}}))
.
In that case the coaction on

Remark 2.3
.

{\mathcal{C}}
be a C*-algebra with a coaction

\dot{\Psi}
is also equivariant.
Indeed, to this end it suffices to show that
\Psi({\mathcal{I}})
is induced, i.e.,

\Psi({\mathcal{I}}\cap[{\mathcal{C}}]_{e})=\Psi({\mathcal{I}})\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)]_{e}.

The inclusion “
\subseteq
” is immediate due to equivariance of
\Psi
.
For the reverse inclusion let the conditional expectation
E
,
E_{q_{\mathcal{I}}}
and
E_{\Psi}
on
{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{I}}
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)
, respectively.
For an element
x\in\Psi({\mathcal{I}})\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)]_{e}
let
y\in{\mathcal{I}}
, so that
x=\Psi(y)
.
Then
q_{\mathcal{I}}E(y)=E_{q_{\mathcal{I}}}q_{{\mathcal{I}}}(y)=0
, and so
E(y)\in{\mathcal{I}}\cap[{\mathcal{C}}]_{e}
.
Hence, we have that
x=E_{\Psi}(x)=E_{\Psi}\Psi(y)=\Psi E(y)
, and so
x\in\Psi({\mathcal{I}}\cap[{\mathcal{C}}]_{e})
.

3.
Product systems and their operator algebras

3.1.
Product systems

Hilbert modules over C*-algebras are by now well understood.
The reader is addressed to
[
50
,
53
]
for an excellent introduction to the subject.

A
C*-correspondence

X
over
A
is a right Hilbert module over
A
with a left action given by a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi_{X}\colon A\to{\mathcal{L}}X
, where
{\mathcal{L}}X
denotes the C*-algebra of adjointable operators on
X
.
We write
{\mathcal{K}}X
for the closed linear span of the rank one adjointable operators
\theta_{\xi,\eta}
.
For two C*-correspondences
X,Y
over the same
A
we write
X\otimes_{A}Y
for the balanced tensor product over
A
.
We say that
X
is
unitarily equivalent
to
Y
(symb.
X\simeq Y
) if there is a surjective adjointable operator
U\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
such that
\left\langle U\xi,U\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle
and
U(a\xi b)=aU(\xi)b
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
and
a,b\in A
.
A representation of a C*-correspondence is a left and right module map on
{}_{A}X_{A}
such that
t(\xi)^{*}t(\eta)=t(\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle)
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
.
Every representation
t
defines a
*
-representation on
{\mathcal{K}}X
, which we will denote again by
t
, such that
t(\theta_{\xi,\eta})=t(\xi)t(\eta)^{*}
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup.
A
concrete product system
X
over
P
is a family
\{X_{p}\mid p\in P\}
of closed subspaces of some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
such that:

(i)

A:=X_{e}
is a C*-algebra.

(ii)

X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
.

(iii)

X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq X_{q}
for all
p,q\in P
.

Since
P
is left-cancellative we have that
q\in P
in item (iii) above is unique (and thus this property is well-defined).
The following proposition shows that a concrete product system is a semigroup representation by C*-correspondences.
In particular, it asserts that every
X_{pq}
is a right Hilbert module over
A
and a left module over
[X_{p}\cdot X_{p}^{*}]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup.
A family of closed subspaces
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
in a
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a concrete product system over
P
, if and only if:

(1)

A:=X_{e}
is a C*-algebra.

(2)

Every
X_{p}
is a C*-correspondence over
A
with the structure inherited by
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.

(3)

X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
.

(4)

X_{p}\cdot X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]
for all
p,q\in P
.

If items
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
hold, then item
(4)
is equivalent to each of the items below:

(4’)

[X_{p}\cdot X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}]=[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]
for all
p,q\in P
.

(4”)

[X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}]=[X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]
for all
p,q\in P
.

Proof.
.

Suppose that
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
is a product system over
P
.
By applying property (ii) of the definition for
q\to e_{P}
we get that
X_{p}
is a right module over
A=X_{e}
.
By applying property (iii) of the definition for
q\to e_{P}
we have that
X_{p}
is a right Hilbert module over
A=X_{e}
with the inner product space structure as inherited from
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Recall here that the trivial Hilbert module structure on
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
defines the same norm with the operator norm on
H
due to the C*-identity.
Applying again property (ii) of the definition for
p\to e_{P}
and
q\to p
we get that
X_{p}
is a left module over
A=X_{e}
.
As the operations take place inside
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
which is a C*-correspondence over itself, we get that the left action is given by adjointable operators, and thus each
X_{p}
is a C*-correspondence over
A=X_{e}
.
Finally, we have that

X_{p}\cdot(X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq})\subseteq X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}],

for all
p,q\in P
by property (iii) of the definition.

Conversely, suppose that the family
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
satisfies items (1)-(4) of the statement.
Since
X_{p}
is a C*-correspondence over
A=X_{e}
, then
X_{p}^{*}X_{p}X_{p}^{*}
is dense in
X_{p}^{*}
, and thus

X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq[X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{p}X_{p}^{*}X_{pq}]\subseteq[X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]\subseteq[X_{e}\cdot X_{q}]\subseteq X_{q}.

To finish the proof, suppose that items (1)-(3) of the statement hold.
Assume that item (4) holds.
Since
X_{p}X_{p}^{*}X_{p}
is dense in
X_{p}
, then the equation

[X_{p}X_{q}]=[X_{p}X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{p}X_{q}]\subseteq[X_{p}X_{p}^{*}X_{pq}]\subseteq[X_{p}X_{q}]

gives item (4’).
Assume now that item (4’) holds.
By multiplying with
X_{p}^{*}
and since
X_{p}^{*}X_{p}X_{p}^{*}
is dense in
X_{p}^{*}
we have that item (4’) implies item (4”).
Likewise, multiplying with
X_{p}
on both sides of item (4”) gives item (4), and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Fibres over invertible elements are compatible with adjoints.
That is, if
r
is invertible in
P
with inverse
r^{-1}
, then
[A\cdot X_{r}]^{*}=[A\cdot X_{r^{-1}}]
.
Indeed, by property (iii) of the definition we have that

[A\cdot X_{r}]^{*}=[A\cdot X_{r}\cdot A]^{*}=[A\cdot X_{r}^{*}\cdot X_{rr^{-1}}]\subseteq[A\cdot X_{r^{-1}}].

Taking adjoints and applying for
r^{-1}
yields the symmetrical
[A\cdot X_{r^{-1}}]\subseteq[A\cdot X_{r}]^{*}
.

As a consequence, if
X=\{X_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a concrete product system over a group
G
such that
X_{e}
acts non-degenerately on every fibre then
X
defines a Fell bundle, since taking adjoints satisfies

X_{g}^{*}=[A\cdot X_{g}]^{*}=[A\cdot X_{g^{-1}}]=X_{g^{-1}}\text{ for all }g\in G.

Conversely, if
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a concrete Fell bundle then it defines a concrete product system over
G
, with
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
acting non-degenerately on every fibre.
Indeed, we have that

{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{gh},\quad\text{and}\quad({\mathcal{B}}_{g})^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}_{gh}={\mathcal{B}}_{g^{-1}}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}_{gh}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{h},

while by definition
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
is a C*-algebra and non-degeneracy follows for example by
[
24
, Lemma 16.9]
.
Therefore, product systems are in some sense a semigroup analogue of (concrete) Fell bundles.

Remark 3.4

(Fock representation)
.

The properties of a concrete product system are enough to provide a Fock space representation.
Consider
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with its trivial C*-correspondence structure.
We will be writing

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\colon X_{p}\times X_{p}\longrightarrow A;(\xi_{p},\eta_{p})\mapsto\left\langle\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\right\rangle:=\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{p},

for the inner product induced on the
X_{p}
.
For every
p,q\in P
we define the multiplication operators

M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\colon X_{q}\longrightarrow X_{pq};\eta_{q}\mapsto\xi_{p}\cdot\eta_{q},

with the multiplication taken in
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
where
X
sits in.
It is clear that the operator norm of
M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}
is bounded by
\left\|\xi_{p}\right\|_{X_{p}}
.
Associativity of the product gives that
M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{p},X_{pq})
with

(M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq})^{*}=M_{\xi_{p}^{*}}^{pq,q}\;\text{ where }\;M_{\xi_{p}^{*}}^{pq,q}(\eta_{pq}):=\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{pq}\in X_{q}.

Indeed, a direct computation gives that

\left\langle\eta_{q},M_{\xi^{*}_{p}}^{pq,q}(\eta_{pq})\right\rangle=\eta_{q}^{*}\xi_{p}^{*}\eta_{pq}=\left\langle M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\eta_{q},\eta_{pq}\right\rangle

for all
\eta_{q}\in X_{q}
and
\eta_{pq}\in X_{pq}
.
Consider the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}X:=\sum^{\oplus}_{r\in P}X_{r}
which is a right Hilbert module over
A
.
For every
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
let

\lambda(\xi_{p}):=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}M_{\xi_{p}}^{r,pr}\;\text{ so that }\;\lambda(\xi_{p})^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}M_{\xi_{p}^{*}}^{pr,r},

where the sum is taken in the s*-topology, and with the understanding that
{\mathcal{L}}(X_{p},X_{pq})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)
as the
(pq,p)
-entry.
By applying on
\eta_{r}\in X_{r}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}X
we derive that

\lambda(\xi_{p})\eta_{r}=\xi_{p}\cdot\eta_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda(\xi_{p})^{*}\eta_{r}=\begin{cases}\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{r}&\text{if }r\in pP,\\
0&\text{if }r\notin pP.\end{cases}

Equivalently, let the Hilbert submodule
\sum_{r}^{\oplus}X_{r}\otimes e_{r}
of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)\otimes\ell^{2}(P)
.
We can then write

\lambda(\xi_{p})=w(M_{\xi_{p}}\otimes V_{p})w^{*}\text{ for all }p\in P,

for the unitary
w\colon{\mathcal{F}}X\to\sum_{r}^{\oplus}X_{r}\otimes e_{r}
with
w\xi_{r}:=\xi_{r}\otimes e_{r}
, and the operators
M_{x}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{B}}(H))
with
M_{x}(y):=xy
for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Consequently, we have that

\lambda(\xi_{p})\lambda(\xi_{q})=\lambda(\xi_{p}\cdot\xi_{q})\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda(\xi_{p})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{pq})=\lambda(\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\xi_{pq}).

We write
\lambda\colon X\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)
for this map and we refer to
\lambda
as the
Fock representation of
X
.

An equivalent categorical definition is available.
The key here is to consider a left module map of the left divisors on each fibre simultaneously.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup.
We say that a triple
\big(\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P},\{i_{p}^{pq}\}_{p,q\in P},\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}\big)
forms an
abstract
product system
X
if:

(i)

The space
X_{p}
is a C*-correspondence over
A:=X_{e}
with a left action
\varphi_{p}
.

(ii)

The space
X_{pq}
(and thus, by associative extension, the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{K}}X_{pq}\simeq X_{pq}\otimes_{A}X_{pq}^{*}
) is a
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-
A
-correspondence by a left action
i_{p}^{pq}
, where
i_{e}^{p}=\varphi_{p}
and
i_{p}^{p}={\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}}
.

(iii)

There are unitary maps

u_{pq,r}\colon X_{pq}\otimes_{A}X_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}[i_{pq}^{pqr}({\mathcal{K}}X_{pq})X_{pqr}]\subseteq X_{pqr}

which are
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-
A
-adjointable, where
X_{pq}\otimes_{A}X_{r}
is a
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-
A
-correspondence by the left action
i_{p}^{pq}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}}
; namely

\iota_{p}^{pqr}(S)u_{pq,r}=u_{pq,r}(i_{p}^{pq}(S)\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})\text{ for all }S\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}.

(iv)

The maps
\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}
are associative in the sense that the following diagram

\textstyle{X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\otimes_{A}X_{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r}}
\scriptstyle{u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}}}
\textstyle{X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{qr}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{p,qr}}
\textstyle{X_{pq}\otimes_{A}X_{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{pq,r}}
\textstyle{X_{pqr}}

is commutative for all

.

In particular, we have that

(3.1)

i_{p}^{pq}(S)u_{p,q}=u_{p,q}(i_{p}^{p}(S)\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}})=u_{p,q}(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}})\text{ for all }S\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}.

The left action of
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
on

and on

is non-degenerate and thus equation (
3.1
) extends to

, i.e.,

(3.2)

i_{p}^{pq}(T)u_{p,q}=u_{p,q}(T\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}})\text{ for all }T\in{\mathcal{L}}X_{p}.

The compatibility relations then yield

(3.3)

i_{p}^{pqr}(S)u_{p,qr}({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})=i_{pq}^{pqr}i_{p}^{pq}(S)u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})\text{ for all }S\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}.

Indeed for the left hand side we apply equation (
3.1
) for

in place of

and

to obtain

\displaystyle i_{p}^{pqr}(S)u_{p,qr}({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})

\displaystyle=u_{p,qr}(S\otimes u_{q,r})

\displaystyle=u_{p,qr}({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}}),

where, in the last line, we used the compatibility of the

For the right hand side we apply the extended formula (
3.2
) for

in place of
p
and

, and for

so that

\displaystyle i_{pq}^{pqr}\left(i_{p}^{pq}(S)\right)u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}(i_{p}^{pq}(S)\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}(i_{p}^{pq}(S)u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}}),

where, in the last line, we applied once more equation (
3.1
).
This proves equation (
3.3
).
Note here that if the maps
u_{p,q}
are unitaries onto

then equation (
3.3
) gives the familiar compatibility relation
i_{p}^{pqr}=i_{pq}^{pqr}i_{p}^{pq}
.

Example 3.6
.

The definition of abstract product systems we use here recovers the one by Fowler
[
27
, Definition 2.1]
.
Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup.
Suppose that
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
is a family of C*-correspondences over the same C*-algebra
A
such that:

(i)

X_{e}=A
.

(ii)

There are unitaries
X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\simeq_{u_{p,q}}X_{pq}
for every
p,q\in P\setminus\{e\}
.

(iii)

There are unitaries
A\otimes_{A}X_{p}\simeq_{u_{e,p}}\overline{A\cdot X_{p}}
and

for all

.

(iv)

u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})=u_{p,qr}({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})\text{ for all }p,q,r\in P.

.

-

-correspondence by

Remark 3.7
.

We claim that an abstract product system
X
attains an isometric representation in a C*-algebra as a concrete product system.
This will be achieved through a Fock space construction.
First we recall that every
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
defines an adjointable operator

\xi_{p}^{*}\colon X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\longrightarrow X_{q};\eta_{p}\otimes\eta_{q}\mapsto\varphi_{q}(\left\langle\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\right\rangle)\eta_{q}.

For every
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
we define the left creation operator

\tau_{q}^{pq}(\xi_{p})\colon X_{q}\longrightarrow X_{pq};\eta_{q}\mapsto u_{p,q}(\xi_{p}\otimes\eta_{q}).

In particular, for
\xi_{p},\eta_{p},\zeta_{p}\in X_{p}
we can use that the map
u_{p,q}
is
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-adjointable and get

\tau_{q}^{pq}(\xi_{p}\left\langle\eta_{p},\zeta_{p}\right\rangle)\eta_{q}=u_{p,q}(\xi_{p}\left\langle\eta_{p},\zeta_{p}\right\rangle\otimes\eta_{q})=i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})u_{p,q}(\zeta_{p}\otimes\eta_{q}).

This map is
A
-linear and direct computations give that it is in
{\mathcal{L}}(X_{q},X_{pq})
with

\tau_{q}^{pq}(\xi_{p}\left\langle\eta_{p},\zeta_{p}\right\rangle)^{*}\eta_{pq}=\zeta_{p}^{*}\left(u_{p,q}^{*}(i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})^{*}\eta_{pq})\right).

Now the association

\tau_{q}^{pq}\colon X_{p}\left\langle X_{p},X_{p}\right\rangle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X_{q},X_{pq})

is
A
-linear and contractive, and thus it extends to the closure of
X_{p}\left\langle X_{p},X_{p}\right\rangle
which is
X_{p}
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\tau_{q}^{pq}(\xi_{p})
is an adjointable operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(X_{q},X_{pq})
.

We can then form the full Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}X:=\sum^{\oplus}_{r\in P}X_{r}
and define the Fock representation

\lambda(\xi_{p}):=\sum_{r\in P}\tau_{r}^{pr}(\xi_{p}),

where the sum is taken in the s*-topology.
We show that the family
\{\lambda(X_{p})\}_{p\in P}
satisfies the items of Proposition
3.2
.
Items (1) and (2) of Proposition
3.2
are straightforward.
Associativity of the maps
\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}
yields

\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{q})\subseteq\lambda(u_{p,q}(X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}))\subseteq\lambda(X_{pq}),

which is item (3) of Proposition
3.2
.
Indeed, a direct computation for
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
,
\xi_{q}\in X_{q}
and
\eta_{r}\in X_{r}
gives that

\displaystyle\lambda(\xi_{p})\lambda(\xi_{q})\eta_{r}

\displaystyle=u_{p,qr}({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})(\xi_{p}\otimes\xi_{q}\otimes\eta_{r})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(\xi_{p}\otimes\xi_{q}\otimes\eta_{r})=\lambda(u_{p,q}(\xi_{p}\otimes\xi_{q}))\eta_{r}.

Moreover, every map
u_{pq,r}
is
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-
A
-adjointable, and thus we get that

\displaystyle\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{p})^{*}\lambda(X_{pq})\subseteq\lambda({\mathcal{K}}X_{p}\cdot X_{pq})\subseteq\lambda(u_{p,q}(X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}))=[\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{q})],

which is item (4) of Proposition
3.2
.
Indeed, first we show that

\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})\equiv\lambda(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})\in{\mathcal{L}}\left(\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}X_{r}\right).

Since
X_{p}X_{p}^{*}X_{p}
is dense in
X_{p}
it suffices to show this for
y_{p}:=\xi_{p}\left\langle\eta_{p},\zeta_{p}\right\rangle
.
One one hand it is clear that

\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}\eta_{r}=0\textup{ whenever }r\notin pP.

On the other hand, fix
\eta_{pr}
so that
i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})^{*}\eta_{pr}\in u_{p,r}(X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{r})
.
Thus we can write

u_{p,r}^{*}(i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})^{*}\eta_{pr})=\sum_{j}v_{p}^{(j)}\otimes w_{r}^{(j)}\in X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{r},

where the summation symbol is a short for convergence with respect to finite subsets (rather than series convergence).
Then we get that

\displaystyle\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}\eta_{pr}

\displaystyle=u_{p,r}\bigg({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes\zeta_{p}^{*}\bigg)\bigg(x_{p}\otimes u_{p,r}^{*}\big(i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})^{*}\eta_{pr}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}u_{p,r}\bigg({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes\zeta_{p}^{*}\bigg)\bigg(x_{p}\otimes(v_{p}^{(j)}\otimes w_{r}^{(j)})\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}u_{p,r}\big(x_{p}\otimes\varphi_{r}(\left\langle\zeta_{p},v_{p}^{(j)}\right\rangle)w_{r}^{(j)}\big)=\sum_{j}u_{p,r}\big(x_{p}\left\langle\zeta_{p},v_{p}^{(j)}\right\rangle\otimes w_{r}^{(j)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}u_{p,r}\big((\theta_{x_{p},\zeta_{p}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(v_{p}^{(j)}\otimes w_{r}^{(j)})\big)=i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{x_{p},\zeta_{p}})\big(u_{p,r}(\sum_{j}v_{p}^{(j)}\otimes w_{r}^{(j)})\big)

\displaystyle=i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{x_{p},\zeta_{p}})i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{\eta_{p},\xi_{p}})\eta_{pr}=i_{p}^{pr}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})\eta_{pr},

where we used that
u_{p,r}
is a unitary onto its range.
Now we can deduce that

\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}\lambda(z_{pq})=\lambda\big(i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})(z_{pq})\big)

for every
x_{p},y_{p}\in X_{p}
and
z_{pq}
.
Indeed, for every
\eta_{r}\in X_{r}
we directly compute

\displaystyle\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}\lambda(z_{pq})\eta_{r}

\displaystyle=\lambda(x_{p})\lambda(y_{p})^{*}u_{pq,r}(z_{pq}\otimes\eta_{r})=i_{p}^{pqr}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})u_{pq,r}(z_{pq}\otimes\eta_{r})

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}\bigg((i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(z_{pq}\otimes\eta_{r})\bigg)

\displaystyle=u_{pq,r}\big(i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})z_{pq}\otimes\eta_{r}\big)=\lambda\big(i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{x_{p},y_{p}})z_{pq}\big)\eta_{r}.

Conversely every concrete product system
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
forms an abstract product system.
First recall that the stabilized tensor product between two C*-correspondences is independent of the faithful representation on a common Hilbert space.
Thus the maps

u_{p,q}\colon X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\longrightarrow[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)

are unitaries onto
[X_{p}X_{q}]
, and the family
\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}
satisfies associativity.
This is compatible with the identification of
{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
with
X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{p}^{*}
, and in turn with
[X_{p}\cdot X_{p}^{*}]
.
We then define the left actions
i_{p}^{pq}
by multiplication, i.e.,

i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})\zeta_{pq}=\xi_{p}\eta_{p}^{*}\zeta_{pq}\in X_{p}X_{p}^{*}X_{pq}\subseteq X_{pq},

where we used property (4) from Proposition
3.2
.
It follows that every
u_{pq,r}
is a right Hilbert
A
-module map and in particular

u_{pq,r}(X_{pq}\otimes_{A}X_{r})=[X_{pq}X_{r}]=[X_{pq}X_{pq}^{*}X_{pqr}]=[i_{pq}^{pqr}({\mathcal{K}}X_{pq})X_{pqr}],

where we used property (4’) from Proposition
3.2
.
Finally, we need to check the compatibility relation, and it suffices to do so for
S=\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}
.
A direct computation yields

i_{p}^{pqr}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})u_{pq,r}(\zeta_{pq}\otimes\zeta_{r})=\xi_{p}\eta_{p}^{*}\zeta_{pq}\zeta_{r}=u_{pq,r}(i_{p}^{pq}(\theta_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}})\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})(\zeta_{pq}\otimes\zeta_{r}).

Hence the triple
\big(\{X_{p}\},\{i_{p}^{pq}\},\{u_{p,q}\}\big)
defines an abstract product system structure.

Remark 3.8
.

If
X
is an abstract product system, then
X
and
\lambda(X)
have unitarily equivalent Fock representations.
Towards this end let
\Lambda
be the Fock space representation of the concrete product system
\lambda(X)
on
{\mathcal{F}}\lambda(X)
.
By using that
\lambda
is injective on each
X_{p}
we can define an isometric map

U\colon{\mathcal{F}}\lambda(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}X;\lambda(\xi_{p})\mapsto\xi_{p},

first on finite sums and then extending by continuity.
In particular,
U
is a unitary so that
U\Lambda(\lambda(\xi_{p}))=\lambda(\xi_{p})U
.
Hence
U
induces a spatial
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Lambda)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda)
that fixes
X
.

3.2.
Representations of product systems

We next define the operators associated to a product system
X
.
As we will see there is no difference in assuming that
X
is concrete.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup and let
X
be a concrete product system over
P

A
Toeplitz representation
t=\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of

t_{p}
of
X_{p}
such that

(i)

t_{e}
is a
*
-representation of
A:=X_{e}
.

(ii)

t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{q}(\xi_{q})=t_{pq}(\xi_{p}\xi_{q})

and
\xi_{q}\in X_{q}

(iii)

for all
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
and
\xi_{pq}\in X_{pq}

A representation is called

The

By definition every

over

The definition of a Toeplitz representation is transferable to the abstract setting.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup and let
X
be an abstract product system.
A
Toeplitz representation
t=\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of
X
consists of a family of linear maps
t_{p}
of

(i)

(t_{e},t_{p},t_{e})

(ii)

(t_{p},t_{pq},t_{e})

{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
-

(iii)

Remark 3.11
.

Remark
3.7
shows that the Fock representation is a Toeplitz representation of an abstract product system

.

Due to Remark

.

To relax notation, we will simply write
t
for any

.
If

\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\equiv\mathrm{C}^{*}(t_{*}):=t_{*}({\mathcal{T}}(X))=\mathrm{C}^{*}(t(\xi_{p})\mid\xi_{p}\in X_{p};p\in P).

Henceforth we will assume that

is a unital subsemigroup of a group

.
Suppose that

is faithfully represented as a representation

of

By the universal property of

\widetilde{\delta}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G):\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p})\longmapsto\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p})\otimes u_{p}.

[

\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\widetilde{t}(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\widetilde{t}(\xi_{q_{n}})\quad\text{for}\quad p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=g.

In light of Remark
2.1
we can extend this result to the generality of our context.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over

.
Let
\widetilde{t}_{*}
be a faithful representation of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.
Then there is a coaction of
G
on
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
such that

\widetilde{\delta}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G);\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p})\longmapsto\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p})\otimes u_{p}.

Moreover, each spectral space
[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}
with

is given by the products of the form

\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\widetilde{t}(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\widetilde{t}(\xi_{q_{n}})\quad\text{for}\quad p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=g.

where
\widetilde{t}(\xi_{p_{i}})=I
or
\widetilde{t}(\xi_{q_{i}})=I
whenever
p_{i}=e_{G}
or
q_{i}=e_{G}
, and
g\neq e_{G}

Proof.
.

By the universal property of

we obtain a well-defined
*
-homomorphism
\widetilde{\delta}
with left inverse
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\chi
.
By considering
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes E_{g}

we derive that each subspace

[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}=\{x\in{\mathcal{T}}(X)\mid\widetilde{\delta}(x)=x\otimes u_{g}\}

is densely spanned by the products of the statement.
Since those subspaces densely span
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
, we derive that
\widetilde{\delta}
is a coaction on
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.
∎

Let
t
be a representation of
X
.
For
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
we define the
\mathbf{K}
-core on
{\mathbf{x}}
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\big\{t(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t(X_{q_{n}})\mid p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots

\displaystyle p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G};

\displaystyle q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P={\mathbf{x}}\big\}.

We do not claim that

For a finite

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Then the following hold for a representation

of
X

(a)

For every
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
, the space
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}
is a closed C*-subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}
is a finite
\cap
-closed set with
\emptyset\in{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},t_{\ast}}
is a C*-subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
.

(c)

If

[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}=\overline{\bigcup\{\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},t_{\ast}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}\textup{ finite and $\cap$-closed},\emptyset\in{\mathcal{F}}\}}

as the inductive limit of C*-algebras.

Proof.
.

(a) First we show that
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}
is a selfadjoint space.
Towards this end, consider

t(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t(X_{q_{n}})\subseteq\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}},

for

and

.
We then have that

\displaystyle q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P

\displaystyle=P\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot P)\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdot P)\cap\cdots\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}\cdot p_{1}\cdot P)

\displaystyle=(p_{1}^{-1}\cdot q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}\cdot q_{n}\cdot P)\cap(p_{1}^{-1}\cdot q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}\cdot P)\cap\cdots\cap P=p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P,

where the multiplications take place in

.
From this we derive that

\left(t(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t(X_{q_{n}})\right)^{*}=t(X_{q_{n}})^{*}t(X_{p_{n}})\cdots t(X_{q_{1}})^{*}t(X_{p_{1}})\subseteq\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}

as well, and thus
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}

Next recall that if we have

{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{y}}=s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P,

with

and

{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P,

and of course
q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}=e_{G}

Hence we obtain that

\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{y}},t_{\ast}}\subseteq\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}},t_{\ast}}.

From this we derive that every
\mathbf{K}
-core is an algebra (as

).

(b) Let an enumeration
{\mathcal{F}}=\{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\}
that dominates the partial order of inclusions in
{\mathcal{F}}
, so that
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}=\emptyset

For

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:=\{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\}

Due to the enumeration and since

is
\cap
-closed.

\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},t_{\ast}}\lhd\mathbf{B}_{\{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\},t_{\ast}}=\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},t_{\ast}}+\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{1},t_{\ast}},

Suppose now that we have shown that

(c) By assumption we have that every non-zero element of the form

3.3.
Exel’s Bessel inequality

The Bessel inequality is an important tool in the Fell bundles theory; see
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
and/or
[
24
, Lemma 17.14]
.
We will see here an application to C*-algebras of product systems.
Towards this end let
t
be an equivariant injective representation of
X
in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with conditional expectation
E_{t_{\ast}}
, and consider the induced Fell bundle

{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast}}=\{[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{g}\}_{g\in G}.

Let
Y_{t_{\ast}}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
-Hilbert module with respect to the inner product structure

\left\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\right\rangle_{Y_{t_{\ast}}}:=E_{t_{\ast}}(\eta_{1}^{*}\eta_{2})\text{ for all }\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\sum_{g}{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast},g}.

By construction we have that
\sum_{g}{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast},g}\hookrightarrow Y_{t_{\ast}}
, and in turn in
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
it is shown that
Y_{t_{\ast}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast}})
by the map
U\colon b_{g}\mapsto j(b_{g})
, for the inclusion
j\colon{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast},g}\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast}})
.
Moreover, in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
it is established that

\left\langle cx,cx\right\rangle_{Y_{t_{\ast}}}\leq\left\|c\right\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}(H)}\left\langle x,x\right\rangle_{Y_{t_{\ast}}}\text{ for all }c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}(t),x\in\sum_{g}{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast},g}.

Applying on
M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(t))
yields a matricial Bessel inequality on
\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t_{\ast}}
given by

\left\langle[c^{ij}][x^{k}],[c^{ij}][x^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t_{\ast}}}\leq\left\|[c^{ij}]\right\|^{2}_{M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))}\left\langle[x^{k}],[x^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t_{\ast}}}

for
c^{ij}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
and
x^{k}\in\sum_{g}{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast},g}
,
i,j,k=1,\dots,n
.

For a product system version, let
Y_{t}
be the
A
-submodule of
Y_{t_{\ast}}
densely spanned just by the
t(X_{p})
.
Since
t
is injective on
X
we may see
{\mathcal{F}}X\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}}_{t_{\ast}})
and thus
Y_{t}\simeq^{U}{\mathcal{F}}X
.
Let
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and consider

b^{ij}\in{\mathcal{T}}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad{\eta}^{k}:=\sum_{q\in P}t(\eta^{k}_{q})\quad\textup{such that}\quad t_{\ast}(b^{ik})\eta^{k}\in\sum_{p}t(X_{p}).

Then
[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})][\eta^{k}]\in\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t}
and, by restriction, Bessel’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\langle[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})][\eta^{k}],[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})][\eta^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})][\eta^{k}],[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})][\eta^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum\limits_{k}Y_{t_{\ast}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})]\right\|^{2}_{M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))}\cdot\left\langle[\eta^{k}],[\eta^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum\limits_{k}Y_{t_{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})]\right\|^{2}_{M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))}\cdot\left\langle[\eta^{k}],[\eta^{k}]\right\rangle_{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t}}.

In particular, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
t\colon X\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be an equivariant injective representation of
X
, and let
Y_{t}
be the
A
-module as constructed above.
For
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
let
\eta^{k}\in Y_{t}
and
b^{ij}\in{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.
If
t_{\ast}(b^{ik})\eta^{k}\in Y_{t_{\ast}}
for all
i,k
, then

\|[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})]\cdot[\eta^{k}]\|_{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t}}\leq\left\|[t_{\ast}(b^{ij})]\right\|_{M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))}\cdot\|[\eta^{k}]\|_{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}Y_{t}}.

In
[
70
, Proposition 3.1]
, Sehnem produces a hands-on Bessel-type inequality for elements in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
and representations of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
In short, let
\Phi
be an equivariant
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that admits a conditional expectation
E
, and let
E_{\lambda}
be the faithful conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
For
\xi:=\sum_{p\in P}\xi_{p}
in
{\mathcal{F}}X
set
\lambda(\xi)=\sum_{p\in P}\lambda(\xi_{p})
.
Then taking
\eta:=\sum_{q\in P}\eta_{q}
in
{\mathcal{F}}X
yields

\displaystyle E\Phi(\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta)^{*}\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta))

\displaystyle=E\Phi(\lambda(\eta)^{*}\lambda(\xi)^{*}\lambda(\xi)\lambda(\eta))\leq\left\|\Phi\lambda(\xi)\right\|^{2}E\Phi(\lambda(\eta)^{*}\lambda(\eta)).

Using that
E\Phi=\Phi E_{\lambda}
gives

E\Phi(\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta)^{*}\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta))=\Phi E_{\lambda}(\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta)^{*}\lambda(\lambda(\xi)\eta))=\Phi\lambda(\sum_{p,q}\left\langle\xi_{p}\eta_{q},\xi_{p}\eta_{q}\right\rangle),

and that

E\Phi(\lambda(\eta)^{*}\lambda(\eta))=\Phi E_{\lambda}(\lambda(\eta)^{*}\lambda(\eta))=\Phi\lambda(\sum_{q\in P}\left\langle\eta_{q},\eta_{q}\right\rangle).

If
\Phi
is injective on
A
, then passing to norms yields

\left\|\lambda(\xi)\eta\right\|_{{\mathcal{F}}X}\leq\left\|\Phi\lambda(\xi)\right\|\cdot\left\|\eta\right\|_{{\mathcal{F}}X}.

Similar arguments apply to the matrix levels.
As a consequence equivariant representations of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that are injective on
A
are completely isometric on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}

[
70
, Corollary 3.5]
.
Corollary
3.14
allows an extension of this result, so that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
receives completely contractive representations from the algebra of any equivariant injective representation of
X
(we will show this in Proposition
5.18
).

4.
Covariant representations of product systems

4.1.
Fock-covariant representations

The Fock representation
\lambda
defines an injective representation of
X
and hence it induces a representation
\lambda_{*}
of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.
It has played a crucial role in the identification of the co-universal object in
[
19
,
33
]
, and it is at the epicenter of the covariant relations.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P
be a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
The
Fock algebra

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
is the C*-algebra generated by the Fock representation
\lambda_{*}
.
The
Fock tensor algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
of
X
is the (possibly nonselfadjoint) subalgebra of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
generated by the image of
X
under
\lambda
.

As observed in
[
19
]
, when
P
is a unital subsemigroup of a group
G
, then the Fock algebra admits a normal coaction by
G
.
Indeed, a direct computation gives that

U\cdot(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes I)=(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes\lambda_{p})\cdot U\text{ for all }p\in P,

for the unitary
U\colon{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G)
given by

U(\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{g})=\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{rg}\text{ for all }r\in P,g\in G.

Recall here that by
[
50
, p.37]
we have

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)\otimes{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(G))\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G)),

where we see
\ell^{2}(G)
as a right Hilbert module over
\mathbb{C}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G)
denotes the exterior product of Hilbert modules.
Hence the faithful
*
-homomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes I\mid p\in P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes\lambda_{p}\mid p\in P)

defines a reduced coaction on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, and thus it lifts to a normal coaction
\delta
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
We can see that
\lambda_{*}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
is equivariant with respect to the coactions of
G
.

The coaction of
G
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
induces a grading and thus a Fell bundle.
We will use this as the defining property for
Fock covariance
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
For
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c},e}^{\operatorname{F}}:=\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
let the induced ideal

{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}:=\left\langle\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}\right\rangle\lhd{\mathcal{T}}(X).

We write
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
for the equivariant quotient of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
by
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
.
We define the
Fock-covariant
bundle of
X
be the Fell bundle

{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X:=\Big\{[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G},

given by the coaction of
G
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
.
A representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X
will be called a
Fock-covariant representation of
X
.

Remark 4.3
.

Since the bimodule properties are graded we have that every representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X
is a representation of
X
.
Moreover
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
is a representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X
by definition, and it admits a normal coaction, thus
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)
.
Hence we have the canonical
*
-epimorphisms

{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X).

We will use the notation
\iota\colon{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)
for the natural embedding of the Fell bundle in its universal C*-algebra.
Therefore, we have

\iota(b_{{\mathbf{x}}}),\iota\lambda(\xi_{r})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\text{ for all }b_{{\mathbf{x}}},\lambda(\xi_{r})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X).

If
t
is a Fock-covariant representation of
X
we thus have

t_{\ast}\iota\lambda(\xi_{r})=t(\xi_{r})\text{ for all }\xi_{r}\in X_{r},

for the induced
t_{\ast}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
.

As we are about to see, Fock-covariant representations satisfy that
\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0)
.
Hence by Proposition
3.13
their fixed point algebras admit an inductive limit realization over finite
\cap
-closed subsets of
{\mathcal{J}}
(discarding the empty set).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
t
is a Fock-covariant representation of
X
, then
\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0)
.

Proof.
.

It suffices to show this for the Fock representation.
We will show that if
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},\lambda_{\ast}}\neq(0)
then
{\mathbf{x}}\neq\emptyset
.
Towards this end, let
p_{i},q_{i}\in P
such that
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
, and
\xi_{p_{i}}
,
\xi_{q_{i}}
such that

\lambda(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{n}})\neq 0.

Then there is an
r\in P
and a
\xi_{r}\in X_{r}
such that

X_{r}\ni\xi_{p_{1}}^{*}\xi_{q_{1}}\cdots\xi_{p_{n}}^{*}\xi_{q_{n}}\xi_{r}=\lambda(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{n}})\xi_{r}\neq 0.

In particular, we get
p_{j+1}^{-1}q_{j+1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r=q_{j}^{-1}p_{j}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}r\in P
for every
j=1,\dots,n
.
Therefore, the ideal
q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P
contains
r
, and hence it is non-empty.
∎

We will be checking at every level that the constructions coming from
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
are independent of the group embedding.
This boils down to showing that their fixed point algebras are independent of the group embedding.
The first part of the proof of
[
68
, Lemma 3.9]
can be transferred in our setting.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Suppose that
P
admits two group embeddings
i_{G}\colon P\to G
and
i_{H}\colon P\to H
.
Then

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{span}}\big\{\lambda(X_{p_{1}})^{*}\lambda(X_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda(X_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(X_{q_{n}})\mid i_{G}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{1})\cdots i_{G}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{n})=e_{G};

\displaystyle\hskip 327.20668ptq_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P={\mathbf{x}}\big\}=

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\big\{\lambda(X_{p_{1}})^{*}\lambda(X_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda(X_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(X_{q_{n}})\mid i_{H}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{1})\cdots i_{H}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{n})=e_{H};

\displaystyle\hskip 327.20668ptq_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P={\mathbf{x}}\big\},

and thus the fixed point algebras of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
coincide, i.e.,

[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{G}}=[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{H}}.

Proof.
.

For convenience, let
G
be the enveloping group of
P
and let
\gamma\colon G\to H
be the canonical epimorphism that fixes
P
.
By definition we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(H)}

and therefore
[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{G}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{H}}
.
In particular, we have the inclusion “
\subseteq
” for the
\mathbf{K}
-cores of the statement.
For the reverse inclusion let a non-zero element

0\neq b_{{\mathbf{x}}}=\lambda(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{n}})\in[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{H}}

for
\gamma(p_{1})^{-1}\gamma(q_{1})\cdots\gamma(p_{n})^{-1}\gamma(q_{n})=e_{H}
.
We will show that
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\in[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{G}}
; equivalently that
p_{1}q_{1}^{-1}\cdots p_{n}q_{n}^{-1}=e_{G}
.
To reach a contradiction suppose that
p_{1}q_{1}^{-1}\cdots p_{n}q_{n}^{-1}\neq e_{G}
and set

X:=q_{1}p_{2}^{-1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P\quad\text{and}\quad Y:=\gamma^{-1}(\gamma(q_{1})\gamma(p_{2})^{-1}\dots\gamma(p_{n})^{-1}\gamma(p_{n})\gamma(P)).

We show that
X=Y=\emptyset
.
Then Proposition
3.13
yields

\lambda(\xi_{q_{1}})\lambda(\xi_{p_{2}})^{*}\cdots\lambda(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda(\xi_{q_{n}})=0,

and thus the contradiction
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}=0
.
First note that since
\gamma
is one-to-one on
P
and a group homomorphism we have that
X=Y
.
Secondly in order to show that
X=Y=\emptyset
, suppose that
X\neq\emptyset
so that there are
r,s\in P
such that
s=q_{1}p_{2}^{-1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r
and thus
\gamma(p_{1}s)=\gamma(r)
.
But as
\gamma
is one-to-one on
P
we have that
p_{1}s=r
and thus
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r=p_{1}s=r
giving the contradiction
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we derive that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
is independent of the group embedding, in the following sense.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Suppose that
P
admits two group embeddings
i_{G}\colon P\to G
and
i_{H}\colon P\to H
.
Then there exists a
*
-isomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{H}X);\xi_{i_{G}(p)}\mapsto\xi_{i_{H}(p)}.

In particular, there exists a Fell bundle isomorphism
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X\to{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{H}X
such that

[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{i_{G}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{1})\cdots i_{G}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{n})}\longrightarrow[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{i_{H}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{1})\cdots i_{H}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{n})},

isometrically for every
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
.

It transpires that the
*
-isomorphism passes down to the C*-algebras, namely

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{H}X)\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X).

In the generality that we work here, we see that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
may not admit a Wick ordering, i.e., it may not be densely spanned by
\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{q})^{*}
.
This happens for compactly aligned product systems over right LCM-semigroups
[
19
,
47
]
.
Product systems over right LCM semigroups generalize product systems over quasi-lattices in the sense of Fowler
[
27
]
.

Example 4.7
.

Let
P
be a right LCM semigroup in the sense of
[
51
]
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{J}}=\{pP\mid p\in P\}\cup\{\emptyset\}
.
A Fowler’s product system
X
over
P
is
non-degenerate
and
compactly aligned
if

(i)

there are associative multiplication rules so that
X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u_{p,q}}}{{\simeq}}X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
.

(ii)

if
pP\cap qP=wP
, then
i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{w}
for all
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{q}
.

Here we use the notation

i_{p}^{pq}(S)=u_{p,q}(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}})u_{p,q}^{*}\text{ for all }S\in{\mathcal{L}}X_{p}.

Property (i) implies that the left action of
A
on every
X_{p}
is non-degenerate.
Property (ii) implies that the Fock representation satisfies the
Nica-covariance
relation in the sense of
[
47
]
, i.e., for
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{q}
we have

\lambda(k_{p})\lambda(k_{q})=\begin{cases}\lambda(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))&\text{ if }pP\cap qP=wP,\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where we use
\lambda
for the induced representation on each
{\mathcal{K}}X_{r}
.
Note that by
[
19
, Proposition 2.4]
we have that
\lambda(i_{p}^{xp}(k_{p}))=\lambda(k_{p})
for all
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}X_{p}
and
x\in P\cap P^{-1}
, and so the above relations do not depend on the choice of the LCM.
Nica-covariance implements a Wick ordering on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{q})^{*}\mid p,q\in P\}.

Hence
\lambda(\xi_{p})\lambda(\xi_{q})^{*}\in[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e}
if and only if
p=q
.
We see that

\lambda(X_{q})\lambda(X_{q})^{*}\in\mathbf{K}_{pP,\lambda_{*}}\textup{ iff }pP=qq^{-1}P=qP.

Then
q=px
for
x\in P\cap P^{-1}
, and therefore by
[
19
, Proposition 2.4]
we get that

\lambda(X_{q})\lambda(X_{q})^{*}\subseteq[\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{x})\lambda(X_{x})^{*}\lambda(X_{p})^{*}]=[\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{p})^{*}].

Consequently, we derive that

\mathbf{K}_{pP,\lambda_{*}}=[\lambda(X_{p})\lambda(X_{p})^{*}].

It is worth noting that the compact alignment of
X
is in fact equivalent with
\lambda
satisfying the Nica-covariance relation.
This has been observed by Katsoulis in
[
37
, Proposition 3.2]
but the proof applies in this more general context as well, see
[
34
, Proposition 4.7]
.
The key point is that Nica-covariance and Fock-covariance coincide in this setting by
[
19
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Example 4.8
.

Let
X_{p}=\mathbb{C}
for every
p\in P
.
In this case we have that
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},\lambda_{\ast}}=\mathbb{C}\cdot E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}
for every
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
.
Laca–Sehnem
[

located the Fock-covariant representations of this trivial product system.
They show that the Fock-covariant C*-algebra

in this case is the universal C*-algebra generated by an isometric representation
\{v_{p}\mid p\in P\}
of
P
and projections
\{e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
with
e_{\emptyset}=0
such that:

\prod_{{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{F}}}(e_{{\mathbf{x}}}-e_{{\mathbf{y}}})=0\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=\bigcup_{{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathbf{y}},\textup{ finite }{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}.

Several semigroup C*-algebras were considered by Li
[
52
]
, and in particular the one obtained by the
constructible
representations.
We denote by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
the universal C*-algebra generated by an isometric representation
\{v_{p}\mid p\in P\}
of
P
and projections
\{e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
with
e_{\emptyset}=0
such that:

v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}=e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P,p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}.

It has been shown independently in
[
33
]
and
[
49
]
, that the canonical

-epimorphism from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)

{\mathcal{J}}
is independent.

It has been noted in
[

that a non-zero equivariant representation

of

descends to a non-zero Fock-covariant representation

of

.
The crux of the argument in
[

is that every equivariant isometric representation of

defines a partial action of

on the spectrum of its fixed point algebra.
Since the fixed point algebra of

has a smallest one such space, denoted by

This is equivalent to having that

for the canonical

We abstract this property of

Definition 4.9
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group

and let

be a product system over

.
We say that an equivariant representation

of

is

if it satisfies

t(A)\cap[t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}})]_{e}=(0),

for the canonical
*
-epimorphism
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
.

Fock-covariant representations are automatically covariant.
Moreover, if
t
is an equivariant representation of
X
, then Remark
2.3
implies that there exists an equivariant Fock-covariant representation
\dot{t}
and a canonical

-epimorphism
q_{t}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(\dot{t})
that makes the following diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{t}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(\dot{t})}

commutative, since

is induced, i.e.,

\ker q_{t}=t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}})=\left\langle t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}})\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}\right\rangle=\left\langle\ker q_{t}\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}\right\rangle.

We now show that covariance allows for injectivity to be preserved both-ways when inducing to Fock-covariant representations.
Therefore, if an injective Fock-covariant representation is terminal for a class of injective representations of

then those need to be covariant.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{t_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{t}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\dot{t}_{\ast}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(\dot{t})}

be the commutative diagram associated with an equivariant representation
t
of
X
.
Then
t
is injective and covariant if and only if
\dot{t}
is injective.

Proof.
.

Suppose that
t
is injective and covariant.
If
\dot{t}q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(a)=0
for
a\in A
, then
q_{t}t(a)=0
.
Hence
t(a)\in[\ker q_{t}]_{e}=[t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}})]_{e}
, and covariance yields
t(a)=0
.
Thus
a=0
as
t
is injective.
For the converse recall that
\lambda|_{A}
, and thus
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}|_{A}
, is injective.
Therefore, if
\dot{t}
is injective, then so is
\dot{t}q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}|_{A}
and hence
t
is injective.
Moreover, if
t(a)=t_{\ast}(y)
for
a\in A\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}(X)
and
y\in\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
, then

\dot{t}q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(a)=q_{t}t(a)=q_{t}t_{\ast}(y)=\dot{t}_{\ast}q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(y)=0,

and injectivity of
\dot{t}q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}|_{A}
gives

, as required.
∎

4.2.
Fixing the multiplication rules

Sims–Yeend
[
72
]
have recognized the following problem with
\lambda
.
For
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},\lambda_{\ast}}
and
\xi_{r}\in X_{r}
, we have
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\xi_{r}\in X_{r}
, and we can define the element
\lambda(b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\xi_{r})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
However,
\lambda(b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\xi_{r})
may not coincide with
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\lambda(\xi_{r})
.
For example, for
r\notin{\mathbf{x}}
we have that
\lambda(b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\xi_{r})=0
, but it may not be that
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\lambda(\xi_{r})\neq 0
, as it may happen that
rs\in{\mathbf{x}}
for some other
s\in P
(consider for example the case of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
).

Sims–Yeend
[
72
]
suggested a

Let

Let

Recall the definition of

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Then

\lambda(A)\bigcap c_{0}({\mathcal{L}}(X_{F})\mid\textup{finite }F\subseteq G)=(0).

Proof.
.

It suffices to show that
\Phi_{F}(\lambda(a))|_{X_{F}}\neq 0
for every finite
F\subseteq G
, so that
\left\|\Phi_{F}(a)|_{X_{F}}\right\|=\left\|a\right\|
.
We move in steps.
If
aI_{e\vee F}\neq(0)
, then there is nothing to show.
If
aI_{e\vee F}=(0)
, then there exists a
g_{1}\in F
such that
a\notin I_{K_{\{e,g_{1}\}}}
.
Thus there exists an
r_{1}\in P\cap g_{1}P
with
\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\neq 0
.
Set

F_{1}:=\{g\in F\mid K_{\{r_{1},g\}}\neq\emptyset\textup{ and }r_{1}\notin K_{\{r_{1},g\}}\}.

Then
g_{1}\notin F_{1}
and
F_{1}\subsetneq F
, satisfying

X_{r_{1}}I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F)}=X_{r_{1}}I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F_{1})}.

If
F_{1}=\emptyset
, then
I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F_{1})}=A
, and so
X_{r_{1}}I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F)}=X_{r_{1}}
.
Since
\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\neq 0
, we are done.
Likewise if
\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)|_{X_{r_{1}}I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F_{1})}}\neq 0
.

Suppose that
F_{1}\neq\emptyset
and that
\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)|_{X_{r_{1}}I_{r_{1}^{-1}(r_{1}\vee F_{1})}}=0
.
Then there exists a
g_{2}\in F_{1}
and a
\xi_{r_{1}}\in X_{r_{1}}
so that

\left\langle\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\xi_{r_{1}},\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\xi_{r_{1}}\right\rangle\notin I_{r_{1}^{-1}K_{\{r_{1},g_{2}\}}}.

Therefore there exists an
r_{2}\in K_{\{r_{1},g_{2}\}}
such that

\left\langle\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\xi_{r_{1}},\varphi_{r_{1}}(a)\xi_{r_{1}}\right\rangle\notin\ker\varphi_{r_{1}^{-1}r_{2}}.

From this we derive that

X_{r_{1}}\cdot X_{r_{1}^{-1}r_{2}}\subseteq X_{r_{1}r_{1}^{-1}r_{2}}=X_{r_{2}}.

This is the only place where the proof differs from that of
[
68
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Let now

F_{2}:=\{g\in F\mid K_{\{r_{2},g\}}\neq\emptyset\textup{ and }r_{2}\notin K_{\{r_{2},g\}}\},

for which we have that

and that

to reduct.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let

be a product system over

.
If

Proof.
.

Let

.
Our aim is to show that for
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite
F_{0}\subseteq G

\|\Phi_{F}\lambda_{\ast}(b)|_{X_{F}}\|<\varepsilon
for all finite
F\supseteq F_{0}

Recall that
\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\lambda_{\ast}}=(0)
by Proposition
4.4

Thus by Proposition
3.13

\varepsilon>0

In particular, we have that

\|t_{\ast}(b^{\prime})\|=\|t_{\ast}(b-b^{\prime})\|\leq\|b-b^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Let

For a fixed

Since

\left\|\eta\right\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}=\|\sum_{r\in P}t(\xi_{r}a_{r})^{*}t(\xi_{r}a_{r})\|=\|\sum_{r\in P}\left\langle\xi_{r}a_{r},\xi_{r}a_{r}\right\rangle_{A}\|=\left\|\xi\right\|_{X_{F}}^{2}.

Since

for all

\displaystyle t_{\ast}(\iota(\lambda_{\ast}(b^{\prime})\xi_{r}a_{r}))

\displaystyle=t_{\ast}(b^{\prime})t(\xi_{r}a_{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{\ast}(\iota(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}))t(\xi_{r}a_{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}\xi_{r}a_{r})\in\sum_{p}t(X_{p}).

Consequently

is in
\sum_{p}t(X_{p})

Thus we can apply injectivity of

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{F}(\lambda_{\ast}(b^{\prime}))\xi\|_{X_{F}}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda_{\ast}(b^{\prime})\xi\|_{{\mathcal{F}}X}=\||\sum_{j,r}\iota(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{j}})\xi_{r}a_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{{\mathcal{F}}X}

\displaystyle=\||\sum_{j,r}t_{\ast}\iota(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{j}})t_{\ast}(\xi_{r}a_{r})|^{1/2}\|_{Y_{t}}=\|t_{\ast}(b^{\prime})\eta\|_{Y_{t}}\leq\left\|t_{\ast}(b^{\prime})\right\|\cdot\left\|\eta\right\|_{Y_{t}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\left\|\xi\right\|_{X_{F}}.

Hence we derive that

for all

∎

[
68
]
.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
G
be the enveloping group of
P
and suppose that
\gamma\colon G\to P
is a group homomorphism that is injective on
P
.
If
r\in P
and
g\in G
satisfy
K_{\{r,g\}}\neq\emptyset
and
r\notin K_{\{r,g\}}
, then
K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}\neq\emptyset
and
\gamma(r)\notin K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}
.

Proof.
.

It is clear that with these assumptions we have that
K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}\neq\emptyset
and also there are
q_{1},q_{2}\in P
such that
rq_{1}=gq_{2}
with
q_{2}q_{1}^{-1}\notin P
.
If it were that
\gamma(r)\in K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}
then
\gamma(r)=\gamma(rq_{1}q_{2}^{-1}s)
for some
s\in P
, and thus that
\gamma(q_{2})=\gamma(sq_{1})
.
As
\gamma|_{P}
is one-to-one we get the contradiction
q_{2}q_{1}^{-1}=s\in P
.
∎

Although the details of this step are omitted from the proof of
[
68
, Lemma 3.9]
, we find that they elucidate the independence from the group.
Practically the elements of the group to be considered come from
Pq_{1}q_{2}^{-1}
with
q_{1}q_{2}^{-1}\notin P^{-1}
, and this subset of
P\cdot P^{-1}
cannot give non-trivial relations in a group generated by
P
.

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Suppose that
P
admits two group embeddings

and

.

which descends to

-isomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{H}X);\xi_{i_{G}(p)}\mapsto\xi_{i_{H}(p)}.

In particular, there exists a Fell bundle isomorphism
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X\to{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{H}X
such that

[A\times_{X}P]_{i_{G}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{1})\cdots i_{G}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{G}(q_{n})}\longrightarrow[A\times_{X}P]_{i_{H}(p_{1})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{1})\cdots i_{H}(p_{n})^{-1}i_{H}(q_{n})},

.

Let
G
be the enveloping group of
P
and

be the canonical epimorphism that fixes

Let us denote by

{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc},e_{G}}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc},e_{H}}
.
Recall that these ideals are kernels of the maps

[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e_{G}}\longrightarrow[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{G}}\longrightarrow{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\prod\limits_{\textup{fin }F\subseteq G}{\mathcal{L}}(X_{F})$}\left/\,\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$c_{0}({\mathcal{L}}(X_{F})\mid\textup{fin }F\subseteq G)$}\right.},

and

[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e_{H}}\longrightarrow[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{H}}\longrightarrow{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\prod\limits_{\textup{fin }\gamma(F)\subseteq H}{\mathcal{L}}(X_{\gamma(F)})$}\left/\,\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$c_{0}({\mathcal{L}}(X_{\gamma(F)})\mid\textup{fin }\gamma(F)\subseteq H)$}\right.}.

By Proposition
4.5
we have that
[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{G}}=[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e_{H}}
, and thus it suffices to show that

for every finite

If
K_{\{r,g\}}=\emptyset
, or if

If

\displaystyle I_{\gamma(r)^{-1}K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\gamma(s)\in K_{\{\gamma(r),\gamma(g)\}}}\ker\phi_{\gamma(r)^{-1}\gamma(s)}=\bigcap_{s\in K_{\{r,g\}}}\ker\phi_{r^{-1}s}=I_{r^{-1}K_{\{r,g\}}}.

*
-epimorphism

5.
Co-universality

5.1.
Boundary represenations

Our goal is to show that the reduced strongly covariant representation is terminal for the class of equivariant injective covariant represenations (and thus also for the corresponding Fock-covariant).
The notion of terminal representations appears in several contexts and constructions of C*-algebras, sometimes under disguise.
Let us set a general framework and provide some examples.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a class of
*
-representations of a C*-algebra.
We say that
\Phi
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-boundary
if and only if
\Phi
is a member of the class
{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\Phi
factors through every member
\Psi
of the class
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Boundary representations generate unique

We will limit the amount of detail in the following examples.
The interested reader may refer to the cited works for more information.

Example 5.2
.

Let

be C*-algebras.
If

-algebraic representations of

*
-representations of

5.2.
Co-universality for product systems through the C*-envelope

We will now show how the C*-envelope of the cosystem on the Fock tensor algebra can be used to answer the co-universality problem for covariant representations.
This first proof we give follows the strategy of

Recall that Fell’s absorption principle for groups requires an intertwining unitary.

In the case of

Proposition 5.14
.

Let

If

Proof.
.

A straightforward computation for
\xi_{p},\eta_{q}\in{\mathcal{F}}X
and injectivity of
t
gives that

\left\langle W\xi_{p}\otimes h_{p},W\xi_{q}\otimes h_{q}\right\rangle=\delta_{p,q}\left\langle h_{p},t(\xi_{p})^{*}t(\xi_{q})h_{q}\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi_{p}\otimes h_{p},\xi_{q}\otimes h_{q}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes_{A}H},

Thus
W
is a well-defined isometry.
We then compute

W(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes I_{H})(\xi_{q}\otimes h)=t(\xi_{p}\xi_{q})h\otimes\delta_{pq}=(t(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})W(\xi_{q}\otimes h)

as required, and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
t
is an equivariant representation of
X
that is injective on
A
, then there exists a completely contractive homomorphism

\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{t(X_{p})\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X);t(\xi_{p})\mapsto\lambda(\xi_{p}).

Proof.
.

First we construct a completely contractive map

\Phi\colon\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{t(X_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X);t(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mapsto\lambda(\xi_{p}).

Let
W
be the intertwining isometry from Proposition
5.14
with final space densely spanned by
t(\xi_{q})h\otimes\delta_{q}
.
Hence
WW^{*}
is an invariant subspace for
\operatorname{alg}\{t(X_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mid p\in P\}
.
Moreover, Proposition
5.14
yields

(t(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})|_{WW^{*}}=W\left(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes I_{H}\right)W^{*},

and injectivity of
t
on
A
implies that the map

\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{H}\colon{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X\otimes_{A}H);S\mapsto S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{H}

is a faithful
*
-homomorphism
[
50
, p. 42]
.
Thus we can define the following completely contractive homomorphism

\Phi\colon\operatorname{alg}\{t(X_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mid p\in P\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|_{WW^{*}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{alg}\{W(\lambda(X_{p})\otimes I_{H})W^{*}\mid p\in P\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\otimes I_{H}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},

so that
\Phi(t(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})=\lambda(\xi_{p})
.

Next consider the unital completely positive map

\phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(P));u_{g}\mapsto\lambda_{g}\mapsto P_{\ell^{2}(P)}\lambda_{g}|_{\ell^{2}(P)},

which is multiplicative on the subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
generated by the
u_{p}
for
p\in P
, with
\phi(u_{p})=V_{p}
.
Then the map
\Phi({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)\delta_{t}
for the coaction
\delta_{t}
of
t
satisfies

\Phi({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)\delta_{t}t(\xi_{p})=\Phi({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)(t(\xi_{p})\otimes u_{p})=\Phi(t(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})=\lambda(\xi_{p}),

and gives the required completely contractive homomorphism.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain a co-action by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
on the Fock tensor algebra where the target space can be over any equivariant injective representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
The following is the analogue of the key result
[

, Proposition 4.5]
.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
\Psi
is an equivariant
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that is injective on

, then there exists a completely isometric homomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P);\lambda(\xi_{p})\mapsto\Psi\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p}.

In particular,
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)
is a C*-cover of the cosystem
({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)
.

Proof.
.

First we note that
\Psi\lambda\otimes V\colon\xi_{p}\mapsto\Psi\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p}
defines a representation of
X
.
Moreover it is equivariant as
\Psi
is so.
Let
\Phi
be the completely contractive homomorphism constructed in the proof of Proposition
5.15
, so that
\Phi(\Psi\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})=\lambda(\xi_{p})
.
Next consider the unital completely positive map

For the coaction

on

Therefore, the map

is a completely contractive left inverse for

is completely isometric.
On the other hand,

is a completely contractive left inverse of
\Psi
, and thus
\Psi
is completely isometric.
∎

We next show that the C*-envelope of the cosystem on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
is the required terminal object.

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group

be a product system over

.
Then

.

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)\simeq A\times_{X,\lambda}P.

.

{\mathcal{F}}_{\operatorname{c}}
be the class of equivariant injective covariant representations of

.
By definition the relations in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
pass to
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)
, and so the latter is Fock-covariant and thus it is indeed in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\operatorname{c}}
.
Now let
t
be an element of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\operatorname{c}}
.
By Proposition
4.10
we can assume without loss of generality that

is actually Fock-covariant.
By Remark
2.2

where

is the induced Fell bundle inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)

Let

\lambda_{t}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(t_{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X)).

be the canonical
*

admits a coaction of
G
.
By Proposition
5.16
we get that the map
\Psi
defines a C*-cover of the cosystem

, and thus there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism

\Psi^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\longrightarrow({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta).

Hence we get the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

For the second part, by Proposition

we have that

5.3.
Co-universality for product systems through strong covariance

We can have an independent proof of the co-universality result that goes through the Fell bundle theory.
We have all tools to implement the strategy of Carlsen–Larsen–Sims–Vittadello
[
9
]
and get the existence of the co-universal representation through
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
We also provide a Fell bundle route for Proposition
5.16
and complete the connection with the C*-envelope.

In Proposition
5.16
we saw how a Fell’s absorption principle induces that representations of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
that are injective on
A
are automatically completely isometric.
This works also in reverse by using the Fell bundle technique of
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
t
is an equivariant representation of
X
that is injective on
A
, then there exists a completely contractive homomorphism

\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{t(X_{p})\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X);t(\xi_{p})\mapsto\lambda(\xi_{p}).

Proof.
.

Let
Y_{t}
be the Hilbert module associated to
t
from Subsection
3.3
, so that

\left\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\right\rangle_{Y}:=E_{t_{\ast}}(\eta_{1}^{*}\eta_{2})\text{ for all }\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\sum_{p}t(X_{p}),

where
E_{t_{\ast}}
is the conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
.
Let
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let

t({\xi}^{ij}):=\sum_{p\in P}t(\xi^{ij}_{p})\quad\text{and}\quad{\eta}^{k}:=\sum_{q\in P}t(\eta^{k}_{q})

be finitely supported vectors in
\sum_{p}t(X_{p})
.
Since
t(\xi^{ik})\eta_{k}\in\sum_{p}t(X_{p})
, by Bessel’s inequality of Corollary
3.14
we have

\displaystyle\|[t({\xi}^{ij})][\eta^{k}]\|\leq\left\|[t({\xi}^{ij})]\right\|\cdot\|[\eta^{k}]\|.

This yields a well-defined completely contractive map

L\colon\operatorname{alg}\{t(X_{p})\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{B}}Y

by
bounded
operators on
Y
, such that
L(t(\xi_{p}))t(\eta_{q})=t(\xi_{p}\eta_{q})
.
In particular we see that this map takes values in
{\mathcal{L}}Y
with

L(t(\xi_{p}))^{*}t(\eta_{q})=\begin{cases}t(\xi_{p}^{*}\eta_{q})&\text{if }q\in pP,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Next we note that
Y_{t}
is unitarily equivalent to the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}X
by the map

U(\sum_{p}t(\xi_{p}))=\sum_{p}\xi_{p}.

Indeed, first define
U
on finite sums; equivariance of
t
implies that
\{t(X_{p})\}_{p\in P}
is a family of linearly independent spaces in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
, and injectivity of
t
yields that
U
is well defined.
Moreover injectivity of
t
gives that
U
is an isometry onto
\sum_{p}t(X_{p})
.
Continuity of
E_{t_{\ast}}
allows to extend the map
U
to a unitary from
Y_{t}
onto
{\mathcal{F}}X
.

Hence we deduce a completely contractive map

\operatorname{ad}_{U}L\colon\operatorname{alg}\{t(X_{p})\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X),

such that

\operatorname{ad}_{U}L(t(\xi_{p}))\xi_{r}=Ut(\xi_{p})t(\xi_{r})=\xi_{p}\xi_{r}=\lambda(\xi_{p})\xi_{r},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.19
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
\Psi
is an equivariant
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that is injective on
A
, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)
is a C*-cover of the cosystem
({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta)
.
Moreover, there exists a completely isometric homomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P);\lambda(\xi_{p})\mapsto\Psi\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p}.

Proof.
.

By applying Proposition
5.18
for
t=\Psi\lambda|_{X}
we have a completely contractive map

\operatorname{ad}_{U}L\colon\operatorname{alg}\{\Psi\lambda(X_{p})\mid p\in P\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},

such that
\operatorname{ad}_{U}L(\Psi\lambda(\xi_{p}))=\lambda(\xi_{p})
.
Consequently
\operatorname{ad}_{U}L
is a completely contractive left inverse to the completely contractive map
\Psi|_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}}
, making the latter a complete isometry.

For the second part, it suffices to show this for
\Psi=\lambda
.
As in Proposition
5.16
, let the isometry

W\colon{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(P)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(P);\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{s}\mapsto\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{rs}.

It has final space densely spanned by
\{\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{rs}\mid r,s\in P;\xi_{r}\in X_{r}\}
, and thus invariant for
\operatorname{alg}\{\lambda(X_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mid p\in P\}
.
Moreover we directly verify that

W(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes I_{\ell^{2}(P)})(\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{s})=\xi_{p}\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{prs}=(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})W(\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{s}).

Hence we can define a completely contractive map
\Phi
by

\Phi\colon\operatorname{alg}\{\lambda(X_{p})\otimes V_{p}\mid p\in P\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|_{WW^{*}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{alg}\{W(\lambda(X_{p})\otimes I_{\ell^{2}(P)})W^{*}\mid p\in P\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},

so that
\Phi(\lambda(\xi_{p})\otimes V_{p})=\lambda(\xi_{p})
.
It follows that
\Phi
has a completely contractive left inverse given by
({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)\delta
, for the unital completely positive map
\phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\to{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(P))
with
\phi(u_{g})=P_{\ell^{2}(P)}\lambda_{g}|_{\ell^{2}(P)}
, and the coaction
\delta
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
Thus
\Phi
is completely isometric.
∎

We can apply the Fell bundle arguments that appear in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
.
There is a key underlying idea that can be traced back to Katayama
[
36
, Proposition 11]
and Raeburn
[
63
, Lemma 3.1]
.
It relies on the fact that any coaction induces a reduced coaction to a quotient by an appropriate ideal; and thus that quotient is minimal for the quotient Fell bundle, hence receiving also from the original Fell bundle.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Then
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
is a boundary representation for the equivariant injective covariant representations of
X
.
Moreover, we have that

A\times_{X,\lambda}P\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+},G,\delta).

Proof.
.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.17
we can reduce to Fock-covariant representations.
Hence, without loss of generality, let
t
be an equivariant injective Fock-covariant representation of
X
.
Recall from Proposition
4.17
that

\ker t_{\ast}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}\subseteq\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e},

for the canonical
*
-epimorphism
q_{\operatorname{sc}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\to A\times_{X}P
that fixes every
X_{p}
.
Remark
2.3
yields

t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}})\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}=t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}),

since
q_{\operatorname{sc}}
is equivariant.
Let
q_{J}
be the quotient map on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
by the ideal

J:=\left\langle t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}})\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}\right\rangle.

The goal is to show that

\ker q_{J}t_{\ast}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{g}=\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{g}\text{ for all }g\in G.

It suffices to show this for
g=e_{G}
, and then the equality for all fibres follows by a standard argument.
With that in hand we deduce that the Fell bundle defined in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)/J
is isomorphic to the strongly covariant bundle
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X
.
Consequently, we obtain an equivariant
*
-epimorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)/J\longrightarrow A\times_{X,\lambda}P.

Towards this end first assume that
x\in\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}
.
Then

t_{\ast}(x)\in t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e})=t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}})\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}\subseteq J,

and so
x\in\ker q_{J}t_{\ast}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}
.
The reverse inclusion will follow by applying Proposition
4.17
on
q_{J}t_{\ast}
.
Towards this end we need to show that
q_{J}t_{\ast}
is injective on
\iota(A)
.
Let
a\in A
so that
q_{J}t_{\ast}(\iota(a))=0
, and so
t_{\ast}(\iota(a))\in J\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}
.
Since

\displaystyle J\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}

\displaystyle\subseteq t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}})\bigcap[\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)]_{e}=t_{\ast}(\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}),

there exists a
y\in\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}
such that
t_{\ast}(\iota(a))=t_{\ast}(y)
.
We deduce that

\iota(a)-y\in\ker t_{\ast}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e}\subseteq\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}\bigcap[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{e},

where we applied Proposition
4.17
for the injective Fock-covariant representation
t
.
It follows that
\iota(a)=(\iota(a)-y)+y\in\ker q_{\operatorname{sc}}
, and thus Proposition
4.16
yields
a=0
as required.

For the second part, Proposition
4.16
and Proposition
5.16
give a canonical
*
-epimorphism

A\times_{X,\lambda}P\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X),G,\delta)

that fixes
X
.
On the other hand, the co-universal property of
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
provides a canonical
*
-epimorphism in the other direction giving the required
*
-isomorphism.
∎

5.4.
Applications and remarks

We close with a set of immediate applications and questions that are related to the theory.
First we observe that co-universality of
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
implies a gauge-invariant uniqueness theorem.

Corollary 5.21

(GIUT)
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
t_{\ast}
be a representation of
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
Then
t_{\ast}
is faithful if and only if
t
is an equivariant injective representation of
X
.

Proof.
.

The forward direction is immediate as
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
shares all these properties.
For the converse, if
t
satisfies these properties then co-universality of
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
gives an inverse to
t_{\ast}
.
∎

One may compare with the following corollary, which is a consequence of the properties of
A\times_{X}P
.

Corollary 5.22

(GIUT II)
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
t
be a strongly covariant representation of
X
.
Then there is a canonical
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\to A\times_{X,\lambda}P
if and only if
t
is injective and equivariant by a normal coaction.

Proof.
.

The forward direction is immediate as
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
shares all these properties.
For the converse by universality
t_{\ast}
defines a
*
-representation
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X
.
Since
t
is injective we have that
t_{\ast}
injective on
[{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}_{G}X]_{e}
and thus by Remark
2.2
there is a canonical
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\to A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
∎

Recall that by construction we have a canonical
*
-epimorphism
q_{\operatorname{sc}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\longrightarrow A\times_{X}P
.
By Remark
2.2
it descends to a canonical
*
-epimorphism

q_{\operatorname{sc},\lambda}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\longrightarrow A\times_{X,\lambda}P.

By construction
\ker q_{\operatorname{sc},\lambda}
contains
\lambda_{\ast}({\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}})
.
In particular, due to the (GIUT II), the canonical
*
-epimorphism

{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{${\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)$}\left/\,\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$\lambda_{\ast}({\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}})$}\right.}\longrightarrow A\times_{X,\lambda}P

is faithful if and only if it inherits a normal coaction from
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
The reason we are interested in this quotient in the Fock representation is because the ideal
\lambda_{\ast}(I_{\operatorname{sc}})
has been oftenly recognized to give a boundary space.
This still happens in many cases.
The following has been observed for right LCM-semigroups in
[
19
]
.

Corollary 5.23

(Fock boundary quotient)
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
G
is exact then
\ker q_{\operatorname{sc},\lambda}=\lambda_{\ast}({\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}})
, i.e., there is a canonical
*
-isomorphism

{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{${\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)$}\left/\,\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{$\lambda_{\ast}({\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}})$}\right.}\simeq A\times_{X,\lambda}P.

Proof.
.

Recall that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
admits a normal coaction and that
\lambda_{\ast}({\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}})
is an induced ideal.
Exactness of
G
implies that the induced coaction on the quotient is normal as well.
∎

Hence under exactness we have a hands-on understanding of the algebraic structure of the boundary quotient.

Remark 5.26

(Fock tensor algebra)
.

We have seen that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
plays a central role in the co-universality results.
It is a direct generalization of Popescu’s noncommutative disc algebra
[
61
]
which has been under significant study.
There are plenty of directions one can go from here starting with identifying its completely contractive representations as in
[
55
]
.
The form of the strong covariance relations may also shed light into hyperrigidity questions, which is resolved by Katsoulis and Ramsey for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}

[
41
]
.
As they have shown in
[
40
]
, such a result will then direct feed back to Takai duality and the Hao-Ng problem.
Finally, there is a lot of work for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
in terms of the w*-analogue of the Fock tensor algebra.
Questions include reflexivity, hyperreflixivity as well as a weak*-calculus, with the list being too long to include it here.

Induced Monoidal structure from the functor
Neha Gupta, Pradip kumar

Abstract
. Let ℬ \mathcal{B} be a subcategory of a given category 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . Let ℬ \mathcal{B} has monoidal structure. In this article, we discuss when can one extend the monoidal structure of ℬ \mathcal{B} to 𝒟 \mathcal{D} such that ℬ \mathcal{B} becomes a sub monoidal category of monoidal category 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . Examples are discussed, and in particular, in an example of loop space, we elaborated all results discussed in this article.

18D10,19D23, 55P35

1.
Introduction

We start from a few examples of a category
\mathcal{B}

(1)

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the category of one dimensional closed oriented manifolds without boundary and arrows as the oriented cobordism classes. It has monoidal structure given by disjoint union and empty set, see
[
3
]
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the category whose objects are the oriented cobordism classes between objects of
\mathcal{B}
with arrows defined in a special way (details are discussed in the example
3.3
).

(2)

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{R}
with usual tensor product of vector spaces. If
\mathcal{D}
be the category having objects as loop spaces and morphisms defined in a special way (details are discussed in the section 5).

For both these setups, we ask a natural question, that from
\mathcal{B}
, can we get a monoidal structure on the category
\mathcal{D}
. In this article we discussed about this type of question. More precisely, in theorem
4.6
, we proved that under certain conditions, we can provide a monoidal structure on a category
\mathcal{D}
from a given monoidal structure on some category
\mathcal{B}

When
\mathcal{B}
is a subcategory, the induced monoidal structure (as in theorem
4.6
) on
\mathcal{D}
, from
\mathcal{B}
is the extension of the monoidal structure in the sense that
\mathcal{B}
becomes the monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
. This extension is not same as the graded extension of the monoidal category discussed in
[
1
]
. In
[
4
]
, Ponto Kate and Shulman Michael have discussed the concept of a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor between two monoidal categories; and using such a functor we have got an induced monoidal structure on the category
\mathcal{D}
. Our article is based on the discussion in
[
4
]
.

In section 2, we revise the basics about the monoidal category, dual objects and strong lax symmetric monoidal functor. Section 3 and 4 discuss the induced tensor with its various properties and examples. In the last section 5, we did explicit calculation for the case of loop space.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Monoidal category

We first set the terminology that we use throughout the article which can be found in
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
etc. We begin with a few terms from category theory. A
monoidal

category
is a collection of data
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I},a,\lambda,\rho)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a category,
\otimes
is a functor from
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{C}
called the tensor
product, an associator
a
for
\otimes
which is a family of isomorphisms

(1)

a_{X,Y,Z}:(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\to X\otimes(Y\otimes Z),

an unit object
\mathbb{I}
of
\mathcal{C}
, and a left unitor
\lambda
and a right unitor
\rho
with respect to
\mathbb{I}
which are also a family of isomorphisms

(2)

\lambda_{X}:\mathbb{I}\otimes X\to X

(3)

\rho_{X}:X\otimes\mathbb{I}\to X

such that these isomorphisms
(a,\lambda,\rho)
satisfy certain coherence conditions (for details we refer
[
2
]
). To name, these conditions are the Pentagon identity and the Triangle identities. Note that the existence of the associator ensures that the tensor product
\otimes
is associative up to isomorphism and the existence of the unitors ensure that
\mathbb{I}
serves as a unit for
\otimes
, up to isomorphism.

Let
X
and
Y
be objects in
\mathcal{C}
. We say
X
has a
left dual

Y
(or
Y
has a
right dual

X
) if there
is

\cdot

a morphism
ev:Y\otimes X\to\mathbb{I}
(called evaluation), and

\cdot

a morphism
coev:\mathbb{I}\to X\otimes Y
(called coevaluation)

such that the compositions

\begin{CD}X@>{\lambda^{-1}_{X}}>{}>\mathbb{I}\otimes X@>{coev\otimes\text{Id}_{X}}>{}>(X\otimes Y)\otimes X@>{a_{X,Y,X}}>{}>X\otimes(Y\otimes X)@>{\text{Id}_{X}\otimes ev}>{}>X\otimes\mathbb{I}@>{\rho_{X}}>{}>X\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}Y@>{\rho^{-1}_{Y}}>{}>\mathbb{I}\otimes Y@>{\text{Id}_{Y}\otimes coev}>{}>Y\otimes(X\otimes Y)@>{a^{-1}_{Y,X,Y}}>{}>(Y\otimes X)\otimes Y@>{ev\otimes\text{Id}_{Y}}>{}>\mathbb{I}\otimes Y@>{\lambda_{Y}}>{}>Y\end{CD}

are both identity. If
X
has a left [right] dual, we say that
X
is left [right] dualizable, If the left and right duals are isomorphic in
\mathcal{C}
, we say
X
is
dualizable
, and in general the dual of
X
is denoted as
X^{*}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be symmetric monoidal categories. There is a special functor which takes dualizable object in
\mathcal{C}
to dualizable object in
\mathcal{D}
. Below we give the precise definition of such a functor and recall required properties (for detail we refer to
[
4
]
).

Definition 2.1

(Lax Symmetric monoidal functor)
.

Lax symmetric monoidal functor between symmetric monoidal categories consists of a functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and natural transformations

c:\mathcal{F}(M)\otimes\mathcal{F}(N)\to\mathcal{F}(M\otimes N)

i:\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}})

satisfying appropriate coherence axioms.

\mathcal{F}
is normal if
i
is an isomorphism and
\mathcal{F}
is strong if both
c
and
i
are isomorphism.

Proposition 2.2

(Prop.6.1,
[
4
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be normal lax symmetric monoidal functor, let
M\in Ob(\mathcal{C})
be dualizable with dual
M^{*}
and assume that
c:\mathcal{F}(M)\otimes\mathcal{F}(M^{*})\to\mathcal{F}(M\otimes M^{*})
is an isomorphism then
\mathcal{F}(M)
is dualizable with dual
\mathcal{F}(M^{*})
.

3.
Monoidal structure on Image of
\mathcal{F}

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two categories where
\mathcal{C}
has a monoidal structure given by
(\otimes,a,\lambda,\rho)
. Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor, then
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
which is the collection of the images of all objects and morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
under
\mathcal{F}
becomes a subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
. With this set up we wish to give a monoidal structure on
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
. If
\mathcal{F}
satisfies certain conditions, which we discuss below, then the category
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
has monoidal structure induced from
\mathcal{C}
.

Let the functor
\mathcal{F}
satifies following conditions: for any pair of objects
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}
, such that whenever we have

(4)

\mathcal{F}(a_{1})=\mathcal{F}(a_{2})\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(a_{3})=\mathcal{F}(a_{4})

then

\mathcal{F}(a_{1}\otimes a_{3})=\mathcal{F}(a_{2}\otimes a_{4})

We have similar conditions on morphisms also. For any pair
f,f^{\prime}
and
g,g^{\prime}
of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, such that

(5)

\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{F}(f^{\prime})\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(g)=\mathcal{F}(g^{\prime})

then, we have

\mathcal{F}(f\otimes g)=\mathcal{F}(f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime})

We use the notation
\mathcal{B}
for
\text{Im}{(\mathcal{F})}
and we define the monoidal functor
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathcal{B}
where,

\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{\mathcal{B}}\times\mathcal{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathcal{\mathcal{B}}

as follows: on objects,

\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}(b_{1},b_{2}):=\mathcal{F}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})

where
b_{1}=\mathcal{F}(a_{1})
and
b_{2}=\mathcal{F}(a_{2})
. The condition (4) on functor ensures that this is well defined on objects. On morphisms,

\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}(f_{1},f_{2}):=\mathcal{F}(h_{1}\otimes h_{2})

where
f_{1}=\mathcal{F}(h_{1})
and
f_{2}=\mathcal{F}(h_{2})
. Condition (5) will make sure that this is also well defined as a map. Compatibility of
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
with composition in
\mathcal{B}
will be followed from the compatibility of the tensor
\otimes
with composition in
\mathcal{C}
. Again, the compatibility of
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
with identity maps of
\mathcal{B}
will require only the compatibility of the tensor
\otimes
with identity maps of
\mathcal{C}
. Explicitly, we want to show that the equation
\text{Id}_{b_{1}\otimes b_{2}}=\text{Id}_{b_{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}\text{Id}_{b_{2}}
is true in
\mathcal{B}
where
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are objects in
\mathcal{B}
, such that
b_{\mathbb{I}}=\mathcal{F}(a_{1})
and
b_{2}=\mathcal{F}(a_{2})
, for some objects
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
b_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}b_{2}=\mathcal{F}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})
and the easy calculations shown below gives the result.

\displaystyle\text{Id}_{b_{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}\text{Id}_{b_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(a_{1}})\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(a_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\text{Id}_{a_{1}})\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{F}(\text{Id}_{a_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(\text{Id}_{a_{1}}\otimes\text{Id}_{a_{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(\text{Id}_{a_{1}\otimes a_{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Id}_{b_{1}\otimes b_{2}}.

This defines a tensor product
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathcal{B}
. For
\mathcal{B}
to have a monoidal structure we want the left and right unitors as well as the associator in
\mathcal{B}
. These are nothing but
\mathcal{F}(a)
,
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
,
\mathcal{F}(\rho)
where
a
,
\lambda
and
\rho
are the respective left unitor, right unitor and associator of
\mathcal{C}
and they will satisfy the triangle and pentagon axioms since they are true in
\mathcal{C}
. Thus we have proved the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a functor from a monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
to a category
\mathcal{D}
that satisfies the following conditions

(1)

for any objects
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathcal{F}(a_{1})=\mathcal{F}(a_{2})\;\;and\;\;\mathcal{F}(a_{3})=\mathcal{F}(a_{4})
, then we have
\mathcal{F}(a_{1}\otimes a_{3})=\mathcal{F}(a_{2}\otimes a_{4})
,

(2)

for any morphisms
f,g,f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}
if

\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{F}(f^{\prime})=h;\;\;\mathcal{F}(g)=\mathcal{F}(g^{\prime})=k\text{ then, we have}

\mathcal{F}(f\otimes g)=\mathcal{F}(f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime})
.

Then
\mathcal{F}
induces a monoidal structure on
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a monoidal category with tensor
\otimes_{D}
and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is also a monoidal category with obvious tensor, defined component-wise. Define a functor

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
such that it associates to each object
(X,Y)
in
\mathcal{C}
to
X
in
\mathcal{D}
and to each morphism
(f,g)\in Hom(X_{1},Y_{1}),(X_{2},Y_{2}))
to
f\in Hom(X_{1},Y_{1}).

One can think of the functor
\mathcal{F}
as a projection onto the first component. Further,
\mathcal{F}
also satisfies both the condition of theorem
3.1
. Therefore we have a monoidal structure
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, both monoidal structures
\otimes_{D}
and
\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}
are same.

Example 3.3
.

(See,
[
3
]
)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the category having 1-dimensional compact oriented manifold without boundary as objects along with the empty set. Morphism between any two objects
X
and
Y
is the oriented cobordism class. We write a representative of this class and also the class as
(W,X,Y)
where
W
denotes a 2-dimensional oriented cobordism between
X
and
Y
. Composition of morphism is given by gluing the representatives of the two cobordism classes. Kock in
[
3
]
has discussed that the result of this gluing does not depend on the actual cobordisms chosen; rather only their class. The class of cylinders is the identity for the composition. Monoidal structure on
\mathcal{B}
is the disjoint union of the objects and the of the cobordisms. The Tensor between two morphisms
(W_{1},X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(W_{2},X_{2},Y_{2})
is a cobordism class
(W_{1}\sqcup W_{2},X_{1}\sqcup X_{2},Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2})
. The unit object of
\mathcal{B}
is the empty set.

Consider the product category
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}
and call it as
\mathcal{B}
. Use the tensor on
\mathcal{A}
to give a monoidal structure on
\mathcal{B}
. It is defined in the obvious way as component-wise. In particular, the unit object here is
(\emptyset,\emptyset)
.

We now define a category
\mathcal{D}
with objects as oriented 2-dimensional cobordism classes, which have oriented cobordisms between the objects of
\mathcal{A}
but without holes only. As before, we write an object of
\mathcal{D}
as
(W,X,Y)
. A morphism between two objects
(W_{1},X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(W_{2},X_{2},Y_{2})
in
\mathcal{D}
is a pair of morphisms in
\mathcal{A}
, one between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
and the other between
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. Since a morphism of
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
so composition of morphism is the pair wise gluing of the cobordism classes.

We define a functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{D}
such that any object
(X,Y)
in
\mathcal{B}
is taken to the unique cobordism class of oriented 2-dimensional cobordisms between
X
to
Y
(without holes).
\mathcal{F}
is identity on morphisms.

Clearly this functor is fully faithful. That is to say that
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
is whole of
\mathcal{D}
. Further, this functor satisfies the two conditions of the above theorem
3.1
. Hence we get an induced tensor on
\mathcal{D}
. We discuss this induced functor explicitly later in examples
4.3
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
be a functor as in theorem
3.1
. If
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
is symmetric then the category
(\text{Im}(\mathcal{F}),\otimes_{\mathcal{F}})
is symmetric as well.

Proof.

We need to give a symmetric structure in
\mathcal{D}
and this will be simply be the image of the symmetry of
\mathcal{C}
under the functor
\mathcal{F}
. Explicitly, if
D_{1},D_{2}\in Ob(Im(\mathcal{F}))
, then there are
C_{1},C_{2}\in Ob(C)
such that
\mathcal{F}(C_{1})=D_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}(C_{2})=D_{2}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is symmetric, therefore there is a symmetric map, say
s_{C_{1},C_{2}}:C_{1}\otimes C_{2}\to C_{2}\otimes C_{1}
which is an isomorphism. Consider
\mathcal{F}(s_{C_{1},C_{2}}):\mathcal{F}(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})\to\mathcal{F}(C_{2}\otimes C_{1})
.
As
\mathcal{F}
is a functor therefore
\mathcal{F}(s_{C_{1},C_{2}})
is an isomorphism between
D_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}D_{2}:=\mathcal{F}(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})
and
D_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}D_{1}:=\mathcal{F}(D_{1}\otimes D_{1})
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

A functor
\mathcal{F}
between a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
to
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
where
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
has the induced monoidal structure from
\mathcal{C}
and which satisfies the conditions of theorem (3.1) is a strong lax symmetric monoidal functor.

Proof.

This is direct from the definition of the induced monoidal structure on
\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})
described in the theorem
3.1
, which is,
D_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}D_{2}=\mathcal{F}(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})
. Thus by definition itself we get

\mathcal{F}(C_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{F}(C_{2})=\mathcal{F}(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})

And

\mathbb{I}_{\text{Im}(\mathcal{F})}=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}}).

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
A\in Ob(\mathcal{C})
is dualizable in
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
, then
\mathcal{F}(A)
is dualizable in
(\text{Im}(\mathcal{F}),\otimes_{\mathcal{F}})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a strong lax symmetric monoidal functor, then by proposition
2.2
, we have the result directly.
∎

4.
Producut category and Base

4.1.
Product Category over Index set
\Lambda

Let
\Lambda
be an indexing set. Abusing the use of notation, we define an indexing category, denoted by
\Lambda
, with objects as elements of the indexing set
\Lambda
, and morphisms as

\text{Hom}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\begin{cases}\{\text{Id}_{\lambda}\}&\text{if }\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Composition is trivial, which will again be the identity map of the source object.
In particular, we will be considering finite sets as well as countably infinite sets (like set of natural numbers) as a category wherever we talk of a product category in the rest of the paper.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a monoidal category with a unit object
\mathbb{I}
. We define the
product

\Pi_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{B})
of a category
\mathcal{B}
over an indexing category
\Lambda
as a collection of functors
\mathcal{F}
from
\Lambda
to
\mathcal{B}
with
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
being an object in
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{F}(\text{Hom}(\lambda,\lambda))=\{\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}\}
. The collection is non empty since
C
(the constant functor) that maps every object in
\Lambda
to the identity
\mathbb{I}
in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}(\text{Id}_{\lambda})
is the identity map of the unit
\mathbb{I}
in
\mathcal{B}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The
product category

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{B}
over an indexing set
\Lambda
is defined as follows:

(1)

an object in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a functor from
\Lambda\to\mathcal{B}
,

(2)

a morphism between two objects
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F}\in Ob(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
is a natural transformation
\eta:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
which gives a family of morphisms
\eta_{\lambda}:\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
in
\mathcal{B}
,

(3)

composition of morphism is the vertical composition of two natural transformations. That is, if
\eta\in Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
and
\zeta\in Hom(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
are two vertically composable natural transformations, then the composition
\zeta\circ\eta
is a natural transformation between
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
given by
\zeta\circ\eta(\lambda)=\zeta(\lambda)\circ\eta(\lambda)
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda.

In particular, if the indexing set
\Lambda
has only two elements, then as expected, the product category
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{B}
over
\Lambda
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
.

Example 4.1
.

Finite product. In case the indexing set is a finite set, then the product category
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
of any category
\mathcal{B}
would be finite copies of
\mathcal{B}
.

4.2.
Base

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a category. We define a base for
\mathcal{D}
as the data
(\mathcal{B},*,\mathcal{G})
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a category, the functor
*
(we call as the pasting functor) from
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
, the decomposition functor (here
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
is the product category of
\mathcal{B}
over some indexing set, say
\Lambda
) such that
*\mathcal{G}\cong\text{Id}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
\mathcal{B}
in base, we call as base category for
\mathcal{D}
. This is like asking for a representation of every object of
\mathcal{D}
in terms of objects in
\mathcal{B}
. Thus for the same category
\mathcal{B}
with different indexing sets in the product category, might give different bases for
\mathcal{D}
.

Example 4.2
.

Every category is a trivial base of itself. Simply take
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{D}
,
\Lambda=\{1\}
with
\mathcal{G}
and
*
as identity functors.

Example 4.3
.

(continuation to example
3.3
). Countable product of the category
\mathcal{A}
of one dimensional compact oriented manifold without boundary over
\Lambda=\{1,2,\cdots\}
serves as a base for the category
\mathcal{D}
of oriented 2-dimensional cobordism classes, which have oriented cobordisms between the objects of
\mathcal{A}
but without holes only. As before, we write an object of
\mathcal{D}
as
(W,X,Y)
. The pasting functor
*:\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{D}
is defined such that
*(\mathcal{H})
is the cobordism between
\mathcal{H}(1)
and
\mathcal{H}(2)
for any functor
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
. The decomposition functor
\mathcal{G}
picks an object in
\mathcal{D}
, which is an oriented cobordism class , say
(W,X,Y)
without holes, and maps it to the functor :
1\mapsto X
,
2\mapsto Y
and the rest of the elements of the indexing category are mapped to the empty set. The combination of the two functors is such that
*\mathcal{G}=\text{Id}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
Note that the pasting functor
*:\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{D}
acts in a way that its restriction onto two copies of
\mathcal{A}
(that is, indexing category with only two objects) becomes the same functor
\mathcal{F}
as discussed in example
3.3
.

Proposition 4.4
.

If the base
\mathcal{B}
is a monoidal category then so is the product category
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\Pi_{\Lambda}\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

For defining a bifunctor (tensor product)
\otimes^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
we have the following data: for any two objects (which are functors)
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
in
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
,

(1)

On level of objects:
\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\prime}\mathcal{G}
is a functor from
\Lambda\to\mathcal{B}
defined as

(a)

On object level

\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\prime}\mathcal{G}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G}(\lambda)\quad\text{ for }\lambda\in\Lambda.

(b)

On the level of morphisms:

\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\prime}\mathcal{G}(\text{Id}_{\lambda})=\mathcal{F}(\text{Id}_{\lambda})\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{G}(\text{Id}_{\lambda})=\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\text{Id}_{\mathcal{G}(\lambda)}=\text{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G}(\lambda)}

(2)

On the level of morphism: For
i=1,2
, if
\eta_{i}
are two natural transformations from
\mathcal{F}_{i}\to\mathcal{G}_{i}
, then
\eta_{1}\otimes^{\prime}\eta_{2}:\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{\prime}\mathcal{F}_{2}\to\mathcal{\mathcal{G}_{1}}\otimes^{\prime}\mathcal{\mathcal{G}_{2}}
is a natural transformation which is given by the collection of morphisms

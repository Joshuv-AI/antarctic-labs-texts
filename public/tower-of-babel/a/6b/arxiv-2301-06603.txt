1.
Introduction and preliminaries

A functional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space of complex valued functions on a (nonempty) set
\Omega
, which has the property that point evaluations are continuous i.e. for each
\lambda\in\Omega
the map
f\mapsto f(\lambda)
is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{H}
. The Riesz representation theorem ensure that for each
\lambda\in\Omega
there is a unique element
k_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
f(\lambda)=\langle f,k_{\lambda}\rangle
, for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. The collection
\{k_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Omega\}
is called the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}
. If
\{e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis for a functional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}
is given by
k_{\lambda}(z)=\sum_{n}\overline{e_{n}(\lambda)}e_{n}(z)
; . For
\lambda\in\Omega
, let
\hat{k_{\lambda}}=\frac{k_{\lambda}}{\|k_{\lambda}\|}
be the normalized reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}
. For a bounded linear operator
A
on
\mathcal{H}
, the function
\widetilde{A}
defined on
\Omega
by
\widetilde{A}(\lambda)=\langle A\hat{k_{\lambda}},\hat{k_{\lambda}}\rangle
is the Berezin symbol of
A
, which firstly have been introduced by Berezin . The Berezin set and the Berezin number of the operator A are defined by

\displaystyle\textbf{Ber}(A):=\{\widetilde{A}(\lambda):\lambda\in\Omega\}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\textbf{ber}(A):=\sup\{|\widetilde{A}(\lambda)|:\lambda\in\Omega\},

respectively. In some recent papers, several Berezin number inequalities have been investigated by authors
.
The Berezin number of operators
A
and
B
satisfies the properties;

\textbf{ ber}(\alpha A)=|\alpha|\textbf{ber}(A)\,\,(\alpha\in\mathbb{C})
, and

\textbf{ber}(A+B)\leq\textbf{ber}(A)+\textbf{ber}(B)
and
\textbf{ber}(A)\leq\|A\|
, where
\|.\|
is the operator norm.

Throughout this paper, the operator matrix
T=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}S&X\\
Y&R\end{array}\right]
is a matrix, which
S\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}_{1}}),X\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{1}})
,
Y\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}})
and
R\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}_{2}})
.

The authors in showed an upper bound for off-diagonal part of operator matrix as follows:

\displaystyle(\textbf{ber}(T))^{r}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|h(f^{2}(|Y|))+h(g^{2}(|Y|))\|+\frac{1}{4}\|h(f^{2}(|X|))+h(g^{2}(|X|))\|,

(1.1)

where
T=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&X\\
Y&0\end{array}\right]
and
f
,
g
are nonnegative continuous functions on
[0,\infty)
such that
f(t)g(t)=t\,\,(t\in[0,\infty))
.

The classical Young inequality says that if
p,q>1
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, then
ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{q}}{q}
for positive real numbers
a,b
. In, the authors showed that a refinement of the scalar Young inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\left(a^{\frac{1}{p}}b^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}+r_{0}^{m}\left(a^{\frac{m}{2}}-b^{\frac{m}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{a}{p}+\frac{b}{q}\right)^{m},

where
r_{0}=\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\}
and
m=1,2,\ldots
. In particular, if
p=q=2
, then

\displaystyle\left(a^{\frac{1}{2}}b^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{m}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{m}\left(a^{\frac{m}{2}}-b^{\frac{m}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq 2^{-m}(a+b)^{m}.

(1.2)

In this paper, we obtain some upper bounds for Berezin number inequalities of off-diagonal part of operator matrix and refinements of them. Moreover, we obtain Berezin number inequalities for diagonal operator matrix.

Y&0\end{array}\right]\in{\mathbb{B}}({\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}})
. Then

\displaystyle\textbf{ber}(\tilde{T}_{t})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\||Y|^{t}|X^{*}|^{1-t}\|+\||X|^{t}|Y^{*}|^{1-t}\|).

(3.1)

Proof.

Let
X=U|X|
and
Y=V|Y|
be the polar decompositions of the operators X and Y. Then

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&X\\
Y&0\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&U\\
V&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}|Y|&0\\
0&|X|\end{array}\right]

is the polar decomposition of T. The generalized Aluthge transform of T is

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{t}=|T|^{t}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&U\\
V&0\end{array}\right]|T|^{1-t}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}|Y|^{t}&0\\
0&|X|^{t}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&U\\
V&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}|Y|^{1-t}&0\\
0&|X|^{1-t}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&|Y|^{t}U|X|^{1-t}\\
|X|^{t}V|Y|^{1-t}&0\end{array}\right].

So

\displaystyle\textbf{ber}(\tilde{T}_{t})

\displaystyle=\textbf{ber}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&|Y|^{t}U|X|^{1-t}\\
|X|^{t}V|Y|^{1-t}&0\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\||Y|^{t}U|X|^{1-t}\|+\||X|^{t}V|Y|^{1-t}\|).\qquad\qquad(\textrm{by Lemma })

Since,
|X^{*}|^{2}=XX^{*}=U|X|^{2}U^{*}
, so
|X|^{1-t}=U^{*}|X^{*}|^{1-t}U
. Thus,
\||Y|^{t}U|X|^{1-t}\|=\||Y|^{t}UU^{*}|X^{*}|^{1-t}U\|=\||Y|^{t}|X^{*}|^{1-t}\|
. Similarly,
\||X|^{t}V|Y|^{1-t}\|=\||X|^{t}|Y^{*}|^{1-t}\|
. Therefore

\displaystyle\textbf{ber}(\tilde{T}_{t})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\||Y|^{t}|X^{*}|^{1-t}\|+\||X|^{t}|Y^{*}|^{1-t}\|).

∎

Multiple almost summing operators
Joilson Ribeiro, Fabrício Santos

Abstract
In this paper, we explore the concept of multilinear operators that are multiple almost summing and present a new concept of type and cotype of multilinear operators and investigate the conditions for this new concept to recover the original concept of type and type for multilinear operators. We also show that these classes are Banach multi-ideals and establish the coherence and compatibility of these classes with the generated homogeneous polynomials.

Multiple almost summing operators

In this section, we will introduce the concept of multiple almost summing multilinear operators and present some of their properties.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a Banach space. The linear space of all
m
-sequences
(x_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}})_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\subset E^{\mathbb{N}^{m}}
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})x_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}

The

\left\|(x_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}})_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Rad\left(E;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}=\left(\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})x_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

When

(b)

If

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\left|\varphi_{1}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\right)\cdots\varphi_{m}\left(x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right|^{2}\times\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{j_{m+1}}^{n_{m}}r_{m+1}(t_{m+1})u\left(x_{j_{m+1}}^{(m+1)}\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{m+1}\right).

Then,

\left(\varphi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{m}\otimes u\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)},x_{j_{m+1}}^{(m+1)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m+1}=1}^{\infty}\in Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m+1}\right)

whenever
\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p_{i}}^{u}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m+1
. Therefore,
\varphi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{m}\otimes u
is a multiple almost
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m+1})
-summing multilinear operator.

In the next result, we will show a characterization of the concept of multiple almost summing multilinear operators.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 2
. A continuous
m
-linear operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is multiple almost
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
-summing if and only if the induced operator

\overset{\wedge}{T}\colon\ell_{p_{1}}^{u}(E_{1})\times\cdots\times\ell_{p_{m}}^{u}(E_{m})\rightarrow Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{n}\right)

given by

\overset{\wedge}{T}\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)=\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}

is an well-defined and continuous
m
-linear operator.

Proof.

It is immediate from the definition of multiple almost
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
-summing operators that
\hat{T}
is an well-defined
m
-linear operator. Now, we will prove the continuity.

Let

\left(\left(x_{j}^{k,(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{k,(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{k,(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{k,(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\right)_{k=1}^{\infty}

be a sequence on the graph of
T
such that

\left(x_{j}^{k,(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\underset{k}{\longmapsto}\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\text{, }i=1,\dots,m

and

T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{k,(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{k,(m)}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\underset{k}{\longmapsto}\left(z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}.

So, for every
i=\{1,\dots,m\}

x_{j}^{k,(i)}\underset{k}{\longmapsto}x_{j}^{(i)}\text{ and }T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{k,(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{k,(m)}\right)\underset{k}{\longmapsto}z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}\text{, }j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\in\mathbb{N}.

By the continuity of
T
and the uniqueness of the limit, we have that

T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)=z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}

for every
j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}=\left(z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}.

Thus,
\hat{T}
has a closed graph and therefore it is continuous.
∎

We define a norm in
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
by

\|T\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}:=\left\|\overset{\wedge}{T}\right\|.

Remark 2.9
.

For every
T\in\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
we have that

\|T\|\leq\|T\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<\infty
and
E_{1},\dots,E_{m},F
be Banach spaces. Then,
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
(T_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, so

\left\|T_{k}-T_{l}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}\underset{k,l}{\longrightarrow}0.

Thus,

\left\|\overset{\wedge}{T}_{k}-\overset{\wedge}{T}_{l}\right\|=\left\|(T_{k}-T_{l})^{\wedge}\right\|\underset{k,l}{\rightarrow}0{\bf\text{.}}

So,
(\overset{\wedge}{T}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\ell_{p_{1}}^{u}(E_{1}),\dots,\ell_{p_{m}}^{u}(E_{m});Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)\right)
is a Cauchy sequence
.
Then there is
B\in\mathcal{L}\left(\ell_{p_{1}}^{u}(E_{1}),\dots,\ell_{p_{m}}^{u}(E_{m});Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)\right)
such that

\overset{\wedge}{T}_{k}\underset{k}{\longrightarrow}B.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|B\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)-\overset{\wedge}{T}_{k}\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)\right\|_{Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|B\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)-\left(T_{k}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}\underset{k}{\longrightarrow}0,

for every
\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p_{i}}^{u}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m
.
We call
B\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)
by
\left(z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}
. It follows from Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\left\|z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}}-T_{k}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right\|\underset{k}{\longrightarrow}0,

so

T_{k}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\underset{k}{\longrightarrow}z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}}\text{, }\forall j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, since
(T_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, by Remark
2.9

(T_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and there is
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
such that

T_{k}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\underset{k}{\longrightarrow}T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right).

Then,

T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)=z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}},

thus

\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}=\left(z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}.

So, if
\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p_{i}}^{u}\left(E_{i}\right)
for
i=1,\dots,m
, then

\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}=\left(z_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}^{x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\in Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right){\bf\text{.}}

Thus,
T\in\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 2
. Then,
\ell_{p_{1}}^{u}(\mathbb{K})\cdots\ell_{p_{m}}^{u}(\mathbb{K})\overset{multi,1}{\hookrightarrow}Rad\left(\mathbb{K};\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)
. Indeed, by Remark
2.2
item (a), for every
\left(\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p_{i}}^{u}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})\lambda_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\lambda_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\lambda_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\right|^{2}\right)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\right|^{p_{i}}\right).

So,

\left(\lambda_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\lambda_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\in Rad\left(\mathbb{K};\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)

and

\left\|\left(\lambda_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\lambda_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Rad\left(\mathbb{K};\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\right)\right\|_{p_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\right)\right\|_{w,p_{i}}.

A consequence of Remark
2.11
is the following result.

Proposition 2.12
.

(a)

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 2
and
E_{1},\dots,E_{m},F
be Banach spaces. Then, the
m
-linear operators of finite type are contained in
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

(b)

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 2
and
T\colon\mathbb{K}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be given by
T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=x_{1}\cdots x_{m}
, then
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}=1
.

Using the results above, we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 2
. Then the class
\left(\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}\right)
is a Banach multi-ideal between Banach spaces.

Proof.

Since we already have the Proposition
2.12
and Theorem
2.10
, we need to show only the property of multi-ideal. Let
t\in\mathcal{L}(F,H)
,
T\in\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
u_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(G_{i};E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|t\left(\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\|t\|^{2}\lim_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})T\left(u_{1}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\right),\dots,u_{m}\left(x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}.

As
Rad\left(\cdot;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)
and
\ell_{p_{i}}^{(i)}(\cdot)
for
i=1,\dots,m
are linearly stable, it follows that
t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}(G_{1},\dots,G_{m};H)
and,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\right)^{\wedge}\left(\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)\right\|_{Rad\left(H;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\|t\|\left\|\left(T\left(u_{1}\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)}\right),\dots,u_{m}\left(x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right)}

\displaystyle=\|t\|\left\|\overset{\wedge}{T}\left(\left(u_{1}\left(x_{j}^{(1)}\right)\right)_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,\left(u_{1}\left(x_{j}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|t\|\left\|\overset{\wedge}{T}\right\|\|u_{1}\|\cdots\|u_{m}\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{w,p_{i}}

\displaystyle=\|t\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}\|u_{1}\|\cdots\|u_{m}\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{w,p_{i}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}=\left\|\left(t\circ T\circ(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\right)^{\wedge}\right\|\leq\|t\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}}\|u_{1}\|\cdots\|u_{m}\|.

∎

3
Coherence and compatibility

The concept of Coherence and compatibility that we use in this section was intrduced by Pelegrino and Ribeiro in
[
15
]
. In this concept a pair of multi-ideals and homogeneous polynomial ideals is used, so it is necessary to define a class of homogeneous polynomials associated to
\mathcal{L}_{al,(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})}^{mult}
. Let’s remember these concepts.

Definition 3.1

(Compatible pair of ideals)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a normed operator ideal and
N\in\left(\mathbb{N}-\{1\}\right)\cup\{\infty\}
. A sequence
\left(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{N}
, with
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathcal{U}
, is compatible with
\mathcal{U}
if there exist positive constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
such that for all Banach spaces
E
and
F
, the following conditions hold for all
n\in\{2,\cdots,N\}:

(CP1)
If
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(E_{1},\dots,E_{n};F)
and
a_{j}\in E_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k\}
, then
T_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},a_{k+1},\dots,a_{n}}\in\mathcal{U}(E_{k};F)

and

\left\|T_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},a_{k+1},\dots,a_{n}}\right\|\leq\alpha_{1}\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\|a_{1}\|\cdots\|a_{k-1}\|\ \|a_{k+1}\|\cdots\|a_{n}\|.

(CP2)
If
P\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(^{n}E;F)
and
a\in E
, then
P_{a^{n-1}}\in\mathcal{U}(E;F)
and

\left\|P_{a^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}}\leq\alpha_{2}\max{\left\{\left\|\overset{\vee}{P}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}},\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{n}}\right\}}\|a\|^{n-1}.

(CP3) If
u\in\mathcal{U}(E_{n};F)
,
\gamma_{j}\in E^{\prime}_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n-1
, then
\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n-1}u\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(E_{1},\dots,E_{n};F)

and

\left\|\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n-1}u\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\leq\alpha_{3}\|\gamma_{1}\|\cdots\|\gamma_{n-1}\|\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{U}}.

(CP4)
If
u\in\mathcal{U}(E;F)
and
\gamma\in E^{\prime}
, then
\gamma^{(n-1)}u\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(^{n}E;F)
.

(CP5)

P
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{n}(^{n}E;F)
if and only if
\overset{\vee}{P}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{n}(^{n}E;F)
.

Definition 3.2

(Coherent pair of ideals)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a normed operator ideal and let
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. A sequence
\left(\mathcal{U}_{k},\mathcal{M}_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{N}
, with
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathcal{U}
, is coherent if there exist positive constants
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
such that for all Banach spaces
E
and
F
the following conditions hold for
k=1,\dots,N-1:

(CH1)
If
T\in\mathcal{M}_{k+1}\left(E_{1},\dots,E_{k+1};F\right)
and
a_{j}\in E_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,k+1
, then

T_{a_{j}}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(E_{1},\dots,E_{j-1},E_{j+1},\dots,E_{k+1};F\right)

and

\left\|T_{a_{j}}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}\leq\beta_{1}\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{k+1}}\|a_{j}\|.

(CH2)
If
P\in\mathcal{U}_{k+1}\left({}^{k+1}E;F\right)
,
a\in E
, then
P_{a}
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{k}\left({}^{k}E;F\right)
and

\left\|P_{a}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{k}}\leq\beta_{2}\max{\left\{\left\|\overset{\vee}{P}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{k+1}},\left\|P\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{k+1}}\right\}}\|a\|.

(CH3)
If
T\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{1},\dots,E_{k};F)
,
\gamma\in E^{\prime}_{k+1}
, then

\gamma T\in\mathcal{M}_{k+1}(E_{1},\dots,E_{k+1};F)

and

\left\|\gamma T\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{k+1}}\leq\beta_{3}\|\gamma\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}.

(CH4)
If
P\in\mathcal{U}_{k}\left({}^{k}E;F\right)
and
\gamma\in E^{\prime}
, then
\gamma P\in\mathcal{U}_{k+1}\left({}^{k+1}E;F\right).

(CH5)
For all
k=1,\dots,N
,
P
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{k}(^{k}E;F)
if and only if
\overset{\vee}{P}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{k}(^{k}E;F)
.

In this section, the class of all multiple almost
p
-summing
m
-linear operators is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}
. The reason for this is to highlight the linearity of the operators. The next result is folklore and introduces the class of homogeneous polynomials that will be considered in this section.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{M},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}}\right)
be a Banach multi-ideal. Then,

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\{P\in\mathcal{P}\text{; }\check{P}\in\mathcal{M}\}\text{, }\|P\|_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}}:=\|\check{P}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

is a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

The class
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}})
is called the Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials generated by
\left(\mathcal{M},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}}\right)
. This class has
been studied extensively in several works, of which we highlight
[
1
,
4
,
9
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
. The class of all multiple almost
p
-summing
m
-homogeneous polynomials and its norm are defined by

\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m}:=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}=\left\{P\in\mathcal{P}\text{; }\check{P}\in\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}\right\}{and}\ \|P\|_{\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}:=\|\check{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}.

As
\left(\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}\right)
is a Banach Multi-ideal,
it
follows from Proposition
3.3
that

\left(\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}\right)

is a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

An immediate consequence of the Polarization Formula is the following:

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}(E;F)
and
a\in E
,

(P_{a})^{\vee}=\check{P}_{a}
.

Now, we can prove the main result in this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
. Then, the sequence of pairs

\left(\left(\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}\right),\left(\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{al,p}^{multi,m}}\right)\right)

is coherent and compatible with
\prod_{al,p}
.

Proof.

To prove the coherence, it is necessary to check properties
(CH1),\dots,(CH5)
. We begin by showing property (CH1). We will do only the case
i=1
. The other cases are analogous. Let
T\in\mathcal{L}_{al,p}^{multi,m}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
a_{1}\in E_{1}
.

Consider
(x_{j}^{(1)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{u}(E_{1})
given by
x_{1}^{(1)}=a_{1}
and
x_{j}^{(1)}=0
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Thus,

\left(T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\in Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m}\right),

for every
\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p_{i}}^{u}(E_{i})
,
i=2,\dots,m
. So,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{m-1}}\left\|\sum_{j_{2},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}r_{j_{2}}(t_{2})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})T_{a_{1}}\left(x_{j_{2}}^{(2)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\left\|\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}r_{j_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots r_{j_{m}}(t_{m})T\left(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right\|^{2}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}<\infty.

Then,

\left(T_{a_{1}}\left(x_{j_{2}}^{(2)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\right)\right)_{j_{2},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{\infty}\in Rad\left(F;\mathbb{N}^{m-1}\right),

for every
\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{u}(E_{i})
,

4
Multi-type and multi-cotype of multilinear operators

We begin by proposing a multi-type and multi-cotype concept of multilinear operators.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
m,n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m},q<\infty
. We say that
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
has:

(a)

Multi-type
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
if there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|\left(T(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)})\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\right\|_{Rad(F;\mathbb{N}^{m})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\right\|_{p_{i}}

(1)

for any choice of finitely many vectors
(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
.

(b)

Multi-cotype
q
if there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|\left(T(x_{j_{1}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{m}}^{(m)})\right)_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}=1}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\right\|_{q}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left(x_{j}^{(i)}\right)_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\right\|_{Rad(E_{i})}

(2)

for any choice of finitely many vectors
(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
.

We denote the linear space of all
m
-linear operators with multi-type
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and with multi-cotype
q
from
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
to
F
by
\tau_{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}}^{multi}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}^{m,multi}(E_{1},\dots,

E_{m};F)
, respectivaly. If
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{m}=p
, we just write
\tau_{p}^{multi}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. It is not difficult to see that the infimum of the constants in (
1
) and (
2
), denoted by
\|T\|_{\tau_{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}}^{multi}}
and
\|T\|_{\mathcal{C}_{q}^{m,multi}}
, are a norm in
\tau_{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}}^{multi}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
\mathcal{C}_{q}^{m,multi}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, respectively.

From now on, we consider an integer
m\geq 2
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{m},q
such that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}\leq 1\text{ and }q\geq\frac{2}{m}.

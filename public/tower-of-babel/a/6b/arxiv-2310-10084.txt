Sectorial covers over fanifolds
Hayato Morimura

Abstract
. For the stopped Weinstein sector associated with any fanifold introduced in [ GS23 ] by Gammage–Shende, we construct a Weinstein sectorial cover which allows us to describe homological mirror symmetry over the fanifold as an isomorphism of cosheaves of categories. In a special case, our Weinstein sectorial cover gives a lift of the open cover for the global skeleton of a very affine hypersurface computed in [ GS22 ] .



1.
Introduction

The Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold is a highly nontrivial invariant.
It is an
A_{\infty}
-category
whose homotopy category has Lagrangians as objects
and
morphisms between two Lagrangians is the linear span of their intersection points up to suitable perturbation.
The
A_{\infty}
-structure is controlled by counts of pseudoholomorphic disks with Lagrangian boundary conditions,
which depend on the global geometry of the symplectic manifold.
Usually,
computing Fukaya categories is extremely hard because of this global nature.
However,
under suitable assumptions,
Fukaya categories exhibit good local-to-global behaviors
which allows us to reduce complicated analysis on the global geometry of symplectic manifolds to more tractable one.

For instance,
via sectorial descent recently established in

[
GPS2
]

by Ganatra–Pardon–Shende,
the wrapped Fukaya category

\mathcal{W}(X)

of a Weinstein sector

X

can be computed by gluing that of local pieces,
provided its Weinstein sectorial cover

X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}
.
More precisely,
the canonical functor from the homotopy colimit

\displaystyle\displaystyle\operatorname{colim}_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\mathcal{W}(\bigcap_{i\in I}X_{i})\to\mathcal{W}(X)

induced by the pushforward functors along inclusions of Weinstein sectors from

[
GPS1
]

is a pretriangulated equivalence.
In other words,
wrapped Fukaya categories are cosheaves with respect to Weinstein sectorial covers.
The result is valid also for partially wrapped Fukaya categories of stopped Weinstein sectors with mostly Legendrian stops away from boundaries.

Considering its computational power,
given a Weinstein sector,
it is natural to seek its Weinstein sectorial cover.
Moreover,
when working with homological mirror symmetry(HMS),
one often needs such a cover to be compatible with that of its mirror.
For instance,
if the latter cover corresponds to an open cover of a relative skeleton,
then a lift of it to a Weinstein sectorial cover will have the desired compatibility.
So far,
there are not so many interesting examples of Weinstein sectorial covers

[
CKL
,
GJ
,
GL22
]
.
One reason for this might be easier access to other local-to-global behaviors

[
GS22
,
GS23
,
Lee16
,
MSZ
,
PS1
,
PS2
,
PS3
]
,
which requires us to compute at least locally a skeleton.
Although they might work to solve a given problem,
construction of Weinstein sectorial covers still should be a fundamental geometric question.

In this paper,
we give a Weinstein sectorial cover of the stopped Weinstein sector associated with a fanifold introduced in

[
GS23
]

by Gammage–Shende to discuss HMS for more general spaces than toric stacks on the
B
-side.
It is a stratified space obtained by gluing rational polyhedral fans of cones,
which provides the organizing
topological
and
discrete
data for HMS at large volume.
To a fanifold

\Phi

they associated an algebraic space

\mathbf{T}(\Phi)

obtained as the gluing of the toric varieties

T_{\Sigma}

associated with the fans

\Sigma
.
Based on an idea from SYZ fibrations,
they constructed its mirror

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

by inductive Weinstein handle attachments.
The guiding principle behind their construction of the stopped Weinstein sector

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

was that
the FLTZ Lagrangians

\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)

and
the projections

\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma

should glue to yield
its relative skeleton

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}(\Phi)

and
a map

\pi\colon\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}(\Phi)\to\Phi
.

As predicted by Gammage–Shende

[
GS23
, Remark 4.5]
,
the canonical lifts of the projections

\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma

glue to yield a version of
A
-side SYZ fibration

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)\to\Phi
,
after some modifications.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Mor
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

There is a filtered stratified fibration

\bar{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)\to\Phi

restricting to

\pi
,
which is homotopic to a filtered stratified integrable system with noncompact fibers.
If the fan

\Sigma_{S}

associated with any stratum

S\subset\Phi

is proper,
then the homotopy becomes trivial.

The lift

\bar{\pi}

completes a global combinatorial duality for the mirror pair

(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi),\mathbf{T}(\Phi))

over

\Phi
.
Moreover,
it enables us to lift any cover of

\Phi

to

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)
.
Inspired by the idea in

[
GPS2
, Example 1.34]

and
using this aspect of

\bar{\pi}
,
after some modification,
we construct a Weinstein sectorial cover of

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

with the desired compatibility in Section

4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a Weinstein sectorial cover of

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)
.
Moreover,
it restricts to an open cover of the relative skeleton

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}(\Phi)

compatible with a cover of

\mathbf{T}(\Phi)
.

When

\Phi

is the fanifold from

[
GS23
, Example 4.23]
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}(\Phi)

coincides with the skeleton

\operatorname{Core}(H)

[
GS22
, Theorem 6.2.4]

of a very affine hypersurface

H
.
In Section

5
,
we show that our cover gives a lift of the open cover

[
GS22
, Corollary 4.3.2]

of

\operatorname{Core}(H)
,
giving an affirmative answer to the conjecture raised in

[
GS22
, Section 2.4]

and
making the discussion of

[
GS22
, Section 2.1]

completely rigorous.

Via sectorial descent we obtain a description of HMS for

(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi),\mathbf{T}(\Phi))

as an isomorphism of cosheaves of categories.
See Section

4.3

for the precise definitions of the associated cosheaves.
The isomorphism can be regarded as a dual to

[
GS23
, Theorem 5.3]

and
encodes the gluing of coherent constructible correspondence

[
Kuw20
]

for local pieces.

Corollary 1.3

(Theorem
4.17
)
.

There is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{*}\operatorname{Fuk}_{*}\cong\operatorname{Coh}_{!}\circ\mathbf{T}

of cosheaves of categories over

\Phi

whose global section yields an equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fuk}(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi))\simeq\operatorname{Coh}(\mathbf{T}(\Phi)).

Our construction of the cover is close to the more general strategy suggested in

[
BC23
]

by Bai–Côté.
When

X=T^{*}M
,
they triangulate

M

and
cover

X

by the cotangent bundles

T^{*}\operatorname{star}(v_{\alpha})

of the stars of the vertices

v_{\alpha}
.
When

X

is a polarizable Weinstein manifold,
they deform

X

to another Weinstein manifold with arborial skeleton
which by

[
BC23
, Section 4.2]

admits
a canonical Whitney stratification,
in particular,
a triangulation due to Goresky.
Cover the deformation of

X

by arboreal thickenings of the stars of the vertices.
Unfortunately,
there are obstructions to making their strategy rigorous
which involves developing a good theory of arboreal sectors.

Another general strategy might come from simplicial decompositions
introduced in

[
Asp23
]

by Asplund.
By

[
Asp23
, Theorem 1.4]

a simplicial decomposition of a Weinstein manifold

X

bijectively corresponds to its sectorial cover
which is good in the sense of

[
Asp23
, Section 1]
.
Since the convex completion of

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

is an ordinary Weinstein manifold,
by

[
Asp23
, Lemma 3.13]

one could refine our Weinstein sectorial cover to be a good sectorial cover of the convex completion.
It would be an interesting problem to find a simplicial decomposition of a Weinstein manifold
corresponding to a Weinstein sectorial cover
and
compatible with the cover of its given mirror.

2.
Gluing of stopped Weinstein sectors

In this section,
we review
some basic properties of Liouville sectors introduced in

[
GPS1
, Section 2]

by Ganatra–Pardon–Shende
and
how to glue them together.
Throughout the paper,
we work with Liouville manifolds of finite type
so that
any Liouville manifold

X

will be the completion of some Liouville domain

X_{0}
.
Then its skeleton

\operatorname{Core}(X)

will always be compact.
We denote by

\partial_{\infty}X_{0}

the actual boundary of

X_{0}
,
which can be identified with the ideal boundary

\partial_{\infty}X

of

X

to preserve the symbols

\partial X_{0},\partial X
.

2.1.
Stopped Weinstein sectors

All Liouville manifolds of our interest are Weinstein.
Namely,
the Liouville vector field

Z

of each

X

is gradient-like for a Morse–Bott function

\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}

which is constant on the cylindrical end

(\mathbb{R}_{t\geq 0}\times\partial_{\infty}X,e^{t}(\lambda|_{\partial_{\infty}X}))
.
Hence

\operatorname{Core}(X)

will also be isotropic by

[
CE12
, Lemma 11.13(a)]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
GPS1
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

A

Liouville sector

is a Liouville manifold-with-boundary satisfying the following equivalent conditions:

•

For some

\alpha>0

there exists an

\alpha
-defining function

I\colon\partial X\to\mathbb{R}

with

ZI=\alpha I

near infinity
and

dI|_{C}>0

for the characteristic foliation of the hypersurface

\partial X\subset X
,
where

C

is oriented
so that

\omega(C,N)>0

for any inward pointing vector

N
.

•

The boundary of the contact hypersurface

\partial_{\infty}X\subset X

is convex
and
there is a diffeomorphism

\partial X\cong F\times\mathbb{R}

sending

C

to the foliation of

F\times\mathbb{R}

by leaves

\{pt\}\times\mathbb{R}
.
We call the symplectic reduction

F
,
which is a Liouville manifold,
the

symplectic boundary

of

X
.

An

inclusion

i\colon X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}

of Liouville sectors is a proper map
which is a diffeomorphism onto its image,
satisfying

i^{*}\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+df

for compactly supported

f
.
A

trivial inclusion

of Liouville sectors is one for
which

i(X)

may be deformed into

X^{\prime}

through Liouville sectors included in

X
.

Remark 2.2
.

The inequality

dI|_{C}>0

is equivalent to the Hamiltonian vector field

X_{I}

being outward pointing along

\partial X
.
Small deformations of

X

within the class of Liouville manifolds-with-boundary are again Liouville sectors.

Definition 2.3

(
[
GPS1
, Remark 2.8]
)
.

An

open Liouville sector

is a pair

(X,\partial_{\infty}X)

of an exact symplectic manifold

X

and
a contact manifold

\partial_{\infty}X

together with a germ near

+\infty

of codimension

0

embedding of the symplectization

S\partial_{\infty}X

into

X
.
Here,
the embedding strictly respects Liouville forms
and
the pair

(X,\partial_{\infty}X)

is exhausted by Liouville sectors.
Being exhausted by Liouville sectors means that
any subset of

X
,
which away from a compact subset of

X

is the cone over a compact subset of

\partial_{\infty}X
,
is contained in some Liouville sector

X_{0}\subset X

with

\partial_{\infty}X_{0}\subset\partial_{\infty}X
.

Example 2.4
.

For any manifold

M

the cotangent bundle

T^{*}M

is an open Liouville sector.
An exhaustion is given by the family of Liouville subsectors

T^{*}M_{0}

for compact codimension

0

submanifolds-with-boundary

M_{0}\subset M
.
For any Liouville sector

X

its interior

X^{\circ}=X\setminus\partial X

is an open Liouville sector.

For a Liouville sector

X

we denote by

\mathcal{W}(X)

the

wrapped Fukaya category

[
GPS1
, Definition 3.36]
.
It is an
A_{\infty}
-category
whose objects are exact cylindrical at infinity Lagrangians inside

X

and
whose morphisms are calculated by wrapping Lagrangians.
According to

[
GPS1
, Section 3.6]
,
any inclusion

X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}

of Liouville sectors induces the

pushforward functor

\mathcal{W}(X)\to\mathcal{W}(X^{\prime})

which makes the assignment

X\mapsto\mathcal{W}(X)

covariantly functorial.
We use the same symbols to denote the counterparts of open Liouville sectors.

Wrapped Fukaya categories are invariant under deformations in the following sense.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
GPS2
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Along a trivial inclusion

X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}

of Liouville sectors,
the pushforward functor defines a canonical quasi-equivalence

\mathcal{W}(X)=\mathcal{W}(X^{\prime})
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
GPS2
, Section 1.1]
)
.

A

stop

\mathfrak{f}

of a Liouville sector

X

is a closed subset of

(\partial_{\infty}X)^{\circ}=\partial_{\infty}X\setminus\partial\partial_{\infty}X
.
We call the pair

(X,\mathfrak{f})

a

stopped Liouville sector
.
An

inclusion

(X,\mathfrak{f})\hookrightarrow(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})

of stopped Liouville sectors is an inclusion of Liouville sectors

X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}

with

\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\cap(\partial_{\infty}X)^{\circ}\subset\mathfrak{f}
.

When

\mathfrak{f}=\emptyset
,
the above definition specializes to Definition

2.1
.
For a stopped Liouville sector

(X,\mathfrak{f})

we denote by

\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{f})

the

partially wrapped Fukaya category

[
GPS2
, definition 2.31]
.
It is an
A_{\infty}
-category
whose objects are exact cylindrical at infinity Lagrangians inside

X

disjoint at infinity from

\mathfrak{f}

and
whose morphisms are calculated by wrapping Lagrangians in

(\partial_{\infty}X)^{\circ}\setminus\mathfrak{f}
.
Similar to wrapped Fukaya categories,
any inclusion

(X,\mathfrak{f})\hookrightarrow(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})

of stopped Liouville sectors induces the

pushforward functor

\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{f})\to\mathcal{W}(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})

which makes the assignment

(X,\mathfrak{f})\mapsto\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{f})

covariantly functorial.

Although the partially wrapped Fukaya category is defined for an arbitrary stopped Liouville sector

(X,\mathfrak{f})
,
the results in

[
GPS2
]

are generally sharpest
when

X

is Weinstein
and

\mathfrak{f}

is mostly Legendrian.

Definition 2.7

(
[
GPS2
, Definition 1.7]
)
.

A closed subset

\mathfrak{f}

of a contact manifold

Y^{2n-1}

is

mostly Legendrian

if it admits a decomposition

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}^{\text{subcrit}}\cup\mathfrak{f}^{\text{crit}}

for which

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{subcrit}}

is closed
and
is contained in the smooth image of a second countable manifold of dimension

<n-1
,
and

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{crit}}\subset Y\setminus\mathfrak{f}^{\text{subcrit}}

is a Legendrian submanifold.
The notion of a mostly Lagrangian closed subset of a symplectic manifold is defined analogously.

2.2.
Weinstein pairs

Lemma 2.8

(
[
GPS1

)

Let

(
[

)

A sutured Liouville domain

\bar{X}_{0}

submanifold-with-boundary

\bar{X}

F_{0}

Remark 2.10
.

Any sutured Liouville domain

(\bar{X}_{0},F_{0})

defines a

[
Eli

\lambda

\bar{X}_{0}

and

Definition 2.11

(
[
GPS1
, Remark 2.16]
)
.

Given a sutured Liouville domain

(\bar{X}_{0},F_{0})

let

\mathbb{L}_{0}\subset\bar{X}_{0}

be the locus of points
which do not escape to the complement of

\operatorname{Core}(F_{0})

at infinity.
We call

\mathbb{L}_{0}

the

relative skeleton

of

(\bar{X}_{0},F_{0})

and

\operatorname{Core}(\bar{X}_{0},F_{0})

Analogous terminology applies to sutured Liouville manifolds.

Remark 2.12
.

The locus

\mathbb{L}\subset\bar{X}

is necessarily noncompact

is empty.
When both

are Weinstein,

In particular,

\bar{X}

deforms down to a small regular neighborhood of

.

Given a sutured Liouville domain

(\bar{X}_{0},F_{0})
,
the Reeb vector field

is transverse to

as

is symplectic

satisfying the hypotheses of

In particular,

gives rise to a Liouville sector.

Conversely,
any Liouville sector arises,

2.3.
Gluing of Weinstein pairs

(

)

Let

4.
Weinstein sectorial covers

In this section,
we construct a Weinstein sectorial cover of

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

by lifting a suitable cover of

\Phi

along

\bar{\pi}
.
The idea is similar to the construction for cotangent bundles.
However,
here we need extra care to make it compatible with the handle attachment process.
Via sectorial descent,
our cover gives rise to a constructible cosheaf

\bar{\pi}_{*}\operatorname{Fuk}_{*}

of categories over

\Phi

with respect to a certain natural topology.
We show that

\bar{\pi}_{*}\operatorname{Fuk}_{*}

is isomorphic to the
B
-side counterpart

\operatorname{Coh}_{!}\circ\mathbf{T}
.

4.1.
Sectorial covers

Due to

[
GPS2
]
,
given a Liouville sector

X
,
if it admits a Weinstein sectorial cover,
then the wrapped Fukaya category

\mathcal{W}(X)

can be computed by gluing that of pieces in the cover.

Definition 4.1

(
[
GPS2
, Definition 12.2]
)
.

Let

X

be a Liouville manifold-with-boundary.
A collection of cylindrical hypersurfaces

H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\subset X

is

sectorial

if their characteristic foliations

C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}

are
\omega
-orthogonal over their intersections
and
there exist functions

I_{i}\colon\operatorname{Nbd}^{Z}_{X}(H_{i})\to\mathbb{R}

linear near infinity satisfying

(4.1)

\displaystyle dI_{i}|_{C_{i}}\neq 0,\ dI_{i}|_{C_{j}}=0\ \text{for}\ i\neq j,\ \{I_{i},I_{j}\}=0.

We also allow immersed hypersurfaces

H\to X
,
with

I_{i}

defined on

\operatorname{Nbd}^{Z}_{X}(H_{i})

regarded as an immersed codimension zero submanifold of

X
,
and
the subscripts

i

in the identities
(
4.1
)
indexing the “local branches” of

H
.

Definition 4.2

(
[
GPS2
, Definition 12.14]
)
.

A

Liouville sector-with-sectorial-corners

is a Liouville manifold-with-corners
whose boundary,
regarded as an immersed hypersurface,
is sectorial.

Definition 4.3

(
[
GPS2
, Definition 12.19, Remark 12.20]
)
.

Let

X

be a Liouville manifold-with-boundary.
Suppose that

X

admits a cover

X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}

by Liouville sectors-with-sectorial-corners

X_{i}\subset X
,
with precisely two faces

\partial^{1}X_{i}=X_{i}\cap\partial X

and
the point set topological boundary

\partial^{2}X_{i}

of

X_{i}

meeting along the corner locus

\partial X\cap\partial^{2}X_{i}=\partial^{1}X_{i}\cap\partial^{2}X_{i}
.
Such a cover is

sectorial

if the collection of boundaries

\partial X,\partial^{2}X_{1},\ldots,\partial^{2}X_{n}

is sectorial.
We also allow

X_{i}

without corners,
requiring

\partial^{2}X_{i}

to be disjoint from

\partial X
,

\partial X,\partial^{2}X_{1},\ldots,\partial^{2}X_{n}

to be sectorial
and

\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}\hookrightarrow X

to be a trivial inclusion.

For any sectorial cover

X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}
,
stratify

X

by strata

(4.2)

\displaystyle X_{J,K,L}=\bigcup_{j\in J}X_{j}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}\partial X_{k}\ \setminus\bigcap_{l\in L}X_{l}

ranging over

J\sqcup K\sqcup L=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The closure of each

X_{J,K,L}

is a submanifold-with-corners,
whose symplectic reduction is a Liouville sector-with-sectorial-corners.

Definition 4.4

(
[
GPS2
, Definition 12.15]
)
.

A sectorial cover

X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}

is

Weinstein

if the convex completions of all of the symplectic reductions of strata
(
4.2
)
are Weinstein up to deformation.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
GPS2
, Theorem 1.35]
)
.

For any Weinstein sectorial cover

X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}

of a Weinstein sector

X
,
the induced functor

(4.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\operatorname{colim}_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\mathcal{W}(\bigcap_{i\in I}X_{i})\to\mathcal{W}(X)

is a pretriangulated equivalence.
Moreover,
the same holds for any mostly Legendrian stop

\mathfrak{r}\subset(\partial_{\infty}X)^{\circ}

which is disjoint
from each

\partial X_{i}

and
from a neighborhood of

\partial X
.

4.2.
Alternative description of
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)
and
\bar{\pi}

Recall that

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

is a Weinstein manifold-with-boundary obtained by Weinstein handle attachments,
special cases of gluing of Weinstein pairs.
The boundary comes from conic completion of the boundaries of the contact surroundings of

T^{*}\mathcal{L}_{k}

in

\partial_{\infty}{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi_{k-1})

Below,
preserving a sufficiently small neighborhood of

[

Whenever we attach a handle

to

as a Liouville sector equipped with the tautological Liouville form.

We use the same symbol

to denote the result,

One can also modify the map

to

.

4.3.
Construction

We construct a Weinstein sectorial cover of the modification

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(\Phi)

as the inverse image under

of a certain cover of

\Phi
.
For expository reason,

\Phi

Since each stratum is closed,

Connect by an edge inside

1
-dimensional lower strata adjacent to it.

1
-strata.
There might be some lower dimensional closed strata

Beginning the process also from such strata,
we obtain a partition of

\Phi

defined by
the additional edges connecting the barycenters
and
the barycenters on those
1
-strata
which are not adjacent to higher dimensional strata.
The partition divides

\Phi

into some finite number of connected components.

For each
0
-stratum

,
there is a unique connected component containing

.
Take a suitable slight enlargement of the rounding the corners of its closure.
We denote by

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(P)

and

\partial^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(P)

the inverse image under

\bar{\pi}

respectively
of the slight enlargement
and
of its boundary.
Perturbing these boundaries if necessary,
we may assume that

\partial^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(P),\partial^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}(P^{\prime})

are

or
intersect transversely

of

to

Repeat the process inductively until reaching the barycenters of

to denote the results.

Remark 4.6

Moreover,

5.
HMS for very affine hypersurfaces

In this section,
we revisit HMS for very affine hypersurfaces established by Gammage–Shende.
Specifically,
we describe an aspect of the mirror pair as the associated spaces with a fanifold.
Then we apply our results in the previous section,
giving an affirmative answer to the conjecture raised in

[
GS22
, Section 2.4]
.
As a consequence,
we promote HMS for very affine hypersurfaces to an isomorphism of constructible cosheaves of categories over the fanifold.

5.1.
Mirror pair for very affine hypersurfaces

To a very affine hypersuface

H\subset M^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong T^{*}T^{d+1}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d+1}

satisfying certain assumptions on the defining Laurent polynomial,
the toric boundary divisor

\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}

gives its mirror

[
GS22
]
.
Here,

\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}
is the associated toric stack with a smooth quasiprojective stacky fan

\Sigma\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
Recall that

\Sigma

is determined by the defining polynomial of

H
.
Under the assumptions,
its stacky primitives span a convex lattice polytope

\Delta^{\vee}

containing the origin
and

\Sigma

defines an adapted star-shaped triangulation

\mathcal{T}

of

\Delta^{\vee}
.
Below,
we review the proof of the following statement.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
GS22
, Theorem 1.0.1]
)
.

Assume that
the Newton polytope

\Delta^{\vee}

contains the origin
and
its triangulation

\mathcal{T}

is star-shaped.
Then there is an equivalence

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fuk}(H)\simeq\operatorname{Coh}(\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}).

Due to the assumptions,
one can easily construct a global skeleton

\operatorname{Core}(H)

of

H
.
Recall that
we restrict to

H

the canonical Weinstein structure on

M^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong T^{*}T^{d+1}
.
In the sequel,
for simplicity we will assume that

\Sigma

is an ordinary simplicial fan.
When

\Sigma

is a smooth quasiprojective stacky fan,
we will replace
the
A
-model with its finite cover
and
the
B
-model with the associated toric stack.
The skeleton

\operatorname{Core}(H)

can be obtained by gluing skeleta of tailored pants

\tilde{P}_{d}

computed by Nadler

[
Nad
]
,
after transported to the ones in the pants decomposition of

H
.
As explained in

[
Nad
]
,
we may identify the skeleton

\operatorname{Core}(\tilde{P}_{d})

with the ideal boundary of the FLTZ skeleton associated with the toric variety

\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
.
Hence we may identify

\operatorname{Core}(H)

with

\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)

for the FLTZ skeleton

\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)

associated with

\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}
.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
GS22
, Corollary 4.3.2]
)
.

Assume that
the Newton polytope

\Delta^{\vee}

contains the origin
and
its triangulation

\mathcal{T}

is star-shaped.
Then

\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)

has an open cover by the subsets

\mathbb{L}(\Sigma/\sigma)\times\partial_{\infty}\sigma

anti-indexed by the poset of nonzero cones in

\Sigma
.

Proof.

Let

\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}\subset\tau

be nonzero cones in

\Sigma
.
Then

\tau/\langle\sigma\rangle,\tau/\langle\sigma^{\prime}\rangle

give cones in the normal fans

\Sigma/\sigma,\Sigma/\sigma^{\prime}

with the isomorphisms

\displaystyle(\tau/\langle\sigma\rangle)^{\perp}\times\tau/\langle\sigma\rangle\times\sigma\cong(\tau)^{\perp}\times\tau\cong(\tau/\langle\sigma^{\prime}\rangle)^{\perp}\times\tau/\langle\sigma^{\prime}\rangle\times\sigma^{\prime}.

Note that
subtori

(\tau/\langle\sigma\rangle)^{\perp},(\tau/\langle\sigma^{\prime}\rangle)^{\perp}

of respective tori

\widehat{M}/\langle\sigma\rangle,\widehat{M}/\langle\sigma^{\prime}\rangle

coincide with

\tau^{\perp}\subset\widehat{M}
.
Hence we obtain an open inclusion

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}(\Sigma/\sigma^{\prime})\times\sigma^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}(\Sigma/\sigma)\times\sigma.

Now,
the claim follows from

\partial_{\infty}(\mathbb{L}(\Sigma/\sigma)\times\sigma)\cong\mathbb{L}(\Sigma/\sigma)\times\partial_{\infty}\sigma
.
∎

To each

\sigma\in\Sigma
,
let

\overline{O(\sigma)}

be the closure of the corresponding toric orbit

O(\sigma)
.
It is the toric variety associated with

\Sigma/\sigma
.
Here,
we take its reduced induced scheme structure.
If

\sigma\subset\tau
,
then there is a closed immersion

\overline{O(\tau)}\hookrightarrow\overline{O(\sigma)}

induced by the quotient

\Sigma/\sigma\to\Sigma/\tau
.
Also,
we have

\overline{O(\sigma)}\cap\overline{O(\sigma^{\prime})}=\overline{O(\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime})}

where

\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime}

denotes the smallest cone in

\Sigma

containing both

\sigma

and

\sigma^{\prime}

if such a cone exists,
otherwise

\emptyset
.
Recall that
by definition

\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}

is the union of the nontrivial toric orbit closures.
Scheme-theoretically,
this amounts to

Lemma 5.3

(
[
GS22
, Lemma 3.4.1]
)
.

The colimit

\displaystyle\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}=\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\overline{O(\sigma)}

exists as an algebraic space.

From the above lemma it follows

\displaystyle\operatorname{Coh}(\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma})=\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\overline{O(\sigma)})=\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\operatorname{Coh}(\overline{O(\sigma)}).

Now,
one can easily obtain
(
5.1
).
Indeed,
by

[
GPS3
, Theorem 1.4]

and
Lemma
5.2

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fuk}(H)=\operatorname{\mu sh}_{\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)}(\mathbb{L}(\Sigma))^{op}=\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\operatorname{\mu sh}_{\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)}(\sigma^{\perp}\times\partial_{\infty}\sigma)^{op}.

As explained in the proof of

[
GS22
, Theorem 7.4.1]
,
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\operatorname{\mu sh}_{\mathbb{L}(\Sigma)}(\sigma^{\perp}\times\partial_{\infty}\sigma)^{op}=\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\sigma\in\Sigma}\operatorname{Coh}(\overline{O(\sigma)}).

5.2.
Mirror pair over a fanifold

Up to tailoring

the mirror pair

Example 5.4

(

\sigma

as the union of

and

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}(\Phi_{\Sigma})

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The algebraic space

\mathbf{T}(\Phi_{\Sigma})

coincides with

\partial\mathbf{T}_{\Sigma}

Proof.

By

As one associates the pair

\overline{O(\rho_{P})}

Let

from

Then by definition of fanifolds

When

runs through the strata of

\Phi_{\Sigma}

the inverse images

Thus we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{colim}_{\operatorname{Exit}(\Phi_{\Sigma})^{op}}\mathbf{T}(S^{(k)})=\operatorname{colim}_{0\neq\tilde{\sigma}_{S^{(k)}}\in\Sigma}\overline{O(\tilde{\sigma}_{S^{(k)}})}.

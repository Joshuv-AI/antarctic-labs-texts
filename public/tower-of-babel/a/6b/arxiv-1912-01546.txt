On the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs
A.R. Davtyan, G.M. Minasyan[MCSD], P.A. Petrosyan[MCSD]

Abstract
An edge-coloring of a graph G G with colors 1 , … , t 1,\ldots,t is an interval t t -coloring if all colors are used, and the colors of edges incident to each vertex of G G are distinct and form an integer interval. It is well-known that there are graphs that do not have interval colorings. The deficiency of a graph G G , denoted by def ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{def}(G) , is the minimum number of pendant edges whose attachment to G G leads to a graph admitting an interval coloring. In this paper we investigate the problem of determining or bounding of the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs. In particular, we obtain a tight upper bound for the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs. We also determine or bound the deficiency for some classes of complete multipartite graphs. Keywords: proper edge-coloring, interval (consecutive) coloring, deficiency, complete multipartite graph.

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected, and have no loops or multiple edges. Let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote the sets of vertices and edges of
G
, respectively. If
S\subseteq V(G)
, then
G[S]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
. The degree of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is denoted by
d_{G}(v)
, the maximum degree of vertices in
G
by
\Delta(G)
, and the chromatic index of
G
by
\chi^{\prime}(G)
. The terms and concepts that we do not define can be found in
[
3
,
12
,
20
]
.

A
proper edge-coloring
of a graph
G
is a mapping

\alpha:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha(e)\neq\alpha(e^{\prime})
for every pair of adjacent edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E(G)
. If
\alpha
is a proper edge-coloring of a graph
G
and
v\in V(G)
, then the
spectrum of a vertex
v
, denoted by
S_{G}\left(v,\alpha\right)
(or
S\left(v,\alpha\right)
), is the set of all colors appearing on edges incident to
v
. A proper edge-coloring
\alpha
of a graph
G
with colors
1,\ldots,t
is an
interval
t
-coloring
if for each vertex
v
of
G
, the set
S\left(v,\alpha\right)
is an interval of integers. A graph
G
is
interval colorable
if it has an interval
t
-coloring for some positive integer
t
. The set of all interval colorable graphs is denoted by
\mathfrak{N}
. The concept of interval edge-coloring of graphs was introduced by Asratian and Kamalian
[
1
]
in 1987. In
[
1
]
, the authors noted that if
G\in\mathfrak{N}
, then
\chi^{\prime}\left(G\right)=\Delta(G)
.
Asratian and Kamalian also proved
[
1
,
2
]
that if a triangle-free graph
G
admits an interval
t
-coloring, then
t\leq\left|V(G)\right|-1
. Generally, it is an
NP
-complete problem to determine whether a bipartite graph has an interval coloring
[
17
]
. In
[
9
,
10
]
,
Kamalian investigated interval colorings of complete bipartite
graphs and trees. In particular, he proved that the complete
bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
has an interval
t
-coloring if and only
if
m+n-\gcd(m,n)\leq t\leq m+n-1
, where
\gcd(m,n)
is the
greatest common divisor of
m
and
n
. In
[
13
,
15
]
,
Petrosyan, Khachatrian and Tananyan proved that the
n
-dimesional
cube
Q_{n}
has an interval
t
-coloring if and only if
n\leq t\leq\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2}
. There are many papers devoted to this topic, in particular, surveys on the topic can be found in some books
[
3
,
12
]
.

There are graphs that have no interval colorings; a smallest example is
K_{3}
. Since not all graphs admit an interval coloring, it is naturally to consider a measure of closeness for a graph to be interval colorable. In

[
7
]
, Giaro, Kubale and Małafiejski introduced such a measure which is called deficiency of a graph. The
deficiency
\mathrm{def}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum number of pendant edges whose attachment to
G
makes it interval colorable.
The concept of deficiency of graphs can be also defined in terms of proper edge-colorings. The
deficiency of a proper edge-coloring
\alpha
at vertex
v\in V(G)
, denoted by
\mathrm{def}(v,\alpha)
, is the minimum number of integers which must be added to
S\left(v,\alpha\right)
to form an interval, and the
deficiency
\mathrm{def}\left(G,\alpha\right)
of a proper edge-coloring
\alpha
of
G
is defined as the sum
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\mathrm{def}(v,\alpha)
. So,
\mathrm{def}(G)=\min_{\alpha}\mathrm{def}\left(G,\alpha\right)
, where minimum is taken over all possible proper edge-colorings of
G
. Clearly,

\mathrm{def}(G)=0
if and only if
G\in\mathfrak{N}
.
In general, the problem of determining the deficiency of a graph is

NP
-complete, even for regular and bipartite graphs

[
1
,
17
,
7
]
. In
[
7
]
,
Giaro, Kubale and Małafiejski obtained some results on the
deficiency of bipartite graphs. In particular, they showed that
there are bipartite graphs whose deficiency approaches the number of vertices. In
[
8
]
, the same authors proved that if
G
is an
r
-regular graph with an odd number of vertices, then
\mathrm{def}(G)\geq\frac{r}{2}
, and determined the deficiency of odd cycles, complete graphs, wheels and broken wheels. In
[
18
]
, Schwartz studied the deficiency of regular graphs. In particular, he obtained tight bounds on the deficiency of regular graphs and proved that there are regular graphs with high deficiency. Bouchard, Hertz and Desaulniers
[
5
]
derived some lower bounds on the deficiency of graphs and provided a tabu search algorithm for finding a proper edge-coloring with minimum deficiency of a graph. Borowiecka-Olszewska, Drgas-Burchardt and Hałuszczak
[
4
]
studied the deficiency of
k
-trees. In particular, they determined the deficiency of all
k
-trees with maximum degree at most
2k
, where

k\in\{2,3,4\}
. They also proved that the following lower bound
on
\mathrm{def}(G)
holds: if
G
is a graph with an odd number of
vertices, then
\mathrm{def}(G)\geq\frac{2|E(G)|-(|V(G)|-1)\Delta(G)}{2}
. Recently, Khachatrian
[
11
]
proved that the following upper bound on
\mathrm{def}(G)
for outerplanar graphs holds: if
G
is an outerplanar graph, then
\mathrm{def}(G)\leq\frac{|V(G)|-2}{og(G)-2}
, where
og(G)
is the odd girth of the graph.

One of the less-investigated problems related to the deficiency of graphs is the problem of determining the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs. Several special cases of complete multipartite graphs were considered by some authors. In partitcular, Giaro, Kubale and Małafiejski
[
8
]
proved the following result for the complete graph
K_{n}
:
\mathrm{def}(K_{n})=0
if
n
is even, and
\mathrm{def}(K_{n})=\frac{n-1}{2}
if
n
is odd. Later, Petrosyan and Khachatrian
[
16
]
proved that for near-complete graphs
\mathrm{def}(K_{2n+1}-e)=n-1
(where
e
is an edge of
K_{2n+1}
), thereby confirming a conjecture of Borowiecka-Olszewska, Drgas-Burchardt and Hałuszczak
[
4
]
. They also proved the following result for the complete tripartite graph
K_{1,m,n}
:
\mathrm{def}(K_{1,m,n})=0
if
\gcd(m+1,n+1)=1
, and
\mathrm{def}(K_{1,m,n})=1
otherwise.

In this paper, we obtain a tight upper bound for the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs. We also determine or bound the deficiency for some classes of complete multipartite graphs.

2
Notation, definitions and auxiliary results

A graph
G
is called a complete
r
-partite (
r\geq 2
) graph if
its vertices can be partitioned into
r
nonempty independent sets

V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
such that each vertex in
V_{i}
is adjacent to all
the other vertices in
V_{j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq r
. Let

K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}
denote a complete
r
-partite graph
with independent sets
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
of sizes

n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs. The join
G\vee H
of graphs
G
and
H
is defined as follows:

V(G\vee H)=V(G)\cup V(H)
,

E(G\vee H)=E(G)\cup E(H)\cup\left\{uv\colon\,u\in V(G)\wedge v\in V(H)\right\}
.

For two positive integers
a
and
b
with
a\leq b
, we denote by

\left[a,b\right]
the interval of integers

\left\{a,a+1,\ldots,b-1,b\right\}
. If
a>b
, then
\left[a,b\right]=\emptyset
.
For an interval
[a,b]
and a nonnegative number
p
, the notation
[a,b]\oplus p
means:
[a+p,b+p]
.

Let
L=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\right)
be an ordered sequence of
nonnegative integers. The smallest and largest elements of
L
are
denoted by
\underline{L}
and
\overline{L}
, respectively. The
length (the number of elements) of
L
is denoted by
|L|
. An
ordered sequence
L=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\right)
is called a

continuous sequence
if it contains all integers between

\underline{L}
and
\overline{L}
.

Let
A
be a finite set of integers. The deficiency
\mathrm{def}(A)
of
A

is the number of integers between
\min A
and
\max A
not
belonging to
A
. Clearly,
\mathrm{def}(A)=\max A-\min A-|A|+1
. A set
A
with
\mathrm{def}(A)=0
is an integer interval. Note that if
\alpha
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
and
v\in V(G)
, then
\mathrm{def}(v,\alpha)=\mathrm{def}\left(S\left(v,\alpha\right)\right)
.

If
\alpha
is a proper edge-coloring of a graph
G
and
v\in V(G)
, then the smallest and largest colors
of the spectrum
S\left(v,\alpha\right)
are denoted by
\underline{S}\left(v,\alpha\right)
and
\overline{S}\left(v,\alpha\right)
,
respectively. Let
\alpha
be a proper edge-coloring of
G
and

V^{\prime}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
. Consider the sets

S\left(v_{1},\alpha\right),\ldots,S\left(v_{k},\alpha\right)
.
For a coloring
\alpha
of
G
and
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
,
define two ordered sequences
LSE(V^{\prime},\alpha)
(
Lower
Spectral Edge
) and
USE(V^{\prime},\alpha)
(
Upper Spectral
Edge
) as follows:

LSE(V^{\prime},\alpha)=\left(\underline{S}\left(v_{i_{1}},\alpha\right),\underline{S}\left(v_{i_{2}},\alpha\right),\dots,\underline{S}\left(v_{i_{k}},\alpha\right)\right)
,

where
\underline{S}\left(v_{i_{l}},\alpha\right)\leq\underline{S}\left(v_{i_{l+1}},\alpha\right)
for
1\leq l\leq k-1
, and

USE(V^{\prime},\alpha)=\left(\overline{S}\left(v_{j_{1}},\alpha\right),\overline{S}\left(v_{j_{2}},\alpha\right),\dots,\overline{S}\left(v_{j_{k}},\alpha\right)\right)
,

where
\overline{S}\left(v_{j_{l}},\alpha\right)\leq\overline{S}\left(v_{j_{l+1}},\alpha\right)
for
1\leq l\leq k-1
.

We will use the following results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
19
]

If
K_{n,n}
is a complete bipartite graph with
bipartition
(U,V)
, then for any continuous sequence
L
with
length
n
,
K_{n,n}
has a proper edge-coloring
\alpha
such that:

1)

for any
u\in U
,
S(u,\alpha)
is an integer interval;

2)

for any
v\in V
,
S(v,\alpha)
is an integer interval;

3)

LSE(U,\alpha)=LSE(V,\alpha)=L
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
9
,
10
]

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,nm}
with bipartition
(U,V)
has an interval
nm
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any
u\in U
,
S(u,\alpha)=[1,nm]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
14
]

If
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph with
n
vertices in each part, then
G\in\mathfrak{N}
if and only if
nr
is even. Moreover, if
nr
is even, then
G
has an interval
(r-1)n
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any
v\in V(G)
,
S(v,\alpha)=[1,(r-1)n]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
(
r\geq 3
) with
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
is odd,

\mathrm{def}\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)\geq\sum_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(n_{1}+1)n_{i}-n_{i}^{2}}{2}.

Proof.

Clearly,
|V(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}})|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
,
|E(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}})|=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq r\end{subarray}}{n_{i}n_{j}}
and
\Delta(K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}})=\sum_{i=2}^{r}n_{i}
.
In
[
4
]
, it was proved that if
G
is a graph with an odd number of
vertices, then
\mathrm{def}(G)\geq\frac{2|E(G)|-(|V(G)|-1)\Delta(G)}{2}
. From this and taking into account that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
is odd, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{def}\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq r\end{subarray}}{2n_{i}n_{j}}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}-1)(n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r})\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq r\end{subarray}}{n_{i}n_{j}}-

\displaystyle-\frac{(n_{1}n_{2}+\ldots n_{1}n_{r}+n_{2}^{2}+\cdots n_{2}n_{r}+\ldots+n_{r}n_{2}+\ldots+n_{r}^{2})-(n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r})}{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}+\cdots+n_{1}n_{r}-(n_{2}^{2}+\cdots+n_{r}^{2})+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r}}{2}=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{r}{\frac{(n_{1}+1)n_{i}-n_{i}^{2}}{2}}.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

[
6
]
If for a graph
G
, there exists a number
d
such that
d
divides
d_{G}(v)
for every
v\in V(G)
and
d
does not divide
|E(G)|
, then
G\notin\mathfrak{N}
.

3
Bounds on the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs

In this section we investigate the problem of determining or bounding of the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs. We begin our consideration with an upper bound on the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
(
r\geq 3
) with
n_{1}\geq n_{r}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r-1}
,

\mathrm{def}\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}n_{i}^{2}.

Proof.

For
0\leq i\leq r
, define a sum
\sigma(i)
as follows:

\sigma(i)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$0$,&if $i=0$,\\
$\sum_{j=1}^{i}n_{j}$,&if $1\leq i\leq r$.\\
\end{tabular}\right.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
be the
r
independent sets of vertices of

K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}
, and let

V_{i}=\left\{v_{\sigma(i-1)+1},\ldots,v_{\sigma(i)}\right\}

for

1\leq i\leq r
. Also, let
\textbf{s}=\min\{i+j:v_{i}v_{j}\in E\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)\}
and
\textbf{S}=\max\{i+j:v_{i}v_{j}\in E\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)\}
.

Define an edge-coloring
\alpha
of
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}

as follows: for any
v_{i}v_{j}\in E\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)
, let

\alpha\left(v_{i}v_{j}\right)=1+i+j-\textbf{s}.

Let us prove that
\alpha
is a proper edge-coloring with
\mathrm{def}\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}},\alpha\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}n_{i}^{2}
.

By the definition, we have that
\alpha
is a proper edge-coloring with colors
1,2,\ldots,1+\textbf{S}-\textbf{s}
and for each
v_{i}\in V_{1}
or
v_{i}\in V_{r}
,
\mathrm{def}(v_{i},\alpha)=0
. Next let
v_{i}\in V_{l}
, where
2\leq l\leq r-1
. By the definition of
\alpha
, we have that
S\left(v_{i},\alpha\right)

contains colors
i+2-\textbf{s},\ldots,1+i+\sigma(r)-\textbf{s}
except for

1+i+\sigma(l-1)+1-\textbf{s},\ldots,1+i+\sigma(l)-\textbf{s}.

This implies that for each
v_{i}\in V_{l}
(
2\leq l\leq r-1
),
\mathrm{def}(v_{i},\alpha)=n_{l}
. Thus,
\mathrm{def}\left(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}n_{i}^{2}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
n,r,t\in\mathbb{N}
, if
nr
is even, then for the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n,trn}
,

\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,trn})=0.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{n,\ldots,n,trn}
be the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph and
V(G)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cup W
, where
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
and
W
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=n(1\leq i\leq r)
and
|W|=trn
.

Let
H_{1}=G\Big[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\Big]
and
H_{2}=\Big(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}G[V_{i}]\Big)\lor G[W]
. Clearly,
E(H_{1})\cap E(H_{2})=\varnothing
and

. Moreover,
H_{1}
is isomorphic to the complete
r
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
and
H_{2}
is isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph
K_{rn,trn}
.

By Lemma
2.3
,

has an interval
(r-1)n
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any

,
S_{H_{1}}(v,\alpha)=[1,(r-1)n]
. By Lemma
2.2
,
H_{2}
has an interval
trn
-coloring
\beta
such that for any

,

. Now we define an edge-coloring
\gamma
as follows: for any

, let

\gamma(e)=\begin{cases}\alpha(e),&\text{if $e\in E(H_{1})$},\\
\beta(e)+(r-1)n,&\text{if $e\in E(H_{2})$}.\end{cases}

Let us show that
\gamma
is an interval
(tr+r-1)n
-coloring.

By the definition of
\gamma
, we have

This shows that
\gamma
is an interval
(tr+r-1)n
-coloring of
G
; thus
\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,trn})=0
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
n,r,t\in\mathbb{N}
, for the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n,(tr+1)n}
,
\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,(tr+1)n})=0

if and only if
n(r+1)
is even.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{n,\ldots,n,(tr+1)n}
be the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph.

The necessity we prove by contradiction. Assume that there are natural numbers
t,r
and
n
such that
\mathrm{def}(G)=0
and
n(r+1)
is odd. First we calculate the number of edges of
G
:

|E(G)|=nr(tr+1)n+\frac{r(r-1)n^{2}}{2}=rn^{2}\Big(tr+1+\frac{r-1}{2}\Big).

Let
d
be the greatest common divisor of degrees of vertices of
G
. Clearly for any
v\in V(G)
either
d_{G}(v)=nr
or
d_{G}(v)=n(r-1)+(rt+1)n
. From here, we obtain

d=\gcd(nr,nr+nrt)=nr\gcd(1,1+t)=nr.

By Theorem
2.5
, we have
|E(G)|\equiv 0\mod d
, which means the number
n(tr+1+\frac{r-1}{2})
must be integer, which is a contradiction, since
n(r+1)
is odd.

For the proof of sufficiency, let

V(G)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cup U\cup W
,
where
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
and
U\cup W
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=n

(1\leq i\leq r)
,
|U|=n
and
|W|=trn
.

Let
H_{1}=G\Big[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cup U\Big]
and
H_{2}=\Big(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}G[V_{i}]\Big)\lor G[W]
. Clearly,
E(H_{1})\cap E(H_{2})=\varnothing
and
E(H_{1})\cup E(H_{2})=E(G)
. Moreover,
H_{1}
is isomorphic to the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
and
H_{2}
is isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph
K_{rn,trn}
.

By Lemma
2.3
,
H_{1}
has an interval
rn
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any
v\in V(H_{1})

S_{H_{1}}(v,\alpha)=[1,rn]
. By Lemma
2.2
,
H_{2}
has an interval
trn
-coloring
\beta
such that for any
u\in\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}
,
S_{H_{2}}(u,\beta)=[1,trn]
. Now we define an edge-coloring
\gamma
as follows: for any
e\in E(G)
, let

\gamma(e)=\begin{cases}\alpha(e),&\text{if $e\in E(H_{1})$},\\
\beta(e)+rn,&\text{if $e\in E(H_{2})$}.\end{cases}

Let us show that
\gamma
is an interval
(t+1)rn
-coloring.

By the definition of
\gamma
, we have

1)

for any
v\in\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}
,

S_{G}(v,\gamma)=S_{H_{1}}(v,\alpha)\cup S_{H_{2}}(v,\beta)=[1,rn]\cup[rn+1,rn+trn]=[1,(t+1)rn],

2)

for any
v\in W
,

S_{G}(v,\gamma)=S_{H_{2}}(v,\beta)\oplus rn=[\underline{S}_{H_{2}}(v,\beta)+rn,\overline{S}_{H_{2}}(v,\beta)+rn].

This shows that
\gamma
is an interval
(t+1)rn
-coloring of
G
; thus
\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,(tr+1)n})=0
.
∎

Now we consider the deficiency of complete multipartite graphs which are close to balanced complete multipartite graphs. For such graphs we prove lower and upper bounds on the deficiency which are differ from each other by one.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, if
n(r+1)
is even, then for the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n,n+1}
,

\frac{n(r-1)}{2}\leq\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,n+1})\leq\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{n,\ldots,n,n+1}
be the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph.

First we prove the lower bound. Since
|V(G)|=n(r+1)+1
is odd, by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\mathrm{def}(G)\geq(r-1)\frac{(n+1)n-n^{2}}{2}+\frac{(n+1)(n+1)-(n+1)^{2}}{2}=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}.

Next we show that
\mathrm{def}(G)\leq\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1
. We distinguish this part of the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

r
is odd.

Let
V(G)=\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}V_{i}\right)\cup\{w\}
, where
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
and
V_{r+1}\cup\{w\}
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=n

(1\leq i\leq r)
and
|V_{r+1}\cup\{w\}|=n+1
. Also, let
V_{i}=\left\{v_{j}^{(i)}:1\leq j\leq n\right\}

(1\leq i\leq r+1)
and
H=G\left[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}V_{i}\right]
.
Clearly,
H
is isomorphic to the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
.
In
[
14
]
, it was proved that
H
has an interval

\big((3r+1)\frac{n}{2}-1\big)
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any
1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2}
and
1\leq j\leq n
,

\displaystyle S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\alpha\right)=S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\alpha\right)=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i-1)n+rn-1]=

\displaystyle=[j+(i-1)n,j+(r+i-1)n-1].

Define an edge-coloring
\beta
of
G
as follows: for any
e\in E(G)
, let

\beta(e)=\begin{cases}\alpha(e)+1,&\text{if }e\in E(H),\\
(i-1)n+j,&\text{if }e=wv^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,\\
(r+i-1)n+j+1,&\text{if }e=wv^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n.\end{cases}

Let us prove that
\beta
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{(3r+1)n}{2}
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\beta)=\mathrm{def}\left(w,\beta\right)=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1
.

By the definition of
\beta
, we have

1)

for
1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\beta\right)=[(i-1)n+j,(i+r-1)n+j],

2)

for
1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\beta\right)=[(i-1)n+j+1,(i+r-1)n+j+1],

3)

for
i=\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\beta\right)=[(i-1)n+j+1,(i+r-1)n+j],

4)

S\left(w,\beta\right)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{(i-1)n+j\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{(r+i-1)n+j+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}[(i-1)n+1,in]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}[(r+i-1)n+2,(r+i)n+1]=

\left[1,\frac{r+1}{2}n\right]\cup\left[rn+2,\left(r+\frac{r-1}{2}\right)n+1\right].

This shows that for any
v\in V(G)\setminus\{w\}
,
S(v,\beta)
is an integer interval. Now we consider the spectrums of the vertex
w
. By
4)
, we obtain

a)

|S(w,\beta)|=\frac{r+1}{2}n+(r+\frac{r-1}{2})n-rn=rn=d_{G}(w),

b)

\mathrm{def}(w,\beta)=\overline{S}(w,\beta)-\underline{S}(w,\beta)-|S(w,\beta)|+1=(r+\frac{r-1}{2})n-rn+1=\frac{r-1}{2}n+1.

This implies that
\beta
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{(3r+1)n}{2}
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\beta)=\mathrm{def}(w,\beta)=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1
.

Case 2:

n
is even.

Let
V(G)=\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2(r+1)}V_{i}\right)\cup\{w\}
, where
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
,
V_{2i}\cup V_{2i+3}

(1\leq i\leq r-1)
and
V_{2r}\cup V_{2(r+1)}\cup\{w\}
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=\frac{n}{2}

(1\leq i\leq 2(r+1))
. Also, let
V_{i}=\left\{v_{j}^{(i)}:1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}\right\}

(1\leq i\leq 2(r+1))
and

H=G\left[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2(r+1)}V_{i}\right]
.
Clearly,
H
is isomorphic to the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
. In
[
14
]
, it was proved that
H
has an interval

\left(\frac{(3r+1)n}{2}-1\right)
-coloring
\gamma
such that for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
and
1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}
,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\gamma\right)=S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\gamma\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}-1\right].

Define an edge-coloring
\varphi
of
G
as follows: for any
e\in E(G)
, let

\varphi(e)=\begin{cases}\gamma(e)+1,&\text{if }e\in E(H),\\
(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+j,&\text{if }e=wv^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r+1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+j+1,&\text{if }e=wv^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r-1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}.\end{cases}

Let us prove that
\varphi
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{(3r+1)n}{2}
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\varphi)=\mathrm{def}(w,\varphi)=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1
.

By the definition of
\varphi
, we have

1’)

for
1\leq i\leq r+1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}\right],

2’)

for
1\leq i\leq r-1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\right],

3’)

for
i\in\{r,r+1\},1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}\right],

4’)

S(w,\varphi)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\left[(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,i\frac{n}{2}\right]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\left[(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+2,(2r+i)\frac{n}{2}+1\right]=

\left[1,\frac{n(r+1)}{2}\right]\cup\left[nr+2,\frac{(3r-1)n}{2}+1\right].

This shows that for any
v\in V(G)\setminus\{w\}
,
S(v,\varphi)
is an integer interval. Now we consider the spectrums of the vertex
w
. By
4^{\prime})
, we obtain

a’)

|S(w,\varphi)|=\frac{n(r+1)}{2}+\frac{n(3r-1)}{2}-nr=nr=d_{G}(w),

b’)

\mathrm{def}(w,\varphi)=\overline{S}(w,\varphi)-\underline{S}(w,\varphi)-|S(w,\varphi)|+1=\frac{n(3r-1)}{2}-nr+1=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1.

This implies that
\varphi
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{(3r+1)n}{2}
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\varphi)=\mathrm{def}(w,\varphi)=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1
.
∎

Next, we consider the deficiency of complete
(r+1)
-partite graphs with
r
parts with the same size
n
and one part with size
n+2
. For such graphs we prove an upper bound on the deficiency.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, if
n(r+1)
is even, then for the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n,n+2}
,

\mathrm{def}(K_{n,\ldots,n,n+2})\leq nr+2.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{n,\ldots,n,n+2}
be the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph.
We distinguish our proof into two cases.

Case 1:

r
is odd.

Let
V(G)=\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}V_{i}\right)\cup\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
, where
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
and
V_{r+1}\cup\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=n

(1\leq i\leq r)
and
|V_{r+1}\cup\{w_{1},w_{2}\}|=n+2
. Also, let
H=G\left[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}V_{i}\right]
and
V_{i}=\left\{v_{j}^{(i)}:1\leq j\leq n\right\}

(1\leq i\leq r+1)
.
Clearly,
H
is isomorphic to the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
.
In
[
14
]
, it was proved that
H
has an interval

\big(\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}-1\big)
-coloring
\alpha
such that for any
1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2}
and
1\leq j\leq n
,

\displaystyle S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\alpha\right)=S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\alpha\right)=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i-1)n+rn-1]=

\displaystyle=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i+r-1)n-1].

Define an edge-coloring
\beta
of
G
as follows: for any
e\in E(G)
, let

\beta(e)=\begin{cases}\alpha(e)+1,&\text{if }e\in E(H),\\
j+(i-1)n,&\text{if }e=w_{1}v^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,\\
j+(r+i-1)n+1,&\text{if }e=w_{1}v^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,\\
j+(r+i-1)n+1,&\text{if }e=w_{2}v^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,\\
j+(i-1)n,&\text{if }e=w_{2}v^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n.\end{cases}

Let us prove that
\beta
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}+1
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\beta)=\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\beta)+\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\beta)=nr+2
.

By the definition of
\beta
, we have

1)

for
1\leq i\leq\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\beta\right)=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i+r-1)n+1],

2)

for
1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\beta\right)=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i+r-1)n+1],

3)

for
i=\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\beta\right)=[j+(i-1)n+1,j+(i+r-1)n],

4)

S\left(w_{1},\beta\right)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{j+(i-1)n\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{j+(r+i-1)n+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\left[(i-1)n+1,in\right]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\left[(r+i-1)n+2,(r+i)n+1\right]=

\left[1,\frac{r+1}{2}n\right]\cup\left[2+rn,1+\left(r+\frac{r-1}{2}\right)n\right],

5)

S\left(w_{2},\beta\right)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{j+(i-1)n\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{n}\{j+(r+i-1)n+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\left[(i-1)n+1,in\right]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\left[(r+i-1)n+2,(r+i)n+1\right]=

\left[1,\frac{r-1}{2}n\right]\cup\left[2+rn,1+\left(r+\frac{r+1}{2}\right)n\right].

This shows that for any
v\in V(G)\setminus\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
,
S(v,\beta)
is an integer interval. Now we consider the spectrums of the vertices
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. By
4)
and
5)
, we obtain

a)

|S(w_{1},\beta)|=|S(w_{2},\beta)|=rn=d_{G}(w_{1})=d_{G}(w_{2}),

b)

\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\beta)=\overline{S}(w_{1},\beta)-\underline{S}(w_{1},\beta)-|S(w_{1},\beta)|+1=(r+\frac{r-1}{2})n-rn+1=\frac{r-1}{2}n+1,

c)

\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\beta)=\overline{S}(w_{2},\beta)-\underline{S}(w_{2},\beta)-|S(w_{2},\beta)|+1=(r+\frac{r+1}{2})n-rn+1=\frac{r+1}{2}n+1.

This implies that
\beta
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}+1
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\beta)=\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\beta)+\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\beta)=nr+2
.

Case 2:

n
is even.

Let
V(G)=\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2(r+1)}V_{i}\right)\cup\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
, where
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
,
V_{2i}\cup V_{2i+3}

(1\leq i\leq r-1)
and
V_{2r}\cup V_{2(r+1)}\cup\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
are parts of
G
with sizes
|V_{i}|=\frac{n}{2}

(1\leq i\leq 2(r+1))
. Also, let
H=G\left[\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2(r+1)}V_{i}\right]
and
V_{i}=\left\{v_{j}^{(i)}:1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}\right\}

(1\leq i\leq 2(r+1))
.
Clearly,
H
is isomorphic to the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph
K_{n,\ldots,n}
.
In
[
14
]
, it was proved that
H
has an interval

\big(\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}-1\big)
-coloring
\gamma
such that for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
and
1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}
,

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\gamma\right)=S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\gamma\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}-1\right].

Define an edge-coloring
\varphi
of
G
as follows: for any
e\in E(G)
, let

\varphi(e)=\begin{cases}\gamma(e)+1,&\text{if }e\in E(H),\\
j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},&\text{if }e=w_{1}v^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r+1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,&\text{if }e=w_{1}v^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r-1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,&\text{if }e=w_{2}v^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r+1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},&\text{if }e=w_{2}v^{(2i)}_{j},1\leq i\leq r-1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}.\end{cases}

Let us prove that
\varphi
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}+1
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\varphi)=\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\varphi)+\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\varphi)=nr+2
.

By the definition of
\varphi
, we have

1’)

for
1\leq i\leq r+1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\right],

2’)

for
1\leq i\leq r-1,1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2},j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\right],

3’)

for
i\in\{r,r+1\},1\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2},

S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\varphi\right)=\left[j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,j+(i+2r-1)\frac{n}{2}\right],

4’)

S\left(w_{1},\varphi\right)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\left[(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,i\frac{n}{2}\right]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\left[(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+2,(2r+i)\frac{n}{2}+1\right]=

\left[1,(r+1)\frac{n}{2}\right]\cup\left[2+2r\frac{n}{2},1+(3r-1)\frac{n}{2}\right],

5’)

S\left(w_{2},\varphi\right)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(i-1)\frac{n}{2}\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\{j+(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1\}=

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\left[(i-1)\frac{n}{2}+1,i\frac{n}{2}\right]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\left[(2r+i-1)\frac{n}{2}+2,(2r+i)\frac{n}{2}+1\right]=

\left[1,\frac{n(r-1)}{2}\right]\cup\left[nr+2,\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}+1\right].

This shows that for any
v\in V(G)\setminus\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
,
S(v,\varphi)
is an integer interval. Now we consider the spectrums of the vertices
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. By
4^{\prime})
and
5^{\prime})
, we obtain

a’)

|S(w_{1},\varphi)|=|S(w_{2},\varphi)|=rn=d_{G}(w_{1})=d_{G}(w_{2}),

b’)

\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\varphi)=\overline{S}(w_{1},\varphi)-\underline{S}(w_{1},\varphi)-|S(w_{1},\varphi)|+1=\frac{n(3r-1)}{2}-nr+1=\frac{n(r-1)}{2}+1,

c’)

\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\varphi)=\overline{S}(w_{2},\varphi)-\underline{S}(w_{2},\varphi)-|S(w_{2},\varphi)|+1=\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}-nr+1=\frac{n(r+1)}{2}+1.

This implies that
\varphi
is a proper edge-coloring of
G
with
\frac{n(3r+1)}{2}+1
colors such that
\mathrm{def}(G,\varphi)=\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\varphi)+\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\varphi)=nr+2
.
∎

Now we consider the deficiency of complete

(1\leq i\leq r-1)

\displaystyle S\left(v_{j}^{(2i-1)},\alpha\right)=S\left(v_{j}^{(2i)},\alpha\right)=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i-1)n+rn-1]=

\displaystyle=[j+(i-1)n,j+(i+r-1)n-1].

\beta

\beta(e)=\begin{cases}\alpha(e)+1,&\text{if }e\in E(H),\\
j+(r+i-1)n+1,&\text{if }e=w_{1}v^{(2i-1)}_{j},1\leq i<\frac{r+1}{2},1\leq j\leq n,\\

is a proper edge-coloring of

\mathrm{def}(G,\beta)=\mathrm{def}(w_{1},\beta)+\mathrm{def}(w_{2},\beta)=n(r-1)

1)

Finally we consider the deficiency of some class of complete

Theorem 3.8
.

For any

\mathrm{def}(K_{l,m,n,l+m+n})\leq\min\{l^{2},m^{2},n^{2}\}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

By the definition of

for

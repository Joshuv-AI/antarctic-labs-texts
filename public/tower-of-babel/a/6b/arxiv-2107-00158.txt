Local available quantum correlations of X states: The symmetric and anti-symmetric cases Preprint: SB/F/492-21
Hermann Albrecht, David Bellorín, Douglas F. Mundarain

Abstract
Local available quantum correlations (LAQC), as defined by Mundarain et al., are analyzed for 2-qubit X states with local Bloch vectors of equal magnitude. Symmetric X-states are invariant under the exchange of subsystems, hence having the same local Bloch vector. On the other hand, anti-symmetric X states have local Bloch vectors with an equal magnitude but opposite direction (anti-parallel). In both cases, we obtain exact analytical expressions for their LAQC quantifier. We present some examples and compare this quantum correlation to concurrence and quantum discord. We have also included Markovian decoherence, with Werner states under amplitude damping decoherence. As is the case for depolarization and phase damping, no sudden death behavior occurs for the LAQC of these states with this quantum channel.



I
Introduction

Quantum correlations and their quantification are of central importance in Quantum Information Theory (QIT). Entanglement
Horodecki-Ent
was seen as the main quantum resource until the development of Quantum Discord
qDiscord-Olliver
;
qDiscord-Henderson
in 2001. Since then, the establishment of other quantum correlations and their quantifiers
Modi-qDiscord
has become a very active field of research in Information Theory, with applications in Quantum Computation and related specialties.

Local measurements play a central role in properly defining correlations. The ability of a local observer to infer the results of another one from his own must be quantified by these. Although already present in entanglement
EPR-1935
;
schrodinger_1935
;
schrodinger_1936
, such a feature is at the core of defining the above-mentioned Quantum Discord:

D_{A}(\rho_{AB})\equiv\min_{\quantity{\Pi_i^A}}\quantity{I(\rho_{AB})-I\qty\big[(\Pi^A\otimes\mathbbm{1}_2)\rho_{AB}]},

(1)

which is based on comparing the quantum Mutual Information, defined for the original state
\rho_{AB}
as

I\quantity(\rho_{AB})\equiv S\quantity(\rho_A)+S\quantity(\rho_B)-S\quantity(\rho_{AB}),

(2)

where
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are the reduced operators, i.e. marginals, with a corresponding post-measurement state in the absence of readout, where the measurement is performed locally on subsystem A. This latter state is referred to as a classical-quantum (or A-classical) state,

\begin{split}\rho^{cq}_{AB}=&\sum_{i}\,p_{i}\,\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i}\\
=&\sum_{i}\,p_{i}\,\Pi^{(A)}_{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i},\end{split}

(3)

and all such 2-qubit states constitute a set, denoted by
\Omega_{o}
. An analogous set, denoted by
\Omega_{o}^{\prime}
, is readily defined when the local measurement is performed on the B subsystem. That is,

D_{B}(\rho_{AB})\equiv\min_{\{\Pi_{i}^{B}\}}\left\{I(\rho_{AB})-I[(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\Pi^{B})\rho_{AB}]\right\}\,.

(4)

Quantum correlations defined using the set
\Omega_{o}
(or
\Omega_{o}^{\prime}
) are called Discords and are in general not symmetric so that
D_{A}(\rho_{AB})\neq{}D_{B}(\rho_{AB})
.

Another set of quantum correlations and quantifiers was developed by considering classical states, that is, post-measurement states in the absence of readout whose measurements were performed locally on both subsystems:

\rho_{AB}^{c}=\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\Pi^{(A)}_{i}\otimes\Pi^{(B)}_{j}\,.

(5)

A special subset of such states are product states,

\rho_{AB}^{\Pi}=\sum_{i,j}p_{i}\Pi^{(A)}_{i}\otimes{}p_{j}\Pi^{(B)}_{j}=\rho^{(A)}\otimes\rho^{(B)},

(6)

also referred to as uncorrelated states. For these, the coefficients
p_{ij}
in eq. (
5
) need to be factorizable,
p_{ij}=p_{i}p_{j}
. We denote the sets of classical and product 2-qubit states as
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega_{p}
, respectively.

Wu et al. considered general quantum correlations defined in terms of local bipartite measurements in
Wu_ComplementaryBases
and, in a brief final appendix, outlined symmetric quantum correlations in relation to mutual information. Mundarain et al. in
LAQC
developed the so-called Local Available Quantum Correlations (LAQC) by focusing on a slightly different version of those symmetric correlations, defining them in terms of mutual information of local bipartite measurements on the so-called complementary basis of a previously determined optimal computational basis.

This work is focused on determining the LAQC quantifier for two particular sets of the so-called X states
EstadosX

\displaystyle\rho^{X}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\rho_{11}&0&0&\rho_{14}\\
0&\rho_{22}&\rho_{23}&0\\
0&\rho_{32}&\rho_{33}&0\\
\rho_{41}&0&0&\rho_{44}\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

This family of 2-qubit states has been studied extensively
Quesada-XStates
and are a widely used set of 2-qubit density matrices in QIT. That is because any arbitrary 2-qubit state
\rho_{AB}
can be mapped to such

, preserving its main characteristics, e.g. quantum correlations

;

.

Experimentally, this type of 2-qubit states has been achieved with cold trapped atoms and ions

and in non-linear crystals

. Also, X states appear naturally in spin chain systems when the reduced matrix of two neighboring spins is studied

;
XStates-Ferromagnets
and any pure 2-qubit spin-
\frac{1}{2}
state evolves to an X state via decoherence due to magnetic field fluctuations

.

An algebraic characterization of 2-qubit X states was achieved by Rau in
Rau_2009-Xstates_algebra
, revealing an underlying symmetry of
su(2)\times{}su(2)\times{}u(1)
. Therefore, the seven operators of this
su(4)
subalgebra are the invariance set of this family of 2-qubit states. Such invariance allows generalizing the definition of X states beyond (
I
). This result facilitates characterizing quantum channels that are closed maps within this set.

Concurrence is a
bona fide
entanglement measure introduced by Wootters

as

\mathcal{C}\equiv\max\{0,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}\},

(12)

where
\quantity{\lambda_i}
are the decreasing ordered eigenvalues of
\mathbf{R}=\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\,\tilde{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}}
, with
\tilde{\rho}=(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})\rho^{*}(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})
,
\rho^{*}
the complex conjugate of
\rho
, and
\sigma_{y}
the corresponding Pauli matrix. For X states, a direct calculation shows that Concurrence takes a much simpler expression

:

\mathcal{C}_{X}=\frac{1}{2}\,\max\left\{0,\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\right\}

(13)

where

\mathcal{C}_{1}\equiv 2\left(|\rho_{14}|-\sqrt{\rho_{22}\,\rho_{33}}\right),\quad\mathcal{C}_{2}\equiv 2\left(|\rho_{23}|-\sqrt{\rho_{11}\,\rho_{44}}\right).

A closed analytical expression of the quantum discord of X states has not been achieved yet. In 2010, Ali et al.
Ali-QD-Xstates
proposed analytical solutions for the general 7-parameter X states, but some counterexamples were reported
Chen-QD-Xstates-Ex1
;
Lu-QD-Xstates-Ex2
;
Huang-QD-Xstates-WorstCaseScenario
. Later, Vinjanampathy and Rau corrected the initial overstatement in
Rau-Vinjanampathy_QD_2012
, and in 2018 Rau found expressions for higher dimensional X states

. For a subgroup of this family of 2-qubit states, where
\rho_{22}=\rho_{33}
, some alternatives have been proposed, such as the one presented by Liao et al.

. In this article, we use the results of Li et al.

, which we briefly present in an appendix.

We can divide the family of X states using different criteria. In particular, we can define two equivalence classes depending on whether their local Bloch vectors have equal norms. In this paper, we are focused on studying canonical X states that have equal-normed local Bloch vectors. For this equivalence class, the corresponding density matrix satisfies
\rho_{22}=\rho_{33}
or
\rho_{11}=\rho_{44}
in (
I
). Since there are two cases for this equivalence class, we divide them into two sets, depending on whether their local Bloch vectors are parallel or anti-parallel, and call them symmetric and antisymmetric X-states, respectively.

This paper is structured as follows. We start with a review of the procedure for determining the quantifier of local available quantum correlations
LAQC
. Then, we study the calculation of X states in two distinct subsets: when both subsystems have the same local Bloch vector and when their local Bloch vectors have the same magnitude but opposite direction. We analyze several examples and include amplitude damping as a quantum channel for Werner states
Werner
.

II
Local available quantum correlations of 2-qubits

In
LAQC
, the starting point for Mundarain et al. is that a density operator

\begin{split}\rho_{AB}=&\sum_{klmn}\rho_{kl}^{mn}\,\outerproduct{km}{ln}\\

,

, with
\mathbbm{U}_{A},\mathbbm{U}_{B}\in\mathbf{U}(2)
. Any of these bases can be used as the new computational one, where we now have the basis of eigenvector of
\sigma_{\hat{\mathbf{u}}}\equiv\overrightarrow{{\bf\it\sigma}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}

the vector whose components are the Pauli matrices and

is a generic unitary vector. Which
\hat{\mathbf{u}}
to choose will depend on several conditions and/or requirements of the system at hand.

For classical states there is a local basis for which
\rho_{AB}^{c}
(
5
) is diagonal. Therefore, one can define
X_{\rho}\in\Omega_{c}
as the classical state induced by a measurement which minimizes

S\quantity(\rho_{AB}||X_\rho)=\min_{\Omega_{c}}S\quantity(\rho_{AB}||\chi_\rho),

(15)

where

is the relative entropy and

\begin{split}\chi_{\rho}=&\sum_{ij}R_{ij}\,\outerproduct{B(i,j)}{B(i,j)},\\
R_{ij}=&\expectationvalue{\rho_{AB}}{B(i,j)}\,.\end{split}

(16)

The minimization in (
15
) is equivalent to determining the coefficients
\quantity\big{R_{ij}^{opt}}
when

. Such coefficients are associated with the optimal basis

which will then serve as the new computational basis, in whose terms local available quantum correlations are then defined.

To determine the optimal basis, we start by defining the most general orthonormal basis for each subsystem:

\displaystyle\ket{\mu_0^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\quantity(\frac{\theta_n}{2})\ket{0^{(n)}}+\sin\quantity(\frac{\theta_n}{2})\mathbf{e}^{i\phi_{n}}\ket{1^{(n)}},

(17)

\displaystyle\ket{\mu_1^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin\quantity(\frac{\theta_n}{2})\ket{0^{(n)}}+\cos\quantity(\frac{\theta_n}{2})\mathbf{e}^{i\phi_{n}}\ket{1^{(n)}},

where

denotes subsystem A and

(18)

where

is the product state nearest to

and its relative entropy can be written as the Mutual Information of state

, as shown by Modi et al.

&\quad\mbox{}\quad\times\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{P_{\theta,\phi}(i_A,j_B)}{P_{\theta,\phi}(i_A)P_{\theta,\phi}(j_B)}],\end{split}

(19)

where

and

Once this new computational basis

\displaystyle\ket{u_0^{(m)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\quantity(\ket{0^{(m)}}_{opt}+\exp^{i\Phi_m}\ket{1^{(m)}}_{opt}),

\displaystyle\ket{u_1^{(m)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\quantity(\ket{0^{(m)}}_{opt}-\exp^{i\Phi_m}\ket{1^{(m)}}_{opt})\,.

In this generic basis, the corresponding probability distributions,

P(i_{A},j_{B},\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})=\expectationvalue{\rho_{AB}}{u_i^{A},u_j^{B}},

(21)

and the marginal probability distributions are determined. The maximization of

III
LAQC of X states

We start by using the Bloch representation of 2-qubit states, also referred to as the Fano form or Fano-Bloch representation

\begin{split}\rho^{X}=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(\mathbbm{1}_{4}+x_{3}\,\sigma_{3}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}&+\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes y_{3}\,\sigma_{3}\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{3}T_{n}\sigma_{n}\otimes\sigma_{n}\Bigg),\end{split}

An important set of (

\quantity(\abs{x_3}=\abs{y_3})

constitute a special subset where

and their LAQC were already analyzed in

It is also worth noting that single parameter mixed states between a Bell state
\quantity{\ket{\psi^\pm},\ket{\phi^\pm}}

belong to this set of X states. These can be thought of as analogous to what Werner states

are to Bell Diagonal ones. That is, as a one-parameter subset of the latter.

III.1.1
Werner states under amplitude damping decoherence

Werner states,

The amplitude damping quantum channel

IV
Conclusions

We have studied the local available quantum correlations (LAQC)
LAQC
of the subset of 2-qubit X states, where their local Bloch vectors have equal magnitude. Such states can be regarded as symmetric or anti-symmetric X states whether their local Bloch vectors are parallel or anti-parallel, respectively. We obtained exact analytical expressions for their classical correlations and LAQC quantifiers in terms of the Bloch parameters of the state. As examples of symmetric X states, we analyzed the (Markovian) amplitude damping decoherence of Werner states and found that their LAQC does not suffer sudden death under this quantum channel. Also, we studied families of one-parameter X states, both symmetric and anti-symmetric. For such states, there is less LAQC than entanglement (as measured via concurrence) as well as quantum discord.

Equivariant (co)module nuclearity of C ∗ -crossed products
Massoud Amini, Qing Meng

Abstract
. We define an equivariant and equicovariant versions of the notion of module nuclearity. More precisely, for a discrete group Γ \Gamma and operator 𝒜 \mathcal{A} - Γ \Gamma -(co)module ℬ \mathcal{B} , ℰ \mathcal{E} over a Γ \Gamma -C ∗ -algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , we define ℰ \mathcal{E} - Γ \Gamma -nuclearity of ℬ \mathcal{B} , as an equivariant version of the notion of ℰ \mathcal{E} -nuclearity, in which the identity map on ℬ \mathcal{B} is required to be approximately factored through matrix algebras on ℰ \mathcal{E} with module structures coming both from the original module structure of ℰ \mathcal{E} and the Γ \Gamma -action on ℰ \mathcal{E} . For trivial actions of Γ \Gamma , this is shown to reduce to the notion of module nuclearity, introduced and studied by the first author. As a concrete example, for a discrete group Γ \Gamma acting amenably on a unital C ∗ -algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , we show that the reduced crossed product 𝒜 ⋊ r Γ \mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma is 𝒜 \mathcal{A} - Γ \Gamma -nuclear. Conversely, if 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a nuclear C ∗ -algebra with a Γ \Gamma -invariant state ρ \rho and 𝒜 ⋊ r Γ \mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma is 𝒜 \mathcal{A} - Γ \Gamma -nuclear, then we deduce that Γ \Gamma is amenable. We show that when 𝒜 ⋊ r Γ \mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma is 𝒜 \mathcal{A} - Γ \Gamma -nuclear and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} has the completely bounded approximation property (resp., is exact), then so is 𝒜 ⋊ r Γ \mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma . We prove similar results for 𝒜 ⋊ r Γ \mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma , regarded as an 𝒜 \mathcal{A} - Γ \Gamma -comodule.

1.
Introduction

Approximation theory plays a central role in the modern theory of operator algebras. There are many significant approximation properties that are defined and studied for C
∗
-algebras, including nuclearity, quasidiagonality, completely bounded approximation property (CBAP), Haagerup property, and weak Haagerup property (see
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
12
]
).

The theory of nuclear C
∗
-algebras has developed for a quite while, and is closely related to the amenability of groups (see
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
). It is natural to try to extend nuclearity to a more general setting.
The first author
[
1
]
introduced the notion of nuclear module morphisms for a C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
which is C
∗
-module over another C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
(or more generally, over an operator system) with compatible actions, and used it to define nuclearity and exactness in this subcategory of C
∗
-modules.

In this paper, we discuss the nuclearity of C
∗
-crossed products, considered as C
∗
-modules on the ambient C
∗
-algebra. It turns out that this case does not fit into the module nuclearity context of
[
1
]
, as
\mathcal{A}
is not just a C
∗
-algebra, but a
\Gamma
-C
∗
-algebra. This suggests that an equivariant version of module amenability is needed, and this is the main objective of the current paper. We show that for a discrete group
\Gamma
acting amenably on a unital C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the reduced crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear. We find applications for other finite approximation properties such as exactness and completely bounded approximation property.

2.
Equivariant (Co)module Nuclearity

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group acting by automorphisms on a C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is the positive cone of
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is the dual space of
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{+}
is the set of all positive linear functional on
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{D}
be operator right
\mathcal{A}
-modules and
\mathcal{E}
be an operator system and operator right
\mathcal{A}
-module. The first author defined in
[
1
]
the
\mathcal{E}
-nuclearity for a module map
\theta:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{D}
by requiring that
\theta
is approximately decomposed through
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{E})
over any given finite subset of
\mathcal{B}
. As it would be too restrictive to
assume that the maps implementing the approximate decomposition are module maps, a class of admissible maps was defined to make the decomposition more likely to happen.

The class of admissible maps is defined as follows. To simplify notations, we use c.p. for “completely positive”, u.c.p. for “unital completely positive”, c.c.p. for “contractive completely positive”, c.i. for “complete isometry”, and c.b. for “completely bounded”.

Definition 2.1
.

[
1
, Definition 2.1]

The class of
\mathcal{A}
-admissible c.p. maps between operator
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{D}
is characterized through the following set of rules:

(1)

a c.p. module map
\varphi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{A}
-admissible,

(2)

if a c.p. map
\varphi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{A}
-admissible, then so are the maps
x\mapsto\varphi(bxb^{*})
and
x\mapsto d\varphi(x)d^{*}
, for each
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
d\in\mathcal{D}
,

(3)

the c.p. maps
\varphi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
of the form
\varphi(x)=\rho(x)d
, where
\rho\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{+}
and
d\in\mathcal{D}^{+}
, are
\mathcal{A}
-admissible,

(4)

the composition, positive multiples, or finite sums of
\mathcal{A}
-admissible c.p. maps are
\mathcal{A}
-admissible.

The notion of nuclear module maps was then defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

[
1
, Definition 2.2]

A c.p. module map
\theta:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is called
\mathcal{E}
-nuclear if there are c.c.p.
\mathcal{A}
-admissible maps
\varphi_{n}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}(\mathcal{E})
and
\psi_{n}:\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
such that

\|\psi_{n}\circ\varphi_{n}(b)-b\|\rightarrow 0

for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
.

A C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
is called a right
\mathcal{A}
-C
∗
-module if
\mathcal{B}
is a Banach right
\mathcal{A}
-module with compatibility conditions,

(ab)\cdot u=a(b\cdot u),\qquad a\cdot uv=(a\cdot u)\cdot v,

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{B}
and
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a right
\mathcal{A}
-C
∗
-module, then
\mathcal{B}
is called
\mathcal{A}
-nuclear if the identity map on
\mathcal{B}
is
\mathcal{A}
-nuclear.

In this paper we are interested in
\mathcal{A}
-nuclearity of the reduced crossed product
A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Though there is a natural way to decompose the identity map via matrix algebras over
\mathcal{A}
, these matrix algebras have canonical module structures coming from the
\Gamma
-action
\alpha
. For a finite subset
F\subseteq\Gamma
with cardinality
n
, a typical element of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
is a finite sum of matrices of the form
a\otimes e_{s,t}
, for
s,t\in F
, where
e_{s,t}
is the standard matrix unit. The module structure on
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
is now defined by
(a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b:=a\alpha_{t^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{s,t}
. This module structure depends not only on
n
but also on the choice of
F
, and so the canonical decomposition of the identity map on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
via such matrix algebras over
\mathcal{A}
could not be used to verify the
\mathcal{A}
-nuclearity of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
in the usual module nuclearity context of
[
1
]
.

The above difficulty could be handled using the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is indeed a
\Gamma
-C
∗
-algebra, and incorporating this fact into the definition of the module structure of the matrix algebras involved. To be more precise, we need to modify our notion not only by using
\Gamma
-action in defining the module structure but also by replacing the usual full matrix algebras
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
by matrix algebras of the form
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
, for finite subsets
F
of the union of all finite products of copies of
\Gamma
.

Consider the
n
-fold Cartesian products
\Gamma^{n}
for
n\geq 1
, and put
\Gamma^{\infty}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Gamma^{n}
. Given
t=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell})\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, we use the formal notations
t^{-1}:=(t_{\ell}^{-1},\cdots,t_{1}^{-1})\in\Gamma^{\infty}
and
\bar{t}:=t_{1}\cdots t_{\ell}\in\Gamma
. Also, for
r\in\Gamma
, we define
rt:=(rt_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{\ell})
, and note that
\overline{t^{-1}}=\bar{t}^{-1}
and
\overline{rt}=r\bar{t}
.

For a finite subset
F\subseteq\Gamma^{\infty}
, let
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
be the full matrix algebra of square matrices of size
|F|\times|F|
with components in
\mathcal{A}
. We define the
\mathcal{A}
-module structure of
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
as follows. Each element of
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
is a finite sum of elements of the form
a\otimes e_{s,t}
, where
e_{s,t}
is the matrix unit indexed by
s,t\in F
. Define the right module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
by
(a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b:=a\alpha_{\bar{t}}^{-1}(b)\otimes e_{s,t}
, for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
s,t\in F
. Note that we have chosen to work with finite subsets of
\Gamma^{\infty}
, not just finite subsets of
\Gamma
, since we need to make sure we get full matrix algebras of any large size, even when
\Gamma
is finite (just imagine the case where
\Gamma
is the trivial group, whereas
\Gamma^{\infty}
is countable).

If
\mathcal{E}
is an operator space (resp., system) and operator right
\mathcal{A}
-module and
\Gamma
acts
\mathcal{E}
by (resp., unital) surjective complete isometries, then the right
\mathcal{A}
-module structure of
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{E})
is defined by

(x\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b:=x\cdot\alpha_{\bar{t}}^{-1}(b)\otimes e_{s,t}
, for
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
x\in\mathcal{E}
and
s,t\in F
, with the understanding that
\alpha_{\bar{t}}
is no longer an automorphism, and just is a (resp., unital) surjective c.i. map. We refer the reader for min and max tensor products of operator systems to
[
7
]
. In this paper,
\otimes
always denotes the min tensor product.

Definition 2.3
.

An operator
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-module
\mathcal{B}
is an operator system with a
\Gamma
-action
\alpha
by complete isometries (i.e., each
\beta_{t}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is a surjective c.i. map and
\beta_{t}\beta_{s}=\beta_{ts}
, for
t,s\in\Gamma
), and a right
\mathcal{A}
-module action, which is compatible with the the
\Gamma
-action
\alpha
on
\mathcal{A}
(i.e.,
\beta_{t}(b\cdot a)=\beta_{t}(b)\cdot\alpha_{t}(a)
, for
t\in\Gamma,a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}
). When
\mathcal{B}
is a C*-algebra, we moreover assume that,
\beta_{t}(bc)=\beta_{t}(b)\beta_{t}(c)
, for
t\in\Gamma,b,c\in\mathcal{B}
, i.e., we let
\Gamma
act on
\mathcal{B}
by automorphisms.

Definition 2.4
.

An operator reduced
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodule
\mathcal{B}
is an operator system with a reduced
\Gamma
-coaction
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}
(i.e.,
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
is a non-degenerate c.p. map), a reduced
\Gamma
-coaction
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
(i.e.,
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
is a non-degenerate homomorphism), and a right
\mathcal{A}
-module structure which is compatible with the
\Gamma
-coactions (i.e.,
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}(b\cdot a)=\delta_{\mathcal{B}}(b)\cdot\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(a)
, for
a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}
). When
\mathcal{B}
is a C*-algebra, we further assume that
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}
is a homomorphism. The operator full
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodules are defined similarly using full
\Gamma
-coactions
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes C^{*}(\Gamma)
.

Note that in the above definition, following
[
15
]
, in both cases we use the minimal tensor products (for a version which uses maximal tensor products, see
[
16
]
).

Remark 1
.

(i)

Note that in the above definition, we have incorporated the
\Gamma

\Gamma
-admissible maps, but also in the module structure of

\Gamma
-sub(co)modules, then the restriction map
\theta|_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{E}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear, as a map between
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-(co)modules.

(ii)

(

)
If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is an

-

, then under any of the following conditions,

is

-

-

,

(2)

such that

on

(ii)

(

3.
Main Results

In this section,
\Gamma
is a discrete group with identity element
e
, that acts on a unital C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
with unit
1
, through an action
\alpha
. We identify
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
with
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
be the linear space of finitely supported functions on
\Gamma
with values in
\mathcal{A}
. We denote elements of
C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
by formal finite sums
\sum_{s\in\Gamma}a_{s}s
, where
a_{s}\in\mathcal{A}
is zero except for finitely many
s\in\Gamma
. This should be interpreted as a function:
\Gamma\to\mathcal{A};s\mapsto a_{s}
. We denote the reduced crossed product of the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},\Gamma,\alpha)
by
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\Gamma
, or simply by
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
when the action is known from the context, and identify
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
as well as
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
via canonical embeddings. We frequently use the facts that
\{as:a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\Gamma\}
is a total set in
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and there is a canonical faithful conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}:\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, sending
\sum_{s\in\Gamma}a_{s}s
to
a_{e}
(see
[
3
]
for details).

Let
Z(\mathcal{A})
be the center of
\mathcal{A}
with positive cone
Z(\mathcal{A})^{+}
. Let us recall the notion of amenable actions on C
∗
-algebras (cf.,
[
3
, Definition 4.3.1]
).

Definition 3.1
.

An action
\alpha
of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a unital C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is amenable if there exists a net
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of finitely supported functions
T_{i}:\Gamma\rightarrow Z(\mathcal{A})^{+}
such
that
\sum\limits_{s\in\Gamma}T_{i}(s)^{2}=1
and for each
t\in\Gamma
,

\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\big(T_{i}(s)-\alpha_{t}(T_{i}(t^{-1}s))\big)^{*}\big(T_{i}(s)-\alpha_{t}(T_{i}(t^{-1}s))\big)\rightarrow 0,

in norm, as
i\to\infty
.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is a C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, the reduced crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
could be thought as a C
∗
-module with module action given by the right multiplication map. Hence, the right
\mathcal{A}
-module structure on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is

as\cdot b:=asb=a\alpha_{s}(b)s,\ \ (a,b\in\mathcal{A},s\in\Gamma).

For a faithful representation
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we define a new representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty})
by

\pi(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{s})=(\alpha_{\bar{s}^{-1}}(a)\xi)\otimes\delta_{s},\ (a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\Gamma^{\infty}).

where with the notations of previous section,
\bar{t}:=t_{1}\cdots t_{\ell}\in\Gamma
, for
t=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell})\in\Gamma^{\infty}
.
In fact, we have that

\pi(a)=\sum_{s\in\Gamma^{\infty}}\alpha_{\bar{s}^{-1}}(a)\otimes e_{s,s},\ (a\in\mathcal{A}),

where
e_{s,t}
are the matrix units of
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty}))
.
Also, compatible with what defined in the previous section, we define the right action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty}))
by

(a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b:=(a\otimes e_{s,t})\pi(b)=a\alpha_{\bar{t}^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{s,t},\ (a,b\in\mathcal{A},s,t\in\Gamma^{\infty}).

These extend to actions on
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty}))
, respectively.

Let
U
be the unitary representation of
\Gamma
on
B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty}))
such that

U_{r}(\delta_{s})=\delta_{rs},\ (r\in\Gamma,s\in\Gamma^{\infty}).

In fact, we have that

U_{r}=\sum_{s\in\Gamma^{\infty}}e_{rs,s},

for all
r\in\Gamma
.
Next, we define the
\Gamma
-actions on these
\mathcal{A}
-modules by

r\cdot(as):=rasr^{-1}=\alpha_{r}(a)rsr^{-1},\ \ (a\in\mathcal{A},s,r\in\Gamma).

and

r\cdot(a\otimes e_{s,t}):=U_{r}(a\otimes e_{s,t})U^{*}_{r}=a\otimes e_{rs,rt},\ (a\in\mathcal{A},r\in\Gamma,s,t\in\Gamma^{\infty}).

It is easy to see that these maps are homomorphism.
Let us next observe that these are
\Gamma
-actions compatible with the module structure in both cases. For
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
s,r\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle r\cdot(as\cdot b)

\displaystyle=r\cdot(a\alpha_{s}(b)s)=\alpha_{r}(a)\alpha_{rs}(b)rsr^{-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{r}(a)\alpha_{rsr^{-1}}(\alpha_{r}(b))rsr^{-1}=\big(r\cdot(as)\big)\cdot\alpha_{r}(b).

Similarly, for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
s,t\in\Gamma^{\infty},r\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle r\cdot\big((a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=r\cdot(a\alpha_{\bar{t}^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{s,t})=a\alpha_{\bar{t}^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{rs,rt}

\displaystyle=a\alpha_{\bar{t}^{-1}r^{-1}}(\alpha_{r}(b))\otimes e_{rs,rt}=\big(r\cdot(a\otimes e_{s,t})\big)\cdot\alpha_{r}(b).

These extend to well defined compatible
\mathcal{A}
-module actions and
\Gamma
-actions on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, and
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\infty}))
, turning them into operator
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-modules.

Theorem 3.2
.

If the action
\alpha
is amenable, then the reduced C
∗
-crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-module.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be represented faithfully in
\mathcal{H}
, and
\varphi
be the regular representation of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
in
\mathcal{H}\otimes\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
, which is defined on
C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
by,

\varphi\big(\sum_{s\in\Gamma}a_{s}s\big)=\sum_{s,t\in\Gamma}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a_{s})\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t},

with only finitely many
a_{s}
being nonzero. For
F\mathrel{\mathrlap{\subset}\hphantom{\ll}\mathllap{\subset}}\Gamma^{\infty}
, consider the map
\psi_{F}:\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
, defined by,

\psi_{F}(\sum_{s,t\in F}a_{s,t}\otimes e_{s,t})=\sum_{s,t\in F}\alpha_{\bar{s}}(a_{s,t}){\bar{s}}{\bar{t}}^{-1},

Then
\varphi
extends to a
*
-homomorphism on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, and it follows, by an argument as in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2.3]
, that
\psi_{F}
is a c.c.p. map.

Next, let us observe that these are
\mathcal{A}
-module maps. For
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, and
s,t\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\varphi(as\cdot b)

\displaystyle=\varphi(a\alpha_{s}(b)s)=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a\alpha_{s}(b))\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\alpha_{t^{-1}s}(b)\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t}=\varphi(as)\cdot b.

Similarly, for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, and
s,t\in F
,

\displaystyle\psi_{F}((a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b)

\displaystyle=\psi_{F}(a\alpha_{\bar{t}^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{s,t})=\alpha_{\bar{s}}(a)\alpha_{\bar{s}\bar{t}^{-1}}(b)\bar{s}\bar{t}^{-1}=\psi_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b.

Next, let us observe that
\varphi
is a
\Gamma
-map and
\{\psi_{F}\}
is a
\Gamma
-family.
For
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and
r,s,t\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\varphi(r\cdot as)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\alpha_{r}(a)rsr^{-1})=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(\alpha_{r}(a))\otimes e_{t,rs^{-1}r^{-1}t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes e_{rt,rs^{-1}t}=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}r\cdot(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t})=r\cdot\varphi(as).

For
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and
r\in\Gamma
, and
s,t\in F
,

\displaystyle\psi_{rF}(r\cdot(a\otimes e_{s,t}))

\displaystyle=\psi_{rF}(a\otimes e_{rs,rt})=\alpha_{r\bar{s}}(a)r\bar{s}\bar{t}^{-1}r^{-1}

\displaystyle=r\cdot(\alpha_{\bar{s}}(a)\bar{s}\bar{t}^{-1})=r\cdot\psi_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t}).

Let
F_{i}
be the support of
T_{i}
, for
i\geq 1
. Let
P_{F_{i}}
be the finite rank projection onto the span of
\{\delta_{g}:g\in F_{i}\}
, then
1\otimes P_{F_{i}}
is in
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
. For the c.c.p admissible maps
\varphi_{i}=(1\otimes P_{F_{i}})\circ\varphi\circ(1\otimes P_{F_{i}})
, we have
\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A})
, for each
i
.

For the selfadjoint element,

X_{i}=\sum_{t\in F_{i}}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(T_{i}(t))\otimes e_{t,t},

and the c.p. compression
\theta_{i}
by
X_{i}
, put
\psi_{i}:=\frac{1}{|F_{i}|}\psi_{F_{i}}\circ\theta_{i}:\mathbb{M}_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, it follows from
[
3
, Lemma 4.3.2]
and
[
3
, Lemma 4.3.3]
that,

\|\psi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}(x)-x\|\rightarrow 0,

for each
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Therefore,
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-module.
∎

Next let us treat the case of coactions. The reduced crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
also carries a canonical reduced
\Gamma
-coaction, given by

\delta(as):=as\otimes\lambda_{s}\ \ (a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\Gamma).

Also
\Gamma
naturally coacts on
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
by

\delta_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t}):=(a\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,t}\ \ (a\in\mathcal{A},s,t\in F),

where we have canonically identified
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
with
\mathbb{M}_{F}\big(\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)\big)
. Note that both coactions restricted to
\mathcal{A}
, identified respectively with
\{a1:a\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
\mathbb{M}_{\{1\}}(\mathcal{A})
boil down to a trivial restricted coaction,

\delta_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma);\ \ a\mapsto a\otimes\lambda_{1}\ \ (a\in\mathcal{A}),

which means that we are not pre-assuming any
\Gamma
-comudule structure on
\mathcal{A}
. The same holds for full coactions, and there are canonical full
\Gamma
-coaction on these two algebras, given the same, but with
\lambda_{s}
replaced by
\omega_{s}
, where
\lambda:\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
is the left regular representation and
\omega:\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{u});\ \omega:=\oplus_{\sigma\in\hat{\Gamma}}\sigma
, is the universal representation on the universal Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{u}:=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\hat{\Gamma}}\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
.

Both in the full and reduced cases, these extend to well defined
\Gamma
-coactions on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
, turning them into operator
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodules. For instance in the reduced case, after natural identifications, for
r,s,t\in\Gamma
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\delta(as\cdot b)

\displaystyle=\delta(a\alpha_{s}(b)s)=a\alpha_{s}(b)s\otimes\lambda_{s}=(as\cdot b)\otimes\lambda_{s}

\displaystyle=(as\otimes\lambda_{s})\cdot(b\otimes\lambda_{1})=\delta(as)\cdot\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(b),

and

\displaystyle\delta_{F}\big((a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=\delta_{F}\big(a\alpha_{t^{-1}}(b)\otimes e_{s,t}\big)=(a\alpha_{t^{-1}}(b)\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,t}

\displaystyle=[(a\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\cdot(b\otimes\lambda_{1})]\otimes e_{s,t}=[(a\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,t}]\cdot(b\otimes\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\delta_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t})\cdot\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(b).

These maps are also homomorphism: for
r,s,t\in\Gamma
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\delta(asbt)

\displaystyle=\delta(a\alpha_{s}(b)st)=a\alpha_{s}(b)st\otimes\lambda_{st}=(as\otimes\lambda_{s})(bt\otimes\lambda_{t})

\displaystyle=\delta(as)\delta(bt),

also,

\displaystyle\delta_{F}\big((a\otimes e_{s,t})(b\otimes e_{u,v})\big)

\displaystyle=\delta_{F}(ab\otimes e_{s,v})=(ab\otimes\lambda_{sv^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,v}

\displaystyle=[(a\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,t}][(b\otimes\lambda_{uv^{-1}})\otimes e_{u,v}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t})\delta_{F}(b\otimes e_{u,v}),

when
t=u
, and all the terms are zero, otherwise.

Now we are ready to prove the same result for the comodule structure.

Theorem 3.3
.

If the action
\alpha
is amenable, then the reduced C
∗
-crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodule.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, let
F_{i}
be the support of
T_{i}
, for
i\geq 1
, and consider the c.c.p. module maps
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}G\rightarrow M_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A})
and
\psi_{i}:M_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}G
.
Let us observe that these are also
\Gamma
-comaps, with respect to the coactions
\delta
and
\delta_{F}
defined above.
After canonical identifications, for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
s,r\in\Gamma
, and
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(\varphi_{i}\otimes{\rm id})\delta(as)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{i}\otimes{\rm id})(as\otimes\lambda_{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in F_{i}\cap sF_{i}}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t})\otimes\lambda_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in F_{i}\cap sF_{i}}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes\lambda_{s})\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in F_{i}\cap sF_{i}}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes\lambda_{tt^{-1}s})\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in F_{i}\cap sF_{i}}\delta_{F}\big(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)\otimes e_{t,s^{-1}t}\big)=\delta_{F}\varphi_{i}(as),

and

\displaystyle(\psi_{i}\otimes{\rm id})\delta_{F}(a\otimes e_{s,t})

\displaystyle=(\psi_{i}\otimes{\rm id})\big((a\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}})\otimes e_{s,t}\big)

\displaystyle=(\psi_{i}\otimes{\rm id})\big((a\otimes e_{s,t})\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|F_{i}|}(\alpha_{s}(a)st^{-1})\otimes\lambda_{st^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|F_{i}|}\delta(\alpha_{s}(a)st^{-1})=\delta\psi_{i}(a\otimes e_{s,t}),

where the third (the second) equality of the first (the second) calculation is due to the fact that we have identified
\mathbb{M}_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A})\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
canonically with
\mathbb{M}_{F_{i}}(\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
. Now compression by selfadjoint element
X_{i}:=\sum_{t\in F_{i}}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(T_{i}(t))\otimes e_{t,t}
is a coadmissible c.p map
\theta_{i}
, and as before,

\psi_{i}\theta_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)-x\rightarrow 0,

in norm, as
i\to\infty
,
for each
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Therefore,
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear as a
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodule.
∎

Since any action of an amenable group on a unital C
∗
-algebra is amenable, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\Gamma
is amenable, then
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear both as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-module and an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodule.

From now on, we call
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear
if
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-module or as an
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-comodule.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\rho
be an
\alpha
-invariant state on
\mathcal{A}
and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho\circ\mathbb{E}
. For every
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and
t\in\Gamma
, we
have
\rho^{\prime}(xt)=\rho^{\prime}(tx)
.

Proof.

For
x:=\sum\limits_{s\in\Gamma}a_{s}s\in C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
and
t\in\Gamma
, we have,

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(xt)

\displaystyle=\rho^{\prime}((\sum\limits_{s\in G}a_{s}s)t)=\rho(a_{t^{-1}})=\rho(\alpha_{t}(a_{t^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\rho^{\prime}(\sum\limits_{s\in G}\alpha_{t}(a_{s})ts)=\rho^{\prime}(tx).

By a density argument, the same holds for all
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is nuclear and has an
\alpha
-invariant state
\rho
. If the reduced crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear, then
\Gamma
is amenable.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is nuclear,
[
3
, Proposition 10.1.7]
shows that
\mathbb{M}_{F}(\mathcal{A})
is nuclear, for each
F\mathrel{\mathrlap{\subset}\hphantom{\ll}\mathllap{\subset}}\Gamma^{\infty}
. It follows from
[
3
, Exercie 2.3.11]
that
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is nuclear. Hence there exists a net
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in I}
of finite rank u.c.p. maps from
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
to itself converge to the identity map in the point norm topology. By the argument of
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
, we can assume that the range of
\Phi_{n}
is in
C_{c}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
.
For each
n
, let

\omega_{n}(s)=\rho^{\prime}(\Phi_{n}(s)s^{-1})

for all
s\in\Gamma
. For all
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in\Gamma
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
, the positivity of
\rho^{\prime}
yields

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{i,j=1}c_{i}\bar{c_{j}}\omega_{n}(s^{-1}_{j}s_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{i,j=1}c_{i}\bar{c_{j}}\rho^{\prime}((\Phi_{n}(s^{-1}_{j}s_{i}))(s^{-1}_{i}s_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{i,j=1}\rho^{\prime}(\bar{c_{j}}s_{j}\Phi_{n}(s_{j}^{-1}s_{i})c_{i}s_{i}^{-1})\geq 0.

Hence,
\omega_{n}
is positive definite on
\Gamma
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle|\omega_{n}(s)-1|

\displaystyle=|\rho^{\prime}(\Phi_{n}(s)s^{-1})-1|=|\rho^{\prime}(\Phi_{n}(s)s^{-1})-\rho^{\prime}(ss^{-1})|

\displaystyle=|\rho^{\prime}((\Phi_{n}(s)-s)s^{-1})|\leq\|\Phi_{n}(s)-s)\|\rightarrow 0,

for all
s\in\Gamma
. Since
\Phi_{n}
is finite rank,
\omega_{n}
has finite support.
∎

It follows from Corollary
3.4
and Theorem
3.6
that if
\mathcal{A}
is nuclear and has an
\alpha
-invariant state
\rho
, then
\Gamma
is amenable if and only if
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear. Let
\gamma
act on
C_{b}(\Gamma)
by conjugation, then there exists a
\gamma
-invariant tracial state
\tau_{e}
on
C_{b}(\Gamma)
, such that

\tau_{e}(f)=f(e)
,
for all
f\in C_{b}(\Gamma)
. Hence, we get the following characterization of amenability of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.7
.

\Gamma
is amenable if and only if
C_{b}(\Gamma)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
C_{b}(\Gamma)
-
\Gamma
-nuclear.

Example 3.8
.

(a) Let
\mathbb{Z}
act on a unital C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
through an action
\alpha
. Since
\mathbb{Z}
is amenable, it follows from Corollary
3.4
that
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\mathbb{Z}
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathbb{Z}
-nuclear.

(b) Let
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the ideal boundary of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. It is known that
\mathbb{F}_{2}
acts amenably on
C(\partial\mathbb{F}_{2})
through an action induced by left multiplication (see
[
3
]
).
It follows from Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
that
C(\partial\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{r}\mathbb{F}_{2}
is
C(\partial\mathbb{F}_{2})
-
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-nuclear.

(c) It follows from
[
2
]
that the special linear group
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
has property
(T)
. Moreover, a discrete group
\Gamma
is finite if and only if
\Gamma
is amenable and has property
(T)
. Hence, Proposition
3.7
shows that
C_{b}(SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))\rtimes_{r}SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is not
C_{b}(SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
-
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
-nuclear.

In
[
6
]
, Haagerup introduced the completely bounded approximation property for C
∗
-algebras and an important isomorphism invariant
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})
for a C
∗
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 3.9
.

We say a C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the completely bounded approximation property (CBAP) if there exist a constant
C>0
and a net of finite rank c.b. maps
\Phi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that

\|\Phi_{i}(a)-a\|\rightarrow 0

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\sup\{\|\Phi_{i}\|_{cb}\}\leq C
.

The Haagerup constant
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})
is the infimum of all
C
for which such a net
\{\Phi_{i}\}
exists. We set
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})=\infty
if
\mathcal{A}
does not have the CBAP.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a net of C
∗
-algebras. Assume that
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\psi_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
are c.b. maps such that
\psi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}\rightarrow{\rm id}_{\mathcal{A}}
in the point-norm topology, and

\sup\|\varphi_{i}\|_{cb}\leq m_{1},\qquad\sup\|\psi_{i}\|_{cb}\leq m_{2}.

If each
\mathcal{A}_{i}
has the CBAP and
\Lambda(\mathcal{A}_{i})\leq M
, then
\mathcal{A}
has the CBAP.
In fact,

\Lambda(\mathcal{A})\leq m_{1}m_{2}M.

Proof.

Let
F\mathrel{\mathrlap{\subset}\hphantom{\ll}\mathllap{\subset}}\mathcal{A}
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. There exists
i\in I
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}(a)-a\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

for all
a\in F
. By assumption, there exists a c.b. map
\Phi:\mathcal{A}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that
\|\Phi\|_{cb}\leq M
,
and

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\varphi_{i}(a))-\varphi_{i}(a)\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2m_{2}}

for all
x\in F
. Let

\Phi_{j}=\psi_{i}\circ\Phi\circ\varphi_{i},

where
j=(F,\varepsilon)
. Then
\Phi_{j}
is a c.b. map on
\mathcal{A}
with
\|\Phi_{j}\|_{cb}\leq m_{1}m_{2}M
.
Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{j}(a)-a\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{i}\circ\Phi\circ\varphi_{i}(a)-\psi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}(a)\|+\|\psi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}(a)-a\|

\displaystyle\leq m_{2}\|\Phi(\varphi_{i}(x))-\varphi_{i}(x)\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\varepsilon

for all
a\in F
.
Hence, the net
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
indexed by
J=\{(F,\varepsilon)\mid F\mathrel{\mathrlap{\subset}\hphantom{\ll}\mathllap{\subset}}\mathcal{A},\varepsilon>0\}
shows that
\mathcal{A}
has the CBAP and
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})\leq m_{1}m_{2}M
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

If
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the CBAP, the
\mathcal{A}
has the CBAP. In fact,

\Lambda(\mathcal{A})\leq\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma).

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a net
\{\Phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of c.b. maps on
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
witnessing the CBAP of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Then
\{\mathbb{E}\circ\Phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
witnesses the CBAP of
\mathcal{A}
. Hence, we have
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})\leq\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma).

∎

Theorem 3.12
.

If
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear and
\mathcal{A}
has the CBAP, then
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the CBAP. In fact,

\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma)=\Lambda(\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

For each
F\mathrel{\mathrlap{\subset}\hphantom{\ll}\mathllap{\subset}}\Gamma^{\infty}
,

\Lambda(M_{F}(\mathcal{A}))=\Lambda(\mathcal{A}).

The
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclearity and Lemma
3.10
show that,

\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma)\leq\Lambda(\mathcal{A}).

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
3.11
that
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})\leq\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma)
.
Hence,
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the CBAP, and
\Lambda(\mathcal{A})=\Lambda(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma)
.
∎

Since
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear whenever the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{A}
is amenable, the above theorem is a generalization of
[
18
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
.
Using a similar argument, we also have the following result, which is a generalization of
[
3
, Theorem 4.2.6]
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear, then
\mathcal{A}
is exact if and only if
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is exact.

Proof.

This follows from
[
3
, Exercise 2.3.12]
and
[
3
, Proposition 10.2.7]
.
∎

Remark 2
.

If
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear and
\mathcal{A}
is exact, then it follows from
[
3
, Proposition 10.2.3]
that the group
\Gamma
is exact.

We conclude this article with the following results on property
(T)
. In the case of possibly uncountable group, we also have the corresponding
statements for strong property
(T)
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is countable,
\mathcal{A}
is nuclear, and there exists an
\alpha
-invariant tracial state on
\mathcal{A}
, then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\Gamma
is finite.

(2)

\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear and
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma,C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
has (strong) property
(T)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) If
\Gamma
is finite, then
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
has strong property
(T)
(see
[
10
]
). Hence,
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma,C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
has strong property
(T)
. Moreover, it follows from Corollary
3.4
that
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-
\Gamma
-nuclear.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) Since there exists an
\alpha
-invariant tracial state on
\mathcal{A}
, it follows from
[
14
, Proposition 3.4]
that
\Gamma
has property
(T)
. Moreover, Theorem
3.6
shows that
\Gamma
is amenable. Hence,
\Gamma
is finite.
∎

Note that if
\Gamma
is finite, then
C_{b}(\Gamma)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has strong property
(T)
. Therefore, we have a characterization of finiteness for countable discrete groups.

Corollary 3.15
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is countable, then
\Gamma
is finite if and only if
C_{b}(\Gamma)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is
C_{b}(\Gamma)
-
\Gamma

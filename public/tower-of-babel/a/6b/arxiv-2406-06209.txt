The study begins with the effective Lagrangian

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{eff}=ig_{HHV}\Big(H_{i}^{\dagger}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}H^{j}\Big)\big(V^{\mu}\big)_{j}^{i}\,,

(1)

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}\equiv\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}

\displaystyle V=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\rho^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}&\rho^{+}&K^{*+}\\
\rho^{-}&~~-\frac{\rho^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}&K^{*0}\\
K^{*-}&\bar{K}^{*0}&\phi\end{array}\right),\,

or
\big(B^{-},\bar{B}^{0},\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\big)

g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}

\langle\,V(p,\varepsilon^{\ast})\,H_{2}(p+q)\,|{\cal L}_{eff}|\,H_{1}(q)\,\rangle\equiv g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}\,(q\cdot\varepsilon^{*})\,,

(6)

where the vector meson

is characterized by its mass

, momentum

This study focuses on the couplings

,

,
g_{D^{+}_{s}D^{0}K^{*+}}
,
g_{D^{+}D^{+}_{s}{\bar{K}}^{*0}}

Other couplings are related to these through isospin symmetry, which dictates relations between couplings involving different isospin states

\displaystyle{\rm D~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{DD\rho}\equiv g_{D^{+}D^{0}\rho^{+}}=-\sqrt{2}g_{D^{+}D^{+}\rho^{0}}=\sqrt{2}g_{D^{0}D^{0}\rho^{0}}=-g_{D^{0}D^{+}\rho^{-}}~,

(7)

\displaystyle g_{D_{s}DK^{*}}\equiv g_{D^{+}_{s}D^{0}K^{*+}}=-g_{D^{+}_{s}D^{+}K^{*0}}~,

\displaystyle\,g_{DD_{s}K^{*}}\equiv g_{D^{+}D^{+}_{s}{\bar{K}}^{*0}}=g_{D^{0}D^{+}_{s}K^{*-}}~;

\displaystyle{\rm B~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{BB\rho}\equiv g_{\bar{B}^{0}{B}^{-}\rho^{+}}=-\sqrt{2}g_{\bar{B}^{0}\bar{B}^{0}\rho^{0}}=\sqrt{2}g_{{B}^{-}{B}^{-}\rho^{0}}=-g_{{B}^{-}\bar{B}^{0}\rho^{-}}~,

(8)

\displaystyle g_{B_{s}BK^{*}}\equiv g_{\bar{B}^{0}_{s}{B}^{-}K^{*+}}=-g_{\bar{B}^{0}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}K^{*0}}~\,,

\displaystyle g_{BB_{s}K^{*}}\equiv g_{\bar{B}^{0}\bar{B}^{0}_{s}\bar{K}^{*0}}=g_{{B}^{-}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}_{s}K^{*-}}~\,.

Through charge conjugation transformation, where initial and final states are swapped, we derive:

\displaystyle{\rm D~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{D^{+}_{s}D^{0}K^{*+}}=-g_{D^{+}_{s}D^{+}K^{*0}}=g_{D^{+}D^{+}_{s}{\bar{K}}^{*0}}=g_{D^{0}D^{+}_{s}K^{*-}};

\displaystyle{\rm B~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{\bar{B}^{0}_{s}{B}^{-}K^{*+}}=-g_{\bar{B}^{0}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}K^{*0}}=g_{\bar{B}^{0}\bar{B}^{0}_{s}\bar{K}^{*0}}=g_{{B}^{-}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}_{s}K^{*-}}~,\,

(9)

SU(3) flavour symmetry allows us to relate the coupling constants listed above, yielding the following relation:

\displaystyle{\rm D~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{DD\rho}=g_{D_{s}DK^{*}}=g_{DD_{s}K^{*}}=g_{D_{s}D_{s}\phi}=\sqrt{2}g_{DD\omega}\,;

(10)

\displaystyle{\rm B~case}:\quad

\displaystyle g_{BB\rho}=g_{B_{s}BK^{*}}=g_{BB_{s}K^{*}}=g_{B_{s}B_{s}\phi}=\sqrt{2}g_{BB\omega}\,.

(11)

D_{(s)}D_{(s)}V
and
B_{(s)}B_{(s)}V

To derive sum rules for the coupling
g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}
, we follow the outlined approach, which involves examining the correlation function between the vacuum and vector meson,

\displaystyle\Pi((p+q)^{2},q^{2})=i\int d^{4}x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\big\langle V(p,\varepsilon^{\ast})\big|T\big\{j_{5}^{\dagger}(x),\,j_{5}(0)\big\}\big|0\big\rangle,

(12)

where
p_{B}=p+q
, and the interpolating current for
D_{(s)}
and
B_{(s)}

and

This analysis utilizes two identical local interpolating currents for the pseudoscalar heavy mesons which serves as effective probes for understanding the relevant properties and dynamics within the heavy meson sector.

For establishing the light-cone sum rules, we need to employ the analytic properties of the correlation function in two invariant variables
(p+q)^{2}
and
q^{2}
corresponding to the momentum square of interpolating currents in the two channels of heavy pseudoscalar meson. Starting with the double dispersion representation, by applying the unitarity relation and including complete sets of intermediate states with quantum numbers of
H
into these channels, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi((p+q)^{2},q^{2})=\frac{\langle\,V(p,\varepsilon^{\ast})H_{2}(p\!+\!q)\,|H_{1}(q)\rangle\langle H_{1}(q)|\,j_{5}^{\dagger}|0\rangle\langle H_{2}(p\!+\!q)|j_{5}|0\rangle}{\big[q^{2}-m_{H_{1}}^{2}\big]\big[(p+q)^{2}-m_{H_{2}}^{2}\big]}+\cdots\,,

(13)

where
\ldots
represents the excited states and continuum part. After substituting the definition of the strong coupling in Eq.(
6
) and expressing the matrix elements of interpolating currents in terms of the decay constants
f_{H_{1}}
and
f_{H_{2}}
, i.e.,
\left\langle 0\left|j_{5}\right|H(q)\right\rangle=f_{H}{m_{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\Pi((p+q)^{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(q\cdot\varepsilon^{\ast})g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}f_{H_{1}}f_{H_{2}}m_{H_{1}}^{2}m_{H_{2}}^{2}}{\big[m_{H_{1}}^{2}-q^{2}\big]\big[m_{H_{2}}^{2}-(p+q)^{2}\big]}\,

\displaystyle\quad+\iint\limits_{\hskip 11.38109pt{\Sigma}}\frac{\rho_{cont}\left(s_{1},s_{2}\right)ds_{1}ds_{2}}{\left[s_{1}-q^{2}\right]\left[s_{2}-(p+q)^{2}\right]}+\cdots

\displaystyle\equiv(q\cdot\varepsilon^{\ast})\,F((p+q)^{2},q^{2})\,,

(14)

where
\Sigma
stands for the double duality region, covering both excited and continuum states.

The leading term in Eq.(
14

g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}
and the corresponding decay constants. The term
\rho_{cont}{(s_{1},s_{2})}

\Sigma

On the other hand, the correlation function
\Pi((p+q)^{2},q^{2})

and
(p+q)^{2}
much smaller than the heavy quark mass
m_{Q}^{2}

The former captures the short-distance perturbative contributions, while the latter describesthe long-distance non-perturbative effects. Conveniently, the OPE computations can be expressed in the form of a double dispersion relation:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\rm OPE}((p+q)^{2},q^{2})=(q\cdot\varepsilon^{\ast})\iint ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\frac{\rho^{\rm OPE}(s_{1},s_{2})}{(s_{1}-(p+q)^{2})(s_{2}-q^{2})}\,,

s_{1}

\displaystyle f_{H_{1}}f_{H_{2}}g_{H_{1}H_{2}V}=\frac{1}{m_{H_{1}}^{2}m_{H_{2}}^{2}}\iint\limits^{\hskip 11.38109pt\widetilde{\Sigma}}ds_{1}ds_{2}\exp\bigg[\frac{m^{2}_{H_{1}}-s_{1}}{M_{1}^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}_{H_{2}}-s_{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\bigg]\rho^{\rm OPE}(s_{1},s_{2})\,.

4.1
Hard-collinear factorization for the correlation function at leading power

At leading power of
\delta_{V}\sim m_{V}/m_{Q}
, within the naive dimensional regularization (NDR) scheme for
\gamma_{5}
, the correlation function in perturbative QCD can be generally expressed as the following factorization form

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm LP}^{\rm OPE}((p+q)^{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=q^{\mu}\,\left(\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(0)}+\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(1)}\right)\otimes\big\langle O^{V}_{\mu}\big\rangle\,,

(26)

where
\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(0)}
and
\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(1)}
are the leading order (LO) and next-to-leading order (NLO) hard coefficient function. The matrix element of the vector light-cone operator is

\displaystyle\big\langle O^{V}_{\mu}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle V(p,\varepsilon^{\ast})\big|\bar{q}^{\prime}(x)\gamma_{\mu}q(0)\big|0\big\rangle\,,

(27)

in the leading power approximation, the light-cone operator can be expanded by using the collinear quark field

\displaystyle O^{V}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}^{\prime}(x)\gamma_{\mu}q(0)\overset{\rm LP}{=}n_{\mu}\bar{\chi}(x)\frac{\bar{n}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss/}}{2}\chi(0)\,,

(28)

\chi(x)=W^{\dagger}(x)\xi(x)
stands for the gauge invariant building block,
\xi(x)
is the collinear quark field in position space,
W(x)
is the collinear Wilson line

\displaystyle W(x)=\mathbf{P}\exp\left[ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}ds\,\bar{n}\cdot A_{c}(x+s\bar{n})\right]\,.

(29)

The collinear operator in position space can be converted into the corresponding momentum label formalism

\displaystyle O^{V}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=n_{\mu}\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\omega_{1}(n\cdot x)}\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\equiv n_{\mu}\mathcal{O}_{V}\,,

(30)

where the operator
\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})

[
39
]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\equiv\bar{\chi}_{n,\,\omega_{1}}\frac{\bar{n}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss/}}{2}\chi_{n,\,\omega_{2}}\,,

(31)

\chi_{n,\,\omega_{1}(\omega_{2})}
is the collinear field in the momentum lable space. Then, the matrix element can be obtained as

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle V(p,\varepsilon^{\ast})\big|\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\omega_{1}(n\cdot x)}\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}du\,e^{\frac{i}{2}u(p\cdot x)}\big\langle\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\big\rangle\,,

(32)

where

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{n}\cdot\varepsilon^{\ast}}{2}m_{V}f_{V}^{\|}\phi_{2;V}^{\|}(u)\,,

(33)

\phi_{2;V}^{\|}(u)
is the twist-2 light-cone distribution amplitude of vector meson.
Therefore, the factorization form of the correlation function can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm LP}^{\rm OPE}((p+q)^{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=(n\cdot q)\int_{0}^{1}du\left(\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(0)}+\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(1)}\right)\big\langle\mathcal{J}_{V}(\vec{\omega})\big\rangle\,.

(34)

The hard coefficient functions can be extracted from the parton level correlation function

\displaystyle\Pi_{q\bar{q}}((p+q)^{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=i\int d^{4}x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\big\langle q(up)\,\bar{q}(\bar{u}p)\big|T\big\{j_{5}^{\dagger}(x),\,j_{5}(0)\big\}\big|0\big\rangle\,,

(35)

expanding the correlator to NLO, we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{q\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}+\Pi_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}+O(\alpha_{s}^{2})

\displaystyle=(n\cdot q)\bigg[H_{V}^{(0)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}+\Big(H_{V}^{(1)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}+H_{V}^{(0)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}\Big)+O(\alpha_{s}^{2})\bigg]\,,

(36)

then, the NLO renormalized hard-scattering kernel

\displaystyle H_{V}^{(1)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{T}_{V}^{(1)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}-H_{V}^{(0)}\otimes\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}\,,

(37)

where the second term is for the subtraction of the infrared divergence.

is the UV renormalized matrix element

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}=\Big[M_{V}^{(1)R}+Z_{V}^{(1)}\Big]\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{V}\big\rangle_{q\bar{q}}^{(0)}\,,

(38)

where

is the bare matrix element computed from the infrared regularization scheme

Inserting this expression into Eq.(

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have conducted a comprehensive investigation into the improved calculation of the strong couplings
g_{D_{(s)}D_{(s)}V}
and
g_{B_{(s)}B_{(s)}V}
, where
V=\rho,\omega,K^{*}
and
\phi
, within the framework of LCSRs.

Our investigation has advanced the understanding of the vacuum-to-vector-meson correlation function near the light-cone by establishing a rigorous factorization scheme, achieving leading power in
\delta_{V}
and next-to-leading order in
\alpha_{s}
through hard-collinear factorization.
The observed cancellation of the factorization scale up to
O(\alpha_{s}^{2})
for the correlation function, facilitated by the utilization of the evolution equation of twist-two distribution amplitudes, underscores the robustness of our calculation. We analytically derived the double spectral representation of the leading power QCD factorization formula, employing the parton-hadron duality ansatz and the double Borel transformation, then obtained the LCSRs for the couplings. We further confirmed the factorization scale independence for the leading power LCSRs governing the strong coupling constants
g_{HHV}
up to
O(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, which bolsters the reliability of our methodology.
Our comprehensive analysis, which includes subleading power corrections (up to
\delta_{V}^{4}
,
\delta_{s}^{1}
, and
(\delta_{V}\delta_{s})^{1}
) and higher-twist corrections (up to two-particle and three-particle twist-4) of the LCDAs of light vector mesons at leading order, culminates in refined computations for the
D_{(s)}D_{(s)}V
and
B_{(s)}B_{(s)}V
couplings, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in this field.

Through numerical exploration of the derived LCSRs governing the strong couplings
g_{HHV}
, it was evident that the twist-two LO terms played a pivotal role in shaping the contributions to the
D_{(s)}D_{(s)}V
and
B_{(s)}B_{(s)}V
couplings, validating earlier results from LCSR calculations
[
10
,
11
]
. The predicted NLO QCD corrections to twist-2 contributions for bottom-meson couplings resulted in an approximate
30\%
enhancement relative to tree-level determinations. Additionally, subleading twist contributions from two-particle and three-particle vector meson DAs are more significant for charm-meson couplings than for bottom-meson couplings. Comparing our LCSR predictions with different QCD techniques
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
8
]
generally yielded a satisfactory agreement for the obtained values of the
HHV
couplings within the theoretical uncertainties.

Have these couplings in hand, we extracted the effective coupling
\beta
in the HM
\chi
PT Lagrangian, yielding results consistent with those reported in Ref.
[
10
]
, albeit smaller than those predicted by the vector meson dominance (VMD) model
[
36
]
. Furthermore, we fulfilled a detailed exploration to the
SU(3)
flavour symmetry breaking effects by comparing the
HHV
(for
V
is
K^{\ast}
,
\omega
and
\phi
) couplings with
g_{DD\rho}
and
g_{BB\rho}
.

The analysis revealed breaking effects ranging from
-14\%
to
1\%
when employing the decay constants of heavy mesons calculated from LQCD, and from
-15\%
to
+5\%
when utilizing the decay constants obtained from two-point QCDSRs and with a range of
-19\%
to
+2\%
observed when employing decay constants obtained from experimental data. Specifically,
g_{HH\omega}
and
g_{HH\phi}
showcases negative symmetry breaking effects, while
g_{HHK^{*}}
demonstrates positive effects. The most obvious
SU(3)_{F}
breaking effects are observed in the coupling
g_{D_{s}D_{s}\phi}
which reach to
-19\%
, this is primarily due to the involvement of the maximum number of strange quarks in this channel.

We introduced a dispersion relation that links the residues of
H\to V
transition form factors
A_{0}
at the
H

HHV

[
24

28
]
. We also included the
g_{D_{(s)}D_{(s)}\rho}

D\to\rho
transition form factors
[
37
]
. Furthermore, our detailed investigation into
g_{B_{(s)}B_{(s)}V}
and

[
11

V

Advancing the LCSRs for the strong coupling
g_{HHV}
requires several critical steps. First, calculating the NLO QCD corrections to higher-twist vector meson DAs is crucial for addressing discrepancies between direct LCSRs calculations and indirect
H\to V
transition form factor
A_{0}
predictions. Second, updating the non-perturbative parameters in the conformal expansion of vector meson DAs is also essential for improving the accuracy of LCSRs predictions. Third, a twist-2 model for the vector meson, similar to the pion LCDA obtained from lattice QCD data

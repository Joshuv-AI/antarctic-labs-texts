On second order conditions for singular optimal control of port-Hamiltonian systems
M. Soledad Aronna, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
We study nonlinear singular optimal control problems of port-Hamil-tonian (descriptor) systems. We employ general control-affine cost functionals that include as a special case the energy supplied to the system. We first derive optimality conditions for the case of ordinary differential equations with and without control bounds by applying the general theory to the specially structured port-Hamiltonian case, and show that this leads to elegant optimality conditions, in particular in the linear case. We then extend these results to classes of nonlinear port-Hamiltonian descriptor systems.

In this paper we consider
a simplified form of real nonlinear port-Hamiltonian (pH) descriptor systems, see

,
30

, that have a governing equation

E\dot{x}=\big(J(x)-R(x)\big)Qx+G(x)u(t),

(1.1)

with an associated energy function

\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\ x\mapsto\frac{1}{2}x^{T}E^{T}Qx,

(1.2)

(often called
Hamiltonian
or

).
As is common in pH (descriptor) systems, the structure of the coefficients is the following:

•

J:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n,n}
is skew-symmetric;

•

R:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n,n}
is symmetric and positive semidefinite;

•

E,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n,n}
are such that

is symmetric and positive semidefinite and represents the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}E^{T}Qx
, i.e.,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial x}(x)=E^{T}Qx
;

•

G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n,m}
.

For (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
the

is given by

y=G^{T}(x)Qx.

(1.3)

Note that if
E
is invertible, then one obtains the classical form of a pH system by a change of variables (see
[
46
]
).

The success of modeling within the class of pH (descriptor) systems is due to its many important properties which include the invariance of the class under power-conserving interconnection, which greatly simplifies modularized automated modeling, and the invariance under Galerkin projection which makes the class very suitable for discretization and model reduction, see
[
8
,
38

. Physical properties like
energy dissipation, Lyapunov stability and passivity
are encoded in the structure of the equations leading directly to another key property of port-Hamiltonian (descriptor) systems, the

and the resulting

,
see e.g.
[
29
,
30
]
or
[
46
]
for the case of ordinary pH systems.
From a physics point of view, pH systems model the interaction of three types of energy/power. The
stored energy
(in the energy storing components) is represented by the nonnegative quadratic form
\mathcal{E}(x)
, the
dissipated energy
by the nonnegative quadratic form

\mathcal{D}(x)=-x^{T}Q^{T}RQx

and the

by the form

\mathcal{S}(y,u)=y^{T}u
.

Example 1.1
.

A classical example of pH modeling are (dissipative) Hamiltonian equations of motion, see e.g.
[

,

q

\tilde{p}

(replacing the usual linear momentum

),
together with a force term
\tilde{B}(q)u
, take the
descriptor form

\begin{bmatrix}M&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\dot{\tilde{p}}\\
\dot{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-D(p,q)&-I\\
I&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&K\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{p}\\
q\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{B}(q)\\
0\end{bmatrix}u

(1.4)

coupled with a collocated output

y=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{B}(q)^{T}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&K\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{p}\\
q\end{bmatrix}.

(1.5)

Here the energy function (Hamiltonian) is

\mathcal{E}(q,\tilde{p})=\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{p}}^{T}M\tilde{p}+\frac{1}{2}q^{T}Kq,

i.e., kinetic plus potential energy, where
M
is a positive (semi-)definite mass matrix,

is a positive semidefinite stiffness matrix, and
D(q,p)=D(q,p)^{T}
is a positive semidefinite coefficient matrix that models internal damping.
This representation has the advantage that small masses can be set to
0

. See
[
31
]
for a detailed analysis of different representations and transformations between the different formulations.
A typical optimal control application is arising in the control of the system into an equilibrium state, e.g. in the stabilization of buildings excited by earthquakes, see e.g.
[
27
,
42
]
.

With an increased understanding of the many advantageous properties of modeling with pH (descriptor) systems, in recent years also feedback control and optimal control problems for this model class have become an important research topic.

While the theoretical analysis from different perspectives is studied in
[

]

In the
optimal control of pH (descriptor) systems, a natural

is the integral of the supplied energy

\mathcal{J}(x,u)=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\mathcal{S}(y(\tau),u(\tau))\,d\tau=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}x(\tau)^{T}Q^{T}G(x)u(\tau)\,d\tau.

It is readily available as a mathematical expression in terms of the ports and it is interesting from an application point of view to employ it as the objective to be minimized in optimal control problems (OCPs)
[
11
,
12
,
34
]
,
where as cost function one typically uses

\tilde{J}(x,u):=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}x^{T}&u^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}&\tilde{S}\\
\tilde{S}^{T}&\tilde{R}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}.

with semidefinite
\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}&\tilde{S}\\
\tilde{S}^{T}&\tilde{R}\end{bmatrix}

While it is in general no problem to drop the quadratic term in

\tilde{Q}=0

, it is usually not possible to set

, as is done when the supplied energy is used as cost function. In this case the resulting OCP is typically singular,

In view of this difficulty, the topic of this paper is the analysis of singular OCPs with constraints given by pH (descriptor) systems without the regularization approach,
i.e. without the term

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section

2
On Goh and generalized Legendre-Clebsch conditions

We obtain our results by applying optimality conditions for singular optimal control
of control-affine systems
, known as
Goh
and
generalized Legendre-Clebsch conditions, that we briefly introduce in the sequel. We consider
problems of the form

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\quad\Psi(x(T))

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\dot{x}=f(x,u)=f_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x)u_{i},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad x(0)=x_{0}.

(2.3)

with
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
i=0\ldots,m
and
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}.

The
state

x
belongs to the state space
\mathcal{X}:=W^{1,\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
(the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m}),
with weak first order derivatives that have a finite
L^{\infty}
-norm, with
L^{\infty}
denoting the usual Lebesgue space) and the
input (control)

u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})^{T}
ranges in the control space
\mathcal{U}:=L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

At this point we recall that any problem with a general control-affine integral cost can be rewritten in the Mayer form (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) by adding an extra scalar state variable (see (
3.1
) below). Therefore, the formulation (
2.1
)-(
2.3
)
covers problems with integral-type costs of the form

\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\ell_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i}(x)u_{i}\,dt,

where
\ell_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for
i=0\ldots,m
.

For nonlinear systems as the one in (
2.2
), one cannot obtain the control straightforwardly from Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle (PMP). More precisely, the maximum condition provided by the PMP results in a maximization with respect to the control variable of the
Hamilton
function, which is itself a control-affine function

\mathcal{H}=\lambda^{T}f=\lambda^{T}(f_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x)u_{i}),

(2.4)

\lambda
here being the
adjoint state,
whose associated
adjoint equation
is given by

-\dot{\lambda}^{T}=\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}=\lambda^{T}\partial_{x}f,\quad\lambda(T)=\nabla\Psi(x(T)).

(2.5)

Consequently, whenever

\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}=\lambda^{T}f_{i}=0,

(2.6)

holds along a non-trivial interval of time for some
i\in\{1,\dots,m\},
no information about the optimal control can be retrieved from (
2.4
). When this situation occurs along an optimal trajectory, it is said that the corresponding control has a
singular arc.
Briefly speaking, singular arcs of a control component
u_{i}
correspond to intervals in which
\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial u_{i}}
vanishes and the corresponding Hessian matrix
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{H}}{\partial u^{2}}
is singular (which holds trivially in the current control-affine framework).
In the absence of control constraints, (
2.6
) results in a necessary condition for optimality.
Since (
2.6
) does not depend explicitly on the control variable, one can differentiate with respect to time and obtains

0=\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}=\dot{\lambda}^{T}f_{i}+\lambda^{\top}f_{i}^{\prime}\dot{x}=\lambda^{T}\big([f_{0},f_{i}]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}[f_{j},f_{i}]\big),

(2.7)

where we used the state equation (
2.2
) and the adjoint equation (
2.5
) to replace
\dot{x}
and
\dot{\lambda},
and where
f_{i}^{\prime}
denotes the Jacobian, i.e., the derivative of
f_{i}
with respect to
x
, and for any pair of smooth vector fields
h,k\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
[h,k]:=k^{\prime}h-h^{\prime}k
is their
Lie bracket
, with
k^{\prime},h^{\prime}
denoting the Jacobians of
k,h
.
A remarkable property, discovered by Goh
[
14
]
(and further explored by Robbins
[
40
]
), is that the coefficients of the control in the first time derivative (
2.7
) of
\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}
vanish. More precisely,
Goh conditions
state the following, see e.g.
[
3
,
13
,
43
]
for rigorous proofs.

Theorem 2.1

(Goh condition)
.

For the optimal control problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
), the

Goh condition

\lambda^{T}[f_{i},f_{j}]=0,\quad\text{for }i,j=1,\dots,m

(2.8)

holds along optimal trajectories.

Remark 2.1
.

At this point it is worth recalling that the traditional technique of adding a regularizing term of the form
\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}^{2}
with positive
\alpha
and then taking
\alpha\to 0
does not work in this context, since (
2.6
) will depend on
u
and then the expression (
2.7
) would not be possible. On the other hand, if one aims at obtaining a feedback formula for the controls on singular arcs by applying Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle to the modified quadratic cost and taking
\alpha\to 0,
the expression contains
\alpha
in the denominator and the limit may fail to exist, see, e.g.
[
17
]
.
Despite this difficulty, it has been shown in the case of linear constant coefficients that this problem has some hidden regularity in a subspace
[
11
]
.

The Goh property (
2.8
) guarantees then that (
2.7
) does not explicitly contain the control variables, which allows one to differentiate the expression (
2.7
) once more. In the second order derivative
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{u},
the matrix coefficient of the control variable turns out to be semidefinite.
Indeed, one gets

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}=\lambda^{T}[f_{0},[f_{0},f_{i}]]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\lambda^{T}[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]].

(2.9)

Thus, from
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}=0
one obtains a linear system for
u
given by

Wu+d=0,

(2.10)

with the
Hessian matrix

W=[w_{ij}]
and the right hand side vector

d
, where for
i,j=1,\dots,m
,

w_{ij}\coloneqq\lambda^{T}[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]],\qquad d_{i}\coloneqq\lambda^{T}[f_{0},[f_{0},f_{i}]].

(2.11)

This leads to a
generalized Legendre-Clebsch condition
, see
e.g.
[
20
, Theorem 6.2 and/or Corollary 6.3]
,
[
3
]
, or
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Generalized Legendre-Clebsch condition)
.

For the nonlinear control-affine problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
), the associated Hessian matrix
W
in (
2.11
) is negative semidefinite
along optimal trajectories.
Additionally, if
W
is actually negative definite,
then, from (
2.10
), one can express the optimal control in
feedback form
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, whenever
W
is actually negative definite, the feedback obtained from (
2.10
) will depend on the state and adjoint variables.

The extensions of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
to control-constrained problems are provided in the subsequent sections, in which we use Goh and the generalized Legendre-Clebsch conditions to deduce properties for pH systems.

3.1
Optimality conditions for ordinary nonlinear pH systems

We first consider the following optimal control problem governed by the nonlinear ordinary pH dynamics (
1.1
) with

,

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle x_{n+1}(T)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\dot{x}=\big(J(x)-R(x)\big)\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial x}(x)+G(x)u(t),

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{n+1}=\ell_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i}(x)u_{i},

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x_{n+1}\end{bmatrix}(t_{0})=\begin{bmatrix}x_{0}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

with the assumption that the control

takes values in an
open set

\mathbb{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For the sake of simplicity in the presentation of the results, we introduce the abbreviations

g_{0}(x)\coloneqq\big(J(x)-R(x)\big)Qx=\big(J(x)-R(x)\big)\frac{\partial{\mathcal{E}}}{\partial x}(x),

(3.2)

and, setting

z\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x_{n+1}\end{bmatrix}

f_{0}(z)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}g_{0}(x)\\

(3.3)

In this case, recalling the

\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\\

In the absence of (state or terminal) constraints, the multiplier

Remark 3.1
.

Note that the use of the supplied energy as cost function in (

Corollary 3.1

(Goh conditions for nonlinear ordinary pH control)
.

For the optimal control problem with nonlinear pH constraint (
3.1
), it holds that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\lambda^{T}[g_{i},g_{j}]&=0,\\
\ell_{j}^{\prime}\,g_{i}-\ell_{i}^{\prime}\,g_{j}&=0,\end{array}\right.\qquad\text{for }\,i,j=1,\ldots,m,

(3.4)

along optimal trajectories.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Using Corollary
3.4
, we compute the expressions for the
generalized Legendre-Clebsch conditions

(see Theorem
2.2
, and
W
and
d
given in (

)) in the context of problem (

[f_{0},f_{i}]=\begin{bmatrix}[g_{0},g_{i}]\\

[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]]=\begin{bmatrix}[g_{j},[g_{0},g_{i}]]\\

Remark 3.2
.

Note that when
W
is non-singular, the control
u
can be expressed in feedback form as a function of the state and costate variables. This representation is useful for establishing the regularity of the control and facilitates the application of numerical techniques, such as the shooting method.

While the Goh and generalized Legendre-Clebsch conditions from Corollaries
3.1
and
3.2
do not offer much insight for general nonlinear pH systems, they simplify significantly, or even hold trivially, in specific examples where symmetry properties naturally arise. This simplification reveals an interesting novelty, which is exemplified in the general nonlinear model for dissipative Hamiltonian equations of motion, introduced above in Example
1.1
.

Example 3.3
.

In Example
1.1
, with the associated cost of minimizing the energy supply

\int_{0}^{T}y^{\top}u\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial p}^{\top}B(q)u\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}p^{\top}M^{-1}B(q)u\,dt

the Lie brackets
[f_{i},f_{j}]
are given by

[f_{i},f_{j}]=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
B_{j}(q)^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}}{\partial p^{2}}B_{i}(q)-B_{i}(q)^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}}{\partial p^{2}}B_{j}(q)\end{bmatrix}.

These vanish identically for any sufficiently regular
\mathcal{E}
. In the current framework, one has
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}}{\partial p^{2}}=M^{-1},
for
M
being symmetric positive definite, so
Goh conditions (
3.4
) hold trivially
.
One gets the following expression for the second-order Lie brackets

[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]]=\begin{bmatrix}B_{i}^{\prime}M^{-1}B_{j}+B_{j}^{\prime}M^{-1}B_{i}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\\
+\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial D}{\partial p}(M^{-1}B_{i})B_{j}+\frac{\partial^{2}D}{\partial p^{2}}(M^{-1}p,B_{i})B_{j}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

which considerably simplifies under additional assumptions on

or
B

3.2
Optimality conditions for nonlinear ordinary pH systems with control bounds

In this section we discuss problem (
3.1
) in the presence of bounds on the control. This means that we no longer assume that
\mathbb{U}
is open, but consider a set of admissible control values

\mathbb{U}=[\underline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{1}]\times\dots[\underline{u}_{m},\overline{u}_{m}],

(3.8)

with real numbers
\underline{u}_{i}\leq\overline{u}_{i},
for each
i=1,\dots,m
.

In this case, the Goh optimality condition is restricted to the components that lie in the interior of the control set and one has the following optimality condition.

Theorem 3.4

(Goh condition for ordinary pH control under control constraints)
.

Consider the nonlinear pH optimal control problem (
3.1
) under the control constraint
u(t)\in\mathbb{U}
, for a.e.
t\in[0,T],
with the set
\mathbb{U}
as in (
3.8
).
Then, on any open interval over which an optimal control
u^{*}
verifies

\underline{u}_{i}<u^{*}_{i}(t)<\overline{u}_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\underline{u}_{j}<u^{*}_{j}(t)<\overline{u}_{j},

for some pair
i,j=1,\ldots,m,

the Goh conditions (
3.4
)
must necessarily hold.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
is not a straightforward consequence of the unconstrained version of Theorem
2.1
. For more details, the reader is referred to
[
3
,
13
]
.

One gets the following generalized Legendre-Clebsch condition for the square submatrix of
W
corresponding to singular components.

Theorem 3.5

(Generalized Legendre-Clebsch condition and feedback formula for nonlinear ordinary pH control under control constraints)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.4
, let
(a,b)
be an open interval on which

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{u}_{i}<u^{*}_{i}(t)<\overline{u}_{i},&\text{for }i\in S,\\
u^{*}_{i}(t)=\underline{u}_{i},&\text{for }i\in\underline{B},\\
u^{*}_{i}(t)=\overline{u}_{i},&\text{for }i\in\overline{B}.\end{array}\right.

Thus,
\{S,\underline{B},\overline{B}\}
is a partition of
\{1,\dots,m\}
into singular, lower-bang and upper-bang components of the control
u^{*}.

One gets,
for
i\in S
,
that

\displaystyle\lambda^{T}[f_{0},[f_{0},f_{i}]]+\sum_{j\in S}u_{j}^{*}\lambda^{T}[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]]

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in\underline{B}}\underline{u}_{j}\lambda^{T}[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]]+\sum_{j\in\overline{B}}\overline{u}_{j}\lambda^{T}[f_{j},[f_{0},f_{i}]]=0.

holds on
(a,b)

4
Optimality conditions for linear pH systems

In the last section we have presented the Goh conditions for pH (descriptor) systems which turned out to be much simpler than those for general systems. This simplicity is even more pronounced in the case of linear pH systems.

Remark 4.1
.

In Theorems

and
4.2

Higher-order conditions (see, e.g.,

4.3
Linear descriptor systems

The results in this section can be directly applied to the class of index-one descriptor systems as in the nonlinear case. One obtains an explicit formula for
x_{2}
as

x_{2}=-[(J_{22}-R_{22})Q_{22}]^{-1}[(J_{22}-R_{22})Q_{21}+(-J_{12}^{T}-R_{12}^{T})Q_{11}]x_{1}.

Inserting this into the first equation gives an ordinary pH system only in
x_{1}
of the form

\begin{split}\dot{x}_{1}&=(\tilde{J}_{11}-\tilde{R}_{11})Q_{11}x_{1}+\tilde{G}_{1}u,\\
y&=\tilde{G}_{1}^{T}Q_{11}x_{1},\end{split}

and
x_{2}
can be determined in a post-processing step.

The cost functional is then

\mathcal{J}(x_{1},u)=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}x_{1}^{T}(\tau)Q_{11}^{T}\tilde{G}u(\tau)d\tau

and thus we have again reduced the problem
to one for which the previous formulas apply.

5
Conclusions and future work

For the singular optimal control problem of controlling a port-Hamiltonian system with the supplied energy as cost function, the Goh optimality conditions are derived. It is shown that the use of the specific port-Hamiltonian structure leads to very elegant solution formulas, in particular in the case of linear port-Hamiltonian systems with quadratic Hamiltonian. Optimality conditions are also presented for the case of control constraints.

It remains an open problem how to give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a feedback control in the case when the matrix
W
in the generalized Legende-Clebsch condition is only semidefinite. This is closely related to the topic to derive the optimality conditions for general port-Hamiltonian descriptor systems.

Another classical application of optimal control is the computation of (feedback) controls
u=k(x)
or output feedbacks
u=k(y)
so that the closed loop system is asymptotically stable. To achieve this, one just chooses an infinite interval, i.e.
T=\infty
, and requires that for the closed loop solution,
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=0
. For this case, analogous results to the ones obtained in this work are expected to hold.
It is also an interesting question what happens when the usual approach, of adding a quadratic penalty term
\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{i}^{2}
to the cost function is used, and one considers the convergence behavior for
\alpha\to 0
, see
[
17
]
. The Goh conditions will not appear in that context, since
\mathcal{H}_{u}
depends on the control and, consequently, it is, in general, not differentiable in time.

We have studied problems without terminal constraints, which admit a unique multiplier that verifies
normality
, i.e., the multiplier associated to the integral cost can be normalized to
1
.
In
[
4
,
3
]
a framework for dealing with general terminal constraints of the form

\varphi_{i}(x(T))\leq 0,

\eta_{j}(x(T))=0,
for
i=1,\dots,d_{\varphi},
and
j=1,\dots,d_{\eta}
was introduced. Also,
[
13
]
consider terminal constraints of the form
x(T)\in K
under an additional hypothesis.

Using the techniques in
[
4
,
3
]
one may as well discuss the derivation of second order sufficient conditions.

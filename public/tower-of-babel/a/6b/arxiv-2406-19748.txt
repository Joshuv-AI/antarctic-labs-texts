Supersingular Ekedahl-Oort strata and Oort’s conjecture
Valentijn Karemaker, Chia-Fu Yu

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 g {\mathscr{A}}_{g} be the moduli space over 𝔽 ¯ p \overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p} of g g -dimensional principally polarised abelian varieties, where p p is a prime. We show that if g g is even and p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , then every geometric generic member in the maximal supersingular Ekedahl-Oort stratum in 𝒜 g {\mathscr{A}}_{g} has automorphism group { ± 1 } \{\pm 1\} . This confirms Oort’s conjecture in the case of p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 and even g g . We also separately prove Oort’s conjecture for g = 4 g=4 and any prime p p .

1.
Introduction

Let
g\geq 1
be a positive integer and
p
a prime number.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}_{g}
be the moduli space over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
of
g
-dimensional principally polarised abelian varieties, and let
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
be the supersingular locus of
{\mathscr{A}}_{g}
.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. For each member
(X,\lambda)
in
{\mathscr{A}}_{g}(k)
, it is a fundamental question to understand what the endomorphism ring
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)
of
X
and the automorphism group
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)
of
(X,\lambda)
may be. Moreover, it is also interesting to understand how these arithmetic invariants vary in the moduli space
{\mathscr{A}}_{g}
or in a subvariety, for example, in
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
.

Chai and Oort
[
5
]
showed that for any prime
\ell\neq p
, the
\ell
-adic monodromy attached to any non-supersingular central leaf
{\mathscr{C}}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}_{g}(k)
is surjective and that
{\mathscr{C}}
is irreducible. It follows that the geometric generic member
(X_{\bar{\eta}},\lambda_{\bar{\eta}})
of
{\mathscr{C}}
has endomorphism ring
\mathbb{Z}
and hence automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
. Using this, every geometric generic member
(X_{\bar{\eta}},\lambda_{\bar{\eta}})
of either a non-supersingular Newton stratum or a non-supersingular Ekedahl-Oort (EO) stratum (i.e., an Ekedahl-Oort stratum that is not entirely contained in
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
) also shares the same property. One may ask what one can say for the supersingular case. Oort’s conjecture
[
10
, Question 4]
states that when
g\geq 2
, every geometric generic member in
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
, even though its endomorphism ring is always of
\mathbb{Z}
-rank
4g^{2}
.
For
g=2
and
p>2
, Oort’s conjecture has been proved by Ibukiyama
[
15
]
, and by the first author and Pries
[
17
]
independently, with a counterexample in
p=2
.

In
[
18
]
the present authors and Yobuko consider the mass function on the supersingular locus:

\Mass:{\mathscr{S}}_{g}\to\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\quad x\mapsto\Mass(x)=\Mass({\mathscr{C}}(x)):=\sum_{(X,\lambda)\in{\mathscr{C}}(x)}\frac{1}{\lvert\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)\rvert},

where
{\mathscr{C}}(x)
is the central leaf passing through the point
x
.
This function decomposes the supersingular locus into pieces of strata, called mass strata, which encode information on the jump of arithmetic invariants.
Concrete descriptions of mass strata and explicit mass formulae on each stratum were obtained by the second author and J.D. Yu
[
33
]
for
g=2
, and by the authors of
[
18
]
for
g=3
.
The latter authors also showed that on the stratum of maximal mass, each geometric point has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
if
p>2
, and
\{\pm 1\}^{3}
if
p=2
, confirming Oort’s conjecture for
g=3
, with again a counterexample in
p=2
.

Chai and Oort
[
5
]
also showed that every non-supersingular Newton stratum is irreducible. Ekedahl and van der Geer
[
11
]
showed that every non-supersingular EO stratum is also irreducible.
For the supersingular case, it is known that the number of irreducible components is large as long as either
p
or
g
is large, as shown by Li and Oort
[
23
]
for
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
, and by Harashita
[
12
]
for supersingular EO strata.
In
[
16
]
the present authors and Ibukiyama determine precisely when the supersingular locus
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
, and a supersingular central leaf are irreducible. For determining the irreducibility of the latter, the authors were led to explore automorphism groups that occur in supersingular EO strata in
{\mathscr{A}}_{4}
.

The present work is a continuation of the authors’ exploration into the variation of arithmetic invariants on supersingular EO strata.
We prove the following results.

Theorem A
.

If
g
is even and
p\geq 5
, then every geometric generic member in the maximal supersingular EO stratum has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
.

The assertion of Theorem
A
fails if either
g
is odd or
p=2
; see Remark
6.18
.

Theorem B
.

Oort’s conjecture holds true for the case where
g
is even and
p\geq 5
.

We explain how Theorem A implies Theorem B. First, we show that the set of irreducible components of
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
is transitive under
\ell
-adic Hecke correspondences, where
\ell\neq p
is a prime. It follows that every irreducible component
Y
of the supersingular locus
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
contains an irreducible component
W
of the maximal supersingular EO stratum. Since the polarised abelian variety corresponding to the geometric generic point of
W
has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
when
g
is even and
p\geq 5
, the same holds for the geometric generic point of
Y
.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem A is as follows. We consider the locus
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}\subseteq{\mathscr{S}}_{g}
which is the union of all supersingular EO strata. Each irreducible component of
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
admits a finite cover which is a Lagrangian variety
{\mathrm{L}}
. We introduce a stratification on
{\rm L}
by the relative endomorphism algebra of its isotropic subspaces.
Using this, we construct a new stratification on

{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
.
It describes the jumps of the endomorphism rings of abelian varieties in
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
. There is a unique maximal stratum which is open and dense in
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
.
We further show that
when
g
is even and
p\geq 5
, every geometric point in the maximal stratum has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
. It follows that
the polarised abelian variety corresponding to each geometric generic point of
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
.

In a recent preprint
[
7
]
, Dušan Dragutinović obtained a different proof of Oort’s conjecture when
g=4
and
p>2
, as well as a new proof when
g=3
and
p>2
, using the moduli space of curves in both cases. While Theorem A does not cover the cases
p=2,3
, we show in Section
7
that this restriction can be removed when
g=4
, by proving Oort’s conjecture for all
p
. This result provides strong evidence for Oort’s conjecture holding true, except when
(g,p)=(2,2)
or
(3,2)
.

A main tool in this paper is the notion of the relative endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V,W)
of pairs of vector spaces
(V,W)
over a field extension
L/K
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
K
,
W\subseteq V\otimes_{K}L
is an
L
-subspace, and

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V,W):=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{K}(V):\alpha(W)\subseteq W\}.

This notion also applies to, e.g., the endomorphism algebras of Drinfeld modules over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, the function field analogue of the complex numbers, and those of complex tori. The computation of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V,W)
already appeared in Albert’s works
[
1
,
2
]
for computing the matrix multiplication of Riemann matrices. In this paper, we exploit this invariant for studying the endomorphism rings and automorphism groups of certain polarised supersingular abelian varieties (see Section 5); through the construction of the stratification by this invariant, we further investigate how the endomorphism rings and automorphism groups may vary for polarised abelian varieties on supersingular EO strata. Besides its application to Oort’s conjecture, we also show that this stratification refines the mass stratification studied in
[
18
]
, and we provide the mass formula for each stratum in Theorem
6.19
. For proving Oort’s conjecture for
g=4
, we use results of Harashita
[
13
]
on the moduli space of four-dimensional rigid polarised flag type quotients.

The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 studies basic algebraic properties for pairs of vector spaces and their endomorphism algebras. In Section 3 we give a method for computing the relative endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
of a pair of vector spaces
(V_{0},W)
and introduce a stratification on the Grassmanian
\mathrm{Gr}
of
r
-dimensional subspaces
W
in a vector space
V_{0}
over a field
k_{0}
by the relative endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
. The main result (Theorem
3.5
) shows that on an open and dense subset of
\mathrm{Gr}
, the relative endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
is equal to
k_{0}
, except when
k_{0}
is either an algebraically closed field, or a real closed field. Section 4 gives the parallel results of Section 3 for the Lagrangian
varieties of
r
-dimensional isotropic subspaces of a
2r
-dimensional symplectic space. The results of this section are exploited in Section 6 to construct the aforementioned stratification on
{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{\rm eo}
.
In Section 5, we show how relative endomorphism algebras are related to the endomorphism algebras of polarised abelian varieties and how the notion of relative endomorphism algebras can be used to compute the endomorphism rings and automorphism groups of certain supersingular polarised abelian varieties.
The proofs of Theorems A and B are given in Section 6.
Finally, Section 7 proves Oort’s conjecture in dimension
g=4
for all primes
p
, by explicit computations for Dieudonné modules and their automorphisms.

2.
Pairs of vector spaces and endomorphism algebras

In this section we present algebraic preliminaries for pairs of vector spaces and their endomorphism algebras.

2.1.
Linear independence of specialisations of monomials

Let
L/K
be a field extension and let
p:={\rm char\,}K\geq 0
. Denote by
\overline{K}
an algebraic closure of the field
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

L/K
is called
essentially finite
if either
p=0
and
L/K
is finite; or
p>0
,
L/K
is algebraic and there exist a finite extension
K^{\prime}/K
in
\overline{K}
and an integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
L\subseteq(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}
. The latter condition is equivalent to the existence of an integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that the field extension
L^{p^{r}}K/K
is finite.

(2)

L/K
is called
strictly infinite
if it is not essentially finite.

Recall that a real closed field is an ordered field
F
, so in particular
{\rm char\,}F=0
, such that every positive element of
F
has a square root in
F^{\times}
and every polynomial of odd degree has a root in
F
.
A field
F
is real closed if and only if
F\neq\overline{F}
and the field extension
\overline{F}/F
is finite.

Definition 2.2
.

We call a field extension
L/K

exceptional
if either
K=L=\overline{K}
, or
K
is real closed and
L=\overline{K}
. Equivalently,
L/K
is exceptional if and only if
L=\overline{L}
and
L/K
is finite.

Let
K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring over
K
of
n
variables, and
K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]_{\leq d}\subseteq K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, be the subspace consisting of polynomials of degree
\leq d
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
L/K
be a field extension.

(1)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

For any non-negative integers
n
and
d
, there exists an element
T=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in L^{n}
such that the evaluation map

{\rm ev}_{T}:K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]_{\leq d}\to L,\quad f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\mapsto f(T)

is injective.

(ii)

For any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there exists an element
\alpha\in L
such that
[K(\alpha):K]\geq N
.

(iii)

L/K
is strictly infinite.

(2)

Assume that there exists a perfect subfield
F\subseteq K
such that
\mathop{\rm trdeg}\nolimits(K/F)<\infty
and
K/F
is separably generated. Then condition (iii) is equivalent to

(iii’)

L/K
is infinite.

(3)

Assume that
L=\overline{L}
. Then condition (iii) is equivalent to

(iii”)

L/K
is not exceptional.

Proof.

(1)

Observe that condition (ii) is equivalent to the
n=1
case of (i). This shows (i)
\implies
(ii).

(ii)
\implies
(i): Take
k_{i}:=(d+1)^{i-1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Using the unique
(d+1)
-adic expression of integers, the integers

i_{1}k_{1}+\dots+i_{n}k_{n}
are mutually distinct for
i_{1},\dots i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
i_{1}+\dots+i_{n}\leq d
. Then the evaluation map

{\rm ev}_{(X^{k_{1}},\dots,X^{k_{n}})}:K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]_{\leq d}\to K[X]_{\leq d^{\prime}}

is injective, where
d^{\prime}=(d+1)^{n}
.
By (ii), there exists
\alpha\in L

such that
{\rm ev}_{\alpha}:K[X]_{\leq d^{\prime}}\to L
is injective. Statement (i) holds.

(iii)
\implies
(ii):
If
\mathop{\rm trdeg}\nolimits(L/K)>0
, letting
\alpha\in L
be a transcendental element, then
[K(\alpha):K]\geq N
for any integer
N
. So we may assume that
L/K
is algebraic and infinite.
Suppose first that
p=0
. As
L/K
is infinite, we may take a finite subextension
K^{\prime}/K
of
L/K
of degree
\geq N
. Since
K^{\prime}/K
is separable,
K^{\prime}=K(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in K^{\prime}
and
[K(\alpha):K]\geq N
.

Suppose now that
p>0
. Let
K_{s}
be the separable closure of
K
in
L
.

Case 1:
K_{s}/K
is infinite. We show (ii) as in the
p=0
case.

Case 2:
K_{s}/K
is finite. One has that
L/K_{s}
is purely inseparable. For any integer
N\geq 1
, take an integer
r
such that
p^{r+1}\geq N
.
Since
L/K
is strictly infinite, one has
L\not\subseteq(K_{s})^{p^{-r}}
. Choose an element
\alpha\in L\setminus(K_{s})^{p^{-r}}
. Then the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
is
T^{p^{k}}-a
for some
a\in K_{s}
and some
k\geq r+1
, otherwise
L\subseteq(K_{s})^{p^{-r}}
. Therefore,
[K(\alpha):K]\geq[K_{s}(\alpha):K_{s}]=p^{k}\geq N
.

(ii)
\implies
(iii): Condition (ii) implies that
L/K
is infinite. If
p=0
, then
L/K
is strictly infinite by definition. Assume
p>0
. Suppose
L/K
is essentially finite. Then there exist a finite subextension
K^{\prime}/K
and an integer
r
such that
L\subseteq(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}
. Then for any
\alpha\in L
, one has
\alpha^{p^{r}}\in K^{\prime}
and

[K(\alpha):K]=[K(\alpha):K(\alpha^{p^{r}})][K(\alpha^{p^{r}}):K]\leq p^{r}[K^{\prime}:K],

a contradiction to condition (ii).

(2)

A strictly infinite field extension is infinite. We show the reverse. It suffices to show the case
p>0
.
Since
K/F
is separably generated and
\mathop{\rm trdeg}\nolimits(K/F)<\infty
, there exist algebraically independent elements
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
in
K
over
F
such that
K/F(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
is algebraic and separable. By definition, we need to show that for any finite subextension
K^{\prime}/K
in
L
and any integer
r
, one has
L\not\subseteq(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}
.

Since
K^{\prime}/K
is finite, the extension
(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}/K^{p^{-r}}
is also finite.
As
K
is algebraic and separable over
F(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
, one has

K^{p^{-r}}=K\cdot F(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})^{p^{-r}}=K\cdot F(t_{1}^{p^{-r}},\dots,t_{m}^{p^{-r}})=K(t_{1}^{p^{-r}},\dots,t_{m}^{p^{-r}})

and then
[K^{p^{-r}}:K]
is finite.
Therefore,
(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}/K
is finite and
L
cannot be contained in
(K^{\prime})^{p^{-r}}
, as
L/K
is infinite.

(3)

If
L=\overline{L}
, then
L/K
is essentially finite if and only if
L=\overline{K}
and
L/K
is finite. By definition, this is equivalent to that
L/K
is exceptional.

∎

2.2.
Pairs of vector spaces and endomorphism algebras

Fix a field extension
L/K
as before.

Definition 2.4
.

(1)

Consider a pair
({V_{0}},W)
, where
{V_{0}}
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
K
and
W
is an
L
-subspace of
V_{0,L}={V_{0}}\otimes_{K}L
. We call
({V_{0}},W)
a
pair of vector spaces over
(K,L)
, and
(\dim_{K}{V_{0}},\dim_{L}W)
the dimension of
({V_{0}},W)
. For two pairs
({V_{0}},W)
and
({V_{0}}^{\prime},W^{\prime})
of vector spaces over
(K,L)
, define

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(({V_{0}},W),({V_{0}}^{\prime},W^{\prime})):=\{f:\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{K}({V_{0}},{V_{0}}^{\prime}):f(W)\subseteq W^{\prime}\}.

By

we understand that

is the extended map
{V_{0}}_{L}\to{V_{0}}^{\prime}_{L}
by linearity.
Each element of
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(({V_{0}},W),({V_{0}}^{\prime},W^{\prime}))

({V_{0}},W)
to
({V_{0}}^{\prime},W^{\prime})

A homomorphism

if there is an
f^{\prime}\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(({V_{0}}^{\prime},W^{\prime}),({V_{0}},W))

.
Let
({\rm Vec}_{L/K})
denote the category of pairs of vector spaces over

.

(2)

When
\dim{V_{0}}\geq 1
, let
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({V_{0}},W)
denote the endomorphism algebra of the pair

. We call
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({V_{0}},W)
the endomorphism algebra of

of

is understood. The

of

.

In the category
({\rm Vec}_{L/K})
, one has the zero object
\underline{0}=(0,0)
, the identity object
{\mathbb{I}}=(K,L)
, the direct sum
\oplus
and the tensor product

satisfying

(V_{0},W)\oplus(V_{0}^{\prime},W^{\prime}):=(V_{0}\oplus V_{0}^{\prime},W\oplus W^{\prime}),\quad(V_{0},W)\otimes(V_{0}^{\prime},W^{\prime}):=(V_{0}\otimes_{K}V_{0}^{\prime},W\otimes_{L}W^{\prime}),

and the dual defined by

, where

{V_{0}}^{\vee}:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{K}({V_{0}},K),\quad W^{\vee}:=\{f\in{V_{0,L}}^{\vee}=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{L}({V_{0,L}},L):f(W)=0\}.

is an additive tensor category but not an abelian category. In particular, it is not equivalent to the category
(R
-mod
)
of

-modules for some ring

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra and
G=E^{\times}
be the multiplicative group. If
K
is infinite, then
E
is equal to the
K
-subalgebra generated by
G
. In this case,

is uniquely determined by
G
.

Proof.

Let
\underline{E}
be the affine space over
K
defined by
E
and
\underline{G}
be the multiplication group scheme associated to
E
over
K
. Let
E^{\prime}
be the
K
-subalgebra generated by
G
. Since
\underline{E}
is rational and
\underline{G}\subseteq\underline{E}
is Zariski-open,
\underline{G}
is rational and

is dense in
\underline{G}
. It follows that
G
is dense in
\underline{E}
. As
\underline{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\underline{E}
is closed, one has
\underline{E}^{\prime}\supseteq\overline{G}=\underline{E}
. This shows

.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

When

.

Let

Lemma 2.7

Assume

(1)

There is a natural bijection between

(2)

One has

.

(3)

If

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({V_{0}},W_{L^{\prime}})=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({V_{0}},W)

Proof.

(1)

By definition, the set

, so of
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits({V_{0}})/\mathop{\rm Stab}\nolimits(E_{0})

Lemma 2.12

(M_{1}+M_{2})\otimes_{A}B=(M_{1}\otimes_{A}B)+(M_{2}\otimes_{A}B),\quad(M_{1}\cap M_{2})\otimes_{A}B=(M_{1}\otimes_{A}B)\cap(M_{2}\otimes_{A}B).

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequence

\begin{split}0\to(M_{1}\cap M_{2})\otimes_{A}B\to&(M_{1}\oplus M_{2})\otimes_{A}B\to(M_{1}+M_{2})\otimes_{A}B\to 0,\\
0\to(M_{1}\otimes_{A}B)\cap(M_{2}\otimes_{A}B)\to&M_{1}\otimes_{A}B\oplus M_{2}\otimes_{A}B\to(M_{1}\otimes_{A}B)+(M_{2}\otimes_{A}B)\to 0.\end{split}

\supseteq
as

and

3.
General linear groups

From now on, we let
k_{0}
be a field and
k
an algebraically closed field containing
k_{0}
. Let
V_{0}
be an
n
-dimensional
k_{0}
-vector space with
n\geq 2
, and
r
be an integer with
0<r<n
. Let
\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)
be the Grassmannian of subspaces of
V_{0}
of dimension
r
. Set
\mathrm{Gr}=\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)\otimes_{k_{0}}k
. As a
k
-algebraic variety, we may identity
\mathrm{Gr}
with the set of
k
-points of
\mathrm{Gr}
.

Two
k
-subspaces
W
and
W^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Gr}
are said to be
isomorphic
if there is an element
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
such that
W^{\prime}=\gamma\cdot W
; equivalently, there is an isomorphism
(V_{0},W)\simeq(V_{0},W^{\prime})
of pairs of vector spaces. In this case, we have

(13)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W^{\prime})=\gamma\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\gamma^{-1},\quad\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(V_{0},W^{\prime})=\gamma\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\gamma^{-1}.

If
P=[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]
is a presenting matrix for
W
with basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
, then
P^{\prime}=\gamma P=[v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{r}^{\prime}]
is a presenting matrix for
W^{\prime}
with basis
v_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma v_{1},\dots,v_{r}^{\prime}=\gamma v_{r}
. The operation
P\mapsto\gamma P
for
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
contains all permutations of rows. Thus, we get the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

Any
r
-dimensional
k
-subspace
W
is isomorphic to a
k
-subspace
W^{\prime}
whose representative matrix
P^{\prime}
with respect to any basis has its last
r\times r
block invertible. In other words, the last
r
row vectors are
k
-linearly independent.

After a suitable translation by
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
, we may represent
W
by

\begin{bmatrix}T\\
{\mathbb{I}}_{r}\end{bmatrix}
, where
T=(t_{ij})\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r,r}(k)
, for a suitable basis
\{v_{i}\}
; see Equation (
4
).
For any element

\alpha=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\in\begin{bmatrix}\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r,n-r}(k_{0})&\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r,r}(k_{0})\\
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{r,n-r}(k_{0})&\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{r,r}(k_{0})\end{bmatrix}=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0}),

one has

\alpha(W)\subseteq W\iff\alpha P=\begin{bmatrix}AT+B\\
CT+D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}T\\
{\mathbb{I}}_{r}\end{bmatrix}\cdot(CT+D).

The right hand side gives the equation

(14)

TCT+TD-AT-B=0\ \text{in }\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r,r}(k).

Thus, we get

(15)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(k_{0}):TCT+TD-AT-B=0\right\}.

Conversely, let
E\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
be a
k_{0}
-subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
. Suppose
E=k_{0}[\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{s}]
is generated by some elements
\alpha_{\mu}\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
and write

\alpha_{\mu}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{\mu}&B_{\mu}\\
C_{\mu}&D_{\mu}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mu=1,\dots,s.

Let
U
be the open affine subset

(16)

U:=\left\{\big\langle\begin{bmatrix}T\\
{\mathbb{I}}_{r}\end{bmatrix}\big\rangle:T\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r,r}(k)\right\}\subseteq\mathrm{Gr},

and let

(17)

U_{E}:=\left\{W\in U:E\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\right\}.

Clearly
U\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}
is an open subvariety over
k
that is defined over
k_{0}
. Then
U_{E}\subseteq U
is the closed subvariety over
k_{0}
defined by the matrix equations

(18)

TC_{\mu}T+TD_{\mu}-A_{\mu}T-B_{\mu}=0,\quad\mu=1,\dots,s.

We define

\mathrm{Gr}_{E}:=\{W\in\mathrm{Gr}:E\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\},

which is a closed subvariety of
\mathrm{Gr}
. Indeed,
we cover
\mathrm{Gr}
by open affine subsets
\gamma U
with
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
. For each open affine subset
\gamma U
, the intersection
(\gamma U)\cap\mathrm{Gr}_{E}=(\gamma U)_{E}\simeq U_{\gamma E\gamma^{-1}}
which is a closed subvariety shown as above. Therefore,
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}
is a closed subvariety. From (
18
), we see that
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
is defined over
k_{0}
.

Clearly, for any two
k_{0}
-subalgebras
E,E^{\prime}\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
, we have

E\subseteq E^{\prime}\iff\mathrm{Gr}_{E}\supseteq\mathrm{Gr}_{E^{\prime}}.

Therefore, we obtain a stratification on
\mathrm{Gr}=\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)
by

\mathrm{Gr}=\bigcup_{E}\mathrm{Gr}_{E}

by closed subvarieties
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
, where
E
runs through all
k_{0}
-subalgebras of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
.

Denote by

(19)

{\mathscr{E}}:=\{\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W):W\in\mathrm{Gr}\}

the set of all
k_{0}
-algebras of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
that occurs as the relative endomorphism algebra of
W
for some
W\in\mathrm{Gr}
.

For each
E\in{\mathscr{E}}
, set

(20)

(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}:=\{W\in\mathrm{Gr}:\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)=E\}.

Then we have

(21)

\mathrm{Gr}=\coprod_{E\in{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0},\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{E}=\coprod_{E^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{E}}:\atop E\subseteq E^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{E^{\prime}})^{0}.

By definition,
(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}
is always non-empty.

We shall call any member
E\in{\mathscr{E}}
a relative
k_{0}
-subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
(of type
(r,n~-~r)
, if necessary).
When the set
{\mathscr{E}}
is finite, which is the case exactly when
k_{0}
is finite by Lemma
2.7
.(1),
(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}
is a locally closed subset and can be viewed as a reduced subscheme over
k_{0}
. However, when
k_{0}
is an infinite field, the set
(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}
is not even constructible, as it is the (non-empty) complement of an infinite union of proper closed subsets in the closed subvariety
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
.
Nevertheless, it still makes sense to ask whether
(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}
is Zariski-dense in
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
and whether
the strata
\{(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}\}_{E\in{\mathscr{E}}}
satisfy the stratification property: that is, whether the closure of each stratum is a union of some strata.
We expect that both questions have affirmative answers.

The group
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
acts on
\mathrm{Gr}
on the left and permutes the strata
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
. Namely, for each element
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
the action gives an isomorphism

\gamma:\mathrm{Gr}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\gamma E\gamma^{-1}},\quad W\mapsto\gamma W.

It is helpful to introduce another intermediate stratification which takes the action of
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0})
into consideration.
Denote by
[{\mathscr{E}}]
the set of conjugacy classes of all
k_{0}
-subalgebras
E
in
{\mathscr{E}}
and by
[E]\subseteq{\mathscr{E}}
the conjugacy class of
E
in
{\mathscr{E}}
. For each
[E]
in
[{\mathscr{E}}]
, set

(22)

\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}:=\bigcup_{E\in[E]}\mathrm{Gr}_{E},\quad(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}:=\bigcup_{E\in[E]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}.

Then we have a stratification

(23)

\mathrm{Gr}=\bigcup_{[E]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]}\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]},\quad\mathrm{Gr}=\coprod_{[E]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}

by the strata
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}
or the strata
(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}
.
When
k_{0}
is finite, each
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}
(resp.
(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}
) is a closed (resp. locally closed) reduced subscheme of finite type over
k_{0}
, and we also expect that the strata
\{(\mathrm{Gr}_{[}E])^{0}\}_{[E]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]}
satisfy the stratification property.

Example 3.2
.

Let
k_{0}={{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
,
(n,r)=(2,1)
, in which case
\mathrm{Gr}\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is defined over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. Let
W_{t}=\langle t_{1}e_{1}+t_{2}e_{2}\rangle\subseteq V_{0,k}
denote the subspace corresponding to the parameter
t=[t_{1}:t_{2}]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k)
. Let
B_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})\subseteq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
be the Borel subalgebra consisting of upper-triangular matrices, which stabilises the subspace
W_{[1:0]}
. One has
\mathrm{Gr}_{B_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})}=\{[1:0]\}
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{[B_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})]}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
, since we know that all Borel subalgebras are
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
-conjugate. We fix an embedding
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
of the quadratic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. For
t\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k)\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})=k-{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, we have

(24)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W_{t})=\begin{cases}{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}+{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}&\text{if $t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}$};\\
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}&\text{if $t\in k\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}$},\end{cases}

where
X^{2}-\alpha X-\beta
is the minimal polynomial of
t
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
; see
[
33
, Section 3.2]
. Thus, we have
(\mathrm{Gr}_{{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}})^{0}=(\mathrm{Gr}_{[{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}]})^{0}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k)\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
,
(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}=\{t,t^{q}\}
, if
E=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W_{t})
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, and
(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In particular, we have
[{\mathscr{E}}]=\{[B_{2}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})],[\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}],[{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}]\}
and

(25)

\mathrm{Gr}=\coprod_{[E]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\coprod\left({\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\right)\coprod({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}).

This shows that the collection of strata
\{(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]})^{0}\}_{[E]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]}
(resp.
\{(\mathrm{Gr}_{E})^{0}\}_{E\in{\mathscr{E}}}
) satisfies the stratification property in this case.

Remark 3.3
.

If
k_{0}
is infinite, then
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}
is a union of possibly infinitely many closed subvarieties
\mathrm{Gr}_{E}
of
\mathrm{Gr}
, so in many situations
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}
is not a subscheme of finite type or even a constructible subset.
We have already seen (in Lemma
2.7
.(1)) that when
E_{0}
is a maximal parabolic
k_{0}
-algebra of type
(r,n-r)
, we have
\mathrm{Gr}_{E_{0}}=\{\mathrm{pt}\}
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E_{0}]}=\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)(k_{0})
.
Thus, an expected minimal stratum
\mathrm{Gr}_{[E_{0}]}=\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)(k_{0})
is Zariski dense in
\mathrm{Gr}
, as
\mathrm{Gr}
is a rational variety. This shows that taking the Zariski closure as our inclusion (or closure) relation is not a good notion to define the partial order on these strata.

For any two members
[E],[E^{\prime}]\in[{\mathscr{E}}]
, we define a partial ordering

[E]\subseteq[E^{\prime}]

if there exist elements
E_{1}\in[E]
and
E_{2}\in[E^{\prime}]
satisfying
E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}
. It follows that for any
E_{1}\in[E]
(resp.
E_{2}\in[E^{\prime}]
) there is
E_{2}\in[E^{\prime}]

(resp.
E_{1}\in[E]
) such that
E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}
.
Clearly, we have

[E]\subseteq[E^{\prime}]\iff\mathrm{Gr}_{[E^{\prime}]}\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]},

\mathrm{Gr}_{[E]}=\coprod_{[E]\subseteq[E^{\prime}]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{[E^{\prime}]})^{0}.

Using this partial ordering, we have the notion of (non-empty) maximal strata and minimal strata.
One might expect that every minimal stratum is zero-dimensional. This is true when
k_{0}
is finite. However, when
k_{0}=k
, we see that
\mathrm{Gr}
itself is a stratum, which is of dimension
r(n-r)
.
Clearly, the stratum
(\mathrm{Gr}_{[k_{0}]})^{0}
is the unique maximal stratum if it is non-empty.

We deduce a criterion for the existence of
W\in\mathrm{Gr}
whose endomorphism algebra equals
k_{0}
, that is, a criterion for deciding whether the stratum
(\mathrm{Gr}_{[k_{0}]})^{0}
is non-empty. Recall that
r=\dim(W)
. Let
k_{0}[\underline{X}]
be the polynomial ring over
k_{0}
with variables
X_{ij}
for
1\leq i\leq n-r
and
1\leq j\leq r
and let
k_{0}[\underline{X}]_{\leq d}
be the
k_{0}
-vector subspace of polynomials of total degree
\leq d
. Set

N_{d}:=\dim_{k_{0}}k_{0}[\underline{X}]_{\leq d}.

For any
T=(t_{ij})\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r\times r}(k)
, let
{\rm ev}_{T}:k_{0}[\underline{X}]\to k
be the evaluation map at
T
.
Set

(26)

{\mathbb{V}}(T):={\rm ev}_{T}(k_{0}[\underline{X}]_{\leq 2}),\quad d(T):=\dim_{k_{0}}{\mathbb{V}}(T).

For each
1\leq i\leq n-r
and
1\leq j\leq r
, let
E_{ij}
be the matrix with
(i,j)
-entry
1
and other entries
0
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-r\times r}(k)
and
W_{T}=\big\langle\begin{bmatrix}T\\
{\mathbb{I}}_{r}\end{bmatrix}\big\rangle
be the corresponding
k
-subspace in
\mathrm{Gr}
.
If
d(T)=N_{2}
, then
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W_{T})=k_{0}
.

Proof.

We write
T=\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}t_{ij}
. Using Equation (
14
), we get

(27)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq n-r\\
1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq r\end{subarray}}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}CE_{i_{2}j_{2}})t_{i_{1}j_{1}}t_{i_{2}j_{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n-r\\
1\leq j\leq r\end{subarray}}(E_{ij}D-AE_{ij})t_{ij}-B=0.

Since
N_{2}=d(T)
, the vectors
1,t_{ij},t_{i_{1}j_{1}}t_{i_{2}j_{2}}
for all
i,j,i_{1},j_{1},i_{2},j_{2}
are
k_{0}
-linearly independent. So

E_{i_{1}j_{1}}CE_{i_{2}j_{2}}+E_{i_{2}j_{2}}CE_{i_{1}j_{1}}=0\ (\text{for }\ (i_{1},j_{1})\neq(i_{2},j_{2})),\quad E_{ij}D-AE_{ij}=0,\quad B=0.

One has
E_{i_{1}j_{1}}CE_{i_{2}j_{2}}+E_{i_{2}j_{2}}CE_{i_{1}j_{1}}=c_{j_{1}i_{2}}E_{i_{1}j_{2}}+c_{j_{2}i_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{1}}=0
. So
c_{j_{1}i_{2}}=0
for
1\leq j_{1}\leq r
,
1\leq i_{2}\leq n-r
and
C=0
. Also from

E_{ij}D=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq r\end{subarray}}E_{ij}d_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{1},j_{1}}=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq r}d_{j,j_{1}}E_{i,j_{1}}=d_{jj}E_{ij}+\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq r\atop j_{1}\neq j}d_{j,j_{1}}E_{i,j_{1}}

AE_{ij}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq n-r}a_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{ij}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq n-r}a_{i_{1}i}E_{i_{1}j}=a_{ii}E_{ij}+\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq n-r\atop i_{1}\neq i}a_{i_{1}i}E_{i_{1}j},

one concludes that
a_{ij}=d_{ij}=0
for
i\neq j
and
d_{jj}=a_{ii}
for
1\leq i\leq n-r
,
1\leq j\leq r
.
It follows that
\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=a{\mathbb{I}}_{n}
for some
a\in k_{0}
. This proves the proposition.
∎

.

Let
\{t_{1}^{\prime}=1,t_{2}^{\prime},\dots,t_{s}^{\prime}\}\subseteq\{1,t_{ij},t_{i_{1}j_{1}}t_{i_{2}j_{2}}\text{ such that }1\leq i,i_{1},i_{2}\leq n~-~r\text{ and }1\leq j,j_{1},j_{2}\leq r\}

Then

consists of all matrices

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq n-r\\

Symplectic groups

Let
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
be a non-degenerate symplectic

-space of dimension

{\rm L}(V_{0})={\rm L}(V_{0},\psi_{0})\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}(V_{0},r)

denote the Lagrangian variety associated to
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
, which parametrises maximal isotropic subspaces of
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
. It is a smooth irreducible projective scheme over
k_{0}
of dimension
r(r+1)/2
. We set
{\mathrm{L}}:={\rm L}(V_{0})\otimes_{k_{0}}k
, and identify
{\rm L}
with the set of
k
-rational points of
{\rm L}
.
Let

\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}):=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(V_{0}):\psi_{0}(\alpha x,\alpha y)=\psi_{0}(x,y)\ \forall\,x,y\in V_{0}\}

denote the symplectic group associated to
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
, viewed as an abstract group. For any
k
-subspace
W\in\mathrm{L}
, one defines the endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
of
W
as before, and the automorphism group of
W
with respect to
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
is defined as

(30)

\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0},W):=\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0})\cap\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)^{\times}.

Choose a symplectic basis

,

of
(V_{0},\psi_{0})
and view
V_{0}=\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{2r\times 1}(k_{0})

5.

5.1.
Endomorphism algebras of complex tori

Let
X=V/\Lambda
be a complex torus, where
V
is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of dimension
n
and
\Lambda
is a lattice in
V
. We regard
V
as a real vector space together with complex structure
J\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(V)
, so
J^{2}=-{\mathbb{I}}_{2n}
. We have a decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=V^{-1,0}\oplus V^{0,-1}
into eigenspaces on which
J
acts by the multiplication by
i
on
V^{-1,0}
(resp.
-i
on
V^{0,-1}
). Put
V_{0}:=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}
so we have
V=V_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. The endomorphism algebra of
X
is

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0}):\alpha\cdot J=J\cdot\alpha\text{ on $V$}\ \}.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},V^{-1,0}).

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)
. Since
\alpha
commutes with
J
, for any
v\in V^{-1,0}
, we have

J\alpha(v)=\alpha(Jv)=i\alpha(v),\text{\ and then\ }\alpha(v)\in V^{-1,0}.

This shows
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},V^{-1,0})
. Conversely, suppose
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},V^{-1,0})
. Extending to
\mathbb{C}
, we have
\alpha\cdot J-J\cdot\alpha=0
on
V^{-1,0}
. Taking the complex conjugation
c
we obtain

c(\alpha\cdot J-J\cdot\alpha)=0
on
V^{0,-1}
.
Since
\alpha,J\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V)
and
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{\mathbb{C}})=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}
, the maps
\alpha
and
J
are invariant under
c
. Thus, we have
\alpha\cdot J-J\cdot\alpha=0
on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
and therefore
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)
.
∎

.

Since
\overline{W}+c(\overline{W})=V_{1,\mathbb{C}}
and
\overline{W}\cap c(\overline{W})=0
, we have
V_{1,\mathbb{C}}=\overline{W}\oplus c(\overline{W})
. Define a complex structure
J
on
V_{1}
by letting
J
act by multiplication by
i
on
\overline{W}
(resp.
-i
on
c(\overline{W})
). By the argument of Lemma
5.1
, we have

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{1},\overline{W})=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(V_{1},J)\simeq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{r-r_{0}}(\mathbb{C}).

This proves the proposition.
∎

5.3.
Endomorphism rings of supersingular abelian varieties

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, where
p
is a prime number. Let
X
be a supersingular abelian variety over
k
of dimension
n>1
. Then the endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(X)
of
X
is isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(B_{p,\infty})
, where
B_{p,\infty}
denotes the unique definite quaternion
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of discriminant
p
. Choose a maximal order
O\subseteq B_{p,\infty}
that contains an element
\Pi
satisfying
\Pi^{2}=-p
. Since the algebra
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(B_{p,\infty})
has class number one, every maximal order is conjugate to the maximal order
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)
.

Let
\varphi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the minimal isogeny of
X
in the sense of
[
23
, Lemma 1.8]
, i.e.
\widetilde{X}
is a superspecial abelian variety over
k
such that any other isogeny from a superspecial abelian variety to
X
factors through
\varphi
. Then
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})
and
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})
is a maximal order of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(\widetilde{X})\simeq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(B_{p,\infty})
. For simplicity, we choose an isomorphism
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})\simeq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)
and make identifications so that
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\subseteq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})
. For any prime
\ell\neq p
, we have
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}=\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O_{\ell})
, where
O_{\ell}:=O\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.

Let
M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
be the (contravariant) Dieudonné modules of
X
and
\widetilde{X}
, respectively. We denote by
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{V}
the Frobenius and Verschiebung operators on
\widetilde{M}
. One has

(47)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}=\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O_{p})\simeq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M})^{\rm opp},\quad\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}\simeq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)^{\rm opp},

where
O_{p}:=O\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
and the superscript
{\rm opp}
denotes the opposite ring (with multiplication
a\circ b:=b\cdot a
). The ring
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)
carries the canonical involution
A\mapsto A^{*}=(\overline{A})^{T}
and this gives an isomorphism
\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)\simeq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O)^{\rm opp}
(noting that
A_{1}\cdot A_{2}\mapsto(A_{1}\cdot A_{2})^{*}=A_{2}^{*}\cdot A_{1}^{*}=A_{1}^{*}\circ A_{2}^{*}
). Composing with this canonical involution, one has

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}=\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(O_{p})\simeq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M}),\quad\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}\simeq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M).

Thus, to find
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)
one is reduced to determining the endomorphism ring
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)
of
M
.

We consider the special case where
\mathsf{V}\widetilde{M}\subseteq M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
, or equivalently,
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\varphi\subseteq\widetilde{X}[F]
, where
F:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}^{(p)}
is the relative Frobenius morphism on
\widetilde{X}
. Put
W:=M/\mathsf{V}\widetilde{M}
, and

V_{0}:=\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}/\mathsf{V}\widetilde{M}^{\diamond},\quad\text{ where }\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}:=\{m\in\widetilde{M}:\mathsf{F}^{2}m=-pm\}.

Then
V_{0}
is an
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and
W\subseteq V_{0,k}
is a
k
-vector subspace.
Recall

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W):=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0}):\alpha(W)\subseteq W\}.

Let
m_{\mathsf{V}}:\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M})\to\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0})
be the natural projection map. We have

(48)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M}):m_{\mathsf{V}}(\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\}.

That is,
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)
is the pre-image of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
under the map
m_{\mathsf{V}}
. From this, one reduces the problem of finding
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)
to computing the relative endomorphism algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)
. We summarise this as follows:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\varphi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the minimal isogeny of a supersingular abelian variety
X
with respective Dieudonné modules
M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
. With the inclusions
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M})
and
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)\subseteq\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M})
, we have

(49)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X})\cap\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)\quad\text{and}\quad[\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X}):\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)]=[\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M}):\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M)].

Moreover, if
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\varphi\subseteq\widetilde{X}[F]
, then

(50)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X}):m_{\mathsf{V}}(\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)\}.

In particular,
[\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(\widetilde{X}):\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)]=[\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0}):\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(V_{0},W)]
.

5.4.
Automorphism groups and masses of polarised supersingular abelian varieties

Let
(X,\lambda)
be a principally polarised supersingular abelian variety over
k
, and let
\varphi:(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})\to(X,\lambda)
be the minimal isogeny, where
\widetilde{\lambda}=\varphi^{*}\lambda
. Note that
\widetilde{\lambda}
has
p
-power degree.
Let
\underline{M}=(M\,\langle\,,\rangle)\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{M}}=(\widetilde{M},\langle\,,\rangle)
be the corresponding Dieudonné modules with quasi-polarisation. Then

\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{\widetilde{M}}):\alpha(M)=M\}

and

\begin{split}\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)&=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda}):\alpha(M)=M\}\\
&=\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})\cap\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M}).\end{split}

This shows that in general the computation of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)
can be reduced to computing the groups
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})
and
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})
. Unfortunately, as far as we know, computing either one is complicated. A small trick is observing that
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)
is a finite subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})
. So we can bound the size of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)
by the sizes of maximal finite subgroups of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})
. In particular, if the torsion subset
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})_{\rm tors}
is equal to
\{\pm 1\}
, then
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X,\lambda)=\{\pm 1\}
.

Concerning the computation of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})
, one can change the problem to its arithmetic counterpart. Choose a supersingular elliptic curve
E
over
k
such that
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)=O
. The functor
\widetilde{X}\mapsto\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(E,\widetilde{X})
induces an equivalence of categories between the category of polarised superspecial abelian varieties over
k
and the category of positive-definite Hermitian right
O
-lattices; see
[
16
, Corollary 4.9]
. Therefore, if
(L,h)
is the Hermitian
O
-lattice corresponding to
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})
under this equivalence, then we have

\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})\simeq\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(L,h).

Kirschmer
[
20
, Chapter 9]
gives a complete (finite) list of the automorphism groups
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(L,h)

for all positive-definite Hermitian right
O
-lattices

(L,h)
with class number one or two.

Let
x=(X,\lambda)
and denote by

\Lambda_{x}
the set of isomorphism classes of polarised supersingular abelian varieties
(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
over
k
such that
(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq(X,\lambda)[\ell^{\infty}]
for all primes
\ell
.
This is a finite set and the mass of
\Lambda_{x}
is defined by

\Mass(\Lambda_{x}):=\sum_{(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\in\Lambda_{x}}\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})|}.

For the minimal isogeny
\widetilde{x}=(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})
we define the finite set
\Lambda_{\widetilde{x}}
and the mass
\Mass(\Lambda_{\widetilde{x}})
in the same way. Then we have, cf.
[
18
, Proposition 2.12]
,

(51)

\Mass(\Lambda_{x})=\Mass(\Lambda_{\widetilde{x}})\cdot[\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{\widetilde{M}}):\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})].

In what follows, we assume that

(52)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{\lambda}=\alpha_{p}^{2c}\subseteq\widetilde{X}[F],\quad\text{for some $c\geq 0$.}

We have the inclusion
M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
. Also
M
is self-dual with respect to the quasi-polarisation
\langle\,,\rangle
, since
\lambda
is a principal polarisation on
X
. So we have
\widetilde{M}^{t}\subseteq M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
, where
{\widetilde{M}}^{t}
is the dual lattice of
\widetilde{M}
with respect to
\langle\,,\rangle
. Let
\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}
be the skeleton of
\widetilde{M}
defined by
\mathsf{F}^{2}+p=0
.
Set

(53)

V_{0}^{\rm min}:=\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}/{\widetilde{M}}^{t,\diamond},

which is a
2c
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Moreover, the pairing
p\langle\,,\rangle:\widetilde{M}\times\widetilde{M}\to W(k)
induces a non-degenerate alternating pairing

(54)

\psi^{\rm min}_{0}:V_{0}^{\rm min}\times V_{0}^{\rm min}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}.

Inside
V_{0,k}^{\rm min}=\widetilde{M}/{\widetilde{M}}^{t}
there is a
c
-dimensional subspace

\overline{M}:=M/{\widetilde{M}}^{t}\subseteq V_{0,k}^{\rm min}

which is isotropic with respect to the symplectic pairing
\psi_{0}^{\rm min}
. We have
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M},\langle\,,\rangle)=\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M},p\langle\,,\rangle)=\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M}^{\diamond},p\langle\,,\rangle)
and a surjective map
\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}\to V_{0}^{\rm min}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The surjection
\widetilde{M}^{\diamond}\to V_{0}^{\rm min}
induces a surjection

m:\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}({\widetilde{M}},p\langle\,,\rangle)\to\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min}).

Proof.

Let
J
be the algebraic group over
{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
of the automorphism group of the quasi-polarised rational Dieudonné module
(\widetilde{M}\otimes W[1/p],\langle\,,\rangle)

[
28
, Proposition 1.12]
. The group
J
is an inner form of
\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits_{2g}
over
{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}

[
28
, Remark 1.15]
, and hence it is semi-simple and simply connected.
Observe that
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\widetilde{M},\langle\,,\rangle)
is a parahoric subgroup of
J({{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p})=\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\widetilde{M}\otimes W[1/p],\langle\,,\rangle)
, since
\langle\,,\rangle
is a polarisation of parahoric type by assumption and since
J
is simply connected; see the proof of Lemma 5.2 of
[
16
]
and
[
27
, Theorem 3.13]
.
By Bruhat-Tits theory
[
3
]
, there exists a connected smooth group scheme
{\mathscr{J}}
over
{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
with generic fibre
J
such that
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{\widetilde{M}})={\mathscr{J}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})
. Then the map
m
is given by the composition

m:{\mathscr{J}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})\to{\mathscr{J}}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})\to({\mathscr{J}}\otimes{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})^{\rm rdt}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}),

where
({\mathscr{J}}\otimes{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})^{\rm rdt}
is the maximal reductive quotient of
{\mathscr{J}}\otimes{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
. The surjectivity of the first map follows from the smoothness of
{\mathscr{J}}
over
{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
. To show the surjecivity of the second map, it suffices to show
that the kernel
U
of
{\mathscr{J}}\otimes{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}\to({\mathscr{J}}\otimes{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})^{\rm rdt}
satifies
H^{1}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p},U)=0
.
Since
U
is unipotent, it is a successive extension of additive groups
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
, and the desired vanishing follows from
H^{1}({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p},{\mathbb{G}}_{a})=0
and induction on the dimension of
U
.
∎

Recall from Equation (
30
) that

\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min},\overline{M}):=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min}):\alpha(\overline{M})=\overline{M}\}.

On the other hand, we have

(55)

\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})=\{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{\widetilde{M}}):m(\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min},\overline{M})\}.

Since the map
m
is surjective, one has

(56)

[\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{\widetilde{M}}):\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(\underline{M})]=[\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min}):\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min},\overline{M})].

Let
\Lambda_{g,p^{c}}
be the set of isomorphism classes of polarised superspecial abelian varieties
(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
of dimension
g
with
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\lambda^{\prime}\simeq\alpha_{p}^{2c}
.
By the assumption Equation (
52
) one identifies
\Lambda_{\widetilde{x}}
with
\Lambda_{g,p^{c}}
, and we have

(57)

\Mass(\Lambda_{\widetilde{x}})=\Mass(\Lambda_{g,p^{c}}).

By
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have the mass formula

\Mass(\Lambda_{g,p^{c}})=\prod_{i=1}^{g}\frac{|\zeta(1-2i)|}{2}\cdot L_{p,p^{c}},

where

(58)

L_{g,p^{c}}=\prod_{i=1}^{g-2c}(p^{i}+(-1)^{i})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{c}(p^{4i-2}-1)\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{g}(p^{2i}-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{2c}(p^{2i}-1)\prod_{i=1}^{g-2c}(p^{2i}-1)}.

By Equations (
51
), (
56
) and (
57
)
we obtain the following result.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\varphi:(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\lambda})\to(X,\lambda)
be the minimal isogeny of a principally polarised supersingular abelian variety
x=(X,\lambda)
with respective quasi-polarised Dieudonné modules
(M,\langle\,,\rangle)\subseteq(\widetilde{M},\langle\,,\rangle)
. Assume that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{\lambda}=\alpha_{p}^{2c}\subseteq\widetilde{X}[F]
for some
c\geq 0
. Let
(V_{0}^{\rm min},\psi_{0}^{\rm min})
be the
2c
-dimensional symplectic space over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
defined in Equations (
53
) and (
54
), and let
\overline{M}:=M/\widetilde{M}^{t}\subseteq V_{0,k}^{\rm min}
be the image of
M
. Then

(59)

\Mass(\Lambda_{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{g}\frac{|\zeta(1-2i)|}{2}\cdot[\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min}):\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits(V_{0}^{\rm min},\overline{M})]\cdot L_{p,p^{c}}

where
L_{g,p^{c}}
is the local factor defined in Equation (
58
).

Following

\varphi(0)=0,\quad\varphi(i)\leq\varphi(i+1)\leq\varphi(i)+1,\ \forall\,0\leq i\leq g-1.

Proposition 6.4

We have isomorphisms of moduli functors

The first isomorphism sends each polarised isogeny

Remark 6.5

The moduli space

was first constructed by Moret-Bailly

Corollary 6.7
.

We have a finite surjective morphism

Proof.

∎

Corollary 7.7
.

We have
m_{\mathsf{V}^{3}}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{DM}}(M_{0},\langle,\rangle_{0}))=\{\pm 1\}

∎

Conditionally strong solution for macroscopic polymeric SSS interaction
Prince Romeo Mensah

Abstract
. The system under study is a solute-solvent-structure (SSS) interaction problem for the interaction of a dilute three-dimensional Oldroyd-B polymeric fluid with a two-dimensional viscoelastic shell. We show that a unique global strong solution to this system exists under the condition that the classical Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin criterion holds for the velocity field and that the shell displacement is essentially bounded in time with values in the space of continuously differentiable functions. No requirement is needed for the polymer number density and the extra stress tensor for the solute component.

1.
Introduction

We consider the Oldroyd-B
[
13
]
model for the flow of a dilute polymeric fluid interacting with a flexible structure in the closure of the deformed spacetime cylinder

\displaystyle I\times\Omega_{\eta}:=\bigcup_{t\in I}\{t\}\times\Omega_{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}.

Here,
I:=(0,T)
is a fixed time horizon and
\Omega_{\eta}:={\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
is a time-dependent flexible spatial domain whose closure is obtained through the parametrization of the boundary
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of some reference configuration
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Further details will be given in Section
2
.
Our goal is to find a structure displacement function
\eta:(t,\mathbf{y})\in I\times\omega\mapsto\eta(t,\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{R}
, a fluid velocity field
\mathbf{u}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a pressure function
p:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto p(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
, a polymer number density
\rho:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\rho(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}

and an extra stress tensor
\mathbb{T}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\mathbb{T}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

such that the system of equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\rho=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{u}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\eta-\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta+\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta=g-(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{n}_{\eta})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}=(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}-2(\mathbb{T}-\rho\mathbb{I})+\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}

holds on
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
(with (
1.4
) defined on
I\times\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
). Here,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}+(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}-p\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{T},

is a tensor field, the vector
\mathbf{n}_{\eta}
is the normal at
\partial\Omega_{\eta}
,
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix and where all underlining dimensional parameters have been set to one for simplicity.
We complement (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) with the following initial and boundary conditions

(1.6)

\displaystyle\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot),\qquad\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\omega,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}(\cdot),\quad\mathbb{T}(0,\cdot)=\mathbb{T}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho=0,\qquad\mathbf{n}_{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega_{\eta}.

Furthermore, we impose periodicity on the boundary of
\omega
and the following condition

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\omega

at the interface between the polymeric fluid and the structure with normal vector
\mathbf{n}
.

The two unknowns
\rho
and
\mathbb{T}
for the solute component of the polymer fluid are related via the identities

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\mathbf{q}\otimes\mathbf{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q},\qquad\rho(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}

where for
B=\mathbb{R}^{3}
with elements
\mathbf{q}\in B
, the function
f
is the probability density function (
f\geq 0
a.e. on
\overline{I}\times\Omega_{\eta}\times B
) satisfying the
Fokker–Planck equation

(1.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{u}f)+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{q}}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbf{q}f)=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{q}}(M\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}(f/M))

in
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\times B
for a Hookean dumbbell spring potential and Maxwellian

\displaystyle U\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2},\qquad\qquad M=\frac{\exp(-U(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}))}{\int_{B}\exp(-U(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}},

respectively.

Very recently, the analysis of polymeric fluid-structure interaction problems was started in
[
4
]
. Whereas Newtonian fluid-structure interaction problems involve a solvent and a structure, a polymeric fluid-structure interaction problem involves the mutual interactions between a solute, a solvent, and a structure. Analyzing such a system, therefore, requires techniques from two hitherto separate fields of analysis in continuum mechanics, i.e. the analysis of polymeric fluids and fluid-structure interactions.

In
[
4
]
, the authors constructed distributional solutions for the coupling between (1) the
3
D Fokker–Planck equation (
1.12
) giving the mesoscopic description of the probability distribution of the solute or polymer, (2) the
3
D incompressible Navier–Stokes equation (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) giving the macroscopic description of the solvent evolution, and (3) a
2
D structure modeled by a shell equation (
1.4
) of Koiter type (where the term
\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta-\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta
is replaced by the gradient of the so-called
Koiter energy
). The uniqueness of these distributional solutions is unknown but not expected. However, the solutions exist until potential degeneracies occur with the Koiter energy or with the structure deformation.
When the
2
D Koiter shell in
[
4
]
is replaced by the
2
D viscoelastic shell equation (
1.4
), the extension to the existence of a unique local-in-time strong solution was then shown in
[
7
]
.
Note that for fixed spatial domains subject to periodic boundary conditions, one can construct solutions that are spatially more regular
[
5
]
than strong solutions. The corresponding result for the system with a structure displacement remains an interesting open problem even in lower dimensions.

The macroscopic closure of the Fokker–Planck equation (
1.12
) gives rise to (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) by using the identities (
1.11
). For the fully macroscopic solute-solvent-structure Oldroyd-B system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
), distributional solutions and solutions that are satisfied pointwise a.e. in spacetime have been constructed in
[
11
]
when the solute-solvent subsystem are posed in
2
D and the structure subsystem is posed in
1
D. Here, provided no degeneracies occur with the structure deformation, both classes of solutions exist globally in time. In
[
12
]
, the same
2
D/
1
D setting but for corotational fluids where
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}
in (
1.5
) is replaced by its anti-symmetric part
\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top})
is considered. It is shown that any family of strong solutions parametrized
by the center-of-mass diffusion coefficient converges to a weak solution of the same system without center-of-mass diffusion (i.e.
\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho=0
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}=0
) but with essentially bounded polymer number density and extra stress.

2.
Preliminaries and main results

For any two non-negative quantities
F
and
G
, we write
F\lesssim G
if there is a constant
c>0
such that
F\leq c\,G
. If
F\lesssim G
and
G\lesssim F
both hold, we use the notation
F\sim G
. The scaler matrix product of the matrices
\mathbb{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}
and
\mathbb{B}=(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathbb{A}:\mathbb{B}=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}b_{ji}
.
The symbol
|\cdot|
may be used in four different contexts. For a scalar function
f\in\mathbb{R}
,
|f|
denotes the absolute value of
f
. For a vector
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|\mathbf{f}|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
\mathbf{f}
. For a square matrix
\mathbb{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
|\mathbb{F}|
shall denote the Frobenius norm
\sqrt{\mathrm{trace}(\mathbb{F}^{T}\mathbb{F})}
. Also, if
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a (sub)set, then
|S|
is the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
S
.

For
I:=(0,T)
,
T>0
, and
\eta\in C(\overline{I}\times\omega)
satisfying
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}\leq L
where
L>0
is a constant, we define for
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v\in L^{1}(I\times\Omega_{\eta}):\begin{subarray}{c}v(t,\cdot)\in L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})\,\,\text{for a.e. }t,\\
\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})}\in L^{p}(I)\end{subarray}\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;W^{1,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\big\{v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta})):\,\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))\big\}.

Higher-order Sobolev spaces can be defined accordingly. For
k>0
with
k\notin\mathbb{N}
, we define the fractional Sobolev space
L^{p}(I;W^{k,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))
as the class of
L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))
-functions
v
for which

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(I;W^{k,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|v|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\frac{|v(\mathbf{x})-v(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|^{r}}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|^{d+kr}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}t

is finite. Accordingly, we can also introduce fractional differentiability in time for the spaces on moving domains.

2.1.
Setup

The reference spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
has a boundary
\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
that may consist of a flexible part
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a rigid part
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. However, because the analysis at the rigid part is significantly simpler, we shall identify the whole of
\partial\Omega
with
\omega
and endow it with periodic boundary conditions. Let
I:=(0,T)
represent a time interval for a given constant
T>0
. The time-dependent displacement of the structure is given by
\eta:\overline{I}\times\omega\rightarrow(-L,L)
where
L>0
is a fixed length of the tubular neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
given by

\displaystyle S_{L}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L\}.

For some
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we now assume that
\partial\Omega
is parametrized by an injective mapping
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{k}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}=\big\{\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}:=\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})\eta(t,\mathbf{y})\,:\,t\in I,\mathbf{y}\in\omega\big\}.

The set
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
represents the boundary of the flexible domain at any instant of time
t\in I
and the vector
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})
is a unit normal at the point
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
.
We also let
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})
be the corresponding normal of
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
at the spacetime point
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Then for
L>0
sufficiently small, we note that
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})
is close to
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})
and
\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}
is close to
\bm{\varphi}
. As a result, it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{1}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}\times\partial_{y_{2}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}\neq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})\neq 0

for
\mathbf{y}\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Thus, in particular, there is no loss of strict positivity of the Jacobian determinant provided that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L
.

For the interior points, we transform the reference domain
\Omega
into a time-dependent moving domain
\Omega_{\eta(t)}
whose state at time
t\in\overline{I}
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta(t)}=\big\{\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x}):\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\big\}.

Here,

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\eta(t,\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere }\end{cases}

is the Hanzawa transform with inverse
\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta(t)}
and where for a point
\mathbf{x}
in the neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
, the vector
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))
is the unit normal at the point
\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathbf{y}\in\omega}|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y})|
. Also,
s(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})))\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a cut-off function that is
\phi\equiv 0
in the neighbourhood of
-L
and
\phi\equiv 1
in the neighbourhood of
0
. Note that
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\bm{\varphi}(y(\mathbf{x}))+\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))[s(\mathbf{x})+\eta(t,\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))]&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere. }\end{cases}

The transform
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}
and its inverse

\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}=\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta}
satisfy the following properties, see
[
3
,
6
]
for details. If for some
\ell,R>0
, we assume that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}+\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<\ell<L\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<R

holds, then for any
s>0
,
\varrho,p\in[1,\infty]
and for any
\eta,\zeta\in B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\omega)
(where
B^{s}_{\varrho,p}
is a Besov space), we have that the estimates

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim 1+\|\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim\|\eta-\zeta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},\qquad\eta\in W^{1,1}(I;B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega))

holds uniformly in time with the hidden constants depending only on the reference geometry, on
L-\ell
and
R
.

2.2.
Concepts of solutions and main results

We make clear in this section, the various notions of a solution we discuss in this paper and state the main result. Let us begin with a weak solution for which the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5

Definition 2.1

(Weak solution)
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},g,\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})

(2.4)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\qquad g\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in W^{2,2}(\omega)\text{ with }\|\eta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<L,\quad\eta_{\star}\in L^{2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),\quad\mathbb{T}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{T}_{0}>0\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}.

We call

(\eta,\mathbf{u},\rho,\mathbb{T})

a

of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) with data
(\mathbf{f},g,\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})
if:

(a)

the following properties

\displaystyle\eta\in W^{1,\infty}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)\big),

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L,

\displaystyle\rho\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{T}>0\quad\text{a.e. in }I\times\Omega_{\eta}

holds;

(b)

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\rho\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}[\rho\partial_{t}\psi+(\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\psi]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t;

(c)

for all

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\mathbb{T}:\mathbb{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}[\mathbb{T}:\partial_{t}\mathbb{Y}+\mathbb{T}:(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{Y}]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}[(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}]:\mathbb{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-2\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}(\mathbb{T}:\mathbb{Y}-\rho\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{Y}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}::\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

where
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}::\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathbb{T}:\partial_{x_{i}}\mathbb{Y}
;

(d)

for all
(\bm{\phi},\phi)\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\overline{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes C^{\infty}(\overline{I}\times\omega)
with
\bm{\phi}(T,\cdot)=0
,
\phi(T,\cdot)=0
and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\omega}\partial_{t}\eta\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\big[\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}:\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{f}\cdot\bm{\phi}+\mathbb{T}:\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\phi}\big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{T}(t))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{w}}(\mathrm{data})

holds.

(f)

In addition, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}

\displaystyle\big(\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim e^{c\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{w}}(\mathrm{data})}\big((1+T)\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}\big).

for the mixed Oldroyd-B and the Giesekus models. The main obstacle in the construction of a weak solution in this 3D case is the lack of a useful estimate for the extra-stress tensor
\mathbb{T}
. Indeed, from the basic energy estimate, the only information one derives is that
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{T})\in L^{\infty}(I;L^{1}(\Omega_{\eta}))
and nothing about
\mathbb{T}
itself. The latter issue primarily stems from unsuitable interpolation estimates in 3D.

For flexible domains, the existence of a weak solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
), in the sense of Definition
2.1
, has been shown in
[
11
]
for the 2D/1D polymeric fluid-structure system. The paper
[
11
]
also includes the construction of a unique global strong solutions
for the 2D/1D version of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) in analogy with
[
8
]
for fixed domains.

Returning to the 3D/2D setting, we now give the precise definition of a strong solution.

Definition 2.2

(Strong solution)
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},g,\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})

be a dataset that satisfies

(2.10)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\qquad g\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\omega)\text{ with }\|\eta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<L,\quad\eta_{\star}\in W^{1,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})\text{ is such that }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{\star}\mathbf{n}\text{ on }\omega,

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),\quad\mathbb{T}_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}}),

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{T}_{0}>0\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}.

We call

(\eta,\mathbf{u},p,\rho,\mathbb{T})

a
strong solution
of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) with dataset
(\mathbf{f},g,\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})
if:

(a)

the structure-function
\eta
is such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L
and

\displaystyle\eta\in W^{1,\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{3,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{2,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{4,2}(\omega)\big);

(b)

the velocity
\mathbf{u}
is such that
\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}
on
I\times\omega
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

(c)

the pressure
p
is such that

\displaystyle p\in L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

(d)

the pair
(\rho,\mathbb{T})
is such that

\displaystyle\rho,\mathbb{T}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

(e)

equations (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) are satisfied a.e. in spacetime with
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
and
\partial_{t}\eta(0)=\eta_{\star}
a.e. in
\omega
, as well as
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
,
\rho(0)=\rho_{0}
and
\mathbb{T}(0)=\mathbb{T}_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega_{\eta_{0}}
.

With this definition in hand, we now state our main result.

Theorem 2.3
.

For a global weak solution
(\eta,\mathbf{u},\rho,\mathbb{T})
of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) with dataset
(\mathbf{f},g,\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})
satisfying (
2.10
), if

(2.11)

\displaystyle\eta\in L^{\infty}(I;C^{1}(\omega)),\qquad\mathbf{u}\in L^{r}(I;L^{s}(\Omega_{\eta})),

holds for
r\in[2,\infty)
and
s\in(3,\infty]
satisfying
2/{r}+3/{s}\leq 1
, then
(\eta,\mathbf{u},p,\rho,\mathbb{T})
is a global strong solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) that is unique in class of weak solutions.

Let us recall that the classical Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin condition for just a solvent modeled by the incompressible Navier–Stokes equation requires that weak solution additionally satisfy
\mathbf{u}\in L^{r}(I;L^{s}(\Omega_{\eta}))
for them to be a strong solution. For a solvent interacting with a viscoelastic structure, a weak solution
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
is expected to satisfy (
2.11
) for them to become a strong solution. Since finding weak solutions to the
3
D Oldroyld-B is a notorious open problem, one would have certainly expected additional assumptions on
\mathbb{T}
for these elusive weak solutions to become strong ones. This turns out not to be the case as stated in Theorem
2.3
.

Remark 2.4
.

Although a weak solution
(\eta,\mathbf{u},\rho,\mathbb{T})
of (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) is unknown, a weaker result is that a weak solution for the solvent-structure problem can constructed for a given tensor
\overline{\mathbb{T}}\in L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
on one hand. On the other hand, for a given (strong) velocity
\overline{\mathbf{u}}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)
(or that
\overline{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)
) and a shell displacement function
\overline{\eta}\in W^{1,\infty}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))
(or that
\overline{\eta}\in W^{1,8}(I;L^{\infty}(\omega))
), a weak solution can also be constructed for the solute subproblem. Once an approximation procedure exists for the solvent-structure subproblem, a fixed-point argument can then be used to close the entire system. The gist of these details can be seen in the arguments presented in the next section below.

Remark 2.5
.

Just as in
[
8
, Proposition 1]
, a solution
\mathbb{T}
of (
1.5
) advected by a velocity field
\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}(I;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{\eta}))
remains strictly positive if it were initially so. Similarly, a solution
\rho
of (
1.2
) also remains nonnegative if it were initially nonnegative.
Indeed, if we test (
1.2
) with the nonpositive part
\rho_{-}=\min\{0,\rho\}
of
\rho
, integrate over
\Omega_{\eta}
and use the boundary condition (
1.9
) together with Reynold’s transport theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\rho_{-}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho_{-}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0.

Therefore, it follows that
\rho_{-}=0
a.e. in
\Omega_{\eta}
for any
t\in I
and thus,
\rho=\rho_{+}=\max\{0,\rho\}
.

3.
Strong solutions to subproblems

The initial step in our strategy for constructing a solution involves solving the solvent-structure subproblem and the solute subproblem independently of each other. After that, in the next section, we will use a fixed-point argument to get a local solution to the fully coupled system. The very last section will then involve the extension from a local to a global solution.

3.1.
The solvent-structure subproblem

In the following, for a given stress tensor
\underline{\mathbb{T}}
, a given pair of body forces
\mathbf{f}
and
g
, we wish to find a strong solution to the following solvent-structure system of equations

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{u}=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathbf{f}+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\underline{\mathbb{T}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\eta-\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta+\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta=g-(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{n}_{\eta})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}),

defined on
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
(with (
3.3
) defined on
I\times\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
) where

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}+(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}-p\mathbb{I}+\underline{\mathbb{T}}.

We then complement (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) with the following initial and interface conditions

(3.4)

\displaystyle\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot),\qquad\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\omega,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}}.

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\omega.

For the setup above, the precise definition of a strong solution is given as follows.

Definition 3.1

(Strong solution)
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\underline{\mathbb{T}})
be a dataset that satisfies

(3.7)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big),\quad g\in L^{2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big),\quad\eta_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\omega)\text{ with }\|\eta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<L,

\displaystyle\eta_{\star}\in W^{1,2}(\omega),\quad\underline{\mathbb{T}}\in L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\quad\mathbf{u}_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})\text{ is such that }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{\star}\mathbf{n}\text{ on }\omega.

We say that

(\eta,\mathbf{u},p)

is a
strong solution
of (
3.1
)–(
3.6
) with dataset
(\mathbf{f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{\star},\mathbf{u}_{0},\underline{\mathbb{T}})
if the following holds:

(a)

the structure displacement
\eta
is such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L
and

\displaystyle\eta\in W^{1,\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{3,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{2,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{4,2}(\omega)\big);

(b)

the velocity
\mathbf{u}
is such that
\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}
on
I\times\omega
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

(c)

the pressure
p
is such that

\displaystyle p\in L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

(d)

the equations (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) are satisfied a.e. in spacetime with
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
and
\partial_{t}\eta=\eta_{\star}
a.e. in
\omega
as well as
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega_{\eta_{0}}
.

The existence of a conditionally unique global-in-time strong solution to (
3.1
)–(
3.6
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
has recently been shown in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
. The statement of the result is:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
be a weak solution to (
3.1
)–(
3.6
). Suppose that we
have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\eta\in L^{\infty}(I;C^{1}(\omega)),\qquad\mathbf{u}\in L^{r}(I;L^{s}(\Omega_{\eta}))

for any
r\in[2,\infty)
and
s\in(3,\infty]
such that
2/{r}+3/{s}\leq 1
.
Then
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
is a strong solution to (
3.1
)–(
3.6
).
Moreover,
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
is unique in the class of weak solutions satisfying the energy inequality.

Here, a weak solution satisfies (
3.1
)–(
3.6
) in the sense of distribution and is shown in
[
10
]
. We now proceed to the construction of a strong solution to the solute subproblem.

3.2.
The solute subproblem

In this section, we construct a strong solution of the solute subproblem for a given flexible domain
\Omega_{\zeta}
and a known solenoidal vector field
\mathbf{v}
. This subproblem is given by:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\rho=\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}=(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}-2(\mathbb{T}-\rho\mathbb{I})+\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}

on
I\times\Omega_{\zeta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
subject to the following initial and boundary conditions

(3.11)

\displaystyle\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}(\cdot),\quad\mathbb{T}(0,\cdot)=\mathbb{T}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\zeta(0)},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{\zeta}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho=0,\qquad\mathbf{n}_{\zeta}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega_{\zeta}.

The two unknowns
\rho
and
\mathbb{T}
for the solute component of the polymer fluid are related via the identities

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\mathbf{q}\otimes\mathbf{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q},\qquad\rho(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}

where
f
solves (
1.12
).
We now state the precise definition of what we mean by a strong solution.

Definition 3.3
.

Assume that
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
satisfies

(3.13)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)}),

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{T}_{0}>0\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{\zeta(0)},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big),

\displaystyle\zeta\in W^{1,\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{3,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{2,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\omega)\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\zeta}=(\partial_{t}\zeta)\mathbf{n}\quad\text{on }I\times\omega,\quad\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L.

We call

(\rho,\mathbb{T})

a
strong solution
of (
3.9
)-(
3.12
) with dataset
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
if

(a)

(\rho,\mathbb{T})
satisfies

\displaystyle\rho,\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})\big);

(b)

equations (
3.9
)–(
3.10
) are satisfied a.e. in spacetime with
\rho(0)=\rho_{0}
and
\mathbb{T}(0)=\mathbb{T}_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega_{\zeta(0)}
.

We now formulate our result on the existence of a unique strong solution of (
3.9
)-(
3.12
).

Theorem 3.4
.

For a dataset
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{v},\zeta)
satisfying (
3.13
), there exists a unique strong solution
(\rho,\mathbb{T})
of (
3.9
)-(
3.12
), in the sense of Definition
3.3
,
such that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sup_{t\in I}\big(\|\rho(t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\big(\|\rho\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}+\|\mathbb{T}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}+\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}+\int_{I}\big(\|\partial_{t}\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

holds.

Since (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) are dissipative and bilinear, a strong solution of (
3.9
)-(
3.12
), and in particular the bound (
3.14
), is directly obtained by way of a limit to a Galerkin approximation. We therefore proceed with just the formal computation.

Proof.

First of all, if we test (
3.9
) with
\rho
and use Reynold’s transport theorem and the boundary condition, we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=0

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in I}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}.

If we also test (
3.10
) with
\mathbb{T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+2\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}[(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}]:\mathbb{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\rho\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{T})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

On one hand, we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\rho\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{T})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{I}\big(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{T})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq T\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+\int_{I}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

and on the other hand, by interpolation

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}[(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top}]:\mathbb{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{3/2}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+c\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{4}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

holds for any
\delta>0
.
Thus, it follows from Grönwall’s lemma that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\int_{I}\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{4}\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigg[\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+T\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))}^{2}\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigg[\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}+T\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})}^{2}\bigg]

since by interpolation

\displaystyle\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{4}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))}^{2}\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We now test (
3.9
) with
\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho
. This yields

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\|\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}((\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\rho)\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\int_{\partial\Omega_{\zeta}}(\partial_{t}\zeta\mathbf{n})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\zeta}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\zeta}|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}

where

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\delta\int_{I}\|\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+c(\delta)\int_{I}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\delta>0
. Now note that by interpolation and the trace theorem,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{1/4,2}(\partial\Omega_{\zeta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{3/4,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{1/4}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{3/4}.

Thus by using
\zeta\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{1,\infty}(\omega))
and
\partial_{t}\zeta\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega))
, we also obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\|(\partial_{t}\zeta\mathbf{n})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\zeta}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\zeta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\|\rho\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{7/4}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{1/4}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\|\partial_{t}\zeta\|_{W^{5/4,2}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\|\rho\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{7/4}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{1/4}\|\partial_{t}\zeta\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{3/4}\|\partial_{t}\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{1/4}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{I}\|\rho\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+c(\delta)\int_{I}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\|\partial_{t}\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

or any
\delta>0
.
Consequently, it follows from Grönwall’s lemma that

(3.17)

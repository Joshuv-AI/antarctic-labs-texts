Homology vanishing theorems for pinched submanifolds
Christos-Raent Onti, Theodoros Vlachos

Abstract
. We investigate the geometry and topology of submanifolds under a sharp pinching condition involving extrinsic invariants like the mean curvature and the length of the second fundamental form. Homology vanishing results are given that strengthen and sharpen previous ones. In addition, an integral bound is provided for the Bochner operator of compact Euclidean submanifolds in terms of the Betti numbers.

1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in differential geometry is to investigate the relationship between geometry and
topology of Riemannian manifolds. The same question can be raised from the point of view of submanifold
geometry. Indeed, it has been an active field of research to study the effect of pinching conditions, involving intrinsic and
extrinsic curvature invariants, on the geometry or the topology of submanifolds.
An important result in this direction was given by Simons
[
Simons68
]
for minimal submanifolds of spheres. Since then, plenty of geometric and topological rigidity results have been obtained, under various pinching conditions, see for instance
[
adc
,
an
,
CDCK70
,
fuxu08
,
guxu12
,
guxu13
,
Savo14
,
ybshen
,
gauchman84
,
Vlachos02
,
hv
,
Vlachos04
]
.
In particular, Lawson and Simons
[
LS73
]
showed that certain
bounds on the second fundamental form for submanifolds of spheres force homology
groups to vanish. In their approach, the second variation of area was exploited to rule out stable minimal currents in
certain dimensions, and since one can minimize area in a homology class, this
trivializes integral homology.

The aim of the present paper is to study the geometry and topology of submanifolds under a sharp pinching condition
involving the mean curvature and the length of the second fundamental form. Throughout the paper,
S
denotes the

squared length
of the second fundamental form
\alpha_{f}
of an isometric
immersion
f
, while the
mean curvature
is defined as the length
H=\|\mathcal{H}\|
of the

mean curvature vector field
given by
\mathcal{H}=(\mathrm{tr}\,\alpha_{f})/n
, where
\mathrm{tr}

means taking the trace. The choice of the pinching condition is
inspired by the standard immersion of a torus

\mathbb{T}^{n}_{p}(r)=\mathbb{S}^{p}(r)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-p}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}})

into the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{p}(r)
denotes the
p
-dimensional sphere
of radius
r<1
. The principal curvatures are
\sqrt{1-r^{2}}/r
and
-r/\sqrt{1-r^{2}}
of multiplicity
p
and

n-p
, respectively. A direct computation gives that
S=a(n,p,H,1)
if
r^{2}\geq p/n
whereas
S>a(n,p,H,1)
if
r^{2}<p/n
, where

a(n,p,t,c)=nc+\frac{n^{3}t^{2}}{2p(n-p)}-\frac{n|n-2p|t}{2p(n-p)}\sqrt{n^{2}t^{2}+4p(n-p)c},\;t,c\in\mathbb{R}.

This example served as the motivation to study isometric immersions
f\colon M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{m}
between
Riemannian manifolds that satisfy the pinching condition

(1)

S\leq a(n,p,H,c)

pointwise, where
p
is an integer with
1\leq p\leq n/2
, under the mild assumption
that the curvature operator of the ambient manifold
\tilde{M}^{m}
is bounded from below by a constant
c
.
In particular, this includes the case of submanifolds of spaces of constant sectional curvature.
Submanifolds that satisfy condition (
1
) have already been investigated by several authors under geometric assumptions (see for instance
[
adc
,
Savo14
,
lin15
,
ybshen
,
Xu1993
,
CN
,
WX
,
Zhang
,
ZZ
]
). In addition, the topology of submanifolds
that satisfy strict inequality in (
1
) was studied in
[
leung83
,
sx97.2
,
Vlachos07
,
xugu10
,
guxu12
]
without any geometric assumptions.

In the present paper, we investigate the geometry and topology of submanifolds that satisfy the
pinching condition (
1
). We show that this
pinching condition either forces homology to vanish in a range of intermediate dimensions, or completely
determines the pinched submanifold. Our results are sharp and strengthen previous ones (see
[
an
,
CS19
,
fuxu08
,
Gr
,
lin15
,
leung83
,
Savo14
,
ybshen
,
sx97.2
,
Vlachos07
]
). A key ingredient in our approach is the Bochner technique that is based
on the Bochner-Weitzenböck formula. This states that the Laplacian of every
p
-form

\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M^{n})
on a manifold
M^{n}
is given by

(2)

\Delta\omega=\nabla^{*}\nabla\omega+\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,

where
\nabla^{*}\nabla
is
the rough Laplacian and

\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\colon\Omega^{p}(M^{n})\to\Omega^{p}(M^{n})

is a certain symmetric endomorphism of the bundle of
p
-forms, called the
Bochner operator
.
The Bochner-Weitzenböck formula implies that every harmonic
p
-form vanishes on compact manifolds,
provided that
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}
is positive definite. Since every de Rham cohomology class is represented by a
harmonic form, it follows that the
p
-th de Rham cohomology group
H^{p}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})
vanishes.

The difficulty when dealing with the Bochner operator
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}
arises from the fact that it is unmanageable
for
p>1
. Our approach is inspired by the interesting paper due to Savo
[
Savo14
]
. The key point
is to establish a sharp inequality for the Bochner operator in
terms of the second fundamental form of any immersed submanifold in a Riemannian manifold whose
curvature operator is bounded from below by a constant. Moreover, we determine the structure of the second
fundamental form at points where equality holds. All the above mentioned, combined with Bochner technique
and Morse theory, enable us to provide homology vanishing results, bounds for the Betti numbers, or determine
the submanifolds that satisfy the pinching condition (
1
) regardless codimension. Examples
show that the pinching condition is sharp.

2.
The results

This section is devoted to the statements of our results. Throughout the paper, all submanifolds under consideration are assumed to be oriented. Our first global result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M^{n},n\geq 3
, be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold isometrically immersed in a
Riemannian manifold whose curvature operator is bounded from below by a constant
c
. Suppose that
inequality (
1
) is satisfied for an integer
1\leq p\leq n/2
and
H^{2}+c\geq 0
.
The following assertions hold:

(i)

The Betti numbers of
M^{n}
satisfy
b_{i}(M^{n})\leq\binom{n}{i}
for each
p\leq i\leq n-p
.

(ii)

If strict inequality holds in (
1
) at a point, then
b_{i}(M^{n})=0
for all
p\leq i\leq n-p
.
If in addition
p=1
, then
M^{n}
is a real homology sphere, i.e.,
H^{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})=0
for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

(iii)

If
b_{p}(M^{n})>0
, then equality holds in (
1
) everywhere on
M^{n}
and every harmonic

p
-form is parallel. In particular,
M^{n}
supports a nontrivial parallel
p
-form.

The above extends previous results due to Cui and Sun
[
CS19
]
and Savo
[
Savo14
, Th. 8]
.

Our second main result is about (not necessarily compact) submanifolds carrying a nontrivial parallel

p
-form and can be formulated as follows (see
[
Gr
]
for related results).

Theorem 2
.

Let
M^{n},n\geq 3
, be a Riemannian manifold isometrically immersed in
a Riemannian manifold whose curvature operator is bounded from below by a constant
c
such that

H^{2}+c\geq 0
. If
M^{n}
supports a nontrivial harmonic
p
-form of constant length (in particular,
a parallel
p
-form), then
S\geq a(n,p,H,c).

Since Kähler manifolds support non-trivial parallel forms in all even degrees, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3
.

If a Kähler manifold
M^{2m}
is isometrically immersed in a Riemannian manifold whose
curvature operator is bounded from below by a constant
c
such that
H^{2}+c\geq 0
, then

S\geq a\big(2m,2[m/2],H,c\big).

In the above results, the assumption that the curvature operator of the ambient manifold is bounded from
below can be weakened by merely assuming that the induced Bochner operator is bounded from below.

Now we focus on submanifolds that satisfy the pinching condition (
1
) in complete simply connected space
forms
\mathbb{Q}^{m}_{c}
of constant sectional curvature
c
, that is, the Euclidean
space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m}
or the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{m}
, according to whether
c=0,c=1
or
c=-1
,
respectively. The next result deals with minimal submanifolds in spheres and extends the results in
[

leung83
]

Theorem 4
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{m},n\geq 3
, be a minimal isometric immersion of a compact Riemannian manifold

M^{n}
. If
S\leq n
on
M^{n}
, then one of the following assertions holds:

(i)

M^{n}
is a real homology sphere, with finite fundamental group, that admits a Riemannian metric of positive
Ricci curvature.

(ii)

f(M^{n})
is isometric to the Clifford torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{p}(\sqrt{p/n})

for an integer
1\leq p\leq n/2
.

(iii)

The immersion
f
is the standard embedding
\psi\colon\mathord{\mathbb{C}}\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{4/3}\to\mathbb{S}^{7}
of the complex
projective plane of constant holomorphic curvature
4/3
.

In particular, for minimal submanifolds of dimension three and four, we have the following result that improves
the bound in
[
ybshen
, Th. 3]
:

Corollary 5
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{m}
be a minimal isometric immersion of a compact Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
with

S\leq n
on
M^{n}
.

(i)

If
n=3
, then either
f(M^{3})
is isometric to the Clifford torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{1}(\sqrt{1/3})
,
or
M^{3}
is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form.

(ii)

If

\mathbb{T}^{4}_{p}(\sqrt{p/n})
,

the immersion

We recall that the Cartan minimal hypersurface in

[

]

Let

3.
Algebraic preliminaries and auxiliary results

This section is devoted to some algebraic preliminaries inspired by the ideas developed by Savo
[
Savo14
]
.
Let
V
be a real
n
-dimensional vector space,
n\geq 3
, equipped with a positive definite
inner product denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We denote by
\mathrm{End}(V)

the set of all self-adjoint endomorphisms of
V
.

For every integer
1\leq p\leq n
, we consider the set
I_{p}
of
p
-multi-indices

I_{p}=\left\{\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}:1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n\right\}.

Let
A\in\mathrm{End}(V)
be an arbitrary endomorphism of
V
with eigenvalues
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
.
For each
a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\in I_{p}
, the associated
p
-
algebraic curvature

K_{a}
of
A
is
the number given by

K_{a}=k_{i_{1}}+\cdots+k_{i_{p}}.

Let
\Lambda^{p}V^{*}
be the
\binom{n}{p}
-dimensional real vector space, defined as the

p
-th exterior power of the dual vector space
V^{*}=\mbox{Hom}(V,\mathbb{R})
of
V
.
To each
A\in\mbox{End}(V)
, we associate the endomorphism
T_{A}^{[p]}\in\mbox{End}(\Lambda^{p}V^{*})
defined by

T_{A}^{[p]}=(\mathrm{tr}A)A^{[p]}-A^{[p]}\circ A^{[p]},

where
A^{[p]}\in\mbox{End}(\Lambda^{p}V^{*})
is given by

A^{[p]}\omega(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\omega(v_{1},\dots,Av_{i},\dots,v_{p}),

with
\omega\in\Lambda^{p}V^{*}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}\in V
. The endomorphism
T_{A}^{[p]}
is self-adjoint
with respect to the natural inner product
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
in
\Lambda^{p}V^{*}
.
It follows from
[
Savo14
, Lem. 2]
that the
\binom{n}{p}
eigenvalues
\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})
of
T_{A}^{[p]}

are given by

(3)

\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})=K_{a}K_{\star a},\;\;a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\in I_{p},

where
\star a\in I_{p}
is defined by
\star a=\{1,\dots,n\}\smallsetminus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}
.

The lowest eigenvalue of
T_{A}^{[p]}
is given by

\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})=\mathop{\min_{\omega\in\Lambda^{p}V^{*}}}_{\|\omega\|=1}{\langle}T_{A}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}.

We denote by
\mathring{A}=A-H_{A}{\rm id}_{V}
the traceless part of every
A\in\mbox{End}(V)
, where

H_{A}=(1/n)\,\mathrm{tr}A
and
{\rm id}_{V}\colon V\to V
being the identity map on
V
.

The inequality in the following lemma was essentially proved in
[
Savo14
, Lem. 14]
.

Lemma 12
.

For every
A\in\mathrm{End}(V)
with
\mathrm{tr}A\geq 0
and each integer
1\leq p\leq n/2
, the lowest eigenvalue
of
T_{A}^{[p]}\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda^{p}V^{*})
satisfies

(4)

\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\geq\frac{1}{4}(\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{n-2p}{n}\,\mathrm{tr}A+\sqrt{4p(n-p)/n}\,\|\mathring{A}\|\Big)^{2}.

If equality holds, then
A
has at most two distinct eigenvalues

\lambda
and
\mu
with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively. If in addition
\mu=0

and
p<n/2
, then
A=0
.

Proof:
Let
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
A
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1
. Suppose that
\mathrm{tr}A=0
and let
a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\in I_{p}
. It follows from (
3
)
that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})
of
T_{A}^{[p]}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big(\sum_{i\in a}k_{i}\big)^{2}\geq-p\sum_{i\in a}k_{i}^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big(\sum_{i\in\star a}k_{i}\big)^{2}\geq-(n-p)\sum_{i\in\star a}k_{i}^{2}.

Summing up, we obtain

(5)

\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\geq-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\|A\|^{2}

which proves the desired inequality in this case. If equality holds in (
4
), then all the
above inequalities become equalities, and this implies that
A
has at most two distinct eigenvalues

\lambda=k_{i_{1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{p}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu=k_{i_{p+1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{n}}

with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively, where
\star a=\{i_{p+1},\dots,i_{n}\}
.

Case 2
. Suppose that
\mathrm{tr}A>0
. The eigenvalues of the traceless part
\mathring{A}
of
A
are

k_{1}-(\mathrm{tr}A)/n,\dots,k_{n}-(\mathrm{tr}A)/n
. It follows from (
3
)
that, for each
a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\in I_{p}
, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})
of

T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]}
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\big(K_{a}-\frac{p}{n}\,\mathrm{tr}A\big)\big(K_{\star a}-\frac{n-p}{n}\,\mathrm{tr}A\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})+\frac{p(n-p)}{n^{2}}\,(\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-\frac{n-p}{n}K_{a}\,\mathrm{tr}A-\frac{p}{n}K_{\star a}\,\mathrm{tr}A.

Using that
K_{a}+K_{\star a}=\mathrm{tr}A
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})-\frac{p^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}A\right)^{2}+\frac{2p-n}{n}K_{a}\,\mathrm{tr}A,

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})-\frac{(n-p)^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}A\right)^{2}+\frac{n-2p}{n}K_{\star a}\,\mathrm{tr}A.

Summing up, we have

2\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})=2\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})-\frac{(n-p)^{2}+p^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}A\right)^{2}+\frac{n-2p}{n}\left(K_{\star a}-K_{a}\right)\mathrm{tr}A.

Since
\mathring{A}
is trace free, inequality (
5
) applies and yields

\lambda_{a}(T_{\mathring{A}}^{[p]})\geq-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\|\mathring{A}\|^{2}.

Combining the above, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})-\frac{(n-p)^{2}+p^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}A\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{n}p(n-p)\|\mathring{A}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n}(n-2p)\left|K_{\star a}-K_{a}\right|\mathrm{tr}A.

Using (
3
), it follows that

|K_{\star a}-K_{a}|=\big((\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-4\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\big)^{1/2},

and consequently

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})-\frac{(n-p)^{2}+p^{2}}{n^{2}}(\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}+\frac{2}{n}p(n-p)\|\mathring{A}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n}\left(n-2p\right)\mathrm{tr}A\big((\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-4\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\big)^{1/2}.

Setting
\rho=\big((\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-4\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\big)^{1/2}
,
the above is written as

n^{2}\rho^{2}-2n(n-2p)\,\mathrm{tr}A\,\rho+(n-2p)^{2}(\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-4np(n-p)\|\mathring{A}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Thus

\rho\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(n-2p\right)\mathrm{tr}A+\sqrt{4p(n-p)/n}\,\|\mathring{A}\|,

or equivalently

\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})\geq\frac{1}{4}(\mathrm{tr}A)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big(\frac{n-2p}{n}\,\mathrm{tr}A+\sqrt{4p(n-p)/n}\,\|\mathring{A}\|\big)^{2}.

Inequality (
4
) follows immediately from the above. If equality holds in (
4
), then
all the above inequalities become equalities, and this implies that
A
has at most two distinct eigenvalues

\lambda=k_{i_{1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{p}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu=k_{i_{p+1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{n}}

with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively, where
\star a=\{i_{p+1},\dots,i_{n}\}
.

Next we suppose that equality holds in (
4
) and

\lambda_{a}(T_{A}^{[p]})=\min_{b\in I_{p}}\lambda_{b}(T_{A}^{[p]})

for some
a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\in I_{p}
. In both Cases 1 and 2, the endomorphism
A
has at most two
distinct eigenvalues

\lambda=k_{i_{1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{p}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu=k_{i_{p+1}}=\cdots=k_{i_{n}}

with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively, where
\star a=\{i_{p+1},\dots,i_{n}\}
. A direct computation
shows that the eigenvalues of
T_{A}^{[p]}
are given by

\lambda_{b}(T_{A}^{[p]})=\left((p-\mathsf{n}_{b})\lambda+\mathsf{n}_{b}\mu\right)\left(\mathsf{n}_{b}\lambda+(n-p-\mathsf{n}_{b})\mu\right)

for each
b=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}\}\in I_{p}
with
1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{p}\leq n
. Here
0\leq\mathsf{n}_{b}\leq p

is the number of common elements of
b
and
a=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}
.

In addition, we suppose that
\mu=0
and
p<n/2
. From the above it follows that

\min_{b\in I_{p}}\lambda_{b}(T_{A}^{[p]})=0.

Computing the right hand side in (
4
) in terms of the eigenvalues of
A
,
and since equality holds in (
4
), we obtain

\min_{b\in I_{p}}\lambda_{b}(T_{A}^{[p]})=\frac{2p^{2}}{n^{2}}(n-p)(2p-n)\lambda^{2}.

It is now obvious that
A=0
.

Let
W
be a
k
-dimensional real vector space equipped with a positive definite
inner product which, by abuse of notation, is again denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
We denote by
\mbox{Hom}(V\times V,W)
the space of all bilinear forms with values in
W
and by

\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
the subspace that consists of all symmetric bilinear forms.
The space
\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
can be viewed as a complete metric space with respect
to the usual Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
. For each
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
,
we define the map

\beta^{\sharp}\colon W\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V),\;\;\xi\mapsto\beta^{\sharp}(\xi)

such that

\langle\beta^{\sharp}(\xi)v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=\langle\beta(v_{1},v_{2}),\xi\rangle\;\;\text{for all}\;\;v_{1},v_{2}\in V.

For every integer
1\leq p\leq n
, we define the map

\mathsf{B}^{[p]}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\to\mbox{End}(\Lambda^{p}V^{*})

given by

\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}T_{\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})}^{[p]},

where
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
is an arbitrary orthonormal basis of
W
. Observe that
\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta)

is a self-adjoint endomorphism of
\Lambda^{p}V^{*}
. Its lowest eigenvalue is denoted by
\varrho_{p}(\beta)
.

For convenience, we set
\mathsf{H}_{\beta}=\left(1/n\right)\mathrm{tr}\beta
and denote by

\mathring{\beta}=\beta-{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}\mathsf{H}_{\beta}

the traceless part of each
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
. The image of
\beta
is
the subspace
\mathrm{Im}\,\beta\subset W
given by

\mathrm{Im}\,\beta={\rm{span}}\left\{\beta(v_{1},v_{2}):v_{1},v_{2}\in V\right\}.

The following will be an important tool in our proofs.

Proposition 13
.

For every
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
and each integer
1\leq p\leq n/2
,
the lowest eigenvalue
\varrho_{p}(\beta)
of
\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta)\in\mathrm{End}\left(\Lambda^{p}V^{*}\right)

satisfies

(6)

\varrho_{p}(\beta)\geq\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|\|\mathring{\beta}\|-\|\mathring{\beta}\|^{2}\big).

Moreover, if equality holds in (
6
) for some
\beta
, then the following hold:

(i)

For every unit vector
u\in W
,
\beta^{\sharp}(u)
has at most two distinct
eigenvalues of multiplicities
p
and
n-p
. In addition, if
p<n/2
and the eigenvalue with
multiplicity
n-p
vanishes, then
\beta^{\sharp}(u)=0
.

(ii)

If
\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\neq 0
and
p<n/2
, then
\mathrm{Im}\,\beta={\rm{span}}\left\{\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\right\}
.

Proof:
Let
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
. If
\dim W=1
, then the proof follows directly from
Lemma
12
. We suppose that
\dim W>1
and distinguish two cases.

Case 1
. Suppose that
\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\neq 0
and consider an orthonormal basis

\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
of
W
such that
\xi_{1}=\mathsf{H}_{\beta}/\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|
.
Then we have
\mathrm{tr}\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})=0
for each
2\leq i\leq k
.
From Lemma
12
, it follows that the eigenvalues of
T_{\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})}^{[p]},1\leq i\leq k
,
satisfy

(7)

\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(T_{\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})}^{[p]})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big((n-2p)\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|+\sqrt{4p(n-p)/n}\,\|\mathring{\beta}^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\|\big)^{2}

and

(8)

\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(T_{\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})}^{[p]})\geq-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\,\|\mathring{\beta}^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})\|^{2}\;\;\text{for each}\;\;2\leq i\leq k.

Note that

\|\mathring{\beta}^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\|^{2}=\|\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\|^{2}-n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mathring{\beta}^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})=\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})\;\;\text{for all}\;\;2\leq i\leq k.

Summing up (
7
) and (
8
) and since

\varrho_{p}(\beta)=\mathop{\min_{\omega\in\Lambda^{p}V^{*}}}_{\|\omega\|=1}{\langle}\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta)\omega,\omega{\rangle}=\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta)),

we obtain

\displaystyle\varrho_{p}(\beta)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!p(n-p)\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(\|\beta\|^{2}-n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\!\!\!-(n-2p)\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|\sqrt{p(n-p)/n}\,\sqrt{\|\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\|^{2}-n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!p(n-p)\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(\|\beta\|^{2}-n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\!\!\!-(n-2p)\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|\sqrt{p(n-p)/n}\,\sqrt{\|\beta\|^{2}-n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}},

and this is inequality (
6
).

Now, we suppose that equality holds in (
6
) for some
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
.
Since all inequalities above become equalities, we obtain
\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})=0
for each
2\leq i\leq k

provided that
p<n/2
. Thus
\mathrm{Im}\,\beta={\rm{span}}\left\{\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\right\}
if
p<n/2
, and the
rest of the proof for this case follows from Lemma
12
.

Case 2
. Suppose that
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
is traceless. Let
u\in W
be an arbitrary
unit vector. We choose an orthonormal base
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
of
W
such that
\xi_{1}=u
. Since

\mathrm{tr}\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})=0
for every
1\leq i\leq k
, from Lemma
12
it follows that

\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(T_{\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})}^{[p]})\geq-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\,\|\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})\|^{2}\;\;\text{for each}\;\;1\leq i\leq k.

Summing over
i
, we obtain

\varrho_{p}(\beta)=\min_{a\in I_{p}}\lambda_{a}(\mathsf{B}^{[p]}(\beta))\geq-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\beta^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})\|^{2}=-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\,\|\beta\|^{2}

and this proves inequality (
6
).

If equality holds in (
6
) for some
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
,
then the above inequalities become equalities and the proof follows from Lemma
12
.

Next we assume that the dimension of
V
is
n\geq 4
. For each integer
1\leq p<n/2
and for every

\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, we denote by
\Lambda_{p}(\beta)
the
subset of the unit
(k-1)
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
in
W
given by

\Lambda_{p}(\beta)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}:p<\mathrm{Index}\,\beta^{\sharp}(u)<n-p\right\}.

The inequality that follows is crucial for the proof of Theorem
11
.

Proposition 14
.

Given integers
n\geq 4,\,k\geq 1
and
1\leq p<n/2
, there exists a constant

\varepsilon(n,k,p)>0
such that the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\varrho_{p}(\beta)-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|\|\mathring{\beta}\|-\|\mathring{\beta}\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon(n,k,p)\Big(\int_{\Lambda_{p}(\beta)}|\det\beta^{\sharp}(u)|dS_{u}\Big)^{2/n}

for all
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
, where
dS_{u}
stands for the volume element
of the unit sphere in
W
.

Proof:
We consider the functions

\phi_{p},\psi_{p}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\phi_{p}(\beta)=\varrho_{p}(\beta)-\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(n\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|^{2}-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,\|\mathsf{H}_{\beta}\|\|\mathring{\beta}\|-\|\mathring{\beta}\|^{2}\big)

and

\psi_{p}(\beta)=\int_{\Lambda_{p}(\beta)}|\det\beta^{\sharp}(u)|dS_{u}.

From Proposition
13
we know that the function
\phi_{p}
is nonnegative. To prove the
desired inequality, it is sufficient to show that
\phi_{p}
attaints a positive minimum on the level set

\Sigma_{n,k,p}=\left\{\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W):\psi_{p}(\beta)=1\right\}.

Let
\{\beta_{m}\}
be a sequence in
\Sigma_{n,k,p}
such that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{p}(\beta_{m})=\inf\phi_{p}(\Sigma_{n,k,p})\geq 0.

Claim I: The sequence
\{\beta_{m}\}
is bounded.
Suppose to the contrary that
there exists a subsequence of
\{\beta_{m}\}
, which by abuse of notation is again
denoted by
\{\beta_{m}\}
, such that
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\beta_{m}\|=\infty
.
Since
\beta_{m}\neq 0
for all
m\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
, we may write
\beta_{m}=\|\beta_{m}\|\hat{\beta}_{m}
, where

\|\hat{\beta}_{m}\|=1
, and assume that
\{\hat{\beta}_{m}\}
converges to some

\hat{\beta}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V\times V,W)
with
\|\hat{\beta}\|=1
. It can be easily checked that

\phi_{p}
is homogeneous of degree
2
, that is

\phi_{p}(t\beta)=t^{2}\phi_{p}(\beta)\;\;\text{for all}\;\;t>0.

Therefore, we obtain
\phi_{p}(\hat{\beta}_{m})=\phi_{p}(\beta_{m})/\|\beta_{m}\|^{2}
.
Thus
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{p}(\hat{\beta}_{m})=0
and consequently

\phi_{p}(\hat{\beta})=0
, which means that
\hat{\beta}
satisfies equality in inequality (
6
)
in Proposition
13
. To reach a contradiction, we distinguish two cases.

Case 1
. Suppose that
\mathsf{H}_{\hat{\beta}}\neq 0
. Proposition
13

\hat{\beta}(\cdot,\cdot)={\langle}{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{\xi})\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}\,\hat{\xi}

and
{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{\xi})
has at most two distinct eigenvalues
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{\mu}
with
multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively, where

\hat{\xi}=\mathsf{H}_{\hat{\beta}}/\|\mathsf{H}_{\hat{\beta}}\|
.
Clearly
\hat{\mu}\neq 0
, since otherwise
\hat{\beta}=0
, and this is a contradiction.

Since
\beta_{m}\in\Sigma_{n,k,p}
, there exists an open subset
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}\subset\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\subset W

such that
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}\subset\Lambda_{p}(\hat{\beta}_{m})
and
\det\hat{\beta}_{m}^{\sharp}(u)\neq 0

for all
u\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{\hat{u}_{m}\}
be any convergent sequence

\hat{u}_{m}\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
for all

m\in\mathbb{N}

\hat{u}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\hat{u}_{m}

.

{\langle}\hat{\beta}(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{\xi}{\rangle}=0

\hat{\beta}=0
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2
. Suppose that
\hat{\beta}
is traceless. Given that
\hat{\beta}
satisfies equality in
inequality (
6
), Proposition
13
yields that for all
\hat{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\subset W

the endomorphism
{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})
has at most two distinct eigenvalues

\hat{\lambda}(\hat{u})
and
\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})
with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively.

Since
\beta_{m}\in\Sigma_{n,k,p}
, it follows that there exists an open subset

\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}\subset\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\subset W
such that
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}\subset\Lambda_{p}(\hat{\beta}_{m})

and
\det\hat{\beta}_{m}^{\sharp}(u)\neq 0
for all
u\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
and

m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{\hat{u}_{m}\}
be any convergent sequence such that
\hat{u}_{m}\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and set
\hat{u}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\hat{u}_{m}
. Since

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\beta}_{m}^{\sharp}(\hat{u}_{m})=\hat{\beta}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})

and
\hat{u}_{m}\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
, it follows that
\mathrm{Index}\,\hat{\beta}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})<n-p
.
We claim that
\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})=0
. Observe that

p\hat{\lambda}(\hat{u})+(n-p)\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})=0.

If
\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})>0
(respectively,
\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})<0
), then
{\rm Index}\,{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})\leq p

(respectively,
{\rm Index}\,{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})\geq n-p
). Thus

{\rm Index}\,{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}(\hat{u}_{m})\leq p
(respectively,
{\rm Index}\,{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}(\hat{u}_{m})\geq n-p
)
for
m
large enough, and this contradicts the fact that
\hat{u}_{m}\in\Lambda_{p}(\hat{\beta}_{m})
.
Hence
\hat{\mu}(\hat{u})=0
and Proposition
13
yields
{\hat{\beta}}^{\sharp}(\hat{u})=0
.

In fact, we proved that

(9)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}(\hat{u}_{m})=0

for any convergent sequence
\{\hat{u}_{m}\}
such that
\hat{u}_{m}\in\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}
for all
m\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
.
We may choose convergent sequences
\{\hat{u}_{m}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\hat{u}_{m}^{(k)}\}
in
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{m}

such that
\hat{u}_{m}^{(1)},\dots,\hat{u}_{m}^{(k)}
span
W
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Using the Gram-Schmidt process we obtain sequences
\{\xi_{m}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\xi_{m}^{(k)}\}
such that

\xi_{m}^{(i)}\in{\rm{span}}\{\hat{u}_{m}^{(1)},\dots,\hat{u}_{m}^{(i)}\}\;\;\text{for each}\>\>1\leq i\leq k.

Thus
\hat{\xi}_{m}^{(a)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{m}^{(\ell)}{\hat{u}}_{m}^{(\ell)}
,
where
\{x_{m}^{(\ell)}\}
are convergent sequences for each

,
and consequently we have

(10)

\hat{\beta}_{m}(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{a=1}^{k}{\langle}{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}({\hat{\xi}}_{m}^{(a)})\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}{\hat{\xi}}_{m}^{(a)}

and
{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}({\hat{\xi}}_{m}^{(a)})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{m}^{(\ell)}{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}({\hat{u}}_{m}^{(\ell)})
.
Using (
9

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}({\hat{\xi}}_{m}^{(a)})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}x_{m}^{(\ell)}{\hat{\beta}}_{m}^{\sharp}({\hat{u}}_{m}^{(\ell)})=0

4.
Submanifolds satisfying the pinching condition

Let
M^{n}
be an oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. For each integer
0\leq p\leq n
,
the Hodge-Laplace operator acting on differential
p
-forms is defined by

\Delta=d\delta+\delta d:\Omega^{p}(M^{n})\to\Omega^{p}(M^{n}),

where
d
and
\delta
are the differential and the co-differential operators, respectively.
When
p=0
, the Hodge-Laplace operator is just the Laplace-Beltrami operator acting on

0
-forms, i.e., scalar functions.

We recall the following well known result (cf.
[
Savo14
, Prop. 3]
).

Proposition 15
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact, oriented Riemannian manifold and let
1\leq p\leq n/2
.
The following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\geq 0
, then
b_{p}(M^{n})\leq\binom{n}{p}
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\geq 0
and the strict inequality holds at some point, then

H^{p}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})=H^{n-p}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})=0
.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\geq 0
and
H^{p}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})\neq 0
, then every harmonic
p
-form is parallel.
In particular,
M^{n}
supports a nontrivial parallel
p
-form.

Proof:
Let
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M^{n})
. From the Bochner-Weitzenböck formula (
2
), we obtain

(14)

{\langle}\Delta\omega,\omega{\rangle}=\|\nabla\omega\|^{2}+{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}+\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta\|\omega\|^{2}.

If
\omega
belongs to the space
\mathscr{H}^{p}(M^{n})
of harmonic
p
-forms, then using
the assumption, we have

0\leq\int_{M^{n}}{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}dM=-\int_{M^{n}}\|\nabla\omega\|^{2}dM.

Thus
\omega
is parallel and therefore

\displaystyle b_{p}(M^{n})\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\dim H^{p}(M^{n};\mathbb{R})=\dim\mathscr{H}^{p}(M^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\dim\Lambda^{p}(T_{x}^{*}M^{n})=\binom{n}{p}.

This proves (i). For the proofs of (ii) and (iii) see
[
Savo14
, Prop. 3]
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{n+k}
be an isometric immersion whose second fundamental form

\alpha_{f}
is viewed as a section of the vector bundle
\mathrm{Hom}(TM\times TM,N_{f}M)
, where

N_{f}M
is the normal bundle. For each unit normal vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(N_{f}M)
, the associated
shape operator
A_{\xi}
is given by

{\langle}A_{\xi}X,Y{\rangle}=\langle\alpha_{f}(X,Y),\xi\rangle,\>\>X,Y\in TM.

Savo
[
Savo14
, Th. 1]
proved that the Bochner operator
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}
splits as

(15)

\mathcal{B}^{[p]}=\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{res}}+\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{ext}},

where
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{res}}
depends on the geometry of the ambient manifold
\tilde{M}^{n+k}

restricted to the submanifold
f(M^{n})
and satisfies

(16)

\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{res}}\geq p(n-p)c,

where
c
is a lower bound for the curvature operator of
\tilde{M}^{n+k}
. In particular, if

\tilde{M}^{n+k}
has constant sectional curvature
c
, then

(17)

\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{res}}=p(n-p)c.

The second operator
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{ext}}
is given
explicitly in terms of the second fundamental form of the immersion
f
.
For each unit vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(N_{f}M)
, we define
the endomorphism

T_{A_{\xi}}^{[p]}=(\mathrm{tr}A_{\xi})A_{\xi}^{[p]}-A_{\xi}^{[p]}\circ A_{\xi}^{[p]},

where

A_{\xi}^{[p]}\colon\Omega^{p}(M^{n})\to\Omega^{p}(M^{n})

is the self-adjoint extension of the shape operator
A_{\xi}
in the direction
\xi
defined by

A_{\xi}^{[p]}\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\omega(X_{1},\dots,A_{\xi}X_{i},\dots,X_{p}),\;X_{1},\dots,X_{p}\in TM.

Then
\mathcal{B}^{[p]}_{\rm{ext}}
is given by (see
[
Savo14
]
)

\mathcal{B}_{\rm{ext}}^{[p]}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}T_{A_{\xi_{i}}}^{[p]},

where
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
is an orthonormal frame of the normal bundle of
f
.

The next proposition provides a sharp estimate for the Bochner operator in terms of the
second fundamental form in arbitrary codimension and gives its structure at points where
equality holds. Inequality (
18
) was proved by Savo
[
Savo14
, Prop. 15]
for
hypersurfaces. Note that, since
\mathcal{B}^{[1]}
is nothing but the Ricci tensor, inequality (
18
) for

reduces to the inequality due to Leung
[
leung92
]
. We recall that the traceless part of the
second fundamental form is given by
\Phi=\alpha_{f}-{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}\mathcal{H}

Proposition 16
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{m},n\geq 3
, be an isometric immersion. If the
curvature operator of
\tilde{M}^{m}
is bounded from below by a constant
c
, then the Bochner
operator of
M^{n}
satisfies pointwise the inequality

(18)

\mathop{\min_{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M^{n})}}_{\|\omega\|=1}{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}\geq\frac{p(n-p)}{n}\big(n(H^{2}+c)-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,H\|\Phi\|-\|\Phi\|^{2}\big).

If equality holds in (
18

, then the following hold:

(i)

The shape operator
A_{\xi}(x)
has at most two distinct
eigenvalues with multiplicities
p
and
n-p
for every unit vector
\xi\in N_{f}M(x)
. If in addition

and the eigenvalue of multiplicity

vanishes, then
A_{\xi}(x)=0
.

(ii)

If
H(x)\neq 0
and
p<n/2
, then
\mathrm{Im}\,\alpha(x)={\rm{span}}\left\{\mathcal{H}(x)\right\}
.

Proof:
It is a direct consequence of Proposition
13
, (
15
) and (
16
).

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We observe that

(19)

a(n,p,H,c)=\left(r(n,p,H,c)\right)^{2}+nH^{2},

where
r(n,p,H,c)
is the largest root of the quadratic polynomial

(20)

P(t;p,H,c)=t^{2}+\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,Ht-n(H^{2}+c).

Using that
\|\Phi\|^{2}=S-nH^{2}
, our pinching assumption
S\leq a(n,p,H,c)
turns out to be equivalent to
\|\Phi\|\leq r(n,p,H,c)
.
Since

0\leq r(n,1,H,c)\leq\cdots\leq r(n,\left[n/2\right],H,c),

we have
\|\Phi\|\leq r(n,i,H,c),
or equivalently

P(\|\Phi\|;i,H,c)\leq 0\;\;\text{for each}\;\;p\leq i\leq n/2.

Proposition
16
implies that

\mathcal{B}^{[i]}\geq 0
for all
p\leq i\leq n/2
. Now, part (i) of the theorem follows from Proposition

15
(i) and the Poincaré duality, while part (ii) follows from Propositions
15
(ii)
and
16
. Part (iii) follows from part (ii) and Proposition
15
(iii).

Proof of Theorem
2
.
Let
\omega\in\mathscr{H}^{p}(M^{n})
be a harmonic
form of constant length. Equation (
14
) yields

{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}=-\|\nabla\omega\|^{2}\leq 0.

From Proposition
16
, it follows that

n(H^{2}+c)-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,H\|\Phi\|-\|\Phi\|^{2}\leq 0.

Hence we have
\|\Phi\|\geq r(n,p,H,c)
, where
r(n,p,H,c)
is the largest root of the polynomial (
20
).
Since
\|\Phi\|^{2}=S-nH^{2}
, using (

) we obtain

.

We recall that an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+k}
, into a space form of constant sectional
curvature
c
, is said to reduce codimension to
m<k
if there exists a totally geodesic submanifold

\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+k}
such that
f(M)\subset\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+m}
. We need the following proposition
(a related result was given in
[
saco12
]
for hypersurfaces in spheres).

Proposition 17
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{Q}_{c}^{n+k},n\geq 3
, be an isometric immersion into a nonflat space form that
satisfies (
1
) for an integer
1\leq p<n/2
and
H^{2}+c\geq 0
. Suppose
that
M^{n}
supports a nontrivial harmonic
p
-form (in particular, a parallel
p
-form) with constant

on
M^{n}
, then the codimension of
f
is reduced to one. Moreover, the hypersurface

has at most two distinct principal curvatures
\lambda
and
\mu

of multiplicities
p
and
n-p
, respectively, such that

\lambda\mu+c=0.

Proof:
Let
\omega\in{\mathscr{H}}^{p}(M^{n})
be a nontrivial harmonic
p
-form of constant length.
Equation (
14
) yields
{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[p]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}\leq 0

2

n(H^{2}+c)-\frac{n(n-2p)}{\sqrt{np(n-p)}}\,H\|\Phi\|-\|\Phi\|^{2}=0.

\alpha_{f}(X,Y)={\langle}A_{\xi}X,Y{\rangle}\xi\;\;\text{for all}\;\;X,Y\in TM,

and

and

, respectively. The Gauss equation is

{\langle}\mathcal{B}^{[1]}\omega,\omega{\rangle}=0
,
at each point there is tangent direction where the Ricci curvature of
M^{n}
vanishes. The Gauss
equation then implies that
\lambda=\mu=1/\lambda
, and this is again a contradiction.

Thus
b_{p}(M^{n})=0
. For each
p<i\leq n/2
we have

S\leq a(n,p,H,-1)\leq a(n,i,H,-1).

Repeating the above argument, we obtain
b_{i}(M^{n})=0
for every
p<i\leq n/2
.

5.
Integral bound for the Bochner operator

For the proof of Theorem
11
, we need to recall some well known facts
on the total curvature and how Morse theory provides restrictions on the Betti numbers.
Let
f\colon M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
be an isometric immersion of a compact Riemannian manifold into
the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
equipped with the usual inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
The unit normal bundle is defined by

UN_{f}=\left\{(x,\eta)\in N_{f}M:\|\eta\|=1\right\}.

The
generalized Gauss map

\nu\colon UN_{f}\to\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
is
given by
\nu(x,\eta)=\eta
. For each
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
, we consider the height function

h_{u}
defined by
h_{u}(x)=\langle f(x),u\rangle,\,x\in M^{n}
.
Since
h_{u}
has a degenerate critical point if and only if
u
is a critical point of
\nu
, by Sard’s theorem
there exists a subset
E\subset\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
of measure zero such that
h_{u}
is a Morse function for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}\smallsetminus E
. We denote by
\mu_{i}(u)
the number of critical
points of
h_{u}
of index
i
for each
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}\smallsetminus E
and set
\mu_{i}(u)=0

for every
u\in E
. Following Kuiper
[
Kuiper
]
, we define the
total curvature of index
i
of
f
by

\tau_{i}(f)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}}\mu_{i}(u)dS,

where
dS
denotes the volume element of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
.
From the weak Morse inequalities (cf.
[
Milnor63
]
), we have

\mu_{i}(u)\geq b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F})\>\>\text{for all}\;\;u\in\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}\smallsetminus E.

By integrating over
\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
, we obtain

(23)

\tau_{i}(f)\geq b_{i}(M^{n};\mathbb{F}).

There is a natural volume element
d\Sigma
on the unit normal bundle
UN_{f}
. In fact, if
dV
is a

(k-1)
-form on
UN_{f}
such that its restriction to a fiber of the unit normal bundle at
(x,\eta)

is the volume element of the unit
(k-1)
-sphere of the normal space of
f
at
x
,
then
d\Sigma=dM\wedge dV
, where
dM
is the volume element of
M^{n}
.
Shiohama and Xu
[
SX
, p. 381]
proved that

(24)

\int_{U^{i}N_{f}}\left|\mathrm{det}A_{\eta}\right|d\Sigma=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}}\mu_{i}(u)dS,

where
U^{i}N_{f}
is the subset of the unit normal bundle of
f
defined by

U^{i}N_{f}=\left\{(x,\eta)\in UN_{f}:\mathrm{Index}\,A_{\eta}=i\right\},\;\;0\leq i\leq n.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Let
f\colon M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
be an isometric immersion with second fundamental
form
\alpha_{f}
and shape operator
A_{\eta}
with respect to
\eta
, where
(x,\eta)\in UN_{f}
.
Using (
15
), (
17
) and Proposition
14
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\varrho_{p}-F_{n,p}(H,\|\Phi\|)\big|^{n/2}\geq(\varepsilon(n,k,p))^{n/2}\int_{\Lambda_{p}(\alpha_{f}(x))}\left|\det A_{\eta}\right|dV_{\eta}

for every point
x\in M^{n}
. Integrating over
M^{n}
and using (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}\big|\varrho_{p}-F_{n,p}(H,\|\Phi\|)\big|^{n/2}dM\geq\left(\varepsilon(n,k,p)\right)^{n/2}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1})\sum_{i=p+1}^{n-p-1}\tau_{i}(f).

Using (
23
) it follows that

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{M^{n}}\big|\varrho_{p}-F_{n,p}(H,\|\Phi\|)\big|^{n/2}dM\!\!\!

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!c(n,k,p)\sum_{i=p+1}^{n-p-1}\tau_{i}(f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!c(n,k,p)\sum_{i=p+1}^{n-p-1}b_{i}(M;\mathbb{F}),

where
c(n,k,p)=\left(\varepsilon(n,k,p)\right)^{n/2}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1})
.

Now, suppose that

\int_{M^{n}}\big|\varrho_{p}-F_{n,p}(H,\|\Phi\|)\big|^{n/2}dM<c(n,p,k).

It follows from
(\ref{57667})
that

\sum_{i=p+1}^{n-p-1}\tau_{i}(f)<1.

Thus there exists

u\in\mathbb{S}^{n+k-1}
such that the height function
h_{u}
is a Morse function whose number of
critical points of index
i
satisfies
\mu_{i}(u)=0
for every
p+1\leq i\leq n-p-1
. By Morse theory,
the manifold
M^{n}
has the homotopy type of a CW-complex with no cells of dimension

p+1\leq i\leq n-p-1
.

If
p=1
, there are no
2
-cells and thus by the cellular approximation theorem the inclusion
of the
1
-skeleton
\mathrm{X}^{(1)}\hookrightarrow M^{n}
induces isomorphism between
the fundamental groups. Therefore,
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is a free group on

b_{1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
elements and
H_{1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
is a free abelian group on
b_{1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
generators.
If
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is finite, then
\pi_{1}(M^{n})=0
and hence
H_{1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})=0
.
From Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem, it follows that
H_{n-1}(M^{n};\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})=0
.
Thus
M^{n}
is a simply connected homology sphere and hence a homotopy sphere.
By the generalized Poincaré conjecture (Smale
n\geq 5
, Freedman
n=4
)
M^{n}
is
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

6.
Concluding remarks

(i) Since homology with real coefficients encodes less topological information that homology with integers ones, it should be interesting to know whether our homology vanishing results hold for homology with integers coefficients.

(ii) It is worth noticing that the sharp estimate of the Bochner operator in terms of the second fundamental
form of a submanifold (see Proposition
16
) can be used to obtain homology vanishing results for submanifolds in
different context. For instance, one can strengthen the results in
[
CalVit
]
, concerning minimal
submanifolds in balls with free boundary, by dropping the assumption therein on the flatness
of the normal bundle.

(iii) DeTurck and Ziller
[
dtz
]
obtained minimal isometric embeddings of
spherical space forms into spheres. None of these minimal submanifolds satisfies the condition
S\leq n
.
In view of Theorem
4
, this observation raises the question whether any minimal isometric immersion of spherical space forms into spheres is totally geodesic provided that
S\leq n
.

Introduction

Given a graph
G
and a subgraph
H
, let
g(G,H)
be the minimum number of colors needed to edge-color
G
so that every copy of
H
sees some color an odd number of times. The problem of determining
g(G,H)
is a natural question motivated by recent work of Alon
[
1
]
introducing the related notion of
graph-codes
.

Let
V=[n]
and let

{\cal H}
be a family of graphs on the set of vertices
[n]

which is closed under
isomorphism.
A collection of graphs
{\cal F}
on
[n]
is called
an
{\cal H}
-code
if it contains no two members whose symmetric difference is a graph in
{\cal H}
. For the special case that
{\cal H}
contains all copies
of a single graph
H
on
[n]
this is called an
H
-code.
Let
D_{{\cal H}}(n)
denote the maximum possible cardinality of an
{\cal H}
-code, and let

d_{{\cal H}}(n)=\frac{D_{{\cal H}}(n)}{2^{n\choose 2}}

be the maximum possible fraction of the total number of graphs
on
[n]
in an
{\cal H}
-code. If
{\cal H}
consists of all graphs
isomorphic to one graph
H
, denote
d_{{\cal H}}(n)
by
d_{H}(n)
.

In the case where
{\cal H}
consists of all graphs with independence number at most 2, a result of Ellis, Filmus
and Friedgut
[
10
]
shows that
d_{{\cal H}}(n)=1/8
for
n\geq 3
. Berger and Zhao
[
5
]
proved the analogous result for the family
{\cal H}
of graphs with independence number at most 3, showing that
d_{{\cal H}}(n)=1/64
for all
n\geq 4
. In
[
2
]
, Alon, Gujgiczer, Körner, Milojević, and Simonyi studied
D_{{\cal H}}(n)
and
d_{{\cal H}}(n)
for several families
{\cal H}
, such as disconnected graphs, graphs that are not 2-connected, non-Hamiltonian graphs, graphs that contain or do not contain a spanning star, graphs that contain an induced or non-induced copy of a fixed graph
T
, and graphs that do not contain such a subgraph. Alon
[
1
]
also studied the cases for cliques, stars, and matchings.

In
[
1
]
, Alon mentions that the case
{\cal H}={\cal K}
, where
{\cal K}
is the family of all cliques, is of particular interest. This case is motivated by a conjecture of Gowers (see
[
14
,
1
]
). Further, he comments that
d_{K_{4}}(n)\geq\frac{1}{n^{o(1)}}.
This result follows from the existence of an edge-coloring of
K_{n}
by
n^{o(1)}
colors with no copy of
K_{4}
in which every color appears an even number of times; that is,
g(K_{n},K_{4})\leq n^{o(1)}
. A coloring with this property was given in
[
7
]
, modifying constructions in
[
8
,
16
]
.
In our first result, we show that the same coloring has the property that there exists no copy of
K_{5}
in which every color appears an even number of times. We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
g(K_{n},K_{5})\leq n^{o(1)}
.

This, together with a similar argument to that in
[
1
]
, implies the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

We have

d_{K_{5}}(n)\geq\frac{1}{n^{o(1)}}.

Very recently, Ge, Xu, and Zhang
[
12
]
also independently obtained this result using a similar method. They further note that
g(K_{n},K_{5})\geq\Omega(\log n)
.

As is observed in
[
1
]
, if every member of
{\cal H}
has an odd number of edges then
d_{{\cal H}}(n)\geq\frac{1}{2}
, as the family of all graphs on
[n]
with an even number of edges forms an
{\cal H}
-code.
Thus, when considering cliques, the next interesting case is the case of
H=K_{8}
. We do not know if the same coloring, or some generalization of it, has the property that there exists no copy of
K_{8}
in which every color appears an even number of times. As our method relies on case analysis, it will be hard to generalize it to the case of
K_{8}
.

An upper bound on
g(K_{n},K_{p})
for larger
p
follows from recent work of Bennett, Delcourt, Li, and Postle
[
3
]
on the
generalized Ramsey number

f(n,p,q)
, that is, the minimum number of colors needed to color
E(K_{n})
so that every copy of
K_{p}
sees at least
q
colors. Until recently, the best upper bound on
f(n,p,q)
for general
p,q
was the original bound of Erdős and Gyárfás
[
11
]
obtained using the Lovász Local Lemma. Bennett, Dudek, and English
[
4
]
used a random greedy process to improve this bound by a logarithmic factor for values of
q
and
p
with
q\leq(p^{2}-26p+55)/4
. In
[
3
]
, their result is extended to all values of
p
and
q
except at the values
q=\binom{p}{2}-p+2
and
q=\binom{p}{2}-\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor+2
, where the local lemma bound is known to be tight.

Theorem 1.3

(Bennett, Delcourt, Li, Postle
[
3
]
)
.

For fixed positive integers
p,q
with
p-2
not divisible by
\binom{p}{2}-q+1
, we have

f(n,p,q)=O\left(\left(\frac{n^{p-2}}{\log n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\binom{p}{2}-q+1}}\right).

In fact, Theorem
1.3
is generalized in
[
3
]
to give an analogous upper bound in a list-coloring setting for any subgraph (not only cliques) and in hypergraphs with higher uniformity. Note that Theorem
1.3
with
q=\binom{p}{2}/2+1
implies that

g(K_{n},K_{p})=O\left(\left(\frac{n^{p-2}}{\log n}\right)^{2/\binom{p}{2}}\right).

Indeed, any
(p,\frac{1}{2}\binom{p}{2}+1)
-coloring of
K_{n}
has the stronger condition that some color must appear exactly once in each
p
-clique.

Our second result focuses on the question of determining
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})
. In order to prove an upper bound, we will construct a coloring using the “forbidden submatching method” recently introduced by Delcourt and Postle
[
9
]
and independently by Glock, Joos, Kim, Kühn, and Lichev
[
13
]
as “conflict-free hypergraph matchings.” In
[
15
]
, Joos and Mubayi use the variant of the method from
[
13
]
along with a probabilistic argument to show that
f(K_{n,n},C_{4},3)=\frac{2}{3}n+o(n)
. Note that this implies
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})\leq\frac{2}{3}n+o(n)
.
We use a similar approach to show the stronger upper bound of
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})\leq\frac{1}{2}n+o(n)
. As such, combined with a short proof that
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})>n/2
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

We have
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})=\frac{1}{2}n+o(n).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section

1.4
, namely the theorem on forbidden submatchings given in
[

]
. Finally, in Section

1.4
.

Forbidden submatching method

We will use the simplified version presented in
[
15
]
of the conflict-free hypergraph matching theorem from
[
13
]
to prove Theorem
1.4
. In order to state this theorem, we will need to introduce some terminology and notation.

Given a hypergraph
{\cal H}
and a vertex

, its
degree

\deg_{{\cal H}}(v)

is the number of edges in
{\cal H}
containing
v
. The maximum degree and minimum degree of
{\cal H}
are denoted by
\Delta({\cal H})
and
\delta({\cal H})
, respectively. For
j\geq 2
,
\Delta_{j}({\cal H})
denotes the maximum number of edges in
{\cal H}
which contain a particular set of
j
vertices, over all such sets.

In addition, for a (not necessarily uniform) hypergraph
{\cal C}
and an integer
k
, let
{\cal C}^{(k)}
be the set of edges in
{\cal C}
of size
k
. For a vertex
u\in V({\cal C})
, let
{\cal C}_{u}
denote the hypergraph
\{C\backslash\{u\}\mid C\in E({\cal C}),u\in C\}
.

Given a hypergraph
{\cal H}
, a hypergraph
{\cal C}
is a
conflict system
for
{\cal H}
if
V({\cal C})=E({\cal H})
. A set of edges
E\subset{\cal H}
is
{\cal C}
-free
if
E
contains no subset
C\in{\cal C}
. Given integers

,
\ell\geq 3
, and
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, we say
{\cal C}
is
(d,\ell,{\varepsilon})
-bounded
if
{\cal C}
satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

3\leq|C|\leq\ell
for all
C\in{\cal C}
;

(C2)

\Delta({\cal C}^{(j)})\leq\ell d^{j-1}
for all
3\leq j\leq\ell
;

(C3)

\Delta_{j^{\prime}}({\cal C}^{(j)})\leq d^{j-j^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}}
for all
2\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq\ell
.

Finally, given a
(d,\ell,{\varepsilon})
-bounded conflict system
{\cal C}
for a hypergraph
{\cal H}
, we will define a type of weight function which can be used to guarantee that the almost-perfect matching given by Theorem
3.1
below satisfies certain quasirandom properties. We say a function
w:\binom{{\cal H}}{j}\rightarrow[0,\ell]
for

is a
test function
for
{\cal H}
if
w(E)=0
whenever
E\in\binom{{\cal H}}{j}
is not a matching, and we say
w
is
j
-uniform
. For a function
w:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a finite set
X\subset A
, let
w(X):=\sum_{x\in X}w(x)
. If
w
is a
j
-uniform test function, then for each
E\subset{\cal H}
, let
w(E)=w(\binom{E}{j})
. Given
j,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\varepsilon}>0
, and a conflict system
{\cal C}
for hypergraph
{\cal H}
, we say a
j
-uniform test function

for
{\cal H}
is
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable
if

satisfies the following conditions:

(W1)

w({\cal H})\geq d^{j+{\varepsilon}}
;

(W2)

w(\{{E\in\binom{{\cal H}}{j}:E\supseteq E^{\prime}\})\leq w({\cal H}})/d^{j^{\prime}+{\varepsilon}}
for all
j^{\prime}\in[j-1]
and
E^{\prime}\in\binom{{\cal H}}{j^{\prime}}

(W3)

|({\cal C}_{e})^{(j^{\prime})}\cap({\cal C}_{f})^{(j^{\prime})}|\leq d^{j^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}}
for all
e,f\in{\cal H}
with
w(\{E\in\binom{{\cal H}}{j}:e,f\in E\})>0
and all
j^{\prime}\in[\ell-1]
;

(W4)

w(E)=0
for all
E\in\binom{{\cal H}}{j}
that are not
{\cal C}
-free.

since there are

choices for
e^{\prime}
and
O(n^{4})
choices for
f^{\prime}
to complete the conflict. Finally,
\Delta_{3}({\cal C}^{(4)})<d^{1-{\varepsilon}}
, because given three vertices
e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\in V({\cal C})
, there are
O(n^{4})
choices for
f^{\prime}
to complete a conflict. Therefore,
{\cal C}
is a
(d,O(1),{\varepsilon})
-bounded conflict system for
{\cal H}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
.

Note that we could apply Theorem
3.1
at this point to obtain a conflict-free matching
M
in
{\cal H}
which would correspond to a coloring of a subgraph
F
of
K_{n,n}
satisfying properties (1) and (2) of Theorem
4.1
.
In order to obtain a coloring which also satisfies properties (3) and (4)
of Theorem
4.1
, we now introduce appropriate test functions.

First, we define a set of 1-uniform test functions as in
[
15
]
. For each
v\in X\cup Y
, let
S_{v}\subset U
be the set of edges incident to
v
in
K_{n,n}
. Let
w_{v}:E({\cal H})\rightarrow\{0,2\}
be the weight function which assigns to each edge
e_{i}\in E({\cal H})
the size of its intersection with
S_{v}
.
Then we have

w_{v}({\cal H})=\sum_{e_{i}\in{\cal H}}w_{v}(e_{i})=\sum_{f\in S_{v}}\deg_{{\cal H}}(f)=nd-O(n^{5})\geq d^{1+{\varepsilon}},

satisfying condition (W1). In addition, conditions (W2)-(W4) are trivially satisfied since
w_{v}
is 1-uniform, so
w_{v}
is a
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable test function for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
and
v\in X\cup Y
.

Let
\delta<{\varepsilon}^{3}\log_{n}(\frac{1}{2}n^{5})
. Note that applying Theorem
3.1
with these test functions would yield a
{\cal C}
-free matching
M\subset{\cal H}
such that for each
v\in X\cup Y
,

w_{v}(M)>(1-d^{-{\varepsilon}^{3}})d^{-1}w_{v}({\cal H})>(1-n^{-\delta})n.

Thus, for each
v\in X\cup Y
, there are at most
n^{1-\delta}
edges in
K_{n,n}
incident to
v
which do not belong to any edge in
M
. Hence, property (3) is satisfied, as the graph
L
containing the uncolored edges has maximum degree
\Delta(L)\leq n^{1-\delta}
.

In order to guarantee that property (4) is also satisfied, we will define four more types of test functions. The first two will help us to show that for each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, there are at most
n^{1-\delta}
edges
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(L)
with
x^{\prime}\in X\backslash\{x\}
and
y^{\prime}\in Y\backslash\{y\}
such that
xy^{\prime}
and
yx^{\prime}
are in distinct edges of the same color in the matching
M\subset{\cal H}
. The next two will help us to show that for each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, there are at most
n^{1-\delta}
edges in the matching that contain both
x
and
y
, and hence at most
3n^{1-\delta}
graph edges

x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(L)
with
x^{\prime}\in X\backslash\{x\}
and
y^{\prime}\in Y\backslash\{y\}
such that
xy
and
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
are contained in the same edge of the matching.

For each pair of vertices
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, let

{\cal P}_{x,y}=\left\{\{e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i}\}:i\in\left[\frac{n}{2}\right],V(e)\cap V(e^{\prime})=\emptyset,x\in V(e),y\in V(e^{\prime})\right\}.

Note that

|{\cal P}_{x,y}|=\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}n^{5}\cdot\frac{1}{2}n^{5}\pm O(n^{10})=\frac{1}{8}n^{11}\pm O(n^{10})>d^{2+{\varepsilon}}.

For each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, define
w_{x,y}
to be the indicator weight function for the pairs in
{\cal P}_{x,y}
. Since each pair in
{\cal P}_{x,y}
is a matching of size 2 in
{\cal H}
,
w_{x,y}
is a 2-uniform test function.
We will show that
w_{x,y}
is
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
, but first we will define a set of 3-uniform test functions.

For each pair of vertices
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, let

{\cal T}_{x,y}=\left\{\{e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i},f_{j}\}:\{e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i}\}\in{\cal P}_{x,y},j\in\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]\backslash\{i\},|V(f)\cap V(e)\cap Y|=1,|V(f)\cap V(e^{\prime})\cap X|=1\right\}.

Thus, the triples in
{\cal T}_{x,y}
are matchings of size 3 in
{\cal H}
. Note that

|{\cal T}_{x,y}|=9(d\pm O(n^{4}))|{\cal P}_{x,y}|.

Similarly to above, define for each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
the function
w^{\prime}_{x,y}
to be the indicator weight function for triples in
{\cal T}_{x,y}
. This is a 3-uniform test function for
{\cal H}
.

Assuming that these test functions
w_{x,y}
and
w^{\prime}_{x,y}
are
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable, we can apply Theorem
3.1
to obtain a matching
M
in
{\cal H}
such that

w_{x,y}(M)=\left|\binom{M}{2}\cap{\cal P}_{x,y}\right|\leq(1+d^{-{\varepsilon}^{3}})d^{-2}|{\cal P}_{x,y}|\leq(1+n^{-2\delta})\frac{n}{2}

and

w^{\prime}_{x,y}(M)=\left|\binom{M}{3}\cap{\cal T}_{x,y}\right|\geq(1-d^{-{\varepsilon}^{3}})d^{-3}|{\cal T}_{x,y}|\geq(1-n^{-2\delta})\frac{9n}{2}.

Note that each edge
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(L)
with
xy^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}y
appearing in distinct edges of
{\cal H}
of the same color corresponds to an edge of
{\cal H}
in a triple of
{\cal T}_{x,y}
extending some pair from
{\cal P}_{x,y}
. Thus, we can bound the number of such
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
as follows:

9\left|\binom{M}{2}\cap{\cal P}_{x,y}\right|-\left|\binom{M}{3}\cap{\cal T}_{x,y}\right|\leq n^{1-\delta}.

Therefore, in order to prove the first case of property (4), we must verify that
w_{x,y}
and
w^{\prime}_{x,y}
are
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
. For this we must check conditions (W1)-(W4) for both functions.

Condition (W1) for
w_{x,y}
is satisfied since
w_{x,y}({\cal H})=|{\cal P}_{x,y}|>d^{2+{\varepsilon}}
. To check condition (W2), fix an edge
e_{i}\in{\cal H}
. The number of edges
e^{\prime}_{i}
which form a pair in
{\cal P}_{x,y}
with
e_{i}
is at most
O(n^{5})<n^{11}/d^{1+{\varepsilon}}
. For condition (W3), note that the only pairs of edges
\{e,f\}\in\binom{{\cal H}}{2}
for which
w_{x,y}(\{e,f\})>0
are pairs
\{e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i}\}
with
x\in e_{i}
and
y\in e^{\prime}_{i}
. Given such a pair, the number of triples of edges in
{\cal H}
which can form a conflict with
e_{i}
or
e^{\prime}_{i}
is at most
O(n\cdot n^{6}\cdot n^{3}\cdot n^{3})=O(n^{13})<d^{3-{\varepsilon}}.
Finally, condition (W4) is vacuously true, so
w_{x,y}
is
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable.

We now verify that
w^{\prime}_{x,y}
is
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
as well. Condition (W1) holds because
w^{\prime}_{x,y}({\cal H})=|{\cal T}_{x,y}|=\Theta(n^{16})>d^{3+{\varepsilon}}
. To see that condition (W2) holds, fix
e_{i}\in{\cal H}
. There are
O(n^{11})<w^{\prime}_{x,y}({\cal H})/d^{1+{\varepsilon}}
triples in
{\cal T}_{x,y}
containing
e_{i}
since we must either pick a second color
j
, six more vertices for
e_{i}^{\prime}
, and four more vertices for
f_{j}
to form a triple
\{e_{i},e_{i}^{\prime},f_{j}\}
or a second color
j
, five more vertices for
f_{j}
, and five more vertices for
f_{j}^{\prime}
to form a triple
\{e_{i},f_{j},f_{j}^{\prime}\}
. And, for each pair of edges
\{e_{i},e_{i}^{\prime}\}
or
\{e_{i},f_{j}\}
in
{\cal H}
, there are
O(n^{5})<w^{\prime}_{x,y}({\cal H})/d^{2+{\varepsilon}}
triples containing this pair.
For condition (W3), fix a pair of edges
\{e,f\}\in\binom{{\cal H}}{2}
which are in at least one triple together in
{\cal T}_{x,y}
. Either we have
\{e,f\}=\{e_{i},e^{\prime}_{i}\}
or
\{e,f\}=\{e_{i},f_{j}\}
. In the first case, as above, there are
O(n^{13})
triples of edges in
{\cal H}
which form a conflict with either
e_{i}
or
e^{\prime}_{i}
. In the second case, there are at most
O(n^{14})<d^{3-{\varepsilon}}
conflicts containing
e_{i}
which use only the colors
i
and
j
, and this bounds the number of triples which form a conflict with either
e_{i}
or
f_{j}
.
Thus, condition (W3) holds. Finally, condition (W4) is vacuously true.

We now define for each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
another pair of test functions to guarantee that there are at most
n^{1-\delta}
edges in the matching
M\in{\cal H}
which contain both
x
and
y
. Set

{\cal S}_{x}=\left\{e_{i}:i\in\left[\frac{n}{2}\right],x\in V(e)\right\}

and

{\cal D}_{x,y}=\left\{e_{i}:i\in\left[\frac{n}{2}\right],x\in V(e),y\notin V(e)\right\}.

Let
u_{x}
and
u^{\prime}_{x,y}
be the indicator functions for
{\cal S}_{x}
and
{\cal D}_{x,y}
, respectively. Note that
u_{x}({\cal H})=|{\cal S}_{x}|=\frac{1}{2}n\cdot\frac{1}{2}n^{5}\pm O(n^{5})
, and similarly
u^{\prime}_{x,y}=|{\cal D}_{x,y}|=\frac{1}{4}n^{6}\pm O(n^{5})
. Thus, property (W1) holds for both
u_{x}
and
u^{\prime}_{x,y}
. Furthermore, (W2)-(W4) are vacuously true since these are 1-uniform test functions, hence both are
(d,{\varepsilon},{\cal C})
-trackable for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
.

Applying Theorem
3.1
with all of these test functions gives a
{\cal C}
-free matching
M\subset{\cal H}
such that for each
x\in X
,

u_{x}(M)\leq(1+d^{-{\varepsilon}^{3}})d^{-1}u_{x}({\cal H})\leq(1+n^{-2\delta})\frac{n}{2},

and for each
y\in Y
,

u^{\prime}_{x,y}(M)\geq(1-d^{-{\varepsilon}^{3}})d^{-1}|{\cal D}_{x,y}|\geq(1-n^{-2\delta})\frac{n}{2}.

Therefore, the number of edges in
M
which contain both
x
and
y
is at most
u_{x}(M)-u^{\prime}_{x,y}(M)\leq n^{1-\delta}
, as desired. Thus, the coloring obtained from
M
satisfies property (4).
∎

We now proceed with the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Applying Theorem
4.1
with
2\delta
in place of
\delta
, we obtain a partial coloring of
K_{n,n}
using at most
n/2
colors for which the uncolored subgraph
L
of
K_{n,n}
has maximum degree at most
n^{1-2\delta}
. To color these remaining edges, we randomly color with a new set
P
of
k=n^{1-\delta}
colors, with each edge in
L
receiving a color in
P
with equal probability
1/k
, independently of the other edges. We will show using the symmetric local lemma that such a coloring exists which creates no 2-colored
C_{4}
, and hence,
g(K_{n,n},C_{4})\leq n/2+n^{1-\delta}
.

To this end, we define three types of “bad events,” without which every
C_{4}
in our coloring will contain some color exactly once. First, for each pair of adjacent edges
e,f\in E(L)
and each color
i\in P
, let
A_{e,f,i}
be the event that
e
and
f
receive the same color
i
. Note that
\mathbb{P}[A_{e,f,i}]=k^{-2}
. Next, for each 4-cycle
D
in
L
, let
B_{D}
be the event that
D
is properly-colored with two colors from
P
. Then
\mathbb{P}[B_{D}]\leq 2k^{-2}
. Finally, for each color
i\in P
and each 4-cycle
D=xyx^{\prime}y^{\prime}
in
K_{n,n}
with
xy,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(L)
and
xy^{\prime},x^{\prime}y\in E(F)
where
xy^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}y
received the same color in the first coloring, let
C_{D,i}
be the event that
xy
and
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
both receive color
i
. Then
\mathbb{P}[C_{D,i}]=k^{-2}
. Let
{\cal E}
be the collection of all bad events of the types
A_{e,f,i}
,
B_{D}
, and
C_{D,i}
.

Fix an event
E\in{\cal E}
. Note that
E
is mutually independent from the set of all events
E^{\prime}
which do not involve any of the same edges as
E
in
L
. We call such events edge-disjoint from
E
. There are at most
8\Delta(L)k\leq 8k^{2}n^{-\delta}
events of the form
A_{e,f,i}
which are not edge-disjoint from
E
. In addition, there are at most
4(\Delta(L))^{2}\leq k^{2}n^{-\delta}
events
B_{D}
and, by property (4) of Theorem
4.1
, at most
4(4n^{1-2\delta})k\leq 16k^{2}n^{-\delta}
events
C_{D,i}
which are not edge-disjoint from
E
. Thus, in total,
E
is mutually independent of all but at most
25k^{2}n^{-\delta}
events in
{\cal E}
. By the local lemma, since
25k^{2}n^{-\delta}\cdot 2k^{-2}\leq 1/4
, there is a coloring of
L
which contains none of the events in
{\cal E}
, as desired.
∎

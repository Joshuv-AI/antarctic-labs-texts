DESY 23-001 UWThPh-2023-1 Investigation of the scale dependence in the MSR and MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} top quark mass schemes for the t ​ t ¯ {\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}} invariant mass differential cross section using LHC data
Toni Mäkelä, André H. Hoang, Katerina Lipka, Sven-Olaf Moch

Abstract
The computation of the single-differential top quark-antiquark pair ( t ​ t ¯ {\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}} ) production cross section at NLO in the fixed-order expansion is examined consistently using the MSR and MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} short-distance top quark mass schemes. A thorough investigation of the dependence of different regions of the t ​ t ¯ {\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}} invariant mass spectrum on the renormalization scales R R and μ m \mu_{m} of the MSR mass m t MSR ​ ( R ) m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R) and MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} mass m ¯ t ​ ( μ m ) \overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m}) , respectively, is carried out. We demonstrate that a scale choice of R ∼ 80 R\sim 80 GeV is important for the stability of the cross-section predictions for the low t ​ t ¯ {\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}} invariant mass range, which is important for a reliable extraction of the top quark mass. Furthermore, a choice of semi-dynamical renormalization and factorization scales is preferred. These findings are expected to remain valid once non-relativistic quasi-bound state effects are included in the low invariant mass region.

Introduction

The top quark mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}
is a fundamental parameter of the Standard Model and
has an important role in many predictions, both directly and via higher-order
corrections.
For instance, together with the values of the strong coupling constant

\alpha_{s}
and the mass of the Higgs boson, it determines the stability of the
electroweak vacuum
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Yet, quark masses are formal parameters of the Standard Model QCD Lagrangian and depend on the adopted renormalization scheme. The frequently used top quark pole mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}
is based on the concept of an on-shell observable particle and entails that real and virtual self-energy radiation can be resolved at arbitrarily small energy scales. While the picture of an on-shell top quark allows for consistent cross section field theory computations, it is not physical because the top quark is a colored object and decays. This results in a sizeable linear infrared (IR) sensitivity of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}

of the order of the QCD scale
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}

[
5
,
6
,
7
]
1
1

1

We note that linear IR sensitivities arise in cross sections whenever cuts on soft radiation are imposed, see e.g. Ref.
[
8
]
. These are associated to physical nonperturbative corrections in contrast to the pole mass, where the IR sensitivity arises purely from the choice of scheme
[
9
]
.
which leads to the pole mass renormalon problem at high orders in perturbation theory.
Quark masses defined in so-called short-distance mass schemes such
as the modified minimal subtraction (
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
) scheme
[
10
,
11
]

mass
\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})
, or the MSR scheme
[
12
,
13
]
mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
,
do not have this issue, and their renormalization scales
\mu_{m}
and
R
, respectively, act as a finite resolution scale. This means that real and virtual self-energy radiation are treated inclusively for the scales below
\mu_{m}
and
R
.
The absence of the
{\cal O}(\Lambda_{\rm QCD})
renormalon problem, together with the additional freedom to adopt suitable choices for
\mu_{m}
and
R
, can be very useful to achieve higher precision concerning the
m_{\mathrm{t}}
dependence in top mass sensitive observables. In particular, the freedom of scheme and scale choice is important for predictions at lower orders, since it leads to a systematic absorption of sizeable corrections, not originating from the pole mass renormalon, into the quark mass parameter.

A well-known example is the highly
m_{\mathrm{t}}
-sensitive
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
cross section at the production threshold in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, where switching from the pole mass scheme to the threshold mass schemes such as the PS (at the scale of around
25
GeV) or the 1S mass schemes can minimize the large

{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
quasi-bound state corrections at any order, since these mass schemes encode a sizeable fraction of the binding energy corrections
\Delta E\sim m_{\mathrm{t}}\alpha_{s}^{2}
into the mass value
[
14
]
. In the case of the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
quasi-bound state, the scale choice for the PS mass is related to the fact that these
m_{\mathrm{t}}
-sensitive bound state corrections arise from physical dynamical scales of the order of the inverse Bohr radius
\langle 1/r_{B}\rangle\sim m_{\mathrm{t}}\alpha_{s}\sim 25
GeV, and the MSR mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(25~\mbox{GeV})
is a suitable choice as well
[
13
]
. Thus, the scale dependence of

\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})
and
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
allows to properly adapt to these dynamical
scales of the top mass sensitivity for an observable under consideration. The respective renormalization group
equations (RGEs) and matching relations provide the tool to unambiguously relate the
m_{\mathrm{t}}
values extracted at different dynamical scales. This concept is well known for the running strong coupling
\alpha_{s}
and applies to the quark masses as well.

In this work, the dependence of the invariant mass of the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

R

15

the “indirect”

\alpha_{s}^{(5)}

\alpha_{s}^{(6)}

\displaystyle=54.161+21.776L+9.2026L^{2}+1.7940L^{3}\,,

\displaystyle=4.8387\,,

Non-relativistic quasi-bound state QCD corrections are important for the region
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\sim 340
-
360
GeV,
where the strongest top quark mass sensitivity arises in the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution.
In this threshold region the produced top
quarks attain small non-relativistic velocities
v\ll 1
in the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

center-of-mass frame, and the dynamics of
the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
system are hence governed by the mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}
, the relative
momentum
m_{\mathrm{t}}v
, and the kinetic energy
m_{\mathrm{t}}v^{2}
of the top quark. Since
m_{\mathrm{t}}\gg m_{\mathrm{t}}v\gg m_{\mathrm{t}}v^{2}
, the appearance of ratios involving the masses, momenta
and kinetic energy of the top quark renders the standard fixed-order expansion
in powers of
\alpha_{s}
unreliable in this
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
range. The most pronounced quasi-bound state effects arise from the Coulomb corrections due to the exchange of gluons between the produced
\mathrm{t}
and
\overline{\mathrm{t}}
yielding a dependence of the
prediction on the ratio
m_{\mathrm{t}}/(m_{\mathrm{t}}v)
. This leads to a singular

(\alpha_{s}/v)^{n}
behavior in the fixed-order perturbative QCD correction at

n

[
52
]
.
The quasi-bound state effects have been considered in
Refs.
[

,

]
, and more recently again

[

{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

description of the lowest

bin in the region between
300

GeV.
This latter aspect is actually better described
by the fixed-order predictions for stable top quarks where the rate vanishes
identically for
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<2m_{\mathrm{t}}
(for a correct top mass scheme choice as discussed below).
Furthermore, a systematic treatment of the intermediate region, where the
non-relativistic and relativistic calculations need to be matched,
is currently not available with a reliable matching error estimate.
3

3

Such a treatment is available only for top quark production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, see Ref.
[

]
.

We also mention that for the electroweak corrections different scheme choices
for the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass are available related to the definition of the vacuum
expectation value
[
43
,
44
]
. Their effects concerning the
MSR mass and their impact on the use of different mass schemes in experimental
observables are unknown.
Overall, there is currently no complete and reliable theory prediction
for the low
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution available for experimental analysis.
For the study of the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
differential cross section as a function of
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
and its dependence on the MSR mass scale
R
,
the NLO fixed order prediction for stable top quarks based on the
MCFM
program is appropriate, since it properly describes the generic size of
subleading QCD corrections and vanishes for
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<2m_{\mathrm{t}}
.
For a reliable measurement of the MSR top quark mass, however, a more complete
code including the features mentioned above has to be made available.

R

60\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
to
80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}

\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{f}
to values below the top quark mass further diminishes the size of the NLO corrections.
The reason is that for this particular
R

\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{f}
,

captures a sizable part of the non-relativistic bound-state dynamics relevant in the region
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\in[333,366]\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
.
In other words, a significant part of the binding effects is already absorbed into the mass value, so that the fixed-order prediction at NLO in the MSR scheme provides a more precise description of the process as compared to the pole mass scheme.
Considering the observations in the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
range of
[300,366]
GeV (Fig.

.
Out of this range, the highest choice for the scale,

is preferred as the default choice, to avoid entering the regime of

while allowing the examination of the

4
Extraction of the top quark MSR mass

The MSR mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
is extracted from the differential
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

production cross section measured by the CMS Collaboration in
\mathrm{p}{}\mathrm{p}{}
collisions
at the LHC at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{Te\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of

35.9\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}

[
15
]
. The
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
cross section is measured as a function of
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
in the ranges:

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<420\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\in[420,550]\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\in[550,810]\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}

and
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}>810\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
.

The theoretical predictions are obtained using the ABMP16 5-flavor PDF set
[
57
]
at NLO.
According to the preferred MSR mass scale settings described in the previous section, the
initial value of the scale
R
is set to
80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
in Eq. (
2.14
), and the cross section is
calculated for a range of assumed values of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
. The function

\chi^{2}=\sum_{i,j}(\sigma^{\textrm{exp}}_{i}-\sigma^{\textrm{th}}_{i})C^{-1}_{ij}(\sigma^{\textrm{exp}}_{j}-\sigma^{\textrm{th}}_{j}),

(4.1)

is computed for each
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})

i,j
in Eq. (
4.1
)
run over the bins of the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution, while

\sigma^{\textrm{exp}}_{i}
are the experimental data and

\sigma^{\textrm{th}}_{i}
the theoretical predictions. The inverse covariance
matrix
C^{-1}_{ij}
provided in Ref.
[
15
]
is used.

The scales
\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{f}
are set to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
for all 4 bins of the

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution or, alternatively, to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})/2
for

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<420\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, to stabilize the prediction against the missing
quasi-bound state corrections, and to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
for the remainder.
Fig.
5
shows a 4th order polynomial fit to the
\chi^{2}
values
resulting from each configuration.

5
Summary and Conclusions

We have presented the first comprehensive study of the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution in its dependence on
the mass renormalization scales
R
and
\mu_{m}
of the MSR and
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
top quark mass schemes.
Our findings suggest that using the MSR mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
with the scale setting of
R
close to
80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
improves
the robustness of the predictions for the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution
against scale variations in general and, in particular, against the impact of quasi-bound state corrections
in the region of
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
close to the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
threshold.
The theory predictions used in our study are based on the NLO fixed order QCD description provided by the
MCFM
program, adapted to the MSR and
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
top quark mass schemes.
The optimized scale choices for those mass schemes are characterized by low values of the renormalization and factorization scales
\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{f}
.
This holds in particular in the vicinity of the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
production threshold region in the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution, where values
\mu_{r}\simeq\mu_{f}\simeq m_{\mathrm{t}}/2
are observed to stabilize cross section predictions and to decrease the scale uncertainty in the determination of the MSR mass.

These settings have been applied in an extraction of the
top quark MSR mass at
R=80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, using
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}

pair production cross section, measured as a function of
m_{{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}}
in

\mathrm{p}{}\mathrm{p}{}
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{Te\hskip-0.80002ptV}
at the LHC by the CMS Collaboration,
using fixed-order perturbative QCD predictions at NLO accuracy and also the semi-dynamical scales for
\mu_{r},~\mu_{f}
in the low-
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
regime.
The fitted value of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
has then been evolved to various low reference scales
R
, rather than computing the cross sections directly at low
R
as performed in earlier analyses.
This procedure yields the value
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(3\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})=174.5^{+0.6}_{-0.7}\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, which is discussed in the context of other recent extractions of the top quark mass from LHC data.
The observed differences are explained to a large part by the scale choice of
R=80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
for the top quark MSR mass, advocated by the present study, signifying the achieved systematic resummation of higher order QCD corrections.
Other reasons for differences are due to the choice of the value for the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}(m_{\mathrm{Z}})
, which directly affects the normalization of the cross section and is anti-correlated with the top quark mass, and, to a lesser extent, due to the particular PDF sets used.

While we have argued that the implementation of the MSR mass scheme in the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
cross section calculation and the optimal scale choice for
R
of
80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
provide more robust
predictions even at NLO accuracy, the findings should be corroborated by extending the analysis to NNLO accuracy.
In addition, the proper treatment of both, the quasi-bound state effects, together with a matching to the relativistic
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
region, and the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
region below the threshold are further important improvements to be implemented.
A final reliable measurement of the top quark MSR mass needs to address those issues as well as the correlation of the top quark mass with the other theoretical parameters, which control the cross section predictions.
We leave these aspects for future studies.

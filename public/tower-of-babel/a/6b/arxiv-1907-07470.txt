Inhomogeneous domain walls in spintronic nanowires
L. Siemer, I. Ovsyannikov, J.D.M. Rademacher

Abstract
In case of a spin-polarized current, the magnetization dynamics in nanowires are governed by the classical Landau-Lifschitz equation with Gilbert damping term, augmented by a typically non-variational Slonczewski term. Taking axial symmetry into account, we study the existence of domain wall type coherent structure solutions, with focus on one space dimension and spin-polarization, but our results also apply to vanishing spin-torque term. Using methods from bifurcation theory for arbitrary constant applied fields, we prove the existence of domain walls with non-trivial azimuthal profile, referred to as inhomogeneous . We present an apparently new type of domain wall, referred to as non-flat , whose approach of the axial magnetization has a certain oscillatory character. Additionally, we present the leading order mechanism for the parameter selection of flat and non-flat inhomogeneous domain walls for an applied field below a threshold, which depends on anisotropy, damping, and spin-transfer. Moreover, numerical continuation results of all these domain wall solutions are presented.

Introduction

Magnetic domain walls (DWs) are of great interest both from a theoretical perspective and for applications, especially in the context of innovative magnetic storages
[
1
]
. Recent developments in controlled movement of DWs via spin-polarized current pulses in nanomagnetic structures, in particular in nanowires, are thought to lead to a new class of potential non-volatile storage memories, e.g. racetrack memory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4

[
5

6
,
7

. This motivates further studies of the existence of magnetic domains and their interaction with spin-polarized currents as a building block for the theory in this context. In this paper we take a mathematical perspective and, in a model for nanomagnetic wires, rigorously study the existence of DWs. This led us to discover an apparently new kind of DWs with a certain inhomogeneous and oscillatory structure as explained in more detail below.

The description of magnetization dynamics in nanomagnetic structures, governed by the
Landau-Lifschitz-Gilbert
(LLG) equation, is based on works by Berger and Slonczewski assuming a spin-polarized current

In the presence of a constant applied field and a spin-polarized current, the dynamics driven by the joint action of magnetic field and spin torque can be studied by adding a spin-transfer term in the direction of the current (current-perpendicular-to-plane (CPP) configuration). In case of a spatially uniform magnetization, the resulting

(LLGS)

2
Model equations and coherent structure form

The classical model for magnetization dynamics was proposed by Landau and Lifschitz based on gyromagnetic precession, and later modified by Gilbert
[
15
,
16
]
. See
[
17
]
for an overview. The
Landau-Lifschitz-Gilbert
equation for unit vector fields
\boldsymbol{m}(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
in one space dimension
x\in\mathbb{R}
and in terms of normalized time in dimensionless form is

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}-\alpha\boldsymbol{m}\times\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}=-\boldsymbol{m}\times\boldsymbol{h}_{\textnormal{eff}}.

(LLG)

Here
\boldsymbol{m}=\boldsymbol{M}/M_{S}
represents the normalized magnetization,
\boldsymbol{h}_{\textnormal{eff}}=\boldsymbol{H}_{\textnormal{eff}}/M_{S}
the effective field, i.e. the negative variational derivative of the total magnetic free energy with respect to
\boldsymbol{m}
, both normalized by the spontaneous magnetization
M_{S}
. For gyromagnetic ratio
\gamma
and saturation magnetization
M_{S}
the time is measured in units of
(\gamma M_{S})^{-1}
, and it is assumed that the temperature of the magnetic body is constant and below the
Curie
temperature
[
5
]
. Finally,
Gilbert
damping
\alpha>0
turns
\boldsymbol{m}
towards
\boldsymbol{h}_{\textnormal{eff}}
and both vectors are parallel in the static solution.

In modern spin-tronic applications, e.g. Spin-Transfer Torque Magnetoresistive Random Access Memories (MRAM), the spin of electrons is flipped using a spin-polarized current. To take these effects into account, the
LLG
equation is supplemented by an additional spin transfer torque term. Using a semiclassical approach, Slonczewski derived an extended effective field

\mathcal{H}_{\textnormal{eff}}=\boldsymbol{h}_{\textnormal{eff}}-\boldsymbol{m}\times\boldsymbol{J},

where
\boldsymbol{J}=\boldsymbol{J}(\boldsymbol{m})
depends on the magnetization and the second term is usually called Slonczewski term
[
9
]
. In contrast to the
LLG
equation, which can be written as the gradient of free ferromagnetic energy, this generalized form is no longer variational and the energy is no longer a Lyapunov functional.

As to the specific form of
\mathcal{H}_{\textnormal{eff}}
, including a leading order form of exchange interaction, uniaxial crystal anisotropy in direction
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
, and
Zeeman
as well as stray-field interactions with an external magnetic field, see e.g.
[
6
]
, gives the well known form (
1
).

In this paper we consider a constant applied magnetic field
h\in\mathbb{R}
along
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
and uniaxial anisotropy with parameter
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, for which the anisotropy energy density is rotationally symmetric w.r.t.
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
. According to the energetically preferred direction in the uniaxial case, minima of the anisotropy energy density correspond to
easy
directions, whereas saddles or maxima correspond to
medium-hard
or
hard
directions, respectively. Therefore, one refers to
\mu<0
as
easy-axis
anisotropy and
\mu>0
as
easy-plane
, both with regard to
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
.

As mentioned before, the LLG equation with its variational structure appears as a special case of (
LLGS
) for
\beta=0
or
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
. While our main focus is the non-variational spintronic case
\beta\cdot c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
, all results contain the case
\beta\cdot c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
and thus carry over to (
LLG
).

It is well-known that (
LLGS
) admits an equivalent form as an explicit evolution equation of quasilinear parabolic type in the form, see e.g.
[
12
]
,

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}=\partial_{x}\left(A(\boldsymbol{m})\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{m}\right)+B\left(\boldsymbol{m},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{m}\right).

As a starting point, we briefly note the existence of spatially homogeneous equilibrium solutions of (
LLGS
) for which
\boldsymbol{m}(x,t)
is constant in
x
and
t
.

Remark 1
.

The only
(
spatially
)
homogeneous equilibria of (
LLGS
) for
\beta>0
are the constant up- and down magnetization states
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
. Indeed, setting
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}=\partial_{x}^{2}\boldsymbol{m}=0
in
(
LLGS
)
, for
\beta\neq 0
the last equation implies that
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
and thus the only solutions
\boldsymbol{m}_{\pm}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
are
\boldsymbol{m}_{\pm}^{\ast}=(0,0,\pm 1)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
.

Remark 2
.

In case
\beta=0
as well as
|h/\mu|<1
there exist a family of additional homogeneous solutions of (
LLGS
) given by

\boldsymbol{m}^{\ast}=(m_{1},m_{2},h/\mu)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}},

with
m^{2}_{1}+m^{2}_{2}=1-(h/\mu)^{2}
. Note that similar cases occur for symmetry axis being
\boldsymbol{e}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{2}
, respectively
(
cf. Brown’s equations
)
.

2.1
Coherent structure ODE

Due to the rotation symmetry around the
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
-axis of (
LLGS
), it is natural to use spherical coordinates

\boldsymbol{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\varphi)\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\varphi)\sin(\theta)\\
\cos(\theta)\end{pmatrix},

where
\varphi=\varphi(x,t)
and
\theta=\theta(x,t)
. This changes (
LLGS
) to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}\varphi\sin(\theta)\\
-\partial_{t}\theta\end{pmatrix}=&\begin{pmatrix}2\partial_{x}\varphi\partial_{x}\theta\cos(\theta)\\
-\partial_{x}^{2}\theta\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\sin(\theta)\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi+\beta/\left(1+c_{\textnormal{cp}}\cos(\theta)\right)\\
(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2}\cos(\theta)+h-\mu\cos(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(3)

Note that the rotation symmetry has turned into the shift symmetry in the azimutal angle
\varphi
, as (
3
) depends on derivatives of
\varphi
only.

Recall that DW solutions spatially connect the up and down magnetization states
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
in a coherent way as relative equilibria with respect to the translation symmetry in
x
and
\phi
, which yields the ansatz

\xi\coloneqq x-st,\,\theta=\theta(\xi),\,\varphi=\phi(\xi)+\Omega t.

(4)

Such solutions are generalized travelling waves that move with constant speed
s\in\mathbb{R}
in space and rotate pointwise with a constant frequency
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
around the
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
-axis; solutions with
\Omega=0
are classical travelling waves.

As in
[
12
]
, applying ansatz (
4
) to (
3
) leads to the so-called
coherent structure ODE

\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(\Omega-s\phi^{\prime})\sin(\theta)\\
s\theta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=&\begin{pmatrix}2\phi^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}\cos(\theta)\\
-\theta^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\sin(\theta)\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{\prime\prime}+\beta/\left(1+c_{\textnormal{cp}}\cos(\theta)\right)\\
(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\cos(\theta)+h-\mu\cos(\theta)\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(5)

where
{}^{\prime}=d/d\xi
. This system of two second-order ODEs does not depend on
\phi
and thus reduces to three dynamical variables
(\theta,\psi=\theta^{\prime},q=\phi^{\prime})
. Following standard terminology for coherent structures, we refer to
q
as the
local wavenumber
.

Writing (
5
) as a first-order three-dimensional system gives

\begin{array}[]{rl}\theta^{\prime}&=\psi\\
\psi^{\prime}&=\sin(\theta)\left[h-\Omega+sq+(q^{2}-\mu)\cos(\theta)\right]-\alpha s\psi\\
q^{\prime}&=\alpha\Omega-\beta/(1+c_{\textnormal{cp}}\cos(\theta))-\alpha sq-\frac{s+2q\cos(\theta)}{\sin(\theta)}\psi\end{array},

(6)

and DWs in the original PDE are in 1-to-1-correspondence with the ODE solutions connecting
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
.

2.1.1
Blow-up charts and asymptotic states

As in
[
12
]
, the singularities at zeros of
\sin(\theta)
in (
6
) can be removed by the singular coordinate change
\psi:=p\sin(\theta)
, which is a blow-up transformation mapping the poles of the sphere
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
to circles thus creating a cylinder. The resulting
desingularized
system reads

\begin{array}[]{rl}\theta^{\prime}&=\sin(\theta)p\\
p^{\prime}&=h-\Omega-\alpha sp+sq-(p^{2}-q^{2}+\mu)\cos(\theta)\\
q^{\prime}&=\alpha\Omega-\beta/(1+c_{\textnormal{cp}}\cos(\theta))-sp-\alpha sq-2pq\cos(\theta).\end{array}

(7)

The coherent structure system (
6
) is equivalent to the desingularized system (
7
) for
\theta\neq n\pi\,,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and therefore also for
\boldsymbol{m}
away from
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
. Furthermore, the planar
blow-up charts

\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
are invariant sets of (
7
), which are mapped to the single points
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
and
-\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
, respectively by the blow-down transformation. System (
7
) has a special structure (cf. Figure
6
) that will be relevant for the subsequent DW analysis. In the remainder of this section we analyze this in some detail.

Lemma 1
.

Consider the equations for
p
and
q
in (
7
) for an artificially fixed value of
\theta
. In terms of
z\coloneqq p+\mathrm{i}q
this subsystem can be written as the complex (scalar) ODE

z^{\prime}=Az^{2}+Bz+C,

(8)

where

A\coloneqq-\cos(\theta)\,,B\coloneqq-(\alpha+\mathrm{i})s\,,\textnormal{ and }C^{\theta}\coloneqq h-\Omega+A\mu+\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha\Omega-\frac{\beta}{1-Ac_{\textnormal{cp}}}\right)
.

For
A\neq 0
the solution with
z_{0}=z(\xi_{0})
away from the equilibria
z^{\theta}_{+}=-\frac{B}{2A}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\gamma^{\theta}}{2A}
and
z^{\theta}_{-}=-\frac{B}{2A}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\gamma^{\theta}}{2A}
, with
\gamma^{\theta}=\gamma(\theta)\coloneqq\sqrt{\textstyle{4AC^{\theta}-B^{2}}}
, reads

z(\xi)=\frac{\gamma^{\theta}}{2A}\tan\left(\frac{\gamma^{\theta}}{2}\xi+\delta_{0}\right)-\frac{B}{2A}\,,

(9)

where

\delta_{0}=\arctan\left(\frac{2Az_{0}+B}{\gamma^{\theta}}\right)-\frac{\gamma^{\theta}}{2}\xi_{0}.

For
A=0
, the solution away from the equilibrium
z^{\pi/2}=-C^{\pi/2}/B
is given by

z(\xi)=\left(z_{0}+\frac{C^{\pi/2}}{B}\right)\mathrm{e}^{B(\xi-\xi_{0})}-\frac{C^{\pi/2}}{B}.

Clearly, the solution of (
8
) relates only to those solutions of (
7
) for which
\theta
is constant, i.e.,
\theta=0,\pi
. Although we are mostly interested in the dynamics on the blow-up charts, we consider

w^{0}_{s}+w^{0}_{u}=w^{\pi}_{s}+w^{\pi}_{u}=3
. Notably
w^{0}_{s}=2
and
w^{0}_{u}=1
for all values of the parameters, and
w^{\pi}_{s}
is either
1
or
3
. Recall the standing assumption
s_{0}\geq 0
.

If
w^{\pi}_{s}=1
, the heteroclinic connection of
E^{0}
and
E^{\pi}
generically has
codimension-2
, while for
w^{\pi}_{s}=3
it has
codimension-0
, and we refer to the transition point between these cases, following the discussion in §
2.1.1
, as the
center case
. From (
20
) we have
w^{\pi}_{s}=1\Leftrightarrow 0\leq s_{0}<\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}
,
w^{\pi}_{s}=3\Leftrightarrow s_{0}>\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}
and the center case at
s_{0}=\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}
.

Hence, within the family of homogeneous DWs given by (
17
) and satisfying (
19
), the different bifurcation cases have speed and frequency relations

codim-0:

\displaystyle s_{0}>\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Omega_{0}>\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-\frac{2\mu}{\alpha^{2}}\,,

center:

\displaystyle s_{0}=\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Omega_{0}=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-\frac{2\mu}{\alpha^{2}}\,,

codim-2:

\displaystyle 0\leq s_{0}<\frac{2\sqrt{-\mu}}{\alpha}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\leq\Omega_{0}<\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-\frac{2\mu}{\alpha^{2}}.

Using (
19
) these can be written in terms of the parameters of (
LLGS
), which gives the characterization mentioned in the introduction §1.

Remark 5
.

The case distinction is also related to the spectral stability of the asymptotic states
\boldsymbol{m}=\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
in the dynamics of the full PDE (
LLGS
) which is beyond the scope of this paper, but see Figure
3
for an illustration. In short, it follows from, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 1]
that
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
is
L^{2}
-stable for
h>\beta/\alpha
, while
-\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
is
L^{2}
-stable for
h<\beta/\alpha-\mu
and unstable for
h>\beta/\alpha-\mu
. Based on this, the stability curves in Figure
3
are defined as follows

\Gamma^{+}\coloneqq\frac{\beta/\alpha}{h-\mu}-1,\qquad\Gamma^{-}\coloneqq 1-\frac{\beta/\alpha}{h+\mu},

which intersect at

h=\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}}+\mu^{2}}.

Since the destabilisation of
-\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
if
\beta>0
corresponds to a Hopf-instability of the
(
purely essential
)
spectrum, it is effectively invisible in the coherent structure ODE, which detects changes in the linearization at zero temporal eigenvalue only. Visible from the PDE stability viewpoint is a transition of absolute spectrum through the origin in the complex plane of temporal eigenmodes, cf.
[
18
]
. Now in the center case, the state
-\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
is already
L^{2}
-unstable since
\alpha>0
as well as
\mu<0
and
h>\beta/\alpha
implies

h=h^{\ast}=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-\frac{2\mu}{\alpha^{2}}(1+\alpha^{2})>\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-\mu

and therefore that
\Gamma^{-}
never intersects the line
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\equiv 0
at
h=h^{\ast}
.

Moreover, it was shown in
[
19
]
that the family of explicit homogeneous DWs (
9
) is (
linearly
) stable for sufficiently small applied fields, actually for
h<-\mu/2
, in case
\beta=0
, hence in the bi-stable case where
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
are
L^{2}
-stable. As mentioned before,
\beta=0
is equivalent to
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
in the
LLGS
equation with an additional shift in
h
and
\beta
, which leads to the (
LLG
) case. We expect these DWs are also stable for small perturbations in
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
, due to the properties of the operator established in
[
19
]
, but further analysis also on the transition from convective/transient to absolute instability will be done elsewhere.

With these preparations, we next state the main results, which concern existence of DWs in the three regimes.

Theorem 1
.

For any parameter set
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},h_{0},\mu_{0})
in the codim-0 case, i.e.,
\mu_{0}<0
and
h_{0}>\beta_{0}/\alpha_{0}-2\mu_{0}-2\mu_{0}/\alpha_{0}^{2}
, the following holds. The explicit homogeneous DWs
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
in (
9
) lies in a smooth family
\boldsymbol{m}_{c_{\textnormal{cp}}}
of DWs parameterized by
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},\alpha,\beta,h,\mu,s,\Omega)
near
(0,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},h_{0},\mu_{0},s_{0},\Omega_{0})
with
s_{0}
,
\Omega_{0}
from (
19
) evaluated at
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},h_{0},\mu_{0})
. Moreover, in case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
and
(s,\Omega)\neq(s_{0},\Omega_{0})
evaluated at
(\alpha,\beta,h,\mu)
, or
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
, these are inhomogeneous flat DWs.

Proof.

As mentioned, in the codim-0 case we have
w_{s}^{\pi}=3
and for all parameters
w_{u}^{0}=1
. Due to the existence of the heteroclinic orbit (
17
), this means
W^{0}_{u}
intersects
W^{\pi}_{s}
transversely and non-trivially for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
in a unique trajectory. Therefore, this DW perturbs to a locally unique family by the implicit function theorem for perturbations of the parameters in (
7
).

For
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
sufficiently small these are inhomogeneous DWs since the derivative of the third equation, the
q
-equation, in (
7
) with respect to
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
is nonzero in this case; hence already the equilibrium states move into the inhomogeneous regime.

For
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
but
(s,\Omega)\neq(s_{0},\Omega_{0})
at
(\alpha,\beta,h,\mu)
, it follows from
[
12
, Theorem 5]
that these DWs cannot be homogeneous.
∎

Next we consider the center case, where
h=h^{\ast}=\beta/\alpha-2\mu-2\mu/\alpha^{2}
. We start with a result that follows from the same approach used in the codim-2 case and give refined results below.

Corollary 1
.

The statement of Theorem
1
also holds for a parameter set in the center case if the perturbed parameters
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},\alpha,\beta,h,\mu,s,\Omega)
satisfy
\Omega>s^{2}/2+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-}/\alpha
. If
\Omega=s^{2}/2+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-}/\alpha
and
\Omega<h+\mu+\frac{s^{2}}{4}(1+\alpha^{2})
the same holds except the DW is possibly non-flat.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
1
and the discussion before that
\Omega>s^{2}/2+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-}/\alpha
for the parameter perturbation implies that the eigenvalues of the perturbed equilibrium
Z^{\pi}_{-}\approx E^{\pi}
satisfy
\mathrm{Re}(\nu_{k,-}^{\pi})<0
,
k=1,2
. Hence, the stable manifold at the target equilibrium is two-dimensional and lies in a smooth family with the center-stable manifold at the transition point. Then the proof is the same as in the codim-0 case. If the perturbation has
\Omega=s^{2}/2+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{-}/\alpha
then we consider as target manifold the three-dimensional stable manifold of a neighborhood of
Z^{\pi}_{-}
within the blow-up chart
\theta=0
. This neighborhood consists of periodic orbits by Proposition
1
if
\Omega<h+\mu+\frac{s^{2}}{4}(1+\alpha^{2})
. By dimensionality the intersection with the unstable manifold of
Z^{0}_{-}
persists and yields a heteroclinic orbit from the perturbed equilibrium at
\theta=0
to the blow-up chart at
\theta=\pi
. Perturbing
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
away from zero moves the left-asymptotic state into the inhomogeneous regime and thus generates an inhomogeneous DWs. Note from Proposition
1
that the right-asymptotic state is either an equilibrium with
q\neq 0
or a periodic orbit along which
q=0
happens at most at two points.
∎

Next we present a refined result in which we show that typical perturbations indeed give non-flat DWs, i.e., heteroclinic connections with right-asymptotic state being a periodic orbit. The existence of flat DWs for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
is severely constrained, but not ruled out by this result. Our numerical results, such as those presented in §
4
, always lead to a selected solution with a periodic asymptotic state.

In addition, attempts to perform numerical continuation (see §
4
) of flat DWs to
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
failed. Here we added the constraint
\widetilde{H}=0
and allowed adjustment the parameters
h
and
s
, but the continuation process did not converge, which confirms numerically the generic selection of a periodic orbit.

As mentioned before, the right asymptotic state is
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
in either case in the PDE coordinates; the difference between flat and non-flat lies in the finer details of how the profile approaches
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
in term of
p
and also
q
, which relates to
\boldsymbol{m}
through (
2
).

Theorem 2
.

Consider the smooth family of DWs from Corollary
1
with
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
for parameters satisfying (
12
) with fixed
\alpha>0
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
\mu<0
. Then there is a neighborhood
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},s,h)
of
(0,s_{0},h^{*})
such that the following holds. Flat DWs occur at most on a surface in the
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},s,h)
-parameter space and, for
\beta\neq 0
, satisfy
|h-h_{0}|^{2}+|s-s_{0}|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(|c_{\textnormal{cp}}|^{3})
, more precisely (
31
) holds, where
h_{0}=h^{\ast}
and
s_{0}=2\sqrt{-\mu}/\alpha
. Otherwise DWs are non-flat, in particular all DWs not equal to
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
or
\beta=0
are non-flat.

Due to its more technical nature, the proof of this theorem is deferred to Appendix
6.1
.

It remains to consider the codim-2 case.

Theorem 3
.

For any parameter set
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},h_{0},\mu_{0})
in the codim-2 case, i.e.,
\mu_{0}<0
and
\beta_{0}/\alpha_{0}\leq h_{0}<\beta_{0}/\alpha_{0}-2\mu_{0}-2\mu_{0}/\alpha_{0}^{2}
, the following holds. The explicit homogeneous DWs
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
in (
9
) lies in a smooth family of DWs parameterized by
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},\alpha,\beta,h,\mu)
near
(0,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},h_{0},\mu_{0})
. Here the values of
(s,\Omega)
are functions of the parameters
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},\alpha,\beta,h,\mu)
and lie in a neighbourhood of
(s_{0},\Omega_{0})
from (
19
). This family is locally unique near
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
and for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
consists of inhomogeneous flat DWs.

The proof of Theorem
3
is presented in Appendix
6.2
and is based on the Melnikov method for perturbing from
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
. As the unperturbed heteroclinic orbit has codimension two, the bifurcation is studied in a three-parametric family with perturbation parameters
\eta\coloneqq\left(c_{\textnormal{cp}},s,\Omega\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
, which yields a two-component splitting function

that measures the mutual displacement of the manifolds
W^{0}_{u}
and
W^{\pi}_{s}
.

Due to the fact that
\mathrm{Re}(\nu^{\pi}_{j,-})<0
also for
\beta=0
in the codim-0 regime and the case
\beta=0
is included in Theorem
2
as well as Theorem
3
, we immediately get the following result.

Corollary 2
.

Inhomogeneous flat DWs also exist in the
LLG
equation
(\beta=0)
, which can be flat or non-flat, respectively.

Theorem
3
completes the existence study of DWs. Therefore, for any value of the applied field
h
there exists a heteroclinic connection between the blow-up charts with

and

, thus an inhomogeneous (typically flat) DW. Recall that we have focused on right moving DWs, but all results are also valid for left moving walls due to symmetry. Therefore inhomogeneous DWs exist with

for any value of the applied field
h\in\mathbb{R}
.

5
Discussion and Outlook

We have presented results pertaining the existence of different types of domain walls for the
LLG
as well as
LLGS
equation. Our main focus has been on a nonzero polarisation parameter
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
for any value of the applied field, including the high-field case, and thus for any domain wall speed. These results extend what is known in particular for inhomogeneously structured DWs, and we have discovered an apparently new type of DWs with certain oscillatory tails, referred to as non-flat here.

In detail, we have provided a classification of DWs based on co-dimension properties in a reduced (spatial) coherent structure ODE, which relates to stability and selection properties that we review next. First, we have proven the existence of inhomogeneous flat DWs in case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
as well as
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
for an applied field above a certain threshold, which is mainly material depending. To our knowledge, the only previous existence result for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
with ’large’ applied fields concerns less relevant non-localized DWs
[
12
]
. Here the existence problem does not select speed and frequency.

Second, we have discussed the so-called center case, which is characterized by non-hyperbolic equilibria in the underlying coherent structure ODE. In this case, we have shown the existence of inhomogeneous DWs including the leading order selection mechanism. These solutions are non-flat in case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
and generically also non-flat for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
away from zero, which was substantiated by numerical results. The fundamental observation has been the existence of a Hamiltonian function in a certain parameter regime in the corresponding coherent structure ODE.

Third, we have proven the existence of inhomogeneous DWs in the so-called codim-2 regime, which is a range of values for the applied field in which the speed
s
is between zero and the center case speed. In this regime, each solution in case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
is uniquely determined by its speed as well as frequency. Here we have also presented the leading order selection function in the coherent structure ODE variables
p
and
q
, which depends on the speed
s
, the frequency
\Omega
, and is independent of
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
for standing fronts.

We believe that these results are not only interesting and relevant from a theoretical and mathematical viewpoint, but also from an application viewpoint. They could help to better understand the interfaces between different magnetic domains in nanostructures, e.g. in the development of racetrack memories, which are a promising prospective high density storage unit that utilize a series of DWs by shifting at high speed along magnetic nanowires through nanosecond current pulses.

In order to illustrate and corroborate these theoretical results, we have presented numerical computations for a variety of values for the applied field in §
4
. On the one hand, the examples in essence show that large applied fields lead to more complex profiles of the DWs in case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
. On the other hand, while in the center case the DWs projected on the sphere appear similar to those for small applied fields, these solutions approach the poles in a qualitatively different ‘non-flat’ manner – as predicted by our analysis. Moreover, we compared the numerical and analytical results of the selection mechanism in the center case, showing that the analytical leading order approximation predicts the effect of small perturbations in the parameters. Notably, for applied fields above a certain threshold, where the existence analysis does not provide a selection of speed and frequency, numerically the DWs selected in the PDE dynamics are in the center case, both for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
as well as
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
. Hence, it might be possible to detect these solutions in a high-field regime in real materials.

One question concerning existence beyond our analysis is whether inhomogeneous (flat or non-flat) solutions exist for any value of
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\in(-1,1)
, and whether this class could be utilized in applications.

A natural step towards the understanding of domain wall motion in nanowires beyond the question of existence concerns the dynamic stability. For inhomogeneous solutions there appears to be no rigorous result in this direction. In particular for larger applied fields, stability results would be an essential step towards understanding the selection mechanism of solutions in terms of speed and frequency; our first numerical investigations show that solutions in the center parameter regime are selected, i.e., inhomogeneous non-flat DWs.

Moreover, preliminary analytic results, for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
as well as
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
, show that selection mechanism is mainly determined by the value of the applied field, where in the bi-stable case (
\pm\boldsymbol{e}_{3}
linearly stable) homogeneous DWs are selected, and in the mono-stable case inhomogeneous non-flat DWs are selected, which will be studied in detail in an upcoming work.

\widetilde{H}
in the limit
\xi\to\infty
with respect to the parameter
\eta
yields conditions for periodic asymptotics. In the following, subindices of
H
denote partial derivatives, e.g.
H_{u}=\partial_{u}H
.

Clearly,
H(u_{0};\eta_{0})=H(Z^{\pi}(\eta_{0});\eta_{0})
, thus
\widetilde{H}_{0}=0
and, since equilibria are critical points of
H
, we have
\widetilde{H}_{u}(u_{0};\eta_{0})=\widetilde{H}_{\eta}(u_{0};\eta_{0})=(0,0,0)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
. The second derivative is given by

\frac{d^{2}}{d\eta^{2}}\widetilde{H}=\langle u_{\eta},\widetilde{H}_{uu}u_{\eta}\rangle+\langle u_{\eta},\widetilde{H}_{u\eta}\rangle+\langle\widetilde{H}_{\eta u},u_{\eta}\rangle\,,

(29)

since
\widetilde{H}_{\eta\eta}
is the zero matrix,
\widetilde{H}_{u}
the zero vector, and
\widetilde{H}_{u\eta}=\widetilde{H}_{\eta u}^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
. Thus

\begin{split}\widetilde{H}(u_{0}+u_{\eta}\eta;\eta)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{\eta}\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{uu}(u_{0};\eta_{0})(u_{\eta}\eta)+(u_{\eta}\eta)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{u\eta}(u_{0};\eta_{0})\eta\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{uu}\left(Z^{\pi}(\eta_{0});\eta_{0}\right)\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)\\
&\quad-\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{u\eta}\left(Z^{\pi}(\eta_{0});\eta_{0}\right)\eta+\mathcal{O}\left(\|\eta\|^{3}\right)\,.\end{split}

(30)

With the derivatives
H_{uu},H_{u\eta}
in (
30
) given by

\displaystyle H_{uu}(u;\eta)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\frac{2}{q-s/2}&-\frac{2p-\alpha s}{(q-s/2)^{2}}\\
0&-\frac{2p-\alpha s}{(q-s/2)^{2}}&2\frac{p^{2}-\alpha sp+h-\beta^{-}/\alpha+\mu-s^{2}/4}{(q-s/2)^{3}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle H_{u\eta}(u;\eta)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\frac{p-\alpha q}{(q-s/2)^{2}}&0\\
\frac{\beta/\alpha}{(1-c_{\textnormal{cp}})^{2}(q-s/2)^{2}}&-\frac{p^{2}-\alpha pq-\frac{\alpha s}{2}p-\frac{s}{2}q+h-\beta^{-}/\alpha+\mu}{(q-s/2)^{3}}&-\frac{1}{(q-s/2)^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

for the right hand side of (
30
) in the limit
\xi\to+\infty
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{\eta}\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{uu}(u_{0};\eta_{0})\left(u_{\eta}\eta\right)+\left(u_{\eta}\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{u\eta}(u_{0};\eta_{0})\eta=-\frac{\alpha\beta^{2}}{4\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}c_{\textnormal{cp}}^{2}

\displaystyle~+\frac{(4+5\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{4})(\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}-4(1+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2})}{4\alpha^{3}\rho^{2}\sqrt{-\mu}}(s-s_{0})^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{4}\rho^{2}-4(1+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2}}{4\alpha\rho^{2}\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}(h-h_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha\beta(2+\alpha^{2})}{2\mu}c_{\textnormal{cp}}(s-s_{0})+\frac{\alpha^{2}\beta}{2\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}c_{\textnormal{cp}}(h-h_{0})

\displaystyle~+\frac{(2+\alpha^{2})(\alpha^{4}\rho^{2}-4(1+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2})}{2\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}\mu}(s-s_{0})(h-h_{0})\,,

as well as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{uu}(Z^{\pi}(\eta_{0});\eta_{0})\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)+\left(Z^{\pi}_{\eta}(\eta_{0})\eta\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}H_{u\eta}(Z^{\pi}(\eta_{0});\eta_{0})\eta=

\displaystyle~-\frac{\alpha\beta^{2}}{4\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}c_{\textnormal{cp}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha(4+5\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{4})}{4\sqrt{-\mu}}(s-s_{0})^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{3}}{4\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}(h-h_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\alpha\beta(2+\alpha^{2})}{2\mu}c_{\textnormal{cp}}(s-s_{0})+\frac{\alpha^{2}\beta}{2\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}c_{\textnormal{cp}}(h-h_{0})+\frac{\alpha^{2}(2+\alpha^{2})}{2\mu}(s-s_{0})(h-h_{0}).

Therefore, the expansion in the limit
\xi\to+\infty
is independent of
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
and reads

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{\xi\to\infty}\widetilde{H}(u_{0}+u_{\eta}\eta;\eta)&=-\frac{(1+\alpha^{2})^{2}(4+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2}}{\alpha^{3}\rho^{2}\sqrt{-\mu}}(s-s_{0})^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2(1+\alpha^{2})(2+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2}}{\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}\mu}(s-s_{0})(h-h_{0})\\
&\quad+\frac{(1+\alpha^{2})\pi^{2}}{\alpha\rho^{2}\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}(h-h_{0})^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|^{3}\right)\,.\end{split}

(31)

Recall
\rho=\exp(\pi/\alpha)-\exp(-\pi/\alpha)
. One readily verifies that the resulting (binary) quadratic form of (
31
) is negative definite for all
\alpha>0
so the only solution to the leading order problem

\frac{d^{2}}{d\eta^{2}}\widetilde{H}(u_{0};\eta_{0})=0

is the trivial one
(s,h)=(0,0)
,
and any non-trivial solution satisfies
|s-s_{0}|^{2}+|h-h_{0}|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(|c_{\textnormal{cp}}|^{3})
.

In particular, for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
there is a neighborhood of
(s_{0},h_{0})
such that the only solution is the trivial one, which is therefore also the case in the
LLG
equation. In case
c_{\textnormal{cp}}\neq 0
, higher orders may lead to a solution with non-zero
s
and/or
h
, but there is numerical evidence that such solutions do not exist (see §
4
for details).

6.2
Proof of Theorem
3

The idea of the proof is to apply Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, i.e., to determine a bifurcation equation whose solutions are in one-to-one correspondence with heteroclinic connections between equilibria (
7
) near one of the explicit solutions
u_{0}
from (
17
) connecting the equilibria
Z^{0}_{-}
and
Z^{\pi}_{-}
. In the present context this is known as Melnikov’s method, see for example
[
22
]
.

Recall
u_{0}
corresponds to a homogeneous DW for
c_{\textnormal{cp}}=0
with speed
s_{0}
and rotation frequency
\Omega_{0}
given by (
19
). In the present codim-2 parameter regime we will show that the bifurcation equation defines a codimension two bifurcation curve in the three-dimensional parameter space
(c_{\textnormal{cp}},s,\Omega)
, which passes through the point
(0,s_{0},\Omega_{0})
. The main part of the proof is to show the existence of certain integrals for the considered parameter set. These integrals are almost identical to the ones studied within the proof of Theorem
2
and we use the same approach.

In this section we denote the parameter vector by
\eta\coloneqq(c_{\textnormal{cp}},s,\Omega)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with initial value
\eta_{0}=(0,s_{0},\Omega_{0})^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
corresponding to the unperturbed values. The solutions of the perturbed system close to
u_{0}
has the form
u(\xi;\eta)=u_{0}(\xi)+u_{\eta}(\xi;\eta_{0})(\eta-\eta_{0})+\mathcal{O}(\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|^{2})
, where
u_{\eta}=(\theta_{\eta},p_{\eta},q_{\eta})^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}=\mathcal{O}(\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|)
.

As discussed in Appendix
6.1
, the linearization (
23
) of system (
7
) around the unperturbed heteroclinic connection
u_{0}
has the fundamental solution matrix
\Phi(\xi)
as defined in (
26
). In the present codim-2 case with
\dim(W^{0}_{u})=\dim(W^{\pi}_{s})=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the bifurcation equation
M(\eta)=0
entails two equations. Here
M(\eta)
measures the displacement of the manifolds
W^{0}_{u}
and
W^{\pi}_{s}
, and we will choose this to be near the point
u_{0}(0)=\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\sqrt{-\mu},0\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}
in the directions given by vectors
v_{1}(0)
and
v_{2}(0)
from adjoint solutions as detailed below. From the Taylor expansion
M(\eta)=M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})(\eta-\eta_{0})+\mathcal{O}(\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|^{2})
we infer by the implicit function theorem that a full rank of
M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})
implies a one-to-one correspondence of solutions to the bifurcation equation with elements in the kernel of
M(\eta_{0})
.

In order to compute
M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})
and its rank, we project onto the transverse directions to
u_{0}
, which means to project the inhomogeneous part of equation (
23
) onto two linearly independent bounded solutions
v_{1},v_{2}
of the adjoint variational equation
v^{\prime}=-A^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}\cdot v
, where

A^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-\mu}\cos(\theta_{0})&0&0\\
\sin(\theta_{0})&-\alpha s_{0}-2\sqrt{-\mu}\cos(\theta_{0})&-s_{0}\\
0&s_{0}&-\alpha s_{0}-2\sqrt{-\mu}\cos(\theta_{0})\end{pmatrix}.

The solutions are given in terms of (
25
) by

v_{1}=\frac{U_{1}\times U_{2}}{\det\Phi}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\frac{1}{r(\xi)}\sin(-s_{0}\xi)\\
\frac{1}{r(\xi)}\cos(-s_{0}\xi)\end{pmatrix}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad v_{2}=\frac{U_{3}\times U_{1}}{\det\Phi}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{r(\xi)}\cos(-s_{0}\xi)\\
\frac{1}{r(\xi)}\sin(-s_{0}\xi)\end{pmatrix}\,.

Implementing the projection onto these, we obtain the so-called

Melnikov integral

\begin{split}M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})&\coloneqq\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\left(v_{1},v_{2}\right)^{\mkern-1.2mu\mathsf{T}}\cdot f_{\eta}(u_{0};\eta_{0})}\,d\xi\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}\beta I_{CC}&\alpha\sqrt{-\mu}I_{S}-\sqrt{-\mu}I_{C}&I_{S}+\alpha I_{C}\\
\beta I_{CS}&-\alpha\sqrt{-\mu}I_{C}-\sqrt{-\mu}I_{S}&-I_{C}+\alpha I_{S}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(32)

where

\displaystyle I_{CC}

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\frac{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s_{0}\xi+2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\cos(-s_{0}\xi)}{\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)^{3}}}d\xi\,,

\displaystyle I_{CS}

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\frac{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s_{0}\xi+2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\sin(-s_{0}\xi)}{\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)^{3}}}d\xi\,,

\displaystyle I_{C}

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s_{0}\xi}\cos(-s_{0}\xi)}{\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)\cdot\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)}}d\xi\,,

\displaystyle I_{S}

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s_{0}\xi}\sin(-s_{0}\xi)}{\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)\cdot\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)}}d\xi\,.

We next show that the second and third columns in (
32
) have non-vanishing determinant so that the rank is always 2, in particular also for
\beta=0
.

For brevity, we present the calculations of
I_{CC}
and
I_{CS}
only, which are based on the same idea as the computations in Appendix
6.1
. The solutions for
I_{C}
and
I_{S}
can be computed in an analogous way.

From Appendix
6.1
we know that the following integral would not exist in case
s_{0}=2\sqrt{-\mu}/\alpha
and one readily verifies the existence for
s_{0}=0
. Therefore, we first assume
0<s_{0}<2\sqrt{-\mu}/\alpha
for the moment and discuss the case
s_{0}=0
later. We set

I\coloneqq\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{g(\xi)}d\xi\,,\quad g(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha s_{0}+2\sqrt{-\mu})\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}s_{0}\xi}}{\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{-\mu}\xi}\right)^{3}}\,,

so that
I_{CC}=\mathrm{Re}(I)
and
I_{CS}=\mathrm{Im}(I)
. Utilizing the same idea for the contour integral as in Appendix
6.1
(cf. Figure
14
) and the residue theorem, we obtain

I-\mathrm{e}^{\alpha s_{0}\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{s_{0}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cdot I=2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum{\mathrm{Res}(g)}\,.

The function
g
has a pole of order three at
\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}
and the
Laurent
series gives

\mathrm{Res}(g)=-\frac{(\alpha-\mathrm{i})^{2}s^{2}_{0}}{8\mu\sqrt{-\mu}}\exp\left((\alpha-\mathrm{i})\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right),

and therefore

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi s_{0}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}\left[-2\alpha\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)+\left(\alpha^{2}-1\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)\right]}{8\mu\sqrt{-\mu}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathrm{i}2\pi s_{0}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}\left[\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)-2\alpha\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)\right]}{8\mu\sqrt{-\mu}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)\right)}\,.

Separating the real and imaginary parts of
I
we get

I_{CC}=\frac{\pi s_{0}^{2}\sqrt{-\mu}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}}{4\mu^{2}}\cdot\frac{2\alpha\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)+\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}-2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}

I_{CS}=\frac{\pi s_{0}^{2}\sqrt{-\mu}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}}{4\mu^{2}}\cdot\frac{\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)+2\alpha\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}-2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}\,.

As mentioned before, one analogously gets

I_{C}=\frac{\pi s_{0}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}}{2\mu}\cdot\frac{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)-\alpha\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}-2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}

I_{S}=\frac{\pi s_{0}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}}}{2\mu}\cdot\frac{\alpha\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)+\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}-2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}\right)}\,.

Having the explicit expressions for
I_{C}
,
I_{S}
,
I_{CC}
, as well as
I_{CS}
, we can study the rank of (
32
) in case
0<s_{0}<2\sqrt{-\mu}/\alpha
. The determinant of the second and third column of (
32
) simplifies to

\left(\alpha I_{S}-I_{C}\right)^{2}+\left(I_{S}+\alpha I_{C}\right)^{2}=\left(1+\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\pi^{2}s_{0}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi s_{0}}{\sqrt{-\mu}}}\neq 0\,,\quad\forall s_{0}\neq 0\,.

Therefore,
M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})
has full rank and we obtain

M(\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}\beta I_{CC}&\alpha\sqrt{-\mu}I_{S}-\sqrt{-\mu}I_{C}&I_{S}+\alpha I_{C}\\
\beta I_{CS}&-\alpha\sqrt{-\mu}I_{C}-\sqrt{-\mu}I_{S}&-I_{C}+\alpha I_{S}\end{pmatrix}\cdot(\eta-\eta_{0})+\mathcal{O}\left(\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|^{2}\right).

(33)

In the remaining case
s_{0}=0
, which means
h=\beta/\alpha
, we have
I_{CC}=I_{CS}=I_{S}=0
,
I_{C}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}
, and thus

M_{\eta}(\eta_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{1}{2}&\frac{\alpha}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\\
0&-\frac{\alpha}{2}&-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-\mu}}\end{pmatrix},

(34)

whose rank is always 2. Thus the splitting directions are independent of
c_{\textnormal{cp}}
in first order in case
h=\beta/\alpha
. Moreover, note that the splitting in
s
is independent of the anisotropy
\mu
to first order in case
h=\beta/\alpha
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

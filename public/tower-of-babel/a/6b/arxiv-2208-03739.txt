Abstract
. This paper studies sharp and rigid isoperimetric comparison theorems and asymptotic isoperimetric properties for small and large volumes on N N -dimensional RCD ⁡ ( K , N ) {\rm RCD}(K,N) spaces ( X , 𝖽 , ℋ N ) (X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}) . Moreover, we obtain almost regularity theorems formulated in terms of the isoperimetric profile and enhanced consequences at the level of several functional inequalities. Most of our statements seem to be new even in the classical setting of smooth, non compact manifolds with lower Ricci curvature bounds. The synthetic theory plays a key role via compactness and stability arguments.

1.
Introduction

The connection between lower Ricci curvature bounds on Riemannian manifolds and the isoperimetric problem is classical and in recent years it has attracted a lot of interest also in the realm of metric measure spaces with lower Ricci curvature bounds in synthetic sense. The goal of this paper is to explore a series of sharp isoperimetric comparison, stability and rigidity theorems in the setting of
N
-dimensional
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
, for finite
N\in[1,\infty)
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
.
Here
K\in\mathbb{R}
plays the role of (synthetic) lower bound on the Ricci curvature,
N\in[1,\infty)
plays the role of (synthetic) upper bound on the dimension and
\mathscr{H}^{N}
indicates the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Given an
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
we introduce the isoperimetric profile
I_{X}:[0,\mathscr{H}^{N}(X))\to[0,\infty)
by

(1.1)

I_{X}(v):=\inf\left\{\Per(E)\,:\,E\subset X\,,\,\,\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)=v\right\}\,,

where we drop the subscript
X
when there is no risk of confusion.
When
E\subset X
attains the infimum in (
1.1
) for
v=\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)
, we call it an isoperimetric region.
Above,
\Per
denotes the perimeter measure of a Borel set
E\subset X
with finite perimeter. For the sake of this introduction we remark that the theory of sets of finite perimeter on
\RCD
spaces is fully consistent with the Riemannian one.

In this setting we obtain:

•

sharp and rigid isoperimetric inequalities when
K=0
and
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
has Euclidean volume growth;

•

the precise asymptotic behaviour of the isoperimetric profile and of isoperimetric regions for small volumes in great generality, and for large volumes under the assumption that
K=0
and
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
has Euclidean volume growth;

•

new global
\varepsilon
-regularity theorems under lower Ricci curvature bounds formulated in terms of the isoperimetric profile;

•

sharp and rigid comparison theorems for some spectral gap inequalities.

Many of the above results seem to be new even for smooth, non compact Riemannian manifolds with lower Ricci curvature bounds and for Alexandrov spaces with lower sectional curvature bounds. They answer to several open questions raised in
[
19
,
74
,
17
]
. The main technical contributions of the paper are a series of Geometric Measure Theory arguments in low regularity, leading to enhanced consequences about classical problems in Geometric Analysis under lower Ricci curvature bounds.

Main results

The first main result of the paper is the characterization of rigidity for the sharp isoperimetric inequality on
\RCD(0,N)
spaces endowed with the volume measure
\mathscr{H}^{N}
and with Euclidean volume growth.

We recall that the asymptotic volume ratio of an
\RCD(0,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is defined by

(1.2)

\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(p))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}\,,

and it is independent of the base point
p\in X
. Here
\omega_{N}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The space is said to have Euclidean volume growth if
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 2
, and let
E\subset X
be a set of finite perimeter. Then

(1.3)

\Per(E)\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}.

Moreover equality holds for some
E
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\in(0,\infty)
if and only if
X
is isometric to a Euclidean metric measure cone (of dimension
N
) over an
\RCD(N-2,N-1)
space and
E
is isometric to a ball centered at one of the tips of
X
.

The novelty in
Theorem 1.1
is the complete characterization of the rigidity in (
1.3
). Moreover, even though the inequality (
1.3
) was already known in the setting of
Theorem 1.1
(see
[
17
]
after
[
1
,
22
,
45
]
), we will give a new proof tailored for the characterization of rigidity in this paper.
The rigidity statement of
Theorem 1.1
in the generality of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces is fundamental in order to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the isoperimetric profile for small and large volumes even on a smooth non compact Riemannian manifold, see the results below.
Furthermore, the characterization of the equality case in
Theorem 1.1
without further regularity assumptions besides the natural ones for the isoperimetric problem is new for Riemannian manifolds with dimension higher than
7

and completely new in the
\RCD
setting.

The proof of
Theorem 1.1
combines the generalized existence of isoperimetric regions obtained in
[
13
]
(see
Theorem 2.11
), the sharp Laplacian comparison for the distance function from isoperimetric sets obtained in
[
16
]
(see
Theorem 2.13
) and a limiting argument. No assumption is made on the existence of isoperimetric regions for any volume. Nevertheless, the recently developed tools of Geometric Measure Theory on
\RCD
spaces (see
[
69
,
16
]
) allow for a proof very much in the spirit of Gromov’s original proof of the Lèvy-Gromov inequality
[
50
]
.

In our companion paper
[
16
]
we obtained sharp second order differential inequalities for the isoperimetric profile of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
with volume of unit balls uniformly bounded from below.
Indeed, we proved that if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is an
\RCD(K,N)
space and there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
, then
the inequality

(1.4)

-I^{\prime\prime}I\geq K+\frac{(I^{\prime})^{2}}{N-1}

holds in the viscosity sense on
(0,\mathscr{H}^{N}(X))
, see
Theorem 2.17
. The statement generalizes several results previously obtained for smooth Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature lower bounds, either in the compact case, or under uniform bounds on the geometry at infinity in the non compact case. See for instance
[
18
,
70
,
19
,
75
,
67
]
and the introduction of
[
16
]
for a more extended bibliography and a detailed comparison with the existing literature.

An elementary consequence of (
1.4
) that we explore in this paper is that the scale invariant isoperimetric profile

(1.5)

v\mapsto\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}

is monotone decreasing on
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
, see
Theorem 3.8
.

The monotonicity formula for the scale invariant isoperimetric profile on spaces with non negative Ricci curvature has a series of consequences for other functional inequalities that can be obtained with the classical monotone rearrangement technique and are investigated in
subsection 3.4
. Several other geometric and functional inequalities could be studied with analogous techniques.

The limit for large volumes in (
1.5
) is explicitly determined by the sharp isoperimetric inequality
Theorem 1.1
and Bishop–Gromov’s inequality. Indeed, it is easily seen that the limit vanishes when the space has not Euclidean volume growth.

A combination of the ideas introduced in
[
11
]
with the tools developed in our previous paper
[
16
]
and several compactness and stability arguments that fully exploit the
\RCD
theory leads to the asymptotic description of
the isoperimetric behaviour for large volumes on a subclass of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces with Euclidean volume growth that includes Alexandrov spaces with non negative sectional curvature and Euclidean volume growth.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
be a pointed
\RCD(0,N)
space with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})>0
. For every sequence
\{r_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{i}\to+\infty
the sequence of pointed metric measure spaces
\{(X,r_{i}^{-1}\mathsf{d},r_{i}^{-N}\mathfrak{m},x)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is precompact in the pointed measured Gromov–Hausdorff (pmGH) topology, see
subsection 2.1
. Every pmGH limit of such a sequence is a metric cone, by a slight modification of the proof of
[
42
, Proposition 2.8]
(see also
[
34
, Theorem 7.6]
). Any such limit is called an
asymptotic cone of
X
.

By
Euclidean metric measure cone of dimension
N
over a metric measure space, we mean a
(0,N)
-cone over that metric measure space, according to
[
55
, Definition 5.1]
, where the reference measure is
\mathscr{H}^{N}
. Given a Euclidean metric measure cone
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
that is an
\RCD(0,N)
space, we call a
tip
any point
x\in X
such that

\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},x]:=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\,.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 0
, and let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})=(\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\tilde{X},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\times\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, and such that
\tilde{X}
splits no lines. Assume that no asymptotic cone of
\tilde{X}
splits a line. Then

•

there exists
V_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
V\geq V_{0}
there exists an isoperimetric region of volume
V
;

•

let
\{E_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of isoperimetric sets such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{i})=V_{i}
with
V_{i}\to+\infty
, and fix
o\in X
. Then up to subsequence and translations along the Euclidean factor
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, the sequence
\overline{E}_{i}\subset(X,V_{i}^{-\frac{1}{N}}\mathsf{d},V_{i}^{-1}\mathscr{H}^{N},o)
converges in the Hausdorff distance to a closed ball of volume
1
centered at one tip of an asymptotic cone of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
;

•

if there exists
V>0
such that

(1.6)

I(V)=N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}({\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{\frac{1}{N}}V^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,

then
N\geq 2
, and
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is a Euclidean metric measure cone over a compact
\RCD(N-2,N-1)
metric measure space.

We stress that
every
Alexandrov space of dimension
N
with nonnegative curvature and Euclidean volume growth falls in the hypotheses of
Theorem 1.2
, since
[
11
, Theorem 4.6]
holds with the same proof in the setting of Alexandrov spaces.

We remark that
an additional smoothness assumption on the cross sections of the asymptotic cone at infinity of an open manifold with non negative sectional curvature is not sufficient to guarantee smooth convergence to the blow-down, after scaling. Hence the results in
[
37
]
, dealing with manifolds that are
C^{2,\alpha}
asymptotically conical, do not apply in the generality considered above.

The isoperimetric behaviour for large volumes on spaces with non negative Ricci curvature and Euclidean volume growth is determined by the large scale geometry of the space and of its pointed limits at infinity. Dual to this statement, we prove that the isoperimetric behaviour for small volumes of any
\RCD(K,N)
space with volume of unit balls uniformly bounded from below is tightly linked to its structure at small scale and to that of its pointed limits at infinity.

Given any
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))>v_{0}>0
for every
x\in X
, we shall say that a pointed
\RCD(K,N)
space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y)
is a pmGH limit at infinity of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
if there exists a sequence
x_{i}\in X
with
\mathsf{d}(x,x_{i})\to\infty
for some
x\in X
such that
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},x_{i})
converge to
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y)
as
i\to\infty
in the pmGH topology, see
subsection 2.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space with isoperimetric profile function
I_{X}
. Let us assume
\inf_{x\in X}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}>0
. Then:

(1)

It holds

(1.7)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I_{X}(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}=N\left(\omega_{N}\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\,,

where

(1.8)

\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}=\lim_{r\to 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}\,>\,0\,

is the minimum of all the possible densities at any point in
X
or in any pmGH limit at infinity of
X
, and
v(N,K/(N-1),r)
denotes the volume of the ball of radius
r
in the simply connected model space with constant sectional curvature
K/(N-1)
and dimension
N
;

(2)

Let
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
be a sequence of sets with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{i})=:V_{i}\to 0
and
\Per(E_{i})=I_{X}(V_{i})
, where
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}})
is either
X
or a pmGH limit at infinity of
X
.
1
1

1

Such sets always exist by
subsubsection 2.6.3
.

Let
(X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be a pmGH limit point of the sequence
\{(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, up to subsequences, the sets
E_{i}
converge in the Hausdorff sense to a point where
\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
is realized.

(3)

Let
E_{i}
be as in the previous item and let
q_{i}\in E_{i}
. Then, up to subsequences, the sets

\overline{E}_{i}\subset(X_{i},V_{i}^{-1/N}\mathsf{d}_{i},V_{i}^{-1}\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},q_{i})\,,

converge in the Hausdorff sense to a ball of volume 1 centered in one tip of a Euclidean metric measure cone with opening
\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
over an
\RCD(N-2,N-1)

The above seems to be new also for smooth non compact manifolds with Ricci curvature and volumes of unit balls uniformly bounded from below. The proof combines the outcomes of
[
16
]
with
Theorem 1.1
and a series of compactness and stability arguments. It is not difficult to build examples of smooth Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature uniformly bounded from below and volumes of unit balls uniformly bounded from below such that the value in (
1.8
) is strictly less than
1
, see
subsection 4.1
.

Starting from
Theorem 1.3
we prove a converse of the almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality in
[
31
]
, to the effect that an almost Euclidean scale invariant isoperimetric profile for some volume
v\in(0,v_{K,N}]
implies Reifenberg flatness of the space below a uniform radius
r(v,K,N)>0
, see
Theorem 4.8
and
subsection 4.2
for the precise statements.

When the lower Ricci curvature bound is strengthened to a two sided Ricci curvature bound, the gap phenomenon of the classical
[
10
,
35
]
reflects into a gap phenomenon for the almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality. Namely, there exists
\varepsilon(n)>0
such that if
(M^{n},g)
is a smooth Riemannian manifold with two sided Ricci curvature bounds, then

(1.9)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}-\varepsilon(n)\,,

if and only if

(1.10)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\,.

Moreover, (
1.10
) holds if and only if the harmonic radius is uniformly bounded away from zero on
(M^{n},g)
, see
subsection 4.2
.

In the setting of noncollapsed
\RCD(K,N)
spaces, these results connect isoperimetry, volume, and regularity. Indeed, globally and under almost non negative Ricci curvature assumptions, almost regularity, almost Euclidean lower volume bounds and almost Euclidean isoperimetric profile at one scale/volume are all equivalent and propagate down to the bottom scale/volume.

We believe that the techniques introduced here and in the companion paper
[
16
]
will be useful to deal in the future with the general case of
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and, in particular, with smooth weighted Riemannian manifolds verifying Curvature-Dimension bounds.
We refer to the introduction of
[
16
]
for a discussion of the main additional difficulties that are encountered when the assumption
\mathfrak{m}=\mathscr{H}^{N}
is dropped.

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper, by a
metric measure space
(briefly, m.m.s.) we mean a triple
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, where
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a complete and separable
metric space, while
\mathfrak{m}\geq 0
is a boundedly-finite Borel measure on
X
.
For any
k\in[0,\infty)
, we denote by
\mathscr{H}^{k}
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
on
(X,\mathsf{d})
.
We indicate with
C(X)
the space of all continuous functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
C_{b}(X)\coloneqq\{f\in C(X)\,:\,f\text{ is bounded}\}
.
We denote by
{\rm LIP}(X)\subseteq C(X)
the space of all Lipschitz functions, while
{\rm LIP}_{bs}(X)
(resp.
{\rm LIP}_{c}(X)
) stands for
the set of all those
f\in{\rm LIP}(X)
whose support
{\rm spt}f
is bounded (resp. compact). More generally, we denote by

{\rm LIP}_{loc}(X)
the space of locally Lipschitz functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
. Given
f\in{\rm LIP}_{loc}(X)
,

{\rm lip\,}f(x)\vcentcolon=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}

is the
slope
of
f
at
x
, for any accumulation point
x\in X
, and
{\rm lip\,}f(x)\coloneqq 0
if
x\in X
is isolated.

We shall also work with the local versions of the above spaces: given
\Omega\subseteq X
open, we will consider the spaces

{\rm LIP}_{c}(\Omega)\subseteq{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)\subseteq{\rm LIP}(\Omega)\subseteq{\rm LIP}_{loc}(\Omega)
. We underline
that by
{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)
we mean the space of all
f\in{\rm LIP}(\Omega)
having bounded support
{\rm spt}f\subseteq\Omega

that verifies
\mathsf{d}({\rm spt}f,\partial\Omega)>0
.

2.1.
Convergence and stability results

We introduce the pointed measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence already in a proper realization even if this is not the general definition. Nevertheless, the simplified definition of Gromov–Hausdorff convergence via a realization is equivalent to the standard definition of pmGH convergence in our setting, because in the applications we will always deal with locally uniformly doubling measures, see
[
47
, Theorem 3.15 and Section 3.5]
. The following definition is taken from the introductory exposition of
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.1

(pGH and pmGH convergence)
.

A sequence
\{(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pointed metric spaces is said to converge in the
pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology, in the
\mathrm{pGH}
sense for short,
to a pointed metric space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},y)
if there exist a complete separable metric space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
and isometric embeddings

\begin{split}&\Psi_{i}:(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i})\to(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z}),\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N}\,,\\
&\Psi:(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y})\to(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})\,,\end{split}

such that for any
\varepsilon,R>0
there is
i_{0}(\varepsilon,R)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\Psi_{i}(B_{R}^{X_{i}}(x_{i}))\subset\left[\Psi(B_{R}^{Y}(y))\right]_{\varepsilon},\qquad\Psi(B_{R}^{Y}(y))\subset\left[\Psi_{i}(B_{R}^{X_{i}}(x_{i}))\right]_{\varepsilon}\,,

for any
i\geq i_{0}
, where
[A]_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{z\in Z\ :\ \mathsf{d}_{Z}(z,A)\leq\varepsilon\}
for any
A\subset Z
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
and
\mu
be given in such a way
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})
and
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mu,y)
are m.m.s.​ If in addition to the previous requirements we also have
(\Psi_{i})_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{i}\rightharpoonup\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
with respect to duality with continuous bounded functions on
Z
with bounded support, then the convergence is said to hold in the
pointed measured Gromov–Hausdorff topology, or in the
\mathrm{pmGH}
sense for short
.

We need to recall a generalized
L^{1}
-notion of convergence for sets defined on a sequence of metric measure spaces converging in the pmGH sense. Such a definition is given in
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
, and it is investigated in
[
3
]
capitalizing on the results in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.2

(
L^{1}
-strong and
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence)
.

Let
\{(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed metric measure spaces converging in the pmGH sense to a pointed metric measure space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mu,y)
and let
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
be a realization as in
subsection 2.1
.

We say that a sequence of Borel sets
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})<+\infty
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
converges
in the
L^{1}
-strong sense
to a Borel set
F\subset Y
with
\mu(F)<+\infty
if
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})\to\mu(F)
and
\chi_{E_{i}}\mathfrak{m}_{i}\rightharpoonup\chi_{F}\mu
with respect to the duality with continuous bounded functions with bounded support on
Z
.

We say that a sequence of Borel sets
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
converges
in the
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-sense
to a Borel set
F\subset Y
if
E_{i}\cap B_{R}(x_{i})
converges to
F\cap B_{R}(y)
in
L^{1}
-strong for every
R>0
.

Definition 2.3

(Hausdorff convergence)
.

Let
\{(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed metric measure spaces converging in the pmGH sense to a pointed metric measure space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mu,y)
.

We say that a sequence of closed sets
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
converges
in Hausdorff distance
(or
in Hausdorff sense
) to a closed set
F\subset Y
if there holds convergence in Hausdorff distance in a realization
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
of the pmGH convergence as in
subsection 2.1
.

In order to avoid confusion, we remark that the notions of convergence for sets that we recalled above do depend on the specific realization of the pmGH convergence of the ambient spaces into a metric space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
. See for instance the discussion after Definition 3.23 in
[
47
]
, where the notion of convergence of points is presented. However, this dependence does not affect any of the forthcoming arguments in the paper.

It is also possible to define notions of uniform convergence and
H^{1,2}
-strong and weak convergences for sequences of functions on a sequence of spaces
X_{i}
converging in pointed measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
8
]
for such definitions.

2.2.
\rm BV
functions and sets of finite perimeter in metric measure spaces

We begin with the definitions of
function of bounded variation
and
set of finite perimeter
in the setting of m.m.s.

Definition 2.4

(
\rm BV
functions and perimeter on m.m.s.)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. Given
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(X,\mathfrak{m})
we define

|Df|(A)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}\int_{A}{\rm lip\,}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\ :\ \text{$f_{i}\in{\rm LIP}_{loc}(A),\,f_{i}\to f$ in $L^{1}_{loc}(A,\mathfrak{m})$}\right\}\,,

for any open set
A\subseteq X
.
We declare that a function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is of
local bounded variation
, briefly
f\in{\rm BV}_{loc}(X)
,
if
|Df|(A)<+\infty
for every
A\subseteq X
open bounded.
A function
f\in L^{1}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is said to belong to the space of
bounded variation functions

{\rm BV}(X)={\rm BV}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
if
|Df|(X)<+\infty
.

If
E\subseteq{\rm X}
is a Borel set and
A\subseteq X
is open, we define the
perimeter
\Per(E,A)
of
E
in
A
by

\Per(E,A)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}\int_{A}{\rm lip\,}u_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\ :\ \text{$u_{i}\in{\rm LIP}_{loc}(A),\,u_{i}\to{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{E}$ in $L^{1}_{loc}(A,\mathfrak{m})$}\right\}\,,

in other words
\Per(E,A)\coloneqq|D{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{E}|(A)
.
We say that
E
has
locally finite perimeter
if
\Per(E,A)<+\infty
for every open bounded set
A
. We say that
E
has
finite perimeter
if
\Per(E,X)<+\infty
, and we denote
\Per(E)\vcentcolon=\Per(E,X)
.

Let us remark that when
f\in{\rm BV}_{loc}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
or
E
is a set with locally finite perimeter, the set functions
|Df|,\Per(E,\cdot)
above are restrictions to open sets of Borel measures that we still denote by
|Df|,\Per(E,\cdot)
, see
[
4
]
, and
[
65
]
.

In the sequel, we shall frequently make use of the following
coarea formula
, proved in
[
65
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Coarea formula)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(X)
be given. Then for any open set

\Omega\subseteq X
it holds that
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\Per(\{f>t\},\Omega)\in[0,+\infty]
is Borel measurable and satisfies

|Df|(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Per(\{f>t\},\Omega)\,\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

In particular, if
f\in{\rm BV}(X)
, then
\{f>t\}
has finite perimeter for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.6

(Semicontinuity of the total variation under
L^{1}_{loc}
-convergence)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. We recall (cf.
[
65
, Proposition 3.6]
) that whenever
g_{i},g\in L^{1}_{loc}(X,\mathfrak{m})

are such that
g_{i}\to g
in
L^{1}_{loc}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, for every open set
\Omega
we have

|Dg|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}|Dg_{i}|(\Omega)\,.

2.3.
Sobolev functions, Laplacians and vector fields in metric measure spaces

The
Cheeger energy
on a metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is defined as the
L^{2}
-relaxation of the functional

f\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm lip\,}^{2}f_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}
(see
[
6
]
after
[
33
]
). Namely, for any function
f\in L^{2}(X)
we define

{\sf Ch}(f)\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int{\rm lip\,}^{2}f_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\;\bigg|\;(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm LIP}_{bs}(X),\,f_{n}\to f\text{ in }L^{2}(X)\bigg\}\,.

The
Sobolev space

H^{1,2}(X)
is defined as the finiteness domain
\{f\in L^{2}(X)\,:\,{\sf Ch}(f)<+\infty\}
of the Cheeger energy.
The restriction of the Cheeger energy to the Sobolev space admits the integral representation
{\sf Ch}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla f|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}
,
for a uniquely determined function
|\nabla f|\in L^{2}(X)
that is called the
minimal weak upper gradient
of
f\in H^{1,2}(X)
.
The linear space
H^{1,2}(X)
is a Banach space if endowed with the Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}\coloneqq\sqrt{\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+2{\sf Ch}(f)}=\sqrt{\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}},\quad\text{ for every }f\in H^{1,2}(X)\,.

Following
[
46
]
, when
H^{1,2}(X)
is a Hilbert space (or equivalently
{\sf Ch}
is a quadratic form) we say that the metric measure
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is
infinitesimally Hilbertian
.

For the rest of this paper, the infinitesimal Hilbertianity of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
will be our standing assumption.
The results of
[
5
]
ensure that
{\rm LIP}_{bs}(X)
is dense in
H^{1,2}(X)
with respect to the
norm topology. We define the bilinear mapping
H^{1,2}(X)\times H^{1,2}(X)\ni(f,g)\mapsto\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\in L^{1}(X)
as

\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\coloneqq\frac{|\nabla(f+g)|^{2}-|\nabla f|^{2}-|\nabla g|^{2}}{2},\quad\text{ for every }f,g\in H^{1,2}(X)\,.

Given
\Omega\subseteq X
open, we define the
local Sobolev space with Dirichlet boundary conditions

H^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
as the closure
of
{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)
in
H^{1,2}(X)
. Notice that
H^{1,2}_{0}(X)=H^{1,2}(X)
. Moreover, we declare that a given function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)

belongs to the
local Sobolev space

H^{1,2}(\Omega)
provided
\eta f\in H^{1,2}(X)
holds for every
\eta\in{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)
and

|\nabla f|\coloneqq{\rm ess\,sup}\big\{\chi_{\{\eta=1\}}|\nabla(\eta f)|\;\big|\;\eta\in{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)\big\}\in L^{2}(X)\,,

where by
{\rm ess\,sup}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}f_{\lambda}
we mean the essential supremum of a set

\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of measurable functions.

Definition 2.7

(Local Laplacian)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an infinitesimally Hilbertian space and
\Omega\subseteq X
an open set. Then we say that a function

f\in H^{1,2}(\Omega)
has
local Laplacian
in
\Omega
,
f\in D(\Delta,\Omega)
for short, provided there exists a (uniquely determined)
function
\Delta f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}g\Delta f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla g\cdot\nabla f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},\quad\text{ for every }g\in H^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\,.

For brevity, we write
D(\Delta)
instead of
D(\Delta,X)
.

More generally, we will work with functions having a measure-valued Laplacian in some open set:

Definition 2.8

(Measure-valued Laplacian)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an infinitesimally Hilbertian space and
\Omega\subseteq X
an open set. Then we say that a function

f\in H^{1,2}(\Omega)
has
measure-valued Laplacian
in
\Omega
,
f\in D(\mathbf{\Delta},\Omega)
for short, provided there
exists a (uniquely determined) locally finite measure
\mathbf{\Delta}f
on
\Omega
such that

\int_{\Omega}g\mathbf{\Delta}f\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{\Delta}f=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla g\cdot\nabla f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,,\quad\text{ for every }g\in{\rm LIP}_{bs}(\Omega)\,.

For brevity, we write
D(\mathbf{\Delta})
instead of
D(\mathbf{\Delta},X)
. Moreover, given functions
f\in{\rm LIP}(\Omega)\cap H^{1,2}(\Omega)

and
\eta\in C_{b}(\Omega)
, we say that
\mathbf{\Delta}f\leq\eta
in the distributional sense
provided
f\in D(\mathbf{\Delta},\Omega)
and

\mathbf{\Delta}f\leq\eta\mathfrak{m}
.

The above two notions of Laplacian are consistent, in the following sense: given any
f\in H^{1,2}(\Omega)
, it holds that
f\in D(\Delta,\Omega)

if and only if
f\in D(\mathbf{\Delta},\Omega)
,
\mathbf{\Delta}f\ll\mathfrak{m}
and
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{\Delta}f}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. If this is
the case, then we also have that the
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. equality
\Delta f=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{\Delta}f}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}
holds.

2.4.
Geometric Analysis on
\RCD
spaces

The focus of this paper will be on
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, i.e. infinitesimally Hilbertian metric measure spaces with Ricci curvature bounded from below and dimension bounded from above, in synthetic sense.

The Riemannian Curvature Dimension condition
\RCD(K,\infty)
was introduced in
[
7
]
coupling the Curvature Dimension condition
\CD(K,\infty)
, previously proposed in
[
79
,
80
]
and independently in
[
62
]
, with the infinitesimally Hilbertian assumption.

The class
\RCD(K,N)
was proposed in
[
46
]
. The (a priori more general)
\RCD^{*}(K,N)
condition was thoroughly analysed in
[
44
]
and (subsequently and independently) in
[
9
]
(see also
[
29
]
for the equivalence between
\RCD^{*}
and
\RCD
in the case of finite reference measure).

Below we recall some of the main properties of
\RCD
spaces that will be relevant for our purposes.

The
\RCD(K,N)
condition is compatible with the smooth notion. In particular, smooth
N
–dimensional Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below by
K
endowed with the canonical volume measure are
\RCD(K,N)
spaces. Smooth Riemannian manifolds with smooth and convex boundary (i.e. non negative second fundamental form with respect to the interior unit normal) are also included in the theory.

Moreover,
N
-dimensional Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded from below by
K/(N-1)
endowed with the
N
–dimensional Hausdorff measure are
\RCD(K,N)
spaces.

A fundamental property of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces is the stability with respect to pmGH-convergence, meaning that a pmGH-limit of a sequence of (pointed)
\RCD(K_{n},N_{n})
spaces for some
K_{n}\to K
and
N_{n}\to N
is an
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space.

Let us define

\sn_{K}(r):=\begin{cases}(-K)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sinh((-K)^{\frac{1}{2}}r)&K<0\,,\\
r&K=0\,,\\
K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin(K^{\frac{1}{2}}r)&K>0\,.\end{cases}

We denote by
v(N,K,r)
the volume of the ball of radius
r
in the (unique) simply connected Riemannian manifold of sectional curvature
K
and dimension
N
, and by
s(N,K,r)
the surface measure of the boundary of such a ball. In particular
s(N,K,r)=N\omega_{N}\mathrm{sn}_{K}^{N-1}(r)
and
v(N,K,r)=\int_{0}^{r}N\omega_{N}\mathrm{sn}_{K}^{N-1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r
, where
\omega_{N}
is the Euclidean volume of the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For an arbitrary
\CD((N-1)K,N)
space
({\rm X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
the classical Bishop–Gromov volume comparison holds. More precisely, for any
x\in{\rm X}
, the function
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/v(N,K,r)
is nonincreasing in
r
and the function
\Per(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r)
is essentially nonincreasing in
r
, i.e., the inequality

\Per(B_{R}(x))/s(N,K,R)\leq\Per(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r)\,,

holds for almost every radii
R\geq r
, see
[
81
, Theorem 18.8, Equation (18.8), Proof of Theorem 30.11]
. Moreover, it holds that

\Per(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r)\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/v(N,K,r)\,,

for any
r>0
. The last inequality follows from the monotonicity of the volume and perimeter ratios together with the coarea formula on balls.

The Bishop–Gromov inequality implies that
\RCD(K,N)
spaces are locally uniformly doubling. Then Gromov’s precompactness theorem guarantees that any sequence of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n},x_{n})
such that
0<\inf_{n}\mathfrak{m}_{n}(B_{1}(x_{n}))<\sup_{n}\mathfrak{m}_{n}(B_{1}(x_{n}))<\infty
is precompact with respect to the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

For most of the results of this paper we will consider
\RCD(K,N)
spaces of the form
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
, for some
K\in\mathbb{R}

and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Notice that we are requiring that the dimension of the Hausdorff measure coincides with the upper dimensional bound
in the
\RCD
condition. These spaces are typically called
non collapsed
\RCD
spaces
(
{\rm ncRCD}(K,N)
spaces for short) or
N
–dimensional
\RCD(K,N)
spaces
(see
[
59
,
42
,
53
]
).

When
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N},x_{n})
are
\RCD(K,N)
spaces and
\inf_{n}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x_{n}))>0
, up to subsequences they converge to some
N
-dimensional
\RCD(K,N)
space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y)
, which amounts to saying that the
N
–dimensional Hausdorff measures converge to the
N
–dimensional Hausdorff measure of the limit when there is no collapse. This is the so-called volume convergence theorem, originally proved in
[
38
,
35
]
for smooth manifolds and their limits and extended in
[
42
]
to the present setting.

If
({\rm X},\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is an
\RCD((N-1)K,N)
space,
\mathscr{H}^{N}
-almost every point has a unique measured Gromov–Hausdorff tangent isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
[
42
, Theorem 1.12]
after
[
66
]
). Therefore by volume convergence

(2.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K,r)}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=1\,,\qquad\text{for $\mathscr{H}^{N}$-almost every $x$}\,,

where
\omega_{N}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, since the density function
x\mapsto\lim_{r\to 0}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))/\omega_{N}r^{N}
is lower semicontinuous (
[
42
, Lemma 2.2]
), it is bounded above by the constant
1
. Hence
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))\leq v(N,K,r)
for every
r>0
and for every
x\in X
.

Let us now recall some results about sets of locally finite perimeter in
\RCD
spaces. Given a Borel set
E\subseteq X
in an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
and any
t\in[0,1]
, we denote by
E^{(t)}
the set
of
points of density
t
of
E
, namely

E^{(t)}\coloneqq\bigg\{x\in X\;\bigg|\;\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(E\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}=t\bigg\}\,.

The
essential boundary
of
E
is defined as
\partial^{e}E\coloneqq X\setminus(E^{(0)}\cup E^{(1)})
. We have that
E^{(t)}

and
\partial^{e}E
are Borel sets. Furthermore, the
reduced boundary

\mathcal{F}E\subseteq\partial^{e}E
of a given set
E\subseteq X

of finite perimeter is defined as the set of those points of
X
where the unique tangent to
E
(up to isomorphism) is the
half-space
\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,x_{N}>0\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
; see
[
3
, Definition 4.1]
for the precise
notion of convergence we are using. We point out that in the classical Euclidean framework this notion of reduced boundary is not fully consistent
with the usual one, since it allows for non-uniqueness of the blow-ups (in the sense that one can obtain different half-spaces when rescaling
along different sequences of radii converging to
0
).

It was proved in
[
25
]
after
[
2
,
3
]
that the perimeter measure
\Per(E,\cdot)

can be represented as

(2.2)

\Per(E,\cdot)=\mathscr{H}^{N-1}|_{\mathcal{F}E}.

As it is evident from (
2.2
), the notion of perimeter that we are using does not charge the boundary of the space under
consideration, if any. Indeed, by the very definition, reduced boundary points for
E
are regular points of the ambient space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
.
We refer to
[
42
,
53
,
23
]
for the relevant background about boundaries of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
and just point out here that the notion is fully consistent with the case of smooth Riemannian manifolds and with the theory of Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded from below.

Moreover, we recall that, according to
[
24
, Proposition 4.2]
,

\mathcal{F}E=E^{(1/2)}=\bigg\{x\in X\;\bigg|\;\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(E\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg\}\,,\quad\text{ up to }\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-null sets}\,.

The primary focus of this note will be isoperimetric sets, that as in the classical Riemannian setting are much more regular than general sets of finite perimeter.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. We say that a subset
E\subset X
is a
volume constrained minimizer for compact variations in
X
if whenever
F\subset X
is such that
E\Delta F\subset K\Subset X
, and
\mathfrak{m}(K\cap E)=\mathfrak{m}(K\cap F)
, then
\Per(E)\leq\Per(F)
.

We say that a subset
E\subset X
, with
\mathfrak{m}(E)<\infty
, is an
isoperimetric set
whenever for any
F\subset X
with
\mathfrak{m}(F)=\mathfrak{m}(E)
we have that
\Per(E)\leq\Per(F)
.

Notice that an isoperimetric set in
X
is a fortiori a volume constrained minimizer for compact variations in
X
.

Let us recall a topological regularity result for volume constrained minimizers borrowed from
[
14
]
.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
14
, Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space with
2\leq N<+\infty
natural number,
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
E
be a volume constrained minimizer for compact variations in
X
. Then
E^{(1)}
is open,
\partial^{e}E=\partial E^{(1)}
, and
\partial E^{(1)}
is locally uniformly
(N-1)
-Ahlfors regular in
X
.

Assume further there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
, and that
E\subset X
is an isoperimetric region. Then
E^{(1)}
is in addition bounded, and
\partial E^{(1)}
is
(N-1)
-Ahlfors regular in
X
.

In the following, when
E
is an isoperimetric region in a space
X
as in
Theorem 2.10
, we will always assume that
E
coincides with its open bounded representative given by
E^{(1)}
.

The proof of
Theorem 2.10
builds on the top of a deformation lemma for general sets of finite perimeter, see
[
14
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 2.35]
.

2.5.
Asymptotic mass decomposition

The statements below are proved in
[
13
, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 4.1]
building on top of
[
73
,
12
,
14
]
. They describe the precise behaviour of a minimizing (for the perimeter) sequence of sets with constant volume in the setting of
N
-dimensional non compact
\RCD(K,N)
spaces with uniformly bounded volumes of unit balls. The following propositions are at the core of several proofs of this paper and its companion
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.11

(Asymptotic mass decomposition)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be a non compact
\RCD(K,N)
space. Assume there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
. Let
V>0
. For every minimizing (for the perimeter) sequence
\Omega_{i}\subset X
of volume
V
, with
\Omega_{i}
bounded for any

2.6.1.
Mean curvature barriers for isoperimetric sets

It is well-known that if an isoperimetric set

is a hypersurface with constant mean curvature. In
[

(after

Theorem 2.13

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let

In the realm of

just yields the existence of some constant as in the statement, hence we are in position to give the following definition.

Definition 2.14

be an
\RCD(K,N)

Exploiting the coarea formula, the Laplacian bounds in (

\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{t}\setminus E)=t\Per(E)

[\mathscr{H}^{N}(E),\infty)
.

Denote by
\mathsf{d}_{\Omega}
the distance from some set
\Omega
. By (
3.1
) we know that
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{E}\leq 0
on
X\setminus\overline{E}
. On the other hand
\mathsf{d}_{E}=t-\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E_{t}}
on
E_{t}\setminus E
, then

(3.4)

\Delta\mathsf{d}_{E}=\Delta\left(-\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E_{t}}\right)\geq 0\qquad\text{on $E_{t}\setminus\overline{E}$}\,,

where the inequality follows from (
3.1
) applied on the isoperimetric set
E_{t}
, since we already know that any mean curvature barrier for
E_{t}
is non negative. Since
t>0
is arbitrary, we proved that

(3.5)

\Delta\mathsf{d}_{E}=0\qquad\text{on $X\setminus\overline{E}$}.

The isomorphic splitting in the last part of the statement then follows analogously as in the recent
[
57
, Theorem 1.4]
, following the lines of
[
58
]
, extending the classical Riemannian result
[
54
, Theorem C]
. See Corollary 3.18, the beginning of Section 5.2, Corollary 5.6, and Theorem 5.10 in
[
58
]
.

Let us show that, if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is an
\RCD(0,N)
space and
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, then
c>0
. Indeed, in this case
X
is non compact and by the previous discussion we have
c\geq 0
. Let us suppose for the sake of contradiction that
c=0
. From (
2.14
), we have that, for every
t>0
,

(3.6)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,\overline{E})\leq t\})\leq t\Per(E)\,.

Let us now fix
p\in E
, and let
D:=\mathrm{diam}E<\infty
. The diameter is finite because of
Theorem 2.10
. For every
t>D
, we have that
B_{t}(p)\setminus B_{D}(p)\subseteq\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,\overline{E})\leq t\}
. Hence

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,\overline{E})\leq t\})\geq

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{t}(p)\setminus B_{D}(p))

(3.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})t^{N}-\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{D}(p))\,,

which is a contradiction with (
2.14
) for
t
large enough, since
N\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We refer to the recent
[
57
, Theorem 1.1]
for the local counterpart of the rigidity statement in
subsection 3.1
.

subsection 3.1
implies an inscribed radius bound in terms of a mean curvature barrier
c
for an isoperimetric set. The bound is classical in the Riemannian setting, see
[
54
]
, and it has been recently extended to essentially non branching
\CD(0,N)
metric measure spaces in
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
(with a formulation slightly different from ours). Moreover, the equality case can also be characterized, see
[
26
, Theorem 1.4]
, in the
\RCD
case.

We report here the details of the proof in our setting for the reader’s convenience. Let us also point out that the result admits natural generalizations to arbitrary lower Ricci curvature bounds.

We first need a well-known topological result (cf.
[
36
,
53
]
) which can now be more directly deduced employing the recent
[
43
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space for some
N\geq 2
. Let
B_{r}(x)\subset X
be a fixed ball and let
x_{0}\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
. Then for
\mathscr{H}^{N}
-almost every
y\in B_{r}(x)
there exists a geodesic from
x_{0}
to
y
contained in
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
. In particular
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
is connected. Moreover, if
E\subset X
is open and connected and
x\in E
, then
E\setminus\{x\}
is connected.

Proof.

Let
Y\subset B_{r}(x)
be the set of points
y\in B_{r}(x)
such that any geodesic from
x_{0}
to
y
passes through
x
. Since
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a length space, it is immediately checked that
\mathsf{d}(x_{0},y)=\mathsf{d}(x_{0},x)+\mathsf{d}(x,y)
for any
y\in Y
. Suppose by contradiction that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(Y)>0
, then

0<\mathscr{H}^{N}(Y)=\int_{0}^{r}\mathscr{H}^{N-1}(Y\cap\partial^{e}B_{t}(x))\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
N\geq 2
, this implies that there are
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
such that
\mathsf{d}(x,y_{1})=\mathsf{d}(x,y_{2})\in(0,r)
and
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
. If
\gamma_{0}
is a geodesic from
x_{0}
to
x
and
\gamma_{1}
(resp.
\gamma_{2}
) is a geodesic from
x
to
y_{1}
(resp.
y_{2}
), joining
\gamma_{0}
with
\gamma_{1}
(resp.
\gamma_{2}
) yields a geodesic
\sigma_{1}
(resp.
\sigma_{2}
) from
x_{0}
to
y_{1}
(resp.
y_{2}
). Then the couple
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
is branching according to
[
43
, Definition 2.23]
, and this contradicts
[
43
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Hence
\mathscr{H}^{N}(Y)=0
, and since balls are path-connected, this implies that
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
is path-connected as well. Indeed, for any
z,w\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
we can find a point
y\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
(actually a set of full measure of such points) such that any minimizing geodesic from
z
to
y
and any minimizing geodesic from
w
to
y
has image contained in
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
. The concatenation of any two of these geodesics is a continuous path from
z
to
w
with image contained in
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
.

Finally, letting
E
open and connected with
x\in E
, there exists a ball
B_{r}(x)\subset E
. If by contradiction
E\setminus\{x\}=A\sqcup B
for two disjoint nonempty open sets
A,B
, then
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}=(A\cap B_{r}(x))\sqcup(B\cap B_{r}(x))
. Hence we have that, for example,
B\cap B_{r}(x)=\emptyset
and
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}\subset A
. Then
A\cup\{x\}
is open and contained in
E
and it is disjoint from
B
. Moreover
(A\cup\{x\})\sqcup B=E
, implying that
E
is not connected.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Inscribed radius bound)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space for some
N\geq 2
. Let
E\subset X
be a bounded open set such that the signed distance function from
E
satisfies (
3.1
) for some
c>0
. Then

(3.9)

\sup_{x\in E}\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)\leq\frac{N-1}{c}\,.

Moreover, if
E
is connected, then equality holds if and only if
E
is isometric to a ball of radius
\frac{N-1}{c}
centered at one tip of some Euclidean metric measure cone of dimension
N
.

Proof.

The inequality immediately follows for otherwise the second inequality in (
3.1
) degenerates.
Let us now assume that
E
is connected and that equality holds, i.e., there exists a ball of maximal radius
B_{\frac{N-1}{c}}(x_{0})\subset E
. In particular
\partial B_{\frac{N-1}{c}}(x_{0})\cap\partial E\neq\emptyset
. For simplicity, denote
R\coloneqq\frac{N-1}{c}
in this proof. By (
3.1
) we know that

(3.10)

\Delta\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}\leq\frac{-c}{1-\frac{c}{N-1}\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}}\qquad\text{on $E$.}

On the other hand, letting
\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}
be the distance from
x_{0}
, the Laplacian comparison theorem (see
[
46
, Corollary 5.15]
) gives that

(3.11)

\Delta\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}\leq\frac{N-1}{\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}}\qquad\text{on $E\setminus\{x_{0}\}$,}

in the sense of distributions. Letting
F\coloneqq\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}+\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}
we find

(3.12)

\begin{split}\Delta F&\leq\frac{N-1}{\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}}-\frac{(N-1)c}{N-1-c\,\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}}=\frac{(N-1)c}{\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}(N-1-c\,\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E})}\left(\frac{N-1}{c}-F\right),\end{split}

on
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. We observe that
F\geq R
on
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. Indeed
F\geq\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}\geq R
on
E\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
, and
F=\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}+\mathsf{d}_{x_{0}}\geq R
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
as well. Hence
\Delta F\leq 0
on
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. On the other hand
F\equiv R
along a geodesic
\gamma
from
x_{0}
to a point in the boundary of
E
. Since
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
is connected by
subsection 3.1
, by the strong maximum principle this implies that
F\equiv R
on
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. In particular
E\setminus\{x_{0}\}\subset B_{R}(x_{0})
, for otherwise
F>R
at some point in
(E\setminus\{x_{0}\})\setminus\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0})
, and then
E=B_{R}(x_{0})
.

From now on, let us denote
E^{*}\coloneqq E\setminus\{x_{0}\}
.
Since
\Delta F=0
on
E^{*}
, then equality holds in (
3.12
), and then
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}=\frac{-c}{1-\frac{c}{N-1}\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}}
on
E^{*}
. Hence by
[
32
, Corollary 4.16]
we get that

(3.13)

(\log h_{\alpha})^{\prime}=\Delta(-\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E})|_{X_{\alpha}}=\frac{c}{1-\frac{c}{N-1}\mathsf{d}_{X\setminus E}}\bigg|_{X_{\alpha}},

along the corresponding geodesic
X_{\alpha}
, where
\{h_{\alpha},X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in Q}
are given by the disintegration of
\mathscr{H}^{N}
with respect to the signed distance function from
B_{R}(x_{0})
. As
X_{\alpha}
is defined from
x_{0}
up to some point in
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
at least, then
h_{\alpha}
is defined on some interval
[-R,b(X_{\alpha})]
, for
b(X_{\alpha})>0
, and then (
3.13
) reads

(3.14)

\frac{h_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)}{h_{\alpha}(t)}=\frac{c}{1+\frac{c}{N-1}t}\qquad\text{for a.e. $t\in(-R,0]$,}

and then for any
t\in(-R,0]
, for
\mathfrak{q}
-a.e.
\alpha
.
Solving for
h_{\alpha}
yields

(3.15)

h_{\alpha}(t)=h_{\alpha}(0)\left(1+\frac{c}{N-1}t\right)^{N-1}\qquad\text{for $t\in[-R,0]$.}

By disintegration, for any
r\in(0,R)
we deduce that

(3.16)

\begin{split}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x_{0}))&=\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{r}h_{\alpha}(t-R)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}(\alpha)=\left(\int_{Q}h_{\alpha}(0)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{q}(\alpha)\right)\left(\frac{c}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}r^{N}.\end{split}

The conclusion follows by the volume-cone to metric-cone theorem in
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Barrier bounds)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
space for
N\geq 2
and assume that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}>0
for any
x\in X
. Let
E
be an isoperimetric region of volume
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\in(0,\infty)
. Let
c\in[0,+\infty)
be a barrier given from
Theorem 2.13
, cf.
subsection 3.1
. Then

(3.17)

c\leq\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{\Per(E)}{\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)}\,.

Moreover, if
c=\tfrac{N-1}{N}\tfrac{\Per(E)}{\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)}
, then
E
is isometric to a ball of radius
\frac{N-1}{c}
centered at one tip of some Euclidean metric measure cone of dimension
N
.

If also
{\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, then

(3.18)

c\geq(N-1)\left(\frac{N\omega_{N}{\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})}{\Per(E)}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\,.

Proof.

By
subsection 3.1
we have the bound on the distance from the complement

(3.19)

\sup_{x\in E}\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)\leq\frac{N-1}{c}\,,

we can apply (
2.15
), substituting
K=0
, to obtain

(3.20)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)\leq r\})\leq\Per(E)\int_{0}^{r}\left(1-\frac{c}{N-1}s\right)^{N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,,

for any
0<r<\frac{N-1}{c}
.
In particular

(3.21)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\leq\Per(E)\int_{0}^{\frac{N-1}{c}}\left(1-\frac{c}{N-1}s\right)^{N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\Per(E)\frac{(N-1)}{cN},

and (
3.17
) follows.

Now assume that
c=\tfrac{N-1}{N}\tfrac{\Per(E)}{\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)}
. Suppose by contradiction that
\sup_{x\in E}\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)<\tfrac{N-1}{c}
. Then by (
2.13
) and
subsection 3.1
we have that

(3.22)

\begin{split}\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)&=\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)\leq(N-1)/c\})\\
&=\int_{0}^{\frac{N-1}{c}}\Per(\{x\in E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)\leq r\})\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&<\Per(E)\int_{0}^{\frac{N-1}{c}}\left(1-\frac{c}{N-1}r\right)^{N-1}\,\mathrm{d}r=\Per(E)\frac{N-1}{cN}=\mathscr{H}^{N}(E),\end{split}

which is impossible. Therefore
\sup_{x\in E}\mathsf{d}(x,X\setminus E)=\tfrac{N-1}{c}
. Since
E
is connected by
subsubsection 2.6.3
, the rigidity part in
subsection 3.1
implies the claim.

Assume now that
{\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
and let
c\in(0,\infty)
be a mean curvature barrier, see
Theorem 2.13
and
subsection 3.1
. As a consequence of (
2.14
) we have the following

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,E)\leq r\})\leq

\displaystyle\Per(E)\int_{0}^{r}\left(1+\frac{c}{N-1}s\right)^{N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

(3.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Per(E)\frac{N-1}{Nc}\left[\left(1+\frac{cr}{N-1}\right)^{N}-1\right]\,,

for any
0<r<\infty
.
Then we can study the asymptotics of the right hand side above as
r\to\infty
to obtain

(3.25)

\Per(E)\frac{N-1}{Nc}\left[\left(1+\frac{cr}{N-1}\right)^{N}-1\right]=\Per(E)\frac{c^{N-1}}{N(N-1)^{N-1}}r^{N}\left(1+O\left(r^{-1}\right)\right).

Since
E
is bounded by
Theorem 2.10
, the Euclidean volume growth condition implies that, letting
x_{0}\in E
be any point, we have

(3.26)

\begin{split}\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,\mathsf{d}(x,E)\leq r\})&\geq\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{x\in X\ :\ \mathsf{d}(x,E)\leq r\})-\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\\
&\geq\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x_{0}))-\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\\
&\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\omega_{N}r^{N}-\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\\
&=\left(1+O\left(r^{-N}\right)\right)\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\omega_{N}r^{N}\end{split}

as
r\to+\infty
, and then (
3.18
) follows.
∎

3.2.
Sharp and rigid isoperimetric inequalities

In the following we give a new proof, tailored for
\RCD(0,N)
spaces with reference measure
\mathscr{H}^{N}
, of the sharp isoperimetric inequality under the Euclidean volume growth assumption.

The approach presented is suited for dealing with the rigidity case, thus extending the rigidity result for the sharp isoperimetric inequality treated in
[
22
,
17
,
1
,
45
]
. Notice also that the rigidity results in
[
22
,
17
]
need an a-priori hypothesis on the regularity of the boundary of the set
E
. Thus our approach to rigidity not only deals with the larger setting of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces with reference measure
\mathscr{H}^{N}
, but also recovers a slightly empowered version of the rigidity results in the smooth setting, without assuming any regularity of the boundary. Compare with the discussion in
[
17
, Section 5.2]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, and let
E\subset X
be a set of finite perimeter. If
E
is an isoperimetric region, then

(3.27)

\Per(E)\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,.

Moreover equality holds for some
E
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\in(0,\infty)
if and only if
X
is isometric to a Euclidean metric measure cone (of dimension
N
) over an
\RCD(N-2,N-1)
space, and
E
is isometric to a ball centered at one of the tips of
X
.

Proof.

By
subsection 3.1
we get the lower bound

(3.28)

\Per(E)\geq\max\left\{\frac{N\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)c}{(N-1)}\,,\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\omega_{N}\cdot\frac{N(N-1)^{N-1}}{c^{N-1}}\right\}.

Hence

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Per(E)\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)c}{(N-1)}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\omega_{N}\cdot\frac{N(N-1)^{N-1}}{c^{N-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}

(3.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}},

which is the sharp isoperimetric inequality.

Now let us assume that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\in(0,\infty)
and equality holds in (
3.29
). This implies that the two competitors in the right hand side of (
3.28
) are equal, that is

(3.31)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)=\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\left(\frac{N-1}{c}\right)^{N}.

By
subsection 3.1
we know that
E
contains a ball
B
of radius
\tfrac{N-1}{c}
. By Bishop–Gromov monotonicity, the measure of
B
satisfies
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B)\geq\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\left(\tfrac{N-1}{c}\right)^{N}=\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)
. As
B\subset E
, we conclude that
E=B
is a metric ball in
X
. Let us write
E=B_{\frac{N-1}{c}}(x)
for some
x
. By Bishop–Gromov monotonicity we obtain that

\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{R}(x))=\left(\frac{R}{\frac{N-1}{c}}\right)^{N}\mathscr{H}^{N}\left(B_{\frac{N-1}{c}}(x)\right),

for any
R\geq\tfrac{N-1}{c}
. As
\RCD(0,N)
spaces are in particular
\RCD^{*}(0,N)
, we are in position to apply the rigidity result in
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
. Since
X
is endowed with the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{N}
, we conclude that item (3) in
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
holds. Since
R\geq\tfrac{N-1}{c}
is arbitrary, we get that
N\geq 2
and
X
is isometric to a metric measure cone on a bounded
\RCD^{*}(N-2,N-1)
space with finite measure, which is, in particular, also an
\RCD(N-2,N-1)
space by
[
29
]
.
The same rigidity result yields that
E
is the ball centered at one of the tips of
X
.
∎

Though giving the rigidity of the sharp isoperimetric inequality in the setting of
subsection 3.2
, the last argument seems to give an alternative proof of the sharp isoperimetric inequality only if we know a priori that isoperimetric regions exist for every volume on
X
. Nevertheless combining

Theorem 2.11
and
subsection 3.2
we can give an alternative proof of the sharp isoperimetric inequality in the setting of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, together with a characterization of the equality case.
In particular, we obtain an alternative proof of the sharp isoperimetric inequality in the setting of Riemannian manfiolds with non negative Ricci curvature and Euclidean volume growth.

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V:=\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)
, and take
\Omega_{i}
a minimizing sequence of bounded sets of volume
V
. In the setting of
Theorem 2.11
, we have that for every
1\leq j\leq\overline{N}
the inequality
\mathrm{AVR}(X_{j},\mathsf{d}_{j},\mathscr{H}^{N})\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
holds as a consequence of the volume convergence from
[
42
]
and the monotonicity of Bishop–Gromov ratios. Hence, by using the latter inequality, together with (
2.3
), the fact that
\Omega,Z_{j}
are isoperimetric, and
subsection 3.2
, we have

(3.32)

\begin{split}\Per(E)&\geq I(V)=\Per(\Omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\Per(Z_{j})\\
&\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\bigg(\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}+\\
&\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X_{j},\mathsf{d}_{j},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(Z_{j})\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\bigg)\\
&\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(Z_{j})\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\right)\\
&\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\mathscr{H}^{N}(Z_{j})\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\\
&=N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}.\end{split}

The rigidity part of the statement follows from
subsection 3.2
.
∎

The rigidity part of
Theorem 1.1
allows to characterize isoperimetric sets in
\RCD(0,N)
cones.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be a Euclidean metric measure cone (of dimension
N
) over an
\RCD(N-2,N-1)
space, for some
N\geq 2
. Let
\vartheta
be the opening of the cone, i.e., the density at any tip. Then

(3.33)

I_{X}(v)=N(\omega_{N}\vartheta)^{1/N}v^{\frac{N-1}{N}},\qquad\text{for all $v>0$}\,.

Moreover, all the isoperimetric regions in
X
are balls centered at one of the tips.

3.3.
Isoperimetric monotonicity on spaces with non negative Ricci curvature

In this section we derive some further consequences of the sharp concavity properties of the isoperimetric profile for
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
.

Recall that in
[
52
]
a notion of isoperimetric cone angle for an
N
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with non negative Ricci curvature was proposed as

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g):=\inf\left\{\frac{\left(\mathscr{H}^{N-1}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)}\,:\,\emptyset\neq\Omega\subset M\,,\quad\text{$\Omega$ open with smooth boundary}\right\}\,.

With this definition, the sharp isoperimetric inequality for smooth Riemannian manifolds with non negative Ricci curvature (and Euclidean volume growth)
[
22
,
1
,
45
]
could be restated as

(3.34)

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)\geq\left(\mathrm{AVR}(M,g)\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\,,

where
\mathrm{AVR}(M,g)\vcentcolon={\rm AVR}(M,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
for the Riemannian distance
\mathsf{d}
on
(M,g)
.
Then, by employing balls with radii going to infinity, see for instance
[
11
, Corollary 3.6]
, it is not difficult to check that also the converse inequality holds, so that

(3.35)

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)=\left(\mathrm{AVR}(M,g)\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\,.

This shows that the large scale geometry of a manifold with non negative Ricci curvature influences its isoperimetric behaviour down to the bottom
volume scale
.

Thanks to
Theorem 2.17
, we understand that this is true for any two intermediate volume scales, namely the scale invariant isoperimetric profile is monotone decreasing with respect to the volume.

Notice that, in terms of the isoperimetric profile function
I
, the isoperimetric cone angle can be equivalently characterized as

(3.36)

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)=\inf\left\{\frac{I(v)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}v}\,:\,v\in(0,\infty)\right\}\,,

or, introducing the scale invariant isoperimetric profile at volume
v\in(0,\mathscr{H}^{N})
as

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)(v):=\frac{I(v)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}v}=\inf\left\{\frac{\left(\mathscr{H}^{N-1}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(N\omega_{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)}\,:\,\emptyset\neq\Omega\subset M\,,\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)=v\right\}\,,

by

c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)=\inf_{v\in(0,\infty)}c_{\mathrm{iso}}(M,g)(v)\,.

Below we prove that, in the greater generality of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
, the scale invariant isoperimetric profile is monotone decreasing with respect to the volume, without any further assumption on the volume growth. Analogous statements for smooth Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature that are either compact or have uniformly controlled geometry at infinity were obtained in
[
18
,
19
,
67
]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
space with isoperimetric profile function
I
. The following hold:

(1)

the function
I^{\frac{N}{N-1}}
is concave on
(0,\mathscr{H}^{N}(X))
. A fortiori
I
is concave on
(0,\mathscr{H}^{N}(X))
and, if
\mathscr{H}^{N}(X)=+\infty
,
I
is nondecreasing on
(0,+\infty)
;

(2)

we have that

(3.37)

v\mapsto\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\,\,\text{is non-increasing on $(0,\mathscr{H}^{N}(X))$}

and, when
\mathscr{H}^{N}(X)=+\infty
,

(3.38)

\lim_{v\to\infty}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}=N\left(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\,;

(3)

when
\mathscr{H}^{N}(X)=+\infty
,

(3.39)

\lim_{v\to\infty}v^{\frac{1}{N}}I_{+}^{\prime}(v)=(N-1)\left(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\,,

where
I_{+}^{\prime}(v)
is the right derivative of
I
;

(4)

if
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, then
I
is strictly increasing and strictly concave.

Proof.

Let us notice that if
\inf_{x}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))=0
, we have
I\equiv 0
, see
[
11
, Proposition 2.18]
, whose proof adapts in the non-smooth setting since it only relies on Bishop–Gromov monotonicity. Thus this case is trivial. Let us suppose, from now on, that
\inf_{x}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))>0
.

Item (1) readily follows from the continuity of
I
, see
subsubsection 2.6.2
, and (
2.16
).

Let us prove item (2). Since
I^{\frac{N}{N-1}}
is concave and
\lim_{v\to 0^{+}}I^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(v)=0
, we get from concavity that

v\mapsto\frac{I^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(v)}{v},

is non-increasing. Hence we get the first part of the assertion. The asymptotic (
3.38
) follows as in
[
11
, Corollary 3.6]
, since it only relies on the Bishop–Gromov monotonicity and the isoperimetric inequality in
Theorem 1.1
.

Item (3) follows verbatim as in
[
11
, Corollary 3.6]
, since it only relies on the Bishop–Gromov monotonicity and the concavity of
I
.

Let us prove item (4). The fact that
I
is strictly increasing follows verbatim as in
[
11
, Corollary 3.8]
by exploiting the previous items. The fact that
I
is strictly concave follows from the fact that
I^{\frac{N}{N-1}}
is concave and strictly increasing.
∎

The monotonicity of the isoperimetric profile in
Theorem 3.8
also directly implies the following consequence.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
space with infinite volume.

If
E\subset X
is an isoperimetric region, then
\Per(E)\leq\Per(F)
whenever
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)\leq\mathscr{H}^{N}(F)
. In particular
E
is outward minimizing, i.e.,
\Per(E)\leq\Per(F)
whenever
E\subset F
.

If also
{\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
, then the previous inequalities are strict and isoperimetric sets are strictly outward minimizing.

3.4.
Consequences for other geometric and functional inequalities

Here we follow a classical strategy to obtain (sharp) functional inequalities from (sharp) isoperimetric inequalities arguing by rearrangement, see for instance
[
77
,
21
,
64
,
48
]
.

Since we are able to characterize the rigidity in the sharp isoperimetric inequalities, the characterization of rigidity in the sharp functional inequalities will follow as well.

We will improve the existing results in several directions:

•

taking into account the new monotonicity of the quotient
v\mapsto I(v)/v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
, our statements will depend on the isoperimetric behaviour of the space on a fixed range of volumes
v\in[0,\bar{v}]
, rather than on the full range (or, equivalently, on the asymptotic volume ratio);

•

we will characterize the rigidity without technical regularity assumptions for smooth Riemannian manifolds, improving upon the recent
[
17
]
;

•

we will characterize the rigidity in the more general setting of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
. This setting includes as remarkable examples Alexandrov spaces with non negative sectional curvature and cones with non negative Ricci curvature.

We will focus on
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
.

Let us borrow the terminology from
[
68
]
(see also
[
76
, Section 3]
). Given a metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, an open set
\Omega\subset X
with
\mathfrak{m}(\Omega)<\infty
and a Borel function
u:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
we will denote by

(3.40)

\mu(t):=\mathfrak{m}\left(\{u>t\}\right)\,,

the distribution function of
u
and by
u^{\sharp}
the generalized inverse function of
\mu
.

Moreover, given
N\geq 1
we choose
0<r<\infty
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{N}([0,r])=\mathfrak{m}(\Omega)
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{N}=N\omega_{N}r^{N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r
and define the monotone rearrangement
u^{*}
of
u
by

(3.41)

u^{*}(x):=u^{\sharp}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{N}([0,x])\right)\,,\quad\text{for any $x\in[0,r]$}\,.

Notice that, by the very definition,
u
and
u^{*}
have the same distribution function, which implies that

(3.42)

\int_{\Omega}f(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\int_{[0,r]}f(u^{*})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{N}\,,\quad\text{for any Borel function $f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$}\,.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. For any non negative function
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let us also introduce a function
f_{u}:[0,\sup u^{*}]\to[0,\infty)
by

(3.43)

f_{u}(t):=\int\left\lvert\nabla u^{*}\right\rvert^{p-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Per(\{u^{*}>t\})\,,

and notice that, by the coarea formula,

(3.44)

\int_{0}^{\sup u^{*}}f_{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{[0,r]}\left\lvert\nabla u^{*}\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{N}\,.

We shall denote by
I_{N}
the isoperimetric profile of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the canonical Euclidean structure. Notice that it coincides with the isoperimetric profile of the model one dimensional metric measure space
\left([0,\infty),|\cdot|,N\omega_{N}r^{N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)
and it holds
I_{N}(v)=N\omega_{N}^{1/N}v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}
for any
v\geq 0
.

The classical rearrangement argument (see
[
21
,
77
]
for the classical formulations and
[
68
,
76
]
for a more recent one in the setting of
\CD(K,N)
spaces) gives the following.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space. Let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)<\infty
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be non negative and let
u^{*}:[0,r]\to[0,\infty)
be its monotone rearrangement, where
r>0
is such that
\mathfrak{m}_{N}([0,r])=\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)
. Then

(3.45)

\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}\geq\int_{0}^{\sup u^{*}}\left(\frac{I_{(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})}(\mu(t))}{I_{N}(\mu(t))}\right)^{p}f_{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,.

We omit the proof that can be obtained arguing as in
[
68
,
76
]
. We just point out, since this will be relevant in order to address the rigidity issue, that the stronger statement

(3.46)

\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}\geq\int_{0}^{\sup u^{*}}\left(\frac{\Per(\{u>t\})}{I_{N}(\mu(t))}\right)^{p}f_{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,

can be obtained for any non negative function
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for
1<p<+\infty
, such that
u^{*}
has
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-almost everywhere non vanishing derivative on
(0,r)
, see
[
68
, Proposition 3.13]
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space. Let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)<\infty
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be non negative and let
u^{*}:[0,r]\to[0,\infty)
be its monotone rearrangement, where
r>0
is such that
\mathfrak{m}_{N}([0,r])=\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)
. Then

(3.47)

\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}\geq\left(\frac{I_{(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})}(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega))}{I_{N}(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega))}\right)^{p}\int_{[0,r]}\left\lvert\nabla u^{*}\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{N}\,.

Proof.

The result follows from
subsection 3.4
, thanks to the monotonicity formula (
3.37
).
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. In
[
1
,
45
,
17
]
under different assumptions the comparison

(3.48)

\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}\geq\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\int_{[0,r]}\left\lvert\nabla u^{*}\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{N}\,

was deduced from the sharp isoperimetric inequality, via symmetric rearrangement.

The estimate (
3.47
) is easily seen to imply (
3.48
) since

(3.49)

v\mapsto\frac{I_{(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})}(v)}{I_{N}(v)}\downarrow\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\,,\quad\text{as $v\to+\infty$}\,,

by
Theorem 3.8
. In fact, it is strictly stronger, since it requires control over the isoperimetric profile up to volume
\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)
instead of requiring control over the isoperimetric profile for all volumes (or, equivalently, on the asymptotic volume ratio of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
).

Given any
1<p<\infty
and any bounded and open domain
\Omega\subset X
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
we shall denote by
\lambda^{1,p}(\Omega)
the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\Omega
. It is well known that it admits the classical variational interpretation

(3.50)

\lambda^{1,p}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}}{\int_{\Omega}f^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{N}}\,:\,f\in\Lip_{c}(\Omega)\right\}\,.

Moreover, following
[
39
,
40
]
we introduce a whole scale of
p
-isoperimetric profile functions
I_{p}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

(3.51)

I_{p}(v):=\inf\left\{\lambda^{1,p}(\Omega)\,:\,\Omega\subset X\,,\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)=v\right\}\,.

We shall also denote by
I_{p,N}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
the function associating to any volume
v\in(0,\infty)
the lowest first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions on open domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\mathscr{L}^{N}(\Omega)=v
. It is well known that
I_{p,N}
is the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions on a ball
B_{r}(0^{N})
such that
\mathscr{L}^{N}(B_{r}(0^{N}))=v
. In particular,
I_{p,N}(v)=C_{p,N}v^{-\frac{p}{N}}
, for some constant
C_{p,N}>0
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space. Then, for any
1<p<\infty
it holds

(3.52)

\frac{I_{p}(v)}{I_{p,N}(v)}\geq\left(\frac{I_{(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})}(v)}{I_{N}(v)}\right)^{p}\geq\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\,,\quad\text{for any $0<v<\infty$}\,.

Proof.

The statement follows directly from
subsection 3.4
and
subsection 3.4
by the classical symmetric rearrangement argument and the variational characterization of the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

The fact that the isoperimetric profile controls the whole scale of
p
-isoperimetric profiles is classical
[
39
,
49
]
. However, in all the references we are aware of, control was intended up to constants. The observation that the isoperimetric profile controls the
p
-spectral gap without the need of additional constants seems to be new even for smooth Riemannian manifolds with non negative Ricci curvature and it fully exploits the monotonicity
Theorem 3.8
.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure space with
\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})>0
. Let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open and bounded domain. If

(3.53)

\lambda^{1,p}(\Omega)=\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}I_{p,N}(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega))\,,

then
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is isomorphic to a metric measure cone over an
\RCD(N-1,N)
metric measure space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N-1})
. In particular, if
(X,\mathsf{d})
is isometric to a smooth Riemannian manifold, then
(X,\mathsf{d})
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the analogous rigidity statement proved for
\RCD(N-1,N)
metric measure spaces in
[
68
]
building on the top of the rigidity statement for the Lévy–Gromov isoperimetric inequality obtained in
[
30
]
, so we just outline it.

Notice that the spectral gap of
\Omega
is attained by a function
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by a classical argument. Under our assumptions, the rearrangement
u^{*}
is a first eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
([0,r],|\cdot|,\mathfrak{m}_{N})
. In particular, it is a classical fact that it has non vanishing derivative
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e. on
[0,r]
and (
3.46
) holds.

Since equality holds in the spectral gap inequality, equality holds in (
3.46
). In particular,

(3.54)

\Per(\{u>t\})=\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}I_{N}(\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{u>t\}))\,,

for
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in[0,\sup u^{*}]
.
This is sufficient to get the first conclusion in the statement, thanks to
subsection 3.2
.

The second conclusion directly follows since the unique
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure cone
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
which is smooth is
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

The rigidity
subsection 3.4
is new also for smooth Riemannian manifolds with non negative Ricci curvature and Euclidean volume growth, since it removes the additional regularity assumptions required in
[
17
]
.

3.5.
Asymptotic isoperimetric behaviour for nonnegatively Ricci curved spaces with stable asymptotic cones and Euclidean volume growth

In the context of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces verifying suitable conditions on their asymptotic cones and with Euclidean volume growth we can show, heavily leveraging on the ideas developed in
[
11
]
, that isoperimetric regions exist for any sufficiently large volume, and that, up to translations along Euclidean factors and scalings, they converge to balls in the asymptotic cone at infinity in the Hausdorff sense.

Moreover, in the same class, the rigidity in
Theorem 1.1
holds just under the assumption that the isoperimetric profile of the space equals the one of the cone with same
{\rm AVR}
and dimension,
for some
volume
V>0
. We stress again that the latter class of non negatively Ricci curved spaces encompasses the class of finite dimensional Alexandrov spaces with nonnegative curvature and Euclidean volume growth, cf.
subsection 3.5
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

The first item comes from a straightforward adaptation of the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
to the setting of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces, taking into account the generalized asymptotic mass decomposition
Theorem 2.11
and the properties proved in
Theorem 3.8
.

Let us sketch the proof, by referring the reader to
[
11
]
for the complete argument. The proof of
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
leverages on
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
, which is already formulated in the non smooth setting, and on
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
. The analogue of
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
can be readily proven in the setting of
\RCD(0,N)
spaces. Indeed, as a consequence of item (3) of
subsubsection 2.6.2
, and item (1) of
subsubsection 2.6.3
, the minimization process on the space, when ran as in
Theorem 2.11
, either produces an isoperimetric region or exactly one piece escaping in a pmGH limit at infinity. Having this at disposal the contradiction argument of
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
applies verbatim also in the non smooth case.

In order to prove the second item, assume first that
X
does not split any line. Hence, from the assumption, no asymptotic cone of it splits any line as well.

Let us fix
o\in X
and denote
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},o):=(X,V_{i}^{-1/N}\mathsf{d},V_{i}^{-1}\mathscr{H}^{N},o)
. Let us also take
q_{i}\in E_{i}
. Taking into account (
2.19
), for every
i\geq 1
, the following hold

(3.55)

\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}}(E_{i})=1,\qquad\mathrm{diam}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}}(E_{i})\leq D\,,

where
D
is a constant depending on
N,\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
.
Moreover,
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},o)\to(C,\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},\tilde{o})
, as
i\to+\infty
, where
(C,\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},\tilde{o})
is an asymptotic cone of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
.

We now distinguish two cases.

•

Suppose

\mathsf{d}_{i}(o,q_{i})=V_{i}^{-1/N}\mathsf{d}(o,q_{i})\to+\infty
. Hence
\mathsf{d}(o,q_{i})\to+\infty
as
i\to+\infty
. Let us call
r_{i}:=\mathsf{d}_{i}(o,q_{i})
. Let
(C^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{\infty}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},\tilde{o}^{\prime})
be an asymptotic cone obtained as a limit of a subsequence of
(X,r_{i}^{-1}\mathsf{d},r_{i}^{-N}\mathscr{H}^{N},o)
. From
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
and the fact that
C^{\prime}
does not split a line by assumption, we have that
\vartheta[C^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{\infty}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},o^{\prime}]\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon
, for some
\varepsilon>0
, and for every
o^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
such that
\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(\tilde{o}^{\prime},o^{\prime})=1
. Arguing as in the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
, by volume convergence there exists
\rho>0
such that

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{\rho r_{i}}(q_{i}))}{\omega_{N}(\rho r_{i})^{N}}\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon/2\,,

for every
i
sufficiently large.

Let
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},q_{i})
converge, up to subsequence, to a pmGH limit
(C^{\prime\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty})
.
By Bishop–Gromov monotonicity, for every
R>0
and for any
i
sufficiently large the following holds

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{RV_{i}^{-1/N}}(q_{i}))}{\omega_{N}\left(RV_{i}^{-1/N}\right)^{N}}\geq\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{\rho r_{i}}(q_{i}))}{\omega_{N}\left(\rho r_{i}\right)^{N}}\,,

since
\mathsf{d}(o,q_{i})\to+\infty
. Hence, by volume convergence,

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{R}(q_{\infty}))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon/2\,,

for every
R>0
, from which
\mathrm{AVR}(C^{\prime\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N})\geq\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon/2
.

By (
3.55
), we can apply
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
. Therefore, up to subsequences,
E_{i}\subset(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},q_{i})
converges in
L^{1}
-strong to a set of finite perimeter
E_{\infty}\subset(C^{\prime\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty})
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{\infty})=1
. Moreover

(3.56)

\begin{split}\Per(E_{\infty})&\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\Per(E_{i})=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}V_{i}^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}\Per(E_{i})\\
&=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}V_{i}^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}I(V_{i})=N(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{\frac{1}{N}}\,,\end{split}

where in the last equality we are using the asymptotic in
Theorem 3.8
. However, from the sharp isoperimetric inequality on
C^{\prime\prime}
and taking into account that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{\infty})=1
, we have

\Per(E_{\infty})\geq N(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(C^{\prime\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq N(\omega_{N}(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon/2))^{\frac{1}{N}}\,,

which is a contradiction with (
3.56
). Thus this case cannot occur.

•

Hence
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{d}_{i}(o,q_{i})<+\infty
. In this case, up to subsequence, there exists a point
o^{\prime}
at finite distance from
o
in
C
such that
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}},q_{i})\to(C,\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},o^{\prime})
. Arguing as in the previous case we get that, up to subsequences, by the lower semicontinuity of the perimeter and the sharp isoperimetric inequality, the sequence
E_{i}
converges in
L^{1}
-strong to
E\subset(C,\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N})
such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E)=1
and

\Per(E)=N(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{\frac{1}{N}}\,.

Hence, by the rigidity of the isoperimetric inequality,
E
is isometric to the ball of volume
1
centered at some tip in
C
.

By
Theorem 2.21
, convergence in
L^{1}
-strong of the
E_{i}
’s implies convergence in Hausdorff distance in some realization. Thus the proof is completed when
X
does not split any line.

In the remaining general case, we can write
X=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\tilde{X}
, with
k\geq 1
, and such that
\tilde{X}
is such that no asymptotic cone of
\tilde{X}
splits a line. Let
o
and
q_{i}
be as above. Up to a translation along the Euclidean factor, we may always assume that
q_{i}=(0,\tilde{q}_{i})
, where
\tilde{q}_{i}\in\tilde{X}
. This prevents the fact that the component along
\mathbb{R}^{k}
of
q_{i}
might go to infinity.
Hence one can argue as in the previous case, by slightly modifying the argument, distinguishing the case in which
\mathsf{d}_{i}(o,q_{i})\to+\infty
, which eventually does not occur, and the case
\sup_{i}\mathsf{d}_{i}(o,q_{i})<+\infty
.

Let us now prove the third item. Let
\Omega_{i}\subset X
be a perimeter minimizing sequence of measure
\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega_{i})=V
, i.e.,
\Per(\Omega_{i})\to I(V)
, and let
p_{i,j},p_{j},\Omega,Z_{j},X_{j},\overline{N}
be given by
Theorem 2.11
. If no mass is lost in the limit, then there exists an isoperimetric region of volume
V
and the result follows from
Theorem 1.1
.

So let us assume that
\overline{N}\geq 1
, and then, by
subsubsection 2.6.3
, we actually have
\overline{N}=1
and
\mathscr{H}^{N}(\Omega)=0
. We have that
X=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\tilde{X}
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
and
\mathsf{d}=\mathsf{d}_{\rm eu}\times\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}}
, where
\tilde{X}
is such that no asymptotic cone of it splits a line.
We can rename the only diverging sequence of points
p_{i,1}
into
p_{i,1}=(q_{i},y_{i})
. It is a standard fact to check that
{\rm AVR}(\tilde{X},\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}},\mathscr{H}^{N-k})={\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
. Observe that in this case,
X_{1}
is the only limit space obtained by applying
Theorem 2.11
.

If
\sup_{i}\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}}(y_{i},y_{0})<+\infty
for some
y_{0}\in\tilde{X}
, then
X_{1}
is isometric to
X
. Hence the limit set
Z_{1}
is an isoperimetric region of volume
V
in a copy of
X
, and then again the claim follows as if no mass were lost.

Assume then that
\lim_{i}\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}}(y_{i},y_{0})=+\infty
.
We can write
X_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\widetilde{Y}
and
p_{1}=(q,y)
, where
(\tilde{X},\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}},\mathscr{H}^{N-k},y_{i})\to(\widetilde{Y},\mathsf{d}_{\widetilde{Y}},\mathscr{H}^{N-k},y)
in the pmGH sense. Recall that no asymptotic cone of
\tilde{X}
splits a line.
It then follows from
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
{\rm AVR}(X_{1},\mathsf{d}_{X_{1}},\mathscr{H}^{N})={\rm AVR}(\widetilde{Y},\mathsf{d}_{\widetilde{Y}},\mathscr{H}^{N-k})\geq{\rm AVR}(\tilde{X},\mathsf{d}_{\tilde{X}},\mathscr{H}^{N-k})+\varepsilon
. Hence, by
Theorem 2.11
and
Theorem 1.1
, we get that

(3.57)

\begin{split}N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}&({\rm AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{\frac{1}{N}}V^{\frac{N-1}{N}}=I(V)=\Per(Z_{1})\\
&\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\left(\mathrm{AVR}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}V^{\frac{N-1}{N}},\end{split}

which yields a contradiction.

∎

) is achieved at some volume

.

4.
Asymptotic isoperimetric behaviour for small volumes and almost regularity theorems

In this last section we combine the second order differential inequalities and monotonicity for the isoperimetric profile with the sharp and rigid isoperimetric inequality for
\RCD(0,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
and the strong stability of isoperimetric regions to determine the asymptotic isoperimetric behaviour for small volumes of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces with a uniform lower bound on the volume of unit balls. Then we prove new global
\varepsilon
-regularity results formulated in terms of the isoperimetric profile.

4.1.
Asymptotic isoperimetric behaviour for small volumes

Given an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, we call
\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x]
the
density at the point
x\in X
defined as

\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x]:=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space such that
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}>0
for every
x\in X
and some
v_{0}>0
. Then the following hold.

(1)

For every
p\in X
we have that

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I_{X}(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},p])^{1/N}.

As a consequence, if
K=0
, for every tangent cone
C_{p}
at
p
we have

I_{X}(v)\leq I_{C_{p}}(v),\qquad\text{for every $v>0$}.

(2)

Let
\{p_{i}\}
be a sequence of points on
X
. Up to subsequences
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},p_{i})
converge to an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},p_{\infty})
. Let
\lambda_{i}\to+\infty
be a diverging sequence. Up to subsequences,
(X,\lambda_{i}\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\lambda_{i}\mathsf{d}},p_{i})
converge to an
\RCD(0,N)
space
(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},p^{\prime})
. Then

\mathrm{AVR}(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N})\geq\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},p_{\infty}].

As a consequence, if
C_{\infty}
is a tangent cone of
X_{\infty}
at
p_{\infty}
, the following holds

I_{C_{\infty}}(v)\leq I_{X^{\prime}}(v),\qquad\text{for every $v>0$}.

Proof.

Let us prove the first item. Let us denote for simplicity
\vartheta:=\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},p]>0
. By definition of density and thanks to the Bishop–Gromov monotonicity and the coarea formula, we get that

\vartheta=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(p))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=\mathrm{esslim}_{r\to 0}\frac{\Per(B_{r}(p))}{N\omega_{N}r^{N-1}}\,.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
v_{\varepsilon}
such that for every
v\leq v_{\varepsilon}
there exists
r_{v}
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{v}}(p))=v
and

\Per(B_{r_{v}}(p))\leq N(\omega_{N}(\vartheta+\varepsilon))^{1/N}v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,.

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
v_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq N(\omega_{N}(\vartheta+\varepsilon))^{1/N},\qquad\text{for all $v\leq v_{\varepsilon}$}\,.

Hence for every
\varepsilon>0
we have that, taking into account the existence of the limit in item (1) in
subsubsection 2.6.2
,

(4.1)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq N(\omega_{N}(\vartheta+\varepsilon))^{1/N}\,.

Thus taking
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
4.1
)
we get the first sought conclusion of the first item.

The second conclusion of the first item follows from the first, the fact that
v\mapsto I(v)/v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}
is nonincreasing thanks to item (2) of
Theorem 3.8
, and
subsection 3.2
, since the opening at any tip of a tangent cone at
p
is
\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},p]
.

Let us prove the second item. Let us call for simplicity
\vartheta:=\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},p_{\infty}]
. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(4.2)

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(p_{\infty}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}>\vartheta-\varepsilon,\qquad\text{for all $0<r\leq r_{\varepsilon}$}\,.

By volume convergence and (
4.2
), for
i
large enough we have

(4.3)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{\varepsilon}/2}(p_{i}))/(v(N,K/(N-1),r_{\varepsilon}/2))>\vartheta-2\varepsilon\,.

Hence by Bishop–Gromov monotonicity we deduce that, for
i
large enough,

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(p_{i}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}>\vartheta-2\varepsilon,\qquad\text{for all $0<r\leq r_{\varepsilon}/2$}\,.

In particular, for every
R>0
, we have that, since
\lambda_{i}\to+\infty
, and since
v(N,K/(N-1),r)/(\omega_{N}r^{N})\to 1
as
r\to 0
, for
i
large enough it holds

(4.4)

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\lambda_{i}\mathsf{d}}(B_{R}^{\lambda_{i}\mathsf{d}}(p_{i}))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}=\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{R/\lambda_{i}}(p_{i}))}{\omega_{N}(R/\lambda_{i})^{N}}>\vartheta-3\varepsilon.

Hence, from (
4.4
) and volume convergence, we get that for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
R>0
it holds

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{R}(x^{\prime}))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}>\vartheta-3\varepsilon\,.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
and
R\to+\infty
we get the first part of the sought claim in item (2).

For the second part it suffices to notice that
I_{C_{\infty}}(v)=N(\omega_{N}\vartheta)^{1/N}v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}
, as a consequence of
subsection 3.2
, and then
I_{C_{\infty}}(v)\leq N(\omega_{N}(\mathrm{AVR}(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N})))^{1/N}v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq I_{X^{\prime}}(v)
by the first part of the item and the isoperimetric inequality.
∎

Below we determine the asymptotic isoperimetric behaviour for small volumes on
\RCD(K,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
with volumes of unit balls uniformly bounded from below. Namely, we give the proof of
Theorem 1.3
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.3
.

Let us first prove that

(4.5)

\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}:=\inf\{\vartheta[Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y]:\text{$y\in Y$, $Y$ is $X$ or a pmGH limit at infinity of $X$}\},

is realized. For simplicity let us call
\vartheta:=\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
.

It suffices to take a minimizing sequence
(Y_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{i})
such that
\vartheta[Y_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{i}]\to\vartheta
. Since
\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{1}(y_{i}))\geq v_{0}
, by volume convergence
[
42
, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 1.3]
we can extract a subsequence such that
(Y_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{i})\to(Y_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{\infty})
as
i\to+\infty
. A simple diagonal argument tells that
Y_{\infty}
is either isometric to
X
or to a pmGH limit at infinity of it. Moreover, from
[
42
, Lemma 2.2]
, we get that

\vartheta[Y_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{\infty}]\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\vartheta[Y_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{i}]=\vartheta,

and thus
\vartheta[Y_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{\infty}]=\vartheta
showing that the infimum is attained and then it must be strictly positive.

In order to verify (
1.8
) let us first prove that

(4.6)

\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}\,.

Let us consider a decreasing sequence
r_{i}\downarrow 0
, such that

\liminf_{r\to 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}\,.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
let us choose
x_{i}\in X
such that

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}\leq\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}+i^{-1}\,.

Up to subsequences,
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},x_{i})\to(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime})
in the pmGH sense. We have, by the very definition of
\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
, that
\vartheta[X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime}]\geq\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Hence there exists
i_{0}
sufficiently large such that for every
i\geq i_{0}
,

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x^{\prime}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}\geq\vartheta[X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime}]-\varepsilon\,.

By volume convergence and Bishop–Gromov comparison, for every
R<r_{i_{0}}
, we have that, for
i
sufficiently large, the following holds

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}\geq\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{R}(x_{i}))}{v(N,K/(N-1),R)}\geq\vartheta[X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime}]-2\varepsilon\,.

Hence, for i sufficiently large, we obtain

\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r_{i})}+i^{-1}\geq\vartheta[X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime}]-2\varepsilon

and by taking
i\to+\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
in the previous, we finally obtain (
4.6
).

Now notice that

(4.7)

\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}\geq\limsup_{r\to 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K/(N-1),r)}\,.

is just a consequence of Bishop–Gromov monotonicity, jointly with the volume convergence when
\vartheta_{\infty,\mathrm{min}}
is reached at infinity. Joining together (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we proved (
1.8
).

Let us prove (
1.7
). We first check that

(4.8)

\liminf_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\geq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta)^{1/N}\,.

Let us take an arbitrary sequence
V_{i}\to 0
as
i\to+\infty
. We apply
Theorem 2.11
, together with
subsubsection 2.6.3
. Up to subsequences, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N})
, which is either isometric to
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
or to a pmGH limit at infinity of it, such that there exists an isoperimetric set
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{i})=V_{i}
, and
I(V_{i})=I_{X_{i}}(V_{i})=\Per(E_{i})
. Let us choose arbitrary points
q_{i}\in E_{i}
. The pointed metric measure spaces
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{i})
converge, up to subsequences, to
(X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty})
thanks to the uniform lower bound on the volume of balls and to volume convergence. By a simple diagonal process, we infer that
X_{\infty}
is either isometric to
X
or to a pmGH limit at infinity of it.

Let us rename, for simplicity,
(X^{\prime}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime}},q_{i}^{\prime}):=(X_{i},V_{i}^{-1/N}\mathsf{d}_{i},V_{i}^{-1}\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{i})
. Up to subsequences, arguing as above, we have

(X^{\prime}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime}},q_{i}^{\prime})\to(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{X^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}^{N},x^{\prime})\,,

as
i\to+\infty
. From
subsection 4.1
we get that

(4.9)

\mathrm{AVR}(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{X^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}^{N})\geq\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty}]\,.

Moreover,
\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime}}(E_{i})=1
and, by
subsubsection 2.6.3
,
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime}}E_{i}\leq k
, where
k
only depends on
N,K,v_{0}
. Hence we can apply
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
to get that, up to subsequences,
E_{i}\subset(X^{\prime}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime},\mathscr{H}^{N}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}^{\prime}},q_{i}^{\prime})
converge in
L^{1}
-strong to a finite perimeter set
E_{\infty}\subset X^{\prime}
with
\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{\infty})=1
. Moreover, from
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
we get that

(4.10)

\begin{split}\Per(E_{\infty})&\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\Per(E_{i})=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}V_{i}^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}\Per(E_{i})\\
&=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}V_{i}^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}I(V_{i})\,.\end{split}

From the sharp isoperimetric inequality (
1.3
) applied on
(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{X^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}^{N})
and (
4.9
), we have

(4.11)

\begin{split}\Per(E_{\infty})&\geq N(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{X^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{1/N}\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{\infty})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\geq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty}])^{1/N}\\
&\geq N(\omega_{n}\vartheta)^{1/N}\,.\end{split}

Joining together (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we conclude that

(4.12)

\liminf_{i\to+\infty}V_{i}^{-\frac{N-1}{N}}I(V_{i})\geq\Per(E_{\infty})\geq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty}])^{1/N}\geq N(\omega_{n}\vartheta)^{1/N},

and thus we obtain
(
4.8
).

Let us now prove that

(4.13)

\limsup_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta)^{1/N}\,.

Let us take
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y)
, with

isometric to

or to some pmGH limit at infinity of it, such that

.
Hence, by

we have that

for every

. Moreover, from the first item of

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I_{Y}(v)}{v^{(N-1)/N}}\leq N(\omega_{N}\vartheta)^{1/N}.

Putting the last two inequalities together, we get
(
4.13
). Then (
1.7
) follows taking also (
4.8
) into account.

The proof of the second item readily comes from the proof of the first item above. Indeed, from (

) and (

) we conclude that all the inequalities in (

) are equalities and thus
\vartheta[X_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},q_{\infty}]=\vartheta
. Hence

is a point at which the minimum in (

) is reached. Moreover, since
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathsf{d}_{i}}E_{i}\to 0
, due to the fact that

and (2) in

, we get that the sets

converge to

in Hausdorff distance in a realization.

For the proof of the third item we exploit the proof of the first item above. Indeed, from (

) and (

) we conclude that all the inequalities in (

) and (

\Per(E_{\infty})=N(\omega_{N}\mathrm{AVR}(X^{\prime},\mathsf{d}_{X^{\prime}},\mathscr{H}^{N}))^{1/N}\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{\infty})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,

thus

saturates the sharp isoperimetric inequality

. Hence, by the rigidity part of

,

is a Euclidean metric measure cone over an

and

is a ball centred at one of the tips of

. Finally, applying

\overline{E}_{i}\subset X_{i}^{\prime}
the the balls

holds in Hausdorff sense.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is a compact
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space, then the statement of

simplifies, since it is not necessary to consider pointed limits at infinity and

is the minimal density at a point

Remark 4.3
.

In (
1.7
), it may occur that
\vartheta_{\infty,\min}<1
also on smooth Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below and volume of unit balls bounded below. For example, let
M=\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
be endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
of the form
g={\rm d}t^{2}+{\rm d}r^{2}+\sigma(t,r)^{2}{\rm d}\theta^{2}
, where
t\in\mathbb{R},

r\in[0,+\infty)
,
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
(\mathbb{S}^{1},{\rm d}\theta^{2})
is the circle of radius
1
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we can arrange

\sigma(t,r)=\begin{cases}r&\text{if }r\in[0,R_{1,t}],\\
h(t)+\frac{r}{2}&\text{if }r\in[R_{2,t},+\infty),\end{cases}

where
0<R_{1,t}<R_{2,t}\to 0
and
0<h(t)\to 0
as
|t|\to+\infty
, and
r\mapsto\sigma(t,r)
is concave for any
t
. Then
(M,g)
is smooth and without boundary. Moreover, a direct computation of the Ricci curvature on the frame
\partial_{t},\partial_{r},\partial_{\theta}/\sigma
yields

\begin{aligned} &\Ric(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=-\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}\sigma}{\sigma},\\
&\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=-\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\sigma}{\sigma},\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} &\Ric\left(\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sigma},\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sigma}\right)=-\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}\sigma}{\sigma}-\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\sigma}{\sigma},\\
&\Ric(\partial_{t},\partial_{r})=-\frac{\partial_{t}\partial_{r}\sigma}{\sigma},\end{aligned}

and
\Ric(\partial_{t},\partial_{\theta}/\sigma)=\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{\theta}/\sigma)=0
. By concavity with respect to
r
, we ensure that
-\tfrac{\partial_{r}^{2}\sigma}{\sigma}\geq 0
, and by taking
t\mapsto R_{1,t},R_{2,t}
varying sufficiently slowly, we can set
\Ric\geq K
on
M
for some
K\leq 0
.

On the other hand,
(M,g)
looks like a smoothing of the cone
C\vcentcolon=(M,{\rm d}t^{2}+{\rm d}r^{2}+(r/2)^{2}{\rm d}\theta)
where the smoothing effect worsens as
|t|\to+\infty
. Indeed, the pmGH limit of
(M,g)
along a sequence of the form
(t_{i},0,q)
for
t_{i}\to+\infty
is the cone
C
. Hence
\vartheta_{\infty,\min}
in this case is achieved by the density at any point
(t,0,q)\in C
, and it is strictly less than
1
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be
\RCD(K,N)
spaces with diameters uniformly bounded from above by
0<D<\infty
, assume that
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{H}^{N}(X_{n})>0
, and assume that they converge to

in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense. Then

(4.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{v\in[0,1]}\left|\frac{\bar{I}_{n}(v)}{\bar{I}(v)}-1\right|=0\,,

if and only if

(4.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\min_{x\in X_{n}}\vartheta[X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N},x]=\min_{x\in X}\vartheta[X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N},x]\,.

Proof.

The pointwise convergence of the normalized isoperimetric profiles under these assumptions is well known and it follows from the convergence and stability of functions of bounded variation and sets of finite perimeter, see
[
8
]
.

The pointwise convergence can be strengthened to locally uniform convergence on compact subsets
[\alpha,\beta]\Subset[0,1]
thanks to the uniform Lipschitz property of the isoperimetric profiles, see
subsubsection 2.6.2
.
Then the uniform convergence to
1
of the ratios
\bar{I}_{n}/\bar{I}
on compact subsets of
(0,1)
follows since
\bar{I}
is locally uniformly bounded away from
0
on
(0,1)
under our assumptions.

The implication from (
4.15
) to (
4.16
) follows from the explicit asymptotic behaviour of the isoperimetric profile for small volumes in terms of the minimal density
Theorem 1.3
.

The implication from (
4.16
) to (
4.15
) follows from item (2) of
subsubsection 2.6.2
,
Theorem 1.3
and
[
19
, Lemma B.3.4]
.
∎

4.2.
Isoperimetric almost regularity theorems

It is a classical result, pointed out for instance in
[
60
]
, that a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with non negative Ricci curvature such that the sharp Euclidean isoperimetric inequality holds is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The result was strengthened in
[
82
]
, where it is proved that if an almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality holds, then
(M^{n},g)
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Both proofs are based on the observation that the isoperimetric inequality controls from below the volume of balls. In particular, a Euclidean isoperimetric inequality forces a Euclidean behaviour of the volume of balls. Therefore rigidity in the Bishop–Gromov inequality holds and the manifold is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Analogously, the almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality forces almost Euclidean lower bounds on the volume of balls, and the statement in
[
82
]
follows from Cheeger–Colding’s Reifenberg’s theorem
[
35
]
.

Conversely, it is known after
[
31
]
that almost Euclidean lower volume bounds imply almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequalities on smaller balls see also the recent
[
76
, Theorem 3.9]
.

As a consequence of our main results, an almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for a given volume is sufficiently strong to guarantee almost-regularity. Moreover, an almost Euclidean lower bound on the volume of balls forces an almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for small volumes. On the one hand this statement is stronger than the almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality inside small balls from
[
31
]
, since small diameter implies small volume by the Bishop–Gromov inequality. On the other hand, the assumptions in
[
31
]
are local and more general (no assumptions on infinitesimal Hilbertianity nor on non collapsing), while we need global conditions in our argument.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space. Then there exists a constant
C(K,N)>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\delta(\varepsilon,K,N)>0
and
v(\varepsilon,K,N)>0
for which the following holds: if there exists
0<v<v(\varepsilon,K,N)
such that

(4.18)

\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\delta\,,

then for any
0<r<C(K,N)v^{\frac{1}{N}}
and any
x\in X
it holds

(4.19)

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}\geq 1-\varepsilon\,.

Conversely, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist constants
r(\varepsilon,K,N)>0
,
v(\varepsilon,r,K,N)
and
\delta(\varepsilon,K,N)>0

for which the following holds: if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
is an
\RCD(K,N)
metric measure space and there exists
0<r<r(\varepsilon,K,N)
such that

(4.20)

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}\geq 1-\delta\,,\quad\text{for any $x\in X$}\,,

then

(4.21)

\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\varepsilon\,,\quad\text{for any $0<v<v(\varepsilon,r,K,N)$}\,.

Proof.

We prove the first part of the statement under the assumption that
K=0
, in which case it is possible to choose

(4.22)

v(\varepsilon,0,N)=+\infty\,,\quad C(0,N)=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}\,.

The case
K<0
can be handled with minor modifications with respect to the argument that we are going to present, as in the case of previous arguments in the note.

If
K=0
and (
4.18
) holds for some
v>0
, then by (
3.37
)

(4.23)

\frac{I(\bar{v})}{\bar{v}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\geq N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\delta\,,\quad\text{for any $0<\bar{v}<v$}\,.

We wish to apply the almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality (
4.23
) to balls with small radii, to estimate their volume via the coarea formula.

Let us consider any
x\in X
and any
0<s<C(0,N)v^{\frac{1}{N}}
. Notice that

(4.24)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{s}(x))\leq v\,,

by the Bishop–Gromov inequality and the very definition of
C(0,N)
. Hence,
B_{s}(x)
is a competitor for the isoperimetric inequality in the range where (
4.23
) holds. Therefore it holds

(4.25)

\Per(B_{s}(x))\geq\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\delta\right)\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{s}(x))\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,\quad\text{for any $0<s<C(0,N)v^{\frac{1}{N}}$}\,.

Let us set
f(s):=\Per(B_{s}(x))
. The coarea formula implies that

(4.26)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))=\int_{0}^{r}f(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad\text{for any $r>0$}\,.

Therefore, (
4.25
) can be turned into

(4.27)

f(r)\geq\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\delta\right)\left(\int_{0}^{r}f(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,\quad\text{for any $r>0$}\,.

By integrating the integral inequality above we easily infer that (
4.19
) holds whenever
x
is a regular point. The statement follows since the function
x\mapsto\mathscr{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))
is continuous, by Bishop–Gromov inequality, and the regular set is dense.

Let us prove the converse implication, focusing again on the case
K=0
. The general case can be handled with minor modifications.

If
K=0
then we can set
r(\varepsilon,K,N)=+\infty
. Notice that, by volume convergence, the lower volume bound in (
4.20
) holds also for any pointed limit at infinity
(Y_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{\infty})
of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
. In particular, by Bishop–Gromov volume montonicity, all the densities at any point in any pointed limit at infinity of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
are bounded from below by
1-\delta
. The almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for small volumes
0<v<v((X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N}))
follows from (
1.7
).

Let us show that actually the conclusion holds for
0<v<v(\varepsilon,r,0,N)
, with
v(\varepsilon,r,0,N)
independent of the metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{N})
verifying the assumptions of the statement.

If this is not the case, then we can find a sequence of
\RCD(0,N)
metric measure spaces
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N})
such that

(4.28)

\frac{\mathscr{H}^{N}(B^{X_{n}}_{r}(x_{n}))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{n}\,,

for any
x_{n}\in X_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and Borel sets
E_{n}\subset X_{n}
such that

(4.29)

\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{n})\downarrow 0\,,\quad\Per(E_{n})\leq\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\varepsilon\right)\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{n})\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,.

By volume convergence, any pointed limit at infinity
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{N},y)
of any of the metric measure spaces
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N})
verifies (
4.28
). Therefore, up to possibly substituting
(X_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N})
with a pointed limit at infinity, we can assume thanks to
subsubsection 2.6.3
that
E_{n}\subset X_{n}
is an isoperimetric set.

Thanks to (2) in
subsubsection 2.6.3
,
\mathrm{diam}E_{n}\leq C\mathscr{H}^{N}(E_{n})^{\frac{1}{N}}
, for a uniform constant
C(N)
, for any sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. After a point picking and scaling argument, we obtain a sequence of metric measure spaces
(Y_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathscr{H}^{N},y_{n})
converging in the pmGH topology to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with canonical metric measure structure and sets
F_{n}\subset B_{2}(y_{n})
with measures uniformly bounded and uniformly bounded away from zero such that

(4.30)

\Per(F_{n})\leq\left(N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}-\varepsilon\right)\left(\mathscr{H}^{N}(F_{n})\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,.

Up to the extraction of a subsequence, the sets
F_{n}
converge in
L^{1}
strong to
F\subset B_{2}(0^{N})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By lower semicontinuity of the perimeter under
L^{1}
strong convergence, this contradicts the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality.
∎

||g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}||_{C^{0}(B_{r}(0^{n}))}+r||\partial_{k}g_{ij}||_{C^{0}(B_{r}(0^{n}))}<10^{-3}\,.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
n\geq 2
be fixed. Then there exists
\eta=\eta(n)>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth and complete Riemannian manifold with bounded Ricci curvature. Then the harmonic radius is uniformly bounded away from zero on

if and only if

(4.38)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}-\eta\,.

Moreover, if (

(4.39)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{I(v)}{v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\,

and all the pointed limits at infinity of

are Riemannian manifolds with

Riemannian metric for any
\alpha<1
and any

.

If the manifold
(M^{n},g)
is Einstein, the same conclusion holds with the regularity scale in place of the harmonic radius and

replaced by

.

Proof.

The statement follows from
Theorem 4.8
thanks to the
\varepsilon
-regularity theorems for Einstein manifolds and manifolds with bounded Ricci curvature from
[

,
35
]
: there exists
\varepsilon(n,v)>0
such that if

satisfies

and
\mathrm{vol}(B_{1}(p))>v
and

(4.40)

\mathsf{d}_{GH}(B_{2}(p),B_{2}(0^{n}))<\varepsilon\,,

then

.

We prove the statement in the case of bounded Ricci curvature, the case of Einstein manifolds being completely analogous.

If (

On Isotropy Groups of Quantum Weyl Algebra and Jordanian Plane
ADRIANO DE SANTANA, RENE BALTAZAR, ROBSON VINCIGUERRA, WILIAN DE ARAUJO

Abstract
We study isotropy groups of σ \sigma -derivations of the quantum Weyl algebra and of ordinary derivations of the Jordanian plane. For the quantum Weyl algebra A q 1 ​ ( 𝕜 ) A_{q}^{1}(\Bbbk) , with q q not a root of unity, we use Brzeziński’s classification to decompose every σ \sigma -derivation into inner and non-inner stable components. This yields an intersection formula for the isotropy group of an arbitrary σ \sigma -derivation and leads to explicit arithmetic descriptions. For the Jordanian plane Λ 2 ​ ( 𝕜 ) \Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk) , we give a necessary and sufficient condition for an automorphism to belong to the isotropy group of an inner derivation. We compute the isotropy groups of monomial inner derivations and of locally nilpotent derivations. These examples show that isotropy groups in the Jordanian plane may contain large triangular subgroups, unlike the quantum Weyl algebra. In this way, isotropy groups provide a natural invariant that reflects the structural difference between the Jordanian plane and the quantum Weyl algebra.

-derivation of the quantum Weyl algebra. Before returning to the quantum Weyl algebra, we state the following general lemma, for an arbitrary
\Bbbk
-algebra

{\rm Aut}_{\delta}^{G}(A)=\{\rho\in G\,|\,\rho\delta\rho^{-1}=\delta\}=G\cap{\rm Aut}_{\delta}^{\sigma}(A),

the isotropy group of

Lemma 11
.

Let
G\subseteq C_{{\rm Aut}(A)}(\sigma)
be a subgroup acting on
{\rm Der}_{\sigma}(A)
by conjugation. Suppose that

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{D}_{r}
is a direct sum of
G
-stable subspaces of
{\rm Der}_{\sigma}(A)
. Let
\delta=\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r}
, with
\delta_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Then

{\rm Aut}_{\delta}^{G}(A)=\bigcap_{\delta_{i}\neq 0}{\rm Aut}_{\delta_{i}}^{G}(A).

Proof.

The inclusion

\bigcap_{\delta_{i}\neq 0}{\rm Aut}_{\delta_{i}}^{G}(A)\subseteq{\rm Aut}_{\delta}^{G}(A)

is immediate. Conversely, let
\rho\in{\rm Aut}_{\delta}^{G}(A)
. Since each
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is
G
-stable, we have
\rho\delta_{i}\rho^{-1}\in\mathcal{D}_{i}

for every
i
. Moreover,

\delta=\rho\delta\rho^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\rho\delta_{i}\rho^{-1}.

Then,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\rho\delta_{i}\rho^{-1}-\delta_{i}\right)=0
. The summand
\rho\delta_{i}\rho^{-1}-\delta_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Since the sum is direct, each summand must be zero. Therefore,
\rho\delta_{i}\rho^{-1}=\delta_{i}
for every
i
such that
\delta_{i}\neq 0
. Thus,

\rho\in\bigcap_{\delta_{i}\neq 0}{\rm Aut}_{\delta_{i}}^{G}(A).

∎

3
Jordanian Plane

The Jordanian plane is the associative
\Bbbk
-algebra

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)=\Bbbk\langle x,y:yx=xy+y^{2}\rangle.

Together with the quantum plane, it constitutes one of the fundamental classes of two-generated quadratic algebras. Although these algebras share several structural features, Shirikov proved, among other results, that they are not
isomorphic; see
[
16
, Theorem 1.4]
. The Jordanian plane has been studied from several points of view, including automorphisms, derivations, modules, prime ideals, noncommutative algebraic geometry, Hopf algebras, and
Nichols algebras; see, for instance,
[
6
]
.

In this section, we study isotropy groups of derivations of

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
. After recalling its PBW basis and the descriptions of its automorphisms and derivations, we obtain a criterion for the isotropy of an arbitrary inner derivation and apply it to monomial inner derivations. We also determine the isotropy groups of locally nilpotent derivations, which are precisely the derivations defined by
\delta_{p}(y)=0
,
\delta_{p}(x)=p(y)
, and, finally, use these computations to compare the resulting isotropy groups with those arising in the quantum Weyl algebra and in the first Weyl algebra.

Lemma 14
.

(Proposition 1.2,
[
16
]
)
The basis of
\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
is

\{x^{i}y^{j}\ |i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

In particular,

y^{m}x^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n\choose l}\frac{(m+n-l-1)!}{(m-1)!}x^{l}y^{m+n-l};\ m,n\in\mathbb{N},

and
\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
is a domain.

Lemma 15
.

(Theorem 4.6,
[
16
]
) If
char(\Bbbk)=0
, then each derivation

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
can be represented in

for some
\alpha\in\Bbbk
,
\psi\in\Bbbk[x]
,

and
\operatorname{ad}_{w}(a)=wa-aw
, for

.

Lemma 16
.

(Theorem 3.1,
[
16
]
)
If
char(\Bbbk)=0
and
\varphi

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
, then

\varphi(x)=\gamma x+g(y),\ \varphi(y)=\gamma y,

for some
\gamma\in\Bbbk^{*}
and

An immediate consequence of the previous theorem is that

{\rm Aut}(\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk))\ {\cong}\ \Bbbk^{*}\times\Bbbk[y]

with respect to the operation

(\gamma_{2},g_{2}(y))\circ(\gamma_{1},g_{1}(y))=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1}g_{2}(y)+g_{1}(\gamma_{2}y)).

Accordingly, we denote by

whenever necessary.

Lemma 17
.

Let
f\in\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
be any element. Then,
yf=fy
if and only if
f\in\Bbbk[y]\cong\Bbbk[y][1;\delta]
. Also,
xf=fx
if and only if
f\in\Bbbk[x]\cong\Bbbk[1][x;\delta]
. In particular,
yf=fy
and
xf=fx
if and only if
f\in\Bbbk
.

Proof.

Note that it is trivial that if
f\in\Bbbk[y]
, then
yf=fy
. So, let
f=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then

\displaystyle yf

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}yx^{i}y^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\sum_{l=0}^{i}c_{ij}\dfrac{i!}{l!}x^{i}y^{i+1-l+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}c_{ij}\dfrac{i!}{l!}x^{i}y^{i+1-l+j}+fy.

Let’s denote
k=j-l
. Thus
j=k+l
and
l=j-k
. As
0\leq l\leq i-1
, we obtain
j-i+1\leq k\leq j
. Thus, the previous expression becomes

yf-fy=\sum_{ij}\sum_{k=j-i+1}^{j}c_{ij}\dfrac{i!}{(j-k)!}x^{i}y^{i+k+1}.

Note that the monomial
x^{i}y^{i+k+1}
only appears once in the above expression and has coefficient
c_{ij}\dfrac{i!}{(j-k)!}
. Furthermore, note that the second summation has terms only for
i>0
. In this case, since
yf-fy=0
, then
c_{ij}=0
for all
i>0
, which implies that
f\in k[y]
.

For the second statement, note that if
f\in\Bbbk[x]
, then
xf=fx
. So again let
f=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then

\displaystyle fx

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}x^{i}(jy^{j+1}+xy^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}x^{i}jy^{j+1}+xf.

That is, if
fx=xf
, then
c_{ij}=0
for all
j>0
, that is,
f\in\Bbbk[x]
. Finally, the last statement follows from the previous two.
∎

Proposition 18
.

Let

(8)

(9)

Remark 19
.

Let
h(x)\in\Bbbk[x]\setminus\Bbbk
. The algebra

\Bbbk
with generators

is locally nilpotent if and only if there exists

such that
\delta(x)=p(y)
and
\delta(y)=0
. Notice that a derivation of this form can be described as the Lemma
15

p(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}p_{j}y^{j}
and let

\psi(x)=p_{0}
and
\alpha=p_{1}

\delta^{*}(x)=\alpha y+\psi(x)+\operatorname{ad}\ w(x),\ \delta^{*}(y)=\psi^{\prime}(x)y+\operatorname{ad}\ w(y)

Note that
\delta^{*}(y)=0
. We obtain
\delta^{*}(x)=p(y)

\operatorname{ad}_{w}(x)=\sum_{j=1}\frac{p_{j+1}}{j}(y^{j}x-xy^{j})=

=\sum_{j=1}\frac{p_{j+1}}{j}(jy^{j+1}+xy^{j}-xy^{j})=\sum_{j=1}p_{j+1}y^{j+1}.

Therefore,
\delta^{*}(x)=p_{1}y+p_{0}+\sum_{j=1}p_{j+1}y^{j+1}=p(y).

Proposition 20
.

Let
p(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
and let
\delta_{p}
be the locally nilpotent derivation of
\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
defined by
\delta_{p}(y)=0

\delta_{p}(x)=p(y)
. Then,

{\rm Aut}_{\delta_{p}}(\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk))=\{\varphi_{\gamma,g}\in{\rm Aut}(\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk))\,|\,p(\gamma y)=\gamma p(y)\}.

Let
\varphi_{\gamma,g}(x)=\gamma x+g(y)

\varphi_{\gamma,g}(y)=\gamma y
be an automorphism of
\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
. Since
\delta_{p}(y)=0

\varphi_{\gamma,g}(\delta_{p}(y))=0=\delta_{p}(\varphi_{\gamma,g}(y))
. On the other hand,

\varphi_{\gamma,g}(\delta_{p}(x))=\varphi_{\gamma,g}(p(y))=p(\gamma y),

∎

Proposition 21

Let

Suppose now that

Corollary 22
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, with
(m,n)\neq(1,0)
. Then

{\rm Aut}_{\operatorname{ad}_{x^{m}y^{n}}}(\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m+n}.

If two
\Bbbk
-algebras are isomorphic then there is a natural correspondence between their isotropy groups. More precisely, let
\rho:A\to B
be an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras and
\delta:A\to A
be a derivation then
{\rm Aut}_{\delta}\cong{\rm Aut}_{\delta^{*}}
, where
\delta^{*}=\rho\circ\delta\circ\rho^{-1}
. Consequently, the collection of isotropy groups arising from derivations is an invariant of the algebra. In particular, if one algebra admits a derivation whose isotropy group has a feature that cannot occur as the isotropy group of any derivation of another algebra, then the two algebras cannot be isomorphic. Although the non-isomorphism considered below is classical, this point of view suggests isotropy groups as a possible strategy for distinguishing algebras.

Corollary 23
.

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)\not\cong A^{1}_{q}(\Bbbk)
.

Proof.

Consider the locally nilpotent derivation

\delta_{1}(y)=0,\quad\delta_{1}(x)=1

of the Jordanian plane
\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)
. By Proposition

20
,

{\rm Aut}_{\delta_{1}}(\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk))=\{\varphi_{1,g}\,|\,g(y)\in\Bbbk[y]\}\cong(\Bbbk[y],+).

In particular, this isotropy group contains an infinite-dimensional triangular subgroup. On the other hand, the isotropy group of any ordinary derivation of
A_{q}^{1}(\Bbbk)
is contained in
{\rm Aut}(A_{q}^{1}(\Bbbk))\cong\Bbbk^{*}
. Thus, every such isotropy group is contained in a one-dimensional torus. This would identify the infinite-dimensional triangular isotropy group
\{\varphi_{1,g}\,|\,g(y)\in\Bbbk[y]\}
with an isotropy subgroup contained in the one-dimensional torus
\Bbbk^{*}
, which is impossible in the natural ind-group structure on automorphism groups. Therefore,

\Lambda_{2}(\Bbbk)\not\cong A_{q}^{1}(\Bbbk).

∎

The behavior described above should be contrasted with the classical first Weyl algebra. While the quantum Weyl algebra
A_{q}^{1}(\Bbbk)
, for
q
not a root of
unity, has only diagonal automorphisms, the first Weyl algebra
A_{1}
admits a much larger automorphism group. This additional flexibility allows isotropy
groups of derivations to contain large triangular subgroups: the next example shows this already for a very simple inner derivation.

Example 25
.

\delta^{*}(\rho(x))=\rho(\delta^{*}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad\delta^{*}(\rho(y))=\rho(\delta^{*}(y)),

ad(xy-yx)=-d

. Therefore,

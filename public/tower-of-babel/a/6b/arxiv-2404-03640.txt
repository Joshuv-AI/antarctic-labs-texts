Abstract
. We prove a version of Sylvester’s law of inertia for the Reflection Equation Algebra (=REA). We will only be concerned with the REA constructed from the R R -matrix associated to the standard q q -deformation of G ​ L ​ ( N , ℂ ) GL(N,\mathbb{C}) . For q q positive, this particular REA comes equipped with a natural ∗ * -structure, by which it can be viewed as a q q -deformation of the ∗ * -algebra of polynomial functions on the space of self-adjoint N N -by- N N -matrices. We will show that this REA satisfies a type I I -condition, so that its irreducible representations can in principle be classified. Moreover, we will show that, up to the adjoint action of quantum G ​ L ​ ( N , ℂ ) GL(N,\mathbb{C}) , any irreducible representation of the REA is determined by its extended signature , which is a classical signature vector extended by a parameter in ℝ / ℤ \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} . It is this latter result that we see as a quantized version of Sylvester’s law of inertia.

Introduction and statement of the main results

Sylvester’s
law of inertia
states that the
GL(N,\mathbb{C})
-adjoint orbit
\{x^{*}zx\mid x\in GL(N,\mathbb{C})\}
of a self-adjoint
N\times N
-matrix

only remembers its
signature

\varsigma(z)=(N_{+},N_{-},N_{0}),

with
N_{0}
the number of zero eigenvalues and
N_{\pm}
the number of positive/negative eigenvalues of
z
. With

\Sign=\{(N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{3}\mid N_{+}+N_{-}+N_{0}=N\},

we have more precisely that, with
H(N)
the space of self-adjoint
N
-by-
N
-matrices, we obtain a bijection

H(N)/GL(N,\mathbb{C})\cong\Sign,\qquad\{x^{*}zx\mid x\in GL(N,\mathbb{C})\}\mapsto\varsigma(z).

(0.1)

In this paper, we will look for a quantum analogue of (
0.1
).

Let
0<q<1
, and let
\hat{R}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be the
braid operator

\hat{R}=\sum_{ij}q^{-\delta_{ij}}e_{ji}\otimes e_{ij}+(q^{-1}-q)\sum_{i<j}e_{jj}\otimes e_{ii},

(0.2)

where
e_{ij}
is the standard matrix unit taking the basis vector
e_{j}
to
e_{i}
. It satisfies the
braid relation

\hat{R}_{12}\hat{R}_{23}\hat{R}_{12}=\hat{R}_{23}\hat{R}_{12}\hat{R}_{23},

where we use the usual leg numbering notation
\hat{R}_{23}=1\otimes\hat{R}
etc. It also satisfies the
Hecke relation

(\hat{R}-q^{-1})(\hat{R}+q)=\hat{R}^{2}+(q-q^{-1})\hat{R}-1=0

and is self-adjoint,
\hat{R}^{*}=\hat{R}
. The matrix
\hat{R}
is in particular invertible, with

\hat{R}^{-1}=\sum_{ij}q^{\delta_{ij}}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{i<j}e_{ii}\otimes e_{jj}.

Definition
.

Each
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
admits a universal C
∗
-envelope

. More generally, a
*
-representation of

Note that the canonical map

Let us return now to a general compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
with associated Hopf

-algebra

. Let

be its associated von Neumann algebra, obtained as the double commutant of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
in its GNS-representation
\lambda_{\standard}
with respect to its invariant state

.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
A
be a unital
*
-algebra with a coaction

\alpha:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}).

We say that a representation

of

is

-homomorphism

.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
A
be a unital
*
-algebra with coaction
\alpha:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
.

(1)

If
\pi
is any representation of
A
, then
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
is a normal
\alpha
-compatible representation, where
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}=(\pi\otimes\lambda_{\standard})\circ\alpha
. Conversely, any normal
\alpha
-compatible representation
\pi
is stably unitarily equivalent to
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
.

(2)

If
\pi
is a normal
\alpha
-compatible representation of
A
, and
\pi_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
defines a compact quantum subgroup, then
\alpha_{\mid\mathbb{H}}=(\id\otimes\pi_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\alpha
is a coaction by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
and
\pi
is a normal
\alpha_{\mid\mathbb{H}}
-compatible representation.

Proof.

Let
V
be the right regular unitary corepresentation

L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),\quad f\otimes g\mapsto\Delta(f)(1\otimes g),\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}).

If then
\pi
is a representation of
A
, we have that

(\pi*\lambda_{\standard}\otimes\lambda_{\standard})(\alpha(x))=V_{23}(\pi*\lambda_{\standard}(x)\otimes 1)V_{23}^{*},

proving that
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
is normal
\alpha
-compatible. This proves the first half of (
1
).

Conversely, if
\pi
is normal
\alpha
-compatible, let
N=\pi(A)^{\prime\prime}
and consider the normal coaction
\alpha_{\pi}:N\rightarrow N\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. Let
E:N\rightarrow N^{\alpha}
be the faithful normal conditional expectation

E(n)=\left(\id\otimes\int_{\mathbb{G}}\right)\alpha_{\pi}(n)\in N^{\alpha},\qquad n\in N.

Then on
L^{2}(N)
, as obtained from
L^{2}(N^{\alpha})
through induction by
E
, we obtain the unitary

V_{\pi,\alpha}:L^{2}(N)\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow L^{2}(N)\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),\qquad n\xi\otimes\eta\mapsto\alpha(n)(\xi\otimes\eta),\qquad n\in N,\xi\in L^{2}(N^{\alpha}),\eta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

Then, with
\pi_{N}
the GNS-representation of
N
,

\pi_{N}*\lambda_{\standard}(n)=V_{\pi,\alpha}(\pi_{N}(n)\otimes 1)V_{\pi,\alpha}^{*}.

As
\pi
and
\pi_{N}
are stably unitarily equivalent (being faithful normal representations of
N
), this finishes the proof of (
1
). Moreover, if
\mathbb{H}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
, consider

V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{H})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{H}),\quad f\otimes h\mapsto(\id\otimes\pi_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\Delta(f)(1\otimes h),\qquad f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),h\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

Then by
[
Vae01
, Theorem 2.7]
, we obtain a unique normal coaction
\alpha_{\pi,\mathbb{H}}
of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
on
N
such that

(V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})_{23}\alpha_{\pi}(n)_{12}(V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})_{23}^{*}=(V_{\pi,\alpha})_{12}\alpha_{\pi,\mathbb{H}}(n)_{13}(V_{\pi,\alpha})_{12}^{*}.

It is then easily verified that

\alpha_{\pi,\mathbb{H}}(\pi(a))=(\pi\otimes\pi_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\alpha(a),\qquad a\in A,

proving (
2
).
∎

The first point of the previous proposition can be strengthened under the condition that
A
has trivial coinvariants.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
A
be a unital
*
-algebra with coaction
\alpha:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
, and assume

. Then any two normal
\alpha
-compatible representations are stably unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

Reprising the notation of the proof of the previous proposition, the conditional expectation
E
now becomes a faithful normal state
\varphi
. It is then clear that
N
must be the von Neumann algebra obtained from the GNS-representation of
A
with respect to
\varphi
. But as this means
N
is independent of
\pi
, this proves the proposition.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a coamenable compact quantum group, and let
\alpha:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
be a coaction on a unital
*
-algebra
A
. Let
\pi
be a representation of
A
. Then
\pi
is weakly contained in
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}=(\pi\otimes\lambda_{\standard})\circ\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
C(\mathbb{G})
be the closure of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
in its standard representation. As
\mathbb{G}
is coamenable, the C
∗
-algebra
C(\mathbb{G})
admits the counit
*
-character
\varepsilon
. So
\pi
factors through
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
by applying
\iota\otimes\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a coamenable compact quantum group, and let
A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
be a coaction on a unital
*
-algebra
A
with trivial coinvariants. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be representations of
A
. Then
\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
.

Proof.

Let
A_{u}
be the universal C
∗
-envelope of
A
, and let
\pi_{u}
be some faithful representation of
A_{u}
. Then
\alpha
extends to a coaction
\alpha_{u}:A_{u}\rightarrow A_{u}\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
which still has trivial coinvariants. In particular, we obtain on
A_{u}
a unique state
\varphi
determined by

\varphi(x)1_{A_{u}}=\left(\id\otimes\int_{\mathbb{G}}\right)\alpha_{u}(x).

Since
\int_{\mathbb{G}}
is faithful on
C(\mathbb{G})
, and
\alpha_{u}
is faithful, it follows that
\varphi
is faithful. Hence
A_{u}\cong C^{*}(\pi*\lambda_{\standard})
, and in particular
\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
.
∎

Returning now to
\mathbb{G}=U_{q}(N)
, let us say that two factor representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
lie in the same
\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
-orbit if there exist
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
such that

\pi\preccurlyeq\pi^{\prime}*\lambda,\qquad\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\lambda.

The following theorem is a quantum analogue of the spectral theorem for finite-dimensional self-adjoint matrices.

Theorem 3.13
.

Two factor representations in
\mathbf{H}_{q}(N)
lie in the same
\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
-orbit if and only if they have the same spectral weight.

Proof.

Assume that
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
have the same spectral weight. Then they factor through a representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
for some
s
, and so
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
lie in the same
\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
-orbit by Corollary
3.12
.

Conversely, assume
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
lie in the same
\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
-orbit, and choose a representation
\rho\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(N)
such that

\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\rho.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))^{\Ad_{q}}=\mathscr{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N)))
, we find that

(\pi*\rho)(\sigma_{k})=\pi(\sigma_{k})\otimes 1,\qquad 1\leq k\leq N

so that
\pi*\rho
factors over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
. As
\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\rho
, the same must be true for
\pi^{\prime}
. This implies that the spectral weights of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
must coincide.
∎

The multiplication maps

\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(B(N))^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(B(N))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q,\epsilon}^{\mathbb{R}}(B(N)),\quad x^{*}\otimes y\mapsto x^{*}y

Let us first prove the result for

Let us show that (

4.3.
Big cell representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))

To classify the irreducible big cell representations of

\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
.

We first introduce some terminology.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
V
be an
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
-module, where
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

We call a vector
\xi\in V
a
weight vector
if it is a joint eigenvector of all
T_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq M
. We call
\xi\in V
a
highest weight vector
if
\xi
is a weight vector and
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(\mathscr{N}(N))^{*}\xi=0
. We call a weight vector
\xi\in V

admissible
if the associated eigenvalues of the
T_{i}
are strictly positive. We then say that
\xi
has
weight
r\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
if
T_{i}\xi=q^{r_{i}}\xi
for all
1\leq i\leq M
.

We call
V

admissible
if it is finitely generated, has a vector basis of admissible weight vectors, and is locally finite as an
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(\mathscr{N}(N))^{*}
-module, i.e. the
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(\mathscr{N}(N))^{*}\xi
are finite dimensional for each
\xi\in V
.

We call
V
an
admissible unitary module
if moreover
V
is a pre-Hilbert space for which the
*
-structure is compatible with the inner product,

\langle\xi,x\eta\rangle=\langle x^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle,\qquad\forall\xi,\eta\in V,x\in\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N)).

Recall the triangular decomposition (
4.15
). It then follows immediately from this decomposition that the weight spaces of admissible
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
-modules are finite dimensional. It is then also easily concluded that any admissible unitary module is a direct sum of
admissible unitary highest weight modules
, i.e. admissible unitary modules generated by a highest weight vector.

More generally, we call an
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
-module
V
a
highest weight module
if it is generated by a highest weight vector. For example, using again the triangular decomposition (
4.15
), we can define a notion of Verma module
M_{r}
at a given highest weight
r
, uniquely determined by the fact that, with
\xi_{r}
the highest weight vector, the map

\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(\mathscr{N}(N))\rightarrow M_{r},\quad x\mapsto x\xi_{r}

is a linear isomorphism. Then, up to a scalar, there exists a unique
*
-invariant Hermitian scalar product
\langle-,-\rangle
on

. Following for example the discussion in
[
JL92
, Section 5]
, we also see that if

is an arbitrary highest weight module with highest weight

, then from the fact that the kernel of the natural map

must necessarily be in the kernel of
\langle-,-\rangle
, it follows that (up to a scalar) there exists a unique
*
-invariant Hermitian scalar product on
V
. We call
V

unitarizable
if this scalar product is positive-definite. Note that if the Hermitian scalar product is only semi-positive-definite, then its kernel will be
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
-invariant, and the quotient by this kernel will be unitarizable. In any case, we always normalize the scalar product such that the highest weight vector has norm
1
. We see in particular that there is a one-to-one correspondence between unitarizable highest weight modules and admissible unitary highest weight modules (with their normalized highest weight vectors fixed).

We summarize the above results in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.11
.

For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, there exists an admissible unitary highest weight-module
V_{r}
for
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
with highest weight
r\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
if and only if the invariant form on the Verma module
M_{r}
is positive semi-definite. Moreover,
V_{r}
is then unique up to unitary equivalence.

Definition 4.12
.

We call a vector
r\in\mathbb{R}^{M}

\epsilon
-admissible
if there exists an admissible unitary highest weight module for
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\epsilon}(T(N))
with highest weight
r
.

Let us now return to
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))

\pi

By irreducibility, it is also clear that the

Proposition 4.13

\pi(Z_{11})=0
and
\Ker(\pi(Z_{11}))=\{0\}
.

In the first case, it follows by
\Ad^{U(N)}_{q}
-compatibility that in fact
\pi(U_{q}(\mathfrak{u}(N))\rhd Z_{11})=0
, hence, by (
2.30
),
\pi(Z_{ij})=0
for all
i,j
, and
\pi
is up to amplification with some multiplicity Hilbert space the unique big cell representation of rank
0
.

Consider now the second case. Denoting by
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U(N))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}(U(N-1))
the restriction map to the lower right corner, we obtain by restriction that
\pi
is an
\Ad^{U(N-1)}_{q}
-compatible normal representation, using again Proposition
3.9
. Through the map
\rho_{2,N}
defined in (
2.35
), we obtain an
\Ad^{U(N-1)}_{q}
-compatible normal representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N-1))
. By induction,
\mathscr{H}_{\pi}
is a big cell representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N-1))
. But since
\Ker(\pi(Z_{11}))=0
by assumption, this makes
\pi
into a big cell representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Any irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
is weakly contained in an irreducible big cell representation.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\pi
is weakly contained in
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
, which by Proposition
5.5
is a big cell representation. Now as
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
acts as a
*
-character on the center of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
, it follows by Lemma
5.2
and Corollary
4.18
that
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
must be an amplification of a finite number of irreducible big cell representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
. Hence
\pi
must be weakly contained in an irreducible big cell representation.
∎

5.2.
Quantized Sylvester’s law of inertia

We prove our main theorems. We start with Theorem
B
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
s
is centrally admissible if and only if all non-zero roots of the polynomial

P_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}s_{k}z^{N-k}

(5.1)

are real with multiplicity
1
, and moreover the quotients of non-zero roots of the same sign lie in
q^{2\mathbb{Z}}
.

In other words,
s
is admissible if and only if we have a factorisation

P_{s}(z)=z^{N_{0}}(z-q^{2\alpha+2m_{1}})\ldots(z-q^{2\alpha+2m_{N_{+}}})(z+q^{2\beta+2n_{1}})\ldots(z+q^{2\beta+2n_{N_{-}}})

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
m_{i},n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with the
m_{i}-m_{j}\neq 0
and
n_{i}-n_{j}\neq 0
for
i\neq j
, which is the formulation in Theorem
B
.

Proof.

Assume that
s
is centrally admissible. By Theorem
5.6
it follows that there exists an irreducible big cell representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
such that
\sigma_{k}^{\pi}=s_{k}
. The theorem now follows from Theorem
4.17
.
∎

To prove Theorem
C
, we start with the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible big cell representation of rank
M
with signature
\eta
, and assume
k<N_{+}+N_{-}
with
\eta_{k}\eta_{k+1}<0
. Then there exists an irreducible big cell representation
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k}
of rank
M
such that
\pi^{\prime}
has signature

\eta^{\prime}=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k-1},\eta_{k+1},\eta_{k},\eta_{k+2},\ldots,\eta_{M}).

Proof.

Assume that
\pi
factors over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
. Clearly we will have

(\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k})(Z_{[r]})=\pi(Z_{[r]})\otimes 1

for
r<k
or
r>k+1
. On the other hand, consider the representation
\pi\circ\rho_{k,k+1}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(2))
with
\rho_{k,k+1}
as in Proposition
2.12
. Then clearly
\pi\circ\rho_{k,k+1}
has fixed signature
(\eta_{[k]},\eta_{[k-1]}\eta_{k+1})
with
\eta_{[k]}(\eta_{[k-1]}\eta_{k+1})=\eta_{k}\eta_{k+1}=-1
by the assumption on
k
. By equivariance of
\rho_{k,k+1}
, we have
(\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k})\circ\rho_{k,k+1}=(\pi\circ\rho_{k,k+1})*\mathbf{s}
, hence by Lemma
5.4
it follows that
\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k}
contains a subrepresentation of signature
\eta^{\prime}
. As
\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k}
splits as a direct sum of irreducible big cell representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
by Lemma
5.2
, it follows that there exists an irreducible big cell representation
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi*\mathbf{s}_{k}
of signature
\eta^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be centrally admissible, and let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(O_{s})
. Then
\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\Ad_{\lambda_{\standard}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

This is a special case of Corollary
3.12
.
∎

For
\lambda\in\mathbf{T}_{q}(N)
an admissible (hence finite dimensional) irreducible representation, let us denote by
\lambda^{c}
the contragredient unitary representation, uniquely determined by the fact that the trivial representation is contained in
\lambda^{c}*\lambda
. By taking direct sums, one can define
\lambda^{c}
for any admissible representation. Then we have the following standard Frobenius reciprocity.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathbf{H}_{q}(N)
be irreducible, and let
\lambda\in\mathbf{T}_{q}(N)
be finite dimensional. If
\pi\subseteq\pi^{\prime}*\lambda
, then
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi*\lambda^{c}
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\eta
be a signature

\eta=\diag(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{N_{+}},\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{N_{-}}),

and let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be two associated irreducible big cell representations with respective weights
r
and
r^{\prime}
.

(1)

If
N_{+}N_{-}=0
, then there exists an admissible
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
-representation
\lambda
with
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi*\lambda
.

(2)

If
N_{+}N_{-}\neq 0
, then there exists an admissible
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
-representation
\lambda
with
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi*\lambda
if and only if
r_{N_{+}+N_{-}}-r_{N_{+}}\in(r_{N_{+}+N_{-}}^{\prime}-r_{N_{-}}^{\prime})+\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We know from Theorem
4.16
that

r=(\alpha+m_{1},\ldots,\alpha+m_{N_{+}},\beta+n_{1},\ldots,\beta+n_{N_{-}}),

with
m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\ldots\leq m_{N_{+}}
and
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\ldots\leq n_{N_{-}}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly

r^{\prime}=(\alpha^{\prime}+m_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha^{\prime}+m_{N_{+}}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}+n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\beta^{\prime}+n_{N_{-}}^{\prime}),

Now any weight vector for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
has weight of the form
(\gamma+k_{1},\ldots,\gamma+k_{N})
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
k_{1}\leq\ldots\leq k_{N}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. This already shows that the conditions in the lemma are necessary. To see that they are sufficient, let us first multiply both representations with a one-dimensional admissible representation
T_{ij}\mapsto c\delta_{ij}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
to ensure
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}=0
. Pick then
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
large enough so that

m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\ldots\leq m_{N_{+}}\leq n_{1}+p\leq\ldots\leq n_{N_{-}}+p,

m_{1}^{\prime}\leq m_{2}^{\prime}\leq\ldots\leq m_{N_{+}}^{\prime}\leq n_{1}^{\prime}+p\leq\ldots\leq n_{N_{-}}^{\prime}+p.

Consider now the finite dimensional admissible representation
\lambda_{1}=\pi_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{N_{+}},n_{1}+p,\ldots,n_{N_{-}}+p,K,\ldots,K)}\in\mathbf{T}_{q}(N)
, where
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n_{N-}+p}
is arbitrary. Let
\pi_{0}=\pi_{S,(0,\ldots,0,\beta-p,\ldots,\beta-p,0,\ldots 0)}
. Then by restricting to the tensor product of the highest weights, we see that

\pi_{S,r}\subseteq\pi_{0}*\lambda_{1}.

Similarly, there exists a finite dimensional admissible representation
\lambda_{2}\in\mathbf{T}_{q}(N)
with

\pi_{S,r^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi_{0}*\lambda_{2}.

Hence, by the Frobenius reciprocity of Lemma
5.10
,

\pi_{S,r^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi_{0}*\lambda_{2}\subseteq\pi_{S,r}*\lambda_{1}^{c}*\lambda_{2}.

∎

We now prove Theorem
C
.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\pi\in\mathbf{H}_{q}(N)
be a representation of extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
. Then
\Ad_{\lambda}(\pi)
has the same extended signature for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{q}(N,\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, if
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathbf{H}_{q}(N)
are irreducible representations of the same extended signature, then there exists such a
\lambda\in\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{q}(N,\mathbb{C})
with

\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\Ad_{\lambda}(\pi).

Proof.

Let us prove the first part of the theorem.

Clearly
\pi_{1}\preccurlyeq\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{2}
of extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
implies
\pi_{1}
of the same extended signature. By the remark following Theorem
1.20
, it is hence enough to show that if
\pi
is of extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
, then also
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}*\lambda^{\prime}
has extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
, for
\lambda_{\standard}
the standard representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U(N))
and
\lambda^{\prime}
an irreducible admissible representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
. Now since the center of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
is
Ad_{q}^{U}
-coinvariant, clearly
\pi*\lambda_{\standard}
has the same signature as
\pi
. By Proposition
3.9
and Proposition
5.5
we may hence assume that
\pi
is an irreducible big cell representation, and we want to show that
\pi*\lambda^{\prime}
has the same extended signature. But since
\pi
factors through
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(H(N))\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(T(N))
, and
\Ad_{q}^{T}
factors through a coaction on
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\leq M}(T(N))
, it follows that
\pi*\lambda^{\prime}
has rank
M
. Moreover, since
\Ad_{q}^{T}(Z_{[k]})=Z_{[k]}\otimes T_{1}^{2}\ldots T_{k}^{2}
, we see that
\pi*\lambda^{\prime}
is a big cell representation. As the
(\pi*\lambda^{\prime})(Z_{[k]})
are diagonalizable,
\pi*\lambda^{\prime}
must be a direct sum of irreducible big cell representations. But since the extended signature of an irreducible big cell representation can be read of from any of its weight spaces, it follows straightforwardly that
\pi*\lambda^{\prime}
has extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
. This proves the first part of the theorem.

To prove the second part of the theorem, assume that
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
are irreducible representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
of extended signature
([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})
. We may assume that
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
are irreducible. To show that there exists
\lambda\in\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{q}(N)
so that
\pi^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\pi*\lambda
, we may by Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.5
assume that
\pi
is an irreducible big cell representation. By applying Lemma
5.8
repeatedly and then applying Lemma
5.9
, we may assume that
\pi^{\prime}
and
\pi
have signature
\diag(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{N_{+}},\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{N_{-}},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{N_{0}})
. But for these the conclusion of the theorem follows immediately from Lemma
5.11
.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

The
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
is C
∗
-faithful and type
I
.

Proof.

The C
∗
-faithfulness of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
follows easily from known results, for example the injectivity of
\pi_{T}
in Proposition
2.10
and the fact that the finite-dimensional
*

\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
separate the elements of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))

Let now
\varsigma_{\ext}=([r],N_{+},N_{-},N_{0})

\chi_{r}
be a
*

\varsigma_{\ext}

N_{+}N_{-}\neq 0
and

*

\chi_{r}:\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))\mapsto\mathbb{C},\qquad Z\mapsto\pm\left(q^{-r}\sum_{i=N_{0}+L+1}^{N}e_{ii}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}(e_{N_{0}+i,N-i}+e_{N-i,N_{0}+i})-q^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{L}e_{N-i+1,N-i+1}\right)

Let
\pi

\mathcal{O}_{q}(H(N))
. Then
\pi
has a well-defined extended signature
\varsigma_{\ext}
. From Theorem

\pi
is weakly contained in
\chi_{r}*\lambda
for some representation
\lambda
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbb{R}}(GL(N,\mathbb{C}))
. However, every irreducible representation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbb{R}}(GL(N,\mathbb{C}))

\mathcal{O}_{q}(U(N))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))

\lambda^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(T(N))
such that
C^{*}(\pi)
is a quotient C
∗

∗
-subalgebra of
C(U_{q}(N))\otimes B(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}})
. As the latter C
∗
-algebra is type

, it follows that
C^{*}(\pi)
is type
I
.
∎

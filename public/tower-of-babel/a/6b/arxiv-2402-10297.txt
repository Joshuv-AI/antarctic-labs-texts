Abstract
. We consider on a closed Riemannian spin manifold ( M n , g , σ ) (M^{n},g,\sigma) the spinorial Yamabe type equation D g ​ φ = λ ​ | φ | 2 n − 1 ​ φ D_{g}\varphi=\lambda|\varphi|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\varphi , where φ \varphi is a spinor field and λ \lambda is a positive constant. For a normalized solution φ \varphi of this equation we find a positive lower bound for λ 2 \lambda^{2} . As an application we obtain an explicit lower bound of the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant for some spin manifolds with positive scalar curvature.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
be a closed Riemannian spin manifold with a spin structure
\sigma:P_{spin}(M)\rightarrow P_{SO}(M)
. We consider the representation
\rho:spin(n)\rightarrow Aut(S_{n})
of the spin group compatible with Clifford multiplication and the complex vector bundle
SM=P_{spin}(M)\times_{\rho}S_{n}
associated to the principal bundle
\sigma:P_{spin}(M)\rightarrow P_{SO}(M)
. This vector bundle is known as the spinor bundle and it is endowed with a natural spinorial Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
, a pointwise Hermitian scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and an elliptic differential operator
D_{g}
(called Dirac operator).

In this paper, we are interested in the spinorial Yamabe-type equation

(1.1)

D_{g}\varphi=\lambda|\varphi|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\varphi,

where
\varphi\in\Gamma(SM)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and the scalar curvature of the metric
g
,
sc_{g}
, is positive.

When
\lambda=1
this equation is referred as the spinorial Yamabe equation (
[
2
]
;
[
3
]
;
[
4
]
). Solutions of this equation are related to some interesting geometric problems. For instance when
n=2
solutions of the spinorial Yamabe equation produce conformally immersed constant mean curvature surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(
[
7
,
2
]
), and when
n\geq 2
it is related to conformally immersed constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Also there is an interesting relation with a conformal spinorial invariant. Indeed, in his Habilitation
[
2
]
B. Ammann introduced the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant
\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)
of the spin manifold
(M,g,\sigma)
defined by

\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma):=\inf\limits_{g^{\prime}\in[g]}\lambda_{1}(D_{g^{\prime}})vol(M,g^{\prime})^{1/n},

where
\lambda_{1}(D_{g^{\prime}})
is the first positive eigenvalue of Dirac operator
D_{g^{\prime}}
with respect to the conformal metric
g^{\prime}
and
vol(M,g^{\prime})
is the volume of
M
with respect to the metric
g^{\prime}
.

J. Lott proved in
[
11
]
that this invariant is positive when the Dirac operator
D_{g^{\prime}}
(for some conformal metric
g^{\prime}
) is invertible. Later B. Ammann in (
[
1
]
) extended the result
to any closed spin Riemannian manifold.

In order to obtain generalized metrics which realize the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant, B. Ammann proved (
[
2
]
) that this problem is equivalent to the existence of a spinor field
\varphi
minimizing the Rayleigh type quotient

(1.2)

J(\psi):=\dfrac{\left(\int_{M}|D_{g}\psi|^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}dv_{g}\right)^{(n+1)/n}}{|\int_{M}\langle D_{g}\psi,\psi\rangle dv_{g}|}

on the space of smooth spinor fields. Here
dv_{g}
is the volume element of
(M^{n},g)
.

Moreover, the infimum of
J
is
\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)
and the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation is

D_{g}\varphi=|\varphi|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\varphi\quad\text{with}\quad\int_{M}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}dv_{g}=\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)^{n}

or equivalently

(1.3)

D_{g}\varphi=\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)|\varphi|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\varphi\hskip 28.45274pt\text{with}\hskip 28.45274pt\int_{M}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}dv_{g}=1.

From this discussion, let us point out here that solutions of equation (
1.1
) normalized by

\int_{M}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}dv_{g}=1
can be related to the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant. With this in mind, we state the main result of this note as follows

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
be a closed Riemannian spin manifold of positive scalar curvature. Assume
n\geq 3
and there is an open bounded domain
\Omega\subset M
such that
(\Omega,g)
is conformally flat.
If
\varphi
is a solution of the equation (
1.1
) with
\int_{M}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}dv_{g}=1
. Then

\lambda^{2}\geq\dfrac{n^{2}}{4}\dfrac{(n-2)}{(n-1)}\omega_{n}^{2/n},

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
.

The general existence of solutions for equation (
1.1
) is a very difficult task. For instance, a standard variational approach can not produce nontrivial solutions because
2n/(n+1)
is the critical exponent in the Sobolev inequality of the spaces involved in the definition of the functional (
1.2
).

However there exists a criterion (similar to the Yamabe problem) to provide solutions to this problem. In fact if the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant satisfies

(1.4)

\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)<\frac{n}{2}\omega_{n}^{1/n}.

Then there is a nontrivial spinor solution of (
1.3
) and therefore there is a generalized metric that attain the invariant
\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)
(see
[
2
]
for more details).

It is well-known that
\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)\leq\frac{n}{2}\omega_{n}^{1/n}
for all closed spin manifolds
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
(see
[
4
]
). Furthermore the equality is attained by the round sphere of dimension
n
i.e.,
\lambda_{min}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{0}],\sigma_{0})=(n/2)\omega_{n}^{1/n}
, where
\sigma_{0}
is the unique spin structure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Nevertheless it is an open problem to determine when a closed spin manifold non conformally equivalent to the round sphere satisfies or not the strict inequality (
1.4
).

There are very few cases where (
1.4
) it is known. For example, in the locally conformally flat setting B. Ammann, E. Humbert and B. Morel give a sufficient condition to get (
1.4
). Specifically, if
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
is locally conformally flat with invertible Dirac operator
D_{g}
and the mass-endomorphism possesses a positive eigenvalue at a point, then the inequality (
1.4
) holds (see
[
3
]
for more details about this result). In a recent paper
[
9
]
T. Isobe and T. Xu constructed a family of metrics
g(\epsilon)
on sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

\lambda_{min}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g(\epsilon)],\sigma_{0})<\frac{n}{2}\omega_{n}^{1/n}

for
n\geq 4
and
\epsilon>0
small enough.
The metrics
g(\epsilon)
restricted to some open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
are equal to the round metric
g_{0}
but are not locally conformally flat. For more about the problem of strict inequality (
1.4
) see for instance
[
13
]
.

The manifolds described above not only admit a nontrivial solution of (
1.3
), also contain an open domain
\Omega
for which
(\Omega,g)
is conformally flat. Then the Theorem (
1.1
) implies that

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
be a closed spin manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with positive scalar curvature. Assume that

•

(M^{n},g)
is locally conformally flat with a positive eigenvalue for the mass endomorphism at some point or,

•

(M^{n},g,\sigma)=(\mathbb{S}^{n},g(\epsilon),\sigma_{0})
for small
\epsilon>0
and
g(\epsilon)
as above.

Then the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant satisfies that

\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)^{2}\in\left(\dfrac{(n-2)}{8(n-1)}n^{2}\omega_{n}^{2/n},\frac{1}{4}n^{2}\omega_{n}^{2/n}\right).

There is another invariant for which our estimation applies. Let
C(M)
be the set of conformal classes of metrics on closed spin manifold
M^{n}
. The
\tau-
invariant of
M^{n}
with spin structure
\sigma
is defined by

\tau(M^{n},\sigma):=\sup\limits_{[g]\in C(M)}\lambda^{+}_{min}(M^{n},[g],\sigma).

Clearly the
\tau-
invariant for manifolds in Corollary 1.2 satisfies

\left(\dfrac{\tau(M^{n},\sigma)}{n\omega_{n}^{1/n}}\right)^{2}\in\left(\dfrac{(n-2)}{8(n-1)},\frac{1}{4}\right].

In the literature there are other type of lower bounds for the Bär-Hijazi-Lott invariant. In dimension
n\geq 3
, O. Hijazi in
[
8
]
proved that

\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)^{2}\geq\frac{n}{4(n-1)}Y(M^{n},[g]),

where
Y(M^{n},[g])
is the Yamabe constant of the conformal class
[g]
on
M
.

This inequality exhibits a strong connection with the Yamabe problem. For instance the Hijazi’s inequality provides a spinorial proof of the Yamabe problem when the inequality (
1.4
) holds.

Therefore, estimations of the invariant
\lambda_{min}^{+}(M^{n},[g],\sigma)
can help to estimate the Yamabe constant from a spinorial point of view.

The plan of this note is the following. In section 2 we will recall some background material on spin manifolds and the equation (
1.1
). In section 3 we shall show all ingredients needed later, and in section 4 we deal with the proof of the main theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic facts about spin manifolds and Dirac operator. Also some results for solutions to Dirac-type equations are recalled. For more details see (
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
).

On a closed oriented Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
we can define a
SO(n)
-principal bundle
P_{SO}M
over
M
of oriented
g-
orthonormal bases at
x\in M
. Let
f_{\alpha,\beta}
be the transition functions. For
n\geq 3
there exists the universal covering
\sigma:spin(n)\rightarrow SO(n)
where
spin(n)
is the group generated by even unit-length vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the real Clifford algebra
Cl_{n}
(the associative
\mathbb{R}-
algebra generated by relation
VW+WV=-2(V,W)
for Euclidean metric
(,)
). The manifold
M
is called spin if there is a
spin(n)-
principal bundle
P_{spin}M
over
M
such that it is a double covering of
P_{SO}M
whose restriction to each fiber is
\sigma:spin(n)\rightarrow SO(n)
. This double covering from
P_{spin}M
to
P_{SO}M
,
\sigma
, it is known as spin structure.

There are four special structures associated to a spin manifold
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
:

(1)

A complex vector bundle
SM:=P_{spin}(M)\times_{\rho}S_{n}
where
\rho:spin(n)\rightarrow Aut(S_{n})
is the restriction to
spin(n)
of an irreducible representation
\rho:\mathbb{C}l_{n}\rightarrow End(S_{n})
of the complex Clifford algebra
\mathbb{C}l_{n}\simeq Cl_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
,
S_{n}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
N=2^{[n/2]}
.

(2)

The Clifford multiplication
m
on
SM
defined by

\displaystyle m:TM\times SM

\displaystyle\rightarrow SM

\displaystyle X\otimes\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto X\cdot_{g}\varphi:=\rho(X)\varphi.

(3)

A Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on sections of
SM
.

(4)

A Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
SM
.

All these structures are compatible in the following sense:

\displaystyle\langle X\cdot\varphi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\varphi,X\cdot\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle X(\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}\varphi,\psi\rangle+\langle\varphi,\nabla_{X}\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(Y\cdot\varphi)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}Y\varphi+Y\cdot\nabla_{X}\varphi,

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\varphi,\psi\in\Gamma(SM)
.

On the other hand, on
\Gamma(SM)
we have a connection Laplacian
\nabla^{*}\nabla
where

\nabla^{*}:\Gamma(Hom(TM,SM))\rightarrow\Gamma(SM)
is the adjoint of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla:\Gamma(SM)\rightarrow\Gamma(Hom(TM,SM))
defined implicitly by

\int_{M}\langle\nabla^{*}F,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}=\int_{M}\langle F,\nabla\varphi\rangle dv_{g}.

This Laplacian satisfies a very useful Bochner-type formula. For spinor field
\varphi
compactly supported it is known that

(2.1)

\Delta\left(\frac{|\varphi|^{2}}{2}\right)=\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi\rangle-\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla\varphi,\varphi\rangle,

where
\Delta
is the non-positive Laplacian-Beltrami on
(M^{n},g)
.

Now identifying
\Gamma(Hom(TM,SM))
as
\Gamma(TM\otimes SM)
we can define the Dirac operator
D_{g}
as the composition of
\nabla
with the Clifford multiplication
m
i.e.
D_{g}:=m\circ\nabla
. For a local orthonormal frame
\{E_{i}\}
we have

D_{g}\varphi=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\cdot_{g}\nabla_{E_{i}}\varphi.

For our purpose we just recall the following properties of Dirac operator

(1)

(Green’s formula) For an open domain with smooth boundary
\Omega\subset M
,

\int_{\Omega}\langle D_{g}\varphi,\psi\rangle dv_{g}=\int_{\Omega}\langle\varphi,D_{g}\psi\rangle dv_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\langle\varphi,Z\cdot\psi\rangle dv_{g}^{n-1},

where
Z
is a unit normal vector field on
\partial\Omega
and
dv_{g}^{n-1}
is the volume element of
\partial\Omega
.

(2)

(Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz formula)

D_{g}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{1}{4}sc_{g}Id_{\Gamma(SM)}.

(3)

(Penrose operator) The composition of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
with the orthogonal projection on the kernel of the Clifford multiplication
m
defines an operator
P
known as Penrose operator. This operator satisfies that

(2.2)

|\nabla\varphi|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}|D_{g}\varphi|^{2}+|P\varphi|^{2}.

We finish this section with a couple of facts about the solutions of equation
1.1
. These can be consult in Ammann’s Habilitation
[
2
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

•

Due to the possible existence of nodal sets for spinor fields
\varphi
solutions of
1.1
by regularity theory we have that
\varphi\in C^{1,\alpha}(SM)\cap C^{\infty}(S(M-\{\varphi^{-1}(0)\})).

•

The equation
1.1
satisfies a weak unique continuation property. This means that the nodal set of a
C^{1}
solution does not contain any nonempty open set
[
2
, Corollary 4.5.2]
.

3.
Estimation of Parameters

In this section we will prove some key results.

We consider the Dirac-type equation

(3.1)

D_{g}\varphi=\lambda|\varphi|^{q}\varphi

where
\lambda,q\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a solution of (
3.1
) without zeros in an open domain
\Omega\subset M
. Assume
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(S\Omega)
. For any
u\in C_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
it satisfies the following integral identity

(3.2)

0=\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}\left\{\frac{-\Delta u}{2}+\frac{u}{4}sc_{g}+\left(\frac{u}{n}-u\right)\lambda^{2}|\varphi|^{2q}\right\}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}u|P\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}.

Proof.

We assume a nontrivial function
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Applying the Bochner-type formula (
2.1
) to
\varphi
, multiplying by
u
and integrating we get

0=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{2}\Delta(|\varphi|^{2})dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}-\int_{\Omega}u\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla\varphi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}.

By the Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz formula it follows that

(3.3)

0=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{2}\Delta(|\varphi|^{2})dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}-\int_{\Omega}u\langle D_{g}^{2}\varphi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}u\;sc_{g}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}.

Using Green’s formula for Dirac operator we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\langle D_{g}^{2}\varphi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle D_{g}\varphi,D_{g}(u\varphi)\rangle dv_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}u\langle D_{g}\varphi,N\cdot\varphi\rangle dv^{n-1}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\langle D_{g}\varphi,uD_{g}\varphi\rangle+\langle D_{g}\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle\right)dv_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}u\langle D_{g}\varphi,N\cdot\varphi\rangle dv^{n-1}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u\lambda^{2}|\varphi|^{2q}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}\lambda|\varphi|^{q}\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle dv_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}u\langle D_{g}\varphi,N\cdot\varphi\rangle dv^{n-1}_{g}.

In last equation we used that
\varphi
is a solution of equation (
3.1
).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\frac{u}{2}\Delta(|\varphi|^{2})dv_{g}=\int_{\Omega}-\frac{\Delta u}{2}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{|\varphi|^{2}}{2}\langle\nabla u,N\rangle-\frac{u}{2}\langle N,\nabla|\varphi|^{2}\rangle\right)dv_{g}.

Since that
supp\;(u)\subset\Omega
,
u=0
and
\nabla u=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Therefore all previous boundary terms vanish i.e.

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\langle D_{g}^{2}\varphi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u\lambda^{2}|\varphi|^{2q}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}\lambda|\varphi|^{q}\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle dv_{g}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\frac{u}{2}\Delta(|\varphi|^{2})dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\frac{\Delta u}{2}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}

Using the spliting (
2.2
) and combining the relations (
3.4
);(
3.5
) into the equation (
3.3
) we obtain,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{2}\Delta(|\varphi|^{2})dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}-\int_{\Omega}u\langle D_{g}^{2}\varphi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}u\;sc_{g}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Delta u}{2}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}u|P\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{n}|D_{g}\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}u\;sc_{g}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}u\lambda^{2}|\varphi|^{2q}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}-\int_{\Omega}\lambda|\varphi|^{q}\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}\left\{\frac{-\Delta u}{2}+\frac{u}{4}sc_{g}+\left(\frac{u}{n}-u\right)\lambda^{2}|\varphi|^{2q}\right\}dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}u|P\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}-\int_{\Omega}\lambda|\varphi|^{q}\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle dv_{g}.

Now note that
\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle
is a pure imaginary:

\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle=-\langle\nabla u\cdot\varphi,\varphi\rangle=-\overline{\langle\varphi,\nabla u\cdot\varphi\rangle}.

Hence the identity (
3.2

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}\{L(u_{\lambda})-\lambda^{2}fu_{\lambda}\}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega}4u_{\lambda}|P\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{2}\{L(u_{\lambda})-\lambda^{2}fu_{\lambda}\}dv_{g}+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}4u_{\lambda}|P\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}.

Hence
\varphi=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
and this is a contradiction.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Similarly to the proof of the previous Theorem, the case
\lambda^{2}=\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)|\varphi|^{2q}}{n},\Omega^{\prime}\right)
implies that
P\varphi=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

We highlight that the equation
P\varphi=0
is completely understood when
\Omega^{\prime}=M
. More precisely

•

(Lichnerowicz,
[
10
]
)(
n\geq 2
) If the zero set of
\varphi
is non-empty, then
(M^{n},g,\sigma)
is conformally equivalent to
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0},\sigma_{0})
.

Moreover, by classification of manifolds with Killing spinors and (
[
5
]
)

•

(

) If the zero set of

4.
Explicit lower bound

In this section we will prove the Theorem (
1.1
). To give so we shall see explicit lower bounds for
\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega^{\prime}\right)
in a particular family of manifolds.

Proof of Theorem (
1.1
).

First, we recall a well-known result that will be useful in the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

[Proposition 2.2,
[
12
]
]
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
n\geq 3
. Suppose there is a domain
\Omega\subset M
such that there exists a conformal immersion from
(\Omega,g)
into
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
. For all
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
supp(u)\subset\Omega
,

\left(\int_{M}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}\leq\dfrac{4}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}^{2/n}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\dfrac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\int_{M}sc_{g}u^{2}dv_{g}\right),

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be an open bounded domain such that
(\Omega,g)
is conformally flat.
We consider a solution
\varphi
of the equation
1.1
with
\int_{M}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}dv_{g}=1
.

From the variational characterization of eigenvalues

\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega\right)}=\frac{4(n-1)}{n}\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)}u^{2}dv_{g}/\int_{\Omega}uLudv_{g}=1\right\}.

Using Hölder inequality we get that,

\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)}u^{2}dv_{g}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2n/(n-1)}dv_{g}\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}.

The normalization of
\varphi
implies that

\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega\right)}\leq\frac{4(n-1)}{n}\sup\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}/\int_{\Omega}uLudv_{g}=1\right\}.

Hence

\frac{n}{4(n-1)}\inf\left\{\dfrac{1}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}}\quad/\int_{\Omega}uLudv_{g}=1\right\}\leq\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\dfrac{1}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}}\;/\int_{\Omega}uLudv_{g}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{supp(u)\subset\Omega}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}uLudv_{g}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}}

\displaystyle=2\inf\limits_{supp(u)\subset\Omega}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}sc_{g}u^{2}dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}}.

Since that
\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}<1/2
we get that

\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega\right)\geq\frac{2n}{4(n-1)}\inf\limits_{supp(u)\subset\Omega}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\int_{\Omega}sc_{g}u^{2}dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2n/(n-2)}dv_{g}\right)^{(n-2)/n}}.

Without loss of generality we can suppose that
\Omega
is simply connected. Since
(\Omega,g)
is conformally flat there exists a conformal immersion from
(\Omega,g)
into
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
. Then by Lemma 4.1

\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{4(n-1)}{n}|\varphi|^{4/(n-1)},\Omega\right)\geq\dfrac{n}{4(n-1)}\dfrac{n(n-2)\omega_{n}^{2/n}}{2}=\dfrac{n^{2}}{8}\dfrac{(n-2)}{(n-1)}\omega_{n}^{2/n}.

Moreover by weak unique continuation property
[
2
, Corollary 4.5.2]
we can assume that the nontrivial solution
\varphi
does not have zero in
\Omega
. Therefore Theorem (
3.3
) implies

\lambda^{2}\geq\dfrac{n^{2}}{8}\dfrac{(n-2)}{(n-1)}\omega_{n}^{2/n}.

∎

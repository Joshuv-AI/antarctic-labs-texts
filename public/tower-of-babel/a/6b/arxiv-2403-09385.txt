PeV-Scale SUSY and Cosmic Strings from F-term Hybrid Inflation
C. Pallis School of Civil Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, GR-541 24 Thessaloniki, GREECE

Abstract
We consider F-term hybrid inflation (FHI) and SUSY breaking in the context of a B − L B-L extension of MSSM which largely respects a global U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) R R symmetry. The hidden sector Kaehler manifold enjoys an enhanced S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 ) / U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(1,1)/U(1) symmetry with the scalar curvature determined by the achievement of a SUSY-breaking de Sitter vacuum without ugly tuning. FHI turns out to be consistent with data, provided that the magnitude of the emergent soft tadpole term is confined in the range ( 1.2 − 100 ) (1.2-100) TeV, and it is accompanied with the production of B − L B-L cosmic strings. If these are metastable they interpret the present observations from PTA experiments on the stochastic background of gravitational waves with dimensionless tension G ​ μ cs ≃ ( 1 − 9.2 ) ⋅ 10 − 8 {G\mu_{\rm cs}}\simeq(1-9.2)\cdot 10^{-8} . The μ \mu parameter of MSSM arises by appropriately adapting the Giudice-Masiero mechanism and facilitates the out-of-equilibrium decay of the R R saxion at a reheat temperature lower than about 71 71 GeV. Due to the prolonged matter dominated era the gravitational wave signal is suppressed at high frequencies. The SUSY mass scale turns out to lie in the PeV region. In memory of Prof. G. 2.1
Superpotential

The superpotential of our model respects totally the symmetries in
Table
1
. Most notably, it carries
R
charge 2 and is linear

with respect to
(
w.r.t.
)
S
and
Z^{\nu}
. It
naturally splits into five parts:

W=W_{\rm I}+W_{\rm H}+W_{\rm GH}+W_{\rm MSSM}+W_{\rm MD},

(1)

where the subscripts “I” and “H” stand for the IS and HS
respectively and the content of each term is specified as follows:

(a)

W_{\rm I}
is the IS part of
W
which
reads
[
1
]

W_{\rm I}=\kappa S\left(\bar{\Phi}\Phi-M^{2}\right),

(2a)

where
\kappa
and
M
are free parameters which may be made positive
by field redefinitions.

(b)

W_{\rm H}
is the HS part of
W

written as
[
29
]

W_{\rm H}=mm_{\rm P}^{2}(Z/m_{\rm P})^{\nu}.

(2b)

Here
m_{\rm P}=2.4~{\mbox{\rm ReV}}
is the reduced Planck mass – with

{\mbox{\rm ReV}}=10^{18}~{\mbox{\rm GeV}}
–,
m
is a positive free parameter with mass
dimensions, and
\nu
is an exponent which may, in principle,
acquire any real value if
W_{\rm H}
is considered as an
effective superpotential valid close to the non-zero
vacuum
expectation value
(
v.e.v
) of
Z
,
\langle{Z}\rangle
. We assume
though that the effective superpotential is such that only
positive powers of
Z
appear. If we also assume that
W
is
holomorphic in
S
then mixed terms of the form

S^{\nu_{s}}Z^{\nu_{z}}
can be forbidden in
W
since the exponent of
a such term has to obey the relation

\displaystyle\nu_{s}+\nu_{z}/\nu=1~~\Rightarrow~~\nu_{z}=(1-\nu_{s})\nu,

leading to negative values of
\nu_{z}
. This conclusion contradicts
with our assumptions above.

(c)

W_{\rm GH}
is a term which mixes the
HS and the
B-L
gauge fields of the IS. It has the form

W_{\rm GH}=-\lambda m_{\rm P}(Z/m_{\rm P})^{\nu}\bar{\Phi}\Phi

(2c)

with
\lambda
a real coupling constant. The magnitude of
\lambda
can be
restricted by the DE requirement as we see below.

(d)

W_{\rm MSSM}
is the part of
W
which
contains the usual trilinear terms of MSSM, i.e.,

W_{\rm MSSM}=h_{ijD}{d}^{c}_{i}{Q}_{j}{H_{d}}+h_{ijU}{u}^{c}_{i}{Q}_{j}{H_{u}}+h_{ijE}{e}^{c}_{i}{L}_{j}{H_{d}}.

(2d)

The selected
R
assignments in Table
1
prohibit the presence
in
W_{\rm MSSM}
of the bilinear
\mu{H_{u}}{H_{d}}
term of MSSM and
other mixing terms – e.g.
\lambda_{\mu}S{H_{u}}{H_{d}}

[
39
]
which is
frequently employed to generate this
\mu
term. This term is
generated here via
K_{\mu}
– see Sec.
2.2
below.

(e)

W_{\rm MD}
is the part of
W
which
is provides masses to neutrinos

W_{\rm MD}=h_{ijN}N^{c}_{i}L_{j}{H_{u}}+\lambda_{[N^{c}}i]\left(S+(Z/m_{\rm P})^{\nu}\right)\bar{\Phi}N^{c2}_{i}.

(2e)

The first term in the right-hand side of Eq. (
2e
) is responsible
for Dirac neutrino masses whereas for the Majorana masses – cf.
Refs.
[
10
,
15
]
. The scale of the latter masses is intermediate
since
\langle{\bar{\Phi}}\rangle\sim 1~{\mbox{\rm YeV}}
and
\langle{Z}\rangle\sim m_{\rm P}
. The
cooperation of both terms lead to the light neutrino masses via
the well-known (type I) seesaw mechanism – see also
Appendix
8
.

2.2
Kähler Potential

The Kähler potential respects the
\mathbb{G}_{B-L}
,
B
and
L
symmetries in
Table
1
. It has the following contributions

K=K_{\rm I}+K_{\rm H}+K_{\mu}+K_{\rm D}+|Y_{\alpha}|^{2},

(3)

which left-handed chiral superfields of MSSM denoted by
Y_{\alpha}

with
{\alpha}=1,...,7
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha}={Q},{L},{d}^{c},{u}^{c},{e}^{c},N^{c},{H_{d}}~\mbox{and}~{H_{u}},

where the generation indices are suppressed.

(a)

K_{\rm I}
is the part of
K
which
depends on the fields involved in FHI – cf. Eq. (
2a
). We adopt
the simplest possible choice – cf. Ref.
[
10
,
2
]
– which
has the form

K_{\rm I}=|S|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2}+|\bar{\Phi}|^{2}.\\

(4a)

Higher order terms of the form
|S|^{2\nu_{S}}/m_{\rm P}^{2\nu_{S}-2}
with

\nu_{S}>1
can not be excluded by the imposed symmetries but may
become harmless if
S\ll m_{\rm P}
and assume low enough coefficients.

(b)

K_{\rm H}
is the part of
K
devoted
to the HS. We adopt the form introduced in Ref.
[
29
]
where

K_{\rm H}=Nm_{\rm P}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{|Z|^{2}-k^{2}Z_{-}^{4}/m_{\rm P}^{2}}{Nm_{\rm P}^{2}}\right)~~\mbox{with}~~Z_{\pm}=Z\pm Z^{*}.

(4b)

Here,
k>0
mildly violates
R
symmetry endowing
R
axion with
phenomenologically acceptable mass. The selected
K_{\rm H}
is
not motivated by the string theory but it can be considered as an
interesting phenomenological option for two reasons: It largely
respects the
R
symmetry, which is a crucial ingredient for FHI,
and it ensures – as we see in Sec.
3
– a dS vacuum of the
whole field system with tunable cosmological constant for

N=\frac{4\nu^{2}}{3-4\nu}~~\mbox{with}~~\frac{3}{4}<\nu<\frac{3}{2}~~\mbox{for}~~N<0.

(5)

Our favored
\nu
range finally is
3/4<\nu<1
. Since
N<0
,

K_{\rm H}
parameterizes the
SU(1,1)/U(1)
hyperbolic Kähler manifold for

k\sim 0
.

(c)

K_{\mu}
includes higher order terms which
generate the needed mixing term between
{H_{u}}
and
{H_{d}}
in the
lagrangian of MSSM – cf. Ref.
[
36
,
29
]
– and has the
form

K_{\mu}=\lambda_{\mu}\left({{Z^{*}}^{2\nu}}/{m_{\rm P}^{2\nu}}\right){H_{u}}{H_{d}}\ +\ {\rm h.c.},

(6a)

where the dimensionless constant
\lambda_{\mu}
is taken real for
simplicity.

(d)

K_{\rm D}
is an unavoidable term
which mixes the observable sector with the HS as follows

K_{\rm D}=\lambda_{ij\rm D}\left(Z^{*\nu}/m_{\rm P}^{\nu+1}\right)N^{c}_{i}L_{j}{H_{u}}+{\rm h.c}.

(6b)

It provides (subdominant) Dirac masses for
\nu_{i}

[
52
]
as
shown in Appendix
8
.

The total
K
in Eq. (
3
) enjoys an enhanced
symmetry for the
Y^{\alpha},S
and
Z
fields, namely

\prod_{\alpha}U(1)_{Y^{\alpha}}\times U(1)_{S}\times\left(SU(1,1)/U(1)\right)_{Z},

(7)

where the indices indicate the moduli which parameterize the

can be ignored
although they may be allowed by the

\tilde{\nu}_{z}=\nu\tilde{\nu}_{s}
. Most notably,

3
SUSY and
\mathbb{G}_{B-L}
Breaking – Dark Energy

The vacuum of our model is determined by minimizing the F–term
(tree level) SUGRA scalar potential
V_{\rm F}
derived

[
28
]
from
W
in Eq. (
1
) and
K
in Eq. (
3
).
Note that D–term contributions to the total SUGRA scalar
potential vanish if we confine ourselves during FHI and at the
vacuum along the D-flat direction

|\bar{\Phi}|=|\Phi|~~\mbox{which assures}~~V_{\rm D}=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\left(|\Phi|^{2}-|\bar{\Phi}|^{2}\right)^{2}=0.

(8)

Here

g
is the unique (considered unified) gauge coupling constant of

\mathbb{G}_{B-L}
and we employ the same symbol for the various superfields

X^{\alpha}=S,Z,\Phi,\bar{\Phi}
and their complex scalar components.

As we can verify numerically,
V_{\rm F}
is minimized at the

\mathbb{G}_{B-L}
-breaking vacuum

\left|\langle{\Phi}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle{\bar{\Phi}}\rangle\right|=M.

(9)

It has also a stable valley along
\langle{\theta}\rangle=0
and

\langle{{\theta_{S}}/m_{\rm P}}\rangle=\pi
, where these fields are defined by

Z=(z+i\theta)/\sqrt{2}~~\mbox{and}~~S=\sigma\ e^{i{\theta_{S}}/m_{\rm P}}/\sqrt{2}.

(10)

Substituting Eq. (
10
) in
V_{\rm F}
and minimizing it w.r.t the
various directions we arrive at the results

\sigma=-2^{(1-\nu)/2}\left(\lambda(M^{2}+m_{\rm P}^{2})-mm_{\rm P}\right)\ z^{\nu}/m_{\rm P}^{(\nu+1)}~~\mbox{and}~~\langle{z}\rangle=2\sqrt{2/3}|\nu|m_{\rm P},

(11)

which yield the constant potential energy density

\langle{V_{\rm F}}\rangle=\left(\frac{16\nu^{4}}{9}\right)^{\nu}\left(\frac{\lambda M^{2}-mm_{\rm P}}{\kappa m_{\rm P}^{2}}\right)^{2}\omega^{N}\left(\lambda(M^{2}+m_{\rm P}^{2})-mm_{\rm P}\right)^{2}~~\mbox{with}~~\omega=e^{\frac{\langle{K_{\rm H}}\rangle}{Nm_{\rm P}^{2}}}\simeq\frac{2(3-2\nu)}{3}.

(12)

Tuning
\lambda
to a value
\lambda\sim m/m_{\rm P}\simeq 10^{-12}
we may wish
identify
\langle{V_{\rm F}}\rangle
with the DE energy density, i.e.,

\langle{V_{\rm F}}\rangle=\Omega_{\Lambda}\rho_{\rm c}=7.3\cdot 10^{-121}m_{\rm P}^{4},

(13)

where the density parameter of DE and the current critical energy
density of the universe are respectively given by
[
58
]

\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.6889~~\mbox{and}~~\rho_{\rm c}=2.31\cdot 10^{-120}h^{2}m_{\rm P}^{4}~~\mbox{with}~~h=0.6766.

(14)

Therefore, we obtain a post-inflationary dS vacuum which explains
the notorious DE problem. Moreover, Eq. (
11
) yields

\langle{\sigma}\rangle\simeq 0
.

The particle spectrum of the theory at the vacuum in
Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
) includes the gravitino (
\widetilde{G}
) which acquires
mass
[
29
]

m_{3/2}=\langle{e^{{K_{\rm H}}/{2m_{\rm P}^{2}}}W_{\rm H}/m_{\rm P}^{2}}\rangle\simeq 2^{\nu}3^{-\nu/2}|\nu|^{\nu}m\omega^{N/2}.

(15a)

Diagonalizing the the mass-squared matrix of the field system

S-\Phi-\bar{\Phi}-Z
we also find out that the IS acquire a common
mass

m_{\rm I}=e^{{K_{\rm H}}/{2m_{\rm P}^{2}}}\sqrt{2}\left(\kappa^{2}M^{2}+(4\nu^{2}/3)^{\nu}(1+4M^{2}/m_{\rm P}^{2})m^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(15b)

where the second term arises due to the coexistence of the IS with
the HS – cf. Ref.
[
10
]
. Similar mixing does not appear in the
mass spectrum of HS which contains the (canonically normalized)
sgoldstino (or
R
saxion) and the pseudo-sgoldstino (or
R

axion) with respective masses

m_{z}\simeq\frac{3\omega}{2\nu}m_{3/2}~~\mbox{and}~~m_{\theta}\simeq 12k\omega^{3/2}m_{3/2}.

(15c)

Applying finally the relevant formulas of Refs.
[
37
,
29
]
, we
find that
K_{\mu}
induces a non-vanishing
\mu
term in the
superpotential of MSSM whereas
W_{\rm MSSM}
and
K_{\mu}+|Y^{\alpha}|^{2}

in Eqs. (
2d
) and (
3
) lead to a common soft SUSY-breaking mass
parameter
\widetilde{m}
, at the vacuum of Eq. (
11
), which
indicatively represents the mass level of the SUSY partners.
Namely, we obtain

W\ni\mu H_{u}H_{d}~~\mbox{with}~~|\mu|=\lambda_{\mu}\left(\frac{4\nu^{2}}{3}\right)^{\nu}(5-4\nu)m_{3/2}~~\mbox{and}~~\widetilde{m}=m_{3/2}.

(16)

Variant form of the terms
|Y^{\alpha}|
in

4
Inflation Analysis

It is well known
[
1
,
3
]
that in global SUSY
FHI takes place for sufficiently large
|S|
values along a F- and
D- flat direction of the SUSY potential

\bar{\Phi}={\Phi}=0,~~\mbox{where}~~V_{\rm SUSY}\left({\Phi}=0\right)\equiv V_{\rm I0}=\kappa^{2}M^{4}~~\mbox{are}~~H_{\rm I}=\sqrt{V_{\rm I0}/3m_{\rm P}^{2}}

(17)

are the constant potential energy density and correspoding Hubble
parameter which drive FHI – the subscript
0
means that this is
the tree level value. In a SUGRA context, though, we first check
– in Sec.
4.1
– the conditions under which such a scheme
can be achieved and then in Sec.
4.2
we give the final form of
the inflationary potential. Lastly, we present our results in
Sec.
4.4
imposing a number of contraints listed in Sec.
4.3
.

4.1
Hidden Sector’s Stabilization

The attainment of FHI is possible if
Z
is well stabilized during
it. The relative mechanism is pretty well known
[
34
]
.
Z

is transported due to
V_{\rm I0}
form its value in Eq. (
9
)
to a value well below
m_{\rm P}
. To determine this, we construct the
complete expression for
V_{\rm F}
along the inflationary trajectory in
Eq. (
17
) and then expand the resulting expression for low
S/m_{\rm P}

values, assuming that the
\theta=0
direction is stable as in
Eq. (
9
). Under these conditions
V_{\rm F}
is minimized for the
value

\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}\simeq\left(\sqrt{3}\cdot 2^{\nu/2-1}H_{\rm I}/m\nu\sqrt{1-\nu}\right)^{1/(\nu-2)}m_{\rm P}.

(18)

This result is in good agreement with its precise value derived
numerically. Note that
\nu<1
assures a real value of
\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}

with
\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}\ll m_{\rm P}
since
H_{\rm I}/m\ll 1
.

The (canonically normalized) components of sgoldstino, acquire
masses squared, respectively,

m_{{\rm I}z}^{2}\simeq 6(2-\nu)H_{\rm I}^{2}~~\mbox{and}~~m_{\rm I\theta}^{2}\simeq 3H_{\rm I}^{2}-m^{2}\left(8\nu^{2}m_{\rm P}^{2}-3\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}\right)\frac{4\nu(1-\nu)m_{\rm P}^{2}+(1-96k^{2}\nu)\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}}{2^{3+\nu}\nu m_{\rm P}^{2\nu}\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2(2-\nu)}},

(19a)

whereas the mass of
\widetilde{G}
turns out to be

m_{\rm I3/2}\simeq\left(\nu(1-\nu)^{1/2}m^{2/\nu}/\sqrt{3}H_{\rm I}\right)^{\nu/(2-\nu)}.

(19b)

It is clear from the results above that
m_{{\rm I}z}\gg H_{\rm I}
and
therefore it is well stabilized during FHI whereas
m_{\rm I\theta}\simeq H_{\rm I}
and gets slightly increased as
k
increases.
However, the isocurvature perturbation is expected to be quite
suppressed since it becomes observationally dangerous only for

m_{\rm I\theta}\ll H_{\rm I}
.

4.2
Inflationary Potential

Expanding
V_{\rm F}
for low
S
values, introducing the canonically
normalized inflaton
\sigma=\sqrt{2}|S|
and taking into account the
RCs
[
1
,
3
]
we derive
[
28
]
the
inflationary potential
V_{\rm I}
which can be cast in the form

V_{\rm I}\simeq V_{\rm I0}\left(1+C_{\rm RC}+C_{\rm SSB}+C_{\rm SUGRA}\right).

(20)

The individual contributions are specified as follows:

(a)

C_{\rm RC}
represents the RCs to

V_{\rm I}/V_{\rm I0}
which may be written as
[
1
,
3
,
2
]

C_{\rm RC}={\kappa^{2}\over 128\pi^{2}}\left(8\ln{\kappa^{2}M^{2}\over Q^{2}}+8x^{2}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}}\right)-4(\ln 4-x^{4}\ln x)+(4+x^{4})\ln(x^{4}-4)\right),

(21a)

with

x=(\sigma-\sqrt{2}\lambda\langle{Z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{\nu}m_{\rm P}^{1-\nu}/\kappa)/M>\sqrt{2}
,
beyond which the expression above ceases to be valid. Here we take
into account that the multiplicity of the waterfall fields is

{\sf N}_{\rm G}=1
since these are
U(1)_{B-L}
non-singlets.

(b)

C_{\rm SSB}
is the contribution to

V_{\rm I}/V_{\rm I0}
from the soft SUSY-breaking effects

[
9
]
parameterized as follows:

C_{\rm SSB}=m_{\rm I3/2}^{2}\sigma^{2}/2V_{\rm I0}-{\rm a}_{S}\,\sigma/\sqrt{2V_{\rm I0}},

(21b)

where the tadpole parameter reads

{\rm a}_{S}=2^{1-\nu/2}m\frac{\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{\nu}}{m_{\rm P}^{\nu}}\left(1+\frac{\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}}{2Nm_{\rm P}^{2}}\right)\left(2-\nu-\frac{3\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}}{8\nu m_{\rm P}^{2}}\right).

(21c)

The minus sign results from the stabilization of
\theta
– see
Eq. (
10
) – at zero and the minimization of the factor

(S+S^{*})=\sqrt{2}\sigma\cos(\theta_{S}/m_{\rm P})
which occurs for

\theta_{S}/m_{\rm P}=\pi~({\sf mod}~2\pi)
– the decomposition of
S
is
shown in Eq. (
10
). We further assume that
\theta_{S}
remains
constant during FHI so that the simple one-field slow-roll
approximation is valid. Possible variation of
\theta_{S}
is
investigated in Ref.
[
11
]
where they found that acceptable
solutions with
\theta_{S}/m_{\rm P}\neq\pi
require a significant amount
of tuning. The first term in Eq. (
21b
) does not play any
essential role in our set-up due to low enough
m_{3/2}
’s –
cf. Ref.
[
10
]
.

(c)

C_{\rm SUGRA}
is the SUGRA correction
to
V_{\rm I}/V_{\rm I0}
, after subtracting the one in
C_{\rm SSB}
, which is

C_{\rm SUGRA}=c_{2\nu}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2m_{\rm P}^{2}}+c_{4\nu}\frac{\sigma^{4}}{4m_{\rm P}^{4}}~~\mbox{with}~~c_{2\nu}=\frac{\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}}{2m_{\rm P}^{2}}~~\mbox{and}~~c_{4\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+{\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}^{2}}{m_{\rm P}^{2}}\right).

(21d)

Thanks to the minimality of
K_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
4a
) and the
smallness of
\langle{z}\rangle_{\rm I}
the coefficients above are low enough and
allow for FHI to be established – cf. Ref.
[
9
,
10
]
.

4.3
Observational Requirements

The analysis of FHI can be performed in the slow-roll
approximation if we calculate the slow-roll parameters
[
4
]

\epsilon={m^{2}_{\rm P}}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}_{\rm I}}{\sqrt{2}V_{\rm I}}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{m_{\rm P}^{2}}{2}\left(C^{\prime}_{\rm RC}+C^{\prime}_{\rm SSB}\right)^{2}~~\mbox{and}~~\eta=m^{2}_{\rm P}~\frac{V^{\prime\prime}_{\rm I}}{V_{\rm I}}\simeq m_{\rm P}^{2}C^{\prime\prime}_{\rm RC},

(22)

where the derivatives of the various contributions read

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{\rm SSB}

\displaystyle\simeq-{\rm a}_{S}/\sqrt{2V_{\rm I0}},

(23a)

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{\rm RC}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{kp^{2}x}{32M\pi^{2}}\left(4\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}}\right)+x^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{4}{x^{4}}\right)\right),

(23b)

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}_{\rm RC}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{32M^{2}\pi^{2}}\left(4\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}}\right)+3x^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{4}{x^{4}}\right)\right).

(23c)

The required behavior of
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
20
) can be obtained
thanks to the relation
C^{\prime}_{\rm RC}\simeq-C^{\prime}_{\rm SSB}
which is
established for carefully selecting
\kappa
(or
M
) and
{\rm a}_{S}
.
Apparently, we have
C^{\prime}_{\rm SSB}<0
and
C^{\prime}_{\rm RC}>0
for

\sigma_{\star}<\sigma_{\rm max}
since

|4\tanh^{-1}\left({2}/{x^{2}}\right)|>|x^{2}\ln(1-4/x^{4})|
. On the contrary,

C^{\prime\prime}_{\rm RC}<0
, since the negative contribution

3x^{2}\ln(1-4/x^{4})
dominates over the first positive one, and so
we obtain
\eta<0
giving rise to acceptably low
n_{\rm s}
values.

Our model of FHI can be qualified if we test it against a number
of observational requirements. Namely:

(a)

The number of e-foldings elapsed
between the horizon crossing of the pivot scale
k_{\star}=0.05/{\rm Mpc}
and the end of FHI has to be adequately large for the
resolution of the horizon and flatness problems of standard Big
Bang cosmology. Taking into account – see Sec.
5
–, that FHI
is followed in turn, by a matter and a radiation dominated era,
the relevant condition takes the form
[
2
,
4
]
:

{N_{\rm I\star}}=\int_{\sigma_{\rm f}}^{\sigma_{\star}}\frac{d\sigma}{m^{2}_{\rm P}}\>\frac{V_{\rm I}}{V^{\prime}_{\rm I}}\simeq 19.4+{2\over 3}\ln{V^{1/4}_{\rm I0}\over{1~{\rm GeV}}}+{1\over 3}\ln{T_{\rm rh}\over{1~{\rm GeV}}},

(24)

where the prime denotes derivation w.r.t.
\sigma
,
\sigma_{\star}
is the
value of
\sigma
when
k_{\star}
crosses outside the horizon of FHI
and
\sigma_{\rm f}
is the value of
\sigma
at the end of FHI.
This is normally obtained by the critical point

\sigma_{\rm c}=\sqrt{2}|S_{\rm c}|
, i.e., the end of inflation coincides
with the onset of the
B-L
phase transition. Note that

\langle{\sigma}\rangle\simeq 0
, as mentioned below Eq. (
13
), and so it does
not disturb the inflationary dynamics which governs the
\sigma

evolution for
\sigma\geq\sigma_{\rm c}
.

(b)

The amplitude
A_{\rm s}
of the power
spectrum of the curvature perturbation generated by
\sigma

during FHI must be consistent with the data
[
58
]
on

cosmic microwave background
(
CMB
), i.e.,

A_{\rm s}=\frac{1}{12\,\pi^{2}m^{6}_{\rm P}}\;\left.\frac{V_{\rm I}^{3}(\sigma_{\star})}{|V^{\prime}_{\rm I}(\sigma_{\star})|^{2}}\right.\simeq\>2.105\cdot 10^{-9}.

(25)

The observed curvature perturbation is generated wholly by

\sigma
since the other scalars are massive enough during FHI –
see Sec.
4.1
.

(c)

The remaining observables – the scalar
spectral index
n_{\rm s}
, its running
\alpha_{\rm s}
, and the scalar-to-tensor
ratio
r
– which are calculated by the following standard
formulas

n_{\rm s}=1-6\epsilon_{\star}\ +\ 2\eta_{\star},\alpha_{\rm s}={2}\left(4\eta_{\star}^{2}-(n_{\rm s}-1)^{2}\right)/3-2\xi_{\star}~~\mbox{and}~~r=16\epsilon_{\star},

(26)

(where
\xi\simeq m_{\rm P}^{4}~V^{\prime}_{\rm I}V^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\rm I}/V^{2}_{\rm I}

and all the variables with the subscript
\star
are evaluated at

\sigma=\sigma_{\star}
) must be in agreement with data. We take into
account the latest Planck release 4 – including TT,TE,EE+lowE
power spectra
[
58
]
–,
Baryon Acoustic Oscillations

(
BAO
), CMB-lensing and BICEP/
Keck
data
[
59
]
.
Fitting it with
\Lambda
CDM
+r
we obtain approximately

n_{\rm s}=0.965\pm 0.009~~\mbox{and}~~r\lesssim 0.032,

(27)

at 95
\%

confidence level
(
c.l.
) with
negligible
|\alpha_{\rm s}|\ll 0.01
.

5
Reheating Process

Soon after FHI, the IS and
z
enter into an oscillatory phase
about their minima in Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
) and eventually decay via
their coupling to lighter degrees of freedom. Note that
\theta

and
{\theta_{S}}
remain well stabilized at their values shown below
Eq. (
9
) during and after FHI and so they do not participate in
the phase of damped oscillations. The commencement of

z
-dominated phase occurs for values of Hubble rate
H_{z\rm I}\sim m_{z}
. Given that
\langle{z}\rangle\sim m_{\rm P}
– see Eq. (
11
) –,
the initial energy density of its oscillations
\rho_{z\rm I}
is
comparable with the energy density of the universe
\rho_{z\rm It}
at the onset of these oscillations since

\rho_{z\rm I}\sim m_{z}^{2}\langle{z}\rangle^{2}~~\mbox{and}~~\rho_{z\rm It}=3m_{\rm P}^{2}H_{z\rm I}^{2}\simeq 3m_{\rm P}^{2}m_{z}^{2}.

(31)

Therefore, we expect that
z
will dominate the energy density of
the universe until completing its decay through its weak
gravitational interactions. Due to weakness of these interactions
we expect that the reheating temperature
T_{\rm rh}
will be rather
low. This is the notorious cosmic moduli problem

[
41
,
40
]
which plagues the vast majority of the SUGRA
settings.

In our model though
T_{\rm rh}
adequately increases thanks to the
large enough
m_{z}
and
\mu
originated by
K_{\mu}
in Eq. (
6a
). To
show this fact we estimate
T_{\rm rh}
by
[
55
]

T_{\rm rh}=\left({72/5\pi^{2}g_{\rm rh*}}\right)^{1/4}{\Gamma}_{{\delta}z}^{1/2}m_{\rm P}^{1/2},

(32)

where
g_{\rm rh*}\simeq 10.75-100
counts for the effective number of the
relativistic degrees of freedom at
T_{\rm rh}
. This is achieved for

\tilde{\uptau}_{\rm rh}\simeq-\frac{2}{3}\ln\frac{2}{5}\sqrt{3}m_{\rm P}{\Gamma}_{{\delta}z}\rho_{z\rm I}^{-1/2},

(33)

where
\tilde{\uptau}=\ln\left(R/R_{\rm I}\right)
with
R
the scale
factor of the universe and
R_{\rm I}
its value at the onset of
the
z
oscillations. Also
{\Gamma}_{{\delta}z}
is the total decay width of the
(canonically normalized) sgoldstino

8
Conclusions

We analyzed the implementation of FHI together with the SUSY
breaking and the CS formation in a B-L extension of MSSM. We
adopted the super- and Kähler potentials in Eqs. (
1
) and (
3
) applying
an approximate
R
symmetry. The model offers the following
interesting achievements:

•

Observationally acceptable FHI of hilltop-type adjusting the
tadpole parameter
{\rm a}_{S}
and the
B-L
breaking scale
M
in the
ranges of Eq. (
30
).

•

A prediction for the SUSY-mass scale
\widetilde{m}
which turns out
to be of the order of PeV.

•

An interpretation of the DE problem without extensive
tuning. We obtain
\lambda\sim 10^{-12}
in Eq. (
2c
).

•

Explantation of the
\mu
term of MSSM with
|\mu|\sim m_{3/2}

– see Eq. (
6a
) – by invoking a appropriate modification of the
Giudice-Masiero mechanism.

•

Reheating generated due to the domination of the sgoldstino
condensate after the end of FHI; since
\mu
is of the order PeV,
the resulting
T_{\rm rh}
is higher than its lower bound from BBN.

•

B-L
CSs which, if metastable, explain rather well the
recent observations on GWs. A characteristic of the obtained
spectra is their suppression at relatively large frequencies
(
f>0.1~{\rm Hz}
) which is due to the long-lasting matter
domination caused by the sgolstino oscillations.

•

Generation of neutrino masses via the type I see-saw
mechanism supported by
W_{\rm MD}
and
K_{\rm D}
in
Eqs. (
2e
) and (
6b
).

A potential shortcoming of our proposal is that baryogenesis is
made difficult due to the low reheat temperature. Non-thermal
leptogenesis is not operative since the sgoldstino (with mass of
order
1~{\mbox{\rm PeV}}
) is lighter than
N_{i}^{c}
which acquire mass of
order
1~{\mbox{\rm ZeV}}
– cf. Ref.
[
10
,
15
]
– and so the direct decay
of
z
into
N_{i}^{c}
is forbidden. However, there are extensions of
MSSM
[
72
]
where the late decay of the sgoldstino may
generate non-thermally the baryon asymmetry of the universe.
Alternatively, this problem may be overcome applying improved
attempts
[
73
]
based on the idea of cold electroweak
baryogenesis
[
74
]
. As regards the CDM, the candidacy of
lightest neutralino has to be investigated thoroughly solving
precisely the relevant Bolzmann equations, as in Ref.
[
55
,
75
]
. In case that the abundance is low we can open up slightly
the decay channel of
z
into
\widetilde{G}
so that we obtain a
controllable production of neutralinos. Another aspect is the fate
of
R
axion which remains stable throughout our setting

[
76
]
.

As regards naturalness, it is a puzzle why the SUSY should appear
at such scale, which is higher than the electroweak scale making
the fine-tuning severe, while it is much smaller than the
fundamental energy scale such as the GUT or Planck scales. Our
scenario provides a possible solution to this issue: this may be
due to the inflationary selection. Namely, the apparent
fine-tuning could be a result of combination of the FHI and a bias
toward high-scale SUSY in the landscape.

We check here if the presence of

in Eq. (
3
)
– which is absent in other similar settings
[
10
,
15
]
–
has some impact on the derivation of the neutrino masses. This is
because
K_{\rm D}
in Eq. (
6b
) contributes to Dirac neutrino
masses according to the formulae of Ref.
[
52
]
. In our case the
non-vanishing contributions are

\displaystyle m_{{\alpha}{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle{\partial^{2}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}W_{\rm MD}}\rangle-\langle{K_{\rm H}^{ZZ^{*}}\partial^{3}_{{\alpha}{\beta}Z^{*}}K_{\rm HO}\partial_{Z}W_{\rm H}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{3/2}}{2}\left(\langle{K_{\rm H}^{ZZ^{*}}\partial^{3}_{{\alpha}{\beta}Z^{*}}K_{\rm HO}\partial_{Z}K_{\rm H}}\rangle-\langle{\partial^{2}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}K_{\rm HO}}\rangle\right),

(63)

where
\partial_{\alpha}:=\partial/\partial{Y^{\alpha}}
,

\partial_{Z}:=\partial/\partial{Z}
and we define

K_{\rm HO}=K_{\rm H}+K_{\mu}+K_{\rm D}+|Y^{\alpha}|^{2}~~\mbox{with}~~Y^{\alpha}={H_{u}},{H_{d}},\Phi,\bar{\Phi},L~~\mbox{and}~~N^{c}.

(64)

If we confine ourselves to the case of the third generation we
obtain the following mass matrix for the neutrino masses

\mbox{
$\left\lgroup\begin{matrix}0&m_{[\rm D}K]+m_{[\rm D}W]\cr m_{[\rm D}K]+m_{[\rm D}W]&m_{\rm M}\end{matrix}\right\rgroup$},

(65)

where the various contributions read

\displaystyle m_{[\rm D}W]=h_{N}\langle{{H_{u}}}\rangle/2~~\&~~m_{\rm M}=(2\nu)^{\nu}M/3^{\nu/2}

(66)

\displaystyle m_{[\rm D}K]=\frac{4^{n}m}{3^{n}m_{\rm P}}\lambda_{\rm D}\nu^{2\nu}\langle{{H_{u}}}\rangle\omega\left(\omega^{N/2-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\nu+\frac{2}{3}\nu^{2}\right)-\frac{3}{8}\right),

(67)

from which those in the first line come from the terms of
Eq. (
2e
) whereas this in the second line originates from
Eq. (
6b
). Note that
\omega
is given in Eq. (
12
). For the inputs
of BPB in Table
2
we obtain from the well-known (type I)
see-saw formula – see e.g. Ref.
[
15
]
– for the mass of the
third generation neutrino,
\nu_{3}
, and
N^{c}
,
N^{c}_{3}
,
respectively

m_{3\nu}\simeq m_{[\rm D}W]^{2}/M\simeq 0.05~{\mbox{\rm eV}}~~\mbox{and}~~M_{3N^{c}}\simeq 63~{\mbox{\rm ZeV}}~~\mbox{if}~~h_{3N}=0.5~~\mbox{and}~~\lambda_{\rm 3N^{c}}=0.03.

(68)

The obtained
m_{3\nu}

assume that the three active
\nu_{i}
’s have the normal mass
hierarchy so that
m_{3\nu}
is equal to the mass induced by
atmospheric
\nu_{i}

In conclusion, our model assures acceptable neutrino masses from
the superpotential term in Eq. (
2e
) without sizable contribution
from the terms of Eq. (
6b

K

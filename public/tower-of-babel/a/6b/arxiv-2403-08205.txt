PMCV hypersurfaces in non-flat pseudo-Riemannian space forms


Abstract




Chao Yang
1
, Jiancheng Liu
1,∗

2

1. College of Mathematics and Statistics, Northwest Normal University, Lanzhou, 730070,

Abstract:

In this paper, we prove that PMCV (i.e.
\Delta\vec{H}
is proportional to
\vec{H}
) hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
of a non-flat pseudo-Riemannian space form
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
with at most two distinct principal curvatures is minimal or locally isoparametric, and compute the mean curvature for the isoparametric ones. As an application, we give full classification results of such non-minimal Lorentzian hypersurfaces of non-flat Lorentz space forms.

Keywords:
proper mean curvature vector field; pseudo-Riemannian space form; isoparameric hypersurface; mean curvature; Lorentz space form

Introduction

Let
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
be the (
n+1
)-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian space form
of constant curvature
c
with index
s\geq 0
, and
x:\!M^{n}_{r}\to N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
be an isometric immersion of
a pseudo-Riemannian hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
into
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
.
Denote by
\vec{H}
and
\Delta
the mean curvature
vector field and the Laplace operator of
M^{n}_{r}
with respect to the induced metric, respectively.

If the hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
satisfies the equation

\Delta\vec{H}=\lambda\vec{H},

for some real constant
\lambda
,
then it is said to have proper mean curvature vector field (abbreviated as PMCV).
Specially, when
\lambda=nc
, the hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
is known as biharmonic one.

The following geometric question was proposed by B.-Y. Chen in
[
1
,
2
]
:

Determine all PMCV submanifolds of pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
. In particular, classify PMCV hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
.

In 1988, B.-Y. Chen
[
1
]
initiated the study of this problem for
s=0
, and proved that the PMCV surface
M^{2}
in
\mathbb{E}^{3}
is minimal, or locally congruent to a circular cylinder. In 1991, A. Ferrández and P. Lucus showed in
[
7
]
that the PMCV hypersurface
M^{n}
of
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
with at most two distinct principal curvatures is minimal, or locally congruent to
\mathbb{E}^{l}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-l}(\frac{\lambda}{n-l})\ (0\leq l\leq n-1)
. Afterwards, B.-Y. Chen and O. J. Garay
[
3
]
derived that
\delta(2)
-ideal PMCV hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
is minimal, or locally congruent to
\mathbb{E}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(\frac{\lambda}{n-1})
.

For the case
s=1
, A. Ferrández and P. Lucus
[
8
]
classified PMCV surfaces, and generalized the result in
[
9
]
to
n
-dimensional hypersurfaces whose minimal polynomial of the shape operator is at most of degree two. In
[
12
]
, C. Yang and J.-C. Liu weaken this condition to the number of distinct principal curvatures be not larger than two and obtained classification result.
For general index
s
, L. Du
[
4
]
classified hypersuraces with diagonalizable shape operator and at most two distinct principal curvatures.

Naturally, it is interesting to consider the classification problem of PMCV hypersurfaces in non-flat pseudo-Riemannian space forms
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
. Under the assumption that the shape operator is diagonalizable, L. Du
[
4
]
classified such hypersurfaces of
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)\ (c\neq 0)
with at most two distinct principal curvatures.
In this paper, we show that such PMCV hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
of
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)\ (c\neq 0)
is minimal or locally isoparametric and give the value or range of the mean curvature, without the restriction that the shape operator is diagonalizable. As an application, we classify completely such non-minimal Lorentzian PMCV hypersurfaces of
N^{n+1}_{1}(c)\ (c\neq 0)
by using classification theory of isoparametric hypersurfaces.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{s}

(0\leq s\leq n)
be the
n
-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space equipped with the canonical pseudo-Euclidean metric of index
s

g_{0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\text{d}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}\text{d}x_{j}^{2},

where
(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
is a rectangular coordinate system of
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{s}
.
Set

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}_{s}(c)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}\Big|-\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n+1}x_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{c}>0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}_{s}(c)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s+1}\Big|-\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=s+2}^{n+1}x_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{c}<0\right\},

where
c
is a nonzero real constant.
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{s}(c)
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{s}(c)
are pseudo-Riemannian manifolds of curvature
c
with index
s
, which together with
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{s}
are called
pseudo-Riemannian space forms
, denoted by
N^{n}_{s}(c)
. In particular, for
s=1
,
N^{n}_{1}(c)
are called
Lorentz space forms
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}(c)
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{1}(c)
known as
de Sitter space
and
anti-de Sitter space
, respectively.
A non-zero vector
X
in
N^{n}_{s}(c)
is said to be
time-like
,

space-like
or
light-like
, according to whether
\langle X,X\rangle
is negative, positive or zero.

Let
M^{n}_{r}
be a nondegenerate hypersurface in
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
,

\vec{\xi}
denote a unit normal vector field to
M^{n}_{r}
,
then
\varepsilon=\langle\vec{\xi},\vec{\xi}\rangle=\pm 1
.
Set
\nabla
and
\tilde{\nabla}
the Levi-Civita connections of
M^{n}_{r}

and
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)
, respectively. For any vector fields
X,Y
tangent to
M^{n}_{r}
,
it follows that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)\vec{\xi},

where
h
is the scalar-valued second fundamental form.
Denote by
A
,
\vec{H}
and
H
the shape operator of
M^{n}_{r}
associated to

\vec{\xi}
, the mean curvature vector field and the mean curvature,
then
\vec{H}=H\vec{\xi}
and
H=\frac{1}{n}\varepsilon\text{tr}A
.
For any vector fields
X,Y,Z
tangent to
M^{n}_{r}
, the Codazzi and the Gauss equations are given by

\langle(\nabla_{X}A)Y,Z\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{Y}A)X,Z\rangle,

(2.1)

R(X,Y)Z=c(\langle Y,Z\rangle X-\langle X,Z\rangle Y)+\varepsilon\langle A(Y),Z\rangle A(X)-\varepsilon\langle A(X),Z\rangle A(Y),

where
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z
.

According to
[
5
]
,
M^{n}_{r}
is a PMCV hypersurface if and only if

A(\nabla H)=-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H(\nabla H),

(2.2)

\Delta H+\varepsilon H\text{tr}A^{2}=\lambda H.

(2.3)

Suppose the eigenpolynomial
\text{det}(\lambda I-A)
of
A
can be expressed as
(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{n_{1}}(\lambda-\lambda_{2})^{n_{2}}\cdots(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{n_{k}}
,
then
n_{i}
is called the
algebraic multiply
of
\lambda_{i}
. The
geometric multiply
of
\lambda_{i}
refers to the dimension of the eigensubspace

\text{ker}(\lambda_{i}I-A):=\{X|AX=\lambda_{i}X\}
.
A hypersurface whose shape operator has constant eigenpolynomial
is said to be
isoparametric
(cf.
[
10
]
),
which is equivalent to the principal curvatures and their algebriac multiplicities being constants.
In this paper, without special statement, multiplicity refers to algebraic multiplicity.

3
PMCV hypersurfaces in non-flat pseudo-Riemannian space forms

In this section, we study PMCV hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}
of
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)\ (c\neq 0)

with at most two distinct principal curvatures, and prove that it is minimal or locally isoparametric (see Theorem 3.1).
Furthermore, we estimate or compute the value of the mean curvature (see Theorems 3.3 and 3.4).

First of all, we recall from
[
2
,
12
]
that the shape operator
A
of the hypersurface
M^{n}_{r}

can be expressed as an almost diagonal matrix

A=\text{diag}\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{t},\bar{A}_{t+1},\cdots,\bar{A}_{m}\},\ \text{for some}\ t,\ 0\leq t\leq m,

where

\displaystyle A_{i}\!=\!\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\lambda_{i}&&&&\\
1&\lambda_{i}&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&&\\
&&1&\lambda_{i}&\\
&&&1&\lambda_{i}\\
\end{array}\right)\!,\ \bar{A}_{j}\!=\!\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\gamma_{j}&\tau_{j}&&&&\\
-\tau_{j}&\gamma_{j}&0&&&\\
1&0&\gamma_{j}&\tau_{j}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0&\gamma_{j}&\tau_{j}\\
&&&1&-\tau_{j}&\gamma_{j}\\
\end{array}\right),\tau_{j}\neq 0,

for
1\leq i\leq t
and
t+1\leq j\leq m
, relative to the basis
\mathfrak{B}\!=\!\{u_{i_{1}},u_{i_{2}},\cdots,u_{i_{\alpha_{i}}},u_{\bar{j}_{1}},

u_{\tilde{j}_{1}},u_{\bar{j}_{2}},u_{\tilde{j}_{2}},\cdots,u_{\bar{j}_{\beta_{j}}},u_{\tilde{j}_{\beta_{j}}}|1\leq i\leq t,\ t+1\leq j\leq m\}
with all scalar products zero except

\langle u_{i_{a}},u_{i_{b}}\rangle=\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
and
\langle u_{\bar{j}_{c}},u_{\bar{j}_{d}}\rangle=1=-\langle u_{\tilde{j}_{c}},u_{\tilde{j}_{d}}\rangle

if

a+b=\alpha_{i}+1
. Here
1\leq i\leq t,\ c+d=\beta_{j}+1,\ t+1\leq j\leq m
and

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}+2\sum_{j=t+1}^{m}\beta_{j}=n.

It follows from the form of
A
that
M^{n}_{r}
has real principal curvatures

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{t}

and imaginary eigenvalues

\gamma_{t+1}+\tau_{t+1}\sqrt{-1},\gamma_{t+1}-\tau_{t+1}\sqrt{-1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}+\tau_{m}\sqrt{-1},\gamma_{m}-\tau_{m}\sqrt{-1}.

Notice that the imaginary principal curvatures appear as conjugate pairs.

Theorem 3.1

Let
M^{n}_{r}
be a PMCV hypersurface of
N^{n+1}_{s}(c)\ (c\neq 0)
.
Suppose that
M^{n}_{r}
has at most two distinct principal curvatures, then
M^{n}_{r}
has constant mean curvature.
Furthermore, it is minimal or locally isoparametric.

Proof
Firstly, we prove that if the mean curvature
H
of
M^{n}_{r}
is a constant,
then
M^{n}_{r}
is minimal or locally isoparametric.
Let
x\in M^{n}_{r}
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{x}
such that
the multiplicities of distinct principal curvatures are constants on
U_{x}
.
We work on
U_{x}
and show that
M^{n}_{r}
is isoparametric.

Suppose
\mu
and
\nu
are distinct principal curvatures of
M^{n}_{r}
with multiplicities
l

(1\leq l\leq n)
and
n-l
, then

n\varepsilon H=\text{tr}A=l\mu+(n-l)\nu,

(3.1)

and
\text{tr}A^{2}=l\mu^{2}+(n-l)\nu^{2}
. If
H
is a nonzero constant, then (
2.3
) implies
\text{tr}A^{2}=\varepsilon\lambda
. So,

l\mu^{2}+(n-l)\nu^{2}=\varepsilon\lambda.

(3.2)

It follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that
\mu
and
\nu
are constants. Hence,
M^{n}_{r}
is isoparametric on
U_{x}
.

In the following, we show that
M^{n}_{r}
has constant mean curvature.

When the principal curvatures are imaginary,
-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H
(a real function) is not an eigenvalue of
A
.
It follows from (
2.2
) that
\nabla H\equiv 0
, i.e.
H=\text{const.}

We need only treat the situation that the principal curvatures are all real.

Assume that
H
is not a constant, then there exists a neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x\in M^{n}_{r}
such that
H\neq 0
and
\nabla H\neq 0
.
From (
2.2
), we get that
-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H
is an eigenvalue of
A
, and
\nabla H
is a corresponding eigenvector.
When the principal curvatures of
M^{n}_{r}
are all the same, denoted by
\mu
, then
\mu=\frac{1}{n}\text{tr}A=\varepsilon H
.
Thus,
-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H\ (\neq\mu)
is not an eigenvalue of
A
, a contradiction.

Now, we assume that the number of distinct principal curvatures is two, i.e.

A=\text{diag}\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
and there are two distinct values among
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}\}
.
Observe the form of
A
,
\nabla H
is in one of the directions
u_{i_{\alpha_{i}}},1\leq i\leq m
.
Without loss of generality, we suppose
\nabla H
is in
the direction of
u_{1_{\alpha_{1}}}
, which may or may not be a light-like vector.
We follow different processes to lead contradictions for those two cases.

At the begining, we provide some important equations. We remark that, if no otherwise specified,
the ranges of
i
and
a
in the index
i_{a}
are as:

1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq a\leq\alpha_{i}
.
And when
a=\alpha_{i}
(or
a=1
), the connection coefficients with the index
i_{a+1}
(or
i_{a-1}
) disappear.

Let
[i]=\{j|\ \lambda_{j}=\lambda_{i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
\nabla_{u_{i_{a}}}u_{j_{b}}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{d=1}^{\alpha_{k}}\Gamma^{k_{d}}_{i_{a}j_{b}}u_{k_{d}}
. In view of the assumption that
\nabla H
is in
the direction of
u_{1_{\alpha_{1}}}
, as well as the compatibility and symmetry of the connection, we obtain

\begin{cases}u_{1_{1}}(H)\neq 0,\ u_{B}(H)=0,\ \Gamma^{1_{1}}_{BC}=\Gamma^{1_{1}}_{CB},\\
\Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{i}-a+1}}_{Di_{a}}=0,\ \Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{i}-b+1}}_{Di_{a}}=-\Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{i}-a+1}}_{Di_{b}},\ \Gamma^{j_{\alpha_{j}-d+1}}_{Di_{a}}=-\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{j}\Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{i}-a+1}}_{Dj_{d}},\end{cases}

(3.3)

for
B,C,D\in\{k_{e},1\leq k\leq m,1\leq e\leq\alpha_{k}\}
and
B,C\neq 1_{1}
.

Putting
X=u_{i_{a}},Y=u_{j_{b}},Z=u_{k_{\alpha_{k}-d+1}}
into (
2.1
), it gives

\displaystyle u_{i_{a}}\!(\lambda_{j})\delta_{j_{b}}^{k_{d}}\!+\!(\lambda_{j}-

\displaystyle\lambda_{k})\Gamma_{i_{a}j_{b}}^{k_{d}}\!+\!\Gamma_{i_{a}j_{b+1}}^{k_{d}}\!-\!\Gamma_{i_{a}j_{b}}^{k_{d-1}}\!=\!u_{j_{b}}\!(\lambda_{i})\delta_{i_{a}}^{k_{d}}\!+\!(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})\Gamma_{j_{b}i_{a}}^{k_{d}}\!+\!\Gamma_{j_{b}i_{a+1}}^{k_{d}}\!-\!\Gamma_{j_{b}i_{a}}^{k_{d-1}},

(3.4)

where
\delta_{j_{b}}^{i_{a}}=1
if
i_{a}=j_{b}
, and
\delta_{j_{b}}^{i_{a}}=0
if
i_{a}\neq j_{b}
.
Using (
3.3
), we deduce from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i=j=k=d=1\Rightarrow\Gamma_{1_{\alpha_{1}-1}1_{\alpha_{1}}}^{1_{\alpha_{1}-1}}\!=\cdots=\!\Gamma_{1_{1}1_{\alpha_{1}}}^{1_{1}}\!=0;

(3.5)

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i=k=d=1,j\in[1],j\neq 1\Rightarrow\Gamma_{1_{a+1}j_{\alpha_{j}}}^{1_{1}}\!=0;

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i=k=d=1,j\notin[1]\Rightarrow\Gamma_{1_{a}j_{b}}^{1_{1}}\!=0;

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i\notin[1],j=1,b=\alpha_{1},k=1\Rightarrow\Gamma_{1_{\alpha_{1}}i_{a}}^{1_{d}}\!=0;

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i,j\neq 1,j\notin[i],k=d=1\Rightarrow\Gamma_{i_{a}j_{b}}^{1_{1}}\!=0;

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i,j\!\neq\!1,j\!\in\![i],k=\!d\!=\!1\!\Rightarrow\!\Gamma_{i_{a}1_{\alpha_{1}}}^{j_{b}}\!=\!\Gamma_{i_{a+1}1_{\alpha_{1}}}^{j_{b+1}}\!,\ \Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}j_{b+1}}^{1_{1}}\!=\!\Gamma_{i_{a}\!1_{\alpha_{1}}\!}^{j_{e}}\!=\!0,e<a;

\displaystyle\text{for}\ i,a\neq 1,j=d=1,b=\alpha_{1},k=i\Rightarrow\Gamma_{1_{\alpha_{1}}i_{a}}^{i_{1}}\!=0.

Let

\begin{cases}A_{i,0,j_{b}}=\Gamma_{i_{1}j_{b}}^{i_{1}}+\Gamma_{i_{2}j_{b}}^{i_{2}}+\cdots+\Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}j_{b}}^{i_{\alpha_{i}}},\\

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
A_{i,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b}}=\Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}j_{b}}^{i_{1}},\end{cases}\ \ \text{for}\ j\in[1],\ i\notin[1],

then (
3.4
) for
k=i
implies

\begin{cases}(-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H-\lambda_{i})A_{i,0,j_{b}}+A_{i,0,j_{b+1}}-A_{i,1,j_{b}}=\alpha_{i}u_{j_{b}}(\lambda_{i}),\\

(-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H-\lambda_{i})A_{i,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b}}+A_{i,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b+1}}=0,\end{cases}

1\leq r\leq\alpha_{i}-2
. It follows that

A_{i,s,j_{b}}=0,\ 1\leq s\leq\alpha_{i}-1,\\

(3.6)

(-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H-\lambda_{i})A_{i,0,j_{b}}+A_{i,0,j_{b+1}}=\alpha_{i}u_{j_{b}}(\lambda_{i}).

(3.7)

Set

\begin{cases}B_{i,k,0,j_{b}}=\Gamma_{i_{1}j_{b}}^{k_{1}}+\Gamma_{i_{2}j_{b}}^{k_{2}}+\cdots+\Gamma_{i_{y_{0}}j_{b}}^{k_{y_{0}}},\\
B_{i,k,1,j_{b}}=\Gamma_{i_{2}j_{b}}^{k_{1}}+\Gamma_{i_{3}j_{b}}^{k_{2}}+\cdots+\Gamma_{i_{y_{1}}j_{b}}^{k_{y_{1}-1}},\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
B_{i,k,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b}}=\Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}j_{b}}^{k_{1}},\end{cases}\!

for
j\in[1]
,
i,k\notin[1]
,
i\neq k
and
y_{r}=\min\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{k}+r\}
,
0\leq r\leq\alpha_{i}-1
, then we obtain from (
3.4
) that

\begin{cases}(-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H-\lambda_{k})B_{i,k,s,j_{b}}+B_{i,k,s,j_{b+1}}-B_{i,k,s+1,j_{b}}=0,\\
(-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H-\lambda_{k})B_{i,k,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b}}+B_{i,k,\alpha_{i}-1,j_{b+1}}=0,\end{cases}

which implies

B_{i,k,r,j_{b}}=0.

(3.8)

We treat the two cases that
\nabla H
is not light-like or light-like respectively and deduce contradictions.

Case 1:
\nabla H
is not light-like.

Since
\nabla H
is in the direction
u_{1_{\alpha_{1}}}
, we have
\langle u_{1_{\alpha_{1}}},u_{1_{\alpha_{1}}}\rangle\neq 0
, which implies
2\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1}+1
, i.e.
\alpha_{1}=1
.
We find from the seventh equation of (
3.5
) that
if
\lambda_{i}=-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H
for some
i\neq 1
, then
u_{1_{1}}(H)=0
,
which contradicts to (
3.3
).
Hence,
\lambda_{i}\neq-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H
for
2\leq i\leq m
.
Combining
\lambda_{1}=-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon H
and
\text{tr}A=n\varepsilon H
, we know
\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{m}=\frac{3n\varepsilon H}{2(n-1)}
.
It follows from the seventh equation of (
3.5
) that

W:=\Gamma^{i_{a}}_{i_{a}1_{1}}\!=-\frac{3u_{1_{1}}(H)}{(n+2)H},\ 2\leq i\leq m.

(3.9)

Lemma 3.2

We have

u_{1_{1}}(W)+W^{2}=\frac{3\varepsilon\varepsilon_{1}n^{2}H^{2}}{4(n-1)}-c\varepsilon_{1}.

(3.10)

Proof
Let
j=b=1,d=1
in (
3.4
), then

-\frac{n(n+2)\varepsilon H}{2(n-1)}\Gamma_{i_{a}1_{1}}^{k_{1}}\!=\Gamma_{1_{1}i_{a+1}}^{k_{1}},\ 2\leq i,k\leq m,\ i\neq k.

(3.11)

Combining the sixth equation of (
3.5
), it leads to

\Gamma_{1_{1}i_{a+1}}^{k_{1}}\!=0,\ 2\leq a\leq\alpha_{i}-1.

(3.12)

Calculate
\langle R(u_{1_{1}},u_{i_{\alpha_{i}}})u_{1_{1}},u_{i_{1}}\rangle
by using the Gauss equation, combining (
3.5
) and (
3.12
), we have

u_{1_{1}}(W)+W^{2}-\frac{3\varepsilon\varepsilon_{1}n^{2}H^{2}}{4(n-1)}+c\varepsilon_{1}=P_{i},\ 2\leq i\leq m,

(3.13)

where

P_{i}=\sum_{2\leq k\leq m,k\neq i}\!\left(\Gamma_{k_{\alpha_{k}}1_{1}}^{i_{\alpha_{i}}}\Gamma_{1_{1}i_{\alpha_{i}}}^{k_{\alpha_{k}}}\!-\Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}1_{1}}^{k_{\alpha_{k}}}\Gamma_{1_{1}k_{\alpha_{k}}}^{i_{\alpha_{i}}}\!+\Gamma_{k_{\alpha_{k}-1}1_{1}}^{i_{\alpha_{i}}}\Gamma_{1_{1}i_{\alpha_{i}}}^{k_{\alpha_{k}-1}}\!-\Gamma_{i_{\alpha_{i}}1_{1}}^{k_{\alpha_{k}}}\Gamma_{k_{\alpha_{k}}1_{1}}^{i_{\alpha_{i}}}\right).

From the sixth equation of (
3.5
), we obtain that
\Gamma^{i_{1}}_{k_{1}1_{1}}\!=\Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{k}}}_{k_{\alpha_{k}}1_{1}}\!=0
if
\alpha_{k}<\alpha_{i}\ (i,k\neq 1)
.
And if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{i}\ (i,k\neq 1)
,
(
3.8
) tells us that

\Gamma^{i_{1}}_{k_{1}1_{1}}\!+\Gamma^{i_{2}}_{k_{2}1_{1}}\!+\cdots+\Gamma^{i_{\alpha_{i}}}_{k_{\alpha_{k}}1_{1}}\!=0,

which together with the sixth equation of (
3.5
) implies
\Gamma^{i_{1}}_{k_{1}1_{1}}\!=0

Opers of higher types, Quot-schemes and Frobenius instability loci
Kirti Joshi, Christian Pauly

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper we continue our study of the Frobenius instability locus
in the coarse moduli space of semi-stable vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
0

over a smooth projective curve defined over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic
p>0
. In a previous paper we identified the “maximal"
Frobenius instability strata with opers (more precisely as opers of type
1
in the terminology of the present paper) and related them
to certain Quot-schemes of Frobenius direct images of line bundles. The main
aim of this paper is to describe for any integer
q\geq 1
a conjectural
generalization of this correspondence between opers of type
q
and
Quot-schemes of Frobenius direct images of vector bundles of rank
q
.
We also give a conjectural formula for the dimension of the Frobenius
instability locus.

Keywords.
Vector bundles, Semi-stability, Frobenius map, Quot-scheme, Oper

1. Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number. Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. Let
X/k
be a connected, smooth projective curve over
k
. We will write
F:X\to X
for the absolute Frobenius morphism of
X
. A foundational classical problem in the theory of vector bundles on smooth, projective curves is the following:

Problem 1.1
.

Describe the Frobenius instability locus, i.e. the locus of all stable (and also semi-stable) vector bundles
V
over
X
such
that
F^{*}(V)
is not semi-stable.

This goal has been partially achieved by us in
[
JP15
]
where, for
p\gg 0
we provided an explicit construction of all stable bundles
V
such that
F^{*}(V)
is not semi-stable. By a well-known theorem of
[
Sha76
]
, one may equip the moduli space of semi-stable vector bundles of fixed degree and rank by a stratification defined using Harder-Narasimhan polygons of
F^{*}(V)
.
The main problem addressed in the present paper is the finer problem:

Problem 1.2
.

Describe all Frobenius instability strata.

One of the important results of
[
JP15
]
asserts that there is an explicit polygon, called the oper polygon (which is always attained in every genus and rank) with the property that the Harder-Narasimhan polygon of every vector bundle
F^{*}(V)
, with
V
stable,
lies on or strictly below the oper polygon.

So to clarify the second problem, one can for example ask: do all possible Harder-Narasimhan polygons occur?
This seems substantially more difficult and complete results are presently available only for
p=2
(see
[
JX00
]
).
We have obtained a number of results in small rank, genus, and characteristic, which we will report in a companion paper under preparation. Recently
[
Li19
]
has also studied the genus-
2
, rank-
3
, characteristic-
3
case in detail (our small genus, rank, characteristic results include his results, but by methods quite different from his).

The description which we provided in our answer to Problem
1.1
is that all Frobenius-destabilized stable bundles arise
from suitable quotients of bundles of the form
F_{*}(Q)
with
Q
a stable vector bundle. In all the small genus, small rank, small characteristic situations, the vector bundle
Q
is of rank
1
. If
\mathrm{rk}(Q)=1
, the Quot-schemes
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

parameterizing subsheaves of given rank
r
and degree
0
of
F_{*}(Q)
are substantially better behaved than for general rank :
in fact, one of the main results of
[
JP15
]
says that if
\mathrm{rk}(Q)=1
then the dimension of

{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
is
0
if its expected dimension is
0
, and its
k
-rational points correspond to opers (of type
1
). As shown
in section 6, this fails if
{\rm rk}(Q)>1
. The existence of these higher-dimensional components of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
stems from
certain line subbundles
L\hookrightarrow Q
of sufficiently high degree, which induce natural embeddings

\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}(F_{*}(L))\hookrightarrow{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
.

In the present paper we lay the ground work for addressing the second problem and its finer special cases. The key tool
we introduce here is the notion of opers of type
q
and rank
\ell q
(see
[
JP15
, Definition 3.1.1]
, recalled in Section 4). This notion generalizes the notion of an oper

(V,\nabla,V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
studied by
[
BD00
]
and consisting of a vector bundle
V
equipped
with an integrable connection
\nabla
and a full flag
V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
satisfying some transversality conditions. An oper of
type
q
should be thought of as the bundle analog of the parabolic induction (and its adjoint the Jacquet Functor) in
the theory of automorphic forms — the parabolic in the present case is of type given by the
\ell
-tuple
(q,\ldots,q)
,
which corresponds to a partial flag whose associated quotients are all of rank
q
.
Given an oper of type
q
and rank
\ell q
we naturally obtain a rank-
q
bundle
Q
as first quotient of the oper filtration.

In the case
{\rm rk}(Q)>1
we conjecture (Conjecture 7.6) a similar statement assuming that one restricts attention
to “non-degenerate" subsheaves of
F_{*}(Q)
, i.e. excluding in particular the above-mentioned sub-Quot-schemes

\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}(F_{*}(L))
. More precisely, we conjecture that, if the expected dimension of
\mathrm{Quot}^{\ell q,0}(F_{*}(Q))

is
0
, then dormant opers of type
q={\rm rk}(Q)
form a non-empty open subset of dimension
0
of the
Quot-scheme
\mathrm{Quot}^{\ell q,0}(F_{*}(Q))
, which has, as mentioned above, components of dimension
>0
.
Here dormant means that the
p
-curvature of the connection
\nabla
is zero.
We check that this conjecture holds when
Q
is a semi-stable direct sum of
q

line bundles (Theorem 3.5).

Finally, somewhat independently of the previous considerations, we give a conjecture for the dimension of the
Frobenius instability locus
\mathcal{J}(r)
in the coarse moduli space of semi-stable rank
r
and degree
0

vector bundles. This conjecture says that

\dim\mathcal{J}(r)\geq(r^{2}-r+1)(g-1)-(r-1).

The conjecture holds for
r=2
. We also conjecture that a general vector bundle in
\mathcal{J}(r)

has “minimal" Harder-Narasimhan filtration (Conjecture 8.3).

2. Preliminaries on Quot-schemes

In what follows, the following notations and assumptions will be in force.
Let
X
be a smooth, projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field
k
of
characteristic
p>0
. Let
F:X\to X
be the absolute Frobenius morphism of
X
. For a vector bundle
V
,
we shall write
V^{*}
for its dual
\mathscr{H}om(V,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

Suppose
\theta
is a theta-characteristic for
X
. Let
\mathscr{G}_{2}
be the unique non-split extension of
\theta^{-1}
by
\theta
: this bundle depends on the choice of the theta-characteristic
\theta
but our notation suppresses this dependence. For
r<p
let
\mathscr{G}_{r}={\rm Sym}^{r-1}(\mathscr{G}_{2})
which will be called the Gunning bundle of rank
r
. This is an indecomposable bundle of degree zero and trivial determinant.

We recall two formulas from
[
JRXY06
]
. Let
Q
be a vector bundle of rank
q
and slope
\mu(Q)
on
X
. Then

(2.1)

\deg(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))=q\mu(Q)+q(p-1)(g-1),

and equivalently

(2.2)

\mu(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))=\frac{\mu(Q)}{p}+\frac{(p-1)(g-1)}{p}.

The first is a consequence of the Riemann-Roch formula
\chi(V)=\deg(V)+{\rm rk}(V)(1-g)
for a vector bundle
V
of rank
{\rm rk}(V)
and degree
\deg(V)
on
X

and the fact that
\chi(V)=\chi(\mathit{F}_{*}({V}))
for the finite map
F
. The second is equivalent to the first.

Let
Q
be a stable bundle on
X
of rank
q
and slope
\mu(Q)=\mu<0
. Let
r\geq 1
be an integer.
For a coherent sheaf
V
and a surjection
V\to G
to a coherent sheaf, we will say that
G
has codegree
d
if the kernel
\ker(V\to G)
has degree
d
. Similarly we will say that
G
has corank
r
if
\ker(V\to G)
has rank
r
. Let
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
be the Quot-scheme of quotients of
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
of codegree
0
and corank
r
. If
V\to G
is a quotient with kernel
E
we will
habitually write
[E]
for the corresponding point of the relevant Quot-scheme corresponding to this quotient.

Let
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
be a point of the Quot-scheme. Then we define the integer

(2.3)

e(E)={\rm exp.\dim}_{[E]}({{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))})=\chi(\mathscr{H}om(E,\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})/E)).

The integer
e(E)
is called the
expected dimension
of

{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
at the point
[E]
(see
[
HL10
, Chapter 2]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer. Let
Q
be a vector bundle on
X
of rank
q\leq r-1
and
slope
\mu(Q)=\mu
. Let
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
be a point of the Quot-scheme. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

The expected dimension of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
at
[E]
is

e(E)=r\deg(Q)+(r^{2}-qr)(g-1).

(2)

We have
e(E)=0
if and only if

\deg(Q)=(r-q)(1-g).

(3)

In particular, if
r=\ell q
for some integer
\ell\geq 2
, then
e(E)=0
if and only if

\deg(Q)=q(\ell-1)(1-g).

(4)

Moreover if
\mu=-\frac{1}{q}
(equivalently
\deg(Q)=-1
) then

e(E)=(r^{2}-qr)(g-1)-r.

(5)

If
\deg(Q)=(r-q)(1-g)+d
with
d\geq 0
, then

e(E)=rd.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the first assertion as the rest are immediate consequences of the first.
Let
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
be a point corresponding to a quotient
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})\to G
. Then by definition the expected dimension is given by
e(E)=\chi(\mathscr{H}om(E,G))
. By the Riemann-Roch formula
we have

\chi(\mathscr{H}om(E,G))=\deg(E^{*}\otimes G)+{\rm rk}(E){\rm rk}(G)(1-g)

which gives

e(E)=r\deg(G)+r(pq-r)(1-g).

As
E
has degree zero, so
\deg(G)=\deg(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))=q\mu+q(p-1)(g-1)
. Substituting this in the above equation and simplifying the result gives the asserted formula.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Q
be a semistable bundle of rank
q
and
\deg(Q)=(r-q)(1-g)+d
with
q<r<pq
and
d\geq 0
.
Then

(1)

The Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
is non-empty.

(2)

Any irreducible component of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
has dimension
\geq rd
.

(3)

If
p>\max(2r(r-1)(g-1),\frac{2rd}{q})
then for any
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
, the quotient
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})/E
is torsion-free, i.e.,
E\hookrightarrow\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
is a subbundle.

Proof.

The assertion (1) is
[
JP15
, Proposition 2.3.2]
. The assertion (2) is

[
JP15
, Proposition 2.3.4]
. So it remains to prove (3).
Let
E^{\prime}
denote the saturation of
E
in
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
. Then
\mu(E^{\prime})\geq 0
.
Assume on the contrary that
E^{\prime}\neq E
, i.e.,
\mu(E^{\prime})>0
. Then we invoke
[
JP15
, Proposition 4.2.1]
with

n=r
and
\delta=\frac{1}{2r}
, and we note that
\mu(Q)=\frac{(r-q)(1-g)}{q}+\frac{d}{q}
and that we have the following
inequality

\frac{\mu(Q)}{p}+\delta=\frac{(r-q)(1-g)}{pq}+\frac{d}{pq}+\frac{1}{2r}<\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{1}{2r}=\frac{1}{r}.

Then
[
JP15
, Proposition 4.2.1]
implies that

0<\mu(E^{\prime})<\frac{\mu(Q)}{p}+\delta<\frac{1}{r},

which leads to a contradiction, since
{\rm rk}(E^{\prime})={\rm rk}(E)=r
.
∎

3. A finiteness theorem

We start with a lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Q
be a line bundle of degree
-(r-1)(g-1)
with
r\geq 2
. If
r^{\prime}<r
then

{\rm Quot}^{r^{\prime},0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))=\emptyset.

Proof.

Suppose the assertion is not true. Then we have
{\rm Quot}^{r^{\prime},0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))\neq\emptyset
. Consider a subsheaf
E\in{\rm Quot}^{r^{\prime},0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))
. We will use the notation of
[
JP15
, Lemma 3.4.2]
.
Let
W=F^{*}(E)
and equip it with the filtration induced by the
natural filtration
V_{\bullet}
on the bundle
V=F^{*}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))
. We shall denote the latter by

0=W_{m+1}\subset W_{m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{1}\subseteq W_{0}=W
. Let
r_{i}={\rm rk}(W_{i}/W_{i+1})
. Then
1=r_{0}\geq r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{m}\geq 1
. So
r_{i}=1
for all
i\geq 0
. Now we have the following inequalities

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0=\deg(W)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}\deg(V_{i}/V_{i+1}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}(\deg(Q)+i(2g-2)),

(3.4)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(m+1)\deg(Q)+\frac{m(m+1)}{2}(2g-2).

As
{\rm rk}(E)=r^{\prime}
so
m=r^{\prime}-1
and hence the last inequality can be written as

0\leq r^{\prime}(r-1)(1-g)+r^{\prime}(r^{\prime}-1)(g-1)=r^{\prime}(g-1)(r^{\prime}-1-r+1)=r^{\prime}(r^{\prime}-r)(g-1)<0.

Thus we have arrived at a contradiction.
∎

We will now prove a finiteness theorem for the Quot-scheme
{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
when
Q
is a
decomposable bundle.
Assume that
q\geq 2,\ell\geq 2
and that
p>r(r-1)(r-2)(g-1)

for
r=\ell q
.
Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{q}
be
q
distinct line bundles of degree
-(\ell-1)(g-1)
. We denote by
Q
the decomposable bundle

Q=\oplus_{i=1}^{q}L_{i}\ \ \text{and}\ \ Q_{j}=\oplus_{i\neq j}L_{i}\subset Q.

Then for any
j=1,\ldots,q
we have an
inclusion

{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q_{j}}))}\hookrightarrow{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}.

By Proposition
2.5
(2) we have

\dim{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q_{j}}))}\geq q\ell(\ell-1)(g-1)>0.

We denote by

\Omega(Q)
the residual component of these
q
Quot-schemes in
{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
, i.e., we have a union

{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{q}{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q_{j}}))}\cup\Omega(Q).

Theorem 3.5
.

We consider the morphism

\Phi:\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}{\rm Quot^{\ell,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))}\longrightarrow{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

defined by

(S_{i}\subset\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))_{i=1}^{q}\longmapsto(\oplus_{i=1}^{q}S_{i}\subset\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})).

Then
\Phi
induces a bijection at the level of
k
-rational
points

\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}{\rm Quot^{\ell,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))}(k)\simeq\Omega(Q)(k).

In particular
\Omega(Q)(k)\neq\emptyset
and

\dim\Omega(Q)=0.

Proof.

Let
[E]\in{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
. We will denote by
E_{i}=\pi_{i}(E)
the image of
E

under the projection

\pi_{i}:F_{*}(Q)\rightarrow F_{*}(L_{i})
. First we note the equivalence
[E]\in{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q_{i}}))}
if and only if

E_{i}=\pi_{i}(E)=0
. Therefore, for any
[E]\in\left({\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}\setminus\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q_{i}}))}\right)
we have
E_{i}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,q
. Moreover, by
[
JP15
, Proposition 4.5.1 and Theorem 4.1.1]
the vector bundles
E
and
E_{i}

are semi-stable and their degrees are zero. We now consider the kernel

K_{i}=\ker(E\to\oplus_{j\neq i}E_{j}).

Then clearly
K_{i}\subset\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}})
. Note that
K_{i}=E\cap\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}})
and so

K_{i}\cap K_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
. Moreover, by our assumption on
E
, we have

K_{i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,q
. As the bundles
E
and
E_{i}
are
semi-stable of degree zero, the bundles
K_{i}
are also semi-stable of degree zero.
We now apply Lemma
3.1
to the line bundle
L_{i}
of degree
-(\ell-1)(g-1)
and use the fact
that
{\rm Quot^{{\rm rk}(K_{i}),0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))}\neq\emptyset
— since this Quot-scheme contains
[K_{i}]
— and
we obtain that
{\rm rk}(K_{i})\geq\ell
. Thus we have a homomorphism

\oplus_{i=1}^{q}K_{i}\to E

which is injective and so both bundles have the same rank and degree. So this map is an isomorphism.
Moreover, we observe that
K_{i}=E_{i}
for any
i=1,\ldots,q
. Thus we have shown that
\Phi
induces a
bijection at the level of
k
-rational points between
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}{\rm Quot^{\ell,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))}(k)
and

\Omega(Q)(k)
.

Since by
[
JP15
, Theorem 6.2.1]
each
{\rm Quot^{\ell,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L_{i}}))}(k)
is a finite set, so
\Omega(Q)(k)
is also a finite
set. Since
k
is algebraically closed, we deduce that

\dim\Omega(Q)=0.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

If the line bundles
L_{i}
are not distinct, one still can show finiteness of

\Omega(Q)
defined as the residual component of the Quot-schemes
{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({\tilde{Q}}))}
for
any subbundle
\tilde{Q}\subset Q
of degree
0
and rank
q-1
.

Remark 3.7
.

We will define in Section 7 an open subscheme
\Omega(Q)\subset{\rm Quot^{\ell q,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
for
any semi-stable vector bundle
Q
, generalizing the above
\Omega(Q)
.

4. Opers of type
q

We now recall the definition of our main object (see
[
JP15
, Definition 3.1.1]
).

Definition 4.1
.

An oper of type
q
on a smooth projective curve
X
is a triple
({V},\nabla,{V}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
, where

(1)

V
is a vector bundle of rank
r=\ell q
on
X
,

(2)

\nabla:V\to V\otimes{}^{1}_{X}
is an integrable connection on
V
,

(3)

V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
is a filtration of length
\ell
on
V

0=V_{\ell}\subset V_{\ell-1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{1}\subset V_{0}=V

such that

(a)

V_{0}/V_{1}=Q
is a vector bundle of rank
q
, and

(b)

\nabla
induces
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear isomorphisms for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1

{\rm gr}^{i}(V)\to{\rm gr}^{i-1}(V)\otimes{}^{1}_{X},

where we define
{\rm gr}^{i}(V):=V_{i}/V_{i+1}
for all
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
.

Note that our terminology suppresses the dependence on
Q
, but occasionally we may need to emphasize the dependence on
Q
and in such contexts will refer to such a triple as an oper of type
Q
.

From the definition it is immediate that one has, for all
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
, isomorphisms of bundles

(4.2)

{\rm gr}^{i}(V)\simeq Q\otimes({}^{1}_{X})^{\otimes i},

and

(4.3)

\det(V_{i})=\det(Q)^{\ell-i}\otimes({}^{1}_{X})^{q((\ell-1)+(\ell-2)+\cdots+i)}=\det(Q)^{\ell-i}\otimes({}_{X}^{1})^{\frac{q(\ell-i)(\ell+i-1)}{2}}

Note that we have the following equivalence

\deg(Q)=-q(\ell-1)(g-1)\ \iff\ \deg(V)=0.

In the next proposition we will show that opers of type
q
exist over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic.
We first recall that
\mathscr{G}_{\ell}
denotes the Gunning bundle of rank
\ell
associated to the theta-characteristic
\theta
.
We equip
\mathscr{G}_{\ell}
with any connection
\nabla_{\mathscr{G}_{\ell}}
and with its natural filtration
\mathscr{G}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}}
. Then it is well-known
that the triple
(\mathscr{G}_{\ell},\nabla_{\mathscr{G}_{\ell}},\mathscr{G}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})

is an oper (of type
1
) under the assumption
p>(\ell-1)(g-1)
, if
\mathrm{char}(k)=p>0
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
k
be any algebraically closed field. If
\mathrm{char}(k)=p>0
, we assume that
p>q(\ell-1)(g-1)
. Let
S
be a stable vector bundle on
X
with
\deg(S)=0
and
{\rm rk}(S)=q
. Then the triple

(V=\mathscr{G}_{\ell}\otimes S,\nabla,V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}=\mathscr{G}_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}\otimes S)

is an oper of type
q
for any connection
\nabla
on
\mathscr{G}_{\ell}\otimes S
.

Proof.

The proof is elementary. Let us note that the space of connections on
\mathscr{G}_{\ell}\otimes S
is non-empty, since
by stability the degree-
0
vector bundle
S
admits a connection
\nabla_{S}
, so
\nabla_{\mathscr{G}_{\ell}}\otimes\nabla_{S}
is a
connection on
\mathscr{G}_{\ell}\otimes S
. Note that, again by stability of
S
, the filtration

V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
coincides with the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
V
. The assumption on
p
implies that
no proper subbundle of the filtration
\mathscr{G}_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}\otimes S

admits a connection. These observations easily show that any connection
\nabla
on
V
is an oper connection. Finally we
note that
V_{0}/V_{1}=S\otimes\theta^{-(\ell-1)}
.
∎

We say that an oper
({V},\nabla,{V}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
of type
q
is
nilpotent
(resp.
dormant
) if the oper connection
\nabla
is nilpotent of exponent at most
{\rm rk}(V)
(resp. has
p
-curvature zero). Before proceeding further let us recall the following result of
[
JP15
, Theorem 3.1.6]
which shows that dormant opers of type
q
exist.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
Q
be any vector bundle of rank
q
. Then the triple

(F^{*}(F_{*}(Q)),\nabla^{can},F^{*}(F_{*}(Q))_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})

forms a dormant oper of type
q
. Here
\nabla^{can}
denotes the canonical connection on the vector bundle
F^{*}(F_{*}(Q))
.

The relationship between opers of type
1
and the Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
when
Q
is a line bundle
was studied in
[
JP15
]
. The main result
[
JP15
, Theorem 6.2.1 and Theorem 5.4.1]
is the
following

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume
p>r(r-1)(r-2)(g-1)
. Let
Q
be a line bundle of degree
\deg(Q)=-(r-1)(g-1)
. Then the set
of dormant opers
(V,\nabla,V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
with
V_{0}/V_{1}\simeq Q
is a non-empty, finite set, in bijection
with the set of
k
-rational points
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}(k)
.

We now give an alternative characterization of the underlying bundle
V
of an oper
(V,\nabla,V_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
of type
q
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X/k
be a smooth, projective curve over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
S
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
\theta
be a line bundle on
X
. Suppose that the following hypotheses are satisfied:

(1)

One has
\theta^{2}\simeq{}^{1}_{X}
i.e.
\theta
is a theta-characteristic on
X
.

(2)

\deg(S)=0
.

(3)

an isomorphism
\mathscr{E}nd(S)={\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules.

(4)

S
is stable.

(5)

\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)^{*}\simeq\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)
.

Then for every integer
\ell\geq 2
there exists a vector bundle
V
on
X
satisfying the following

(1)

\deg(V)=0

(2)

V
is equipped with a filtration
V=V_{0}\supset V_{1}\supset V_{2}\supset\cdots\supset V_{\ell}=0
,

(3)

{\rm gr}^{i}(V)\simeq S\otimes\theta^{2i-(\ell-1)}=:S_{i}

(4)

for all
i=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1
the extension

0\to{\rm gr}^{i+1}(V)\to V_{i}/V_{i+2}\to{\rm gr}^{i}(V)\to 0

is the unique non-split extension of this type.

Up to an isomorphism, there is only one vector bundle
V
with these properties.

Remark 4.8
.

If
{\rm rk}(Q)
is not divisible by
p
then one has an isomorphism
\mathscr{E}nd(S)={\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)
. Further one then also has

\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)^{*}\simeq\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)
. Hence in particular the hypotheses of the theorem can easily be satisfied for any stable bundle
S
of
degree zero and of rank coprime to the characteristic of the ground field. In particular these hypotheses are easily satisfied
in characteristic zero.

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\theta_{i}=\theta^{2i-(\ell-1)}
. Then for all
i\geq 1
,

\theta^{*}_{i-1}\otimes\theta_{i}\simeq{}^{1}_{X}.

Proof.

From the definition

\theta^{*}_{i-1}\otimes\theta_{i}=\theta^{-(2(i-1)-(\ell-1))+(2i-(\ell-1))}\simeq\theta^{2i-2(i-1)}\simeq\theta^{2}\simeq{}^{1}_{X}.

Hence the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For
i=0,\ldots,\ell-1
one has

Ext^{1}({S_{i-1}},{S_{i}})\simeq H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X}).

Proof.

One has

(4.11)

\displaystyle Ext^{1}({S_{i-1}},{S_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,\mathscr{H}om(S_{i-1},S_{i}))

(4.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S_{i-1}^{*}\otimes S_{i})

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S^{*}\otimes S\otimes\theta_{i-1}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i})

(4.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S^{*}\otimes S\otimes{}^{1}_{X})\text{ [by Lemma\penalty\ \ref{le:con1}]}.

Hence

(4.15)

\displaystyle Ext^{1}({S_{i-1}},{S_{i}})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S^{*}\otimes S\otimes{}^{1}_{X})

(4.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,({\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S))\otimes{}^{1}_{X})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X})\oplus H^{1}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)\otimes{}^{1}_{X}).

By Serre duality

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)\otimes{}^{1}_{X})\simeq H^{0}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)),

and as
S
is stable of degree zero (by hypothesis) so
H^{0}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S))=0
. This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

For
i=2,\ldots,\ell-1
one has

Ext^{1}({S_{i-2}},{S_{i}})=0.

Proof.

One has

(4.19)

\displaystyle Ext^{1}({S_{i-2}},{S_{i}})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,\mathscr{H}om(S_{i-2},S_{i}))

(4.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S^{*}\otimes S\otimes\theta_{i-2}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i})

(4.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,\theta_{i-2}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i}\oplus\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)\otimes\theta_{i-2}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i}).

Now

\theta_{i-2}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i}\simeq\theta^{-(2(i-2)-(\ell-1))+(2i-(\ell-1))}\simeq\theta^{-2i+4+2i}\simeq{{}^{1}_{X}}^{\otimes 2}.

Therefore

H^{1}(X,\theta_{i-2}^{*}\otimes\theta_{i})=H^{1}(X,{{}^{1}_{X}}^{\otimes 2})=0,

and

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)\otimes{{}^{1}_{X}}^{\otimes 2})\simeq H^{0}(X,\mathscr{E}nd_{0}(S)\otimes({{}^{1}_{X}})^{-1})=0

as
S
is stable, hence there are no global homomorphisms
S\rightarrow S\otimes({}^{1}_{X})^{-1}

since
\mu(S\otimes({}^{1}_{X})^{-1})<\mu(S)
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.22
.

For all
j\geq 3
one has

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{E}nd(S)\otimes\theta^{j})=0.

Proof.

This is clear by stability of
S
and Serre duality.
∎

Lemma 4.23
.

For
j_{0}>i_{0}+1
we have

Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},S_{j_{0}-1})=0.

Proof.

We have

Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},S_{j_{0}-1})\simeq H^{1}(\mathscr{E}nd(S)\otimes\theta^{2(j_{0}-i_{0})})

and as
j_{0}>i_{0}+1
so
j_{0}-i_{0}>1
. Hence this space is of the form
H^{1}(\mathscr{E}nd(S)\otimes\theta^{m})
with
m\geq 3
.
So we are done by Lemma
4.22
.
∎

Lemma 4.24
.

Suppose for some
i_{0}\geq 0
the bundle
V_{i_{0}}
has been constructed with asserted properties. Then one has

Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{j})=0\qquad\text{ for }j\geq i_{0}+1.

Proof.

Clearly the claim is true for
j=\ell
as
V_{\ell}=0
. We prove the claim by descending induction on
j
. Suppose the lemma is true for some
j_{0}
with
i_{0}+1<j_{0}\leq\ell
. Then we claim that the assertion is also true for
j_{0}-1
. As
j_{0}>i_{0}+1
, one has the exact sequence

0\to V_{j_{0}}\to V_{j_{0}-1}\to S_{j_{0}-1}\to 0.

Then applying
Hom(S_{i_{0}-1},-)
one gets

\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{j_{0}})\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{j_{0}-1})\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},S_{j_{0}-1})\to 0.

By induction hypothesis the first term is zero and by Lemma
4.23
the last term is zero. Hence the middle term is zero and the claim is proved.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.7
.

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

The construction of the bundle
V
whose existence is asserted in the theorem is inductive.
We let
V_{\ell}=0
and
V_{\ell-1}=S_{\ell-1}
. Now we define
V_{\ell-2}
as the unique non-split extension

0\to S_{\ell-1}=V_{\ell-1}\to V_{\ell-2}\to S_{\ell-2}\to 0,

which is given by the isomorphism of Lemma
4.10
:

Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-2}},{S_{\ell-1}})\simeq H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X}).

Now, if
\ell-3\geq 0
we define
V_{\ell-3}
using
V_{\ell-2}
as follows. Apply
{Hom}(S_{\ell-3},-)
to the exact sequence defining
V_{\ell-2}
. This gives

(4.25)

0\to{Hom}(S_{\ell-3},S_{\ell-1})\to{Hom}(S_{\ell-3},V_{\ell-2})\to{Hom}(S_{\ell-3},S_{\ell-2})\to\\
\to Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{S_{\ell-1}})\to Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{V_{\ell-2}})\to Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{S_{\ell-2}})\to 0.

By Lemma
4.18
we get
Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{S_{\ell-1}})=0
, and by Lemma
4.10
we get

Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{S_{\ell-2}})=H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X}).

Therefore we get

Ext^{1}({S_{\ell-3}},{V_{\ell-2}})\simeq H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X}).

So we define
V_{\ell-3}
as the unique non-split extension given by this isomorphism. Then we have by construction

0\to V_{\ell-2}\to V_{\ell-3}\to S_{\ell-3}\to 0.

Now we repeat this process to obtain the general construction. Suppose that for
some
i_{0}\geq 0
,
V_{i_{0}}
has been constructed with the asserted properties. Then one has an exact sequence

0\to V_{i_{0}+1}\to V_{i_{0}}\to S_{i_{0}}\to 0.

Then we claim that there is a unique non-split extension in

which gives
V_{i_{0}-1}
. We proceed as follows: apply
Hom(S_{i_{0}-1},-)
to the above short exact sequence to get

\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{i_{0}+1})\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{i_{0}})\to Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},S_{i_{0}})\to 0.

By Lemma
4.10
one as
Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},S_{i_{0}})\simeq H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X})
, and so to prove our claim it suffices to prove that
Ext^{1}(S_{i_{0}-1},V_{i_{0}+1})=0
. This follows from Lemma
4.24
. Repeating this process eventually one gets

as the unique non-split extension

0\to V_{1}\to V_{0}\to Q_{0}\to 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

5. Properties of the Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
when
q=1

The following proposition gives some properties on the Quot-scheme

{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
when
Q
is a line bundle. It generalizes the case
r=2
, which was already shown in
[
JP15
, Lemma 7.1.3]

and in the proof of
[
JP15
, Theorem 7.1.2]
.

We also note that there is a gap in the proof of

[
JP15
, Theorem 7.1.2]
and therefore
we shall give the proof of the following theorem with all the details.

Let
Q
be a line bundle of
degree
\deg(Q)=-(r-1)(g-1)+d
with
d\geq 0
and let
\mathscr{C}

be an irreducible component of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
. We recall from Proposition
2.4
(5) that the expected
dimension of
\mathscr{C}
is
rd
.

Definition 5.1
.

We will say that
\mathscr{C}
contains a dormant oper if there exists an effective divisor
D
of degree
d
and
a point
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-D)}))}
— hence
[E]
corresponds to a dormant oper — such that

[E]\in\mathscr{C}
under the natural inclusion

{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-D)}))}\subset{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}.

Remark 5.2
.

We note that the above definition of
\mathscr{C}
containing a dormant oper is more restrictive than the
natural one: there exists a
[E]\in\mathscr{C}
such that the triple
(F^{*}E,\nabla^{can},F^{*}E_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
, where
the filtration
F^{*}E_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
is the induced filtration from
F^{*}(F_{*}(Q))
, is an oper of type
1
.
Note that the latter definition was used in
[
JP15
]
for
r=2
. It can be checked that both
definitions coincide for small
d
.

Theorem 5.3
.

With the above notation and assuming that
p>r(r-1)(r-2)(g-1)
we have for any irreducible
component
\mathscr{C}
containing a dormant oper

(1)

\dim(\mathscr{C})=rd
.

(2)

For a general vector bundle

[E]\in\mathscr{C}
the map
f_{E}
obtained by adjunction

f_{E}:F^{*}(E)\rightarrow Q

is surjective.

Proof.

(1)
We prove the result by induction on
d
. For
d=0
this is exactly
[
JP15
, Theorem 6.2.1]
.
Assume that the statement holds for an integer
d\geq 0
and consider a line
bundle
Q
with
\deg(Q)=-(r-1)(g-1)+(d+1)
. Let
\mathscr{C}\subset{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
be an
irreducible component containing a dormant oper
[E]
, i.e. by definition
there exists an effective divisor
D
of degree
d+1
such that

E\hookrightarrow F_{*}(Q(-D))\subset F_{*}(Q)
. From now on the proof goes along the lines
of
[
JP15
, Lemma 7.1.3]
. For the convenience of the reader we include it here.

We decompose
D=x+D^{\prime}
with
x\in X

and
D^{\prime}
effective of degree
d
. Let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be an irreducible component of

{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x)}))}\cap\mathscr{C}
containing
[E]
. By induction we have
\dim(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})=rd
. Now we
claim that
{\rm codim}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}\leq r
. To prove this, note that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Since

\mathscr{C}
is an irreducible component of the Quot-scheme, it is equipped with a universal
quotient sheaf
\mathcal{Q}
over
X\times\mathscr{C}
.

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the kernel
\ker(p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q})
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
and
p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))
are
\mathscr{C}
-flat,
\mathcal{E}

is also
\mathscr{C}
-flat and
\forall c\in\mathscr{C}
the homomorphism
\mathcal{E}_{|X\times\{c\}}\rightarrow F_{*}(Q)
is injective (see e.g.
[
HL10
]
). Hence, since
F_{*}(Q)
is locally free,

\mathcal{E}_{|X\times\{c\}}
is also locally free (since torsion free over a smooth curve)
and by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
we conclude that
\mathcal{E}
is locally free over
X\times\mathscr{C}
.
Since
p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))=(F\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}})_{*}(p_{X}^{*}(Q))
we obtain by adjunction
a non-zero map
(F\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}})^{*}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow p_{X}^{*}(Q)
, hence
a non-zero global section
\sigma
of the rank-
r
vector bundle

\mathcal{V}:=\mathscr{H}om((F\times{\rm id}_{\mathscr{C}})^{*}(\mathcal{E}),p_{X}^{*}(Q))

over
X\times\mathscr{C}
. It is clear that
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x)}))}\cap\mathscr{C}
is the zero-scheme
of the section
\sigma_{|\{x\}\times\mathscr{C}}\in H^{0}(\mathscr{C},\mathcal{V}_{|\{x\}\times\mathscr{C}})
obtained after restriction.
Hence
{\rm codim}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}\leq r
and therefore
\dim(\mathscr{C})\leq rd+r
. On the other hand, by
the dimension estimates of the Quot-schemes in Proposition
2.5
(2)
we have
\dim(\mathscr{C})\geq rd+r
. Therefore
\dim(\mathscr{C})=rd+r
and we are done.

(2)

First consider the case
d=0
. Then by
[
JP15
, Theorem 5.4.1 (1)]
any
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

is an oper, which implies that
f_{E}^{*}:F^{*}(E)\rightarrow Q
is surjective.

We now assume that
d>0
. With the previous notation we denote by
\Sigma
the zero-scheme of
the global section
\sigma
of the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
and by
p_{X}
and
p_{\mathscr{C}}
projections onto
X
and
\mathscr{C}
respectively.
Thus one has a diagram

(5.4)

\textstyle{\Sigma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\times\mathscr{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{X}}
\scriptstyle{p_{\mathscr{C}}}
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}}

We have the set-theoretical equalities

\displaystyle\Sigma_{|_{\{x\}\times\mathscr{C}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x)}))}\cap\mathscr{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{E\in\mathscr{C}:f_{E}:F^{*}(E)\to Q\ {\rm\ not\ surjective\ at\ }x\right\}.

Since
\Sigma
is closed and
p_{\mathscr{C}}
is a proper map, so
\Sigma^{\prime}=p_{\mathscr{C}}(\Sigma)
is a closed subset of the irreducible component
\mathscr{C}
. So there are two possibilities:

(I)

Either
\Sigma^{\prime}\neq\mathscr{C}
, or

(II)

\Sigma^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}
.

Now suppose we are in Case
(I)
: in this case for a general
[E]\in\mathscr{C}
, one has
(X\times\{E\})\cap\Sigma=\emptyset
, which is equivalent to surjectivity of
f_{E}
. So in Case
(I)
one has surjectivity of
f_{E}
for general
[E]
as claimed.

So let us suppose we are in Case
(II)
. In this case there is at least one irreducible component of
\Sigma
, which we will again denote by
\Sigma
, which surjects onto
\mathscr{C}
under
p_{\mathscr{C}}
. Hence

\dim(\Sigma)\geq\dim(\mathscr{C})\geq rd.

Consider the restriction
{\bar{p}}_{X}:\Sigma\to X
of
p_{X}:X\times\mathscr{C}\to X
to
\Sigma
. First we assume
that
{\bar{p}}_{X}
is surjective. Since
X
is smooth and
\Sigma
is irreducible, by
[
Har77
, Chapter III, Proposition 9.7]

{\bar{p}}_{X}
is flat. Hence by
[
Har77
, Chapter III, Corollary 9.6]
any irreducible component
of the fiber
{\bar{p}}_{X}^{-1}(x)
has dimension equal to
\dim(\Sigma)-1\geq rd-1
.
By assumption
\mathscr{C}
contains a dormant oper
[E]
and as
\Sigma^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}
, there exists an
x_{0}\in X
such that

(x_{0},[E])\in\Sigma
. Since any irreducible component of
{\bar{p}}_{X}^{-1}(x_{0})
has dimension
\geq rd-1
, we see that the irreducible component of
{\bar{p}}_{X}^{-1}(x_{0})
containing
[E]
has dimension at least
rd-1
and this irreducible component is contained in
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x_{0})}))}
. But this contradicts part (1) with
d-1\geq 0
.

Finally, we need to consider the case when
{\bar{p}}_{X}
is not surjective. Since
\Sigma
is
irreducible,
{\bar{p}}_{X}(\Sigma)=\{x_{0}\}
for some point
x_{0}\in X
. Hence
\Sigma\subset{\bar{p}}_{X}^{-1}(x_{0})=\{x_{0}\}\times\mathscr{C}
, which implies that

\Sigma\subset{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x_{0})}))}\cap\mathscr{C}.

By assumption
\mathscr{C}
contains a dormant oper
[E]
, hence there exists an effective divisor
D
of degree
d\geq 1

such that
[E]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-D)}))}
. Then clearly
x_{0}\in D
and
(x_{0},[E])\in\Sigma
. We then
obtain a contradiction, since
\dim(\Sigma)\geq rd
and by part (1) the irreducible component
of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q(-x_{0})}))}
containing
[E]
has dimension

.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The gap in the proof of
[

, Theorem 7.1.2]
is related to the fact that the intersection

is not necessarily irreducible.

6. The Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
is bigger than expected
when
q\geq 2

We assume that
q={\rm rk}(Q)\geq 2
and that

-(r-q)(g-1)\leq\deg(Q)=-(r-q)(g-1)+d\leq-1,

or equivalently,

0\leq d\leq(r-q)(g-1)-1.

Note that the last inequality implies that
d<(r-1)(g-1)-1
. With this notation we
have the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
[E]\in{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
. Then we have

\dim{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}>\mathrm{exp.dim}_{[E]}\ {\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}=rd.

Proof.

By
[
JP15
, Theorem 2.3.1]
there exists a line subbundle
L\subset Q
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\deg L

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu(Q)-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)(g-1)-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)

(6.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{r}{q}+1\right)(g-1)+\frac{d}{q}-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)(g-1)-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)

(6.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}(r-1)(g-1)+\frac{d}{q}-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right).

Note that the expression on the right-hand side is not necessarily an integer.
We then introduce the quantity
\delta=\deg L+(r-1)(g-1)
. Then clearly

{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L}))}\subset{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

and by Proposition
2.5
(2)
\dim{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({L}))}\geq r\delta
. Therefore in order to
show the proposition it will be enough to show the inequality
\delta>d
.
We have

(6.5)

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg L+(r-1)(g-1),

(6.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(r-1)(g-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)+\frac{d}{q}-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right),

(6.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left[(r-1)(g-1)-1\right]+\frac{d}{q}.

Now we observe that the inequality

\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left[(r-1)(g-1)-1\right]+\frac{d}{q}>d

is equivalent to

(r-1)(g-1)-1>d,

which holds by assumption on
d
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In particular if
e(E)=0
the Quot-scheme
{\rm Quot^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
has some positive-dimensional
components, as already observed in the previous section, see also Theorem
3.5
.

7. A characterization of dormant opers of type
q

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
Q
be any vector bundle of rank
q
and
\deg(Q)=-q(\ell-1)(g-1)
. We denote by
({V},\nabla,{V}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
the
oper
(F^{*}(F_{*}(Q)),\nabla^{can},F^{*}(F_{*}(Q))_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
introduced in Theorem
4.5
.
Let
[W^{\prime}]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
and let
W=F^{*}(W^{\prime})\subset V
. Then the triple
(W,\nabla^{can},W_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
, where

\nabla^{can}
is the canonical connection on
F^{*}(W^{\prime})
and
W_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
the induced flag defined by

W_{i}=W\cap V_{i}
, is an oper of type
q
if and only if

W_{\ell}=W\cap V_{\ell}=\{0\}.

Proof.

Suppose that the triple
(W,\nabla^{can},W_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
is an oper of type
q
. By
[
JP15
, Lemma 3.4.2(i)]

we have an inclusion
W_{0}/W_{1}\subset Q
and since both vector bundles have same degree and rank, they are isomorphic.
It also follows from
[
JP15
, Lemma 3.4.2(ii)]
that
{\rm rk}(W_{\ell})=0
, hence
W_{\ell}=\{0\}
.

Conversely, assume that
W_{\ell}=\{0\}
. Then consider the sequence
r_{i}={\rm rk}(W_{i}/W_{i+1})
. So
by
[
JP15
, Lemma 3.4.2(ii)]
we have the inequalities

q\geq r_{0}\geq r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{\ell}=0.

As
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}r_{i}=q\ell
it follows that all
r_{i}=q
. Thus
{\rm rk}(W_{i}/W_{i+1})=r_{i}=q
for all
i
.

Now consider the increasing sequence of degrees
\deg(V_{i}/V_{i+1})=\deg(Q)+iq(2g-2)
for
i=0,\ldots,\ell-1
.
Since we have an injective map
W_{i}/W_{i+1}\hookrightarrow V_{i}/V_{i+1}
and both the bundles are of the same rank, we obtain

\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})\leq\deg(V_{i}/V_{i+1})
and one has

0=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\deg(V_{i}/V_{i+1}).

This gives

0=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}(\deg(Q)+iq(2g-2)).

The last sum evaluates to

0=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})\leq q(g-1)(\ell(\ell-1)-\ell(\ell-1))=0.

Thus one sees that
\deg(W_{i}/W_{i+1})=\deg(V_{i}/V_{i+1})
. Therefore the above injective maps are isomorphisms:

W_{i}/W_{i+1}\simeq V_{i}/V_{i+1}
for
i=0,\ldots,\ell-1
. This proves the assertion.
∎

One important consequence of this lemma is the following fundamental result.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
Q
be a semi-stable vector bundle with
\deg(Q)=-q(\ell-1)(g-1)
and
{\rm rk}(Q)=q
for some
integer
\ell\geq 2
.
We put
r=\ell q
and use the notation of Lemma
7.1
.

(1)

For every
[W^{\prime}]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
satisfying

W_{\ell}=W\cap V_{\ell}=\{0\}

the triple
(W=F^{*}(W^{\prime}),\nabla^{can},W_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})

is a dormant oper of type
q
.

(2)

If
p>r(r-1)(g-1)
then conversely every dormant oper
({W},\nabla,{W}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
of type
q
with
W_{0}/W_{1}\simeq Q
is of the
form
(W=F^{*}(W^{\prime}),\nabla^{can},W_{\scriptstyle{\bullet}})
for some
W^{\prime}\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is immediate from Lemma
7.1
.

Now suppose
({W},\nabla,{W}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
is a dormant oper of type
q
. Then by Cartier’s Theorem (see
[
Kat70
]
)

W=F^{*}(W^{\prime})
for some vector bundle
W^{\prime}
and as
W=F^{*}(W^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow W_{0}/W_{1}=Q
one gets by adjunction
a non-zero map
W^{\prime}\to F_{*}(Q)
. Let us first show that
W^{\prime}
is semi-stable (of degree zero).
Suppose this is not the case. Then there exists a subbundle
W^{\prime\prime}\hookrightarrow W^{\prime}

for which
\mu(W^{\prime\prime})>0
. Then in fact one has
\mu(W^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{r-1}
. But then

F^{*}(W^{\prime\prime})\hookrightarrow W
such that
\mu(F^{*}(W^{\prime\prime}))=p\mu(W^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{p}{r-1}
. On the other hand as
W

carries the structure of an oper of type
q
, and one has
\mu(W_{\ell-1})=q(2(\ell-1)-(\ell-1))(g-1)=q(\ell-1)(g-1)

and this is the destabilizing subbundle of largest degree, so
\frac{p}{r-1}\leq\mu(F^{*}(W^{\prime\prime}))\leq q(\ell-1)(g-1)
.
Hence

p<(r-1)q(\ell-1)(g-1)=(r-1)(r-q)(g-1).

On the other hand we have assumed that
p>r(r-1)(g-1)
. Therefore we have arrived at a contradiction. Thus the vector bundle
W^{\prime}
is semi-stable.

Now if
W^{\prime}
does not map injectively into
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
, then the image is a subsheaf of some degree
d\geq 1
and
rank
\leq r-1
and hence it has slope
\geq\frac{1}{r-1}
. Again by
[
JP15
, Proposition 4.2.1]
one sees that
\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
does not have any subsheaves of suitably positive degree and of rank
\leq r-1
. So
W^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathit{F}_{*}({Q})
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Using the above notation, the set of points
[W^{\prime}]\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
satisfying
W_{\ell}=\{0\}
is an open subset.

Proof.

The Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
comes equipped with a universal quotient
{\mathscr{G}}
defined over the product

X\times{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
which is flat over
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
:

(7.4)

0\to{\mathscr{W}}^{\prime}\to p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))\to{\mathscr{G}}\to 0.

Since
p_{X}^{*}(F_{*}(Q))
is locally free, it is flat over
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
and therefore for any point
y\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
the
homomorphism
{\mathscr{W}}^{\prime}|X\times\{y\}\rightarrow F_{*}(Q)
is injective. Thus
{\mathscr{W}}^{\prime}|X\times\{y\}
is locally free for any

y
, which in turn implies that
{\mathscr{W}}^{\prime}
is locally free. Hence, with the notation of Lemma
7.1
we obtain a homomorphism between locally free sheaves over

X\times{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

\Phi:F^{*}({\mathscr{W}}^{\prime})\rightarrow p_{X}^{*}(V/V_{\ell}).

By Lemma
7.1
the condition
W_{\ell}=\{0\}
, with
W=F^{*}({\mathscr{W}}^{\prime}|X\times\{y\})
,
is equivalent to
\Phi_{X\times\{y\}}
being an isomorphism. But the set of points
y\in{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}

where
\Phi_{X\times\{y\}}
is not an isomorphism is clearly a closed subset.
∎

We shall denote this open subscheme

\Omega(Q)\subset{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}.

We recall that by Theorem
7.2
the open
subscheme
\Omega(Q)

parameterizes dormant opers
({W},\nabla,{W}_{{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}})
of type
q
with
W_{0}/W_{1}\simeq Q
.

Remark 7.5
.

We observe that the Quot-scheme
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
has expected
dimension
0
if
{\rm rk}(Q)=q
,
\deg(Q)=-q(\ell-1)(g-1)
and
r=q\ell
, but in the case
q\geq 2
we have
shown in Proposition
6.1
that
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
always contains a Quot-scheme

\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}(F_{*}(L))
for some line subbundle
L\subset Q
, which has dimension
>0
.
Clearly, the Quot-scheme
\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}(F_{*}(L))
is not contained in the open subscheme

\Omega(Q)
and does not correspond to dormant opers.

Conjecture 7.6
.

Let
Q
be a semi-stable vector bundle with
\deg(Q)=-q(\ell-1)(g-1)
and
{\rm rk}(Q)=q
for some
integer
\ell\geq 2
. Put
r=q\ell
. Then, for
p>r(r-1)(r-2)(g-1)

\Omega(Q)\text{ is non-empty and of dimension}\ 0.

We now list some evidence for this conjecture:

(1)

If
q=1
, the conjecture is true. This is shown in
[
JP15
]
— see Theorem
4.6
.
Note that in this case we have equality
\Omega(Q)={{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
.

(2)

The conjecture is true for a decomposable bundle of the form
Q=\oplus_{i=1}^{q}L_{i}
, where the

are
q
distinct line bundles of degree

. This is shown in
Theorem

. We note that in this case points of

correspond
to direct sums of

.

8. Dimension of Frobenius instability loci

Throughout this section we assume that
p>r(r-1)(r-2)(g-1)
.

Let
{{\mathcal{M}}}(r)
be the coarse moduli space of semi-stable vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
0
over
X
.
Let
\mathcal{J}(r)\subset{{\mathcal{M}}}(r)
be the closed subscheme of
{{\mathcal{M}}}(r)
parameterizing semi-stable vector bundles
E
such that

F^{*}(E)
is not semi-stable. The closed subscheme
\mathcal{J}(r)
will be referred to as the Frobenius instability locus.
The purpose of this section is to make a conjecture on the dimension of
\mathcal{J}(r)
. Before stating the conjecture
we recall some facts on the structure of
\mathcal{J}(r)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)
be the moduli space of semi-stable bundles of rank
q
and degree
-1
over
X
.
Note that as we are in the coprime case any semi-stable bundle of rank
q
and degree
-1
is also stable.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be the universal bundle over
X\times{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)
. Consider the relative Quot-scheme

\pi:\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}((F\times\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)})_{*}\mathcal{U})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)

with fiber
\pi^{-1}(Q)={{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
over a stable bundle
Q\in{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)
.

For
q=1,\ldots,r-1
we denote by
\mathcal{J}_{q}\subset\mathcal{J}(r)
the closure of the forgetful map

\alpha_{q}:\mathrm{Quot}^{r,0}((F\times\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)})_{*}\mathcal{U})\longrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}}(r),\ \ [E\subset F_{*}(Q)]\rightarrow E.

Then by
[
JP15
, Theorem 4.4.1]
we have the inclusions

\mathcal{J}^{s}(r)\subset\bigcupop\displaylimits_{q=1}^{r-1}\mathcal{J}_{q}\subset\mathcal{J}(r),

where
\mathcal{J}^{s}(r)
denotes the subset of stable vector bundles
E
such that
F^{*}(E)
is not
semi-stable.

In order to compute the dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{q}
we need to know the following
dimensions

(1)

\dim{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
for general
Q\in{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)
.

(2)

\dim\alpha_{q}^{-1}(E)
for general
E\in\mathcal{J}_{q}
.

Unfortunately we only know
\dim\mathscr{C}
for certain irreducible components
\mathscr{C}\subset{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
when
q=1
.

We therefore focus on the case
q=1
. Let
Q
be a line bundle of degree
-1
and let

\mathscr{C}
be an irreducible component of
{{\rm Quot}^{r,0}(\mathit{F}_{*}({Q}))}
containing a dormant oper.
Then by Theorem
5.3

\dim\mathscr{C}=r((r-1)(g-1)-1)
and for a general
vector bundle
[E]\in\mathscr{C}
the map
f_{E}:F^{*}(E)\rightarrow Q
obtained by
adjunction is surjective. Let us denote the kernel of
f_{E}
by
S=\ker f_{E}
. Note that

\deg(S)=1
for general
[E]
.

With this notation we make the following

Conjecture 8.1
.

For a general vector bundle
E\in\mathscr{C}
the bundle
S
is semi-stable.

As a consequence of this conjecture we obtain that for a general
E\in\mathscr{C}
the
filtration
0\subset S\subset F^{*}(E)
is the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of

F^{*}(E)
. Therefore the quotient
F^{*}(E)\twoheadrightarrow Q=F^{*}(E)/S
is
uniquely determined and
\dim{\rm Hom}(E,F_{*}(Q))=1
. This means that the forgetful
map
\alpha_{1}
is generically injective on components
\mathscr{C}
containing dormant opers.

Hence if Conjecture
8.1
holds, then we have the following formula

\dim\mathcal{J}(r)\geq\dim\mathcal{J}_{1}\geq\dim{\mathcal{M}}(q,-1)+\dim\mathscr{C}=(r^{2}-r+1)(g-1)-(r-1).

Remark 8.2
.

Conjecture
8.1
holds trivially for
r=2
and the lower bound of the
dimension of
\mathcal{J}(2)
was already worked out in
[
JP15
, Theorem 7.1.2]
.

Finally, we conjecture the following structure of
\mathcal{J}_{q}
for any
q=1,\ldots,r-1
.

Conjecture 8.3
.

The Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
F^{*}(E)
of a general bundle
E\in\mathcal{J}_{q}
is
minimal, i.e., of the form

0\subset S\subset F^{*}(E),

where
\deg(S)=1
and
{\rm rk}(S)=r-q
.

Remark 8.4
.

The above two conjectures hold in the case
p=2,r=2
by
[
JRXY06
]
and for
g=2,r=3,p=3
. This is worked out in
[
Li19
]
.

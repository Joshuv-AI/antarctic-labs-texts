On the rotational invariance and hyperbolicity of shallow water moment equations in two dimensions
Matthew Bauerle, Andrew J. Christlieb, Mingchang Ding, Juntao Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the two-dimensional extension of a recently introduced set of shallow water models based on a regularized moment expansion of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations [ 21 , 20 ] . We show the rotational invariance of the proposed moment models with two different approaches. The first proof involves the split of the coefficient matrix into the conservative and non-conservative parts and proves the rotational invariance for each part, while the second one relies on the special block structure of the coefficient matrices. With the aid of rotational invariance, the analysis of the hyperbolicity for the moment model in 2D is reduced to the real diagonalizability of the coefficient matrix in 1D. Then we analyze the real diagonalizability by deriving the analytical form of the characteristic polynomial. We find that the moment model in 2D is hyperbolic in most cases and weakly hyperbolic in a degenerate edge case. With a simple modification to the coefficient matrices, we fix this weakly hyperbolicity and propose a new global hyperbolic model. Furthermore, we extend the model to include a more general class of closure relations than the original model and establish that this set of general closure relations retains both rotational invariance and hyperbolicity.

Shallow water equations; incompressible Navier-Stokes equations; moment approximation; rotational invariance; hyperbolicity

Introduction

This paper deals with the two-dimensional extension of a recently introduced set of shallow water models based on a regularized moment expansion of the incompressible Navier-Stokes (NS) equations
[
21
,
20
]
. Moment models have a long history of constructing computationally efficient representations of complex dynamics arising in a higher dimensional model. Historically, this approach has been widely used in the reduction of kinetic equations to hierarchies of moment models, where the resulting evolution equation for the
m^{th}
moment equation depends on the
(m+1)^{st}
moment
[
13
]
. At some point, one needs to restrict the expansion to
m
moments and introduce a model for the
(m+1)^{st}
moment in terms of lower moments to obtain a solvable system of equations.
This leads to the well-known moment closure problem, which is the study of what kind of model preserves the desired hyperbolic structure
[
22
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
7
]
.

In this work, we are looking at a new class of models for describing systems traditionally modeled with shallow water equations
[
27
]
and multi-layer shallow water equations
[
2
,
24
,
9
,
8
]
. In
[
21
]
, the new class of models, called the shallow water moment equations (SWME), was derived by taking moments with respect to the Legendre polynomials of the vertical direction of the three-dimensional (3D) incompressible NS equations. The zeroth order moment of the momentum equation together with the incompressibility condition yield the traditional shallow water equations.
In principle, higher-order moments offer an approach to include vertical information without introducing a mesh in the vertical direction, as one would need for the 3D NS equations. Fundamentally, this is seeking to address a multi-scale problem by providing a path to increased fidelity of the flow dynamics without needing to introduce a mesh to resolve the vertical direction. The system as originally proposed is mathematically elegant, but does not preserve hyperbolicity for higher numbers of moments in 1D and 2D. The developers of the model realized this and introduced a class of regularizations in 1D called the hyperbolic shallow water moment equations (HSWME) and proceeded to show that this regularized system was provably hyperbolic
[
20
,
18
]
. In addition, they have extended the 1D system to a variety of settings and compared depth averaged results of the incompressible 2D Navier Stokes to the regularized moment based models. In their work, they demonstrated increased fidelity when adding additional moments
[
21
,
20
]
.
More recently, there are some follow-up works, including the equilibrium stability analysis
[
18
]
, well-balanced schemes
[
19
]
, efficient time discretizations
[
1
]
, axisymmetric model
[
26
,
25
]
, multilayer-moment models
[
12
]
, and extension to sediment transport
[
11
]
.
In a very recent work
[
23
]
, the non-hydrostatic pressure and the velocity in the vertical direction were taken into consideration based on a splitting of the pressure and a polynomial expansion of the velocity and pressure. It has been demonstrated that the new reduced model retains the correct dispersive effects of the original system.
We also mention that in
[
25
]
the explicit form and the characteristic polynomial of the coefficient matrices of the HSWME in 2D are analyzed. However, the proof in
[
25
]
only shows that the eigenvalues of the two coefficient matrices in
x
and
y
directions are real but not necessarily distinct. Moreover, it is not enough to conclude that the HSWME in 2D is hyperbolic since the linear combination of the two matrices is not necessarily real diagonalizable.

In this work, our main result is establishing that the 2D extension to the regularized moment expansion of the incompressible NS equations is both rotational invariant and hyperbolic for an arbitrary number of moments. We present two different proofs of the rotational invariance. The first proof involves the split of the coefficient matrix into the conservative and non-conservative parts and proves the rotational invariance for each part, see section
3.1
. The second proof relies on the special block structure of the coefficient matrix, see section
3.2
.
With the aid of rotational invariance, the hyperbolicity in 2D reduces to the real diagonalizability of the matrix in 1D. To analyze the eigenvalues, we make use of the associated polynomial sequence and derive the characteristic polynomial of the coefficient matrix analytically. We find that the eigenvalues are related to the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points (i.e. the zeros of derivative of Legendre polynomials) and Gauss-Legendre quadrature points (i.e. the zeros of Legendre polynomials). In particular, we show that the eigenvalues of the moment system are real and distinct for arbitrary number of moments, see section
4.1.2
.
Moreover, we find that the moment model is weakly hyperbolic in a degenerate edge case, see section
4.1.1
. With a simple modification to the coefficient matrices, we fix this weakly hyperbolicity and propose a new global hyperbolic model, see section
4.2
.
More importantly, we establish the general closure relations such that the rotational invariance and hyperbolicity are guaranteed, see sections
4.4
and
4.5
. This opens the door to the development of data-driven closures that preserve hyperbolicity, as in our past work for the radiation transfer equations
[
14
,
16
,
15
]
. Data-driven closures are the subject of our future work. In addition, we conduct numerical simulations to compare the performance of different models, see section
5
.

The remaining parts of this paper are structured as follows: Section
2
introduces the models. In Section
3
, we show two proofs to the rotational invariance of the models. Section
4
analyzes the eigen structure of the models and establishes hyperbolicity of the models. In Section
5
, we show the numerical results for different models. In Section
6
, we give conclusions and talk about future directions.

2
Shallow water moment equations

In this section, we review the main ideas and the results for the derivation of the shallow water moment model in
[
21
]
. We also show the 1D hyperbolic shallow water moment model proposed in
[
20
]
.

We start by considering the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot U

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U+\nabla\cdot(UU)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p+\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\cdot{\sigma}+{g}.

Here,
U=(u,v,w)^{T}
is the velocity vector,
p
is the pressure and
\sigma
is the stress tensor. The density
\rho
is constant and
g=(e_{x},e_{y},e_{z})g
with
(e_{x},e_{y},e_{z})
a constant unit vector denotes the gravitational acceleration. The often used shallow water coordinate system is recovered by choosing
e_{x}=e_{y}=0
and
g=(0,0,-1)^{T}g
.

Under the shallowness assumption, i.e., the horizontal scales of the flow are much larger than the vertical scale, the Navier-Stokes equations (
1
) can be reduced by an asymptotic analysis to
[
21
]
:

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}v+\partial_{z}w

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}(u^{2})+\partial_{y}(uv)+\partial_{z}(uw)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{x}p+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{z}\sigma_{xz}+ge_{x},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}(uv)+\partial_{y}(v^{2})+\partial_{z}(vw)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{y}p+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{z}\sigma_{yz}+ge_{y},

where the hydrostatic pressure is given by

p(x,y,z,t)=(h_{s}(x,y,t)-z)\rho ge_{z},

with
h_{s}(x,y,t)
being the profile of the upper free surface.

To derive the shallow water moment model from (
2
), the first idea in
[
21
]
is to introduce a scaled vertical variable
\zeta(x,y,t)
given by

\zeta(x,y,t):=\frac{z-h_{b}(x,y,t)}{h_{s}(x,y,t)-h_{b}(x,y,t)}=\frac{z-h_{b}(x,y,t)}{h(x,y,t)},

with
h(x,y,t):=h_{s}(x,y,t)-h_{b}(x,y,t)
is the water height from the bottom
h_{b}(x,y,t)
to the surface
h_{s}(x,y,t)
. This transforms the
z
-direction from a physical space
z\in[h_{b},h_{s}]
to a projected space
\zeta\in[0,1]
. For any function
\psi=\psi(x,y,z,t)
, the corresponding mapped function
\tilde{\psi}=\tilde{\psi}(x,y,\zeta,t)
is given by

\tilde{\psi}(x,y,\zeta,t):=\psi(x,y,h(x,y,t)\zeta+h_{b}(x,y,t)).

The complete vertically resolved shallow flow system has the form
[
21
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+\partial_{x}(hu_{m})+\partial_{y}(hv_{m})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(h\tilde{u})+\partial_{x}(h\tilde{u}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}e_{z}h^{2})+\partial_{y}(h\tilde{u}\tilde{v})+\partial_{\zeta}(h\tilde{u}\omega-\frac{1}{\rho}\tilde{\sigma}_{xz})

\displaystyle=gh(e_{x}-e_{z}\partial_{x}h_{b}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(h\tilde{v})+\partial_{x}(h\tilde{u}\tilde{v})+\partial_{y}(h\tilde{v}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}e_{z}h^{2})+\partial_{\zeta}(h\tilde{v}\omega-\frac{1}{\rho}\tilde{\sigma}_{yz})

\displaystyle=gh(e_{y}-e_{z}\partial_{y}h_{b}),

where
u_{m}(x,y,t)=\int_{0}^{1}u(x,y,\zeta,t)d\zeta
and
v_{m}(x,y,t)=\int_{0}^{1}v(x,y,\zeta,t)d\zeta
denote the mean velocities and
\omega
is the vertical coupling

\omega=\frac{1}{h}\overline{\partial_{x}(h\tilde{u})+\partial_{y}(h\tilde{v})},

with the average for any function
\psi=\psi(\zeta)
defined by

\bar{\psi}(\zeta):=\int_{0}^{\zeta}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\psi(\check{\zeta})d\check{\zeta}-\psi(\hat{\zeta})\right)d\hat{\zeta}.

Note that, for a constant flow profile in
\zeta
, the vertical coupling coefficient
\omega
vanishes. In that case, if in addition shear stresses are negligible
\sigma_{xz}=\sigma_{yz}=0
, the system reduces to the shallow water equations.

Before deriving the moment equation, we introduce some assumptions. First, we use the Newtonian constitutive law:

\sigma_{xz}=\mu\partial_{z}u,\quad\sigma_{yz}=\mu\partial_{z}v,

where
\mu
stands for the dynamic viscosity and
\nu=\mu/\rho
the kinematic viscosity. In order to solve it, we need to specify dynamic boundary conditions in the form of a velocity boundary condition both at the free-surface, and at the bottom topography. At the free-surface, the stress-free conditions are assumed:

\partial_{z}u=\partial_{z}v=0,\quad\text{at}\quad z=h_{s}(x,y,t).

At the basal surface, the slip boundary conditions are assumed:

u-\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\sigma_{xz}=v-\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\sigma_{yz}=0,\quad\text{at}\quad z=h_{b}(x,y,t).

Here,
\lambda
stands for the slip length.

By assuming a polynomial expansion of the velocity components:

u(x,y,{\zeta},t)=u_{m}(x,y,t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}(x,y,t)\phi_{j}({\zeta}),

v(x,y,{\zeta},t)=v_{m}(x,y,t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_{j}(x,y,t)\phi_{j}({\zeta}),

with the scaled Legendre polynomials
\phi_{j}
, orthogonal on the interval
[0,1]
and normalized by
\phi_{j}(0)=1
, the shallow water moment equations (SWME) can be derived
[
21
]
:

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+\partial_{x}(hu_{m})+\partial_{y}(hv_{m})=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(hu_{m})+\partial_{x}\left(h(u_{m}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}}{2j+1})+\frac{g}{2}e_{z}h^{2}\right)+\partial_{y}\left(h(u_{m}v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}}{2j+1})\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(u_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j})+hg(e_{x}-e_{z}\partial_{x}h_{b}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(hv_{m})+\partial_{x}\left(h(u_{m}v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}}{2j+1})\right)+\partial_{y}\left(h(v_{m}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{j}^{2}}{2j+1})+\frac{g}{2}e_{z}h^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_{j})+hg(e_{y}-e_{z}\partial_{y}h_{b}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(h\alpha_{i})+\partial_{x}\left(h(2u_{m}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{ijk}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k})\right)+\partial_{y}\left(h(u_{m}\beta_{i}+v_{m}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{ijk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=u_{m}D_{i}-\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}B_{ijk}D_{j}\alpha_{k}-(2i+1)\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\left(u_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{ij})\alpha_{j}\right),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(h\beta_{i})+\partial_{x}\left(h(u_{m}\beta_{i}+v_{m}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{ijk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k})\right)+\partial_{y}\left(h(2v_{m}\beta_{i}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{ijk}\beta_{j}\beta_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=v_{m}D_{i}-\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}B_{ijk}D_{j}\beta_{k}-(2i+1)\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\left(v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{ij})\beta_{j}\right),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N.

Here the right-hand-side (RHS) contains non-conservative terms involving the expression

D_{i}:=\partial_{x}(h\alpha_{i})+\partial_{y}(h\beta_{i})

and the constants
A_{ijk}
,
B_{ijk}
,
C_{ij}
are related to the integrals of the Legendre polynomials:

\displaystyle A_{ijk}

\displaystyle=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}d\zeta,\quad i,j,k=1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle B_{ijk}

\displaystyle=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}^{\prime}\left(\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi_{j}d\hat{\zeta}\right)\phi_{k}d\zeta,\quad i,j,k=1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle C_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}^{\prime}\phi_{j}^{\prime}d\zeta,\quad i,j=1,\cdots,N.

The above system (
3
) can be written as

(4)

\partial_{t}U+A(U)\partial_{x}U+B(U)\partial_{y}U=S(U),

with the unknown variables

U=(h,\,{hu_{m}},\,{hv_{m}},\,h\alpha_{1},\,h\beta_{1},\,h\alpha_{2},\,h\beta_{2},\,\cdots,h\alpha_{N},\,h\beta_{N})^{T}.

The coefficient matrices in (
4
) can be split into the conservative and non-conservative parts:

(5)

A(U)=\partial_{U}F(U)+P(U),\quad B(U)=\partial_{U}G(U)+Q(U).

Here the physical fluxes
F(U)
and
G(U)
for the conservative parts are

(6)

\displaystyle F(U)=(h{u_{m}},

\displaystyle h({u_{m}^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}}{2j+1})+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2},\,h({u_{m}}{v_{m}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}}{2j+1}),

\displaystyle h(2{u_{m}}\alpha_{1}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{1jk}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}),\,h({u_{m}}\beta_{1}+{v_{m}}\alpha_{1}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{1jk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}),

\displaystyle\cdots,

\displaystyle h(2{u_{m}}\alpha_{n}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{njk}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}),h({u_{m}}\beta_{N}+{v_{m}}\alpha_{n}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{njk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}))^{T},

(7)

\displaystyle G(U)=(h{v_{m}},

\displaystyle h({u_{m}}{v_{m}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}}{2j+1}),\,h({v_{m}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{j}^{2}}{2j+1})+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2},

\displaystyle h({u_{m}}\beta_{1}+{v_{m}}\alpha_{1}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{1jk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}),\,h(2{v_{m}}\beta_{1}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{1jk}\beta_{j}\beta_{k}),

\displaystyle\cdots,

\displaystyle h({u_{m}}\beta_{n}+{v_{m}}\alpha_{n}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{njk}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}),\,h(2{v_{m}}\beta_{n}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{njk}\beta_{j}\beta_{k}))^{T}.

The matrices for the non-conservative part can be further decomposed into two parts:

(8)

P(U)=P_{1}(U)+P_{2}(U),\quad Q(U)=Q_{1}(U)+Q_{2}(U).

Here
P_{1}(U)
and
Q_{1}(U)
describe the terms
u_{m}D_{i}
and
v_{m}D_{i}
on the RHS of (
3
):

(9)

P_{1}(U)=\mbox{diag}(0_{3\times 3},p(U),\cdots,p(U))\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N+3)\times(2N+3)}

and

(10)

Q_{1}(U)=\mbox{diag}(0_{3\times 3},q(U),\cdots,q(U))\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N+3)\times(2N+3)}

with

(11)

p(U)=\begin{pmatrix}-{u_{m}}&0\\
-{v_{m}}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\quad q(U)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-{u_{m}}\\
0&-{v_{m}}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

The second part
P_{2}(U)
and
Q_{2}(U)
describe the terms
\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}B_{ijk}D_{j}\alpha_{k}
and
\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}B_{ijk}D_{j}\beta_{k}
on the RHS of (
3
):

(12)

P_{2}(U)=\mbox{diag}(0_{3\times 3},G(U))\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N+3)\times(2N+3)}

and

(13)

Q_{2}(U)=\mbox{diag}(0_{3\times 3},H(U))\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N+3)\times(2N+3)}

with

(14)

G(U)=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N},\quad H(U)=(h_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}

and

(15)

g_{ij}(U)=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k}B_{ijk}\alpha_{k}&0\\
\sum_{k}B_{ijk}\beta_{k}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\quad h_{ij}(U)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sum_{k}B_{ijk}\alpha_{k}\\
0&\sum_{k}B_{ijk}\beta_{k}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

The source term
S(U)
in (
4
) reads as

\displaystyle S(U)=(0,

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(u_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j})+hg(e_{x}-e_{z}\partial_{x}h_{b}),\,-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_{j})+hg(e_{y}-e_{z}\partial_{y}h_{b}),

\displaystyle-\frac{3\nu}{\lambda}(u_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{1j})\alpha_{j}),\,-\frac{3\nu}{\lambda}(v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{1j})\beta_{j}),

\displaystyle\dots,

\displaystyle-(2N+1)\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(u_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{Nj})\alpha_{j}),\,-(2N+1)\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(v_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\frac{\lambda}{h}C_{Nj})\beta_{j}))^{T}.

This system (
4
) is called the shallow water moment equations (SWME). We also remark that other models are available in
[
21
]
.

In the one-dimensional case, the SWME (
4
) with
N\geq 2
is not globally hyperbolic. In
[
20
]
, the author proposed to linearize the system matrix around linear deviations from equilibrium/constant velocity.

(16)

\partial_{t}U+A_{H}(U)\partial_{x}U+B_{H}(U)\partial_{y}U=S(U),

with

A_{H}(U):=A(h,\,hu_{m},\,hv_{m},\,h\alpha_{1},\,h\beta_{1},\,0,\,0,\,\cdots,\,0,\,0),

and

B_{H}(U):=B(h,\,hu_{m},\,hv_{m},\,h\alpha_{1},\,h\beta_{1},\,0,\,0,\,\cdots,\,0,\,0).

Keeping
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
allows to capture a large part of the structure despite its simplicity. For example, there will still be a coupling between the different higher order equations. This system is called the hyperbolic shallow water moment equations (HSWME). In 1D, it is proved to be hyperbolic in
[
20
,
18
]
. However, it remains unclear whether the HSWME (
16
) is hyperbolic in 2D.

3
Analysis of rotational invariance

In this section, we present two approaches to prove the rotational invariance of the SWME (
4
) and the HSWME (
16
). The first approach is to first show that the SWME (
4
) is indeed rotational invariant by decomposing the convection term into conservative part and non-conservative part. The second approach is to exploit the systems block structure. Motivated by the second approach, we propose the general closure relation such that the rotational invariance is satisfied.

3.1
Rotational invariance of the SWME and the HSWME

In this part, we show that the SWME (
4
) is invariant under the rotation of the coordinate system. We first introduce the definition of rotational invariance, which guarantees that the form of the system remains unchanged under a new rotated coordinate system.

Definition 3.1
(rotational invariance).

Consider the first-order system

(17)

\partial_{t}U+A(U)\partial_{x}U+B(U)\partial_{y}U=S(U,X)

with
U=(h,\,h{u_{m}},\,h{v_{m}},\,h\alpha_{1},\,h\beta_{1},\,\cdots,\,h\alpha_{N},\,h\beta_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+3}
and
X=(x,y)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is said to satisfy the rotational invariance property if

(18)

\cos\theta\,A(U)+\sin\theta\,B(U)=T^{-1}A(TU)T,

and

(19)

TS(U,X)=S(TU,T_{2}X)

for any angle
0\leq\theta<2\pi
and any vector
U
.
Here
T=T(\theta)
is the rotation matrix given by

(20)

T(\theta)=\mbox{diag}(1,T_{2}(\theta),T_{2}(\theta),\cdots,T_{2}(\theta))\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N+3)\times(2N+3)}

with

(21)

T_{2}(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

Remark 3.1
.

We remark that the first diagonal block in
T(\theta)
is 1, while the remaining diagonal blocks are the rotation matrix
T_{2}(\theta)
of size
2\times 2
. This is due to the fact that the first component of
U
, i.e., the water height, is a scalar function and the remaining components, i.e.,
(hu_{m},\,hv_{m})
and
(h\alpha_{i},\,h\beta_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
, are vector-valued functions.

Proposition 3.1
.

If the first-order system (
17
) satisfies the rotational invariance in Definition
3.1
, then the form of the system remains unchanged under a new rotated Cartesian coordinate system.

Proof.

See the proof in Appendix
A
.

We note that it is easy to show the rotational invariance (
19
) of the source term in (
4
). Therefore, we will focus on the rotational invariance (
18
) of the convection terms in this paper.
Since the coefficient matrices in the SWME (
4
) can be split into the conservative part and non-conservative part in (
5
), we will prove the rotational invariance property in two steps. Before the proof, we prepare a set of equalities used in the proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
u,v,\alpha,\beta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the rotated variables in the new coordinate system:

(22)

u_{\theta}:=\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,v,\quad v_{\theta}:=-\sin\theta\,u+\cos\theta\,v,

and

(23)

\alpha_{\theta}:=\cos\theta\,\alpha+\sin\theta\,\beta,\quad\beta_{\theta}:=-\sin\theta\,\alpha+\cos\theta\,\beta.

Then the following equalities hold true:

(24)

\cos\theta\,u_{\theta}-\sin\theta\,v_{\theta}=u,

(25)

\sin\theta\,u_{\theta}+\cos\theta\,v_{\theta}=v,

(26)

\cos\theta\,(u_{\theta})^{2}-\sin\theta\,u_{\theta}v_{\theta}=uu_{\theta},

(27)

\sin\theta\,(u_{\theta})^{2}+\cos\theta\,u_{\theta}v_{\theta}=vu_{\theta},

(28)

2\cos\theta\,u_{\theta}\alpha_{\theta}-\sin\theta\,\left(u_{\theta}\beta_{\theta}+v_{\theta}\alpha_{\theta}\right)=2\cos\theta\,u\alpha+\sin\theta\,(u\beta+v\alpha),

(29)

3.3
General closure relation with the rotational invaiance

From the above alternative proof, we observe that the rotational invariance of the HSWME relies on the rotational invariance of each sub-block (of size
2\times 2
) of the coefficient matrices.
Motivated by this observation, we would like to analyze the rotational invariance of matrices of size
2\times 2
and find out some general relations which satisfy the rotational
invariance. We note that this will be key in deriving our new model, which has a more general closure relation than that of HSWME.

Definition 3.2
(rotational invariance of
2\times 2
matrices).

Consider two matrices
A(V)
and
B(V)
of size
2\times 2
given by

A(V)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}(V)&a_{12}(V)\\
a_{21}(V)&a_{22}(V)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

and

B(V)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}(V)&b_{12}(V)\\
b_{21}(V)&b_{22}(V)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

with
V=(p,\,q)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We say that
A(V)
and
B(V)
satisfy the rotational invariance if

(38)

T_{2}^{-1}A(T_{2}V)T_{2}=\cos\theta\,A(V)+\sin\theta\,B(V),

holds true for any
0\leq\theta<2\pi
and any
V\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
T_{2}
being the rotational matrix

T_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.

Note that, in the definition, the dummy variables
(p,q)
could be
({u_{m}},{v_{m}})
or
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
in the shallow water moment model.
In the following part, we will derive some conditions for the rotational invariance of
2\times 2
matrices to be satisfied. We first present several necessary conditions:

Before the discussion, we review important properties of the Hessenberg matrix. These properties facilitate directly relating the eigenvalues of a Hessenberg matrix to the roots of some associated polynomial. We start with the definitions of the (unreduced) lower Hessenberg matrix and the associated polynomial sequence

Definition 4.1
(lower Hessenberg matrix).

The matrix

is called lower Hessenberg matrix if
a_{ij}=0
for
j>i+1
. It is called unreduced lower Hessenberg matrix if further
a_{i,i+1}\neq 0
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
.

Definition 4.2
(associated polynomial sequence
[
6
]
).

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
be an unreduced lower Hessenberg matrix. The associated polynomial sequence
\{q_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
is defined as follows:

(60)

\displaystyle q_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle q_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{i,i+1}}\left(xq_{i-1}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{ij}q_{j-1}(x)\right),\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

with
a_{n,n+1}:=1
.

Theorem 4.3
(
[
6
]
).

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
be an unreduced lower Hessenberg matrix and
\{q_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
is the associated polynomial sequence with

. The following conclusions hold true:

1.

If
\lambda
is a root of

, then
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the matrix

and a corresponding eigenvector is
(q_{0}(\lambda),q_{1}(\lambda),\cdots,q_{n-1}(\lambda))^{T}
.

If all the roots of

are simple, then the characteristic polynomial of

is given by

\det(xI-A)=\rho q_{n}(x),

with

.

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle q_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{3(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-3gh+\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}},

Here

with the standardization condition

Proof.

For convenience, we first introduce the notations:

\xi:=\frac{x-u_{m}}{\alpha_{1}},

and

p_{g}(x):=(x-u_{m})^{2}-gh-\alpha_{1}^{2}.

The first several associated polynomials can be obtained by direct computation:

q_{0}(x)=1.

q_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{a_{12}}(xq_{0}(x)-a_{11}q_{0}(x))=\frac{1}{1}(x-0)=x.

\displaystyle q_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{23}}(xq_{1}(x)-(a_{21}q_{0}(x)+a_{22}q_{1}(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}}\left(x^{2}-(gh-u_{m}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{1}^{2}+2{u_{m}}x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-3gh+\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}}.

\displaystyle q_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{34}}\left(xq_{2}(x)-(a_{31}q_{0}(x)+a_{32}q_{1}(x)+a_{33}q_{2}(x))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{3}{5}\alpha_{1}}\left(x\frac{3(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-3gh+\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}}-(-2{u_{m}}\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{1}x+{u_{m}}\frac{3(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-3gh+\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{5(x-{u_{m}})((x-{u_{m}})^{2}-gh-\alpha_{1}^{2})}{2\alpha_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{5\xi}{2\alpha_{1}}p_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{6\alpha_{1}}P_{2}^{\prime}(\xi)p_{g}(x).

\displaystyle q_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{45}}(xq_{3}(x)-(a_{41}q_{0}(x)+a_{42}q_{1}(x)+a_{43}q_{2}(x)+a_{44}q_{3}(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{4}{7}\alpha_{1}}\left((x-{u_{m}})\frac{5\xi}{2\alpha_{1}}p_{g}(x)-(-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}^{2}+0+\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{1}\frac{3(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-3gh+\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{7(5(x-{u_{m}})^{2}-\alpha_{1}^{2})((x-{u_{m}})^{2}-gh-\alpha_{1}^{2})}{8\alpha_{1}^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{7(5\xi^{2}-1)}{8\alpha_{1}}p_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{12\alpha_{1}}P_{3}^{\prime}(x)p_{g}(x).

Now we prove by induction and assume that (

\displaystyle q_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2k+1}{(k+1)\alpha_{1}}\left(\frac{2k-1}{k(k-1)}\xi P^{\prime}_{k-1}(\xi)-\frac{1}{k-1}P^{\prime}_{k-2}(\xi)\right)p_{g}(x)

where we use the relation

Lastly, we compute

and

We notice that in the analysis of in section

. However, it is easy to see that

.

In the above procedure, we take the simple choice to keep

4.4
A framework for constructing general closure relations with rotational invariance and hyperbolicity

Besides the previous hyperbolic shallow water moment models, we can also modify both
\tilde{A}_{11}
and
\tilde{A}_{22}
in (
51
), as long as each of them has real and distinct eigenvalues and they have no common eigenvalues. In this case, the real diagonalizability of the matrix
\tilde{A}
in (
51
) is guaranteed. Here, we can borrow the idea from
[
20
]
to modify the entries in the last row of the matrix such that the modified matrix has predefined propagation speeds. Since only the last row is modified, the associated polynomial sequence remains unchanged except for the last one. Therefore, it is easy to derive the analytical form of the characteristic polynomial where the coefficients have a linear dependence on the entries in the last row. Next, by matching the coefficients using Vieta’s formulas which relate the coefficients of a polynomial to sums and products of its roots, or using the appropriate recurrence relation, the entries in the last row can be solved analytically. Similar ideas are also applied in machine learning moment closures for radiative transfer equation
[
15
]
where the roots are represented by the neural networks.

After the modified
\tilde{A}_{11}
and
\tilde{A}_{22}
are obtained, the next step is to determine the form of
\tilde{A}_{21}
by the rotational invariance constraint in Theorem
3.3
. The coefficient matrix
B
in the
y
direction can be derived by this constraint as well. Then we have a moment model in 2D with provable rotational invariance and hyperbolicity.

We note that the corner case of the weakly hyperbolicity with
\alpha_{1}=0
should be addressed separately in this framework. The algebraic multiplicities of the eigenvalue should be equal to the geometric multiplicities and this has to be checked by analyzing the eigenvectors of the coefficient matrix, as shown in section
4.1.1
.

6
Concluding remarks

In this work, we investigate the rotational invariance and hyperbolicity of the shallow water moment equations in 2D. We find that the moment model in 2D is hyperbolic in most cases and weakly hyperbolic in a degenerate edge case. With a simple modification to the coefficient matrices, we fix this weakly hyperbolicity and propose a new global hyperbolic model. We establish a general closure relation such that the resulting shallow water moment equations are hyperbolic and rotationally invariant. Moreover, we propose a new class of shallow water moment equations, which is a generalization of the HSWME model. We conduct numerical simulations for these SWME models and compare with the SWE model. It shows that the model generates different solution profiles with different numbers of moments. We also mention that the new class of shallow water moment equations is only proven to be hyperbolic up to
N=100
. Proving that the model is hyperbolic for an arbitrary number of moments remains an open question, i.e.,
N>100
.

In future work, it remains to be seen whether the derived shallow water moment models are good approximations of the original incompressible NS equations both numerically and theoretically.
Another interesting direction is to apply data-driven methods to learn the closure relations while preserving the rotational invariance and hyperbolicity, as we have done in
[
17
,
14
,
16
,
15
]
.
In addition, the analysis in the current paper could not be easily generalized to the Shallow Water Linearized Moment Equations in
[
19
]
due to the different structure of the Jacobian matrix of the model.
These topics are the subject of our ongoing work.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
3.1

We first introduce the following lemma, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma A.1
.

If the matrices
A(U)
and
B(U)
satisfy the rotational invariance property

(87)

\cos\theta\,A(U)+\sin\theta\,B(U)=T^{-1}A(TU)T,\quad\forall\,\,0\leq\theta<2\pi,

then they also satisfy

(88)

-\sin\theta\,A(U)+\cos\theta\,B(U)=T^{-1}B(TU)T,\quad\forall\,\,0\leq\theta<2\pi.

Here
T=T(\theta)
is the rotation matrix defined in (
20
).

Proof.

Taking
\theta
to be
-\theta
in (
87
), we have

(89)

\cos\theta\,A(U)-\sin\theta\,B(U)=T(\theta)A(T^{-1}(\theta)U)T^{-1}(\theta),

where we use the fact that
T^{-1}(\theta)=T(-\theta)
.
Taking
U
to be
T(\theta)U
in (
89
), we have

(90)

\cos\theta\,A(T(\theta)U)-\sin\theta\,B(T(\theta)U)=T(\theta)A(U)T^{-1}(\theta).

Multiplying both sides of (
90
) by
T^{-1}(\theta)
from the left and by
T(\theta)
from the right, we obtain

(91)

\cos\theta\,T^{-1}(\theta)\,A(T(\theta)U)\,T(\theta)-\sin\theta\,T^{-1}(\theta)\,B(T(\theta)U)\,T(\theta)=A(U).

Substituing (
87
) to the first term on the left-hand side of (
91
), we obtain

\cos\theta\,(\cos\theta\,A(U)+\sin\theta\,B(U))-\sin\theta\,T^{-1}(\theta)\,B(T(\theta)U)\,T(\theta)=A(U),

which is equivalent to

-\sin^{2}\theta\,A(U)+\cos\theta\sin\theta\,B(U)=\sin\theta\,T^{-1}(\theta)\,B(T(\theta)U)\,T(\theta).

Under the assumption that
\sin\theta\neq 0
, we can divide both sides of the above equation by
\sin\theta
and obtain (
88
).

Therefore, it suffices to prove the case of
\sin\theta=0
, i.e.,
\theta=0
or
\theta=\pi
. It is obvious that (
88
) holds for
\theta=0
. Next, we only need to prove the case of
\theta=\pi
, in which (
88
) is reduced to

-B(U)=T^{-1}(\pi)\,B(T(\pi)U)\,T(\pi).

Taking
\theta
to be
\pi
in (
87
), we have

(92)

-A(U)=T^{-1}(\pi)\,A(T(\pi)U)\,T(\pi).

Taking
\theta
to be
\frac{\pi}{2}
in (
87
), we have

(93)

B(U)=T^{-1}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\,A\left(T\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)U\right)\,T\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right).

Therefore, we compute

\displaystyle T^{-1}(\pi)\,B(T(\pi)U)\,T(\pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eq:rotation-invariance-property-1-pi-2}}}}{{=}}{}

\displaystyle T^{-1}(\pi)\,T^{-1}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\,A\left(T\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)T(\pi)U\right)\,T\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\,T(\pi)

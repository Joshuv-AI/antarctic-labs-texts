Background

Definition 2.1

(Eisenstein integers)
.

The set
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]=\{a+b\omega\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\}
, under the usual operations of addition and multiplication of complex numbers, is the ring of Eisenstein integers, where
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
.

Figure 1.
The Eisenstein integers form a triangular lattice over the complex plane

Throughout this paper, it might be helpful for the reader to visualize the Eisenstein integers as a subset of the complex plane. See figure
1
. Like the complex plane is partitioned symmetrically into four quadrants, the Eisenstein integers is symmetrically and radially partitioned into six sextants. Each sextant is defined as follows.

(1)

First sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid 0\leq\Arg(\eta)<\frac{\pi}{3}\}

(2)

Second sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid\frac{\pi}{3}\leq\Arg(\eta)<\frac{2\pi}{3}\}

(3)

Third sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid\frac{2\pi}{3}\leq\Arg(\eta)<\pi\}

(4)

Fourth sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid-\pi<\Arg(\eta)<-\frac{2\pi}{3}\text{ or }\Arg(\eta)=\pi\}

(5)

Fifth sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid-\frac{2\pi}{3}\leq\Arg(\eta)<-\frac{\pi}{3}\}

(6)

Sixth sextant:
\{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\mid-\frac{\pi}{3}\leq\Arg(\eta)<0\}

The Eisenstein integers are endowed with a Euclidean function
N
and which we will call the norm. It is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2

(Norm function)
.

N:\mathbb{Z[\omega]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is defined by the equation
N(a+\omega b)=|a+\omega b|^{2}=(a+\omega b)\overline{(a+\omega b)}=a^{2}-ab+b^{2}=(a-b)^{2}-ab
is the norm function in
\mathbb{Z[\omega]}

Remark 2.3
.

Equipped with this norm, the ring of Eisenstein integers is a Euclidean domain and thus a unique factorization domain.

Proposition 2.4
.

N
is completely multiplicative.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=a+\omega b
and let
\beta=c+\omega d
. Then,
\alpha\cdot\beta=ac-bd+(ad+bc-bd)\omega
and

(2.1)

\begin{split}N(\alpha\cdot\beta)&=(ac-bd)^{2}-(ac-bd)(ad+bc-bd)+(ad+bc-bd)^{2}\\
&=(a^{2}-ab+b^{2})(c^{2}-cd+d^{2})=N(\alpha)N(\beta)\end{split}

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The units of
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
are
\pm 1,\pm\omega,\text{ and }\pm(1+\omega)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\eta
has a multiplicative inverse. Then,
N(\eta)N(\eta^{-1})=N(\eta\eta^{-1})=N(1)=1
. Write
\eta=a+\omega b
. Then we have
N(\eta)=(a-b)^{2}+ab=1
. One can check that the only solutions to this equation are:
(\pm 1,0)

(\pm 1,\pm 1)

(0,\pm 1)

∎

\epsilon

Proposition 2.16

Proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

If

, then

(3.3)

N(1+z+...+z^{k})>N(z^{k-1})(N(z)+2x-1)

Moreover, if
|y|\leq x-1
, then

(3.4)

N(1+...+z^{k})\geq N(z^{k-1})(N(z)+2x+1)

with equality if and only if
k=1
.

Proof.

If
k=1
,

(3.5)

N(1+z)=N(z)+2x+1

If
k=2
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}N(1+z+z^{2})&=N(z)N(z^{-1}+1+z)\\
&=N(z)\Big(N(z)+2x+1+\frac{2x+1+2(x^{2}-y^{2})}{N(z)}\Big)\\
&>\begin{cases}N(z)(N(z)+2x-1)\text{ for all }y\\
N(z)(N(z)+2x+1)\text{ for all }y\text{ such that }|y|\leq x\end{cases}\end{split}

Let
z=x+iy=re^{i\theta}
. If
k\geq 3
,

(3.7)

\begin{split}N(1+z+...+z^{k})&=N\Big(\frac{z^{k+1}-1}{z-1}\Big)=\frac{(z^{k+1}-1)(\overline{z}^{k+1}-1)}{(z-1)(\overline{z}-1)}\\
&=\frac{N(z^{k+1})+1-(z^{k+1}+\overline{z}^{k+1})}{r^{2}-x+1}\\
&=\frac{N(z^{k-1})(r^{4}+r^{-2(k+1)}-2r^{3-k}\cos{(k+1)\theta}}{r^{2}-2x+1}\\
&>\frac{N(z^{k-1})(r^{4}-2)}{r^{2}-2x+1}\end{split}

Since

,

(3.8)

(r^{2}+2x-1)(r^{2}-2x+1)=r^{4}-(2x-1)^{2}<r^{4}-2

Hence,
N(1+...+z^{k})>N(z^{k-1})(N(z)+2x-1)
. If also
|y|\leq x-1
, then

(3.9)

\begin{split}(r^{2}+2x+1)(r^{2}-2x+1)&=(r^{2}+1)^{2}-4x^{2}=r^{4}-2(x^{2}-y^{2})+1\\
&\leq r^{4}-(4x-3)\leq r^{4}-2\end{split}

Hence
N(1+z+...+z^{k})>N(z^{k-1})(N(z)+2x+1)

3^{n}N(1-\epsilon\tau^{mn-n})=N(1-\epsilon)

. By the same argument,

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

,
\delta

Proof.

By lemma
3.7

for some

\tau

We are left to show that for

M_{p}
prime,
\eta=\epsilon\tau^{p-1}M_{p}^{t}\delta

\eta=\epsilon\tau^{p-1}\overline{M_{p}}^{t}\delta

\eta=\epsilon\tau^{p-1}M_{p}^{t}\delta

\tau
-norm-perfect; and that, for

M_{p}

\eta=\epsilon\tau^{p-1}\overline{M_{p}}^{t}\delta

\tau
-norm-perfect.

Consider
\eta=\tau M_{2}^{t}\delta
.
M_{2}=\sigma(\tau)=1+\tau=3+\omega
.
M_{2}^{*}=M_{2}

(3.24)

\begin{split}\frac{N(\sigma(\eta))}{N(\tau\eta)}&=\frac{N(\sigma(\tau))}{N(\tau)^{2}}\frac{N(\sigma(M_{2}^{t}))}{N(M_{2}^{t})}\frac{N(\sigma(\delta))}{N(\delta)}\geq\frac{N(\sigma(\tau))}{N(\tau)^{2}}\frac{N(\sigma(M_{2}^{t}))}{N(M_{2}^{t})}\\

Let

M_{p}
prime and

By the same argument we also have that

\frac{N(\sigma(\overline{M_{p}^{t}}))}{N(\overline{M_{p}^{t}})}\geq\frac{N(\sigma(\overline{M_{p}}))}{N(\overline{M_{p}})}

Suppose that
\eta
is
\tau
-norm-perfect number, then, by lemma
3.9

4.

2
-norm-perfect Eisenstein Integers

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
. If
\eta=2^{k-1}\mu
is
2
-norm-perfect, then
\sigma(2^{k-1})
is prime.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a first-sextant prime factor of
\sigma(2^{k-1})
with
\R\pi\geq 2
. Let
a
be the largest rational integer such that
\pi^{a}|\sigma(2^{k-1})
.

(4.1)

2^{2}\eta\overline{\eta}=N(2\eta)=N(\sigma(2^{k-1})\sigma(\mu))=\pi\overline{\pi}N\Big(\frac{\sigma(2^{k-1})}{\pi}\sigma(\mu)\Big)

Thus it follows that
\pi|2^{2}\eta\overline{\eta}
. Since
(\pi,2)=1
, it follows that
\pi|\eta\overline{\eta}
. Since
\pi
is prime.
\pi|\eta
or
\overline{\pi}|\eta
. As shown in the proof of lemma
3.7
,

(4.2)

N(\pi)>\frac{3(2^{k}-1)^{2}}{2^{2k}-(2^{k}-1)^{2}}=\frac{3(2^{k}-1)^{2}}{2^{k+1}-1}

Since

(4.3)

\frac{3(2^{k}-1)}{2^{k+1}-1}>1

it follows that

(4.4)

N(\pi)>\sigma(p^{k-1})

If
\sigma(p^{k-1})
is composite and write
\sigma(p^{k-1})=\pi_{0}\pi_{1}...\pi_{r}
where
\pi_{0}
is a prime with the least norm among the norm of all prime factors of
\sigma(p^{k-1})
, then

(4.5)

N(\pi_{0})>N(\pi_{1})...N(\pi_{r})

This is a contradiction. Thus,
\sigma(p^{k-1})
is prime.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

There are no

-norm-perfect Eisenstein integers divisible by

Proof.

By proposition

,

5.
Discussion

In regard to future work, we are interested in studying
\tau
-norm-perfect and
\tau
-perfect numbers for other values of
\tau
, and in studying
\tau
-norm-perfect and
\tau
-perfect numbers that are not divisible by
\tau
for
\tau=2
and
\tau=\omega+2
. Thus far, in the Gaussian and in the Eisenstein integers, there are only characterizations for
\tau
-norm-perfect and
\tau
-perfect numbers that are also divisible by
\tau
.

In the Eisenstein integers, two potentially promising start points are to attempt to characterize or prove nonexistence of
\tau
-norm-perfect integers for
\tau=\omega+3
or
2\omega+3
as these are the first-sextant primes that follow
\omega+2
and
2
in norm. Alternaively, rational primes

may be studied. We also would like to remark that many of our computations become infeasible after certain modifitication. In particular, computing
\sigma(\eta^{k})
for nonprime
\eta
and general
k\in\mathbb{N}
usually turns to be a cumbersome task.

A Combinatorial Approach to Frobenius Numbers of Some Special Sequences (Complete Version) Thanks: ∗ * This work was partially supported by NSFC(12071311).
Feihu Liu 1 and Guoce Xin 2,∗

Abstract
. Let A = ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a n ) A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}) be relative prime positive integers with a i ≥ 2 a_{i}\geq 2 . The Frobenius number g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) is the greatest integer not belonging to the set { ∑ i = 1 n a i ​ x i | x i ∈ ℕ } \big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\ |x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\big\} . The general Frobenius problem includes the determination of g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) and the related Sylvester number n ⁡ ( A ) n(A) and Sylvester sum s ⁡ ( A ) s(A) . We present a new approach to the Frobenius problem. Basically, we transform the problem into an easier optimization problem. If the new problem can be solved explicitly, then we will be able to obtain a formula of g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) . We illustrate the idea by giving concise proof of some existing formulas and finding some interesting new formulas of g ⁡ ( A ) , n ⁡ ( A ) , s ⁡ ( A ) g(A),n(A),s(A) . Moreover, we find that MacMahon’s partition analysis applies to give a new way of calculating n ⁡ ( A ) , s ⁡ ( A ) n(A),s(A) by using a rational function representation of a polynomial determined by A A .

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we shall always use the following standard notation:
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of all integers,
\mathbb{N}
is the set of all non-negative integers, and
\mathbb{P}
is the set of all positive integers.

Let
A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})
be a positive integer vector. Sylvester
[
22
]
defined the function
d(a_{0};A)
, called the denumerant of
a_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with respect to
A
, by

d(a_{0};A)=d(a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})=\#\Big\{(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=a_{0}\Big\}.

That is,
d(a_{0};A)
is the number of non-negative integer representations of
a_{0}
by
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
.
We say
a_{0}
is
representable
by
A
if
d(a_{0};A)>0
and
not representable
by
A
if otherwise.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(A)
the set of numbers representable by
A
, and by
\mathcal{NR}=\mathcal{NR}(A)
the set of numbers not representable by
A
.

When
\gcd(A)=1
, it is not hard to see that
\mathcal{NR}(A)
is finite, i.e., any sufficiently large
a_{0}
is representable by
A
. Then a natural question is: what is

g(A)=\max\mathcal{NR}(A)=\max\{a_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\mid d(a_{0};A)=0\},

i.e., the maximum number not representable by
A
? This number is now called the Frobenius number of
A
since it was first studied by Frobenius.
The Frobenius problem is also known as the Coin Exchange Problem. It refers to two
problems: compute the Frobenius number
g(A)
; for a given
m
, determine if
m
is representable by
A
, and find a representation if possible.

The determination of
g(A)
turns out to be a very hard problem even when
n
is fixed.
For
n=2
, it is well known that
g(a_{1},a_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}
. This case was studied as early as in 1882 by Sylvester
[
23
]
.
For
n=3
, a formula related to rational function appears in
[
5
]
. Amitabha Tripathi also discussed it in
[
29
]
. For
n=4
, no general formula for
g(A)
is known, but many formulas for special cases have been determined.

Algorithmically, the determination of
g(A)
was shown by Ramírez Alfonsín
[
18
]
to be NP-hard under Turing reduction. Thus
an algorithm for the fixed
n
case is valuable. A theoretical solution was given by Kannan
[
10
]
, who use the concept of covering radius
to gave a polynomial time algorithm for
g(A)
when
n
is fixed. But the algorithm is impractical due to its high complexity. Many algorithms have been developed
for the small
n
case. For
n=3
, H. Greenberg provides a fast algorithm with runtime
O(\log a_{1})
in
[
8
]
. D. Einstein, D. Lichtblau, A. Strzebonski and S. Wagon
[
7
]
gave a fast (but not polynomial) algorithm for computing the Frobenius number
g(A)
for up to
n=11
parameters.

We start to study the Frobenius problem because of the following known result lie in the conjunction of Algebraic Combinatorics and Computational Geometry.
The denumerant
d(a_{0};A)
can be computed in polynomial time when
n
is fixed. This problem can be solved by: i) Barvinok’s algorithm for counting lattice points in a rational convex polytope, with a practical implementation by LattE in
[
17
]
; ii) the constant term method in
[
32
]
, where a polynomial time algorithm was described and a simple (but not polynomial time) implementation by CTEuclid. We believe that the tools developed there can also be applied to the quite related
g(A)
.

The method we are going to present arises from trying to give simple proofs of some known results. For example: Hujter
[
9
]
gave the formula
g(A)
for
A=(a,a^{2}+1,a^{2}+a),a>2
; Dulmage and Mendelsohn
[
6
]
obtained some formulas for
A=(a,a+1,a+2,a+4)
,
A=(a,a+1,a+2,a+5)
,
A=(a,a+1,a+2,a+6)
; For
A=(a,ha+d,ha+bd,ha+b^{2}d,...,ha+b^{k}d)
is discussed in
[
28
]
by A. Tripathi. For more formulas, see
[
19
]
.

The case

A=(a,a+1,...,a+k)
is our break point. Brauer
[
2
]
first computed
g(A)
for this case, Roberts
[
20
]
extended this result to numbers in arithmetic progression. For
g(A)
and
n(A)
, Selmer
[
24
]
further generalized to the case
A=(a,ha+d,ha+2d,...,ha+kd),\ (a,d)=1
.
Our proof (of Theorem
4.1
) is essentially equivalent to that in
[
27
]
. But our treatment is neat, and easy to generalize. This leads to a method
for Forbenius problems.

We follow some notations in
[
7
]
. By the fundamental result in Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to determine
N_{r}=\min\{a_{0}\pmod{a_{1}}=r:a_{0}\in\mathcal{R}\}
for
0\leq r\leq a_{1}-1
. Now consider the case
A=(a,ha+dB)=(a,ha+db_{1},ha+db_{2},...,ha+db_{k})
where
B=(b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k})
. We reduce the computation of
N_{r}
to solving a much simpler optimization problem
O_{B}(M)
with parameter
M
. For some special sequences, these optimization problems
O_{B}(M)
are easy to solve, and we may further analysis to obtain desired formulas of
g(A)
.
This approach succeeds for all the above displayed cases. It is also used to prove a conjecture about
g(A)
for the square sequence
A=(a,a+1^{2},a+2^{2},...,a+k^{2})
in
[
16
]
. That paper also studies power sequence and prime sequence, but the resolution of
g(A)
needs the support of Number Theory.

In some special cases, our approach can give explicit formulas of
N_{r}
. Such formulas can also be used to study
g(A)
and some other statistics. For instance,
the
Frobenius number

g(A)=\max\mathcal{NR}
, the
Sylvester number

n(A)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{NR}}1
, the
Sylvester sum

s(A)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{NR}}n
, and the
Sylvester power sum

s_{\mu}(A)=\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{NR}}(A)}n^{\mu}
, which was recently introduced by Takao Komatsu. See
[
11
,
12
,
13
]

and its references.

It should be pointed out that
g(A),n(A),s(A)
and
s_{\mu}(A)
are all related to
N_{r}
, especially to
\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}^{p},p\geq 1
. This article mainly focuses on
g(A),n(A)
and
s(A)
. Basically if we obtain exact formulas for
N_{r}
, then we can compute their power sums. But the formulas become complicated for symbolic
a
. We find the constant term method applies if
N_{r}
is a piecewise linear function about
r
. In such a situation, MacMahon’s partition analysis can be used to write the generating function
f(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}x^{N_{r}}
as a short sum of simple Elliott rational functions. Then the formula
(x\frac{d}{dx})^{p}f(x)|_{x=1}
combined with constant term extractions can be used to give a systematical approach to the computation of
\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}^{p},p\geq 1
.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 1 is this introduction.
In Section 2, we will use A. Brauer and J. E. Shockley
[
3
]
, E. S. Selmer
[
24
]
and A. Tripathi’s conclusion
[
26
]
to get crude formulas of
g(A),n(A),s(A)
about
N_{r}
. When
A=(a,a+B)
, we transform
N_{r}
into a much simpler optimization problem
O_{B}(M)
.
In Section 3, we solve the optimization problem
O_{B}(M)
to obtain the
g(A),n(A),s(A)
for some special short sequences including
A=(a^{2},a^{2}+1,a^{2}+a,a^{2}+a+1)
, which is a hard instance in
[
7
]
. Meanwhile, we will give concise proofs of many known formulas. This illustrates the wide application of our method.
In Section 4, we use the same method to calculate some formulas
g(A),n(A),s(A)
for long sequences, such as
A=(a,ha+d,ha+3d,ha+5d,...,ha+(2k+1)d)
, etc.
In Section 5, we describe how to use the constant term method to calculate
n(A)
,
s(A)
and
s_{\mu}(A)
.

2.
A Reduction to a Minimization Problem

This paper focus on the computation of
g(A),n(A)
and
s(A)
for some special
A
.
A. Brauer and J. E. Shockley
[
3
]
, E. S. Selmer
[
24
]
, A. Tripathi
[
26
]
and T. Komatsu
[
11
]
gave the following results respectively, which are widely used. It is convenient to use the short hand notation

A:=(a,B)=(a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k})
,
\gcd(A)=1
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
A:=(a,B)=(a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k})
,
\gcd(A)=1
and

N_{r}:=N_{r}(a,B)=\min\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv r\mod a,\ d(a_{0};B)>0\}.

Then the Frobenius number, Sylvester number, Sylvester sum and Sylvester power sum are respectively:

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=g(a,B)=\max_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}N_{r}-a,

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=n(a,B)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}-\frac{a-1}{2},

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=s(a,B)=\frac{1}{2a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}+\frac{a^{2}-1}{12},

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(A)

\displaystyle=s_{\mu}(a,B)=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu+1}{\kappa}\mathcal{B}_{\kappa}a^{\kappa-1}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}^{\mu+1-\kappa}+\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}(a^{\mu+1}-1),

where
\mu
is a positive integer,
\mathcal{B}_{\kappa}
is the Bernoulli number.

Note that
N_{0}=0
for all
A
, so we need only consider
N_{r}
for
1\leq r\leq a-1
. Also note that
N_{r}=N_{r+ka}
, so in the formula, we can let
r
ranges over any set whose remainders are
\{0,1,\dots,a-1\}
. More precisely, we will use the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A=(a,b_{1},...,b_{k})
,
\gcd(A)=1
,
\gcd(a,d)=1,\ d\in\mathbb{P}
. Then we have

\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\}=\{N_{d\cdot 0},N_{d\cdot 1},N_{d\cdot 2},...,N_{d\cdot(a-1)}\}.

(1)

Proof.

When
r
takes one of the remaining classes of
a
, so does
dr
.
∎

We study the case
A=(a,ha+dB)=(a,ha+db_{1},...,ha+db_{k})
. We find

g(a,ha+dB)
is closely related to a much simpler minimization problem defined by:

O_{B}(M):=\min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

For the sake of convenience, in what follows we shall always assume
x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k
unless specified otherwise.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
A=(a,ha+db_{1},...,ha+db_{k})
,
k,h,d\in\mathbb{P}
and
\gcd(A)=1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
. For a given
0\leq r\leq a-1
, we have

N_{dr}=\min\{O_{B}(ma+r)\cdot ha+(ma+r)d\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(2)

Proof.

We have the following equations

\displaystyle N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\min\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv dr\mod a;\ d(a_{0};ha+db_{1},...,ha+db_{k})>0\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ha+db_{i})x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ha+db_{i})x_{i}\equiv dr\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\big)\cdot ha+d\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\mid d\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\equiv dr\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\big)\cdot ha+d\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\equiv r\mod\frac{a}{(a,d)},\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\big)\cdot ha+d(ma+r)\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=ma+r,\ m,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

Now for fixed
m
, and hence fixed
M=ma+r
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}
is minimized to
O_{B}(ma+r)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that the argument in the proof also works for
h=0
. This leads to a simple proof of the following result.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
A=(a,dB)=(a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k})
, with
d\in\mathbb{P}
and
\gcd(A)=1
. Then:

g(a,db_{1},...,db_{k})=dg(a,b_{1},...,b_{k})+(d-1)a.

(3)

Proof.

By the definition of
N_{dr}
, for a given
r
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}(a,dB)

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}(db_{i})\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}(db_{i})\equiv dr\mod a,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle=d\cdot\min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}b_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}b_{i}\equiv r\mod a,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle=d\cdot N_{r}(a,B).

It follows that

\displaystyle g(a,dB)+a=\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}\{N_{dr}(a,dB)\}=d\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}\{N_{r}(a,B)\}=d(g(a,B)+a).

The theorem then follows.
∎

Note
: This proof is quite simple, compared with existing proofs
[
19
]
. Moreover, we do not require
d=\gcd(db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k})
.

For the convenience of the following discussion and by Lemma
2.3
, we can define an intermediate function with respect to
m
, namely:

N_{dr}(m):=ha\cdot O_{B}(ma+r)+(ma+r)d,\quad\text{where}\ A=(a,ha+dB).

(4)

Lemma
2.3
suggests the following strategy for
N_{dr}
where

A=(a,ha+dB)
: we first try to solve
O_{B}(M)
for general
M
. If we have a formula that is nice enough, then we can analyze
N_{dr}(m)
. In fact, if
N_{dr}(m)
increases with
m
, then we have a formula for
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)
. Hence we can further obtain the formula of
g(A)
,
n(A)
, and
s(A)
.

This strategy succeeds in many situations, as we shall illustrate in the next two sections.
We need one more easy fact.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
\frac{a}{b}>0
, then
f(x)=\frac{ax}{b}
,
g(x)=\lfloor\frac{ax}{b}\rfloor
and
h(x)=\lceil\frac{ax}{b}\rceil
are increasing with respect to
x
.

3.
Frobenius Formula for Some Short Sequences

This section is divided into four subsections. In each subsection, we use our scheme to find the formulas of
g(A),n(A),s(A)
where
A=(a,ha+dB)
for
one typical example. The first two are for
A=(a,ha+d,ha+jd)
and
A=(a,ha+d,ha+2d,ha+jd)
.
The corresponding
O_{B}(M)
can be solved by the greedy algorithm, and the situation becomes easy when
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)
.
The third is for a generalization of
A=(a^{2},a^{2}+1,a^{2}+a,a^{2}+(a+1))
. This
A
was considered as a hard instance
from another view. The fourth is for
A=(a,ha-d,ha+d)
, where we need to solve
O_{B}(M)
for integer
M
(allowed to be negative).

3.1.
The Case of
A=(a,ha+d,ha+jd)

There is a large class of
B
so that
O_{B}(M)

can be achieved by the greedy algorithm.
Even in these cases
g(A)
might be too complicated to solve.
Our scheme works for many instances.

In this subsection, we consider the case
A=(a,ha+d,ha+jd)
which is already complicated, though the following minimization problem is easy.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
B=(1,j)
,
j>2
. If
M=sj+r_{1}
,
0\leq r_{1}\leq j-1
, then we have

O_{B}(M)=r_{1}+s=M-(j-1)\lfloor M/j\rfloor,

with the minimum achieved at
(x_{1},x_{2})=(r_{1},s)
.

We start by proving the following result.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
A=(a,a+1,a+j)
,
a,j>2
. Then

\displaystyle g(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{a(a+1)}{j}+(j-3)a-1&\text{if}\ \ a\equiv-1\mod j;\ \ a\geq j^{2}-5j+3,\\
\lfloor\frac{a+1}{j}\rfloor(a+j)+(j-3)a-1&\text{if}\ \ a\not\equiv-1\mod j;\ \ a\geq j^{2}-4j+2.\\
\end{array}\right.

Remark 3.3
.

We first see this theorem (with a typo) in the book
[
19
]
, which was cited as a result in
[
20
]
. The theorem was not proved, as the author said that the proof process is rather long.

Now we can give a simple proof of a slight generalization of Theorem
3.2
as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A=(a,ha+d,ha+jd)
,
a,j>2
, and
a=kj-t,k\geq 1,0\leq t\leq j-1,gcd(a,d)=1,h\geq d
, where
a,h,d,j,k,t\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle g(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{ha^{2}}{j}+(j-2)ha+(a-1)d-a&\text{if}\ \ t=0;\\
\frac{ha(a+1)}{j}+(j-3)ha+(a-1)d-a&\text{if}\ \ t=1;\\
\lfloor\frac{a}{j}\rfloor(ha+jd)+(j-2)ha-d-a&\text{if}\ \ 2\leq t\leq j-1;\ \ hk+d-ht\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{(a-1)(ha+d-1)}{2}-\frac{h(j-1)(a-t)}{2}(\frac{a+t}{j}-1),\ \ \text{if}\ \ hk+d-ht\geq 0,

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{(ha+d)^{2}(2a^{2}-3a+1)}{12}+\frac{h^{2}a(j-1)^{2}(k-1)}{6}(jk^{2}-\frac{jk}{2}-3tk+3t)

\displaystyle-\frac{h(ha+d)(j-1)(k-1)}{6}(2j^{2}(k^{2}+\frac{k}{4})-2kj(3t+\frac{3}{4})+3t(t+1))

\displaystyle-\frac{a(ha+d)(a-1)}{4}+\frac{ha(j-1)(a-t)}{4}(\frac{a+t}{j}-1)+\frac{a^{2}-1}{12},\ \ \text{if}\ \ hk+d-ht\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
B=(1,j)
.
By Lemma
2.3
, for a given
r
,
if
M=ma+r=s\cdot j+r_{1},\ s\geq 0,0\leq r_{1}<j
, then
s=\lfloor\frac{ma+r}{j}\rfloor
, and

\displaystyle N_{dr}(m)

\displaystyle=(s+r_{1})ha+(ma+r)d=(s(1-j))ha+(ma+r)(ha+d)

\displaystyle=(ma+r)(ha+d)-\big\lfloor\frac{ma+r}{j}\big\rfloor(j-1)ha.

(5)

Now assume
a=kj-t,k\geq 1,0\leq t\leq j-1,\ a,j>2
. Then we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}(m+1)-N_{dr}(m)

\displaystyle=((m+1)a+r)(ha+d)-\big\lfloor\frac{(m+1)a+r}{j}\big\rfloor(j-1)ha

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -(ma+r)(ha+d)+\big\lfloor\frac{ma+r}{j}\big\rfloor(j-1)ha

\displaystyle=a(ha+d)-(j-1)ha\Big(\big\lfloor\frac{(m+1)a+r}{j}\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor\frac{ma+r}{j}\big\rfloor\Big)

\displaystyle\geq a(ha+d)-(j-1)ha\cdot\lceil\frac{a}{j}\rceil

\displaystyle=a(hkj-ht+d)-(j-1)hak=a(hk+d-ht).

It follows that if
hk+d-ht\geq 0
, then
N_{dr}(m)
is increasing,
and hence minimizes at
m=0
.
Now, we write

N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)=(s+r_{1})ha+rd=(s+r_{1})ha+(sj+r_{1})d=s(ha+jd)+(ha+d)r_{1},

which is increasing with respect to
s
and to
r_{1}
, respectively.

By
1\leq r\leq a-1=kj-t-1
, we see that
s\leq k-1
. We need to consider the following three cases of
t
.

i) When
t=0
, the condition
hk+d-ht\geq 0
always holds true, and
(s,r_{1})=(k-1,j-1)
gives rise
\max\{N_{dr}\}=(k-1)(ha+jd)+(ha+d)(j-1)=ha^{2}/j+(j-2)ha+(a-1)d.

ii) When
t=1
, the condition
hk+d-ht\geq 0
always holds true. We need to consider two values:
(s,r_{1})=(k-1,j-2)

gives rise
N_{d(a-1)}=(k-1)(ha+jd)+(ha+d)(j-2)
;
(s,r_{1})=(k-2,j-1)
, which gives rise
N_{d(a-j)}=(k-2)(ha+jd)+(ha+d)(j-1)

Lemma 3.9

Suppose

,

Otherwise, we have
O_{B}(M)=M-a\lfloor\frac{M}{a+1}\rfloor

Proof.

Suppose

where
0\leq r_{1}\leq a
. We claim that if the minimum occurs at
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, then
x_{1}=0
or
x_{2}=0
or both. If not, say

and
x_{2}
are both positive, then

(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime})=(x_{1}-1,x_{2}-1,x_{3}+1)

but has smaller

, a contradiction.

Firstly, since

is equivalent to

, we have
x_{1}=0,x_{2}\neq 0
or
x_{1}\neq 0,x_{2}=0
. Since

M\geq a(\lfloor\frac{M}{a+1}\rfloor+1)
is equivalent to

, we have

M=s(a+1)+r_{1}=(s-a+r_{1})(a+1)+(a-r_{1}+1)a
. At this time, we have

and
the minimum occurs at

. Therefore

.

Secondly, for other cases, it is easy to see that

so that the minimum occurs at
(r_{1},0,s)
. Therefore
O_{B}(M)=s+r_{1}=M-a\lfloor\frac{M}{a+1}\rfloor
.

\displaystyle=ha^{3}+(d-h-1)a^{2}-d,

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}ha^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(d-h-1)a^{2}-\frac{1}{6}ha+\frac{1}{2}(1-d),

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\Big(6h^{2}a^{6}+(9dh-8h^{2}-8h)a^{5}+(4d^{2}-5dh-6d+6h+2)a^{4}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +(2h^{2}-11dh+2h)a^{3}+(5dh-6d^{2}+6d)a^{2}+2dha+2d^{2}-2\Big).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
,
O_{B}(M)=\min\{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\mid x_{1}+x_{2}a+x_{3}(a+1)=M\}
.
Suppose

M=ma^{2}+r=(a+1)\cdot s+r_{1},

where
\ s\geq 0,0\leq r_{1}<a+1
. By Lemma
3.9
, we have the following two types:

\bullet
If
r_{1}\neq 0
and
a-s\leq r_{1}\leq a
, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+1
and

N_{dr}(m)=(s+1)ha^{2}+(ma^{2}+r)d=(\lfloor\frac{ma^{2}+r}{a+1}\rfloor+1)ha^{2}+(ma^{2}+r)d
.

\bullet
If
0\leq r_{1}<a-s
, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+r_{1}
and

N_{dr}(m)=(s+r_{1})ha^{2}+(ma^{2}+r)d=(\lfloor\frac{ma^{2}+r}{a+1}\rfloor+r_{1})ha^{2}+(ma^{2}+r)d
.

For the above two types, as
m
increases by
1
, the value of
s
at least increases by

\lfloor\frac{a^{2}}{a+1}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{(a+1)(a-1)+1}{a+1}\rfloor=a-1
. Obviously,
N_{dr}(m)
is increasing with respect to
m
. So, we have
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)=O_{B}(r)ha^{2}+rd
.
When
r=a^{2}-1=(a+1)(a-1)
, both
O_{B}(r)=a-1
and
r
reach the maximum.
Then we have

\max\{N_{dr}\}=(a-1)ha^{2}+(a^{2}-1)d=ha^{3}+(d-h)a^{2}-d
, and
g(A)=\max\{N_{dr}\}-a^{2}=ha^{3}+(d-h-1)a^{2}-d
.

Next we rewrite
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)
as follows.

\bullet
If
r_{1}\neq 0
and
a-s\leq r_{1}\leq a
, we have

N_{dr}=(\lfloor\frac{r}{a+1}\rfloor+1)ha^{2}+rd
.

\bullet
If
0\leq r_{1}<a-s
, we have

N_{dr}=(\lfloor\frac{r}{a+1}\rfloor+r_{1})ha^{2}+rd
.

Then for
n(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}\Big((\lfloor\frac{r}{a+1}\rfloor+1)ha^{2}+rd\Big)+\sum_{r=1\atop 0\leq r_{1}<a-s}^{a^{2}-1}(r_{1}-1)ha^{2}

\displaystyle=ha^{2}((1+\cdots+(a-1))(a+1)+a-1)+\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}rd+ha^{2}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\sum_{i=0}^{a-m-1}i\Big)-ha^{2}\Big(\sum_{r=1\atop 0\leq r_{1}<a-s}^{a^{2}-1}1-1\Big)

\displaystyle=ha^{2}\Big(\frac{(a-1)a(a+1)}{2}+a\Big)+\frac{da^{2}(a^{2}-1)}{2}+ha^{2}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\frac{(a-m)(a-m-1)}{2}\Big)-\frac{ha^{3}(a+1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}ha^{5}+\frac{1}{2}(d-h)a^{4}-\frac{1}{6}ha^{3}-\frac{1}{2}da^{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle n(A)=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}-\frac{a^{2}-1}{2}=\frac{2}{3}ha^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(d-h-1)a^{2}-\frac{1}{6}ha+\frac{1}{2}(1-d).

For
s(A)
, we omit some calculations and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\sum_{i=0}^{a-m-1}((m+i)ha^{2}+(m(a+1)+i)d)^{2}+\sum_{m=0}^{a-2}\sum_{i=0}^{m}((m+1)ha^{2}+((m+1)a+i)d)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{12}(6h^{2}a^{6}+(9dh-8h^{2})a^{5}+(4d^{2}-5dh)a^{4}+(2h^{2}-11dh)a^{3}+(5dh-6d^{2})a^{2}+2dha+2d^{2}),

which implies that

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a^{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}+\frac{a^{4}-1}{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\big(6h^{2}a^{6}+(9dh-8h^{2}-8h)a^{5}+(4d^{2}-5dh-6d+6h+2)a^{4}+(2h^{2}-11dh+2h)a^{3}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +(5dh-6d^{2}+6d)a^{2}+2dha+2d^{2}-2\big).

The proof of the theorem is now complete.
∎

Further more, we can calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a^{2}-1}N_{dr}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\sum_{i=0}^{a-m-1}\big((m+i)ha^{2}+(m(a+1)+i)d\big)^{p}+\sum_{m=0}^{a-2}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\big((m+1)ha^{2}+((m+1)a+i)d\big)^{p},

thus obtain a formula for

, but such a formula is complicated.

3.4.
The Case of
A=(a,ha-d,ha+d)

We conclude this subsection by the following theorem with possible negative
M
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
A=(a,ha-d,ha+d)
,
d,h\in\mathbb{P},\gcd(a,d)=1
,
ha-d>1
and
s=\lfloor\frac{ha-d}{2h}\rfloor
. Then:

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\lfloor\dfrac{ha-d}{2h}\rfloor(ha+d)-a,\big(a-\lceil\dfrac{ha-d}{2h}\rceil\big)(ha-d)-a\Big\},

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{ha+d}{2a}s(s+1)+\frac{ha-d}{2a}(a-s)(a-s-1)-\frac{a-1}{2},

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{(ha+d)^{2}(2s+1)}{12a}-\frac{ha+d}{4}\Big)s(s+1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\Big(\frac{(ha-d)^{2}(2a-2s-1)}{12a}-\frac{ha-d}{4}\Big)(a-s)(a-s-1)+\frac{a^{2}-1}{12},

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu+1}{\kappa}\mathcal{B}_{\kappa}a^{\kappa-1}\Big((ha+d)^{\mu+1-\kappa}\sum_{r=0}^{s}r^{\mu+1-\kappa}+(ha-d)^{\mu+1-\kappa}\sum_{r=1}^{a-s-1}r^{\mu+1-\kappa}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}(a^{\mu+1}-1).

where
\mu
is a positive integer,
\mathcal{B}_{\kappa}
is the Bernoulli number.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, we need to solve
O_{B}(M)=\min\{x_{1}+x_{2}\mid x_{2}-x_{1}=M\}
for
M=ma+r,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have to distinguish two cases: 1) If
m\geq 0
, then
x_{1}+x_{2}=2x_{1}+M
minimizes to
M
at
x_{1}=0
, so that
N_{dr}
minimizes to
T_{1}:=(ha+d)r
at
m=0,\ x_{1}=0
. 2) If
m<0
then
M<0
and
x_{1}+x_{2}=2x_{2}-M
minimizes to
-M
at
x_{2}=0
, so that
N_{dr}
minimizes to
T_{2}:=(ha-d)(a-r)
at
x_{2}=0,\ m=-1
.

Solving
T_{1}-T_{2}\geq 0
gives
r\geq(ha-d)/2h
. Thus
N_{dr}=T_{2}

for
r\geq(ha-d)/2h
, and
N_{dr}=T_{1}
for otherwise. Obviously
0<(ha-d)/2h<a
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,s\}}\{N_{dr}\}&=\lfloor\dfrac{ha-d}{2h}\rfloor(ha+d)\ &\text{if}\ &s\leq(ha-d)/2h,\\
\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{s,...,a-1\}}\{N_{dr}\}&=\big(a-\lceil\dfrac{ha-d}{2h}\rceil\big)(ha-d)\ &\text{if}\ &s\geq(ha-d)/2h.\end{aligned}\right.

Finally by
g(x)=\max\{N_{dr}\}-a
, we get the formula of
g(A)
in this theorem.

For
n(A)
and
s(A)
:

\bullet
If
r\leq(ha-d)/2h
, we have

N_{dr}=T_{1}=(ha+d)r
.

\bullet
If
r\geq(ha-d)/2h
, we have

N_{dr}=T_{2}=(ha-d)(a-r)
.

Let
s=\lfloor\frac{ha-d}{2h}\rfloor
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{s}(ha+d)r+\sum_{r=s+1}^{a-1}(ha-d)(a-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{ha+d}{2}s(s+1)+\frac{ha-d}{2}(a-s)(a-s-1),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{s}(ha+d)^{2}r^{2}+\sum_{r=s+1}^{a-1}(ha-d)^{2}(a-r)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(ha+d)^{2}}{6}s(s+1)(2s+1)+\frac{(ha-d)^{2}}{6}(a-s-1)(a-s)(2a-2s-1).

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}-\frac{a-1}{2}=\frac{ha+d}{2a}s(s+1)+\frac{ha-d}{2a}(a-s)(a-s-1)-\frac{a-1}{2},

and

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}+\frac{a^{2}-1}{12}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{(ha+d)^{2}(2s+1)}{12a}-\frac{ha+d}{4}\Big)s(s+1)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{(ha-d)^{2}(2a-2s-1)}{12a}-\frac{ha-d}{4}\Big)(a-s)(a-s-1)+\frac{a^{2}-1}{12}.

Further more, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{s}(ha+d)^{p}r^{p}+\sum_{r=s+1}^{a-1}(ha-d)^{p}(a-r)^{p}

\displaystyle=(ha+d)^{p}\sum_{r=1}^{s}r^{p}+(ha-d)^{p}\sum_{r=1}^{a-s-1}r^{p}.

thus obtain a formula for

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(A)=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu+1}{\kappa}B_{\kappa}a^{\kappa-1}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}^{\mu+1-\kappa}+\frac{B_{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}(a^{\mu+1}-1)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu+1}{\kappa}B_{\kappa}a^{\kappa-1}\Big((ha+d)^{\mu+1-\kappa}\sum_{r=0}^{s}r^{\mu+1-\kappa}+(ha-d)^{\mu+1-\kappa}\sum_{r=1}^{a-s-1}r^{\mu+1-\kappa}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{B_{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}(a^{\mu+1}-1).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Frobenius Formula for Almost Arithmetic Sequences

In this section, we will calculate the formula
g(A),n(A),s(A)
of almost arithmetic sequences.
We give a short proof of the following theorem and then establish some new formulas using the same idea.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
24
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
A=(a,ha+d,ha+2d,...,ha+kd)
,
(a,d)=1
,
a,h,d,k\in\mathbb{P}
and
1\leq k\leq a-1
. Suppose
a-1=sk+r_{1}
,
s\geq 0
and
0\leq r_{1}\leq k-1
, then

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=ha\Big(\lfloor\dfrac{a-2}{k}\rfloor+1\Big)+(d-1)(a-1)-1,

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=h\Big(\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor+1\Big)\Big(a-1-\frac{k}{2}\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor\Big)+\frac{(d-1)(a-1)}{2},

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{a(s+1)h^{2}}{6}\big(ks^{2}+\frac{1}{2}ks+3sr_{1}+3r_{1}\big)+\frac{1}{6}(a-1)(d-1)\big(ad-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{a}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{h(s+1)}{4}\Big(-\frac{4}{3}dk^{2}s^{2}+\big(\frac{kd}{3}-4dr_{1}-d+a\big)ks+2r_{1}(-r_{1}d-d+a)\Big),

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu+1}{\kappa}B_{\kappa}a^{\kappa-1}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}\big(ha\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil+dr\big)^{\mu+1-\kappa}+\frac{B_{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}(a^{\mu+1}-1).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, for a given
r
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}=\min_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\{O_{B}(ma+r)\cdot ha+d\cdot(ma+r)\},

where
O_{B}(M)=\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}i\cdot x_{i}=M\}
.

If
M=s\cdot k+t
with
1\leq t\leq k
, then
O_{B}(M)=s+1
, which
minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{t}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,t)
when
k\neq t
,
and minimizes at
x_{k}=s+1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k)
when
k=t
.

Now
N_{dr}(m)=(s+1)ha+d(ma+r)=ha\lceil\frac{ma+r}{k}\rceil+d(ma+r)
is increasing with respect to
m
. It follows that

N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)=ha\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil+dr.

Using this explicit formula, we can compute
g(A)
,
n(A)
and
s(A)
as follows.

For
g(A)
, notice
N_{dr}
is increasing with respect to
r
. Hence

\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}\{N_{dr}\}=N_{d(a-1)}=ha\Big(\lfloor\dfrac{a-2}{k}\rfloor+1\Big)+d(a-1).

For
n(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}

\displaystyle=ha\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil+\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}dr

\displaystyle=ha((1+2+\cdots+s)k+(s+1)r_{1})+d\frac{a(a-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=ha\Big(\frac{s(s+1)k}{2}+(s+1)r_{1}\Big)+\frac{da(a-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=ha\Big(\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor+1\Big)\Big(a-1-\frac{k}{2}\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor\Big)+\frac{da(a-1)}{2}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
gives

\displaystyle n(A)=h\Big(\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor+1\Big)\Big(a-1-\frac{k}{2}\lfloor\frac{a-1}{k}\rfloor\Big)+\frac{(d-1)(a-1)}{2}.

For
s(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{dr}^{2}

\displaystyle=h^{2}a^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}(\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil)^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}d^{2}r^{2}+2had\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}r(\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil)

\displaystyle=h^{2}a^{2}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{s}km^{2}+(s+1)^{2}r_{1}\Big)+\frac{ad^{2}(a-1)(2a-1)}{6}+2had\Bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{s}\sum_{r=(m-1)k+1}^{mk}mr+\sum_{r=sk+1}^{sk+r_{1}}(s+1)r\Bigg)

\displaystyle={h}^{2}{a}^{2}\left({\frac{s\left(s+1\right)\left(2\,s+1\right)k}{6}}+\left(s+1\right)^{2}{r_{1}}\right)+{\frac{a{d}^{2}\left(a-1\right)\left(2\,a-1\right)}{6}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +2had\Big(-\frac{3k^{2}(s+1)^{2}}{4}+\frac{5(s+1)k^{2}}{12}+\frac{k(s+1)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(s+1)k^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{k^{2}(s+1)^{3}}{3}+\frac{r_{1}(s+1)(2sk+r_{1}+1)}{2}\Big).

Applying Lemma
2.1
gives

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{a(s+1)h^{2}}{6}\big(ks^{2}+\frac{1}{2}ks+3sr_{1}+3r_{1}\big)+\frac{1}{6}(a-1)(d-1)\big(ad-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{a}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{h(s+1)}{4}\Big(-\frac{4}{3}dk^{2}s^{2}+(\frac{kd}{3}-4dr_{1}-d+a)ks+2r_{1}(-r_{1}d-d+a)\Big).

The formula for
s_{\mu}(A)
is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Next we extend a recent result (corresponding to our

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A=(a,ha+(K+1)d,ha+(K+2)d,...,ha+kd)
, where
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
a,h,d,K,k\in\mathbb{P}
,

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=ha\cdot\lceil\frac{a+K}{k}\rceil+d(a+K)-a,

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &h\Big((q+1)\big(\frac{qk}{2}+r_{1}-1\big)-K\Big)+\frac{(a-1)(d-1)}{2}+Kd\ &\text{if}&\ \ K+1\leq r_{1}\leq k,\\
&h\Big((q+1)\big(\frac{qk}{2}+r_{1}\big)-K-q\Big)+\frac{(a-1)(d-1)}{2}+Kd\ &\text{if}&\ \ 0\leq r_{1}\leq K.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, for a given
r
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\min\{O_{B}(ma+r)\cdot ha+d(ma+r)\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\},

where
O_{B}(M)=\min\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(K+i)\cdot x_{i}=M\big\}
.

If
M=ma+r=k\cdot s+t
, where
s\geq 0,1\leq t\leq k
.
When
K+1\leq t\leq k
, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+1
, which minimizes at
x_{k-K}=s,x_{t-K}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k-K,t-K),k\neq t
and
x_{k-K}=s+1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k-K),k=t

, there is no solution for

. For

, we have

N_{dr}(m)=(s+1)ha+d(ma+r)=\lceil\frac{ma+r}{k}\rceil\cdot ha+d(ma+r),

which is increasing with respect to

. Therefore
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)
for

and

\displaystyle N_{dr}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &ha\lceil\frac{a+r}{k}\rceil+d(a+r)\ &\text{if}&\ 1\leq r\leq K,\\
&ha\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil+dr\ &\text{if}&\ K+1\leq r\leq a-1.\\

This explicit formula allow us to compute
g(A),n(A),s(A)
easily. We only work out the

case, the other two cases
are omitted since the process is similar to that of Takao Komatsu.

Since
N_{dr}
is increasing with respect to
r
, we have
\max\{N_{dr}\}=\max\{N_{dK},N_{d(a-1)}\}=N_{dK}
. This gives

\displaystyle g(A)=\max\{N_{dr}\}-a=\lceil\frac{a+K}{k}\rceil\cdot ha+d(a+K)-a.

∎

Next, we construct some interesting sequences and find their Frobenius formulas.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A=(a,ha+d,ha+3d,ha+5d,...,ha+(2k+1)d)
, where
(a,d)=1
,
a,h,d,k\in\mathbb{P}
,
a>2
,
3\leq 2k+1\leq a-1
. And let
a-1=(2k+1)s+t

1\leq t\leq 2k+1
.

, we have

g(A)=ha\Big(\lfloor\dfrac{a-2}{2k+1}\rfloor+2\Big)+(a-1)d-a.

When

Further more, we have

n(A)=hs\big(ks+t+\frac{1}{2}s-\frac{1}{2}\big)+(a-1)\big(\frac{d}{2}+h-\frac{1}{2}\big)+h\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\rfloor.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, for a given
r
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\min_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\{O_{B}(ma+r)\cdot ha+d(ma+r)\},

where
O_{B}(M)=\min\{\sum_{i=0}^{k}x_{i}\mid x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(2i+1)\cdot x_{i}=M\}

Suppose
M=(2k+1)\cdot s+t
, where
1\leq t\leq 2k+1
.

\bullet
When
t
is even, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+2
, which minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{(t-2)/2}=1,x_{0}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,(t-2)/2,0)
for
t\neq 2
, and minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{0}=2,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,0)
for
t=2
. Then

N_{dr}(m)=ha(s+2)+d(ma+r)=ha\Big(\lfloor\dfrac{ma+r-1}{2k+1}\rfloor+2\Big)+d(ma+r).

\bullet
When
t
is odd, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+1
, which minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{(t-1)/2}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,(t-1)/2)
for
t\neq 2k+1
and
t\neq 1
, and minimizes at
x_{k}=s+1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k)
for

, and minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{0}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,0)
for

. Then

N_{dr}(m)=ha(s+1)+d(ma+r)=ha\Big(\lfloor\dfrac{ma+r-1}{2k+1}\rfloor+1\Big)+d(ma+r).

Obviously
N_{dr}(m+1)-N_{dr}(m)\geq ha(\lfloor\frac{a}{2k+1}\rfloor-1)+da\geq 0
. In order to minimize

Another Idea: Extract Constant Term

In this section, we introduce “Constant Term Method” to calculate

,
\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}N_{r}^{2}
,
\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}N_{r}^{3}

The idea can be carried out by Maple.

5.1.
Introduction to Constant Term Method

In
[
32
]
, Xin gave a polynomial time algorithm for MacMahon’s partition analysis in a suitable condition, and develop a Euclid style algorithm with an implementation by the Maple package CTEuclid. For a more detailed description, see
[
31
,
32
]
, our ideas mainly come from these two articles.
The new point that the ideas in
[
32
, Section 3]
can be adapted to solve problems involving symbolic integer parameters. Such problems arise naturally in this article, but cannot be handled by existing packages.

Suppose
E(x)
is written as a sum of
simple Elliott-rational functions
in the following form:

\displaystyle E(x)=\sum_{i}\frac{L_{i}(x)}{(1-x^{b_{i1}})(1-x^{b_{i2}})\cdots(1-x^{b_{im}})},

where the
L_{i}(x)
’s are Laurent polynomials, and
b_{ij},1\leq j\leq m
are integers. Under the premise that
E(1)
exists, our purpose is to calculate
E(1)
.

In
[
32
]
, two methods are introduced. One is by computing
\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{t}E(1+t)
, and the other is by computing
\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{t}E(e^{t})
, where
\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{t}F(t)
denotes taking constant term in the Laurent series expansion of
F(t)
. We adopt the exponential substitution
x\mapsto e^{t}
, which leads to

\displaystyle E(e^{t})=\sum_{i}\frac{L_{i}(e^{t})}{(1-e^{b_{i1}t})(1-e^{b_{i2}t})\cdots(1-e^{b_{im}t})}.

Regards both sides of the above equation as formal Laurent series in
t
. Then by the linearity of the constant term operator
\mathrm{CT}_{t}
, we have

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
E(1)
exists, we have

\displaystyle E(1)=E(e^{0})=\underset{t}{\mathrm{CT}}E(e^{t})=\sum_{i}\underset{t}{\mathrm{CT}}\frac{L_{i}(e^{t})}{(1-e^{b_{i1}t})(1-e^{b_{i2}t})\cdots(1-e^{b_{im}t})}.

For fix
m
, we only need the following two formulas and do polynomial multiplications:

\frac{t}{1-e^{t}}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}-\frac{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{n!}t^{n}+o(t^{m})=-1+\frac{1}{2}t-\frac{1}{12}t^{2}+\frac{1}{720}t^{4}+\cdots+o(t^{m}),

(6)

e^{t}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}t^{n}+o(t^{m})=1+t+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{6}t^{3}+\cdots+o(t^{m}).

(7)

The
\mathcal{B}_{n}
are the well-konwn Bernoulli numbers.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
L(x)
be a Laurent polynomial with
U
terms, and
b_{j},1\leq j\leq m
are integers. The following formula can be quickly calculated in finite steps:

\displaystyle\underset{t}{\mathrm{CT}}\frac{L(e^{t})}{(1-e^{b_{1}t})(1-e^{b_{2}t})\cdots(1-e^{b_{m}t})}.

Proof.

By Equation (
6
), we can use
m-1
multiplications to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{t^{m}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-e^{b_{j}t})}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{n\geq 0}-\frac{\mathcal{B}_{n}b_{j}^{n-1}}{n!}t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}c_{n}t^{n}+o(t^{m}).

This is a power series in
t
. By Equation (
7
), we have the expansion

L(e^{t})=\sum_{n=0}^{m}d_{n}t^{n}+o(t^{m}).

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\underset{t}{\mathrm{CT}}\frac{L(e^{t})}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-e^{b_{j}t})}=[t^{m}]\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{m}d_{n}t^{n}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{m}c_{n}t^{n}\Big)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}d_{n}c_{m-n}.

∎

Observe that the above process works for symbolic
b_{ij}
. It is not hard to rewrite the code in Maple to carry out the above process. We store
Equations (
6

7
) for

in advance. This is sufficient for problems with

. Maple can help us quickly calculate

5.2.

and

In
[
4
]
, Brown and Shiue use the following ideas to calculate

. Let

be any set of positive integers with

f_{{\mathcal{NR}}(A)}(x)=\frac{1}{1-x}-f_{{\mathcal{R}}(A)}(x),

In
[

(below) as a short sum of simple rational functions. We also have a decomposition of

Let

The relevant conclusions can be obtained in
[

for Stirling number of the first kind

and the second kind

Now, let

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

where

is a positive integer,

If the optimization problem

A robust explanation of CMB anomalies with a new formulation of inflationary quantum fluctuations
João Marto

Abstract
The presence of CMB Hemispherical Asymmetry (HPA) challenges the current understanding of inflationary cosmology which does not generically predict the parity violation in the primordial correlations. In this paper, we shall review the recently proposed resolution to this based on a new formulation of quantizing inflationary fluctuations by focusing on the discrete spacetime transformations in a gravitational context. The predictive power of this formulation is that one can generate a scale dependent HPA in the context of single field inflation for all the primordial modes including scalar and tensor fluctuations without introducing any additional parameters. This result can be seen as an indication of spontaneous breaking of 𝒞 ​ 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{C}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T} symmetry in an expanding Universe, if confirmed by future observations it would be a great leap in the subject of quantum field theory in curved spacetime.

2
Direct-sum quantum field theory (DQFT) in de Sitter spacetime

The dS manifold is maximally symmetric spacetime like Minkowski and it is a solution of GR with a positive cosmological constant. Before we go to the context of quantum fields during inflation, it is important to understand the subject in dS.
The dS spacetime is characterized the following relations between curvature tensors and the metric as

R^{\mu}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{R}{12}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-\delta^{\mu}_{\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}\right),\quad R_{\nu\sigma}=\frac{R}{4}g_{\nu\sigma},\quad R=\mathrm{const}\,.

(3)

The above definition is coordinate independent. In the context of cosmology the relevant form of expressing dS metric is in terms of
flat FLRW coordinates which is given by

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}d\textbf{x}^{2}

(4)

where the scale factor is

a(t)=e^{Ht},\quad H^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{dt}\right)^{2}>0\,,

(5)

Here
H
is called the Hubble parameter. In this dS space, one has the comoving horizon defined by the radius

r_{H}=\Big|\frac{1}{aH}\Big|

(6)

which decreases during expansion since scale factor grows. For the purpose of quantization it is useful to express (
4
) in terms of conformal time defined by

d\tau=-\frac{dt}{a(t)}\,.

(7)

Integrating this equation, we obtain

\tau=\frac{1}{a(t)H}\,\text{,}

Now the dS metric becomes conformal to Minkowski as

ds^{2}=\frac{1}{H^{2}\tau^{2}}\left(-d\tau^{2}+d\textbf{x}^{2}\right)

(8)

When the time coordinate
\tau\gtrless 0
we get a coordinate singularity at
\tau=0
but it is just an artifact of the coordinate transformation. In reality, There is no spacetime singularity because all the curvature invariants in dS are constants. Just like Minkowski spacetime the dS metric satisfies
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}
symmetries as

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}:\tau\to-\tau,\quad\textbf{x}\to-\textbf{x}\,.

(9)

We can notice that the time (
\tau
) reversal operation actually leads to time reversal in cosmic time, as well as the change of sign for the Hubble parameter
H
i.e.,

\mathcal{T}:\tau\to-\tau\implies t\to-t,\,H\to-H\,.

(10)

We can verify that
H\to-H
operation does not effect at all the definition of dS (
3
). For example, the Ricci scalar
R=12H^{2}
in dS is completely symmetric under
H\to-H
. Remember that time is a parameter in quantum theory and here
\tau
plays the role as a parameter for quantum field theory in dS. Since the metric (
8
) is completely time symmteric (i.e.,
\tau\to-\tau
) quantum fields cannot see the difference between whether
\tau
is positive or negative.
Indeed from (
4
), we can notice that

{\rm Expanding\,Universe:}\implies\begin{cases}t:-\infty\to+\infty,\quad H>0&(\tau:+\infty\to 0)\\
t:+\infty\to-\infty,\quad H<0&(\tau:-\infty\to 0)\end{cases}

(11)

In (
11
) we see two arrows of cosmic time
t
(together with sign of
H
), both of which correspond to expanding Universe with the definition that the
r_{H}
decreases as scale factor increases (
6
). Therefore, (
11
) exactly serves the definition of time reversal in an expanding Universe and this is the cue for our formulation of direct-sum quantum field theory (DQFT).

Let us start with direct-sum quantization of a massless scalar field whose action for the metric (
4
) can be computed as

S_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau d^{3}xa^{2}\phi\left(\partial_{\tau}^{2}+2\mathcal{H}\partial_{\tau}+k^{2}\right)\phi\,,

(12)

where
\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\tau}=aH
.
The above action is invariant under the
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}
defined in (
9
).
To quantize it is convenient to rescale the field
\phi\to a\phi
, which bring us to a harmonic oscillator form with time dependent mass

S_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau d^{3}x\Big[\phi^{\prime 2}-\left(\partial_{i}\phi\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{\tau^{2}}\phi^{2}\Big]\,.

(13)

In the process of quantization, we promote the scalar field
\phi
as an operator
\hat{\phi}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)
which in DQFT formulation expressed as direct-sum
[
17
]

\hat{\phi}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)\oplus\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{\phi}_{II}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)&0\\
0&\hat{\phi}_{II}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)\end{pmatrix}\,.

(14)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{3/2}}\int d^{3}k\Bigg[\hat{c}_{I\,\textbf{k}}{\phi}_{I\,k}\left(\tau\right)e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}+\hat{c}_{I\,\textbf{k}}^{\dagger}{\phi}^{\ast}_{I\,k}\left(\tau\right)e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Bigg]\,

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{II}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{3/2}}\int d^{3}k\Bigg[\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}}{\phi}_{-\,k}\left(-\tau\right)e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}+\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}}^{\dagger}{\phi}^{\ast}_{II\,k}\left(-\tau\right)e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Bigg]\,,

The creation and annihilation operators here
\hat{c}_{\pm\textbf{k}},\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\pm\textbf{k}}
satisfy the following commutation relations

\displaystyle=[\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}},\,\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta^{(3)}\left(\textbf{k}-\textbf{k}^{\prime}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle[\hat{c}_{I\,\textbf{k}},\,\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{c}_{I\,\textbf{k}},\,\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=[\hat{c}_{I\,\textbf{k}}^{\dagger},\,\hat{c}_{II\,\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=0\,.

which leads to

\displaystyle=i\delta\left(\textbf{x}-\textbf{x}^{\prime}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle[\hat{\phi}_{II}\left(-t,-\textbf{x}\right),\,\pi_{II}\left(-t,\,-\textbf{x}^{\prime}\right)]

\displaystyle=-i\delta\left(\textbf{x}-\textbf{x}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right),\,\hat{\phi}_{II}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=0\,,

where

\pi_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)=\partial_{\tau}\phi_{I},\quad\pi_{II}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)=-\partial_{\tau}\phi_{II}\,.

(18)

The physical meaning of DQFT is the following. In the dS with flat FLRW we demand the a quantum field within the comoving horizon evolves forward in time at position
x
while it evolves backward in time at the position
-\textbf{x}
. The third line of (

In fact, in DQFT we divide the spatial region into two parts by parity, lets call them as region I and II. A quantum field in region one evolves forward in time and in the region II evolves backward in time in two different Fock spaces called
{\rm dS}_{I}
and
{\rm dS}_{II}

The mode functions

\displaystyle\phi_{I,\,k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{I\,k}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1+\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)+\beta_{I\,k}\frac{e^{-ik\tau}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1-\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\phi_{m\,k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{2}{\tau^{2}}\right)\phi_{m\,k}=0\,.

(20)

where

This fixes the Bogoliubov coefficients as

which are compatible with the Wronskian conditions obtained from (

\displaystyle\phi_{I,\,k}\phi^{\prime\ast}_{I,\,k}-\phi^{\ast}_{I,\,k}\varphi^{\prime}_{I,\,k}

\displaystyle\phi_{II,\,k}\phi^{\prime\ast}_{II,\,k}-\phi^{\ast}_{II,\,k}\phi^{\prime}_{II,\,k}

The dS spacetime vacuum is now represented by the direct-sum

|0\rangle_{dS}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|0\rangle_{\rm dS_{I}}\oplus|0\rangle_{\rm dS_{II}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}|0\rangle_{\rm dS_{I}}\\
|0\rangle_{\rm dS_{II}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(23)

where

are

(24)

We can verify this, observing that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{2}}{}_{dSI}\langle 0|\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)\hat{\phi}_{I}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}^{\prime}\right)|0\rangle_{dSI}=

(25)

This (

DQFT in inflationary spacetime

In the previous section, we have discussed quantization in dS spacetime by clear understanding of time reversal operation. In the context of inflationary spacetime time reversal would be much more involved than dS. As we learned in the previous section, the conformal time
\tau
plays the role of parameter in quantum theory and
\tau\to-\tau
actually mean time reversal in an expanding Universe for which we would have to change
t\to-t
and
H\to-H
. This implies, if we want to understand time reversal in the context of curved spacetime or when there is gravity we would need additional parameters associated with gravity or background curved spacetime. For example, the Hubble parameter in the context of dS is the additional parameter along with cosmic time (of course together we can just use
\tau
as a parameter). In the context of dynamical gravity like inflation, we would expect more parameters to play the role in time reversal. This is a very subtle task but however, with the generic expectation of
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}
invariance must be spontaneously broken in curved spacetime we define the time reversal transformations in the inflationary context as

t\to-t\implies H\to-H,\quad\epsilon\to-\epsilon,\quad\eta\to-\eta\,.

(26)

where
\epsilon=-\dot{H}/H^{2},\eta=\dot{\epsilon}/(H\epsilon)
are although usual slow-roll parameters, here in the context of time reversal their role is completely quantum mechanical and there is no classical analog (See
[
16
]
for more details, also see
[
18
]
for more thought provoking discussions on time reversal in quantum gravity).

Similar to the dS DQFT, we should split the field as direct-sum of the two components as

\hat{v}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{v}_{\rm+}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)\oplus\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{v}_{\rm-}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{v}_{+}\left(\tau,\,\textbf{x}\right)&0\\

(27)

where

\hat{v}_{-}\left(-\tau,\,-\textbf{x}\right)

The total vacuum of inflationary spacetime is the direct-sum of the two represented as

|0\rangle_{\rm qdS}=|0\rangle_{\rm qdS_{I}}\oplus|0\rangle_{\rm qdS_{II}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}|0\rangle_{\rm qdS_{I}}\\

compatible with the Wronskian

(37)

4
HPA of primordial power spectra from DQFT of inflatonary quantum fluctuations

Inflationary quantum fluctuations in the context of DQFT come with deformations when they exit the comoving horizon on the two opposite sides.
To calculate the two point correlations of curvature perturbation on super-horizon scales we need to rescale the canonical fields
\hat{v}

\displaystyle{}_{\rm qdS}\langle 0|\zeta_{\textbf{k}}\zeta_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}|0\rangle_{\rm qdS}

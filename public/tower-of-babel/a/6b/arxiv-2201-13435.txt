Twisted chiral algebras of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} and mixed Feigin–Frenkel gluing
Christopher Beem and Sujay Nair

Abstract
The correspondence between four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal field theories and vertex operator algebras, when applied to theories of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , leads to a rich family of VOAs that have been given the monicker chiral algebras of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . A remarkably uniform construction of these vertex operator algebras has been put forward by Tomoyuki Arakawa in Arakawa:2018egx . The construction of Arakawa:2018egx takes as input a choice of simple Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , and applies equally well regardless of whether 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is simply laced or not. In the non-simply laced case, however, the resulting VOAs do not correspond in any clear way to known four-dimensional theories. On the other hand, the standard realisation of class 𝒮 {\cal S} theories involving non-simply laced symmetry algebras requires the inclusion of outer automorphism twist lines, and this requires a further development of the approach of Arakawa:2018egx . In this paper, we give an account of those further developments and propose definitions of most chiral algebras of class 𝒮 {\cal S} with outer automorphism twist lines. We show that our definition passes some consistency checks and point out some important open problems.

Introduction

Since their rise to prominence in the late aughts, theories of class
{\cal S}

Gaiotto:2009we
;
Gaiotto:2009hg
have proven to constitute an important and highly structured family of four-dimensional,
{\cal N}=2
superconformal field theories (SCFTs).

Throughout this paper we restrict without further comment to the
{\cal N}=2
superconformal case when referencing SCFTs or “theories” more generally.
These theories are best understood as those which arise in the low energy limit when six-dimensional
(2,0)
SCFTs are compactified (in an appropriately supersymmetry-preserving fashion) on a compact two-manifold (the
UV curve
), possibly in the presence of codimension-two supersymmetric defects. Many properties of class
{\cal S}
theories are well-characterised in terms of two-dimensional structures,
e.g.
, the marginal (gauge) couplings are identified with complex structure moduli of the curve. Indeed, a cottage industry of identifying four-dimensional observables with dual computations taking place in two dimensions on the UV curve has emerged and achieved a good degree of maturity.

One such observable (to use the term expansively) that is tractable yet structurally intricate is the associated vertex operator algebra (VOA) or chiral algebra
Beem:2013sza
. The VOAs associated to theories of class
{\cal S}
were first systematically studied in
Beem:2014rza
where they went under the name
chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
. In that work, a number of key properties of these VOAs were identified and some explicit computations performed in simple cases (with underlying
(2,0)
theory of type
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}_{1}
or
\mathfrak{a}_{2}
). A speculative vision was also put forward wherein general chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
might be uniquely determined by their various duality properties—a
theory space bootstrap
—which was an idea that had seen prior success in the case of the superconformal index
Gaiotto:2012xa
.

This speculation was largely confirmed several years later in a remarkable paper by Tomoyuki Arakawa
Arakawa:2018egx
. As we will review later, Arakawa’s construction involves a gluing operation, which we refer to as
Feigin–Frenkel gluing
, that has no obvious four-dimensional counterpart but which can heuristically be thought of as gluing UV curves together along interior points as opposed to marked points. The crucial insight pointing towards the construction in question is that maximal punctures in class
{\cal S}
give rise to critical-level affine Kac-Moody subalgebras of the associated VOA, and the centres of these subalgebras (their
Feigin-Frenkel centres
) should be identified amongst the different punctures. This is an affine version of a set of well-known Higgs branch chiral ring relations for class
{\cal S}
theories, which themselves play the key role in the construction of the Moore–Tachikawa holomorphic symplectic varieties
Moore:2011ee
by Ginzburg–Kazhdan
Ginzburg:2018
and Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima
Nakajima:2015txa
;
Braverman:2016wma
;
Braverman:2017ofm
;
Braverman:2016pwk
.

A curious feature of the construction in
Arakawa:2018egx
is that it makes perfect sense for any choice of underlying simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, whether simply laced or not. This state of affairs is to be contrasted with the physical reality of class
{\cal S}
, where the basic story applies only for
\mathfrak{g}
simply laced (because
\mathfrak{g}
is the algebra labelling the parent six-dimensional
(2,0)
theory), and the incorporation of non-simply-laced (gauge and global) symmetry algebras normally requires the extra structure of (discrete) outer automorphism twist lines being introduced on the UV curve
Vafa:1997mh
;
Tachikawa:2009
;
Tachikawa:2011
. This raises the question of how/whether to interpret in four-dimensional terms the VOAs constructed in
Arakawa:2018egx
for the non-simply laced case. Perhaps more importantly, it leaves open the problem of defining in comparable generality the
twisted chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
corresponding to theories that are realised using outer automorphism twist lines.

The purpose of the present work is to address the latter problem. We propose a definition
à la Arakawa
for a large family of twisted chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
. Our definition allows for the realisation of all trinion chiral algebras with one untwisted and two twisted punctures. This is still restricted, however, in that it does not allow for the case of the
\mathfrak{d}_{4}
-type trinion theory with three twisted punctures, or more general theories whose realisation require such constituent fixtures. While we can prove a number of duality properties of these algebras, we encounter an obstruction in the proof of the most general expected dualities. This obstruction is analogous to one that appears already in
Arakawa:2018egx
in the case of higher-genus chiral algebras.

-duality for the twisted chiral algebras of class

with respect to a subset of duality transformations. In Section

Arakawa:2018egx
in Appendix
A

B
and
C
, we prove two important technical theorems used in our analysis of mixed Feigin–Frenkel gluing. In Appendix

2
Review of (twisted) class
{\cal S}

To begin, let us review some important features of four-dimensional SCFTs of class
{\cal S}
, paying special attention to the generalisation to included twist lines. This is not meant to be a comprehensive overview; much more background on these theories can be found in,
e.g.
,
Gaiotto:2009we
;
Gaiotto:2009hg
;
Tachikawa:2011
.

Untwisted theories of class
{\cal S}
are specified by a triple
(\mathfrak{g},{\cal C}_{g,s},\{\Lambda_{i}\})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is the simply-laced Lie algebra labelling the underlying six-dimensional theory, the UV curve
{\cal C}_{g,s}
is a genus
g
Riemann surface with
s
marked points, upon which the
(2,0)
is compactified, and the
\{\Lambda_{i}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
are a set of labels for the marked points, which amount to specifications of maximally supersymmetric, codimension-two defect operators inserted at those points in six dimensions. The only geometric data of
{\cal C}_{g,s}
that are relevant are its complex structure moduli, which get identified with marginal (gauge) couplings of the associated four-dimensional SCFT.

The six-dimensional
(2,0)
theories enjoy discrete global symmetries corresponding to outer automorphisms of the underlying Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
—these can be identified with graph automorphisms of the corresponding Dynkin diagrams. One may then include twists/monodromies by these outer automorphism symmetries when compactifying; said differently, one generalises the compactification procedure to incorporate a local system of Dynkin diagrams on the UV curve. The data of this local system can be represented pictorially by including consistent networks of (potentially oriented) twist lines on the UV curve labelled by elements of the outer automorphism group. This generalisation of the class
{\cal S}
construction also introduces new types of punctures, called
twisted punctures
, which correspond to codimension two defects that carry an outer automorphism monodromy. We will be specialising to the genus zero case in our construction and so for convenience we introduce the notation,
{\cal C}_{0,m,n}
to denote the Riemann sphere with
m
untiwsted punctures and
2n
twisted punctures. One cannot specify, unambiguously, a higher genus surface with twisted punctures by the genus and number of each type of puncture.

2.1
Untwisted and twisted punctures

Marked points in class
{\cal S}
can be either
regular
or
irregular
, and in this work we will restrict our attention entirely to the regular case. Regular, untwisted punctures in a theory of type
\mathfrak{g}\in\{\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{d}_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}\}
are labelled by conjugacy classes of homomorphisms
\Lambda:\mathfrak{sl}(2)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. The defect operator corresponding to such a puncture enjoys some global symmetry that it then contributes to the total global flavour symmetry of four dimensional theory. The factor contributed takes the form
\mathfrak{f}_{\Lambda}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\Lambda(\mathfrak{su}(2))\right)
,
i.e.
, the commutant of the image of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
in
\mathfrak{g}
. As a consequence of the Jacobson–Morozov theorem
Collingwood:1993
, the marked points can therefore be equivalently labelled by nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Any simple Lie algebra has at least two especially important nilpotent orbits: the principal orbit (which is the unique, largest nilpotent orbit) and the trivial orbit. The trivial orbit corresponds to the trivial homomorphism
x\mapsto 0,\forall x\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
, while an explicit expression for the principal orbit can be found in
Collingwood:1993
. Their respective commutants are
\mathfrak{f}=0
for the principal orbit and
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{g}
for the trivial orbit. A puncture labelled by the trivial embedding is called a
maximal
puncture, while a puncture labelled by the principal embedding contributes no flavour symmetry and is equivalent to having no puncture at all. With an eye towards the twisted case, we will nevertheless adopt the convention of referring to a hypothetical puncture labelled by the principal embedding as an
empty
puncture.

Given a theory where a particular puncture is maximal, the corresponding theory with that puncture replaced by a sub-maximal puncture can be realised by partially Higgsing the
\mathfrak{g}
flavour symmetry associated to that puncture in the four dimensional theory. A choice of nontrivial
\Lambda
is realised by assigning an expectation value to the “moment map” Higgs branch operator in the conserved current multiplet that lies in the corresponding nilpotent orbit. Consequently, for many purposes it is sufficient to be able to construct theories associated to surfaces with maximal punctures, with other punctures structures being subsequently reached via partial Higgsing. This will be our approach in this paper.

2.2
Trinions, gluing, and duality

Weak coupling limits of class
{\cal S}
theories correspond to degeneration limits of the UV curve, with maximally weakly coupled limits corresponding to pair-of-pants decompositions. The basic entities are the theories corresponding to pairs of pants/spheres with three maximal punctures; these are often referred to as
trinion theories
or
fixtures
. Even in the untwisted setting, there are myriad possible (combinations of) marked point labels that can appear on a single trinion; these have been extensively detailed by (various subsets of) Chacaltana, Distler, Tachikawa, and Trimm in
Chacaltana:2012zy
;
Chacaltana:2010ks
;
Chacaltana:2011ze
. These (generically strongly coupled) theories can be combined by exactly marginal gauging to realise general class
{\cal S}
theories, but the equivalence of the SCFTs realised in this manner by inequivalent pair-of-pants decompositions constitutes a highly nontrivial set of quantum dualities often referred to as generalised
S
-dualities. The class
{\cal S}
dictionary identifies the (compactified) moduli space of the UV curve with the space of complexified gauge couplings of the associated theory. The various pants decompositions are, therefore, the various weak coupling limits of the associated theory—all of them related by generalised
S
-duality.

In the untwisted case, all pants decompositions can be reached by iterating two types of elementary move.

For the first type of move, consider the four puncture sphere
{\cal C}_{0,4}
with some labelling
1,\dots,4
of its punctures. We move to the singular point on the moduli space where the sphere decomposes into two trinions connected by a long tube, one of which has punctures labelled by
1,2
and the other by
3,4
. In Figure
1
, this is represented by the duality frame on the left. By moving to a different singular point, one can decompose
{\cal C}_{0,4}
into two connected trinion labelled by
1,3
and
2,4
—shown on the right hand side of Figure
1
. We call such a move, moving between the various decompositions of
{\cal C}_{0,4}
, a
4
-move. This can be lifted to a
4
-move acting on any collection of four punctures on a general
{\cal C}_{g,s}

{\cal C}_{g,s}

{\cal C}_{g,s-4}
. Then one applies a
4
-move to swap between decompositions of
{\cal C}_{0,4}
, before moving back out of the singular loci.

For surfaces

We are primarily interested in observables that do not depend on exactly marginal deformations, such as gauge couplings. Such observables are blind to continuous paths in the moduli space and only see the various degeneration points. In the untwisted case, the
ab
-move is trivial for such observables and the action of the
4
-moves can be phrased in terms of the action of permutations on punctures. The
4
-moves, in the untwisted case, act by permuting the flavour symmetries associated to the punctures, which shows up as permutations on the flavour fugacities (see the next section) of the index or permuting the moment-maps of the Higgs branch and the associated vertex algebra. It is, therefore, useful to phrase the action of
S
-duality on these observables in terms of the action of a permutation group. For a surface
{\cal C}_{g,n}
the
4
-moves generate an action of
S_{n}
.

The twisted case, however, is not so simple. The first and second types of
4
-move, acting on four punctures of the same type, can be rephrased as the action of
S_{4}
on each collection of untwisted or twisted punctures. The third type of four move,
i.e.
the move of Figure
2
, cannot be rephrased in this way. The two duality frames are not simply related by the action of a permutation on their moment maps or flavour fugacities. Instead one must check the non-trivial agreement between both types of BRST gauging on each side. Matters are further complicated by the
ab
-move of Figure
3
. Like the
4
-move of Figure
2
, both sides of the duality feature a different type of BRST gauging, and again one must check the agreement between both gluings.

In Section
4.7
, after we have constructed the twisted vertex algebras, we will construct the vertex algebraic version of these moves. We are unable to establish invariance across the full duality web, but we do prove invariance under the various types of
4
-moves we have defined. In the genus zero case, to which we are mostly specialising, this does recover the full duality web (under some reasonable assumptions about the properties of gauging).

2.3
The superconformal index

The superconformal index of a four dimensional
{\cal N}=2
SCFT is defined as a graded trace over the radially quantised Hilbert space of the theory
Kinney:2005ej
;
Rastelli:2014jja
,

\mathcal{I}(p,q,t,\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\,(-1)^{F}e^{-\beta(E-2j_{2}-2R+r)}p^{\frac{E+2j_{1}-2R-r}{2}}q^{\frac{E-2j_{1}-2R-r}{2}}t^{R+r}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}_{F}}x_{i}^{T_{i}}~,

(1)

where
p,q,t
are superconformal fugacities and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{F}})
are further fugacities conjugate to generators,
T_{i}
, of a Cartan subalgebra of the flavour symmetry
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
. A standard argument implies that the index can only receive a nonvanishing contribution from states obeying

E-2j_{2}-2R+r=0~,

(2)

and so is actually independent of
\beta
. As written, the index counts minimally supersymmetric states. There are a number of limits with enhanced supersymmetry, and we will be specifically interested in the Schur limit
Gadde:2011uv
,

q\rightarrow t~,\quad p\text{ arbitrary}~.

(3)

In this limit, superconformal representation theory ensures that the index is in fact independent of
p
and takes the schematic form

\mathcal{I}(q)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(-1)^{F}q^{E-R}~,

(4)

with the regularising
\beta
-dependent term suppressed. The Schur index only counts states whose quantum numbers satisfy the further linear relations

E-j_{1}-j_{2}=2R~,\qquad r=j_{1}-j_{2}~.

(5)

This index is a key ingredient in the SCFT/VOA correspondence
Beem:2013sza
. As a graded vector space, the associated VOA can be identified with the space of Schur operators,
i.e.
, the local operators satisfying the above constraints, with the holomorphic dimension of a given state being given by

h=\frac{E+(j_{1}+j_{2})}{2}=E-R~,

(6)

so the Schur index is precisely the vacuum (super)character of the associated vertex algebra
Beem:2013sza
.

For theories of class
\mathcal{S}
, the form of the full superconformal index has been shown to follow from duality properties
Gaiotto:2012xa
, though for our purposes here we will restrict attention to the Schur limit. The index takes the form of a sum over highest weights in the set of integrable dominant weight representations
P^{+}
(
i.e.
, a sum over finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
representations), weighted by some “structure constants”
C_{\lambda\lambda\lambda}
, which are functions of
q
. For a UV curve
{\cal C}_{g,s}
, which can be realised by gluing
2g-2+s
trinions, the index takes the form
Aganagic:2005
;
Gadde:20102d
;
Gadde:2011uv
;
Alday:2013kda

{\cal I}(q;\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{s})=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}(C_{\lambda\lambda\lambda}(q))^{2g-2+s}\prod_{i=1}^{s}{\cal K}(q;\mathbf{x}_{i})\chi^{\lambda}(\mathbf{x}_{i})~,

(7)

where
\mathbf{x}_{i}
are the flavour fugacities arising from the flavour symmetries of the
i\,
th puncture. The
\chi^{\lambda}
are Schur polynomials,
i.e.
, characters of the finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
representation with highest weight
\lambda
. The
{\cal K}
factors are defined by

{\cal K}(\mathbf{x})=\sqrt{{\cal I}^{V}(\mathbf{q;x})}~,

(8)

where
{\cal I}^{V}
is the Schur index for a free vector multiplet in the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

To introduce non-maximal punctures at the level of the index amounts to a fugacity replacement (with a subtraction of certain divergences) that is detailed in
Gadde:2011uv
;
Rastelli:2014jja
;
Beem:2014rza
. Since reducing to an empty puncture amounts to removing the puncture all together, one has the relation

C_{\lambda\lambda\lambda}(q)=\frac{1}{{\cal K}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}(\times)}~,

(9)

where
\times
represents the regularised fugacity replacement for the empty puncture, which is purely a function of
q
.

We can recast the above in more representation theoretic terms by using Appendix A of
Lemos:2014lua
, which contains a useful dictionary. Let
{\cal R}
denote the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, then

{\cal K}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}}\prod_{\alpha\in{\cal R}}\frac{1}{(qe^{\alpha}(\mathbf{x});q)_{\infty}}~,

(10)

where
e^{\alpha}:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are the roots of the simply connected Lie group,
G
, with
Lie(G)=\mathfrak{g}
and maximal torus
T
. For the empty puncture, we have

{\cal K}(\times)=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\frac{1}{(q^{d_{i}},q)_{\infty}}~,

(11)

which one can identify with the character of the Feigin–Frenkel centre,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Similarly, the summand,

{\cal K}(q;\mathbf{x})\chi^{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}(q^{D}\mathbf{x}^{T})~,

{\rm ch}~\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}=\frac{{\rm ch}~\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}{{\rm ch}~\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}}=\frac{{\cal K}(q;\mathbf{x})\chi^{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})}{{\cal K}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}(\times)}~,

(13)

where
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}

and will also be introduced in Section

{\cal I}(q;\mathbf{x_{1}}\mathbf{x_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{s})=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}({\rm ch}~\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda})^{2-2g}\prod_{i=1}^{s}{\rm ch}~\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}({\rm ch}~\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{{\rm ch}~\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}{{\rm ch}~\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}}~.

(14)

The Macdonald limit (and so Schur limit by further specialisation) of the superconformal index in the twisted setting was studied for type
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
theories in

P^{+}
to representations that are invariant under the action of the outer automorphism twist, which is equivalent to summing over the set of highest weight representations of the twisted algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
(which we denote by
P^{+}_{t}
). The overall structure constants are also modified, though they are expressed in terms of the same building blocks. For a surface of genus
g
with
s
untwisted punctures and
2s_{t}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-twisted punctures such that no twist lines wrap any cycles, the index then takes the form

\mathcal{I}(q;\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{s},\mathbf{y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{y}_{2s_{t}})=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in P^{+}_{t}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u}}(q;\mathbf{x}_{i})\chi^{\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u}}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{2s_{t}}\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{g}_{t}}(q;\mathbf{y}_{j})\chi^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{t}}(\mathbf{y}_{j})}{{(\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u}}(\times)\chi^{\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u}}(\times))}^{2g-2+s_{u}+2s_{t}}}~.

(15)

Here

\mathbf{y}_{j}

One can also rewrite the twisted index in terms of Weyl modules as

\mathcal{I}(q;\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{s},\mathbf{y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{y}_{2s_{t}})=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in P_{t}^{+}}({\rm ch}~\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u})^{2-2g}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{L}_{\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}}^{u}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{2s_{t}}\mathbb{L}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{t}\bigg)~,

(16)

where we use the superscripts

It may be worth remarking that all structure constants are of “untwisted type” in spite of the
{\cal K}

This means that when closing a twisted puncture, the specialised puncture factor in the numerator won’t cancel against a corresponding factor in the denominator. This is the index-level incarnation of the fact that minimal twisted punctures are nontrivial and cannot be completely erased from the UV curve.

2.4
Residual gauge symmetry and derived structures

An interesting trichotomy in four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
theories is between those theories with “pure” Higgs branches (there is no unbroken continuous gauge symmetry at generic points), those with “enhanced” Higgs branches (generic Higgs vacua still support massless Abelian vector multiplets), and those with interacting Higgs branches (where generic Higgs vacua support a nontrivial interacting SCFT). To the best of our knowledge, in the presence of regular punctures only pure and enhanced Higgs branches can arise, whereas interacting Higgs branches are common in Argyres–Douglas type theories that can be engineered with irregular punctures.

In the untwisted class
{\cal S}
setting, there is a simple characterisation of under what circumstances a theory will have an enhanced rather than a pure Higgs branch: theories of type
\mathfrak{a_{n}}
,
\mathfrak{d_{n}}
, or
\mathfrak{e_{n}}
with genus
g
have generic residual gauge symmetry with rank equal
n\times g
. This admits a straightforward heuristic explanation: maximal Higgsing will involve first closing all punctures and then moving to the maximal Higgs branch of the unpunctured theory. For the unpunctured theory, one can adopt a six-dimensional perspective and first consider moving to a point on the tensor branch of the
(2,0)
where the effective theory is that of
n
free tensor multiplets, and subsequently compactifying.

For twisted theories, the situation is a bit more complicated. To illustrate, we consider the
\mathfrak{d_{2}}
theory. Due to the accidental isomorphism
\mathfrak{d}_{2}\cong\mathfrak{a}_{1}\times\mathfrak{a}_{1}
, we can recast theories of type
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
into
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
theories. The twisted subalgebra is just
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
, and a full twisted puncture becomes a conventional (untwisted) puncture of the
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
theory. In particular, the
Spin(4)
gauge theory with
N_{f}=4
flavours can be engineered via compactification of the
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
theory on a sphere two maximal and two minimal twisted punctures. Equivalently, it can be described as the
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
theory compactified on a genus-one surface with two punctures. Thus, the
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
surface is a double cover of the
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
surface, treating the twist lines as branch cuts. The
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
theory has residual gauge symmetry at a generic point of the Higgs branch—despite being superficially of genus zero. We will see that this phenomenon is characteristic of the twisted theories.

Somewhat more generally, for
SO(2n)
superconformal QCD—realised in type
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
using a sphere with four twisted punctures (two minimal and two maximal)—a generic point of the Higgs branch has precisely a residual
U(1)
gauge symmetry
Argyres:1996hc
. Though for
n\geqslant 3
there is not an immediate relation to a higher genus class
\mathcal{S}
theory like the
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
case, we can nevertheless observe that the presence of outer automorphism twist lines means that there is a natural covering space for the UV curve (thought of as the space where the corresponding local system of Dynkin diagrams has no monodromy), and in this example the covering space is genus one. We believe that in the general case of twisted class
\mathcal{S}
theories, it is precisely the value of the genus (zero or nonzero) of this covering space that controls whether a theory in question has a pure or an enhanced Higgs branch. More generally, we take away the lesson that in the presence of twisted punctures, genus zero theories should perhaps nevertheless be thought of as more analgous to higher-genus untwisted theories than genus-zero untwisted theories.

From an algebraic perspective, we can think of the presence of an enhanced Higgs branch as a shadow of an underlying
derived structure
. In particular, the Hall–Littlewood chiral ring for higher genus (untwisted) class
\mathcal{S}
theories is known to include additional fermionic sectors beyond the Higgs branch chiral ring, which can be understood as a consequence of the Higgs branches of these theories being more naturally understood as derived symplectic varieties
CJBDBS:HL
. We propose that for twisted class
\mathcal{S}
theories as well there will be a correspondence between the presence of an enhanced Higgs branch and higher-fermion-number (derived) elements of the Hall–Littlewood chiral ring.

For our purposes in this paper, this nontrivial derived structure is most relevant as an indicator that the BRST cohomology that encodes the associated vertex operator algebra of a gauge theory (see the review in the next section) is not concentrated in degree zero. Consequently, we expect a construction in the twisted setting to be able to mimic the corresponding untwisted genus-zero story only so long as there are not too many twisted punctures. This expectation will be borne out in the detailed analysis of Section
4.6
.

Chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
after Arakawa

{\cal N}=2

{\cal S}

3.1
Critical level current algebras and the Kazhdan–Lusztig category

If an SCFT has a (continuous) global flavour symmetry with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
, then the superconformal primaries in the corresponding conserved current multiplets are moment map operators in the Higgs branch chiral ring, which are Schur operators. Their images in the associated vertex algebra are affine currents that generate an
\hat{\mathfrak{f}}
affine Kac-Moody (AKM) vertex algebra at level

k_{2d}=-\frac{1}{2}k_{4d}~,

(17)

where
k_{4d}
is the (positive for unitary theories) four dimensional flavour central charge. Theories of class
{\cal S}
have flavour symmetries arising from marked points, and for a maximal puncture the four-dimensional level of the
\mathfrak{g}_{u}
or
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
flavour symmetry is
k_{4d}=2h^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u,t}}
. The corresponding
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,t}
current subalgebras in the associated VOA are therefore at the
critical level

k_{2d}=\kappa_{c}=-h^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{g}_{u,t}}
. This is an important value of the level where there is a large null ideal.
3
3

3

In fact, the null ideal is a commutative vertex operator subalgebra (centre) known as the
Feigin–Frenkel centre
, to which we will return momentarily.

Null states in these current subalgebras need not be null with respect to the full VOA. To describe the situation, it is useful to introduce the universal affine vertex algebra
V^{k_{2d}}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
none
of the null states are set to zero,

V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}}^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}}\mathbb{C}=U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)}\mathbb{C}~.

(18)

Here
\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]
acts trivially on
\mathbb{C}
and
K
is the central element, which acts as the constant level
k\in\mathbb{C}
. This can be thought of as the specialisation of the generic-level current algebra to any particular level
k
without taking any further quotient if this turns out to be reducible. (By contrast, taking the maximal quotient gives the simple affine vertex algebra
V_{k_{2d}}(\mathfrak{g})
, whose underlying graded vector space can jump as a function of
k

V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is generically conformal with stress tensor given by the Sugawara construction. At the critical level, this construction fails and the vertex algebra is no longer conformal. Rather it is
quasiconformal
and still satisfies many nice properties one expects from a conformal vertex algebra
Frenkel:2004

Chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
for theories with at least one maximal marked point will have the structure of modules over (potentially several copies of)
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
, and this turns out to be a useful organising structure. The Kazhdan–Lusztig category (at the critical level),
\mathrm{KL}
, is the full subcategory of modules of the universal affine vertex algebra
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
whose objects are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
modules that have a locally finite action of
\mathfrak{g}
,
4
4

4

A module
M
over
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is a smooth
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
modules,
i.e.
, for each
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
m\in M

(xt^{n})m=0
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\forall m\in M~,\quad{\rm dim}\left(U(\mathfrak{g})m\right)<\infty~.

(19)

This definition allows a full
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, so includes modules with conformal weight unbounded below. For a VOA associated to a physical four-dimensional theory thought of as an element of
\mathrm{KL}
, this would be pathological. We take
\mathrm{KL}^{ord}
to denote the full subcategory of
\mathrm{KL}

\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}

An important example of an object in

\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]\oplus\mathbb{C}K}^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}}}V_{\lambda}~,

where

\mathfrak{g}

A vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}

{\cal S}

3.2
The Feigin–Frenkel centre

The critical-level universal affine vertex algebra,
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
has a large centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
known as the Feigin–Frenkel centre. This centre plays a starring role in Arakawa’s construction of chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
, though its properties may not be common knowledge to all four-dimensional physicists.

The Feigin–Frenkel centre is a commutative vertex algebra with strong generators
\{P_{i}(z)\}
of (quasi)conformal weight
\Delta_{i}=d_{i}
, where
d_{i}
are the degrees of the fundamental invariants of
\mathfrak{g}

Frenkel:2007
(see Table
2
). For many purposes, it is useful to work in terms of the universal enveloping algebra of the Fourier modes of the generators of

{\cal Z}

{\cal Z}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[P_{i(n)}~|~i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}]~,

(21)

where we are adopting the mathematician’s conventions for the grading of Fourier modes,

P_{i}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i,(n)}z^{-n-1}~.

(22)

By contrast, the physicist’s gradings are shifted by the (quasi)conformal weights minus one,

P_{i}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i,n}z^{-n-\Delta_{i}}~.

(23)

Two important subalgebras of
{\cal Z}
are as follows,

{\cal Z}_{<0}=\{P_{i,n}\in{\cal Z}~|~n<0\}~,\quad{\cal Z}_{(<0)}=\{P_{i,(n)}\in{\cal Z}~|~n<0\}~.

(24)

The second of these can be identified with
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
itself with respect to the operation of normally ordered product (which is commutative and associative since

is a commutative vertex algebra), while the first includes a subset of modes that encode the singular terms subleading to the zero mode in the OPE action of the

A positive-energy representation of

\mathfrak{g}

The potential for interesting structure then lies with its behaviour as a module over
{\cal Z}_{<0}

\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}\coloneqq{\cal Z}_{<0}/\mathrm{Ann_{{\cal Z}_{<0}}}(\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})~,

(25)

the action of
{\cal Z}_{<0}

3.3
BRST and semi-infinite cohomology

Exactly marginal gauging of flavour symmetries in four dimensions is realised at the level of the associated VOA by a BRST construction
Beem:2013sza
. In particular, the associated VOA of an
\mathfrak{f}
-valued vector multiplet is the subalgebra of an adjoint-valued
(b,c)
ghost system that is annihilated by the zero mode of the
b
ghosts—these are sometimes called symplectic fermions, or the “small algebra” of the ghost system. The vertex algebra of a gauge theory is realised in terms of the vertex algebra
{\cal V}
of the original ungauged theory and these symplectic fermions. One constructs a BRST current,

J_{\rm BRST}(z)=(J_{i}c^{i})(z)+\frac{1}{2}f_{ij}\,^{k}(c^{i}c^{j}b_{k})(z)~,

(27)

where the
J_{i}
generate an
\hat{\mathfrak{f}}
affine current subalgebra of
{\cal V}
and
f_{ij}\,^{k}
are the structure constants of
\mathfrak{f}
. Precisely when that current subalgebra has level
\kappa_{\mathfrak{f}}=-2h^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{f}}
, the zero mode squares to zero so defines a differential on
C^{\bullet}={\cal V}\otimes\langle b,c\rangle
. The grading on
C^{\bullet}
is by ghost number, which in many cases can be identified with
U(1)_{r}
charge.

Restricting to the kernel of
b_{0}
(
i.e.
, dropping the zero-modes of the
c

\mathfrak{f}
invariants defines the relative subcomplex
C_{rel}^{\bullet}
,

C_{rel}^{\bullet}\coloneqq\{v\in{\cal V}\otimes\langle b,c\rangle~|~b^{i}_{(0)}v=0\text{ for}\,i=1,\dots,{\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{f}\}^{\mathfrak{f}}~.

(28)

The BRST differential descends to the relative subcomplex and its cohomology defines the relative BRST cohomology. This gives the vertex algebra of the gauged four dimensional theory. Importantly, one cannot neglect the higher cohomology groups in this construction.
6
6

6

Indeed, in examples such as
{\cal N}=4

\mathfrak{a}_{1}

The above construction can be realised as an example of the semi-infinite cohomology functor of Voronov

{\cal S}
. The state space of the
\mathfrak{f}
-valued ghost system is the same thing as the space of
semi-infinite forms
on
\mathfrak{f}
and denoted
\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}\mathfrak{f}

\hat{\mathfrak{f}}
-module

\kappa_{\mathfrak{f}}

is

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}\left(\hat{\mathfrak{f}},M\right)=\left(\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}\hat{\mathfrak{f}}\otimes M,d\right)~,

(29)

where
d=J_{{\rm BRST},(0)}
. The semi-infinite cohomology of
\hat{\mathfrak{f}}
with coefficients in
M
is just the cohomology of this complex,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}\left(\hat{\mathfrak{f}},M\right)=H^{\bullet}\left(C^{\frac{\infty}{2}}\left(\hat{\mathfrak{f}},M\right),d\right)~.

(30)

Voronov identifies this cohomology as a two-sided derived functor of the functor of semi-variants, and proves an important vanishing theorem that characterises situations where semi-infinite cohomology is concentrated in degree zero.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Theorem 2.1 of
Voronov:1993aw
)
Let

be a semijective module, i.e., it is injective as a
\mathfrak{f}_{+}

\mathfrak{f}_{-}
module. Then

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\mathfrak{f},M)=\begin{cases}\makebox[19.91684pt][l]{$M^{\mathfrak{f}_{+}}_{\mathfrak{f}_{-}}$}\quad p=0~,\\
\makebox[19.91684pt][l]{$0$}\quad p\neq 0~,\end{cases}

(31)

where
M^{\mathfrak{f}_{+}}_{\mathfrak{f}_{-}}
is the projection of
\mathfrak{f}_{+}
-invariants onto the
\mathfrak{f}_{-}
-coinvariants. More concretely,

M^{\mathfrak{f}_{+}}_{\mathfrak{f}_{-}}=\{m\in M\,|\,\mathfrak{f}_{+}m=0\}{\bigg/}\{m\in M\,|\,\mathfrak{f}_{+}m=0\quad\text{and}\quad m=xm^{\prime}\,\,\,\text{for some }x\in\mathfrak{f}_{-}\}~.

(32)

The relative BRST cohomology described above is also understood as relative semi-infinite cohomology with respect to the
\mathfrak{f}\subset\hat{\mathfrak{f}}_{\kappa}
. As in
Voronov:1993aw
;
Arakawa:2018egx

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{f}}_{\kappa},\mathfrak{f},M)~,

(33)

so the prescription for gauging a flavour symmetry
\mathfrak{f}

{\cal S}

V_{1}
and

\mathfrak{g}
then amounts to taking the relative semi-infinite cohomology,

V_{1}\circ V_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},V_{1}\otimes V_{2})~.

(34)

An application of Theorem
3.1
in the relative context gives the following vanishing theorem, which is important in establishing generalized
S
-duality at the level of vertex algebras.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Section 3.11.2 of
Voronov:1993aw
and Proposition 3.4 of
Arakawa:2018egx
)
Suppose
V\in\mathrm{KL}_{-\kappa_{g}}
and
V
is projective as a
U(t^{-1}\mathfrak{g}[t^{-1}])
module and injective as a
U(t\mathfrak{g}[t])
module, i.e.,

is a semijective object in
\mathrm{KL}

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},V)=0\quad\text{for }p\neq 0~.

(35)

3.4
Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction

Starting with chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
for theories with only maximal punctures, one realises the VOAs with non-maximal punctures by a chiral analogue of the Higgsing procedure described in Section
2
. Namely, this type of Higgsing is accomplished via quantum Drinfel’d--Sokolov (DS) reduction, which we review briefly below.
7
7

7

This identification of Higgsing with DS reduction was presented as a
conjecture
in
Beem:2014rza
. It remains an open problem to prove this from first principles—there is by now extensive evidence for the claim.

A nontrivial homomorphism
\Lambda:\mathfrak{su}(2)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
induces a grading on
\mathfrak{g}
by the image of the Cartan generator,
8
8

8

If the grading is genuinely half-integral and not integral, then there is a subtlety and one must introduce an alternate Cartan generator, whose grading is integral, and impose modified constraints in what follows. See
Feher:1992ed
;
deBoer:1992sy
;
deBoer:1993iz
for more details.

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{m\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{m}\,,\quad\mathfrak{g}_{m}\coloneqq\{t\in\mathfrak{g}\,|\,\mathrm{ad}_{\Lambda(h)}\,(t)=mt\}~.

(36)

Let
\{t_{\alpha}\}
denote a basis for the negatively graded part of
\mathfrak{g}
, with corresponding affine currents
J_{\alpha}
in
V^{k_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
. Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction can be thought of as imposing the constraint

J_{\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha\alpha_{-}}~,

(37)

where
t_{\alpha_{-}}
is the image of the negative weight generator
f\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
. This constraint is imposed via BRST by introducing a ghost system
(c^{\alpha},b_{\alpha})
valued in the adjoint of
\bigoplus_{m<0}\mathfrak{g}_{m}
and forming the BRST current

J_{DS}(z)=\sum_{\alpha}\left((J_{\alpha}-\delta_{\alpha\alpha_{-}})c^{\alpha}\right)(z)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\gamma}(b_{\gamma}c^{\alpha}c^{\beta})(z)~,

(38)

with the BRST differential
d_{DS}=J_{{\rm DS},(0)}
. We define the DS reduction of a vertex algebra
{\cal V}
, which has the structure of a
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
module, as the cohomology

H^{\bullet}_{f,DS}(V)=H_{\rm BRST}^{\bullet}(V\otimes\langle c^{\alpha},b_{\beta}\rangle,d_{DS})~.

(39)

Taking the coefficients to be exactly
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
gives the
{\cal W}
-algebra
{\cal W}(f,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa})=H^{0}_{f,DS}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
.

Like the BRST operation associated to gauging, DS reduction can be phrased in terms of semi-infinite cohomology (see,
e.g.
,
Arakawa2017introduction
). We will make extensive use of the result
Arakawa:2007rep
.

Theorem 3.3

(Main Theorem 1 of
Arakawa:2007rep
)
.

For any
M\in\mathrm{KL}
,
H^{i}_{f,DS}(M)=0
for
i\neq 0
.

The case of principal DS reduction at the critical level is especially interesting because
{\cal W}(f_{prin},\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}})\cong\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. For non vacuum modules, there is an isomorphism
Frenkel:2010

H^{0}_{f_{prin},DS}(\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})\cong\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}~.

(40)

In general we get an exact functor
H^{0}_{f_{prin},DS}:\mathrm{KL}\rightarrow{\cal Z}
-Mod. We will mostly be interested in principal DS reduction in what follows, and will suppress the explicit
f_{prin}
argument from now on.

Pictorially, principal DS reduction corresponds to removing a puncture. The key idea in the construction of chiral algebras of class
\mathcal{S}
is to develop a method to
invert
this operation and add punctures back onto the UV curve. We discuss this in the following sections.

3.5
Feigin–Frenkel gluing

For chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
with maximal punctures, the current subalgebras are all at the critical level, so there could theoretically be several copies of the Feigin–Frenkel centre present. This turns out to not be the case; in fact the current subalgebras all
share a common Feigin–Frenkel centre
, so the chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
with
s
punctures are modules over
\mathbb{V}_{0,s}
.

This phenomenon of shared FF centres is a chiral analogue of certain well-known Higgs branch chiral ring relations for theories of class
{\cal S}
. We recall that for theory of type
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
associated to a UV curve with
s
punctures, there are
s
moment map operators
\mu_{s}
subject to the relations

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\mu_{1}^{k}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\mu_{2}^{k}=\cdots=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\mu_{s}^{k}~,\qquad k=2,\ldots,n+1~.

(41)

More generally, for the
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
theories there are analogous relations corresponding to the respective fundamental invariants of those algebras. These play a central role in the construction of Higgs branches of class
{\cal S}
by Ginzburg–Kazhdan
Ginzburg:2018
.

It is not immediate that these Higgs chiral ring relations lead to the identification of Feigin--Frenkel centres. This is because the Feigin--Frenkel generators are related to, but not equal to, the corresponding Higgs branch operators.
9
9

9

To be precise, the Feigin–Frenkel operators are identified with the corresponding Higgs branch operators upon passing to the associated graded of the
R
filtration
Beem:2017ooy
.
To illustrate, consider the case
k=2
of (
41
). The Higgs branch operators associated to the quadratic fundamental invariant are related to the Segal–Sugawara operators,
P_{1,i}
but also receive a nonzero contribution from the VOA stress-energy tensor
T

Beem:2018duj
,

P_{1,i}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\mu^{2}_{i}+\alpha T~,

(42)

where
\alpha
is a fixed (nonzero) constant computed in
Beem:2018duj
and
P_{1,i}
is the quadratic Feigin–Frenkel generator associated to the
i
’th puncture. The Higgs chiral ring relations set the
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\mu^{2}_{i}
equal and, importantly, there is a
unique

\hat{{\cal C}}_{0(0,0)}
multiplet (the four-dimensional stress tensor multiplet) so the operator
T
is the same for each
i
. As a result, the quadratic generators of the Feigin–Frenkel centre are identified across different punctures,
i.e.
,
P_{1,1}=P_{1,2}=\dots P_{1,s}
. For higher order invariants, the same argument doesn’t go through without additional information about the structure of the VOA; in the case of the cubic invariant of
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
there will be mixing between Higgs branch operators (
\widehat{\cal B}_{3}
multiplets) and
{\cal C}_{1(0,0)}
multiplets, but the uniqueness of the latter is not apparent. Nevertheless, precisely for the class
\mathcal{S}
theories, the known expression for the Schur index indicates that the identification of the higher Feigin–Frenkel generators should indeed hold.

As in the definition of
\mathbb{V}_{0,2}
, given two modules
M,N\in{\cal Z}
-Mod, we can equip
M\otimes N
with a
{\cal Z}
-Mod structure via a twisted diagonal action,

P\otimes 1-1\otimes\tau(P)\quad\forall P\in{\cal Z}~.

(43)

The twist by
\tau
is a matter of convention, but will prove convenient in later constructions when Feigin–Frenkel gluing and gauging appear together—roughly speaking, one can anticipate this issue by observing that when gauging together two VOAs, the gauge invariant states arise from pairing representations with highest weights
\lambda
and
\lambda^{*}
respectively, so if one wishes to be able to identify a common action of
{\cal Z}
on these two representations then there should be a twist by
\tau
.

As an abelian Lie algebra,
{\cal Z}
has a semi-infinite structure and so can be used to define semi-infinite cohomology with coefficients in some object of
{\cal Z}
-Mod. Specifically, we will have coefficients of the form
M\otimes N
for
M,N\in{\cal Z}
-Mod. The Feigin standard complex is then given by

C^{\bullet}({\cal Z},M\otimes N)\coloneqq M\otimes N\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}{\cal Z}~,

(44)

with BRST current

Q(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(P_{i}c^{i})(z)~,

(45)

whose zero mode
Q_{(0)}
acts as a differential for the cochain complex. Suppose we have two objects
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
{\cal Z}
-Mod, we define Feigin–Frenkel (FF) gluing as

V_{1}\ast V_{2}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},V_{1}\otimes V_{2})~.

(46)

In the case when
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are vertex algebras,
V_{1}\ast V_{2}
is also a vertex algebra. The vector space
\mathbf{V}_{1}\otimes V_{2}
also has the structure of a
{\cal Z}
-module via the left (or right) action of
{\cal Z}
on the tensor factors, which have become identified through the gluing.

As was the case with the gauge theory BRST problem, we have a vanishing theorem here.

Theorem 3.4

(Theorem 9.10 of
Arakawa:2018egx
)
.

Let
M\in{\cal Z}
-Mod be free as a
{\cal Z}_{(<0)}
module, then

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+i}({\cal Z},M)=0\quad\textrm{for}~~i<0~.

This is a weaker conclusion than in the vanishing theorem
3.2
, as the cohomology is not necessarily concentrated in degree zero. Nevertheless for many purposes it is sufficient.
10
10

10

From a derived perspective, truncating the cohomology at degree zero is somewhat unnatural. We will return to this point in a later section.

We see that the BRST procedure enforces that the action of the Feigin–Frenkel centre on
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are identified. This is a chiral version of the Hamiltonian reduction procedure described in
Ginzburg:2018
.

3.6
Vertex algebras at genus zero

The pieces are now all in place to recount the construction of (genus zero) untwisted chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
from

.

Chiral differential operators and the cylinder

The starting point of the construction of
Arakawa:2018egx
is the cylinder VOA; from here one can define the cap chiral algebra by Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction and, it turns out, construct all genus zero VOAs by FF gluing. The form of the cylinder algebra for
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
theories was identified concretely in
Beem:2014rza
, but it was subsequently recognised in
Arakawa:2015ro
that this reproduced a more general, and purely algebraic, construction that makes no explicit reference to four dimensional physics. The construction is universal and depends only on the choice of an algebraic group
G
, which here we take to be semisimple.

Starting from such a
G
, the arc space
J_{\infty}G
is a scheme over
\mathbb{C}
defined as

J_{\infty}G\coloneqq Hom_{Sch/\mathbb{C}}(D,G)~,

(47)

where
D
is the formal disc
D=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
. See
Arakawa2017introduction
for more concrete discussion of arc spaces. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=Lie(G)
acts on
{\cal O}(G)
via derivations, and this action lifts to one of
J_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]
on
{\cal O}(J_{\infty}G)
. Therefore,
{\cal O}(J_{\infty}G)
has the structure of a
\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]\oplus\mathbb{C}K
module where
K
acts as the level
k\in\mathbb{C}
. One can further produce a
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}}
module via induction, which defines the
chiral differential operators
on
G
(at the critical level)
ag
(see also
Gorbounov:1992
;
Gorbounov:2001
;
Gorbounov:2004
;
Malikov:1999
),

\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}=U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}[\![t]\!]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)}{\cal O}(J_{\infty}G)~.

(48)

This has the structure of a conformal vertex algebra
Gorbounov:2001
with central charge

c_{2d}=2\,{\rm dim}\,G~,

(49)

which matches with the central charge for a twice punctured sphere (see,
e.g.
,
Chacaltana:2012zy
).

By construction,
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
is a vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}
and there is an embedding of the universal affine vertex algebra
\pi_{L}:V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
. This vertex algebra homomorphism is induced by the embedding of
\mathfrak{g}
as left invariant vector fields of
G
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is also isomorphic to the right invariant vector fields of
G
and this embedding is also lifted to a vertex algebra homomorphism
\pi_{R}:V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
, such that the images of
\pi_{L}
and
\pi_{R}
commute
Gorbounov:2001
. The left and right embeddings of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
restrict to embeddings of the Feigin–Frenkel centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and the two embeddings of the Feigin–Frenkel centre coincide
frenkel2004
,

\pi_{L}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong\pi_{R}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G})^{\mathfrak{g}[t]\times\mathfrak{g}[t]}~.

(50)

\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
is free as a module over
U(t^{-1}\mathfrak{g}[t^{-}1])
and cofree over
U(t\mathfrak{g}[t])

Arakawa:2018egx
; thus the conditions of Theorem
3.2
are met and for any
M\in\mathrm{KL}
, the cohomology
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}\circ M
is concentrated in degree zero, and furthermore
Arakawa:2018egx
,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+i}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}\otimes M)\cong\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+i}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},M\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G})\cong\delta_{i,0}M~.

(51)

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
is the unique vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}
, which satisfies this property. Physically, the chiral algebra of the cylinder must act as the identity object under
\circ
, when restricted to the chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
. This is a weaker condition than what is satisfied by
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
. One could a imagine situation in which the chiral algebra of the cylinder acts as the identity for the chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
but not on all of
\mathrm{KL}
. In this case, the chiral algebra of the cylinder may not necessarily be isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
. However, this pathological possibility seems to not be realised.

Equivariant affine
{\cal W}
-algebras and the cap

Starting from the cylinder VOA, the cap algebra is recovered by completely reducing one maximal puncture. Arakawa has named the resulting VOA the (principal)
equivariant affine
W
-algebra

\mathbf{W}_{G}
(it is an affine analogue of the equivariant
W
-algebra of
Losev:2007Qua

\mathbf{W}_{G}\coloneqq H^{0}_{DS}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G})~.

(52)

In the usual way, this vertex algebra inherits a conformal structure from the cylinder, with central charge

c_{\mathbf{W}_{G}}={\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}+24\kappa_{g}(\rho,\rho^{\vee})~,

(53)

where
\rho
is half the sum of all positive roots and
\rho^{\vee}
is the half sum of all positive coroots.

By Propositions
6.4
and
6.5
of
Arakawa:2018egx
,
\mathbf{W}_{G}
is free over
U(t^{-1}\mathfrak{g}[t^{-1}])
and cofree over

, and so is the

module

. The cohomology when gauging is therefore concentrated in degree zero. Theorem
6.8
of
Arakawa:2018egx
then establishes that for any

\mathrm{KL}
,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},\mathbf{W}_{G}\otimes M)=H^{0}_{DS}(M)~.

(54)

When
M
is a chiral algebra of class
{\cal S}
, this corresponds, pictorially, to the fact that gluing a cap and a surface together along a maximal puncture has the effect of closing the puncture.

Feigin–Frenkel gluing a cap onto another vertex algebra provides a sort of inverse to the principal DS reduction functor; by FF gluing a cap onto a vertex algebra
V\in{\cal Z}
-Mod we provide it with a
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
action, and it becomes a vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}
. The cap is free over
{\cal Z}_{(<0)}

Arakawa:2018egx
and so
\mathbf{W}_{G}\ast-:{\cal Z}\text{-Mod}\rightarrow\mathrm{KL}
is a left-exact functor that acts, almost, as an inverse to

V
in
\mathrm{KL}
, Theorem
9.11
of
Arakawa:2018egx
guarantees that

V\cong\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},\mathbf{W}_{G}\otimes H^{0}_{DS}(V))~,

(55)

and for the subcategory of
V
-module objects in
\mathrm{KL}
,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},\mathbf{W}_{G}\otimes H^{0}_{DS}(-))~,

(56)

is the identity functor. Indeed, this means that the cylinder VOA can be recovered from the equivariant affine
W
-algebra by FF gluing two caps together
Arakawa:2018egx
,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},\mathbf{W}_{G}\otimes\mathbf{W}_{G})\cong\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}~.

(57)

This is only almost an inverse functor because
\mathrm{KL}
and
{\cal Z}
-Mod are not equivalent. If we restrict to the subcategory
\mathrm{KL}_{0}
of
{\cal Z}
-Mod, which is the image of

, then
H^{0}_{DS}(\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},\mathbf{W}_{G}\otimes V))\cong V
for any

General genus zero VOAs

This situation leads to a conceptually straightforward construction of all genus zero chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
: one starts with
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}
and repeatedly applies
\mathbf{W}\ast-
to add punctures. If one takes for granted the general expectations of chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
as outlined in
Beem:2014rza
, then this must give the correct answer. However, the formalism of
Arakawa:2018egx
allows Arakawa to go further and demonstrate,
a priori
, the expected behaviour of these VOAs
vis à vis
their gluing and duality properties.

To this end, the construction given in
Arakawa:2018egx
is superficially different, amounting to gluing together
s
cap VOAs
at once
rather than iteratively adding punctures. Take the chain complex

C^{\bullet}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s-1}{\cal Z}^{i,i+1},\mathbf{W}^{s}\right)\coloneqq\mathbf{W}^{s}\otimes\bigg(\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))\bigg)^{s-1}~,

(58)

for
s>1
, with differential

\begin{split}Q(z)&=\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}Q^{i,i+1}(z)~,\\
Q^{i,i+1}(z)&=\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(\rho_{i}(P_{j})-\rho_{i+1}(\tau(P_{j})))\rho_{gh,i}(c^{j})(z)~,\end{split}

(59)

where
\rho_{i}
represents the action of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
on the
i
-th factor of
\mathbf{W}
and
\rho_{gh,i}(c^{j})
acts on the
i
-th factor of the ghost system
\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))
. The vertex algebra of a sphere of type
\mathfrak{g}
with
s
maximal punctures is then defined to be

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}_{G,1}&=\mathbf{W}~,\\
\mathbf{V}_{G,s}&=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s-1}{\cal Z}^{i,i+1},\mathbf{W}^{s})~.\end{split}

(60)

It is a lemma of
Arakawa:2018egx
, that this agrees with the recursive construction of the genus zero vertex algebras.

As a consequence of their construction in terms of the cylinder VOA, the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
are endowed with an increasing filtration
Arakawa:2018egx
,
{\cal F}^{\bullet}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
such that
{\cal F}^{0}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}=0
and

\frac{{\cal F}^{i}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}}{{\cal F}^{i-1}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}}\cong\mathbb{V}_{s,\lambda}~,

(61)

for some integral, dominant weight
\lambda
. They further possess a decreasing filtration
{\cal G}^{\bullet}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
, with
{\cal G}^{0}\mathbf{V}_{G,s}=\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
and successive quotients isomorphic to
D(\mathbb{V}_{s,\lambda})
for some integral dominant
\lambda
. This means that the genus zero chiral algebras are limits of projective and injective objects in
\mathrm{KL}
, and so, importantly, satisfy the requirements of Theorem
3.2
. This means that when gluing these VOAs together via gauge theory BRST, the cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},\mathbf{V}_{G,s}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{G,s^{\prime}})
is concentrated in degree zero, and via a spectral sequence argument this establishes the associativity of gauging for the genus zero chiral algebras.
11
11

11

Furthermore, all cohomologies being concentrated in degree zero is compatible with the expectation that in genus zero there is no residual gauge symmetry on the Higgs branch, and so no Hall–Littlewood chiral ring beyond the Higgs chiral ring.

An interesting feature of this construction is that it trivialises
S
-duality. The caps involved in the construction are identical and the gluing is done simultaneously. The
\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
are manifestly invariant under permutations. Since the construction makes no reference to a choice of pants decomposition, there is a disconnect to the physical chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
—built out of the BRST gauging of trinion algebras. Arakawa has shown in
Arakawa:2018egx
that the operations of FF-gluing and BRST gauging are compatible, in the sense that the cap gluing construction agrees with the pants decomposition.

4
Twisted trinions from mixed Feigin–Frenkel gluing

Arakawa’s construction is universal and is well defined for any semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
---including non simply laced Lie algebras.
12
12

12

The same is true for the putative construction of Moore–Tachikawa symplectic varieties in
Ginzburg:2018
.
At the same time, there is no construction for vertex algebras that have both
\mathfrak{g}_{u}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
symmetries (both twisted and untwisted punctures). A calculation comparing the character of
Arakawa:2018egx
and the index of

. There are some notable exceptions; namely, the index of the twisted cylinder and the character of

agree. Consequently, so must the index of the twisted cap and the character of
\mathbf{W}_{G_{t}}
.
13
13

13

Strictly speaking, the characters of the cylinder and cap VOAs don’t exist due to infinite-dimensional weight spaces. However, one can proceed formally by working term-by-term in the sum over integral dominant weights; this can be understood from a vertex algebra perspective as considering the decomposition into blocks belonging to
\mathrm{KL}^{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in P^{+}
.
This suggests that
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G_{t}}
and
\mathbf{W}_{G_{t}}
will be key building blocks for our construction; indeed, the algebras associated to the sphere with two maximal twisted punctures and one maximal/one minimal twisted puncture should be identified with
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G_{t}}
and
\mathbf{W}_{G_{t}}
, respectively.

In this section, we posit a construction for more general genus zero mixed vertex algebras. Our proposal takes advantage of Feigin–Frenkel gluing to define the mixed trinion with one untwisted and two twisted punctures. From this, in principle, all other genus zero vertex algebras can be constructed. Recalling our discussion of residual gauge symmetries in Section
2.4
, we have some expectations regarding the behaviour of these mixed vertex algebras under gauging. In particular, unlike the untwisted case, we expect that the BRST cohomology of the
untwisted
gauging of two such vertex algebras will not be concentrated in degree zero, but will rather enjoy some derived enhancement.

To establish such properties regarding untwisted and twisted gauging, we need to examine the decomposition of the mixed vertex algebras into semijective objects in both twisted and untwisted
\mathrm{KL}
categories. This will be depend in a crucial way on the structure of critical-level Weyl modules over
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,\kappa_{c}}
as modules over the untwisted Feigin–Frenkel centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
. This will lead us to a technical interlude involving opers.

In this section we assume that we are dealing with commuting twists, either
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. We will remark on some peculiarities of the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
case near the end of the section.

4.1
The (un)twisted Feigin–Frenkel centre and the mixed trinion

The mixed vertex algebras should simultaneously be vertex algebra objects in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
and in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}

4.4
Rearrangment lemmas

Having established many key properties of the mixed trinion, which is the building block of the twisted chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
, we would like to extend our results to other genus zero, mixed vertex algebras. As we increase the number of twisted and untwisted punctures, we are naturally required to consider how the various types of gluing interact with each other. It will be useful in this endeavour to have a collection of rearrangement lemmas that establish the extent to which the various gluings associate.

In this subsection we establish a series of technical rearrangement lemmas concerning the interplay between the various cohomological operations we have defined thus far. The proofs of these lemmas, which are modifications of proofs of

Now we come to the rearrangement of twisted and untwisted gluing. The results here are more limited; the obvious generalisations of the above spectral sequence arguments often fail in the twisted setting, since the mixed vertex algebras are not semijective in

. Nevertheless, we will manage to demonstrate that some properties of the gluing of twisted algebras are as we expect. To start with, we have a result for the interchange of twisted and untwisted gauging.

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose
V_{1}
is in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
,
V_{2}
is in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
and in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
, and
V_{3}
is in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
. Furthermore, suppose that
V_{3}
is semijective for
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
. Then, we have the isomorphism

V_{1}\circ_{u}(V_{2}\circ_{t}V_{3})\cong(V_{1}\circ_{u}V_{2})\circ_{t}V_{3}~.

Proof.

As usual, the proof is via a spectral sequence, but with the new feature that cohomology is not necessarily concentrated in degree zero. We define the bicomplex

C^{p,q}=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes V_{3}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u})\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t})~,

with the differential
d_{u}
and
d_{t}
acting on
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u})
and
V_{2}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t})
respectively. The differentials anticommute, so we can form the total complex
C_{tot}^{m}=\bigoplus_{p+q=m}C^{p,q}
with the differential
d_{tot}=d_{u}+(-1)^{p}d_{t}
. There are two spectral sequences converging to the total cohomology, given by

\begin{split}{}_{I}E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},V_{1}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{t},V_{2}\otimes V_{3}))~,\\
{}_{II}E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{t},\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},V_{1}\otimes V_{2})\otimes V_{3})~.\end{split}

Since
V_{3}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, the spectral sequences will collapse on the second page and
{}_{I}E^{p,q}_{2}=0
for
q\neq 0
and
{}_{II}E^{p,q}_{2}=0
for
p\neq 0
. Thus we have the isomorphism

H^{m}_{tot}=\,_{I}E^{m,0}_{2}=\,_{II}E^{0,m}_{2}~.

∎

We can also show that the twisted gauging and untwisted Feigin–Frenkel gluing are nicely compatible,

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
M
be an object of
{\cal Z}_{u}
-Mod such that it is free over
{\cal Z}_{u,<0}
and let
W
be semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
. Suppose,
V
is a vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
, then we have the follwoing isomorphism.

M\ast_{u}(V\circ_{t}W)\cong(M\ast_{u}V)\circ_{t}W~.

Proof.

Let
C^{p,q}
be the bicomplex

C^{p,q}=M\otimes V\otimes W\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t})~,

with the differentials
d_{\mathfrak{z}}
acting on
M\otimes V\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))
and
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
acting on
V\otimes W\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}_{t}})
. The differentials
d_{\mathfrak{z}}
and
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
anticommute, so we can form the total complex
C^{m}_{tot}=\bigoplus_{p+q=m}C^{p,q}
with the total differential
d_{tot}=d_{\mathfrak{z}}+(-1)^{p}d_{\mathfrak{g}}
. There are two spectral sequences converging to the total cohomology, given by

\begin{split}{}_{I}E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}({\cal Z}_{u},M\otimes\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{t},V\otimes W))~,\\
{}_{II}E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{t},\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}({\cal Z}_{u},M\otimes V)\otimes W)~.\end{split}

The cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{t},-\otimes W)
is concentrated in degree zero, so both spectral sequence will collapse in the second page. Thus, we have the isomorphism

H^{0}_{tot}(C_{tot},d_{tot})\cong_{I}E^{0,0}_{2}\cong_{II}E^{0,0}_{2}~.

∎

4.5
Mixed vertex algebras at genus zero

We first give a construction of the vertex algebras associated to spheres with only one pair of twisted punctures:
{\cal C}_{0,m,1}
before considering the more general case. As in the untwisted case, one could provide a recursive definition of
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
by repeatedly gluing untwisted caps. We elect, instead, to perform a simultaneous gluing and will show the equivalence between the two definitions later on. We define the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
by

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}_{0,1}&\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}~,\\
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}&\coloneqq H^{0}(C_{m,1},Q^{m})~,\end{split}

(86)

where
C_{m,1}
is the chain complex

C^{\bullet}_{m,1}=\mathbf{W}_{u}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\otimes\bigg(\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))\bigg)^{m}~,

(87)

with differential

\begin{split}Q^{m}(z)&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Q^{i,i+1}~,\\
Q^{i,i+1}(z)&=\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{u}}:(\rho_{i}(P_{j})-\rho_{i+1}(\tau(P_{j})))\rho_{gh_{i}}(c^{j}):(z)~.\end{split}

(88)

Here
\rho_{i}
, for
i\leqslant m
denotes the action of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
on the
i
th factor of
\mathbf{W}_{u}
and
\rho_{m+1}
denotes the action of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
on
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
along the projection
{\cal Z}_{u}\twoheadrightarrow{\cal Z}_{t}
. The vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
live entirely in cohomological degree zero no fermions because we restrict to the zeroth cohomology—this is compatible with our expectation on the basis of residual gauge symmetries. We will later reinforce this by showing that the cohomology of
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\circ_{t}\mathbf{V}_{n,1}
is concentrated in degree zero.

The naive generalisation to
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
of our previous construction would be to take the zeroth cohomology of

C^{\bullet}_{m,n}=\mathbf{W}^{m}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{G_{t},2n}\otimes\bigg(\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))\bigg)^{m}~.

(89)

However, this cannot be quite right. Indeed, the vertex algebras associated to
{\cal C}_{0,m,n}
for
n>1
should be supported outside of cohomological degree zero in order to express the presence of enhanced Higgs branches for the corresponding SCFTs. On the other hand, the vertex algebras,
\mathbf{W}_{u}
and
\mathbf{V}_{G_{t},2n}
, lie in degree zero, and the truncation to zeroth cohomology means this will persist. One should also expect to see a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry exchanging positive and negative cohomological degree (a shadow of CPT in four dimensions). However, the untwisted caps
\mathbf{W}_{u}
are projective over
{\cal Z}_{u,(<0)}
and as a consequence the cohomology vanishes in negative degree. Therefore, even if we do not truncate to degree zero, the resulting vertex algebra would not have the right form.

Thus we will have to define the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
, by going to some (non-canonically chosen) duality frame. We will choose to recursively consider the decomposition of
{\cal C}_{0,m+1,n-1}
as
{\cal C}_{0,m+1,n-1}
and
{\cal C}_{0,1,1}
connected by an untwisted cylinder. This gives us our definition:

\mathbf{V}_{m,n}\coloneqq\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n-1}~.

(90)

As defined, it is not clear whether the
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
are independent of our choice of duality frame in which to define them. For example, we could have also obtained this from a twisted gluing of
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
to
\mathbf{V}_{m-1,n}
. This is just the vertex algebra question of generalised
S
-duality, which is now not made manifest by our definition (
90
). In the following sections, we shall work to establish how our definitions fit in with the duality web of class
{\cal S}
.

4.6
Properties of the genus zero mixed vertex algebras

First, we will check that our definition of the mixed vertex algebras with
n=1
agrees with the recursive definition,
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\overset{?}{\cong}\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m-1,1}
. We have an extension of Lemma
10.1
of Arakawa to the twisted case.

Lemma 4.13
.

For
m\geqslant 1
, we have that

(i)

H^{n}(C_{m,1},Q^{m-1}_{(0)})\cong 0\text{ for }n<0~,

(ii)

\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m-1,1}\cong\mathbf{V}_{m,1}~.

Proof.

This proof is largely adapted from the proof of Lemma
10.1
in
Arakawa:2018egx
.
We proceed by induction on
m
. For the base case,
m=1
,
(i)
is true, since
\mathbf{W}_{u}
is projective over
{\cal Z}_{u,(<0)}
. The second statement is true by definition, since
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\coloneqq\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
. Next, suppose
m>1
and consider the bicomplex

C^{\bullet\bullet}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes C_{m-1,1}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))~,

with differentials
Q^{m-1}_{(0)}
acting on
C_{m-1,1}
and
d
acting on
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes C_{m-1,1}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))
. The two differentials anticommute and the corresponding total complex is just
C_{m,1}
with differential
Q^{m}_{(0)}
. There is a spectral sequence, with second page

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes H^{q}(C_{m-1,1},Q^{m-1}_{(0)}))~,

which converges to the total cohomology. By the inductive assumption,
H^{q}(C_{m,1},Q^{m-1}_{(0)})
vanishes for
q<0
and
\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}-
is left exact. Therefore,
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
for
p,q<0
, so
H^{n}(C_{m,1},Q^{m}_{(0)})
vanishes for
n<0
. Moreover, the entry
E_{2}^{0,0}
is stable and we have

E_{2}^{0,0}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m-1,1}\cong H^{0}(C_{m,1},Q^{m}_{(0)})\cong\mathbf{V}_{m,1}~,

(91)

as desired.
∎

Next, we examine the case of untwisted DS reduction.

Lemma 4.14
.

The mixed vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
are objects in
\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}
. In particular, for
m\geqslant 0
and
n\geqslant 1
,

H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n})\cong\mathbf{V}_{m,n}~.

Proof.

We proceed by double induction on
m
and
n
, first examining the base case of
n=1
and arbitrary
m
. Note that for
m=0
,
H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{1,1})=\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
from Theorem
4.3
. Next, suppose
m>0
; for any object in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
,
H^{0}_{DS}(u,-)
and
\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}-
are isomorphic. Therefore,

H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+1,1})\cong\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+1,1}\cong\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m,1})~,

where we have used Lemma
4.13
. Consider the bicomplex

C^{\bullet\bullet}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\otimes\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}})~,

with differential
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
acting on
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}})
and
d_{\mathfrak{z}}
acting on
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\otimes\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))
. The two differentials anticommute, so we form the total complex
C^{n}_{tot}=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}C^{pq}
, with total differential
d_{tot}=d_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{q}d_{\mathfrak{z}}
. There are two spectral sequences converging to the total cohomology, given by

\begin{split}{}_{I}E^{pq}_{2}&=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\otimes\mathbf{W}_{u}))~,\\
{}_{II}E^{pq}_{2}&=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{m,1})\otimes W_{u})~.\end{split}

The cohomology,
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},-\otimes\mathbf{W}_{u})
is concentrated in degree zero, and
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+i}({\cal Z}_{u}\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes-)

\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m,1})=\ _{I}E^{00}_{2}\cong\ _{II}E^{00}_{2}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m,1})~.

Therefore,

H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+1,1})\cong\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m,1})\cong\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}(H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m,1})~,

but
\mathbf{W}_{u}\ast_{u}(H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m,1}))\cong\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
by Theorem 9.11 of
Arakawa:2018egx
.

Now suppose
n>1
. Then, we have that

H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n})\cong\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}(\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+2,n-1})~.

Since,
\mathbf{W}_{u}
is semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
, Lemma
4.8
applies and we have that

\begin{split}H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n})&\cong\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{u}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+2,n-1})~,\\
&\cong\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}(H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m+2,n-1}))~,\\
&\cong\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n-1}~,\end{split}

where we have used the inductive assumption. Of course,
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}\mathbf{V}_{m+1,n-1}
is
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
by definition and we are done.
∎

Our uniqueness result for
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
can now be extended to all of the genus zero vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
. To reiterate,
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
is the unique vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}^{\otimes m}
such that
H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m-1,n})
.

Using Lemma
4.14
, we can present our version of Proposition

of

.

Proposition 4.15
.

The vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
have an ascending filtration whose successive quotients are

\mathbb{V}^{u}_{\lambda,m}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda*})~,

for some
\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}
and
\iota(\lambda)\in P_{u}^{+}
. Additionally, the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}

Therefore, the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
are semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}

\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\circ_{t}V\cong\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g},\mathbf{V}_{m,1}\otimes V)~.

First, note that the objects
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda,m}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda*})\big)
are in
\mathrm{KL}_{u},0

. We proceed via induction, noting that for the base case

Suppose the statement is true for some

,
i.e.
,
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
has a filtration

0=N_{0}\subset N_{1}\subset\dots\subset N=\bigcup_{i}N_{i}~,

with successive quotients
N_{i}/N_{i-1}\cong\mathbb{V}_{\lambda,m}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}}((\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda*})
for some

\iota(\lambda)\in P_{u}^{+}

One could hope to strengthen this to the statement that all
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
are semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
but such a result is beyond what is easily realised by our technologies. With the current arguments, we would have to independently establish that the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{0,n}
are semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}

From a similar argument as with
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
, the mixed vertex algebra,
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}

\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\circ_{u}-

On the other hand, this is a rather large roadblock to our spectral sequence powered proofs of associativity. We will only be able to provide partial results for how
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
fit in the duality web.

Similar to the mixed trinion, we can derive the characters and central charges of the mixed vertex algebras.

Proposition 4.16
.

The character of the vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
is given by

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{V}_{m,1}}\bigg(q^{L_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{a}_{i}\,\,\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{2}\bigg)=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\cal K}_{u}(\mathbf{a}_{i})\chi^{\lambda}_{u}(\mathbf{a}_{i}){\cal K}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\chi^{\lambda}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{1}){\cal K}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{2})\chi^{\lambda}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{2})}{\big({\cal K}_{u}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{u}(\times)\big)^{m}}~.

so the vertex algebra,
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
is conical for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

For
m=1

4.5
, so we take
m>1
. We have that
H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{m,1})\cong\mathbf{V}_{m-1,1}
. As graded vector spaces, we have the decomposition

\mathbf{V}_{m-1,1}\cong\sum_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}\mathbb{V}^{u}_{\lambda,m-1}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}}(\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda*})~.

Applying Proposition 8.4 of
Arakawa:2018egx
gives us the desired result. The fact that
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
are conical follows from the same argument as Proposition
4.5
.
∎

The vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
is constructed by repeatedly gauging
\mathbf{V}_{m+n-1,1}
with copies of
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
. We know how the character behaves under gauging, so this result can be extended to

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{V}_{m,n}}\bigg(q^{L_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{a}_{i}\,\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\mathbf{b}_{j}\bigg)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\cal K}_{u}(\mathbf{a}_{i})\chi^{\lambda}_{u}(\mathbf{a}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{2n}{\cal K}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{j})\chi^{\lambda}_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{j})}{\big({\cal K}_{u}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{u}(\times)\big)^{m+2n-2}}~,

(92)

which agrees with the expression in
Lemos:2012ph

Proposition 4.17
.

The vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
are conformal with central charge

c_{\mathbf{V}_{m,1}}=m\,{\rm dim}\mathfrak{g}_{u}+2{\rm dim}\mathfrak{g}_{t}-24m\,\rho_{u}\cdot\rho_{u}^{\vee}-m\,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{u}~.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.6
, we know that
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
is conformal with central charge
c_{\mathbf{V}_{1,1}}=2{\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{g}_{t}+{\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{g}_{u}-\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{u}-24\rho_{u}\cdot\rho_{u}^{\vee}
so the statement is true of
m=1
.

We shall first show that
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
has a conformal vector, then show that it is the unique conformal vector whose grading agrees with the character and finally show that this results in the correct central charge.

Consider the vector
\omega_{m}\in C_{m,1}
, defined by

\omega_{m}=\omega_{\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{\mathbf{W}_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{gh,i}~,

where
\omega_{\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}}
is the conformal vector of
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
,
\omega_{\mathbf{W}_{i}}
is the conformal vector of the
i
th factor of
\mathbf{W}_{u}
and
\omega_{gh,i}
is the conformal vector of the
i
th ghost system,
\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))
. Clearly,
\omega_{m}
defines a conformal vector on the complex,
C_{m,1}
. Like we did for
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
we shall argue that this descends to a conformal vector in cohomology.

For an element
P_{i}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
, where we think of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
as a subalgebra of one of the
\mathbf{W}_{u}
factors,
\omega_{m}
acts as

\omega_{m}(z)P_{i}(w)\sim\frac{\partial P_{i}}{z-w}+\frac{(d_{i}+1)P_{i}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\sum_{j=2}^{d_{i}+2}\frac{(-1)^{j}j!}{(z-w)^{j+1}}q_{j}^{(i)}(w)~,

where
q_{j}^{(i)}
is some homogeneous state in
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
with weight
d_{i}-j+2
. Let us denote by
\widetilde{P_{i}}
the image of
P_{i}
under the projection
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{t})

To conclude this subsection, we should comment on the general issue of associativity. In
Arakawa:2018egx
, the cohomology
\mathbf{V}_{G,s}\circ\mathbf{V}_{G,s^{\prime}}
was concentrated in degree zero, so “gauging is associative” as a consequence of a by-now-standard spectral sequence argument. In our case, the argument is not so simple—we have repeatedly remarked that zero genus is no longer sufficient for a gluing to be concentrated in degree zero. The obvious spectral sequence no longer collapses on the second page, so the proofs of rearrangement lemmas no longer hold. Nevertheless, it is our belief that associativity must still hold in general. Yanagida
Yanagida:2020kim
has defined a derived version of the construction of
Arakawa:2018egx
in the sense of derived algebraic geometry. In addition, that work also imported the Moore–Tachikawa TQFT to the derived setting. In the derived Moore–Tachikawa analysis, associativity of gauging follows from general properties of the derived pushforward—even for nonzero genus. However, the notion of associativity in the derived setting is not quite what would be expected by physicists. In short, the physical prescription is normally to pass cohomology before the second gauging—unlike the derived case, where one does not pass to cohomology but instead holds on to the full data of the chain complex (as an object in an appropriate derived category). A sufficient condition for the derived associativity to imply our version of associativity is to show that the relevant chain complexes are formal,
i.e.
, are isomorphic to their cohomology (thought of as a complex with zero differential) in that derived category. While this is an interesting problem, we do not address it in this work.

The machinery of Feigin-Frenkel gluing has been invaluable to our proof of invariance under the
4
-move. Yet, in some sense it is an unnatural construction in that we must truncate to the zeroth cohomology by hand. In the twisted case, the higher derived functors would hopefully capture the fermions that arise from residual gauge symmetry in the genus-zero case. Unfortunately, we do not currently have a good candidate for such a functor.

4.8
The
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
twist of
\mathfrak{d}_{4}

Much of the preceeding discussion goes through for case of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-twisted punctures in the
\mathfrak{d}_{4}
theory, but there are some new features that are worth mentioning. First, let us lay out the details of the construction.

The chiral differential operators over
G_{2}
are well defined. Here we take
G_{2}
to be the exponentiated form of the
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
Lie algebra,
i.e.
,
G_{2}
is a simply connected, semi-simple, algebraic Lie group. The superconformal index assigned to the twisted cylinder agrees (summand by summand) with the character of
\mathbf{V}_{G_{2},2}
, and this once more motivates our construction.

Pictorially, one imagines the
G_{2}
cylinder as having a puncture twisted by
\omega
and the other by
\omega^{2}
. One might,
a priori
, expect that there are two possible
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
caps,
\mathbf{W}_{\omega}
and
\mathbf{W}_{\omega^{2}}
, depending on which puncture is closed. Yet, from the uniqueness argument of
Arakawa:2018egx
, the two putative caps are isomorphic. The outer automorphism,
\sigma
, that exchanges
\omega
with
\omega^{2}
should therefore lift to an isomorphism of vertex-algebras
\mathbf{W}_{\omega}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\mathbf{W}_{\omega^{2}}
.

The total monodromy around all punctures must be trivial. This can be satisfied in a number of ways, but for now we will restirct our attention to the case where punctures labelled by
\omega
and
\omega^{2}
, respectively, come in pairs (one might think of them as having twist lines connecting them pairwise). We denote a genus zero surface with
m
untwisted punctures and
n

\omega,\omega^{2}
pairs of punctures by
{\cal C}_{0,m,n}
. We define the mixed trinion,
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
as

\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\coloneqq\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{d}_{4}}\ast_{u}\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G_{2}}~,

(106)

and the construction of the
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
proceeds analogously. The ambiguity in the two versions of the cap is present here again. Namely, is there an
S
-duality move that swaps two punctures with
\omega
and
\omega^{2}
labels? We will show that the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
are indeed invariant under such a move, though there is no expectation that the underslying SCFTs will enjoy the same symmetry.

Let us first establish some rearrangement lemmas for the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
case. The isomorphism of vertex algebras
\mathbf{W}_{\omega}\cong\mathbf{W}_{\omega^{2}}
lifts to a natural transformation of functors,

\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z}_{t},\mathbf{W}_{\omega}\otimes-)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z}_{t},\mathbf{W}_{\omega^{2}}\otimes-)~.

(107)

Though unphysical, one can consider the vertex algebra
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1,1}\coloneqq\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{d}_{4}}\ast_{u}(\mathbf{W}_{\omega}\ast_{t}\mathbf{W}_{\omega})
. This would, naively, correspond to the trinion with two maximal punctures twisted by
\omega
and one untwisted puncture.
From the natural isomorphism, however, we have that

\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1,1}\cong\mathbf{V}_{1,1}~.

(108)

We can, therefore, use the trinion
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1,1}
as the building block for an equivalent construction of genus zero vertex algebras, which are isomorphic to the
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
construction. This construction, however, makes manifest the enhanced symmetry of the vertex algebras,
i.e.
, the labelling by
\omega

versus

\omega^{2}
is redundant.

Let us reiterate, as this strikes us as a surprising result. At the level of the associated vertex algebra, there is are additional automorphisms that swap
\omega
and
\omega^{2}
punctures which, as far as we know, don’t arise from an underlying
S
-duality of the four-dimensional physics. For example, the naive symmetry of
{\cal C}_{0,0,2}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, which swaps punctures with the same label. This is enhanced to
S_{4}
—swapping between all four punctures—as if these were all identical punctures!

In addition to the mixed trinion that we described above, one can compactify on a curve with a three pronged twist line—connecting three punctures each with
\omega
(or
\omega^{2}
) monodromy. The trinion with three
\omega
punctures does indeed correspond to a physical SCFT, and one might expect that this trinion is
\mathbf{V}_{G_{2},3}
. Comparing the superconformal index of this trinion theory with the character of
\mathbf{V}_{G_{2},3}
, however, exposes this as wishful thinking. Indeed, this trinion is something of a mystery, and we currently have no way of constructing it with the machinery of
Arakawa:2018egx
. For now, we shall refer to the surface with the three pronged twist line by
{\cal C}_{0,0,3\omega}
and its corresponding vertex algebra by
\mathbf{V}_{0,3\omega}
. We also introduce their
\sigma
-conjugates
{\cal C}_{0,0,3\omega^{2}}
and
\mathbf{V}_{0,3\omega^{2}}
, which correspond to the surface with a three pronged twist line between three
\omega^{2}
twisted punctures and its associated vertex algebra.

Unlike all of the other trinions we have considered, we expect
\mathbf{V}_{0,3\omega}
to be derived/contain fermionic operators/have a cohomological grading in which it is not concentrated in degree zero. (This is predicted by our proposed diagnostic concerning the covering space of the UV curve, and is compatible with some speculative analyses of the superconformal index.) Any construction involving Feigin–Frenkel gluing is forced to be in degree zero, since we manually truncate to the zeroth cohomology, so this expectation implies the necessity of other tools to get at this vertex algebra.

Further observations

5.1
Three dimensional mirrors and the non-simply laced case

Having identified the appropriate vertex algebras to identify with the twisted theories of type
{\cal C}_{0,m,n}
, there remains a question of how to understand Arakawa’s
\mathbf{V}_{G_{t},s}
for non-simply laced
G
. We wish to speculatively suggest a physical interpretation of these vertex algebras, but our suggestion will require a diersion to three dimensions.

The Higgs branches of class
{\cal S}
theories, which in the mathematical literature have come to be known as
Moore–Tachikawa varieties
following the work of
Moore:2011ee
, are at present most uniformly understood in terms of circle reduction to three-dimensions. Reducing a four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
theory on a circle results in a three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
theory with the same Higgs branch, and when that three-dimensional theory has a mirror dual then the Coulomb branch of that dual theory will reproduce the Higgs branch in question. The three dimensional mirrors of the
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
series of class
{\cal S}
theories were derived in
Benini:2010uu
using brane web technology. In the same work, mirror theories for
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
theories with twisted punctures were also presented. The twisted
\mathfrak{a}_{2n}
case has also been explored recently in
Beratto:2020wmn
.

In all of these cases, the three dimensional mirrors are quiver gauge theories—they are
star shaped quivers
with a central node from which tails radiate outwards, one for each puncture. Each type of puncture gives rise to a different tail. For example, the maximal untwisted punctures in type
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
give rise to tails matching the Lagrangian description for the theory
T[SO(2n)]
seen in Figure
4

Gaiotto:2009sym
. For a genus zero theory with maximal punctures, the quiver is a central
SO(2n)
node with
T[SO(2n)]
tails radiating off. For genus
g>0
, the quiver is the same but with the addition of
g
many hypermultiplets valued in the adjoint of
SO(2n)
, which look like
g
loops starting and ending on the central
SO(2n)
node.

When there is a mixture of twisted and untwisted punctures, the central node is then replaced by
O(2n-1)
, and the tails for maximal twisted punctures correspond to the Lagrangian for the
T[SO(2n-1)]
theory, which is displayed in Figure
5
. Finally, in addition to the extra adjoint matter arising from positive genus, there are an extra
2s_{t}+2g-2
fundamental hypermultiplets of
O(2n-1)
, where
2s_{t}
is the number of twisted punctures. Note that at genus zero, these additional fundamental hypermultiplets only appear in the presence of
four or more twisted punctures
. In light of our discussions on residual gauge symmetries, these extra fundamental hypers might be seen as indicative of the residual gauge symmertry/derived structure that arises in the twisted setting. (An interesting special case is for the
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
theory, where the extra fundamentals can be directly identifid with extra adjoints of the twisted algebra
\mathfrak{a}_{1}

5.2
Unexpected Feigin–Frenkel gluings

Looking at Table
1
, one sees that both
\mathfrak{a}_{2n}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
share the same twisted algebra,
\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}
. Our Theorem
4.1
is quite flexible, and in principle allows for gluings beyond the mixed vertex algebras we have described. The vertex algebra object
\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{d}_{n}}\ast_{\mathfrak{d}_{n}}\mathbf{W}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}
is part of our construction, and indeed corresponds to a nice physical SCFT. However, since this object is in
\mathrm{KL}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}
and so in
{\cal Z}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}-
Mod, there is nothing stopping us from considering the gluing

\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{a}_{2n}}\ast_{\mathfrak{a}_{2n}}(\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{d}_{n}}\ast_{\mathfrak{d}_{n}}\mathbf{W}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}})~,

(109)

where
{\cal Z}_{\mathfrak{a}_{2n}}
acts on the right by the surjection,
{\cal Z}_{\mathfrak{a}_{2n}}\twoheadrightarrow{\cal Z}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}
.

This might seem like a forced construction, but it is part of a larger “duality” web. Consider, for example, the mixed trinions
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}^{\mathfrak{a}_{2n}}
and
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}^{\mathfrak{d}_{n}}
. Both of these are vertex algebra objects in
\mathrm{KL}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}
and so, in principle, there is nothing stopping us from considering the BRST gauging

\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{2n},1,1}\circ_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{d}_{n},1,1}~.

(110)

This is a vertex algebra with
\mathrm{KL}
actions of
V^{-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}_{2n},\mathfrak{d}_{n},
and
\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}
. This does not, obviously, correspond to any of our—or Arakawa’s—constructions. Thinking pictorially, this vertex algebra should have another decomposition, given by the gauging
\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\circ_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}\mathbf{V}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1},3}
, where
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1},3}
is the genus zero trinion of type
{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}
—constructed as in
Arakawa:2018egx
.

From a physical perspective, the gauging (
110
) is inconsistent; the theory associated to
{\cal C}_{0,1,1}
for type
\mathfrak{a}_{2n}
has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Witten anomaly, while the
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
theory does not. The diagonal action of
\mathfrak{c}_{n-1}
on the product is anomalous and cannot be gauged. Yet, at the level of vertex algebras, there seem to be no immediate obstructions to this.

Since we have opened the doors, one might wonder whether there are other such unexpected/unphysical constructions, but we don’t believe this to be the case. Indeed, let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be Lie algebras such that there exists some surjection
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
. Unlike the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is the twisted algebra, there is no guarantee that this surjection survives to the quotients
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})_{\lambda}
. While the operation
\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\ast_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
is well-defined, we expect that it will not share many of the desirable properties of the mixed vertex algebras. As a graded vector space, the zeroth cohomology decomposes into a sum
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P^{+}_{2}}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{1}\otimes_{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})_{\lambda}}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{2}
. Here, we seek an embedding
P^{+}_{2}\hookrightarrow P_{1}^{+}
such that the image of
\lambda\in P^{+}_{1}
is the highest weight of a finite dimensional, irreducible
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
representation on which the
P_{i}
act as zero if
P_{i,(0)}
is in the kernel of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
. When this embedding does not arise from an outer-automorphism, the set of valid
\lambda
is small and possibly contains only the trivial representation.

Indeed, the constuction of
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}
vertex algebras represents the only real flexibility offered by Theorem
4.1
. Repeating our arguments for the characters of
\mathbf{V}_{m,n}
, we can derive the character of the vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}
. This character is given by

{\rm ch}\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{n-1}}^{+}}\frac{{\cal K}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathbf{a})\chi^{\lambda}_{A}(\mathbf{a}){\cal K}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\times){\cal K}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathbf{c})\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathbf{c}){\cal K}_{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathbf{d})\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathbf{d})}{{\cal K}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\times){\cal K}_{\mathfrak{d}}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{d}}(\times)}~,

(111)

where we use the subscripts
\mathfrak{a},{\mathfrak{c}},\mathfrak{d}
to distinguish between the
{\cal K}
-factors and Schur polynomials of each type and
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{c}
, and
\mathbf{d}
are fugacities of
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
,
{\mathfrak{c}}_{n}
, and
\mathfrak{d}_{n}
respectively.

These triply mixed vertex algebras form another class of examples that seem to have no corresponding four-dimensional SCFT. Nevertheless, one can imagine associating (mirror) quivers, in the obvious way, to these vertex algebras in three dimensions. So it is conceivable that these vertex algebras arise from the boundary construction of
Gaiotto:2016wcv
;
Costello:2018swh
;
Costello:2019
as well.

5.3
A six dimensional perspective on the index

There is a particularly nice interpretation of the twisted class
{\cal S}
characters in light of our undersatnding of Feigin–Frenkel gluing and the six-dimensional origins of these SCFTs. The character for an untwisted three punctured sphere,
\mathbf{V}_{G,3}
can be rewritten in terms of characters of Weyl modules as

{\rm ch}\,\mathbf{V}_{G,3}=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{G}^{+}}\frac{{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}{{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}}~.

(112)

Each Weyl module in the numerator arises from the decomposition of
\mathbf{W}
into
\mathrm{KL}^{ord}
objects. We then identify each pair of caps through their Feigin–Frenkel centres, and in doing so we quotient by a copy of
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}
for each gluing. However, we have not accounted for the generators of the cap that are not present in the Weyl module. These additional generators can be heuristically understood in terms of descendants of local operators in six dimensions.

We aim to interpret Schur operators in four dimensions as being realised directly as descendants of Schur-type operators in six dimensions, possibly in the presence of wrapped defects.
17
17

17

Such an interpretation has been explored previously in
gaioul
and
BRZ
. The organisation of local operator indices (in the presence of intersecting defects) in six dimensions in terms of affine Kac–Moody and principal
W
-algebra characters has also appeared previously in
Bullimore:2014upa
.
The Schur-type operators in six-dimensions themselves can be given the structure of a vertex algebra—specifically the vertex algebra
{\cal W}(\mathfrak{g},f_{prin})

Beem:2014kka
—though in the present arrangement they should be thought of as realising the commutative limit of said
{\cal W}
-algebra. When reducing to four dimensions, local operators in six dimensions can be directly reduced (integrated against the holomorphic volume form on the UV curve), or they can be reduced after performing supersymmetric descent and integrating against closed one- or two-forms on the UV curve, with one-forms getting paired with fermionic descendants. Consequently, as a graded vector space, the six-dimensional
{\cal W}(\mathfrak{g},f_{prin})
operators give rise to four-dimensional operators with character

{\rm ch}{\cal W}(\mathfrak{g}_{u},f_{prin})^{\chi({\cal C})}=({\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{0})^{2-2g}~,

(113)

and for
g\neq 0
this includes fermionic operators that reflect the derived structure/residual gauge symmetry of these theories. There are additional contributions from wrapped codimension two defects, which in six dimensions support the vertex algebra
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})

Beem:2014kka
. These contribute factors of
{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{0}
to the character. However, the Feigin–Frenkel centres of the subalgebras are identified through the bulk of the UV curve, so the contribution is
{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{0}/{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{0}
. For a general surface
{\cal C}_{g,s}
, this gives

\left(\frac{{\rm ch}\mathbb{V}_{0}}{{\rm ch}\mathfrak{z}_{0}}\right)^{s}\cdot({\rm ch}\mathfrak{z}_{0})^{2-2g}~.

(114)

The four-dimensional descendants of local operators in six dimensions (in the presence of codimension-two defects), therefore account for the
\lambda=0
summand of the index. The higher terms in the TQFT expansion of the index arise from codimension four defects, which are labelled by dominant integral weights
\lambda\in P^{+}
. Upon compactification, the codimension four defects can wrap around the surface,
{\cal C}_{g,s}
and intersect with the codimension two defects, leading to a
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}}
module structure. Indeed, these modules are classified by modules of highest weight
\lambda
. The fact that the Feigin–Frenkel centre is shared can be, in a loose sense, seen as a consequence of the fact that the modules all arise from the same wrapped defect. Similarly, the local operators in six-dimensions can also be inserted on the wrapped defects, leading to modules—of highest weight
\lambda
—of the
{\cal W}
-algebra.

After descent, the image of these operators account for the
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}
factors in the summand, and for a general surface
{\cal C}_{g,s}
, we have

{\rm ch}\,\mathbf{V}({\cal C}_{g,s})=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{G}^{+}}\left(\frac{{\rm ch}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}{{\rm ch}\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}}\right)^{s}\cdot({\rm ch}\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda})^{2-2g}~.

(115)

So far, this has amounted to little more than a refactoring and reinterpretation of the summands in the untwisted index. To reap some intuitive benefit from this perspective, we will consider the twisted vertex algebras. Let us start with the mixed trinion
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}
, whose character is

{\rm ch}\,\mathbf{V}_{1,1}=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}^{u}_{\lambda}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}}\right)\cdot{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}~.

(116)

The Weyl module characters in the numerator arise from the intersection of the codimension four and two defects, as in the untwisted case. Since there are twisted punctures present, only the invariant generators of the six-dimensional
{\cal W}
-algebra can survive the compactification, so we have extra characters of
{\cal W}(\mathfrak{g}_{t},f_{prin})
-modules appearing in the numerators. The characters of the vertex algebras of the form
\mathbf{V}_{m,1}
can all be interpreted in the same way.

Now let us consider introducing more twisted punctures. Consider
\mathbf{V}_{0,2}
, whose character we can suggestively organise as follows,

{\rm ch}\mathbf{V}_{0,2}=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{t}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{t}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{t}{\rm ch}\,\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{t}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}}\right)^{3}\cdot{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}\cdot\left(\frac{{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}}{{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}}\right)^{2}~.

(117)

Ignoring the final factor of
{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}/{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}
, we see a match to the character of
\mathbf{V}_{G_{t},4}
,
i.e.
the genus zero vertex algebra of Arakawa. Unlike the
{\cal C}_{m,1}
case, from a six-dimensional perspective, there should now be one-forms with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
monodromy around the twisted punctures. Not all generators of
{\cal W}(\mathfrak{g}_{u},f_{prin})
can be paired with these one-forms. Rather, it is precisely those in the kernel of
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}
. Like the positive genus case, these pairings with one-forms give rise to fermionic Schur operators in four dimensions. The kernel of
\mathfrak{z}^{u}_{\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}^{t}_{\lambda}
has character given by
{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}/{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}
but since these are now descending as fermionic operators in four dimensions, they contribute to the index with minus signs, and hence show up in the denominator. The number of one-forms available for pairing can be determined via a Riemann–Hurwitz type computation. In the example above, the factor of
{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}/{\rm ch}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}
should be interpreted as the contribution from these non-invariant pairings; the power of two comes from the number of (holomorphic and antiholomorphic) one-forms with monodromy.

This six dimensional perspective has been present as an undercurrent throughout this work, and certainly warrants further investigation in the future.

Arakawa:2018egx
, the rearrangement lemmas of Section
4.7
rely primarily upon the machinery of spectral sequences. The most general treatment of spectral sequences is beyond our needs, but for the unfamiliar reader we review some key statements that underlie our results.

We will be exclusively concerned with spectral sequences associated to a bicomplex. Let
{\cal A}
be an abelian category. Suppose

C^{\bullet\bullet}=\bigoplus_{p,q}C^{p,q}~,

(118)

is a bicomplex,
i.e.
,
C^{p,q}
are objects of
{\cal A}
with differentials

\begin{split}d_{h}:C^{p,q}\rightarrow C^{p+1,q}~,\\
d_{v}:C^{p,q}\rightarrow C^{p,q+1}~,\end{split}

(119)

satisfying

d_{h}\circ d_{h}=d_{v}\circ d_{v}=d_{h}\circ d_{v}+d_{v}\circ d_{h}=0~.

(120)

There are two notions of total complex of a bicomplex that are common.

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tot}_{\oplus}(C)^{p}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m+n=p}C^{m,n}~,\\
\mathrm{Tot}_{\otimes}(C)^{p}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{m+n=p}C^{m,n}~,\end{split}

(121)

and for our purposes we will only require the former, and hereafter we omit the
\oplus
subscript. The total complex is equipped with a differential

d_{tot}=d_{h}+(-1)^{q}d_{v}~,

(122)

making it an honest cochain complex. In this setting, spectral sequences are a tool to “approximate” the total cohomology,

H_{tot}^{\bullet}=H^{\bullet}(C_{tot},d_{tot})~,

(123)

in terms of the cohomology of
C^{\bullet\bullet}
with respect to
d_{h}
and
d_{v}
in an iterative process which converges on to the total cohomology
H_{tot}
.

We follow
Weibel:1994
for our definition of a (cohomology) spectral sequence. A spectral sequence is a family of objects
\{E_{r}^{p,q}\}
with differentials

d_{r}^{pq}:E_{r}^{p,q}\rightarrow E_{r}^{p+r,q-r+1}~,

(124)

satsifying
d^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}\circ d^{p,q}_{r}=0
and isomorphisms

E^{p,q}_{r+1}=\frac{\mathrm{ker}(d_{r}^{p,q})}{\mathrm{im}(d_{r}^{p-r,q+r-1})}.

(125)

For certain spectral sequences there exists some finite
r_{0}
such that

E_{r}^{p,q}\cong E_{r_{0}}^{p,q}~,\qquad r>r_{0}~.

(126)

This “stable page” is usually written as
E_{\infty}^{p,q}
. There are several technical definitions of what it means for a spectral sequence to converge. For our purposes a spectral sequence,
E_{r}^{p,q}
, converges to a cohomology
H^{\bullet}
if there exists a finite, increasing filtration

0=F^{s}H^{n}\subset\dots\subset F^{p}H^{n}\subset\dots\subset F^{t}H^{n}=H^{n}~,

(127)

where
s
and
t
are finite and

E_{\infty}^{pq}\cong F^{p}H^{p+q}/F^{p-1}H^{p+q}~.

(128)

One can then extract the filtration from the stable page and thus compute the total cohomology. There is a special case, where one can simply read off the cohomology without worrying about a filtration, known as
collapse
. A spectral sequence collapses at some page
r>1
, if on that page there is only one nonzero row or column. In practice, we rely on spectral sequences that collapse at the second page.

The total complex
C_{tot}
comes equipped with a natural filtration

F_{n}C_{tot}^{p}=\mathrm{Tot}_{\oplus}(\tau_{n}C)^{p}~,

(129)

where

(\tau_{n}C)^{p,q}=\begin{cases}\makebox[22.76228pt][l]{$C^{p,q}$}\text{ if }p\leq n\\
\makebox[22.76228pt][l]{$0$}\text{ if }p>n\end{cases}~.

(130)

This filtration is natural in the sense that the subcomplex
\tau_{n}C
is a truncation of
C
at the
n
th column. It is a result in homological algebra that such a filtration gives rise to a spectral sequence that, under certain technical conditions, converges on the total cohomology. The zeroth page of the spectral sequence is simply the bicomplex

{}_{I}E_{0}^{p,q}=C^{p,q}~.

(131)

The differential of the first page must act vertically and is simply
d_{v}
. The first page is therefore

{}_{I}E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(C^{p,\ast},d_{v})~,

(132)

with differential
d_{2}=d_{h}
. The second page is therefore

{}_{I}E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(H^{q}(C,d_{v}),d_{h})~.

(133)

The differential
d_{3}
is more complicated, in practice it is best if the spectral sequence collapses by the second page, in which case

H_{tot}^{p}=E^{p,n}_{2}~,

(134)

assuming the
n
th row is the only nonzero row.

There is another spectral sequence converging to
H_{tot}
—associated to the filtration by rows. The second page of this spectral sequence is given by

{}_{II}E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(H^{q}(C,d_{h}),d_{v})~.

(135)

We refer to the earlier spectral sequence as
{}_{I}E^{p,q}_{r}
.

For our rearrangement lemmas, we are frequently interested in when cohomology operations commute with each other,
i.e.
,

H^{p}\tilde{H}^{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\cong}}\tilde{H}^{q}H^{p}~.

(136)

If the two cohomologies conspire to form a bicomplex, then this is equivalent to asking whether

{}_{I}E_{2}^{p,q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\cong}}\,_{II}E_{2}^{p,q}~.

(137)

We see that a sufficient condition for this equivalence is if the two sequences both collapse on the second page, since the two sides are then both isomorphic to the total cohomology. If we are only interested in whether the zeroth cohomologies commute then we can replace the above by a different and often weaker condition. As long as the spectral sequence is first quadrant—
E_{r}^{p,q}=0
for
p,q<0
—the
E^{0,0}_{r}
stabilizes by the second page. Thus, if
H^{p}
and
\tilde{H}^{p}
vanish for
p<0
then

H^{0}\tilde{H}^{0}\cong\tilde{H}^{0}H^{0}~.

(138)

Appendix B
Miura opers and a proof of Theorem
4.1

The proof of Theorem
4.1
relies on the machinery of Miura opers and Cartan connections. We give a brief introduction to both of these before giving a proof of the theorem.

B.1
Miura opers and the no-monodromy condition

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group and
B
a Borel subgroup of
G
. A Miura
G
-oper on an algebraic curve
X
is the tuple
(\mathscr{F},\nabla,\mathscr{F}_{B},\mathscr{F}_{B}^{\prime})
, where
(\mathscr{F},\nabla,\mathscr{F}_{B})
is a
G
-oper and
\mathscr{F}_{B}^{\prime}
is another
B
-reduction of
\mathscr{F}
that is preserved by
\nabla
. We denote the space of Miura opers over
X
by
\mathrm{MOp}_{G}(X)
and there is a natural forgetful morphism

\mathrm{MOp}_{G}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Op}_{G}(X)~.

(139)

A Miura oper is called
generic
if the two bundles
\mathscr{F}_{B}
and
\mathscr{F}_{B}^{\prime}
are in generic relative position to each other. The space of such generic Miura opers over
X
is denoted by
\mathrm{MOp}_{G}(X)_{gen}
. Once again we restrict to the cases where
X
is the formal disc,
D
, or the formal punctured disc,
D^{\times}
.

Now let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of
B
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
and
H
be a choice of the maximal torus so
B=H\ltimes N
. We can define a principal
H
-bundle
\mathscr{F}_{H}
on
X
according to

\mathscr{F}_{H}=\mathscr{F}_{B}\underset{B}{\times}H=\mathscr{F}_{B}/N~.

(140)

It can be shown (see,
e.g.
, Lemma 4.2.1 of
Frenkel:2007
) that
\mathscr{F}_{H}
is isomorphic to the
H
-bundle
\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}}
—characterised in terms of its transition functions, which for a change of coordinates
t=\phi(s)
, are given by

\phi^{\prime}(s)\rho^{\vee}~.

(141)

Here,
\rho^{\vee}
is half the sum of positive coroots that we identify as an element of the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
using the identification
(\mathfrak{h}^{\vee})^{*}\cong\mathfrak{h}
. The space of connections of
\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}})_{D}
on the bundle
\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}}
over
D
consists of connections of the form

\nabla=\partial_{t}+\mathbf{u}(t)\,,\qquad\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathfrak{h}[\![t]\!]~,

(142)

for some choice of coordinate
t
on the disc. Under a coordinate change
t=\phi(s)
, the connection transforms as

\tilde{\mathbf{u}}=\phi(s)\mathbf{u}(\phi(s))-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)}{\phi^{\prime}(s)}\rho^{\vee}~.

(143)

For the
H
-bundle
\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}}
on
D^{\times}
, the space of connections
\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}})_{D^{\times}}
is similarly populated but with
\mathrm{u}\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
.

We introduce this Cartan connection to exploit the isomorphism (see
Frenkel:2007
, Proposition
8.2.2
),

\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}})_{D}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{MOp}_{G}(D)_{gen}~.

(144)

Composing with the forgetful morphism (
139
) gives the Miura transform

\mu:\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}})_{D}\rightarrow\mathrm{Op}_{G}(D)~.

(145)

Every Miura oper on
D^{\times}
is generic
Frenkel:2007
, and so we have a Miura transform

\mu:\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho^{\vee}})_{D^{\times}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{MOp}_{G}(D^{\times})\rightarrow\mathrm{Op}_{G}(D^{\times})~.

(146)

As we are ultimately interested in the Feigin-Frenkel centre for
\mathfrak{g}
, we work over the Langlands dual group,
{}^{L}G
, with Borel subgroup
{}^{L}B
and Cartan subgroup
{}^{L}H
. The
{}^{L}H
-bundle of interest is
\Omega^{\rho}
, and the Miura transform is the morphism

\mu:\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Op}_{{}^{L}G}(D^{\times})\cong\mathrm{Spec}~{\cal Z}~.

(147)

Now let us specialise to the subspace
\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})^{\lambda}_{D}\subset\mathrm{Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}
, consisting of connections of the form

\nabla=\partial_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{t}+\mathbf{u}(t)\,,\qquad\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}[[t]]~,

(148)

where we think of
\lambda
as an element in the Cartan subalgebra,
\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
, via the identification
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
. The ring of functions over such connections is

\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}^{\lambda}\cong\mathbb{C}[u_{i,n}|\,i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}^{\vee};n<0]~,

(149)

where
u_{i}
are the coordinates with respect to some choice of basis,
(h_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
, of the Cartan subalgebra.

The image of this subspace under the Miura transform is exactly the subscheme
\mathrm{Op}_{G}^{\lambda}\cong\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}
(
Frenkel:2007
, Lemma
9.6.2
). Furthermore, at the level of functions,

\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}\cong\mathrm{Op}_{G}^{\lambda}\cong(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}^{\lambda})^{\mathfrak{n}^{\vee}}~.

(150)

The action of
\mathfrak{n}^{\vee}
on
\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}^{\lambda}
is realised by the screening charges,

V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]=\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}a_{ji}\sum_{n\geq\lambda_{i}}x_{i,n-\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j,-n-1}}~,

(151)

where the polynomials
x_{i,n}
are determined via

\sum_{n\leq 0}x_{i,n}t^{-n}=\mathrm{exp}\,\left(\sum_{m>0}\frac{u_{i,-m}}{m}t^{m}\right)~.

(152)

In this sense,
\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{D^{\times}}^{\lambda}
, provides a kind of free-field realisation of the opers satisfying the no-monodromy condition.

B.2
Proof of Theorem
4.1

Let us now apply the above machinery to our problem. We have the following morphisms

\begin{split}\mathfrak{z}_{u,\lambda}&\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{u},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}~,\\
\mathfrak{z}_{t,\lambda}&\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{t},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}~.\end{split}

(153)

There is a natural action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
on
\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{u},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}
. We also have a canonical projection
\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{u},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{t},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}
, via the isomorphism

\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{t},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}\cong(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{u},D^{\times}}^{\lambda})_{\sigma}~,

(154)

where
(-)_{\sigma}
, denotes the functor of coinvariants with respect to the subgroup
\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by the twisting automorphism. Concretely, we have the isomorphism

\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{t},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}\cong\mathbb{C}\left[\tilde{u}_{i,n}|i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{t};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right]~,

(155)

where

\tilde{u}_{i,n}=\frac{1}{|\langle\sigma\rangle|}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\langle\sigma\rangle}u_{\sigma^{\prime}{i},n}~.

(156)

These are precisely the linear combinations of the generators of
\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{\rho})_{{}^{L}G_{u},D^{\times}}^{\lambda}
that are invariant under
\langle\sigma\rangle
.

Assembling, we have the diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun\,Op}_{G_{u}}^{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho})^{\lambda}_{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun\,Op}_{{}^{L}G_{t}}^{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho})^{\lambda}_{D})_{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma}}

(157)

where the horizontal morphisms are the natural inclusions of
\mathfrak{n}^{\vee}
-invariants and the vertical arrow is the natural projection to the coinvariants. We can define a map
\pi
that, we claim, makes the diagram commute via
\pi=\pi_{\sigma}\circ\iota
. The map
\pi
is then nothing more than the projection of
\mathrm{Fun\,Op}^{\lambda}_{G_{u}}
to its
\langle\sigma\rangle
coinvariants.

Our Theorem
4.1
, is therefore equivalent to the claim that
\pi
is surjective. Indeed, surjectivity of
\pi
is precisely the statement that
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{u}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}
is a surjection.

Theorem B.1
.

The map
\pi:\mathrm{Fun\,Op}_{G_{u}}^{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathrm{Fun\,Op}_{{}^{L}G_{t}}^{\lambda}
, defined as above, is surjective.

Proof.

The action of
\mathfrak{n}_{u}^{\sigma}
on the space of coinvariants can be realised through the symmetrised screening charges

\tilde{V}_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]=\frac{1}{|\langle\sigma\rangle|}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\langle\sigma\rangle}V_{\sigma(i)}[\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+1]~.

(158)

A polynomial
P\in\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho})
is in the space of invariants,
(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho}))^{\mathfrak{n}_{u}}
, if and only if it is in the intersection of the kernels of the screening charges,
i.e.

V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P\overset{!}{=}0\,,\qquad\text{for }i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{u}~.

(159)

Similarly, a polynomial
P\in(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho}))_{\sigma}
in the space of coinvariants is an
\mathfrak{n}_{u}^{\sigma}
invariant, if and only if it lies in the intersection of the kernels of the symmetrised screening charges
\tilde{V}_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]
.

It is, therefore, sufficient to show that if
P
is a
\sigma
-coinvariant and lies in the interection of the kernels of the symmetrised screening charges, then it must lie in the intersection of the kernels of the
\mathfrak{n}_{u}
screening charges.
We make the following observations. Suppose
P
lies in the space of coinvariants, then we can realise it as
P\in\mathbb{C}[\tilde{u}_{i,n}|i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}_{t};\,n<0]
. Therefore,

\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{j,n}}=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{j,n}}\right)=\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{\sigma(j),n}}~.

(160)

Furthermore, the
x_{i,m}
must transform as,

\sigma(x_{i,m})=x_{\sigma(i),m}~,

(161)

which follows straightforwardly from the definition. Combining, we have that

\sigma\left(V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P\right)=V_{\sigma(i)}[\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+1]P~.

(162)

Let
P\in(\mathrm{Fun\,Conn}(\Omega^{-\rho}))_{\sigma}
and suppose it lies in the intersection of kernels of the symmetrised operators. Additionally, let us restrict to the case where
\sigma^{2}=1
and we only consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
twist—we shall address the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
twist for
\mathfrak{d}_{4}
at the end. Now, we have that

(V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]+V_{\sigma(i)}[\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+1])P=0~,

(163)

but from (
162
), we must have that

\sigma\left(V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P\right)=-V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P~.

(164)

We shall now show that for any
P
in the space of coinvariants, the polynomial
V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P
, cannot have eigenvalue
-1
under
\sigma
.

The image of
P
under the
i
th screening charge is

V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P=\sum_{n\geq\lambda_{i}}x_{i,n-\lambda_{i}}\cdot\left(a_{ij}\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{j,-n-1}}\right)~,

(165)

and we have

\sigma\left(V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P\right)=V_{\sigma(i)}[\lambda_{i}+1]P=\sum_{n\geq\lambda_{i}}x_{\sigma(i),n-\lambda_{i}}\cdot\left(a_{ij}\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{j,-n-1}}\right)~,

(166)

where we have made use of the fact that the derivatives of
P
and the highest weight,
\lambda
, are invariant under
\sigma
. From inspection, it is clear that

V_{\sigma(i)}[\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+1]P\neq-V_{i}[\lambda_{i}+1]P~,

(167)

unless both are identically zero, as desired.

Now we address the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
case of
\mathfrak{g}_{u}=\mathfrak{d}_{4}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{t}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. A
\mathfrak{n}_{u}^{\sigma}
invariant
P
must satisfy the following:

\begin{split}(V_{1}[\lambda_{1}+1]+V_{3}[\lambda_{1}+1]+V_{4}[\lambda_{1}+1])P=0~,\\
V_{2}[\lambda_{2}+1]P=0~.\end{split}

(168)

We have, once more, made use of the fact that the derivatives of
P
and the highest weight
\lambda
are invariant under the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Expanding the first requirement, we have that

\sum_{n\geq\lambda_{1}}\left(x_{1,n-\lambda_{1}}+x_{3,n-\lambda_{1}}+x_{4,n-\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(-\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{2,-n-1}}+2\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{i,-n-1}}\right)=0~.

(169)

Once again, this can only hold if each screening charge individually acts as zero.

Appendix C
The proof of Theorem
4.3

First, we establish fix notation. Let
{\cal F}
denote the composition
H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes-))
, which is an endofunctor on
{\cal Z}_{u}-
Mod. Notably, on the subcategory
\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}
,
{\cal F}(M)\cong M
for any
M\in\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}
(see Proposition 9.12 of
Arakawa:2018egx
), and in general
{\cal F}
is left-exact. We wish to show that
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})\cong\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
.

Let
\chi_{\lambda}:{\cal Z}_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a character defined by
\chi_{\lambda}(P_{i,n})=0
for
n\neq 0
and
P_{i,0}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}=\chi_{\lambda}(P_{i,0})\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}
. The Kazhdan–Lusztig category decomposes into blocks
\mathrm{KL}_{t}\cong\bigoplus\mathrm{KL}_{t}^{[\lambda]}
where
\mathrm{KL}_{t}^{[\lambda]}
is the subcategory of objects on which
P_{i,0}
acts as the
generalised
eigenvalue
\chi_{\lambda}(0)
,
i.e.
, these are objects which are
set-theoretically
supported on the vanishing locus of
(P_{i,0}-\chi_{\lambda}(P_{i,0}))
in
\mathrm{Spec}\,{\cal Z}_{t}
.

As
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
is a vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
, it decomposes as

\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P^{+}_{t}}\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}~,

where
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}
are objects in
\mathrm{KL}_{t}^{[\lambda]}
. The increasing filtration on
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
induces a filtration on each
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}
,

0=N_{0}\subset N_{1}\subset\dots\subset N=\bigcup_{i}N_{i}=\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}~,

such that successive quotients are isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda,2}^{t}
. While the character of
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
is ill-defined (since each weight space is infinite dimensional), the character of each
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}
is well-defined. We have that
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{[\lambda]}\cong{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]})
since
{\cal F}
is left exact. Therefore,

{\rm ch}~{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{[\lambda]}\cong{\rm ch}~{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]})~.

The filtration on
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}
induces a filtration on
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]})
and since
{\cal F}
is left exact, we have that
{\cal F}(N_{i})/{\cal F}(N_{i-1})\subseteq{\cal F}(N_{i}/N_{i-1})
, and so

{\rm ch}~{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]})\leqslant\sum_{i}{\rm ch}~{\cal F}(N_{i}/N_{i-1})~.

Each subquotient,
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is an object in
\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}
by Proposition
4.2
. Consequently,

{\rm ch}~{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]})\leqslant\sum_{i}{\rm ch}~(N_{i}/N_{i-1})={\rm ch}\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t,[\lambda]}~.

From this we conclude that
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
must be
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
graded. Now, since
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
is simple, if we can show that there is a non-trivial vertex algebra morphism
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\rightarrow{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
, then we will have
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})\cong\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
. From here we are in a very similar situation as the proof of Theorem 9.9 of
Arakawa:2018egx
, so we adapt that proof to our setting. Before we do so, however, we will need some subsidiary lemmas. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{+}_{t}\subset\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,-\kappa_{c}}=t\mathfrak{g}_{t}[t]
. Then we have:

Lemma C.1
.

\mathbf{V}_{1,1}^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}})
.

Proof.

Let
C^{\bullet,\bullet}
be defined by

C^{p,q}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))\otimes\bigwedge\!^{q}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*})~,

where
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
is the Feigin standard complex for computing
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
and
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\otimes\bigwedge\!^{q}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*}
is the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex for computing the
ordinary
Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}
. We denote the differentials of each complex by
d_{\mathfrak{z}}
and
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
, respectively, and extend them to
C^{\bullet\bullet}
by letting
d_{\mathfrak{z}}
act trivially on
\bigwedge\!^{\bullet}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*}
and letting
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
act trivially on
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}_{u}))
. The two differentials anticommute and so
C^{\bullet\bullet}
is a bicomplex from which we form the total complex
C^{i}_{tot}=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}C^{p,q}
with differential
d=d_{\mathfrak{z}}+(-1)^{q}d_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

There are two spectral sequences converging to the total cohomology
H_{tot}^{\bullet}(C)
, with second pages given by

\begin{split}{}_{I}E_{2}^{p,q}&=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes H^{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}))~,\\
{}_{II}E_{2}^{p,q}&=H^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}))~.\end{split}

Note that
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}
is injective over
U(t\mathfrak{g}_{t}[t])
, so
H^{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
is concentrated in degree zero. Furthermore, both
H^{i}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},-)
and
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+i}({\cal Z}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes-)
vanish in negative degrees because
\mathbf{W}_{u}
is free over
{\cal Z}_{u,(<0)}
and
(-)^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}
is left exact. Therefore,
{}_{I}E_{r}^{p,q}
collapses at the second page and
{}_{II}E_{2}^{0,0}
is stable. This gives the isomorphism

{}_{I}E_{2}^{0,0}\cong H^{0}_{tot}(C)\cong\ _{II}E_{2}^{0,0}~,

as desired.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}=H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{1,1})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}\cong H^{0}_{DS}(u,\mathbf{V}_{1,1}^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.8
of
Arakawa:2018egx
, the functors
H^{0}_{DS}(u,-)
and
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+0}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes-)
are isomorphic. Let
C^{\bullet\bullet}
be defined by

C^{p,q}=\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})\otimes\bigwedge\!^{q}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*})~.

Let
d_{u}
be the differential on
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
, which computes the relative
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}}
semi-infinite cohomology, and we extend
d_{u}
to
C^{\bullet\bullet}
by letting it act trivially on
\bigwedge\!^{q}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*})
. Similarly, let
d_{t}
be the differential on
\mathbf{V}_{1,1}\otimes\bigwedge\!^{q}((\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+})^{*})
, which computes the ordinary Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}
, and we can extend this to
C^{\bullet\bullet}
by letting it act trivially on
\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathrm{\bigwedge}\,^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\mathfrak{g}_{u})
. The two differentials anticommute and so
C^{\bullet\bullet}
is a bicomplex. We can form the total complex
C^{i}=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}C^{p,q}
with total differential
d_{tot}=d_{u}+(-1)^{q}d_{t}
.

There are two spectral sequences converging to the total cohomology
H_{tot}(C)
, with second pages given by

\begin{split}{}_{I}E_{2}^{p,q}&=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+p}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes H^{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},\mathbf{V}_{1,1}))~,\\
{}_{II}E_{2}^{p,q}&=H^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+},\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{1,1}))~.\end{split}

Since
\mathbf{W}_{u}
is semijective in
\mathrm{KL}_{u}
,
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{\infty}{2}+\bullet}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{u,-\kappa_{g}},\mathfrak{g}_{u},\mathbf{W}_{u}\otimes-)
is concentrated in degree zero. Therefore, both spectral sequences collapse on the second page, and we have

{}_{I}E_{2}^{0,0}\cong H^{0}_{tot}(C)\cong_{II}E_{2}^{0,0}~,

as desired.
∎

Combining both of the above lemmas, we have

{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}\cong{\cal F}((\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}{\cal F}(\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes V^{t}_{\lambda^{*}})~,

where we have used the fact that

(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}\cong U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,\kappa_{c}})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{t}[t])\oplus\mathbb{C}K}{\cal O}(G_{t})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes V^{t}_{\lambda^{*}}~.

The
\mathbb{V}^{t}_{\lambda}\otimes V^{t}_{\lambda^{*}}
are naturally modules over
\mathfrak{z}_{\lambda}^{t}
and are therefore objects in
\mathrm{KL}_{u,0}
by Proposition
4.2
. As a result, we have that

{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{t}^{+}}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda^{*}}~.

Looking at the
\Delta=0
subspace,
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})^{\mathfrak{g}_{t}^{+}}_{0}
, and comparing with our constraint on the character, we have that

{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{t}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda^{*}}\cong{\cal O}(G_{t})~,

as
\mathfrak{g}_{t}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{t}
modules. Since,
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
is
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
-graded, the zero weight subspace
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}
is a unital commutative, associative algebra under the normal product. The quadratic Casimir provides a
\mathbb{Q}_{\geqslant 0}
-grading,

{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geqslant 0}}{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}(d)~,

where
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}(d)
has eigenvalue
d
with respect to the quadratic Casimir. The natural projection
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}\twoheadrightarrow{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}(0)
is an algebra homomorphism.

These observations mean that we are exactly in the situation of the proof of Theorem 9.9 in
Arakawa:2018egx
and so we can apply Lemma 9.10 of
loc. cit.
to conclude that
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})_{0}
is isomorphic to
{\cal O}(G_{t})
as a commutative
G_{t}\times G_{t}
algebra. Additionally,
{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
is a
\mathrm{KL}_{t}
object and so we have an action of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}_{t})
. All together, this gives a nonzero homomorphism
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t}\rightarrow{\cal F}(\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{t})
, as desired.

Appendix D
Rewriting Arakawa’s character

The character of the genus-zero chiral algebra of class
{\cal S}
of type
\mathfrak{g}
with
b
punctures is given in
Arakawa:2018egx
as follows,

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{V}_{G,b}^{\mathcal{S}}}(q^{L_{0}}z_{1}\dots z_{b})=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{+}}\left(\frac{q^{\langle\lambda,\rho^{\vee}\rangle}(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\left(1-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\right)}\right)^{(b-2)}\prod_{k=1}^{b}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}(q^{-D}z^{k})~,

(174)

where
L_{0}
is the Virasoro zero mode,
z_{i}
are elements of the maximal torus of
G
, and
D
is the degree operator of the Weyl-module
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
. The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Killing form.

It will be useful to be able to rewrite this expression so that it resembles (
7
). The characters of the Weyl modules are given by the Weyl–Kac formula,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}(q^{-D}z)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{sgn}(w)e^{w\circ\lambda}}{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(e^{-\alpha},q)_{\infty}(qe^{\alpha},q)_{\infty}}~,

(175)

where for simplicity we suppress flavour fugacities. The sum in the numerator is over the Weyl group
W
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined according to

(a,q)_{\infty}\coloneqq\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{j})~.

(176)

Our first observation is as follows:

\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}(q^{-D}z)&=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{sgn}(w)e^{w\circ\lambda}}{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(qe^{-\alpha},q)_{\infty}(qe^{\alpha},q)_{\infty}(1-e^{-\alpha})}\\
&=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{sgn}(w)e^{w\circ\lambda}}{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(qe^{-\alpha},q)_{\infty}(qe^{\alpha},q)_{\infty}e^{-\alpha/2}(e^{\alpha/2}-e^{-\alpha/2})}\\
&=\frac{1}{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}(qe^{\alpha},q)_{\infty}}\;\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{sgn}(w)e^{w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}e^{-\alpha/2}(e^{\alpha/2}-e^{-\alpha/2})}\\
&=\frac{1}{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}(qe^{\alpha},q)_{\infty}}\;\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{sgn}(w)e^{w(\lambda+\rho)}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(e^{\alpha/2}-e^{-\alpha/2})}~.\\
\end{split}

(177)

Using the Weyl character formula
Fulton:1991
and the expression for
\mathcal{K}
-factors given in
Lemos:2012ph
, we achieve a final expression for the character of the Weyl module,

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}}(q^{-D}z)=\mathcal{K}(z)\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(z)~.

(178)

What remains is to rewrite the prefactor from (
174
). We can write the character as

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{V}^{\cal S}_{G,b}}(q^{L_{0}}z_{1}\dots z_{b})=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{+}}(C_{\lambda\lambda\lambda})^{b-2}\prod_{i=1}^{b}{\cal K}(z_{i})\chi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}(z_{i})~,

(179)

which is precisely the form in (
7
). The Higgsing prescription for the character is identical to that of the index. Therefore, we may apply the same argument we used to calculate
C_{\lambda\lambda\lambda}
for the index, which results in the final expression of

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{V}^{\mathcal{S}}_{G,b}}(q^{L_{0}}z_{1}\cdots z_{b})=\sum_{\lambda\in P_{+}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{b}\mathcal{K}(z_{i})\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(z_{i})}{(\mathcal{K}(\times)\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\times))^{b-2}}~.

(180)

We would like to draw attention to a point of interest. One can attempt to massage the prefactor

\frac{q^{\langle\lambda,\rho^{\vee}\rangle}(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\left(1-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\right)}=\frac{(q,q)^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}~,

(181)

into a more suitable form. We largely follow the method of Appendix A of
Lemos:2014lua
, generalised to arbitrary simple
\mathfrak{g}
.

\begin{split}\frac{q^{\langle\lambda,\rho^{\vee}\rangle}(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\left(1-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\right)}&=\frac{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}\\
&=\frac{(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(1-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle})}\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}~.\end{split}

(182)

Using Equation
(76)
of
Arakawa:2018egx
we rewrite the product over positive roots as a product over degrees
d_{i}
of fundamental invariants,

\begin{split}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(1-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle})&=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}-1}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}\frac{(q,q)_{\infty}}{(q^{d_{i}},q)_{\infty}}\,,\\
&=(q,q)^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}{\cal K}(\times)~.\end{split}

(183)

We have substituted the expression for the
\mathcal{K}
-factor of an empty puncture from
Lemos:2012ph
in the last line. Thus, the prefactor takes the simplified form

\frac{q^{\langle\lambda,\rho^{\vee}\rangle}(q,q)_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathfrak{g}}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\left(1-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\right)}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}(\times)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}~.

(184)

Comparing the above to (
180
), we arrive at the identity

\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\times)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}~.

(185)

While this identity is interesting in its own right, it is strikingly similar to the
q
-deformed dimension. It is known that the Schur limit of the superconformal index of Class
{\cal S}
theories is related to
q
-deformed two dimensional Yang-Mills Theory. The
q
-deformed dimension arises naturally in this setting and for simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
, we have

{\rm dim}_{q}\,R_{\lambda}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{(q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{(q^{-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}=\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\times)~.

(186)

The representation
R_{\lambda}
is the tilting module
Andersen1992
of the
q
-deformed universal enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

Jimbo:1985zk
associated to the highest weight
\lambda\in P^{+}
. We wish to bring attention to this since this correspondence does not hold for non-simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
. For non-simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
, the
q
-dimension is defined as

{\rm dim}_{q}\,R_{\lambda}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{(q^{-d_{\alpha}\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{d_{\alpha}\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}{(q^{-d_{\alpha}\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle}-q^{d_{\alpha}\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}/2\rangle})}~.

(187)

The
d_{\alpha}\in\{1,2,3\}
are defined for simple roots,
\alpha_{i}

i\in\{1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}\}
as the integers such that if
A_{ij}
is the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
,
d_{i}A_{ij}
is symmetric. For positive roots one sets

if there is a Weyl transformation sending the positive root
\alpha

\alpha_{i}

It is worth noting that this expression for the

-deformed dimension no longer agrees with the expression for the fully reduced character,
\chi^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\times)
, in the non-simply laced case. We are, as yet, unsure about the significance of this.

Appendix E
Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction of the free hypermultiplet

In this appendix we present some supporting evidence for the equivalence (
83

We do not elucidate the full structure of the vertex algebra, but rather produce a list of generators, quantum numbers and null relations that are consistent with the expected identification.

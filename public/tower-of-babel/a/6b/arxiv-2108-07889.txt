Remarks on a Bernstein-type operator of Aldaz, Kounchev and Render Journal: …
Ana-Maria Acu, Heiner Gonska, Margareta Heilmann, Dedicated to professor Ioan Rasa on the occasion of his 70th birthday

Abstract
The Bernstein-type operator of Aldaz, Kounchev and Render (2009) is discussed. New direct results in terms of the classical second order modulus as well as in a modification following Marsden and Schoenberg are given.



2020 MSC
41A25, 41A36.

1
Introduction

This brief note is dedicated to our dear friend and long term collaborator
Ioan Rasa
on the occasion of his 70th birthday. Over the years he did a lot, most fruitful work on quite a number of Bernstein-type and related operators, among others. Here we deal with a then surprising, 12-year old object of this type offering a lot of challenges.

Starting from the classical Bernstein operators
B_{n}
defined for
f\in C[0,1]
as

B_{n}(f;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}f\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\right)p_{n,k}(x),\,\,\text{ where}

p_{n,k}(x)={n\choose k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k},

during recent years many, probably much too many, modifications have been considered. One of the present hypes follows a 2003 paper written by J.P. King
[
16
]
in order to obtain linear positive operators which preserve two functions different from the classical test functions
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
. Here we used the convention
e_{j}(x)=x^{j},\,\,j=0,1,\dots

King modified the classical Bernstein operators as follows:

f\to\left(B_{n}f\right)\circ r_{n},\,\,f\in C[0,1],

(1.1)

where

r_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{2},&n=1,\\
\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+4n(n-1)x^{2}}\right),&n=2,3,\dots\\
\end{array}\right.

These operators preserve the functions
e_{i}(x)=x^{i}
for
i=0,2
. A slight extension was considered by Cárdenas et al. in
[
6
]

where a sequence of operators
B_{n,\alpha}
that preserve
e_{0}
and
e_{2}+\alpha e_{1}
,
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
was introduced.
It is clear that the operator of King does not produce polynomials in the general case. Therefore, in their 2005 remarks on the article of King, Gonska and Piţul (see
[
15
]
) asked the question if there exist linear and positive
polynomial
operators that reproduce
e_{2}
.

Using a continuous strictly increasing function
\tau
defined on
[0,1]
with
\tau(0)=0
and
\tau(1)=1
,
\tau^{\prime}(x)>0,x\in[0,1]
, Cárdenas-Morales et al. (see
[
6
]
) introduced a modification of the Bernstein operator as follows:

B_{n}^{\tau}(f;x):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\tau(x)^{k}(1-\tau(x))^{n-k}(f\circ\tau^{-1})\left(k/n\right),\,\,f\in C[0,1],\,\,x\in[0,1].

(1.2)

Note that
\tau^{\prime}(0)>0
is essential for their results.
A predecessor can be found in Cottin et al.
[
9
]
, but
\tau(x)=\sqrt{x}
there.

These operators preserve the functions
e_{0}
and
\tau
. So, for
\tau(x)=x^{j}
, one has

B_{n}^{\tau}(e_{0};x)=e_{0}\textrm{ and }B_{n}^{\tau}(e_{j};x)=e_{j}.

In this case
B_{n}^{\tau}
produces polynomials of degree
jn.

If the function
\tau
is not a polynomial, then the modified operator (
1.2
) is not a polynomial operator.

Fix
j>1
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\Pi_{n}
be the space of polynomials over
[0,1]
of degree less than or equal to
n
. For every
n\geq j
, Aldaz, Kounchev and Render
[
3
]
introduced a
polynomial
Bernstein operator
B_{n,0,j}:C[0,1]\to\Pi_{n}
that fixes
e_{0}
and
e_{j}
, and converges in the strong operator topology to the identity as
n\to\infty
. The operator is a linear combination of the classical Bernstein basis
\{p_{n,k}\}_{k=0,\dots,n}
, thus produces polynomials of degree
n
and is explicitly given by

B_{n,0,j}(f;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}f\left(t_{n,k}^{j}\right)p_{n,k}(x),

(1.3)

where

t_{n,k}^{j}=\left(\dfrac{k(k-1)\dots(k-j+1)}{n(n-1)\dots(n-j+1)}\right)^{1/j}.

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

For
j=1
this is the classical Bernstein operator (reproducing
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
).

(ii)

B_{n,0,j}
is linear, positive and of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}l_{n,k}(f)p_{n,k}(x),

(1.4)

where
l_{n,k}
are positive linear functionals with
l_{n,k}(e_{0})=1
.
Obviously all the
B_{n,0,j}
s map into
\Pi_{n}[0,1]
(in contrast to the
B_{n}^{\tau}
s from above, case
\tau=e_{j}
).

(iii)

For
j
fixed and
n=j
we have
(t_{n,k}^{j})_{k=0,\dots,n}=(0,\dots,0,1)
with
n
zeros preceding the
1
.

For
n=j+1
one obtains
(t_{n,k}^{j})_{k=0,\dots,n}=\left(0,\dots,0,n^{\frac{1}{j}},1\right)
, and so on. That is, only for
n
big enough all the nodes will be distinct.

(IV)

The notation
B_{n,0,j}
is motivated by the fact that the operator reproduces
e_{0}
and
e_{j}
. As shown by Finta in
[
10
]
, see Theorem 2.1 there, no sequence
(L_{n})
of type (
1.4
) can reproduce
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
,
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots\},\,\,i<j
. So reproduction of
e_{0}
is a must!

Moreover, there exist infinitely many sequences of operators
L_{n}
of type (
1.4
) which approximate each continuous function on
[0,1]
,
and have the functions
e_{0}
and
e_{j}
as fixed points, where
j\in\{1,2,\dots\}
is given (see
[
10
, Th. 2.4]
).

2
On direct estimates for
B_{n,0,j}

We start with some brief history.
For the case
j=2
,
n\geq 2,
an inequality involving
\omega_{1}
(following Shisha
\&
Mond) was given by Cardenas-Morales et al. in
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
as follows

|B_{n,0,2}f(x)-f(x)|\leq\omega_{1}(f,\delta)\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\delta}\sqrt{2x(1-x)\dfrac{1-(1-x)^{n-1}}{n-1}}\right),

f\in C[0,1],\,x\in[0,1]\textrm{ and }\delta>0.

Moreover, in 2014 Finta showed (see
[
11
]
)

|B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq 2\omega_{1}\left(f;\left(\dfrac{2j(3+4j)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2j}}\right),

f\in C[0,1],\,x\in[0,1]\textrm{ and }n\geq j\geq 2.

In 2018 Aldaz and Render considered a generalization of the classical Bernstein operators on the polynomial spaces, which reproduce
{\bf 1}
and a polynomial
f_{1}
, strictly increasing on
(0,1)
. Denote by
B_{n}^{f_{1}}
this Bernstein type operator. For
f\in C[0,1]
,
x\in[0,1]
(see
[
4
, Remark following Th. 6.5]
)

|B_{n}^{f_{1}}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq(c_{S}+1)\omega_{1}\left(f,n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
c_{S}=\dfrac{4306+837\sqrt{6}}{5832}
, Sikkema’s constant. The drawback here is that the latter is shown to be valid only for
n
sufficiently large.

We will improve the estimates known so far in three different ways.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in C[0,1]
,
x\in[0,1]
,
n\geq j\geq 1
. Then

|f(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|\leq 1\cdot\omega_{2}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\omega_{1}\left(f;\dfrac{j-1}{n}\right).

(2.1)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|f(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)-B_{n}(f;x)|+|B_{n}(f;x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|

\displaystyle\leq 1\cdot\omega_{2}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\omega_{1}\left(f;\dfrac{j-1}{n}\right).

The first summand is taken from Păltănea
[
18
,
19
]
, while the second was given by Acu and Raşa
[
1
]
. For
j=1
this reduces to a best possible result for classical Bernstein operators.
∎

There is a second way to prove a similar inequality. To this end we use Păltănea’s general theorem for positive linear operators reproducing constant functions (see
[
19
, Cor.2.2.1, p.31]
).

\displaystyle|B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq\dfrac{1}{h}|B_{n,0,j}(e_{1}-x;x)|\cdot\omega_{1}(f;h)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}B_{n,0,j}\left((e_{1}-x)^{2};x\right)\right)\cdot\omega_{2}(f;h),\,\,0<h\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f\in C[0,1]
,
x\in[0,1]
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Then

a)

|B_{n,0,2}(f;x)\!-\!f(x)|\!\leq\!\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}}d_{n}(x)\omega_{1}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n\!-\!1}}\right)\!+\!\left(1\!+\!xd_{n}(x)\right)\omega_{2}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n\!-\!1}}\right)
,
n\geq 5
, where
d_{n}(x):=(1-x)\left[1-(1-x)^{n-1}\right]
;

b)

|B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq(j-1)\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\omega_{1}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{8}+j\right)\omega_{2}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right),\,n\geq 4.

Proof.

a) In order to use Păltănea’s result we estimate
B_{n,0,2}(e_{1}-x;x)
and
B_{n,0,2}((e_{1}-x)^{2};x)
.

According to
[
8
, (3.7)]
there holds

\displaystyle|B_{n,0,2}(e_{1}-x;x)|

\displaystyle=|B_{n,0,2}(e_{1};x)-x|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1-x}{n-1}\left(1-(1-x)^{n-1}\right)=\dfrac{d_{n}(x)}{n-1}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle 0\leq B_{n,0,2}\left((e_{1}-x)^{2};x\right)

\displaystyle=2x\left(x-B_{n,0,2}(e_{1};x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2xd_{n}(x)}{n-1}.

Taking
h=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}}
, we get the estimation a).

In order to illustrate the nonsymmetry of the situation, in Fig.
1
are the graphs of the function
d_{n}(x)
for
n=5
and
n=10
,
x\in[0,1]
.

Figure 1:
d_{n}(x)
for
n=5
and
n=10
,
x\in[0,1]

b) One has to determine global estimates for
|B_{n,0,j}(e_{1}-x;x)|
and
B_{n,0,j}\left((e_{1}-x)^{2};x\right)
, using again
B_{n,0,j}(e_{0};x)=1
.
We have

\displaystyle B_{n,0,j}(e_{1}-x;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\dots\dfrac{k-j+1}{n-j+1}\right)^{1/j}-\dfrac{k}{n}\right]p_{n,k}(x).

The result from
[
1
, Section 4.7]
, namely

-\dfrac{j-1}{n}\leq\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\dots\dfrac{k-j+1}{n-j+1}\right)^{1/j}-\dfrac{k}{n}\leq 0,

yields

|B_{n,0,j}(e_{1}-x;x)|\leq\dfrac{j-1}{n}.

(2.2)

The second moment can be written as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq B_{n,0,j}\left((e_{1}-x)^{2};x\right)

\displaystyle=B_{n,0,j}(e_{2};x)-x^{2}-2x\left[B_{n,0,j}(e_{1};x)-x\right]

\displaystyle\leq B_{n,0,j}(e_{2};x)-x^{2}+2x\dfrac{j-1}{n},

(2.3)

where we used (
2.2
).

We have

\displaystyle B_{n,0,j}(e_{2};x)-x^{2}

\displaystyle=B_{n,0,j}(e_{2};x)-B_{n}(e_{2};x)+B_{n}(e_{2};x)-x^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\cdots\dfrac{k-j+1}{n-j+1}\right)^{\frac{2}{j}}-\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\right)^{2}\right]p_{n,k}(x)+\dfrac{x(1-x)}{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\cdots\dfrac{k-j+1}{n-j+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{j}}-\dfrac{k}{n}\right]\left[\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\cdots\dfrac{k-j+1}{n-j+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{j}}+\dfrac{k}{n}\right]p_{n,k}(x)+\dfrac{x(1-x)}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{x(1-x)}{n}.

Together with (
2
) this yields

0\leq B_{n,0,j}\left((e_{1}-x)^{2};x\right)\leq\dfrac{x(1-x)}{n}+2x\dfrac{j-1}{n}.

So we arrive at

\displaystyle|B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{j-1}{n}\dfrac{1}{h}\omega_{1}(f;h)+\left\{1+\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}\left[\dfrac{x(1-x)}{n}+2x\dfrac{j-1}{n}\right]\right\}\omega_{2}(f;h)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{j-1}{n}\dfrac{1}{h}\omega_{1}(f;h)+\left\{1+\dfrac{1}{2nh^{2}}\left(\dfrac{1}{4}+2j\right)\right\}\omega_{2}(f;h).

(2.4)

Let
h=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
,
n\geq 4
. This leads to our proposition.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

a) The right hand side of inequality (
2
) is not a pointwise one and so does not express interpolation at the endpoints. There is room for improvement.

b) If one compares the estimate from Proposition
2.2
b) and the estimate from Proposition
2.1
it is clear that
\omega_{2}\left(f;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
is always the worse (i.e., dominant) term. In case
f\in C^{2}[0,1]
both inequalities give
{\mathcal{O}}\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)
. This cannot be reached by any of the previous inequalities in terms of
\omega_{1}
only .

The inequalities (
2.1
) and those from Proposition
2.2
do not imply that the function
e_{j}
is reproduced by
B_{n,0,j}
if
j\geq 2
. In order to achieve this we follow an idea of Marsden and Schoenberg
[
17
, p.82]
(see also
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
). Below we will use a variation of an analogue of their
\omega_{1}^{*}(f;\delta)
, namely
\omega_{j}^{*}(f;\delta)
given by

\omega_{j}^{*}(f;\delta):=\displaystyle\inf_{l}\left\{\omega_{1}(f-l;\delta)\right\},\textrm{ where }l(x)=ae_{j},\,\,a\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that we won’t loose anything in doing so!

Proposition 2.3
.

For
f\in C[0,1]
,
x\in[0,1]
and
n\geq j
we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|\leq\displaystyle\inf_{l}\left\{\omega_{2}\left(f-l;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\omega_{1}\left(f-l;\dfrac{j-1}{n}\right)\right\}.

Proof.

Since
B_{n,0,j}
reproduces
e_{j}
we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|

\displaystyle=\left|(f-l)(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f-l;x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1\cdot\omega_{2}\left(f-l;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\omega_{1}\left(f-l;\dfrac{j-1}{n}\right).

Since
l=ae_{j}
was arbitrary, we pass to the
inf
and get the result.

Moreover, if
f
is of the form
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{j}x^{j},\,a_{0},a_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we arrive at

|f(x)-B_{n,0,j}(f;x)|=0.

∎

Analogous statements follow from the inequalities in Proposition
2.2
.

Remark 2.2
.

Except for the papers previously cited we did not find any reference mentioning or using
\omega_{l}^{*}
or modifications thereof. The similarity to K-functionals is obvious.

In
[
14
]
it is mentioned that

\omega_{1}^{*}(f;\delta)\leq c\cdot\omega_{2}(f;\sqrt{\delta}).

3
Iterates of
B_{n,0,j}

Using the approach of Agratini and Rus (see
[
2
]
) the following result for the sequence of iterates
(B_{n,0,j}^{m})_{m\geq 1}
is obtained.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geq j
fixed and
m\geq 1
, the iterates sequence
(B_{n,0,j}^{m})_{m\geq 1}
satifies

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}B_{n,0,j}^{m}(f;x)=f(0)+\left[f(1)-f(0)\right]e_{j}(x).

Proof.

Denote

X_{\alpha,\beta}:=\left\{f\in C[0,1]\,\,\left|\,\,f(0)=\alpha,\,f(1)=\beta\right.\right\},\,\,(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}}.

The operators
B_{n,0,j}
interpolate
0
and
1
:

\displaystyle B_{n,0,j}(f;0)=f(t_{n,0}^{j})p_{n,0}(x)=f(0),

\displaystyle B_{n,0,j}(f;1)=f(t_{n,n}^{j})p_{n,0}(x)=f(1).

Let
f,g\in X_{\alpha,\beta}
. Then, for
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle|B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-B_{n,0,j}(g;x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}[f(t_{n,k})-g(t_{n,k})]p_{n,k}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-p_{n,0}(x)-p_{n,n}(x)\right)\cdot\|f-g\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\right)\|f-g\|_{\infty}.\

Therefore,
B_{n,0,j}|_{X_{\alpha,\beta}}:X_{\alpha,\beta}\to X_{\alpha,\beta}
is a contraction for every
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\geq j
fixed.

The function
p_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}:=f(0)+\left(f(1)-f(0)\right)e_{j}
belongs to
X_{f(0),f(1)}
and is a fixed point of
B_{n,0,j}
.

By contradiction we obtain the claim.

∎

The above result was also obtained by Gavrea and Ivan as an example for a general theorem concerning the limit of the iterates of positive linear operators (see
[
13
]
). No corresponding quantitative version is known to us.

4
Voronovskaya result for the Bernstein-type operators
B_{n,0,j}

The asymptotic formula of the operator
B_{n,0,j}
was stated as a conjecture by Cárdenas-Morales et al.
[
8
]
and proved by Birou in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(see
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
) For all
f\in C[0,1]
,
x\in(0,1)
and
j\geq 1
, whenever
f^{\prime\prime}(x)
exists,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n(B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x))=\dfrac{x(1-x)}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x)-\dfrac{(j-1)(1-x)}{2}f^{\prime}(x).

(4.1)

A quantitative
pre-Voronovskaya theorem
for the Bernstein type operator
B_{n,0,j}
was proved by Finta
[
12
]
using the first order Ditzian-Totik modulus of smoothness
defined by

\omega_{\varphi}^{1}(f;\delta)=\displaystyle\sup_{0<h\leq\delta}\sup_{x\pm\frac{1}{2}h\varphi(x)}\left|f\left(x+\frac{1}{2}h\varphi(x)\right)-f\left(x-\frac{1}{2}h\varphi(x)\right)\right|,

where
\varphi(x)=\sqrt{x(1-x)}
,
x\in[0,1]
.
We call this "pre-Voronovskaya" since it appears to point into a good direction but the limit is not explicitly given.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
12
]
There exists
C>0
depending only on
j
such that

\left|n(B_{n,0,j}(f;x)-f(x))+f^{\prime}(x)nB_{n,0,j}(xe_{0}-e_{1};x)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x)nB_{n,0,j}((e_{1}-xe_{0})^{2};x)\right|

\leq C\omega_{\varphi}^{1}\left(f^{\prime\prime};\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

for all
x\in[0,1]
,
f\in C^{2}[0,1]
and
n\geq j\geq 1
.

No quantitative version of a Voronovskaya-type theorem for the operators of Aldaz et al. is known to us.

Let

of dimension
n^{2}
given in
[
20
]

[
12

defined the rank as a measure of complexity for Hopf algebras and classified all finite dimensional pointed Hopf algebras of rank one over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero by using group datums. Scherotzke
[
17

classified such Hopf algebras for the case of char

[
16

. The Taft algebra

and the Radford algebra

The finite dimensional pointed rank one Hopf algebras are clarified into two types: nilpotent type and non-nilpotent type
[
12
]
. The Taft algebra

is of nilpotent type, but the Radford algebra

is of non-nilpotent type.

The representations of finite dimensional pointed Hopf algebras of rank one was well studied. For instance, Cibils
[
10
]

[
9
]
and

14

, the authors studied respectively the Green rings of the Taft algebras and the generalized Taft algebras. Wang, Li and Zhang studied the representations and Green rings of finite dimensional pointed rank one Hopf algebras of nilpotent type and non-nilpotent type over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero in
[
21
,
22
]
, respectively. The representations of Drinfeld doubles of finite dimensional pointed Hopf algebras of rank one also got many achievements. The second author of this paper described the structures of Drinfeld doubles of Taft algebras and classified the finite dimensional indecomposable modules over such Drinfeld doubles in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5

[

,
7
,
8
,
18
,
23

When

2.
Drinfeld doubles of Radford Hopf algebras

In this section, we recall the Radford Hopf algebras and their Drinfeld doubles.

A quadruple
\mathcal{D}=(G,\chi,g,\mu)
is called a
group datum
if
G
is a finite group,
\chi
is a
\Bbbk
-valued character of
G
,
g
is a central element of
G
and
\mu\in\Bbbk
subject to
\chi^{n}=1
or
\mu(g^{n}-1)=0
, where
n
is the order of
\chi(g)
. The group datum
\mathcal{D}
is said to be of
nilpotent type
if
\mu(g^{n}-1)=0
, and it is of
non-nilpotent type
if
\mu(g^{n}-1)\neq 0
and
\chi^{n}=1
. For any group datum
\mathcal{D}=(G,\chi,g,\mu)
, Krop and Radford constructed an associated finite dimensional pointed rank one Hopf algebra
H_{\mathcal{D}}
and classified such Hopf algebras. They also described the Drinfeld doubles
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
of
H_{\mathcal{D}}
, see
[
12
]
.

Let
n>1
and
m>1
be integers and let
q\in\Bbbk
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Then the Radford Hopf algebra
R_{mn}(q)
can be described by a group datum as follows. Let
G
be a cyclic group of order
mn
with generator
g
and let
\chi
be the
\Bbbk
-valued character of
G
defined by
\chi(g)=q
. Then
\mathcal{D}=(G,\chi,g,1)
is a group datum of non-nilpotent type, and the associated Hopf algebra
H_{\mathcal{D}}
is exactly isomorphic to
R_{mn}(q)
. Regarding
R_{mn}(q)=H_{\mathcal{D}}
from now on. Then
R_{mn}(q)
is generated as an algebra by
g
and
x
subject to the relations:

g^{mn}=1,\quad x^{n}=g^{n}-1,\quad xg=qgx.

The comultiplication
\triangle
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
are given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\triangle(x)=x\otimes g+1\otimes x,&\varepsilon(x)=0,&S(x)=-xg^{-1},\\
\triangle(g)=g\otimes g,&\varepsilon(g)=1,&S(g)=g^{-1}=g^{mn-1}.\end{array}

R_{mn}(q)
has a
\Bbbk
-basis
\{g^{i}x^{j}|i\in\mathbb{Z}_{mn},0\leqslant j\leqslant n-1\}
. For the details, one can refer to
[
12
,
24
]
.

Let
\xi\in\Bbbk
be a primitive
mn
-th root of unity with
q=\xi^{m}
. Then by
[
12
, Subsection 2.1]
, the Drinfeld double
D(R_{mn}(q)))
can be described as follows.
D(R_{mn}(q)))
is generated as an algebra by
a,b,c
and
d
subject to the following relations:

\begin{array}[]{c}a^{n}=b^{n}-1,\ b^{mn}=1,\ c^{mn}=1,\ d^{n}=0,\\
ab=qba,\ dc=\xi cd,\ bc=cb,\ bd=qdb,\ ad-da=b-c^{m},\\
ac=\xi^{-1}ca+\frac{\xi^{-1}-\xi^{n-1}}{(n-1)!_{q}}(c^{m+1}-qbc)d^{n-1}.\\
\end{array}

The comultiplication, the counit and the antipode of
D(R_{mn}(q)))
are given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\triangle(a)=a\otimes b+1\otimes a,\quad\triangle(b)=b\otimes b,\quad\triangle(d)=c^{m}\otimes d+d\otimes 1,\\
\triangle(c)=c\otimes c+(\xi^{n}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(k)!_{q}(n-k)!_{q}}c^{mk+1}d^{n-k}\otimes cd^{k},\\
\varepsilon(a)=0,\quad\varepsilon(b)=1,\quad\varepsilon(c)=1,\quad\varepsilon(d)=0,\\
S(a)=-ab^{-1},\quad S(b)=b^{-1},\quad S(c)=c^{-1},\quad S(d)=-c^{-m}d.\\
\end{array}

Throughout the following, assume that
\Bbbk
contains a primitive
mn
-th root
\xi
of unity with
q=\xi^{m}
. Then
{\rm char}(\Bbbk)\nmid mn
. Let
H_{mn}(\xi):=D(R_{mn}(q))
be the Drinfeld double of
R_{mn}(q)
as described above. Obviously,
H_{mn}(\xi)
has a
\Bbbk
-basis
\{a^{i}b^{j}c^{l}d^{k}|j,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{mn},0\leqslant i,k\leqslant n-1\}
, and
{\rm dim}(H_{mn}(\xi))=m^{2}n^{4}
.
For simplicity, we denote the image of an integer
i
under the canonical epimorphism
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{mn}
still by
i
.

and

\Bbbk G
is a subalgebra of

and the Jacobson radical

(d)
generated by

A/J(A)
is exactly isomorphism to the group algebra
\Bbbk G

A
-module

is semisimple if and only if

, and there is a 1-1 correspondence between the simple
A
-modules and the simple
\Bbbk G
-modules. This implies that the simple

-modules are in 1-1 correspondence with the
\Bbbk
-valued characters of
G
since

is abelian. For each character
\lambda
, let
\Bbbk_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding one dimensional
A
-module, and let
Z(\lambda):=H_{mn}(\xi)\otimes_{A}\Bbbk_{\lambda}
denote the induced
H_{mn}(\xi)
-module. Since

is a free right

-module with an
A
-basis
\{a^{i}|0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\}
,
Z(\lambda)
is an
n
-dimensional
H_{mn}(\xi)
-module
with a
\Bbbk
-basis
\{a^{i}\otimes_{A}1_{\lambda}|0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\}
, where

is a nonzero element of
\Bbbk_{\lambda}
.

For any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is a character
\lambda_{ij}
of
G
given by
\lambda_{ij}(b)=\xi^{j}
and
\lambda_{ij}(c)=\xi^{i}
. Clearly, any character of

has this form.
Moreover, for any

Let

. Then by
[
12

is simple if and only if
\lambda_{ij}(bc^{-m})=\xi^{j-mi}\notin\{1,q,q^{2},\cdots,q^{n-2}\}
if and only if

. In this case,
\{a^{k}\otimes_{A}1_{\lambda_{ij}}|0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
is a
\Bbbk
-basis of

. Moreover, a straightforward computation shows that

Z(\lambda_{ij})^{d}=\Bbbk(1\otimes_{A}1_{\lambda_{ij}})
. Now suppose
(i,j)\notin I_{0}
. Then there is a unique integer

with
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
such that
\xi^{j-mi}=q^{l-1}
. In this case,
Z(\lambda_{ij})
contains a unique proper submodule
{\rm rad}Z(\lambda_{ij})={\rm span}\{a^{k}\otimes_{A}1_{\lambda_{ij}}|l\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
by

12
, Proposition 6(b)]
. Denote the quotient module

by

. Then

is simple. Define

for

m_{1}=1\otimes_{A}1_{\lambda_{ij}}+{\rm rad}Z(\lambda_{ij})

-basis of

-module action on

is determined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 2.84544ptam_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{k}(l,i)m_{k+1},&1\leqslant k<l-1,\\
0,&k=l,\\
\end{array}\right.&dm_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&k=1,\\

3.2.

\Omega^{2}M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\cong M_{t}(l,i,\eta)
. Hence an argument similar to
T_{2}(l,i)
shows that
\Omega^{2}M_{t+1}(l,i,\eta)\cong M_{t+1}(l,i,\eta)
.

(4) By (2) and Theorems
4.18
(2) and
4.19
(2),
M_{t+1}(l,i,\eta)\ncong T_{(t+1)m}(l^{\prime},i^{\prime})
and
M_{t+1}(l,i,\eta)\ncong\overline{T}_{(t+1)m}(l^{\prime},i^{\prime})
for any
1\leqslant l^{\prime}\leqslant n-1
and
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Summarizing the discussion above, one gets the following theorem.

Theorem 4.37
.

For any
\eta\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
l,i,t\in\mathbb{Z}
with

and

H_{mn}(\xi)
-module

of
(tm,tm)
-type. Moreover,

(1)

\Omega^{2}M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\cong M_{t}(l,i,\eta)
,
{\rm soc}M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}tM(l,i+kn)
and

M_{t}(l,i,\eta)/{\rm soc}M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}tM(n-l,i+l+kn)

(2)

If
M_{t}(l,i,\eta)

(s,s)

M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\ncong T_{tm}(l^{\prime},i^{\prime})

There are AR-sequences

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M_{1}(l,i,\eta)\xrightarrow{f_{1}}M_{2}(l,i,\eta)\xrightarrow{g_{1}}M_{1}(l,i,\eta)\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M_{t}(l,i,\eta)\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{matrix}g_{t-1}\\

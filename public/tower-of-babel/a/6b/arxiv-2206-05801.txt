An Elementary Proof of the Local Kronecker-Weber Theorem
Jochen Koenigsmann, Benedikt Stock

Abstract
We will present a novel elementary and self-contained proof of the local Kronecker-Weber theorem. Apart from discrete valuation theory, it does not make use of any tools beyond those introduced in a second undergraduate course on algebra. In particular, we will not make use of results from local class field theory or Galois cohomology.



“There are several ways to prove the Kronecker–Weber theorem, even if class field theory […] seems inevitable.”
– P. Guillot (Preface,
[
3
]
)

1
Introduction

The Kronecker-Weber theorem states that every finite abelian extension of the rational numbers is contained in a cyclotomic extension. A number of proofs of this result are known. Culler
[
2
]
presents two proofs; an elementary approach based on David Hilbert’s original proof and one that makes use of (global) class field theory. Another classical approach goes via the local Kronecker-Weber theorem – the subject of this article – where the role of
\mathbb{Q}
is replaced by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Once the local version is established, it is not hard to deduce the global version via completions, Minkowski’s bound, and basic properties of discriminants (cf.
[
3
]
, Chapter 14 for an excellent presentation of a complete proof).

Similar to the global version, a few different proofs of the local Kronecker-Weber theorem are known. Perhaps the most classical one goes via the fundamental theorem of local class field theory, a result that itself is usually either proved via the cohomological approach (cf.
[
3
]
) or Lubin-Tate theory (cf.
[
1
,
4
]
). Our proof is distinct from those as it makes no mention of local class field theory at all. Papers by Rosen
[
8
]
and Kozuka
[
6
]
have a similar goal in mind. However, the new approach presented here differs from these in that it is self-contained and does not even mention Lubin-Tate constructions or Galois cohomology. Instead, our considerations are merely based on discrete valuation theory combined with basic results from (infinite) Galois theory and Kummer theory.

In the next section we will remind the reader of some facts about local fields before presenting the proof of the local Kronecker-Weber theorem in section
3
.

2
Some facts about local number fields

Let us first fix some notation. We call finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

local number fields
. For a finite local number field
L
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
its ring of integers, by
\M_{L}
the unique maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, and by
\overline{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}/\M_{L}
its residue field. Moreover, we take
v_{L}
to mean the unique extension of the
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}
to
L
. Finally, for an extension
L/F
of local number fields of degree
n
, we denote by
e=e(L/F)
and
f=f(L/F)
the ramification index and inertia degree of the extension, respectively.

We remind the reader of the fundamental equality
n=ef
(cf.
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.38;
[
7
]
, II. 6.8). What is more, there exists an intermediate field
L_{0}
, called the
inertia subfield
of
L
, with the property that for any intermediate field
F\subseteq K\subseteq L
, the extension
K/F
is unramified if and only if
K\subseteq L_{0}
(cf.
[
3
]
, Corollary 2.43).

We shall also remind the reader that we have very explicit descriptions for the cyclotomic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(cf.
[
3
]
, Propositions 2.46 and 2.48) as summarized in the next lemma. Here and in the remaining text, we let
C_{n}
,
\mu_{n}
, and
\zeta_{n}
denote the cyclic group of order
n
, the group of
n
th roots of unity, and a primitive
n
th root of unity, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{N})
be a cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

•

If
(N,p)=1
, then the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified, and its degree
n
is the smallest natural number such that
p^{n}\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)
. In particular,
L=L_{0}
and
\text{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong C_{n}
.

•

If
N=p^{n}
, then
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is totally ramified of degree
p^{n-1}(p-1)
. Moreover,
\text{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and
1-\zeta_{p^{n}}
is a uniformizer of
L
with
v_{L}(1-\zeta_{p^{n}})=\frac{1}{p^{n-1}(p-1)}
and field-theoretic norm
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(1-\zeta_{p^{n}})=p
.

Corollary 2.2
.

The roots of unity in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are exactly
\mu_{p-1}
for
p
odd, and
\mu_{2}
for
p=2
.

The following subgroups of the unit groups of local number will play a central role in our argument.

Definition 2.3

(higher unit groups)
.

Let
L
be a local number field. The
n
-th higher unit group
is given by
U^{(0)}_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
for
n=0
and
U^{(n)}_{L}=\{x\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:x\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \M_{L}^{n})\}
for
n\geq 1
.

One can easily classify the quotients of consecutive higher unit groups. For a local number field
L
,

U_{L}^{(0)}/U_{L}^{(1)}\cong\overline{L}^{\times}

(consider the map
x\mapsto\overline{x}
from
U_{L}^{(0)}=\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
to
\overline{L}^{\times}
), and

U_{L}^{(n)}/U_{L}^{(n+1)}\cong\overline{L}

for any integer
n\geq 1
(take a uniformizer
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
and consider the map
x\mapsto\overline{~\frac{x-1}{\pi^{n}}~}
from
U^{(n)}_{L}
to
\overline{L}
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L
be a local number field,
\pi
denote a uniformizer of
L
, and
f=f(L/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Then we may represent every
a\in U_{L}^{(1)}/U_{L}^{(n+1)}
uniquely as

a=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\pi^{i},

with coefficients
a_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mu_{p^{f}-1}
.

Proof.

From the isomorphisms just presented, we see that the size is correct. To show uniqueness, assume two sums
1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\pi^{i}
and
1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\prime}\pi^{i}
represented the same element. Then there would be a smallest
i
with
a_{i}\neq a_{i}^{\prime}
and we derive a contradiction by considering both sums modulo
U_{L}^{(i+1)}
.
∎

The following lemma is a neat result that will come in handy multiple times.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with
\zeta_{p}\in F
and valuation
v
extending
v_{p}
. Then
\overline{\frac{p}{(1-\zeta_{p})^{p-1}}}=-1
.

Proof.

Write

g(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}X^{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}(X-\zeta_{p}^{i}),

so

\frac{p}{(1-\zeta_{p})^{p-1}}=\frac{g(1)}{(1-\zeta_{p})^{p-1}}=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\frac{1-\zeta^{i}_{p}}{1-\zeta_{p}}=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\zeta_{p}^{j}.

By lemma
2.1
,
\zeta_{p}-1
is a uniformizer of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
. Hence,
\zeta_{p}-1\in\M_{F}
, i.e.,
\zeta_{p}\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \M_{F})
. This implies
\zeta_{p}^{j}\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \M_{F})
for all integers
j
, so
\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\zeta_{p}^{j}\equiv i\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \M_{F})
. This means that
\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\zeta_{p}^{j}\equiv(p-1)!\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \M_{F})
, so the claim follows from Wilson’s theorem since
p\in\M_{F}
.
∎

The next two corollaries and their proofs are slight adaptations of an idea first observed by Koenigsmann (
[
5
]
, Lemma 3.2). Following our philosophy of a self-contained presentation, we present it here again for the convenience of the reader.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
finite with
\zeta_{p}\in F
and ramification index
e
. Then
U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\subseteq\left(U^{(\frac{e}{p-1})}\right)^{p}
.

Proof.

First, note that the statement makes sense as
\zeta_{p}\in F
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is totally ramified of degree
p-1
by lemma
2.1
, so
p-1|e
.

Now let us denote by
\Pi
a uniformizer of
F
and by
v
the unique valuation of
F
extending
v_{p}
. Moreover, define
\pi:=1-\zeta_{p}
, which is a uniformizer of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
with
v(\pi)=\frac{1}{p-1}
by lemma
2.1
. Now, by (the extension of) Lagrange’s theorem, we see that
v\left(\Pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}}\right)=v(\pi)
, so
\left(\Pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}}\right)=(\pi)
and
\left(\Pi^{\frac{ep}{p-1}}\right)=(\pi)^{p}
. This implies in particular that we may rewrite
U^{(\frac{e}{p-1})}=1+(\pi)
and
U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}=1+\pi^{p}(\Pi)
.

So take
x\in U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
and write
x=1+\pi^{p}a
with
a\in(\Pi)
. We need to show that the polynomial
Y^{p}-(1+\pi^{p}a)=0
has a root in
1+(\pi)
, i.e., a solution of the form
Y=1+\pi X
, where
X\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Dividing the polynomial by
\pi^{p}
and plugging in
Y=1+\pi X
, this becomes equivalent to finding a root
X\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
of

f(X)=\left(\frac{Y}{\pi}\right)^{p}-\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{p}}+a\right)=\left(X+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{p}-\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{p}}+a\right).

Now,

\displaystyle f(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\binom{p}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{p-i}X^{i}-\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{p}}+a\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\binom{p}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{p-i}X^{i}-a,

and we claim that
f(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}[X]
. Indeed, as observed above,
v(\pi)=\frac{1}{p-1}
, so the coefficients of
X^{i}
(
i>0
) satisfy

v\left(\binom{p}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{p-i}\right)=\underbrace{v\left(\binom{p}{i}\right)}_{\geq 1\text{~since~}p|\binom{p}{i}}-(p-i)\underbrace{v\left(\pi\right)}_{=\frac{1}{p-1}}\geq 0.

We can say even more. For
1<i\leq p
, the above inequality is strict while we get equality for
i=1
. As
a\in(\Pi)
, this means that

\displaystyle\overline{f}(X)=\overline{\frac{p}{\pi^{p-1}}}X+X^{p}.

(1)

But by the previous lemma
2.5
,\overline{\frac{p}{\pi^{p-1}}}=-1
, so the claim follows immediately from Hensel’s lemma since
\overline{f}(X)
is separable and splits in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
∎

Adapting the proof of the previous lemma slightly even gives rise to a partial converse.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with
\zeta_{p}\in F
and assume
1+(1-\zeta_{p})^{\frac{e}{p-1}}a\in F^{p}
for some
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Then there exists
x\in\overline{F}
such that
x^{p}-x-\overline{a}=0

Proof.

Setting
\pi=1-\zeta_{p}
as before,
1+\pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}}a\in F^{p}
means that the equation
Y^{p}-(1+\pi^{p}a)=0
has a root in
F
. Now, analogous to the above, we set
X=\frac{Y-1}{\pi}\Leftrightarrow Y=1+\pi X
, giving rise to a root of the polynomial
f(X)
from above. Previously,
a\in(\pi)
(so
\overline{a}=0
), while now we have
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Therefore, equation (
1
) now becomes
\overline{f}(X)=X^{p}-X-\overline{a}
, and the claim follows.
∎

In some cases, the inclusion stated in corollary
2.6
even becomes an equality.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
, then
U_{K}^{(p+1)}=\left(U_{K}^{(1)}\right)^{p}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
U^{(p+1)}\subseteq\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}
was shown in corollary
2.6
(as
e(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})=p-1
by lemma
2.1
).

For the reversed inclusion, we let
x\in\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}\subseteq U^{(1)}
. Then it suffices to show that
x
represents a unit in
U^{(1)}/U^{(p+1)}
. Write
x=(1+a\pi)^{p}
for
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
and
\pi=1-\zeta_{p}
. As
\frac{p}{\pi^{p-1}}\in-1+(\pi)
by lemma
2.5
, we see that
p\in-\pi^{p-1}+(\pi)^{p}
. Then

x=(1+a\pi)^{p}=1+\underbrace{pa\pi}_{\in-a\pi^{p}+(\pi)^{p+1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}\binom{p}{i}(a\pi)^{i}}_{\in p(\pi)^{2}=(\pi)^{p+1}}+a^{p}\pi^{p}\equiv 1+(a^{p}-a)\pi^{p}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ (\pi^{p+1})),

Hence, by lemma
2.4
,
x
may be represented as
1+(a^{p}-a)\pi^{p}
in
U^{(1)}/U^{(p+1)}
. But
\overline{a}^{p}-\overline{a}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
, so
x
is indeed reduced to 1 modulo
U^{(p+1)}
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension of degree
n
with
\zeta_{p}\in F
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(F^{\times}/F^{\times p})=n+2
.

Proof.

As usual, we let
\pi
denote a uniformizer of
F
and let
f
be the inertia degree of
F
. It is then easy to see that
F^{\times}=\langle\pi\rangle\times\mu_{p^{f}-1}\times U_{F}^{(1)}
(cf.
[
3
]
, Proposition 4.2). Now
\mu_{p^{f}-1}\cong\overline{F}^{\times}\cong\overline{F}^{\times p}
by the properties of finite fields. Hence, if we add
\pi
to our basis, all that remains to be shown is that
U^{(1)}/(F^{\times p}\cap U^{(1)})=U^{(1)}/\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}
has
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
n+1
. In other words, we want to show that
\left|U^{(1)}/\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}\right|=p^{n+1}
. To do so, we note that

U^{(1)}/\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}\equiv\frac{U^{(1)}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}}{(U^{(1)})^{p}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}}

since
U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\subseteq\left(U^{(\frac{e}{p-1})}\right)^{p}
by corollary
2.6
. Now we already know that

\left|U^{(1)}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\right|=\left(p^{f}\right)^{\frac{ep}{p-1}}=p^{\frac{np}{p-1}}=p^{n+\frac{n}{p-1}}

by lemma
2.4
. Next, we want to calculate the cardinality of
(U^{(1)})^{p}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
. To do so, consider an element of
a\in U^{(1)}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
and represent it as

a=1+a_{1}\pi+\dots+a_{\frac{e}{p-1}}\pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}}+b\pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}+1}

with
a_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mu_{p^{f}-1}
and
b\in\mathcal{O}
by lemma
2.4
. It is easy to see that
a^{p}\in U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
if
a_{i}=0
for all
i\in[\frac{e}{p-1}]
, independent of
b
(note that
p\in(\pi)^{e}
). Hence, elements of
(U^{(1)})^{p}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
can be just represented as

a^{p}=(1+a_{1}\pi+\dots+a_{\frac{e}{p-1}}\pi^{\frac{e}{p-1}})^{p}.

Multiplying this out shows that
a^{p}\in U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}
if and only if
a_{i}=0
for all
i\in[\frac{e}{p-1}-1]
and
a_{\frac{e}{p-1}}
is a
p
th power. Hence,
\left|(U^{(1)})^{p}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\right|=(p^{f})^{\frac{e}{p-1}}/p=p^{\frac{n}{p-1}-1}
, so

\left|U^{(1)}/\left(U^{(1)}\right)^{p}\right|=\frac{\left|U^{(1)}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\right|}{\left|(U^{(1)})^{p}/U^{(\frac{ep}{p-1}+1)}\right|}=\frac{p^{n+\frac{n}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{n}{p-1}-1}}=p^{n+1}

as required.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
, then the elements of the form
p^{a_{0}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\pi^{i}\right)K^{\times p}
with
a_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
form a set of representatives of
K^{\times}/K^{\times p}
.

Proof.

By corollary
2.9
, we know that
K^{\times}/K^{\times p}
has
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
p+1
, so the size is correct. Hence, it suffices to show that each element in
K^{\times}/K^{\times p}
is represented by one of these elements. So take some element in
K^{\times}/K^{\times p}
and represent it as
\pi^{n}u
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
.

Let us first show that we may represent
\pi^{n}\equiv p^{a_{0}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ O_{K}^{\times}K^{\times p})
for some
a_{0}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. Indeed, if we write
p=\pi^{p-1}u^{\prime}
for some
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, then all that remains to be shown is that there exists an
a_{0}
such that
\pi^{n}\equiv\pi^{(p-1)a_{0}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ K^{\times p})
. Now just take
a_{0}
to be a solution of the linear congruence
(p-1)a_{0}\equiv n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
.

Let us now focus on the representation of
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. We claim that, modulo
K^{\times p}
, we may assume
u\in U_{K}^{(1)}
. Indeed, it suffices to find
a\in K^{\times}
such that
ua^{p}\in U_{K}^{(1)}
, and this can be achieved by Hensels’ lemma. Then, by lemma
2.8
,

\displaystyle\frac{U_{K}^{(1)}K^{\times p}}{K^{\times p}}\cong\frac{U_{K}^{(1)}}{K^{\times p}\cap U_{K}^{(1)}}=\frac{U_{K}^{(1)}}{\left(U_{K}^{(1)}\right)^{p}}=\frac{U_{K}^{(1)}}{U_{K}^{(p+1)}},

(2)

so the result follows from lemma
2.4
.
∎

3
Proof of the local Kronecker-Weber theorem

We shall prove the following explicit version of the local Kronecker-Weber theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Local Kronecker-Weber Theorem)
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an abelian extension of degree
p^{n}\cdot m
with integers
m
and
n
such that
(p,m)=1
. Then
F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{n+2}},\zeta_{p^{p^{n}\cdot m}-1})
.

Assume that
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
has ramification index
e=p^{r}e^{\prime}
(where
(e^{\prime},p)=1
) and inertia degree
f
. We want to split the proof of the theorem into two steps, considering the cases
e=p^{r}
and
e=e^{\prime}
with
(e,p)=1
separately. To see why this is possible, note that
G=\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
being finite abelian implies that it is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. We let
H_{1}
denote the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
,
H_{2}
be the subgroup of
G
generated by all Sylow
l
-subgroups with
l\neq p
, and denote the corresponding fixed fields by
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, respectively. Then, if we let
s
be maximal with
p^{s}|f
, the fundamental equality and the Galois correspondence will give us that
[L_{1}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=\frac{ef}{p^{r+s}}
and
[L_{2}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=p^{r+s}
. By maximality of
r
and
s
, this means that
L_{1}L_{2}=F
. Hence, if we show that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are contained in cyclotomic extensions, we get that
F
is contained in the compositum of those cyclotomic extensions (which, of course, is itself cyclotomic). Hence, we are only left with showing that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are indeed contained in the cyclotomic extension described in theorem
3.1
.

We will treat both cases separately, starting with
L_{1}
. Following standard terminology, we shall refer to the
L_{1}
-case as the
tame
case and to the
L_{2}
-case as the
wild
case.

3.1
The tame case

Note that since
[L_{1}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=\frac{ef}{p^{r+s}}
and by maximality of
r
and
s
,
(e(L_{1}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}),p)=(f(L_{1}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}),p)=1
. Hence, the following lemma (with
F
taking the role of
L_{1}
) applies in our setting.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be abelian with ramification index
e
and inertia degree
f
such that
(e,p)=1
. Then
e|p-1
.

Proof.

We will establish the claim by showing that every prime power dividing
e
also divides
p-1
. So let us fix some prime
q|e
(in particular
q\neq p
) and let
r
and
s
be maximal such that
q^{r}|e
and
q^{s}|f
. Now, let
H
be the group generated by all Sylow
l
-subgroups of
G=\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
l\neq q
and let
L
denote the fixed field of
H
. Then
[L:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=[G:H]
is the maximal power of
q
in
|G|=ef
, so
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is of degree
q^{r+s}
by the choice of
r
and
s
. Moreover, let
L_{0}
denote the inertia subfield of
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Again by the choice of
r
and
s
, we get
[L_{0}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=q^{s}
. Hence,
L_{0}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{q^{s}}-1})
by lemma
2.1
and the uniqueness of inertia fields. We also set
E=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{q^{s+r}}-1})
, so
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified of degree
q^{s+r}
by lemma
2.1
. In particular, the tower law implies
[\overline{E}:\overline{L_{0}}]=q^{r}
. The following diagram gives an overview over all the extensions just described.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}

Let now
\pi
be a uniformizer of
L
. As
L
is ramified of degree
q^{r}
, we have
v_{L}\left(\frac{\pi^{q^{r}}}{p}\right)=0
so
\frac{\pi^{q^{r}}}{p}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
. Then, since
\overline{L_{0}}=\overline{L}
, we may find
u\in\mathcal{O}_{L_{0}}^{\times}
such that
\overline{u}=\overline{\frac{~\pi^{q^{r}}}{p}~}

has dimension
4
by corollary
2.9
. To show that
(1+i,1+2i,2,5)
is indeed a basis of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}(i)
, it hence suffices to show that the four elements are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}(i)
. So assume
x=(1+i)^{\alpha}\cdot(1+2i)^{\beta}\cdot 2^{\gamma}\cdot 5^{\delta}\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}(i)
for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\{0,1\}
, say
y^{2}=x
. Then, denoting by
N
the field-theoretic norm map of the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
,

2^{\alpha}\cdot 5^{\beta}\cdot 4^{\gamma}\cdot 25^{\delta}=N(x)=N(y^{2})=N(y)^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2},

so
\alpha=\beta=0
by (the proof of) lemma
3.5
(i). It remains to exclude the three cases
(\gamma,\delta)=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)
.

•

(\gamma,\delta)=(1,0)
would mean that
2\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)^{\times 2}
, so
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{2})
. But
\zeta_{8}=\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{2})
, and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{8})
totally ramified of degree
4\neq 2
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
by lemma
2.1
.

•

(\gamma,\delta)=(1,0)
would mean that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{5})=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)
. Modulo
U_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}^{(3)}=\left(U_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)^{\times 2}
,
1+1\cdot 4\equiv 1+(-1)\cdot 4=-3
, so
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{5})=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{-3})
. Now
\zeta_{3}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{-3})
. But
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{3})
is the unique unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
of degree 2 by lemma
2.1
.

•

(\gamma,\delta)=(1,1)
would mean that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{2\cdot 5}
). However, this would imply that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{5})=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,\sqrt{2})
, which contradicts what we have shown in the previous two items.

∎

We can now deduce lemma
3.6
.

Proof (lemma
3.6
).

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
be a Galois extension of degree 4 containing
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)
. Then
[F:\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)]=2
, so
F=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)(\sqrt{x})
by Kummer theory. By the previous lemma, we may assume
x=(1+i)^{\alpha}\cdot(1+2i)^{\beta}\cdot 2^{\gamma}\cdot 5^{\delta}
for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\{0,1\}
. Our goal is to show that
\alpha=\beta=0
. In that case,
x
is one of 2, 5, 10, and either way
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\sqrt{x})
is an intermediate extension of degree 2, so
F/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
has two distinct proper non-trivial intermediate fields, so it cannot be a
C_{4}
-extension.

So let us show
\alpha=\beta=0
. If
\sigma
denotes the generator of
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
(i.e.,
\sigma
is complex conjugation), then, by the uniqueness of splitting fields and Kummer theory (as in footnote
2
), we have
\sigma(x)\in\langle x\rangle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}
, i.e.,
x\equiv\sigma(x)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2})
. Now assume
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
. Then, in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rrll}&x&\equiv\sigma(x)&\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2})\\
\Longleftrightarrow&(1+i)\cdot(1+2i)\cdot 2^{\gamma}\cdot 5^{\delta}&\equiv(1-i)\cdot(1-2i)\cdot 2^{\gamma}\cdot 5^{\delta}&\\
&&\equiv(1-i)\cdot(1-2i)\cdot 2^{\gamma}\cdot 5^{\delta}(1+i)^{2}(1+2i)^{2}&\\
&&\equiv(1+i)\cdot(1+2i)\cdot 2^{\gamma+1}\cdot 5^{\delta+1}&\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2})\\
\Longleftrightarrow&0&\equiv 10&\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2}),\end{array}

contradicting the linear independence proved before. Similarly, we get contradictions
2,5\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times 2})
for
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0),(0,1)
, respectively.
∎

For the next step of our argument, we want to show that
\tilde{F}
from corollary
3.7
is cyclotomic. To do so, we prove that the groups calculated in corollary
3.7
actually appear as the Galois groups of cyclotomic extensions of
K
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{p^{\infty}}-1},\zeta_{p^{\infty}})
. Then

(i)

\text{Gal}(F/K)=\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
p=2
, and

(ii)

\text{Gal}(F/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
p>2
.

Proof.

(ii).
From elementary infinite Galois theory, we have
\text{Gal}(F/K)\cong\varprojlim\text{Gal}(E/K)
, where
E
runs over the directed partially ordered set
I
of finite Galois subextensions of
F/K
(cf.
[
9
]
, Proposition 1.3.5). By definition of
F
, these finite subextensions are of the form
E=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{p^{n}}-1},\zeta_{p^{m}})
for some non-negative integers
m,n
. Now
K(\zeta_{p^{p^{n}}-1})/K
is unramified of degree
p^{n}
with
\text{Gal}(K(\zeta_{p^{p^{n}}-1})/K)\cong C_{p^{n}}
by lemma
2.1
.
K(\zeta_{p^{m}})/K
, on the other hand, is totally ramified by lemma
2.1
, and

\text{Gal}(K(\zeta_{p^{m}})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cong C_{p^{m-1}(p-1)}\cong C_{p^{m-1}}\times C_{p-1}.

Hence, as
(p^{m-1},p-1)=1
,
\text{Gal}(K(\zeta_{p^{m}})/K)\cong\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{m}})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})/\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong C_{p^{m-1}}
. Finally,
K(\zeta_{p^{p^{n}}-1})
and
K(\zeta_{p^{m}})
are linearly disjoint as one extension is unramified and the other one totally ramified. Thus,
\text{Gal}(E/K)=C_{p^{n}}\times C_{p^{m-1}}
, and the claim follows since
\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\varprojlim_{k}C_{p^{k}}
.

(i).
As
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
is totally ramified and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{2^{\infty}}-1})/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
is unramified, we see that both extensions are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, so
\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\times\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{2^{\infty}}-1})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. By lemma
2.1
,

\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{2^{\infty}}-1})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\varprojlim_{k}\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{2^{k}}-1})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\varprojlim_{k}C_{2^{k}}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Hence, it remains to show that
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. To do so, we will set
a_{k}:=\zeta_{2^{k}}+\zeta_{2^{k}}^{-1}
and show that all finite subextensions
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
(
k\geq 2
) are given as
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i,a_{k})
, where
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2}
,
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2^{k-2}}
, and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})
are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. From elementary infinite Galois theory, we then indeed get

\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\varprojlim_{k}\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\varprojlim_{k}\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\times\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong\varprojlim_{k}C_{2}\times C_{2^{k-2}}=C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

So let us show all the required properties. We already know that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
is Galois with
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2}
from lemma
2.1
, so let us show the more difficult claim
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cong C_{2^{k-2}}
. We first show that
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]=2^{k-2}
and then exhibit a generator of the Galois group of order
2^{k-2}
. As a first step, we note that
a_{k}=\zeta_{2^{k}}+\zeta_{2^{k}}^{-1}
is given recursively by
a_{2}=0
and
a_{k+1}=\sqrt{2+a_{k}}
. Indeed, inductively we see that
a_{2}=i+(-i)=0
and

a_{k+1}^{2}=(\zeta_{2^{k+1}}+\zeta_{2^{k+1}}^{-1})^{2}=\underbrace{\zeta_{2^{k+1}}^{2}+\left(\zeta_{2^{k+1}}^{-1}\right)^{2}}_{=\zeta_{2^{k}}+\zeta_{2^{k}}^{-1}=a_{k}}+2(\underbrace{\zeta_{2^{k+1}}\zeta_{2^{k+1}}^{-1}}_{=1})=a_{k}+2.

Similarly, we see that
a_{k}
is the root of a polynomial of degree
2^{k-2}
, so
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]\leq 2^{k-2}
.

Next, if
v
denotes the unique extension of
v_{2}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}}+\zeta_{2^{k}}^{-1})
for
k\geq 3
, we claim that then
v(a_{k})=\frac{1}{2^{k-2}}
. Indeed, for
k=3
, we have
v(a_{3})=v(\sqrt{2})=\frac{1}{2}v(2)=\frac{1}{2}
. Inductively, it follow that

v(a_{k})=v\left(\sqrt{a_{k-1}+2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}v(a_{k-1}+2)=\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}

as
v(a_{k-1}+2)=\min\{v(a_{k-1}),v(2)\}
since
v(a_{k})=\frac{1}{2^{k-2}}<1=v(2)
by the inductive hypothesis. This means that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})
is ramified of degree
2^{k-2}
, so
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]\geq 2^{k-2}
. Together with the previous paragraph, we see that
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]=2^{k-2}
.

It remains to show that we can find a generator of order
2^{k-2}
of
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
for
k\geq 3
. To do so, it suffices to find
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i)/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
of order
2^{k-2}
with
\sigma(i)=i
(then
\sigma|_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})}
has the required properties). The advantage of exhibiting
\sigma
is that it automatically implies
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-linear disjointness of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})
. Indeed, if we let
L
denote the non-trivial fixed field of
\langle\sigma\rangle
, then

\underbrace{[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i):L]}_{=|\langle\sigma\rangle|=2^{k-2}}\underbrace{[L:\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}_{>1}=[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]=[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i):\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})]\underbrace{[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}_{2}]}_{=2^{k-2}},

so
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i):\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})]>1
, which implies
i\notin\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})
and hence that
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(i)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k})
are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}

To find
\sigma
, observe that over
\mathbb{Q}
, we can easily see that
\mathbb{Q}(i,a_{k})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})
. Indeed, clearly
\mathbb{Q}(i,a_{k})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})
, and the degree of the minimal polynomial of
a_{k}
over
\mathbb{Q}
is at least the degree over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
so
[\mathbb{Q}(a_{k}):\mathbb{Q}]\geq 2^{k-2}
. Now, by the recursive formula, the extension
\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q}
is totally real. Hence,
i\notin\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})
and so
[\mathbb{Q}(a_{k},i):\mathbb{Q}]\geq 2^{k-1}=[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}}):\mathbb{Q}]
, which shows
\mathbb{Q}(i,a_{k})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})
. The argument even shows that
\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})
and
\mathbb{Q}(i)
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent, so
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q})\times\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q})\cong C_{2}\times\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q})
. On the other hand, by Galois theory, we know that
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\text{Aut}(\mu_{2^{k}})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cong C_{2}\times C_{2^{k-2}}
. Therefore, necessarily
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(a_{k})/\mathbb{Q})\cong C_{2^{k-2}}
. In particular, we can find an element
\sigma^{\prime}\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q})
of order
2^{k-2}
with
\sigma^{\prime}(i)=i
. Now recall that we can embed
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
in
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q})
via
\iota^{*}
with
\iota^{*}(\tau)=\iota^{-1}\circ\tau\circ\iota
, where
\iota:\mathbb{Q}(a_{k},i)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{2}(a_{k},i)
is a completion. By lemma
2.1
,
|\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})|=2^{k-1}=|\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q})|
, so
\iota^{*}
is an isomorphism. In particular, it is surjective so we can find
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{k}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
with
\iota^{*}(\sigma)=\sigma^{\prime}
. By definition of
\iota^{*}
,
\sigma
will then be of order
2^{k-1}
and satisfy
\sigma(i)=i
.
∎

By lemma
2.1
,
F
from the previous lemma is a pro-
p
extension of
K
that is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In particular,
F\subseteq\tilde{F}
. Hence, together with corollary
3.7
and the previous lemma, this gives rise to maps

C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\text{Gal}(\tilde{F}/K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|_{F}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}\text{Gal}(F/K)\cong C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

for
p=2
, and

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}\twoheadrightarrow\text{Gal}(\tilde{F}/K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|_{F}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}\text{Gal}(F/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

for
p>2
. In either case, all epimorphisms are necessarily isomorphisms. Therefore,
F=\tilde{F}
by the infinite Galois correspondence.

We can now readily deduce the wild case. Indeed, given an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of degree
p^{n}
, we can see that the maximal abelian
p
-extension of
K
of exponent

\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{4},\zeta_{2^{n+2}},\zeta_{2^{2^{n}}-1})=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}},\zeta_{2^{2^{n}}-1})

\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{n}},\zeta_{p^{p^{n}}-1})

.

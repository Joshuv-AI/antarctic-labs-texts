On radial solutions for a Fully Non Linear degenerate or singular PDE.


Abstract
In this paper we consider equations − | ∇ u | α ​ F ​ ( D 2 ​ u ) = | u | p − 1 ​ u -|\nabla u|^{\alpha}F(D^{2}u)=|u|^{p-1}u in an annulus. Proposition 3.4
.

-|\nabla u|^{\alpha}F(D^{2}u)=u^{p}

[a,\rho(\gamma))

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow+\infty}\tau(\gamma)=a

,

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow 0}\tau(\gamma)=+\infty

Proof.

Let us denote in the following
\tau=\tau(\gamma)
.

By Lemma
3.1
, in the intervall
[a,\tau]
,
u
is increasing, concave and satisfies
Case
1. Using the left-side inequality in
Case
1, we have immediatly that, for
n_{-}
as in Lemma

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(r)=r^{(2+\alpha)(n_{-}-1)}\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{\alpha+2}}{\alpha+2}+\frac{u^{p+1}}{\lambda(p+1)}\right)

\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime}(r)\geq{(2+\alpha)(N-1)\Lambda\over\lambda}r^{(2+\alpha)(n_{-}-1)}{u^{p+1}\over\lambda(p+1)}\geq 0
in
[a,\tau]

, and this yields a first lower bound on

\displaystyle u^{p+1}(\tau,\gamma)\geq\dfrac{\lambda(p+1)}{\alpha+2}\left(\frac{a}{\tau}\right)^{(2+\alpha)(n_{-}-1)}\gamma^{\alpha+2}.

(3.2)

The left-side inequality of
Case

\displaystyle(u^{\prime})^{\alpha}u^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{(n_{-}-1)}{r}(u^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}\geq-\frac{u^{p}}{\lambda}.

Multiplying both sides by

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}(u^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\right)^{\prime}\geq-\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}}{\lambda}u^{p}

and integrating from
a
to

we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}(u^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}(r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{a^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}\gamma^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{a}^{r}s^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}u^{p}(s)ds

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{a^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}\gamma^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}-\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}u^{p}(r)(r-a)}{\lambda}.

Let us divide both sides by
\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}}{\alpha+1}
, and let us distinguish between the cases
\alpha\geq 0
and
\alpha<0

When
\alpha\geq 0
, we use the inequality
((X-Y)^{+})^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\geq X^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}-Y^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}

, with

and

. Taking the power
{1\over 1+\alpha}
of both the left hand side and the positive part of the right hand side of (

), one gets

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\geq a^{n_{-}-1}r^{1-n_{-}}\gamma-\left(\dfrac{(\alpha+1)u^{p}(r)(r-a)}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.

When

Z^{1+\alpha}={\small\dfrac{r^{(n_{-}-1)(\alpha+1)}(u^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}}

Hence the two cases can be summarized by

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-\alpha^{-}\over-\alpha^{-}+1}a^{n_{-}-1}r^{1-n_{-}}\gamma-\left(\dfrac{(\alpha+1)u^{p}(r)(r-a)}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.

\displaystyle u(\tau,\gamma)\geq 2^{-\alpha^{-}\over-\alpha^{-}+1}\dfrac{a^{n_{-}-1}\gamma}{2-n_{-}}\left[\tau^{2-n_{-}}-a^{2-n_{-}}\right]-(\alpha+1)^{2+\alpha\over\alpha+1}(\alpha+2)^{-1}\left(\dfrac{u^{p}(\tau,\gamma)(\tau-a)}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}(\tau-a).

(3.4)

with
2-n_{-}<0
.

On the other hand, using the right hand side inequality in
Case
1, we have that the second energy function

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}(r)=\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{\alpha+2}}{\alpha+2}+\frac{u^{p+1}}{\Lambda(p+1)}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}(r))|^{\alpha+2}}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\prime}\geq\dfrac{u^{p}(r)u^{\prime}(r)}{\Lambda}.

By integrating the above inequality from

to
\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(r)\geq\left(\dfrac{\alpha+2}{\Lambda(p+1)}[u^{p+1}(\tau)-u^{p+1}(r)]\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}.

Integrating once again on
[a,\tau]
, and by changing the variable, we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2+\alpha}{\Lambda(p+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}(\tau-a)\leq\int_{0}^{u(\tau)}{\dfrac{ds}{(u^{p+1}(\tau)-s^{p+1})^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}}}=\dfrac{1}{u{(\tau)}^{\frac{p-\alpha-1}{\alpha+2}}}\int_{0}^{1}{\dfrac{dt}{(1-t^{p+1})^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}}}.

So, letting

\displaystyle c_{p}=\int_{0}^{1}{\dfrac{dt}{(1-t^{p+1})^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}}},

we get the following second upper bound:

\displaystyle u(\tau,\gamma)\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda(p+1)}{2+\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\alpha-1}}\left(\dfrac{c_{p}}{\tau-a}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p-\alpha-1}}.

(3.6)

Using (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) we have in both cases
\alpha>0
and
\alpha\leq 0
, that

\displaystyle u(\tau,\gamma)\geq 2^{-\alpha^{-}\over 1-\alpha^{-}}\dfrac{a^{n_{-}-1}\gamma}{2-n_{-}}\left[\tau^{2-n_{-}}-a^{2-n_{-}}\right]-C_{\Lambda,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\tau-a)^{-\frac{\alpha+2}{(p-\alpha-1)}}.

(3.7)

with

a constant depending only on
\Lambda,\ \lambda,\ p\ \mbox{and}\ \alpha
.

Let us now suppose, by contradiction that there exist a positive constant

and a diverging sequence

such that

\displaystyle u(\tau(\gamma_{k}),\gamma_{k})\leq M.

Using (
3.2
) we have that

, which implies, by (

\displaystyle u(\tau(\gamma_{k}),\gamma_{k})\geq 2^{-\alpha^{-}\over 1-\alpha^{-}}\dfrac{a^{n_{-}-1}\gamma_{k}}{n_{-}-2}a^{2-n_{-}}+o(1)\rightarrow+\infty.

That is a contradiction. So

holds. Hence, by (

),

follows. Moreover, (
3.5
) immediately gives

. Finally, we observe that, by the concavity, or more correctly by the nonincreasing behaviour of

, one has

\displaystyle u(\tau,\gamma)\leq\gamma(\tau-a)<\gamma\tau.

Using the above inequality and (

), it then follows

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma)^{p+1+(2+\alpha)(n_{-}-1)}\geq\dfrac{\lambda(p+1)}{(\alpha+2)\gamma^{p-\alpha-1}}a^{(2+\alpha)(n_{-}-1)}

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
We first prove the existence of a positive solution for (
1.1
) and deduce the existence of a negative one.

For every
\gamma>0
, let
u(\rho(\gamma),\gamma)
be the maximal positive solution of the Cauchy problem (
2.1
), defined on the maximal interval
[a,\rho(\gamma))
, and satisfying
u(\rho(\gamma),\gamma)=0
if
\rho(\gamma)<+\infty
. We know that
u(a,\gamma)=0
,
u
is positive, radial and satisfy

|\nabla u|^{\alpha}F(D^{2}u)+u^{p}=0\ \ \ \ \ \

\mbox{in}\ \ \mathcal{A}_{a,\rho(\gamma)}
. So we only need to prove the existence of a
\gamma>0
such that
\rho(\gamma)=b
.

We follow
[
12
]
and define the set

\displaystyle D=\{\gamma\geq 0,\ \ \rho(\gamma)<+\infty\}.

Comming back to the problem (
2.1
), we have that
D
is an open set and
\rho:\gamma\mapsto\rho(\gamma)
is continuous on
D
. From Proposition
3.5
, we have that for
\gamma
large enough,
\rho(\gamma)<+\infty
. This implies that
D
is nonempty and contains a neighborhood of
+\infty
.
Let
(\gamma^{*},+\infty)
be the unbounded connected component of
D
, with
\gamma^{*}\geq 0
.

If
\gamma^{*}=0
, since
\rho(\gamma)>\tau(\gamma)
, from Proposition
3.4

d)
, we obtain that

\lim_{\gamma\searrow 0}{\rho(\gamma)}=+\infty.

If
\gamma^{*}>0
, using again the continuous dependence on the initial data for problem (
2.1
), we have

\lim_{\gamma\searrow\gamma^{*}}{\rho(\gamma)}=+\infty.

By Proposition
3.5
, we also deduce that
\rho
is onto from
[\gamma^{\star},+\infty)
in

. Hence if

, there exists
\gamma

so that

. So there exists a positive solution of (

) in

.

Let us now set

) (

) and (

On the fundamental regularity theorem for mass-minimizing flat chains
Brian White

Abstract
. In the theory of flat chains with coefficients in a normed abelian group, we give a simple necessary and sufficient condition on a group element g g in order for the following fundamental regularity principle to hold: if a mass-minimizing chain is, in a ball disjoint from the boundary, sufficiently weakly close to a multiplicity g g disk, then, in a smaller ball, it is a C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} perturbation with multiplicity g g of that disk.

Primary 49Q15

1.
Introduction

The fundamental regularity theorem for mass-minimizing integral flat chains can be stated
in various ways, one of which is the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
.
Suppose
M
is a a mass-minimizing integral
m
-chain in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

M
has no boundary in the open unit ball
B(0,1)
. Suppose also that
M\llcorner B(0,1)

is weakly sufficiently close to a multiplicity-
1
disk through the origin.
Then
M\llcorner B(0,1/2)
is an
m
-manifold of multiplicity
1
.
Furthermore, the
m
-manifold is the graph of
a function
f
over a domain in the given disk with
\|f\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq 1
.

More generally, the theorem is also true for “almost minimizing” chains.
In particular, it applies to chains that are homologically mass minimizing in smooth Riemannian
manifolds, or, more generally, in sets of positive reach. See §
3
.

If
G
is any normed abelian group, then the theory of integral flat
chains generalizes to flat chains with coefficients in
G
.
In this paper, we address the question: for which coefficient groups
G
and for which
multiplicities
g\in G
does Theorem
1
hold?

It is natural to require that
G
be complete with respect to its norm
|\cdot|
, i.e., that

G
be a complete metric space with respect to the metric
d(x,y)=|x-y|
.
(Otherwise, replace
G
by its metric space completion.)
It is also natural to require that

(1)

If
R<\infty
, then
\{x\in G:|x|\leq R\}
is compact.

Condition (
1
)
is the necessary and sufficient condition for the fundamental compactness
theorem for flat chains to hold. (This follows easily from the
Deformation Theorem
[
9
]
*Theorem 7.3 or
[
13
]
.)

For such normed abelian coefficient groups
G
, we prove

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
g\in G
.
Theorem
1
holds for
m
-chains with coefficients in
G

and with “multiplicity
g
” in place of “multiplicity
1
”
if and only
g
satisfies the following
strong triangle inequality:

(2)

|g|<\inf\{|a|+|b|:a,b\in G\setminus\{0\},\,a+b=g\}.

We remark that (as is easily shown) the strong triangle inequality (
2
)
is equivalent to

(i)

\inf_{x\in G\setminus\{0\}}|x|>0
, and

(ii)

|g|<|a|+|b|
for all
a,b\in G\setminus\{0\}
for which
a+b=g
.

Simple examples show that the strong triangle inequality is necessary for the regularity
theorem to hold; see §
5
.
Most of the paper is devoted to showing that sufficiency of the strong triangle inequality
follows from the Allard Regularity Theorem
[
1
]
.
See
[
6
]
for a greatly simplified proof of Allard’s theorem.

Theorem
1
holds more generally for integral flat chains that minimize (or almost minimize)
parametric elliptic functionals.
See
[
10
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
*5.3.14,
or (for the original, slightly different theorem)
[
2
]
.
I conjecture that Theorem
2
also holds for parametric elliptic functionals.
(If the group has elements of order
2
, then the parametric elliptic integrand needs to be even for
the functional to make sense.)
The proofs in this paper rely on monotonicity and on the Allard Regularity Theorem and hence
only work for mass.

In a different direction, De Pauw and Züst
[
5
]
have proved a regularity theorem
for mass-minimizing or almost mass-minimizing flat chains (with coefficients in an abelian group)
in possibly infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces. Specifically, they prove that
if
\{|x|:x\in G\}
is a discrete set, then the regular set of the chain is a dense open subset of the support.
(In the finite dimensional setting, and with the notion of almost minimizing used in this paper,
their result follows immediately from the Allard Regularity Theorem.)

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
G
is a normed abelian group, i.e., an abelian group with a translation invariant metric; the norm
of an element is its distance to
0
.
We will always assume that
G

is complete and satisfies the compactness property (
1
).
In this paper, “
m
-chain” will mean “flat
m
-chain with coefficients in
G
”.
We do not require that the chains have compact support. Thus, in the terminology
of Federer’s book
[
8
]
, they
would be called “locally flat chains”.
The appendix of
[
14
]

indicates how to extend the theory
of compactly supported chains to the general case.
See
[
11
]
for a quick introduction to flat chains with coefficients in a normed
abelian group. Fleming’s original paper
[
9
]
is the standard reference.
See also
[
13
]
and
[
12
]
,
or, for a different approach,
[
4
]
.

If
M
is an
m
-chain in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we let
|M|
denote its mass.
If
M
has locally finite mass
(which will be the case for all
m
-chains that arise in this paper) and if
S

is a Borel subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, then
M
has a well-defined portion in
S
,
denoted
M\llcorner S
. Furthermore,
M
determines a Radon
measure
\mu_{M}

on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\mu_{M}(S)=|M\llcorner S|

for every Borel set
S
.

Except in §
5
, we will be working with groups that also have the property:

(3)

\inf_{x\in G,\,x\neq 0}|x|>0.

(If
G
does not satisfy (
3
), then no element of
G
satisfies the
strong triangle inequality (
2
).)
For coefficient groups satisfying (
3
), every compact set is finite.
In particular, by (
1
),

If
R<\infty
then
\{x\in G:|x|\leq R\}
is a finite set.

For such normed groups
G
, every
m
-chain
M
of finite (or locally finite) mass
is rectifiable
[
12
]
. That is,
M
can be written as

M=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}[S_{i}],

where the
g_{i}\in G
, where the
S_{i}
are disjoint Borel sets,
and and where each
S_{i}
is contained in a
C^{1}
, oriented
m
-manifold
\Sigma_{i}
.
If
W
is a Borel subset of the ambient space,
then

\displaystyle M\llcorner W

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}[S_{i}\cap W],

\displaystyle|M|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\,\mathcal{H}^{m}(S_{i}),

\displaystyle\mu_{M}(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|g_{i}|\,\mathcal{H}^{m}(S_{i}\cap W).

We also define flat norms
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,K)

and flat seminorms
\mathcal{F}_{W}(\cdot)
as follows.

Definition 3
.

Let
M
be a flat
m
-chain in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
We define

\mathcal{F}(M)
to be the infimum
of

(4)

|M-\partial Q|+|Q|.

over all
(m+1)
-chains
Q
.

If
K
is a closed subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we define
\mathcal{F}(M;K)

to be the infimum of (
4
) among all
(m+1)
-chains
Q
supported in
K
.

If
U
is an open subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we let

\mathcal{F}_{U}(M)

be the infimum of

|(M-\partial Q)\llcorner U|+|Q\llcorner U|

over all finite-mass
(m+1)
-chains
Q
.

The term
(M-\partial Q)\llcorner U
needs explanation, since we have only defined
A\llcorner U

when
A
has locally finite mass.
Suppose
A
is any
m
-chain and
U
is an open set. If there is a
m
-chain
B
supported in
U^{c}

such that
A-B
has locally finite mass, then we let
A\llcorner U=(A-B)\llcorner U
; otherwise
we leave
A\llcorner U
undefined. If
A\llcorner U
is undefined, we take
|A\llcorner U|
to be infinite.

Note that if
U\subset W
, then

\mathcal{F}_{U}(M)\leq\mathcal{F}_{W}(M).

If
\overline{U}
is compact, then
\mathcal{F}_{U}(M)<\infty
.
It follows that if
M
is compactly supported, then
\mathcal{F}(M)<\infty
.
We say that
M_{n}
converges (weakly) to
M
and write
M_{n}\to M
provided

\mathcal{F}_{U}(M_{n}-M)\to 0

for all bounded open sets
U
of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. If
M_{i}
and
M
are supported
in a compact set, then
M_{n}\to M
if and only if
\mathcal{F}(M_{n}-M)\to 0
.

3.
\lambda
-Minimizing Chains

Definition 4
.

For
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
,
an
m
-chain
M
of locally finite mass
in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is called
\lambda
-minimizing
provided it has the following property.
If
K
is a compact subset of
U

and if
Q
is an
(m+1)
-chain compactly supported in

\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{spt}M)<r\},

then

(5)

(1-\lambda r)\,|M\llcorner K|\leq|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|.

Thus “
0
-minimizing” is the same as “mass-minimizing”.

Remark 5
.

If
M
is
\lambda
-minimizing, then (
5
)
also follows from the slightly weaker hypothesis

\operatorname{spt}Q\subset\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{spt}M)\leq r\},

for, in that case,

(1-\lambda R)\,|M\llcorner K|\leq|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|

holds for every
R>r
and therefore also for
R=r
.

If
C
is a closed subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, if
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
, and if there is a unique point
y\in C

closest to
x
, we let
\pi_{C}(x)=y
; otherwise
\pi_{C}(x)
is not defined (i.e.,
x
is not in the domain
of
\pi_{C}
.)
We define
\operatorname{reach}(C)
to be the smallest number
R
such that

\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,C)<R\}

is contained in the domain of
\pi_{C}
.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
C\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
is a set with
R:=\operatorname{reach}(C)>0
.
Suppose that
M
is homologically minimizing in
C
i.e., that
M
is supported in
C
,
and that if
K
is a compact subset of
\operatorname{spt}M
,
then

|M\llcorner K|\leq|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|

for every
(m+1)
-chain
Q
supported in
C
.
Then
M
is
(m/R)
-minimizing.

Proof.

For
r<R
, the restriction
\pi
of
\pi_{C}
to
\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,C)\leq r\}

is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
\leq R/(R-r)
.
(See
[
7
]
*Theorem 4.8(8).)
Suppose that
Q
is an
(m+1)
-chain supported in

\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,K)\leq r\}\subset\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,C)\leq r\}.

Then

\displaystyle|M\llcorner K|

\displaystyle\leq|M\llcorner K+\partial(\pi_{\#}Q)|

\displaystyle=|\pi_{\#}(M\llcorner K+\partial Q)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{m}|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{r}{R}\right)^{-m}|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|,

so

\displaystyle|M\llcorner K+\partial Q|

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{r}{R}\right)^{m}|M\llcorner K|

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{m}{R}r\right)|M\llcorner K|.

∎

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
M
is a rectifiable,
\lambda
-minimizing
m
-chain in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Then in
\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial M)
, the varifold
V
associated to
M
has mean curvature bounded by
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
X
be smooth vectorfield supported in a compact subset
C
of
\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial M)

with
|X(p)|\leq 1
for all
p
.
Let
K=C\cap\operatorname{spt}M
.
Let
\phi_{t}(p)=p+tX(p)
. Let
\Sigma_{t}=\phi_{\#}(M\llcorner K)
.
Let
Q_{t}
be the image of

(M\llcorner K)\times[0,t]

under the map
(p,\tau)\mapsto\phi_{\tau}(p)
.

Then

(1-\lambda t)|M\llcorner K|\leq|\Sigma_{t}|

Thus, differentiating, and using the first variation formula,

-\lambda\mu_{V}(K)\leq\delta(V;X).

Since the same holds for
-X
, we see that

|\delta(V;X)|\leq\lambda\mu_{V}(K),

which is equivalent to the assertion of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Suppose that
M_{i}
and
M
are
m
-chains of locally finite mass in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
that
M_{i}
is
\lambda
-minimizing,
and that
M_{i}\to M
.
Then
M
is
\lambda
-minimizing and

\mu_{M_{i}}\to\mu_{M}.

Proof.

By passing to a subsequence, we may suppose that
\operatorname{spt}M_{i}
converges
to a closed set
X
. Thus

\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\operatorname{spt}M_{i})\to\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,X).

In particular, if
V
is any compact subset of
U
,
then

\max_{q\in V}\operatorname{dist}(q,\operatorname{spt}M_{i})\to\max_{q\in V}\operatorname{dist}(q,X).

Let
0<r<1/\lambda
.
Let
Q
be an
(m+1)
-chain compactly supported in

\{x\in U:\operatorname{dist}(x,X)<r\}.

Thus

\max_{\operatorname{spt}Q}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,X)<r.

Hence

\max_{\operatorname{spt}Q}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,M_{i})<r

for all sufficiently large
i
.

Now let
K
be a compact subset of
X
, let
f:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
be the distance function to
K
,
and let

\displaystyle K[s]

\displaystyle=\{f\leq s\}=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,K)\leq r\},

\displaystyle M[s]

\displaystyle=M\llcorner K[s],

\displaystyle M_{i}[s]

\displaystyle=M_{i}\llcorner K[s].

By passing to a subsequence, we can
assume that for almost every
s\in(0,r)
,

\mathcal{F}(M_{i}[s]-M[s];K[s])\to 0.

(See Theorem
15
in the appendix.)
For such an
s
, there exist
(m+1)
-chains
Q_{i}
supported in
K[s]

such that

|M_{i}[s]-M[s]+\partial Q_{i}|+|Q_{i}|\to 0.

Now

\max_{K[s]}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,X)\leq s<r.

Thus

\max_{K[s]}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\operatorname{spt}M_{i})<r

for all sufficiently large
i
. In particular,

\max_{\operatorname{spt}Q_{i}}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\operatorname{spt}M_{i})<r

for all sufficiently large
i
.

Now

\displaystyle(1-\lambda r)|M_{i}[s]|

\displaystyle\leq|M_{i}[s]-\partial(Q+Q_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq|M[s]-\partial Q|+|M_{i}[s]-M[s]-\partial Q_{i}|

Thus

(6)

(1-\lambda r)\limsup_{i}|M_{i}[s]|\leq|M[s]-\partial Q|.

In particular (letting
Q=0
), we see that every compact set
K
in
X

is contained in the interior of a set
K[s]
for which
\limsup_{i}|M\llcorner K[s]|<\infty
.
Thus, after passing to a subsequence, the Radon measures
\mu_{i}=\mu_{M_{i}}

converge weakly to a Radon measure
\mu
on
U
.

By (
6
) and lower semicontinuity of mass with respect to flat convergence,

(7)

\displaystyle(1-\lambda r)|M[s]|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda r)\liminf_{i}|M_{i}[s]|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda r)\limsup_{i}|M_{i}[s]|

\displaystyle\leq|M[s]-\partial Q|.

Letting
s\to 0
gives

(1-\lambda r)|M[0]|\leq|M[0]-\partial Q|.

Hence
M
is
\lambda
-minimizing.

Now consider (
7
) in the case
Q=0
.
For almost every
s
,
\mu_{M}(K[s])\to\mu(K[s])
.
For such
s
, we can rewrite (
7
) as

(1-\lambda r)\mu_{M}(K[s])\leq(1-\lambda r)\mu(K[s])\leq\mu_{M}(K[s]).

Letting
s\to 0
and then
r\to 0
gives

\mu_{M}(K)\leq\mu(K)\leq\mu_{M}(K).

∎

4.
Main Theorem

Throughout this section, we assume that
g
satisfies the strong triangle inequality,
and we prove the fundamental regularity theorem under that assumption.
Note that the strong triangle inequality for
g
is
equivalent to the existence of an
\epsilon>0
such that

(8)

|a|+|b|\geq|g|+\epsilon\quad\text{if $a+b=g$ and $a,b\in G\setminus\{0\}$}.

This implies that

(9)

\inf_{x\in G,\,x\neq 0}|x|>0.

(Indeed, if
x\neq 0
, then
|x|\geq\min\{\epsilon/2,\,|g|\}
.)

The condition (
9
) implies that every
flat chain of locally finite mass is rectifiable
[
12
]
.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose
M
is a rectifiable, compactly supported
m
-chain such that

\pi_{\#}M=g[\Omega],

where
\Omega
is a Borel subset of an oriented
m
-plane
P

and where
\pi:M\to P
is the orthogonal projection.
Suppose also that
M
has no points of multiplicity
g
.
Then

|M|\geq(|g|+\epsilon)\mathcal{H}^{m}(\Omega).

Here, “point of multiplicity
g
” means “point that has a plane of multiplicity
g

as a tangent cone”.

Proof.

Since
M
is rectifiable, it can be written
as

M=\sum_{i}g_{i}[S_{i}],

where the
S_{i}
are disjoint Borel sets and each
S_{i}
is contained in an oriented
C^{1}

m
-manifold
\Sigma_{i}
.
By subdividing the
S_{i}
and
\Sigma_{i}
and reorienting (if necessary), we can assume that
if
p\in S_{i}
and if

\pi|\operatorname{Tan}(\Sigma_{i},p):\operatorname{Tan}(\Sigma_{i},p)\to P

is an isomorphism, then it preserves orientation.
Let
S=\cup S_{i}
and define

\displaystyle\gamma:S\to G,

\displaystyle\gamma(p)=g_{i}\quad\text{for $p\in S_{i}$}.

Since
M
has no points of multiplicity
g
, we can assume that
\gamma(p)
is never
equal to
g
or
-g
.

Since
\pi_{\#}M=g[\Omega]
, we see that

\sum_{p\in S\cap\pi^{-1}x}\gamma(p)=g

for almost every
x\in\Omega
, and therefore that

\sum_{p\in\pi^{-1}x}|\gamma(p)|\geq|g|+\epsilon

since
\gamma(p)\neq g
.

Thus

\displaystyle|M|

\displaystyle=\int_{S}|\gamma(p)|\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}p

\displaystyle\geq\int_{x\in\Omega}\sum_{p\in S\cap\pi^{-1}x}|\gamma(p)|\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}(|g|+\epsilon)\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}

\displaystyle\geq(|g|+\epsilon)\mathcal{H}^{m}(\Omega).

∎

If
M
is an
m
-chain of locally finite mass, and if
B(p,r)
is a ball, we let

\Phi(M,p,r)

be the minimum of

\mathcal{F}_{B(0,1)}\left(\frac{1}{r}(M-p),g[P]\right)

among all oriented
m
-planes
P
with
0\in P
.

We also let

\displaystyle\Theta(M,p,r)=\frac{|M\llcorner B(p,r)|}{\omega_{m}r^{m}},

\displaystyle\Theta(M,p)=\lim_{r\to 0}\Theta(M,p,r)\quad\text{(if the limit exists), and}

\displaystyle\Theta(M)=\sup_{p\in\mathbf{R}^{d},\,r>0}\Theta(M,p,r).

Lemma 10
.

There is a
\delta>0
with the following property.
Suppose
M
is a mass-minimizing
m
-chain in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

(1)

\partial M=0
.

(2)

\Theta(M)\leq|g|
.

(3)

\Phi(M,0,r)\leq\delta
for all
r>1
.

Then
M=g[P]
for some oriented
m
-plane
P
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M_{i}
is a sequence of
m
-chains
satisfying (
1
) and (
2
), and such that

\delta_{i}:=\sup_{r>1}F(M_{i},0,r)\to 0.

It suffices to prove that
M_{i}
is a
m
-plane of multiplicity
g
for all
sufficiently large
i
.
By rotating the
M_{i}
, we can assume that

(10)

\mathcal{F}_{B(0,1)}(M_{i}-g[P])\to 0

where
P=\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}^{N-m}
.

It follows that the
M_{i}
converge weakly to
g[P]
.

(If this is not clear, note that if
R>1
, then, after passing to a subsequence,
there is a
P^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{B(0,R)}(M_{i}-g[P^{\prime}])\to 0
.
By (
10
),
P^{\prime}=P
, and thus it was not necessary to pass to a subsequence.)

Let

K[r]=\overline{B^{m}(0,r)\times B^{d-m}(0,r)}.

That is,
K[r]=\{f\leq r\}
, where

\displaystyle f:\mathbf{R}^{d}\to\mathbf{R},

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\max\{|x|,|y|\}\quad(x\in\mathbf{R}^{m},\,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d-m}).

After passing to a subsequence, we
can assume (by Theorem
15
) that

M_{i}\llcorner\Omega(r)\to g[D_{r}]

for almost every
r
,
where
D_{r}=P\cap B(0,r)
.
Fix such an
r
, and let
M_{i}^{\prime}=M_{i}\llcorner K[r]
and
D=D_{r}
.

Since
\operatorname{spt}(M_{i})\to P
, if follows that

\operatorname{spt}(M_{i}^{\prime})\subset\overline{B(0,r)\times B(0,\eta_{i})}

where
\eta_{i}\to 0
.
For all sufficiently large
i
,
\eta_{i}<r
, and thus

\operatorname{spt}(\partial M_{i}^{\prime})\subset\partial B^{m}(0,r)\times\overline{B(0,\eta_{i})}.

Consequently,

\pi_{\#}M_{i}=g_{i}[D]

for some
g_{i}
. Since
M_{i}\to g[D]
, we see
that
|g_{i}-g|\to 0
and therefore (since
\inf_{x\in G,\,x\neq 0}|x|>0
)
that
g_{i}=g
for all sufficiently large
i
.

The
\lambda
-minimizing property implies (see Theorem
8
) that

|M_{i}^{\prime}|\to|g[D]|=|g|\mathcal{H}^{m}(D).

Hence, by Lemma
9
, for large
i
,
M_{i}^{\prime}
must have a point
p_{i}

at which the tangent cone is a multiplicity
m
-plane.
Since
\Theta(M_{i}^{\prime})\leq|g|
, it follows from monotonicity
that
M_{i}^{\prime}
is a cone with vertex
p_{i}^{\prime}
,
and therefore a multiplicity
g
plane.
∎

Corollary 11
.

If
M
is a
\lambda
-minimizing

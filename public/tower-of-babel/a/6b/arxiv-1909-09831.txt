Observation of dipolar splittings in high-resolution atom-loss spectroscopy of 6 Li p p -wave Feshbach resonances
Manuel Gerken, Binh Tran, Stephan Häfner, Eberhard Tiemann, Bing Zhu, Matthias Weidemüller

Abstract
We report on the observation of dipolar splitting in 6 Li p p -wave Feshbach resonances by high-resolution atom-loss spectroscopy. The Feshbach resonances at 159 G and 215 G exhibit a doublet structure of 10 mG 10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G} and 13 mG 13\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G} , respectively, associated with different projections of the orbital angular momentum. The observed splittings agree very well with coupled-channel calculations. We map out the temperature dependence of the atom-loss spectrum allowing us to extrapolate resonance positions and the corresponding widths to zero temperature. The observed dipolar splitting in fermionic lithium might be useful for the realization of the quantum phase transition between the polar and axial p p -wave superfluid phases.



I
Introduction

It is generally known that magnetic
p
-wave Feshbach resonances (FRs) in ultracold atomic collisions exhibit a dipolar splitting, resulting in two resonance features corresponding to
m_{l}=0
and
m_{l}=\pm 1

Ticknor et al. 2004
;
Strauss et al. 2010
. Here,
m_{l}
is the projection of the orbital angular momentum
l
along the external magnetic field. This non-degeneracy was predicted to give rise to a phase transition from a
p_{x}
to a
p_{x}+ip_{y}
state in a
p
-wave superfluid (SF)
Gurarie and Radzihovsky 2007
, which plays an essential role in SF liquid
3
He
Volovik 1992
and neutron SFs inside neutron stars
Page et al. 2011
;
Shternin et al. 2011
. The dipolar splitting was observed in a spin-polarized gas of
40
K
Ticknor et al. 2004
and a large variety of quantum-gas mixtures
Pilch et al. 2009
;
Wang et al. 2011
;
Repp et al. 2013
;
Wang et al. 2013
;
Dong et al. 2016
. It is attributed to effective spin-spin (
ss
) interactions including the magnetic dipole-dipole interaction and the second-order spin-orbit coupling
Strauss et al. 2010
. Recently, the observation of dipolar splitting has been extended to
d
-wave resonances
Cui et al. 2017
;
Yao et al. 2019
, and an additional splitting mechanism has been discovered for
m_{l}=+1
and
m_{l}=-1
due to spin-rotation interaction in
p
-wave FRs
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Häfner et al. 2019
.

Due to the favorable properties of its FRs, the fermionic isotope of lithium,
6
Li, one of the only two stable fermionic isotopes among the alkali metals, has been a major workhorse in experiments with quantum-degenerate Fermi gases. Prime applications include the study of the BCS-BEC crossover
Jochim et al. 2003
;
Zwierlein et al. 2003
;
Bartenstein et al. 2004a
;
Chin et al. 2004
;
Bourdel et al. 2004
;
Zwierlein et al. 2004
;
Bartenstein et al. 2004b
, the Efimov effect
Ottenstein et al. 2008
;
Huckans et al. 2009
;
Wenz et al. 2009
;
Williams et al. 2009
;
Lompe et al. 2010
, the double SF
Ferrier-Barbut et al. 2014
, and the Fermi-Hubbard model in optical lattices
Chin et al. 2006
;
Parsons et al. 2015
;
Duarte et al. 2015
;
Omran et al. 2015
;
Greif et al. 2016
;
Mitra et al. 2018
. Although dipolar splitting in
6
Li
p
-wave FRs was predicted to be
\sim

10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}

Chevy et al. 2005
, it was not resolved so far due to insufficient magnetic resolution in previous experiments
Zhang et al. 2004
;
Schunck et al. 2005
;
Nakasuji et al. 2013
;
Yoshida et al. 2018
;
Waseem et al. 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2018
. By using an optical lattice
p
-wave Feshbach molecules in the
m_{l}=\pm 1
component were selectively formed without resolving the dipolar splitting
Waseem et al. 2016
, while the studies of the molecular binding energy and lifetime in Refs.
Fuchs et al. 2008
;
Inada et al. 2008
;
Maier et al. 2010
neglected this effect.

In this work we report high-resolution trap-loss spectroscopy on three
6
Li
p
-wave FRs in the two lowest-energy hyperfine states. Dipolar splittings of about
10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
are resolved in two of the three resonances and systematic effects of the sample temperature on the observed splitting are investigated. We model the observations with a coupled-channels (cc) calculation and find an excellent agreement between experiment and modeling.

A detailed description of our experimental apparatus has already been given in previous publications

The finite temperature of the gas causes systematic effects on Feshbach resonances in atom-loss spectra, such as asymmetries, shifts, broadenings, and saturation
Ticknor et al. 2004
;
Dong et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2004
;
Waseem et al. 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2018
. Averaging over the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of the collision energy results in an asymmetric lineshape
Ticknor et al. 2004
;
Chevy et al. 2005
shifting the resonance center and broadening the spectrum. Saturation of atom loss stems from the unitarity limit at finite temperatures, rendering it impossible to resolve magnetic resonance splittings below magnetic fields of order
3k_{B}T/2\delta\mu

Waseem et al. 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2018
, with
\delta\mu
denoting the relative magnetic moment between the molecular and atomic states. To ensure that our measurements (magnetic field resolution of
\sim

1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
) are not limited by such effects, temperatures below
T\sim

0.7\text{\,}\mu\mathrm{K}
near the
214\text{\,}\mathrm{G}
resonance (
\delta\mu=k_{B}\times

118(8)\text{\,}\mu\mathrm{K}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{G}

Fuchs et al. 2008
) are required.

We investigate the temperature effects on the resonance lineshapes near the
214\text{\,}\mathrm{G}

p
-wave FR.
Evaporation is stopped at varying trap depths resulting in
T\leq

0.3\text{\,}\mu\mathrm{K}
, and atomic loss spectra are recorded, as shown in Fig.
2(a)
. Above
0.3\text{\,}\mu\mathrm{K}
the line-shape becomes asymmetric and the doublet spitting is unresolvable due to the increase of both the temperature-broadened width and the influence of effects due to unitarity, as discussed above. The extracted loss peak positions and widths are plotted in Fig.
2(b)
.

As expected
Ticknor et al. 2004
, the resonance peak moves towards lower magnetic field at a lower temperature. We notice that the observed loss width
w^{e}
is considerably larger than the expected temperature-broadened width
k_{B}T/\delta\mu
, ranging between
1.1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
and
2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
within the temperature range of the experiments. To the lowest order, the observed temperature dependence of the loss peak position and width can be well approximated by linear functions (black and red lines in Fig.
2(b)
). This yields for the resonance positions slopes of
16(3)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
and
10(3)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
for the
m_{l}=0
and
m_{l}=\pm 1
components, respectively. Although the slopes are slightly different for the two
|m_{l}|
values, the doublet splitting
\delta^{e}
remains almost constant within the experimental uncertainty when
T
is varied. Extrapolation to zero temperature gives a splitting of
13(1)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
.

Using the Breit-Wigner theoretical approach described in Refs.
Waseem et al. 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2018
and assuming
k_{B}T
as the smallest energy scale, we have estimated the shift of the resonances for two- and three-body loss processes to be
5k_{B}/2\delta\mu=

21.2\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
and
k_{B}/\delta\mu=

8.5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
, respectively. By comparing the experimental and theoretical slopes we infer that both two- and three-body loss contributes to the observed temperature dependence. Disentangling these loss channels by time-resolved measurements is beyond the scope of this paper. In order to realize a
p
-wave SF, one would have to mitigate loss by identifying appropriate temperature and density regimes, or by using dimensional control
Zhou and Cui 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2017
.

For the observed widths of the loss resonances, we obtain slopes of
37(7)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
and
14(4)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{/}\mu\mathrm{K}
for the
m_{l}=\pm 1
and
m_{l}=0
components, while the zero-temperature widths are
8(1)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
and
6(1)\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
, respectively. Contributions to the observed widths include the resonance intrinsic width
\gamma

Chevy et al. 2005
;
Waseem et al. 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2018
, the thermal broadening, and other experimental broadening effects like the trap-induced density inhomogeneity and magnetic-field noise. However, from the observed linearity and the fact that
w_{e}(T)>k_{B}T/\delta\mu
in Fig.
2(b)
one may infer that
\gamma\gg k_{B}T
in our experiment. This conclusion, however, is in disagreement with recent estimations of
\gamma
extracted from the temperature- and interaction-strength-dependent two-
Waseem et al. 2017
and three-body
Waseem et al. 2018
loss rate constants near the
p
-wave resonances above the Fermi temperature.

IV
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have performed high-resolution atom-loss spectroscopy of
p
-wave Feshbach resonances in an optically trapped ultracold
6
Li gas and resolved the splittings of about
10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{G}
between
m_{l}=0
and
m_{l}=\pm 1
components. Our measurements agree excellently with a full cc calculation including spin-spin interactions.
In spin-polarized Fermi gases near
p
-wave FRs, there have been predictions of the phase transition from a polar
p_{x}
state to an axial
p_{x}+ip_{y}
state as well as the topological transition from a gapless to a gapped
p_{x}+ip_{y}
phase
Botelho and Melo 2005
;
Gurarie et al. 2005
;
Cheng and Yip 2005
. In the past, stabilities of single-component Fermi gases and
p
-wave Feshbach molecules near
p
-wave FRs were investigated, while the realization of
p
-wave SF in quantum gases is still challenging due to severe inelastic losses
Regal et al. 2003
;
Zhang et al. 2004
;
Schunck et al. 2005
;
Gaebler et al. 2007
;
Fuchs et al. 2008
;
Inada et al. 2008
;
Maier et al. 2010
. Dimensional confining has been identified as a promising route for stabilizing Fermi gases near
p
-wave FRs and realizing
p
-wave SF pairing
Zhou and Cui 2017
;
Waseem et al. 2017
. Resolving the predicted dipolar splitting of FRs in
6
Li provides a good starting point for future investigations in this direction exploring the individual control of
m_{l}=0,\pm 1
scattering processes
Günter et al. 2005
. Furthermore, experimental studies of the
p
-wave contact can now be extended to
6
Li, which was, previously, only accessible in a
40
K gas due to its larger splitting of the
m_{l}
components
Luciuk et al. 2016
.

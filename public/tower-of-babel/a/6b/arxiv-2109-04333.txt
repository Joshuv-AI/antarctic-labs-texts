be fixed, where
\mathbb{N}

Here for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
[m]
is the
q

[m]=\frac{1-q^{m}}{1-q}
. H. Bachmann, Y. Takeyama and K. Tasaka studied the special values of the finite multiple harmonic
q
-series at roots of unity in
[
1
,
2
]

4
]
. Based on the main result of
[

q
-series on

indices at root of unity in this paper.

Now let
\zeta_{n}
be a fixed primitive
n
-th root of unity. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
l,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the set of the non-negative integers. We set

W_{n}^{r}(l,s)=\sum\limits_{d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=l\atop d_{0},\ldots,d_{s}\geq 0}z_{n}\left(\{r\}^{d_{0}},r+1,\{r\}^{d_{1}},r+1,\ldots,r+1,\{r\}^{d_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right),

which is the sum of the finite multiple harmonic
q
-series on
r\text{-}(r+1)
indices at roots of unity. For example, when
r=1,2,3
, we have

\displaystyle W_{n}^{1}(l,s)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=l\atop d_{0},\ldots,d_{s}\geq 0}z_{n}\left(\{1\}^{d_{0}},2,\{1\}^{d_{1}},2,\ldots,2,\{1\}^{d_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right),

\displaystyle W_{n}^{2}(l,s)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=l\atop d_{0},\ldots,d_{s}\geq 0}z_{n}\left(\{2\}^{d_{0}},3,\{2\}^{d_{1}},3,\ldots,3,\{2\}^{d_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right),

\displaystyle W_{n}^{3}(l,s)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=l\atop d_{0},\ldots,d_{s}\geq 0}z_{n}\left(\{3\}^{d_{0}},4,\{3\}^{d_{1}},4,\ldots,4,\{3\}^{d_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right).

We also give the following notation. Let

\displaystyle A(n,l,s)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s+l+1}\frac{-(s+1)}{i\binom{s+l}{l}}\binom{i}{s+l+1-i}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2i-s-l-1}(-4)^{j}\binom{2i-s-l-1}{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\binom{n+2s+2l+1-i+j}{4s+4l+3-2i+2j}\binom{2s+2l+2-2i}{s+1-j}.

One can see that the right-hand side of the above formula is complicated. It is better to give a concise one, and we are supposed to find a suitable solution.

The main results of this paper are the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
l,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{n}^{1}(l,s)=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{n(s+1)}\binom{s+l}{l}\binom{n+s}{2s+l+1}(1-\zeta_{n})^{2s+l}.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
l,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{n}^{2}(l,s)=\frac{(-1)^{l}}{n^{2}(s+1)}\binom{s+l}{l}\left[\binom{n+2s+l+1}{3s+2l+2}+(-1)^{s}\binom{n+s+l}{3s+2l+2}\right](1-\zeta_{n})^{3s+2l}.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
l,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{n}^{3}(l,s)=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{s}}{n^{3}(s+1)}\binom{s+l}{l}\left[\binom{n+3s+2l+2}{4s+3l+3}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+(-1)^{l}\binom{n+s+l}{4s+3l+3}+A(n,l,s)\right](1-\zeta_{n})^{4s+3l}.

2
Generating function

In this section, we first recall
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
. For a multi-index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define its weight and depth respectively by

\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{k})=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d},\quad\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{k})=d.

And for an integer
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the
i
-height is defined by

i\text{-}\operatorname{ht}(\mathbf{k})=\#\{j\mid k_{j}\geq i+1\}.

Let
t
be a formal parameter. Then the interpolated finite multiple harmonic
q
-series
z_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q)
is defined as

z_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q)=z_{n}^{t}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d};q)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}}(1-q)^{k-\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{p})}z_{n}(\mathbf{p};q)t^{d-\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{p})},

where
k=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{k})
and
\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}}
is the sum where
\mathbf{p}
runs over all multi-indices of the form
\mathbf{p}=(k_{1}\square\cdots\square k_{d})
, in which each
\square
is filled by “,”,“+” or “
-1+
”. It is easy to see that

z_{n}^{0}(\mathbf{k};q)=z_{n}(\mathbf{k};q),\quad z_{n}^{1}(\mathbf{k};q)=z_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{k};q).

Here
z_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{k};q)
is the finite multiple harmonic
q
-series of star-version defined by

z_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{k};q)=z_{n}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d};q)=\sum\limits_{n>m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{d}>0}\frac{q^{(k_{1}-1)m_{1}+\cdots+(k_{d}-1)m_{d}}}{[m_{1}]^{k_{1}}\cdots[m_{d}]^{k_{d}}}.

In
[
4
]
, instead of
z_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q)
a modified version was used, which is defined as

\bar{z}_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q)=(1-q)^{-\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{k})}z_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q).

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. In
[
4
]
, Z. Li and E. Pan considered the generating function of the sums of the interpolated finite multiple harmonic
q
-series with fixed weight, depth and
1
-height,
2
-height,
\ldots
,
r
-height. Precisely, for any non-negative integers
k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}
, set

G_{n}^{t}(k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r};q)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I(k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})}\bar{z}_{n}^{t}(\mathbf{k};q),

where
I(k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})
is the set of multi-indices of weight
k
, depth
d
,
1
-height
h_{1}
,
\ldots
,
r
-height
h_{r}
. The sum is treated as
0
whenever the index set is empty except for
G_{n}^{t}(0,0,\ldots,0;q)=1
. For formal variables
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+2}
, we define the generating function

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}^{t}(q)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{n}^{t}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+2};q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\geq 0}G_{n}^{t}(k,d,h_{1},\ldots,h_{r};q)u_{1}^{k-d-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}h_{j}}u_{2}^{d-h_{1}}u_{3}^{h_{1}-h_{2}}\cdots u_{r+1}^{h_{r-1}-h_{r}}u_{r+2}^{h_{r}}.

Then the generating function was computed in
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+2}
be variables. Set

\displaystyle x_{1}=\frac{u_{1}}{1+u_{1}}

and

\displaystyle x_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=i}^{r+1}(-1)^{j-i}\binom{j-2}{i-2}\left(u_{j}-\frac{u_{r+2}}{u_{1}^{r+2-j}}\right)+\frac{u_{r+2}}{u_{1}^{r+2-i}(1+u_{1})^{i-1}}

for
i=2,\cdots,r+2
. Then we have

\Psi_{n}^{t}(q)=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}P^{t-1}(1-q^{j})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}P^{t}(1-q^{j})},

where

\displaystyle P^{t}(T)=T^{r+1}-(x_{1}+tx_{2})T^{r}-t\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}(x_{r+2-i}-x_{1}x_{r+1-i})T^{i}.

(2.1)

Now we consider the sums of the interpolated finite multiple harmonic
q
-series on
r\text{-}(r+1)
indices. It is enough to set
u_{1}=\cdots=u_{r}=0
. And in this case, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
u_{1}=\cdots=u_{r}=0
, then we have
x_{1}=0
and

\displaystyle x_{i}=(-1)^{r+1-i}\binom{r-1}{i-2}u_{r+1}+(-1)^{r-i}\binom{r}{i-2}u_{r+2}

for
i=2,\ldots,r+2
.

Proof.
We get the result by the same way as in
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

Using Lemma
2.2
and (
2.1
), if
u_{1}=\cdots=u_{r}=0
, we have

\displaystyle P^{t}(T)

\displaystyle=T^{r+1}-tx_{2}T^{r}-t\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}x_{r+2-i}T^{i}

\displaystyle=T^{r+1}-tT(1-T)^{r-1}u_{r+1}-t(1-T)^{r}u_{r+2}.

Hence we get the following corollary, which is about the generating function of the sums of the interpolated finite multiple harmonic
q
-series on
r\text{-}(r+1)
indices at roots of unity.

Corollary 2.3
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
and formal variables
u,v
, we have

\sum\limits_{l,s\geq 0}\sum\limits_{a_{0}+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}=l\atop a_{0},\ldots,a_{s}\geq 0}\bar{z}_{n}^{t}\left(\{r\}^{a_{0}},r+1,\{r\}^{a_{1}},r+1,\ldots,r+1,\{r\}^{a_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right)u^{l}v^{s}=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{P}^{t-1}(\zeta_{n}^{j})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{P}^{t}(\zeta_{n}^{j})},

where

\widetilde{P}^{t}(T)=(1-T)^{r+1}-t(1-T)T^{r-1}u-tT^{r}v.

Instead of
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{P}^{t}(\zeta_{n}^{j})
, it is convenient to use

H(n,r,t)=-\frac{v}{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{P}^{t}(\zeta_{n}^{j}).

Since we want to study the sum of finite multiple harmonic
q
-series at roots of unity, the following lemma is necessary.

Lemma 2.4
.

H(n,r,0)=-n^{r}v
.

Proof.
By the definition of
H(n,r,t)
, we find

\displaystyle H(n,r,0)=-\frac{v}{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta_{n}^{j})^{r+1}=-\frac{v}{n}\cdot n^{r+1}=-n^{r}v,

as desired.
∎

Now we set

\widetilde{P}^{t}(T)=(\beta_{1}^{t}-T)(\beta_{2}^{t}-T)\cdots(\beta_{r+1}^{t}-T).

Then the elementary symmetric polynomials of
\beta_{1}^{t},\ldots,\beta_{r+1}^{t}
are given by

\displaystyle e_{r,j}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq r+1}\beta_{i_{1}}^{t}\cdots\beta_{i_{j}}^{t}=\begin{cases}r+1+(-1)^{r}t(u-v)&\text{if\;}j=1,\\
\binom{r+1}{2}+(-1)^{r}tu&\text{if\;}j=2,\\
\binom{r+1}{j}&\text{if\;}j=3,\cdots,r+1.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Using the formula
\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(T-\zeta_{n}^{j})=\frac{T^{n}-1}{T-1}
, we have

\displaystyle H(n,r,t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{v}{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}(\beta_{i}^{t}-\zeta_{n}^{j})=-\frac{v}{n}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}\frac{(\beta_{i}^{t})^{n}-1}{\beta_{i}^{t}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}[(\beta_{i}^{t})^{n}-1]}{tn}.

(2.3)

Let
w
be a variable. We consider the generating function

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}H(n,r,t)w^{n}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r+1}[(\beta_{i}^{t})^{n}-1]}{tn}w^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{n}}{n}\left[(-1)^{r+1}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}(-1)^{r+1-j}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq r+1}(\beta_{i_{1}}^{t}\cdots\beta_{i_{j}}^{t})^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{r}}{t}\log(1-w)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}\frac{(-1)^{r-j}}{t}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq r+1}\log(1-\beta_{i_{1}}^{t}\cdots\beta_{i_{j}}^{t}w).

Hence we get following result.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
and formal variables
u,v
, let
\beta_{1}^{t},\ldots,\beta_{r+1}^{t}
be determined by (
2.2
). Let
\widetilde{F}(t,r,0)=1-w
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}(t,r,j)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq r+1}(1-\beta_{i_{1}}^{t}\cdots\beta_{i_{j}}^{t}w),\quad(1\leq j\leq r+1).

(2.4)

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l,s\geq 0}\sum\limits_{a_{0}+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}=l\atop a_{0},\ldots,a_{s}\geq 0}\bar{z}_{n}^{t}\left(\{r\}^{a_{0}},r+1,\{r\}^{a_{1}},r+1,\ldots,r+1,\{r\}^{a_{s}};\zeta_{n}\right)u^{l}v^{s}=\frac{H(n,r,t-1)}{H(n,r,t)},

where
H(n,r,t)
is determined by

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}H(n,r,t)w^{n}=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{t}\log\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r+1}\widetilde{F}(t,r,j)^{(-1)^{j}}\right).

And in particular, we have

\sum\limits_{l,s\geq 0}W_{n}^{r}(l,s)(1-\zeta_{n})^{-(r+1)s-rl}u^{l}v^{s}=-\frac{H(n,r,-1)}{n^{r}v}.

Therefore the rest of the task is to compute
H(n,r,-1)
. We do in the special cases
r=1,2,3
in the next section.

\displaystyle=

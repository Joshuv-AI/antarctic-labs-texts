Time-reversal of rank-one quantum strategy functions
Yuan Su, John Watrous

Abstract
The quantum strategy (or quantum combs ) framework is a useful tool for reasoning about interactions among entities that process and exchange quantum information over the course of multiple turns. We prove a time-reversal property for a class of linear functions, defined on quantum strategy representations within this framework, that corresponds to the set of rank-one positive semidefinite operators on a certain space. This time-reversal property states that the maximum value obtained by such a function over all valid quantum strategies is also obtained when the direction of time for the function is reversed, despite the fact that the strategies themselves are generally not time reversible. An application of this fact is an alternative proof of a known relationship between the conditional min- and max-entropy of bipartite quantum states, along with generalizations of this relationship.

General semidefinite programming formulation

As mentioned previously, the six-message example just described generalizes to
any finite number of message exchanges.
If the number of message exchanges is equal to
n
, the input registers to
Alice (the player whose actions are being optimized) are

\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{n}
, and the output registers of Alice are

\mathsf{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Y}_{n}
, then the possible strategies for Alice are
represented by channels of the form

\Xi_{n}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n})

(14)

that obey constraints that generalize (
3
) and
(
5
).
Specifically, there must exist channels

\begin{gathered}\Xi_{n-1}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n-1},\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n-1})\\
\vdots\\
\Xi_{1}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{1})\end{gathered}

(15)

such that

\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}\circ\Xi_{k}=\Xi_{k-1}\circ\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}

(16)

for all
k\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
.
For the maximization of a real-valued linear function over all strategies for
Alice, represented by a Hermitian operator

H\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n}),

(17)

one obtains the semidefinite program described in
Figure
3
.
The primal problem corresponds to an optimization over all Choi
representations of the channels
\Xi_{1},\ldots,\Xi_{n}
.
This semidefinite programming formulation is implicit in
[
9
]
,
and first appeared explicitly in
[
8
]
.
It also appears in
[
1
]
, where it was used to define
a generalized notion of min-entropy for quantum networks.

Primal problem

maximize:

\displaystyle\langle H,X_{n}\rangle

subject to:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{n}}(X_{n})=X_{n-1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{n}},

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{2}}(X_{2})=X_{1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{2}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}(X_{1})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{1}},

\displaystyle X_{n}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n}),

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle X_{2}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2}),

\displaystyle X_{1}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}).

Dual problem

minimize:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(Y_{1})

subject to:

\displaystyle Y_{n}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{n}}\geq H,

\displaystyle Y_{n-1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{n-1}}\geq\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}(Y_{n}),

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle Y_{1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\geq\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}(Y_{2}),

\displaystyle Y_{n}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n}),

\displaystyle Y_{n-1}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n-2}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n-1}),

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle Y_{1}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{X}_{1}).

Statement and proof of the main result

The main result of the current paper concerns the optimization problem
described in the previous section, as represented by the semidefinite program
in Figure
3
, in the case that
H=uu^{\ast}

is a rank one positive semidefinite operator.
The result to be described does not hold in general when
H
does not take this
form.

In order to explain the main result in precise terms, it will be helpful to
introduce some notation.
Suppose that a positive integer
n
along with spaces
\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n}
and

\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n}
have been fixed.
For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{k};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{k})\subset\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k})

(18)

denote the primal-feasible choices for the operator
X_{k}
in the semidefinite
program specified in Figure
3
.
That is, we define

\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{1};\mathcal{Y}_{1})=\bigl\{X_{1}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1})\,:\,\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}(X_{1})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}\bigr\}

(19)

(which is the set of all Choi operators of channels of the form

\Xi_{1}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{1})
), and

\begin{multlined}\mathcal{S}_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{k};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{k})\\[5.69054pt]
=\bigl\{X_{k}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k})\,:\,\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}(X_{k})=X_{k-1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\\
\text{for some}\;X_{k-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{k-1};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{k-1})\bigr\}\end{multlined}

(20)

for

.
The primal form of the semidefinite program described in
Figure
3
can therefore be expressed succinctly
as

\displaystyle\text{maximize:}

\displaystyle\langle H,X\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle\text{subject to:}

\displaystyle X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n}).

We will refer to operators in the sets defined above as

strategy operators
, as they represent
n
-turn strategies with respect
to the quantum strategy framework.

Let us also define an isometry

W\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathcal{X}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{1})

(22)

by the action

W(y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{n}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=x_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{1}\otimes y_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{1}

(23)

for all vectors
x_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
and

y_{1}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}
.
In words,
W
simply reverses the order of the tensor factors of the space

\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n}
, yielding a
vector in
\mathcal{X}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{1}

that, aside from this re-ordering of tensor factors, is the same as its input
vector.

Statement of the main result

With the notation just introduced in hand, the main theorem may now be stated.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n}
be complex Euclidean spaces,
for
n
a positive integer, let

u\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n}

(24)

be a vector, and let

X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})

(25)

be a strategy operator.
There exists a strategy operator

Y\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{1};\mathcal{X}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{1})

(26)

such that

\langle Wuu^{\ast}W^{\ast},Y\rangle\geq\langle uu^{\ast},X\rangle.

(27)

If it is the case that

then the operator

Interpretations of the main theorem

Theorem
1
establishes a
time-reversal property
of
rank-one strategy functions.
Intuitively speaking, the linear function

Y\mapsto\langle Wuu^{\ast}W^{\ast},Y\rangle

(32)

defined on
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{1};\mathcal{X}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{1})
represents the

time-reversal
of the linear function

X\mapsto\langle uu^{\ast},X\rangle

(33)

defined on
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})
, in the sense that the two
functions differ only in the reversal of the ordering of the register
exchanges:

\mathsf{X}_{1}
,
\mathsf{Y}_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\mathsf{X}_{n}
,
\mathsf{Y}_{n}
for the function
corresponding to
uu^{\ast}
and
\mathsf{Y}_{n}
,
\mathsf{X}_{n}
,
\ldots
,

\mathsf{Y}_{1}
,
\mathsf{X}_{1}
for the function corresponding to

Wuu^{\ast}W^{\ast}
.

For a given choice of
X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})
, it is
generally not the case that

W^{\ast}XW\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{1};\mathcal{X}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{1})
.
It may not even be the case that

is the Choi representation of a
channel, and in the case that
W^{\ast}XW
is the Choi representation of a
channel, it will generally not be the case that this channel obeys the
constraints necessary for it to be a valid strategy operator.
When combined with the observation that

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})
and

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{1};\mathcal{X}_{n},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{1})
are compact and convex sets, this
fact implies that the main theorem cannot possibly hold for all Hermitian
operators

by the separating hyperplane theorem.
For small values of

and for spaces having small dimensions, simple
examples of operators

obtained through random selections.

In Section

we discuss another
interpretation of Theorem
1
, which concerns multiple round
entanglement manipulation.

In this section we connect the main result proved in the previous section to

These function, which were first introduced in

First, one defines the max- and min-relative entropy of

Online pure state entanglement manipulation

The following three statements are equivalent for a given operator

X\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes\mathcal{X})

1.

X\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
.
(Equivalently,
X\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes\mathcal{X})
and
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}(X)=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}}

for some channel
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
.

3.

X=(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y})}\otimes\Psi)(\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}})\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}})^{\ast})

for some completely positive and unital map
\Psi\in\mathrm{CP}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
.

(Here and throughout this section,

refers to the vectorization
mapping, which is the mapping obtained by extending the transformation

\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}})

of a system whose state space is

\Phi
and

and

.
To obtain a Kraus representation for
\Psi
, for instance, one may simply
take a Kraus representation of
\Phi
and transpose each of the Kraus
operators.
(The transpose of an arbitrary map can be defined in a manner that is
consistent with these statements, but it is sufficient for our needs to focus
on channels and completely positive unital maps.)

framework may also be verified.

(59)

satisfies

X=\bigl(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n})}\otimes\Lambda_{n}\bigr)(\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})^{\ast}).

(60)

Through this equivalence, for a given state

\rho\in\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n})
,
one arrives at an alternative interpretation of the semidefinite program

\displaystyle\text{maximize:}

\displaystyle\langle\rho,X\rangle

(61)

\displaystyle\text{subject to:}

\displaystyle X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})

that concerns an online variant of entanglement manipulation, as is explained
shortly.
The term “online” in this context refers to a situation in which a quantum
state must be manipulated in multiple turns, where an output is required
immediately after each input system arrives and prior to the next input system
being made available, similar to an online process.

By the equivalence of the third statement above to the first, a maximization
over all
X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n};\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{n})
is equivalent to a
maximization over all operators

\bigl(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n})}\otimes\Lambda_{n}\bigr)(\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})^{\ast})

(62)

for

\Lambda_{n}=\bigl(\Psi_{1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{n})}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n-1})}\otimes\Psi_{n}\bigr)

(63)

and
\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}
being completely positive and unital maps of
the form

\Psi_{k}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{Y}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{k},\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k}).

(64)

The value of the objective function
\langle\rho,X\rangle
may therefore be expressed
as

\bigl\langle(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n})}\otimes\Lambda_{n}^{\ast})(\rho),\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})\operatorname{vec}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}})^{\ast}\bigr\rangle,

5
Conclusion

We have identified a time-reversal property for rank-one quantum strategy
functions, explained its connection to conditional min- and max-entropy, and
described an alternative view of this property through an online variant of
pure state entanglement manipulation.
An obvious question arises: are there interesting applications or implications
of this property beyond those we have mentioned?

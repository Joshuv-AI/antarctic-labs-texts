An extension of the stochastic sewing lemma and applications to fractional stochastic calculus
Toyomu Matsuda, Nicolas Perkowski

Abstract
We give an extension of Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma [ Electron. J. Probab. 25: 1 - 55, 2020]. The stochastic sewing lemma proves convergence in L m L_{m} of Riemann type sums ∑ [ s , t ] ∈ π A s , t \sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}A_{s,t} for an adapted two-parameter stochastic process A A , under certain conditions on the moments of A s , t A_{s,t} and of conditional expectations of A s , t A_{s,t} given ℱ s \mathcal{F}_{s} . Our extension replaces the conditional expectation given ℱ s \mathcal{F}_{s} by that given ℱ v \mathcal{F}_{v} for v < s v<s , and it allows to make use of asymptotic decorrelation properties between A s , t A_{s,t} and ℱ v \mathcal{F}_{v} by including a singularity in ( s − v ) (s-v) . We provide three applications for which Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma seems to be insufficient. The first is to prove the convergence of Itô or Stratonovich approximations of stochastic integrals along fractional Brownian motions under low regularity assumptions. The second is to obtain new representations of local times of fractional Brownian motions via discretization. The third is to improve a regularity assumption on the diffusion coefficient of a stochastic differential equation driven by a fractional Brownian motion for pathwise uniqueness and strong existence. Keywords and phrases. stochastic sewing lemma, fractional Brownian motion, stochastic integrals, local time, stochastic differential equations. MSC 2020 . 60G22, 60H05, 60H10, 60J55

Introduction and the main theorem

In analysis and probability theory, we often consider the convergence of sums

\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}A_{s,t}.

(1.1)

Here
\pi
is a partition of an interval
[0,T]
and we consider the limit of

\lvert\pi\rvert\vcentcolon=\max_{[s,t]\in\pi}\lvert t-s\rvert\to 0.

For instance, if
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=f(s)(t-s)
, then we consider a Riemann sum approximation of
\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
,
and if
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=X_{s}(W_{t}-W_{s})
, where
W
is a Brownian motion and
X
is an adapted process,
then we consider the Itô approximation of the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{T}X_{r}\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}
.

Gubinelli
[
18
]
, inspired by Lyons’ results on almost multiplicative functionals in the theory of rough paths
[
29
]
, showed that if

\delta A_{s,u,t}\vcentcolon=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t},\quad 0\leq s<u<t\leq T,

(1.2)

satisfies
\lvert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rvert\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{1+\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then
the sums (
1.1
) converge. This result is now called the
sewing lemma
, named so in the work
of Feyel and de La Pradelle
[
14
]
. This lemma is so powerful that many applications and many extensions are known.
For instance, it can be used to define rough integrals, see
[
18
]
and the monograph
[
15
]
of Friz and Hairer.

When
(A_{s,t})_{s\leq t}
is random and when we want to prove the convergence of the sums (
1.1
),
the above sewing lemma is often not sufficient. For instance, if
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=(W_{t}-W_{s})^{2}
, the sums converge to the
quadratic variation of the Brownian motion. However, we only have

\lvert\delta A_{s,u,t}(\omega)\rvert\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\omega}\lvert t-s\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}

almost surely
for every
\varepsilon>0
, and hence we cannot apply the sewing lemma.

Lê
[
24
]
proved a stochastic version of the sewing lemma (
stochastic sewing lemma
):
if a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is given
such that

•

A_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and

•

for some
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
and
m\in[2,\infty)
, we have for every
s<u<t
,

\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{1+\varepsilon_{2}},

(1.3)

\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1}},

(1.4)

then the sums (
1.1
) converge in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
.
As usual, the Banach space
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
is equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\lVert X\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\vcentcolon=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\lvert X\rvert^{m}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m}}.

If
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=(W_{t}-W_{s})^{2}
, then we have
\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]=0
and
(
1.4
) is satisfied with
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
.
Therefore, we can prove the convergence of (
1.1
) in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
.
The stochastic sewing lemma has been already shown to be very powerful in the original work
[
24
]
of Lê,
and an increasing number of papers are appearing that take advantage of the lemma.

However, there are situations where Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma seems insufficient.
For instance, consider

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\lvert B_{t}-B_{s}\rvert^{\frac{1}{H}},

(1.5)

where
B
is a fractional Brownian motion
with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
. It is well known that the sums (
1.1
) converge to

c_{H}T
in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
. Although we have the estimate (
1.4
), we fail to obtain the estimate
(
1.3
) unless
H=\frac{1}{2}
.

To get an idea on how Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma should be modified for this problem,
observe the following trivial fact:

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}]=0.

This suggests that we consider estimates that interpolate
\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}]
and

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
. In fact, we can obtain the following estimates:

\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}(t-s),\quad 0\leq v<s<u<t\leq T.

(1.6)

We can prove (
1.6
) for instance by applying Picard’s result
[
36
, Lemma A.1]
on the asymptotic independence of fractional Brownian increments,
or more directly by doing a similar calculation as in Section
4
.
This discussion motivates the following main theorem of our paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that we have a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and a family of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variables

(A_{s,t})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
such that

A_{s,s}=0
for every
s\in[0,T]
and such that
A_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
We define
\delta A_{s,u,t}
by (
1.2
).
Furthermore, suppose that there exist constants

m\in[2,\infty),\quad\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},M\in[0,\infty),\quad\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in[0,\infty)

such that the following conditions are satisfied.

•

For every
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{1}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{-\alpha}(t_{3}-t_{1})^{\beta_{1}},\quad\text{if }M(t_{3}-t_{1})\leq t_{1}-t_{0},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{2}(t_{2}-t_{0})^{\beta_{2}}.

(1.8)

•

We have

\beta_{1}>1,\quad\beta_{2}>\frac{1}{2},\quad\beta_{1}-\alpha>\frac{1}{2}.

(1.9)

Then, there exists a unique, up to modifications,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued stochastic process
(\mathcal{A}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
with
the following properties.

•

\mathcal{A}_{0}=0
,
\mathcal{A}_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and
\mathcal{A}_{t}
belongs to
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
.

•

There exist non-negative constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that

\lVert\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{A}_{t_{2}}-\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}}-A_{t_{1},t_{2}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq C_{1}\lvert t_{1}-t_{0}\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert t_{2}-t_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{1}},

(1.10)

\lVert\mathcal{A}_{t_{2}}-\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}}-A_{t_{1},t_{2}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq C_{2}\lvert t_{2}-t_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{1}-\alpha}+C_{3}\lvert t_{2}-t_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{2}},

(1.11)

where
t_{2}-t_{1}\leq M^{-1}(t_{1}-t_{0})
is assumed for the inequality (
1.10
).

In fact, we can choose
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
so that

C_{1}\lesssim_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma_{1},\quad C_{2}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{1},\quad C_{3}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{2},

where
\kappa_{m,d}
is the constant of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, see (
1.14
).
Furthermore, for
\tau\in[0,T]
, if we set

A^{\pi}_{\tau}\vcentcolon=\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}A_{s,t},\quad\text{where $\pi$ is a partition of $[0,\tau]$},

then the family
(A^{\pi}_{\tau})_{\pi}
converges to
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
as

\lvert\pi\rvert
tends to
0
.

Remark 1.2
.

We discuss the optimality of the condition (
1.9
). By considering the deterministic
(A_{s,t})
, we see that
the condition
\beta_{1}>1
is necessary. To see that the conditions
\beta_{2}>\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta_{1}-\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
are necessary,
let
B^{1}
and
B^{2}
be two independent one-dimensional fractional Brownian motions with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{4}
(see Definition
3.1
), and we set

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=B^{1}_{s}B^{2}_{s,t}
. It is well-known since the work
[
10
]
of Coutin and Qian that the iterated integral
\int B^{1}\mathrm{d}B^{2}
does not exist,
and therefore the Riemann sum with respect to
(A_{s,t})
should not converge.
In fact, the family
(A_{s,t})
, with filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
generated by
(B^{1},B^{2})
, satisfies (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) with

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{3}{2},\quad\beta_{1}=2,\quad\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

To see this, we observe
\delta A_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=-B^{1}_{t_{1},t_{2}}B^{2}_{t_{2},t_{3}}
, and

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{m}(t_{3}-t_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To compute the conditional expectation, we observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}]=-\mathbb{E}[B^{1}_{t_{1},t_{2}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}]\mathbb{E}[B^{2}_{t_{2},t_{3}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}],

and by the estimate (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{m}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(t_{3}-t_{1})^{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

The proof shows that if

1+\alpha-\beta_{1}<2\alpha\beta_{2}-\alpha,

(1.12)

then we have
C_{2}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\Gamma_{1}
and we can omit the factor
\kappa_{m,d}
.
This is similar to
[
24
]
, where
C_{2}
also does not depend on
\kappa_{m,d}
.
If
\alpha=0
and
M=0
,
Theorem
1.1
recovers Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
.
If
\alpha=0
and
M>0
, it recovers a lemma
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
by Gerencsér.

Recently, Gerecsér’s stochastic sewing lemma is called
shifted
stochastic sewing lemma.
In the follow-up works, we continue to refer to Theorem
1.1
by the same name.

Remark 1.4
.

The proof shows that there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})>0
such that

\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\tau}-A^{\pi}_{\tau}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M,m,d,T}(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})\lvert\pi\rvert^{\varepsilon}

for every
\tau\in[0,T]
and every partition
\pi
of
[0,\tau]
.
A similar remark holds in the setting of Corollary
2.7
.

Remark 1.5
.

As in another work
[
25
]
of Lê, it should be possible to extend
Theorem
1.1

so that the stochastic process
(A_{s,t})_{s,t\in[0,T]}
takes values in a certain Banach space.

Remark 1.6
.

A multidimensional version of the sewing lemma is the
reconstruction theorem

[
19
, Theorem 3.10]
of
Hairer. A stochastic version of the reconstruction theorem was obtained by Kern
[
21
]
.
It could be possible to extend Theorem
1.1
in the multidimensional setting, but we will not pursue it

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
2
.
If
A_{s,t}
is given by (
1.5
), then we can apply Theorem
1.1
with

\alpha=1-H,\quad\beta_{1}=2-H,\quad\beta_{2}=1.

However,
the application of Theorem

Indeed, in Section

with

Finally, we remark that one of the most interesting applications of Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma lies in
the phenomenon of
regularization by noise
, see e.g.
[
24
]
, Athreya, Butkovsky, Lê and Mytnik
[
2
]
,

[
17
]
and Anzeletti, Richard and Tanré
[
1
]
.
In these works, they consider the stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\,\mathrm{d}Y_{t}

(1.13)

with an
additive
noise
Y
, which is often a fractional Brownian motion.
It is interesting that, although in absence of noise the coefficient
b
needs to belong to
C^{1}
for
well-posedness, the presence of noise enables us to prove certain well-posedness of (
1.13
) under much weaker assumption,
in fact
b
can be even a distribution; hence the name
regularization by noise
.

In Section
5
of our paper, we are interested in a related but different problem.
Indeed, we are interested in improving the regularity of the diffusion coefficient rather than the drift coefficient.
We consider the Young SDE

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}

driven by a fractional Brownian motion
B
with Hurst parameter
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
The pathwise theory of Young’s differential equation requires that the regularity of
\sigma
is better than

1/H
for uniqueness, and this condition is sharp for general drivers
B
of the same regularity as the fractional Brownian motion. We will improve this regularity assumption for pathwise uniqueness and strong existence.
Again, a stochastic sewing lemma (Lemma
5.5
), which is a variant of Theorem
1.1
, will play a key role.

Notation

We write

We denote by

\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\kappa_{m,d}\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}^{2}\rVert_{L_{\frac{m}{2}}(\mathbb{P})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.14)

Rather than (

), we mostly use the inequality

\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\kappa_{m,d}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lVert X_{n}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.15)

for
m\geq 2
, which follows from (

) by Minkowski’s inequality.
We write
A\lesssim B
or

if there exists a non-negative constant

Proof of the main theorem

The overall strategy of the proof is the same as that of the original work
[

]

Namely, we combine the argument of the deterministic sewing lemma (

]
,
[

]
and

]

.

As in
[

]
, the following lemma, which originates from

]

Lemma 2.1

(
[
24
, Lemma 2.14]
)
.

Under the setting of Theorem
1.1
,
let

0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<t_{N}\leq T.

Then, we have

A_{t_{0},t_{N}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{t_{i-1},t_{i}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}R^{n}_{i},

(2.1)

where

R^{n}_{i}\vcentcolon=\delta A_{s^{n,i}_{1},s^{n,i}_{2},s^{n,i}_{3}}+\delta A_{s^{n,i}_{1},s^{n,i}_{3},s^{n,i}_{4}},

(2.2)

and

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{n}-1\},\quad s^{n,i}_{j}\in[t_{0}+\frac{i(t_{N}-t_{0})}{2^{n}},t_{0}+\frac{(i+1)(t_{N}-t_{0})}{2^{n}}],

and where

.

The next two lemmas (Lemma

and Lemma

) correspond to the estimates

[

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the setting of Theorem
1.1
,
let

0\leq s<t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<t_{N}\leq T,\quad t_{N}-t_{1}\leq\frac{t_{0}-s}{M}.

Then,

\lVert\mathbb{E}[A_{t_{0},t_{N}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{t_{i-1},t_{i}}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma_{1}\lvert t_{0}-s\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert t_{N}-t_{0}\rvert^{\beta_{1}}.

Proof.

In view of the decomposition (
2.1
), the triangle inequality gives

\lVert\mathbb{E}[A_{t_{0},t_{N}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{t_{i-1},t_{i}}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}\lVert\mathbb{E}[R_{i}^{n}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}.

By (
1.7
) and (

),

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[R_{i}^{n}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\leq 2\Gamma_{1}(t_{0}-s)^{-\alpha}(2^{-n}\lvert t_{N}-t_{0}\rvert)^{\beta_{1}}=2\Gamma_{1}2^{-n\beta_{1}}\lvert t_{0}-s\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert t_{N}-t_{0}\rvert^{\beta_{1}}.

Therefore, recalling
\beta_{1}>1
from (
1.9
), the claim follows.
∎

The following lemma is the most important technical ingredient for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the setting of Theorem
1.1
,
let

0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<t_{N}\leq T.

Then,

\lVert A_{t_{0},t_{N}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{t_{i-1},t_{i}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{1}\lvert t_{N}-t_{0}\rvert^{\beta_{1}-\alpha}+\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{2}\lvert t_{N}-t_{0}\rvert^{\beta_{2}}.

Under (
1.12
), we can replace

Proof under (
1.12
).

To simplify the proof, here we assume (

We again use the representation (
2.1
).
We fix a large

and set
\mathcal{F}^{n}_{k}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}+\frac{k}{2^{n}}(t_{N}-t_{0})}
.
Fix an integer

We have

\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}R^{n}_{i}=\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\
Lj+l<2^{n}\end{subarray}}\Big(R^{n}_{Lj+l}-\mathbb{E}[R^{n}_{Lj+l}|\mathcal{F}_{L(j-1)+l+1}^{n}]\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}\Big)\\
+\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\
Lj+l<2^{n}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[R^{n}_{Lj+l}|\mathcal{F}_{L(j-1)+l+1}^{n}].

(2.3)

We estimate the first term of (
2.3
).
By the BDG inequality together with Minkowski’s inequality (see (
1.15
)), we have

\displaystyle\lVert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\
Lj+l<2^{n}\end{subarray}}\Big(R^{n}_{Lj+l}-\mathbb{E}[R^{n}_{Lj+l}|\mathcal{F}_{L(j-1)+l+1}^{n}]\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}\Big)\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{m,d}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\
Lj+l<2^{n}\end{subarray}}\lVert R^{n}_{Lj+l}-\mathbb{E}[R^{n}_{Lj+l}|\mathcal{F}_{L(j-1)+l+1}^{n}]\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\kappa_{m,d}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\
Lj+l<2^{n}\end{subarray}}\lVert R^{n}_{Lj+l}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}.

Using (
1.8
) and (
2.2
) and noting that we include more terms in the sum by requiring

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\

).
The triangle inequality yields

\lVert\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\

\lVert\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0:\\

(2.4)

3
Integration along fractional Brownian motions

The goal of this section is to prove the convergence of Itô and Stratonovich approximations of

\int_{0}^{t}f(B_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{t}f(B_{s})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}

along a multidimensional fractional Brownian motion
B
with Hurst parameter
H
, using Theorem
1.1
.
For Itô’s case, we let
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and for Stratonovich’s case, we let
H\in(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{F})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a filtration.
We say that a process
B
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)

if

•

a two-sided
d
-dimensional
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion
(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is given;

•

a random variable
B(0)
is a (not necessarily centered)
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable

\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian random variable and is independent of
(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
;

•

if we set

K(t,s)\vcentcolon=K_{H}(t,s)\vcentcolon=[(t-s)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-s)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}],

then we have the
Mandelbrot–van Ness representation
(
[
30
]
)

B_{t}=B(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{H}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

(3.1)

If
B
has the representation (
3.1
), then

\mathbb{E}[(B_{t}^{i}-B_{s}^{i})(B_{t}^{j}-B_{s}^{j})]=\delta_{ij}c_{H}\lvert t-s\rvert^{2H},\quad c_{H}\vcentcolon=\frac{3/2-H}{2H}\mathcal{B}(2-2H,H+1/2)

where we write
B=(B^{i})_{i=1}^{d}
in components and
\mathcal{B}
is the Beta function

\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Regarding the expression of the constant
c_{H}
, see
[
37
, Appendix B]
.
In particular, we have

\mathbb{E}[(B_{s}^{i}-B(0)^{i})(B_{t}^{i}-B(0)^{i})]=\frac{c_{H}}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-\lvert t-s\rvert^{2H}).

(3.2)

In this section, we always write
B
for an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fractional Brownian motion.
An advantage of the representation (
3.1
) is that given
v<s
, we have the decomposition

B_{s}-B(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{v}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}+\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r},

where the second term
\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
Later we will need to estimate the correlation of

\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r},\quad s>v.

We note that for
s\leq t

\mathbb{E}[\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{i}\int_{v}^{t}K(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{j}]=\delta_{ij}\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)K(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Lemma 3.2

Let

\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)K(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\\

\lvert g_{H}(v,s,t)\rvert\lesssim_{H}(s-v)^{2H-2}(t-s)^{2}.

uniformly over such
v,s,t
.

See Appendix
A
.

An advantage of the stochastic sewing lemma is that

We denote by

\mathbb{R}^{d}

We write

\mathbb{R}^{d}

0\leq s<t\leq T
,

\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(B_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{d,H,m}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lvert t-s\rvert^{H}

Remark 3.4
.

We can replace
f(B_{s})
by
f(B_{u})
for any
u\in[s,t]
. It is well known that the sums converge to the Young integral
if
f\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})
with
\gamma>H^{-1}(1-H)
.
Yaskov
[
39
, Theorem 3.7]
proves that the sums converge in some
L_{p}(\mathbb{P})
-space if
f
is of bounded variation.

Proof.

We will not write down dependence on
d
,
H
and
m
.
The filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is generated by the Brownian motion
W
appearing in
the Mandelbrot–van Ness representation (
3.1
).
We will apply Theorem
1.1
with
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=f(B_{s})\cdot(B_{t}-B_{s})
.
Let
m\geq 2
.
We have

\lVert A_{s,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}}\lvert t-s\rvert^{H}.

To estimate conditional expectations, let
0\leq v<s<t
be such that
t-s\leq s-v
and set

Y_{s}\vcentcolon=\int_{-\infty}^{v}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r},\quad\tilde{B}_{s}\vcentcolon=\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}.

We write
y_{s}\vcentcolon=Y_{s}
, if conditioned under
\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
Namely, we write for instance

\mathbb{E}[g(y_{s},\tilde{B}_{s})]\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[g(Y_{s},\tilde{B}_{s})|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=\mathbb{E}[g(y,\tilde{B}_{s})]|_{y=Y_{s}}.

We are going to compute

\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]
.
Conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{v}
, we have the Wiener chaos expansion
[
34
, Theorem 1.1.1]

f(B_{s})=f(y_{s}+\tilde{B}_{s})=a_{0}(s)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(s)\tilde{B}^{i}_{s}+\tilde{B}^{\perp}_{s},

where
\tilde{B}^{\perp}_{s}
is orthogonal in
L_{2}(\mathbb{P})
to the subspace spanned by the constant
1
and

\displaystyle(\tilde{B}^{i}_{r})_{i=1,\ldots,d;r\geq v}.

Note that

\displaystyle a_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[f(y_{s}+\tilde{B}_{s})],

\displaystyle a_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{B}_{s}^{i})^{2}]^{-1}\mathbb{E}[f(y_{s}+\tilde{B}_{s})\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}]\overset{\text{Lem.\penalty\ \ref{lem:kernel_correlation}}}{=}2H(s-v)^{-2H}\mathbb{E}[f(y_{s}+\tilde{B}_{s})\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}].

Then, by the orthogonality of the Wiener chaos decomposition,

\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=a_{0}(s)\cdot Y_{s,t}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(s)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B_{s}^{i}}\tilde{B}_{s,t}].

Hence, for
u\in(s,t)
,

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=A^{0}_{s,u,t}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}A^{i}_{s,u,t},

where

\displaystyle A^{0}_{s,u,t}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle a_{0}(s)\cdot Y_{s,t}-a_{0}(s)\cdot Y_{s,u}-a_{0}(u)\cdot Y_{u,t}=(a_{0}(s)-a_{0}(u))\cdot Y_{u,t},

\displaystyle A^{i}_{s,u,t}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle a_{i}(s)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}]-a_{i}(s)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,u}]-a_{i}(u)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{u}^{i}\tilde{B}_{u,t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a_{i}(s)\cdot\bm{e}_{i}]\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}]-[a_{i}(s)\cdot\bm{e}_{i}]\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,u}^{i}]-[a_{i}(u)\cdot\bm{e}_{i}]\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{u}^{i}\tilde{B}_{u,t}^{i}].

Here
\bm{e}_{i}
is the
i
th unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We first estimate
A^{0}_{s,u,t}
, for which we begin with estimating
a_{0}(s)-a_{0}(u)
. We set

F(m,\sigma)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[f(m+\sigma X)],\quad m\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\sigma\in(0,\infty),

where
X
has the standard normal distribution in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that

a_{0}(s)=F(Y_{s},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})

and similarly for
a_{0}(u)
.
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{m^{i}}F(m,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{i}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}f(x+m)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{-d}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(m+x)e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert x\rvert^{2}f(m+x)e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore,

\lvert\partial_{m}F(m,\sigma)\rvert+\lvert\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sigma^{-1}.

This yields

\displaystyle\lvert a_{0}(s)-a_{0}(u)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\lvert F(Y_{s},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})-F(Y_{u},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})\rvert

\displaystyle+\lvert F(Y_{u},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})-F(Y_{u},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)^{H})\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert+\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}(\lvert u-v\rvert^{H}-\lvert s-v\rvert^{H})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert+\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-1}(t-s).

Therefore,

\lvert A^{0}_{s,u,t}\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert\lvert Y_{u,t}\rvert+\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-1}(t-s)\lvert Y_{u,t}\rvert.

(3.3)

The random variable
Y_{s,u}
is Gaussian and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert^{2}]=

\displaystyle d\int_{-\infty}^{v}(K(s,r)-K(u,r))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=d\int_{s-v}^{\infty}((u-s+r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-r^{H-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(u-s)^{2}\int_{s-v}^{\infty}r^{2H-3}\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-2}(u-s)^{2}.

(3.4)

We have a similar estimate for
Y_{u,t}
. Therefore,

\lVert A^{0}_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{H-2}(t-s)^{2}\quad\text{if }t-s\leq v-s.

Now we move to estimate
A^{i}_{s,u,t}
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}]

\displaystyle=\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)K(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{v}^{s}K(s,r)K(s,r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-s)+O((t-s)^{2H}).

Therefore, if we write
a_{i}^{i}(s)\vcentcolon=a_{i}(s)\cdot\bm{e}_{i}
,

A^{i}_{s,u,t}=\frac{1}{2}\big[a_{i}^{i}(s)(s-v)^{2H-1}-a_{i}^{i}(u)(u-v)^{2H-1}\big](t-u)\\
+O((\lvert a_{i}^{i}(s)\rvert+\lvert a_{i}^{i}(u)\rvert)\lvert t-s\rvert^{2H}).

If we set

G_{i}(m,\sigma)\vcentcolon=\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{E}[f^{i}(m+\sigma X)X^{i}],\quad m\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\sigma\in(0,\infty),

then
a_{i}^{i}(s)=G_{i}(Y_{s},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})
and similarly for
a_{i}^{i}(u)
.
Since

G_{i}(m,\sigma)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{-d-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{i}(y)(y^{i}-m^{i})e^{-\frac{\lvert y-m\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

we have

(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{2}\partial_{m^{j}}G_{i}(m,\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{i}(m+\sigma x)[-\delta_{ij}+x^{i}x^{j}]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{2}\partial_{\sigma}G_{i}(m,\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{i}(m+\sigma x)x^{i}[-(d+2)+\lvert x\rvert^{2}]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore,

\lvert G_{i}(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sigma^{-1},

\lvert\partial_{m}G_{i}(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sigma^{-2},\quad\lvert\partial_{\sigma}G_{i}(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sigma^{-2}

and thus

\lvert a^{i}_{i}(s)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H},

\displaystyle\lvert a^{i}_{i}(s)-a^{i}_{i}(u)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-2H}\big(\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert+(u-v)^{H}-(s-v)^{H}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-2H}\big(\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert+(s-v)^{H-1}(u-s)\big).

This yields

\lvert A^{i}_{s,u,t}\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big[(s-v)^{-1}(t-s)\lvert y_{s,u}\rvert+(s-v)^{H-2}(t-s)^{2}\\
+(s-v)^{H-2}(t-s)^{2}+(s-v)^{-H}(t-s)^{2H}\big]

and

\displaystyle\lVert A^{i}_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big[(s-v)^{H-2}(t-s)^{2}+(s-v)^{-H}(t-s)^{2H}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}(t-s)^{2H}

(3.5)

if
t-s\leq s-v
.

Therefore, by (
3.3
) and (
3.5
),

\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(s-v)^{-H}(t-s)^{2H}

if
t-s\leq s-v
. Hence,
(A_{s,t})
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
1.1

with

\alpha=H,\quad\beta_{1}=2H,\quad\beta_{2}=H,\quad M=1.\qed

Next, we consider the case
H\in(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2})
. The following result
reproduces
[
33
, Theorem 3.5]
, with a more elementary proof and with improvement of the regularity of
f

while here
f\in C^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>\frac{1}{2H}-1

works for all
H\in(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2})
.
We denote by
C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of
\gamma
-Hölder maps from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\vcentcolon=\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\gamma}}

if
\gamma\in(0,1)
and

\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\vcentcolon=\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lVert\partial_{i}f\rVert_{C^{\gamma-1}}

if
\gamma\in(1,2)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H\in(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2})
,
\gamma>\frac{1}{2H}-1
and
f\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
d>1
, assume furthermore that

\partial_{i}f^{j}=\partial_{j}f^{i},\quad\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.

(3.6)

Then, for every
m\in[2,\infty)
and
\tau\in[0,T]
, the family of Stratonovich approximations

\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}\frac{f(B_{s})+f(B_{t})}{2}\cdot B_{s,t},\quad\text{where $\pi$ is a partition of $[0,\tau]$},

converges in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
as
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0
.
Moreover, if we denote the limit by
\int_{0}^{\tau}f(B_{r})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}
and if we write

\int_{s}^{t}f(B_{r})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}f(B_{r})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}f(B_{r})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},

then for every
0\leq s<t\leq T
we have

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(B_{r})\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-\frac{f(B_{s})+f(B_{t})}{2}\cdot B_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{d,H,m,\gamma}\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\lvert t-s\rvert^{(\gamma+1)H}.

Proof.

We will not write down dependence on
d
,
H
,
m
and
\gamma
.
The filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is generated by the Brownian motion
W
appearing in
the Mandelbrot–van Ness representation (
3.1
).
We can assume

\gamma<\mathbbm{1}_{\{H>\frac{1}{4}\}}+2\mathbbm{1}_{\{H\leq\frac{1}{4}\}}.

We will apply Theorem
1.1
with

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=(f(B_{s})+f(B_{t}))\cdot B_{s,t}.

We first claim

\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\lvert t-s\rvert^{(\gamma+1)H}.

(3.7)

Observe

\delta A_{s,u,t}=f(B)_{u,t}\cdot B_{s,u}+f(B)_{u,s}\cdot B_{u,t}.

If
H>\frac{1}{4}
, the claim (
3.7
) follows from the estimates

\lvert f(B)_{u,t}\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\lvert B_{u,t}\rvert^{\gamma},\quad\lvert f(B)_{u,s}\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\lvert B_{u,s}\rvert^{\gamma}.

If
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
, then
\gamma>1
and we have

\delta A_{s,u,t}=\Big(f(B)_{u,t}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}f(B_{u})B_{u,t}^{j}\Big)\cdot B_{s,u}+\Big(f(B)_{u,s}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}f(B_{u})B_{u,s}^{j}\Big)\cdot B_{u,t},

where (
3.6
) is used.
Then, the claim (
3.7
) follows again from the Hölder estimate of
f
.
Note that the condition
\gamma>\frac{1}{2H}-1
is equivalent to
(\gamma+1)H>\frac{1}{2}
.

The rest of the proof consists of estimating the conditional expectation

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]
.
Let

t-s\leq s-v
.
We will use the same notation as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
We have

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=D^{0}_{s,u,t}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D^{i}_{s,u,t},

where

\displaystyle D^{0}_{s,u,t}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle(a_{0}(s)+a_{0}(t))\cdot Y_{s,t}-(a_{0}(s)+a_{0}(u))\cdot Y_{s,u}-(a_{0}(u)+a_{0}(t))\cdot Y_{u,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{0}(t)-a_{0}(u))\cdot Y_{s,u}+(a_{0}(s)-a_{0}(u))\cdot Y_{u,t}.

(3.8)

and

D^{i}_{s,u,t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[(a_{i}^{i}(s)\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}+a_{i}^{i}(t)\tilde{B}_{t}^{i})\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\\
-\mathbb{E}[(a_{i}^{i}(s)\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}+a_{i}^{i}(u)\tilde{B}_{u}^{i})\tilde{B}_{s,u}^{i}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[(a_{i}^{i}(u)\tilde{B}_{u}^{i}+a_{i}^{i}(t)\tilde{B}_{t}^{i})\tilde{B}_{u,t}^{i}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]

We first estimate
D^{0}_{s,u,t}
.
Suppose that
H>\frac{1}{4}
.
Recall

\displaystyle\partial_{m^{i}}F(m,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{i}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}[f(x+m)-f(m)]\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{-d}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(m+x)-f(m)]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert x\rvert^{2}[f(m+x)-f(m)]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore,

\lvert\partial_{m^{i}}F(m,\sigma)\rvert+\lvert\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\sigma^{\gamma-1}.

This yields

\lvert D^{0}_{s,u,t}\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\big[(s-v)^{(\gamma-1)H}\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert\lvert Y_{u,t}\rvert+(s-v)^{\gamma H-1}(t-s)(\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert+\lvert Y_{u,t}\rvert)\big].

(3.9)

Therefore, by (
3.4
),

\lVert D^{0}_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma+1)H-2}(t-s)^{2}.

(3.10)

Now suppose that
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
. To simplify notation, we write
I(m,\sigma)\vcentcolon=F(m,(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma)
.
Since (
3.6
) gives
\partial_{m^{i}}I^{j}=\partial_{m^{j}}I^{i}
for every
i,j
, we have

\displaystyle D^{0}_{s,u,t}=

\displaystyle[I(Y_{s},(u-v)^{H})-I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{m^{i}}I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})Y_{u,s}^{i}]\cdot Y_{u,t}

\displaystyle+[I(Y_{t},(u-v)^{H})-I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{m^{i}}I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})Y_{u,t}^{i}]\cdot Y_{s,u}

\displaystyle+[I(Y_{s},(s-v)^{H})-I(Y_{s},(u-v)^{H})]\cdot Y_{u,t}

\displaystyle+[I(Y_{t},(t-v)^{H})-I(Y_{t},(u-v)^{H})]\cdot Y_{s,u}.

Since

\partial_{m^{i}}\partial_{m^{j}}F(m,\sigma)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{i}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}[\partial_{j}f(x+m)-\partial_{j}f(m)]\,\mathrm{d}x,

we have

\lvert I(Y_{s},(u-v)^{H})-I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{m^{i}}I(Y_{u},(u-v)^{H})Y_{u,s}^{i}\rvert\\
\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma-2)H}\lvert Y_{s,u}\rvert^{2}.

Notice

\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)=\frac{-d}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(m+x)-f(m)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}f(m)x^{i}]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{d+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert x\rvert^{2}[f(m+x)-f(m)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}f(m)x^{i}]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore,

\lvert\partial_{\sigma}F(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\sigma^{\gamma-1}.

This yields

\lvert I(Y_{s},(s-v)^{H})-I(Y_{s},(u-v)^{H})\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{\gamma H-1}(t-s).

Hence, we obtain the estimate (
3.10
) when
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

We move to estimate
D^{i}_{s,u,t}
. By using the identity,

\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{B}_{a}^{i}+\tilde{B}_{b}^{i})\tilde{B}_{a,b}^{i}]=\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{B}_{b}^{i})^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{B}_{a}^{i})^{2}],

we obtain

D^{i}_{s,u,t}=(a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{t}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}]+(a_{i}^{i}(s)-a_{i}^{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}]\\
-(a_{i}^{i}(s)-a_{i}^{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,u}^{i}]-(a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{t}^{i}\tilde{B}_{u,t}^{i}].

(3.11)

Since the other terms can be estimated similarly, we only estimate

(a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{t}^{i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{i}]
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\lvert\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{t}\tilde{B}_{s,t}]\rvert\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{2H}.

Now we estimate
\lvert a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u)\rvert
.
Recall
a_{i}^{i}(s)=G_{i}(Y_{s},(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s-v)^{H})
,

(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{2}\partial_{m^{j}}G_{i}(m,\sigma)=-\delta_{ij}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)]e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)]x^{i}x^{j}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sigma^{2}\partial_{\sigma}G(m,\sigma)=-(d+2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)]x^{i}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)]x^{i}\lvert x\rvert^{2}e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

If
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
, we can replace
f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)
by

f^{i}(m+\sigma x)-f^{i}(m)-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}f^{i}(m)\sigma x^{k}.

Therefore,

\lvert\partial_{m^{j}}G_{i}(m,\sigma)\rvert+\lvert\partial_{\sigma}G_{i}(m,\sigma)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}\sigma^{\gamma-2}.

This yields

\lvert a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u)\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma-2)H}(\lvert Y_{u,t}\rvert+(s-v)^{H-1}(t-s))

and hence

\lVert a_{i}^{i}(t)-a_{i}^{i}(u)\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma-1)H-1}(t-s).

Therefore, we obtain

\lVert D^{i}_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma-1)H-1}(t-s)^{1+2H}.

(3.12)

By (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), we conclude

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}[(s-v)^{(1+\gamma)H-2}(t-s)^{2}+(s-v)^{(\gamma-1)H-1}(t-s)^{1+2H}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{\gamma}}(s-v)^{(\gamma-1)H-1}(t-s)^{1+2H}

if
t-s\leq s-v
.
Therefore, we can apply Theorem
1.1
with

\alpha=1-(\gamma-1)H,\quad\beta_{1}=1+2H,\quad\beta_{2}=(\gamma+1)H,\quad M=1.\qed

4
Local times of fractional Brownian motions

In this section, we set
d=1
and we are interested in local times of fractional Brownian motions.
In case of a Brownian motion
W
, or more generally semi-martingales
as discussed in Łochowski, Obłoj, Prömel and Siorpaes
[
27
]
,
there are three major methods to construct its local time.

(a)

Via occupation measure.
The local time
L_{T}^{W}(\cdot)
of
W

is defined as the density with respect to the Lebesgue measure of

A\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(W_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Heuristically,

L_{T}^{W}(a)=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(W_{s}-a)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\delta
is Dirac’s delta function concentrated at
0
.

(b)

Via discretization.
The local time
L_{T}^{W}(a)
is defined by

L_{T}^{W}(a)\vcentcolon=\lim_{\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0}\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}\lvert W_{t}-a\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{(\min\{W_{s},W_{t}\},\max\{W_{s},W_{t}\})}(a),

where
\pi
is a partition of
[0,T]
and the convergence is in probability.
This representation of the local time is often used in the pathwise stochastic calculus,
see Wuermli
[
38
]
, Perkowski and Prömel
[
35
]
,
Davis, Obłoj and Siorpaes
[
12
]
,
Cont and Perkowski
[
9
]
and Kim
[
22
]
.

(c)

Via numbers of interval crossing.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
\tau^{n}_{0}\vcentcolon=0
and inductively

\tau^{n}_{k}\vcentcolon=\inf\{t>\tau^{n}_{k-1}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}W_{t}\in 2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{W_{\tau^{n}_{k-1}}\}\}.

Then, the local time
L_{T}^{W}(a)
is defined by

L^{W}_{T}(a)\vcentcolon=\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbbm{1}_{[\frac{k}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1}{2^{n}})}(a)\#\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\{W_{\tau_{\ell}^{n}},W_{\tau^{n}_{\ell+1}}\}=\{k2^{-n},(k+1)2^{-n}\},\tau^{n}_{k+1}\leq T\},

where the convergence holds almost surely.
See the monograph
[
31
]
for the Brownian motion. For general semi-martingales, see El
Karoui
[
13
]
, Lemieux
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
.

In case of a fractional Brownian motion, the construction of its local time via the method (a) is well-known,
see the survey
[
16
]
and the monograph
[
5
]
. In contrast,
there are few results in the literature in which the local time of
a fractional Brownian motion is constructed via the method (b) or (c).
Because of this, the construction of the local time via the method (c) was stated as a conjecture in
[
9
]
.
We are aware of only two results in this direction.
One is the work
[
4
]
of Azaïs, who proves Corollary
4.8
below.
The other is the work
[
32
]
of Mukeru,
who proves that the local time
L_{T}(a)
of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter less than
\frac{1}{2}

is represented as

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n(2H-1)}\sum_{k\leq\lfloor T2^{n}\rfloor}2\lvert B_{k2^{-n}}-a\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{\{(B_{k2^{-n}}-a)(B_{(k-1)2^{-n}}-a)<0\}}\quad\text{a.s.}

Our goal in this section is to give new representations of the local times of fractional Brownian motions
in the spirit of the method (b) along deterministic partitions.
The representation in Corollary
4.9
is compatible with
[
9
, Definition 3.1]
.

if

Lemma 4.7
.

We have

where

satisfies the estimate (

Proof.

[
3

.

[
7

.
By Lê’s stochastic sewing lemma
[
24
]
, to prove (

), it suffices to show

•

the estimate

\lVert e^{-\frac{H(Y_{s}-a)^{2}}{c_{H}(r-s)^{2H}}}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{T}\begin{cases}f_{H}(a),&H<\frac{1}{2},\\

•

and the identity

4.16

5
Regularization by noise for diffusion coefficients

Let
y\in C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. We consider a Young differential equation

\,\mathrm{d}x_{t}=b(x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}y_{t},\quad x_{0}=x.

(5.1)

We suppose that the drift coefficient
b
belongs to
C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of
continuously differentiable bounded functions between
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with bounded derivatives. If the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
belongs to
C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
, where
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is the space of
d\times d
matrices, then we can prove the existence of
a solution to (
5.1
). However, to prove the uniqueness of
solutions, the coefficient
\sigma
needs to be more regular.
The following result is well-known (e.g.
[
28
]
), but we give a proof for the sake of later discussion.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
b\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\sigma\in C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
with
\delta>\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}
. Then the Young differential equation
(
5.1
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

The argument is very similar to that of
[
24
, Theorem 6.2]
.
Let
x^{(i)}
(
i=1,2
) be two solutions to (
5.1
). Then,

\displaystyle x^{(1)}_{t}-x^{(2)}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\{b(x^{(1)}_{s})-b(x^{(2)}_{s})\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma(x^{(1)}_{s})-\sigma(x^{(2)}_{s})\}\,\mathrm{d}y_{s}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\{x^{(1)}_{s}-x^{(2)}_{s}\}\,\mathrm{d}v_{s},

where

v_{t}\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla b(\theta x^{(1)}_{s}+(1-\theta)x^{(2)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\sigma(\theta x^{(1)}_{s}+(1-\theta)x^{(2)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}y_{s}.

Note that the second term is well-defined as a Young integral since

s\mapsto\nabla\sigma(\theta x^{(1)}_{s}+(1-\theta)x^{(2)}_{s})

is
\delta\alpha
-Hölder continuous and
\delta\alpha+\alpha>1
by
our assumption of
\delta
. Therefore,
x^{(1)}-x^{(2)}
is a solution
of the Young differential equation

\,\mathrm{d}z_{t}=z_{t}\,\mathrm{d}v_{t},\quad z_{0}=0.

The uniqueness of this linear Young differential equation is known. Hence
x^{(1)}-x^{(2)}=0.

∎

Proposition
5.1
is sharp in the sense that for any
\alpha\in(1,2)
and any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha})
, we can find

\sigma\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathcal{M}_{2})
and
y\in C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that the Young differential equation

\,\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\sigma(x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}y_{t}

has more than one solution, see Davie
[
11
, Section 5]
.
However, if the driver
y
is random,
we could hope to obtain the uniqueness of solutions in a probabilistic sense even when the regularity of
\sigma
does not satisfy the assumption of
Proposition
5.1
.
For instance, if the driver
y
is a Brownian motion and the integral is understood
in Itô’s sense, the condition
\sigma\in C^{1}_{b}
is sufficient to prove pathwise uniqueness.

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
B
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)

in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
Let
b\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\sigma\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
.
Assume one of the following.

(a)

We have
b\equiv 0
and
\sigma\in C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
with

\delta>\frac{(1-H)(2-H)}{H(3-H)}.

(5.2)

(b)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the matrix
\sigma(x)
is symmetric and satisfies

y\cdot\sigma(x)y>0,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and
\sigma\in C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
with
\delta
satisfying (
5.2
).

(c)

We have
\sigma\in C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{M}_{d})
with

\delta>\frac{(1-H)(2-H)}{1+H-H^{2}}.

(5.3)

Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
a unique, up to modifications, process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
with the following properties.

•

The process

is
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted and is

-Hölder continuous for every

.

•

The process

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad X_{0}=x.

(5.4)

Furthermore, in that case the process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
is a strong solution, i.e. it is adapted to the natural filtration generated by the Brownian motion
W
appearing in the Mandelbrot–van Ness representation (

).

Remark 5.4
.

Since the seminal work
[
8

of Catellier and Gubinelli, many works have appeared to establish

for an irregular drift

and a fractional Brownian motion

.
In contrast, there are much fewer works that attempt to optimize the regularity of the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
.
The work
[
20
]
by Hinz, Tölle and Viitasaari, where
b\equiv 0
, considers certain
existence and uniqueness for

that is merely of bounded variation,
at the cost of additional restrictive assumptions
(variability and
[
20

It seems that Theorem
5.2
is the first result
to improve the regularity of
\sigma
without any additional assumption (except
\sigma
being invertible
for the case (b)).
However, we believe that our assumption of
\delta
is not optimal, see Remark
5.9
.

The proof of Proposition
5.1
suggests that the pathwise uniqueness holds if,
for any two
(\mathcal{F}_{t})

and

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\sigma(\theta X^{(1)}_{s}+(1-\theta)X^{(2)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}.

(5.5)

If

is replaced by

,
then the integral is constructed in Proposition
3.3
.
The difficulty here is that

is not Gaussian and the Wiener chaos decomposition crucially used in

cannot be applied.
Yet, the process

is

B
(whose precise meaning will be clarified later), and by taking advantage of this fact, we can

As a technical ingredient, we need a variant of Theorem

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(A_{s,t})_{0\leq s<t\leq T}

be a family of two-parameter random variables and let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a filtration such that
A_{s,t}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for every
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
.
Suppose that for some
m\geq 2
,
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2,}\Gamma_{3}\in[0,\infty)
and
\alpha,\gamma,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in[0,\infty)
, we
have for every
0\leq v<s<u<t\leq T

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\|_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{1}|s-v|^{-\alpha}|t-s|^{\beta_{1}}+\Gamma_{2}|t-v|^{\gamma}|t-s|^{\beta_{2}},\quad\text{if }t-s\leq s-v,

\displaystyle\|\delta A_{s,u,t}\|_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{3}|t-s|^{\beta_{3}}.

Suppose that

\min\{\beta_{1},2\beta_{3}\}>1,\quad\gamma+\beta_{2}>1,\quad 1+\alpha-\beta_{1}<\alpha\min\Big\{\frac{\gamma+\beta_{2}-1}{\gamma},2\beta_{3}-1\Big\}.

(5.6)

Finally, suppose that there exists a stochastic process
(\mathcal{A}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{t}=\lim_{\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0;\text{$\pi$ is a partition of $[0,t]$}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}A_{u,v},

where the convergence is in
L_{m}(\mathbb{P})
.
Then, we have

\|\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}-A_{s,t}\|_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\Gamma_{1}|t-s|^{\beta_{1}-\alpha}+\Gamma_{2}|t-s|^{\gamma+\beta_{2}}+\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{3}|t-s|^{\beta_{3}}.

Remark 5.6
.

It should be possible to formulate Lemma
5.5
at the generality of Theorem
1.1
.
However, such generality is irrelevant to prove Theorem
5.2
,

Proof.

Here we consider dyadic partitions. Fix

Since
\mathcal{A}_{s,t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}A^{n}_{s,t}
, it suffices to show

\lVert A^{n}_{s,t}-A^{n+1}_{s,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim 2^{-n\delta}(\Gamma_{1}|t-s|^{\beta_{1}-\alpha}+\Gamma_{2}|t-s|^{\gamma+\beta_{2}}+\kappa_{m,d}\Gamma_{3}|t-s|^{\beta_{3}})

for some

and all sufficiently large

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we decompose

\displaystyle A^{n}_{s,t}-A^{n+1}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{j:l+jL\leq 2^{n}-1}\{\delta A^{n}_{l+jL}-\mathbb{E}[\delta A^{n}_{l+jL}|\mathcal{F}_{l+(j-1)L+1}^{n}]\}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{j:l+jL\leq 2^{n}-1}\mathbb{E}[\delta A^{n}_{l+jL}|\mathcal{F}_{l+(j-1)L+1}^{n}].

By the BDG inequality,

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

We first prove the pathwise uniqueness. We suppose that
the assumption (b) mentioned in Theorem
5.2
holds,
and the other cases will be discussed later.
Let
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
be
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted solutions.
Our strategy is similar to Proposition
5.1
, but here we must construct the integral (

) stochastically.
For each

, we set

\lambda_{k}\vcentcolon=\inf_{x:\lvert x\rvert\leq k}\inf_{y:\lvert y\rvert=1}y\cdot\sigma(x)y,

we let
\sigma^{k}\in C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{M}_{d})
be such that

\sigma^{k}=\sigma
in
\{x\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lvert x\rvert\leq k\}
and

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf_{y:\lvert y\rvert=1}y\cdot\sigma^{k}(x)y\geq\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2},

and we set

X^{(i),k}_{t}\vcentcolon=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(X^{(i)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{k}(X^{(i)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},\quad i=1,2.

If we write

\Omega_{k}\vcentcolon=\{\omega\in\Omega\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\max\{\lvert X^{(1)}_{t}(\omega)\rvert,\lvert X^{(2)}_{t}(\omega)\rvert\}\leq k\},

then in the event
\Omega_{k}
we have
X^{(i)}_{t}=X^{(i),k}_{t}
,

.

Let
\{\sigma^{k,n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a smooth approximation of
\sigma^{k}
.
In general we can only guarantee the convergence in
C^{1+\delta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})
for any
\delta^{\prime}<\delta
, which is still sufficient to make the following argument work. To simplify the notation we assume that we can take
\delta^{\prime}=\delta
.

We have

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{k}(X^{(i)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{k,n}(X^{(i)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}

and in
\Omega_{k}

\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma^{k,n}(X^{(1)}_{r})-\sigma^{k,n}(X^{(2)}_{r})\}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}\{X^{(1),k}_{r}-X^{(2),k}_{r}\}\,\mathrm{d}V^{k,n}_{r},

where

V^{k,n}_{t}\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\sigma^{k,n}(\theta X^{(1),k}_{r}+(1-\theta)X^{(2),k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

For a fixed
\theta\in(0,1)
, we set

Z_{t}^{\theta,k}\vcentcolon=\theta X_{t}^{(1),k}+(1-\theta)X_{t}^{(2),k}.

By the a priori estimate (
5.9
), for
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},H)
, we have

Z_{t}^{\theta,k}-Z_{s}^{\theta,k}=\{\theta\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})+(1-\theta)\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})\}B_{s,t}\\
+\{\theta b(X_{s}^{(1),k})+(1-\theta)b(X_{s}^{(1),k})\}(t-s)+R_{s,t}

with

\lvert R_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim_{T,\alpha}(1+\lvert x\rvert+\lVert b\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}\lVert B\rVert_{C^{\alpha}})^{3}\lvert t-s\rvert^{2\alpha}.

Note that we have

\inf_{y:\lvert y\rvert=1}y\cdot\{\theta\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})+(1-\theta)\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})\}y\geq\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}

and hence

\lvert\{\theta\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})+(1-\theta)\sigma^{k}(X_{s}^{(1),k})\}^{-1}\rvert\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

Therefore, we have
Z^{\theta,k}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\alpha,2\alpha)
with

\lVert Z^{\theta,k}\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\alpha,2\alpha)}\lesssim_{T,\alpha}(1+\lvert x\rvert+\lVert b\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}\lVert B\rVert_{C^{\alpha}})^{3}+(1+\lambda_{k}^{-1})(\lVert b\rVert_{L_{\infty}}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L_{\infty}}).

Since

\max\Big\{\frac{1-H}{2\alpha},\frac{2-3H+H^{2}}{2\alpha+H-H^{2}},\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{2H}-1\Big\}=\frac{2-3H+H^{2}}{2\alpha+H-H^{2}}<\delta

if
\alpha
is sufficiently close to
H
,
by Proposition
5.8
,

\lVert V^{k,n_{1}}_{s,t}-V^{k,n_{2}}_{s,t}\rVert_{L_{m}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{T,\alpha,\delta,b,\sigma,k,m}\lVert\sigma^{k,n_{1}}-\sigma^{k,n_{2}}\rVert_{C^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{M}_{d})}\lvert t-s\rvert^{H}.

By Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, the sequence
(V^{k,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some
V^{k}
in
C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Therefore, we conclude that almost surely in
\Omega_{k}
, the path
z=X^{(1)}-X^{(2)}
solves the linear Young equation

z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}z_{r}\,\mathrm{d}U_{r}^{k},\quad U_{t}^{k}\vcentcolon=V_{t}^{k}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla b(\theta X^{(1),k}_{r}+(1-\theta)X^{(2),k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}r,

and hence
X^{(1)}=X^{(2)}
. Since
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{k})\to 1
, we conclude
X^{(1)}=X^{(2)}
almost surely.
Thus, we completed the proof of the uniqueness under (b). The other cases can be handled similarly.
Indeed, under (a) we have
X^{(i)}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\alpha,2\alpha)
and under (c) we have
X^{(i)}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\alpha,1)
.

Now, it remains to prove the existence of a strong solution. However, in view of the Yamada-Watanabe theorem (Proposition
B.2
),
it suffices to show the existence of a weak solution, which will be proved in Lemma
B.3
based on a standard compactness argument.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

We believe that our assumption in Theorem
5.2
is not optimal.
One possible approach to relax the assumption is to consider a higher order approximation in (
5.7
).
Yet, we believe that this will not lead to an optimal assumption, as long as we apply Lemma
5.5
.
Thus, finding an optimal regularity of
\sigma
for the pathwise uniqueness and the strong existence remains an interesting open question
that is likely to require a new idea.

Suppose that a weak solution to (
B.1
) exists and that the pathwise uniqueness holds for (
B.1
).
Then, there exists a strong solution to (
B.1
).

Proof.

We would like to apply
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
. For this purpose, we need a setup. We follow the notation in
[
23
]
.
We fix
\beta>0
that is less than but sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
.
As before we set
K_{H}(t,r)\vcentcolon=(t-r)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-r)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}
and we set
S_{1}\vcentcolon=C^{\alpha}([0,T])
and define

S_{2}
as a subspace of

\{w\in C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R})\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lim_{r\to-\infty}\lvert w_{r}\rvert(-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}=0,\,\,\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\lvert w(r)\rvert(-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty\}

that is Polish and the Brownian motion lives in
S_{2}
.
We note that for
w\in S_{2}
, the improper integral

\int_{-\infty}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}w_{r}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{-M}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}w_{r}

is well-defined.
For
t\in[0,T]
, we denote by
(\mathcal{B}^{S_{1}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(\mathcal{B}^{S_{2}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the filtration generated by
the coordinate maps in
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
respectively. We set

\mathcal{C}\vcentcolon=\{(\mathcal{B}^{S_{1}}_{t},\mathcal{B}^{S_{2}}_{t})\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}t\in[0,T]\}

as our compatibility structure in the sense of
[
23
, Definition 3.4]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma,\mathcal{C},W}
the set of probability measures
\mu
on
S_{1}\times S_{2}
such that

•

we have

\mu(\{(x,y)\in S_{1}\times S_{2}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}x_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(x_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{r})\,\mathrm{d}Iy_{r}\,\,\text{for all }t\in[0,T]\})=1,

where
(Iy)_{t}\vcentcolon=\int_{-\infty}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}y_{r}
;

•

\mu
is
\mathcal{C}
-compatible in the sense of
[
23
, Definition 3.6]
;

•

\mu(S_{1}\times\cdot)
has the law of the Brownian motion.

By
[
23
, Lemma 3.8]
,
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma,\mathcal{C},W}
is convex. In view of
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
,
the existence of weak solutions implies
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma,\mathcal{C},W}\neq\varnothing
.

Therefore, to apply
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
, it remains to prove the pointwise uniqueness in the sense of
[
23
, Definition 3.12]
.
Suppose that
(X_{1},X_{2},W)
are defined on a common probability space, that the laws of
(X_{1},Y)
and
(X_{2},Y)
belong to
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma,\mathcal{C},W}

and that
(X_{1},X_{2})
are jointly compatible with
W
in the sense of
[
23
, Definition 3.12]
.
But then, if we denote by
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
the filtration generated by
(X_{1},X_{2},W)
,
by
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
, the joint compatibility implies that
W
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion,
and therefore the pathwise uniqueness implies
X_{1}=X_{2}
almost surely.

Hence, by
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
, there exists a measurable map
F:S_{2}\to S_{1}
such that for a Brownian motion
W
,
the law of
(F(W),W)
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma,\mathcal{C},W}
. Then,
[
23
, Lemma 3.11]
implies that
F(W)
is a strong solution.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

Let
b\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\sigma\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, there exists a weak solution to (
B.1
).

Proof.

Let
(\sigma^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a smooth approximation to
\sigma
and let
X^{n}
be the solution to

X^{n}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(X^{n}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{n}(X^{n}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

Let
W
be the Brownian motion such that
B_{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}
.
Let
\varepsilon
be greater than but sufficiently close to
0
and let
S
be
a subspace of

\{w\in C^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lim_{r\to-\infty}\lvert w_{r}\rvert(-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}=0,\,\,\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\lvert w(r)\rvert(-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty\}

that is Polish and where the Brownian motion lives.
By the a priori estimate (
5.8
), we see that a sequence of the laws of
(X^{n},W)
is tight in
C^{H-\varepsilon}([0,T])\times S
.
Thus, replacing it with a subsequence, we suppose that the sequence
(X^{n},W)
converge to some limit
(\tilde{X},\tilde{W})
in law.

To see that
(\tilde{X},\tilde{W})
solves (
5.4
),
we write
Iw_{t}\vcentcolon=\int_{-\infty}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}w_{r}
and for
\delta>0
we set

A_{\delta}\vcentcolon=\{(y,w)\in C^{H-\varepsilon}([0,T])\times S\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert y_{t}-x-\int_{0}^{t}b(y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}(Iw)_{r}\rvert>\delta\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}((\tilde{X},\tilde{W})\in A_{\delta})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}((X^{n},W)\in A_{\delta})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma-\sigma^{n}\}(X^{n}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rvert>\delta).

However, by the estimate of Young’s integral,

\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma-\sigma^{n}\}(X^{n}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rvert\lesssim_{H,\varepsilon}\lVert\sigma-\sigma^{n}\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}\lVert B\rVert_{C^{1}_{b}}.

Thus, combined with the a priori estimate (
5.8
), we observe

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma-\sigma^{n}\}(X^{n}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rvert>\delta)=0

and hence
\mathbb{P}((\tilde{X},\tilde{W})\in A_{\delta})=0
.
Since
\delta
is arbitrary, this implies

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{X}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(\tilde{X}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\tilde{X}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}(I\tilde{W})_{r}\,\,\forall t\in[0,T])=1.

Finally, since
W_{t}-W_{s}
is independent of the
\sigma
-algebra generated by

(X^{n}_{r})_{r\leq s}
and
(W_{r})_{r\leq s}
, we know that
\tilde{W}_{t}-\tilde{W}_{s}
is independent of

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\vcentcolon=\sigma(\tilde{X}_{r},\tilde{W}_{r}|r\leq s),

or equivalently
\tilde{W}
is
(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t})
-Brownian motion.
∎

Forcing constellations of Cichoń’s diagram by using the Tukey order
Miguel A. Cardona, Diego A. Mejía

Abstract
We use known finite support iteration techniques to present various examples of models where several cardinal characteristics of Cichoń’s diagram are pairwise different. We show some simple examples forcing the left-hand side of Cichoń’s diagram, and present the technique of restriction to models to force Cichoń’s maximum (original from Goldstern, Kellner, Shelah and the second author). We focus on how the values forced in all the constellations are obtained via the Tukey order.



Introduction

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of subsets of
X
such that
\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}

\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{I})=\min\{|A|:\,A\subseteq X,\,A\notin\mathcal{I}\}

Cofinality of

\mbox{\rm cof}(\mathcal{I})=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|:\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\ \forall\,A\in\mathcal{I}\ \exists\,B\in\mathcal{J}\colon A\subseteq B\}
.

For

The relationship between these cardinals is best illustrated by

In the context of this diagram, a natural question arises:

Is it consistent that all the cardinals in

It turns out that the answer to this question is positive and was proved by Goldstern, Kellner and Shelah
[
GKS19
]
, who used four strongly compact cardinals to obtain the consistency of Cichoń’s diagram divided into 10 different values, situation known as
Cichoń’s maximum
. In this same direction. This was improved by Brendle and the authors
[
BCM21
]
who used only three strongly compact cardinals; finally, Goldstern, Kellner, Shelah and the second author
[
GKMS21
]
proved that no large cardinals are needed for the consistency of Cichoń’s maximum.

The previously cited work occurs in the context of finite support (FS) iterations of ccc posets. In fact, when calculating the values of the cardinals in Cichoń’s diagram in generic extensions, Tukey connections appear implicitly. This appears a bit more explicitly in

GKMS21
]
with the notions of

To complement this last part, this work summarizes some of the techniques required to force Cichoń’s maximum, but making the role of the Tukey order very explicit. This allows to reformulate all technical results and main theorems in a very beautiful and concise way.

1
Relational systems and cardinal characteristics

Many cardinal characteristics of the continuum and their relations can be represented by relational systems as follows. This presentation is based on
[
Voj93
,
Bar10
,
Bla10
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
is a
relational system
if it consists of two non-empty sets
X
and
Y
and a relation
\sqsubset
.

(1)

A set
F\subseteq X
is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded
if
\exists\,y\in Y\ \forall\,x\in F\colon x\sqsubset y
.

(2)

A set
E\subseteq Y
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating
if
\forall\,x\in X\ \exists\,y\in E\colon x\sqsubset y
.

We associate two cardinal characteristics with this relational system
\mathbf{R}
:

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|F|:\,F\subseteq X\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-unbounded}\}
the
unbounding number of
\mathbf{R}
, and

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|D|:\,D\subseteq Y\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-dominating}\}
the
dominating number of
\mathbf{R}
.

A very representative general example of relational systems is given by directed preorders.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
\langle S,\leq_{S}\rangle
is a
directed preorder
if it is a preorder (i.e.
\leq_{S}
is a reflexive and transitive relation on
S
) such that

\forall\,x,y\in S\ \exists\,z\in S\colon x\leq_{S}z\text{ and }y\leq_{S}z.

A directed preorder
\langle S,\leq_{S}\rangle
is seen as the relational system
S=\langle S,S,\leq_{S}\rangle
, and their associated cardinal characteristics are denoted by
\mathfrak{b}(S)
and
\mathfrak{d}(S)
. The cardinal
\mathfrak{d}(S)
is actually the
cofinality of
S
, typically denoted by
\mbox{\rm cof}(S)
or
\mbox{\rm cf}(S)
.

Fact 1.3
.

If a directed preorder
S
has no maximum element then
\mathfrak{b}(S)
is infinite and regular, and
\mathfrak{b}(S)\leq\mbox{\rm cf}(\mathfrak{d}(S))\leq\mathfrak{d}(S)\leq|S|
. Even more, if
L
is a linear order without maximum then
\mathfrak{b}(L)=\mathfrak{d}(L)=\mbox{\rm cof}(L)
.

Inequalities between cardinal characteristics associated with relational systems can be determined by the dual of a relational system and also via Tukey connections, which we introduce below.

Definition 1.7
.

If
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
is a relational system, then its
dual relational system
is defined by
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}:=\langle Y,X,\sqsubset^{\perp}\rangle
where
y\sqsubset^{\perp}x
if
\neg(x\sqsubset y)
.

Fact 1.8
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
be a relational system.

(a)

(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathbf{R}
.

(b)

The notions of
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
-dominating set and
\mathbf{R}
-unbounded set are equivalent.

(c)

The notions of
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
-unbounded set and
\mathbf{R}
-dominating set are equivalent.

(d)

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})
.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\sqsubset^{\prime}\rangle
be relational systems. We say that
(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
is a
Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
if

\Psi_{-}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{+}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
are functions such that

\forall\,x\in X\ \forall\,y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\colon\Psi_{1}(x)\sqsubset^{\prime}y^{\prime}\Rightarrow x\sqsubset\Psi_{2}(y^{\prime}).

The
Tukey order
between relational systems is defined by

\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
iff there is a Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
.
Tukey equivalence
is defined by
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
iff
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}

Fact 1.10
.

Assume that
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\sqsubset^{\prime}\rangle
are relational systems and that
(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
is a Tukey connection.

(a)

If
D^{\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
is
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
-dominating, then
\Psi_{+}[D^{\prime}]
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating.

(b)

(\Psi_{+},\Psi_{-})\colon(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})^{\perp}\to\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
is a Tukey connection.

(c)

If
E\subseteq X
is
\mathbf{R}
-unbounded then
\Psi_{-}[E]
is
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
-unbounded.

Corollary 1.11
.

(a)

\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})^{\perp}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
.

(b)

\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
.

(c)

\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
.

Lemma 1.15

If
\theta

\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}[X]^{<\theta}

The relation
\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}

\mathbf{C}_{[Z]^{<\theta}}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}

Motivated by the previous explanation, we look at characterizations of the Tukey order between
\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}

Lemma 1.16
.

Let
\theta
be an infinite cardinal,
I
a set of size
\geq\theta
and let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
be a relational system. Then:

(a)

If
|X|\geq\theta
, then
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
iff
\forall\,A\in[X]^{<\theta}\ \exists\,y_{A}\in Y\ \forall\,x\in A\colon x\sqsubset y_{A}
, i.e. any subset of
X
of size
{<}\theta
is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded.

In this case, when
\theta
is regular,
\theta\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})\leq|X|
.

(b)

\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\theta}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
iff
\exists\,\langle x_{i}:\,i\in I\rangle\subseteq X\,\forall\,y\in Y\colon|\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset y\}|<\theta
.

In this case, when
\theta
is regular,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})\leq\theta
and
|I|\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})
.

Proof.

(a):
The implication from right to left is immediate by using the maps
x\mapsto x
(identity on
X
) and
A\mapsto y_{A}
as a Tukey connection. For the converse, assume
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
, i.e., there is a Tukey connection
(F,G)\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
. For
A\in[X]^{<\theta}
,
y_{A}:=G(F[A])
is as desired.
The latter part uses
1.6
.

(b): The implication from right to left follows by using the maps
i\mapsto x_{i}
and
y\mapsto\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset y\}
. To see the converse, assume that
(F,G)\colon\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\theta}}\to\mathbf{R}
is a Tukey connection. For
i\in I
, let

, so
\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset y\}\subseteq G(y)

|G(y)|<\theta
.

.

We say that
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle

2
Forcing and Tukey connections

We present general results illustrating the effect of FS iterations of ccc posets on the cardinal characteristics associated with a definable relational system of the reals. More concretely, if
\mathbf{R}
is such a relational system, we show how to force statements of the form
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\theta}}
and
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\theta}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
.

We start by looking at special types of generic reals.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
be a relational system and let
M
be a set (commonly a model).

(1)

Say that
y\in Y
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating over
M
if
\forall\,x\in X\cap M\colon x\sqsubset y
.

(2)

Say that
x
is
\mathbf{R}
-unbounded over
M
if it is
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
-dominating over
M
, that is,
\forall\,y\in Y\cap M\colon\neg(x\sqsubset y)
.

Example 2.2
.

The following are examples of very typical Suslin ccc forcing notions and the type of dominating (or unbounded) reals they add over the ground model. For precise definitions, see e.g.
[
BJ95
]
.

(1)

Cohen reals are precisely the
\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{M}}
-unbounded reals, which are precisely the
\mathbf{Mg}
-unbounded reals in the context of
2^{\omega}
. We denote Cohen forcing by
\mathds{C}
.

(2)

Random reals are precisely the
\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
-unbounded reals, which are precisely the
\mathbf{Cn}^{\perp}
-dominating reals. We denote random forcing by
\mathds{B}
.

(3)

The eventually different real forcing
\mathds{E}
adds a
\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{M}}
-dominating real in
\omega^{\omega}
, which can be transformed into a
\mathbf{Mg}
-dominating real (in
2^{\omega}
).

(4)

Hechler forcing
\mathds{D}
adds an
\omega^{\omega}
-dominating real (usually called
dominating real)
.

(5)

\mathds{LOC}
adds an
\mathbf{Lc}^{*}
-dominating real, which also adds an
\mathcal{N}
-dominating real.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\nu
be an ordinal. An iteration
\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
has
finite support
(FS for short) if

(i)

\xi<\delta\leq\nu\Rightarrow\mathds{P}_{\xi}\lessdot\mathds{P}_{\delta}
,

(ii)

\mathds{P}_{\xi+1}=\mathds{P}_{\xi}\ast\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}
, and

(iii)

\mathds{P}_{\delta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}\mathds{P}_{\xi}
for all limit
\delta\leq\nu
.

We usually denote
V_{\xi}:=V^{\mathds{P}_{\xi}}
for all
\xi\leq\nu
.

It is important to have a reasonably good picture of a FS iteration before plunging into technical facts, see
Figure 4
.

\nu
.

Remark 2.4
.

For limit
\delta\leq\nu
,
V_{\delta}\neq\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}V_{\xi}
in general.

Below, we state some well-known facts (most of them without proofs) for FS iterations of forcing notions.
The following lemma states that, in FS
iterations of certain forcing notions (e.g. ccc forcing notions), no new reals are added at limit stages of uncountable cofinality, a result which will be used often in forthcoming results.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\theta
be a regular uncountable cardinal. If
\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
is a FS iteration of
\theta
-cc posets, i.e.
\Vdash_{\mathds{P}_{\xi}}\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}
is
\theta
-cc for all
\xi<\nu
, then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
is
\theta
-cc.

If, in addition,
\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)\geq\theta
, then
\omega^{\omega}\cap V_{\nu}=\omega^{\omega}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\nu}V_{\xi}
.

FS iterations add Cohen reals, which is sometimes considered as a limitation of the method.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
\mathds{P}_{\nu}=\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
is a FS iteration of non-trivial posets.
If
\xi<\nu
and
\nu
is limit, then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
adds a Cohen real over
V_{\xi}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\nu
be a limit ordinal of uncountable cofinality and let
\mathds{P}_{\nu}=\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
be a FS iteration of non-trivial
\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)
-posets. Then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\nu\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Mg}
. In particular,
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)\leq\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

Since
L\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\nu
for any cofinal subset
L
of
\nu
, it is enough to show that
L^{*}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{M}}
where
L^{*}
is the set of limit ordinals smaller than
\nu
. For each
i\in L^{*}
let
c_{i}\in V_{i+\omega}
be a Cohen real over
V_{i}
(which exists by
2.6
). Then the Tukey connection is given by the maps
i\mapsto c_{i}
and
B\mapsto j_{B}
, the latter defined by: whenever
B
is a Borel meager set of reals,
j_{B}\in L^{*}
is chosen such that
B
is coded in
V_{j_{B}}
(which exists by
2.5
).
∎

One starting point to force a statement of the form
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\theta}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
is the following result.

Fact 2.8
.

Let
\mathds{C}_{I}
be the poset that adds Cohen reals indexed by
I
. If
I
is uncountable then
\mathds{C}_{I}
forces
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\aleph_{1}}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Mg}
.

Proof.

Apply
1.16
(b) to the sequence
\langle c_{i}:\,i\in I\rangle
of Cohen reals added by
\mathds{C}_{I}
.
∎

The following results illustrates the effect of adding cofinally many
\mathbf{R}
-dominating reals along a FS iteration.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathbf{R}
be a definable relational system of the reals, and let
\nu
be a limit ordinal of uncountable cofinality.
If
\mathds{P}_{\nu}=\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
is a FS iteration of
\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)
-cc posets that adds
\mathbf{R}
-dominating reals cofinally often, then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\nu
.

In addition, if
\mathbf{R}
is a Prs and all iterands are non-trivial, then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Mg}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\nu
.
In particular,
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})=\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the set of
\xi<\nu
such that
\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}
add an
\mathbf{R}
-dominating real over
V_{\xi}
. Since
L
is cofinal in
\nu
,
L\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\nu
. We show that, in
V_{\nu}
,
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}L
. Consider the maps
F\colon X^{V_{\nu}}\to L
such that
x\in V_{F(x)}
, and
G\colon L\to Y^{V_{\nu}}
such that
G(\xi)
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating over
V_{\xi}
. Clearly,
(F,G)
is a Tukey connection.

The second part follows from
1.20
and
2.7
.
∎

In most of the cases, we force a Tukey connection between a definable relational system of the reals and some relational system
R
fixed in the ground model. To calculate the cardinal characteristics in the extension, we need to know when
\mathfrak{b}(R)
and
\mathfrak{d}(R)
stay the same in generic extensions.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\theta>\aleph_{0}
be a regular cardinal and let
R=\langle A,B,\sqsubset\rangle
be a relational system.

(a)

If
V\models\mathfrak{d}(R)\geq\theta
then, in any
\theta
-cc generic extension of
V
,
\mathfrak{d}(R)=\mathfrak{d}(R)^{V}
.

(b)

If
V\models\mathfrak{b}(R)\geq\theta
then, in any
\theta
-cc generic extension of
V
,
\mathfrak{b}(R)=\mathfrak{b}(R)^{V}
.

Here,
R
is considered as the same object in both
V
and in the generic extension (not an interpretation).

Proof.

We show (a) (note that (b) follows by (a) applied to
R^{\perp}
).
In
V
, assume that
\lambda:=\mathfrak{d}(R)\geq\theta
and that
D\subseteq B
is an
R
-dominating family of size
\lambda
. Let
W
be a
\theta
-cc generic extension of
V
. In
W
, it is clear that
D
is
R
-dominating, so
\mathfrak{d}(R)^{W}\leq\lambda
. Now assume, in
W
, that
E\subseteq B
has size
{<}\lambda
. Since
W
is a
\theta
-cc generic extension of
V
and
\lambda\geq\theta
, we can find
E^{\prime}\in V
of size
{<}\lambda
such that
E\subseteq E^{\prime}\subseteq B
. In
V
,
|E^{\prime}|<\lambda=\mathfrak{d}(R)
, so
E^{\prime}
is not
R
-dominating, hence there is some
x\in X
which is
R
-unbounded over
E^{\prime}
. It is clear that, in
W
,
x
is
R
-unbounded over
E
. This concludes that
\mathfrak{d}(R)^{W}\geq\lambda
.
∎

In our applications,
R
will be a directed set like
[X]^{<\theta}\cap V
, or a relational system of the form
\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}\cap V}
. In terms of Tukey equivalence, this will be the same as looking at
[X]^{<\theta}
and
\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
, respectively, in the generic extension.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\theta>\aleph_{0}
be a regular cardinal and assume
|X|\geq\theta
. Then, in any
\theta
-cc generic extension,
[X]^{<\theta}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}[X]^{<\theta}\cap V
and
\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}\cap V}
. Moreover,
\mbox{\rm add}([X]^{<\theta})=\mbox{\rm add}([X]^{<\theta})^{V}
, likewise for the other cardinal characteristics associated with the ideal
[X]^{<\theta}
.

Proof.

This follows because, in any
\theta
-cc generic extension of
V
, any member of
A\in[X]^{<\theta}
is contained in some member of
[X]^{<\theta}\cap V
. The “moreover” is a consequence of
2.10
applied to
[X]^{<\theta}\cap V
and
\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}\cap V}
.
∎

The following result is a general criteria to force statements of the form
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
be a definable relational system of the reals,
\theta
an uncountable regular cardinal, and let
\mathds{P}_{\nu}=\langle\mathds{P}_{\xi},\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\xi}:\,\xi<\nu\rangle
be a FS iteration of
\theta
-cc posets with
\mbox{\rm cf}(\nu)\geq\theta
. Assume that, for all
\xi<\nu
and any
A\in[X]^{<\theta}\cap V_{\xi}
, there is some
\eta\geq\xi
such that
\dot{\mathds{Q}}_{\eta}
adds an
\mathbf{R}
-dominating real over
A
. Then
\mathds{P}_{\nu}
forces
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}\cap V}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}[X]^{<\theta}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}[X]^{<\theta}
, in particular,
\theta\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})\leq|X|=\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

In
V_{\nu}
: for
A\in[X]^{<\theta}
we have that
A\in V_{\xi}
for some
\xi<\nu
, so it is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded by the hypothesis. Hence
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{[X]^{<\theta}}
by
1.16
. The rest follows by
1.12
and
2.11
.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

In connection with the theorem above,
[
GKS19
]
defines the following property:

\mathrm{COB}(\mathbf{R},\mathds{P},\lambda,\vartheta)

states that there are a directed preorder
S
of size
\nu
and with
\mathfrak{b}(S)\geq\lambda
, and a sequence
\langle\dot{y}_{s}:\,s\in S\rangle
of
\mathds{P}
-names of members of
Y
such that,
for every
\mathds{P}
-name
\dot{x}
of a member of
X
, there exists an
s_{\dot{x}}\in S
such that, for all
t\geq_{S}s_{\dot{x}}
,
\Vdash\dot{x}\sqsubset\dot{y}_{t}
.

This property implies that there exist a directed preorder
S
in the ground model such that

\lambda\leq\mathfrak{b}(S)
,
\mathfrak{d}(S)\leq\vartheta
and
\Vdash_{\mathds{P}}\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}S
. Even more, equivalence holds when
\mathds{P}
is
\lambda
-cc and
\lambda
is uncountable regular.

We conclude this section with general results to force statements of the form
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\mu}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
. For this purpose, we restrict to Polish relational systems and use Judah’s and Shelah’s
[
JS90
]
and Brendle’s
[
Bre91
]
preservation theory.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
be a Prs and let
\theta
be an infinite cardinal.
A poset
\mathds{P}
is
\theta
-
\mathbf{R}
-good
if, for any
\mathds{P}
-name
\dot{h}
for a member of
Y
, there is a non-empty set
H\subseteq Y
(in the ground model) of size
{<}\theta
such that, for any
x\in X
, if
x
is
\mathbf{R}
-unbounded over
H
then
\Vdash x\nsqsubset\dot{h}
.

We say that
\mathds{P}
is
\mathbf{R}
-good
if it is
\aleph_{1}
-
\mathbf{R}
-good.

Good posets allow us to preserve the Tukey order as follows.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\theta
be regular uncountable, and let
\mathbf{R}
be a Prs. Assume that
\mathds{P}
is a
\theta
-cc
\theta
-
\mathbf{R}
-good poset. If
\mu
is a cardinal,
\mbox{\rm cf}(\mu)\geq\theta
,
|I|\geq\mu
and
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\mu}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
, then
\mathds{P}
forces that
\mathbf{C}_{[I]^{<\mu}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
.

Proof.

Choose a sequence
\langle x_{i}:\,i\in I\rangle
as in
1.16
(b). We show that
\mathds{P}
forces
|\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset y\}|<\mu
for all
y\in Y
. Let
\dot{y}
be a
\mathds{P}
-name of a member of
Y
and choose
H
as in
2.14
. Let
B:=\bigcup_{y^{\prime}\in H}\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset y^{\prime}\}
, so
|B|<\mu
. Since
\mathds{P}
forces
x_{i}\sqsubset\dot{y}\Rightarrow i\in B
, then
\mathds{P}
forces
|\{i\in I:\,x_{i}\sqsubset\dot{y}\}|<\mu
.
∎

6
Discussion

In our Cichoń’s maximum result we get Tukey connections with products of ordinals, but it is unclear whether we actually have Tukey equivalence.

Question 6.1
.

Can we force Tukey equivalence in
Theorem 5.1
(a)?

Similarly, in
Theorem 3.7
(b), it is unclear whether we can force
\mathbf{R}_{4}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\lambda_{5}\times\lambda_{4}
.

Recall that, in
2.7
, we showed that the method of FS iterations restrict us to constellations of Cichoń’s diagram where
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})
. There are four instances of Cichoń’s maximum under this condition: the one proved in
Theorem 5.1
, the one in
Figure 22
(after applying the same arguments in
Section 5
to the forcing from
Theorem 3.6
), and the two addressed in the following open question.

Figure 22:
Another instance of Cichoń’s maximum proved consistent with
\mathrm{ZFC}
. Here
\mu_{i}<\mu_{j}
whenever
i<j
.

Question 6.2
.

When
\mu_{i}<\mu_{j}
for
i<j
, are the constellations of
Figure 23
consistent with
\mathrm{ZFC}
?

Figure 23:
Instances of Cichoń’s maximum in the context of
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})
that have not been proved consistent with
\mathrm{ZFC}
.

On the other hand, no instance of Cichoń’s maximum with
\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})
has been proved consistent so far.

We finish with some remarks about forcing singular values in Cichoń’s diagram. In the models presented in this paper only
\mathfrak{c}
can be singular, but there are some models with two singular values
[
Mej19
]
. There are also some instances of Cichoń’s maximum with two singular values, but their consistency use large cardinals
[
GKMS22
]
. The latter reference also presents interesting constellations in the random model.

Recently, Goldstern, Kellner, Shelah and the second author proved, using large cardinals, the consistency of Cichoń’s maximum with the five cardinals on the right side possibly singular. Concretely, with the notation of
Theorem 5.1
, it is forced
\mathbf{R}_{i}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}[\lambda^{\mathfrak{d}}_{i}]^{<\lambda^{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
by allowing
\lambda^{\mathfrak{d}}_{i}
to be singular. However, it is still unknown how to adapt the methods of
Section 4
and
5
to prove this result (without using large cardinals).

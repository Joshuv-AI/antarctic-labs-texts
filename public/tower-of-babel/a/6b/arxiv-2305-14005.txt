Skyrme crystals with massive pions
Derek Harland, Paul Leask, Martin Speight

Abstract
The crystalline structure of nuclear matter is investigated in the standard Skyrme model with massive pions. A semi-analytic method is developed to determine local minima of the static energy functional with respect to variations of both the field and the period lattice of the crystal. Four distinct Skyrme crystals are found. Two of these were already known – the cubic lattice of half-skyrmions and the α \alpha -particle crystal – but two are new. These new solutions have lower energy per baryon number and less symmetry, being periodic with respect to trigonal but not cubic period lattices. Minimal energy crystals are also constructed under the constraint of constant baryon density, and its shown that the two new non-cubic crystals tend to chain and multi-wall solutions at low densities.



1
Introduction

The Skyrme model
[
1
]
is a nonlinear field theory of
\pi
mesons that accommodates nucleons as topological solitons. It emerges as a low energy effective theory of QCD in the regime where the number of colours (or equivalently, the rank of the gauge group) grows large
[
2
,
3
]
.
The model has only one field, taking values in the Lie group
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
. Field configurations are classified topologically by an integer-valued homotopy
invariant
B
which is interpreted physically as the baryon number of the configuration. There is a topological lower bound on static energy of the form

E\geq E_{\textrm{top}}B
, where
E_{\textrm{top}}
is some positive constant, originally due to
Faddeev
[
4
]
and subsequently improved by one of us
[
5
]
. (Improved in this context means that the constant
E_{\textrm{top}}
is increased.) Let
E(B)
denote the minimum static energy among all fields of baryon number
B
. The
energy bound
E(B)=E_{\textrm{top}}B
is never attained, but numerical studies suggest that the ratio

E(B)/B
decreases monotonically, and hence converges to some limit
E_{*}
as
B\rightarrow\infty
. This suggests that, as
B
grows large, minimal energy Skyrme fields may tend to some regular, spatially periodic crystalline structure, with baryon number
B
and energy
E_{*}B
per unit cell.

Such a crystal structure was first proposed by Klebanov
[
6
]
.
He found a crystal of
B=1
skyrmions arranged in a simple cubic (SC) lattice so that every unit skyrmion is internally oriented to be in the attractive channel with respect to its nearest neighbours.
Manton snd Goldhaber
[
7
]
later found that at high densities this crystal undergoes a phase transition to a body centred cubic (BCC) lattice of half-skyrmions with a lower energy per baryon (
E/B
) than Klebanov’s SC crystal.
Then, independently, Kugler and Shtrikman
[
8
]
and Castillejo
et al.

[
9
]
determined a new solution with
lower
E/B
, wherein skyrmions are initially arranged in a face centred cubic (FCC) lattice and relax to a SC lattice of half-skyrmions.
In all of these studies, the model has massless pions,
m_{\pi}=0
, the unit cell has baryon number
B=4
, and the energy functional has been varied only over
cubic period lattices, that is, only the side length of the cube is varied.

The phase structure of the massless pion Skyrme model has been studied by Jackson and Verbaarschot
[
10
]
. Perapechka and Shnir
[
11
]
investigated phase transitions in the Skyrme model with
m_{\pi}>0
. (They also considered the effect of incorporating a non-standard sextic term in the energy functional. Such terms are of current interest because they arise in so-called “near BPS” variants of the model
[
12
]
,
but will not be of central relevance to our considerations.)
Two candidates have been previously proposed as the minimal
E/B
crystal with massive pions: the cubic lattice of half-skyrmions
[
8
,
9
]
and the
\alpha
-particle lattice
[
13
]
. Once again, these studies impose periodicity with respect to a cubic period lattice, and vary only the side length of the cube.

In this paper, we study Skyrme crystals in the model with
m_{\pi}>0
, minimizing the energy with respect to variations of
both
the Skyrme field
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)

and
its period lattice
\Lambda
. To achieve this, we identify every 3-torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
with the fixed
3-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}=S^{1}\times S^{1}\times S^{1}={\mathbb{R}}^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
by means of the obvious
diffeomorphism
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
, and equip
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
with the pullback of the Euclidean metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
,
g=f^{*}g_{\textrm{Euc}}
. Varying over all
period lattices
\Lambda
is then equivalent to varying over all flat metrics

g
on the fixed torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. This approach was introduced in

[
14
]
, in which the interpretation of the gradient of the energy with respect to the metric
g
as the stress tensor of the field
was repeatedly exploited. In the current paper, we will find it convenient to think of the metric variational problem more concretely, by identifying
g
with the constant
symmetric positive definite matrix
(g_{ij})
representing it with respect to the canonical coordinate system on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
.

So, the numerical task we set ourselves is, for fixed topological degree
B
, to minimize
E(\varphi,g)
among all degree
B
maps
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
and flat metrics
g
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. It is known that, for fixed
g
, the function

\varphi\mapsto E(\varphi,g)
attains a minimum (in a function space of rather low
regularity)
[
15
]
. The complementary problem of minimising in
g
for fixed
\varphi
was first studied in
[
14
]
and numerically implemented in
[
16
,
17
]
.
In
[
14
]
it was shown that in a two-dimensional toy model (the baby Skyrme model), any critical metric
g
is automatically a local minimimum of
E
. The problem of extending this result to the Skyrme model was discussed, but unfortunately the proof used in two dimensions did not generalise. Existence of critical metrics was not addressed in
[
14
]
.

In the present paper we obtain a much stronger result. We show in Corollary
4
that, for fixed
\varphi
satisfying very mild assumptions, there is a unique
flat metric with respect to which
E(\varphi,g)
is minimal, and hence a unique period lattice
\Lambda
(up to automorphism) with respect to which

\varphi
has minimal energy per unit cell. In the special case
m_{\pi}=0
, we can even write down this metric explicitly. In the
(more interesting) massive case, we can resort to a gradient based
numerical minimization scheme to find
g
. Applying a similar scheme to minimize over
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
in tandem, we can find the energetically optimal field and period lattice for a given
B
, without ever imposing any symmetry assumptions on the lattice.

The results reveal that, for
m_{\pi}>0
, the energetically optimal lattice (with
B=4
per unit cell) does
not
have cubic symmetry. In fact there are two
crystal solutions with trigonal period lattices (orthorhombic with
side lengths
L_{1}=L_{2}\neq L_{3}
) which have lower energy than the lowest
strictly cubic lattice. This fact persists if, instead of minimizing over all flat
g
, we minimize only over the subset of metrics with fixed total volume. This is equivalent to minimizing under the constraint of fixed average baryon density, a problem of phenomenological interest
[
18
]
. As might be expected, the energy difference between the trigonal and cubic lattices becomes negligible
as baryon density grows very large, but is significant at lower densities.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In section
2
, we formulate the model mathematically, considering in detail how its energy functional depends on the metric on physical space.
In section
3
, we prove existence and uniqueness of an energy minimizing metric
g
for any given fixed field. In section
4
we describe our numerical scheme in detail, while section
5
presents the results of this scheme. In section
6
we determine minimal energy crystals under the constraint of fixed baryon density. Finally, section
7
presents some concluding remarks.

2
The Skyrme model

We wish to study the Skyrme model under the assumption that the Skyrme field

\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
is periodic with respect to some
3
-dimensional lattice

\Lambda=\{n_{1}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{1}+n_{2}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{2}+n_{3}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{3}:\mbox{\boldmath{$n$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\},

(1)

that is,

\varphi({\bf x}+\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}})=\varphi({\bf x})
for all
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}\in\Lambda
, where
\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{3}
is an oriented basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,. In this case, we may equally well interpret the field as a map
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
, where the torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
inherits a metric
g_{\Lambda}
from the Euclidean metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. It is convenient to identify
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
with
the standard torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}={\mathbb{R}}^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
via the diffeomorphism

f_{\Lambda}:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda,\qquad(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mapsto x_{1}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{1}+x_{2}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{2}+x_{3}\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{3},

(2)

and denote by
g
the pullback of the metric
g_{\Lambda}
by
f_{\Lambda}
. Explicitly,

g=g_{ij}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{j},\qquad g_{ij}=\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$X$}}_{j}.

(3)

Note that the matrix
(g_{ij})
is a symmetric, positive definite real
3\times 3
matrix. We denote the set of such matrices
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
and note that every such matrix arises as the metric on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
corresponding to some lattice
\Lambda
and lattices producing the same matrix are related by an oriented isometry of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Hence, instead of considering the Skyrme model on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}/\Lambda
for all lattices
\Lambda
, we may restrict to the standard torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
but consider all flat metrics on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
,
g=g_{ij}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{j}
,
(g_{ij})\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
.
It is convenient to abuse notation slightly and denote the matrix
(g_{ij})
by
g
.

The energy of a Skyrme field
\varphi:({\mathbb{T}}^{3},g)\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
is
defined in stages as follows. Denote by
\mu
the left Maurer-Cartan form on
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
, that is, the
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
-valued one-form that maps a tangent vector
X\in T_{U}{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
to the vector
\mu(X)\in T_{{\mathbb{I}}_{2}}{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
whose image under left translation by
U
is
X
. The pullback of
\mu
by
\varphi
,

\varphi^{*}\mu=\varphi^{-1}\mathrm{d}\varphi=:L_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i},

(4)

is usually called the
left current
. We equip
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
with the usual
Ad
invariant inner product
(X,Y)_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}=-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(XY)
and define the Dirichlet energy of
\varphi
to be

E_{2}(\varphi,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|\varphi^{*}\mu|_{g}^{2}{\rm vol}_{g}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}g^{ij}\Tr(L_{i}L_{j})\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0}

(5)

where
g^{ij}
are the components of the inverse matrix
g^{-1}
,

|g|=\det g
and
{\rm vol}_{0}=\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{3}
is the canonical volume form on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. We are following the standard convention that a numerical subscript on an energy functional denotes the degree of its integrand considered as a polynomial in spatial derivatives. It is important to note that we regard
E_{2}
as a function of both
\varphi
and
g
.

To define the Skyrme term
E_{4}(\varphi,g)
, we introduce the
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
-valued two-form

\Omega
on
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
by
\Omega(X,Y)=[\mu(X),\mu(Y)]
. Then

E_{4}(\varphi,g)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|\varphi^{*}\Omega|_{g}^{2}{\rm vol}_{g}=-\frac{1}{16}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}g^{ik}g^{jl}\Tr([L_{i},L_{j}][L_{k},L_{l}])\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0}.

(6)

We will also include a potential term

E_{0}(\varphi,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}V(\varphi){\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}V(\varphi)\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0}

(7)

where
V:{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is some smooth function. The usual choice is

V(U)=m_{\pi}^{2}\Tr({\mathbb{I}}_{2}-U),

(8)

which has the effect of giving the pions of the theory (small amplitude waves about the vacuum
\varphi={\mathbb{I}}_{2}
) mass
m_{\pi}
.

In summary, the Skyrme energy of a field
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow SU(2)
and metric

g\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
is

E(\varphi,g)=E_{2}(\varphi,g)+E_{4}(\varphi,g)+E_{0}(\varphi,g).

(9)

Since
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
is diffeomorphic to
S^{3}
the homotopy class of the field
\varphi
is, by the Hopf degree theorem, labelled by its topological degree
B\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

B=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\varphi^{*}{\rm vol}_{{\mathrm{SU}}(2)},

(10)

where
{\rm vol}_{{\mathrm{SU}}(2)}
is the volume form on
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
defined by the bi-invariant metric
h
which, at
U={\mathbb{I}}_{2}
coincides with
(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}
(equivalently, the round metric of radius
1
on
S^{3}
). The mathematical problem
that this paper addresses is to minimize
E(\varphi,g)
, with respect to both

\varphi
and
g
, among all fields of fixed degree
B
and all metrics
g\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
.

3
Existence and uniqueness of minimizing metrics

For fixed
g\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
,
({\mathbb{T}}^{3},g)
is a fixed, compact oriented Riemannian
3
-manifold, and it follows from a direct application of the calculus of variations that the
functional
\varphi\mapsto E(\varphi,g)
attains a minimum in each degree class in the space of
finite energy maps in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3},{\mathrm{SU}}(2))

[
15
]
.
In this section we address the complementary variational problem: we fix a map

\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
and establish existence, and uniqueness, of a minimizer of the function
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
,
g\mapsto E(\varphi,g)
which, for brevity, we will denote
E(g)
.

It is convenient (and makes the result more comprehensive) to include the possibility of a sextic term in the Skyrme energy,

E_{6}(\varphi,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|\varphi^{*}\Xi|^{2}{\rm vol}_{g}

(11)

where
\Xi
is some
3
-form on
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
, for example, a constant multiple of

{\rm vol}_{{\mathrm{SU}}(2)}
. Terms of this kind are of phenomenological interest since they arise in so-called near BPS variants of the Skyrme model
[
12
]
. To specialize to the model of primary interest, we simply choose
\Xi=0
.

We begin by analyzing in more detail the
g
dependence of the terms in
E
. We first note that

E_{2}(\varphi,g)=g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(L_{i},L_{j})_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}{\rm vol}_{0}=\sqrt{\det g}\Tr(Hg^{-1})

(12)

where

H_{ij}(\varphi)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(L_{i},L_{j})_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}{\rm vol}_{0}

(13)

is a symmetric positive semi-definite matrix depending on
\varphi
but independent of
g
. Let us assume that
\varphi
is
C^{1}
(so that this matrix is well-defined) and is immersive somewhere, meaning that there is some point
p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
at which
\mathrm{d}\varphi_{p}
is invertible. Note that this follows immediately for all maps with
B\neq 0
.
By continuous differentiability, it follows that
\varphi
is immersive on some neighbourhood of
p
. Then the matrix
H
is actually positive definite, for if not, there exists

{\bf v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
{\bf v}\cdot H{\bf v}=0
, whence

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|{\bf v}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$L$}}|_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}^{2}{\rm vol}_{0}=0

(14)

and hence
\mathrm{d}\varphi(v_{i}\partial/\partial x_{i})=0
almost everywhere. This contradicts immersivity of
\varphi
on a neighbourhood of
p
. We conclude that
H\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
.

To understand
E_{4}(g)
we appeal to an isomorphism peculiar to
3
dimensions. Having chosen a
3
-form
{\rm vol}_{0}=\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{3}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
, there is an isomorphism
(T_{p}{\mathbb{T}}^{3})\otimes{\mathfrak{su}}(2)\rightarrow(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}_{p}{\mathbb{T}}^{3})\otimes{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
defined by
X\mapsto\iota_{X}{\rm vol}_{0}
. Denote by
X_{\varphi}
the section of
T{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\otimes{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
whose image under this isomorphism is
\varphi^{*}\Omega
,

\varphi^{*}\Omega=\iota_{X_{\varphi}}{\rm vol}_{0},

(15)

and note that
X_{\varphi}
depends on

\varphi
, but is independent of
g
. We may similarly define a
g
-dependent section
X_{\varphi}^{g}
of
T{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\otimes{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
by using the isomorphism between
2
-forms and tangent vectors defined by
{\rm vol}_{g}
instead of
{\rm vol}_{0}
:

\varphi^{*}\Omega=\iota_{X_{\varphi}^{g}}{\rm vol}_{g}=\iota_{\sqrt{|g|}X_{\varphi}^{g}}{\rm vol}_{0}.

(16)

Clearly
X_{\varphi}^{g}=X_{\varphi}/\sqrt{|g|}
. An alternative interpretation of
X_{\varphi}^{g}
is that it is the vector field metrically dual to the Hodge dual of
\varphi^{*}\Omega
with respect to the metric
g
, that is,

*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\musicd_{g}X_{\varphi},

(17)

where
*_{g}:\Lambda^{2}T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
denotes the Hodge isomorphism and
\musicd_{g}:T{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
the metric isomorphism defined by
g
. Hence

E_{4}(\varphi,g)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|X_{\varphi}/\sqrt{|g|}|_{g}^{2}{\rm vol}_{g}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|g|}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}g(X_{\varphi},X_{\varphi}){\rm vol}_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\Tr(Fg)

(18)

where

F_{ij}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(X_{\varphi,i},X_{\varphi,j})_{{\mathfrak{su}}(2)}{\rm vol}_{0}

(19)

is another symmetric positive semi-definite matrix depending on
\varphi
but independent of
g
, and
X_{\varphi}=:X_{\varphi,i}\partial/\partial x_{i}
. In terms of the left currents

X_{\varphi,i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}[L_{k},L_{l}],

(20)

and so the matrix
F
takes the explicit form

F_{ij}(\varphi)=-\frac{1}{32}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jmn}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\Tr([L_{k},L_{l}][L_{m},L_{n}]){\rm vol}_{0}.

(21)

Once again, our non-degeneracy assumption on
\varphi
(that it is
C^{1}
and somewhere immersive) implies that
F
is positive definite. For if not, there exists
{\bf v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

{\bf v}\cdot F{\bf v}=0
, whence
{\bf v}\cdot X_{\varphi}=0
and so

*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Omega(v_{i}\partial/\partial x_{i})=0
. But then
\varphi^{*}\Omega

vanishes on every plane in
T{\mathbb{T}}^{3}

g
-orthogonal to
{\bf v}
, which contradicts nondegeneracy of
\Omega
and immersivity of
\varphi
.

The remaining terms of
E
are more straightforward.

E_{0}(\varphi,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}V(\varphi){\rm vol}_{g}=C_{0}\sqrt{\det g},

(22)

where

C_{0}(\varphi)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}V(\varphi){\rm vol}_{0}\geq 0

(23)

is a constant. Finally, we note that
\varphi^{*}\Xi=f_{\Xi}{\rm vol}_{0}
for some real function
f_{\Xi}:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
independent of
g
. Then

f_{\Xi}{\rm vol}_{0}=(*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Xi){\rm vol}_{g}=(*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Xi)\sqrt{\det g}{\rm vol}_{0},

(24)

so
*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Xi=f_{\Xi}/\sqrt{\det g}
. Hence

E_{6}(\varphi,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\varphi^{*}\Xi\wedge*_{g}\varphi^{*}\Xi=\frac{C_{6}}{\sqrt{\det g}}

(25)

where

C_{6}(\varphi)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}f_{\Xi}^{2}{\rm vol}_{0}\geq 0

(26)

is a constant. Note that we allow the possibility that
C_{0}
or
C_{6}
is
0
, to accommodate versions of the model with no potential or sextic term.

In summary, for a fixed
C^{1}
map
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
which is immersive somewhere, the total Skyrme energy as a function of the metric
g
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
is

E(g):=E(\left.\varphi\right|_{\textrm{fixed}},g)=\sqrt{\det g}\Tr(Hg^{-1})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\Tr(Fg)+C_{0}\sqrt{\det g}+\frac{C_{6}}{\sqrt{\det g}},

(27)

where
H,F\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
and
C_{0},C_{6}\in[0,\infty)
are constants.
We wish to prove that the function
E:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
attains a unique global minimum, and has no other critical points. Before doing so, we note that

E=\widetilde{E}\circ\sigma
where

\widetilde{E}:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\widetilde{E}(\Sigma)=\Tr(H\Sigma^{-1})+\Tr(F\Sigma)+\frac{C_{0}}{\det\Sigma}+{C_{6}}{{\det\Sigma}}

(28)

and
\sigma
is the map

\sigma:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3},\qquad g\mapsto\Sigma=\frac{g}{\sqrt{\det g}}.

(29)

Since
\sigma
is a diffeomorphism, we may equivalently prove that
\widetilde{E}:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
attains a unique global minimum and has no other critical points. We do this in two stages.

Proposition 1

The function
\widetilde{E}:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
of equation (
28
) attains a global
minimum.

Proof.

Clearly
\widetilde{E}
is bounded below (by
0
). Let
E_{*}=\inf\widetilde{E}
. We must show that there exists
\Sigma_{*}\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
with
\widetilde{E}(\Sigma_{*})=E_{*}
.

Consider the map

f:(0,\infty)^{3}\times O(3)\rightarrow{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3},\qquad f({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}},\mathscr{O})=\mathscr{O}D_{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}\mathscr{O}^{T},\quad D_{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}:=\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}).

(30)

This map is surjective: given any
\Sigma\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
we may take
\lambda_{i}
to be its eigenvalues and
\mathscr{O}
to be an orthogonal matrix whose columns are its corresponding eigenvectors. Hence, it suffices to prove that

(\widetilde{E}\circ f)({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}},\mathscr{O})=\Tr(\mathscr{O}^{T}H\mathscr{O}D_{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}^{-1})+\Tr(\mathscr{O}^{T}F\mathscr{O}D_{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}})+\frac{C_{0}}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}}+C_{6}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}

(31)

attains the value
E_{*}
.

Let
({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})
be a sequence in
(0,\infty)^{3}\times O(3)
such that

(\widetilde{E}\circ f)({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})\rightarrow E_{*}.

(32)

Such a sequence exists since
f
is surjective. Consider the six functions

(\mathscr{O}^{T}H\mathscr{O})_{ii},(\mathscr{O}^{T}F\mathscr{O})_{ii}:O(3)\rightarrow(0,\infty),\qquad i=1,2,3,

(33)

mapping
\mathscr{O}
to the diagonal entries of
\mathscr{O}^{T}H\mathscr{O},\mathscr{O}^{T}F\mathscr{O}\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
, and note that these functions are strictly positive since
H,F
are positive definite. Since these functions are smooth and
O(3)
is compact, there exists
\alpha>0
such that, for all
\mathscr{O}\in O(3)
,
(\mathscr{O}^{T}H\mathscr{O})_{ii},(\mathscr{O}^{T}F\mathscr{O})_{ii}\geq\alpha
. Hence, for all
({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}},\mathscr{O})\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times O(3)
,

(\widetilde{E}\circ f)({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}},\mathscr{O})\geq\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\right).

(34)

We may assume that
(\widetilde{E}\circ f)({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})\leq E_{*}+1
for all
n
, so
{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n}\in[K^{-1},K]^{3}
for all
n
, where
K=E_{*}/\alpha
. Hence, the sequence
({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})
takes values in a compact subset of
(0,\infty)^{3}\times O(3)

say, which, without loss of generality, we may assume is

itself. So

(\widetilde{E}\circ f)({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})\rightarrow E_{*}

({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{n},\mathscr{O}_{n})\rightarrow({\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{*},\mathscr{O}_{*})
. But
(\widetilde{E}\circ f)
is continuous, so
(\widetilde{E}\circ f)(\lambda_{*},\mathscr{O}_{*})=E_{*}
.

It follows that
\widetilde{E}(\Sigma_{*})=E_{*}
where

\Sigma_{*}=\mathscr{O}_{*}D_{{\mbox{\boldmath{$\lambda$}}}_{*}}\mathscr{O}^{T}_{*},

(35)

which completes the proof.
∎

We note in passing that the minimizing

(36)

It remains to prove that

Definition 2

A function

on a Riemannian manifold

\gamma(t)
in

(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0

(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(t)>0

To apply this definition to

[

.
It is invariant under the

(38)

Finally, it is complete and between any pair of distinct points

The function

Proof.

Given a constant

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\>}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}(\det A)^{2}\det\exp(t\xi)=(\det A)^{2}\frac{d^{2}\>}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}\exp(t\Tr\xi)

has a second critical point

∎

In the case of the standard Skyrme model without a potential (

\displaystyle=

4
The numerical method

We now return to the problem of primary interest: to minimize
E(\varphi,g)
among all smooth maps
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
of fixed degree
B
, and all flat metrics
g\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
. Our numerical
scheme is similar to ones introduced in
[
16
,
17
]
and based on the idea of arrested Newton flow. For fixed
\varphi
, we interpret
E
as a potential energy on the manifold
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
and solve Newton’s law of motion

\ddot{g}=-\grad_{g}E(\varphi,g)

(50)

with initial data
g(0)=g_{0}
. This solution begins to run “downhill” in
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
. We monitor
E(\varphi,g(t))
and, at any time
t_{*}
where
\frac{d}{dt}{E}(\varphi,g(t))>0
we arrest the flow, that is, stop and restart it at the current position but with velocity
0
. The flow converges to the unique minimising metric
g_{\varphi}
. We minimise over
\varphi
by a similar technique, solving

\ddot{\varphi}=-\grad_{\varphi}E(\varphi,g_{\varphi})

(51)

with initial data
\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0}
(here
\grad_{\varphi}E
is the derivative in the first argument,
\varphi
, with
g
treated as constant). Again, we arrest the flow if
E
starts to increase.

In practice, we discretize space, placing
\varphi
on a cubic grid of

N^{3}
points with lattice spacing
h=1/N
and periodic boundary conditions. We replace the spatial derivatives of
\varphi
occurring in
E
by finite difference approximations. This reduces the (
51
) to a system of ODEs in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4N^{3}}
, which we then solve using a 4th order Runge-Kutta scheme with fixed time step
\delta t
. The arresting criterion is that

E(t+\delta t)>E(t)
. After each iteration of the Runge-Kutta scheme, the metric
g_{\varphi}
is recalculated in a similar way by solving the ODE (
50
), using the metric
g_{\varphi}
from the previous iteration as initial datum. Once the flow for
g
has converged, the next iteration of
\varphi
is calculated, and so on. Each flow is deemed to have converged to a static solution if the sup norm of
\grad E
falls below some tolerance. The numerical results presented hereafter were obtained with
N=201
. The time steps
\delta
used were 0.0017 for the flow in
\varphi
and 0.1 for the flow in
g
. The tolerances were
10^{-5}
for the flow in
\varphi
and
10^{-7}
for the flow in
g
.

Implementing the method also entails choosing (formal) Riemannian metrics on
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
and
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{3},SU(2))
. These are required to make sense of both
\grad E
and the connexions

\nabla
occurring implicitly in (
50
) and (
51
). On
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
we choose the Euclidean metric induced by identifying
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
as a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{9}
in the obvious way, that is

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})_{{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}}=\Tr(\xi_{1}^{T}\xi_{2}).

(52)

Note that this differs from the metric
G
used in section
3
; it is simpler for the current purpose.
On
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{3},SU(2))
we choose the
L^{2}
metric defined by the volume form
{\rm vol}_{0}
,

\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}h(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}){\rm vol}_{0},

(53)

which is independent of
g
.

For the purposes of numerics, it is convenient to use the sigma model formulation of the model, that is, exploit the isometry between

({\mathrm{SU}}(2),h)
and
S^{3}
with its round metric of unit radius. So, we identify

{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\ni\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi_{0}+i\varphi_{3}&i\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\\
i\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}&\varphi_{0}-i\varphi_{3}\end{array}\right)\leftrightarrow(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3})\in S^{3}

(54)

and interpret the Skyrme field as a unit length vector in Euclidean
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. The terms occurring in the Skyrme energy are easily converted to this formulation,

\displaystyle E_{2}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}g^{ij}(\partial_{i}\varphi\cdot\partial_{j}\varphi)\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0}

(55)

\displaystyle E_{4}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(g^{ij}g^{kl}-g^{il}g^{jk})(\partial_{i}\varphi\cdot\partial_{j}\varphi)(\partial_{k}\varphi\cdot\partial_{l}\varphi)\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0}

(56)

\displaystyle E_{0}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2m_{\pi}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(1-\varphi_{0})\sqrt{|g|}{\rm vol}_{0},

(57)

as are the matrices defined in section
3
,

\displaystyle H_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\partial_{i}\varphi\cdot\partial_{j}\varphi){\rm vol}_{0}

(58)

\displaystyle F_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{ikl}\varepsilon_{jmn}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\left\{(\partial_{k}\varphi\cdot\partial_{m}\varphi)(\partial_{l}\varphi\cdot\partial_{n}\varphi)-(\partial_{k}\varphi\cdot\partial_{n}\varphi)(\partial_{l}\varphi\cdot\partial_{m}\varphi)\right\}{\rm vol}_{0}.

(59)

Now, the gradient
E=E_{2}+E_{4}+E_{0}

(\grad_{\varphi}E)(\varphi,g)_{C^{\infty}}=\sqrt{|g|}P_{\varphi}\left(-2g^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi-2(g^{li}g^{jk}-g^{ij}g^{kl})\partial_{i}(\partial_{j}\varphi\cdot\partial_{k}\varphi\,\partial_{l}\varphi)-2m_{\pi}^{2}{\bf e}_{0}\right)

(60)

where
{\bf e}_{0}=(1,0,0,0)
and

\varphi

P_{\varphi}({\bf v})={\bf v}-(\varphi\cdot{\bf v})\varphi.

(61)

The gradient of
E
regarded as a function on
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
is easily deduced from (
28
) and the diffeomorphism
\sigma:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
. We note that

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{E}_{\Sigma}(\xi)=\Tr((F-\Sigma^{-1}H\Sigma^{-1}-\frac{C_{0}\Sigma^{-1}}{\det\Sigma})\xi),

(62)

and so

(\grad\widetilde{E})(\Sigma)=F-\Sigma^{-1}H\Sigma^{-1}-\frac{C_{0}\Sigma^{-1}}{\det\Sigma}.

(63)

Furthermore

\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\left(\xi-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(g^{-1}\xi)g\right),

(64)

whence

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}E_{g}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\widetilde{E}_{\sigma(g)}\circ\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}(\xi)

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\left\{\grad\widetilde{E}(|g|^{-1/2}g)|g|^{-1/2}(\xi-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(g^{-1}\xi)g)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\left\{|g|^{-1/2}\left[\grad\widetilde{E}(|g|^{-1/2}g)-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(g\grad\widetilde{E}(|g|^{-1/2}g))g^{-1}\right]\xi\right\},

and so

(\grad_{g}E)(\varphi,g)_{{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}}=|g|^{-1/2}\left[\grad\widetilde{E}(|g|^{-1/2}g)-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(g\grad\widetilde{E}(|g|^{-1/2}g))g^{-1}\right].

(66)

Skyrme crystal solutions

. Our approach is to treat the pion mass as a continuous variable parameter

We begin by reviewing the lowest energy solution known in the massless case,

\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
obtained by cyclic permutation, where

to either the upper (

(b)

It is important to note that

lying in a

-crystal

lie in a curve

E_{(t)}^{p}(\varphi,g)=E_{2}(\varphi,g)+E_{4}(\varphi,g)+t\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(1-p\cdot\varphi){\rm vol}_{g}.

(69)

(We recover the original function

To answer this question, we will need to understand the symmetries of the

Denote by

the subgroup of
{\mathrm{SO}}(3)
consisting of matrices that map the integer lattice

to itself. This is a finite group of order
24
, the rotational symmetries of the cube. The manifold
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
is itself an abelian Lie group whose group operation is translation modulo
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
. The semidirect product
\Aut({\mathbb{T}}^{3})=O\ltimes{\mathbb{T}}^{3}

(A_{1},{\bf v}_{1})\bullet(A_{2},{\bf v}_{2})=(A_{1}A_{2},A_{1}{\bf v}_{2}+{\bf v}_{1})

(70)

acts on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
by

. This induces a right action of
\Aut({\mathbb{T}}^{3})
on

by

or, more explicitly,

(A,{\bf v})\cdot(\varphi({\bf x}),g)=(\varphi(A({\bf x}+{\bf v})),A^{T}gA).

(71)

Having completed these preliminaries, we observe that the
energy of the massless Skyrme model
E_{(0)}:\mathscr{M}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is invariant under the left action of
G={\mathrm{SO}}(4)\times\Aut({\mathbb{T}}^{3})

(R,S)\cdot(\varphi,g)=(R\circ\varphi\circ S^{-1},(S^{-1})^{*}g).

(72)

The

(\varphi_{1/2},g_{1/2})

\begin{array}[]{ll}R(\varphi)=(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3},\varphi_{1}),&S({\bf x})=(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}),\\

(73)

The image of the natural projection

Once we turn on the perturbation, the symmetry group of the energy function

By the Principle of Symmetric criticality, a point

For generic choices of

to be trivial, so that

, and this observation confers no advantage. The interesting case is when the intersection of

(meaning that the Hessian of

applied to

. That is, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a (unique)

such that
\gamma(0)=(\varphi_{1/2},g_{1/2})

\mathrm{d}E_{(t)}^{p}|_{\gamma(t)}=0

.

To summarize, we expect

to smoothly deform into a critical point of

(as

) if

in

only at
(\varphi_{1/2},g_{1/2})
. Let us call this condition the

6
Skyrme crystals at prescribed average baryon density

If we are to use Skyrme crystals as a model of dense nuclear matter (for example, in astrophysical contexts) it is important to understand the properties of the lowest energy configuration among all those with a fixed average baryon
density
, treating this density as a parameter of our system.
This problem was first approached by Hen and Karliner
[
23
]
in the context of the baby Skyrme model.
Therein they extremized the baby Skyrme energy functional with respect to variations of the period lattice at a constant skyrmion density.
This method was carried out at various densities, producing an energy-density curve.
However, they did not address the nature of the critical points they obtained, stating that they could in fact turn out to be maxima or saddle points.
Our method is similar but it is more general and robust.

Let us fix
B
, the baryon number per unit cell. Then the average baryon density of a configuration

(\varphi,g)
is

\rho_{B}=\frac{B}{\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}{\rm vol}_{g}}=\frac{B}{\sqrt{\det g}}.

(87)

Hence, finding the minimal crystal with fixed baryon density (and baryon number
B
per unit cell) amounts to minimizing
E:\mathscr{M}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
over a level set of
\det g
. Once again, we can address the partial minimization problem where we fix the field
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow SU(2)
(assumed to be
C^{1}
and somewhere immersive) and
a density
\rho_{B}=B/\nu
then seek a minimum of
E(\varphi,\cdot):\det^{-1}(\nu^{2})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. It turns out that, like the unconstrained minimization problem studied in section
3
, this problem has a unique global minimum and no other critical points.

Proposition 5

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
be a fixed
C^{1}
map that is immersive somewhere and
\nu>0
be a constant. Then the function
{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\supset\det^{-1}(\nu^{2})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
mapping each flat metric
g
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
of volume
\nu
to the Skyrme energy
E(\varphi,g)
attains a unique global minimum, and has no other critical points.

Proof.

As before, it is equivalent to prove that the associated function

\widetilde{E}:{\det}^{-1}(\nu^{-1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\quad\widetilde{E}=E\circ\sigma^{-1}

(88)

attains a unique global minimum and has no other critical points, where
\sigma:{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}\rightarrow{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
is the diffeomorphism
\sigma(g)=g/\sqrt{\det g}
.
Now

\widetilde{E}(\Sigma)=\Tr(H\Sigma^{-1})+\Tr(F\Sigma)+C_{0}\nu+C_{6}\nu^{-1}

(89)

where
H,F\in{\mathrm{SPD}}_{3}
and
C_{0},C_{6}\in[0,\infty)
are the
\varphi
-dependent constants previously defined. Existence of a global minimum of
\widetilde{E}
follows
mutatis mutandis
from Proposition
1
, since the bound (
34
) still holds irrespective of the extra constraint

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}=\nu^{-1}
(equivalent to
\det\Sigma=\nu^{-1}
). This confines the minimizing sequence to a compact subset of the hypersurface
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}=\nu^{-1}
in
(0,\infty)^{3}\times O(3)
, whence a convergent subsequence can be extracted, whose limit attains the infimum of
\widetilde{E}
by continuity.

It remains to prove uniqueness. Assume towards a contradiction that

\widetilde{E}:{\det}^{-1}(\nu^{-1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
has two distinct critical points

\Sigma_{*}
,
\Sigma_{**}
. Then there exists a geodesic

\gamma(t)=A\exp(\xi t)A^{T}

(90)

in
({\mathrm{SPD}}_{3},G)
with
\gamma(0)=\Sigma_{*}
and
\gamma(1)=\Sigma_{**}
. Now

\det(\gamma(t))=(\det A)^{2}e^{t\Tr\xi}

(91)

and
\det(\gamma(0))=\det(\gamma(1))
, so
\Tr\xi=0
and we conclude that
\det(\gamma(t))
is constant. Hence the geodesic
\gamma
remains on the level set
{\det}^{-1}(\nu^{-1})
. Since
\Sigma_{*}
,
\Sigma_{**}
are critical points of
\widetilde{E}:{\det}^{-1}(\nu^{-1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\gamma
is tangent to the level set for all

(\widetilde{E}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(0)=0=(\widetilde{E}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(1)
, so by Rolle’s Theorem
(\widetilde{E}\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}
vanishes somewhere on
(0,1)
, contradicting the convexity of
\widetilde{E}
(Proposition
3
). Hence no second critical point may exist.
∎

In the course of the proof above we established that all level sets of
\det
are connected totally geodesic submanifolds of
({\mathrm{SPD}}_{3},G)
, and hence the restriction of
\widetilde{E}
to any such level set is strictly convex. Note that, in general, the restriction of a convex function to a submanifold may fail to be convex, so total geodesicity of the level sets is crucial here.

We can again solve the minimization problem for
E:{\det}^{-1}(\nu^{2})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
numerically by arrested Newton flow, but now we must take care to project the gradient of
E
tangent to the level set. Given a curve
g(t)
in
\det^{-1}(\nu^{2})

\frac{d\>}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\det g(t)=\nu^{2}\Tr(g(0)^{-1}\dot{g}(0))=0

so
\dot{g}(0)
is orthogonal to
g(0)^{-1}
with respect to the Euclidean metric
\langle X,Y\rangle=\Tr(X^{T}Y)
. Hence
T_{g}\det^{-1}(\nu^{2})=\langle g^{-1}\rangle^{\perp}
.
Now

E(g(t))=\nu\Tr(Hg^{-1})+\frac{\Tr(Fg)}{\nu}+C_{0}\nu+\frac{C_{6}}{\nu},

(93)

and hence

\mathrm{d}E_{g}(v)=\frac{1}{\nu}\langle F-\nu^{2}g^{-1}Hg^{-1},v\rangle.

(94)

It follows that, with respect to the metric on
\det^{-1}(\nu^{2})
induced by the Euclidean metric,

(\grad E)(g)=\frac{1}{\nu}\left\{F-\nu^{2}g^{-1}Hg^{-1}-\frac{\Tr((F-\nu^{2}g^{-1}Hg^{-1})g^{-1})}{\Tr(g^{-1}g^{-1})}g^{-1}\right\}.

(95)

We solve the Newton flow
\ddot{g}=-(\grad E)(g)

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we developed methods to obtain Skyrme crystals in a general class of Skyrme models, and
presented a detailed numerical study of crystals in the standard Skyrme model with massive pions. To achieve this, we minimized the model’s energy with respect to variations of both the field and its period lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
A key idea is to reformulate the latter variation as a variation over all flat metrics on the fixed unit torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. We obtained strong results on the partial minimization problem in which the field is fixed and only the metric varied: under a mild nondegeneracy assumption on the field, there exists a unique flat metric that globally minimizes the Skyrme energy, and no other critical metrics. This result holds also if we constrain the problem to vary only over metrics of fixed volume, a variant relevant to constructing Skyrme crystals of prescribed average baryon density. Our methods impose no symmetry on the period lattice
a priori
, and hence go beyond previous studies which imposed a cubic unit cell.

We find that the minimal energy crystal (with baryon number
4
per unit cell) has trigonal but not cubic periodicity. At low densities it tends to a double sheet solution. The next lowest energy crystal is also trigonal and not cubic, tending to a chain solution at low densities. Both these crystals are new. Above them in energy are two already known solutions, the
\alpha
-crystal and the
1/2
-crystal. All these crystals, except the most energetic, the
1/2
-crystal, have anisotropic isospin inertia tensors. The existence of four distinct crystals can be understood semi-analytically by means of the Principle of Symmetric Criticality and the Inverse Function Theorem.

The methods detailed in this paper could be applied to the study of isospin asymmetric nuclear matter within the Skyrme model.
The next step would be to investigate neutron crystals by considering the quantum corrections to the energy due to the quantization of the isospin degrees of freedom, and improve on the work done on the massless model by Baskerville
[
21
]
.
In particular, one could determine “nuclear pasta” phases in neutron stars
[
28
]
by considering the quantization of generalized Skyrme crystals in the low density regime.
The chain-crystal we have found could correspond to the so-called “spaghetti” phase, and the sheet-crystal the “nuclear lasagne”.

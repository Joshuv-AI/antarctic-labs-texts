Almost commuting self-adjoint operators and measurements
Huaxin Lin

Abstract
We study the problem when an n n -tuple of self-adjoint operators in an infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space H H with small commutators is close to an n n -tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators on H . H. We give an affirmative answer to the problem when the synthetic-spectrum and the essential synthetic-spectrum are close. In the case that n = 2 , n=2, we show that a pair of almost commuting self-adjoint operators in an infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space is close to a commuting pair of self-adjoint operators if and only if a corresponding Fredholm index vanishes outside of an essential synthetic-spectrum. Examples are also exhibited that, in general, the answer to the problem when n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 is negative even the associated Fredholm index vanishes. This is an attempt to solve a problem proposed by David Mumford related to quantum theory and measurements.

Introduction

In the First International Congress of Basic Science held in 2023, David Mumford
gave the opening plenary lecture on Consciousness, robots and DNA (
[
33
]
).
At the very beginning, he cautioned the audience that there would be no mathematics in his talk.
Nevertheless, by the very end of this magnificent lecture, when he ended his talk
on DNA being a measuring instrument opening a Pandora’s box, he proposed a problem in

C^{*}
-algebras about non-commuting self-adjoint operators to be approximated by commuting ones.

A version of this problem proposed by Mumford may be reformulated below:
Let
\epsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer.
Is there a constant
\delta>0
such that the following statement holds?
If
H
is any separable Hilbert space and

T_{1},T_{2},...,T_{n}
are self-adjoint operators on
H
(with
\|T_{j}\|\leq 1
) such that

\displaystyle\|T_{i}T_{j}-T_{j}T_{i}\|<\delta,\,\,\,1\leq i,j\leq n,

(e 1.1)

there exist self-adjoint operators
S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{n}
on
H

such that

\displaystyle S_{i}S_{j}=S_{j}S_{i}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\|S_{j}-T_{j}\|<\epsilon,\,\,\,1\leq i,j\leq n.

(e 1.2)

In the case that
n=2
and
H
is any finite dimensional Hilbert space (no bound on the dimension)
the exactly the same problem is known
as von-Neumann-Kadison-Halmos problem for almost commuting self-adjoint matrices
(see
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
). In that case, the problem has an affirmative solution

[
20
]
(see also
[
8
]
and
[

]
, and, for a fixed dimension, see
[

]
).
In an infinite dimensional Hilbert space, as observed by Mumford in his new book (p.182),
the answer in general is negative (see also Example 4.6 of
[
8
]
) in general.
The affirmative solution for almost commuting self-adjoint matrices led the further study
in the subject of weakly semi-projectivity in
C^{*}
-algebras. It also has a deep impact on the Elliott
program of classification
of amenable

-algebras (see
[

]

[

]

[

]
and
[

The Mumford’s problem opens a new door to the future applications
of this part of
C^{*}
-algebra  theory in previous unexpected
areas such as DNA and artificial intelligence (
[
34
]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

If

(for some integer

We prove this by induction on the number of convex sets.

Write

Put

Then

Then

\displaystyle C(Y)=\{(f,g):(f,g)\in C(X_{1})\oplus C(Y_{m+1}):f|_{X_{1}\cap Y_{m+1}}=g|_{X_{1}\cap Y_{m+1}}\}

-algebras (see
[
14
]
),

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccc}{\small{K_{0}(C(Y))}}&{\longrightarrow}&K_{0}(C(X_{1}))\oplus K_{0}(C(Y_{m+1}))&{\rightarrow}&K_{0}(C(X_{1}\cap Y_{m+1}))\\
\uparrow&&&&\downarrow\\

In quantum theory, one may view macroscopic observables as some

Let

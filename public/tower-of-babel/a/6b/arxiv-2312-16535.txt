On Quantum States for Angular Position and Angular Momentum of Light


Abstract
In the present paper we construct a properly defined quantum state expressed in terms of elliptic Jacobi theta functions for the self-adjoint observables angular position θ \theta and the corresponding angular momentum operator L = − i d / d θ L=-id/d\theta in units of ℏ = 1 \hbar=1 . The quantum uncertainties Δ ​ θ \Delta\theta and Δ ​ L \Delta L for the state are well-defined and are, e.g., shown to give a lower value of the uncertainty product Δ ​ θ ​ Δ ​ L \Delta\theta\Delta L than the minimal uncertainty states of Ref. [ 1 ] . The mean value ⟨ L ⟩ \langle L\rangle of the state is not required to be an integer. In the case of any half-integer mean value ⟨ L ⟩ \langle L\rangle the state constructed exhibits a remarkable critical behaviour with upper and lower bounds Δ ​ θ < π 2 / 3 − 2 \Delta\theta<\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3-2} and Δ ​ L > 1 / 2 \Delta L>1/2 .

We consider the following periodic extension of the minimal dispersion intelligent pure states as discussed in Ref.

[

\lambda>0

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=N\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\theta-\bar{\theta}+2\pi n)~,

(1)

\displaystyle f(x)=e^{\displaystyle{ix\bar{l}}}e^{\displaystyle{-\lambda x^{2}/2}}~.

(2)

\theta

\psi(\theta)

In passing we notice that a periodic extension of the form as in Eq.(

[

We will comment on these fundamental issues below where we, in particular, exhibit an exponential sensitivity to the difference between rational and real numbers for the observables under consideration.

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2\pi\lambda}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\displaystyle{in(\theta-\bar{\theta})}}e^{-\displaystyle{(n-\bar{l})^{2}/2\lambda}}\,\,,

The explicit representation of

in the form as in Eq.(

\displaystyle(\psi,\psi)\equiv\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta|\psi(\theta)|^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\displaystyle{(n-\bar{l})^{2}/\lambda}}=N^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\vartheta_{3}[-\bar{l}\pi,e^{\displaystyle{-\lambda\pi^{2}}}]=1\,\,.

(9)

The normalization factor
N
is therefore in general only a function of the parameters
\bar{l}
and
\lambda
. With the state
\psi(\theta)
in the representation as in Eq.(
7

p(l)
to find the integer angular momentum

\psi_{l}(\theta)=\exp(il\theta)/\sqrt{2\pi}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p(l)=|(\psi_{l},\psi)|^{2}=|\hskip-2.84526pt\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\psi_{l}^{*}(\theta)\psi(\theta)\,|^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}e^{\displaystyle{-(l-\overline{l})^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,,

(10)

which, of course, can be used to compute various expectation values of the observable
L

The discrete nature of this Gaussian-like distribution is now such that
\lambda
is not directly related to the uncertainties
\Delta\theta

\Delta L
. Below we, e.g., show that

independent of
\bar{\theta}

\lambda

It now follows form Eqs.(

\displaystyle\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\vartheta_{3}[0,e^{\displaystyle{-1/\lambda}}]=1\,\,.

\displaystyle\langle L\rangle=\bar{l}\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,(\Delta L)^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{2}e^{\displaystyle{-n^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,.

In the analysis of the physical properties of the state
\psi(\theta)
we are going to make use of the general form of the uncertainty relation for the observables

\displaystyle(\Delta{\theta})^{2}(\Delta{L}^{2})\geq|((\theta-\langle\theta\rangle)\psi,(L-\langle L\rangle)\psi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}|({\theta}\psi,{L}\psi)-({L}\psi,{\theta}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|({\theta}\psi,{L}\psi)+({L}\psi,{\theta}\psi)-2\langle\theta\rangle\langle L\rangle|^{2}~~~,

(18)

where

[

\Delta{\theta}\Delta{L}\geq|1-2\pi|\psi(\pi)|^{2}|/2
after a partial integration. If we, as used in Fig.

\displaystyle({\theta}\psi,{L}\psi)=-i\frac{N^{2}}{2\lambda}\sum_{m\neq n}\cos[(m-n)\pi])e^{\displaystyle{-\left((m-\overline{l})^{2}+(n-\overline{l})^{2}\right)/2\lambda}}

\bar{l}

\Delta{\theta}\Delta{L}=|1-2\pi|\psi(\pi)|^{2}|/2.

The well-defined quantum state as constructed in the present work therefore provides for an explicit example with observables that exhibit exponential sensitivity to the difference between rational numbers and real numbers in quantum mechanics. It would, of course, be of great interest if the state

according to Eq.(

[

perhaps by extending the experimental

In summary we have studied a well-defined quantum state
\psi(\theta)
which exhibits some remarkable properties. For
\langle L\rangle
arbitrarily close to a half-integer we have, e.g., shown that the state
\psi(\theta)
leads to two orthogonal eigenstate of
L
in the limit as
\Delta\theta
approaches the conventional
\pi/\sqrt{3}
. In the case of an exact half-integer mean value of the angular momentum
L
the uncertainty
\Delta\theta
has, furthermore, an upper precise bound
\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3-2}
which is smaller than conventional bound
\pi/\sqrt{3}
and
\Delta L\geq 1/2
. We have also exhibited an explicit exponential sensitivity to the difference of rational and real numbers in terms of an allowed and well-defined quantum state.

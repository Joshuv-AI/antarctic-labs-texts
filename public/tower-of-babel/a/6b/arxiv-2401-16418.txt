Boolean Logic as an Error feedback mechanism
Louis Leconte

Abstract
The notion of Boolean logic backpropagation was introduced to build neural networks with weights and activations being Boolean numbers. Most of computations can be done with Boolean logic instead of real arithmetic, both during training and inference phases. But the underlying discrete optimization problem is NP-hard, and the Boolean logic has no guarantee. In this work we propose the first convergence analysis, under standard non-convex assumptions.

Introduction

Training machine learning models can often be a very challenging process, requiring significant computational resources and time. The use of DNNs on computing hardware such as mobile and IoT devices is becoming increasingly important. IoT devices often have limitations in terms of memory and computational capacity. Quantization is a potential solution to this problem
(
Courbariaux et al. 2015
,
Chmiel et al. 2021
,
Leconte et al. 2023
)
. And in particular, Binary Neural Networks (BNNs) is a remarkably promising direction because it reduces both memory and inference latency simultaneously
(
Nguyen 2023
)
.

Formaly, BNN training can be formulated as minimising the training loss with binary weights, i.e.,

\min_{w\in\mathbf{Q}}f(w);\,\,f(w)=\mathbb{E}_{(w,y)\sim p_{\operatorname{data}}}[\ell(\mathrm{NN}(x,w),y)],

The quantization constraints in the above program make it an extremely difficult task: the underlying optimization problem is non-convex, non-differentiable, and combinatorial in nature.

To the best of our knowledge, in the quantized neural network literature and in particular BNN, one can only prove the convergence up to an irreducible error floor
Li et al. 2017

Continuous Abstraction of
Nguyen 2023

Boolean optimizer is discrete, proving its convergence directly is a hard problem. The idea is to find a continuous equivalence so that some proof techniques existing from the BNN and quantized neural networks literature can be employed.

In existing frameworks, quantity
\widetilde{\nabla}f(\cdot)
denotes the stochastic gradient computed on a random mini-batch of data. Boolean Logic does not have the notion of gradient, it however has an optimization signal (
q_{i,j}^{l,t}
in
Algorithm

1
) that plays the same role as
\widetilde{\nabla}f(\cdot)
. Therefore, these two notions, i.e., continuous gradient and Boolean optimization signal, can be encompassed into a generalized notion. That is the root to the following continuous relaxation in which
\widetilde{\nabla}f(\cdot)
stands for the optimization signal computed on a random mini-batch of data.

3
Non-convex analysis

In the following, we prove that Boolean logic optimizer
(
Nguyen 2023
)
converges towards a first-order stationary point, as
T
the number of global epochs grows.

3.1
Preliminaries

Our analysis is based on the following standard non-convex assumptions on
f
:

A. 1
.

Uniform Lower Bound: There exists
f_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
s.t.
f(w)\geq f_{*}
,
\forall w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A. 2
.

Smooth Derivatives: The gradient
\nabla f(w)
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous for some
L>0
, i.e.,
\forall w,\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\left\|\nabla f(w)-\nabla f(v)\right\|\leq L\|w-v\|.

A. 3
.

Bounded Variance: The variance of the stochastic gradients is bounded by some
\sigma^{2}>0
, i.e.,
\forall w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\nabla}f(w)]=\nabla f(w)
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\widetilde{\nabla}f(w)\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}.

A. 4
.

Compressor: There exists
\delta<1
s.t.
\forall w,\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|Q_{1}(v,w)-v\|^{2}\leq\delta\|v\|^{2}
.

A. 5
.

Bounded Accumulator: There exists
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
s.t.
\forall t
and
\forall i\in[d]
, we have
|m_{t}|_{i}\leq\eta\kappa
.

A. 6
.

Stochastic Flipping Rule: For all
w\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{0}(w)\,\right|w\right]=w
.

In particular,
5
and
6
enable us to obtain
\mathbb{E}[h_{t}]=0
and to bound the variance of
h_{t}
.
Based on all these assumptions, we prove the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
1
to
6
. Boolean Logic applied to Boolean weights
w
converges at rate:

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}{\left\|\nabla f\left(w_{t}\right)\right\|^{2}}\leq\frac{A^{*}}{T\eta}+B^{*}\eta+C^{*}\eta^{2}+Lr_{d},

(7)

where
A^{*}={2(f(w_{0})-f_{*})}
,
B^{*}=2L\sigma^{2}
,
C^{*}=4L^{2}\sigma^{2}\frac{\delta}{(1-\delta)^{2}}
,
r_{d}=\frac{d\kappa}{2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Our analysis is independent of the quantization function
Q_{0}
. We impose a weak assumption on
Q_{0}
(assumption
6
), which holds for standard quantization methods such as stochastic rounding.

Remark 3.3
.

An important remark is that we only consider parameter quantization in the analysis. Nonetheless, our results remain valid when an unbiased quantization function is used to quantize computed gradients. Indeed, the stochastic gradients remain unbiased under such quantization methods. The only effect of the quantization would be an increased variance in the stochastic gradients.

Remark 3.4
.

Assumptions
1
to
3
are standard. Assumptions
4
to
6
are non-classic but dedicated to Boolean Logic strategy.
4
is equivalent to assuming Boolean Logic optimization presents at least one flip at every iteration
t
.
4
is classic in the literature of compressed SGD
Karimireddy et al. 2019
,
Alistarh et al. 2017
. Moreover,
5
and
6
are not restrictive, but algorithmic choices. For example, rounding (
Q_{0}
function) can be stochastic based on the value of the accumulator
m_{t}
. Similar to STE clipping strategy, the accumulator can be clipped to some pre-defined value
\kappa
before applying the flipping rule to verify
5
.

Remark 3.5
.

Our proof assumes that the step size
\eta
is constant over iterations. But in practice, we gently decrease the value of
\eta
at some time steps. Our proof can be adapted to this setting by defining a gradient accumulator
a_{t}
such that
a_{t+1}=a_{t}+q_{t}
. When
\eta
is constant we recover the accumulation definition and we obtain
m_{t}=\eta a_{t}
. In the proposed algorithm, gradients are computed on binary weight

and accumulated in

. Then, one applies the flipping rule on the quantity
\tilde{w}_{t}=\eta a_{t}
(
\tilde{w}_{t}=m_{t}
when
\eta
is constant), and one (may) reset the accumulator
a_{t}
.

We start by stating a key lemma which shows that the residual errors

Lemma 3.6
.

Under
3
and
4
, the error can be bounded as
\mathbb{E}[\|e_{t}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2\delta(1+\delta)}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

Proof.

We start by using the definition of the error sequence:

\displaystyle\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}=\|Q_{1}(m_{t},w_{t})-m_{t}\|^{2}.

Next we make use of
4
:

\displaystyle\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq\delta\|m_{t}\|^{2}.

We develop the accumulator update:

\displaystyle\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq\delta\|e_{t}+\eta\widetilde{\nabla}f(w_{t})\|^{2}.

We thus have a recurrence relation on the bound of
e_{t}
. Using Young’s inequality, we have that for any
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq\delta(1+\beta)\|e_{t}\|^{2}+\delta(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\eta^{2}\|\widetilde{\nabla}f(w_{t})\|^{2}.

Rolling the recursion over and using
3
we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle\delta(1+\beta)\mathbb{E}[\|e_{t}\|^{2}]+\delta(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\widetilde{\nabla}f(w_{t})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(1+\beta)\mathbb{E}[\|e_{t}\|^{2}]+\delta(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r}^{t}(\delta(1+\beta))^{r}\delta(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(1+\frac{1}{\beta})}{1-\delta(1+\beta)}\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}.

Taking
\beta=\frac{1-\delta}{2\delta}
and plugging it in the above bounds gives:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|e_{t+1}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2\delta(1+\delta)}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}.

∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|h_{t}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i}^{d}1+(w_{t}-Q_{1}(m_{t},w_{t}))^{2}-2Q_{0}(w_{t}-Q_{1}(m_{t},w_{t})(w_{t}-Q_{1}(m_{t},w_{t})]

\displaystyle 1-u_{t}^{2}

Proof.

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left.x_{t+1}\,\right|w_{t}\right]=x_{t}-\eta\nabla f(w_{t}).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[f(x_{t+1})-f(x_{t})]

We now reuse

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[f(x_{t+1})-f(x_{t})]

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\langle\nabla f(x_{t})-\nabla f(w_{t})+\nabla f(w_{t}),\nabla f(w_{t})\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|h_{t}-\eta\widetilde{\nabla}f(w_{t})\|^{2}].

\displaystyle-\eta\langle\nabla f(x_{t})-\nabla f(w_{t})+\nabla f(w_{t}),\nabla f(w_{t})\rangle

Making use again of smoothness and Young’s inequality we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[f(x_{t+1})-f(x_{t})]\leq

\displaystyle-\eta\|\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}-\eta\langle\nabla f(x_{t})-\nabla f(w_{t}),\nabla f(w_{t})\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}(1+\beta)\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|h_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{L}{2}\eta^{2}(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle-\eta\|\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\rho}{2}\|\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2\rho}\|\nabla f(x_{t})-\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}(1+\beta)\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|h_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{L}{2}\eta^{2}(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\eta\|\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta\rho}{2}\|\nabla f(w_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta L^{2}}{2\rho}\underbrace{\|x_{t}-w_{t}\|^{2}}_{\|e_{t}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(x_{t+1})]-\mathbb{E}[f(x_{t})]\leq

4
Conclusion

The bound in
Theorem

3.1
contains 4 terms. The first term is standard for a general non-convex target and expresses how initialization affects convergence. The second and third terms depend on the fluctuation of the minibatch gradients. Another important aspect of the rate determined by
Theorem

3.1
is its dependence on the quantization error. Note that there is an "error bound" of
2Ld\kappa
that remains independent of the number of update iterations. The error bound is the cost of using discrete weights as part of the optimization algorithm. Previous work with quantized models also includes error bounds
(
Li et al. 2017
,
Li and De Sa 2019
)
.

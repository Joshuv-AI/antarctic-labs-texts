Multi- Z ′ Z^{\prime} signatures of spontaneously broken local U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} symmetry
Takaaki Nomura, Kei Yagyu

Abstract
We discuss multi- Z ′ Z^{\prime} signatures coming from decays of Higgs bosons in models with a spontaneously broken U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} symmetry, which can be observed as “lepton jets” or multi-lepton final states depending on the mass range of new bosons. We consider anomaly-free U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} models without introducing new fermions except for right-handed neutrinos, in which the Higgs sector is composed of an isospin doublet and a singlet fields with zero and non-zero U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} charges, respectively. The multi- Z ′ Z^{\prime} signatures can then be obtained via the decays of the discovered (extra) Higgs boson h h ( ϕ \phi ), i.e., h → Z ′ ​ Z ′ h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} , ϕ → Z ′ ​ Z ′ \phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} and/or h → ϕ ​ ϕ → 4 ​ Z ′ h\to\phi\phi\to 4Z^{\prime} as far as kinematically allowed. We give the upper limit on the branching ratios of h h into Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} and 4 ​ Z ′ 4Z^{\prime} from the current experimental data in each model. We also show the deviation in the h ​ h ​ h hhh coupling from the standard model prediction at one-loop level, and find that its amount is typically smaller than 1%.

Introduction

The electroweak (EW) gauge symmetry and its spontaneous breakdown have been confirmed by various experiments to date.
In particular, the discovery of the Higgs boson at LHC has revealed the realization of the spontaneous symmetry breaking via the Higgs mechanism.
Although there is no contradiction between
the structure of
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\to U(1)_{\rm em}
implemented in the Standard Model (SM) and current experimental data, one can regard such a pattern of the symmetry breaking as a part of larger structures.

Models with a new
U(1)
gauge symmetry, denoting
U(1)^{\prime}
, have often been discussed as one of the simplest and well-motivated examples of such an extended EW symmetry. In fact, grand unified theories such as
SO(10)
and
E_{6}
models predict extra
U(1)
gauge symmetries at the EW scale, see e.g., Ref.
[
1
]
and references therein.
In addition, gauging the
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
can be regarded as a natural extension of the EW symmetry because the global
U(1)_{B-L}
accidentally appears in the SM.
In models with the gauged
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry, three right-handed neutrinos are inevitably introduced to maintain the gauge anomaly cancellation, and they make left-handed neutrinos massive via the seesaw mechanism
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
More generally, one can consider the
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry
[
9
,
10
]
whose
charges of SM fields are expressed by two free parameters, and the well-known examples, e.g.,
U(1)_{B-L}
,
U(1)_{D}
(dark photon)
[
11
]
and
U(1)_{R}
(right-handed)
[
12
,
13
]
correspond to its special cases. Furthermore, if we allow a flavor dependence of the charge assignments of
U(1)^{\prime}
, three possibilities, i.e.,
U(1)_{L_{i}-L_{j}}
(

and

being the lepton number of
i
-th and
j
-th flavor, respectively)
[
14
,
15

, appear under the anomaly free condition without introducing any exotic fermions

One can also consider
U(1)_{Q_{i}-Q_{j}}
symmetries as the anomaly free choice, where

and

are the quark number of
i

j
-th flavor, respectively. However, these models predict a diagonal form of the quark Yukawa matrices for the case with one Higgs doublet without charged under
U(1)_{Q_{i}-Q_{j}}
, and they cannot explain the CKM matrix.
. In particular, models with

.
As the other possibilities, one can also consider linear combinations of

Therefore, it is important to comprehensively investigate scenarios with

A common feature of models with

is the appearance of an extra neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
.
We discuss a massive

.
Since we have already confirmed the spontaneous breaking of the EW symmetry via the Higgs mechanism, it is quite natural to consider that the
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry is also spontaneously broken by the Higgs mechanism.

2

Alternatively, one can introduce the
Z^{\prime}
mass via the Stueckelberg mechanism
[
21
,
22
]
without introducing Higgs fields while keeping the gauge invariance.
However, this is nothing but a special case of the Higgs mechanism where a new physical Higgs boson is decoupled from the theory and the so-called Stueckelberg scalar field can be regarded as a Nambu-Goldstone boson associated with the spontaneous
U(1)^{\prime}
breaking.

The simplest way to do that is to introduce a complex scalar field which is singlet under the SM gauge symmetry.
In this class of models, an additional physical neutral Higgs boson
\phi
also appears in addition to
Z^{\prime}
, and their masses are naturally expected to be given in a similar order because they come from the Vacuum Expectation Value (VEV) of the singlet scalar.
Therefore, the phenomenological impacts of two new bosons should be taken into account at the same time.
In particular, if the masses of
Z^{\prime}
and
\phi
are smaller than the discovered Higgs boson (
h

the latter can decay into a
Z^{\prime}

\phi

The

[
25
,
29

.
Charged leptons produced via the decay of

[
30

31

Z^{\prime}/\phi

highly boosted and collimated charged leptons.
As for the other

models,

[
32
]

[

In this paper, we discuss the phenomenology of models with various
U(1)^{\prime}
extensions of the SM with a complex scalar field.
We focus on the impact of the collider phenomenology in the presence of
Z^{\prime}
and
\phi

We show the branching ratios of the new decay channels of

We then clarify the upper limit on these branching ratios in each model.
For the case (iii), we show the branching ratio of the new Higgs boson
\phi
into a
Z^{\prime}
pair as a function of
g_{X}
and
\sin\alpha

In addition to these branching ratios, we also analyze the deviation of the Higgs trilinear coupling
hhh

and find that the magnitude of the deviation is typically less than
1\%

II.1
Higgs potential and Higgs boson masses

Here, we formulate eigenvalues for scalar bosons and corresponding eigenstates after the spontaneous gauge symmetry breaking.

The Higgs potential
V
is generally written by

\displaystyle V(H,\Phi)=-\mu_{H}^{2}H^{\dagger}H-\mu_{\Phi}^{2}\Phi^{*}\Phi+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\Phi}}{2}(\Phi^{*}\Phi)^{2}+\lambda_{H\Phi}(H^{\dagger}H)(\Phi^{*}\Phi),

(2)

where
\mu_{H}
and
\mu_{\Phi}
are the mass parameters, and
\lambda_{H},~\lambda_{\Phi}
and
\lambda_{H\Phi}
are the quartic couplings,
respectively.
We can parameterize the
H
and
\Phi
fields as

\displaystyle H=\begin{pmatrix}G^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+\tilde{h}+iG)\end{pmatrix},\quad\Phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v_{\Phi}+\tilde{\phi}+iG_{\Phi}),

(3)

where
\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{\phi}
denote physical CP-even scalar components while
G^{\pm}
,
G
and
G_{\Phi}
are the Nambu-Goldstone bosons that are absorbed by the weak gauge bosons
Z,~W
and extra
Z^{\prime}
boson, respectively.
The mass squared matrix of the CP-even scalar bosons is given by

\displaystyle M^{2}_{\mathrm{even}}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{H}v^{2}&\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}\\
\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}&\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where we adopted the stationary conditions,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tilde{h}}\Bigg|_{0}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tilde{\phi}}\Bigg|_{0}=0,

(5)

with
|_{0}
denoting all the component fields to be zero.
Then, we can diagonalize the mass matrix by introducing an orthogonal matrix
O_{\mathrm{even}}
,

\displaystyle O^{T}_{\mathrm{even}}M^{2}_{\mathrm{even}}O_{\mathrm{even}}=\mathrm{diag}(m_{h}^{2},m_{\phi}^{2}),

(6)

where

\displaystyle O_{\mathrm{even}}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\
\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

The mass eigenvalues are also given by

\displaystyle m_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{H}v^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha+\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha+2\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha,

(8a)

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha+\lambda_{H}v^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha-2\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha.

(8b)

The scalar mixing angle
\alpha
is obtained as

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha=\frac{2\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}}{\lambda_{H}v^{2}-\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}}.

(9)

The corresponding mass eigenstates are written by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}=O^{T}_{\mathrm{even}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

where
we identify
h
with the discovered Higgs boson with a mass of 125 GeV.
From Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
), the quartic couplings can be
expressed in terms of the masses and the mixing as

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2v^{2}}\big[m_{h}^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}+(m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2})\cos 2\alpha\big],

(11a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2v_{\Phi}^{2}}\big[m_{h}^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}-(m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2})\cos 2\alpha\big],

(11b)

\displaystyle\lambda_{H\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2vv_{\Phi}}(m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2})\sin 2\alpha.

(11c)

Notice that
\lambda_{H}
and
\lambda_{\Phi}
are always positive due to positive mass squared of
h
and
\phi

while
\lambda_{H\Phi}
can be negative.

The trilinear couplings for the scalar bosons are then expressed in their mass bases as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset

\displaystyle\left(\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{v}Z_{\mu}Z^{\mu}+\frac{2m_{W}^{2}}{v}W^{+}_{\mu}W^{-\mu}-\sum_{f_{\rm SM}}\frac{m_{f_{\rm SM}}}{v}\overline{f_{\rm SM}}f_{\rm SM}\right)(h\cos\alpha-\phi\sin\alpha)

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{v_{\Phi}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}Z^{\prime\mu}(\phi\cos\alpha+h\sin\alpha)+\sum_{\rm scalars}\lambda_{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k},

(12)

where
m_{f_{\rm SM}}
represents the mass of the SM fermions
f_{\rm SM}
(
f_{\rm SM}\neq\nu_{R}
). The last term shows the scalar trilinear terms, and the relevant couplings are given as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{hhh}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{h}^{2}(v_{\Phi}\cos^{3}\alpha+v\sin^{3}\alpha)}{2vv_{\Phi}},

(13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi hh}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin 2\alpha(2m_{h}^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2})(v_{\Phi}\cos\alpha-v\sin\alpha)}{4vv_{\Phi}},

(14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi\phi h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sin 2\alpha(m_{h}^{2}+2m_{\phi}^{2})(v\cos\alpha+v_{\Phi}\sin\alpha)}{4vv_{\Phi}},

(15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{G_{\Phi}G_{\Phi}h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{h}^{2}\sin\alpha}{2v_{\Phi}}.

(16)

II.2
Z^{\prime}
mass and interactions

Because of the existence of the kinetic mixing term in Eq. (
1
), we first need to diagonalize the
kinetic terms of
X_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
by the transformation

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X^{3}_{\mu}\\
B_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}r&0\\
-\epsilon r&1\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{B}_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right),

(17)

where
r=1/\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}
.
After the spontaneous breaking of the EW and the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetries, the mass terms of electrically neutral gauge fields are induced as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{Z}_{\mu},\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}&\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}\\
\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}&r^{2}\left(g_{X}^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\right)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}^{\mu}\\
\tilde{Z}^{\prime\mu}\end{pmatrix},

(18)

where
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}=\cos\theta_{W}W^{3}_{\mu}-\sin\theta_{W}\tilde{B}_{\mu}

and

m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}=v^{2}g^{2}/(4\cos^{2}\theta_{W})

with
\theta_{W}
and
g
being the Weinberg angle and the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge coupling, respectively.
The above mass matrix can be diagonalized by introducing the following orthogonal transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\chi&\sin\chi\\
-\sin\chi&\cos\chi\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Z_{\mu}\\
Z_{\mu}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(19)

where
Z_{\mu}
can be identified with the observed
Z
boson.
The mixing angle
\chi
is written by

\displaystyle\sin 2\chi=-\frac{2\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}m_{Z_{{\rm SM}}}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}.

(20)

Taking a tiny kinetic mixing parameter
\epsilon\ll 1
, deviation of the
Z
boson mass from the SM value is negligible and
the
Z^{\prime}
boson mass is written by

\displaystyle m_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=g_{X}v_{\Phi}[1+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})].

(21)

The gauge current
J_{X}^{\mu}
is given by

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{X}=\sum_{f}X_{f}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}f,

(22)

where
f
indicates the fermions in the models.
The relevant interaction Lagrangian for
Z^{\prime}
and fermions
f
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}f\bar{f}}=g_{X}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}(v^{\prime}_{f}-a^{\prime}_{f}\gamma_{5})fZ^{\prime}_{\mu},

(23)

where

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{f}

\displaystyle=X_{f}r\cos\chi+\frac{e}{g_{X}}Q_{f}\epsilon r\cos\theta_{W}\cos\chi

\displaystyle-\frac{g}{g_{X}\cos\theta_{W}}\left(\frac{T_{3f}}{2}-Q_{f}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\right)(\sin\chi+\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}\cos\chi),

(24a)

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{f}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g}{g_{X}\cos\theta_{W}}\frac{T_{3f}}{2}(\sin\chi+\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}\cos\chi),

(24b)

where
T_{3f}
is the diagonal
SU(2)_{L}
generator,
Q_{f}
is the electric charge, and
X_{f}
is the
U(1)^{\prime}
charge given in Table
2
, respectively.
We note that the dark photon (
X_{f}=0
) interaction
is simply proportional to the charge
Q_{f}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}ff}\simeq eQ_{f}\epsilon r\cos\theta_{W}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}fZ^{\prime}_{\mu}

for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\ll m_{Z}
.

III
Decays of the Higgs bosons and
Z^{\prime}

We discuss the decays of the Higgs bosons
\phi
and
h
as well as those of
Z^{\prime}
.

First, the decay rates of
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
\phi\to hh
are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}\cos^{2}\alpha}{8\pi v_{\Phi}^{2}m_{\phi}}\beta(x_{Z^{\prime}})\left[2+\frac{1}{4x_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(1-2x_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right],

(25a)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi\to hh)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\phi hh}^{2}}{8\pi m_{\phi}}\beta(x_{h}),

(25b)

where
x_{i}=m_{i}^{2}/m_{\phi}^{2}
and
\beta(x)=\sqrt{1-4x}
.
The scalar trilinear coupling
\lambda_{\phi hh}
is given in Eq. (
14
).
The decay rates of
\phi

into a pair of SM particles (except for
h
)
are obtained
from the corresponding expression of the SM Higgs boson by multiplying the factor of
\sin^{2}\alpha
and
replacing
m_{h}\to m_{\phi}
.

Second, the decay rates for the
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
h\to\phi\phi
modes are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}\sin^{2}\alpha}{8\pi v_{\Phi}^{2}m_{h}}\beta(z_{Z^{\prime}})\left[2+\frac{1}{4z_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(1-2z_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right],

(26a)

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to\phi\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\phi\phi h}^{2}}{8\pi m_{h}}\beta(z_{\phi}),

(26b)

where
z_{i}=m_{i}^{2}/m_{h}^{2}
and
\lambda_{\phi\phi h}
is given in Eq. (
15

The other decay modes of

IV
One-loop corrections to the
hhh
vertex

We discuss the one-loop corrections to the Higgs trilinear vertex
hhh
.
In order to obtain the finite results, we perform the renormalization of parameters, particularly those of the Higgs potential.

We first shift the relevant bare parameters into the renormalized ones and the counterterms as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}\to\delta T_{\varphi},~~m_{\varphi}^{2}\to m_{\varphi}^{2}+\delta m_{\varphi}^{2},~~v\to v+\delta v,~~v_{\Phi}\to v_{\Phi}+\delta v_{\Phi},~~\alpha\to\alpha+\delta\alpha+\delta\alpha_{\rm PT},

(29)

where
\varphi=h
or
\phi
.
For the shift of the mixing angle
\alpha
, we introduce not only the ordinal counterterm
\delta\alpha
but also the one coming from the pinch-term
[
34
,
35
]

\delta\alpha_{\rm PT}
which is required to maintain the gauge independent result
[
36
,
37
]
3
3

3

It has been known that the on-shell renormalization scheme, that is applied in this paper, generally gives rise to a gauge dependence in the renormalization of mixing parameter
[
38
]
.
.
In Eq. (
29
),
\delta T_{\varphi}
represent the counterterms for the tadpole of
\varphi
.
There are two ways for the treatment of
\delta T_{\varphi}
, i.e., the so-called standard tadpole scheme
[
39
]
and the alternative tadpole scheme
[
40
]
.
In the former scheme, tadpole counterterms are determined by requiring that renormalized scalar one-point functions vanish.
On the other hand, in the latter, tadpole counterterms are not introduced, but all contributions from one-particle irreducible (1PI) diagrams are modified to include those with tadpole-inserted diagrams.
We analytically confirm that there is no difference between the renormalized
hhh
vertex obtained by using the standard tadpole scheme and that by using the alternative tadpole scheme.
In the following, the alternative tadpole scheme is applied to clarify the discussion.
In addition to the parameter shift given in Eq. (
29
), the bare fields are shifted as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}\to\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{1}{2}\delta Z_{h}&\delta Z_{h\phi}\\
\delta Z_{\phi h}&1+\frac{1}{2}\delta Z_{\phi}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix},

(30)

where
\delta Z_{\phi h}
and
\delta Z_{h\phi}
can be decomposed as

\delta Z_{h\phi}=\delta\alpha+\delta C_{h}
and
\delta Z_{\phi h}=-\delta\alpha+\delta C_{h}
with
\delta C_{h}
being the wavefunction renormalization factor
[
41
]
.

The renormalized scalar two-point functions are then expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{\varphi\varphi}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=(p^{2}-m_{\varphi}^{2})(1+\delta Z_{\varphi})-\delta m_{\varphi}^{2}+\Gamma_{\varphi\varphi}^{\rm 1PI}(p^{2}),~~(\varphi=h,~\phi)

(31)

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi h}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=(p^{2}-m_{h}^{2})\delta Z_{h\phi}+(p^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2})\delta Z_{\phi h}+\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm 1PI}(p^{2}),

(32)

where
\Gamma_{XY}^{\rm 1PI}
denotes the contribution from 1PI diagrams to the two-point function for
X
and
Y
particles.
We impose the on-shell conditions as

\displaystyle\Re[\hat{\Gamma}_{\varphi\varphi}(m_{\varphi}^{2})]=0,\quad\Re\left[\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\hat{\Gamma}_{\varphi\varphi}(p^{2})\right]_{p^{2}=m_{\varphi}^{2}}=1,\quad\Re[\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi h}(m_{h}^{2})]=\Re[\hat{\Gamma}_{\phi h}(m_{\phi}^{2})]=0.

(33)

These lead to

\displaystyle\delta m_{\varphi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Re[\Gamma_{\varphi\varphi}^{\rm 1PI}(m_{\varphi}^{2})],\quad\delta Z_{\varphi}=-\Re\left[\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\Gamma_{\varphi\varphi}^{\rm 1PI}(p^{2})\right]_{p^{2}=m_{\varphi}^{2}},

(34)

\displaystyle\delta Z_{\phi h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}\Re[\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm 1PI}(m_{h}^{2})],\quad\delta Z_{h\phi}=\frac{1}{m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}\Re[\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm 1PI}(m_{\phi}^{2})].

(35)

The unpinched counterterm for the mixing angle
\delta\alpha
is then given by

\displaystyle\delta\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta Z_{h\phi}-\delta Z_{\phi h}}{2}=\frac{1}{m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}}\Re\left[\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm 1PI}(m_{h}^{2})+\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm 1PI}(m_{\phi}^{2})\right].

(36)

The pinch-term part is given by
[
37
]

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{\rm PT}=\frac{1}{m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}}\Re\left[\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm PT}(m_{h}^{2})+\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm PT}(m_{\phi}^{2})\right],

(37)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi h}^{\rm PT}(p^{2})=\frac{g^{2}}{64\pi^{2}}\sin 2\alpha(2p^{2}-m_{h}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2})\left[B_{0}(p^{2};m_{W},m_{W})+\frac{1}{2c_{W}^{2}}B_{0}(p^{2};m_{Z},m_{Z})\right],

(38)

with
B_{0}
being the Passarino-Veltman’s scalar
B
function
[
42
]
.

The counterterms for the VEVs
\delta v
and
\delta v_{\Phi}
can be determined from the renormalization of the EW parameters
[
39
]
which can significantly be different from those in the SM if we introduce the non-zero
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing. Although the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing can play an important role in the decay of
Z^{\prime}
even for a small mixing angle, its impact on the radiative correction to the
hhh
vertex is negligibly small.
We thus neglect the effect of the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing, i.e.,
\chi\to 0
or equivalently
\epsilon\to 0
, on the renormalization of the
hhh
vertex.
In this setup, the renormalization of the EW parameters can be performed in a similar way as in the SM. Since the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing is neglected, the transverse part of the renormalized two-point function for
Z^{\prime}
is simply expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(p^{2})=\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(p^{2})+(p^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})(1+\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}})+\delta m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2},

(39)

where
\delta m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
and
\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}

are respectively the counterterms for the mass and wavefunction of
Z^{\prime}
.
Imposing the on-shell conditions, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Re[\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})]=0,\quad\Re\left[\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(p^{2})\right]_{p^{2}=m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}=1,

(40)

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}=\Re[\Gamma^{\rm 1PI}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})],\quad\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}=-\Re\left[\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\Gamma^{\rm 1PI}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(p^{2})\right]_{p^{2}=m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}.

(41)

The counterterm of the singlet VEV
\delta v_{\Phi}
is then determined via the tree level relation,
v_{\Phi}=m_{Z^{\prime}}/g_{X}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta v_{\Phi}}{v_{\Phi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{2m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}=\frac{\Gamma^{\rm 1PI}_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})}{2m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}.

(42)

The counterterm for the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge coupling
\delta g_{X}
is determined via the renormalization of the
\bar{f}fZ_{\mu}^{\prime}
vertex as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}(p^{2})\Big|_{p^{2}=0}.

(43)

See Appendix
A
for details of the renormalization of the
\bar{f}fZ^{\prime}
vertex.

Now, we determine all the necessary counterterms for the computation of the renormalized
hhh
vertex which is expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{hhh}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{hhh}^{\rm tree}+\Gamma_{hhh}^{\rm 1PI}+\delta\Gamma_{hhh},

(44)

where the first, second and third terms respectively denote the contributions from the tree level diagram, 1PI diagrams and the counterterms.
The tree-level contribution is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{hhh}^{\rm tree}=3!\lambda_{hhh},

(45)

where
\lambda_{hhh}
is given in Eq. (
13
).
The counterterm contribution is given by

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma_{hhh}

\displaystyle=3!\left(\delta\lambda_{hhh}+\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{hhh}\delta Z_{h}+\lambda_{\phi hh}\delta Z_{\phi h}\right),

(46)

where

\displaystyle\delta\lambda_{hhh}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{h}^{2}\cos^{3}\alpha}{2v}\frac{\delta v}{v}+\frac{m_{h}^{2}\sin^{3}\alpha}{2v_{\Phi}}\frac{\delta v_{\Phi}}{v_{\Phi}}-\frac{1}{2vv_{\Phi}}(v\sin^{3}\alpha+v_{\Phi}\cos^{3}\alpha)\delta m_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3m_{h}^{2}}{4vv_{\Phi}}\sin 2\alpha(v_{\Phi}\cos\alpha-v\sin\alpha)(\delta\alpha+\delta\alpha_{\rm PT}).

(47)

We will see in the next section that the deviation of the
hhh
coupling from the SM prediction is very small such as
\sim 1\%
or smaller.
This is because the contribution to the
hhh
vertex from the
Z^{\prime}
loop is suppressed by
(g_{X}^{2}\sin\alpha)^{3}
which is negligibly small for
g_{X}\ll 1
.
In addition, the contribution from the
\phi
loop is
proportional to
\lambda_{\phi\phi h}^{3}
which is suppressed by
\sin^{3}\alpha
for
|\sin\alpha|\ll 1
.
Thus, the situation is completely different from models with singlet-scalar extensions without a
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry, where the latter can predict
{\cal O}(100)
% deviation from the SM, see e.g.,
[
43
]
. This can be realized because more parameters appear in the potential and the
\lambda_{\phi\phi h}
coupling can independently be taken of the mixing angle.

VI
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we have explored models with a spontaneously broken
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetry, and have shown possibilities for obtaining multi-
Z^{\prime}
signatures
from decays of the Higgs bosons.
In particular, we have focused on anomaly-free
U(1)^{\prime}
models without introducing new fermions (except for right-handed neutrinos), in which the SM Higgs field is not charged under the symmetry.
More specifically, we have considered the models with the
U(1)_{B-L}
,
U(1)_{L_{i}-L_{j}}
and hidden
U(1)_{D}
symmetries where the last one is also known as the dark photon model.
The
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry is spontaneously broken by the VEV of a new scalar field
\Phi
which is singlet under the SM gauge symmetry.

We then have discussed decays of scalar bosons
h
(SM-like) and
\phi
(singlet-like) and a new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
. We have seen that the new decay channels
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
h\to\phi\phi
can be significant for a larger mixing angle
\sin\alpha
and/or a larger new gauge coupling
g_{X}
.
Furthermore, we have evaluated one-loop corrections to the Higgs trilinear vertex by applying the on-shell renormalization scheme without gauge dependence.

Finally, multi-
Z^{\prime}
signatures from the scalar boson decays have been discussed, where we have chosen three reference points for the masses of
\phi
and
Z^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\{m_{Z^{\prime}},m_{\phi}\}
to be (i)
\{0.02,0.05\}
GeV, (ii)
\{20,50\}
GeV, and (iii)
\{200,800\}
GeV.
For case (i), we have shown the branching ratios of

h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
h\to\phi\phi\,(\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
modes
taking into account constraints on the new gauge coupling and the scalar mixing from
h\to\text{invisible}
decay,
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\phi
process and searches for
Z^{\prime}
interactions with SM fermions by various experiments.
For the dark photon case, it has been found that we can have the sizable branching ratios for
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\to(e^{+}e^{-})(e^{+}e^{-})
and
h\to\phi\phi\to(Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})(Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})\to(e^{+}e^{-}e^{+}e^{-})(e^{+}e^{-}e^{+}e^{-})
where particles inside bracket will be highly collimated, while those of
h\to\phi\phi/Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
are much smaller in the other
U(1)^{\prime}
models than the dark photon model. Thus, we can expect “electron-jets” signals from the processes at collider experiments.
For case (ii), we have also shown the Higgs decay branching ratios.
In this case, we have discussed constraints from multi-lepton signals from the decays of
h
and
\phi
at LHC.
The new gauge coupling is also constrained from searches for
Z^{\prime}
by flavor experiments.
It has been found that BR
(h\to 2Z^{\prime})\simeq
BR
(h\to 2\phi\to 4Z^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})
is allowed in the dark photon and
U(1)_{L_{e}-L_{\tau}}
cases while the branching ratios have to be smaller by orders in the other cases.
In these cases, we obtain the cross section of the
gg\to h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}/4Z^{\prime}
process to be
{\cal O}
(1-0.1) fb which can be detected by future LHC experiments.
In addition, we have shown the deviation of
hhh
coupling from the SM, and found that its magnitude is less than 1%.
For case (iii), the SM-like Higgs boson does not decay into
\phi
or
Z^{\prime}
, and we have discussed production and decays of
\phi
. In this mass range,
g_{X}
and
\sin\alpha
are not strongly constrained by experiments compared with the other mass ranges.
We have then shown the production cross section of
\phi
, BR
(\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
and the deviation of the
hhh
coupling.
In this case,
\phi
production induces multi-fermion signals via
Z^{\prime}
where the fraction of signals depends on the
U(1)^{\prime}
type.

In summary, we have found possibilities for obtaining a detectable number of multi-
Z^{\prime}
events from the decay of
h
and the production/decay of
\phi
, where a type of signals depends on the mass scale of new bosons as well as that of the
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry.
Exploration of such signals is important to test spontaneously broken
U(1)^{\prime}
models.
Detailed analysis of signals is required for further testing models and it will be done in upcoming works.

Appendix A
Renormalization of the
\bar{f}fZ_{\mu}^{\prime}
vertex

The renormalized
\bar{f}fZ^{\prime}_{\mu}
vertex can be decomposed into the following form factors:

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{\mu}=g_{X}\gamma^{\mu}(\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V}-\gamma_{5}\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A})+i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{M},

(50)

where
q^{\mu}
is the outgoing four-momentum for
Z^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}].

(51)

Each form factor is further decomposed into
the tree, 1PI and counterterm contributions as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{i}=\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{i,{\rm tree}}+\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{i,{\rm 1PI}}+\delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{i},~~(i=V,~A,~M),

(52)

where the tree-contribution is given by
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V,{\rm tree}}=v_{f}^{\prime}
,
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A,{\rm tree}}=a_{f}^{\prime}
and
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{M,{\rm tree}}=0
with
v_{f}^{\prime}
and
a_{f}^{\prime}
given in Eq. (
24
).
The counterterm is given by

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V}

\displaystyle=v_{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}+\frac{\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}+\delta Z_{V}^{f}\right)+a_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{A}^{f},

(53)

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A}

\displaystyle=a_{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}+\frac{\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}+\delta Z_{V}^{f}\right)+v_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{A}^{f},

(54)

where
\delta Z_{V}^{f}
and
\delta Z_{A}^{f}
are
the wavefunction renormalization factors for a fermion
f
.
These are expressed as

\displaystyle\delta Z_{V}^{f}=\frac{\delta Z_{L}^{f}+\delta Z_{R}^{f}}{2},\quad\delta Z_{A}^{f}=\frac{\delta Z_{L}^{f}-\delta Z_{R}^{f}}{2},

(55)

with
\delta Z_{L}^{f}
(
\delta Z_{R}^{f}
) being the wavefunction renormalization factors for a left-handed (right-handed) fermion.
Due of the Ward-Takahashi identity, the 1PI contributions to the vector and axial-vector parts take the following form in the limit of
q^{2}\to 0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{q^{2}\to 0}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V,\text{1PI}}

\displaystyle=-v_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{V}^{f}-a_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{A}^{f},

(56)

\displaystyle\lim_{q^{2}\to 0}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A,\text{1PI}}

\displaystyle=-a_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{V}^{f}-v_{f}^{\prime}\delta Z_{A}^{f}.

(57)

Therefore, the renormalized form factors can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V}=v_{f}^{\prime}+\Delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V}(q^{2})+v_{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}+\frac{\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A}=a_{f}^{\prime}+\Delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{A}(q^{2})+a_{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}+\frac{\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}\right),

(59)

where
\Delta\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{X}(q^{2})=\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{X,\text{1PI}}(q^{2})-\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{X,\text{1PI}}(0)
with
X=V,A
.

Now, we impose the renormalization condition as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z^{\prime}ff}^{V}(q^{2})\Big|_{q^{2}=0}=v_{f}^{\prime},

(60)

by which we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g_{X}}{g_{X}}=-\frac{\delta Z_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}.

(61)

Combing Eqs. (
42
) and (
61
), we can determine the counterterm of the singlet VEV
\delta v_{\Phi}/v_{\Phi}
.

1.
introduction

The study of heat type equations on Riemannian manifolds is an active area of research in modern geometric analysis. Gradient estimation is a method from theory of PDE analysis that is used to derive bounds for gradient of some solution of a PDE without explicitly calculating the solution. There are various gradient estimations that gives different properties of solutions. The Harnack estimation was originated after the work of Harnack
[
10
]
where he gave an inequality comparing the heat of two different points on a space. This inequality proves Harnack’s theorem and Hölder regularity for weak solutions of harmonic functions. We consider
(M,g,d\nu)
as a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with weighted volume measure
d\nu=e^{-f}d\mu
, where
d\mu
denotes the Riemann volume measure of
M
and the potential function
f
is smooth. The weighted Laplace operator is defined by

\Delta_{f}u=\Delta u-\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle,

(1.1)

for some smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
\Delta
is the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator. In this paper we consider the equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f})u=A(u)p(x,t)+B(u)q(x,t)+\mathcal{G}(u)

(1.2)

along with the
(k,m)
super Perelman-Ricci flow defined by

\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}(x,t)+2Ric_{f}^{m}(g)(x,t)\geq-2kg(x,t),

(1.3)

where
Ric_{f}^{m}
is the Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor. The functions
p(x,t)
and
q(x,t)
are smooth functions or at least of class
C^{1}
in
x
and
C^{0}
in
t
.
A(u)
,
B(u)
and
\mathcal{G}(u)
are sufficiently smooth functions.

Hamilton
[
8
,
9
]
was the person who introduces the notion of Ricci flow and successfully derived Harnack estimates for Ricci flow. After the work of Li and Yau
[
15
]
this study gained a massive popularity. They introduced the idea of Li-Yau type gradient estimate and derived Harnack estimation for positive solution of Schrödinger type operator on complete Riemannian manifold. Later Perelman’s
[
18
]
revolutionary contribution gave a new perspective to study PDE over static as well as evolving Riemannian manifolds. One of the important application of gradient estimation is Liouville type theorems, in which one can derive curvature restrictions to find whether a solution is constant or not. One can see the work of Li
[
14
]
, Sun
[
22
]
, Wu
[

]
and the references therein for more information. After the popularity of the studies on gradient type estimation several mathematicians derived numerous results in this area, for instance
[

]
and the references therein for more details. Serrin
[

[

studied gradient estimation for

\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)u(x,t)=0,\ (x,t)\in M\times[0,T],

(1.4)

along Ricci flow, which can be obtained by setting
\mathcal{G}=0,\ A(u)=0,\ B(u)=0\text{ and }f=
constant in equation (

[

derived gradient estimation for

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f})u=Au^{p}+Bu^{q}+au\log(u)+bu

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f})u=Ae^{pu}+Be^{-qu}+D.

If we put

2.
Preliminaries

On a complete weighted Riemannian manifold
(M,g,e^{-f}d\mu)
the Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor is defined by

Ric_{f}^{m}(g)=Ric(g)+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{\nabla f\otimes\nabla f}{m-n},

(2.1)

where
\nabla^{2}f
is the hessian of
f
. It is to be mentioned that the case
m=n
occurs only the potential function
f
is constant, i.e.,
Ric_{f}^{m}(g)=Ric(g)
. And when
m\to\infty
we have
Ric_{f}^{\infty}(g)\equiv Ric_{f}(g)=Ric(g)+\nabla^{2}f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the weighted Bochner formla is given by

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}{|\nabla u|^{2}}=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle+Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

(2.2)

Corresponding the generalized Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor, from (
2.2
), we get the following

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}{|\nabla u|^{2}}=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle+Ric_{f}^{m}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{\langle\nabla u,\nabla f\rangle^{2}}{m-n},

here the inner product is given by
\langle X,Y\rangle:=g(X,Y)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any smooth function
u,v\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have the followings

\displaystyle\Delta(uv)=u\Delta v+2\nabla u\nabla v+v\Delta u,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{u}{v})=\frac{\Delta u}{v}-\frac{2\nabla(\frac{u}{v})\nabla v}{v}-\frac{u\Delta v}{v^{2}}

(2.4)

For any two points
x,y\in M
and for any
t\in(0,T]
the quantity
d(x,y;t)

the geodesic distance between
x
and
y
under the metric g(t). For any fixed
x_{0}\in M
and
R>0
we define a compact set

Q_{R,T}=Q_{R,T}(x_{0})=\{(x,t):d(x,x_{0};t)\leq R,0\leq t\leq T\}

(2.5)

We denote
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
and
a_{-}=\max\{-a,0\}
.

Let

It is to be noted that

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let

It is easily seen that

\displaystyle\nabla w=\frac{\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\nabla h}{(1-h)^{3}}

2.2
we find that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w=

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\left(\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{f}|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2}{(1-h)^{2}}\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}\right)\nabla(1-h)^{2}-\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{4}}\Delta_{f}(1-h)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{f}|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{4\langle\nabla|\nabla h|^{2},\nabla h\rangle}{(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\Delta_{f}h}{(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{6|\nabla h|^{4}}{(1-h)^{4}}.

(3.2)

Using

\partial_{t}|\nabla h|^{2}=-[\partial_{t}g](\nabla h,\nabla h)+2\langle\nabla h,\nabla\partial_{t}h\rangle

(3.3)

From (
3.1

Thus we can conclude

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h,\nabla\partial_{t}h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla h,\nabla[\Delta_{f}h+|\nabla h|^{2}+\delta^{-1}e^{-h}[A(\delta e^{h})p+B(\delta e^{h})q+\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})]]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla h,\nabla\Delta_{f}h\rangle+\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle+\delta^{-1}e^{-h}[A(\delta e^{h})\langle\nabla h,\nabla p\rangle+B(\delta e^{h})\langle\nabla h,\nabla q\rangle]

\displaystyle+|\nabla h|^{2}\delta^{-1}e^{-h}[{p\delta e^{h}A^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})-pA(\delta e^{h})}+{q\delta e^{h}B^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})-qB(\delta e^{h})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +{\delta e^{h}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})-\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})}].

(3.4)

Putting (
3.4

) we infer

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w

\displaystyle=\frac{-[\partial_{t}g](\nabla h,\nabla h)}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla\Delta_{f}h\rangle}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2A(\delta e^{h})\langle\nabla h,\nabla p\rangle}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2p|\nabla h|^{2}A(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2p|\nabla h|^{2}A^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2B(\delta e^{h})\langle\nabla h,\nabla q\rangle}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2q|\nabla h|^{2}B(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2q|\nabla h|^{2}B^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\Delta_{f}h}{(1-h)^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{4}}{(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{2p|\nabla h|^{2}A(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{2q|\nabla h|^{2}B(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}

(3.5)

Next we combine (

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f})w=

\displaystyle\frac{-[\partial_{t}g]}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{\Delta_{f}|\nabla h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla\Delta_{f}h\rangle}{(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{4\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2q|\nabla h|^{2}B^{\prime}(\delta e^{h})}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{2}}+\frac{2p|\nabla h|^{2}A(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}+\frac{2q|\nabla h|^{2}B(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2|\nabla h|^{2}\mathcal{G}(\delta e^{h})}{\delta e^{h}(1-h)^{3}}.

(3.6)

Applying weighted Bochner formula in (

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f})w=

\displaystyle\frac{-[\partial_{t}g+2Ric_{f}]}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{3}}-\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-[\partial_{t}g+2Ric_{f}]}{(1-h)^{2}}-\frac{2}{(1-h)^{2}}\left|\nabla\nabla h+\frac{\nabla h\otimes\nabla h}{(1-h)}\right|^{2}-\frac{2\langle\nabla h,\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle}{(1-h)^{3}}

(3.7)

. So we have

\displaystyle w\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\left(\frac{|A(\delta e^{h})|}{\delta e^{h}}\right)^{4/3}+\left(\frac{|B(\delta e^{h})|}{\delta e^{h}}\right)^{4/3}+N_{p}^{4}+N_{q}^{4}+M_{A}^{2}+M_{B}^{2}+{M_{\mathcal{G}}}+{\frac{1}{\tau}}+{k}\bigg]

\displaystyle\sqrt{w}\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +M_{B}+\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{\tau}}+\sqrt{k}\bigg].

(3.43)

As
\tau>0
is arbitrary so by above we get a special case of (
3.10
).
Thus we see that in either case the estimate holds true. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Considering the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
and
Ric_{f}^{m}\geq-\text{k}_{m}g,\ \partial_{t}g\geq-2\tilde{h}g
on
M\times[0,T]
, for any bounded positive solution
u
of (
1.2
) satisfying
0<u\leq D
on
M
and
0<t<T
we infer

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}+\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}

(3.44)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\bigg(1-\log(\frac{u}{\delta})\bigg)

Here the supremum is taken over
M\times[0,T]
and it is assumed to be finite.

Proof.

As the estimate is true for every
R\geq 2
and as the constants are independent of
R
, by making
R\to\infty
in (
3.10
) we get the desired result.
∎

4.
Application

4.1.
Parabolic Harnack Inequalities

In this section we discuss consequences of the Theorem
3.1
. Here we shall prove some Harnack inequality and a global gradient estimate with dimension free constants.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u
be a positive bounded solution of (
1.2
) with
0<u\leq\delta
in
M\times[0,T]
. Let
Ric_{f}^{m}\geq-k_{m}g
and
\partial_{t}g\geq-2\tilde{h}g
in
M\times[0,T]
for some
k_{m},\ \tilde{h}\geq 0
, and
T>0
. For any
x_{1},\ x_{2}\in M,t\in(0,T]
we have

\frac{u(x_{1},t)}{e\delta}\leq\left[\frac{u(x_{2},t)}{e\delta}\right]^{\alpha},

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\exp\biggl\{-Cd(x_{1},x_{2};t)\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}+\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\biggr\}

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\ x_{2}\in M
,
0<t<T
and also consider
\phi=\phi(s),\text{ with }0\leq s\leq 1
, to be the geodesic curve joining
x_{1},\ x_{2}
in
M
satisfying
\phi(0)=x_{1}
and
\phi(1)=x_{2}
. Also let
N_{p}\ N_{q},\ M_{A},\ M_{B},\ M_{\mathcal{G}}
all are finite otherwise the proof will be trivial. Using Corollary
3.1
and noting
h=\log(\frac{u}{\delta})
and
(1-h)>0
we find that

\displaystyle\log\bigg(\frac{1-h(x_{2},t)}{1-h(x_{1},t)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\log[1-h(\phi(s),t)]ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}-\frac{\langle\nabla h(\phi(s),t),\phi^{\prime}(s)\rangle}{(1-h(\phi(s),t))}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla h|}{(1-h)}|\phi^{\prime}(s)|ds.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1-h(x_{1},t)}{1-h(x_{2},t)}\leq

\displaystyle\exp\biggl\{-Cd(x_{1},x_{2};t)\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}+\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\biggr\}.

So,

\displaystyle\frac{\log\left[\frac{e\delta}{u(x_{1},t)}\right]}{\log\left[\frac{e\delta}{u(x_{2},t)}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\biggl\{-Cd(x_{1},x_{2};t)\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}+\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\biggr\}.

Therefore,

\frac{u(x_{1},t)}{e\delta}\leq\left[\frac{u(x_{2},t)}{e\delta}\right]^{\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u
be a bounded positive solution to (
1.2
) along the flow (
1.3
) with
0<u\leq\delta
. Let
\gamma(t)
to be smooth, non-negative but arbitrary function and consider

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]=\frac{\gamma(t)|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-u\log\left(\frac{\delta}{u}\right),

(4.2)

then we have

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg]\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\gamma}{u}\bigg[A(u)\langle\nabla u,\nabla p\rangle+B(u)\langle\nabla u,\nabla q\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle+(\gamma^{\prime}+2k\gamma-1)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+A(u)p

\displaystyle+B(u)q+\frac{2\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(u)-\frac{\mathcal{G}(u)}{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma p|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[A^{\prime}(u)-\frac{A(u)}{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma q|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[B^{\prime}(u)-\frac{B(u)}{u}\bigg]+\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]\frac{\mathcal{G}(u)}{u},

(4.3)

Proof.

Let’s start by evaluating each term on the left of (
4.3
).

\partial_{t}\left[\frac{\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right]=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+\frac{\gamma}{u}\bigg[-[\partial_{t}g](\nabla u,\nabla u)+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\partial_{t}u\rangle-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\partial_{t}u\bigg],

\Delta_{f}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right)=\frac{\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-\frac{2\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta_{f}u}{u^{2}}+\frac{2|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{3}},

\partial_{t}(u\log(\frac{\delta}{u}))=[\log(\frac{\delta}{u}-1)]\partial_{t}u,

and

\Delta_{f}(u\log(\frac{\delta}{u}))=[\log(\frac{\delta}{u}-1)]\Delta_{f}u-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}.

Thus (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg]\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+\gamma\bigg(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{|\nabla u^{2}|}{u}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\bigg(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg)\bigg(u\log(\frac{\delta}{u})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{u}\bigg[2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\partial_{t}u\rangle-[\partial_{t}g](\nabla u,\nabla u)-\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg](u)-2\gamma p\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\frac{A(u)}{u}-2\gamma q\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\frac{B(u)}{u}

\displaystyle-[\log(\frac{\delta}{u}-1)]\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg](u)

\displaystyle+A(u)p+B(u)q+(\gamma^{\prime}-1)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}.

Using (
3.10
) and

2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\partial_{t}u\rangle=2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla[A(u)p+B(u)q+\mathcal{G}(u)]\rangle
we see that

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg]\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{u}\bigg[-[\partial_{t}g](\nabla u,\nabla u)+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla[A(u)p+B(u)q+\mathcal{G}(u)]

\displaystyle-\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}\bigg]+(\gamma^{\prime}-1)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+A(u)p

\displaystyle+B(u)q-[\log(\frac{D}{u}-1)]\mathcal{G}(u)-\frac{\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{G}(u)+2\gamma A(u)p+2\gamma B(u)q\bigg].

(4.4)

From the weighted Bochner formula we have

2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle=\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}-2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-2Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

Using this in (
4.4
) we infer

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg]\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{u}\bigg[[\partial_{t}g](\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\frac{2\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u}\bigg]-\frac{2\gamma}{u}\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma}{u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(A(u)p)\rangle+\frac{2\gamma}{u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(B(u)q)\rangle+\frac{2\gamma}{u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\mathcal{G}(u))\rangle+(\gamma^{\prime}-1)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}

\displaystyle-[log(\frac{\delta}{u}-1)]\mathcal{G}(u)+A(u)p+B(u)q-\frac{\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{G}(u)+2\gamma A(u)p+2\gamma B(u)q\bigg].

Using the facts that
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-\frac{\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}=|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\nabla u\otimes\nabla u}{u}|^{2}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}g+2Ric_{f}\geq-2kg
in the above equation we have our desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
M
be closed weighted Riemannian manifold and
u
be a positive bounded solution of (
1.2
) with
0<u\leq\delta
in
M\times[0,T]
and metric-potential pair evolves under (
1.3
) with
k\geq 0
. Also suppose that
A(u),\ B(u),\ \mathcal{G}(u)\geq 0
and
A^{\prime}(u),\ B^{\prime}(u),\ \mathcal{G}^{\prime}(u)\leq 0
. Then

\displaystyle t|\nabla\log(u)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+2kt)[1+\log(\frac{\delta}{u})]+\frac{2(1+2kt)^{2}}{ut}(|\nabla p|^{2}+|\nabla q^{2}|^{2})+\frac{(1+2kt)A(u)p}{u}+\frac{(1+2kt)B(u)q}{u}}{1-\frac{(1+2kt)^{2}}{ut^{2}}A^{2}(u)-\frac{(1+2kt)^{2}}{ut^{2}}B^{2}(u)}.

Proof.

Note that the function
\gamma(t)=\frac{t}{1+2kt}
is non negative and it satisfies
\gamma^{\prime}+2k\gamma-1\leq 0
. Now choose
\bar{\delta}=e\delta
in place of
\delta
in Theorem
4.2
then clearly
u\leq\bar{\delta}
. Referring to (
4.3
) we get,

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}-\Delta_{f}-\frac{A(u)}{u}p(x,t)-\frac{B(u)}{u}q(x,t)\bigg]\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u]-\frac{2\gamma}{u}\bigg[A(u)\langle\nabla u,\nabla p\rangle+B(u)\langle\nabla u,\nabla q\rangle\bigg]-A(u)p-B(u)q

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma^{\prime}+2k\gamma-1)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+\frac{2\gamma|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(u)-\frac{\mathcal{G}(u)}{u}\bigg]+\frac{2\gamma p|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[A^{\prime}(u)-\frac{A(u)}{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma q|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\bigg[B^{\prime}(u)-\frac{B(u)}{u}\bigg]-log[\frac{\bar{\delta}}{u}-1]\mathcal{G}(u)\leq 0.

Clearly for all
x\in M
we have,
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u](x,0)\leq 0
. Thus by an application of Maximum principle (to
\Delta_{f}
) we deduce,
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}[u](x,t)\leq 0
in
M\times[0,T]
. From which after some simplification and applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Young inequality we get the desired result.
∎

4.2.
Liouville Type Result

Let
\mathcal{B_{R}}
be the closed geodesic ball in
M
with radius
R>0
and centre at
x_{0}
and let
(M,g,e^{-f}d\mu)
be a complete weighted static Riemannian manifold with
Ric_{f}^{m}\geq-k_{m}g
in
\mathcal{B}\subset M
. Let
u
be the solution of

\Delta_{f}u+A(u)p(x)+B(u)q(x)+\mathcal{G}(u)=0

(4.5)

in
\mathcal{B}
then there exists a constant
C
such that in
\mathcal{B}_{R/2}
we have,

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\frac{1}{R}+\sqrt{\frac{[\gamma_{\Delta_{f}}]_{+}}{R}}+\sqrt{k}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}+\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\bigg(1-\log(\frac{u}{\delta})\bigg),

(4.6)

Letting
R\to\infty
we get a global estimate as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\sqrt{k}+N_{p}^{2}+N_{q}^{2}+M_{A}+M_{B}+\sqrt{M_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\bigg(1-\log(\frac{u}{\delta})\bigg).

(4.7)

If
Ric_{f}^{m}\geq 0
,
p=0,q=0
and
u
is a positive bounded solution of
\Delta_{f}u+\mathcal{G}(u)=0
such that
M_{\mathcal{G}}=0
then we find that,

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[M_{A}+M_{B}+\left(\frac{|A(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{|B(u)|}{u}\right)^{2/3}\bigg]\bigg(1-\log(\frac{u}{\delta})\bigg),

(4.8)

which in particular for
A(u)=0,B(u)=0
gives the result
as following,

If
Ric_{f}^{m}\geq 0
,
p=0,q=0
,
A(u)=B(u)=0
and
u
is a positive bounded solution of
\Delta_{f}u+\mathcal{G}(u)=0
such that
M_{\mathcal{G}}=0
then
u
is constant.

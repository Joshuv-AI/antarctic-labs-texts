Representation functions in the set of natural numbers
Shi-Qiang Chen, Csaba Sándor, Quan-Hui Yang

Abstract




Abstract:
Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of all nonnegative integers. For
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
R_{S}(n)
denote the number of solutions of the equation

n=s+s^{\prime}
,
s,s^{\prime}\in S
,
s<s^{\prime}
.
In this paper, we determine the structure of all sets
A
and
B
such that
A\cup B=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{r+mk:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
A\cap B=\emptyset
and
R_{A}(n)=R_{B}(n)
for every positive integer
n
, where
m
and
r
are two integers with
m\geq 2
and
r\geq 0
.

Keywords:
representation function; binary representation;
Thue-Morse sequence; characteristic function

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11B34

1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of all nonnegative integers. For
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the representation function

R_{1}(S,n)
,
R_{2}(S,n)
,
R_{3}(S,n)
denote
the number of representations of
n
in the form:
(1)
n=s+s^{\prime}
,
s,s^{\prime}\in S
, (2)
n=s+s^{\prime}
,
s<s^{\prime}
,
s,s^{\prime}\in S
, (3)
n=s+s^{\prime}
,
s\leq s^{\prime}
,
s,s^{\prime}\in S
, respectively. Clearly,

R_{1}(S,n)=R_{2}(S,n)+R_{3}(S,n)
for all nonnegative integers
n
.

In
[
20
]
,
Nathanson studied the function
R_{1}(S,n)
and called it the representation function. After that, these three functions were studied by Erdős, Sárközy and Sós in a series of papers (see
[
9
]
-
[
13
]
).
They showed that these functions have some different properties. In recent decades, representation functions have been extensively studied by
many authors [1,2,7,14,15,21,22,24,27]
and are still a fruitful area of research in additive number
theory.

For

i\in\{1,2,3\},
Sárközy asked whether there are sets

A,B\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with infinite symmetric difference such
that
R_{i}(A,n)=R_{i}(B,n)
for all sufficiently large integers
n
.
In 2002, Dombi
[
8
]
proved that the answer is negative for
i=1
and positive for
i=2
.
Chen and Wang
[
5
]
proved that the answer is positive for
i=3
.
In fact, Dombi (for
i=2
), Chen and Wang (for
i=3
) proved that there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}

such that
R_{i}(A,n)=R_{i}(\mathbb{N}\backslash A,n)
for all
n\geq n_{0}.
Lev
[
17
]
gave a simple common proof to these
results. Later, Sándor
[
23
]
gave a complete answer by
using generating functions and Tang
[
25
]
gave
an elementary proof.

Let
[a,b]
denote the set of all integers
n
such that
a\leq n\leq b
. For
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
a+S
denotes the set of all integers of
the form
a+s
,
s\in S
.
Let
U
be the set of all nonnegative integers which contains an even
number of digits
1
in their binary representations and
V=\mathbb{N}\setminus U
. Put
U_{l}=U\cap[0,2^{l}-1]

and
V_{l}=V\cap[0,2^{l}-1]
. In Dombi’s result, the set
A
is the set
U
here.

In 2012, Yu and Tang
[
28
]
considered a special case where the intersection of two sets is non-empty, and proposed the following conjecture: Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
R\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
.
If
\mathbb{N}=A\cup B
and
A\cap B=\{r+km:~k\in\mathbb{N},r\in R\}
, then
R_{i}(A,n)=R_{i}(B,n)
cannot hold for all sufficiently large integers
n
, where
i=2,3
.
In 2016, Tang
[
26
]
confirmed this conjecture in the case that
R=\{0\}
.
In 2016, Chen and Lev
[
6
]
disproved this conjecture for
i=2
.

In this paper, based on Chen and Lev’s result, we focus on the function
R_{2}(S,n)
. For brevity, we denote

R_{2}(S,n)
by
R_{S}(n)
from now on. Chen and Lev
[
6
]
proved that for any given
positive integer
l
, there exist two subsets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
\mathbb{N}=A\cup B
and
A\cap B=\{2^{2l}-1+k(2^{2l+1}-1):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

R_{A}(n)=R_{B}(n)
for every positive integer
n
. The following two
problems are posed in
[
6
]
.

Problem 1.
Given that
R_{C}(n)=R_{D}(n)
for every
positive integer
n
,
C\cup D=\mathbb{N}
, and
C\cap D=r+m\mathbb{N}
with integers
r\geq 0
and
m\geq 2
, must there
exist an integer
l\geq 1
such that
r=2^{2l}-1,m=2^{2l+1}-1
?

Problem 2.
Given that
R_{C}(n)=R_{D}(n)
for every
positive integer
n
,
C\cup D=[0,m]
, and
C\cap D=\{r\}
with
integers
r\geq 0
and
m\geq 2
, must there exist an integer

l\geq 1
such that
r=2^{2l}-1,m=2^{2l+1}-2,C=U_{2l}\cup(2^{2l}-1+V_{2l})
and

D=V_{2l}\cup(2^{2l}-1+U_{2l})
?

In 2017, Kiss and Sándor

[
18
]
solved Problem 2 affirmatively. In 2019, Chen, Tang and Yang

[
4
]
solved Problem 1 affirmatively under the
assumption
0\leq r<m
. In 2021, Chen and Chen
[
3
]
solved Problem 1 affirmatively.

Notice that all of these results are taking into account the union of two sets is the set of all natural numbers or complete interval. In 2012, Jiao et al.
[
16
]
determined all sets
C
and
D
with
C\cup D=[0,m]\setminus\{r\}
, where
0<r<m
and
C\cap D=\emptyset

such that
R_{C}(n)=R_{D}(n)
for any nonnegative integer
n
. In this paper, we consider the case

C\cup D=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{r+mk:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
m\geq 2
,
k\geq 0
and
C\cap D=\emptyset
.

First, we give some notations. Let

H_{0}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots)=\Big\{\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}h_{i}:\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\},\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}h_{i}<+\infty,2\mid\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\Big\}

and

H_{1}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots)=\Big\{\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}h_{i}:\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\},\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}h_{i}<+\infty,2\nmid\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\Big\},

where
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

For a nonnegative integer
l
, let

S_{1}(l)=H_{0}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots),~~~T_{1}(l)=H_{1}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots),

where
h_{1}=1
,
h_{2}=2
,
h_{3}=4,\ldots,h_{l}=2^{l-1},h_{l+1}=2^{l}+1,h_{l+2}=2(2^{l}+1),h_{l+3}=4(2^{l}+1),\ldots
, and let

S_{2}(l)=H_{0}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots),~~~T_{2}(l)=H_{1}(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots),

where
h_{1}=1
,
h_{2}=2
,
h_{3}=4,\ldots,h_{l}=2^{l-1}+1,h_{l+1}=2^{l}+1,h_{l+2}=2(2^{l}+1),h_{l+3}=4(2^{l}+1),\ldots

In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
r\geq 0
be two
integers and let
A
and
B
be two sets with
A\cup B=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{r+mk:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Then
R_{A}(n)=R_{B}(n)
for every positive integer
n
if and only if

A=S_{1}(l),~B=T_{1}(l)

or

A=S_{2}(l),~B=T_{2}(l)

or

A=S_{1}(l)+1,~B=T_{1}(l)+1
,
where
l
is a nonnegative integer.

Throughout this paper, the characteristic function of the set
C
is denoted by

\displaystyle\chi_{C}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ $t\not\in C$},\\
1&\mbox{ $t\in C$}.\\
\end{cases}

Let
C(x)
be the set of integers in
C
which are less than or
equal to
x
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
R_{A,B}(n)
denote the
number of the equation
n=a+b
,
a\in A,b\in B
. For any positive
integer
n
, let
D_{2}(n)
denote the number of
1
in the binary
representation of
n
. Let
D_{2}(0)=0
. It is clear that

D_{2}(n+2^{k})=1+D_{2}(n)
if
0\leq n<2^{k}
.

Preliminary Lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

(See
[
18
, Claim 1]
.) Let
0<r_{1}<\ldots<r_{s}\leq m
be integers(
s=0
is allowed). Then there exists at most one pair of sets
(C,D)
such that
C\cup D=[0,m]\setminus\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{s}\}
,
C\cap D=\emptyset
,
0\in C
, and
R_{C}(k)=R_{D}(k)
for every integer
k\leq m
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(See
[
18
, Theorem 3]
.) Let
C
and
D
be sets of nonnegative integers such that
C\cup D=[0,m]
,
C\cap D=\emptyset
and
0\in C
. Then
R_{C}(n)=R_{D}(n)
for every positive integer
n
if and only if there exists an natural number
l
such that
C=U_{l}
and
D=V_{l}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
and
L
be two positive integers with
L\leq K\leq 2L
and let
A,B,C,D,T
be five sets
such that
L\in T
,
L\not\in C
,
0\in A\cap C
,
0\notin B\cup D
and

A\cup B=\mathbb{N}\setminus T,\quad A\cap B=\emptyset,

C(K)\cup D(K)=[0,K]\setminus T(L-1),\quad C(K)\cap D(K)=\emptyset,

A(L-1)=C(L-1),\quad B(L-1)=D(L-1).

Then for any integer
n
and integer
N
with
L\leq n\leq N\leq K
, we have

\displaystyle R_{A(n)}(N)+R_{D(n)}(N)-R_{B(n)}(N)-R_{C(n)}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))|-R_{T,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)+R_{D,T(L,n)\cap D}(N)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle|C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))|+R_{T,C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)-R_{C,T(L,n)\cap C}(N)-\varepsilon,

where
T(L,n)=T\cap[L,n]
and
\varepsilon=1
if
N=2L
, otherwise
\varepsilon=0
.

Proof.

For any integer
n
and integer
N
with
L\leq n\leq N\leq K
,
if
N=a+b
with
a,b\in A(n)
and
a<b
, then by
2a<a+b=N\leq K\leq 2L
, we have
a<L
. It follows from
A(L-1)=C(L-1)
and
a\in A(n)

that
a\in C
and so
a\in(C\cap A)(n)
. If
b\in C
, then
b\in(C\cap A)(n)
. If
b\notin C
, then
b\in A(n)\setminus C(n)
. It
is clear that

(C\cap A)(n)\cap(A(n)\setminus C(n))=\emptyset,\quad(C\cap A)(n)\cup(A(n)\setminus C(n))\subseteq A(n).

Thus,

\displaystyle R_{A(n)}(N)=R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{(C\cap A)(n),A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N).

For

a\in A(n)\setminus C(n)
, by
A(L-1)=C(L-1)
and
L\not\in A
, we have
a>L
. Thus,

N-a<2L-L=L\leq n
. Again, by
A(L-1)=C(L-1)
we have

N-a\in A\Leftrightarrow N-a\in C\Leftrightarrow N-a\in(C\cap A)(n).

It follows that

R_{(C\cap A)(n),A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N)=R_{A,A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N)=R_{C,A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N).

Hence

R_{A(n)}(N)=R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{C,A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N)

(2.1)

and

R_{A(n)}(N)=R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{A,A(n)\setminus C(n)}(N).

Similarly,

\displaystyle R_{D(n)}(N)=R_{(D\cap B)(n)}(N)+R_{D(L-1),D(n)\setminus B(n)}(N)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{(D\cap B)(n)}(N)+R_{D,D(n)\setminus B(n)}(N)-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{(D\cap B)(n)}(N)+R_{D,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup(T(L,n))}(N)+R_{D,T(L,n)\cap D(n)}(N)-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=1
if
N=2L
, otherwise
\varepsilon=0
.

Now we prove that

A(n)\setminus C(n)=D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n)).

(2.3)

In
fact, if
t\in A(n)\setminus C(n)
, then
t\in A(n)
,
t\notin C(n)
,
t>L
and
t\notin T(L,n)
. By
C(n)\cup D(n)=[0,n]\setminus T(L-1)
, we have
t\in D(n)
.
By
A\cap B=\emptyset
, we have
t\notin B(n)
.
So
t\in D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))
. On the other hand, if
t\in D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))
, then
t\in D(n)
,
t\notin B(n)
,
t\notin T(L,n)
. Noting that
D(L-1)=B(L-1)
and
L\in T
, we have
t>L
and
t\notin T(n)
. By
C(n)\cap D(n)=\emptyset
, we have
t\notin C(n)
.
By
A(n)\cup B(n)=[0,n]\setminus T(n)
and
A\cap B=\emptyset
, we have
t\in A(n)
.
So
t\in A(n)\setminus C(n)
.

Therefore,
(
2.3
) holds. In view of (
2.1
),
(
2.3
), we have

R_{A(n)}(N)=R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{C,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N).

(2.4)

For any
t\in D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))
, by
D(L-1)=B(L-1)
and
L\in T(L,n)
,
we have
t>L
. So
N-t\in[0,L-1]
.
It follows from
C(K)\cup D(K)=[0,K]\setminus T(L-1)
that

\displaystyle R_{C,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)+R_{D,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))|-R_{T(L-1),D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))|-R_{T,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N).

For the last equality, we have used fact that
N-t\leq L-1
for all

t\in D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))
. By (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and
(
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle R_{A(n)}(N)+R_{D(n)}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{(D\cap B)(n)}(N)+|D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))|

\displaystyle-R_{T,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)+R_{D,T(L,n)\cap D}(N)-\varepsilon.

where
\varepsilon=1
if
N=2L
, otherwise
\varepsilon=0
.

Noting that
L\not\in C
, similar to the proof above, we have

\displaystyle R_{B(n)}(N)+R_{C(n)}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{(C\cap A)(n)}(N)+R_{(D\cap B)(n)}(N)+|C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))|

\displaystyle-R_{T,C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)+R_{C,T(L,n)\cap C}(N).

Thus,

\displaystyle R_{A(n)}(N)+R_{D(n)}(N)-R_{B(n)}(N)-R_{C(n)}(N)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))|-R_{T,D(n)\setminus(B(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)+R_{D,T(L,n)\cap D}(N)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle|C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))|+R_{T,C(n)\setminus(A(n)\cup T(L,n))}(N)-R_{C,T(L,n)\cap C}(N)-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=1
if
N=2L
, otherwise
\varepsilon=0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K
and
L
be two positive integers with
L\leq K\leq 2L

be five sets

,

and
R_{C}(n)=R_{D}(n)

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

A(L-1)=C(L-1),\quad B(L-1)=D(L-1).

(2.7)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{C}(L)-R_{D}(L)

\displaystyle=

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A,B
and

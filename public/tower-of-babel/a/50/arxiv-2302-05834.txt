Introduction

We consider the following inhomogeneous fractional Schrödinger equation (NLS)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)+V(x)u(x)-a|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\beta^{2}}u(x)=\mu u(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(1.1)

where
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
N>2s
,
a>0
,
0<b<\min\{\frac{N}{2},1\}
and
\beta=\sqrt{\frac{2s-b}{N}}
denotes the
L^{2}
-critical exponent,
V(x)
and

describe the trapping potential and the chemical potential, respectively. Note from
[
7

that
(-\Delta)^{s}u
is a fractional Laplace operator satisfying

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\frac{C(N,s)}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{u(x+y)+u(x-y)-2u(x)}{|y|^{N+2s}}dy\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Here

C(N,s)=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{1-cos\xi_{1}}{|\xi|^{N+2s}}d\xi\Big)^{-1}.

(1.3)

Equation (
1.1

[
1

21
,
27
]

(-\Delta)^{s}
can be seen as the infinitesimal generators of L
\acute{e}
vy stable diffusion processes
[
4

[

,
25

and so on. The function

1.1

V(x)
satisfies

(
V_{1})
.

0\leq V(x)\in{L^{\infty}_{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

In this paper, we are concerned with the

-constraint variational problem for inhomogeneous fractional Schrödinger equation (

E_{a}(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2})dx-\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}}{|x|^{b}}dx,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx=\frac{C(N,s)}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy.

When

-constraint variational problem of Schrödinger equations. In the last two decades, the existence and nonexistence of constrained minimizers and various quantitative properties of these equations have been investigated extensively, see

For different kinds of trapping potentials, the authors in

u_{a}
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{a}(x)+V(x)u_{a}(x)=\mu_{a}u_{a}(x)+au_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}(x)|x|^{-b}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(3.1)

where
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier satisfying

\mu_{a}=e(a)-\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx.

(3.2)

Define

\varepsilon_{a}:=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{a}|^{2}dx\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2s}},

(3.3)

and

w_{a}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}_{a}u_{a}(\varepsilon_{a}x).

(3.4)

Using the simialr argument of
[
22
, Lemma B.2 ]
, one can check that
w_{a}
satisfies the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
N>2s
,
0<b<\min\{\frac{N}{2},1\}
and
\beta^{2}=\frac{2s-b}{N}
. Suppose
w_{a}(x)\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, then
w^{q}_{a}(x)|x|^{-b}\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, where
m\in\big(\frac{2N}{Nq+2b},\frac{2_{s}^{*}N}{Nq+2_{s}^{*}b}\big)
.

Based on above facts, we then complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We first claim that
\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
. Actually, suppose on the contrary, there exists a sequence
\{a_{k}\}
satisfying
a_{k}\nearrow a^{\ast}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, such that the sequence
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is bounded uniformly for
k
in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
), we obtain that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{a_{k}}|^{2}dx\leq e(a_{k})\leq C
uniformly for
k
and hence
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is bounded uniformly in
\mathcal{H}
.
Following Lemma
2.1
, there exist a subsequence, still denoted by
\{a_{k}\}
, of
\{a_{k}\}
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

{u_{a_{k}}}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ \mathcal{H}\,\ \hbox{and}\,\ {u_{a_{k}}}\rightarrow u_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,(2\leq q<2_{s}^{*})\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ k\to\infty.

Combining the above convergence with (
2.25
) further gives that

0=e(a^{\ast})\leq E_{a^{\ast}}(u_{0})\leq\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}E_{a_{k}}(u_{a_{k}})=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}e(a_{k})=0,

and thus
u_{0}
is a minimizer of
e(a^{\ast})
, which however contradicts with Theorem
1.2
(2). Therefore we conclude that
\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
.

We then prove that

w_{a}\rightarrow w_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ a\nearrow a^{*},

(3.5)

where
w_{0}
is an optimizer of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
). Noting from Theorem
1.2
(2) that
0=e(a^{*})=\lim_{a\nearrow a^{*}}e(a)
, thus
\{u_{a}\}
is also a minimizing sequence of
e(a^{*})
. Moreover, we obtain from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w_{a}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{a}|^{2}dx=1.

Hence
\{w_{a}\}
is bounded in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
uniformly for
a
, and passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists a function
w_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that

w_{a}\rightharpoonup w_{0}\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ w_{a}\rightarrow w_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L_{loc}^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ 2\leq q<2_{s}^{*}.

Note that the proof of the claim
(
2.34
)
does not rely on the assumption
0\leq e(a^{*})<V(0)
. Thus, the same argument of proving (
2.34
) then yields that (
3.5
) holds true.

Next, we prove (
1.14
) holds true. Recall from (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) that
w_{a}
satisfies the following equation

(-\Delta)^{s}w_{a}+\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)w_{a}=\mu_{a}\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}w_{a}+aw_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}\ \ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(3.6)

where
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier satisfying (
3.2
). We derive from (
2.37
) and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}\mu_{a}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}e(a)-\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}e(a)-\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|w_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\to-\beta^{2}\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.7)

Hence for
a\nearrow a^{*}
, it follows from (
3.6
) that

(-\Delta)^{s}w_{a}-c(x)w_{a}(x)\leq 0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ c(x)=aw^{2\beta^{2}}_{a}|x|^{-b}.

(3.8)

In view of above facts, we shall prove (
1.14
) through the following two steps.

Step 1.
We prove that there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
0<a<a^{*}
, such that

\|w_{a}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C.

(3.9)

Actually, for any
L>0
and
\alpha>0
, set

\gamma(t)=tt_{L}^{2\alpha}\,\ \mbox{for}\,\ t>0,\,\ \mbox{where}\,\ t_{L}=\min\{t,L\}.

(3.10)

Since
\gamma(t)
is a nondecreasing function, for any
a,b\geq 0
, we have

(b-a)(\gamma(b)-\gamma(a))\geq 0.

Define

\Gamma(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\gamma^{\prime}(\tau))^{\frac{1}{2}}d\tau.

For any
a,b\geq 0
with
a<b
, applying Schwartz inequality, we deduce that

\begin{split}(b-a)(\gamma(b)-\gamma(a))&=(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}\gamma^{\prime}(t)dt\\
&=(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}(\Gamma^{\prime}(t))^{2}dt\\
&\geq\Big(\int_{a}^{b}\Gamma^{\prime}(t)dt\Big)^{2}=\big(\Gamma(b)-\Gamma(a)\big)^{2}.\end{split}

(3.11)

Similarly, one can verify that (
3.11
) also holds for any
a\geq b
. This further implies that

(b-a)(\gamma(b)-\gamma(a))\geq\big|\Gamma(b)-\Gamma(a)\big|^{2}\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ a,b\geq 0.

Together with (
3.10
), it then yields that

\big|\Gamma(w_{a}(x))-\Gamma(w_{a}(y))\big|^{2}\leq\big(w_{a}(x)-w_{a}(y)\big)\big((w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha})(x)-(w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha})(y)\big).

(3.12)

Taking
\gamma(w_{a})=w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as a test function in (
3.8
), we then derive from (
3.12
) that

\begin{split}\big[\Gamma(w_{a})\big]_{s}^{2}&\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{(w_{a}(x)-w_{a}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\big((w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha})(x)-(w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha})(y)\big)dxdy\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}c(x)w_{a}^{2}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha}(x)dx.\end{split}

(3.13)

Note that
\Gamma(t)\geq\frac{tt_{L}^{\alpha}}{1+\alpha}
for any
t\geq 0
. Applying Sobolev’s inequality, we then deduce that

\big[\Gamma(w_{a})\big]_{s}^{2}\geq S_{*}\|\Gamma(w_{a})\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\geq\Big(\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\Big)^{2}S_{*}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(3.14)

We derive from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\Big)^{2}S_{*}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}c(x)w_{a}^{2}(w_{a})_{L}^{2\alpha}(x)dx\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}c(x)(w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x))^{2}dx.\end{split}

(3.15)

Recall from Lemma
3.1
that
c(x)\!\in\!L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, where
p\!\in\!\big(\frac{N}{2s},\frac{N^{2}}{2s(N+b-2s)}\big)
. By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}c(x)(w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x))^{2}dx\leq\|c(x)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\big\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x)\big\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(3.16)

Since
p\!\in\!\big(\frac{N}{2s},\frac{N^{2}}{2s(N+b-2s)}\big)
, it yields that
\frac{2p}{p-1}\in(2,2_{s}^{*})
. By Young’s inequality and interpolation inequality, we then deduce from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) that there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}&\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\\
\leq&C(1+\alpha)^{2}\varepsilon\|c(x)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\\
&+\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}}(1+\alpha)^{2}\|c(x)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.\end{split}

(3.17)

Taking
\varepsilon>0
such that

C(1+\alpha)^{2}\varepsilon\|c(x)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=\frac{1}{2},

it then follows from (
3.17
) that

\begin{split}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}&\leq C(1+\alpha)^{\frac{2}{1-\theta}}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\\
&:=M_{\alpha}\|w_{a}(w_{a})_{L}^{\alpha}(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.\end{split}

(3.18)

Letting
L\to\infty
, by the monotone convergence theorem, we then obtain from (
3.18
) that

\|w_{a}^{\alpha+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq M_{\alpha}\|w_{a}^{\alpha+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(3.19)

Note that

M_{\alpha}\leq C(1+\alpha)^{\frac{2}{1-\theta}}\leq M_{0}^{2}e^{2\sqrt{\alpha+1}},

where
M_{0}
is independent of
\alpha
. Combining this with (
3.19
), it further indicates that

\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}(\alpha+1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}e^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\alpha}}}\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2(\alpha+1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Choosing
\alpha_{0}=0
and
2(\alpha_{n+1}+1)=2_{s}^{*}(\alpha_{n}+1)
, by iteration, we deduce that

\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}(\alpha_{n}+1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq M_{0}^{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{i}}}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}}}}\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2(\alpha_{0}+1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(3.20)

Since
1+\alpha_{n}=\Big(\frac{N}{N-2s}\Big)^{n}
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{i}}<\infty\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}}}<\infty.

Together with (
3.20
), it then yields that

\|w_{a}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}(\alpha_{n}+1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}<C\|w_{a}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=C,

(3.21)

and (
3.9
) is thus proved.

Step 2.
We prove that

w_{a}(x)\rightarrow 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly\ \ for}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.22)

Indeed, denote

g_{a}(x):=aw_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ h_{a}(x):=g_{a}(x)-\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}V(\varepsilon_{a}x),

we deduce from (
3.6
) and (
3
) that

(-\Delta)^{s}w_{a}(x)+\eta w_{a}(x)=h_{a}(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ \eta=-\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}\mu_{a}>\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}>0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.23)

For any
\lambda>0
, let
G_{\lambda}
be the Fourier transform of
(|\xi|^{2s}+\lambda)^{-1}
. We note from
[
11
, Lemma C.1]
that there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that

0<G_{\eta}(x)\leq\frac{K_{1}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\ \ \mbox{for\ \ any}\ \ |x|>1,

(3.24)

and

G_{\eta}(x)\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ \ \mbox{for\ \ any}\ \ q\in\Big[1,\frac{N}{N-2s}\Big).

(3.25)

It then follows from (
3.23
) that

\begin{split}0\leq w_{a}(x)=G_{\eta}\ast h_{a}(x)&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}G_{\eta}(x-y)h_{a}(y)dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}G_{\eta}(x-y)g_{a}(y)dy-\varepsilon_{a}^{2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}G_{\eta}(x-y)V(\varepsilon_{a}y)dy\\
&:=B_{1}-B_{2}\leq B_{1}.\end{split}

(3.26)

We now estimate
B_{1}
as follows. Motivated by
[
2
,
3
]
, for any
0<\delta<1
, we define

A_{\delta}:=\big\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:|y-x|\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\big\}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ B_{\delta}:=\big\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:|y-x|<\frac{1}{\delta}\big\}.

We deduce that

\begin{split}0\leq B_{1}&=\int_{A_{\delta}}G_{\eta}(x-y)g_{a}(y)dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}G_{\eta}(x-y)g_{a}(y)dy\\
&:=D_{1}+D_{2}.\end{split}

(3.27)

Since
w_{a}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
w_{a}\rightarrow w_{0}
in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
a\nearrow a^{*}
, we derive that
w_{a}\rightarrow w_{0}
in
L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for any
r\in[2,\infty)
. Using a similar argument to (
2.25
) then yields that

g_{a}(x)\rightarrow a^{*}w_{0}^{1+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}:=g(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ m\in\Big[1,\frac{N}{b}\Big).

(3.28)

We thus derive from (
3.24
) that

\begin{split}D_{1}&\leq K_{1}\int_{A_{\delta}}\frac{g_{a}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\leq K_{1}\|g_{a}\|_{L^{2}(A_{\delta})}\Big(\int_{A_{\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2N+4s}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq K_{1}\delta^{2s}\|g_{a}\|_{L^{2}(A_{\delta})}\Big(\int_{A_{\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2N}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\delta^{2s}.\end{split}

(3.29)

As for
D_{2}
, we deduce from (
3.27
) that

D_{2}\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}G_{\eta}(x-y)|g_{a}(y)-g(y)|dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}G_{\eta}(x-y)g(y)dy.

(3.30)

Since
\frac{b}{N}+\frac{N-2s}{N}<1
, there exist
m\in[1,\frac{N}{b})
and
q\in[1,\frac{N}{N-2s})
such that
\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, one can obtain from (
3.30
) that

D_{2}\leq\|G_{\eta}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\|g_{a}-g\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\|G_{\eta}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{m}(B_{\delta})}.

Noting that
\|g_{a}-g\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\rightarrow 0
as
a\nearrow a^{*}
and
\|g\|_{L^{m}(B_{\delta})}\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
, we then deduce from (
3.25
) that

D_{2}\leq\delta\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\to\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly\ \ for}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

Together with (
3.29
), it further yields that

B_{1}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}G_{\eta}(x-y)g_{a}(y)dy\leq C\delta^{2s}+\delta\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\to\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly for}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

Let
\delta\rightarrow 0
, we deduce that
B_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
uniformly for
a\nearrow a^{*}
. We then derive from (
3.26
) that (
3.22
) holds true. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is therefore complete.∎

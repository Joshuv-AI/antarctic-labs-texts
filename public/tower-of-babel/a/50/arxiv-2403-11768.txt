Abstract
The theory of low-rank tensor-train approximation is well understood when the approximation error is measured in the Frobenius norm. The entrywise maximum norm is equally important but is significantly weaker for large tensors, making the estimates obtained via the Frobenius norm and norm equivalence pessimistic or even meaningless. In this article, we derive a direct estimate of the entrywise approximation error that is applicable in some of these cases. The estimate is given in terms of the higher-order generalization of the matrix factorization norm, and its proof is based on the tensor-structured Hanson–Wright inequality. The theoretical results are accompanied by numerical experiments carried out with the method of alternating projections.

Introduction

The entrywise storage and processing of
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}
tensors become expensive when
n=\max\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\}
is large. This fundamental difficulty, common in data-intensive areas such as scientific computing and data analysis, necessitates the use of low-parametric tensor representations. The reduction of complexity, however, comes at a typically inevitable cost of introducing errors in the entries of the tensor. It is therefore important to have a priori estimates that relate the compression rate to the achievable approximation errors for a specific representation format.

Tensor networks are among the most widespread and effective low-parametric representations: they leverage the idea of separation of variables and generalize low-rank matrix factorizations. A particularly successful example of a tensor network is the tensor-train (TT) format
[
30
,
29
]
, also known as matrix product states
[
39
,
32
]
, which reduces the storage requirements from
\mathcal{O}(n^{d})
to
\mathcal{O}(dnr^{2})
. The effective rank,
r
, measures the complexity of the TT representation.

The known a priori estimates of the error induced by TT approximation are formulated for the Frobenius norm
\|\bm{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}|\bm{\mathcal{A}}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})|^{2}
in terms of the singular values of the unfolding matrices
[
29
]
. An estimate in the maximum norm
\|\bm{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\max}=\max_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}|\bm{\mathcal{A}}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})|
can be deduced from the norm inequality
\|\bm{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\max}\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
, but becomes too pessimistic if the singular values decay slowly. We aim to derive a new a priori estimate of the entrywise TT approximation error given in terms of the
higher-order generalization of the matrix factorization norm
.

1.2
Tensor-train factorization quasinorm

We aim to extend
theorem

2
to order-
d

\bm{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
is said to be represented

2

2

Every tensor admits a TT factorization; it can be obtained with the TT-SVD algorithm
[
29
]
.
in the TT format with TT cores
\{\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{s}\}_{s=1}^{d}
of size
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{s-1}\times n_{s}\times r_{s}}

\bm{\mathcal{A}}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})=\sum_{\alpha_{1}=1}^{r_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\alpha_{d-1}=1}^{r_{d-1}}\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(1,i_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{2}(\alpha_{1},i_{2},\alpha_{2})\ldots\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{d}(\alpha_{d-1},i_{d},1).

(3)

In short,

To generalize
\gamma_{2}(\bm{\mathrm{A}})

\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}\|_{2,\infty}=\max_{i\in[n_{1}]}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}(i,:)\|_{2}

\bm{\mathrm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}

and a matrix norm

\|\bm{\mathcal{G}}\|_{\circ,\infty}=\max_{i\in[n_{2}]}\|\bm{\mathcal{G}}(:,i,:)\|_{\circ}.

This is a norm, and we use it to introduce an analogue of (
1

d
tensors:

\gamma_{\circ}^{\mathrm{TT}}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})=\inf\Big\{\prod\nolimits_{s=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{s}\|_{\circ,\infty}:\{\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{s}\}_{s=1}^{d}~\text{such that}~\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{TT}(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{d})\Big\}.

(4)

We will work with
\gamma_{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{TT}}
and call it the
TT factorization (Frobenius) quasinorm
.

Proof.

1. Let
\gamma_{\circ}^{\mathrm{TT}}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})=0
and take a minimizing sequence of TT cores

\bm{\mathcal{A}}

4. Note that

\sum_{\bm{i}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega)}\prod_{\tau\in[\kappa]}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{i}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}=\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\bigg(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\{\omega\})}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}_{\bm{\ell}\PindJoin\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\bigg)\prod_{\tau\neq t}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}\|_{\mathrm{F}(\{\omega\})}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega_{t}\setminus\{\omega\})
. For every fixed
\bm{j}
, the Frobenius norm of the partial array
\bm{\ell}\mapsto\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}_{\bm{\ell}\PindJoin\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}
is equal to
\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}
. Then, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\bigg(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\{\omega\})}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}_{\bm{\ell}\PindJoin\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\bigg)\prod_{\tau\neq t}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}\leq\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\big(\sqrt{m_{\omega}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\big)\prod_{\tau\neq t}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}.

This removes the presence of
\omega
and reduces the problem to the case of
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega\setminus\{\omega\}
. We can modify
\pi
to construct a new partition

\pi^{\prime}=(\pi\setminus\mho_{t})\cup(\mho_{t}\setminus\{\omega,\omega+N\})\in\begin{cases}\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa-1}(\Omega^{\prime}\oplus\Omega^{\prime}),&\mho_{t}\setminus\big\{\omega,\omega+N\big\}=\varnothing,\\
\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}(\Omega^{\prime}\oplus\Omega^{\prime}),&\mho_{t}\setminus\big\{\omega,\omega+N\big\}\neq\varnothing.\end{cases}

Note that
\theta_{\pi,\omega^{\prime}}=\theta_{\pi^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}
for
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis and
lemma

14
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{i}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega)}\prod_{\tau\in[\kappa]}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{i}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}

\displaystyle\leq m_{\omega}^{\theta_{\pi,\omega}}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\prod_{\tau\neq t}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}

\displaystyle\leq m_{\omega}^{\theta_{\pi,\omega}}\prod_{\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}}m_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta_{\pi^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}}\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\prod_{\tau\neq t}\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\prod_{\omega\in\Omega}m_{\omega}^{\theta_{\pi,\omega}}\prod_{\tau\in[\kappa]}\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

Suppose now that
\theta_{\pi,\omega}=0
, that is,
\omega
lies in two distinct
\Omega_{t},\Omega_{q}
. Then a similar argument, with
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}\|_{\mathrm{F}(\{\omega\})}
and
\bm{\mathcal{E}}=\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(q)}\|_{\mathrm{F}(\{\omega\})}
, leads us to

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{i}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega)}\prod_{\tau\in[\kappa]}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{i}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\bigg(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\{\omega\})}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(t)}_{\bm{\ell}\PindJoin\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(q)}_{\bm{\ell}\PindJoin\bm{j}_{\Omega_{q}}}\bigg)\prod_{\tau\neq t,q}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{m}}(\Omega\setminus\{\omega\})}\big(\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{t}}}\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{q}}}\big)\prod_{\tau\neq t}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{j}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}.

We can build a partition
\pi^{\prime}
of
\Omega^{\prime}\oplus\Omega^{\prime}
with the number of cells equal to

|\pi^{\prime}|=\begin{cases}\kappa-2&\text{if both}~\mho_{t},\mho_{q}~\text{are singletons},\\
\kappa-1&\text{if one of}~\mho_{t},\mho_{q}~\text{is a singleton},\\
\kappa&\text{if neither of}~\mho_{t},\mho_{q}~\text{is a singleton}.\end{cases}

It remains to use the induction hypothesis and
lemma

14
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\varnothing\neq\Omega\subseteq[N]
,
\pi\in\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}(\Omega\oplus\Omega)
, and
\Theta_{\pi}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\theta_{\pi,\omega}
. Then

2\Theta_{\pi}\leq\begin{cases}|\Omega|,&1\leq\kappa\leq|\Omega|,\\
2|\Omega|-\kappa,&|\Omega|<\kappa\leq 2|\Omega|.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
2\Theta_{\pi}\leq|\Omega|
for every
1\leq\kappa\leq 2|\Omega|
since there are
|\Omega|
pairs of
\omega
and
\omega+N
in total. Assume
\kappa>|\Omega|
and denote
\tilde{\pi}=\{\mho\in\pi:|\mho|=1\}
. Then

|\Omega\oplus\Omega|=2|\Omega|=\sum_{\mho\in\pi}|\mho|=|\tilde{\pi}|+\sum_{\mho\in\pi\setminus\tilde{\pi}}|\mho|\geq|\tilde{\pi}|+2(\kappa-|\tilde{\pi}|)

and
|\tilde{\pi}|\geq 2(\kappa-|\Omega|)
. It follows that there are at most
2|\Omega|-|\tilde{\pi}|\leq 4|\Omega|-2\kappa
dimensions to be paired in the remaining
\kappa-|\tilde{\pi}|
cells, hence at most
2|\Omega|-\kappa
pairs can be formed.

2.7
Analysis of the specific quadratic form

Recall that
\Delta(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{d-1})
of
eq.

9
is parametrized by
d
matrices
\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{s-1}\times k_{s}}
with
k_{0}=k_{d}=1
and takes
\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{s}\times r_{s}}
as input. Let
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d-1})
and
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d-1})
. Let us define

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}=\Phi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{\bm{k}^{\times 2}},

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}=\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1}(1,\bm{i}_{1})\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{2}(\bm{j}_{1},\bm{i}_{2})\ldots\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d}(\bm{j}_{d-1},1),\quad\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}.

More generally, let
\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{|\Omega|}\}\subseteq[d-1]
with
\omega_{1}<\cdots<\omega_{|\Omega|}
and take
|\Omega|+1
matrices
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times k_{\omega_{1}}}
,
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{\omega_{s-1}}\times k_{\omega_{s}}}
,
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{\omega_{|\Omega|}}\times 1}
. We define

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{\widehat{C}}}=\Phi_{\Omega}(\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{\bm{k}^{\times 2}}(\Omega\oplus\Omega),

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{\widehat{C}}}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}=\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1}(1,\bm{i}_{\omega_{1}})\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{2}(\bm{j}_{\omega_{1}},\bm{i}_{\omega_{2}})\ldots\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1}(\bm{j}_{\omega_{|\Omega|}},1),\quad\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{\widehat{D}}}=\Psi_{\Omega}(\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{\overline{\bm{r}\bm{k}}^{\times 2}}(\Omega\oplus\Omega),

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{\widehat{D}}}_{\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}}}=\bm{\mathcal{\widehat{C}}}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod\nolimits_{\omega\in\Omega}\delta_{\bm{\alpha}_{\omega},\bm{\beta}_{\omega}},\quad\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\Omega),\quad\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega).

Lemma 18
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
. Then

\Delta(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{d-1})=\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}}\sum_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}}}\prod_{s=1}^{d-1}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{i}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{j}_{s},\bm{\beta}_{s}).

Proof.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{C}}=\Phi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
. Then the right-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}}\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod_{s=1}^{d-1}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{i}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{j}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}}[\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{1}](1,\bm{\alpha}_{1})\cdot[\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{1}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{2}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{2}](\bm{\alpha}_{1},\bm{\alpha}_{2})\ldots[\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{d-1}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d}](\bm{\alpha}_{d-1},1).

Our first step is to understand what the partial traces of
\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
are. Thanks to
lemma

14
, it suffices to study partial traces along singletons. Recall that
{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}=[d-1]\setminus\Omega
denotes the complement of
\Omega\subseteq[d-1]
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{C}}=\Phi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
and
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
. Then

\begin{aligned} \bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}&=\Phi_{{\{s\}}^{\mathsf{c}}}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s+1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d}),\\
\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}&=\Psi_{{\{s\}}^{\mathsf{c}}}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s+1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})\cdot r_{s},\end{aligned}\quad s\in[d-1].

Proof.

Let
\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}({\{s\}}^{\mathsf{c}})
. By the definition of partial traces,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\{s\})}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1}(1,\bm{i}_{1})\ldots\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}(\bm{j}_{s-1},\bm{\ell}_{s})\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s+1}(\bm{\ell}_{s},\bm{i}_{s+1})\ldots\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d}(\bm{j}_{d-1},1)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1}(1,\bm{i}_{1})\ldots[\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s+1}](\bm{j}_{s-1},\bm{i}_{s+1})\ldots\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d}(\bm{j}_{d-1},1),

which proves the first part. Let
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}({\{s\}}^{\mathsf{c}})
. Then, using
lemma

12
, we get

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}_{\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overline{\bm{\rho}\bm{\ell}}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\overline{\bm{r}\bm{k}}}(\{s\})}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{(\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindJoin\overline{\bm{\rho}\bm{\ell}})\PindCat(\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}}\PindJoin\overline{\bm{\rho}\bm{\ell}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\rho}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\{s\})}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\{s\})}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\overline{(\bm{\alpha}\PindJoin\bm{\rho})(\bm{i}\PindJoin\bm{\ell})}\PindCat\overline{(\bm{\beta}\PindJoin\bm{\rho})(\bm{j}\PindJoin\bm{\ell})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\rho}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\{s\})}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\{s\})}\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{(\bm{i}\PindJoin\bm{\ell})\PindCat(\bm{j}\PindJoin\bm{\ell})}\prod_{t=1}^{d-1}\delta_{(\bm{\alpha}\PindJoin\bm{\rho})_{t},(\bm{\beta}\PindJoin\bm{\rho})_{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\rho}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\{s\})}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod_{t\neq s}\delta_{\bm{\alpha}_{t},\bm{\beta}_{t}}=r_{s}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle\{s\}\rangle}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod_{t\neq s}\delta_{\bm{\alpha}_{t},\bm{\beta}_{t}}.

As a consequence of
lemmas

14
and
19
, we deduce that partial traces preserve the inherent structure of the arrays
\Phi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
and
\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
.

Corollary 20
.

For every
\varnothing\neq\Omega\subseteq[d-1]
,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}=\Phi_{\Omega}(\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1}),\quad\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}=\Psi_{\Omega}(\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{|\Omega|+1})\cdot\prod\nolimits_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega},

where
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{t}=\prod_{s=s_{t}}^{s_{t+1}-1}\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}
with
1=s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{|\Omega|+2}=d+1
.

Proof.

Apply
lemma

19
successively for each
\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}
. This partitions the set
[d]
into
|\Omega|+1
intervals
[s_{t},s_{t+1}-1]
. At each step, the interval to which
\omega
belongs is joined with the neighboring interval on its right.

This leads us to the next step: estimating the
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
norms of such partial arrays.

Lemma 21
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{C}}=\Phi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
and
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
. For every
\varnothing\neq\Omega\subseteq[d-1]
and
\pi\in\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}(\Omega\oplus\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}

\displaystyle\leq\prod\nolimits_{s=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\|_{\mathrm{F}},\quad\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\left(\prod\nolimits_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\right)\left(\prod\nolimits_{\omega\in\Omega}r_{\omega}^{\theta_{\pi,\omega}}\right).

Proof.

For the first bound, we use
eq.

10
,
corollary

20
, and the submultiplicativity of the Frobenius norm for matrices:

\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\prod\nolimits_{t=1}^{|\Omega|+1}\|\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{W}}}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\prod\nolimits_{s=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{s}\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

Next, let
\pi=\{\mho_{\tau}\}_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}
. By definition, the norm
\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}
is equal to

\sup\Bigg\{\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\Omega)}\sum_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega)}\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}_{\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}_{(\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\beta}\bm{j}})_{\mho_{\tau}}}:\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\overline{\bm{r}\bm{k}}}(\mho_{\tau}),~\|\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=1\Bigg\}.

According to
corollary

20
and the definition of
\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\cdot\sup_{\{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\}}\left[\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega)}\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\Omega)}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}_{(\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{i}}\PindCat\overline{\bm{\alpha}\bm{j}})_{\mho_{\tau}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\cdot\sup_{\{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\}}\left[\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega)}\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}(\Omega)}\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}_{\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}[\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}]^{\{\bm{\alpha}\PindCat\bm{\alpha}\}_{\mho_{\tau}}}_{(\bm{i}\PindCat\bm{j})_{\mho_{\tau}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\cdot\sup_{\{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\}}\left[\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}\|[\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}]^{\{\bm{\alpha}\PindCat\bm{\alpha}\}_{\mho_{\tau}}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right].

Let
\Omega_{\tau}=(\mho_{\tau}\cup(\mho_{\tau}-N))\cap\Omega
and define
\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega_{\tau})
by
\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{\alpha}}=\|[\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}]^{\{\bm{\alpha}\PindCat\bm{\alpha}\}_{\mho_{\tau}}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
for
\alpha\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega_{\tau})
. Then we can apply
lemma

16
to these auxiliary partial arrays:

\displaystyle\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\prod_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\cdot\sup_{\{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\}}\left[\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}(\Omega)}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{(\tau)}_{\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega_{\tau}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\langle{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}\rangle}\|_{\pi}\prod_{\omega\in{\Omega}^{\mathsf{c}}}r_{\omega}\cdot\sup_{\{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\}}\left[\prod_{\omega\in\Omega}r_{\omega}^{\theta_{\pi,\omega}}\prod_{\tau=1}^{\kappa}\|\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right].

Finally, note that
\|\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\|\bm{\mathcal{Z}}^{(\tau)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=1
.

Use
lemma

21
and the definition of
\Theta_{\pi}
in

.

2.8
Moment and tail bounds

Theorem 23
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Let
\{\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}\}_{s=1}^{d-1}
be random matrices of size
\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{s}\times r_{s}}
with independent sub-Gaussian entries satisfying

\mathbb{E}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{i}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})=0,\quad\mathbb{E}|\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{i}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})|^{2}=\eta_{s}^{2},\quad\|\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{s}(\bm{i}_{s},\bm{\alpha}_{s})\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\eta_{s}L

for some constants
\eta_{s}>0
and
L\geq 1
and all
\bm{i}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{k}}
,
\bm{\alpha}\in\bm{\mathfrak{I}}^{\bm{r}}
. Then there exists an absolute constant
\hat{c}_{d}>0
that depends only on
d
such that the quadratic form
\Delta(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{d-1})

eq.

9
satisfies, for all
p\geq 1
and
r=\max_{s\in[d-1]}r_{s}
, the bounds

\|\Delta-\mathbb{E}\Delta\|_{\mathrm{L}_{p}}\leq\hat{c}_{d}\prod_{s=1}^{d-1}(\eta_{s}L\sqrt{r})^{2}\prod_{t=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{2d-2}\frac{p^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}}{r^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}}.

Proof.

By
lemma

18
, the array of
\Delta
is
\bm{\mathcal{D}}=\Psi_{[d-1]}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{d})
. Combining
theorems

15
and
22
, we obtain, with
\Lambda=\prod_{s=1}^{d-1}(\eta_{s}L\sqrt{r})^{2}\prod_{t=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
,

\|\Delta-\mathbb{E}\Delta\|_{\mathrm{L}_{p}}\leq\mathring{c}_{d-1}\Lambda\sum_{\varnothing\neq\Omega\subseteq[d-1]}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{2|\Omega|}p^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}\sum_{\pi\in\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}(\Omega\oplus\Omega)}r^{\Theta_{\pi}-|\Omega|}.

lemma

17
provides an upper bound on
\Theta_{\pi}
so that

\displaystyle\|\Delta-\mathbb{E}\Delta\|_{\mathrm{L}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathring{c}_{d-1}\Lambda\sum_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{\kappa=1}^{|\Omega|}\left[\sum_{\pi\in\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}}\frac{p^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}}{r^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}}\right]+\frac{p^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}}{r^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}}\sum_{\tau=1}^{|\Omega|}\left[\sum_{\pi\in\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{|\Omega|+\tau}}\frac{p^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}{r^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}_{d}\Lambda\sum_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}}\left(\sum_{\kappa=1}^{|\Omega|}p^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}+p^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}\sum_{\tau=1}^{|\Omega|}\frac{p^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}{r^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}\right)

with
\tilde{c}_{d}=\mathring{c}_{d-1}\cdot\max_{\kappa\in[2d-2]}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2d-2}{\kappa}
. Here,
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2d-2}{\kappa}
are Stirling’s numbers of the second kind and
|\bm{\mathfrak{P}}_{\kappa}(\Omega\oplus\Omega)|=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2|\Omega|}{\kappa}\leq\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2d-2}{\kappa}
. The expression under the outer sum depends only on
|\Omega|
, and we can rewrite it
in terms of the binomial coefficients:

\displaystyle\|\Delta-\mathbb{E}\Delta\|_{\mathrm{L}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\check{c}_{d}\Lambda\sum_{m=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{m}{2}}}\left(\sum_{\kappa=1}^{m}p^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}+p^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\tau=1}^{m}\frac{p^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}{r^{\frac{\tau}{2}}}\right)

with
\check{c}_{d}=\tilde{c}_{d}\cdot\max_{m\in[d-1]}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{d-1}{m}

are not assumed to possess any specific structure. If we request them to have low-rank factorizations themselves, the quality of approximation should deteriorate. It follows from

\mu_{<}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{<t>})\leq\prod_{s=1}^{t}\mu_{\mathrm{F},<}^{(s)}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})

\mu_{>}(\bm{\mathrm{A}}^{<t>})\leq\prod_{s=t+1}^{d}\mu_{\mathrm{F},>}^{(s)}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})
.
The gap in these inequalities and the

4
Numerical experiments

The numerical computation of optimal low-rank approximation in the maximum norm is substantially more challenging than in the Frobenius norm. The problem is known to be NP-hard even in the simplest case of rank-one matrix approximation
[
13
]
: the algorithms with provable convergence to the global minimizer combine alternating minimization with an exhaustive search over the sign patterns of the initial condition
[
28
]
. Instead of computing the optimal entrywise approximation error, we rely on a simple
heuristic
algorithm to bound it from above.

4.1
Algorithm

The method of alternating projections is designed to find a point in the intersection of two sets by computing successive Euclidean projections:

\Pi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})=\Big\{\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\in\mathbb{Y}:\|\bm{\mathcal{Y}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\inf_{\bm{\mathcal{Y^{\prime}}}\in\mathbb{Y}}\|\bm{\mathcal{Y^{\prime}}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big\},\quad\mathbb{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}.

Our two sets are low-rank tensors and tensors close to
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
in the maximum norm:

\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}=\{\bm{\mathcal{X}}:\mathrm{rank_{TT}}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})=\bm{r}\},\quad\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})=\{\bm{\mathcal{Y}}:\|\bm{\mathcal{A}}-\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\|_{\max}\leq\varepsilon\}.

Starting from
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}
, the algorithm computes successive Euclidean projections

\bm{\mathcal{Y}}_{t+1}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t}),\quad\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t+1}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}}(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}_{t+1}),\quad t=0,1,2,\ldots,

and locally converges to
\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})
if the intersection is nonempty. In
[
3
, section 7.5]
and
[
4
]
, alternating projections were combined with a binary search over
\varepsilon
to obtain an upper bound on the optimal entrywise approximation error.

The Euclidean projections minimize the Frobenius norm, making each step of the algorithm tractable. The Euclidean projection onto
\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})
can be computed as
\Pi_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})=\{\bm{\mathcal{X}}+\Pi_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(0)}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{A}})\}
and amounts to clipping the entries of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{A}}
whose absolute value exceeds
\varepsilon
. The Euclidean projection onto
\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}
is given by the truncated SVD for matrices, but is not computable for tensors. However, the TT-SVD algorithm achieves quasioptimal approximation
[
29
]
, which motivated the use of quasioptimal alternating projections in
[
35
,
3
]
with TT-SVD in place of
\Pi_{\mathbb{M}_{\bm{r}}}(\cdot)
.

4.3
Numerical results

In
fig.

1
, we study how the entrywise approximation error depends on the approximation rank
r
. The rank bounds of
theorems

2
and
6
suggest the decay rate
r^{-1/2}
, but then the error must drop to zero as
r\to n
. We observe the rate
\mathcal{O}((n-r)^{\alpha}r^{-\beta})
with
\alpha,\beta>0
for identity tensors and random uniform matrices, but have no solid theory to explain this behavior. For random tensors, the rate is better described as
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{polylog}(n-r))
, and we suppose that
\mathcal{O}((n-r)^{\alpha}r^{-\beta})
could be detected with larger
n
and
r
.

Note how close the error curves are for identity matrices and identity tensors—this is a manifestation of the fact that the TT factorization quasinorm (
4
) of identity tensors is bounded by one for all
n
and
d
. This is not the case for random tensors: the increase of the order of the tensor significantly impacts the approximation error.

In
fig.

Discussion

theorem

6
provides a new a priori bound on the entrywise approximation error achievable with tensor networks. While we focused on the TT format—a tensor network with the simplest linear structure—our argument can be applied to more general tree tensor networks
[
15
,
14
,
41
,
22
]
. Several modifications are, of course, in order. First, the analogue of
\gamma_{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{TT}}

eq.

4
needs to take into account the cores that depend on the “inner” indices, but not on the “outer” indices. For example, the appropriate formula for the Tucker factorization
[
21
]

\bm{\mathcal{A}}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}}\bm{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{1}(i_{1},\alpha_{1})\ldots\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{d}(i_{d},\alpha_{d})

would look like
\gamma^{\mathrm{Tucker}}(\bm{\mathcal{A}})=\inf\{\|\bm{\mathcal{G}}\|_{\mathrm{sp}}\prod_{s=1}^{d}\|\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{s}\|_{2,\infty}\}
and involve the spectral
tensor
norm
[
40
]
. Second, the analogues of
\Phi_{\Omega}
,
\Psi_{\Omega}
, and
lemma

19
would reflect the graph structure of the tensor network with partial traces corresponding to edge contractions.

theorem

6
is most suitable for the analysis of families of tensors whose TT factorization quasinorm
\gamma_{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{TT}}
can be uniformly bounded with respect to the size of the tensor. The family of identity tensors in
example

9
and function-generated tensors of
[
5
]
are illustrative examples. The TT factorization quasinorm could be of interest on its own with potential applications in tensor completion (cf.
[
12
,
16
,
9
]
).

Taylor’s series expansions for real powers of functions containing squares of inverse (hyperbolic) cosine functions, explicit formulas for special partial Bell polynomials, and series representations for powers of circular constant Thanks: This paper was typeset usingAmS- L a T e X
Feng Qi

Abstract
. In the paper, by virtue of expansions of two finite products of finitely many square sums, with the aid of series expansions of composite functions of (hyperbolic) sine and cosine functions with inverse sine and cosine functions, and in the light of properties of partial Bell polynomials, the author establishes Taylor’s series expansions of real powers of two functions containing squares of inverse (hyperbolic) cosine functions in terms of the Stirling numbers of the first kind, presents an explicit formula of specific partial Bell polynomials at a sequence of derivatives of a function containing the square of inverse cosine function, derives several combinatorial identities involving the Stirling numbers of the first kind, demonstrates several series representations of the circular constant Pi and its real powers, recovers series expansions of positive integer powers of inverse (hyperbolic) sine functions in terms of the Stirling numbers of the first kind, and also deduces other useful, meaningful, and significant conclusions.

1.
Simple preliminaries

In this paper, we use the notation

\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dotsc\},\quad\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dotsc\},\quad\mathbb{N}_{-}=\{-1,-2,\dotsc\},\quad\mathbb{Z}=\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dotsc\}.

The classical Euler gamma function
\Gamma(z)
can be defined
[
37
, Chapter 3]
by

\Gamma(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!n^{z}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n}(z+k)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dotsc\}.

The rising factorial, or say, the Pochhammer symbol, of
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
is defined
[
11
, p. 7497]
by

(\beta)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\beta+k)=\begin{cases}\beta(\beta+1)\dotsm(\beta+n-1),&n\in\mathbb{N};\\
1,&n=0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha+\beta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dotsc\}
, extended Pochhammer symbol
(\beta)_{\alpha}
is defined
[
12
]
by

(\beta)_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\beta)}.

(1.2)

The falling factorial for
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
is defined by

\langle\beta\rangle_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\beta-k)=\begin{cases}\beta(\beta-1)\dotsm(\beta-n+1),&n\in\mathbb{N};\\
1,&n=0.\end{cases}

The extended binomial coefficient
\binom{z}{w}
is defined
[
39
]
by

\binom{z}{w}=\begin{cases}\frac{\Gamma(z+1)}{\Gamma(w+1)\Gamma(z-w+1)},&z\not\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad w,z-w\not\in\mathbb{N}_{-};\\
0,&z\not\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad w\in\mathbb{N}_{-}\text{ or }z-w\in\mathbb{N}_{-};\\
\frac{\langle z\rangle_{w}}{w!},&z\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad w\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\\
\frac{\langle z\rangle_{z-w}}{(z-w)!},&z,w\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad z-w\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\\
0,&z,w\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad z-w\in\mathbb{N}_{-};\\
\infty,&z\in\mathbb{N}_{-},\quad w\not\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

The Stirling numbers of the first kind
s(n,k)
for
n\geq k\geq 0
can be generated
[
37
, p. 20, (1.30)]
by

\frac{[\ln(1+x)]^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}s(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!},\quad|x|<1

and satisfy diagonal recursive relations

\frac{s(n+k,k)}{\binom{n+k}{k}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^{\ell}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{\ell}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{m}\binom{\ell}{m}\frac{s(n+m,m)}{\binom{n+m}{m}}

and

\displaystyle s(n,k)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{n}(-1)^{m}\sum_{\ell=k-m}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k-m}s(n-\ell,k-\ell)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{\ell-1}{k-n-1}s(n-\ell,k-\ell)

in
[
24
, p. 23, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
28
, p. 156, Theorem 4]
.

The partial Bell polynomials, or say, the Bell polynomials of the second kind, can be denoted and defined by

\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n-k+1\\
\ell_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}i\ell_{i}=n\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\biggl(\frac{x_{i}}{i!}\biggr)^{\ell_{i}}.

in
[
7
, p. 412, Definition 11.2]
and
[
8
, p. 134, Theorem A]
.

3.
Important lemmas

For attaining our aims mentioned just now, we need the following four important lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j+1}^{2k+1}\binom{\ell}{2j+1}s(2k+1,\ell)k^{\ell-2j-1}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

(3.1)

and

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}(\ell+1)s(2k+1,\ell+1)k^{\ell}=(-1)^{k}(k!)^{2}.

(3.2)

For
0\leq j\leq k-1
, we have

\sum_{\ell=2j+1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j}=-s(2k-1,2j).

(3.3)

Proof.

In
[
35
, p. 165, (12.1)]
, there exists the formula

n!\binom{z}{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}s(n,k)z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\quad n\geq 0.

(3.4)

It is not difficult to verify that

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl[(\ell-1)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\frac{(\ti\alpha)_{k}}{(\ti\alpha+1)_{-k}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\ti\alpha\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha+k)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha-k+1)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\ti\alpha(2k-1)!\binom{\ti\alpha+k-1}{2k-1}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\ti=\sqrt{-1}\,
is the imaginary unit and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.
Substituting the formula (
3.4
) into (
3.5
) results in

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\ti\alpha\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k-1}s(2k-1,\ell)(\ti\alpha+k-1)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\ti\alpha\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k-1}s(2k-1,\ell)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}(\ti\alpha)^{j}(k-1)^{\ell-j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr](\ti\alpha)^{j+1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\alpha^{j+1}\cos\frac{(j+1)\pi}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\ti(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\alpha^{j+1}\sin\frac{(j+1)\pi}{2}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where we used the identity

\ti^{k}=\cos\frac{k\pi}{2}+\ti\sin\frac{k\pi}{2},\quad k\geq 0.

(3.6)

As a result, equating the real and imaginary parts, we obtain

\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\cos\frac{(j+1)\pi}{2}\Biggl[s(2k-1,j)+\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\alpha^{j+1}

(3.7)

and

\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\sin\frac{(j+1)\pi}{2}\Biggl[s(2k-1,j)+\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\alpha^{j+1}=0.

(3.8)

Since
\cos(j\pi)=(-1)^{j}
and
\cos\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2}=0
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the equality (
3.7
) can be simplified as (
3.1
).

Taking
\alpha\to 0
in (
3.1
) reduces to (
3.2
).

Since
\sin(j\pi)=0
and
\sin\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2}=(-1)^{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the equality (
3.8
) becomes

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[s(2k-1,2j)+\sum_{\ell=2j+1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j+1}=0.

Further regarding
\alpha
as a variable leads to

(-1)^{j}\Biggl[s(2k-1,2j)+\sum_{\ell=2j+1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j}\Biggr]=0

which is equivalent to the combinatorial identity (
3.3
).
The proof of Lemma
3.1
is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl[(2\ell-1)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr]=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\binom{\ell}{2j}(2k-1)^{\ell-2j}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

(3.9)

and

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}s(2k,\ell)\biggl(k-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{\ell}=(-1)^{k}\biggl[\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}\biggr]^{2}.

(3.10)

For
0\leq j<k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{\ell=2j+1}^{2k}\binom{\ell}{2j+1}s(2k,\ell)\biggl(k-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{\ell}=0.

(3.11)

Proof.

The identities in (
3.5
) can be rearranged as

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{(\ti\alpha)_{k+1}}{\alpha^{2}(\ti\alpha+1)_{-(k+1)}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}\ti\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha+k+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(\ti\alpha-k)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}\ti\frac{(2k+1)!}{\alpha}\binom{\ti\alpha+k}{2k+1}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By this, we acquire

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl[(2\ell-1)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{2k}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}[\alpha^{2}+(2\ell)^{2}]}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{2k}\bigl(\ell^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr)}{4^{k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}[(\alpha/2)^{2}+\ell^{2}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{k}}(-1)^{2k+1}\ti\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha+2k+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(\ti\alpha-2k)}\frac{1}{(-1)^{k+1}\ti}\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2-k)}{2\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{2k+1}}\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2-k)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha-2k)}\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha+2k+1)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k+1)}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Making use of the Gauss multiplication formula

\Gamma(nz)=\frac{n^{nz-1/2}}{(2\pi)^{(n-1)/2}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\Gamma\biggl(z+\frac{k}{n}\biggr)

in
[
1
, p. 256, 6.1.20]
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha-2k)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2-k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2-k)}\frac{2^{\ti\alpha-2k-1/2}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\ti\alpha}{2}-k\biggr)\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\ti\alpha}{2}-k+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ti\alpha-2k-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,}\Gamma\biggl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}-k\biggr)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\ti\alpha+2k+1)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\ti\alpha+2k)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k+1)}\Gamma(2(\ti\alpha/2+k))

\displaystyle=\frac{(\ti\alpha+2k)}{\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k+1)}\frac{2^{2(\ti\alpha/2+k)-1/2}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\Gamma(\ti\alpha/2+k)\Gamma\biggl(\ti\alpha/2+k+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ti\alpha+2k}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,}\Gamma\biggl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}+k\biggr)

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Substituting these two equalities into (
3.12
), utilizing the formula (
3.4
), interchanging the order of double sums, and employing the identity (
3.6
) yield

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\bigl[(2\ell-1)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{2k+1}}\frac{\frac{2^{\ti\alpha+2k}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,}\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}+k\bigr)}{\frac{2^{\ti\alpha-2k-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,}\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}-k\bigr)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}+k\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1+\ti\alpha}{2}-k\bigr)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}(2k)!\binom{\frac{\ti\alpha}{2}+k-\frac{1}{2}}{2k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}s(2k,\ell)\biggl(\frac{\ti\alpha}{2}+k-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}(\ti\alpha)^{j}(2k-1)^{\ell-j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\binom{\ell}{j}(2k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr](\ti\alpha)^{j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\binom{\ell}{j}(2k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\biggl(\cos\frac{j\pi}{2}\biggr)\alpha^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\ti(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\binom{\ell}{j}(2k-1)^{\ell-j}\Biggr]\biggl(\sin\frac{j\pi}{2}\biggr)\alpha^{j}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and

From the facts that

and

for

(z)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}s(n,k)z^{k}.

(3.13)

Comparing this with the definition (

k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2m)}{(2j+2m)!}=0,\quad 1\leq m<k.

(5.2)

Accordingly, we derive

\displaystyle\bell_{n+k,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n+1})=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}

\displaystyle\times\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td x^{n}}\Biggl(\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}2^{m}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2m)}{(2j+2m)!}\Biggr](x-1)^{m-k}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=(-2)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td x^{n}}\Biggl(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{m}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2m+2k)}{(2j+2m+2k)!}\Biggr](x-1)^{m}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=(-2)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}2^{m}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2m+2k)}{(2j+2m+2k)!}\Biggr]\langle m\rangle_{n}(x-1)^{m-n}

\displaystyle=(-2)^{k}(2n)!!\binom{n+k}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2n+2k)}{(2j+2n+2k)!}

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}
. Replacing
n+k
by
m
results in

\bell_{m,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{m-k+1})=(-2)^{k}[2(m-k)]!!\binom{m}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(2j)!\binom{k}{j}\frac{Q(2j,2m)}{(2j+2m)!}

for
m\geq k\in\mathbb{N}
. The required result is thus proved.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\alpha}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\frac{\langle\alpha\rangle_{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell)!\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{Q(2\ell,2n)}{(2\ell+2n)!}\Biggr][2(x-1)]^{n}.

(5.3)

Proof.

By virtue of the Faà di Bruno formula (
3.32
) and the formula (
5.1
) in Theorem
5.1
, we obtain

\biggl(\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\alpha}\biggr)^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\alpha\rangle_{j}\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\alpha-j}\bell_{n,j}\biggl(\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\prime},\dotsc,\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{(n-j+1)}\biggr)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the limit
x\to 1^{-}
and employing (
5.1
) in Theorem
5.1
lead to

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\biggl(\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\alpha}\biggr)^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\alpha\rangle_{j}\bell_{n,j}\biggl(\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{\prime},\dotsc,\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\biggl[\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\biggr]^{(n-j+1)}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\alpha\rangle_{j}(-2)^{j}[2(n-j)]!!\binom{n}{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell)!\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{Q(2\ell,2n)}{(2\ell+2n)!}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the required result (
5.3
) is proved.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell)!\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{Q(2\ell,2n)}{(2\ell+2n)!}=(2k)!\frac{Q(2k,2n)}{(2k+2n)!}.

(5.4)

Proof.

This follows from letting
\alpha=k\in\mathbb{N}
in (
5.3
) and equating coefficients of factors
(x-1)^{n}
in (
4.1
).
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{9}\biggr)^{\alpha}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\frac{\langle\alpha\rangle_{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell)!\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{Q(2\ell,2n)}{(2\ell+2n)!}.

(5.5)

Proof.

This follows from setting
x=\frac{1}{2}
in (
5.3
).
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The formula (
5.1
) in Theorem
5.1
can be used to compute Taylor’s series expansions of functions like
f\bigl(\frac{(\arccos x)^{2}}{2(1-x)}\bigr)
around the point
x=1^{-}
, only if all the derivatives of
f(x)
at
x=1^{-}
are explicitly computable.

6.
Recovering Maclaurin’s series expansion of
\bigl(\frac{\arcsin x}{x}\bigr)^{k}

In this section, by virtue of some conclusions in Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.3
, we recover Maclaurin’s series expansions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) in Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

It is easy to see that

\cosh t=\frac{\te^{t}+\te^{-t}}{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Then, making use of the identity (
3.1
) in Lemma
3.1
, the series expansion (
3.16
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\arcsin x)^{2k}}{(2k)!}\alpha^{2k}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}4^{k-1}\Biggl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\biggl[\ell^{2}+\biggl(\frac{\alpha}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr]\Biggr)\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\alpha^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-4)^{k-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{4^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j-1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j-1}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j+1}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-4)^{k-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}\ell s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-1}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}\sum_{j=2}^{k}(-4)^{k-j}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j-1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j-1}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j+1}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-4)^{k-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}(-1)^{k-1}[(k-1)!]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}(-4)^{k-j}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j-1}^{2k-1}\binom{\ell}{2j-1}s(2k-1,\ell)(k-1)^{\ell-2j+1}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}[(2k-2)!!]^{2}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2m}}{(2m)!}(-4)^{m-k}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2k-1}^{2m-1}\binom{\ell}{2k-1}s(2m-1,\ell)(m-1)^{\ell-2k+1}\Biggr]\alpha^{2k},

where we used the identity (
3.2
) or (
3.14
). Regarding
\alpha
as a variable and equating coefficients of
\alpha^{2k}
arrive at

\frac{(\arcsin x)^{2}}{2}=\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}[(2k-2)!!]^{2}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-2)!!}{(2k-1)!!}\frac{x^{2k}}{k}

(6.1)

and

\begin{split}\frac{(\arcsin x)^{2k}}{(2k)!}&=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}(-4)^{m-k}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2k-1}^{2m-1}\binom{\ell}{2k-1}s(2m-1,\ell)(m-1)^{\ell-2k+1}\Biggr]\frac{x^{2m}}{(2m)!}\\
&=\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\binom{2k+2m}{2k}}Q(2k,2m)\frac{(2x)^{2m}}{(2m)!}\end{split}

(6.2)

for
k\geq 2
.

Making use of the series expansion (
3.17
) and the identity (
3.9
) in Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\arcsin x)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\alpha^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell}}\binom{\ell}{2j}(2k-1)^{\ell-2j}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{k=\lceil{j/2}\rceil}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(2x)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sum_{\ell=2j}^{2k}\frac{s(2k,\ell)}{2^{\ell+1}}\binom{\ell}{2j}(2k-1)^{\ell-2j}\Biggr]\alpha^{2j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\Biggl[\sum_{m=\lceil{k/2}\rceil}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{(2x)^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}\sum_{\ell=2k}^{2m}\frac{s(2m,\ell)}{2^{\ell+1}}\binom{\ell}{2k}(2m-1)^{\ell-2k}\Biggr]\alpha^{2k+1},

where we used the identity (
3.10
) or (
3.15
) and
\lceil x\rceil
stands for the ceiling function which gives the smallest integer not less than
x
. Regarding
\alpha
as a variable and equating coefficients of
\alpha^{2k+1}
reduce to

\begin{split}\frac{(\arcsin x)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}&=(-1)^{k}\sum_{m=\lceil{k/2}\rceil}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=2k}^{2m}\frac{s(2m,\ell)}{2^{\ell+1}}\binom{\ell}{2k}(2m-1)^{\ell-2k}\Biggr]\frac{(2x)^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}\\
&=\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\binom{2k+2m+1}{2k+1}}Q(2k+1,2m)\frac{(2x)^{2m}}{(2m)!}.\end{split}

(6.3)

Combining the series expansions (
6.1
), (
6.2
), and (
6.3
) leads to the series expansion (
2.1
).

By similar arguments as above, from the series expansions (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), we can recover series expansion (
2.1
) once again.

Utilizing the relation
\arcsinh t=-\ti\arcsin(\ti t)
or, equivalently, the relation
\arcsin t=-\ti\arcsinh(\ti t)
, the series expansions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) can be derived from each other.

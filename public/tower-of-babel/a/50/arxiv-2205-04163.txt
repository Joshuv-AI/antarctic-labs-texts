Homological Shifts of Polymatroidal ideals Thanks: .
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. We study the homological shifts of polymatroidal ideals. In our main theorem we prove that the first homological shift ideal of any polymatroidal ideal is again polymatroidal, supporting a conjecture of Bandari, Bayati and Herzog that predicts that all homological shift ideals of a polymatroidal ideal are polymatroidal. As a nice consequence, we recover a result of Bayati which proves this conjecture in the squarefree case.

1.
Introduction

The study of the minimal free resolutions of monomial ideals is a main topic in combinatorial commutative algebra. Let
K
be a field,
I
a monomial ideal of the standard graded polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
{\mathbb{F}}
be its minimal multigraded free
S
-resolution. Then, the free modules in the resolution
{\mathbb{F}}
are of the form
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}(I)}S(-{\bf a}_{i,j})
, where
{\bf a}_{i,j}
are integral vectors with non negative entries, called the
multigraded shifts
of
I
. For an integral vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})
, we let
{\bf x^{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. Using this notation, the
i
th homological shift ideal
of
I
is defined as
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=({\bf x}^{{\bf a}_{i,j}}:j=1,\dots,\beta_{i}(I))
, see
[
15
]
. Note that
\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
and
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)=(0)
for
j>\operatorname{pd}(I)
.

The basic goal of this theory is to understand how much of the homological and combinatorial properties of a monomial ideal
I
are inherited by its homological shift ideals. We refer to any combinatorial or homological property enjoyed by
\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
and by
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
, for all
1\leq j\leq\operatorname{pd}(I)
, as an
homological shift property
of
I
. This rather new trend of research, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
15
,
16
]
, has its origins in a meeting between Somayeh Bandari, Shamila Bayati and Jürgen Herzog that took place in Essen in 2012. Due to experimental evidence, the three authors conjectured that the property of being
polymatroidal
is an homological shift property. For a monomial
u
, we define the
x_{i}
-degree
of
u
as
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:x_{i}^{j}\ \text{divides}\ u\}
. Recall that a
polymatroidal
ideal
I
is an equigenerated monomial ideal of
S
whose minimal generating set
G(I)
corresponds to the base of a
discrete polymatroid
. The bases of a discrete polymatroidal can be characterized in term of the so–called
exchange property
. Thus an ideal
I\subset S
is polymatroidal if and only if the following exchange property holds: for all
u,v\in G(I)
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
, there exists
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
. The Bandari–Bayati–Herzog conjecture is widely open. Until now it is solved only in two cases: for squarefree polymatroidal,
i.e.
,
matroidal ideals

[
3
]
, and for polymatroidal ideals that satisfy the
strong exchange property

[
15
]
.

Polymatroidal ideals are
one of the most distinguished classes of monomial ideals. Indeed, the product of polymatroidal ideals is polymatroidal. Any polymatroidal ideal has linear quotients and thus a linear resolution. Thus, they have
linear powers
,
[
12
, Corollary 12.6.4]
, a rare property among monomial ideals. In this paper our aim is to study the homological shift ideals of polymatroidal ideals. See also
[
1
,
2
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some basic facts on homological shift ideals of equigenerated monomial ideals with linear quotients. Recall that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
has
linear quotients
if for some
admissible order

u_{1}>\dots>u_{m}
of its minimal generating set
G(I)
, the colon ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}
are generated by a subset of the variables, for
k=2,\dots,m
. Quite generally, to determine the ideals
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
for a monomial ideal
I
is difficult. Nonetheless, this is easy for ideals with linear quotients as shown in Proposition
2.2
.

In Section
3
, we prove our main theorem. We are able to show that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
is polymatroidal if
I
is polymatroidal (Theorem
3.2
). This is the first result valid for
all
polymatroidal ideals, that supports Conjecture
3.1
. Our proof is based on Proposition
2.4
, whose main advantage consists in the fact that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
does not depend upon the admissible order of
I
. To study the higher homological shift ideals, firstly we note that for
j\geq 1
,
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\subseteq(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))_{>j+1}
, (Corollary
2.7
), where
J_{>j+1}
is the monomial ideal with
G(J_{>j+1})=\{u\in G(J):|\operatorname{supp}(u)|>j+1\}
as a minimal generating set and
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{i:x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. Unfortunately, equality in the above inclusion does not hold in general, (Example
3.6
). Nonetheless, it holds for matroidal ideals, (Proposition
3.4
). As a consequence Conjecture
3.1
holds for all matroidal ideals, (Corollary
3.5
). It would be of interest to classify all polymatroidal ideals satisfying the equation
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)=(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))_{>j+1}
for all
j<\operatorname{pd}(I)
.

2.
Generalities on homological shift ideals

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
. For a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S
, the vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is called the
multidegree
of
u
. We put
u={\bf x^{a}}
. For
{\bf a}={\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
,
{\bf x^{0}}=1
. Whereas
\deg(u)=a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}
is the
degree
of
u
. Let
G(I)
be the unique minimal monomial generating set of
I
, and
G(I)_{d}=\{u\in G(I):\deg(u)=d\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal, and let
(\mathbb{F},\partial)
be the minimal multigraded free resolution of
I
. The
i
th homological shift ideal
of
I
is defined as

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}\ :\ \beta_{i,{\bf a}}(I)\neq 0).

Here
\beta_{i,{\bf a}}(I)
is a multigraded Betti number.

Clearly
\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
and
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)=(0)
for
j>\operatorname{pd}(I)
. In general, we ask what properties of
I
are inherited by its homological shift ideals.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal with minimal generating set
G(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. We say that
I
has
linear quotients
with respect to the order
u_{1}>\dots>u_{m}
of its minimal generators, if for all
k=2,\dots,m
, the colon ideal
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}
is generated by a subset of the variables,
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Any such ordering of
G(I)
is called an
admissible order
of
I
. Set

\textup{set}(u_{k})\ =\ \{i:x_{i}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}\},

for
k=2,\dots,m
and
\textup{set}(u_{1})=\emptyset
. Furthermore, put
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
if
F
is non empty, and
{\bf x}_{\emptyset}=1
. By
[
18
, Lemma 1.5]
, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear quotients with admissible order
u_{1}>\dots>u_{m}
of
G(I)
. Then,

\textup{HS}_{j}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{F}u\ :\ u\in G(I),\ F\subseteq\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=j).

(1)

Let
u={\bf x^{a}}\in S
, the
x_{i}
-degree
of
u
is
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}=a_{i}
. Given monomials of the same degree
u,v\in S
, the
distance
between
u
and
v
is

d(u,v)\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\big|\deg_{x_{i}}(u)-\deg_{x_{i}}(v)\big|.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u,v
monomials of
S
of the same degree. Then,
u=x_{k}(v/x_{\ell})
for some
k\neq\ell
, if and only if
d(u,v)=1
.

Proof.

Let
v=x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{b_{n}}
, then
u=x_{k}(v/x_{\ell})=(\prod_{i\neq k,\ell}x_{i}^{b_{i}})x_{k}^{b_{k}+1}x_{\ell}^{b_{\ell}-1}
. Note that

\big|\deg_{x_{i}}(u)-\deg_{x_{i}}(v)\big|\ =\ \begin{cases}|b_{\ell}-1-b_{\ell}|&\text{if}\ i=\ell,\\
\hfil|b_{k}\!+1-b_{k}|&\text{if}\ i=k,\\
\hfill 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus
d(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}|\deg_{x_{i}}(u)-\deg_{x_{i}}(v)|=\frac{1}{2}\big(|b_{k}+1-b_{k}|+|b_{\ell}-1-b_{\ell}|\big)=\frac{1}{2}(1+1)=1
.

Conversely, assume
d(u,v)=1
. From the definition of
d(u,v)
it is clear that either
u=x_{k}^{2}v
or
u=x_{k}(v/x_{\ell})
, for some
k\neq\ell
. But the first possibility does not occur, lest
\deg(u)>\deg(v)
. Therefore, the desired conclusion follows.

∎

Combining Lemma
2.3
with
[
15
, Proposition 1.3]
we have

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear quotients. Then

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\ =\ (\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)\ :\ u,v\in G(I),\ d(u,v)=1).

Let
\succ
be a
monomial order
on
S
. Up to a relabeling on the variables, we may assume that
x_{1}\succ\dots\succ x_{n}
. We say that
\succ

is induced by

x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. We say that
I
has linear quotients with respect to
\succ
if
I
has linear quotients with admissible order
u_{1}\succ u_{2}\succ\dots\succ u_{m}
of
G(I)
. A particular monomial order is the
lex order

>_{\operatorname{lex}}
induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Let
{\bf x^{a}},{\bf x^{b}}
be monomials of
S
. Then
{\bf x^{a}}>_{\operatorname{lex}}{\bf x^{b}}
if
a_{1}=b_{1}
,
\dots
,
a_{s-1}=b_{s-1}
and
a_{s}>b_{s}
, for some
1\leq s\leq n
.

We set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For a monomial
u\in S
, we define its
support
as
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{i\in[n]:\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>0\}
and we set
\max(u)=\max\operatorname{supp}(u)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I\subset S
be an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear quotients with respect to
\succ
induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Then, for all
u\in G(I)
,

\textup{set}(u)\ \subseteq\ [\max(u)-1].

Proof.

Indeed, let
G(I)
ordered as
u_{1}\succ\cdots\succ u_{m}
and let
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. If
i\in\textup{set}(u_{j})
, then
x_{i}u_{j}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1})
. Since
\deg(u_{1})=\dots=\deg(u_{j-1})=\deg(u_{j})
, there exists
s\in\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})
,
s\neq i
such that
x_{i}(u_{j}/x_{s})=u_{p}
for some
p\leq j-1
. But
x_{i}(u_{j}/x_{s})=u_{p}\succ u_{j}
. Since
\succ
is a monomial order, this implies that
x_{i}u_{j}\succ x_{s}u_{j}
, thus
x_{i}\succ x_{s}
. Hence
i<s
. But
s\leq\max(u_{j})
and so
i<\max(u_{j})
.

∎

In
[
15
]
, the following general inclusion was shown.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
15
, Proposition 1.4]
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear quotients. Then
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
, for all
j
.

However, in general
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\neq\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
. Let
I=(x_{2}x_{4},x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3})
. Then
I
has linear quotients with admissible order
x_{2}x_{4}>x_{1}x_{2}>x_{1}x_{3}
. We have
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{2}x_{4})
and
\textup{HS}_{2}(I)=(0)
, but
\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I))=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})\neq(0)=\textup{HS}_{2}(I)
.

For a monomial ideal
J\subset S
, let
J_{>\ell}
be the monomial ideal whose minimal generating set is
G(J_{>\ell})=\{u\in G(J):|\operatorname{supp}(u)|>\ell\}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
I\subset S
be an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear quotients with respect to a monomial order
\succ

(
e.g.,
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
)
induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Then,

\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\ \subseteq\ \big(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))\big)_{>j+1}.

Proof.

For
j=0
the assertion is immediate. Let
j>0
. Firstly we show the inclusion
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
. By Proposition
2.4
,

\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))=(\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})\ :\ w_{1},w_{2}\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)),\ d(w_{1},w_{2})=1).

Take
w={\bf x}_{F}u\in G(\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I))
with
u\in G(I)
,
F\subseteq\textup{set}(u)
and
|F|=j+1
. Since
j+1\geq 2
we can find
r,s\in F
,
r\neq s
. Then
w_{1}={\bf x}_{F\setminus\{r\}}u,w_{2}={\bf x}_{F\setminus\{s\}}u\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
and
d(w_{1},w_{2})=1
. Thus
\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})=w\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))
, as desired. It remains to prove that any
w={\bf x}_{F}u\in G(\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I))
has
\operatorname{supp}(w)>j+1
. By Lemma
2.5
we have
F\subseteq\textup{set}(u)\subseteq[\max(u)-1]
. Hence
\max(u)\notin F
and
F\cup\{\max(u)\}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(w)
, and since
|F|=j+1
we have that
\operatorname{supp}(w)\geq|F|+1=j+2
, as desired.

∎

3.
The first homological shift ideal of polymatroidal ideals

Recall that an equigenerated monomial ideal
I\subset S
is a
polymatroidal
ideal if it satisfies the following
exchange property
,

(*)

for all
u,v\in G(I)
and all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
, there exists
j
with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and such that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
.

Such ideals are called
polymatroidal
because their minimal generating set
G(I)
corresponds to the basis of a
discrete polymatroid
, see
[
12
, Chapter 12]
for more information on this subject. For later use, we recall that a polymatroidal ideal satisfies also the following
dual exchange property
,
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
,

(**)

for all
u,v\in G(I)
and all
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
, there exists
i
with
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
and such that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
.

It is expected that the following is true.

Conjecture 3.1
.

(Bandari–Bayati–Herzog),
[
3
,
15
]
.
Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then all homological shift ideals
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
are again polymatroidal, for all
j\geq 0
.

It is known that a polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
. Using the description of
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
given in Proposition
2.4
we can prove

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
is a polymatroidal ideal.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
,

\displaystyle\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ (x_{i}u\ :\ u\in G(I),\ i\in\textup{set}(u))

\displaystyle=\ (\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)\ :\ u,v\in G(I),\ d(u,v)=1).

We must prove the following exchange property,

(*)

for all monomials
u,v\in G(I)
, all integers
k\in\textup{set}(u)
,
\ell\in\textup{set}(v)
such that
u_{1}=x_{k}u\neq x_{\ell}v=v_{1}
and all
i
with
\deg_{x_{i}}(u_{1})>\deg_{x_{i}}(v_{1})
, there exists
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u_{1})<\deg_{x_{j}}(v_{1})
and
x_{j}(u_{1}/x_{i})=x_{j}(x_{k}u)/x_{i}\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I))
.

We may assume that
i
is different both from
k
and
\ell
. Indeed, if
k=i
, then as
k\in\textup{set}(u)
we have
u_{1}=x_{p}z
for some
z\in G(I)\setminus\{u\}
,
p\neq k
, and we may use the element
x_{p}z
with
p\neq k=i
. The same reasoning applies for
\ell
. In particular, since
i\neq k
,
i\neq\ell
and by hypothesis
\deg_{x_{i}}(u_{1})>\deg_{x_{i}}(v_{1})
, we have
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
as well. Since
I
is polymatroidal, the set

\Omega\ =\ \{h\in[n]\setminus\{i\}\ :\ \deg_{x_{h}}(u)<\deg_{x_{h}}(v)\ \text{and}\ x_{h}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)\}.

is non empty. Let
h\in\Omega
and set
w=x_{h}(u/x_{i})
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that
k\in\Omega
. For
h=k\in\Omega
, we have
\deg_{x_{k}}(u)<\deg_{x_{k}}(v)
and
w=x_{k}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
. We distinguish two more cases.

Subcase 1.1.
Assume
w=v
. By hypothesis
v_{1}=x_{\ell}v\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I))
. We show that the property
(*)
is verified for the integer
j=\ell
. Indeed, as
i\neq\ell
and
w=v
,

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{\ell}}(u_{1})

\displaystyle=\deg_{x_{\ell}}(x_{k}u)=\deg_{x_{\ell}}(x_{k}u/x_{i})=\deg_{x_{\ell}}(w)

\displaystyle=\deg_{x_{\ell}}(v)<\deg_{x_{\ell}}(x_{\ell}v),

and
v_{1}=x_{\ell}v=x_{\ell}w=x_{\ell}(x_{k}u)/x_{i}\in\ G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I))
, as desired.

Subcase 1.2.
Assume
w\neq v
. Thus, for some
r
,
\deg_{x_{r}}(w)>\deg_{x_{r}}(v)
. Since
I
is polymatroidal, there exists an integer
m
with
\deg_{x_{m}}(w)<\deg_{x_{m}}(v)
and such that
w_{1}=x_{m}(w/x_{r})\in G(I)
. Clearly
m\neq r
. Hence, Lemma
2.3
implies that
d(w,w_{1})=1
and Proposition
2.4
implies that

\operatorname{lcm}(w,w_{1})=x_{m}w=x_{m}(x_{\ell}u)/x_{i}\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I)).

(2)

It remains to prove that the integer
m
satisfies the first condition of property
(*)
, namely
\deg_{x_{m}}(u_{1})<\deg_{x_{m}}(v_{1})
. First note that
m\neq i
. Lest, if
i=m
, by hypothesis
\deg_{x_{i}}(w)<\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
and then
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\deg_{x_{i}}(w)+1\leq\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
, against the fact that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
. Thus, since
m\neq i
, we have

\deg_{x_{m}}(u)\leq\deg_{x_{m}}(w)<\deg_{x_{m}}(v).

This inequality together with equation (
2
) show that the integer
j=m
satisfies the property
(*)
in such a case.

Case 2.
Suppose that
k\notin\Omega
. Nonetheless, for some
h\in\Omega
, with
h\neq k
, we have
\deg_{x_{h}}(u)<\deg_{x_{h}}(v)
and
w=x_{h}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
. Since
k\in\textup{set}(u)
, there exist
z\in G(I)
,
z\neq u
and
x_{p}\neq x_{k}
such that
u_{1}=x_{k}u=x_{p}z
.

Subcase 2.1.
Suppose
d(w,z)=1
. As
h\in\Omega
but
k\notin\Omega
we have
h\neq k
. Thus, as
w=x_{h}(u/x_{i})
and
z=x_{k}(u/x_{p})
it follows that
p=i
, lest
d(w,z)>1
. Hence
p=i
and Proposition
2.4
implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{lcm}(w,z)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lcm}(x_{h}(u/x_{i}),x_{k}(u/x_{p}))=x_{h}(x_{k}u)/x_{i}\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I)).

Finally, we just need to check that
\deg_{x_{h}}(u_{1})<\deg_{x_{h}}(v_{1})
. Indeed, as
h\neq k
,

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{h}}(x_{k}u)=\deg_{x_{h}}(u)<\deg_{x_{h}}(v)\leq\deg_{x_{h}}(x_{\ell}v).

Subcase 2.2.
Suppose
d(w,z)>1
. Then
p\neq i
, lest
d(w,z)=1
by
Subcase 2.1
. Thus
d(w,z)=d(x_{h}(u/x_{i}),x_{k}(u/x_{p}))=2
,
i\neq h
,
h\neq k
,
k\neq p
,
p\neq i
and

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{i}}(w)

\displaystyle<\deg_{x_{i}}(z),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{h}}(w)

\displaystyle>\deg_{x_{h}}(z),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{k}}(w)

\displaystyle<\deg_{x_{k}}(z),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{p}}(w)

\displaystyle>\deg_{x_{p}}(z).

Moreover, for all
q\neq i,h,k,p
we have
\deg_{x_{q}}(w)=\deg_{x_{q}}(z)
. Since
w,z\in G(I)
and
\deg_{x_{i}}(z)>\deg_{x_{i}}(w)
we have
z_{1}=x_{h}(z/x_{i})\in G(I)
or
z_{2}=x_{p}(z/x_{i})\in G(I)
. We distinguish two more cases.

Subcase 2.2.1.
Suppose
z_{1}=x_{h}(z/x_{i})\in G(I)
. Note that

x_{p}(z_{1}/x_{k})=x_{p}(x_{h}(z/x_{i}))/x_{k}=x_{p}x_{h}x_{k}((u/x_{p})/x_{i})/x_{k}=x_{h}(u/x_{i})=w.

Since
k\neq p
, Lemma
2.3
implies that
d(z_{1},w)=1
. Thus, by Proposition
2.4

\displaystyle\operatorname{lcm}(z_{1},w)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lcm}(x_{h}(z/x_{i}),x_{h}(u/x_{i}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lcm}(x_{h}(x_{k}(u/x_{p})/x_{i}),x_{h}(u/x_{i}))

\displaystyle=x_{h}(x_{k}u)/x_{i}\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I)),

and the property
(*)
is satisfied as
h\in\Omega
, that is
\deg_{x_{h}}(u)<\deg_{x_{h}}(v)
and as
h\neq k
, we have
\deg_{x_{h}}(u_{1})=\deg_{x_{h}}(x_{k}u)=\deg_{x_{h}}(u)<\deg_{x_{h}}(v)\leq\deg_{x_{h}}(x_{\ell}v)=\deg_{x_{h}}(v_{1})
.

Subcase 2.2.2.
Suppose
z_{2}=x_{p}(z/x_{i})\in G(I)
. Note that

\displaystyle z_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{p}(z/x_{i})=x_{p}(x_{k}(u/x_{p})/x_{i})=x_{k}(u/x_{i})

and
d(z_{2},w)=1
. Thus, Proposition
2.4
implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{lcm}(z_{2},w)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lcm}(x_{k}(u/x_{i}),x_{h}(u/x_{i}))=x_{h}(x_{k}u)/x_{i}\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(I)),

and as before
\deg_{x_{h}}(u_{1})<\deg_{x_{h}}(v_{1})
. The proof is complete.

∎

By
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
, a polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
(induced by any ordering of the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
). Thus Corollary
2.7
implies that for all
j\geq 0
,

\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\ \subseteq\ \big(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))\big)_{>j+1}.

Next we study when equality holds. This is the case when
I
is actually matroidal, that is, a squarefree polymatroidal ideal
[
3
, Corollary 2.3]
. The proof in
[
3
]
uses matroid and graph theory. We provide a purely algebraic proof. Firstly, we note the following general fact.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree ideal. Then, for all
j\geq 0
,

\big(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))\big)_{>j+1}\ =\ \textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)).

Proof.

Since
I
is squarefree, all
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
are squarefree. It follows from
[
14
, Lemma 4.4]
that all monomials
w\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
have
|\operatorname{supp}(w)|>j
.

It is clear that
(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))_{>j+1}\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
. We show the opposite inclusion. Let
y\in G(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))
. We claim that
|\operatorname{supp}(y)|>j+1
. By Proposition
2.4
,
y=\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})
with
w_{1},w_{2}\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
such that
d(w_{1},w_{2})=1
. By Lemma
2.3
,
w_{1}=x_{k}(w_{2}/x_{\ell})
for some
k\neq\ell
. Thus
y=\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})=x_{\ell}w_{1}
. We have shown that
w_{1}\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
has
|\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})|\geq j+1
. Since
\ell\notin\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})
,
|\operatorname{supp}(y)|=1+|\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})|\geq j+2>j+1
, as desired.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a matroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)=\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
for all
j<\operatorname{pd}(I)
.

Proof.

Since
I
is squarefree, all homological shift ideals involved in the proof are squarefree. Fix the lex order
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Then
I
has linear quotients with respect to
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
,
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
. For
u\in G(I)
, we denote by
\textup{set}(u)
the following set
\{i:x_{i}\in(v\in G(I):v>_{\operatorname{lex}}u):u\}
. By equation (
1
),

\displaystyle\textup{HS}_{j}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{F}u\ :\ u\in G(I),\ F\subseteq\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=j).

(3)

For
j=0
, there is nothing to prove. Let
1\leq j<\operatorname{pd}(I)
. By Proposition
2.6
we have
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
. So we only need to prove that
\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))\subseteq\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)
. By Proposition
2.4
, we have

\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))=(\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})\ :\ w_{1},w_{2}\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)),\ d(w_{1},w_{2})=1).

Thus, we must show that for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in G(\textup{HS}_{j}(I))
with
d(w_{1},w_{2})=1
we have
\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})\in G(\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I))
. By equation (
3
),
w_{1}={\bf x}_{F}u\neq w_{2}={\bf x}_{G}v
with
u,v\in G(I)
,
F\subseteq\textup{set}(u)
,
G\subseteq\textup{set}(v)
and
|F|=|G|=j
. Since
d(w_{1},w_{2})=1
, Lemma
2.3
gives
w_{1}=x_{k}(w_{2}/x_{\ell})
for some
k\neq\ell
. As observed before,
w_{1},w_{2}
are squarefree. Hence,
\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})=\{k\}\cup(\operatorname{supp}(w_{2})\setminus\{\ell\})
. Note that as
\ell\in\operatorname{supp}(w_{2})
, we can find
z\in G(I)
such that
\ell\in\operatorname{supp}(z)
. Indeed if
\ell\in\operatorname{supp}(v)
then we can choose
z=v
. Otherwise,
\ell\in G\subseteq\textup{set}(v)
, and
x_{\ell}v=x_{s}z
with
z\in G(I)\setminus\{v\}
,
s\neq\ell
and so
\ell\in\operatorname{supp}(z)
.

Since
\ell\in\operatorname{supp}(z)\setminus\operatorname{supp}(u)
it is
\deg_{x_{\ell}}(z)>\deg_{x_{\ell}}(u)
. By the dual exchange property
(**)
, the set of integers
h
such that
\deg_{x_{h}}(u)>\deg_{x_{h}}(z)
and
x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})\in G(I)
is non empty. Hence, the following set is non empty too

\Omega\ =\ \{h\in[n]\setminus\{\ell\}\ :\ x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})\in G(I)\}.

Case 1.
Assume there exists
h\in\Omega
with
h>\ell
. Then
x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})>_{\operatorname{lex}}u
and
\ell\in\textup{set}(u)
. Now
\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})=\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},x_{\ell}(w_{1}/x_{k}))=x_{\ell}w_{1}=x_{\ell}{\bf x}_{F}u
. Since
\ell\notin\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})
, we also have
\ell\notin F
. Since
\ell\in\textup{set}(u)
, we have that
F\cup\{\ell\}
is a subset of
\textup{set}(u)
having cardinality
j+1
and
\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})={\bf x}_{F\cup\{\ell\}}u\in G(\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I))
by equation (
1
), as desired.

Case 2.
Assume that for all
h\in\Omega
we have
h<\ell
. Choose some
h\in\Omega
. Then,
u>_{\operatorname{lex}}x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})\in G(I)
. Set
w=x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})
. Hence, this time
h\in\textup{set}(w)
. Note that
h\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
and since
w_{1}={\bf x}_{F}u
is squarefree,
h\notin F
. We are going to show that
F\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Hence, we will have
F\cup\{h\}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
and then the desired conclusion:

\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},w_{2})=x_{\ell}{\bf x}_{F}u=x_{h}{\bf x}_{F}(x_{\ell}(u/x_{h}))={\bf x}_{F\cup\{h\}}w\in G(\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)).

Let
m\in F
, then for some
p\neq m
,
x_{m}(u/x_{p})\in G(I)
and
x_{m}(u/x_{p})>_{\operatorname{lex}}u
. So
m<p
.

Subcase 2.1.
Let
p=h
. Then
\ell>h=p>m
. Hence
x_{m}(u/x_{p})=x_{m}(u/x_{h})>_{\operatorname{lex}}x_{\ell}(u/x_{h})=w
. Whence,
m\in\textup{set}(w)
in this case, as desired.

Subcase 2.2.
Let
p\neq h
. Then
d(x_{m}(u/x_{p}),w)=d(x_{m}(u/x_{p}),x_{\ell}(u/x_{h}))=2
,
h\neq m
,
h\neq\ell
,
\ell\neq m
,
p\neq h
,
p\neq m
, and

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{h}}(w)

\displaystyle<\deg_{x_{h}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p})),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{\ell}}(w)

\displaystyle>\deg_{x_{\ell}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p})),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{m}}(w)

\displaystyle<\deg_{x_{m}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p})),

\displaystyle\deg_{x_{p}}(w)

\displaystyle>\deg_{x_{p}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p})).

Whereas, for all
q\neq h,\ell,m,p
we have
\deg_{x_{q}}(w)=\deg_{x_{q}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p}))
. Since
x_{m}(u/x_{p})
,
w\in G(I)
and
\deg_{x_{m}}(x_{m}(u/x_{p}))>\deg_{x_{m}}(w)
, by the dual exchange property
(**)
we have either
x_{m}(w/x_{\ell})\in G(I)
or
x_{m}(w/x_{p})\in G(I)
.

Subcase 2.2.1.
Assume
x_{m}(w/x_{\ell})\in G(I)
. Note that

x_{m}(w/x_{\ell})=x_{m}(x_{\ell}(u/x_{h}))/x_{\ell}=x_{m}(u/x_{h})\in G(I).

Thus
m\in\Omega
and by assumption
m<\ell
. Hence
x_{m}(w/x_{\ell})>_{\operatorname{lex}}w
and so
m\in\textup{set}(w)
.

Subcase 2.2.2.
Assume
x_{m}(w/x_{p})\in G(I)
. In this case, since
m<p
we have
x_{m}(w/x_{p})>_{\operatorname{lex}}w
, and again
m\in\textup{set}(w)
. The proof is complete

∎

3.4
and Theorem
3.2

3.1

[

Corollary 3.5
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
Let
I\subset S
be a matroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
is again a matroidal ideal for all
j
.

Unfortunately, in general we could have
\textup{HS}_{j+1}(I)\neq(\textup{HS}_{1}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I)))_{>j+1}
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
I=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{5}]
. Using the
Macaulay2

\textup{HS}_{j}(I)

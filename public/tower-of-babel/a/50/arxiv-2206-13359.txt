Abstract
. Let 𝐤 \mathbf{k} be an algebraically closed field of characteristic ≥ 7 \geq 7 or zero. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a tame order of global dimension 2 2 over a normal surface X X over 𝐤 \mathbf{k} such that Z ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) = 𝒪 X \operatorname{Z}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{O}_{X} is locally a direct summand of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . We prove that there is a μ N \mu_{N} -gerbe 𝒳 \mathcal{X} over a smooth tame algebraic stack whose generic stabilizer is trivial, with coarse space X X such that the category of 1-twisted coherent sheaves on 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves of modules on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . Moreover, the stack 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is constructed explicitly through a sequence of root stacks, canonical stacks, and gerbes. This extends results of Reiten and Van den Bergh to finite characteristic and the global situation. As applications, in characteristic 0 0 we prove that such orders are geometric noncommutative schemes in the sense of Orlov, and we study relations with Hochschild cohomology and Connes’ convolution algebra.



1.
Introduction

Noncommutative algebraic geometry is a burgeoning field connecting noncommutative algebra, representation theory, derived categories, and algebraic geometry.
Understanding the extent to which noncommutative objects are determined by commutative ones is of central interest and is a theme which underlies the noncommutative McKay correspondence. In particular, we are interested in understanding when given orders
\mathcal{A}
over schemes
X
, we can find stacks
\mathcal{X}
with
\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{A}\simeq\operatorname{qcoh}\mathcal{X}.

This question can be modified by asking for
\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{A}
to be a component of the category
\operatorname{qcoh}\mathcal{X}
,
or a semi-orthogonal component of the derived category
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X}
. The following two examples are well known instances of this situation.

Example 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
R
a commutative ring with
G
-action.
Let
R\ast G
be the skew group ring and

\left[\operatorname{Spec}R/G\right]

be the quotient stack. It follows immediately from the definitions that we have the following equivalence of categories

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}(R\ast G)\simeq\mbox{($G$-equivariant $R$-modules)}\simeq\operatorname{qcoh}\left[\operatorname{Spec}R/G\right].

Example 1.2

(cf.
Corollary

5.5
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an Azumaya algebra of order
N
on a scheme
X
. Then we can associate a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{A}
such that the following equivalences of categories hold. Here,
\operatorname{qcoh}^{(i)}\mathcal{X}
denotes the category of
i
-twisted quasi-coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes i}\simeq\operatorname{qcoh}^{(i)}\mathcal{X}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes i}\simeq\operatorname{mod}\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes i}\simeq\operatorname{qcoh}\mathcal{X}

Inspired by work of Connes
[
Con94
]
, Chan and the second author
[
CI04
]
showed that to every stack
\mathcal{X}
with a choice of finite flat presentation, there is an associated noncommutative coordinate ring (the convolution algebra); this ring may be thought of as a noncommutative scheme. Furthermore, they showed that in the case of noncommutative
curves
, their construction is reversible: every noncommutative curve is the noncommutative coordinate ring of a smooth Deligne–Mumford curve
[
CI04
, Theorem 7.7]
.
Finding a groupoid whose convolution algebra is a given algebra has been studied in the setting of
C^{\ast}
-algebras, see for example
[
Kum86
,
LR19
,
Bic21
]
.

It is desirable to have a converse to
[
CI04
]
beyond the curve case since it allows one to import (stacky) geometric techniques in the study of noncommutative rings. It is even further desirable to ensure that in such a converse construction, the stack is Morita equivalent to the module category of the noncommutative ring. We say that an additive category
\mathcal{C}
is a component of
the additive category

\mathcal{D}
if there is an additive category
\mathcal{E}
such that

\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to the direct sum

\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathcal{C}\oplus\mathcal{E}
.
The following questions are of interest:

Question 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a sheaf of algebras over a scheme
S
.

•

When does there exist a stack
\mathcal{X}
such that
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
is a component of

\operatorname{qcoh}\mathcal{X}
?

•

When does there exist a stack
\mathcal{X}
with coarse moduli space

\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to S

with a vector bundle
V
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

\mathcal{A}\simeq\pi_{\ast}\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}\nolimits(V)
?

We achieve both of these goals in the case of noncommutative surfaces. Our result is much more involved than the case of noncommutative curves due to the presence of singularities. In this paper we consider the following class of noncommutative surfaces (see
2.3
for its local version).

.

Let

be an algebraically closed field of characteristic

.

•

There is an equivalence of Hochschild cohomology

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A})\simeq\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\operatorname{coh}^{(1)}\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\mathcal{X}\right)=\prod_{{\overline{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\operatorname{coh}^{({\overline{m}})}\mathcal{X}\right)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}^{\bullet}\left(\Delta_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\Delta_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\right)=\prod_{{\overline{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}^{\bullet}\left(\left(\Delta_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\right)_{({\overline{m}},-{\overline{m}})},\left(\Delta_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\right)_{({\overline{m}},-{\overline{m}})}\right)

By definition

is equivalent to a direct summand of the Hochschild cohomology of

.
It is conceivable that the deformations of

can be investigated by means of the Hochschild cohomology of the gerbe

\mathcal{X}

via (

),
which in turn is expected to admit a version of the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg isomorphism. Unfortunately, however, the HKR isomorphism for stacks is not fully established in the literature and in particular not for our gerbe
\mathcal{X}
(see, say,
[
ACH19
]
, where the HKR isomorphism is proved for global quotients of smooth varieties by finite groups in characteristic

).

Our construction of
\mathcal{X}
itself draws an interesting parallel between noncommutative algebraic geometry and the “bottom-up” construction
[
GS17
]
due to Geraschenko and the fourth author. Given a smooth tame Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathcal{Y}
with trivial generic stabilizer, one may consider the effective Weil divisor
D=\sum_{i}e_{i}D_{i}
on
Y
where the support of
D
is the branch divisor of the coarse space
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to Y
and where
e_{i}
is the ramification degrees of
\pi
along
D_{i}
. The bottom-up construction
[
GS17
, Theorem 1]
proves that
D
completely determines
\mathcal{Y}
. Moreover, one may reconstruct
\mathcal{Y}
from
D
through an iterated procedure of two simple operations known as root stacks
[
Cad07
,
AGV08
]
and canonical stacks
[
Vis89
]
; specifically,
\mathcal{Y}
is the canonical stack over the root stack over the canonical stack of
Y
, where the root stack is taken along
D
. It is important to note that not every
(Y,D)
arises as the branch divisor of a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack, see
[
GS17
, Example 7]
.

In analogy with the branch divisor of the coarse space map
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to Y
, one may consider the branch divisor of a noncommutative surface over its underlying commutative surface, i.e. given a smooth tame split order
(X,\mathcal{A})
of dimension
2
, one may consider the ramification locus
D
of
\mathcal{A}
. Our proof of
Theorem

1.5
shows that
D
arises as the branch locus of a smooth tame stack
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, our construction of
\mathcal{X}
follows the bottom-up procedure described above and hence gives precise information about the geometric structure of
\mathcal{X}
. Specifically, we construct morphisms of stacks and orders

which sequentially move structure from
\mathcal{A}
into stack. More precisely, the morphisms and algebras satisfy the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{X}_{r}
is normal and
\mathcal{A}_{r}
is a maximal order Azumaya in codimension one such that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}_{r}\simeq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}.

The map
r
resolves the singularities of the pair
(X,D)
in codimension one, where
D
is the

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with standard coefficients determined by
\mathcal{A}
.

(2)

\mathcal{X}_{c}
is smooth and
\mathcal{A}_{c}
is the reflexive hull of
c^{\ast}\mathcal{A}_{r}
; it is an Azumaya algebra and satisfies

(1.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}_{c}\simeq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}_{r}.

(3)

g\colon\mathcal{X}_{g}\to\mathcal{X}_{c}
is the
\mu_{N}
-gerbe corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{c}
, where

[\mathcal{A}_{c}]\in H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{X}_{c},\mu_{N})
.
There is an equivalence of categories

(1.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}^{(1)}\mathcal{X}_{g}\simeq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}_{c}.

Our constructions described above are both global as well as positive characteristic generalizations of those by Reiten and Van den Bergh
[
RV89
, Section 5]
; the latter results are established in the case of complete local rings of surfaces in characteristic zero.
In the case considered in
[
RV89
]
, the extensions from
X
to
\mathcal{X}_{r}
and from
\mathcal{X}_{r}
to
\mathcal{X}_{c}
are both Galois (when interpreted appropriately). In
[
RV89
]
they prove that the composition of extensions is again Galois, which allows them to directly show the Morita equivalence between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{c}
.
On the contrary, in our situation the extensions are not necessarily Galois but are quotients by finite linearly reductive group schemes. In particular, it is not clear whether the composition of the extensions is also such a quotient.
In this paper we use the Galois theory of Hopf algebras as explained in
[
Mon93
]
, and instead of trying to combine the extensions into one, we prove the Morita equivalence in two steps; namely, we prove the local version of the equivalence between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{r}
first in
Corollary

2.19
and then (the local version of) the equivalence between
\mathcal{A}_{r}
and
\mathcal{A}_{c}
in
Corollary

2.27
.

Remark 2.22
.

For schemes, linearly reductive quotient singularities are defined in terms of étale covers.
However, for stacky surfaces

we defined linearly reductive quotient singularities in terms of the existence of

If the characteristic of

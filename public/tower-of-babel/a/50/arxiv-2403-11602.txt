Abstract
. We present how to collapse a manifold equipped with a closed flat regular Riemannian foliation with leaves of positive dimension on a compact manifold, while keeping the sectional curvature uniformly bounded from above and below. From this deformation, we show that a closed flat regular Riemannian foliation with leaves of positive dimension on a compact simply-connected manifold is given by torus actions. This gives a geometric characterization of aspherical regular Riemannian foliations given by torus actions.

1.
Introduction

Effective actions by compact Lie groups are a classical example of symmetry in differential topology and differential geometry. In particular a lot of consideration has been given to torus actions since they correspond to the abelian part of a Lie group. Recently singular Riemannian foliations have been considered as a generalized form of symmetry. These are smooth foliations with a Riemannian metric that is “bundle like”. The justification for considering singular Riemannian foliations as a notion of symmetry comes from the fact that Lie group actions by isometries and Riemannian submersions are examples of such foliations.

In the setting of singular Riemannian foliations, Galaz-García and Radeschi in
[
22
]
introduced an analogous concept to torus actions via isometries by considering singular Riemannian foliations whose leaves are closed and aspherical manifolds. These foliations are called
A
-foliations
and examples of such foliations include effective torus actions by isometries. In general due to
[
22
]
and
[
8
]
, on a simply-connected manifold the leaves of an
A
-foliation are finitely covered by tori, and moreover, the leaves of highest dimension are all homeomorphic to a torus. Thus in this way,
A
-foliations resemble torus actions. But in
[
16
]
examples are given of
A
-foliations which are not given by torus actions. From this discussion, it follows that it is natural to ask if we may characterize those
A
-foliations given by a torus action.

In
[
22
]
the authors also introduced the notion of a
B
-foliation
. These are
A
-foliations such that the leaves are homeomorphic to manifolds that admit a flat metric, i.e. they are homeomorphic to
Bieberbach manifolds
. In
[
8
, Theorem C]
it was proven that for a simply-connected manifold the leaves of any
A
-foliation are homeomorphic to Bieberbach manifolds, except possibly for the singular leaves of dimension
4
. Due to this observation, it was proposed in
[
8
]
to redefine
B
-foliations
as those
A
-foliations
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
such that for any leaf
L\in\mathcal{F}
we have that the leaf
(L,g|_{L})
with the induced metric is a flat manifold (in particular the leaves are Bieberbach manifolds).

In this work we show that on a simply-connected manifold under the updated definition, such
B
-foliations with leaves of positive constant dimension are given by torus actions.

Theorem A
.

Consider
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a regular
B
-foliation on a compact simply-connected Riemannian manifold. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is induced by an isometric torus action.

To prove ?THM?
we compare regular
B
-foliations to the so called
\mathsf{F}
-structures and
\mathsf{N}
-structures introduced by Cheeger, Gromov and Fukaya in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
. These are other types of foliations, and were introduced when studying the notion of “collapse with bounded curvature” onto metric spaces. We recall that an
\mathsf{F}
-structure is given by an open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathfrak{I}}
of
M
, together with a
T^{k_{i}}
-action on a finite normal (or Galois) cover
\pi_{i}\colon\tilde{U}_{i}\to U_{i}
for each index, such that the torus actions satisfy certain compatibility conditions when passing from one neighborhood to another, and they are compatible with the action of the deck-transformation group of
\pi_{i}
(see for example
[
20
, Definition 19.2]
). An
\mathsf{F}
-structure induces a partition of
M
into submanifolds called the orbits of the
\mathsf{F}
-structure. We say that a sequence of Riemannian metrics
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on a compact manifold
M

collapses with bounded curvature
if there exist constants
\lambda\leq\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lambda\leq\mathrm{Sec}(g_{n})\leq\Lambda,

and for any
p\in M
the injectivity radius of
g_{n}
at
p
goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
(see for example
[
38
, p. 69]
). By
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
we have that
\mathsf{F}
-structures characterize collapse with bounded curvature: A compact manifold collapses with bounded curvature if and only if it admits an
\mathsf{F}
-structure whose orbits have positive dimension.

For the proof of ?THM?
, we prove that given a regular
B
-foliation
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
on a compact manifold with leaves of positive dimension we may always collapse
M
with bounded curvature and bounded diameter.

Theorem B
.

Consider
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a regular non-trivial
B
-foliation on a compact Riemannian manifold. Then by shrinking the directions tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
we may collapse with bounded curvature and uniformly bounded diameter the manifold
(M,g)
.

We recall that in the study of collapse with bounded curvature in
[
5
]
, given such a collapsing sequence the authors construct
\mathsf{N}
-structures and highly symmetric
\mathsf{N}
-invariant Riemannian metrics which are bounded by the collapsing sequence (see ?THM?
). When the manifold
M
has finite fundamental group an
\mathsf{N}
-structure is given by an
\mathsf{F}
-structure (see ?THM?
). In the case when
M
has finite fundamental group we use the relationship between
\mathsf{N}
-structures and
\mathsf{F}
-structures, the invariant metrics and the explicit collapsing sequence given in the proof of ?THM?
to show that the geometry of
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
where
\mathcal{F}
is a
B
-foliation with leaves of positive dimension is approximated by geometries invariant under
\mathsf{F}
-structures. We can also point to a relationship between the tangent spaces of the leaves of the foliations and the tangent spaces of the orbits of the
\mathsf{F}
-structure.

Theorem C
.

Consider
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a regular non trivial
B
-foliation on a compact
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with finite fundamental group and
m>2
. Then there exists a sequence of Riemannian metrics
\{\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}_{N}(\varepsilon)\}
with each metric invariant under an
\mathsf{F}
-structure, and such that as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have that
(M,\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}_{N}(\varepsilon))
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
(M,g)
. Moreover, given
p\in M
fixed and taking
x\in T_{e}T^{k}
fixed in the Lie algebra of the torus tracing the leaves of the
\mathsf{F}
-structure around
p
, we have that the action field
X^{\ast}(p)
converges to a direction tangent to the leaf
L_{p}
.

We remark that by ?THM?
, given a regular
B
-foliation on a compact manifold
M
with leaves of positive dimension we have a continuous family
g_{\delta}
of Riemannian metrics collapsing with bounded curvature to the leaf space. Combining this with
[
40
, Theorem 0.4]
we conclude that we have an upper bound for the minimum of the sectional curvature of a compact manifold equipped with a
B
-foliation whose leaves have positive dimension.

Corollary D
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
be a regular
B
-foliation on a compact manifold with leaves of positive dimension. Then we have for
\lambda
equal to the infimum of the sectional curvature of
g
, that
\lambda\leq 0
.

We recall that an
\mathsf{F}
-structure is
pure
, if for any point
p\in M
in the intersection of two neighborhoods
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
given in definition of the
\mathsf{F}
-structure, the images containing
p
of the orbits of the torus actions under
\pi_{i}
and
\pi_{j}
coincide (see
[
20
, p. 228]
). By
[
6
]
, pure
\mathsf{F}
-structures are examples of
B
-foliations as defined in this work. This fact motivated the following question in
[
22
]
:

Question 1
.

How do the concepts of
A
-foliations and
\mathsf{F}
-structures relate to each other?

?THM?
sheds some light on this question: Regular flat
A
-foliations (i.e.
B
-foliations) on simply-connected manifolds are given by torus actions, and thus are
\mathsf{F}
-structures.

We highlight that on a simply-connected manifold, an
\mathsf{F}
-structure is induced by a torus action (see for example
[
42
, Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 4.1]
). Thus it is a pure
\mathsf{F}
-structure, and therefore a
B
-foliation. Still such
B
-foliations may have singular leaves. For example torus actions of cohomogeneity
2
can have singular leaves
[
37
]
,
[
8
]
. Nonetheless, when
M
has finite fundamental group a regular
A
-foliation with flat leaves is the limit of
\mathsf{F}
-structures by ?THM?
.

We point out that not all regular
A
-foliations admit a Riemannian metric making it a
B
-foliation. In
[
16
]
the authors construct manifolds of dimension
m\geq 9
with finite fundamental group and each equipped with a regular
A
-foliation whose leaves are homeomorphic to the
(m-4)
-dimensional torus but not diffeomorphic to the
(m-4)
-dimensional torus. These foliations do not admit any Riemannian metric making them into a
B
-foliation, since by
[
3
]
a flat manifold is homeomorphic to the torus if and only if it is diffeomorphic to the torus.

It would be interesting to know if there exists an example of an
A
-foliation on a compact simply-connected manifold whose leaves are exotic tori. Since torus actions induce
B
-foliations, a more general question is if an
A
-foliation on a simply-connected manifold can be made into a
B
-foliation by a change of the metric.

Question 2
.

Is there an
A
-foliation on a simply-connected manifold which is not given by a torus action?

From our results, this is related to the following weaker question for foliations with leaves of positive dimension: Given any
A
-foliation on a simply-connected manifold, is there an other foliated Riemannian metric
\bar{g}
on
M
making it into a
B
-foliation?

In
[
8
, Theorem A]
a negative answer to ?THM?
is given for
A
-foliations of codimension
2
. Namely in
[
8
]
, the author explored the problem of whether we can compare the diffeomorphism type of two compact simply-connected manifolds with
A
-foliations via their leaf spaces equipped with local information of the foliation, focusing on the case of
A
-foliations of codimension
2
. The positive answer given in
[
8
, Theorem C and Lemma 5.11]
to this comparison problem together with the classification of tours actions of cohomogeneity
2
in
[
37
]
, allows us to conclude that an
A
-foliation of codimension
2
on a compact simply-connected manifold is given up to a foliated homeomorphism by a torus action. Then, by analyzing the local information of the foliation we are able to improve the conclusion to a foliated diffeomorphism.

A crucial point in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem A]
is that for an
A
-foliation of codimension
2
on a compact simply-connected manifold the stratification of the leaf space is simple and can be explicitly described. In general, even for singular Riemannian foliations on vector spaces with finite dimension, it is not clear how to compare the foliations via their leaf spaces (see
[
23
, Question 1.2]
). We point out that the proof presented in
[
8
]
is of a topological nature and only uses the fact that the presence of a foliated Riemannian metric guarantees a nice local structure of the foliation on tubular neighborhoods of leaves (see
[
33
]
). Moreover, to be able extend the ideas in
[
8
]
to higher dimensions, we would require to impose extra topological conditions on
M
and on the leaf space of the foliation. Thus, to the best of our knowledge, the approach presented in this manuscript is a different approach for comparing foliated structures to what exists in the literature.

The present work was motivated by the need to give geometric conditions on
A
-foliations that force them to be given by torus actions. Due to
[
5
]
and
[
42
]
it follows that on simply-connected manifolds, torus actions with orbits of positive dimension are characterized by the phenomenon of collapse with bounded curvature (see ?THM?
). Thus, a natural approach to finding sufficient geometric conditions for an
A
-foliation to be homogeneous, is to find sufficient geometric conditions which lets us collapse with bounded curvature the manifold by shrinking the leaves of a given
A
-foliation on a simply-connected manifold.

We highlight that to the best of our knowledge in the literature there are few references on the subject of deforming foliations. In
[
14
]
the authors show that for a particular class of
1
-dimensional regular foliations, a portion of the manifold has a cover into “long and thin” submanifolds extending the notions in
[
5
]
to the foliated setting. In
[
9
]
the authors consider a parametrized family of Lie groupoids and give rigidity results for such “deformations” of Lie groupoids. Lie groupoids are closely related to regular foliations (see
[
34
]
), and also singular Riemannian foliations (see
[
2
]
). Applying these rigidity results for Lie groupoids, the authors in
[
11
]
consider on a compact manifold a family of regular foliations
\mathcal{F}_{t}
parametrized by a real parameter
t\in[0,1]
, each having compact leaves and Hausdorff leaf space, and show that at
t=0
the existence of a leaf
L
with
H^{1}(L,\mathbb{R})=0
is a sufficient condition for such a parametrization to be rigid, i.e.
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{0}
. This is an analogous result to the conclusions presented in
[
13
]
and in
[
28
]
: Consider two regular foliations
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
with compact leaves on a fixed manifold
M
without boundary, such that they are close to each other in an appropriate sense, and such that the leaf spaces of the foliations are Hausdorff spaces. In the case that there exists a leaf
L
in one of the foliations with
H^{1}(L,\mathbb{R})=0
, there exists a foliated diffeomorphism between the foliated manifolds. For group actions there is a general rigidity result by Grove and Karcher
[
25
]
: Given a compact Lie group
G
, a compact manifold
M
, and two group actions
\mu_{1}\colon G\to\mathrm{Diff}(M)
and
\mu_{2}\colon G\to\mathrm{Diff}(M)
, if the images
\mu_{1}(G)
and
\mu_{2}(G)
are
C^{1}
close enough in
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
, then the images are conjugated in
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
.

We point out that the proof of ?THM?
is also a rigidity result for a deformation procedure, since the proof consist of approximating our foliation by torus actions using ?THM?
, and showing that there exists a limit torus action whose orbits agree with the leaves of the foliation. Nonetheless, we point out that to the best of our knowledge there are no general rigidity results for singular Riemannian foliations as those presented in
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
28
]
. Moreover, the rigidity result presented in ?THM?
agree with the conclusions in
[
25
]
. One can also compare ?THM?
with
[
40
, Proposition 0.8]
and comments after. In our work we also point to the existence of a fixed torus action on
M
, but we do show that we can take our foliated metric as the limit of the perturbed collapsing sequence.

Our article is organized as follows: In Section
2
we review the necessary preliminaries on singular Riemannian foliations, as well as the concepts of
A
-foliations,
B
-foliations, and
\mathsf{N}
-structures. We also review Alexandrov spaces and equivariant convergence of metric spaces. At the end of Section

we give the proofs of our main theorems. We begin by presenting the proof of ?THM?
, and then proceed with the proof of ?THM?

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present the preliminaries necessary for our results. We begin by presenting the definition of a singular Riemannian foliation, then present the notion of the inifinitesimal foliation and the holonomy of a leaf. We then present the notions of
A
- and
B
-foliations. We also present the necessary results from the theories of convergence of Riemannian manifolds with curvature bounds, equivariant convergence of metric spaces, and metric spaces with a lower curvature bound.

2.1.
Singular Riemannian foliations

A
singular Riemannian foliation

(M,\mathcal{F},g)
on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is a partition
\mathcal{F}
of
M
into connected injectively immersed submanifolds
L\in\mathcal{F}
called
leaves
, such that the following hold:

(i)

There exists a family of smooth vector fields
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
on
M
, such that for each point
p\in M
the vector fields span the tangent space at
p
of the leaf
L_{p}
containing
p
.

(ii)

Given
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
a geodesic perpendicular to
L_{\gamma(0)}
, then
\gamma
is perpendicular to
L_{\gamma(t)}
, for all
t\in[0,1]
.

The first condition insures that the singular distribution
\Delta
in
TM
given by
\Delta(p)=T_{p}L_{p}
is smooth. The second condition is equivalent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
being locally equidistant. When the leaves of the foliation are closed submanifolds, we say that
the foliation is closed
. We point out that in this case the leaves are embedded submanifolds.

We define the leaves of maximal dimension to be
regular leaves
, and the ones that do not have maximal dimension to be
singular leaves
. The
codimension of the foliation

\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})
is equal to the codimension of any regular leaf in
M
, and the
dimension of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted as
\dim(\mathcal{F})
, to be the dimension of the regular leaves.

For a closed singular Riemannian foliation
(M,g,\mathcal{F})
and
\ell\leq\dim(\mathcal{F})
we define

\Sigma_{\ell}=\{p\in M\mid\dim(L_{p})=\ell\}.

for
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,\dim(\mathcal{F})\}
the each connected component
C
of
\Sigma_{\ell}
is an embedded submanifold of
M
. We denote by
\Sigma^{p}
the connected component of
\Sigma_{\ell}
containing
p
, where
\ell=\dim(L_{p})
. The collection
\{\Sigma^{p}\mid p\in M\}
give the
canonical stratification of
M
by
\mathcal{F}
. For the top dimension
\dim(\mathcal{F})
, the set
\Sigma_{\dim(\mathcal{F})}
is open, dense and connected. We refer to it as the
regular stratum
, and denote it by
M_{\mathrm{reg}}=\Sigma_{\dim(\mathcal{F})}
. In the case when
M_{\mathrm{reg}}=M
we say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
regular
. We also remark that, all other strata
\Sigma_{\ell}
have codimension at least
2
(
[
22
, Section 2.3]
).

We equip the
quotient space

M/\mathcal{F}
with the quotient topology, making the
quotient map

\pi\colon M\to M/\mathcal{F}
continuous. We refer to
M/\mathcal{F}
equipped with the quotient topology as the
leaf space of
\mathcal{F}
. When the foliation is closed and
M
is complete, the Riemannian distance on
M
induces an inner metric, called the
quotient metric
on the leaf space, and it induces quotient topology. Moreover as pointed out in
[
26
, p. 2943]
, for a closed foliation on a complete manifold, the leaf space equipped with the quotient metric is locally an Alexandrov space (see Section
2.5
for a definition of an Alexandrov space).

Given a singular Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a vector space
V
equipped with an inner product
g
, we say that
(V,\mathcal{F},g)
is an
infinitesimal foliation
if
(V,\mathcal{F},g)
is a singular Riemannian foliation, and for the origin
\bar{0}\in V
we have
L_{\bar{0}}=\{\bar{0}\}
.

We now consider
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a singular Riemannian foliation on a complete Riemannian manifold, and fix
p\in M
. We denote by
\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})==\{v\in T_{p}M\mid g(p)(v,x)=0\mbox{ for any }x\in T_{p}L_{p}\}
the set of
normal space to the leaf at
p
. Given
\varepsilon>0
we consider the
closed normal disk of radius
\varepsilon

\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})=\{v\in\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})\mid\|v\|^{2}_{g}\leq\varepsilon\}
. Then by fixing
\varepsilon>0
small enough (for example
\varepsilon
smaller than the injectivity radius at
p
), we may assume that
\expo_{p}\colon\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})\to S_{p}=\exp_{p}(\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p}))\subset M
is a diffeomorphism, where

\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})=\{v\in T_{p}M\mid g(p)(v,x)=0\mbox{ for any }x\in T_{p}L_{p},\>\|v\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon\}.

For
v\in\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})
we define
\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})
to be the connected component of
\expo_{p}^{-1}(L_{\expo_{p}(v)}\cap S_{p})
containing
v
. In this way, we obtain a partition
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\varepsilon)
on
\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})
. By
[
35
, Lemma 6.2]
the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\varepsilon)
is invariant under homotheties
h_{\lambda}\colon\nu_{p}^{\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})\to\nu_{p}^{\lambda\varepsilon}(M,L_{p})
, with
h_{\lambda}(v)=\lambda v
. From this we see that
\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)
is independent of
\varepsilon
, and thus we can extend
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\varepsilon)
to a partition
\mathcal{F}_{p}
on
\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})
in a unique way. Moreover,
[
35
, Proposition 6.5]
states that
(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p},g_{p}^{\perp})
is an infinitesimal foliation, where
g_{p}^{\perp}=g(p)|_{\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})}
. We refer to
(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p},g_{p}^{\perp})
as the
infinitesimal foliation of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
.

The following local description of the foliation is going to be of use later on:

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 2.17 in
[
41
]
)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
be a singular Riemannian foliation on an
m
-dimensional manifold, and for any
p\in M
we write
k_{p}=\dim(L_{p})
. Then for
p\in M
fixed there exists a coordinate system
(W,\phi)
, with
W\subset M
and
\phi\colon W\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that:

(1)

p\in W
,
\phi(W)=U\times V
, where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k_{p}}
and
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m-k_{p}}
are open and bounded subsets containing the origin with smooth boundary.

(2)

\phi(p)=(0,0)\in U\times V
.

(3)

\phi^{-1}(U\times\{0\})=W\cap L_{p}
.

(4)

For any
q\in W
,
U\times\mathrm{proj}_{2}(\phi(q))\subset\phi(L_{q}\cap W)
.

(5)

For fixed
(u_{0},v_{0})\in U\times V
the curve
\phi(u_{0},tv_{0})
is a geodesic of
M
perpendicular to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

2.2.
Holonomy

In this section we present for
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a singular Riemannian foliation the notion of the
holonomy
of a closed leaf
L_{p}\in\mathcal{F}
. We denote by
\nu(L)\to L
the normal bundle to
L
, and we have the following construction:

Theorem 2.2

(Corollary 1.5 in
[
33
]
)
.

Let
L
be a closed leaf of
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
a singular Riemannian foliation, and let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to L
a piecewise smooth curve with
\gamma(0)=p
. Then there exists a map
G\colon[0,1]\times\nu_{p}(M,L)\to\nu(L)
such that:

(i)

G(t,v)\in\nu_{\gamma(t)}(M,L)
for every
(t,v)\in[0,1]\times\nu_{p}(M,L)
.

(ii)

For every
t\in[0,1]
, the restriction
G_{t}\colon\{t\}\times\nu_{p}(M,L)\to\nu_{\gamma(t)}(M,L)
is a linear map; moreover with respect to the infinitesimal foliations the map

G_{t}\colon(\nu_{p}(M,L),\mathcal{F}_{p},g_{p}^{\perp})\to(\nu_{\gamma(t)}(M,L),\mathcal{F}_{\gamma(t)},g_{\gamma(t)}^{\perp})

is a foliated isometry.

Let
\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})
denote the group of
foliated isometries
of the infinitesimal foliation. That is, a map
f\in\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})
is an isometry
f\colon(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),g_{p}^{\perp})\to(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),g_{p}^{\perp})
such that for any leaf
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
we have
f(\mathcal{L})\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
. We denote by
\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})\subset\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}^{\perp}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})
the subgroup of foliated isometries that leave the infinitesimal foliation invariant, i.e.
f\in\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})
if and only if
f(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{L}
for all
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
. Observe that for a closed piecewise loop
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to L_{p}
with start point
p
we have by ?THM?
a map
G_{\gamma}\colon\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})\to\nu_{p}(M,L_{p})
with
G_{\gamma}\in\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})
. Moreover, from
[
8
, Proposition 2.5]
it follows that the coset
G_{\gamma}\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})\in\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})/\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})
depends only on the homotopy class of
\gamma
. Thus, we have a well defined map
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(L_{p},p)\to\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})/\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})
.

We define the
holonomy of the leaf
L_{p}
at
p
as the image

\mathrm{Hol}(L_{p})=\rho(\pi_{1}(L_{p},p))\subset\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})/\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p}).

Observe that if
q\in L_{p}
is an other point in
L_{p}
and we fix
\alpha\colon I\to L_{p}
a path from
q
to
p
, then we have that
G_{\alpha}\mathrm{O}(\nu_{q}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{q})G_{\alpha}^{-1}=\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}),\mathcal{F}_{p})
and
G_{\alpha}\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{q})G_{\alpha}^{-1}=\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{F}_{p})
. Thus, the holonomy is defined up to conjugation in the group of isometries
\mathrm{O}(\nu_{p}(M,L_{p}))
of the normal space to the leaf.

A leaf
L_{p}
is called
principal
if and only if it is regular and has trivial holonomy. Given a closed singular Riemannian foliation
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
, the collection of all principal leaves is called the
principal stratum of
\mathcal{F}
and denoted by
M_{\mathrm{prin}}
. We point out that

is an open and dense subset by
[
8
, Proposition 2.8]
.

2.3.
A
- and
B
-foliations

We now consider closed singular Riemannian foliations whose leaves are aspherical manifolds, i.e. such that for
L\in\mathcal{F}
we have
\pi_{k}(L)=0
, for
k\neq 1
. These foliations are called
A
-foliations
, and where introduced in
[
22
]
as a generalization of torus actions by isometries. The authors showed that on a compact simply-connected manifold
M
, the regular leaves of an
A
-foliation are homeomorphic to tori.

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem B in
[
22
]
)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
be an
A
-foliation on a compact simply-connected manifold, and let
L\in\mathcal{F}
be a regular leaf. Then
L
is homeomorphic to a torus.

Remark 2.4
.

We recall that for
n\geq 5
by
[
31
]
, there exists
n
-manifolds which are homoeomorphic to the
n
-torus, but not diffeomorphic. These manifolds are known as
exotic tori
. We recall that there are examples of
A
-foliations
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
on compact manifolds whose leaves are exotic tori, but
M
is not simply connected (see
[
16
]
).

In
[
22
]
the authors also introduced the concept of
B
-foliations, which are
A
-foliations whose leaves are homeomorphic to Bieberbach manifolds. Recall that an
n
-dimensional
Bieberbach manifold

L
is an aspherical manifold such that
\pi_{1}(L)
is isomorphic to a discrete subgroup
G
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{O}(n)\subset\mathrm{Aff}(n)
which is torsion free, and such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}/G
is compact. Such a group
G
is called a
Bieberbach group
. We recall that Bieberbach manifolds are those homeomorphic to smooth manifolds that admit a flat Riemannian metric, i.e. a Riemannian metric whose sectional curvature is equal to
0
for any
2
-plane in the tangent space at any point. By the following result, it follows that the leaves of
A
-foliations on compact simply-connected manifolds have fundamental group isomorphic to a Bieberbach group, and except for leaves of dimension
4
, they are homeomorphic to Bieberbach manifolds.

Theorem 2.5

(Corollary 3.2 and Proposition 3.3 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
be an
A
-foliation on a compact simply-connected manifold. Then the leaves with trivial holonomy are homeomorphic to tori. Moreover, for any leaf
L\in\mathcal{F}
with non-trivial holonomy the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(L,p)
, at
p\in L
, is a Bieberbach group, and for
\dim(L)\neq 4
the leaf
L
is homeomorphic to a Bieberbach manifold.

This implies that, except for the leaves of dimension
4
, from a topological viewpoint
A
-foliations and the
B
-foliations as defined in
[
22
]
are indistinguishable on compact simply-connected manifolds. Thus in
[
8
, Remark 3.4]
the author proposed to define
B
-foliations
as those
A
-foliations
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
such that for any
L\in\mathcal{F}
we have that
(L,g|_{L})
is flat. This definition immediately introduces some rigidity to the diffeomorpshism type of the foliation: Combining ?THM?
with the work of Bieberbach
[
3
]
(and more generally by
[
15
, Theorem 3]
) we obtain that for simply-connected manifolds the regular leaves of an
A
-foliation
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
such that
(L,g|_{L})
is flat, i.e.
B
-foliation as defined in this paragraph, are diffeomorphic to the standard torus.

2.4.
Compact flat metric spaces

For the sake of completeness, we give a short review of the geometry of flat manifolds. We consider
(M,g)
to be a compact smooth Rieamannian
m
-dimensional manifold, such that at every point
p\in M
and any
2
-dimensional plane in
T_{p}M
we have the sectional curvature equal to
0
, i.e. a flat manifold. By the Cartan-Hadamard theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
) it follows that the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
of
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We consider
\widetilde{g}=\pi^{\ast}(g)
the pull back metric of
g
under the covering map
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
. Observe that by construction
\pi\colon(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})\to(M,g)
is a local isometry, and thus
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
is a flat manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. By
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
it follows that
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
is isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\sigma)
where
\sigma
denotes the Euclidean Riemannian metric. Thus we have that there exists a covering map
\bar{\pi}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{m},\sigma)\to(M,g)
which is a local isometry, i.e.
\bar{\pi}^{\ast}(g)=\sigma
. By letting
B
be the group of deck transformations of
\bar{\pi}
, it follows that
B
is a subgroup of the isometry group
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{m},\sigma)
which is torsion free, i.e.
B
is a Bieberbach group. Thus
(M,g)
is isometric to the homogeneous Riemannian space
(\mathbb{R}^{m}/B,\bar{\sigma})
.

2.5.
Alexandrov spaces

We now recall the definition of an Alexandrov space. A locally compact and locally complete inner metric space
(X,d_{X})
, is called a
local Alexandrov space
if we have the following: For any
x\in X
there exist
\kappa_{x}\in\mathbb{R}
and an open neighborhood
U\subset X
of
x
, such that for each geodesic triangle
\triangle
contained in
U
, there exists a geodesic triangle
\widetilde{\triangle}
in
S^{2}_{\kappa_{x}}
, the
2
-dimensional surface of constant sectional curvature
\kappa_{x}
, with edges having the same lengths as the edges of
\triangle
satisfying the following condition: For a vertex
y\in\triangle
,
\tilde{y}
the corresponding vertex in
\widetilde{\triangle}
, and
w
any point in the opposing edge to
y
in
\triangle
with corresponding point
\tilde{w}
in the opposing edge to
\tilde{y}
in
\widetilde{\triangle}
, then

d(y,w)\geq d_{S^{2}_{\kappa_{x}}}(\tilde{y},\tilde{w}).

We call
x
a
point of
X
with curvature at least
\kappa_{x}
. When there exist
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
such that any point of
X
is a point with curvature at least
\kappa
, we say that
(X,d_{X})
is an
Alexandrov space of curvature at least
\kappa
, and we denote it by
\curv(X)\geq\kappa
. We denote the collection of all Alexandrov spaces with curvature at least
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
by
\mathrm{Alex}(\kappa)
.

With respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff topology (see Section
2.6
) we have the following stability result for Alexandrov spaces:

Theorem 2.6

(Perelman’s stability Theorem in
[
39
]
,
[
32
]
)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a compact
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space of
\curv(X)\geq\kappa
. Then there exists an
\epsilon=\epsilon(X)>0
such that for any
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space
(Y,d_{Y})
of
\curv(Y)\geq\kappa
with
d_{GH}(X,Y)<\epsilon
,
Y
is homeomorphic to
X
.

Remark 2.7
.

The homeomorphism in ?THM?
can be taken so that it preserves the stratification of
X
and
Y
by extremal subsets (see
[
32
]
,
[
30
, Theorem 4.3]
).

2.6.
Equivariant convergence

We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of all isometry classes of pointed metric spaces
(X,d_{X},x)
such that for each
r>0
the open ball
B_{r}(x)
is relatively compact. We define the following notion of convergence on
\mathcal{M}
as follows:

We begin by considering first
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
metric spaces. We define the
Gromov-Hausdorff distance
between
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
, denoted by
d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y}))
, to be the infimum of all Hausdorff distances
d_{H}(f(X),g(Y))
for all metric spaces
(Z,d_{Z})
and all isometric embeddings
f\colon X\to Z
and
g\colon Y\to Z
(see
[
4
, Section 7.3]
).

We say that a a pointed sequence
\{(X_{i},d_{X_{i}},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
converges to
(Y,d_{Y},y)\in\mathcal{M}
if for each
r>0
and each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for
n\geq N
there exists a map
f_{n}\colon B_{r}(x_{n})\to X
such that

(i)

f(x_{n})=y
,

(ii)

the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
f_{n}(B_{r}(x_{n}))\subset Y
contains the ball
B_{r-\varepsilon}(y)
,

(iii)

\mathrm{dis}f_{n}=\sup\{d_{Y}(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))-d_{X_{n}}(z_{1},z_{2})\mid z_{1},z_{2}\in B_{r}(x_{n})\}<\varepsilon
.

We call this notion of convergence on
\mathcal{M}
, convergence in the
pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense
.

Remark 2.8
.

When
(X_{i},d_{X_{i}})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are locally compact metric spaces, we have that
(X_{i},d_{X_{i}},x_{i})
converges to
(Y,d_{Y},y)
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense if and only if for any
R>0
the open balls
(B_{R}(x_{i}),d_{X_{i}})
converge to
(B_{R}(y),d_{Y})
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense.

Remark 2.9
.

When we consider a sequence of compact spaces
(X_{i},d_{X_{i}})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
, then convergence with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff distance is equivalent to convergence in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense.

Moreover, for compact spaces we have a nice characterization of converge in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Recall that given
\varepsilon>0
, a subset
S\subset X
of a metric space is
an
\varepsilon
-net
if we have that
d_{X}(x,S)=\inf\{d_{X}(x,s)\mid s\in S\}\leq\varepsilon
. Given
X
,
Y
two compact metric spaces, and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, we say that
X
and
Y
are
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-approximations of each other
if there exists
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset X
,
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset Y
such that

(1)

The sets
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
,
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are
\varepsilon
-nets,

(2)

|d_{X}(x_{i},x_{j})-d_{Y}(y_{i},y_{j})|<\delta
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
.

When
\varepsilon=\delta
, we say that
X
and
Y
are
\varepsilon
-approximations of each other
. With this we can write the characterization of convergence in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense for compact spaces:

Proposition 2.10

(Proposition 7.4.11)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be compact metric spaces. Then we have

(1)

If
Y
is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
approximation of
X
, then
d_{\mathrm{GH}}(X,Y)<2\varepsilon+\delta
.

(2)

If
d_{\mathrm{GH}}(X,Y)<\varepsilon
, then
Y
is a
5\varepsilon
-approximation of
X
.

We also consider tuples
(X,d_{X},H,x)
, where
(X,d_{X},x)\in\mathcal{M}
and
H\subset\mathrm{Isom}(X,d_{X})
is a closed subgroup acting effectively on
X
. We say that
(X,d_{X},H,x)
is equivalent to
(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)
if there exists
f\colon(X,d_{X},x)\to(Y,d_{Y},y)
an isometry with
f(x)=y
, and
\phi\colon H\to H^{\prime}
a group isomorphism such that for any
h\in H
and
x^{\prime}\in X
we have
f(h\cdot x^{\prime})=\phi(h)\cdot f(x^{\prime})
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
the collection of all equivalent tuples
(X,d_{X},H,x)
. For
(X,d_{X},H,x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
and
r>0
we set
H(r)=\{h\in H\mid h\cdot x\in B_{r}(x)\}
.

We define the following topology on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
: Given
(X,d_{X},H,x)
,
(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we define an
\varepsilon
-equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff approximation
between
X
and
Y
to be a triple of functions
(f,\phi,\psi)
,

\displaystyle f\colon B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\to Y,

\displaystyle\phi\colon H(1/\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\to H^{\prime}(1/\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\psi\colon H^{\prime}(1/\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\to H(1/\varepsilon),

such that

(1)

we have
f(x)=y
.

(2)

The
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
f(B_{1/\varepsilon}(x))
contains
B_{1/\varepsilon}(y)
.

(3)

For
p,q\in B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)
we have
|d_{Y}(f(p),f(p^{\prime}))-d_{X}(p,p^{\prime})|<\varepsilon
.

(4)

Given
h\in H(1/\varepsilon)
,
p\in B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)
with
h\cdot p\in B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)
we have

d_{Y}(f(h\cdot p),\phi(h)\cdot f(p))<\varepsilon.

(5)

For
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}(1/\varepsilon)
,
p\in B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)
with
\psi(h^{\prime})\cdot p\in B_{1/\varepsilon}(x)
, then

d_{Y}(f(\psi(h^{\prime})\cdot p),h^{\prime}\cdot f(p))<\varepsilon.

For
(X,d_{X},H,x),(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
we define the
equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance
,
d_{\mathrm{eqGH}}\Big((X,d_{X},H,x),(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)\Big)
, to be the infimum of all numbers
\varepsilon>0
such that there exists an
\varepsilon
-equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff approximation from
(X,d_{X},H,x)
to
(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)
and from
(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)
to
(X,d_{X},H,x)
. This induces on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
the
equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology
.

Given a sequence
\{(X_{i},d_{X_{i}},H_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Y,d_{Y},H^{\prime},y)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
, we have the following relation between convergence with respect to the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance, and convergence in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense of the pointed quotient spaces,
(X/H,d_{X}^{\ast},x^{\ast})
and
(Y/H^{\prime},d_{Y}^{\ast},y^{\ast})
.

Theorem 2.11

(Theorem 2.1 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\{(X_{i},d_{X_{i}},H_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{eq}}
have limit

with respect to the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance. Then we have that

is the limit of the sequence
\{(X_{i}/H_{i},d_{X_{i}}^{\ast},x_{i}^{\ast})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

Moreover, we have then the following information on the limit of sequences in

with respect to the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence:

Theorem 2.12

(Proposition 3.6 in
[

]
)
.

Assume that for a sequence

Moreover in the case of Alexandrov spaces of constant dimension we have the following result:

Theorem 2.13

(Proposition 4.1 in

)

Let

2.7.
\mathsf{N}
- and
\mathsf{F}
-structures

The concept of an
\mathsf{F}
-structure was introduced by Cheeger and Gromov in
[
6
]
generalizing the notion of torus actions on manifolds. Moreover they proved that for
\mathsf{F}
-structure of positive rank on a compact manifold we can find a family of Riemannian metrics
\{g_{t}\mid 1\geq t>0\}
on the manifold such that the
manifold collapses with bounded curvature and diameter to a metric space
. That is, the injectivity radius of
(M,g_{t})
goes to
0
as
t\to 0
, there exist
\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
0<D
such that
\lambda\leq\mathrm{Sec}(g_{t})\leq\Lambda
and
\mathrm{Diam}(M,g_{t})\leq D
for all
0<t\leq 1
.

In
[
18
,
19
]
Fukaya studied the phenomena of collapse when the sequence
\{(M,g_{t})\}
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a Riemannian manifold, and then in
[
5
]
it was proven that collapse is controlled by so called
Nilpotent structures
, or
\mathsf{N}
-structures
. We now recall the definition of such a structure.

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
m
we consider
\pi\colon F(M)\to M
to be the
\mathrm{O}(m)
-principal frame bundle of
M
. A pure
\mathsf{N}
-structure on
(M,g)
is given by a fibration
\tilde{\eta}\colon F(M)\to B
such that:

(i)

The fiber is a nilmanifold isometric to
(N/\Gamma,\nabla^{\mathrm{can}})
, where
N
is a simply-connected nilpotent group and
\nabla^{\mathrm{can}}
is the canonical connection on
N
making all left-invariant vector fields parallel.

(ii)

The structure group of
\tilde{\eta}\colon F(M)\to B
is contained in the group of affine automorphisms of the fiber.

(iii)

The action of
\mathrm{O}(m)
preserves the fibers and the structure group of the fibration.

A pure
\mathsf{N}
-structure induces a partition of
M
into submanifolds called
“orbits”
. We refer to this decomposition of
M
as a
pure
\mathsf{N}
-structure on
M
. An
\mathsf{N}
-structure has
positive rank
if the orbits have positive dimension.

We observe that the centers of the fibers of a pure
\mathsf{N}
-structure on
F(M)
determine a torus bundle on the nilpotent group
N
. This gives rise to a second
\mathrm{O}(m)
-invariant fiber bundle
\tilde{f}\colon F(M)\to B_{\tilde{f}}
whose fiber is a torus, and has affine structure group. We call
\tilde{f}
the
canonical torus bundle of
\tilde{\eta}
. We have the following relationship between
\tilde{\eta}
and
\tilde{f}
when the fundamental group of
M
is finite.

Theorem 2.14

(Lemma 4.1 and Lemma 3.2 in
[
42
]
)
.

An
\mathsf{N}
-structure
\tilde{\eta}\colon F(M)\to B_{\tilde{\eta}}
on
M
a manifold with finite fundamental group coincides with its canonical torus bundle
\tilde{f}\colon F(M)\to B_{\tilde{f}}
. That is,
B_{\tilde{\eta}}=B_{\tilde{f}}
and
\tilde{\eta}=\tilde{f}
. Moreover, when
M
is simply connected then an
\mathsf{N}
-structure on
M
is given by a torus action on
M
.

By
[
5
]
, given an
\mathsf{N}
-structure
\tilde{\eta}\colon F(M)\to B_{\tilde{\eta}}
there exists a sheaf of Lie algebras on
F(M)
whose local sections restrict to local right invariant vector fields on the fibers of
\tilde{\eta}
. We say that a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
is
\mathsf{N}
-invariant with respect to
\tilde{\eta}
if these local sections project under
\pi
to local Killing fields for the metric
g
. In particular, given
V\subset(M,g)
a tubular neighborhood of an orbit of the
\mathsf{N}
-structure there exists a normal covering
\widetilde{V}\to V
admitting an isometric
N
-action.

In
[
5
]
the authors showed that a sufficiently collapsed manifold
(M,g)
admits an
\mathsf{N}
-structure. And moreover there exist an
\mathsf{N}
-invariant Riemannian metric on
M
related to
g
.

Theorem 2.15

(Theorems 1.3 and 1.7 in
[
5
]
)
.

For
m>2
and
D>0
we denote by
\mathfrak{M}(m,D)
the class of
m
-dimensional compact connected Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
with
|\mathrm{Sec}(g)|\leq 1
, and
\mathrm{Diam}(M,g)\leq D
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
v=v(m,D,\varepsilon)>0
such that given
(M,g)\in\mathfrak{M}(m,D)
with
\mathrm{Vol}(g)<v
, then
M
admits a pure
\mathsf{N}
-structure
\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\colon F(M)\to B
of positive rank. Moreover,

(a)

there is a smooth metric
g^{\varepsilon}

Characters and spin characters of alternating and symmetric groups determined by values on l ′ l^{\prime} -classes Thanks: This is the accepted manuscript for an article to be published in Arkiv för Matematik.
Eoghan McDowell

Abstract
. This paper identifies all pairs of ordinary irreducible characters of the alternating group which agree on conjugacy classes of elements of order not divisible by a fixed integer l l , for l ≠ 3 l\neq 3 . We do likewise for spin characters of the symmetric and alternating groups. We find that the only such characters are the conjugate or associate pairs labelled by partitions with a certain parameter divisible by l l . When l l is prime, this implies that the rows of the l l -modular decomposition matrix are distinct except for the rows labelled by these pairs. When l = 3 l=3 we exhibit many additional examples of such pairs of characters.

20C15, 20C20, 20C30.

1.
Introduction

Does knowing its values on
l^{\prime}
-classes suffice to identify an ordinary irreducible character of a group
G
?
Here, an
l^{\prime}
-class means a conjugacy class of elements of order not divisible by a positive integer
l\geqslant 2
.
The answer is certainly “no” if
l
is prime and
G
is an
l
-group, since the only
l^{\prime}
-class is the identity;
on the other hand, if
G
is a symmetric group and
l>2
then the answer is “yes”: Wildon showed that all irreducible characters of the symmetric group are uniquely determined by their values on
l^{\prime}
-classes when
l>2

[
Wil08
, Corollary 2.1.3]
.

In this paper we resolve this question for the alternating group, and for the spin characters of the symmetric and alternating groups, when
l\neq 3
.

When
l
is prime, answering this question gives us information about the modular representation theory of
G
.
Indeed, for ordinary irreducible characters
\chi
and
\psi
of a group
G
, is is straightforward to show (see the proof of
Corollary

1
) that the following are equivalent:

(1)

\chi
and
\psi
agree on the
l^{\prime}
-classes of
G
;

(2)

the rows of the
l
-modular decomposition matrix of
G
labelled by
\chi
and
\psi
are equal.

This second property says that the
l
-modular reductions of the representations affording
\chi
and
\psi
have the same multiset of composition factors (that is, the same Brauer character; for an account of decomposition numbers and Brauer characters, see for example
[
Web16
, Chapter 10]
).
Thus we obtain as a corollary to our main theorems a classification of repeated rows in the decomposition matrices of the alternating group when
l\neq 3
, and of the double covers of the symmetric and alternating groups when
l\not\in\{2,3\}
.

1.1.
Main theorems

Our first main theorem concerns the alternating group
A_{n}
;
see
Section

2.1
for the labelling of its characters.
The case of
l=2
is due to
[
Wil08
, Theorem 3.2.1]
.

1.1
.

Let
l\neq 3
.

If
l
is even,
irreducible characters of
A_{n}
are uniquely determined by their values on
l^{\prime}
-classes.

If
l
is odd,
a conjugate pair of characters of
A_{n}
labelled by a self-conjugate partition with a principal hook length divisible by
l
agree on
l^{\prime}
-classes; all other irreducible characters of
A_{n}
are uniquely determined by their values on
l^{\prime}
-classes.

Our next main theorems concern
spin characters
.
The projective representation theory of the symmetric and alternating groups
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
is controlled by the linear representation theory of their double covers
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
;
characters of a double cover which are not lifts from the original group are those which send the central element of order
2
to
-I
, and are called
spin characters
(or
projective characters
or
negative characters
).
Note that there is a choice of two double covers of
S_{n}
, but our results hold for either choice; see
Section

2.2
for a justification of this, as well as for the explicit presentation we will use and the labelling of its characters.

1.2
.

Let
l\neq 3
.
An associate pair of spin characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
labelled by an odd partition with a part divisible by
l
agree on
l^{\prime}
-classes;
all other irreducible spin characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
are uniquely determined by their values on
l^{\prime}
-classes.

1.3
.

Let
l\neq 3
.
A conjugate pair of spin characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
labelled by an even partition with a part divisible by
l
agree on
l^{\prime}
-classes;
all other irreducible spin characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
are uniquely determined by their values on
l^{\prime}
-classes.

When
l=2
, the unique determination stated in
Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
relies on the character being known to be spin: there are spin and non-spin characters which agree on
2^{\prime}
-classes (see
Section

1.3
below).
If
l\neq 2
, however, no spin character agrees with a non-spin character on
l^{\prime}
-classes, since they disagree on the central element of order
2
.
Thus, together with
Theorem

1.1
and
[
Wil08
, Corollary 2.1.3]
, these theorems completely classify pairs of irreducible characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
which agree on
l^{\prime}
-classes for
l\not\in\{2,3\}
.

Our results have the following interpretation in terms of decomposition matrices.

1
.

Let
p
be prime.
With the exceptions of the repeated rows labelled by the pairs of characters agreeing on
p^{\prime}
-classes described in
Theorems

1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
,
there are no repeated rows in the decomposition matrices of
A_{n}
when
p\neq 3
, nor in the decomposition matrices of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
when
p\not\in\{2,3\}
.

Proof.

We argue as in
[
Wil08
, §3]
.
Let
D
be the decomposition matrix,
B
the Brauer character table, and
X
the ordinary character table restricted to
p^{\prime}
-elements (for any finite group).
Note that
DB=X
and that
B
is invertible.
This implies that equal rows in
D
correspond to equal rows in
X
(which are precisely characters agreeing on
p^{\prime}
-classes):
if the rows of
D
labelled by ordinary characters
\chi
and
\psi
are equal, and
g
is any
p^{\prime}
-element of the group, then

\chi(g)=\sum_{\varphi}D_{\chi,\varphi}\varphi(g)=\sum_{\varphi}D_{\psi,\varphi}\varphi(g)=\psi(g)

where the sums are over all Brauer characters
\varphi
of the group; similarly for the converse by writing
D=XB^{-1}
.
∎

In all three of our main theorems, the infinite family of pairs of characters agreeing on
l^{\prime}
-classes can be identified immediately:

•

for a self-conjugate partition
\lambda
, the conjugate characters
\chi^{\lambda^{+}}
and
\chi^{\lambda^{-}}
of
A_{n}
differ only on the split class of cycle type consisting of the principal hook lengths of
\lambda
;

•

for an odd partition
\lambda
with distinct parts, the spin character
\langle\lambda\rangle
of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and its associate differ only on the split class labelled by
\lambda
;

•

for an even partition
\lambda
with distinct parts, the restriction to
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
of the spin character
\langle\lambda\rangle
decomposes as a sum of two conjugate characters which differ only on the split class labelled by
\lambda
.

The force of the theorems is that no other pairs of characters agree on the
l^{\prime}
-classes.

This is false, however, for
l=3
.
That is, there are pairs of characters which agree on the
3^{\prime}
-classes – and hence there are repeated rows in the
3
-modular decomposition matrix – in addition to the conjugate and associate pairs identified above.
For
A_{n}
, we identify two infinite families of such pairs (
Theorem

6.1
), and remark on a striking similarity with known characters of
A_{n}
with equal vanishing sets.
For
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
, we record all such pairs for
n\leqslant 14
(
Proposition

13
).

To prove our main theorems, we consider the subalgebra of the centre of the group algebra generated by the
l^{\prime}
-class sums, and use central characters to determine the genuine characters.
This is the technique used by Wildon in
[
Wil08
]
for the symmetric group.
Unlike for the symmetric group, in our cases it is not possible to generate the entire centre; nevertheless we show we can generate enough to distinguish characters up to conjugacy or associates.

This paper is structured as follows. We recall background on the character theory of symmetric and alternating groups and their double covers in
Section

2
.
We prove a sufficient condition for a pair of characters of a normal subgroup to be conjugate in
Section

3
, and prove the conjugacy class sum generation results required to use this condition in
Section

4
.
We deduce our main theorems in
Section

5
.
We exhibit characters agreeing on
3^{\prime}
-classes in
Section

6
.

1.2.
Remarks on the size of a set needed to determine a character

For each group in consideration, as
n
grows, the number of
l^{\prime}
-classes grows exponentially, but the proportion of
l^{\prime}
-classes amongst all conjugacy classes decays exponentially.
These facts can be deduced from the numbers and proportions for
S_{n}
(see
[
HR18
, (1.36)]
and
[
Hag71
, Corollary 4.2]
), noting that the numbers of classes and
l^{\prime}
-classes for the related groups are bounded linearly in terms of those for
S_{n}
.
(More precisely,
[
Hag71
]
asymptotically enumerates the partitions with no part repeated
l
or more times; this is equal to the number of partitions with no part divisible by
l
by Glaisher’s Theorem (see for example
[
Jam78
, Chapter 10]
).
This is an upper bound on the number of
l^{\prime}
-classes of
S_{n}
, attained when
l
is a prime power;
for a lower bound, consider the number of
q^{\prime}
-classes for
q
a prime power factor of
l
.)

Our theorems therefore say that characters are determined (with the specified exceptions) by their values on a small proportion of classes.
In fact, the proof of
Theorem

4.1
requires only
l^{\prime}
-classes of cycle type having at most four parts greater than
1
, and so the characters are determined by their values on this restricted set of
l^{\prime}
-classes, of size
O(n^{4})
.

Nevertheless, we can determine characters by their values on smaller sets of classes if we remove the
l^{\prime}
restriction.
As noted in
[
Wil08
]
, for the symmetric group
S_{n}
, the values on cycles – a set of size
n
– suffice to determine a character: the class sums of the cycles generate the centre of the group algebra
[
Kra66
]
, and arguing as in
Section

3
gives the claim.
An analogous argument shows that for the alternating group
A_{n}
, the values on cycles (of odd length) and values on products of two cycles of even length – a set of size
O(n^{2})
– suffice to determine a character.
For the double covers
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
, it can be shown that class sums of preimages of cycles of odd length together with all non-split even classes generate enough of the centre of the group algebra to determine characters up to associates or conjugacy; since spin characters vanish on non-split classes, this implies that values on one of each of the split classes of cycles of odd length – a set of size
\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
– suffice to determine a spin character up to associates or conjugacy.

A related endeavour of Chow and Paulhus
[
CP21
]
gives an algorithm to determine a character of the symmetric group from its values on a set of size
O(n)
– although they require more values than just those on the cycles, they explicitly construct the indexing partition from these character values without requiring knowledge of the full character table.

1.3.
Remarks on spin characters modulo 2

Although
Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
rule out two spin characters agreeing on
2^{\prime}
-classes (except for the specified associate or conjugate pairs), there do exist spin characters which agree with a non-spin character on the
2^{\prime}
-classes.
That is, although there are no repeated rows in the
2
-modular decomposition matrices of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
with both rows labelled by spin characters, there are repeated rows with one row labelled by a spin character and the other by a non-spin character.
For example, when
n=5
the spin character
\langle(4,1)\rangle
agrees with the non-spin character
\chi^{(3,1^{2})}
on
2^{\prime}
-classes
[
ATLAS
, p. 2]
, and the corresponding rows of the decomposition matrix are equal
[
Fay18
, p. 885]
.
(There are also repeated rows in the
2
-modular decomposition matrix of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
labelled by conjugate pairs of partitions, i.e. the repeated rows in the decomposition matrix of
S_{n}
classified by
[
Wil08
, Theorem 1.1.1(ii)]
.)

In fact, there exist infinitely many pairs of spin and non-spin characters which agree on
2^{\prime}
-classes, and moreover whose affording representations are isomorphic after reduction modulo
2
.
(Here the
2
-modular reduction of a representation of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
is viewed as a representation of
S_{n}
, as is possible because the image of the central element of order
2
is trivial modulo
2
).
Indeed, consider
\lambda=(k,k-1,\ldots,1)
with
k\geqslant 2
, the
2
-core partition of
n=\frac{1}{2}k(k+1)
.
Then
\chi^{\lambda}
is the only ordinary irreducible character of
S_{n}
lying in its own
2
-block, and hence its
2
-modular reduction remains irreducible and is the only irreducible
2
-modular character in that block.
It is known that, for
\mu
a strict partition, the spin character
\langle\mu\rangle
lies in the same
2
-block as
\chi^{\mathrm{dblreg}(\mu)}
, where
\mathrm{dblreg}(\mu)
denotes the
2-regularisation
of the
double
of
\mu
, and furthermore
\chi^{\mathrm{dblreg}(\mu)}
occurs as a composition factor of
\langle\mu\rangle
with multiplicity
2^{\left\lfloor m_{0}(\mu)/2\right\rfloor}
where
m_{0}(\mu)
denotes the number of even parts of
\mu

[
BO97
, Theorem 5.2]
.
Pick
\mu=(2k-1,2k-5,\ldots,7,3)
or
\mu=(2k-1,2k-5,\ldots,5,1)
; it is easily verified that
\mathrm{dblreg}(\mu)=\lambda
and
2^{\left\lfloor m_{0}(\mu)/2\right\rfloor}=1
, and hence the
2
-modular reductions of the representations affording
\langle\mu\rangle
and
\chi^{\lambda}
are isomorphic.
Similar reasoning establishes isomorphisms between spin and non-spin representations in the
2
-block of weight
1
.
Nevertheless, not all such coincidences arise in this way (such as the example when
n=5
in the previous paragraph).

2.
(Spin) characters of symmetric and alternating groups

2.1.
Symmetric and alternating groups and their characters

Here we state well-known results on the character theory of the symmetric and alternating groups; for a complete account, see, for example,
[
JK84
, Chapter 2]
.

The ordinary irreducible characters of
S_{n}
are indexed by partitions of
n
, and we denote the character corresponding to
\lambda
as
\chi^{\lambda}
.
If
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\chi^{\lambda}\mathord{\downarrow}_{A_{n}}=\chi^{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathord{\downarrow}_{A_{n}}
is an irreducible character of
A_{n}
.
If
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\chi^{\lambda}\mathord{\downarrow}_{A_{n}}
splits into a conjugate pair of irreducible characters of
A_{n}
denoted
\chi^{\lambda^{\pm}}
(the signs can be assigned arbitrarily).
Moreover, we obtain a complete irredundant set of ordinary irreducible characters of
A_{n}
in this way.

We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
the
S_{n}
-conjugacy class of permutations of cycle type
\lambda
.
The
S_{n}
-conjugacy classes which split in
A_{n}
are precisely those of cycle type with distinct parts all of which are odd; for
\lambda
with distinct parts all odd, we denote the resulting
A_{n}
-conjugacy classes by
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
(the signs can be assigned arbitrarily).

A
principal hook length
of a partition refers to a hook length of a box on the main diagonal in the Young diagram of the partition.
For
\lambda
self-conjugate, the principal hook lengths of
\lambda
are distinct odd integers; write
\diag(\lambda)
for the partition whose parts are the principal hook lengths of
\lambda
.
The pair of characters of
A_{n}
labelled by a self-conjugate partition
\lambda
differ only on the split class of cycle type
\diag(\lambda)
, from which we deduce the infinite family of characters agreeing on
l^{\prime}
-classes stated in
Theorem

1.1
.

2.2.
Double covers and spin characters

Here we state elementary results on the projective representation theory of the symmetric and alternating groups; for a complete account, see, for example,
[
HH92
]
.

A projective representation of a group
G
is a group homomorphism
G\to\PGL(V)
for some
V
.
There is a correspondence between projective representations of
G
and linear representations of a central extension of
G
by its Schur multiplier.
When
n\geqslant 4
the Schur multiplier of the alternating and symmetric groups are cyclic of order
2
(except for
n\in\{6,7\}
for the alternating group), so it suffices to consider the linear representations of their double covers.

We use the double cover
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
described in
[
HH92
, pp. 18–19]
, which has generators
z,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle\makebox[56.46193pt][c]{$z^{2}=1;$}\quad\qquad\makebox[56.46193pt][c]{$t_{j}^{2}=z;$}\quad\qquad(t_{j}t_{j+1})^{3}=z;

\displaystyle t_{j}t_{k}=zt_{k}t_{j}\,\text{ for $\left\lvert j-k\right\rvert>1$}.

There is a projection map
\theta\colon\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}\to S_{n}
with kernel
\{1,z\}
.
Define the sign map
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}\to\{\pm 1\}
by composition of the usual sign map with
\theta
; its kernel is
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
, a double cover of the alternating group.

The image of
z
under a representation of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
or
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
is
\pm I
; if it is
I
, then the representation corresponds to a linear representation of
S_{n}
or
A_{n}
; if it is
-I
, then the representation is called
spin
and it corresponds to a projective representation of
S_{n}
or
A_{n}
which is not linear.
Since the linear representations of
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
are already described, we are interested in the spin representations of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
.

There is another choice of double cover of
S_{n}
(though not for
A_{n}
– both covers of
S_{n}
yield isomorphic covers of
A_{n}
)
[
HH92
, pp, 22-23]
.
However, the spin characters of one double cover can be obtained from the other by multiplying the values on
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}\setminus\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
by
i

[
ATLAS
, §6.7]
,
and hence the choice of double cover makes no difference to the determination of the character by its values.

For each strict partition (that is, partition with distinct parts)
\lambda
of
n
, there is a spin ordinary irreducible character
\langle\lambda\rangle
of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
.
Each character of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
has an
associate
, denoted
{-}^{\mathsf{a}}
, which takes values of opposite sign on odd elements.
We call a partition
even
or
odd
according to whether permutations of that cycle type are even or odd.
If
\lambda
is odd, then
\langle\lambda\rangle\neq\langle\lambda\rangle^{\mathsf{a}}
, and
\langle\lambda\rangle\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}}=\langle\lambda\rangle^{\mathsf{a}}\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}}
is irreducible and self-conjugate.
If
\lambda
is even, then
\langle\lambda\rangle=\langle\lambda\rangle^{\mathsf{a}}
, and
\langle\lambda\rangle\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}}
splits into a conjugate pair of irreducible characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
denoted
\langle\lambda\rangle^{\pm}
(the signs can be assigned arbitrarily).
We obtain complete irredundant sets of ordinary irreducible spin characters of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
and
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{A}$}}_{n}
in this way.

Write
\textstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}
for the preimage in
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
of an
S_{n}
-conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}
.
Say elements of
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}_{\lambda}
are of cycle type
\lambda
.
The set
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}}$}}_{\lambda}
is either itself an
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
-conjugacy class, or splits into two
\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{S}$}}_{n}
-conjugacy class.
Splitting occurs if and only if
\lambda
has all parts odd or
\lambda

3.
Character values and conjugacy of characters

Let
G
be a finite group.
Given a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}
, let
s_{\mathcal{C}}\in Z(\mathbb{Q}G)
denote the class sum, and given a character
\chi
we write
\chi(\mathcal{C})
for the value
\chi(g)
for any
g\in\mathcal{C}
.
More generally, if
\mathcal{D}
is a union of conjugacy classes of
G
, write
s_{\mathcal{D}}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}s_{\mathcal{C}}
for the sum of the constituent class sums, and
\chi(\mathcal{D})=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}\chi(\mathcal{C})
for the sum of character values on each constituent class.

Suppose
G
has a normal subgroup
H
.
Recall that any
G
-conjugacy class is either disjoint from
H
, or is a union of
H
-conjugacy classes of equal size.
The aim of this section is to prove a sufficient condition on the values of a pair of characters of
H
for the characters to be
G
-conjugate (
Proposition

5
).

3.1.
Central characters

The following is the key lemma which allows us to deduce further relations between character values from a given set.
This argument was used by Wildon in
[
Wil08
]
, though he considers only the case where the unions are single classes.

2
.

Let
\chi
and
\psi
be irreducible characters of
G
of equal degree.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a set of unions of
G
-conjugacy classes, such that all classes in a given union are of the same size.
Suppose that
\chi(\mathcal{D})=\psi(\mathcal{D})
for all
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{U}
.
If
\mathcal{E}
is a union of
G
-conjugacy classes of equal size such that
s_{\mathcal{E}}
lies in the algebra generated by
\{\,s_{\mathcal{D}}\,\mid\,\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{U}\,\}
,
then
\chi(\mathcal{E})=\psi(\mathcal{E})
.

To prove this lemma, we use central characters.
A
central character
of a group
G
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism
Z(\mathbb{Q}G)\to\mathbb{C}
(this is sometimes defined with other fields in place of
\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{C}
, but for our purposes this suffices).
Given an irreducible character
\chi
of
G
, there is a corresponding central character
\omega_{\chi}
which sends an element
\alpha\in Z(\mathbb{Q}G)
to the scalar by which
\alpha
acts on the representation afforded by
\chi
; thus on a conjugacy class sum
s_{\mathcal{C}}
the central character
\omega_{\chi}
is defined by

\omega_{\chi}(s_{\mathcal{C}})=\frac{\left\lvert\mathcal{C}\right\rvert\chi(\mathcal{C})}{\chi(1)}.

Proof of
Lemma

2
.

Write
d
for the common value
\chi(1)=\psi(1)
.
Let
\omega_{\chi}
,
\omega_{\psi}
denote the central characters corresponding to
\chi
and
\psi
respectively.
If
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{U}
is a union of conjugacy classes of size
m
, we have

\omega_{\chi}(s_{\mathcal{D}})=\frac{m}{d}\chi(\mathcal{D})=\frac{m}{d}\psi(\mathcal{D})=\omega_{\psi}(s_{\mathcal{D}}),

so
\omega_{\chi}
and
\omega_{\psi}
agree on
\{\,s_{\mathcal{D}}\,\mid\,\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{U}\,\}
and hence on the algebra that set generates.
Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a union of
G
-conjugacy class of equal size
r
such that
s_{\mathcal{E}}
lies in that algebra.
Then

\chi(\mathcal{E})=\frac{d}{r}\omega_{\chi}(s_{\mathcal{E}})=\frac{d}{r}\omega_{\psi}(s_{\mathcal{E}})=\psi(\mathcal{E}).\qed

Distinguishing characters up to conjugates

.

Let

4.1.
Centre of the group algebra of the alternating group

We will induct on the following statistic.

6
.

The
support
of a partition
\lambda
, denoted
\supp(\lambda)
, is the number of non-fixed points of a permutation of cycle type
\lambda
.

That is, the support of
\lambda
is the sum of the parts of
\lambda
strictly greater than
1
.

Throughout this section, for convenience we omit writing the parts of a partition which are equal to
1
, and allow a partition of any integer
m\leqslant n
to be viewed as a partition of
n
by appending
1
s as necessary.
Again for convenience, we do not necessarily write the parts of a partition in decreasing order.
These abuses of notation are acceptable here as they do not alter which conjugacy class of
S_{n}
is determined via cycle type.

Write
s_{\lambda}=s_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}
for the sum of the class
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
of permutations of cycle type
\lambda
.
Given partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, we say
\nu
is
involved in
the product
s_{\lambda}s_{\mu}
if
s_{\nu}
appears with nonzero (hence positive integer) coefficient in the product (when written with respect to the
S_{n}
-conjugacy class basis).
This occurs if and only if there exist permutations
\sigma,\tau,\rho
of cycle types
\lambda,\mu,\nu
such that
\sigma\tau=\rho
.

Given partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
,
let
\lambda\sqcup\mu
denote the partition consisting of all the parts of
\lambda
and
\mu
(parts strictly greater than
1
only, arranged in decreasing order).
The following simple fact concerning multiplication of class sums was used by Kramer to show that the set
\{\,s_{(i)}\,\mid\,1\leqslant i\leqslant n\,\}
generates
Z(\mathbb{Q}S_{n})

[
Kra66
]
.

7
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions such that
\supp(\lambda)+\supp(\mu)\leqslant n
.
The unique partition of support greater than or equal to
\supp(\lambda)+\supp(\mu)
which is involved in the product
s_{\lambda}s_{\mu}
is
\lambda\sqcup\mu
.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma,\tau
are permutations of cycle type
\lambda,\mu
respectively.
In order for the product
\sigma\tau
to have at least
\supp(\lambda)+\supp(\mu)
non-fixed points, the non-fixed points of
\sigma
and
\tau
must be disjoint, in which case
\sigma
and
\tau
commute and the product
\sigma\tau
has cycle type
\lambda\sqcup\mu
.
Indeed such permutations exist given
\supp(\lambda)+\supp(\mu)\leqslant n
.
∎

We require a stronger version of
Lemma

7
in the case of multiplying by a cycle.

8
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition with
m
parts (all strictly greater than
1
), and let
r>1
be a positive integer such that
\supp(\lambda)+r\leqslant n
.

(1)

The unique partition of support
\supp(\lambda)+r
involved in the product
s_{\lambda}s_{(r)}
is
\lambda\sqcup(r)
.

(2)

The partitions of support
\supp(\lambda)+r-1
involved in the product
s_{\lambda}s_{(r)}
are (up to reordering parts) precisely those of the form
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i}+r-1,\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
.

(3)

A partition of support
\supp(\lambda)+r-2
involved in the product
s_{\lambda}s_{(r)}
either has
m+1
parts strictly greater than
1
, or is (up to reordering parts) of the form
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+r-2,\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{j-1},\lambda_{j+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
for some
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m
.

Proof.

The first part is a special case of
Lemma

7
.
For the second part, observe that the product of two non-identity permutations has exactly one fewer non-fixed point than the sum of their numbers of non-fixed points if and only if the two permutations have exactly one non-fixed point in common.
The product of a
\lambda_{i}
-cycle and
r
-cycle with exactly one non-fixed point in common is a
(\lambda_{i}+r-1)
-cycle, yielding the specified form.

For the third part, observe that if the product of two non-identity permutations has exactly two fewer non-fixed points than the sum of their numbers of non-fixed points, then the two permutations have exactly two non-fixed point in common (though not conversely).
If an
r
-cycle has two non-fixed points in common with a
\lambda_{i}
-cycle, then either their product has cycle type
(a,b)
with
a+b=\lambda_{i}+r-2
and we are in the case of having
m+1
parts strictly greater than
1
, or the product is a
(\lambda_{i}+r-3)
-cycle and we do not obtain a permutation of the specified support; if an
r
-cycle has one non-fixed point in common with a
\lambda_{i}
-cycle and one non-fixed point in common with a disjoint
\lambda_{j}
-cycle, the product is an
(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+r-2)
-cycle and we are in the case of the form specified.
∎

4.1
.

Let
l\geqslant 4
and
n\geqslant l
.

The order of an element of

Otherwise, let

We consider three cases depending on the number and parity of the parts of

7

\lambda

Let

All elements of

Suppose

Since an element of a conjugacy class appears in a product of class sums if and only if all the elements of that class do (and since an element

(that is, that

Our strategy is to show that

.

such that

and

and

are conjugate in

and

and hence

There are two cases depending on the support of

and
\theta(h)
(and
\theta(g^{\prime})
and
\theta(h^{\prime})
) have in common.

If

be the lengths of the cycles in

containing this non-fixed point.
Without loss of generality, suppose these cycles are

and
(a\ \ a{+}1\ \cdots\ a{+}b{-}1)

\sigma
and
\tau

- and

-cycles in
\theta(g^{\prime})
and
\theta(h^{\prime})
whose product is an
(a{+}b{-}1)
-cycle

.
Since
\theta(gh)=\theta(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})
,
either

or

and
\rho
are disjoint.
If

9
to find a permutation by which

and

and

If

and

are disjoint, consider the product of disjoint transpositions swapping the entries of

and

(respecting the cyclic ordering).
In either case,

and
\theta(h^{\prime})
are conjugate by the chosen permutation to

and

respectively, as required.
∎

q q -Analogue of the degree zero part of a rational Cherednik algebra
Misha Feigin, Martin Vrabec

Abstract
Inside the double affine Hecke algebra ℍ n , q , τ \mathbb{H}_{n,q,\tau} of type G ​ L n GL_{n} , we define a subalgebra ℍ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}} that may be thought of as a q q -deformation of the degree zero part of the corresponding rational Cherednik algebra. We prove that the algebra ℍ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}} is a flat τ \tau -deformation of the semi-direct product of the group algebra ℂ ​ 𝔖 n \mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n} of the symmetric group with the image of the Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum group U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) under the q q -oscillator (Jordan–Schwinger) representation. We find all the defining relations and an explicit PBW basis for the algebra ℍ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}} . We describe its centre and establish a double centraliser property. Further, we develop the connection with integrable systems introduced by van Diejen, which we also generalise.



1
Introduction

Rational Cherednik algebras (RCA) are a remarkable class of algebras associated to finite Coxeter groups
W

[
18
]
. They have deep relations with integrable systems, geometry, and combinatorics, as well as a rich representation theory (see e.g.
[
16
]
).

They admit a faithful representation on a space of polynomials. In this representation, and in the case of the symmetric group
W=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(
n\geq 1
), the corresponding RCA
H_{n}=H_{n,c}
(
c\in\mathbb{C}
) acts on
\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, and it is generated by the transpositions
s_{ij}=(i,j)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, multiplication operators
X_{i}
, and the rational Dunkl operators
[
15
]

\nabla_{i}=\partial_{X_{i}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{c}{X_{i}-X_{j}}(1-s_{ij}).

Here
\partial_{X_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
is a partial derivative.

The RCA
H_{n}
is a graded algebra, where the grading is determined by assigning degree 0 to elements of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, degree
1
to the multiplication operators
X_{i}
, and degree
-1
to Dunkl operators.
The degree zero subalgebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}=H_{c}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is also interesting in its own right from various points of view.
It is generated by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and the operators
X_{i}\nabla_{j}
(
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
).

The algebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
enjoys, as the notation for it suggests, a link to Lie theory. More precisely, it is a flat
c
-deformation of the semi-direct product of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with a certain quotient
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})/I
of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
over a two-sided ideal
I
, as was established by Hakobyan and one of the authors in
[
19
]
.
The quotient
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})/I
is the image of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
under the so-called oscillator (also known as Jordan–Schwinger) representation
\rho_{\text{JS}}
that maps the standard generators of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
to the operators
X_{i}\partial_{X_{j}}
.

Similarly to the RCA itself, the algebra

H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is a quadratic algebra of Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) type. In contrast to the RCA, the defining relations of
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
include relations that are not of a commutator type. The associated graded algebra is the semi-direct product of
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with the algebra of polynomial functions on the space of
n\times n
complex matrices of rank at most one
[
19
]
.

The centre of the RCA is trivial
[
6
]
, but the RCA has a commutative subalgebra which acts (in the polynomial representation) on symmetric polynomials as the rational Calogero–Moser operator and its quantum integrals
[
22
]
.
On the other hand, the centre of the degree zero subalgebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is generated by the Euler operator
eu
, which can be related to the rational quantum Calogero–Moser Hamiltonian with an additional harmonic potential term by an automorphism of the RCA
[
19
]
. The central quotient
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}/(eu+\,\text{const})
is isomorphic to the algebra of global sections of a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on the projective space
[
17
,
3
]
.

In this paper, we generalise the above theory to the
q
-deformed setting by introducing and studying a certain subalgebra
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
inside Cherednik’s double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA)
\mathbb{H}_{n}=\mathbb{H}_{n,q,\tau}
of type
GL_{n}
. The role of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
will be played by the standard Drinfeld–Jimbo type quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. The latter admits a representation
\rho
which is a
q
-multiplicative generalisation of the map
\rho_{\text{JS}}
. We consider the image
A=\rho(U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
, and define an algebra
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes A
, where the symmetric group acts in a natural way. We then define inside the DAHA
\mathbb{H}_{n}
a subalgebra
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
whose generators are
\tau
-deformations of those of
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, in a suitable
q\to 1
limit, the algebra
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
reduces to the degree zero part
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
of the RCA.

We give all the defining relations of
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
, and show that it is an algebra of PBW type by explicitly constructing a PBW basis. We show that
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is a flat
\tau
-deformation of the algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
We prove that the centre of
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is generated by a single invertible element
\widetilde{Y}
. When
q\to 1
, the central element
(1-q)^{-1}(1-\widetilde{Y})
reduces to the generator
eu
of the centre
\mathcal{Z}(H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}})
.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the definitions and properties of the rational and trigonometric Cherednik algebras associated to the group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In Section

, we review

and its Jordan–Schwinger representation

. We define the algebras
A

, and study their properties. In Section
4.1
, we recall the definition of the DAHA

. It contains pairwise-commuting elements

which can be thought of as a
q
-generalisation of the Dunkl operators, and which we use in Section
4.2
to define the algebra
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
. We study
D_{i}
themselves more closely in Section
4.3
. In Section
4.4
, we give all the defining relations of
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
and a linear basis for it. In Section
5
, we describe its centre and give a double centraliser property that
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
satisfies as a subalgebra of a certain generalisation of the
q
-Weyl algebra contained inside the DAHA.

In Section
6
, we consider pairwise-commuting elements
\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathcal{D}^{(l_{1},l_{2})}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
of a more general form than
D_{i}
. The former depend on additional parameters
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
(
j=-l_{1},\dots,l_{2}
). In the case
l_{2}=0
, they are equivalent to the DAHA elements considered by Braverman, Etingof, and Finkelberg in
[
4
]
. By looking at the action of symmetric combinations of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
on the space of symmetric Laurent polynomials, we arrive at families of known as well as new commuting
q
-difference operators related to the Macdonald–Ruijsenaars system with Morse term introduced van Diejen
[
13
,
14
]
.
More specifically, in the case of
l_{2}=0
and
l_{1}=1
, such an operator appeared in the paper
[
2
]
by Baker and Forrester. A more general version of
their
q
-difference operator was found earlier by van Diejen without using
q
-Dunkl operators

13
]
.
Higher

[

, which recover as a special case Chalykh’s operators from
[
8
]
.

, we generalise van Diejen’s operator to a Hamiltonian involving two types of particles, and we explain how to obtain quantum integrals for it.

2
Rational and trigonometric Cherednik algebras of type
GL_{n}

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
be a parameter. The RCA
H_{n}=H_{n,c}
of type
GL_{n}
is the (unital, associative) algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by the simple transpositions
s_{k}=(k,k+1)
from the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(
1\leq k\leq n-1
), and elements
X_{i}
and
y_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) subject to the following relations
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle[y_{i},y_{j}]=0=[X_{i},X_{j}],

\displaystyle s_{k}X_{k}s_{k}=X_{k+1},\quad\ [s_{k},X_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle s_{k}y_{k}s_{k}=y_{k+1},\quad\ \ \ [s_{k},y_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle S_{ij}\coloneqq[y_{i},X_{j}]=\begin{cases}1-c\sum_{l\neq i}s_{il}\textnormal{ if }i=j,\\
cs_{ij}\textnormal{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Here
j,l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
s_{ij}
denote the transpositions
(i,j)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. The bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the commutator.

The algebra
H_{n}
admits a faithful representation, called the polynomial representation, on the space of polynomials
\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
The elements
s_{k}
act by swapping
X_{k}
and
X_{k+1}
, the elements
X_{i}
act by multiplication, and
y_{i}
act as the standard commuting rational Dunkl operators
\nabla_{i}
introduced in
[
15
]
, which
c
-deform
\partial_{X_{i}}

=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
and are given by

\nabla_{i}=\partial_{X_{i}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{c}{X_{i}-X_{j}}(1-s_{ij}).

The trigonometric Cherednik algebra (also known as degenerate double affine Hecke algebra)
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig}=\mathbb{H}_{n,c}^{\trig}
of type
GL_{n}
is the (unital, associative) algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
s_{k}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(
1\leq k\leq n-1
), and elements
X_{i}^{\pm 1}
and
\widehat{y}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle[\widehat{y}_{i},\widehat{y}_{j}]=0=[X_{i},X_{j}],\qquad X_{i}X_{i}^{-1}=X_{i}^{-1}X_{i}=1,

\displaystyle s_{k}X_{k}s_{k}=X_{k+1},\qquad\quad\quad[s_{k},X_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle s_{k}\widehat{y}_{k+1}-\widehat{y}_{k}s_{k}=c,\qquad\quad[s_{k},\widehat{y}_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle(\widehat{y}_{1}+\cdots+\widehat{y}_{n})X_{i}=X_{i}(1+\widehat{y}_{1}+\cdots+\widehat{y}_{n}),

\displaystyle\widehat{y}_{2}-X_{1}\widehat{y}_{2}X_{1}^{-1}=cs_{1},

where
1\leq j\leq n
.
These can be obtained by taking the relations of the DAHA of type
GL_{n}
given in
[
11
, p. 100]
and performing a trigonometric degeneration (see Section
4.1
below).
Slightly different but equivalent sets of generators and relations are used in
[
1
]
(see also
[
28
, Section 3]
).

The algebra
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig}
admits a faithful representation, called the polynomial representation, on the space of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. The elements
s_{k}
act by swapping
X_{k}
and
X_{k+1}
, while
X_{i}^{\pm 1}
act by multiplication, and
\widehat{y}_{i}
act as the commuting trigonometric Dunkl operators
[
9
]

\nabla_{i}^{\trig}=X_{i}\partial_{X_{i}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{c}{1-X_{j}X_{i}^{-1}}(1-s_{ij})-c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j>i\end{subarray}}^{n}s_{ij}=X_{i}\nabla_{i}-c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j>i\end{subarray}}^{n}s_{ij}.

From the respective polynomial representations, one sees that there is an algebra embedding of
H_{n}
into
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig}
given as follows
[
28
, Proposition 4.1(i)]
:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle s_{k}\mapsto s_{k},\qquad X_{i}\mapsto X_{i},

(2.2)

\displaystyle y_{i}\mapsto X_{i}^{-1}\left(\widehat{y}_{i}+c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j>i\end{subarray}}^{n}s_{ij}\right)=X_{i}^{-1}(s_{i}s_{i+1}\cdots s_{n-1})\widehat{y}_{n}(s_{n-1}\cdots s_{i+1}s_{i}).

On the other hand, the degenerate
affine
Hecke algebra that is the subalgebra of
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig}
generated by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\mathbb{C}[\widehat{y}_{1},\dots,\widehat{y}_{n}]
embeds into
H_{n}
via
s_{k}\mapsto s_{k}
,
\widehat{y}_{i}\mapsto X_{i}y_{i}-c\sum_{j>i}s_{ij}

[
18
, Proposition 4.3(ii)]
.

2.1
Degree zero part of
H_{n}

The RCA
H_{n}
admits a grading in which
\deg s_{k}=0
,
\deg X_{i}=1
, and
\deg y_{i}=-1
. The subalgebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}=H_{n}^{(0)}
of those elements that have degree zero was studied in
[
19
]
. It is generated by the elements
s_{k}
and the products
E_{ij}\coloneqq X_{i}y_{j}
(
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
) with relations

\displaystyle s_{k}E_{ij}=E_{s_{k}(i),s_{k}(j)}s_{k},

\displaystyle E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{il}E_{kj}=E_{il}S_{jk}-E_{ij}S_{kl},

\displaystyle E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{kj}E_{il}=S_{jk}E_{il}-S_{ij}E_{kl}.

Equivalently, the third set of relations could be replaced by the commutator-type relations

[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=E_{il}S_{jk}-S_{il}E_{kj}+[S_{kl},E_{ij}].

The associated graded algebra is
\gr H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}=\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}]
, where

\mathcal{M}=\{M\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\colon\rk M\leq 1\}.

The algebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
admits a PBW-type basis consisting of monomials

wE_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{k_{r}},

where
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
k_{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}\leq n
, and
1\leq j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{r}\leq n
with
i_{u}=i_{u+1}\Rightarrow j_{u}<j_{u+1}
.
The algebra is a flat
c
-deformation of
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes\rho_{\text{JS}}(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
, where
\rho_{\text{JS}}
is the oscillator (also known as Jordan–Schwinger) representation of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
mapping the standard basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
to the operators
X_{i}\partial_{X_{j}}
(
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
).
The algebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
is an example of a non-homogeneous quadratic algebra over
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of PBW type (cf.
[
5
]
).

The element
eu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}y_{i}-c\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}
(which in the polynomial representation is equal up to a constant to the Euler operator
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\partial_{X_{i}}
) generates the centre
\mathcal{Z}(H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}})
.

The algebra
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig}
also has a grading, given by
\deg s_{k}=\deg\widehat{y}_{i}=0
and
\deg X_{i}^{\pm 1}=\pm 1

[
4
]
. The embedding (
2.2
) preserves the respective gradings. The degree zero part
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig,\,(0)}
is generated by the set of elements
s_{k}
,
\widehat{y}_{i}
, and
X_{i}X_{j}^{-1}
. The algebra
H^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}=H_{n}^{(0)}
embeds into
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\trig,\,(0)}
via a restriction of the mapping (
2.2
).

3
Quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be not a root of unity.
The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is the (unital, associative) algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
g_{i}^{\pm 1}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) and
e_{k}
,
f_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq n-1
) subject to the following relations
[
25
, p. 163–164]
(cf.
[
23
]
):

\displaystyle g_{i}g_{i}^{-1}=g_{i}^{-1}g_{i}=1,\qquad[g_{i},g_{j}]=0,

\displaystyle g_{i}e_{k}g_{i}^{-1}=q^{\delta_{ik}-\delta_{i,k+1}}e_{k},\qquad g_{i}f_{k}g_{i}^{-1}=q^{\delta_{i,k+1}-\delta_{ik}}f_{k},

\displaystyle[e_{k},f_{l}]=\delta_{kl}\frac{g_{k}g_{k+1}^{-1}-g_{k}^{-1}g_{k+1}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle[e_{k},e_{l}]=0=[f_{k},f_{l}]\qquad\textnormal{ if }|k-l|>1,

\displaystyle e_{k}^{2}e_{l}-(q+q^{-1})e_{k}e_{l}e_{k}+e_{l}e_{k}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle f_{k}^{2}f_{l}-(q+q^{-1})f_{k}f_{l}f_{k}+f_{l}f_{k}^{2}=0\qquad\textnormal{ if }|k-l|=1.

Here
1\leq j\leq n
,
1\leq l\leq n-1
, and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
It follows from these relations that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}
lies in the centre
\mathcal{Z}(U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
of this algebra, and
[
23
, Proposition 4]
provides some further central elements.

In this section, we recall a representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
on the space of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. Let us firstly set up some notations.
Let
t_{i}=q^{X_{i}\partial_{X_{i}}}
be the
q
-shift operator which acts on functions
f
by
(t_{i}f)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=f(X_{1},\dots,qX_{i},\dots,X_{n})
. Let us also consider the following operator

d_{i}=\frac{X_{i}^{-1}(t_{i}-t_{i}^{-1})}{q-q^{-1}}.

(3.1)

In the
q\to 1
limit, it satisfies
d_{i}\to\partial_{X_{i}}
.

The next lemma collects some properties of
d_{i}
and
t_{i}
, which can be checked by a direct computation. We use the notation
[a,b]_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
to mean
ab-\lambda ba
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

1.

[t_{i},X_{j}]_{q^{\delta_{ij}}}=0
,

2.

[d_{i},d_{j}]=0=[t_{i},t_{j}]
,

3.

[d_{i},t_{j}]_{q^{\delta_{ij}}}=0
,

4.

d_{i}X_{i}=(q-q^{-1})^{-1}(qt_{i}-q^{-1}t_{i}^{-1})
, and
[d_{i},X_{j}]_{q^{\pm\delta_{ij}}}=\delta_{ij}t_{i}^{\mp 1}
.

In terms of
d_{i}
,
t_{i}
, and the multiplication operators
X_{i}
, one can write down a representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
on
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
as follows. It is called the Jordan–Schwinger or
q
-oscillator representation.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
12
]

There is a representation
\rho
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
given on the generators by
\rho\colon g_{i}^{\pm 1}\mapsto t_{i}^{\pm 1}
,
e_{k}\mapsto X_{k}d_{k+1}
, and
f_{k}\mapsto X_{k+1}d_{k}
.

In fact, this representation
\rho
has a submodule
\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, which we revisit later in Section
5
.

Let us consider the algebra
A
generated by the images of the generators of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
under the representation
\rho
:

A=\langle t_{i}^{\pm 1}\ (1\leq i\leq n),\ X_{k}d_{k+1},\ X_{k+1}d_{k}\ (1\leq k\leq n-1)\rangle.

By the isomorphism theorems,
A
is isomorphic to
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})/I_{q}
for
I_{q}=\ker(\rho)
.
We next describe the algebra
A
abstractly by (a different set of) generators and relations.

Let
E_{ij}^{q}=X_{i}d_{j}
(
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
).
Thus,
E_{ii}^{q}=(q-q^{-1})^{-1}(t_{i}-t_{i}^{-1})\in A
.
The operators
E_{ij}^{q}
for
|i-j|>1
are related to Jimbo’s analogue of the non-simple root vectors of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
from
[
23
, Proposition 1]
. The following formulae hold for all
1\leq i<j\leq n-1

[
12
, (3.3)]
:

\displaystyle E_{i,j+1}^{q}=\rho\left([e_{i},[e_{i+1},\cdots[e_{j-1},e_{j}]_{q}\cdots]_{q}]_{q}\ g_{i+1}g_{i+2}\cdots g_{j}\right),

\displaystyle E_{j+1,i}^{q}=\rho\left([f_{j},[f_{j-1},\cdots[f_{i+1},f_{i}]_{q^{-1}}\cdots]_{q^{-1}}]_{q^{-1}}g_{i+1}^{-1}\ g_{i+2}^{-1}\cdots g_{j}^{-1}\right).

It follows that
E_{ij}^{q}\in A
for all
i,j
, and
that
A=\langle t_{i}^{\pm 1},\ E_{ij}^{q}\ (i\neq j)\rangle
as algebra.

In the
q\to 1
limit, the representation
\rho
recovers the oscillator representation of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, since
\lim_{q\to 1}E_{ij}^{q}=X_{i}\partial_{X_{j}}
for all
i,j
.

The next two propositions describe relations satisfied by the generators
E_{ij}^{q}
and
t_{i}
.
Let us introduce the notation

S_{ij}^{q}\coloneqq[d_{i},X_{j}]=\begin{cases}(q+1)^{-1}\left(qt_{i}+t_{i}^{-1}\right)\textnormal{ if }i=j,\\
0\textnormal{ if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

where the second equality follows from Lemma
3.1
(4).
Then the following proposition is a straightforward consequence of the definitions and the commutativity of the operators
d_{i}
. (It will also follow from the
\tau=1

Proposition 3.3
.

For all
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle E_{ij}^{q}E_{kl}^{q}-E_{il}^{q}E_{kj}^{q}=E_{il}^{q}S_{jk}^{q}-E_{ij}^{q}S_{lk}^{q},

(3.2)

\displaystyle E_{ij}^{q}E_{kl}^{q}-E_{kj}^{q}E_{il}^{q}=S_{jk}^{q}E_{il}^{q}-S_{ji}^{q}E_{kl}^{q}.

The following statement holds as a result of Lemma
3.1
(1) and (3).

Proposition 3.4
.

For all
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

t_{i}E_{jk}^{q}t_{i}^{-1}=q^{\delta_{ij}-\delta_{ik}}E_{jk}^{q}.

(3.3)

The preceding two propositions lead to a PBW-type basis and presentation for the algebra
A
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The elements

\displaystyle(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}},

(3.4)

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
k_{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
m_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}\leq n
,
1\leq j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{r}\leq n
with
i_{u}=i_{u+1}\Rightarrow j_{u}<j_{u+1}
, and none of the indices
i_{u}
equal any of the indices
j_{v}
,
form a (linear) basis of the algebra
A
.

The algebra
A
has a presentation by generators
t_{i}^{\pm 1}
,
E_{ij}^{q}\ (i\neq j)
and relations (
3.2
) with
i\neq j
and
k\neq l
, (
3.3
) with
j\neq k
, and the Laurent relations for
t_{i}^{\pm 1}
, namely
t_{i}t_{i}^{-1}=t_{i}^{-1}t_{i}=1
and
[t_{i},t_{j}]=0
for all
i,j
.

Proof.

It follows from the relations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that any element of
A
can be written as a linear combination of elements of the form (
3.4
), thus they span
A
. We now show that they are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}
as operators on
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, by using Lemma
3.1
we get

\displaystyle(E_{ij}^{q})^{k}=(q-q^{-1})^{-k}X_{i}^{k}X_{j}^{-k}\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}(q^{-l}t_{j}-q^{l}t_{j}^{-1}).

More generally,

(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}\sim X_{i_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{r}}^{k_{r}}X_{j_{1}}^{-k_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{r}}^{-k_{r}}t^{k_{1}}_{j_{1}}\cdots t^{k_{r}}_{j_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}+\,\dots,

where
\dots
denotes terms in which the overall sum of the exponents of the
t_{l}
’s is lower than in the above leading term, and
\sim
denotes proportionality by a non-zero factor, which may depend on
q
.

Assume a non-trivial linear dependence of some terms of the form (
3.4
). This implies a non-trivial linear dependence of their corresponding leading (with highest degree in
t_{l}
) terms

X_{i_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{r}}^{k_{r}}X_{j_{1}}^{-k_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{r}}^{-k_{r}}t^{k_{1}}_{j_{1}}\cdots t^{k_{r}}_{j_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}.

(3.5)

By the assumptions on the indices of the monomials in (
3.4
), all the monomials in (
3.5
) are different, and
since
\prod_{l=1}^{n}X_{l}^{n_{l}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{n^{\prime}_{l}}
(
n_{l},n^{\prime}_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
) are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}
as operators on
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
(we are using here that
q
is not a root of unity), we get a contradiction. The statement follows.
∎

The above basis allows for the following proof of what the centre of
A
is.

Proposition 3.6
.

The centre
\mathcal{Z}(A)
is generated by
(\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i})^{\pm 1}
.

Proof.

If
i_{1}=i_{2}=\cdots=i_{\widetilde{r}}<i_{\widetilde{r}+1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}
then

t_{i_{1}}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}\right)t_{i_{1}}^{-1}=q^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\widetilde{r}}}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}.

This fact, together with our assumption that
q
is not a root of unity, implies for any
f\in\mathcal{Z}(A)
that its expansion in the PBW basis from Proposition
3.5
cannot involve any basis elements for which
r>0
.

Similarly, since for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
we have

\left(\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}\right)E_{k,k+1}^{q}=q^{m_{k}-m_{k+1}}E_{k,k+1}^{q}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}},

we get that the expansion of
f
can contain only terms of the form
\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}
where all
m_{l}
are equal. Conversely, all such terms do belong to the centre. The statement follows.
∎

Additionally, let us consider the semi-direct product
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes A
with the natural action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
given by

s_{k}t_{i}^{\pm 1}=t_{s_{k}(i)}^{\pm 1}s_{k},\qquad s_{k}E_{ij}^{q}=E_{s_{k}(i),s_{k}(j)}^{q}s_{k},

(3.6)

1\leq k\leq n-1
, where
s_{k}=(k,k+1)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
This action is well-defined as it preserves the defining relations of
A
given in Proposition
3.5
.
The algebra
\mathcal{A}
has a presentation analogous to that of
A
, just with the extra generators
s_{k}
and the extra relations (
3.6
) along with the Coxeter relations among
s_{k}
that hold in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
A basis of PBW type for
\mathcal{A}
consists of elements of the form

\displaystyle w(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}},\qquad\qquad(w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n})

(3.7)

with the same restrictions on the indices as above in Proposition
3.5
.

Proposition 3.7
.

The centre of
\mathcal{A}
satisfies
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{Z}(A)=\langle(\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i})^{\pm 1}\rangle
.

Proof.

Since all elements of
\mathcal{Z}(A)
are
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant by Proposition
3.6
, we have
\mathcal{Z}(A)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
. As
A
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\cap A\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(A)
. We will argue that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq A
, in other words
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\cap A=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
, from which it will follow that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(A)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
, as required.

Denote the elements of the basis (
3.7
) schematically as
wET
where
E=(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{q})^{k_{1}}\cdots(E_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{q})^{k_{r}}
and
T=\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{l}}
. We have
t_{i}wET\sim wETt_{w^{-1}(i)}
, where the proportionality factor is a power of
q
. For any
f\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
, in its expansion in the basis (
3.7
) consider all the terms that have the same
w
and
E
parts. They have the form
wE\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}
, where
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
T^{(k)}=\prod_{l=1}^{n}t_{l}^{m_{kl}}
. Any
t_{i}
has to commute individually with each such group by Proposition
3.5
. Suppose
w\neq\id
. Take
i,j
such that
j=w^{-1}(i)\neq i
. We have
t_{j}E=q^{a}Et_{j}
for some
a
. Commutativity requires

wE\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}t_{i}=t_{i}wE\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}=q^{a}wE\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}t_{j},

or equivalently,
wE(q^{a}-t_{i}t_{j}^{-1})\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}=0
, which forces
\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}T^{(k)}=0
.

This showed for any
f\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
that its expansion in the basis (
3.7
) cannot involve any basis elements for which
w\neq\id
, as was required.
∎

In the next section, we define inside the DAHA of type

a subalgebra that deforms the algebra

in a natural way.

4

4.1
DAHA of type

We start by recalling the definition of the DAHA of type

.
Let

be a formal parameter, and

not a root of unity. Let
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}

(

\displaystyle(T_{k}-\tau)(T_{k}+\tau^{-1})=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle T_{l}T_{l+1}T_{l}=T_{l+1}T_{l}T_{l+1}\quad(1\leq l\leq n-2),\quad\ [T_{k},T_{l}]=0\textnormal{ if }|k-l|>1,

\displaystyle T_{k}X_{k}T_{k}=X_{k+1},\qquad[T_{k},X_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

(4.3)

\displaystyle[X_{i},X_{j}]=0,\ 1\leq j\leq n;\textnormal{ similarly for }Y_{i}^{\pm 1}\textnormal{)},

As in

and

The algebra
\mathbb{H}_{n}
admits a faithful representation, known as the polynomial representation, on the space of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. This representation is determined by

\displaystyle T_{k}\mapsto\tau s_{k}+\frac{\tau-\tau^{-1}}{X_{k}X_{k+1}^{-1}-1}(s_{k}-1),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}(X_{1}^{a_{1}}X_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}})=q^{a_{1}}X_{1}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{a_{n}}X_{n}^{a_{1}}\qquad(a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}),

The trigonometric degeneration of

4.3
Properties of
D_{i}

We begin by some technical preliminaries. The following lemma and its corollary record some braid group identities.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
n-1\geq k>j\geq i\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{j+1}^{\varepsilon}T_{ik}^{+}=T_{ik}^{+}T_{j}^{\varepsilon},\qquad T_{ki}^{-}T_{j+1}^{\varepsilon}=T_{j}^{\varepsilon}T_{ki}^{-}.

Proof.

By using the braid relations, we compute

T_{j+1}^{\varepsilon}T_{ik}^{+}=T_{i,j-1}^{+}T_{j+1}^{\varepsilon}T_{j}T_{j+1}T_{j+2,k}^{+}=T_{i,j-1}^{+}T_{j}T_{j+1}T_{j}^{\varepsilon}T_{j+2,k}^{+}=T_{ik}^{+}T_{j}^{\varepsilon},

as required. Similarly for the other relation.
∎

The following is a straightforward corollary of the preceding lemma.

Corollary 4.3
.

For all
n\geq j>i\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

1.

(T^{\varepsilon})_{j,n-1}^{+}T_{i,n-1}^{+}=T_{i,n-1}^{+}(T^{\varepsilon})_{j-1,n-2}^{+}
,

2.

(T^{\varepsilon})_{n-1,j}^{-}T_{i,n-1}^{+}=T_{i,n-1}^{+}(T^{\varepsilon})_{n-2,j-1}^{-}
,

3.

T_{n-1,i}^{-}(T^{\varepsilon})_{n-1,j}^{-}=(T^{\varepsilon})_{n-2,j-1}^{-}T_{n-1,i}^{-}
,

4.

T_{n-1,i}^{-}(T^{\varepsilon})_{j,n-1}^{+}=(T^{\varepsilon})_{j-1,n-2}^{+}T_{n-1,i}^{-}
.

The next lemma gives some identities for
(\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon})_{ji}^{\pm}
in the Hecke algebra.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
n\geq j>i\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

(\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon})_{ji}^{\pm}=(T^{-\varepsilon})_{i,j-2}^{+}T_{j-1}^{\pm 2}(T^{\varepsilon})_{j-2,i}^{-}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon=-1
.
The claim trivially holds if

, so let

Proposition 5.5
.

The algebra
\mathfrak{A}
has a

-basis

where

,

Proof.

Consider any monomial

,
X_{i}
,
D_{i}
,

. Firstly, we will show that we can write

In step one, we use the relations from Lemma

We then repeat steps one and two until in each monomial term all

We now apply Lemma

Assume a non-trivial linear dependence of some monomials (

Lemma 5.6

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}=C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\widetilde{Y}),

has a natural grading such that its faithful polynomial representation is a graded one. The element

Let us now provide a proof of relations (

From Theorem

, it follows that
\mathbb{H}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\widetilde{Y})

Suppose now that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathcal{D}^{(l_{1},l_{2})}_{i}

\displaystyle=X_{i}^{-1}(T^{-1})_{i,n-1}^{+}\left(\sum_{j=-l_{1}}^{l_{2}}a_{j}Y_{n}^{j}\right)T_{n-1,i}^{-}.

(6.1)

We have
T_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{k}T_{k}^{-1}=\mathcal{D}_{k+1}

by an analogous proof as for Lemma

.
In Proposition

below, we prove that
\mathcal{D}_{i}
pairwise commute. Let us first set up some tools to be used in the proof.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{-}
be the (unital, associative)
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}
-algebra generated by
T_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq n-1
) and
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}]
,
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[Y_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,Y_{n}^{\pm 1}]
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle(T_{k}-\tau)(T_{k}+\tau^{-1})=0,

\displaystyle T_{l}T_{l+1}T_{l}=T_{l+1}T_{l}T_{l+1}\quad(1\leq l\leq n-2),\quad\ [T_{k},T_{l}]=0\textnormal{ if }|k-l|>1,

\displaystyle T_{k}^{-1}Z_{k}T_{k}^{-1}=Z_{k+1},\qquad[T_{k},Z_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle T_{k}^{-1}Y_{k}T_{k}^{-1}=Y_{k+1},\quad\,[T_{k},Y_{i}]=0\textnormal{ for }i\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}Z_{i}=q^{-1}Z_{i}\widetilde{Y},

\displaystyle Y_{2}Z_{1}=Z_{1}Y_{2}T_{1}^{2},

(6.4)

where

There is an algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi(T_{k})=T_{k},\quad\phi(Z_{i})=X_{i}^{-1},\quad\phi(Y_{i}^{\pm 1})=Y_{i}^{\pm 1},

whose image contains the elements
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.

The next proposition gives a family of endomorphisms of the algebra
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{-}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}]
be an arbitrary single-variable Laurent polynomial. There is an endomorphism
\theta=\theta_{f}
of
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{-}
given by
\theta(T_{k})=T_{k}
,
\theta(Y_{i})=Y_{i}
, and

\theta(Z_{i})=T_{i,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}\qquad\left(=Z_{i}(T^{-1})_{i,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to check that
\theta
preserves the relations (
6.2
)–(
6.4
).
Firstly,

\theta(T_{k}^{-1}Z_{k}T_{k}^{-1})=T_{k+1,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,k+1}^{-}=\theta(Z_{k+1}),

as required. Suppose now that
i\neq k,k+1
. Then either
i>k+1
, in which case it is easy to see that
\theta(T_{k})
commutes with
\theta(Z_{i})
. Or
i<k
, in which case twice using Lemma
4.2
and that
[T_{k-1},Z_{n}]=0=[T_{k-1},f(Y_{n})]
, we get

\displaystyle\theta(T_{k}Z_{i})=T_{k}T_{i,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}=T_{i,n-1}^{+}T_{k-1}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}

\displaystyle=T_{i,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{k-1}T_{n-1,i}^{-}=T_{i,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}T_{k}=\theta(Z_{i}T_{k}).

This completes the proof that
\theta
preserves relations (
6.2
).

Secondly, we have

\displaystyle\theta(\widetilde{Y}Z_{i})=T_{i,n-1}^{+}\widetilde{Y}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}=q^{-1}T_{i,n-1}^{+}Z_{n}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,i}^{-}\widetilde{Y}=q^{-1}\theta(Z_{i}\widetilde{Y}),

hence
\theta
preserves relations (
6.3
).

Finally, since
\theta(Z_{1})=Z_{1}(T^{-1})_{1,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,1}^{-}
, we see, due to relation (
6.4
), that it will follow that
\theta(Y_{2}^{-1}Z_{1}Y_{2})=\theta(Z_{1}T_{1}^{-2})
if we show

\displaystyle T_{1}^{-1}Y_{2}^{-1}(T^{-1})_{1,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,1}^{-}Y_{2}=(T^{-1})_{2,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,2}^{-}T_{1}^{-1}.

The left-hand side of that can be rearranged as

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle(T^{-1})_{2,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,1}^{-}Y_{2}=(T^{-1})_{2,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,2}^{-}Y_{1}^{-1}T_{1}Y_{2}

\displaystyle=(T^{-1})_{2,n-1}^{+}f(Y_{n})T_{n-1,2}^{-}T_{1}^{-1},

as required. Thus,
\theta
preserves the relation (
6.4
) as well.
∎

The elements
\mathcal{D}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
defined by (

Proposition 6.2
.

We have
[\mathcal{D}_{i},\mathcal{D}_{j}]=0
for all

.

Proof.

Let

Lemma 6.5
.

We have
[T_{k},D]=0
for any

-type affine Hecke algebra, whose centre

[

[

We have

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
. Then the action of
D
on
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
preserves the space of invariants

\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
.

Proof.

From formula (
4.6
), it follows that
p\in\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant if and only if
T_{k}(p)=\tau p
for all
k
.
The claim thus follows from the fact that
D
commutes with all

by Lemma

.
∎

Let
f
be any operator on
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
of the form

f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\\
j\in\mathbb{Z},\,w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}g_{i,j,w}t_{i}^{j}w,\qquad g_{i,j,w}\in\mathbb{C}_{\tau}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

For instance, the action of any
D\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
can be written in that form. The operator
\Res(f)
is defined by

\Res(f)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\\
j\in\mathbb{Z},\,w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}g_{i,j,w}t_{i}^{j}.

Thus,
\Res(f)
is a
q
-difference operator with rational coefficients. On elements of the space
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
it acts identically to
f
. In particular, if the latter preserves this space then so does
\Res(f)
.

We note that the elements
D
are not invariant with regard to the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, but the operators
\Res(D)
are.

Theorem 6.7
.

The operators
\Res(D)
for
D\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
are pairwise-commuting,
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant, and preserve the space

\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
.

Proof.

Preservation of
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
follows from Proposition
6.6
.

Let
D,\widetilde{D}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
and
p\in\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
. By using Proposition
6.2
, we get
\Res(D)\Res(\widetilde{D})p=D\widetilde{D}p=\widetilde{D}Dp=\Res(\widetilde{D})\Res(D)p
.
Thus
\Res(D)
and
\Res(\widetilde{D})
commute when restricted to
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
, which implies the commutativity of
\Res(D)
and
\Res(\widetilde{D})
(see
[
24
, Theorem 4.5]
and
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
).

For any
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have
w\Res(D)w^{-1}p=Dp
thanks to Proposition
6.6
. Thus
w\Res(D)w^{-1}
and
\Res(D)
are equal as operators on
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
.
As in the preceding paragraph, it follows that
w\Res(D)w^{-1}=\Res(D)
.
∎

Explicitly, for
l_{1}=l_{2}=1
, and for the degree one symmetric combination
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}
, we get the following formula for the corresponding integrable Hamiltonian.

Proposition 6.8
.

With
a=a_{1}
,
b=a_{-1}
, and
c=a_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{a,b,c}\coloneqq\Res

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{(1,1)}\right)=a\tau^{1-n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\tau^{2}X_{i}-X_{j}}{X_{i}-X_{j}}\right)t_{i}

(6.7)

\displaystyle+b\tau^{1-n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{X_{i}-\tau^{2}X_{j}}{X_{i}-X_{j}}\right)t_{i}^{-1}+c\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}.

The proof will follow from the next lemma. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle=X_{i}^{-1}(T^{-1})_{i,n-1}^{+}\pi^{-1}(T^{-1})_{1,i-1}^{+},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle=X_{i}^{-1}T_{i-1,1}^{-}\pi T_{n-1,i}^{-},

so that by relation (

\Res\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{(1,1)}\right)=a\Res\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{+}\right)+b\Res\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{-}\right)+c\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}.

Then the following statement holds.

Lemma 6.9

(cf.
[
2
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

For all
m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, if we let

E_{m}^{+}=\tau^{1-n}\sum_{i=m}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}A_{i,m}t_{i}\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad A_{i,m}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=m\\

\Res\left(\sum_{i=m}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{+}\right)=E_{m}^{+}.

(6.8)

And if we let

E_{m}^{-}=\tau^{n-2m+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{X_{i}}B_{i,m}t_{i}^{-1}\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad B_{i,m}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

The proof is analogous to that of

2

Proof.

We give the proof of equality (

) works similarly.
By using formulas (
4.6
) and (

), we get

\Res(\mathcal{D}_{i}^{-})=\tau^{n-i}\Res(X_{i}^{-1}T_{i-1,1}^{-}t_{1}^{-1})=\tau^{n-i}\Res\left((T^{-1})_{i-1,1}^{-}X_{1}^{-1}t_{1}^{-1}\right).

For

For more general values of

and

Proposition 6.11

We have

\displaystyle\Res

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{(l_{1},l_{2})}\right)=\tau^{l_{2}(1-n)}a_{l_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{l_{2}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

\displaystyle+\tau^{l_{1}(1-n)}a_{-l_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{l_{1}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

The proof is similar to the calculation of the leading term of a general Macdonald operator, polynomial in

Proof.

By using (

\displaystyle\Res

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{(l_{1},l_{2})}\bigg)=\Res\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{2}}a_{j}\left((T^{-1})_{i,n-1}^{+}t_{n}(n,\dots,1)(T^{-1})_{1,i-1}^{+}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{1}}a_{-j}\left(T_{i-1,1}^{-}t_{1}^{-1}(1,\dots,n)T_{n-1,i}^{-}\right)^{j}+a_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{-1}\bigg).

From there we see, due to formulas (
6.5
), that the term containing
t_{1}^{l_{2}}
can only come from

\Res\bigg(a_{l_{2}}X_{1}^{-1}\left((T^{-1})_{1,n-1}^{+}t_{n}(n,\dots,1)\right)^{l_{2}}\bigg).

Hence, by using (
6.5
), we can compute this
t_{1}^{l_{2}}
term to be

\displaystyle\tau^{l_{2}(1-n)}

\displaystyle a_{l_{2}}\frac{1}{X_{1}}\bigg(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\tau^{2}X_{1}-X_{j}}{X_{1}-X_{j}}t_{1}\bigg)^{l_{2}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{l_{2}(1-n)}a_{l_{2}}\frac{1}{X_{1}}\bigg(\prod_{k=0}^{l_{2}-1}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{q^{k}\tau^{2}X_{1}-X_{j}}{q^{k}X_{1}-X_{j}}\bigg)t_{1}^{l_{2}}.

We can use
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariance (see Theorem
6.7
) to deduce the coefficient at
t_{i}^{l_{2}}
for any
i
.
Similarly, one can compute explicitly the coefficient at
t_{n}^{-l_{1}}
, and then use
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariance again to complete the proof of the proposition.
∎

6.1
Systems with two types of particles

In this subsection, we obtain a generalisation of the Macdonald–Ruijsenaars system with Morse term
[
14
, (2.1)]
(a particular limit of which,
[
14
, (3.13a)]
, was mentioned in Remark
6.10
above). That system was introduced by van Diejen in
[
13
]
and studied further by van Diejen and Emsiz in
[
14
]
. Our generalisation introduces into the system a second, different set of particles interacting with each other and also with the original set of particles. In the case of Macdonald–Ruijsenaars systems, such two-types-of-particles generalisations were considered in
[
7
,
27
]
.

One way to obtain such a generalisation is to make use of representation theory of the DAHA of type
GL_{n}
(see Remark
6.12
below). There are though advantages in taking another approach as follows.
The operator
[
14
, (2.1)]
can be obtained from the Koornwinder operator (i.e. the operator of Macdonald–Ruijsenaars type for the root system
BC_{n}
) by a limit in which the centre of mass is sent to infinity
[
13
]
. We now take the generalised Koornwinder operator
[
20
, (5.12)]
introduced by Silantyev and one of the authors, and apply to it an analogous centre-of-mass-to-infinity limit.

In the operator
[
20
, (5.12)]
, let us make the substitutions
x_{i}\to x_{i}+R
,
y_{i}\to y_{i}+R+\log(qs^{-1})
,
a\to ae^{R}
,
b\to be^{R}
,
c\to ce^{-R}
,
d\to de^{-R}
, and then take
R\to\infty
. In order to make connection with the notations used in
[
14
]
(recall that
q=e^{\hbar/2}
and
s=e^{\xi/2}
in
[
20
]
), let us in the resulting limit make the replacements
q\to q^{-1/2}
,
s=t^{1/2}
(so that now
q=e^{-\hbar}
and
t=e^{\xi}
),
a=t_{1}
,
b=t_{2}
,
c=t_{0}^{-1}
,
d=t_{3}^{-1}
,
x_{i}\to\hbar x_{i}
, and
y_{i}\to\hbar y_{i}
(so that the shift operators
\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}^{\varepsilon\hbar}
and
\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{\varepsilon\xi}
for
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
become
\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{-\varepsilon\log(t)/\log(q)}
, respectively). Then we get the following Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(1-t_{1}q^{x_{i}})(1-t_{2}q^{x_{i}})\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{t^{-1}-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 110.00017pt\times\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{q-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{qt_{0}t_{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(1-t_{0}q^{x_{i}})(1-t_{3}q^{x_{i}})\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{t-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 110.00017pt\times\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{t-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}{qt-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}^{-1}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1-q}{1-t^{-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}(1-t_{1}q^{y_{i}})(1-t_{2}q^{y_{i}})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{t^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 110.00017pt\times\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{q-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{-\log(t)/\log(q)}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{t_{1}t_{2}(1-q^{-1})}{qt_{0}t_{3}(1-t)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}(1-t_{0}tq^{y_{i}+1})(1-t_{3}tq^{y_{i}+1})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{q^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}{q^{-1}t^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 110.00017pt\times\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{q^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{\log(t)/\log(q)}-1).

(6.12)

For
N_{2}=0
and
N_{1}=n
, the operator (
6.1
) reduces to the van Diejen–Emsiz operator
[
14
, (2.1)]
up to a factor of
\sqrt{qt_{0}t_{3}/(t_{1}t_{2})}
. Also, if in (
6.1
) we put
n=N_{1}+N_{2}
,
y_{i}=x_{N_{1}+i}
, and
t=q^{-1}
(that is
\xi=\hbar
), then we get the operator
[
14
, (2.1)]
with
t=q^{-1}
.

By applying the same limiting procedure to the set of quantum integrals of the generalised Koornwinder operator found in
[
20
, Proposition 5.6]
, we get quantum integrals for the Hamiltonian (
6.1
).

We also obtain a generalisation to the case of two types of particles of the Hamiltonian
[
14
, (3.13a)]
. Indeed, if in (
6.1
) we put
t_{3}=1
, define
\widehat{t}_{i}
(
i=0,1,2
) by
t_{0}=q^{-1}\widehat{t}_{1}\widehat{t}_{2}
,
t_{1}=\widehat{t}_{0}\widehat{t}_{2}
, and
t_{2}=\widehat{t}_{0}\widehat{t}_{1}
following
[
14
, (3.12b)]
, and then take the limit
\widehat{t}_{2}\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle H_{\widehat{t}_{0},\widehat{t}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(1-\widehat{t}_{0}\widehat{t}_{1}q^{x_{i}})\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{t^{-1}-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{q-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}-1)

\displaystyle+\widehat{t}_{0}{}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(1-q^{x_{i}})\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{t-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{x_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{t-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}{qt-q^{x_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)(\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}^{-1}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1-q}{1-t^{-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}(1-\widehat{t}_{0}\widehat{t}_{1}q^{y_{i}})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{t^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{q-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 220.00034pt\times(\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{-\log(t)/\log(q)}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{\widehat{t}_{0}{}^{2}(1-q^{-1})}{1-t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}(1-tq^{y_{i}+1})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{q^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}{q^{-1}t^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-x_{j}}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{q^{-1}-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}{1-q^{y_{i}-y_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 220.00034pt\times(\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{\log(t)/\log(q)}-1).

(6.13)

For
N_{2}=0
and
N_{1}=n
, this reduces to the operator
[
14
, (3.13a)]
up to a factor of
\widehat{t}_{0}
.

Let us now consider the analogous limit of the operator (
6.1
) as the limit described in Remark
6.10
, just additionally making also the change
q^{y_{i}}\to\kappa^{-1}q^{y_{i}}
and introducing the variables
Y_{i}=q^{-y_{i}}
. Let us denote a
q
-multiplicative shift operator in the variables
X_{i}
by
t_{X_{i}}^{q}
and analogously for the variables
Y_{i}
(so that
\mathcal{T}_{x_{i}}^{\pm 1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{y_{i}}^{\pm\log(t)/\log(q)}
become respectively
t_{X_{i}}^{q^{\mp 1}}
and
t_{Y_{i}}^{\tau^{\mp 2}}
). Then, in this limit, we obtain the multiplicative operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\widehat{t}_{0},\widehat{t}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{a}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{\tau^{2}X_{i}-X_{j}}{X_{i}-X_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{\tau^{2}X_{i}-Y_{j}}{q\tau^{2}X_{i}-Y_{j}}\right)t_{X_{i}}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\widetilde{a}(1-q)}{1-\tau^{-2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{Y_{i}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{q^{-1}Y_{i}-X_{j}}{q^{-1}\tau^{-2}Y_{i}-X_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{q^{-1}Y_{i}-Y_{j}}{Y_{i}-Y_{j}}\right)t_{Y_{i}}^{\tau^{-2}},

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{b}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{X_{i}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{\tau^{-2}X_{i}-X_{j}}{X_{i}-X_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{qX_{i}-Y_{j}}{X_{i}-Y_{j}}\right)t_{X_{i}}^{q^{-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\widetilde{b}(1-q)}{1-\tau^{-2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{Y_{i}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\tau^{-2}Y_{i}-X_{j}}{Y_{i}-X_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{qY_{i}-Y_{j}}{Y_{i}-Y_{j}}\right)t_{Y_{i}}^{\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{X_{i}}+\frac{\widetilde{c}(1-q)}{1-\tau^{-2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{1}{Y_{i}},

(6.14)

where

\widetilde{a}=-\widehat{t}_{0}{}^{2}
,
\widetilde{b}=-\widehat{t}_{0}\widehat{t}_{1}
, and
\widetilde{c}=\widehat{t}_{0}(\widehat{t}_{0}+\widehat{t}_{1})
, and where we twice used the following polynomial identity (which generalises to two types of particles the identity from the top of
[
14
, p. 1621]
):

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}z_{i}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}}\frac{tz_{j}-z_{i}}{z_{j}-z_{i}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{sw_{j}-z_{i}}{w_{j}-z_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-s}{1-t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}w_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{tz_{j}-w_{i}}{z_{j}-w_{i}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N_{2}}\frac{sw_{j}-w_{i}}{w_{j}-w_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}z_{i}+\frac{1-s}{1-t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}w_{i},

once with
z_{i}=X_{i}^{-1}
,
w_{i}=Y_{i}^{-1}
,
t=\tau^{-2}
,
s=q
, and the second time with
z_{i}=X_{i}^{-1}
,
w_{i}=q\tau^{2}Y_{i}^{-1}
,
t=\tau^{2}
,
s=q^{-1}
.

The operator (
6.1
) generalises the Hamiltonian
M_{a,b,c}
given by (
6.7
), for special values of
a,b,c
,
to a Hamiltonian of a system containing two types of particles.
Indeed, if we put
N_{2}=0
,
N_{1}=n
in (
6.1
), we recover
M_{a,b,c}
with
a=-\tau^{n-1}\widehat{t}_{0}{}^{2}
,

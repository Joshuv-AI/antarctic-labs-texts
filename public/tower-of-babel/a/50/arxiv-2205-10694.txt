Abstract
. In this short note, the second dual of generalized group algebra ( ℓ 1 ​ ( G , 𝒜 ) , ∗ ) (\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A}),\ast) equipped with both Arens product is investigated, where G G is any discrete group and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a Banach algebra containing a complemented algebraic copy of ( ℓ 1 ​ ( ℕ ) , ∙ ) (\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\bullet) . We give an explicit family of annihilators(w.r.t both the Arens product) in the algebra ℓ 1 ​ ( G , 𝒜 ) ∗ ⁣ ∗ \ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})^{**} , arising from non-principal ultrafilters on ℕ \mathbb{N} and which are not in the toplogical center. As a consequence, we also deduce the fact that ℓ 1 ​ ( G , 𝒜 ) \ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A}) is not Strongly Arens irregular.

1.
Introduction

For any Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, Richard Arens (in
[
1
]
) defined
two products
\Box
and
\Diamond
on its bidual space
\mathcal{A}^{**}
such that each product
makes
\mathcal{A}^{**}
into a Banach algebra and the canonical isometric
inclusion
J:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{**}
becomes a homomorphism with respect to both
the products. A Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
Arens
regular
if the two products
\Box
and
\diamond
agree on

\mathcal{A}^{**}
, i.e. if
f\Box g=f\diamond g
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{**}
. Even
when a Banach algebra is not Arens regular, the size of its
topological center carry a great deal of information. Dales and Lau
introduced the notion of strong Arens irregularity to distinguish
between non-Arens regular Banach algebras.
Briefly speaking, a Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
left

(resp.,
right
)
strongly Arens irregular
if its so called

left topological center

Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
(resp.,
right topological center

Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
) equals
\mathcal{A}
(see
2.1
for definitions). And

\mathcal{A}
is said to be
strongly Arens irregular
(in short, S.A.I.)
if it is both left and right strongly Arens irregular. Interestingly,
on the other extreme,
\mathcal{A}
is Arens regular if and
only if
Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})=\mathcal{A}^{**}=Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
. Reflexive spaces turn out to be at the junction of Arens
regularity and strong Arens irregularity because

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{**}=Z^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})=Z^{(t)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
.

After the pioneering paper
[
1
]
by Richard Arens, people
started investigating the Arens regularity of various Banach algebras
that appear in Abstract Harmonic Analysis. The group algebra
L^{1}(G)

has been a center of attention for quite some time. Arens himself
proved that the semi-group algebra
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
(with respect
to convolution) is not Arens regular. N.J Young in
[
8
]
showed that the group algebra
L^{1}(G)
is never Arens regular for any infinite locally compact group
G
. Graham, in
[
2
]
,
gave an easy proof for the irregularity of
L^{1}(G)
for the case when
G
is infinite discrete or amenable locally compact group.
Losert and Lau in
[
4
]

proved that
L^{1}(G)
is strongly Arens irregular for all infinite
locally compact groups.

In a more general setup, one can consider the space
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
of all
\mathcal{A}
valued Bochner integrable functions, where
G
is any locally compact group and
\mathcal{A}
is a Banach algebra. The space
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
becomes a Banach algebra with respect to convolution multiplication and
||\cdot||_{1}
norm (see
2.2.1
for details). It is then quite natural to analyze the strong Arens irregularity of
generalized group algebras
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
for infinite locally compact
groups
G
and Banach algebras
\mathcal{A}
. This short article is just a
report on some progress made in this direction. A lot still remains
to be explored. Since
L^{1}(G)
is not Arens regular, and because

L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})\cong L^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}
, we know from
[
6
]
that

L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is also not Arens regular. Question remains under what
conditions on
\mathcal{A}
, the Banach algebra
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is strongly
Arens irregular. Needless to mention, if
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
point-wise multiplication and
G
is infinite, then

L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is strongly Arens irregular (due to

[
4
]
). Further, since there is an isometric algebra
isomorphism from
L^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}L^{1}(H)
onto
L^{1}(G\times H)

(w.r.t. convolution on both the legs) - see
[
3
]
, it follows
again from
[
4
]
that
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is strongly Arens
irregular for
\mathcal{A}=L^{1}(H)
, where
H
is an infinite group. However,

L^{1}(G,\ell^{1}):=\ell^{1}(L^{1}(G))
is not strongly Arens irregular when
\ell^{1}
is equipped with pointwise product. We shall prove this in the next section.
This is interestingly in contrast to the above mentioned fact that
the (infinite) group algebra
L^{1}(G)
is always S.A.I. - see

[
4
]
.
We study the bidual of
L^{1}(G,\ell^{1})
for discrete groups
G
and
\ell^{1}
equipped with pointwise product, and show that it is rich in annihilators. We give a family of functionals, generated by non-principal ultrafilters, in the bidual of
L^{1}(G,\ell^{1})
, which are annihilators of the bidual with respect to both the Arens product and are not an element of
L^{1}(G,\ell^{1})
. As a consequence, we also deduce the failure of SAI in generalized group algebras explicitly (which is well known as mentioned in previous paragraph). Getting hold of functionals in bidual of a Banach algebra which are not in the algebra itself is usually a difficult task. The ultrafilter technique demonstrated in third section seems to be a powerful tool to study biduals of some Banach algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Arens regularity and strong Arens irregularity

We quickly recall the definitions of the two products
\Box
and

\Diamond
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach algebra. For
a\in\mathcal{A}
,

\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
,
f\in\mathcal{A}^{**}
, consider the functionals

\omega_{a},{}_{a}\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
,
\omega_{f},{}_{f}\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{**}

given by

w_{a}=(L_{a})^{*}(\omega),{}_{a}\omega=(R_{a})^{*}(\omega);\omega_{f}(a)=f({}_{a}\omega)\text{ and }{}_{f}\omega(a)=f(\omega_{a}).

Then, for
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{**}
the
operations
\Box
and
\Diamond
are given by

(f\Box g)(\omega)=f({}_{g}\omega)\text{ and }(f\Diamond g)(\omega)=g(\omega_{f})

for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. And,
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
Arens regular
if the
products
\Box
and
\Diamond
agree.

Further, the left and the right topological centers of
\mathcal{A}
are defined,
respectively, as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})&=&\{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{**}:\varphi\Box\psi=\varphi\Diamond\psi\text{ for all }\psi\in\mathcal{A}^{**}\};\text{ and }\\
Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})&=&\{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{**}:\psi\Box\varphi=\psi\Diamond\varphi\text{ for all }\psi\in\mathcal{A}^{**}\}.\end{array}

Identifying
\mathcal{A}
with
J(\mathcal{A})
, where
J:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{**}
is the
canonical isometry, it is known that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})\cap Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
. It is easily seen that
\mathcal{A}
is Arens
regular if and only if
Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})=\mathcal{A}^{**}=Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
. A Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is said
to be
left
(resp.,
right
)
strongly Arens irregular

if
Z_{t}^{(l)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})=\mathcal{A}
(resp.,
Z_{t}^{(r)}(\mathcal{A}^{**})=\mathcal{A}
). And,
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
strongly Arens irregular
if it
is both left and right strongly Arens irregular.

2.2.
Projective tensor product

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces. Then, there
are various ways of imposing a normed space structure on their algebraic tensor product
X\otimes Y
. For instance, for each
u\in X\otimes Y
, its
projective norm is given by

\|u\|_{\gamma}=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|\|y_{i}\|~:~u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\right\}.

This norm turns out to be a
cross norm
, i.e.,
\|x\otimes y\|_{\gamma}=\|x\|\|y\|
for all
(x,y)\in X\times Y
; and, the completion of the normed space
X\otimes Y
with
respect to this norm is usually denoted by

X\otimes^{\gamma}Y
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be Banach algebras. Then, there is a natural
multiplication structure on their algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
satisfying
(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}\otimes b_{1}b_{2}
for all
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
and
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
,

i=1,2
. And, there are various ways of imposing a normed algebra
structure on
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
. For instance, completing
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
with respect to the projective norm gives a Banach algebra
structure on
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{B}
, which occupies a prominent space in the
theory of tensor products of Banach algebras.

2.2.1.
Generalized group algebras

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach algebra and
\mu
denote the Haar measure on
G
. Then,
recall that,
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
denotes the space of all Bochner integrable
functions from
G
to
\mathcal{A}
with the norm
||f||_{1}:=\int_{G}||f||d\mu

and forms a Banach algebra with respect to the convolution product
defined as
f\star g(x)=\int_{G}f(s)g(s^{-1}x)ds
- see
[
3
]

for further details on generalized group algebras. When
G
is a
discrete group, then
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is nothing but the collection of all
absolutely summable
\mathcal{A}
-valued functions and
||f||_{1}=\sum_{g\in G}||f(g)||
while the convolution reduces to
f\ast g(x)=\sum_{s\in G}f(s)g(s^{-1}x)
(notice that this is a sum of the elements of
\mathcal{A}
). We will use the notion
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
for the
\mathcal{A}
-valued group algebra when
G
is discrete.

The following natural identifications are well known and will be used ahead - see, for
instance,
[
3
,
5
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(1)

For any two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, there is a canonical
surjective isometry
\theta:B(X,Y^{*})\to(X\otimes^{\gamma}Y)^{*}

satisfying

\theta(f)(x\otimes^{\gamma}y)=f(x)(y)\text{ for all }f\in B(X,Y^{*}),x\in X,y\in Y.

(
B(X,Y^{*})
denotes the space of all bounded linear maps from
X
to
Y^{*}
).

(2)

For any locally compact group
G
and Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, there
exists an isometric algebra isomorphism
T:L^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}\to L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})

satisfying

T(f\otimes a)(g)=f(g)a\text{ for all }f\in f\in L^{1}(G),a\in\mathcal{A},g\in G.

The following elementary observations which are easy to prove will be used
ahead.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be an infinite discrete group and
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach
algebra. Then,

(1)

if
x=\sum_{n\in F}\delta_{g_{n}}\otimes a_{n}\in L^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}

for some
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, a collection
\{g_{n}:n\in F\}\subseteq G
with no repetitions and a collection
\{a_{n}:n\in F\}\subset\mathcal{A}
, then
\|x\|_{\gamma}=\sum_{n\in F}\|a_{n}\|
; and,

(2)

for each
x\in L^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}
, there exists
an
F\subset\mathbb{N}
, a collection
\{h_{n}:n\in F\}\subset G
with no repetitions and
an absolutely summable collection
\{b_{n}:n\in F\}
in
\mathcal{A}\setminus\{0\}
such that
x=\sum_{n\in F}\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes b_{n}
.

3.
Annihilators in Bidual of
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})

Throughout this section,
G
will denote an infinite discrete
group. We will give a proof of the fact that the convolution algebra
\ell^{1}(G,\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}))
is not S.A.I (where
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
is equipped with pointwise multiplication).

Theorem 3.1
.

The Banach algebra
(\ell^{1}(G,\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})),\ast)
is not strongly Arens irregular.

Proof.

Since,
\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\ast\right):=\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),\ast\right)\otimes^{\gamma}\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\bullet\right)=\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),\ast)),\bullet\right)
and
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
is Arens irregular, we know by
[
6
]
(Cor. 3.5 ) that
\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})),\ast\right)
is not Arens regular. Let
X
be the Banach algebra
X=(\ell^{1}(G),\ast)
. Then by above identifications, our generalized group algebra
\left(\ell^{1}(G,\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})),\ast\right)
is isometrically isomorphic to the Banach algebra
(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X),\bullet)
.

Consider the trivial embedding
i:c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})=\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{*}
and a natural map

K:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X^{**})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}:=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})^{*}

where
K(f)(g)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left<f(n),g(n)\right>
for each
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X^{**})
and
g\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})
. Clearly
K
is an isometry(not necessarily surjective). Let
P=K\circ i^{*}:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}
. One can easily verify that the map
P
is a projection i.e
P^{2}=P
and the range of
P
is
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X^{**})
. Thus, we have the direct sum decomposition of Banach spaces
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}=\text{Range}(P)\oplus\text{Range}(I-P)
. But
\text{Range}(I-P)=i\left(c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})\right)^{\perp}
. Hence, we have the following Banach space decomposition

(3.1)

\displaystyle\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}=\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X^{**})\oplus i(c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*}))^{\perp}

Now let
\theta:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{*}
be the dual identification given by
\theta(f)(g)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left<f(n),g(n)\right>
. For any
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})
and
h,g\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\theta(f)\right)_{h}(g)

\displaystyle={{}_{h}}\left(\theta(f)\right)(g)

\displaystyle=\theta(f)(h\bullet g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left<f(n),h(n)\ast g(n)\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left<f(n)\ast\widecheck{h(n)},g(n)\right>

\displaystyle=\theta(f\bullet\hat{h})(g)

where
\widecheck{h(n)}(m)=h(n)(-m)
and
\hat{h}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)
is defined as
\hat{h}(n)=\widecheck{h(n)}
. It can be easily seen that
f\bullet\hat{h}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})
. Thus
\omega_{h}={{}_{h}}w\in\theta(c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*}))
for each
h\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)
and
w\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{*}
. Now if
\nu\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}
and
\eta\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{***})^{\perp}\subset\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{***})^{*}
, then for the restriction
\eta_{o}
of
\eta
to
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})
we have

\displaystyle\nu\square\eta_{o}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\nu(\omega_{\eta_{o}})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\left(\text{because}~\omega_{\eta_{o}}(h)=\eta_{o}(\omega_{h})=0~\text{as}~\omega_{h}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N},X^{*})\right)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\nu\diamond\eta_{o}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\eta_{o}(\omega_{\nu_{1}})+\eta_{o}(\omega_{{\nu}_{2}})

\displaystyle\left(\nu=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}~\text{using the decomposition }~\ref{decompose}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta_{o}(\omega_{v_{1}})+0

\displaystyle\left(\because\omega_{\nu_{2}}=0\right)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\left(\because\omega_{\nu_{1}}\in c_{o}(\mathbb{N},X^{***})\right)

Thus, we see that
\eta_{o}
is right annihilator with respect to both the Arens product on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)^{**}
. Further,
\eta_{o}
does not belong to
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X)
. Hence, the right topological center of generalized group algebra
\left(\ell^{1}(G,\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})),\ast\right)^{**}:=\left(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},X),\bullet\right)^{**}
is non-trivial.
∎

For any general Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, it is not always possible to take the above given approach for the topological center of
(\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A}),\ast)
. The direct sum decomposition as in
3.1
cannot be given for
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})^{**}
for any arbitrary Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
. We propose a direct access to annihilators in the bidual of
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})^{**}
with respect to both the Arens product, using non-principle ultrafilters in the case when
\mathcal{A}
contains a complemented algebraic copy of
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
. To do so, we first observe the following crucial result regarding Banach algebras which contains an isomorphic copy of
(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\bullet)
. We say that a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}
of a Banach space
\mathcal{A}
is complemented in
\mathcal{A}
if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}\oplus\mathcal{C}
for some closed subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{A}
, where
||(b,c)||=||b||+||c||
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach algebra such that there exists an algebra isomorphism
\rho:(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\bullet)\to\mathcal{A}
onto the its image such that
\rho(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}))
is complemented in
\mathcal{A}
and let
X=\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
. Then, associated to each non-principal ultrafilter

\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a functional
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}
in
X^{**}
such that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\notin J(X)
. This functional is precisely defined as

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(\tau)=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\tau(\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n}))\text{ for all }\tau\in X^{*}.

where,
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable set of elements in
G
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Fix a countable set
S=\{h_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
consisting of distinct elements of
G
.

Notice that, for each
\tau\in X^{*}
, via the identifications illustrated in
Theorem

2.1
, we have

|\tau(\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n}))|\leq\|\tau\|\|\rho\|\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus, since limit along an ultrafilter in a compact set always
exists, we have a map
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}:X^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

(3.2)

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(\tau)=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\tau(\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n}))\text{ for all }\tau\in X^{*}.

Clearly,
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}
is a bounded linear functional on

X^{*}
.
We first assert that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\neq 0
. Consider
\eta:\ell^{1}(G)\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
defined
as

\eta(\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}\delta_{g})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{h_{i}}E_{i}^{*}.

where,
E_{i}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
are some fixed Hahn-Banach extensions of point evaluations
E_{i}^{*}(\rho(\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}))=x_{i}
on the subpsace
\rho(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}))
of
\mathcal{A}
.
One can
easily verify
\eta\in B(\ell^{1}(G),\mathcal{A}^{*})
and that
||\eta||\leq 1
. Clearly,
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(\eta)=1
. Hence,
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\neq 0
.

Next, consider the subset
\Delta:=\operatorname{span}\{\mathcal{T}_{g,E^{*}}~|~g\in G,E^{*}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{1}\}
of
X^{*}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{g,E^{*}}:\ell^{1}(G)\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
is
defined as

\mathcal{T}_{g,E^{*}}(\sum_{h\in G}c_{h}\delta_{h})=c_{g}E^{*}.

(One can easily see that
\mathcal{T}_{g,E^{*}}\in B(\ell^{1}(G),\mathcal{A}^{*})
and are uniformly bounded and, hence, in
view of
Theorem

2.1
,
\Delta
is a well defined non-trivial family
in
X^{*}
.)

We now claim that
J(x)
does not vanish on
\overline{\Delta}
for any

0\neq x\in X
, while
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}
vanishes (on
\Delta
and
hence) on
\overline{\Delta}
. Since
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\neq 0
, this will then imply that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\notin J(X)
.

The latter claim is evident from the fact that

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{T}_{g,i})=0
for each
g\in G
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

To prove the first part of the claim, suppose
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
. Then, as observed in
Lemma

2.2
,
there exist countable sets
F\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\{u_{j}:j\in F\}\subset G
and
\{b_{j}:j\in F\}\subset\mathcal{A}\setminus\{0\}
such that

x=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{u_{j}}\otimes b_{j}
.
For some fixed
i
, choose
E^{*}
such that
E^{*}(b_{i})\neq 0
.
Now, notice that

\displaystyle J(x)(\mathcal{T}_{u_{i},E^{*}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{u_{i},E^{*}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{u_{i},E^{*}}(\delta_{u_{j}})(b_{j})

\displaystyle=E^{*}(b_{i})\neq 0

Thus,
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\in X^{**}\setminus J(X)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be an infinite discrete group,
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach
such that
\rho:(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}),\bullet)\to\mathcal{A}
is an isomorphism onto its image such that the image is complemented in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{U}
be a
non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}
is left annihilator of
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})^{**}
for both the Arens products.

Proof.

Let
X:=\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
. Cosider
S:=\{h_{n}\}\subseteq G
as in

3.2
. Then, for any
\psi\in X^{**}
and
\mathcal{T}\in X^{*}
, we
have

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\square\psi)(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\ {{}_{\psi}}\mathcal{T}(\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}).

We claim that
\lim_{n}\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})})=0
.

To prove this we assume the contrary. Then, there exists an

\epsilon>0
and an increasing sequence
\{n_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

|\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})})|\geq\epsilon

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the functionals

h_{N}:=\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{n_{t}}\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where

c_{n_{t}}:=\frac{\overline{\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})})}}{|\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})})|}
. Let
x:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}\otimes a_{s_{i}}\in\ell^{1}(G)\otimes\mathcal{A}
be an arbitrary element, where
\{g_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}\subset G
and
\{s_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}\subset\mathbb{N}
(with possible repetitions) are two infinite sets with possible repitions. Further, let
a_{s_{i}}=\rho(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x^{(s_{i})}_{j}e_{j})+v_{s_{i}}
(since image of
\rho
is complemented in
\mathcal{A}
).

\displaystyle|h_{N}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{T}(\sum_{t=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}c_{n_{t}}\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\star\delta_{g_{i}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})\cdot a_{s_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{T}(\sum_{t=1}^{N}\lambda_{n_{t}}c_{n_{t}}\delta_{h_{n_{t}}g_{n_{t}}}\otimes x_{n_{t}}^{(s_{i})})\right|

(
\because

\rho(e_{n_{t}})\cdot a_{s_{i}}=x_{n_{t}}^{(s_{i})}
)

\displaystyle\leq||\mathcal{T}||\sum_{t=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n_{t}}||c_{n_{t}}||x_{n_{t}}^{(s_{t})}|

\displaystyle\leq C||\mathcal{T}||\cdot||x||

Since such
x
’s form a dense subspace of
\ell^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}
, it follows
that
||h_{N}||\leq C||\mathcal{T}||
for all
N
. Now notice that

\psi(h_{N})=\sum_{t=1}^{N}\left|\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n_{t}}}\otimes\rho(e_{n_{t}})})\right|\geq N\epsilon

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. But this is
absurd because
\psi
is a bounded linear functional and
\{h_{N}\}
is a
bounded sequence. Thus, our assumption is wrong, and we must have

\lim_{n}\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes a_{n}})=0.

Thus,
(\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\square\psi)(\mathcal{T})=0
(because the limit
along an ultrafilter in a compact Hausdorff space is unique and
every non-principal ultrafilter contains the cofinite
filter). Since
\psi
and
\mathcal{T}
were arbitrary, we conclude
that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\square\psi=0
for all
\psi\in X^{**}
.

Next, we turn to the second Arens product. We have

(\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\Diamond\psi)(\mathcal{T})=\psi(\mathcal{T}_{\varphi_{{}_{\mathcal{U}}}})
for all
\psi\in X^{**}
,
\mathcal{T}\in X^{*}
. Notice that for any
u\in\ell^{1}(G)\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\varphi_{{}_{\mathcal{U}}}}(u)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}({{}_{u}}{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}{{}_{u}\mathcal{T}}(\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}(u).

Thus,

\mathcal{T}_{\varphi_{{}_{\mathcal{U}}}}=w^{*}-\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}
and hence

\varphi_{{}_{\mathcal{U}}}\Diamond\psi(\mathcal{T})=\psi\left(w^{*}-\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}\right)
for all
\psi\in X^{**}
,
\mathcal{T}\in X^{*}
. Further,
for an arbitrary
u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}f_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\left|\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{h_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\left|\mathcal{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\delta_{h_{n}}\star f_{i}\otimes\rho(e_{n})\cdot b_{i}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle(\because\lim_{n}\rho(e_{n})\cdot b_{i}=0~\forall i)

Thus,

w^{*}-\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{g_{n}}\otimes\rho(e_{n})}=0
. Hence,
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\Diamond\psi(\mathcal{T})=0
for all

\psi\in X^{**}
and all
\mathcal{T}\in X^{*}
. Thus, we have shown that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\square\psi=0=\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}\Diamond\psi
for all
\psi\in X^{**}
.
∎

As a consequence, we can explicitly see the failure of Strong Arens irregularity in
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
when
\mathcal{A}
contains a complemented algebraic copy of
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
(for example Banach algebra
S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
of trace class operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
).

Corollary 3.4
.

X:=\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is not strongly Arens irregular when
\mathcal{A}
contains a complemented algebraic copy of
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
.

Remark 3.5
.

It has been noticed so far that when
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with pointwise multiplication or
L^{1}(H)
with convolution, then
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is strongly Arens irregular for any infinite locally compact group
G
. On the other hand if
\mathcal{A}
contains an algebraic copy of
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
w.r.t pointwise multiplication, then
\ell^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is not strongly Arens irregular for infinite discrete group
G
. All these examples points toward the following conjecture which is worth exploring.

Conjecture 3.6
.

If
G
is any infinite locally compact group and
\mathcal{A}
is a Banach algebra then
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is S.A.I. if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is S.A.I..

Recently, it has been proved in
[
9
]
that if
G
is a compact abelian group and
\mathcal{A}
is a refelexive Banach algebra then
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{A})
is strongly Arens irregular.

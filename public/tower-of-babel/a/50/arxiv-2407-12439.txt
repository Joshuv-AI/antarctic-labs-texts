Abstract
. In this paper we prove a fractional version of a Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type interpolation inequality on hypersurfaces M ⊂ ℝ n + 1 M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} which are boundaries of convex sets. The inequality carries a universal constant independent of M M and involves the fractional mean curvature of M . M. In particular, it interpolates between the fractional Micheal-Simon Sobolev inequality recently obtained by Cabré, Cozzi, and the first author, and a new fractional Hardy inequality on M M . Our method, when restricted to the plane case M = ℝ n M=\mathbb{R}^{n} , gives a new simple proof of the fractional Hardy inequality. To obtain the fractional Hardy inequality on a hypersurface, we establish an inequality which bounds a weighted perimeter of M M by the standard perimeter of M M (modulo a universal constant), and which is valid for all convex hypersurfaces M M .

1.
Introduction

The classical Michael-Simon and Allard inequality, proven for the first time independently by
[
1
]
and
[
18
]
, extends the classical Sobolev inequality to submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with an universal constant independent of the manifold. In the context of
C^{2}
hypersurfaces
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
it states that there
exists a constant
C
depending only on
n
and
p
, such that

(1.1)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(M)}\leqslant C\,\Big(\|\nabla_{\!M}u\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|Hu\|_{L^{p}(M)}\Big)\quad\text{for all }u\in C_{c}^{1}(M),

where
1\leqslant p<n
,
p^{\ast}:={np}/({n-p}),

\nabla_{\!M}
is the tangential gradient on
M
, and
H
is the mean curvature of
M.

A few years ago a new proof has been found by Brendle
[
2
]
, who established the best constant in (
1.1
) in the case
p=1
. This solved the long standing open problem on the isoperimetric problem on minimal surfaces (i.e.
H=0
).
We recommend Cabré and Miraglio
[
6
]
for a proof of (
1.1
) which combines the original ideas of
[
1
]
and
[
18
]
in an optimal way to give the shortest and easiest proof currently available in the literature.

We emphasize that the constant
C
does not depend on
M
and therefore all the information about the geometry of
M
is encoded in its mean curvature term
H
that appears on the right-hand side of (
1.1
). Henceforth, by universal constant we mean such a constant, which depends only on parameters, but not on the manifold. The parameters in this paper will be some powers
p
in
L^{p}
norms and powers of the weight
|x|
, i.e., the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

The Hardy inequality also has extensions to submanifolds with a universal constant. A good survey of these extensions, with several generalizations and new proofs can be found in Cabré and Miraglio
[
6
]
. Among these, of particular interest to the present paper is
[
6
, Corollary 1.5 and 4.1]
which shows the following inequality for hypersurfaces
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
n\geqslant 3
:

(1.2)

\||x|^{\gamma}u\|_{L^{\tau}(M)}\leqslant C\Big(\|\nabla_{\!M}u\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|Hu\|_{L^{p}(M)}\Big)\quad\text{for all }u\in C_{c}^{1}(M),

where
1\leqslant p<n,

\gamma\in[-1,0]
,
\tau\in[p,p^{*}]
and

\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}\quad\text{ and }\quad p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}.

Note that (
1.2
) gives an interpolation between the Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) (taking
\tau=p^{*}
and
\gamma=0
) and Hardy inequality (taking
\tau=p
and
\gamma=-1
) on manifolds.

In the present paper we deal with the nonlocal analogues of the interpolation Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.2
). Actually, we will obtain something more general, namely a Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type interpolation inequality with universal constant for fractional Sobolev spaces on hypersurfaces of Euclidean space which are the boundary of a convex set. For this purpose we shall define the following seminorm on a hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

(1.3)

[u]_{W^{s,p}(M)}:=\left(\int_{M}\int_{M}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Here
|x-y|
is the Euclidean distance in the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
Let us denote by
W^{s,p}(M)
the space of those functions
u\in L^{p}(M)
such that
[u]_{W^{s,p}(M)}<\infty.

This seminorm (
1.3
) was first introduced in Cabré and Cozzi
[
3
]
where it was used to obtain a gradient estimate for nonlocal minimal graphs. It was precisely in
[
3
, Proposition 5.2]
that for a first time a universal fractional Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality was established, albeit for surfaces satisfying a certain density estimate (which includes local minimal surfaces or nonlocal minimizing minimal surfaces). They proved on such hypersurfaces the inequality

(1.4)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(M)}\leqslant C\,[u]_{W^{s,p}(M)}\quad\text{for all }u\in W^{s,p}(M),

where
p_{s}^{*}=np/(n-sp)
is the fractional Sobolev exponent and
C
is a universal constant. The inequality (
1.4
) holds true on nonlocal minimal surfaces as well (surfaces with vanishing fractional mean curvature), thanks to the recent result of Thompson
[
23
]
, who established the required density estimate for such surfaces.

We point out some other reasons why (
1.3
) is a natural definition of the seminorm on a hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The seminorm
[\cdot]_{W^{(1+s)/2,2}(\partial E)}
appears in the second variation formula for the nonlocal
s
-perimeter of a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, see
[
11
, Appendix B]
or
[
13
, Section 6]
, in exact analogy of how the norm
\|\nabla_{\partial E}\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}
appears in the second variation formula of the classical perimeter (see also
[
13
, Section 6]
). Finally, in
[
4
]
, this definition of the seminorm was precisely the right one to obtain an estimate for the extinction time of the fractional mean curvature flow for convex hypersurfaces. For a further justification of this definition we refer to
[
5
, Appendix A]
, where the corresponding half-Laplacian has been defined on the unit circle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
—meaning that
[u]_{W^{1/2,2}(M)}^{2}=\int_{M}u(-\Delta)^{1/2}u
.

Our definition (
1.3
) of the seminorm on a manifold does not agree with the “canonical” definition in Caselli, Florit, and Serra
[
8
]
where a different definition is given for
p=2
and for general closed Riemannian manifold. This is due to the fact that
|x-y|
in our definition of
[\cdot]_{W^{s,p}}
is the Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which for general manifolds is not comparable with the geodesic distance on the manifold. In particular, our corresponding fractional Laplacian on the hypersurface, which is determined by partial integration using our definition (
1.3
), does not satisfy
(-\Delta)^{s}\circ(-\Delta)^{t}=(-\Delta)^{s+t},
in contrast to the canonical definition given in
[
8
]
. This property cannot be satisfied even if we modify our definition by some multiplicative constant.

1

1

This can be easily seen by taking the hypersurface as the boundary of
(-\infty,\infty)\times(0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, whose boundary consists of two parallel lines
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. Taking
u
to be equal to two different constants on each
M_{i}
gives that the Laplace-Beltrami operator is
-\Delta_{M}u=0
, but
(-\Delta_{M})^{1/2}\circ(-\Delta_{M})^{1/2}u\neq 0
provided we take the definition of the fractional Laplacian induced by (
1.3
). The same example works if
M
is the union of two concentric circles.

However, observe that all the motivations mentioned in the previous paragraph come from problems posed in the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, using that the manifold is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and has codimension
1
. This makes our setting different from the application which motivates
[
8
]
: their main application is Caselli, Florit, and Serra
[
9
]
which deals with the existence of nonlocal minimal surfaces on closed Riemannian manifolds.

Before stating our main theorem, we recall the definition of the nonlocal (or fractional)
\alpha
-mean curvature
H_{\alpha}(x)
of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
at the point
x\in\partial\Omega
. It is given by

(1.5)

\begin{split}H_{\alpha}(x)=H_{\alpha}[\Omega](x):=&\frac{\alpha}{2}\,\mbox{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\frac{\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\Omega}(y)-\chi_{\Omega}(y)}{|y-x|^{n+1+\alpha}}\,dy\\
=&\mbox{P.V.}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{(y-x)\cdot\nu(y)}{|y-x|^{n+1+\alpha}}\,dy,\end{split}

where
\nu
denotes the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

In order to state our theorem, note first that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is an open convex set, then
\partial\Omega
is a Lipschitz hypersurface and thus, by Rademacher’s theorem, differentiable at almost every point
x\in\partial\Omega.
On the other hand, by either one of the expressions in (
1.5
), we see that
H_{\alpha}(x)
is a well-defined quantity in
[0,+\infty],
since
\Omega
is convex. Furthermore, by Aleksandrov’s theorem,
\partial\Omega
is (pointwise) twice differentiable at almost every
x\in\partial\Omega.
At these points, the nonlocal mean curvature
H_{\alpha}(x)
is finite.

In Cabré, Cozzi, and Csató
[
4
]
a fractional analogue of the classical Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) was proven for convex hypersurfaces.
Here we prove an extension of that result to a Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type interpolation inequality. Our main theorem is the following, which gives exactly
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
for the choice
a=1
and
\gamma=0.
Both results are valid on manifolds which are boundaries of convex sets and it is an open problem whether it is also true on more general manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer,
a\in(0,1]
,
\alpha,s,\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\leqslant 0
,
p,q\geqslant 1
, and
\tau>0
be such that
sp<n
,

(1.6)

\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\frac{a}{p_{s}^{*}}+\frac{1-a}{q},\hskip 8.53581pt\textrm{where},\ p_{s}^{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp},

and

(1.7)

\gamma\geqslant-as\quad\text{ if }\quad a=1\text{ or }q=p_{s}^{*}.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open convex set. Then, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
a
,
n
,
\alpha
,
s
,
\gamma
,
p
,
q
, and
\tau
such that

(1.8)

\||x|^{\gamma}u\|_{L^{\tau}(\partial\Omega)}\leqslant C\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy+\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\alpha}(x)^{\frac{sp}{\alpha}}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{a}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}^{1-a},

for every
u\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega).

Note that no relation between the parameters
s
and
\alpha
is assumed within the theorem. Note that we also make no assumption on where the domain
\Omega
is located with respect to the origin, which could be on its boundary
\partial\Omega
; the term
|x|^{\gamma}
on the left hand side of the inequality is just the Euclidean distance of
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to the power
\gamma.

We recall that (
1.6
) is a necessary condition and follows from dimensional balance, since the theorem must hold for
\lambda\Omega
too, for all
\lambda>0.

The nonlocal analogue of the classical result of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg
[
7
]
, in Euclidean space, has been established in full generality in Nguyen and Squassina
[
19
]
. Both of these references establish interpolation inequalities where the product on the right hand side of the inequality also contains weighted norms, weighted by powers of
|x|
. The conditions that
[
19
]
, respectively its analogues in the local case
[
7
]
, impose on the parameters coincide with our conditions, when taking the powers of these weights equal to zero.
In view of (
1.6
) and
a\in(0,1]
the condition (
1.7
) is equivalent to

\gamma\geqslant-as\quad\text{ if }\quad\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*}},

which is a more adequate formulation if one wishes to compare with
[
19
]
or
[
7
]
.

For an elementary proof of the fractional Hardy inequality in
1
-dimension and references on its earliest proofs we refer to
[
16
, Section 3]
. The fractional Hardy inequality with optimal constant in terms of the limiting behaviour in parameter
s
was obtained in
[
17
]
, i.e., they prove the fractional Hardy inequality such that it contains the local Hardy inequality if taking the limit
s\to 1
. A major improvement of this last result was given by Frank and Seiringer
[
14
]
who determined exactly the sharp constant in the fractional Hardy inequality.

Our idea of the proof is based on the observation that the proof of the fractional Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
presented in
[
12
]
(and originally based on the technique developed in Savin and Valdinoci
[
21
,
22
]
) actually also gives the fractional Hardy inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if combined with the following elementary rearrangement inequality. If
E^{*}
denotes the Schwarz rearrangement of
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e.,
E^{*}
is a ball centered at the origin, and with the same measure as
E
), then

(1.9)

\int_{E}|x|^{-\beta}dx\leqslant\int_{E^{*}}|x|^{-\beta}dx=\frac{C(n)}{n-\beta}|E|^{\frac{n-\beta}{n}},

where
\beta\in[0,n)
and
C(n)
is a dimensional constant depending only on
n.
Our proof of Theorem
1.1
restricted to the plane case (
\partial\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
) gives a new simple proof of the fractional Hardy inequality in the plane, and is based on precisely this combination of
[
12
, Section 6]
with (
1.9
).

It is clear that (
1.9
) cannot hold for some universal constant for all
E\subset M
if
M
can be any hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. To see this it is sufficient to take a sequence of manifolds
M
which “oscillate” more and more near the origin (in the sense that
M
tends to have infinitely many leaves which all pass close to the origin, like tending to a single plane with infinite multiplicity, or to a set which is dense in a neighborhood of the origin). Nevertheless, if
M=\partial\Omega
and
\Omega
is convex, then this cannot happen and we can show the following result. In the next theorem the word
measurable
refers to the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}
on a hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open convex set and
\beta\in[0,n)
. Then, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
n
and
\beta
such that for any measurable subset
E
of
\partial\Omega
,

(1.10)

\int_{E}|x|^{-\beta}dx\leqslant C|E|^{\frac{n-\beta}{n}}.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
is quite elementary and certainly does not give the best constant
C
in (
1.10
). We expect that the best constant is achieved by a limiting set
\Omega
which is an infinitely thin circular cylinder with the origin at its center, and whose boundary is thus twice an
n
-dimensional plane ball centered at
0
. This is supported by many examples we have calculated. In particular, the best constant is certainly not
C(n)/(n-\beta)
appearing in the analogous inequality (
1.9
) of the plane case, which is sharp. But we were not able to prove (
1.10
) with this conjectured sharp constant.

The proof of our main Theorem
1.1
for the case
1/\tau\leqslant a/p+(1-a)/q
uses, among others, the methods developed in Cabré, Cozzi, and Csató
[
4
]
, Theorem
1.2
, and the new rearrangement based proof of the fractional Hardy inequality. To extend the result to the full range of parameters, i.e., to
1/\tau>a/p+(1-a)/q
we need some variants and applications of Theorem
1.2
; see Lemma
4.1
. That lemma provides the estimates

\int_{M\setminus B_{1}(0)}|x|^{-\beta}dx\leqslant C(n,\beta)\quad\text{ if }\beta>n\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{M\cap B_{1}(0)}|x|^{-\beta}dx\leqslant C(n,\beta)\quad\text{ if }\beta<n,

for some universal constant
C
,
provided
M
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which is the boundary of a convex set and
B_{1}(0)
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
These estimates are needed in a certain interpolation argument —already known in the plane case— to obtain the full range of parameters appearing in our main theorem. Such estimates cannot hold without the additional convexity assumption, as can be easily seen by similar examples as the ones indicated prior to Theorem
1.2
.

Notation

\mathcal{H}^{n}
on a hypersurface
M

E\subset M

|E|

B_{R}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\,|y-x|<R\}

If
x=0

B_{R}=B_{R}(0),

\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial B_{1}

n

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

\omega_{n}=|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\}|,

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.2
. The idea is to write the boundary of
\Omega
as a union of graphs over the
n+1
canonical hyperplanes passing through the origin, such that the slopes of these graphs is not too big. Essential will be that for convex sets there is a universal bound on the number of such graphs. Since we do not assume that
\Omega
is bounded, and also for the sake of settling the notation, we first summarize in an elementary lemma what can happen when constructing these graphs by projecting
\Omega
onto a hyperplane.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open convex set and define
P
as the projection map
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mapsto x^{\prime}
restricted to
\overline{\Omega}
. If
K_{n+1}=P(\overline{\Omega})
is the image, we denote for
x^{\prime}\in K_{n+1}

g^{-}(x^{\prime}):=\inf\{(P^{-1}(x^{\prime}))_{n+1})\},\qquad g^{+}(x^{\prime}):=\sup\{(P^{-1}(x^{\prime}))_{n+1}\},

where
(P^{-1}(x^{\prime}))_{n+1}
is the last coordinate of the preimage of
x^{\prime}
.

Then
g^{-}(x^{\prime})
(respectively
g^{+}(x^{\prime})
) is either finite for all
x^{\prime}\in K_{n+1}
or
g^{-}(x^{\prime})=-\infty
(respectively
g^{+}(x^{\prime})=+\infty
) for all
x^{\prime}\in K_{n+1}.

Moreover,

\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n+1}):\,x^{\prime}\in K_{n+1}\text{ and }g^{-}(x^{\prime})<x_{n+1}<g^{+}(x^{\prime})\}

and the function
g^{-}
is convex (respectively
g^{+}
is concave) in the convex set
K_{n+1}
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists two points
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in K_{n+1}
such that
g^{-}(x^{\prime})
is finite but
g^{-}(y^{\prime})=-\infty
. Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})
and
(y^{\prime},y_{n+1})
be in
\Omega.
But then by convexity the segment
[(x^{\prime},x_{n+1}),(y^{\prime},r)]\subset\Omega
for all
r\leqslant y_{n+1}.
By letting
r\to-\infty
we obtain that every point
(x^{\prime},t)
is in
\overline{\Omega}
if
t<g^{-}(x^{\prime})
, which is a contradiction to the definition and finiteness of
g^{-}(x^{\prime})
, since
\overline{\Omega}
is convex.

The second statement of the lemma is obvious, since by convexity of
\Omega
we have that
(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\Omega
if
g^{-}(x^{\prime})<x_{n+1}<g^{+}(x^{\prime})
.

Let us first prove the statement of the lemma claiming that
g^{-}
is convex in the case that
g^{-}
is finite and
g^{+}=+\infty
. In that case
\lambda(x^{\prime},s)+(1-\lambda)(y^{\prime},t)\in\Omega
for every
s>g^{-}(x^{\prime})
and
t>g^{-}(y^{\prime})
and every
0<\lambda<1
. Thus by definition of
g^{-}
we have that
g^{-}(\lambda x^{\prime}+(1-\lambda)y^{\prime})\leqslant\lambda s+(1-\lambda)t
. Letting
s
go to
g^{-}(x^{\prime})
, and
t
go to
g^{-}(y^{\prime})
respectively, gives the desired inequality. The other cases are proven in a similar way. In the case that
g^{-}(x^{\prime})=g^{+}(x^{\prime})
for some
(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=(x^{\prime},g^{\pm}(x^{\prime}))
, then one only needs to introduce the parameter
t
, but
s
has to be taken to equal to
x_{n+1}
in the previous argument.
∎

In the next Lemma we prove that
\partial\Omega
is the union (up to rotations) of not more than
2(n+1)
graphs whose slopes are uniformly bounded by little more than
\sqrt{n}.
For the next lemma to make sense, recall that a Lipschitz function is differentiable almost everywhere.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a convex open set. Then there exists at most
2(n+1)
closed sets
W_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty interior,
i=1,\ldots,2(n+1)
, and Lipschitz functions
g_{i}:W_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that the graphs of
g_{i}

\{(z,g_{i}(z)):\,z\in W_{i}\}=:M_{i}\,,

satisfy that
\partial\Omega
is the union

\partial\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2(n+1)}M_{i}\qquad\text{modulo rotations of each $M_{i}$}

up to a set of zero measure,
and

|\nabla g_{i}|\leqslant\sqrt{n+\epsilon}\quad\text{ in }W_{i}\,.

Note that the last statement on the upper bound on
|\nabla g_{i}|
makes sense in the closed set
W_{i}
(and not only in the interior), since a Lipschitz function can be extended to whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the same Lipschitz constant; see for instance
[
10
, Theorem 16.16]
. Hence
\nabla g_{i}
exists almost everywhere in
W_{i}
.

Each function
g_{i}
is actually either convex or concave, but we will not need this statement.

We have introduced
\epsilon>0
to make the proof as short and easy as possible, which certainly makes, in general, many
M_{i}
overlap. We have put no effort in constructing disjoint
M_{i}
, since even in that case the method of proof would not give what we believe is the best constant in Theorem
1.2
, and this would just have added unnecessary technical difficulties.

Proof.

Step 1
Recall that on the boundary
\partial\Omega
of a convex set the unit outward normal vector
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n+1})
exists almost everywhere. We define

\Gamma_{1}^{-}:=\left\{x\in\partial\Omega:\,\nu(x)\text{ exists and }\nu_{1}(x)<-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+n+\epsilon}}\right\}.

We assume that
\Gamma_{1}\neq\emptyset
(if this is not the case, we can jump to the next step) and denote
\widehat{x}_{1}:=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, using the projection map
P
of Lemma
2.1
onto the plane
\{x_{1}=0\}
(instead of the plane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
), the function
g_{1}^{-}(\widehat{x}_{1}):=\inf\{(P^{-1}(\widehat{x}_{1}))_{1}\}
is convex (
g_{1}^{-}
must be finite, since the tangent plane at

The proof of the above lemma is a direct application of Hölder inequality with the exponents
\alpha/\tau
and
\alpha/(\alpha-\tau)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
1/\tau>a/p+(1-a)/q
.

Recall that
\gamma<-as
by (

) yields that

a\neq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad q\neq p_{s}^{*}.

By assumption the parameters satisfy the identity

(4.12)

\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\frac{a}{p_{s}^{*}}+\frac{1-a}{q}=:A(a).

Observe that

(4.13)

\begin{split}A\text{ is increasing in $a$ }\quad&\text{ if $q>p_{s}*$},\\
A\text{ is decreasing in $a$ }\quad&\text{ if $q<p_{s}*$}.\end{split}

Since the inequality of Theorem
1.1
that we wish to prove is scaling invariant under the substitution
\Omega\mapsto\lambda\Omega
and
u(x)\mapsto cu(x/\lambda)
we can assume without loss of generality that

\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy+\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\alpha}(x)^{\frac{sp}{\alpha}}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx=1

and

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}=1,

since these two norms scale differently. In view of this it is sufficient to prove that

\||x|^{\gamma}u\|_{L^{\tau}(\partial\Omega)}\leqslant C

for some constant depending only on the parameters and the dimension, but not on
u
nor
\Omega
.
Since
0<a<1
we can choose
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that

\begin{split}0<a_{1}<a<a_{2}<1\quad\text{ if $q>p_{s}^{*}$},\\
0<a_{2}<a<a_{1}<1\quad\text{ if $q<p_{s}^{*}$}.\end{split}

The values of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

a,

n,

p,

q,

s,

\gamma,
and
\tau
.
In view of (
4.13

A(a_{1})<A(a)<A(a_{2}).

We now choose

and

\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}=\frac{a_{1}}{p_{s}^{*}}+\frac{1-a_{1}}{q}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma_{1}=0.

With this choice one has that the dimensional balance condition (

\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}+\frac{\gamma_{i}}{n}=A(a_{i}),\ i=1,2

1/p_{s}^{*}+s/n=1/p
and the dimensional balance condition leads to the two identities

(4.14)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}=&(a-a_{i})\left(\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+\frac{\gamma_{i}-\gamma}{n}\\
=&(a-a_{i})\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+\frac{\gamma_{i}-\gamma-(a-a_{i})s}{n},\end{split}

for

\tau_{i}>\tau\quad\text{ for }i=1,2.

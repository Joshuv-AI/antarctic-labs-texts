Some Density Results for HyperKähler Manifolds
Yajnaseni Dutta, Elham Izadi, Ljudmila Kamenova, Lisa Marquand

Abstract
. Lagrangian fibrations of hyperkähler manifolds are induced by semi-ample line bundles which are isotropic with respect to the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form. For a non-isotrivial family of hyperkähler manifolds over a complex manifold S S of positive dimension, we prove that the set of points in S S , for which there is an isotropic class in the Picard lattice of the corresponding hyperkähler manifold represented as a fiber over that point, is analytically dense in S S . We also prove the expected openness and density of the locus of polarised hyperkähler manifolds that admit a nef algebraic isotropic line bundle.

Introduction

A hyperkähler manifold is a simply connected compact Kähler manifold
X
of dimension
2n

admitting a unique (up to scaling) global homolomorphic symplectic 2-form
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})
. The existence of such a 2-form makes
X
Calabi–Yau in the sense that its canonical bundle is trivial. In dimension 2, these are K3 surfaces. In higher dimensions, two infinite series and two sporadic examples are known
[
Bea83
,
O’G99
,
O’G03
]
, up to deformation.

Since any hyperkähler manifold
X
comes equipped with a holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma
, Lagrangian subvarieties of
X
, i.e., subvarieties
Z\subset X
of dimension
n=\frac{1}{2}\dim X
such that
\sigma|_{Z^{\rm reg}}=0
, are interesting to study.
It is known
[
Mat99
]
that any morphism
f\colon X\to B
where
0<\dim B<2n
, is in fact a Lagrangian fibration, meaning the general fiber of
f
is a Lagrangian subvariety. The general fibers are in fact abelian varieties, as proved by Voisin (see
[
Cam06
, Prop. 2.1]
). Moreover, whenever
B
is smooth, it is in fact isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}

[
Hwa08
]
. Hence, such a Lagrangian fibration is induced by a semi-ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{n+1}=0
.

The second cohomology
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
comes equipped with a unique non-degenerate quadratic form
q_{X}
, the Beaville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form, which controls a great deal of geometric information about the hyperkähler manifold
X
. It has signature
(3,b_{2}-3),
and there exists a non-zero constant
c_{X}\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfying
\alpha^{2n}=c_{X}\cdot q_{X}(\alpha)^{n}
. In particular, admitting a semi-ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{n+1}=0
is equivalent to
q_{X}(\mathcal{L})=0.
Therefore, the first step in studying Lagrangian fibrations is the study of isotropic classes.

We prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
be a non-isotrivial family of hyperkähler manifolds with
b_{2}(X_{t})\geq 5
over a complex manifold
S
with
\dim S>0.
Then the set

\{t\in S\mid\exists\alpha\in H^{1,1}({\mathcal{X}}_{t},\mathbb{Z}),q_{X}(\alpha)=0\}

is either empty or dense in
S
in the analytic topology.

The case when
S=\Def(X)
, the whole deformation space of
X
, is already known.
For hyperkähler manifolds, there is a global Torelli theorem which allows one to prove such density statements directly on the period domain (i.e., the type IV symmetric domain associated to
(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),q_{X})
; see §
1
for details). On the period domain, these types of density results follow from ergodic properties
[
Huy16
, Prop. 7.1.3]
. For certain moduli spaces of lattice polarised hyperkähler manifolds, Mongardi and Pacienza establish similar density results in
[
MP23
, Theorem 3.15]
.
However, the question of density in an arbitrary family demands different methods. Our argument is Hodge theoretic and hence applies more generally to any non-isotrivial family of K3-type Hodge structures (see Theorem
1.2
for a precise statement).

One should compare Theorem
1.2
to the classical density result for Noether–Lefschetz loci, which, in the set-up of the theorem, states that the locus of hyperkähler manifolds where the Picard rank jumps is analytically dense in the base. This result is attributed to Oguiso
[
Ogu03
, Theorem 1.1]
and the key ingredient (also a key ingredient in our proof) is the criterion for the density of the Hodge classes, due to Green
[
Voi07
, Proposition 5.20]
.
For certain families of polarized hyperkähler manifolds, the density of the loci of hyperkählers with algebraic isotropic classes follows from equidistribution results of Tayou
[
Tay20
]
, and Tayou and Tholozan
[
TT23
]
.

(1)

A hyperkähler manifold

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler manifold and
L
an isotropic nef line bundle on
X
.
The locus
\Def(X,L)\setminus\Def(X,L)_{\rm nef}
is a countable union of hypersurfaces in
\Def(X,L).
Moreover, if, in addition,
X

\Def(X,L,H)_{\rm nef}
is open and dense in
\Def(X,L,H)

The countable union of hypersurfaces in the complement of the locus
\Def(X,L)_{\rm nef}
, as mentioned in Theorem
4
, is in fact induced by a countable union of the so-called
wall divisors
(see Definition
2.2

Roughly speaking, the closedness follows from the properly discontinous action of the group of isometries of the lattice on the period domain of polarised marked hyperkähler manifolds. Our proof critically uses this - see §
2
.

Let us finally note that questions about openness of the nef locus have been addressed in the literature beyond the hyperkähler setting
[
Mor92

. As a consequence of Theorem

on it, Moriwaki’s result
[

]
gives a bound on the minimum degree of the wall divisor that makes

2.5

1.
Density of isotropic classes

Throughout, we denote by
(S,{\mathbb{V}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},\nabla,q)
a variation of Hodge structures (VHS), with a bilinear form
q
, over a connected complex manifold
S
. Here
{\mathbb{V}}
is a local system of finite dimensional
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces with a Hodge filtration
{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}
of the holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{V}}:={\mathbb{V}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
, and
\nabla\colon{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}\otimes\Omega_{S}^{1}
is the Gauss–Manin connection. The form
q
is a flat bilinear form
{\mathbb{V}}\otimes{\mathbb{V}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}
whose restriction to each fiber
V_{s}
of
{\mathbb{V}}
at a point
s\in S
is non-degenerate on
V_{s}
.

Definition 1.1
.

A finite dimensional vector space
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
with a quadratic form
q
is of K3-type if the signature of
q
is
(3,\dim V_{\mathbb{R}}-3)
with
\dim V_{\mathbb{Q}}\geq 4
.
We say that a VHS
(S,{\mathbb{V}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},\nabla,q)
is of
K3
-type
if it is a variation of weight 2 Hodge structures with
\rank\mathcal{F}^{2}=1
and each fiber is a K3-type vector space.

Given a K3-type vector space
(V_{\mathbb{Q}},q)
, the period domain of K3-type Hodge structures on
(V_{\mathbb{Q}},q)
is defined as

\Omega\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathbb{P}(V_{\mathbb{C}})\mid q(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=0,q(\sigma)>0\}.

Let
(S,{\mathbb{V}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},\nabla,q)
be a VHS of K3-type with a trivialisation
\varphi\colon\mathbb{V}\overset{\sim}{\to}V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{S}
.
We have the holomorphic period map:

\mathcal{P}\colon S\to\Omega

defined by
t\mapsto\varphi_{t}(\mathcal{F}^{2}_{t})
. We say that a VHS of K3-type is
non-isotrivial
if the holomorphic period map
\mathcal{P}
is non-constant.

For a class
\alpha\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we denote by
\alpha^{\perp}(S)\subset S
the locus:

\alpha^{\perp}(S):=\{t\in S\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{t}\}.

Note that
\alpha^{\perp}(S)=\{t\in S\mid\alpha\in V^{1,1}_{t}\}
, since
\alpha
is equal to its complex conjugate.

We let
Q:=\{x\mid q(x)=0\}\subset V_{\mathbb{C}}
be the quadric cone defined by the form
q
; we will assume that
Q
has a nonzero rational point. Note that, if
\rank({\mathbb{V}})\geq 5
, this is automatically satisfied by Meyer’s theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(S,{\mathbb{V}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},\nabla,q)
be a non-isotrivial VHS of
K3
type with trivialisation
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{V}}\overset{\sim}{\to}V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}
as above. Assume that the quadric cone
Q
has a nonzero rational point.
Then the union

S_{\rm isot}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}\\
q(\alpha)=0\end{subarray}}\alpha^{\perp}(S)

is dense in
S
in the analytic topology.

Proof.

Consider the subset

Q^{1}:=\{(\lambda,s)\mid\lambda\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{s},\,q(\lambda)=0\}\subset Q\times S

and denote
p\colon Q^{1}\rightarrow Q
the restriction of the projection to the first factor. By Lemma
1.3
below, the map
p
is open around some point
(\lambda,s)\in Q^{1}
. Let
\pi\colon Q^{1}\to S
be the restriction of the second projection. For any
s\in S
, there is an open neighbourhood
U\ni s
such that
\pi^{-1}(U)\subset Q^{1}
contains an open neighbourhood
V
of
(\lambda,s)
surjecting onto
U
where
p|_{V}\colon V\to Q
is open.

Note that the induced map on the real points
p_{\mathbb{R}}\colon V(\mathbb{R})\to Q(\mathbb{R})
is also open.
The sets
Q({\mathbb{Q}})
and
Q({\mathbb{R}})
are non-empty by assumption. Therefore, since
Q({\mathbb{Q}})\subset Q({\mathbb{R}})
is dense, it follows that the subset
\{(\alpha,s)\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{s},q(\alpha)=0\}\cap V\subset V
is dense. Therefore its image
\displaystyle\bigcup_{q(\alpha)=0}\alpha^{\perp}(U)
in
U
is also dense.
∎

It remains to show that the map
p:Q^{1}\rightarrow Q
is a submersion. We do so below: we adapt the proof of
[
Voi07
, Lemma 5.21]
to our specific situation.

Lemma 1.3
.

For any
s\in S
, there exists
\lambda\in Q^{1}_{s}
such that the map
p\colon Q^{1}\rightarrow Q
is a submersion at
(\lambda,s)
.

Proof.

Denote by
\widetilde{p}\colon\mathcal{F}^{1}\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{C}}

the projection to the first factor.
By
[
Voi07
, Lemma 5.21]
,
the map
\widetilde{p}
is a submersion at
(\lambda,s)\in\mathcal{F}^{1}
if the map

\overline{\nabla}(\lambda)\colon T_{S,s}\rightarrow V_{s}^{0,2}

induced by the Gauss–Manin connection is surjective.
The map
\overline{\nabla}
is also the differential
d\mathcal{P}
of the period map
{\mathcal{P}}\colon S\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
. This is because

\overline{\nabla}\colon T_{S,s}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(V_{s}^{1,1},V_{s}^{0,2})

and we have isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}(V_{s}^{1,1},V_{s}^{0,2})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}((V_{s}^{0,2})^{*},(V_{s}^{1,1})^{*})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(V_{s}^{2,0},V_{s}^{1,1})\simeq T_{[V_{s}^{2,0}]}\Omega.

Since the VHS
(S,{\mathbb{V}},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},\nabla,q)
is not isotrivial, this differential is not zero and hence there exists
v\in T_{S,s}
such that
d\mathcal{P}(v)\colon V_{s}^{1,1}\to V^{0,2}_{s}
is non-trivial, hence surjective. Therefore there exists
\lambda\in V_{s}^{1,1}
such that
\overline{\nabla}(\lambda)
is surjective.

For a fixed
s
, the quadric
Q^{1}_{s}\cap V^{1,1}_{s}\subset V^{1,1}_{s}
is not contained in a hyperplane by the non-degeneracy of the quadratic form. Since the dimension of
V_{s}^{1,1}
is at least 2,
Q^{1}_{s}\cap V^{1,1}_{s}
cannot be contained in the linear subspace
\operatorname{Ker}(d\mathcal{P}(v))\subset V_{s}^{1,1}
. Therefore, the map
\widetilde{p}
is a submersion at
\lambda\in Q^{1}_{s}
.

Since
{Q^{1}}=\widetilde{p}^{-1}(Q)
, we obtain that for any
s\in S
, there exists
\lambda
such that
p
is a submersion at
(\lambda,s)
.
∎

We can apply Theorem
1.2
to non-isotrivial families of hyperkähler manifolds to recover Theorem

:

Corollary 1.4
.

[Theorem

]

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
be a non-isotrivial family of hyperkähler manifolds over a complex manifold
S

\dim S>0

b_{2}(X_{t})\geq 5

S_{\rm isot}:=\bigcup_{q(\alpha)=0}\alpha^{\perp}(S)

is either empty or dense in

Remark 1.5
.

One could also apply Theorem
1.2
to the Fano variety
F(X)
of lines associated to a cubic 4-fold

. The Fano variety

is a projective hyperkähler manifold of K3
[2]

Question 5

In the light of the SYZ conjecture (see Conjecture

Remark 1.6

Notice that the period domain

2.1.
Wall Divisors and the Kähler cones

Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{X}\subset H^{1,1}(X)\cap H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
the closed positive cone of
X
, i.e., the connected component of

\{x\in H^{1,1}(X)\cap H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})\mid q_{X}(x)>0\}

that contains a Kähler class. Recall that the Kähler cone
\mathcal{K}_{X}\subset\mathcal{C}_{X}
is the open convex cone of all Kähler classes on
X
. The birational Kähler cone
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{K}_{X}\subset\mathcal{C}_{X}
is the union of
f^{*}\mathcal{K}_{X^{\prime}}
, where
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is a birational map with
X^{\prime}
a hyperkähler manifold. An isotropic primitive line bundle
L\in H^{1,1}(X)
is nef if and only if
L\in\overline{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}}
, and hence we have:

\Def(X,L)_{\rm nef}\coloneqq\{t\in\Def(X,L)\mid{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{K}_{X_{t}}}\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
be a primitive divisor, not necessarily effective. Then
D
is a
wall divisor
if
q_{X}(D)<0,
and
\gamma(D^{\perp})\cap\mathcal{B}\mathcal{K}_{X}=\varnothing
for all
\gamma\in\Mon^{2}(X)_{\rm Hdg}.

Here,
\Mon^{2}(X)_{\rm Hdg}
is the subgroup of monodromy operators that preserve the Hodge structure on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and
D^{\perp}=\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{X}}\mid q_{X}(\alpha,D)=0\}.
Thus a line bundle

is nef on

provided
q_{X}(L,D)\geq 0
for all wall divisors

Note that by

is bounded below, i.e., there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
-N\leq q_{Y}(D)<0
. We thus define:

\mathcal{W}:=\{v\in\Lambda\mid-N\leq v^{2}<0,v\text{ primitive }\}.

For any

and any wall divisor

we have

The notion of wall divisors is equivalent to that of MBM classes

Proof of Theorem

Proposition 2.3
.

In the notation of Theorem
4
, the locus
\Def(X,L)\setminus\Def(X,L)_{\nef}
is a countable union of hypersurfaces in
\Def(X,L).

Proof.

Let
t\in\Def(X,L)\setminus\Def(X,L)_{\nef}
. Since
L_{t}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}|_{X_{t}}
is not nef on
X_{t}
, there exists a wall divisor
D_{t}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_{t})
such that
D_{t}^{\perp}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{X_{t}}}\neq\varnothing
, i.e., the wall
D_{t}^{\perp}
is a wall of the Kähler cone, and
q_{X_{t}}(D_{t},L_{t})<0

Then the class
D_{t}\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
generates an extremal ray of the Mori cone and some positive rational multiple
kD_{t}
is represented by an effective rational curve
C\subset X_{t}.

Let
v:=\varphi_{t}(D_{t})\in{\mathcal{W}}
. Recall, in the notation of §
1

v^{\perp}(\Def(X,L)):=\{s\in\Def(X,L)\mid\varphi_{s}^{-1}(v)\in H^{1,1}(X_{s})\cap H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\}.

Consider
t^{\prime}\in v^{\perp}(\Def(X,L))
. Since the rational curve
C
deforms exactly to the locus where
D_{t}
remains of type
(1,1)
, it follows that there is a curve
C_{t^{\prime}}
(possibly non integral) on
X_{t^{\prime}}
such that

L_{t^{\prime}}\cdot C_{t^{\prime}}=kq_{X}(L_{t},D_{t})<0

(see
[
AV20
, Discussion after Defn 2.10]
). It follows that
L_{t^{\prime}}
is not nef on
X_{t^{\prime}}
, and
v^{\perp}(\Def(X,L))\cap\Def(L,X)_{\nef}=\varnothing.
Thus

\Def(X,L)\setminus\Def(X,L)_{\nef}=\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v\cdot\varphi(L))<0\\
v\in\mathcal{W}\end{subarray}}v^{\perp}(\Def(X,L)),

a union of hypersurfaces. Since
{\mathcal{W}}
is countable, the union is also countable.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a polarised hyperkähler manifold with ample bundle
H
. Then
\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}
is open and dense in
\Def(X,L,H).

Proof.

By
Proposition

2.3
we see that

\Def(X,L,H)\setminus\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}=\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v\cdot\varphi(L))<0\\
v\in\mathcal{W}\end{subarray}}v^{\perp}(\Def(X,L,H)).

We will show that, for any
t\in\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}
, there is an open subset
U
containing
t
such that
U\subset\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}
.

Put
h:=\varphi_{t}(H)
, and denote by
\Omega_{h}\coloneqq\Omega\cap\mathbb{P}(h^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C})
, the associated period domain. We denote by
\Mon(\Lambda,h)\coloneqq\{g\in\Mon(X_{t})\mid g(h)=h\}
.
Since the lattice
h^{\perp}\subset\Lambda
has signature
(2,*)
, by
[
Huy16
, Remark 6.1.10]
the group
\Mon(\Lambda,h)
acts properly discontinuously on
\Omega_{h}
and hence for any
v\in h^{\perp}
, the union
\bigcup_{g\in\Mon(\Lambda,h)}g(v)^{\perp}
is closed. By
[
AV20
, Theorem 3.7]
, the monodromy group
\Mon(\Lambda,h)
acts on the set of wall divisors
{\mathcal{W}}
with finitely many orbits. Therefore the set

\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\Mon(\Lambda,h)\\
v^{2}<0\\
q(v,\varphi_{t}(L_{t}))<0\end{subarray}}g(v)^{\perp}

is closed in
\Omega_{h}
. The complement
U
of this set in
\Def(X,L,H)
is an open set containing
t
. Further,
U
is contained in
\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}
since, by the universality of the Kuranishi space, we have
\Def(X_{t},L_{t},H_{t})=\Def(X,L,H)
for
t
sufficienly close to
0
.

The density follows immediately since the complement of
\Def(X,L,H)_{\nef}
is a countable union of complex analytic codimension one subsets.
∎

As a consequence of the theorem above, the equivalent criterion for such openness due to Moriwaki
[
Mor92
, Theorem 1]
implies the following:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\to S
be a non-isotrivial family of marked hyperkähler manifolds with a fixed polarization
H
over a smooth base scheme
S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
{\mathcal{X}}
such that under the marking we have
\varphi_{s}(\mathcal{L}_{s})=\alpha
for a fixed
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Define

\sigma_{s}\coloneqq\min\{q_{X_{s}}(H,D)\mid D\text{ is a wall divisor, }D^{\perp}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{X_{s}}\neq\varnothing\text{ and }q_{X_{s}}(D,\alpha)<0\}.

Then there exists an integer
N
, such that for all
s\in S
,
\sigma_{s}<N
.

Proof.

Since, on hyperkähler manifolds, we can detect nefness by intersecting with wall divisors (with respect to
q_{X}
), we are able to mimic Moriwaki’s argument to obtain the result. Indeed, let
X={\mathcal{X}}_{0}
, then by
[
Bea83
, Théorèm 5, Corollaire 1]
there is a universal family over
\Def(X,H,L)
and there is a map
\varphi\colon S\to\Def(X,H,L)
such that
{\mathcal{X}}
is the pullback of the universal family over
\Def(X,H,L)
. By Theorem
4
above, there is an open subset
U\subset S
such that for all
s\in U
,
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is nef on
X_{s}
. In particular, for all
s\in U
,
\sigma_{s}=0
. Let
S^{\prime}\subset S\setminus U
be an irreducible component. We can conclude from
[
Mor92
, Lemma 2]
that for all
s\in S^{\prime}
we have
\sigma_{s}<\sigma_{\eta_{S^{\prime}}}
where
\eta_{S^{\prime}}
is the generic point of
S^{\prime}
. This concludes the result.

∎

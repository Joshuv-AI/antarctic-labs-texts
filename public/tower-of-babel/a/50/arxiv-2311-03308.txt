Revisiting α ′ \alpha^{\prime} corrections to heterotic two-charge black holes
Stefano Massai, Alejandro Ruipérez, Matteo Zatti

Abstract
We find solutions of the heterotic string effective action describing the first-order α ′ \alpha^{\prime} corrections to two-charge black holes at finite temperature. Making explicit use of these solutions, we compute the corrections to the thermodynamic quantities: temperature, chemical potentials, mass, charges and entropy. We check that the first law of black hole mechanics is satisfied and that the thermodynamics agrees with the one extracted from the Euclidean on-shell action. Finally, we show that our results are in agreement with the corrections for the thermodynamics recently predicted by Chen, Maldacena and Witten.

Introduction

The study of two-charge black holes has attracted much attention since the first investigations of black holes in string theory. This is mainly due to the fact that they are supposed to describe perhaps the simplest configuration in string theory which has a non-vanishing degeneracy of BPS states. This microscopic system consists of a fundamental heterotic string with winding
Q_{w}
and momentum
Q_{p}
charges along a compact direction
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{y}
. The degeneracy of BPS states of this system was computed by Dabholkar and Harvey
Dabholkar:1989jt
;
Dabholkar:1990yf
, and it is given by

S_{\rm{micro}}(Q_{p},Q_{w})=\log d\left(Q_{p},Q_{w}\right)=4\pi\sqrt{Q_{p}Q_{w}}\,.

(1.1)

Being a BPS degeneracy, it must be protected when extrapolating it to the finite string-coupling regime where an effective black hole description is expected to exist (a priori). In other words, it should be possible to match this BPS degeneracy with the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the corresponding black hole. However, when trying to do so one finds a puzzle: even though there is a supergravity solution with the same charges and preserving the same supersymmetries as the Dabholkar-Harvey states
Sen:1994eb
;
Cvetic:1995uj
;
Dabholkar:1995nc
;
Callan:1995hn
, it describes a singular black hole with vanishing horizon area. Hence, the naive macroscopic entropy that can be associated to the two-charge system vanishes.

In order to explain this mismatch, Sen proposed
Sen:1995in
that two-charge black holes have a small horizon of string size, and therefore it cannot be resolved by supergravity unless the latter is supplemented with higher-derivative terms capturing stringy
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections.

For this reason, these black holes are often referred to as
small
black holes.
Almost ten years after this proposal, it was claimed in
Dabholkar:2004yr
;
Dabholkar:2004dq
that four-derivative corrections in the context of type IIA on
{\mathbb{K}}_{3}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
(which is dual to heterotic on
{\mathbb{T}}^{6}
) stretch the horizon of two-charge black holes (hiding the singularity behind) and, what is even more remarkable, also give the precise contribution to the black hole entropy so that it reproduces the microstate counting of the two-charge system.

These results, however, have been recently questioned in a series of papers
Cano:2018hut
;
Ruiperez:2020qda
;
Cano:2021dyy
in which, working directly within the heterotic theory, it has been shown that
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections do not remove the singularity of BPS two-charge black holes. Furthermore, it has been argued that the configuration studied in
Dabholkar:2004yr
;
Dabholkar:2004dq
should correspond to a regular four-dimensional black hole whose entropy accidentally matches the microscopic degeneracy of the two-charge system, but which carries different charges and preserves less supersymmetry. The fact that it preserves less supersymmetry is indeed a smoking gun of the presence of additional sources (NS5 branes and Kaluza-Klein monopoles), which would be the ultimate reason explaining why this four-dimensional black hole has a regular horizon.

The fact that the two-charge system does not seem to admit a black hole description in the BPS limit is something which appears rather natural from the point of view of the correspondence between black holes and fundamental strings
Susskind:1993ws
;
Horowitz:1996nw
;
Horowitz:1997jc
;
Damour:1999aw
(see also
Chen:2021emg
;
Chen:2021dsw
;
Brustein:2021cza
;
Matsuo:2022kvx
;
Balthazar:2022hno
;
Ceplak:2023afb
for recent discussions). According to this proposal, black holes should turn into highly-excited strings when their sizes are of the order of the string scale. This has been recently discussed by Chen, Maldacena and Witten
Chen:2021dsw
precisely in the context of the two-charge system. Let us consider a two-charge black hole at finite temperature. This can be described in supergravity in terms of a solution with a large (macroscopic) horizon. However, if the black hole starts losing its mass it will reach the string size before reaching extremality, which would imply that the right description of the system near extremality should be a sort of self-gravitating string solution
Horowitz:1997jc
;
Damour:1999aw
rather than a solution with a horizon
Chen:2021dsw
.

\alpha^{\prime}

2.1
Two-charge black holes at leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}

Let us begin reviewing the two-derivative solution describing non-extremal two-charge black holes in
d
dimensions
Horowitz:1996nw
. Given that in subsection
2.2
we will solve the corrected ten-dimensional equations of motion, here we directly present the solution in its ten-dimensional form. However, since the solutions have a
{\mathbb{T}}^{(9-d)}
torus playing a trivial role, we feel free to ignore these torus directions from now on.
4
4

4

Taking them into account just amounts to add the flat metric on the torus
-d{\vec{z}}^{2}_{(9-d)}
to the
(d+1)
-dimensional metric (
2.1
).
Doing so, the resulting
(d+1)
-dimensional solution is given by

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}{\hat{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f}{{f}_{p}{f}_{w}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t^{2}-f^{-1}{\mathrm{d}}\rho^{2}-\rho^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}_{(d-2)}-k^{2}_{\infty}\frac{{f}_{p}}{{f}_{w}}\left({\mathrm{d}}y+\beta_{p}k^{-1}_{\infty}\left({f}_{p}^{-1}-1\right){\mathrm{d}}t\right)^{2}\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\hat{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{w}k_{\infty}\left({f}_{w}^{-1}-1\right){\mathrm{d}}t\wedge{\mathrm{d}}y\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle e^{2\hat{\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\hat{\phi}_{\infty}}{f}_{w}^{-1}\,,

(2.3)

where
\mathrm{d}{\hat{s}}
represents the line element in the string frame and

{f}_{p}=1+\frac{q_{p}}{\rho^{d-3}}\,,\hskip 28.45274pt{f}_{w}=1+\frac{q_{w}}{\rho^{d-3}}\,,\hskip 28.45274ptf=1-\frac{\rho_{s}^{d-3}}{\rho^{d-3}}\,.

(2.4)

The parameters
q_{p}
,
q_{w}
and
\rho_{s}
are related to the charges and mass of the solutions. Together with the moduli
{\hat{\phi}}_{\infty}
and
k_{\infty}
(representing the asymptotic values of the dilaton and the Kaluza-Klein scalar), they constitute the set of independent parameters of the solutions since
\beta_{p}
and
\beta_{w}
are subject to the following constraints,

\rho_{s}^{(d-3)}=q_{p}\left(\beta^{2}_{p}-1\right)=q_{w}\left(\beta^{2}_{w}-1\right)\,,

(2.5)

implying that

\beta_{i}=\epsilon_{i}\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho_{s}^{d-3}}{q_{i}}}\,,\hskip 28.45274pti=\left\{p,w\right\}\,.

(2.6)

where
\epsilon^{2}_{i}=1
. These correspond to the signs of the winding and momentum charges, respectively. In the BPS limit (
\rho_{s}\to 0
), the solution with
\epsilon_{w}=\epsilon_{p}
is supersymmetric, while the one with
\epsilon_{w}=-\epsilon_{p}
does not preserve any supersymmetry. The analysis of the Killing spinor equations for these configurations can be found for instance in
Ruiperez:2020qda
;
Cano:2021dyy
;
Cano:2021nzo
.

2.2
\alpha^{\prime}
-corrected solutions

Our aim now is to compute the first-order
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections to these two-charge black holes. As usual, we treat the
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections in a perturbative fashion and ignore
{\cal O}(\alpha^{\prime 2})
terms. The first-order
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections in the effective action of the heterotic superstring were studied in
Gross:1986mw
;
Metsaev:1987zx
;
Bergshoeff:1989de
. While different approaches were used, it was later shown in
Chemissany:2007he
that the resulting effective actions are equivalent up to field redefinitions. Here we choose to work in the Bergshoeff-de Roo scheme,
Bergshoeff:1989de
. In order to establish our conventions, we review the effective action and equations of motion in appendix
A
.

Before entering into the details of the corrected solutions, let us briefly explain the general strategy we have followed in order to find the corrected solutions. The interested reader is referred to appendix
B
or to
Cano:2022tmn
for more details. It turns out that an educated ansatz to solve the corrected equations of motion is the following,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}{\hat{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f}{{f}_{p}{\tilde{f}}_{w}}{\mathrm{d}}t^{2}-{g}\left(f^{-1}{\mathrm{d}}\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}_{(d-2)}\right)-k^{2}_{\infty}\frac{{f}_{p}}{{\tilde{f}}_{w}}\left[{\mathrm{d}}y+\beta_{p}k^{-1}_{\infty}\left({f}_{p}^{-1}-1\right){\mathrm{d}}t\right]^{2}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\hat{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{w}k_{\infty}\left({f}_{w}^{-1}-1\right){\mathrm{d}}t\wedge{\mathrm{d}}y\,,

(2.8)

where the functions
f,f_{p},f_{w},{\tilde{f}}_{w},g
and the dilaton
\hat{\phi}
are assumed to depend only on the radial coordinate
\rho
. For consistency with the perturbative approach, they must be of the form

\displaystyle{f}_{p}=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{q_{p}}{\rho^{d-3}}+\alpha^{\prime}{\delta f_{p}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt{\tilde{f}}_{w}=\,1+\frac{q_{w}}{\rho^{d-3}}+\alpha^{\prime}{\delta{\tilde{f}}_{w}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt{g}=\,1+\alpha^{\prime}{\delta{g}}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle{f}=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\rho_{s}^{d-3}}{\rho^{d-3}}+\alpha^{\prime}{\delta f}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt{f}_{w}=\,1+\frac{q_{w}}{\rho^{d-3}}+\alpha^{\prime}{\delta{f}_{w}}\,.

After linearization in
\alpha^{\prime}
, the equations of motion boil down to a linear system of inhomogeneous second-order ODEs for the unknown functions
\delta f_{p},\delta{\tilde{f}}_{w},\delta g,\delta f,\delta f_{w}
and
\hat{\phi}
. The strategy we are going to follow to solve them is the same as in
Cano:2022tmn
, which consists of performing an asymptotic expansion (large
\rho
) of the unknown functions and solve the equations of motion order by order. Following this procedure, we can determine all the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion except for a few of them which remain free, the integration constants. Once the form of the asymptotic solution has been found, we resum the asymptotic series with the help of
Mathematica
. The final step is to fix the integration constants by imposing regularity at the horizon and suitable boundary conditions. Our choice here will be such that we keep the asymptotic charges and the mass fixed:
i.e.
, we are going to give the form of the corrected solution in the micro-canonical ensemble.

In what follows we give the corrected solutions in
d=5
and
d=4
, as well as its dimensional reduction on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{y}
. Finally, we study their BPS limits and check that they agree with the corrected solutions found in
Cano:2018hut
;
Ruiperez:2020qda
;
Cano:2021dyy
.

Temperature and chemical potentials.

The chemical potentials associated to the Kaluza-Klein and winding vectors are obtained as usual,

Black hole entropy.
In higher-derivative theories the entropy follows from Wald’s formula

Namely, that the transformation of the

4
Thermodynamics from the Euclidean on-shell action

In the saddle-point approximation the Euclidean on-shell action of the black hole gives the dominant contribution to the grand-canonical partition function
Gibbons:1976ue
. This leads to the so-called quantum statisical relation,

I_{\infty}=\beta\,{\cal G}=\beta\left(E-\Phi_{p}\,Q_{p}-\Phi_{w}\,Q_{w}\right)-S\,,

(4.1)

where
I_{\infty}
is the renormalized Euclidean on-shell action and
{\cal G}
is the grand-canonical potential, which is regarded as a function of the (inverse) temperature and the chemical potentials. Knowing
{\cal G}={\cal G}\left(\beta,\Phi_{p},\Phi_{w}\right)
suffices to extract all the thermodynamic quantities since the mass, charges and entropy can be obtained (assuming the first law of black hole mechanics) as follows:

Q_{p}=-\frac{\partial{\cal G}}{\partial{\Phi}_{p}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptQ_{w}=-\frac{\partial{\cal G}}{\partial{\Phi}_{w}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptS=-\frac{\partial{\cal G}}{\partial{\beta^{-1}}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptE={\cal G}+\Phi_{p}\,Q_{p}+\Phi_{w}\,Q_{w}+\beta^{-1}S\,.

(4.2)

As shown e.g. in
Reall:2019sah
;
Bobev:2022bjm
;
Cassani:2022lrk
, this method to obtain the thermodynamics is particularly useful when dealing with higher-derivative corrections.

The purpose of this section is to evaluate the Euclidean on-shell action of the two-charge black holes at first order in
\alpha^{\prime}
and check that the thermodynamics that we get match the ones obtained in the previous section. For simplicity, we are going to evaluate the
(d+1)
-dimensional Euclidean effective action in the string frame, since its dimensional reduction on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{y}
gives rise to much more terms

;
Elgood:2020xwu
;
Ortin:2020xdm
;
Eloy:2020dko
;
Liu:2023fqq
. Instead, the
(d+1)
-dimensional action coincides with the ten-dimensional one (
A.1
) up to an overall factor which is absorbed in the
(d+1)
-dimensional Newton constant
{\hat{G}}_{N}
.

Then, the heterotic Euclidean on-shell action
I
for a manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M

Appendix A
Effective action and equations of motion

The effective action of the heterotic string at first order in
\alpha^{\prime}
in the Bergshoeff-de Roo formulation

is given by,

\displaystyle S_{\rm{eff}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{10}x\sqrt{-{\hat{g}}}\,e^{-2\hat{\phi}}\left[\hat{R}-4\,\partial^{\hat{\mu}}{\hat{\phi}}\,\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\hat{\phi}+\frac{1}{2\cdot 3!}{\hat{H}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{8}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}_{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}\hat{a}\hat{b}}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}\hat{a}\hat{b}}\right]\,,

(A.1)

where
{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}=\frac{1}{2}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}_{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}\,dx^{\hat{\mu}}\wedge dx^{\hat{\nu}}
is defined as the curvature two-form associated to the torsionful spin connection,

{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}-{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{c}}\wedge{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{c}}{}_{\hat{b}}\,,

(A.2)

where

{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}={\hat{\omega}}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}-\frac{1}{2}{\hat{H}}_{c}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}\,{\hat{e}}^{\hat{c}}\,.

(A.3)

In the formula above,
{\hat{\omega}}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}
represents the standard Levi-Civita spin connection and the torsion is determined by the three-form field strength
{\hat{H}}
, which satisfies the modified Bianchi identity

\mathrm{d}{\hat{H}}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}\wedge{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{b}}{}_{\hat{a}}=0\,.

(A.4)

This implies that, locally,

is given by

{\hat{H}}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{B}}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}{\hat{\Omega}}_{(-)}\,,

(A.5)

where
{\hat{\Omega}}_{(-)}

{\hat{\Omega}}_{(-)}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}\wedge{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{b}}{}_{\hat{a}}-\frac{2}{3}\,{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{a}}{}_{\hat{b}}\wedge{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{b}}{}_{\hat{c}}\wedge{\hat{\omega}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{c}}{}_{\hat{a}}\,.

\hat{H}

{\hat{H}}^{(0)}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{B}}^{(0)}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt{\hat{H}}^{(1)}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{B}}^{(1)}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}{\hat{\Omega}}^{(0)}_{(-)}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt...\hskip 14.22636pt{\hat{H}}^{(n)}=\mathrm{d}{\hat{B}}^{(n)}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}{\hat{\Omega}}^{(n-1)}_{(-)}\,,

(A.7)

where

\displaystyle=

(A.8)

\displaystyle 2{\hat{\nabla}}^{2}e^{-2\hat{\phi}}+e^{-2\hat{\phi}}\left[\hat{R}-4\,\partial^{\hat{\mu}}{\hat{\phi}}\,\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\hat{\phi}+\frac{1}{2\cdot 3!}{\hat{H}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{8}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}_{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}\hat{a}\hat{b}}{\hat{R}}_{(-)}{}^{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}\hat{a}\hat{b}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha^{\prime 2}\right)\,,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(e^{-2\hat{\phi}}\hat{\star}\hat{H}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha^{\prime 2}\right).

(A.10)

As observed in

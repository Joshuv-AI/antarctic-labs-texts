Generalized translation operator and the Heat equation for the canonical Fourier Bessel transform
Sami Ghazouani, Jihed Sahbani

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to introduce a translation operator associated to the canonical Fourier Bessel transform ℱ ν 𝐦 \mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}} and study some of the important properties. We derive a convolution product for this transform and as application we study the heat equation and the heat semigroup related to Δ ν 𝐦 . \Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}.



KEYWORDS: Canonical Fourier Bessel transform, Translation operator, Heat equation

AMS CLASSIFICATION: 33D15; 47A05

\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})

,
20

[

,

16

]

[

]
. In
[
19
]
, Wolf enlarged the concept of LCT to the Hankel setting. He built a class of linear integral transform with a kernel involving a Bessel function and matrix parameter
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}).
Special cases of this
transformation are the Hankel transform and the fractional Hankel transform
[
13
]
. In
[
9
]
, the authors introduced the Dunkl linear canonical transform (DLCT) which is a generalization of the LCT in the framework of Dunkl transform
[
4

. DLCT includes many well-known transforms such as the Dunkl transform
[
4
,

[

[

. In
[
6
]
, the authors developed a harmonic analysis related the canonical Fourier Bessel transform
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}.
Several properties, such as a Riemann-Lebesgue lemma, inversion formula, operational formulas, Plancherel
theorem and Babenko inequality and several uncertainty inequalities are established.

In the present work, we continue the analysis begun in
[
6
]
by studying the translation operator and the convolution product related to this transformation. Following the framework of Delsarte
[
3
]
and Levitan
[
15
]
, we introduce a generalized translation

T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}}f(y)=u(x,y)\ (x,y\geq 0,\ \mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}))
of a function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,+\infty[)
as the solution to the following Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{\nu,x}^{\mathbf{m}}\ u(x,y)=\Delta_{\nu,y}^{\mathbf{m}}\ u(x,y),\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,0)=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\left(\frac{2\nu+1}{x}-2i\frac{d}{b}x\right)\frac{d}{dx}-\left(\frac{d^{2}}{b^{2}}x^{2}+2i\left(\nu+1\right)\frac{d}{b}\right)

and where
\Delta_{\nu,x}^{\mathbf{m}}
act in the
x

T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}}f(y)=u(x,y)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}(x^{2}+y^{2})}T_{x}^{\nu}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}s^{2}}f(s)\right](y)

where
T_{x}^{\nu}
is the generalized translation operators associated with the Bessel operator
\Delta_{\nu}

[
15
]
. We also study some of the important properties of the translation operator
T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}}
such that the continuity property with respect to the norm
\|.\|_{p,\nu}.
Next, we give a generalized convolution product
\underset{\nu,\mathbf{m}}{*}
on
(0,+\infty)
tied to the differential operator
\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}
, by putting

\displaystyle f\underset{\nu,\mathbf{m}}{*}g(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left[T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}}f(y)\right]\ \left[e^{-i\frac{d}{b}y^{2}}g(y)\right]y^{2\nu+1}dy

and study some of its very basic properties. As application, we conclude this paper by studying the heat equation associated to
\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}u(t,x),\ (t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\

2.1
Notations and functional spaces

We denote by:

\bullet\ \mathcal{C}_{*,0}(\mathbb{R})
the space of even continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
and vanishing at infinity. We provide
\mathcal{C}_{*,0}(\mathbb{R})
with the topology of uniform convergence.

\bullet\ \mathcal{C}_{*,c}(\mathbb{R})
the space of even continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
and with compact support.

\bullet\ \mathcal{C}_{*,b}(\mathbb{R})
the space of even and bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}.
We provide
\mathcal{C}_{*,b}(\mathbb{R})
with the topology of uniform convergence.

\bullet\ \mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}
the Lebesgue space of measurable functions on
[0,+\infty[
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|f(y)\right|^{p}y^{2\nu+1}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,\ \mbox{if}\ 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{y\in[0,\infty[}{\mbox{ess sup}}|f(y)|<\infty,\ \mbox{if}\ p=\infty.

We provide
\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}
with the topology defined by the norm
\left\|.\right\|_{p,\nu}
.

\bullet\ \mathcal{L}_{2,\nu}
the Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
\langle.,.\rangle_{\nu}
given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\nu}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(y)\overline{g(y)}y^{2\nu+1}dy.

\bullet\ \mathcal{E}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
the space of even
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}.
We provide it with the topology of uniform
convergence on all compacts of
\mathbb{R},
for functions and their derivatives.

\bullet\ \mathcal{S}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
the Schwartz space of even
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
and rapidly decreasing together with their
derivatives. We provide
\mathcal{S}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
with the topology defined by the seminorms:

q_{n,m}(f)=\sup_{x\geq 0}(1+x^{2})^{n}\left|\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}f(x)\right|;\ f\in\mathcal{S}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R}),\ n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

\bullet\ \mathcal{D}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
the space of even
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support. We have

\mathcal{D}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})=\underset{a\geq 0}{\cup}\mathcal{D}_{\ast,a}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{\ast,a}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of even
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
with support in the interval
[-a,a].
We provide
\mathcal{D}_{\ast,a}(\mathbb{R})
with the topology of uniform convergence of functions and their derivatives.

\bullet\ \mathbb{H}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C})
the space of even entire functions on
\mathbb{C}
rapidly decreasing and of exponential type. We have

\mathbb{H}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C})=\underset{a\geq 0}{\cup}\mathbb{H}_{a}(\mathbb{C}),

where
\mathbb{H}_{a}(\mathbb{C})
is the space of even entire functions
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and satisfying for all
m\in\mathbb{N}

P_{m}(f)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}(1+|\lambda|)^{m}|f(\lambda)|e^{-a|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|}<\infty.

We provide
\mathbb{H}_{a}(\mathbb{C})
with the topology defined by the seminorms
P_{m},\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

2.2
Canonical Fourier Bessel transform

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbf{m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)
an arbitrary matrix in
SL(2,\mathbb{R}).
For
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
b\neq 0,
the canonical Fourier Bessel transform of a function
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
is defined by
[
6
,
9
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}f(x)=\displaystyle\frac{c_{\nu}}{(ib)^{\nu+1}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\ K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(x,y)f(y)y^{2\nu+1}dy,

where
c_{\nu}^{-1}=2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)
and

\displaystyle K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(x,y)=e^{\frac{i}{2}(\frac{d}{b}x^{2}+\frac{a}{b}y^{2})}\ j_{\nu}\left(\frac{xy}{b}\right).

Here
j_{\nu}
denotes the normalized Bessel function of order
\nu>-1/2
and defined by
[
8
,
18
]

j_{\nu}(x)=2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)\displaystyle\frac{J_{\nu}(x)}{x^{\nu}}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{n!\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n};\ x\in\mathbb{C}.

It is well known that, for every
y\in\mathbb{C},
the function
j_{\nu}(y\cdot):x\mapsto j_{\nu}(yx)

is the unique solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\nu}f=-y^{2}f\\
f(0)=1,\ f^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{\nu}
is the Bessel operator given by

\Delta_{\nu}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}.

For every
\nu>-\frac{1}{2},
the normalized Bessel function
j_{\nu}
has the Mehler integral representation

j_{\nu}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{2\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-t^{2}\right)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\cos(xt)dt.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{C},\quad|j_{\nu}(x)|\leq e^{|\mathrm{Im}(x)|}.

The normalized Bessel function
j_{\nu}
satisfies, for all
x,z\in[0,+\infty[
, the following product formula
[
18
]

j_{\nu}(xy)j_{\nu}(yz)=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}j_{\nu}\left(y\sqrt{x^{2}+z^{2}-2xz\cos(\theta)}\right)\sin^{2\nu}(\theta)d\theta.

It is easy to see that, for each
y\in\mathbb{R},
the kernel
K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(\cdot,y)
of the canonical Fourier Bessel transform
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}
is the unique solution of
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\ K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(\cdot,y)=-\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}\ K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(\cdot,y),&\\
K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(0,y)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b}y^{2}},\ \frac{d}{dx}K_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(0,y)=0,\end{array}\right.

where for each
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
b\neq 0,\ \Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}
denotes the differential operator

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\left(\frac{2\nu+1}{x}-2i\frac{d}{b}x\right)\frac{d}{dx}-\left(\frac{d^{2}}{b^{2}}x^{2}+2i\left(\nu+1\right)\frac{d}{b}\right).

(2.3)

Many properties of the Fourier Bessel transform carry over to the canonical Fourier Bessel transform. In particular:

Theorem 2.1

[
6
]

(1)
Let
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}).

(a)
The reversibility property
: For all
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
with
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}f\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\right)f=\left(\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\right)f=f,\ a.e.

(b)
The canonical Fourier Bessel transform
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}
is a topological isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
into itself. More precisely, for each
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
there exist
n_{1},\dots,n_{k},m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle q_{n,m}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}f\right)\leq c\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{n_{i},m_{i}}(f).

(2.4)

(2)
Operational formulas
: Let
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
b\neq 0
and
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\ast}(\mathbb{R}).
Then

(a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\left[y^{2}f(y)\right](x)=-b^{2}\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}f\right](x).

(b)

\displaystyle x^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}(f)(x)=-b^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\left[\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f\right](x).

(2.5)

(3)
Babenko inequality
: Let
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
b\neq 0.
Let
p
and
q
be real numbers such that
1<p\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}
extends to a bounded linear operator
on
\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}
and we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}f\right\|_{q,\nu}\leq|b|^{(\nu+1)(\frac{2}{q}-1)}\left(\frac{(c_{\nu}p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(c_{\nu}q)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\nu+1}\|f\|_{p,\nu}.

Translation operators associated with

We shall begin the section by recalling some well-known facts about the generalized translation operators
T_{x}^{\nu}
associated
with the Bessel operator
\Delta_{\nu}.

3.1
Generalized translation operators associated with
\Delta_{\nu}

Levitan
[
15
]
defined the generalized translation operators
T_{x}^{\nu}
associated
with
\Delta_{\nu}
by:

\displaystyle T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)=u(x,y)

where
u(x,y)
is the unique solution of the Cauchy problem
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)u(x,y)=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)u(x,y),\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\ f\in\mathcal{E}_{*}(\mathbb{R}),\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,0)=0.\end{array}\right.

The solution of (
3.1
) can be represented in the following form
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}f\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos(\theta)}\right)\left(\sin(\theta)\right)^{2\nu}d\theta.

It is interesting to note that
T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)
is an even function with respect to both variables and then we can assume that
x\geq 0
and
y\geq 0.
By a
change of variables we get

T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(z)\ \mathcal{W}_{\nu}(x,y,z)\ z^{2\nu+1}dz;\ x,y\geq 0.

(3.5)

Here, the kernel
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(x,y,z)
is given by

\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(x,y,z)=\frac{2^{1-2\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}\frac{\Delta^{2\nu-1}}{(xyz)^{2\nu}}\upharpoonleft_{[|x-y|,\ x+y]}

where

\Delta=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{(x+y+z)(x+y-z)(x-y+z)(y+z-x)}

denotes the area of the triangle with sides
x,y,z>0
and
\upharpoonleft_{A}
is the indicator function of
A.

We list some standard properties of the generalized translation operators
T_{x}^{\nu}
that we shall use in this article.

Theorem 3.1

[
15
]
The operators
T_{x}^{\nu},x\in\mathbb{R},
satisfy:

1)

Symmetry:

T_{0}^{\nu}=\mbox{identity}
and
T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)=T_{y}^{\nu}f(x).

2)

Linearity:

T_{x}^{\nu}\left[af+g\right](y)=aT_{x}^{\nu}f(y)+T_{x}^{\nu}g(y).

3)

Positivity:
If
f(y)\geq 0
then
T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)\geq 0.

4)

Compact support:
If
f(y)=0
for
y\geq a
then
T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)=0
for
|x-y|\geq a.

5)

Product formula:
For all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},
we have the product formula

T_{x}^{\nu}\left[s\mapsto j_{\nu}(\lambda s)\right](y)=j_{\nu}(\lambda x)j_{\nu}(\lambda y).

5)

Continuity of translation operators
T_{x}^{\nu}
:
The operator
T_{x}^{\nu}
is continuous from:

\bullet

\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R})
into itself. (The space
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R})
of all continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
is equipped with the topology of uniform convergence
on compact sets)

\bullet

\mathcal{E}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
into itself.

\bullet

\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}
into itself. More precisely, for all
f\in\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu},\ p\in[1,+\infty],
and
x\geq 0,
the function
T_{x}^{\nu}f
is defined
almost everywhere on
[0,+\infty[,
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}
and we have

\left\|T_{x}^{\nu}f\right\|_{p,\nu}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{p,\nu}.

6)

Commutativity:

\bullet
The following equality hold in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}):\ T_{x}^{\nu}\circ T_{y}^{\nu}=T_{y}^{\nu}\circ T_{x}^{\nu}.

\bullet
The following equality hold in
\mathcal{E}_{*}(\mathbb{R}):\ \Delta_{\nu}\circ T_{x}^{\nu}=T_{x}^{\nu}\circ\Delta_{\nu}.

7)

Self-adjointness of
T_{x}^{\nu}
:
Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
and
g\in\mathcal{C}_{*,b}(\mathbb{R}).
Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)g(y)y^{2\nu+1}dy=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(y)T_{x}^{\nu}g(y)y^{2\nu+1}dy.

In particular:

\bullet
For
g(y)=1,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}T_{x}^{\nu}f(y)y^{2\nu+1}dy=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(y)y^{2\nu+1}dy.

\bullet
For
g(y)=j_{\nu}(\lambda y),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\left[T_{x}^{\nu}f\right](\lambda)=j_{\nu}(\lambda x)\ \mathcal{F}_{\nu}f(\lambda).

Generalized translation operators associated with
\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}

We begin with the following Proposition

Proposition 3.1

Let
\mathbf{m}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
b\neq 0.
For each
f\in\mathcal{E}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{\nu,x}^{\mathbf{m}}\ u(x,y)=\Delta_{\nu,y}^{\mathbf{m}}\ u(x,y),\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,0)=0,\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution given by:

\displaystyle u(x,y)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}(x^{2}+y^{2})}T_{x}^{\nu}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}s^{2}}f(s)\right](y).

Here
\Delta_{\nu,x}^{\mathbf{m}}
act in the
x
variable and given by (
2.3
).

Proof.
From the transmutation property

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}\circ\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}}\ \circ e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}=\Delta_{\nu},

the system (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)\tilde{u}(x,y)=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\tilde{u}(x,y),\\
\tilde{u}(x,0)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}f(x),\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\tilde{u}(x,0)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{u}(x,y)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}(x^{2}+y^{2})}u(x,y).
By (
3.1
) we obtain

\tilde{u}(x,y)=T_{x}^{\nu}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}s^{2}}f(s)\right](y)

and then

\displaystyle u(x,y)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}(x^{2}+y^{2})}T_{x}^{\nu}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}s^{2}}f(s)\right](y).

f\in\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}\ (1\leq p\leq\infty)
In view of (
3.16
) and (
4.8
) we can write

u(t,x)=[\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\underset{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}{*}f](x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)e^{i\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}f(y)y^{2\nu+1}\ dy.

For any
(t,x)\in]0,+\infty[\times\mathbb{R}
the function
y\mapsto G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)
is in
\mathcal{L}_{q,\nu}
for
1\leq q\leq\infty.
Then
[\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\underset{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}{*}f]
is well defined and by (
4.9
), we can show that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)e^{i\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}f(y)y^{2\nu+1}\ dy.

Now a rough calculation shows that

\displaystyle\left|\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)(4\sigma t)^{\nu+1}}\left(\frac{r^{2}+y^{2}}{4\sigma t^{2}}+\frac{\nu+1}{t}+\frac{ry}{2\sigma t^{2}}\right)e^{-\frac{(y-r)^{2}}{4\sigma t}}

for
|x|\leq r
and
y\geq r.
Hence

\displaystyle\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}u(t,x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)\ e^{i\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}f(y)y^{2\nu+1}\ dy

which yields the desired result by virtue of
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y)=\sigma\Delta_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}G_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(x,y).

2)
We begin by showing that if
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast,c}(\mathbb{R}),
then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f-f\right\|_{p,\nu}=0.

By (
3.13
), we can write

\displaystyle S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}[T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}](y)[e^{i\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}f(y)]y^{2\nu+1}dy

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{i\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(y)\ [T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f](y)y^{2\nu+1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)(4\sigma t)^{\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b}y^{2}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\sigma t}}\ [T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f](y)y^{2\nu+1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{2i\frac{a}{b}\sigma ty^{2}}e^{-y^{2}}\ [T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f]\left(\sqrt{4\sigma t}y\right)\ y^{2\nu+1}dy.

Since
\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y^{2}}y^{2\nu+1}dy=1,
the formula (
4.11
) implies

\displaystyle S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f(x)-f(x)=a_{t}(x)+b_{t}(x)

(4.12)

where

\displaystyle a_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\left(e^{2i\frac{a}{b}\sigma ty^{2}}-1\right)f(x)\ y^{2\nu+1}dy,

\displaystyle b_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{2i\frac{a}{b}\sigma ty^{2}}e^{-y^{2}}\left([T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f]\left(\sqrt{4\sigma t}y\right)-f(x)\right)\ y^{2\nu+1}dy.

The relation (
4.12
) implies that

\displaystyle\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f-f\|_{p,\nu}\leq\|a_{t}\|_{p,\nu}+\|b_{t}\|_{p,\nu}

with

\displaystyle\|a_{t}\|_{p,\nu}\leq\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\left|e^{2i\frac{a}{b}\sigma ty^{2}}-1\right|y^{2\nu+1}dy\right]\ \|f\|_{p,\nu}\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow 0,

and by Minkowski’s inequality for integrals

\displaystyle\|b_{t}\|_{p,\nu}\leq\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\left\|T_{\sqrt{4\sigma t}y}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f-f\right\|_{p,\nu}y^{2\nu+1}dy\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow 0.

This is clear from the dominated convergence theorem since:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\sqrt{4\sigma t}y}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f-f\right\|_{p,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f\|_{p,\nu}\quad(\mbox{by}\ (\ref{inequa})),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\|T_{\sqrt{4\sigma t}y}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f-f\|_{p,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad(\mbox{see Theorem \ref{theo13}}).

Now suppose
f\in\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu}.
If
\epsilon>0
and since
\mathcal{C}_{\ast,c}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
\mathcal{L}_{p,\nu},
there exists
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast,c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\|f-g\|_{p,\nu}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3},
so

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f-f\right\|_{p,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)(f-g)\right\|_{p,\nu}+\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)g-g\right\|_{p,\nu}+\left\|g-f\right\|_{p,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|g-f\right\|_{p,\nu}+\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)g-g\right\|_{p,\nu}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{3}+\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)g-g\right\|_{p,\nu}

and
\left\|S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)g-g\right\|_{p,\nu}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
if
t
is sufficiently small.

3)
By Young’s inequality (
3.17
)

\displaystyle\|u(t,.)\|_{r,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\underset{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}{*}f\|_{r,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\ \|_{q,\nu}\ \|f\|_{p,\nu}.

The desired result is therefore a consequence of

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}\|_{q,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)(4\sigma t)^{(\nu+1)}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{qy^{2}}{4\sigma t}}y^{2\nu+1}\ dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)(4\sigma t)^{(\nu+1)}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\frac{\nu+1}{q}}.

4)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=S_{\nu}^{\mathbf{m}^{-1}}(t)f(x)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\ e^{2i\frac{a}{b}\sigma ty^{2}}[T_{x}^{\nu,\mathbf{m}^{-1}}f]\left(\sqrt{4\sigma t}y\right)\ y^{2\nu+1}dy.

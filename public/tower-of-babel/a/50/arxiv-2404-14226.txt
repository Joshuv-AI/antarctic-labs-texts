General degree divergence-free finite element methods for the Stokes problem on smooth domains Thanks: The second author was supported in part by the NSF, grant DMS-2309425. Thanks: All data generated or analysed during this study are included in this article.
Rebecca Durst, Michael Neilan

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct and analyze divergence-free finite element methods for the Stokes problem on smooth domains. The discrete spaces are based on the Scott-Vogelius finite element pair of arbitrary polynomial degree greater than two. By combining the Piola transform with the classical isoparametric framework, and with a judicious choice of degrees of freedom, we prove that the method converges with optimal order in the energy norm. We also show that the discrete velocity error converges with optimal order in the L 2 L^{2} -norm. Numerical experiments are presented, which support the theoretical results.



1.
Introduction

Divergence-free methods for the Stokes problem have grown in popularity due to the various advantages they present. This includes pressure-robustness, which allows the errors of the pressure and velocity to be decoupled so that the scheme is well-suited to systems in which the pressure term in the Stokes problem is dominant (i.e. systems with a large pressure gradient or small viscosity). Other advantages include mass-conservation and parameter robustness. Consequently, these methods have become an active area of research (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
9
,
6
,
8
,
10
]
). However, most work on these methods is focused on polyhedral domains. The extension to smooth domains (with optimal-order convergence) is non-trivial and only recently have various approaches been proposed
[
15
,
13
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we propose an arbitrary degree, divergence-free, isoparametric finite element scheme based
on the Scott-Vogelius pair
[
17
]
.
On polygonal domains, this approach approximates the velocity
with continuous, piecewise polynomials of degree
k
,
and approximates the pressure with discontinuous polynomials
of degree
(k-1)
. It is well known that the stability of this pair
depends on both the triangulation and the polynomial degree
k
.
We will work on Clough-Tocher splits which
yield a stable element pair provided
k\geq 2
. This is a commonly used method allowing greater flexibility with respect to polynomial degree.

In our approach, we combine this Scott-Vogelius pair with an isoparametric paradigm. To do so,
we apply
k
-degree polynomial diffeomorphisms to define the curvilinear triangulation
and the finite element spaces. While this approach is classical for isoparametric elements (see
[
4
,
16
]
),
its extension to divergence-free methods is non-standard and a direct application
of this approach fails to lead to divergence-free and pressure-robust schemes.
In particular, using classical isoparametric Lagrange finite element spaces
for velocity approximations disrupts the divergence-free and pressure-robust properties of the scheme.
Instead, we employ the divergence-preserving Piola transform in the definition
of the discrete velocity space. This transform is defined
on the macro (unrefined) triangulation, and we treat the resulting finite element spaces
as macro elements defined on the unrefined triangulation.

The primary challenge in this approach lies in the fact that the Piola transform pollutes the continuity of functions in the Lagrange finite element space. More specifically, when the functions in the discrete velocity space are defined by the Piola transform,
only normal continuity across interior edges is guaranteed.
Thus, the resulting space is only
H(div)
-conforming. Nonetheless, the spaces are designed to have weak continuity properties that are leveraged to ensure consistency and stability so that no additional terms in the bilinear form (e.g., penalty terms) are required in the method.

Consequently, one of the main contributions of this paper is to design a finite element space
that combines the Lagrange finite element space with the Piola transform
and possesses sufficient weak continuity properties
across interior edges. We achieve such a space via a judicious choice of edge degrees of freedom; specifically, these are taken as the Gauss-Lobatto points of interior edges.
This construction allows us to derive a general estimate
of the jumps of discrete velocity functions across interior edges (cf. Lemma
4.7
).

This work is an extension of
[
15
]
where the lowest-order case
k=2

was considered. As expected, some of the results in
[
15
]
extend to the general case, such as scaling arguments and inf-sup stability. However, the weak continuity properties of the discrete velocity space
is subtle, and a naive extension of
[
15
]
to arbitrary polynomial
degree does not necessarily lead to an optimal-order convergent method. Another contribution is
L^{2}
error estimates. Again, this requires new estimates of the discrete velocity functions
across interior edges.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In the next section, we introduce notation, state the properties of the polynomial diffeomorphism, describe the domain discretization, and introduce the Piola transform. We also establish some necessary preliminary results that
are later used in the convergence analysis. In Section
3
, we define the local finite element spaces and the degrees of freedom and introduce the global spaces in Section
4
. Also in Section
4
, we discuss the weak continuity properties of the function spaces and show that the method is inf-sup stable. In Section
5
, we introduce the finite element method and derive optimal-order
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
error estimates for the velocity and pressure solutions, respectively. Then, in Section
6
, we prove optimal-order convergence in
L^{2}
for the discrete velocity solution, and in Section
7
, we provide numerical experiments to verify our theoretical results. Finally, some auxiliary results are proved in Appendices
A
and
B
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded,
and sufficiently smooth domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We then construct a mesh following the divergence-free isoparametric method outlined in
[
15
]
.

2.1.
Isoparametric framework

We begin with a shape regular, affine (simplicial) triangulation
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, with sufficiently small mesh size

h=\max_{\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\rm diam}(\tilde{T})
.
Furthermore, we assume that the boundary vertices lie on
\partial\Omega
, that

\tilde{\Omega}_{h}:={\rm{int}}\big(\cup_{\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\bar{\tilde{T}}\big)

is an
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
polygonal approximation of
\Omega
, and each
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
has at most two boundary vertices.

Next, we let
G:\tilde{\Omega}_{h}\to\Omega
be a bijective map between the domain and the mesh with
\|G\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\leq C
. Here and throughout the paper,
C
denotes
a generic positive constant that is independent of any mesh parameter and may take on different values
at each occurrence. We define
G
such that
G|_{\tilde{T}}(x)=x
at all vertices of
\tilde{T}
. Furthermore, we assume that
G
is the identity map on interior edges, i.e., edges containing at most one vertex on the boundary.

From here, we define a mesh with curved boundaries following a standard isoparametric framework (see e.g.
[
4
,
2
,
12
,
5
]
). In particular, we define
G_{h}
to be the piecewise polynomial nodal interpolant of
G
of degree
\leq k
(
k\geq 2
), with
\|G_{h}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\tilde{T})}\leq C
and
\|G_{h}^{-1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\tilde{T})}\leq C
for all
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. Then, the isoparametric triangulation and computational domain are given by

\mathcal{T}_{h}:=\{G_{h}(\tilde{T}):\ \tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},\quad\Omega_{h}:={\rm{int}}\big(\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bar{T}\big).

In particular,
\Omega_{h}
is an
O(h^{k+1})
approximation to
\Omega
.
We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}
the piecewise norm with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, i.e.,

\|q\|_{H^{m}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|q\|^{2}_{H^{m}(T)}.

We also denote by
\nabla_{h}
the piecewise gradient operator with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, so
that
\nabla_{h}q|_{T}=\nabla(q|_{T})
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

2.2.
The mappings
F_{\tilde{T}}
and
F_{T}

To define the finite element spaces, we must first construct mappings to and from the affine and curved triangulations,
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. To do so, we define
\hat{T}
to be the reference triangle with vertices
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
, and
(0,0)
. Then for each
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we
let
F_{\tilde{T}}:\hat{T}\to\tilde{T}
be an affine bijection with
|F_{\tilde{T}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}

and
|F_{\tilde{T}}^{-1}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\tilde{T})}\leq Ch_{\tilde{T}}^{-1}

for
h_{\tilde{T}}={\rm{diam}}(\tilde{T})
.
Subsequently, we may define
F_{T}:\hat{T}\to T
by

F_{T}=G_{h}\circ F_{\tilde{T}}
. For each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
the polynomial diffeomorphism
F_{T}
and its inverse
and assumed to satisfy the following estimates:

(2.1)

\begin{split}|F_{T}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}&\leq Ch_{T}^{m}\ \ (0\leq m\leq k),\qquad|F_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{m,\infty}}\leq Ch_{T}^{-m}\ \ (0\leq m\leq(k+1)),\\
c_{1}h_{T}^{2}&\leq\det(DF_{T})\leq c_{2}h_{T}^{2}.\end{split}

Here, we have
h_{T}={\rm{diam}}(G_{h}^{-1}(T))
, and
c_{1},c_{2}
are generic constants independent of
h_{T}
. Furthermore, we note that, due to the assumptions on
G
, the mappings
F_{T}
and
F_{\tilde{T}}
are oriented so that they match at the vertices of
\hat{T}
. Consequently, the mappings are the same on triangles with three interior edges, so that for all such triangles
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
we have
T=G_{h}(\tilde{T})=\tilde{T}
.

2.3.
The boundary regions of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{h}

With the isoparametric triangulation established, we
define
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h}=(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h})\cup(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)

and note it may be shown that (see e.g.
[
3
, Equation 3.9]
for proof)

(2.2)

|\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h}|\leq Ch^{k+1}.

Next, by the construction of
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h}
, we have a bound of
the
H^{1}
semi-norm in this boundary region.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
be extended into
\mathbb{R}^{2}

in a way such that
\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Then for
h
sufficiently small,

\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\triangle\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
d
be the signed distance function
of
\Omega
with the convention
d(x)<0
for
x\in\Omega
.
For
\delta>0
, define

U_{\delta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ d(x)<\delta\},

and note that, because
{\partial}\Omega
is
C^{2}
, there holds
{\partial}U_{\delta}\in C^{2}

for
\delta>0
sufficiently small.
We then set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\delta}:=\{x\in U:|d(x)|<\delta\}

to be the tubular region around
{\partial}U_{\delta}
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 4.10]
, there holds for all
w\in H^{1}(U_{\delta})
:

\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\delta})}\leq C\delta^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{1}(U_{\delta})}.

Now set
\delta_{h}=2{\rm dist}\{{\partial}\Omega_{h},{\partial}\Omega\}=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
,
so that
\Omega\triangle\Omega_{h}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta_{h}}
,
and assume
h
is sufficiently small such that
{\partial}U_{\delta_{h}}\in C^{2}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\triangle\Omega_{h})}\leq C\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\delta_{h}})}\leq C\delta_{h}^{1/2}\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(U_{\delta_{h})}}\leq Ch^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

2.4.
Clough-Tocher Split

To guarantee inf-sup stability of the proposed divergence-free method, we introduce
on each element a local triangulation given by the Clough-Tocher split. Let
\hat{T}^{ct}=\{\hat{K}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
be the Clough-Tocher triangulation of the reference triangle, obtained
by connecting the vertices of
\hat{T}
to its barycenter. We define analogous splits on our affine and curved triangulations via
F_{\tilde{T}}
and
F_{T}

(cf. Figures
1
–
2
):

\tilde{T}^{ct}=\{F_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{K}):\ \hat{K}\in\hat{T}^{ct}\},\quad T^{ct}=\{F_{T}(\hat{K}):\ \hat{K}\in\hat{T}^{ct}\}.

From (
2.1
) and the shape-regularity of
\tilde{T}_{h}
, it follows that
|T|\leq C|K|
for all
K\in T^{ct}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We note that for the
macroelement

T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, only edges containing both vertices on
\partial\Omega_{h}
may be curved. However, it may be that interior edges of the local triangulations
K\in T^{ct}
may indeed be curved as well.

2.5.
The Piola transform

The final piece we need to construct the divergence-free method is the Piola transform.
Given
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the matrix
A_{T}:\hat{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
to be
the matrix arising in this transform

(2.3)

A_{T}(\hat{x}):=\frac{DF_{T}(\hat{x})}{\det(DF_{T}(\hat{x}))}.

In what follows, the local function spaces on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
will be constructed through
A_{T}
. Specifically, given a function
\hat{\bm{v}}:\hat{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, its Piola transform yields
the function
\bm{v}:T\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\bm{v}=(A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}})\circ F_{T}^{-1}
(cf. Section
3
).
It is well-known that this transform is divergence-preserving and normal-continuity preserving,
and its use in the definition of the spaces given
below allows us to maintain these properties of the Scott-Vogelius pair on curved triangulations. We emphasize that this transform
is defined with respect to
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, not with respect to the triangles in the Clough-Tocher split.

2.6.
Bounds and scaling results

The following results give bounds on the matrix
A_{T}
and its
inverse. We refer to the appendix of
[
15
]
for a proof for
the case
k=2
. The arguments given there generalize trivially
for
k\geq 2
, and therefore the proof of the following lemma is omitted.

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, there holds

(2.4)

|A_{T}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}^{m-1}\ (m\geq 0),\qquad|A_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Ch_{T}^{1+m}&0\leq m\leq k-1,\\
0&k\leq m.\end{array}\right.

Additionally, we will make use of the following scaling results from
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\bm{w}\in\bm{W}^{m,p}(T)
with
m\geq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Let
\hat{\bm{w}}\in\bm{W}^{m,p}(\hat{T})
be the image of
\bm{w}
on
\hat{T}

with
\hat{\bm{w}}(\hat{x})=\bm{w}(x)
,
x=F_{T}(\hat{x})
and set
\hat{K}=F_{T}^{-1}(K)

for each
K\in T^{ct}
.
Then for any
K\in T^{ct}
,

(2.5)

\begin{split}|\bm{w}|_{W^{m,p}(K)}&\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p-m}\sum_{r=0}^{m}h_{T}^{2(m-r)}|\hat{\bm{w}}|_{W^{r,p}(\hat{K})},\\
|\hat{\bm{w}}|_{W^{m,p}(\hat{K})}&\leq Ch_{T}^{m-2/p}\sum_{r=0}^{m}|\bm{w}|_{W^{r,p}(K)}.\end{split}

In the results that follow, we let
\bm{n}
denote the outward unit normal of a given domain (understood from context),
and set
\bm{t}
to be the unit tangent vector obtained by rotating
\bm{n}
90 degrees counterclockwise.

3.
Local spaces

The full derivation of the local spaces for the divergence-free isoparametric framework can be found in
[
15
]
for
the case
k=2
. Below, we have the analogous results for general
k
.

To begin, we define the local function space on the reference triangle
\hat{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with Clough-Tocher triangulation
\hat{T}^{ct}=\{\hat{K}_{1},\hat{K}_{2},\hat{K}_{3}\}
. Without including boundary conditions, the polynomial spaces on the reference triangle are

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}=

\displaystyle\{\hat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\hat{T}):\hat{\bm{v}}|_{\hat{K}}\in{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k}(\hat{K})\ \forall\hat{K}\in\hat{T}^{ct}\},

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{k-1}=

\displaystyle\{\hat{q}\in L^{2}(\hat{T}):\hat{q}|_{\hat{K}}\in\EuScript{P}_{k-1}(\hat{K})\ \forall\hat{K}\in\hat{T}^{ct}\},

where
\EuScript{P}_{k}(S)
is the space of scalar polynomials of degree
\leq k
on domain
S
, and
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{k}(S)=[\EuScript{P}_{k}(S)]^{2}
.

With
\tilde{x}=F_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{x})
, we define the local spaces on the affine triangle
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
via composition:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}(\tilde{T})=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\tilde{T}):\tilde{\bm{v}}(\tilde{x})=\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{x}),\ \exists\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k-1}(\tilde{T})=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{q}\in L^{2}(\tilde{T}):\tilde{q}(\tilde{x})=\hat{q}(\hat{x}),\ \exists\hat{q}\in\hat{Q}_{k-1}\}.

To incorporate boundary conditions, we further define

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{V}}_{k,0}=\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}\cap\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\hat{T}),\quad

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{k-1,0}=\hat{Q}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\hat{T}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k,0}(\tilde{T})=\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}(\tilde{T})\cap\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\tilde{T}),\quad

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k-1,0}(\tilde{T})=\tilde{Q}(\tilde{T})\cap L_{0}^{2}(\tilde{T}),

where
L_{0}^{2}(\tilde{T})
is the space of
L^{2}(\tilde{T})
-functions with vanishing mean.

We then define the function spaces on the triangles
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
(which may have a curved edge), using
the notation
x=F_{T}(\hat{x})
and the Piola transform:

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{k}(T)=

\displaystyle\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(T):\bm{v}(x)=A_{T}(\hat{x})\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{x}),\ \exists\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}\},\quad\bm{V}_{k,0}(T)=\bm{V}_{k}(T)\cap\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(T),

\displaystyle Q_{k-1}(T)=

\displaystyle\{q\in L^{2}(T):q(x)=\hat{q}(\hat{x}),\ \exists\hat{q}\in\hat{Q}_{k-1}\},

\displaystyle Q_{k-1,0}(T)=

\displaystyle\{q\in L^{2}(T):q(x)=\hat{q}(\hat{x}),\ \exists\hat{q}\in\hat{Q}_{k-1,0}\},

where the matrix
A_{T}
is given by (
2.3
).
Note that if
F_{T}
is affine, then
\bm{V}(T)=\tilde{\bm{V}}(\tilde{T})
and
Q(T)=\tilde{Q}(\tilde{T})
.

It is important to note that functions in
\bm{V}_{k-1}(T)
and
Q_{k-1}(T)
are not necessarily piecewise-polynomial spaces if
T
is not affine. In addition, on curved triangles the matrix
A_{T}
is not necessarily constant
on straight edges, and therefore functions in
\bm{V}_{k-1}(T)
are not necessarily
polynomials on such edges.
However, the following lemma shows that the the normal component of
\bm{v}
will be a polynomial when restricted to a straight edge.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{k}(T)
, and suppose that
e
is a straight edge of
\partial T
with unit normal
\bm{n}
. Then
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}|_{e}\in\EuScript{P}_{k}(e)
.

The proof of this result is found in
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
for the case
k=2

and essentially uses the well-known normal-preserving properties
of the Piola transform.
However, the result extends trivially to
\hat{\bm{v}}\cdot\hat{\bm{n}}
a polynomial of arbitrary degree
k
.

With the local spaces now defined, we may state the following lemma showing
that functions in the finite element space
\bm{V}_{k}(T)
enjoy
inverse estimates similar to those for piecewise polynomials.
In addition, similar to isoparametric (polynomial) elements
defined via composition, high-order Sobolev norms
of functions in
\bm{V}_{k}(T)
are controlled by
k
th-order Sobolev norms.
Its proof is based on scaling arguments and is found in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
0\leq m\leq\ell
be
integers. Then for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

and
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{k}(T)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(K)}\leq Ch_{T}^{m-\ell+2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{m,q}(K)}\qquad\forall K\in T^{ct}.

Moreover,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(K)}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k,p}(K)}\qquad\forall\ell\geq k,\ \forall K\in T^{ct}.

3.1.
Degrees of Freedom on
\bm{V}(T)

To describe the degrees of freedom
of the local velocity space
\bm{V}(T)
,
we first summarize the canonical
degrees of freedom for the reference space
\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
, i.e.,
the
k
th-degree Lagrange finite element space defined on the
Clough-Tocher split.
It is well known that a function in this space
is uniquely determined by (i) its values at the four vertices in
\hat{T}^{ct}
(4 nodes); (ii)
its values at
(k-1)
distinct points for each of the six (open) edges in
\hat{T}^{ct}
(
6(k-1)
nodes);
and (iii) its values at
\frac{1}{2}(k-1)(k-2)
distinct points for each of the three (open) subtriangles
in
\hat{T}^{ct}
(
\frac{3}{2}(k-1)(k-2)
nodes). In (iii), the
\frac{1}{2}(k-1)(k-2)
points for each subtriangle must be chosen such that they uniquely determine a polynomial of degree
(k-3)
.
We see that the total number of nodes is
M_{k}:=4+6(k-1)+\frac{3}{2}(k-1)(k-2)=\frac{3}{2}k(k+1)+1
,
and therefore the dimension of
\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
is
3k(k+1)+2
.
By setting
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}=\{\hat{a}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M_{k}}
to be
the set of these points, then a function
\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
is uniquely determined
by the values
\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a}_{i})
for all
a_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}
.

To ensure sufficient weak continuity properties
of the global finite element spaces defined below,
we specify that the location of the points
on the three boundary edges of
\hat{T}
correspond
to the nodes of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature scheme.
In particular, for a boundary edge
\hat{e}\subset{\partial}\hat{T}
,
the nodes on the closure of the edge, denoted by
\{\hat{m}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k+1}\subset\bar{\hat{e}}
,
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\hat{\omega}_{i}\hat{q}(\hat{m}_{i})=\int_{\hat{e}}\hat{q}\qquad\forall\hat{q}\in\EuScript{P}_{2k-1}(\hat{e}),

and two of the nodes in this set correspond to the vertices of
\hat{e}
.
The other nodes (i.e., nodes on interior edges and the interior of subtriangles)
can be chosen such that they satisfy the above properties to form a unisolvent set
of degrees of freedom on
\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
. However,
to simplify the implementation of the resulting finite element spaces,
we also take nodes on the interior edges to be the Gauss-Lobttto points,
and let the nodes in the interior of subtriangles to be the canonical
Lagrange nodes (cf. Figures
1
and
2
).

We map these nodes to
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

via the mappings
F_{\tilde{T}}
and
F_{T}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k}(\tilde{T})=\{F_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{a}_{i}):\ \hat{a}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}\},\quad\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)=\{F_{T}(\hat{a}_{i}):\ \hat{a}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}\}.

Due to the invariance of spaces of polynomials under affine transformations,
we see that the nodes in
\mathcal{N}_{k}(\tilde{T})
that lie
on an edge
\tilde{e}\subset\bar{\tilde{T}}
correspond to the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature

is a straight edge of
T
,

\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)
that lie on
\bar{e}

are the nodes of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature rule on
e
.

\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}
,

A_{T}
is invertible, it follows that any
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{k}(T)

determined by the values
\bm{v}(a_{i})
,
a_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)
.

4.
Global Spaces

On the affine triangulation
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define the Scott-Vogelius pair

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h}):\tilde{\bm{v}}|_{\tilde{T}}\in\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}(\tilde{T})\ \forall\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k-1}^{h}=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{q}\in L_{0}^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h}):\tilde{q}|_{\tilde{T}}\in\tilde{Q}_{k-1}(\tilde{T})\ \forall\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

We see that
\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}
is the
k
th degree Lagrange finite element space
with respect to the Clough-Tocher refinement of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and
\tilde{Q}_{k-1}^{h}
is
the space of discontinuous polynomials of degree
(k-1)
,
again with respect to the Clough-Tocher refinement.
The finite elements
\tilde{\bm{V}}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}
represents
a stable and divergence–free Stokes pair
[
1
]
, however its use formally leads
to a suboptimal scheme on smooth domains due to geometric error.

To define the isoparametric spaces, we define the operators
{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}
and
\Upsilon_{k-1}
such that

{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}|_{T}:\tilde{\bm{V}}(\tilde{T})\to\bm{V}(T)
and
\Upsilon_{k-1}|_{T}:\tilde{Q}(\tilde{T})\to Q(T)

and are uniquely determined on each
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
T=G_{h}(\tilde{T})
as follows:

1.

({\bm{\Psi}}_{k}|_{T}\tilde{\bm{v}})(a)=\tilde{\bm{v}}(\tilde{a})

\forall\tilde{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(\tilde{T})
, with
a=G_{h}(\tilde{a})\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)
, and

2.

(\Upsilon_{k-1}|_{T}\tilde{q})=\tilde{q}\circ G_{h}^{-1}

Thus,
{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}
maps functions in
\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}
to the isoparametric
domain
\Omega_{h}
via the Piola transform and interpolation, and

\Upsilon_{k-1}
maps functions in
\tilde{Q}_{k-1}^{h}
to
\Omega_{h}
via composition.

In the following proposition, we state some properties
of the mapping
{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}
without proof, as the result is proven for
k=2
in
[
15
, Theorem 3.7]
.
To extend the results to arbitrary degree
k
, one only needs to recognize
that a
k
-th degree polynomial along an edge
e\subset\partial T
is uniquely determined
by its values at the
k+1
nodal points that lie on this edge.

Proposition 4.1
.

The following properties are satisfied:

1.

If
F_{T}
is affine, then
{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}|_{T}
is the identity operator.

2.

If
e\subset\partial T
is a straight edge (so
e\subset\partial\tilde{T}
with
T=G_{h}(\tilde{T})
), then

({\bm{\Psi}}_{k}|_{T}\tilde{\bm{v}})\cdot\bm{n}|_{e}=\tilde{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}|_{e}\qquad\forall\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}.

3.

There holds
\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}|_{T}\tilde{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(T)}\leq C\|\tilde{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\tilde{T})}
for all
\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}
.

Consequently, global function spaces defined on the isoparametric mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
are given by

\bm{V}_{k}^{h}:=\{\bm{v}:\bm{v}={\bm{\Psi}}_{k}\tilde{\bm{v}},\ \exists\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}\},\quad Q_{k}^{h}:=\{q:q=\Upsilon_{k-1}\tilde{q},\ \exists\tilde{q}\in\tilde{Q}_{k}^{h}\}.

Remark 4.2
.

From the boundary conditions applied to the space
\tilde{\bm{V}}_{k}^{h}
and the definition of
{\bm{\Psi}}_{k}
, we see
that functions in
\bm{V}_{k}^{h}
are continuous at the degrees of freedom, and vanish on
\partial\Omega_{h}
.

With these spaces defined, we have the following results.

Lemma 4.3
.

There holds
\bm{V}_{k}^{h}\subset\bm{H}_{0}({\rm{div}};\Omega_{h})=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{h}):\ {\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{h}),\ \bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\partial\Omega_{h}}=0\}
.

This result follows immediately from the construction of
\bm{V}_{k}^{h}
and the continuity of the normal component on interior edges imposed by part 2 of Proposition
4.1
.
See
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
for details.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists an operator
\bm{I}_{k}^{h}:\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})\to\bm{V}_{k}^{h}

such that for
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega_{h})\cap\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})\ (s\geq 2)
and for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
there holds

(4.1)

\|\bm{u}-\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}\|_{H^{m}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{\ell-m}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(T)}\quad 0\leq m\leq\ell:=\min\{k+1,s\}.

Proof.

Recall
\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)
and
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}

are the sets of nodes on
T
and
\hat{T}
, respectively. We uniquely define the operator
\bm{I}_{k}^{h}
such that on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

(\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u})|_{T}(a)=\bm{u}(a)\quad\forall a\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(T).

Set
\bm{v}=\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}|_{T}\in\bm{V}_{k}(T)
, for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then set
\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
and
\hat{\bm{u}}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\hat{T})
such that

\bm{v}(x)=(A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}})(\hat{x}),\quad\bm{u}(x)=(A_{T}\hat{\bm{u}})(\hat{x}).

Consequently,

(A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}})(\hat{a})=(A_{T}\hat{\bm{u}})(\hat{a})\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a})=\hat{\bm{u}}(\hat{a})\quad\forall\hat{a}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}

because the matrix
A_{T}
is invertible.
Thus,
\hat{\bm{v}}
is the
k
th degree nodal interpolant of
\hat{\bm{u}}
with respect to
\hat{T}^{ct}
,
so, by standard interpolation theory, we have

(4.2)

\|\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{m}(\hat{T})}\leq C|\hat{\bm{u}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{T})}\quad 0\leq m\leq\ell=\min\{k+1,s\}.

Thus it follows from (
4.2
), Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, and an application of the product rule that

\displaystyle|\bm{u}-\bm{v}|_{H^{m}(T)}\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{1-m}\|A_{T}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\|\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{m}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}^{-m}|\hat{\bm{u}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{T})},

and so, by using Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
once again,

\displaystyle|\bm{u}-\bm{v}|_{H^{m}(T)}\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{-m}|A_{T}^{-1}A_{T}\hat{\bm{u}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}|A_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{j,\infty}(\hat{T})}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{u}}|_{H^{\ell-j}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}h_{T}^{1+j}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{u}}|_{H^{\ell-j}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{\ell-m}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(T)}.

∎

4.1.
Weak continuity properties

The next result shows
that, while functions in
\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
are only

\bm{H}_{0}({\rm div};\Omega_{h})
-conforming,
they do have weak continuity properties
across interior edges of the mesh.
In particular, they are “close” to an
\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})
-conforming relative. The lemma
is a generalization of
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]

to general polynomial degree
and to higher-order Sobolev norms;
its proof is given in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists an operator
\bm{E}_{h}:\bm{V}^{h}_{k}\to\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
such that for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}

(4.3)

\|\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{T}\|\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{m+1}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}(T)}\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

for
m=0,1,\ldots,k
. Moreover,
\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{T}=\bm{v}

if
T
is affine.

Corollary 4.6
.

For
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}
, it holds

\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},

where
\nabla_{h}
denotes the piecewise gradient operator
with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
C>0
is a constant depending only on the size of
\Omega_{h}

and the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\bm{v}=\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}
on affine triangles, so
\|\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
may only be
nonzero on curved
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, all of which will have at least two vertices on the boundary. We denote the set of triangles with two boundary vertices as
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}
so that

\bm{v}|_{T}=\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{T}
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}
.
Because
\bm{v}|_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}=0
, we have
\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}
.

Thus, recalling that
\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
, we may apply the triangle inequality, Lemma
4.5
(twice with
m=0
), and the Poincaré inequality (twice) to determine

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\|\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}}\|\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}}h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}}\|\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

∎

Using the
H^{1}
-conforming relative in Lemma
4.5

and the fact that the Lagrange DOFs
are Gauss-Lobatto nodes,
we show that functions in
\bm{V}_{k}^{h}

possess weak continuity properties across interior edges.
To describe the result, we set
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}
to denote
the set of interior edges of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and define the jump
of a vector-valued function across an edge
e={\partial}T_{+}\cap{\partial}T_{-}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}\ (T_{\pm}\in\mathcal{T}_{h})
as

[\bm{v}]|_{e}=\bm{v}_{+}\otimes\bm{n}_{+}|_{e}+\bm{v}_{-}\otimes\bm{n}_{-}|_{e},

where
\bm{v}_{\pm}=\bm{v}|_{T_{\pm}}
and
\bm{n}_{\pm}
is the outward unit normal
of
{\partial}T_{\pm}
restricted to
e
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\bm{w}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega)
with
s\geq 2
,
and set
r=\min\{s-1,k-1\}
. We extend

\bm{w}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(\Omega)}
.
Then there holds for all

\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
, and
m=0,1,\ldots,k
,

(4.4)

\bigg|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{w}:[\bm{v}]\bigg|\leq Ch^{r+m}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(\Omega)}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}.\quad

Proof.

For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}
, let
T_{+},T_{-}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

such that
e={\partial}T_{+}\cap{\partial}T_{-}
.
We let
G_{e}\in[H^{1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})]^{2\times 2}

such that
G_{e}|_{T_{\pm}}\circ F_{T_{\pm}}\in[\EuScript{P}_{k-2}(\hat{T})]^{2\times 2}
and

\int_{T_{+}\cup T_{-}}G_{e}:Q=\int_{T_{+}\cup T_{-}}\nabla\bm{w}:Q

for all
Q\in[H^{1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})]^{2\times 2}

with
Q|_{T_{\pm}}\circ F_{T_{\pm}}\in[\EuScript{P}_{k-2}(\hat{T})]^{2\times 2}
.
That is
G_{e}
is the
L^{2}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})
projection of
\nabla\bm{w}

with respect to the local
(k-2)
-degree Lagrange (isoparametric) finite element space.
Note that because
F_{T_{\pm}}
is affine on the interior edge
e
,
there holds
G_{e}|_{e}\in[\EuScript{P}_{k-2}(e)]^{2\times 2}
.
We also have by standard approximation theory,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{w}-G_{e}\|_{H^{m}(T_{\pm})}\leq Ch^{r-m}_{T}\|\nabla\bm{w}\|_{H^{r}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}\leq Ch^{r-m}_{T}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}\quad m=0,1,\ldots,r,

where
h_{T}=\max\{h_{T_{+}},h_{T_{-}}\}
.
Thus, by a trace inequality,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{w}-G_{e}\|_{L^{2}(e)}\leq Ch_{T}^{r-1/2}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}.

We then write

(4.7)

\begin{split}\bigg|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{w}:[\bm{v}]\bigg|&\leq\bigg|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}(\nabla\bm{w}-G_{e}):[\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}]\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}G_{e}:[\bm{v}]\bigg|\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

To estimate
I_{1}
, we use (
4.6
),
Lemma
4.5
, and a trace inequality:

(4.8)

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}h_{e}\|\nabla\bm{w}-G_{e}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2r}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2m}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}(T)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Ch^{r+m}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(\Omega)}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}.\end{split}

To estimate
I_{2}
, we first observe that,
by construction, for
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
and
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}
, we have
[\bm{v}]|_{e}(a)=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)
with
a\in\bar{e}
.
Recalling that these edge degrees of freedom are placed
at Gauss-Lobatto nodes,
it follows from the error of the
(k+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto rule and the fact that
G_{e}
is a polynomial of degree

(k-2)
on
e
that

(4.9)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{e}[\bm{v}]:G_{e}\right|&\leq C|e|^{2k+1}\left|[\bm{v}]:G_{e}\right|_{W^{2k,\infty}(e)}\\
&\leq Ch_{T}^{2k+1}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{2k,\infty}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{2k,\infty}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I},\end{split}

where
K_{\pm}\in T_{\pm}^{ct}
share edge
e
.

Note that a standard inverse/trace estimate yields

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|G_{e}\|_{H^{k-2}(T_{\pm})}.

From here, we consider two cases:

Case 1:
k-2\leq r
. For this case, we recall that
r=\min\{s-1,k-1\}
, and so
r-k\leq-1
. It therefore holds that we have
h^{-1}_{T}\leq h^{r-k}_{T}
. With this, (
4.5
),
and
(k-1)\leq(r+1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{-1}\big(\|G_{e}-\nabla\bm{w}\|_{H^{k-2}(T_{\pm})}+\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{k-1}(T_{\pm})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{r-k}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}.

Case 2:
r\leq k-2
. For the second case, we may apply another inverse estimate to (
4.10
) before applying (
4.5
). This yields

\displaystyle\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{-1}h_{T_{\pm}}^{r-(k-2)}\|G_{e}\|_{H^{r}(T_{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{r-k+1}\big(\|G_{e}-\nabla\bm{w}\|_{H^{r}(T_{\pm})}+\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{\pm})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch_{T}^{r-k+1}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}.

Consequently, we take the less sharp estimate in these cases in (
4.9
)
and apply the inverse estimates (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) to
\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{2k,\infty}(K_{\pm})}

to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{e}[\bm{v}]:G_{e}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{k+r}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2k}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{2k}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{k+r}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{k}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{k}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{r+m}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(T_{+}\cup T_{-})}.

Summing this expression over
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}

we obtain an upper bound for
I_{2}
:

(4.11)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq Ch^{r+m}\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{r+1}(\Omega)}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{m}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}.

Applying the estimates (
4.8
) and (
4.11
)
towards (
4.7
) yields the result.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

We note that the result above is not as sharp if Newton-Cotes (uniformly spaced) nodes are used instead of Gauss-Lobatto nodes. Indeed, Newton-Cotes integration on
m
points is exact on polynomials in
\EuScript{P}^{m}
, if
m
is odd, and
\EuScript{P}^{m-1}
if
m
is even, so the bound on the right-hand side of (
4.9
) becomes

Ch_{T}^{k+2}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k+1,\infty}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k+1,\infty}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I},

if
k
is odd, and

Ch_{T}^{k+3}\bigg(\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k+2,\infty}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k+2,\infty}(K_{-})}\bigg)\|G_{e}\|_{W^{k-2,\infty}(e)}\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I},

if
k
is even. Thus, if we use equidistant points, the bound loses
k-1
powers of
h
if
k
is odd, and
k-2
if
k
is even.

4.2.
Inf-sup stability

An inf-sup stability result for the finite element pair
\bm{V}_{k}^{h}\times Q_{k}^{h}
was proven in
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
in the case
k=2
.
The argument given there are essentially valid for all
k\geq 2
. Consequently, we only provide a sketch
of the proof in the general case.

Theorem 4.9
.

There holds

(4.12)

\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})q}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\geq C\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\quad\forall q\in Q^{h}_{k-1}.

Sketch of proof for Theorem
4.9

Fix
q\in Q_{k-1}^{h}
, and set
\bar{q}\in Q_{k-1}^{h}
to be piecewise constant with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}

satisfying
\int_{T}(q-\bar{q})/\det(DF_{T}\circ F_{T}^{-1})=0
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
By a change of variables, we see that
(q-\bar{q})\circ F_{T}\in\hat{Q}_{k-1,0}
.

Next, the results in, e.g.,
[
9
]
show that

\widehat{\rm div}:\hat{\bm{V}}_{k,0}\to\hat{Q}_{k-1,0}
is surjective with bounded
right inverse.
Consequently, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, there exists
\hat{\bm{v}}_{1,T}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k,0}

such that
\widehat{\rm div}\,\hat{\bm{v}}_{1,T}=h_{T}^{2}(q-\bar{q})|_{T}\circ F_{T}
.
and
\|\hat{\bm{v}}_{1,T}\|_{H^{1}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}^{2}\|(q-\bar{q})|_{T}\circ F_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
.
Setting
\bm{v}_{1,T}=(A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}})\circ F_{T}^{-1}\in\bm{V}_{k,0}
,
we have
{\rm div}\,\bm{v}_{1,T}=h^{2}_{T}(q-\bar{q})/(\det(DF_{T}\circ F_{T}^{-1}))
by the divergence-preserving
properties of the Piola transform, and
\|\nabla\bm{v}_{1,T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq C\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
by a scaling argument.

We then define
\bm{v}_{1}\in\bm{V}_{k}^{h}
such that
\bm{v}_{1}|_{T}=\bm{v}_{1,T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Thus
\|\bm{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}
,
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{k}^{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}\bm{v})q}{\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}\bm{v}_{1})q}{\|\nabla\bm{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}=\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}\bm{v}_{1})(q-\bar{q})}{\|\nabla\bm{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\int_{T}|q-\bar{q}|^{2}/(\det(DF_{T}\circ F_{T}^{-1}))}{\|\nabla\bm{v}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{0}\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Next, Theorem 4.4 in
[
15
]
shows
that

\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{k}^{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,\bm{v})\bar{q}}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\geq\gamma_{1}\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\gamma_{0}^{-1}+\gamma_{1}^{-1}(1+\gamma_{0}^{-1}))\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}\bm{v})q}{\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}.

5.
The Stokes System and Finite Element Method

We let
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
be the solution to the Stokes problem

(5.1)

\begin{cases}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p={\bm{f}}\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{u}}=0\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\bm{u}=0\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\nu>0
is the viscosity. We assume
the domain
\Omega
and source function
{\bm{f}}
are sufficiently
smooth such that
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s-1}(\Omega)

with
s\geq 2
. We then extend the velocity solution
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the extension (still denoted by
\bm{u}
)
is divergence-free and satisfies
[
11
]

(5.2)

\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

Likewise, we extend the pressure solution
p
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\|p\|_{H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|p\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}

and extend the source function by setting
{\bm{f}}=-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We define the continuous bilinear forms

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla\bm{v},\qquad b(\bm{v},p):=-\int_{\Omega}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})p,

and the discrete bilinear forms

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nu\nabla_{h}\bm{u}_{h}:\nabla_{h}\bm{v},\qquad b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h}):=-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})p_{h}.

Clearly, the solution to (
5.1
) solves the variational problem

(5.3)

a(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\int_{\Omega}{\bm{f}}\cdot\bm{v}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}:=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\ {\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}}=0\}.

We define the finite element method as finding
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}\times Q^{h}_{k-1}
such that

(5.4a)

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v})+b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h})=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}\quad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k},

(5.4b)

\displaystyle-b_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},q)=

\displaystyle 0\quad

\displaystyle\forall q\in Q^{h}_{k},

where
{\bm{f}}_{h}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{h})
is a suitable (and computable) approximation to
{\bm{f}}|_{\Omega}
.
It follows from the inf-sup condition in Theorem
4.9

and the Poincare inequality in Corollary
4.6

that there exists a unique solution to (
5.4
).
Moreover, by a simple generalization of
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
,
the method (
5.4
) yields divergence-free velocity approximations.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
satisfy (
5.4b
).
Then
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}\bm{u}_{h}=0
in
\Omega_{h}
.

5.1.
Energy estimates

In this section, we derive error estimates
for the approximation velocity and pressure solutions
in the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms, respectively.
To this end, we define the discrete space
of divergence-free functions

{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}:=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}:{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}}=0\}\nsubseteq{\bm{X}}:=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega):{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}}=0\},

and note that functions in this space are not necessarily in
\bm{H}_{0}^{1}
.
Lemma
5.1
shows that
\bm{u}_{h}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}^{k}
,
and thus the velocity solution
\bm{u}_{h}
is uniquely characterized
as the solution of the Poisson-type problem

(5.5)

a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v})=\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s-1}(\Omega)
satisfy (
5.1
) with
s\geq 2
.
Then there holds

(5.6)

\|\nabla_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\left(h^{\ell-1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\right),

where
\ell=\min\{k+1,s\}
, and

|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}_{k}^{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h})\cdot\bm{v}}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}.

The pressure approximation,
p_{h}
, satisfies

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(h^{\ell-1}(\nu\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{H^{\ell-1}(\Omega)})+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\right).

Proof.

From standard theory of non-conforming finite elements (see, for example,
[
4
]
)
and the inf-sup condition (
4.12
),

(5.8)

\begin{split}\nu\|\nabla_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq&\inf_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}}\nu\|\nabla_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\\
\leq&C\inf_{\bm{w}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}}\nu\|\nabla_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\\
\leq&Ch^{\ell-1}\nu\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})}{\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}},\end{split}

where the final step follows from Lemma
4.4
.

To address the consistency term, we note that we have
\forall\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}

(5.9)

\begin{split}a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})=&\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot\bm{v}-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})+\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}\\
=&-\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot\bm{v}-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})+\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}.\end{split}

Note that the last step uses the fact that
\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}
, therefore
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}}=0
and
\bm{v}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{h}
.

We then apply a standard integration-by-parts formula in (
5.9
),
Lemma
4.7
(with
m=1
, and noting
r=\min\{s-1,k-1\}\leq\ell-1
) and Corollary
4.6

to obtain

(5.10)

\begin{split}a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})=&-\nu\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{u}:[\bm{v}]+\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}\\
\leq&C\nu h^{\ell-1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|{\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}}\|_{X_{k}^{*}}\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.\end{split}

Finally, to complete the velocity bound (
5.6
), we apply this estimate to (
5.8
).

To prove the pressure bound (
5.7
), we fix
q\in Q^{h}_{k-1}
.
For any
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
, we then have the following identity, using integration by parts and (
5.1
):

(5.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})(p_{h}-q)=&a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v})-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})q-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot\bm{v}\\
=&a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v})+\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot\bm{v}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla p\cdot\bm{v}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})q-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}\\
=&a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{v})-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})(q-p)-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}_{h}-{\bm{f}})\cdot\bm{v}+\nu\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{u}:[\bm{v}].\end{split}

Then, applying (
4.4
) to (
5.11
) and Corollary
4.6
, we have

(5.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})(p_{h}-q)\leq&C\bigg(\nu\|\nabla_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|q-p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\\
&\quad+\nu h^{\ell-1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega_{h})}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\bigg)\|\nabla_{h}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.\end{split}

Finally, by triangle inequality and Theorem
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\|p-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|p_{h}-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|p-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{v}})(p_{h}-q)}{\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}.

Applying (
5.12
) to this result, taking the infimum over
q\in Q^{h}_{k-1}
, and using (
5.6
) completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume the conditions in Theorem
5.2
are satisfied,
and in addition, assume the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform.
Then the solution
\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}
to (
5.4
) satisfies

\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{\ell}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\bigg(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+h^{1-\ell}\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\bigg).

Proof.

Define
\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{k}^{h}
to be the approximation to
\bm{u}

given in Lemma
4.4
.
Then, applying the inverse inequality (
3.1
), Lemma
4.4
, and Theorem
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{\ell}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|\bm{u}-\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+h^{1-\ell}\|\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|\bm{u}-\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+h^{1-\ell}\|\bm{I}_{k}^{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+h^{1-\ell}\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+h^{1-\ell}\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\big).

∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
{\bm{f}}
is sufficiently smooth, and
{\bm{f}}_{h}
is, for example, the
k
th degree nodal (isoparametric) interpolant, then

|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\leq\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h})}
.
Thus, Corollary
5.3
yields

(5.13)

\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{\ell}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\left(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+h^{k-\ell+2}\nu^{-1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h})}\right).

6.
Convergence analysis in
L^{2}

In this section, we prove the following optimal-order

L^{2}
error estimate.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume the conditions in Theorem
5.2
are satisfied,
and in addition, assume the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform.
We have

(6.1)

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C\bigg(h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+(\nu^{-1}h+1)|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}^{*}_{h}}\bigg),

where
C
is a constant that does not depend on the mesh parameter
h
,
and we recall
\ell=\min\{s,k+1\}
.

Proof.

To derive (
6.1
), we first write

(6.2)

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\cap\Omega)}=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

To bound
J_{1}
, we introduce
\bm{E}_{h}:\bm{V}^{h}_{k}\to\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
as defined in Lemma
4.5
. Consequently, we may write

J_{1}\leq\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+\|\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}.

A bound of the second term in this sum follows from Lemma
4.5
and Corollary
5.3
:

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+\|\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+Ch^{\ell}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{\ell-1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+C\bigg(h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega_{h})}+h\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{h}^{*}}\bigg).

To bound the remaining term, begin with Hölder’s inequality and recall that
H^{1}
embeds in
L^{6}
and
k\geq 2
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega|^{1/3}\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{(k+1)/3}\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h})}.

It follows from Theorem
5.2
, Lemma
4.5
, and Corollary
5.3
that

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle Ch\bigg(\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+{h}\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\bigg).

Combining this with the result above, we have

(6.3)

J_{1}\leq C\bigg(h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+h\nu^{-1}|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{k}^{*}}\bigg).

To bound
J_{2}
, we let
\bm{\phi}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega\cup\Omega_{h})

such that
\bm{\phi}|_{\Omega\cap\Omega_{h}}=(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})|_{\Omega\cap\Omega_{h}}

and
\bm{\phi}|_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{h}\backslash(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}=0
.
We then define
(\bm{\psi},r)\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
to be the solution to the auxiliary problem

(6.4)

\begin{cases}-\nu\Delta\bm{\psi}+\nabla r=\bm{\phi}\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
{\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}}{\bm{\psi}}=0\quad&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Because
{\partial}\Omega
is smooth and
\bm{\phi}|_{\Omega}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
there holds
\bm{\psi}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
with
\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=C\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}

by elliptic regularity theory.
Similar to the solution
\bm{u}
in (
5.2
), we extend
\bm{\psi}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in such
a way that preserves the divergence–free condition
(cf.
[
11
]
) and

(6.5)

\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}.

Finally, we define
\bm{\psi}_{h}\in{\bm{X}}^{h}_{k}
to be the approximation of
\bm{\psi}
on
\Omega_{h}
satisfying

(6.6)

a_{h}(\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{v})=\int_{\Omega_{h}}\bm{\phi}\cdot\bm{v}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}_{k}.

We note that
\bm{\psi}
and
\bm{\psi}_{h}
are analogous to
\bm{u}
and
\bm{u}_{h}
, respectively, in Theorem
5.2
when
s=2
(so that
\ell=2
), with
\bm{\phi}
replacing both
{\bm{f}}
and
{\bm{f}}_{h}
. Therefore, the following estimate holds:

(6.7)

\|\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}.

Additionally, applying Corollary
5.3
yields

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{H^{2}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}.

Next, we write

(6.9)

\begin{split}(J_{2})^{2}=\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}^{2}=&\int_{\Omega\cap\Omega_{h}}\bm{\phi}\cdot(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\bm{\phi}\cdot\bm{u}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}\bm{\phi}\cdot\bm{u}_{h}\\
=&a(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\\
=&[a(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})]+a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}).\end{split}

We now consider the terms of (
6.9
) separately.

Bound of
[a(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})]

To bound the first terms of (
6.9
), we begin with

\displaystyle|a(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})|=

\displaystyle\nu\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla\bm{\psi}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla\bm{\psi}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left|\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h}}\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla\bm{\psi}-\int_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla\bm{\psi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h})}\|\nabla\bm{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h})}.

The result of Lemma
2.1
implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\Delta\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{(k+1)/2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\|\nabla\bm{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\Delta\Omega)}\leq Ch^{(k+1)/2}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

from which we get

(6.10)

\displaystyle|a(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})|\leq Ch^{k+1}\nu\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Bound of
a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})

It now remains to bound the last two terms in (
6.9
). To begin, we write

(6.11)

\begin{split}a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})=&a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\\
\leq&\nu\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h})}\|\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h})}+a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\\
\leq&C\bigg(\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+h|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}^{*}_{h}}\bigg)\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}\\
&+a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\end{split}

by Theorem
5.2
and (
6.7
).

Recalling (
5.10
), we have
by Lemma
4.7
(with
m=2
, and noting
\ell=\min\{s,k+1\}\leq\min\{s-1,k-1\}+2=r+2
)

(6.12)

\begin{split}a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})=&-\nu\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{u}:[\bm{\psi}_{h}]+\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h})\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}\\
\leq&C\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{H^{2}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}+|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{X_{h}^{*}}\|\nabla\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\\
\leq&C\left(\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{X_{h}^{*}}\right)\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}.\end{split}

By an analogous argument, but with
s=2
and
m=k
in Lemma
4.7
(so that
r=1
) , we have

(6.13)

\begin{split}a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})=&-\nu\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}}\int_{e}\nabla\bm{\psi}:[\bm{u}_{h}]\\
\leq&C\nu h^{k+1}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{k}_{h}(\Omega_{h})}\\
\leq&C\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Combining (
6.11
)–(
6.13
) yields

(6.14)

a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{h})+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\leq C\left(\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{h}^{*}}\right)\|\bm{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{h})},

and so applying this estimate and (
6.10
) to (
6.9
) (recalling that
\bm{\phi}=\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}
on
\Omega\cap\Omega_{h}
),
we obtain

(6.15)

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq C\left(\nu h^{\ell}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}+|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{f}}_{h}|_{{\bm{X}}_{h}^{*}}\right).

Finally, applying (
6.3
) and (
6.15
) to (
6.2
)
completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
3.2

Proof.

Write
\bm{v}(x)=A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{x})

for some
\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{k}
.
We then use Lemma
2.3
, (
2.5
),
and equivalence of norms
to obtain

(A.1)

\begin{split}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(K)}&\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p-\ell}\|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(\hat{K})}\\
&\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p-\ell}\|A_{T}\|_{W^{j,\infty}(\hat{K})}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(\hat{K})}\\
&\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p-\ell-1}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{\ell,p}(\hat{K})}\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p-\ell-1}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(\hat{K})}.\end{split}

Likewise, we have

(A.2)

\begin{split}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(\hat{K})}\leq\|A_{T}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{K})}\|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(\hat{K})}\leq Ch_{T}^{1-2/q}\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{q}(K)}.\end{split}

Combining (
A.1
)–(
A.2
)
yields (
3.1
) for the case
m=0
.
The estimate (
3.1
) for general
m

then follows by standard arguments
(cf.
[
4
, Lemma 4.5.3]
).

To prove (
3.2
), we first use (
2.5
):

\displaystyle|\bm{v}|_{W^{\ell,p}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[\underbrace{h_{T}^{2/p+\ell}\sum_{r=0}^{k}h_{T}^{-2r}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{r,p}(\hat{K})}}_{=:I}+\underbrace{h_{T}^{2/p+\ell}\sum_{r=k+1}^{\ell}h_{T}^{-2r}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{r,p}(\hat{K})}}_{=:II}\Big].

To bound
I
, we use (
2.5
) once again to obtain

\displaystyle I\leq h_{T}^{2/p+\ell}\sum_{r=0}^{k}h_{T}^{-2r}\cdot h_{T}^{r-2/p}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{r,p}(K)}\leq Ch_{T}^{\ell-k}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k,p}(K)}.

For
II
, we use the fact that
\hat{\bm{v}}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq k
on
K
to obtain

\displaystyle|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{r,p}(\hat{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}|A_{T}|_{W^{r-j,\infty}(\hat{K})}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{j,p}(\hat{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}h_{T}^{r-j-1}|A_{T}^{-1}A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{j,p}(\hat{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{T}^{r-j-1}|A_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{j-i,\infty}(\hat{K})}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{i,p}(\hat{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{T}^{r-i}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{i,p}(\hat{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{T}^{r-i}\cdot h_{T}^{i-2/p}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{i,p}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{r-2/p}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k,p}(K)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle II\leq Ch_{T}^{2/p+\ell}\sum_{r=k+1}^{\ell}h_{T}^{-r-2/p}\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k,p}(K)}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{k,p}(K)}.

Combining the bounds for
I
and
II
completes the proof of (
3.2
).
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
4.5

Proof.

Define
\bm{E}_{h}:\bm{V}^{h}\to\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{h})

such that, for
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{h}
,

\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{T}=(\tilde{\bm{v}}\circ F_{\tilde{T}}\circ F_{T}^{-1})|_{T},

where
\tilde{\bm{v}}
is the function in
\tilde{\bm{V}}
uniquely defined by

\bm{v}|_{T}(a)=\tilde{\bm{v}}|_{\tilde{T}}(\tilde{a})\quad\forall{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{T},\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

where
T=G_{h}(\tilde{T})
. In other words, in a
standard
isoparametric,
k
th degree Lagrange
finite element method,
\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}
would be the function on the isoparametric element
associated with
\tilde{\bm{v}}
on
\tilde{T}
. Thus,
\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
.

As shown in
[
15
]
,
\tilde{\bm{v}}=\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}
on affine triangles, and we may conclude

\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{T}(a)=\bm{v}|_{T}(a)\quad\forall{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{T},\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

Our goal is to estimate
\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}
, and our proof follows closely with the proof of Lemma 4.5 in
[
15
]
. However, here we provide a more general result.

As
\bm{v}=\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}
on affine triangles, we only consider
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with curved boundaries. Additionally, we know
\bm{v}|_{\partial T\cap\partial\Omega_{h}}=0
. We may write
\bm{v}|_{T}(x)=A_{T}(\hat{x})\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{x})
, for some
\hat{\bm{v}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}
, where
A_{T}=DF_{T}/\det{(DF_{T})}
. Furthermore, there exists
\hat{\bm{w}}\in\hat{\bm{V}}
such that
\hat{\bm{w}}(\hat{x})=\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{T}(x)
. Consequently,

A_{T}(\hat{a})\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a})=\hat{\bm{w}}(\hat{a})\quad\forall\hat{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{\hat{T}},

so
\hat{\bm{w}}
is the piecewise
k
th degree Lagrange interpolant of
A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}
on
\hat{T}^{CT}
.

By the Bramble-Hilbert lemma, we have

(B.1)

\|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{i}(\hat{K})}\leq C|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{k+1}(\hat{K})}\quad\forall\hat{K}\in\hat{T}^{CT},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,k.

We may then bound the right-hand side using Lemma
2.3
and recognizing that
\hat{\bm{v}}
is a polynomial of degree
k
. Thus we have

(B.2)

\begin{split}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{k+1}(\hat{K})}\leq&C\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}|A_{T}|_{W^{k+1-j}(\hat{K})}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{j}(\hat{K})}=C\sum_{j=0}^{k}|A_{T}|_{W^{k+1-j}(\hat{K})}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{j}(\hat{K})}\\
\leq&C\sum_{j=0}^{k}h_{T}^{k-j}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{j}(\hat{K})}.\end{split}

Using Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we have

(B.3)

\begin{split}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{j}(\hat{K})}&=|A_{T}^{-1}A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{j}(\hat{K})}\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}|A_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{j-\ell}(\hat{K})}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{K})}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}h_{T}^{1+j-\ell}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{K})}\\
&\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}h_{T}^{1+j-\ell}h_{T}^{\ell-1}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{\ell}(K)}\leq Ch_{T}^{j}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{j}(K)}.\end{split}

Inserting this estimate into (
B.2
) yields

|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{k+1}(\hat{K})}\leq Ch_{T}^{k}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{k}(K)},

and therefore by (
B.1
) and Lemma
2.4
,

(B.4)

\displaystyle|\bm{v}-\bm{E}_{h}\bm{v}|_{H^{i}(K)}\leq Ch_{T}^{1-i}\|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{i}(\hat{K})}\leq Ch_{T}^{1-i}|A_{T}\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{k+1}(\hat{K})}\leq Ch_{T}^{k+1-i}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{k}(K)}.

An applicaiton of the inverse inequality (
3.1
) then yields the desired
estimate (
4.3
).
∎

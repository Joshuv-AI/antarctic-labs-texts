Correlators of double scaled SYK at one-loop
Kazumi Okuyama, and Kenta Suzuki

Abstract
In this paper, we study one-loop contributions in the double-scaling limit of the SYK model from the chord diagrams and Liouville type effective action. We compute and clarify the meaning of each component consisting of the one-loop corrections for the two- and time-ordered four-point functions of light operators. We also reproduce the exact expression of the out-of-time-ordered four-point function at arbitrary temperatures within the one-loop level, which were previously computed from different methods.

2
Review of double scaled SYK

In this section, we give a brief review of the double-scaled SYK model.
The Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model
Sachdev1993
;
Kitaev1
;
Kitaev2
is a quantum mechanical many body system with all-to-all
p
-body interactions on fermionic
N
sites (
N\gg 1
), represented by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H\,=\,\mathrm{i}^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq N}J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{p}}\,\psi_{i_{1}}\,\psi_{i_{2}}\,\cdots\psi_{i_{p}}\,,

(1)

where
\psi_{i}
are Majorana fermions, which satisfy
\{\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}
.
The coupling constant
J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{p}}
is random with a Gaussian distribution

\displaystyle\qquad\big\langle J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{p}}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,0\,,\qquad\big\langle J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{p}}^{2}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\binom{N}{p}^{-1}\quad(\textrm{no\ sum\ for\ indices})\,.

(2)

The double-scaled SYK (DSSYK) model is defined by taking the double scaling limit

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad N,\,p\,\to\,\infty\quad\textrm{with}\quad\lambda\,:=\,\frac{2p^{2}}{N}\quad\textrm{fixed}\,.

(3)

There are two ways to study the DSSYK.
The one is by the chord diagrams
Berkooz:2018qkz
;
Berkooz:2018jqr
which leads to complicated but exact results.
The other is by the
G\Sigma
formalism
Cotler:2016fpe
which leads to a Liouville type action well suited for small
\lambda
perturbations.
In this paper, we study both formalisms.

Figure 1
:
An example of a chord diagram for
k=8
. The black circle represents a trace over the fermions.
Each orange dot represents an insertion of a Hamiltonian and each blue line denotes a contraction of capital index
I
.

The chord diagram method consider, for example, the disorder averaged partition function (but the same method also works for correlation functions as well)

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,:=\,\big\langle\Tr e^{-\beta H}\big\rangle_{J}\,,

(4)

and expands
e^{-\beta H}
to rewrite in terms of summation over the moments
m_{k}

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\beta)^{k}}{k!}\,m_{k}\,,\qquad m_{k}\,:=\,\big\langle\Tr\big(H^{k}\big)\big\rangle_{J}\,.

(5)

The evaluation of the moments is reduced to a product between a trace over fermions and disorder average over the coupling constants as

\displaystyle m_{k}\,=\,\mathrm{i}^{\frac{kp}{2}}\,\big\langle J_{I_{1}}\cdots J_{I_{k}}\big\rangle_{J}\Tr(\psi_{I_{1}}\cdot\psi_{I_{k}})\,,

(6)

where the capital index
I
represents a set of
p
indices
i_{1}\cdots i_{p}
and
\psi_{I}=\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{p}}
.
Evaluation of the disorder average over the coupling constants by (
2
) leads to contraction of the indices.
Then the trace over the fermions is represented by the chord diagrams (for example see Figure
1
for
k=8
chord diagram.)
After resummining over the moments, the disorder averaged partition function is found as
Berkooz:2018qkz
;
Berkooz:2018jqr

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,\mu(\theta)e^{-\beta E(\theta)}\,,

(7)

where

\displaystyle E(\theta)\,=\,-\frac{2\cos\theta}{\sqrt{1-q}}\,,\qquad q\,:=\,e^{-\lambda}\,,

(8)

and

\displaystyle\mu(\theta)\,=\,(q;q)_{\infty}\,(e^{2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}\,(e^{-2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}\,.

(9)

Here the
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}\,=\,\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k})\,.

(10)

Similarly, using the chord diagram method, the two-point function of operators with dimension
\Delta

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\,=\,\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{k=1,2}\frac{d\theta_{k}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{k})e^{-\beta_{k}E(\theta_{k})}\frac{(e^{-2\Delta};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{2})};q)_{\infty}}\,,

(11)

the uncrossed four-point function of pairs of operators with dimension
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

\widetilde{G}_{4}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})\,=\,\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{d\theta_{i}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{i})e^{-\beta_{i}E(\theta_{i})}\right)\frac{(e^{-2\Delta_{1}};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{3})};q)_{\infty}}\frac{(e^{-2\Delta_{2}};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{2}\pm\theta_{3})};q)_{\infty}}\,,

(12)

are obtained.
By
(e^{-\Delta+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{2})};q)_{\infty}
, we mean to take a product of all four possible combination of the signs.
The crossed four-point function is also obtained by the chord diagram method in
Berkooz:2018jqr
,
but we do not write the result explicitly here since we don’t use it.
We study small
\lambda
expansions of these results obtained from chord diagrams in section
3
.

The other
G\Sigma
formalism, we introduce bi-local fields
G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
and
\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

as in the usual Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation to integrate out the original fermions.
Then, the disorder averaged partition function is written as

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int\mathcal{D}G\mathcal{D}\Sigma\,e^{-S}\,,

(13)

where

\displaystyle S\,=\,-\,\frac{N}{2}\log\det(\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma)\,+\,\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left[\Sigma G-\frac{1}{2p^{2}}(2G)^{p}\right]\,.

(14)

In order to take the double scaling limit (
3

\displaystyle G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})}{2}\left(1+\frac{g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}{p}\right)\,,\qquad\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{\sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}{p}\,,

(15)

where
\tau_{12}:=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}

Substituting this expression into the action, one can integrate out the

Hence, in the double scaling limit, we find the action given by

\displaystyle S\,=\,\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left[\,\frac{1}{4}\,\partial_{1}g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\partial_{2}g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,-\,e^{g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}\right]\,.

(16)

We study this small
\lambda
Liouville theory in section
4
and
5
.

follows from the computation in the Liouville theory.
We note in passing that
f(x)
in (
51
) satisfies

\displaystyle f(u)=0,\qquad\left(\partial_{x}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x}\right)f(x)=0\,,

(57)

as this is the zero-mode wave function of the Liouville theory as we explain in the next section.

3.3
Uncrossed four-point function

Next, let us consider the small
\lambda

expansion of the uncrossed four-point function

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{4}

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{d\theta_{i}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{i})e^{-\beta_{i}E(\theta_{i})}\frac{(e^{-2\Delta_{1}};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{3})};q)_{\infty}}\frac{(e^{-2\Delta_{2}};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{2}\pm\theta_{3})};q)_{\infty}}

(58)

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{d\theta_{i}}{2\pi}e^{-\lambda^{-1}F+h+\mathcal{O}(\lambda)},

where

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Biggl[2\left(\theta_{i}-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{2}-2\beta_{i}\cos\theta_{i}\Biggr]+\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-2\Delta_{1}})+\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-2\Delta_{2}})

(59)

\displaystyle-\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-\Delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{3})})-\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-\Delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{2}\pm\theta_{3})}),

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Biggl[\frac{1}{2}\beta_{i}\cos\theta_{i}+\log(2\sin\theta_{i})\Biggr]+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\Delta_{1}})+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\Delta_{2}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-e^{-\Delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{3})})-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-e^{-\Delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{2}\pm\theta_{3})}).

The saddle point equation reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{1}-\pi+\beta_{1}\sin\theta_{1}+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{1}}-\cos(\theta_{1}+\theta_{3})}\right)+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{1}}-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{3})}\right),

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{2}-\pi+\beta_{2}\sin\theta_{2}+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{2}}-\cos(\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})}\right)+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{2}-\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{2}}-\cos(\theta_{2}-\theta_{3})}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta_{3}}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{3}-\pi+\beta_{3}\sin\theta_{3}+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{1}}-\cos(\theta_{1}+\theta_{3})}\right)-\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{1}}-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{3})}\right)

\displaystyle+\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{2}}-\cos(\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})}\right)-\arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\theta_{2}-\theta_{3})}{e^{\Delta_{2}}-\cos(\theta_{2}-\theta_{3})}\right).

As in the previous subsection, one can solve this saddle point equation
order by order in the small
\Delta_{i}
expansion.
To this end, it is convenient to parameterize
\beta_{i}~(i=1,2,3)
as

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{u-\phi_{1}}{\cos u},\quad\beta_{2}=\frac{u-\phi_{2}}{\cos u},\quad\beta_{3}=\frac{\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}}{\cos u}.

(61)

Then the saddle point solution is expanded as

\displaystyle\theta_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}-u+\frac{a_{1}+\tan\phi_{1}}{2}\Delta_{1}+\frac{a_{2}+\tan\phi_{2}}{2}\Delta_{2}+b_{1}\Delta_{1}^{2}+b_{2}\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},

(62)

\displaystyle\theta_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}-u+\frac{a_{1}+\tan\phi_{1}}{2}\Delta_{1}+\frac{a_{2}+\tan\phi_{2}}{2}\Delta_{2}+c_{1}\Delta_{2}^{2}+c_{2}\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},

\displaystyle\theta_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}-u+\frac{a_{1}-\tan\phi_{1}}{2}\Delta_{1}+\frac{a_{2}-\tan\phi_{2}}{2}\Delta_{2},

where

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\phi_{1}\tan\phi_{1})\tan u}{1+u\tan u},

(63)

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\cos^{2}\phi_{1}}\left[-(1+u\tan u)\tan\phi_{1}+\frac{\phi_{1}(1+\phi_{1}\tan\phi_{1})\tan^{2}u}{1+u\tan u}\right],

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\cos^{2}\phi_{1}}\left[(1+u\tan u)\tan\phi_{2}+\frac{\phi_{1}(1+\phi_{2}\tan\phi_{2})\tan^{2}u}{1+u\tan u}-(1+\phi_{2}\tan\phi_{2}+\phi_{1}\tan\phi_{2})\tan u\right],

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\Big|_{\phi_{1}\leftrightarrow\phi_{2}},\quad c_{1}=b_{1}\Big|_{\phi_{1}\leftrightarrow\phi_{2}},\quad c_{2}=b_{2}\Big|_{\phi_{1}\leftrightarrow\phi_{2}}.

We also expand the saddle point value of
F
up to
\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{2})

\displaystyle-F_{*}=f_{0}+\sum_{i=1,2}f_{i}\Delta_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1,2}I_{ij}\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}.

(64)

Here
f_{0}
is the leading order free energy (
27
) and

f_{i}=\log\frac{\cos^{2}u}{\cos^{2}\phi_{i}}~(i=1,2)
. We find that the diagonal part of
I_{ij}
is equal to the corresponding
I

in the two-point function (
50
)

\displaystyle I_{ii}=I\big|_{\phi=\phi_{i}}=\frac{f(\phi_{i})f(-\phi_{i})}{1+u\tan u},\quad(i=1,2),

(65)

and the off-diagonal part
I_{12}
is given by

\displaystyle I_{12}=\frac{f(\phi_{1})f(\phi_{2})}{1+u\tan u},

(66)

where
f(x)
is defined in (
51
).
The computation of the one-loop determinant
\det F_{ij}
is almost parallel to
the two-point function.
After some algebra,
we find the uncrossed four-point function at the one-loop level

\displaystyle G_{4}=e^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}I_{ij}\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}}\prod_{i=1,2}\left[\frac{\cos^{2}u}{\cos^{2}\phi_{i}}(1+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{i})\right]^{\frac{\Delta_{i}}{\lambda}},

(67)

where
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is the same as the one-loop correction

\mathcal{A}
(
54
)
appeared in the two-point function

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}|_{\phi=\phi_{i}}.

(68)

Our result (
67
)
is a generalization of the one-loop computation of the two-point function
in
Goel:2023svz
.
We should stress that the factor
\frac{1}{2\lambda}I_{ij}\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}
was not considered in

Goel:2023svz
, but it should be included in the scaling regime
(
46
).

3.3.1
Relation to the energy fluctuation

If we normalize the four-point function (
67
)
by the two-point function (
53
) at the one-loop level, we find

\displaystyle\frac{G_{4}}{G_{2}(\phi_{1})G_{2}(\phi_{2})}=e^{\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{\lambda}I_{12}}.

(69)

This relation suggests that
I_{12}
can be thought of as the interaction term of the two
particles in the bulk spacetime corresponding to the boundary operators
with dimension
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
.
As discussed in
Maldacena:2016hyu
, this interaction
can be understood from the coupling of the matter operator
to the energy fluctuation

\widehat{H}=H-\langle H\rangle
.
Let us repeat the argument in
Maldacena:2016hyu
.
The saddle-point value of the energy is

\displaystyle E=-\frac{2\cos\theta_{*}}{\lambda}=-\frac{2\sin u}{\lambda},

(70)

where we used (
25
).
Thus the energy fluctuation is related to
\delta u
by

\displaystyle\delta E=-\frac{2\cos u}{\lambda}\delta u.

(71)

Using the relation

\displaystyle\phi=\left(1-\frac{2\tau}{\beta}\right)u,

(72)

the fluctuation of
\phi
under the variation of

\beta
is written as

\displaystyle\delta\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{2\tau u}{\beta}\frac{\delta\beta}{\beta}+\phi\frac{\delta u}{u}

(73)

\displaystyle=(1+u\tan u-\phi\tan u)\delta u.

Here we have used

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\beta}{\beta}=\delta\log\beta=\delta\log\frac{2u}{\cos u}=(1+u\tan u)\frac{\delta u}{u}.

(74)

Then the change of two-point function is

\displaystyle\delta\log G_{2}

\displaystyle=\delta\log\left(\frac{\cos^{2}u}{\cos^{2}\phi}\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}}

(75)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Delta}{\lambda}(\tan u\delta u-\tan\phi\delta\phi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\Delta}{\lambda}f(\phi)\delta u,

where
f(x)
is defined in (
51
).
From (
71
), the variance of
\delta u
is estimated as

\displaystyle\langle(\delta u)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\cos^{2}u}\langle(\delta E)^{2}\rangle

(76)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\cos^{2}u}\partial_{\beta}^{2}\log Z.

Plugging the leading order free energy

\displaystyle\log Z=\frac{2}{\lambda}(-u^{2}+2u\tan u)

(77)

into (
76
), we find

\displaystyle\langle(\delta u)^{2}\rangle=\frac{\lambda}{4}\frac{1}{1+u\tan u}.

(78)

Finally, combining (
75
) and (
78
) we find

\displaystyle\big\langle\delta\log G_{2}(\phi_{1})\delta\log G_{2}(\phi_{2})\rangle=\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{\lambda}\frac{f(\phi_{1})f(\phi_{2})}{1+u\tan u}=\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{\lambda}I_{12}.

(79)

This precisely matches the interaction we found in
(
69
).
This result implies that the off-diagonal part
I_{12}
represents a total energy exchange of the external operators.
Note that
\delta u
corresponds to
\sqrt{\lambda}\varepsilon
in (
30
)
and the variance
\langle(\delta u)^{2}\rangle

in (
78
) agrees with the one
obtained from the quadratic action for
\varepsilon
in (
30
).

4
One-loop correction from Liouville theory

In this section, we will show that the one-loop correction to the two- and four-point functions obtained in the previous section can be reproduced from
the Liouville theory.
As shown in
Cotler:2016fpe
,
the double scaling limit of the
G\Sigma
action of the SYK model
reduces to the Liouville action for the bi-local field

g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\lambda}\int d\tau d\nu\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\tau}g)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\nu}g)^{2}-2e^{g}\right],

(80)

where

\displaystyle\tau=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2},\quad\nu=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}.

(81)

Introducing the coordinate
x,y
by

\displaystyle x=u-\tau\cos u,\quad y=\nu\cos u,

(82)

the Liouville action is written as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\lambda}\int_{-u}^{u}dx\int_{0}^{4u}dy\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}g)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{y}g)^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}u}e^{g}\right].

(83)

Note that
x
corresponds to
\phi
in (
41
).
We assumed that
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are ordered pair of points on the thermal circle
S^{1}_{\beta}

\displaystyle 0<\tau_{2}<\tau_{1}<\beta.

(84)

Then the range of
\tau,\nu
and
x,y
are related by

\displaystyle 0<\tau<\beta,~0<\nu<2\beta\quad\Rightarrow\quad-u<x<u,~0<y<4u,

(85)

where
\beta
and
u
are related by (
26
).

The equation of motion following from the action (
83
)
is

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}g-\partial_{y}^{2}g-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}u}e^{g}=0.

(86)

One can easily see that

\displaystyle g_{\text{cl}}(x)\,=\,\log\left(\frac{\cos^{2}u}{\cos^{2}x}\right)\,,

(87)

is a “static” (i.e. independent of
y
) classical solution of the equation of motion (
86
), with boundary conditions
g(x=\pm u)=0
.
Let us consider the expansion of the Liouville action
(
83
)
around the classical solution (
87
)

\displaystyle g(x,y)=g_{\text{cl}}(x)+\sqrt{\lambda}\varepsilon(x,y).

(88)

The classical action is given by

\displaystyle S_{\text{cl}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\lambda}\int_{-u}^{u}dx\int_{0}^{4u}dy\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}g_{\text{cl}})^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}u}e^{g_{\text{cl}}}\right]

(89)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\lambda}(u^{2}-2u\tan u),

which agrees with the leading order free energy in (
27
).
The quadratic part of the action of
\varepsilon
becomes

\displaystyle S_{2}=\frac{1}{8}\int dxdy\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}\varepsilon)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{y}\varepsilon)^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right].

(90)

Then the propagator of
\varepsilon
is defined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x}\right)\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta(x-x^{\prime})\widehat{\delta}(y-y^{\prime}),

(91)

where
\widehat{\delta}(y-y^{\prime})
is the periodically extended
\delta
-function

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta}(y-y^{\prime})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(y-y^{\prime}+4um)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}}{4u},

(92)

with
\widetilde{n}=n/v
(see (
28
) for the relation between
u
and
v
). Note that the translation symmetry of
x
-coordinate is spontaneously broken by choosing the classical background
g_{\text{cl}}
, but the
y
-coordinate remains periodic with periodicity
4u=2\pi v
.

The propagator is also written as

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{2}{u}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})},

(93)

where
D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x}+\widetilde{n}^{2}\right]D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})=\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(94)

One can solve (
94
) under the boundary condition

\displaystyle D_{n}(u,x^{\prime})=D_{n}(-u,x^{\prime})=0,\qquad D_{n}(x^{\prime},x)=D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})=D_{n}(-x,-x^{\prime}).

(95)

As is well-known, the solution of
(
94
) can be constructed from the
two independent solutions of the homogeneous equation

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x}+\widetilde{n}^{2}\right]f_{n}(x)=0,

(96)

whose explicit form is easily obtained as

\displaystyle f_{n}^{(1)}=\cos(\widetilde{n}x)\tan x-\widetilde{n}\sin(\widetilde{n}x),\quad f_{n}^{(2)}=\sin(\widetilde{n}x)\tan x+\widetilde{n}\cos(\widetilde{n}x).

(97)

We can take a linear combination
of
f^{(1)}_{n}(x)
and
f^{(2)}_{n}(x)

so that
f_{n}(x)
vanishes at
x=u

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=f^{(1)}_{n}(x)f^{(2)}_{n}(u)-f^{(1)}_{n}(u)f^{(2)}_{n}(x).

(98)

Then the propagator for
n\neq 0
is given by

\displaystyle D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{\theta(x-x^{\prime})f_{n}(x)f_{n}(-x^{\prime})+\theta(x^{\prime}-x)f_{n}(-x)f_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\{f_{n}(x),f_{n}(-x)\}},

(99)

where the denominator is the Wronskian

\displaystyle\{f_{n}(x),f_{n}(-x)\}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(-x)-f_{n}(x)\partial_{x}f_{n}(-x)

(100)

\displaystyle=2(-1)^{n}\widetilde{n}^{2}(\widetilde{n}^{2}-1)\tan u.

For the zero mode, we have
3
3

3

The zero-mode propagator
D_{0}(x,x^{\prime})
has been considered in
Tarnopolsky:2018env
.

\displaystyle D_{0}(x,x^{\prime})=-\frac{\theta(x-x^{\prime})f(x)f(-x^{\prime})+\theta(x^{\prime}-x)f(-x)f(x^{\prime})}{2\tan u(1+u\tan u)},

(101)

where
f(x)
is defined in (
51
).
Note that the zero-mode wavefunction
f(x)
is formally related to the non-zero mode

f_{n}(x)
as

\displaystyle f(x)=\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{\widetilde{n}}f_{n}(x).

(102)

Plugging
D_{0}(x,x^{\prime})
(
101
) and

D_{n}(x,x^{\prime})
(
99
) into (
93
), we find

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{u\tan u}\left[-\frac{f(x)f(-x^{\prime})}{1+u\tan u}+\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(-x^{\prime})}{\widetilde{n}^{2}(\widetilde{n}^{2}-1)}e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}\right].

(103)

Here we assumed
x>x^{\prime}
.
The sum over
n
can be performed
using the formula in (
151
) and we find

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(-x^{\prime})}{\widetilde{n}^{2}(\widetilde{n}^{2}-1)}e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=f(x)f(-x^{\prime}).

(104)

Finally, the propagator becomes

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{f(x)f(-x^{\prime})}{1+u\tan u},\quad(x>x^{\prime}).

(105)

Note that this propagator is
y
-independent;
the
y
-independence of the time-ordered
four-point function was also mentioned in
Maldacena:2016hyu
.
Note also that (
105

is finite at the coincident point
(x,y)=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and hence
there is no need of the normal ordering to define
the bi-local operator
e^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}g(x,y)}
at the perturbative level.

4.1
One-loop correction of two-point function

Let us compute the one-loop correction to the two-point function
from the Liouville theory.
The two-point function is defined by

\displaystyle G_{2}=\Bigl\langle e^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}g(\phi,y_{0})}\Bigr\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int\mathcal{D}g\,e^{-S}e^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}g(\phi,y_{0})},

where
S
is the Liouville action (
83

y_{0}
is some reference point but the result is independent of

To compute the one-loop correction, we expand the action

\displaystyle S-S_{\text{cl}}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}=S_{2}+S_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\int dxdy\,\varepsilon(x,y)K\varepsilon(x,y),\qquad K=\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}x},

(108)

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{4}\int dxdy\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}\frac{\varepsilon(x,y)^{n}}{n!},\quad(n\geq 3),

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 3}S_{n}.

Then the two-point function is written as

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}g_{\text{cl}}(\phi)}\Bigl\langle e^{\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})-S_{\text{int}}}\Bigr\rangle_{S_{2}}=\left(\frac{\cos^{2}u}{\cos^{2}\phi}\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}}\Bigl\langle e^{\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})-S_{\text{int}}}\Bigr\rangle_{S_{2}}

(109)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{S_{2}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\langle\cdots\rangle_{S_{2}}=\int\mathcal{D}\varepsilon(\cdots)e^{-S_{2}}.

(110)

At the one-loop level, we find

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle e^{\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})-S_{\text{int}}}\Bigr\rangle_{S_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Biggl[1+\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2\lambda}\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})^{2}+\cdots\Biggr]\Biggl[1-S_{3}+\cdots\Biggr]\right\rangle_{S_{2}}

(111)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2\lambda}\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})^{2}\rangle+\frac{\Delta}{4}\left\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})\int dxdy\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}\frac{\varepsilon(x,y)^{3}}{3!}\right\rangle_{S_{2}}+\cdots

The second term reproduces
I
in (
50

\displaystyle I=\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})^{2}\rangle=\frac{f(\phi)f(-\phi)}{1+u\tan u},

(112)

and the last term corresponds to
\mathcal{A}
in (
54

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})\int dxdy\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}\frac{\varepsilon(x,y)^{3}}{3!}\right\rangle_{S_{2}}

(113)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int dxdy\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})\varepsilon(x,y)\rangle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)^{2}\rangle.

From the expression of the propagator (
105
),
one can show that
\mathcal{A}
is independent of
y_{0}
.
Using this property, one can show that
\mathcal{A}
in (
113

satisfies the same relation (
56
)
as we found for the one-loop correction in the previous section

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\phi}^{2}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}\phi}\right)\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{\phi}^{2}-\partial_{y_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\cos^{2}\phi}\right)\mathcal{A}

(114)

\displaystyle=\int dxdy\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}\delta(x-\phi)\widehat{\delta}(y-y_{0})\langle\varepsilon(x,y)^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\phi}\langle\varepsilon(\phi,y_{0})^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\phi}I.

This indeed reproduces (

5
Out-of-time-order correlators

In this section, we study direction evaluation of the summation over
n
in (
103
) for the out-of-time-ordered case:
\tau_{1}>\tau_{3}>\tau_{2}>\tau_{4}
.

Let us first consider a special case with
\tau_{3}=\pi
and
\tau_{4}=0
as in
Maldacena:2016hyu
, where we used
\beta=2\pi
unit.
This corresponds to
x^{\prime}=0
and
y^{\prime}=u
, as well as
\pi<\tau_{1}<2\pi
and
0<\tau_{2}<\pi
.
In this case, one of the wave function is reduced to

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)\,=\,-\widetilde{n}\,f_{n}^{(1)}(u)\,,

(121)

so that the Fourier series of the non-zero modes is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\geq 1}D_{n}(x\,,x^{\prime})\,e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}\,=\,\frac{\cot u}{2}\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\widetilde{n}(\widetilde{n}^{2}-1)}\,f_{n}(x)f_{n}^{(1)}(u)\,e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}y}\,e^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}n\pi}{2}}\,.

(122)

The summation over
n
can be explicitly performed by using the formulae (
152
) - (
154
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\geq 1}D_{n}(x\,,x^{\prime})\,e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\left[\,-\tan x+(1+x\tan x)\tan u\,-\,u\,\frac{\cos(2u-y)}{\cos u\cos x}\right]\,.

(123)

Finally combining with the zero mode contribution (
101
), the out of time ordered two-point function is found as

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(0,u)\rangle\,=\,\frac{\tan^{2}u(1+x\tan x)}{1+u\tan u}\,-\,\frac{\cos(2u-y)}{\cos u\cos x}\,.

(124)

This agrees with the results found in
Streicher:2019wek
;
Choi:2019bmd
.
One can also check that the low temperature limit of this result agrees with the one found in
Maldacena:2016hyu
(see section
6
).

In order to obtain the Lyapunov exponent, we set
x=0
and
y=-\mathrm{i}2\pi vt/\beta
, which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\geq 1}D_{n}(x\,;x^{\prime})\,e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}\,=\,-\,\frac{u\cos u}{2}\,\cosh\left(\frac{2\pi vt}{\beta}\right)\,+\,\cdots\,.

(125)

From this we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{L}\,=\,\frac{2\pi v}{\beta}\,,

(126)

which agrees with the Lyapunov exponent found in
Maldacena:2016hyu
.

Next, let us try produce the
x^{\prime}
-dependence as well.
For this purpose, we keep
x^{\prime}
general (but assume close to
0
) and set
y^{\prime}=u
.
We also assume
\pi<\tau_{1}<2\pi
and
0<\tau_{2}<\pi
and use the formulae (
152
) - (
154
).
This leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\geq 1}D_{n}(x\,,x^{\prime})\,e^{\mathrm{i}\widetilde{n}(y-y^{\prime})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\tan u\,f^{(2)}(x)f^{(2)}(x^{\prime})\,+\,\tan x^{\prime}f^{(2)}(x)\,-\,\tan x\,f^{(2)}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\,-\,\frac{u\cos(2u-y)}{\cos u\cos x\cos x^{\prime}}\,+\,\big((y-2u)u\tan u-(u+\cot u)\big)\tan x\tan x^{\prime}\bigg]\,,

(127)

where
f^{(2)}(x)=1+x\tan x
.
Finally combining with the zero mode contribution (
101
), the out of time ordered two-point function is found as

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(x,y)\varepsilon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\tan^{2}u}{1+u\tan u}\,f^{(2)}(x)f^{(2)}(x^{\prime})

(128)

\displaystyle\qquad\,-\,\frac{\cos(2u-y)}{\cos u\cos x\cos x^{\prime}}\,+\,(y-2u)\tan u\tan x\tan x^{\prime}\,.

this result completely agrees with the results found in
Streicher:2019wek
;
Choi:2019bmd
.

6
Low temperature limit

In this section, we will give low temperature expressions of our results obtained above, and compare with previous works
Maldacena:2016hyu
.

Let us start from the two-point function. For the function
\mathcal{A}
defined in (
54
),
using

\displaystyle u\,=\,\frac{\pi}{2}\,-\,\frac{\pi}{\beta}\,,\qquad\phi\,=\,\frac{\pi}{2}\,-\,\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\,,

(129)

the low temperature limit of
\mathcal{A}
is found as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\,=\,\frac{\beta}{2\pi^{2}}\left[1\,-\,\frac{\frac{\pi^{2}\tau_{12}}{\beta}(1-\frac{\tau_{12}}{\beta})}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta})}\,+\,\frac{\pi(1-\frac{2\tau_{12}}{\beta})}{\tan\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}}\right]\,.

(130)

Up to an overall coefficient, this agrees with
\langle\mathcal{C}(u_{1},u_{2})\rangle
computed in Schwarzian theory in the next subsection
6.1
.
The low temperature limit of the function
f(x)
defined in (
51
) is given by

\displaystyle f(x)\,=\,-\,\frac{\beta}{\pi}\left(1\,-\,\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\cot\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)\,.

(131)

This guarantees that the four-point function in the low temperature limit agrees with the low temperature result found in
Maldacena:2016hyu
.
This also shows that the low temperature limit of the function
I
defined in (
50
) agrees with
\langle\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})\mathcal{B}(u_{3},u_{4})\rangle

computed in Schwarzian theory, up to an overall coefficient.

In appendix
C
, we will also show that the zero-temperature factorization holds at arbitrary
\lambda
in the DSSYK model.

6.1
One-loop correction from Schawrzian mode

In this subsection, we study the one-loop correction of two-point functions in Schwarzian theory.
Here we follow the notation of
Maldacena:2016upp
and in particular
u_{i}=2\pi\tau_{i}/\beta
.
The reparametrization symmetry of two-point function transforms

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{\Delta}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{1}{|\tau_{12}|^{2\Delta}}\,\Rightarrow\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(u_{1})f^{\prime}(u_{2})}{(f(u_{1})-f(u_{2}))^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}\,,

(132)

where
\tau_{ij}:=\tau_{i}-\tau_{j}
.
Parametrizing
f(u)=\tan((u+\varepsilon(u))/2)
and expanding up to
\varepsilon^{2}
order, we find

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{\Delta}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{1}{(2\sin\frac{u_{12}}{2})^{2\Delta}}\left[1\,+\,\Delta\,\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})\,+\,\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})^{2}\,+\,\Delta\,\mathcal{C}(u_{1},u_{2})\,+\,\cdots\right]\,,

(133)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})\,

\displaystyle=\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(u_{1})\,+\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(u_{2})\,-\,\frac{\varepsilon(u_{1})-\varepsilon(u_{2})}{\tan\frac{u_{12}}{2}}\,,

(134)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(u_{1},u_{2})\,

\displaystyle=\,-\,\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}(u_{1})^{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime}(u_{2})^{2}}{2}\right)\,+\,\frac{\big(\varepsilon(u_{1})-\varepsilon(u_{2})\big)^{2}}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{u_{12}}{2})}\,.

(135)

Now we would like to evaluate the expectation value of the RHS of (
133
) with using Schwarzian mode propagator

\displaystyle\big\langle\varepsilon(u_{1})\varepsilon(u_{2})\big\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi C}\left[-\frac{(|u_{12}|-\pi)^{2}}{2}\,+\,(|u_{12}|-\pi)\sin|u_{12}|+a+b\cos u_{12}\right]\,,

(136)

where
C
is the Schwarzian coupling and
a
and
b
are gauge parameters which should not appear in any physical quantities.
Since the one-point function of the Schwarzian mode vanishes, we have

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})\big\rangle\,=\,0\,.

(137)

The two-point function of
\mathcal{B}
is evaluated in
Maldacena:2016upp
as

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{B}(u_{1},u_{2})\mathcal{B}(u_{3},u_{4})\big\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi C}\left(-2+\frac{u_{12}}{\tan\frac{u_{12}}{2}}\right)\left(-2+\frac{u_{34}}{\tan\frac{u_{34}}{2}}\right)\,.

(138)

Finally we can also evaluate the one-point function of
\mathcal{C}
as

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{C}(u_{1},u_{2})\big\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi C}\left[1\,+\,\frac{u_{12}(u_{12}-2\pi)}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{u_{12}}{2})}\,+\,\frac{(\pi-u_{12})}{\tan\frac{u_{12}}{2}}\right]\,.

(139)

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have studied the one-loop correction to the correlators of DSSYK
from two approaches: the saddle point approximation of the exact result obtained from the chord diagrams, and the perturbative computation in the
Liouville theory.
We found that the
relation (
56
) obeyed by the one-loop correction
\mathcal{A}

naturally follows from the computation in the Liouville theory.
In particular,
\mathcal{A}
and
I_{ij}
are closely related to the propagator
of the fluctuation
\varepsilon(x,y)
around the classical solution

g_{\text{cl}}
in the Liouville theory.
We also found that the out-of-time-order propagator

Appendix C
Zero-temperature factorization

In Schwarzian theory, it is known that in zero temperature limit, the uncrossed four-point function factorizes into a product of two-point functions
Mertens:2017mtv
.
In this subsection, we will show that this zero-temperature factorization is also true for the DSSYK for any value of
q
(or
\lambda
).

For this purpose, we first define finite temperature correlators by

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{2}(\tau)\,

\displaystyle:=\,\widetilde{G}_{2}(\tau,\beta-\tau)\,,

(155)

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{4}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\widetilde{G}_{4}(\tau_{12},\tau_{23},\tau_{34},\tau_{41}+\beta)\,,

(156)

where the RHS’ are defined in (
11
) and (
12
). Each
\tau_{i}
represents the matter operator insertion time.
We also shift the energy as

\displaystyle E(\theta)\,\Rightarrow\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-q}}\,(1-\cos\theta)\,.

(157)

This sets the ground state energy
E(0)=0
.

Let us first study the zero-temperature limit of the two-point function:

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{2}(\tau)\,=\,\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\,\mu(\theta_{1})\mu(\theta_{2})e^{-\tau E(\theta_{1})}e^{(\tau-\beta)E(\theta_{2})}\,\frac{(e^{-2\Delta};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta+i(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{2})};q)_{\infty}}\,.

(158)

Due to the Boltzmann factor
e^{-\beta E(\theta_{2})}
, the contribution to the
\theta_{2}
integral is localized to the ground state, i.e.
\theta_{2}\to 0
.
In this limit, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\theta)\,=\,-4(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}\,\sin^{2}\theta\,+\,\cdots\,.

(159)

Therefore, the zero-temperature two-point function is given by

\displaystyle G^{\infty}_{2}(\tau)\,=\,-\frac{2\sqrt{1-q}}{\beta}\,(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}\,e^{-\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}}I_{1}\left(\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,\mu(\theta)e^{-\tau E(\theta)}\,\frac{(e^{-2\Delta};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta\pm i\theta};q)_{\infty}^{2}}\,.

(160)

Before studying the four-point function, let us here consider the late time behavior of this zero-temperature two-point function.
Evaluating the late time behavior by the same method as zero-temperature limit discussed above, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\inf}G^{\infty}_{2}(\tau)\,=\,\frac{4(1-q)}{\beta\tau}\,e^{-\frac{2(\beta+\tau)}{\sqrt{1-q}}}\,(q;q)_{\infty}^{6}\frac{(e^{-2\Delta};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{-\Delta};q)_{\infty}^{4}}\,I_{1}\left(\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)I_{1}\left(\frac{2\tau}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)\,.

(161)

Since

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}I_{1}\left(\frac{2\tau}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)\,=\,\frac{(1-q)^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{4\pi\tau}}\,e^{\frac{2\tau}{\sqrt{1-q}}}\,+\,\cdots\,,

(162)

The late time behavior of the zero-temperature two-point function is
G^{\infty}_{\Delta}(\tau)\propto\tau^{-3/2}
, which agrees with the late time behavior in Schwarzian theory.

Now we study zero-temperature limit of the four-point function:

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{4}\,

\displaystyle=\,\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=1,3}\left(\frac{d\theta_{i}}{2\pi}\,\mu(\theta_{i})e^{-(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i+1})E(\theta_{i})}(e^{-2\Delta_{i}};q)_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{2})e^{-(\tau_{23}+\tau_{41}+\beta)E(\theta_{2})}\prod_{j=1}^{2}\frac{1}{(e^{-\Delta+i(\pm\theta_{j}\pm\theta_{j+1})};q)_{\infty}}\,,

(163)

where
\Delta_{3}=\Delta_{2}
.
Again, zero-temperature limit
\beta\to\infty
localizes
\theta_{2}\to 0

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{4}\,=\,-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{1-q}}\,(q;q)_{\infty}^{-3}\,e^{\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}}\left(I_{1}\left(\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)\right)^{-1}G^{\infty}_{2}(\tau_{12})\,G^{\infty}_{2}(\tau_{34})\,.

(164)

We note that as in Schwarzian theory, the zero-temperature four-point function can be factorized only in this
s

t

u

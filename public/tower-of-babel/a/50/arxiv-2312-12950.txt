Bounds for characteristic numbers of conic-line arrangements in the plane
Rita Pardini and Piotr Pokora

Abstract
. The main aim of the note is to provide an upper-bound for the characteristic number of conic-line arrangements with ordinary singularities in the complex projective plane. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) 14C20, 14N25, 14J29 Keywords conic-line arrangements, logarithmic Chern classes, ordinary singularities, abelian covers



1.
Introduction

The main aim of the present note is to answer a question regarding log-surfaces that are defined as the complement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of a conic-line arrangements with ordinary singularities. This is a very classical problem in the theory of algebraic surfaces and it dates back (at least) to work due to Iitaka
[
5
]
.
An arrangement of curves
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a collection of smooth projective curves with
m\geqslant 3
such that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}=\emptyset
;
\mathcal{D}
is said to be simple crossing if any two of the
D_{i}
intersect transversally. For
2\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
, a
k
-point is a point in
\mathcal{D}
which belongs to exactly
k
curves. Following
[
1
]
, the number of
k
-points will be denoted by
t_{k}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a simple crossing arrangement. Consider the blow-up
\tau\colon Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex plane at all the
k
-points of
\mathcal{D}
with
k\geqslant 3
, and denote by
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
the reduced total transform of
\mathcal{D}
under
\tau
, which is a simple normal crossings divisor. The logarithmic Chern numbers
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})
,
\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})
of the pair
(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})
are defined as the Chern numbers of the vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{Y}(\mathop{\rm log}\nolimits\overline{\mathcal{D}})
and
they can be computed in terms of the numerical invariants of
\mathcal{D}
, as follows

(1)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})=9-\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}^{2}+4\sum_{i=1}^{m}(g(D_{i})-1)+\sum_{r\geqslant 2}(3r-4)t_{r},

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})=3+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}(g(D_{i})-1)+\sum_{r\geqslant 2}(r-1)t_{r}.

Following Hirzebruch’s paper
[
4
]
, we define the characteristic number of
(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})
(aka the log-Chern slope of the log surface
(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})
) as

\gamma(\mathcal{D}):=\frac{\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})}{\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{D}})}.

It is well-known by
[
6
,
15
]
that
\gamma(\mathcal{D})\leqslant 3
, but it is natural to wonder whether, if we consider a particular class of plane curves, we can do better.

If we restrict our attention to line arrangements
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, then by a result due to Sommese
[
12
, (5.3) Theorem]
one has

\gamma(\mathcal{L})\leqslant\frac{8}{3},

and we get equality if and only if
\mathcal{L}
is the dual Hesse arrangement consisting of
m=9
lines and
t_{3}=12
. If we now look at the arrangements of smooth conics
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with only ordinary singularities, then the second author showed in
[
9
]
that

\gamma(\mathcal{C})<\frac{8}{3},

but we do not know much about conic arrangements with characteristic numbers close to
\frac{8}{3}
. In his Ph.D. thesis
[
14
, Section 2.5.1]
, G. Urzúa asked a question that can be formulated as follows.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement consisting of
d
lines and
k
smooth conics such that
t_{d+k}=0
and all the singularities of
\mathcal{CL}
are
ordinary
. Is it true that

\gamma(\mathcal{CL})\leqslant\frac{8}{3}

always holds?

The main result of the present note is an affirmative answer to the above question under the assumption that
k\geqslant 3
:

Main Theorem

(see Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics such that
t_{d+k}=0
. Then
\gamma(\mathcal{CL})<\frac{8}{3}
.

Here is the structure of our paper. In Section 2, we show how to construct, for any prime
p
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{k+d-1}
-covers of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
branched precisely on the curves of the given conic-line arrangement. In Section 3, we prove Hirzebruch-type inequalities for conic-line arrangements with ordinary singularities - this result allows us to prove in Section 4 our Theorem
4.1
, which is the main result of our note.

We work over the complex numbers and all varieties are projective.

2.
Preliminaries on abelian covers

For the reader’s convenience here we recall from
[
8
, § 2]
the structure theorem for abelian covers in the special case when the Galois group is
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{m}
for a prime
p
.

Consider a
G
-cover

f\colon X\rightarrow Y
with
Y
smooth and
X
a normal variety. The irreducible components of the branch divisor of
f
are labelled by pairs of the form
(H,\Psi)
, where
H<G
is a cyclic subgroup of
G
and
\Psi\in H^{*}
is a generator. If we fix a primitive
p
-th root
\xi
of
1
, we have the following bijection:

\quad g\in G\setminus\{0\}\quad\iff\quad(H,\Psi)\text{ is a pair as above}

that sends a non-zero element
g\in G
to the pair consisting of the subgroup
H
generated by
g
and of the character
\Psi
of
H^{*}
such that
\Psi(g)=\xi
.
Similarly, we have the identification:

G^{*}:={\rm Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong{\rm Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

defined by sending
\psi\in{\rm Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
to the character

g\mapsto\xi^{\psi(g)}.

Finally, given a class
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we write
\hat{\alpha}
for the only representative of
\alpha
such that
0\leqslant\alpha<p
. Then, given a
G
-cover, we have the following

(2)

pL_{\chi}\equiv_{{\rm lin}}\sum_{g\neq 0}\widehat{\chi(g)}D_{g},\quad\forall\chi\in G^{*},

where
\equiv_{{\rm lin}}
denotes linear equivalence of divisors.
Conversely, if
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in G^{*}
is a basis and
L_{\chi_{1}},\dots,L_{\chi_{m}}
are line bundles satisfying

pL_{\chi_{i}}\equiv_{{\rm lin}}\sum_{g\neq 0}\widehat{\chi_{i}(g)}D_{g}.

then there exists a
G
-cover
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
branched along the
D_{g}
’s.

Let
\mathcal{CL}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d},C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement consisting of
d
lines and
k
smooth conics.

Let
p
be an odd prime; we are going to show the existence of an abelian covering with
G\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{d+k-1}
branched along
\mathcal{CL}
. Let
G
be the group defined by the following exact sequence:

(3)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\delta\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 0

with the mapping
\delta
given by

1\rightarrow(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k\text{ times}},\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{d\text{ times}}).

Denote by
g_{i}\in G
the image of the
i
-th vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{k+d}
. We set

D_{g_{i}}=\ell_{i}\,\,\text{ for }\,\,i\in\{1,\dots,d\},

D_{g_{i}}=C_{i-d}\,\,\text{ for }\,\,i\in\{d+1,\dots,k+d\}

and
D_{g}=0
for all the remaining
g\in G
.
Dualizing the sequence (
3
), we obtain:

0\longrightarrow G^{*}\longrightarrow\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k}\right)^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow 0,

i.e., the character
\chi=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{1},\dots,y_{d})\in\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k}\right)^{*}
is in
G^{*}
if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}=0\text{ holds in }\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Equation (
2
) now reads

pL_{\chi}\equiv_{{\rm lin}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{x_{i}}\ell_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\hat{y_{i}}C_{i}\equiv_{{\rm lin}}\bigg(\sum_{i}^{k}\hat{x_{i}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\hat{y_{i}}\bigg)\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}}(1)

and it can be solved for any
\chi\in G^{*}
. This shows that there exists a
G
-cover with
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k-1}
branched along
\mathcal{CL}
.

The same construction can be performed also for
p=2
, if
d>1
: the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k+d-1}
-cover that one obtains is the fiber product of the Kummer cover with Galois group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{d-1}
branched on the lines and of the
k
double covers branched on
C_{1},\dots C_{k}
, respectively.

Remark 2.1
.

One can construct
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k-1}
-covers of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
branched over the components of
\mathcal{CL}
for any odd integer
p
, not necessarily prime. Here we only treat the case when
p
is prime because it is sufficient for our purposes and is easier to describe.

In the next section we will use these constructions to obtain Hirzebruch-type inequalities for conic-line arrangements in the complex plane with only ordinary singularities.

3.
Inequalities for conic-line arrangements via abelian covers

In this section we derive some inequalities for the numerical invariants of conic-line arrangements. The technique, that goes back to Hirzebruch’s work on line arrangements
[
3
]
, boils down to constructing singular abelian covers branched on the curves of the arrangement, explicitly computing the desingularization and then applying to it the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality or the log version due to Miyaoka
[
7
]
.
Proposition
3.5
is the key ingredient for the proof of our main result in the next section.

Throughout all the section we make the following

Assumption 3.1
.

\mathcal{CL}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d},C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of lines and smooth conics admitting only ordinary singularities and such that
k\geqslant 3
and
t_{d+k}=0
.

Let
p
be a prime. As explained in §
2
, there is an abelian cover
f_{p}\colon X_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with Galois group
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k-1}
branched on the curves of
\mathcal{CL}
(if
p=2
we also assume that
d\geqslant 3
or
d=0
, and in the latter case the Galois group is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
).

We use the notation introduced in §
2
. Assume first that
p
is odd. By
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
a point
Q\in X
is singular if and only if
f(Q)
is a point of
\mathcal{CL}
of multiplicity
\geqslant 3
, since any subset of the
g_{i}
of cardinality
\leqslant d+k-1
is independent in
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d+k-1}
(notation as in §
3
). Denote by
\tau\colon Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
the blow-up of the complex projective plane at all singular points of
\mathcal{CL}
of multiplicity
\geqslant 3
and consider the following diagram obtained by taking base change and normalization:

(4)

\textstyle{W_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{p}}
\textstyle{X_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{p}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{2}.}

Let
P\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a point of multiplicity
r\geqslant 3
of
\mathcal{CL}
and let
g_{i_{1}},\dots g_{i_{r}}\in G
be the elements corresponding to the curves of the arrangement containing
P
. By the normalization algorithm of
[
8
, § 3]
, the exceptional curve
E_{P}
of
\tau
lying over
P
appears in the branch locus of
\sigma
with label
g_{P}:=g_{i_{1}}+\dots+g_{i_{r}}
, unless
g_{p}=0
. Since
r<d+k
by the assumption, as already noted above we have
g_{P}\neq 0
and so
E_{P}
is contained in the branch locus of
\sigma_{p}
. By the criterion recalled above,
W
is smooth over
E_{P}
since
g_{P}
and
g_{i_{j}}
are independent for every
j=1,\dots r
. Summing up,
W_{p}
is a smooth cover of
Y
branched over
\overline{\mathcal{CL}}:=\sum_{P}E_{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j}
, where
E_{P}
are the
\tau
-exceptional curves and
\bar{\ell}_{i}
,
\bar{C}_{j}
, are the strict transforms of
\ell_{i}
,
C_{j}
.

Assume now
p=2
; in this case we also assume that either
d=0
or
d\geqslant 3
and there is no point lying on all the lines and on no conic of
\mathcal{CL}
. Under these assumptions all the above claims are still true, except for the fact that if
d=0
the Galois group is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
.

The canonical class of
W_{p}
is the pull-back of the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor:

(5)

K_{p}=K_{Y}+\frac{p-1}{p}(\sum_{P}E_{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j})=\tau^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}+\frac{2p-1}{p}\sum_{P}E_{P}+\frac{p-1}{p}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j}).

If
p<q
, then
K_{p}<K_{q}
; we set

K:=\lim_{p}K_{p}=K_{Y}+\sum_{P}E_{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j}.

Note that
K
is the logarithmic canonical divisor
\bar{c_{1}}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})
.

We start by studying positivity properties of
K_{p}
or, equivalently, of
K_{W_{p}}
. Since
\mathcal{CL}
contains conics, any line
\ell_{i}
of
\mathcal{CL}
meets the rest of the arrangement in at least two points. We say that a line of
\mathcal{CL}
is
exceptional
if it meets the rest of the arrangement at exactly two points.

Example 3.2
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
be non collinear points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, let
\ell_{i}
be the line joining
P_{j}
and
P_{k}
, where
i,j,k
is a permutation of 1,2,3, and let
\mathcal{CL}
be the arrangement
\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},C_{1},\dots C_{k}\}
where the
C_{j}
are conics containing
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
. The lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
are exceptional for
\mathcal{CL}
.
Taking the Cremona transformation centered at
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
we see that
\mathcal{CL}
is equivalent to a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
of degree
k+3
that contains 3 lines that meet the rest of the arrangement only at double points of
\mathcal{L}
.

It is not hard to see that a conic-line arrangement has at most three exceptional lines and that if it has three exceptional lines then it is the arrangement
\mathcal{CL}
that we have just described.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime. In the above setting:

(i)

K_{p}
is effective;

(ii)

K_{p}
is big for
p\geqslant 3
;

(iii)

K_{p}
is nef iff
\mathcal{CL}
contains no exceptional line;

(iv)

K
is nef and big.

Proof.

(i) Since
K_{p}<K_{q}
if
p<q
, it is enough to prove the claim for
p=2
. Let
P_{1},\dots P_{m}
be the points of multiplicity
\geqslant 3
of
\mathcal{CL}
and let
\mu_{t}\leqslant 3
be the multiplicity of
P_{t}
for
C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}
, and let
E_{t}
be the exceptional curve corresponding to
P_{t}
,
t=1,\dots m
. Then (
5
) can be rewritten for
p=2
as

\displaystyle K_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\tau^{*}(C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3})+\frac{3}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{m}E_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j})=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{3-\mu_{t}}{2}E_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=4}^{k}\bar{C}_{j})\geqslant 0

(ii) Again, it is enough to prove the claim for
p=3
. Arguing as above we have

\displaystyle K_{3}=\frac{1}{6}(\bar{C}_{1}+\bar{C}_{2}+\bar{C}_{3})+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{10-3\mu_{t}}{6}E_{t}+\frac{2}{3}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=4}^{k}\bar{C}_{j})\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{18}(\sum_{t=1}^{s}3E_{t}+\bar{C}_{1}+\bar{C}_{2}+\bar{C}_{3})\geqslant\frac{1}{3}\tau^{*}h,

where
h
is the class of a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Since
\tau^{*}h
is big,
K_{3}
is also big.

(iii) The divisor
K_{W_{p}}=\sigma_{p}^{*}K_{p}
is effective by (i), and therefore it is nef iff
W_{p}
contains no
(-1)
-curve. A
(-1)
-curve of
W_{p}
maps to a curve
\Gamma
of
Y
with
K_{p}\Gamma<0
. Since by (i)
K_{p}
is effective supported on the components of
\overline{\mathcal{CL}}=\sum_{P}E_{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j}
,
\Gamma
must be a component of
\mathcal{CL}
. If
\Gamma
is the strict transform of an exceptional line
\ell_{i}
of
\mathcal{CL}
, then
\Gamma^{2}=-1
. In fact all the conics of
\mathcal{CL}
must contain the points
P_{1},P_{2}
of intersection of
\ell_{i}
with the rest of the arrangement, so
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have multiplicity
\geqslant 4
for
\mathcal{CL}
.
By the Hurwitz formula the components of
\sigma_{p}^{*}\Gamma
are rational. Since the stabilizer of
\sigma_{p}^{*}\Gamma
has order
p^{2}
by construction and
\Gamma
is in the branch locus, we see that
\sigma_{p}^{*}\Gamma
is a disjoint union of
(-1)
-curves.
The only other case in which a component
\Gamma
of
\overline{\mathcal{CL}}
pulls back to a union of rational curve of
W_{p}
is when
p=2
and
\Gamma
meets the rest of the branch locus at three points, namely
\Gamma
is either the strict transform of a line blown up at three points or it is the exceptional curve over a point of multiplicity 3 of
\mathcal{CL}
. In the either case, the stabilizer of
\sigma_{2}^{*}\Gamma
has order 8, therefore the components of
\sigma_{2}^{*}\Gamma
have even self-intersection, equal to
2\Gamma^{2}
, and are not
(-1)
-curves.

(iv) The divisor
K
is big by (ii). Since it is effective supported on the components of
\overline{\mathcal{CL}}
, it is enough to check
K\Gamma\geqslant 0
for every component
\Gamma
of
\overline{\mathcal{CL}}
. Since
K\Gamma=\lim_{p}K_{p}\Gamma
, by the proof of (iii) it is enough to show
K\Gamma\geqslant 0
when
\Gamma
is the strict transform of an exceptional line of
\mathcal{CL}
. In that case a direct computation shows that
K\Gamma=0
.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.3
:

Corollary 3.4
.

In the above set-up the following hold:

(i)

W_{p}
has non-negative Kodaira dimension;

(ii)

W_{p}
is of general type for
p\geqslant 3
;

(iii)

W_{p}
is minimal iff
\mathcal{CL}
contains no exceptional line;

(iv)

(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})
is of log general type.

For an arrangement of curves
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with only ordinary singularities, we denote by
t_{r}
the the number of points of multiplicity
r
and we set

f_{0}=\sum_{r\geqslant 2}t_{r},\quad f_{1}=\sum_{r\geqslant 2}rt_{r},\quad f_{2}=\sum_{r\geqslant 2}r^{2}t_{r}.

With the above notation we have:

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})^{2}=9-8k-5d+3f_{1}-4f_{0},

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})=3-2k-2d+f_{1}-f_{0}.

Now we are ready to present our Hirzebruch-type inequalities for conic-line arrangements. For
d=0
next result recovers
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d},C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics admitting only ordinary singularities and such that
t_{d+k}=0
. Then one has

5k+t_{2}+t_{3}\geqslant d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(r-4)t_{r}.

Proof.

We use the notation introduced so far.
We compute the Chern numbers of the cover
\sigma_{3}\colon W_{3}\to Y
. Since this is a rather standard computation (
[
1
,
11
]
), we only outline it.
We start with the Euler characteristic of
W
, namely

e(W_{3})/3^{d+k-3}=9\cdot(3-2k-2d+f_{1}-f_{0})+6\cdot(d+k-f_{1}+f_{0})+f_{1}-t_{2}=

27-12k-12d+4f_{1}-3f_{0}-t_{2}.

Next we compute
K_{W_{3}}^{2}
. We have seen above that
K_{W_{3}}=\sigma_{3}^{*}K_{3}
, where

\displaystyle K_{3}=K_{Y}+\frac{2}{3}(\sum_{P}E_{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\ell}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{C}_{j})

where the summation above is taken over all singular points
P
of
\mathcal{CL}
having multiplicity
\geqslant 3
.
Following the path of
[
1
, Kapitel 1.3]
we get

9(K_{3})^{2}=9(9-8k-5d+3f_{1}-4f_{0})+12(k+d-f_{1}+f_{0})+4k+d+f_{1}-f_{0}+t_{2}=

81-56k-32d+16f_{1}-25f_{0}+t_{2}.

By Corollary
3.4
the surface
W_{3}
is of general type, therefore we can apply the Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality

\frac{3e(W_{3})-K_{W_{3}}^{2}}{3^{d+k-3}}\geqslant 0.

By the previous computations, this is the same as:

3\cdot\bigg(27-12k-12d+4f_{1}-3f_{0}-t_{2}\bigg)-81+56k+32d-16f_{1}+25f_{0}-t_{2}\geqslant 0,

which finally gives us

5k+t_{2}+t_{3}\geqslant d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(r-4)t_{r},

as claimed.
∎

The next result improves
[
11
, Thm. 4.2]
:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d},C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d\geqslant 3
(or
d=0
) lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics with ordinary singularities. Assume that:

(a)

there is no point
P\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
that lies on all the lines and on no conic of
\mathcal{CL}
;

(b)

\mathcal{CL}
does not contain an exceptional line.

Then:

8k+t_{2}+\frac{3}{4}t_{3}\geqslant d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(2r-9)t_{r}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
3.5
, but we take
p=2
and apply a log version of the Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality due to Miyaoka.

Consider the cover
\sigma_{2}\colon W_{2}\to Y
. By Corollary
3.4
the canonical class
K_{W_{2}}=\sigma_{2}^{*}K_{2}
is nef.
Therefore if
A_{1},...,A_{m}
,
B_{1},\dots B_{n}
are smooth disjoint curves in
W_{2}
such that the
A_{i}
are elliptic and the
B_{j}
are rational with
B_{j}^{2}=-2
, then the following inequality holds by
[
7
, Cor. 1.3]
:

(7)

3e(W)-K^{2}_{W}\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-A_{i}^{2})+\frac{9}{2}n.

In our situation, let
E
be an exceptional curve of
\tau
mapping to a point of
\mathcal{CL}
of multiplicity
r
of
\mathcal{CL}
: if
r=4
then
\sigma_{2}^{-1}(E)
is the disjoint union of
2^{k+d-5}
(
2^{k-4}
if
d=0
) elliptic curves of self-intersection
-4
, while if
r=3
then
\sigma_{2}^{-1}(E)
is the disjoint union of
2^{k+d-4}
(
2^{k-3}
if
d=0
) rational curves of self-intersection
-2
.
So (
7
) gives

3e(W)-K_{W}^{2}\geqslant 4t_{4}\cdot 2^{k+d-5}+\frac{9}{2}t_{3}\cdot 2^{k+d-4}

if
d\geqslant 3
and

3e(W)-K_{W}^{2}\geqslant 4t_{4}\cdot 2^{k-4}+\frac{9}{2}t_{3}\cdot 2^{k-3}

if
d=0
.
After simple manipulations in either case we arrive at

8k+t_{2}+\frac{3}{4}t_{3}\geqslant d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(2r-9)t_{r},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Assume that
\mathcal{CL}
contains no exceptional line and that
k\geqslant 3
.
Then arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, but for
p=3
, we can derive the following inequality, which slightly improves Proposition
3.5
:

5k+t_{2}+\frac{3}{4}t_{3}\geqslant d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(r-4)t_{r}.

The next result is proven in
[
11
, Prop. 6.2]
by combinatorial methods under the additional assumption that
t_{d+k-1}=0
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d},C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics admitting only ordinary singularities and such that
t_{d+k}=0
.
Then

3-2k-2d+f_{1}-f_{0}>0.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
we have
\bar{c}_{1}(Y,\mathcal{CL})=K^{2}>0
. Since
K=\lim_{p}K_{p}
we have
K_{p}^{2}>0
for large
p
. Applying Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality to
W_{p}
for large
p
we obtain:

(8)

\frac{3e(W_{p})-K_{W_{p}}^{2}}{p^{d+k-1}}=\frac{3e(W_{p})}{p^{d+k-1}}-K_{p}^{2}\geqslant 0.

It is easy to check that

\lim_{p}\frac{e(W_{p})}{p^{d+k-1}}=e(Y\setminus\overline{\mathcal{CL}})=\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}}),

hence
taking the limit in (
8
) and recalling (
6
) we obtain:

3-2k-2d+f_{1}-f_{0}=\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})\geqslant\frac{K^{2}}{3}>0.

∎

4.
Bounds for the characteristic numbers of conic-line arrangements

Now we are ready to prove the main result of our note.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics such that
t_{k+d-1}=0
. Then
\gamma(\mathcal{CL})<\frac{8}{3}
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\gamma(\mathcal{CL})\geqslant\frac{8}{3}
, namely

\frac{9-8k-5d+3f_{1}-4f_{0}}{3-2d-2k+f_{1}-f_{0}}\geqslant\frac{8}{3}.

By Proposition
3.8
(cf. also
[
11
, Proposition 6.2]
), the denominator
3-2d-2k+f_{1}-f_{0}
is strictly positive, so we get

3+d+f_{1}-4f_{0}\geqslant 8k.

By the Hirzebruch-type inequality of Proposition
3.5
, we have

5k-t_{2}\geqslant d+f_{1}-4f_{0},

which implies that

3+5k-t_{2}\geqslant 3+d+f_{1}-4f_{0}\geqslant 8k.

This gives us

3\geqslant 3k+t_{2},

but
k\geqslant 3
and
t_{2}\geqslant 0
, so we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.5
, we have

8k+2t_{2}+t_{3}>3+d+\sum_{r\geqslant 5}(r-4)t_{r}.

Now we provide a sharper bound on the characteristic numbers under the assumption that intersection points are only double and triple points.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{CL}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines and
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics such that the only intersection points are ordinary double and triple points. Then:

\gamma(\mathcal{CL})<\frac{5}{2}.

Proof.

Recall that

\bar{c}_{2}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{CL}})=3-2d-2k+t_{2}+2t_{3}>0

by Proposition
3.8
.

In order to prove our statement, assume by contradiction
\gamma(\mathcal{CL})\geqslant\frac{5}{2}
. It means that

\frac{9-8k-5d+2t_{2}+5t_{3}}{3-2d-2k+t_{2}+2t_{3}}\geqslant\frac{5}{2}.

This gives us

18-16k-10d+4t_{2}+10t_{3}\geqslant 15-10d-10k+5t_{2}+10t_{3},

so finally we arrive at

3\geqslant 6k+t_{2}.

Since
k\geqslant 3
, we get a contradiction.
∎

Finally we to present a sharp lower bound on the characteristic numbers for arrangements consisting of conics and only having ordinary singularities. Our result is in the spirit of
[
2
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an arrangement of
k\geqslant 3
smooth conics in the plane such that
t_{k}=0
.
Then

\gamma(\mathcal{C}):=\frac{9-8k+3f_{1}-4f_{0}}{3-2k+f_{1}-f_{0}}\geqslant\frac{4k^{2}-12k+9}{2k^{2}-4k+3}.

Moreover, the lower bound is achieved when our arrangement has only double points as intersections.

Proof.

Observe that if
\mathcal{C}
admits only double intersection points, then

\gamma(\mathcal{C})=\frac{9-8k+2t_{2}}{3-2k+t_{2}}=\frac{4k^{2}-12k+9}{2k^{2}-4k+3}.

Now we want to show that the case when we have only double intersection points as singularities gives a lower bound for
\gamma(\mathcal{C})
. We wish to show that

(9)

\frac{9-8k+3f_{1}-4f_{0}}{3-2k+f_{1}-f_{0}}\geqslant\frac{4k^{2}-12k+9}{2k^{2}-4k+3}.

Observe that the above inequality is equivalent to

(10)

-8k^{3}+14k^{2}-6k+2k^{2}f_{1}+(-4k^{2}+4k-3)f_{0}\geqslant 0.

Recall that the following combinatorial count holds

4\cdot\binom{k}{2}=2(k^{2}-k)=\sum_{r\geqslant 2}\binom{r}{2}t_{r}.

Multiplying by
2
the above formula we get

(11)

4k^{2}-4k=\sum_{r\geqslant 2}r^{2}t_{r}-\sum_{r\geqslant 2}rt_{r}=f_{2}-f_{1}.

and observe that

-8k^{3}+14k^{2}-6k=-2k(f_{2}-f_{1})+\frac{3}{2}(f_{2}-f_{1})=\bigg(-2k+\frac{3}{2}\bigg)f_{2}+\bigg(2k-\frac{3}{2}\bigg)f_{1}.

Plugging this into (
10
) we obtain

\bigg(-2k+\frac{3}{2}\bigg)f_{2}+\bigg(2k^{2}+2k-\frac{3}{2}\bigg)f_{1}+(-4k^{2}+4k-3)f_{0}=

\sum_{r\geqslant 2}\bigg(\bigg(-2k+\frac{3}{2}\bigg)r^{2}+\bigg(2k^{2}+2k-\frac{3}{2}\bigg)r+(-4k^{2}+4k-3)\bigg)t_{r}\geqslant 0,

so in order to finish the proof we have to show that

(12)

\bigg(\bigg(-2k+\frac{3}{2}\bigg)r^{2}+\bigg(2k^{2}+2k-\frac{3}{2}\bigg)r+(-4k^{2}+4k-3)\bigg)\geqslant 0

for
r\in\{2,...,k-1\}
and suitably taken values of
k
, i.e., for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
the inequality must hold with
k\geqslant r+1
, since
t_{k}=0
. In order to finish our proof, if we plug in
k=r+h
with
h\geqslant 1
then the left-hand side of (
12
) has the following form:

(13)

(r-2)\cdot(4h(h+r-1)-r+3).

Since
r\geqslant 2
and
h\geqslant 1
we see that
(r-2)\cdot(4h(h+r-1)-r+3)\geqslant 0
, which completes our proof.
∎

Let us recall the world-record conic-line arrangements having the highest known characteristic number.

Example 4.5

(Klein’s arrangement of conics and lines)
.

In
[
10
]
, the second author with J. Roé described in detail an interesting conic-line arrangement
\mathcal{CL}
consisting of
21
lines and
21
conics (these curves are polars to Klein’s quartic curve at the
21
nodes of the associated Steinerian curve), and it has
42
double points,
252
triple points, and
189
quadruple points. We can compute that

\gamma(\mathcal{CL})=\frac{9-8\cdot 21-5\cdot 21+2\cdot 42+5\cdot 252+8\cdot 189}{3-2\cdot 21-2\cdot 21+42+2\cdot 252+3\cdot 189}\approx 2.512,

and to the best of our knowledge, this is the highest known value.

Finishing our note, let us formulate the following difficult conjecture which is strictly motivated by the prediction regarding characteristic numbers for both line and conic arrangements in the complex plane.

Conjecture 4.6
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.1
, for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
there exist only finitely many conic-line arrangements
\mathcal{CL}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with only ordinary singularities with
\gamma(\mathcal{CL})>\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon
.

Non-Homotopic Drawings of Multigraphs
António Girão, Freddie Illingworth, Alex Scott, David R. Wood

Abstract
A multigraph drawn in the plane is non-homotopic if no two edges connecting the same pair of vertices can be continuously deformed into each other without passing through a vertex, and is k k -crossing if every pair of edges (self-)intersects at most k k times. We prove that the number of edges in an n n -vertex non-homotopic k k -crossing multigraph is at most 6 13 ​ n ​ ( k + 1 ) 6^{13n(k+1)} , which is a big improvement over previous upper bounds. We also study this problem in the setting of monotone drawings where every edge is an x-monotone curve. We show that the number of edges, m m , in such a drawing is at most 2 ​ ( 2 ​ n k + 1 ) 2\binom{2n}{k+1} and the number of crossings is Ω ⁡ ( m 2 + 1 / k n 1 + 1 / k ) \Omega\bigl(\frac{m^{2+1/k}}{n^{1+1/k}}\bigr) . For fixed k k these bounds are both best possible up to a constant multiplicative factor.

A central question of graph drawing research asks for the maximum number of edges in an
n

The situation for multigraphs (allowing parallel edges and self-loops) is less clear, since there are planar multigraphs with one vertex and arbitrarily many edges. Notation

A

of a multigraph
G
is a function
\phi
that maps each vertex

to a point
\phi_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and maps each edge

to a continuous curve
\phi_{e}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with endpoints
\phi_{v}
and
\phi_{w}

•

every vertex is represented by a different point; that is, the points
(\phi_{v}\colon v\in V(G))
are distinct;

•

no edge passes through any vertex; that is,
\phi_{e}(t)\neq v
for all

, and

•

is an unordered pair of distinct pairs

with
\phi_{e}(s)=\phi_{f}(t)
.

We note that if

crossings. Parallel edges

with end-vertices

with respect to a drawing

to

without passing through any vertices and while keeping the end-points fixed. As illustrated in

if no two parallel edges are homotopic. It is natural to consider non-homotopic drawings, since homotopic edges can be considered to be equivalent. The non-homotopic assumption immediately eliminates the example of a planar multigraph with exactly one vertex and more than one edge.

A drawing

of a multigraph

is

is an x-monotone curve for each edge

The winding number

•

If

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a set of monotone drawing sequences of length

Let

denote the size of the largest set of length

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{B}
be those sequences of

which, upon deleting their first entry, are no longer monotone drawing sequences. The only sequence in

. Let
\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{0},\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{\ast}
be those sequences in

which start with a

,
0
,
-
,
\ast
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}
be those sequences obtained by deleting the first entry (which, of course, is a

) from sequences in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. Similarly define
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\ast}
. Note that each of these is a set of monotone drawing sequences of length
n
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\ast}
be those sequences obtained by deleting the first entry from a sequence in
\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{B}
. Note
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a set of monotone drawing sequences of length

where every pair of sequences crosses at most
k

.

In any monotone drawing sequence, the symbol

can be only be succeeded or preceded by a

or
\ast
. In particular, the first entry of a sequence in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\ast}
is a
\ast
or a

. If a sequence in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}
starts with a
\ast
, then the original sequence (before deleting the first entry) must have been

and this was discounted when we removed

. Further, the only sequence in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}

is

. Hence,
\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\ast}\cap(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-})\rvert\leqslant 1
.

We now consider those sequences that are in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cap(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-})
and show that every pair of them crosses at most

(a_{i}),(b_{i})\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cap(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-})

. We first show that neither

nor

is a
\ast
. Suppose that
a_{j}=\ast
. Since
(a_{i})\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}
, its first entry is a

,

, or

. Hence, from the definition of monotone drawing sequences, there is some

. By the minimality of

is also

(b_{i})\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}

is a

.

Since
a_{j}

are distinct and neither is
\ast

(a_{i})
is either above or below
(b_{i})
at

is below
(b_{i})
at

(a_{i})
and
(b_{i})
agree before

and
(a_{i})

. As

, the sequence obtained by appending a

, while, since

, the sequence obtained by appending a

. Similarly,
\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}\cap(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+})\rvert\leqslant g(n,k-1)

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{-}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\ast}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{B}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\lvert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rvert+2g(n,k-1)+2

\displaystyle\leqslant g(n,k)+2g(n,k-1)+2.

for all

\displaystyle g(n+1,k)

\displaystyle\leqslant g(n,k)+2g(n,k-1)+2\leqslant 2\tbinom{2n}{k+1}+4\tbinom{2n}{k}+2

where the last inequality holds as

Let

Theorem

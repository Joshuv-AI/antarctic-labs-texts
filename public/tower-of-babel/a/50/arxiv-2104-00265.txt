Schrödinger equation on non-compact symmetric spaces
Jean-Philippe Anker, Stefano Meda, Vittoria Pierfelice, Maria Vallarino and Hong-Wei Zhang

Abstract
. We establish sharp-in-time kernel and dispersive estimates for the Schrödinger equation on non-compact Riemannian symmetric spaces of any rank. Due to the particular geometry at infinity and the Kunze-Stein phenomenon, these properties are more pronounced in large time and enable us to prove the global-in-time Strichartz inequality for a larger family of admissible couples than in the Euclidean case. Consequently, we obtain the global well-posedness for the corresponding semilinear equation with lower regularity data and some scattering properties for small powers which are known to fail in the Euclidean setting. The crucial kernel estimates are achieved by combining the stationary phase method based on a subtle barycentric decomposition, a subordination formula of the Schrödinger group to the wave propagator and an improved Hadamard parametrix.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Strichartz inequality

The Strichartz inequality, which has proved to be an efficient tool
in the study of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation,
has been extensively investigated over the past five decades.
Let us start by recalling some well-known results in the Euclidean setting.
Consider the free Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)\,+\,\Delta_{x}u(t,x)\,=\,0,\qquad\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.0pt]
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{cases}

whose solution is given by the convolution of the initial data with the kernel of the Schrödinger
propagator:

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(t,\,.\,)\,=\,e^{it\Delta}\,f\,=\,f*\big\{{(4\pi{i}t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,e^{i\frac{|\,.\,|^{2}}{4t}}}\big\}.

The Strichartz inequality refers to an a priori estimate of the solution to (
1.1
):
in dimension
d\geq 2
, if a couple
(p,q)
is
admissible
, in the sense that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\,+\frac{d}{q}\,=\,\frac{d}{2}\qquad\textnormal{with}\,\,\,p,q\,\geq\,2\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,(p,q,d)\,\neq\,(2,\infty,2),

then there exists a constant
C>0
such that global solutions to (
1.1
) satisfy

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};\,L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\,\leq\,C\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Such an inequality means that, endowed with suitable space-time Lebesgue norms,
the solution to the Schrödinger equation can be estimated in terms of the
L^{2}
norm of the initial data.
This type of estimate appeared in the pioneering
works
[
Tom75
,
Seg76
,
Str77
]
around 1976 and was fully developed after Keel-Tao
completed the proof for the endpoint in 1998, see
[
KeTa98
]
.
Over these twenty years, there have been many remarkable contributions to this problem,
and we refer to
[
GiVe95
,
Caz03
,
Tao06
]
for more details.

A related fundamental problem is to figure out how the underlying geometry affects the Strichartz inequality.
This helps us to understand the behavior of solutions to the partial differential equations in the non-Euclidean background.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
and
\Delta
be the
Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}
. The question is, for which
(p,q)
and
s\geq 0
,
the following inequality holds:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}(I;\,L^{q}(\mathcal{M}))}\,\leq\,C\,\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathcal{M})}.

We say that (
1.5
) holds global-in-time if
I=\mathbb{R}
.
Here
H^{s}
denotes the standard
L^{2}
Sobolev spaces.
If (
1.5
) is available only for some
s>0
,
we say that the Strichartz inequality holds
with loss of
s
derivatives
.
The inequality (
1.4
) shows that, when
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the global-in-time Strichartz inequality holds without loss
for all
(p,q)
satisfying the admissible condition (
1.3
).
However, this is not always the case on manifolds.

Apart from some
non-trapping
manifolds
(see, for instance,
[
StTa02
,
HTW05
,
BoTz07
]
),
the Strichartz inequality is more or less understood
on
compact
manifolds.
In general, (
1.5
) cannot hold
global-in-time in the compact case.
Bourgain proved that, on the flat torus
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
if a couple
(p,q)
satisfies the admissible condition (
1.3
),
then (
1.5
) holds for
I=\mathbb{T}
and
s>\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}
,
see
[
Bou93
,
Bou93a
]
. Later, Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
BGT04
]
showed that,
on arbitrary compact Riemannian manifolds without boundary, (
1.5
) holds
with loss of
s=1/p
derivatives for any finite time interval
I
and
(p,q)

satisfying (
1.3
). This result is sharp in some particular cases,
such as on a three-dimensional sphere, where a
1/2
loss must occur in (
1.5
).

In this paper we consider
non-compact
Riemannian symmetric spaces
of
any rank
, which form an important class of non-positively curved Riemannian manifolds. Due to their exponential volume growth at infinity and the validity of the Kunze-Stein phenomenon,
one expects stronger dispersive phenomena than in the Euclidean setting,
hence better Strichartz inequality, well-posedness and scattering results.
This was indeed brought to light in the study of the Schrödinger equation on
real hyperbolic spaces, which are the simplest symmetric spaces of non-compact type
and
rank one
, see for instance
[
Pie06
,
Ban07
,
BCS08
,
AnPi09
,
IoSt09
]
.
See also
[
APV11
]
for similar results on Damek-Ricci spaces
(a class of harmonic manifolds which includes all non-compact symmetric spaces of rank one).
Extending these rank one results to higher rank is a natural but challenging problem
since the Schrödinger kernel is sensitive to the geometry of the underlying manifolds,
and the spherical Fourier analysis is well-known to be much more intricate on the higher rank
symmetric spaces. In the next subsections, we will explain in detail the difficulties involved
and share some new ideas to overcome them.
Roughly saying, we establish in the present paper a stronger global-in-time Strichartz inequality,
in the sense that the related family of admissible pairs
is
significantly larger
than the best possible in the Euclidean setting.
This is achieved by a subtle analysis of the Schrödinger kernel.
Once the Schrödinger equation is understood on globally symmetric spaces,
studying it on locally symmetric spaces is a further natural problem.
See
[
BGH10
,
FMM18
,
Wan19
]
for some first results in rank one.

1.2.
Statements of main results

We adopt the standard
TT^{*}
duality argument to establish the Strichartz inequality, see
[
Kat87
,
GiVe95
]
. This argument relies on the dispersive estimates of the Schrödinger propagator, which can be easily obtained in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
since the corresponding convolution kernel is explicitly defined. However, such a fundamental piece of information is lacking on a general manifold. Our primary task is to find sharp pointwise estimates of the Schrödinger kernel.

Consider a non-compact symmetric space
\mathbb{X}=G/K
of rank
\ell
, where
G
and
K
are suitable Lie groups. Let
d\geq 2
and
D\geq 3
be its manifold dimension and dimension at infinity (see
Section

2
for more details about these notations). We denote by
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{X}
and consider the free Schrödinger equation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)\,+\,\Delta_{x}u(t,x)\,=\,0,\qquad\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{X},\\[5.0pt]
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{cases}

whose solution is given by

\displaystyle u(t,x)\,=\,e^{it\Delta}f(x)\,=\,f*s_{t}(x)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{X}}\,dy\,f(y)\,s_{t}(y^{-1}x).

Here
s_{t}
is the bi-
K
-invariant convolution kernel of the Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta}
. As mentioned above, except for a few particular cases, for instance when
G
is complex (see
[
Gan68
]
), the kernel
s_{t}
has no explicit expression as in (
1.2
). Moreover, the expression obtained by using the inverse Abel transform on real hyperbolic spaces (see
[
AnPi09
, p.1859]
) is also unavailable in higher rank. Our main result is the the following sharp-in-time kernel estimates on non-compact symmetric spaces of any rank.

Theorem 1.1

(Pointwise kernel estimates)
.

There exist
C\!>\!0
and
N\!>\!0

such that the following estimates hold,
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
x\in\mathbb{X}:

(1.7)

\displaystyle|s_{t}(x)|\,\leq\,C\,(1+|x^{+}|)^{N}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}\,\begin{cases}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}&\textnormal{if \,}0<|t|<1,\\[5.0pt]
|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}&\textnormal{if \,}|t|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
x^{+}
denotes the radial component of
x
in the Cartan decomposition and
\rho
is the half sum of all positive roots.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that
\langle{\rho,x^{+}}\rangle>0
as
\rho
and
x^{+}
are two
\ell
-dimensional vectors in the so-called positive Weyl chamber, see
Section

2
for more details. Hence the large polynomial factor
(1+|x^{+}|)^{N}
is harmless for proving the dispersive estimates because of the exponential factor
e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}
.

In contrast to the Euclidean setting, the kernel
s_{t}
here (expressed as (
3.1
) according to the inverse spherical Fourier transform) behaves differently for small and large times and satisfies no rescaling. Let us emphasize that the existing methods in rank one fail to produce desired estimates since the
Plancherel density

|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}
occurring in (
3.1
) is not always a differential symbol in general, hence the standard stationary phase method fails. This is in fact a well-known difficulty in the study of higher rank spherical harmonic analysis, see for instance
[
BOS95
]
. As for the recent works about the wave equation on symmetric spaces
[
AnZh20
,
Zha21
]
, the Schrödinger propagator does not enjoy the finite propagation speed as the wave propagator, and a more involved analysis is required. However, by borrowing some ideas from previous works and by combining them cleverly, we can obtain
Theorem

1.1
on general symmetric spaces of non-compact type, see
Section

3
.

Once we establish these key pointwise kernel estimates, the dispersive properties of
e^{it\Delta}
follow from the Kunze-Stein phenomenon (instead of Young’s convolution inequality) and interpolation. Then we deduce the Strichartz inequality by using the
TT^{*}
argument. The proofs are not so different from the ones on real hyperbolic spaces, but we still include the details in
Section

4
for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 1.3

(Dispersive properties)
.

Let
2\leq{q},\widetilde{q}<+\infty
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that
following estimates hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{\widetilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\,\rightarrow\,L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\leq C\begin{cases}|t|^{-\max(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}})\,d}&\textnormal{if \,}0<|t|<1,\\[5.0pt]
|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}&\textnormal{if \,}|t|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{q}
and
\widetilde{q}^{\prime}
are dual indices
in the sense that
\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}^{\prime}}=1
.

.

Theorem

1

D=3

1.8
) becomes faster in higher rank, see (
2.1

1.8

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\rightarrow\,L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\leq\,C\,|t|^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})d}\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}.

In particular, the large time decay in (
1.8

1.3.
Well-posedness and scattering for the semilinear Schrödinger equation

The
fixed point argument
, which consists in finding a suitable contraction mapping defined on an appropriate Banach space, is nowadays a standard method to prove the
well-posedness
of nonlinear partial differential equations. Due to the stronger Strichartz inequality proved in
Theorem

1.5
, we are able to obtain the following better results on non-compact symmetric spaces. The proofs are adapted straightforwardly from the rank one case considered in
[
AnPi09
,
APV11
]
and are therefore omitted.

Consider the semilinear Schrödinger equation:

(NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)\,+\,\Delta_{x}u(t,x)\,=\,F(u(t,x)),\qquad\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{X},\\[5.0pt]
u(0,x)=f(x).\end{cases}

where
F
is a
power-like
nonlinearity
of order
\gamma>1
in the sense that

\displaystyle|F(u)|\,\leq\,C\,|u|^{\gamma}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad|F(u)-F(v)|\,\leq\,C\,(|u|^{\gamma-1}+|v|^{\gamma-1})\,|u-v|.

Let
H^{1}(\mathbb{X})
be the Sobolev space defined as the image of
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})
under the operator
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. We have the following well-posedness results for the semilinear Schrödinger equation (
NLS
):

•

If
1<\gamma\leq 1+\frac{4}{d}
, the Cauchy problem
(
NLS
) is globally well-posed for small

L^{2}
data.

•

If
1<\gamma<1+\frac{4}{d}
, the Cauchy problem
(
NLS
) is locally well-posed for
arbitrary

L^{2}
data. Moreover, if
F
is in addition
gauge invariant
,
namely, if
\im\{{F(u)\bar{u}}\}=0
,
then the
L^{2}
conservation of mass
implies the global well-posedness for arbitrary

L^{2}
data in this subcritical case.

•

If
1<\gamma\leq 1+\frac{4}{d-2}
, the Cauchy problem
(
NLS
) is globally well-posed for small

H^{1}
data.

•

If
1<\gamma<1+\frac{4}{d-2}
, the Cauchy problem
(
NLS
) is locally well-posed for
arbitrary

H^{1}
data. Moreover, if
F
is in addition
defocusing
,
namely, if there exists a nonnegative
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function

G
such that
F(u)=G^{\prime}(|u|^{2})u
,
then the
H^{1}
conservation of energy implies the
global well-posedness for arbitrary
H^{1}
data
in this subcritical case.

Notice that these results are better than the known ones on Euclidean spaces.
For instance, global well-posedness for small
L^{2}
initial data holds
for any exponent
1<\gamma\leq 1+\frac{4}{d}
on
\mathbb{X}
,
while on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
one must assume in addition gauge
invariance.
However, under this condition, one can handle arbitrary
L^{2}

data by using conservation laws.

The Strichartz inequality can also be used to prove
scattering
results, which means that global solutions to the
nonlinear
Schrödinger equation behave asymptotically as solutions to the
linear
equation. Specifically,
Theorem

1.5
implies the following scattering results.

•

If
1<\gamma\leq 1+\frac{4}{d}
, then the global
solution
u(t,x)
to the Cauchy problem (
NLS
)
corresponding to small
L^{2}
data satisfies the following
scattering property: there exists
u_{\pm}\in{L}^{2}(\mathbb{X})

such that

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\qquad t\,\longrightarrow\,\pm\infty.

•

If
1<\gamma\leq 1+\frac{4}{d-2}
, then the global
solution
u(t,x)
to the Cauchy problem (
NLS
)
corresponding to small
H^{1}
data satisfies the following
scattering property: there exists
u_{\pm}\in{H}^{1}(\mathbb{X})

such that

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\qquad t\,\longrightarrow\,\pm\infty.

This scattering property remains valid for
any

H^{1}
initial data in the subcritical case
1<\gamma<1+\frac{4}{d-2}
if the nonlinearity
F
is assumed to be defocusing.

Notice that, on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, these scattering properties are known to fail for small powers
\gamma\in(1,1+\frac{2}{d}]
. However, our stronger Strichartz inequality on non-compact symmetric spaces makes these properties possible in the full range.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review briefly harmonic analysis on Riemannian
symmetric spaces of non-compact type. We adopt the standard notation
and refer to
[
Hel78
,
Hel00
,
GaVa88
]
for more details.
Throughout this paper, the symbol
A\lesssim{B}
between two positive expressions means that there is a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
. The symbol
A\asymp B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

2.1.
Non-compact symmetric spaces

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group, connected, non-compact,
with finite center, and
K
be a maximal compact subgroup
of
G
. The homogeneous space
\mathbb{X}=G/K
is
a Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
be
the Cartan decomposition of the Lie algebra of
G
,
the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
induces a
K
-invariant inner product

\langle.\,,\,.\rangle

on
\mathfrak{p}
, hence a
G
-invariant
Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{X}
.
Fix a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
in

\mathfrak{p}
. The rank of
\mathbb{X}
is the dimension

\ell
of
\mathfrak{a}
.
We identify
\mathfrak{a}
with its dual
\mathfrak{a}^{*}

by means of the inner product inherited from
\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
\Sigma\subset\mathfrak{a}

be the root system of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
and
denote by
W
the Weyl group associated to
\Sigma
.
Once a positive Weyl chamber

\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}
has been selected,

\Sigma^{+}
(resp.
\Sigma_{r}^{+}
or
\Sigma_{s}^{+}
)
denotes the corresponding set of positive roots
(resp. positive reduced roots or simple roots).
Let
d
be the dimension of
\mathbb{X}
and let
D
be the
so-called dimension at infinity or pseudo-dimension of
\mathbb{X}
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\textstyle d\,=\,\ell\,+\,\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad D\,=\,\ell\,+\,2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|,

where
m_{\alpha}
is the dimension of the positive
root subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Notice that these two dimensions behave quite differently.
For example,
D=3
while
d\geq 2
is arbitrary in rank one,

D=d
if
G
is complex,
and
D>d
(actually
D=2d-\ell
) if
G
is split.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the nilpotent Lie subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\Sigma^{+}

and let
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
be the corresponding
Lie subgroup of
G
. We have the decompositions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,G\,=\,N\,(\exp\mathfrak{a})\,K\quad&\textnormal{(Iwasawa)},\\
\,G\,=\,K\,(\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}})\,K\quad&\textnormal{(Cartan)}.\end{cases}

In the Cartan decomposition, the Haar measure on
G
writes

\displaystyle\int_{G}\,dx\,f(x)\,=\,\const\,\int_{K}\,dk_{1}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\,dx^{+}\,\delta(x^{+})\,\int_{K}\,dk_{2}\,f(k_{1}(\exp x^{+})k_{2})\,,

with density

(2.2)

\displaystyle\delta(x^{+})\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,(\sinh\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle)^{m_{\alpha}}\,\asymp\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\Big\{\frac{\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}{1+\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}\Big\}^{m_{\alpha}}\,e^{2\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}\quad\forall\,x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Here
\rho\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
denotes the half sum of all positive roots
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
counted with their multiplicities
m_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\rho\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\,\alpha.

2.2.
Spherical Fourier analysis on symmetric spaces

Let
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G}/K)
be the Schwartz space
of bi-
K
-invariant functions on
G
.
The spherical Fourier transform
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\,=\,\int_{G}\,dx\,\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)\,f(x)\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{a},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K}),

where
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G/K})
denotes the spherical function
of index
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
,
which is a smooth bi-
K
-invariant eigenfunction
of all invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{X}
,
in particular of the Laplace-Beltrami operator:

-\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,=\,(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x).

The spherical functions have the following integral representation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{K}dk\ e^{\langle{i\lambda+\rho,A(kx)}\rangle}\quad\forall\,\lambda\in{\mathfrak{a}},

where
A(kx)
denotes the
\mathfrak{a}
-component
in the Iwasawa decomposition of
kx
.
They satisfy the basic estimate

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\lambda}(x)|\,\leq\,\varphi_{0}(x)\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{a},\ \forall\,x\in\,G,

where

(2.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\exp{x^{+}})\,\asymp\,\Big\{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}(1+\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle)\Big\}\,e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}\quad\forall\,x\in G.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
the subspace
of
W
-invariant functions in the Schwartz space

\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is an
isomorphism between
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K})

and
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
.
The inverse spherical Fourier transform is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle f(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c(\lambda)}|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\quad\forall\,x\in{G},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W},

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant depending only
on the geometry of
\mathbb{X}
.
By using the Gindikin-Karpelevič formula of
the Harish-Chandra
\mathbf{c}
-function
(see
[
Hel00
]
or
[
GaVa88
]
),
we can write the Plancherel density as

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\,|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle)|^{-2},

with

\displaystyle\textstyle c_{\alpha}(v)\,=\,\overbrace{\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle})}\,\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}}^{C_{\alpha}}\,\frac{\Gamma(iv)}{\Gamma(iv+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}v+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}v+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}.

Notice that
|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}|^{-2}
is an inhomogeneous
differential symbol on
\mathbb{R}
of order
m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}
,
for every
\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}
. Hence
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}

is a product of one-dimensional symbols, but not a symbol on

\mathfrak{a}
in general.
It satisfies indeed

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\lesssim\,|\lambda|^{D-\ell}&\textnormal{if \,}|\lambda|\leq 1,\\[5.0pt]
|\nabla_{\mathfrak{a}}^{k}\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\lesssim\,|\lambda|^{d-\ell}&\textnormal{if \,}|\lambda|\geq 1,\end{cases}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The estimate away from the origin means that one cannot obtain any additional decay from general derivatives, hence the standard stationary phase method fails. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce the following barycentric decomposition.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a smooth partition of unity

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in W}\,\sum_{1\leq{j}\leq\ell}\chi_{w.S_{j}}\,=\,1\qquad\textnormal{on}\qquad\mathfrak{a}\smallsetminus\{0\}

consisting of homogeneous symbols
\chi_{w.S_{j}}
of order
0
, and some nonzero vectors
w.\Lambda_{j}\in\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}
such that for all
w\in{W}
and
1\leq{j}\leq\ell
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle|\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle|\asymp|\lambda|\qquad\,\forall\,\lambda\in\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}

and the following dichotomy holds: for every
\alpha\in\Sigma
,

•

either
\langle\alpha,w.\Lambda_{j}\rangle=0
,

•

or
|\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle|\asymp|\lambda|
for all
\lambda\in\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}
.

This theorem allows us to split up the Cartan subspace
\mathfrak{a}
into several subcones
\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}
by using some appropriate cut-off functions
\chi_{w.S_{j}}
. In each subcone, we can choose a vector
w.\Lambda_{j}
such that if a root
\alpha
is not orthogonal to
w.\Lambda_{j}
, then the inner product between
\alpha
and any vector
\lambda
in this subcone is comparable to
|\lambda|
, see
[
AnZh20
, Subsection 2.2]
for more details. Thanks to this decomposition, the Plancherel density can be handled as if it were a differential symbol,
provided that we differentiate it in
\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}
along the well-chosen vector
w.\Lambda_{j}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

For every
w\in{W}
,
1\leq{j}\leq\ell
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{k}|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{d-\ell-k})\qquad\,\forall\lambda\in\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an arbitrary root in
\Sigma
. If
\langle\alpha,w.\Lambda_{j}\rangle=0
, then all terms

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{k}\,|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle)|^{-2}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

vanish. Otherwise, we deduce from the previous dichotomy that

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{k}\,|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle)|^{-2}\big|\,\lesssim\,(1+|\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle|)^{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}-k}\,\asymp\,(1+|\lambda|)^{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}-k}

for all
\lambda\in\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}}
, since
|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}|^{-2}
is an inhomogeneous differential symbol of order
m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}
. We conclude by using (
2.1
) and (
2.6
).
∎

3.
Pointwise estimates of the Schrödinger kernel

For simplicity, we consider in this section the shifted
Schrödinger propagator
e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2}}
with

\mathbf{D}=\sqrt{-\Delta-|\rho|^{2}}
, and denote by

s_{t}
its bi-
K
-invariant convolution kernel.
By using the inverse formula of the spherical Fourier transform,
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle s_{t}(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}^{*},\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{X}.

As usual, such an oscillatory integral makes sense
by applying standard procedures
(as a limit of convergent integrals and/or
after performing several integrals by parts).
We will study (
3.1
) differently,
depending whether
|t|
is large or small.
Let us begin with the easier case where
|t|
is large.

3.1.
Large time kernel estimate

Assume that
|t|\geq 1
. In this case, we establish the
following pointwise kernel estimate, by using the
standard stationary phase method based on the elementary
estimate (
2.7
).

Theorem 3.1
.

There exist an integer
N>\max\{{d,D}\}
and a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|s_{t}(x)|\,\leq\,C\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,{\textstyle\big(1+|x|\big)^{N}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\qquad\forall\,|t|\geq 1,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{X}.

Proof.

By using the integral expression (
2.3
)
of the spherical function, we write

(3.2)

\displaystyle s_{t}(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{K}\,dk\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(kx)}\rangle}\,\underbrace{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,e^{i\langle{\lambda,A(kx)}\rangle}}_{I(t,A(kx))}

where
A(kx)
denotes the
\mathfrak{a}
-component
in the Iwasawa decomposition of
kx
, which
satisfies
|A(kx)|\leq|x|
and which we abbreviate by

A
in the sequel.

Theorem

3.1
will follow from the estimate

(3.3)

\displaystyle|I(t,A)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,{\textstyle\big(1+|A|\big)^{N}}.

Let us split up

\displaystyle I(t,A)\,=\,I_{0}(t,A)\,+\,I_{\infty}(t,A)\,=\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)\,\dots\,+\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,\dots

where
\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)=\chi(\sqrt{|t|}|\lambda|)

is a radial cut-off function such that

\supp\chi_{t}^{0}\subset\,B(0,2|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}})
,

\chi_{t}^{0}=1
on
B(0,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}})

and
\chi_{t}^{\infty}=1-\chi_{t}^{0}
.
On the one hand, by using (
2.7
),
we easily estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle|I_{0}(t,A)|\,\lesssim\,\int_{|\lambda|\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}.

On the other hand, after performing
N
integrations by
parts based on

(3.5)

\displaystyle\textstyle e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,=\,-\frac{1}{2it}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\,\frac{\lambda_{j}}{|\lambda|^{2}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}},

we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,A)\,=\,(2it)^{-N}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,\Big\{{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\,\circ\,\frac{\lambda_{j}}{|\lambda|^{2}}}\Big\}^{N}\Big\{{\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{i\langle{\lambda,A}\rangle}}\Big\}.

Assume that

•

N_{0}
derivatives are applied to the cut-off
function
\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}})
,

•

N_{1}
derivatives are applied to the factors

\frac{\lambda_{j}}{|\lambda|^{2}}
, which produces

\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{-N-N_{1}})
,

•

N_{2}
derivatives are applied to the Plancherel density
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}
,
which is not a symbol in general and which produces

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{D-\ell})&\textnormal{if \,}|\lambda|\leq 1,\\[5.0pt]
\,\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{d-\ell})&\textnormal{if \,}|\lambda|\geq 1,\end{cases}

•

N_{3}
derivatives are applied to the exponential factor
e^{i\langle{\lambda,A}\rangle}
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|A|^{N_{3}})
,

with
N_{0}+N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N
.
If some derivatives hit the cut-off function
\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)
,
i.e., if
N_{0}\geq 1
, then the integral reduces to a spherical shell where

|\lambda|\asymp|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and the contribution
to (
3.6
) is estimated by

(3.7)

\displaystyle|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{1}}{2}}\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,|A|^{N_{3}}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,{\textstyle\big(1+|A|\big)^{N}},

since
|t|\geq 1
.
If
N_{0}=0
, then

\displaystyle|I_{\infty}(t,A)|\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-N}\,\int_{|\lambda|\gtrsim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\lambda\,\big|\nabla_{\lambda}^{N_{2}}\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\big|\,|\lambda|^{-N-N_{1}}\,|A|^{N_{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-N}\,|A|^{N_{3}}\,\Big\{{\int_{|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|\lambda|\leq 1}\,d\lambda\,|\lambda|^{D-\ell-N-N_{1}}\,+\,\int_{|\lambda|\geq 1}\,d\lambda\,|\lambda|^{d-\ell-N-N_{1}}\,}\Big\}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,(1+|A|)^{N}+|t|^{-N}\,(1+|A|)^{N}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,(1+|A|)^{N}

provided that
N>d
and
N\geq\frac{D}{2}
.
In conclusion, (
3.3
) follows
from (
3.4
), (
3.7
)
and (
3.1
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The analysis carried out in the proof of
Theorem

3.1

yields at best the following small time estimate

(3.9)

\displaystyle|s_{t}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-d}\,(1+|x|)^{d}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\qquad\forall\,0<|t|<1,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{X}.

3.2.
Small time kernel estimate

Assume that
0<|t|<1
.
Our aim is to reduce the negative power
|t|^{-d}

in (
3.9
) to
|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}
.
We shall use different tools, depending on the size
of
\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
.
If
\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
is small, we decompose the
Weyl chamber into several subcones according to the
barycentric decompositions described in

Theorem

2.1
, and perform in each subcone
several integrations by parts along a well chosen direction.
If
\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
is large, we express
in addition the Schrödinger propagator in terms of
the wave propagator
and use the Hadamard parametrix.

Theorem 3.3
.

The following estimate holds, for
0<|t|<1

and
|x|\leq\sqrt{|t|}
:

\displaystyle|s_{t}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x).

Proof.

By resuming the notation in the proof
of
Theorem

3.1
, we have

\displaystyle s_{t}(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{K}\,dk\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(kx)}\rangle}\,\big(I_{0}(t,A)+I_{\infty}(t,A)\big).

Clearly,

(3.10)

\displaystyle|I_{0}(t,A)|\,=\,\Big|\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,e^{i\langle{\lambda,A}\rangle}\Big|\lesssim\int_{|\lambda|\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

In order to estimate

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,A)\,=\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,e^{i\langle{\lambda,A}\rangle},

we split up

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,A)\,=\,\sum_{w\in W}\,\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\,\underbrace{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,e^{i\langle\lambda,A\rangle}}_{I_{w.S_{j}}(t,x)}.

according to the barycentric decomposition
(
2.8
).
Next, we study
I_{w.S_{j}}(t,x)
by performing

N
integrations by parts based on

\displaystyle e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\,=\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2it}\,\tfrac{1}{\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle}\,\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}},

which yields

\displaystyle I_{w.S_{j}}(t,x)\,=\,(2it)^{-N}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,e^{-it|\lambda|^{2}}\Big\{\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}\circ\tfrac{1}{\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle}\Big\}^{N}\,\Big\{\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{i\langle\lambda,A\rangle}\Big\}.

As in the the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, we assume that

•

N_{0}
derivatives are applied to the cut-off function

\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)
:

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{N_{0}}\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}),

•

N_{1}
derivatives are applied to the factors

\tfrac{1}{\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle}
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{-N-N_{1}})
,

•

N_{2}
derivatives are applied to the factor

\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)
, which is a homogeneous
symbol of order
0
:

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{N_{2}}\,\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{-N_{2}}),

•

N_{3}
derivatives are applied to the factor

e^{i\langle\lambda,A\rangle}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{N_{3}}\,e^{i\langle\lambda,A\rangle}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|A|^{N_{3}}),

•

N_{4}
derivatives are applied to the Plancherel density

|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}
(see
Corollary

2.2
):

\displaystyle\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}^{N_{4}}\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{d-\ell-N_{4}}),

with
N_{0}+N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}+N_{4}=N
.
Therefore, if no derivative hits the cut-off function

\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)
, i.e., if
N_{0}=0
, then

\displaystyle|I_{w.S_{j}}(t,x)|\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-N}\,\int_{|\lambda|\gtrsim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\lambda\,|\lambda|^{-N-N_{1}-N_{2}+d-\ell-N_{4}}\,|A|^{N_{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-N}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N_{1}}{2}+\frac{N_{2}}{2}+\frac{N_{4}}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{3}}{2}}\,\leq\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}

provided that
N>d
.
If
N_{0}\geq 1
, then the integral
is reduced to a spherical shell where

|\lambda|\asymp|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and hence

\displaystyle|I_{w.S_{j}}(t,x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-N}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N_{1}}{2}+\frac{N_{2}}{2}+\frac{N_{4}}{2}}\,|A|^{N_{3}}\,\leq\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}},

since
|A|\leq|x|\leq\sqrt{|t|}
.
Together with (
3.10
),
we conclude that
|I(t,A)|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}

and

\displaystyle|s_{t}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x),

for all
0<|t|<1
and
x\in\mathbb{X}

such that
|x|\leq\sqrt{|t|}
.
∎

The above proof shows that, for every
\lambda\in(\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}})\cap(\supp\chi_{t}^{\infty})
,
the Plancherel density
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}

behaves like an inhomogeneous symbol of order
d-\ell

if we differentiate it along the direction
w.\Lambda_{j}
.
When
|x|>\sqrt{|t|}
,
we write the Schrödinger propagator in terms of the wave propagator according to the subordination principle, and use an improved Hadamard parametrix.
Let us express the Schrödinger propagator

\displaystyle e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2}}\,=\,\underbrace{\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sign{(t)}}}_{C_{1}}|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\cos(s\mathbf{D})

in terms of the wave propagator and correspondingly

(3.11)

\displaystyle s_{t}(x)\,=\,C_{1}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\Phi_{s}(x)

for their bi-
K
-invariant convolution kernels.
On the one hand, by finite propagation speed,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(x)\,=\,0\qquad\textnormal{if}\quad|x|>|s|.

On the other hand, recall the Hadamard parametrix

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(\exp{H})\,=\,J(H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,|s|\,\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\,4^{-k}\,U_{k}(H)\,R_{+}^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}(s^{2}-|H|^{2})\qquad\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}^{*},\,\forall\,H\in\mathfrak{p},

where
J
denotes the Jacobian of the exponential map

\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow{G/K}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\textstyle J(H)\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,\big(\frac{\sinh\langle\alpha,H\rangle}{\langle\alpha,H\rangle}\big)^{m_{\alpha}}\qquad\forall\,H\in\mathfrak{a}^{+},

and
\{R_{+}^{z}\,|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}
denotes the analytic family
of Riesz distributions on
\mathbb{R}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\,R_{+}^{z}(r)\,=\,\begin{cases}\,\Gamma(z)^{-1}\,r^{z-1}&\textnormal{if \,}r>0\\
\;0&\textnormal{if \,}r\leq 0\end{cases}\qquad\forall\,\re z>0.

This parametrix was constructed and used in various
settings, see for instance
[
Ber77
,
Hor94
,
CGM01
]
.
We refer to
[
AnZh20
, Appendix B]
for the details about
the wave propagator
\cos{(t\sqrt{-\Delta})}

associated to the unshifted Laplacian
\Delta

on non-compact symmetric spaces.
Notice that the same results hold for
\cos{(t\mathbf{D})}
.
Specifically (
3.13
) is an
asymptotic expansion

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(\exp{H})\,=\,J(H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,|s|\,\sum_{k=0}^{[d/2]}\,4^{-k}\,U_{k}(H)\,R_{+}^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}(s^{2}-|H|^{2})\,+\,E(s,H)

where the coefficients
U_{k}
are
\Ad{K}
-invariant
smooth functions on
\mathfrak{p}
, which are bounded
together with their derivatives, while the remainder
satisfies

(3.15)

\displaystyle|E(s,H)|\,\lesssim\,(1+|s|)^{3(\frac{d}{2}+1)}\,e^{-\langle\rho,H\rangle}\qquad\forall{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{*},\,\forall{H}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Let us split up

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,ds\,=\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{0}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,+\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{1}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,+\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})

in (
3.11
) by means of a smooth
partition of unity

on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\supp\chi_{0}\,\subset\,(-1,1),\\[5.0pt]
\supp\chi_{1}\,\subset\,(-2C_{2},-\frac{1}{2})\,\cup\,(\frac{1}{2},2C_{2}),\\[5.0pt]
\supp\chi_{\infty}\,\subset\,(-\infty,-C_{2})\,\cup\,(C_{2},+\infty)\end{cases}

where the choice of
C_{2}>1
will be specified later.
Then the contribution of the first integral vanishes
according to (
3.12
) and we are
left with

\displaystyle s_{t}(x)\,=\,\underbrace{C_{1}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{1}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\Phi_{s}(x)}_{s_{t}^{1}(x)}\,+\,\underbrace{C_{1}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\Phi_{s}(x)}_{s_{t}^{\infty}(x)}

where
s_{t}^{1}(x)
and
s_{t}^{\infty}(x)

are bi-
K
-invariant.
Let us first study
s_{t}^{\infty}(x)
by using again
the barycentric decomposition. In comparison with the
proof of
Theorem

3.3
, we have now
|x|>\sqrt{|t|}

and there is an additional integral over
s\in(1,\infty)

to control. Let us state the theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

The following estimate holds, for all
0<|t|<1

and
|x|>\sqrt{|t|}:

\displaystyle|s_{t}^{\infty}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x).

Proof.

We express

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\infty}(x)\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{0}\,C_{1}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-is|\lambda|}

by evenness and by expressing the wave kernel
\Phi_{s}

by means of the inverse spherical Fourier transform.
Let us split up

s_{t}^{\infty}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{0}\,C_{1}\,(s_{t}^{\infty,0}+s_{t}^{\infty,\infty})
,
where

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\infty,0}(x)\,=\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\underbrace{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-is|\lambda|}}_{I_{0}(s,t,x)}

and

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\infty,\infty}(x)\,=\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\underbrace{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-is|\lambda|}}_{I_{\infty}(s,t,x)}.

Recall that

\chi_{t}^{0}(\lambda)=\chi(\sqrt{|t|}|\lambda|)

is a radial cut-off function such that

\supp\chi_{t}^{0}\subset\,B(0,2|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}})
,

\chi_{t}^{0}=1
on
B(0,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}})

and
\chi_{t}^{\infty}=1-\chi_{t}^{0}
.

Estimate of
s_{t}^{\infty,0}
.

Notice that the obvious estimate

|I_{0}(s,t,x)|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}|\varphi_{0}(x)

is not enough for our purpose. We need indeed to
compensate on the one hand the factor
|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and to get on the other hand enough decay in
|s|

to ensure the convergence of the external integral.
To this end, we perform two integrations by parts based on

(3.16)

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,=\,-\tfrac{2it}{s}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}

and obtain this way

(3.17)

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\infty,0}(x)\,=\,-4\,|t|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,ds\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\big(\tfrac{1}{s}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\big)\,\big\{\tfrac{1}{s}\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,I_{0}(s,t,x)\big\}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\big|(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}})^{k}\,I_{0}(s,t,x)\big|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d+k}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

If any derivative hits
\chi_{\infty}(\frac{s}{|x|})
in (
3.17
),
the integral reduces to two intervals where
|s|\asymp|x|
,
and the corresponding contribution is estimated by

\displaystyle|t|^{-\frac{d-3}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\,\int_{|s|\asymp|x|}\,ds\,\{s^{-2}\,|x|^{-2}\,+\,s^{-3}\,|x|^{-1}\,+\,s^{-2}\,|x|^{-1}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x),

since
|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}<|x|
.
Otherwise we end up with the estimate

\displaystyle|t|^{-\frac{d-3}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\,\int_{|s|\gtrsim|x|}\,ds\,\{s^{-4}\,+\,s^{-3}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,+\,s^{-2}\,|t|^{-1}\}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x).

In conclusion,

\displaystyle|s_{t}^{\infty,0}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x),

for all
0<|t|<1
and
x\in\mathbb{X}
such that

|x|>\sqrt{|t|}
.

Estimate of
s_{t}^{\infty,\infty}
.

Let us turn to

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\infty,\infty}(x)\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{\infty}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,{I_{\infty}(s,t,x)}.

We will prove the following estimate, for any integer
N>d
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle|I_{\infty}(s,t,x)|\lesssim\,|s|^{-N}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\qquad\forall\,|s|\geq C_{2}|x|.

Then, as
|x|>\sqrt{|t|\,}
, we conclude easily that

(3.19)

\displaystyle|s_{t}^{\infty,\infty}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\,\int_{|s|\gtrsim|x|}ds\,|s|^{-N}\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{|t|}}{|x|}\big)^{N-1}\,\varphi_{0}(x)\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(x).

In order to establish (
3.18
),
we express

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(s,t,x)\,

\displaystyle=\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-is|\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\,\int_{K}dk\,e^{-\langle\rho,A\rangle}\sum_{w\in W}\,\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\,\underbrace{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,d\lambda\,\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,e^{-i(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}}_{I_{w.S_{j}}(s,t,A)}

by using again the integral formula (
2.3
)
and the barycentric decomposition (
2.8
).
According to (
2.9
), we can choose
C_{2}>0

such that, if
|s|\geq{C_{2}|x|}
, then

\displaystyle|\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)|\,

\displaystyle=\,\Big|s\tfrac{\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle}{|\lambda|}\,-\,\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},A}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\,|s|\,\underbrace{\tfrac{|\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},\lambda}\rangle|}{|\lambda|}}_{\gtrsim 1}-\,\underbrace{|\langle{w.\Lambda_{j},A}\rangle|}_{\lesssim\,|x|}\gtrsim\,|s|,

for every

\lambda\in(\supp\chi_{t}^{\infty})\cap(\supp\chi_{w.S_{j}})
.
Under these assumptions, the phase function

\lambda\mapsto\,s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle

has no critical point along the direction
w.\Lambda_{j}
.
By performing
N
integrations by parts based on

\displaystyle e^{-i(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}\,=\,\tfrac{i}{\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}\;\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}\,e^{-i(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)},

we write

\displaystyle I_{w.S_{j}}(s,t,A)\,=\,(is)^{-N}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,

\displaystyle d\lambda\,e^{-i(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}

\displaystyle\Big\{\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}\circ\tfrac{s}{\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}\Big\}^{N}\,\Big\{\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)\,\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\Big\}.

Assume that

•

N_{0}
derivatives are applied to the cut-off function
\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}})
,

•

N_{1}
derivatives are applied to the factors

\tfrac{s}{\partial_{w.\Lambda_{j}}(s|\lambda|-\langle\lambda,A\rangle)}
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{-N_{1}})
,

•

N_{2}
derivatives are applied to the cut-off functions
\chi_{w.S_{j}}(\lambda)
,
which produces
\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{-N_{2}})
,

•

N_{3}
derivatives are applied to the Plancherel density
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}
,
which produces

\mathrm{O}(|\lambda|^{d-\ell-N_{3}})
,

with
N_{0}+N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N
.
Again, if some derivatives hit
\chi_{t}^{\infty}(\lambda)
, i.e., if
N_{0}\geq 1
,
then the integral reduces to a spherical shell where
|\lambda|\asymp\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
and its contribution is estimated by

\displaystyle|s|^{-N}\,|t|^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{1}}{2}}\,|t|^{\frac{N_{2}}{2}}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\ell}{2}+\frac{N_{3}}{2}}\,=\,|s|^{-N}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}}.

If
N_{0}=0
, then

\displaystyle|I_{w.S_{j}}(s,t,A)|\,\lesssim\,|s|^{-N}\int_{|\lambda|\gtrsim\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\lambda\,|\lambda|^{-N_{1}}\,|\lambda|^{-N_{2}}\,|\lambda|^{d-\ell-N_{3}}\,\lesssim\,|s|^{-N}\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{N}{2}}

provided that
N>d
.
This proves (
3.18
) and hence
(
3.19
).
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The following estimate holds, for all
0<|t|<1
and
|x|>\sqrt{|t|}
:

\displaystyle|s_{t}^{1}(x)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,(1+|x|)^{\frac{3}{2}d+4}\,e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}.

Proof.

Since
s_{t}^{1}
is bi-
K
-invariant, we have

\displaystyle s_{t}^{1}(x)\,

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{C_{1}}{2}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,J(x^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{[d/2]}\,4^{-k}\,U_{k}(x^{+})\,\overbrace{\int_{0}^{+\infty}d(s^{2})\,\chi_{1}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,R_{+}^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}(s^{2}\!-|x|^{2})}^{I_{k}(t,|x|)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\tfrac{C_{1}}{2}\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\underbrace{\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{1}(\tfrac{s}{|x|})\,e^{\frac{i}{4t}s^{2}}\,E(s,x^{+})}_{\widetilde{E}(t,|x|)}

according to (
3.14
).
On the one hand, the remainder estimate

(3.20)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{E}(t,|x|)|\,\lesssim\,|x|\,(1+|x|)^{3(\frac{d}{2}+1)}\,e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}

follows from (
3.15
).
On the other hand, we claim that

(3.21)

\displaystyle|I_{k}(t,|x|)|\,\lesssim\,|t|^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}

if
|x|\!>\!\sqrt{|t|\,}\!>\!0
.
Let us first prove (
3.21
)
when
d
is odd. By a change of variables and by using the fact that

R_{+}^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}(s\!-\!1)=\bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\bigr)^{\!\frac{d-1}{2}-k}R_{+}^{0}(s\!-\!1)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{k}(t,|x|)|\,

\displaystyle=\,|x|^{2k-d+1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,\chi_{1}(\sqrt{s})\,e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s}\,R_{+}^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}(s-1)

\displaystyle=\,|x|^{2k-d+1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds\,R_{+}^{0}(s\!-\!1)\,\bigl(-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\bigr)^{\!\frac{d-1}{2}-k}\,\big|\{{\chi_{1}(\sqrt{s})\,e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s}}\bigr\}.

As
R_{+}^{0}(s\!-\!1)
is the Dirac measure at
s\!=\!1
,
we conclude that

\displaystyle I_{k}(t,|x|)\,=\,|x|^{2k-d+1}\,\bigl(-\tfrac{i}{4}\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{t}\bigr)^{\!\frac{d-1}{2}-k}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(|t|^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}).

When
d
is even, we obtain similarly

\displaystyle|I_{k}(t,|x|)|\,=\,\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,|x|^{2k-d+1}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\tfrac{ds}{\sqrt{s-1}}\,\bigl(-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\bigr)^{\frac{d}{2}-k}\,\bigl\{{\chi_{1}(\sqrt{s})\,e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s}}\bigr\},

which is a linear combination of expressions

\displaystyle t^{j+k-\frac{d}{2}}\,|x|^{1-2j}\underbrace{\int_{1}^{+\infty}\,\tfrac{ds}{\sqrt{s-1}}\,\theta_{j}(s)\,e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s}}_{J_{j}(t,|x|)}

where
0\leq{j}\leq\frac{d}{2}-k
and

\theta_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}{(\mathbb{R})}

with
\supp\theta_{j}\subset(-4C_{2}^{2},4C_{2}^{2})
.
Notice that the elementary estimate
J_{j}(t,|x|)=\mathrm{O}(1)
,
together with the assumption
|x|\!>\!\sqrt{|t|\,}
implies that

|I_{k}(t,|x|)|\lesssim|x|\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}+k},

which might be enough for our purpose as long as
k\!>\!0
.
The case
k\!=\!0
requires actually a more careful analysis.
Let us show that

\displaystyle J_{j}(t,|x|)\,\lesssim\,\tfrac{\sqrt{|t|}}{|x|}

by splitting up

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}ds\,=\,\int_{1}^{1+\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}}ds\,+\,\int_{1+\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}}^{+\infty}ds

in the definition of
J_{j}(t,|x|)
.
The contribution of the first integral is easily estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\,1+\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}}ds\,\tfrac{ds}{\sqrt{s-1}}\,=\,2\,\sqrt{s\!-\!1}\,\Big|_{s=1}^{s=1+\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}}=\,2\,\tfrac{\sqrt{|t|}}{|x|}\,.

After performing an integration by parts based on

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s}\,=\,-\,i\,\tfrac{4t}{|x|^{2}}\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\,e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}s},

the contribution of the second integral is also estimated by

\displaystyle\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}\int_{1+\tfrac{|t|}{|x|^{2}}}^{\,4C_{2}^{2}}ds\,\bigl\{(s\!-\!1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+(s\!-\!1)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\bigr\}\,\lesssim\,\tfrac{\sqrt{|t|}}{|x|}

under the assumption
|x|\!>\!\sqrt{|t|\,}
.
Thus (
3.21
) holds as well
when
d
is even. In conclusion,

\displaystyle|s_{t}^{1}(x)|\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,J(x^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,+\,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,|x|\,(1+|x|)^{3(\frac{d}{2}+1)}\,e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,(1+|x|)^{\frac{3}{2}d+4}e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}

when
0<|t|<1
and
x\in\mathbb{X}
satisfies
|x|>\sqrt{|t|\,}
.
∎

In summary, we have divided our kernel analysis into three parts
and deduced
Theorem

1.1
from
Theorem

3.1
,

Theorem

3.3
and
Theorem

3.5
.
Notice that the method used to prove small time kernel
estimates can be also used for large time.

4.
Dispersive estimates and Strichartz inequalities on symmetric spaces

Once pointwise kernel estimates are available on symmetric spaces,
one can deduce dispersive properties for the corresponding propagator,
by using an interpolation argument based on the Kunze-Stein phenomenon.
The following bi-
K
-invariant version is a straightforward generalization of
[
APV11
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\kappa
be a reasonable bi-
K
-invariant function on
G
. Then

\displaystyle\|\cdot*\,\kappa\,\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\leq\Big\{\int_{G}dx\,\varphi_{0}(x)\,|\kappa(x)|^{\frac{q}{2}}\,\Big\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

for every
q\in[2,+\infty)
. In the limit case, we have

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\cdot*\,\kappa\,\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}=\sup_{x\in{G}}|\kappa(x)|.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

At the endpoint
q=\widetilde{q}=2
,
t\mapsto\,e^{it\Delta}

is a one-parameter group of unitary operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{X})
:

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\,=\,1.

For
2<q<\infty
, we deduce from estimates (
1.7
) and (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\|s_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,\omega(t)\,\Big\{{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,dx^{+}\,(1+|x^{+}|)^{qN}\,e^{-(q-2)\langle{\rho,x^{+}}\rangle}}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\lesssim\,\omega(t)

where

\displaystyle\omega(t)\,=\,\begin{cases}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}&\textnormal{if \,}0<|t|<1,\\[5.0pt]
|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}&\textnormal{if \,}|t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Moreover, since
s_{t}
is the bi-
K
-invariant convolution kernel of the propagator
e^{it\Delta}
,
we deduce, by combining the above lemma with (
1.7
), (
2.2
), and (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}^{\frac{q}{2}}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,dx^{+}\,\delta(x^{+})\,\varphi_{0}(\exp{x^{+}})\,|s_{t}(\exp{x^{+}})|^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\omega(t)^{\frac{q}{2}}\,\underbrace{\Big\{{\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,dx^{+}\,(1+|x^{+}|)^{\frac{qN+D-\ell}{2}}\,e^{-(\frac{q}{2}-1)\langle{\rho,x^{+}}\rangle}}\Big\}}_{=\,\mathrm{O}(1)}.

We conclude by interpolation between

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,\|s_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}},\\[5.0pt]
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,\|s_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}},\\[5.0pt]
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\,=\,1,\end{cases}

when
|t|\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,\|s_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}},\\[5.0pt]
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,\|s_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}},\\[5.0pt]
\|e^{it\Delta}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})\rightarrow{L}^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}},\end{cases}

when
|t|\geq 1
.
∎

Finally, we establish our Strichartz inequality (
1.11
) by using the
TT^{*}
argument. Notice that the solution to the Schrödinger equation (
1.10
) is given by the Duhamel formula:

\displaystyle u(t,x)\,=\,e^{it\Delta}\,f(x)\,-\,i\int_{0}^{t}\,ds\,e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\,F(s,x).

Consider the operator
T
and its formal adjoint
T^{*}
:

\displaystyle Tf(t,x)\,=\,e^{it\Delta}f(x)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad T^{*}F(x)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,ds\,e^{-is\Delta}\,F(s,x).

By duality,
T
is bounded from
L_{x}^{2}
to
L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}
if and only if

T^{*}
is bounded from
L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}
to
L_{x}^{2}
.
Equivalently, the operator

(4.1)

\displaystyle TT^{*}F(t,x)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,ds\,e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\,F(s,x)

is bounded from
L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}
to
L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}
.
We prove the latter for all
(p,q)
in the admissible triangle (
1.9
). The Strichartz inequality (
1.11
) follows from the Christ-Kiselev lemma, see
[
ChKi01
]
or
[
Tao06
, Section 2.3]
.

Proof.

The endpoint
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=(0,\frac{1}{2})
is settled by
L^{2}
conservation and
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d})
can be handled by using the method carried out in
[
KeTa98
]
. Let us focus on the non-endpoint cases where
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\frac{n}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n}<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{2}
.
According to the dispersive estimates (
1.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|TT^{*}F\|_{L^{p}(I;L^{q}(\mathbb{X}))}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,ds\,\big\|e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\,F(s,\,.\,)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\Big\|\int_{|t-s|\leq 1}ds\,|t-s|^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})d}\,\big\|F(s,\,.\,)\big\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I)}

\displaystyle\quad+\,\Big\|\int_{|t-s|\geq 1}ds\,|t-s|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,\big\|F(s,\,.\,)\big\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I)}.

On the one hand, the convolution kernel
|t-s|^{-\frac{D}{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|t-s|\geq 1\}}
on
\mathbb{R}
defines a bounded operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
for all
2\leq{p}\leq\infty
. On the other hand, the convolution kernel
|t-s|^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})d}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|t-s|\leq 1\}}
is bounded from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
for all
2\leq{p}<\infty
such that
0\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}\leq 1-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})d
, or in other words, when
p
satisfies
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\frac{n}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We do not take full advantage of our strong large time decay
|t|^{-\frac{D}{2}}
in the dispersive estimates (
1.8
). Indeed, the convolution kernel
|t-s|^{-1-\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|t-s|\geq 1\}}
defines a bounded operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
2\leq{p}\leq\infty
.

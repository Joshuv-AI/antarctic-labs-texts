Random points are good for universal discretization
F. Dai, V. Temlyakov

Abstract
There has been significant progress in the study of sampling discretization of integral norms for both a designated finite-dimensional function space and a finite collection of such function spaces (universal discretization). Sampling discretization results turn out to be very useful in various applications, particularly in sampling recovery. Recent sampling discretization results typically provide existence of good sampling points for discretization. In this paper, we show that independent and identically distributed random points provide good universal discretization with high probability. Furthermore, we demonstrate that a simple greedy algorithm based on those points that are good for universal discretization provides excellent sparse recovery results in the square norm.

Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP).

2
A conditional theorem on universal sampling discretization

We first recall the definition of entropy numbers in Banach spaces. Let
A
be a compact subset of a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
.
Given a positive number
\varepsilon
, the covering number
N_{\varepsilon}(A,X)
of the set
A
in the space
X
is defined to be the smallest positive integer
n
for which there exist
n
elements
g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}\in A
such that

\max_{f\in A}\min_{1\leq j\leq n}\|f-g_{j}\|\leq\varepsilon.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(A,X)

We begin this section with the observation that the universal discretization of the
L_{2}
norm is closely connected with the concept of Restricted Isometry Property (RIP), which is very important in
compressed sensing.

Let
U=\{\mathbf{u}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be a system of column vectors from

or the system

and

if for any subset

with cardinality

Let

with the probability measure

For a dictionary

Suppose that
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
is an orthonormal system. On one hand,
for any

2

|\Gamma(M)|\leq v^{c(a,d)}
, with
the bound for
f\in\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A}

\|f-A_{v,\xi}(f)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mu_{\xi})}\leq C(a,b,d)v^{-a-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(a+b)}.

For completeness we
will give a proof of a somewhat more general statement at the end of this section.

Let
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
be given and let
M
be from Lemma
5.1
. Consider the orthonormal
basis
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}:=\{e^{i(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}\}_{\mathbf{k}\in\Gamma(M)}
of the space
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma(M))
.
Then Lemma
5.1
, Corollary
5.1
and the bound (
5.10
) imply the following Theorem
5.4
.

Theorem 5.4
.

There exist two constants
c^{\prime}(a,d)
and
C^{\prime}(a,b,d)
such that for any
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have that for
a>0
,
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\varrho_{m}^{o}(\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A},L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\leq C^{\prime}(a,b,d)v^{-a-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(a+b)}

(5.11)

provided that
m
is an integer satisfying

m\geq c^{\prime}(a,d)v(\log(2v))^{3}.

Theorem
5.4
gives the following bound for the classical classes
\mathbf{W}^{r}_{p}
of functions with bounded mixed derivative (see
[
17
]
, p.130, for their definition and
[
6
]
for some recovery results).

Corollary 5.2
.

There exist two constants
c^{\prime}(r,d,p)
and
C^{\prime}(r,d,p)
such that for any
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have that for
r>1/p
and
1<p<2
,

\varrho_{m}^{o}(\mathbf{W}^{r}_{p},L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\leq C^{\prime}(r,d,p)v^{-r+1/p-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(r+1-2/p)}

(5.12)

provided that

m\geq c^{\prime}(r,d,p)v(\log(2v))^{3}.

The authors of
[
9
]
(see Corollary 4.16 in v2) proved the following interesting bound for
1<p<2
,
r>1/p
and
m\geq c(r,d,p)v(\log(2v))^{3}
,

\varrho_{m}^{o}(\mathbf{W}^{r}_{p},L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\leq C(r,d,p)v^{-r+1/p-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(r+1-2/p)+1/2}

(5.13)

The bound (
5.12
) is better than (
5.13
) by the
(\log v)^{-1/2}
factor. The proof of (
5.13
) in
[

]
is based on known results from
[

]
on sparse trigonometric approximation in the uniform norm. We proved (

) by using inequality (
5.8
) and estimating the sparse trigonometric approximation in the norm
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mu_{\xi})
, which is weaker than the uniform norm.

We conclude this section with a proof of Lemma
5.1
. Indeed, we shall prove a somewhat more general result, where the trigonometric system is replaced by a more general system
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g_{\mathbf{k}}\}_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
uniformly bounded functions on a domain
\Omega
:

|g_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x})|\leq 1,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\quad\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(5.14)

As in the trigonometric case, for

f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\mathbf{k}}g_{\mathbf{k}}\ \ \text{ with }\ \ \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}|<\infty,

(5.15)

we define

\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,{\mathcal{D}}):=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rho(\mathbf{s})}a_{\mathbf{k}}g_{\mathbf{k}},\ \ f_{j}:=\sum_{\|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}=j}\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,{\mathcal{D}}),j=0,1,\cdots.

and

\|f\|_{A({\mathcal{D}})}:=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}|.

For parameters
a\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
define
\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A}({\mathcal{D}})
to be the class of all functions
f
with a representation (
5.15
) satisfying

\|f_{j}\|_{A({\mathcal{D}})}\leq 2^{-aj}(\bar{j})^{(d-1)b},\quad j=0,1,\cdots,

(5.16)

where
\bar{j}=\max\{j,1\}
.
Given a finite set
Q
of points in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote

{\mathcal{D}}(Q):=\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in Q}a_{\mathbf{k}}g_{\mathbf{k}}:\ \ a_{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \ \mathbf{k}\in Q\right\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist two constants
c(a,d)
and
C(a,b,d)
such that for any
\xi\in\Omega^{m}
and
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is a constructive method
A_{v,\xi}
based on greedy algorithms, which provides a
v
-term approximant
from
{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma(M))
,
|\Gamma(M)|\leq v^{c(a,d)}
, with
the bound for
f\in\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A}({\mathcal{D}})

\|f-A_{v,\xi}(f)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})}\leq C(a,b,d)v^{-a-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(a+b)}.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for
v\asymp 2^{n}n^{d-1}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let

f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}a_{\mathbf{k}}g_{\mathbf{k}}

be a representation of
f\in\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A}({\mathcal{D}})
satisfying (
5.16
). We approximate
f_{j}
in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})
by
a
v_{j}
-term approximant from
{\mathcal{D}}(Q_{j}):=\cup_{\|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}=j}\rho(\mathbf{s})
. The sequence
\{v_{j}\}
will be defined later.
Since
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})
is a Hilbert space and by (
5.14
)

\|g_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}|g_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x})|\leq 1,\ \mathbf{k}\in Q_{j},

(5.17)

using Theorem 2.19 of
[
14
, p. 93]
, we deduce from (
5.16
)
that for every
j
there exists an
h_{j}\in\Sigma_{v_{j}}({\mathcal{D}}(Q_{j}))
(provided by a greedy algorithm – Orthogonal Matching Pursuit)
such that

\|f_{j}-h_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})}\leq v_{j}^{-1/2}2^{-aj}(\bar{j})^{(d-1)b}.

We take
\beta\in(0,a)
(for instance,
\beta=a/2
) and specify

v_{j}:=[2^{n-\beta(j-n)}j^{d-1}],\quad j=n,n+1,\dots.

It is clear that there exists
j(\beta,d)
such that
v_{j}=0
for
j\geq j(\beta,d)n
. For
j\geq j(\beta,d)n

we set
h_{j}=0
.
In addition to
\{h_{j}\}_{j=n}^{j(\beta,d)n}
we include in the approximant

S_{n}(f,{\mathcal{D}}):=\sum_{\|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}<n}\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,{\mathcal{D}}).

Define

A_{v}(f,\beta):=S_{n}(f,{\mathcal{D}})+\sum_{j\geq n}h_{j}=S_{n}(f,{\mathcal{D}})+\sum_{j=n}^{j(\beta,d)n}h_{j}.

Then, we have built a
v
-term approximant of
f
with

v\ll 2^{n}n^{d-1}+\sum_{j\geq n}v_{j}\ll 2^{n}n^{d-1}.

The error of this approximation in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})
is bounded from above by

\|f-A_{m}(f,p,\beta)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})}\leq\sum_{j\geq n}\|f_{j}-h_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})}\leq C\sum_{j\geq n}(\bar{v}_{j})^{-1/2}2^{-aj}j^{(d-1)b}

\leq C\sum_{j\geq n}2^{-1/2(n-\beta(j-n))}j^{-(d-1)/2}2^{-aj}j^{(d-1)b}\leq C2^{-n(a+1/2)}n^{(d-1)(b-1/2)}.

This completes the proof of lemma.

∎

6
A more general version of the conditional theorem

Our goal in this section is to prove the following conditional theorem, which is the random version of Theorem 5.1 of
[
5
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}\subset L_{\infty}(\Omega)
and let
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{W}}:\ \ \|f\|_{p}\leq 1\}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume that
{\lambda}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}
for any
{\lambda}>0
, and
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}
is a compact subset of
L_{\infty}
. Let

R:=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{W}}_{p}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|.

Let
\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}
be independent random points on
\Omega
satisfying

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mu_{\xi^{k}}=\mu,

(6.1)

where
\mu_{\xi^{j}}
denotes the probability distribution of
\xi^{j}
.
Then there exist positive constants
C_{p},c_{p}
depending only on
p
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and any
integer

m\geq C_{p}\varepsilon^{-5}\left(\int_{c_{p}\varepsilon^{1/p}}^{R}u^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Bigl(\int_{u}^{R}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{c_{p}\varepsilon t}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})}{t}\,dt\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}du\right)^{2},

(6.2)

the inequalities,

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{p}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in{\mathcal{W}}_{p},

(6.3)

hold with probability

\geq 1-\varepsilon\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Bigr).

It can be easily seen that either of the following two conditions implies the condition (
6.1
):

(i)

\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}
are identically distributed according to
\mu
;

(ii)

there exists a partition
\{\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{m}\}
of
\Omega
such that
\mu(\Lambda_{j})=\frac{1}{m}
and
\xi^{j}\in\Lambda_{j}
is distributed according to
m\cdot\mu\Bigl|_{\Lambda_{j}}
for each
1\leq j\leq m
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
follows along the same line as that of Theorem 5.1 of
[
5
]
. We sketch it as follows.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}:=\{f/{\|f\|_{p}}:\ \ f\in{\mathcal{W}},\ \ \|f\|_{p}>0\}\subset{\mathcal{W}}_{p}.

Let
c^{\ast}=c_{p}^{\ast}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
be a sufficiently small constant depending only on
p
.
Let
a:=c^{\ast}\varepsilon
. Let
J,j_{0}
be two integers such that
j_{0}<0\leq J
,

(1+a)^{J-1}\leq R<(1+a)^{J}\ \ \text{and}\ \ (1+a)^{j_{0}p}\leq\frac{1}{10}\varepsilon\leq(1+a)^{(j_{0}+1)p}.

(6.4)

For
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let

\mathcal{A}_{j}:=\mathcal{N}_{2a(1+a)^{j}}({\mathcal{S}}_{p},L_{\infty})\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{p}

denote the minimal
2a(1+a)^{j}
-net of
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
in the norm of
L_{\infty}
. For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
, we define
A_{j}(f)
to be the function in
{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
such that
\|A_{j}(f)-f\|_{\infty}\leq 2a(1+a)^{j}
.

For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
and
j>j_{0}
, let

U_{j}(f):=\{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega:|A_{j}(f)(\mathbf{x})|\geq(1+a)^{j-1}\},

and

D_{j}(f):=U_{j}(f)\setminus\underset{k>j}{\bigcup}U_{k}(f).

Define

h(f,\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}(1+a)^{j}\chi_{D_{j}(f)}(\mathbf{x}).

(6.5)

It can be easily verified that

(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{8})|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}\leq|h(f,\mathbf{x})|^{p}\leq(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{8})|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p},

(6.6)

if
\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{j_{0}<j\leq J}D_{j}(f)
,
and
|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}
otherwise
3
3

3

For details, we refer to the proof of Theorem 5.1 in
[
5
]
.
. As a result, for any probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
, and any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
, we have

\Bigl|\|h(f)\|_{L_{p}(\nu)}^{p}-\|f\|_{L_{p}(\nu)}^{p}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\nu)}^{p}+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.

(6.7)

Now for
j_{0}+1\leq j\leq J
, let

{\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{p}:=\left\{M_{j}\cdot\chi_{D_{j}(f)}:\ f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\right\},\ \ \ \text{ where $M_{j}:=(1+a)^{pj}$.}

Let
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}
be a sequence of positive numbers satisfying
\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\varepsilon_{j}\leq\varepsilon/4
, which will be specified later.
By (
6.6
),
for each
j_{0}<j\leq J
,

\|M_{j}\chi_{D_{j}(f)}\|_{1}\leq\|h(f)\|_{p}^{p}\leq(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{8})\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2.

Thus, using (
6.1
) and Lemma
3.2
,
we conclude that the inequalities ,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{p}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varphi(\xi^{k})-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,d\mu(x)\Bigr|\leq\varepsilon_{j},\ \ \ j_{0}<j\leq J,

(6.8)

hold simultaneously with probability

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}|{\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{p}|\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}{16M_{j}}\Bigr).

(6.9)

Now we fix a set of random points
\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}
for which (
6.8
) is satisfied. Using (
6.5
),
(
6.8
) and the triangle inequality, we then deduce

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|h(f,\xi^{j})|^{p}-\|h(f)\|_{p}^{p}\Bigr|\leq\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\varepsilon_{j}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

This together with (
6.7
) implies that for any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-1\Bigr|\leq\frac{3}{8}\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p},

from which the desired inequality (
6.3
) follows.

Thus,
to complete the proof, it remains to construct a sequence of positive numbers
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}
such that

\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\varepsilon_{j}\leq\varepsilon/4,

(6.10)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}|{\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{p}|\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}{16M_{j}}\Bigr)\leq C\varepsilon\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Bigr).

(6.11)

Since
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\subset{\mathcal{W}}_{p}
, we can estimate the cardinalities of the sets
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{p}
as follows:

|{\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{p}|\leq|\mathcal{A}_{j}|\times\cdots\times|\mathcal{A}_{J}|\leq\prod_{k=j}^{J}N_{a(1+a)^{k}}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})=:L_{j}.

(6.12)

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\log L_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=j}^{J}\mathcal{H}_{a(1+a)^{k}}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{(1+a)^{j-1}}^{R}\mathcal{H}_{at}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})\frac{dt}{t}.

(6.13)

Now we specify the numbers
\varepsilon_{j}
as follows:

\varepsilon_{j}:={\delta}+\tau_{j},\ \ j_{0}<j\leq J,

where
{\delta}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{8(J-j_{0})}
, and
\tau_{j}>0
is defined by

\displaystyle 2\log L_{j}=\frac{m\tau_{j}^{2}}{16M_{j}},\ \ \ \text{that is}\ \ \tau_{j}:=4\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\frac{M_{j}\cdot\log L_{j}}{m}}.

(6.14)

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\varepsilon_{j}

\displaystyle=4\sqrt{2}m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}(M_{j}\log L_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{8},

which, by (
6.13
) and straightforward calculations, is estimated above by

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}+Cm^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{4^{-1}(10^{-1}\varepsilon)^{1/p}}^{R}u^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Bigl(\int_{u}^{R}\mathcal{H}_{c_{p}\varepsilon t}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})\frac{dt}{t}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}du.

Thus, to ensure (
6.10
), it is enough to assume that

m\geq C\varepsilon^{-5}\Bigg[\int_{4^{-1}(10^{-1}\varepsilon)^{1/p}}^{R}u^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Bigl(\int_{u}^{R}\mathcal{H}_{c_{p}\varepsilon t}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})\frac{dt}{t}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}du\Bigg]^{2}

with
C=C_{p}
being a large constant depending only on
p
.

Finally, we estimate the sum

S:=\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}|{\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{p}|\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}{16M_{j}}\Bigr).

Since
\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\geq\tau_{j}^{2}+{\delta}^{2}
, we obtain from (
6.12
) that

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\exp\Bigl(\log L_{j}-\frac{m\tau_{j}^{2}}{16M_{j}}-\frac{m{\delta}^{2}}{16M_{j}}\Bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m{\delta}^{2}}{16M_{J}}\Bigr)\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\frac{1}{L_{j}}.

Using (
6.4
), we have

\frac{m{\delta}^{2}}{16M_{J}}=m\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{8(J-j_{0})}\Bigr)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{16M_{J}}\geq\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}.

Since

L_{j}\geq N_{a(1+a)^{j}}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty}),

this implies

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Bigr)\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}\frac{1}{N_{a(1+a)^{j}}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})}.

On the other hand, from the proof of Theorem 5.1 in
[
5
]
, we know that

N_{t}({\mathcal{W}}_{p},L_{\infty})\geq\frac{R}{4t},\ \ \ \forall 0<t<R.

(6.15)

It follows that

\displaystyle S\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Bigr)\cdot 4c_{\ast}R^{-1}\varepsilon\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{J}(1+a)^{j}\leq\varepsilon\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm\varepsilon^{4}}{R^{p}(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Bigr),

which is as desired. This completes the proof.

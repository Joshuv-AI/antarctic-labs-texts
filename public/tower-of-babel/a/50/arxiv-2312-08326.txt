Cell decompositions of persistent minimal models
Kathryn Hess, Samuel Lavenir, Kelly Maggs

Abstract
. In this article we generalize the theory of minimal models in rational homotopy theory to the context of persistence. Our main motivation is the computation of an explicit finite, cellular presentation of the persistent minimal model that computes persistent rational type of copersistent simply-connected spaces. We give an explicit construction of minimal models of maps between commutative differential-graded algebras, which we use to construct minimal models of tame persistent CDGAs. We then build a model structure on persistent CDGAs for which these minimal models become cell complexes. As an application of our results, we construct an explicit rational Postnikov tower of a simply-connected copersistent spaces.

1.
Introduction

Persistence theory studies how algebraic invariants vary along a parametrized family of objects in a category
[
Oud15
,
ZC05
]
. Parametrized objects are encoded as functors
F:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{C}
from a poset
\mathcal{P}
into a category
\mathcal{C}
. Post-composing with the functorial invariant of interest yields the associated persistent invariant. Functors
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\leq)\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbf{k}}
from the standard poset
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\leq)
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to vector spaces
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbf{k}}
over a field
\mathbf{k}
form the category of (real-indexed)
persistence modules
and have been studied extensively. Such cases traditionally arose from studying the homology of sub-level sets of compact manifolds filtered by a Morse function
[
Bar94
]
, or the filtered Rips complexes of finite metric spaces
[
Rob99
,
ZC05
,
ELZ02
]
. The name persistence module stems from the observation that functors
\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbf{k}}
can be equivalently described as graded modules over the
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-graded ‘polynomial ring’
\mathbf{k}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
with real exponents over a field
\mathbf{k}

[
ZC05
,
BL23
]
. In this paper we work in the context of rational homotopy theory, so we are interested only in the case that
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
.

Under reasonable assumptions, persistence modules admit an extremely useful decomposition, namely, the
interval decomposition

\mathbb{V}\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}

[
Web85
,
ZC05
,
CDSGO16
,
CB15
]
. An interval module
\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}
represents a functor that is constant at
\mathbb{Q}
over the half-open interval
[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})\subset\mathbb{R}
and zero elsewhere. From the perspective of commutative algebra, the interval modules
\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}
are precisely the indecomposable cyclic compact graded modules over the ring
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
. We focus in particular on the subclass of interval-decomposable persistence modules that are
tame
, in other words, those for which there is a finite discretization of the positive real line such that the module is constant over each half-open interval.

Most attention within the persistent homology community has been devoted to generalising the tools of persistence to new, multi-parameter indexing categories
[
CFF+13
,
BL22
,
BL23
]
. The difficulty of this goal lies in the fact that there is no longer a simple description of the interval indecomposable to work with. We focus instead on examining new target categories, rather than varying the source categories. Given that the central ideas of persistence theory are based on top of the interval decomposition, one has much to gain by transferring such decompositions into other categories. The goal of this article is thus to translate and interpret the interval decomposition in the context of persistent rational homotopy theory at the model-categorical level, which we achieve by generalizing the inductive construction of Sullivan’s minimal model to the persistent setting. Indeed, since interval decomposition is possible only over a field, rational homotopy theory is a natural context for studying persistent homotopy theory using Topological Data Analysis (TDA) methods.

In classical rational homotopy theory, the homotopy theory of spaces is compared with various homotopy theories of commutative differential-graded algebras obtained by transferring a model structure on the category of cochain complexes along free-forgetful adjunctions. We take a similar approach, defining a homotopy theory of
persistent
cochain complexes before transferring it to persistent commutative differential-graded algebras. As in the classical case, model categories provide a helpful language for studying persistent rational homotopy theory, following their introduction into the TDA literature in non-rational persistence settings
[
BL23
,
CGL21
]
.

The main tool we introduce is a model-categorical notion of persistent cell attachment (
6.1
), which we use to formally adjoin intervals to persistent cochain complexes and commutative differential graded algebras (CDGAs) — a procedure we call
interval surgery
. We show that these cell attachments are the cofibrant generators for a novel combinatorial, compactly generated model structure on the category of persistent cochain complexes, which we call the
interval-sphere model structure
(
Section

3.2.2
), and that with respect to this model structure, there exists a Quillen pair relating the the interval-sphere model structure on persistent CDGAs and the
\mathbb{Q}
-projective model structure on copersistent simplicial sets (
Proposition

8.3
).

Our motivation for emphasizing cell attachment arises from classical rational homotopy theory, where it is an essential tool for performing computations. Broadly speaking, the minimal model of a simply-connected, finite-type space is a special type of finitely generated CDGA that is weakly equivalent to the CDGA of polynomial differential forms on the space. In the usual cofibrantly generated model structure on CDGAs, the classical construction of the minimal model (Cf. Diagram
3.1
below) is performed by inductively attaching "algebraic CDGA cells" degree-by-degree. The algebraic
k
-cells in the minimal model bijectively correspond to the generators of the
k
-th rational homotopy groups of the space and make explicit computations possible.

The main results of this paper concern extending the correspondence between CDGA cells in the minimal model and generators of the rational homotopy groups to the persistent setting. We overcome two non-trivial obstructions in the process. Firstly, modelling the CDGA of polynomial differential forms on a space with a minimal CDGA is
homotopy functorial
(in the sense of Diagram
3.2
) rather than functorial on the nose. This requires particular care in the persistent case as the structure maps between algebras of polynomial differential forms are part of the data. Nevertheless, we successfully generalize the inductive CDGA construction first for maps in
Theorem

4.4
, then for tame persistent CDGAs in
Theorem

6.11
. Secondly, there exist non-canonical choices for how to define persistent cell attachment. We justify our choice (
Definition

6.1
) of such attachments by showing that one successfully retains the correspondence between the CDGA cells of the persistent minimal model and the generators of the rational homotopy groups of a (co)persistent space (
Corollary

8.4
).

The possibility to performing calculations is an important objective in both persistent topology and rational homotopy theory. The explicit construction of the minimal model, whose cellular structure functions as a finite
presentation
of rational homotopy type, is the driver of its utility in applications. The tools and constructions introduced in this work show that such a presentation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Big\{\,\,\gamma_{p}\in\ \mathbb{M}^{k}(p)\,\,\Big\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Bigg\{\,\,d\gamma_{p}=v_{p},\gamma_{q}=u_{q}\,\,\Bigg\}}}
Presentation
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Generators }\mathscr{G}}}

exists for persistent minimal models
\mathbb{M}
(

6.11
), despite the homotopy coherence issues. In this setting, intervals are generators with relations describing how the interval is attached into a CDGA by a coboundary and structure map. To conclude the paper, we use this structure to describe the algebraic constraints on the possible rational homotopy types corresponding to a given persistent rational homotopy group arising from the naturality of coboundary operator.

Related work

It has earlier been observed that the persistent rational homotopy groups admit an interval decomposition under certain tameness assumptions
[
BdSS14
]
. The nascent field of (non-rational) persistent homotopy theory has been studied in
[
BL23
,
LS23
,
Jar19
,
Jar20
]
. In particular, the work of
[
MZ22
]
seems to be the first to address persistent rational homotopy theory, doing so in the context of Rips complexes of metric spaces. Indeed, a persistent minimal model for rational homotopy theory was introduced in the concurrent work of
[
Zho23
]
, which shares a similar definition. Here we do not address questions related to stability and interleaving, which are central to that work. The structural aspects of the persistent minimal model we study here should provide complementary insights, useful in addressing such questions.

Notation and conventions

•

We apply cohomological conventions, i.e., differentials increase degree.

•

We use the word
space
as a synonym for
simplicial set
.

•

All vector spaces are over
\mathbb{Q}
.

•

We write

•

For

is defined by

with differentials
d^{\prime}_{k}=(-1)^{n}d_{k}
.

•

A commutative differential graded algebra (CDGA)

is connected if
H^{0}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Q}
and simply-connected if
H^{1}(\mathcal{A})=0
additionally.

•

If

is connected then

–

\overline{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{n>0}\mathcal{A}^{n}
is the kernel of the augmentation
\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{Q}
;

–

Q(\mathcal{A})
is the complex of
indecomposables

\mathrm{Coker}\>(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{A}}\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}).

•

We use the word
finite type
in three settings.

–

A simplicial set is finite type if it has finitely many non-degenerate simplices.

–

A cochain complex is finite type if it is degree-wise finite dimensional.

–

A CDGA is finite type if its cohomology is degree-wise finite dimensional.

3.
Background

We first recall the basic properties of minimal models and their role in rational homotopy theory. The main reference for this section is
[
GM13
]
.

3.1.
Cones and homotopies

3.1.1.
CDGA homotopies

Let
\Lambda(t,dt)
be the free CDGA representing the polynomial differential forms on the interval, where the generator
t
is of degree 0. This algebra admits two natural augmentations

\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}:\Lambda(t,dt)\to\mathbb{Q}
defined by
\varepsilon_{0}(t)=0
and
\varepsilon_{1}(t)=1
, corresponding to the restrictions to the start and end of the interval. Note that for degree reasons
\varepsilon_{0}(dt)=\varepsilon_{1}(dt)=0
. A
homotopy
between CDGA maps
f,g:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is an algebra homomorphism
H:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)
such that
(id\cdot\epsilon_{0})H=f
and
(id\cdot\epsilon_{0})H=g
.

3.1.2.
Cochain complexes cones

Let
f:A\to B
be a cochain map. We denote by
C_{f}
the mapping cone of
f
, which has components
C_{f}^{n}=A^{n+1}\oplus B^{n}
and differential
d:C_{f}^{n}\to C_{f}^{n+1}
given by

d(a,b)=(da,f(a)-db).

There is a natural map
B\to C_{f}
given by
b\mapsto(0,-b)
. The sequence
A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{}C_{f}
induces a long exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\to H^{n-1}C_{f}\xrightarrow{}H^{n}A\xrightarrow{f^{*}}H^{n}B\xrightarrow{}H^{n}C_{f}\xrightarrow{}H^{n+1}A\to\cdots

in which the connecting homomorphisms are induced by the projection map
C_{f}\to A[1]
given by
(a,b)\mapsto a
. Given a homotopy commutative square of cochain complexes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

there is an induced cochain map on mapping cones

given by the formula

\varphi(a,b)=(u(a),v(b)+h(a)).

3.1.3.
Integrating homotopies

A homotopy in CDGA can be integrated fiber-wise to a homotopy in
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}(\mathbb{Q})
. The assignments

t^{k}\mapsto 0\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>t^{k}dt\mapsto\frac{1}{k+1}t^{k+1}\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\forall k\geq 0

define a linear map

of degree

. We denote by
\int_{0}^{1}
the composite
\varepsilon_{1}\circ\int_{0}^{t}:\Lambda(t,dt)\to\Lambda(t,dt)\to\mathbb{Q}
. If

is a homotopy between CDGA homomorphisms

, the composite

\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{H}\mathcal{B}\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{id}\otimes\int_{0}^{1}}\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathcal{B}

is a linear map
\int_{0}^{1}H:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of degree
-1
. A straightforward calculation shows that it satisfies the identity

for every

. In other words,
\int_{0}^{1}H
is a cochain homotopy between the maps

and
g
. We say that
\int_{0}^{1}H
is the cochain homotopy
underlying
the CDGA homotopy

.

3.2.
Minimal models

In this section we briefly review the theory of Sullivan minimal models.

3.2.1.
Cochain spheres and disks

Each vector space
V
has associated spheres
S^{k}(V)
and disks
D^{k}(V)
for every integer
k\geq 0
. The complex
S^{k}(V)
consists of a copy of
V
in degree
k
. Similarly, the complex
D^{k}(V)
consists of a copy of
V
in each of degrees
(k-1)
and
k
, where the only non zero differential is the identity. When
k=0
, we adopt the convention that
D^{0}(V)=0
. We also write
S^{k}=S^{k}(\mathbb{Q})
and
D^{k}=D^{k}(\mathbb{Q})
. Given a graded
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space

, we define an associated sphere

and disk

. These are cochain complexes are defined by

S(V)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(V_{k})\>\>\>\>\>\>\>,\>\>\>\>\>\>\>D(V)=\bigoplus_{k>0}D^{k}(V_{k}).

Note that there are evident inclusions
S(V)\subseteq D(V)
and that
D(V)/S(V)=S(V[-1])=S(V)[-1]
for any choice of graded vector space

.

3.2.2.
Model structures

There is a well known model structure on the category

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\{\>D^{k}\to 0\>|\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\}.

Transfer along the free-forgetful adjunction

Cell attachments along generating cofibrations

3.2.4.
Minimal algebras

A

an

-cell complex, or equivalently, a CDGA that is realized as a transfinite colimit of Hirsch extensions over the base field

When the filtration above terminates at level

is a quasi-isomorphism

is a minimal algebra. If

-connected map

5.
Persistence theory and tameness

Before generalizing the previous construction from the level of maps to persistent CDGAs, we review the basic notation and terminology about persistent objects in a category. We recall the notion of tame persistent objects from
[
CGL21
]
, introduce the persistent spheres and disks and finish with a description of compact objects in several persistent categories of interest.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the canonical poset category over
[0,\infty).

•

The category
p\mathcal{C}
of
persistent objects
in a category
\mathcal{C}
is the functor category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{C})
.

•

If letters
X,Y,Z,\cdots
are used for the objects of the category
\mathcal{C}
, we use corresponding boldface symbols
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{Z},\cdots
to name the objects of
p\mathcal{C}
.

•

Dually, we label the functor category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{-},\mathcal{C})
of
copersistent objects
as
p^{*}\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{-}=(-\infty,0]\cong\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathsf{op}}
is the dual poset to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

5.1.
Tameness

The use of
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
as the indexing category is central to the definition of the interleaving distance and stability theorems. However, in practice and applications, we take as input a finite sequence of spaces/chain complexes/vector spaces, so the resulting functor changes at only finitely many index values. The following definitions of discretizations and tameness are presented in
[
CGL21
]
, and formalise this notion.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\in pC
be a persistent object of
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

A finite sequence
t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
of non-negative reals is said to
discretize

\mathbb{X}
if a morphism
\mathbb{X}(s<t)
may fail to be an isomorphism only if there exists an
i
such that
s<t_{i}\leq t
.

(2)

We say that
\mathbb{X}
is
tame
if it admits a discretization. We denote by
(p\mathcal{C})^{\mathsf{tame}}\subseteq p\mathcal{C}
the full subcategory of
p\mathcal{C}
on the tame objects.

The data of a tame object
\mathbb{X}\in(p\mathcal{C})^{\mathsf{tame}}
amounts to a finite sequential diagram

X_{0}\to X_{1}\to\cdots\to X_{n}

in
\mathcal{C}
, together with a sequence of non-negative reals
t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. Given such sequences, the associated persistent object
\mathbb{X}
is obtained by the left Kan extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{t_{0}<\ldots<t_{n}\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}=\mathsf{Lan}_{t}X}

along the poset inclusion
\{t_{0}<\ldots<t_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The Kan extension is guaranteed to exist without assumptions on
\mathcal{C}
, as is explained in
[
CGL21
, Section 2.1]
. This extension is constant over the half-open intervals
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
; its components are given by
\mathbb{X}(t)=X_{t_{i}}
when
t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1})
,
\mathbb{X}(t)=X_{t_{n}}
when
t\geq t_{n}
and
\mathbb{X}(t)=0
when
t<t_{1}
.

5.2.
Persistence Modules

A persistence module
[
CDSGO16
]
is a persistent object in the category
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. In other words, a persistence module is a functor
\mathbb{M}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}.
A persistence module can equivalently be described as a graded module over a certain
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-graded ring, as follows. We denote by
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
the ring of formal polynomials in one variable
X
with
non-negative real exponents
. Its elements are formal sums

\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{t_{i}}

where
n\geq 0
is an integer, the exponents
t_{i}
are non-negative
real numbers
and the coefficients
a_{i}
belong to
\mathbb{Q}
. The multiplication on
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
is given by a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear extension of
X^{s}\cdot X^{t}=X^{s+t}
. This ring admits a natural
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-grading

\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]\cong\bigoplus_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{Q}X^{t}

so that a persistent module
\mathbb{V}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be described as a graded
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
-module. This identification can be promoted to an isomorphism of categories

p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]}.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
s<t
be non-negative real numbers. The
interval module

\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}
is the persistence module with components given by:

\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}(i)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}&\text{ if }s\leq i<t\\
0&\text{ else}\end{cases}

with structure maps
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}(i\leq j)
given by identities when
s\leq i\leq j<t
, and zero otherwise.

Direct sums of persistence modules are constructed degree-wise on both objects and morphisms.
Interval modules are the atomic units into which all (tame) persistence modules decompose, as stated below.

Theorem 5.4
.

[
Web85
, Thm. 1]

If
\mathbb{V}
is a tame persistence module, then there exists a set of intervals
[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})\subseteq[0,\infty)
such that
\mathbb{V}
splits as a direct sum of interval modules

\mathbb{V}\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}.

Moreover, this decomposition is unique up to permutation of summands.

Remark 5.5
.

The half-open intervals are well-suited to the study of tame objects, as

Remark 5.6
.

5.3.
Persistent cochain complexes

A
persistent cochain complex
is a persistent object in the category
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of complexes. A persistent complex can equivalently be described as a complex of persistence modules. More precisely, there is an equivalence of categories

showing that

is an abelian category. As a category of presheaves valued in an abelian category,

is itself an abelian category whose limits and colimits are computed pointwise. It is moreover a locally finitely presentable category hence the small object argument can be applied to
any

5.4.
Compactness in categories of persistent objects

In this section we study the compact objects in various categories of persistent objects. Recall that an object
X
in a category
\mathcal{C}
is
compact
if the functor
\mathsf{Hom}(X,-)
preserves filtered colimits. We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
the full subcategory on the compact objects. We use the terms
finite type
and
finite
for spaces, (graded) vector spaces, complexes and CDGAs. For each of these categories, we say a (co)persistent object
\mathbb{X}
is of finite type (resp. finite) if its components
\mathbb{X}(t)
are finite type (resp. finite) for all indices
t
.

Proposition 5.11
.

The compact objects in
p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are exactly the tame modules of finite type.

Proof.

Let us write
R=\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
for the ‘polynomial’ ring of
Section

5.2
. In view of the identification
p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{R}
, the compact objects in
p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are exactly the graded
R
-modules of finite presentation. Given a persistence module
\mathbb{V}
, such a finite presentation amounts to a short exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{j\in J}X^{t_{j}}R\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}X^{s_{i}}R\to\mathbb{V}\to 0

where both
I,J
are finite sets. This shows that
\mathbb{V}
is a quotient of tame modules of finite type, hence is itself tame and of finite type. Conversely, if
\mathbb{V}
is tame and of finite type, it admits an interval decomposition

\mathbb{V}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}

consisting of only a finite number of intervals. Such a decomposition implies the existence of a short exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[t_{i},\infty)}\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},\infty)}\to\mathbb{V}\to 0

which translates to a finite presentation of
\mathbb{V}
in view of the isomorphism of persistence modules
X^{t}R\cong\mathbb{I}_{[t,\infty)}
.
∎

Proposition 5.12
.

The compact objects of
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are exactly the tame finite complexes.

Proof.

In view of the identification
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{Ch}^{*}(\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]})
, the compact objects in
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are the bounded complexes of finitely presented
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-graded
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
-modules. By
Proposition

5.11
, these are the bounded complexes of tame persistence modules of finite type.
∎

Proposition 5.13
.

The compact objects in copersistent spaces are precisely the tame finite spaces.

Proof.

The category of copersistent spaces can equivalently be described as the presehaf category

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathsf{op}}\times\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Set}).

The compact objects in a presheaf category are precisely the finite colimits of representables. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, the representable
\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbf{\Delta}}(-,t\times[n])
, when seen as a copersistent space, can be described as follows

(\mathbb{1}_{\leq t}\times\Delta^{n}):s\mapsto\begin{cases}\Delta^{n}&\text{when $s\leq t$},\\
\varnothing&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with structure maps being either identities or the trivial map
\varnothing\to\Delta^{n}
. As such, it is tame and finite. Accordingly, any compact copersistent space
\mathbb{X}
is of tame and finite since it admits a surjection

\coprod_{1\leq i\leq n}(\mathbb{1}_{\leq t_{i}}\times\Delta^{n_{i}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{X}

from a tame and finite copersistent space.
∎

The next statement is a persistent analogue of the fact that an ordinary Hirsch extension

\mathbb{A}

7.2.

7.2.2.
Locally compact persistence modules

Locally compactness is the central concept that will prove to be a necessary condition for

-cell complexes. We first define and study local compactness at the level of persistence modules.

Definition 7.15

Let

(1)

Example 7.24

(Non-equivalence with injective model structure)
.

For

Remark 7.25

Since there are no non-trivial maps

contains no non-trivial compact sub-modules and thus fails (spectacularly) to be locally compact.

Given that cofibrant implies locally compact (above degree 0), a natural question is whether the converse is true. Over the tame objects, which are a sub-class of locally compact persistence complexes, the converse holds.

Lemma 7.26

on cocycles. Since

and

are tame, the quotient is tame and thus has a graded interval decompostion

at the level of graded persistence modules. For each interval pick generators

and note that

must be in

by the interval decomposition. We add missing cocycles into

via the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{S}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{D}_{s_{\gamma}}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0,y_{t_{\gamma}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{s_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which is clearly still tame. Since quotients of tame objects by tame subobjects are tame, assign an interval decomposition

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

∎

Corollary 7.27
.

If
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is tame then
\mathbb{X}
is cofibrant in the interval sphere model structure.

8.
Persistent rational homotopy theory

The interval-sphere model structure is the basis for relating the homotopy theory of persistent CDGAs to that of homotopy theory of copersistent simplicial sets.

8.1.
Model structures

We begin with a transfer of the interval sphere model structure to persistent CDGAs, followed by its comparison with a certain projective model structure on copersistent simplicial sets.

8.1.1.
Transfer model structure on
p\mathsf{CGDA}

Post-composition of persistent objects with the free-forget adjunction yields an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda:p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}:U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}}

at the level of persistent objects.
The category
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is locally finitely presentable, hence the small object argument can be applied to any set of maps. Its compact objects are the persistent CDGAs
\mathbb{A}
such that
\mathbb{A}(t)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of finite presentation for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We denote by
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
the class of maps
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}
in
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
Uf\in\mathbb{W}
. In other words,
f(s)
is a quasi-isomorphism for every
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We also write

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle=\big\{\>\Lambda\mathbb{S}^{k}_{[s,t)}\to\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}\>|\>0\leq s\leq t\>\big\}

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathbb{J}

\displaystyle=\big\{\>\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{t}\to\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}\>|\>0\leq s\leq t\>\big\}

for the images of
\mathbb{I},\mathbb{J}\subseteq p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
under the functor
\Lambda
.

Theorem 8.1
.

There is a combinatorial model structure on
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for which
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
is the class of weak equivalences and the sets
\Lambda\mathbb{I},\Lambda\mathbb{J}
are respectively the generating cofibrations and trivial cofibrations.

Proof.

It suffices to check that the conditions for the recognition theorem of D. Kan
[
Hir03
, Theorem 11.3.2]
are satisfied. For this it is enough to show that

\mathsf{Cell}(\Lambda\mathbb{J})\subseteq U^{-1}\mathbb{W}

where
\mathsf{Cell}(\Lambda\mathbb{J})\subseteq p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the class of relative
\Lambda\mathbb{J}
-cell complexes. Since
\Lambda\mathbb{J}\subseteq U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
, it suffices to show that the class
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
is closed under cobase change and transfinite composition. The latter condition is clear since the forgetful functor
U
preserves filtered colimits. We thus need to prove that the class
\Lambda\mathbb{J}
is closed under cobase change. To this end consider a pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{t}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

in
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. One sees immediately that
\mathbb{A}(u)\to\mathbb{B}(u)
is an isomorphism when
u<s
or
t\leq u
. When
s\leq u<t
, we find
\mathbb{B}(u)\cong\mathbb{A}(u)\otimes\Lambda(a_{u},da_{u})
so that the inclusion
\mathbb{A}(u)\subseteq\mathbb{B}(u)
is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

The following corollary – a direct consequence of
8.1
and
6.11
by construction – is a generalization of the classical cell complex definition of minimal models.

Corollary 8.2
.

Persistent minimal models are cell complexes in the above model structure.

8.1.2.
Model structure on
p^{*}\mathsf{sSet}

The first task is to establish a persistent variant of the classical Quillen pair, setting the ground for the study of
persistent rational homotopy theory
. Denote by

p^{*}\mathsf{sSet}=\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{sSet})\cong\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{-},\mathsf{sSet})

the category of
copersistent
spaces. Similarly as above, the functors

and

can be extended to persistent CDGAs and copersistent spaces, respectively. This yields the following contravariant adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle-\rangle:p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p^{*}\mathsf{sSet})^{\mathsf{op}}:\mathcal{A}_{PL}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}}

The category

of copersistent spaces admits the projective

-model structure which is described as follows. A map

is:

(1)

a fibration if

-fibration for every

(2)

a weak equivalence if

\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence for every

.

8.1.3.
The Quillen pair

Proposition 8.3
.

The contravariant adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle-\rangle:p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}}

It suffices to check that
\langle-\rangle
sends the generating (trivial) cofibrations in

to (trivial)
\mathbb{Q}
-fibrations in

. Given a map

of the form

8.2.

The results of the previous section generalize the classical correspondence (
3.3

8.2.1.
Eilenberg-Maclane Spaces

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a compact, copersistent graded rational vector space concentrated in degree

. A
copersistent Eilenberg Maclane space
over
\mathbb{V}
is a copersistent simplicial set

such that

\pi_{*}K(\mathbb{V},k)=\mathbb{V}.

Since homotopy groups commute with products naturally we have

\mathbb{V}^{\vee}\cong\bigoplus\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}\hskip 20.00003pt\Rightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\prod_{\gamma}K(\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})},k)\text{ is a }K(\mathbb{V},k).

Applying
Equation

3.3
pointwise and examining the structure maps, a basic observation is that

\langle\Lambda\mathbf{S}^{k}(\mathbb{V})\rangle\text{ is a }K(\mathbb{V},k),

i.e., that interval spheres are algebraic models of copersistent Eilenberg-Maclane spaces.

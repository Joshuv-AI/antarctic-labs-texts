Improved wave function for heavy-light mesons in QCD potential model approach and parameterization of the Cornell potential.
A

Abstract
We report improved wave function for mesons in QCD potential model approach using multiplicative method for solution of Schrödinger equation for the extreme cases of inter-quark separations ( r → 0 r\rightarrow 0 and r → ∞ r\rightarrow\infty ). Using the wave function we find the range of the scale factor c of the Cornell potential with reference to the mass scale of B and D sectors heavy-flavoured mesons. With our computed range of c, we also explore the critical inter-quark separation which should govern the choice of parent-child terms in perturbation method out of the linear and Coulombic terms of the Cornell potential.



Key words : Cornell potential, scale factor, Schrödinger equation. heavy-light meson.

PACS Nos. : 12.39.-x, 12.39.Jh , 12.39.Pn, 14.40.-n, 14.40.Lb, 14.40.Nd.

Introduction

The potential model approach has been very successful and reliable in the studies of the properties of mesons, specially, heavy sector mesons
[
6
]
. It is well known that the Cornell potential is the well accepted phenomenological potential to represent quark-antiquark interaction in a meson, which is of the form
V(r)=-a/r+br+c

[
6
,
7
]
. With its linear plus Coulombic combination, both the confinement and asymptotic freedom of QCD are preserved within it. With Cornell potential, the static and dynamic properties of mesons are extensively studied by our group recently
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. The steps are, first to develop meson wave function using non-relativistic Schrödinger equation
[
8
]
and then by using that equation study the properties of mesons.

In the development of meson wave function in such potential model approach using perturbation technique, the choice of parent-child is most crucial. In our earlier work
[
3
]
, we have taken linear term as parent in perturbation method which resulted in Airy’s polynomial function in the wave function
[
10
]
. In the present work we have improved formalism by developing multiplicative form of the wave function considering the two extreme cases (
r\rightarrow 0
and
r\rightarrow\infty
)
[
4
]
.

Further, in earlier works, in developing the meson wave function, the scale factor ’c’ of the Cornell potential has been taken to be either zero
[
4
]
or 1 GeV
[
1
,
3
]
to make it compatible with meson masses in the calculations. However, this seems to be a crude parameterization as the significance of ’c’ lies in scaling the potential to make it suitable for representing meson interaction. With this consideration, in this work we have condidered ’c’ to be non-zero, deduce the wave function and find the closest range of ’c’ which best suits the calculations for masses of heavy-flavoured mesons
[
11
]
.

Furthermore, there should be valid argument for the choice of parent-child in perturbation technique. However, in works referred above, the condition for the consideration of linear confinement term of potential as parent has not been discussed clearly. It is generally accepted that the Coulombic term is dominant at short distance (ultraviolet energy scale) whereas the linear confinement term controls the potential at large distance (infrared energy scale). Here, the boundary condition is set by
V(r_{o})=0
, where
r_{0}
is termed as the critical distance. With
\langle r\rangle<r_{0}
, the Coulombic term can be chosen as parent and with
\langle r\rangle>r_{0}
, the linear term
[
12
]
.
r_{0}
being crucial for the choice of parent-child, the calculation of
r_{0}
for a particular meson is governed by the choices for parameters involved in the potential (
\alpha_{s}
, b, c).

In this work, using improved multiplicative form of wave function, we compute the range of
r_{0}
corresponding to our calculated range of ’c’. This sets the bound for taking linear term or Coulombic term as parent term or perturbation term.

We also comment on the restrictions on free parameterizations of coupling constant and confinement parameter appearing in the Cornell potential
[
13
,
14
]
.

2
Formalism

2.1
Wave function

The quark-antiquark Cornell potential is of the form:

V(r)=-\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3r}+br+c\\

(1)

where
\alpha_{s}
is the QCD coupling constant,
b
is the confinement parameter and
c
is the scale factor. We take
\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3}=a
to get the simpler form:

V(r)=-\frac{a}{r}+br+c

(2)

The Hamiltonian is:

H=-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2\mu}-\frac{a}{r}+br+c

(3)

Here
\mu=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
is the reduced mass of the meson,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
being the masses of the constituent quarks.
The two-body radial Schrödinger equation in terms of radial wave function
R(r)
is:

[-\frac{1}{2\mu}(\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}})-\frac{a}{r}+br+c]R(r)=ER(r)

(4)

For ground state (
l=0
):

[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+2\mu(E+\frac{a}{r}-br-c)]R(r)=0

(5)

Introducing,
U(r)=rR(r)
, the equation (5) transforms to:

\frac{d^{2}U(r)}{dr^{2}}=2\mu(br-\frac{a}{r}-E^{\prime})U(r)\\

(6)

where
E^{\prime}=E-c
.

Now, to extract the wave function, we consider two extreme conditions.

Case-I (
r\rightarrow\infty
)

Taking
r\rightarrow\infty
, when
1/r
term vanishes and equation (6) reduces to:

\frac{d^{2}U(r)}{dr^{2}}=2\mu(br-E^{\prime})U(r)

(7)

Solution of this equation comes out in terms of Airy’s function
[
1
,
10
]
as:

U(r)\sim Ai[\varrho]=Ai[\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}]

(8)

Here,
\varrho=\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}
, with
\varrho_{1}=(2\mu b)^{1/3}
and
\varrho_{0}=-(\frac{2\mu}{b^{2}})^{1/3}E^{\prime}
.
\varrho_{0}
is the zero of the Airy’s function (
Ai[\varrho_{0}]=0
).

Case-II (
r\rightarrow 0
)

We take
r\rightarrow 0
when
1/r
term in equation (6) will prevail:

\frac{d^{2}U(r)}{dr^{2}}=2\mu(-\frac{a}{r}-E^{\prime})U(r)

(9)

The solution comes out as:

U(r)\sim e^{-r/a_{0}}

(10)

Here,
a_{0}=\frac{1}{\mu a}=\frac{3}{4\mu\alpha_{s}}
.
We construct the ground state wave function as the multiplication of the solutions of these two extreme cases (equations 8 and 10):

U(r)\sim Ai[\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}]e^{-r/a_{0}}

(11)

With
N
as the normalisation factor, our radial wave function has thus the form :

\Psi(r)=\frac{N}{r}Ai[\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}]e^{-r/a_{0}}

(12)

Here, it is worthwhile to mention that although Schrödinger equation is non-relativistic in nature, it stands for the studies of heavy-light mesons on the ground of large quark masses involved allowing to consider the velocities of heavy particles to be non-relativistic. However, the relativistic effect in the wave function can be incorporated following Dirac prescription
[
9
]
, which yields:

\Psi_{rel}(r)=\frac{N}{r}Ai[\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}]e^{-r/a_{0}}(\frac{r}{a_{0}})^{-\epsilon}

(13)

Here,

\epsilon=1-\sqrt{1-(\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3})^{2}}=1-\sqrt{1-a^{2}}

(14)

Truncating Airy’s infinite series up to
O(r^{3})
, the wave function can be simplified to the form [Appendix-A]:

\Psi_{rel}(r)=\frac{N}{a_{0}^{-\epsilon}}[k_{0}r^{-1-\epsilon}+k_{1}r^{-\epsilon}+k_{2}r^{1-\epsilon}+k_{3}r^{2-\epsilon}]e^{-r/a_{0}}

(15)

2.2
Meson mass

As in our previous works, here also we use the following relation for calculation of pseudoscalar meson mass.

M_{P}=m_{Q}+m_{\overline{Q}}+\triangle E\\

(16)

Where
\triangle E=<H>
. The Hamiltonian operator
H
is:

H=-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2\mu}+V(r)

(17)

So, for ground state meson, we have:

\nabla^{2}\equiv\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d}{dr}

(18)

This gives:

\displaystyle<H>=<-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2\mu}>+<-\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3r}>+<\sigma r>+<c>

\displaystyle=<H_{1}>+<H_{2}>+<H_{3}>+<H_{4}>

(19)

The expressions for the specific terms are given below.
Here,

\displaystyle<H_{1}>=\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}\Psi_{rel}(r)[-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2\mu}]\Psi_{rel}(r)dr

(20)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}\Psi_{rel}(r)[-\frac{1}{2\mu}(\frac{d^{2}\Psi(r)}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d\Psi_{rel}(r)}{dr})]dr

(21)

\displaystyle<H_{2}>=\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}(-\frac{B}{r})|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr=-4\pi B\int_{0}^{\infty}r|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr

(22)

\displaystyle<H_{3}>=\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}(br)|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr=4\pi b\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{3}|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr

(23)

\displaystyle<H_{4}>=\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}(c)|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr=4\pi c\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}|\Psi_{rel}(r)|^{2}dr

(24)

4
Conclusion and remarks

In the work, we have mainly developed improved meson wave function with the complete form of the Cornell potential considering non-zero value of scale factor c. With that we have explored the range of c that fits in the calculation of the masses of the B and D sector mesons.

1.

We find that c has reasonable effect on the masses of the mesons and as such is also a deciding term of the Cornell potential in the studies of meson properties. It serves as a fitting parameter in computing static and dynamic properties of mesons.

2.

Our computed range of c comes out to be improvement of the works
[
13
,
14
]
in which the c range has been taken on random trial basis. Also, our range of c is in conformity with the value used in work on flux tube model for hybrid charmonium states
[
19
]
.

3.

Although in this work we have avoided perturbation method in finding wave function for mesons, but to make the picture of the choice of parent-child clear in that method, we have studied the range of critical inter-quark separation
r_{0}
which sets the boundary for that choice. We find that taking
\alpha_{s}
within 0.19 and 4.2
[
18
]
and our computed range of c with b to be 0.183
GeV^{-1}
the lower bound of
r_{0}
comes down to 3.17 from 3.5 which we have obtained working with constant values of
\alpha_{s}
for B and D mesons.

4.

It is to be mentioned that in recent work on parameterisation of Cornell potential
[
14
]
all of c, b and
\alpha_{s}
parameters have been kept as free parameters treating these to be fitting parameters. However, the choice of
\alpha_{s}
and b values have some guiding references on which we relied upon in our present work. For
\alpha_{s}
we have taken the range considering its governing factors like size of the hadron and momentum transfer
[
18
,
16
,
17
,
6
]
. Regarding the confinement parameter, we would like to mention that it resembles the string tension of string potential for mesons which controls the slope and intercept of the Regge trajectory
[
20
]
. There is as such not much flexibility for the choice of its value. In our work, we have chosen to keep it fixed at 0.183
GeV^{-1})
as predicted in holographic QCD which fits remarkably well with quarkonium data
[
18
]
.

5.

For the scale factor c in the potential, we have started with a possible range of
-1.5
GeV to
1.5
GeV and by fitting our computed meson masses with PDG masses
[
11
]
we have computed a refined range of c.

Finally, it is worth commenting that the bounds of the critical inter-quark separation
r_{0}
inspires us to compare the results of meson properties for both the choices of linear parent and Coulombic parent in perturbation technique. This will clear the ambiguity prevailing in the choice of parent-child. This work is presently in progress.

Appendix-A

Airy’s infinite series as a function of
\varrho=\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}
can be expressed as
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle Ai[\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0}]=a_{1}[1+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})^{3}}{6}+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})^{6}}{180}+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})^{9}}{12960}+...]-

\displaystyle b_{1}[(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})^{4}}{12}+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})^{7}}{504}+\frac{(\varrho_{1}r+\varrho_{0})10}{45360}+...]

(.25)

with
a_{1}=\frac{1}{3^{2/3}\Gamma(2/3)}=0.3550281
and
b_{1}=\frac{1}{3^{1/3}\Gamma(1/3)}=0.2588194.

We consider Airy’s series up to
O(r^{3})
:

Ai[\varrho]=a_{1}[1+\frac{(\varrho)^{3}}{6}]-b_{1}\varrho

(.26)

From this, we get the Airy function as an explicit function of
r
as:

Ai[r]=k_{0}+k_{1}r+k_{2}r^{2}+k_{3}r^{3}

(.27)

with
k_{i}s
having their explicit form as given below:

\displaystyle k_{0}=a_{1}+\frac{a_{1}\varrho_{0}^{3}}{6}-b_{1}\varrho_{0}

(.28)

\displaystyle k_{1}=\frac{a_{1}\varrho_{0}^{2}\varrho_{1}}{2}-b_{1}\varrho_{1}

(.29)

\displaystyle k_{2}=\frac{a_{1}\varrho_{0}\varrho_{1}^{2}}{2}

(.30)

\displaystyle k_{3}=\frac{a_{1}\varrho_{1}^{3}}{6}

(.31)

With this truncated expression of Airy’s function, we now construct the wave function as:

\displaystyle\Psi_{rel}(r)=\frac{N}{r}[k_{0}+k_{1}r+k_{2}r^{2}+k_{3}r^{3}]e^{-r/a_{0}}(\frac{r}{a_{0}})^{-\epsilon}

(.32)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{a_{0}^{-\epsilon}}[k_{0}r^{-1-\epsilon}+k_{1}r^{-\epsilon}+k_{2}r^{1-\epsilon}+k_{3}r^{2-\epsilon}]e^{-r/a_{0}}

(.33)

It is to be mentioned that the wave function with Airy’s polynomial series brings in divergences in carrying out the integrations with infinite
upper limit in the calculation of
<H>
. For that we have used truncated Airy series. Regarding sensitivity of the order of polynomial approximation of the Airy’s infinite series, it is to be mentioned that Airy’s function and its derivatives fall very sharply and almost dies out with increasing r-value (
Ai[3]=0.0066
)
[
1
]
. This allows us to truncate the infinite Airy series up to third order of
r
without compromising the result.

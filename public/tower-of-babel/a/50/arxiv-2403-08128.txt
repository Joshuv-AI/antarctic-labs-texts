Singular loci of algebras over ramified discrete valuation rings
Nawaj KC

Abstract
. When k k is a field, the classical Jacobian criterion computes the singular locus of an equidimensional, finitely generated k k -algebra as the closed subset of an ideal generated by appropriate minors of the so-called Jacobian matrix. Recently, Hochster-Jeffries and Saito have extended this result for algebras over any unramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic via the use of p p -derivations. Motivated by these results, in this paper, we state and prove an analogous Jacobian criterion for algebras over ramified discrete valuation rings of mixed characteristic.

Introduction

Suppose
R
is a finitely generated, equidimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We consider the set
\Sing R=\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}R\mid R_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is singular}\}
. A local ring is called singular if it is not a regular local ring, i.e. the minimal number of generators of the maximal ideal is strictly greater than the Krull dimension of the ring. Under our hypothesis,
\Sing R
is a closed subset of
{\operatorname{Spec}}R
and, in fact, we can explicitly compute it. We may assume
R=S/I
where
S=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
I
is an ideal
{I=(\bm{f})=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S}
with
h=\height I
. The
Jacobian matrix
on the set
\bm{f}
is defined as follows.

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{f})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
I_{h}(\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{f}))
denote the ideal generated by
h\times h
minors of
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{f})
in
R
. By the classical Jacobian criterion
[
4
, Ch. 16]
, we have the equality:

\Sing R={\rm{V}}(I_{h}(\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{f}))).

This theorem which goes back to Zariski
[
10
]
holds with suitable modifications for all finitely generated, equidimensional
k
-algebras for
k
any field
[
9
,
7
,
1
]
. However, this result does not naively extend to algebras of finite type over discrete valuation rings of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
where
p
is a prime integer. For example, consider
V=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}-p)
which is isomorphic to a ramified discrete valuation ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\sqrt{p}]
with uniformizer
\sqrt{p}
. Thinking of it as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
algebra, the ideal of one by one minors of the naive Jacobian matrix
[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^{2}-2)]
is
(2x)
. So if the Jacobian criterion held,
(x)
would be a singular prime, which it is not.

Suppose
R=S/I
is equidimensional where
S=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
with
p\not\in I
. Let
X={\operatorname{Spec}}R
. If
\mathfrak{p}\in X
such that
p\not\in\mathfrak{p}
, then
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has
p
inverted, whence we reduce to the case of an algebra over the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[p^{-1}]
. So the usual Jacobian criterion suffices. Hence

\Sing R=(\Sing R\setminus V(p))\cup(\Sing R\cap V(p)).

The first component is equal to
{{\rm{V}}(I_{h}(\mathcal{J}(\bm{f})))\setminus{\rm{V}}(p)}
so the problem is to compute the component
{\Sing R\cap V(p)}
. For every prime
\mathfrak{p}
in this component,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a ring such that char
R_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
but char
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=p
, so we are in the mixed characteristic proper. Due to the recent results of Hochster-Jeffries
[
5
]
and Saito
[
8
]
independently, we can now compute
\Sing R\cap V(p)
. The new ingredient is what is called a
p
-derivation. Going mod
p
,
S
reduces to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. The Frobenius on
S/pS
is uniquely determined by the assignment
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}^{p}
for all
i
. Therefore, for a polynomial
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in S
,
f(x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p})-f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{p}
is divisible by
p
. Since
S
is also
p
-torsion free,
\delta_{p}:S\to S
defined below is a well-defined function:

\delta_{p}(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))=\frac{f(x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p})-f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{p}}{p}.

The key property of this operator is that it decreases the
p
-adic order of an element. By the formula for
\delta_{p}
given above, for an element
s\in(p^{n})
, one can check that
\delta_{p}(s)\in(p^{n-1})\setminus(p^{n})
. In this sense,
\delta_{p}
, called a
p
-derivation, acts like a “derivative with respect to
p
” (see also
[
2
,
3
]
). Now one defines the following
mixed
Jacobian matrix.

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{f}))=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{p}(f_{1})&\ldots&\delta_{p}(f_{t})\\
\displaystyle(\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}})^{p}&\ldots&\displaystyle(\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{1}})^{p}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\displaystyle(\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}})^{p}&\ldots&\displaystyle(\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{n}})^{p}\end{pmatrix}.

The recent results of Hochster-Jeffries and Saito states that

\Sing R\cap{\rm{V}}(p)={\rm{V}}(I_{h}(\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{f})))\cap{\rm{V}}(p).

Armed with this result, let us now go back to the example
V=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}-p)
. One can now compute that the ideal of one by one minors of the mixed Jacobian matrix is the unit ideal whence
\Sing V\cap{\rm{V}}(p)=\varnothing
as it ought to be. Furthermore, this mixed Jacobian criterion of Hochster-Jeffries and Saito generalizes to all finitely generated, equidimensional
V
-algebras where
V
is an unramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic. This is due to the fact that all unramified discrete valuation rings admit a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
(see
[
5
, Proposition 2.9]
) which suffices to state and prove a mixed Jacobian criterion.

In contrast, if
V
is a ramified discrete valuation ring,
V
cannot admit a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
, so the theorem of Hochster-Jeffries and Saito cannot be directly extended to algebras of finite type over a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic; see
Remark

2.14
and
[
2
, Example 2.5]
. In this paper, we state and prove a Jacobian criterion for such rings. Assume
V
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with a perfect residue field
k
and set
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Our key observation is that the ramified discrete valuation rings admit an honest
\mathbb{Z}
-derivation that plays an analogous role to
\delta_{p}(-)
in the ramified setting. More precisely, we show that there exists an additive derivation
\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}:S\to S/\pi S
such that
\frac{\partial(\pi)}{\partial\pi}=1
; see Corollary
2.4
. Analogously, we then define the following
mixed
Jacobian matrix over
S/\pi S
:

\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial(f_{1})}{\partial\pi}&\ldots&\frac{\partial(f_{t})}{\partial\pi}\\[2.84544pt]
\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem A
.

(
Corollary

2.13
)
Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with perfect residue field. Let
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with an ideal
I=(\mathbf{f})=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S
. Assume
R=S/I
is equidimensional and let
h=\height I
. Then

\Sing R\cap{\rm{V}}(p)={\rm{V}}(I_{h}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})))\subseteq{\operatorname{Spec}}(R/(\pi)R)

where we identify
V(\pi)=V(p)\subseteq{\operatorname{Spec}}R
with
{\operatorname{Spec}}(R/(\pi)R)
and
I_{h}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})))
denotes the ideal generated by
h\times h
minors of
\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})
in
R/(\pi)R
.

For
R
as in the theorem above, set
\overline{R}=R/(\pi)R
. We will show that this mixed Jacobian matrix above is a presentation for the universal module, denoted
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
, that represents the functor
\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,-):\overline{R}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{R}\text{-Mod}
. Below, we say a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
is
F
-finite if the Frobenius map on
k
is a finite map.

Theorem B
.

(
Corollary

2.16
)
Let
(V,\pi)
be a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi
such that
k=V/\pi V
is
F
-finite. Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
is a local algebra of essentially finite type over
V
such that
p\in\mathfrak{m}
. Set
\overline{R}=R/(\pi)R
and
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}=\Omega_{R/\mathbb{Z}}/(\pi)\Omega_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
R
is a regular local ring if, and only if,
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
is a free
\overline{R}
module of rank
\dim R+\log_{p}[K:K^{p}]
.

1.
Detecting nonsingularity

1.1
.

Some recollections on derivations:
Fix a pair of commutative rings
{A\subseteq R}
with
1
and an
R
-module
M
. An
A
-linear map
d:R\to M
such that
{d(rs)=rd(s)+sd(r)}
for all
r,s\in R
is an
A
-derivation from
R
to
M
. The set of all such derivations, denoted
\Der_{A}(R,M)
, is an
R
-module under the rules
{(d+d^{\prime})(r)=d(r)+d^{\prime}(r)}
and
{(r\cdot d)(s)=rd(s)}
for
d,d^{\prime}
derivations and
r,s\in R
. Furthermore, note that given an
R
-linear map
f:M\to N
, we have an
R
-linear map
{\Der_{A}(R,M)\to\Der_{A}(R,N)}
via post-composition, i.e.
{d\mapsto f\circ d}
. This defines an additive functor

{\Der_{A}(R,-):R\text{-Mod}\to R\text{-Mod}}

and it is representable. We have the universal
A
-derivation
{d_{R/A}:R\to\Omega_{R/A}}
from
R
to the
R
-module of Kähler differentials
\Omega_{R/A}
which induces a natural isomorphism of functors
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{R}(\Omega_{R/A},-)\cong\Der_{A}(R,-)
where
(f:\Omega_{R/A}\to M)
maps to a derivation
{(f\circ d_{R/A}:R\to M)}

[
4
, Chapter 16]
.

Example 1.2
.

Suppose
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then we have
\Omega_{R/A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot dx_{i}
and
d_{R/A}:R\to\Omega_{R/A}
is the derivation
f\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial(f)}{\partial x_{i}}dx_{i}

[
4
, Proposition 16.1]
.

1.3
.

Derivations to modules killed by an ideal:
Suppose
R
is a commutative ring and we have some ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq R
. We want to consider
A
-linear derivations to
R
-modules that are killed by
{\mathfrak{a}}
. This turns out to be useful in computing
{\Sing(R)\cap{\rm{V}}({\mathfrak{a}})}
. Let
\overline{R}=R/{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{R/A}=\overline{R}\otimes_{R}\Omega_{R/A}\cong\Omega_{R/A}/{\mathfrak{a}}\Omega_{R/A}
. If
M
is an
R
-module such that
\mathfrak{a}M=0
, via Hom-Tensor adjunction along
R\to\overline{R}
, we have isomorphisms
\Der_{A}(R,M)\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}_{R}(\Omega_{R/A},M)\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\overline{R}}(\overline{R}\otimes_{R}\Omega_{R/A},M)
. In other words,

\overline{d}_{R/A}:R\xrightarrow{d_{R/A}}\Omega_{R/A}\to\overline{\Omega}_{R/A}

represents the functor

\Der_{A}(R,-):\overline{R}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{R}\text{-Mod}.

Note also that for any derivation
d:R\to M
we have
d({\mathfrak{a}}^{n})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{n-1}M
for all
n\geq 1
. If
{\mathfrak{a}}M=0
,
d
factors through
R/{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}
. So
\Der_{A}(R,M)=\Der_{A}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}^{2},M)
for all
R
-modules such that
{\mathfrak{a}}M=0
. Therefore,
\overline{d}_{R/A}
equivalently represents the functor
\Der_{A}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}^{2},-):\overline{R}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{R}\text{-Mod}
.

Example 1.4
.

If
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq R=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, then
{\Omega_{R/\mathbb{Z}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\cdot dx_{i}}
by Example
1.2
. Suppose
\mathfrak{a}=(p)
for some prime integer
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
{\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\overline{R}\cdot dx_{i}}
and
{\overline{d}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}dx_{i}}
modulo
(p)
. Note
{\overline{R}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}
has the same universal derivation. Therefore, when
\mathfrak{a}=(p)
derivations from
R
to
\overline{R}
-modules is the same as derivations from
\overline{R}
itself. The situation is different when
{\mathfrak{a}}=(x_{1})
. We have
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\overline{R}\cdot dx_{i}
. But
\Omega_{\overline{R}/\mathbb{Z}}=\bigoplus_{i=2}^{n}\overline{R}\cdot dx_{i}
as
\overline{R}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
. So
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}\not\cong\Omega_{\overline{R}/\mathbb{Z}}
. We have strictly more derivations from
R
to
\overline{R}
modules compared to derivations from
\overline{R}
itself.

Our proofs crucially use the following basic and fundamental result on derivations. For the convenience of the reader, we include a proof.

Proposition 1.5
.

Consider rings maps
A\to S\onto S/I
. For all
S/I
-modules
M
, we have a left exact sequence

0\to\Der_{A}(S/I,M)\to\Der_{A}(S,M)\to{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S/I}(I/I^{2},M)

This sequence is split exact for all
S/I
-modules
M
if
S/I^{2}\onto S/I
splits as a map of
A
-algebras.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is an
S/I
-module. An
A
-linear derivation
d:S/I\to M
maps to an
A
-linear derivation
d\circ p:S\to M
where
p:S\to S/I
is the natural surjection. Clearly, if
d\circ p=0
, then
d=0
. On the other hand, given a derivation
d:S\to M
, we have
d(I^{2})\subseteq IM=0
, so it factors uniquely through
S/I^{2}
. Therefore,
\Der_{A}(S,M)=\Der_{A}(S/I^{2},M)
. Restricting the map
d:S/I^{2}\to M
to the submodule
I/I^{2}
, it is easy to verify it is an
S/I
-linear map. Hence, we have a left exact sequence as claimed. Now assume
\sigma:S/I^{2}\onto S/I
splits as a map of
A
-algebras and let
\iota:S/I\to S/I^{2}
denote the splitting, i.e.
\sigma\circ\iota=\id_{S/I}
. Let
\alpha=\iota\circ\sigma-\id_{S/I^{2}}
. Note that since
\sigma\circ\alpha=0
,
\alpha
is an
A
-linear map to
I/I^{2}
. On the other hand, as
\alpha+\id_{S/I^{2}}=\iota\circ\sigma
is a ring homomorphism, one can verify that
\alpha:S/I^{2}\to I/I^{2}
must be a derivation. Finally, noting
\Der_{A}(S,M)=\Der_{A}(S/I^{2},M)
, we may now define a map
\phi:{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S/I}(I/I^{2},M)\to\Der_{A}(S,M)
where
f\mapsto f\circ-\alpha
. Finally,
\phi
splits the map
\Der_{A}(S,M)\to{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S/I}(I/I^{2},M)
as
(f\circ-\alpha)(I/I^{2})=f
.
∎

Proposition 1.6
.

Consider ring maps
A\to S\onto S/I=R
. Consider an ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{S}\subseteq S
and let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
denote its corresponding image in
R
. Let
\overline{S}=S/\mathfrak{a}_{S}
and
\overline{R}=R/\mathfrak{a}
and consider the induced map
\overline{S}\to\overline{R}
. There is an exact sequence of
\overline{R}

\frac{I}{(I^{2}+\mathfrak{a}_{S}I)}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{J}}\overline{R}\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}\to\overline{\Omega}_{R/A}\to 0

where
\mathcal{J}([f])=1\otimes\overline{d}_{S/A}(f)
for
f\in I
and
\overline{d}_{S/A}:S\to\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}
. Furthermore, if
S/I^{2}\onto S/I
splits as a map of
A

Given an
\overline{R}

M
, we have by Proposition
1.5
, an exact sequence
0\to\Der_{A}(R,M)\to\Der_{A}(S,M)\to{\operatorname{Hom}}_{R}(I/I^{2},M)
. If

\overline{R}

. Then we have by the universality of Kähler differentials and Hom-Tensor adjunction the following commutative diagram:

1.5

Corollary 1.7
.

Consider an inclusion
A\subseteq S
of rings and let
\mathfrak{a}_{S}
be some ideal of
S
. Set
\overline{S}=S/\mathfrak{a}_{S}
. Then for
\mathfrak{q}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}S
such that
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_{S}
, we have a map of
k(\mathfrak{q})=S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
modules

\frac{\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}}{\mathfrak{q}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}}\xrightarrow{1\otimes\overline{d}_{S/A}}k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}

where
[f]\mapsto 1\otimes\overline{d}_{S/A}(f)
for
f\in\mathfrak{q}
. Furthermore, if
S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}\onto S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
splits as a map of
A
-algebras, this map is split injective.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
1.6
to maps
A\to S_{\mathfrak{q}}\to S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
, we have a map

\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}\to k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}/A}

where
[f]\mapsto 1\otimes\overline{d}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}/A}(f)
and it is split injective if
S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}\onto S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
splits as a map of
A
-algebras. Now note that
k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}\cong k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{q}}}\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}\cong k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{q}}}\overline{\Omega}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}/A}
and the universal map
\overline{d}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}/A}
is the universal map
\overline{d}_{S/A}
localized at
\mathfrak{q}
. Therefore the assertion follows.

Detecting singularity:

Setup 1
.

Fix a ring
A
and let

A

\mathfrak{a}_{S}\subseteq S

\frac{S_{\mathfrak{q}}}{{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}}\onto\frac{S_{\mathfrak{q}}}{{\mathfrak{q}}S_{\mathfrak{q}}}

Example 1.9
.

If
A=V=k

satisfies the conditions of Setup
1
for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{S}\subseteq S
. The main example in this paper is when
A=\mathbb{Z}
and

(0,p)
; see Proposition
2.8
which we will discuss in Section
2
below.

Theorem 1.10
.

Suppose
S
is as in the Setup
1
above and let
R=S/I
for some ideal
I\subseteq S
. Suppose
\mathfrak{q}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}S
such that
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq I+\mathfrak{a}_{S}
and let
k(\mathfrak{q}):=S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a regular local ring if, and only if,
\rank_{k(\mathfrak{q})}(\mathcal{J}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}))=\height IS_{\mathfrak{q}}
where
\mathcal{J}
is the
\overline{R}
-linear map
\frac{I}{I^{2}+I\mathfrak{a}}\xhookrightarrow{\mathcal{J}}\overline{R}\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}
as in Proposition
1.6
.

Proof.

Note as
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq I+\mathfrak{a}_{S}
,
k(\mathfrak{q})
is indeed an
\overline{R}
module. Also note that

\frac{I}{I^{2}+I\mathfrak{a}}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q})\cong\frac{I}{I^{2}+I\mathfrak{a}}\otimes_{\overline{S}}k(\mathfrak{q})\cong IS_{\mathfrak{q}}/I\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}

Hence
{\mathcal{J}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}):IS_{\mathfrak{q}}/I\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}\to k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}}
. We then have a commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{IS_{\mathfrak{q}}/I{\mathfrak{q}}S_{{\mathfrak{q}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}^{2}S_{\mathfrak{q}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{\mathfrak{q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes\overline{d}_{S/A}}

where

is the natural map induced by the inclusion

. That this triangle commutes and that

Lemma 1.11
.

Suppose

is a regular local ring and

is an ideal of height

. We have

is regular if, and only if,

.

Proof.

Suppose

is minimally generated by
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
where

. Observe that
\rank(J/\mathfrak{m}J\to\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})=h
if, and only if,

, where
\bm{f}

2.
Derivations over a ramified discrete valuation ring

In this section, we fix
(V,\pi)
to be a discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with a uniformizer
\pi
and
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda:\lambda\in V\}
be a subset of
V
that maps bijectively to a
p
-base of
k:=V/(\pi)
. We have that
\Omega_{k/k^{p}}
is a free
k
-module with basis
\{d\lambda:\lambda\text{ $p$-basis element of $k$ over $k^{p}$}\}

[
4

. We denote by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}
the corresponding

-linear derivation that maps

, and by abuse of notation, its extension to

[

for more information on

{d_{\overline{S}/k^{p}}:\overline{S}\to\Omega_{\overline{S}/k^{p}}}
where
{d_{\overline{S}/k^{p}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}
represents the functor
{\Der_{k^{p}}(\overline{S},-):\overline{S}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{S}\text{-Mod}}
. In fact, it represents the functor
{\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{S},-):\overline{S}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{S}\text{-Mod}}
due to the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
k
is a field of characteristic

. Then for any

-module,
\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],M)=\Der_{k^{p}}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],M)
.

Proof.

Indeed, as
\mathbb{Z}\to k^{p}
, any

-linear derivation is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear derivation, so

is clear. Conversely, if

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear derivation, for

is a
k^{p}
-linear derivation.
∎

Assume further that

is ramified, i.e.

. As in Section
1
, we will consider
\overline{S}=S/(\pi)S
and the functor
\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(S,-):\overline{S}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{S}\text{-Mod}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

. The natural map

splits as map of
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras.

Proof.

As

,

is an artinian, and therefore complete, ring of characteristic

. By Cohen’s structure theorem, it will contain a copy of its residue field

. In other words, the map

splits as a map of
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras. Tensoring with
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we see that
S/\pi^{2}S\onto S/\pi S
splits as a map of
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi

\overline{S}=S/(\pi)S

\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(S,M)\cong\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{S},M)\oplus{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\overline{S}}\left(\frac{\pi S}{\pi^{2}S},M\right)

Proof.

Given Lemma

, this follows by applying Proposition
1.5
to maps
{\mathbb{Z}\to S\to\overline{S}}

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi
. Set

\overline{S}=S/(\pi)S
. There exists a

Proof.

We apply Proposition

to

. Then the
\overline{S}
-linear map

Remark 2.5
.

We now explain the derivation
\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}
that is a lift of the map
\bar{\pi}\mapsto 1
in
Corollary

2.4
in more detail. Let
\overline{S}=S/\pi S
. Given
Lemma

2.2
, we have
\sigma:S/\pi^{2}S\twoheadrightarrow\overline{S}
and its splitting
\iota:\overline{S}\xhookrightarrow{}S/\pi^{2}S
. Following the proof of
Proposition

1.5
, the map
D=\iota\circ\sigma-\id_{S/\pi^{2}S}:S/\pi^{2}S\to\pi S/\pi^{2}S
is an additive derivation. Note that
S/\pi^{2}S=(S/\pi^{2}S)[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, so an arbitrary

can be represented as a finite sum of monomials of the type
u\pi^{k}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
where

and

. Now one can observe that
D(ux_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}})=0
and
D(u\pi x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}})=-u\pi x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
.

The derivation

induces a split injection of
\overline{S}
-modules
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\overline{S}}\left(\frac{\pi S}{\pi^{2}S},\overline{S}\right)\to\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(S,\overline{S})
where
s\mapsto-s\circ D
. As an
\overline{S}
-module,
\pi S/\pi^{2}S\cong S/\pi S
where
\pi\mapsto 1
. Therefore,

{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\overline{S}}\left(\frac{\pi S}{\pi^{2}S},\overline{S}\right)\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\overline{S}}(\overline{S},\overline{S})

where
\cdot s:\overline{S}\to\overline{S}
for
s\in\overline{S}
corresponds to a map
\pi S/\pi^{2}S\to\overline{S}
where
{\pi\mapsto s}
. The derivation

corresponding to this map is such that
d(ux_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}})=0
and
d(u\pi x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}})=s\cdot ux_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
. The derivation of interest is the derivation

corresponding to the linear map where

.

We also note that given any
\overline{S}
-module

and any

defined as

. Indeed, this is well defined as

is a
\overline{S}
-module and since

is a derivation, it is easy to check that

is a derivation too and we have

.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi
. Set
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\overline{S}=S/\pi S
. Let

and

to be the map

. Then

represents the functor
{\Der_{\mathbb{Z}}(S,-):\overline{S}\text{-Mod}\to\overline{S}\text{-Mod}}
.

Proof.

Consider some
\overline{S}
-module
M
and some arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}
-derivation

. Suppose

for some

. Take the derivation

as in Remark

and so

is such that
(d-m\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi})(\pi)=0
. Therefore, given the direct sum decomposition in Proposition
2.3
,

corresponds uniquely to a derivation from

. Hence there exists a unique
\overline{S}
-linear map
\phi_{1}:\Omega_{\overline{S}/k^{p}}\to M
such that

and noting Proposition

. We extend
\phi_{1}
to
\overline{S}
-linear map

. On the other hand let
\phi_{2}:\Omega^{\pi}_{S/\mathbb{Z}}\to M
be the
\overline{S}
-linear map where
\phi_{2}(d\pi)=m

\phi_{2}(dy)=0
for all basis elements

\phi

\overline{S}
-linear map such that

. If
\phi^{\prime}
is any other
\overline{S}
-linear map such that
\phi^{\prime}\circ d^{\pi}_{S/\mathbb{Z}}=d

. Since there is a
b\in S
such that

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose

and
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with a uniformizer
\pi
. Set

,

Mixed Jacobian Matrix:
Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
and
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Due to Proposition
2.8
, we can apply Theorem
1.10
to detect nonsingularity of a prime ideal of a finitely generated
V
-algebra that contains the element
\pi
or equivalently the prime integer
p
. On the other hand, for prime ideals that do not contain
\pi
, the usual Jacobian criterion suffices.

Remark 2.9
.

Suppose
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S
is an ideal and let
R=S/I
. Suppose
\mathfrak{q}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}R\setminus V(\pi)={\operatorname{Spec}}R\setminus V(p)
. Then
R_{\mathfrak{q}}=V[\pi^{-1}][x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{q}}/I
. Note that
V[\pi^{-1}]
is a characteristic
0

is detected by the usual Jacobian criterion.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S
be an ideal and set
R=S/I
. We have the ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{S}=(\pi)S
and its image
\mathfrak{a}=(\pi)R
in

, and as usual we set
\overline{S}=S/\mathfrak{a}_{S}
and
\overline{R}=R/\mathfrak{a}
. The “mixed” Jacobian matrix,

with rows:

•

[\frac{\partial(f_{1})}{\partial\pi}\,\cdots\,\frac{\partial(f_{t})}{\partial\pi}]

•

[\frac{\partial(f_{1})}{\partial x_{i}}\,\cdots\,\frac{\partial(f_{t})}{\partial x_{i}}]
for

.

•

[\frac{\partial(f_{1})}{\partial\lambda}\,\cdots\,\frac{\partial(f_{t})}{\partial\lambda}]
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
for a
p
-base
\Lambda
of

.

Theorem 2.11
.

Suppose
R=S/I
for some ideal
I\subseteq S
and
\mathfrak{q}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}R
such that
\pi\in\mathfrak{q}
. Let
h=\height IR_{\mathfrak{q}}
and let
\mathfrak{q}
also denote the image of
\mathfrak{q}
in

. Then

is a regular local ring if, and only if,
\mathfrak{q}
does not contain all

R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a regular local ring if, and only if,
{\rank_{k(\mathfrak{q})}(\mathcal{J}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}))=\height IR_{\mathfrak{q}}}
where
\mathcal{J}
is the
\overline{R}
-linear map
{\frac{I}{I^{2}+I\mathfrak{a}}\xhookrightarrow{\mathcal{J}}\overline{R}\otimes_{\overline{S}}\overline{\Omega}_{S/A}}

{\mathcal{J}([f])=1\otimes\overline{d}_{S/\mathbb{Z}}(f)}

{f\in I}
; see Theorem

. Due to Corollary

, we have
{\overline{d}_{S/\mathbb{Z}}=d^{\pi}_{S/\mathbb{Z}}}

{\rank_{k(\mathfrak{q})}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}))=\height IR_{\mathfrak{q}}}

Remark 2.12
.

For any minimal prime
\mathfrak{q}
of an ideal
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S
, we have
\rank_{k(\mathfrak{q})}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}))\leq h=\height I
. Indeed, localizing at
\mathfrak{q}
,
S_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a regular local ring of dimension

. Then we have
\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{h})S_{\mathfrak{q}}
minimally. As

, we have that

in
S_{\mathfrak{q}}
for all

. We have by the Leibniz rule
\partial f_{i}/\partial\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{h}r_{ik}(\partial g_{k}/\partial\pi)+\sum_{k=1}^{h}(\partial r_{ik}/\partial\pi)g_{k}
for

,
\partial f_{i}/\partial x_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{h}r_{ik}(\partial g_{k}/\partial x_{j})+\sum_{k=1}^{h}(\partial r_{ik}/\partial x_{j})g_{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,t
and

and
\partial f_{i}/\partial\lambda=\sum_{k=1}^{h}r_{ik}(\partial g_{k}/\partial\lambda)+\sum_{k=1}^{h}(\partial r_{ik}/\partial\lambda)g_{k}
for

and
\lambda\in\Lambda
for
\Lambda
a
p
-base of
V/\pi V
. Now modulo
\mathfrak{q}S_{\mathfrak{q}}
, the second summand goes away in all cases. Therefore,
J^{\pi}_{R}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q})=[\bar{r}_{ij}]J^{\prime\pi}
, where

denotes the mixed jacobian matrix on the generators of

. Note then that

is a matrix with

minors are

. This implies that

\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q})
is also

as this matrix factors through a

-vector space of dimension

. Therefore,
\rank_{k(\mathfrak{q})}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}\otimes_{\overline{R}}k(\mathfrak{q}))\leq h
.

.

Suppose
R=S/I
for some ideal
I\subseteq S
such that
I
is of pure height

(i.e. all minimal primes of

have the same height and

has no embedded primes). Then

\Sing R\cap{\rm{V}}(p)={\rm{V}}(I_{h}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}))\subseteq{\operatorname{Spec}}(R/(\pi)R)

where we identify

with
{\operatorname{Spec}}(R/(\pi)R)
and

denotes the ideal generated by

\mathcal{J}^{\pi}_{R}
in

.

Note that for any
\mathfrak{q}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}S
,
\height IS_{\mathfrak{q}}=h

. By Remark

, for minimal primes

is singular (i.e. not a regular local ring) if, and only if,

. Therefore, the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Noting

2.5
, given a presentation for a finitely generated algebra over a ramified discrete valuation ring,
Corollary

2.13
gives an easy way to compute its singular locus of primes containing the prime integer

. We now explain how the results of Hochster-Jeffries and Saito apply to compute singular loci of algebras over ramified discrete valuation rings.

Suppose
V
is a ramified discrete valuation of mixed characteristic with uniformizer
\pi
. We claim that
V
does not admit a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
. For sake of contradiction, assume
\delta_{p}:V/p^{2}V\to V/pV
is a
p
-derivation modulo

. Then
\delta_{p}(p)
is a unit. However, as
V
is ramified
(\overline{\pi})
is a nonzero maximal ideal of

and since
p\in(\pi)^{2}
,
\delta_{p}(p)\subseteq\delta_{p}(\pi)(\overline{\pi})
which is a contradiction. So the results in
[
5
,
8
]
cannot be extended directly.

Suppose
V
is a finite algebra over an unramified discrete valuation subring, i.e.

V\cong U[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/I

where
(U,pU)
is an unramified discrete valuation ring. In this case, any finitely generated
V
algebra can be viewed as a finitely generated
U
algebra. If we can find such a presentation for
V
, then the criterion of Hochster-Jeffries and Saito applies. For example, if
(V,\pi)
is a
\pi
-complete discrete valuation ring, then by Cohen’s structure theorem,
V
admits the desired presentation. However, given an arbitrary ramified discrete valuation
V
, it is not easy to write down the presentation as above, even for a
\pi
-complete
V
. In this sense,
Corollary

2.13
gives a simpler alternative. On the other hand, we suspect there exists a non-complete ramified discrete valuation ring that is not finitely generated over any of its unramified discrete valuation subring, but we have not been able to find such an example in the literature.

Remark 2.15
.

Suppose
(V,\pi)
is a ramified discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
\pi
such that its residue field
k=V/\pi V
is
F
-finite. Set
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and fix
\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}S\cap{\rm{V}}(\pi)
. Let
a=\log_{p}[k:k^{p}]
and
b=\log_{p}[k(\mathfrak{p}):k(\mathfrak{p})^{p}]
. We want to show that
\dim S+a=\height\mathfrak{p}+b
. This argument is carried out in proof of
[
5
, Theorem 4.13]
but their argument has a small typo so we spell out all the details here. We have
\overline{S}=S/\pi S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is an
F
-finite ring of characteristic
p>0
, so given prime ideals
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
in
\overline{S}
, we have the formula
\height(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{q})=\log_{p}[k(\mathfrak{q}):k(\mathfrak{q})^{p}]-\log_{p}[k(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}):k(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})^{p}]

[
6
]
. Applying this formula for
(0)\subseteq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, note that we get
n+a=\log_{p}[\text{Frac}(\overline{S})-\text{Frac}(\overline{S})^{p}]
. Then applying it with
(0)\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}/(\pi)
, we get
\height(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})=(n+a)-\log_{p}[k(\mathfrak{p}):k(\mathfrak{p})^{p}]=(n+a)-b
, noting that
k(\mathfrak{p})=k(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
as
\pi\in\mathfrak{p}
. Finally, since
\height(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})=\height\mathfrak{p}-1
and
\dim S=n+1
, we have
\dim S+a=\height\mathfrak{p}+b
.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
(V,\pi)
be a ramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi
such that
k=V/\pi V
is an
F
-finite field. Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
is a local algebra of essentially finite type over
V
such that
p\in\mathfrak{m}
. Set
\overline{R}=R/(\pi)R
and
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}=\Omega_{R/\mathbb{Z}}/(\pi)\Omega_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
R
is a regular local ring if, and only if,
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
is a free
\overline{R}
module of rank
\dim R+\log_{p}[K:K^{p}]
.

Proof.

We closely follow the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 4.13]
. Suppose
R=(S/I)_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
S=V[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
I=(\mathbf{f})=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\subseteq S
an ideal such that
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq I
. Let
\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})
be the mixed Jacobian matrix on the generators of
I
as in Definition
2.10
considered as a matrix over
\overline{R}
. We have by Proposition
1.6
and Corollary
2.7
that
\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})
is a presentation matrix for
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
. In particular,
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
is generated by
\dim S+a
elements where
a=\log_{p}[k:k^{p}]
which is the size of a
p
-basis of
k
. Set
b=\log_{p}[K:K^{p}]
. We have
\dim S+a=\height\mathfrak{p}+b=\dim S_{\mathfrak{p}}+b=\height I_{\mathfrak{p}}+\dim R+b
by the calculation in Remark
2.15
. Now by the characterization of locally free modules via Fitting ideals,
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
is locally free of rank
\dim R+b
if and only if
F_{\dim R+b}(\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}})=\overline{R}
and
F_{<\dim R+b}(\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}})=0
. Using the fact that
\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f})
is a presentation matrix for
\overline{\Omega}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}
and that
(\dim S+a)-(\dim R+b)=\height I
, this translates to saying
I_{\height I}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f}))=\overline{R}
and
I_{>\height I}(\mathcal{J}^{\pi}(\mathbf{f}))=0
. But this is if and only if
\rank(J^{\pi}_{S/I}\otimes_{S/I}k(\mathfrak{p}))=\height I
which by Theorem
2.11
is equivalent to
(S/I)_{\mathfrak{p}}=R
being a regular local ring.
∎

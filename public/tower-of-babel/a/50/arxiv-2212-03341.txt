On Nörlund summability of Taylor series in weighted Dirichlet spaces Thanks: ∗ berdellima@gmail.com, † \dagger nbraha@gmail.com
A. Bërdëllima ∗ and N. L. Braha †

Abstract
. In this note we show that the Taylor series of a function in a weighted Dirichlet space is (generalized) Nörlund summable, provided that the sequence determining the Nörlund operator is non-decreasing and has finite upper growth rate. In particular the Taylor series is Nörlund summable for all α > 1 / 2 \alpha>1/2 , and the rate of convergence is of the order O ( n − 1 / 2 ) O(n^{-1/2}) . The inequality α > 1 / 2 \alpha>1/2 is sharp. On the other hand if the Taylor series is Nörlund summable and the partial sums of the determining sequence enjoy a certain growth condition then the determining sequence has finite lower growth rate. An analogue result is derived for a non-increasing sequence that is uniformly bounded away from zero.



1.
Introduction

A topic of interest in complex analysis is the approximation of a holomorphic function by its Taylor series. It is known that for certain spaces of holomorphic functions defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Taylor series of a function
f
converges in norm of the space to
f
itself. Such results hold in particular for the (general) Hardy space
H^{p}
for any
1<p<+\infty
, or the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, the space of all holomorphic functions
f
defined on
\mathbb{D}
for which the Dirichlet integral

(1)

\mathcal{D}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dA(z)

is finite, where
dA
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand there are spaces of holomorphic functions where approximation in norm by Taylor series fails. Such examples include for instance the Hardy space
H^{1}
or the disk algebra
A(\mathbb{D})
as witnessed by the classical example of du Bois-Raymond
[
4
]
. However by a result of
[
5
,
6
, Fejér]
if instead the Cesàro sum is considered then Cesàro sums of Taylor series of a function converge in norm to the function itself in both aforementioned spaces. Later
[
12
, Riesz]
refined the result of Fejér for the generalized Cesáro sums for functions in
A(\mathbb{D})
. Recently as an application of their work about Hadamard multipliers on weighted Dirichlet spaces
[
8
, Mashreghi et al.]
, Mashreghi–Ransford consider generalized Cesàro summability of Taylor series for functions in a weighted Dirichlet space
[
7
]
. A weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
is similar to the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, except for (
1
) is replaced by

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z),

where
\omega
is a superharmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
. These spaces were studied by
[
1
, Aleman]
, which are a generalization of the case where
\omega
is a harmonic function. The latter were originally introduced by
[
10
, Richter]
and later studied by
[
11
, Richter–Sundberg]
.
In view of these recent developments we consider a different summability method, that of (generalized) Nörlund sums in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.
We show convergence results for the Nörlund method and we also provide rates of convergence. Differently from Cesàro method, the growth rate of the sequence that determines Nörlund’s operator plays a pivotal role. In particular we prove that when this sequence is non-decreasing and enjoys finite upper growth rate, then the Taylor series of a holomorphic function in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
is Nörlund summable in the norm of
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
(Theorem
3.1
(i)) and that this rate of convergence is of the order
O(n^{-1/2})
(Theorem
3.2
). Moreover under a certain growth condition for partial sums of the determining sequence of Nörlund operator, we get that this sequence has finite lower growth rate, whenever Taylor series are Nörlund summable (Theorem
3.1
(ii)). Like in the case of generalized Cesàro method, the lower bound
\alpha>1/2
, the parameter in the generalized Nörlund method, is sharp (Theorem
3.1
(ii)). An analogue result (Theorem
3.3
) is derived for non-increasing sequences that are uniformly bounded away from zero.

2.
Preliminary results

2.1.
Nörlund sums

Let
f(z)
be a holomorphic function of the complex variable
z
and denote by
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
its formal power series expansion. Let

(3)

s_{k}[f](z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots+a_{k}z^{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

be the
k
-th degree Taylor polynomial of
f
. Given a sequence of non-negative numbers
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that
P_{n}=p_{0}+p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the Nörlund operator
(N,(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})
acts on a sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by the formula

(4)

(Nx)_{n}:=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n-k}x_{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For more on Nörlund method and other summability methods we refer to
[
2
, Boos]
.
We define Nörlund sums of a holomorphic function
f
as follows

(5)

N_{n}[f](z):=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n-k}s_{k}[f](z),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following holds

(6)

N_{n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n-k}a_{k}z^{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proof.

From (
5
) we have

\displaystyle N_{n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}

\displaystyle p_{n-k}s_{k}[f](z)=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n-k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}z^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P_{n}}((P_{n}a_{0})+(P_{n-1}a_{1}z)+\cdots+(P_{0}a_{n}z^{n}))=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n-k}a_{k}z^{k}.

∎

In view of Lemma
2.1
we define

(7)

N^{\alpha}_{n}[f](z):=\frac{1}{P_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}P^{\alpha}_{n-k}a_{k}z^{k},\quad f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}

to be the generalized Nörlund sum
N_{n}^{\alpha}[f]
with parameter
\alpha>0
of a function
f
. It is evident that
N^{\alpha}_{n}
is a linear operator acting on the space of all holomorphic functions and its image is a certain subset of polynomials of degree
\leq n
defined on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.
Superharmonic functions and weighted Dirichlet spaces

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open set. A function
\omega:U\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is subharmonic on
U
if it is upper semicontinuous and given
z\in U
there exists
R>0
such that

(8)

\omega(z)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\omega(z+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta\quad(0\leq r<R).

A function
\omega:U\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is superharmonic on
U
if
-\omega
is subharmonic on
U
. In particular a harmonic function is both sub- and superharmonic, see e.g.
[
9
, Ransford §2.2]
. Denote by
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|z|<1\}
the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, let
\omega>0
be superharmonic, and
f
holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}
. Define

(9)

\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\omega(z)\,dA(z),

where
dA
denotes the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. The weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
is the set of all holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f)<+\infty
.
When equipped with the inner product

(10)

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}:=f(0)\,\overline{g(0)}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z),

\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
becomes a Hilbert space. This induces the norm

(11)

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}:=|f(0)|^{2}+\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f),\quad f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}.

If
\omega
is a positive superharmonic function, then there exists a unique positive finite Borel measure
\mu_{\omega}
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, such that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
it holds

(12)

\omega(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\Big|\frac{1-\overline{\zeta}\,z}{\zeta-z}\Big|\,\frac{2}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}\,d\mu_{\omega}(\zeta)+\int_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\,d\mu_{\omega}(\zeta).

When
\mu_{\omega}=\delta_{\zeta}
(the Dirac mass at
\zeta
), we write
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}
for
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 4.5.1]
. Note that one of the integrals above vanishes depending on
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
or
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. From the properties of Dirac delta function
\delta_{\zeta}
for a given holomorphic function
f
we then have

(13)

\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\Big|\frac{1-\overline{\zeta}\,z}{\zeta-z}\Big|\,\frac{2}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}\,|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z),&\zeta\in\mathbb{D}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\,|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dA(z),&\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}.\end{array}\right.

By virtue of Fubini’s Theorem one can recover
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f)
from
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)
from the integral formula
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(f)=\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)\,d\mu_{\omega}(\zeta)
. Often
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}
is called a local Dirichlet space. If
H^{2}
denotes the Hardy space, the space of holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}<\infty
,
then we have the characterization due to
[
1
, Aleman §4.1]
, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}
if and only if there exists
g\in H^{2}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that
f(z)=a+(z-\zeta)\,g(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and in this case
D_{\zeta}(f)=\|g\|_{H^{2}}
.

Given a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
we say that the Taylor series of
f
is (Nörlund)
(N,\alpha)
–summable, if
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

2.3.
Hadamard product

Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
and
g(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
be two formal power series. The Hadamard product of
f
and
g
is defined as

(14)

(f\ast g)(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}b_{k}z^{k}.

It is clear that Hadamard product is commutative and associative operation. Moreover if
f
or
g
is a polynomial then
(f\ast g)
is a polynomial too, and if both
f
and
g
are holomorphic then so is
f\ast g
. If
h
is a power series with the property that
h\ast f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
whenever
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, then
h
is a Hadamard multiplier.

Proposition 2.1
.

h
is a Hadamard multiplier for any positive superharmonic weight
\omega
, whenever
h
is a polynomial.

Proof.

Let
h=b_{0}+b_{1}z+\cdots+b_{n}z^{n}
be a polynomial then so is
h\ast f
and in particular
h\ast f
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}
. It only remains to show that
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(h\ast f)<+\infty
. Note that by definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(h\ast f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(h\ast f)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}b_{k}z^{k}|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}||b_{k}||z|^{k})^{2}\omega(z)\,dA(z)\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\omega(z)\,dA(z)

where
C_{n}:=(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}||b_{k}|)^{2}
is a certain positive constant depending on
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. From the general theory of harmonic functions, e.g. see
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.1 §2.5]
, if
\omega
is positive and superharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
then
\omega
is integrable on
\mathbb{D}
. Consequently
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(h\ast f)<+\infty
.
∎

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

(15)

h^{\alpha}_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{P_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n-k}^{\alpha}z^{k}.

In view of Hadamard product we can then write
N^{\alpha}_{n}[f](z)=(h^{\alpha}_{n}\ast f)(z)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Since
h^{\alpha}_{n}
in (
15
) is a polynomial for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
then
h^{\alpha}_{n}
is a Hadamard multiplier for any positive superharmonic weight
\omega
.

Define the matrix
T^{\alpha}_{n}
as

(16)

T^{\alpha}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}&(c_{2}-c_{1})&(c_{3}-c_{2})&\cdots\\
0&c_{2}&(c_{3}-c_{2})&\cdots\\
0&0&c_{3}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{where}\;c_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}&1\leq k\leq n\\[10.00002pt]
0&k>n.\end{array}\right.

Since
h^{\alpha}_{n}
is a polynomial for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then by
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
we have:

Corollary 2.1
.

For every superharmonic weight
\omega
it holds that
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}
, with equality if
\omega(z)=(1-|z|^{2})/|1-z|^{2}
.

As a consequence of
[
7
, Theorem 2.2 (i)]
we have the lemma:

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(17)

\|T_{n}^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq(n+1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-\Big(\frac{P_{n-k-1}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big|^{2}.

2.4.
Growth rate

The upper growth rate
\rho
of the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is defined as

(18)

\rho:=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{n},\quad\rho_{n}:=\frac{n\,p_{n}}{P_{n}}.

If
\rho<+\infty
we say that the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has finite upper growth rate. Similarly one can define the lower growth rate if limsup is replaced by liminf. Evidently a sequence with finite upper growth rate has finite lower growth rate. For instance the sequence
p_{n}=n^{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
has finite growth rate. An example of a sequence with unbounded growth rate is
p_{n}=r^{n}
for any
r>1
or the sequence
p_{n}=\ln(n+1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that the quantity
\rho_{n}
appears in
[
3
]
and plays a crucial role in determining the
\ell_{p}
norm of the Nörlund operator.
The following lemma is necessary for our main results; it ties together the importance of growth rate of the determining sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with convergence of Nörlund method.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
have finite upper growth rate, then for any polynomial
f
it holds that
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

N^{\alpha}_{n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{P_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}P_{n-k}^{\alpha}a_{k}z^{k}

implies

\displaystyle\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|N_{n}^{\alpha}[f]^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)ka_{k}z^{k-1}|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)\leq C^{\alpha}_{m,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\omega(z)\,dA(z)

where

(19)

C^{\alpha}_{m,n}=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)k|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}.

We claim that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{n-k}/P_{n}=1
for every
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
. We proceed by strong induction. For
k=1
we have

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{n}}=1-\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}=1-\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{np_{n}}{P_{n}}=1-0\cdot\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{np_{n}}{P_{n}}=1.

Note that finite upper growth rate implies finite lower growth rate and therefore the last equality is justified. Similarly

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{n}}=1-\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}=1-\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{np_{n}}{P_{n}}=1-0\cdot\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{np_{n}}{P_{n}}=1.

Consequently
\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{n-1}/P_{n}=1
. Now let the claim holds for all
k\in\{2,\cdots,m-1\}
and set
k=m
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-m}}{P_{n}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-m}}{P_{n-m+1}}\frac{P_{n-m+1}}{P_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-m}}{P_{n-m+1}}\cdot\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-m+1}}{P_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-m}}{P_{n-m+1}}

\displaystyle=1-\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{p_{n-m+1}}{P_{n-m+1}}=1-\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n-m+1}\cdot\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(n-m+1)p_{n-m+1}}{P_{n-m+1}}

\displaystyle=1-0\cdot\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(n-m+1)p_{n-m+1}}{P_{n-m+1}}=1.

Similarly one computes
\liminf_{n\to+\infty}P_{n-m}/P_{n}=1
, hence
\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{n-m}/P_{n}=1
.
Then it follows

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}C^{\alpha}_{m,n}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)k|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)k|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}=0.

Consequently
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Nörlund summability of Taylor series

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(N,(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}})
be the Nörlund operator such that
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is non-decreasing and let
\alpha>1/2
. Then the followings are true:

(i)

If
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has finite upper growth, then
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

(ii)

If additionally the growth condition

(20)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{2n}}>0

is satisfied, then
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
implies that
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has finite lower growth rate. Moreover the inequality
\alpha>1/2
is sharp.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\rho=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{n}<+\infty
. By Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq(n+1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-\Big(\frac{P_{n-k-1}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big|^{2}=\frac{n+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|P_{n-k}^{\alpha}-P_{n-k-1}^{\alpha}|^{2}.

By Mean Value Theorem, e.g. see
[
13
, Theorem 8, pp.178-9]
for each
k=1,2,\cdots,n
there is
\xi_{k}\in(P_{n-k-1},P_{n-k})
such that

|P_{n-k}^{\alpha}-P_{n-k-1}^{\alpha}|=|P_{n-k}-P_{n-k-1}|\,\alpha\xi^{\alpha-1}_{k}
and consequently we get the upper estimate

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2}\frac{n+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|P_{n-k}-P_{n-k-1}|^{2}P^{2\alpha-2}_{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2}\frac{n+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)^{2\alpha-2}\,p^{2\alpha}_{n-k}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\frac{n+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2\alpha-2}\,p^{2\alpha}_{n-k}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\frac{n+1}{n}\rho_{n}^{2\alpha}&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\frac{(n+1)(n^{2\alpha-1}+2\alpha-2)}{n^{2\alpha}}\rho_{n}^{2\alpha}&1/2<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}<\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\,\rho^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\,\rho^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon&1/2<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.\quad\text{for all}\;n\geq n(\varepsilon).

Now let
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, since polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
then there exists a sequence of polynomials
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
. Moreover

\displaystyle\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\leq\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+1)\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}.

By Lemma
2.4

\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. By Corollary
2.1
we have that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\alpha\,\rho^{\alpha}&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{(2\alpha-1)^{1/2}}\,\rho^{\alpha}&1/2<\alpha<1,\end{array}\right.,

consequently by letting
k\to+\infty
the first term in the last inequality vanishes.

(ii)

Now suppose that
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has unbounded lower growth rate. Let

\omega(z)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

then by Corollary
2.1
we have
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 2.2 (ii)]
, but instead applying it to the
(n-m+1)\times m
submatrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}(c_{2}-c_{1})&(c_{3}-c_{2})&\cdots&(c_{n-m+1}-c_{n-m})\\
(c_{2}-c_{1})&(c_{3}-c_{2})&\cdots&(c_{n-m+1}-c_{n-m})\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
(c_{2}-c_{1})&(c_{3}-c_{2})&\cdots&(c_{n-m+1}-c_{n-m})\end{pmatrix},\;\text{where}\;c_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big(\frac{P_{n-k}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}&1\leq k\leq n\\[10.00002pt]
0&k>n\end{array}\right.

we get for any
1\leq m\leq n

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq\|A\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|AA^{*}\|=\frac{n-m+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|P_{n-k}^{\alpha}-P_{n-k-1}^{\alpha}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\frac{n-m+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|P_{n-k}-P_{n-k-1}|^{2}\,\xi_{k}^{2\alpha-2},

where
\xi_{k}\in(P_{n-k-1},P_{n-k})
. From monotonicity of the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and the recurrence relation
P_{n-k}=P_{n-k-1}+p_{n-k}
we obtain the lower bound

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\frac{n-m+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}p_{n-k}^{2}\,P_{n-k-1}^{2\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\frac{n-m+1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(m-k+1)^{2\alpha-2}\,p_{n-m-1}^{2\alpha}=\alpha^{2}(n-m+1)\Big(\frac{p_{n-m-1}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{m}k^{2\alpha-2}.

For
\alpha>1/2
the last finite sum can be estimated by the elementary integrals

(21)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}k^{2\alpha-2}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int^{m}_{0}t^{2\alpha-2}\,dt=\frac{m^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\int^{m+1}_{1}t^{2\alpha-2}\,dt=\frac{(m+1)^{2\alpha-1}-1}{2\alpha-1}&1/2<\alpha<1,\end{array}\right.

implying finally the lower bound for the operator norm as follows

\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\frac{n-m+1}{m}\Big(\frac{m\,p_{n-m-1}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\frac{(n-m+1)((m+1)^{2\alpha-1}-1)}{m^{2\alpha}}\Big(\frac{m\,p_{n-m-1}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}&1/2<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.

Restricting to even indices
2n
and taking
m=n
yields

\displaystyle\|T^{\alpha}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\frac{n+1}{n}\Big(\frac{n\,p_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\Big(\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{2n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}&\alpha\geq 1\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\frac{(n+1)((n+1)^{2\alpha-1}-1)}{n^{2\alpha}}\Big(\frac{n\,p_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\Big(\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{2n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}&1/2<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.

Passing in lower limit for any
\alpha>1/2
yields

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{\alpha}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big[\Big(\frac{n\,p_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\Big(\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{2n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{n\,p_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha}\cdot\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{P_{n-1}}{P_{2n}}\Big)^{2\alpha}.

By growth condition (
20
) we have that
\beta=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}P_{n-1}/P_{2n}>0
.
Moreover
\beta\leq 1
, since
P_{n-1}\leq P_{2n}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{\alpha}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{2\alpha}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{n\,p_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha},

which tends to infinity if the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has unbounded lower growth rate for any
\alpha>1/2
.
Consequently from the inequalities

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\geq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{\alpha}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{\alpha}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}

and
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}
it follows that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=+\infty
and so
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=+\infty
. By virtue of uniform boundedness principle (Banach–Steinhaus Theorem, e.g. see
[
14
, Theorem 1, p.68]
), there exists
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
satisfying
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=+\infty
, i.e.
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\neq 0
.
Now let
\alpha=1/2
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-1}\geq\int^{n+1}_{1}t^{-1}\,dt=\ln(n+1).

On the other hand note that
P_{n}\leq(n+1)p_{n}
implies
\rho_{n}\geq n/(n+1)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus we obtain

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\|T^{1/2}_{2n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq\beta\,\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(\ln(n+1)\rho_{n})\geq\beta\,\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\ln(n+1)=+\infty.

Again by uniform boundedness principle the claim follows.

∎

Example 3.1
.

Let
p_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
P_{n}=n+1
and
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{n}=1
. Moreover
p_{n}\leq p_{n+1}
, conditions of Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled, thus
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
p_{n}=n
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
P_{n}=n(n-1)/2
and
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{n}=2
. Moreover
p_{n}<p_{n+1}
, conditions of Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled, thus
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
.

Another aspect we would like to touch is the rate at which
N_{n}^{\alpha}[f]
converges towards
f
as
n\to+\infty
.
The next result gives essentially a quantitative statement of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(N,(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}})
be the Nörlund operator with
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
non-decreasing and
\alpha>1/2
. If
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has finite upper growth, then
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=O(n^{-1/2})
for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho<+\infty
be the upper growth rate of
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. We consider only the case when
\alpha\geq 1
. The situation
1/2<\alpha<1
is treated analogously by just replacing the corresponding operator norms from Theorem
3.1
.
It suffices to prove that there is some positive constant
C
, possibly depending on
f,\omega,\rho
and
\alpha
such that
\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq C/n^{1/2}
for all sufficiently large
n
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a polynomial
f_{k}
with
\|f-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}<1/k^{5}
. On the other hand we have the estimate

(22)

\displaystyle\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}.

For the first term we have

\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq\|N^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\,\|f-f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq 2\alpha\rho^{\alpha}\,\frac{1}{k^{5}},\quad\text{for sufficiently large}\;n.

Next we get an upper estimate for the middle term in (
22
). Take
n\geq k
then

\displaystyle\|N_{n}^{\alpha}[f_{k}]-f_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f_{k}]^{\prime}(z)-f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)ja^{(k)}_{j}z^{j-1}\Big|^{2}\,\omega(z)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|j|a^{(k)}_{j}|\Big)^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{D}}\omega(z)\,dA(z)=C(n,\alpha,f_{k})\cdot C(\omega).

We need only then to show that the positive constant
C(n,\alpha,f_{k})
depends only on
\alpha,f
for sufficiently large
k
(and also
n
). Note that
C(\omega)>0
since
\omega
is a positive superharmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
. Next we find an upper estimate for the first constant above

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,f_{k})=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|j|a_{j}^{(k)}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|^{2}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}|a_{j}^{(k)}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|^{2}\Big).

By the Mean Value Theorem for every
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
there is
\xi_{j}\in(P_{n-j},P_{n})
such that
|P_{n-j}^{\alpha}-P_{n}^{\alpha}|=\alpha\xi_{j}^{\alpha-1}\,|P_{n-j}-P_{n}|
.
The last sum can be estimated from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{P_{n}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha^{2}|P_{n-j}-P_{n}|^{2}\,P^{2\alpha-2}_{n}\leq\alpha^{2}\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{P^{2}_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}\frac{k^{3}}{n^{2}}\rho_{n}^{2}.

Therefore for sufficiently large
n
we get the estimate

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{P_{n-j}}{P_{n}}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big|^{2}\leq 2\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}\frac{k^{3}}{n^{2}},

consequently
C(n,\alpha,f_{k})\leq 2\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}\,\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}k^{5}/n^{2}
. Convergence
\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}
implies
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f_{k})\to\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
for
\mu
-a.a.
\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
For
\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then by Lemma
2.2
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is
g_{k}\in H^{2}
such that
f_{k}(z)=a^{(k)}+(z-\zeta)\,g_{k}(z)
for some
a^{(k)}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f_{k})=\|g_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}
. Similarly for
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}
there is
g\in H^{2}
with
f(z)=a+(z-\zeta)\,g(z)
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)=\|g\|^{2}_{H^{2}}
.
Taking
\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f_{k})\to\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f)
implies
\|g_{k}\|_{H^{2}}\to\|g\|_{H^{2}}
. On the other hand if we write
g_{k}(z)=b_{0}^{(k)}+b_{1}^{(k)}z+\cdots+b_{k-1}^{(k)}z^{k-1}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then we have

\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\leq 2(|a^{(k)}|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}\,\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|b^{(k)}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|b_{j}^{(k)}|^{2})=2(|a^{(k)}|^{2}+(1+|\zeta|^{2})\,\|g_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}).

Moreover
\|f_{k}-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\to 0
yields in particular that
|f_{k}(0)-f(0)|=|a^{(k)}_{0}-a_{0}|\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, which together with
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|b^{(k)}_{0}|<+\infty
(since
\|g_{k}\|_{H^{2}}\to\|g\|_{H^{2}}
as
k\to+\infty
), give
\limsup_{k\to+\infty}|a^{(k)}|=|a^{*}|
for a certain
a^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
. Consequently
\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\leq 2(|a^{*}|^{2}+(1+|\zeta|^{2})\|g\|^{2}_{H^{2}})\leq 2(|a^{*}|^{2}+2\|g\|^{2}_{H^{2}})
. Let
C(f)=2(|a^{*}|^{2}+2\|g\|^{2}_{H^{2}})=2(|a^{*}|^{2}+2\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}(f))
then for sufficiently large
k
(and hence for sufficiently large
n
) we have
C(n,\alpha,f_{k})\leq 4\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}C(f)k^{5}/n^{2}
. Taking
n=k^{5}
we can write
C(n,\alpha,f_{k})\leq 4\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}C(f)/n
and so

\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\leq(2\alpha\rho^{\alpha}+2\alpha\rho(C(f)C(\omega))^{1/2}+1)\frac{1}{n^{1/2}},\quad\text{for all sufficiently large}\;n.

∎

We close this section with an analogue result when the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is non-increasing, provided that
\liminf_{n\to+\infty}p_{n}>0
. This last condition is essential in getting uniform upper bound for the operator norm
\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}
.
In this situation, unlike for non-decreasing sequences, the assumption of finite upper growth rate is redundant, since from non-increasing property it holds that
P_{n}\geq(n+1)p_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, consequently
\rho_{n}\leq n/(n+1)
and in particular
\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{n}\leq 1
. We state the next result without proof.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(N,(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}})
be the Nörlund operator with
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
non-increasing and satisfying
\liminf_{n\to+\infty}p_{n}>0
, and let
\alpha>1/2
. Then
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]-f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}=0
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Moreover the convergence rate is of the order
O(n^{-1/2})
.

Question 3.1
.

Is
\alpha=1/2
a sharp bound in this case? Can the convergence rate in Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
be improved?

Question 3.2
.

Is generalized Nörlund method monotone? In other words does
N^{\alpha}_{n}[f]\to f
imply
N^{\beta}_{n}[f]\to f
when
\alpha\leq\beta
?

Discussion

The sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is assumed to be monotone. In fact this condition could be relaxed to eventually monotone, that is there is some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
(p_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
is monotone. Indeed this would not affect the analysis since both the upper and lower estimates for the operator norm
\|T^{\alpha}_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}
would only differ by an additive term (sum of
P_{k}
–like terms up to index
n_{0}
divided by
p_{n}^{2\alpha}
) which in the limit would be bounded by a uniform positive constant. The situation for a general sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, not necessarily monotone, remains also to be investigated.

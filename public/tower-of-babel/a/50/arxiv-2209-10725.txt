Para-Bannai–Ito Polynomials
Jonathan PELLETIER a, Luc VINET ab and Alexei ZHEDANOV c

Abstract
New bispectral polynomials orthogonal on a Bannai–Ito bi-lattice (uniform quadri-lattice) are obtained from an unconventional truncation of the untruncated Bannai–Ito and complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials. A complete characterization of the resulting para-Bannai–Ito polynomials is provided, including a three term recurrence relation, a Dunkl-difference equation, an explicit expression in terms of hypergeometric series and an orthogonality relation. They are also derived as a q → − 1 q\to-1 limit of the q q -para-Racah polynomials. A connection to the dual − 1 -1 Hahn polynomials is also established.

Introduction

Following the classification of Leonard
[
14
]
, Bannai and Ito introduced a new classification of orthogonal polynomials as the polynomials that satisfy the Leonard duality property
[
1
]
. With it, they introduced the first
-1
orthogonal polynomials: the Bannai–Ito polynomials. Vinet, Zhedanov and collaborators have since lead the way in research on
-1
orthogonal polynomials starting with
[
17
,
18
]
and giving an almost complete description of the
-1
part of the Askey scheme. In parallel, the same group of people introduced a new category of orthogonal polynomials: the para-polynomials
[
12
,
19
]
. The two categories are, a priori, not exclusive, which raise the question of the existence of a family of orthogonal polynomials at the crossing of the two. In this paper, we answer that question at the affirmative by presenting the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials.

There are many reasons to be interested in a
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials. From a mathematical physics standpoint, since the discovery of the para-Krawtchouk polynomials
[
19
]
, para-polynomials have been linked to perfect state transfer (PST) and fractional revival (FR) in
XX
-spin chains with nearest neighbour couplings (see also
[
11
]
). A necessary feature for PST is the persymmetry (i.e., symmetry under anti-diagonal reflections) of the underlying Jacobi matrix and, for FR
[
6
]
, the existence of an isospectral deformation of this persymmetric matrix. The models with these state transport properties that are based on
-1
orthogonal polynomials have not been developed. From a mathematical viewpoint, para-polynomials have appeared as basis functions for the finite-dimensional representations of algebras of the Sklyanin type
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
. The
q
-para-Racah polynomials arise in particular in connection with the degenerate Sklyanin algebra
[
8
]
. The
q\to-1
limits of these
q
-para-Racah polynomials therefore stand to provide a representation basis for a
-1
version of this algebra. The study of the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials is also of interest in the elaboration of the scheme of -1 orthogonal polynomials, but also to obtain the complete set of para-polynomials; Indeed, of the three types of grid obtained from the general Askey–Wilson grid in the cases
|q|\neq 1
,
q=1
and
q=-1
, only the Bannai–Ito grid (
q=-1
) has not been investigated as a potential candidate to yield a new family of orthogonal polynomials of the para type (appreciating that the quadratic bi-lattice grid leads to the para-Racah polynomials
[
12
]
and that the
q
-quadratic bi-lattice grid takes one to the
q
-para-Racah polynomials
[
13
]
). As such, the category of para-polynomials is characterized by three main properties. Each family of para-polynomials is a finite system of orthogonal polynomials obtained from a unconventional truncation of a classical family, the Jacobi matrix representing the recurrence relation is persymmetric or an isospectral deformation of a persymmetric matrix and the polynomials are orthogonal on the union of two regular lattices (regular lattices are limits of the
q
-quadratic lattice).

The goal of this paper is to fully characterize the
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials. We name them para-Bannai–Ito polynomials, as they are obtained from an unconventional truncation of the general Bannai–Ito
[
15
]
and general complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials
[
7
]
. The paper has the following structure. In Section
2
, we review some important properties of the general Bannai–Ito and complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials, as well as their relations via Christoffel/Geronimus transformations
[
9
,
20
]
. In Section
3
, the truncation condition for the general complementary Bannai–Ito with
N=2j
(
j
even) is presented with the corresponding three-term recurrence relation, the Dunkl-difference equation, the explicit expression in terms of hypergeometric series and the orthogonality relation. A Geronimus transformation is used to obtain, in Section
4
, the recurrence relation of the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials for
N=2j+1
(
j
even) through the same truncation, albeit in the case of the general Bannai–Ito polynomials. The corresponding cases for
j
odd are treated in Appendixes
A
and
B
. In Section
5
, the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials are obtained as a
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials and a connection to the dual
-1
Hahn polynomials is presented.

2
General Complementary Bannai–Ito and Bannai–Ito

Let us first review some properties of the general untruncated complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials. Together with the general Bannai–Ito polynomials, they sit atop the
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-Askey scheme and depend on four parameters
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
,
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
. They admit an explicit expression given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2n}(x;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},r_{1},r_{2})=l_{n}^{(1)}\mathbf{W}_{n}\left({({\rm i}x)^{2};\rho_{2},\rho_{1}+1,-r_{1}+\frac{1}{2},-r_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\right),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2n+1}(x;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},r_{1},r_{2})=(x-\rho_{2})l_{n}^{(2)}\mathbf{W}_{n}\left({({\rm i}x)^{2};\rho_{2}+1,\rho_{1}+1,-r_{1}+\frac{1}{2},-r_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\right),

(2.2)

where
l_{n}^{(i)}
are normalizing factor to ensure monicity,
\mathbf{W}_{n}\big(x^{2}\big)
are Wilson polynomials given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{n}\bigl(x^{2};a,b,c,d\bigr)

\displaystyle={}_{4}F_{3}\biggl(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n,n+a+b+c+d-1,a-{\rm i}x,a+{\rm i}x}{a+b,a+c,a+d};1\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n,n+a+b+c+d-1,a-{\rm i}x,a+{\rm i}x)_{k}}{(1)_{k}(a+b,a+c,a+d)_{k}},

(2.3)

and
(a)_{k}=(a)(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
and
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})_{k}=(a_{1})_{k}(a_{2})_{k}\cdots(a_{n})_{k}
are the standard Pochhammer symbols.

These polynomials obey the three term recurrence relation

\displaystyle x\mathbf{I}_{n}(x)=\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(x)+(\rho_{1}-A_{n}-C_{n})\mathbf{I}_{n}(x)+A_{n-1}C_{n}\mathbf{I}_{n-1}(x),

with recurrence coefficients

\displaystyle A_{n}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\left(n+2\rho_{2}-2r_{2}+1\right)\left(n+2\rho_{2}-2r_{1}+1\right)}{4(n+g+1)},&n\text{ even},\\
-\dfrac{(n+1)\left(n-2r_{1}-2r_{2}+1\right)}{4(n+g+1)},&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\left(n+2\rho_{1}-2r_{1}+1\right)\left(n+2\rho_{1}-2r_{2}+1\right)}{4(n+g+1)},&n\text{ even},\\
\dfrac{(n+2g+1)\left(n+2\rho_{1}+2\rho_{2}+1\right)}{4(n+g+1)},&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}

(2.4)

where
g=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}-r_{1}-r_{2}
.

The general Bannai–Ito polynomials can be presented as the Geronimus transformation of those
\mathbf{I}_{n}
with parameter
\rho_{1}
. The general Bannai–Ito are hence given in terms of general complementary Bannai–Ito as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{n}(x;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},r_{1},r_{2})=\mathbf{I}_{n}(x;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},r_{1},r_{2})-A_{n-1}\mathbf{I}_{n-1}(x;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},r_{1},r_{2}).

They obey the three term recurrence relation

\displaystyle x\mathbf{B}_{n}(x)=\mathbf{B}_{n+1}(x)+(\rho_{1}-A_{n-1}-C_{n})\mathbf{B}_{n}(x)+A_{n-1}C_{n-1}\mathbf{B}_{n-1}(x),

(2.5)

where
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
are as in (
2.4
).

Dunkl-difference equations are presented for the complementary Bannai–Ito and Bannai–Ito polynomials in
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively. It can be seen from formulas (
2.4
) that the positivity condition
u_{n}=A_{n-1}C_{n}>0
cannot be achieved for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for the general complementary Bannai–Ito. Nevertheless, it is possible to obtain a finite set of
N+1
orthogonal polynomials by using as truncation conditions
u_{0}=A_{-1}C_{0}=0
and
u_{N+1}=A_{N}C_{N+1}=0
. The first condition is always respected, but a parametrization dependent on
N
and its parity is needed for the second condition, and it can be realized in many ways. Suppose that N is even. Then, the truncation conditions
(N+2\rho_{2}-2r_{2}+1)=0
,
(N+2\rho_{2}-2r_{1}+1)=0
or
(N+2\rho_{1}+2\rho_{2}+2)=0
all lead to the usual complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials. However, using a parametrization such that

\displaystyle\left({N+2g+2}\right)=0,

(2.6)

when
N
is even leads to an admissible truncation and to a different result. One should be careful when choosing a parametrization for this last truncation, since a zero is introduced in the denominator of the coefficients
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
for
n\sim N/2
. The parametrization needs to ensure that a cancellation occurs to have finite expressions in those cases. A very similar truncation arises for the general Bannai–Ito but for
N
odd, and it reads

\displaystyle(N+2g+1)=0.

(2.7)

These truncation conditions lead to a new family of orthogonal polynomials: the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials. Taking
N=2j
in (
2.6
) and
N=2j+1
in (
2.7
), both truncation conditions are identical and correspond to

\displaystyle(j+g+1)=0.

(2.8)

The parametrization then only depends on the parity of
j
.

3
Para-Bannai–Ito polynomials for
\boldsymbol{N=2j}
,
\boldsymbol{j}
even

In this section, we obtain the recurrence relation of the para-Bannai–Ito for
N=2j
,
j
even, by applying the truncation condition (
2.8
) to the general complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials. An appropriate parametrization is

\displaystyle\rho_{1}-r_{1}=-\frac{j+1}{2}+e_{1}t,\qquad\rho_{2}-r_{2}=-\frac{j+1}{2}+e_{2}t,

(3.1)

and (
2.8
) is achieved in the limit
t\to 0
.

3.1
Recurrence relation

Inserting (
3.1
) in (
2.4
) and using the change of parameters

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\frac{b-j-1+a}{4},\qquad\rho_{2}=\frac{b-j-1-a}{4},

\displaystyle\frac{e_{1}}{e_{1}+e_{2}}=\alpha,\qquad\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}+e_{2}}=(1-\alpha),

(3.2)

it is seen that
A_{j}^{0}
and
C_{j}^{0}
only depend on
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, through combinations. That can be described in terms of a single parameter
\alpha
as above. The recurrence relation for the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials
\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x;a,b,\alpha,2j)
reads

\displaystyle x\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x)=\mathbf{P}_{n+1}^{(0)}(x)+\left({\frac{b-j-1+a}{4}-A_{n}^{0}-C_{n}^{0}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x)+A_{n-1}^{0}C_{n}^{0}\mathbf{P}_{n-1}^{(0)}(x),

(3.3)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{0}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{4}(n-j-a),&\ n\text{ even},\ n\neq j,\\
-\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{(n+1)(n-j-b)}{(n-j)},&n\text{ odd},\\
\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)a,&n\text{ even},\ n=j.\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{0}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{4}(n-j+a),&n\text{ even},\ n\neq j,\\
\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{(n-2j-1)(n-j+b)}{(n-j)},&n\text{ odd},\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\alpha a,&n\text{ even},\ n=j.\end{cases}

(3.4)

It is now clear that the truncation condition
u_{0}=u_{2j+1}=0
is achieved. In order to respect the positivity condition
u_{n}=A_{n-1}^{0}C_{n}^{0}>0
for
n\in\{1,2,\dots,2j\}
and obtain a finite set of orthogonal polynomials, one must choose between the two sets of restrictions on the parameters,

\displaystyle a\leq-j-1,\qquad|b|\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1,\qquad\text{ or }

\displaystyle b\geq j,\qquad|a+1|\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1.

(3.5)

It is useful to note that the coefficients of the recurrence relation (
3.3
) are persymmetric when
\alpha=1/2
(i.e.,
u_{n}=u_{N-n+1}
and
b_{n}=b_{N-n}
where
b_{n}
is the coefficient in front of the diagonal term in the recurrence relation). This persymmetry is seen to be tantamount to both conditions
A_{n}^{0}+C_{n}^{0}=A_{2j-n}^{0}+C_{2j-n}^{0}
and
A_{n-1}^{0}C_{n}^{0}=A_{2j-n}^{0}C_{2j-n+1}^{0}
. These conditions are fulfilled when
\alpha=1/2
because one can verify that
A_{n}^{0}=C_{2j-n}^{0}
and
C_{n}^{0}=A_{2j-n}^{0}
which together solve the two conditions. This property will be useful to obtain the orthogonality relation.

3.2
Dunkl-difference equation

It is possible to obtain the Dunkl-difference equation for the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials from the one of the complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials via the truncation (
3.1
). This procedure is straightforward for this equation since no divergence occur. It reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\beta}\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x)=\Lambda_{n}^{(\beta)}\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x),

with the following eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2n}^{(\beta)}=n(n-j),\qquad\Lambda_{2n+1}^{(\beta)}=n(n+1-j)+\beta.

The operator
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\beta}=\mathcal{D}_{0}+\beta\frac{(x-\rho_{2})}{2x}(I-R),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}=A(x)T^{+}+B(x)T^{-}+C(x)R+D(x)T^{+}R-(A(x)+B(x)+C(x)+D(x))I,

where
T^{\pm}f(x)=f(x\pm 1)
,
Rf(x)=f(-x)
and

\displaystyle A(x)=\frac{\left(x+\rho_{1}+1\right)\left(x+\rho_{2}+1\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{1}-j\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{2}-j\right)}{8(x+1)(2x+1)},

\displaystyle B(x)=\frac{\left(x-\rho_{2}\right)\left(x-\rho_{1}-1\right)\left(2x+2\rho_{1}+j\right)\left(2x+2\rho_{2}+j\right)}{8x(2x-1)},

\displaystyle C(x)=\frac{\left(x-\rho_{2}\right)\left(4x^{2}+\omega\right)}{8x}-\frac{\left(x-\rho_{2}\right)\left(x+\rho_{1}+1\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{1}-j\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{2}-j\right)}{8x(2x+1)}-B(x),

\displaystyle D(x)=\frac{\rho_{2}\left(x+\rho_{1}+1\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{1}-j\right)\left(2x-2\rho_{2}-j\right)}{8x(x+1)(2x+1)},

\displaystyle\omega=(2\rho_{1}+j)(2\rho_{2}+j)-4(1+\rho_{1})(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+j).

One needs to use the change of parameters (
3.2
) in order to obtain the result above. Observe that
\Lambda_{n}^{(\beta)}=\Lambda_{2j-n}^{(\beta)}
and that the spectrum is hence degenerate. This means that the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials are bispectral, but not classical.

3.3
Explicit expression

The explicit expression for the para-Bannai–Ito is obtained by using the limit described after (
3.1
) in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Let
A_{n,k}^{(I)}
and
B_{n,k}^{(I)}
be the coefficients of decomposition of the Wilson polynomials as in (
2.3
) for
\mathbf{I}_{2n}(x)
and
\mathbf{I}_{2n+1}(x)
. The complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials can then be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2n}(x)=l_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}^{(I)},\qquad\mathbf{I}_{2n+1}(x)=l_{n}^{(2)}(x-\rho_{2})\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}^{(I)}.

Using this decomposition, the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials can be obtained by taking the limit directly in those coefficients,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2n}^{(0)}(x)=\kappa_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lim_{t\to 0}A_{n,k}^{(I)}=\kappa_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k},

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2n+1}^{(0)}(x)=\kappa_{n}^{(2)}(x-\rho_{2})\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lim_{t\to 0}B_{n,k}^{(I)}=\kappa_{n}^{(2)}\left({x-\frac{b-j-1-a}{4}}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k},

3.4
Orthogonality relation

To obtain the orthogonality relation, we first need to determine the grid or orthogonality lattice. It is provided by the eigenvalues of the Jacobi matrix or, equivalently, by the zeroes of the characteristic polynomial
\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)}(x)
. We can define
\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)}(x)
using the recurrence relation (
3.3
) and inserting the expression of
\mathbf{P}_{2j}^{(0)}(x)
and
\mathbf{P}_{2j-1}^{(0)}(x)
given in Section
3.3
. An extraction of the zeroes can be done with the help of the Saalschütz summation formula to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)}(x)=\prod_{k=0}^{j}\left({x+(-1)^{k}\left({\frac{2k-j-b+a}{4}}\right)+\frac{1}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)}(x) =}{}\times\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}\left({x+(-1)^{k}\left({\frac{2k-j-b-a}{4}}\right)+\frac{1}{4}}\right).

The set of
2j+1
para-Bannai–Ito polynomials will thus be orthogonal on a Bannai–Ito bi-lattice with
2j+1
grid points.

\displaystyle x_{2s}=-(-1)^{s}\left({\frac{2s-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j\},

\displaystyle x_{2s+1}=-(-1)^{s}\left({\frac{2s-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j-1\}.

A depiction of the grid is given in Appendix
C

\displaystyle x_{4s}=-\left({\frac{4s-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+1}=-\left({\frac{4s-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2-1\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+2}=\left({\frac{4s+2-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2-1\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+3}=\left({\frac{4s+2-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2-1\}.

From the theory of orthogonal polynomials
[
4
]
, the weights are given by

\displaystyle w_{s}=\frac{u_{1}\cdots u_{2j}}{\mathbf{P}_{2j}^{(0)}(x_{s})\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)\prime}(x_{s})},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,2j,

(3.6)

in terms of which the orthogonality relation of the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials will read

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{2j}w_{s}\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x_{s})\mathbf{P}_{m}^{(0)}(x_{s})=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}\delta_{nm}.

(3.7)

In
[
6
]
, a method to derive the weight for persymmetric orthogonal polynomials was presented together with a procedure to also achieve that after an isospectral deformation. This is exactly the situation that the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials present. Now, the usual expression

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{N}^{(0)}(x_{s})=(-1)^{N+s}\sqrt{u_{1}\cdots u_{N}}

(3.8)

for
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{(0)}(x_{s})
is predicated on the eigenvalues of the Jacobi matrix ordered in an increasing fashion which is not the case in the presentation of the bi-Bannai–Ito grid given above. Nonetheless, it is possible to alter the increasing order condition and to require instead that between any two zeroes of even index, there must be a zero of odd index and vice versa. Using this modified condition, the interlacing zeroes property and the persymmetry, when
N=2j
, we still find for
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{(0)}(x_{s})
the expression (
3.8
). In the case of the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials, positivity ensures that the modified condition for the eigenvalues is achieved. Recalling that the polynomials are persymmetric, we then have that for
\alpha=1/2
,

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{s}=\frac{(-1)^{s}\sqrt{u_{1}\cdots u_{N}}}{\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)\prime}(x_{s})},

(3.9)

with the
u_{n}
also evaluated at
\alpha=1/2
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{4s}=\frac{h_{2j}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s}\bigl(-s-\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1+a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{4s+1}=-\frac{h_{2j}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s-1}\bigl(-s+\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1-a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{4s+2}=-\frac{h_{2j}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s-1}\bigl(-s-\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1+a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{4s+3}=\frac{h_{2j}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s-1}\bigl(-s+\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}},

with

\displaystyle h_{2j}=\sqrt{u_{1}\cdots u_{2j}}=\frac{(1)_{j}\bigl(\frac{b+1-j}{2}\bigr)_{j}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{j}}{2^{2j}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{j}}.

It was also shown in
[
6
]
that after an isospectral deformation, the new weights are related to the persymmetric weights by a multiplicative factor dependent on
\alpha
in our case,

\displaystyle w_{s}=C\left({1+\beta(-1)^{s}}\right)\tilde{w}_{s}.

Using (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) for the first few
j
, we can solve for
C
and
\beta
. One can verify that
C=1
and
\beta=2\alpha-1
which gives

\displaystyle w_{2s}=2\alpha\tilde{w}_{2s},\qquad w_{2s+1}=2(1-\alpha)\tilde{w}_{2s+1}.

We also have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{j}w_{2s}=\alpha,\qquad\sum_{s=0}^{j}w_{2s+1}=1-\alpha,

which generalize a known result for mirror-symmetric Jacobi matrices. Finally, we give a general expression for
h_{n}=\sqrt{u_{1}\cdots u_{n}}
which can be squared and inserted in (
3.7
) to complete the characterization of the para-Bannai–Ito for
N=2j
with
j
even,

\displaystyle h_{2n}=\frac{\sqrt{(-j)_{n}(1)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1+b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(\frac{2+a-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}}}{2^{2n}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}}\times\begin{cases}1,&n<\frac{j}{2},\\[2.84544pt]
\sqrt{2\alpha},&n=\frac{j}{2},\\[2.84544pt]
2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)},&n>\frac{j}{2},\end{cases}

\displaystyle h_{2n+1}=\frac{\sqrt{(-1)(-j)_{n+1}(1)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1+b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n+1}\bigl(\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{n+1}\bigl(\frac{2+a-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}}}{2^{2n+1}\sqrt{\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{n+1}}}

\displaystyle\phantom{h_{2n+1}=}{}\times\begin{cases}1,&n<\frac{j}{2},\\[2.84544pt]
2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)},&n\geq\frac{j}{2}.\end{cases}

\boldsymbol{N=2j+1}
,

The general complementary Bannai–Ito and the general Bannai–Ito polynomials are related by a Geronimus transformation with parameter
\rho_{1}

N=2j+1
,

N+1=2j+2
polynomials that correspond to the truncation (
3.1
) of the general Bannai–Ito polynomials. The eigenvalue
{x_{s}=\rho_{1}}
is then added to the spectrum. The treatment of the case
N=2j+1
,
j
even, is analogous to the case
N=2j
,
j
even. The results are presented below.

4.1
Recurrence relation

Using the truncation (
3.1
) in (
2.5
) gives the recurrence relation for
\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}\left({x;a,b,\alpha,2j+1}\right)

\displaystyle x\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x)=\mathbf{P}_{n+1}^{(1)}(x)+\left({\frac{b-j-1+a}{4}-A_{n}^{1}-C_{n}^{1}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x)+A_{n-1}^{1}C_{n}^{1}\mathbf{P}_{n-1}^{(1)}(x),

(4.1)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{1}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{4}(n-j+a),&n\text{ even},\ n\neq j,\\[8.5359pt]
\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{(n-2j-1)(n-j+b)}{(n-j)},&n\text{ odd},\\[8.5359pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\alpha a,&n\text{ even},\ n=j,\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{1}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{n(n-j-1-b)}{(n-j-1)},&n\text{ even},\\[8.5359pt]
-\dfrac{1}{4}(n-j-1-a),&n\text{ odd},\ n\neq j+1,\\[8.5359pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)a,&n\text{ odd},\ n=j+1.\end{cases}

The positivity condition is achieved if one chooses the parameters such that

\displaystyle|a|\geq j+1,\qquad|b|\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1.

Again, the polynomials are persymmetric if
\alpha=1/2
.

4.2
Dunkl-difference equation

Since no divergences appear in the Dunkl-difference equation of the general Bannai–Ito polynomials under the para truncation, it follows that the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials are solutions to the same equation except for a change of variable. It reads as

\displaystyle L\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x)=\lambda_{n}\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x),

with the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{2n}=n,\qquad\lambda_{2n+1}=\left({j-n}\right).

The Dunkl-difference operator is

\displaystyle L=F(x)(I-R)+G(x)\bigl(T^{+}R-I\bigr),

where

\displaystyle G(x)=\frac{(4x+1+a-b-j)(4x+1-a-b-j)}{16(2x+1)},

\displaystyle F(x)=\frac{(4x+1+j+a-b)(4x+1+j-a-b)}{32x}.

4.3
Explicit expression

Since the para-Bannai–Ito for
N=2j
and
N=2j+1
are related by a Geronimus transformation, we obtain directly the expression for the case
N=2j+1
with
j
even as a combination of two para-Bannai–Ito polynomials with
N=2j
. The explicit expression is

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x)=\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x)-C_{n}^{1}\mathbf{P}_{n-1}^{(0)}(x),

where
C_{n}^{1}
is as in (
4.1
).

4.4
Orthogonality relation

We know that the Geronimus transformation will only add

to the spectrum provided by the zeros of
\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(0)}
. It turns out that this fits with the grid of Section

\displaystyle x_{2s}=-(-1)^{s}\left({\frac{2s-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j\},

\displaystyle x_{2s+1}=-(-1)^{s}\left({\frac{2s-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j\},

(4.3)

or again as a linear quadri-lattice

\displaystyle x_{4s}=-\left({\frac{4s-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+1}=-\left({\frac{4s-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+2}=\left({\frac{4s+2-j-b+a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2-1\},

\displaystyle x_{4s+3}=\left({\frac{4s+2-j-b-a}{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{4},\qquad s\in\{0,1,\dots,j/2-1\}.

The orthogonality relation is again of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{2j+1}w_{s}\mathbf{P}_{n}^{(1)}(x_{s})\mathbf{P}_{m}^{(1)}(x_{s})=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}\delta_{nm},

where

\displaystyle w_{s}=\frac{u_{1}\cdots u_{2j+1}}{\mathbf{P}_{2j+1}^{(1)}(x_{s})\mathbf{P}_{2j+2}^{(1)\prime}(x_{s})},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,2j+1.

The computation in the persymmetric case followed by the generalization with an isospectral deformation gives the weights

\displaystyle w_{4s}=\frac{2\alpha h_{2j+1}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s}\bigl(-s-\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1+a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}},

\displaystyle w_{4s+1}=-\frac{2(1-\alpha)h_{2j+1}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s}\bigl(-s+\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1-a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}},

\displaystyle w_{4s+2}=-\frac{2\alpha h_{2j+1}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s-1}\bigl(-s-\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1+a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}},

\displaystyle w_{4s+3}=\frac{2(1-\alpha)h_{2j+1}}{(-s)_{s}(1)_{j/2-s-1}\bigl(-s+\frac{a}{2}\bigr)_{j/2}\bigl(s+\frac{1-a-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}\bigl(s+\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j/2+1}},

with

\displaystyle h_{2j+1}=\frac{(1)_{j}\bigl(\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{j}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{j+1}}{2^{2j+1}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{j}}.

To complete the characterization for
N=2j+1
with
j

h_{n}=\sqrt{u_{1}\cdots u_{n}}
with

are

\displaystyle h_{2n}=\frac{\sqrt{(-j)_{n}(1)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1+b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(-\frac{j-a}{2}\bigr)_{n}}}{2^{2n}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}}\times\begin{cases}1,&n\leq\frac{j}{2},\\[2.84544pt]
2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)},&n>\frac{j}{2},\end{cases}

\displaystyle h_{2n+1}=\frac{\sqrt{(-1)(-j)_{n}(1)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1-b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(\frac{1+b-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}\bigl(-\frac{j+a}{2}\bigr)_{n+1}\bigl(-\frac{j-a}{2}\bigr)_{n+1}}}{2^{2n+1}\bigl(\frac{1-j}{2}\bigr)_{n}}

\displaystyle\phantom{h_{2n+1}=}{}\times\begin{cases}1,&n<\frac{j}{2},\\[2.84544pt]
2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)},&n\geq\frac{j}{2}.\end{cases}

5
Para-Bannai–Ito polynomials as a
\boldsymbol{q\to-1}
limit

of the
\boldsymbol{q}
-para-Racah polynomials

In
[
13
]
, the
q
-para-Racah polynomials were introduced as a
q
generalization of the para-Racah polynomials. In this section, we show that the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials can be obtained as a
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials, reinforcing their position in the family of para polynomials. The monic
q
-para-Racah polynomials for
N=2j+1
satisfy the recurrence relation

\displaystyle x\mathbf{R}_{n}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{n+1}(x)+\left({\frac{c+c^{-1}}{2}-A_{n}^{R}-C_{n}^{R}}\right)\mathbf{R}_{n}(x)+A_{n-1}^{R}C_{n}^{R}\mathbf{R}_{n-1}(x),

with

\displaystyle A_{n}^{R}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\left(1-cdq^{n}\right)\left(d-cq^{n-j}\right)\left(1-q^{n-2j-1}\right)}{2cd\left(1-q^{2n-2j-1}\right)\left(1+q^{n-j}\right)},&n\neq j,\\[5.69046pt]
\dfrac{\alpha\left(1-cdq^{j}\right)(d-c)\left(1-q^{-j-1}\right)}{2cd\left(1-q^{-1}\right)},&n=j,\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{R}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\left(1-q^{n}\right)\left(c-dq^{n-j-1}\right)\left(cd-q^{n-2j-1}\right)}{2cd\left(1+q^{n-j-1}\right)\left(1-q^{2n-2j-1}\right)},&n\neq j+1,\\[5.69046pt]
\dfrac{(1-\alpha)\left(1-q^{j+1}\right)(c-d)\left(cd-q^{-j}\right)}{2cd(1-q)},&n=j+1.\end{cases}

In order to recover the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials, one needs to use the parametrization

\displaystyle q=-{\rm e}^{\varepsilon},\qquad c={\rm i}\text{exp}\left(\varepsilon\left[{\frac{a+b-j}{2}}\right]\right),\qquad d={\rm i}\text{exp}\left(\varepsilon\left[{\frac{-a+b-j}{2}}\right]\right).

(5.1)

After redefining the resulting family of polynomials through the following affine transformation of the variable

\displaystyle x\to 2{\rm i}\varepsilon\left({x+\frac{1}{4}}\right),

we have

\displaystyle x\mathbf{\tilde{R}}_{n}(x)=\mathbf{\tilde{R}}_{n+1}(x)+\left({\frac{c+c^{-1}}{4{\rm i}\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{A_{n}^{R}}{2{\rm i}\varepsilon}-\frac{C_{n}^{R}}{2{\rm i}\varepsilon}}\right)\mathbf{\tilde{R}}_{n}(x)+\frac{A_{n-1}^{R}}{2{\rm i}\varepsilon}\frac{C_{n}^{R}}{2{\rm i}\varepsilon}\mathbf{\tilde{R}}_{n-1}(x).

(5.2)

Using the parametrization (
5.1
) and taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, equivalent to
q\to-1
, one sees that (
5.2
) goes into the recurrence relation of the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials with
N=2j+1
and
j
even. While this was not discussed in
[
13
]
, the
N=2j+1
and
N=2j
cases of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials are related by Christoffel and Geronimus transformations, which is the analog of the relation presented before. So the limit process also works for the
q
-para-Racah with
N=2j
, and gives the para-Bannai–Ito with
N=2j
and
j
even and the recurrence relation (
3.4
).

7
Conclusion

To summarize, the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials have been introduced and characterized. They are obtained by a
q\to-1
limit of the
q
-para-Racah polynomials, but also as a special truncation of the general Bannai–Ito and complementary Bannai–Ito polynomials. Their explicit expressions in terms of hypergeometric series was derived as well as their recurrence relation and Dunkl-difference equation. It was shown that the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials are orthogonal on a finite bi-Bannai–Ito grid (linear quadri-lattice). The para-Bannai–Ito have a connection to the dual
-1
Hahn polynomials (orthogonal on single Bannai–Ito grid or linear bi-lattice) and to the shifted Krawtchouk polynomials (orthogonal on a linear lattice).

The Jacobi matrix of the para-Bannai–Ito is an isospectral deformation of the persymmetric one when
\alpha=1/2
. Such matrices have been used in the design of spin chains with fractional revival. It would be of interest to examine if models based on the para-Bannai–Ito polynomials exhibit similar properties.

Depiction of the Bannai–Ito bi-lattice

We show here an example of the Bannai–Ito bi-lattice for

and
c>0
, which correspond to the first set of restrictions of (
3.5
) with
j

c
, and consequently
a
, controls the gap between the positive and negative part of the grid. As for the parameter
b

and the distances
d_{3}
and

\displaystyle d_{1}=\frac{c+2-b}{4},\qquad d_{2}=\frac{b+c}{4},\qquad d_{3}=\frac{1+b}{2},\qquad d_{4}=\frac{1-b}{2}.

\displaystyle d_{1}=\frac{c}{4}+\frac{1}{2},\qquad d_{2}=\frac{c}{4},\qquad d_{3}=\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, if we use
c=0
(or
a=-j-1
), we obtain one simple linear grid where the points are all separated by
d=1/2

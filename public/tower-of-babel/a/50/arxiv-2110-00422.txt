Domain walls in the coupled Gross–Pitaevskii equations with the harmonic potential
Andres Contreras, Dmitry E. Pelinovsky, Valeriy Slastikov

Abstract
. We study the existence and variational characterization of steady states in a coupled system of Gross–Pitaevskii equations modeling two-component Bose-Einstein condensates with the magnetic field trapping. The limit with no trapping has been the subject of recent works where domain walls have been constructed and several properties, including their orbital stability have been derived. Here we focus on the full model with the harmonic trapping potential and characterize minimizers according to the value of the coupling parameter γ \gamma . We first establish a rigorous connection between the two problems in the Thomas-Fermi limit via Γ \Gamma -convergence. Then, we identify the ranges of γ \gamma for which either the symmetric states ( γ < 1 ) (\gamma<1) or the uncoupled states ( γ > 1 ) (\gamma>1) are minimizers. Domain walls arise as minimizers in a subspace of the energy space with a certain symmetry for some γ > 1 \gamma>1 . We study bifurcation of the domain walls and furthermore give numerical illustrations of our results.

Binary Bose-Einstein condensates trapped by a magnetic field are modeled by a coupled system of the Gross–Pitaevskii equations with a harmonic potential. A coupling parameter

We also study rigorously how these domain walls are related to those in the limiting problem without the trapping potential obtained in
[
5

We consider the domain walls in the one-dimensional settings (see

Domain walls also arise in the two-dimensional and multi-dimensional geometry

of one dimension is opened for future work.

1.1.
The Thomas-Fermi limit

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter for the semi-classical (Thomas–Fermi) limit.
We consider a real-valued solution
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
to the following coupled system of Gross–Pitaevskii equations with the harmonic potential
and the cubic nonlinear terms:

\left.\begin{matrix}-\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)+x^{2}\psi_{1}(x)+\left(\psi_{1}(x)^{2}+\gamma\psi_{2}(x)^{2}-1\right)\psi_{1}(x)=0,\\
-\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(x)+x^{2}\psi_{2}(x)+\left(\gamma\psi_{1}(x)^{2}+\psi_{2}(x)^{2}-1\right)\psi_{2}(x)=0,\end{matrix}\right\}\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where
\gamma>0
is the coupling parameter. The Thomas-Fermi limit corresponds to the asymptotic regime with
\varepsilon\to 0
.

System (
1.1
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation
for the following energy functional

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}(\psi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}(\psi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{2}-1)(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})^{2}+\left.(\gamma-1)\psi_{1}^{2}\psi_{2}^{2}\right]dx

(1.2)

for
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
defined in the energy space
\mathcal{E}:=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{2,1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
L^{2,1}({\mathbb{R}}):=\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}):\;|x|f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\}
.

\eta_{\varepsilon}
versus
x
for
\varepsilon=0.1
.

In order to introduce the steady states of the coupled system of Gross–Pitaevskii equations, let us first describe the ground state of the scalar Gross–Pitaevskii equation with the harmonic potential.

Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})
be the positive solution of the differential equation

\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)+(1-x^{2}-\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x))\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

The positive solution shown on Fig.
1
has been studied
in the literature in many details
[
15
,
21
,
22
]
.
The formal limit
\varepsilon\to 0
corresponds to the so-called Thomas-Fermi cloud

\eta_{0}
of the form

\eta_{0}(x):=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\;{\bf 1}_{\{\left|{x}\right|<1\}}.

(1.4)

By Proposition 2.1 in
[
21
]
, the positive solution
\eta_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

0<\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 1,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}

(1.5)

and

\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\eta_{0}(x),\quad x\in[-1+\varepsilon^{1/3},1-\varepsilon^{1/3}],

(1.6)

moreover,
\eta_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\eta_{0}
in the sense that for every compact subset
K
of
\{\left|{x}\right|<1\}
, there exists a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\left\|{\eta_{\varepsilon}-\eta_{0}}\right\|_{C^{1}(K)}\leq C_{K}\varepsilon^{2}.

(1.7)

Refined asymptotic properties of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
were further obtained in
[
15
]
, where existence of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
was obtained from
the Hasting–McLeod solution of the Painlevé-II equation.
By Theorem 1 in
[
15
]
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|{\eta_{\varepsilon}-\eta_{0}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad\left\|{\eta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

(1.8)

Equation (
1.3
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation for the following energy functional

F_{\varepsilon}(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}(\eta^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{2}-1)\eta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{4}\right]\,dx.

(1.9)

By using the transformation

\psi_{1,2}(x)=\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\phi_{1,2}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)

(1.10)

and making the change of variables
x\to z:=x/\varepsilon
,
we obtain
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=F_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)
,
where

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)^{2}(\phi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)^{2}(\phi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)^{4}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}-1)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+(\gamma-1)\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)^{4}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\bigg]\,dz

(1.11)

for
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
defined in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for which
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)<\infty
.

Since
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon z)=\eta_{0}(0)=1
for every
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the formal limit of (
1.11
) is the energy functional
studied in
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle J_{0}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(\phi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\phi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}-1)^{2}+(\gamma-1)\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\right]\,dz.

(1.12)

The Euler–Lagrange equations for
J_{0}(\Phi)
is the coupled system
of homogeneous Gross–Pitaevskii equations without the harmonic potential:

\left.\begin{aligned} -\phi^{\prime\prime}_{1}(z)+\left(\phi_{1}(z)^{2}+\gamma\phi_{2}(z)^{2}-1\right)\phi_{1}(z)&=0,\\
-\phi^{\prime\prime}_{2}(z)+\left(\gamma\phi_{1}(z)^{2}+\phi_{2}(z)^{2}-1\right)\phi_{2}(z)&=0,\end{aligned}\right\}\quad z\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(1.13)

A symmetric pair of domain wall solutions exists for
\gamma>1

as minimizers of
J_{0}
by Theorem 1.1 in
[
5
]
(see
[
39
]
for earlier mathematical results and
[
31
]
for the first fundamental result
in physics literature). One particular domain wall solution
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
to the system (
1.13
)
satisfies the following boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} \phi_{1}(z)\to 0,\quad\phi_{2}(z)\to 1,\qquad\text{as $z\to-\infty$,}\\
\phi_{1}(z)\to 1,\quad\phi_{2}(z)\to 0,\qquad\text{as $z\to+\infty$,}\end{aligned}\right\}

(1.14)

whereas the other domain wall can be obtained by the transformation

\phi_{1}\leftrightarrow\phi_{2}
.

Theorems 2.1, 2.4, and 3.1 in
[
5
]
state that
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})

for the domain wall solution with the boundary conditions (
1.14
) satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\phi_{2}(z)=\phi_{1}(-z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
;

(b)

\phi_{1}^{2}(z)+\phi_{2}^{2}(z)\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}

(c)

\phi_{1}^{\prime}(z)>0
and
\phi_{2}^{\prime}(z)<0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

Uniqueness of domain walls with properties (a)–(c) was shown in
[
4
]
and more generally in
[
13
]
.
Orbital stability of domain walls in the coupled system of Gross–Pitaevskii equations was established in a weighted Hilbert space in

[
9
]

1.2.
Uncoupled, Symmetric and Domain Wall States

We introduce three steady state solutions to system (
1.1
) that will be studied in this work.

(S1)

For every
\gamma>0
, there exist
uncoupled states

\psi_{1}(x)=\eta_{\varepsilon}(x),\quad\psi_{2}(x)=0,\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\psi_{1}(x)=0,\quad\psi_{2}(x)=\eta_{\varepsilon}(x).

(1.15)

(S2)

For every
\gamma>0
, there exists the
symmetric state

\psi_{1}(x)=\psi_{2}(x)=(1+\gamma)^{-1/2}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x).

(1.16)

(S3)

We say that the solution is the
domain wall state
if
\psi_{1}(x)=\psi_{2}(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
but
\psi_{1}(x)\not\equiv\psi_{2}(x)
.

Figure 2.
The profile of the domain wall state versus
x
for
\varepsilon=0.1
and
\gamma=3
.

Both the symmetric state (S2) and the domain wall state (S3) are defined in the energy space with the symmetry given by

\mathcal{E}_{s}:=\{\Psi\in\mathcal{E}\,:\quad\psi_{1}(x)=\psi_{2}(-x),\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

(1.17)

One domain wall state (S3) with the property
\psi_{1}(x)>\psi_{2}(x)>0
for
x>0
is shown on Fig.
2
. Another domain wall state can be obtained
by the transformation
\psi_{1}\leftrightarrow\psi_{2}
.
Existence, variational characterization, and bifurcations of
the domain wall state (S3) are the main subjects of this work.

1.3.
Main results

Our first theorem establishes a rigorous connection between problems (
1.1
) and (
1.13
). Recall the product form (
1.10
)
which reduces the minimization problem for
G_{\varepsilon}
to one for
J_{\varepsilon}
. A consequence of Lemma
2.1
below is that
\Psi\in\mathcal{E}
minimizes
G_{\varepsilon}
if and only if
\Phi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
minimizes
J_{\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, we prove that
J_{\varepsilon}
converges to
J_{0}
in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then,

(i)

If
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})<\infty
uniformly in
\varepsilon
then as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have a subsequence (not relabelled)
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma-\liminf
inequality)
If
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\to\Phi
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\geq J_{0}(\Phi).

(1.18)

(iii)

(
\Gamma-\limsup
inequality)
If
\Phi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then there is a sequence

\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\to\Phi
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\leq J_{0}(\Phi).

(1.19)

In view of the decomposition
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=F_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)
,
Theorem
1
establishes a relation between minimizers of
G_{\varepsilon}
for small
\varepsilon>0
and those of the limiting functional
J_{0}
. This relation was one of the motivations for studying domain walls in
[
5
,
9
]
as minimizers of
J_{0}
for
\gamma>1
with prescribed limits at infinity.

The functional
J_{0}
also possesses critical points
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
, and
(1+\gamma)^{-1/2}(1,1)
, which correspond to the steady states (S1) and (S2) in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. The question of their variational characterization for
\gamma>1
was left open in
[
5
]
.
The case
0<\gamma<1
was not considered in
[
5
]
. The following two theorems clarify the variational characterization of the three steady states (S1), (S2), and (S3) as critical points of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, the symmetric state (S2) is a global minimizer of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in the energy space
\mathcal{E}
if
\gamma\in(0,1)
and a saddle point of the energy if
\gamma\in(1,\infty)
. Moreover, the symmetric state is the only positive solution of the coupled system (
1.1
) if
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The uncoupled states (S1) are saddle points of the energy if
\gamma\in(0,1)
and the only global minimizers of the energy if
\gamma\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 3
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
there is
\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon)\in(1,\infty)
such that the symmetric state (S2) is a global minimizer of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in the energy space with symmetry
\mathcal{E}_{s}
if

\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon)]
and a saddle point of the energy in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
if
\gamma\in(\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon),\infty)
, where
\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Two domain wall states (S3) are global minimizers of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
if
\gamma\in(\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon),\infty)
: one satisfies
\psi_{1}(x)>\psi_{2}(x)>0
for

and the other one obtained by the transformation
\psi_{1}\leftrightarrow\psi_{2}
.

The domain wall state in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
corresponds to the restriction
\theta_{1}(y)=\theta_{2}(-y)
for

In particular,

u(0)=\frac{\pi}{4}

The boundary conditions

\theta_{1}(y)\to 1

(1.26)

2.
\Gamma
-convergence of the energy functionals

The following splitting lemma was pioneered in
[
27
]
and later explored in
[
21
,
22
]
in the context of vortices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We adopt it to the case of steady states that include the domain wall states in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathcal{E}
and define
\Phi:=\Psi/\eta_{\varepsilon}
, where
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})
is the positive solution of (
1.3
). Then,
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=F_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)
, where
F_{\varepsilon}
is given by (
1.9
) and

J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\phi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\phi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}-1)^{2}+(\gamma-1)\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\right]\,dx.

(2.1)

Proof.

First consider
\Psi=\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with compact support. We conclude by integrating by parts and (
1.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varepsilon^{2}|\Psi^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}|\Phi|^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\Phi^{\prime}|^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\Phi|^{2}-1)^{\prime}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\Phi^{\prime}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}(\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1-x^{2}-\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2})(|\Phi|^{2}-1)\right]\,dx.

For the other parts of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
, simple calculations yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi|^{4}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}|\Phi|^{4}\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}-1)|\Psi|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}-1)\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle(\gamma-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}\psi_{2}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=(\gamma-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\,dx.

We notice that

\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1-x^{2}-\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2})(|\Phi|^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}|\Phi|^{4}+(x^{2}-1)\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}+\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}(|\Phi|^{2}-1)^{2}.

Combining the above equalities we obtain the splitting
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=F_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)
with
F_{\varepsilon}
and
J_{\varepsilon}
given by
(
1.9
) and (
2.1
).

For the general case, let
\chi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a cutoff function such that
\chi\equiv 1
in
[0,1]
,
\chi\equiv 0
in
[2,\infty)
, and
|\chi|,|\chi^{\prime}|\leq 1
almost everywhere. Let

\Psi_{n}(x):=\chi(|x|/n)\Psi(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\Psi_{n}
is compactly supported, we represent
\Psi_{n}=\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi_{n}
and obtain

G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})=F_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{n}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Next, we note that
|\Psi_{n}|\leq|\Psi|
,
|\Psi_{n}^{\prime}|\leq|\Psi^{\prime}|+|\Psi|,
and that
\Psi_{n}\to\Psi
,
\Psi_{n}^{\prime}\to\Psi^{\prime}
pointwise as
n\to\infty
. These facts, together with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}|\Psi|^{2}dx<\infty
, allow us to apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem to conclude that

G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\to G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)\quad\mbox{\rm as}\quad n\to\infty.

Now, since
\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi_{n}\to\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi_{n}^{\prime}\to\eta_{\varepsilon}\Phi^{\prime}
pointwise as
n\to\infty
and

\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\leq C(|\Psi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\Psi|^{2}+|\eta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\Phi|^{2}),\quad\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}(|\Phi_{n}|^{2}-1)^{2}\leq C(|\Psi|^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}.

we obtain by the same Dominated Convergence Theorem that

J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{n})\to J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)\quad\mbox{\rm as}\quad n\to\infty,

which concludes the proof of the splitting formula in the general case.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Since positive
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,1}({\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{R})
is a minimizer of
F_{\varepsilon}
,
we have that
\Psi\in\mathcal{E}
is a minimizer of
G_{\varepsilon}
if and only if
\Phi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a minimizer of
J_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Expression (
2.1
) for
J_{\varepsilon}
coincides with (
1.11
) after the change of variables
x\mapsto z:=x/\varepsilon
and writing
\Phi
as a function of
z
. In what follows we use the rescaled form of

given by (
1.11

We proceed with the proof of Theorem
1
about the
\Gamma
-convergence of
J_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.11
) to
J_{0}
in (
1.12
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We first note that as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)\to 1
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, using (
1.8
) yields
\|\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\varepsilon\cdot)\|_{\infty}=\varepsilon\|\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon^{2/3}
and since
\eta_{\varepsilon}(0)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have locally uniform convergence
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)\to 1
on
\mathbb{R}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

(i) Since
\gamma>1
every term in
J_{\varepsilon}
is non-negative and if
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\leq C<\infty
then for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
we clearly have
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\big|_{K}\leq C(K)
. Due to locally uniform convergence of
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)
to
1
we can find
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough such that
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)\geq\frac{1}{2}
on
K
for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left[|\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]\,dz\leq C(K).

(2.2)

Therefore, there is a subsequence (not relabelled) such that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

(ii) Let
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\to\Phi
strongly in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. If
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})=\infty
there is nothing to prove as
the inequality (
1.18
) is trivially satisfied and therefore we can assume
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})<\infty
. Taking a subsequence
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
(not relabelled) such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})<\infty

we know that there is
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
we have
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\leq C<\infty
with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. Therefore, using part (i), there is a subsequence (not relabelled) such that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Fixing
R>0
and using non-negativity of terms in
J_{\varepsilon}
together with the lower semicontinuity of the
H^{1}
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\Big|_{[-R,R]}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{[-R,R]}\left(|\phi_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}-1)^{2}+(\gamma-1)\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\right)\,dz.

Taking the limit as
R\to\infty
and using the Monotone Convergence Theorem we obtain
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})\geq J_{0}(\Phi)
.

(iii) Let
\Phi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
J_{0}(\Phi)=\infty
, then the inequality (
1.19
) is trivially satisfied by taking
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\equiv\Phi
and therefore we assume that
J_{0}(\Phi)<\infty
. In this case we also define
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\equiv\Phi
and clearly have
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\to\Phi
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since
0<\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)\leq 1
follows from (
1.5
), we have
J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)\leq J_{0}(\Phi)
and hence
\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon})=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)\leq J_{0}(\Phi)
.

\Box

3.
Classification of minimizers of
G_{\varepsilon}

Here we characterize global minimizers of
G_{\varepsilon}
in the energy space
\mathcal{E}
for small
\varepsilon>0
. We start by showing that minimizers of
G_{\varepsilon}
always exist.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma>0
, there exists a minimizer of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

It is clear from (
1.2
) that there is a fixed constant
C>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that for all
\Psi\in\mathcal{E}
we have
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)\geq-C
. Therefore, we can take a minimizing sequence
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\to\inf_{\Psi\in\mathcal{E}}G_{\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{\rm as}\quad n\to\infty.

Since
\inf_{\Psi\in\mathcal{E}}G_{\varepsilon}<0
, we can choose
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\leq 0
and therefore
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{E}
. This implies (possibly on a subsequence) that as
n\to\infty
,

\Psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\Psi\hbox{ in }\mathcal{E}

and

\Psi_{n}\to\Psi\hbox{ in }C([-R,R];\mathbb{R}^{2})\hbox{ for any fixed }R>0.

It follows from (
1.2
) that since the energy density of
G_{\varepsilon}
is non-negative outside
[-1,1]
, we have
G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\geq G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\big|_{[-R;R]}
for
R>1
. Hence, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\big|_{[-R,R]}=G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)\big|_{[-R,R]}.

Passing to the limit as
R\to\infty
and using the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we obtain
\liminf G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{n})\geq G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)
with
\Psi\in\mathcal{E}
. Therefore
\Psi
is a minimizer.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Imposing the symmetry constraint in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
does not change
the argument, hence Lemma
3.1
holds in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
as well.
Indeed, due to the symmetry, the minimization problem can be reduced to the half line
\mathbb{R}_{+}
subject to the condition
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(0)
. This condition can be applied due to Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
into
C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
.

Regarding the minimizers of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
, the following lemma gives the precise property of minimizers.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
be a minimizer of
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}

with
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(0)>0
. Then, either

\psi_{1}(x)>\psi_{2}(x)>0
or
\psi_{2}(x)>\psi_{1}(x)>0
or
\psi_{1}(x)=\psi_{2}(x)
for
x>0
.

Proof.

If
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
is a minimizer of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
, then it is clear that
\Psi^{\prime}=(|\psi_{1}|,|\psi_{2}|)
is also a minimizer of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
. Both satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.1
) and hence they are smooth. Using the Strong Maximum Principle
[
18
, Theorem 2.10]
for
|\psi_{1}|
and
|\psi_{2}|
we deduce that they are strictly positive and since it is assumed that
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(0)>0
, then for any minimizer
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
we have
\psi_{1}(x)>0
and
\psi_{2}(x)>0
for
x>0
.

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}+(x^{2}+\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}-1)\xi-(\gamma-1)\psi_{1}\psi_{2}\xi=0.

Using the Strong Maximum Principle again, we deduce that either

or
\xi(x)<0

∎

Remark 3.2

According to the definitions of the steady states, minimizers with

Lemma 3.3

The symmetric state

is the only positive solution of system (

) for

.

Proof.

Define the functional

for

Let

be such that there is

such that

and
w_{2}+tv_{2}>0
for

. A direct calculation shows that for any admissible

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}E(W+tV)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{(v_{1}^{\prime}w_{1}-v_{1}w_{1}^{\prime})^{2}}{2(w_{1}+tv_{1})^{3}}+\frac{(v_{2}^{\prime}w_{2}-v_{2}w_{2}^{\prime})^{2}}{2(w_{2}+tv_{2})^{3}}\right]dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+2\gamma v_{1}v_{2}\right]dx,

which is strictly positive definite if

. In particular, for any positive

the function

) and let

be another positive function such that
\Psi\neq\Psi^{\prime}
. Since
\Psi
is a critical point of
G_{\varepsilon}
, we have that
(f_{W,V})^{\prime}(0)=0.
Since,
(f_{W,V})^{\prime\prime}(t)>0,
for

this implies that

G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi^{\prime})-G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=\int_{0}^{1}(f_{W,V})^{\prime}(t)\,dt>0.

Thus,
\Psi^{\prime}
is not a critical point of

, otherwise we could reverse the roles of

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

By virtue of Lemma

, the problem of minimizing

in

is equivalent to the problem of minimizing

in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

Moreover, it is clear that

J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)^{4}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}-1)^{2}+(\gamma-1)\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\right]dz.

We proceed separately for
\gamma\in(0,1)

\gamma\in(1,\infty)

Case

In this case, the quadratic form

\Phi=(1+\gamma)^{-1/2}(1,1)

due to the product form (

1.1

3.3

If
\Phi=(1,0)
or

) are not minimizers of
G_{\varepsilon}
.

0&H^{(2)}_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}.

[
14

15
]

\varepsilon

The diagonal entry

[

Case
\gamma>1.
Since

\gamma>1

\Phi=(0,1)

.

for which both diagonal entries are strictly positive for

Next we show that the symmetric state (S2) is a saddle

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2}+\frac{3+\gamma}{1+\gamma}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1&\frac{2\gamma}{1+\gamma}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\varepsilon^{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{2}-1)(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})+\frac{3+\gamma}{1+\gamma}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})+\frac{4\gamma}{1+\gamma}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{2}\Big].

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{4}(\tilde{\phi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\phi}_{2}^{2}+2\gamma\tilde{\phi}_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi+\tilde{\Phi})-J_{\varepsilon}(\Phi)

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi+\tilde{\Psi})-G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\varepsilon^{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{2}+\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}-1)(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(2\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+2\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+(\gamma-1)[(2\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2})(2\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})+\psi_{1}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}]\Big]dx

\tilde{\psi}_{1}(0)=\tilde{\psi}_{2}(0)=0

\tilde{\phi}_{1}(0)
and
\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{2}|\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[(\xi^{\prime})^{2}|\tilde{\Phi}|^{2}+\xi^{2}|\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi^{\prime}\xi(|\tilde{\Phi}|^{2})^{\prime}\right]dx

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi+\tilde{\Psi})-G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\varepsilon^{2}\xi^{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\xi^{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{2}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(2\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+2\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+(\gamma-1)[(2\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2})(2\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})+\psi_{1}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})]\Big]dx,

where the last term is non-negative since

\displaystyle(2\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2})(2\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})+\psi_{1}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{2})^{2}+(\psi_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{2}+\psi_{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{2})^{2}\geq 0.

Thus, for any
\tilde{\Psi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi+\tilde{\Psi})-G_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)\geq 0,

with equality holding if and only if either
\tilde{\Psi}=0
or
\tilde{\Psi}=(\psi_{2}-\psi_{1},\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})
. Therefore, the critical points
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
and
\Psi^{\prime}=(\psi_{2},\psi_{1})
are the only global minimizers of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
.

\Box

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\mu>0
. Let
\Theta_{\gamma}
be a sequence in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
\gamma>1
. Then,

(i)

If
\gamma\to 1^{+}
and
I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})<\infty
uniformly in
\gamma
then we have a subsequence (not relabelled)
\Theta_{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\Theta
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}},\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
|\Theta(x)|^{2}=1
for
x\in{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}=\left(-\frac{1}{\mu},\frac{1}{\mu}\right)
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma-\liminf
inequality)
If
\gamma\to 1^{+}
and
\Theta_{\gamma}\to\Theta
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then

\liminf_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\geq I_{\mu,1}(\Theta).

(4.7)

(iii)

(
\Gamma-\limsup
inequality)
If
\Theta\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then there is a sequence

\Theta_{\gamma}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that as
\gamma\to 1^{+}
we have
\Theta_{\gamma}\to\Theta
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}},\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

\limsup_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\leq I_{\mu,1}(\Theta).

(4.8)

Proof.

For fixed
\mu>0
, we take
\varepsilon=\mu\sqrt{\gamma-1}
and define the energy functional
I_{\mu,\gamma}
in the form (
1.22
) for
\gamma>1
.
Using the properties of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
we know that

\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)=\eta_{\mu\sqrt{\gamma-1}}(\mu\cdot)\to\eta_{0}(\mu\cdot)

locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
as
\gamma\to 1^{+}
.

(i) Assume
I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})<\infty
uniformly in
\gamma
, we want to show that
\Theta_{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\Theta
weakly in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We first observe that if
I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})<\infty
then for any
0<R<\frac{1}{\mu}
we have
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)>C_{1}(R)>0
on the interval
(-R,R)
for
\varepsilon
small enough and hence

\int_{-R}^{R}|\Theta_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\Theta_{\gamma}|^{2}\,dy\leq C.

Therefore, we have
\Theta_{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\Theta
weakly in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
(up to a subsequence). Moreover, we also have

\int_{-R}^{R}(|\Theta_{\gamma}|^{2}-1)^{2}\,dy\leq C(\gamma-1),

where
C>0
depends on
R
but is independent of
\gamma
. Therefore, taking a limit in
\gamma
we obtain
\int_{-R}^{R}(|\Theta|^{2}-1)^{2}\,dy=0
for any
0<R<\frac{1}{\mu}
and hence
|\Theta(y)|^{2}=1
for all
y\in{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}
.

(ii) Let
\Theta_{\gamma}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\Theta_{\gamma}\to\Theta
strongly in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
\liminf\limits_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})=\infty
there is nothing to prove, therefore we can assume
\liminf\limits_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})<\infty
. Taking a subsequence
\Theta_{\gamma}
(not relabelled) such that

\lim_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})=\liminf_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})<\infty,

we know that there is
\tilde{\gamma}>1
such that for all
1<\gamma<\tilde{\gamma}
we have
I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\leq C<\infty
with
C>0
independent of
\gamma
. Therefore, using part (i), there is a subsequence (not relabelled) such that
\Theta_{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\Theta
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
|\Theta(x)|^{2}=1
for
x\in{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}
. Fixing
0<R<\frac{1}{\mu}
and using non-negativity of terms in
I_{\mu,\gamma}
together with the properties of the
H^{1}
-norm and local uniform convergence
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)\to\eta_{0}(\mu\cdot)
on
[-R,R]
we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\Big|_{[-R,R]}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{[-R,R]}\left(\eta_{0}^{2}(\mu\cdot)|\Theta^{\prime}|^{2}+\eta_{0}^{4}(\mu\cdot)\theta_{1}^{2}\theta_{2}^{2}\right)\,dy.

Taking the limit as

and using the Monotone Convergence Theorem we obtain
\liminf\limits_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\geq I_{\mu,1}(\Theta)
.

(iii) Let
\Theta\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, due to Sobolev embeddings we have
\Theta\in C_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
I_{\mu,1}(\Theta)=\infty
then the statement is trivially satisfied by taking

\Theta_{\gamma}(x)=\begin{cases}\Theta(x)&\hbox{ if }x\in\left[-\frac{1}{\gamma\mu},\frac{1}{\gamma\mu}\right],\\
\Theta(\frac{1}{\gamma\mu})&\hbox{ if }x\notin\left[-\frac{1}{\gamma\mu},\frac{1}{\gamma\mu}\right],\end{cases}

It is clear that
\Theta_{\gamma}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\Theta_{\gamma}\to\Theta
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Therefore we assume that
I_{\mu,1}(\Theta)<\infty
. In this case we know that
|\Theta(x)|^{2}=1
for
x\in{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}
and we define

\Theta_{\gamma}(x)=\begin{cases}\Theta(x)&\hbox{ if }x\in\left[-\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu},\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu}\right],\\
\Theta(\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu})&\hbox{ if }x\notin\left[-\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu},\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu}\right],\end{cases}

to obtain
\Theta_{\gamma}\to\Theta
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathcal{I}_{\mu}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
(recall that
\varepsilon=\mu\sqrt{\gamma-1}
). We want to prove that
\limsup\limits_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\leq I_{\mu,1}(\Theta)
. We define
{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}_{\varepsilon}=(-\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu},\frac{1-\varepsilon^{1/3}}{\mu})
to obtain

\displaystyle I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{2}|\Theta_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{4}\theta_{\gamma,1}^{2}\theta_{\gamma,2}^{2}\right]\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}_{\varepsilon}}\left[\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{2}|\Theta_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{4}\theta_{\gamma,1}^{2}\theta_{\gamma,2}^{2}\right]\,dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}_{\varepsilon}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{4}\theta_{\gamma,1}^{2}\theta_{\gamma,2}^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}_{\varepsilon}}\left[\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{2}(|\theta_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\theta_{2}^{\prime}|^{2})+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mu\cdot)^{4}\theta_{1}^{2}\theta_{2}^{2}\right]\,dy+C\varepsilon^{1/3},

where in the last inequality we used

and

\displaystyle I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta)

\limsup\limits_{\gamma\to 1^{+}}I_{\mu,\gamma}(\Theta_{\gamma})\leq I_{\mu,1}(\Theta)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

For

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}I_{\mu,1}(u+tv)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}}\left[\eta_{0}(\mu\cdot)^{2}(v^{\prime})^{2}+\eta_{0}(\mu\cdot)^{4}\cos(4(u+tv))v^{2}\right]dy

the function

and

.

Lemma 4.3

The nonconstant state of the Euler–Lagrange equations
(
4.11
) with
u(0)=\frac{\pi}{4}
and
u^{\prime}(0)\neq 0

bifurcates to
\mu<\mu_{0}
and satisfies

\|u-\frac{\pi}{4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sqrt{\mu_{0}-\mu}

for some
\mu
-independent constant
C>0
.

Proof.

We rescale again
x=\mu y
and write
u(y)=\frac{\pi}{4}+v(x)
. The stationary equation (
4.11
) is rewritten in the form of the boundary-value problem

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}-\mu^{2}\frac{d}{dx}\left[(1-x^{2})\frac{dv}{dx}\right]-\frac{1}{4}(1-x^{2})^{2}\sin(4v)=0,\quad 0<x<1,\\
v(0)=0,\quad v^{\prime}(1)=0.\end{array}\right\}

(4.17)

The computation of the normal form for the local (pitchfork) bifurcation relies
on the standard method of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction
[
10
]
as the zero eigenvalue of the linearized operator at
\mu=\mu_{0}
is simple.

Let
v_{0}
be the eigenfunction of the spectral problem (
4.14
) for the smallest eigenvalue
\nu_{0}>0
, where
\mu_{0}:=\nu_{0}^{-1/2}
.
To compute the coefficients of the normal form, we write

v=av_{0}+w
and
\mu^{2}=\mu_{0}^{2}+\delta
, where
a
and
\delta
are parameters of the Lyapunov–Schmidt decomposition and
w
is assumed to satisfy the orthogonality condition

\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{2}v_{0}(x)w(x)dx=0.

(4.18)

Decomposing (
4.17
) into the projection to
v_{0}
and to
w
yields a system of two equations of the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction:

-\mu_{0}^{2}\frac{d}{dx}\left[(1-x^{2})\frac{dw}{dx}\right]-(1-x^{2})^{2}\left[w+F(w;a,\delta)\right]=0

(4.19)

and

\delta\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{2}v_{0}^{2}dx=\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{4a}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{2}v_{0}\left[\sin(4av_{0}+4w)-4av_{0}\right]dx,

(4.20)

where

F(w;a,\delta):=\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{4(\mu_{0}^{2}+\delta)}\sin(4av_{0}+4w)-av_{0}-w

and
w(x)
satisfies the boundary conditions
w(0)=0
and
w^{\prime}(1)=0
.
Since
F(0;a,\delta)=\mathcal{O}(|a|^{3}+|a\delta|)
as
(a,\delta)\to(0,0)
, there exists a unique solution
w
to the nonlinear equation (
4.19
) under the conditions (
4.18
) and (
4.20
),
for every small
(a,\delta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which satisfies the estimate

\|\sqrt{1-x^{2}}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1-x^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(a^{2}+|\delta|)|a|

(4.21)

for some
(a,\delta)
-independent constant
C>0
. Moreover,

w
is in the domain of the linear operator associated with the spectral
problem (
4.14
) satisfying the boundary conditions

w(0)=0
and
w^{\prime}(1)=0
so that the estimate (
4.21
)
also extends to the
L^{\infty}(0,1)
norm of the solution
w
.

Substituting the solution
w
of equation (
4.19
) into equation (
4.20
) and using (
4.18
) defines uniquely
\delta
for every small
a\in\mathbb{R}
with the estimate

|\delta|\leq C_{0}a^{2}

(4.22)

for some
a
-independent constant
C_{0}>0
. Moreover, the leading-order part in the expansion
\delta=\delta_{2}a^{2}+\mathcal{O}(a^{4})
can be found explicitly

\delta_{2}=-\frac{8}{3}\mu_{0}^{2}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})v_{0}^{4}\,dx}{\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})v_{0}^{2}\,dx}.

Since
\delta_{2}<0
, the nonconstant solution bifurcates for
\mu<\mu_{0}
and satisfies

|\mu^{2}-\mu_{0}^{2}|\leq C_{0}a^{2},\qquad\|v-av_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}|a|^{3}

for some
a
-independent constant
C>0
.
Hence
a=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\mu_{0}-\mu})
and the solution satisfies

\|u-\frac{\pi}{4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sqrt{\mu_{0}-\mu}

for some
\mu
-independent constant
C>0
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The sign of
\delta_{2}
in the normal form in Lemma
4.3
suggests that the domain wall states are global minimizers of the energy
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
in the region of their existence at least for small
\varepsilon>0
. The proof of non-existence of the domain wall states for
\gamma\in(1,\gamma_{0}(\varepsilon))
for general
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
is left open for further studies.

Quantum character theory
Sam Gunningham, David Jordan and Monica Vazirani

Abstract
. We develop a 𝚚 \mathtt{q} -analogue of the theory of conjugation equivariant 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules on a complex reductive group G G . In particular, we define quantum Hotta-Kashiwara modules and compute their endomorphism algebras. We use the Schur-Weyl functor of the second author, and develop tools from the corresponding double affine Hecke algebra to study this category in the cases G = GL N G=\mathrm{GL}_{N} and SL N \mathrm{SL}_{N} . Our results also have an interpretation in skein theory (explored further in a sequel paper), namely a computation of the GL N \mathrm{GL}_{N} and SL N \mathrm{SL}_{N} -skein algebra of the 2-torus.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Classical character theory

Conjugation equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-modules on a reductive group and on its Lie algebra have a rich history in representation theory. Harish-Chandra observed that the distributional characters of irreducible unitary representations of semisimple groups satisfy a holonomic system of differential equations, which he used to initiate the character theory of unitary representations
[
Har65
]
. Hotta and Kashiwara defined and studied the corresponding
\mathcal{D}
-module, revealing a striking connection with Springer theory – the study of representations of the Weyl group on certain cohomology groups of Springer fibers
[
HK84
]
. These ideas have since come to be understood in a wider context including Lusztig’s generalized Springer correspondence and his theory of character sheaves
[
Lus84
,
Lus85
]
, as interpreted in the
\mathcal{D}
-module setting by Ginzburg
[
Gin89
]
, and further developed in a categorical context by Ben-Zvi and Nadler
[
BN09
]
and the first author
[
Gun18
]
.

Let us recall some of the basic objects from this theory. Given a connected complex reductive group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, maximal torus
H
and corresponding Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have the ring of algebraic differential operators
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and the category of strongly equivariant modules (for the adjoint action)
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-mod}^{G}
.
The prototypical example of a strongly equivariant
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
-module is the
universal Hotta-Kashiwara module
,

\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}=\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}).

The endomorphism algebra of
\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}
is naturally identified with its
G
-invariants; this is known as the quantum Hamiltonian reduction:

\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g}){/\!/}G=\End(\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}})\cong\left(\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}\right)^{G}.

A well-known result of Levasseur and Stafford
[
LS95
,
LS96
]
provides an isomorphism

\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g}){/\!/}G\cong\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h})^{W}.

Note that we have an embedding
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}]^{W}\cong\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
. A strongly equivariant
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
-module is called
admissible
if this subalgebra acts locally finitely (see
[
Gin89
]
). The primary
examples of admissible modules are the Hotta-Kashiwara modules associated to a fixed character
\zeta\in\mathfrak{h}/W
:

\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}(\zeta)=\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})$}\Bigg/\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\left(\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}}\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})(y-\zeta(y))\right)$}.

One of the main results of
[
HK84
]
is to identify the endomorphism algebra of these modules with the group algebra of the subgroup
W_{\zeta}
of the Weyl group
W
corresponding to the stabilizer of a lift of
\zeta
to
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, giving a
\mathcal{D}
-module interpretation of the Springer representations.

1.2.
Quantum character theory

The goal of this paper is to
\mathtt{q}
-deform these notions using the representation theory of quantum groups.
Let
\mathcal{K}
denote a ground field containing the quantum parameter
\mathtt{q}
, which we assume is not a root of unity (see Notation
2.1
).

Given a reductive group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, maximal torus
H
and corresponding Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have the ring of quantum differential operators
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
and the category of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
modules (for the quantum adjoint action)
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}({G}){\textrm{-mod}}^{\hskip 0.5pt{G}}
, determined by a quantum moment map
\operatorname{ad}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
constructed in
[
VV10
]
, and further studied in
[
Jor14
]
,
[
BBJ18
]
,
[
Saf19
]
.
The prototypical example of a strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-module is the
universal Hotta-Kashiwara module
,

\operatorname{HK}={\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}/{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}C(\operatorname{ad}),

where
C(\operatorname{ad})=\{\operatorname{ad}(\ell)-\epsilon(\ell)\mid\ell\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\}
and
\epsilon:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to\mathcal{K}
is the counit. As in the classical case, the endomorphism algebra of
\operatorname{HK}
is naturally identified with its
G
-invariants; this is known as the quantum multiplicative Hamiltonian reduction:

{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}{/\!/}G=\End(\operatorname{HK})\cong\left(\operatorname{HK}\right)^{G}.

We have an embedding
\mathcal{O}(H)^{W}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
. A strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-module is called
admissible
(or a
\mathtt{q}
-character sheaf
) if this subalgebra acts locally finitely. The primary
examples of admissible modules are the Hotta-Kashiwara modules associated to a fixed character
\chi\in H/W
:

(1.1)

\operatorname{HK}(\chi)=\raisebox{0.0pt}{${\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}$}\Bigg/\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\left({\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}C(\operatorname{ad})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}}{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}(y-\chi(y))\right)$}

We will typically focus our attention on reductive groups of type
A
, specifically
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. This is because our main technical tool is the Schur-Weyl functor
F_{N}
defined by the second author
[
Jor09
]
, which relates strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules with modules for a certain specialization of the double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA)
\mathrm{\mathbb{H}_{N}}
(see Definition
3.8
). Our first main result computes the endomorphism algebra of
\operatorname{HK}
in these cases.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
or
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
. We have a canonical isomorphism,

\End(\operatorname{HK})\cong\mathrm{e}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbb{H}_{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\mathrm{e}

Here,
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{\mathbb{H}_{N}}
is the sign idempotent. We refer to
\mathrm{e}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbb{H}_{N}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\mathrm{e}
as the antispherical DAHA. As a special case of the
shift isomorphism
(see Section
5.1
), we can identify the righthand side of Theorem
1.1
with the algebra
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(H)}^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}
of
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}
-invariant differential operators, and hence we interpret Theorem
1.1
as giving a quantum Levasseur-Stafford isomorphism:

(1.2)

{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}/\!/G\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(H)^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}.

Recall that a key result of Hotta and Kashiwara was an identification of
\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}(0)
with the pushforward of the constant sheaf along the Springer resolution, implying in particular an isomorphism between its endomorphism algebra and the group algebra of the Weyl group. The quantum analogue of
\operatorname{HK}^{\operatorname{\mathtt{cl}}}(0)
is
\operatorname{HK}(\epsilon)
, where
\epsilon:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to\mathcal{K}
denotes the counit character, corresponding classically to the identity element in
G
. Our second main result may be regarded as a
\mathtt{q}
-deformed Springer correspondence à la Hotta–Kashiwara.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
or
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
. We have a canonical isomorphism

\End(\operatorname{HK}(\epsilon))\cong\mathcal{K}[{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}]^{\mathrm{op}}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider the resulting decomposition

\displaystyle\operatorname{HK}(\epsilon)=\bigoplus_{\lambda}M_{\lambda}\boxtimes S^{\lambda},

as
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-
\mathcal{K}[{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}]
bimodules
where the direct sum is indexed by partitions
\lambda
of
N
and
S^{\lambda}
is the corresponding irreducible for
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}
. Then the factors
M_{\lambda}
appearing are distinct indecomposable unipotent
\mathtt{q}
-character sheaves.

More generally we compute the endomorphism algebra of the modules
\operatorname{HK}(\chi)
, under mild a combinatorial assumption (see Section
5
for details) on the character
\chi
. It similarly leads to a decomposition as bimodules.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\chi\in H/W
and pick a lift
\bar{\chi}\in H
.
Then
\bar{\chi}
has stabilizer
\sigma W_{J}\sigma^{-1}
for some
\sigma\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, where
W_{J}\subset W={{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}
is a standard parabolic subgroup. Suppose that
\chi
is in transverse position, i.e., that
W\cap\sigma W_{J}\sigma^{-1}=\{\operatorname{Id}\}
. Then we have an isomorphism,

\End(\operatorname{HK}(\chi))\cong\mathcal{K}[W_{J}]^{op}.

Consider the resulting decomposition

\displaystyle\operatorname{HK}(\chi)=\bigoplus_{{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}}}M_{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}}\boxtimes S^{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}},

as
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-
W_{J}
bimodules,
where the direct sum is indexed by multi-partitions
{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}}
refining the composition corresponding to
J
and of total size
N
, and
S^{{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}}}
is the corresponding irreducible for
W_{J}
. Then the factors
M_{{\underline{{\mathbf{\lambda}}}}}
appearing are distinct indecomposable
\mathtt{q}
-character sheaves.

Remark 1.5
.

Let us emphasize that the category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}({G}){\textrm{-mod}}^{\hskip 0.5pt{G}}
is not semi-simple, and so the semi-simplicity of endomorphism algebras as in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
is not automatic. In Example
6.19
we illustrate a
\chi
not in transverse position, such that
\End(\operatorname{HK}(\chi))
is not semisimple.

1.4.
Characters of classical and quantum Harish-Chandra bimodules

Replacing
\mathfrak{g}
with
G
, one may also consider the category
\mathcal{D}(G)\textrm{-mod}^{G}
of strongly equivariant
\mathcal{D}(G)
-modules.
The similarly defined category of strongly equivariant
\mathcal{D}(G)
-modules may be identified with the categorical trace of the monoidal category of Harish-Chandra bimodules
\operatorname{HC}(G)
, that is,
U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules with an action of
G
integrating the diagonal
\mathfrak{g}
-action (see
[
BGN19
]
for a proof in the derived setting). Modules for
\operatorname{HC}(G)
are sometimes known as categorical
G
-representations (more formally, these are the weak invariants of a categorical
G
-representation, namely a module category for the categorical convolution algebra
(\mathcal{D}(G),\ast)
; see
[
BGO18
]
for further details). This leads to an interpretation of strongly equivariant
\mathcal{D}(G)
-modules as characters of categorical representations.

The formalism of factorization homology gives us a satisfying parallel to this classical story in the quantum setting. Whereas the category of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules (“quantum character sheaves”) is attached by factorization homology to the two-torus, the same theory attaches to the circle a monoidal category
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
of
quantum Harish-Chandra
bimodules (see Section
[
Saf19
]
for discussion).

The fact that characters of quantum Harish-Chandra bimodules are indeed strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules is therefore simply a formal consequence of the fact that the two-torus is obtained from the circle by taking the Cartesian product with another circle, and that in topological field theory crossing with a circle produces the appropriate form of Hochschild homology, i.e. the universal receptacle for characters. For example, one may interpret the module
\operatorname{HK}(\chi)
as coming from the solid torus
S^{1}\times D^{2}
, with a line defect along
S^{1}\times\{0\}
labeling the conjugacy class. This is precisely the character
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-module of the object of
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}
which is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}}\chi
, the quantization of the conjugacy class determined by
\chi
.

This approach to quantum character theory strongly echoes the ideas of the classical character field theory papers
[
BN09
]
by Ben-Zvi and Nadler, and subsequently in their work
[
BGN19
]
with the first author.

1.5.
Quantum Springer theory

Recall that the main objects of study in this paper, namely the category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}({G}){\textrm{-mod}}^{\hskip 0.5pt{G}}
and
\operatorname{HK}
, may be defined for any connected reductive group
G
(together with the additional data required to defined the corresponding ribbon category of representations of the quantum group). Although our techniques in this paper are rooted in the type
A
-specific philosophy of Schur-Weyl duality, combining our results with the analogous results in the
\mathcal{D}
-module setting leads us to the following natural conjecture.

Conjecture 1.9
.

The isomorphism (
1.2
) and the results of Theorems
1.2
and
1.4

all hold for an arbitrary connected reductive group
G
(replacing the symmetric group
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}
with the Weyl group
W
of
G
).

Proving these conjectures would require a deeper understanding of
\mathtt{q}
-deformed Springer theory, as outside of type
A
we lack elliptic Schur-Weyl duality, hence the reformulation via intertwiners for the double affine Hecke algebra, which are our main tools in proving Theorem
1.2
.

We believe that the correct approach to these conjectures is to develop a “quantum Springer theory”, which is to say a theory of parabolic induction and restriction functors for categories of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules. The required functors should arise in the factorization homology framework by considering the domain wall given by
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}B
, placed at the surface
T^{2}\times\{\frac{1}{2}\}
inside the 3-manifold
T^{2}\times I
. We plan to return to this construction in future work.

Given such a formalism, one can the define the notion of a
cuspidal

{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-module: one killed by parabolic restriction for every proper parabolic subgroup. The generalization of Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
for other groups
G
requires the following more general notion: a
\mathtt{q}
-cuspidal datum
is defined to be a
G
-conjugacy class of pairs
(L,C)
, where
L
is an elliptic-pseudo Levi subgroup of
G
(in the sense of
[
FGL22
]
) and
C
is a simple unipotent cuspidal
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(L)}
-module. For example, if
L=C
, a
\mathtt{q}
-cuspidal datum is the same thing as a unipotent simple cuspidal
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-module. At the other extreme, for any
G
, there is a canonical cuspidal datum
Spr=(H,\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(H))
, where
H
is the maximal torus of
G
, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(H)
is the canonical module.
The following statement is the
\mathtt{q}
-analogue of Theorem A of
[
Gun18
]
.

Conjecture 1.10
.

For each connected reductive group
G
, there is a finite block decomposition of the category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}({G}){\textrm{-mod}}^{\hskip 0.5pt{G}}
indexed by the set of
\mathtt{q}
-cuspidal data. Moreover, the block corresponding to a cuspidal datum
(L,C)
is equivalent to the category of modules for the smash product of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(Z(L)^{\circ})
by a twisted group algebra of a certain finite group.

In particular, there is always at least one block in the above decomposition, namely the
Springer block
, corresponding to the Springer cuspidal datum. We additionally conjecture that this block is generated by the universal Hotta-Kashiwara module
\operatorname{HK}
. According to Theorem
1.6
, this is the only block in the case
G=GL_{N}
. On the other hand, by
Theorem
1.7
, there are five blocks: the Springer block, and four blocks corresponding to the four distinct simple cuspidal modules. More generally, we expect the cuspidal
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(SL_{N})}
-modules to be those whose central character
\overline{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}=Z(SL_{N})^{\vee}
satisfies
\gcd(n,N)=1
. Applying the Schur-Weyl functor
F_{n}
to such modules produces finite-dimensional modules for the corresponding DAHA in parallel with the results of
[
CEE09
]
in the rational case.

Finally, we note that the category of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules is the category appearing on the
B
-side in the quantum Betti geometric Langlands conjectures for a 2-torus
[
BN18
]
. One may also wonder how this category is related to the corresponding category in the more traditional de Rham quantum geometric Langlands program. In forthcoming work of the first author, an analogous generalized Springer decomposition is proved for generically twisted
\mathcal{D}
-modules on the moduli stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}(E)
of
G
-bundles on an elliptic curve
E
(see
[
BN15
]
and
[
FGL22
]
for some context). In particular, there is an analogous set
\operatorname{Cusp}_{\operatorname{ell}}({G})
of
elliptic cuspidal data
indexing the blocks. Although the categorical structure of the individual blocks is quite different in the elliptic and the
\mathtt{q}
-cases, one still expects the following comparison.

Conjecture 1.11
.

There is a natural bijection between the set of simple cuspidal
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules and simple cuspidal generically twisted
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{ss,0}(E)
.

Note that the cuspidal
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules have been explicitly determined for each simply connected quasi-simple group
G
(see the table on the second page of
[
Lus84
]
). As part of ongoing work of the first author with Penghui Li and Dragos Fratila, we will describe how the set of
\mathtt{q}
-cuspidal data (or equivalently, elliptic cuspidal data) for any given group
G
can be explicitly determined from Lusztig’s table, giving a complete description of the category of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules.

1.6.
Skeins dictionary

Theorem
1.1
and its corollaries are of independent interest in the study of skein algebras. The works
[
BBJ
,
BBJ18
,
Coo19
,
GJS23
]
establish, for any group
G
, a canonical equivalence of categories,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}({G}){\textrm{-mod}}^{\hskip 0.5pt{G}}\simeq\mathrm{SkCat}(T^{2})\textrm{-mod},

between the category of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules and the free co-completion
\mathrm{SkCat}_{G}(T^{2})\textrm{-mod}
of the
G
-skein category of the two-torus.

Under this equivalence,
\operatorname{HK}
maps to the empty skein object: indeed, the presentation we gave for
\operatorname{HK}
is precisely the presentation of the distinguished object in factorization homology via quantum Hamiltonian reduction given in
[
BBJ18
]
, which is shown in
[
Coo19
]
to coincide with the empty skein object. We immediately obtain an isomorphism between the
skein algebra

\mathrm{SkAlg}(T^{2})
of the torus
T^{2}
– i.e. the endomorphism algebra in the skein category of the empty object – and the endomorphism algebra
\End(\operatorname{HK})
within strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules. To summarize:

Corollary 1.12
.

We have isomorphisms,

\mathrm{SkAlg}_{G}(T^{2})\cong\End(\operatorname{HK})\cong\mathrm{e}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbb{H}_{N}}^{op}\cdot\mathrm{e}\cong(\mathcal{D}_{q}({H})^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}})^{op},

Recall that the case
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
of this isomorphism is a fundamental computation in skein theory due to Frohman and Gelca
[
FG00
]
. Hence, we may regard Corollary
1.12
as a generalization of Frohman and Gelca’s result from
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
to the groups
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N},\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. As justification for the categorical approach taken in this paper, we note that a completely elementary/computational proof of this corollary appears intractable due to the well-known combinatorial complexity of a generators-and-relations presentation of
\mathcal{D}_{q}({H})^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}
. In the sequel paper
[
GJVY
]
, we extend the isomorphism to all of
\mathbb{H}_{N}
, and apply this isomorphism to compute dimensions of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
- and
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
-skein modules of the three-torus
T^{3}
.

In this formulation, Corollary
1.12

may also be compared to a theorem of Morton and Samuelson
[
MS17
]
, where analogous descriptions are obtained relating the Homflypt skein algebra to the
q=t
specialization of the elliptic Hall algebra.

It should be possible to recover Morton and Samuelson’s result from ours, by simultaneously regarding the Homflypt skein as a “limit” (polynomial interpolation) of the categories
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
, and likewise the elliptic Hall algebra as an inverse limit of spherical double affine Hecke algebras. It does not seem possible to reverse this process to obtain our result as a consequence of theirs, and indeed understanding the relation at finite rank rather than in the limit was a primary motivation for the present paper.

In the forthcoming paper
[
GJVY
]
, we prove a stronger result (using the Morita equivalence discussed in Theorem
1.6
), which identifies certain relative skein algebras with (full rather than anti-spherical) double affine Hecke algebras. That result bears an analogous relation to recent work
[
BCMN21
]
, where the double affine Hecke algebra was realised as a relative skein algebra for the Homflypt skein relations.

1.7.
Structure of the paper

The paper is laid out as follows. In Section
2
we give the basic definitions relating to quantum groups and
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules. Many of these are recollections, but some are new definitions. In Section
3
we recall the basic definitions for double affine Hecke algebras attached to
\mathrm{GL}
and
\mathrm{SL}
. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
, by showing that a quantum spherical Schur-Weyl duality homomorphism constructed there is an isomorphism. In Section
5

\operatorname{HK}(\epsilon)
and
\operatorname{HK}(\chi)
, proving Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
. The paper ends with Section
6
, which can be read independent of the other results in the paper. It proves via the method of intertwiners a statement purely about certain representations of double affine Hecke algebras, which taken together with Theorem
1.6
provides an alternative proof of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
. We include it in the present paper rather than the forthcoming paper as it is closer in spirit to the results obtained here, despite the fact that its application depends on the results from
[
GJVY

2.
Quantum groups, their differential operators and Hotta-Kashiwara modules

In this section we recall basic definitions about quantum groups, braided quantum coordinate algebras, and quantum differential operators. Finally, we recall the notion of strongly equivariant
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
-modules, and we give the construction of the Hotta-Kashiwara modules
\operatorname{HK}(\chi)
.

Notation 2.1
.

Fix a positive integer
N
. In this section, we let
\mathcal{R}
denote a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra that is an integral domain together with an element
\mathtt{q}^{\frac{1}{N}}\in\mathcal{R}^{\times}
such that
\frac{\mathtt{q}^{m}-1}{\mathtt{q}-1}
is invertible for all
m\geq 1
, and let
\mathcal{K}
denote its field of fractions. Our main example for
\mathcal{R}
is the local ring
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Q}[\mathtt{q}^{\frac{1}{N}}]_{(\mathtt{q}-1)}
at
\mathtt{q}=1
, in which case
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathtt{q}^{\frac{1}{N}})
. Unless otherwise specified, everything in this section will be defined over the base ring
\mathcal{R}
.

Remark 2.2
.

To straddle the various conventions in the literature, we have had to introduce different fonts for our parameters: (teletype)
\mathtt{q}
for quantum groups of Section
2
, (Roman)
q
(respectively bold Roman
\boldsymbol{q}
) for the loop parameter of the DAHA of type
\mathrm{GL}
in Section
3.1
(resp. type
\mathrm{SL}
in Section
3.2
). As we shall see below, the quadratic parameter in the Hecke algebras is usually denoted
t
, and for Schur-Weyl considerations we later specialize
t=\mathtt{q}
. All of the parameters come together in Section
4
.

Notation 2.3
.

We will have occasion to use the quantum integers denoted
[k]_{r}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and for various elements
r\in\mathcal{R}
. We take the (unbalanced) convention

[k]_{r}=\frac{r^{k}-1}{r-1}

and

[n]_{r}!=[n]_{r}\cdots[2]_{r}[1]_{r}
.
As usual
{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{r}=\frac{[n]_{r}[n-1]_{r}\cdots[n-k+1]_{r}}{[k]_{r}\;[k-1]_{r}\;\,\cdots\,\;[1]_{r}}
.

2.1.
The quantum groups
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}),U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})

We may work over Lusztig’s integral form of the quantum group over
\mathcal{R}
, but as
[n]_{\mathtt{q}}
is a unit in
\mathcal{R}
, there is no appreciable difference working over this integral form as compared to other conventions.
We refer to
[
KS97
]
for detailed definitions, in particular the Serre presentation of the quantum groups
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
and
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
, the formulas for
R
-matrices, and the Peter-Weyl theorem.

We will consider the
\mathcal{R}
-linear braided tensor categories
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{SL}_{N})
of integrable
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
-modules (resp.
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-modules). Note that our hypotheses on the ring
\mathcal{R}
mean that the Weyl modules
V(\lambda)
form a collection of compact projective generators as
\lambda
ranges over the set of integral dominant weights. There are no nontrivial homomorphisms nor extensions between different Weyl modules. Thus every object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
may be written as a direct sum

(2.1)

V\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}M_{\lambda}

where
M_{\lambda}
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.
The braiding is specified by an
R
-matrix over the ring
\mathcal{R}
.

2.2.
The vector and fundamental representations

The vector representation
\mathcal{R}^{N}
for either
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
and
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
will be simply denoted
V
. We fix
v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}
to be the standard basis for
V
.

Let
\tau:V\otimes V
denote the tensor flip,
\tau(v\otimes w)=w\otimes v
.
The braiding,
\sigma_{V,V}=\tau\circ R
,
where
R=R_{V,V}
denotes the
R
-matrix for
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
,
satisfies a Hecke relation

(\sigma_{V,V}-\mathtt{q})(\sigma_{V,V}+\mathtt{q}^{-1})=0.

The
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
braiding on the vector representation is equal to the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
braiding, multiplied by a factor of
\mathtt{q}^{-\frac{1}{N}}
. To avoid confusion, we will reserve the notation
R
and
\sigma_{V,V}
for the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}

R
-matrix and braiding, and write
\mathtt{q}^{-\frac{1}{N}}R
and
\mathtt{q}^{-\frac{1}{N}}\sigma_{V,V}
to reference the
\mathrm{SL}_{N}

R
-matrix and braiding.

The operators
T_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{V^{\otimes(i-1)}}\otimes\sigma_{V,V}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{V^{\otimes n-i-1}}
(in both the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
case) define an action of the finite Hecke algebra
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
with quadratic parameter
\mathtt{q}
.
We denote by
\Lambda^{k}V:=\bigwedge\nolimits_{\mathtt{q}}^{k}(V)
the
k
th fundamental representation of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
. In particular
\Lambda^{N}V
is the one-dimensional determinant representation.
We can embed
\Lambda^{N}V
into
V^{\otimes N}
as the span of

(2.2)

\displaystyle z=\mathrm{e}_{{N}}\cdot v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{N}=\frac{1}{[N]_{\mathtt{q}^{-2}}!}\sum_{\sigma\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}(-\mathtt{q}^{-1})^{\ell en(\sigma)}v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma(N)}.

In the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
the vector
z
defined by
(
2.2
) is now an invariant vector.
Note as in (
3.1
) below for
\mathtt{q}=t
, that
\mathrm{e}_{{N}}
is the sign idempotent in
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{N}
.

2.3.
The quantum coordinate algebra

Definition 2.4
.

The reflection equation algebra of type
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\MatN)
, is the
\mathcal{R}
-algebra generated by symbols
\ell^{i}_{j}
, for
i,j=1,\ldots N
, subject to the relations,

R_{21}L_{1}R_{12}L_{2}=L_{2}R_{21}L_{1}R_{12},

where
L:=\sum_{i,j}\ell^{i}_{j}E^{j}_{i}
is a matrix with entries the generators
\ell^{i}_{j}
, and for a matrix
X
, we write
X_{1}=X\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{V}
,
X_{2}=\operatorname{Id}_{V}\otimes X
, so that the matrix equation above is equivalent to the list of relations, for
i,j,n,r\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
:

(2.3)

\sum_{k,l,m,p}R^{ij}_{kl}\ell^{l}_{m}R^{mk}_{np}\ell^{p}_{r}=\sum_{s,t,u,v}\ell^{i}_{s}R^{sj}_{tu}\ell^{u}_{v}R^{vs}_{nr}.

Remark 2.5
.

We note that, since
R
appears quadratically on both sides of the defining relation, the relations are unchanged by replacing
R\leadsto\mathtt{q}^{-\frac{1}{N}}R
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
JW20
]
)
.

The element,

\operatorname{det}_{\mathtt{q}}(L):=\sum_{\sigma\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}(-\mathtt{q})^{\ell en(\sigma)}\cdot\mathtt{q}^{e(\sigma)}\ell^{1}_{\sigma(1)}\cdots\ell^{N}_{\sigma(N)},

is central in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\MatN)
.

Here
\ell en(\sigma)
denotes the length, i.e. the number of pairs
i<j
such that
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)
, and
e(\sigma)
denotes the excedence, i.e. the number of elements
i
such that
\sigma(i)>i
.

Definition 2.7
.

The quantum coordinate algebras
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{SL}_{N})
are the algebras obtained from
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\MatN)
, by inverting,
respectively specializing to one, the central element
\operatorname{det}_{\mathtt{q}}(L)
. That is,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\MatN)[\operatorname{det}_{\mathtt{q}}(L)^{-1}],\qquad\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathrm{SL}_{N})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(\MatN)/\langle\operatorname{det}_{\mathtt{q}}(L)-1\rangle.

The
\mathcal{R}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
is naturally an algebra object in the tensor category
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
, in other words it is a locally finite
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
-module algebra. We recall the quantum Harish-Chandra isomorphism,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}\cong\mathcal{R}[H]^{W},

which holds for generic
\mathtt{q}
, and identifies the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}
of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
with the
W
-invariant functions on the maximal torus
H
.

Notation 2.8
.

We denote by
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}
the canonical generators – “quantum Casimirs” – which are obtained as the quantum trace of the
k
th fundamental representation
\Lambda^{k}V
(see (
4.7
) below for a depiction). In particular, we have
c_{N}=\operatorname{det}_{\mathtt{q}}(L)
.
Working over
\mathcal{K}
the elements
c_{k}
generate the center of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
.

2.4.
The quantum Harish–Chandra category
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)

The discussion in this subsection is not strictly necessary to establish the results in this paper, but is meant to aid the reader in interpreting those results.

Let
A
be some algebra object in the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
. Recall that this means
A
is an object of
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}
, with an associative product
m:A\otimes A\to A
internal to
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
, i.e.
m
is a homomorphism of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.

Definition 2.9
.

The category of
weakly equivariant
(left)
A
-modules consists of objects
M\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
, equipped with associative action maps
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}\otimes M\to M
internal to
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
.

The quantum Harish–Chandra category
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
is the category of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
-modules internal to the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}G
. It carries the cp-rigid monoidal structure of relative tensor product. See
[
BBJ18
,
Saf19
]
for further details.

Remark 2.10
.

Specializing the category
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
at
\mathtt{q}=1
(that is, working over the ring
\kappa=\mathcal{R}/(\mathtt{q}-1)
), we obtain the category
\mathrm{QCoh}([G/G])
of quasi-coherent sheaves on the quotient stack
[G/G]
. On the other hand, as explained in
[
Saf19
]
, the quasi-classical degeneration
\mathtt{q}\to 1
of
\mathrm{HC}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
becomes the classical category of Harish-Chandra
U(\mathfrak{g}
)-bimodules.

2.5.
Quantum differential operators

The algebra of quantum differential operators on
G
, which we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
, was studied in many different settings. The presentation below as a twisted tensor product is adapted from the paper
[
VV10
]
(see also
[
BJ17
]
), and hence matches the conventions of
[
Jor09a
]
(see footnote 3 there, however) and
[
JV19
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, or
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
, the algebra
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
is the twisted tensor product,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G).

Denoting
a^{i}_{j}
and
b^{i}_{j}
to be the generators of the first and second factors (henceforth denoted
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
), the cross relations are given in matrix form by:

(2.5)

\displaystyle B_{2}R_{21}A_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{21}A_{1}R_{12}B_{2}R_{21},

if
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}

(2.6)

\displaystyle B_{2}R_{21}A_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{21}A_{1}R_{12}B_{2}R_{21}\mathtt{q}^{-2/N},

if
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}

where
A=\sum_{i,j}a^{i}_{j}E^{j}_{i}
and
B=\sum_{i,j}b^{i}_{j}E^{j}_{i}
.

Remark 2.12
.

The matrices
A
and
B
here are defined using the defining representation
V
of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
, and its dual
V^{*}
. We note for later reference that in fact for any representation
X
we can define a matrix
A_{X}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\otimes\End_{\mathcal{R}}(X)
, which will satisfy the relations of (
2.3
), with
R
replaced by
R_{X,X}
. Similarly, in the algebra
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
we can define matrices
A_{X}
and
B_{Y}
for any representations
X
and
Y
, and their cross relations will be as in (
2.5
), with
R
replaced by
R_{X,Y}
. We refer to
[
Jor09a
]
for details about these matrices and the resulting diagrammatic calculus for computing with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
.

Notation 2.13
.

We denote by
i_{\mathcal{A}}
and
i_{\mathcal{B}}
the inclusions into the first and second tensor factor of (
2.4
).
We denote by
i_{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}
the inclusion of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}
into
\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
followed by
i_{\mathcal{B}}
, whose image is denoted
\mathcal{B}^{G}
.

Observe that
i_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes i_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)
is the tautological isomorphism of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules (however it is not an algebra homomorphism).

Definition 2.14
.

The quantum moment map,

\operatorname{ad}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}

is defined by the matrix formulas by
\operatorname{ad}(L)=B^{-1}ABA^{-1}
.

We have the Rosso homomorphism
\Rosso:\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\rhd
denote the quantum adjoint action of
U_{\mathtt{q}}(\mathfrak{g})
on
{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}
. Then the homomorphism
\operatorname{ad}
satisfies the quantum moment map equation,

(2.7)

\operatorname{ad}(\ell)y=(\Rosso(\ell)_{(1)}\rhd y)\operatorname{ad}(\Rosso(\ell)_{(2)}),\qquad\textrm{for $\ell\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)$, $y\in{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)}$.}

We refer to
[
Saf19
]
for a more in-depth discussion of quantum moment maps.

Remark 2.15
.

If we work over
\mathcal{K}
, the Rosso homomoprhism
\Rosso
is an algebra embedding, and pulling back along
\Rosso
defines a fully faithful functor
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)\textrm{-mod}
.

.

We require notation for the following
\mathcal{R}
-submodules of

\displaystyle:=\{i_{\mathcal{B}}(y)-\chi(y)\,\,|\,\,y\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}\}

where

\mathcal{O}_{\mathtt{q}}(G)^{G}
to

, i.e., a character.

Here, we denote by

3.
Double affine Hecke algebras

Notation 3.1
.

When discussing the finite and affine Hecke algebra we will use the following notation. Fix a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
which is an integral domain and contains fixed element
t\in\mathcal{R}^{\times}
, such that
\frac{t^{m}-1}{t-1}
is invertible for all
m\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the field of fractions of
\mathcal{R}
. Unless stated otherwise everything will be defined with base ring
\mathcal{R}
.

Definition 3.2
.

The finite Hecke algebra
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{n}(t)
is the
\mathcal{R}
-algebra with generators
T_{1},\ldots T_{n-1}
, and relations:

T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1},\textrm{ for $i=1,\ldots,n-2$},

\quad T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i},\textrm{ if $|i-j|\geq 2$ },\qquad(T_{i}-t)(T_{i}+t^{-1})=0,\textrm{ for $i=1,\ldots n-1$}.

Given a reduced word
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{m}}\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}
we have a corresponding well-defined element
T_{w}=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{m}}\in\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{n}(t).
We will write
\ell en(w)=m
for the length of
w
. By convention
T_{\operatorname{Id}}=1.
Note that the set

\{T_{w}|w\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\}
forms a basis of
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{n}(t).

The finite Hecke algebra has two one-dimensional representations. Corresponding to the trivial and the sign representation, respectively, we have the idempotents

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{+}_{n}=\frac{1}{[n]_{t^{2}}!}\sum_{w\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}t^{\ell en(w)}T_{w},\qquad\mathrm{e}_{{n}}=\frac{1}{[n]_{t^{-2}}!}\sum_{w\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}(-t^{-1})^{\ell en(w)}T_{w}.

These satisfy
(T_{i}-t)\mathrm{e}^{+}_{n}=0
and
(T_{i}+t^{-1})\mathrm{e}_{{n}}=0
, for
1\leq i<n
.

Definition 3.3
.

The extended affine Hecke algebra
\operatorname{H}_{n}(t)
is generated by the algebras
\mathcal{R}[Y_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots Y_{n}^{\pm 1}]
and
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{n}(t)
, with relations:

T_{i}Y_{i}T_{i}=Y_{i+1},\textrm{for $i=1,\ldots,n-1$},\quad T_{i}Y_{j}=Y_{j}T_{i},\textrm{ for $j\neq i,i+1$}

Remark 3.4
.

A common alternative presentation imposes instead the relations
T_{i}Y_{i}^{-1}T_{i}=Y_{i+1}^{-1}
. An isomorphism between the presentations may be given by inverting
Y_{i}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{Y}_{n}]
the
\mathcal{R}
-subalgebra generated by
Y_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots Y_{n}^{\pm 1}
.

Notation 3.5
.

We denote by
S(\mathcal{Y}_{n})=\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{Y}_{n}]^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}
the space of symmetric Laurent polynomials, which is also the center of
\operatorname{H}_{n}(t)
.
To simplify notation, we will write the parameters
t
and
n
only when required, and otherwise abbreviate
\operatorname{H}_{n}(t)
by either
\operatorname{H}_{n}
or simply
\operatorname{H}
. Similarly for
\operatorname{H}^{\mathrm{fin}}
,
\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{Y}]
, and
S(\mathcal{Y})
.

Theorem 4.14

We have the following:

(1)

The map
(\mathrm{e}\ddot{\mathrm{SW}}\mathrm{e})_{{\kappa}}

(2)

(\mathrm{e}\ddot{\mathrm{SW}}\mathrm{e})_{\mathcal{R}}

Thus it remains to prove Claim (1). First note that the source

\kappa[\mathcal{X}^{+},\mathcal{Y}^{+}]^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}:=\kappa[\bar{X}_{1},\ldots,\bar{X}_{N},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}]^{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{N}}

Putting these observations together, we may identify

\displaystyle\kappa[\mathcal{X}^{+},\mathcal{Y}^{+}]

6.1.
Poles and zeros of intertwiners

Our strategy is to define the required endomorphisms
\Phi_{w}
in terms of renormalized intertwiners

from Definition

. The expressions
f_{{i,i+1}}^{\pm 1}
can introduce poles and zeroes into the definition, and hence much of the technical complication in making sense of the

is to keep track of these.

To this end, let us begin by collecting some simple observations concerning zeros of the
f_{{i,j}}
when evaluated at the character
q^{\rho}=t^{-2\rho}
appearing in Corollary
6.5
. We have:

(Y_{i}-Y_{j})|_{t^{-2\rho}}=0\quad\textrm{if, and only if, $j-i\equiv 0\mod(n+1)$}.

With the
q=t^{-2}
specialization we observe:

f_{{i,j}}=tY_{i}-t^{-1}Y_{j}=t^{-1}(Y_{i+n}-Y_{j})=t(Y_{i}-Y_{j-n}),

hence we conclude:

f_{{i,j}}|_{q^{\rho}}=0\quad\textrm{ if, and only if $j-i\equiv n\mod(n+1)$}.

Let us first rewrite

\nu_{i}=T_{i}\frac{tf_{{i-n,i+1}}}{f_{{i,i+1}}}+\frac{(t-t^{-1})Y_{i+1}}{f_{{i,i+1}}}.

As a consequence of this formula and Equation (

), if we consider the normal ordering
\colon\!{\nu_{w}}\colon\!
of some operator
\nu_{w}
as described in Section
6.2
(see Definition
6.10
) below, we see that the poles and zeros of
\colon\!{\nu_{w}}\colon\!
are controlled by the set of inversions of
w
. This is captured in the following Definition, as will become clearer in the proof of Theorem
6.11
.

Definition 6.8
.

Fix
w\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. We say that an inversion
(i,j)\in\operatorname{Inv}(w)
is
vanishing
if
(j-i)\equiv 0\mod(n+1)
, and
singular
if
(j-i)\equiv n\mod(n+1)
. We denote by
\operatorname{Inv}_{0}(w)
and
\operatorname{Inv}_{\infty}(w)
, respectively, the sets of vanishing and singular inversions.

Recall that
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
is the stabilizer of
(0,\ldots,0)
with respect to the action on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Let us denote by
\gamma\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
the translation by the element
-\rho=(0,-1,\ldots,1-n)
, so that
\gamma\cdot\rho=(0,\ldots,0)
.
Recall the symbol
\cdot
denotes our usual (not dot) action from Section
3.3
.
Clearly, the stabilizer of
\rho
with respect to the above action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is

\operatorname{Stab}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}(\rho)=\gamma^{-1}{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\gamma.

We work with
-\rho
in this section since
q^{\rho}=t^{-2\rho}
but conventionally
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, not
(2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.
The stabilizer is therefore generated by the elements

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}s_{i}\gamma

\displaystyle=[1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,\,i\!+1\!+n,\,\,i\!-\!n,\,\,\cdots,\,n]

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{s_{i}s_{i+1}\cdots s_{n-1}s_{i-1}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}}_{\delta_{i}^{-1}}s_{0}\underbrace{s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{i-1}s_{n-1}\cdots s_{i+1}s_{i}}_{\delta_{i}},

expressed first in “window notation” (see
[
BB05
]
), and then as a reduced expression. In particular we record the equation
\ell en(\gamma^{-1}s_{i}\gamma)=2n-1
.
Note that
\delta_{i}=[2,\,3,\,\cdots,\,i,\,n,\,1,\,\,i\!+\!1,\,\cdots,\,n\!-\!1]
is an

-cycle.

The following lemma is straightforward; we omit the proof, but instead give an example in Figure

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. We have bijections,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\alpha:&\operatorname{Inv}(w)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{0}(\gamma^{-1}w\gamma)\\
&(i,j)\mapsto(i,j+(j-i)n)\end{array},\qquad\begin{array}[]{rl}\beta:&\operatorname{Inv}(w)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{\infty}(\gamma^{-1}w\gamma),\\

6.2.
Normal orderings

Recall that
\mathbb{H}_{n}(N)
has a vector space decomposition
\mathbb{H}_{n}(N)=\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathcal{K}[\mathcal{Y}]

\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}=\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
. Let us fix the standard basis of
\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{X})
consisting of elements
T_{w}
, for
w\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}

Definition 6.10
.

Given

, we will write
\colon\!{h}\colon\!\in\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
for its

\colon\!{h}\colon\!=\sum_{w\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}T_{w}g_{w},

where
g_{w}\in\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
and all but finitely many
g_{w}
are nonzero. In particular, we have

if, and only if
g_{w}\in\mathcal{K}[\mathcal{Y}]\subset\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
for all

Given a normally ordered element
\colon\!{h}\colon\!=\sum_{x}T_{x}g_{x}
of

w\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
such that
g_{w}\neq 0
, and for

g_{x}\neq 0
implies
\ell en(x)<\ell en(w)
. In this case we call
T_{w}
the
leading term
and
g_{w}\in\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
the
leading coefficient
. We stress that, because length does not define a total ordering, not every normally ordered expression has a leading term.

We now turn to the key technical result of this section.

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}

\colon\!{\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}}\colon\!=\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}T_{x}g_{x}.

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
via intertwiners

Let us now outline an alternative proof of Theorem
1.2
that relies on Proposition
6.1
.

In the next two theorems, we use
\underline{v}
to denote a generator of a one-dimensional
\mathcal{K}[\mathcal{Y}]
-module.
Observe that if
\underline{v}
has
\mathcal{Y}
-weight
t^{-2\rho}=q^{\rho}
then for a
g\in\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}]
without zeros or poles at
t^{-2\rho}
we have
\colon\!{g}\colon\!|_{t^{-2\rho}}\otimes\underline{v}=\colon\!{g}\colon\!\otimes\underline{v}
.

Theorem 6.13
.

The map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{K}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}]^{op}

\displaystyle\to\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathcal{Y}}(t^{-2\rho})),

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto\Phi_{w},

where
\Phi_{w}(h\otimes\underline{v})=(h\colon\!{\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}}\colon\!)\otimes\underline{v}
, for
h\in\mathbb{H}
defines a
\mathcal{K}
-algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

First, let us confirm that each
\Phi_{w}
is indeed a
\mathbb{H}
-linear endomorphism of
\Ind^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathcal{Y}}(t^{-2\rho})
. Theorem
6.11
ensures the well-definedness of each expression
(\colon\!{\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}}\colon\!)\otimes\underline{v}
, while the intertwiner property implies that
(\colon\!{\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}}\colon\!)\otimes\underline{v}
lies in the
t^{-2\rho}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space. Hence Frobenius reciprocity for the functor
\Ind^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathcal{Y}}
produces the asserted module-homomorphism.

The homomorphism property for
\Phi
follows from the following identity in
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}
:

\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}u\gamma}}=\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}wu\gamma}}.

Finally, we observe that, as a consequence of Theorem
6.11
, the set
\{(\colon\!{\nu_{{\gamma^{-1}w\gamma}}}\colon\!)\otimes\underline{v}\mid w\in{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}\}
forms a basis of the
t^{-2\rho}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space, hence
\Phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem
1.2
now follows, using Proposition
6.3
and Corollary
6.5
.

6.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
via intertwiners

In this section, we consider the endomorphism algebra
\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})
for more general
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
than
t^{-2\rho}=q^{\rho}
, but still descending. Note
q^{\rho}=t^{-2\rho}
is descending.
In other words, by Theorem
3.22
, we consider
\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind_{{\mathcal{S}H}}^{\mathbb{H}}\{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}\}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn})
.

It is convenient to introduce the following terminology:

Definition 6.14
.

Let
a,b,r\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
. We say that
a
and
b
are
in the same
r
-line
if
a/b=r^{z}
for some
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, and otherwise that they are
in distinct
r
-lines
.

Theorem 6.15
.

Let
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}\in(\mathcal{K}^{\times})^{n}
be descending
such that entries in the same
t^{2}
-line are in consecutive position.
Further assume
that
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
is
transverse
, meaning that
\Stab_{\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}({\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})\cap{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}=\Stab_{{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}}({\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})=\{\operatorname{Id}\}
, i.e., that
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
if
i\neq j
. Let
\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
be of minimal length such that
\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}\Stab_{\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}({\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}^{-1}=W_{J}\subseteq{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}
for appropriate standard parabolic subgroup.
Then the map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{K}[W_{J}]^{op}

\displaystyle\to\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}),

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto\Phi_{w},

where
\Phi_{w}(h\otimes\underline{v})=(h\colon\!{\nu_{{{\gamma_{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}^{-1}w\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}}}}\colon\!|_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}})\otimes\underline{v}
,
defines a
\mathcal{K}
-algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

Let us outline how to modify the proof of Theorem
6.13
to apply here.
Note that
J
is determined by the different
t^{2}
-lines that the
a_{i}
lie on.
In particular, the generalized
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space of
\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
has dimension
|W_{J}|
.
Similar to Lemma
6.9
, if
w\in W_{J}
, we still have bijections between
\operatorname{Inv}(w)
, the inversions of
\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}^{-1}w\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}
that potentially introduce poles to the coefficients of
\colon\!{\nu_{{{\gamma_{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}^{-1}w{\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}}}}}\colon\!
, and those inversions that potentially introduce zeros. (The criterion for an inversion being vanishing or singular is more complicated in this case and not just determined by height.)
As before, we are able to construct
|W_{J}|
linearly independent
\mathcal{Y}
-weight vectors of weight
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
and corresponding endomorphisms to yield the isomorphism
\Phi
.
∎

Theorem
1.4
now follows, using
Proposition
6.3
.

Remark 6.16
.

({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\diamond})

Very few modifications need be made for the
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}
case of the above proof. Wherever
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
appears, we instead take its quotient by the subgroup generated by
\pi^{n}
. In computing the stabilizer of a weight we use the modified action of
\overline{\pi}
as in (
3.7
).
We also compare entries in the same
t^{2/N}
-lines, and similarly we declare these to be descending if whenever
a_{i}/a_{j}=t^{2z/N}
with
z\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i<j
then
z\geq 0
. The
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
we will consider furthermore are required to have
\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}=\boldsymbol{Z}
.

Remark 6.17
.

The non-transverse case, where
\Stab_{\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}({\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})\cap{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}=W_{K}\neq\{\operatorname{Id}\}
, is more complicated. We may still write
\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}\Stab_{\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}({\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})\gamma_{{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}}^{-1}=W_{J}\subseteq{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{n}
, but we have
K\subseteq J
.
While the
generalized

{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space of
\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
still has dimension
|W_{J}|
, the
ordinary

{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space only has dimension
|W_{J\setminus K}|
.
Hence
\dim\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})=|W_{J\setminus K}|
in this case.
However the ring structure of
\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}})
is more complicated than that of the semisimple algebra
\mathcal{K}[W_{J\setminus K}]
, and can contain nilpotent elements. This is related to the fact that the induced module is not semisimple. See Example
6.19
below.

Example 6.18
.

This example shows the necessity of
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
being descending for various results in this paper.
Let
n=N=2
. Let
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}=t^{-2\rho}=(t^{0},t^{-2})
and
{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}=(t^{-2},t^{0})
. Then
\Ind_{{\mathcal{S}H}}^{\mathbb{H}}\{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}\}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}\simeq\Ind_{{\mathcal{S}H}}^{\mathbb{H}}\{{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}\}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}\simeq\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}\simeq{\mathbf{T}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}}
.
We have let
{\mathbf{T}}=\Ind_{\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathbb{H}}{\mathtt{triv}}
for
{\mathtt{triv}}
the one-dimensional
\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{Y})
-module on which
(T_{1}-t),\,(Y_{1}-t^{-2}),(Y_{2}-1)
all vanish.
We let
{\mathbf{S}}=\Ind_{\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathbb{H}}{\mathtt{sgn}}
for
{\mathtt{sgn}}
the one-dimensional
\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{Y})
-module on which
(T_{1}+t^{-1}),\,(Y_{1}-1),(Y_{2}-t^{-2})
all vanish. As in Theorem
6.13
,
\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\Ind^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathcal{Y}}(t^{-2\rho}))\simeq\End_{\mathbb{H}}({\mathbf{T}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}})\simeq\mathcal{K}[{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{{2}}]^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Next consider
\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}
. Observe
{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}
is not descending, but since it is in the same
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
-orbit as
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
, it will have the same composition factors as above. In fact

0\to{\mathbf{S}}\to\Ind_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathbb{H}}{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}\to{\mathbf{T}}\to 0
is non-split and so the induced modules has trivial endomorphism algebra. To see the above exact sequences does not split, one can compute that the
{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight space of
{\mathbf{S}}
is zero. (This is a “rectangular” representation from
[
JV19
]
.) On the other hand, the generalized
{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}
weight space of
{\mathbf{T}}
is two-dimensional, but the ordinary weight space is just one-dimensional.
In particular
{\mathbf{T}}
is not
\mathcal{Y}
-semisimple.
(Note, we could have also taken
{\underline{{\mathbf{b}}}}=(t^{0},t^{0})
as in Section
5.2
, keeping Remark
5.9
in mind.)

Example 6.19
.

This example shows the necessity of
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
being transverse for Theorem
6.15
to hold. We give an example of an induced module that has a nonzero nilpotent endomorphism, hence neither it nor its endomorphism algebra is semisimple. Let
n=N=3
and
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}=(t^{0},t^{0},t^{-2})
which is descending but clearly not transverse. We take
\gamma
to be translation by
(0,0,-1)
,
so the stabilizer of
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
is

\gamma^{-1}{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{{3}}\gamma=\{\operatorname{Id},\,s_{1},\,s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2},\,s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2},\,s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}s_{1},\,s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}\}.

The first difference is that
\nu_{{s_{1}}}\underline{v}=\underline{v}
and
T_{1}\underline{v}
is a generalized
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\mathcal{Y}
-weight vector. It is easy to see the generalized
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
weight space has dimension 6 and the ordinary
{\underline{{\mathbf{a}}}}
weight space has dimension
\leq 3
. But in fact, as asserted above, the weight space only has dimension 2, as indicated by
|J\setminus K|=1
. Next, consider
\gamma^{-1}s_{2}\gamma=s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}

Abstract
. It is known that the large cardinal strength of the Axiom of Determinacy when enhanced with the hypothesis that all sets of reals are universally Baire is much stronger than the Axiom of Determinacy itself. Sargsyan conjectured it to be as strong as the existence of a cardinal that is both a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of strong cardinals. Larson, Sargsyan and Wilson used a generalization of Woodin’s derived model construction to show that this conjectured result would be optimal. In this paper we introduce a new translation procedure for hybrid mice extending work of Steel, Zhu and Sargsyan and apply it to prove Sargsyan’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

A central theme in inner model theory is the deep connection between determinacy principles and inner models with large cardinals. One hierarchy of determinacy principles is given by imposing additional structural properties on a model of the Axiom of Determinacy (
\operatorname{AD}
). Examples of such structural properties are “
\theta_{0}<\Theta
”, “all sets of reals are Suslin”, “
\Theta
is regular”, or the Largest Suslin Axiom, see for example
[
27
,
24
,
13
,
17
]
. Woodin used his famous derived model construction to show that if there is a cardinal
\lambda
that is a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of
{<}\lambda
-strong cardinals, then there is a model of
\text{``}\operatorname{AD}^{+}+\text{ all sets of reals are Suslin''},
see
[
27
]
. Approximations to the converse were proved by Woodin,
and the full converse was proved by Steel
[
24
]
, using Woodin’s work together with precursors of the translation procedures we use in this paper. By results of Martin and Woodin, see
[
27
, Theorems 9.1 and 9.2]
, assuming
\operatorname{AD}
, the statement “all sets of reals are Suslin” is equivalent to the Axiom of Determinacy for games on reals (
\operatorname{AD}_{\mathbb{R}}
).

Being universally Baire as defined by Feng, Magidor and Woodin
[
2
]
is a non-trivial strengthening of being Suslin. Larson, Sargsyan and Wilson showed in
[
6
]
via an extension of Woodin’s derived model construction that if there is a cardinal
\lambda
that is a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of strong cardinals, then there is a model of
\text{``}\operatorname{AD}+
all sets of reals are universally Baire”. One challenge in their argument is that no model of the form
L(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
is a model of
\text{``}\operatorname{AD}+
all sets of reals are universally Baire”. So the resulting model of determinacy needs to have non-trivial structure above
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
. Sargsyan conjectured that a cardinal
\lambda
that is a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of strong cardinals is the optimal hypothesis to construct a model of
\text{``}\operatorname{AD}+
all sets of reals are universally Baire”. We prove here that this conjecture is true.
More precisely, we extend Sargsyan’s translation procedure from
[
14
]
to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose there is a proper class model of the theory “
\operatorname{AD}+
all sets of reals are universally Baire”. Then there is a transitive model
\mathcal{M}
of
\operatorname{ZFC}
containing all ordinals such that
\mathcal{M}
has a cardinal
\lambda
that is a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of strong cardinals.

Translation procedures can be used to transform a hybrid strategy mouse (a
hod mouse
) into an ordinary mouse while generating additional large cardinals from the strategy predicate. They originate from constructions in the
core model induction
method due to Woodin. The idea behind this method is to construct new mice with iteration strategies of high desciptive set theoretic complexity. Once this method reaches infinitely many Woodin cardinals, a key step in every core model induction is to pass from a hybrid strategy mouse to an ordinary mouse without loosing its descriptive set theoretic complexity. Here is where translation procedures start coming into play: They translate hybrid strategy mice into ordinary mice while ensuring that the derived models of the original strategy mouse and the resulting ordinary mouse are the same. This is what we mean by
preserving the descriptive set theoretic complexity
here.
In
[
25
]
, Steel develops a translation procedure that can be used to prove Woodin’s result that

\operatorname{ZF}+\operatorname{AD}^{+}+\;\theta_{0}<\Theta

is equiconsistent with

\displaystyle\operatorname{ZFC}\text{ + }\text{ there is a pair of cardinals }\kappa,\lambda\text{ such that }

\displaystyle\lambda\text{ is a limit of Woodin cardinals and }\kappa\text{ is }{<}\lambda\text{-strong.}

Steel’s translation procedure in
[
25
]
originates from work of Closson, Neeman, and Steel and was later extended by Zhu in
[
33
]
. Steel’s and Zhu’s constructions use
\operatorname{AD}^{+}
theory and work in a specific hod mouse, more specifially, they use a real parameter for the translation. Therefore, their constructions have the disadvantage that the translation cannot be done in
\operatorname{HOD}
itself but only in
\operatorname{HOD}
of a real.

This was improved by Sargsyan in
[
14
]
, where he developed a translation procedure that works locally and constructs a model with infinitely many Woodin cardinals and one strong cardinal. Sargsyan’s construction does not depend on the choice of a specific hod mouse or a fixed real parameter and can therefore be done in
\operatorname{HOD}
itself. However, it was open how to continue Sargsyan’s translation after the first level reaching a strong cardinal. We extend Sargsyan’s work and push it through infinitely many levels by developing a new translation procedure that allows us to construct a model with a cardinal that is both a limit of Woodin cardinals and a limit of strong cardinals, cf. Definition
4.3
. The model
\mathcal{M}
we construct will in fact be a countably iterable premouse. Note that the premouse obtained from Sargsyan’s translation in
[
14
]
was not known to be iterable.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a translatable structure as in Definition

The amount of iterability we obtain for the translated premouse

By combining our work here with the results in

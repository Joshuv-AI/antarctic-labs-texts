Rigidity of free boundary MOTS
Abraão Mendes

Abstract
. The aim of this work is to present an initial data version of Hawking’s theorem on the topology of back hole spacetimes in the context of manifolds with boundary. More precisely, we generalize the results of G. J. Galloway and R. Schoen [ 13 ] and G. J. Galloway [ 11 , 12 ] by proving that a compact free boundary stable marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) Σ \Sigma in an initial data set with boundary satisfying natural dominant energy conditions (DEC) is of positive Yamabe type, i.e. Σ \Sigma admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary, provided Σ \Sigma is outermost. To do so, we prove that if Σ \Sigma is a compact free boundary stable MOTS which does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary in an initial data set satisfying the interior and the boundary DEC, then an outer neighborhood of Σ \Sigma can be foliated by free boundary MOTS Σ t \Sigma_{t} , assuming that Σ \Sigma is weakly outermost. Moreover, each Σ t \Sigma_{t} has vanishing outward null second fundamental form, is Ricci flat with totally geodesic boundary, and the dominant energy conditions saturate on Σ t \Sigma_{t} .



1.
Introduction

In a seminal paper, S. W. Hawking
[
14
]
showed, among other things, that closed cross-sections of the event horizon in
(3+1)
-dimensional asymptotically flat stationary black hole spacetimes obeying the
spacetime
dominant energy condition (DEC) are topologically
2
-spheres. Hawking’s theorem extends to outer apparent horizons, i.e.
weakly outermost
marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS) in more general (not necessarily stationary) black hole spacetimes.

In
[
13
]
, G. J. Galloway and R. Schoen extended Hawking’s theorem to higher dimensional black hole spacetimes by proving that cross-sections of the event horizon (in the stationary case) and outer apparent horizons (in the general case) are of positive Yamabe type, i.e. they admit metrics of positive scalar curvature. Their result is consistent with the example presented by R. Emparan and H. S. Reall
[
10
]
of a
(4+1)
-dimensional stationary vacuum black hole spacetime with horizon topology
S^{2}\times S^{1}
.

We paraphrase Galloway-Schoen’s result as follows. Definitions are given in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set satisfying the DEC. If
\Sigma
is a closed stable MOTS in
(M,g,K)
, then either:

(1)

\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature or

(2)

\Sigma
is Ricci flat, the DEC saturates on
\Sigma
, and
\Sigma
has vanishing outward null second fundamental form.

Assuming the initial data set
(M,g,K)
can be embedded into a spacetime obeying the spacetime DEC and
\Sigma
is a closed
outermost
MOTS in
(M,g,K)
, Galloway
[
11
]
was able to rule out the ‘exceptional circumstances’ (item (
2
)) of Theorem
1.1
; that is, under these hypotheses,
\Sigma
in fact admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

A few years ago, Galloway
[
12
]
was able to drop the embeddedness assumption on the initial data set and prove a purely initial data version of the results presented in
[
11
]
and
[
13
]
. Among other things, he proved the following rigidity result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set satisfying the DEC. Suppose
\Sigma
is a closed weakly outermost MOTS in
(M,g,K)
that does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature. Then there exists an outer neighborhood
U\cong[0,\epsilon)\times\Sigma
of
\Sigma
in
M
such that, for each
t\in[0,\epsilon)
, the following hold:

(1)

\Sigma_{t}\cong\{t\}\times\Sigma
is a MOTS. In fact,
\Sigma_{t}
has vanishing outward null second fundamental form.

(2)

\Sigma_{t}
is Ricci flat with respect to the induced metric.

(3)

The DEC saturates on
\Sigma_{t}
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
, he obtained the following:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set satisfying the DEC. If
\Sigma
is a closed outermost MOTS in
(M,g,K)
, then
\Sigma
is of positive Yamabe type, i.e.
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

Our main goal in the present work is to extend Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
to the context of compact
free boundary
MOTS in initial data sets with boundary satisfying the
interior
and the
boundary
dominant energy conditions.

Our first result is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set with boundary and
\Sigma
be a compact free boundary stable MOTS in
(M,g,K)
. Suppose
(M,g,K)
satisfies the interior and the boundary DEC. If
\Sigma
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary and is weakly outermost in
(M,g,K)
, then there exists an outer neighborhood
U\cong[0,\epsilon)\times\Sigma
of
\Sigma
in
M
such that, for each
t\in[0,\epsilon)
, the following hold:

(1)

\Sigma_{t}\cong\{t\}\times\Sigma
is a free boundary MOTS. In fact,
\Sigma_{t}
has vanishing outward null second fundamental form.

(2)

\Sigma_{t}
is Ricci flat and has totally geodesic boundary with respect to the induced metric.

(3)

The interior DEC saturates on
U
and
J|_{\Sigma_{t}}=0
.

(4)

The boundary DEC saturates along
\partial\Sigma_{t}
and
(\iota_{\varrho}\pi)^{\top}|_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}=0
.

The 1-forms
J
and
(\iota_{\varrho}\pi)^{\top}
are defined in Section
2
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.4
, we obtain the following topological obstruction result for the existence of free boundary outermost MOTS in initial data sets under natural energy hypotheses, which can be seen as an initial data version of Hawking’s theorem in the context of manifolds with boundary.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set with boundary and
\Sigma
be a compact free boundary stable MOTS in
(M,g,K)
. Suppose
(M,g,K)
satisfies the interior and the boundary DEC. If
\Sigma
is outermost in
(M,g,K)
, then
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary.

It is important to mention that the compact-with-boundary orientable manifolds which admit metrics of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary are completely characterized in
n=2,3
dimensions. In
n=2
, this follows directly from the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. In particular, they are topologically a disk. In
n=3
, the characterization of such manifolds was done by A. Carlotto and C. Li
[
6
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review some background material on MOTS. In Section
3
, we state and prove several auxiliary results. In Section
4
, we present the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Finally, in Section
5
, we establish a splitting result under natural convexity and volume-minimizing conditions.

2.
Marginally Outer Trapped Surfaces

An
initial data set

(M,g,K)
consists of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
K
on
M
. We assume that
M
is oriented throughout
this paper.

The
local energy density

\mu
and the
local current density

J
of
(M,g,K)
are given by

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{2}\left(R-|K|^{2}+(\tr K)^{2}\right)\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,J=\divergente(K-(\tr K)g),

where
R
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
. The initial data set
(M,g,K)
is said to satisfy the
dominant energy condition
(DEC) if

(1)

\displaystyle\mu\geq|J|\,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\,M.

The
momentum tensor

\pi
of
(M,g,K)
is given by

\displaystyle\pi=K-(\tr K)g.

Then
\mu
and
J
can be written in terms of
\pi
by

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{2}\left(R-|\pi|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}(\tr\pi)^{2}\right)\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,J=\divergente\pi,

where
n+1
is the dimension of
M
,
n\geq 2
.

When
M
is a manifold with boundary, we denote the second fundamental form of
\partial M
in
(M,g)
by
\II^{\partial M}
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\II^{\partial M}(Y,Z)=\langle\nabla_{Y}\varrho,Z\rangle,\,\,\,\,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\partial M),

where
\varrho
is the outward unit normal of
\partial M
in
(M,g)
. The mean curvature
H^{\partial M}
of
\partial M
in
(M,g)
is given by

\displaystyle H^{\partial M}=\tr\II^{\partial M}=\divergente_{\partial M}\varrho.

We define
(\iota_{\varrho}\pi)^{\top}
as the restriction of
\iota_{\varrho}\pi=\pi(\varrho,\cdot)
to tangent vector fields of
\partial M
and say that
(M,g,K)
satisfies the
boundary DEC
if

\displaystyle H^{\partial M}\geq|(\iota_{\varrho}\pi)^{\top}|\,\,\,\,\mbox{along}\,\,\,\,\partial M.

The above boundary DEC was introduced by S. Almaraz, L. L. Lima, and L. Mari
[
2
]
in the context of proving spacetime positive mass inequalities for asymptotically flat and asymptotically hyperbolic initial data sets with noncompact boundary.

In order to avoid ambiguity, we call (
1
) the
interior DEC
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected two-sided hypersurface in
(M,g)
with unit normal
N
and

\displaystyle H=\divergente_{\Sigma}N

be its associated mean curvature. The
null mean curvatures

\theta^{+}
and
\theta^{-}
of
\Sigma
in
(M,g,K)
are defined by

\displaystyle\theta^{+}=\tr_{\Sigma}K+H\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\theta^{-}=\tr_{\Sigma}K-H.

Then the hypersurface
\Sigma
is said to be
outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}<0
,
weakly outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}\leq 0
, and
marginally outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}=0
. In the latter case, we refer to
\Sigma
as a
marginally outer trapped surface
(MOTS).

The
null second fundamental forms

\chi^{+}
and
\chi^{-}
of
\Sigma
in
(M,g,K)
are defined by

\displaystyle\chi^{+}=K|_{\Sigma}+A\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\chi^{-}=K|_{\Sigma}-A,

where
A
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
in
(M,g)
. Our sign convention is such that
H=\tr A
and, in particular,
\theta^{\pm}=\tr\chi^{\pm}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Initial data sets arise naturally in general relativity. In fact, let
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
be a spacetime, i.e. a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold. Consider a spacelike hypersurface
M
in
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
and let
g
be the induced Riemannian metric on
M
and
K
be the second fundamental form of
M
with respect to the future-pointing timelike unit normal
u
of
M
in
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
. Then
(M,g,K)
is an initial data set. As before, let
\Sigma
be a two-sided hypersurface in
(M,g)
with unit normal
N
. In this setting,
\chi^{+}
and
\chi^{-}
are the second fundamental forms of
\Sigma
in
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
with respect to the
null normal fields

\displaystyle\ell^{+}=u|_{\Sigma}+N\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\ell^{-}=u|_{\Sigma}-N,

respectively. Therefore
\theta^{+}=\tr\chi^{+}=\divergente_{\Sigma}\ell^{+}
is the
future outgoing null expansion scalar
and
\theta^{-}=\tr\chi^{-}=\divergente_{\Sigma}\ell^{-}
is the
future ingoing null expansion scalar
of
\Sigma
in
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
.

Let
(\Sigma_{t})_{|t|<\epsilon}
be a variation of
\Sigma
in
M
,
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
, with variation vector field

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0}=\phi N\,\,\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,\,\,\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma).

We may view the null mean curvature
\theta^{+}
of
\Sigma_{t}
in
(M,g,K)
as a parameter-dependent function on
\Sigma
. Computations as in
[
4
]
show that

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\theta^{+}(t,\cdot)=L\phi+\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\theta^{+})^{2}+\theta^{+}\tau\right)\phi,

where

\displaystyle L\phi\triangleq-\Delta\phi+2\langle X,\nabla\phi\rangle+(Q-|X|^{2}+\divergente X)\phi

and

\displaystyle Q\triangleq\frac{1}{2}R^{\Sigma}-(\mu+J(N))-\frac{1}{2}|\chi^{+}|^{2}.

Here,
\Delta
is the nonpositive Laplace-Beltrami operator,
\nabla
the gradient,
\divergente
the divergence, and
R^{\Sigma}
the scalar curvature of
\Sigma
with respect to the induced metric. Moreover,
X
is the tangent vector field of
\Sigma
that is dual to the
1
-form
K(N,\cdot)|_{\Sigma}
, and
\tau=\tr K
.

When
\Sigma
is a MOTS,
L
is called the
MOTS stability operator
of
\Sigma
. This is because in the Riemannian case (
K=0
), saying that
\Sigma
is a MOTS is equivalent to saying that
\Sigma
is a minimal hypersurface and, in this case,
L
reduces to the classical stability operator of minimal surfaces theory.

It is possible to show that, when
\Sigma
is a closed MOTS, the operator
L
admits a real eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(L)
, called its
principal eigenvalue
, such that
\lambda_{1}\leq\real\lambda
for any other eigenvalue
\lambda
. Moreover, the associated eigenfunction
\phi_{1}
,
L\phi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}
, is unique up to a multiplicative constant, and
\phi_{1}
can be chosen to be positive. Also,
\lambda_{1}
is nonnegative if, and only if, there exists a positive function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that
L\phi\geq 0
(see
[
5
]
). In analogy with the minimal surface case, a closed MOTS
\Sigma
is said to be
stable
provided
\lambda_{1}(L)\geq 0
.

Now, we are going to present a notion of
stability
for
capillary
MOTS in initial data sets with boundary introduced by A. Alaee, M. Lesourd, and S.-T. Yau
[
1
]
. We restrict our attention to the
free boundary
case.

Assume that
M
is a manifold with boundary and
\Sigma
is a compact-with-boundary
properly embedded
hypersurface in
M
, which means that
\Sigma
is embedded in
M
and
\partial\Sigma=\Sigma\cap\partial M
. We say that
\Sigma
is
free boundary
in
(M,g)
if
\Sigma
meets
\partial M
orthogonally, that is,
\varrho=\nu
along
\partial\Sigma
, where
\nu
is the outward unit normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
with respect to the induced metric.

Remark 2.2
.

The mean curvature
H^{\partial\Sigma}
of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
with respect to the induced metric is given by

\displaystyle H^{\partial\Sigma}=\divergente_{\partial\Sigma}\nu.

In particular, if
\Sigma
is free boundary (equivalently,
N
is tangent to
\partial M
along
\partial\Sigma
), then

\displaystyle H^{\partial M}=\divergente_{\partial M}\varrho=\divergente_{\partial\Sigma}\nu+\langle\nabla_{N}\varrho,N\rangle=H^{\partial\Sigma}+\II^{\partial M}(N,N).

Let
(\Sigma_{t})_{|t|<\epsilon}
be a variation of
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
in
(M,g,K)
by compact-with-boundary properly embedded hypersurfaces and consider the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\Sigma_{t}]=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\theta^{+}(t)\langle\mathcal{V},N_{t}\rangle dv_{t}+\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\langle\mathcal{V},\nu_{t}\rangle ds_{t},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

If
\Sigma
is a free boundary MOTS in
(M,g,K)
, then

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\mathcal{F}[\Sigma_{t}]=\int_{\Sigma}\phi L\phi\hskip 0.28436ptdv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}-\II^{\partial M}(N,N)\phi\right)ds,

where
\phi=\langle\mathcal{V},N\rangle
(see
[
1
, Section 5]
). The corresponding eigenvalue problem to functional (
3
) is the following:

\displaystyle L\phi

\displaystyle=-\Delta\phi+2\langle X,\nabla\phi\rangle+(Q-|X|^{2}+\divergente X)\phi=\lambda\phi\,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\,\Sigma,

\displaystyle B\phi

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}-\II^{\partial M}(N,N)\phi=0\,\,\,\,\mbox{along}\,\,\,\,\partial\Sigma.

Then a compact free boundary MOTS
\Sigma
in
(M,g,K)
is said to be
stable
provided there exists a nonnegative function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,
\phi\not\equiv 0
, satisfying the Robin boundary condition
B\phi=0
such that
L\phi\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, by the maximum principle for nonpositive functions, we may assume that
\phi>0
.

Before finishing this section, let us present some important definitions.

Suppose
\Sigma
is a separating MOTS in
(M,g,K)
. By definition, the unit normal
N
is said to be
outward-pointing
. We say that
\Sigma
is
weakly outermost
if there is no outer trapped (
\theta^{+}<0
) surface outside of, and homologous to,
\Sigma
. Analogously, we say that
\Sigma
is
outermost
if there is no weakly outer trapped (
\theta^{+}\leq 0
) surface outside of, and homologous to,
\Sigma
.

3.
Auxiliary Results

The first auxiliary result we are going to prove is the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, initial data set with boundary and
\Sigma
be a compact free boundary MOTS in
(M,g,K)
. If
\Sigma
is stable, then the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})
of
\mathcal{L}_{0}\triangleq-\Delta+Q
on
\Sigma
with Robin boundary condition
B_{0}u\triangleq Bu+\langle X,\nu\rangle u=0
is nonnegative.

The proof of Lemma
3.1
is essentially contained in the proof of Proposition 5.4 in
[
1
]
. But, for the sake of completeness, we include it here. The inspiring closed case was first proved by Galloway and Schoen
[
13
]
(see also
[
5
, Section 4]
and
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a positive function satisfying the boundary condition
B\phi=0
such that
L\phi\geq 0
. Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{L\phi}{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}+2\langle X,\nabla\ln\phi\rangle+Q-|X|^{2}+\divergente X

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}+|\nabla\ln\phi|^{2}-|X-\nabla\ln\phi|^{2}+Q+\divergente X

\displaystyle=\divergente Y-|Y|^{2}+Q,

where
Y=X-\nabla\ln\phi
. Therefore

\displaystyle u^{2}\frac{L\phi}{\phi}

\displaystyle=\divergente(u^{2}Y)-2u\langle Y,\nabla u\rangle-|uY|^{2}+Qu^{2}

\displaystyle=\divergente(u^{2}Y)-|uY+\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\divergente(u^{2}Y)+|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2},

for any function
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.

Then, taking
u
as a first eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
\mathcal{L}_{0}u=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})u
, with Robin boundary condition
B_{0}u=0
and using that
L\phi\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2}+\divergente(u^{2}Y)\right)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2}\right)dv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}u^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\phi}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2}\right)dv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}u^{2}\left(\II^{\partial M}(N,N)-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Qu^{2}\right)dv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}u\mathcal{L}_{0}u\hskip 0.28436ptdv=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}dv,

where above we have used that
B\phi=0
and
B_{0}u=0
. Thus
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0})\geq 0
.
∎

Our second auxiliary result is the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\Sigma,\gamma)
be an
n
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, compact-with-boundary, connected, orientable, Riemannian manifold. Suppose there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,
u>0
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u\triangleq-\Delta u+\left(\frac{1}{2}R^{\Sigma}-P\right)u\geq 0\,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\,\Sigma,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}u\geq 0\,\,\,\,\mbox{along}\,\,\,\,\partial\Sigma,

where
R^{\Sigma}
is the scalar curvature of
(\Sigma,\gamma)
,
H^{\partial\Sigma}
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma
in
(\Sigma,\gamma)
, and
P
is a nonnegative function on
\Sigma
. Then either:

(1)

\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary or

(2)

(\Sigma,\gamma)
is Ricci flat with totally geodesic boundary,
P=0
, and
u
is constant.

Proof.

Suppose
\Sigma
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary. Consider the metric
\hat{\gamma}=u^{2/(n-1)}\gamma
. The scalar curvature
R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}
of
(\Sigma,\hat{\gamma})
and the mean curvature
H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}
of
\partial\Sigma
in
(\Sigma,\hat{\gamma})
are given by

(4)

\displaystyle R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=u^{-\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\left(-2\Delta u+R^{\Sigma}u+\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right)=u^{-\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\left(2(\mathcal{L}u+Pu)+\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle=u^{-\frac{n}{n-1}}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}u\right).

In particular,
R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}
and
H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}
are nonnegative.

If
n=2
, by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem,
\chi(\Sigma)\geq 0
. On the other hand, in dimension
n=2
, saying that
\Sigma
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature (positive Gaussian curvature) with minimal boundary (geodesic boundary) is equivalent to saying that
\chi(\Sigma)\leq 0
(see
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
). Therefore
\chi(\Sigma)=0
and, by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem,
R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}
and
H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}
vanish, since they are nonnegative. From (
4
) and (
5
), we obtain that
u
is constant and the functions
P
,
R^{\Sigma}
, and
H^{\partial\Sigma}
vanish. This finishes the proof in case
n=2
.

Assume that
n\geq 3
.

Claim 1.

R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}
and
H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}
vanish. If not, consider the eigenvalue problem

(6)

\displaystyle-c_{n}\Delta_{\hat{\gamma}}\psi+R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}\psi

\displaystyle=\lambda\psi\,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\,\Sigma,

\displaystyle c_{n}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}}+2H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}\psi

\displaystyle=0\,\,\,\,\mbox{along}\,\,\,\,\partial\Sigma,

where
c_{n}=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}
, and let
\psi_{1}
be an eigenfunction associated with the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
for this problem. We may assume that
\psi_{1}>0
. Multiplying (
6
) by
\psi=\psi_{1}
and integrating over
(\Sigma,\hat{\gamma})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(c_{n}|\nabla\psi_{1}|_{\hat{\gamma}}^{2}+R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}\psi_{1}^{2}\right)dv_{\hat{\gamma}}+2\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}\psi_{1}^{2}ds_{\hat{\gamma}}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\psi_{1}^{2}dv_{\hat{\gamma}}}.

Since
R_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Sigma}
and
H_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\left(\langle X,\nu_{t}\rangle-\II^{\partial M}(N_{t},N_{t})\right)ds_{t},

where above we have used item (

that

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|X-\nabla\ln\varphi|^{2}dv_{t}=0,

i.e.

\displaystyle X=\nabla\ln\varphi\,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\,\Sigma_{t}.

Now, observing that

, since

is

-convex, we obtain

\displaystyle H(t)\leq\tr_{\Sigma_{t}}K+H(t)=\theta^{+}(t)=0,

in

. Then the first variation formula of volume (see

, for each

.

Remember that

\displaystyle g|_{U}=\varphi^{2}dt^{2}+\gamma_{t},

where

. We claim that each leaf

is totally geodesic in

(12)

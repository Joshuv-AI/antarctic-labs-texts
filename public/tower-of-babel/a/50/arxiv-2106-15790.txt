Formulas for

,

We now derive a very useful relation for all
k
-order Fibonacci sequences.

Lemma 2
.

Let
k\geq 2
.
For any generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}(k)\}_{n\geq 0}
of order
k
, we have

(i) If
n\geq k+1
,
F_{n}=2F_{n-1}-F_{n-k-1}

(ii) If
n\geq m\geq k+1
,

F_{n}=2^{n-m+1}F_{m-1}-\sum_{i=m-k-1}^{n-k-1}{2^{n-k-1-i}F_{i}}.

Proof.

(i) If
n\geq k+1
,
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+\dots+F_{n-k}
and
F_{n-1}=F_{n-2}+\dots+F_{n-k-1}
, so
F_{n}=2F_{n-1}-F_{n-k-1}
.

(ii) If
n\geq k+1
, by (i),

2^{-n}F_{n}-2^{-(n-1)}F_{n-1}=-2^{-n}F_{n-k-1}.

So for any
m\geq k+1
, taking the sum

\sum_{i=m}^{n}{(2^{-i}F_{i}-2^{-(i-1)}F_{i-1})}=-\sum_{i=m}^{n}{2^{-i}F_{i-k-1}}

we obtain

2^{-n}F_{n}-2^{-(m-1)}F_{m-1}=-\sum_{i=m}^{n}{2^{-i}F_{i-k-1}}=-\sum_{i=m-k-1}^{n-k-1}{2^{-i-k-1}F_{i}}

and so

F_{n}=2^{n-m+1}F_{m-1}-\sum_{i=m-k-1}^{n-k-1}{2^{n-k-1-i}F_{i}}.

∎

The following theorem gives an explicit formula for
S_{n}(k)

Theorem 2
.

Let

The

S_{n}(k)=1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\rfloor}{(-1)^{i}{{n-ik}\choose{i-1}}2^{n+1-i(k+1)}}.

(2)

Proof.

The desired formula can be proved by induction on

2
(i).
The formula is correct for

For

two separate cases need to be considered,
n+1\equiv 0\pmod{k+1}
and
n+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{k+1}
.

Case 1:
n+1\equiv 0\pmod{k+1}
.
Write
n+1=a(k+1)
.

By Lemma
2
(i) and the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle S_{n}=

\displaystyle 2S_{n-1}-S_{n-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2-2(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}{(-1)^{i}{{a-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{(a-i)(k+1)-1}}

\displaystyle-1+(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}{(-1)^{i}{{a-2+(a-1-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{(a-1-i)(k+1)}}

In the second summation, let
i:=i+1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{n}=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}{(-1)^{i}{{a-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{(a-i)(k+1)}}

\displaystyle-(k-1)\sum_{i=2}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-2}}2^{(a-i)(k+1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a-1+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{(a-i)(k+1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\rfloor}{(-1)^{i}{{n-ik}\choose{i-1}}2^{n+1-i(k+1)}}.

Case 2:
n+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{k+1}
.
Write
n+1=a(k+1)+r
where
1\leq r\leq k
.

By Lemma
2
(i) and the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle S_{n}=

\displaystyle 2S_{n-1}-S_{n-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2-2(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a+r-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{r+(a-i)(k+1)-1}}

\displaystyle-1+(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}{(-1)^{i}{{a+r-2+(a-1-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{r+(a-1-i)(k+1)}}

In the second summation, let
i:=i+1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{n}=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a+r-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{r+(a-i)(k+1)}}

\displaystyle-(k-1)\sum_{i=2}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a+r-2+(a-i)k}\choose{i-2}}2^{r+(a-i)(k+1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{a}{(-1)^{i}{{a+r-1+(a-i)k}\choose{i-1}}2^{r+(a-i)(k+1)}}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\rfloor}{(-1)^{i}{{n-ik}\choose{i-1}}2^{n+1-i(k+1)}}.

∎

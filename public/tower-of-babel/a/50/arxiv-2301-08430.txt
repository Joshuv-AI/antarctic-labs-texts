Heat kernel on Ricci shrinkers (II)
Yu Li, and, Bing Wang

Abstract
This paper is the sequel to our study of heat kernels on Ricci shrinkers in [ 28 ] . In this paper, we improve many estimates in [ 28 ] and extend the recent progress of Bamler [ 2 ] . In particular, we drop the compactness and curvature boundedness assumptions and show that the theory of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -convergence holds naturally on any Ricci flows induced by Ricci shrinkers.



1
Introduction

A Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
is a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
coupled with a smooth function
f
satisfying

\displaystyle Rc+\text{Hess}\,f=\frac{1}{2}g,

(1.1)

where the potential function
f
is normalized so that

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=f.

(1.2)

The study of shrinkers is an essential component of analyzing the singularity formation of solutions to the Ricci flow. For a Ricci flow with type-I curvature bound, it is proved by Enders-Müller-Topping
[
19
]
that any proper blow-up sequence converges smoothly to a nontrivial Ricci shrinker. For general compact Ricci flows, it is proved by Bamler
[
4
]
that the finite-time singularities are modeled on Ricci shrinkers containing a singular set by using the theory of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence developed in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In dimension
2
or
3
, all Ricci shrinkers are completely classified (cf.
[
21
]
[
31
]
[
33
]
[
8
]
, etc). We know that
\mathbb{R}^{2},S^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3},S^{3},S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and their quotients form the complete list. In particular, all low-dimensional Ricci shrinkers have bounded and nonnegative sectional curvature.

In higher dimensions, the complete classification of Ricci shrinkers seems out of reach. Subject to an additional curvature positivity assumption, some partial classifications are also known (cf.
[
31
]
[
29
]
[
26
]
[
25
]
[
32
]
). In general, it is still unclear if there exists any Ricci shrinker with unbounded sectional curvature.

On the one hand, Ricci shrinkers can be regarded as critical metrics which generalize the classical positive Einstein manifolds. On the other hand, for any Ricci shrinker, there exists an associated self-similar solution to the Ricci flow (cf. Section 2). As a special class of Ricci flows, Ricci shrinkers have many known important properties of compact Ricci flows. In
[
28
]
, many fundamental analytic tools, including the maximum principle, optimal log-Sobolev constant estimate, the no-local-collapsing theorems, etc., are established for Ricci flows associated with Ricci shrinkers. Many heat kernel estimates include the differential Harnack inequality and the pseudolocality theorem are also known in
[
28
]
.

In this paper, we continue to focus on Ricci flows associated with Ricci shrinkers without any curvature assumption. Based on the techniques and results in
[
28
]
and
[
2
]
, we further obtain results, including a Gaussian bound on the heat kernel, no-local-collapsing and non-expanding estimates, an
\epsilon
-regularity theorem, etc. All those results are stronger than their counterparts in
[
28
]
.
It is important to notice that we have no assumption of curvature at all. If we assume bounded curvature on non-compact manifold, then many results are already known (cf.
[
5
]

[
10
]
).

The pointed Nash entropy (cf. Definition
3.18
) plays an important role in
[
2
]
, which first appears in
[
34
, Section 5]
and is systematically studied in
[
23
]
.
In
[
28
]
, we use Perelmam’s entropy
\boldsymbol{\mu}
(see (
2.1
)) to characterize the optimal log-Sobolev constant and the local non-collapsing. The pointed Nash entropy, which is always bounded below by
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, has the advantage of being local in the spacetime of Ricci flows. In
[
23
]
, it is proved that the Nash entropy is Lipschitz. Moreover, the oscillation of the Nash entropy in the spacetime is established in
[
2
]
. We generalize the Nash entropy and its fundamental estimates to the Ricci flows associated with Ricci shrinkers; see Theorem

and Corollary

.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Then for any
s<t<1
, the Nash entopy
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x,t):=\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(t-s)
is smooth and satisfies the following estimates on
M\times(s,1)
.

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(t-s)}}\quad\text{and}\quad-\frac{n}{2(t-s)}\leq\square\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}\leq 0.

(1.3)

The proof of (
1.3
) is based on an integral estimate of the heat kernel (cf. Theorem
3.16
), which was initially obtained in
[
2
]
for compact Ricci flows. A key application of Theorem
1.1
is to estimate the local oscillation of the Nash entropy (cf. Corollary
3.25
). Using the Nash entropy properties and the heat kernel estimates, we obtain the improved no-local-collapsing and non-expanding result (cf. Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.7
).

Theorem 1.2

(
No-local-collapsing and non-expanding
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. For any
x\in M
and
t<1
,

\displaystyle|B_{t}(x,r)|_{t}\leq C(n)\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\right)r^{n}

and if
R\leq r^{-2}
on
B_{t}(x,r)
, then

\displaystyle|B_{t}(x,r)|_{t}\geq c(n)\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\right)r^{n}.

Note that
\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\leq 0
(cf. Corollary
3.22
), it is clear that Theorem
1.2
provides a uniform volume ratio upper bound, independent of base point and radius.
This clearly improves the known volume upper bounds (cf.
[

]
,
[
22
]
,
[
24
]
).
On the other hand, as
\boldsymbol{\mu}\leq\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})
, the non-collapsing estimate in Theorem

also improves the one in
[

]

An important concept introduced in

-Wasserstein distance between them can be roughly measured by the distance between two

-centers. We prove the existence of an
H_{n}
-center, where
H_{n}=(n-1)\pi^{2}/2+4
, for any conjugate heat kernel, by generalizing the monotonicity of the variance obtained in
[

]
to our setting (cf. Proposition

, Theorem
4.15
, Theorem
4.16

Theorem 1.3

(
Heat kernel estimates
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker satisfying
\boldsymbol{\mu}\geq-A
. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

There exists a constant
C=C(n,A,\delta)>1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{C^{-1}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(x,y)}{C^{-1}(t-s)}\right)\leq H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(x,y)}{C(t-s)}\right)

(1.4)

for any
-\delta^{-1}\leq s<t\leq 1-\delta
and
d_{t}(p,x)\leq\delta^{-1}
.

(ii)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\epsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{C\exp\left(-\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(t-s)\right)}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(z,y)}{(4+\epsilon)(t-s)}\right),

(1.5)

for any
s<t<1
and any
H_{n}
-center
(z,s)
of
(x,t)
.

Here, the point
p
is a minimum point of
f
, regarded as the Ricci shrinker’s base point. The Gaussian estimate (
1.5
) is previously proved in
[
2
]
for compact Ricci flows, with
4+\epsilon
replaced by
8+\epsilon
. Our proof uses an iteration argument by showing that if (
1.5
) fails, one can find a new spacetime point
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with an
H_{n}
-center
(z^{\prime},s)
such that
H(x^{\prime},t^{\prime},y,s)
has a worse bound than (
1.5
). Eventually, we will arrive at a contradiction if
t^{\prime}
is sufficiently close to
s
. The proof in our case is more involved since we do not have a global heat kernel bound as (
1.5
) when
t
is close to
s
, which is always available for compact Ricci flows. Therefore, in the iteration process, we must carefully choose the sequence of spacetime points, so they all fall into a compact set. Then the contradiction comes from the local heat kernel estimate (cf. Corollary
4.12
) since locally the scalar curvature is bounded.

Once we have the estimate (
1.5
), the upper bound in (
1.4
) follows since the distance between
(x,s)
and
(z,s)
can be well-controlled. Moreover, the lower bound in (
1.4
) is already contained in
[
28
]
in a different guise. We also obtain the gradient estimate of the heat kernel; see Theorem
4.6
.

W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance between two conjugate heat kernels (cf. Proposition

-parabolic neighborhoods in the spacetime of the Ricci flow, as pointed out in
[

(cf. Definition

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker satisfying
\boldsymbol{\mu}\geq-A
. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

Given
\delta\in(0,1)
,
t_{0}\in(-\infty,1)
,
T^{\pm}\geq 0
and
S\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,A,\delta)>1
such that

\displaystyle P^{*}(p,t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset Q(p,t_{0};\sqrt{2}S+C,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset P^{*}(p,t_{0};\sqrt{2}S+2C,-T^{-},T^{+})

provided that
t_{0}-T^{-}\geq-\delta^{-1}
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
\rho=\rho(n,A)\in(0,1)
satisfying the following property.
Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
r>0
, suppose that
R\leq r^{-2}
on
P(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho r)^{2},(\rho r)^{2})
. Then

\displaystyle P(x_{0},t_{0};\rho r)

\displaystyle\subset P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho r)^{2},(\rho r)^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};\rho r)\subset P(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho r)^{2},(\rho r)^{2}).

The proof of Theorem
1.4
involves the distance distortion estimates globally with respect to
p
and locally under the scalar curvature control. Moreover, one needs to locate the
H_{n}
-center of
(p,t_{0})
or
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Notice that, if

Theorem 1.5

(
\epsilon
-regularity theorem
)
.

There exists a small constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>0
satisfying the following property.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Given
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
r>0
, suppose that
\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(r^{2})\geq-\epsilon
, then
r_{\emph{Rm}}(x,t)\geq\epsilon r
.

Based on the results and techniques generalized (or slightly improved) from
[
2
]
, we can generalize the theory about metric flows and
\mathbb{F}
-convergence in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
from compact Ricci flows to the setting of Ricci flows associated with or induced by Ricci shrinkers (cf. Definition

Theorem 1.6

(
Two-sided pseudolocality theorem
)
.

For any
\alpha>0
, there is an
\epsilon(n,\alpha)>0
such that the following holds.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be a Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
r>0
, if

|B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r)|\geq\alpha r^{n},\qquad|Rm|\leq(\alpha r)^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r),

then

|Rm|\leq(\epsilon r)^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad P(x_{0},t_{0};(1-\alpha)r,-(\epsilon r)^{2},(\epsilon r)^{2}).

Another application of the
\mathbb{F}
-converge is the following integral estimate of curvature, which originates from the estimate of Cheeger-Naber
[
12
]
.
For more details, see Theorem

and Corollary

.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a Ricci shrinker in
\mathcal{M}(A)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{d(p,\cdot)\leq r}|Rm|^{2-\epsilon}\,dV\leq

\displaystyle\int_{d(p,\cdot)\leq r}r_{\emph{Rm}}^{-4+2\epsilon}\,dV\leq Cr^{n+2\epsilon-2},

\displaystyle\int_{d(p,\cdot)\geq 1}\frac{|Rm|^{2-\epsilon}}{d^{n+2\epsilon-2}(p,\cdot)}\,dV\leq

\displaystyle\int_{d(p,\cdot)\geq 1}\frac{r_{\emph{Rm}}^{-4+2\epsilon}}{d^{n+2\epsilon-2}(p,\cdot)}\,dV\leq C

for any
\epsilon>0
and
r\geq 1
, where
r_{\emph{Rm}}(\cdot)=r_{\emph{Rm}}(\cdot,0)
and
C=C(n,A,\epsilon)
.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 discusses some properties of Ricci flows associated with Ricci shrinkers, including the existence of cutoff functions and maximum principles. In Section 3, we prove some estimates and properties regarding the variance,
H

\epsilon

Preliminaries

For any Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
, the scalar curvature
R\geq 0
from

]
and

With the normalization (
1.2
), the entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\coloneqq\log\int\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\,dV.

(2.1)

Notice that
e^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is uniformly comparable to the volume of the unit ball
B(p,1)
(cf.
[
24
, Lemma
2.5
]
). It was proved in
[
28
, Theorem
1
]
that
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is the optimal log-Sobolev constant for all scales. Following
[
27
]
, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(A)
be the family of Ricci shrinkers
(M^{n},g,f)
satisfying

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\geq-A.

(2.2)

Recall that any Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
can be considered a self-similar solution to the Ricci flow. Let
{\psi^{t}}:M\to M
be a family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\dfrac{1}{1-t}\nabla f
and
\psi^{0}=\text{id}
.
In other words, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\psi^{t}}(x)=\frac{1}{1-t}\nabla f\left({\psi^{t}}(x)\right).

(2.3)

It is well known that the rescaled pull-back metric
g(t)\coloneqq(1-t)(\psi^{t})^{*}g
satisfies the Ricci flow equation for any
-\infty<t<1
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g=-2Rc_{g(t)}\quad\text{and}\quad g(0)=g.

(2.4)

Sometimes we encounter Ricci flow obtained from the above Ricci flow through time-shifting and rescaling.
We emphasize whether there exist extra time-shifting and rescaling by the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

For any Ricci shrinker, the Ricci flow defined in (
2.4
) is called the
associated Ricci flow
.
Any Ricci flow obtained from the associated Ricci flow via time-shifting and rescaling is called the
Ricci flow induced by a Ricci shrinker
.

Clearly, a Ricci flow associated to a Ricci shrinker must be a Ricci flow induced by a Ricci shrinker, but the reverse is generally not true.
In this article, if not mentioned explicitly, the associated Ricci flow is the default one.

Next, we recall the function
F(x,t):=\bar{\tau}f(x,t)
, where
\bar{\tau}:=1-t
and
f(x,t):=(\psi^{t})^{*}f
, satisfies the following identities (see
[
28
, Section 2]
for proofs):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=|\nabla f|^{2},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F=-\bar{\tau}R,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}R+\Delta F=\frac{n}{2},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}^{2}R+|\nabla F|^{2}=F,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\square F=-\frac{n}{2}.

(2.9)

Here, we define
\square:=\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t}
and have dropped the subscript
g(t)
or
t
if there is no confusion. Based on these identities, we have the following estimates of
F
.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 1 of
[
28
]
)
.

There exists a point
p\in M
where

attains its infimum and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(d_{t}(x,p)-5n\bar{\tau}-4\right)^{2}_{+}\leq F(x,t)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d_{t}(x,p)+\sqrt{2n\bar{\tau}}\right)^{2}

(2.10)

for all
x\in M
and

, where
a_{+}:=\max\{0,a\}
.

Thanks to (

grows like

and hence one can obtain a family of cutoff functions by composing

with a cutoff function on

. More precisely, we fix a function
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that

,

on

\displaystyle\phi^{r}\coloneqq\eta\left(\frac{F}{r}\right).

Then

\displaystyle(\phi^{r})^{-1}|\nabla\phi^{r}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-1},

(2.13)

\displaystyle|\Delta\phi^{r}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\bar{\tau}^{-1}+r^{-1}),

\displaystyle|\square\phi^{r}|

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-1},

Theorem 2.5

(Maximum principle on Ricci shrinkers I)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Given any closed interval
[a,b]\subset(-\infty,1)
and a function
u

\square u\leq 0

M\times[a,b]

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\int_{M}u^{2}_{+}(x,t)e^{-2f(x,t)}\,dV_{t}(x)\,dt<\infty.

(2.16)

If
u(\cdot,a)\leq c
, then
u(\cdot,b)\leq c
.

We also need the following version of the maximum principle, which is proved in

Theorem 2.6

(Maximum principle on Ricci shrinkers II)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Given any closed interval
[a,b]\subset(-\infty,1)
and a function
u
which satisfies

Lu:=\square u-\left\langle\nabla u,X(t)\right\rangle\leq 0

on
M\times[a,b]
, suppose that
X(t)
is a bounded vector field on
M\times[a,b]
and

\displaystyle u(x,t)\leq Ke^{kf(x,t)}

(2.17)

on
M\times[a,b]
for some constants
K>0
and
k<1
. If
u(\cdot,a)\leq c
, then
u(\cdot,b)\leq c
.

Proof.

We first construct a barrier function

\displaystyle\phi(x,t):=Ke^{B(t-a)+(1-\epsilon)f(x,t)},

where
1-\epsilon>k
and
B
is a constant determined later.

Claim:
There exists a constant
B>0
such that

\displaystyle L\phi\geq\phi.

(2.18)

Proof of Claim
: By direct computations, we have

\displaystyle L\phi

\displaystyle=\phi\left(B+(1-\epsilon)f_{t}-(1-\epsilon)^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}-(1-\epsilon)\Delta f-(1-\epsilon)\langle\nabla f,X\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(B+\epsilon(1-\epsilon)|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{n(1-\epsilon)}{2\bar{\tau}}+(1-\epsilon)R-(1-\epsilon)\langle\nabla f,X\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\geq\phi\left(B+\epsilon(1-\epsilon)|\nabla f|^{2}-C_{1}|\nabla f|-\frac{n(1-\epsilon)}{2(1-b)}\right),

where we have used (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and the assumption that
|X|\leq C_{1}
. Therefore, (
2.18
) holds if we choose

B=\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{4\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}+\frac{n(1-\epsilon)}{2(1-b)}+1.

Now, we assume
c=0
by considering
u-c
instead of
u
. To complete the proof, we only need to verify that for any
\delta>0
,
u\leq\delta\phi
on
M\times[a,b]
. Otherwise, then there exists
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in M\times[a,b]
such that
\left(u-\delta\phi\right)(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})>0
. Due to the estimate (
2.17
) and our definition of
\phi
, we know that
\left(u-\delta\phi\right)(x,t)\longrightarrow-\infty
as
d_{t}(x,p)\longrightarrow+\infty
uniformly in

, i.e.,
u-\delta\phi<0
for
d_{t}(x,p)
large enough independent of
t
. Moreover,
\left(u-\delta\phi\right)(x,a)<0
for all
x\in M
. Consequently, there exists
(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\in M\times(a,t^{\prime})
such that
\left(u-\delta\phi\right)(x,t)\leq 0
for all
(x,t)\in M\times[a,t^{\prime\prime}]
and
\left(u-\delta\phi\right)(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})=0
. At
(x^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})
, we compute

\displaystyle 0\leq L\left(u-\delta\phi\right)\leq-\delta\phi<0,

which is a contradiction. In sum, our proof is complete.
∎

Next, we prove

Proposition 3.3
.

where we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the last inequality. From Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M\times M}\int_{M}H(x,t,z,c)d_{c}^{4}(z,p)e^{-2f(x,t)-2f(y,t)}\,dV_{c}(z)dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}\int_{M}H(x,t,z,c)d_{c}^{4}(z,p)e^{-f(x,t)-2f(y,t)}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{c}(z)dV_{t}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}\left(\frac{1-t}{1-c}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}d_{c}^{4}(z,p)e^{-f(z,c)-2f(y,t)}\,dV_{c}(z)dV_{t}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}d_{c}^{4}(z,p)e^{-f(z,c)-2f(y,t)}\,dV_{c}(z)dV_{t}(y)\,dt<\infty

by Lemma

. Similarly, the second term in (

) is also bounded. Therefore, we have proved that

for any

.

By our assumption,

and

\displaystyle w_{i}(x,t)\leq C\bar{w}(x,t)

(3.23)

for some constant

.

Next, we set

,
w_{2}=w_{2}(y,t)
,

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}\int_{M}uw_{1}w_{2}\phi^{r}_{x}\phi^{r}_{y}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\int_{M}(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}-\Delta_{y})(u\phi^{r}_{x}\phi^{r}_{y})w_{1}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\int_{M}\left(-H_{n}\phi^{r}_{x}\phi^{r}_{y}-u(\Delta_{x}\phi^{r}_{x}\phi^{r}_{y}+\Delta_{y}\phi^{r}_{y}\phi^{r}_{x})\right)w_{1}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)

\displaystyle+2\int_{M}\int_{M}(\Delta_{x}\phi^{r}_{x}+\langle\nabla\phi^{r}_{x},\nabla w_{1}\rangle)u\phi^{r}_{y}w_{2}+(\Delta_{y}\phi^{r}_{y}+\langle\nabla\phi^{r}_{y},\nabla w_{2}\rangle)u\phi^{r}_{y}w_{1}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y).

(3.24)

From (
3.23
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi^{r}_{x}||\nabla w_{1}||u|\phi^{r}_{y}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi^{r}_{x}|^{2}u^{2}(\phi^{r}_{y})^{2}w_{1}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)dt\right)\left(\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla w_{1}|^{2}}{w_{1}}\,dV_{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi^{r}_{x}|^{2}u^{2}w_{1}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)dt.

(3.25)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi^{r}_{y}||\nabla w_{2}||u|\phi^{r}_{x}w_{1}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle C\int_{c}^{b}\int_{M}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi^{r}_{y}|^{2}u^{2}w_{1}w_{2}\,dV_{t}(x)dV_{t}(y)dt.

Combining (

), (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and the fact that

Now, we assume

for

Therefore, one can use the same arguments as above, thanks to (

.

In order to obtain the estimates on the Nash entropy, we first generalize the improved gradient estimate
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
to our setting. We define the following antiderivative of the

-dimensional heat kernel:

\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^{2}/4}\,dt.

(3.31)

Notice that
\Phi_{t}(x):=\Phi(t^{-1/2}x)
is a solution to the
1
-dimensional heat equation with initial condition
\chi_{[0,\infty)}
.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Given
[a,b]\subset(-\infty,1)
and a solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[a,b])
to the heat equation
\square u=0
and a constant
T\geq 0
, suppose that
u
only takes values in
(0,1)
and
|\nabla(\Phi_{T}^{-1}(u(\cdot,a)))|\leq 1
if
T>0
. Then
|\nabla(\Phi^{-1}_{T+t-a}(u(\cdot,t)))|\leq 1
for all
t\in[a,b]
.

Proof.

We may assume that
u
takes values in
(\epsilon,1-\epsilon)
. Indeed, we can consider
(1-2\epsilon)u+\epsilon
instead and let
\epsilon\searrow 0
. With the extra assumption, it follows from
[
28
, Lemma 18]
that

\displaystyle|\nabla u|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{t-a}}

(3.32)

on
M\times(a,b]
. It is clear from the definition of
\Phi_{t}
that
\sup_{M}|\nabla(\Phi_{T}^{-1}(u(\cdot,a+\epsilon)))|\to 0
if
T\searrow 0
. Therefore, we only need to prove the case for
T>0
and then let
T\searrow 0
and
\epsilon\searrow 0
.

Now, we set
u(x,t)=\Phi_{T+t-a}\circ h(x,t)
. It follows from the definition of
\Phi_{t}
that

\displaystyle|h|\leq C_{2}

(3.33)

on
M\times[a,b]
. Moreover, since
|\nabla h(\cdot,a)|\leq 1
, it follows from (
3.33
) and Lemma
3.2
(i) that

\displaystyle|\nabla h|\leq C_{3}

(3.34)

on
M\times[a,b]
. By direct computation, see
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
for details, we have

\displaystyle\square|\nabla h|^{2}=-2|\text{Hess }h|^{2}-\frac{1}{T+t-a}\langle\nabla h^{2},\nabla|\nabla h|^{2}\rangle+\frac{1}{2(T+t-a)}(1-|\nabla h|^{2})|\nabla h|^{2}.

(3.35)

Therefore, if we set
v=(|\nabla h|^{2}-1)_{+}
, then it follows from (
3.35
) that

\displaystyle\square v+\frac{1}{T+t-a}\langle\nabla h^{2},\nabla v\rangle\leq 0.

Since
|\nabla h^{2}|
and
v
are uniformly bounded on
M\times[a,b]
by (
3.33
) and (
3.34
), it follows from Theorem
2.6
that
v\leq 0
on
M\times[a,b]
. In other words,
|\nabla h|\leq 1
on
M\times[a,b]
.
Thus the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}

. Then for any
x\in M
,

(t-s)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{X}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla_{x}H(x,t,\cdot,s)\right|}{H(x,t,\cdot,s)}\right)^{p}\,dv\leq C(n,p)v\left(X\right)\left(-\log\left(\frac{v\left(X\right)}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
dv=H(x,t,\cdot,s)\,dV_{s}
is the conjugate heat kernel measure. Moreover, for any
x\in M
and
w\in T_{x}M
with
|w|_{t}=1
, there holds that

(t-s)\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial_{w}H(x,t,\cdot,s)}{H(x,t,\cdot,s)}\right)^{2}\,dv\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(3.36)

In particular, we have

(t-s)\int_{M}\left|\frac{\nabla_{x}H(x,t,\cdot,s)}{H(x,t,\cdot,s)}\right|^{2}\,dv\leq\frac{n}{2}.

(3.37)

Another application of Theorem
3.15
is the following

-Poincaré inequality; see
[
2
, Theorem 11.1]
.

Theorem 3.17

(
L^{p}
-Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be a Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Then for
p\geq 1
and any
[s,t]\subset(-\infty,1)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{s}\leq C(p)(t-s)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dv_{s},

for any
u\in W^{1,p}(M,dv_{s})
with
\int_{M}u\,dv_{s}=0
. Here,
dv_{s}(y)=H(x,t,y,s)dV_{s}(y)
. One may choose

and

.

Proof.

The proof for

follows verbatim from
[
2
, Theorem 11.1]
. Only the last statement for

needs to be proved. It follows from
[
28
, Theorem 13]
that the probability measure

satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality with the constant

. It is a standard fact that the log-Sobolev condition implies the Poincaré inequality; see
[
35
, Theorem 22.17]
.

Next, we recall the definitions of the Nash entropy and

-entropy based at
(x_{0},t_{0})

.

Given a Ricci flow
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}

\displaystyle dv=dv_{x_{0},t_{0};t}(x)=(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-b(x,t)}\,dV_{t}=H(x_{0},t_{0},x,t)\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)

(3.38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}b\,dv-\frac{n}{2}.

(3.39)

Now, we prove some basic properties of

The following properties hold with Definition

(a)

\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(0)=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{0})=-2\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\tau\int_{M}\left|Rc+\emph{Hess}b-\frac{g}{2\tau}\right|^{2}\,dvd\tau.

(3.40)

(b)

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(0)=0
and for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\,d\tau\geq\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{0}).

(3.41)

(c)

For any
0<\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{1})-\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}\right)\leq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{2})\leq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{1}).

Proof.

Given
(x_{0},t_{0})
and

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)

\tau

[

Therefore, there exists

\displaystyle\int_{d_{t}(p,x)\geq r}dv_{t}(x)\leq C_{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{2}}}

(3.44)

if

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}\leq b(x,t)\leq-3\boldsymbol{\mu}+\frac{d_{t_{0}}^{2}(x_{0},x)}{3\tau}+\frac{4\tau}{3(1-t_{0})^{2}}F(x,t_{0}).

Corollary 3.20
.

Under the same assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}(|\nabla b|^{2}+R)dv\leq\frac{n}{2\tau}.

(3.54)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(b-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\,dv\leq n.

Proof.

From the fact that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{M}(2\Delta b-|\nabla b|^{2})\phi^{r}+R\,dv\leq\frac{n}{2\tau},

(3.56)

\displaystyle\int_{M}(2\Delta b-|\nabla b|^{2})\phi^{r}\,dv=\int_{M}|\nabla b|^{2}\phi^{r}-2\langle\nabla b,\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\,dv.

(3.57)

In addition, we can estimate

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla b||\nabla\phi^{r}|\,dv\leq\int_{M}|\nabla b||\nabla\phi^{r}|\,dv\leq\int_{M}\epsilon|\nabla b|^{2}\phi^{r}+\epsilon^{-1}\frac{|\nabla\phi^{r}|^{2}}{\phi^{r}}\,dv

(3.58)

Therefore, it follows from (
2.12
), (
3.56
), (
3.57
) and (
3.58
) that

\displaystyle\int_{M}(1-\epsilon)|\nabla b|^{2}+R\,dv\leq\frac{n}{2\tau}.

By letting
\epsilon\searrow 0
, we obtain (
3.54
).

Now, it follows from the Poincaré inequality Theorem
3.17
and (
3.54
) that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(b-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\,dv\leq 2\tau\int|\nabla b|^{2}\,dv\leq n

and (
3.55
) is proved.
∎

Remark 3.21
.

From the proof of (
3.54
),
\mathcal{W}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)=\int_{M}\left(\tau(|\nabla b|^{2}+R)+b-n\right)\,dv,

which agrees with the original definition of Perelman
[
34
, Formula (3.1)]
.

Corollary 3.22
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)\in\mathcal{M}(A)
, then

\displaystyle 0\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\geq\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}\geq-A

(3.59)

for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
\tau>0
. In particular, given a Ricci shrinker, the Nash entropy is always uniformly bounded.

Proof.

For fixed
(x_{0},t_{0})
and
\tau>0
, it follows from
[
28
, Theorem 20]
that
b
increases quadratically. Therefore, it is easy to see the function
u
, defined by
u^{2}=(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-b}
, belongs to
W_{*}^{1,2}(M)
defined in
[
28
, (92)]
. From
[
28
, Theorem 1]
, we immediately conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}(g(t_{0}-\tau),\tau)\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}\geq-A.

∎

Following
[
2
]
, we use the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x,t):=\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(t-s).

Similar to
[
2
, Theorem 5.9]
, we have

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
be the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker. Then for any
s<t<1
, the following properties hold.

(i)

\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
\sqrt{\dfrac{n}{2(t-s)}}
.

(ii)

In the distribution sense, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2(t-s)}\leq\square\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}\leq 0.

(3.60)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
s=0
and consider
t\in(0,1)
. We first define the following modified Nash entropy:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{r}=\mathcal{N}^{r}(x,t):=\int b\phi^{r}\,dv-\frac{n}{2},

(3.61)

where, as before,
b=b_{(x,t)}(y,0)=-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi t)-\log H(x,t,y,0)
and
dv=H(x,t,y,0)\,dV_{0}(y)
.

Claim
:
\mathcal{N}^{r}
converges to
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}
in
C^{0}_{\text{loc}}
on
M\times(0,1)
, as
r\to\infty
.

Proof of Claim
: Given a spacetime compact set
K\subset M\times(0,1)
, all constants
C_{i}>1
below depends only
K
and the Ricci shrinker.

Similar to (
3.44
), there exists
C_{1}>1
such that

\displaystyle\int_{d_{0}(p,y)\geq r}dv(y)\leq C_{1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}}}

(3.62)

for any
r\geq C_{1}
. From the same argument leading to (
3.47
), we have

\displaystyle|b_{(x,t)}(y,0)|\leq C_{2}(1+d_{0}^{2}(p,y)).

(3.63)

Combining (
3.62
), (
3.63
) and the fact that
\text{supp}(\phi^{r})\cap M\times\{0\}\subset\{C_{3}r\leq d_{0}^{2}(p,\cdot)\leq C_{4}r\}
, it is easy to show as (
3.48
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{M}|b_{(x,t)}(y,0)|(1-\phi^{r}(y))\,dv(y)=0

(3.64)

uniformly for
(x,t)\in K
. From (
3.64
), the Claim is proved.

Next, for any vector
w\in T_{x}M
with
|w|_{t}=1
we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{w}\mathcal{N}^{r}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{(\partial_{w}b)H\phi^{r}+b(\partial_{w}H)\phi^{r}\right\}dV_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{-(\partial_{w}H)\phi^{r}+b(\partial_{w}H)\phi^{r}\right\}dV_{0}=:I+II,

(3.65)

where
H=H(x,t,y,0)
. Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{M}H\phi^{r}\,dV_{0}=\int_{M}H(x,t,y,0)\phi^{r}(y)\,dV_{0}(y)

is the heat solution starting from
\phi^{r}
. Therefore, it follows from (
3.7
) and (
2.12
) that

\displaystyle|I|\leq\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{M}H(x,t,y,0)\phi^{r}(y)\,dV_{0}(y)\right|\leq Cr^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.66)

Next, we estimate

\displaystyle II=\int_{M}b\frac{\partial_{w}H}{H}\phi^{r}\,dv=\int_{M}\left(b-\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}-\frac{n}{2}\right)\frac{\partial_{w}H}{H}\phi^{r}\,dv-\left(\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}+\frac{n}{2}\right)I.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|II|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}\left(b-\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial_{w}H}{H}\right)^{2}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+Cr^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2t}}+Cr^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(3.67)

where we have used (
3.36
), (
3.55
) and (
3.66
). Combining (
3.65
), (
3.66
) and (
3.67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{N}^{r}(x,t)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2t}}+Cr^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.68)

Since
\mathcal{N}^{r}
converges to
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}
locally uniformly by the Claim, we immediately conclude from (
3.68
) that
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}
is
\sqrt{\frac{n}{2t}}
-Lipschitz.

Next, by direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\square\mathcal{N}^{r}(x,t)=\int_{M}\left|\frac{\nabla_{x}H}{H}\right|^{2}\phi^{r}\,dv-\frac{n}{2t}\int_{M}\phi^{r}\,dv.

(3.69)

Combining (
3.37
), (
3.69
) and the Claim, it follows immediately that

-\frac{n}{2t}\leq\square\mathcal{N}_{0}^{*}\leq 0

in the distribution sense.
∎

Remark 3.24
.

Later, we will show that the conclusions in Theorem
3.23
hold in the classical sense once we know the decay of the conjugate heat kernel; see Corollary
4.19
.

As an application of Theorem

, we prove the following oscillation of the Nash entropy.

Corollary 3.25
.

For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{1},t_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{2},t_{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(t^{*}-s)}}d^{t^{*}}_{W_{1}}\left(v_{x_{1},t_{1};t^{*}},v_{x_{2},t_{2};t^{*}}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{t_{2}-s}{t^{*}-s}\right).

In particular, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{1},t_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{2},t_{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(t_{2}-s)}}d^{t_{2}}_{W_{1}}\left(v_{x_{1},t_{1};t_{2}},\delta_{x_{2}}\right).

If we further assume
(x_{2},t_{2})
is an
H_{n}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{1},t_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}(x_{2},t_{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{nH_{n}(t_{1}-t_{2})}{2(t_{2}-s)}}.

(3.72)

Proof.

The proof follows verbatim from
[
2
, Corollary 5.11]
. The only difference is that we consider
\mathcal{N}^{r}
as defined in (

) instead and let
r\to\infty
.
∎

4
Heat kernel estimates

Throughout this section, we assume
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
is the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker in
\mathcal{M}(A)
. First, we recall the following no-local-collapsing theorem proved in
[
28
, Theorem 22]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
x\in M
and
t<1
, if
R\leq r^{-2}
on
B_{t}(x,r)
, then

\displaystyle|B_{t}(x,r)|_{t}\geq ce^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}r^{n}

(4.1)

for some constant
c=c(n)>0
.

One can improve (
4.1
) by using the Nash entropy. Based on the Lipschitz property of the Nash entropy, we can follow the same proof of
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
to obtain the following result. Notice that by (
3.59
), (
4.2
) is stronger than (
4.1
)

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
x\in M
and
t<1
, if
R\leq r^{-2}
on
B_{t}(x,r)
, then

\displaystyle|B_{t}(x,r)|_{t}\geq c\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\right)r^{n}

(4.2)

for some constant
c=c(n)>0
.

By using (
3.55
), we also have the following volume estimate around an
H_{n}
-center by following the same proof of
[
2
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any
x\in M
and
t<1
, if
(z,t-r^{2})
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(x,t)
, then

\displaystyle|B_{t-r^{2}}(z,r)|_{t-r^{2}}\geq c\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\right)r^{n}

(4.3)

for some constant
c=c(n)>0
and any
r\geq 0
.

Next, we recall the following upper bound estimate of the heat kernel proved in
[
28
, Theorem 15]
, which has already been used in the last section.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any
x,y\in M
and
s<t<1
, we have

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{e^{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}}{\left(4\pi(t-s)\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}.

(4.4)

Instead of using the entropy
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, one can include the Nash entropy and obtain the following result; see
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any
x,y\in M
and
s<t<1
, we have

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{C(n)}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s)\right).

(4.5)

Proof.

The proof follows almost the same as
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
. The main idea is to improve the bound
Z
of the estimate

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{Z}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s)\right).

Notice that such
Z
always exists by (
4.4
) and (
3.59
), which may depend on the Ricci shrinker. Thanks to (
3.70
) and (
4.3
), we can follow the same argument as in
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
to improve
Z
to be
Z/2
, if
Z\geq\bar{Z}(n)
.
∎

With the help of Theorem
3.16
, Corollary
3.25
and Theorem
4.5
, we obtain the following gradient estimate of the heat kernel as
[
2
, Theorem 7.5]
, which improves
[
28
, Lemma 18]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For any
x,y\in M
and
s<t<1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla_{x}H|(x,t,y,s)}{H(x,t,y,s)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{C}{t-s}}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{C\exp\left(-\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s)\right)}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}H(x,t,y,s)}\right)}

(4.6)

for some constant
C=C(n)>0
.

With the gradient estimate (
4.6
), one obtains the following non-expanding estimate as
[
2
, Theorem 8.1]
. Notice that (
4.7
) generalizes the global volume estimate Lemma
2.4
.

Theorem 4.7
.

For any
x\in M
,
t<1
and
r\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|B_{t}(x,r)|_{t}\leq C(n)\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\right)r^{n}\leq C(n)r^{n}.

(4.7)

Before we prove more refined heat kernel estimates, we first prove a series of lemmas.

Lemma 4.8

(Distance comparison)
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
L_{1}=L_{1}(n,\delta)>1
such that

\displaystyle d_{t}(x,p)\leq d_{s}(x,p)+L_{1}\leq\frac{L_{1}}{\sqrt{1-t}}(d_{t}(x,p)+1)+L_{1}^{2}

(4.8)

for any
x\in M
and
-\delta^{-1}\leq s\leq t<1
.

Proof.

From (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle-\frac{F}{1-t}\leq\partial_{t}F=-(1-t)R\leq 0.

Therefore, for any
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\frac{1-t}{1-s}F(x,s)\leq F(x,t)\leq F(x,s).

(4.9)

Consequently, (
4.8
) follows from the combination of Lemma
2.3
and (
4.9
).
∎

As an application of the distance comparison, we have the following lower bound of the heat kernel.

Theorem 4.9
.

For any
K>1
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
A>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,K,\delta,A)>1
satisfying the following property.

Suppose
-\delta^{-1}\leq s<t\leq 1-\delta
and
d_{t}(x,p)\leq K
, then

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\geq\frac{C^{-1}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(x,y)}{C^{-1}(t-s)}\right).

(4.10)

Proof.

In the proof, all constants
C_{i}>1
depend on
n,K,\delta
and
A
.

It follows from
[
28
, Formula (203)]
that

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\geq\frac{C_{1}^{-1}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{t}^{2}(x,y)}{3(t-s)}-\frac{4(t-s)}{3(1-t)^{2}}F(y,t)\right).

(4.11)

From (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle d_{t}^{2}(x,y)\leq 2(d_{t}^{2}(x,p)+d_{t}^{2}(p,y))\leq C_{2}(d_{s}^{2}(x,p)+d_{s}^{2}(p,y)+1)\leq C_{3}(d_{s}^{2}(x,y)+1),

(4.12)

where we have used
d_{s}(x,p)\leq C(d_{t}(x,p)+1)\leq C(K+1)
by (
4.8
).

In addition, since
F
is decreasing with respect to
t
,

\displaystyle F(y,t)\leq F(y,s)\leq C_{4}(d_{s}^{2}(p,y)+1)\leq C_{5}(d_{s}^{2}(x,y)+1),

(4.13)

by Lemma
2.3
. Combining (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), it is easy to see (
4.10
) holds for some
C
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
K>1
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
A>0
, there exist constants
L_{2}=L_{2}(n,K,\delta,A)>1
and
L_{3}=L_{3}(n,\delta,A)>1
satisfying the following property.

Suppose
-\delta^{-1}\leq s<t\leq 1-\delta
and
d_{t}(p,x)\leq K
, then for any
H_{n}
-center
(z,s)
of
(x,t)
, we have

\displaystyle d_{s}(x,z)\leq L_{2}\sqrt{t-s}

(4.14)

and

\displaystyle d_{s}(z,p)\leq d_{t}(x,p)+L_{3}\sqrt{t-s}.

(4.15)

Proof.

Since
d_{t}(p,x)\leq K
, it follows from
[
28
, Theorem 19]
that

\displaystyle v_{x,t;s}\left(M\setminus B_{s}(x,r\sqrt{t-s})\right)\leq C_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{5}\right)

(4.16)

for any
r\geq 1
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(n,K,\delta,A)>0
. On the other hand, by Proposition
3.14
, we have

\displaystyle v_{x,t;s}\left(M\setminus B_{s}(z,r\sqrt{t-s})\right)\leq C(n)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{5}\right)

(4.17)

for any
r\geq 0
.
Combining (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), (
4.14
) follows immediately.

If we assume
(z^{\prime},s)
to be an
H_{n}
-center of
(p,t)
, then (
4.14
) indicates that

\displaystyle d_{s}(z^{\prime},p)\leq C_{3}(n,\delta,A)\sqrt{t-s}.

(4.18)

Then it follows from Proposition
3.7
and (
3.29
) that

\displaystyle d_{s}(z,p)\leq

\displaystyle d_{s}(z,z^{\prime})+d_{s}(z^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{s}(\delta_{z},\delta_{z^{\prime}})+C_{3}\sqrt{t-s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{s}(v_{x,t;s},v_{p,t;s})+d_{W_{1}}^{s}(\delta_{z},v_{x,t;s})+d_{W_{1}}^{s}(\delta_{z^{\prime}},v_{p,t;s})+C_{3}\sqrt{t-s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{t}(x,p)+2\sqrt{H_{n}(t-s)}+C_{3}\sqrt{t-s}.

Therefore, (
4.15
) holds for
L_{3}=C_{3}+2\sqrt{H_{n}}
.
∎

Next, we prove the following rough heat kernel estimate.

Proposition 4.11
.

For any
K>1
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
A>0
, there exists a constant
L_{4}=L_{4}(n,K,\delta,A)>1
satisfying the following property.

Suppose
-\delta^{-1}\leq s<t\leq 1-\delta
and
d_{s}(x,p)+d_{s}(y,p)\leq K
, then

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{L_{4}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(x,y)}{L_{4}(t-s)}\right).

(4.19)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
s=0
. In the proof, all constants
C_{i}
depend on
n,K,\delta
and
A
.

Given
0<t\leq 1-\delta
and
x,y\in M
with
d_{0}(x,p)+d_{0}(y,p)\leq K
, we set
d:=d_{0}(x,y)
. It follows from Lemma
4.8
that

\displaystyle d_{l}(x,p)+d_{l}(y,p)\leq C_{1}

(4.20)

for any
l\in[0,t]
. Therefore, it follows from the local distance distortion estimate
[
28
, Theorem 18]
that there exists
C_{2}>1
such that if
d\geq C_{2}\sqrt{t}
,

\displaystyle C_{2}^{-1}d\leq d_{l}(x,y)\leq C_{2}d

(4.21)

for any
l\in[0,t]
. Notice that if
d\leq C_{2}\sqrt{t}
, (
4.19
) follows immediately from (
4.4
). Consequently, we may assume
d\geq C_{2}\sqrt{t}
and hence (
4.21
) holds.

For any
l\in[0,t/2]
, we apply
[
28
, Theorem 14]
for sets
B_{l}(x,\sqrt{t})
,
B_{l}(y,\sqrt{t})
and parameter
\sigma=1
to obtain

\displaystyle v_{x,t;l}\left(B_{l}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)v_{x,t;l}\left(B_{l}(y,\sqrt{t})\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{(d_{l}(x,y)-2\sqrt{t})_{+}^{2}}{16t}\right)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C_{3}t}\right)

(4.22)

for some
C_{3}>1
, where we have used (
4.21
). In addition, for any
l\in[0,t/2]
and
d_{l}(x,z)\leq\sqrt{t}
, it follows from
[
28
, Theorem 18]
that
d_{t}(x,z)\leq C_{4}\sqrt{t}
. Therefore, it follows from
[
28
, Theorem 17]
that

\displaystyle H(x,t,z,l)\geq C_{5}^{-1}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}

and hence

\displaystyle v_{x,t;l}\left(B_{l}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)\geq C_{5}^{-1}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}|B_{l}(x,\sqrt{t})|\geq C_{6}^{-1},

(4.23)

where we have used the fact that
R
is bounded on
B_{l}(x,\sqrt{t})
and the no-local-collapsing Theorem
4.1
.

Combining (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), we obtain for any
l\in[0,t/2]
that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{l}(y,\sqrt{t})}H(x,t,z,l)\,dV_{l}(z)\leq C_{7}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C_{3}t}\right).

(4.24)

In light of (
4.4
), for any
l\in[0,t/2]
, the above inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{l}(y,\sqrt{t})}H^{2}(x,t,z,l)\,dV_{l}(z)\leq\frac{C_{8}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C_{3}t}\right).

(4.25)

Integrating
l
from
0
to
t/2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\int_{B_{l}(y,\sqrt{t})}H^{2}(x,t,z,l)\,dV_{l}(z)ds\leq\frac{C_{9}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C_{3}t}\right).

(4.26)

Consequently, the desired heat kernel estimate (
4.19
) follows from (
4.26
) and a parabolic mean value inequality
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
. Here,
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
can be applied in our setting since the key ingredient is the existence of a nice local cutoff function, which is constructed in
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also Proposition
5.12
). Once the existence of the local cutoff function is guaranteed, one can follow verbatim the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
to obtain the mean value inequality.
∎

We immediately obtain the following result by combining Lemma
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
.

Corollary 4.12
.

For any
K>1
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
A>0
, there exists a constant
L_{5}=L_{5}(n,K,\delta,A)>1
satisfying the following property.

Suppose
-\delta^{-1}\leq s<t\leq 1-\delta
and
d_{t}(x,p)+d_{s}(y,p)\leq K
, then for any
H_{n}
-center
(z,s)
of
(x,t)
, we have

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{L_{5}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(z,y)}{L_{5}(t-s)}\right).

(4.27)

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
and (

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(t-s)}}\quad\text{and}\quad-\frac{n}{2(t-s)}\leq\square\mathcal{N}_{s}^{*}\leq 0

in the classical sense.

We end this section by proving the following hypercontractivity; see
[
2
, Theorem 12.1]
.

Theorem 4.20
.

Suppose that
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
. Let
u\in C^{2}(M\times[t_{0}-\tau_{2},t_{0}-\tau_{1}])
be a nonnegative function satisfying
\square u\leq 0
and having at most polynomial spatial growth in the sense that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)|\leq m(d^{m}_{t}(p,x)+1)

(4.66)

for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
1<q_{0}\leq p_{0}<\infty
with

\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}\geq\frac{p_{0}-1}{q_{0}-1},

then for
dv_{t}:=dv_{x_{0},t_{0};t}
,

\left(\int_{M}u^{p_{0}}dv_{t_{0}-\tau_{1}}\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq\left(\int_{M}u^{q_{0}}dv_{t_{0}-\tau_{2}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}.

(4.67)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
t_{0}=0
. We set
p=p(t)=1+\tau_{2}(q_{0}-1)|t|^{-1}
for
t<0
. Notice that
p(-\tau_{2})=q_{0}
and
p(-\tau_{1})\geq p_{0}
by our assumption.

When
t<0
, direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}-\int_{M}u^{p}\square\phi^{r}dv_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\dot{p}u^{p}\log u+pu^{p-1}\square u-p(p-1)|\nabla u|^{2}u^{p-2}\right)\phi^{r}-2\langle\nabla u^{p},\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\,dv_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\int_{M}\phi^{r}u^{p}\log u^{p}\,dv_{t}-\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla u^{p},\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\,dv_{t}.

(4.68)

Moreover, we have for any
\epsilon\ll 1
,

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla u^{p}||\nabla\phi^{r}|\,dv_{t}\leq\epsilon\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}+\epsilon^{-1}\int_{M}u^{p}|\nabla\phi^{r}|^{2}\,dv_{t}.

(4.69)

Combining (
4.68
) and (
4.69
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\left(\partial_{t}\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)\log\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\left(\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\int_{M}\phi^{r}u^{p}\log u^{p}\,dv_{t}-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)\log\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\left(\left(\epsilon-\frac{p-1}{p}\right)\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}+\epsilon^{-1}\int_{M}u^{p}\left\{|\nabla\phi^{r}|^{2}+\square\phi^{r}\right\}dv_{t}\right).

(4.70)

We integrate (
4.70
) from
-\tau_{2}
to
-\tau_{1}
, let
r\to\infty
and then let
\epsilon\to 0
. By Theorem
4.4
, (
4.66
), (
2.12
) and (
2.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left.\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right|_{-\tau_{2}}^{-\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\tau_{2}}^{-\tau_{1}}\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\left(\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\int_{M}u^{p}\log u^{p}\,dv_{t}-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)\log\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{-\tau_{2}}^{-\tau_{1}}\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\left(-\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\,dv_{t}\right)\,dt.

(4.71)

Note that the log-Sobolev inequality
[
28
, Theorem 13]
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\int_{M}u^{p}\log u^{p}\,dv_{t}-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)\log\left(\int_{M}u^{p}\,dv_{t}\right)\leq\frac{\dot{p}}{p}|t|\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\,dv_{t}=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u^{p}|^{2}}{u^{p}}\,dv_{t}.

Therefore, it follows from (
4.71
) that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}u^{p_{0}}\,dv_{-\tau_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\leq\left(\int_{M}u^{p(-\tau_{1})}\,dv_{-\tau_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(-\tau_{1})}}\leq\left(\int_{M}u^{q_{0}}\,dv_{-\tau_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.21
.

If
u\in C^{2}(M\times[t_{0}-\tau_{2},t_{0}-\tau_{1}])
and satisfies
\square u=0
and (
4.66
), then

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{p_{0}}dv_{t_{0}-\tau_{1}}\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq\left(\int_{M}|u|^{q_{0}}dv_{t_{0}-\tau_{2}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}.

Indeed, one can apply (
4.67
) to
\sqrt{u^{2}+\epsilon}
since
\square\sqrt{u^{2}+\epsilon}\leq 0
, and let
\epsilon\to 0
.

5

\epsilon
-regularity theorem

In this section, we assume
(M^{n},g(t))_{t<1}
is the Ricci flow associated with a Ricci shrinker in
\mathcal{M}(A)
.

Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
, we first recall the conventional parabolic neighborhoods are defined by

\displaystyle P(x_{0},t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+}):=

\displaystyle B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},S)\times\left([t_{0}-T^{-},t_{0}+T^{+}]\cap(-\infty,1)\right)

(5.1)

\displaystyle Q(x_{0},t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+}):=

\displaystyle\left\{d_{t}(x,x_{0})\leq S,\,t\in[t_{0}-T^{-},t_{0}+T^{+}]\cap(-\infty,1)\right\}

(5.2)

for any
S,T^{\pm}\geq 0
. Based on the monotonicity of
W_{1}
-distance in Proposition
3.7
, we follow
[
2
]
to define the following new parabolic neighborhoods.

Definition 5.1

(
P^{*}
-parabolic neighborhoods)
.

Suppose that
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
S,T^{\pm}\geq 0
.
The
P^{*}
-parabolic neighborhood
P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset M\times(-\infty,1)
is defined as the set of points
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,1)
with
t\in[t_{0}-T^{-},t_{0}+T^{+}]
and

d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{x_{0},t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{x,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})<S.

For any
r>0
, we also define

\displaystyle P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r):=

\displaystyle P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r,-r^{2},r^{2})

\displaystyle P^{*+}(x_{0},t_{0};r):=

\displaystyle P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r,0,r^{2})

\displaystyle P^{*-}(x_{0},t_{0};r):=

\displaystyle P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r,-r^{2},0).

Similar definitions are also made for
P^{\pm}
.

Some basic properties of
P^{*}
-parabolic neighborhoods can be found in
[
2
, Proposition 9.4, Corollary 9.6]
. We state the following containment result from
[
2
, Proposition 9.4 (d)]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
A_{1},A_{2},T_{1}^{\pm},T_{2}^{\pm}\geq 0
and
(x_{1},t_{1})\in P^{*}(x_{2},t_{2};A_{2},-T_{2}^{-},T_{2}^{+})
, then

P^{*}(x_{1},t_{1};A_{1},-T_{1}^{-},T_{1}^{+})\subset P^{*}(x_{2},t_{2};A_{1}+A_{2},-(T_{1}^{-}+T_{2}^{-}),T_{1}^{+}+T_{2}^{+}).

We immediately have the following result from the distance comparison Lemma
4.8
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
\delta\in(0,1)
,
t_{0}\in(-\infty,1)
,
T^{\pm}\geq 0
and
S\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,A,\delta)>1
such that

\displaystyle P(p,t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset

\displaystyle Q(p,t_{0};C(S+1),-T^{-},T^{+})

\displaystyle Q(p,t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset

\displaystyle P(p,t_{0};C(S+1),-T^{-},T^{+})

provided that
-\delta^{-1}\leq t_{0}-T^{-}\leq t_{0}+T^{+}\leq 1-\delta
.

In order to investigate the relation between
P^{*}
-parabolic neighborhoods and conventional ones, we first prove

Proposition 5.4
.

Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
r>0
, suppose
R(x_{0},t)\leq r^{-2}
for any

. Then

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{t_{0}-r^{2}}(v_{x_{0},t_{0};t_{0}-r^{2}},\delta_{x_{0}})\leq C(n,A)r.

(5.3)

Proof.

It follows from
[
28
, Theorem 16]
that

\displaystyle H(x_{0},{t_{0}},x_{0},{t_{0}-r^{2}})\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi r^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-l_{(x_{0},{t_{0}})}(x_{0},{t_{0}-r^{2}})\right),

(5.4)

From the definition of
l_{(x_{0},{t_{0}})}(x_{0},{t_{0}-r^{2}})
we have

\displaystyle l_{(x_{0},{t_{0}})}(x_{0},{t_{0}-r^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2r}\int_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\sqrt{{t_{0}}-s}\,R(x_{0},s)\,ds\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(5.5)

Combining (
4.53
) for
\epsilon=1
, (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), it is clear that

\displaystyle d_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{2}(x_{0},z)\leq C_{1}r^{2}

for some constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,A)
, where
(z,t_{0}-r^{2})
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{t_{0}-r^{2}}(v_{x_{0},t_{0};t_{0}-r^{2}},\delta_{x_{0}})\leq d_{W_{1}}^{t_{0}-r^{2}}(v_{x_{0},t_{0};t_{0}-r^{2}},\delta_{z})+d_{t_{0}-r^{2}}(x_{0},z)\leq C_{2}r,

where we have used (
3.29
) and
C_{2}:=\sqrt{H_{n}}+\sqrt{C_{1}}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

From the proof, we conclude that (
5.3
) also holds for a constant
C=C(n,A,\alpha)
if we assume

\displaystyle R(x_{0},t)\leq\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}(t_{0}-t)}

for some

and any

.

Corollary 5.6
.

For any
s_{0}<t_{0}<1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{s_{0}}(v_{p,t_{0};s_{0}},\delta_{p})\leq C(n,A)\sqrt{t_{0}-s_{0}}.

(5.6)

Proof.

From the self-similarity of the flow, we know that

\displaystyle R(p,t)=\frac{R(p,0)}{1-t}\leq\frac{n}{2(1-t)}\leq\frac{n}{2(t_{0}-t)}

for any
t<t_{0}
. Therefore, the conclusion follows from Proposition
5.4
and Remark
5.5
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Given
\delta\in(0,1)
,
t_{0}\in(-\infty,1)
,
T^{\pm}\geq 0
and
S\geq 0
, there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle Q(p,t_{0};S,-T^{-},T^{+})\subset P^{*}(p,t_{0};S+C,-T^{-},T^{+})

Proof.

For any

, we have

\displaystyle d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{x,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{x,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})+d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{t}(x,p)+d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})\leq S+d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}}),

(5.7)

where we have used Proposition
3.10
. In addition, it follows from Corollary

that

\displaystyle d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}})\leq d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}},\delta_{p})+d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(v_{p,t;t_{0}-T^{-}},\delta_{p})\leq C(n,A,\delta).

(5.8)

Therefore, the conclusion follows from (
5.7
) and (
5.8
).
∎

Thanks to Proposition
5.4

, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.9
.

There exists a constant
\rho_{2}=\rho_{2}(n,A)\in(0,1)

(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)

\displaystyle\left(\text{resp.}\quad P(x_{0},t_{0};\rho_{2}r)\subset P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho_{2}r)^{2},(\rho_{2}r)^{2})\right).

\rho_{1}
is from Lemma

and
\delta
.

\displaystyle d^{t_{0}-T^{-}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{p},v_{p,t_{0};t_{0}-T^{-}})\leq C_{1}.

(5.16)

Next, we recall the following existence of the local cutoff function from

, Theorem 1.3]
.

Proposition 5.12
.

Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and

, there exists a constant
\rho_{3}=\rho_{3}(n,A)\in(0,1)

\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}])

.

(b)

\varphi>\rho_{3}
on

.

(c)

\varphi=0
outside

(d)

(e)

\square\varphi\leq 0
on

.

Proof.

We sketch the proof for readers’ convenience. In
[

, Theorem 1.3]
,
\varphi
is constructed as the smoothing of
\psi^{2}
, where

\displaystyle\psi(x,t):=c_{1}\max\{K(x,t)-c,0\}

for some constants

on

for some open set

, where
\psi=0
on

outside

Next, we prove

Proposition 5.13
.

There exists a constant

(

\displaystyle P^{*-}(x_{0},t_{0};\rho_{4}r)\subset P(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho_{4}r)^{2},0)

(5.19)

\displaystyle\left(\text{resp.}\quad P^{*}(x_{0},t_{0};\rho_{4}r)\subset P(x_{0},t_{0};r,-(\rho_{4}r)^{2},(\rho_{4}r)^{2})\right).

(5.20)

Proof.

In the proof, all positive constants

depend only on

. Moreover, we set

to be determined later.

For any

\displaystyle\quad d_{t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}}(z,x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq d^{t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{x_{0}},v_{x_{0},s;t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}})+d^{t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}}_{W_{1}}(v_{y,s;t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}},v_{x_{0},s;t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}})+d^{t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{z},v_{y,s;t_{0}-(\tau r)^{2}})

(5.21)

We assume

and consider the cutoff function

Next, we recall the definition of the curvature radius.

Definition 5.14

(Curvature radius)
.

For any
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,1)
, the curvature radii at
(x,t)
are defined as

\displaystyle r_{\emph{Rm}}(x,t):=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{r>0\mid|Rm|\leq r^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad P(x,t;r)\right\},

\displaystyle r_{\emph{Rm}}^{-}(x,t):=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{r>0\mid|Rm|\leq r^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad P^{-}(x,t;r)\right\},

\displaystyle r_{\emph{Rm}}^{s}(x,t):=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{r>0\mid|Rm|\leq r^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad B_{t}(x,r)\right\}.

It is clear from the definition that
r_{\text{Rm}}(x,t)\leq r_{\text{Rm}}^{-}(x,t)\leq r_{\text{Rm}}^{s}(x,t)
. In addition, it follows from Theorem
4.1
and the pseudolocality theorem
[
28
, Theorem 24]
on Ricci shrinkers that there exists a constant
C=C(n,A)>1
such that

\displaystyle r_{\text{Rm}}^{-}(x,t)\leq Cr_{\text{Rm}}(x,t).

(5.23)

We are in a position to obtain the following
\epsilon
-regularity theorem; see
[
2
, Theorem 10.2]
.

Theorem 5.15

(
\epsilon
-regularity)
.

There exists a small constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>0
satisfying the following property.
Given
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and
r>0
, suppose that
\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(r^{2})\geq-\epsilon
, then
r_{\emph{Rm}}(x,t)\geq\epsilon r
.

Proof.

We only sketch the proof as the details can be found in
[
2
, Theorem 10.2]
. The key step is a point-picking argument in the spacetime with respect to the curvature radius
r_{\text{Rm}}
. More precisely, one needs to show that for any
A>0
with
10Ar_{\text{Rm}}(x,t)\leq 1/2
, there exists a point
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in P^{*-}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime};10Ar_{\text{Rm}}(x,t))
such that
r_{\text{Rm}}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\leq r_{\text{Rm}}(x,t)
and
r_{\text{Rm}}\geq r_{\text{Rm}}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})/10
on
P^{*-}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime};Ar_{\text{Rm}}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))
. Otherwise, one can iteratively pick a sequence of spacetime points
(x_{i},t_{i})
in a compact set of
M\times(-\infty,1)
satisfying
r_{\text{Rm}}(x_{i},t_{i})\to 0
.
In light of Lemma
5.2
, all
(x_{i},t_{i})
fall into a given
P^{*-}
-parabolic neighborhood, which is precompact by Corollary
5.11
.
Note that the curvature radius of
(x_{i},t_{i})
shrinks by a definite portion in each step, the bounded geometry of a compact set implies that the process must terminate in finite steps, say
(x_{k},t_{k})=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})
.
Such choice of
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})
guarantees that it has almost maximal curvature radius in spacetime neighborhood.
Notice that similar point-picking arguments can be found in
[
34
, Theorem 10.1]
and
[
16
, Proposition 3.43]
.

If the
\epsilon
-regularity theorem fails, we could obtain a sequence of pointed Ricci flows such that
r_{\text{Rm}}=1
at the base points after the point-picking and appropriate rescalings.
Since nearby points have curvature radii uniformly bounded from below, the sequence converges smoothly to a limit Ricci flow which is the Euclidean spacetime by the assumption of the Nash entropy.
Therefore,
r_{\text{Rm}}=1
must be violated and we obtain a contradiction.
∎

Using the
\epsilon
-regularity theorem, one immediately has the following gap property, following the same proof of
[
28
, Theorem 3]
.

Corollary 5.16
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g,f,p)
is a non-flat Ricci shrinker. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{(p,0)}(\epsilon^{-2})<-\epsilon,

where
\epsilon
is the same constant in Theorem
5.15
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{N}_{(p,0)}(\epsilon^{-2})\geq-\epsilon
and
(M,g)
is a non-flat Ricci shrinker, it follows from Theorem
5.15
that
r_{\text{Rm}}(p,0)\geq 1
.
In particular, it implies that
|Rm(p,t)|\leq 1
for any
t\in[0,1)
. By the self-similarity of the flow,
we have
|Rm|(p,0)=|Rm|(p,t)(1-t)
and hence
|Rm|(p,0)=0
, which contradicts the fact that
R>0
for non-flat Ricci shrinkers.
∎

In previous sections, we have generalized (or slightly improved) the theorems and tools in

]
.
Notice that these results also hold for Ricci flows induced by Ricci shrinkers (cf. Definition

Based on these results and techniques, one can generalize the theory of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
from compact Ricci flows to the setting of Ricci flows induced by Ricci shrinkers.

Notice that the results in

]
and
[

[

]

and Theorem

, it is known that the conjugate heat kernel decays exponentially and the function

induced by the conjugate heat kernel increases quadratically (cf. Remark

) constructed in

have at most quadratic spatial growth. Moreover, it follows from
[

that one can construct a bounded strong splitting map with bounded gradient from a given weak splitting map.

At various places in
[

]
, one also needs to consider integral
\int u\phi^{r}
instead of
\int u
, and take the limit for

after all the estimates (e.g.,
u=\square|\omega_{l}|
in
[

). This technique has already appeared multiple times in previous sections. As a showcase, we generalize the integral estimates in

Now, we recall the following definition of the metric flow from
[
3
, Definition 3.2]
.

Definition 6.1

(Metric flow)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a subset.
A metric flow over
I
is a tuple of the form

(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{t},(d_{t})_{t\in I},(v_{x;s})_{x\in\mathcal{X},s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)})

with the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is a set consisting of points.

(2)

\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{X}\to I
is a map called time-function.
Its level sets
\mathcal{X}_{t}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(t)
are called time-slices and the preimages
\mathcal{X}_{I^{\prime}}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(I^{\prime})
,
I^{\prime}\subset I
, are called time-slabs.

(3)

(\mathcal{X}_{t},d_{t})
is a complete and separable metric space for all
t\in I
.

(4)

v_{x;s}
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}_{s}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
s\in I
,
s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)
. For any
x\in\mathcal{X}
the family
(v_{x;s})_{s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)}
is called the conjugate heat kernel at
x
.

(5)

v_{x;\mathfrak{t}(x)}=\delta_{x}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

(6)

For all
s,t\in I
,
s<t
,
T\geq 0
and any measurable function
u_{s}:\mathcal{X}_{s}\to[0,1]
with the property that if
T>0
, then
u_{s}=\Phi\circ f_{s}
for some
T^{-1/2}
-Lipschitz function
f_{s}:\mathcal{X}_{s}\to\mathbb{R}

(
if
T=0
, then there is no additional assumption on
u_{s}
)
, the following is true.
The function

u_{t}:\mathcal{X}_{t}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad x\longmapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}u_{s}\,dv_{x;s}

is either constant or of the form
u_{t}=\Phi\circ f_{t}
, where
f_{t}:\mathcal{X}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
is
(t-s+T)^{-1/2}
-Lipschitz. Here,
\Phi
is given by (
3.31
).

(7)

For any
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\in I
,
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}
,

we have the reproduction formula

v_{x;t_{1}}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t_{2}}}v_{\cdot;t_{1}}dv_{x;t_{2}},

meaning that for any Borel set
S\subset\mathcal{X}_{t_{1}}

v_{x;t_{1}}(S)=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t_{2}}}v_{y;t_{1}}(S)dv_{x;t_{2}}(y).

Given a metric flow
\mathcal{X}
over
I
, we recall the following definitions from
[
3
, Definition 3.20, 3.30]
.

Definition 6.2

(Conjugate heat flow)
.

A family of probability measures
(\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{t}))_{t\in I^{\prime}}
over
I^{\prime}\subset I
is called a conjugate heat flow if for all
s,t\in I^{\prime}
,
s\leq t
we have

\mu_{s}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}v_{x;s}\,d\mu_{t}(x).

Definition 6.3

(

-Concentration)
.

Given a constant

, a metric flow
\mathcal{X}
is called

-concentrated if for any
s\leq t
,
s,t\in I
,

\emph{\text{Var}}(v_{x_{1};s},v_{x_{2};s})\leq d^{2}_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})+H(t-s).

, Definition 5.1, 5.2]
. Roughly speaking, two metric flow pairs are equivalent if they are the same in the metric measure sense almost everywhere.

Definition 6.4

(Metric flow pair)
.

A pair

I^{\prime}\subset I

2.

\mathcal{X}

.

, then we say that
(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
is fully defined over
J
. We denote by

the set of equivalence classes of metric flow pairs over
I
that are fully defined over

. Here, two metric flow pairs

and

.

We will only consider

for simplicity. Then for any pointed Ricci flow
(M^{n},g(t),x_{0})_{t\in I}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{X}:=M\times(I\setminus\{0\})\sqcup x_{0}\times\{0\},\mathfrak{t}:=\text{proj}_{I},(d_{t})_{t\in I},(v_{x,t;s})_{(x,t)\in M\times I,s\in I,s\leq t},\mu_{t}:=v_{x_{0},0;t}\right).

Then we have

.

The pair

The conditions (1)-(5) in the definition of the metric flow can be easily checked. Condition (6) follows from (

Definition 6.14

(Metric soliton)

A metric flow pair
(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})

Definition 6.15

(Almost self-similarity)
.

Let

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-\epsilon^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\epsilon r^{2}}\int_{M}\tau\Big|Rc+\nabla^{2}b-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\Big|^{2}dv_{x_{0},t_{0};t}dt\leq\epsilon,

Lemma A.1

) sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle|X^{\prime}|\leq\epsilon(r)+\frac{1}{10}\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})\phi^{r}e^{sb}\,dv_{t}dt+\log\theta^{-1}.

(A.19)

Similarly, we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}\tau Re^{sb}w\phi^{r}\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{\square(\tau Re^{sb})\phi^{r}w-\tau Re^{sb}\square^{*}(\phi^{r}w)\right\}dV_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{\left(\square(\tau R)e^{sb}+\tau R\square e^{sb}-2\tau\langle\nabla R,\nabla e^{sb}\rangle\right)\phi^{r}w+\tau Re^{sb}\left((\Delta\phi^{r}+\phi^{r}_{t})w+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\right)\right\}dV_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{\left(\square(\tau R)e^{sb}+\tau R\square e^{sb}\right)\phi^{r}w+2\tau R\left(\Delta e^{sb}-\langle\nabla e^{sb},\nabla b\rangle\right)\phi^{r}w\right\}dV_{t}+Y_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{2\tau|Rc|^{2}-R+\tau R\left(s\square b+(s^{2}-2s)|\nabla b|^{2}+2s\Delta b\right)\right\}\phi^{r}e^{sb}dv_{t}+Y_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{2\tau|Rc|^{2}-R-Cs\left(\tau(R^{2}+|\nabla b|^{4}+(\Delta b)^{2})+R\right)\right\}\phi^{r}e^{sb}dv_{t}+Y_{t},

(A.20)

where

\displaystyle Y_{t}:

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tau Re^{sb}\left((\Delta\phi^{r}+\phi^{r}_{t})w+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle+2s\langle\nabla b,\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\right)\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tau R\left(\Delta\phi^{r}+\phi^{r}_{t}+(2s-2)\langle\nabla b,\nabla\phi^{r}\rangle\right)e^{sb}\,dv_{t}.

We define similarly
Y:=\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}|Y_{t}|\,dt
. Then it follows from Lemma
A.2
and inequalities (
2.12
) to (
2.15
) that

\displaystyle|Y^{\prime}|\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon(r)+C\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\left(\delta^{-1}|\nabla\phi^{r}|^{2}(\phi^{r})^{-1}\tau|\nabla b|^{2}+\delta\tau R^{2}\phi^{r}\right)e^{sb}\,dv_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon(r)+C\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\delta\tau R^{2}\phi^{r}e^{sb}\,dv_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon(r)+\frac{1}{10}\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})\phi^{r}e^{sb}\,dv_{t}dt,

(A.21)

for
\delta
sufficiently small.

Combining (
A.16
) and (
A.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}(\tau R+u)e^{sb}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{-Cs\left(\tau(|\nabla b|^{4}+|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+R^{2})+\tau^{-1}+R\right)+2\tau\left|Rc+\nabla^{2}b-\frac{g}{2\tau}\right|^{2}+2\tau|Rc|^{2}-R\right\}\phi^{r}e^{sb}dv_{t}

\displaystyle+X_{t}+Y_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{-Cs\left(\tau(|\nabla b|^{4}+|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+R^{2})\right)+\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})\right\}\phi^{r}e^{sb}dv_{t}+X_{t}+Y_{t}-C^{\prime}\tau^{-1},

(A.22)

where we have used Lemma
A.2
.

If
s
is sufficiently small, it follows from (
A.22
) and (
A.14
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}(\tau R+u)e^{sb}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})e^{sb}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}+X_{t}+Y_{t}-C^{\prime}\tau^{-1}+\epsilon(r).

(A.23)

By integration from
-\tau_{0}
to
-\theta\tau_{0}
, we obtain from (

), Lemma
A.2

\displaystyle\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})e^{sb}\phi^{r}\,dv_{t}dt\leq C^{\prime}\log\theta^{-1}+\epsilon(r).

Letting

\displaystyle\int_{-\tau_{0}}^{-\theta\tau_{0}}\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla^{2}b|^{2}+|Rc|^{2})e^{sb}\,dv_{t}dt\leq C^{\prime}\log\theta^{-1}.

(A.24)

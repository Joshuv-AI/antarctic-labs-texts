Global existence for a 3D Tropical Climate Model with damping and small initial data in H ˙ 1 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) \dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
Diego Berti, Luca Bisconti, Davide Catania

Abstract
We consider a 3D Tropical Climate Model with damping terms in the equation of the barotropic mode u u and in the equation of the first baroclinic mode v v of the velocity. The equation for the temperature θ \theta is free from dampings. We prove global existence in time for this system assuming the initial data ( u 0 , v 0 , θ 0 ) (u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0}) small, in terms of the homogeneous space H ˙ 1 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) \dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) .

35Q35; 76D03, 35Q30,

Introduction

We consider the following 3D tropical Climate Model with damping, i.e.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\sigma_{1}|u|^{\alpha-1}u+\nabla\pi+\,\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(u\cdot\nabla)v-\eta\Delta v+\sigma_{2}|v|^{\beta-1}v+(v\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\theta=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta-\mu\Delta\theta+\,\mathrm{div}\,v=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u=0,

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0},\,\,v(x,0)=v_{0},\,\,\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0},

where, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
t\geq 0
,
u=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))

and
v=(v_{1}(x,t),v_{2}(x,t),v_{3}(x,t))
denote, respectively, the barotropic mode
and the first baroclinic mode of the velocity,
\theta=\theta(x,t)
indicates
the temperature, and
\pi=\pi(x,t)
represents the pressure that acts as a Lagrange multiplier for this system.
Also,
\nu>0
,
\eta>0
, and
\mu>0
.

Here, we assume
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
, while

\alpha,\beta\geq 1
. In particular,
\alpha
and
\beta
are the
damping coefficients, for which further appropriate restrictions will
be introduced later.
When
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
, the damping terms in (
1
) are
dropped and the system takes the form of the viscous Tropical Climate
Model. The original version of this model, without diffusion
(i.e.
\nu=\eta=\mu=0
) and set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, was
derived first by Frierson, Majda and Pauluis
[
12
]
(see also

[
23
]
), while for the analysis of the viscous version of it
(still in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
) we refer the reader to Li and
Titi
[
18
]
(see also
[
19
]
).

As its name suggests, in
[
12
]
the system arises from the
context of meteorology, in formalizing the interaction of large scale
flows and precipitations in the tropical atmosphere. Roughly speaking,
system (
1
), with
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\nu=\eta=\mu=0
,
comes from the hydrostatic Boussinesq equations by performing a vertical
decomposition of the velocity field, followed by a Galerkin truncation
up to its first baroclinic mode.

While the 2D case (without dampings) was covered by the seminal work
[
18
]
(see also
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
20
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
), in studying (
1
) and its variants (in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
), a main open problem
is the issue of global well-posedness of strong solutions: See
[
3
,
30
,
31
]
for the 3D Tropical Climate
Model with damping. For the 3D case without damping but still modified with respect
to the standard viscous system (e.g. due to the presence of fractional diffusion or differential regularizing terms)
see the recent papers
[
4
,
8
,
32
,
34
]
. In
[
29
]
, the authors deal with
regularity under small initial data in the case of temperature-dependent systems.

Without damping terms, what is known is the local existence of strong
solutions
[
21
]
and the global existence under
small initial data (see
[
27
,
32
]
). The global well-posedness of
solutions to a 2D tropical climate model with dissipation in the
equation of the first baroclinic mode of the velocity, under the
hypotheses of small initial data, was studied by Wan
[
26
]
, Ma
and Wan
[
22
]
.

In the case of a
large damping effect
, namely if
\alpha,\beta\geq 4
, the related
terms help to deal with a priori and energy estimates, having a direct
regularizing action. In
[
31
]
, under the previous
assumptions, the authors prove global existence and
uniqueness. With similar techniques, but only requiring

\alpha\geq 4
, in
[
30
]
the authors still obtain the same
results. On the other hand, if
1\leq\alpha,\beta<4
,
the situation is, in principle, rather far from the previous ones, and
in fact the presence of the damping terms seems to play a more subtle
role. About this point, we refer to
[
3
]
where
the authors establish a regularity criterion in Besov spaces,
under the assumption
3\leq\alpha,\beta<4
.

In this paper, requiring
5/2\leq\alpha,\beta<4
, we give a new
regularity criterion, in terms of small initial data in

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, actually bounded in

H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and sufficiently small in

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, for the global strong solutions to
system (
1
) (see Theorem
2.2
below). The existence,
regularity and uniqueness of local solutions is given in Lemma

3.1
.

In order to prove Theorem
2.2
, we first obtain a new
blow-up criterion (see Theorem
2.1
) in terms of
proper integrability in time of BMO-norms of the local solution

(u,v,\theta)
. To apply Theorem
2.1
, it
suffices to bound uniformly in time the
\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}
-norm of

(u,v,\theta)
(see Remark
4.1
). A key ingredient
that we use is stated in Corollary
4.4
: By means of
the smallness hypothesis, we are able to prove that the low-order
norms in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\dot{H}^{1}
of
(u,v,\theta)
remain
bounded by the corresponding norms of the initial data. This is
obtained combining together the
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
- and

\dot{H}^{1}
-energy estimates given in Section
4
.

The main difficulties arise from the fact that we need to perform
some delicate estimates in the spaces
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and in doing so we
consider fractional derivatives of the various terms involved in
the equations (
1
) and, among others, of the damping
terms (on this regard see also
[
6
,
7
,
33
,
32
]
). The approach used here derives from the analysis
proposed in
[
6
,
7
]
and, when
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0

(see the Appendix), it still works and allows us to generalize, or
to provide an alternative proof, to the results given in

[
32
]
and
[
27
]
, respectively.

The plan of the paper is as follows: In Section

2
, we give the basic notations, we
collect the main tools from literature and we state the main
outcomes of the paper. Section
3
takes care of
providing local well-posedness and the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Section
4
is dedicated to the proof of
Theorem
2.2
, passing through the demonstration of Corollary

4.4
.

2
Preliminaries and main results

For
p\geq 1
, we indicate by
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the usual Lebesgue
space (not distinguishing scalar from vector fields, with a safe abuse of
notations), and with
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{p}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p}}
its usual
norm; we set
\|\,\cdot\,\|=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{2}
when
p=2
. For

s>0
and
p\geq 1
, we denote by
W^{s,p}=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

the Sobolev space, with its dual
W^{-s,p^{\prime}{}}
, where

1/p+1/p^{\prime}{}=1
. When
p=2
, we write
H^{s}=W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with norm

;
\dot{H}^{s}
denotes the standard
homogeneous Sobolev space with norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
(see, e.g.
[
2
]
). With

\widehat{\Lambda^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\hat{f}(\xi)
, for

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Also, with BMO we denote the space of

bounded mean oscillation functions
.
Moreover, we set
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\doteq H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

As a further matter of notation, let us introduce

\|(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}\doteq\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}+\|\theta\|^{2}
,
and the same for
\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}
,
\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}

\|\nabla\Delta(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}
and

.

For the remainder of the paper, the symbol
C
indicates a generic constant
independent of the solution.

2.2
The main results

In Section
3
, we derive a
blow-up criterion
for the local solution of (
1
).

Theorem 2.1

(Blow-up criterion)
.

Assume

5/2\leq\alpha,\beta<4
, and

(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, with
\,\mathrm{div}\,u_{0}=0
. Let
(u,v,\theta)(t)
be the local solution of (
1
). Define

\Pi(T)\doteq\int_{0}^{T}C\big(1+\|u(t)\|_{{}_{\textrm{BMO}}}^{8}+\|v(t)\|_{{}_{\textrm{BMO}}}^{8}+\|\theta(t)\|_{{}_{\textrm{BMO}}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|^{\alpha+1}_{\alpha+1}+\|v(t)\|^{\beta+1}_{\beta+1}\big)dt.

(7)

Then , for
0<T^{\ast}<+\infty
, we have that

\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(T)\|<+\infty,\ \mbox{ for every }\ 0<T<T^{\ast}\ \mbox{ and }\ \limsup_{T\to{T^{\ast}}}\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(T)\|=+\infty,

if and only if

\Pi(T)<+\infty\ \mbox{ for every }\ 0<T<T^{\ast}\ \mbox{ and }\ \Pi(T^{\ast})=+\infty.

If a time
T^{\ast}>0
as in Theorem
2.1

does exist, it is called the
first blow-up time
. Observe
that, for every
0<T<T^{\ast}
, we also have

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\,dt<+\infty.

In Section
4
, by means of energy estimates, we make use
of Theorem
2.1
in order to obtain the following
global existence result,
in terms of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
-smallness of the initial data.

Theorem 2.2

(Global existence and regularity under small initial data)
.

Assume

5/2\leq\alpha,\beta<4
, and

(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, with
\,\mathrm{div}\,u_{0}=0
. Let

R>0
be such that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\leq R
.
Then, there exists a sufficiently small constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(R)>0
such that, if

\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq c_{0},

(8)

the system (
1
) admits a unique global-in-time strong
solution
(u,v,\theta)
, satisfying

u,v,\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,T);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\ \mbox{ for every }\ T>0.

Note that the necessity to bound

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\leq R
is an
inheritance of Corollary
4.4
. Hence, as in Corollary

4.4
, we actually do not need this bound in the case

5/2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 3
, when the conclusion of Theorem

2.2
follows with
c_{0}
not depending on
R
. In the other
cases, we cannot remove the assumption, since

c_{0}(R)=o(1)
as
R\to+\infty
.

3
Local well-posedness and blow-up criterion

We begin with the local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions.

Lemma 3.1

(Local well-posedness)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
, there exists a
positive time
\hat{T}>0
and a unique strong solution

(u,v,\theta)(t)
on
[0,\hat{T})
to the system (
1
),
satisfying

(u,v,\theta)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\,\,\textrm{ for every }\,\,0<T<\hat{T}.

Proof.

We proceed formally, in order to obtain a priori estimates. A
standard approximation procedure gives then the desired result (see
e.g.
[
6
]
, see also
[
21
]
).

Let us start by taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of

\eqref{TCM-gen}_{1}
–
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{3}
,
respectively, with
u
,
v
and
\theta
, and then
by adding the resulting equations up. This gives

\frac{d}{dt}\|(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\nu\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\eta\,\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\mu\,\|\nabla\theta\|^{2}+\sigma_{1}\,\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\sigma_{2}\,\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}=0,

from which we get, after integrating in time variable in the interval

(0,T)
,

\|(u,v,\theta)(T)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|v(t)\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}\right)\,dt\leq C\,\|(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\|^{2},

(9)

where we recall that

\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}=\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}+\|\nabla v(t)\|^{2}+\|\nabla\theta(t)\|^{2}
.
As a consequence of the local existence, and in particular thanks to
(
9
), we will also have that
u\in L^{\alpha+1}([0,T);L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and

v\in L^{\beta+1}([0,T);L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, for every
0<T<\hat{T}
.

Further, applying
\Delta
to
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{1}
–
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{3}
and multiplying
the resulting equations in
L^{2}
by
\Delta u
,
\Delta v
and

\Delta\theta
, respectively, lead to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta

\displaystyle(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\nu\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}+\eta\|\nabla\Delta v\|^{2}+\mu\|\nabla\Delta\theta\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((u\cdot\nabla)u\big)\cdot\Delta u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((u\cdot\nabla)v\big)\cdot\Delta v\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((u\cdot\nabla)\theta\big)\cdot\Delta\theta\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((v\cdot\nabla)v\big)\cdot\Delta u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big(v\,\,\mathrm{div}\,v\big)\cdot\Delta u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((v\cdot\nabla)u\big)\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle-\sigma_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta(|u|^{\alpha-1}u)\cdot\Delta u\,dx-\sigma_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta(|v|^{\beta-1}v)\cdot\Delta v\,dx\doteq\sum_{i=1}^{8}I_{i},

where we used the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\nabla\theta\cdot\Delta vdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta(\,\mathrm{div}\,v)\cdot\Delta\theta dx=0.

Also, using the fact that
\,\mathrm{div}\,u=0
(see
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{4}
), we have, by means of the (continuous) embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

\|\nabla w\|\leq C\|w\|^{\frac{1}{4}}\,\|\Delta w\|^{\frac{3}{4}}
as in

[
21
]
,

\displaystyle|I_{i}|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\big((u\cdot\nabla)w\big)\cdot\Delta w\,dx\Big|\leq C\|\nabla\Delta u\|\,\|\Delta w\|^{2},\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle i=1,2,3,

\displaystyle|I_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\nabla\Delta u\|+\|\nabla\Delta v\|\big)\,\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)\|^{2},\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle i=4,5,6,

where
w
plays the role of
u
,
v
or
\theta
.

The contributions of the terms involving the damping terms are instead dealt with as follows.
First, we consider
I_{7}
. From Holder’s and Young’s inequalities, the embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s inequality

\|u\|_{3(\alpha-1)}\leq C\,\|u\|^{1-\kappa}\,\|\Delta u\|^{\kappa}\leq C\,\|\Delta u\|^{\kappa}\ \mbox{ with }\ \kappa(\alpha)=\frac{3\alpha-5}{4(\alpha-1)}\in(0,1),

(11)

we get

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta(|u|^{\alpha-1}u)\cdot\Delta u\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\big(|u|^{\alpha-1}u\big)\|\,\|\nabla\Delta u\|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\||u|^{\alpha-1}\nabla u\|\,\|\nabla\Delta u\|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\||u|^{\alpha-1}\|_{3}^{2}\,\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u\|_{3(\alpha-1)}^{2(\alpha-1)}\,\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\Delta u\|^{\frac{3\alpha-5}{4(\alpha-1)}}\,\|\Delta u\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\Delta u\|^{3})+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}.

Similarly, for
I_{8}
we obtain

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta(|v|^{\beta-1}v)\cdot\Delta v\,dx\Big|\leq C(1+\|\Delta v\|^{3})+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta v\|^{2}.

Plugging the above estimates in (
10
), and
set
\ell\doteq\min\{\nu,\eta,\mu\}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\ell\|\nabla\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\leq C\,\|\nabla\Delta(u,v)\|\,\big(1+\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{3}\big).

(12)

Then, an application of Young’s inequality to the right-hand
side of (
12
), and straightforward manipulations lead to

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\big(1+\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\big)+\big(\ell-\varepsilon\big)\|\nabla\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\leq C\,\big(1+\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\big)^{3}.

Now, set
X(t)=1+\|\Delta(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}
and

\hat{T}=\frac{3}{8\,C_{0}\,X^{2}(0)}.

As a consequence of

\frac{d}{dt}X\leq C_{0}X^{3}\,\,\,\textrm{ and so }\,\,\,X(t)\leq\frac{X(0)}{\sqrt{1-2\,C_{0}\,X^{2}(0)\,t}},

we obtain that
X(t)\leq 2X(0)
, for
t\in[0,T]
, and for
every
0<T<\hat{T}
.

Uniqueness and continuous dependence are even more standard and can be achieved, for instance, as done
in
[
30
]
(see also
[
31
]
).

$\alpha>8/3$)}.

Similarly, we get

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}(|v|^{\beta-1}v)\cdot\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dx\right|\leq\\
\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\,\|\nabla v\|^{*}\,\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|^{*}+C\,\big(1+\|\nabla v\|^{*}\big)+\varepsilon\||v|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\,\nabla v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}v\|^{2},\,\,\,\,\,&&\mbox{ if $5/2\leq\beta\leq 3$},\\
&C\,\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(u,v,\theta)\|^{*}\,\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{*}+\varepsilon\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}(u,v,\theta)\|^{2},\,\,\,\,\,&&\mbox{ if $3<\beta<4$}.\end{aligned}\right.

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\big(\min\{\nu,\eta,\mu\}-\varepsilon\big)\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(u,v,\theta)\|^{*}\,\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{*}{+\varepsilon\||u|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\nabla u\|^{2}+\varepsilon\||v|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\nabla v\|^{2},}

for every
0<t\leq T
, and
T<T^{\ast}
.

Adding (
4.2
) with (
26
), we obtain in
particular that

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\big)\leq C_{0},

from which we get

\sup_{0\leq t<T^{*}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\leq C_{0}+T^{*}C_{0}<+\infty.

(40)

We then proved Theorem
2.2
, with
c_{0}
as in Corollary
4.4
, from (
40
), the continuous embedding

\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
Theorem
2.1
and Remark
4.1
.

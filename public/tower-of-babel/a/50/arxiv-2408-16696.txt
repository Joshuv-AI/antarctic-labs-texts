Perturbations of APS boundary conditions for Lorentzian Dirac operators
Lennart Ronge

Abstract
We study how far APS-boundary conditions for a Lorentzian Dirac operator may be perturbed without destroying Fredholmness of the Dirac operator. This is done by developing criteria under which the perturbation of a compact pair of projections is a Fredholm pair.



1
Introduction

For Riemannian manifolds with boundary, the question which boundary conditions make a Dirac operator Fredholm is well understood. However, in the Lorentzian case much less is known (see
[
BH18
]
for what is known there). In general, in order to find out whether or not a Dirac operator on a globally hyperbolic spacetime with spacelike boundary is Fredholm with given boundary conditions, one needs knowledge of the associated evolution operator, which is fairly hard to obtain.

For specific boundary conditions known as Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) boundary conditions, the Lorentzian Dirac operator is always Fredholm and an index formula analogous to the Riemannian APS index theorem holds (see
[
BS19
]
and
[
BS23
]
). In fact, with these boundary conditions, the operator is, loosely speaking, ”as Fredholm as possible” and this makes it possible to extend the result to boundary conditions that are close to APS boundary conditions. In
[
BH18
, Proposition 4.5]
it is shown that boundary conditions given by the graphs of certain operators (such that the zero operators correspond exactly to APS-conditions) still make the Dirac operator Fredholm as long as the product of the operators’ norms is less than some unspecified constant
\epsilon>0
.

The goal of this note is to improve this condition and study in more detail, how far boundary conditions may be allowed to deviate from APS conditions without becoming non-Fredholm. We find that the
\epsilon
in
[
BH18
, Proposition 4.5]
may be chosen to be 1 (Theorem
4.2
), which is the best constant that one could hope to achieve. We also provide a different condition in terms of the difference in the norms (modulo compact operators) of the boundary projections and the APS projections (Theorem
4.1
).

All the Lorentzian geometry we will need is already present in
[
BH18
]
. The actual work lies purely in the study of general pairs of projections, for which we derive Fredholmness criteria. These criteria, applied to the setting of
[
BH18
]
will then yield the improved results.

The structure is as follows: We provide background information in Section
2
, derive Fredholmness criteria for general pairs of projections in Section
3
and use them in Section
4
to obtain results for Lorentzian boundary value problems.

2
Background

2.1
Fredholm pairs and Calkin norm

For this subsection let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be complex Hilbert spaces. Let
\mathcal{B}(H)
denote bounded operators on
H
,
\mathcal{K}(H)
denote compact operators on
H
and
\mathcal{C}(H):=\mathcal{B}(H)/\mathcal{K}(H)
the Calkin algebra of
H
. Let
\mathcal{B}(H,H^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{K}(H,H^{\prime})
denote bounded operators and compact operators from
H
to
H^{\prime}
.
We recall the definition and well-known characterizations of Fredholm operators:

Definition/Proposition 2.1
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(H,H^{\prime})
is called Fredholm, if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

•

\Ker(T)
and
\Coker(T):=H^{\prime}/\Ran(T)
are finite dimensional.

•

\Ker(T)
and
\Ker(T^{*})
are finite dimensional and
T
has closed range

If
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
,
T
is Fredholm if and only if its equivalence class in
\mathcal{C}(H)
is invertible.
T^{*}
is Fredholm if and only if
T
is Fredholm. The index of a Fredholm operator
T
is defined by

\ind(T):=\Dim\Ker(T)-\Dim\Coker(T)=\Dim\Ker(T)-\Dim\Ker(T^{*}).

A central role in this paper is played by Fredholm pairs of projections.

Definition 2.2
.

Whenever we refer to a Projection (in
H
) in this paper, we shall mean an orthogonal Projection onto a closed subspace (of
H
).

Definition/Proposition 2.3
.

A pair
(Q,P)
of projections in
H
is called Fredholm (of index
k
), if the restricted operator

P\colon\Ran(P)\rightarrow\Ran(Q)

is Fredholm (of index
k
). W will denote this restricted operator by
P_{Q}
.

As the adjoint of
Q_{P}
is
P_{Q}
, interchanging the order of
P
and
Q
preserves Fredholmness and flips the sign of the index. For more background on Fredholm pairs, see
[
ASS94
]
.

The norm of the Calkin algebra will be important for our study of Fredholm pairs. It can be viewed in different ways:

Definition/Proposition 2.4
.

For
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
, denote by
\|T\|_{C}
the norm of the equivalence class of
T
in
\mathcal{C}(H)
. This is equal to each of the following:

1.

\inf\{\|S\|\mid S\in\mathcal{B}(H),S-T\in\mathcal{K}(H)\}

2.

\inf\{\|S\|\mid S\in\mathcal{B}(H),S-T\text{ has finite rank}\}

3.

\inf\{\|T|_{V}\|\mid V\subseteq H\text{ is a closed finite codimension subspace}\}

Proof.

1
is the definition of the quotient norm.

\ref{1}=\ref{2}
: Any compact operator can be approximated up to any
\epsilon>0
by finite rank operators. As
\epsilon
is arbitrary, the infimum we get is the same.

\ref{2}=\ref{3}
: For
K
finite rank,
\Ker(K)
has finite codimension and we have

\|T+K\|\geq\|T|_{\Ker(K)}\|,

so we have
\ref{2}\geq\ref{3}
.

Conversely, if
V
has finite codimension, let
P_{V}
denote the projection onto
V
. As
T-TP_{V}
has a kernel of finite codimension , it must have finite rank. As
\|T|_{V}\|=\|TP_{V}\|
, we can conclude
\ref{3}\geq\ref{2}
.
∎

An alternative criterion for Fredholmness is the following (see
[
DSBW23
, Corollary 5.3.14]
):

Proposition 2.5
.

A pair of projections
(P,Q)
is a Fredholm pair if and only if

\|P-Q\|_{C}<1.

In particular, whether a pair is Fredholm (but not its index) is determined by the classes of the projections in the Calkin algebra. If the difference
P-Q
is compact, then it has norm
0
in the Calkin algebra, thus the pair is in some sense ”as Fredholm as possible”. One can also immediately see that
(P,Q)
is Fredholm if and only if
(1-P,1-Q)
is Fredholm.

2.2
Lorentzian index theory

We use the same setting as
[
BH18
]
. As we mostly talk about abstract Fredholm pairs anyways, we keep our description of the Lorentzian setting brief and refer the interested reader to
[
BH18
, section 2]
for more detailed explanations. More generally, any setting that has a unitary evolution operator, allows the definition of APS boundary conditions and in which theorems
2.10
and
2.11
hold will work.

Setting 2.6
.

We assume the following Lorentzian setting:

•

M
is an even-dimensional spacially compact globally hyperbolic time-oriented Lorentzian spin manifold

•

\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
are smooth spacelike Cauchy hypersurfaces, with
\Sigma_{0}
in the past of
\Sigma_{1}

•

M_{0}
is the spacetime region between
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}

•

E
is a Hermitean vector bundle over
M
with a connection

•

S(M)\otimes E
is the associated twisted spinor bundle,
S^{R}(M)\otimes E
is the subbundle of right handed spinors

•

D
is the twisted Dirac operator acting on right handed spinor fields of the twisted bundle

•

Q\colon L^{2}(\Sigma_{0},S^{R}(M)\otimes E)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma_{1},S^{R}(M)\otimes E)
is the evolution operator associated to
D
, i.e. the unitary operator that maps
f|_{\Sigma_{0}}
to
f|_{\Sigma_{1}}
for
f\in\Ker(D)
.

•

A_{i}
is the twisted Dirac operator over the Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma_{i}
. We identify the twisted spinor bundle of
\Sigma_{i}
with the restriction of the right handed twisted spinor bundle of
M
using the past directed unit normal vector field to
\Sigma_{i}
.

We can equip the Dirac operator with various boundary conditions.

Definition 2.7
.

For Projections
P_{i}
in
L^{2}(\Sigma_{i},S^{R}(M)\otimes E)
Let
D_{P_{0},P_{1}}
be the Dirac operator with boundary conditions given by the ranges of
P_{i}
. This means we take (the closure of)
D
over
M_{0}
and restrict its domain to those spinor fields
\psi
such that
\psi|_{\Sigma_{i}}\in\Ran(P_{i})
.

The exemplary boundary conditions are given by the spectral projections of
A_{i}
:

Definition 2.8
.

Define the following spectral projections (with
\chi_{I}
denoting the characteristic function of an interval
I
):

P_{-}(0):=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A_{0})

and

P_{+}(1):=\chi_{(0,\infty)}(A_{1}).

The boundary conditions defined by these projections are known as Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) boundary conditions.

Remark 2.9
.

We can also allow
P_{-}(0)
and
P_{+}(1)
to be any finite rank perturbation of these projections. This will not affect any of the properties needed for our purposes. In particular, we could replace them by
\chi_{(-\infty,a)}(A_{0})
and
\chi_{(b,\infty)}(A_{1})
for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and still obtain the same results. Moreover, we could also interchange positive and negative spectral projections. Any change that preserves the statement of Theorem
2.11
will not affect any of the further considerations.

We shall use two crucial results about the Lorentzian setting, that are also essential in
[
BH18
]
. The rest will only be abstract considerations about Fredholm pairs.
Firstly, the index of
D_{P_{0},P_{1}}
can be expressed in terms of the boundary projections and the evolution operator:

Theorem 2.10
.

The operator
D_{P_{0},P_{1}}
is Fredholm with index
k
if and only if

(Q^{-1}P_{1}Q,1-P_{0})

is a Fredholm pair of index
k
.

This is essentially
[
BH18
, Theorem 4.2]
, using that the index of the pair of subspaces
(\Ran(P_{0}),Q\Ran(P_{1}))
as defined there is exactly the index of
(Q^{-1}P_{1}Q,1-P_{0})
.
Roughly speaking, this theorem means the Dirac operator is Fredholm if and only if the boundary projections are ”almost complementary” when related via time evolution.

Secondly, we need that the APS boundary conditions are ”as Fredholm as they can be”, i.e. the relevant projections have compact difference:

Theorem 2.11
.

The difference

1-P_{-}(0)-Q^{-1}P_{+}(1)Q

is a compact operator.

Remark 2.12
.

This means in particular that
(Q^{-1}P_{+}(1)Q,1-P_{-}(0))
and hence
D_{P_{-}(0),P_{+}(1)}
is Fredholm.

proof of Theorem
2.11
.

This is a fairly easy reformulation of
[
BS19
, Lemma 2.6]
, which states that

Q_{+-}:=P_{+}(1)QP_{-}(0)

and

Q_{-+}:=(1-P_{+}(1))Q(1-P_{-}(0))

are compact operators.
We have

\displaystyle 1-P_{-}(0)-Q^{-1}P_{+}(1)Q

\displaystyle=Q^{-1}(Q(1-P_{-}(0))-P_{+}(1)Q)

\displaystyle=Q^{-1}(P_{+}(1)Q(1-P_{-}(0))+(1-P_{+}(1))Q(1-P_{-}(0))

\displaystyle\ -(P_{+}(1)QP_{-}(0)+P_{+}(1)Q(1-P_{-}(0)))

\displaystyle=Q^{-1}(Q_{-+}-Q_{+-}).

As compact operators are an ideal, this is compact.
∎

3
Fredholm Pairs

We derive some further criteria for the Fredholmness of a pair, eventually leading to the characterization that we want. Throughout this section, let
P
and
P^{\prime}
be projections in a complex Hilbert space
H
. For later use, we first compute the difference of certain two-dimensional projections:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be the projection onto
\spann\{(a,b)\}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, for
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
q
be the projection onto
{\mathbb{C}}\times 0
. Then
\|p-q\|=\frac{|b|}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

This is the sine of the angle between the ranges of
p
and
q
.

proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Assume without loss of generality that
\|(a,b)\|^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}=1
, as the result is invariant under rescaling
(a,b)
.
The Matrix representation of
p-q
is

p-q=(a,b)\otimes(a,b)-{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}}={\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}-1&ab\\
ab&b^{2}\end{pmatrix}}=b{\begin{pmatrix}-b&a\\
a&b\end{pmatrix}}.

As the last matrix (without prefactor) is unitary, the norm of this is given by
|b|
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.3

(two dimensional model)
.

In order to give the reader a better intuition of what is going on, we will look at what happens for direct sums of two dimensional real projections as above.
Assume for these remarks that
P=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}
and
P^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}^{\prime}
for
p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime}
projections on a line in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
canonically extended to projections in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
given by the same matrix. The pair will be Fredholm if and only if
\|p_{i}-p_{i}^{\prime}\|<1-\epsilon
for all but finitely many
i
(as in that case
\|P-P^{\prime}\|_{C}<1-\epsilon
). This means that any condition enforcing Fredholmness of
P-P^{\prime}
must prevent the ranges of
p_{i}
and
p_{i}^{\prime}
from coming arbitrarily close to being mutually orthogonal for all but finitely many
i
, i.e. the angle between these ranges should be bounded by a constant less than
\tfrac{\pi}{2}
.

For arbitrary projections, the condition ”for all but finitely many
i
” in the remark translates into finite codimension subspaces:

Lemma 3.4
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

The pair
({P^{\prime}},P)
is a Fredhollm pair.

2.

There is
\epsilon>0
and a closed subspace
V
of finite codimension such that we have
\|{P^{\prime}}x\|\geq\epsilon\|x\|
for
x\in\Ran(P)\cap V
and
\|Px\|\geq\epsilon\|x\|
for
x\in\Ran({P^{\prime}})\cap V
.

3.

There are closed finite codimension subspaces
V_{1}\subseteq\Ran(P)
and
V_{2}\subseteq\Ran({P^{\prime}})
(finite codimension in the respective range, not in
H
) and
\epsilon>0
such that we have
\|{P^{\prime}}x\|\geq\epsilon\|x\|
for
x\in V_{1}
and
\|Px\|\geq\epsilon\|x\|
for
x\in V_{2}
.

Proof.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
: By Proposition
2.5
,
(P^{\prime},{P})
is Fredholm if and only if
\|P-{P^{\prime}}\|_{C}<1
. This means that there is a closed
V
of finite codimension such that
\|P-{P^{\prime}}|_{V}\|<1-\epsilon
for some
\epsilon\geq 0
. For
x\in V\cap\Ran(P)
, we have

\|{P^{\prime}}x\|\geq\|Px\|-\|(P-{P^{\prime}})x\|\geq\|x\|-(1-\epsilon)\|x\|=\epsilon\|x\|.

For
x\in V\cap\Ran({P^{\prime}})
we proceed analogously.

2.\Rightarrow 3.
: Choose
V_{1}:=V\cap\Ran(P)
and
V_{2}:=V\cap\Ran({P^{\prime}})
. As intersecting with a subspace cannot increase the codimension and intersections of closed spaces are closed, these are closed, finite codimensional and have the desired properties.

3.\Rightarrow 1.
:
P_{P^{\prime}}
has finite dimensional kernel, as the kernel of
P
cannot intersect
V_{1}
non-trivially. As the kernel of
{P^{\prime}}
cannot intersect
V_{2}
non-trivially, we obtain that
{P^{\prime}}_{P}
has finite dimensional kernel. As this is the adjoint of
P_{P^{\prime}}
, the only thing left to show is that
P_{P^{\prime}}
has closed range.

Let
z_{n}
be a sequence in
\Ran(P_{P^{\prime}})
that converges to a limit
z\in\Ran(P)
. We need to show that
z\in P(\Ran(P^{\prime}))
. Assume without loss of generality that the
z_{n}
are non-zero. Choose
x_{n}\in\Ran({P^{\prime}})
orthogonal to
\Ker(P_{P^{\prime}})
such that
Px_{n}=z_{n}
(this can be obtained from an arbitrary preimage sequence by subtracting the projection to the kernel). Choose
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
in
\Ran({P^{\prime}})
such that
a_{n}\in V_{1}
,
b_{n}\bot V_{1}
and
a_{n}+b_{n}=x_{n}
.

claim:

x_{n}
is bounded.

Assume
x_{n}
were unbounded. Without loss of generality, assume that
\|x_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty
, otherwise restrict to a subsequence.
Px_{n}\rightarrow z
, so in particular
Px_{n}
is bounded. Thus we have

P\tfrac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\rightarrow 0.

As
\|a_{n}\|^{2}+\|b_{n}\|^{2}=\|x_{n}\|^{2}
by orthogonality, we know that
\tfrac{b_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
is bounded. Thus it has a convergent subsequence converging to some limit
\beta
, as the orthogonal complement of
V_{1}
in
\Ran({P^{\prime}})
is finite dimensional. By restricting to a subsequence, we may assume without loss of generality that
\tfrac{b_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
converges to
\beta
. Then

P\tfrac{a_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=P\tfrac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}-P\tfrac{b_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\rightarrow-P\beta.

In particular,
P\tfrac{a_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
is Cauchy. As
P
is bounded below on
V_{1}
,
\tfrac{a_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
is also Cauchy and converges to some limit
\alpha
, which must satisfy
P\alpha=-P\beta
. We obtain that

\tfrac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\rightarrow\alpha+\beta\in\Ker(P_{P^{\prime}}).

As
\tfrac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
are unit Vectors orthogonal to
\Ker(P_{P^{\prime}})
, this is a contradiction, which finishes the proof of the claim.

We now use a similar argument without normalization to conclude that the range is closed. We know that
x_{n}
and thus also
b_{n}
is bounded. As
b_{n}
is contained in a finite dimensional space, it has a convergent subsequence. Assume without loss of generality that
b_{n}
converges to a limit
b
. Thus

Pa_{n}=z_{n}-Pb_{n}\rightarrow z-b.

This means that
Pa_{n}
is Cauchy. As
P
is bounded below on
V_{1}
,
a_{n}
is Cauchy, so it converges to some limit
a
. By continuity,

P(a+b)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}Px_{n}=z.

Thus any
z
in the closure of
\Ran(P_{P^{\prime}})
is already in
\Ran(P_{P^{\prime}})
, i.e.
P_{P^{\prime}}
has closed range.
∎

Two projections of difference smaller than 1 can always be deformed into each other by a path of projections that does not increase the distance from one endpoint. The same can also be done with the Calkin norm instead of the full operator norm:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(P_{0},P_{1})
be a Fredholm pair of projections. Then there is a continuous path of projections
P(t)
such that
P(0)-P_{0}
is compact,
P(1)=P_{1}
and

\|P_{t}-P_{0}\|_{C}\leq\|P_{1}-P_{0}\|_{C}.

Proof.

We use the construction of
[
DSBW23
, Propositions 5.3.18 and 5.3.19]
in order to deform
P
to a compact perturbation of
S
. The path we use is the same as in
[
DSBW23
, Proposition 5.3.19]
. We repeat the construction and check that it satisfies the desired norm bounds.

Set
F(x):=1-2x
. Given two projections
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
with
\|P_{1}-P_{0}\|<1
, define a path by

\displaystyle W(P_{0},P_{1})(t):=

\displaystyle F^{-1}\Big((1+(F(P_{0})F(P_{1})-F(P_{1})F(P_{0}))\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t)\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t))^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot(F(P_{0})\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t)+F(P_{1})\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t))\Big).

It is shown in the proof of
[
DSBW23
, Propositions 5.3.18]
that this is a well-defined, continuous path of projections from
P_{0}
to
P_{1}
satisfying

\|W(P_{0},P_{1})(t)-P_{0}\|\leq\|P_{1}-P_{0}\|.

The proof works in an arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra.

Now let
(P_{0},P_{1})
be any Fredholm pair of projections.
Set
V_{0}:=\Ran(P_{0})\cap\Ker(P_{1})
and
V_{1}:=\Ker(P_{0})\cap\Ran(P_{1})
and define
V^{\prime}:=(V_{0}\oplus V_{1})^{\bot}
. As the
V_{i}
either lie in the image or the kernel of the
P_{i}
, the restrictions of the
P_{i}
to
V^{\prime}
are projections in
V^{\prime}
. Moreover, as we removed the
\pm 1
-Eigenspaces of
P_{1}-P_{0}
and
\pm 1
were not in the essential spectrum of
P_{1}-P_{0}
, we have

\|P_{1}-P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}}\|<1.

Define

P(t):=W(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}},P_{1}|_{V^{\prime}})\oplus 0_{V_{0}}\oplus 1_{V_{1}}.

As
W(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}},P_{1}|_{V^{\prime}})
is a continuous path of projections, so is
P(t)
. We also have
P(1)=P_{1}
and
P(0)-P_{0}=0_{V^{\prime}}\oplus 1_{V_{0}}\oplus-1_{V_{1}}
is compact.It remains to check the norm bound.

Let
\pi
denote the projection onto the Calkin algebra. For operators on
V^{\prime}
, define

\tilde{\pi}(T):=\pi(T\oplus 0_{V_{0}\oplus V_{1}}).

As
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
are finite dimensional, we get the same result when extending with something other than zero. Moreover,
\tilde{\pi}
is a
*
-homomorphism, so everything used to construct

is preserved. We get

\displaystyle\|P(t)-P_{0}\|_{C}

\displaystyle=\|\pi(P(t)-P_{0})\|

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\pi}(W(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}},P_{1}|_{V^{\prime}})(t)-P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}})\|

\displaystyle=\|W(\tilde{\pi}(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}}),\tilde{\pi}(P_{1}|_{V^{\prime}}))(t)-\tilde{\pi}(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\pi}(P_{1}|_{V^{\prime}})-\tilde{\pi}(P_{0}|_{V^{\prime}})\|

\displaystyle=\|\pi(P_{1})-\pi(P_{0})\|

\displaystyle=\|P_{1}-P_{0}\|_{C}.

∎

We want to show that a pair of projections is Fredholm if both projections are not too far away from some reference projection
S
.
Thus we let
S
be another projection and let
R:=P-S
and
R^{\prime}:=P^{\prime}-S
denote the differences from this. In our application,
S
will correspond to one of the APS boundary projections.

Proposition 3.6
.

If

\|R\|_{C}^{2}+\|R^{\prime}\|_{C}^{2}<1,

then
(P,P^{\prime})
is Fredholm and

\ind(P,P^{\prime})=\ind(P,S)+\ind(S,P^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
be small enough such that

\|R\|_{C}^{2}+\|R^{\prime}\|_{C}^{2}<(1-\epsilon)^{2}.

Choose a finite codimension subspace
V
such that

\|R|_{V}\|^{2}+\|R^{\prime}|_{V}\|^{2}<(1-\epsilon)^{2}.

Let
x\in V\cap\Ran(P)
with
\|x\|=1
. By the parallelogram identity, we have

\|Rx+R^{\prime}x\|^{2}+\|Rx-R^{\prime}x\|^{2}=2(\|Rx\|^{2}+\|R^{\prime}x\|^{2})<2(1-\epsilon)^{2}.

Thus we must have either

\|Rx-R^{\prime}x\|^{2}<(1-\epsilon)^{2}

or

\|Rx+R^{\prime}x\|^{2}<(1-\epsilon)^{2}.

In the first case, we have

(1-\epsilon)\|x\|>\|Rx-R^{\prime}x\|=\|Px-P^{\prime}x\|\geq\|Px\|-\|P^{\prime}x\|=\|x\|-\|P^{\prime}x\|,

in the second case, we have

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)\|x\|

\displaystyle>\|Rx+R^{\prime}x\|

\displaystyle=\|(P+P^{\prime}-2S)x\|

\displaystyle=\|(1-2S)x+P^{\prime}x\|

\displaystyle\geq\|(1-2S)x\|-\|P^{\prime}x\|

\displaystyle=\|x\|-\|P^{\prime}x\|,

as
1-2S
is unitary.
In either case, we can conclude
\|P^{\prime}x\|>\epsilon\|x\|
. The same works with
P
and
P^{\prime}
interchanged (for
x\in V\cap\Ran(P^{\prime})
). Thus the pair is Fredholm by Lemma
3.4
.

It remains to show the second part of the claim. Let
P(t)
be a path (as in Lemma
3.5
) of projections such that
P(1)=P
,
P(0)-S
is compact and

\|P(t)-S\|_{C}\leq\|P-S\|.

Then
(P(t),S)
is a Fredholm pair and we have

\|P(t)-S\|_{C}^{2}+\|P^{\prime}-S\|_{C}^{2}\leq\|R\|_{C}^{2}+\|R^{\prime}\|_{C}^{2}<1,

Without loss of generality (WLOG) we may assume

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume that
H=Y_{i}\oplus Z_{i}
for
i=0
and
i=1
, such that
1-S_{1}-S_{0}
is compact, where

denotes the projection onto

. Let

. Let
P_{i}
be the projection onto the Graph of
G_{i}

1.

(P_{1},1-P_{0})
is Fredholm.

2.

\ind(P_{1},1-P_{0})=\ind(S_{1},1-S_{0})

Proof.

1.

Let
a_{i}:=\|G_{i}\|_{C}
. By Lemma
3.8
,

\|P_{i}-S_{i}\|_{C}\leq\frac{a_{i}}{\sqrt{1+a_{i}^{2}}},

\|P_{1}-S_{1}\|_{C}^{2}+\|P_{0}-S_{0}\|_{C}^{2}\leq\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{1+a_{1}^{2}}+\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{1+a_{0}^{2}}.

The condition that this be less than 1 can now be reformulated via the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\ \ \frac{a_{1}^{2}}{1+a_{1}^{2}}+\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{1+a_{0}^{2}}<1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ a_{1}^{2}(1+a_{0}^{2})+a_{0}^{2}(1+a_{1}^{2})<(1+a_{1}^{2})(1+a_{0}^{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ a_{1}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}a_{0}^{2}+a_{0}^{2}+a_{0}^{2}a_{1}^{2}<1+a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}+a_{0}^{2}a_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ a_{1}^{2}a_{0}^{2}<1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ \|G_{0}\|_{C}\|G_{1}\|_{C}=a_{1}a_{0}<1

\displaystyle\|P_{1}-S_{1}\|_{C}^{2}+\|(1-P_{0})-S_{1}\|_{C}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{1}-S_{1}\|_{C}^{2}+\|(1-P_{0})-(1-S_{0})\|_{C}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{1}-S_{1}\|_{C}^{2}+\|P_{0}-S_{0}\|_{C}^{2}

\displaystyle<1.

3.6

is Fredholm.

2.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a projection in

\ind(D_{P_{0},P_{1}})=\ind(D_{APS})+\ind(P_{0},P_{-}(0))+\ind(P_{1},P_{+}(1)).

Proof.

As

, we have

\|P_{1}-P_{+}(1)\|_{C}=\|Q^{-1}P_{1}Q-Q^{-1}P_{+}(1)Q\|_{C}=\|Q^{-1}P_{1}Q-(1-P_{-}(0))\|_{C}.

Moreover, we have

\|P_{0}-P_{-}(0)\|_{C}=\|(1-P_{0})-(1-P_{-}(0))\|_{C}.

The condition is thus equivalent to

\|Q^{-1}P_{1}Q-(1-P_{-}(0))\|_{C}^{2}+\|(1-P_{0})-(1-P_{-}(0))\|_{C}^{2}<1.

Applying Theorem
3.6
, this means that

(Q^{-1}P_{1}Q,1-P_{0})

2.10
, this means that

D_{P_{1},P_{2}}

\displaystyle\ind(D_{P_{0},P_{1}})

\displaystyle=\ind(P_{0},P_{-}(0))+\ind(D_{APS})+\ind(P_{1},P_{+}(0))

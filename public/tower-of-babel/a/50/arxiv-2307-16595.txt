A note on Fujimoto’s uniqueness theorem with ( 2 ​ n + 3 ) (2n+3) hyperplanes
Kai Zhou

Abstract
Hirotaka Fujimoto proved in [Nagoya Math. J., 1976(64): 117–147] and [Nagoya Math. J., 1978(71): 13–24] a uniqueness theorem for algebraically non-degenerate meromorphic maps into ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}) sharing ( 2 ​ n + 3 ) (2n+3) hyperplanes in general position. The proof of Lemma 3.6 in [Nagoya Math. J., 1976(64): 117--147] is found to contain a mistake. This mistake can be corrected easily from a new point of view. This note explains the idea of the correction and provides a complete proof. † † 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 32H30, 20K15.

Theorem 1.1

Let

To state Fujimoto’s Lemma 3.6 in
[
2
]
, we need some preparations.

Let
G
be a torsion-free abelian group and let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G.
Denote by
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
the subgroup of
G
generated by
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}.
Since
G
is torsion-free, the subgroup
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
is free and is of finite rank. We denote by rank
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
the rank of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle.

The following notion is introduced by Fujimoto.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
q\geq r>s\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. A
q
-tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
of elements in
G
is said to
have the property

(P_{r,s})
if arbitrarily chosen
r
elements
\alpha_{l(1)},\dots,\alpha_{l(r)}
in
A
(
1\leq l(1)<\dots<l(r)\leq q
) satisfy the condition that, for any
s
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in\{1,\dots,r\},
there exist distinct indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
with
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}\neq\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}
such that

\alpha_{l(i_{1})}\cdot\alpha_{l(i_{2})}\cdots\alpha_{l(i_{s})}=\alpha_{l(j_{1})}\cdot\alpha_{l(j_{2})}\cdots\alpha_{l(j_{s})}.

We also need the following notation.

Notation
.

For elements
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{q},\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2},\dots,\tilde{\alpha}_{q}
in an abelian group
(G,\cdot),
we write

\alpha_{1}:\alpha_{2}:\dots:\alpha_{q}=\tilde{\alpha}_{1}:\tilde{\alpha}_{2}:\dots:\tilde{\alpha}_{q},

if there is an element
\beta\in G
such that
\alpha_{i}=\beta\tilde{\alpha}_{i}
for any

Now the Lemma 3.6 in
[
2
]
is stated as follows.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
s
and
q
be positive integers with
2\leq s<q\leq 2s.
Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G
that has the property
(P_{q,s}),
and assume that at least one
\alpha_{i}
equals the unit element
1
of
G.
Then

(i)

rank
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}=:t\leq s-1;

(ii)

if
t=s-1,
then
q=2s
and there is a basis
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{s-1}\}
of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
such that the
\alpha_{i}
are represented, after a suitable change of indices, as one of the following two types:

(A)

s
is odd and

\alpha_{1}:\alpha_{2}:\dots:\alpha_{2s}=1:1:\beta_{1}:\beta_{1}:\beta_{2}:\beta_{2}:\dots:\beta_{s-1}:\beta_{s-1};

(B)

\alpha_{1}:\alpha_{2}:\dots:\alpha_{2s}=1:1:\dots:1:\beta_{1}:\dots:\beta_{s-1}:(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{a_{1}})^{-1}:(\beta_{a_{1}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{2}})^{-1}:\dots:(\beta_{a_{k-1}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{k}})^{-1},

where
0\leq k\leq s-1
,
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k}\leq s-1,
and the unit element
1
appears
(s+1-k)
times in the right hand side.

Fujimoto’s proof of the above lemma essentially uses the assertion that, for any
q
-tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
of elements in a torsion-free abelian group
(G,+),
there exists a basis
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}\}
of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
with the following property: there are distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
and nonzero integers
l_{1},\dots,l_{t}
such that

\alpha_{i_{\tau}}=l_{\tau}\eta_{\tau},\quad 1\leq\tau\leq t.

Such a basis
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}\}
is called by Fujimoto an adequate basis for
A.
Unfortunately, adequate basis does not exist in general. This is illustrated by the following example.

Example 1.4
.

Consider the free abelian group
(\mathbb{Z}^{3},+).
Let

\alpha_{1}=(1,0,0),\quad\alpha_{2}=(1,1,0),\quad\alpha_{3}=(1,2,2),\quad\alpha_{4}=(1,2,5)

be four elements in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}.

Since

(0,1,0)=\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad(0,0,1)=-2\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}+2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},

we see that

\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}^{3}.

So, if there exists an adequate basis for
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}),
then one sees easily that there are distinct indices
i,j,k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
such that
\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}.
However, one verifies easily that
\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\}
is not a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
for any three distinct indices
i,j,k\in\{1,2,3,4\}.
This shows that adequate basis does not exist for
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}).

Fujimoto’s proof of Lemma
1.3
can be corrected easily from a new point of view. Roughly speaking, we take an “adequate basis” in
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and regard
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
as the ambient space. I shall explain more.

Let
(G,+)
be a torsion-free abelian group and let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G.
We regard the free abelian group
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
as a subgroup of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
which can be naturally regarded as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. Because the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
is generated by
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q},
there exist distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
t=\mbox{\rm rank}\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
such that
\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{t}}\}
is a basis of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.
Then we see that there exist positive integers
l_{1},\dots,l_{t}
such that, for every
j\in\{1,\dots,q\}\setminus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\},

\alpha_{j}=l(j,1)\cdot\frac{1}{l_{1}}\alpha_{i_{1}}+\dots+l(j,t)\cdot\frac{1}{l_{t}}\alpha_{i_{t}}

for suitable integers
l(j,1),\dots,l(j,t).

The above discussion shows the following.

Observation 1.5
.

Let
(G,+)
be a torsion-free abelian group and let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G.
We regard the free abelian group
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
as a subgroup of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.
There exist distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
t=\mbox{\rm rank}\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\},
positive integers
l_{1},\dots,l_{t}
and a basis
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}\}
of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q},
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{i_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=l_{\tau}\eta_{\tau},\quad 1\leq\tau\leq t,

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=l(j,1)\eta_{1}+\dots+l(j,t)\eta_{t},\quad j\in\{1,\dots,q\}\setminus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\},

where
l(j,1),\dots,l(j,t)
are integers.

A complete proof of Lemma
1.3
will be given in Section
2
. In the proof, we take a basis
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}\}
as in Observation
1.5
, and we regard the
\alpha_{i}
as elements in
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
whenever it is needed. The proof basically follows Fujimoto’s original strategy. But simplifications are made in some steps of Fujimoto’s strategy.

Observation 1.6
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
and
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\alpha}_{q})
be two

.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{t+k}
be elements in a torsion-free abelian group
(G,\cdot),
which can be represented as follows:

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{t},x_{t+1},\dots,x_{t+k})

\displaystyle\qquad=\big(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{t},(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{a_{1}})^{-1},(\beta_{a_{1}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{2}})^{-1},\dots,(\beta_{a_{k-1}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{k}})^{-1}\big),

,

,
1\leq a_{1}<\dots<a_{k}\leq t,
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{t}

For any

among

Proof of Lemma
1.3

Lemma
1.3
will be proved by the induction on

Consider first the case of
s=2.
In this case,
q=3
or
q=4.

When
q=3,
we claim that
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}.
Assume this is not true. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}.
Because
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\neq\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3},
by assumption, we see
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},
which implies
\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{2}.

Similarly, we get
\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{1}.
Then
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2},
which gives a contradiction. Thus
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=1
and
rank
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\}=0.
This shows the conclusions of Lemma
1.3
hold in this case.

Now assume
q=4.
It follows from Lemma
1.8
that there are distinct indices
i_{0},j_{0}\in\{1,\dots,4\}
such that
\alpha_{i_{0}}=\alpha_{j_{0}}.

Then by Observation
1.6
and considering the new 4-tuple
(\alpha_{i_{0}}^{-1}\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i_{0}}^{-1}\alpha_{4}),
we may assume, after a suitable change of indices, that
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=1.

By assumption, there are distinct indices
i,j\in\{1,\dots,4\}
with
\{i,j\}\neq\{1,2\}
such that

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}.
If
\{i,j\}\neq\{3,4\},

\alpha_{3}=1
or
\alpha_{4}=1.
If
\{i,j\}=\{3,4\},
then
\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{4}^{-1}.
So at least one of the following three cases occurs:

•

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})=(1,1,1,\beta);

•

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})=(1,1,\beta,1);

•

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})=(1,1,\beta,\beta^{-1}).

It follows that rank
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4}\}\leq 1,
and if rank
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4}\}=1,
then
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4})
is of the type (B).

Set

By Lemma

We may regard
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle

such that, the
\alpha_{i},
as elements in
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q},
are represented as follows:

\alpha_{i}=\eta_{1}^{l(i,1)}\cdot\cdots\cdot\eta_{t}^{l(i,t)},\quad 1\leq i\leq q,

(2.1)

where
l(i,1),\dots,l(i,t)
are integers and, for each

The rest of the proof is divided into the following two cases:

Case
(\alpha)

For every

the

(\beta)

There is some

The proof for the Case
(\alpha)

For each
1\leq\tau\leq t,

M_{\tau}:=\big\{i\in\{1,\dots,q\}\,|\,l(i,\tau)>0,\,l(i,\tau+1)=\dots=l(i,t)=0\big\}

Claim 2.2
.

For any subset
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{p}\}
of
\{0,1,\dots,t\}
with
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\dots<\tau_{p},

m_{\tau_{1}}+\dots+m_{\tau_{p}}\neq s.

Proof.

Assume on the contrary that
m_{\tau_{1}}+\dots+m_{\tau_{p}}=s
for some
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{p}
with
0\leq\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{p}\leq t.
Write

M_{\tau_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup M_{\tau_{p}}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}=:I.

Consider the
s
elements
\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{s}}.
By assumption, there exist distinct indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}=:J\neq I,
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot\alpha_{i_{s}}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot\alpha_{j_{s}}.

(2.3)

We regard the
\alpha_{i}
as elements in
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and substitute (
2.1
) into the above equation. Then we get

\eta_{1}^{l(i_{1},1)+\dots+l(i_{s},1)}\cdots\eta_{t}^{l(i_{1},t)+\dots+l(i_{s},t)}=\eta_{1}^{l(j_{1},1)+\dots+l(j_{s},1)}\cdots\eta_{t}^{l(j_{1},t)+\dots+l(j_{s},t)}.

For each
1\leq\tau\leq t,
the exponent of
\eta_{\tau}

We consider the following three cases.

\bullet\quad\tau_{1}\geq 1.

Let
\tau\in\{\tau_{p}+1,\dots,t\}.
Since

the exponent of
\eta_{\tau}
in the left hand side of (

For, if
J\cap M_{\tau}\neq\emptyset,
then the exponent of
\eta_{\tau}
in the right hand side of (

Then by observing the exponents of

in the both sides of (

J\cap(M_{\tau_{p}+1}\cup\cdots\cup M_{t})=\emptyset\quad\mbox{and}\quad J\supseteq M_{\tau_{p}}.

\alpha_{i}\,(i\in M_{\tau_{p}})

We see that

Then by

which also gives a contradiction.

In any case, we get a contradiction. Therefore the Claim

2.2

\displaystyle 2s\geq q

\displaystyle=m_{0}+m_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+m_{\sigma(p)}+(m_{\sigma(p+1)}+\cdots+m_{\sigma(t-r)})+r

\displaystyle\geq m_{0}+p\cdot m_{\sigma(p)}+2(t-r-p)+r

\displaystyle=m_{0}+p\cdot m_{\sigma(p)}+2t-2p-r

\displaystyle\geq m_{0}-2+(m_{\sigma(p)}-2)(p-1)+2s

\displaystyle\geq 2s.

,

2.2

\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{s-1}.
Assume first that
J^{\prime}\cap\{2s-1,2s\}=\emptyset.
By observing the exponents of
\eta_{i}
with
i\in\{1,a_{1}+1,a_{1}+2,\dots,s-2\},
we see

J^{\prime}\cap\{s-k+a_{1}+1,\,s-k+a_{1}+2,\dots,2s-k-1\}=\emptyset.

Then since
2s-k-1\not\in J^{\prime},
by observing the exponents of
\eta_{i}\,(2\leq i\leq a_{1}),
we see
J^{\prime}\cap\{s-k+2,\dots,s-k+a_{1}\}=\emptyset.
Thus by
\#J^{\prime}=\#I^{\prime},
we get
J^{\prime}=I^{\prime},
which is a contradiction. Therefore
J^{\prime}\cap\{2s-1,2s\}\neq\emptyset,
and as before, we get
J^{\prime}\supseteq\{2s-1,2s\}.
Since
l^{\prime}_{1}<0,
we see that the exponent of
\eta_{1}
in the right hand side is strictly less than that in the left hand side. We get a contradiction and thus (
2.15
) is proved.

By (
2.15
), we conclude that there is a subset
\Lambda
of
\{a_{k}+1,\dots,s-1\}
with
s-1\in\Lambda
such that

\alpha_{2s}=(\prod_{i\in\Lambda}\beta_{i})^{-1}.

Thus, suitably changing indices, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}:\alpha_{2}:\dots:\alpha_{2s}

\displaystyle=1:1:\dots:1:\beta_{1}:\dots:\beta_{s-1}:

\displaystyle(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{a_{1}})^{-1}:\dots:(\beta_{a_{k-1}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{k}})^{-1}:(\beta_{a_{k}+1}\cdots\beta_{a_{k+1}})^{-1},

where
1\leq k+1\leq s-1
,
1\leq a_{1}<\dots<a_{k}<a_{k+1}\leq s-1,
and
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{s-1}\}
is a basis of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{2s}\rangle.
This shows that
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{2s})
is of the type (B) and therefore we complete the proof of Lemma
1.3
.

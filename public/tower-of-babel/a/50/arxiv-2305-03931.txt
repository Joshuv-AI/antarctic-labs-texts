The Calogero–Bogoyavlenskii–Schiff breaking soliton equation: recursion operators and higher symmetries Thanks: The work of ISK was partially supported by the RSF Grant 21-71-20034.
I.S. Krasil’shchik, O.I. Morozov

Abstract
. We find two one-parametric families of recursion operators and use them to construct higher symmetries for the Calogero–Bogoyavlenskii–Schiff breaking soliton equation. Then we prove that the recursion operators from the first family pair-wise commute with respect to the Nijenhuis bracket (are compatible).

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the Calogero–Bogoyavlenskii–Schiff breaking
soliton equation (
CBS
),
[
4
,
14
]
. This 3D equation
depends on several numerical parameters and under a certain choice of them
is Lagrangian. It has a number of remarkable properties. First of all, it
admits Lax representations with either a non-removable real-valued
parameter
[
14
]
or a functional
parameter
[
4
]
. In
[
14
]
, this equation is obtained
by applying an integro-differential recursion operator of the KdV equation to
the symmetry
u_{y}
(see the footnote at
[
14
, p. 401]
), the
Painlevé property was checked, and the Hirota bilinear form was
presented and implemented to construct a two-soliton solution. An infinite
series of local conservation laws for
CBS
was derived via the
generalized Miura transformation in
[
4
]
.

In our paper, we revise some integrability properties of
CBS
via the
technique developed in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
. We construct a
Lax representation with two non-removable parameters and derive two families
of recursion operators for symmetries of
CBS
. Since this equation is
Lagrangian, the recursion operators are most probably Hamiltonian and
symplectic ones simultaneously, though we have no well-defined techniques to
check these properties for general nonlocal operators. Then we study the
action of the first family to the symmetries of
CBS
and obtain an
infinite collection of symmetries of increasing odd order. Furthermore, we
prove that the recursion operators from the first family pair-wise commute
with respect to the Nijenhuis bracket. Using this fact, we prove commutativity
of the above-mentioned family of symmetries.

We follow the definitions and notation from
[
8
,
9
,
10
]

and recall briefly the main definitions and notation
(Section
2
).

Coordinates

Let

The Jacobi bracket of

3.
The breaking soliton equation and its coverings

The Calogero–Bogoyavlenskii–Schiff breaking soliton equation, as it is
presented in
[
4
]
, belongs to the family of equations

(3.1)

su_{xxxy}-ku_{x}u_{xy}-mu_{y}u_{xx}+u_{xt}=0,

where
s\neq 0
,
k
, and
m
are real constants. Computations of symmetries
and cosymmetries of Equation (
3.1
) reveal the following inequivalent
cases: (a) the generic one, (b)
m=0
, (c)
m=k
, (d)
k=2m
. Below we
mainly deal with the last case which is the most interesting one. Note that
the equation is Lagrangian with the Lagrangian density

L=\frac{1}{2}(mu_{x}^{2}u_{y}-u_{x}u_{t}+su_{xx}u_{xy})

in this case. In what follows, we present (
3.1
) in the form

(3.2)

u_{tx}=2\,u_{y}\,u_{xx}+4\,u_{x}\,u_{xy}-u_{xxxy}.

Remark
1
.

The generic case is of no interest in our context. When
m=0
, the
equation acquires the form

su_{xxxy}-\frac{k}{2}(u_{x}^{2})_{y}+u_{xt}=0

and after the substitution
u_{x}=v
transforms to

(3.3)

sv_{xxy}-\frac{k}{2}(v^{2})_{y}+v_{t}=0

which is a rather straightforward 3D-generalization of the pKdV
equation. Finally, in the case (c) we have

su_{xxxy}-ku_{x}u_{xy}-ku_{y}u_{xx}+u_{xt}=(su_{xxy}-ku_{x}u_{y}+u_{t})_{x}=0,

i.e., we arrive to a generalization

(3.4)

su_{xxy}-ku_{x}u_{y}+u_{t}=0

of the KdV equation.

It is interesting to note that symmetry algebras of Equations (

and (
3.3

many other multi-dimensional equations.

We prove here that all the recursion operators

To simplify subsequent computations, we rewrite system (

,

whenever

the tangent equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}u_{tx}&=&2\,u_{y}\,u_{xx}+4\,u_{x}\,u_{xy}-u_{xxxy},\\

to Equation (

Direct computations.

Proposition 6.2
.

The shadow
S^{u}
given by Equation (
6.2
) admits a unique lift
to
W
.

Proof.

To begin with, let us recall two basis facts on
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}
:

(1)

the variables
q_{\sigma}
and
w
are odd of parity
1
, i.e., they
anti-commute;

(2)

the space
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}
is endowed with the nilpotent odd
vector field
\mathbf{X}
such that
\mathbf{X}(u_{\sigma})=q_{\sigma}

and
\mathbf{X}(q_{\sigma})=0
;

(3)

\mathbf{X}
is a symmetry of parity
1
that (anti)commutes with all the
other symmetries.

The lift of
S^{u}
has to be of the form

S=\sum_{\sigma}D_{\sigma}(S^{u})\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\sigma}}+\sum_{\sigma}D_{\sigma}(S^{q})\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{\sigma}}+S^{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w},

where summation is accomplished over all admissible
multi-indices
\sigma
. So, we must find expressions for
S^{q}

and
S^{w}
. But the first necessary step is to lift the field
\mathbf{X}
to
W
.

Step 1: lift of
\mathbf{X}
.
Applying
\mathbf{X}
to the first equation
in (
6.1
), one obtains

\mathbf{X}(w_{x})=D_{x}(\mathbf{X}w)=\mathbf{X}(2u_{x}q_{x})=2q_{x}q_{x}=0.

In a similar way,

\mathbf{X}(w_{y})=D_{y}(\mathbf{X}w)=\mathbf{X}(q_{t}+q_{xxy}-2u_{x}q_{y}-2u_{y}q_{x})=-2(q_{x}q_{y}+q_{y}q_{x})=0.

Thus,
D_{x}(\mathbf{X}(w))=D_{y}(\mathbf{X}(w))=0
and consequently,
\mathbf{X}(w)=0
.

Step 2: construction of
S^{q}
.
One has

S^{q}=S(q)=S(\mathbf{X}u)=-\mathbf{X}(S^{u})=-\mathbf{X}((2\gamma u_{x}-\mu u)q-\gamma q_{xx}+\gamma w)=-2\gamma q_{x}q,

i.e.,

.

Step 3: construction of
S^{w}
.
Let us apply
S
to the first
equation of (
6.1
):

S(w_{x})=D_{x}(S^{w})=S(2u_{x}q_{x})=2(D_{x}(S^{u})q_{x}+u_{x}D_{x}(S^{q})).

The fist summand in braces is

D_{x}((2\gamma u_{x}-\mu y)q-\gamma q_{xx}+\gamma w)q_{x}\\
=(2\gamma u_{xx}q+(2\gamma u_{x}-\mu y)q_{x}-\gamma q_{xxx}+2\gamma u_{x}q_{x})q_{x}=\gamma(2u_{xx}qq_{x}-q_{xxx}q_{x})

while the second one equals

u_{x}D_{x}(2\gamma qq_{x})=2\gamma u_{x}(q_{x}q_{x}+qq_{xx})=2\gamma u_{x}qq_{xx}.

Thus,

D_{x}(S^{w})=2\gamma(2u_{xx}qq_{x}-q_{xxx}q_{x}+2u_{x}qq_{xx})=2\gamma D_{x}(2u_{x}qq_{x}-q_{xx}q_{x}).

We omit similar, but quite lengthy computations of

Let us collect the results:

(6.3)

S^{u}=(2\gamma u_{x}-\mu y)q-\gamma q_{xx}+\gamma w,\quad S^{q}=2\gamma qq_{x},\quad S^{w}=2\gamma(2u_{x}q-q_{xx})q_{x},

and this finishes the proof.

7.
Concluding remarks

In late eighties, A.M. Vinogradov conjectured that multi-dimensional (
n>2
)
nonlinear PDEs generically do not admit higher local
symmetries. In
[
1
,
9
]
, we presented several counterexamples
to this statement, but these examples were quite specific: all the equations
had the form of the cotangent one
[
12
]
. Besides, these higher symmetries
were “standing-alone” ones, i.e., were not terms of infinite hierarchies.

In this paper that, we constructed a recursion operator for the equation of
breaking solitons and proved that it generates an infinite hierarchy of higher
symmetries that depend on an arbitrary function in
t
and proved that a
sub-hierarchy independent of
t
is commutative.

We also showed that if
CBS
is rewritten in the nonlocal evolutionary
form, like it is done in
[
4
]
, it admits two local symplectic
structures. Using these structures, one easily constructs recursion operators
for symmetries and cosymmetries and consequently for conservation laws.

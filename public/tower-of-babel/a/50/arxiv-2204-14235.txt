Permuting the roots of univariate polynomials whose coefficients depend on parameters
Alexander Esterov and Lionel Lang

Abstract
. We address two interrelated problems concerning permutation of roots of univariate polynomials whose coefficients depend on parameters. First, we compute the Galois group of polynomials φ ⁡ ( x ) ∈ ℂ ⁡ [ t 1 , ⋯ , t k ] ​ [ x ] \varphi(x)\in\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}][x] over ℂ ⁡ ( t 1 , ⋯ , t k ) \mathbb{C}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}) . Provided that the corresponding multivariate polynomial φ ⁡ ( x , t 1 , ⋯ , t k ) \varphi(x,t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}) is generic with respect to its support A ⊂ ℤ k + 1 A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k+1} , we determine the latter Galois group for any A A . Second, we determine the Galois group of systems of polynomial equations of the form p ⁡ ( x , t ) = q ⁡ ( t ) = 0 p(x,t)=q(t)=0 where p p and q q have prescribed supports A 1 ⊂ ℤ 2 A_{1}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2} and A 2 ⊂ { 0 } × ℤ A_{2}\subset\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z} respectively. For each problem, we determine the image of an appropriate braid monodromy map in order to compute the sought Galois group. Among the applications, we compute the Galois group of any rational function that is generic with respect to its support. We also provide general obstructions on the Galois group of enumerative problems over algebraic groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
BKK-enumerative problems

Recall that the
support
of a
n
-variate Laurent polynomial
f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
is the finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
consisting of tuples
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
such that
f
has a monomial of the form
cx_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
with
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Any collection
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}A:=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})}}
of finite subsets
A_{j}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
gives rise to the following enumerative problem. For any generic tuple
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}f:=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})}}
of Laurent polynomials
f_{j}
with support
A_{j}
, the Bernstein-Kouchnirenko-Khovanskii Theorem guarantees that the system of equations
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n}=0
has
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}N}}
distinct solutions in
({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n}
, where
N
is the mixed volume
\mathop{\rm MV}\nolimits(A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})
. Define the
space of conditions

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathscr{C}_{A}}}\subset\prod_{j}({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{A_{j}}
as the Zariski-open subset of systems
f
with
N
distinct solutions. Consider now the
incidence variety

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathscr{U}_{A}}}:=\left\{(x,f)\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n}\times\mathscr{C}_{A}:f_{1}(x)=\cdots=f_{n}(x)=0\right\}.

Then, the
A
-enumerative problem
is the degree
N
covering

(1)

\mathscr{U}_{A}\to\mathscr{C}_{A}

induced by the projection
({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n}\times\mathscr{C}_{A}\to\mathscr{C}_{A}
.

In the context of enumerative geometry, it is of interest to compute the monodromy group of the covering (
1
), see
[
SY21
]
for an overview. In particular, the latter monodromy group can be interpreted as a Galois group, according to Harris
[
Har79
]
. Therefore, we denote this group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}G_{A}}}
and refer to its computation as the
Galois
A
-problem
.

The most classical case is
n=1
and
A=\{0,1,\cdots,N\}
where
G_{A}
is the Galois group of the univariate polynomial
f(x):=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{N}x^{N}
over the field
\mathbb{C}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})
. Plainly, the group
G_{A}
is the full symmetric group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathfrak{S}_{N}}}
. For arbitrary
n
, consider the case where each
A_{j}
is the set of integer points of the standard simplex of size
N_{j}
. In other words, each
f_{j}
is an honest polynomial of degree
N_{j}
.
Again, the group
G_{A}
is the full symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
where
N=\prod_{j}N_{j}
.

In general, the tuple
A
can manifest two geometric defects that prevent
G_{A}
from being the full symmetric group. First, the tuple
A
can be
non-reduced
, that is when each subset
A_{j}
can be translated to a common strict sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For
n=1
, the support
A
of the polynomial
f
is non-reduced whenever
f(x)=g(x^{d})
for some other polynomial
g
and some integer
d>1
. The Galois group of
f
is necessarily imprimitive, see Section
1.2
. Second, the tuple
A
can be
reducible
, that is when
k
of the polynomials
f_{j}
depend only on
k
variables (in some appropriate coordinate system). The simplest instance of reducibility is
n=2
with
A_{2}\subset\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}
. In that case, the roots of the system
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{2}(x_{2})=0
are distributed among the collection of horizontal lines
f_{2}(x_{2})=0
, leading again to an imprimitive Galois group.

Remark
1.1
.

Non-reduced supports are also called lacunary in the literature, while reducible supports are also called triangular. Throughout the paper, we adopt the former terminology which is rather common in Khovanskii’s school. This terminology is motivated by the fact that a support is non-reduced (respectively reducible) if and only if the corresponding discriminant is non-reduced (respectively reducible).

In
[
Est19
]
, the first author showed that
G_{A}
is the full symmetric group, provided that
A
is free from the above defects. In
[
EL22b
]
, we studied tuples
A
that are non-reduced but irreducible,
i.e.
there is a strict sublattice in the background but no square subsystem. In that case, the Galois group is at most as large as an explicit wreath product, see Section
1.2
. In
[
EL22b
]
, we gave combinatorial criteria on
A
that ensure that
G_{A}
is equal to the latter wreath product, but also criteria that ensure that
G_{A}
is strictly smaller. Already for
n=2
, the group
G_{A}

is not known in general.

That the Galois group
G_{A}
is smaller than expected indicates that the corresponding enumerative problem possesses some intrinsic structure that we failed to uncover from the start. For instance, the set of roots of any univariate polynomial
f(x):=g(x^{d})
is invariant under multiplication by any
d^{th}
root of unity, while the solutions to any system
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{2}(x_{2})=0
are equi-distributed on the collection of horizontal lines defined by
f_{2}(x_{2})=0
. In each case, the corresponding Galois group has to preserve this internal structure of the set of solutions. Another wide category of enumerative problems carrying intrinsic structures is provided by Schubert calculus. We refer once again to
[
SY21
]
, and references therein.

The general goal of this paper is to make progress in the Galois
A
-problem and in particular to identify the missing intrinsic structure that would explain the unsatisfied expectations of
[
EL22b
]
. To do so, we consider two types of yet unsolved Galois
A
-problems, in the hope that the latter missing structure will reveal itself.
The first type of problem is a parametric version of the univariate
A
-enumerative problems considered in
[
EL21
]
and
[
EL22b
, Section 1.1]
. The second type concerns reducible systems in two variables, that is systems of the form
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{2}(x_{2})=0
. We determine the associated Galois group for both problems (see Section
2
) and uncover an additional intrinsic structure for arbitrary non-reduced tuples
A
(see Remark
3.3
). To achieve this, we take a wider perspective on the Galois
A
-problem, both for computational reasons and because there are other interesting problems within reach.

First, the covering (
1
) possesses an invariant that is finer than
G_{A}
, namely its braid monodromy group, that we denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{A}}}
. The group
B_{A}
is the image of the map
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{A})\to\pi_{1}\big(\UConf_{N}(({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n})\big)
induced by the map
\mathscr{C}_{A}\to\UConf_{N}(({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n})
sending a system of equations to its set of solutions. Here,
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\UConf_{N}(X)}}
denotes the configuration space of
N
unordered points on the topological space
X
. For
n=1
, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\UConf_{N}({\mathbb{C}}^{\star}))
is the braid group on
N
strands of
{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
and the group
B_{A}
has been computed in
[
EL21
]
. As far as we know, the case
n>1
remains unexplored although there are several motivations to study this problem. To start with, the computation of
B_{A}
leads to a solution to the Galois
A
-problem, since
G_{A}
is the image of
B_{A}
under the natural map
\pi_{1}(\UConf_{N}({\mathbb{C}}^{\star}))\to\mathfrak{S}_{N}
. Also, braid monodromy is a classical tool used to compute fundamental groups of complement to hypersurfaces, see
[
Lib21
]
and references therein. Eventually, the group
B_{A}
has functorial properties in
A
that
G_{A}
does not have, see
[
EL21
, Remark 1.1.(i)]
and Remark
5.3
. This functoriality greatly simplifies computations. We refer to the computation of
B_{A}
as the
braid
A
-problem
.

As a second variation of the Galois
A
-problem, one can ask for the computation of the monodromy of group of the restriction of the covering (
1
) over a connected subvariety
Y\subset\mathscr{C}_{A}
. An instance of such subvarieties arises from tuples
\hat{A}:=(\hat{A}_{1},\cdots,\hat{A}_{n})
,
\hat{A}_{j}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, whose projection under
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
A
. Choose a tuple of generic polynomials
f_{j}(x,t)
,
x\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{n}
,
t\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}
, with respective support
\hat{A}_{j}
.
The under-determined system
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n}=0
in
(x,t)
and supported at
\hat{A}
becomes a determined system supported at
A
once we assign a generic value for
t
. Denote
Y:=Y(\hat{A})\subset\mathscr{C}_{A}
the set of systems obtained in this way and denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}G_{\hat{A}}}}\subset G_{A}
the monodromy group of the restriction of (
1
) over
Y
. We refer to the computation of
G_{\hat{A}}
as the
Galois
\hat{A}
-problem
or as a
Galois
A
-problem with parameters
.
Since the group
G_{\hat{A}}
is again a Galois group, the latter problem fits into the inverse Galois problem. In particular, the computation of the Galois group of a rational function
p(x)/q(x)\in\mathbb{C}(x)
, whenever
p
and
q
are generic with respect to their respective support, amounts to a certain Galois
\hat{A}
-problem (see Remark
3.1
.3). This is also the case for the computation of the monodromy of rational curves in weighted projective planes, see
[
Lan20
, Theorem 3]
. To our knowledge, the only general statements about Galois
A
-problems with parameters are confined to tuples
\hat{A}
such that the coefficients of the polynomials
x\mapsto f_{j}(x,t)
are linear forms in
t
, see for instance
[
Coh80
]
. Under this linearity assumption, Zariski’s Theorem
[
Che75
]
allows to reduce Galois
A
-problems with parameters to the underlying Galois
A
-problem. In this paper, we solve all Galois
A
-problems with parameters in the case
n=1
.

Eventually, one can consider a combination of the two variations of the Galois
A
-problem, that is the computation of the braid monodromy group of the restriction of the covering (
1
) over a subvariety
Y\subset\mathscr{C}_{A}
. Again, the computation of this braid monodromy group enjoys computational advantages and relates to other interesting problems (see Remark
3.1
.2). This is the approach we will take to solve Galois
A
-problems with parameters in the case
n=1
.

1.2.
Imprimitive permutation groups and wreath products

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a finite set, with corresponding permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
. A subgroup
G\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
is imprimitive if its action by permutation on
\mathcal{N}
preserves a partition
\mathcal{N}=\sqcup_{y\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{N}_{y}
where
\mathcal{Q}
is a set with at least 2 elements and each subset
\mathcal{N}_{y}\subset\mathcal{N}
is non-empty. In particular, any element of
G
induces a permutation on the set
\mathcal{Q}
of blocks of the partition. As mentioned above, the Galois groups of the polynomial systems
f(x^{d})=0
and
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{2}(x_{2})=0
are imprimitive. Imprimitive groups associated to
A
-enumerative problems are often isomorphic to wreath products, whose definition we now recall.

Given an action
\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
of a group
\mathcal{P}
on the finite set
\mathcal{Q}
and another group
U
, the group
\mathcal{P}
acts on the group
U^{\mathcal{Q}}
by permutation of the coordinates. By definition, the
wreath product

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}H{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathcal{P}}}
of
H
with
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathcal{Q}
(and relative to the action
\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
) is the corresponding semi-direct product
H^{\mathcal{Q}}\rtimes\mathcal{P}
. In particular, this group is equal to
H^{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathcal{P}
, as a set. Throughout the paper, the group
\mathcal{P}
will either be a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
or a subgroup of a certain braid group
B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}
with a natural projection
B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}\to\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Consider the example
A=\{0,d,2d,\cdots,dN\}
for some integers
d\geqslant 2
and
N\geqslant 1
. Any univariate polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{A}
is of the form
g(x^{d})
where
g\in\mathbb{C}^{B}
and
B:=\{0,1,\cdots,N\}
. In particular, the set
\{f=0\}
is acted upon by the group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}U_{d}}}
of
d^{th}
roots of unity. Any permutation
\sigma
in the Galois group
G_{A}
is
U_{d}
-equivariant, in particular it preserves the partition of
\{f=0\}
into
U_{d}
-orbits. These orbits are in natural correspondence with the set
\mathcal{Q}:=\{g=0\}
through the covering
x\mapsto x^{d}
. It turns out that the Galois group
G_{A}
is isomorphic to the subgroup of
U_{d}
-equivariant permutations of
\{f=0\}
, see
[
EL22b
, Proposition 1.5]
. The latter group is itself isomorphic to the wreath product
U_{d}\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. To see this, fix a
U_{d}
-equivariant permutation
\sigma
of
\{f=0\}
. Its second factor in
U_{d}\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is the induced permutation on the set
\mathcal{Q}
of
U_{d}
-orbits of
\{f=0\}
. For the first factor
(U_{d})^{\mathcal{Q}}
, some choices are needed. Fix a reference point in each
U_{d}
-orbit of
\{f=0\}
, and thus identify each orbit with
U_{d}
itself. These identifications allow to encode the restriction of
\sigma
from one orbit to another as an element in
U_{d}
. It defines a map
\mathcal{Q}\to U_{d}
that is the first factor of
\sigma
in the wreath product. The reader can check that the composition of
U_{d}
-equivariant permutations translates to the group law of
U_{d}\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
via the latter identification.

The above example fits into a more general context. Consider a surjective morphism of finite degree
\psi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
between Abelian algebraic groups, with kernel
K\subset\mathcal{F}
.
For a finite subset
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{G}
, the finite subset
\mathcal{N}:=\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{Q})
is acted upon by
K
. The subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
consisting of
K
-equivariant permutations is isomorphic to
K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Such an isomorphism can be constructed exactly as above, by choosing a reference point in every
K
-orbit of
\mathcal{N}
.

In the context of the BKK-enumerative problem, the relevant example is

\psi:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n},(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1}^{a_{11}}\cdots x_{1}^{a_{1n}},\cdots,x_{1}^{a_{n1}}\cdots x_{1}^{a_{nn}})

with
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}.

A system
f=0
supported on a given tuple
B
induces a second system
f\circ\psi=0
whose support we denote
A
. The Galois group
G_{A}
consists of
K
-equivariant permutations and is therefore isomorphic to a subgroup of
K\,{\rm wr}_{\{f=0\}}\,G_{B}
.

Remark
1.2
.

Two different choices of reference points lead to different identifications of the
K
-equivariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
with
K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. These two identifications differ from each other by an isomorphism of
K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
that we now describe. The second choice of reference points corresponds to a tuple
(\xi_{y})_{y\in\mathcal{Q}}\in K^{\mathcal{Q}}
according to the first choice. The corresponding isomorphism of
K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is given by

\big((\zeta_{y})_{y\in\mathcal{Q}},\sigma\big)\mapsto\big((\zeta_{y}\cdot\xi_{y}^{-1}\cdot\xi_{\sigma(y)})_{y\in\mathcal{Q}},\sigma\big).

In particular, this isomorphism restricts on each factor
K^{\mathcal{Q}}\times\{\sigma\}
of
K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
to a translation on
K^{\mathcal{Q}}
by an element in the kernel of the map

\prod:K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}\to K,\quad\big((\zeta_{y})_{y\in\mathcal{Q}},\sigma\big)\mapsto\prod_{y\in\mathcal{Q}}\zeta_{y}.

As a consequence, for any subgroup
L\subset K
, the pullback of the subgroup
\prod^{-1}(L)\subset K\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
to the
K
-equivariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
does not depend on the choices made above. This observation will be relevant while studying one of the morphisms
\prod\in\{\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}},\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}},\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A},\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}\}
that we will meet later on.

2.
Main results

2.1.
The first problem

Fix a support
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\underline{A}}}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
\{0,N\}\subset\underline{A}\subset\{0,\cdots,N\}
for some integer
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}N}}\geqslant 0
. Fix another support
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}A}}\subset\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
whose projection to the first factor
\mathbb{Z}
is
\underline{A}
. We aim at computing the Galois group of a generic polynomial
\varphi(x,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{A}
over the field
\mathbb{C}(t)
, where
x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
and
t\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}
. In other words, we are interested in the monodromy group of the map

(2)

\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}:\varphi(x,t)=0\}\;\to\;({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}\,,\;\;\;(x,t)\;\mapsto\;t

which is a degree
N
covering over the complement to a Zariski-closed subset
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathscr{B}}}\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}
. This subset is classically referred to as the
bifurcation set
or the
discriminant
. Fix a base point
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}t_{0}}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}\setminus\mathscr{B}
and define
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{N}}}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}:\varphi(x,t_{0})=0\}
. As in Section
1
, we denote by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}G_{A}}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
the monodromy group of the covering (
2
). In the above terminology, the computation of
G_{A}
amounts to a Galois
\underline{A}
-problem with parameters.

We will identify two geometric defects of
A
that prevent
G_{A}
from being the full symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
The first defect is captured by the largest integer
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}d}}:=d(A)
such that
\underline{A}\subset d\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\varphi(x,t)=\widetilde{\varphi}(x^{d},t)
for some other polynomial
\widetilde{\varphi}
, the set
\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}:\varphi(x,t)=0\}
is acted upon by the group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}U_{d}}}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}:x^{d}=1\}
for any given
t\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}\setminus\mathscr{B}
. We denote by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
the subgroup of
U_{d}
-equivariant permutations,
i.e.
permutations
\sigma
for which
\sigma(\xi\cdot x)=\xi\cdot\sigma(x)
for any
\xi\in U_{d}
and
x\in\mathcal{N}
. Plainly, we have
G_{A}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
.

The second defect of
A
is captured by the integer
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\vartheta}}:=\vartheta(A)
that we now define. We say that
A
is
sharp
if the sets
A\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k})
and
A\cap(\{N\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k})
consists of a single point.
Writing

\varphi(x,t)=c_{0}(t)+c_{1}(t)x+\cdots+c_{N}(t)x^{N},

the support
A
is sharp if and only if
c_{0}(t)/c_{N}(t)=ct_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{a_{k}}
for some
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
. Define
\vartheta:=\GCD(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
(with the convention that
\GCD(0,\cdots,0)=0
) and set
\vartheta=1
if
A
is not sharp.

The way
\vartheta
affects the Galois group
G_{A}
is seen through the map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}
that we now define.
Call a
representative
any subset
E\subset\mathcal{N}
that intersect each
U_{d}
-orbit of
\mathcal{N}
exactly once. Denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}x_{E}}}:=\prod_{x\in E}x
and define
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{P}}}:=\{x_{E}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}:E\text{ representative}\}
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is acted upon by
U_{d}
and consists of a single orbit. Observe moreover that for any representative
E
and any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
, the set
\sigma(E)
is again a representative. Therefore, we have the following surjective map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}}}\;:\;\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}&\to&\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}\\
\sigma&\mapsto&\big(x_{E}\mapsto x_{\sigma(E)}\big)\end{array}

where
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}}}
is the
U_{d}
-equivariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
consists of a single orbit, we have a canonical isomorphism between
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}
and the Abelian group
U_{d}
. In particular, the subset
(\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d})^{M}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}
of
M
-powers of elements in
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}
is a subgroup.
We prove the following.

Theorem
2.1
.

For any support
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k+1}
not contained in a line, the group
G_{A}
is the subgroup
\big(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}\big)^{-1}\big((\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}))^{\vartheta}\big)
of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
. In particular,
G_{A}
is the full symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
if and only if
d=1
, and it is the subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
if and only if
d
and
\vartheta
are coprime.

The support sets
A
for which
G_{A}
is the full symmetric group can also be described via the study of spatial symmetric curves, see
[
EL22a
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Let us briefly comment on Theorem
2.1
. Recall from Section
1.2
that the group
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
is non-canonically isomorphic to the wreath product
U_{d}{\rm\,wr\,}\mathfrak{S}_{N/d}
. Via this identification, the map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}
reads as

(3)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}U_{d}{\rm\,wr\,}\mathfrak{S}_{N/d}&\to&U_{d}\\
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N/d},\sigma)&\mapsto&\prod_{j}\xi_{j}\end{array}.

In particular, Theorem
2.1
states that
G_{A}
is isomorphic to

(4)

\left\{(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N/d},\sigma)\in U_{d}{\rm\,wr\,}\mathfrak{S}_{N/d}:\prod(\xi_{j})^{g}=1\right\}.

where
g:=d/\GCD(d,\vartheta)
.
Although the isomorphism between
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
and
U_{d}{\rm\,wr\,}\mathfrak{S}_{N/d}
is not canonical, the description (
4
) of
G_{A}
does not depend on the choice of the latter isomorphism, see Remark
1.2
. To see why
G_{A}=\big(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}\big)^{-1}\big((\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}))^{\vartheta}\big)
, observe that when
t
travels along a loop in
({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{k}\setminus\mathscr{B}
, the product of the roots of
\widetilde{\varphi}(x,t)
closes a loop in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
. The map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}
records the reduction
mod\,d
of the winding number of the latter loop. That this reduction is a multiple of
\vartheta
follows from Vieta’s formula, see Section
4.1
.

In order to prove the above theorem, we compute the braid monodromy group of the covering (
2
). Recall that
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\UConf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})}}
is the
unordered configuration space
of
N
distinct points in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
and denote

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}}}:=\pi_{1}(\UConf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*}),\mathcal{N})
.
The map
\mathbb{C}^{k}\setminus\mathscr{B}\rightarrow\UConf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
that associates to every tuple
t
the set of roots of
\varphi(x,t)
induces at the level of fundamental groups the map that we denote

(5)

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mu^{br}_{\varphi}}}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{k}\setminus\mathscr{B},t_{0})\rightarrow B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}.

Since the group
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
is known as the
braid group
of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
with
N
strings, see
[
FN62
]
, we will refer to
\mu^{br}_{\varphi}
as the
braid monodromy map
associated to
\varphi
. Since
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}^{A}
is generic, the image of
\mu^{br}_{\varphi}
depends only on
A
. We denote it by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{A}}}
and refer to it as the
braid monodromy group
of
A
.

Denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\pi_{\mathcal{N}}}}:B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
the natural projection. The composition of
\mu^{br}_{\varphi}
with
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}
leads to the
monodromy map

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mu_{\varphi}}}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{k}\setminus\mathscr{B},\underline{t}_{0})\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}

of the covering (
2
). In particular, we have that
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}(B_{A})=G_{A}
.

In order to describe
B_{A}
, there are 2 types of subgroups of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
of interest. First, recall that
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
is acted upon by
U_{d}
, since
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
is. Following
[
EL21
]
, we denote by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{\mathcal{N},d}^{*}}}\subset B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
the corresponding invariant subgroup.

For the second type of subgroup, consider the multiplication map
\UConf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
given by
c\mapsto\prod_{x\in c}x
, and denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}x_{0}}}
the image of
\mathcal{N}
. We denote by

(6)

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}}}:B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*},x_{0})\simeq\mathbb{Z}

the induced map at the level of fundamental groups. Define
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}R^{\vartheta}_{\mathcal{N}}}}:=\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}(\vartheta\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem
2.2
.

For any support
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k+1}
not contained in a line, the group
B_{A}
is the subgroup
B_{\mathcal{N},d}^{*}\cap R^{\vartheta}_{\mathcal{N}}
of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
.

Theorem
2.1
will come as a consequence of Theorem
2.2
. In Section
4.5
, we will use a commutative diagram

\textstyle{B_{\mathcal{N},d}^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{\mathcal{N}}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}}
\textstyle{\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*},x_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}}

to show that the image under
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}
of
B_{\mathcal{N},d}^{*}\cap R^{\vartheta}_{\mathcal{N}}
is the subgroup
(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}})^{-1}\big((\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},d}))^{\vartheta}\big)
of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},d}
.

Remark
2.3
.

If the support set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k+1}
is contained in a line, then it is sharp and
\varphi(x,t)
is equal to
\widetilde{\varphi}(x^{d}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{a_{k}})
for some non-singular univariate polynomial
\widetilde{\varphi}
. The integer
\vartheta
equals
N\cdot\GCD(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
and the bifurcation set
\mathscr{B}
is the union of the coordinate hyperplanes
\{t_{j}=0\}
for which
a_{j}\neq 0
. The image of
\mu^{br}_{\varphi}
is the cyclic group generated by
\tau^{\vartheta}
where
\tau
is defined in Figure
1
. The corresponding Galois group is isomorphic to the cyclic group
\vartheta\cdot(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})
.

2.2.
The second problem

The second problem we consider is the Galois
A
-problem for reducible pairs
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}A}}:=(A_{1},A_{2})
,
A_{j}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Using a harmless affine linear change of coordinates in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we can assume that
A_{2}\subset\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}
and that the projection
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\underline{A}_{1}}}\subset\mathbb{Z}
of
A_{1}
onto the first factor satisfies
\{0,n\}\subset\underline{A}_{1}\subset\{0,\cdots,n\}
for some integer
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}n}}\geqslant 1
.
Geometrically, we are interested in the monodromy group of the covering

(7)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\{\big((x,y),(p,q)\big)\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{2}\times\mathscr{C}_{A}:\;p(x,y)=q(y)=0\right\}&\to&\mathscr{C}_{A}\\
\big((x,y),(p,q)\big)&\mapsto&(p,q)\end{array}

where
\mathscr{C}_{A}
is the
space of conditions
defined previously. As before, we denote by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}G_{A}}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
the monodromy group of the covering (
7
).

Similarly to the first problem, we will deduce
G_{A}
from a certain braid monodromy group associated to the covering (
7
).
In order to define the underlying braid monodromy map, we fix a base point
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}(p_{0},q_{0})}}
in
\mathscr{C}_{A}
, define the set
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{N}}}:=\{(x,y)\in(({\mathbb{C}}^{\star}))^{2}:p_{0}(x,y)=q_{0}(y)=0\}
and denote its cardinality by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}N}}
. Then, denote by
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathscr{U}_{A}}}
the subset of
\UConf_{N}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2})
consisting of point configurations
of the form

(8)

\{(x,y)\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{2}:q(y)=0,\;p_{y}(x)=0\}

for some non-singular polynomials
q\in\mathbb{C}^{A_{2}}
and
p_{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{\underline{A}_{1}}
,
y\in\{q=0\}
. Plainly, configurations of the form
\{p(x,y)=q(y)=0\}
, with
(p,q)\in\mathscr{C}_{A}
, are contained in
\mathscr{U}_{A}
. Thus, we have a map
\mathscr{C}_{A}\to\mathscr{U}_{A}
,
(p,q)\mapsto\{p=q=0\}
. The induced map at the level of fundamental groups is our
braid monodromy map

(9)

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mu^{br}_{A}}}\;:\;\pi_{1}\big(\mathscr{C}_{A},(p_{0},q_{0})\big)\rightarrow B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}

where
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}}}:=\pi_{1}(\mathscr{U}_{A},\mathcal{N})
.
The image of
\mu^{br}_{A}
, that we denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{A}}}
, is our
braid monodromy group
. The natural projection
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\pi_{\mathcal{N}}}}:B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
maps
B_{A}
onto the sought Galois group
G_{A}
.

The description of
B_{A}
inside
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
relies on a second system of equations constructed from the initial system
p(x,y)=q(y)=0
. If we write
p(x,y)\;=\;c_{0}(y)+c_{1}(y)x+\cdots+c_{n}(y)x^{n}
, then the second system is

(10)

c_{n}(y)x-(-1)^{n}c_{0}(y)=0\,,\quad q(y)=0.

The system (
10
) relates to the initial system as follows. If we denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}h}}
the degree of
q
, then Vieta’s formula implies that the map

(11)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathscr{U}_{A}&\to&\UConf_{h}(({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{2})\\
\{(x,y):q(y)=p_{y}(x)=0\}&\mapsto&\left\{\big(\prod_{x\in\{p_{y}=0\}}x,y\big):q(y)=0\right\}\end{array}

sends the set
\{p(x,y)=q(y)=0\}
to the set of solutions to (
10
).

Denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\nu(A)}}:=({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\nu(A_{1})}},A_{2})
the reducible support of the system (
10
). We define its braid monodromy group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{\nu(A)}}}
exactly as we defined
B_{A}
. In particular, the map (
11
) takes values in the set
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathscr{U}_{\nu(A)}}}
of configurations in
\UConf_{h}(({\mathbb{C}}^{\star})^{2})
that are equi-distributed among the
h
many connected components of
\{(x,y):q(y)=0\}
for some non-singular
q\in\mathbb{C}^{A_{2}}
, and
B_{\nu(A)}
is a subgroup of
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}}}:=\pi_{1}(\mathscr{U}_{\nu(A)},\mathcal{Q})
where
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{Q}}}
the image of
\mathcal{N}
under (
11
). The map (
11
) induces at the level of fundamental groups the surjective map that we denote

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}}}:B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}\to B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}.

We prove the following.

Theorem
2.4
.

For any pair
A:=(A_{1},A_{2})
such that
A_{2}
is contained in a line and
A_{1}
is not, the braid monodromy group
B_{A}
is the subgroup
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{-1}\big(B_{\nu(A)}\big)
of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
.

According to the above theorem, the explicit map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}
reduces the description of
B_{A}
to the description of the smaller group
B_{\nu(A)}
. We describe explicitly the groups
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
,
B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}
and
B_{\nu(A)}
in Remark
3.2
.1.

The Galois group
G_{A}
will admit a description similar to the one of
B_{A}
. To see this, recall that the Galois group
G_{A_{2}}
of the univariate polynomial
q
, as considered in
[
EL22b
, Section 1.1]
, acts by permutation on the blocks of the partition of
\mathcal{N}
among the horizontal lines
{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}\times\{y\}
,
y\in\mathcal{Q}
. Moreover, for
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}d}}:=d(\underline{A}_{1})
, the action
\xi\cdot(x,y):=(\xi x,y)
of
U_{d}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
preserves the set
\mathcal{N}
. Define
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}}}
as the intersection of the
G_{A_{2}}
-invariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
with the
U_{d}
-equivariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
is non canonically isomorphic to the wreath product
G_{\underline{A}_{1}}\,{\rm wr}_{\mathcal{Q}}\,G_{A_{2}}
, where
G_{\underline{A}_{1}}
is the Galois group of the univariate polynomials
p_{y}
from (
8
). Plainly, we have that
G_{A}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
.

The description of
G_{A}
inside
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
relies on another system of equations constructed from the initial system
p(x,y)=q(y)=0
, namely the system

(12)

c_{n}(y)x^{d}-(-1)^{n/d}c_{0}(y)=0\,,\quad q(y)=0.

The system (
12
) relates to the initial system as follows. Denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{N}_{y}}}:=\mathcal{N}\cap({\mathbb{C}}^{\star}\times\{y\})
for any
y\in\mathcal{Q}
and call a
representative
any subset
E\subset\mathcal{N}_{y}
that intersect each
U_{d}
-orbit of
\mathcal{N}_{y}
exactly once.
Denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}x_{E}}}:=\prod_{x\in E}x
and define

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathcal{P}}}:=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}\times\mathcal{Q}:\,x=x_{E}\text{ for some representative }E\subset\mathcal{N}_{y}\}.

Vieta’s formula implies that the set
\mathcal{P}
, constructed from the set of solutions
\mathcal{N}
to the initial system, is the set of solutions to (
12
).

Plainly, the subset
\mathcal{P}
is acted upon by
U_{d}
. We denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},A}}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
the corresponding
U_{d}
-equivariant subgroup. For any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
, denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathcal{Q}$}}}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
the induced permutation on the blocks
\mathcal{N}_{y}\subset\mathcal{N}
,
y\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Then, we have a surjective map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}}}\;:\;\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}&\to&\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},A}\\
\sigma&\mapsto&\big((x_{E},y)\mapsto(x_{\sigma(E)},\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathcal{Q}$}}(y))\big)\end{array}.

The map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}
is a well defined group morphism. Indeed, for any representative
E\subset\mathcal{N}_{y}
and any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
, the subset
\sigma(E)
is a representative of
\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathcal{Q}$}}(y)
. Moreover, for any two representatives
E,\,E^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{N}_{y}
such that
x_{E}=x_{E^{\prime}}
, then
x_{\sigma(E)}=x_{\sigma(E^{\prime})}
.

We denote
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\nu_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}(A)}}:=({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\nu_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}(A_{1})}},A_{2})
the reducible support of the system (
12
) and define its Galois group
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}G_{\nu_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}(A)}}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},A}
exactly as we defined
G_{A}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N},A}
. We prove the following.

Theorem
2.5
.

For any pair
A:=(A_{1},A_{2})
such that
A_{2}
is contained in a line and
A_{1}
is not, the Galois group
G_{A}
is the subgroup
(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}})^{-1}\big(G_{\nu_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}(A)}\big)
of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{P},A}

According to the above theorem, the explicit map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\mathfrak{S}$}}
reduces the description of
G_{A}
to the description of the smaller group

that we provide in Remark

2.
There are at least 2 reasons to consider the braid monodromy map

rather than

. First, it is not possible to deduce the case

3.
Theorem
2.1
allows to determine the Galois group of any rational function

, provided that

and

are generic with respect to their support. Indeed, the monodromy group of the latter function is the image of

4.

The case of rational functions justifies the genericity assumption in Theorems
2.2
and
2.1
. Indeed, Galois groups of such functions come in many more shapes than the ones appearing in Theorem

, see for instance

5.
Theorem
2.2

[
EL21

in which we studied the braid monodromy of the universal univariate polynomial supported on

, the braid monodromy of the latter univariate polynomial is the image of

for a generic polynomial

with support

homotopy group of the complement to (quasi) projective hypersurface with the

homotopy group of the complement of its pullback under a linear map. It is natural to investigate what happens when linear maps are replaced with arbitrary polynomial maps. Theorem
2.1

homotopy group with the polynomial map

and the hypersurface

from part

6.
To our knowledge, Galois groups

were unknown except for supports of the form

as in part

and references therein. In this context, the appearance of groups of the form (

7.
Theorem
2.1
shows that there might be a discrepancy between the sectional monodromy group of a given smooth projective curve and the monodromy of linear subspaces of sections. Indeed, according to Theorem

8.
To prove Theorem

We take

.

and

.

4.3.1.
Braids and their diagrams

Braids in
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
can be represented by braid diagrams similarly to the elements of the
Artin braid group

B_{\mathcal{N}}:=\pi_{1}(\UConf_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{N})
. Braids live in the 3-dimensional space
\mathbb{C}\times{\rm timeline}
and their diagram are obtained by a real linear projection on
\mathbb{R}\times{\rm timeline}
, see for instance
[
FM11
, Chapter 9.1.1]
. Here, we associate to any braid in
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
a braid diagram using the argument map
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\arg}}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow S^{1}
as our projection, as done in
[
EL21
, Section 2.1]
.

Precisely, a braid
\beta\in B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
is an isotopy class of continuous map
\beta:[0,1]\rightarrow\Conf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})

into the configuration space
\Conf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
such that
\beta(0)=\beta(1)=\mathcal{N}
. The argument map
\arg:\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow S^{1}
defines a map from the open dense subset
U\subset\Conf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
of configurations with pairwise distinct arguments to
\Conf_{N}(S^{1})
. If
C\subset\Conf_{N}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
is the complement of
U
, we can ensure, using an isotopy if necessary, that exactly
2
points of
\beta(s)
have common argument whenever
\beta(s)\in C
. At such meeting point of the projection under
\arg
of
2
strands of
\beta
, we can make sense of which of them has smaller modulus in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
than the other. We choose to represent the strand with smaller modulus as crossing over the other strand. This way, we can represent the element
\beta
by the corresponding diagram in
S^{1}\times[0,1]
picturing simultaneously the trajectories of the
N
points
\beta(s)
, keeping track of which strand is passing over which one at a crossing. In order to be able to draw braid diagrams in
S^{1}\times[0,1]
, we will choose a fundamental domain
[\theta,\theta+2\pi[
of
S^{1}
and draw the diagrams in
[\theta,\theta+2\pi[\times[0,1]
instead. Thus, strings in a diagram are allowed to hit boundary points
(\theta,s)
, disappear and reappear at
(2\pi+\theta,s)
.

In Figure
1
, we represent the braid diagrams of some useful elements of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
. From the braids
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}b_{1}}}
and
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\tau}}
, we define
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}b_{j}}}:=\tau^{j-1}b_{1}\tau^{-j+1}
,
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
. By a slight abuse of language, we will identify braids with their diagrams and therefore view braid diagrams as elements of the relevant braid group.

Figure 1.
The braid diagrams of
b_{1}
,
b_{N}
and
\tau
in
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
.

Lemma
4.5
.

The elements
b_{1},\cdots,b_{N}\in B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
generates
\Ker(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}})
.

Proof.

Plainly, the elements
b_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, belong to
\Ker(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}})
. As shown in
[
EL21
, Lemma 2.2]
, the elements
b_{j}
generate
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
together with
\tau
. Moreover, any element of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
can be written as
b\tau^{k}
for some integer
k
where
b
is a word in the
b_{j}
’s. Indeed, the commutator
\tau b_{j}\tau^{-1}b_{j}^{-1}
equals the product
b_{j+1}b_{j}^{-1}
, or equivalently
\tau b_{j}=b_{j+1}\tau
. Eventually, since
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}(b\tau^{k})=k
, the element
b\tau^{k}
is in
\Ker(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}})
if and only if

. Thus, we have the inclusion
\Ker(\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}})\subset\left\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{N}\right\rangle
and the result follows.
∎

Corollary
4.7
.

Theorem
4.4
holds only if it holds for at least one trinomial in each orbits of the following group action

(p,q,d,\nu)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\{-1,1\},\quad(p,q,d,\nu)\cdot\varphi(x,t):=x^{p}t^{q}\varphi(x^{d},t^{\nu}).

In particular, it suffices to prove Theorem
4.4
for trinomials
\varphi
whose support set
A
is of either of the following types:

(1)

A=\left\{(0,0),(m,a),(n,b)\right\}
with
0<m<n
,
\GCD(m,n)=1
with
na-mb>0
,

(2)

A=\left\{(0,a),(0,b),(1,0)\right\}
with
a<b
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from Proposition
4.3
, Lemma
4.6
and the fact that the fibration
\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times(\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathscr{B}):\,\varphi(x,t)=0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathscr{B}
associated to
\varphi
is the same as the fibration associated to

.

For the second part, observe that the involutions
x\mapsto x^{-1}
and
t\mapsto t^{-1}
induce the symmetry along the first and second coordinate axis in the lattice of monomials
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The multiplication by
x^{p}t^{q}
induces the translation by
(p,q)
in the lattice of monomials.

If every vertical line intersects
A
in at most 1 point, then we can translate
A
so that
\{(0,0)\}\subset A\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
. Using the involution

If there is a vertical line intersecting

and
t\mapsto t^{-1}
, we can achieve

4.3.4.
The bifurcation set

From now until the end of Section
4.3.6
, we restrict to trinomials of type (1) according to the dichotomy given in Corollary
4.7
, that is
\varphi(x,t)=1+t^{a}x^{m}+t^{b}x^{n}
with
0<m<n
,
\GCD(m,n)=1
and
na-mb>0
.

Let us determine the bifurcation set
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathbb{C}
of
\varphi
. To that aim, consider for a moment the polynomial
f(x)=1+ux^{m}+vx^{n}
. Besides the obvious case
v=0
, the polynomial
f(x)
has strictly less than
n
roots if the triple
(u,v,x)
satisfies

(18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1+ux^{m}+vx^{n}&=0\\
mux^{m}+nvx^{n}&=0\end{array}\right.\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}m-nvx^{n}+mvx^{n}&=0\\
mux^{m}+nvx^{n}&=0\end{array}\right.\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}v&=\frac{m}{n-m}x^{-n}\\
u&=\frac{n}{m-n}x^{-m}\end{array}\right..

Therefore, the polynomial

is singular if and only if
v\big(\big(v\frac{n-m}{m}\big)^{m}-\big(u\frac{m-n}{n}\big)^{n}\big)=0
(this is because
\GCD(m,n)=1
).
It follows that the bifurcation set is defined by the equation
t^{b}\big(\big(t^{b}\frac{n-m}{m}\big)^{m}-\big(t^{a}\frac{m-n}{n}\big)^{n}\big)=0
. Therefore
\mathscr{B}
is the disjoint union of

with the
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\delta}}:=na-mb

In particular, the points of
\mathscr{B}\setminus\{0\}
are equi-distributed on the circle
\{t\in\mathbb{C}:\,|t|=\rho\}
for some
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\rho}}:=\rho(a,b,m,n)>0
.

Lemma
4.8
.

The
\delta
many singular vertices of
\Arg_{\varphi}
have pairwise distinct
\nu
-coordinates.

Proof.

The set
\mathscr{S}\subset\Arg_{\varphi}
is the preimage under the covering
(\theta,\nu)\to(m\theta+a\nu,n\theta+b\nu)
from
S^{1}
to itself of the vertex
(0,\pi)
of
\Arg_{\ell}
. This is because the latter covering maps L- and R-geodesics respectively to
\{\nu=\pi\}
and
\{\theta+\nu=\pi\}
which intersect at
(0,\pi)
. Therefore,
\mathscr{S}
consists of
\delta
many points and it is equal, up to a translation, to the projection onto
(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
of the lattice

\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&-a\\
-n&m\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
Two points in that lattice have the same
\nu
-coordinate in
(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
if their difference, which is of the form

\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\big(\lambda\binom{b}{-n}+\mu\binom{-a}{m}\big)

2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, that is
\mu m-\lambda n=\kappa\delta
for
some
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
. The general solution to the latter equation is the sum of a particular solution, for instance
(\lambda,\mu)=-\kappa\cdot(a,b)
, with a solution to
\mu m-\lambda n=0
, which is of the form
(\lambda,\mu)=\ell\cdot(m,n)
since
\GCD(n,m)=1
. Thus
(\lambda,\mu)=(\ell m-\kappa a,\ell n-\kappa b)
and the first coordinate of
\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\big(\lambda\binom{b}{-n}+\mu\binom{-a}{m}\big)
is therefore
\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\big(b(\ell m-\kappa a)-a(\ell n-\kappa b)\big)=\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\ell(bm-an)=-2\pi\ell\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. In conclusion, the two points have the same image in
(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}

Let

be as in (
24
). Then, the braid monodromy group
B_{A}
is contained in the subgroup
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}^{-1}\big(B_{\nu(A)}\big)
of
B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
. Moreover, we have
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}(B_{A})=B_{\nu(A)}
.

This lemma is straightforward once we unravel all the definitions. Recall that we write

p(x,y)\;=\;c_{0}(y)+c_{1}(y)x+\cdots+c_{n}(y)x^{n}

and that the map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}:B_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}\to B_{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}
comes from the map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\{(x,y):q(y)=p_{y}(x)=0\}&\mapsto&\left\{\big(\prod_{x\in\{p_{y}=0\}}x,y\big):q(y)=0\right\}\end{array}

that takes the product of all the points of a given configuration in
\mathscr{U}_{A}
that lie on the horizontal slice given by some root
y
of
q
. When the configuration in question comes from a system
\{p(x,y)=q(y)=0\}\in\mathscr{C}_{A}
, the product corresponding to a root
y
of
q
is the solution to the degree 1 equation

c_{n}(y)x-(-1)^{n}c_{0}(y)=0

according to Vieta’s formula. It follows that the map
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}
takes values in the braid group
B_{\nu(A)}
associated to systems of the form

c_{n}(y)x-(-1)^{n}c_{0}(y)=0\,,\quad q(y)=0.

Moreover, the linear map
\mathbb{C}^{A}\to\mathbb{C}^{\nu(A)}
that sends the system
\{p(x,y)=q(y)=0\}
to the corresponding system supported on
\nu(A)
maps the
A
-bifurcation set inside the
\nu(A)
-bifurcation set. It follows that any loop in the configuration space
\mathscr{C}_{\nu(A)}
has a lift to
\mathscr{C}_{A}
and therefore that
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits_{A}(B_{A})=B_{\nu(A)}
.

In order to prove the Lemma

Lemma

.

For
A_{1}
is as in (

Since

. Moreover, for any pair

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\omega^{b(0)}=u\\

5.2.
Reducible pairs with two one-dimensional supports

We now consider the same problem as in the previous section in the special case where both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are contained in a line. The two lines are assumed to be skew, otherwise the system

(25)

p(x,t)=q(x,t)=0

has no solutions for generic
(p,q)
. We assume with no loss of generality that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
contain
(0,0)
as an extremity. In such case, there exist primitive vectors
(a,b),\,(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that

p(x,t)=\tilde{p}(x^{a}y^{b})\quad\text{and}\quad q(x,y)=\tilde{q}(x^{u}y^{v})

where
\tilde{p}(x)
and
\tilde{q}(y)
are univariate polynomials.
To the equation system (
25
), we can associate the following systems

(26)

\displaystyle p(x,y)=0,\quad x^{u}y^{v}=1\quad\text{with support }{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}A_{p}}}

(27)

\displaystyle x^{a}y^{b}=1,\quad q(x,y)=0\quad\text{with support }{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}A_{q}}}

Denote the set of solutions to (
25
), (
26
) and (
27
) respectively by
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
. Plainly, we have a surjective map

(28)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{N}_{1}\times\mathcal{N}_{2}&\to&\mathcal{N}\\
(s_{1},s_{2})&\mapsto&s_{1}\cdot s_{2}\end{array}

where
s_{1}\cdot s_{2}
is the multiplication in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.
The subgroup
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}K}}:=\{x^{a}y^{b}=x^{u}y^{v}=1\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
acts on
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
by multiplication. Define the action
\xi.(s_{1},s_{2}):=(\xi\cdot s_{1},\xi^{-1}\cdot s_{2})
on the product
\mathcal{N}_{1}\times\mathcal{N}_{2}
. Then, the product map (
28
) induces a bijection between the
K
-orbits of
\mathcal{N}_{1}\times\mathcal{N}_{2}
(relative to the latter action) and the set
\mathcal{N}
.

The action of
K\to\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}_{2}}
of
K
on
\mathcal{N}_{1}\times\mathcal{N}_{2}
induces a morphism
K\to G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
and the map (
28
) induces a morphism
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}\to G_{A}
. These morphisms fit into to the following short exact sequence

(29)

1\to K\to G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}\to G_{A}\to 1.

We will see that the latter sequence is spilt and exhibit a section of
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}\to G_{A}
. First, we will describe the Galois groups
G_{A_{p}}
and
G_{A_{q}}
.

To begin with, we will change coordinate. Choose
(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
(a,b)
and
(m,n)
form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and set
(s,t):=(x^{a}y^{b},x^{m}y^{n})
. The system (
26
) now reads as

(30)

\tilde{p}(s)=0,\quad s^{e}t^{f}=1

for some
(e,f)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Observe that
K=\{s=1,s^{e}t^{f}=1\}\simeq U_{f}
in these new coordinates. The solutions to (
30
) is acted upon by
K
, but also by the group
U_{d}
where
d
is the largest integer so that
\tilde{p}(s)=\hat{p}(s^{d})
for some other polynomials
\hat{p}
. Plainly, the Galois group
G_{A_{p}}
is contained in the
(U_{d}\times K)
-invariant subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
. The latter subgroup is isomorphic to
(U_{d}\times K)\,{\rm wr}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}
where
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
is the set of solutions to

\hat{p}(s)=0,\quad s^{e}t^{f}=1

We claim that the containment
G_{A_{p}}\subset(U_{d}\times K)\,{\rm wr}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}
is an equality.

Indeed, from
[
EL21
, Theorem 1]
, we know two things. First, the Galois group of
\hat{p}
is the full symmetric group. Second, for any tuple in
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}
, we can find a loop in the space of non-singular
\hat{p}
-s such that each root of
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
closes a loop whose class in
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
is prescribed by the latter tuple. When a root
\hat{r}
in
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
realises the class
kd+l\in\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
, with
k
and
l
integers so that
0\leqslant l<d
, the fiber over
\hat{r}
in
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is translated by
([l],[k])\in U_{d}\times K
. We deduce that the
(U_{d}\times K)
-orbits of
\mathcal{N}_{1}
can be permuted arbitrarily and that we can realise any tuple of translations on these orbits. The claim follows.

Let us now exhibit a subgroup of
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
that is isomorphic to
G_{A}
. To that aim, consider the map
(U_{d}\times K)\,{\rm wr}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\to K
that associates to any tuple
\big((\xi_{\hat{r}},\zeta_{\hat{r}})_{\hat{r}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}},\sigma\big)
the product
\prod_{\hat{r}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\zeta_{\hat{r}}
. The kernel of this map defines a subgroup
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbb{P}G_{A_{p}}}}\subset G_{A_{p}}
that is independent of the identification
G_{A_{p}}\simeq(U_{d}\times K)\,{\rm wr}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}\,\mathfrak{S}_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}
, see Section
1.2
. We claim that
\mathbb{P}G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
is isomorphic to
G_{A}
. Indeed, the former group intersect trivially the image of
K
in
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
and has index
|K|
in
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
. Thus, the restriction of
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}\to G_{A}
to
\mathbb{P}G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
is an isomorphism onto
G_{A}
.

Observe that we can also define the subgroup
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbb{P}G_{A_{q}}}}\subset G_{A_{q}}
in the same way we define
\mathbb{P}G_{A_{q}}
. In this section, we proved the following.

Theorem
5.8
.

Let
A:=(A_{1},A_{2})
,
A_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, be any pair such that
A_{i}
is contained in a line
L_{i}
with
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
skew. Then, the Galois group
G_{A}
is isomorphic to either of the subgroups
\mathbb{P}G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
and
G_{A_{p}}\times\mathbb{P}G_{A_{q}}
of
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}
. The isomorphism is provided by the arrow
G_{A_{p}}\times G_{A_{q}}\to G_{A}
of the short exact sequence (
29
).

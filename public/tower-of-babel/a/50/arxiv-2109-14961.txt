Hyperbolic plane curves near the non-singular tropical limit
Cédric Le Texier

Abstract
. We determine necessary and sufficient conditions for real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit to be hyperbolic with respect to a point, thus generalising Speyer’s classification of stable curves near the tropical limit. In order to obtain the conditions, we develop tools of real tropical intersection theory. We introduce the tropical hyperbolicity locus and the signed tropical hyperbolicity locus of a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit. In the case of honeycombs, we characterise the tropical hyperbolicity locus in terms of the set of twisted edges on the tropical limit.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study hyperbolic plane curves near the non-singular tropical limit by the means of real intersection theory of tropical curves.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a real algebraic curve of degree
d
.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
the set of real points of the curve
\mathcal{C}
, and by
\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
the set of real points of the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
hyperbolic with respect to
a real point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
if for every real line
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
containing
p
, the intersection
\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{C}
consists of
d
real points, counted with multiplicity.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
hyperbolic
if there exists a real point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\mathcal{C}
is hyperbolic with respect to
p
.
The
hyperbolicity locus
of
\mathcal{C}
is the subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
of real points
p
such that
\mathcal{C}
is hyperbolic with respect to
p
.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
stable
if the hyperbolicity locus of
\mathcal{C}
contains the whole positive orthant
({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}
of the algebraic torus
({\mathbb{R}}^{\times})^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Hyperbolic varieties are studied in several domains such as convex optimization (
[

]
,
[

]
),
topology of real algebraic varieties (
[

]
,
[

]

Stable polynomials, which gave the name to the stable hypersurfaces they define, have a particularly interesting connection to matroid theory (
[

]

[

]
,
[

Speyer
[

]

[

]
studied the combinatorial properties of the tropicalisation of stable polynomials, and Rincón, Vinzant, Yu
[

studied the tropicalisation of positively hyperbolic varieties, an analogue of stable varieties in higher codimension, and their relation with positroids.

The relative topology of non-singular hyperbolic curves in the projective plane is well-known.
Helton and Vinnikov
[
HV07
, Theorem 5.2]
showed that the real part of a non-singular hyperbolic curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
consists of
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
nested
ovals
, which are connected components of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
homeomorphic to
S^{1}
and disconnecting
\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, plus a
pseudo-line
(which are connected components of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
homeomorphic to
S^{1}
and not disconnecting
\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
) if
d
is odd.
Rokhlin
[
Rok78
]
proved that a non-singular real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
nested ovals is
dividing
, meaning that the real part of
\mathcal{C}
disconnects the complex part of
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover, Orevkov
[
Ore07
, Proposition 1.1]
showed that a non-singular real curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree
d
is hyperbolic if
\mathcal{C}
is dividing and
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
has exactly
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
ovals (see
Proposition

5.1
).
We obtain then an equivalence between hyperbolicity and being dividing with
\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil
connected components in the real part (see
Corollary

5.2
)

This last fact can be translated into a combinatorial condition for hyperbolic curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}

near the non-singular tropical limit
(see
Definition

3.4
and
3.8
).
Haas
[
Haa97
, Section 5.4]
gave a characterisation of dividing curves obtained via combinatorial patchworking (see
Theorem

5.4
), which can be formulated in terms of
twisted edges
on a non-singular tropical curve
C
as follows (see
Definition

3.9
and
Figure

3(c)
).
A curve
\mathcal{C}
near the non-singular tropical limit is dividing if and only if every cycle of
C
has an even number of twisted edges, for
C
the non-singular tropical curve given as support of the tropical limit.
Thus we call a set of twisted edges
T
on a non-singular tropical curve
C

dividing
if every cycle of
C
contains an even number of element of
T
.
Renaudineau and Shaw
[
RS21
, Theorem 7.2]
introduced a way to compute the number of real components of a real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
near the non-singular tropical limit with support a non-singular tropical curve
C
with set of twisted edges
T
, using a homomorphism
\partial_{T}
of tropical homology groups determined by
T
(see
Theorem

5.6
).
Thanks to those results, we can first characterise hyperbolic plane curves near the non-singular tropical limit in terms of twisted edges as follows.

Proposition 1.2

(
Proposition

5.7
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a real algebraic curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
near the non-singular tropical limit with support a non-singular tropical curve
C
of degree
d
in the tropical projective plane
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Let
T
be the set of twisted edges on
C
induced by
\mathcal{C}
.
The curve
\mathcal{C}
is hyperbolic if and only if
T
is dividing and the kernel of the homomorphism
\partial_{T}
determined by
T
has dimension
\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil-1
.

For
\mathcal{C}
a hyperbolic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
near the non-singular tropical limit with support a tropical curve
C
, we define its
signed tropical hyperbolicity locus

{\mathbb{R}}H
as the interior of the innermost connected component of the real part of

in the real projective plane, and its
tropical hyperbolicity locus

as a subset of the complement
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
so that each connected component of

has a symmetric copy in
{\mathbb{R}}H
(

The closure of a tropical hyperbolicity locus is a tropical spectrahedron in the sense of
[
AGS20
]
, ie. the tropicalisation of a spectrahedron, and the signed tropical hyperbolicity locus

, as an open set, satisfies the conditions to be signed tropically convex
[
LV19
]
.

The description of hyperbolicity in
Proposition

1.2
is not geometric.
In fact, it involves the computation of the kernel of a symmetric matrix modulo 2 (see
Theorem

5.6
, which is a reformulation of
[
RS21
, Theorem 7.2]
), hence it does not give any information on the tropical hyperbolicity locus.
This motivates our study of the intersection theory of real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit.

Brugallé and López de Medrano
[
BDM12
, Proposition 3.11]
gave an upper bound on the number of intersection points of two algebraic curves tropicalising to two distinct tropical curves, based on the intersection locus of these tropical curves.
This bound is sharp whenever the intersection locus is compact.
Starting from this result (recalled in
Proposition

4.4
), we give an analogous statement in the real case (
Proposition

4.5
).
Next in
Proposition

4.6
, we compute the number of real and complex conjugated intersection points when the real algebraic curves tropicalise to non-singular tropical curves intersecting in a
transverse
point (
Definition

4.1
),
in terms of
real phase structure
on the edges of the non-singular tropical curves (see
Definition

3.5
,
Figure

2(c)
and
Remark

3.6
).

A real phase structure
\mathcal{E}
on a non-singular tropical curve curve
C
is a collection of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-affine spaces
(\mathcal{E}_{e})_{e}
, for
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}:=({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
and
e
running through the edges of
C
, such that
\mathcal{E}_{e}
is a 1-dimensional affine subspace of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2}
parallel to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space spanned by the direction modulo 2 of the edge
e
, and satisfying an additional condition around each 3-valent vertex of
C
.
A real phase structure
\varepsilon
on a tropical point
v
is an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{2}
.
Thus in the following, we will specify for real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit the pair
(C,\mathcal{E})
given as tropical limit (see
3.8
), and we will specify for real points near the tropical limit the pair
(v,\varepsilon)
given as tropical limit.
Moreover, the support of a tropical limit is the first element of the pair, ie. either a non-singular tropical curve or a tropical point depending on context.

In order to study the hyperbolicity locus, we also need to compute the number of real intersection points of two tropical curves with real phase structures in two non-transverse cases.
In the following, we consider that any edge of a tropical curve contains its adjacent vertices.
Said otherwise, the edges are considered to be closed in the Euclidean topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
In the case where the connected component
E
of the intersection is an edge of a non-singular tropical curve
C
, strictly contained in an edge of a non-singular tropical curve
C^{\prime}
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3

(
Theorem

4.7
)
.

Let
C,C^{\prime}
be two non-singular tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that there exists a closed bounded edge
e
of
C
contained in the interior of an edge
e^{\prime}
of
C^{\prime}
(see
Figure

4(a)
).
Let
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be real phase structures on
C,C^{\prime}
, inducing sets of twisted edges on
C
and
C^{\prime}
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
, or if
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
e
is twisted, then for any two real algebraic curves
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}
near the non-singular tropical limits
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, the intersection
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has exactly two distinct real points near the tropical limit with support in
e
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
e
is non-twisted, then for
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C^{\prime}}\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}
two non-singular real algebraic curves near the tropical limits
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, the intersection
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has exactly either two distinct real points, two distinct complex conjugated points, or a multiplicity 2 real point, near the tropical limit with support in
e
.
Moreover, there exist infinitely many curves satisfying the two first intersection types, and there exist exactly two pairs of curves satisfying the third intersection type.

Furthermore, in the case where the real phase structures are distinct on the intersection, we obtain in
Corollary

4.8
that the two distinct real intersection points are near the tropical limit with support the two vertices of the edge
e
.
We deduce from
Theorem

1.3
a necessary condition for the intersection edge
e
to lift to a multiplicity 2 real point, see
Corollary

4.10
.

Cueto, Len and Markwig have extensively studied the lifts of tropical bitangents, notably in the case of tropical quartic curves

)

Let

Thanks to these intersection results, we compute the necessary and sufficient conditions for every real algebraic curve near a common non-singular tropical limit to be hyperbolic with respect to real points near a common tropical limit.
In the following, we use a decomposition
\Sigma_{v}
of the tropical projective plane
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with respect to a point

, see

Figure

The idea is to subdivide
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
by three half-lines
\tau_{\eta}
with origin

and outward primitive integer direction
\eta\in\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, such that
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash\bigcup\tau_{\eta}
has three connected components
\sigma_{\eta}
with
\tau_{\eta}
not contained in the boundary of
\sigma_{\eta}
.
We will say that a point

in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
is

if the rays of
\Sigma_{v}
intersect

Theorem 1.5

(
Theorem

5.19
)
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve of degree
d
in the tropical projective plane
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a real phase structure on
C
.
Let

be a tropical point in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
, and let
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2}

Every real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree

in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and near the non-singular tropical limit

is hyperbolic with respect to a point

the polyhedral subdivision of

with respect to a point

(1)

Every vertex of

of
\Sigma_{v}
is incident to an edge of primitive integer direction

.

(2)

For every edge

of

of

(3)

For every bounded edge

of

of primitive integer direction

of
\Sigma_{v}
, the edge

is twisted if

, and otherwise the component

is relatively non-twisted with respect to the unique real tropical line

A
honeycomb
is a tropical curve with all edges of primitive integer direction in
\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
.
Speyer
[
Spe05
, Section 5.3]
proved that a non-singular stable curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
near the tropical limit induces the data of a honeycomb (possibly with some contracted edges) in the tropical projective plane
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, with constant distribution of signs on the dual subdivision.
Conversely, he proved that a non-singular honeycomb in the tropical projective plane
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with constant distribution of signs on the dual subdivision (hence with every bounded edge twisted) is the non-singular tropical limit of a stable curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
We give in
Theorem

5.23
an alternative approach to Speyer’s result in the case of non-singular tropical limit based on
Theorem

1.5
.

We can treat the case of honeycombs further, as these tropical curves automatically satisfy
Item

1
and
Item

2
of
Theorem

1.5
for any point
v^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
A multi-bridge on a honeycomb

is a set
B
of parallel bounded edges of
C
, dual to edges lying on a common line in the dual subdivision, and such that

has two connected components (see

The multi-bridges on a honeycomb form a basis for dividing patchworking (

We show that if a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit converges to a non-singular honeycomb, checking if a connected component belongs to the tropical hyperbolicity locus reduces to the following criterion on twisted edges.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
be an integer point in the dual subdivision of a non-singular honeycomb

of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
\alpha^{\vee}
be the connected component of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
dual to
\alpha
.
In the following result, we say that a multi-bridge
B
is on the
left
of
\alpha^{\vee}\subset{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
if the dual set of edges

lies in a vertical line through an integer point
(\beta_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
\beta_{1}<\alpha_{1}
.
Similarly, we say that
B
is
below

\alpha^{\vee}
if
B^{\vee}
lies in a horizontal line through a point
(\alpha_{1},\beta_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
\beta_{2}<\alpha_{2}
, and we say that
B
is
diagonally above

\alpha^{\vee}
if
B^{\vee}
lies in a line of direction
(1,-1)
through a point
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}
.

(

)

Let

2.
Tropical curves

We recall in this section several definitions in tropical geometry.
For more details and examples, one can read
[
BIMS15
, Section 2]
,
[
IMS09
, Chapter 1]
,
[
MS09
]
.

2.1.
Tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

A
tropical polynomial in two variables
is

P(x,y)=\max\limits_{i,j\in A}(a_{i,j}+ix+jy),

where
A
is a finite subset of
({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}
and the coefficients
a_{i,j}
are in the tropical semi-ring
{\mathbb{T}}=({\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\},\max,+)
.
Note that we use the “max” operation, while some references in the literature use the “min” operation instead.
Thus, a tropical polynomial is a convex piecewise affine function, with corner locus

V_{P}=\{(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}~|~\exists(i,j)\neq(k,l),~P(x_{0},y_{0})=a_{i,j}+ix_{0}+jy_{0}=a_{k,l}+kx_{0}+ly_{0}\}.

The set
V_{P}
is a 1-dimensional polyhedral complex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
weight function
is defined on the edges
e
of
V_{P}
as

w(e):=\max\limits_{(i,j),(k,l)}(\gcd(|i-k|,|j-l|))

for all pairs
(i,j)
and
(k,l)
such that the value of
P(x,y)
on
e
is given by the corresponding monomials.
The
tropical curve

C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
defined by
P(x,y)
is the 1-dimensional polyhedral complex
V_{P}
equipped with the weight function
w
on the edges.

2.2.
Dual subdivision

Let
P(x,y)=\max\limits_{i,j\in A}(a_{i,j}+ix+jy)
be a tropical polynomial defining a tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
The
Newton polygon
of
P(x,y)
, denoted by
\Delta(P)
, is defined as the convex hull

\Delta(P)=\Conv\{(i,j)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}~|~a_{i,j}\neq-\infty\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

The tropical polynomial
P
also determines a subdivision of
\Delta(P)
, as follows.
Given
(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, let

\Delta(x_{0},y_{0})=\Conv\{(i,j)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}~|~P(x_{0},y_{0})=a_{i,j}+ix_{0}+jy_{0}\}\subset\Delta(P).

The tropical curve
C
induces a polyhedral decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and the polygon
\Delta(x_{0},y_{0})
only depends on the cell
F\ni(x_{0},y_{0})
of the decomposition given by
C
.
Thus, we define the face
F^{\vee}:=\Delta(x_{0},y_{0})
dual to
F
for
(x_{0},y_{0})\in F
.
The
dual subdivision

\Delta_{C}
of the tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is the union

\Delta_{C}:=\bigcup_{F}F^{\vee},

for
F
running through the faces of the decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
induced by
C
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a tropical curve
C
is
non-singular
if its dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
is a triangulation with each 2-dimensional cell of Euclidean area
1/2
.

Equivalently, a tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with Newton polytope
\Delta
is non-singular if and only if
C
has
2\Area(\Delta)
vertices.
In particular, if a tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is non-singular, every vertex of
C
is 3-valent and every edge
e
of
C
has weight
w(e)=1
, and all integer points of
\Delta_{C}
are vertices in the subdivision.

2.3.
Puiseux series and tropicalisation

Definition 2.3

(
[
IMS09
]
)
.

A
locally convergent generalized Puiseux series
is a formal series of the form

\alpha(t)=\sum\limits_{r\in R}\alpha_{r}t^{r}

where
R\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a well-ordered set, the coefficient
\alpha_{r}
belongs to
{\mathbb{C}}
, and the series is convergent for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
small enough.
We denote by
{\mathbb{K}}
the set of all locally convergent generalized Puiseux series.
An element
\alpha:=\alpha(t)
of
{\mathbb{K}}
is said to be
real
if all its coefficients
\alpha_{r}
, with
r\in R
, belong to
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We denote by
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
the subset of real locally convergent generalized Puiseux series.

The sets
{\mathbb{K}}
and
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
are both fields of characteristic 0.
The field
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, and the subfield
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is real closed (ie. its field extension by the square root of
-1
is algebraically closed,
[
BCR13
, Theorem 1.2.2]
).
The field
{\mathbb{K}}
has a
non-archimedean valuation
defined as follows (see
[
BCR13
]
):

\displaystyle\val:{\mathbb{K}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\mapsto\infty

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in R}\alpha_{r}t^{r}\neq 0

\displaystyle\mapsto\min_{R}\{r~|~\alpha_{r}\neq 0\}.

The restriction of
\val
to the subfield
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
allows to define an
ordering
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
as follows (see
[
BCR13
]
).
An element

is

, and we denote
\alpha>0
, if the leading coefficient
\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}},r:=\val(\alpha)
of

is positive with respect to the standard ordering of
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Similarly, an element
\alpha\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is
negative
, and we denote
\alpha<0
, if the leading coefficient
\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}},r:=\val(\alpha)
of
\alpha
is negative with respect to the standard ordering of
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then for any two elements
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\alpha<\beta
if and only if
\beta-\alpha>0
.

Since the elements of
{\mathbb{K}}
are convergent for

small enough, an algebraic hypersurface
\mathcal{X}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
can be seen as a one-parameter family
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t}
of algebraic hypersurfaces over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and an algebraic hypersurface
\mathcal{X}
over
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be seen as a one-parameter family

of algebraic hypersurfaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The valuation map

allows to define a map from

3.1.
Combinatorial patchworking

Given a non-singular tropical curve
C
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
or
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
and a distribution of signs
\delta
on the integer points of the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
, we recall the construction of the
real part
of the tropical curve
C
with respect to
\delta
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{2}
be the group of symmetries in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
generated by the reflections with respect to the coordinate hyperplanes.
Throughout the article, for
A
a topological set, we will denote by
A^{*}
the disjoint union of symmetric copies

A^{*}:=\bigsqcup\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}}\varepsilon(A).

For
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}:={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, we identify
\mathcal{R}^{2}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2}
as follows.
If
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2}
, then
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
is the symmetry defined by

\varepsilon(x,y)=((-1)^{\varepsilon_{1}}x,(-1)^{\varepsilon_{2}}y),

for
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
seen as integers.
For
\Delta_{C}
the dual subdivision of a non-singular tropical curve

\delta:\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\rightarrow\{+1,-1\}

on
\Delta_{C}
.
We extend a distribution of signs
\delta
to a distribution of signs on the integer points of the union of symmetric copies
\Delta_{C}^{*}
by the following rule.
For
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
an integer point in
\Delta_{C}^{*}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
a symmetry, we have

\delta(\varepsilon(v))=(-1)^{\varepsilon_{1}v_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}v_{2}}\delta(v),

again with
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
seen as integers (see for example the signs on
Figure

2(b)
).
A face of
\Delta_{C}^{*}
is said to be

if at least two of its vertices have opposite signs by
\delta

We can now construct the real part of
C
with respect to
\delta
as follows.
For every edge
e
of
C
, let
e^{\vee}
be its dual edge in
\Delta_{C}
.
The real part

of

is defined as

{\mathbb{R}}e_{\delta}:=\bigcup\limits_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon(e)\subset({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{*},

for
\varepsilon
running through the symmetries of
\mathcal{R}^{2}
such that
\varepsilon(e^{\vee})
is non-empty (see for example the blue edges in
Figure

2(b)

4.1.
Transverse real intersection

Definition 4.1

(
[

]
)

Let

Definition 4.2

(
[

]

Let

a

The

at

(C\circ C^{\prime})_{v}:=\Area(v^{\vee})-\xi_{v},

for
\Area(\bullet)
the Euclidean area, for

the 2-dimensional face dual to
v
in the
mixed dual subdivision
associated to the union

, and

•

C^{\prime}
, then

•

Note that if

Definition 4.3
.

Let
C,C^{\prime}
be two tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Let

.
The

of

(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}=\sum\limits_{v}(C\circ C^{\prime})_{v},

for

Proposition 4.4

(
[
BDM12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be two algebraic curves in
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
, with tropicalisation

respectively, such that
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
intersect in a finite number of points.
Let

and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with tropicalisation in

(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}
, with equality if

is compact.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be two real algebraic curves in
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
, with tropicalisation
C,C^{\prime}
respectively, such that
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
intersect in a finite number of points.
Let
E
be a connected component of
C\cap C^{\prime}
.
Then the number of real intersection points (counted with multiplicity) of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with tropicalisation in

is less or equal to
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}
, with equality modulo 2 if
E
is compact.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.4
, the number of intersection points of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with tropicalisation in
E
is at most
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}
, counted with multiplicity.
Moreover, we have equality if
E
is compact.
Hence the number of real intersection points with tropicalisation in
E
is at most
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}
.
Assume that
E
is compact.
Any pair
(p,\bar{p})
of complex conjugated points satisfies
\Trop(p)=\Trop(\bar{p})
.
In particular, the point
p
has tropicalisation in
E
if and only if its complex conjugated
\bar{p}
has tropicalisation in
E
.
Since the intersection of the real curves
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
consists of a finite number of points, we can split those points into the real ones and the pairs of complex conjugated ones.
Then for
E

E
is equal to
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{E}
modulo 2.
∎

From now on, we will consider intersection of non-singular real tropical curves.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
be two non-singular real tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, such that there exists a transverse intersection point
u
in
C\cap C^{\prime}
of tropical intersection multiplicity
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{u}=m\geq 1
.
Let
e
be the edge of
C
containing
u
, and let
e^{\prime}
be the edge of
C^{\prime}
containing
u
.
For every realisation
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
in
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, there are exactly
m
distinct points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
with
\Trop(p_{i})=u
in the intersection
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C^{\prime}}
, partitioned as follows,

•

if
m
is even, the set
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}
consists of 2 real points and
\frac{m-2}{2}
pairs of complex conjugated points if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
, and it consists of
\frac{m}{2}
pairs of complex conjugated points if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
;

•

if
m
is odd, the set
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}
consists of 1 real point and
\frac{m-1}{2}
pairs of complex conjugated points.

Note that Brugallé and López de Medrano already proved that all the intersection points lifted from a transverse intersection point are of multiplicity 1, see
[
BDM12
, Lemma 3.13]
.
Moreover, the condition
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
in the case

even is equivalent to
\mathcal{E}_{e}\cap\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, since in that case the two edges have the same primitive integer direction modulo 2.

Proof of
Proposition

4.6
.

We use a similar setting as the proof of
[
BDM12
, Lemma 3.13]
.
After an affine linear transformation, we can assume that the edge
e
has primitive integer direction
(1,1)
, the edge
e^{\prime}
has primitive integer direction
(1,-m+1)
, and those edges intersect at the point
(0,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Since the tropical curves are non-singular, the edges have all weight 1.
Then the local equations in
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}[z,w]
of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are of the form

(2)

\displaystyle z^{k}w^{l}(\delta_{k+1,l}z+\delta_{k,l+1}w)

\displaystyle=0

(3)

\displaystyle z^{p}w^{q}(\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}z^{m-1}w+\varepsilon_{p,q})

\displaystyle=0

with the coefficients
\varepsilon_{i,j},\delta_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
of valuation 0.
Since we consider the solutions inside
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
, we obtain from
Equation

2
that
w=-\frac{\delta_{k+1,l}}{\delta_{k,l+1}}z
.
Again since we consider the solutions inside
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
, we can substitute
w=-\frac{\delta_{k+1,l}}{\delta_{k,l+1}}z
in the second term of
Equation

3
to obtain the following new equation in one variable:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}\delta_{k+1,l}}{\delta_{k,l+1}}\right)z^{m}+\varepsilon_{p,q}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow z^{m}-\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{p,q}\delta_{k,l+1}}{\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}\delta_{k+1,l}}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

The coefficient
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{p,q}\delta_{k,l+1}}{\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}\delta_{k+1,l}}\right)
is non-zero, hence the polynomial above (seen in
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}[z]
) has
m
distinct roots of multiplicity 1 in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
.
Moreover, if
m
is odd, we have exactly one real root and
\frac{m-1}{2}
pairs of complex conjugated roots, while if
m
is even, we have exactly two real roots if and only if
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{p,q}\delta_{k,l+1}}{\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}\delta_{k+1,l}}\right)>0
and no real roots if and only if
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{p,q}\delta_{k,l+1}}{\varepsilon_{p+m-1,q+1}\delta_{k+1,l}}\right)<0
.
For
m
even, seeing the signs of the coefficients
\varepsilon_{ij},\delta_{kl}
as the distributions of signs on the corresponding dual subdivisions of the local model, we obtain two real roots if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and no real roots if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
.
∎

Let us now consider non-singular real tropical curves
(C,\mathcal{E}),(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
with a non-transverse connected component

, where
e
is a bounded edge of
C
contained in the interior of a closed edge of
C^{\prime}
(see for example

).
Since the tropical curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
are non-singular, we obtain that
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{e}=2
(using the balancing condition and the fact that all edges have weight 1).
By
Proposition

4.4
and
Proposition

4.5
, every realisation of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
intersect in two points (counted with multiplicity) with tropicalisation in
e
, which are either two distinct real points, two distinct complex conjugated points, or a multiplicity 2 real point with tropicalisation in
e
.
Note that we cannot obtain a multiplicity 2 non-real intersection point
p
, as this would imply that its complex conjugated
\overline{p}
is also a multiplicity 2 intersection point, hence by
Proposition

4.4
we would obtain
(C\circ C^{\prime})_{e}>2
, leading to a contradiction.

Theorem 4.7

(
Theorem

1.3
)
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E}),(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
be two non-singular real tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that there exists a closed bounded edge
e
of
C
contained in the interior of a closed edge
e^{\prime}
of
C^{\prime}
(see
Figure

4(a)

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
, or is
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
e
is twisted, then all realisations of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
intersect in two distinct real intersection points of multiplicity 1 with tropicalisation in
e
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
e
is non-twisted, then a realisation of
(C,\mathcal{E})
can intersect a realisation of
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
in either two distinct real points of multiplicity 1 with tropicalisation in
e
, two distinct complex conjugated points of multiplicity 1 with tropicalisation in
e
, or a multiplicity 2 real point with tropicalisation in

.
Moreover, there exist infinitely many realisations satisfying the first two intersection types, and there exist exactly two pairs of realisations satisfying the third intersection type.

Proof.

After an affine linear transformation, we can assume that the edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
have primitive integer direction
(1,1)
and that the vertices of
e
are
v_{1}=(0,0)
and
v_{2}=(b,b)
with
b>0
.
Then the local equations of some realisations in
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
are of the form

(4)

\displaystyle z^{k}w^{l}\left(\varepsilon_{k+1,l}z+\varepsilon_{k,l+1}w\right)

\displaystyle=0

(5)

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}t^{b}z^{m}w^{n}+\delta_{p+1,q}z^{p+1}w^{q}+\delta_{p,q+1}z^{p}w^{q+1}+\delta_{r,s}z^{r}w^{s}

\displaystyle=0

with

and the coefficients
\varepsilon_{ij},\delta_{ij}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
are of valuation

(see

4(b)
).
Note that the conditions on the exponents are implied by the fact that
C
is non-singular.
In
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{2}
,
Equation

4
is equivalent to
w=-\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}z
.
After substituting
w=-\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}z
in
Equation

5
, we can factor the left-hand side by
z^{p+q}
, so that the solutions of the new equation in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
are the roots of the following degree 2 polynomial in
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}[z]
:

\displaystyle z^{2}\left(\delta_{m,n}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{n}t^{b}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle z\left(\delta_{p+1,q}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{q}+\delta_{p,q+1}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\delta_{r,s}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{s}.

By Tarski-Seidenberg’s principle
[
BCR13
, Theorem 1.4.2]
applied to the real closed field

, we can compute the discriminant in order to determine possible real roots.
The discriminant with respect to

is
D=B^{2}-4At^{b}
, with

B:=\left(\delta_{p+1,q}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{q}+\delta_{p,q+1}\left(\frac{-\varepsilon_{k+1,l}}{\varepsilon_{k,l+1}}\right)^{q+1}\right)

Note that
\val(B)\geq 0
, and
\val(B)=0
unless the leading terms of the two summands cancel each other.

We can now prove the two statements of the theorem.

(I)

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{e}\neq\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
(first case of
Item

1
).
Then the product

\delta_{p+1,q}\delta_{p,q+1}\varepsilon_{k+1,l}\varepsilon_{k,l+1}

is negative, hence the leading coefficients of the two summands of

do not cancel.
Thus
\val(B)=0
, so the sign of the discriminant
D
is determined by the sign of
B^{2}
, hence
D
is positive.
Therefore, all realisations of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
intersect in two distinct real points with tropicalisation in
e
.

(II)

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
e
is twisted (second case of
Item

1
).
We have
n+s=0\mod 2
if and only if
(m,n)=(r,s)\mod 2
.
Then by
Proposition

3.11
, since
e
is twisted, the discriminant
D
is positive.
Therefore, all realisations of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
intersect in two distinct real points with tropicalisation in
e
.

(III)

Assume that
e
is non-twisted and
\mathcal{E}_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime}
.
Using
Proposition

3.11
, we obtain the sign of the discriminant depending on the valuation of
B
and the values of each summand of

as follows.
The discriminant

is positive if either
\val(B)<\frac{b}{2}
or
\val(B)=\frac{b}{2}

(C,\mathcal{E})
and
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
intersect in two distinct real points with tropicalisation in
e
.
Moreover, the condition
\val(B)<\frac{b}{2}

The discriminant
D
is negative if either
\val(B)>\frac{b}{2}

\val(B)=\frac{b}{2}

and

\val(B)>\frac{b}{2}

The discriminant

is zero if
\val(B)=\frac{b}{2}
and

, and in that case all realisations of
(C,\mathcal{E})
and

5.1.

Definition 5.24
.

Remark 5.25
.

Let
g
be the number of primitive cycles of a non-singular tropical curve
C
of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}

The space
\Div(C)
has codimension

in
\Adm(C)
, where

\gamma
in
C
such that
\gamma
contains at least one edge of direction
(1,0)
modulo 2, one edge of direction

modulo 2 and one edge of direction

modulo 2.

Let
\Hyp(C)
be the set of configuration of twists

such that a non-singular real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
inducing

For

\Div(C)

corresponding to the empty set of edges.

-vector space structure from

.

.

{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
is of dimension
3(d-1)
with basis given by the set of multi-bridges on
C
, seen as vectors in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Edge^{0}(C)
.

Proof.

Let
g
be the number of primitive cycles in
C
.
Since
C
is of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have
g=\binom{d-1}{2}
.
Since
C
is a non-singular honeycomb, every primitive cycle of
C
contains exactly two edges of direction
(1,0)
, two edges of direction
(0,1)
and two edges of direction
(1,1)
.
Then by
Definition

5.24
and
Remark

5.25
, the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
is a subspace of
\Edge^{0}(C)
of codimension
3\binom{d-1}{2}
.
A non-singular honeycomb of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
has
3\binom{d}{2}
bounded edges (with
1+\cdots+(d-1)=\binom{d}{2}
bounded edges in each direction in
\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
).
By definition of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space structure on
\Edge^{0}(C)
, we obtain
\dim\Edge^{0}(C)=3\binom{d}{2}
.
Therefore the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
is of dimension
3\binom{d}{2}-3\binom{d-1}{2}=3(d-1)
.
Now each multi-bridge
B
on a non-singular honeycomb
C
has edge support dual to edges in
\Delta_{C}
contained in a common line.
For each
s\in\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
, there are
(d-1)
distinct lines of direction
s
modulo 2 that contain edges of
\Delta_{C}
dual to bounded edges of
C
.
Then
C
contains exactly
3(d-1)
multi-bridges, such that any two distinct multi-bridges have disjoint edge supports, hence the vectors in
\Edge^{0}(C)
coming from multi-bridges are all linearly independent.
Since a multi-bridge is twist-admissible and dividing, the set of multi-bridges on
C
form a basis of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
.
∎

Using
Proposition

5.28
, we obtain a way to check if a point belongs to the tropical hyperbolicity locus of a non-singular real honeycomb, without going through the relatively non-twisted condition of
Item

3
in
Theorem

5.19
.

Theorem 5.29
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real honeycomb of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
and with induced dividing set of twisted edges
T
.
Let
v
be a point of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
generic with respect to
C
, and let
\Sigma_{v}
be the polyhedral subdivision of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with respect to
v
.
Then the real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to
(v,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
if and only if for every bounded edge
e\subset C\cap\operatorname{int}(\sigma_{\eta})
of direction
\eta
, the unique multi-bridge
B
on
C
containing
e
is twisted.

Proof.

If
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to a symmetric copy of
v
, then for every bounded edge
e\subset C\cap\operatorname{int}(\sigma_{\eta})
of direction
\eta
, the edge
e
is twisted by
Theorem

5.19
.
Since
T
is a dividing set of twisted edges, we obtain by
Proposition

5.28
that for every edge
e
satisfying those conditions, the unique multi-bridge
B
containing
e
is contained in
T
.

Conversely, assume that for every bounded edge
e\subset C\cap\operatorname{int}(\sigma_{\eta})
of direction
\eta
, the unique multi-bridge
B
containing
e
is contained in the dividing set of twisted edges
T
on
C
.
We want to prove iteratively on the degree
d
of
C
that
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to
(v,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
.

By
Remark

5.25
, for
d=1,2,3
, the sets
\Div(C)
and
\Hyp(C)
are equal (as sets and as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector spaces), hence the fact that
T
is dividing already implies that
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic.
If
d=1
, then
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to all real tropical points
(v^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})
not lying on
(C,\mathcal{E})
.
If
d=2,3
, one can check by hand using
Theorem

5.19
that
Theorem

5.29
holds.

Assume as an iterative hypothesis that
Theorem

5.29
is true up to degree
d-1
, with
d\geq 4
.
Let
C
be of degree
d
.
Since
C
is a non-singular honeycomb, there exists a multi-bridge
B
on
C
such that
C
is given as the gluing along
B
of a honeycomb
C^{\prime}
of degree
d-1
with another honeycomb
C^{\prime\prime}
(see for example
Figure

9(a)
with
C^{\prime}
of degree
d-1
in black and
C^{\prime\prime}
in purple, glued along the multi-bridge
B
marked with blue dots).
This gluing induces a decomposition of the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
into
\Delta_{C^{\prime}}\cup\Delta_{C^{\prime\prime}}
, with intersection
\Delta_{C^{\prime}}\cap\Delta_{C^{\prime\prime}}
given as the edges dual to edges of
B
(see for example in
Figure

9(b)
the gluing of dual subdivisions
\Delta_{C^{\prime}}
of degree
d-1
, in black, with
\Delta_{C^{\prime\prime}}
, in purple, along the edges dual to
B
, in blue).
Since
d\geq 4
, we can choose
B
such that the point
v
belongs to a connected component
D\subset{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C
dual to an integer point
(p_{1},p_{2})\in(\Delta_{C^{\prime}}\backslash B^{\vee})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

For
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
, the non-singular real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to the real tropical point
(v,\varepsilon)
if and only if the symmetric copy
\varepsilon(D)
is contained in the tropical hyperbolicity locus of
(C,\mathcal{E})
.
Then the set of integer points
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that their dual connected component
D^{\prime}
is contained in the tropical hyperbolicity locus of
(C,\mathcal{E})
depends only on the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
of
C
and the real phase structure on
C
, or equivalently on the distribution of signs on
\Delta_{C}
.
In particular, it does not depend on the length of the bounded edges of
C
.
Choose a point
v^{\prime}
in
D
so that every bounded edge of
C
intersected by the 1-dimensional faces of the polyhedral subdivision
\Sigma_{v^{\prime}}
of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
is dual to an edge of
(\Delta_{C^{\prime}}\backslash B^{\vee})
(such a point
v^{\prime}
always exist up to replacing
C
by another non-singular honeycomb of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, also called
C
, with distinct bounded edges lengths).
In particular, the multi-bridge
B
is contained in the face
\sigma_{\eta}
of
\Sigma_{v^{\prime}}
, for
\eta
the direction of the edges of
B
.
By assumption, we obtain that the multi-bridge
B
is contained in the set of twisted edges
T
.

By induction hypothesis, there exists
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
such that the non-singular real tropical curve
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}|_{C^{\prime}})
is hyperbolic with respect to
(v^{\prime},\varepsilon)
.
Since every edge
e
of the multi-bridge
B
on
C
is twisted, for any tropical line
L
through
v
containing
e
, by
Theorem

4.7
the connected component
e
of
C\cap L
lifts to two distinct real points of multiplicity 1.
All intersection components

which are segments strictly contained in a bounded edge of

are in fact strictly contained in a bounded edge of
C^{\prime}
.
Since
(C^{\prime},\mathcal{E}|_{C^{\prime}})
is hyperbolic with respect to
(v^{\prime},\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{R}^{2}
, all these intersection components

give rise to real points of multiplicity 1 by
Item

3
of
5.19
and
Theorem

4.14
.
Therefore, the real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
satisfies
Item

3
of
Theorem

5.19
with respect to the real tropical point

, hence
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to

, and thus
(C,\mathcal{E})
is hyperbolic with respect to

.
∎

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
A multi-bridge
B
is said to be
on the left
of
\alpha^{\vee}
if

lies in a vertical line through a point

.

.
We deduce from the proof of
Theorem

5.29
the following corollary, stated in terms of the dual subdivision.

Corollary 5.30

(
Corollary

1.6
)
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real honeycomb with induced dividing set of twisted edges
T
.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

The dual connected component
\alpha^{\vee}\subset({\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash C)
is contained in the tropical hyperbolicity locus

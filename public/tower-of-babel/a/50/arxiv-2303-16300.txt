1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a (complex, separable) Hilbert space,
and let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be the algebra of all (bounded, linear) operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. A (closed) subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{H}
is called
invariant

for an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, if
T\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}
.
The complete lattice of all invariant subspaces of
T
is denoted by
\operatorname{Lat}T
.

The
multiplicity

\mu_{T}
of an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})

is the minimum dimension of its reproducing subspaces:

(1.1)

\mu_{T}=\min\{\dim E:E\subset\mathcal{H},\ \ \vee_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}E=\mathcal{H}\}.

For Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
, let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
denote the space of (bounded, linear)
transformations acting from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
.
For
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and
B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})

set

\mathcal{I}(A,B)=\{X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})\ :\ XA=BX\}.

Then
\mathcal{I}(A,B)
is the set of all transformations
X
which
intertwine

A
and
B
.
Let
X\in\mathcal{I}(A,B)
.
If
X
is unitary, then
A
and
B

are called
unitarily equivalent
, in notation:
A\cong B
. If
X
is invertible, that is,
X^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})
,
then
A
and
B
are called
similar
, in notation:
A\approx B
.
If
X
is a
quasiaffinity
, that is,
\ker X=\{0\}
and
\operatorname{clos}X\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}
, then

A
is called a
quasiaffine transform
of
B
, in notation:
A\prec B
.
If
\ker X=\{0\}
, we write
A\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\prec}\limits^{i}}B
.
If
\operatorname{clos}X\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}
, we write
A\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\prec}\limits^{d}}B
.
If
A\prec B
and

B\prec A
, then
A
and
B
are called
quasisimilar
, in notation:
A\sim B
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called
expansive
if
\|Tx\|\geq\|x\|
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

An operator
T
is called
power bounded
, if
\sup_{n\geq 0}\|T^{n}\|<\infty
.
An operator
T
is called a
contraction
, if
\|T\|\leq 1
.
Clearly, a contraction is power bounded.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be a power bounded operator.
It is easy to see that the space

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}_{T,0}=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\ :\ \|T^{n}x\|\to 0\}

is invariant for
T
(cf.
[
NFBK
, Theorem II.5.4]
). Classes
C_{ab}
,
a
,
b=0,1,\cdot
, of power bounded operators
are defined as follows (see
[
NFBK
, Sec. II.4]
and
[
K89
]
). If
\mathcal{H}_{T,0}=\mathcal{H}
, then
T
is
of class

C_{0\cdot}
, while if
\mathcal{H}_{T,0}=\{0\}
, then
T
is

of class

C_{1\cdot}
. Furthermore,
T
is
of class

C_{\cdot a}
, if
T^{\ast}
is of class
C_{a\cdot}
,
and
T
is
of class

C_{ab}
, if
T
is of classes
C_{a\cdot}
and
C_{\cdot b}
,
a
,
b=0,1
.

For a power bounded operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})

\text{ the \emph{isometric asymptote} }(X_{+,T},T_{+}^{(a)})

can be defined using a Banach limit
\mathop{\text{\rm Lim}}
, see
[
K89
]
. (For the isometric and unitary asymptotes of a contraction
T
see also
[
NFBK
, Sec. IX.1]
).
Here
T_{+}^{(a)}
is an isometry on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{+}^{(a)}
, and
X_{+,T}
is the
canonical intertwining mapping
:

X_{+,T}T=T_{+}^{(a)}X_{+,T}
. Recall that
the range of
X_{+,T}
is dense.
Thus,
X_{+,T}
realizes the relation
T\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\prec}\limits^{d}}T_{+}^{(a)}
.
We do not recall the construction of the canonical intertwining mapping from
[
K89
]
here.
We recall only that
\|X_{+,T}x\|^{2}=\mathop{\text{\rm Lim}}_{n}\|T^{n}x\|^{2}
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
.
It easy follows from this relation that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is similar
to an isometry if and only if
T
is power bounded and there exists
c>0
such that

\|T^{n}x\|\geq c\|x\|
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case,
X_{+,T}
is invertible
and realizes the relation
T\approx T_{+}^{(a)}
.

The
unitary asymptote

(X_{T},T^{(a)})
of a power bounded operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a pair where

T^{(a)}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{(a)})

(here
\mathcal{H}^{(a)}
is a some Hilbert space)
is the minimal unitary extension of
T_{+}^{(a)}
, and
X_{T}
is a natural extension of
X_{+,T}
.

The isometry
T_{+}^{(a)}
and the unitary operator
T^{(a)}
will also be called the isometric and unitary asymptotes of
T
, respectively.

Let
S
be the simple unilateral shift,
that is, the multiplication by the independent variable on the Hardy space
H^{2}
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
.
A particular case of
[
K07
]
is the following (see also
[
NFBK
, Sec. IX.3]
).
Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be an absolutely continuous (a.c.) contraction (the definition is recalled in Sec. 2 of the present paper),
and let
T^{(a)}
contain the bilateral shift as an orthogonal summand.
Then

(1.3)

\mathcal{H}=\vee\{\mathcal{M}\ :\ \mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Lat}T,\ T|_{\mathcal{M}}\approx S\}.

In
[
G22
]
this result is generalized to a.c. polynomially bounded operators (the definition
can be found, for example, in
[
Pe
, Ch. 15]
, see also
[
NFBK
, Ch. I.13]
where other terminology is used; see references therein).
Also it is shown in
[
G22
]
that the quantity of subspaces

in (

) can be equal to

,
if
\mu_{T}\geq 2
, and to

, if
\mu_{T}=1
. On the other hand, there exists power bounded operator

such that

and
there is no

such that

[
G16

.
The purpose of this paper is to show that (

) is fulfilled for expansive operators

which are quasisimilar
to the unilateral shift

of finite multiplicity

, and
the quantity of subspaces

in (

) is as decribed above (Theorems
4.12
and

). Expansive operators are right inverses for contractions.
The proof is based on the result for contractions from
[
K07
]
(see also
[

, Sec. IX.3]
) and
on representations of unimodular functions on

given in
[
B
]
and developed in
[

]
.
Some other properties
of an expansive operator

such that

, where

, are studied. In particular, it is proved that

for every
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Lat}T
, and

I-T^{*}T\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
, where

is the trace class operators (Theorems

and

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. 2 some simple observations are collected, some of them are of own interest, and some of them will be used in the sequel. In Sec. 3 a special kind of finite perturbations of

(

) that are expansive operators is considered. Sec. 4 is the main part of the paper. In Sec. 5 the relationship between similarity to an isometry
of expansive operator and its Cauchy dual (adjoint of the standard left inverse) is studied. In Sec. 6 it is shown that there exist expansive operators

such that

, but
the intertwining quasiffinities do not give the isomorphism of

and

(in contrast with the case when

2.
General observations

The following lemma is well known and can be proved easily, so its proof is omitted.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
,
B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
BA=I_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\dim\ker A^{*}<\infty
.
Then the following are equivalent:
\mathrm{(i)}

I_{\mathcal{H}}-A^{*}A\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
;
\mathrm{(ii)}

I_{\mathcal{H}}-AA^{*}\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
;
\mathrm{(iii)}

I_{\mathcal{H}}-B^{*}B\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

I_{\mathcal{H}}-BB^{*}\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
.

Recall that
A\in L(\mathcal{H})
is called a Fredholm operator, if
A\mathcal{H}
is closed,
\dim\ker A<\infty
, and

\dim\ker A^{*}<\infty
.
Denote by
\operatorname{ind}
the Fredholm index of a Fredholm operator
A
, that is,

\operatorname{ind}A=\dim\ker A-\dim\ker A^{*}
. See, for example,
[
Co
, Ch. XI]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
A\in L(\mathcal{H})
,
\ker A=\{0\}
,
\dim\ker A^{*}=N
,
and
Y\in\mathcal{I}(S_{N},A)
is such that
\operatorname{clos}YH^{2}_{N}=\mathcal{H}
.
Then
\ker Y=\{0\}
.

Proof.

We have

S_{N}=\left[\begin{matrix}S_{N}|_{\ker Y}&*\\
\mathbb{O}&R\end{matrix}\right],

and
Y|_{H^{2}_{N}\ominus\ker Y}
realizes the relation
R\prec A
. This relation implies that
\ker R=\{0\}
and

\dim\ker R^{*}\geq N
. By
[
Co
, Theorem XI.3.7]
,

-N=\operatorname{ind}S_{N}=\operatorname{ind}S_{N}|_{\ker Y}+\operatorname{ind}R\leq\operatorname{ind}S_{N}|_{\ker Y}-N.

This means that
\operatorname{ind}S_{N}|_{\ker Y}=0
. Therefore,
\ker Y=\{0\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3

Let

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be expansive.
Then

Proof.

Let

T\mathcal{R}^{\infty}(T)=\mathcal{R}^{\infty}(T)

\|P_{\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{R}^{\infty}(T)}Tx\|^{2}=\|T(x-y)\|^{2}\geq\|x-y\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\geq\|x\|^{2}.\qed

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be expansive. Then

is a contraction.
Furthermore, the following statements hold true.

(i)

Suppose that

\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Lat}T
,

\mathcal{M}

(ii)

is an a.c. contraction.

(iii)

If

The estimate

(i) Since

(ii) Assume that

Proposition 5.1

L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra of braided electrodynamics
M. Toman

Abstract
Using the recently developed formalism of braided noncommutative field theory, we construct an explicit example of braided electrodynamics, that is, a noncommutative U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theory coupled to a Dirac fermion. We construct the braided L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra of this field theory and apply the formalism to obtain the braided equations of motion, action functional and conserved matter current. The braided deformations leads to a modification of the charge conservation. Finally, the Feynman integral appearing in the one-loop contribution to the vacuum polarization diagram is calculated. There are no non-planar diagrams, but the UV/IR mixing appears nevertheless. We comment on this unexpected result.

Introduction

L_{\infty}
-algebras are generalizations of differential graded Lie algebras with infinitely-many graded antisymmetric
brackets, related to each other by higher homotopy versions of the
Jacobi identity. In
[
1
]
it was suggested that the complete data of
any classical field theory with generalized gauge symmetries fit into cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebras
with finitely-many non-vanishing brackets, encoding both gauge
transformations and dynamics. It was then showed that the existence of such an

L_{\infty}
-algebra formulation is a consequence of the duality with the BV–BRST
formalism for perturbative field theories
[
2
]
.

L_{\infty}
-algebras naturally appear in noncommutative gauge theory
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
and noncommutative gravity
[
7
]
(see also the contribution
[
8
]
to these proceedings for a brief review). In
[
3
]
it was shown that the semi-classical limit of a noncommutative and/or nonassociative gauge theory can be encoded in an infinite dimensional
L_{\infty}
-algebra which is constructed order by order in the deformation parameter. Furthermore, it was shown in
[
5
]
that the Seiberg-Witten map relating noncommutative gauge theory with the corresponding commutative gauge theory is a
L_{\infty}
quasi-isomorphism. Using the Drinfel’d twist deformation method in our recent work
[
7
,
9
]
we constructed a deformation of a
L_{\infty}
-algebra, the braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The corresponding field theory is then the braided gauge theory. It is different compared to the usual noncommutative gauge theory, the
\star
-gauge theory. The braided gauge transformations close in the Lie algebra of the undeformed gauge symmetry and they have a braided Leibniz rule.

In this paper we illustrate our construction of braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra and braided gauge theories on the example of braided electrodynamics: braided
U(1)
gauge theory coupled to a charged Dirac spinor. We start by reviewing some basic facts about
L_{\infty}
-algebras and their relation with classical field theories. Then we introduce the braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra and the corresponding braided gauge theory. To illustrate our construction, we discuss in details the braided electrodynamics and its properties. In particular, the theory remains abelian and there are no three and four photon vertices. The quantization of field theories with braided symmetries is currently under development. To gain some preliminary insight, here we calculate the standard Feynman integrals which appear in the one-loop contribution to the vacuum polarization. We find UV/IR mixing, but no non-planar diagrams. This unexpected result should be understood once the full quantum field theory of braided (gauge) field theories is constructed. Some preliminary results on these problems can be found in
[
10
,
11
]
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,V_{k}
with graded antisymmetric multilinear maps called
n
-brackets

\displaystyle\ell_{n}:\text{\Large$\otimes$}^{n}V\longrightarrow

\displaystyle V\ ,\quad a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\longmapsto\ell_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(\dots,a,a^{\prime},\dots)

\displaystyle=\>-(-1)^{|a|\,|a^{\prime}|}\,\ell_{n}(\dots,a^{\prime},a,\dots)\ ,

where
|a|
is the degree of a homogeneous element
a\in V
. The
n
-brackets must also fulfil homotopy relations. The first three relations are given by

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle 1:\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{1}(a)\big)=0,

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle 2:\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{2}(a_{1},a_{2})\big)=\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{1}(a_{1}),a_{2}\big)+(-1)^{|a_{1}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(a_{1},\ell_{1}(a_{2})\big)\ ,

(1)

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle 3:\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{3}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\big)=-\ell_{3}\big(\ell_{1}(a_{1}),a_{2},a_{3}\big)-(-1)^{|a_{1}|}\,\ell_{3}\big(a_{1},\ell_{1}(a_{2}),a_{3}\big)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|}\,\ell_{3}\big(a_{1},a_{2},\ell_{1}(a_{3})\big)

\displaystyle-\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(a_{1},a_{2}),a_{3}\big)-(-1)^{(|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|)\,|a_{3}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(a_{3},a_{1}),a_{2}\big)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|)\,|a_{1}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(a_{2},a_{3}),a_{1}\big)

Cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebras contain an additional structure called cyclic pairing that is a graded, symmetric, and non-degenerate bilinear map
\langle-,-\rangle:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle a_{0},\ell_{n}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\rangle=(-1)^{n+(|a_{0}|+|a_{n}|)\,n+|a_{n}|\,\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\,|a_{i}|}\ \langle a_{n},\ell_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\rangle,\>\>n\geq 1.

(2)

In order to relate this formalism to a (gauge) field theory, we first define the graded vector space
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
. This space contains gauge parameters
\rho\in V_{0}
, gauge fields
A\in V_{1}
, equations of motion
F_{A}\in V_{2}
, and II Noether identities
{\rm d}_{A}F_{A}\in V_{3}
. The gauge transformations
\delta_{\rho}A
, equations of motion
F_{A}=0
, gauge invariant action functional

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}A

\displaystyle=\ell_{1}(\rho)+\ell_{2}(\rho,A)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{3}(\rho,A,A)+\dots,

(3)

\displaystyle F_{A}

\displaystyle=\ell_{1}(A)-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{2}(A,A)-\frac{1}{3!}\ell_{3}(A,A,A)+\dots,

(4)

\displaystyle S(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle A,\ell_{1}(A)\rangle-\frac{1}{3!}\langle A,\ell_{2}(A,A)\rangle+\dots,

(5)

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{A}F_{A}

\displaystyle=\ell_{1}(F_{A})+\ell_{2}(F_{A},A)+\dots

(6)

In field theories the cyclic pairing of degree
-3

\langle-,-\rangle:V_{k}\otimes V_{3-k}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad k\leq 3\ .

The variation principle is then written as

\delta S(A)=\langle\delta A\,,\,F_{A}\rangle\ .

Braided gauge theory

Noncommutative deformation of gauge theories can be defined in different ways. One of the most studied examples is that of
\star
-gauge theories
[
13
,
12
]
. Let us again use the gauge group
G
with the corresponding hermitian Lie algebra generators
T^{a}
,
a=1\dots n
, the invariant quadratic form
\textrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and commutation relations
\left[A^{a},A^{b}\right]=if^{abc}T^{c}
. Let the gauge field
A
be a Lie algebra valued one-form,
A=A^{a}_{\mu}T^{a}{\rm d}x^{\mu}
1
1

1

Note that we work with the matrix Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
. An infinitesimal gauge transformation is defined as

\tilde{\delta}_{\rho}^{\star}A={\rm d}\rho+i[\rho\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}A]={\rm d}\rho+i\big(\rho\star A-A\star\rho\big)\

with the undeformed Leibniz rule

\triangle(\tilde{\delta}^{\star}_{\rho})=\tilde{\delta}^{\star}_{\rho}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\tilde{\delta}^{\star}_{\rho}\ .

However, it is easily checked that

[\rho^{a}T^{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}A^{b}T^{b}]=\frac{1}{2}\{\rho^{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}A^{b}\}[T^{a},T^{b}]+\frac{1}{2}[\rho^{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{,}}A^{b}]\{T^{a},T^{b}\}.

Therefore, in general these transformations do not close in the corresponding Lie algebra. To
circumvent this problem, one either works with the
u(n)
algebra in its fundamental representation, or enlarges the algebra to the universal enveloping algebra. Working with the universal enveloping algebra results in infinitely many new degrees of freedom, and one might then use the Seiberg-Witten map to express all of the new degrees of freedom in terms of the corresponding classical (commutative) degrees of freedom
[
14
]
.

Let us now define a noncommutative gauge theory using the notion of a braided Lie algebra
[
15
]
. A short review of the twist formalism,
R
matrix and the
\star
-products is presented in Appendix
A
. For simplicity, we consider the example of braided Chern-Simons non-abelian gauge theory in
3D
. The gauge field
A=A^{a}_{\mu}T^{a}{\rm d}x^{\mu}
transforms as (
\rho=\rho^{a}T^{a}
)

\delta_{\rho}^{\star}A={\rm d}\rho+i[\rho,A]_{\star}={\rm d}\rho+i\big(\rho\star A-\mathrm{R}_{k}(A)\star\mathrm{R}^{k}(\rho)\big)\ .

(7)

It is easily verified that the braided commutator closes in the Lie algebra

[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]_{\star}=if^{abc}\rho_{1}^{b}\star\rho_{2}^{c}T^{a}.

Transformations (
7
) have the braided Leibniz rule

\triangle_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta^{\star}_{\rho})=\delta^{\star}_{\rho}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{R}_{k}\otimes\delta^{\star}_{\mathrm{R}^{k}(\rho)\ ,}

(8)

and close the braided algebra

\displaystyle\big[\delta_{\rho_{1}}^{\star},\delta_{\rho_{2}}^{\star}\big]_{\circ}^{\star}\>=

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho_{1}}^{\star}\circ\delta_{\rho_{2}}^{\star}-\delta_{\mathrm{R}_{k}(\rho_{2})}^{\star}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{R}^{k}(\rho_{1})}^{\star}=\delta_{-i[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]_{\star}}^{\star}\ .

Note that in this setting we can define left and right gauge transformations

\delta_{\rho}^{\star{\mbox{\tiny L}}}A={\rm d}\rho+i[\rho,A]_{\star}\>{\mbox{ and }}\>\delta_{\rho}^{\star{\mbox{\tiny R}}}A={\rm d}\rho-i[A,\rho]_{\star}.

They are different. We will work only with left gauge transformations, analogous conclusions also hold for the right gauge transformations, see
[
7
]
for more details.

The braided curvature of the gauge field
A
is given by

F_{A}^{\star}={\rm d}A-i\frac{1}{2}\,[A,A]_{\star}=\big({\rm d}A^{a}+\frac{1}{2}f^{abc}A^{b}\wedge_{\star}A^{c}\big)T^{a}\ ,

and transforms covariantly

\delta_{\rho}^{\star}F_{A}^{\star}=i[\rho,F_{A}^{\star}]_{\star}\ .

The gauge invariant action is defined as

S_{\star}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{M}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big(A\wedge_{\star}{\rm d}A-\frac{i}{3}\,A\wedge_{\star}[A,A]_{\star}\Big)\ .

Unlike in the commutative case, the braided second Noether identity is not linear in field equations

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{A}^{\star}F_{A}^{\star}=

\displaystyle\>{\rm d}F_{A}^{\star}-\frac{i}{2}\,[A,F_{A}^{\star}]_{\star}-\frac{i}{2}\,[F_{A}^{\star},A]_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{4}\,[\mathrm{R}_{k}(A),[\mathrm{R}^{k}(A),A]_{\star}]_{\star}=0.

There is an additional term on the right-hand side that depends only on the gauge field.

Braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra

Starting with a suitable classical
L_{\infty}
-algebra,
\mathscr{L}
, a braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra,
\mathscr{L}^{\star}
, can be constructed using Drinfel’d twist deformation techniques as in
[
7
]
. More details on the Drinfel’d twist deformation formalism can be found in
[

]

Following the prescription outlined in Appendix
A
, we set the first bracket
\ell_{1}^{\star}:=\ell_{1}
and

\displaystyle\ell_{n}^{\star}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}):=\ell_{n}(a_{1}\otimes_{\star}\cdots\otimes_{\star}a_{n})

(9)

for
n\geq 2
, where
a\otimes_{\star}a^{\prime}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a\otimes a^{\prime})=\bar{\mathrm{f}}^{k}(a)\otimes\bar{\mathrm{f}}_{k}(a^{\prime})
for
a,a^{\prime}\in V
. These define multilinear maps
\ell_{n}^{\star}:V^{\otimes n}\to V
which are braided graded antisymmetric:

\displaystyle\ell_{n}^{\star}(\dots,a,a^{\prime},\dots)=-(-1)^{|a|\,|a^{\prime}|}\,\ell_{n}^{\star}\big(\dots,\mathrm{R}_{k}(a^{\prime}),\mathrm{R}^{k}(a),\dots\big)\ .

The first and second homotopy relations are unchanged with respect to the corresponding classical homotopy relations, that is, the braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathscr{L}^{\star}
still has underlying cochain complex
(V,\ell_{1})
and
\ell_{2}^{\star}
is again a cochain map. The third homotopy relation is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ell^{\star}_{2}\big(\ell^{\star}_{2}(a_{1},a_{2}),a_{3}\big)-(-1)^{|a_{2}|\,|a_{3}|}\,\ell^{\star}_{2}\big(\ell^{\star}_{2}(a_{1},\mathrm{R}_{k}(a_{3})),\mathrm{R}^{k}(a_{2})\big)\\
&\hskip 184.9429pt+(-1)^{(|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|)\,|a_{1}|}\,\ell^{\star}_{2}\big(\ell^{\star}_{2}(\mathrm{R}_{k}(a_{2}),\mathrm{R}_{l}(a_{3})),\mathrm{R}^{l}\,\mathrm{R}^{k}(a_{1})\big)\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 42.67912pt=-\ell^{\star}_{3}\big(\ell_{1}(a_{1}),a_{2},a_{3}\big)-(-1)^{|a_{1}|}\,\ell^{\star}_{3}\big(a_{1},\ell_{1}(a_{2}),a_{3}\big)-(-1)^{|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|}\,\ell^{\star}_{3}\big(a_{1},a_{2},\ell_{1}(a_{3})\big)\\
&\hskip 184.9429pt-\ell_{1}\big(\ell^{\star}_{3}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\big)\ .\end{split}

(10)

We see that the non-trivial braiding now appears in this relation, which indicates that the braided graded Jacobi identity for
\ell_{2}^{\star}
is violated by the cochain homotopy
\ell_{3}^{\star}
. Nontrivial braiding also appears in the higher homotopy relations
[
7
]
.

The graded symmetry of the cyclic pairing
\langle-,-\rangle

implies that the twisted pairing
\langle-,-\rangle_{\star}
is naturally
braided graded symmetric

\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle_{\star}=\langle\bar{\mathrm{f}}^{k}(a_{1}),\bar{\mathrm{f}}_{k}(a_{2})\rangle=(-1)^{|a_{1}|\,|a_{2}|}\,\langle\mathrm{R}_{k}(a_{2}),\mathrm{R}^{k}(a_{1})\rangle_{\star}\ .

(11)

However, for applications to field theory, we have to restrict to compatible Drinfel’d twists
[
7
]
that result in a strictly graded symmetric pairing

\displaystyle\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle_{\star}=(-1)^{|a_{1}|\,|a_{2}|}\,\langle a_{2},a_{1}\rangle_{\star}

for all homogeneous
a_{1},a_{2}\in V
. In this case,
\mathscr{L}^{\star}
becomes a strictly cyclic braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Let
\mathscr{L}^{\star}=(V,\{\ell^{\star}_{n}\})
be a
4
-term braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra, obtained by twist deformation of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathscr{L}=(V,\{\ell_{n}\})
, which organizes the symmetries and dynamics of a classical field theory. For a gauge parameter
\rho\in V_{0}
, we define the braided gauge variation of a field
A\in V_{1}
by

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}^{\star}A=\ell_{1}(\rho)+\ell_{2}^{\star}(\rho,A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell_{3}^{\star}(\rho,A,A)+\cdots\ .

(12)

Braided covariant dynamics are described by the equations of motion
F_{A}^{\star}=0

\displaystyle F_{A}^{\star}=\ell_{1}(A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell_{2}^{\star}(A,A)-\tfrac{1}{6}\,\ell_{3}^{\star}(A,A,A)+\cdots

(13)

that transform covariantly as

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}^{\star}F_{A}^{\star}=\ell_{2}^{\star}(\rho,F_{A}^{\star})+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(\ell_{3}^{\star}(\rho,F_{A}^{\star},A)-\ell_{3}^{\star}(\rho,A,F_{A}^{\star})\big)+\cdots\ ,

(14)

for all gauge parameters
\rho\in V_{0}
.

For the field theories considered in this paper, the braided gauge transformations obey the off-shell closure relation in terms of the braided commutator:

\displaystyle\big[\delta_{\rho_{1}}^{\star},\delta_{\rho_{2}}^{\star}\big]_{\circ}^{\star}:=\delta_{\rho_{1}}^{\star}\circ\delta_{\rho_{2}}^{\star}-\delta_{\mathrm{R}_{k}(\rho_{2})}^{\star}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{R}^{k}(\rho_{1})}^{\star}=\delta_{-\ell^{\star}_{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})}^{\star}\ .

(15)

Corresponding to the braided gauge symmetry, a suitable combination of the braided homotopy relations leads to an identity

\displaystyle{\rm d}^{\star}_{A}F_{A}^{\star}:

\displaystyle\!=\ell_{1}(F_{A}^{\star})+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(\ell^{\star}_{2}(F_{A}^{\star},A)-\ell^{\star}_{2}(A,F_{A}^{\star})\big)+\tfrac{1}{6}\,\ell_{1}\big(\ell^{\star}_{3}(A,A,A)\big)+\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\quad\,+\tfrac{1}{8}\,\big(\ell_{2}^{\star}(\ell_{2}^{\star}(A,A),A)-\ell_{2}^{\star}(A,\ell_{2}^{\star}(A,A))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\quad\,+\tfrac{1}{12}\,\big(\ell_{2}^{\star}(\ell_{3}^{\star}(A,A,A),A)-\ell_{2}^{\star}(A,\ell_{3}^{\star}(A,A,A))\big)+\cdots\ =\ 0\ .

(16)

We have already seen in the example of the braided Chern-Simons theory that, unlike the classical Noether identity (
6
), the braided Noether identity (
3
) is no longer linear in the equations of motion
F^{\star}_{A}
and contains inhomogeneous terms involving brackets of the fields
A
themselves. This is related to the violations of the Bianchi identities in braided gauge theories
[
7
]
. In the classical limit, where
\mathcal{R}=1\otimes 1
, the braided Noether identity (
3
) reduces to the classical formula (
6
).

For a Lagrangian field theory, using the (strictly) cyclic inner product one can define an analogue of the action functional for the braided field theory as

\displaystyle S_{\star}(A):=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle A,\ell_{1}(A)\rangle_{\star}-\tfrac{1}{6}\,\langle A,\ell^{\star}_{2}(A,A)\rangle_{\star}-\tfrac{1}{24}\,\langle A,\ell^{\star}_{3}(A,A,A)\rangle_{\star}+\cdots\ ,

(17)

whose variational principle yields the braided equations of motion
F_{A}^{\star}=0
.
This action functional is invariant under braided gauge transformations:

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}^{\star}S_{\star}(A)=0\ ,

(18)

for all
\rho\in V_{0}
and
A\in V_{1}
. Note that the free fields of braided field theory are unchanged from the classical field theory. Only the interaction vertices, corresponding to the higher brackets
\ell_{n}^{\star}
for
n\geq 2
, are modified by the braided noncommutative deformation.

A first look at quantization

The usual starting point for quantization of a field theory is the classical action. In the case of braided electrodynamics, we can start from (
71
) and split it into three pieces

\displaystyle S_{\star}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,\Big\{-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}\star F_{\mu\nu}+\bar{\psi}\star i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\frac{e}{2}\Big(\bar{\psi}\star A_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}\star\psi+\bar{\psi}\star\mathrm{R}_{k}(A_{\mu})\gamma^{\mu}\star\mathrm{R}^{k}(\psi)\Big\}

Since the action (

symmetry. The full action is then given by

5
Outlook

In this paper we applied the recently developed formalism of braided
L_{\infty}
-algebras to construct a noncommutative deformation of the classical electrodynamics. The obtained theory, the braided electrodynamics is invariant under the braided
U(1)
gauge symmetry, which is still abelian. As a consequence, there are no three and four photon interaction vertices in the action
S_{\star}
(
71
). Therefore, the only interaction vertex is the fermion-photon vertex. This is different compared to the
\star
-gauge deformation of the classical electrodynamics which is nonabelian and as a consequence the three and four photon interaction vertices appear.

We calculated the Feynman integrals which appear in the one-loop contribution to the vacuum polarization. Although there are no non-planar diagrams contributions, we find the UV/IR mixing. This unexpected result is consistent with claims about equivalence of various combinations of (non)commutative field theories and (un)braided statistics made in
[
11
]
.

The correct quantization of theories with braided symmetries should be implemented with braided homotopy algebraic techniques, and will be addressed in
[
18
]
. The results of
[
10
,
11
]
suggest that there is no UV/IR mixing, at least in the scalar field theories which do not possess gauge symmetries. The results reported in this preliminary investigation suggest that there are still many new physical features to be uncovered in braided quantum field theory.

Kenmotsu Lie groups
Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
Kenmotsu manifolds constitute an important subclass of the class of contact Riemannian manifolds. In this note, we determine entirely connected and simply-connected Lie groups having a left invariant Kenmotsu structure. We show also that these Lie groups are Einstein Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction and main result

Contact geometry is a crucial area in modern differential geometry. K. Kenmotsu introduced a distinctive class of contact Riemannian manifolds which later became known as Kenmotsu manifolds. Kenmotsu demonstrated that a Kenmotsu manifold is locally a warped product
I\times_{f}N

where
I

is an interval and
N

is a Kähler manifold, with the warping function
f(t)=ke^{t}

where
k

is a non-zero constant. Over the years, many researchers have explored the geometry of Kenmotsu manifolds

.

An almost contact metric manifold is a
(2n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,h)
that admits a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi
and a characteristic vector field
\xi
satisfying the following relations

\phi^{2}(X)=-X+\eta(X)\xi,\;h(\phi(X),\phi(Y))=h(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),

(1)

for any
X,Y\in TM
where
\eta(X)=h(X,\xi)
. If the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of an almost contact metric manifold
(M,g,\phi,\xi)

satisfies

\nabla_{X}(\phi)Y=h(\phi X,Y)-\eta(Y)\phi(X)

(2)

for any
X,Y\in TM
, then it is called a Kenmotsu manifold
[
1
]
.

A
Kenmotsu Lie group
is a Lie group
G
endowed with a Kenmotsu structure
(h,\phi,\xi)
where
(h,\phi,\xi)
are left invariant. In this Note, we give a complete description of Kenmotsu Lie groups by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,h,\phi,\xi)
be a connected and simply-connected Kenmotsu Lie group of dimension
2n+1
. Then there exists
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(G,h,\phi,\xi)
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n},h_{0},\phi_{0},\xi_{0})
where the group product is given by

(t_{1},(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})).(t_{2},(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{n}^{\prime}))=(t_{1}+t_{2},(z_{1}+z_{1}^{\prime}e^{-t_{1}(1+\imath\lambda_{1})},\ldots,z_{n}+z_{n}^{\prime}e^{-t_{1}(1+\imath\lambda_{n})})),

h_{0}=dt^{2}+e^{2t}\mathrm{can}
,
\phi_{0}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})=0
, the restriction of
\phi_{0}
to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the canonical complex structure of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\xi_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
\mathrm{can}
is the canonical metric of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Moreover,
(G,h)
is Einstein and its Ricci curvature satisfies
{\mathrm{ric}}=-2nh
.

Beside the introduction, this Note contains one section devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we aim to prove Theorem
1.1
. To do so, we proceed by establishing many intermediary results.

We start by describing our model of Kenmotsu Lie groups. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the Lie group
G_{\lambda}
whose underlying manifold is
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the multiplication is given by

(t_{1},(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})).(t_{2},(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{n}^{\prime}))=(t_{1}+t_{2},(z_{1}+z_{1}^{\prime}e^{-t_{1}(1+\imath\lambda_{1})},\ldots,z_{n}+z_{n}^{\prime}e^{-t_{1}(1+\imath\lambda_{n})})).

(3)

The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda}
of
G_{\lambda}
can be identified as a vector space to
T_{(0,0)}G_{\lambda}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For any
(a,u)\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda}
, we denote by
(a,u)^{\ell}
the left invariant vector field on
G_{\lambda}
associated to
(a,u)
.
We have

\displaystyle(a,u)^{l}(t,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}_{|s=0}(t,z).(sa,su)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}_{|s=0}(t+sa,(z_{1}+su_{1}e^{-t(1+\imath\lambda_{1})},\ldots,z_{n}+su_{n}e^{-t(1+\imath\lambda_{n})}))

\displaystyle=(a,u_{1}e^{-t(1+\imath\lambda_{1})},\ldots,u_{n}e^{-t(1+\imath\lambda_{n})}))

Denote by
e_{0}=1
a generator of
\mathbb{R}
,
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a complex vector space and put
f_{j}=\imath e_{j}
. We have, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

e_{0}^{\ell}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\;e_{j}^{l}=e^{-t}\left(\cos(\lambda_{j}t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\sin(\lambda_{j}t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{j}^{l}=e^{-t}\left(\sin(\lambda_{j}t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\cos(\lambda_{j}t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right),

where
(t,x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{n})
is the coordinates system associated to the basis
(e_{0},e_{1},f_{1},\ldots,e_{n},f_{n})
. We have obviously that, for any
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

[e_{j}^{\ell},e_{k}^{\ell}]=[f_{j}^{\ell},f_{k}^{\ell}]=[e_{j}^{\ell},f_{k}^{\ell}]=0,\;[e_{0}^{\ell},e_{j}^{\ell}]=-e_{j}^{\ell}-\lambda_{j}f_{j}^{\ell}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[e_{0}^{\ell},f_{j}^{\ell}]=-f_{j}^{\ell}+\lambda_{j}e_{j}^{\ell}.

(4)

Let
h_{0}
be the left invariant metric on
G_{\lambda}
for which
(e_{0}^{\ell},e_{1}^{\ell},f_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,e_{n}^{\ell},f_{n}^{\ell})
is orthonormal. From the relations

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}=e^{t}\left(\cos(\lambda_{j}t)e_{j}^{\ell}+\sin(\lambda_{j}t)f_{j}^{\ell}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}=e^{t}\left(-\sin(\lambda_{j}t)e_{j}^{\ell}+\cos(\lambda_{j}t)f_{j}^{\ell}\right)

we deduce that

h_{0}=dt^{2}+e^{2t}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(dx_{j}^{2}+dy_{j}^{2}\right).

Finally, put
\xi_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=e_{0}^{\ell}
and denote by
\phi_{0}
the left invariant
(1,1)
-tensor field given by
\phi_{0}(e_{0}^{\ell})=0
,
\phi_{O}(e_{j}^{\ell})=f_{j}^{\ell}
and
\phi_{O}(f_{j}^{\ell})=-e_{j}^{\ell}
for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Now we establish a useful lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be a real vector space of dimension
2n
,
\langle\;,\;\rangle
a scalar product on
V
,
J:V\longrightarrow V
a skew-symmetric isomorphism such that
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}_{V}
and
D:V\longrightarrow V
an endomorphism such that
[J,D]=0
and
D+D^{t}=-2\mathrm{Id}_{V}
where
D^{t}
is the adjoint of

D
with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Then there exists
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},Je_{1},\ldots,e_{n},Je_{n})
of
V
such that, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

De_{j}=-e_{j}-\lambda_{j}Je_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad DJe_{j}=-Je_{j}+\lambda_{j}e_{j}.

Proof.

The real vector space
V
has also a structure of complex vector space given by

(a+\imath b).v=av+bJv,\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R},v\in V,

and since
[J,D]=0
then
D
is a complex endomorphism. Moreover, the symmetric bilinear form given by

h(u,v)=\langle u,v\rangle+\imath\langle u,Jv\rangle

is a Hermitian metric and
D^{t}
is also the adjoint of
D
with respect to
h
.
From the relation
D+D^{t}=-2\mathrm{Id}_{V}
, we deduce that

[D,D^{t}]=0
, i.e.,
D
is normal and hence it is diagonalizable. Thus there exists
(\alpha_{1},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n})
and a
h
-orthonormal complex basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
such that, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

De_{j}=(\alpha_{j}-\imath\lambda_{j})e_{j}=\alpha_{j}e_{j}-\lambda_{j}Je_{j}.

But
A=D+\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is skew-symmetric and

\langle Ae_{j},e_{j}\rangle=\langle(\alpha_{j}+1)e_{j}-\lambda_{j}Je_{j},e_{j}\rangle=(\alpha_{j}+1)\langle e_{j},e_{j}\rangle=0

and hence
\alpha_{j}=-1
which completes the proof.
∎

Let
G
be a Lie group and
{\mathfrak{g}}
its Lie algebra identified with the space of left invariant vector fields on
G
. For any left invariant metric
h
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we denote by
\langle\;,\;\rangle
its restriction to
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The Levi-Civita product
\mathrm{L}:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is the restriction of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
h
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
and it is given by

2\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}Y,Z\rangle=\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle[Z,X],Y\rangle+\langle[Z,Y],X\rangle,\quad X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

(5)

This product is characterized by the facts that, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\mathrm{L}_{X}+\mathrm{L}_{X}^{t}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{L}_{X}Y-\mathrm{L}_{Y}X=[X,Y].

(6)

A left invariant almost complex structure on
G
is entirely determined by an isomorphism
J:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
J^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. When
h
is Hermitian with respect to
J
and
\nabla J=0
,
(G,h,J)
is called a Kähler Lie group and
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle,J)
is called a Kähler Lie algebra. Note that the condition
\nabla J=0
is equivalent to
[\mathrm{L}_{X},J]=0
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Recall that a Kenmotsu Lie group is a Lie group
G
endowed with a left invariant metric
h
, a left invariant
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi:TG\longrightarrow TG
and a left invariant vector field
\xi
satisfying (
1
) and (
2
). Since
(h,\phi)
are left invariant, they are entirely determined by their restrictions
(\langle\;,\;\rangle,\phi)
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
The relations (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent to

\begin{cases}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle=1,\;\phi^{2}(X)=-X+\eta(X)\xi,\;\langle\phi(X),\phi(Y)\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle-\eta(X)\eta(Y),\\
\mathrm{L}_{X}\phi(Y)-\phi(\mathrm{L}_{X}Y)=\langle\phi(X),Y\rangle\xi-\eta(Y)\phi(X),\end{cases}

(7)

for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
\eta
is the left invariant 1-form given by
\eta(X)=\langle X,\xi\rangle
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\begin{cases}\phi(\xi)=0,\;\eta\circ\phi=0,\;\phi^{t}+\phi=0,\\
\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi=X-\eta(X)\xi,\;[\mathrm{L}_{\xi},\phi]=0,\;\prec\eta,\mathrm{L}_{X}Y\succ=-\langle X,Y\rangle+\eta(X)\eta(Y),\end{cases}

(8)

where
\phi^{t}
is the adjoint of
\phi
with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.
In particular,
d\eta=0
.

Proof.

The relations in the first line of (
7
) can be checked easily. From the last relation in (
7
) and (
6
), we have
\phi(\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi)=\phi(X)
and
\eta(\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi)=\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi,\xi\rangle=0
.
Thus

\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi=X-\eta(X)\xi.

Since
\phi(\xi)=0
, we deduce from the last relation in (
7
) that

[\mathrm{L}_{\xi},\phi]=0
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\prec\eta,\mathrm{L}_{X}Y\succ

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}Y,\xi\rangle=-\langle Y,\mathrm{L}_{X}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle X,Y\rangle+\eta(X)\eta(Y).

Finally, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

d\eta(X,Y)=-\eta([X,Y])=-\eta(\mathrm{L}_{X}Y)+\eta(\mathrm{L}_{Y}X)=0.

∎

Put
{\mathfrak{h}}=\xi^{\perp}=\ker\eta
. From the relation
d\eta=0
, we have
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}
and hence
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Moreover, since
\eta\circ\phi=0
,
\phi
leaves
{\mathfrak{h}}
invariant and induces a isomorphism
J
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
. Denote by
D
(resp.
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}
) the restriction of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{\xi}
(resp.
\langle\;,\;\rangle
) to
{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}},J)
is a Kähler Lie algebra and
D
is an invertible derivation of
{\mathfrak{h}}
satisfying
[D,J]=0
and
D+D^{t}=-2\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Proof.

We have obviously from (
7
) that
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
h
is Hermitian with respect to
J
. Let us compute the Levi-Civita product
\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y
of
({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
. For any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y,Z\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=2\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}Y,Z\rangle

and hence

\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y-\mathrm{L}_{X}Y\in\mathbb{R}\xi
. Moreover, since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal and by virtue of (
6
),

2\langle\mathrm{L}_{X}Y,\xi\rangle=\langle DX,Y\rangle+\langle DY,X\rangle.

We deduce that

\mathrm{L}_{X}Y=\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y+\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle DX,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}+\langle DY,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\xi,\quad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

From the last relation in (
7
) and since
\phi(\mathrm{L}_{X}Y)=\phi(\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y)
, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathrm{L}_{X}\phi(Y)-\phi(\mathrm{L}_{X}Y)-\langle JX,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\xi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}JY+\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle DX,JY\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}+\langle DJY,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\xi-J(\mathrm{L}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}}Y)-\langle JX,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\xi.

Thus

[\mathrm{L}_{X},J]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad D+D^{t}=-2\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}}.

On the other hand,

2\langle\mathrm{L}_{\xi}X,Y\rangle=\langle DX,Y\rangle-\langle DY,X\rangle

and hence

\mathrm{L}_{\xi}X=\frac{1}{2}(DX-D^{t}Y),\quad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

From the last relation in (
7
),

\mathrm{L}_{\xi}JX-J\mathrm{L}_{\xi}X=0

and hence

[J,D-D^{t}]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[J,D]=0.

∎

Now, we get all the needed ingredients to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(G,h,\phi,\xi)
be a Kenmotsu Lie group of dimension
2n+1
. We have shown that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathbb{R}\xi
where
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal having an invertible derivation
D=({\mathrm{ad}}_{\xi})_{|{\mathfrak{h}}}
and a complex isomorphism
J
such that
({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}},J)
is a Kähler Lie algebra,
[J,D]=0
and
D+D^{t}=-2\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
. According to
[
2
]
, a Lie algebra with an invertible derivation must be nilpotent. On the other hand, a nilpotent Kähler Lie algebra must be abelian (see
[
3
]
). Now, according to Lemma
2.1
, there exists
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},Je_{1},\ldots,e_{n},Je_{n})
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
such that, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

De_{j}=-e_{j}-\lambda_{j}Je_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad DJe_{j}=-Je_{j}+\lambda_{j}e_{j}.

By comparing these Lie brackets to (
4
), one can see that
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle,\phi,\xi)
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of the model
(G_{\lambda},h_{0},\phi_{0},\xi_{0})
. The fact that

(G_{\lambda},h_{0})
is Einstein and its Ricci curvature satisfies
{\mathrm{ric}}=-2nh_{0}
can be checked by a straightforward computation.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

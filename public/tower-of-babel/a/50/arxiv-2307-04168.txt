Possible open charm molecular pentaquarks from Λ c ​ K ( ∗ ) / Σ c ​ K ( ∗ ) \Lambda_{c}K^{(*)}/\Sigma_{c}K^{(*)} interactions

Abstract
In this work, we adopt the one-boson-exchange model to study the Y c ​ K ( ∗ ) ​ ( Y c = Λ c , Σ c ) Y_{c}K^{(*)}(Y_{c}=\Lambda_{c},\Sigma_{c}) interactions. After considering both of the S − D S-D wave mixing effects and the coupled channel effects, we can predict several possible open-charm molecular pentaquarks, i.e., the single Σ c ​ K ∗ \Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecular states with I ⁡ ( J P ) = 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) I(J^{P})=1/2(1/2^{-}) , 1 / 2 ​ ( 3 / 2 − ) 1/2(3/2^{-}) and 3 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) 3/2(1/2^{-}) , the coupled Λ c ​ K ∗ / Σ c ​ K ∗ \Lambda_{c}K^{*}/\Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecular states with 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) 1/2(1/2^{-}) and 1 / 2 ​ ( 3 / 2 − ) 1/2(3/2^{-}) , and the coupled Σ c ​ K / Λ c ​ K ∗ / Σ c ​ K ∗ \Sigma_{c}K/\Lambda_{c}K^{*}/\Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecular state with 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) 1/2(1/2^{-}) . I

In the past decades, the observations of
X/Y/Z/P_{c}/T_{cc}
structures have stimulated theorist’s extensive interest in

X/Y/Z/P_{c}/T_{cc}
structures, but also can improve our knowledge of the non-perturbative behavior of the quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

III
The OBE effective potentials and the numerical results

Following the above procedures, we can deduce the concrete OBE effective potentials for the
Y_{c}K^{(*)}
systems with different quantum configurations. After that, we adopt the obtained OBE effective potentials to solve the coupled channel Schrödinger equations. By doing this, we can search for the bound state solutions. A system with the reasonable bound state solutions can be recommended as the good hadronic molecular candidate, where the binding energy is taken from several MeV to several tens MeV, and the root-mean-square (RMS) radius is a few fm or larger.

III.1
The
\Lambda_{c}K^{(*)}
systems

The total OBE effective potentials for the single
\Lambda_{c}K
system can be written as

\displaystyle V_{\Lambda_{c}K\to\Lambda_{c}K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{B}g_{\sigma}\chi_{3}^{{\dagger}}\chi_{1}Y(\Lambda,m_{\sigma},r)

(26)

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta_{B}g_{V}g}{4}\chi_{3}^{{\dagger}}\chi_{1}Y(\Lambda,m_{\omega},r).

Here, we define

\displaystyle Y(\Lambda,m,{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi r}(e^{-mr}-e^{-\Lambda r})-\frac{\Lambda^{2}-m^{2}}{8\pi\Lambda}e^{-\Lambda r}.

(27)

As shown in Eq. (
26

\sigma

\omega

\sigma
exchange provides an attractive interaction, whereas the
\omega

\rho

\Lambda_{c}-\Lambda_{c}-\rho
coupling. Since the

After solving the Schrödinger equation, we don’t find bound state solutions in the cutoff region
0.8\leq\Lambda\leq 5.0

In summary, our results can predict several possible open charm molecular pentaquarks, the coupled
\Lambda_{c}K^{*}/\Sigma_{c}K^{*}

IV
Summary

The study of the exotic states is an important and interesting issue in the hadron physics. Searching for the hadronic molecular states can not only enrich the family of the exotic states, but also help us to understand the essential hadron-hadron interactions. Very recently, the LHCb collaboration observed two open heavy flavor multiquarks
T_{c\bar{s}}^{a0(++)}
. Their near threshold behavior inspires the isovector
D^{*}K^{*}
molecular explanations to them. In our former paper, we found the
D^{*}K^{*}
state with
I(J^{P})=1(0^{+})
can be possible molecular candidate by adopting the OBE effective potentials
Chen:2016ypj
.

molecule with

. We expect the experimentalists to search for these predicted open charm molecular pentaquarks.

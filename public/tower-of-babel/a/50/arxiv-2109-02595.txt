Global Calderón–Zygmund theory for parabolic p p -Laplacian system: the case 1 < p ≤ 2 ​ n n + 2 1<p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2}
Ke Chen, Quoc-Hung Nguyen and Na Zhao

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to establish global Calderón–Zygmund theory to parabolic p p -Laplacian system: u t − div ( | ∇ u | p − 2 ∇ u ) = div ( | F | p − 2 F ) in Ω × ( 0 , T ) ⊂ ℝ n + 1 , u_{t}-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F)~\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}, proving that F ∈ L q ⇒ ∇ u ∈ L q , F\in L^{q}\Rightarrow\nabla u\in L^{q}, for any q > max ⁡ { p , n ⁡ ( 2 − p ) 2 } q>\max\{p,\frac{n(2-p)}{2}\} and p > 1 p>1 . Acerbi and Mingione [ 2 ] proved this estimate in the case p > 2 ​ n n + 2 p>\frac{2n}{n+2} . In this article we settle the case 1 < p ≤ 2 ​ n n + 2 1<p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2} . We also treat systems with discontinuous coefficients having small BMO (bounded mean oscillation) norm.

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we consider the following inhomogeneous parabolic system:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}u_{t}-\operatorname{div}(a(x,t)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F)&\text{in }\Omega_{T},\\
u=0&\text{on}\ \partial_{p}\Omega_{T}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

with a given function
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
. Here
a(x,t)
satisfies

c_{a}\leq a(x,t)\leq C_{a},\quad\quad\forall(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}

(1.2)

for some constants
c_{a},C_{a}>0
. And

\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T),T>0
, is a cylindrical domain with parabolic boundary
\partial_{p}\Omega_{T}:=(\partial\Omega\times[0,T])\cup(\bar{\Omega}\times\{0\})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, is a bounded open domain with
C^{2}
boundary.

Recall that
u\in C^{0}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
N\geq 1
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) if it satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}(u\phi_{t}-a(x,t)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi-|F|^{p-2}F\cdot\nabla\phi)dxdt=0

for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{T})
. The Calderón–Zygmund estimate is a classical and interesting problem in partial differential equations. The aim of this type of estimates is to explore whether the solution
u
is as good integrable as the inhomogeneity
F
, i.e.,

\displaystyle F\in L^{q}(\Omega_{T})\Rightarrow\nabla u\in L^{q}(\Omega_{T})

(1.3)

holds for any
q\geq p
or not. It is well-known that if
|F|\in L^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, then the equation (
1.1
) has a unique weak solution
u
satisfying the following Calderón–Zygmund estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.

(1.4)

It is natural to ask whether we can extend the result to various function spaces. There are many significant results on this topic, including both elliptic and parabolic equations. See for example

Our method can also be applied to obtain Calderón–Zygmund estimates for all

We first mention some previous results in related directions.
For the elliptic case with distributional data, we have the full result, i.e. (

We also mention some significant results concerning elliptic

-Laplacian type equations with measure data.
Mingione

More recently, based on various

However, to the best of our knowledge, there are no such Calderón–Zygmund estimates in the case
1<p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2}
. The main strategy to prove such estimates is combining higher regularity of the solution to the homogeneous system (

) with proper comparison estimates. The range

is always unavoidable because of the structure of the homogeneous system. Take the classical
p
-Laplacian (

[

\nabla w\in L^{p}\Rightarrow\nabla w\in L^{\infty},\quad\text{if}\quad p>\frac{2n}{n+2}.

They also proved the Hölder regularity for

[

, the above is not true for
1<p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2}

Choe

[

\nabla w\in L^{\infty}

is implicit in the notion of weak solution. While for

p>\frac{2n}{n+2}
) is sharp as shown by counterexamples in
[
14
]
(see also
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
19
]

In this paper, we will consider system (

Note that

We introduce some notations that will be used throughout the paper. We use the notation

Let

\displaystyle(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w):\nabla\left(|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}\mathbf{f}\right)

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}|\nabla\mathbf{f}|^{p}-C|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla\mathbf{f}||\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}.

Then (

(2.7)

Denote

\begin{split}&E_{1}=\int_{Q_{2R}^{\lambda}}|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}|\nabla\mathbf{f}||\mathcal{G}|,\\
&E_{3}=\sup_{-\lambda^{p-2}(2R)^{2}\leq t\leq 0}\int_{B_{2R}}|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta+2}dx,\end{split}\quad\quad\quad\begin{split}&E_{2}=\int_{Q_{2R}^{\lambda}}|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}|\nabla\mathbf{f}||\nabla u|^{p-1},\\
&E_{4}=\int_{Q_{2R}^{\lambda}}|\mathbf{f}|^{\vartheta}|\nabla\mathbf{f}|^{p}.\end{split}

(2.8)

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta s(p-1)}{p-s}=\frac{p(\vartheta+2)}{n}+p+\vartheta=\sigma.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}|\leq|F|^{p-1}+|\nabla u|^{p-1}|a(t)-(a(t))_{B_{2R}}|.

Boundary estimates

In this subsection, we consider the corresponding estimates near the boundary. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary and let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
where
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{2}
, there exist
\bar{R}>0
and a
C^{2}
function
\mathfrak{g}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\Omega\cap B_{\bar{R}}(x_{0})=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in B_{\bar{R}}(x_{0}):x_{n}>\mathfrak{g}(x^{\prime})\},

and

|\nabla\mathfrak{g}(x^{\prime})|\lesssim|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|,\quad|\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{g}(x^{\prime})|\lesssim 1,

(2.37)

for any
|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<\bar{R}
. With
u
being a weak solution to (
1.1
), we consider the unique solution

w\in L^{p}((-\lambda^{p-2}(4R)^{2}+t_{0},t_{0}),W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{4R}(x_{0})))+u

to the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{t}-\operatorname{div}((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}(x_{0})}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)=0&\text{in}\ (-\lambda^{p-2}(4R)^{2}+t_{0},t_{0})\times\Omega_{4R}(x_{0}),\\
w=u&\text{on}\ (-\lambda^{p-2}(4R)^{2}+t_{0},t_{0})\times\partial\Omega_{4R}(x_{0}),\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.38)

where we define
\Omega_{4R}(x_{0})=\Omega\cap B_{4R}(x_{0})
, and
0<R<R_{0}
with
R_{0}
will be fixed later in our proof. We have the following boundary counterpart of Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 2.10

Let
w
be a weak solution to equation (
2.38
). There exists
R_{0}>0
small depending on
\partial\Omega
such that for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T]
, any
0<R<R_{0}
,
and any
\beta_{1}>\frac{n(2-p)}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\fint_{{{Q}}^{\lambda}_{R/2}(x_{0},t_{0})}(\lambda|\nabla{w}|)^{\beta_{1}}\ \leq C{A}_{1}^{\frac{2\beta_{1}}{p}}+C{A}_{1}+CG_{\frac{2p-2}{p}}\left(\fint_{{Q}^{\lambda}_{4R}(x_{0},t_{0})}\left(\lambda|\nabla{w}|\right)^{p}\right),

(2.39)

where

{A}_{1}=\left[\fint_{t_{0}-\lambda^{p-2}(4R)^{2}}^{t_{0}}\left(\fint_{B_{4R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{\lambda}{R}|{w}|\right)^{q_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{\sigma_{2}}{q_{0}}}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}},

(2.40)

with
q_{0}=\frac{2\beta_{1}}{p}>\frac{n(2-p)}{p}
and
\sigma_{2}=\min\{\frac{2\beta_{1}(n+p)}{2\beta_{1}+(n+2)p},2\}
. And the function
G_{\frac{2p-2}{p}}
is defined in (
1.5
).

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
x_{0}=0\in\partial\Omega
and
t_{0}=0
.
Now let
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{2}
-diffeomorphism defined by

y=\mathcal{T}(x)=\mathcal{T}((x^{\prime},x_{n}))=(x^{\prime},x_{n}-\mathfrak{g}(x^{\prime}))

and also
\mathcal{T}(0)=0
. Set

x=\mathcal{T}^{-1}(y).

Next, we can choose
M
large enough such that for any
0<R<R_{0}:=\bar{R}/M
, there holds

B_{R/2}\cap\Omega\subset\mathcal{T}^{-1}(B^{+}_{R})\subset\mathcal{T}^{-1}(B^{+}_{3R})\subset B_{4R}\cap\Omega\subset B_{\bar{R}}\cap\Omega,

(2.41)

where
B^{+}_{3R}=B_{3R}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
Define

\widetilde{w}(t,y)=w(t,\mathcal{T}^{-1}(y))

for all
y\in B^{+}_{3R}
.
After a change of variables on (
2.38
), we see that
\widetilde{w}
is a solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widetilde{w}_{t}-\operatorname{div}_{y}\left((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}}|D\mathcal{T}\nabla_{y}\widetilde{w}|^{p-2}(D\mathcal{T})^{T}D\mathcal{T}\nabla_{y}\widetilde{w}\right)=0&\text{in}~~(-\lambda^{p-2}(3R)^{2},0)\times B^{+}_{3R},\\
\widetilde{w}=0&\text{on}~~(-\lambda^{p-2}(3R)^{2},0)\times\left[B_{3R}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right].\\
\end{array}\right.

By the definition of
\mathcal{T}
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle D\mathcal{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n-1}&-\nabla\mathfrak{g}\\
0&1\end{array}\right),\quad\quad\quad\quad(D\mathcal{T})^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n-1}&\nabla\mathfrak{g}\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}(D\mathcal{T})=\operatorname{det}(D\mathcal{T})^{-1}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\|D\mathcal{T}-I\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(D\mathcal{T})^{-1}-I\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CR.

(2.42)

Since
\widetilde{w}=0
on
B_{3R}(0)\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we make an odd extension of
\widetilde{w}
in
B_{3R}(0)
and denote it as
\overline{w}
, i.e. for
|y|\leq 3R
, define

\overline{w}(y)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{w}(y),\qquad\qquad~\text{if}\ y_{n}\geq 0;\\
-\widetilde{w}(y^{\prime},-y_{n}),~~~\text{if}\ y_{n}<0.\end{cases}

Then
\overline{w}
satisfies

\displaystyle\overline{w}_{t}-\operatorname{div}_{y}\left((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}}\mathbf{A}_{0}(y,\nabla_{y}\overline{w})\right)=0\quad\text{in}\ Q^{\lambda}_{3R},

(2.43)

where

\mathbf{A}_{0}(y,\xi)=|D\mathcal{T}\xi|^{p-2}(D\mathcal{T})^{T}D\mathcal{T}\xi\quad\quad\text{for}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Like the proof of Lemma
2.5
, we will approximate
\overline{w}
by
\overline{v}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\overline{v}
be a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{v}_{t}-\operatorname{div}_{y}\left((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}}\mathbf{A}(y,\nabla_{y}\overline{v})\right)=0&\text{in}\quad Q^{\lambda}_{3R},\\
\overline{v}=\overline{w}&\text{on}\quad\partial_{p}Q^{\lambda}_{3R},\end{array}\right.

(2.44)

where

\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)=(|D\mathcal{T}\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}(D\mathcal{T})^{T}D\mathcal{T}\xi.

By the classical regularity theory,
\overline{v}
is smooth for any

\overline{v}\to\overline{w}
in
C^{1}_{loc}(Q_{3R}^{\lambda})
.

\mathbf{A}(0,\xi)=(|\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi
and

2.37
) it is easy to check that

|\partial_{\xi}\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)-\partial_{\xi}\mathbf{A}(0,\xi)|\leq c|y|(|\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

Hence for any
|y|\leq 3R\leq\frac{p-1}{2c}

\partial_{\xi}\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)\geq\frac{p-1}{2}(|\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Id.

(2.45)

2.37
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)|\lesssim(|\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

(2.46)

We now estimate
\overline{v}

) and (

,

replaced by
\nabla_{y}\overline{v}
and

\begin{split}&\int_{Q^{\lambda}_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu}{2}}|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}\psi^{k}\\
&\lesssim\left(\int_{-\lambda^{p-2}R_{1}^{2}}^{0}\left(\int_{B_{R_{1}}}|\overline{v}|^{q_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{\sigma_{2}}{q_{1}}}dt\right)^{\frac{\theta_{0}}{\sigma_{2}}}\left(\int_{Q^{\lambda}_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu+p-2}{2}}\left|\nabla_{y}^{2}\overline{v}\right|^{2}\psi^{k+1}\right)^{\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\sup_{-\lambda^{p-2}R_{1}^{2}\leq t\leq 0}\int_{B_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu+2}{2}}\psi^{k+1}dy\right)^{\frac{(\mu+2-p)\theta_{0}}{2(\mu+2)}+1-\theta_{0}}\\

&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\sup_{-\lambda^{p-2}R_{1}^{2}\leq t\leq 0}\int_{B_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu+2}{2}}\psi^{k+1}dy\right)^{\frac{(\mu+1)\theta_{0}}{\mu+2}+1-\theta_{0}}.\end{split}

(2.47)

Next, we differentiate (
2.44

to get

\left(\overline{v}_{y_{l}}\right)_{t}-\operatorname{div}_{y}\left((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}}\partial_{\xi}\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)|_{\xi=\nabla_{y}\overline{v}}\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{y_{l}}\right)=\operatorname{div}_{y}\left((a(t))_{\Omega_{4R}}\partial_{y_{l}}\mathbf{A}(y,\xi)\right).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p-2}R^{2}}\int_{Q^{\lambda}_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu+2}{2}}\psi^{k}+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{Q^{\lambda}_{R_{1}}}(|\nabla_{y}\overline{v}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\mu+p}{2}}\psi^{k-1}

Combining this with (

Remark 3.3

Note that when
p>\frac{2n}{n+2}
, by Remark
1.3
we obtain Lemma
3.2
with
\sigma_{1}=p
and
\kappa_{0}=0
.

We recall an useful lemma which concerns weak (1,1)-estimates for
\lambda
-maximal function. The proof can be found in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.4

[
7
, Lemma 2.12]

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, then there exists a constant
c=c(n)\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\mathbf{M}^{\lambda}f(y,s)>2\alpha\}\right|\leq\frac{c}{\alpha}\int_{\{|f|>\alpha\}}|f(x,t)|dxdt,

(3.11)

for any
\alpha>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For any
q>\frac{n(2-p)}{2}
, we can find
\sigma_{1},\kappa_{0}>0
such that
q>\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}
and
\frac{n(2-p)}{2}<\sigma_{1}<n+p
. Next, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1
. Assume
\nabla u\in L^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}(\Omega_{T})
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\delta,\delta_{1},\Lambda,\lambda_{0}
be as defined in Lemma
3.2
.
One has

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}=q\Lambda^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-1}\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|d\alpha\\
&=q\Lambda^{q}\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\alpha^{q-1}\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|d\alpha+q\Lambda^{q}\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-1}\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|d\alpha\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2},\end{split}

(3.12)

where we take

\alpha_{0}=\lambda_{0}^{-1}=(\varepsilon\Lambda^{\sigma_{1}}/C_{0})^{-\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}+2-p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\sigma_{1}}(\Omega_{T})}^{\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{1}+2-p}}.

It is easy to check that

\begin{split}&I_{1}\leq q\Lambda^{q}\alpha_{0}^{q-p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{p-1}\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|d\alpha\leq q\Lambda^{q-p}\alpha_{0}^{q-p}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p}\\
&\quad\leq C\varepsilon^{{-\frac{q-p}{\sigma_{1}+2-p}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\sigma_{1}}(\Omega_{T})}^{\frac{\sigma_{1}(q-p)}{\sigma_{1}+2-p}}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p}.\end{split}

Note that
p<\sigma_{1}<q
, by interpolation inequality and Young’s inequality we obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C(\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}\\
&\overset{\eqref{DuLp}}{\leq}C(\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Next we deal with
I_{2}
. First, we estimate

\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|.

Note that
|\nabla u|(x,t)\leq\mathbf{M}^{\lambda}(|\nabla u|)(x,t)

for any
\lambda>0
and
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
. Let
\lambda=\frac{1}{\alpha}
, we can use Lemma
3.2
and (
3.11
) to get

\begin{split}\left|\{|\nabla u|>\Lambda\alpha\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|&\leq\left|\{\mathbf{M}^{\lambda}(|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}})>(\Lambda\alpha)^{\sigma_{1}}\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\left|\{{\bf M}^{\lambda}(|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}})>\alpha^{\sigma_{1}}\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|+\left|\{\mathbf{M}^{\lambda}(|F|^{\sigma_{1}})>(\delta\alpha)^{\sigma_{1}}\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|\\
&\qquad+\left|\{{\bf M}^{\lambda}(|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}})>(\alpha/\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}\}\cap\Omega_{T}\right|\\
&\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{\alpha^{\sigma_{1}}}\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{C}{(\delta\alpha)^{\sigma_{1}}}\int_{\{|F|>\delta\alpha/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|F|^{\sigma_{1}}\\
&\qquad+\frac{C\varepsilon^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}}{\alpha^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}}\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/(2\varepsilon)\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}.\end{split}

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-\sigma_{1}-1}\left[\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}}\right]d\alpha+\frac{C}{\delta^{\sigma_{1}}}\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-\sigma_{1}-1}\left[\int_{\{|F|>\alpha\delta/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|F|^{\sigma_{1}}\right]d\alpha

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C\varepsilon^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-(\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0})-1}\left[\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/(2\varepsilon)\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}\right]d\alpha.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-\sigma_{1}-1}\left[\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}}\right]d\alpha\leq\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left[\int_{0}^{2|\nabla u|}\alpha^{q-\sigma_{1}-1}d\alpha\right]|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-\sigma_{1}-1}\left[\int_{\{|F|>\alpha\delta/2\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|F|^{\sigma_{1}}\right]d\alpha\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{q-\sigma_{1}}}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha_{0}}^{\infty}\alpha^{q-(\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0})-1}\left[\int_{\{|\nabla u|>\alpha/(2\varepsilon)\}\cap\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}\right]d\alpha\leq C\varepsilon^{q-(\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0})}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}.

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\tilde{C}\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}+\frac{\tilde{C}}{\delta^{q}}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q}.

(3.14)

Plugging (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) into (
3.12
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}\leq(\tilde{C}+1)\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}+\frac{C}{\delta^{q}}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}.

Let us take
\varepsilon
small enough to get
(\tilde{C}+1)\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, then we can determine the corresponding
\delta
and
C(\varepsilon)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}},

provided
\nabla u\in L^{\sigma_{1}+\kappa_{0}}(\Omega_{T})
.

Step 2
. To complete the proof, we consider the approximate system. For any
0<\kappa<\kappa_{1}
, let
u_{\kappa}
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\kappa}-\operatorname{div}(a_{\kappa}(x,t)|\nabla u_{\kappa}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\kappa})=\operatorname{div}(|F_{\kappa}|^{p-2}F_{\kappa})&\text{in }\Omega_{T},\\
u_{\kappa}=0&\text{on}\ \partial_{p}\Omega_{T}.\\
\end{array}\right.

where
a_{\kappa}(x,t)=a(x,t)\star\varphi_{\kappa}(x)
,
F_{\kappa}=F(x,t)\star\varphi_{\kappa}(x)
and
\varphi_{\kappa}(x)
is the standard mollifier, i.e.,
\varphi_{\kappa}(x)=\kappa^{-n}
\varphi(\frac{x}{\kappa})
where
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
is a nonnegative radial function with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. Clearly,
F_{\kappa}\in L^{q}(0,T;C^{\infty}(\Omega))
. Moreover,
\|F_{\kappa}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\|F\|_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}
for
m\in\{p,q\}
and we can take
\kappa_{1}
small enough such that
[a_{\kappa}]_{R_{0}}\leq 3[a]_{R_{0}}
. By the classical regularity theory, we have
\nabla u_{\kappa}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
. Apply the result in Step 1 with
[a]_{R_{0}}<\delta_{1}/3=:\delta_{0}
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u_{\kappa}|^{q}&\leq C\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F_{\kappa}|^{q}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F_{\kappa}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}.\end{split}

(3.15)

By uniqueness of the problem (
1.1
) in
C([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega))
, we have

\nabla u_{\kappa}\to\nabla u

strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega_{T})
as
\kappa\to 0
. Hence there exists a subsequence, which can be still denoted by
\{u_{\kappa}\}
, such that
\nabla u_{\kappa}\to\nabla u
a.e. as
\kappa\to 0
. Then, letting
\kappa\to 0
in (
3.15
) and using Fatou’s lemma, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla u|^{q}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{q}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|F|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2-p+q}{2}}.

We complete the proof.

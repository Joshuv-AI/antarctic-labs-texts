Stout-smearing, gradient flow and c SW c_{\text{SW}} at one loop order
Stephan Dürr

Abstract
The one-loop determination of the coefficient c SW {c_{\text{SW}}} of the Wilson quark action has been useful to push the leading cut-off effects for on-shell quantities to 𝒪 ⁡ ( α 2 ​ a ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}a) and, in conjunction with non-perturbative determinations of c SW {c_{\text{SW}}} , to 𝒪 ⁡ ( a 2 ) \mathcal{O}(a^{2}) , as long as no link-smearing is employed. These days it is common practice to include some overall link-smearing into the definition of the fermion action. Unfortunately, in this situation only the tree-level value c SW ( 0 ) = 1 c_{\text{SW}}^{(0)}=1 is known, and cut-off effects start at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α ​ a ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha a) . We present some general techniques for calculating one loop quantities in lattice perturbation theory which continue to be useful for smeared-link fermion actions. Specifically, we discuss the application to the 1-loop improvement coefficient c SW ( 1 ) c_{\text{SW}}^{(1)} for overall stout-smeared Wilson fermions.

3
Perturbative stout smearing and Wilson flow

Stout smearing with smearing parameter
\rho
of the link variable
U_{\mu}(x)
is defined through

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)=e^{i\rho Q_{\mu}(x)}U_{\mu}(x)

(16)

with

\displaystyle Q_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\left(W_{\mu}(x)-\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}[W_{\mu}(x)]\right)

(17)

\displaystyle W_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\neq\mu}(P_{\mu\nu}^{\dagger}(x)-P_{\mu\nu}(x)),

(18)

where
P_{\mu\nu}(x)
is the plaquette in the
\mu,\nu
-plane at lattice position
x
.
As
Q_{\mu}(x)
is anti-hermitian,
U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)
is automatically in
SU(3)
again and the smearing can be iterated leading to
U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
after
n
smearing steps.

Wilson flow is the gradient flow of the Wilson action
\mathcal{S}_{W}
and defined through the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-g_{0}^{2}\left\{\partial_{x,\mu}\mathcal{S}_{W}[U(x,t)]\right\}U_{\mu}(x,t)=iQ_{\mu}(x,t)U_{\mu}(x,t)

(19)

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x,0)

\displaystyle=U_{\mu}(x).

(20)

Therefore it is easily seen, that the Wilson flow is generated by infinitesimal stout-smearings. This means calculations done in the stout-smearing formalism with parameters
\rho
and
n
can be transformed into the Wilson flow formalism with flow time
t
by performing the limit
n\to\infty
,
\rho\to 0
with
n\rho=t=\,
const. (
t
in lattice units, i.e.
t/a^{2}
).

Our goal is to perform perturbative calculations of
{c_{\text{SW}}}
as sketched in section
2
including stout smearing and eventually Wilson flow. Therefore we need a perturbative expansion of the smearing.

3.1
Leading order

The un-smeared original link variable
U_{\mu}(x)
has an expansion in terms of the gluon field
A_{\mu}(x)
:

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x)=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{2}\{T^{a},T^{b}\}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{3})

(21)

At leading order the smeared link variable
U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)
has a similar expansion with a modified gluon field
A^{a(1)}_{\mu}(x)
:

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a(1)}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{2})

(22)

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{a(1)}(x)

\displaystyle=A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+\rho(Q^{a}_{\mu}(x))^{\mathrm{LO}}

(23)

with

\displaystyle(Q^{a}_{\mu}(x))^{\mathrm{LO}}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{4}\left(A_{\nu}^{a}(x)+A_{\mu}^{a}(x+\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu})\right.

(24)

\displaystyle\left.-A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\mu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})-2A_{\mu}^{a}(x)\right).

(25)

After a Fourier transform
A^{a(1)}_{\mu}(k)
can be expressed as
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{a(1)}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(f(k)\delta_{\mu\nu}-(f(k)-1)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\right)A^{a}_{\nu}(k)=:\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\rho,k)A^{a}_{\nu}(k)

(26)

with
\hat{k}_{\mu}=2\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{\mu})
and
f(k)=1-\rho\hat{k}^{2}
. After
n
smearing steps the overall structure remains the same except for the powers of
n

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{a(n)}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\rho,k)A^{a}_{\nu}(k)=\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(f(k)^{n}\delta_{\mu\nu}-(f(k)^{n}-1)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\right)A^{a}_{\nu}(k),

(27)

which in turn makes it easy to perform the aforementioned limit to the Wilson flow formalism:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{a}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(e^{-t\hat{k}^{2}}\delta_{\mu\nu}-(e^{-t\hat{k}^{2}}-1)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\right)A^{a}_{\nu}(k,0)=:\sum\limits_{\nu}B_{\mu\nu}(k,t)A^{a}_{\nu}(k,0).

(28)

3.2
Next-to-leading order

At next-to-leading order in addition to the quadratic term involving the already known modified gluon field
A^{a(1)}_{\mu}(x)
there is also an anti-symmetric part
A_{\mu}^{ab(1)}(x)
:

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a(1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\{T^{a},T^{b}\}A_{\mu}^{a(1)}(x)A_{\mu}^{b(1)}(x)+\frac{1}{2}[T^{a},T^{b}]A^{ab(1)}_{\mu}(x)\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{3})

(29)

with

\displaystyle A^{ab(1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=4\rho\cdot\left[\frac{1}{2}(Q^{a}_{\mu}(x))^{\mathrm{LO}}A_{\mu}^{b}(x)+(Q^{ab}_{\mu}(x))^{\mathrm{NLO}}\right].

(30)

Fourier transforming gives:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{ab(1)}(k_{1},k_{2})=4i\rho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2}),

(31)

where

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu\nu}\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}(2k_{1\rho}+k_{2\rho}))\cos(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{2\mu})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu\rho}\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}(2k_{2\nu}+k_{1\nu}))\cos(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{1\mu})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu\rho}\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}(k_{1\mu}-k_{2\mu}))\cos(\tfrac{1}{2}(k_{1\nu}+k_{2\nu})).

(32)

After
n
iterations we get

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\rho,k_{1}+k_{2})A_{\nu}^{(n-1)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle+4i\rho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(n-1)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(n-1)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})

(33)

which we can express as a sum over the
A_{\mu}^{(m)}(x)
from all previous smearing steps:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}4i\rho\,g_{\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\nu}(\rho,k_{1}+k_{2})A^{(m)a}_{\rho}(k_{1})A^{(m)b}_{\sigma}(k_{2})

(34)

And finally in the Wilson flow limit the sum turns into an integral over the flow time

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\nu\rho}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1}+k_{2},-t^{\prime})4ig_{\beta\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\,.

(35)

Outlook

In order to calculate diagram (d), which involves the qqggg-vertex, the smearing relation at next-to-next-to-leading order is also needed. There the expressions become rather lengthy. The next step will then be to insert the smearing relations into the expanded action to obtain the Feynman rules, which can only be partly compared to
[
4
]
, as the ones coming from the clover term are not included there.

Another question we would like to investigate concerns the structure of the smearing expansion. Because the smeared link variable
U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)
is again in
SU(3)
it is expected to have an expansion

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)=e^{ig_{1}T^{a}\tilde{A}^{a}_{\mu}(x)}

(36)

with a modified (renormalised) coupling
g_{1}
.
However this expansion does not coincide with the expansion in
g_{0}
and finding a link between the two may help to simplify calculations.

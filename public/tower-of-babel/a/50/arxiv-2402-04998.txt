Revealing the KH 2 ​ PO 4 \text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} soft-mode coupling mechanism with infrared spectroscopy under pressure
D. Santos-Cottin, S. Nasrallah, F. Capitani, P. Simon, C. C. Homes, A. Akrap, R. P. S. M. Lobo

Abstract
We measured the far-infrared reflectivity of a KH 2 ​ PO 4 \text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} single crystal up to pressures of 2 GPa 2\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa} in the ferroelectric and paraelectric phases. We find that the ν 4 \nu_{4} vibrational mode of the PO 4 \text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} tetrahedron is strongly affected by the applied pressure. At ambient pressure this phonon is destabilized by the presence of the H ions and hence shows a highly damped character, beyond the phonon propagation threshold. Applying a pressure close to 0.6 GPa 0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa} makes this phonon clearly underdamped. Its behavior closely follows the soft-mode behavior observed in Raman spectroscopy. Our results solve a long standing open problem, demonstrating that the ν 4 \nu_{4} mode is the excitation mediating the coupling of the hydrogen network to the lattice modes that create the ferroelectic polarization in KH 2 ​ PO 4 \text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} .



The riches of ferroelectrics and new phenomena arising from their large dielectrict constant are closely tied to the soft mode mechanism in these materials
Kamba 2021
.
Soft-mode-driven remarkable properties have recently brought long-known materials with lattice instabilities into the limelight.
The soft-mode and the proximity to the ferroelectric instability are the key mechanism for the extreme dilute superconductivity in
\text{SrTiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Lin et al. 2014

Rischau et al. 2022
.

\text{KTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
is an incipient ferroelectric
Shirane et al. 1967
;
Z̆elezný et al. 2004
;
Ichikawa et al. 2005
where the soft-mode underpins new opportunities in spintronics
Gupta et al. 2022
and 2D superconductivity
Liu et al. 2023

In this paper we study a classic ferroelectric and find a missing link in its peculiar soft-mode.

\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
(KDP) is a ferroelectric material with a Curie temperature of
T_{\rm C}\simeq$123\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$

Busch 1938
.
It has a tetragonal (
I\overline{4}2d
) paraelectric phase, which becomes orthorhombic (
Fdd2
) in the ferroelectric state.
Each H atom in KDP connects two O atoms.

Slater 1941

Slater 1941
proposed that the proton sitting in the middle of the O-H-O bonding would be an average position.
In his model the H atom stays closer to one of the O atoms in the
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
tetrahedron.
He found that 6, out of the 16 possible configurations, are energetically more stable.
Looking at the free energy of the system he calculated an electrical polarization that remains constant below a critical temperature
T_{\rm C}
.

Slater 1941
’s order-disorder theory gives an overall good description of KDP but fails in two important points.
The first problem is the constant, temperature independent, polarization below
T_{\rm C}
.
The second is the lack of an explanation for why
T_{\rm C}
increases to
222\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
when deuterium substitutes for hydrogen.

Pirenne 1949

Pirenne 1949
incorporated the kinetic energy of the proton vibrations into
Slater 1941
’s picture, thus explaining the difference in
T_{\rm C}
between H and D materials.
Elaborating on
Pirenne 1949
’s ideas,
Blinc 1960

Blinc 1960
placed the proton in a double well potential and proposed that the proton could tunnel between the two wells.
He assumed a symmetric potential in the high temperature phase and asymmetric wells in the ferroelectric phase.
The tunneling probability of D being much smaller than that for H,
T_{\rm C}
is higher for the former material.
Within this formalism, KDP is no longer a simple order-disorder ferroelectric but it rather has a mixed character with a soft-mode composed of a proton collective mode strongly coupled to an optical phonon and to an acoustic mode.

Curiously, according to this description, ferroelectricity in KDP comes from a Hamiltonian dominated by the proton network with heavy ions entering as a perturbative term.
Nevertheless, the ferroelectric polarization comes from the displacement of heavier ions.

Gradually,
Blinc 1960

Some experimental evidence supporting this picture started to emerge with Raman measurements of an overdamped soft mode
Kaminow and Damen 1968

Later, neutron structural studies showed the existence of the proton double-well potential
Nelmes 1984

More recently, neutron Compton scattering
Reiter et al. 2002

\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

Zhang et al. 2001

\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

Sugimoto and Ikeda 1991

The importance of the H coupling to the
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

Simon and Gervais 1985

Simon and Gervais 1985
;
Simon et al. 1988

They showed that the infrared reflectivity of
\text{RbH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
has an overdamped soft mode, seen by Raman, and that the same mechanism occurs in
\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
and
\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{AsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

Their spectra also shows a highly-damped
\nu_{4}

\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

They proposed that the nearby proton tunneling between their two equilibrium positions destabilizes the
\nu_{4}

As this vibration has a dipole moment, they conjectured that this mode would be the missing link coupling the proton network to the external translation overdamped mode.

Here, we utilized infrared measurements under pressure to give a definite proof that this phonon is indeed coupled to the soft mode.

Peercy 1973

We find that the damping of the

This shows that the two modes are coupled and that the
\nu_{4}

\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

We measured the infrared reflectivity of a KDP single crystal in Bruker IFS 113v and 66v/S interferometers.
The absolute value of the reflectivity was obtained in an ARS Helitran cryostat utilizing an
in situ
gold overfilling technique
Homes et al. 1993
.
We obtained data in the ferroelectric and paraelectric phases with the electric field of light polarized along the ferroelectric axis between 10 and
6\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
.

High pressure infrared reflectivity data, up to
2.7\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}
, were collected at the SMIS beamline, SOLEIL Synchrotron, in a home-made horizontal microscope with custom objectives.
The microscope is coupled to a Thermo-Fisher iS50 interferometer with synchrotron radiation as its light source; a solid substrate beamsplitter; and a 4K Si:B bolometer to cover the 100 to
600\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
region.
We employed a membrane-driven diamond anvil cell (DAC) with a
600\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
diameter culet, a
60\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
thick pre-indented stainless steel gasket with a
250\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
diameter hole filled with polyethylene as hydrostatic medium
Celeste et al. 2019
.
The sample was placed in contact with one of the culets and we employed a ruby sphere for the
in situ
pressure measurement.
The DAC was attached to the cold head of a
\ell
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
flow cryostat.
We utilized the diamond table as a reference.
The thus-obtained reflectivity in the DAC at the lowest pressure was then corrected to match the measurement taken outside the cell at the same temperature.
We applied this correction function to the other higher pressure measurements.

The data below
1\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}
were measured at BNL within a clamped pressure cell using kerosene as the pressure transmitting medium; the entire arrangement sits at the bottom of a compact open-flow cryostat.
This cell employs a wedged diamond window (
\simeq 7^{\circ}
) with a working diameter of about 1.5 mm, allowing measurements down to
\simeq 20
–
30\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
on a Bruker 66v/S using conventional infrared sources.
The reflectivity unit uses toroidal mirrors with a long focal length.
Rotations allow the reflectivity from the sample in the cell to be separated from the reflectivity obtained from the front of the diamond window, which, similar to the approach used with the DAC, is then used as an optical reference
Kézsmárki et al. 2007
.

In order to quantify our results, let us take a look at the fitting parameters at
140\text{\,}\mathrm{K}

For this, besides an analysis utilizing the factorized form of the dielectric function from Eq. (

We looked at the imaginary part of the dielectric susceptibility
\chi_{F}^{\prime\prime}

\Delta\chi_{F}^{\prime\prime}=R\left[\frac{(\omega_{F}-\omega-\gamma_{F}q)^{2}}{(\omega_{F}-\omega)^{2}+\gamma_{F}^{2}}-1\right].

(2)

Here,
\omega_{F}

\gamma_{F}

As

The

It must sit on top of a larger background as a differential correction to the dielectric function.
In our case, we utilized two large Lorentzian oscillators in the background to describe surrounding phonons.

In summary, we measured the infrared reflectivity of
\text{KH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
under pressure up to
2.7\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}
.
We found that the
\nu_{4}
vibration mode of the
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
tetrahedron is strongly damped at ambient pressure and it becomes underdamped upon increasing pressure.
Its behavior closely follows the underdamping observed by
Peercy 1973
for the KDP soft-mode in Raman scattering, indicating a coupling between these two modes.
The
\nu_{4}
mode, which has an electric dipole and involves only heavier P and O ions, is strongly destabilized by the presence of the nearby protons.
It is the missing piece of KDP soft-mode as it represents the excitation responsible for coupling the hydrogen network to the lattice modes.
In particular it explains how the in-plane proton dynamics affects the out-of-plane phonon at the origin of the electric polarization.

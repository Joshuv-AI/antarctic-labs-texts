Abstract
. We provide conditions ensuring that the KKT-type conditions characterizes the global optimality for quadratically constrained (possibly nonconvex) quadratic programming QCQP problems in Hilbert space. The key property is the convexity ofa image-type set related to the functions appearing in the formulation of the problem. The proof of the main result relies on a generalized version of the (Jakubovich) S-Lemma in Hilbert spaces. As an application, we consider the class of QCQP problems with a special form of the quadratic terms of the constraints. Keywords: Global solvability, QCQP problems, KKT conditions, Jakubovich lemma, S-lemma, image set MSC2020: 90C20, 90C23, 90C26, 90C46

Introduction

The aim of this work is to establish a sufficient condition under which the KKT-type conditions characterizes the global optimality of a possibly non convex Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Programming (QCQP) problem in Hilbert spaces.

Let

\begin{split}&{Minimize\,}_{x\in H}\ \ J(x):=\langle x,A_{J}x\rangle+2\langle b_{J},x\rangle+c_{J}\\

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
f_{k}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
k=0,1,...,m
be a finite collection of functionals of the form (
3.2
). Consider the set
GIS
, defined as

\Omega_{0}:=\{(f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),...,f_{m}(x))\ |\ x\in H\}+int\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}.

Assume that the set
GIS
is convex.
Then, exactly one of the following statements is valid:

(i)

\exists\,\bar{x}\in H
such that
f_{k}(\bar{x})<0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(ii)

(\exists\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}\backslash\textbf{0}_{m+1})

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq 0

\forall\,x\in H

Proof.

The implication [not(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)] is immediate (by contradiction).
To show the implication [not(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)], assume that (i) does not hold, i.e., the system

(3.3)

f_{k}(x)<0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

has no solution.
By the definition of
GIS
, the inconsistency of the system (
3.3
) implies that

(3.4)

\Omega_{0}\cap\ (-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})=\emptyset.

To see this, suppose by contrary, that there exists
y\in\Omega_{0}\cap(-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})
. By the definition of
\Omega_{0}
(with
f_{k}
,
k=0,...,m
, defined by (
3.2
)), there exist
x_{0}\in H
,
C_{0},C_{1}\in\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}
such that

y_{0}=(f_{0}(x_{0}),...,f_{m}(x_{0}))+C_{0}=-C_{1}\in(-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}),

i.e.,
(f_{0}(x_{0}),...,f_{m}(x_{0}))=-C_{0}-C_{1}\in(-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})
contradictory to (
3.3
). This proves (
3.4
).

Since
\text{int}\Omega_{0}
and
-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}
are
(m+1)
-dimensional sets in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, non-empty and convex, by (
3.4
),

int\,\Omega_{0}\cap\ \,(-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})=\emptyset,

we can apply
Theorem 4
.
So, there exists a hyperplane which separates
\Omega_{0}
and
-\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}
properly, i.e. there exists
\gamma=(\gamma_{0},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}\backslash 0
such that

(3.5)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}y_{k}\geq 0\ \ \forall\ y=(y_{0},...,y_{m})\in\Omega_{0}

and
\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}z_{k}\leq 0\ \ \forall\ z=(z_{0},...,z_{m})\in(-int\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}).
This latter inequality implies that it must be
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}\backslash 0
.

Consequently, by the definition of
\Omega_{0}
,

y=(f_{0}(x),...,f_{m}(x))+C\in\Omega_{0},\ \ C=(C_{0},...,C_{m})\in\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1},\ \ x\in H

and the formula (
3.5
), we get

(3.6)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}(f_{k}(x)+C_{k})\geq 0\ \ \forall\ x\in H,\ \ C_{k}\in\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}\ k=0,...,m.

We show that it must be

(3.7)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq 0\ \ \forall\ x\in H.

Otherwise,

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(\bar{x})<0\ \ \text{for some }\ \bar{x}\in H,
and it would be possible to choose
C\in\text{int}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1}
with components
C_{k}>0
small enough so as

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\gamma_{k}(f_{k}(\bar{x})+C_{k})<0

which would contradict (
3.5
) since
(f_{0}(\bar{x})+\ell_{0},...,f_{m}(\bar{x})+\ell_{m})\in\Omega_{0}
for any
\ell=(\ell_{0},...,\ell_{m})\in\text{int}\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}\
.
Thus, (
3.7
) holds and it proves
(ii)
.
∎

In the following, we exploit
Theorem 7
to get necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for general
QCQP
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
Assumption 2
hold and

x^{*}
be a global minimizer of
QCQP
.
Define

f_{0}(x):=J(x)-J(x^{*})=\langle x,A_{J}x\rangle+\langle b_{J},x\rangle-\langle x^{*},A_{J}x^{*}\rangle-2\langle b_{J},x^{*}\rangle.

Consider the collection of quadratic functionals formed by
f_{0}(x)
and the constraints of (
QCQP
)
f_{i}(x)
,
i=1,...,m
.
Denote
GIS
with
\Omega_{0}
and let
\Omega_{0}
be convex.
The following Fritz-John conditions are necessary for optimality, i.e.
there exists a vector
(\gamma_{0},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}\backslash\ 0
such that

(3.8)

\begin{split}&(i)\ \ \ \nabla(\gamma_{0}J+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k})(x^{*})=0,\\
&(ii)\ \ \gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0\ \ k\in\{1,...,m\},\\
&(iii)\ \gamma_{0}A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\text{ is non negative}.\end{split}

Moreover, if there exists a point
x_{0}\in H
such that

(3.9)

f_{k}(x_{0})<0\ \ \ \forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\},

then
there exists a vector
(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\backslash 0
such that

(KKT)

\begin{split}&(i)\ \ \ \nabla(J+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k})(x^{*})=0,\\
&(ii)\ \ \gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0\ \ k\in\{1,...,m\},\\
&(iii)\ A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\text{ is non negative}\end{split}

are necessary for optimality. Given a feasible
x^{*}
for problem
QCQP
, if (
3.9
) holds, the conditions (
KKT
) are also sufficient for global optimality of
x^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{0}(x):=J(x)-J(x^{*})
. Since
x^{*}
is a global minimizer of
QCQP
,
f_{0}(x)\geq 0

\forall\,x
feasible for
QCQP
.
Hence, the system
f_{k}(x)<0

k=0,...,m
has no solution.
By
Theorem 7
, there exists
(\gamma_{0},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}\backslash 0
such that

(3.10)

\gamma_{0}f_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq 0\ \ \text{for all }x\in H.

In particular, for
x=x^{*}
, we have
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})\geq 0
.
Since
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})\leq 0

\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
, it must be
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0

\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
which proves (ii) of (
3.8
).
Moreover, by (
3.10
) and (ii) of (
3.8
), for all
x\in H

(3.11)

\tilde{L}(x):=\gamma_{0}J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq\gamma_{0}J(x^{*})=\tilde{L}(x^{*}).

Hence
\tilde{L}(x)
attains its minimum over
H
at
x^{*}
.
Notice that we can rewrite
\tilde{L}(x)
as

(3.12)

\tilde{L}(x):=\gamma_{0}J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)=\langle x,\tilde{A}(\gamma)x\rangle+2\langle\tilde{b}(\gamma),x\rangle+\tilde{c}(\gamma)

with
\tilde{A}(\gamma)=\gamma_{0}A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}
,
\tilde{b}(\gamma)=\gamma_{0}b_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}b_{k}
and
\tilde{c}(\gamma)=\gamma_{0}c_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}c_{k}
.

The operator
\tilde{A}(\gamma)
must be non negative, otherwise we would have
min_{x\in H}\,\tilde{L}(x)=-\infty
, which is contradiction with (
3.11
).
Hence,
\tilde{A}(\gamma)
is non negative, i.e. condition (iii) of (
3.8
) holds and
\tilde{L}(x)
is convex with respect to
x
.
We can apply
Theorem 2
for the convex and twice continuously differentiable function
\tilde{L}(x)
. The optimality condition
\nabla_{x}\tilde{L}(x^{*})=0
is equivalent to
the conditions (i) of (
3.8
).
The proof of the first part of the theorem is finished.

Suppose now that (
3.9
) holds, i.e., there exists a point
x_{0}
such that
{f_{k}(x_{0})<0}

\forall\,k=1,...,m
.
If it were
\gamma_{0}=0
, then by (
3.10
), it would be
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which would contradict (
3.9
). Hence
\gamma_{0}>0
and the Fritz-John conditions becomes the KKT condition, i.e. (
KKT
) holds.

To complete the proof, we show that conditions (
KKT
) are also sufficient for optimality.
Assume that there exists
x^{*}\in H
which is feasible to
QCQP
and
(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\backslash 0
such that (
KKT
) holds.
The Lagrangian for
QCQP
is:

(3.13)

L(x,\gamma):=J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)=\langle x,A(\gamma)x\rangle+2\langle b(\gamma),x\rangle+c(\gamma)

with
A(\gamma)=A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}
,
b(\gamma)=b_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}b_{k}
and
c(\gamma)=c_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}c_{k}
.

Notice that the Lagrangian
L(x,\gamma)
is convex with respect to
x
, since
A(\gamma)=A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}
is non negative by (
KKT
).
Hence
x^{*}
such that
{\nabla_{x}L(x^{*})=0}
is the minimum of
L(x,\gamma)
for
\gamma
fixed, by
Theorem 2
.

By (
KKT
),
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0
for
k=1,...,m
. We have

(3.14)

J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq J(x^{*})+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=J(x^{*})\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For any
x
feasible for
QCQP
,
f_{k}(x)\leq 0
,
k+1,...,m
, and hence

(3.15)

J(x)\geq J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)

Combining (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), for any
x
feasible for
QCQP
, we have

(3.16)

J(x)\geq J(x^{*})

which proves that
x^{*}
is a global minimum for
QCQP
.

∎

Example 9
.

Fix

,

4.
Global minima characterization for (S-QCQP)

In the present section we use the results of Section
3
to provide (
KKT
) characterization of global minima for
S-QCQP
. The main result of this section is Theorem
11
.

Let
x^{*}
be the global minimum of
S-QCQP
.
As in
Theorem 8
, we use the notation

f_{0}(x):=J(x)-J(x^{*})=a_{J}\|x\|^{2}+\langle b_{J},x\rangle-a_{J}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-2\langle b_{J},x^{*}\rangle

Also in the case of
S-QCQP
,
GIS
takes the form

\Omega_{0}:=\{(f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),..,f_{m}(x))|x\in H\}+int\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}

where

f_{k}(x):=a_{k}\|x\|^{2}+2\langle b_{k},x\rangle+c_{k}\ \ k=0,...,m

with
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 11
.

Consider problem
S-QCQP
with
m
constraints. Let the Hilbert space
H
be such that one of the following holds:

(i)

H is infinite dimensional,

(ii)

H has
n
-dimensional,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case, any maximally linearly independent subset of the system
\langle b_{k},x\rangle=0
,
k=0,...,m
has cardinality
\bar{m}<n
.

Then,
GIS
, denoted with
\Omega_{0}
, is convex.

Proof.

In order to show that
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is convex, take any
{v:=(v_{0},...,v_{m})}
,
{w:=(w_{0},...,w_{m})\in\Omega_{0}}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
There exist
x_{v},x_{w}\in H
such that

(4.1)

f_{k}(x_{v})<v_{k}\ \ \text{and }f_{k}(x_{w})<w_{k}\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.

Consider the convex combination
\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
. Let
(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}
be the
k
-th component of
\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
.
By (
4.1
), we have

(4.2)

\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w})<(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.

In order to prove that
\Omega_{0}
is convex, we show that the convex combination

\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
belongs to
\Omega_{0}
, i.e. there exists
\tilde{x}
such that
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.3)

f_{k}(\tilde{x})<(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}.

Formulas (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) together imply that
\Omega_{0}
is convex if there exists
\tilde{x}\in H
such that
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.4)

f_{k}(\tilde{x})\leq\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w}).

Note that if
x_{v}=x_{w}
, then (
4.4
) trivially holds for
\tilde{x}=x_{v}=x_{w}
. From now on, we assume that
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors,
x_{v}\neq x_{w}
.

Let us consider

(4.5)

\mathcal{S}(\lambda):=\{x\in H\ |\ \|x\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\},

i.e. a sphere centered at zero with radius
\sqrt{\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}}
. In particular, when both
x_{v}=x_{w}=0
the set
\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
reduces to
\{0\}
, but this is impossible, since we assumed that
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors.

The idea of proving the existence of
\tilde{x}
satisfying (
4.3
) is based on the following observations:

–:

any
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
satisfies (
4.3
) if
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}
:

(4.6)

\begin{split}&f_{k}(\tilde{x})\leq\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w})<\lambda v_{k}+(1-\lambda)w_{k}.\\
&a_{k}\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+2\langle b_{k},\tilde{x}\rangle+c_{k}\leq a_{k}(\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2})+2\langle b_{k},(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\rangle+c_{k},\end{split}

–:

to ensure (
4.6
), we need to show that we can choose
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
satisfying (
4.5
) and such that
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\langle b_{k},\tilde{x}\rangle\leq\langle b_{k},(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\rangle,\ i.e.,\\
&\langle b_{k},(\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}))\rangle\leq 0.\end{split}

In (
4.7
), we replace the inequality with the equality. We get

(4.8)

\langle b_{k},(\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}))\rangle=0\ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.

Let
V
be the set of the solutions of (
4.8
).

Assume that

(4.9)

V\cap\mathcal{S}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset

and take
\tilde{x}\in V\cap\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
.
Then, we have
for all
k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.10)

\begin{split}&f_{k}(\tilde{x})=a_{k}\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+2\langle b_{k},\tilde{x}\rangle+c_{k}=\\
&a_{k}(\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2})+2\langle{b_{k}},(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\rangle+c_{k}=\\
&\lambda(a_{k}\|x_{v}\|^{2}+2\langle b_{k},x_{v}\rangle+c_{k})+(1-\lambda)(a_{k}\|x_{w}\|^{2}+2\langle b_{k},x_{w}\rangle+c_{k})=\\
&\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w}),\end{split}

which proves Theorem
11
.
Hence, in order to finish the proof, we need to show that (
4.9
) holds, i.e.
V\cap\mathcal{S}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset
.
We change variable by setting

(4.11)

y=\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}).

The sets
\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
and
V
become

(4.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{S}(\lambda):=\{y\in H\ |\ \|y+(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\},\\
&V:=\text{ solution set of }\langle b_{k},y\rangle=0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Notice that, in the space of the variable
y\in H
,
\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
is a sphere centered in
(-\lambda x_{v}-(1-\lambda)x_{w})
of radius

\sqrt{\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}}
, while
V
is the space of solutions of the homogeneous system of equations of the form

(4.13)

\langle b_{k},y\rangle=0\ \ k\in\{0,...,m\}

We choose a maximally linearly independent
subset of cardinality
\bar{m}
of the vectors
b_{k}
,
k\in\{0,...,m\}
. Without loss of generality, we will indicate the vectors in the maximally linearly independent subset with
b_{k}
,
k\in\{1,...,\bar{m}\}
.
Then, the solution set
V
of system (
4.13
), coincides with the kernel of the linear continuous operator
\mathcal{B}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}
, defined as

(4.14)

\mathcal{B}y:=\begin{bmatrix}&\langle b_{1},y\rangle\\
&\vdots\\
&\langle b_{\bar{m}},y\rangle\end{bmatrix}.

The operators is continuous because, by the Riesz theorem, each functional
\langle b_{k},y\rangle
,
k=1,...,\bar{m}
is continuous on
H
.
Notice that the range of the operator
\mathcal{B}
is finite-dimensional, i.e.,

\text{range}(\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{R}^{\bar{m}}
.

We can write
\text{ker }\mathcal{B}=V=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{\bar{m}}\{x\in H\ |\ \langle b_{k},x\rangle=0\},

for the linearly independent set
\{b_{l},b_{2},\cdots,b_{\bar{m}}\}
in
H
.
By (
2.4
), we have

(4.15)

H=\text{ker }\mathcal{B}\oplus\text{range }\mathcal{B}^{*}=V\oplus\text{range }\mathcal{B}^{*}.

By Theorem
5
,
V
is a subspace of codimension
\bar{m}
.
By the definition of codimension,
\text{dim range }\mathcal{B}^{*}=\bar{m}
.

We prove by contradiction that
V\neq\{0\}
, in the case that
H
is infinite dimensional, assumption
i.
, and in the case where
H
is finite dimensional with
n>\bar{m}
, assumption
ii.

By (
4.15
), if
V=\{0\}

,
\forall\,y\in H
and for
0\in H
, there exists
\bar{y}\in\text{range }\mathcal{B}^{*}
such that
y=\bar{y}+0
.
Then a basis of
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, which consist in a set of
\bar{m}
maximally linearly independent vectors by Definition
3
, is also a basis for
H
. This is a contradiction with
V=\{0\}
in case that
H
is infinite dimensional, since
\bar{m}<\infty
, see Example 2.27 of
[
2
]
, and also in case
H
is
n

\bar{m}<n
, by assumption.

Hence, there exists a point
y_{v}\in V
of norm
\|y_{v}\|>0
.

We can prove that there exists
\tilde{y}\in V\cap\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
analitically, as follows.
We want to find
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{y}:=\alpha y_{v}\in V\cap\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
.

We always have
\tilde{y}:=\alpha y_{v}\in V
, since,
\forall\,k\in\{1,...,\bar{m}\}
,
{\langle b_{k},\tilde{y}\rangle=\alpha\langle b_{k},y_{v}\rangle=0}
, in fact
V
is a subspace and every subspace is a cone.

We recall that
\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
is the sphere defined in (
4.12
). Hence, in order to prove that
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
, there should exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\tilde{y}=\alpha y_{v}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda),\ i.e.\\
&\|\alpha y_{v}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}.\end{split}

Consequently, we need to find
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.17)

\begin{split}&\|\alpha y_{v}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}=\\
&=\alpha^{2}\|y_{v}\|^{2}+\|\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}+2\alpha\langle y_{v},\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\rangle=\\
&=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\end{split}

By Corollary 2.15 of
[
2
]
,

(4.18)

\|(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}.

Hence, (
4.17
) becomes

(4.19)

\alpha^{2}\|y_{v}\|^{2}+2\alpha\langle y_{v},\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\rangle-\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}=0.

Note that by (
4.19
) it must be
\alpha\neq 0
since
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors.
There exists
\alpha
such that (
4.16
) is satisfied (i.e.
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
), if and only if

\displaystyle\Delta:=(\langle y_{v},(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\rangle)^{2}+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|y_{v}\|^{2}\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}\geq 0,

which holds for every
x_{v},x_{w}\in H
.

∎

Conclusions

According to Theorem

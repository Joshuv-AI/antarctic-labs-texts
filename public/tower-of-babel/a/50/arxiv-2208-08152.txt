Distortion of Hausdorff measures under Orlicz-Sobolev maps
Andrea Cianchi

Abstract
A comprehensive theory of the effect of Orlicz-Sobolev maps, between Euclidean spaces, on subsets with zero or finite Hausdorff measure is offered. Arbitrary Orlicz-Sobolev spaces embedded into the space of continuous function and Hausdorff measures built upon general gauge functions are included in our discussion. An explicit formula for the distortion of the relevant gauge function under the action of these maps is exhibited in terms of the Young function defining the Orlicz-Sobolev space. New phenomena and features, related to the flexibility in the definition of the degree of integrability of weak derivatives of maps and in the notion of measure of sets, are detected. Classical results, dealing with standard Sobolev spaces and Hausdorff measures, are recovered, and their optimality is shown to hold in a refined stronger sense. Special instances available in the literature, concerning Young functions and gauge functions of non-power type, are also reproduced and, when not sharp, improved.

1
Introduction and main results

A continuous map
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
in an open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is said to satisfy the Lusin
N
condition if
it maps Lebesgue null sets into Lebesgue null sets. This amounts to requiring that, for sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.1)

if
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(E)=0
,   then
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(u(E))=0
,

where
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}
denotes the outer Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The Lusin
N
property plays a fundamental role in various results from classical real analysis and differentiation theory. Early applications primarily concerning the case
n=1
can be found in

[
Sa37
]
.
Its crucial importance in dimension
n>1
is witnessed by its role for the validity of various formulas in geometric measure theory
and geometric function theory – see e.g.
[
RR55
, Th. 3, p. 364]
and
[
MSZ02
]
. In particular, the Lusin
N
condition is
critical in the proof of central properties of quasiconformal maps and, more generally, of
maps with bounded distortion and of quasiregular maps. The latter were introduced by Yu.G. Reshetnyak in
[
Re67
]
and subsequently
extensively analyzed and applied by several authors. A comprehensive treatment of the pertaining
theory and further references can be found in the monographs
[
Re89
,
Vai71
,
Ri93
,
IM01
]
.

More recently a natural extension of the Lusin
N
condition, where the Lebesgue measure is replaced
by lower dimensional Hausdorff measures, was decisive in works extending the Morse-Sard theorem and related results to classes of Sobolev maps
which need neither be Lipschitz nor everywhere differentiable – see, in particular,
[
BKK13
,
BKK15
,
KK18
]
.

In this connection, note that a map

u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, which is locally Lipschitz continuous, or everywhere differentiable,
is easily seen to possess the property that, for
sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.2)

if
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(E)=0
,    then
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(u(E))=0
,

where
\alpha\in(0,n]
and
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}
denotes the
\alpha
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. In particular, the Lusin
N
property (
1.1
) follows from (
1.2
), when
m=n
.

Information on the Hausdorff measure of the image of a set, which still implies the Lusin
N
condition (
1.1
), remains available if the map
u
,
though neither locally Lipschitz continuous nor everywhere differentiable, has yet a suitable degree of regularity. With this regard, recall that the
space of locally Lipschitz continuous maps from
\Omega
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
agrees with the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Here,
\mathrm{V}^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
stands for the local counterpart of the homogeneous Sobolev space
\mathrm{V}^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\mathrm{V}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is defined, for

p\in[1,\infty]
, as

(1.3)

\mathrm{V}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\big\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\,\text{: $u$ is weakly differentiable and
$|\nabla u|\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)$}\big\}.

On extending earlier results from
[
GV73
]
about dimension distortion under quasiconformal maps, in
[
Ka99
]
Kaufman established a modified version of property
(
1.2
) for maps in super-critical Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{V}^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, with
p>n
.
Recall that the latter assumption ensures that each map
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
has a (locally Hölder)
continuous representative.
The result of
[
Ka99
, Theorem 1]
asserts that, with this choice of the representative,
if
\alpha\in(0,n)
, then for sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.4)

if
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(E)<\infty
,    then
\mathscr{H}^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha+p-n}}(u(E))=0
.

The shift of the exponent from
\alpha
to
\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha+p-n}
is usually referred to as distortion of
Hausdorff measure under the map
u
.
Let us notice that, by contrast, such a property does not hold for all maps in
\mathrm{V}^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
if
1\leq p\leq n
, even
under the additional assumption of continuity. With this regard, see
[
Ce42
,
Po71
,
MM95
]
and, specifically,
[
HK14
, Theorem 4.3]
, for a streamlined presentation of Cesari’s
example of a continuous map
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, with
n\geq 2
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
u\bigl([-1,1]\times\{0\}^{n-1}\bigr)=[-1,1]^{m}
.
This shows that the Lusin
N
condition fails in
\mathrm{V}^{1,n}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
n\geq 2
.
Let us also observe that the distortion property is neither
guaranteed by the mere membership of
u
in the space of locally Hölder continuous maps with exponent
1-\frac{n}{p}
, into which

\mathrm{V}^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is embedded under the assumption that
p>n
.

Several contributions on related questions are available in the literature – see e.g.
[
ACTUTV13
,
BMT13
,
BKK15
,
FKR19
,
HH15
,
KKM99
,
KK14
,
KMZ15
,
KZ15
,
LSUT10
,
RZZ11
]
.
Specifically,
[
KKM99
, Theorem C]
ensures that the Lusin
N
property
holds for the continuous representative of every function from the Sobolev type space
\mathrm{V}^{1}\mathrm{L}^{n,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of those weakly differentiable maps
whose gradient belongs to the Lorentz space
\mathrm{L}^{n,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This borderline Sobolev space, which coincides with
\mathrm{V}^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
n=1
,
is in fact optimal in the class of all Sobolev type spaces built upon a rearrangement-invariant space for this property to hold. The same space is
even optimal for embeddings into the space of continuous functions
[
CP98
]
. On the other hand, the space
\mathrm{V}^{1}\mathrm{L}^{n,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
not embedded into any space of locally uniformly continuous functions (with a common modulus of continuity), and no analogue of property (
1.4
)
holds for maps in
\mathrm{V}^{1}\mathrm{L}^{n,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since they can map sets of any positive Hausdorff dimension into sets of Hausdorff dimension
n
. This means that they can map sets having zero
\alpha
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, for any given
\alpha\in(0,n]
, into sets of positive
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

The present paper is aimed at
a comprehensive description of the distortion of Hausdorff measures
in the broader class of Orlicz-Sobolev maps.
These are maps in the homogeneous Orlicz-Sobolev space

(1.5)

\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\big\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\,\text{: $u$ is weakly differentiable and
$|\nabla u|\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega)$}\big\},

or in its local counterpart
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
where
A
is a Young function and
\mathrm{L}^{A}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
is the associated Orlicz space.

In view of the discussion above, it is natural to focus on Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
which are embedded into a space of locally
uniformly continuous functions. A necessary and sufficient condition on
A
for embeddings of this kind to hold, as well as the optimal
modulus of continuity are well known
[
Ci96
]
, and are recalled in Section
2
. As a matter of fact, if
n\geq 2
the same condition is also necessary and sufficient
for the embedding of
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
into the space of plainly continuous functions, and even into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. In what follows, when dealing with a map from a space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
associated with a Young function
A
satisfying this condition, we shall always refer to its continuous representative.

A treatment of the relevant questions, tailored for the realm of general Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, finds its proper environment in
a wider range of Hausdorff measures
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
, built upon gauge functions
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which need not be just powers. In fact, this extended
class of measures already appears in Hausdorff’s original paper
[
Ha1918
]
. The corresponding Hausdorff content, denoted by
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}
,
will also come into play in our discussion.

Little seems to be known on the effect of Orlicz-Sobolev maps on general Hausdorff measures of sets. The available results in this regard are apparently limited to
the very special case when
A(t)\sim t^{n}(\log t)^{q}
near infinity for some
q>0
, and
\varphi(r)\sim r^{\alpha}(\log\frac{1}{r})^{\beta}
near

0
for some
\beta>0
. They can be found in
[
KZ15
,
KMZ15
,
RZZ11
]
. Here, and in what follows, the relation
“
\sim
” between two quantities means that they are bounded by each other, up to positive multiplicative constants.

Our contribution approaches the question in full generality and offers a handy recipe which, from any Young function
A
and any gauge function
\varphi
as above,
produces another gauge function
\psi
with the property that if
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then, for sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.6)

if
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)=0
then
\mathscr{H}^{\psi}(u(E))=0
.

Besides capturing wider classes of Sobolev type maps and Hausdorff measures, broadening the functional framework entails the surfacing of certain traits of the theory, which, by contrast, remain hidden for customary Sobolev spaces and Hausdorff measures defined in terms of plain power type functions. Allowing for a more flexible choice of function spaces and measures also enables us to supply sharper information about the optimality of well known results.

For instance, when
\varphi(r)=r^{n}
property (
1.6
) is shown to hold with
\psi(r)=r^{n}
. Hence, we infer that the Lusin
N
property is supported
by any Orlicz-Sobolev space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is embedded into a space of locally uniformly continuous functions.
This conclusion reproduces
[
KRSS14
, Corollary 2]
, and can also be reached via Lusin
N
property of the space
\mathrm{V}^{1}_{\rm loc}\mathrm{L}^{n,1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
recalled above,
owing to the inclusion
\mathrm{L}^{A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{L}^{n,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, which is known to hold whenever

\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is embedded into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Even more interestingly, the comprehensive environment of the present paper provides us with the required precision to disclose a seemingly new stability phenomenon, which is
not visible when the Sobolev spaces and the Hausdorff measures at disposal are the standard ones. It shows that
the validity of property (
1.6
) with
\mathscr{H}^{\psi}\sim\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
is not peculiar to the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{n}
, and hence, when
m=n
, to the
Lebesgue measure.
Indeed, classes of Young functions
A
and of gauge functions
\varphi
are identified for which such an equivalence holds.
Roughly speaking, this happens when the function
A(t)
has an essentially faster growth than the limiting power
t^{n}
near
infinity – e.g.
A(t)=t^{p}
with
p>n
– and the gauge function
\varphi
decays at zero essentially more slowly than any power. One striking aspect
of this result is that it parallels the one holding for the Lebesgue measure, but at the opposite endpoint, in a sense, of the scale of Hausdorff
measures. Conversely, for gauge functions
\varphi
of intermediate strength, or for Young functions
A
with almost critical
n
-th power type
growth, a gap always occurs between the measures
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
and
\mathscr{H}^{\psi}
.

Property (
1.6
) will follow as a corollary of Theorem
3.1
. The latter amounts to a robust form of this property,
and tells us that, given any function
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that, for sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.7)

if
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)<\delta
then
\mathscr{H}^{\psi}(u(E))<\varepsilon
.

An analogue of property (
1.7
) is also established with Hausdorff measures replaced by Hausdorff contents.

These results are refined in Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
.
The former provides us with a criterion on the Young function
A
and the gauge function
\varphi
for an augmented version of property (
1.6
) to hold.
This version tells us that the vanishing assumption on
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)
can just be replaced by a finiteness hypothesis, with the same conclusion. Namely,
if
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then, for sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(1.8)

if
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)<\infty
then
\mathscr{H}^{\psi}(u(E))=0
.

The examples alluded to above, when
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}\sim\mathscr{H}^{\psi}
, demonstrate that property (
1.8
) fails
if
A
and
\varphi
do not meet the requirements of the relevant criterion. However, in this case
a quantitative form of property (
1.6
) is yet offered, which tells us that

(1.9)

\mathscr{H}^{\psi}\bigl(u(E)\bigr)\leq c\,\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)

for maps
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, for all sets
E\subset\Omega
and
for some constant
c
depending, in an explicit way, on
n
,
\psi
and
\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega)}
.

Finally, Theorem
3.5
contains a variant of inequality (
1.9
) involving Hausdorff contents instead of measures.
The inequality in question holds for maps in
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Moreover, the dependence on
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}(E)
on the right-hand side is nonlinear.
A slightly more general version of this inequality can be established, for maps defined in arbitrary open sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the case
when
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}(E)
agrees with the outer Lebesgue measure. This result is enucleated in Theorem
3.7
.

Precise statements of the results outlined above are given in Section
3
, where applications to special choices of the functions
A

and
\varphi
are also proposed. In particular, they recover Kaufman’s theorem (
1.4
),
improve a theorem of
[
RZZ11
]
, and specify the results highlighted above. Section
4
is devoted to preliminary technical lemmas. The proofs of the main results are then accomplished in Section
5
.
In the final Section
6
, the sharpness of some of the examples
worked out is discussed. In particular, as hinted above, thanks to the use of general Hausdorff measures, an enhanced optimality of the
results already available in the literature can be established.

2
Hausdorff measures and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

In this section we collect a few definitions and properties about the Hausdorff measures and the underlying gauge functions considered
throughout, and the Young functions for the admissible Orlicz-Sobolev spaces in our main result.

2.1
Gauge functions and Hausdorff measures

A Hausdorff measure can be built upon any gauge function, an expression whereby we understand any function
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which is increasing, continuous
and vanishes only at
0
. Set, for
\sigma\in(0,\infty]
,

(2.1)

\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\varphi}(E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varphi\bigl(d(E_{i})\bigr)\ :\ E_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,d(E_{i})\leq\sigma,\ \ E\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right\}

for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here,
d(\cdot)
stands for diameter.
Then
the relevant measure
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
is defined as

(2.2)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)=\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\varphi}(E)

The set function
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{\varphi}
also enters our results and is called Hausdorff content.

For sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has that

(2.3)

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{\varphi}(E)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)=0,

see
[
AH96
, Proposition 5.1.5]
.

In order to rule out trivial cases, we shall assume that

(2.4)

\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(r)}{r^{n}}>0.

Actually, if condition (
2.4
) fails, then
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)=0
for every set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This follows from
[
AH96
, Proposition 5.1.8]
.
In fact, a minor variant of this proposition also ensures that, under (
2.4
), one can additionally assume, without loss of generality, that the function

(2.5)

\frac{\varphi(r)}{r^{n}}
is non-increasing,

see Lemma
2.1
below.

We shall therefore suppose throughout, without explicit mentioning, that all gauge functions
\varphi
fulfill condition (
2.5
).

Clearly, choosing
\varphi(t)=t^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,n]
, in the definitions of
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{\varphi}
reproduces (up to a multiplicative constant) the
customary Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}
and Hausdorff content
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}_{\infty}
.

One has that
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}\neq\mathcal{L}^{n}
is and only if

(2.6)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(r)}{r^{n}}=\infty.

Observe that, thanks to property (
2.5
),

(2.7)

\varphi(kr)\leq\max\{1,k^{n}\}\,\varphi(r)\qquad\text{for $k>0$ and $r\geq 0$.}

An alternate Hausdorff type measure
\Lambda^{\varphi}
, defined in terms of coverings by dyadic closed cubes, will be needed in our proofs. These measures are sometimes called net measures, see

[
Fa85
, Chap. 5]
. On setting

(2.8)

\Lambda_{\sigma}^{\varphi}(E)=\inf\bigg\{\sum_{i}\varphi(d(Q_{i})):E\subset\bigcup_{i}Q_{i},\,\,Q_{i}\,\text{is a dyadic cube and }d(Q_{i})\leq\sigma\bigg\}

for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the relevant measure
is defined as

(2.9)

\Lambda^{\varphi}(E)=\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\sigma}^{\varphi}(E).

Thanks to property
(
2.7
),

(2.10)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\sigma}(E)\leq\Lambda^{\varphi}_{\sigma}(E)\leq c_{n}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\frac{\sigma}{2}}(E)

for
\sigma\in(0,\infty]
and for every
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, and hence

(2.11)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)\leq\Lambda^{\varphi}(E)\leq c_{n}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)

for every set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, where the constant
c_{n}
only depends on the
dimension
n
(see, e.g.,
[
BKK15
, Lemma 2.2]
).
Moreover,
by property (
2.7
) again, given any
k>0
, on defining the gauge function
\varphi_{k}
as

(2.12)

\varphi_{k}(t)=\varphi(kt)\quad\text{ for $t\geq 0$,}

one has that

(2.13)

\Lambda^{\varphi_{k}}(E)\approx\Lambda^{\varphi}(E)

for every set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, up to multiplicative constants depending only on
k
.

Since
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
is a regular outer measure, it enjoys the ascending sets property, which tells us that, if
\{E_{j}\}
is any sequence of sets in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
such that

E_{j}\subset E_{j+1}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(2.14)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}\Big(\bigcup_{j}E_{j}\Big)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E_{j}),

see
[
Ro70/98
]
.
A deeper result from
[
Da70
]
(see also
[
Ro70/98
]
) ensures that the same conclusion holds for the Hausdorff content. Namely, if
\{E_{j}\}
is a sequence as above, then

(2.15)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}\Big(\bigcup_{j}E_{j}\Big)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}(E_{j}).

Notice that we shall only make use of (
2.15
) in the case when the gauge function
\varphi
is a power. In fact, we could, at the expense of an additional multiplicative constant, use the much more elementary result
[
Fa85
, Lemma 5.3]
. Instead of (
2.15
), the latter yields the bound

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}\Big(\bigcup_{j}E_{j}\Big)\leq c\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\infty}(E_{j})

for some constant that only depends on
\varphi
and
n
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing function such that
\varphi(0)=0
and satisfying condition (
2.4
).
Then, there exist a continuous increasing function
\varphi^{\circ}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and a positive constant
c=c(n)
such that
\varphi^{\circ}(0)=0
,

(2.16)

\frac{\varphi^{\circ}(r)}{r^{n}}
is non-increasing,

and

(2.17)

c\,\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\delta}(E)\leq\mathscr{H}^{\varphi^{\circ}}_{\delta}(E)\leq\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\delta}(E)

for every
\delta\in(0,\infty]
and every set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Define the function
\varphi^{\circ}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
as

\varphi^{\circ}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{r^{n}\inf_{0<t\leq r}\frac{\varphi(t)}{t^{n}}}&\mbox{ if }r>0\\
0&\mbox{ if }r=0.\end{array}\right.

One can verify that the function
\varphi^{\circ}
is increasing and fulfills property (
2.16
). These monotonicity properties imply that
\varphi^{\circ}
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
, and, since
\varphi
is continuous in
[0,\infty)
and vanishes at
0
, the function
\varphi^{\circ}
is continuous in
[0,\infty)
as well.

Inasmuch as
\varphi^{\circ}\leq\varphi
, the second inequality in equation (
2.17
) is trivial. In order to prove the first one, fix
\delta\in(0,\infty]
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We may assume that
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi^{\circ}}_{\delta}(E)<\infty
,
otherwise there is nothing to prove. Given any
\varepsilon>0
, pick a covering
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\supset E
, with
0<d(E_{i})<\delta
and such that

(2.18)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{\circ}\bigl(d(E_{i})\bigr)<\mathscr{H}^{\varphi^{\circ}}_{\delta}(E)+\varepsilon.

For each
i
we choose
d_{i}\in(0,d(E_{i})]
with the property that
\varphi^{\circ}(d(E_{i}))(1+\varepsilon)>\bigl(d(E_{i})/d_{i}\bigr)^{n}\varphi(d_{i})
. Now, observe that there exists a dimensional constant
c
such that, given
0<d\leq D
, any subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of diameter

D
can be covered by a family of sets with cardinality less that
c\bigl(D/d\bigr)^{n}
, and diameter at most
d
. This fact can be verified by considering
a suitable translation and dilation of the grid defined by
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Hence, for each
i
the set
E_{i}
can be decomposed as
E_{i}=\bigcup_{j}E_{i,j}
where the cardinality of the family of the sets

E_{i,j}
does not exceed
c\bigl(d(E_{i})/d_{i}\bigr)^{n}
, and
d(E_{i,j})\leq d_{i}
. Consequently,

(2.19)

(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varphi^{\circ}\bigl(d(E_{i})\bigr)>\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{d(E_{i})}{d_{i}}\right)^{n}\varphi(d_{i})\geq\frac{1}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j}\varphi\bigl(d(E_{i,j})\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{c}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}_{\delta}(E).

The conclusion follows from inequalities (
2.18
) and (
2.19
).

2.2
Young functions and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

A Young function
A\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is a (non trivial) convex function such that
A(0)=0
.
As a consequence, the function

(2.20)

\frac{A(t)}{t}
is non-decreasing in
(0,\infty)
.

The Young conjugate
\widetilde{A}
of
A
is also a Young function and is defined by

(2.21)

\widetilde{A}(t)=\sup\bigl\{\tau t-A(\tau):\,\tau\geq 0\bigr\}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

One has that
\widetilde{\widetilde{A}}=A
.

A Young function
A
is said to dominate another Young function
A_{0}
globally
if there exists a positive
constant
c
such that

(2.22)

A_{0}(t)\leq A(ct)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0.$}

The function
A
is said to dominate
A_{0}
near infinity if there
exists
t_{0}>0
such that (
2.22
) holds for
t\geq t_{0}
.
The functions
A
and
A_{0}
are called equivalent globally [near infinity] if they dominate each other globally [near infinity].

Let
\Omega
be a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
A
be a Young function. The Orlicz space
\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega)
is the Banach
space of measurable functions
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
for which the Luxemburg norm

(2.23)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}\,

is finite.

If a Young function
A
dominates another Young function
A_{0}
globally [near infinity], then

(2.24)

\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{A_{0}}(\Omega)

for every measurable set
\Omega
[for every measurable set
\Omega
with
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(\Omega)<\infty
]. Moreover, embedding (
2.24
) holds as an equality (up to equivalent norms), if equivalence holds between the Young function
A
and
A_{0}
instead of domination.

When
\Omega
is an open set, the corresponding homogeneous Orlicz-Sobolev space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is defined by (
1.5
).
The local Orlicz-Sobolev space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is defined accordingly.

As explained in Section
1
, our analysis applies to Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
supporting an embedding of the form

(2.25)

\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{\omega}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})

for some space of (locally) uniformly continuous functions

\mathrm{C}^{\omega}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, with modulus of continuity
\omega\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
.
By
[
Ci96
, Theorems 1a and 3]
, such an embedding holds
if and only if

(2.26)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t}{A(t)}=0&\quad\text{if $n=1$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle\int^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{t}{A(t)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,dt<\infty&\quad\text{if $n\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

When
n\geq 2
the condition in (
2.26
) is in fact also necessary and sufficient for the embedding of
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})

into the space of plainly continuous functions, and even into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. The sufficiency of this condition was earlier established in
[
C51
]
.

Specifically, in connection with (
2.25
),
denote by
B
the Young function given by

(2.27)

B(t)=\begin{cases}A(t)&\quad\text{if $n=1$}\\
\\
\Bigg(t^{n^{\prime}}\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(s)}{s^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,ds\Bigg)^{\widetilde{\hskip 8.19447pt}}&\quad\text{if $n\geq 2$,}\end{cases}

where the notation
(\dots)^{\widetilde{\hskip 8.19447pt}}
stands for the Young conjugate of the function in parentheses.
Observe that, by
[
Ci04
, Lemma 4.4]
, the convergence of the integral in (
2.27
) is equivalent to that in (
2.26
).
Then
there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa(n,m)
such that the Morrey type inequality

(2.28)

\frac{d(Q)^{n}}{\kappa}B\bigg(\frac{d(u(Q))}{\kappa\,\lambda\,d(Q)}\bigg)\leq\int_{Q}A\bigg(\frac{|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,dx,

holds for the continuous representative of any map in
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Here
Q
denotes any cube compactly contained in

\Omega
and
\lambda
any positive number which makes the integral finite – see
[
AC05
, Proof of Theorem 1.1]

(and also
[
CC16
, Theorem 4.1]
). Moreover, if
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, inequality (
2.28
) holds also for every open cube
Q\subset\Omega
.

In inequality (
2.28
), the continuous representative of
u
is considered.
As already mentioned above, such a representative will always be considered also in what follows, without explicitly mentioning,
when dealing with maps in the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with a Young function
A
fulfilling assumption (
2.26
).

In order to avoid additional unnecessary technicalities, in our discussion of the distortion of Hausdorff measures we shall assume that the Young function
A
defining the ambient Orlicz-Sobolev space is such that

(2.29)

0<A(t)<\infty\quad\text{for }t>0,

and

(2.30)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{t}{A(t)}=\infty&\quad\text{if $n=1$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}\bigg(\frac{t}{A(t)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,dt=\infty&\quad\text{if $n\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

A few comments on assumptions (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) are in order.
The assumptions that
A(t)>0
for
t>0
and that
A
satisfies (
2.29
) are immaterial when dealing with the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. The same is true for the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, provided that
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(\Omega)<\infty
.
Indeed, the function
A
can be replaced, if necessary, by a Young function
A_{0}
equivalent near infinity such that
A_{0}(t)>0
for
t>0
and satisfying condition (
2.30
).
Thanks to equation (
2.24
), after this replacement,
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\mathrm{V}^{1,A_{0}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and,
if
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(\Omega)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\mathrm{V}^{1,A_{0}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
(up to equivalent norms). By contrast, such a replacement does affect the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
when
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(\Omega)=\infty
. However, a close inspection of our proofs will reveal versions of our main results stated for maps in the space
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
continue to hold in the broader space
of those weakly differentiable maps
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
|\nabla u|\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(E)
for every set
E\subset\Omega
such that
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}(E)<\infty
. The latter space is again invariant under the replacement of
A
by a function
A_{0}
as above.

As for the requirement that
A(t)<\infty
for
t>0
, it can actually been dropped in our results about properties (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), and in their counterparts about Hausdorff contents. This is due to the fact that, if
A(t)=\infty
for large values of
t
, then
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{V}^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Since the proofs of the properties in question only make use of the local behaviour of maps in
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, our results follow via the classical results for Lipschitz continuous maps recalled above, inasmuch as maps in
\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
are locally Lipschitz continuous. On the other hand, the case of Lipschitz continuous functions demonstrates that the finiteness assumption on
A
cannot be dispensed with when the stronger property (
1.8
) is in question.

Let us finally observe that, when
n\geq 2
, the function
A
dominates globally the function
B
given by (
2.27
)
[
CC16
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Moreover, the functions
A
and
B
are equivalent near infinity if and only if

(2.31)

i_{\infty}(A)>n,

where
i_{\infty}(A)
denotes the lower Matuszewska–Orlicz index of
A
at infinity. For Young functions
A
fulfilling condition (
2.29
), this index is defined as

(2.32)

i_{\infty}(A)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\log\Big(\liminf_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{A(\lambda t)}{A(t)}\Big)}{\log\lambda}.

The functions
A
and
B
are equivalent globally if and only if

(2.33)

i(A)>n\,

where
i(A)
denotes the global lower Matuszewska–Orlicz index of
A
, given by

(2.34)

i(A)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\log\Big(\inf_{t>0}\tfrac{A(\lambda t)}{A(t)}\Big)}{\log\lambda}.

See
[
St79
, Lemma 2.3]
and, for more details,
[
CM19
, Proposition 4.1]
.

J(s)\sim\begin{cases}s^{\frac{p+\alpha-n}{p}}\big(\log\tfrac{1}{s}\big)^{\frac{\beta-q}{p}}&\quad\text{if $0\leq\alpha<n$}\\
s\big(\log\tfrac{1}{s}\big)^{\frac{\beta}{p}}\big(\log(\log\tfrac{1}{s})\big)^{-\frac{q}{p}}&\quad\text{if $\alpha=n$ and $\beta>0$}\\
s&\quad\text{if $\alpha=n$ and $\beta=0$}\end{cases}\quad\text{near zero.}

Consequently,

J^{-1}(r)\sim\begin{cases}r^{\frac{p}{p+\alpha-n}}\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\frac{q-\beta}{p+\alpha-n}}&\quad\text{if $0\leq\alpha<n$}\\
r\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{-\frac{\beta}{p}}\big(\log(\log\tfrac{1}{r})\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}&\quad\text{if $\alpha=n$ and $\beta>0$}\\
r&\quad\text{if $\alpha=n$ and $\beta=0$}\end{cases}\quad\text{near zero,}

whence equation (
3.23
) follows.

Example 3.9

We emphasize that the result concerning the case when

3.26

Example 3.11

Here, we exhibit classes of Young functions

\psi

) is equivalent to

. Loosely speaking, this is the case when the function

decays more slowly than any power near zero.
These instances provide us with a generalization of example (

Clearly, they also show that property (

) may actually fail if assumption (

) is not fulfilled.

Precisely, assume that

is any Young function such that

i_{\infty}(A)>n,

and let

be any gauge function such that

\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(r^{\gamma})}{\varphi(r)}>0\quad\text{for every $\gamma>0$.}

Then property (
3.3
) holds with

\psi(r)\sim\varphi(r)\quad\text{near zero.}

To verify this assertion, recall that the current assumption on
A
ensures that
B
is equivalent to

near infinity.

\psi(r)\geq c\,\varphi(r)\quad\text{near zero}

The last inequality reads

\varphi(s)\leq s^{n}B\big(s^{\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}\big)\quad\text{near zero.}

Choosing

By our choice of

Lemma 4.1

Assume that
A
is a Young function fulfilling conditions (
2.26
), (
2.29
) and (
2.30
).

(i) We have that

(4.1)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{A(t)}{t}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t}=\infty.

Moreover, the function

(4.2)

\frac{A(t)}{t}
is increasing in
(0,\infty)
.

(ii) The Young conjugate
\widetilde{A}
is such that
0<\widetilde{A}(t)<\infty
for
t>0
, and

(4.3)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\widetilde{A}(t)}{t}=0\quad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(t)}{t}=\infty.

(iii)
The Young function
B
given by (
2.27
) is such that
0<B(t)<\infty
for
t>0
, and

(4.4)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{B(t)}{t}=0\quad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B(t)}{t}=\infty.

In particular, the function
B:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is bijective.

Proof
(i) The first limit in equation (
4.1
) follows from assumption (
2.30
) and property (
2.20
). The second limit is a consequence of the same property and of assumption (
2.26
). The strengthening of property (
2.20
) stated in equation (
4.2
) holds owing to the first limit in (
4.1
).

(ii) The properties of the function
\widetilde{A}
claimed here are classically known to follow, via its very definition, from parallel properties enjoyed by
A
.

(iii)
The statement is trivial when
n=1
, thanks to Part (i). Assume that
n\geq 2
. As observed in the proof of Part (ii), it suffices to prove the statement with
B
replaced by
\widetilde{B}
. Since

\widetilde{B}(t)=t^{n^{\prime}}\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(s)}{s^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,ds\quad\text{for $t>0$,}

one clearly has that
0<\widetilde{B}(t)<\infty
for
t>0
. Moreover, thanks to equation (
4.3
),

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{n^{\prime}-1}\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(s)}{s^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,ds=0,

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{n^{\prime}-1}\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(s)}{s^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,ds=\infty.

The following result ensures that the inverse
J^{-1}
of

is well defined.

Lemma 4.2

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
A
be a Young function fulfilling conditions (
2.26
), (
2.29
), (
2.30
), and let
\varphi
be a gauge function.
Then the function
J\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, given by (
3.1
),
is increasing and such that

(4.5)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}J(t)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}J(t)=\infty.

In particular,
J
is bijective.

Proof
To begin with, by Lemma
4.1
the Young function
B
is bijective, and hence its inverse
B^{-1}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is classically well defined.
Suppose first that
n\geq 2
. One has that the function

(4.6)

\frac{B(t)}{t^{n}}\quad\text{is non-decreasing}

Indeed, property (
4.6
) is equivalent to the fact that the function

(4.7)

\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t^{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{is non-increasing}

and the latter holds, since

(4.8)

\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t^{n^{\prime}}}=\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(s)}{s^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,ds\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

As a consequence of property (
4.6
), the function

(4.9)

tB^{-1}(t^{-n})\quad\text{is non-decreasing.}

Hence, the function

(4.10)

J(t)=tB^{-1}\Big(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t^{n}}\Big)=\frac{t}{\varphi(t)^{\frac{1}{n}}}B^{-1}\Big(\frac{\varphi(t)}{t^{n}}\Big)\varphi(t)^{\frac{1}{n}}\quad\text{is increasing,}

inasmuch as the function
\varphi
is increasing and satisfies condition (
2.5
).

Next, owing to equations (
2.4
) and (
4.10
), and to the fact that the function
\varphi
is increasing, we have that

(4.11)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}J(t)\leq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}tB^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Big).

Moreover, a property of Young functions tells us that

(4.12)

t\leq B^{-1}(t)\widetilde{B}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t\qquad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Thus,

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}J(t)=0\quad\text{if}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B^{-1}(t)}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}=0.

Now, notice that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B^{-1}(t)}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{\widetilde{B}^{-1}(t)}=0\quad\text{ if}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t^{n^{\prime}}}=0,

and the last limit holds, owing to equation (
4.8
). The first limit in (
4.5
) is thus established.

As for the second one, from equations (
2.4
) and (
4.10
) again, and the monotonicity of the function
\varphi
, one has that

(4.14)

\lim_{t\to\infty}J(t)\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}\lim_{t\to\infty}tB^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Big),

where
c=\min\bigl\{1,\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)\bigr\}
. Therefore, by an analogous chain as above,

(4.15)

\lim_{t\to\infty}J(t)=\infty\quad\text{if}\quad\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{B^{-1}(t)}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}=\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{t^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{\widetilde{B}^{-1}(t)}=\infty\quad\text{if}\quad\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\widetilde{B}(t)}{t^{n^{\prime}}}=\infty.

By
[
Ci04
, Lemma 4.4]
, condition (
2.30
) is equivalent to

(4.16)

\int_{0}\frac{\widetilde{A}(t)}{t^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,dt=\infty.

Hence, the last limit in chain (
4.15
) holds. This proves the second limit in (
4.5
).

These pieces of information conclude the proof,
since the function
J
is continuous, inasmuch as the function
B^{-1}
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
.

Assume next that
n=1
, and hence
B=A
. Since
A
is a Young function, equation (
4.9
) still holds. Hence, we conclude as above that
J
is increasing. The limits

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}J(t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}J(t)=\infty

follow from equations (
4.11
) and (
4.14
), with
B=A
, via property (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.3

Lemma 4.4

Our analysis of the scaling functions begins with the next result, which, in particular, deals with a submultiplicativity property.

Lemma 4.5

Let
h\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing continuous function

(4.24)

\frac{h(t)}{t^{\gamma}}

\Theta(rs)\leq\Theta(r)\Theta(s)\quad\text{for $r,s>0$.}

We consider the case when
\Theta=\Theta_{h}^{0}
, the proof in the remaining cases being analogous. The monotonicity of the function
h
and property (
4.24
)
ensure that
r^{\gamma}\leq\Theta_{h}^{0}(r)\leq 1
for

.
Indeed, if

As for inequality (
4.26

fix

Lemma 4.7

), and let

Moreover,

(4.30)

\varphi\bigl(J_{r}^{-1}(st)\bigr)\leq\varphi(t)+\frac{t^{n}}{r}B(s)\quad\text{for $s,t>0$.}

Proof.

The function
J_{r}
is defined as the function

given by (

), with the gauge function
\varphi
replaced by

.
The asserted properties of the function

4.4

4.30
) follows on replacing the function
\varphi
with
r\varphi

Lemma 4.8

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
A
be a Young function fulfilling conditions (
2.26
), (
2.29
), (
2.30
), and let
B
be the function given by (
2.27
). Assume that
\varphi
is a gauge function satisfying property (
2.6
), and let
\psi
be the gauge function defined by (
3.2
). Let
J_{r}
be the function defined by (

Then

(4.31)

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi\bigl(J_{r}^{-1}(t)\bigr)}{\psi(t)}\geq\frac{1}{\Theta_{\psi}^{0}\bigl(\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)^{+}\bigr)}\quad\text{for $r>0$,}

and

(4.32)

\inf_{t>0}\frac{\varphi\bigl(J_{r}^{-1}(t)\bigr)}{\psi(t)}\geq\frac{1}{\Theta_{\psi}\bigl(\Theta_{B^{-1}}(r)^{+}\bigr)}\quad\text{for $r>0$.}

Proof.
We limit ourselves to proving equation (
4.31
), the proof of (
4.32
) being analogous. Fix

. By the very definitions of the functions
\psi
,
J
and

, and the properties of the latter established in Lemma

, one has that

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi\bigl(J_{r}^{-1}(t)\bigr)}{\psi(t)}=\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(s)}{\psi(J_{r}(s))}=\left(\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(r\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}\right)^{-1}.

Therefore, inequality (
4.31
) will follow if we show that

(4.33)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{z}{+}}\frac{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(r\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}\leq\Theta_{\psi}^{0}\bigl(\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\infty}(r)^{+}\bigr).

To verify inequality (
4.33
), observe that, owing to assumption (
2.6
), for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

such that

B^{-1}\bigl(r\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)<\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)(1+\varepsilon)B^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\delta)$.}

\frac{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(r\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}\leq\frac{\psi\left(\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)(1+\varepsilon)sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\delta)$.}

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(r\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}{\psi\left(sB^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\varphi(s)}{s^{n}}\bigr)\right)}\leq\Theta_{\psi}^{0}\bigl(\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)(1+\varepsilon)\bigr).

Remark 4.9

Under the additional condition that
i_{\infty}(A)>n
, the Young function
B
defined by (
2.27
) is equivalent to
A
at infinity. Namely,

(4.34)

B\bigl(c_{1}t\bigr)\leq A(t)\leq B\bigl(c_{2}t\bigr)

for every
t\geq t_{0}
, where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
t_{0}
are positive constants. As a consequence,

(4.35)

\Theta_{A^{-1}}^{\infty}(0^{+})=0\,\mbox{ if and only if }\,\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(0^{+})=0.

Indeed, inequalities (
4.34
) imply that

(4.36)

\frac{B^{-1}(s)}{c_{2}}\leq A^{-1}(s)\leq\frac{B^{-1}(s)}{c_{1}}

for large
s
. Hence,

\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)\leq\Theta_{A^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)\leq\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\Theta_{B^{-1}}^{\infty}(r)
for
r>0
, whence assertion (
4.35
) follows.

Analogously, if
i(A)>n
, then

(4.37)

\Theta_{A^{-1}}(0^{+})=0\,\mbox{ if and only if }\,\Theta_{B^{-1}}(0^{+})=0.

We conclude with a proof of the monotonicity of the function
\Xi_{\psi,B}
.

Lemma 4.10

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
A
be a Young function fulfilling conditions (

), (

),(

Proof.
It clearly suffices to show that, for fixed

Proof of Theorem

6
Sharpness

This final section is devoted to discussing the
sharpness of the conclusions of Theorems
3.1
for some examples from Section
3
.

I.

Let
A
and
\varphi
be as in Example
3.11
. Namely, let
A
be any Young function such that
i_{\infty}(A)>n
and let
\varphi
be a gauge function fulfilling

\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(r^{\gamma})}{\varphi(r)}>0
for every
\gamma>0
.
We have shown that

\psi(r)\sim\varphi(r)\quad\text{near zero.}

Thus the result is sharp, since this is the best possible choice of the gauge function
\psi
one can hope for. In particular, this includes the case exhibited in equation (
3.25
), i.e. maps
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p>n
,
and gauge functions satisfying
\varphi(r)\sim\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\beta}
near zero, for some
\beta<0
.

II.

As displayed in equation (
3.24
), when the classical Sobolev space
\mathrm{V}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
p>n
, and the standard Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}
, with

\alpha\in(0,n]
, are in question, a special instance of Example
3.8
reproduces Kaufman’s theorem. In Kaufman’s paper, the exponent in the Hausdorff
measure
\mathscr{H}^{\frac{\alpha p}{p+\alpha-n}}
is shown to be optimal.

The next result augments this conclusion and ensures that the gauge function
\psi(r)=r^{\frac{\alpha p}{p+\alpha-n}}
, which defines the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{\frac{\alpha p}{p+\alpha-n}}
in the target space, does not even
admit an improvement in the scale of powers of a logarithm, at least if these powers are not too small.

Theorem 6.1

Assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p>n
and
\alpha\in(0,n)
. Set
\sigma=\frac{\alpha p}{p+\alpha-n}
and let
\gamma>1+\sigma
.
Let
\phi
be a gauge function such that

(6.1)

\phi(r)\sim r^{\sigma}\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\gamma}\quad\text{near zero.}

Then there exist a mapping
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(6.2)

0<\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(E)<\infty\qquad\mbox{\rm and }\qquad\mathscr{H}^{\phi}(u(E))=\infty.

III.

Here we focus on maps from Example
3.9
, dealing with Young functions

A(t)
equivalent to
t^{n}(\log t)^{q}
near infinity,

in the special case when the gauge function

\varphi(r)\sim\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\beta}\quad\text{ near zero,}

for some
\beta<0
.
The result presented in that example asserts that the corresponding gauge function in the target space is of merely power type, and fulfills

\psi(r)\sim r^{\frac{n\beta}{n-q-1+\beta}}\quad\text{ near zero.}

The following theorem tells us that not only is this gauge function the best possible among all standard Hausdorff measures of power type, but it is also
optimal in the scale of “powers times powers of a logarithm” type gauge functions, at least if the power of the logarithm is not too small.

Theorem 6.2

Assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.3)

A(t)\sim t^{n}(\log t)^{q}\quad\text{\rm near infinity }

for some
q>n-1
, and

(6.4)

\varphi(r)\sim\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\beta}\quad\text{near zero}

for some
\beta<0
. Set
\sigma=\frac{n\beta}{n-q-1+\beta}
, let
\mu>1+\sigma
, and let

\phi
be a gauge function such that

(6.5)

\phi(r)\sim r^{\sigma}\big(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\big)^{\mu}\quad\text{near zero.}

Then there exist a mapping
u\in\mathrm{V}^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(6.6)

0<\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(E)<\infty\qquad\mbox{\rm and }\qquad\mathscr{H}^{\phi}(u(E))=\infty.

The proofs of Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
are reminiscent of arguments from
[
Ka99
]
and their modification in
[
HH15
, section 4.2]
.
In particular, they involve a construction resting upon probabilistic techniques. Additional difficulties arise in the present situation,
which are specific of the Orlicz ambient space. Theorem
6.2
will be established in detail. The proof of Theorem
6.1

follows along the same lines, and it is in fact simpler, since the Orlicz-Sobolev space agrees with a classical Sobolev space in this case.
For brevity, this proof will be omitted.

The question of whether the lower bounds on the exponents
\gamma
and
\mu
in Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
, respectively, can be removed is open. This is a natural interesting problem, whose solution seems however to require approaches and techniques substantially different from those employed in the present paper and in previous contributions in the literature. An even more challenging question is of course whether
\psi
is best possible among all gauge functions, for any given
A
and
\varphi
.

In preparation for the proof of Theorem
6.2
,
we recall a few definitions and establish a few preliminary lemmas.

The Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(E)
of a set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
is defined as

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(E)=\inf\{\alpha>0:\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(E)=0\}.

One also has that
\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(E)=\sup\{\alpha>0:\mathscr{H}^{\alpha}(E)=\infty\}
.

Given a Radon measure
\mu
on a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a number
\alpha>0
, we set

I_{\alpha}(\mu)=\int\limits_{E}\int\limits_{E}|x-y|^{-\alpha}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

It is well known that

(6.7)

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(E)=\sup\{\alpha>0:\text{there exists $\mu\neq 0$ such that $I_{\alpha}(\mu)<\infty$}\},

see, e.g.,
[
Ma95
, Theorem 8.9]
.

The following result (see
[
Ma95
, Theorem 8.7]
) will also be needed in our proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Lemma 6.3

Let
\phi
be a gauge function, and let
E
is compact set in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
. Assume that there exists a nonnegative Radon measure
\mu
such that
0<\mu(E)<\infty
and

\int\limits_{E}\int\limits_{E}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\phi\bigl(|x-y|\bigr)}<\infty.

Then
\mathscr{H}^{\phi}(E)=\infty
.

The following probabilistic lemma extends, with analogous proof,
[
BMT13
, Lemma 4.4]
.
In its statement,
\mathbf{E}
denotes expectation, and
\|\{a_{i}\}\|_{\infty}
the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of a sequence
\{a_{i}\}
. Henceforth,
\log
stands for
\log_{2}
.

Lemma 6.4

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let

\phi(r)=r^{\sigma}\log^{\mu}\bigl(b+\frac{1}{r}\bigr)\quad\text{for $r\geq 0$,}

with

, and

\{a_{i}\}\in\ell_{1}
. Then there exists a constant

,
\sigma
,
\mu
, and

, such that

(6.8)

\mathbf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\phi\biggl(\biggr|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}X_{i}\biggr|\biggr)\biggr)^{-1}\biggr]\leq c\,\frac{1}{\phi\bigl(\|\{a_{i}\}\|_{\infty}\bigr)}

Let us denote by
L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
the Orlicz space built upon the Young function

A(t)=t^{n}\log^{q}(t+2)
, with

, and equipped with the Luxemburg norm. The corresponding Orlicz-Sobolev space will be denoted by
\mathrm{V}^{1}L^{n}\log^{2}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})

We use the notation

for a cube in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, and

for its sidelength. By

Finally,

.

Lemma 6.5

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a function
\eta_{j}\in\mathrm{V}^{1}L^{n}\log^{2}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
such that

0\leq\eta(x)\leq 1\quad\text{for $x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}$,}

\eta_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}1\qquad\text{if \, $x\in Q(2^{-2^{j}})$}\\[10.0pt]
0\qquad\text{if \, $x\notin Q(2^{-2^{j-1}})$,}\end{cases}and

\|\nabla\eta_{j}\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}\leq c\,2^{j(q-n+1)/n},

Proof.

Let us define, for
j\in\mathbb{N}

\eta_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}1\qquad&\text{if $2|x|_{\infty}\leq 2^{-2^{j}}$}\\
\frac{1}{2^{(j-1)}}\biggl(-2^{(j-1)}+\log\frac{1}{2|x|_{\infty}}\biggr)\qquad&\text{if $2^{-2^{j}}<2|x|_{\infty}<2^{-2^{(j-1)}}$}\\
0\qquad&\text{if $2|x|_{\infty}\geq 2^{-2^{(j-1)}}$,}\end{cases}

where

and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

In particular, given

x\in Q(r)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad 2|x|_{\infty}\leq r.

Thus, the function
\eta_{j}

As for inequality (

|\nabla\eta_{j}(x)|\begin{cases}\sim\frac{1}{2^{j}\,|x|}\quad&\text{if $2^{-2^{j}}<2|x|_{\infty}<2^{-2^{(j-1)}}$ }\\

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\bigg(\frac{|\nabla\eta_{j}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,dx

Lemma 6.6

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}

(6.10)

\biggl\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\nabla v_{i}\biggr\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}\leq N^{\frac{1}{n}}\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}.

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\!|\nabla v_{i}|^{n}\log^{q}\bigl(|\nabla v_{i}|+2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x=1\qquad\text{for $i=1,\dots,N$.}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\!A\biggl(\frac{1}{\lambda}\biggr|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\nabla v_{i}\biggr|\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}x

6.10

We are now ready to provide a proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.
For simplicity of notation, throughout the proof we rename the parameter
\beta
, and set

\nu=-\beta

\nu>0

(6.11)

\varphi(r)=\bigl(\log\tfrac{1}{r}\bigr)^{-\nu}\quad\text{for $r>0$.}

For each

\ell(Q)=2^{-2^{j}}\qquad\text{for every $Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j}$},

the cardinality
\#\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{j}\bigr)

(6.12)

\#\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{j}\bigr)=[2^{j\nu}],

\mbox{ for every }Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j+1}\quad\mbox{ there exists }\,\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}\quad\mbox{ such that }\,Q\subset\tilde{Q}.

Let us set

\mathcal{A}=\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad M=\bigcap\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j}}Q.

(6.13)

0<\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(M)<\infty.

Moreover, we can assume that the cubes
are evenly distributed, and hence that there exists a universal constant

(6.14)

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(Q\cap M)\leq c\,2^{-j}

(6.15)

if
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}

\#\{Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j+1}:Q\subset\widetilde{Q}\}\leq c

Set

(6.16)

\sigma=\frac{n\nu}{q-n+1+\nu},

and fix
\mu>1+\sigma

\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

\phi(r)=\begin{cases}r^{\sigma}\log^{\mu}\bigl(b+\tfrac{1}{r})\quad&\text{if $r>0$}\\

Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j}

\eta_{Q}

\eta_{j}
from Lemma

(i)
\eta_{Q}

\ell(Q^{\prime})=2^{-2^{j-1}}

(ii)
0\leq\eta_{Q}\leq 1

(iii)
\eta_{Q}=1
on
Q

(iv)
\eta_{Q}=0

;

(v)
\|\nabla\eta_{Q}\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}\leq c\,2^{j(q-n+1)/n}

\xi=\{\xi_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{A}}

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\delta>1

(6.17)

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}

(6.18)

adjacent dyadic cubes of the same size.
These pieces of information, combined with property (
6.15
), enable one to complete the proof of (
6.18
).

From equations (
6.12
), (
6.18
) and Lemmas
6.5
–
6.6
we deduce that there exists a constant
c
, independent of
\xi
and
j
, such that

\|\nabla u_{\xi,j}\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}\leq c\,\frac{1}{j^{\delta}}2^{-\frac{j\nu}{\sigma}}\ 2^{j\nu/n}\ 2^{j(q-n+1)/n}=c\frac{1}{j^{\delta}}.

Therefore,

\|\nabla u_{\xi}\|_{L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})}\leq c\sum\limits_{j}\frac{1}{j^{\delta}}<\infty.

This shows that the mapping
u_{\xi}\in\mathrm{V}^{1}L^{n}\log^{q}L({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
, and hence, in particular,

u_{\xi}
is continuous, since we are assuming that
q>n-1
. Also, we have that
\mathop{\rm supp}u_{\xi}\subset[0,1]^{n}
.

The remaining part of the proof is devoted to showing that, for a generic choice
of
\xi
, the map
u_{\xi}
enjoys the property

(6.19)

\mathscr{H}^{\phi}\bigl(u_{\xi}(M)\bigr)=\infty.

The desired conclusion will then follow by coupling equation (
6.19
) with (
6.13
).

In order to establish property (
6.19
), let us regard
\xi=\{\xi\}_{Q\in\mathcal{A}}

as a sequence of independent random variables, which are identically distributed according to
the uniform probability distribution on
B
.
Consider
the measure
\mu_{\xi}=(u_{\xi})_{\#}(\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}\begin{picture}\put(2.0,0.0){\line{0}{1}{7.0}}\put(1.8,0.0){\line{1}{0}{7.0}}\end{picture}M)
supported on the compact set
u_{\xi}(M)
, i.e.,
\mu_{\xi}
is the push-forward of the measure
\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}
restricted to
M
via the map

u_{\xi}
. We claim that its expectation satisfies the condition

(6.20)

\mathbf{E}_{\xi}\biggl(\int\limits_{u_{\xi}(M)}\int\limits_{u_{\xi}(M)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{\xi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\xi}(y)}{\phi\bigl(|x-y|\bigr)}\biggr)<\infty.

In order to prove inequality (
6.20
), we make use Fubini’s theorem and rewrite the left-hand side of inequality (
6.20
) as

(6.21)

\int\limits_{M}\int\limits_{M}\mathbf{E}_{\xi}\biggl(\frac{1}{\phi\bigl(\bigl|u_{\xi}(x)-u_{\xi}(y)\bigr|\bigr)}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(y).

We have that

u_{\xi}(x)-u_{\xi}(y)=\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{A}}a_{Q}(x,y)\xi_{Q}\quad\text{for $x,y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}$,}

where

(6.22)

a_{Q}(x,y)=\frac{1}{j^{\delta}}2^{-\frac{\nu j}{\sigma}}\bigl(\eta_{Q}(x)-\eta_{Q}(y)\bigr)\quad\text{ for $Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j}$.}

The sequence
\{a_{Q}\}
clearly belongs to
\ell^{\infty}
.
Thus, thanks to inequality (
6.8
), inequality (
6.21
) will follow if we show that

(6.23)

\int\limits_{M}\frac{1}{\phi\bigl(\|a(x,y)\|_{\infty}\bigr)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(x)\leq c,

for some constant
c
is independent of
y
. Let us fix
y\in M
. For each
x\in M
, let us denote
by
j(x)
the largest integer such that both
x
and
y
either lie in the same cube or in adjacent cubes
from
\mathcal{A}_{j(x)}
. Hence, there exist cubes
\widetilde{Q}_{1}\ni x
and
\widetilde{Q}_{2}\ni y
such that
\widetilde{Q}_{1},\widetilde{Q}_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{j(x)+1}
and
2\tilde{Q}_{1}\cap Q_{2}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, there exist cubes

Q_{1}\ni x
and
Q_{2}\ni y
such that
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{j(x)+2}
and

\bigl(\mathop{\rm supp}\eta_{Q_{1}}\bigr)\cap\bigl(\mathop{\rm supp}\eta_{Q_{2}}\bigr)=\emptyset.

Consequently,
\eta_{Q_{1}}(x)=1
and

\eta_{Q_{1}}(y)=0
. Therefore, there exists a nonzero coefficient
a_{Q}

of size
\frac{2^{-(j(x)+2)\frac{\nu}{\sigma}}}{(j(x)+2)^{\delta}}
.
Hence

\|a(x,y)\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{2^{-j(x)\frac{\nu}{\sigma}}}{j(x)^{\delta}}.

The monotonicity of the function
\phi
guarantees that

(6.24)

\frac{1}{\phi\bigl(\|a(x,y)\|_{\infty}\bigr)}=\frac{1}{\|a(x,y)\|^{\sigma}_{\infty}\log^{\mu}\bigl(b+\frac{1}{\|a(x,y)\|_{\infty}}\bigr)}\leq c\,j(x)^{\delta\sigma-\mu}\,\,2^{\nu j(x)}

for some constant
c
.

Now, for each

y\in M
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist at most
3^{n}
dyadic cubes
Q\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
with the property that there exists
x\in Q\cap M

with
j(x)=j
. Thereby, equation (
6.14
) implies that

\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}\bigl(\bigl\{x\in M:j(x)=j\bigr\}\bigr)\leq 3^{n}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(Q\cap M)\leq c\ 2^{-j\nu}.

Finally, owing to inequality (
6.24
) and assumption (
6.17
),

\int\limits_{M}\frac{1}{\phi\bigl(\|a(x,y)\|_{\infty}\bigr)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{\varphi}(x)\leq c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-j\nu}}\biggl(j^{\delta\sigma-\mu}\,2^{\nu j}\biggr)=c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\delta\sigma-\mu}<\infty.

Inequality (
6.23
), and hence property (
6.20
), are thus established.

On the other hand, property (
6.20
)
clearly entails that, almost surely with respect to
\xi
,

\int\limits_{u_{\xi}(M)}\int\limits_{u_{\xi}(M)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{\xi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\xi}(y)}{\phi\bigl(|x-y|\bigr)}<\infty.

Hence, equation (
6.19
) follows via Lemma
6.3
. The proof is complete.

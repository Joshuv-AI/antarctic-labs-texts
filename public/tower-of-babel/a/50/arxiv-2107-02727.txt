Dependent finitely homogeneous rosy structures
Alf Onshuus, Pierre Simon

Abstract
We study finitely homogeneous dependent rosy structures, adapting results of Cherlin, Harrington and Lachlan proved for ω \omega -stable ω \omega -categorical structures. In particular, we prove that such structures have finite þ-rank and are coordinatized by a þ-rank 1 set. We show that they admit a distal, finitely axiomatizable, expansion. These results show that there are, up to inter-definability, at most countably many dependent rosy structures M M which are homogeneous in a finite relational language.

Introduction

The work towards classifying totally categorical structures is considered by many to be the starting point of “geometric stability theory”.
This began with the characterization of
\omega
-categorical strictly minimal sets (proved by Zilber using combinatorial and model-theoretic methods, and by Cherlin – Mills using group theory) as either degenerate geometries, or affine or projective geometries over finite fields. Cherlin, Harrington and Lachlan then proved in
[
CHL85
]
the Coordinatization Theorem for
\omega
-categorical
\omega
-stable theories which made it possible to analyze
\omega
-categorical
\omega
-stable structures in terms of strictly minimal sets. This was then used by Ahlbrandt and Ziegler (
[
AZ86
]
) followed by Hrushovski who proves in
[
Hru89
]
that any totally categorical theory is quasi-finitely axiomatizable and classifies such theories in the disintegrated case.

In
[
Sim21b
]
, a characterization of unstable þ-rank 1 sets in
\omega
-categorical dependent theories was given, providing a starting point analogue to the one given by Zilber and Cherlin – Mills in the stable case. This opens a path towards generalizing the results of
[
CHL85
]
and
[
Hru89
]
to dependent theories. The analogue of the rank given by
\omega
-stability is the thorn-rank (denote þ-rank) and introduced in
[
Ons06
]
. Theories for which this rank is ordinal-valued are called superrosy. Hence we expect that it is possible to generalize the aforementioned results to the class of superrosy dependent
\omega
-categorical theories. In particular, all such theories should be quasi-finitely axiomatizable. In this paper, we make a first step towards this. We avoid difficulties coming from proving finiteness of the rank and non-trivial strongly minimal sets by replacing the
\omega
-categorical hypothesis by the stronger one of being finitely homogeneous, namely having quantifier elimination in a finite relational language. For such theories, we show that being merely rosy implies having finite þ-rank.

Recall from
[
CHL85
]
that a
\emptyset
-definable set
X
coordinatizes
M
if for every
a\in M
,
\acl(a)\cap X\neq\emptyset
. If
M
is primitive, it follows that
M
is isomorphic to the set of conjugates in
X
of some finite subset of
X
(a structure called a
grassmanian
of
X
.)

The central result from this paper is Theorem
3.3
, which generalizes
[
CHL85
, Theorem 4.1]
to our context.

Theorem

(Coordinatization)
.

Let
M
be finitely homogeneous, dependent and rosy. Then
M
has finite rank and is coordinatized by a þ-rank 1 formula.

The second main theorem proved in Section
5
is an analogue of Lachlan’s result in
[
Lac84
]
that a finitely homogeneous stable structure is interpretable in dense linear orders.

Theorem

(Distal expansion)
.

Let
M
be a finitely homogeneous dependent rosy structure, then
M
admits an expansion which is distal and finitely axiomatizable. In particular, there are only countably many such structures up to interdefinability.

Finally, note that by
[

, Lemma 7.1]
any structure which eliminates quantifiers in a binary language has finite þ-rank. It follows that our results apply in particular to dependent structures homogeneous in a finite binary language.

4
The geometry of a finitely homogeneous dependent structure of finite þ-rank

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
a,b
be tuples in
M
. Then the following hold.

1.

(Modularity:)

a\thind_{\acl^{eq}(a)\cap\acl^{eq}(b)}b.

2.

(Order triviality:)
If
V
is an ordered þ-rank one definable set, and

\overline{V}
is its completion, then

\acl(ab)\cap\overline{V}=\left(\acl(a)\cup\acl(b)\right)\cap\overline{V}.

(Stable triviality:) If

is a
\emptyset
-definable strongly minimal þ-rank one set, then

\acl(ab)\cap X=\left(\acl(a)\cup\acl(b)\right)\cap X.

Proof.

To prove modularity, it is enough to show that if

then
\acl(a)\cap\acl(b)\neq\acl(\emptyset)
. The result then follows by induction on
\thr(a/b)
(adding
\acl(a)\cap\acl(b)
to the language). The base case is when
a\in\acl(b)
where the result holds trivially.

So assume that
a\nthind b
.

By Corollary
3.6
, we know that
a
as inter-algebraic with a tuple
(a_{i})_{i\leq N}
such that
\tp(a_{i+1}/a_{1}\dots a_{i})
has þ-rank one and is either minimal ordered or indiscernible (strictly minimal sets in a finitely homogeneous structure are indiscernible). By
\thr
-rank 1,
a_{i+1}\in\acl(a_{1}\dots a_{i}b)
if and only if
a_{i+1}\nthind_{a_{1}\dots a_{i}}b
.

By transitivity of þ-rank and our hypothesis there is a smallest
i
such that
a_{i}\in\acl(a_{<i}b)
. By transitivity of þ-independence and minimality of
i
, if
d:=a_{<i}
then
d\thind b
.

Let
X_{d}=\tp(a_{i}/d)
and let
\{W_{e^{\prime}}\}_{e^{\prime}\in F}
be the sets that we get applying Fact
2.30
to the family
\{X_{d^{\prime}}\}_{d^{\prime}\models\tp(d/\emptyset)}
. Let
e
be the parameter of the set into which

injects and
\alpha
the image of
a_{i}
, so that
e\in\acl(d)
and
\alpha\in\acl(a_{i}d)\subseteq\acl(db)
. By Fact
2.33
(adding

to the language and noticing that

\alpha\in\acl(be)
.

As

and

,
\alpha\in\acl(b)

, which completes the proof of modularity.

Order triviality now follows from (and generalizes) Proposition
2.33
. Let

so that by modularity

. Adding

is still an ordered þ-rank one definable set. Proposition

gives that

\acl_{A}(ab)\cap\overline{V}=\left(\acl_{A}(a)\cup\acl_{A}(b)\right)\cap\overline{V}.

\acl(ab)\cap\overline{V}=\left(\acl(a)\cup\acl(b)\right)\cap\overline{V},

Stable triviality is proved in the same way.
∎

We prove that any dependent, finitely homogeneous structure

\omega
-categorical, dependent structure of finite þ-rank. Then for any tuple

We prove the result by induction on
\thr(a/\emptyset)

By Theorem
3.3
, let

be a þ-rank one formula coordinatizing
\tp(a/\emptyset)
. Let
\alpha\in X\cap\acl(a)
. Let

be the maximal 0-definable equivalence relation with infinite classes on
\tp(\alpha/\emptyset)
and let

has þ-rank one,

has finitely many classes and

\alpha_{0}

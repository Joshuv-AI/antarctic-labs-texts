Jet Veto Resummation with Jet Rapidity Cuts
and Frank J. Tackmann

Abstract
Jet vetoes are widely used in experimental analyses at the LHC to distinguish different hard-interaction processes. Experimental jet selections require a cut on the (pseudo)rapidity of reconstructed jets, | η jet | ≤ η cut |\eta_{\rm jet}|\leq\eta_{\rm cut} . We extend the standard jet- p T p_{T} (jet-veto) resummation, which implicitly works in the limit η cut → ∞ \eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty , by incorporating a finite jet rapidity cut. We also consider the case of a step in the required p T cut p_{T}^{\rm cut} at an intermediate value of | η | ≃ 2.5 |\eta|\simeq 2.5 , which is of experimental relevance to avoid the increased pile-up contamination beyond the reach of the tracking detectors. We identify all relevant parametric regimes, discuss their factorization and resummation as well as the relations between them, and show that the phenomenologically relevant regimes are free of large nonglobal logarithms. The η cut \eta_{\rm cut} dependence of all resummation ingredients is computed to the same order to which they are currently known for η cut → ∞ \eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty . Our results pave the way for carrying out the jet-veto resummation including a sharp cut or a step at η cut \eta_{\rm cut} to the same order as is currently available in the η cut → ∞ \eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty limit. The numerical impact of the jet rapidity cut is illustrated for benchmark q ​ q ¯ q\bar{q} and g ​ g gg initiated color-singlet processes at NLL ′ + {}^{\prime}+ NLO. We find that a rapidity cut at high η cut = 4.5 \eta_{\rm cut}=4.5 is safe to use and has little effect on the cross section. A sharp cut at η cut = 2.5 \eta_{\rm cut}=2.5 can in some cases lead to a substantial increase in the perturbative uncertainties, which can be mitigated by instead using a step in the veto.

Introduction

Measurements that involve a veto on additional jets, or more generally that divide events
into exclusive jet bins, play an important role at the LHC, e.g. in Higgs and diboson
measurements or in searches for physics beyond the Standard Model.
The jet binning differentiates between hard processes that differ in the number of hard
signal jets, and hence allows one to separate signal and background processes.
The separation into
0
-jet and
\geq 1
-jet bins also provides a
model-independent way to discriminate between
q\bar{q}
and
gg
initiated
processes
[
1

\alpha_{s}

These logarithms dominate the perturbative series when
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll Q

and represent an important source of theory uncertainty
[
2

.
They can be systematically resummed to improve the perturbative predictions and
assess the associated uncertainties, which has been well-developed in Drell-Yan
and Higgs production
[

,

Experiments can only reconstruct jets up to some maximal pseudorapidity

\lvert\eta\rvert\leq\eta_{\rm cut}
due to the range of the detector, e.g. for ATLAS and
CMS
\eta_{\rm cut}\sim 4.5
. In principle, the utility of the jet binning to
discriminate between different hard processes increases for a tighter jet veto
(smaller
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
). However, jets with small transverse momenta are difficult to
reconstruct experimentally, especially for pseudorapidity
\lvert\eta\rvert\gtrsim 2.5
beyond the reach of the tracking detectors, which are important to suppress

conditions become harsher, the contamination from unsuppressed pile-up jets
grows worse and must be avoided. One option is to increase the overall

.
For example, in the context of Higgs measurements, the increased pile up in Run
2 has forced raising the jet threshold from
25\,\mathrm{GeV}
to
30\,\mathrm{GeV}
. This however
weakens the jet veto and thus reduces its utility. Alternatively, to avoid
raising the jet threshold, one can consider jets only in a restricted
pseudorapidity range of
\lvert\eta\rvert\lesssim 2.5
. However, this looses the
discrimination power from forward jets, which are a distinguishing feature of
some processes (most notably weak-boson fusion topologies in Higgs and diboson
production). The best possible option combines both approaches and performs a

p_{T}^{\rm cut}
threshold for central jets and a
somewhat higher
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
threshold for forward jets. For example, recent
ATLAS Higgs measurements
[
27

reconstruct jets using
p_{T}^{\rm cut}=25\,\mathrm{GeV}
for
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.4
and
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}=30\,\mathrm{GeV}
for
\lvert\eta\rvert>2.4

(and no jets beyond rapidity
\lvert y\rvert=4.4
).

A discontinuous step in the jet threshold can also pose challenges on its own,
as it makes the experimental measurements more complex. Theoretically, we will see that it can
complicate the resummation of logarithms in some extreme cases. An alternative
to a step is to use jet vetoes that smoothly depend on the jet
rapidity
[
16
,
28

central rapidities and a looser one at forward rapidities. These rapidity-dependent
vetoes can also be supplemented with an additional sharp jet rapidity cut, which we briefly

2.1
Overview of parametric regimes

We consider exclusive 0-jet cross sections, where the veto is applied by
identifying jets with radius
R
(the details of the jet-clustering algorithm are
not relevant at the order we are working) and cutting on the transverse momentum

p_{T}^{\rm jet}
of the leading jet within
\lvert\eta_{\rm jet}\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}
,

\max_{k\in\text{jets}:\,\lvert\eta_{k}\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}}\lvert\vec{p}_{T,k}\rvert<p_{T}^{\rm cut}\,.

(2.1)

The resulting constraints on the rapidities and transverse momenta of
initial-state radiation (ISR) are displayed as black lines in

figure
2
. We can identify two distinct power-counting
parameters that govern the typical angular size of energetic collinear ISR with
energy
E\sim Q
, where
Q
is the momentum transferred in the hard
interaction: First, the
p_{T}
of the emissions is constrained by
p_{T}<p_{T}^{\rm cut}

for
\lvert\eta\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}
, corresponding to a maximum opening angle

\frac{p_{T}}{E}\lesssim\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q}\,.

(2.2)

Second, the
p_{T}
of an energetic emission at rapidity
\eta
is parametrically
p_{T}\sim Qe^{-\lvert\eta\rvert}
. The rapidity cut removes the first constraint for
\lvert\eta\rvert>\eta_{\rm cut}
. Hence, if
\eta_{\rm cut}
is central enough, emissions beyond
\eta_{\rm cut}

can reach a characteristic
p_{T}\lesssim Qe^{-\lvert\eta_{\rm cut}\rvert}
, corresponding to
a maximum opening angle

\frac{p_{T}}{E}\lesssim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\,.

(2.3)

There are three parametric regimes for
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
and
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
, which are
illustrated in
figure
2
for
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.5
. The thick black
lines show the veto for different values of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
. The thick gray curve
shows the relation
p_{T}/Q=e^{-\lvert\eta\rvert}
, while the thin gray lines show the
values of
\eta_{\rm cut}
and
p_{T}/Q=e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.

The first parametric regime is
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\gg e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
. As we will
demonstrate in
section
2.2
, in this regime effects due to the rapidity
cut are power suppressed by
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}
. Hence, they can be treated
as a fixed-order power correction to the standard jet-veto resummation, which
implicitly works in the limit
\eta_{\rm cut}=\infty
. For Higgs measurements with

p_{T}^{\rm cut}=25\,\mathrm{GeV}
,
\eta_{\rm cut}=4.5
,
Q\equiv m_{H}=125\,\mathrm{GeV}
, this parametric
assumption is well justified, as
m_{H}e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim 5\%
.

For heavier final states and/or more central rapidity cuts the relevant
parametric regime is
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
. This is the case for example
for
Q=1\,\mathrm{TeV}
and
\eta_{\rm cut}=4.5
or
Q=125\,\mathrm{GeV}
and
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.5
at
p_{T}^{\rm cut}=25\,\mathrm{GeV}
. In
section
2.3
, we show that in this regime the rapidity cut
effects must be treated as a leading-power correction, and that they can be
seamlessly incorporated into the existing jet-veto resummation without
rapidity cut. We will see that they affect only the boundary terms
in the resummed cross section, but not the anomalous dimensions
and evolution factors. Hence, they start contributing at NLL
′
or NNLL.

Finally in
section
2.4
, we discuss the parametric regime
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
. This case is conceptually interesting, since logarithms of the
ratio of scales
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
and
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
appear, changing the logarithmic
structure already at leading-logarithmic (LL) order. In addition, formally large
nonglobal logarithms of the same ratio appear. This regime is of
very limited phenomenological relevance for typical jet-binned analyses at the
LHC. For example, for
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.3
corresponding to
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}=0.1
, it
would require an extremely tight jet veto
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll 0.1\,Q
, which is
unrealistic as it would leave almost no signal in the
0
-jet cross
section. For the purpose of explicitly probing this regime experimentally, one
could lower
\eta_{\rm cut}\simeq 1.0-1.5
, such that the jet veto only acts
on radiation in the very central region.

2.2
Regime 1:
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\gg e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(standard jet veto resummation)

As usual, the scaling of the modes in the EFT follows from the nontrivial constraints imposed on emissions by the measurement. Soft emissions at central rapidities are always restricted by the jet veto. Collinear emissions with energy
\sim Q
and rapidity
\eta
have a transverse momentum
\sim Qe^{-\lvert\eta\rvert}
and are constrained by the measurement if
Qe^{-\lvert\eta\rvert}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, which determines their scaling.
Since
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, these collinear modes are parametrically not forward enough to
be sensitive to the rapidity cut, such that the description of their dynamics is simply governed by the power counting in
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
. The relevant EFT modes in this regime are thus the same as for a jet veto without any rapidity cut,

soft:

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(p_{T}^{\rm cut},p_{T}^{\rm cut},p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(\tfrac{(p_{T}^{\rm cut})^{2}}{Q},Q,p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{b}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Q,\tfrac{(p_{T}^{\rm cut})^{2}}{Q},p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\,.

(2.4)

Here and below, we give the scaling of momenta in terms of light-cone components defined as
(with
n\equiv n_{a}
,
\bar{n}\equiv n_{b}
),

p^{\mu}=\bar{n}\!\cdot\!p\,\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}+n\!\cdot\!p\,\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}+p^{\mu}_{\perp}\equiv(n\!\cdot\!p,\bar{n}\!\cdot\!p,\vec{p}_{\perp})\equiv(p^{+},p^{-},\vec{p}_{\perp})\,.

(2.5)

In addition, there are the usual inclusive collinear modes that describe the initial protons
at the scale
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
, and which are not specific to our discussion here.

In principle, we can consider collinear emissions that are forward enough to resolve rapidities

\lvert\eta\rvert\sim\eta_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear ($\eta_{\rm cut}$):}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Q,Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{b}\text{-collinear ($\eta_{\rm cut}$):}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Q,Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(2.6)

However, since
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, these emissions have too little transverse momentum to be affected
by the jet veto, and are therefore unconstrained and integrated over without
requiring additional modes in the EFT.
To explicitly see that the
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependence is power suppressed, note that the
full jet-veto measurement for the collinear modes contains a
\theta
function

\theta(\eta_{\rm cut}-\lvert\eta\rvert)=\theta(1-e^{\lvert\eta\rvert-\eta_{\rm cut}})=1+\mathcal{O}(Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut})\,,

(2.7)

which thus only induces power corrections in
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}
.

Therefore, at leading order in the power expansion,

2.3
Regime 2:
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependent beam functions)

In this regime, the scaling of soft and collinear modes is unchanged from the
previous case. However, the characteristic rapidity of the collinear modes now
coincides parametrically with
\eta_{\rm cut}
, i.e.,

soft:

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(p_{T}^{\rm cut},p_{T}^{\rm cut},p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(\tfrac{(p_{T}^{\rm cut})^{2}}{Q},Q,p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\sim\Bigl(Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Q,Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{b}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Q,\tfrac{(p_{T}^{\rm cut})^{2}}{Q},p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)\sim\Bigl(Q,Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(2.11)

Thus, collinear emissions resolve the rapidity cut, and are constrained by the
jet veto for
|\eta|<\eta_{\rm cut}
, while for
|\eta|>\eta_{\rm cut}
they are
unconstrained. As a result, the cross section factorizes at leading power as

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\Phi)

\displaystyle=H_{\kappa}(\Phi,\mu)\,B_{a}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{a},\mu,\nu)\,B_{b}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{b},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times S_{\kappa}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\mu,\nu)\,\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},R^{2}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(2.12)

The beam functions now explicitly depend on both
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
and
\eta_{\rm cut}
, while
the hard and soft functions are unchanged (with their characteristic scales still
given by eq. (
2.10

The RG consistency of the cross section fixes the anomalous dimensions
of the beam function in terms of those for the soft and hard functions. Thus,
the
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependence cannot change the renormalization of the beam function, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\ln B_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,x,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\nu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\ln B_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,x,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu)\,,

where the anomalous dimensions are the same as in the
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{B}(\omega,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma^{i}_{\rm cusp}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,\ln\frac{\nu}{\omega}+\gamma^{i}_{B}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\eta_{\Gamma}^{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\mu)+\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}[\alpha_{s}(p_{T}^{\rm cut}),R]\,,

(2.14)

and
\eta_{\Gamma}^{i}
in the resummed rapidity anomalous dimension is given by

\eta_{\Gamma}^{i}(\mu_{0},\mu)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\,\Gamma^{i}_{\rm cusp}[\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime})]\,.

(2.15)

Hence, the
\eta_{\rm cut}
effects do not affect the RG evolution itself, but only change the
beam function boundary conditions, and therefore first appear at NLL
′
.
The RG evolution between
\mu_{B}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
and
\mu_{H}\sim Q

now resums all large logarithms of
\mu_{B}/\mu_{H}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim

e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
,
while the beam function boundary condition now explicitly
depends on the ratio
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, which in contrast to
regime 1 is not power suppressed anymore.

In analogy to eq. (
2.9
) the beam functions can be factorized into collinear matching
coefficients, which now also depend on
\eta_{\rm cut}
, and the PDFs.
We write the matching coefficients as the sum of the usual
\eta_{\rm cut}
-independent
matching coefficients plus a correction term that encodes the
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)+\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)\,.

(2.16)

The
\eta_{\rm cut}
-independent
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
are given in
appendix
A.2
,
and in the following we focus on the
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
.

Consistency between the cross sections in eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) implies
that
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
vanishes as
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty
.
Specifically, defining

\zeta_{\rm cut}\equiv\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}\,,

(2.17)

the
\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
scale like

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}\bigl(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu\bigr)\sim\mathcal{O}(\zeta_{\rm cut})\qquad\rm{for}\,\,\zeta_{\rm cut}\to 0\,,

(2.18)

which is simply the statement from the previous subsection that the
\eta_{\rm cut}
effects
are power suppressed in
\zeta_{\rm cut}
for
\zeta_{\rm cut}\ll 1
.

In fact,
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
vanishes altogether for
z>\zeta_{\rm cut}/(1+\zeta_{\rm cut})
, which can be seen from purely kinematic
considerations as follows: For the
n
-collinear sector the term
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
accounts for the case where at least one jet with
p^{\rm jet}_{T}\geq p_{T}^{\rm cut}
and
\eta_{\rm jet}\geq\eta_{\rm cut}
is reconstructed (and no
jet with
\eta_{\rm jet}<\eta_{\rm cut}
). For
R\ll 1
all radiation in this jet
has
\eta\geq\eta_{\rm cut}
, as well. Thus, contributions to
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
can only appear if

\displaystyle p_{T}^{\rm cut}\leq|\vec{p}^{\,\text{jet}}_{T}|\leq\sum_{k\in\text{jets}}|\vec{p}_{T,k}|=\sum_{k\in\text{jets}}p_{k}^{-}e^{-\eta_{k}}\,,

(2.19)

where the second equality follows from the jets being massless for
R\ll 1
.
Rewriting this in terms of momentum fractions
p_{k}^{-}=z_{k}\,P_{n}^{-}=z_{k}\,\omega/z
yields, with
\sum_{k}z_{k}+z=1
and
P_{n}^{-}
the momentum of the
initial state proton,

\displaystyle p_{T}^{\rm cut}\leq\sum_{k\in\text{jets}}\frac{z_{k}}{z}\,\omega e^{-\eta_{k}}\leq\frac{1-z}{z}\,\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\,.

(2.20)

The second inequality follows from all reconstructed
n
-collinear jets having

\eta_{k}>\eta_{\rm cut}
. This implies that eq. (
2.18
) is trivially satisfied
since the domain of integration in
z
scales as
x\leq z\lesssim\zeta_{\rm cut}
.
Hence
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
is parametrically important for
\zeta_{\rm cut}\sim z\sim 1
, but vanishes in the threshold limit
z\to 1
. This leads to an
additional numerical suppression due to the falloff of the PDFs towards larger
partonic momentum fractions.

The RGE of
\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
follows from the beam-function RGE eq. (
2.3
) and
the analogue of the matching onto the PDFs in eq. (
2.9
). It is given by (with the
remaining arguments of
\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
understood)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{B}(\omega,\mu,\nu)\,\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z,\mu,\nu)-\sum_{k}\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ik}(z,\mu,\nu)\otimes_{z}2P_{kj}[\alpha_{s}(\mu),z]\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu)\,\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z,\mu,\nu)\,.

(2.21)

The Mellin convolution
\otimes_{z}
is defined as

g(z)\otimes_{z}h(z)=\int_{z}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\xi}\,g(\xi)\,h\Bigl(\frac{z}{\xi}\Bigr)\,,

(2.22)

and
2P_{ij}(\alpha_{s},z)
is the standard PDF anomalous dimension with respect to
\mu
,

\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}f_{i}(x,\mu)=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,2P_{ij}[\alpha_{s}(\mu),z]\,f_{j}\Bigl(\frac{x}{z},\mu\Bigr)\,.

(2.23)

Note that the RGE in eq. (
2.3
) does not mix
\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
with
{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}

and therefore does not change the
\zeta_{\rm cut}
scaling in eq. (
2.18
).
Solving eq. (
2.3
) order by order in perturbation theory, we find the following structure through two loops:

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\,\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(1)}(z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu)}{(4\pi)^{2}}\,\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(2)}(z)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta I_{ij}^{(1)}\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}},z\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(2)}(z)

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{\mu}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}\Bigl[2\Gamma^{i}_{0}\ln\frac{\nu}{\omega}+2\beta_{0}+\gamma^{i}_{B\,0}\Bigr]\,\Delta I_{ij}^{(1)}\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}},z\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad-2\ln\frac{\mu}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}\sum_{k}\Delta I_{ik}^{(1)}\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}},z\Bigr)\otimes_{z}P^{(0)}_{kj}(z)+\Delta I_{ij}^{(2)}\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}},R,z\Bigr)\,,

(2.24)

where
\Delta I_{ij}^{(n)}
is the boundary condition of the RGE at
\mu=p_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
\nu=\omega
, and the required anomalous dimension coefficients are
collected in
appendix
A.1
. By dimensional analysis and boost invariance,

\Delta I_{ij}^{(n)}
can only depend on
\zeta_{\rm cut}=\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}

in addition to
R
and
z
.

In
appendix
A.3
we determine the one-loop contribution

\Delta I_{ij}^{(1)}
, which has the simple form

\displaystyle\Delta I^{(1)}_{ij}\bigl(\zeta_{\rm cut},z\bigr)=\theta\Bigl(\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}}{1+\zeta_{\rm cut}}-z\Bigr)\,2P^{(0)}_{ij}(z)\,\ln\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}(1-z)}{z}\,,

(2.25)

with the one-loop splitting functions
P^{(0)}_{ij}(z)
as given in
eq. (
A.1
). The correction vanishes at the kinematic threshold encoded
in the overall
\theta
-function, which also cuts off the singular distributions
in
P^{(0)}_{ij}(z)
at
z=1
. The Mellin convolutions of
\Delta I_{ik}^{(1)}\otimes_{z}P^{(0)}_{kj}
appearing in the coefficient of
\ln(\mu/p_{T}^{\rm cut})
in
\Delta{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(2)}(z)
are given in

appendix
A.6
.

While the computation of the full two-loop contribution
\Delta I_{ij}^{(2)}

is beyond the scope of this paper,
we analytically compute its leading contribution in the small-
R
limit, which contains
a clustering logarithm of
R
. We write the full two-loop result as

\displaystyle\Delta I^{(2)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},R,z)

\displaystyle=\ln R\,\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)+\Delta I^{(2,c)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,.

(2.26)

In the limit
R\ll 1
, we exploit that for the emission of two close-by
collinear partons with relative rapidity
\Delta\eta\sim R
, the collinear
matrix element factorizes into two sequential collinear splittings at the scale

\mu\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}
and
\mu\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}R
, respectively. This allows us to
evaluate the coefficient of
\ln R
in a generic two-loop beam function as a
convolution of a primary on-shell emission and (the anomalous dimension of) the
semi-inclusive jet function of ref.
[
36
]
. Specifically, for the
case of
\Delta I^{(2)}_{ij}
we find

\displaystyle\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)

\displaystyle=\theta\Bigl(\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}}{1+\zeta_{\rm cut}}-z\Bigr)\,2P^{(0)}_{ij}(z)\Bigl[\theta\Bigl(z-\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}}{2+\zeta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)c^{R,\rm cut}_{ij}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\zeta_{\rm cut}(1-z)}\Bigr)-c^{R}_{ij}\Bigr]\,,

(2.27)

where the coefficient functions
c^{R,\rm cut}_{ij}
are given by

\displaystyle c^{R,\rm cut}_{gg}(x)=c^{R,\rm cut}_{qq}(x)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1/2}^{x}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\int_{1/2}^{z}\!\mathrm{d}z_{J}\Bigl[P^{(0)}_{gg}(z_{J})+2n_{f}P^{(0)}_{qg}(z_{J})\Bigr]\,,

\displaystyle c^{R,\rm cut}_{gq}(x)=c^{R,\rm cut}_{qg}(x)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1/2}^{x}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\int_{1/2}^{z}\!\mathrm{d}z_{J}\Bigl[P^{(0)}_{qq}(z_{J})+P^{(0)}_{gq}(z_{J})\Bigr]\,,

(2.28)

depending on whether the primary emission we split is a gluon (first line) or a quark (second line).
Their explicit expressions read

\displaystyle c^{R,\rm cut}_{gg}(x)=c^{R,\rm cut}_{qq}(x)

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\Bigl[\frac{5}{8}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-3x+\frac{9}{2}x^{2}-2x^{3}-2\ln^{2}x-4\,\textrm{Li}_{2}(x)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+2\beta_{0}\Bigl[-\frac{29}{24}-\ln 2+3x-\frac{3}{2}x^{2}+\frac{2}{3}x^{3}-\ln x\Bigr]\,,

\displaystyle c^{R,\rm cut}_{gq}(x)=c^{R,\rm cut}_{qg}(x)

\displaystyle=2C_{F}\Bigl[-3+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-3\ln 2+6x-3\ln x-2\ln^{2}x-4\,\textrm{Li}_{2}(x)\Bigr]\,.

(2.29)

The coefficients
c^{R}_{ij}
in eq. (
2.27
) are the (in principle known)

\ln R
in the
\eta_{\rm cut}
-independent two-loop beam

[

, which we also verified.

2.4
Regime 3:
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(collinear NGLs)

The hierarchy
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(with
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll 1
) exhibits
different features than the regimes discussed before. The typical transverse
momentum for emissions with
|\eta|>\eta_{\rm cut}
is parametrically
Qe^{-|\eta|}
,
indicated by the horizontal gray line in
figure
2
, which is
now much larger than for the strongly constrained emissions at
\lvert\eta\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}
. While the soft modes at central rapidities are not affected, there are
now two types of collinear modes at forward rapidities with
\lvert\eta\rvert\sim\eta_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim Q\Bigl(e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},1,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-soft-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim\Bigl(p_{T}^{\rm cut}e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},p_{T}^{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},p_{T}^{\rm cut}\Bigr)=p_{T}^{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigl(e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},1,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

(2.34)

and analogously for the
n_{b}
-collinear sector.

The collinear and soft-collinear modes have the same angular resolution and only
differ in their energy. This makes their all-order factorization challenging and
leads to the appearance of nonglobal logarithms
\ln(Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut})

starting at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
. Their factorization and resummation
requires the marginalization over all possible configurations of energetic
collinear emissions, involving soft-collinear matrix elements with a separate
Wilson line along each individual energetic collinear emission, see e.g. refs.
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
]
.

Since this regime has no immediate phenomenological relevance, we will not carry
out this complete procedure but restrict ourselves to the configuration with
soft-collinear Wilson lines along
n
and
\bar{n}
, i.e, along the two main
collinear emitters. This is sufficient for the LL resummation, for isolating the
nonglobal effects, and for discussing the relation to the other regimes. Our
discussion here is in close analogy to the regime 3 in the factorization of the
exclusive jet mass spectrum with small jet radius
R
in
ref.
[
55
]
, where the rapidity cut
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
here takes
the role of
R
there.
4
4

4

The main difference is that here, emissions for
|\eta|<\eta_{\rm cut}
are constrained
by their
p_{T}
relative to the same collinear (beam) direction. In the jet mass
case, emissions outside the jet are not constrained by their
p_{T}
relative to
the same collinear (jet) direction (but also relative to the beam direction).

The factorized cross section takes the form

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\Phi)

\displaystyle=H_{\kappa}(\Phi,\mu)\,\mathcal{B}_{a}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{a},\mu,\nu)\,\mathcal{B}_{b}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{b},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times S_{\kappa}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)\,\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},R^{2}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(2.35)

The initial-state collinear functions
\mathcal{B}_{i}
encode the contributions
of both soft-collinear and energetic collinear modes. They are related to the

\eta_{\rm cut}
dependent beam functions
B_{i}
in eq. (
2.3
) by an expansion in
the limit
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/(\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})\ll 1
,

\displaystyle B_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{B}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(2.36)

Without further factorization,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
contains large unresummed Sudakov
double logarithms
\alpha_{s}^{n}\ln^{2n}(p_{T}^{\rm cut}/\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})
. To resum
the leading double logarithms, we can decompose
\mathcal{B}_{i}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,\mu)\,\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[1+\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}},\omega,R\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(2.37)

The function
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
mainly describes contributions from the
energetic collinear modes. It was dubbed “unmeasured” beam function in
refs.
[
33
,
34
]
, in analogy to the unmeasured jet
function
[
56
]
. At one loop its matching coefficients account for
an energetic collinear emission with
|\eta|>\eta_{\rm cut}
. They are calculated in

appendix
A.3
and read

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{gg}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{A}}{4\pi}\biggl[\delta(1-z)\biggl(4\ln^{2}\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+4P_{gg}(z)\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu\,z}+8\mathcal{L}_{1}(1-z)+8\Bigl(\frac{1}{z}-2+z-z^{2}\Bigr)\ln(1-z)\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{gq}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{F}}{4\pi}\biggl[4P_{gq}(z)\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}{\mu\,z}+2z\biggr]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{qq}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{F}}{4\pi}\biggl[\delta(1-z)\biggl(4\ln^{2}\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}-6\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+4P_{qq}(z)\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu\,z}+8\mathcal{L}_{1}(1-z)-4(1+z)\ln(1-z)+2(1-z)\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{qg}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,T_{F}}{4\pi}\biggl[4P_{qg}(z)\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}{\mu\,z}+4z(1-z)\biggr]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

(2.38)

where
\mathcal{L}_{n}(1-z)\equiv[\ln^{n}(1-z)/(1-z)]_{+}
,

P_{ij}(z)
are the color-stripped LO splitting functions given in eq. (
A.1
),
and the flavor structure is trivial,

\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}=\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{q_{i}q_{j}}=\delta_{ij}\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{qq}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,\qquad\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}=\mathcal{I}^{(\rm cut)}_{\bar{q}_{i}q_{j}}=\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(2.39)

As argued in ref.
[
33
]
the results are directly related to the
matching coefficients for fragmenting jet functions in ref.
[
57
]
.

The function
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
in eq. (
2.4
) mainly describes
contributions from soft-collinear modes. At one loop it accounts for a
soft-collinear emission that couples eikonally to the incoming collinear parton

i
. The emission is constrained to
p_{T}<p_{T}^{\rm cut}
for
|\eta|<\eta_{\rm cut}
by the
jet veto, and is unconstrained for
|\eta|>\eta_{\rm cut}
. Using the
\eta

regulator
[
58
,
59
]
it is given by (see
appendix
A.4
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\,\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut,1)}_{i}+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu)}{(4\pi)^{2}}\,\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut,2)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut,1)}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=C_{i}\biggl(4\ln^{2}\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\mu}-8\ln\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\mu}\ln\frac{\nu e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\biggr)\,,

(2.40)

where
C_{i}=C_{F}
for an incoming quark or antiquark and
C_{A}
for an incoming gluon.
We checked explicitly that the above results obey the consistency constraint in eq. (
2.36
).
For this purpose, one has to note that eq. (
2.25
) becomes distribution valued in
(1-z)

when taking the limit
\zeta_{\rm cut}\gg 1
.

At two loops
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
contains a
\ln R
enhanced term.
Focusing on the constant terms not predicted by the RG evolution, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut,2)}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu=p_{T}^{\rm cut},\nu=\mu e^{\eta_{\rm cut}})=\ln R\,\mathcal{S}^{({\rm cut},2,\ln R)}_{i}+\mathcal{S}^{({\rm cut},2,c)}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,,

(2.41)

with
\mathcal{S}^{({\rm cut},2,c)}_{i}
an unknown two-loop constant.
The coefficient of
\ln R
is obtained by expanding
the
\ln R
coefficient in the
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependent beam function [see eqs. (
2.27
) and (
A.21
)]
to leading power in
1/\zeta_{\rm cut}
.
In the limit
\zeta_{\rm cut}\gg 1
, the sum
I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}+\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}
becomes proportional to
\delta(1-z)
,
as the arguments of both
\theta
-functions in eq. (
2.27
) approach
z=1
.
The coefficient of
\delta(1-z)
is then given by the
\zeta_{\rm cut}\to\infty
limit
of the integral of
\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}
,
which vanishes for
i\neq j
and for
i=j
leaves

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{({\rm cut},2,\ln R)}_{i}=8C_{i}\int_{1/2}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}c^{R,\rm cut}_{ii}(x)

(2.42)

\displaystyle=C_{i}\biggl\{C_{A}\Bigl[\frac{1622}{27}-\frac{548}{9}\ln 2-\frac{88}{3}\ln^{2}2-8\zeta_{3}\Bigr]+n_{f}T_{F}\Bigl[-\frac{652}{27}+\frac{232}{9}\ln 2+\frac{32}{3}\ln^{2}2\Bigr]\biggr\}\,.

The anomalous dimensions of
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}

have the general structure

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}(\eta_{\rm cut},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma^{i}_{\rm cusp}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,\ln\frac{\nu e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}+\gamma^{i}_{\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\nu,\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\eta^{i}_{\Gamma}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\mu)+\gamma^{i}_{\nu,\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}[\alpha_{s}(p_{T}^{\rm cut}),R]\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{B^{\rm cut}}\bigl(\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},\mu\bigr)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma^{i}_{\rm cusp}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,\ln\frac{\mu}{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}+\gamma^{i}_{B^{\rm cut}}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,

(2.43)

where the coefficients of the cusp anomalous dimension follow from our explicit one-loop calculation.
Consistency with eq. (
2.3
) implies

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}(\alpha_{s})+\gamma^{i}_{B^{\rm cut}}(\alpha_{s})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{B}(\alpha_{s})\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\nu,\mathcal{S}^{\rm cut}}(\alpha_{s},R)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}(\alpha_{s},R)=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{i}_{\nu}(\alpha_{s},R)\,.

(2.44)

All of the above noncusp anomalous dimensions vanish at one loop.
The canonical scales for
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
are

\displaystyle\mu^{(\rm cut)}_{B}\sim Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\,,\quad\mu^{(\rm cut)}_{\mathcal{S}}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}\,,\quad\nu^{(\rm cut)}_{\mathcal{S}}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}\,.

(2.45)

With these choices and the anomalous dimensions in eq. (
2.4
) one may
resum logarithms of
e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}
,
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
to any logarithmic order, and at
LL also logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.

Starting at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, the
\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}
term in eq. (
2.4
)
contains nonglobal logarithms of the form
\alpha_{s}^{n}\ln^{n}(p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})
.
A boost by
\eta_{\rm cut}
translates the measurement
into two hemispheres with one loose (
\eta>\eta_{\rm cut}
) and one tight constraint (
\eta<\eta_{\rm cut}
) on emissions.
The nonglobal structure in such a scenario is well understood
[
60
]
.
Depending on the desired accuracy, the NGLs may be included at fixed order via

\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}
as indicated in eq. (
2.4
),
or (partially) summed using more steps in a dressed parton expansion
[
53
]
.

Note that beyond one loop there is some freedom in the choice of measurement
that defines the
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
.
In particular, different measurements that reduce to eqs. (
2.4
) and (
2.4
)
for a single emission could give rise to different results for the two-loop
noncusp anomalous dimensions and finite terms
because the difference can be absorbed into
\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}
.
We stress that the result eq. (
2.42
) for the
\ln R
coefficient
in the two-loop soft-collinear function is, however, still unique.
This is because a
\ln R
contribution to
\mathcal{B}^{(\rm NG)}

requires a collinear parton in the unconstrained region
to emit a soft-collinear gluon into the constrained region,
which then undergoes a further collinear splitting.
This is only possible starting at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
.

To illustrate the numerical relevance of regime 3,
we again consider the fixed
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})

p_{T}^{\rm jet}
spectrum.
In regime 2, it is given to leading power in
p_{T}^{\rm jet}/Q
by the derivative of eq. (
2.3
),
while in regime 3, it is given to leading power in
p_{T}^{\rm jet}/(Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})
by the derivative of eq. (
2.4
).

In
figure
6
we compare the two results for
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.5
.
In regime 3, the 0-jet cross section at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
contains only single logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
because the double logarithms cancel between the soft and soft-collinear functions.
For this reason, the dashed-blue regime 3 spectrum with respect to
\ln p_{T}^{\rm jet}
is just a constant.
The exact regime 2 result (solid orange)
becomes well approximated by the further factorized regime 3 expression for
p_{T}^{\rm jet}\to 0
,
with their difference (dotted green) behaving like a power in
p_{T}^{\rm jet}
.
This provides a strong check of the regime 3 ingredients,
more precisely, of the
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
dependence encoded in the soft-collinear function.
(Since the beam function in regime 3 is independent of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
it drops out when computing the fixed-order spectrum.)

We also observe that for
gg\to H
and Drell-Yan at
Q=m_{Z}
, the regime 3 limit
is applicable only at very small
p_{T}^{\rm jet}\lesssim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}
and
already at
p_{T}^{\rm jet}\sim 10-20\,\mathrm{GeV}
the power corrections with respect to regime 2
are of the same size as the full regime 2 result. This means that
one would have to turn off the additional regime 3 resummation above this region.
For
gg\to X
with
m_{X}=1\,\mathrm{TeV}
and Drell-Yan at
Q=1\,\mathrm{TeV}
, the canonical regime 3 resummation
region, i.e., the region where the regime 3 singular corrections clearly dominate, extends
up to
p_{T}^{\rm jet}\lesssim 10\,\mathrm{GeV}
,
while regime 2 power corrections become
\mathcal{O}(1)
around
p_{T}^{\rm jet}\sim 60\,\mathrm{GeV}
.

Hence, we find that the additional resummation of logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm jet}/(Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})
in regime 3 is not relevant for jet veto analyses at the LHC,
where the lowest jet cuts are
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim 25\,\mathrm{GeV}
, for
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.5
and
final states in the
Q\sim 100\,\mathrm{GeV}
range. This also holds for final states at
very high invariant mass, e.g. in new physics searches, since in this case one
would typically also apply higher jet thresholds to retain enough signal in the

0
-jet bin. Realistically, one would not go below
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim 0.1Q
, which
means one never enters the limit where the regime 3 resummation is necessary.
This of course does not exclude the possibility that measurements designed to
probe simultaneously very high
Q
and very low
p_{T}^{\rm jet}
could benefit from the
regime 3 resummation. To explicitly explore this regime experimentally, the best
option is to restrict the jet veto to the very central region with
\eta_{\rm cut}\sim 1-1.5
.

2.5
Comparison to the literature

Jet vetoes in a restricted rapidity range were already encountered in
ref.
[
33
]
for the case of dijet production. Without spelling it
out explicitly, ref.
[
33
]
used a factorization for the regime 3 hierarchy
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll Q
, but did not distinguish between the soft and
soft-collinear modes necessary in this regime. As a result,
parametrically large rapidity logarithms
\ln e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}
were not captured, which are
relevant starting at NLL. The numerical results in ref.
[
33
]
were obtained for
Q\sim 1\,\mathrm{TeV}
,
\eta_{\rm cut}=5
, and
p_{T}^{\rm cut}=20
GeV, which rather corresponds to the opposite regime 1,

p_{T}^{\rm cut}\gg Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
. The difference between regimes 1 and 3 already
matters at LL.

In ref.
[
34
]
, the soft and soft-collinear modes in regime 3 are
distinguished and the presence of nonglobal logarithms in this regime is
recognized. Their factorization for dijet production is carried out at a level analogous to ours in
the previous subsection. That is, at NLL and beyond it only captures
logarithms of “global” origin, but does not capture nonglobal logarithms that are
parametrically of the same size. Our results for the one-loop quark matching
coefficients in eq. (
2.4
) and the one-loop soft-collinear function in
eq. (
2.4
) agree with ref.
[
34
]
[see their eqs. (3.27), (B.3),
and (B.5)]. Our results for the gluon channels and the two-loop clustering
corrections are new.

Ref.
[
34
]
does not consider regime 2 as a separate parametric
regime. Instead, it attempts to extend the validity of the regime 3
factorization into regime 2.
This is done by effectively adding the regime 2 nonsingular corrections
appearing in eq. (
2.36
) to the unmeasured beam functions.
Since some of the regime 3 modes become redundant in regime 2, this
also requires them to account for a nontrivial soft-collinear zero bin.
At fixed order, the sum of all their contributions must reproduce our result for the
regime 2 beam function; in
appendix
A.5
we check that this is
indeed the case for the quark matrix elements given in ref.
[
34
]
.
As we have seen in
figure
6
, outside the canonical regime 3,
there are large cancellations between the terms that are singular in
the regime 3 limit and the remaining regime 2 nonsingular contributions. This means
that the distinction between these contributions becomes arbitrary in regime 2
and that they must not be treated differently, as otherwise one risks inducing
large miscancellations.
(This is completely analogous to the situation when matching to full QCD, in which
case the
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
resummation must be turned off when entering the fixed-order
region at large
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
to properly recover the full-QCD result.)
In particular, in regime 2 all contributions that belong to the full

\eta_{\rm cut}
-dependent regime 2 beam function must be evaluated at a common scale

\mu\simeq p_{T}^{\rm cut}
and evolved together according to eq. (
2.3
).
This is not the case in ref.
[
34
]
,
where individual contributions to the regime 2 beam function are evaluated at different scales
throughout (
\mu_{B}^{\mathrm{cut}}
and
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{cut}}
in our notation).

Recently, the setup of ref.
[
34
]
was applied in ref.
[
35
]

to the case of transverse energy
E_{T}
in a restricted rapidity range in Drell-Yan.
In ref.
[
35
]
, profile scales are used to combine regimes 3 and
1, requiring that asymptotically
\mu^{(\rm cut)}_{B}=\mu^{(\rm cut)}_{\mathcal{S}}

in the regime 1 limit
E_{T}\gg Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
. While this can alleviate
the issue raised above, formally this relation must be satisfied already
in regime 2 for
E_{T}\sim Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.

As we have seen in
section
2.3
, there is no need to distinguish collinear
and soft-collinear modes in regime 2. Since for jet-veto analyses regimes 1 and
2 are the phenomenologically relevant ones, doing so unnecessarily complicates
the description. Recovering the NNLL
′
structure in regime 2
[see eq. (
2.3
)] based on regime 3
would be quite challenging due to the intricate nonglobal structure in regime 3.
Our dedicated treatment of regime 2 makes the absence of
nonglobal logarithms manifest, avoiding the associated complications,
and automatically ensures the correct treatment of the regime 2 nonsingular terms.
Furthermore, it shows how regime 2 generalizes the well-understood regime 1,
and as we will see in the next section allows for the generalization to a step
in the jet veto.

Concerning regime 1, ref.
[
35
]
also gave an argument that regime 1
holds up to power corrections in
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/E_{T}
, which was more intricate
due to immediately comparing regime 1 to regime 3.
The power suppression of
\eta_{\rm cut}

effects at sufficiently large
\eta_{\rm cut}
was also pointed out briefly in a
somewhat different context in ref.
[
61
]
.

3
Generalization to a step in the jet veto at
\eta_{\rm cut}

3.1
Overview of parametric regimes

We now generalize our results to the experimentally relevant scenario of the step-like jet veto
illustrated in the right panel of
figure
1
.
Here, jets with
p_{T}^{\rm jet}>p_{T}^{\rm cut}
are vetoed if
|\eta_{\rm jet}|<\eta_{\rm cut}
,
while for
|\eta_{\rm jet}|>\eta_{\rm cut}
the veto is loosened to
p_{T}^{\rm jet}>\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}>p_{T}^{\rm cut}
.
The 0-jet cross section is thus defined by the following measurement:

\max_{k\in\text{jets}:\,\lvert\eta_{k}\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}}\lvert\vec{p}_{T,k}\rvert<p_{T}^{\rm cut}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\max_{k\in\text{jets}:\,\lvert\eta_{k}\rvert>\eta_{\rm cut}}\lvert\vec{p}_{T,k}\rvert<\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\,.

(3.1)

There are now three relevant power-counting parameters
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
,
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
, and
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}

with four distinct parametric regimes (assuming
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\leq\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
),
illustrated in
figure
7
:

•

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(collinear step, top left),

•

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(collinear NGLs, top right),

•

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(soft-collinear step, bottom left),

•

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(soft-collinear NGLs, bottom right).

We discuss each of them in turn in the following subsections.
For
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(top left) the only relevant case is
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
leading to a modified measurement on the collinear modes, a collinear step,
compared to the case without a step (
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}=p_{T}^{\rm cut}
).

For
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
, we have to distinguish three
cases depending on
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
. Keeping
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
implies
the hierarchy
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(top right). Here, the mode setup is the same
as for regime 3 without step (corresponding to
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}=\infty
). As in that
case, the large difference in the constraints on collinear radiation above and
below
\eta_{\rm cut}
gives rise to collinear NGLs.

For
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
, we can then have either

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(bottom left)
or
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(bottom right).
For the former, the standard jet veto factorization
is recovered except that there are additional soft-collinear modes that resolve the
shallow step at
\eta_{\rm cut}
. For the latter, the steep step
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
at
\eta_{\rm cut}

gives rise to two distinct sets of soft-collinear modes with parametrically large
soft-collinear NGLs between them.

Emissions above the black lines are vetoed, and the thick gray line corresponds

, while those on

3.2
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(collinear step)

We first note that the hierarchy
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q

is effectively equivalent to the case without any jet veto beyond
\eta_{\rm cut}

(regime 2 in
section
2.3
).
Since collinear emissions with
\lvert\eta\rvert>\eta_{\rm cut}
cannot resolve the loose veto
at
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
, its effect is suppressed by
1/\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
and vanishes for
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\to\infty
.

The first nontrivial hierarchy is
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
,
illustrated in the top left panel of
figure
7
.
In this regime, the required modes are the same as in regime 2 in
section
2.3
.
The collinear radiation resolves the step at
\eta_{\rm cut}
while soft emissions are
insensitive to it, leading to a generalization of eq. (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\Phi)

\displaystyle=H_{\kappa}(\Phi,\mu)\,

\displaystyle\quad\times B_{a}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{a},\mu,\nu)\,B_{b}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{b},\mu,\nu)\,

\displaystyle\quad\times S_{\kappa}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q},\frac{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},R^{2}\Bigr)\biggr]\,,

(3.2)

with the beam functions now additionally depending on
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
.
In analogy to eq. (
2.16
) we write the modified beam function matching coefficients as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)+\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)\,.

(3.3)

The first term on the right-hand side is again the matching coefficient for a single
veto at
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
without any rapidity dependence.
The second term is the correction due to the step in the jet veto at
\lvert\eta\rvert=\eta_{\rm cut}
,
which vanishes for
p_{T}^{\rm cut}=\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
.
The correction is again renormalized according to eq. (
2.3
), which as before
follows from RG consistency.
In particular, its two-loop structure predicted by the RGE is the same as in
eq. (
2.3
), where the finite terms now depend on two dimensionless ratios,

\zeta_{\rm cut}=\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut}=\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}\,.

(3.4)

The one-loop and
\ln R
enhanced two-loop finite terms in
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}

can be written in terms of the results in eqs. (
2.25
) and (
2.27
) as

\displaystyle\Delta I^{(1)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut},z)

\displaystyle=\Delta I^{(1)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)-\Delta I^{(1)}_{ij}(\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut},z)\,,

\displaystyle\Delta I^{(2)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut},R,z)

\displaystyle=\ln R\,\Bigl[\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)-\Delta I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut},z)\Bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta I^{(2,c)}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},\tilde{\zeta}_{\rm cut},z)+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,,

(3.5)

since for a single (primary)
n_{a}
-collinear emission at
(\eta,p_{T})
the measurement
function for the step correction can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\theta(\eta-\eta_{\rm cut})\bigl[\theta(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}-p_{T})-\theta(p_{T}^{\rm cut}-p_{T})\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad=\theta(\eta-\eta_{\rm cut})\,\theta(p_{T}-p_{T}^{\rm cut})-\theta(\eta-\eta_{\rm cut})\,\theta(p_{T}-\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut})\,.

(3.6)

Due to the presence of correlated emissions with rapidities smaller and larger than
\eta_{\rm cut}
at two loops,
this decomposition no longer applies for the full two-loop finite term
\Delta I^{(2,c)}_{ij}
,
which therefore needs to be determined separately.

This regime is free of large nonglobal logarithms and is of direct phenomenological
interest. The parametric assumptions are satisfied e.g. for high-mass searches,

Q\gtrsim 300\,\mathrm{GeV}
, a realistic rapidity cut
\eta_{\rm cut}=2.5
, and veto
parameters
p_{T}^{\rm cut}=25\,\mathrm{GeV}
,
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}=50\,\mathrm{GeV}
, which clearly warrant
resummation of logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.
Evolving the beam function from
\mu_{B}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
to
\mu_{H}\sim Q

achieves this resummation for all of the above large ratios in the cross section,
while the full (logarithmic and nonlogarithmic) dependence on all of the
\mathcal{O}(1)
ratios

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, and
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}

is included at fixed order via the beam function boundary condition.

3.3
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}

This regime is a direct extension of regime 3 without a step in

.
The nontrivial new hierarchy is

,
shown in the top right panel of

In this regime, the mode setup is as in

2.4
.
However, the collinear modes are now additionally constrained for

by the jet veto at
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
, making them sensitive to both
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
and the
kinematic scale
Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.

2.4
) and (
2.36
) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu)\,\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[1+\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}},\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}},\omega,R\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(3.10)

Here
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
is the same soft-collinear function as in eq. (
2.4
).
By RG consistency the functions
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
have the same renormalization as those in eq. (
2.4
),
i.e., the additional dependence on
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
does not change their renormalization.
The associated matching coefficients at one loop are given by subtracting the correction term

\Delta I_{ij}^{(1)}
in eq. (
2.25
), which accounts for an
n
-collinear emission with
\eta>\eta_{\rm cut}
and
p_{T}>\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
from the coefficient
\mathcal{I}^{({\rm cut})}_{ij}
in eq. (
2.4
),
which accounts for an
n
-collinear emission with
\eta>\eta_{\rm cut}
without constraints from a jet veto, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{({\rm cut})}_{ij}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu)=\mathcal{I}^{({\rm cut})}_{ij}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)-\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\Delta I^{(1)}_{ij}\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}},z,R\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(3.11)

The
\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}
term in eq. (

) contains nonglobal
logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
.

3.4
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(soft-collinear step)

In this regime (bottom left panel of
figure
7
), the mode setup in

section
2.2
is extended by soft-collinear modes that resolve the step in
the jet veto at
\eta_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-soft-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}(e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},1)\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}(e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},1)\,,

\displaystyle n_{b}\text{-soft-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}(e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},1)\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}(e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},1)\,.

(3.12)

At the same time, the collinear modes only see the jet
veto at
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
, while the soft modes only see the veto at
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
.
This yields the factorized cross section

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\Phi)

\displaystyle=H_{\kappa}(\Phi,\mu)\,B_{a}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)\,B_{b}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\omega,\mu,\nu)\,S_{\kappa}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{S}_{a}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)\,\mathcal{S}_{b}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q},\frac{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Q},\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}},\frac{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}{Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}},R^{2}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(3.13)

The soft-collinear function
\mathcal{S}_{i}
encodes the actual step at
\eta_{\rm cut}
and is
defined by the measurement eq. (
3.1
).
For
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}=p_{T}^{\rm cut}
there is no step in the jet veto and
\mathcal{S}_{i}
has to vanish.
The RG consistency of the cross section implies that its
\mu
anomalous dimension vanishes in general, while its resummed
\nu
anomalous dimension is given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}_{\nu,\mathcal{S}}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},R)=2\eta_{\Gamma}^{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut})+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\{\gamma_{\nu}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}),R]-\gamma_{\nu}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(p_{T}^{\rm cut}),R]\Bigr\}\,.

(3.14)

It does not depend on
\mu
at all, as required by exact path independence in the
(\mu,\nu)
plane.
Note that the beam functions in eq. (
3.4
) depend on
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}
(rather than
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
)
because collinear radiation is too forward to be constrained by the tighter central veto.
This is reflected in the somewhat curious rapidity anomalous dimension of
\mathcal{S}_{i}
in eq. (
3.14
),
which accounts for the mismatch between the logarithms of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
and
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}

generated by the soft and beam rapidity evolution, respectively.

Solving eq. (
3.14
) order by order in
\alpha_{s}

we find the following very simple structure of the soft-collinear function through two loops:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\Bigl[2\Gamma^{i}_{0}\ln\rho\,L^{\nu}_{\mathcal{S}}+\mathcal{S}_{i,1}(\rho)\Bigr]

(3.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu)}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Bigl\{2(\Gamma^{i}_{0})^{2}\!\ln^{2}\!\!\rho\,(L^{\nu}_{\mathcal{S}})^{2}\!+\!2\ln\rho\,L^{\nu}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[2L^{\mu}_{\mathcal{S}}\beta_{0}\Gamma^{i}_{0}\!+\!\Gamma^{i}_{0}\mathcal{S}_{i,1}(\rho)\!+\!\Gamma^{i}_{1}\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\beta_{0}L^{\mu}_{\mathcal{S}}\,\mathcal{S}_{i,1}(\rho)+\mathcal{S}_{i,2}(\rho,R)\Bigr\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

where

\rho\equiv\frac{\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}\,,\qquad L^{\nu}_{\mathcal{S}}\equiv\ln\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{p_{T}^{\rm cut}\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}}\,,\qquad L^{\mu}_{\mathcal{S}}\equiv\ln\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{p_{T}^{\rm cut}\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}}\,.

(3.16)

It is straightforward to check that the one-loop finite term vanishes (see
appendix
A.4
),

\mathcal{S}_{i,1}=0\,.

(3.17)

The two-loop finite term is a generic function of the dimensionless ratio
\rho

and the jet radius parameter
R
, which must satisfy
\mathcal{S}_{i,2}(\rho=1,R)=0
.
As usual, we can decompose it according to its
R
dependence as

\mathcal{S}_{i,2}(\rho,R)=-8C_{i}c_{ii}^{R}\,\ln\rho\ln R+\mathcal{S}_{i,2}^{(c)}(\rho)+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,,

(3.18)

where
c_{ii}^{R}
is given by eq. (
2.3
) and
C_{i}=C_{F}\,(C_{A})
for
i=q\,(g)
.
The coefficient of
\ln R
at this order is completely determined
by the
R
dependence of the noncusp rapidity anomalous dimensions in eq. (
3.14
).
The full two-loop finite term
\mathcal{S}_{i,2}(\rho,R)
could readily be obtained
numerically using the methods of refs.
[
62
,
63
]
,
which would enable the full NNLL
′
resummation.

This regime is again free of nonglobal logarithms
and hence can easily be applied to phenomenological studies.
It can be used to supplement the EFT setup from
section
3.2
,
which enables the resummation of logarithms of the ratio
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
,
with an additional resummation of logarithms of the ratio

p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}/Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
by choosing the canonical scales

\displaystyle\mu_{B}

\displaystyle\sim\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{p_{T}^{\rm cut}\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\mu_{S}

\displaystyle\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}\,,

\displaystyle\nu_{B}

\displaystyle\sim Q\,,\quad

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{p_{T}^{\rm cut}\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\nu_{S}

\displaystyle\sim p_{T}^{\rm cut}\,.

(3.19)

Here, the rapidity evolution between
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\nu_{S}
is responsible
for resumming the large logarithms of
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\sim\nu_{S}/\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
.

4.1
Fixed-order matching and perturbative uncertainties

The resummed cross section obtained from eq. (

to power corrections in

p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim Q
.
We account for them by the usual additive matching,

\sigma_{0}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})=\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{res}}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})+\bigl[\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{FO}}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})-\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{sing}}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\bigr]\,.

(4.1)

Here,
\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{res}}
is the resummed singular cross section obtained from eq. (
2.3
),

\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{sing}}
is its fixed-order expansion, and

\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{FO}}
is the fixed-order result in full QCD.
By construction, the difference in square brackets is nonsingular and
vanishes as
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\to 0,\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty
and can therefore be included at
fixed order even at small
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
. The dominant corrections at small
p_{T}^{\rm cut}

are resummed in
\sigma_{0}^{\rm res}
. At large
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, fixed-order perturbation theory is
the appropriate description, so eq. (
4.1
) should recover
\sigma_{0}^{\rm FO}
.
This is achieved by turning off the resummation in
\sigma_{0}^{\rm res}
as a function
of
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
, and by constructing
\sigma_{0}^{\rm res}
such that
it precisely reproduces
\sigma^{\mathrm{sing}}
when the resummation is fully
turned off.

To smoothly turn off the resummation as we approach
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\to Q
,
we use profile scales
[
64
,
65
]
, following the setup developed
in ref.
[
13
]
.
We stress that the profile scales for regime 2 are in one-to-one correspondence
with the standard treatment in regime 1, since both regimes have the same RG structure.
Similarly, our treatment of perturbative uncertainties is based on profile scale variations
following ref.
[
13
]
.
We distinguish an overall yield uncertainty
\Delta_{\mu 0}
,
which is determined by a collective variation of all scales up and down,
and a resummation (jet bin migration) uncertainty
\Delta_{\mathrm{resum}}

from varying individual scales in the beam and soft functions.
For the gluon-induced processes, we follow ref.
[
66
]
and include an
additional uncertainty
\Delta_{\varphi}
from varying the complex phase of the hard scale,
which was not considered in ref.
[
13
]
.
The total uncertainty is then obtained by considering the
different uncertainty sources as independent, and hence uncorrelated, and
adding them in quadrature,

\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}=\Delta_{\mu 0}\oplus\Delta_{\varphi}\oplus\Delta_{\mathrm{resum}}\equiv\bigl(\Delta_{\mu 0}^{2}+\Delta_{\varphi}^{2}+\Delta_{\mathrm{resum}}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

(4.2)

5
Conclusion

We have developed a systematic framework to
seamlessly incorporate a cut on the rapidity of reconstructed jets,

\lvert\eta_{\rm jet}\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}
, into the theoretical description of jet-vetoed processes at the LHC.
We have shown that the standard jet-veto resummation, which neglects the rapidity cut,
is correct up to power corrections of
\mathcal{O}(Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}/p_{T}^{\rm cut})
,
with
Q
the hard-interaction scale and
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
the jet veto cut.

We calculated the necessary
\eta_{\rm cut}
-dependent corrections at one loop as well as
all logarithmic contributions to them at two loops (including both small-
R
clustering
logarithms and all jet-veto logarithms predicted by the RGE; see
section
2.3
).
The remaining ingredients required for a full NNLL
′
analysis with
\eta_{\rm cut}

effects are finite nonlogarithmic pieces that could be either calculated explicitly
or extracted numerically from the full-QCD results, which we leave to future work.
In addition, we considered for the first time the case of a step in the jet veto,
i.e., an increase in the veto parameter to
\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}>p_{T}^{\rm cut}
beyond
\eta_{\rm cut}
,
and showed how to similarly incorporate it into the jet-veto resummation (see
section
3.2
).

We also considered the jet veto cross section in the limit
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\ll Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
,
corresponding to either very tight vetoes or very central rapidity cuts (see
section
2.4
).
In this regime, the jet-veto resummation becomes impaired by the presence of nonglobal logarithms,
requiring a refactorization of the cross section.
However, we have argued that this parametric
region will most likely not play a role for typical jet binning analyses
at the LHC. If experimentally necessary, it can be avoided by replacing the sharp rapidity cut by
a moderate step in the jet veto, which is free of nonglobal logarithms (see
section
3.4
).

There are several important outcomes of our analysis. First, a jet rapidity cut at very forward
rapidities due to the finite detector acceptance,
\eta_{\rm cut}\simeq 4.5
,
is theoretically safe and unproblematic. In contrast, restricting the
jet veto to the more central region, with a sharp rapidity cut at the end of
the tracking detectors,
\eta_{\rm cut}\simeq 2.5
, leads to an increase in the perturbative
uncertainties (which may not be captured if the jet rapidity cut
is not included in the resummation). This loss in theoretical precision can become
particularly severe for gluon-induced processes and for processes at high
scales. It can however be mitigated by replacing the sharp
rapidity cut by a moderate step in the jet veto. We expect this to be a generic feature
that also holds at higher orders. It will be interesting to extend our resummed predictions
to the next order (NNLL
′
) to confirm this as well as to reduce the overall size of the
theoretical uncertainties.
We encourage our experimental colleagues to take full advantage of
such step-like jet vetoes in order to benefit from suitably tight jet vetoes at
central rapidities, while avoiding the increased pile-up contamination in the forward region.

A.1
Anomalous dimensions

We expand the
\beta
function of QCD as

\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=\beta[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,\qquad\beta(\alpha_{s})=-2\alpha_{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{n+1}\,,

(A.1)

with the one-loop and two-loop coefficients in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme given by

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}\,C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}\,T_{F}\,n_{f}\,,\qquad\beta_{1}=\frac{34}{3}\,C_{A}^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{20}{3}\,C_{A}\,+4C_{F}\Bigr)\,T_{F}\,n_{f}\,.

(A.2)

The cusp and all noncusp anomalous dimensions
\gamma(\alpha_{s})
are expanded as

\Gamma^{i}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\alpha_{s})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{i}_{n}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{n+1}\,,\qquad\gamma(\alpha_{s})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{n+1}\,.

(A.3)

The coefficients of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
cusp anomalous dimension through two loops are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{q}_{n}

\displaystyle=C_{F}\Gamma_{n}\,,\qquad\Gamma^{g}_{n}=C_{A}\Gamma_{n}\,,\qquad\text{(for $n=0,1,2$)}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=4\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=4\Bigl[C_{A}\Bigl(\frac{67}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\Bigr)-\frac{20}{9}\,T_{F}\,n_{f}\Bigr]=\frac{4}{3}\bigl[(4-\pi^{2})C_{A}+5\beta_{0}\bigr]\,.

(A.4)

The PDF anomalous dimension in eq. (
2.23
) is expanded as

P_{ij}(\alpha_{s},z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P^{(n)}_{ij}(z)\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{n+1}\,.

(A.5)

Note that we expand the PDF anomalous dimension in
\alpha_{s}/(4\pi)
and not
\alpha_{s}/(2\pi)

as is often done.
The one-loop coefficients of the PDF anomalous dimension read

\displaystyle P^{(0)}_{q_{i}q_{j}}(z)

\displaystyle=P^{(0)}_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}(z)=2C_{F}\,\delta_{ij}\,\theta(z)P_{qq}(z)\,,\quad

\displaystyle P^{(0)}_{gg}(z)

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\,\theta(z)P_{gg}(z)+\beta_{0}\,\delta(1-z)\,,

\displaystyle P^{(0)}_{q_{i}g}(z)

\displaystyle=P^{(0)}_{\bar{q}_{i}g}(z)=2T_{F}\,\theta(z)P_{qg}(z)\,,\quad

\displaystyle P^{(0)}_{gq_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle=P^{(0)}_{g\bar{q}_{i}}(z)=2C_{F}\,\theta(z)P_{gq}(z)\,,

(A.6)

in terms of the standard color-stripped one-loop QCD splitting functions

\displaystyle P_{qq}(z)

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)-\theta(1-z)(1+z)+\frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\theta(1-z)\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}\Bigr]_{+}\,,

\displaystyle P_{gg}(z)

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)+\theta(1-z)\Bigl[2z(1-z)+\frac{2(1-z)}{z}-2\Bigr]\,

\displaystyle=\,2\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)\frac{(1-z+z^{2})^{2}}{z}\,,

\displaystyle P_{qg}(z)

\displaystyle=\theta(1-z)\bigl[1-2z(1-z)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle P_{gq}(z)

\displaystyle=\theta(1-z)\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}\,.

(A.7)

The two-loop coefficients were calculated in
refs.
[
67
,
68
,
69
]

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{q_{i}q_{j}}(z)=P^{(1)}_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}(z)

\displaystyle=4C_{F}\,\theta(z)\bigl[\delta_{ij}P^{1}_{qqV}(z)+P^{1}_{qqS}(z)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{q_{i}g}(z)=P^{(1)}_{\bar{q}_{i}g}(z)

\displaystyle=4T_{F}\,\theta(z)\,P^{1}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}(z)=P^{(1)}_{\bar{q}_{i}q_{j}}(z)

\displaystyle=4C_{F}\,\theta(z)\bigl[\delta_{ij}P^{1}_{q\bar{q}V}(z)+P^{1}_{qqS}(z)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{gg}(z)

\displaystyle=4\theta(z)\bigl[C_{A}P^{1}_{ggA}+T_{F}n_{f}\,P^{1}_{ggF}\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{gq_{i}}(z)=P^{(1)}_{g\bar{q}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle=4C_{F}\theta(z)\,P^{1}_{gq}\,,

(A.8)

where explicit expressions for the

functions on the right-hand side can be found

A.2
Beam function master formula for
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty

In analogy to eq. (
2.3
) the matching coefficient
{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu,\nu)

of the
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty
beam functions satisfies (suppressing all other arguments of
{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{B}(\omega,\mu,\nu)\,{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)-\sum_{k}{\mathcal{I}}_{ik}(z)\otimes_{z}2P_{kj}[\alpha_{s}(\mu),z]\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{i}_{\nu,B}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},R,\mu)\,{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)\,.

(A.12)

Solving this order by order in
\alpha_{s}
yields the beam function master formula,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\,{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(1)}(z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu)}{(4\pi)^{2}}\,{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(2)}(z)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)\,L^{\mu}_{B}(2\Gamma^{i}_{0}L^{\nu}_{B}+\gamma^{i}_{B\,0})-2L^{\mu}_{B}P_{ij}^{(0)}(z)+I_{ij}^{(1)}(z)\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{(2)}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)\biggl\{(L^{\mu}_{B})^{2}\Bigl[2(\Gamma^{i}_{0})^{2}(L^{\nu}_{B})^{2}+L^{\nu}_{B}(2\beta_{0}\Gamma^{i}_{0}+2\Gamma^{i}_{0}\gamma^{i}_{B\,0})+\beta_{0}\gamma^{i}_{B\,0}+\frac{(\gamma^{i}_{B\,0})^{2}}{2}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+L^{\mu}_{B}\Bigl[2\Gamma^{i}_{1}L^{\nu}_{B}+\gamma^{i}_{B\,1}\Bigr]-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{i}_{\nu\,1}(R)L^{\nu}_{B}\biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+P_{ij}^{(0)}(z)\,(L^{\mu}_{B})^{2}\Bigl[-4\Gamma^{i}_{0}L^{\nu}_{B}-2\beta_{0}-2\gamma^{i}_{B\,0}\Bigr]+I_{ij}^{(1)}(z)\,L^{\mu}_{B}\Bigl[2\Gamma^{i}_{0}L^{\nu}_{B}+2\beta_{0}+\gamma^{i}_{B\,0}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-2L^{\mu}_{B}\sum_{k}I_{ik}^{(1)}(z)\otimes_{z}P_{kj}^{(0)}(z)-2L^{\mu}_{B}P_{ij}^{(1)}(z)+2(L^{\mu}_{B})^{2}\sum_{k}P_{ik}^{(0)}(z)\otimes_{z}P_{kj}^{(0)}(z)

\displaystyle\quad+I_{ij}^{(2)}(R,z)\,.

(A.13)

where we abbreviated

L_{B}^{\mu}=\ln\frac{\mu}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}\,,\quad L_{B}^{\nu}=\ln\frac{\nu}{\omega}\,.

(A.14)

The one-loop finite terms
I_{ij}^{(1)}
using the
\eta
regulator
[
58
,
59
]

are given by (see e.g. refs.
[
13
,
19
,
24
]
)

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{q_{i}q_{j}}(z)=I^{(1)}_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\,\delta_{ij}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\,2(1-z)\,,

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{q_{i}g}(z)=I^{(1)}_{\bar{q}_{i}g}(z)

\displaystyle=T_{F}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\,4z(1-z)\,,

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{gg}(z)

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{gq_{i}}(z)=I^{(1)}_{g\bar{q}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\,2z\,.

(A.15)

Their convolutions with leading-order splitting functions always appear in the form

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k}I_{ik}^{(1)}(z)\otimes_{z}P_{kj}^{(0)}(z)\,.

(A.16)

For quark-to-(anti)quark transitions we decompose the above flavor structure as

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{q_{i}q_{j}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{ij}\,\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{qqV}+\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{qqS}\,,

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{qqS}\,.

(A.17)

The building blocks on the right, together with the gluon-to-quark case, are given by

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{qqV}

\displaystyle=4C_{F}^{2}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\,(1-z)\Bigl[2\ln(1-z)-\ln z-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{qqS}

\displaystyle=4T_{F}C_{F}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\,\Bigl(\frac{4}{3}z^{2}+\frac{2}{3z}-2z\ln z-2\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{q_{i}g}

\displaystyle=\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{\bar{q}_{i}g}=\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\biggl\{4C_{F}T_{F}\,\bigl[z^{2}+z-(2z+1)\ln z-2\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+4T_{F}C_{A}\,\biggl[\frac{34}{3}z^{2}-10z+\frac{2}{3z}-8z\ln z-2+4z(1-z)\ln(1-z)\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+4T_{F}\beta_{0}\,z(1-z)\biggr\}\,.

(A.18)

The convolutions required for the gluon beam function read

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{gg}=4C_{F}(2n_{f})T_{F}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\bigl(1+z-2z^{2}+2z\ln z\bigr)\,,

(A.19)

\displaystyle\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{gq_{i}}=\bigl[I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}\bigr]_{g\bar{q}_{i}}=4C_{F}^{2}\,\theta(z)\theta(1-z)\Bigl[1+\frac{z}{2}-z\ln z+2z\ln(1-z)\Bigr]\,.

These expressions agree with the color-stripped convolutions
given in refs.
[
13
,
19
]
,
accounting for different conventions for splitting functions.
The two-loop finite terms in eq. (
A.2
) depend on
R
. Expanding them as

I^{(2)}_{ij}(R,z)=\ln R\,I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(z)+I^{(2,c)}_{ij}(z)+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,,

(A.20)

the coefficient of
\ln R
can be written as

I^{(2,\ln R)}_{ij}(z)=c^{R}_{ij}\Bigl[2P_{ij}^{(0)}(z)-\gamma^{i}_{B\,0}\,\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)\Bigr]\,.

(A.21)

We explicitly recomputed the coefficients
c^{R}_{ij}
,
for which we found some discrepancies in the literature.
[See eq. (
2.3
) in the main text.]
Note that the terms proportional to
\delta(1-z)
cancel in eq. (
A.21
) when
the distributional structure of the splitting function is written purely in terms
of
\delta(1-z)
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(1-z)
, and regular terms in
1-z
.

\displaystyle\langle g_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{g}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|g_{n}\rangle^{(e,\eta>\eta_{\rm cut})}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{\pi}\,\theta(z)\,\theta(1-z)\biggl\{\delta(1-z)\biggl[\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}+\ln^{2}\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)\biggl[-\frac{1}{\epsilon}+2\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu\,z}\biggr]+2\mathcal{L}_{1}(1-z)+(2+z)\biggl[\frac{1}{2\epsilon}-\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}{\mu\,z}\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\biggr\}\,.

(A.29)

The matching coefficient
\mathcal{I}_{gq}
is computed from diagram (f), giving

\displaystyle\langle q_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{g}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|q_{n}\rangle^{(f,\eta<\eta_{\rm cut})}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\,\theta\Bigl(z-\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{p_{T}^{\rm cut}+\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}\Bigr)\,\theta(1-z)\,\frac{2-2z+z^{2}}{z}\ln\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}z}{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\,,

\displaystyle\langle q_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{g}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|q_{n}\rangle^{(f,\eta>\eta_{\rm cut})}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\,\theta(z)\,\theta(1-z)\biggl\{\frac{2-2z+z^{2}}{z}\biggl[-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+\ln\frac{\omega e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}{\mu\,z}\biggr]+\frac{z}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\biggr\}\,.

(A.30)

Since PDF diagrams are scaleless in pure dimensional regularization,
the renormalized beam function matching coefficients are given by the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0}\eta^{0})
terms in these expressions.
From the results for
\mathcal{M}^{(\eta>\eta_{\rm cut})}_{B}
we get
\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{(\rm cut,1)}
in eq. (
2.4
),
while adding
\mathcal{M}^{(\eta<\eta_{\rm cut})}_{B}
gives the sum of eq. (
2.25
) and the second line of eq. (
A.2
).

A.4
Soft-collinear functions

We again use pure dimensional regularization and the
\eta
regulator,
so virtual diagrams and soft zero-bin subtractions are scaleless.
Note that we expand the
\eta
regulator to leading power using the soft-collinear scaling,
i.e., for a single emission we insert
\lvert k^{-}/\nu\rvert^{-\eta}
rather than
\lvert 2k^{3}/\nu\rvert^{-\eta}
.
This choice leads to a scaleless soft zero bin.
In Feynman gauge the bare one-loop real contribution to the
n
-soft-collinear
function
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
in eq. (
2.4
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})

\displaystyle=4g^{2}C_{i}\Bigl(\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{\epsilon}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\Bigl|\frac{\nu}{k^{-}}\Bigr|^{\eta}\,\frac{2\pi\delta^{+}(k^{\mu})}{k^{-}k^{+}}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm cut)}(k^{\mu},p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\,,

(A.31)

where
\delta^{+}(k^{\mu})=\delta(k^{2})\,\theta(k^{0})
, and the measurement function reads

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm cut)}(k^{\mu},p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})=\theta(p_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)\,\theta\Bigl(e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}-\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\Bigr)+\theta\Bigl(\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}-e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(A.32)

The second term yields a scaleless contribution, while the first term corresponds to a boosted hemisphere and leads to the result

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{i}}{\pi}\biggl\{\frac{1}{\eta}\biggl[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-2\ln\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\mu}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\biggr]-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{\nu e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}

\displaystyle\quad+\ln^{2}\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\mu}-2\ln\frac{p_{T}^{\rm cut}}{\mu}\ln\frac{\nu e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}+\mathcal{O}(\eta,\epsilon)\biggr\}\,.

(A.33)

Absorbing the divergent terms (including contributions of the form
\epsilon^{n}/\eta
, which are not shown)
into counterterms yields the renormalized one-loop result in eq. (
2.4
).

The bare one-loop contribution to the soft-collinear function resolving the step
in eq. (
3.4
) is again given by eq. (
A.31
),
but this time the measurement reads

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm step)}(k^{\mu},p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})=\theta(p_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)\,\theta\Bigl(e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}-\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\Bigr)+\theta(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)\,\theta\Bigl(\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}-e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(A.34)

Successively dropping terms that yield scaleless integrals we can replace (
\mapsto
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm step)}(k^{\mu},p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})

\displaystyle\mapsto\theta\Bigl(\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}-e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\Bigl[\theta(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)-\theta(p_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\mapsto\theta\Bigl(e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}-\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\Bigr)\Bigl[\theta(p_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)-\theta(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut}-|\vec{k}_{T}|)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm cut)}(k^{\mu},p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})-\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\rm cut)}(k^{\mu},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\,,

(A.35)

so at one loop we find a simple relation between bare results,

\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})=\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})-\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(\tilde{p}_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\,.

(A.36)

Remapping the measurement on the primary emission as in eq. (
A.4
)
yields the analogous relation for the small-
R
clustering contributions.

A.5
Comparison to quark beam function results in the literature

In ref.
[
34
]
the regime
p_{T}^{\rm cut}\sim Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
was accounted for
by adding a finite contribution
\Delta B_{i/j}^{(1)}
from so-called out-of-jet radiation
to the unmeasured beam function in eq. (
2.4
) as

\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{(\rm cut,1)}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)\mapsto\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{(\rm cut,1)}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z,\mu)+\Delta B_{i/j}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},z,\omega,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})\,.

(A.37)

One-loop consistency with our eq. (
2.3
) reads, at the level of bare ingredients,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij\,{\rm bare}}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z)

(A.38)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{I}_{ij\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z)+\Delta B_{i/j}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},z,\omega,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})+\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)\,\mathcal{S}_{i\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\,,

where
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(\rm cut,1)}
is the bare soft-collinear function at one loop, see eq. (
A.4
).
By eq. (
A.3
) we have, in terms of bare collinear matrix elements up to scaleless PDF diagrams,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{qq\,{\rm bare}}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z)=\mathcal{I}_{qq\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,z)+\langle q_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|q_{n}\rangle^{(\eta<\eta_{\rm cut})}\,,

(A.39)

and similarly for
\mathcal{I}_{qg}
.
With this, eq. (
A.38
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\langle q_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|q_{n}\rangle^{(\eta<\eta_{\rm cut})}

\displaystyle=\Delta B_{q/q}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},z,\omega,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})+\delta(1-z)\,\mathcal{S}_{q\,{\rm bare}}^{(\rm cut,1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut})\,,

\displaystyle\langle g_{n}|\theta(\omega)\,\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\rm bare}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},\omega)|g_{n}\rangle^{(\eta<\eta_{\rm cut})}

\displaystyle=\Delta B_{q/g}^{(1)}(p_{T}^{\rm cut},z,\omega,e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}})\,.

(A.40)

Both relations are readily checked after summing over all contributing diagrams.

A.6
Mellin convolutions in the two-loop rapidity dependent beam function

The PDF and beam function RGEs together predict Mellin convolutions of the following form
in the two-loop matching kernels eq. (
2.3
) for the rapidity dependent beam function:

\sum_{k}\Delta I_{ik}^{(1)}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)\otimes_{z}P^{(0)}_{kj}(z)\equiv[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)\,.

(A.41)

The relevant partonic channels read, leaving all arguments implicit,

\displaystyle[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{q_{i}q_{j}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\,8C_{F}^{2}\,P^{w}_{qq}\otimes_{z}P_{qq}+8T_{F}C_{F}\,P^{w}_{qg}\otimes_{z}P_{gq}=[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}\,,

\displaystyle[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}}

\displaystyle=8T_{F}C_{F}\,P^{w}_{qg}\otimes_{z}P_{gq}=[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{\bar{q}_{i}q_{j}}\,,

\displaystyle[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{q_{i}g}

\displaystyle=8C_{F}T_{F}\,P^{w}_{qq}\otimes_{z}P_{qg}+8T_{F}\Bigl[C_{A}\,P^{w}_{qg}\otimes_{z}P_{gg}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}P^{w}_{qg}\Bigr]=[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{\bar{q}_{i}g}\,,

\displaystyle[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{gg}

\displaystyle=8C_{A}\Bigl[C_{A}\,P^{w}_{gg}\otimes_{z}P_{gg}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}P^{w}_{gg}\Bigr]+8C_{F}T_{F}(2n_{f})\,P^{w}_{gq}\otimes_{z}P_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{gq_{i}}

\displaystyle=8C_{A}C_{F}P^{w}_{gg}\otimes_{z}P_{gq}+8C_{F}^{2}P^{w}_{gq}\otimes_{z}P_{qq}=[\Delta I^{(1)}\otimes P^{(0)}]_{g\bar{q}_{i}}\,,

(A.42)

where
n_{f}
is the number of light quark flavors.
Here we introduced a shorthand for weighted color-stripped splitting functions that depend on
\zeta_{\rm cut}
in addition to
z
,

P^{w}_{ij}(\zeta_{\rm cut},z)=\theta\Bigl(\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}}{1+\zeta_{\rm cut}}-z\Bigr)\ln\frac{\zeta_{\rm cut}(1-z)}{z}P_{ij}(z)\,.

(A.43)

The Mellin convolutions
P^{w}_{ik}\otimes_{z}P_{kj}
are straightforward to evaluate
analytically, but the resulting expressions are lengthy.
They are available from the authors upon request.

Appendix B
Jet rapidity cuts in
{\mathcal{T}}_{B}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{C}
vetoes

Here we comment on how the factorization setup for the smoothly rapidity dependent
jet vetoes introduced in ref.
[
16
]

is modified when an additional sharp jet rapidity cut is introduced.
The restriction on reconstructed jets reads in this case

\displaystyle\max_{k\in\text{jets}:\,\lvert\eta_{k}\rvert<\eta_{\rm cut}}\bigl\{\lvert\vec{p}_{T,k}\rvert\,f(\eta_{k})\bigr\}<{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}\,,

(B.1)

where
f(\eta)e^{|\eta|}\to 1
for
\eta\to\pm\infty
.
Examples are the beam thrust veto with
f(\eta)=e^{-|\eta|}
and the
C-parameter veto with
f(\eta)=1/(2\cosh\eta)
. The discussion of an
additional sharp rapidity cut largely parallels the case of the
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
veto in

section
2
. We again distinguish three hierarchies between
\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}/Q}

and
e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
, where now
\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}/Q}
replaces
p_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
as the
characteristic angular size of collinear radiation constrained by the jet veto.
The hierarchy
\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}/Q}\gg e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(regime 1) reduces to the
factorization for
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty

[
8
,
16
,
28
]
, up to power corrections of

\mathcal{O}(e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\sqrt{Q/{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}})
.

For
\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}/Q}\sim e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(regime 2) the relevant EFT modes scale as

soft:

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut})\,,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},Q,\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}Q}\Bigr)\sim\Bigl(Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Q,Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{b}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Q,{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}Q}\Bigr)\sim\Bigl(Q,Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(B.2)

The factorized 0-jet cross section reads

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\Phi)

\displaystyle=H_{\kappa}(\Phi,\mu)\,B_{a}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{a},\mu)\,B_{b}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega_{b},\mu)\,S_{\kappa}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},R,\mu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}}{Q},e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}},R^{2}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(B.3)

The beam and soft function are different from the
p_{T}^{\rm cut}
veto.
The rapidity cut again affects only the beam functions without changing their RG
structure or anomalous dimension.
In analogy to eq. (
2.16
) we can write the matching coefficients as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu)=\mathcal{I}_{ij}(\omega{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},R,z,\mu)+\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu)\,,

(B.4)

where the first term on the right-hand side is the
\eta_{\rm cut}\to\infty
matching coefficient
as calculated to two loops in ref.
[
28
]
,
which only depends on the boost-invariant product
\omega{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}
.
The correction
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}
vanishes for
\omega e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}}\ll{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}

and at one loop is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\,\theta\Bigl(\frac{\omega e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\omega e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}}+{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}}-z\Bigr)\,P^{(0)}_{ij}(z)\,\ln\frac{\omega e^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}}(1-z)}{z{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(B.5)

For
\sqrt{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}/Q}\ll e^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}
(regime 3) we again distinguish two types of collinear modes,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl(Qe^{-2\eta_{\rm cut}},Q,Qe^{-\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle n_{a}\text{-soft-collinear:}

\displaystyle\quad p^{\mu}\sim\Bigl({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}},{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}\Bigr)\,.

(B.6)

The contributions from these modes can be encoded in a function
\mathcal{B}_{i}

which can be refactorized in analogy to eq. (
2.4
) to resum Sudakov logarithms of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}/Q
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut},\eta_{\rm cut},R,\omega,z,\mu)

\displaystyle=B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}(\eta_{\rm cut},\omega,\mu)\,\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}\bigl({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},R,\mu\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[1+\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}\Bigl(\frac{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{2\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\omega},\omega,R\Bigr)\biggr]\,.

(B.7)

Here, the
\eta_{\rm cut}
dependent and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}
independent piece
B^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
is identical to the one in eq. (
2.4
),
while the soft-collinear function
\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}
is different and reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\rm cut)}_{i}\bigl({\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}},\mu\bigr)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{i}}{4\pi}\biggl(4\ln^{2}\frac{{\mathcal{T}}_{\rm cut}e^{\eta_{\rm cut}}}{\mu}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(B.8)

The
\mathcal{B}^{\rm(NG)}_{i}
piece, which contains nonglobal logarithms starting at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
,
is again different from the one in eq. (
2.4
).
We verified that, up to power corrections, the explicit one-loop expressions in eqs. (
2.4
) and (
B.8
)
reproduce the sum of eq. (
B
) and the matching coefficients
without a rapidity cut given in app. B of ref.
[
16
]
.

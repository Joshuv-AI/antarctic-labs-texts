Abstract
We construct families of asymptotically locally hyperbolic Riemannian metrics with constant scalar curvature (i.e., time symmetric vacuum general relativistic initial data sets with negative cosmological constant), with prescribed topology of apparent horizons and of the conformal boundary at infinity, and with controlled mass. In particular we obtain new classes of solutions with negative mass.

Introduction

Jean–Pierre Bourguignon made lasting contributions to differential geometry, to French mathematics, and to European research. Einstein metrics and their deformations are part of his research interests. This work is concerned with deformations of initial data for Lorentzian Einstein metrics, and it is a pleasure to dedicate to him this contribution to the subject.

In recent work
[
6
]
we derived a formula for the mass of three-dimensional asymptotically locally hyperbolic (ALH) manifolds obtained by gluing together two such manifolds “at infinity”.
(This procedure is also known as “Maskit gluing”, with the name introduced in
[
13
]
.) We used the formula to prove the main result there, namely existence of conformally compactified manifolds without boundary, or with a toroidal black hole boundary, with conformal infinity of genus larger than or equal to 2, with constant scalar curvature (CSC) and with negative total mass.

The first step in
[
6
]
was to use a glue-in of an exactly hyperbolic region near infinity
as done in
[
4
]
, introducing a small perturbation parameter
\epsilon
, which will be referred to as the “exotic-gluing parameter”. The key point of the analysis in
[
6
]
was to control the limit of the mass when
\epsilon
tends to zero. This was done for a symmetric gluing of
two ALH manifolds with identical toroidal boundaries at conformal infinity.

The object of this work is to extend the analysis of
[
6
]
to the gluing of any two CSC
ALH manifolds
with non-spherical topology at infinity. Thus, we can control the mass for gluings that do not have to be mirror-symmetric anymore, the manifolds being glued do not have to be identical, they can contain arbitrarily many black holes with arbitrary topology, and they are allowed to have more complicated topology at infinity.
We show that the mass of the manifold obtained by connecting-at-infinity two such manifolds tends to a well-defined limit when the exotic-gluing parameter
\epsilon
tends to zero; see (
2.8
) below, which generalises the formula proved in
[
6
]
for two identical components.
This formula allows one to control the sign of the mass, obtaining in particular the following result, where the manifold resulting from the gluing has at least two boundary components, one at infinity with genus
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}
, and another one at finite distance with genus
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}}
(where “BH” stands for “black hole”):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}},{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}\in\mathbb{N}
,
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}\geq{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}}
, with
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}\geq 2
if
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}}=0
.
There exist conformally compactifiable ALH manifolds of constant scalar curvature with a boundary of genus
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}}
with vanishing mean curvature, with a conformal boundary at infinity of genus
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}
, and with mass of any prescribed sign.

The condition of constant scalar curvature corresponds to vacuum general relativistic time-symmetric initial data sets; an identical construction can be done for initial data sets for time-symmetric data sets with prescribed energy density.

We note that boundaries with vanishing mean curvature typically lie inside, or at the boundary, of the intersection of a black hole region with a time-symmetric initial-data slice.

The restriction
{{\mathbf{g}}_{\infty}}\geq{{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{BH}}}
is necessary, cf.
[
7
]
.

Theorem
1.1
is a slightly less precise version of Corollary
2.3

The question of controlling the mass when gluing-at-infinity two manifolds across a single neck, with one manifold having spherical topology at infinity and the other not, remains to be settled.

2.1
)–(
2.6
) that the metric

has constant scalar curvature.

The Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives

\int_{\partial M}{\rm e}^{u_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{{\hat{h}_{i}}}=2\pi\big(2-\chi(\partial M_{1})-\chi(\partial M_{2})\big),

where
{\chi}\big(\mathbf{M}^{2}\big)
denotes the Euler characteristic of a two-dimensional manifold
\mathbf{M}^{2}
.

For
0<a<b
let

\Gamma(a,b):=D(b)\setminus\overline{D(a)},

where
D(c)
denotes an open disc of radius
c
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
By construction,
there exists a function
{\hat{u}_{i}}
defined
on

\Gamma_{i}:=\Gamma\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2},\rho_{0}\big)

so that there we have

{\hat{h}_{i}}={\rm e}^{{\hat{u}_{i}}}h_{0}.

Then the metric

{\rm e}^{u_{i}+{\hat{u}_{i}}}h_{0},

defined on
\Gamma_{i}
, has scalar curvature equal to minus two.
Since
h_{0}
is flat, the function

\hat{\omega}_{i}:=u_{i}+{\hat{u}_{i}}

satisfies on
\Gamma_{i}
the equation

\Delta_{h_{0}}\hat{\omega}_{i}=2{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}.

(2.13)

When
\kappa_{1}<0
there exist on
M_{1}\setminus D\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2}\big)
two metrics of negative scalar curvature conformal to each other, namely the metric
{\rm e}^{u_{i}}{\hat{h}_{i}}
and the original metric
h_{\kappa_{1}}
. We write

{\rm e}^{u_{i}}{\hat{h}_{i}}={\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}h_{\kappa_{1}}.

(2.14)

The functions
\omega_{i}
are solutions of the equation

\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\omega_{i}=2{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}+2\kappa_{1}=2{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}-2.

The maximum principle shows that
\omega_{i}
has neither a positive
interior maximum nor a negative interior minimum on the compact manifold with boundary
\partial M_{1}\setminus D(a)
for
a\in\big[\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2},\rho_{0}\big]
.

When
\kappa_{1}=0

we write again (
2.14
), except that now we have

\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\omega_{i}=2{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}.

The maximum principle ensures then the property, that
\omega_{i}
has no interior maximum on the compact manifold with boundary
\partial M_{1}\setminus D(a)
for
a\in\big[\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2},\rho_{0}\big]
.

It also follows from (
2.7
) that on the annulus
D(\rho_{0})\setminus\overline{D\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2}\big)}

we can rewrite (
2.14
) as

{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}h_{0}={\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}h_{\kappa_{1}}={\rm e}^{\omega_{i}+\mathring{\omega}_{1}}h_{0}.

We will need the property (cf., e.g.,
[
14
]
) that solutions of the equation

\Delta\omega=2{\rm e}^{\omega}+\varpi,

where
\varpi
is a function independent of
\omega
(in the cases of interest here
\varpi\equiv 0
or
\varpi\equiv-2

)),
satisfy a comparison principle: given a conditionally compact domain with boundary:

\hat{\omega}>\omega\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega\ \Longrightarrow\ \hat{\omega}>\omega\quad\text{on}\ \Omega.

The following metric, which has constant negative scalar curvature equal to
-2
, provides a useful comparison function:

{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}h_{0}:=\Bigg(\frac{\pi}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})\sin\Big(\pi\frac{\log(\rho/\rho_{0})}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})}\Big)\rho}\Bigg)^{2}\big({\rm d}\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}{\rm d}\varphi^{2}\big),\qquad\rho\in\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{0}i^{2}},\rho_{0}\right),

(2.15)

The conformal factor
{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}
tends to infinity at
\rho=\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2}
and at
\rho=\rho_{0}
.
In the coordinates
(\hat{\rho},\varphi)
the metric (
2.15
) reads

{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}h_{0}=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\cos(\pi\hat{\rho}/2)}\right)^{2}\left({\rm d}\hat{\rho}^{2}+\frac{1}{\log^{2}(\rho_{0}i)}{\rm d}\varphi^{2}\right),\qquad\hat{\rho}\in(-1,1).

(2.16)

Note that the circle
\hat{\rho}=0
is a closed geodesic minimising length for the metric (
2.16
), of length
\pi^{2}/\log(\rho_{0}i)
.

Since the function
{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}
, defined in (
2.15
), tends to infinity as the boundary of
\Gamma_{i}
is approached, the comparison principle gives:

Lemma 2.6
.

On
D(\rho_{0})\setminus\overline{D\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2}\big)}
it holds that

{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}\leq{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}.

(2.17)

Remark 2.7
.

On the circle
\rho=1/i
the function

{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}
tends to infinity as

, but the metric length
\ell_{i}
of

equals

\ell_{i}=\frac{1}{i}\int_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}\big({\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}/2}\big)_{|\rho=1/i}\,{\rm d}\varphi\leq\frac{1}{i}\int_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}\big({\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}/2}\big)_{|\rho=1/i}\,{\rm d}\varphi=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\log{(\rho_{0}i)}},

so that
\ell_{i}
approaches zero as
i
tends to infinity.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any
\rho_{1}\in\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2},\rho_{0}\big)
,
there exists a constant
\hat{c}=\hat{c}(\rho_{1})
such that

\omega_{i}\leq\hat{c}

on
\partial M_{1}\setminus D(\rho_{1})
, independently of
i
.

Proof.

At
\rho=\rho_{1}
we have

{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}\leq{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}=\Bigg(\frac{\pi}{\log\big(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2}\big)\sin\Big(\pi\frac{\log(\rho/\rho_{0})}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})}\Big)\rho}\Bigg)^{2}\longrightarrow_{i\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{1}^{2}\log^{2}(\rho_{1}/\rho_{0})}.

This shows that
the
\omega_{i}
’s are bounded by a constant
\hat{c}(\rho_{1})>0
independently of
i
on
S(\rho_{1})
for
\rho_{1}\in\big(\rho_{0}^{-1}i^{-2},\rho_{0}\big)
.
The result follows now from the maximum principle.
∎

The corollary gives an estimation of the conformal factors from above. In order to prove convergence of the sequence
\omega_{i}
away from the puncture, we also need to bound the sequence of conformal factors away from zero. As a tool towards this we
consider the sequence of “half-areas”:

0<A_{1,i}:=\int_{{\hat{M}}_{1,i}}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}=\int_{{\hat{M}}_{1,i}}{\rm e}^{u_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{{\hat{h}_{i}}}<\int_{\partial M}{\rm e}^{u_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{{\hat{h}_{i}}}=2\pi\big(2-\chi(\partial M_{1})-\chi(\partial M_{2})\big).

Thus the sequence
\{A_{1,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, and so passing to a subsequence
\{i_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, if necessary, we can assume that the limit exists:

A_{1}:=\lim_{j\to\infty}A_{1,i_{j}}.

Using analogous definitions on
\partial M_{2}
, we have

A_{1,i}+A_{2,i}=2\pi\big(2-\chi(\partial M_{1})-\chi(\partial M_{2})\big)\ \Longrightarrow\ A_{2}=2\pi\big(2-\chi(\partial M_{1})-\chi(\partial M_{2})\big)-A_{1}.

It follows that at least one of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
is not zero. Exchanging
M_{1}
with
M_{2}
, we can without loss of generality assume that

A_{1}\neq 0.

(2.18)

In our next result the parameter
{\varepsilon}>0
should not be confused with the parameter
\epsilon
introduced by the exotic gluing of
M_{1}
with
M_{2}
:

Lemma 2.9
.

Assuming (
2.18
), there exist constants
C_{1}
and

such that, for all
{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small,

\limsup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\inf_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\omega_{i_{j}}\Big)\leq C_{1},\qquad C_{2}\leq\liminf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\sup_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\omega_{i_{j}}\Big).

In other words, if
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small, then for all

sufficiently large we have

\inf_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\omega_{i_{j}}\leq C_{3},\qquad C_{4}\leq\sup_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\omega_{i_{j}},

(2.19)

for some constants
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
.

Proof.

It holds

\int_{\Gamma(a,b)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}=-\frac{2\pi^{2}\cot\Big(\frac{\pi\log(\rho/\rho_{0})}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})}\Big)}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})}\Bigg|^{b}_{a},

and note that

\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}=\frac{2\pi^{2}\cot\Big(\frac{\pi\log(2/(\rho_{0}{\varepsilon}))}{\log(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2})}\Big)}{\log\big(\rho_{0}^{2}i^{2}\big)}\to_{i\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{\log\big(2/(\rho_{0}{\varepsilon})\big)}\to_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}0.

(2.20)

We have, for all
\epsilon<2\rho_{0}
and for all

larger than
2/{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle A_{1,i}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\hat{M}}_{1,i}}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}=\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}+\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}+\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}.

(2.21)

The estimate (

) shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}\leq\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}.

It follows from (
2.20
) that there exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}
such that for all
2/{i}<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
the last term in (

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}A_{1,i}\leq\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}<A_{1,i}.

The conclusion readily follows from

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\inf\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\leq\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\leq\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\sup\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}.

∎

We are ready now to prove the equivalent of Lemma 5.9 of

Lemma 2.10
.

Under (
2.18
), there exists a smooth function

\omega_{\infty}\colon\ \partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}\to\mathbb{R}

such that a subsequence of
\{\omega_{i_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to
\omega_{\infty}
on every compact subset of
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
.
Similarly derivatives of any order of
\omega_{i_{j}}
converge to derivatives of
\omega_{\infty}
, uniformly on every compact subset of
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation to our setting of the arguments given in
[
6
]
, we present here the details for completeness.

We will need a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}\big(M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}\big)
which satisfies the equation

\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\psi=-2+c\delta_{p_{1}},

(2.22)

where
\delta_{p_{1}}
is the Dirac measure centered at
p_{1}
, with
c
equal to twice the
h_{\kappa_{1}}
-area of
\partial M_{1}
. This choice of
c
ensures existence of
\psi
, which can be seen as follows:
Let
(\rho,\varphi)
be any coordinates near
p_{1}
such that
h_{\kappa_{1}}={\rm e}^{\omega_{1}}\big({\rm d}\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}{\rm d}\varphi^{2}\big)
there. Let
\psi_{1}\in C^{\infty}\big(\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}\big)
be any function which equals
\ln\rho
near
p_{1}
. There exists a constant
c_{1}\neq 0
such
that

f:=\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\psi_{1}-c_{1}\delta_{p_{1}}\in C^{\infty}(\partial M_{1}).

Letting
\langle T,f\rangle
denote the action of a distribution
T
on a smooth function
f
, we have

0=\big\langle\psi_{1},\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}1\big\rangle=\big\langle\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\psi_{1},1\big\rangle=\int_{\partial M_{1}}f\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}+c_{1},

where
d\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}
is the measure associated with the metric
{h_{\kappa_{1}}}
.
Hence

\int_{\partial M_{1}}f\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}=-c_{1}.

Consider the equation

\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\psi_{2}=-2-\frac{c}{c_{1}}f.

By choice of
c
the right-hand side has zero-average over
\partial M_{1}
, which guarantees existence of a smooth function
\psi_{2}
solving the equation. Then

\psi:=\frac{c}{c_{1}}\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}

solves (
2.22
).

To continue, as in
[
6
]
we let
K
be any compact subset of
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
. There exists
\rho_{K}>0
such that
K\subset\partial M_{1}\setminus D(\rho_{K})
. It thus suffices to prove the result with
K=\partial M_{1}\setminus D(\rho_{K})
, which will be assumed from now on.

Let
K_{1}=\partial M_{1}\setminus D(\rho_{K}/2)
. Taking
{\varepsilon}=\rho_{K}
in Lemma
2.9
ensures that (
2.19
) holds on
K_{1}
for all
i\geq i_{1}
for some
i_{1}<\infty
.

Let
i\geq i_{1}
. By Corollary
2.8
,
there exists a constant
c_{1}
, independent of
i
, such that

v_{i}:=\psi-\omega_{i}\geq c_{1}\quad\text{on}\ K_{1}.

On
K_{1}
it also holds

c_{2}\leq\psi\leq c_{3},

(2.23)

for some constants
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
.
Define

\hat{v}_{i}:=v_{i}-c_{1}+1.

It holds that
\hat{v}_{i}\geq 1
on
K_{1}
.

Moreover,
\hat{v}_{i}
satisfies the equation

\Delta_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\hat{v}_{i}=\psi_{i}\hat{v}_{i},

where

0\geq\psi_{i}:=-2\frac{{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}}{\hat{v}_{i}}=-2\frac{{\rm e}^{\psi}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}-\psi}}{\hat{v}_{i}}\geq-2{\rm e}^{c_{3}}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}-\psi}\geq-2{\rm e}^{c_{3}-c_{1}}.

By Harnack’s inequality, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(K,K_{1})>0
such that on
K
we have

\sup_{K}\hat{v}_{i}\leq C_{1}\inf_{K_{1}}\hat{v}_{i}.

This, together with the definition of
\hat{v}_{i}
, shows that

\sup_{K}v_{i}\leq C_{1}\inf_{K_{1}}v_{i}+d_{1}=C_{1}\big({-}\sup_{K_{1}}({\omega_{i}}-\psi)\big)+d_{1},

(2.24)

for some constant
d_{1}
.
Equation (
2.19
) shows that there exists a constant
c_{4}
such that

-\sup_{K_{1}}(\omega_{i}-\psi)\leq c_{4}.

From (
2.24
) we obtain

\sup_{K}(v_{i}-\psi)\leq C_{1}c_{4}+d_{1}\ \Longrightarrow\ \inf_{K}\omega_{i}=-\sup_{K}(v_{i}-\psi)\geq-(C_{1}c_{4}+d_{1}).

This, together with (
2.23
), shows that
that for every compact subset
K
of
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}

there exists a constant
\hat{C}_{K}
such that

-\hat{C}_{K}\leq\omega_{i}\leq\hat{C}_{K}.

Elliptic estimates, together with a standard diagonalisation argument, show that there exists a subsequence
\omega_{i_{j}}
which
converges uniformly on every compact subset of
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
to a solution
\omega_{\infty}

of (
2.13
) on
\partial M_{1}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
. Convergence of derivatives follows again from elliptic estimates.
∎

To continue, we wish to show that
A_{2}\neq 0
. As a step towards this we claim that

A_{1}=\int_{\partial M_{1}}{\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}.

(2.25)

In order to prove (
2.25
), for any
{\varepsilon}>0
we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M_{1}}{\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}+\int_{D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\!\!({\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}-{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}})\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\!+\underbrace{\int_{\partial M_{1}\setminus D({\varepsilon}/2)}\!\!{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}\!+\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}\!\!{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}}_{=A_{1,i}}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}+\int_{D({\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{\infty}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}.

Recall that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{\kappa_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\hat{\omega}_{i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma(1/i,{\varepsilon}/2)}{\rm e}^{\omega_{*,i}}\,{\rm d}\mu_{h_{0}}\to_{i\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{\log(2/(\rho_{0}{\varepsilon}))}\to_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}0.

Let

\eta/4
for all

. Equation (

3
Instabilities?

We start with the following observation:
Consider a pair of two-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds
\big(\mathbf{M}^{2}_{a},h_{a}\big)
,
a=1,2
with a boundary (at finite distance)
\partial\mathbf{M}^{2}_{a}
with zero-mean curvature. Thus, the boundaries are closed curves which satisfy the geodesic equation. Suppose that the lengths of the boundary curves coincide.
Any two such manifolds can be identified at that boundary to yield a hyperbolic manifold in which the boundary curves become a closed geodesic.

The above allows us to provide a
construction kit
for producing nontrivial manifolds with constant scalar curvature, higher-genus topology at infinity, and mass which is additive under Maskit gluing.

The simplest collection of the relevant building blocks is obtained as follows: Let
(M,g)
be any ALH manifold with genus
\mathbf{g}_{\infty}\geq 1
. Let us denote by
\partial_{\infty}M
the conformal boundary at infinity of
M
and let
p\in\partial_{\infty}M
. We can carry out the construction of the proof of Theorem
2.1
by a gluing-at-infinity of a copy of
(M,g)
to itself in a symmetric way, as in
[
6
]
,
and where the discrete parameter
1/i\to 0
is replaced by a continuous parameter
\lambda\to 0
. There results a family of constant-scalar-curvature manifolds
(M_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})
which are exactly hyperbolic in a neighborhood of a
totally geodesic two-dimensional half-sphere
\partial{\mycal U}_{\lambda}

cutting
(M_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})
in half
(in the example of Figure
1
this is the half-sphere
\partial{\mycal U}_{1,\epsilon}
with
\epsilon=\lambda
). The resulting cut-in-half manifolds will be referred to as
building blocks
.
The half-spheres
\partial{\mycal U}_{\lambda}
have a boundary at infinity which is a closed geodesic
\gamma_{\lambda}
cutting
\partial_{\infty}M_{\lambda}

in half, with the length
\ell_{\lambda}
of
\gamma_{\lambda}
varying continuously with
\lambda
by
[
6
, Remark 5.11]
, and with
\ell_{\lambda}
tending to zero as
\lambda
does.

Any building blocks with matching lengths of their closed geodesics at infinity can be joined together at the boundaries
\partial{\mycal U}_{\lambda}
to obtain a CSC ALH manifold with compact negatively curved conformal boundary. It should be clear from (
2.5
) that the mass of the manifold so obtained
is the sum of the masses of the building blocks. Depending upon the sign of the mass of the second summand the new manifold can have a mass larger or smaller than that of the first summand.

The fact that a summand with positive mass can be replaced by one with negative one, thus lowering the mass of the connected manifold without changing the geometry of the other summand away from a small subset of its exactly hyperbolic region, suggests instability under time evolution:

Conjecture 3.1
.

Three-dimensional CSC ALH manifolds with higher genus topology at conformal infinity and thin necks at conformal infinity are unstable.

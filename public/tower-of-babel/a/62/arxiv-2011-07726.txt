Multi-point distribution of discrete time periodic TASEP
Yuchen Liao

Abstract
. We study the one-dimensional discrete time totally asymmetric simple exclusion process with parallel update rules on a spatially periodic domain. A multi-point space-time joint distribution formula is obtained for general initial conditions. The formula involves contour integrals of Fredholm determinants with kernels acting on certain discrete spaces. For a class of initial conditions satisfying certain technical assumptions, we are able to derive large-time, large-period limit of the joint distribution, under the relaxation time scale t = O ⁡ ( L 3 / 2 ) t=O(L^{3/2}) when the height fluctuations are critically affected by the finite geometry. The assumptions are verified for the step and flat initial conditions. As a corollary we obtain the multi-point distribution of discrete time TASEP on the whole integer lattice ℤ \mathbb{Z} by taking the period L L large enough so that the finite-time distribution is not affected by the boundary. The large time limit for the multi-time distribution of discrete time TASEP on ℤ \mathbb{Z} is then obtained for the step initial condition.



1.
Introduction

Models in the one-dimensional Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class are expected to have the same limiting height fluctuations under the
t:t^{2/3}:t^{1/3}
KPZ scaling for temporal correlations, spatial correlations and height fluctuations. Describing the universal limiting fluctuating field and proving the convergence of concrete models to the universal limiting field have been the main goals in the field and attracted active research over the last two decades. The convergence to the limiting fluctuating field (at one-point or multi-point level) have been obtained for a large class of models (mostly exactly solvable), either for the whole space models
[
2
,
11
,
8
,
9
,
20
,
21
,
16
,
10
,
13
,
15
]
, or for half space models
[
1
,
6
,
7
]
.

More recently similar limit theorems were obtained for models on periodic domains. Such models are defined on a finite ring of size
L
instead of on the infinite or semi-infinite lattices. The crucial interest here is to understand the effect of this finite geometry on the height fluctuations. For periodic models the one-point marginals of limiting height fluctuations were obtained for classical step, flat and stationary initial conditions in
[
3
,
14
]
, and independently in
[
19
]
under the so-called relaxation time scale
t=O(L^{3/2})
when the height fluctuations are critically affected by the finite geometry. Further extensions including formulas for multi-point space-time joint distributions were subsequently obtained in
[
4
]
for step initial condition and
[
5
]
for more general initial conditions. These limiting formulas are expected to be universal for all related models on periodic domains. So far all limiting distribution formulas were obtained as scaling limits of finite-time joint distributions of a single model in the KPZ universality class, namely the continuous time periodic totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP on a ring).

The goal of this paper is to study another classical model in the KPZ universality class, the discrete time totally asymmetric exclusion process with parallel updates, on periodic domains. On the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, this model has been well studied. The one-point marginal distribution was obtained in
[
11
]
for the equivalent geometric last passage percolation model and joint distributions of several locations at equal time was obtained in
[
9
]
. Recently the joint distributions along the time direction have also been studied, in
[
12
]
for two-time case and
[
13
]
for general multi-time joint distributions. However on the periodic domain there are fewer results concerning height fluctuations (see
[
18
]
for some results on transition probability and stationary distributions), which are the main focuses of this paper. The main results of this paper are summarized as follows:

(1)

For general initial conditions we obtain a finite-time multi-point (in both space and time) joint distribution formula for discrete time parallel periodic TASEP. The formula consists of an
m
-fold (
m
is the number of space-time points being considered in the joint distribution) contour integral with integrand involving a Fredholm determinant, where the Fredholm determinant has kernels acting on certain discrete sets related to the roots of some polynomials.

(2)

Under the relaxation time scale
t=O(L^{3/2})
and the
1:2:3
KPZ scaling, we obtain large-time, large-period limits for the multi-point joint distributions under certain assumptions on the initial condition, which are verified for the step and flat cases. These limiting formulas agree with those obtained in
[
4
,
5
]
, thus providing an evidence that the height fluctuations for periodic models in the KPZ class are in fact universal.

(3)

We also obtain the same type of multi-point joint distribution formula for discrete time TASEP on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, by relating it with the corresponding periodic model with large enough period so that the finite-time distribution is not affected by the boundary. The large time limit for the multi-time distribution formula under KPZ scaling is then derived for step initial condition, which agrees with the one in
[
15
]
.

Comparing to the previous work
[
4
,
5
]
and
[
15
]
on the multi-point distributions of continuous time TASEP, on either periodic domain or
\mathbb{Z}
, our work consists of formulas with similar structures but involves an extra parameter
p
describing the hopping probability, which makes the algebraic properties a bit more complicated. In particular the polynomial whose roots are related to the kernels in the periodic formulas now depends on the extra parameter
p
. We point out that all the formulas for continuous time TASEP can be obtained from our formulas by rescaling the time and taking
p\to 0
.

On the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, the multi-time distribution of discrete time TASEP (more precisely the equivalent geometric last passage percolation model) has been considered recently in
[
13
]
, where a formula involving contour integrals of Fredholm determinants was obtained. We expect our formulas to agree with the formulas in
[
13
]
, both in finite time and for the large time limit. Currently we are not able to prove the equivalence of the two types of formulas though they share a lot of similarities.

Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we describe the discrete time parallel TASEP models and state the main results involving several multi-point joint distribution formulas, for both periodic domain and infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, finite time and large time limit. From Sections
3

we derive the main finite-time algebraic formulas for multi-point distribution of discrete time parallel periodic TASEP and we regard them as the main technical novelties in this paper. In particular in Section
3
we prove a novel transition probability formula for discrete time parallel TASEP on the periodic domain. The formula involves integral of determinants and is proved using coordinate Bethe ansatz. In Section
4

The key ingredients are certain Cauchy-type summation identities over the eigenfunctions of the generator, which might be of independent interests so we discuss the proof in Section
5
. In Section
6
and Section
7
we discuss the large time, large period asymptotics for the multi-point distribution under the relaxation time scale
t=O(L^{3/2})
. Finally in Section
8
we derive the multi-time distribution for infinite discrete time parallel TASEP and perform large time asymptotics under KPZ scaling for the step initial condition.

2.
Models and main results

Let
N<L
be positive integers. We consider discrete time TASEP with parallel updates with
N
particles on a spatially periodic domain of size
L
. It is convenient to view the dynamics as particles moving to the right on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}
while periodicity forces particle configurations to be identical copies of each other every
L
sites. More precisely this means that the occupation functions
\eta_{j}(t)
which equals
1
if there is a particle at site
j\in\mathbb{Z}
at time
t
and equals
0
otherwise should satisfy

(2.1)

\eta_{j}(t)=\eta_{j+kL}(t),\quad\text{for all }j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{N}.

We fix a single period of size
L
and denote the locations of totally
N
particles in this period at time
t
as

x_{1}(t)>x_{2}(t)>\cdots>x_{N}(t).

Here
x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}
for
1\leq i\leq N
and we index the particles from right to left. The locations of all the particles then satisfy
x_{i+kN}(t)=x_{i}(t)-kL
for
1\leq i\leq N
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
so that we have

\cdots>x_{N}(t)+L=x_{0}(t)>x_{1}(t)>x_{2}(t)>\cdots>x_{N}(t)>x_{N+1}(t)=x_{1}(t)-L>\cdots.

Thus the natural configuration space for the particles should be

(2.2)

\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}:=\{\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:x_{N}+L>x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{N}\}.

The discrete time parallel periodic TASEP with
N
particles, period
L
and hopping probability
0<p<1
, which we denote by
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,p)
, is the following Markovian dynamics on particle configurations
\vec{x}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
: at each time step, each particle in a single period hops to its right neighbor site independently with probability
p=1-q
provided that the site is empty, otherwise it stays at its current position. As a special case, the discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
which we will denote by
\mathrm{dTASEP}(p)
corresponds to particles following the same evolution rules with the period
L\to\infty
. In this case the configuration space for the first
N
particles (from right to left, we assume the existence of a right-most particle) becomes

(2.3)

\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(\infty)}:=\{\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{N}\}.

Notation 2.1
.

Throughout the paper there will be several very similar formulas and quantities corresponding to either discrete time parallel TASEP on a periodic domain with size
L
or on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, as well as their scaling limits. To avoid confusion we will always follow the convention used in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) by putting superscripts
(L)
to any quantities related to periodic model in finite-time and
(\infty)
to any quantities related to model on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
in finite-time. On the other hand quantities describing the large-time limits will have superscripts
(\mathrm{per})
and
(\mathrm{kpz})
for periodic and infinite models, respectively.

The main results of the paper consist of several formulas for multi-point joint distributions of discrete time parallel TASEP on periodic domain and on
\mathbb{Z}
as well as their large time scaling limits. All of them are expressed as contour integrals of Fredholm determinants. The main difference is that the formulas for periodic models are expressed in terms of discrete sets related to roots of certain algebraic equations, while the Fredholm determinant appearing in formulas for discrete time TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
has kernels acting on continuous contours.

2.1.
Multi-point distribution formulas for
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,p)

The first theorem is a finite-time multi-point joint distribution formula for discrete time TASEP on a periodic domain. This is the starting point of all other results in this paper.

Theorem 2.2

(Finite-time multi-point joint distribution for
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,p,\vec{y})
)
.

Let
N<L
be integers. Consider discrete time periodic parallel TASEP with hopping probability
0<p<1
,
N
particles and
L
sites per period (denoted by
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,p,\vec{y})
). Here
\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
is the initial condition, i.e.,
x_{i}(0)=y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. Set
\rho=N/L
.
Fix a positive integer
m
and let
(k_{i},t_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq m
be
m
distinct points in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
0\leq t_{1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}
. Then for any integers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
,

(2.4)

\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\}\right)=\oint\cdots\oint\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{m}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{m}}.

Hre we denote
\vec{z}:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
. The contours are over nested circles centered at the origin:
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, where
\mathbbm{r}_{c}
is a parameter depending on
p
and
\rho
that is defined in (
2.16
). Here and in all the remaining results we suppress the dependence of the integrand on the parameters
a_{i},k_{i},t_{i}
as well as the hopping probability
0<p<1
. The function
\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
is defined in Definition
2.14
. The function
\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
is a Fredholm determinant

(2.5)

\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})=\det(I-\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}),

where the operators
\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}
and
\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}
are defined in Definition
2.15
.

Remark 2.3
.

Theorem
2.2
generalizes Theorem 3.1 of
[
5
]
(and also Theorem 4.6 of
[
4
]
for the special step initial condition) on the finite-time multi-point distribution of continuous time periodic TASEP. In fact their formulas can be obtained from our formula (
2.4
) by taking
p=\epsilon
,
\hat{t}=\epsilon t
and letting
\epsilon\to 0
.

Next we state the theorem on the large-time, large-period scaling limit of (
2.4
) under the relaxation time scale
t=O(L^{3/2})
. To emphasize the dependence on the initial condition, we add the subscript “
\mathrm{ic}
” for the terms in the limit which depend on the initial conditions. For convenience we make the following choice of labelling: we assume that
x_{1}(0)\leq 0<x_{0}(0)
. This is equivalent with assuming that the initial condition satisfies
y_{1}\leq 0<y_{N}+L
.

Theorem 2.4

(Relaxation time limit)
.

Consider a sequence of Markovian dynamics
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,\vec{y}(L))
depending on the period
L
, where
\rho=\rho_{L}=N/L
stays in a compact subset of
(0,1)
and
y_{1}\leq 0<y_{N}+L
.
Suppose that the sequence of initial conditions
\vec{y}(L)
satisfies certain assumptions (see Assumption
6.2
).
Fix a positive integer
m
and let
\mathrm{p}_{j}=(\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})
be
m
points in the region

\mathrm{R}:=[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}

satisfying

0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\cdots<\tau_{m}.

Then for every fixed
\mathrm{x}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{x}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
and parameters
a_{i},k_{i},t_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
given by

(2.6)

t_{i}=c_{1}\tau_{i}L^{3/2}+O(1),\quad a_{i}=c_{2}t_{i}+\gamma_{i}L+O(1),\quad k_{i}=c_{3}t_{i}+c_{4}\gamma_{i}L+c_{5}\mathrm{x}_{i}L^{1/2}+O(1),

we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{(L)}_{\vec{y}}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\right\}\right)=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{ic}}^{\mathrm{per}}(\mathrm{x}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{x}_{m};\mathrm{p}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{p}_{m}).

Here the constants
c_{i}
depend explicitly on particle density
0<\rho<1
and hopping probability
0<p<1
and are given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\frac{\nu^{5/2}}{\rho^{1/2}(1-\rho)^{1/2}},\quad c_{2}=\frac{p(1-2\rho)}{\nu},

\displaystyle c_{3}=\frac{2\rho^{2}\cdot p(1-p)}{\nu(1+\nu-2p\rho)},\quad c_{4}=-\rho,\quad c_{5}=-\rho^{1/2}(1-\rho)^{1/2}\nu^{1/2},

where we set
\nu:=\sqrt{1-4p\cdot\rho(1-\rho)}
for convenience. We recall that in (
2.7
)
\mathbb{P}^{(L)}
denotes the probability associated to
\mathrm{dpTASEP}(L,N,\vec{y}(L))
. The function
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{ic}}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot)
agrees with the one defined in Section 6.4 of
[
5
]
as the relaxation time limit of multi-point distribution of continuous time periodic TASEP. We recall the definition of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{ic}}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot)
in Section
6.3
for completeness. The convergence is locally uniform in
\mathrm{x}_{j},\tau_{j}
, and
\gamma_{j}
.

2.2.
Multi-point distribution formulas for
\mathrm{dTASEP}(p)

The finite-time multi-point joint distribution for discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
can be obtained from Theorem
2.2
as a corollary. The rough idea is to take the period
L
sufficiently large so that the finite-time formula is unaffected by the boundary. The precise procedure is more delicate and will be explained in Section
8
. For simplicity we will only state result on step initial condition but we point out that in
[
15
]
similar multi-time formulas for continuous time TASEP was obtained for general initial conditions and we believe their arguments can also be adapted to our model. The main result is the following:

Theorem 2.5

(Finite-time multi-point joint distribution for
\mathrm{dTASEP}(p)
)
.

Assume
\vec{y}=(-1,-2,\cdots,-N)
. Consider discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
with initial particle locations
x_{i}(0)=y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. Let
m\geq 1
be a positive integer and
(k_{1},t_{1}),\cdots,(k_{m},t_{m})
be
m
distinct points in
\{1,\cdots,N\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Assume that
0\leq t_{1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}
. Then, for any integers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
,

(2.9)

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)=\oint\cdots\oint\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}\right]\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\theta_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\theta_{m-1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{m-1}}

where the integral contours are circles centered at the origin of radii less than
1
. The function
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
is defined in terms of a Fredholm determinant in Definition
2.16
.

Remark 2.6
.

Under the standard coupling between discrete time parallel TASEP and geometric last passage percolation, we have the following equality in distributions:

(2.10)

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{GLPP}}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{G(k_{\ell},a_{\ell}+k_{\ell})\leq t_{\ell}\right\}\right),

where
G(m,n)
is the point-to-point last passage time from
(0,0)
to
(m,n)
in the usual geometric last passage percolation model with parameter
q=1-p
. For the right-hand side of (
2.10
) a formula of similar form as (
2.9
) was obtained in Theorem 2 of
[
13
]
. We expect the two formulas to be equivalent but currently we are not able to prove it for
m\geq 2
.

Starting from the finite-time formula, we can then take large-time limit under the
1:2:3
KPZ scaling.

Theorem 2.7
.

Consider discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
with step initial condition
x_{i}(0)=-i
. We rescale the parameters such that

(2.11)

t_{i}=\frac{2q^{1/4}}{1-q}\tau_{i}T+O(1),\quad a=\gamma_{i}T^{2/3}+O(1),\quad k_{i}=\frac{q^{1/4}}{1+\sqrt{q}}\tau_{i}T-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{i}T^{2/3}-\frac{q^{1/4}}{2}\mathrm{x}_{i}T^{1/3}+O(1),

where
q=1-p
. Then

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\mathrm{x}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{x}_{m};(\tau_{1},\gamma_{1}),\cdots,(\tau_{m},\gamma_{m}))

\displaystyle=\oint\cdots\oint\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}\right]\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\theta_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\theta_{m-1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{m-1}}.

Here
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
is a Fredholm determinant defined in Definition
8.4
. We remark that the right-hand side of (
2.12
) agrees with the right-hand side of equation (2.19) in
[
15
]
, with the re-scaled parameters changed from
(\tau_{i},\gamma_{i},\mathrm{x}_{i})
to
(\tau_{i},2x_{i},h_{i})
. Our parameters are chosen to be consistent with the periodic cases.

2.3.
Bethe equations and Bethe roots

For our analysis on the discrete time periodic TASEP, the following polynomial and its roots play essential role:

Definition 2.8

(Bethe roots)
.

Given
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
0<p<1
. Define the degree
L
polynomial
q_{z}(w)
by

(2.13)

q_{z}(w):=w^{N}(1+w)^{L-N}-z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}.

We call this polynomial the Bethe polynomial associated to
z
and its roots the Bethe roots. We denote the set of all roots of the Bethe polynomial
q_{z}(w)
by
\mathcal{S}_{z}
:

(2.14)

\mathcal{S}_{z}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:q_{z}(w)=0\}.

The Bethe root set
\mathcal{S}_{z}
is contained in the level set
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|^{\rho}|1+w|^{1-\rho}=|z|\cdot|1+pw|^{\rho}\}
, which is sometimes called a deformed Cassini oval. For given jumping rate
p
and density
\rho
, there exists a critical radius
|z|=\mathbbm{r}_{c}
for the level sets. For such critical
z
, the level set contains a self-intersecting point at
w=w_{c}
. Here

(2.15)

w_{c}:=-\frac{2\rho}{1+\sqrt{1-4p\cdot\rho(1-\rho)}},

and

(2.16)

\mathbbm{r}_{c}:=\left(\frac{-w_{c}}{1+pw_{c}}\right)^{\rho}(1+w_{c})^{1-\rho}.

It is not hard to check that for
|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, the level set consists of two disjoint contours while for
|z|>\mathbbm{r}_{c}
the two contours merge to a single contour. For
z=\mathbbm{r}_{c}
there is a self-intersecting point for the contour at
w=w_{c}
. See Figure
1
for an illustration. We remark that the Bethe polynomials (and their roots) we are considering here are one-parameter generalizations of those considered in
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
which is related to continuous time periodic TASEP and corresponds to
p=0
degenerations of (
2.13
).

Figure 1.
The solid dots are roots of
q_{z}(w)
with
L=16
,
N=4
,
p=5/6
and
|z|=\frac{11}{10}\mathbbm{r}_{c},\mathbbm{r}_{c}\text{ and }\frac{9}{10}\mathbbm{r}_{c}
from outside to inside. The dashed lines are the corresponding level sets.

Definition 2.9

(Left and right Bethe roots)
.

For
|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, we define the sets

(2.17)

\mathcal{L}_{z}:=\{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}:\text{Re}(w)<w_{c}\},\quad\mathcal{R}_{z}:=\{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}:\text{Re}(w)>w_{c}\},

where
w_{c}
and
\mathbbm{r}_{c}
are defined in (
2.15
) and (
2.16
).
Then it is straightforward to check that
|\mathcal{L}_{z}|=L-N
and
|\mathcal{R}_{z}|=N
. Roots in
\mathcal{L}_{z}
and
\mathcal{R}_{z}
are called left and right Bethe roots, respectively. We also define the left and right Bethe polynomials
q_{z,\mathrm{L}}(w)
and
q_{z,\mathrm{R}}(w)
as the monic polynomials with roots in
\mathcal{L}_{z}
and
\mathcal{R}_{z}
:

(2.18)

q_{z,\mathrm{L}}(w):=\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}}(w-u),\quad q_{z,\mathrm{R}}(w):=\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}}(w-v).

Then by definition we have

\mathcal{S}_{z}=\mathcal{L}_{z}\cup\mathcal{R}_{z},\quad q_{z}(w)=q_{z,\mathrm{L}}(w)q_{z,\mathrm{R}}(w).

2.4.
A symmetric function related to initial conditions

In our finite-time multi-point distribution formula (
2.4
), the quantities encoding information in the initial condition are all related to the following symmetric function:

Definition 2.10

(Symmetric function)
.

Given
p\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}
. We define

(2.19)

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(w_{1},\cdots,w_{N};p):=\frac{\det\left[w_{j}^{N-i}(pw_{j}+1)^{i-1}(w_{j}+1)^{\lambda_{i}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}}{\det[w_{j}^{N-i}]_{i,j=1}^{N}}.

Remark 2.11
.

The symmetric function
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
defined here is a one-parameter generalization of the Grothendieck-like symmetric function defined in equation (3.6) of
[
5
]
which corresponds to the
p=0
degeneration in our situation.

The following two quantities related to
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
encode the initial condition:

Definition 2.12

(Global energy and characteristic function)
.

For
\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
, we set

(2.20)

\lambda(\vec{y})=(y_{1}+1,y_{2}+2,\cdots,y_{N}+N).

For
|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, we define the global energy
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)
by

(2.21)

\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z):=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda(\vec{y})}(\mathcal{R}_{z};p).

When
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)\neq 0
, we define the characteristic function
\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z)
for a left Bethe root
u
and a right Bethe root
v
by

(2.22)

\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z):=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda(\vec{y})}(\mathcal{R}_{z}\cup\{u\}\backslash\{v\};p)}{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda(\vec{y})}(\mathcal{R}_{z};p)},\quad\text{for }u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}\text{ and }v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}.

Remark 2.13
.

A straightforward calculation shows that for step initial condition
\vec{y}=(-1,\cdots,-N)
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda(\vec{y})}\equiv 1
. Hence the global energy function and characteristic function are both constant
1
for the step initial condition. In general since the roots of the Bethe polynomial
q_{z}(w)
depend analytically on
z
, the function
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)
is analytic for
|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
. Furthermore
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)
can not be constant zero. In fact
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(0)=1
since when
|z|\to 0
all the right Bethe roots converge to
0
. As a consequence
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda(\vec{y})}(\mathcal{R}_{z};p)
is nonzero for all but finitely many
z
in any compact subset of
\{|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}\}
, which means
\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z)
is a well-defined meromorphic function in
z
on
\{|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}\}
for fixed
u,v
.

2.5.
Definition of
\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and
\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})

Definition 2.14

(Definition of
\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
)
.

With the global energy function
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)
defined in (
2.21
), we define

(2.23)

\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}):=\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z_{1})\mathscr{C}_{\text{step}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}),

where

(2.24)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\text{step}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})

\displaystyle:=\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{E_{\ell}(z_{\ell})}{E_{\ell-1}(z_{\ell})}\right]\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell}}}(-u)^{N}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}}}(v+1)^{L-N}}{\Delta(\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}};\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell}})}\right]

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\frac{z_{\ell-1}^{L}}{z_{\ell-1}^{L}-z_{\ell}^{L}}\right]\left[\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}};\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell-1}})}{\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell-1}}}(-u)^{N}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}}}(v+1)^{L-N}}\right].

Here

(2.25)

E_{\ell}(z):=\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}}(-u)^{-k_{\ell}}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}}(v+1)^{-a_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}(pv+1)^{t_{\ell}-k_{\ell}},

for
1\leq i\leq m
and
E_{0}(z):=1
.

\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
is a Fredholm determinant with kernel acting on certain
\ell^{2}
space over discrete sets related to the Bethe roots. More precisely for
m
distinct complex numbers
z_{i}
satisfying
|z_{i}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, we define the discrete sets

(2.26)

\mathscr{S}_{1}:=\mathcal{L}_{z_{1}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{z_{2}}\cup\mathcal{L}_{z_{3}}\cup\cdots\cup\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{z_{m}},&\text{if $m$ is odd},\\
\mathcal{R}_{z_{m}},&\text{if $m$ is even},\end{cases}

and

(2.27)

\mathscr{S}_{2}:=\mathcal{R}_{z_{1}}\cup\mathcal{L}_{z_{2}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{z_{3}}\cup\cdots\cup\begin{cases}\mathcal{R}_{z_{m}},&\text{if $m$ is odd},\\
\mathcal{L}_{z_{m}},&\text{if $m$ is even}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.15

(Definition of
\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
)
.

Let
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
.
Assume
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z_{1})\neq 0
so that
\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z_{1})
is well defined.
Define

(2.28)

\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})=\det(I-\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}),

where
\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}:\ell^{2}(\mathscr{S}_{2})\to\ell^{2}(\mathscr{S}_{1})
and
\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}:\ell^{2}(\mathscr{S}_{1})\to\ell^{2}(\mathscr{S}_{2})
have kernels given by

(2.29)

\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}(w,w^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{i}(j)+\delta_{i}(j+(-1)^{i})\right)\frac{J(w)f_{i}(w)(H_{z_{i}}(w))^{2}}{H_{z_{i-(-1)^{i}}}(w)H_{z_{j-(-1)^{j}}}(w^{\prime})(w-w^{\prime})}Q_{1}(j)

and

(2.30)

\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(w^{\prime},w):=\left(\delta_{j}(i)+\delta_{j}(i-(-1)^{j})\right)\frac{J(w^{\prime})f_{j}(w^{\prime})(H_{z_{j}}(w^{\prime}))^{2}}{H_{z_{j+(-1)^{j}}}(w^{\prime})H_{z_{i+(-1)^{i}}}(w)(w^{\prime}-w)}Q_{2}(i)\Lambda(i,w,w^{\prime}),

for

w\in(\mathcal{L}_{z_{i}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{z_{i}})\cap\mathscr{S}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad w^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{L}_{z_{j}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{z_{j}})\cap\mathscr{S}_{2}

with
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Here

(2.31)

\Lambda(i,w,w^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\chi_{\vec{y}}(w,w^{\prime};z_{1})\quad&\text{for }i=1,\\
1\quad&\text{for }2\leq i\leq m.\end{cases}

The functions
J(w)
and
Q_{i}(j)
are defined as follows:

(2.32)

J(w):=\frac{w(w+1)(pw+1)}{N+Lw+p(L-N)w^{2}},

and

(2.33)

Q_{1}(j):=1-\left(\frac{z_{j-(-1)^{j}}}{z_{j}}\right)^{L},\quad Q_{2}(j):=1-\left(\frac{z_{j+(-1)^{j}}}{z_{j}}\right)^{L},

for
j=1,\cdots,m
. Here we set
z_{m+1}:=0
for convenience.

To define
H_{z}(w)
and
f_{i}(w)
we recall the definition of left and right Bethe polynomials and Bethe roots discussed in Section
2.3
. With the notation there we set

(2.34)

H_{z}(w):=\begin{cases}\frac{q_{z,\mathrm{R}}(w)}{w^{N}}\quad&\text{for }\text{Re}(w)<w_{c},\\
\frac{q_{z,\mathrm{L}}(w)}{(w+1)^{L-N}}\quad&\text{for }\text{Re}(w)>w_{c}.\end{cases}

Finally the functions
f_{i}(w)
encodes the information of the parameters
a_{i},k_{i},t_{i}
’s:

(2.35)

f_{\ell}(w):=\begin{cases}\frac{F_{\ell}(w)}{F_{\ell-1}(w)}\quad\text{for }\text{Re}(w)<w_{c},\\
\frac{F_{\ell-1}(w)}{F_{\ell}(w)}\quad\text{for }\text{Re}(w)>w_{c},\end{cases}

where

(2.36)

F_{\ell}(w):=w^{k_{\ell}}(1+w)^{-a_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}(1+pw)^{t_{\ell}-k_{\ell}},

for
1\leq\ell\leq m
and
F_{0}(w):=1
.

-1

-1/p

(2)

\Omega_{\mathrm{R}}

\Sigma_{m,\mathrm{L}}^{+},\cdots,\Sigma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{+}

\Sigma_{1,\mathrm{R}}

2m-1

P_{j}(w):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{j-1}},\quad&w\in\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{+}\cup\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{+},\quad j=2,\cdots,m\\
\frac{-\theta_{j-1}}{1-\theta_{j-1}},\quad&w\in\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{-}\cup\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{-},\quad j=2,\cdots,m\\

3.
Transition probability

In this section we give an explicit integral formula for the transition probability of discrete time parallel TASEP in the configuration space
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
. This is the starting point for deriving the finite-time joint distribution formulas.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given two particle configurations
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}),\ \vec{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
. Let
P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})
be the transition probability of observing configuration
\vec{x}
at time
t
under the discrete time periodic TASEP dynamics with initial configuration
\vec{y}
. With the convention
x_{0}:=x_{N}+L
we have

(3.1)

P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Here
\Gamma
is any simple closed contour with
0
inside and
\mathcal{S}_{z}
consists of all the roots of the degree
L
polynomial
q_{z}(w):=w^{N}(w+1)^{L-N}-z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}
, i.e.,

(3.2)

\mathcal{S}_{z}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:w^{N}(w+1)^{L-N}-z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}=0\}.

The functions
F_{i,j}(w;t)
and
J(w)
are given by

(3.3)

F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=w^{j-i}(w+1)^{-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1}(1+pw)^{t+i-j},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq N,

and

(3.4)

J(w):=\frac{w(w+1)(1+pw)}{N+Lw+p(L-N)w^{2}}.

Remark 3.2
.

We remark that a different formula for the transition probability of discrete time parallel TASEP on a ring was obtained in
[
18
]
. The key difference is that the formula in
[
18
]
is expressed as an infinite sum of determinants while our formula is a single contour integral of determinants. The main reason for this is that in
[
18
]
the authors do not distinguish particle configurations which differ by a translation of an integer multiple of the period, so in our language their transition probability really is

P_{t}([\vec{y}]\to[\vec{x}])=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x}+(kL,kL,\cdots,kL)).

We believe our formula is simpler and more suitable for deriving finite-time joint distributions.

Remark 3.3
.

If we take the continuous time limit by setting
p=\epsilon
,
t=T/\epsilon
and send
\epsilon\to 0
, the dynamics then becomes continuous time periodic TASEP considered in
[
3
]
and our formula (
3.1
) reduces to equation (5.4) in
[
3
]
for the transition probability of continuous time periodic TASEP up to an index reversing.

We first list a few elementary properties of the transition probability formula (
3.1
) before discussing the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.4

(Properties of the transition probability formula)
.

The right hand side of (
3.1
) satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The right hand side of (
3.1
) can also be written as:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}_{z}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\left(1+\frac{z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}\right)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where
\hat{q}_{z}(w)=q_{z}(w)(1+pw)^{-N}=w^{N}(1+pw)^{-N}(1+w)^{L-N}-z^{L}
has the same roots as
q_{z}(w)
for any
|z|>0
and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}_{z}}
is any simple closed contour with all the roots in
\mathcal{S}_{z}
inside and
-1
and
-1/p
outside.

(ii)

The outer integral with respect to
z
in (
3.1
) (and also (
3.5
)) does not depend on the contour
\Gamma
.

(iii)

Assume further that
L\geq x_{1}-y_{N}+2
. Then the right-hand side of (
3.1
) can be further written as

(3.6)

\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where
\Gamma_{0,-1}
is any simple closed contour enclosing
0
and
-1
as the only possible poles for the integrand. Note that (
3.6
) agrees with the transition probability for discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
, see for example equation (3.21) of
[
9
]
.

(iv)

The right-hand side of (
3.1
) is invariant under cyclic translation and re-indexing. Namely, for any fixed
1\leq k\leq N
, set
\vec{x}^{\prime}:=(x_{k},x_{k+1},\cdots,x_{N},x_{1}-L,x_{2}-L,\cdots,x_{k-1}-L)
and

\vec{y}^{\prime}:=(y_{k},y_{k+1},\cdots,y_{N},y_{1}-L,y_{2}-L,\cdots,y_{k-1}-L)
. Then the right-hand side of (
3.1
) is invariant if we replace
\vec{x}
and
\vec{y}
by
\vec{x}^{\prime}
and
\vec{y}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(i)

It is easy to check that
J(w)=\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}w}\hat{q}_{z}(w)}
. Hence by the residue theorem we have

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)=\oint_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{R}_{z}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}.

Note that
F_{i,j}(w,\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}
is analytic at
w=0
for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
and the only possible poles of the integrand besides
\mathcal{S}_{z}
are
w=-1
and
w=-1/p
.

(ii)

Choose
R>0
large enough and
\epsilon>0
small enough so that all the roots in
\mathcal{S}_{z}
are inside the region
\{\epsilon<|w+1|<R\}\backslash\{|1+pw|\leq\epsilon\}
. Then

(3.7)

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)=\left(\oint_{|w+1|=R}-\oint_{|w+1|=\epsilon}-\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}.

Since
R
and
\epsilon
can be arbitrarily large or small, the right hand side of (
3.7
) as a function in
z
is analytic for any
|z|>0
. Hence the integral with respect to
z
in (
3.1
) is independent of
\Gamma
since the integrand has an analytic continuation to
\{|z|>0\}
.

(iii)

For
L>x_{1}-y_{N}+2
, we have
-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j+L-N-1\geq 0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N
. Hence for any
|z|>0
, the integrand
F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}
in (
3.7
) is analytic at
w=-1
. This implies that

(3.8)

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)=\left(\oint_{|w+1|=R}-\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}.

Now for fixed
R
large enough and
\epsilon
small enough, the right hand side of (
3.8
) is an analytic function in
z
for
|z|
sufficiently small such that all the roots of
\hat{q}_{z}
are in the region
\{|w+1|\leq R\}\backslash\{|pw+1|\leq\epsilon\}
. Now by the residue theorem the outer contour integral with respect to
z
in (
3.5
) equals the integrand evaluated at
z=0
, which equals

\det\left[\left(\oint_{|w+1|=R}-\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

(iv)

We remark that this property can be easily understood if we use the probabilistic interpretation since
\vec{x}
and
\vec{x}^{\prime}
(and also
\vec{y}
and
\vec{y}^{\prime}
) actually represent the same particle configuration on
\mathbb{Z}
(we just use particles in different period as representatives), hence the transition probability between
\vec{y}
and
\vec{x}
and
\vec{y}^{\prime}
and
\vec{x}^{\prime}
should be the same. Here however we can not directly use this since we have not proven (
3.1
). In fact, we will need this fact in our proof of (
3.1
) so we give an independent algebraic proof here.
It suffices to assume
k=2
. Then
\vec{x}^{\prime}=(x_{2},\cdots,x_{N},x_{1}-L)
,
\vec{y}^{\prime}=(y_{2},\cdots,y_{N},y_{1}-L)
. Clearly we have

\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}}=1)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x^{\prime}_{i-1}-x^{\prime}_{i}}=1)
.
So it suffices to show

\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\tilde{F}_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where
\tilde{F}_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=w^{j-i}(w+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-j+1}+i-j-1}(1+pw)^{t+i-j}
. By multi-linearity we have

\displaystyle\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\sum_{w_{1},\cdots,w_{N}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\det\left[F_{i,j}(w_{i};\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w_{i})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w_{1},\cdots,w_{N}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}^{\sigma(i)-i}(w_{i}+1)^{-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-\sigma(i)+1}+i-\sigma(i)-1}(1+pw_{i})^{t+i-\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w_{1},\cdots,w_{N}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}^{\sigma(i)-i}(w_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i}+y^{\prime}_{N-\sigma(i)}+i-\sigma(i)-1}(1+pw_{i})^{t+i-\sigma(i)}.

Here again
x^{\prime}_{0}=x^{\prime}_{N}+L=x_{1}-L+L=x_{1}
. Now we fix a permutation
\tau:=(N\cdots 21)\in S_{N}
and set
\tilde{w}_{i}:=w_{\tau(i)}
for
1\leq i\leq N
and
\tilde{\sigma}:=\tau^{-1}\sigma\tau
. Then the last line in the above equation equals

(3.9)

\sum_{\tilde{w}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{w}_{N}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\sum_{\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\tilde{\sigma})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-\tau(i)}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(i)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}.

We claim that for any fixed
\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{N}
,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-\tau(i)}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(i)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tilde{\sigma}(i)-i}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(i)+1}+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)}.

This then implies

\displaystyle\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{w}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{w}_{N}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\sum_{\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\tilde{\sigma})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tilde{\sigma}(i)-i}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(i)+1}+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\tilde{F}_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

To see the claim note that for
i\neq 1
we have
\tau(i)=i-1
and for
\tilde{\sigma}(i)\neq 1
we have
\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)=\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1
. Hence

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-\tau(i)}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(i)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tilde{\sigma}(i)-i}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(i)+1}+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)},

for
i\neq 1
and
\tilde{\sigma}(i)\neq 1
. For the other situations we split into two cases:

Case 1
:
\tilde{\sigma}(1)\neq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{1}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)-\tau(1)}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(1)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)}+\tau(1)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{1})^{t+\tau(1)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{1}^{\tilde{\sigma}(1)-1}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(1)+1}-\tilde{\sigma}(1)}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+1-\tilde{\sigma}(1)}\cdot\tilde{w}_{1}^{-N}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{N-L}(1+p\tilde{w}_{1})^{N}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{1}^{\tilde{\sigma}(1)-1}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(1)+1}-\tilde{\sigma}(1)}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+1-\tilde{\sigma}(1)}\cdot z^{-L}.

Similarly if we denote
\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}(1)=j
, then the term involving
\tilde{w}_{j}
among the sum in (
3.9
) equals

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{j}^{\tau(1)-\tau(j)}(\tilde{w}_{j}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(j)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau(1)}+\tau(j)-\tau(1)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{j})^{t+\tau(j)-\tau(1)}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{j}^{\tilde{\sigma}(j)-j}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-j+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(j)+1}-\tilde{\sigma}(j)+j-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{j})^{t+j-\tilde{\sigma}(j)}\cdot\tilde{w}_{j}^{N}(\tilde{w}_{j}+1)^{L-N}(1+p\tilde{w}_{j})^{-N}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{j}^{\tilde{\sigma}(j)-j}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-j+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(j)+1}-\tilde{\sigma}(j)+j-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{j})^{t+j-\tilde{\sigma}(j)}\cdot z^{L}.

Hence

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-\tau(i)}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(i)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+\tau(i)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(i)}

\displaystyle=z^{L}\cdot z^{-L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tilde{\sigma}(i)-i}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(i)+1}+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{i}^{\tilde{\sigma}(i)-i}(\tilde{w}_{i}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-i+1}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(i)+1}+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+i-\tilde{\sigma}(i)}.

Case 2
:
\tilde{\sigma}(1)=1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{1}^{\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)-\tau(1)}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N-\tau(1)}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)}+\tau(1)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)-1}(1+p\tilde{w}_{1})^{t+\tau(1)-\tau\tilde{\sigma}(1)}

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}_{1}^{\tilde{\sigma}(1)-1}(\tilde{w}_{1}+1)^{-x^{\prime}_{N}+y^{\prime}_{N-\tilde{\sigma}(1)+1}-\tilde{\sigma}(1)}(1+p\tilde{w}_{i})^{t+1-\tilde{\sigma}(1)},

so the claim follows and this completes the proof of the cyclic invariance.

3.1.
Proof of the transition probability formula

Now we turn to the proof of formula (
3.1
). The proof basically follows the idea of
[
9
,
3
]
where we replace the Kolmogorov forward equation by a free evolution equation with additional boundary conditions.
The main extra difficulty here is that due to parallel update rule, the stationary distribution for the dynamics is non-uniform. In fact one can check
\mu(\vec{x})\propto\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})
is the stationary distribution. As a result it turns out that
\frac{P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})}
satisfies a relatively simpler dynamics than
P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})
. More precisely we have:

Lemma 3.5
.

Given
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Let
G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
be the (unique) solution of the following free evolution equation

(3.10)

G(\vec{x},t+1;\vec{y},0)=\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{N}\in\{0,1\}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}p^{b_{i}}(1-p)^{1-b_{i}}G(\vec{x}-\vec{b},t;\vec{y},0),

together with the boundary conditions:

(3.11)

\displaystyle(1-p)\left[G(\cdots,x_{i-1}=x_{i}+1,x_{i},x_{i+1},\cdots,t;\vec{y},0)-G(\cdots,x_{i-1}=x_{i},x_{i},x_{i+1},\cdots,t;\vec{y},0)\right]

\displaystyle=p\left[G(\cdots,x_{i},x_{i}-1,x_{i+1},\cdots,t;\vec{y},0)-G(\cdots,x_{i}+1,x_{i}-1,x_{i+1},\cdots,t;\vec{y},0)\right],\text{ for }1\leq i\leq N,

and the initial condition:

(3.12)

G(\vec{x},0;\vec{y},0)=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\vec{x}=\vec{y}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{1}=1})}.

Then

(3.13)

G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)=\frac{P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{1}=1})},\quad\text{for all }\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}.

Note that we have used the convention
x_{0}=x_{N}+L
so when
i=1
, (
3.11
) should be interpreted as

(3.14)

\displaystyle(1-p)\left[G(x_{1},\cdots,x_{1}-L+1,t;\vec{y},0)-G(x_{1},\cdots,x_{1}-L,t;\vec{y},0)\right]

\displaystyle=p\left[G(x_{1}-1,\cdots,x_{1}-L,t;\vec{y},0)-G(x_{1}-1,\cdots,x_{1}-L+1,t;\vec{y},0)\right].

Proof.

Set

(3.15)

H(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0):=\frac{P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})}.

It suffices to show that for
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
,
H(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
and
G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
satisfy the same evolution equation. This then implies that
H(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)=G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
for all
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
since they satisfy the same initial condition. To better describe the evolution equation for
H(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
, it is convenient to introduce the notion of clusters of a particle configuration
\vec{x}
. Given
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
, for
1\leq i\leq N
and
1\leq k\leq N
we say
[x_{i},x_{i-1},\cdots,x_{i-k+1}]
is a cluster of size
k
of
\vec{x}
if

x_{i+1}+1<x_{i}=x_{i-1}-1=\cdots=x_{i-k+1}-k+1<x_{i-k}-k.

Namely particle
i
through
i-k+1
are right next to each other while there are at least one empty site to the left of
x_{i}
and right of
x_{i-k+1}
. Here we abuse notation by allowing the index to exceed
\{1,\cdots,N\}
and this should be understood with the convention
x_{i+kN}=x_{i}-kL
for
1\leq i\leq N
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. For convenience when
[x_{i},x_{i-1},\cdots,x_{1},x_{0},\cdots x_{-j}]
is a cluster for some
0\leq j<N
, we will also say
[x_{i},\cdots,x_{1},x_{N},\cdots,x_{N-j}]
forms a cluster so that all the indices appearing will be between
1
and
N
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{c}(\vec{x})
be the number of clusters in configuration
\vec{x}
and let
x_{c_{j}}
,
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{N}_{c}(\vec{x})
be the locations of the left-most particles in each cluster. Then it is straightforward to check that
H(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
satisfies

(3.16)

H(\vec{x},t+1;\vec{y},0)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{c_{j}}\in\{0,1\},\\
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{N}_{c}(\vec{x})\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{c}(\vec{x})}p^{b_{c_{j}}}(1-p)^{1-b_{c_{j}}}H(\vec{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{c}(\vec{x})}b_{c_{j}}\vec{e}_{c_{j}},t;\vec{y},0),

where
\vec{e}_{c_{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
has
1
in the
c_{j}
-th coordinate and
0
in the other coordinates.

We claim that for
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
, (
3.16
) and (
3.10
) take the same form (with
H
replaced by
G
) provided that
G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)
satisfies boundary conditions (
3.11
). Due to the sum of products form of (
3.16
) and (
3.10
) it suffices to check

(3.17)

\sum_{b_{i}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{i}}(1-p)^{1-b_{i}}G(\vec{x}-b_{i}\vec{e}_{i},t;\vec{y},0)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{j}\in\{0,1\}\\
j=i-m+1,\cdots,i\end{subarray}}\prod_{j}p^{b_{j}}(1-p)^{1-b_{j}}G(\vec{x}-\sum_{j}b_{j}\vec{e}_{j},t;\vec{y},0),

for
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
and a single cluster
[x_{i},x_{i-1},\cdots,x_{i-m+1}]
of size
m
. We will show the stronger statement: (
3.17
) actually holds for any
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
x_{i},x_{i-1},\cdots,x_{i-m+1}
merely satisfying
x_{i}=x_{i-1}-1=\cdots=x_{i-m+1}-m+1
. We do not require empty sites at the left and right ends so they may not form a cluster.

We prove this by induction on
m
. For
m=1
this is trivial. Assume the claim is true for any clusters of size
\leq m
. Now let
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
x_{i}=x_{i-1}-1=\cdots=x_{i-m}-m
for some
1\leq i\leq N
. Here without loss of generality we can assume
i-m\geq 1
, otherwise replace
j
by
j+N
for indices
j\leq 0
. Then

(3.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{j}\in\{0,1\}\\
j=i-m,\cdots,i\end{subarray}}\prod_{j}p^{b_{j}}(1-p)^{1-b_{j}}G(\vec{x}-\sum_{j}b_{j}\vec{e}_{j},t;\vec{y},0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{i}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{i}}(1-p)^{1-b_{i}}\cdot\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{j}\in\{0,1\}\\
j=i-m,\cdots,i-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=i-m}^{i-1}p^{b_{j}}(1-p)^{1-b_{j}}G(\vec{x}-\sum_{j=i-m}^{i}b_{j}\vec{e}_{j},t;\vec{y},0)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{i}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{i}}(1-p)^{1-b_{i}}\cdot\Big(\sum_{b_{i-1}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{i-1}}(1-p)^{1-b_{i-1}}G(\vec{x}-b_{i}\vec{e}_{i}-b_{i-1}\vec{e}_{i-1},t;\vec{y},0)\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{2}G(\vec{x},t)+p(1-p)G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i},t)+p(1-p)G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i-1},t)+p^{2}G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i}-\vec{e}_{i-1},t),

where we used the induction hypothesis in the second equality of (
3.18
) for the sum inside the brackets. For notational conveniece we have suppressed the dependence on
\vec{y}
in the last line to save space. Now by the boundary conditions (
3.11
) (possibly (
3.14
)) we have

(3.19)

(1-p)[G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i-1},t;\vec{y},0)]=p[G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i}-\vec{e}_{i-1},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i},t;\vec{y},0)].

Inserting (
3.19
) into (
3.18
) we see the last line of (
3.18
) simplifies to

(3.20)

(1-p)G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)+pG(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i},t;\vec{y},0),

which is precisely the left hand side of (
3.17
) and this completes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Now we discuss the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By Lemma
3.5
it suffices to prove

(3.21)

G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

To see this, we check that the right-hand side of (
3.21
) satisfies the free evolution equation (
3.10
), the boundary conditions (
3.11
) and the initial condition (
3.12
).

For the free evolution equation (
3.10
), note first that it is straightforward to check

(3.22)

\sum_{b_{N-i+1}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{N-i+1}}(1-p)^{1-b_{N-i+1}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{b},\vec{y};t)=F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{b}+b_{N-i+1}\vec{e}_{N-i+1},\vec{y};t+1).

Here
\vec{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
has
1
in the
i
-th entry and
0
for the others. By multi-linearity of determinants we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{k}\in\{0,1\}\\
k=1,\cdots,N\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}p^{b_{k}}(1-p)^{1-b_{k}}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{b},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\sum_{b_{N-i+1}\in\{0,1\}}p^{b_{N-i+1}}(1-p)^{1-b_{N-i+1}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{b},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{b}+b_{N-i+1}\vec{e}_{N-i+1},\vec{y},t+1)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t+1)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Here in the last equality above we used the fact that

F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)
only depends on the

(N-i+1)
-th entry of
\vec{x}
. Now (
3.10
) follows from linearity of the integral.

Next we check the boundary conditions (
3.11
). Given
1\leq k\leq N
, assume that
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
satisfies
x_{k-1}=x_{k}+1
. Note that when
k=1
this means
x_{1}=x_{N}+L-1
. Then the boundary conditions (
3.11
) can be expressed as

(3.23)

(1-p)[G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k-1},t;\vec{y},0)]=p[G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k-1}-\vec{e}_{k},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{i},t;\vec{y},0)],

for
2\leq k\leq N
, and

(3.24)

(1-p)[G(\vec{x},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{N},t;\vec{y},0)]=p[G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{N}-\vec{e}_{1},t;\vec{y},0)-G(\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{1},t;\vec{y},0)],

for
k=1
. We prove (
3.23
) first. Note that for
2\leq k\leq N
and
x_{k-1}=x_{k}+1
we have

F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)-F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k-1},\vec{y},t)=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\text{if }i\neq N-k+2,\\
-w\cdot F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t),\quad&\text{if }i=N-k+2.\end{cases}

Hence by multi-linearity we have

\displaystyle\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}-\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k-1},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot(1-(1+w)\mathbf{1}_{i=N-k+2})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Similarly

\displaystyle\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}-\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k}-\vec{e}_{k-1},\vec{y},t)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot(1-(1+w)\mathbf{1}_{i=N-k+2})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Now since
F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k},\vec{y},t)=F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)
for
i\neq N-k+1
and

(1-p)F_{N-k+1,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)+pF_{N-k+1,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k},\vec{y},t)=w\cdot F_{N-k+2,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t),

we have

\displaystyle(1-p)\cdot\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot(1-(1+w)\mathbf{1}_{i=N-k+2})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle+p\cdot\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x}-\vec{e}_{k},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot(1-(1+w)\mathbf{1}_{i=N-k+2})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[M_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}=0.

Here
M_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)
for
i\neq N-k+1,N-k+2
and
M_{N-k+1,j}^{(k)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=-M_{N-k+2,j}^{(k)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{N-k+2,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot w
. The determinant is
0
since there are two proportional rows. Now (
3.23
) follows from linearity of the integral.

The proof of (
3.24
) is similar. The only thing changes is that when
k=1
, we have
M_{i,j}^{(1)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)
for
i\neq 1,N
while

(3.25)

M_{N,j}^{(1)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}w^{-N}(w+1)^{N-L}(1+pw)^{N}F_{1,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},t)J(w)\cdot w=-z^{-L}M_{1,j}^{(1)}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t).

Hence
\det[M^{(1)}_{i,j}(\vec{x},\vec{y},t)]=0
since row
1
and
N
are proportional. Note that in the last equality of (
3.25
) we used the fact that
w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}
.

Finally we check the initial condition (
3.12
). We need to show

(3.26)

\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\vec{x}=\vec{y}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{1}=1})}.

Thanks to the cyclic-shift invariance of both sides of (
3.26
) (see (iv) of Proposition
3.4
) we can assume without loss of generality that
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
satisfies
x_{1}<x_{N}+L-1
. In fact since
N<L
there is at least one
1\leq i\leq N
such that
x_{i}<x_{i-1}-1
and we can replace
\vec{x}
and
\vec{y}
by
\vec{x}^{\prime}:=(x_{i},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{N},x_{1}-L,\cdots,x_{i-1}-L)
and
\vec{y}^{\prime}:=(y_{i},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{N},y_{1}-L,\cdots,y_{i-1}-L)
. Then the two sides of (
3.26
) remain the same and
x_{1}^{\prime}<x_{N}^{\prime}+L-1
. By (
3.7
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)J(w)=\left(\oint_{|w+1|=R}-\oint_{|w+1|=\epsilon}-\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)\frac{\hat{q}_{z}(w)+z^{L}}{\hat{q}_{z}(w)}

\displaystyle=\left(\oint_{|w+1|=R}-\oint_{|w+1|=\epsilon}-\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{w^{j-i+N}(w+1)^{-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1+L-N}(1+pw)^{i-j}}{w^{N}(w+1)^{L-N}-z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(i,j)-I_{2}(i,j)-I_{3}(i,j),

where
I_{1}(i,j),I_{2}(i,j),I_{3}(i,j)
are the integrals over the three contours, respectively. Here we recall that
R
and
\epsilon
are chosen to be large (small) enough so that
\mathcal{S}_{z}
is contained in the region
\{\epsilon<|w+1|<R\}\backslash\{|1+pw|\leq\epsilon\}
. For
I_{3}(i,j)
we note that

\displaystyle I_{3}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)+z^{L}\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|pw+1|=\epsilon}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0),

where the second term vanishes since
F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)(1+pw)^{N}
is analytic at
w=-1/p
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N
and
q_{z}(w)
is nonzero at
w=-1/p
. For the other parts we write

(3.27)

\displaystyle I_{1}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\oint_{|w+1|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)+z^{L}\oint_{|w+1|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}

\displaystyle:=\oint_{|w+1|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)+z^{L}I_{1}^{\prime}(i,j),

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle I_{2}(i,j)

\displaystyle=z^{-L}\oint_{|w+1|=\epsilon}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{w^{j-i+N}(w+1)^{-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1+L-N}(1+pw)^{i-j-N}}{z^{-L}w^{N}(w+1)^{L-N}(1+pw)^{-N}-1}

\displaystyle:=z^{-L}I_{2}^{\prime}(i,j).

Depending on the analytical properties of the integrands in
I_{1}^{\prime}
and
I_{2}^{\prime}
we split into two cases:

Case 1:

x_{1}<y_{1}
. First note the basic fact that for any
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
we have

(3.29)

x_{1}-L+i\leq x_{N-i+1}\leq x_{1}-N+i,\quad\text{and}\quad y_{1}-L+j\leq y_{N-j+1}\leq y_{1}-N+j,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq N.

Now if
x_{1}<y_{1}
, then for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
we have

-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1+L-N\geq y_{1}-x_{1}-1\geq 0.

Hence the integrand of
I_{2}^{\prime}(i,j)
is analytic at
w=-1
, so
I_{2}^{\prime}(i,j)=0
. Therefore in this case we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)+z^{L}I_{1}^{\prime}(i,j)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Here in the last equality of (
3.30
) we take the outer integral contour
\Gamma
to be
|z|=r
and let
r\to 0
.
\Gamma_{0,-1}
is any simple closed contour with
0
and
-1
inside and
-1/p
outside.

Case 2:

x_{1}\geq y_{1}
. Write

(3.31)

I_{1}^{\prime}(i,j)=\oint_{|w+1|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{w^{j-i-N}(w+1)^{-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1-L+N}(1+pw)^{i-j+N}}{1-z^{L}w^{-N}(w+1)^{-L+N}(1+pw)^{N}}.

Again by (
3.29
) we have

(3.32)

-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1-L+N\leq-x_{1}+L-i+y_{1}-N+j+i-j-1-L+N\leq-1.

We claim that the first inequality in (
3.32
) is strict and hence
-x_{N-i+1}+y_{N-j+1}+i-j-1-L+N\leq-2
. This is due to our original assumption that
x_{N}+L-1>x_{1}
and hence
x_{N-i+1}>x_{1}-L+i
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. Owing to this fact, the integrand in (
3.31
) is
O(R^{-2})
since
w^{j-i-N}(1+pw)^{i-j+N}
remains bounded. Hence
I_{1}^{\prime}(i,j)\to 0
as
R\to\infty
. But since it is independent of large enough
R
, we have
I_{1}^{\prime}(i,j)=0
for all
R
large enough. Now a similar argument as in (
3.30
) with
\Gamma
instead be a large circle
|z|=r
with
r\to\infty
implies:

(3.33)

\displaystyle\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)J(w)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)+z^{-L}I_{2}^{\prime}(i,j)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

In conclusion we have reduced checking (
3.26
) to checking the following:

(3.34)

\det\left[\oint_{\Gamma_{0,-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}F_{i,j}(w;\vec{x},\vec{y},0)\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\vec{x}=\vec{y}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{1}=1})},

for any
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
with
x_{1}<x_{N}+L-1
. But this is precisely equation
(3.30)
of
[
9
]
which appears in checking that the determinantal formula for the transition probability of discrete parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
satisfies the initial condition. We will not repeat the proof here but point out that due to the assumption
x_{1}<x_{N}+L-1
, we have
1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}-x_{1}=1}=1
so (
3.34
) is really identical to equation
(3.30)
of
[
9
]
.
∎

4.
Finite-time Multi-point joint distribution under general initial conditions

4.1.
A Toeplitz-like determinant formula

In this section we derive a formula for the finite-time multi-point joint distributions of discrete time parallel periodic TASEP under arbitrary initial condition. The proof basically follows the strategy of
[
4
]
by performing a multiple sum of transition probabilities over suitable particle configurations. The main technical part is a Cauchy-type identity for summation of left and right eigenfunctions (see Proposition
5.4
) which generalizes Proposition 3.4 of
[
4
]
and some new difficulties appear.

Theorem 4.1

(Multi-point joint distribution for discrete time parallel TASEP in
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
)
.

Given
\vec{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
. Let
\vec{x}(t)=(x_{1}(t),\cdots,x_{N}(t))\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
be particle configurations evolving according to the discrete time parallel TASEP in
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
at time
t
with initial configuration
\vec{x}(0)=\vec{y}
. Fix a positive integer
m
. Let
(k_{1},t_{1}),\cdots,(k_{m},t_{m})\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\times\mathbb{N}
be distinct with
0\leq t_{1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}
. Let
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\}\right)=\oint\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{m}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{m}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}}\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}),

where the contours for the integrals are nested circles
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|
. Here
\vec{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
. The functions
\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and
\mathcal{D}^{(L)}_{\vec{y}}(\vec{z})
are defined by

(4.2)

\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z}):=(-1)^{(N-k_{m})(N-1)}z_{1}^{(N-k_{1})L}\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\left[z_{\ell}^{(k_{\ell-1}-k_{\ell})L}\left(\left(\frac{z_{\ell}}{z_{\ell-1}}\right)^{L}-1\right)^{N-1}\right],

and

(4.3)

\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}):=\det\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}_{z_{\ell}}\\
\ell=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\frac{w_{1}^{i-1-N}(1+pw_{1})^{N-i+1}(1+w_{1})^{y_{N-i+1}+N-i+1}\cdot w_{m}^{-j}}{\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}(w_{\ell}-w_{\ell-1})}\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}G_{\ell}(w_{\ell})\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where for
1\leq\ell\leq m
,

(4.4)

G_{\ell}(w):=\frac{w(w+1)(1+pw)}{N+Lw+p(L-N)w^{2}}\cdot\frac{w^{k_{\ell}}(1+w)^{-a_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}(1+pw)^{t_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}}{w^{k_{\ell-1}}(1+w)^{-a_{\ell-1}-k_{\ell-1}}(1+pw)^{t_{\ell-1}-k_{\ell-1}}}.

Here
k_{0}=t_{0}=a_{0}:=0
and we suppress the dependence on
a_{i}
,
k_{i}
and
t_{i}
’s in
\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
.

Proof.

We start with the case
m=1
for a warm-up. First by Cauchy-Binet formula we can rewrite the transition probability (
3.1
) as

P_{t}(\vec{x}\to\vec{x}^{\prime})=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\sum_{\vec{w}\in(\mathcal{S}_{z})^{N}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}^{\prime}}^{r}(\vec{w})\mathcal{Q}(\vec{w};t),

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})=\det\left[\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{j-1}(1+w_{i})^{x_{N-j+1}-j+1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\cdot\det\left[\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

and

(4.7)

\mathcal{Q}(\vec{w};t)=\frac{1}{N!}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{(1+pw_{j})^{t}J(w_{j})}{w_{j}+1}=\frac{1}{N!}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{w_{j}(1+pw_{j})^{t+1}}{N+Lw_{j}+p(L-N)w_{j}^{2}}.

Now to get the one-point distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(x_{k}(t)\geq a)
we perform a summation over all configurations
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
with
x_{k}\geq a
of the transition probability and interchange the order of integration and summation:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(x_{k}(t)\geq a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k}\geq a\}}P_{t}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{\vec{w}\in(\mathcal{S}_{z})^{N}}\Psi_{\vec{y}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})Q(\vec{w};t)\left(\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\right).

Now by Corollary
5.3
in Section
5
we have
\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
equals

(4.9)

(-1)^{(N-k)(N-1)}z^{(N-k)L}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{1+pw_{i}}{w_{i}}\right)^{N-k}\cdot(1+w_{i})^{-a+N-k+1}\right]\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Inserting (
4.9
) back to (
4.8
) and use Cauchy-Binet formula backwards we conclude that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(x_{k}(t)\geq a)=\frac{(-1)^{(N-k)(N-1)}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{z^{1-(N-k)L}}\det\left[\sum_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}}\frac{w^{j-i+k-N}(1+pw)^{t-j+N-k+2}(1+w)^{y_{N-j+1}-j-a+N-k+2}}{N+Lw+p(L-N)w^{2}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Here in order to interchange the order of summation and integration as in (
4.8
) we need to make sure that the summation over
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k}\geq a\}
converges absolutely. This is guaranteed if we assume
|w+1|>1
for all
w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}
(see the discussion in Proposition
5.2
). By choosing the contour
\Gamma
to be a circle with large radius
R
we can make sure that
|w+1|>1
for all
w\in\mathcal{S}_{z}
since for
|z|=R
and all
w
satisfying
w^{N}(1+pw)^{-N}(w+1)^{L-N}=z^{L}
we have
|w+1|=O(|z|^{\frac{L}{L-N}})>1
. Finally a similar argument as in (ii) of Proposition
3.4
shows that the right-hand side of (
4.10
) does not depend on the choice of
\Gamma
, so we can deform
\Gamma
to be any simple closed contour containing
0
.

Now assume
m\geq 2
. Then

(4.11)

\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}\left(\cap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}^{(\ell)}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x^{(\ell)}_{k_{\ell}}\geq a_{\ell}\}\\
\ell=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}P_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(\vec{y}\to\vec{x}^{(1)})\cdots P_{t_{m}-t_{m-1}}(\vec{x}^{(m-1)}\to\vec{x}^{(m)}),

where
t_{0}:=0
. As in the
m=1
case we rewrite the transition probability using Cauchy-Binet formula and interchange the order of summation and integration (will be justified later) so that
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}(\cap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\})
equals

(4.12)

\displaystyle\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{m}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{w}^{(\ell)}\in(\mathcal{S}_{z_{\ell}})^{N}\\
\ell=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{P}(\vec{w}^{(1)},\cdots,\vec{w}^{(m)})\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathcal{Q}(\vec{w}^{(\ell)};t_{\ell}-t_{\ell-1}).

Here
\vec{w}^{(\ell)}=(w^{(\ell)}_{1},\cdots,w^{(\ell)}_{m})
and

(4.13)

\mathcal{P}(\vec{w}^{(1)},\cdots,\vec{w}^{(m)})=\Psi_{\vec{y}}^{(\ell)}(\vec{w}^{(1)})\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\mathcal{H}_{k_{\ell},a_{\ell}}(\vec{w}^{(\ell)};\vec{w}^{(\ell+1)})\right]\cdot\left[\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k_{m}}\geq a_{m}\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w}^{(m)})\right].

Where

(4.14)

\mathcal{H}_{k,a}(\vec{w};\vec{w}^{\prime}):=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime}).

Now similar as in the
m=1
case we evaluate the sums appearing in (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) using Corollary
5.3
and Corollary
5.6
in Section
5
and apply Cauchy-Binet formula backwards to conclude that

(4.15)

\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\}\right)=\oint\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{m}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{m}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}}\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}),

for
\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
defined in (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). Similar as the discussion before, in order to interchange summation and integration we need the absolute convergence of all the infinite sums which is guaranteed if we assume

(4.16)

\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}^{(1)}+1|>\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}^{(2)}+1|>\cdots>\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}^{(m)}+1|>1.

By the same reasoning as in
m=1
case this can be achieved assuming the integral contours for
z_{i}
’s are large nested contours
|z_{\ell}|=r_{\ell}
with
r_{\ell}-r_{\ell+1}
also large enough for all
1\leq\ell\leq m
. Finally we can deform the integral contours in (
4.1
) into arbitrary nested contours with
0
inside, not necessarily with large radius. This is due to the analyticity of
\mathcal{C}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
in
\vec{z}
for any
z_{i}
’s nonzero and distinct, which can be shown in a similar way as (ii) of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

4.2.
Toeplitz-like determinant to Fredholm determinant

The finite time formula obtained in Theorem
4.1
contains a factor
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
inside the integrals which is a Toeplitz-like determinant and is hard to take large-time limits. In this section we rewrite the formula
based on a remarkable finite determinantal identity obtained in
[
5
]
. It is an identity between a Toeplitz-like determinant with symbol supported on a finite set and a Fredholm determinant with kernel acting on the
\ell^{2}
space supported on the same finite set.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

The proof is almost verbatim to the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
5
]
so we omit most of the details. We apply Proposition 4.1 of
[
5
]
with the functions
p_{i}(w)
,
q_{i}(w)
and
h_{i}(w)
given by

(4.17)

p_{i}(w)=w^{i-1}(1+pw)^{N-i}(1+w)^{y_{N-i+1}+N-i+1},\quad\text{and }\ q_{i}(w)=w^{N-i},

for
1\leq i\leq N
and

(4.18)

h_{i}(w):=\begin{cases}G_{i}(w)w^{-N}(1+pw)\quad&i=1,\\
G_{i}(w)\quad&2\leq i\leq m-1,\\
G_{i}(w)w^{-N}\quad&i=m.\end{cases}

Then the Toepliz-like determinant
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
can be rewritten as the product of a function and a Fredholm determinant. Combining with the definition of the function
\mathcal{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})
and using the algebraic relation satisfied by the Bethe roots we obtain the identity

(4.19)

\mathcal{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\mathcal{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})=\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})\mathscr{D}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z}).

∎

5.
Summation identities of eigenfunctions

In this section we state and prove the summation identities used in computing the multi-point joint distribution in Section
4
. First we recall the left and right eigenfunctions defined in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
). They are certain (anti)-symmetric functions appearing naturally in the transition probability formula (
3.1
). Our summation identities should be viewed as Littlewood or Cauchy type identities for these symmetric functions, but over configuration spaces
x_{N}+L>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}
(partitions wrapped on a cylinder).

Definition 5.1

(Left and right eigenfunctions)
.

Given
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(\infty)}
and
p\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the functions
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})
and
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
for
\vec{w}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
as follows:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})=\det\left[\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{j-1}(1+w_{i})^{x_{N-j+1}-j+1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\cdot\det\left[\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

The rest of this section is organized as follows: we list all the summation identities we need in Section
5.1
and postpone the proofs to the next few subsections. The proof of the summation identity over a single eigenfunction (Proposition
5.2
) consists of straightforward manipulations of determinants and will be discussed in Section
5.2
. The summation identity over left and right eigenfunctions (Proposition
5.4
) is more involved and the proof will be splitted into three steps discussed in Section
5.3
to Section
5.6
. Throughout the proof several rank-one perturbation formulas for Cauchy determinants are frequently used so we collect all these elementary formulas in a separate section for convenience, see Section
5.7
for details. Finally in Section
5.8
we discuss the proof of Corollary
5.3
and
5.6
which are simple consequences of the previous propositions and the periodic nature of the identities.

5.1.
Summation identities over eigenfunctions

We start with a summation identity for
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
:

Proposition 5.2

(Summation over a single eigenfunction)
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
be nonzero. Let
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
be as in (
4.6
) where
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{N})\in(\mathcal{S}_{z})^{N}
satisfies
\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}+1|>1
. Then

(5.3)

\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}\geq 0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

The following corollary which allows slightly more general constraints on the summation is a simple consequence of Proposition
5.2
and the periodic nature of
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Under the same assumption as in Proposition
5.2
we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N-k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(-1)^{k(N-1)}z^{kL}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{1+pw_{i}}{w_{i}}\right)^{k}\cdot(1+w_{i})^{-a+k+1}\right]\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N},

for all
0\leq k\leq N-1
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The above summation identities over a single eigenfunction are sufficient for computing the one-point distribution. To get the multi-point distribution we also need the following Cauchy-type summation identities over products of left and right eigenfunctions.

Proposition 5.4

(Cauchy-type summation identity over left and right eigenfunctions)
.

Let
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
be nonzero such that
(z^{\prime})^{L}\neq z^{L}
. Let
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
and
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
and
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime})
be as in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), where
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{N})\in(\mathcal{S}_{z})^{N}
and
\vec{w}^{\prime}=(w_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,w_{N}^{\prime})\in(\mathcal{S}_{z^{\prime}})^{N}
satisfy
\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}+1|>\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}^{\prime}+1|
. Then

(5.5)

\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}\geq 0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime})=\left(1-\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}\right)^{L}\right)^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(w_{j}+1)\cdot\det\left[\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\right]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}.

Remark 5.5
.

It is interesting to note that the right-hand side of (
5.5
) does not depend on
p
explicitly (of course the
w_{i}
’ should satisfy certain algebraic equations which depend on
p
). Taking
p\to 0
, Proposition
5.4
degenerates to Proposition
3.4
of
[
4
]
, which can be understood as the Cauchy identity (a periodic version) for the Grothendieck polynomial (and its dual), and can be derived from the finite-sum Cauchy identity for Grothendieck polynomials obtained in Theorem 5.3 of
[
17
]
. For
0<p<1
, to the best of our knowledge the corresponding Cauchy-type identity (
5.5
) has not been discussed in the existing literature, at least for the periodic case. The key point here is instead of summing over all configuration
\vec{x}
with
0\leq x_{N}<x_{N-1}<\cdots<x_{1}
as in the usual Cauchy identity, we are only summing over those configurations satisfying the extra constraint
x_{1}<x_{N}+L
. For general spectral parameters
\vec{w}
and
\vec{w}^{\prime}
this sum only gives a deformed or generalized Cauchy determinant. It further reduces to a genuine Cauchy determinant when we impose the conditions as in Proposition
5.4
that the spectral parameters satisfy suitable Bethe equations.

Similar as in Corollary
5.3
, we can easily extend Proposition
5.4
using periodicity to the summations over
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N-k}\geq a\}
for any
0\leq k\leq N-1
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
:

Corollary 5.6
.

Under the same assumption as in Proposition
5.4
we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N-k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{z}{z^{\prime}}\right)^{kL}\left(1-\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}\right)^{L}\right)^{N-1}\cdot\det\left[\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\right]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{w_{j}^{-k}(1+pw_{j})^{k}(w_{j}+1)^{-a+k+1}}{(w_{j}^{\prime})^{-k}(1+pw_{j}^{\prime})^{k}(w_{j}^{\prime}+1)^{-a+k}},

for all
0\leq k\leq N-1
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.2

First we write

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}\geq 0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+w_{j})^{-a}\sum_{\vec{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}^{\prime}=0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}^{\prime}}^{r}(\vec{w}),

where
\vec{x}^{\prime}=\vec{x}-(a,\cdots,a)
. The summation over
a
converges absolutely provided that
\prod_{j=1}^{N}|1+w_{j}|>1
. Thus we have reduced the computation to a summation with constraint
x_{N}=0
which is a finite sum so there is no convergence issue. We will see the telescoping nature of the summation over
a
.

The main difficulty for computing the sum comes from the
\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})
factor in
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
which gives different weights to different terms in the sum. To handle this we first split the sum according to the position of the first empty site to the right of
x_{N}
in the configuration
\vec{x}
. More precisely we set

\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}:=\{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}:x_{N}=x_{N-1}-1=\cdots x_{N-k}-k=0,x_{N-k-1}-k-1>0\}.

Intuitively
\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
consists of all configurations in
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=0\}
with the first empty site to the right of
x_{N}
(which equals
0
) be at
k+1
. Then clearly we have
\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=0\}=\cup_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w}):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Sigma_{k}.

We will first compute the summations over
\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
for each
k
and then sum over
k
. The results for each of these steps are summarized in the following two lemmas, whose proofs will be at the end of this subsection.

Lemma 5.7
.

We have

\Sigma_{k}:=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(1-p)^{k}\det[R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&k+2\leq j\leq N.\\
\end{cases}

Lemma 5.8
.

Furthermore,

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Sigma_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-p)^{k}\det[R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}-\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Now by Lemma
5.8
and (
5.7
) we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}\geq 0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})^{-a}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}-\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proof of Lemma
5.7
.

Note that configurations
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
take the form
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N-k-1},k,\cdots,0)
where
x_{N-k-1}>k+1
. Hence for
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
we have

\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(1-p)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{N-k-1}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\det[R_{i,j}^{(k,k+1)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

R_{i,j}^{(k,k+1)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1},\quad&k+2\leq j\leq N.\end{cases}

We sum over configurations in
\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}
in the following order:

\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N,k}^{(L)}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\sum_{x_{1}=N}^{L-1}\sum_{x_{2}=N-1}^{x_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{x_{N-k-1=k+2}}^{x_{N-k-2}-1}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w}).

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{N-k-1}=k+2}^{x_{N-k-2}-1}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{N-k-2}-x_{N-k-1}=1})R_{i,k+2}^{(k+1)}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=w_{i}^{-2-k}(1+pw_{i})^{1+k}-w_{i}^{-2-k}(1+pw_{i})^{2+k}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-k-2}+k+2}

\displaystyle=w_{i}^{-2-k}(1+pw_{i})^{1+k}-R_{i,k+3}^{(k,k+1)}(\vec{w}).

Adding the
(k+3)
-th column to the
(k+2)
-th column we get

\sum_{x_{N-k-1}=k+2}^{x_{N-k-2}-1}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(1-p)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{N-k-2}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\det[R_{i,j}^{(k,k+2)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

R_{i,j}^{(k,k+2)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&j=k+2,\\
w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1},\quad&k+3\leq j\leq N.\end{cases}

Now we repeat this procedure and perform the sum over
x_{N-k-2},\cdots,x_{2}
to get

\sum_{x_{2}=N-1}^{x_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{x_{N-k-1}=k+2}^{x_{N-k-2}-1}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(1-p)^{k}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}-x_{1}=1})\det[R_{i,j}^{(k,N-1)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

R_{i,j}^{(k,N-1)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&k+2\leq j\leq N-1,\\
w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1},\quad&j=N.\end{cases}

Finally note that

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}=N}^{L-1}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}-x_{1}=1})R_{i,N}^{(k,N-1)}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=w_{i}^{-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N-1}-w_{i}^{-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N}(1+w_{i})^{-L-N}

\displaystyle=w_{i}^{-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N-1}-z^{-L}R_{i,1}^{(k,N-1)}(\vec{w}),

where we used the fact that
w_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{z}
. Thus we conclude that

\Sigma_{k}:=\sum_{x_{1}=N}^{L-1}\cdots\sum_{x_{N-k-1}=k+2}^{x_{N-k-2}-1}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(1-p)^{k}\det[R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&k+2\leq j\leq N.\\
\end{cases}

∎

Proof of Lemma
5.8
.

For the purpose of summing over
k
we further rewrite
R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})
slightly. Given
0\leq k\leq N-3
, we add the
j
-th column of
R^{(k)}(\vec{w})
to its
(j+1)
-th column,
j=N,\cdots,k+2
so that

\det[R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\det[\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq k+1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&j=k+2,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1}(1+w_{i}),\quad&k+3\leq j\leq N.\\
\end{cases}

For
k=N-2,N-1
we just set
\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})=R_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})
. Now we perform the sum over
k
in the order
k=N-1,N-2,\cdots,0
. Note that for each
0\leq k\leq N-1
,
\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(k)}(\vec{w})=\hat{R}^{(k+1)}_{i,j}(\vec{w})
except for
j=k+1
. Hence by multi-linearity of the determinants we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{N-1}+\Sigma_{N-2}

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{N-2}\left((1-p)\det[\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(N-1,N)}(\vec{w})]+\det[\hat{R}_{i,j}^{(N-2)}(\vec{w})]\right)

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{N-2}\det[T_{i,j}^{(N-2)}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

T_{i,j}^{(N-2)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&1\leq j\leq N-1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-2}(1+w_{i}),&j=N.\\
\end{cases}

Here to simplify the expression we have multiplied the
(N-1)
-th column by
-p(1-p)
and added it to the sum of the
N
-th column of
\hat{R}^{(N-1)}(\vec{w})
and
\hat{R}^{(N-2)}(\vec{w})
, using the simple identity that

\left[(1-p)w_{i}+1\right]\cdot w_{i}^{-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N-1}=w_{i}^{-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N-2}(1+w_{i})+p(1-p)w_{i}^{2-N}(1+pw_{i})^{N-2}.

Repeating this procedure we get

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Sigma_{k}=\det[T^{(0)}_{i,j}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

T_{i,j}^{(0)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-1},\quad&j=1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+pw_{i})^{j-2}(1+w_{i}),&2\leq j\leq N.\\
\end{cases}

Multiplying the
j
-th column of
T^{(0)}(\vec{w})
by
-p
and adding to the
(j+1)
-th column for
j=N-1,\cdots,2
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Sigma_{k}=\det[\hat{T}^{(0)}_{i,j}(\vec{w})]_{i,j=1}^{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+w_{i})\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}-\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N},

where

\hat{T}_{i,j}^{(0)}(\vec{w}):=\begin{cases}w_{i}^{1-j},\quad&j=1,\\
w_{i}^{-j}(1+w_{i}),&2\leq j\leq N.\\
\end{cases}

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.8
.
∎

5.3.
Proof of Proposition
5.4
: Strategy

The proof of Proposition
5.4
is rather lengthy so we divide it into three steps and discuss them one by one in the next few subsections. The proof mainly follows the proof strategy of Proposition 3.4 of
[
4
]
but there are several new technicalities. The non-uniform term
\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})
appearing in
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})
leads to extra difficulty for the sum. In Step 1 we overcome this by introducing a different way (and slightly more convenient way in our opinion) of expressing the sum in (
5.5
) comparing to the proof in
[
4
]
, see Lemma
5.9
for details. In Step 2 we establish a key summation identity (see Lemma
5.12
) which generalizes Lemma 5.4 in
[
4
]
while the computation is more delicate. Finally in Step 3 we combine the formula obtained in Step 1 and the summation identity obtained in Step 2 to conclude the final result.

5.4.
Proof of Proposition
5.4
: Step 1

Similar as in the proof of Proposition
5.2
we first write

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}\geq 0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1+w_{j}^{\prime}}{1+w_{j}}\right)^{a}\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=0\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1+w_{j}^{\prime}}{1+w_{j}}\right)^{a}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{N}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime}),

so that it suffices to compute the sum over
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}=0\}
, which is a finite sum so there is no convergence issue. The summation over
a
converges absolutely by our assumption that
\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}+1|>\prod_{j=1}^{N}|w_{j}^{\prime}+1|
. Expanding the determinants in
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{\ell}(\vec{w})
and
\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w}^{\prime})
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\sigma\sigma^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}(1+pw_{\sigma(j)})}{w_{\sigma(j)}(1+pw^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)})}\right)^{j-1}\mathcal{H}^{(N)}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime}),

where

(5.8)

\mathcal{H}^{(N)}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime}):=\sum_{L=x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{j-1}-x_{j}=1})\left(\frac{w^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}+1}{w_{\sigma(j)}+1}\right)^{x_{N-j+1}-j+1}.

By Lemma
5.9
below we have
\mathcal{H}^{(N)}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=i}^{k}\frac{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}^{\prime}+1}{w_{\sigma(j)}+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}^{\prime}+1}{w_{\sigma(i)}+1}\right)^{L-N}\cdot\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=k+1}^{i}\frac{w_{\sigma(j)}+1}{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}^{\prime}+1}}\right)\right].

Hence

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{N}}\text{sgn}(\sigma\sigma^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}(1+pw_{\sigma(j)})}{w_{\sigma(j)}(1+pw^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)})}\right)^{j-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=i}^{k}\frac{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}^{\prime}+1}{w_{\sigma(j)}+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}^{\prime}+1}{w_{\sigma(i)}+1}\right)^{L-N}\cdot\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=k+1}^{i}\frac{w_{\sigma(j)}+1}{w_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}^{\prime}+1}}\right)\right].

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and
w_{1},\cdots,w_{N}
and
w_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,w_{N}^{\prime}
be distinct complex numbers. Then for any integer
L>N
we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{L=x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{x_{N-i+1}-i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=i}^{k}\frac{w_{j}^{\prime}+1}{w_{j}+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{L-N}\cdot\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=k+1}^{i}\frac{w_{j}+1}{w_{j}^{\prime}+1}}\right)\right].

Here any empty product is set to be
1
.

Proof.

We use an induction on
N
. For
N=1
the identity is obvious. Assume now
N\geq 2
and the identity holds for all indices less than
N
. We split the sum into two sums depending on whether
x_{1}=L-1
or not:

\sum_{L=x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0}=\sum_{L-1=x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0}+\sum_{L-1>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0}:=T_{1}+T_{2}.

For
T_{1}
we first relabel the indices so that
(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{N-1}^{\prime}):=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N})
and
L^{\prime}:=L-1
. Then by induction hypothesis we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-p)\left(\frac{w_{N}^{\prime}+1}{w_{N}+1}\right)^{L-N}\sum_{L^{\prime}=x_{0}^{\prime}>x_{1}^{\prime}>\cdots>x_{N-1}^{\prime}=0}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x^{\prime}_{i-1}-x^{\prime}_{i}=1})\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{x_{N-i}^{\prime}-i+1}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=i}^{k}\frac{w_{j}^{\prime}+1}{w_{j}+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{L-N}\cdot T_{1}^{(k)}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})\right].

Where

T_{1}^{(k)}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime}):=(1-p)\prod_{i=k+1}^{N-1}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=k+1}^{i}\frac{w_{j}+1}{w_{j}^{\prime}+1}}\right),

for
1\leq k\leq N-1
and we set
T_{1}^{(N)}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime}):=0
for convenience.

For
T_{2}
we calculate the sum directly in the order
L-1>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{N}=0
using Lemma
5.10
below which gives:

(5.12)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{N-1}=1}^{L-N-1}\cdots\sum_{x_{1}=x_{2}+1}^{L-2}\prod_{i=2}^{N}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}=1})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{x_{N-i+1}-i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=i}^{k}\frac{w_{j}^{\prime}+1}{w_{j}+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\left(\frac{w_{i}^{\prime}+1}{w_{i}+1}\right)^{L-N}\cdot T_{2}^{(k)}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})\right].

Where

\displaystyle T_{2}^{(k)}(\vec{w},\vec{w}^{\prime})

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
be complex numbers such that
\prod_{j=\ell}^{k}z_{j}\neq 1
for all
1\leq\ell\leq k\leq n
. Then

(5.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{1=s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq n}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\prod_{j=s_{i}}^{s_{i+1}-1}z_{j}\right)-1}\cdot\prod_{j=s_{i}+1}^{s_{i+1}-1}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{m=j}^{s_{i+1}-1}z_{m}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(-p+\frac{1}{1-\prod_{j=1}^{i}z_{j}^{-1}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\right)-1}.

Proof.

Fixing an integer
M>n
, consider the following sum:

\sum_{M>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}\geq 0}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}}=1)\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{x_{n-j+1}-j+1}.

We calculate the sum in two different ways: from right to left or from left to right. Namely we set

S^{r\to\ell}_{M}:=\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{M-n}\cdots\sum_{x_{1}=x_{2}+1}^{M-1}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}}=1)\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{x_{n-j+1}-j+1},

and

S^{\ell\to r}_{M}:=\sum_{x_{1}=n-1}^{M-1}\cdots\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{x_{n-1}-1}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-p\mathbf{1}_{x_{i-1}-x_{i}}=1)\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{x_{n-j+1}-j+1}.

Then clearly
S^{r\to\ell}_{M}=S^{\ell\to r}_{M}
. Now the two sums are calculated by calculating the (almost) geometric sums one by one either from left to right or vice versa. There are
2^{n}
terms in total for both sums since every single term produces two terms after performing the geometric sum once. Each of the terms contains of a factor of the form
\prod_{j=n-k+1}^{n}z_{j}^{M-n+1}
for some
0\leq k\leq n
where
k=0
corresponds to the terms containing no such factor. For each
k
we combine all the terms with the same factor
\prod_{j=n-k+1}^{n}z_{j}^{M-n+1}
and write

S^{r\to\ell}_{M}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[C^{r\to\ell}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\cdot\prod_{j=n-k+1}^{n}z_{j}^{M-n+1}\right],

and

S^{\ell\to r}_{M}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[C^{\ell\to r}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\cdot\prod_{j=n-k+1}^{n}z_{j}^{M-n+1}\right].

Where the coefficients
C^{r\to\ell}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
’s and
C^{\ell\to r}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
’s are some very explicit functions in
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
independent of
M
which are analytic for all
z_{i}
’s satisfying the assumption that
\prod_{j=\ell}^{k}z_{j}\neq 1
for all
1\leq k\leq\ell\leq n
. In particular it is straightforward to check (see Lemma
5.10
for example) that

\text{LHS of }\eqref{eq: reduction of the sum over partitions}=C^{r\to\ell}_{n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),\quad\text{RHS of }\eqref{eq: reduction of the sum over partitions}=C^{\ell\to r}_{n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}).

We claim that
C^{r\to\ell}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=C^{\ell\to r}_{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
for all
0\leq k\leq n
. This in particular implies (
5.18
). Due to analyticity it suffices to check this for the
z_{j}
’s satisfying
|z_{j}|<1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. In this case by letting
M\to\infty
in the equality
S^{r\to\ell}_{M}=S^{\ell\to r}_{M}
we see

C_{0}^{r\to\ell}=C_{0}^{\ell\to r}
. Similarly

C_{1}^{r\to\ell}=\lim_{M\to\infty}z_{n}^{-M+n-1}\cdot(S_{M}^{r\to\ell}-C_{0}^{r\to\ell})=\lim_{M\to\infty}z_{n}^{-M+n-1}\cdot(S_{M}^{\ell\to r}-C_{0}^{\ell\to r})=C_{1}^{\ell\to r}.

Repeat this procedure we see
C_{k}^{r\to\ell}=C_{k}^{\ell\to r}
for all
0\leq k\leq n
.
∎

(5.35)

\displaystyle\det[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)^{N}\det\left[\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\frac{1}{w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}(1+pw_{i})}\right]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)^{N}\det[C(i,i^{\prime})]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\left(\frac{B(-1/p)A(0)}{A(-1/p)B(0)}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)^{N-1}\det[C(i,i^{\prime})]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}\cdot\left(1-\mu\cdot\frac{B(-1/p)A(0)}{A(-1/p)B(0)}\right).

Here
C(i,i^{\prime})=\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}
and
A(0)
is the evaluation at
z=0
of the polynomial
A(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-w_{j})
. The other terms involving
A(\cdot)
and
B(\cdot)
are defined in a similar way with
B(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-w_{j}^{\prime})
. Now by Lemma
5.15
we have

(5.36)

\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})\right]-\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})-\frac{1}{w_{i}+1}\right]=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[\hat{C}^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{\ell}+1},

where
\hat{C}^{\ell,k}
is the matrix obtained by removing row
\ell
and column
k
from
\hat{C}
. Similarly

(5.37)

\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})\right]-\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})+\frac{1}{w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}+1}\right]=-\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[\hat{C}^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{k}^{\prime}+1}.

Now since
\hat{C}^{\ell,k}
has the same entries as
\hat{C}
only omitting row
\ell
and column
k
, by (
5.35
) we have

(5.38)

\det[\hat{C}^{\ell,k}]=(1-\mu)^{N-2}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\left(1-\mu\cdot\frac{B(-1/p)A(0)}{A(-1/p)B(0)}\cdot\frac{w_{k}^{\prime}(1+pw_{\ell})}{w_{\ell}(1+pw_{k}^{\prime})}\right).

Where
C^{\ell,k}
is obtained from removing row
\ell
and column
k
from the
N\times N
Cauchy matrix
C
with
C(i,i^{\prime})=\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}
and
A(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-w_{j})
and
B(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-w_{j}^{\prime})
. Inserting (
5.38
) into (
5.36
) we see

(5.39)

\displaystyle\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})\right]-\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})-\frac{1}{w_{i}+1}\right]=(1-\mu)^{N-2}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{\ell}+1}

\displaystyle-\mu(1-\mu)^{N-2}\cdot\frac{B(-1/p)A(0)}{A(-1/p)B(0)}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{w_{k}^{\prime}(1+pw_{\ell})}{w_{\ell}(1+w_{\ell})(1+pw_{k}^{\prime})}.

Similarly

(5.40)

\displaystyle\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})\right]-\det\left[\hat{C}(i,i^{\prime})+\frac{1}{w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}+1}\right]=-(1-\mu)^{N-2}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{k}^{\prime}+1}

\displaystyle+\mu(1-\mu)^{N-2}\cdot\frac{B(-1/p)A(0)}{A(-1/p)B(0)}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{w_{k}^{\prime}(1+pw_{\ell})}{w_{\ell}(1+w_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime})(1+pw_{k}^{\prime})}.

Now by (
5.45
) and Lemma
5.17
we have

(5.41)

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{\ell}+1}=\det[C]\cdot\left(1-\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{1}{w_{k}^{\prime}+1}=\det[C]\cdot\left(\frac{A(-1)}{B(-1)}-1\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{w_{k}^{\prime}(1+pw_{\ell})}{w_{\ell}(1+w_{\ell})(1+pw_{k}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p}\det[C]\cdot\left[\left(1-\frac{A(-1/p)}{B(-1/p)}\right)\frac{B(0)}{A(0)}+\left(p-1+\frac{A(-1/p)}{B(-1/p)}\right)\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}-p\right],

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[C^{\ell,k}]\cdot\frac{w_{k}^{\prime}(1+pw_{\ell})}{w_{\ell}(1+w_{\ell})(1+pw_{k}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-p}\det[C]\cdot\left[\left(\frac{B(0)}{A(0)}-1\right)\frac{A(-1/p)}{B(-1/p)}+\left(p-\frac{B(0)}{A(0)}\right)\frac{A(-1)}{B(-1)}+1-p\right].

Inserting (
5.41
) into (
5.39
) and (
5.40
) and combine with (
5.32
), after some tedious simplification we conclude that

(5.42)

\hat{S}_{N}=(1-\mu)^{N}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}(w_{j}+1)-\prod_{j=1}^{N}(w_{j}^{\prime}+1)\right)\cdot\det\left[\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\right]_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
5.4
.

5.7.
Perturbation formulas for Cauchy determinants

In this section we collect all the elementary linear algebra facts needed in the proof of Proposition
5.4
. Some of them have already been discussed in
[
4
]
. First we state a general linear algebra lemma on rank-one perturbations:

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
D=[D_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
be an
n\times n
matrix. Then for any function
f,g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
and complex numbers
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
,
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}
we have

(5.43)

\displaystyle\det\left[D_{ij}+f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\det[D]+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[D^{\ell,k}]g(y_{k})f(x_{\ell}),

where
D^{\ell,k}
is obtained by removing row
\ell
and column
k
from
D
.

Proof.

For
D
invertible, by the rank-one property and the Cramer’s rule we have

\displaystyle\det\left[D_{ij}+f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle=\det[D]\left(1+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}g(y_{k})(D^{-1})_{k,\ell}f(x_{\ell})\right)

\displaystyle=\det[D]+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}(-1)^{\ell+k}\det[D^{\ell,k}]g(y_{k})f(x_{\ell}).

For general matrix
D
we pick
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and
D+\epsilon_{k}I_{n}
are invertible for all
\epsilon_{k}
. Now apply the above argument for
D+\epsilon_{k}I_{n}
and let
k\to\infty
.
∎

Next we specialize to the case of
C
being a Cauchy matrix when the minors can be explicitly calculated:

Lemma 5.16
.

Assume further that the matrix
C
is a Cauchy matrix with
(i,j)
-th entry
\frac{1}{x_{i}-y_{j}}
for distinct complex numbers
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}
. Then we further have

(5.44)

\det\left[C_{ij}+f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\det[C]\cdot\left(1-\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\frac{f(x_{\ell})B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}\right).

Here
A(z):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-x_{i})
and
B(z):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-y_{i})
are monic polynomials with roots at
x_{i}
’s and
y_{i}
’s.

Proof.

For Cauchy matrix
C
we have

\det[C]=\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})}{\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(x_{i}-y_{j})}.

Note that
C^{\ell,k}
is also a Cauchy matrix so we have

\det[C^{\ell,k}]=\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\neq\ell,j\neq k\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq n\\
i\neq\ell,j\neq k\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-y_{j})}.

Hence

(5.45)

\frac{\det[C^{\ell,k}]}{\det[C]}=(-1)^{\ell+k+1}\frac{B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}.

Now (
5.43
) and (
5.45
) imply (
5.44
).
∎

For special choices of
f
and
g
, (
5.44
) can be further simplified using the residue theorem. We list here all the situations encountered in the proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Lemma 5.17
.

Given distinct complex numbers
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}
. Let
C
be the Cauchy matrix with
(i,j)
-th entry
\frac{1}{x_{i}-y_{j}}
and
A(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-x_{i})
and
B(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-y_{i})
. Then

(1)

For
f(x)=\frac{x}{(1+x)(1+px)}
and
g(y)=\frac{1+py}{y}
we have
\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\frac{f(x_{\ell})B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}
equals

(5.46)

-\frac{1}{1-p}\cdot\left[\left(\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}-1\right)\frac{B(-1/p)}{A(-1/p)}+\left(p-\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}\right)\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}+1-p\right].

(2)

For
f(x)=\frac{x}{1+px}
and
g(y)=\frac{1+py}{y(1+y)}
we have
\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\frac{f(x_{\ell})B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}
equals

(5.47)

-\frac{1}{p}\cdot\left[\left(1-\frac{B(-1/p)}{A(-1/p)}\right)\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}+\left(p-1+\frac{B(-1/p)}{A(-1/p)}\right)\frac{A(-1)}{B(-1)}-p\right].

(3)

For
f(x)=\frac{1}{1+x}
and
g(y)=1
we have

(5.48)

\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\frac{f(x_{\ell})B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}=\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}-1.

Proof.

We will only prove part
(1)
since the arguments for the other parts are similar. For
f(z)=\frac{z}{(1+z)(1+pz)}
and
g(\xi)=\frac{1+p\xi}{\xi}
we consider the following double contour integral:

\oint_{|z|=R}\oint_{|\xi|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\xi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}f(z)g(\xi)\frac{B(z)A(\xi)}{(z-\xi)A(z)B(\xi)}.

Where
R>r
are both large enough so that all the possible poles of the integrand are inside the integral contours. Now since for fixed
r
the integrand is of order
O(R^{-2})
, the double integral goes to
0
as
R\to\infty
. Thus for all
R
large enough the double contour integral equals
0
. On the other hand by the residue theorem we have

(5.49)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=R}\oint_{|\xi|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\xi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}f(z)g(\xi)\frac{B(z)A(\xi)}{(z-\xi)A(z)B(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{f(z)}{z}\frac{B(z)A(0)}{A(z)B(0)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\oint_{|z|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}z}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}f(z)g(y_{k})\frac{B(z)A(y_{k})}{(z-y_{k})A(z)B^{\prime}(y_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{A(y_{k})}{B^{\prime}(y_{k})}\left(\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}\frac{1+py_{k}}{y_{k}(1+y_{k})}-\frac{B(-1/p)}{A(-1/p)}\frac{1}{y_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}f(x_{\ell})g(y_{k})\frac{B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}.

Where in the first equality the first contour integral is
O(R^{-2})
hence
0
for
R
large enough. The single sum over
1\leq k\leq n
can be obtained as the residue terms for the following single contour integral:

\oint_{|\xi|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\xi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{1+p\xi}{\xi(1+\xi)}\frac{A(\xi)}{B(\xi)}=\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}-(1-p)\frac{A(-1)}{B(-1)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{A(y_{k})}{B^{\prime}(y_{k})}\frac{1+py_{k}}{y_{k}(1+y_{k})}.

Here
r
is large enough so that
|\xi|=r
contains all possible poles of the integrand inside. On the other hand by considering the residue at
\infty
we have
\oint_{|\xi|=r}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi i}\frac{1+p\xi}{\xi(1+\xi)}\frac{A(\xi)}{B(\xi)}=p
. Hence

(5.50)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{A(y_{k})}{B^{\prime}(y_{k})}\frac{1+py_{k}}{y_{k}(1+y_{k})}=p-\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}+(1-p)\frac{A(-1)}{B(-1)}.

Similar residue analysis on the contour integral
\oint_{|\xi|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\xi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{A(\xi)}{B(\xi)}
gives

(5.51)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{A(y_{k})}{B^{\prime}(y_{k})}\frac{1}{y_{k}}=1-\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}.

Inserting (
5.50
) and (
5.51
) into (
5.49
) we obtain

(5.52)

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}f(x_{\ell})g(y_{k})\frac{B(x_{\ell})A(y_{k})}{(x_{\ell}-y_{k})A^{\prime}(x_{\ell})B^{\prime}(y_{k})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-p}\cdot\left[\left(p-\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}\right)\frac{B(-1)}{A(-1)}+1-p-\left(1-\frac{A(0)}{B(0)}\right)\frac{B(-1/p)}{A(-1/p)}\right].

This completes the proof of part (1).
∎

5.8.
Proof of Corollary
5.3
and
5.6

Finally we discuss how Corollary
5.3
and
5.6
follows from the Propositions using periodicity. For given
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}
and
0\leq k\leq N-1
, set
\vec{x}^{\prime}:=(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{N}^{\prime})=(x_{N-k+1}+L,\cdots,x_{N}+L,x_{1},\cdots,x_{N-k})
. Then the condition
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N-k}\geq a\}
is the same as
\vec{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N}^{\prime}\geq a\}
. Now consider

\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=\det\left[\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}+j-1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

We move the first
k
columns of the matrix to the end. The resulting determinant equals
(-1)^{k(N-1)}
times the determinant of the matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry has the form
\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j-k}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}^{\prime}+j+k-1}
for
1\leq j\leq N-k
and the form
\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j-k+N}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}^{\prime}+j+k-1+L-N}
for
N-k+1\leq j\leq N
. But since
\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{N}(1+w_{i})^{L-N}=z^{L}
for all
1\leq i\leq N
, we have

\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j-k+N}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}^{\prime}+j+k-1+L-N}=z^{L}\cdot\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{1-j-k}(1+w_{i})^{-x_{N-j+1}^{\prime}+j+k-1}.

After factoring out the common factor
\left(\frac{w_{i}}{1+pw_{i}}\right)^{-k}(1+w_{i})^{k}
from each row and
z^{L}
from the last
k
columns we conclude that

(5.53)

\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})=(-1)^{k(N-1)}z^{kL}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{1+pw_{i}}{w_{i}}\right)^{k}\cdot(1+w_{i})^{k}\right]\Psi_{\vec{x}^{\prime}}^{r}(\vec{w}).

Hence

(5.54)

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}^{(L)}\cap\{x_{N-k}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}}^{r}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k(N-1)}z^{kL}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{1+pw_{i}}{w_{i}}\right)^{k}\cdot(1+w_{i})^{k}\right]\cdot\sum_{\vec{x}^{\prime}\in\Omega_{L,N}\cap\{x_{N}^{\prime}\geq a\}}\Psi_{\vec{x}^{\prime}}^{r}(\vec{w})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k(N-1)}z^{kL}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{1+pw_{i}}{w_{i}}\right)^{k}\cdot(1+w_{i})^{-a+k+1}\right]\cdot\det[w_{i}^{-j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}.

Corollary
5.6
follows from Proposition
5.4
in a similar way.

6.
Large-time asymptotics under relaxation time scale

In this section we discuss the large time limit of the multi-point distribution of dpTASEP(
L,N,\vec{y}
) under the relaxation time scale
t=O(L^{3/2})
. In Theorem
2.4
we state the limit theorem for general initial condition satisfying certain assumptions. Below are the precise assumptions on the initial conditions we need:

6.1.
Assumptions on the initial condition

We now state the assumptions on the sequence of the initial conditions
\vec{y}(L)
under which we prove the limit theorem.
The conditions are in terms of the global energy function and the characteristic function defined in Definition
2.12
.

Recall that the finite-time formula (
2.4
) involves a
m
-fold contour integral with respect to
\vec{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
for
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
where the critical radius
\mathbbm{r}_{c}
is defined in (
2.16
). It turns out that we need to rescale the parameters
z_{i}
to be close to the critical value
\mathbbm{r}_{c}
in order to make the large time limits converge.

Notation 6.1
.

For given complex parameter
z
with
0<|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, we introduce the rescaled parameter
\mathrm{z}
defined by

(6.1)

z^{L}=(-1)^{N}\mathbbm{r}_{c}^{L}\mathrm{z}.

The constraint
0<|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
then translates to
0<|\mathrm{z}|<1
. We introduce a similar rescaling for the parameters
\vec{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
. Then for
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
, the rescaled parameters satisfy
0<|\mathrm{z}_{m}|<\cdots<|\mathrm{z}_{1}|<1
. Throughout the rest of the paper we will always use
z_{i}
to represent the unscaled parameters and
\mathrm{z}_{i}
to represent the rescaled ones satisfying (
6.1
).

Assumption 6.2
.

We assume that the sequence of the initial profiles
\vec{y}=\vec{y}(L)
satisfies the following three conditions as
L\to\infty
.

(A)

(Convergence of global energy)
There exist a constant
r\in(0,1)
and a non-zero function
E_{\mathrm{ic}}(\mathrm{z})
such that for every
0<\epsilon<1/2
,

\mathcal{E}_{\vec{y}}(z)=E_{\mathrm{ic}}(\mathrm{z})\left(1+O(L^{\epsilon-1/2})\right),

uniformly for
|\mathrm{z}|<{r}
as
L\to\infty
.

(B)

(Convergence of characteristic function)
There exist constants
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<1
and a function
\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{ic}}(\eta,\xi;\mathrm{z})

such that for every
0<\epsilon<1/8
,

\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z)=\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{ic}}(\eta,\xi;\mathrm{z})+O(L^{4\epsilon-1/2}),

uniformly for
r_{1}<|\mathrm{z}|<r_{2}
,
u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}^{(\epsilon)}
and
v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}^{(\epsilon)}
as
L\to\infty

where

\xi=\mathcal{M}_{L,\mathrm{left}}(u)\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{z}}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta=\mathcal{M}_{L,\mathrm{right}}(v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{z}}

are the images under the maps defined in Lemma
7.4
.

(C)

(Tail estimates of characteristic function)
Let
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
be same as in (B).
There are constants
\epsilon^{\prime\prime},C^{\prime}>0
such that

(6.2)

|\chi_{\vec{y}}(v,u;z)|\leq C^{\prime}L^{\epsilon^{\prime\prime}},

for all
(v,u)\in\mathcal{R}_{z}\times\mathcal{L}_{z}
for all
r_{1}<|\mathrm{z}|<r_{2}
.

6.2.
Step and flat initial conditions

It turns out that Assumption
6.2
is not easy to check in general. Nevertheless we are able to verify them for at least the classical step and flat initial conditions. The following proposition combined with Theorem
2.4
gives the corresponding limit theorems for dpTASEP starting with step and flat initial conditions.

Proposition 6.3
.

(i)

For the step initial condition
\vec{y}_{\mathrm{step}}=(-1,-2,\cdots-N)
, Assumption
6.2
holds with

(6.3)

E_{\mathrm{step}}(\mathrm{z})=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{step}}(\eta,\xi;\mathrm{z})=1.

(ii)

For the flat initial condition
\vec{y}_{\mathrm{flat}}=(-d,\cdots,-Nd)
, where we assume
d=L/N\in\mathbb{N}
,
Assumption
6.2
holds with

(6.4)

E_{\mathrm{flat}}(\mathrm{z})=(1-\mathrm{z})^{-1/4}e^{-B(\mathrm{z})}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\eta,\xi;\mathrm{z})=e^{-\mathrm{h}(\xi,\mathrm{z})-\mathrm{h}(\eta,\mathrm{z})}\eta(\eta-\xi)\mathbf{1}_{\xi=-\eta}

for
0<|\mathrm{z}|<1
, where
B(\mathrm{z})
is defined in (
6.14
),
\mathrm{h}(\zeta,\mathrm{z})
is defined in (
6.15
) and (
6.16
).

The step case is trivial since by the discussion in Remark
2.13
we have
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{step}}(z)=\chi_{\mathrm{step}}(v,u;z)\equiv 1
. The calculation for the flat case is a bit more involved and we postpone the proof to Section
7.5
.

6.3.
Formula for the limiting distribution

The following formula for the relaxation-time limiting distribution was first obtained in
[
4
]
for the step initial condition and
[
5
]
for more general initial conditions.
The formula involves
\mathrm{C_{\mathrm{ic}}^{\mathrm{per}}}(\vec{\mathrm{z}})
which are limits of
\mathscr{C}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\vec{z})

and operators
\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{per}}
and
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{ic}}^{\mathrm{per}}
which are limits of
\mathscr{K}_{1}^{(L)}
and
\mathscr{K}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}

Definition 7.3

Given
|z|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}

(7.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{L}

(7.4)

(7.5)

to the corresponding roots of the limiting equation.

Lemma 7.4
.

For any

\left|\mathcal{M}_{L,\text{left}}(u)-L^{1/2}c_{0}^{-1}(u-w_{c})\right|\leq L^{-1/2+3\epsilon}\log L,

7.2.
Asymptotics of various products over Bethe roots

In this section we collect all the results involving limits of products of the Bethe roots appearing in the finite-time formula that are independent of the parameters
\vec{y}
,
a_{i}
,
t_{i}
and
k_{i}
. The starting point is the following simple integral formula for the sums of functions evaluated at the left or right Bethe roots.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\phi(w)
be a function analytic in the interior and a neighborhood of
\Lambda_{R}
. Then

(7.9)

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}}\phi(v)=N\phi(0)+\oint_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}\frac{\phi(w)}{J(w)},

where we recall that
J(w)=\frac{w(w+1)(1+pw)}{N+Lw+p(L-N)w^{2}}
. Similarly if
\phi(w)
be a function analytic in the interior and a neighborhood of
\Lambda_{L}
, then

(7.10)

\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}}\phi(u)=(L-N)\phi(-1)+\oint_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}\frac{\phi(w)}{J(w)}.

Here
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}}
are simple closed contours lie in the half-plane
\{w:\mathrm{Re}(w)<w_{c}\}
(respectively
\{w:\mathrm{Re}(w)>w_{c}\}
) with
\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
(respectively
\Lambda_{\mathrm{R}}
) inside. Taking the function
\phi
to be the constant function
1
implies in particular that

(7.11)

\oint_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}\frac{1}{J(w)}=\oint_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}\frac{1}{J(w)}=0.

Proof.

A direct differentiation shows

\displaystyle q_{z}^{\prime}(w)=q_{z}(w)\cdot\left(\frac{N}{w}+\frac{L-N}{w+1}\right)+z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}\cdot\frac{1}{J(w)}.

Hence by the residue theorem we know

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}}\phi(u)=\oint_{\Sigma_{R}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{q_{z}^{\prime}(w)}{q_{z}(w)}\phi(w)=N\phi(0)+\oint_{\Sigma_{R}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{z^{L}(1+pw)^{N}}{q_{z}(w)}\frac{1}{J(w)}\phi(w).

The proof for (
7.10
) is similar.
∎

As taking logarithm transforms products into sums, the following lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma

(7.15)

\begin{split}\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}}\sqrt{w-u}&=(\sqrt{w+1})^{L-N}e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{h}(\zeta,\mathrm{z})}\left(1+O(L^{\epsilon-1/2}\log L)\right)\quad\text{if $\mathrm{Re}(\zeta)\geq 0$},\\

For

Proof.

These estimates are again one-parameter generalizations of Lemma 8.2 and Lemma 8.4 of

Lemma 7.7

Note that

) we conclude that

(7.44)

\chi_{\mathrm{flat}}(v,u;z)=\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\eta,\xi;\mathrm{z})+O(L^{4\epsilon-1/2}),\quad\text{as}\ L\to\infty.

Finally part (C) in Assumption
6.2
is clearly true since by Lemma
7.6
every factor in for
\chi_{\mathrm{flat}}(v,u;z)
is
O(1)
except
\frac{1}{J(v)}
which is
O(L)
. Thus
|\chi_{\mathrm{flat}}(v,u;z)|\leq C\cdot L
and part (C) of Assumption
6.2
is satisfied.
∎

8.
Multi-time distribution of discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}

So far we have been focusing on the so-called relaxation time scale when the period
L
is comparable to
t^{2/3}
and the height fluctuation is critically affected by the boundary conditions. In this section we discuss another important regime, the sub-relaxation time scale
L\gg t^{2/3}
when the particles are not affected by the boundary and are expected to behave the same as when the underlying space has infinite-volume.

8.1.
Discrete time parallel TASEP with large period

Instead of taking the re-scaled time parameter
\tau\to 0
in the relaxation-time limiting distribution formula, we will start with the pre-limit joint distribution formula (
2.4
). The following elementary proposition confirms the intuition that for fixed spatial and time parameters
a_{i},k_{i}
and
t_{i}
, when
L
is large enough, the joint distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\cap_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{k_{i}}(t_{i})\geq a_{i}\})
agrees with the one when the underlying space is the whole integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, hence should be independent of
L
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Given
\vec{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}
. Let
m
be a positive integer and
k_{i}
be integers in
\{1,\cdots,N\}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then for any integers
a_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, as long as
L
is large enough such that

(8.1)

L>y_{1}-y_{N}\quad\text{and }\ L>\max\{a_{1}+k_{1},\cdots,a_{m}+k_{m}\}-y_{N},

we have

(8.2)

\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(\infty)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right),

for any positive integers
t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}
. Here
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}
is the probability with respect to discrete time parallel TASEP on a periodic domain with period
L
and
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{y}}^{(\infty)}
is the probability with respect to an independent discrete time parallel TASEP on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
, both starting with the initial condition satisfying
x_{i}(0)=y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the similar statement for continuous time TASEP as in Lemma 8.1 of
[
4
]
where the particle ordering is reversed. We omit the details.
∎

In principle for every
L
satisfying (
8.1
), the left-hand side of (
8.2
) which has an expression as contour integrals of Fredholm determinant given by (
2.4
) gives a formula for the multi-point joint distribution of discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
for fixed parameters
a_{i},k_{i}
and
t_{i}
. The main obstacle here is the dependence on the extra parameter
L
for the left-hand side of (
8.2
). The key idea to get rid of the dependence on
L
was already illustrated in Proposition
3.4
(iii) and can be summarized as follows:

(1)

By rewriting the sum over the Bethe roots as contour integrals using the residue theorem we get an analytic continuation of the integrand (for the outer integral with respect to
z
) to
\{|z|>0\}
.

(2)

Under proper assumption on the parameter
L
(in fact exactly (
8.1
)), one can further show the integrand is analytic at
z=0
, thus the outer integral with respect to
z
equals the evaluation of the integrand at
z=0
, which turns out to be independent of
L
.

Now as in the above discussion, we would like to send
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}
all to
0
in (
2.4
) while still keep the nested relation
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
. For this we change the integral variables as follows: for given
L
satisfying (
8.1
), we set

(8.3)

\theta_{i}:=\frac{z_{i+1}^{L}}{z_{i}^{L}},\quad\text{for }0\leq i\leq m-1,

where
z_{0}:=1
and
0<|z_{m}|<\cdots<|z_{1}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}
. Then the corresponding relations between
\theta_{i}
’s are precisely
0<|\theta_{0}|<\mathbbm{r}_{c}^{L}:=\mathbf{r}_{c}
and
0<|\theta_{i}|<1
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
. After this change of variable, the finite-time multi-point joint distribution formula (
2.4
) can be rewritten as

(8.4)

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)=\oint\cdots\oint\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\frac{d\theta_{0}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{0}}\cdots\frac{d\theta_{m-1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{m-1}},

where the integral contours are circle centered at origin with radius smaller than
\mathbf{r}_{c}
for
\theta_{0}
and circles centered at origin with radii less than
1
for
\theta_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Here the integrand is defined such that

(8.5)

\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1}):=\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}),\quad\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1}):=\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}),

for any
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}
satisfying the relations
z_{i}^{L}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\theta_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Note that by definition (see Section
2.5
),
\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
and
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
only depend on
z_{1}^{L},\cdots,z_{m}^{L}
so
\hat{\mathscr{C}}^{(L)}
and
\hat{\mathscr{D}}^{(L)}
are well-defined functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathbf{r}_{c})\times(\mathbb{D}_{0}(1))^{m-1}
, where
\mathbb{D}_{0}(r)
is the disk centered at
0
with radius
r
with the origin removed.

8.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

As discussed above, Theorem
2.5
follows immediately once we establish the analyticity of
\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
and
\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
at
\theta_{0}=0
so that we can get rid of the integral with respect to
\theta_{0}
whose value equals the integrand evaluated at
\theta_{0}=0
. The precise statement needed are summarized in the following two lemmas which will be proved in the next two subsections.

Lemma 8.2
.

The function
\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
is in fact analytic in
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{r}_{c})\times(\mathbb{D}(1))^{m-1}
, where
\mathbb{D}(r)
is a disk centered at
0
with radius
r
. Moreover we have

(8.6)

\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(0,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}},

Lemma 8.3
.

For large enough integer
L
satisfying (
8.1
), the function
\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\vec{y}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{r}_{c})\times(\mathbb{D}_{0}(1))^{m-1}
. Moreover we have for any
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\in(\mathbb{D}_{0}(1))^{m-1}

(8.7)

\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(0,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
is defined in Section
2.6
.

Combing Lemma
8.2
and Lemma
8.3
we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{x_{k_{\ell}}(t_{\ell})\geq a_{\ell}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\oint\cdots\oint\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}(0,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{step}}(0,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\frac{d\theta_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}}\cdots\frac{d\theta_{m-1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\oint\cdots\oint\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}\right]\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\frac{d\theta_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}}\cdots\frac{d\theta_{m-1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_{m-1}}.

Finally using (
8.2
) we complete the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

8.3.
Proof of Lemma
8.2

By the definition of the function
\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}
(see Definition
2.14
) we know

(8.8)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}\right]\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell}}}(-u)^{N}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}}}(v+1)^{L-N}}{\Delta(\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}};\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell}})}\right]

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{E_{\ell}(z_{\ell})}{E_{\ell-1}(z_{\ell})}\right]\left[\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}};\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell-1}})}{\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z_{\ell-1}}}(-u)^{N}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z_{\ell}}}(v+1)^{L-N}}\right]

\displaystyle:=\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}\right]\mathscr{A}_{1}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})\mathscr{A}_{2}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})\mathscr{A}_{3}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}).

where
E_{\ell}(z)=\prod_{u\in\mathcal{L}_{z}}(-u)^{-k_{\ell}}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{z}}(v+1)^{-a_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}(pv+1)^{t_{\ell}-k_{\ell}}
and the functions
\mathscr{A}_{i}
represents the products inside each brackets. The parameters
\theta_{i}
and
z_{i}
are related by the equations
z_{i}^{L}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\theta_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Now if we send
\theta_{0}\to 0
, then all the
z_{i}
’s will converge to
0
. As a result, all the roots in
\mathcal{L}_{z_{i}}
converge to
-1
while all the roots in
\mathcal{R}_{z_{i}}
converge to
0
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
. It is then easy to see that for
j=1,2,3

(8.9)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{i}\to 0\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\mathscr{A}_{j}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})=1

is well-defined and hence
\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
can be analytically continued to the point
\theta_{0}=0
with

(8.10)

\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(L)}(0,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{\ell}}.

8.4.
Proof of Lemma
8.3

This Lemma is an analogue of Lemma 5.2 of
[
15
]
and the proof is similar so we omit most of the details. What we want to prove is an identity between two Fredholm determinants, with the first one acting on discrete sets consisting of roots of certain polynomials and the other one acting on continuous contours. By looking at the standard series expansions of both Fredholm determinants, it suffices to show that every term in the two series expansions matches. These type of identities between multiple sums over roots of certain algebraic equations and multiple contour integrals can be understood as a highly nontrivial consequence of the residue theorem, which is stated and proved with enough generality for our purpose in Proposition 4.4 of
[
15
]
. We take
q(w)=w^{N}(w+1)^{L-N}(1+pw)^{-N}
in the proposition and follow the same argument as in Lemma 5.2 of
[
15
]
.

8.5.
KPZ scaling limit

In this final section we briefly discuss the large-time asymptotics for the multi-time distribution of discrete time parallel TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
under the
1:2:3
KPZ scaling. For completeness we first recall the definition of the limiting distribution function
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
which essentially agrees with the function
F_{\mathrm{step}}
appearing in Theorem 2.20 of
[
15
]
with a slight different choices of the parameters. The structure of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
is similar to the finite-time distribution as contour integrals of a Fredholm determinant
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
.

8.5.1.
Spaces of the operators

Given integer
m\geq 1
. We first define the contour in the complex plane

\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}:=\{w=m+re^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}:r\geq 0\}\cup\{w=m+re^{\frac{-\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}:r\geq 0\},

oriented from
e^{-\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\infty
to
e^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\infty
. Then for
2\leq j\leq m
, we define the contours

\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{\pm}:=\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}\pm(j-1)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:w=\tilde{w}\pm(j-1)\text{ for some }\tilde{w}\in\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}\}.

The left contours
\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{\pm}
,
j=2,\cdots,m
and
\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}
are defined simply as the reflections of the corresponding right contours about the
y
-axis, with orientation from
e^{-\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\infty
to
e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\infty
. We further denote

\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{L}}:=\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{+}\cup\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{-},\qquad\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}:=\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{+}\cup\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{-},\qquad\Gamma_{j}=\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{L}}\cup\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}},\qquad j=1,\cdots,m,

and

\mathbb{S}_{1}:=\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}\cup\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}\cup\cdots\cup\begin{cases}\Gamma_{m,\mathrm{L}},&\text{ if $m$ is odd},\\
\Gamma_{m,\mathrm{R}},&\text{ if $m$ is even},\end{cases}

and

\mathbb{S}_{2}:=\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}\cup\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}\cup\cdots\cup\begin{cases}\Gamma_{m,\mathrm{R}},&\text{ if $m$ is odd},\\
\Gamma_{m,\mathrm{L}},&\text{ if $m$ is even}.\end{cases}

8.5.2.
Operators
\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
and
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}

Now we introduce the operators
\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
and
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
to define
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}
in Theorem
2.7
.

Definition 8.4
.

We define

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})=\det\left(I-\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}\right),

where the two operators

\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{1}),\qquad\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{1})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{2})

are defined by the kernels

(8.11)

\mathrm{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{i}(j)+\delta_{i}(j+(-1)^{i})\right)\frac{\mathrm{f}_{i}(\zeta)}{\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}(j)P_{j}(\zeta^{\prime})\quad\text{for }\zeta^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}\cap\mathbb{S}_{2},\ \zeta\in\Gamma_{i}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1},

and

(8.12)

\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta):=\left(\delta_{j}(i)+\delta_{j}(i-(-1)^{j})\right)\frac{\mathrm{f}_{j}(\zeta^{\prime})}{\zeta^{\prime}-\zeta}Q_{2}^{(\infty)}(i)P_{i}(\zeta)\quad\text{for }\zeta\in\Gamma_{i}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1},\ \zeta^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}\cap\mathbb{S}_{2},

where
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Here the function
\mathrm{f}_{i}(\zeta)
is the same as the one appearing in the kernels for periodic case defined in (
6.17
) and
Q_{1}^{(\infty)}
and
Q_{2}^{(\infty)}
are defined in (
2.39
) and (
2.40
). We also set

(8.13)

P_{j}(\zeta):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1-\theta_{j-1}},\quad&\zeta\in\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{+}\cup\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{+},\quad j=2,\cdots,m\\
\frac{-\theta_{j-1}}{1-\theta_{j-1}},\quad&w\in\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{L}}^{-}\cup\Gamma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{-},\quad j=2,\cdots,m\\
1,\quad&w\in\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}\cup\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}.\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

The proof is a standard steepest descent analysis and most of the key ingredients are similar to Lemma
7.8
so we only provide the essential calculations. First it is not difficult to see that for any nonzero constants
c_{1},\cdots,c_{m}
, the Fredholm determinant part
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}
is invariant if we replace the functions
F_{i}
appearing in the kernels by
\tilde{F}_{i}:=c_{i}F_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. For our purpose we set
c_{i}=\frac{1}{F_{i}(w_{c})}
where
w_{c}=-\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{q}}
. We point out that this agrees with the critical point for periodic cases defined in (
2.15
) when
\rho=1/2
so the notation is consistent. To ensure the kernels have sufficiently fast decay on each variable we make the same conjugation as in (
7.29
) by setting

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{1}^{(\infty)}(w,w^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{i}(j)+\delta_{i}(j+(-1)^{i})\right)\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{f}_{i}(w)}\sqrt{\tilde{f}_{j}(w^{\prime})}}{w-w^{\prime}}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}(j)P_{j}(w^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(w,w^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{j}(i)+\delta_{j}(i+(-1)^{j})\right)\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{f}_{j}(w^{\prime})}\sqrt{\tilde{f}_{i}(w)}}{w-w^{\prime}}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}(j)P_{i}(w),

for
w^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{j}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2},\ w\in\Sigma_{i}\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Here
\tilde{f}_{i}
is obtained by replacing
F_{i}
with
\tilde{F}_{i}
in (
2.35
). We also conjugate the limiting kernels in a similar way:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{K}}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{i}(j)+\delta_{i}(j+(-1)^{i})\right)\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{f}_{i}(\zeta)}\sqrt{\mathrm{f}_{j}(\zeta^{\prime})}}{\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}(j)P_{j}(\zeta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{K}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}):=\left(\delta_{j}(i)+\delta_{j}(i+(-1)^{j})\right)\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{f}_{j}(\zeta^{\prime})}\sqrt{\mathrm{f}_{i}(\zeta)}}{\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}(j)P_{i}(\zeta),

for
\zeta^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}\cap\mathbb{S}_{2},\ \zeta\in\Gamma_{i}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}
. These conjugations do not change the Fredholm determinants.

Now a straightforward Taylor expansion shows that for
w=w_{c}+\frac{q^{1/4}}{1+\sqrt{q}}\zeta T^{-1/3}
with
|\zeta|\leq T^{\epsilon/4}
for
0<\epsilon<1/3
we have

(8.14)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{i}(w)}{F_{i}(w_{c})}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(k_{i}\log\left(\frac{w}{w_{c}}\right)-(a_{i}+k_{i})\log\left(\frac{w+1}{w_{c}+1}\right)+(t_{i}-k_{i})\log\left(\frac{pw+1}{pw_{c}+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{3}\tau_{i}\zeta^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{i}\zeta^{2}+\mathrm{x}_{i}\zeta+O(T^{\epsilon-\frac{1}{3}})\right),

where the parameters
a_{i},k_{i}
and
t_{i}
satisfies (
2.11
). On the other for
w
far away from
w_{c}
,
\frac{F_{i}(w)}{F_{i}(w_{c})}
decays or grows exponentially fast. Hence it is straightforward to show that for
w=-\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{q}}+\frac{q^{1/4}}{1+\sqrt{q}}\zeta T^{-1/3}
and
0<\epsilon<1/3
we have

(8.15)

\tilde{f}_{j}(w)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{f}_{j}(\zeta)(1+O(T^{\epsilon-1/3}))\quad&\text{if }|\zeta|\leq T^{\epsilon/4},\\
O(e^{-cT^{\epsilon/2}})\quad&\text{if }|\zeta|\geq T^{\epsilon/4}.\end{cases}

Now we deform the contours
\Sigma_{j}
to be sufficiently close to the critical points
w_{c}=-\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{q}}
such that locally they look like the limiting contours
\Gamma_{j}
. More concretely we deform
\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{j,\mathrm{R}}^{+}\cap\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w-w_{c}|\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{q}}{q^{1/4}}T^{\epsilon/4-1/3}\}

\displaystyle=\{w_{c}+T^{-1/3}(m+j-1+re^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}):0\leq r\leq T^{\epsilon/4}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{w_{c}+T^{-1/3}(m+j-1+re^{-\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}):0\leq r\leq T^{\epsilon/4}\},

and similar for other contours. Then by (
8.15
) it is straightforward to show that

(1)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<|\theta_{i}|<1
we have

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{1}^{(\infty)}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}\right)^{n}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\tilde{\mathrm{K}}_{1}^{\mathrm{kpz}}\tilde{\mathrm{K}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}\right)^{n}.

(2)

There exists constant
C>0
independent of
T
and
n
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\left|\oint_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\cdots\oint_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\det\left[\left(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{1}^{(\infty)}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}\right)(w_{i},w_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\cdots\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w_{n}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\right|\leq C^{n}.

Now (1) and (2) immediately implies
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{(\infty)}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})\text{ converges to }\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{step}}^{\mathrm{kpz}}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{m-1})
locally uniformly for
0<|\theta_{i}|<1
as
T\to\infty
, thus proving Theorem
2.7
.

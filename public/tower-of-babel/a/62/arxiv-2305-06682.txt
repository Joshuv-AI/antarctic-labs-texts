Regularization properties of dual subgradient flow
Vassilis Apidopoulos, Cesare Molinari, Lorenzo Rosasco, Silvia Villa

Abstract
Dual gradient descent combined with early stopping represents an efficient alternative to the Tikhonov variational approach when the regularizer is strongly convex. However, for many relevant applications, it is crucial to deal with regularizers which are only convex. In this setting, the dual problem is non smooth, and dual gradient descent cannot be used. In this paper, we study the regularization properties of a subgradient dual flow, and we show that the proposed procedure achieves the same recovery accuracy as penalization methods, while being more efficient from the computational perspective.



I
Introduction

We study a continuous dynamical system to recover an
unknown signal
x_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
from noisy linear measurements

f^{\delta}=Ax_{*}+\epsilon.

(1)

In this equation,
f^{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the vector of measurements,
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a linear operator and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the noise. We assume that the noise is bounded, namely that
\|\epsilon\|\leq\delta
. Moreover, we consider the case
d<p
and from the modeling point of view we assume that among all the solutions of the equation
Ax_{*}=Ax
, the vector
x_{*}
minimizes a given function
J
. The main example we have in mind is sparsity, where
J
is the
\ell_{1}
norm. Such problems have been widely studied in machine learning
[
16
]
, inverse problems
[
14
,
3
]
, and signal processing/compressed sensing
[
11
,
15
]
. The most classical approach is based on explicit (Tikhonov/variational) regularization, which is obtained by the minimization of the weighed sum of two terms: a penalization of the equality constraints
Ax=f^{\delta}
(data fit term); and the regularizer
J
. This requires the selection of an appropriate trade-off parameter, which is typically the computational bottleneck of the regularization approach. Motivated by the stability and the reconstruction properties of first order methods in machine learning, in this paper we follow a different route, based on the so-called
implicit regularization
(iterative regularization in the inverse problems literature). Such an approach combines regularization properties with computational efficiency, since it is based on the early stopping of an iterative procedure used to minimize
J
among the solutions of (
1
). The most well-known example is gradient descent, a.k.a. Landweber iteration
[
14
]
. Several extensions of this basic approach have been proposed to deal with more general regularizations, which are strongly convex
[
18
]
, or just convex
[
19
,
20
]
. These regularization methods depend on the choice of a suitable discrete algorithm or continuous dynamical system. In this paper we focus on the convex case, and we propose to find a stable reconstruction of
x_{*}
by early stopping a subgradient flow in the dual space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We prove that such a procedure has the same reconstruction properties of Tikhonov regularized solutions in terms of dependence from the error, while being cheaper from the computational perspective. This problem has been studied already in
[
8
,
9
,
5
]
. We leave the comments and the comparison of our work with the previous literature after the main results.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
we present the main problem, the setting considered and the precise assumptions on the objects involved. In Section
III
we present the dual subgradient flow and the main theoretical results about iterative regularization through early-stopping. Some comments on the assumptions, on the dynamical system and on the results are reported in Section
IV
. In Section
V
we provide the convergence and stability analysis for the dual subgradient flow. We conclude with a summary of the work done and some possible future developments in Section
VI
.

II
Problem and assumptions

We assume that the unknown signal
x_{*}
is a solution of the constrained optimization problem

\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}{\min}\quad J(x)~:~~Ax=f,

(2)

where
f:=Ax_{*}
is the exact datum, typically not available in practice.
We make the following general assumptions on the objects involved.

Assumption II.1
.

The operator
A:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is linear,
J:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper, convex and lower semi-continuous function and
f
is a given vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We illustrate examples of regularizers
J
which are relevant in applications and satisfy the previous assumptions.

•

When
J(\cdot)=\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
, problem (
2
) is used to find the minimal norm solution to the linear equation
Ax=f
. The unique minimizer is given by the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of
A
applied to the exact data, namely
A^{\dagger}f
(see e.g.
[
14
]
).

•

The choice of
J(\cdot)=\|\cdot\|_{1}
is well-known to induce sparsity in the solution. Indeed, it is the convex relaxation of the
0-
seminorm, that counts the non-zero elements in a vector
[
11
]
.

•

J(\cdot)=\|\cdot\|_{TV}
is the total variation regularizer. It is used in image reconstruction to maintain sharp edges between different zones with constant color
[
23
]
.

•

J(\cdot)=\|\cdot\|{}_{*}
is the nuclear norm, namely the
1-
norm of the singular values of a matrix. It is an approximation of the rank and so its minimization is used to maximize the linear correlation between rows or columns
[
10
]
.

Except for the
2-
norm, the examples above involve regularizers that are neither differentiable nor strongly-convex, but only lower-semicontinuous and convex.

We also introduce the dual problem associated to the exact problem (
2
), namely

\underset{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\min}\quad\mathcal{D}(y):=J^{\star}(-A^{*}y)+\langle f,y\rangle,

(3)

where
J^{\star}
is the Fenchel conjugate of
J
, defined as
J^{\star}(x):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\ \langle x,z\rangle-J(z)
. We say that a pair
\left(\bar{x},\bar{y}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
primal-dual solution
of (
2
)-(
3
) if it satisfies the following KKT conditions:

\begin{cases}-A^{*}\bar{y}\in\partial J(\bar{x})\\
~A\bar{x}=f,\end{cases}

(4)

where
\partial J(x)
is the subdifferential of
J
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, namely the set-valued operator defined by

\partial J(x):=\left\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\ J(z)\geq J(x)+\langle x^{\prime},z-x\rangle\ \forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\right\}.

In the next, we assume the existence of a primal-dual solution.

Assumption II.2
.

There exists an optimal primal-dual pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for the exact problem, namely satisfying (
4
).

The previous assumption is classical. Some comments are given in the discussion paragraph
IV-A
.

Notice that while we are searching to approximate a solution of problem (
2
), the related datum
f
is typically not available in practice. In this setting, the aim of regularization is to recover, in a stable way with respect to the noise level
\delta
, an approximate solution to (
2
) where only a noisy version of
f
, denoted by
f^{\delta}
, is available. As already anticipated in the Introduction, we focus on the worst-case deterministic scenario, in which
\delta\geq 0
denotes the noise level. More precisely, we make the following assumption on the inexact data.

Assumption II.3
.

For some
\delta\geq 0
, the element
f^{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

\|f^{\delta}-f\|\leq\delta.

(5)

Any regularization method should be such that when
\delta
approaches 0, the regularized solution approaches the ideal vector
x_{*}
.
In the next section, we will show that the trajectories of a dual dynamical system can be used to build a regularization method.

III
Dual flow and main results

In this section we describe the proposed implicit/iterative regularization method to approximate a solution of (
2
) in a stable way with respect to the noise level in the data
f
. Then we present the main theoretical results of the paper.

Given
f^{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying Assumption
II.3
, we define the inexact dual function as

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(y):=J^{\star}(-A^{*}y)+\langle f^{\delta},y\rangle.

Consider
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\partial\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(y_{0})\neq\emptyset
. For
t\geq 0
we are interested in the behavior of the trajectory
\{y(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by the subgradient flow on
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
, i.e.:
y(0)=y_{0}
and, for every
t>0
,

\dot{y}(t)+\partial\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(y(t))\ni 0.

(6)

In the next, we assume a technical condition on the composition of
J^{\star}
with the linear operator
A
, that will allow us to re-write the dual subgradient flow as a coupled dynamical system.

Assumption III.1
.

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following chain rule equality holds:

\partial\left[J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}\cdot)\right](y)=-A\partial J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}y).

(7)

The previous assumption is classical. Some comments are given in the discussion paragraph
IV-A
. By using the chain rule in (
7
), the differential inclusion in (
6
) is equivalent to

\dot{y}(t)\in A\partial J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}y(t))-f^{\delta}

and so to the following dynamical system:

\begin{cases}x(t)&\in\partial J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}y(t))\\
\dot{y}(t)&=Ax(t)-f^{\delta}.\end{cases}

(8)

More discussion on the dual flow can be found in paragraph
IV-B
.

In order to introduce the main results, we recall the definition of the
Bregman divergence
related to
J
. For
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
u\in\partial J(x_{2})
, the Bregman divergence of
J
between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
is given by

D^{u}(x_{1},x_{2}):=J(x_{1})-J(x_{2})-\left\langle{u},{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right\rangle.

(9)

To show that
x(t)
, the trajectory of the dual subgradient flow in (
8
), is indeed approaching a solution of the exact problem (
2
), one option is to show bounds for the classical duality gap (see the definition in (
19
) in the discussion section). In this work, we follow a different strategy, proving rates for both the
Lagrangian gap

D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(x(t),\bar{x})
and the
feasibility gap

\|Ax(t)-f\|
. Indeed, if
D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(x^{\prime},\bar{x})=0
and
Ax^{\prime}=f
for some
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then
x^{\prime}
is a solution for problem (
2
) (see e.g.
[
19
, Lemma 5]
). So the combination of the two quantities is indeed a measure of (primal) optimality for the point
x^{\prime}
. We are now ready to give the main results of the paper, concerning the regularization properties of the dual subgradient flow (
8
).

Theorem III.1

(Rates)
.

Let Assumptions
II.1
,
II.2
,
II.3
and
III.1
hold true and
(x(t),y(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a solution of (
8
). Consider the tail-average sequence

\hat{x}(t):=\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}x(s)ds.

(10)

Then the following rates of convergence hold true for all
t\geq 0
: for
C_{0}:=\|y(0)-\bar{y}\|
,

D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(\hat{x}(t),\bar{x})\leq\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{t}+2C_{0}\delta+\delta^{2}t;

(11)

\|A\hat{x}(t)-f\|^{2}\leq\frac{2C_{0}^{2}}{t^{2}}+6C_{0}\frac{\delta}{t}+6\delta^{2};

(12)

D^{-A^{\ast}y(t)}(\bar{x},x(t))\leq\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{2t}+\frac{3C_{0}}{2}\delta+\frac{3}{2}\delta^{2}t.

(13)

The proof of the Theorem
III.1
can be found in the related paragraph
V-C
. From Theorem
III.1
, we deduce the following early-stopping result.

Corollary III.1

(Early stopping)
.

Let Assumptions
II.1
,
II.2
,
II.3
and
III.1
hold true,
(x(t),y(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a solution of (
8
) and set
\hat{x}(t):=\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}x(s)ds
. Then, for
t^{\ast}:=\frac{1}{\delta}\|y(0)-\bar{y}\|
, it holds:

D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(\hat{x}(t^{*}),\bar{x})\leq 4\|y(0)-\bar{y}\|\delta,

(14)

\|A\hat{x}(t^{*})-f\|\leq\sqrt{14}\delta.

(15)

For the comments on the previous results, see the discussion paragraph
IV-C
.

IV
Discussion

In this section, we discuss the assumptions used in our analysis; the proposed iterative regularization method, the dual subgradient flow; and the theoretical results obtained. We also compare our results first with the ones for the same dual flow but under stronger assumptions, namely when
J
is strongly convex; and then with the results given by explicit/variational regularization by Tikhonov path. We conclude with some remarks about possible discretization of the dual subgradient flow, in order to get optimization algorithms.

IV-A

Discussion on the assumptions

Assumption
II.2
and
III.1
are classical in saddle-point problems and they hold, for instance, under the so called
qualification conditions

(see e.g.
[
21
, Proposition
3.28
, Theorem 3.30, Corollary 3.31 and Theorem 3.51]
). In particular, we recall that a sufficient condition for (
7
) is the existence of a point
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
J^{\star}
is continuous at
-A^{*}y_{0}
.

Regarding Assumption
II.2
, note that, if
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a primal-dual solution, in particular
\bar{x}
is a minimizer for (
2
) and
\bar{y}
for (
3
). The opposite is not true, in general. We briefly discuss here sufficient conditions in order to ensure existence of a primal-dual solution.
If there exists a (dual) solution
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to problem (
3
) and the condition in (
7
) holds, then Assumption
II.2
holds true. Indeed, by generalized Fermat’s rule (see, for instance,
[
21
, Theorem 3.24]
),
\bar{y}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\partial\left[J^{\star}(-A^{*}\cdot)+\langle f,\cdot\rangle\right](\bar{y})

\displaystyle=-A\partial J^{\star}(-A^{*}\bar{y})+f.

Denoting by
\bar{x}
an element in
\partial J^{\star}(-A^{*}\bar{y})
for which the previous inclusion is satisfied and using the fact that
(\partial J^{\star})^{-1}=\partial J
(see, for instance,
[
21
, Proposition 3.59]
, we get that the pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a primal-dual solution accordingly to the KKT conditions in (
4
).

On the other hand, if there exists a primal solution
\bar{x}
and a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
Ax_{0}=f
(feasibility) and where
J
is continuous, then there exists a dual solution
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is an optimal primal-dual pair. Indeed, it is easy to show that, under the general Assumption
II.1
, the following chain rule holds:

\partial\left[\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}\circ A\right](\cdot)=A^{*}\partial\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}(A\cdot),

where
\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}
denotes the classical indicator function of convex analysis; namely,
\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}(y)
is
0
if
y=f
and
+\infty
otherwise. Moreover, at the point
x_{0}
, the function
\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}\circ A
assumes a bounded value and
J
is continuous. So also the sum rule for subdifferentials holds (see, for instance,
[
21
, Theorem 3.30]
). Then the primal solution
\bar{x}
satisfies
A\bar{x}=f
and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\partial\left[J+\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}\circ A\right](\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\partial J(\bar{x})+A^{*}\partial\iota_{\left\{f\right\}}(A\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\partial J(\bar{x})+A^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The previous means precisely that there exists a point
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
satisfies the KKT conditions in (
4
).

IV-B

Discussion on the dual flow

If
f^{\delta}
belongs to the range of operator
A
, a natural approach to deal with problem (
2
) is to consider the subgradient flow on the (noisy) primal objective function, i.e. on
x\mapsto J(x)+\iota_{\{f^{\delta}\}}(Ax)
. Under standard qualification conditions, for
t\geq 0
, the primal flow reads as

\dot{x}(t)+\partial J(x(t))+A^{\ast}\partial\iota_{\{f^{\delta}\}}(Ax(t))\ni 0.

(16)

Nevertheless, we shall stress out that this system forces the trajectory to be feasible accordingly to the inexact constraint (namely,
Ax(t)=f^{\delta}
for every
t\geq 0
), which is not desirable in general. Furthermore, the assumption
f^{\delta}\in\text{Im}(A)
is not necessary in our setting for considering the dual flow (
6
), contrarily to the primal one (
16
). These are the main motivations for studying the dual flow (
6
) instead of (
16
).

Notice that, if the function
J
is twice differentiable with invertible Hessian, the system (
8
) can be also written as

\dot{x}(t)=\left[\nabla^{2}J(x(t))\right]^{-1}A^{*}\left(f^{\delta}-Ax(t)\right).

(17)

So, under strong regularity assumptions that we do not suppose in this work, the system (
8
) may be seen as a generalized mirror flow associated to the least-squares problem
Ax=f^{\delta}
, with mirror function given by
J
.

IV-C

Discussion on the results

In order to explain the results in Theorem
III.1
and Corollary
III.1
, we first recall the definition of the Lagrangian functional related with (
2
):

\mathcal{L}(x,y):=J(x)+\left\langle{y},{A{x}-f}\right\rangle.

Note that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a primal-dual solution accordingly to (
4
) if and only if it is a saddle-point for the Lagrangian; namely if and only if, for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y)\leq\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{y}).

(18)

Then the quantity

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},y)-\mathcal{L}(x,y^{\prime}),

(19)

called
duality gap
, is a reasonable measure of optimality for the point
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see, for instance,
[
12
]
. From (
18
), another interesting quantity to measure the optimality of
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is

\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},\bar{y})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

(20)

where
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a given primal-dual solution. The term
Lagrangian gap
, used above for
D^{-A^{*}\bar{y}}(x^{\prime},\bar{x})
, comes from the fact that it corresponds to (
20
): indeed, as
A\bar{x}=f
, we have

\displaystyle D^{-A^{*}\bar{y}}(x^{\prime},\bar{x})

\displaystyle=J(x^{\prime})-J(\bar{x})-\langle-A^{*}\bar{y},x^{\prime}-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},\bar{y})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

In
[
9
]
, the authors proved similar results to the ones in Corollary
III.1
, but only for
D^{-A^{*}y(t)}(\bar{x},x(t))
. The previous quantity is not directly related to optimality of the primal variable
x(t)
if the function
J
is not strongly-convex. The main difference between our result and the one in
[
9
]
is that we are able to control two different quantities: the
Lagrangian gap

D^{-A^{*}\bar{y}}(\cdot,\bar{x})
, with respect to a primal-dual solution pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
; and the
feasibility gap

\|A\cdot-f\|
, with respect to the exact data
f
. As already remarked in the previous section, the combination of these two quantities is a good measure of optimality. Indeed, when they are both zero at
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then
x^{\prime}
is a solution of the primal optimization problem (
2
); see
[
19
,
20
]
. The bounds that we derive show that the dual subgradient flow (
8
) on the inexact data
f^{\delta}
, if opportunely early-stopped, can be used to implicitly regularize the solution of a linear inverse problem affected by worst-case deterministic noise. More precisely, the upper-bounds in Theorem
III.1
on the Lagrangian gap and the feasibility are composed by the sum of two different terms. The first term is related with the
optimization
procedure and so it is vanishing for
t\to+\infty
. The second one is related with
stability
with respect to the error on the data. This term is bounded of order
\delta
for the feasibility and it is diverging for
t\to+\infty
as
\delta t
in the case of the Lagrangian gap. Minimizing the upper-bound with respect to
t
suggests the choice of an optimal stopping time
t^{*}=C/\delta
for some constant
C>0
, as shown in Corollary
III.1
. For this choice, both the quantities
D^{-A^{*}\bar{y}}(\hat{x}(t^{*}),\bar{x})
and
\|A\hat{x}(t^{*})-f\|
are of order
\delta
, where
\hat{x}
is the tail averaged trajectory. This reflects the idea of stability by iterative regularization: an error of order
\delta
on the data is reflected to an error of order
\delta
on the solution, without amplifications.

IV-D

Comparison with results for strongly convex

When
J
is strongly convex with constant
\mu>0
,
J^{\star}
is smooth (see, for instance,
[
2
, Proposition 14.2]
); meaning that
J^{\star}
is differentiable with Lipschitz continuous gradient. Then, the differential inclusion in (
8
) becomes the classical differential equation

\begin{cases}x(t)&=\ \nabla J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}y(t))\\
\dot{y}(t)&=\ Ax(t)-f^{\delta}.\end{cases}

Moreover, the Bregman divergence verifies

D^{-A^{*}\bar{y}}(x,\bar{x})\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}

and so the Lagrangian gap controls the distance to the solution.
In particular, our bounds on the Lagrangian gap in Theorem
III.1
and Corollaries
III.1
automatically transfer to the quantity
\|\hat{x}(t)-\bar{x}\|
. It can be shown that similar bounds holds indeed for the non-averaged iterate
x(t)
. See
[
14
,
8
]
, the recent work
[
17
]
and, for the discrete case,
[
18
]
.

IV-E

Comparison with Tikhonov path

Under Assumptions
II.1
,
II.2
and
II.3
, the Tikhonov regularization path for
s\geq 0
is defined by

u(s)\in\mathrm{argmin}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left\{J(u)+\frac{s}{2}\|Au-f^{\delta}\|^{2}\right\}

(21)

or, equivalently, by

\begin{split}0&\in\partial\left(J(\cdot)+\frac{s}{2}\|A\cdot-f\|^{2}\right)\left(u(s)\right)\\
&=\partial J(u(s))+sA^{*}\left(Au(s)-f^{\delta}\right).\end{split}

(22)

Denoting
v(s):=Au(s)-f^{\delta}
, we have that
-sA^{*}v(s)\in\partial J(u(s))
and so that
(u(s),v(s))
satisfies the system

\begin{cases}u(s)\ \in&\partial J^{\star}(-sA^{*}v(s));\\
v(s)\ =&Au(s)-f^{\delta}.\end{cases}

(23)

Assuming enough regularity on the functional
J
and differentiating (
22
) with respect to the variable
s
, we get
0=\nabla^{2}J(u(s))\dot{u}(s)+A^{*}\left(Au(s)-f^{\delta}\right)+sA^{*}A\dot{u}(s)
and

\dot{u}(s)=\left[\nabla^{2}J(u(s))+sA^{*}A\right]^{-1}A^{*}\left(f^{\delta}-Au(s)\right).

(24)

Notice the similarity between (
8
)-(
17
) and (
23
)-(
24
). While (
8
) is a dynamical inclusion, the inclusion in (
23
) does not involve any derivative.

Analogously to Theorem
III.1
and Corollary
III.1
, for the Tikhonov path we have the following bounds and regularization properties.

Theorem IV.1
.

Under Assumptions
II.1
,
II.2
and
II.3
, let
s>0
and
u(s)
be the Tikhonov path defined in (
22
) and
v(s)=Au(s)-f^{\delta}
. Then the following bounds hold true:

D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(u(s),\bar{x})+D^{-sA^{\ast}v(s)}(\bar{x},u(s))\leq\frac{1}{4s}\left(\|\bar{y}\|+s\delta\right)^{2};

(25)

\|Au(s)-f\|\leq\frac{\|\bar{y}\|}{s}+\delta.

(26)

In particular, for
s^{*}:=\|\bar{y}\|/\delta
,

D^{-A^{\ast}\bar{y}}(u(s^{*}),\bar{x})+D^{-sA^{\ast}v(s^{*})}(\bar{x},u(s^{*}))\leq\|\bar{y}\|\delta

and

\|Au(s^{*})-f\|\leq 2\delta.

The proof of Theorem
IV.1
can be seen as a straightforward generalization of classic results (see for instance
[
14
,
8
]
) and is given for completeness in Section
VI-A
.
Note the similarities and differences between the results for the iterative (implicit) regularization properties given by the dual subgradient flow in Theorem
III.1
and Corollary
III.1
and the ones for the Tikhonov (explicit) regularization in Theorem
IV.1
. The main difference is that for Tikhonov path the results hold for the trajectory
u(s)
without averaging. On the other hand, the final results are qualitatively very similar. Indeed, in the two cases the best early-stopping

or

IV-F

Discretization

There are two possibilities in order to obtain algorithms by time discretization of the flow defined in (
8
). The first option is a backward-implicit discretization, that leads to the following scheme: given
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{J^{\ast}(-A^{*}y)+\langle f^{\delta},y\rangle+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|y-y_{k}\|^{2}\right\};

namely, the proximal-point algoritm on the inexact dual function
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
. Another way to see the implicit discretization, on the primal variable, is the following: consider
-A^{\ast}y_{k+1}\in\partial J(x_{k+1}),

where

-A^{*}y_{k+1}=-A^{*}y_{k}-\gamma A^{*}\left(Ax_{k+1}-f^{\delta}\right).

The relations above imply that

0\in\partial J(x_{k+1})+\gamma A^{*}Ax_{k+1}-\gamma A^{*}f^{\delta}+A^{*}y_{k}
.
The previous means that
x_{k+1}
is a solution to the optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left\{J(x)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|Ax-\left(f^{\delta}-\frac{y_{k}}{\gamma}\right)\|^{2}\right\}.

Note that also in this case each iterative step is implicit and so computationally expensive. This is also known as Bregman iteration and has been studied in
[
9
]
.

On the other hand, a forward-explicit discretization of (
8
) leads to a subgradient-type algorithm of the following form: pick
x_{k}\in\partial J^{\ast}(-A^{\ast}y_{k})
and update the dual variable by

y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\gamma\left(Ax_{k}-f^{\delta}\right).

(27)

We leave the study of the regularization properties of this algorithm for future work. We remark here that in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
another method, namely the primal-dual algorithm
[
12
,
13
,
24
,
22
]
, has been studied in order to perform iterative regularization for problem (
2
).

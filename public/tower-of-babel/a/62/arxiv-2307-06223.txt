Residues of quadratic Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series
Adrian Diaconu, Bogdan Ion, Vicenţiu Paşol, Alexandru A. Popa

Abstract
. We give explicit formulas for the residue of the Chinta-Gunnells average attached to a finite irreducible root system, at the polar divisor corresponding to a simple short root. The formula describes the residue in terms of the average attached to the root subsystem orthogonal to the relevant simple root. As a consequence, we obtain similar formulas for the residues of quadratic Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series over the rational function field and over the Gaussian field. The residue formula also allows us to obtain a new expression for the Chinta-Gunnells average of a finite irreducible root system, as an average over a maximal parabolic subgroup of a rational function that has an explicit description reflecting the combinatorics of the root system.

Introduction

1.1.

The genesis of the concept of Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series (WMDS) can be traced back to the work of Goldfeld and Hoffstein
[
GH85
]
, where (using present terminology) a quadratic double Dirichlet series over
\mathbb{Q}
, of Cartan type
A_{2}
, was constructed as the Mellin transform of an Eisenstein series of half-integral weight for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(4)

[
S
]
. Other examples, obtained as integral transforms of Eisenstein series (and other automorphic objects) on the metaplectic double covers of
{\rm GL}_{3}
and
{\rm GSp(4)}
, were investigated; for the relevant results, see the survey
[
BFH96
]
and the references therein. The main application at the time was to obtain non-vanishing results for quadratic twists of central values of automorphic
L
-functions and their derivatives. In higher rank, such constructions, based on integral transforms of Eisenstein series on covers of reductive groups are difficult to obtain and analyze. These initial investigations revealed the structural properties of such multiple Dirichlet series, and it has gradually emerged
[

,
BFH96
,
BFH04
,
FF
]
that these properties can be used to define a class of multiple Dirichlet series without making use of integral transforms of automorphic forms.
The general principles used to construct and analyze multiple Dirichlet series associated to finite reduced root systems were laid out in
[

,
BBFH07
,
CG
,
CG1
]
.

1.9.

To highlight the main difficulty encountered in the proofs of Theorem

is different from the characterization of

]
mentioned above.

We treat simply-laced and double-laced root systems on equal footing. However, we could have taken advantage of the following relationship between double-laced and simply-laced root systems. We denote by
\Phi^{s}\subseteq\Phi
the root sub-system consisting of all short roots, and by

its root lattice. The inclusion of root lattices

induces canonical morphisms

as an element of

in this fashion. As it turns out (see Proposition

\Phi

(1.6)

This is perhaps of independent interest.

1.10.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha\rangle=-1
. Then, there exists a simple root
\alpha_{j}
and
w\in W
such that
\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=-1
and

(6.4)

w\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i},\ w\alpha_{j}=\alpha.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Phi
is simply-laced.
Let
\beta=\tau\alpha
with
\tau\in W^{(i)}
be a smallest positive root (with respect to the order
\leqslant
) in the orbit
W^{(i)}\alpha
.
Clearly
\langle\beta,\alpha_{i}\rangle=\langle\tau\alpha,\tau\alpha_{i}\rangle=-1
.
Let
\alpha_{j}
be such that
\sigma_{\beta}\alpha_{j}\in\Phi^{-}
. If
\beta=\alpha_{j}
, then
w=\tau^{-1}
has the required properties since
\langle\beta,\alpha_{i}\rangle=-1
implies
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle=-1
. If
\langle\beta,\alpha_{j}\rangle=1
, then
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle=-1
, otherwise we would have
\sigma_{j}\in W^{(i)}
and
\sigma_{j}\beta=\beta-\alpha_{j}<\beta
, contradicting the minimality of
\beta
.
Let
\gamma=\sigma_{j}\beta=\beta-\alpha_{j}
, and define
w=\tau^{-1}\sigma_{\gamma}
.
Note that
\langle\gamma,\alpha_{i}\rangle=0
and
\langle\gamma,\alpha_{j}\rangle=-1
, so
w\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}
and

w\alpha_{j}=\tau^{-1}(\alpha_{j}+\gamma)=\tau^{-1}\beta=\alpha,

proving (
6.4
).

Assume now
\Phi
is double-laced. Observe that the hypothesis implies
that
\alpha\in\Phi^{s}
, so
\Phi
is not of type
B_{r}
(for which all short roots are mutually orthogonal).
In particular
\Phi^{s}
is irreducible (see Table
2
in §
4.2
), and
we can use the simply-laced case just proved. We conclude that there exists a
\Phi^{s}
-simple
root
\alpha^{\prime}
which is a neighbor of
\alpha_{i}
in the diagram of
\Phi^{s}
, and
w^{\prime}
in the Weyl group generated by reflections in
\Phi^{s}
,
such that

w^{\prime}\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i},\ w^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha.

If
\alpha^{\prime}
is a simple root in
\Phi
, we take
w=w^{\prime}
. If not,
then
\alpha_{i}
does not have a long neighbor in the diagram of
\Phi
(see Table
2
). In particular
\alpha_{i}
is fixed by the action of reflections associated to simple long roots, and there exists an element
v
in the subgroup generated by
these reflections such that
v\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha_{j}
, with
\alpha_{j}
a short neighbor of
\alpha_{i}
. The element
w=w^{\prime}v^{-1}
then satisfies the required properties.
∎

Lemma 6.3

Let

\operatorname*{Res}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=1/\penalty u\\

With

We have

\operatorname*{Res}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=1/\penalty u\\

\mathrm{\bf x}^{\alpha}=\pm 1/\penalty u\end{subarray}}\Pi_{\underline{\delta}}~~,

where

and

We have

By definition, we have

\operatorname*{Res}_{x_{i}=1/u}Z_{\Phi}^{[i]}(\mathrm{\bf x};u)=\frac{N_{\Phi}(\mathrm{\bf x};u)|_{x_{i}=1/u}}{2\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{-1}^{+}\cup\Phi_{0}^{+}\cup(\Phi^{\ell,+}\setminus\Phi_{0}^{+})}(1-u^{2}\underline{x}^{m_{\alpha}\alpha})},

where

denotes the positive long roots.
By Lemma

, we also have

N_{\Phi}(\mathrm{\bf x};u)|_{x_{i}=1/u}=2\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{-1}^{+}\cup(\Phi^{\ell,+}\setminus\Phi_{0}^{+})}(1-u^{2}\underline{x}^{m_{\alpha}\alpha})\cdot N_{0}(\underline{x};u),

(the factor 2 comes from the fact that

This gives the formula in the statement.

To analyze the support of

For

and for a set of roots

.
Since

Combining this with (

7.5.

The next result shows that a rational function is determined uniquely by its residues at

For a rational function

the degree of its numerator minus the degree of its denominator with respect to the variable

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
\Phi
be a simply-laced root system and fix
\alpha_{i}
, a simple root. Let
W^{\prime}
be a parabolic subgroup corresponding to a subdiagram of the Dynkin
diagram of
\Phi
such that
\sigma_{i}\not\in W^{\prime}
. Let

be a rational function that satisfies the following properties

(a)

f(\mathrm{\bf x})=f(\mathrm{\bf x}){\kern 1.0pt\rule[0.0pt]{0.5pt}{10.79993pt}\kern 1.0pt^{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\rm CG$}}\hskip-6.99997pt}w
for all

;

(b)

f(\mathrm{\bf x})
has only simple poles as a function of the variable

,
and all poles involving
x_{i}
occur among
\mathrm{\bf x}^{w\alpha_{i}}=\pm 1/\penalty u
, for

;

(c)

.

Then,

is uniquely determined by the two residues
\displaystyle\operatorname*{Res}_{x_{i}=\pm 1/\penalty u}f(\mathrm{\bf x})
.

Proof.

Using the fact that
\deg_{x_{i}}f(\mathrm{\bf x})<0
, we can decompose

into simple fractions

f(\mathrm{\bf x})=\sum_{w\in W^{\prime}/W^{\prime\prime}}\frac{f_{w}(\mathrm{\bf x})}{1-u^{2}\mathrm{\bf x}^{2w\alpha_{i}}}~,

where
W^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{Stab}_{W^{\prime}}\alpha_{i}
, the sum is over a system of representatives
for the coset space
W^{\prime}/\penalty W^{\prime\prime}
and

for

polynomials in
\underline{x}
.
Since
the denominators are even with respect to all the signs
\varepsilon_{i}
, it follows that for

we have

f(\mathrm{\bf x})=f(\mathrm{\bf x})\kern 1.0pt\rule[0.0pt]{0.5pt}{10.79993pt}\kern 1.0pt^{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\rm CG$}}\hskip-6.99997ptw^{\prime}=\sum_{w\in W^{\prime}/W^{\prime\prime}}\frac{f_{w}(\mathrm{\bf x}){\kern 1.0pt\rule[0.0pt]{0.5pt}{10.79993pt}\kern 1.0pt^{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\rm CG$}}\hskip-6.99997pt}w^{\prime}}{1-u^{2}\mathrm{\bf x}^{2w^{\prime-1}w\alpha_{i}}}

.

Proof.

(i) Since

is clear. For the reverse inclusion, let
\alpha\in S^{s}
, and let

. If
\beta

\alpha_{j}
simple such that

\beta

\sigma_{\beta-\alpha_{i}}\alpha_{i}=\beta

\beta\in W^{i}\alpha_{i}

(ii) Let

. Since
\alpha+2\alpha_{i}\in T_{2}

\alpha+\alpha_{i}\in S_{0}^{s}
. The root
\alpha+\alpha_{i}
is short because
both
\alpha,\alpha+2\alpha_{i}
are long. We consider two cases.

If
\Phi
is of type
B_{r}
or

, the root system
\Phi_{0}
is irreducible, and there is a unique short root

\gamma\in\Pi_{\mathrm{new}}(\Phi_{0})
. The set

consists of those roots
\gamma
in
\Phi_{0}
having

and, by part (i), we deduce

. Therefore,

\alpha+\alpha_{i}=w\gamma
for some

, so
\gamma=w^{-1}\alpha+\alpha_{i}\in U_{-2}+\alpha_{i}
, as
W^{(i)}
permutes both the

and

. It follows that

.

If
\Phi
is of type

, the root system
\Phi_{0}
has an irreducible component of type

generated by a short
root
\gamma\in\Pi_{\mathrm{new}}(\Phi_{0})
. If
i=1
, or

, we have that

; otherwise, part (i) implies that

(iii) The inclusion

is clear. The reverse inclusion follows if we show that if

has the largest height in its

-orbit, then

is dominant. This is indeed the case. For

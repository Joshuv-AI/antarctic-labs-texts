Best proximity points in ultrametric spaces
K. Chaira 1 and S. Lazaiz 2

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study the existence of best proximity pair in ultrametric spaces. We show, under suitable assumptions, that the proximinal pair ( A , B ) (A,B) has a best proximity pair. As a consequence we generalize a well known best approximation result and we derive some fixed point theorems. Moreover, we provide examples to illustrate the obtained results.

Primary: 37C25. Secondary: 54E50; 41A50

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
T:A\rightarrow B
be a map where
A
and
B
are two nonempty subsets of a metric space
M
. Recall that a point
x\in M
is called a fixed point of a
T
if
Tx=x
. It is known that such an equation does not always have a solution. However, in the absence of the fixed point (for example if
A\cap B=\varnothing
), it is possible to consider the problem of finding a point
x\in A
that is as close as possible to
Tx
in
B
; i.e., to minimize the quantity
d(x,Tx)
over
A
. This type of problem is considered as part of approximation theory, more specifically best approximation point results.

Definition 1.1
.

[
6
]

A subset
A\subset M
is said to be proximinal if given any
x\in M
there exists
a_{0}\in A
such that

d(x,a_{0})=dist(x,A)=\inf\{d(x,z):\;z\in A\}.

Such an
a_{0}
, if it exists, is called a best approximation to
x
in
A
.

In the literature, some positive results concerning the existence of best approximation point were given whether in the archimedean or non-archimedean spaces, for more details see
[
9
,
10
,
14
]
.

However, proximinality results guaranteed the existence of best approximation point without being optimal, for this reason the authors in
[
6
]
introduce the notion of best proximity pair which gives both the existence and the optimality. Recall that for a bounded set
B
in
M
,
\delta(B)
stands for the diameter of
B
, i.e.,

\delta(B):=\sup\{d(x,y)\;:\;x,y\in B\},

and
dist(A,B)=\inf\{d(a,b):\;a\in A\;\text{and}\;b\in B\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

[
6
, Definition 1.1.]

Let
M
be a metric space and let
A
and
B
be nonempty subsets of
M
. Let

A_{0}=\{x\in A:\;d(x,y)=dist(A,B)\;\text{for some}\;y\in B\};

B_{0}=\{y\in B:\;d(x,y)=dist(A,B)\;\text{for some}\;x\in A\}.

A pair
(x,y)\in A_{0}\times B_{0}
for which
d(x,y)=dist(A,B)
is called a best proximity pair for
A
and
B
.

Motivated by the preceding definition, the authors in
[
6
]
raised the following question:

Question 1.3
.

Let
(A,B)
be proximinal pair of
(M,d)
. Does there exists a best proximity pair
(a,b)\in A_{0}\times B_{0}
? If so, does the pair
(A_{0},B_{0})
also proximinal ?

If this question has an affirmative answer, this inspires to formulate the following:

Question 1.4
.

Given a mapping
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
with
T(A)\subset A
and
T(B)\subset B
(also called noncyclic mapping), does there exists an ordered pair
(a,b)\in A\times B
such that

Ta=a,\quad Tb=b\quad\text{and}\quad d(a,b)=dist(A,B).

There is an extensive literature contains partial affirmative answers to these two questions in the context of metric spaces and linear spaces (see
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
). To the best knowledge of the authors, this is the first time these questions are considered in the case of ultrametric spaces.

Recall that an ultrametric space is a metric space
M
in which strong triangle inequality viz.
d(x,y)\leq\max\{d(x,z),d(z,y)\}
is satisfied for all
x,y,z\in M
.

Definition 1.5
.

[
13
]

An ultrametric space
(M,d)
is called spherically complete if each nested sequence of balls
B_{1}\supset B_{2}\supset\cdots
has a non-empty intersection.

In
[
8
]
the authors prove the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A
be a spherically complete subspace of an ultrametric space
(M,d)
. Then
A
is proximinal in
M
.

Definition 1.7
.

[
7
]

Assume that
T:M\rightarrow M
is a map and
B=B(x,r)\subset M
a closed ball of
M
. We say that
B
is minimal
T
-invariant ball if :

(i)

T(B)\subseteq B
, and

(ii)

d(y,Ty)=r
for each
y\in B
.

The following definition is well known.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
A\subseteq M
.
T:A\rightarrow A
is said to be nonexpansive if

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y)\quad\text{for each}\;x,y\in A

The next fixed point theorem is the most important and significant result in ultrametric spaces.

Theorem 1.9
.

[
7
,
11
]

Suppose
M
is a spherically complete ultrametric space and
T:M\rightarrow M
is a nonexpansive map. Then every ball of the form
B(x,d(x,Tx))
contains either a fixed point of
T
or a minimal
T
-invariant ball.

Note that the proof provided of this Theorem, shows that the fixed point for nonexpansive mappings is closely related with the lack of the condition
d(y,Ty)=d(z,Tz)>0
, for any
y
and
z
in a minimal
T
-invariant ball. That is to say, this condition must be violated. Thereby, other conditions must be added in order to ensure the existence of fixed point.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
(M,d)
be an ultrametric space and
T:M\rightarrow M
a mapping. We say that :

(1)

T
is strictly contractive if
d(Tx,Ty)<d(x,y)
whenever
x\neq y
.

(2)

T
is strictly contractive on orbit if
Tx\neq x
implies
d(T^{2}x,Tx)<d(Tx,x)
for each
x\in M
.

It is clear that if
T
is strictly contractive then it is strictly contractive on orbit. On the other hand, the authors in
[
1
]
introduced a more general class of mappings that contains the preceding one.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
(M,d)
be an ultrametric space. We will say that
T
has the weak-regular property if

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\ d(T^{n}x,T^{n+1}x)<d(x,Tx),

for each
x
in
M
such that
x\neq Tx
.

Using this definition, the authors in
[
1
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
(M,d)
be a spherically complete ultrametric space. Let
T:M\rightarrow M
be a nonexpansive map which has the weak-regular property. Then
T
has a fixed point in any
T
-invariant closed ball.

In this paper, we will see that the answer to the above questions is positive under natural assumptions. In the lack of this assumptions, we show by an example that
(A_{0},B_{0})
may be an empty pair. As a consequence we generalize a best approximation result due to Kirk and Shahzad (see
[
7
, Theorem 11]
) and we derive some fixed point theorems. Throughout this paper, we provide some examples to illustrate the obtained results.

2.
Main results

We first give a useful lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(A,B)
be a proximinal pair of an ultrametric space
(M,d)
with
B
is bounded. Assume that
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
, then
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
and
B_{0}=B
.

Proof.

•

Since
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
we have for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
a\in A

d(b,b^{\prime})\leq d(a,b^{\prime}).

If there exist
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
a\in A
such that
d(b,b^{\prime})=d(a,b^{\prime})
, then
d(a,b^{\prime})=dist(A,B)
which implies that
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
and
B_{0}\neq\varnothing
.

Assume now that for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and all
a\in A
we have
d(b,b^{\prime})<d(a,b^{\prime})
, then

d(a,b^{\prime})=d(a,b).

Thus,

dist(b,A)=dist(b^{\prime},A),

which implies that
\varphi(b)=dist(b,A)
is a constant mapping over
B
. Thus,

\inf_{b\in B}\varphi(b)=\varphi(b)

that is
dist(A,B)=dist(b,A)=d(b,a)
since
A
is proximinal. Then
(A_{0},B_{0})
is a nonempty pair.

•

B_{0}=B
. Indeed, let
b\in B
and
a_{0}\in A_{0}
then there exists
b_{0}\in B_{0}
such that

d(a_{0},b_{0})=d

using the strong inequality we get :

d(a_{0},b)\leq\max\{d(a_{0},b_{0}),d(b_{0},b)\}=dist(A,B)

then
b\in B_{0}
.

∎

Next, we give an example to show that if
\delta(B)>dist(A,B)
, the pair
(A_{0},B_{0})
may be an empty pair.

Example 2.1
.

Let
M=\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the set of positive integers and define the ultrametric distance
d
on
M
as follows:

d(n,m)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if}\;n=m\\
\max\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{m}\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\end{cases}

Then
(M,d)
is an ultrametric space. Set
A=2\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{2,4,\ldots\}
and
B=2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1=\{1,3,\ldots\}
.

It is clear that
A
and
B
are proximinal sets. Moreover,
dist(A,B)=0
and
\delta(B)=1
and
\delta(A)=\frac{1}{2}
, thus
dist(A,B)<\min\{\delta(B),\delta(A)\}
. Note that
A_{0}=B_{0}=\varnothing
.

The following result gives a positive answer to the question
1.3
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(A,B)
be a spherically complete pair in an ultrametric space
(M,d)
. Assume that
B
is bounded and
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Then
(A_{0},B_{0})
is nonempty spherically complete pair of
M
.

Proof.

Since every spherically complete set is proximinal (see Theorem
1.6
) we get
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
and
B_{0}=B
by Lemma
2.1
. Thus, obviously
B_{0}
is spherically complete.

Next, we show that
A_{0}
is spherically complete. Let
\{B(a_{i},r_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a descending collection of closed balls centered at points
a_{i}\in A_{0}
. Let
i\in I
, for each
a_{i}
in
A_{0}
there exists
b_{i}\in B_{0}
such that

d(a_{i},b_{i})=d.

Case 1:

Assume that
d\leq r_{i}
for all
i\in I
. As any ball is central we have
B(a_{i},r_{i})=B(b_{i},r_{i})
for all
i\in I
, thus

B\cap B(a_{i},r_{i})\neq\varnothing

since
B
is spherically complete subset we get

B\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i})\neq\varnothing.

Let
b\in B\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i})
, then
b\in B
and

\begin{array}[]{ccl}d(a_{i},b)&\leq&\max\{d(a_{i},b_{i});d(b_{i},b)\}\\
&\leq&\max\{d,\delta(B)\}\\
&\leq&d\end{array}

then
b\in B_{0}
. So, let
a\in A_{0}
such that
d(a,b)=d
; thus

\begin{array}[]{ccl}d(a,a_{i})&\leq&\max\{d(a,b);d(b,a_{i})\}\\
&\leq&d\end{array}

then
a\in B(a_{i},d)\subset B(a_{i},r_{i})
for all
i\in I
, i.e.,

a\in A_{0}\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i}).

Case 2:

There exists
i_{0}\in I
such that
r_{i_{0}}\leq d
, then
B(a_{i_{0}},r_{i_{0}})\subset B(a_{i_{0}},d)
. As
A
is spherically complete, let
a
be in
A\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i})
and since

A\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i})\subset A\cap B(a_{i_{0}},r_{i_{0}})

we have
d(a,a_{i_{0}})\leq d
thus by the strong inequality we get also
d(a,b_{i_{0}})=d
. And since
b_{i_{0}}\in B_{0}
we have
a\in A_{0}
and then

a\in A_{0}\cap\bigcap_{i}B(a_{i},r_{i}).

Thus,
A_{0}
is a spherically complete subset of
M
.

∎

The next Theorem answers partially question
1.4
for nonexpansive mappings.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(A,B)
be a spherically complete pair of an ultrametric space
M
for which
B
is bounded and
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Suppose
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
is a noncyclic nonexpansive mapping on
A\cup B
. Then there exists a best proximity pair
(a^{*},b^{*})\in A\times B
satisfying one of these statements:

(i)

a^{*}
and
b^{*}
are fixed points of
T
.

(ii)

a^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
and
B(b^{*},d(b^{*},Tb^{*}))
is a minimal
T
-invariant set in
B
, each point of which is a nearest point to
a^{*}
.

(iii)

B(a^{*},d(a^{*},Ta^{*}))
(resp.
B(b^{*},d(b^{*},Tb^{*}))
) is a minimal
T
-invariant set in
A
(resp. in
B
).

Remark 2.4
.

Let
B_{1}
be a ball,
a\in M
,
a\notin B
. Then
d(a,x)=d(a,y)
for each
x,y\in B
. More generally, let
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
be disjoint balls. Then, for
x\in B_{1}
and
y\in B_{2}
, the number
d(x,y)
is constant.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Using Proposition
2.2
, we have
(A_{0},B_{0})
is a nonempty spherically complete pair of
M
. Let
x\in A_{0}
, then there exists
y\in B_{0}
such that

d(x,y)=dist(A,B)

and since
T
is nonexpansive on
A\cup B
, we have

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y)=dist(A,B),

using the fact that
T
is noncyclic we get that
T(A_{0})\subset A_{0}
.

Fix
x\in A_{0}
and let
z\in B(x,d(x,Tx))
, then we have

\begin{array}[]{ccl}d(x,Tz)&\leq&\max\{d(x,Tx);d(Tx,Tz)\}\\
&\leq&\max\{d(x,Tx);d(x,z)\}\\
&\leq&d(x,Tx),\end{array}

that is
Tz\in B(x,d(x,Tx))
, thus
B(x,d(x,Tx))
is
T
-invariant subset of
A
. Set
X=B(x,d(x,Tx))\cap A_{0}
. Using the above we have
T(X)\subset X
. In the same manner we prove for
y\in B_{0}
that
Y=B(y,d(y,Ty))\cap B_{0}
is a
T
-invariant subset of
B
. Finally, we obtain that
(X,Y)
is a spherically complete pair of
M
.

Hence, by Theorem
1.9
, one of the following statements holds:

(i)

X
contains a fixed point
a^{*}
of
T
and
Y
contains a fixed point
b^{*}
of
T
.

(ii)

X
contains a fixed point
a^{*}
of
T
and
Y
contains a minimal
T
-invariant ball.

(iii)

X
contains a minimal
T
-invariant ball
B(a^{*},d(a^{*},Ta^{*}))
and
Y
contains a minimal
T
-invariant ball
B(b^{*},d(b^{*},Tb^{*}))
.

Let
u\in B(a^{*},d(a^{*},Ta^{*}))
, then
u\in A_{0}
, hence there exists
v_{u}\in B_{0}
such that

d(u,v_{u})=dist(A,B).

Thus for all
v\in B(b^{*},d(b^{*},Tb^{*}))
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ccl}d(u,v)&\leq&\max\{d(u,v_{u}),d(v_{u},v)\}\\
&\leq&dist(A,B)\end{array}

then
d(u,v)=dist(A,B)
for all
u\in B(a^{*},d(a^{*},Ta^{*}))
and
v\in B(b^{*},d(b^{*},Tb^{*}))
. The proof is completed.
∎

In the next example, we shall consider a mapping
T
that has fixed points in every ball of the form
B(x,d(x,Tx))
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
M
be the set of all infinite sequences of natural numbers endowed with the ultrametric distance
d
defined by :

d(x,y)=\frac{1}{1+k(x,y)},

where
k(x,y)=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\;x_{n}\neq y_{n}\}
.

We define
T:M\rightarrow M
by
T(x)(0)=x_{0}
,
T(x)(1)=x_{1}
and
T(x)(n)={\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}}
for all
n\geq 2
.

Let
x,y\in M
and set
d(x,y)=\frac{1}{1+k(x,y)}
, then we have two cases :

•

If there exists
n
such that
n<k(x,y)
and
x_{n}=0
. We have
y_{n}=0
, hence,
Tx=Ty
which implies that
d(Tx,Ty)=0
.

•

If for all
n<k(x,y)
we have
x_{n}\neq 0
, then
x_{i}=y_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq k(x,y)-1

so, we have

Tx=(x_{0},x_{1},\prod_{i=0}^{2}x_{i},\ldots,\prod_{i=0}^{n}x_{i},\ldots,\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)-1}x_{i},\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)}x_{i},\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)+1}x_{i},\ldots),

and

Ty=(x_{0},x_{1},\prod_{i=0}^{2}x_{i},\ldots,\prod_{i=0}^{n}x_{i},\ldots,\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)-1}x_{i},\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)}y_{i},\prod_{i=0}^{k(x,y)+1}y_{i},\ldots)

which implies that
d(Tx,Ty)=\frac{1}{1+k(x,y)}
.

Thus, in both cases

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y),

that is
T
is nonexpansive.

We shall show that the mapping
T
satisfies all the hypothesis of Theorem
2.3
. Denote by
\overline{n}
the constant sequence
\overline{n}:=(n,n,\ldots)\in M
and set
X_{n}=B(\overline{n},\frac{1}{2})
. It is clear that
T(X_{n})\subset X_{n}
and
d(\overline{n},T\overline{n})=\frac{1}{3}
.

Moreover, let
n\neq m
then,

dist(X_{n},X_{m})=d(\overline{n},\overline{m})=1

and

\delta(X_{n})=\frac{1}{2},

which leads to
\delta(X_{n})\leq dist(X_{n},X_{m})
.

So, using Theorem
2.3
, the mapping
T
has a fixed point in every set
X_{n}
. Indeed,

(1)

if
n=0
or
n=1
then
\overline{0}
respectively
\overline{1}
is a fixed point of
T
in
X_{0}
respectively in
X_{1}
;

(2)

if
n>1
then
(n,n,0,0,\ldots)
which lies in
X_{n}
is a fixed point for
T
.

In the next example, we shall consider a mapping which has stable balls but no fixed points.

Example 2.6
.

Let
M=\mathbb{Z}_{3}
be the ring of the
3
-adic integers endowed with the
3
-adic valuation
|\cdot|_{3}
. Let
p
be a fixed larger natural number. Define the pair
(A,B)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
to be
A=B(0,\frac{1}{3^{p}})
and
B=B(1,\frac{1}{3^{p}})
. Note that
A\cap B=\varnothing
. Indeed, if
x\in A\cap B
then

|x|_{3}\leq 1/3^{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|x-1|_{3}\leq 1/3^{p},

so,
1\leq\max\{|1-x|_{3},|x|_{3}\}=\frac{1}{3^{p}}
which is a contradiction. Also, it is clear that
B
is spherically complete whith
\delta(B)=\frac{1}{3^{p}}
and
dist(A,B)=1
.

Let
T
be the mapping defined by
Tx=x+3^{p}
for all
x\in M
. It is clear that
T
is an isometry and noncyclic mapping from
A\cup B
to itself. Since
T
does not have any fixed point, by Theorem
2.3
the pair
(A,B)
has minimal
T
-invariant pair. Note that,
(A,B)
is exactly the minimal
T
invariant pair, since for all
z\in A\cup B
we have

|z-Tz|_{3}=\left|3^{p}\right|_{3}=1/3^{p}.

We consider in the next example a mapping
T
which has both stable ball and fixed point.

Example 2.7
.

Let
M
be the set of all infinite sequences of nonnegative integers endowed with the ultrametric
d
as Example
2.5
. Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in M
and define the mapping
T:M\rightarrow M
by

Tx(n)={\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}},\quad\forall n\geq 1.

Let
a^{*}=(1,1,\ldots)
and let
b^{*}=(1,2,2,\ldots)
. Define
A=\{a^{*}\}
and

. It is clear that
B
is spherically complete whith
\delta(B)=\frac{1}{4}
and

. Moreover,
T
is nonexpansive and noncyclic mapping from
A\cup B
to itself.

Note that
a^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
and
B
is a minimal
T
-invariant ball. Indeed, let
z\in B
then

d(z,b^{*})\leq\frac{1}{4}
, that is
k(z,b^{*})\geq 3
(see Example
2.5
for notation), which implies
z=(1,2,z_{3},z_{4},\ldots)
, thus
Tz=(1,2,2z_{3},\ldots)
. Then
d(z,Tz)=\frac{1}{4}
, i.e.,
B
is a minimal
T
-invariant ball.

Corollary 2.8
.

[
7
, Theorem 11]

Let
A
be a spherically complete subspace of an ultrametric space
M
, and let
b^{*}\in M\setminus A
. Suppose
T:M\rightarrow M
is a mapping for which
Tb^{*}=b^{*}
. Also assume that
T
is nonexpansive on
A\cup\{b^{*}\}
and that
A
is
T
-invariant. Then
T
has a fixed point in

which is a nearest point of
b^{*}
in
A
, or
A
contains a minimal
T
-invariant set, each point of which is a nearest point to
b^{*}
in
A
.

Next, we derive some fixed point results that also answers the question
1.4
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(A,B)
be a spherically complete pair of an ultrametric space
M
for which
B
is bounded and
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Suppose
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
is a noncyclic nonexpansive mapping on
A\cup B
which has the weak-regular property. Then there exist
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that

Ta=a,\quad Tb=b\quad\text{and}\quad d(a,b)=dist(A,B).

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.2
there exists a best proximity pair
(x,y)\in A_{0}\times B_{0}
. Set

X=B(x,d(x,Tx))\cap A_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad Y=B(y,d(y,Ty))\cap B_{0}.

As the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we show that
T(X)\subseteq X
and
T(Y)\subseteq Y
and since
(X,Y)
is spherically complete pair we get by theorem
1.12
the existence of two points
a^{*}\in X
and
b^{*}\in Y
such that

Ta^{*}=a^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad Tb^{*}=b^{*}

and since
B_{0}=B
by Lemma
2.1
, we get that
d(a^{*},b^{*})=dist(A,B)
.
∎

Since every strictly contractive mapping on orbit has the weak-regular property (see
[
1
]
), we have the following result.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(A,B)
be a spherically complete pair of an ultrametric space
M
for which
B
is bounded and
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Suppose
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
is a noncyclic nonexpansive mapping on
A\cup B
which is strictly contractive on orbit. Then there exist
a^{*}\in A
and
b^{*}\in B
such that

Ta^{*}=a^{*},\quad Tb^{*}=b^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad d(a^{*},b^{*})=dist(A,B).

On the other hand, since every strictly contractive mapping is nonexpansive mapping and the fact that any strictly contractive mapping in a spherically complete ultrametric space has a unique fixed point see
[
12
]
, we obtain :

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
(A,B)
be a spherically complete pair of an ultrametric space
M
for which
B
is bounded and
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Suppose
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
is a noncyclic strictly contractive mapping on
A\cup B
. Then there exist
a^{*}\in A
and
b^{*}\in B
such that

Ta^{*}=a^{*},\quad Tb^{*}=b^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad d(a^{*},b^{*})=dist(A,B).

As a consequence of theorem
2.11
we obtain the following result which can be found in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 2.12
.

[
7
, Corollary 12]

Let
A
be a spherically complete subspace of an ultrametric space
M
and suppose
T:M\rightarrow M
is a mapping having a fixed point
b^{*}\in M\setminus A
. Assume that
T
is strictly contractive on
A\cup\{b^{*}\}
and
A
is
T
-invariant. Then there exists a unique fixed point
a^{*}
of
T
which is a nearest point of
b^{*}
in
A
.

3.
Concluding remarks

We have studied in this paper the problem of existence of the best proximity point for nonexpansive noncyclic mappings
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
. We have shown that if
(A,B)
is proximinal pair and
B
is bounded such that
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
, then there exists a proximity pair
(a^{*},b^{*})\in A_{0}\times B_{0}
such that
d(a^{*},Tb^{*})=dist(A,B)
.

Now, if one want to deal with cyclic mapping
T:A\cup B\rightarrow A\cup B
i.e.,
T(A)\subseteq B
and
T(B)\subseteq A
, the result is obvious with the assumption
\delta(B)\leq dist(A,B)
. Indeed, Let
a^{*}\in A_{0}
then there exists
b^{*}\in B_{0}
such that
d(a^{*},b^{*})=dist(A,B)
. By the strong inequality we get

d(a^{*},Ta^{*})\leq\max\{dist(A,B),d(b^{*},Ta^{*})\}\leq dist(A,B)

thus each element of
A_{0}
is a best proximity point of
T
. Note that
T
is not even assumed to be nonexpansive map.

Before discussing another important observation, let’s recall the following surprising result.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
2
, Proposition 1.2]

Let
(M,d)
be an ultrametric space and let
B
be a closed ball with radius
r
. Then, the actual radius of
B
is equal to the diameter of
B
.

Let
B
be a ball. Suppose that
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
and
\delta(B)>dist(A,B)
. Then, there exist
a^{*}\in A
and
b^{*}\in B
such that

d(a^{*},b^{*})=dist(A,B).

Thus,
d(a^{*},b^{*})\leq\delta(B)
. By the previous proposition, we obtain
a^{*}\in B
. Then
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
, hence
dist(A,B)=0
.

Now if one assume that
B
is no longer a ball we have the following example which shows that
dist(A,B)\neq 0
. This example is borrowed from Kirk and Shahzad in
[
7
]
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
M=\{a,b,c,d\}
with
d(a,b)=d(c,d)=\frac{1}{2}
;
d(a,c)=d(a,d)=d(b,c)=d(b,d)=1
. Then
(M,d)
is a spherically complete ultrametric space. Define
A=\{a,c\}
and
B=\{b,d\}
. Then
(A,B)
is spherically complete pair with
\delta(B)>dist(A,B)
and
A_{0}=\{a,c\}
. Note that
dist(A,B)\neq 0
.

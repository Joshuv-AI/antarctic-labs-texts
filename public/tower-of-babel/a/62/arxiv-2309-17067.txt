Non-convex functionals penalizing simultaneous oscillations along two independent directions: structure of the defect measure
M. Goldman, B. Merlet

Abstract
We continue the analysis of a family of energies penalizing oscillations in oblique directions: they apply to functions u ⁡ ( x 1 , x 2 ) u(x_{1},x_{2}) with x l ∈ ℝ n l x_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}} and vanish when u ⁡ ( x ) u(x) is of the form u 1 ​ ( x 1 ) u_{1}(x_{1}) or u 2 ​ ( x 2 ) u_{2}(x_{2}) . We mainly study the rectifiability properties of the defect measure ∇ 1 ∇ 2 ​ u \nabla_{1}\nabla_{2}u of functions with finite energy. The energies depend on a parameter θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \theta\in(0,1] and the set of functions with finite energy grows with θ \theta . For θ < 1 \theta<1 we prove that the defect measure is ( n 1 − 1 , n 2 − 1 ) (n_{1}-1,n_{2}-1) -tensor rectifiable in Ω 1 × Ω 2 \Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2} . We first get the result for n 1 = n 2 = 1 n_{1}=n_{2}=1 and deduce the general case through slicing using White’s rectifiability criterion. When θ = 1 \theta=1 the situation is less clear as measures of arbitrary dimensions from zero to n 1 + n 2 − 1 n_{1}+n_{2}-1 are possible. We show however, in the case n 1 = n 2 = 1 n_{1}=n_{2}=1 and for Lipschitz continuous functions, that the defect measures are 1 1\, -rectifiable. This case bears strong analogies with the study of entropic solutions of the eikonal equation.

Remark 1.18

Remark 1.20
.

We call entropy any mapping

which writes as

It is then not hard to see that if

]

We now establish an elementary measure theoretical lemma used in the proofs of Theorem

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a locally compact, complete and separable metric space, let
\mu
be a finite Radon measure on
\mathcal{X}
. For
k\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint Borel subsets of
\mathcal{X}
such that
d_{k}:=\sup\{\diam Q\,:\,Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}\}
goes to
0
as
k\uparrow\infty
. Then,

|\mu|\left(\cap_{k}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right)\leq\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}}|\mu(Q)|.

Remark 2.3
.

The lemma is false if we do not assume that the elements of
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
are disjoint.
In the sequel we apply the result with
\mathcal{X}=\Omega
, a bounded open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and with
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
a finite set of disjoint boxes.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

In the proof we denote
{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}:=\cup\mathcal{Q}^{k}
and
\mathcal{Y}:=\cap_{k}{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}
.

Writing
\mu=\zeta|\mu|
the polar decomposition of
\mu
, the function
\zeta
is Borel measurable and takes values in
\{-1,1\}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{X},[-1,1])
such that

\|\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\zeta\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{X},|\mu|)}<\varepsilon.

(2.6)

For
k\geq 1
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}
, we set

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(Q):=\dfrac{1}{|\mu|(Q)}\int_{Q}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\,d|\mu|\quad\text{ with the convention }\zeta_{\varepsilon}(Q)=0\text{ if }|\mu|(Q)=0.

By construction,
-1\leq\zeta_{\varepsilon}(Q)\leq 1
and we deduce

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}}|\mu(Q)|\geq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(Q)\mu(Q)=\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\,d\mu+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}}\int_{Q}\left(\zeta_{\varepsilon}(Q)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu.

(2.7)

The last term is bounded from below by

-|\mu|(\mathcal{X})\max\{|\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}(y)|:x,y\in\mathcal{X},\,|x-y|\leq d_{k}\}.

By uniform continuity of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
and the assumption

, this goes to

as
k\uparrow\infty
.

Taking the infimum limit of (

, we obtain,

\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}}|\mu(Q)|\,\geq\,\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\,d\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lemma:tech_prf1}}}{{\geq}}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\zeta\,d\mu-\varepsilon=|\mu|(\mathcal{Y})-\varepsilon.

Recalling that

\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k}(\Omega)
,

\left<T,\omega\right>=\int_{\Sigma}m\,\left<\omega,\xi\right>d\mathcal{H}^{k}.

(2.24)

We introduce the “partial” differentials
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}d_{1}:\mathcal{D}^{k_{1}}(\Omega_{1})\wedge\mathcal{D}^{k_{2}}(\Omega_{2})&\to&\mathcal{D}^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}(\Omega)&&d_{2}:\mathcal{D}^{k_{1}}(\Omega_{1})\wedge\mathcal{D}^{k_{2}}(\Omega_{2})&\to&\mathcal{D}^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}(\Omega)\\
\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}&\mapsto&d\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2},&&\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}&\mapsto&\omega_{1}\wedge d\omega_{2}.\end{array}

For
0\leq k\leq n
, we then extend by linearity and density the operators
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
on

\mathcal{D}^{k}(\Omega)=\overline{\Span\left(\oplus_{l=0}^{k}\mathcal{D}^{l}(\Omega_{1})\wedge\mathcal{D}^{k-l}(\Omega_{2})\right)}.

By duality, this defines continuous partial boundary operators on the space of currents:

\left<\partial_{1}T,\omega\right>:=\left<T,d_{1}\omega\right>,\quad\left<\partial_{2}T,\omega\right>:=\left<T,d_{2}\omega\right>,\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{D}_{k}(\Omega),\ \omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k-1}(\Omega).

For
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we define the current

[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt]:=u\,e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n_{1}}\wedge f_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{n_{2}}\ \in\mathcal{D}_{n}(\Omega),

from which, we derive the
(n-2)\,
-dimensional current

T[u]:=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt].

For every
(n-2)\,
-current
T
we define the
\theta\,
-mass of
T
by

\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|m|^{\theta}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}&\text{if }T\text{ is rectifiable,}\\[8.0pt]
\qquad+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We say that a rectifiable
(n-2)\,
-current is tensor-rectifiable if we can choose the set
\Sigma
from (
2.24
) such that
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{1}+\Sigma^{2}
where
\Sigma^{l}\subset\Omega_{l}
is
(n_{l}-1)\,
-rectifiable. Notice that in this case, the
k\,
-vector field
\xi
tangent to
\Sigma
must be of the form
\xi=\xi^{1}\wedge\xi^{2}
with
\xi^{l}
tangent to
\Sigma^{l}
.
For
l=1,2
we set

dx^{l}=dx_{1}^{l}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n_{l}}^{l}

and introduce the following simple multi-covectors for

,

dx^{l}_{\bar{\imath}}:=dx^{l}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{l}_{i-1}\wedge dx^{l}_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{l}_{n_{l}}.

Similarly, we define the simple multi-vectors

e_{\bar{\imath}}:=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i-1}\wedge e_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n_{1}},\qquad f_{\bar{\jmath}}:=f_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{j-1}\wedge f_{j+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{n_{2}}.

We define the Hodge star operator on
\mathbb{X}_{1}
, first on the simple
(n_{1}-1)\,
-vectors
e_{\bar{\imath}}
by
\star e_{\bar{\imath}}=(-1)^{i}e_{i}
, and then extend it by linearity. Similarly we set
\star f_{\bar{\jmath}}=(-1)^{j}f_{j}
.

Every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{n-2}(\Omega)
decomposes as

\omega=\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n_{1}\\
1\leq j\leq n_{2}\end{subarray}}\omega_{i,j}\,dx^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}\wedge dx^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}\right]+\omega_{1}\,\wedge dx^{2}\,+\,dx^{1}\wedge\omega_{2}.

(2.25)

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
we have with the notation (
2.23
),

T[u]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n_{1}\\
1\leq j\leq n_{2}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i+j}\mu_{i,j}\,e_{\bar{\imath}}\wedge f_{\bar{\jmath}}.

(2.26)

As a consequence,
\mu[u]
is rectifiable (respectively tensor-rectifiable) if and only if
T[u]
is rectifiable (respectively tensor-rectifiable). Moreover
\partial T[u]=0
(we say that
T[u]
is a cycle),

\mathbf{M}(T[u])\lesssim|\mu[u]|(\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u])=\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(\mu[u]).

(2.27)

Proof.

We first establish (
2.26
). Fix
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{n-2}(\Omega)
and decompose it as in (
2.25
). Since

d_{2}(\omega_{1}\wedge dx^{2})=\omega_{1}\wedge d(dx^{2})=0=d_{1}(dx^{1}\wedge\omega_{2}),

(2.28)

we have

\left<T[u],\omega\right>=\left<[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt],d_{2}d_{1}\omega\right>=\sum_{i,j}\left<[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt],d_{2}d_{1}\left(\omega_{i,j}\,dx^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}\wedge dx^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}\right)\right>\\
=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\omega_{i,j}}{\partial x^{1}_{i}\partial x^{2}_{j}}u\,dx=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{i,j}\,d\mu_{i,j}.

This proves (
2.26
).

Assume now that
T[u]
is rectifiable. Let
(\Sigma,\xi,m)
be as in (
2.24
). First, by (
2.26
) we can write
\xi=\xi^{1}\wedge\xi^{2}
for some simple
(n_{l}-1)\,
-vectors
\xi^{l}
of

. We let

be the normals to
\Sigma
. If we decompose

\xi^{1}=\sum_{i}\xi^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}e_{\bar{\imath}}

we have by definition of the Hodge star operator
\xi^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}=(-1)^{i}\nu^{1}_{i}
. Similarly
\xi^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}=(-1)^{j}\nu^{2}_{j}
. Using
(
2.26

\mu_{i,j}=m(-1)^{i+j}\xi^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}\xi^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\Sigma=m\nu^{1}_{i}\nu^{2}_{j}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\Sigma.

This proves that
\mu[u]=m\nu^{1}\otimes\nu^{2}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\Sigma
and thus that

is rectifiable. If moreover

is tensor rectifiable we see that also
\mu[u]
is tensor rectifiable. Since we can revert the argument, this also proves that if

is rectifiable (respectively tensor rectifiable).

Since on

we have

. We finally prove (

). For the first identify we write using the decomposition (

) and (
2.26
),

\mathbf{M}(T[u])=\sup_{|\omega|\leq 1}\left<T[u],\omega\right>=\sup_{|\omega|\leq 1}\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{i,j}\,d\mu_{i,j}\lesssim|\mu|(\Omega).

By the previous discussion, the equality
\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u])=\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(\mu[u])
is immediate (notice that both terms are finite only if
T[u]
is rectifiable).
∎

Thanks to Proposition
2.6
, we can reduce the proof of Theorem

to the analog result for
T[u]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u):=\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{1\leq i\leq n_{1},1\leq j\leq n_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x,r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx<\infty.

Then
T[u]
is a tensor rectifiable cycle with

\mathbf{M}(T[u])\lesssim\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\theta}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)\qquad\text{and }\qquad\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u])\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u).

(2.29)

Moreover, if
u
is integer-valued then
T[u]
is integer rectifiable.

Proof.

Step 1. Preliminary observations.

We first notice that by Remark
1.5
, we have
|\mu[u]|(\Omega)\lesssim\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\theta}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)
and thus by Proposition
2.6
,
T[u]
is a normal current and the first inequality in (
2.29
) holds. We thus need to show that
T[u]
is tensor rectifiable and the second inequality in (
2.29
).

Step 2. The case where
\Omega
is a cube.

In this step we prove the claim assuming that
\Omega
is a
n
-cube. By scaling we assume without loss of generality that
\Omega=Q^{n}:=(0,1)^{n}
.

We start by recalling the definition of slicing of currents by coordinate
(n-2)\,
-planes (see
[
GMS98
,
Fed69
,
Whi99b
,
Jer02
]
for more details). For this we introduce some notation. For
\alpha^{1}\subset\{1,\cdots,n_{1}\}
and
\alpha^{2}\subset\{1,\cdots,n_{2}\}
we set

\alpha:=\alpha_{1}\cup(n_{1}+\alpha_{2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{\alpha}:=\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus\alpha.

We then define

\mathbb{X}_{\alpha^{1}}=\Span\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\alpha^{1}}
,
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha^{2}}=\Span\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\alpha^{2}}
and set
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{X}_{\alpha^{1}}\oplus\mathbb{X}_{\alpha^{2}}
so that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathbb{X}_{\bar{\alpha}}
. We decompose correspondingly every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
x=x^{\alpha}+x^{\bar{\alpha}}
. With a slight abuse of notation we set
Q^{\alpha}=Q^{n}\cap\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
. For a function

and
x\in Q^{n}
we set
u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}(x^{\alpha}):=u(x)
so that by Fubini
u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}\in L^{1}(Q^{\alpha})
for almost every
x^{\bar{\alpha}}
. For every
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=2
and every
T\in\mathcal{D}_{n-2}(Q^{n})
, we define the slices
\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T\in\mathcal{D}^{0}(Q^{\alpha})
by the requirement (see
[
Fed69
, Theorem 4.3.2]
or
[
GMS98
, Section 2.5]
) that for every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{0}(Q^{n})
,

\left<T,\omega\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}\right>=\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\left<\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T,\omega_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}\right>\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}.

(2.30)

Here
dx^{\bar{\alpha}}
denotes the canonical
(n-2)\,
-form.

Step 2.1.
We claim that for every
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=2
and almost every
x^{\bar{\alpha}}
, the slices
\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T[u]
are
0\,
-rectifiable. Using White’s rectifiability criterion
[
Whi99b
,
Jer02
]
this would prove that
T[u]
is
(n-2)\,
-rectifiable.

By (

), if
\alpha_{1}=\emptyset
or
\alpha_{2}=\emptyset
, then
\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T[u]=0
and there is nothing to prove. Indeed, if for instance
\alpha_{2}=\emptyset
then
\{n_{1}+1,\dots,n\}\subset\bar{\alpha}
so that for every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{0}(Q^{n})
we have
\omega\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}=\omega_{1}\wedge dx^{\bar{\alpha}}
in the decomposition (
2.25
). Hence, using (
2.28
) as in the proof of (
2.26
), we compute

\left<T[u],\omega\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}\right>=\left<[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt],d_{2}d_{1}[\omega dx^{\bar{\alpha}}]\right>=\left<[\hskip-1.68pt[u]\hskip-1.65pt],d_{2}0\right>=0.

We conclude by the definition (
2.30
) that
\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T[u]=0
. The case
\alpha_{1}=\emptyset
is similar using

.

We may thus assume that
\alpha_{1}=\{i\}
and
\alpha_{2}=\{j\}
so that
dx^{\bar{\alpha}}=dx^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}\wedge dx^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}
. Let us first prove that the operator
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}
commutes with slicing in the sense that

\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T[u]=(-1)^{i+j}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}[\hskip-1.68pt[u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}]\hskip-1.65pt]\qquad\text{ for a.e.\hbox{} }\,x^{\bar{\alpha}}\in Q^{\bar{\alpha}}.

Indeed, for

Then by Fubini,

\displaystyle\left<T[u],\omega dx^{1}_{\bar{\imath}}\wedge dx^{2}_{\bar{\jmath}}\right>

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\left[\int_{Q^{\alpha}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\omega_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}}{\partial x^{1}_{i}\partial x^{2}_{j}}\,u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}\,dx^{\alpha}\right]\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\left<\partial_{1}\partial_{2}[\hskip-1.68pt[u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}]\hskip-1.65pt],\omega_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}\right>\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}.

By definition (

) of

).

We now prove that for almost every

-rectifiable. To simplify notation we write
Q^{n}_{\varepsilon}
for

. Let us prove that

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}})\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}.

(2.32)

For this we use Fubini and Fatou to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\int_{Q^{n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x,r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}(x^{\bar{\alpha}})\left[\int_{Q^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x^{\alpha},r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx^{\alpha}\right]\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\boldsymbol{1}_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}(x^{\bar{\alpha}})\left[\int_{Q^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x^{\alpha},r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx^{\alpha}\right]\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\left[\int_{Q^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x^{\alpha},r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx^{\alpha}\right]\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}})\,dx^{\bar{\alpha}}.

This proves (
2.32
).
Therefore, for almost every
x^{\bar{\alpha}}\in Q^{\bar{\alpha}}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}})<\infty
. By Theorem
1.9
, for every such
x^{\bar{\alpha}}
, there exist countable sequences
x^{l}\in Q^{\alpha}
and

with

and such that

\sum_{l}|m_{l}|\lesssim\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\theta}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}])=\sum_{l}|m_{l}|^{\theta}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}).

(2.33)

In particular,
T[u_{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}]
is
0\,
-rectifiable. Moreover, if
u
is integer-valued then by Theorem

, so is

.

Step 2.2.
We now prove the second inequality in (
2.29
). For this we notice that if
(\Sigma,\xi,m)
are as in (

) for

\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u])=\int_{\Sigma}|m|^{\theta}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}=\int_{\Sigma}|m|^{\theta}|\xi^{1}\wedge\xi^{2}|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\leq\sum_{i,j}\int_{\Sigma}|m|^{\theta}|\xi^{1}_{i}\xi^{2}_{j}|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}.

(2.34)

Fix

and let
\alpha_{1}=\{i\}
,
\alpha_{2}=\{j\}
. By the co-area formula for rectifiable sets (see

\int_{\Sigma}|m|^{\theta}|\xi^{1}_{i}\xi^{2}_{j}|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}=\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\sum_{x^{\alpha}\in\Sigma\cap(Q^{\alpha}+x^{\bar{\alpha}})}|m(x)|^{\theta}dx^{\bar{\alpha}}=\int_{Q^{\bar{\alpha}}}\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(\Sl_{\bar{\alpha}}^{x^{\bar{\alpha}}}T[u])dx^{\bar{\alpha}}.

Plugging this in (

We finally show that

is tensor-rectifiable. Since it is a local statement it is enough to prove that around each point

such that

is also a normal and rectifiable current for every

and almost every

(depending on

) such that

is a rectifiable flat chain (notice however that in general

is not a cycle). Moreover, the tangent

-vector

to

can be written as
\xi^{1}\wedge\xi^{2}
with

so that for
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}
-every

-plane

for

Step 3. The general case.

In this final step we use a covering argument to prove the claim in a more general domain
\Omega
. For this we need to introduce localized versions of the energy. For any open set

, we set

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u,A):=\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{1\leq i\leq n_{1},1\leq j\leq n_{2}}\int_{A^{\varepsilon_{k}}}\dfrac{|D[Du(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x,r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}}\,dx.

We then set for
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
u_{A}:=u\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(A)
so that as elements of
\mathcal{D}_{n-2}(A)
(this is of course not true in
\mathcal{D}_{n-2}(\Omega)
),

T[u_{A}]=T[u]\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,A.

Let
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
be a Whitney partition of
\Omega
, see e.g.
[
Gra14
, Appendix J]
. By definition, there exists
\lambda=\lambda(d)>0
such that

\mathcal{Q}=\{\lambda Q\ :\ Q\in\mathcal{Q^{\prime}}\}

is a cover of
\Omega
with finite overlap,
i.e
.

\boldsymbol{1}_{\Omega}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\boldsymbol{1}_{Q}\lesssim\boldsymbol{1}_{\Omega}.

(2.35)

Therefore, letting

F_{k}(x)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n_{1},1\leq j\leq n_{2}}\dfrac{|D[Du(\cdot,r_{k}f_{j})](x,r_{k}e_{i})|^{\theta}}{r_{k}^{2}},

we have

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)=\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon_{k}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\Omega}(x)F_{k}(x)\,dx\gtrsim\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon_{k}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{Q}(x)F_{k}(x)\,dx\\
\geq\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}}F_{k}(x)\,dx\geq\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\int_{Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}}F_{k}(x)\,dx=\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u,Q).

As a consequence, for every

we have
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u_{Q})=\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u,Q)<\infty
so that by

,

is tensor rectifiable with

\mathbf{M}_{\theta}(T[u_{Q}])\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u,Q).

On the one hand this yields that

is also tensor rectifiable. On the other hand, by (

We are left with the proof of the vanishing energy limit (
3.61
). Let us first notice the following scaling identity. There holds for
\varepsilon>0
,
r>0
and
A
Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\mu\left[v^{\varepsilon}(\cdot/r)\right](A)=r\mu[v^{\varepsilon}]((1/r)A).

In particular we have for
j\geq 0
and
r>0
,

\mu[\widetilde{v}^{j}](B_{r})=r_{j}\mu[v^{\varepsilon_{j}}](B_{r/r_{j}}).

(3.64)

Next, by direct computation, we get for
\varepsilon>0
and
s>0
,

\left|\mu[v^{\varepsilon}]\right|(B_{s})\leq 4\varepsilon s\qquad\ \text{ and }\ \qquad\left|\mu[(v^{\varepsilon})]\right|(\mathbb{R}^{2})=4.

With (
3.64
), we deduce for
j\geq 0
and
r>0
,

\left|\mu[\widetilde{v}^{j}]\right|(B_{r})\leq 4\varepsilon_{j}r\qquad\ \text{ and }\ \qquad\left|\mu[(\widetilde{v}^{j})]\right|(\mathbb{R}^{2})\leq 4r_{j}.

(3.65)

Now we fix
r\in(0,r_{0}/2]
and we denote
k=k(r)
the unique positive integer such that
r_{k}/2<r\leq r_{k-1}/2
. Taking into account the supports of the
{\widetilde{v}^{j}}
’s, we compute

|\mu[v]|(B_{r})=\left|\mu[\widetilde{v}^{k}]\right|(B_{r})+\sum_{j\geq k+1}\left|\mu[\widetilde{v}^{j}]\right|(\mathbb{R}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ctrex_mu_veps}}}{{\leq}}4\varepsilon_{k}r+4\sum_{j\geq k+1}r_{j}.

(3.66)

To estimate the second term in the right-hand side we deduce from (
3.63
) the following chain of inequalities:
r_{j}<r_{j-1}/2<\dots<r_{k+1}/2^{j-k-1}=\varepsilon_{k+1}r_{k}/2^{j-k}
for
j\geq k+1
. Hence,

\sum_{j\geq k+1}r_{j}<\varepsilon_{k+1}r_{k}\left(\sum_{j\geq k+1}\dfrac{1}{2^{j-k}}\right)=\varepsilon_{k+1}r_{k}.

Using this estimate in (
3.66
) we obtain,

|\mu[v]|(B_{r})<4\varepsilon_{k}r+4\varepsilon_{k+1}r_{k}<12\varepsilon_{k}r.

Dividing by
r
and sending
r
to 0, the condition
r_{k(r)}<2r
leads to
k(r)\to+\infty
. Thus
\varepsilon_{k(r)}\to 0
and (
3.61
) follows. This ends the proof of the proposition.

Remark 3.21
.

Let
\alpha>0
. The sequence defined recursively by

r_{0}:=2^{-1/\alpha}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad r_{k+1}:=r_{k}^{\alpha+1}\quad\text{for }k\geq 0

complies to the assumptions of the proposition. With this choice we have

|\mu[v]|(B_{r})\leq 48\,r^{\beta}\qquad\text{ with}\quad\beta:=2-\dfrac{1}{\alpha+1}.

As we can make
\beta
arbitrarily close to 2 by choosing
\alpha
large enough we conclude that even a constraint of the form

\left|\mu[v]\right|(B_{r})\leq Cr^{2-\varepsilon},

for some
\varepsilon>0
and
C\geq 1
does not ensure that
0
is a Lebesgue point of either
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
.

Yong Yang, Jing-Kun Zhao, Miho N. Ishigaki, Jian-Zhao Zhou, Cheng-Qun Yang, Xiang-Xiang Xue, Xian-Hao Ye and Gang Zhao

Abstract
By mining the data from G ​ a ​ i ​ a Gaia EDR3, SDSS/SEGUE DR16 and LAMOST DR8, 11 member stars of the NGC 5466 tidal stream are detected and 7 of them are newly identified. To reject contaminators, a variety of cuts are applied in sky position, color-magnitude diagram, metallicity, proper motion and radial velocity. We compare our data to a mock stream generated by modeling the cluster’s disruption under a smooth Galactic potential plus the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC). The concordant trends in phase-space between the model and observations imply that the stream might have been perturbed by LMC. The two most distant stars among 11 detected members trace the stream’s length to 60 ​ ° 60° of sky, supporting and extending the previous length of 45 ​ ° 45° . Given that NGC 5466 is so distant and potentially has a longer tail than previously thought, we expect that NGC 5466 tidal stream could be a useful tool in constraining the Milky Way gravitational field.

. The discoveries of stellar streams offer an opportunity to probe the shape of the Galactic gravitational field
(

et al. 2015
;

, e.g.,)
and the formation history of the stellar halo
(

(

et al. 2008
;
Malhan
et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

Streams formed from globular clusters are typically thin and dynamically cold, and have proved to be a powerful tool for studying the Milky Way potential
(
Lux
et al. 2013
;
Küpper
et al. 2015
;
Bovy et al. 2016
;
Malhan &
Ibata 2019
, e.g.,)
. An example is the NGC 5466 tidal stream, which was independently detected by
Belokurov et al. 2006
and
Grillmair &
Johnson 2006
. In
Belokurov et al. 2006
, a
\sim 4°
stream was reported using neural networks to extract the probability distribution of the cluster stars. However, by applying an optimal matched filter technique to SDSS photometric data,
Grillmair &

found a
45°
stream extending from
\alpha\simeq 180°
to
\alpha\simeq 225°
. The authors also noted that a part of stream with
\alpha<190°
did not lie perfectly along computed orbits of the globular cluster but deviated from a smooth curve. They attributed this “deviation” to irregularities of the Galactic halo potential or a weak encounter with a mass concentration in the disk. The claim of
45°

Fellhauer et al. 2007

\ga 100°
tails was produced. By investigating the “deviation”,
Lux
et al. 2012
examined the constrains provided by the stream on the Galactic halo shape and pointed out that only for either oblate or fully triaxial halo potentials such “deviation” was possible.

Although a long stellar stream around NGC 5466 was reported, no individual members of the stream have been distinguished until two recent pioneering works. In
Ibata
et al. 2021
, more than 20 member stars of the stream were successfully detected for the first time with
STREAMFINDER
algorithm
(
Malhan &
Ibata 2018
)
. In
Jensen
et al. 2021

\sim 31°

\alpha\la 200°
in
Grillmair &
Johnson 2006
might be not real, where few stream members were detected and the trajectory of their model deviated from the claimed matched filter path.

Given the contending conclusions about the stream’s length and its significant functionality in unraveling the Milky Way halo shape
(
Lux
et al. 2012
;
Lux
et al. 2013
)
, in this work we aim to search for supplementary member stars, especially for those located far from the globular cluster, to provide more conclusive results on the length and trajectory of the NGC 5466 tidal stream. The paper is organized as follows. In Section

For each region, it is assumed that the velocity distribution can be described as a combination of two Gaussians, one for the stream stars and the other for the Milky Way field stars. The component of stream stars has a mean
V_{r}^{st}
, a dispersion
\sigma_{V_{r}}^{st}
and a fraction
f^{st}

V_{r}^{mw}
and a dispersion
\sigma_{V_{r}}^{mw}

\Theta=\{f^{st},V_{r}^{st},\sigma_{V_{r}}^{st},V_{r}^{mw},\sigma_{V_{r}}^{mw}\}

\mathcal{L}^{i}(V_{r}^{i}|\Theta)=f^{st}\mathcal{N}(V_{r}^{i}|V_{r}^{st},\sigma_{V_{r}}^{st})+(1-f^{st})\mathcal{N}(V_{r}^{i}|V_{r}^{mw},\sigma_{V_{r}}^{mw}),

(2)

\mathcal{N}(x|\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}{\rm exp}\left[-\frac{(x-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right].

(3)

\mathcal{P}(\Theta|V_{r})\varpropto\mathcal{L}(V_{r}|\Theta)\mathcal{I}(\Theta),

(4)

where

4.2
Adding the Large Magellanic Cloud

El-Falou &
Webb 2022
has discussed the effects of dwarf galaxies on the tidal tails of Globular Clusters. They claimed that NGC 5466 stream could be perturbed due to one or more close interactions with the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC). Motivated by this, we examine the smooth Galactic potential with an addition of LMC. This is also done through
Gala

Following
El-Falou &
Webb 2022

(
Hernquist 1990
)
as the internal potential of LMC:

\Phi_{\rm LMC}(r^{\prime})=\frac{-GM_{\rm LMC}}{r^{\prime}+a_{\rm LMC}}

Collaboration et al. 2018

4.1

Hence the “deviation” of NGC 5466 tidal stream is possibly due to the effect from LMC.

5
Discussion

As introduced in Section
1
, different results on the length of this tenuous stream have been concluded. To break the deadlock, this work is dedicated to search for the stream’s member stars which could be a direct indicator of the stream’s length. Compared to a
31°
tail traced by
Jensen
et al. 2021
, our results contend a
\sim 60°
long stream from S1 at leading side to S2 at trailing side. S1 is
\sim 27°
away from the cluster (
\alpha=211.3640°
), which supports the statement of
Grillmair &
Johnson 2006
that the stream extends
\sim 30°
towards the leading direction. S2 has a celestial position of
(\alpha,\delta)
=
(242.6515°,9.7092°)
beyond the sky coverage of data used in
Grillmair &
Johnson 2006
and
Jensen
et al. 2021
.

Although a longer stream is traced in this work, it should be noted that some of members are far apart from the others. For example, S2 and No.12 are spaced by 22
°
in
\alpha
, between which there is no star detected. We consider that this might be caused by incompleteness of the spectroscopic data, and we do some tests to illustrate it. In Fig.
9
, just using
Gaia
data, we obtain about 850 stream candidates through sky position, CMD and proper motion cuts based on the model stream and plot them with the red points. It can be seen that those candidates are generally continuously distributed in space, although there must be contaminants because they still need to be selected through metallicity and radial velocity which are not provided by
Gaia
. The number of stars is reduced substantially after cross-matching with LAMOST and SEGUE data, only 36 common stars remained as shown with the blue squares in the figure. There are large gaps now. Therefore, we think the number of spectra is very limited and most (even more than 95%) of
Gaia
sources are lost during cross-matching, which probably include stream stars that we need. In other words, some stream stars are not observed in those spectroscopic surveys. However, we can not completely exclude that the stream truly has large spatial gaps due to some physical mechanism, because the data about the stream we have are limited yet. It is expected that this problem could be solved through more detailed observations provided by the upcoming surveys.

Individual stream members can be used to constrain the Galactic potential
(
Lux
et al. 2013
;
Küpper
et al. 2015
;
Bovy et al. 2016
;
Malhan &

. Therefore, the search for those members are fundamental and crucial. We anticipate that our results could be helpful in detecting more stream stars of NGC 5466 in the later observations, such as the follow-up spectroscopic campaign PFS-SSP (Subaru Strategic Program with Prime Focus Spectrograph) with deeper limiting magnitude.

6
Summary

With astrometric and photometric data from
Gaia
EDR3, spectroscopic data from SDSS/SEGUE DR16 and LAMOST DR8, 11 member stars of the NGC 5466 tidal stream are identified after applying a variety of cuts in sky position, color-magnitude diagram, metallicity, proper motion and radial velocity. Among the 11 stars, 4 have been detected by
Jensen
et al. 2021
and 7 are newly identified. By modeling the cluster’s disruption, a mock stream in concordance with observation is reproduced under a smooth Galactic potential combined with the Large Magellanic Cloud, which might have perturbed the stream. The detection of S1 at leading side supports the claim of
Grillmair &
Johnson 2006
. From S1 to S2, another highly probable member at trailing tail, the stream’s length is traced to
60°
of sky. Given that NGC 5466 is so distant and potentially has a longer tail than previously thought, we expect that like Pal 5 and GD-1, NGC 5466 tidal stream will be another useful tool in constraining the Milky Way potential.

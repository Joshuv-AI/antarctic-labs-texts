Maximum tolerable excess noise in CV-QKD and improved lower bound on two-way capacities
Francesco Anna Mele, Ludovico Lami, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract




The two-way capacities of quantum channels determine the ultimate entanglement and secret-key distribution rates achievable by two distant parties that are connected by a noisy transmission line, in absence of quantum repeaters. Since repeaters will likely be expensive to build and maintain, a central open problem of quantum communication is to understand what performances are achievable without them. In this paper, we find a new lower bound on the energy-constrained and unconstrained two-way quantum and secret-key capacities of all phase-insensitive bosonic Gaussian channels, namely thermal attenuator, thermal amplifier, and additive Gaussian noise, which are realistic models for the noise affecting optical fibres or free-space links. Ours is the first nonzero lower bound on the two-way quantum capacity in the parameter range where the (reverse) coherent information becomes negative, and it shows explicitly that entanglement distribution is always possible when the channel is not entanglement breaking. This completely solves a crucial open problem of the field, namely, establishing the maximum excess noise which is tolerable in continuous-variable quantum key distribution. In addition, our construction is fully explicit, i.e. we devise and optimise a concrete entanglement distribution and distillation protocol that works by combining recurrence and hashing protocols
.

Theorem 1

Improved lower bound on two-way capacities

While (
7

Here we do precisely that, finding a new lower bound on the two-way capacities of the piBGCs that constitutes a significant improvement upon the state-of-the-art lower bounds

;
Pirandola18
;

;

The best known lower bounds on the two-way capacities of the piBGCs, prior to our work, are the (reverse)
coherent information
lower bounds

;
holwer

;
reviewEDP_dur
to distil entanglement. Our protocol improves on both steps. It draws inspiration from techniques used for distilling entanglement from two-qubit Werner states

;

Bennett-error-correction
;
Bennett-distillation-mixed
;
reviewEDP_dur
before the hashing protocol. In essence, our protocol involves sending halves of a suitably encoded finite-dimensional entangled state into the channel; projecting the channel’s output into an appropriate finite-dimensional subspace; applying recurrence protocols

;
Bennett-distillation-mixed
;
reviewEDP_dur
;

Discussion

In this work, we have determined a simple necessary and sufficient condition for achieving continuous-variable quantum-key distribution without intermediate nodes
Pirandola20
and entanglement distribution without quantum repeaters
repeaters
;
Munro2015
: point-to-point key distribution and entanglement distribution across an optical link of transmissivity
\lambda
and added thermal noise
\nu
are achievable if and only if
\lambda>\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
. This solves completely the problem of determining the ultimate limitations to CV-QKD imposed by the laws of quantum physics
Pirandola18
. By leveraging this result, we have established the maximum achievable distance of CV-QKD, demonstrating that the current Internet infrastructure can in principle support CV-QKD only up to distances of approximately
990\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{m}
, which is not so far from the distance records of approximately
200\,\mathrm{km}
achieved by state-of-the-art experiments
Record1
;
Record2
;
Record3
;
Record4
;
Record5
. Consequently, surpassing these distance records by an order of magnitude at standard telecom wavelengths will necessarily require the use of intermediate nodes.

Moreover, we have derived the best lower bound to date on the two-way quantum and secret-key capacities of all phase-insensitive bosonic Gaussian channels in the regime of large noise — in a large parameter region, ours is in fact the
only
non-zero lower bound. Our new bound constitutes a significant improvement upon state-of-the-art lower bounds
Ottaviani_new_lower
;
Pirandola2009
;
Pirandola18
;
Wang_Q2_amplifier
;
Noh2020
. We remark that the last improvement on the (unconstrained) two-way quantum capacity prior to our work dates back to 2009
Pirandola2009
. It was an open question whether the latter could equal the true two-way quantum capacities: our work provides a negative answer to this question, showing that entanglement distribution is possible in a much broader parameter region than previously known. Our results are fully explicit: to prove our lower bound, we constructed a general yet relatively simple entanglement distribution (and hence also key distribution) protocol that works whenever the underlying channel is not entanglement breaking.

In conclusion, we have discovered new protocols and fundamental limitations to quantum communication across optical links, establishing the ultimate noise threshold at which entanglement and secret keys can be distributed. This is likely to bear a significant impact on the design of practical QKD and entanglement distribution protocols on optical networks.

\Phi

(

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\Phi)\leq K(\Phi)\,,

\pazocal{H}

I.2
Two-way capacities of a quantum channel

The two-way quantum capacity
Q_{2}(\Phi)
and the secret-key capacity
K(\Phi)
of a quantum channel
\Phi
are the maximum achievable rate of qubits and secret-key bits, respectively, that can be reliably transmitted through
\Phi
by assuming that the sender Alice and the receiver Bob have free access to a public, noiseless, two-way classical communication line. The rate of qubits (resp. secret-key bits) is defined as the ratio between the number of reliably transmitted qubits (resp. secret-key bits) and the number of uses of
\Phi

[
14
, Chapters 14 and 15]
. An ebit is a Bell state
\ket{\psi_{00}}_{AB}
shared between Alice and Bob. For any
\Phi
, the two-way capacities satisfy

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\Phi)\leq K(\Phi)\,.

(S15)

Indeed, by recalling that Alice and Bob can freely send an infinite amount of bits to each other, an ebit can generate a secret-key bit, thanks to E91 protocol
[
16
]
, and hence
Q_{2}(\Phi)\leq K(\Phi)
. The two-way quantum capacity
Q_{2}(\Phi)
and the secret-key capacity
K(\Phi)
are collectively called the
two-way capacities of
\Phi
.

In practice, Alice has access to a limited budget (
N_{s}
) of energy to produce each input signal. Here, by definition, the energy of a signal initialised in a state
\rho
is equal to its mean photon number
\Tr[\rho\,a^{\dagger}a]
. Fixed
N_{s}>0
, the energy-constrained (EC) two-way capacities
Q_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})
and
K(\Phi,N_{s})
are defined as above but the maximisation of the rate is restricted to the strategies such that the average photon number less or equal to
N_{s}
. In other words,
N_{s}
is the maximum allowed average photon number of the input signals to the channel
\Phi
.
In addition note that the generalisation of
S15
to the EC case holds, i.e.

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})\leq K(\Phi,N_{s})\,,

(S16)

and that any EC capacity is upper bounded by the corresponding unconstrained capacity and tends to it in the limit
N_{s}\rightarrow\infty
.

I.3
Entanglement distillation

The goal of an entanglement distillation protocol is to turn a large number
n
of copies of a bipartite entangled state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{A}\otimes\pazocal{H}_{B})
shared between Alice and Bob into a smaller number
m
of ebits by LOCCs (local operations and classical communication). The yield of an entanglement distillation protocol is defined by the ratio
m/n
. The two-way distillable entanglement
E_{d}(\rho_{AB})\,
of
\rho_{AB}
is defined as the maximum yields over all the possible entanglement distillation protocols
[
38
]

[
14
, Chapter 8]
. The state
\rho_{AB}
is said to be
distillable
if
E_{d}(\rho_{AB})>0
. The coherent information of
\rho_{AB}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptI_{\text{c}}(\rho_{AB})\coloneqq S(\Tr_{A}\rho_{AB})-S(\rho_{AB})

(S17)

and it is a yield achievable by an entanglement distillation protocol which requires classical communication only from Alice to Bob
[
37
]
.
By exchanging the roles of Alice and Bob in such an entanglement distillation protocol, the reverse coherent information of
\rho_{AB}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptI_{\text{rc}}(\rho_{AB})\coloneqq S(\Tr_{B}\rho_{AB})-S(\rho_{AB})\,,

(S18)

is a yield achievable by an entanglement distillation protocol which only requires classical communication only from Bob to Alice
[
37
]
. In particular, the following inequality, known as
hashing inequality
, holds:

E_{d}(\rho_{AB})\geq\max\{I_{\text{c}}(\rho_{AB})\,,I_{\text{rc}}(\rho_{AB})\}\,.

(S19)

Let us briefly link the notions of distillable entanglement
E_{d}
and two-way quantum capacity
Q_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})
. Suppose that Alice produces
n
copies of a state
\rho_{AA^{\prime}}
such that the mean photon number of the half
A^{\prime}
is less or equal to
N_{s}
. Then, she uses
n
times the channel
\Phi
to send the halves
A^{\prime}
, which satisfy the energy constraint, to Bob. Hence,
n
copies of
\Id_{A}\otimes\Phi(\rho_{AA^{\prime}})
are shared between Alice and Bob and can be used to generate ebits by means of an entanglement distillation protocol. Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})\geq E_{d}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\Phi(\rho_{AA^{\prime}})\right)

(S20)

for all
N_{s}\geq 0
and all
\rho_{A^{\prime}A}
satisfying
\Tr[a^{\dagger}a\,\rho_{A^{\prime}A}]\leq N_{s}
, where
a
denotes the annihilation operator on
A^{\prime}
.

If a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
is such that the hashing inequality is trivial (i.e. the right-hand side of (
S19
) is negative), in order to obtain a non-trivial lower bound on
E_{d}(\rho_{AB})
, one can adopt a sufficiently large number of iterations of a
recurrence protocol
on
\rho_{AB}
prior to apply the hashing inequality. In the context of entanglement distillation, the goal of a recurrence protocol is to transform a certain number of copies of the state
\rho_{AB}
into fewer copies of another state
\rho^{\prime}_{AB}
such that
\bra{\psi_{00}}\rho^{\prime}_{AB}\ket{\psi_{00}}>\bra{\psi_{00}}\rho_{AB}\ket{\psi_{00}}

[
39
,
40
,
38
]
. Examples of recurrence protocols for qubits can be found in
[
40
,
48
,
49
]
, and their generalisations to the case of qudits in
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
. In the present paper we will exploit the recently introduced P1-or-P2 recurrence protocol
[
41
]
. To achieve a nonzero yield, one may adopt a suitable number of iterations of a recurrence protocol and then apply the hashing or breeding protocol
[
39
,
40
]
.
The latter protocols, which exploit only one-way classical communication, achieves the yield of the hashing inequality in (
S19
).
Improvements of the hashing and breeding protocols, which exploit two-way classical communication, have been provided in
[
42
]
: the two-way distillable entanglement of a convex combination of Bell states
\rho_{AB}\coloneqq\sum_{ij=0}^{1}\alpha_{ij}\ket{\psi_{ij}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{ij}}
is lower bounded by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle Y(\alpha_{00},\alpha_{01},\alpha_{10},\alpha_{11})\coloneqq\max\left(0,1-H(\{\alpha_{ij}\})+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{00}+\alpha_{10})(\alpha_{11}+\alpha_{01})\left[H_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{00}}{\alpha_{00}+\alpha_{10}}\right)+H_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{11}}{\alpha_{01}+\alpha_{11}}\right)\right]\right),

(S21)

with
H(\{\alpha_{ij}\})\coloneqq-\sum_{m,n=0}^{1}\alpha_{mn}\log_{2}\alpha_{mn}
being the Shannon entropy and
H_{2}(x)\coloneqq-x\log_{2}x-(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)
for all
x\in[0,1]
being the binary entropy. The yield in (
S21
) is larger than the yield achieved by the hashing protocol, which is
I_{\text{c}}\left(\sum_{ij=0}^{1}\alpha_{ij}\ket{\psi_{ij}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{ij}}\right)=1-H(\{\alpha_{ij}\})
. Protocols with larger yiels than (
S21
) may be obtained by exploiting the numerical methods introduced in
[
53
]
.

Now, let us briefly review the definition, the relevant properties, and the known bounds on the two-way capacities of phase-insensitive bosonic Gaussian channels, namely thermal attenuator, thermal amplifier, and additive Gaussian noise.

I.4
Thermal attenuator

Let
\pazocal{H}_{S}
and
\pazocal{H}_{E}
be single-mode systems and let
a
and
b
denote their annihilation operators, respectively.
For all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\nu\geq 0
, a thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}:\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S})\to\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S})
is a quantum channel defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\rho)\coloneqq\Tr_{E}\left[U_{\lambda}^{SE}\big(\rho^{S}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{E}\big){U_{\lambda}^{SE}}^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(S22)

where
U_{\lambda}^{SE}
denotes the unitary operator associated with a beam splitter of transmissivity
\lambda
, i.e.

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptU_{\lambda}^{SE}\coloneqq\exp\left[\arccos\sqrt{\lambda}\left(a^{\dagger}b-a\,b^{\dagger}\right)\right]\,,

(S23)

and
\tau_{\nu}\in\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{E})
denotes the thermal state with mean photon number equal to
\nu
. The beam splitter unitary can be expressed via the following disentangling formula
[
54
, Appendix 5]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptU_{\lambda}^{SE}=e^{-\sqrt{\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}}ab^{\dagger}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\ln\lambda\,\left(a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b\right)}e^{\sqrt{\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}}a^{\dagger}b}\,.

(S24)

By writing the quadrature vector
\mathbf{\hat{R}}
with respect the ordering
(S,E)
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{R}}\,U_{\lambda}^{SE}=S_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{\hat{R}}\,,

(S25)

where

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptS_{\lambda}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\lambda}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}&\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\\
-\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}&\,\sqrt{\lambda}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S26)

This implies that for all
\sigma_{SE}\in\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S}\otimes H_{E})
it holds that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\left(U^{SE}_{\lambda}\sigma_{SE}\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\right)=S_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{m}(\sigma_{SE})\,,

(S27)

\displaystyle V\left(U^{SE}_{\lambda}\sigma_{SE}\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\right)=S_{\lambda}\,V(\sigma_{SE})\,S_{\lambda}^{\intercal}\,.

In terms of the annihilation operators
a
and
b
, the transformation in (
S25
) reads

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}a\,U_{\lambda}^{SE}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\lambda}\,a+\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,b\,,

(S28)

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}^{SE}a\,\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\lambda}\,a-\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,b\,,

\displaystyle\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}b\,U_{\lambda}^{SE}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,a+\sqrt{\lambda}\,b\,,

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}^{SE}b\,\left(U_{\lambda}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,a+\sqrt{\lambda}\,b\,.

It can be shown that for any single-mode state
\rho
it holds that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\rho)\right)=\sqrt{\lambda}\,\mathbf{m}(\rho)\,,

(S29)

\displaystyle V\left(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\rho)\right)=\lambda\,V(\rho)+(1-\lambda)(2\nu+1)\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,

and, in terms of the characteristic function, for all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\rho)}(\mathbf{r})=\chi_{\rho}(\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbf{r})e^{-\frac{1}{4}(1-\lambda)(2\nu+1)|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}\,.

(S30)

By exploiting (
S30
) and the fact that quantum states and characteristic functions are in one-to-one correspondence, for all
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1]
and
\nu\geq 0
the following composition rule holds:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{1},\nu}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{2},\nu}=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},\nu}\,.

(S31)

In Theorem
S5
we will provide a simple Kraus representation of the thermal attenuator.

Bounds on two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator

The best known upper bound on the two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator, shown by Pirandola-Laurenza-Ottaviani-Banchi (PLOB)
[
17
]
, is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptK(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}-h(\nu)-\log_{2}[(1-\lambda)\lambda^{\nu}],&\text{if $\lambda\in(\frac{\nu}{\nu+1},1]$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(S32)

where

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pth(\nu)\coloneqq(\nu+1)\log_{2}(\nu+1)-\nu\log_{2}\nu

(S33)

is the so-called bosonic entropy. The parameter region in which such an upper bound vanishes coincides with the parameter region in which the thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
is entanglement breaking, i.e.
\nu\geq 0
and
\lambda\in[0,\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}]

[
33
,
34
]
.
The best known lower bound (before our work) on
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})
is given by
[
25
]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})\geq\max\{0,-h(\nu)-\log_{2}(1-\lambda)\}\,.

(S34)

Although this is also a lower bound on
K(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})
, it is not the best among those currently known. Indeed, an improved lower bound on
K(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})
has been shown by Ottaviani et al.
[
27
]
. In the energy-constrained case, the best known lower bound (before our work) on the EC two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator has been found by Noh-Pirandola-Jiang (NPJ)
[
26
]
, while the best known upper bound is — depending on the parameters
\lambda
,
\nu
, and
N_{s}
— the bound found by Davis-Shirokov-Wilde (DSW)
[
15
]
or the PLOB bound in (
S32
).

The lower bound in (
S34
) on the two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator can be proved first by applying (
S20
) with the choice
\rho_{AA^{\prime}}=\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}}_{AA^{\prime}}
, where
\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}
is the two-mode squeezed vacuum states with local mean photon number equal to
N_{s}
defined in (
S9
),
second by applying the hashing inequality in (
S19
), and finally by proving that the reverse coherent information satisfies

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N_{s}\rightarrow\infty}I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)=-h(\nu)-\log_{2}(1-\lambda)\,.

(S35)

Analogously, the coherent information

I_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)

and the reverse coherent information

I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)

are lower bounds on the EC two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
with energy constraint equal to
N_{s}
:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{I_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right),\,I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)\right\}\,

(S36)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}I_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right),&\text{if $N_{s}\leq\nu$,}\\
I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right),&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It holds that
[
22
,
17
,
25
,
26
]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptI_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)

\displaystyle=h\left(\lambda N_{s}+(1-\lambda)\nu\right)-h\left(\frac{D+(1-\lambda)(N_{s}-\nu)-1}{2}\right)-h\left(\frac{D-(1-\lambda)(N_{s}-\nu)-1}{2}\right)\,,

(S37)

\displaystyle I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)

\displaystyle=h(N_{s})-h\left(\frac{D+(1-\lambda)(N_{s}-\nu)-1}{2}\right)-h\left(\frac{D-(1-\lambda)(N_{s}-\nu)-1}{2}\right)\,,

where
D\coloneqq\sqrt{\left((1+\lambda)N_{s}+(1-\lambda)\nu+1\right)^{2}-4\lambda N_{s}(N_{s}+1)}
. The NPJ lower bound, proved by mixing forward (coherent information) and backward (reverse coherent information) strategies, is
[

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1],\,N_{1},N_{2}\geq 0\\
xN_{1}+(1-x)N_{2}=N_{s}\end{subarray}}\left[x\,I_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{1}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{1}}})\right)+(1-x)I_{\text{rc}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{2}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{2}}})\right)\right]\,.

(S38)

Fixed

and
\nu

I.5
Thermal amplifier

Let
\pazocal{H}_{S}
and
\pazocal{H}_{E}
be single-mode systems and let
a
and
b
denote their annihilation operators, respectively.
For all
g\geq 1
and
\nu\geq 0
, a thermal amplifier
\Phi_{g,\nu}:\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S})\to\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S})
is a quantum channel defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{g,\nu}(\rho)\coloneqq\Tr_{E}\left[U_{g}^{SE}\big(\rho^{S}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{E}\big){U_{g}^{SE}}^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(S39)

where
U_{g}^{SE}
denotes the unitary operator associated with two-mode squeezing of parameter
g
, i.e.

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptU_{g}^{SE}\coloneqq\exp\left[\arccosh\sqrt{g}\left(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-a\,b\right)\right]\,.

(S40)

The two-mode squeezing unitary can be expressed via the following disentangling formula
[
54
, Appendix 5]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptU_{g}^{SE}=e^{\sqrt{\frac{g-1}{g}}a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)\,\left(a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b+1\right)}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{g-1}{g}}ab}\,.

(S41)

By writing the quadrature vector
\mathbf{\hat{R}}
with respect the ordering
(S,E)
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{R}}\,U_{g}^{SE}=S_{g}\,\mathbf{\hat{R}}\,,

(S42)

where

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptS_{g}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{g}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\,&\,\sqrt{g-1}\,\sigma_{z}\\
\sqrt{g-1}\,\sigma_{z}\,&\,\sqrt{g}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S43)

This implies that for all
\sigma_{SE}\in\mathfrak{S}(\pazocal{H}_{S}\otimes H_{E})
it holds that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\left(U^{SE}_{g}\sigma_{SE}\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\right)=S_{g}\,\mathbf{m}(\sigma_{SE})\,,

(S44)

\displaystyle V\left(U^{SE}_{g}\sigma_{SE}\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}\right)=S_{g}\,V(\sigma_{SE})\,S_{g}^{\intercal}\,.

In terms of the annihilation operators
a
and
b
, the transformation in (
S42
) reads

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}a\,U_{g}^{SE}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g}\,a+\sqrt{g-1}\,b^{\dagger}\,,

(S45)

\displaystyle U_{g}^{SE}a\,\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g}\,a-\sqrt{g-1}\,b^{\dagger}\,,

\displaystyle\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}b\,U_{g}^{SE}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g-1}\,a^{\dagger}+\sqrt{g}\,b\,,

\displaystyle U_{g}^{SE}b\,\left(U_{g}^{SE}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{g-1}\,a^{\dagger}+\sqrt{g}\,b\,.

It can be shown that for any single-mode state
\rho
it holds that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\left(\Phi_{g,\nu}(\rho)\right)=\sqrt{g}\,\mathbf{m}(\rho)\,,

(S46)

\displaystyle V\left(\Phi_{g,\nu}(\rho)\right)=g\,V(\rho)+(g-1)(2\nu+1)\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,

and, in terms of the characteristic function, for all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\chi_{\Phi_{g,\nu}(\rho)}(\mathbf{r})=\chi_{\rho}(\sqrt{g}\mathbf{r})e^{-\frac{1}{4}(g-1)(2\nu+1)|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}\,.

(S47)

By exploiting (
S47
) and the fact that quantum states and characteristic functions are in one-to-one correspondence, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\geq 1
and
\nu\geq 0
the following composition rule holds:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{g_{1},\nu}\circ\Phi_{g_{2},\nu}=\Phi_{g_{1}g_{2},\nu}\,.

(S48)

In Theorem
S5
we will provide a simple Kraus representation of the thermal amplifier.

Bounds on two-way capacities of the thermal amplifier

The best known upper bound on the two-way capacities of the thermal amplifier, shown by PLOB
[
17
]
, is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptK(\Phi_{g,\nu})

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}-h(\nu)+\log_{2}\left(\frac{g^{\nu+1}}{g-1}\right),&\text{if $g\in[1,1+\frac{1}{\nu})$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(S49)

where

is the bosonic entropy defined in (
S33
). The parameter region in which such an upper bound vanishes coincides with the parameter region in which the thermal amplifier
\Phi_{g,\nu}
is entanglement breaking, i.e.
\nu\geq 0
and
g\geq 1+\frac{1}{\nu}

[

,
34
]
. The best known lower bound (before our work) on
Q_{2}(\Phi_{\lambda,\nu})
is given by
[

]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{2}(\Phi_{g,\nu})\geq\max\left\{0,-h(\nu)+\log_{2}\left(\frac{g}{g-1}\right)\right\}\,,

(S50)

which can be proved, analogously as it has been done in (
S35
), by showing that the coherent information satisfies

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N_{s}\rightarrow\infty}I_{\text{c}}\left(\Id_{A}\otimes\Phi_{g,\nu}(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}})\right)=-h(\nu)+\log_{2}\left(\frac{g}{g-1}\right)\,.

(S51)

The best known lower bound on the secret-key capacity

has been shown by Wong-Ottaviani-Guo-Pirandola (WOGP)
[

]
. In the energy-constrained scenario, the best known lower bound is the NPJ bound
[

Additive Gaussian noise

Let
\pazocal{H}_{S}

In this section we establish properties of the channel composition between pure amplifier channel and pure loss channel.

Definition 1

For all

Lemma S3
.

The channel

is entanglement breaking if and only if
(1-\lambda)g\geq 1
.

Proof.

First, let us determine the parameter region of

and

where the channel

is entanglement breaking. Since

is a Gaussian channel, we can apply Lemma

. By using (
S72
), one can show that
\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}
transforms the first moment and the covariance matrix as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}(\rho)\right)=\sqrt{g\lambda}\,\mathbf{m}(\rho)\,,

(S67)

\displaystyle V\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}(\rho)\right)=g\lambda\,V(\rho)+(2g-1-g\lambda)\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,

for all quantum states
\rho
. Hence, Lemma
S2
establishes that
\Phi
is entanglement breaking if and only if there exists
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\text{ with }\alpha\geq i\,\Omega_{1}\,\text{ and }\,\gamma\geq i\lambda g\,\Omega_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt(2g-1-g\lambda)\,\mathbb{1}_{2}=\alpha+\gamma\,.

(S68)

The condition in (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt(2g-1-g\lambda)\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\geq i\,(1+\lambda g)\,\Omega_{1}\,.

(S69)

Indeed, if the condition in (

, i.e. also the condition in (
S69

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptx\,\mathbb{1}_{2}\geq i\,\Omega_{1}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad x\geq 1\,,

(S70)

implies that

and

and by using (

maximally entangled state
, then the state
C_{AB}\coloneqq\Id_{A}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\ket{\psi}\!\!\bra{\psi}_{AA^{\prime}})
is simply referred to as the Choi state of the channel
\Phi
. Additionally, it is well known that a quantum channel is completely characterised by its Choi state
[
14
]
. The following lemma extends this result, showing that a quantum channel can also be completely characterised in terms of its
generalised
Choi state.

Lemma S8
.

Let
\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}
be a quantum channel and let be
C_{AB}
a generalised Choi state. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(X_{A^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left(X_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\,C_{AB}^{t_{A}}\,(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\right]\quad\text{for all linear operators }X_{A^{\prime}}\,,

(S103)

where, by using the notation introduced in Definition
S7
,
D_{A}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{-1/2}\ket{e_{i}}\!\!\bra{e_{i}}_{A}
and
t_{A}
is the partial transpose on
A
, i.e.
(\ket{e_{i}}\!\!\bra{e_{j}}_{A})^{t_{A}}=\ket{e_{j}}\!\!\bra{e_{i}}_{A}
for all
i,j
.

Proof.

By exploiting that
(\ket{e_{i}}_{A})_{i}
are orthonormal, we have that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}\!\left(\ket{e_{i}}\!\!\bra{e_{j}}_{A^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}}\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left(\ket{e_{j}}\!\!\bra{e_{i}}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,C_{AB}\right],\quad\forall\,i,j\,.

(S104)

By writing

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptX_{A^{\prime}}=\sum_{ij}\bra{e_{i}}X\ket{e_{j}}\ket{e_{i}}\!\!\bra{e_{j}}_{A^{\prime}}

(S105)

and by exploiting the linearity of
\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}
, it thus follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(X_{A^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left((D_{A}X_{A}D_{A})^{t_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,C_{AB}\right]

(S106)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left(D_{A}X_{A}D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,C_{AB}^{t_{A}}\right]

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left(X_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\,C_{AB}^{t_{A}}\,(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\right]\,.

∎

In finite dimensions, it is well known that a quantum channel is entanglement breaking if and only if its Choi state is separable. The following lemma generalises this result, demonstrating that a quantum channel is entanglement breaking if and only if its
generalised
Choi state is separable.

Lemma S9
.

Let
\Phi
be a quantum channel and let
C_{AB}
be a generalised Choi state of
\Phi
. Then,
\Phi
is entanglement breaking if and only if
C_{AB}
is separable.

Proof.

By definition, if
\Phi
is entanglement breaking, then
\Id_{R}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\rho_{RA^{\prime}})
is separable for all bipartite states
\rho_{RA^{\prime}}
. In particular, any generalised Choi state of an entanglement breaking channel is separable.

Conversely, let us assume that the generalised Choi state
C_{AB}
is separable, that is there exists a probability distribution
p_{x}
and states
(\rho_{A}^{(x)})_{x},(\sigma_{B}^{(x)})_{x}
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptC_{AB}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\,\rho_{A}^{(x)}\otimes\sigma_{B}^{(x)}\,.

(S107)

Let us show that
\Phi
is entanglement breaking. To this end let us consider an arbitrary bipartite state
\rho_{RA^{\prime}}
and let us show that
\Id_{R}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\rho_{RA^{\prime}})
is separable. By exploiting Lemma
S8
, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Id_{R}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\rho_{RA^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\!\left[\left(\rho_{RA}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\,C_{AB}^{t_{A}}\,(D_{A}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\right)\right]

(S108)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p_{x}\,\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]\otimes\sigma_{B}^{(x)}\,.

Since the operator
D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}
is positive semidefinite, we can write its spectral decomposition as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptD_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}=\sum_{i}\eta^{(x)}_{i}\ket{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}\!\!\bra{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}

(S109)

with the eigenvalues
(\eta^{(x)}_{i})_{i}
being positive. This implies that the operator
\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]
is positive semidefinite, as it can be written as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]=\sum_{i}\eta^{(x)}_{i}\bra{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}_{A}\rho_{RA}\ket{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}_{A}\,.

(S110)

and
\bra{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}_{A}\rho_{RA}\ket{\phi^{(x)}_{i}}_{A}
is positive semidefinite. In particular, the trace of the operator
\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]
vanishes if and only if it is the zero operator. Consequently, (
S108
) implies that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Id_{R}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\rho_{RA^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:\,q_{x}\neq 0}q_{x}\,\omega_{R}^{(x)}\otimes\sigma_{B}^{(x)}\,,

(S111)

where we defined

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptq_{x}

\displaystyle\coloneqq p_{x}\Tr_{RA}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]\,,

(S112)

\displaystyle\omega_{R}^{(x)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]}{\Tr_{RA}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]}\,.

The fact that the operator
\Tr_{A}\!\left[\rho_{RA}\,\left(\mathbb{1}_{R}\otimes D_{A}\,(\rho_{A}^{(x)})^{t_{A}}\,D_{A}\right)\right]
is positive semidefinite implies that
(q_{x})_{x}
is a probability distribution and that
\omega_{R}^{(x)}
is a quantum state. Hence, we conclude that
\Id_{R}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}(\rho_{RA^{\prime}})
is separable.
∎

The following theorem establishes that the energy-constrained two-way capacities of a single-mode Gaussian channel are strictly positive if and only if the channel is not entanglement breaking.

Theorem S10
.

Let
\Phi
be a single-mode Gaussian channel and let
N_{s}>0
. The energy-constrained two-way quantum capacity
Q_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})
and secret-key capacity
K(\Phi,N_{s})
are strictly positive if and only if
\Phi
is not entanglement breaking.

Proof.

Since any entanglement-breaking channel has vanishing two-way capacities
[
14
]
, it suffices to consider the case where
\Phi
is not entanglement breaking. Assume that Alice prepares many copies of the two-mode squeezed vacuum state
\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}_{AA^{\prime}}
with mean local photon number
N_{s}
and sends the systems
A^{\prime}
through the Gaussian channel
\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}
. Now Alice and Bob share many copies of the two-mode Gaussian state
C_{N_{s}}\coloneqq\big(\Id_{A}\otimes\,\Phi_{A^{\prime}\to B}\big)(\ket{\Psi_{N_{s}}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N_{s}}}_{AA^{\prime}})
, which is a generalised Choi state of
\Phi

[
57
,
2
]
. As such,
C_{N_{s}}
is entangled, as established by Lemma
S9
. By exploiting the fact that a two-mode Gaussian state is entangled if and only if it is not PPT
[
30
,
31
,
2
]
, it thus follows that
C_{N_{s}}
is not PPT. Since any two-mode Gaussian state that it is not PPT is also
distillable

[
32
]
— i.e. it can be converted into ebits with a strictly positive rate — we conclude that
K(\Phi,N_{s})\geq Q_{2}(\Phi,N_{s})>0
.
∎

Proof.

After applying the Pauli-based twirling on the state
\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}
, the resulting state
\sigma^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}
is transformed into a Bell-diagonal form, that is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sigma^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}p_{ij}\ket{\psi^{(M)}_{ij}}\!\!\bra{\psi^{(M)}_{ij}}_{AB}\,,

(S166)

where
p_{ij}\coloneqq\bra{\psi^{(M)}_{ij}}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}\ket{\psi^{(M)}_{ij}}
and
\{\ket{\psi^{(M)}_{ij}}_{AB}\}_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}
are the Bell states defined in (
S125
). In particular, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptp_{00}+p_{10}

\displaystyle=\bra{0}_{A}\bra{0}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{0}_{A}\ket{0}_{B}+\bra{M}_{A}\bra{M}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{M}_{A}\ket{M}_{B}\,,

(S167)

\displaystyle p_{01}-p_{11}

\displaystyle=2\bra{0}_{A}\bra{0}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{M}_{A}\ket{M}_{B}\,,

\displaystyle p_{01}+p_{11}

\displaystyle=\bra{0}_{A}\bra{M}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{0}_{A}\ket{M}_{B}+\bra{M}_{A}\bra{0}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{M}_{A}\ket{0}_{B}\,,

\displaystyle p_{00}-p_{10}

\displaystyle=2\bra{0}_{A}\bra{0}_{B}\rho^{(g,\lambda,M,c)}_{AB}\ket{M}_{A}\ket{M}_{B}\,.

Lemma
S15
guarantees that if
p_{01}+p_{11}-|p_{00}-p_{10}|<0
then the state
\sigma^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}
is distillable. By using (
S163
) and (
S167
), the condition
p_{01}+p_{11}-|p_{00}-p_{10}|<0
is satisfied if and only if

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\bar{c}^{2}f_{0,0,M}(g,\lambda)+(1-\bar{c}^{2})f_{M,M,0}(g,\lambda)-2\bar{c}\sqrt{1-\bar{c}^{2}}f_{M,0,0}(g,\lambda)<0\,,

(S168)

that is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\bar{c}^{2}(g-1)^{M}+(1-\bar{c}^{2})(1-\lambda)^{M}g^{M}-2\bar{c}\sqrt{1-\bar{c}^{2}}(\lambda g)^{M/2}<0\,,

(S169)

where we have exploited (
S165
). By hypothesis, the channel
\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}
is entanglement breaking and hence
(1-\lambda)g<1
, as established by Lemma
S4
. Consequently, for all
g>1
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\bar{c}^{2}(g-1)^{M}+(1-\bar{c}^{2})(1-\lambda)^{M}g^{M}-2\bar{c}\sqrt{1-\bar{c}^{2}}(\lambda g)^{M/2}

\displaystyle<\bar{c}^{2}(g-1)^{M}+(1-\bar{c}^{2})-2\bar{c}\sqrt{1-\bar{c}^{2}}(g-1)^{M/2}

(S170)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{c}(g-1)^{M/2}-\sqrt{1-\bar{c}^{2}}\right)^{2}=0\,,

where we have used that
\bar{c}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(g-1)^{M}}}
. Hence, for all
M\in\mathds{N}^{+}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and
g>1
with
(1-\lambda)g<1
, it holds that
\sigma^{(g,\lambda,M,\bar{c})}_{AB}
is distillable.
∎

Lemma S15
.

Let
\{\ket{\psi_{ij}}\}_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}
be the Bell states defined in (
S1
). A convex combination of Bell states
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}p_{ij}\ket{\psi_{ij}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{ij}}
is distillable if and only if
p_{00}+p_{10}<|p_{01}-p_{10}|
or
p_{01}+p_{11}<|p_{00}-p_{10}|
.

Proof.

The matrix associated with
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}p_{ij}\ket{\psi_{ij}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{ij}}
, written with respect the basis
\{\ket{0}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}_{B},\ket{0}_{A}\otimes\ket{1}_{B},\ket{1}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}_{B},\ket{1}_{A}\otimes\ket{1}_{B}\}
, is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}p_{00}+p_{10}\quad&0&0&p_{00}-p_{10}\\
0&p_{10}+p_{11}\quad&p_{10}-p_{11}\quad&0\\
0&p_{10}-p_{11}\quad&p_{10}+p_{11}\quad&0\\
p_{00}-p_{10}&0&0&p_{00}+p_{10}\quad\end{matrix}\right)\,.

Its partial transpose on
B
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}p_{00}+p_{10}\quad&0&0&p_{10}-p_{11}\\
0&p_{10}+p_{11}\quad&p_{00}-p_{10}\quad&0\\
0&p_{00}-p_{10}\quad&p_{10}+p_{11}\quad&0\\
p_{10}-p_{11}&0&0&p_{00}+p_{10}\quad\end{matrix}\right)\,.

Hence, the state
\rho_{AB}
is PPT if and only if
p_{00}+p_{10}\geq|p_{01}-p_{10}|
and
p_{01}+p_{11}\geq|p_{00}-p_{10}|
. Consequently, the fact that any two-qubit state is distillable if and only if it is not PPT
[
59
]
implies the validity of the thesis.
∎

III.3
Experimental challenges regarding our protocol

As demonstrated in the main text, applying our main result regarding the maximum tolerable excess noise to the current Internet infrastructure shows that continuous-variable quantum key distribution is feasible if and only if the fibre length is approximately less than
1000
kilometres. Hence, any practical QKD protocol, which is based on the existing Internet infrastructure, must adhere to this fundamental limit. Furthermore, this limit of
1000
kilometres can now serve as a benchmark for evaluating the quality of any new CV-QKD protocol, underscoring the significant impact of our results on practical implementations.

The potential benefit of our protocol (presented both in the main text and in the proof of Theorem
S13
above) lies in its
faithfulness
— it can distil entanglement (and hence generate secret keys) whenever the channel is not entanglement breaking. With the current Internet infrastructure based on optical fibres, our protocol could
theoretically
achieve the ultimate limit set by quantum physics of transmitting entanglement and secret keys over distances up to
1000
kilometres. This is a unique feature of our protocol, which stands in stark contrast with
all
existing entanglement distribution and key distribution protocols.

While there exist CV-QKD protocols that are relatively easy to implement with current technology (capable of distributing secret keys across optical fibres of at most
200
kilometres
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
), this is not the case for entanglement distribution. Indeed,
all
known entanglement-distribution protocols are experimentally challenging with current technology. For example, the best known entanglement-distribution protocol prior to our work
[
25
]
— i.e. the hashing protocol applied to the Choi state of the channel — is not experimentally feasible.

Our protocol is an entanglement-distribution protocol and, as such, is experimentally challenging at present. We emphasise that this limitation is not unique to our protocol but is a common challenge faced by
all
entanglement distribution protocols due to current technological constraints. A major factor is the lack of a noiseless quantum memory, which makes it challenging to perform even a few iterations of a recurrence entanglement distillation protocol. Nevertheless, given the significant recent experimental advancements regarding quantum memories
[
60
,
61
]
and entanglement distillation
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
, we are optimistic about the future experimental viability of our protocol. Hence, we stress that, although our protocol is experimentally challenging with current technology, there is no way that a protocol as simple as our ours will
not
be realisable in a few decades at worst.

Let us provide further details about a possible practical realisation of our protocol. To perform Step 1, it suffices that Alice produces the state

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\ket{\Psi}_{\!AA^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{\ket{0}_{\!A}\!\otimes\!\ket{0}_{\!A^{\prime}}+\ket{1}_{\!A}\!\otimes\!\ket{1}_{\!A^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}

(S171)

in order to make the rate of the protocol faithful. However, without changing the rate (as explained below), Alice can instead produce the
NOON state

[
43
]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\ket{\Psi^{\prime}}_{\!AA^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{\ket{0}_{\!A}\!\otimes\!\ket{1}_{\!A^{\prime}}+\ket{1}_{\!A}\!\otimes\!\ket{0}_{\!A^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(S172)

which can be experimentally prepared
[
62
,
63
]
.
After Alice has sent the sub-system
A^{\prime}
through the channel to Bob, Step 2 involves performing a non-demolition measurement with the POVM operator
\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{B}+\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1}_{B}
. Although this measurement appears challenging to implement experimentally, fortunately the problem has been studied already, and several promising approaches do exist. Specifically, one may exploit either: the pre-certification scheme employed in
[
64
]
; the coupling scheme between optical signals and trapped cold atomic gas designed in
[
65
]
; single photon filters based on Rydberg blockade
[
66
,
67
]
to implement single photon subtraction
[
68
,
69
]
; single atoms inside an optical cavity to perform single photon subtraction
[
70
,
71
]
. After this non-demolition measurement, Alice and Bob share a two-mode state in the subspace spanned by
\{\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0},\ket{0}\otimes\ket{1},\ket{1}\otimes\ket{0},\ket{1}\otimes\ket{1}\}
. At this point, they can transfer their state from the optical modes to a qubit solid-state platform (e.g., superconducting or trapped ion platform). This transfer can be experimentally performed in several ways, for example by exploiting: the quantum-memory based approaches introduced in
[
60
,
61
]
; the aforementioned single photon subtraction methods
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
]
, which map the photonic state onto the atomic state; quantum transduction from optical to microwave photons that are compatible with the superconducting qubits
[
72
,
73
,
74
]
. This means that only the first two steps of the protocol involve optical platforms, which is advantageous because all the two-qubit unitaries used in the subsequent steps of our protocol are much easier to experimentally implement in a qubit platform. To address the fact that Alice has sent the NOON state in (
S172
) instead of the state in (
S171
), she simply needs to apply the Pauli
\sigma_{x}
before initiating Step 3.

[

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\ket{\phi^{(2)}_{0}}_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}

(S176)

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{(2)}_{2}}_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{(2)}_{3}}_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\ket{1}_{A_{1}}\otimes\ket{0}_{A_{2}}\otimes\ket{1}_{A_{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\ket{\Psi_{N,K}}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{K},A^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,A^{\prime}_{K}}

(S177)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{N+K-1}{N}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{n}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\

\binom{N+K-1}{N}

K
modes with total photon number equal to

K,F\in\mathds{N}

\Pi^{(K)}_{F}
onto the subspace of
K

(S178)

The following lemma will be useful in order to calculate the rate of our entanglement distribution protocol.

Lemma S16
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,1],g\geq 1,N\in\mathds{N},K\in\mathds{N}^{+}
, and
\textbf{n}\in\mathds{N}^{K}
such that
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N
. Assume that Alice transmits the
K
-mode Fock state
\ket{\textbf{n}}
to Bob via
K
parallel uses of the piBGC
\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}\coloneqq\Phi_{g,0}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}
and suppose further that Bob measures the total photon number of the
K
received modes. The probability
\mathcal{P}_{F}
that Bob gets the outcome
F\in\mathds{N}
is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{P}_{F}\coloneqq\Tr\left[\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\lambda^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\,.

(S179)

In particular, note that
\mathcal{P}_{F}
depends on
n
only through the total photon number
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N
.
Specifically, if the communication channel is the pure loss channel
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}=\mathcal{N}_{1,\lambda}
, the probability of getting the outcome
F\in\mathds{N}
is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\left[\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]

\displaystyle=\binom{N}{F}\lambda^{F}(1-\lambda)^{N-F}\Theta(N-F)\,,

(S180)

where we have introduced the Heaviside function
\Theta(x)
defined as
\Theta(x)=1
if
x\geq 0
, and
\Theta(x)=0
if
x<0
.
In addition, if the communication channel is the pure amplifier channel
\Phi_{g,0}=\mathcal{N}_{g,1}
, the probability of getting the outcome
F\in\mathds{N}
is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]

\displaystyle=\binom{K+F-1}{F-N}\frac{(g-1)^{F-N}}{g^{K+F}}\Theta(F-N)\,.

(S181)

Therefore, the probability
\mathcal{P}_{F}
in (
S179
) of getting
F
photons at the output of
K
parallel uses of the composition between pure loss channel and pure amplifier channel can be expressed as the sum over
P\in\mathds{N}
of the conditional probability of getting
F
photons at the output of the
K
pure amplifier channels conditioned on the event of getting
P
photons at the output of the
K
pure loss channels, multiplied by the probability of the latter event.

Proof.

As a consequence of (
S98
), for all
n\in\mathds{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}(\ket{n}\!\!\bra{n})=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\lambda^{l}(1-\lambda)^{n-l}\ket{l}\!\!\bra{l}

(S182)

and hence

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\leq\textbf{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)\lambda^{\|\textbf{l}\|_{1}}(1-\lambda)^{N-\|\textbf{l}\|_{1}}\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}\,,

(S183)

where the inequality between vectors
\textbf{a}\geq\textbf{b}
means that
a_{j}\geq b_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,K
.
Consequently, by using that
\sum_{P=0}^{\infty}\Pi^{(K)}_{P}=\mathbb{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{P}_{F}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\right)\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]=\sum_{P,P^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}\left(\Pi^{(K)}_{P}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\Pi^{(K)}_{P^{\prime}}\right)\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]

(S184)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=0}^{N}\lambda^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\leq\textbf{n}\\
\|\mathbf{l}\|_{1}=P\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}\left(\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}\right)\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]\,.

Moreover, (
S99
) implies that for all
l\in\mathds{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{g,0}(\ket{l}\!\!\bra{l})=\frac{1}{g^{l+1}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{l+m}{l}\left(\frac{g-1}{g}\right)^{m}\ket{m+l}\!\!\bra{m+l}

(S185)

and hence

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}})=\frac{1}{g^{P+K}}\sum_{\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)\left(\frac{g-1}{g}\right)^{\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}}\ket{\textbf{m}+\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{m}+\textbf{l}}\,.

(S186)

Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}\left(\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}\right)\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]=\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}=F-P\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}\,.

(S187)

The sum

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}=F-P\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}\,,

(S188)

which appears in (
S187
), is the coefficient of the term
x^{F-P}
of the power series
Q(x)
in the variable
x\in(0,1)
defined as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)x^{\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}}\,.

(S189)

By exploiting that for all
l\in\mathds{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+l}{m}x^{m}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{l+1}}\,,

(S190)

one obtains that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)x^{\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{K}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{l_{j}+m}{m}x^{m}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{K}\frac{1}{(1-x)^{l_{j}+1}}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{P+K}}

(S191)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{P+K+m-1}{m}x^{m}\,.

It follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{m}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{m}\|_{1}=F-P\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{l_{j}+m_{j}}{l_{j}}=\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\Theta(F-P)

(S192)

and hence

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\left[\Phi_{g,0}^{\otimes K}\left(\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}\right)\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]=\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\Theta(F-P)\,,

(S193)

where we have introduced the Heaviside function
\Theta(x)
defined as
\Theta(x)=1
if
x\geq 0
, and
\Theta(x)=0
if
x<0
.
Consequently, (
S184
) implies that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{P}_{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\lambda^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\leq\textbf{n}\\
\|\mathbf{l}\|_{1}=P\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)

(S194)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\lambda^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\,,

where in the last equality we have exploited that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\leq\textbf{n}\\
\|\mathbf{l}\|_{1}=P\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)=\binom{N}{P}\,.

(S195)

This follows from the fact that the sum in (
S195
) is equal to the coefficient of the term

x^{P}
of the following polynomial in the variable
x\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\leq\textbf{n}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_{j}}{l_{j}}\right)x^{\|\textbf{l}\|_{1}}=\prod_{j=1}^{K}(1+x)^{n_{j}}=(1+x)^{N}=\sum_{l=0}^{N}\binom{N}{l}x^{l}\,.

(S196)

\frac{x}{1-x}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\tau_{\frac{x}{1-x}}^{\otimes K}=(1-x)^{K}\sum_{\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}}x^{\|\textbf{l}\|_{1}}\ket{\textbf{l}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}\,.

(S197)

Consequently, the quantity

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{P}_{F}=\Tr\left[\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\,\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right]

(S198)

is the coefficient of the term
x^{F}
of the power series
P(x)
in the variable

defined as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptP(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(1-x)^{K}}\Tr\left[\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\,\tau_{\frac{x}{1-x}}^{\otimes K}\right]\,.

(S199)

By using the characteristic function properties reported in (
S2
), (
S3
), (
S72
), and the fact that the characteristic function of a thermal state
\tau_{\nu}
is
\chi_{\tau_{\nu}}(\mathbf{r})=e^{-\frac{1}{4}(2\nu+1)|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}
, one obtains that for any single-mode state
\rho
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\left[\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}(\rho)\,\tau_{\frac{x}{1-x}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}}{2\pi}\chi_{\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}(\rho)}(\mathbf{r})\,\chi_{\tau_{\frac{x}{1-x}}}(\mathbf{r})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}}{2\pi}\chi_{\rho}(\sqrt{g\lambda}\,\mathbf{r})e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(2g-g\lambda+2\frac{x}{1-x}\right)|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}

(S200)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}}{2\pi}\chi_{\rho}(\mathbf{r})e^{-\frac{1}{4g\lambda}\left(2g-g\lambda+2\frac{x}{1-x}\right)|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{g\lambda}\Tr\left[\rho\,\,\tau_{\frac{g-g\lambda+(1+g\lambda-g)x}{g\lambda(1-x)}}\right]\,.

Hence, by exploiting (
S190
) and the fact that
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N
, the power series
P(x)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptP(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{K}(g\lambda)^{K}}\Tr\left[\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}}\tau_{\frac{g-g\lambda+(1+g\lambda-g)x}{g\lambda(1-x)}}^{\otimes K}\right]=\frac{\left[g(1-\lambda)+(1+g\lambda-g)x\right]^{N}}{[g-(g-1)x]^{N+K}}

(S201)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=0}^{N}\binom{N}{P}(1+g\lambda-g)^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}g^{-P-K}x^{P}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{N+K-1+l}{l}\left(\frac{g-1}{g}\right)^{l}x^{l}\,.

It follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{P}_{F}=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\binom{N+K+F-P-1}{F-P}(1+g\lambda-g)^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{F+K}}\,.

(S202)

Incidentally, by comparing the two expressions of

in (

) and (

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\binom{K+F-1+N-P}{F-P}\left(\frac{g\lambda-(g-1)}{(1-\lambda)(g-1)}\right)^{P}=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\left(\frac{\lambda}{(1-\lambda)(g-1)}\right)^{P}\,.

(S203)

Let us now introduce an additional entanglement distribution protocol to distribute ebits across any piBGC
\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}
. The protocol depends on two parameters,

1.

Alice prepares the state
\ket{\Psi_{N,K}}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{K},A^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,A^{\prime}_{K}}
of

\displaystyle\Id_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\Psi_{N,K}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N,K}})

(S204)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{N+K-1}{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{n}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{i}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{i}\|_{1}=N\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\textbf{l}\geq\max(\textbf{i}-\textbf{n},\textbf{0})\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}f_{n_{j},i_{j},l_{j}}(g,\lambda)\right)\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{i}}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{K}}\otimes\ket{\textbf{l}+\textbf{n}-\textbf{i}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{l}}_{B_{1}\ldots B_{K}}\,,

where
\textbf{0}\in\mathds{N}^{K}
is the zero vector and the inequality between vectors

means that

for all

2.

, where

is the projector onto the subspace whose total photon number equals

(see (

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Tr\left[\left(\mathbb{1}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right)\,\left(\Id_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\Psi_{N,K}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N,K}})\right)\right]=\frac{1}{\binom{N+K-1}{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{n}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N\end{subarray}}\Tr\left[\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\,\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\textbf{n}}\!\!\bra{\textbf{n}})\right]

(S205)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=0}^{\min(F,N)}\binom{N}{P}\binom{K+F-1}{F-P}\lambda^{P}(1-\lambda)^{N-P}\frac{(g-1)^{F-P}}{g^{K+F}}\,,

where we have exploited Lemma
S16
. The post-measurement state

conditioned on the outcome

is given by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\rho^{(F)}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}B_{1}\ldots B_{k}}

(S206)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{F}}\left(\mathbb{1}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right)\left(\Id_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{g,\lambda}^{\otimes K}(\ket{\Psi_{N,K}}\!\!\bra{\Psi_{N,K}})\right)\left(\mathbb{1}_{A_{1}\ldots A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{(K)}_{F}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{F}\binom{N+K-1}{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{n}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{n}\|_{1}=N\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{i}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{i}\|_{1}=N\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{l}\in\mathds{N}^{K}\\
\|\textbf{l}\|_{1}=F\\

(S207)

otherwise

3.

(S215)

I_{\text{rc}}^{(F)}
is defined as

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptI_{\text{rc}}^{(F)}\coloneqq\log_{2}\binom{N+K-1}{N}-S\left(\sum_{n,i,h,l=0}^{d-1}\eta_{n,i,h,l}\ket{n}\!\!\bra{i}\otimes\ket{h}\!\!\bra{l}\right)\,,

(S236)

where
S(\cdot)
denotes the von Neumann entropy.
Finally, the term
\mathcal{I}_{d}(\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})})
is defined differently depending on the value of
d
:

•

If
d=2
, then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{I}_{2}(\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})})\coloneqq\mathcal{I}(\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{00},\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{01},\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{10},\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{11})\,,

(S237)

where
\mathcal{I}
is defined in (
S130
).

•

If
d>2
, then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{I}_{d}(\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})})\coloneqq\log_{2}d+\sum_{m,n=0}^{d-1}\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{mn}\log_{2}\alpha^{(F,\bar{k})}_{mn}\,.

(S238)

V
Results on the two-way capacities of piBGCs

In this subsection, for each of the piBGCs, first we determine the parameter region where the two-way capacities vanish, second we find a new lower bound on the two-way capacities, and finally we compare our results with the existing literature.

V.1
Results on the two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator

Let us consider the thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
of transmissivity
\lambda\in[0,1]
and thermal noise
\nu\geq 0
. Since the PLOB bound in (
S32
) vanishes for
\lambda\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
, it is already known that the two-way capacities of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
vanish for
\lambda<\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
. The following theorem establishes that also the vice-versa is true.

Theorem S18
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
N_{s}>0
. The energy-constrained two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})
and
K(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})
vanish if and only if
\lambda\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
, i.e. if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
is entanglement breaking. In particular, the same holds for the unconstrained two-way capacities.

Proof.

Theorem
S18
is a direct consequence of Lemma
S4
and Theorem
S12
.
∎

The validity of Theorem
S18
was not known before the present work. Indeed, in
[
12
,
4
]
the authors says that it is an open problem to determine the exact value of the maximum tolerable excess noise, which is defined by

\epsilon(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\max\{\nu\geq 0\,:\,K(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})>0\}\,.

(S239)

Theorem
S12
implies that
\varepsilon(\lambda)=1
for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Hence, we have answered to the question, which was deemed “crucial” in
[
12
, Section 7]
, “What is the maximum
excess noise that is tolerable in QKD? I.e., optimizing over all QKD protocols?”
In
[
12
,
4
]
the authors showed, by applying the PLOB bound, the upper bound
\varepsilon(\lambda)\leq 1
and provided also a lower bound on
\varepsilon(\lambda)
which was far from
1
.

Except for the special case
\nu=0
, it is an open question whether the reverse coherent information lower bound in Eq.
S34
equals the true two-way quantum capacity of the thermal attenuator
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})
: Theorem
S18
provides a negative answer to this question. Indeed, although
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})=0
if and only if
\lambda\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
(thanks to Theorem
S18
), the reverse coherent information lower bound vanishes for all
\lambda\leq 1-2^{-h(\nu)}
. Hence, since
1-2^{-h(\nu)}>\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}
for all
\nu>0
, the reverse coherent information lower bound is not equal to
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})
at least in the region
\nu>0
and
\lambda\in(\frac{\nu}{\nu+1},1-2^{-h(\nu)}]
. In the following theorem we obtain an improved lower bound on the two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator.

Theorem S19
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
N_{s}\geq 0
. The EC two-way capacities
Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})
and
K(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})
of the thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
satisfy the following lower bound

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptK(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})

\displaystyle\geq Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu},N_{s})\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in(0,1),\,M\in\mathds{N}^{+},\,k\in\mathds{N}\\
(1-c^{2})M\leq N_{s}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{R}\left(1+(1-\lambda)\nu,\frac{\lambda}{1+(1-\lambda)\nu},M,c,k\right)\,,

(S240)

and, in particular, the unconstrained two-way capacities satisfy

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptK(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})

\displaystyle\geq Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu})\geq\sup_{c\in(0,1),\,M\in\mathds{N}^{+},\,k\in\mathds{N}}\mathcal{R}\left(1+(1-\lambda)\nu,\frac{\lambda}{1+(1-\lambda)\nu},M,c,k\right)\,,

(S241)

where the quantity
\mathcal{R}
is defined in (
S158
).

Proof.

Theorem
S19
is a direct consequence of Lemma
S4
and Theorem
S14
.
∎

Theorem
S19
shows a new lower bound, reported in (
S241
), on the two-way capacities of the thermal attenuator
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
. Our new lower bound outperforms all the previous known lower bounds in a large region of the parameters
\lambda
and
\nu
.
In Fig.

a and in Fig.

where the transmissivity is chosen to be equal to

\lambda

(i.e. the reverse coherent information lower bound reported in (

)) vanishes. From Fig.

and

and it achieves the

of the PLOB bound for

. For example, if

and if the transmissivity is equal to

, its ratio with the PLOB bound is

, and the optimal parameters of the supremum present in the expression of our new bound in (

) are

,

, and

Our new bound can outperform also the best known lower bound (before our work) on the secret-key capacity

. As an example, for

of the supremum present in the expression of our new bound in (

We numerically observe that for all

Theorem
S19
shows also the bound in (

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\nu}
. This new lower bound can outperform the NPJ lower bound

.

.

Theorem S20

(Multi-rail lower bound)
.

For all

S17

∎

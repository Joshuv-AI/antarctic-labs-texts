Transverse-momentum-dependent gluon distributions of proton within basis light-front quantization
Hongyao Yu, Zhi Hu, Siqi Xu, Chandan Mondal, Xingbo Zhao, James P. Vary, (BLFQ Collaboration)

Abstract
Gluon transverse-momentum dependent distributions (TMDs) are very important for revealing the internal structure of the proton. They correspond to a variety of experiments and are among the major tasks of the future electron-ion colliders. In this paper, we calculate the T-even gluon TMDs at the leading twist within the Basis Light-front Quantization framework. We employ the light-front wave functions of the proton obtained from a light-front quantized Hamiltonian with Quantum Chromodynamics input determined for its valence Fock sector with three constituent quarks and an additional Fock sector, encompassing three quarks and a dynamical gluon, with a three-dimensional confinement. After obtaining the numerical results we investigate the properties of gluon TMDs by checking whether they satisfy the Mulders-Rodrigues inequalities and investigate the correlations between transverse and longitudinal degrees of freedom. With their connection to unintegrated gluon distributions in mind, we further investigate the limiting behaviours of gluon TMDs in the small- x x and large- x x regions.

Within the BLFQ framework we obtain light-front wave functions (LFWFs) by solving the following eigenvalue equation:

\displaystyle P^{-}P^{+}|P,\Lambda\rangle=M^{2}|P,\Lambda\rangle,

(1)

where
|P,\Lambda\rangle
is the bound state with momentum
P=~(P^{+}=\sum\limits_{i}p_{i}^{+},P^{-}=\frac{M^{2}}{P^{+}},P^{\perp}=0^{\perp})
,

40

41

\displaystyle|P,\Lambda\rangle=\Psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}=3}|qqq\rangle+\Psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}=4}|qqqg\rangle,

(2)

\Psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}=3}

With the truncation in Eq. (

P^{-}=P^{-}_{\textrm{QCD}}+P^{-}_{\textrm{C}}

\displaystyle P^{-}_{\textrm{QCD}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}x^{\perp}dx^{-}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}\frac{m^{2}_{0}+(i\partial^{\perp})^{2}}{i\partial^{+}}\psi\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}A^{i}_{a}[m^{2}_{g}+(i\partial^{\perp})^{2}]A^{i}_{a}+g_{s}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}T^{a}A^{\mu}_{a}\psi

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}g^{2}_{s}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}T^{a}\psi\frac{1}{(i\partial^{+})^{2}}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}T^{a}\psi\right\}.

(3)

The first two terms in Eq. (

m_{0}

A^{i}_{a}

g_{s}

44

[

]

We introduce confinement in the
|qqq\rangle
Fock-sector as:

\displaystyle P^{-}_{C}P^{+}=\frac{\kappa^{4}}{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\left\{\vec{r}_{ij\perp}^{2}-\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}(x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}})}{(m_{i}+m_{j})^{2}}\right\}.

(4)

\vec{r}_{ij\perp}=\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}(\vec{r}_{i\perp}-\vec{r}_{j\perp})

[
47
]

\partial_{x}\equiv(\partial/\partial x)_{r_{ij\perp}}

For our BLFQ basis space, we adopt a plane-wave basis in the longitudinal direction, which is confined in a one-dimensional box with length
2L
and accompanied by antiperiodic (periodic) boundary condition for the fermion (boson). Therefore, the longitude momentum is
p^{+}=2\pi k/L

\Phi_{nm}(\vec{p}_{\perp};b)
with energy scale
b

[
48
]

m

and the many-body basis states are identified as the direct product of Fock-particle basis states
|\alpha\rangle=\otimes_{i}|\alpha_{i}\rangle

|qqqg\rangle
has two color singlet states, so we need an additional index to identify the specific color singlet state.

We truncate the infinite basis by introducing cutoffs
K

in the longitudinal and transverse direction, respectively. The dimensionless variable

acts as truncation for the total energy of the 2D-HO basis states as:
N_{\mathrm{max}}\geq\sum\limits_{i}(2n_{i}+|m_{i}|+1)

implies the infrared (IR) and ultraviolet (UV) cutoffs in the transverse direction as
\lambda_{\mathrm{IR}}\backsimeq b/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{max}}}

\lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}\backsimeq b\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{max}}}

After diagonalizing the Hamiltonian matrix, we obtain the proton mass

\displaystyle\Psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}}(\{p_{i},\lambda_{i}\})=\sum\limits_{\{n_{i},m_{i}\}}\psi_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Lambda}(\{\alpha_{i}\})\prod\limits^{\mathcal{N}}_{i=1}\phi_{n_{i},m_{i}}(\vec{p}^{\perp}_{i},b).

(5)

Here

of each Fock-particle, and
\psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}=3}(\{\alpha_{i}\})

\psi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{N}=4}(\{\alpha_{i}\})

|qqqg\rangle

4.1
Positivity bounds for gluon TMDs

When all T-odd gluon TMDs vanish, the positivity bounds from Ref.
[
61
]
reduce to the Mulders-Rodrigues inequalities
[
20
,
31
,
36
]
:

\displaystyle f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\geq\sqrt{\left[g_{1L}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\right]^{2}},

(16)

\displaystyle f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\geq\sqrt{\left[g_{1L}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\right]^{2}+\left[\frac{|\boldsymbol{k_{\perp}}|}{M}g_{1T}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\right]^{2}},

(17)

\displaystyle f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\geq\sqrt{\left[g_{1L}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\right]^{2}+\left[\frac{|\boldsymbol{k_{\perp}}|^{2}}{2M^{2}}h_{1}^{\perp g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\right]^{2}}.

(18)

We further search for the points where the left-hand side (LHS) quantities and the right-hand side (RHS) quantities are nearest to each other for all three inequalities. During the search, we set
\Delta|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|=0.01\ (\mathrm{GeV})
and
\Delta x=1/16.5
for the increments in the transverse and longitudinal directions respectively. It is interesting to see that, contrary to the quark results in Ref.
[
62
]
, LHS and RHS quantities of Eqs. (
16
-
18
) get constantly closer to each other as
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}
grows and
x
approaches 1.

4.2
Transverse moments of the unpolarized gluon TMD

We define the
n
-th order transverse moment of the unpolarized gluon TMD as:

\displaystyle\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{n}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}(x)=\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{n}f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}).

(19)

From the second and the zeroth order moments, we can calculate the averaged transverse momentum squared by

\displaystyle\frac{\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{2}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}}{\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{0}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}}.

(20)

The BLFQ results for this averaged transverse momentum squared are shown in the left panel of Fig.
4
as a function of the longitudinal momentum fraction. In other words, Fig.
4
presents a correlation between the gluon’s transverse and longitudinal degrees of freedom. The BLFQ calculations of Eq. (
20
) are relatively larger in the middle-
x
region and decrease rapidly at large-
x
and small-
x
regions.

It is interesting to probe the compatibility of our BLFQ results with the Gaussian ansatz,
i.e.
, whether we could model the BLFQ unpolarized gluon TMD with

\displaystyle f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})\approx a\frac{\exp({-\frac{|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{2}}{r}})}{\pi r}.

(21)

It is easy to show that under Gaussian ansatz,
a=\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{0}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}
and
r=\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{2}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}
. Thus, if the Gaussian ansatz holds, the following quantity

\displaystyle\frac{\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{2}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}\times\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{0}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}}}{(\langle|\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}|^{1}\rangle_{f_{1}^{g}})^{2}}\times\frac{\pi}{4}

(22)

should be unity at every
x
. The deviation of the value of Eq. (
22
) from 1 is a measure of the deviation of the unpolarized TMD from a Gaussian distribution.

In the right panel of Fig.
4
, we show the results of Eq. (
22
) evaluated with the BLFQ results. We find that in the middle-
x
region, our results are close to a Gaussian distribution and this measure is relatively stable. But at large-
x
and small-
x
regions, large deviations are evident. Since we work in a transverse 2D HO basis in which the transverse kinetic plus confinement portion of the Hamiltonian is diagonal, we can surmise that the Gaussian-like behavior in the middle-
x
region reflects the dominance of kinetic plus confining physics in that region. On the other hand, the deviations in the small-
x
and large-
x
regions from simple Gaussian behavior signals a rise in the importance of gluon dynamics in these regions.

5
Summary

In this paper, we calculate the T-even gluon TMDs at the leading twist within the BLFQ framework. Our results are consistent with the currently available theoretical calculations and the experimental extractions. However, unlike the results obtained in Refs.
[
30
,
31
,
36
]
, the BLFQ gluon TMDs are dominated by the S wave components, leading to the monotonical behaviours in the transverse direction. Further, due to the limiting behaviours in the transverse direction of all four TMDs calculated within the BLFQ framework, all polarized densities available with T-even TMDs do not exhibit large asymmetry. It remains an open question whether the contributions from higher Fock-sectors in BLFQ introduce significant asymmetry in the gluon polarized densities. Our future work will address this issue.

Our calculations of gluon TMDs do not support the model relation proposed in Ref.
[
36
]
that relates the square of
f_{1}^{g}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2})
to the sum of the squares of other three T-even gluon TMDs, validating its model-dependent origin. On the other hand, restrictions of the gluon TMDs can be obtained from purely QCD considerations. Here, we investigate two of them, the first being the Mulders-Rodrigues inequalities
[
61
,
66
]
and the second being the limiting behaviours of moments of TMDs from Refs.
[
63
,
64
,
65
]
. T-even gluon TMDs obtained within the BLFQ framework fulfill all of them, strengthening the validity of the BLFQ calculations and our future plans to further investigate gluon TMD-related experimental results.

Due to the dependences on both the transverse and longitudinal directions, it is very convenient and interesting to investigate the correlations between those two degrees of freedom with the help of TMDs. Here we point out two such correlations, Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
), which we investigate using the BLFQ results. We find relatively mild correlations in the middle-
x
region and strong correlations in the small-
x
and large-
x
regions. We interpret these results as indicating the more complicated dynamics of the gauge boson at the extremes of longitudinal momentum fraction.

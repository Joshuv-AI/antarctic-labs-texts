Abstract
. Suppose that G G is a graph of cardinality μ + \mu^{+} with chromatic number χ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ μ + \chi(G)\geq\mu^{+} . One possible reason that this could happen is if G G contains a clique of size μ + \mu^{+} . We prove that this is indeed the case when the edge relation is stable. When G G is a random graph (which is simple but not stable), this is not true. But still if in general the complete theory of G G is simple, G G must contain finite cliques of unbounded sizes.

1.
Introduction

The chromatic number
\chi(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimal cardinal
\kappa
for which there exists a vertex coloring with
\kappa
colors such that connected vertices get different colors.

Research around graphs having an uncountable chromatic number has a long history, see e.g.,
[
Kom11
, Section 3]
. This topic is set-theoretic in nature, with many results being independent of the axioms of set theory (ZFC). In
[
HKS22
,
HKS23
]
we studied a specific conjecture (Taylor’s strong conjecture) in the context of
stable
graphs (in ZFC): a graph whose first order theory is stable (a model-theoretic notion of tameness, see Section
2
for all the definitions). It turns out that a very close relative of this conjecture holds for stable graphs (although it does not hold in general).

More specifically, we showed that if a stable graph has chromatic number
>\beth_{2}(\aleph_{0})
then this implies the presence of all the finite subgraphs of a shift graph
\text{Sh}_{n}(\omega)
for some
0<n<\omega
, where for a cardinal
\kappa
, the shift graph
\text{Sh}_{n}(\kappa)
is the graph whose vertices are increasing
n
-tuples of ordinals in
\kappa
, and two such tuples
s,t
are connected if for every
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
s(i)=t(i-1)
or vice-versa (see Example
2.4
). In turn, this implies that the chromatic numbers of elementary extensions of said graph are unbounded. An important example for this paper is the case
n=1
:
\text{Sh}_{1}(\kappa)
is the complete graph on
\kappa
. For more on this result, see
[
HKS22
,
HKS23
]
.

In this paper, we take the first step towards identifying the
n
in the previous paragraph, by considering not only the chromatic number of the graph, but its
cardinality
as well, as we now explain.

Bounds for the chromatic number of the shift graph was computed by Erdös and Hajnal
[
EH66
]
: assuming the generalized continuum hypothesis (
GCH
),

\chi(\text{Sh}_{n+1}(\kappa^{+n}))=\kappa

for all
n<\omega
, see Fact
2.6
. In particular (and trivially)
\chi(\text{Sh}_{1}(\kappa))=\kappa
. Thus, it makes sense to ask the following question:

Question
1.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a stable graph and for simplicity also GCH. Assume that for every cardinal
\kappa
, there is some
G^{\prime}\equiv G
(i.e.,
\mathrm{Th}(G)=\mathrm{Th}(G^{\prime})
) of cardinality
|G^{\prime}|\leq\kappa^{+n}
satisfying that
\chi(G^{\prime})\geq\kappa^{+}
, then is it true that for some
m\leq n
,
G
contains all finite subgraphs of
\text{Sh}_{m}(\omega)
?

Remark 1.2
.

Proposition
3.11
and Remark
3.12
explain why we restricted ourselves to successor cardinals.

In this paper we deal with the case
n=1
, and we manage to give a satisfying solution to this case (and more) assuming that the theory of
G
is simple or that the edge relation is stable (both weaker assumptions than stability of the theory).
The following sums up the main results of this paper:

Main Theorem 1.3

(Propositions
3.2
and
3.10
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
T=\mathrm{Th}(G,E)
be its first order theory. Assume that
|G|=\mu^{+}
and
\chi(G)\geq\mu^{+}
for some infinite cardinal
\mu
.

(1)

Assuming
T
is a simple theory then
G
contains cliques of any finite size.

(2)

Assuming the edge relation
E
is stable then
G
contains an infinite clique of cardinality
\mu^{+}
.

Note that the conclusion of item (1) (together with Löwenheim-Skolem and compactness) implies the existence of
G^{\prime}\succ G
of cardinality
\mu^{+}
that contains an infinite clique of cardinality
\mu^{+}
. The conclusion of item (2) is stronger: we can find such a clique already in
G
itself.

A theory

is

if all formulas are stable.

Next we define simplicity; we give an equivalent definition, using the notion of dividing for types, see
[
TZ12
, Proposition 7.2.5]
. Given a first order theory
T
with a monster model
\mathbb{U}
, a formula
\varphi(x,b)
with
b\in\mathbb{U}

over

if there is a sequence of realizations
\langle b_{i}\in\mathbb{U}:i<\omega\rangle
of
\tp(b/A)
such that

is inconsistent). A complete type

over

if it contains some formula which divides over
A

The theory

is

if for every complete type

over

there is some

with

such that

does not divide over

The main tool we will use from simplicity theory is forking calculus. Non-forking independence is 3-place relation on sets (or tuples) denoted by
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
. We will not go over all the properties that non-forking independence enjoys in simple theories; see
[
TZ12
, Chapter 7]
for more information.

2.2.
Graph theory

Here we gather some facts on graphs and the chromatic number of graphs (see also
[
HKS22
]
).

By a
graph
we mean a pair
G=(V,E)
where
E\subseteq V^{2}
is symmetric and irreflexive. A
graph homomorphism
between
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
is a map
f:V_{1}\to V_{2}
such that
f(e)\in E_{2}
for every
e\in E_{1}
. If
f
is injective we will say that
f
embeds
G_{1}
into
G_{2}
a subgraph. If in addition we require that
f(e)\in E_{2}
if and only if
e\in E_{1}
we will say that
f
embeds
G_{1}
into
G_{2}
as an induced subgraph.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.

(1)

For a cardinal
\kappa
, a
vertex coloring
(or just coloring) of cardinality
\kappa
is a function
c:V\to\kappa
such that
x\mathrel{E}y
implies
c(x)\neq c(y)
for all
x,y\in V
.

(2)

The
chromatic number

\chi(G)
is the minimal cardinality of a vertex coloring of
G
.

The following is easy and well known.

Fact 2.3
.

Let
G=(V,\mathrel{E})
be a graph.
If
V=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_{i}
then
\chi(G)\leq\sum_{i\in I}\chi(V_{i},E\restriction V_{i})
.

Proof.

Let
c_{i}:V_{i}\to\mu_{i}
be a coloring of
(V_{i},E\restriction V_{i})
. Define a coloring
c:V\to\bigcup\{\mu_{i}\times\{i\}:i\in I\}
by choosing for any
v\in V
an
i_{v}\in I
such that
v\in V_{i_{v}}
and setting
c(v)=(c_{i_{v}}(v),i_{v})
.
∎

Example 2.4
.

For any finite number
r\geq 1
and any linearly ordered set
(A,<)
, let
\text{Sh}_{r}(A)
, (

) be the following graph: its set of vertices is the set of strictly increasing

Example 2.5

(Symmetric Shift Graph)
.

Let
r\geq 1
be any natural number and
A
any set. The

\text{Sh}_{r}^{sym}(A)
is defined similarly as the shift graph but with set of vertices the set of distinct

Note that
\text{Sh}_{r}(A)
is an induced subgraph of

Since for any infinite set

Lemma 2.10
.

For any cardinal
\mu
, the following holds for the graph
(\text{Sh}_{2}(\mu),E)

(1)

Its complete theory is not stable.

(2)

Its graph relation is stable.

(3)

It is triangle-free.

Proof.

(1) For any

X_{n}=\{(n,k):k>n\}
. For any

Y_{m}=\{x\in\text{Sh}_{2}(\mu):(x\mathrel{E}(m,m+1))\wedge\neg(x\mathrel{E}(m-1,m+1))\}

Y_{m}=\{(k,m):k<m\}
.

For any pair of natural numbers
(n,m)

\psi_{n,m}(x,y)
be the formula (with parameters

\exists z(x\in X_{n}\wedge y\in Y_{m}\wedge(z\mathrel{E}x)\wedge(z\mathrel{E}y))

For any

, and for any

(2) One can either see directly that the graph relation is stable, or note that since

(3) Suppose that

3.1.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T
be a complete simple theory of graphs in the language of graphs
L=\{E\}

Let
\mu
be an infinite cardinal and

|G|\leq 2^{\mu}

|G|=2^{\mu}

As

\alpha\in V

\tp(\alpha/\{\beta:\beta<\alpha\})
does not fork over some nonempty countable subset

\langle c_{\alpha,n}:n<\omega\rangle
, possibly with repetitions. Let
F_{n}
be the function mapping a nonzero
\alpha\in V
to
c_{\alpha,n}
(and
F_{n}(0)=0
).

Let
L^{\prime}\supseteq L\cup\{F_{n}:n<\omega\}\cup\{<\}
be a language containing Skolem functions and
G^{\prime}
an expansion of
G

Geometric hyperplanes of the Lie geometry A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
Antonio Pasini

Abstract
In this paper we investigate hyperplanes of the point-line geometry A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) of point-hyerplane flags of the projective geometry PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) . Renouncing a complete classification, which is not yet within our reach, we describe the hyperplanes which arise from the natural embedding of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) , that is the embedding which yields the adjoint representation of SL ⁡ ( n + 1 , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{SL}(n+1,\mathbb{F}) . By exploiting properties of a particular sub-class of these hyerplaes, namely the singular hyperplanes , we shall prove that all hyperplanes of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) are maximal subspaces of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) . Hyperplanes of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) can also be contructed starting from suitable line-spreads of PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) (provided that PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) admits line-spreads, of course). Explicitly, let 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} be a composition line-spread of PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) such that every hyperplane of PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) contains a sub-hyperplane of PG ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F}) spanned by lines of 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} . Then the set of points ( p , H ) (p,H) of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) such that H H contains the member of 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} through p p is a hyperplane of A n , { 1 , n } ​ ( 𝔽 ) A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F}) . We call these hyperplanes hyperplanes of spread type . Many but not all of them arise from the natural embedding.



1
A selection of results

1.1
Basic properties of the geometry
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})

Following a well established notation, we denote by
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
the geometry of point-hyperplane flags of the projective geometry
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, for
n\geq 2
and
\mathbb{F}
a given field, namely a commutative division ring.

Explicitly,
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
is the point-line geometry the points of which are the ordered pairs
(p,H)
where
p
and
H
are a point and a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
respectively and
p\in H
; the lines of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
are the sets
\{(p,H)\mid p\in\ell\}
for
\ell
a line of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
and
H
a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
containing
\ell
and the sets
\{(p,H)\mid H\supset L\}
for
L
a sub-hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
(namely a subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
of codimension 2) and
p
a point of
L
. Accordingly, two points
(p,H)
and
(q,K)
of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
are collinear if and only if either
p=q
or
H=K
. If
p\neq q
and
H\neq K
but either
p\in K
or
q\in H
, then
(p,H)
and
(q,K)
are at distance 2, otherwise they are at distance 3. Thus, the diameter of the collinearity graph of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
is equal to 3.

1.1.1
Polar and special pairs

When
n=2
the point-line geometry
A_{n,\{1,n\}}
is a generalized hexagon where every point belongs two just two lines.

Let
n>2
. Then
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
is a parapolar space of symplectic rank 2, as defined by Shult
[
5
]
, the symps of which are grids isomorphic to the hyperbolic quadric
{\cal Q}^{+}_{3}(\mathbb{F})
. Recall that a pair of points
\{X,Y\}
at distance 2 of a parapolar space is called
polar
if it is contained in a symp, equivalently at least two points exist which are collinear with both
X
and
Y
. Otherwise, when just one point exists which is collinear with both
X
and
Y
, the pair
\{X,Y\}
is said to be
special
. A pair
\{(p,H),(q,K)\}
of points of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
at distance 2 is polar precisely when
p\in K
and
q\in H
. If this is the case, the points of the unique symp containg
(p,H)
and
(q,K)
are the point-hyperplane flags
(x,X)
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
with
x
a point of the line
\ell=\langle p,q\rangle
and
X
a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
containing the sub-hyperplane
H\cap K
. On the other hand, let
\{(p,H),(q,K)\}
be a special pair, say
p\in K
but
q\not\in H
, to fix ideas. Then
(p,K)
is the unique point of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
collinear with both
(p,H)
and
(q,K)
.

1.1.2
Maximal singular subspaces

Given a point
a
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, let
{\cal M}_{a}
be the set of pairs
(a,H)
with
H
a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
containing
a
. This set is a maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
. Dually, for a hyperplane
A
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, the set
{\cal M}_{A}:=\{(p,A)\mid p\in A\}
is a maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
. We say that
{\cal M}_{a}
and
{\cal M}_{A}
are
based
at
a
and
A
respectively. Every maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
admits one of these two descriptions. So, the maximal singular subspaces of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
are partioned in two families: those which are based at a point of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
and those based at a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
. Two distinct maximal singular subspaces have at most one point in common; they meet in a point only if they do not belong to the same family. Moreover. if
M
is a maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
and
(p,H)
a point of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
exterior to
\cal M
, then
(p,H)
is collinear with at most one point of
\cal M
.

1.1.3
The natural embedding

It is well known that
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
admits a (full) projective embedding in the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F}))
of the vector space
M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
of null-traced square matrices of order
n+1
with entries in
\mathbb{F}
, which yields the adjoint representation of the special linear group
\mathrm{SL}(n+1,\mathbb{F})
. We call it the
natural embedding
of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
and we denote it by the symbol
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}
.

Explicitly, recall that
M^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
is a hyperplane of the vector space
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
of square matrices of order
n+1
with entries in
\mathbb{F}
and the latter is the same as the tensor product
V\otimes V^{*}
, where
V=V(n+1,\mathbb{F})
and
V^{*}
is the dual of
V
. The pure tensors
{\bf x}\otimes\xi
of
V\otimes V^{*}
, with
{\bf x}
and
\xi
non-zero vectors of
V
and
V^{*}
respectively, yield the matrices of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
of rank
1
. With
{\bf x}
and
\xi
as above, let
[{\bf x}]
and
[\xi]
be the point and the hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
represented by
{\bf x}
and
\xi
. Then
([{\bf x}],[\xi])
is a point of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
if and only if
\xi({\bf x})=0
. The pure tensor
{\bf x}\otimes\xi
, regarded as a square matrix of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
of rank 1, is null-traced if and only if
\xi({\bf x})=0
. The natural embedding

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}:A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\mathrm{PG}(M^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}))

maps the point
([{\bf x}],[\xi])
of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
onto the point
\langle{\bf x}\otimes\xi\rangle
of
\mathrm{PG}(M^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}))
. These points form a projective variety, commonly known as the Segre variety.

1.2
Generalities on hyperplanes of point-line geometries

In this subsection we recall some definitions and basics facts on subspaces and geometric hyperplanes of point-lines geometries, to be exploited in the sequel.

Throughout this subsection
\Gamma
is an arbitrary point-line geometry as defined in Shult
[
5
]
, but we assume that the lines of
\Gamma
are subsets of the point-set of
\Gamma
. A
subspace
of
\Gamma
is a subset
X
of the point-set of
\Gamma
such that, for every line
\ell
of
\Gamma
, if
|\ell\cap X|>1
then
\ell\subseteq X
. A proper subspace of
\Gamma
is said to be a
geometric hyperplane
of
\Gamma
(a
hyperplane
of
\Gamma
for short) if every line of
\Gamma
meets it non-trivially. Equivalently, a hyperplane of
\Gamma
is a proper subset
{\cal H}
of the point-set of
\Gamma
such that, for every line
\ell
of
\Gamma
, either
\ell\subseteq{\cal H}
or
|\ell\cap{\cal H}|=1
.

Assuming that
\Gamma
is embeddable, let
\varepsilon:\Gamma\rightarrow\Sigma
be an embedding of
\Gamma
in a projective space
\Sigma
, as defined by Shult in
[
4
]
. We say that a hyperplane
{\cal H}
of
\Gamma

arises
from
\varepsilon
when
\varepsilon({\cal H})
spans a projective hyperplane
H
of
\Sigma
and
{\cal H}=\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
. Conversely, the
\varepsilon
-preimage
\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
of a projective hyperplane
H
of
\Sigma
is a hyperplane of
\Gamma
and
\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{-1}(H))=H\cap\varepsilon(\Gamma)
, where
\varepsilon(\Gamma)
is the
\varepsilon
-image of the point-set of
\Gamma
. Hwever in general
\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
does not arise from
\varepsilon
. Indeed
\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
arises from
\varepsilon
if and only if
\langle H\cap\varepsilon(\Gamma)\rangle=H
.

Proposition 1.1

Given a projective hyperplane
H
of
\Sigma
, suppose that
\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
is maximal in the family of proper subspaces of
\Gamma
. Then
\langle H\cap\varepsilon(\Gamma)\rangle=H
.

Proof.
For a contradiction, let
\langle H\cap\varepsilon(\Gamma)\rangle\subset H
. Let
p
be a point of
\Gamma
not in
{\cal H}:=\varepsilon^{-1}(H)
. Then
\varepsilon(p)\not\in H
and, since
\varepsilon({\cal H})=H\cap\varepsilon(\Gamma)
does not span
H
, the set
\varepsilon({\cal H})\cup\{\varepsilon(p)\}
does not span
\Sigma
. However,
{\cal H}\cup\{p\}
generates
\Gamma
, since
{\cal H}
is a maximal subspace of
\Gamma
by assumption, and
\varepsilon(\Gamma)
spans
\Sigma
(by the definition of embedding). Hence
\varepsilon({\cal H})\cup\{\varepsilon(p)\}
spans
\Sigma
. Contradiction.
\Box

1.3
The hyperplanes of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
which arise from
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}

Let
f:M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\times M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be the symmetric bilinear form defined as follows: for any two matrices
X,Y\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
, we put

f(X,Y)~:=~\mathrm{trace}(XY)

(1)

where
XY
is the usual row-times-column product. More explicitly, with
X=(x_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n}
and
Y=(y_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n}
, we have

f((x_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n},(y_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n})~=~\sum_{i,j}x_{i,j}y_{j,i}.

(2)

In particular, when
X
and
Y
have rank 1, namely they are pure tensors, say
X={\bf x}\otimes\xi
and
Y={\bf y}\otimes\upsilon
, then

f({\bf x}\otimes\xi,{\bf y}\otimes\upsilon)~=~\upsilon({\bf x})\xi({\bf y}).

(3)

In tensor language, with a matrix
X=(x_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n}
regarded as a tensor
x^{i}_{.j}
, the scalar
\mathrm{trace}(XY)
is the complete saturation of the tensors
X
and
Y
. So, we call
f
the
saturation form
of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
.

The saturation form
f
is non-degenerate (and trace-valued when
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
). Therefore, if
\perp_{f}
is the orthogonality relation associated to
f
, the hyperplanes of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
are the sets
M^{\perp_{f}}
for
M\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\setminus\{O\}
(where
O
stands for the null matrix). In particular,
M^{\perp_{f}}=M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
if and only if
M
is proportional to the identity matrix
I
(and
M\neq O
, of course). For
M\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\setminus\{\lambda I\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}
, we put

{\cal H}_{M}~:=~\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}^{-1}([M^{\perp_{f}}\cap M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})])

where
[M^{\perp_{f}}\cap M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})]
is the hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(M^{0}_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}))
corresponding to the hyperplane
M^{\perp_{f}}\cap M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
of
M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
. So,
{\cal H}_{M}
is a geometric hyperplane of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
. We call it a
hyperplane of plain type
.

As we shall see later (Corollary
1.7
), the hyperplanes of plain type are precisely the hyperplanes of
A_{1,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
whch arise from
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}
.

Remark 1

Our notation
{\cal H}_{M}
conceals the fact that the mapping
M\mapsto{\cal H}_{M}
is not injective. Explicitly, for
M,N\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\setminus\{\lambda I\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}
, we have
{\cal H}_{M}={\cal H}_{N}
if and only if the pairs
\{M,I\}
and
\{N,I\}
span the same subspace of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
. The hyperplanes
{\cal H}_{M}
of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
are indeed the points of a projectve geometry isomorphic to the star of the point
\langle I\rangle=\{\lambda I\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}
in
\mathrm{PG}(M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}))
. Of course, nothing forbids us to modify our notation in such a way as to keep a record of these facts in it but the resulting notation would be not so nice. We prefer to stick to our slightly incorrect notation.

The next proposition will be proved in Section
3

Proposition 1.2

The following are equivalent for a hyperplane
{\cal H}_{M}
of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
of plain type:

(1)

the hyperplane
{\cal H}_{M}
contains a maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
based at point of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
;

(2)

the hyperlane
{\cal H}_{M}
contains a maximal singular subspace of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
based at a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
;

(3)

the matrix
M
admits at least one eigenvalue in
\mathbb{F}
.

In fact we shall prove the following sharper statement. For a point
a
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
let
{\bf a}\in V
a representative vector of
a
. Then
{\cal H}_{M}
contains the maximal singular subspace
{\cal M}_{a}
if and only if
{\bf a}
is a (right) eigenvector of
M
, namely
M{\bf a}\in\langle{\bf a}\rangle
. Dually, for a hyperplane
A
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, let
\alpha\in V^{*}
represent
A
. Then
{\cal H}_{M}\supseteq{\cal M}_{A}
if and only if
\alpha M\in\langle\alpha\rangle
(namely
\alpha
is a left eigenvector of
M
). Since
M
admits right eigenvectors (in
V
) if and only if it admits left eigenvectors (in
V^{*})
if and only if it admits eigenvalues in
\mathbb{F}
, Propositon
1.2
follows.

In particular, let
\mathrm{rank}(M)=1
, say
M={\bf a}\otimes\alpha
for a nonzero vector
{\bf a}\in V
and a non-zero linear functional
\alpha\in V^{*}
. Then
M{\bf a}={\bf a}\cdot\alpha({\bf a})
and
\alpha M=\alpha({\bf a})\cdot\alpha
, namely
{\bf a}
and
\alpha
are respectively a right eigenvector and a left eigenvector of
M
and
\alpha({\bf a})
is the eigenvalue associated with both
{\bf a}
anf
\alpha
. Accordingly,
{\cal H}_{M}={\cal H}_{{\bf a}\otimes\alpha}
contains both
{\cal M}_{a}
and
{\cal M}_{A}
, where
a
and
A
are the point and the hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
represented by
{\bf a}
and
\alpha
respectively.

As noticed in Remark
1
, if
{\bf a}\otimes\alpha
and
{\bf b}\otimes\beta
define the same hyperplane then
{\bf b}\otimes\beta\in\langle{\bf a}\otimes\alpha,I\rangle
. However, all rank 1 matrices contained in
\langle{\bf a}\otimes\alpha,I\rangle
are proportional to
{\bf a}\otimes\alpha
. Hence
{\cal H}_{{\bf a}\otimes\alpha}={\cal H}_{{\bf b}\otimes\beta}
if and only if
{\bf a}\otimes\alpha
and
{\bf b}\otimes\beta
are proportional, namely they correspond to the same pair
(a,A)
, where
a
and
A
are the point and the hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
represented by
{\bf a}
and
\alpha
respectively. In view of this fact, henceforth we write
{\cal H}_{a,A}
instead of
{\cal H}_{{\bf a}\otimes\alpha}
. The following will be proved in Section
3
.

Proposition 1.3

Let
a
and
A
be a point and a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
. Then
{\cal H}_{a,A}
is the set of points of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
which are collinear with at least one point of
{\cal M}_{a}\cup{\cal M}_{A}
. In particular, if
a\in A
then
(a,A)
is a point of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
and
{\cal H}_{a,A}
is the set of points of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
at non-maximal distance from
(a,A)
.

We call the hyperplanes as
{\cal H}_{a,A}

quasi-singular
hyperplanes. When
a\in A
we say that the hyperplane
{\cal H}_{a,A}
is
singular
with
(a,A)
as its
deepest point
.

A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})

.

, we shall prove the following.

,

Let
{\cal H}

1.5
Hyperplanes of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
of spread type

Recall that a
line-spread
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
is a family
\mathfrak{S}
of lines of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
such that every point of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
belongs to exactly one member of
\mathfrak{S}
. A
dual line-spread
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
is a line-spread of the dual of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
. The lines of the dual of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
are in fact sub-hyperplanes of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
. So, a dual line-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
is a family
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
of sub-hyperplanes of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
such that every hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
contains exactly one member of
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
. (Note that
n=1
is allowed in these definitions: a
1
-dimensional projective space is a line, say
\ell
, and we regard
\{\ell\}
as the unique line-spread of
\ell
.)

As
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
and its dual are isomorphic (since
\mathbb{F}
is a field), if
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
admits line-spreads then it also admits dual line-spreads. We recall that
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
admits a line-spread only if
n
is odd.

When
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
admits line-spreads, an intriguing family of hyperplanes of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
also exists, to be described in a few lines (see below, Theorem
1.5
). We firstly state a couple of lemmas, to be proved in Section
2.3.3
.

Lemma 1.8

Let
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
be a line-spread and a dual line-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
. The following two properties are equivalent

(\mathrm{S})

for every
L\in\mathfrak{S}^{*}
, the members of
\mathfrak{S}
contained in the sub-hyperplane
L
form a line-spread of the projective space
L
.

(\mathrm{S}^{*})

for every
\ell\in\mathfrak{S}
, the members of
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
containing the line
\ell
form a dual line-spread in the star of
\ell
.

Lemma 1.9

For every line-spread
\mathfrak{S}
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, there is at most one dual line-spread
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
such that the pair
\{\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}^{*}\}
satisfies property
(\mathrm{S})
(equivalently,
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
) of Lemma
1.8
.

Of course, the dual of Lemma
1.9
also holds true: for every dual line-spread
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
, there exists at most one line-spread
\mathfrak{S}
such that
\{\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}^{*}\}
satifies
(\mathrm{S})
.

For a line-spread
\mathfrak{S}
of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
, if a dual line-spread
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
exists such that
\{\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}^{*}\}
satisfies property
(\mathrm{S})
of Lemma
1.8
, we say that
\mathfrak{S}

admits the dual
and we call
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
(unique by Lemma
1.9
) the
dual
of
\mathfrak{S}
.

Remark 2

Recall that a composition line-spread is a line-spread
\mathfrak{S}
such that, for every nonempty subset
X
of
\mathfrak{S}
, the lines of
\mathfrak{S}
contained in the span
\langle\cup_{\ell\in X}\ell\rangle

2.2
Properties of the saturation form

Let

be the saturation form of

, as defined in (
1
) and (
2
) of Section
1.3
. Formula (
2

, the form

is represeted by a block-diagonal matrix, with
(n+1)n/2
blocks of order

equal to

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)

and one more block equal to the identity matrix

of order

. This matrix is non-singular. Hence

is non-degenerate, as claimed in Section
1.3
. We denote by
{\cal S}_{f}
the set of

-isotropic vectors of

, namely the set of matrices

such that

.

If
\mathrm{rank}(X)=1
, namely

, then

by formula (
3
) of Section
1.3
. Hence
f(X,X)=0
if and only if
\xi({\bf x})=0
, namely
X\in M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
. Accordingly,

{\cal S}_{f}\cap\mathrm{Pure}(V\otimes V^{*})~=~\mathrm{Pure}((V\otimes V^{*})_{0}).

Note that
[\mathrm{Pure}((V\otimes V^{*})_{0})]
is just the
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nat}}
-image of the point-set of
A_{n,\{1,n\}}(\mathbb{F})
.

Remark 3

When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
the set
{\cal S}_{f}
spans
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
. In this case the points of
\mathrm{PG}(M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}))
represented by the non-zero vectors of
{\cal S}_{f}
form a non-singular quadric. On the other hand, let
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})=2
. Then

if and only if
\mathrm{trace}(X)=0
. So, if
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})=2
then
{\cal S}_{f}=M_{n+1}^{0}(\mathbb{F})
. With the terminology of Tits
[
7
, chp. 8]
, the form

is trace-valued or non-trace-valued according to whether
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
or
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F})=2
, but this discrepancy has no relevance for the matter of this paper.

As in Section
1.3
, we denote by
\perp_{f}
the orthogonality relation associated to
f
. As
f
is non-degenerate, the hyperplanes of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
are the perps
M^{\perp_{f}}
for
M\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\setminus\{O\}
and, for two matrices
M,N\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})\setminus\{O\}
, we have

if and only if

. It is clear from formula (
1
) of Section
1.3
that

. Therefore, for

, we have

if and only if

.

Every hyperplane of

is the intersection of

with a hyperplane
M^{\perp_{f}}
of
M_{n+1}(\mathbb{F})
for a suitable matrix

. Of course the equality

does not imply that

. The next statement, already mentioned in Remark

, follows from well known properties of the polarities associated to non-degenerate reflexive sesqulinear forms.

Proposition 2.1

For

Let

-dimensional subspace over

. Given any two scalars

\mathbb{F}
-space

form a basis of the
\mathbb{F}
-space

as an (

-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-space with

are non-proportional as vectors of the
\mathbb{F}
-space
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
. With

as above, we say that the
m
-tuples

and
\mathfrak{b}=(b_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
are

(
over
\mathbb{F}
).

Given a basis

of

and two elementwise non-proportional

-tuples

and

\bar{{\bf e}}_{j}b_{j}

With

as above, for every non-zero vector

2.3.2
Constructing line-spreads by means of collineations

As in the previous subsection,

of a scalar

Lemma 2.3

Given a scalar

and a basis

(1)

(2)

are equal to

.

(4)

f^{2}_{\omega}({\bf v})\in\langle{\bf v},f_{\omega}({\bf v})\rangle
for every

. Equivalently, the collineation
[f_{\omega}]
of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
represented by

stabilizes all lines of
\mathfrak{S}_{E,\omega}

Proof.
Let

-linear mapping which maps every vector

For every line-spread
\mathfrak{S}
of

of standard type there exists an invertible linear mapping

such that:

(2)

f

.

(3)

In view of Proposition

, given

Remark 4

2.3.3
The dual of a line-spread

As recalled in Remark
2
, a line-spread admits the dual only if it is a composition line-spread.
Composition line-spreads are well known objects, thoroughly studied by a number of authors.
Some of the proofs we are going to give in this subsection can be shortened or even dropped by referring to properties of composition spreads. However, since all of these proofs are easy, we prefer to expose them in full length, without asking the reader to look for appropriate references in the literature.

Our definition ofthe dual of a line spread is based on two properties, called
(\mathrm{S})
and
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
in Section
1.5
. In Lemma
1.8
we claimed that these two properties are equivalent but we didn’t prove this claim. We prove it here.

Proof of Lemma
1.8
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
satisfy
(\mathrm{S})
. We shall prove that they also satisfy
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
. Let
\ell\in\mathfrak{S}
, let
H
be a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
and
L
the unique sub-hyperplane of
\mathfrak{S}^{*}
contained in
H
. According to
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
, we must have
\ell\subseteq L
. This is indeed the case. Indeed
H
contains both
\ell
and
L
. Hence
\ell\cap L\neq\emptyset
. Let
p\in\ell\cap L
. According to
(\mathrm{S})
, the sub-hyperplane
L
contains the unique line of
\mathfrak{S}
through
p
. However that line is just
\ell
. Hence
L\supseteq\ell
. So,
(\mathrm{S})
does imply
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
. The converse implication, from
(\mathrm{S}^{*})
to
(\mathrm{S})
, can be proved by dualizing the above argument.
\Box

In Lemma
1.9
we claim that every line-spread admits at most one dual. Here is a proof of that claim.

Proof of Lemma
1.9
.
For a contradiction, suppose that two disctinct dual line-spreads

and
\mathfrak{S}^{*}_{2}
exist such that property
(\mathrm{S})
holds for both pairs

and
\{\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}^{*}_{2}\}
. As
\mathfrak{S}^{*}_{1}\neq\mathfrak{S}^{*}_{2}
, there exists a hyperplane

of
\mathrm{PG}(n,\mathbb{F})
such, if
L_{i}
is the member of
\mathfrak{S}^{*}_{i}
contained in
H
for

, then
L_{1}\neq L_{2}
. Choose

and let
\ell_{p}
be the line of
\mathfrak{S}
through

. Then
\ell_{p}\cap L_{i}\neq\emptyset
for

, as

is a hyperlane of

then forces

.
\Box

Proof of Lemma

.
Let

admit the dual and let

be its dual. For a point-hyperplane flag

of

Two more lemmas stated in Section

2.3.4
Line-spreads of semi-standard type and their duals

In Proposition

we claim that, for a linear collineation
\varphi
of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
acting fixed-point freely on
\mathrm{PG}(V)
, the set of lines
\mathfrak{S}_{\varphi}

with

is a spread of

.

Lemma 2.9

A non-linear collineation of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
acting fixed-point freely on
\mathrm{PG}(V)
defines a line-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
if and only if it is an involution.

Proof.
The ‘if’ part of the lemma is obvious. Turning to the ‘only if’ part, let
\varphi
be a non-linear collineation of
\mathrm{PG}(V)
which fixes no points of

\mathrm{PG}(V)
and suppose that
\mathfrak{S}_{\varphi}
is a spread. Let
\sigma
be the (non-trivial) automorphism of
\mathbb{F}
assocated to
\varphi
and

a semi-linear transformation of

corresponding to
\varphi
. As

is a spread, for every vector

there exist two scalars

f^{2}({\bf x})~=~{\bf x}\lambda_{\bf x}+f({\bf x})\mu_{\bf x}

(7)

and at least one of them is different from

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f^{2}({\bf x}+{\bf y})&=&f^{2}({\bf x})+f^{2}({\bf y})={\bf x}\lambda_{\bf x}+{\bf y}\lambda_{\bf y}+f({\bf x})\mu_{\bf x}+f({\bf y})\mu_{\bf y},\\

(8)

Suppose that
{\bf y}\not\in\langle{\bf x},f({\bf x})\rangle

By (

.

. Moreover,

1. Introduction

The very well-known and very important algebraic relations between type I and type II Hermite–Padé (HP) polynomials were discovered by K. Mahler (see
[
4
]
). These relations connect to each other type I and type II HP polynomials associated with a tuple of formal power series given at zero point
\zeta=0
. Thus from those relations it follows that type I and type II HP polynomials are not independent. In the last decade based on G. Chudnovsky ideas
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
some generalizations of Mahler’s identities were obtained and successfully applied to the monodromy problems of Fuchsian systems of differential equations; see
[
12
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and the bibliography therein.
Similar to Mahler’s results, these generalizations were produced for power series given at zero point
\zeta=0
. But after then while applying the identities to monodromy problems it was made the changing of the variable from
\zeta
to
z=1/\zeta
; see
[
12
]
and
[
6
]
. The reason is that it is namely in such a form the identities are applicable to monodromy problems.

The main purpose of the current paper is to propose a construction of type I and type II HP polynomials associated with a tuple
[f_{0},\dots,f_{m}]
of formal power series given not at zero point but at the infinity point
z=\infty
. Under some assumption on the ‘‘general position’’ of the tuple (cf.
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
) this construction provides two
(m+1)\times(m+1)
polynomial matrices
M_{1}(z)
and
M_{2}(z)
,
M_{1}(z),M_{2}(z)\in\mathrm{GL}(m+1,\mathbb{C}[z])
, with the following property:
M_{1}(z)M_{2}(z)\equiv I_{m+1}
, where
I_{m+1}
is the identity
(m+1)\times(m+1)
-matrix.

2. The case of
m=1

To make our idea of the construction of HP polynomials with the prescribed properties, we start with the case when
m=1
, i.e. the case of Padé polynomials. Thus we are given a tuple
[f_{0},f_{1}]
of two formal power series at infinity point. We assume that the tuple
[f_{0},f_{1}]
is in a ‘‘general position’’. For the case
m=1
that means that all the indexes of the Padé table for the series
f=f_{1}/f_{0}
are normal (see
[
9
]
). Evidently it is also the case for the Padé table for the reciprocal series
1/f
.

Let
\mathbf{n}_{0}:=(n,n-1)
and
\mathbf{n}_{1}:=(n-1,n)
be two multi-indexes,
n\geqslant 1
.

It is easy to see that for multi-index
\mathbf{n}_{0}
there exist two polynomials
Q^{(0)}_{0}
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(0)}_{0}}\leqslant n
, and
Q^{(0)}_{1}
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(0)}_{1}}\leqslant{n-1}
with the following property
1
1

1

Here and in what follows we consider relations similar to (
1
) only as formal relations in the space of formal power series.
:

(1)

Q^{(0)}_{0}f_{0}+zQ^{(0)}_{1}f_{1}=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right),\quad z\to\infty.

From relation (
1
) it directly follows that
Q^{(0)}_{0}(0)\neq 0
. Indeed, for otherwise we have that
Q^{(0)}_{0}=z\widetilde{Q}^{(0)}_{0}
, where
\operatorname{deg}{\widetilde{Q}^{(0)}_{0}}\leqslant{n-1}
. Therefore from (
1
) it would follow that

(2)

\widetilde{Q}^{(0)}_{0}+Q^{(0)}_{1}f=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}\right),\quad z\to\infty.

The relation (
2
) implies that the index
(n-1,n-1)
is not normal for the series
f=f_{1}/f_{0}
which is in contradiction to our assumption. Thus we have that
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(0)}_{1}}=n-1
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(0)}_{0}}=n
and
Q^{(0)}_{0}(0)\neq 0
. Therefore we can normalize
Q^{(0)}_{0}
as
Q^{(0)}_{0}(0)=1

Just in the similar way we obtain that for multi-index
\mathbf{n}_{1}
there exist two polynomials
Q^{(1)}_{0}
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(1)}_{0}}\leqslant{n-1}
, and
Q^{(1)}_{1}
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(1)}_{1}}\leqslant{n}
, with the property

(3)

zQ^{(1)}_{0}f_{0}+Q^{(1)}_{1}f_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right),\quad z\to\infty,

Based on normality of Padé table for the series
1/f
it follows from (
3
) that
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(1)}_{0}}=n-1
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(1)}_{1}}=n
,
Q^{(1)}_{1}(0)\neq 0
and thus we can introduce the normalization
Q^{(1)}_{1}(0)=1
.

From (
1
) and (
3
) it easy follows the relation

(4)

Q^{(0)}_{0}Q^{(1)}-z^{2}Q^{(0)}_{1}Q^{(1)}_{0}=O(1),\quad z\to\infty.

Therefore the polynomial

\mathcal{P}:=Q^{(0)}_{0}Q^{(1)}-z^{2}Q^{(0)}_{1}Q^{(1)}_{0}
is constant. To find this constant we evaluate this polynomial at zero point to obtain that

\mathcal{P}(0)=1
. Thus we obtain that
Q^{(0)}_{0}Q^{(1)}-z^{2}Q^{(0)}_{1}Q^{(1)}_{0}\equiv 1
. Therefore for the
2\times 2
-matrix

M(z):=\begin{pmatrix}Q^{(0)}_{0}&zQ^{(0)}_{1}\\
zQ^{(1)}_{0}&Q^{(1)}_{1}\end{pmatrix}

we have that
\operatorname{det}M(z)\equiv 1
and

M^{-1}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}Q^{(1)}_{1}&-zQ^{(0)}_{1}\\
-zQ^{(1)}_{0}&Q^{(0)}_{0}\end{pmatrix},

where
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(0)}_{0}}=\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(1)}_{1}}=n
(cf.
[
12
]
).

3. The case of general
m

Let now consider the tuple
[f_{0},\dots,f_{m}]
of
m\geqslant 2
series in nonnegative powers of
1/z
,
f_{j}(\infty)\neq 0
, and such that the tuple is in a ‘‘general position’’. Here ‘‘general position’’ means that all multi-indexes
\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},\dots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
are normal for the HP polynomials associated with the tuple at the infinity point (see
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
).

Set
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{n}_{k}:=(n-1,\dots,n-1,\underbrace{n}_{k+1},n-1,\dots,n-1)\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
,
k=0,\dots,m
, be a multi-index. It is easy to see that for each
k=0,\dots,m
and the corresponding
\mathbf{n}_{k}
there exist polynomials
Q^{(k)}_{j}
,
j=0,\dots,m
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(k)}_{j}}\leqslant{n-1}
,
j\neq k
,
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(k)}_{k}}\leqslant{n}
, with the following property

(5)

zQ^{(k)}_{0}f_{0}+\dots+zQ^{(k)}_{k-1}f_{k-1}+Q^{(k)}_{k}f_{k}+zQ^{(k)}_{k+1}+\dots+zQ^{(k)}_{m}f_{m}=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{mn}}\right).

The relation (
5
) implies that
\operatorname{deg}{Q^{(k)}_{k}}=k
and
Q^{(k)}_{k}(0)\neq 0
because otherwise we would obtain that multi-index
(n-1,\dots,n-1)\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
is not normal for the given tuple
[f_{0},\dots,f_{m}]
. Therefore we can normalize
Q^{(k)}_{k}(0)=1
,

k=0,\dots,m
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathbf{d}_{s}:=(mn-1,\dots,mn-1,\underbrace{mn}_{s+1},mn-1,\dots,mn-1)\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
be a multi-index,
s=0,\dots,m
. It is easy to see that for each
s=0,\dots,m
and the corresponding
\mathbf{d}_{s}
there exist polynomials
P^{(s)}_{j}
,
j=0,\dots,m
,
\operatorname{deg}{P^{(s)}_{j}}\leqslant{mn-1}
,
j\neq s
,
\operatorname{deg}{P^{(s)}_{s}}\leqslant{mn}
, with the following property

(6)

zf_{s}P^{(s)}_{j}-f_{j}P^{(s)}_{s}=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right),\quad j=0,\dots,m,\quad j\neq s.

The relations (
6
) imply that
\operatorname{deg}{P^{(s)}_{s}}=mn
and
P^{(s)}_{s}(0)\neq 0
because otherwise we would obtain that the multi-index
(mn-1,\dots,mn-1)
is not normal for the given tuple
[f_{0},\dots,f_{m}]
. Thus we can normalize
P^{(s)}_{s}(0)=1
.

The following result is valid.

Theorem 1
.

Let

\displaystyle M_{1}(z):

\displaystyle=\bigl(zQ^{(k)}_{0},\dots,zQ^{(k)}_{k-1},Q^{(k)}_{k},zQ^{(k)}_{k+1},\dots,zQ^{(k)}_{m}\bigr)_{k=0,\dots,m},

\displaystyle M_{2}(z):

\displaystyle=\bigl(zP^{(s)}_{0},\dots,zP^{(s)}_{s-1},P^{(s)}_{s},zP^{(s)}_{s+1},\dots,zP^{(s)}_{m}\bigr)_{s=0,\dots,m}

be two polynomial
(m+1)\times(m+1)
-matrices. Then
M_{1}(z)M_{2}(z)\equiv I_{m+1}
, where
I_{m+1}
is the identity
(m+1)\times(m+1)
-matrix.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k}(z):

\displaystyle=\bigl(zQ^{(k)}_{0},\dots,zQ^{(k)}_{k-1},Q^{(k)}_{k},zQ^{(k)}_{k+1},\dots,zQ^{(k)}_{m}\bigr),\quad k=0,\dots,m,

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{s}(z):

\displaystyle=\bigl(zP^{(s)}_{0},\dots,zP^{(s)}_{s-1},P^{(s)}_{s},zP^{(s)}_{s+1},\dots,zP^{(s)}_{m}\bigr),\quad s=0,\dots,m,

be two polynomial vectors. Then the scalar product
\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(z):=\mathbf{u}_{k}(z)\cdot\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\mathrm{T}}(z)
is a polynomial is
z
. Set
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{0},\dots,f_{m})
. From (
5
) and (
6
) it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f_{s}}\mathbf{u}_{k}(z)\cdot f_{s}\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{1}{f_{s}}\mathbf{u}_{k}(z)\cdot\left(P^{(s)}_{s}\mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{T}}+O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right)\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f_{s}}\bigl(P^{(s)}_{s}\mathbf{u}_{k}(z)\cdot\mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{T}}+O(1)\bigr)=\frac{1}{f_{s}}\biggl(P^{(s)}_{s}\cdot O\left(\frac{1}{z^{mn}}\right)+O(1)\biggr)=O(1).

From (
7
) we obtain that
\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(z)=O(1)
as
z\to\infty
.
Thus polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(z)\equiv\mathrm{const}=\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(0)
.

We have to consider two different cases.

1) If
k=s
then

\mathcal{P}_{k,k}(z)=\mathbf{u}_{k}(z)\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{T}}(z)=Q^{(k)}_{k}P^{(k)}_{k}+zp(z).

Thus
\mathcal{P}_{k,k}(0)=Q^{(k)}_{k}(0)P^{(k)}_{k}(0)=1
.

2) If
k\neq s
then
\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(z)=zq(z)
and thus
\mathcal{P}_{k,s}(0)=0
.

Theorem
1
is proved.

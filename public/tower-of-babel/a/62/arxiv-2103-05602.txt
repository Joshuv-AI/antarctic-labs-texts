A Godunov type scheme and error estimates for multidimensional scalar conservation laws with Panov-type discontinuous flux
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, John D. Towers, Ganesh Vaidya

Abstract
This article concerns a scalar multidimensional conservation law where the flux is of Panov type and may contain spatial discontinuities. We define a notion of entropy solution and prove that entropy solutions are unique. We propose a Godunov-type finite volume scheme and prove that the Godunov approximations converge to an entropy solution, thus establishing existence of entropy solutions. We also show that our numerical scheme converges at an optimal rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( Δ ​ t ) . \displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta t}). To the best of our knowledge, convergence of the Godunov type methods in multi-dimension and error estimates of the numerical scheme in one as well as in several dimensions are the first of it’s kind for conservation laws with discontinuous flux. We present numerical examples that illustrate the theory.

In this article we study the initial value problem for the following conservation law in several dimensions,

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{t}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}A_{i}(\mathbf{x},u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(0,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{0}(\mathbf{x})\,\,\quad\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}|u-k|+\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\left[\mathop{\mathrm{}}{sgn}(u-k)(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x},u)-\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x},k))\right]+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{sgn}(u-k)\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x},k)\leq 0,\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

When the spatial discontinuities are discrete, the uniqueness of weak solutions is obtained by imposing certain additional conditions (known as interface entropy conditions) along the spatial discontinuities of the flux, which require the existence of traces. Another aim of this article is to study the error analysis of our numerical method. From a practical point of view, along with the convergence, it is also important to understand how fast the scheme converges, i.e. how fast the error of approximation of the exact solution

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle d=1

Of late,

However the rates can be shown to be the optimal rate of

The techniques introduced in this paper can be adapted to scalar conservation laws with discontinuous flux (with finitely many discontinuities) and the rate of convergence depends on the temporal total variation bounds of the finite volume approximation in the cells adjacent to the spatial of discontinuities of the flux (see section 7.3,
[

To the best of our knowledge proofs for the optimal rate

In this article, for a certain class of fluxes we prove that Godunov type schemes converge to the adapted entropy solution with the optimal rate 1/2, thus dispensing with the assumption of strict monotonicity and finitely many points of discontinuity of
[

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first error estimate for conservation laws with discontinuous flux, where the set of spatial discontinuities of
\displaystyle A_{i}(\mathbf{x},u)

\displaystyle T-s>\epsilon.
Thus invoking the definition of
\displaystyle\nu(u,\epsilon)
we get,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}w_{\epsilon}(T-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big(|u(T,\mathbf{x})-u(s,\mathbf{x})|\Big)w_{\eta}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{x}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\nu(u,\epsilon).

Combining all these estimates, we get the desired lower bound on
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
.

Claim
II

We have the following upper bound on
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\leq{\left\|u(\cdot,0)-v(\cdot,0)\right\|}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\Big(L\epsilon+\TV(r)|\eta|+\TV(v)|\eta|+\nu(u,\epsilon)\Big).

(4.16)

Claim follows by repeating the arguments done in the estimation of
\displaystyle\mathcal{A},

for
\displaystyle|u_{0}(\mathbf{x})-\tilde{v}(s,\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})|+|v_{0}(\mathbf{y})-\tilde{v}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|.

Claim
III

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=0.

(4.17)

By the definition of
\displaystyle\tilde{u}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{v}
we have,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta(\mathbf{x},u(t,\mathbf{x}))=au(t,\mathbf{x})+r(\mathbf{x})=a\tilde{u}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+r(\mathbf{y})=\beta(\mathbf{y},\tilde{u}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta(\mathbf{x},\tilde{v}(s,\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}))=a\tilde{v}(s,\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})+r(\mathbf{x})=av(s,\mathbf{y})+r(\mathbf{y})=\beta(\mathbf{y},v(s,\mathbf{y})).

Which implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(t,\mathbf{x})-\tilde{v}(s,\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})=\tilde{u}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-v(s,\mathbf{y}),

and hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\absolutevalue{u(t,\vb{x})-\tilde{v}(s,\vb{y},\vb{x})}=\absolutevalue{\tilde{u}(t,\vb{x},\vb{y})-v(s,\vb{y})}.

Thus we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=0
and claim is proved.

Substituting the values of (
4.12
)-(
4.17
) in (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\left\|u(T,\cdot)-v(T,\cdot)\right\|}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq{\left\|u_{0}-v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\Big[L\epsilon+\TV(r)|\eta|+\TV(v)|\eta|+\nu(u,\epsilon)\Big]-\wedge_{\eta,\epsilon}(u,v).

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Abstract
. Let D D be an effective divisor on a smooth projective variety X X over an algebraically closed field k k of characteristic 0 0 . We show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the class of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) parabolic vector bundles on X X with parabolic structure along D D and having rational weights and the class of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) vector bundles on certain root stacks associated to this data. Using this, we describe the orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) vector bundles on the root stack as reductions of the structure group to orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) groups. When D D is a divisor with strict normal crossings, we prove a one-to-one correspondence between the class of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) parabolic connections on X X with rational weights, and the class of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) logarithmic connections on certain fiber product of root stacks with poles along a divisor with strict normal crossings.

Introduction

Parabolic vector bundles were introduced by Mehta and Seshadri
[
MehSes80
]

on a compact Riemann surface
X
together with a set
D
of distinct marked points.
They generalized the celebrated Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem in the case of Riemann surfaces with punctures. Later, Simpson in
[
Sim90
]
reformulated and extended the definition of parabolic bundles to the case of schemes
X
of higher dimension with a normal crossings divisor
D
. When the weights are all rational, and therefore can be assumed to be in
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
r
, it amounts to a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of finite rank, together with a filtration

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{1}{r}}\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{2}{r}}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{r-1}{r}}\supset\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}(-D)

Parabolic bundles, parabolic connections and their moduli spaces have since been an active area of research, and have found themselves used in a number of important applications. More recently, parabolic connections have made appearance in the work of Donagi and Pantev on Geometric Langlands conjecture using Simpson’s non-abelian Hodge theory
[
DonPan19
]
.

When the weights are rational, it is known that parabolic bundles can be
interpreted equivalently as equivariant bundles on a suitable ramified
Galois cover
[
Bis97
]
, and later, a more intrinsic interpretation was
found using vector bundles on certain algebraic stacks associated to
(X,D)
,
namely the stack of roots
[
Bor07
]
. More precisely, there is a Fourier-like
correspondence between parabolic vector bundles on a scheme and ordinary vector
bundles on certain stack of roots. This naturally raised the question of
understanding the parabolic connections on smooth varieties through such
Fourier-like correspondence over root stacks. This has been shown to be
true over curves
[
BisMajWon12
,
LorSaiSim13
]
, and very recently over higher
dimensions
[
BorLar23
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
together with an effective Cartier divisor
D
. Let
r
be a positive integer. To this data, one can associate a stack
\mathfrak{X}:=\mathfrak{X}_{(\mathcal{O}(D),s_{D},r)}
of
r
-th roots for the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
and its canonical section
s_{D}
vanishing along
D
. It was shown by N. Borne
[
Bor07
]
that there is an equivalence between the category of algebraic vector bundles on
\mathfrak{X}
and the category of parabolic vector bundles on
X
with parabolic structures along
D
and having rational weights as multiples of
\frac{1}{r}
.
Our first main result is an extension of this equivalence to the case of orthogonal and symplectic parabolic vector bundles. More precisely, fix a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathfrak{X}
, and let
L_{\bullet}
be the corresponding parabolic line bundle on
X
. We prove the following:

Theorem 1.0.1

(
Theorem
4.0.17
)
.

There is an equivalence between the category of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) parabolic vector bundles on
X
taking values in
L_{\bullet}
(Defintiion
3.1.16
) and with rational weights as multiples of
\frac{1}{r}
, with the category of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) vector bundles on
\mathfrak{X}
taking values in
\mathcal{L}
(Definition
2.3.5
).

As an application, we show the following:

Proposition 1.0.2

(
Proposition
5.0.2
)
.

Let

and

be as above. The class of orthogonal (respectively, symplectic) bundles of rank
n
on the stack
\mathfrak{X}
of
r

(cf. Definition

-bundles (respectively, principal

-bundles) on

, where in the symplectic case,

In the final section, we assume

to be a divisor with strict normal crossings, meaning its irreducible components are smooth and they intersect each other transversally. If
\textbf{D}:=(D_{i})_{1\leq i\leq s}
are the of components of

, we fix positive integers

. The data of

gives rise to a divisor
\mathfrak{D}
on
\mathfrak{X}
with strict normal crossings. Inspired by
[
BorLar23
]
, where the authors establish an equivalence between the category of parabolic connections on

and the category of logarithmic connections on certain root stacks, we extend their result to orthogonal and symplectic parabolic connections.

(

2.
Orthogonal and symplectic vector bundles on Deligne-Mumford stacks

We would like to extend the notion of symmetric and anti-symmetric bilinear forms on parabolic vector bundles on schemes as well as on vector bundles defined on stacks. This can be achieved using what is known as the
transpose
of a morphism between such bundles. Let us first describe it for vector spaces over a field. Let
V
and
L
be two vector spaces over a field
K
of dimensions
n
and
1
respectively. Let

T:V\rightarrow\Hom(V,L)

be a
K
-linear map. The
transpose
of
T
is the unique
K
-linear map

T^{t}:V\rightarrow\Hom(V,L)

satisfying

(2.0.1)

\displaystyle(T(v))(v^{\prime})=((T^{t})(v^{\prime}))(v)\,\,\forall\,v,v^{\prime}\in V\,.

It is not hard to see that
T^{t}
coincides with the following composition of linear maps:

(2.0.2)

\displaystyle V\simeq V\otimes K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\otimes(L^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}(V,L)^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes\text{Id}_{L}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}V^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L\simeq\text{Hom}(V,L)

where
T^{{}^{\vee}}:\text{Hom}(V,L)^{{}^{\vee}}\rightarrow V^{{}^{\vee}}
is the dual map of
T
.

Next, with
V
and
L
as above, start with a
K
-bilinear map
\phi:V\otimes V\rightarrow L
. Via the Hom-tensor adjunction, consider the map

(2.0.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}:V

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{Hom}(V,L)

(2.0.4)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi(v,\rule{5.69046pt}{0.42677pt})

then
(\widetilde{\phi})^{t}
in (
2.0.1
) is given by sending
v\mapsto\phi(\ \rule{5.69046pt}{0.42677pt}\ ,v)\ \ \forall\,v\in V
.
Thus, checking if the bilinear form

is symmetric or anti-symmetric in the usual sense is same as checking whether
(\widetilde{\phi})^{t}=\widetilde{\phi}
or
(\widetilde{\phi})^{t}=-\widetilde{\phi}
, respectively.

As the maps involved in the description of
T^{t}
in (
2.0.2
) are canonical, it follows that the notion of transpose makes sense if we replace vector spaces with vector bundles. More precisely, let
K
be a field as before. Let

be a vector bundle and

be a line bundle on a
K
-scheme

. Let
\alpha:E\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E\ ,L)
be a vector bundle morphism. Consider the composition (

) where

is replaced with
\alpha
and

is replaced with the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Namely,

(2.0.5)

\displaystyle E\simeq E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\otimes(L^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E\ ,L)^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes\text{Id}_{L}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}E^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L\simeq\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E\ ,L)

We call this composition as the
transpose
of
\alpha
:

(2.0.6)

\displaystyle\alpha^{t}:E\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E\ ,L)\ .

It is clear that
\alpha^{t}
satisfies the relation (
2.0.1
) when

and

are replaced by local sections of

.
The same result was proved in

as well, which we recall here for reference.

Lemma 2.0.7
.

Let

be a scheme over

If the vector bundle

3.
Orthogonal and symplectic parabolic bundles

The notion of parabolic vector bundles over connected smooth projective curves were introduced by Mehta and Seshadri in
[
MehSes80
]
. It was later extended to higher dimensional varieties by Simpson, Maruyama and Yokogawa
[
MarYok92
,
Yok95
,
Sim90
]
. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
. Let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor on
X
. Let us fix a positive integer
r
. We consider the set
\mathbb{R}
as an indexing category, where we have a unique arrow
a\rightarrow b
if and only if
a\leq b
. Let
\textnormal{\bf Vect}(X)
denote the category of algebraic vector bundles on
X
. The following is taken from
[
Yok95
]
.

Definition 3.0.1
.

A
parabolic vector bundle
on
X
with parabolic structure along
D
is a tuple
(E_{\bullet},{\mathfrak{j}}_{E_{\bullet}})
, where

(3.0.2)

\displaystyle E_{\bullet}:{\mathbb{R}}^{op}\longrightarrow\textnormal{\bf Vect}(X)

is a functor, and

(3.0.3)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{j}}_{E_{\bullet}}:E_{\bullet+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)

is a natural isomorphism, making the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{E_{\bullet+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\mathfrak{j}}_{E_{\bullet}}}
\textstyle{E_{\bullet}}
\textstyle{E_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the left vertical arrow is the canonical inclusion. Furthermore, for each
\alpha,\beta
such that
\alpha\leq\beta<\alpha+1
, the quotient
\dfrac{E_{\alpha}}{E_{\beta}}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{D}
-module.
We also require that there exists a sequence of real numbers

(3.0.8)

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots\alpha_{m}<1

such that each
E_{\alpha_{i}}\rightarrow E_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism for all real numbers
\alpha_{i-1}<\alpha\leq\alpha_{i}
(by convention,
\alpha_{0}=0,\alpha_{m+1}=1
). The collection
\{\alpha_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\}
are called
weights
of
(E_{\bullet},{\mathfrak{j}}_{E_{\bullet}})
if furthermore
E_{\alpha_{i+1}}\rightarrow E_{\alpha_{i}}
are not isomorphisms
\forall\ 1\leq i\leq m
.

We shall sometime suppress the isomorphism
{\mathfrak{j}}_{\bullet}
, and denote a parabolic bundle as above simply by
E_{\bullet}
. Any algebraic vector bundle
E
on
X
can be seen as a parabolic vector bundle with parabolic structure along
D
. The resulting parabolic bundle is denoted by
(E)_{\bullet}
, and it is defined as

(3.0.9)

\displaystyle(E)_{t}=E(-[t]D)\ \ \forall\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}\ .\qquad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yok95}{}{}]}}

We say that the resulting parabolic bundle has
special structure
.

For two parabolic bundles
E_{\bullet}
and
E_{\bullet}
, a natural transformation
\phi_{\bullet}:E_{\bullet}\rightarrow F_{\bullet}
is said to be a
parabolic morphism
if the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{E_{\bullet+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{\bullet+1}}
\scriptstyle{{\mathfrak{j}}_{E_{\bullet}}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{F_{\bullet+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\mathfrak{j}}_{F_{\bullet}}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{E_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{\bullet}\otimes\text{Id}}
\textstyle{F_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)}

For any two parabolic bundles
E_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}
, this category admits their tensor product
(E_{\bullet}\otimes F_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
as well as their internal Hom object, namely the parabolic Hom-sheaf
\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,F_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

Bis03
,
BalaBisNag01

Yok95

. In particular, any parabolic bundle
E_{\bullet}
admits a parabolic dual, denoted by
E^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}
:

E_{\bullet}^{{}^{\vee}}:=\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,({\mathcal{O}}_{X})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

where
({\mathcal{O}}_{X})_{\bullet}
is the trivial line bundle

with special parabolic structure as in (

). We refer the reader to
[

]
for further details.

Unless specified otherwise, from here onward we shall consider parabolic bundles with parabolic structure along
D
.

3.1.
Orthogonal and symplectic parabolic bundles

When
X
is a curve, the notion of orthogonal and symplectic parabolic vector bundles over
X
were introduced in
[

]
. Here we extend their definition for higher dimensions. Fix a parabolic line bundle

on
X
. Let
E_{\bullet}
be a parabolic vector bundle of rank
n
together with a parabolic morphism

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{\bullet}:(E_{\bullet}\otimes E_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\longrightarrow L_{\bullet}

The identity parabolic endomorphism of

gives rise to an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module homomorphism

(3.1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet},E_{\bullet})

Now, let
(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}
denotes the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
with the special structure. Recall from (
3.0.9
) that it is defined as

(3.1.3)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{t}=\mathcal{O}_{X}([-t]D)\,\,\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,.\qquad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yok95}{}{}]}}

We claim that the morphism in (

(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,E_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\,.

To see this, first of all, any parabolic sheaf

(3.1.4)

\displaystyle F_{t}\simeq\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}((\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\ ,F_{\bullet})_{t}\ \ \ \,\forall\,{t\in\mathbb{R}}\qquad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yok95}{}{}, Example 3.3]}}

(3.1.5)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle F_{0}\simeq\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}((\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\ ,F_{\bullet})_{0}

(3.1.6)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(\mathcal{O}_{X},F_{0})\simeq\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}((\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\ ,F_{\bullet})\qquad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yok95}{}{}, Definition 3.2]}}

In particular, for the parabolic sheaf

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}\left(E_{\bullet},E_{\bullet}\right)\right)

(3.1.8)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}\left((\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\ ,\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet},E_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\right)\quad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yok95}{}{}, Example 3.3]}}

Thus the morphism (
3.1.2
) gives rise to a morphism of parabolic bundles

(3.1.9)

\displaystyle\iota_{\bullet}:(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet},E_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\simeq(E_{\bullet}^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes E_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

where the last isomorphism follows from
[
Yok95

. Consider the composition

(3.1.10)

\displaystyle E_{\bullet}\simeq(E_{\bullet}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Id}_{E_{\bullet}}\otimes\iota_{\bullet}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}(E_{\bullet}\otimes(E^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes E_{\bullet})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\simeq(E_{\bullet}^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes(E_{\bullet}\otimes E_{\bullet})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Id}\otimes\phi_{\bullet}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}(E_{\bullet}^{{}^{\vee}}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi_{\bullet}}:E_{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\,,L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

Now, one can define the transpose of
\widetilde{\phi_{\bullet}}
along the same lines as we did for usual vector bundles. More generally, assume that we have a parabolic morphism

\psi_{\bullet}:E_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\ .

We have already seen in (

gives rise to a parabolic morphism

(3.1.12)

\displaystyle i_{\bullet}:(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\longrightarrow(L^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

Next, consider the composition

(3.1.13)

\displaystyle E_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}(E_{\bullet}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Id}\otimes i_{\bullet}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}(E_{\bullet}\otimes(L^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}(\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,L_{\bullet})^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes\text{Id}}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}(E^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

of

(3.1.14)

\displaystyle\psi_{\bullet}^{t}:E_{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

Remark 3.1.15
.

The morphism
i_{\bullet}
constructed in (
3.1.12
) is in fact a parabolic isomorphism. To see this, we observe that since
L_{\alpha}
is of rank
1
, for any
\alpha\in[0,1)
it follows from Definition
3.0.1
that
L_{\alpha}=L_{0}
or
L_{\alpha}=L_{1}=L(-D)
. Thus, the set of weights for a line bundle
L_{\bullet}
should be singleton, namely
\{\text{min}(0\leq\alpha<1\mid L_{\alpha}=L_{0})\}
. Now, the collection of weights for the dual line bundle
L^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}
and the tensor product
(L^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}\otimes L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
is known explicitly (cf.
[
Bis03
, §2.1 pp. 310]
), from which it directly follows that

is always a parabolic weight of

, and thus it is the only weight (as the set of weights is singleton). Thus

has the special parabolic structure (

), namely

Thus the morphism
i_{\bullet}:(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(L_{\bullet}\ ,L_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
is completely determined by the map

, which is an isomorphism since
\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(L_{\bullet}\ ,L_{\bullet})\subset\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(L_{0}\ ,L_{0})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. It follows that
i_{\bullet}
is also an isomorphism.

4.
Biswas-Borne correspondence for orthogonal and symplectic parabolic bundles

Consider the stack of
r
-th roots
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{(\mathcal{O}(D),s_{D},r)}
together with the natural morphism of stacks
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow X
as in §
2.4
. Let
\textbf{\text{Vect}}(\mathfrak{X})
denote the category of algebraic vector bundles on
\mathfrak{X}
. Let
\textbf{\text{PVect}}_{\frac{1}{r}}(X,D)
denote the category of parabolic bundles over
X
with parabolic structure along
D
and weights in
\frac{1}{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
. It is clear from Definition
3.0.1
that parabolic bundles with weights in
\frac{1}{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
can as well be regarded as functors

It has been shown in
[
Bor07
]
that the categories
\textbf{\text{PVect}}_{\frac{1}{r}}(X,D)
and
\text{{Vect}}(\mathfrak{X})
are equivalent. Let us recall the correspondence.

Theorem 4.0.1
.

[
Bor07
, Théorème 2.4.7]

There is an equivalence of categories between
\Vect(\mathfrak{X})
and
\textnormal{{PVect}}_{\frac{1}{r}}(X,D)

given by the following correspondence:

\bullet

A vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathfrak{X}
gives rise to the

on
X

given by

\frac{\ell}{r}\longmapsto E_{\frac{\ell}{r}}:=\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}}\mathscr{M}^{-\ell}\right),\,\,\forall\,\ell\,\in\mathbb{Z}.

\bullet

Conversely, suppose
\mathscr{M}
denote the tautological line bundle on

satisfying

[
Bor07

on
X

gives rise to the vector bundle

on
\mathfrak{X}
given by

\mathcal{E}:=\int^{\frac{\ell}{r}\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{*}(E_{\frac{\ell}{r}})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}}}\mathscr{M}^{\otimes\ell}\ ,

Both
\ParVect_{\frac{1}{r}}(X,D)
and
\Vect(\mathfrak{X})
are additive tensor categories which also admit internal Hom objects. The following lemma shows that the equivalence of categories above preserves tensor products, duals and internal Hom objects as well.

Lemma 4.0.2
.

Consider the equivalence between
\Vect(\mathfrak{X})
and
\ParVect_{\frac{1}{r}}(X,D)
in Theorem
4.0.1
. Let
E_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}
be two parabolic bundles on
X
which correspond to the vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathfrak{X}
respectively. Then the parabolic dual
(E_{\bullet}\otimes F_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
on

corresponds to the dual
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}
on
\mathfrak{X}
, and the parabolic

\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\,,F_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
on

corresponds to the

\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}}(\mathcal{E}\,,\mathcal{F})

\mathfrak{X}
. Consequently, the parabolic dual

\mathfrak{X}

Proof.

The correspondence between tensor products follows from

[

. Also, note that by definition

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\vee}}=\mathcal{H}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(\mathcal{E}\ ,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})\ \ \text{and}\ \ E^{{}^{\vee}}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{PH}\,\,\kern-5.0pt{\large om}(E_{\bullet}\ ,(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet})_{\bullet}

where
(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}
denote the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
with special structure (

\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\vee}}
corresponds to

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
on
\mathfrak{X}
corresponds to the parabolic sheaf
(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bullet}
on

To see this, we use the following isomorphism of vector bundles on

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{\ell}{r}\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\left[-\frac{\ell+m}{r}\right]D\right)\right)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}}}\mathscr{M}^{\otimes\ell}\simeq\mathscr{M}^{\otimes-m}\ \ \forall\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\qquad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Bor07}{}{}, Lemme 3.17]}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{\ell}{r}\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\left[-\frac{\ell}{r}\right]D\right)\right)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{X}}}\mathscr{M}^{\otimes\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}\qquad(\text{for}\ m=0)

6.3.
Orthogonal and symplectic logarithmic connections on Deligne-Mumford stacks

The notions of algebraic and logarithmic connections have their natural analogues for a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathfrak{X}

k
. An
effective Cartier divisor

\mathfrak{X}
is a closed substack
\mathfrak{D}\subset\mathfrak{X}
such that its ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{D}}
is invertible. Equivalently, for any étale atlas
U\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}

\mathfrak{D}

strict normal crossing divisor
(sncd for short) if the same is true for its pullback on an étale chart (cf.
[
BorLar23
, pp. 8]

We can construct the sheaf of differentials
\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X}/k}
by taking the sheaf of differentials
\Omega^{1}_{U/k}
for each étale atlas
U\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
(cf. §
2.1
). More generally, for a sncd
\mathfrak{D}
on
\mathfrak{X}
one can obtain a stacky version of the sheaf of logarithmic differentials
\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X}/k}(log\mathfrak{D})
. By a
logarithmic connection
on
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{D})
, we mean a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
together with a
k
-linear morphism

\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X}/k}(\log\mathfrak{D})

such that it gives rise to a logarithmic connection on étale atlas upon pull-back.
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
is said to be
algebraic
if its image lies in
\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X}/k}
. In particular, one can construct a canonical logarithmic connection
d(\mathfrak{D})
on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{D})
. (cf.
[
BorLar23
]
for more details).

Fix a sncd
\mathfrak{D}
, and a logarithmic connection
(\mathcal{L},\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
on
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{D})
so that
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle. Let
(\mathcal{E}\ ,\nabla_{\mathcal{E}})
be another logarithmic connection on
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{D})
. We naturally have a tensor product logarithmic connection

(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{L}\ ,\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\text{Id}_{\mathcal{L}}+\text{Id}_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})

on
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{D})
. Suppose the underlying bundle
\mathcal{E}
also admits an orthogonal or symplectic bilinear form

taking values in
\mathcal{L}
(cf. Definition
2.3.5
). Let us Consider the morphism

associated to it (
2.3.4
), which is an isomorphism by definition. Imitating what we did in Definition
6.2.2
, let us call the logarithmic connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
to be
compatible
with
\varphi
if the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{\mathcal{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\varphi}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X}/k}(\log\mathfrak{D})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

6.4.
Biswas-Borne correspondence for orthogonal and symplectic parabolic connections

We Start with a smooth projective variety
X
over
k
together with a sncd
D
with components
\textbf{D}=(D_{i})_{1\leq i\leq s}
and a multi-index
\textbf{r}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{s}
as before. Consider the stack
\mathfrak{X}_{(\bf{D,r})}
(
6.0.1
) together with the natural projection
\pi:\mathfrak{X}_{({\bf D,r})}\rightarrow X

1\leq i\leq s

on
\mathfrak{X}_{(\bf{D,r})}

\mathfrak{X}_{(\bf{D,r})}

[

, Remark 2.7]

Lemma 6.4.1
.

The induced morphism
\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/k}(\log D)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathfrak{X_{(\bf D,r)}}/k}(\log\mathfrak{D})

Proof.

This is a stacky version of
[
BorLar23

∎

]

with weights in

and the category of strongly parabolic connections on

(Definition

Let

[

]

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varphi}\otimes\text{Id})\circ\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}\circ(\widetilde{\varphi})^{-1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \nabla_{\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\vee}}}\otimes\text{Id}_{\mathcal{L}}+\text{Id}_{\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\vee}}}\otimes\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\ .

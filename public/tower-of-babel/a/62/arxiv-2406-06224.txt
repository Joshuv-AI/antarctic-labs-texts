Introduction

For any positive integer
\ell>1,
a partition of a positive integer is called an
\ell
-regular partition if none of its parts is divisible by
\ell.
Let
b_{\ell}(n)
denotes the number of
\ell
-regular partitions of
n.
The generating function for
b_{\ell}(n)
is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{\ell}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{\ell}}{f_{1}},

where
f_{\ell}
is defined by
f_{\ell}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{\ell m}).

Gordan and Ono
[
7
]
proved a density result on
\ell
-regular partition function
b_{\ell}(n)
. In particular, they proved that if
p
is a prime number and
p^{\hbox{ord}_{p}(\ell)}\geq\sqrt{\ell},
then for any positive integer
j,
the arithmetic density of positive integers
n
such that
b_{\ell}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{j}}
is one. Andrews et al.
[
2
]
found infinite families of congruences modulo
3
for
b_{4}(n).

A
(k,\ell)
-regular bipartition of
n
is a bipartition
(\lambda,\mu)
of
n
such that
\lambda
is a
k
-regular partition and
\mu
is an
\ell
-regular partition.
An
\ell
-regular bipartition of
n
is an ordered pair of
\ell
-regular partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
such that the sum of all of the parts equals
n.
Let
B_{\ell}(n)
denote the number of
\ell
-regular bipartitions of
n.
The generating function of
B_{\ell}(n)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{\ell}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f^{2}_{\ell}}{f_{1}^{2}}.

(1.1)

In this article, we study the arithmetic density of
B_{\ell}(n)
and prove new results similar to that of Gordan and Ono for
b_{\ell}(n)
(see
[
7
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For a positive integer
m,
let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}
be positive integers. Let
\ell=p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\ldots p_{m}^{a_{m}},

If

then for every positive integer

the set

\left\{0<n\leq X:B_{\ell}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{p_{i}^{j}}\right\}

We have the following immediate result as a particular case of the above theorem.

Corollary 1.2

Let

is almost always divisible by

\lim\limits_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{0<n\leq X:B_{p}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{j}}\right\}}{X}=1.

Preliminaries

We recall some basic facts and definition on modular forms. For more details, one can see
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
. We start with some matrix groups. We define

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},ad-bc=1\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\infty}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Let
N
be a positive integer. Then
\Gamma_{0}(N),\Gamma_{1}(N)
and
\Gamma(N)
are defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N):a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{N}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Gamma(N):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}):a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{N},b\equiv c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

A subgroup of
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
is called a congruence subgroup if it contains
\Gamma(N)
for some positive integer
N
and the smallest
N
with this property is called its level. Note that
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and
\Gamma_{1}(N)
are congruence subgroup of level
N,
whereas
\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
are congruence subgroups of level
1.
The index of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in
\Gamma
is

\displaystyle[\Gamma:\Gamma_{0}(N)]=N\prod\limits_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

where
p
runs over the prime divisors of
N
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
denote the upper half of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. The group

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},ad-bc>0\right\},

acts on
\mathbb{H}
by
\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.
We identify
\infty
with
\frac{1}{0}
and define
\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\frac{r}{s}=\frac{ar+bs}{cr+ds},
where
\frac{r}{s}\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
. This gives an action of
\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})
on the extended half plane
\mathbb{H}^{*}=\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
. Suppose that
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
. A cusp of
\Gamma
is an equivalence class in
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
under the action of
\Gamma
.

The group
\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})
also acts on functions
g:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. In particular, suppose that
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})
. If
g(z)
is a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
and
k
is an integer, then define the slash operator
|_{k}
by

\displaystyle(g|_{k}\gamma)(z):=(\det\gamma)^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}g(\gamma z).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of level
N
. A holomorphic function
g:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called a modular form of integer weight
k
on
\Gamma
if the following hold:

(1)

We have

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=(cz+d)^{k}g(z)

for all
z\in\mathbb{H}
and
\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma
.

(2)

If
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
(g|_{k}\gamma)(z)
has a Fourier expnasion of the form

\displaystyle(g|_{k}\gamma)(z):=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}a_{\gamma}(n)q_{N}^{n}

where
q_{N}:=e^{2\pi iz/N}
.

For a positive integer
k
, the complex vector space of modular forms of weight
k
with respect to a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
is denoted by
M_{k}(\Gamma)
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
11

If
\chi

N

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=\chi(d)(cz+d)^{k}g(z)

\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)

.

The relevant modular forms for the results obtained in this article arise from eta-quotients. Recall that the Dedekind eta-function

\displaystyle\eta(z):=q^{1/24}(q;q)_{\infty}=q^{1/24}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})

r=(r_{\delta_{1}},r_{\delta_{2}},\dots,r_{\delta_{i_{M}}})

by

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{r}(n)q^{n}:=\prod\limits_{\delta|M}(q^{\delta};q^{\delta})_{\infty}^{r_{\delta}}=\prod\limits_{\delta|M}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n\delta})^{r_{\delta}}.

Radu and Sellers
[

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{*}

and

\displaystyle P_{m,r}(t):=\left\{t^{{}^{\prime}}:\exists[s]_{24m}\textrm{ such that }t^{{}^{\prime}}\equiv ts+\frac{s-1}{24}\sum\limits_{\delta|M}\delta r_{\delta}\pmod{m}\right\}.

Definition 6.1
.

For positive integers
m
,
M
and
N

and

If

and

) or (

For positive integers

Next, we note that

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
p\geq 3
be a prime number. For a positive integer

We note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\delta|M}r_{\delta}\frac{\gcd^{2}(\delta a+\delta\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{\delta m}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\gcd^{2}(pa+p\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{pm}-2\frac{\gcd^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\gcd^{2}(pa+p\kappa\lambda c,mc)-p\gcd^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,mc))}{pm}

, we have

which implies that
\gcd(a,c)=1
. Thus it follows that
\gcd(a+\kappa\lambda c,c)=1
. Therefore it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle G:={\gcd}^{2}(pa+p\kappa\lambda c,mc)-p{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)\geq-(p-1)m^{2}

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,mc_{p})-p{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)-p{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)>0.

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle={\gcd}^{2}(p(a+\kappa\lambda c),m)-p{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m).

We now assume that
p|m

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m_{p})-p{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m).

7.
Conclusion

By Radu and Seller’s method, for a prime

, we obtain an algorithm in Theorem
1.14
for congruences of the type
